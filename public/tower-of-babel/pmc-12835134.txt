A neuro-fuzzy multi-topology adaptive routing framework for QoS-aware healthcare IoT communications
Authors: M. Shabana Parveen, P. T. V. Bhuvaneswari
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Healthcare IoT network loss their potential of reliability transmission, continuous monitoring, and intervention system due to critical network conditions along with its traffic characteristics. Thereby, it can directly affect the performance of the remote healthcare applications includes healthcare life quality check, patient safety, and clinical effectiveness in terms of QoS and robustness performance. In this paper presents a novel neuro-fuzzy multi-topology adaptive routing (NF-MTAR) method for enhancing reliable transmission of the healthcare IoT network. It integrates the neuro-fuzzy intelligence with multi-topology virtual partitioning enables dynamic optimization of the network resources based on network condition and its traffic characteristic. NF-MTAR method incorporates two unique innovations such as (i) neuro-fuzzy search engine identifies an optimum path to reach specific root node selected by incorporating five key parameters includes traffic flow intensity, resource utilization, residual energy, link quality, and node connectivity. (ii) Virtual Software Defined Networking (V-SDN) provides multi-topology virtual partitioning (elliptical, linear, and random) within the network, carry data transmission over multiple topologies for different traffic critical simultaneously. COOJA simulator is used to create three-layer 6LoWPAN architecture which capable of allowing dynamic network configuration and improve centralized policy management. Evaluation metrics are confirmed that the reasonable improvement is achieved such as high throughput (94.3%), reduce end to end delay (18.4ms), improve energy efficiency (31.2%), network lifetime (42.8%), and reliability (99.8%) by the proposed NF-MTAR method as compared to other state-of-art-methods. Thus, it provides potential improvement for next-generation medical monitoring and intervention system.

Introduction

Healthcare monitoring system has been redefined by allowing patients to be closely monitored, consulting advice to be provided in real time and personal treatment options to be used. Internet of Things (IoT) platform supporting those wider applications within healthcare IoT ecosystems, several medical devices, sensors and different ways of communicating are used to collect, analyze and send vital information about patients onto complex networks1,2. Still, because healthcare data covers emergencies requiring instant response to everyday monitoring and office data, traditional networks find it challenging to ensure the right Quality of Service and reliable delivery needed for both patient and medical staff safety.

Typically, in healthcare IoT, IPv6 over 6LoWPAN routing protocols come with fixed root node choices and equal traffic handling which do not address the changing and many requirements of modern medical monitoring. These common methods have problems such as prioritizing urgent medical data poorly, not adjusting resources promptly as network variables vary and lacking response to changing patient and clinical workload on the network3. Poor network quality can result in delayed emergency help, faulty monitoring of vital signs and unstable methods for the clinic to make medical judgments which may risk patient safety. By adopting Software-Defined Networking (SDN) paradigms, healthcare IoT problems such as network management, enforcing rules and resource optimization, can be addressed more easily4,5. However, the IoT solutions based on SDN do not have the necessary intelligence to address the difficult optimization issues that health applications face which must evaluate minimizing latency, improving throughput, making the network energy efficient and ensuring reliability. Also, today’s adaptive routing systems react to issues after they happen instead of predicting and stopping such issues when needed for critical healthcare services.

Recent studies clearly illustrated research gaps are present and limit the effectiveness of the existing IoT-based healthcare networking. First, the current adaptive routing protocols are normally single objective in terms of optimization, and they tend to favor either energy efficiency or latency minimization but do not offer the multi-dimensional performance requirements of healthcare applications6. Secondly, intelligent routing methods are frequently based on centralized machine learning models, which impose severe computational and possible single points of failure, rendering them unsuitable to healthcare IoT resource-constrained settings7. Third, existing multi-topology routing systems do not have the dynamic reconfiguration of their routing mechanisms needed to support the dynamism of healthcare processes and patient conditions. Lastly, current IoT network QoS architectures are not detailed enough to support medical services, wherein dissimilar medical data demands radically diverse service level assurances8.

Neuro-fuzzy intelligence together with multi-topology adaptive routing is a new technology, which can possibly fill these research gaps by offering intelligent decision-making possibilities to fit in more dynamic network conditions and retain the level of transparency and interpretability needed in healthcare applications9. The integration of the learning capabilities of neural networks with the reasoning capabilities of fuzzy logic systems can allow neuro-fuzzy methods to offer strong performance with uncertain conditions and thus be able to continuously adjust to changing network patterns. This intelligent framework applied to multi-topology network architecture allows dynamically optimizing network partitioning of the virtual network depending on real-time traffic features and QoS needs, which can potentially offer substantial increases in the performance of healthcare IoT networks10–12.

Research motivation and significance

IoT based healthcare applications require reliable data transmission for real time continuous monitoring is essential and it completely relay on IoT technologies. For that purpose, ultra-low latency network is normally considered for collecting the patient data remotely and it would be properly segregated, and it stored in the respective healthcare monitoring database. Such network follows certain defined network connections, where the edge router has permanent connection with root nodes to receive the patient data instantaneously. Due to this, repetitive data and different patient data are overwritten on the same patent field. Thereby, performance degradation may occur if this information is incorporated with intervention system (refer: Fig. 1a). Therefore, to avoid that, advance ultra-low latency network say Extended 6LoWPAN architecture provides more than one edge router is associated to fulfil the specific data acquisition from its fixed root nodes. The problem is to reach the root node of corresponding edge router is difficult under loss of root node connection due to the dynamic network conditions as well as varying traffic characteristic (refer: Fig. 1b). As a result, the corresponding edge router cannot receive the data and wastage of resource availability to compensate for the performance degradation towards impacting patient safety and clinical effectiveness. Therefore, to sort out this problem, dynamic root node selection can be done based on traffic characteristics and virtual portioning of network using software defined network.

Related works

In this section covers the detailed review of recent contribution of work towards Low-latency and high-efficiency signaling is possible as the digital networking landscape improves through new routing solutions, ways to optimize network traffic and advanced communication protocols.

Multi-topological routing in 6G

An open-source software-defined Wide Area Network (SD-WAN) is presented to handle communications between different clouds. Their work demonstrates that SD-WAN is key to linking all sorts of cloud services, resulting in networks that are easy to adapt, secure and reliable13,14. These researchers found that cloud networks play a crucial role in guiding traffic efficiently in high bandwidth and low latency circumstances. In event of real-time systems15, offered a multi-topology routing method to enhance traffic with the applications of IEEE 802.1 Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN). Due to its flexibility to adapt to the new conditions of a network to enable Quality of Services (QoS), this method is significant to systems in industrial automation and vehicular networks because of time and data constraints. Moreover, emphasized on the creation of the routing protocols16 through the design of timely routing style of networks based on MEO and LEO satellites. Therefore, their concepts of merging satellite communication with other diverse networks are quite appropriate to modern requirements of gaining internet access worldwide and supplement to intra-cloud communication plans.

In addition17, discusses the variation in data center interconnectivity through introduction of FMPTCP that is aimed at utilizing the network resources in a more efficient way and minimizing the latency. This work shows that there is a need to have better routing protocols that enable data-driven applications to achieve their speed and data processing goals. Moreover18, touch upon the issue of QoS through building a versatile adaptive multi-hop topology that is intended to meet the needs of a demand-sensitive data center. Through real-time studies of the traffic, they determine how topology in data centers must be changed, which can be an extension of the routing concepts proposed in the previous publications. This flexible nature of this type of network is reminiscent of the multi-agent reinforcement learning in dynamic routing19. Another technique has also emerged, and it is known as meta heuristics that integrates Takagi-Sugeno fuzzy systems with optimization to enhance adaptive routing within the wireless body area network20,21. The article contributes to the discussion of intelligent networks as it demonstrates that AI can have an impact on how routing is performed. Finally, concentrate on time-varying IoT networks with the help of Q-learning to enhance the performance indicators, which is close to the spirit of communication effectiveness optimization in large-scale distributed networks. Their paper highlights that there is a growing dependence on the use of machine learning to improve the ability to dynamically route in a wide range of networking conditions22.

Network slicing approaches

IoT routing protocols are numerous studies that aim at making the transfer of data in 5G networks more efficient and flexible. Consequently, Adaptive Multipath Multi-Channel Energy Efficient (AMMEE) is also provided that gives priority to data rate and energy conservation, relevant in the implementation of IoT in 5G23. The comparison with QoS-AMMEE and QMR tests showed that their approaches are superior in terms of routing and resource management that make the network more reliable due to load- and bandwidth-aware patterns. Besides relying on routing mechanisms, taking advantage of reinforcement learning is key to increased network quality. As an example24, came up with multi-agent techniques to restructure the topology of wireless mesh networks. It deals with the inconveniences associated with changeable network environments by providing flexible routing that can readily adjust to the changes. In addition25, presents a model of routing and scheduling based on a multi-policy deep reinforcement learning strategy. Their methodology is basic when it comes to networks when multiple attributes of operation need to be targeted concurrently.

These observations make it apparent that AI-guided adaptive methodologies are a major factor in contemporary networking solutions. Intelligent networks and machine learning are also used to review the IoT energy harvesting systems that imply the identification of the most suitable path to choose to get the information in energy-saving environments26–28. They demonstrate that strategies in which numerous factors are treated as units can greatly enhance performance of networks in terms of addressing energy issues. Moreover29, said network management is also manageable using controlled SDN overlays that increase the ductility of virtual LANs that result in converging tools that enhance the effectiveness of networks. Moreover, the ideas of graph reinforcement learning introduced in30 are a foundation of a new approach to resource management in ad hoc networks. This new technique is the best way of routing and allocating radio resources because it addresses the numerous problems encountered by wireless networks nowadays31. This paper contributes to understanding the ways in which intelligent systems contribute to the flexible and successful management of networks under various conditions. Lastly, the researchers investigated how federated learning could be organized using blockchain in 5G to manage resources efficiently and sustain network slicing32,33. The paper also describes the reasons why the increasing requirements of ionic solutions are increasingly relying on networking which emphasizes efficiency, security and flexibility.

Materials and methodology

The proposed NF-MTAR framework provides a complete and structured solution to improve health monitoring systems with intelligent routing and management of networks. The architecture starts from the top with the SDN controller layer handling all the control and direction for network management. This layer controls and organizes the network functions by using main rules and flow settings, while also choosing the root in the network, organizing quality-of-service policies and classifying traffic to uphold efficient performance in all kinds of healthcare monitoring. Figure 2 shows the Proposed NF-MTRA framework architecture diagram.

Overview of proposed NF-MTAR

The neuro-fuzzy multi-topology decision engine layer plays a central role including computer ranks and builds the objective function which allows it to react intelligently to recent changes on the network. After evaluating traffic load, the engine sets its route, manages key nodes and looks for ways to keep itself running smoothly and efficiently. Such a system can improve accuracy in reading and update by itself even under the unpredictable changes in the network. This layer makes the extended 6LoWPAN architecture workable, by forming three different virtual networks to manage healthcare dataindicating strong potential for future application in real medical settings upon hardware implementation and clinical validation. Elliptical Topology is created to support critical activities like emergency alerts and monitoring essential services, providing these with high-priority policies and guaranteed speed and allocation of bandwidth. Regular checks on a patient’s vital signs and routine updates about their health are managed through Linear Topology by assuring all traffic receives equal attention. Random Topology is an appropriate choice where the non-vital traffic like background backup and general monitoring is concerned, best-effort progress is used, and the bandwidth can be adjusted and rerouted as the demands vary. Each of the topologies has a method of picking the dynamic root node, which makes the network more workable as per the occurrences within the network and the traffic. NF-MTAR framework suitable for specific real time medical monitoring environment as shown in the healthcare application layer. That implies, this framework has potential of supporting tools of critical chronic diseases such as ECG and blood pressure monitoring and those of regular readings such as temperature or SpO2 levels. Experience layer consists of general data gathering for activity, sleep monitoring and background syncing, as well as analytics for tracking the system’s performance and making health estimates. Combining application demands with selecting the right network topology guarantees every kind of healthcare data gets the appropriate network support it needs according to its criticality and timeliness.

Results and discussion

This section provides proper comprehensive evaluation of the proposed NF-MTAR framework against other state of art methods such as traditional RPL methods (Multi-topology TSN3, Bandwidth aware N-channel16, adaptive methods (Dynamic multi-hop7, adaptive FANET21, Graph RL routing24, optimization methods (TSFIS-GWO12, opt RISQL14, multi-agent RL method (Fuzzy baseline18, Intelligent energy harvesting23, respectively. Evaluation mainly focused on (i) traffic type throughput analysis, (ii) traffic type latency analysis, (iii) temporal and scalability analysis and (iv) statistical validation. Each analysis is performed with more detailed aspects such as comparison with respect to traffic types, improvement profile, distribution analysis, and percentile analysis. These analyses provide strong evidence for performance superiority of the proposed NF-MTAR framework.

Simulation environment and parameters

The proposed NF-MTAR framework is studied by running computer simulations on the COOJA simulator which is part of the Contiki-NG operating system used for 6LoWPAN networks. In the simulation, we used a healthcare scenario with over 100–200 sensor nodes in a heterogeneous network that offered three different virtual formations (elliptical, linear and random) for different types of traffic. The parameters measured by the network were a 50-meter range, different packages sizes from 64 bytes (critical alert data) to 1024 bytes (regular monitoring) and up to 10 packets/second during critical care monitoring versus 1 packet/second for routine logs. The search engine was created using MATLAB Fuzzy Logic Toolbox and needed five input parameters(traffic load, available resources, energy, connectivity degree and past history). It was set up with 125 fuzzy rules taken from expert recommendations within healthcare networking standards. By using back propagation learning with a learning rate of 0.01 and momentum factor of 0.9, the mechanism allowed the neural network to gradually make better routing choices over time as it captured performance data. The extended 6LoWPAN architecture used SDN, with the Open Daylight framework handling central rules for policy and dividing virtual networks properly.

Limitations and future research directions

However, the proposed NF-MTAR framework has achieved major improvements, but some limitations should be considered in the future study. Currently, the neuro-fuzzy search engine follows rule base approach needs expert knowledge to be configured in the first place, but the neural network adaptation mechanism allows continuous optimization. Future research examines automated methods of rule generation by employing the latest machine learning techniques to minimize the need to perform initial configuration. The performance of the NF-MTAR framework under the extreme network conditions, like extreme node failures or extreme interference. Furthermore, security concerns of the V-SDN controller layer and ensuring protection of sensitive healthcare information during dynamic routing adjustments are other critical research directions to be taken. The development of block chain technology to ensure safe and decentralized networks may strengthen the framework when it comes to critical healthcare uses.

Conclusion

This study presented a novel Neuro-Fuzzy Multi-Topology Adaptive Routing (NF-MTAR) framework provides a comprehensive solution to the healthcare IoT networks includes enhance network performance (throughput and latency), reliability, QoS, and energy efficiency under critical challenges. This framework is employed both virtual software defined network and neuro-fuzzy search engine which enables dynamic optimization of network resources based on real-time traffic characteristics and network conditions. The multi-topology virtual portioning is associated with virtual topology arrangement such as elliptical topology for critical traffic, linear topology for regular monitoring data, and random topology for non-critical information instantaneously. Thereby, successfully provides the heterogeneous QoS requirements of healthcare applications by preventing interference between different traffic types. This enables fine-grained resource allocation and optimized performance closely aligns with clinical requirement of achieving 98.7% of packet delivery ratio and 97.3% of QoS satisfaction for critical healthcare data. The neuro-fuzzy search engine utilizes five critical network parameters generated 125 expertly fuzzy rules, provides an optimum path with best root node selection. As a result, achieved 94.3% of network throughput and reduced latency to 18.4ms for critical traffic significantly outperform than other methods include traditional RPL methods, adaptive methods, optimization methods, and multi-agent RL methods, respectively. The V-SDN controller’s policy enforcement matrix ensures consistent QoS delivery across all virtual topologies and contributing to overall system reliability and optimized performance for healthcare applications. The experimental validation has been conducted through comprehensive simulations using COOJA simulator with Contiki-NG operating system demonstrates substantial performance improvements across all critical metrics compared to ten state-of-the-art methods.

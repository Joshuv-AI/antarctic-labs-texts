Signatures in TQFT : Asymptotics and Modularity
Julien Marché, Gregor Masbaum

Abstract
. We study the signature σ g ​ ( q p ) \sigma_{g}(\frac{q}{p}) of SU 2 \mathrm{SU}_{2} -TQFT vector spaces associated to surfaces of genus g g , as a function of the defining root of unity ζ = e i ​ π ​ q / p \zeta=e^{i\pi q/p} . We prove that 1 p 2 ​ σ 2 ​ ( q p ) \frac{1}{p^{2}}\sigma_{2}(\frac{q}{p}) converges to Λ ⁡ ( θ ) = 16 π 3 ​ ∑ n ≥ 1 , odd 1 n 3 ​ sin ⁡ ( n ​ π ​ θ ) \Lambda(\theta)=\frac{16}{\pi^{3}}\sum\limits_{n\geq 1,\textrm{ odd}}\frac{1}{n^{3}\sin(n\pi\theta)} when q p \frac{q}{p} goes to an irrational number θ ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] \theta\in[0,1] under certain conditions. 1.
Introduction

Let
p
be an odd positive integer and
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)
be the cyclotomic field generated by a primitive root of unity
\zeta
of order
2p
. The SU
2
-TQFT constructs for any surface
S_{g}
of genus
g
a finite dimensional
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)
-vector space
\mathcal{V}_{p}(S_{g})
endowed with an Hermitian form
h_{g}
defined over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)
. These vector spaces are part of a modular functor, meaning that they support a projective unitary action of the corresponding mapping class groups and satisfy a list of compatibilities when cutting the surfaces along simple closed curves.

The dimension of
\mathcal{V}_{p}(S_{g})
, given by the celebrated Verlinde formula, has received considerable attention since the nineties. On the other hand, taking
0<q<p
two coprime odd integers, one can embed
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)
into
\mathbb{C}
by sending
\zeta
to
e^{i\pi q/p}
. The Hermitian form
h_{g}
has a signature that we denote by

\sigma_{g}(\textstyle{\frac{q}{p}})=\Sign\Big(\mathcal{V}_{p}(S_{g})\!\!\underset{\zeta=e^{i\pi q/p}}{\otimes}\!\!\mathbb{C},\,h_{g}\Big)

and which has been very little studied. It was shown in
[
DM
]
that for a fixed
\zeta=e^{i\pi q/p}
, these signatures have cut and paste properties very similar to those of the dimensions, which allows to encode them in a single Frobenius algebra
V_{\frac{q}{p}}
. Later it was proved in
[
M
]
that
V_{\frac{q}{p}}
is closely related to the hyperbolic geometry of the two-bridge knot
K(p,q)
. The purpose of this paper is to study signatures in a different direction, by analyzing the asymptotic behavior of
\sigma_{g}(\frac{q}{p})
when
\frac{q}{p}\to\theta
for a fixed
\theta\in[0,1]
.

If we take
q=1
, the Hermitian form
h_{g}
becomes positive definite,
so that
\sigma_{g}(\frac{1}{p})
coincides with the dimension of
\mathcal{V}_{p}(S_{g})
. The problem of computing the asymptotic behavior of the dimensions when
p\to\infty
is well-known, and related to considerations of semi-classical analysis on character varieties
\mathcal{M}_{g}=\Hom(\pi_{1}(S_{g}),\mathrm{SU}_{2})/\mathrm{SU}_{2}
. This led Witten
[
W
]
to show that for
g>1
,

\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{\dim\mathcal{V}_{p}(S_{g})}{p^{3g-3}}=\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal{M}_{g})=2(2\pi^{2})^{1-g}\zeta(2g-2).

In genus
0
and
1
, the hermitian form is positive definite for all values of
q
, so let us consider the first non trivial case
g=2
. In this case, one can write a simple formula for
\sigma_{2}(\frac{q}{p})
:

(1)

\sigma_{2}({\textstyle{\frac{q}{p}}})=\sum_{(j,k,\ell)\in\Delta_{p}}(-1)^{\lfloor{jq}/{p}\rfloor+\lfloor{kq}/{p}\rfloor+\lfloor{\ell q}/{p}\rfloor}

where the sum runs over the set
\Delta_{p}
of triples of integers satisfying

\begin{cases}0<j,k,\ell<p,\\
j<k+\ell,k<j+\ell,\ell<j+k,\\
j+k+\ell<2p,\textrm{ and }j+k+\ell\textrm{ odd}.\end{cases}

Our main result is the following

Theorem
.

For almost all irrational
\theta\in[0,1]
, if we denote by
q_{k}/p_{k}
the sequence of convergents of the continued fraction expansion of
\theta
, then one has

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\sigma_{2}(q_{k}/p_{k})}{p_{k}^{2}}=\frac{16}{\pi^{3}}\sum_{n\geq 1\textrm{ odd}}\frac{1}{n^{3}\sin(n\pi\theta)}.

Figure
1
suggests that the convergence is rapid: the graph of the map on the right hand side is shown in green whereas red dots represent the points
(q/p,\sigma_{2}(q/p)/p^{2})
for
0<q<p<31
.

\sigma_{2}

The proof of this theorem involves several steps and occupies Sections
3
and
4
. We first apply in Section
3
a finite Fourier transform in Equation (
1
) to transform this expression into a sum of evaluations of a rational function at roots of unity indexed by
\Delta_{p}
. We then apply Brion’s theorem to get an explicit trigonometric sum computing
\sigma_{2}(q/p)
(see Theorem
3.1
for the precise statement): this step already presents miraculous cancellations for which we do not have any conceptual explanation.

Then in Section
4
, we analyse the asymptotic behavior of the trigonometric sum in the most technical part of the paper. In order to control its denominators, we need to impose that the coefficients of the continued fraction expansion of
\theta
do not grow too quickly. We use for that some classical results of Khinchin on metric Diophantine approximation and a variation on a technique due to Zaremba.

In higher genus one can also write down a formula for

\sigma_{g}(q/p)
as the sum of signs indexed by lattice points inside some polytope (see
[
BHMV
, Remark 4.12]

Our
\mathcal{V}_{p}
corresponds to the
V_{2p}
of
[
BHMV
]
.
.) But the signs are much more complicated than in Formula (
1
) which prevents us from generalizing our theorem. We briefly discuss in Section
5
how the asymptotics might look like for general surfaces including marked points, and illustrate this by some numerical experiments.

In the last section of the paper, we discuss some modular properties of the signature. We observe that the function

\Lambda(\theta)=\frac{16}{\pi^{3}}\sum_{n\geq 1\textrm{ odd}}\frac{1}{n^{3}\sin(n\pi\theta)}

is the boundary value of an Eichler integral of a modular form of weight
4
for the level
2
group
\Gamma(2)
. In addition to the obvious property
\Lambda(\theta+1)=-\Lambda(\theta)
, it satisfies the following transformation law:

(2)

\Lambda\Big(\frac{\theta}{2\theta+1}\Big)(2\theta+1)^{2}-\Lambda(\theta)=2\theta^{2}+2\theta+1.

This functional equation suggests that a similar integral version holds for signatures as stated in the following conjecture, checked by computer for all
0<q<p<100
.

Conjecture
.

For any odd coprime integers
0<q<p
one has

\sigma_{2}\Big(\frac{q}{2q+p}\Big)-\sigma_{2}\Big(\frac{q}{p}\Big)=2q^{2}+2qp+p^{2}-1.

This formula is reminiscent of the reciprocity formula of Dedekind sums: in fact we will make this analogy more precise in Section
6.1
. We prove that the signature at
\zeta=e^{i\pi q/p}
of the TQFT-vector space associated to the 4 times punctured sphere (with colors
(p-1)/2
at the marked points) is actually equal to a 2-smoothed version,
S(q/p)
, of the Dedekind sum
s(q,p)
. It is then classical that this function
S(q/p)
is the boundary value of a modular form (this time of weight
1/2
) and that it satisfies the following transformation law

S\Big(\frac{q}{2q+p}\Big)-S\Big(\frac{q}{p}\Big)=1.

We provide an elementary proof of these facts for the benefit of the non-specialist reader.

This very satisfactory state of things would hopefully generalize to higher genus as well. However, even in genus 2, a precise (non asymptotic) relation between the signature
\sigma_{g}(q/p)

and the Eichler integral is still lacking. Such a relation would presumably lead to a proof of the conjectured transformation law for
\sigma_{2}
.

To end this introduction, we would like to mention the parallel paper
[
MY
]
in which the first author and S. Yoon compute the asymptotics of the function

\sigma_{g}(q/p)
, but for sequences of the form
\frac{q_{n}}{p_{n}}=\frac{a+bn}{c+dn}
where
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma(2)
. This regime is somewhat orthogonal to the one described in the present paper. The techniques used and the results are also very different. A synthesis of the two papers has yet to be made.

2.
Signatures in TQFT
and signed Verlinde algebras

In this section, we recall the general formula for the signature which is used in the proof of Formula (
1
)
for
\sigma_{2}(\frac{q}{p})
(proved in Lemma
2.3
below). The reader willing to take Formula (
1
) for granted may skip this section at first reading.

Given an odd integer
p
and a coprime odd integer
q
satisfying
0<q<p
, we set
\zeta=e^{i\pi q/{p}}
. We define a semi-simple, commutative Frobenius algebra over the rationals
V_{\frac{q}{p}}
in the following way.

As a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space,
V_{\frac{q}{p}}
has dimension
p-1
, with basis
e_{0},e_{1},\ldots,e_{p-2}
. We endow
V_{\frac{q}{p}}
with a non-degenerate bilinear symmetric form
\eta
such that
\eta(e_{j},e_{k})=0
for
j\neq k
and

(3)

\eta(e_{j},e_{j})=(-1)^{j}\varepsilon_{j+1},\text{ where }\varepsilon_{j}=(-1)^{\lfloor\frac{jq}{p}\rfloor}=\Sign([j]).

Here we have used the quantum integer
[j]=\frac{\zeta^{j}-\zeta^{-j}}{\zeta-\zeta^{-1}}
. In the sequel, we will also use the quantum factorial
[n]!=[1][2]\cdots[n]
.

Let
T_{p}
be the set of triples
(j,k,\ell)
of integers satisfying
0\leq j,k,\ell\leq p-2
,
j+k+\ell\leq 2p-4
,
j+k+\ell
even,
\ell\leq j+k,j\leq k+\ell,k\leq j+\ell
.
Notice that increasing all indices by
1
gives a bijection from
T_{p}
to the set
\Delta_{p}
defined in the introduction. From now on, we follow the TQFT convention that index colors (corresponding to the basis vectors of
V_{\frac{q}{p}}
) from
0
to
p-2
rather than from
1
to
p-1
.

We define a trilinear symmetric form
\omega
on
V_{\frac{q}{p}}
by setting

\omega(e_{j},e_{k},e_{\ell})=0
if
(j,k,\ell)\notin T_{p}
, and otherwise

\omega(e_{j},e_{k},e_{\ell})=(-1)^{\frac{j+k+\ell}{2}}\Sign\Big(\frac{[\frac{j+k+\ell}{2}+1]![\frac{j+k-\ell}{2}]![\frac{j+\ell-k}{2}]![\frac{\ell+k-j}{2}]!}{[j]![k]![\ell]!}\Big)

It was proven in
[
DM
]
that the product
\cdot
implicitly defined by the formula
\omega(x,y,z)=\eta(x\cdot y,z)
defines a structure of commutative, associative, semi-simple Frobenius algebra on
V
, with unit
e_{0}
and co-unit
\varepsilon:V_{\frac{q}{p}}\to\mathbb{Q}
defined by
\varepsilon(e_{0})=1
and
\varepsilon(e_{j})=0
for
j>0
.

This Frobenius algebra was designed for computing signatures of TQFT. Recall that the Witten-Reshetikhin-Turaev SU
2
-TQFT provides a compatible family of projective unitary representations

\rho_{p}^{\lambda}:\mathrm{Mod}(S_{g,n})\to\mathrm{PU}(\mathcal{V}_{p}(S_{g,n},\lambda))

In this formula
S_{g,n}
denotes a compact oriented topological surface of genus
g
with
n
marked points and
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
is a coloring of these points, that is, a collection of
n
integers in
\{0,1,\ldots,p-2\}
. The group
\mathrm{Mod}(S_{g,n})
is the usual mapping class group fixing the marked points and
\mathcal{V}_{p}(S_{g,n},\lambda)
is a finite dimensional vector space over the cyclotomic field
K
of order
2p
. It is endowed with an invariant Hermitian form
h_{g}
defined over
K
. Once
K
is embedded in
\mathbb{C}
by choosing the defining root to be
\zeta=e^{i\pi q/p}
, the Hermitian form on
\mathcal{V}_{p}(S_{g,n},\lambda)\otimes_{K}\mathbb{C}
has signature
\sigma_{g,n}(\frac{q}{p};\lambda)
which strongly depends on the choice of
q
. Notice that as a vector space over
K
,
\mathcal{V}_{p}(S_{g,n},\lambda)
does not depend on the choice of
q
, which justifies the notation.

Any Frobenius algebra has a special element
\Omega
which can be defined by the formula
\Omega=\sum x_{i}y_{i}
where
\eta^{-1}=\sum x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\in V\otimes V
. As
V
is semi-simple,
\Omega
is invertible and satisfies
\varepsilon(x)=\Tr_{V/\mathbb{Q}}(\Omega^{-1}x)
for any
x\in V
.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
DM
]
)
.

One has for any
g,n\geq 0
and any
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\in\{0,\ldots,p-2\}
:

\sigma_{g,n}({\textstyle\frac{q}{p}};\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})=\varepsilon(\Omega^{g}e_{\lambda_{1}}\cdots e_{\lambda_{n}})

where the right hand side is computed in the Frobenius algebra
V_{\frac{q}{p}}
.

In the sequel, when
n=0
, we will denote the signature simply by
\sigma_{g}(\frac{q}{p})
.

Remark 2.2
.

Just like
V_{\frac{q}{p}}
, the
K
-vector space
\mathcal{V}_{p}(S_{1})
has dimension
p-1
and is in a natural way a Frobenius algebra known as the Verlinde algebra of the TQFT. This algebra only depends on
p
, not on
q
. The Frobenius algebra
V_{\frac{q}{p}}
(which does depend on
q
) may be thought of as a signed Verlinde algebra, because the structure constants of the multiplication in
V_{\frac{q}{p}}
coincide up to sign with those of the multiplication in
\mathcal{V}_{p}(S_{1})
in the standard bases. For
q=1
,
\mathcal{V}_{p}(S_{1})
is isomorphic to
V_{\frac{1}{p}}\otimes K
as a Frobenius algebra,
hence
\sigma_{g,n}({\textstyle\frac{1}{p}};\lambda)=\dim\mathcal{V}_{p}(S_{g,n},\lambda)
for all
g,n\geq 0
.

In genus zero
\mathcal{V}_{p}(S_{0})
is the ground field, with the canonical Hermitian form, and in genus
1
the Hermitian form on
\mathcal{V}_{p}(S_{1})
is positive definite for any choice of
q
.
So
\sigma_{0}(\frac{q}{p})=1
and
\sigma_{1}(\frac{q}{p})=p-1
don’t depend on
q
. Thus, for surfaces without marked points interesting things happen only for
g\geq 2
.

Let us illustrate the Frobenius algebra formalism by giving the proof of Formula (
1
) for
\sigma_{2}(\frac{q}{p})
mentioned in the introduction. After the shift of indices from
\Delta_{p}
to
T_{p}
, Formula (
1
) reads as follows.

Lemma 2.3
.

\sigma_{2}({\textstyle\frac{q}{p}})=\sum_{(j,k,\ell)\in T_{p}}\varepsilon_{j+1}\varepsilon_{k+1}\varepsilon_{\ell+1}.

Proof.

The trilinear form
\omega:V^{3}\to\mathbb{Q}
can be viewed in the standard equivalence between Frobenius algebras and (1+1)-dimensional TQFTs, as the image of a pair of pants, interpreted as a cobordism from
S^{1}\amalg S^{1}\amalg S^{1}
to
\emptyset
. Contracting two copies of it using the bilinear form
\eta
corresponds topologically to gluing two pairs of pants along their boundaries, hence to a genus 2 surface. In formulas, this gives (using the orthogonality of the basis):

\sigma_{2}({\textstyle\frac{q}{p}})=\sum_{j,k,\ell}\frac{\omega(e_{j},e_{k},e_{\ell})^{2}}{\eta(e_{j},e_{j})\eta(e_{k},e_{k})\eta(e_{\ell},e_{\ell})}.

The lemma follows from the fact that
\omega(e_{j},e_{k},e_{\ell})=\pm 1
if
(j,k,\ell)\in T_{p}
and
0
otherwise, together with the observation that
(-1)^{j+k+\ell}=1
for
(j,k,\ell)\in T_{p}
.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

By definition, the signature depends on
\zeta=e^{i\pi q/{p}}
, that is, on the choice of
q
modulo
2p
, not just modulo
p
. But
q
and
2p-q
give the same signature, because the formulas only depend on the signs of the quantum integers
[j]
, and these signs are preserved by the transformation
q\mapsto 2p-q
or equivalently

\zeta\mapsto\zeta^{-1}
. Thus we may assume
0<q<p
.

Remark 2.5
.

The
\mathrm{SU}_{2}
-TQFT
\mathcal{V}_{p}
corresponds in Conformal Field Theory to the Wess-Zumino-Witten model at odd level
\ell=p-2
. Similar
\mathrm{SU}_{2}
-TQFTs exist at even levels, that is, when
p
is even. Their signature functions can again be computed using the Frobenius algebra formalism of Theorem
2.1
. But we won’t consider even level
\mathrm{SU}_{2}
-TQFTs in this paper.

3.
A trigonometric formula for the signature in genus 2

Let us now start our investigation of the asymptotic behavior of the signature function. The first result of this paper is the following trigonometric formula for the signature in genus
2
.

Theorem 3.1
.

For
0<q<p
coprime odd integers, we have

\sigma_{2}({{\textstyle\frac{q}{p}}})=\frac{1-p^{2}}{6p^{2}}+\frac{1}{4p^{2}}\sum_{n=1,\,\mathrm{odd}}^{p-2}\frac{f(n,p,q)}{\sin^{3}(\frac{n\pi}{2p})\sin^{2}(\frac{qn\pi}{p})}

where

\displaystyle f(n,p,q)

\displaystyle=(3p-3)\sin(\frac{(2q-1)n\pi}{2p})+(p+1)\sin(\frac{(2q-3)n\pi}{2p})

\displaystyle+(p-1)\sin(\frac{(2q+3)n\pi}{2p})+(3p+3)\sin(\frac{(2q+1)n\pi}{2p}).

Proof.

By a finite Fourier transform over
\mathbb{Z}/2p\mathbb{Z}
, we can write for
m\in\mathbb{Z}
:

(4)

(-1)^{\lfloor m/p\rfloor}=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{n=1,\text{ odd}}^{2p-1}\frac{\sin(\pi n(2m+1)/2p)}{\sin(\pi n/2p)}=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{n=1,\text{ odd}}^{2p-1}\frac{t^{2m+1}-t^{-2m-1}}{t-t^{-1}}\Big|_{t=e^{\frac{i\pi n}{2p}}~.}

In the sequel, we set
\mathrm{O}_{p}=\{1,3,\ldots,2p-1\}
and
\mathrm{O}_{p}^{+}=\{1,3,\ldots,p\}
. Using Formula (
4
) for each sign
\varepsilon_{j+1}=(-1)^{\lfloor q(j+1)/p\rfloor}
and plugging it into the formula of Lemma
2.3
, we get

\sigma_{2}({\textstyle{\frac{q}{p}}})\,\,=\sum_{(j,k,\ell)\in T_{p}}\varepsilon_{j+1}\varepsilon_{k+1}\varepsilon_{\ell+1}=\frac{1}{p^{3}}\sum_{(a,b,c)\in\mathrm{O}_{p}^{3}}\psi(a,b,c)

where

\psi(a,b,c)=\!\!\!\!\sum\limits_{(j,k,l)\in T_{p}}\!\!\!\!\frac{(x^{2q(j+1)+1}-x^{-2q(j+1)-1})(y^{2q(k+1)+1}-y^{-2q(k+1)-1})(z^{2q(\ell+1)+1}-z^{-2q(\ell+1)-1})}{(x-x^{-1})(y-y^{-1})(z-z^{-1})}

with
x=e^{\frac{i\pi a}{2p}}
,
y=e^{\frac{i\pi b}{2p}}
and
z=e^{\frac{i\pi c}{2p}}
.

The function
\psi
is clearly symmetric in its three variables and satisfies also
\psi(2p-a,b,c)=\psi(a,b,c)
. We can hence rewrite
\sigma_{2}(\frac{q}{p})
as

(5)

\sigma_{2}({\textstyle{\frac{q}{p}}})=\frac{1}{p^{3}}\sum_{a,b,c\in\mathrm{O}_{p}^{+}}2^{m_{a}+m_{b}+m_{c}}\psi(a,b,c)

where
m_{a}=1
if
a\neq p
and
m_{p}=0
.

In order to compute
\psi(a,b,c)
, we rewrite it as follows:

(6)

\psi(a,b,c)=\sum\limits_{\varepsilon,\mu,\nu=\pm 1}\varepsilon\mu\nu\frac{x^{\varepsilon}y^{\mu}z^{\nu}F(x^{2q\varepsilon},y^{2q\mu},z^{2q\nu})}{(x-x^{-1})(y-y^{-1})(z-z^{-1})}

where
F
is the polynomial defined by
F(X,Y,Z)=\sum_{(j,k,l)\in T_{p}}X^{j+1}Y^{k+1}Z^{\ell+1}
.

The computation of
F(x^{2q\varepsilon},y^{2q\mu},z^{2q\nu})
is simplified by the following observation: we have

F(X,Y,Z)=X^{p}Y^{p}F(X^{-1},Y^{-1},Z)~,

since the involution
(j,k,\ell)\mapsto(p-2-j,p-2-k,\ell)
preserves the set
T_{p}
. Therefore, since
F
is symmetric in its three variables, whenever
F(X,Y,Z)
is evaluated at numbers
X=x^{2q}
,
Y=y^{2q}
, and
Z=z^{2q}

satisfying
X^{p}=Y^{p}=Z^{p}=-1
, then for any
\varepsilon,\mu,\nu=\pm 1
, we have that
F(X^{\varepsilon},Y^{\mu},Z^{\nu})
is equal to either
F(X,Y,Z)
(when
\varepsilon\mu\nu=1
) or to
F(X^{-1},Y^{-1},Z^{-1})=\overline{F(X,Y,Z)}
(when
\varepsilon\mu\nu=-1
).

Remark 3.2
.

For later use, we note the following special case: when
F(x^{2q},y^{2q},z^{2q})
happens to be real, then
F(x^{2q\varepsilon},y^{2q\mu},z^{2q\nu})

does not depend at all on
\varepsilon,\mu
, or
\nu
, and formula (
6
) for
\psi(a,b,c)
simply becomes
\psi(a,b,c)=F(x^{2q},y^{2q},z^{2q})
.

We are now ready to compute
\psi(a,b,c)
. We will need to consider different cases depending on the number of distinct values taken by
a,b,c
.

I. The case when
a,b,c\in O_{p}^{+}
are pairwise distinct.
We claim that
\psi(a,b,c)=0
in this case. To see this, it suffices to show that
F(x^{2q},y^{2q},z^{2q})=0
, where
x=e^{\frac{i\pi a}{2p}}
,
y=e^{\frac{i\pi b}{2p}}
and
z=e^{\frac{i\pi c}{2p}}
are as above. We prove this by evaluating
F(x^{2q},y^{2q},z^{2q})
using Brion’s formula
[
Br
]
(see
[
BHS
]
for an elementary treatment), as follows.
We can view
T_{p}
as the intersection
\Lambda\cap\Delta
where
\Delta
is a simplex whose vertices are
(0,0,0)
,
(p-2,p-2,0)
,
(p-2,0,p-2)
and
(0,p-2,p-2)
and
\Lambda
is the lattice of triples in
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
with even sum. As
\Lambda
is abstractly isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, we can apply Brion’s formula to write the polynomial
F
as
F(X,Y,Z)=XYZ\,{\mathcal{R}}_{p}(X,Y,Z)
where
{\mathcal{R}}_{p}(X,Y,Z)
is the following sum of four rational functions corresponding to the four vertices of
\Delta
:

{\mathcal{R}}_{p}(X,Y,Z)=\frac{1}{(1-XY)(1-XZ)(1-YZ)}+\frac{X^{p}Y^{p}}{(XY-1)(X-Z)(Y-Z)}\\
+\frac{Y^{p}Z^{p}}{(YZ-1)(Y-X)(Z-X)}+\frac{X^{p}Z^{p}}{(XZ-1)(X-Y)(Z-Y)}

When
a,b,c\in O_{p}^{+}
are pairwise distinct, we can use this rational function
{\mathcal{R}}_{p}
to compute
F(x^{2q},y^{2q},z^{2q})
, as then all denominators are non-zero: the numbers
X=x^{2q}
,
Y=y^{2q}
, and
Z=z^{2q}
are pairwise distinct and satisfy
XY\neq 1,XZ\neq 1,YZ\neq 1
. Furthermore, since these numbers satisfy the relations
X^{p}=Y^{p}=Z^{p}=-1
, we may also use the rational function
{\mathcal{R}}
obtained from

6.1.1.
Relation to Dedekind sums

Let us recall (one of) the definitions of the Dedekind sum, for coprime integers

We give an explicit relation between the two sums,

and

In the terminology of number theorists,

Proposition 6.1
.

For coprime integers
p,q
with

and
q

Remark 6.2

Note that

Proof.

From the finite Fourier transform formula (

By exchanging the sums and computing the geometric sum over

Let us now cut the sum

s(q,2p)=\frac{1}{8p}\sum_{n=1}^{2p-1}\cot(\frac{\pi n}{2p})\cot(\frac{\pi nq}{2p})

This yields the equality

from which the

∎

6.1.2.
A modular interpretation for

The Dedekind sums are known to have a chaotic behavior so that we cannot hope for a result in the spirit of Theorem
4.1
. However, it is known that we can write

as an integral of a modular form. We give the details in this section as we will observe that the limiting series of Theorem

has a similar interpretation. We believe that this similarity hides a general structure that deserves to be discovered.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be the upper half-plane and
\eta:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{C}

\eta(\tau)=e^{i\pi\tau/12}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-e^{2i\pi n\tau})

S\Bigl(\frac{q}{p}\Bigr)=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{\frac{q}{2p}}^{i\infty}d\arg g(\tau)=-\frac{2}{\pi}\lim_{t\raisebox{1.0pt}{{\scalebox{0.5}{$\searrow$}}}0}\arg g\Bigl(\frac{q}{2p}+it\Bigr).

Here the integration goes along the vertical line in
\mathcal{H}
of real part

, and
\arg g(\tau)
stands for the function on
\mathcal{H}
defined by setting
\arg g(i\infty)=\arg 1=0
and extending this to
\tau\in\mathcal{H}
by continuity along any path from
i\infty
to
\tau

Proof.

This can be proved from the expression of

as
2

[
DD
, Section 2.5]

as the half-sum of signs
\varepsilon_{n}

(34)

Here, the first two occurrences of

]-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}[

For

Note that by (

But in (

Proof of Lemma

For

Proof of Lemma

∎

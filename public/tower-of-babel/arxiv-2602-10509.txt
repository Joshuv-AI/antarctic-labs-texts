Stationary periodic solutions to Nonlinear Dirac equations with non-coercive potentials Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject classification: 35A15, 35Q41, 35J50, 81Q05
Fuping Zhang, Ruijun Wu

Abstract
. We obtain periodic solutions for nonlinear Dirac equations with a nonlinear term that is not necessarily coercive. This amounts to study the equation on a three-dimensional torus. The Palais-Smale condition is enhanced by involving a coercive perturbation. Uniform estimates for the critical levels as well as the Sobolev norms for the perturbed solutions are obtained, making it possible to pass to a limit which gives a nontrivial solution.



Keywords
: nonlinear Dirac equation, periodic solution, stationary solution, linking, perturbation

1.
Introduction

In physical models for fermionic fields, the equations of motion often appear as Dirac equations with various nonlinearities.
In this work we consider periodic solutions on
\mathbb{R}^{1+3}
, which are actually spatially periodic solutions and stationary in physical sense i.e. they propagate via a complex phase factor without changing shapes.
Such solutions fails to decay at infinity, and we have to employ a perturbative variational scheme to obtain a nontrivial solution.

More precisely, we will be concerned with the following nonlinear Dirac equation

(1)

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu=0}^{3}i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\Psi-m\Psi+\gamma^{0}\nabla F(\Psi)=0

where
\Psi\colon\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}^{4}
stands for a spinor field,
m>0
corresponds to the mass of the particles, and
F\colon\mathbb{C}^{4}\to\mathbb{R}
is the self-coupling of particles in the system, which are chosen according to the complexity of the physical and chemical models, see e.g.
[
6
,
11
,
16
,
24
,
29
,
30
]
and the references therein.
The
\gamma^{\mu}

The matrix
\gamma^{0}
takes the form

\displaystyle\gamma^{0}=\begin{pmatrix}I_{2}&0\\
0&-I_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&&&\\
&1&&\\
&&-1&\\
&&&-1\end{pmatrix}

and induces a decomposition of the spinors into

As for the others, we denote by

\displaystyle{\sigma^{1}}=\left({\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}{0}&1\\

i&{0}\end{array}}\right),\;{\sigma^{3}}=\left({\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}1&{0}\\
{0}&{-1}\end{array}}\right)

for the Pauli matrices, and the gamma matrices are

\displaystyle{\gamma^{k}}=\left({\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}{0}&{{\sigma_{k}}}\\
{{-\sigma_{k}}}&{0}\end{array}}\right),\quad\text{for}\quad k=1,2,3.

Note that they satisfy the following Clifford relations: for

\displaystyle\sigma^{j}\sigma^{k}+\sigma^{k}\sigma^{j}=2\delta^{jk}I_{2},

\displaystyle\gamma^{j}\gamma^{k}+\gamma^{k}\gamma^{j}=-2\delta^{jk}I_{4}.

Moreover,

(12)

\displaystyle\gamma^{0}\gamma^{k}+\gamma^{k}\gamma^{0}=0,

\displaystyle(\gamma^{0})^{2}=I_{4}.

Then
\sum_{\mu}i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}
is the 4D space-time Dirac operator.
With the time direction involved, it is not elliptic.

An typical ansatz for the solutions are the so-called

\displaystyle\Psi(t,\vec{x})=e^{-iat}\psi(\vec{x})

\displaystyle i\gamma^{k}\partial_{k}\psi+a\gamma^{0}\psi-m\psi+\gamma^{0}\nabla F(\psi)=0.

Equivalently, we are led to consider the equation

\displaystyle-i\gamma^{0}\gamma^{k}\partial_{k}\psi+m\gamma^{0}\psi-a\psi-\nabla F(\psi)=0.

They are referred as the standing periodic waves for (

In quantum fields theory it is often required that the Hamiltonian has some symmetry, and the periodicity is thus frequently imposed.
In one-dimensional dynamics this is usually the case.
According to the features of the particle system, sometimes it is required that the nonlinearity has spatial periodicity, which is not the case here since our

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that (F1-5) hold true.
Then there exists a nontrivial periodic solution for (

The regularity of the solution can be improved if

We outline the proof here.
The equation (
*
‣
1
) admits a variational structure, namely it is the Euler–Lagrange equation of an action functional

Unfortunately this functional

As a remedy we introduce a coercive perturbation multiplied by a small

in Section

Then we show that the perturbed functional meets the Palais-Smale condition, although the

sequences have a bound depending on

Moreover, by crucially using the spectral information of the Dirac operator, we will see that these perturbed functionals admits a linking structure in levels, which turn out to be uniform, i.e. with level estimates being uniform in the perturbation parameter

Though the linking sets are both infinite dimensional, we use the negative gradient flow of the functional together with the Leray-Schauder degree to find a nontrivial linking.
Thus we obtain min-max solutions for the perturbed equations in the end of Section

By using the special structure of

This allows to pass along a subsequence to a nontrivial limit which is the desired nontrivial periodic solution, see Section

We also remark that, when there is an external field, the following equation is also considered:

Assume that (F1-5) and (

Preliminary

In this section we collect some basic facts about the Dirac operators and the working space

2.1.
The Dirac operators

We will denote

(22)

\displaystyle\not{D}=\sum_{k=1}^{3}-i\gamma^{0}\gamma^{k}\partial_{k},

and

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}=\sum_{k=1}^{3}-i\gamma^{0}\gamma^{k}\partial_{k}+m\gamma^{0}.

Note that the coefficients of
\not{D}
satisfy

(23)

\displaystyle(-i\gamma^{0}\gamma^{k})(-i\gamma^{0}\gamma^{j})+(-i\gamma^{0}\gamma^{j})(-i\gamma^{0}\gamma^{k})=-2\delta^{jk}I_{4},\qquad\forall j,k\in\left\{1,2,3\right\}.

Thus
\not{D}
satisfies the geometers’ convention.
Actually, the relation between the gamma matrices and the Pauli matrices already indicates that
\not{D}
is intimately related to the intrinsic Dirac operator of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
or
\mathbb{T}^{3}
.
In matrix form, we have

(24)

\displaystyle\not{D}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\mathbf{\sigma}\cdot\nabla\\
-i\mathbf{\sigma}\cdot\nabla&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\not{\partial}\\
\not{\partial}&0\end{pmatrix}

where

(25)

\displaystyle\not{\partial}\equiv-i\mathbf{\sigma}\cdot\nabla=\sum_{k=1}^{3}-i\sigma^{k}\partial_{k}

denotes the intrinsic Dirac operator on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
(equipped
with the trivial spin structure) acting on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
-valued functions which are precisely the spinors on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
or
\mathbb{T}^{3}
.
This agrees with the fact that the spinor bundle on
\mathbb{R}^{1+3}
restricts on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
to a double of the spinor bundles of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
which is inherited by the quotient
\mathbb{T}^{3}
.
If multiplied by the timelike normal vector field (by Clifford multiplication), namely

(26)

\displaystyle\nu\cdot\not{D}=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_{2}\\
I_{2}&0\end{pmatrix}\not{D}=\begin{pmatrix}\not{\partial}&0\\
0&\not{\partial}\end{pmatrix}

which is a double of the intrinsic Dirac operator.
Thus we will temporarily call
\not{D}
the
geometric Dirac operator
.
Note that the constant functions are parallel spinors for this spinor bundle, which are also in the kernel of
\not{D}
.

Remark 2.1
.

For the case of nontrivial spin structures, the spinor bundle is also nontrivial and the constant functions are not any more sections of the spinor bundle; indeed there are no harmonic spinors for the nontrivial spin structure.

The presence of the kernel of
\not{D}
, which coincides with the constant sections, is crucial for our construction of the variational scheme in this work.
The case of nontrivial spin structures will be the topic of a future work.

In physical models of massive particles, the operator

30

When the spacetime is

This fact will also be used in our treatment of the equation (

‣

Note that

is the operator originally found by P. Dirac in 1928

For these reasons we will call

2.2.
Spectrum of the Dirac operators

2.3.
The working space

With the above spectral information of
\mathbf{D}

We have seen that
\mathbf{D}
is an self-adjoint elliptic operator and the spectrum consists of nonzero eigenvalues.
Let
\tau\colon\mathcal{\mathbb{R}}\to\operatorname{Proj}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3},\mathbb{C}^{4}))

\mathbf{D}
, where
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})

\mathbb{R}

Then the operator
\mathbf{D}
admits the spectral resolution

(47)

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lambda\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau(\lambda).

The absolute value of
\mathbf{D}
, denoted by
|\mathbf{D}|
, is a non-negative self-adjoint operator defined by

|\mathbf{D}|=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\lambda|\,d\tau(\lambda),

with domain
\Dom(|\mathbf{D}|):=\left\{\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3},\mathbb{C}^{4})\mid\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\lambda|^{2}\,\|\tau(d\lambda)\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3},\mathbb{C}^{4})}^{2}<\infty\right\}
.
The operator
|\not{D}|^{\frac{1}{2}}
is similarly defined by

|\mathbf{D}|^{1/2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\lambda|^{1/2}\,d\tau(\lambda).

with domain

(48)

\displaystyle\Dom(|\mathbf{D}|^{1/2})=\left\{\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3},\mathbb{C}^{4})\mid\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\lambda|\,\|\tau(d\lambda)\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3},\mathbb{C}^{4})}^{2}<\infty\right\}.

We will work with the space

(49)

\displaystyle H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T}^{3},\mathbb{C}^{4})\coloneqq\left\{\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3};\mathbb{C}^{4})\;\mid\;\|\psi\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}\equiv\left(\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\||\mathbf{D}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}<+\infty\right\}.

Note that for each
\psi\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T}^{3},\mathbb{C}^{4})
, we have

\int_{M}||\mathbf{D}|^{1/2}\psi|^{2}\,dv=\langle|\mathbf{D}|^{1/2}\psi,|\mathbf{D}|^{1/2}\psi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3},\mathbb{C}^{4})}=\langle|\mathbf{D}|\psi,\psi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3},\mathbb{C}^{4})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\lambda|\,\|E(d\lambda)\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3},\mathbb{C}^{4})}^{2}.

According to the spectral information of
\mathbf{D}
, we define the closed subspaces spanned by eigenspinors of positive [resp. negative] eigenvalues by

(50)

\displaystyle H^{\frac{1}{2},+}\coloneqq\overline{\bigoplus_{\lambda>0}\Eigen(\mathbf{D};\lambda)}^{H^{\frac{1}{2}}},\quad\mbox{resp.}\quad H^{\frac{1}{2},-}\coloneqq\overline{\bigoplus_{\lambda<0}\Eigen(\mathbf{D};\lambda)}^{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

Then we have the following orthogonal decomposition

(51)

\displaystyle H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T}^{3},\mathbb{C}^{4})=H^{\frac{1}{2},+}\oplus H^{\frac{1}{2},-}.

Furthermore, we let

(52)

\displaystyle P^{\pm}\colon H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T}^{3},\mathbb{C}^{4})\to H^{\frac{1}{2},\pm}

denote the orthogonal projections onto the subspaces
H^{\frac{1}{2},\pm}
.
For any
\psi\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}
, we can decompose it as

(53)

\displaystyle\psi=P^{+}\psi+P^{-}\psi\equiv\psi^{+}+\psi^{-}\in H^{\frac{1}{2},+}\oplus H^{\frac{1}{2},-}.

Note that this is also a
L^{2}
orthogonal projection:

(54)

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|\psi^{+}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\psi^{-}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

With the above projectors, we have

(55)

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}=\mathbf{D}(P^{+}+P^{-})=\mathbf{D}P^{+}+\mathbf{D}P^{-},

and

\displaystyle|\mathbf{D}|=\mathbf{D}P^{+}-\mathbf{D}P^{-}.

3.
The variational structure

The equation (
*
‣
1
) is the Euler-Lagrange equation of the functional

(56)

\displaystyle J\colon H^{\frac{1}{2}}(M,\mathbb{C}^{4})\to\mathbb{R}

given by

(57)

\displaystyle J(\psi)=\int_{M}\frac{1}{2}\left<\psi,\mathbf{D}\psi\right>-\frac{a}{2}|\psi|^{2}-F(\psi)\dv.

Recall that the nonlinearity
F
satisfies the hypotheses (F1-5).
In particular,
F(\psi)
fails to be coercive in
\psi
in general.
Actually, in the model example,
F(\psi)=\frac{1}{2}G(\bar{\psi}\psi)
,
using the notation from (
32
), we have

(58)

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}\psi=\left<\gamma^{0}\psi,\psi\right>=|\psi_{\up}|^{2}-|\psi_{\low}|^{2},

\displaystyle|\psi|^{2}=|\psi_{\up}|^{2}+|\psi_{\low}|^{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{4}|\psi^{k}|^{2}.

Note that
\bar{\psi}\psi
may be small, while
|\psi|^{2}
is large.
This kind of nonlinearity frequently arises in various particle models in quantum field theory, which is more complicated than the nonlinearities of the form
G(|\psi|^{2})
.
The approach we take here is motivated from
[
5
,
15
,
21
,
26
]
.
Aside from the strongly indefinite nature of Dirac operators, there are new challenges in the periodic setting, such as the construction of the min-max structure, and the loss of radial solutions.
Methodologically, the powerful Fourier transform on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, which provides rich variational information in
[
15
]
, is no longer available.
Though one can still appeal to the Fourier series expansion of the spinors on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
, we struggle to find a suitable linking structure for this problem which relies heavily on the spectral analysis of
\mathbf{D}
.
We aim to fix these issues and look for new variational periodic solutions.

Ding and Liu
[
9
]
also worked on periodic solutions for nonlinear Dirac equations.
The nonlinearities in their consideration take the form of Ambrosetti-Rabinowitz type, namely of the form
G(|\psi|^{2})
for suitable function
G
.
By detecting the variational structure near
0
and near
\infty
they succeeded in finding min-max periodic solutions.
For the nonlinearity considered here, which fails to be coercive globally, we have to employ a perturbation method to obtain the desired variational periodic solution.

We observe that, with the help of the spectral analysis in Lemma
2.2
, the functional
J
admits a local linking structure as in
[
15
]
.
However, due to the loss of the global coercivity of the nonlinearity
F
in
\psi
, we cannot verify the Palais–Smale condition for
J
.
Fortunately we can still employ the perturbation method in
[
15
]
and obtain firstly the perturbative solutions and secondly an uniform estimates of such solutions, finally a subsequence limit which serves as a nontrivial periodic solution.

4.
Perturbations

For each
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
, consider the functional

(59)

\displaystyle J_{\varepsilon}(\psi)=

\displaystyle J(\psi)-\varepsilon\int_{M}|\psi|^{\alpha_{2}}\dv

(60)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\frac{1}{2}\left<\psi,\mathbf{D}\psi\right>-\frac{a}{2}\left<\psi,\psi\right>-F(\psi)-\varepsilon|\psi|^{\alpha_{2}}\dv.

We will abbreviate
F(\psi)+\varepsilon|\psi|^{\alpha_{2}}
as
F_{\varepsilon}(\psi)
.
Note that
F_{\varepsilon}
’s satisfy similar hypotheses as
F
with possibly different constant
A_{j}
’s.
With an abuse of notation let us assume that

(F1-5)
are satisfied by
F_{\varepsilon}
for any
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
.
The Euler-Lagrange equation of
J_{\varepsilon}
is

(61)

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}\psi-a\psi-\nabla F(\psi)=\varepsilon\alpha_{2}|\psi|^{\alpha_{2}-2}\psi.

4.1.
Verification of the Palais–Smale condition for the perturbed functionals

Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, and let
(\psi_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a Palais-Smale sequence for
J_{\varepsilon}
at a level
c
, namely

(62)

\displaystyle J_{\varepsilon}(\psi_{n})=\int_{M}\frac{1}{2}\langle\psi_{n},\mathbf{D}\psi_{n}\rangle-\frac{a}{2}|\psi_{n}|^{2}-F_{\varepsilon}(\psi_{n})\dv\to c,

(63)

\displaystyle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}J_{\varepsilon}(\psi_{n})=\mathbf{D}\psi_{n}-a\psi_{n}-dF_{\varepsilon}(\psi_{n})\;\to\;0,\qquad\mbox{ in }H^{-\frac{1}{2}}

as
n\to+\infty
.
We need to show that there exists a subsequence converging in
H^{\frac{1}{2}}
to a limit
\psi_{\infty}
which is a weak solution of (
61
).

We first show that the sequence
(\psi_{n})
is bounded in
H^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
To see this, we test (
63
) against
\psi_{n}
and take the difference with (
62
), then by (F3)

(64)

\displaystyle 2J_{\varepsilon}(\psi_{n})-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}J_{\varepsilon}(\psi_{n})[\psi_{n}]=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}F(\psi_{n})[\psi_{n}]-2F(\psi_{n})+\varepsilon(\alpha_{2}-2)|\psi|^{\alpha_{2}}\dv\geq\varepsilon(\alpha_{2}-2)\int_{M}|\psi_{n}|^{\alpha_{2}}\dv,

that is,

(65)

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\psi_{n}|^{\alpha_{2}}\dv\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon(\alpha_{2}-2)}\left(2c+o(1)+o(\|\psi_{n}\|)\right).

As a consequence, in the equation

(66)

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}\psi_{n}=a\psi_{n}+\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}F(\psi_{n})+\varepsilon\alpha_{2}|\psi_{n}|^{\alpha_{2}-2}\psi_{n}+\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}J_{\varepsilon}(\psi_{n}),

the right hand side has the bound

(67)

\displaystyle\|RHS\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\leq

\displaystyle a\|\psi_{n}\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}}+(A_{2}+\varepsilon\alpha_{2})\||\psi_{n}|^{\alpha_{2}-1}\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}}+o(1)

(68)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(a,A_{2},\varepsilon,\alpha_{2},\vol(\mathbb{T}^{3}))\|\psi\|_{L^{\alpha_{2}}}^{\alpha_{2}-1}+o(1)

(69)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(a,A_{2},\varepsilon,\alpha_{2},\vol(\mathbb{T}^{3}))\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon(\alpha_{2}-2)}\left(2c+o(1)+o(\|\psi_{n}\|)\right)\right)^{\frac{\alpha_{2}-1}{\alpha_{2}}}.

Since
\mathbf{D}
has no kernel, so

(70)

\displaystyle\|\psi_{n}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}\leq\|\mathbf{D}\psi_{n}\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}}.

It follows that

(71)

\displaystyle\|\psi_{n}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}\leq C(c,a,A_{2},\varepsilon,\alpha_{2},\vol(\mathbb{T}^{3})).

Here we note that
\alpha_{2}>2
and
\varepsilon>0
are essential for the estimates, and the estimate above blows up as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.
That is also the reason of introducing this perturbation, otherwise we cannot be sure of the boundedness of Palais-Smale sequences for
J
.

Since
H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T}^{3},\mathbb{C}^{4})
is a Hilbert space, we can extract a
weakly
convergent subsequence which will still be denoted by
\psi_{n}
, with a limit
\psi_{\infty}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
It remains to show that
\psi_{\infty}
is a critical point of
J_{\varepsilon}
and the convergence is actually strong.

By the compactness of the Sobolev embedding
H^{\frac{1}{2}}(M)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(M)
for
q<3
and
M
closed, we have

\psi_{n}\to\psi_{\infty}\quad\text{strongly in }L^{q},\quad\forall q<3.

For any test function
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3},\mathbb{C}^{4})
,

dJ_{\varepsilon}(\psi_{n})[\phi]=\int_{M}\left<\phi,\;\mathbf{D}\psi_{n}-a\psi_{n}-\nabla F(\psi_{n})-\varepsilon\alpha_{2}|\psi_{n}|^{\alpha_{2}-2}\psi_{n}\right>\dv=o(\|\phi\|)\to 0.

As
n\to\infty
, we have

•

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left<\phi,\mathbf{D}\psi_{n}\right>\dv\to\int_{M}\left<\phi,\mathbf{D}\psi_{\infty}\right>\dv
by weak
H^{\frac{1}{2}}
-convergence;

•

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left<\phi,a\psi_{n}\right>\dv\to\int_{M}\left<\phi,a\psi_{\infty}\right>\dv
by weak (actually strong)
L^{2}
-convergence;

•

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left<\phi,\nabla F(\psi_{n})\right>\dv\to\int_{M}\left<\phi,\nabla F(\psi_{\infty})\right>\dv
by strong
L^{p}
-convergence and
\nabla F
satisfies subcritical growth condition (F1) and (F2);

•

\displaystyle\varepsilon\alpha_{2}\int_{M}\left<\phi,|\psi_{n}|^{\alpha_{2}-2}\psi_{n}\right>\dv\to\varepsilon\alpha_{2}\int_{M}\left<\phi,|\psi_{\infty}|^{\alpha_{2}-2}\psi_{\infty}\right>\dv
by the
L^{\alpha_{2}-1}
convergence (noting that
\alpha_{2}-1<3
).

Hence, we can pass to the limit to obtain that

\int_{M}\left<\phi,\;\mathbf{D}\psi_{\infty}-a\psi_{\infty}-\nabla F(\psi_{\infty})-\varepsilon\alpha_{2}|\psi_{\infty}|^{\alpha_{2}-2}\psi_{\infty}\right>\dv=0,\qquad\forall\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3},\mathbb{C}^{4}).

That is,
dJ_{\varepsilon}(\psi_{\infty})=0
.

Finally, since
\psi_{n}-\psi_{\infty}\rightharpoonup 0
in
H^{\frac{1}{2}}
, hence strongly in
L^{q}
for any
1<q<3
, we thus have

(72)

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}(\psi_{n}-\psi_{\infty})=a(\psi_{n}-\psi_{\infty})+\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}F(\psi_{n})-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}F(\psi_{\infty})+\varepsilon\alpha_{2}\left(|\psi_{n}|^{\alpha_{2}-2}\psi_{n}-|\psi_{\infty}|^{\alpha_{2}-2}\psi_{\infty}\right)+\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}J_{\varepsilon}(\psi_{n})

and the right hand side above converges to
0
in
H^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, hence

(73)

\displaystyle\|\psi_{n}-\psi_{\infty}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}\leq C\|\mathbf{D}(\psi_{n}-\psi_{\infty})\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\to 0.

Therefore, the Palais-Smale condition holds for
J_{\varepsilon}
, for each
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
.

4.2.
Linking structures for perturbed functionals

We now show that
J_{\varepsilon}
admits a local linking structure in a uniform manner.
Let

(74)

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}=\Span_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\\
0\\
0\end{pmatrix},\;\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\\
0\\
0\end{pmatrix}\right\}

which is a vector space of complex dimension 2.
It consists of parallel spinors on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
which are of upper type.
The key feature is that

(75)

\displaystyle\gamma^{0}\psi=\psi,\qquad\forall\psi\in\mathscr{P}.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
\mu\in(0,m)
, and for any
N\in\left\{1,2,3,4\right\}
, there exists a real
N

\mathscr{E}_{N}\subset H^{\frac{1}{2},+}
and a positive number

(76)

\displaystyle\left(\|P^{-}\psi\|\leq R,\;\mbox{ and }\;P^{+}\psi\in\mathscr{E}_{N},\,\|P^{+}\psi\|=R\right)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\int_{M}\frac{1}{2}\left<\psi,\mathbf{D}\psi\right>-F_{\varepsilon}(\psi)\dv\leq\int_{M}\frac{\mu}{2}|\psi|^{2}\dv.

Note that
R
is chosen independently of
N
and
\varepsilon
.

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

Let
\mathscr{E}_{N}
be a
real

N
-dimensional subspace of
\mathscr{P}
, which consists of constant spinors and has zero lower components, i.e.
\psi_{\low}=0
.
In particular, for any

, we have

(77)

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}e=\left(-i\gamma^{0}\gamma^{k}\partial_{k}+m\gamma^{0}\right)e=\not{D}e+m\gamma^{0}e=me,

(78)

\displaystyle P^{+}e=e,\quad P^{-}\gamma^{0}e=P^{-}e=0,

and

(79)

\displaystyle\bar{e}e=\left<\gamma^{0}e,e\right>=\left<e,e\right>=|e|^{2}=|e_{\up}|^{2}.

Thus,

(80)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\frac{1}{2}\left<e,\mathbf{D}e\right>\dv=\int_{M}\frac{m}{2}|e|^{2}\dv=\frac{1}{2}\|e\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}R^{2}.

To obtain the desired result, we notice that
F_{\varepsilon}(\psi)\geq 0
, thus if

(81)

\displaystyle\|P^{-}\psi\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}=-\int_{M}\frac{1}{2}\left<P^{-}\psi,\mathbf{D}P^{-}\psi\right>\dv\geq\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{\mu}{m})R^{2},

then we immediately have

(82)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\frac{1}{2}\left<\psi,\mathbf{D}\psi\right>-F_{\varepsilon}(\psi)\dv\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\left<P^{+}\psi,\mathbf{D}P^{+}\psi\right>+\left<P^{-}\psi,\mathbf{D}P^{-}\psi\right>\dv

(83)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\mu}{2m}R^{2}=\frac{\mu}{2}\int_{M}|e|^{2}\dv\leq\frac{\mu}{2}\int_{M}|\psi|^{2}\dv.

Otherwise,
\|P^{-}\psi\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}<\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{\mu}{m})R^{2}
, then by Lemma
2.2
we have

(84)

\displaystyle\|P^{-}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{m}\|P^{-}\psi\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2m}\left(1-\frac{\mu}{m}\right)R^{2}.

Then, using (F4), we see that

(85)

\displaystyle-\int_{M}F_{\varepsilon}(\psi)\dv\leq

\displaystyle-\int_{M}A_{3}|\bar{\psi}\psi|^{\nu}-A_{4}\dv=-A_{3}\int_{M}|\bar{\psi}\psi|^{\nu}\dv+A_{4}\cdot\vol(\mathbb{T}^{3}).

The term involving

can be estimated by

(86)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{M}|\bar{\psi}\psi|^{\nu}\dv\right)^{\frac{1}{\nu}}\left(\int_{M}1\dv\right)^{1-\frac{1}{\nu}}\geq

\displaystyle\int_{M}\bar{\psi}\psi\dv

(87)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left<\gamma^{0}\left(P^{+}\psi+P^{-}\psi\right),P^{+}\psi+P^{-}\psi\right>\dv

(88)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}|e|^{2}+\left<\gamma^{0}P^{-}\psi,P^{-}\psi\right>\dv

(89)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\|e\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|P^{-}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

(90)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2m}\left(1+\frac{\mu}{m}\right)R^{2}

(91)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2m}R^{2},

that is,

(92)

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\bar{\psi}\psi|^{\nu}\dv\geq\frac{1}{\vol(\mathbb{T}^{3})^{\nu-1}}\left(\frac{R^{2}}{2m}\right)^{\nu}.

This is essentially the estimates in
[
15

Now we can estimate

(93)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\frac{1}{2}\left<\psi,\mathbf{D}\psi\right>-F_{\varepsilon}(\psi)\dv\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|P^{+}\psi\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}-\|P^{-}\psi\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}-\int_{M}F_{\varepsilon}(\psi)\dv

(94)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}R^{2}-\frac{A_{3}}{\vol(\mathbb{T}^{3})^{\nu-1}(2m)^{\nu}}R^{2\nu}+A_{4}\vol(\mathbb{T}^{3}).

Since
\nu>1
and

, we can choose

such that the RHS above is negative.
Note that this choice of

and

is independent of
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
.
∎

Lemma 5.1

Proposition 5.4
.

There exists a constant
C=C(\vol(\mathbb{T}^{3}),m,a,A_{5},\alpha,\beta,\alpha_{2},c_{2})>0

\displaystyle\|\psi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq C.

Proof.

We proceed by contradiction.
Assume, on the contrary, that

(125)

\displaystyle\lambda_{\varepsilon}\equiv\|\psi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{3}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\rightarrow+\infty

along a sequence
\varepsilon_{n}\rightarrow 0^{+}

For simplicity of notation, we write the divergence for
\varepsilon\to 0
, though we actually mean the divergence along a subsequence.

Note that, for
\delta>0
enough small,

By construction, we have

(128)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\delta\lambda_{\varepsilon}}(0)}|\varphi_{\varepsilon}|^{3}dy=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\delta\lambda_{\varepsilon}}(0)}\frac{|\psi_{\varepsilon}(\theta_{\varepsilon}+\frac{y}{\lambda_{\varepsilon}})|^{3}}{\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{6}}dy

(129)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{3}}\int_{B_{\delta}(\theta_{\varepsilon})}|\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{3}\dv

(130)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{B_{\delta}(\theta_{\varepsilon})}|\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{3}\dv}{\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{3}\dv}\in[C(\delta),1].

Moreover, the rescaled spinor
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
satisfies

(131)

\displaystyle\not{D}\varphi_{\varepsilon}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{3}}(\not{D}\psi_{\varepsilon})(\theta_{\varepsilon}+\frac{y}{\lambda_{\varepsilon}})

(132)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{3}}(-m\gamma^{0}\psi_{\varepsilon}+a\psi_{\varepsilon}+dF(\psi_{\varepsilon})+\varepsilon\alpha_{2}|\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{\alpha_{2}-2}\psi_{\varepsilon})(\theta_{\varepsilon}+\frac{y}{\lambda_{\varepsilon}})

\displaystyle C(m,a)\int_{B_{\delta}(\theta_{\varepsilon})}\frac{|\psi_{\varepsilon}(u)|^{p}}{|\lambda_{\varepsilon}|^{3p-3}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{u}

The perturbation term can be estimated by

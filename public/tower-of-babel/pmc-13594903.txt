Clinical and genomic determinants of early treatment discontinuation in non-small-cell lung cancer: an analysis of the AACR Project GENIE Biopharma Collaborative NSCLC dataset
Authors: D. Hamze, S. Mishra, A. Mohsin, S. Minuit, E.D. Gamsiz Uzun, J.L. Warner
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundEarly treatment discontinuation (ETD) is common in cancer treatment but understudied. We sought to identify clinical and treatment-related factors associated with ETD in a real-world cohort of patients with non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC).Patients and methodsWe analyzed lines of therapy (LoTs) including drugs intended for an indefinite treatment sourced from the American Association for Cancer Research Project Genomics Evidence Neoplasia Information Exchange Biopharma Collaborative NSCLC cohort. Associations between treatment exposure and ETD were evaluated using multivariable logistic regression adjusting for age at LoT initiation, sex, stage, and context (first-line, second-line, third-line, or later). Sensitivity analyses examined mutation-specific targeted therapy exposures and ETD within 14 days of death.ResultsETD occurred in 883 of 2098 eligible LoTs (42%). Programmed cell death protein 1/programmed death-ligand 1 inhibitor exposure was associated with higher odds of ETD [odds ratio (OR) = 2.07, 95% confidence interval (CI) 1.27-3.38], whereas small-molecule inhibitor exposure was associated with lower odds (OR = 0.43, 95% CI 0.26-0.70). Context showed a graded increase in ETD risk: second-line versus first-line (OR = 1.46, 95% CI 1.14-1.87) and third-line or later versus first-line (OR = 2.63, 95% CI 2.09-3.32). In 302 LoTs with an epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) in patients with EGFR-activating mutations, EGFR TKI naivety was associated with lower odds of ETD (OR = 0.34, 95% CI 0.19-0.59). Eighty LoTs ended within 14 days of death, of which 54 (68%) were ETDs.ConclusionsETD in NSCLC is associated with treatment modality, timing, and biology. The drivers of ETD are complex and span medical, social, and psychological domains. This study highlights ETD as a measurable quantity and provides a framework for future studies.

Introduction

Early treatment discontinuation (ETD) is common in oncology practice and represents a clinically meaningful event, often unrelated to disease progression. Prior real-world data analyses of the leading cause of cancer-related death, non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC), have shown that ETD is associated with worse overall survival.1,2 Despite a general understanding that ETD is a clinically relevant endpoint that may reflect treatment toxicity, disease biology, and/or patient vulnerability, it remains understudied.

Prior studies of ETD in NSCLC have largely focused on immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs).3, 4, 5, 6 However, systemic therapies used in NSCLC differ substantially in toxicity profiles, dosing schedules, and expected treatment duration. Although many cytotoxic chemotherapy agents, notably the platinum drugs, are given for a limited duration, most ICIs, tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs), and some cytotoxic chemotherapy agents are typically continued indefinitely. To our knowledge, a method to define ETD across systemic anticancer therapy (SACT) in NSCLC has not yet been determined. Although prior studies clearly demonstrate that ETD matters, less is known about which factors may be associated with ETD across the different classes of SACT used in routine clinical practice.

To address these gaps, we leveraged the American Association for Cancer Research (AACR) Project Genomics Evidence Neoplasia Information Exchange (GENIE) Biopharma Collaborative (BPC), a large multi-institutional real-world oncology dataset that links detailed systemic therapy exposure data to standardized clinical and genomic variables.7 The AACR Project GENIE initiative was established as a freely available public registry to support large-scale analyses of precision oncology outcomes across multiple institutions and addresses key limitations of single-center observational studies.8,9 Using an integrated analytic framework, we examined clinical and genomic factors associated with ETD across therapy classes.

Materials and methods

The GENIE BPC NSCLC v2.0-public dataset was used, which is available through the GENIE consortium website.10,11 In brief, the dataset includes patients diagnosed with NSCLC who underwent clinical-grade next-generation sequencing (NGS) between 2014 and 2018 at four participating GENIE institutions: Dana-Farber Cancer Institute, Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center, University Health Network, and Vanderbilt University.

We restricted our analysis to drugs used for the treatment of NSCLC and most likely to be administered indefinitely: (i) programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) and programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) inhibitors; (ii) small-molecule inhibitors (SMIs) of ALK receptor tyrosine kinase (ALK)/ROS proto-oncogene 1, receptor tyrosine kinase (ROS1), B-Raf proto-oncogene, serine/threonine kinase (BRAF)/mitogen-activated protein kinase kinase (MEK), epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR), or multiple tyrosine kinases; (iii) monoclonal antibodies targeting EGFR or vascular endothelial growth factor receptor (VEGFR); and (iv) select cytotoxic chemotherapy (Table 1). Multidrug regimens were allowed provided that either (i) all drugs were in the eligible set of drugs or (ii) companion drugs were any of the following drugs typically prescribed for a finite period: (i) platinum agents, (ii) taxanes other than docetaxel, (iii) gemcitabine, or (iv) cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated protein 4 (CTLA-4) inhibitors. To decrease the confounding introduced by differential discontinuation, we required the regimens containing multiple eligible drugs to have an end date for these drugs within 1 month. Given that docetaxel is often given for a finite duration (e.g. as part of a platinum doublet or in combination with vinorelbine), only docetaxel monotherapy and docetaxel in combination with ramucirumab were allowed.

Only lines of therapy (LoTs) for the index NSCLC were considered; given that multiple primary malignancies are common, patients with nonindex cancers were not excluded. For the small number of patients with more than one NSCLC diagnosis, only the first was included. LoTs were eligible for analysis if all drugs had a definite start and stop date and were discrete from the preceding or subsequent LoT. For each treatment, the LoT was derived on the basis of the chronological order of regimens received for the index cancer and categorized as first-line (1L), second-line (2L), or third-line or later (3L+).

ETD cut-offs for ICIs and SMIs were defined using prespecified clinical expectations and refined using data-driven modeling approaches (Gaussian mixture modeling and classification and regression trees; additional details are provided in the Supplementary Methods, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmorw.2026.100779).12,13 These methods yielded consensus thresholds of 4.3 months for ICIs and 3 months for SMIs. For cytotoxic chemotherapy, ETD was defined as discontinuation before a third cycle of therapy, i.e. 6 weeks for a typical 3-week cyclic regimen. For LoTs with more than one modality (e.g. chemotherapy plus an ICI), ETD was defined by the earliest cut-off of the modalities present in the regimen.

In the primary analysis, multivariable logistic regression was used to test for associations between ETD and age, biologic sex, stage, LoT, and treatment modality. These variables were selected on the basis of clinical relevance and data completeness. Treatment modality was modeled as binary treatment exposures, such that odds ratios (ORs) for treatment exposure represent the association between exposure to a given treatment class and no exposure to that class, holding all other covariates constant. To assess whether results were sensitive to the specific ETD cut-offs chosen, a sensitivity analysis varying each modality’s threshold across a clinically plausible range was conducted.

Genomic alterations were excluded from the primary analysis because of heterogeneity in data availability and limited sample sizes for mutation-defined subgroups. Three mutation-based analyses were conducted as predefined sensitivity analyses, with multivariable modeling carried out when event counts permitted.

For EGFR-mutated NSCLC, a cohort was defined on the basis of expected responsiveness to EGFR TKIs. This subgroup included patients with activating EGFR mutations (exon 19 deletions and/or p.L858R substitutions) who received an EGFR TKI. LoTs were excluded if an EGFR p.T790M mutation was ever present and the administered TKI was other than osimertinib.14 Within the EGFR TKI-exposed cohort, patients were further stratified by EGFR TKI naivety, defined as the first LoT in which an EGFR TKI was administered for a given patient.

Parallel sensitivity analyses were conducted for BRAF/MEK and ALK/ROS inhibitor exposures, defined on the basis of receipt of the corresponding targeted agents.

In a final sensitivity analysis, we assessed the frequency with which the last treatment for the index cancer was discontinued within 14 days of death. This assessment was separate from the main analysis because some of the patients in the analyzed cohort were still living at the time the dataset was assembled and many patients received multiple LoTs.

All analyses were conducted in R version 4.5.2 (R Foundation, Vienna, Austria). The code used for analysis is provided in the Supplementary Methods (available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmorw.2026.100779).

Results

Cohort attrition and LoT eligibility

Of the initial 1846 patients with NSCLC, 1397 (76%) had one or more systemic treatment regimens recorded in the BPC data, with a total of 4032 LoTs. After application of the inclusion/exclusion criteria outlined above, 1035 patients with a total of 2098 systemic treatment regimens remained. The major reasons for attrition were LoTs that did not have any of the drugs of interest, followed by removal of LoTs for nonindex cancers (Supplementary Table S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmorw.2026.100779).

Patient-level characteristics

At the patient level (N = 1035), 599 (58%) experienced one or more ETD events. There was a slightly higher proportion of females who never experienced ETD compared with those who did (60% versus 54%). There was also a higher proportion of patients with stage I-III cancer who never experienced ETD compared with those who did (45% versus 38%).15 Patients who never experienced ETD had fewer LoTs compared with those who did (2 [interquartile range {IQR} 1-3] versus 3 {IQR 2-5}). Overall, 65% of the patients in the cohort were reported as deceased, with a higher proportion of those who experienced ETD being reported as deceased (77%) compared with those who did not experience ETD (49%). Age at diagnosis, race, ethnicity, and smoking status were similar across the groups. Summary characteristics are shown in Table 3. Primary analysis results

Results of the primary analysis are shown in Figure 2. Exposure to PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors (alone or in combination) was associated with significantly higher odds of ETD [OR = 2.07, 95% confidence interval (CI) 1.27-3.38, P < 0.01]. In contrast, exposure to SMIs (alone or in combination) was associated with significantly lower odds of ETD (OR = 0.43, 95% CI 0.26-0.70, P < 0.01). Exposure to EGFR/VEGFR monoclonal antibodies (alone or in combination) also seemed to be associated with lower odds of ETD (OR = 0.71, 95% CI 0.52-0.96, P = 0.02). Chemotherapy exposure (alone or in combination) was not associated with ETD in the adjusted model (OR = 0.71, 95% CI 0.45-1.13, P = 0.14).Figure 2Forest plot of multivariable logistic regression conveying associations with ETD. LoT demonstrated a strong graded association with ETD. Compared with 1L treatment as the reference, 2L regimens were associated with increased odds of ETD (OR = 1.46, 95% CI 1.14-1.87, P < 0.01), whereas 3L+ regimens exhibited a large magnitude of odds (OR = 2.63, 95% CI 2.09-3.32, P < 0.01). An advanced disease stage at diagnosis was also independently associated with ETD. Regimens administered in patients with stage IV disease were associated with higher odds of ETD compared with those administered in patients with earlier-stage disease (OR = 1.29, 95% CI 1.06-1.57, P = 0.01). Age at the time of LoT initiation and sex were not significantly associated with ETD in the primary model (P = 0.47 and P = 0.26, respectively) (Figure 2). When varying the ETD cut-off, the direction and statistical significance of the ICI and SMI associations were stable across the range, whereas the chemotherapy-associated OR was not stable in direction across a range of plausible cut-offs (Supplementary Table S2 and Supplementary Figures S1 and S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmorw.2026.100779).

Targeted therapy secondary analyses

In the predefined secondary analysis focused on EGFR TKI-containing regimens, 359 of the total 539 LoTs in this subpopulation included an EGFR TKI in patients with an activating EGFR mutation. Of these, 57 (16%) were removed because of the presence of an EGFR p.T790M mutation and treatment with a drug other than osimertinib. Of the remaining 302 EGFR TKI-containing regimens that were included, 77 (25%) met the criteria for ETD.

When stratified by EGFR TKI exposure history, a discernable difference in ETD rates was observed. Among EGFR-naïve LoTs, 21 of 140 (15%) experienced ETD, compared with 56 of 162 (35%) among EGFR-exposed LoTs. In a multivariable logistic regression model adjusting for age at the initiation of the LoT, sex, stage, and EGFR TKI exposure history, only EGFR-naïve status was independently associated with reduced odds of ETD (OR = 0.34, 95% CI 0.19-0.59, P < 0.01). Full results of the model are shown in Supplementary Table S3 (available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmorw.2026.100779).

The BRAF/MEK and ALK/ROS secondary analyses are reported in Supplementary Results (available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmorw.2026.100779).

Secondary analysis of treatment discontinuation near death

A total of 689 LoTs (33%) were determined to be the final LoT for the index NSCLC. Final LoTs were split evenly across treatment contexts, with 218 (32%) occurring in 1L, 188 (27%) in 2L, and 283 (41%) in 3L+. Overall, 481 of 689 (70%) were associated with a documented death. The median interval from final LoT discontinuation to death was 52 days (IQR 22-129 days), with 80 LoTs (17%) discontinued within 14 days of death. Of these 80 discontinuations, 54 (68%) were considered to be ETD.

Discussion

In this multi-institutional real-world analysis, ETD was common in NSCLC and varied by treatment modality and LoT, with higher rates observed in later-line regimens. Age and sex were not independently associated with ETD. These findings suggest that ETD reflects a convergence of treatment-specific tolerability, disease biology, and clinical context rather than patient characteristics alone. By evaluating ETD across treatment classes within a unified analytic framework, this study extends prior single-modality analyses and highlights ETD as a meaningful real-world indicator of treatment durability.2

The higher likelihood of early discontinuation observed among ICI-containing regimens should be interpreted in the context of real-world treatment complexity. In this cohort, ICIs were commonly administered in later LoTs and to clinically heterogeneous populations, where early discontinuation may reflect rapid disease progression, immune-related toxicity, or competing clinical priorities. Prior real-world studies have similarly reported substantial rates of ICI discontinuation that are not fully attributable to trial-defined adverse events, underscoring the differences between trial protocols and routine practice.5 In contrast, exposure to SMIs was associated with a lower likelihood of ETD. Oral targeted therapies are typically self-administered in a home environment and are taken with the expectation of prolonged disease control. They are often continued despite low-grade chronic toxicities, supported by dose modifications and outpatient management strategies.16 Patients receiving these agents also represent a biologically selected population with oncogene-driven disease and higher initial treatment responsiveness. These features likely contribute to fewer instances of ETD in routine care, although discontinuation may still occur with the development of resistance or cumulative toxicity over time.17 Notably, cytotoxic chemotherapy exposure was not significantly associated with ETD. This may reflect situations in which planned completion and premature discontinuation coincide, despite our efforts to exclude agents typically stopped on a schedule (e.g. platinum agents). Patients receiving chemotherapy also spanned a broad range of clinical contexts and LoTs, potentially diluting any true signal, and the shorter threshold itself presents a narrower window for detecting meaningful separation between early and nonearly discontinuation. Because treatment selection reflects underlying histology, disease trajectory, prior treatment exposure, and clinical decision making rather than random assignment, the results may reflect differences in the patients selected for each therapy rather than intrinsic properties of the therapies themselves.

LoT emerged as a strong and consistent correlate of ETD, with progressively higher number of ETDs observed in later treatment lines. This pattern likely reflects a combination of diminishing therapeutic benefit, cumulative treatment-related toxicity, and greater clinical frailty as the disease advances. Importantly, the association between later LoTs and ETD persisted after adjustment for treatment modality, suggesting that treatment sequencing itself plays a central role in determining real-world treatment durability. These findings align with other studies demonstrating decreasing treatment persistence and effectiveness with successive LoTs.18 Of note, our definitions of LoT preceded the publication of the European Society for Medical Oncology adaptation of the Lines of Systemic Therapy framework.19 It is possible that the application of this or similar standardized frameworks to GENIE BPC and related real-world datasets may facilitate more consistent LoT enumeration across studies.

Prior clinical trial data provide limited context for interpreting the observed differences in ETD between EGFR TKI-naïve and non-naïve LoTs. To our knowledge, there are no published phase III randomized clinical trials examining a different EGFR TKI after progression on a first EGFR TKI and such trials are unlikely to be conducted given the rise of effective alternative therapies such as antibody-drug conjugates and bispecific antibodies.20,21 The existing prospective trial literature does support the general expectation that progression-free survival intervals may differ between EGFR TKIs. However, trial populations are highly selective and closely monitored, which may not reflect real-world treatment conditions.22 Our findings build on this framework by showing, at a multi-institutional level, that prior EGFR TKI exposure is associated with a higher likelihood of ETD in real-world settings. This pattern may reflect the combined effects of resistance biology and cumulative toxicity after prior targeted therapy. By capturing these trends across diverse institutions and LoTs, our analysis supports ETD as a practical real-world indicator of reduced treatment durability in EGFR-mutated NSCLC.

It is important to note that ETD is not synonymous with ‘inappropriate’ discontinuation and it does not reflect the provider-centric terminology of ‘medication nonadherence’ used in the older literature.23 In fact, ETD may be entirely appropriate in certain circumstances. The secondary analysis of ETD within proximity to death showed a much higher rate of ETD within 14 days of death (68%) compared with the overall observed rate of ETD of 42%. Given that avoidance of anticancer therapies within 14 days of death is a quality indicator per programs such as the American Society of Clinical Oncology’s Quality Oncology Practice Initiative (QOPI) and the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services’ Enhancing Oncology Model, ETD in this setting appears to be entirely appropriate.24,25 ETD may also reflect enrolment in new clinical trials or evolving standards of care. Future work should focus on further refinement of our ETD definition to attempt to distinguish appropriate ETD from inappropriate ETD considering differing perspectives from patients and providers and the importance of shared decision making.26

We chose a binary ETD cut-off rather than modeling treatment duration as a continuous or time-to-event outcome for several reasons. A binary threshold is clinically intuitive and readily comparable across treatment modalities with fundamentally different expected durations, which would be difficult to achieve with a single continuous duration scale. It also aligns with how discontinuation is already operationalized in existing quality frameworks such as QOPI. We view the binary ETD framework as an interpretable starting point; future work modeling duration continuously may provide a complementary and more granular perspective on treatment discontinuation.

ETD is a complex phenomenon with clinical, economic, and social determinants. Although side effects of treatment are likely to be a major driver of ETD, numerous other factors should be considered. For example, financial toxicity has long been described as a major driver of ETD and this is likely worsening with the increasing expense of cancer drugs in the United States and globally.27,28 Nonclinical factors including transportation barriers, limited caregiver support, and misalignment of patient and provider goals also contribute to early discontinuation.29, 30, 31, 32, 33 As a health care behavior that is stigmatized by health care providers, patient communities, and many patients themselves, ETD in cancer and other disease domains is particularly difficult to study in real-world settings.34, 35, 36, 37

We acknowledge that our study has some limitations. Although it adds complexity, modality-specific ETD thresholds were believed to be necessary because modalities differ fundamentally in administration and/or onset of effect: chemotherapy has rapid onset of effect and is given in discrete, fixed-length cycles; ICIs are given at intervals but have a delayed onset of effect; and SMIs are prescribed for indefinite, continuous self-administration—making a single universal cut-off clinically inappropriate across modalities. We concede that our definition of specific ETD cut-offs is empirically derived and that alternative definitions could be considered.

Restricting EGFR TKIs other than osimertinib in patients with EGFR p.T790M mutations likely resulted in overexclusion as many EGFR p.T790M mutations are acquired during therapy.14 However, few patients in the GENIE BPC NSCLC cohort have more than one sequenced sample, such that a full reconstruction of the acquisition of resistance in relation to EGFR TKI exposure is not possible. Importantly, the GENIE dataset lacks information on key social determinants of health, including financial well-being, caregiver support, and psychological factors, which may influence treatment and early discontinuation. The dataset also does not capture comorbidity or metastatic burden, concomitant medications, or granular treatment setting (e.g. main infusion center versus satellite facility); each of these factors could plausibly influence both treatment selection and the likelihood of ETD and residual confounding is likely. All four participating GENIE BPC institutions are large, NGS-capable academic centers, further limiting our ability to assess or generalize the effect of treatment setting, such as community practice, on ETD. In addition, the analytic unit in this study was LoT rather than the individual patient and 58% of patients contributed more than one LoT. Observations from the same patient are therefore unlikely to be fully independent, which the primary logistic regression model does not account for. Although methods such as generalized estimating equations or mixed-effects models can address within-patient clustering, the sparse event counts within several subgroups limited our ability to reliably implement such approaches in the present analysis. As a result, standard errors in the primary model may be modestly underestimated and this should be considered when interpreting the precision of the reported associations.

The period of GENIE data collection may limit generalizability to contemporary practice, particularly given the evolving 1L use of targeted therapies and ICIs in metastatic NSCLC.38 Another practical limitation is that comprehensive information on all molecular testing carried out is unavailable. ALK rearrangements are a notable example but patients may also have undergone single-gene testing of EGFR and other actionable targets before NGS, which is not captured in GENIE. As a result, we cannot fully assess whether nontargeted 1L therapy was clinically appropriate in all cases. Our approach could not account for partial ETD, where one agent is prematurely discontinued and others are continued; although most LoTs in GENIE BPC were single agents, recent trends in NSCLC treatment are toward combination agents of different modalities with the intention to continue treatment for a prolonged period.39, 40, 41 We were unable to include race or ethnicity in the model as most patients in the GENIE BPC NSCLC cohort were non-Hispanic white. Additionally, patients who present to and are retained at tertiary care institutions may self-select on the basis of disease severity, socioeconomic resources, insurance access, or willingness to pursue specialized care and this institutional selection may also limit the generalizability of our findings to community-based or nonacademic practice settings.

Despite these limitations, we have established a generalizable analytic framework for evaluating ETD that can be applied to future releases of GENIE BPC and to other real-world data and real-world evidence sources. Collectively, our analyses demonstrate that ETD is common in real-world oncology practice and is strongly influenced by treatment exposure, LoT, and molecular context. Binary exposure modeling revealed distinct risk profiles across treatment classes, whereas secondary analyses highlighted the clinical importance of treatment sequencing, particularly in EGFR-mutated NSCLC.

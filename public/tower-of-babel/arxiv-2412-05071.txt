A Motivic Riemann-Roch Theorem for Deligne-Mumford Stacks
Utsav Choudhury, Neeraj Deshmukh, Amit Hogadi

Abstract
. We develop a motivic cohomology theory, representable in the Voevodsky’s triangulated category of motives, for smooth separated Deligne-Mumford stacks and show that the resulting higher Chow groups are canonically isomorphic to the higher K K -theory of such stacks. This generalises the Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch theorem to the category of smooth Deligne-Mumford stacks.



1.
Introduction

Let
k
be a field and let
X
be a smooth
k
-scheme. The (higher) algebraic
K
-theory of
X
is defined by the higher homotopy groups of the classifying space of a category associated with the category of vector bundles on
X
. On the other hand Bloch’s (higher) Chow groups
[
Blo
]
are described as cohomology groups of Bloch’s cycle complexes in terms of generators (special algebraic cycles on
X\times\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
) and relations (homotopies parametrized by the path object
\mathbb{A}^{1}
). The Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch theorem provides an isomorphism between the rational higher
K
-groups of
X
and higher Chow groups
[
Blo
]
. Later, Voevodsky proved that the higher Chow groups of
X
are representable in the triangulated category of motives
[
MVW
]
,
\mathbf{DM}^{\mathit{eff}}(k,{\mathbb{Z}})
. More precisely, there are motivic complexes
{\mathbb{Z}}(i)[n]\in\mathbf{DM}^{\mathit{eff}}(k,{\mathbb{Z}})
and
M(X)\in\mathbf{DM}^{\mathit{eff}}(k,{\mathbb{Z}})
(the motive of
X
) such that we have the following chain of natural isomorphisms,

H^{n,i}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})\cong Hom_{\mathbf{DM}^{\mathit{eff}}(k,{\mathbb{Z}})}(M(X),{\mathbb{Z}}(i)[n])\cong CH^{i}(X,2i-n)

between motivic cohomology groups (defined as hypercohomology of a complex of sheaves) on the left
[
MVW
]
and higher Chow groups on the right. In particular, for smooth
k
-schemes we get a canonical isomorphism between suitable motivic cohomology groups and higher
K
-groups, rationally.

Algebraic stacks arise naturally in the study of families of algebraic varieties because many moduli problems do not give fine moduli space and therefore many important constructions (e.g, universal families) are not possible. Enlarging the category of schemes to include algebraic stacks resolve these issues. Therefore it is natural to search for extensions to the category of algebraic stacks of theorems which are true for varieties. In this article we extend the results mentioned in the previous paragraph to the category of smooth Deligne-Mumford stacks.

Our first goal is to construct a suitable notion of higher Chow groups for a Deligne-Mumford stack rationally which is representable in
\mathbf{DM}^{\mathit{eff}}(k,{\mathbb{Q}})
. The integral Borel-style higher Chow groups of quotient stacks defined by Edidin and Graham is known to be representable in
\mathbf{DM}^{\mathit{eff}}(k,{\mathbb{Z}})
(see
[
CDH
]
). Rationally, these Borel-style higher Chow groups of a smooth Deligne-Mumford stack are isomorphic to the higher Chow groups of its coarse moduli space. Hence, it satisfies étale descent. Therefore, these higher Chow groups of Deligne-Mumford stacks are not suitable for Riemann-Roch type isomorphisms as
K
-theory of Deligne-Mumford stacks does not satisfy étale descent rationally. In
[
Toe3
]
, Toën proved that rationally
K
theory of a smooth Deligne-Mumford stack
\mathcal{X}
is isomorphic to the twisted (by character sheaf) étale
K
-theory of
C^{t}_{\mathcal{X}}
, the stack classifying cyclic subgroups of automorphisms. Inspired by this, we construct a motive
M_{\chi}(\mathcal{X})\in\mathbf{DM}^{\mathit{eff}}(k,{\mathbb{Q}})
of a smooth Deligne-Mumford stack
\mathcal{X}
and using this define motivic cohomology
H^{p,q}_{\chi}(\mathcal{X},{\mathbb{Q}})
. This is the main construction of this article. We use this construction to obtain the following motivic version of Toën’s Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch isomorphism
[
Toe1
,
Toë2
]
,

Theorem A

(see Theorem
4.2
)
.

Let
F
be a smooth separated Deligne-Mumford stack over a field
k
. Then the motivic Chern character induces an isomorphism

ch:K_{i}(F)_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\prod_{j}H_{\chi}^{2j-i,j}(F,{\mathbb{Q}}).

The above isomorphism is covariantly functorial along the coarse moduli morphism
p:F\rightarrow X
.

We also show that in case of smooth proper Deligne-Mumford stack
\mathcal{X}
, the Chow groups
H^{2*,*}_{\chi}(\mathcal{X},{\mathbb{Q}})
is canonically isomorphic to Toën’s Chow-groups
CH^{*}_{\chi}(\mathcal{X},{\mathbb{Q}})
defined in
[
Toë2
]
.

The above theorem gives us an interpretation of the rational
K
-groups of
F
in terms of the motivic cohomology of the motivic complexes
M_{\chi}({\mathcal{X}})
. It is not immediately clear how the groups
H_{\chi}^{2j-i,j}(F,{\mathbb{Q}})
are related to the intersection theory of
F
. Nevertheless, one can show that after adding enough root of unity to the coefficient ring
{\mathbb{Q}}
, the groups
H_{\chi}^{2j-i,j}(F,{\mathbb{Q}})
become isomorphic to the rational motivic cohomology of the inertia stack of
{\mathcal{X}}
.

Theorem B

(see Theorem
3.9
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{X}}
be a smooth separated Deligne-Mumford defined over an algebraically closed field. Denote by
I_{\mathcal{X}}
the inertia stack of
{\mathcal{X}}
. Then we have an isomorphism,

H_{\chi}^{2j-i,j}({\mathcal{X}},{\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{\infty}))\simeq H^{2j-i,i}(I_{\mathcal{X}},{\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{\infty}))

The above isomorphism is
not
canonical and depends on the choice of roots of unity.

In the case when
{\mathcal{X}}
is a quotient stack of the form
[X/GL_{n}]
with
X
smooth quasi-projective, the above result tells us that the groups
H_{\chi}^{2j-i,j}({\mathcal{X}},{\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{\infty}))
can be computed as the Edidin-Graham-Totaro higher Chow groups of the inertia stack
I_{\mathcal{X}}
with
{\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{\infty})
-coefficients (see
[
CDH
, Theorem 1.5]
). By
[
Cho
, Theorem 3.3]
, when
{\mathcal{X}}
is tame, this is the same as computing the higher Chow groups of the coarse space
I_{\mathcal{X}}
. This recover the result of Krishna-Sreedhar
[
KS
]
. In fact, to the best of our knowledge, the above two Theorems recover all the currently known Riemann-Roch formalisms of Krishna-Sreedhar
[
KS
]
, Edidin-Graham
[
EG1
,
EG2
]
, Toën
[
Toe3
,
Toë2
]
, etc.

Outline.
We begin by outlining the constructions of the basic motivic categories and functors in Section
2
. We primarily work with Ayoub’s category of motivic sheaves without transfers
\mathbf{DA}({\mathcal{X}},{\mathbb{Q}})
and the rational motivic stable homotopy category
\textbf{SH}({\mathcal{X}})_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Also, we freely use that equivalence between
\mathbf{DM}_{\acute{e}t}(-,{\mathbb{Q}})
and
\mathbf{DA}_{\acute{e}t}(-,{\mathbb{Q}})
as proved in
[
Ayo1
,
CD2
]
.

In Section
3
, we present our main contruction. We use stack
C^{t}_{\mathcal{X}}
(see Section
3
) to define the
twisted motive

M_{\chi}({\mathcal{X}})\in\mathbf{DM}^{\mathit{eff}}(k,{\mathbb{Q}})
associated to
{\mathcal{X}}
.

In Section
4
, we prove the Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch isomorphism for smooth Deligne-Mumford stacks (Theorem
4.2
), where the target of the isomorphism is the new motivic cohomology
H^{p,q}_{\chi}
defined in Section
3
. Further, in Theorem
3.9
, we show that, after tensoring with
{\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{\infty})
, the new motivic cohomology
H^{p,q}_{\chi}
is precisely the motivic cohomology of the coarse moduli space of the inertia stack.

In Section
5
, we prove some useful formulas for the new motive
M_{\chi}(\mathcal{X})
. While most of the usual properties of motives hold for
stabiliser preserving morphisms
, the projective bundle formula fails to hold when the structure map
{\mathbb{P}}({\mathcal{E}})\rightarrow{\mathcal{X}}
of the projective bundle is not stabiliser preserving (Example
5.1
).
M_{\chi}(\mathcal{X})
is a geometric motive for smooth Deligne-Mumford stack
\mathcal{X}
and it is a Chow motive if moreover
\mathcal{X}
is proper. Using this, it is shown that
M_{\chi}(\mathcal{X})
is isomorphic to
\iota\circ h_{\chi}(\mathcal{X})
for
\mathcal{X}
smooth and proper. Here
\iota
is the inclusion of the category Chow motives into Voevodsky’s category of motives and
h_{\chi}(\mathcal{X})
is the Chow motive of
\mathcal{X}
defined by Toën (see
[
Toë2
]
).

Finally, in Section
6
, we discuss some explicit examples of the motive
M_{\chi}({\mathcal{X}})
.

Basic constructions

Let
k
be a field. For any Deligne-Mumford stack
{\mathcal{X}}
, let
Sm/{\mathcal{X}}
denote the category of smooth schemes over
{\mathcal{X}}
and let
Smrep/{\mathcal{X}}
be the category of smooth Deligne-Mumford stack over
{\mathcal{X}}
such that the structre map is representable.

Let
M
be either the category of simplicial sets equipped with the usual model structure or the category of unbounded chain complexes of
R

), equipped with the projective model structre. For any category

, let
\textrm{PSh}(C,M)
denote the category of presheaves on

M
. Let
e:\textrm{PSh}(Sm/{\mathcal{X}},M)\to\textrm{PSh}(Smrep/{\mathcal{X}},M)
be the extension of presheaf functor which is left adjoint to the restriction
r:\textrm{PSh}(Smrep/{\mathcal{X}},M)\to\textrm{PSh}(Sm/{\mathcal{X}},M)
induced by the inclusion
Sm/{\mathcal{X}}\to Smrep/{\mathcal{X}}
. Note that this gives a Quillen adjunction , where both sides are equipped with the global projective model structure induced from
M
.

Let
\mathbf{DA}_{\acute{e}t}({\mathcal{X}},{\mathbb{Q}})
(resp.
\mathbf{DA}_{\acute{e}t}^{\mathit{eff}}({\mathcal{X}},{\mathbb{Q}})
) be the category of stable etale motives without transfers with
\mathbb{Q}
coefficients ( resp. effective etale motives without transfers with
\mathbb{Q}
-coefficients) with respect to the etale stable
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-(projective ) model structure on the category of
T
-spectra on
\textrm{PSh}(Sm/{\mathcal{X}},M)
(resp.
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-étale model structure on
\textrm{PSh}(Sm/{\mathcal{X}},M)
), where
M
is the category of chain complexes of
\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces. Same construction could be done on
T
-spectra on
\textrm{PSh}(Smrep/{\mathcal{X}},M)
(resp. on
\textrm{PSh}(Smrep/{\mathcal{X}},M)
) to get the category of stable etale motives without transfer with
\mathbb{Q}
-coefficients
\mathbf{DA}_{\acute{e}t}(Smrep/{\mathcal{X}},{\mathbb{Q}})
(resp. effective etale motives without transfers with
\mathbb{Q}
-coefficients

\mathbf{DA}_{\acute{e}t}^{\mathit{eff}}(Smrep/{\mathcal{X}},{\mathbb{Q}})

Lemma 2.1
.

The adjunction
(e,r)
induces a Quillen equivalence

\mathbf{DA}_{\acute{e}t}({\mathcal{X}},{\mathbb{Q}})\cong\mathbf{DA}_{\acute{e}t}(Smrep/{\mathcal{X}},{\mathbb{Q}})

(resp.
\mathbf{DA}_{\acute{e}t}^{\mathit{eff}}({\mathcal{X}},{\mathbb{Q}})\cong\mathbf{DA}_{\acute{e}t}^{\mathit{eff}}(Smrep/{\mathcal{X}},{\mathbb{Q}})
).

Proof.

We will show that the Quillen adjunction

e\!:\!\textrm{PSh}(Sm/{\mathcal{X}},M)\rightleftarrows\textrm{PSh}(Smrep/{\mathcal{X}},M)\!:\!r

induces Quillen equivalence

e:\mathbf{DA}_{\acute{e}t}^{\mathit{eff}}({\mathcal{X}},{\mathbb{Q}})\rightleftarrows\mathbf{DA}_{\acute{e}t}^{\mathit{eff}}(Smrep/{\mathcal{X}},{\mathbb{Q}}):r.

We will show that
Le\circ Rr\cong Id
and
Rr\circ Le\cong Id
. Here
Le
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
left derivation of
e
and
Rr
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-right derivation of
r
. Note that for any presheaf
F
on
Sm/{\mathcal{X}}
,
F\to r\circ e(F)
is an isomorphism. Let
K
be a
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-fibrant cofibrant presheaf of complex of vector spaces on
Sm/{\mathcal{X}}
. Let
e(K)\to K^{\prime}
be an étale fibrant replacement. Then
K\cong re(K)\to r(K^{\prime})
is an étale weak equivalence. As
r(K^{\prime})
is also étale fibrant, the morphism
K\to r(K^{\prime})
is an étale weak equiavelnce of étale fibrant objects. This implies that
K\to r(K^{\prime})
is sectionwise weak equivalence. Therefore to show
Id\cong Rr\circ Le
, it is enough to show that
K^{\prime}
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-fibrant. As
K^{\prime}
is étale fibrant, we need to show that for any
{\mathcal{Y}}\to{\mathcal{X}}
, smooth representable, the canonical morphism
\underline{Hom}({\mathcal{Y}},K^{\prime})\to\underline{Hom}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{{\mathcal{Y}}},K^{\prime})
is an étale local equivalence. But
rK^{\prime}
is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-fibrant being sectionwise equivalent to
K
. On the other hand for any smooth simplicial scheme
U_{\bullet}
over
{\mathcal{X}}
, we have

\underline{Hom}(U_{\bullet},rK^{\prime})\cong\underline{Hom}(U_{\bullet},K^{\prime}).

Let
U_{\bullet}\to{\mathcal{Y}}
be an étale weak equivalence where
U_{\bullet}
is a smooth simplicial scheme over
{\mathcal{X}}
. Then we have the following commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{Hom}({\mathcal{Y}},K^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{Hom}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{{\mathcal{Y}}},K^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{Hom}(U_{\bullet},K^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{Hom}(\mathbb{A}^{1}\times U_{\bullet},K^{\prime})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{f}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

such that the vertical arrows are etale local weak equivalence by étale descent and the bottom horizontal arrow is an étale local weak equivalence. Therefore the top horizontal morphism is a local weak equivalence. Thus we get
Id\cong Rr\circ Le
.

Now we want to show
Le\circ Rr\cong Id
. This follows from the fact that for any presheaf
S
on
Smrep/{\mathcal{X}}
,
e\circ r(S)\to S
induces isomorphism on étale stalks.

∎

3.
The stack
C^{t}_{F}

In this section, we describe the stack
C^{t}_{F}
classifying cyclic subgroups of automorphism associated to any Deligne-Mumford stack
F
as defined in
[
Toë2
]
. We call it the character stack of
F
.

Let
F
be a tame Deligne-Mumford stack over a field
k
. The stack
C^{t}_{F}
is defined as follows:

For any
k
-scheme
U
, the objects of
C^{t}_{F}
are pairs
(u,C)
, where
u:U\rightarrow F
and
C\subset Aut(u)
is a cyclic subgroup. An isomorphism between any two such objects
(u,C)
and
(u^{\prime},C^{\prime})
is described by an isomorphism
\alpha:u\rightarrow u^{\prime}
which conjugates the cyclic subgroups, i.e,
\alpha^{-1}C^{\prime}\alpha=C
. We have a canonical map
\pi:C^{t}_{F}\rightarrow F
which sends any
(u,C)
to
u
.

We can define the sheaf of characters
\chi
on the big étale site of
C^{t}_{F}
as

\begin{matrix}\chi:&(Sch/C^{t}_{F})_{\acute{e}t}&\rightarrow&\text{Ab}\\
&((u,C):U\rightarrow C^{t}_{F})&\mapsto&Hom_{Gps}(C,{\mathbb{G}}_{m,U})\end{matrix}

Consider the sheaf of group-algebras
{\mathbb{Q}}[\chi]
(resp.
{\mathbb{Z}}[\chi]
) associated to
\chi
. This is étale locally (on
(Sch/C^{t}_{F})_{\acute{e}t}
) isomorphic to the constant sheaf with fibre
{\mathbb{Q}}[T]/(T^{m}-1)
(
{\mathbb{Z}}[T]/(T^{m}-1)
). We have maps
p:{\mathbb{Q}}[T]/(T^{m}-1)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{m})
(p :
{\mathbb{Z}}[T]/(T^{m}-1)\to{\mathbb{Z}}[T]/(\psi_{m})
where
\psi_{m}
is the minimal polynomial of the primitive
m
-th root of unity), where
{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{m})
is a cyclotomic extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}
. The kernels
ker(p)
glue to give a well-defined ideal sheaf,
{\mathcal{I}}
. We define a sheaf

\Lambda_{F}:={\mathbb{Q}}[\chi]/{\mathcal{I}}\;\;\;\;(resp.\,\,\Lambda_{F,{\mathbb{Z}}}:={\mathbb{Z}}[\chi]/{\mathcal{I}})

The above discussion culminates into the following:

Lemma 3.1
.

\Lambda_{F}
(resp.
\Lambda_{F,{\mathbb{Z}}}
) is a locally constant sheaf on
(Sch/C^{t}_{F})_{\acute{e}t}
and it is locally given by the constant sheaf
{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{m})
(resp.
{\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta_{m}]
).

Let
\mathbf{DA}_{\acute{e}t}(C^{t}_{F},{\mathbb{Q}})
(respectively
\mathbf{DA}_{\acute{e}t}(C^{t}_{F},{\mathbb{Z}})
) be the triangulated category of motives without transfers in the étale topology (see
[
Ayo2
]
).

Lemma 3.2
.

\Lambda_{F}
is a dualisable object in
\mathbf{DA}_{\acute{e}t}(C^{t}_{F},{\mathbb{Q}})
(Also true for
\Lambda_{F,{\mathbb{Z}}}
).

Proof.

By the previous lemma,
\Lambda_{F}
is locally constant. Thus, there is an étale covering
p:U\rightarrow C^{t}_{F}
such
\Lambda_{F}
is free of finite rank. Then by étale descent, we get the required result.
∎

Definition 3.3
.

(New higher Chow groups and motivic cohomology) We define the
\Lambda
-higher Chow groups as

CH^{j}(C^{t}_{F},i)_{\Lambda_{F}}:=Hom_{C^{t}_{F}}(\mathbf{1},\Lambda_{F}(j)[2j-i])

Proposition 3.4
.

The
\Lambda
-higher Chow groups defined above are representable in
\mathbf{DA}_{\acute{e}t}(k,{\mathbb{Q}})
. That is, there exists a motive
N\in\mathbf{DA}_{\acute{e}t}(k,{\mathbb{Q}})
such that
Hom_{\mathbf{DA}_{\acute{e}t}(k,{\mathbb{Q}})}(N,{\mathbb{Q}}(j)[2j-i])\simeq CH^{j}(C^{t}_{F},i)_{\Lambda_{F}}
.

Proof.

As
\Lambda_{F}
is a dualisable object in
\mathbf{DA}_{\acute{e}t}(C^{t}_{F},{\mathbb{Q}})
, we have that

\displaystyle Hom_{C^{t}_{F}}(\mathbf{1}_{C^{t}_{F}},\Lambda_{F}(j)[2j-i])

\displaystyle=Hom_{C^{t}_{F}}(\mathbf{1}_{C^{t}_{F}}\otimes\Lambda_{F}^{\vee},\mathbf{1}(j)[2j-i])

\displaystyle=Hom_{k}(f_{\#}(\mathbf{1}_{C^{t}_{F}}\otimes\Lambda_{F}^{\vee}),\mathbf{1}(j)[2j-i])

where
f:C^{t}_{F}\rightarrow{\rm Spec\,}k
is the structure map. Thus, the
\Lambda
-motivic cohomology of
C^{t}_{F}
is represented by the motive
f_{\#}(\mathbf{1}_{C^{t}_{F}}\otimes\Lambda_{F}^{\vee})
in
\mathbf{DA}_{\acute{e}t}(k,{\mathbb{Q}})

∎

Remark 3.5
.

The above proposition is also true with
{\mathbb{Z}}
coefficients in
\mathbf{DA}_{\acute{e}t}(k,{\mathbb{Z}})
and we get an integral étale motive.

Let
F
be a Deligne-Mumford stack. The motive
M(F)
as described in
[
Cho
,
CDH
]
is “too small" for computing its
K
-Theory. To get a reasonable comparison with

-Theory, we need to “enlarge" the motive of

Definition 3.6
.

Let
F
be a Deligne-Mumford stack and consider the associated Character stack
f:C^{t}_{F}\rightarrow{\rm Spec\,}k
. We define the motive

M_{\chi}(F):=M(f_{\#}(\mathbf{1}_{C^{t}_{F}}\otimes\Lambda_{F}^{\vee}))

We denote the associated motivic cohomology by

H_{\chi}^{p,q}(F,{\mathbb{Q}}):=Hom\big(M_{\chi}(F),{\mathbb{Q}}(q)[p]\big)

Proposition 3.7

(Basic functoriality)
.

(1)

Let
f:F\to F^{\prime}
be a morphism of smooth Deligne-Mumford stacks oevr
k
. Then
f
induces a natural morphism
M_{\chi}(f):M_{\chi}(F)\to M_{\chi}(F^{\prime})
such that we get a functor
M_{\chi}:SmDM/k\to\mathbf{DM}^{\mathit{eff}}(k,{\mathbb{Q}})
sending
F\mapsto M_{\chi}(F)
and
f:F\to F^{\prime}\mapsto M_{\chi}(f)

5.
Properties of the motive

While the new motive which we have defined gives a comparison with
K
-theory, there are other interesting things which happen. For instance it fails to have a projective bundle, and many of the exact triangles only work for stabiliser preserving morphisms.

Example 5.1

(Failure of the Projective bundle formula)
.

The motive constructed using the character stack does not have a projective bundle formula. Consider the stack
BC_{2}
in characteristic different from
2
. Consider the projective bundle on

defined by action

on

, given by

has the form:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[{\mathbb{P}}^{1}/C_{2}]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\coprod}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces BC_{2}\sqcup BC_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces BC_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\coprod}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces BC_{2}}

The failure of projective bundle formula is a direct consequence of the fact that a general representable morphism between stacks only induces an injection on automorphism groups. For stabilizer perserving morphisms, the new motive behaves exactly like the ordinary motive.

Definition 5.2

A morphism of stacks

is said to be stabiliser preserving if the induced map on the inertia stacks

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{X}}
be a smooth proper Deligne-Mumford stack with a
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
action. Let
x:X\to{\mathcal{X}}
be a
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-equivariant affine étale surjective atlas. Let
{\mathcal{Z}}:={\mathcal{X}}^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}=\coprod_{i}{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}
be the decomposition into connected components of the fixed point locus. Then
M_{\chi}({\mathcal{X}})\cong\oplus_{i=0}^{n}M_{\chi}({\mathcal{Z}}_{i})(c_{i})[2c_{i}]
, for
c_{i}
’s in
\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{X}}
be a smooth proper Deligne-Mumford stack with a
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
action and let
x:X\to{\mathcal{X}}
be a
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-equivariant affine étale atlas. Let
{\mathcal{Z}}
be the fixed point closed substack
{\mathcal{X}}
and let
{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}
be connected componenets of
{\mathcal{Z}}
. Then by
[
Opr
, Proposition 5]
, we get that
{\mathcal{X}}
can be covered by locally closed disjoint substacks
{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}^{+}
, containing
{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}
which are affine fibrations over
{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}
. Note that
C^{t}_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}^{+}}={\mathcal{Z}}_{i}^{+}\times_{{\mathcal{X}}}C^{t}_{{\mathcal{X}}}
are locally closed disjoint substacks covering
C^{t}_{{\mathcal{X}}}
and
C^{t}_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}}={\mathcal{Z}}_{i}\times_{{\mathcal{X}}}C^{t}_{{\mathcal{X}}}={\mathcal{Z}}_{i}\times_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}^{+}}C^{t}_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}^{+}}
. Therefore, the affine fibration
{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}^{+}\to{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}
, induces an affine fibration
{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}^{+}\times_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}}C^{t}_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}}\to C^{t}_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}}
, but
{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}^{+}\times_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}}C^{t}_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}}\cong C^{t}_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}^{+}}
, by what we have described. Therefore we get a covering of
C^{t}_{{\mathcal{X}}}
by disjoint localy closed
C^{t}_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}^{+}}
containing
C^{t}_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}}
together with affine fibrations
C^{t}_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}^{+}}\to C^{t}_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}}
. Therefore,
M(C^{t}_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}^{+}})\cong M(C^{t}_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}})
(using utsav choudhury and skowera, corollary 3.2), which implies that
M_{\chi}({\mathcal{Z}}_{i}^{+})\cong M_{\chi}({\mathcal{Z}}_{i})
. As
{\mathcal{X}}
is smooth proper so are
{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}
’s. Therefore
M_{\chi}({\mathcal{Z}}_{i}^{+})
are Chow motives by Theorem
3.11
. Therefore using similar arguments as in
[
CS
, Proposition 4.4]
together with Proposition
5.3
, we get
M_{\chi}({\mathcal{X}})\cong\oplus_{i=0}^{n}M_{\chi}({\mathcal{Z}}_{i}^{+})(c_{i})[2c_{i}]
, where
c_{i}=codim_{{\mathcal{X}}}({\mathcal{Z}}_{i}^{+})
.
∎

Theorem 6.5

{\mathcal{X}}

Abstract
. In this paper, we propose two linearized finite difference schemes for solving the logarithmic Schrödinger equation (LogSE) without the need for regularization of the logarithmic term. These two schemes employ the first-order and the second-order backward difference formula, respectively, for temporal discretization of the LogSE, while using the second-order central finite difference method for spatial discretization. We overcome the singularity posed by the logarithmic nonlinearity f ⁡ ( u ) = u ​ ln ⁡ | u | f(u)=u\ln|u| in establishing optimal l 2 l^{2} -error estimates for the first-order scheme, and an almost optimal l 2 l^{2} -error estimate for the second-order scheme. Compared to the error estimates of the LogSE in the literature, our error bounds not only greatly improve the convergence rate but also get rid of the time step restriction. Furthermore, without enhancing the regularity of the exact solution or imposing any requirements on the grid ratio, we establish error estimates of the two proposed schemes in the discrete H 1 H^{1} norm. However, the existing results available in the literature either fail to provide H 1 H^{1} error estimates or require certain restrictions on the grid ratio. Numerical results are reported to confirm our error estimates and demonstrate rich dynamics of the LogSE.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the following initial-boundary value problem of the logarithmic Schrödinger equation (LogSE):

(1.1)

\begin{cases}{\rm i}\partial_{t}u(\bm{x},t)+\Delta u(\bm{x},t)=\lambda u(\bm{x},t)\ln(|u(\bm{x},t)|^{2}),\quad\bm{x}\in\Omega,\;\;t>0,\\[2.0pt]

Like the cubic Schrödinger equation which is a closely related model to the LogSE, the initial-boundary value problem (

\displaystyle=\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\int_{\Omega}|u(\bm{x},t)|^{2}d\bm{x}\equiv\int_{\Omega}|u_{0}(\bm{x})|^{2}d\bm{x}=M(0),

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(\bm{x},t)|^{2}d\bm{x}+(F(|u(\cdot,t)|^{2}),1)\equiv\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{0}(\bm{x})|^{2}d\bm{x}+(F(|u_{0}|^{2}),1)=E(0),

From a mathematical level, the LogSE (

Besides the first-order derivatives of the nonlinear term with respect to time and space blow up, whenever

Recently, there has been growing interest in constructing numerical schemes for the non-regularized logarithmic PDE. With the understanding that

In

Finite difference methods are designed with simplicity, making them easy to program and highly popular for applications in practical problems. Therefore this paper is devoted to developing and analyzing two efficient finite difference schemes for approximating the solution to the LogSE (
1.1
), without applying regularization to the nonlinear term. These two schemes are introduced utilizing the first-order and second-order backward difference formulas, respectively, for the temporal discretization of the LogSE, while employing the second-order central finite difference method for spatial discretization. Moreover, each scheme employs a different linearized discrete method for the nonlinear term of the LogSE. For simplicity, we use BDF1 and BDF2 to denote the first-order and second-order schemes, respectively. The highlights of this paper are outlined as follows:

•

These two schemes are linearly implicit, which means that only a system of linear algebraic equations needs to be solved at each time level. This can be quickly achieved using the FFT-type solver. Therefore, they are very efficient in practical computations.

•

The BDF1 scheme exhibits unconditional convergence and optimal error estimate of
\mathcal{O}(\tau+h^{2})
in the discrete
L^{2}
norm, where
\tau
and
h
are the mesh size and time step, respectively;

•

The BDF2 scheme exhibits unconditional convergence and almost optimal error estimate, the convergence order in the discrete
L^{2}
norm is of
\mathcal{O}(h^{2}+\tau^{2}{\ln|\tau|})
;

•

The error estimates of the two BDF schemes are also established in the discrete
H^{1}
norm without enhancing the regularity of the exact solution or imposing any requirements for grid ratio.

2.1.
Numerical methods

To simplify the notation, we only demonstrate the methods in a two-dimensional context, meaning that

, and
\Omega
, which represents a rectangle in

(a,b)\times(c,d)

(2.1)

\begin{cases}{\rm i}\partial_{t}u(x,y,t)+\Delta u(x,y,t)=\lambda u(x,y,t)\ln(|u(x,y,t)|^{2}),\;\;(x,y)\in\Omega,\;\;t>0,\\[2.0pt]
u(x,y,t)=0,\;\;(x,y)\in\partial\Omega,\;\;t\geq 0;\\[2.0pt]
u(x,y,0)=u_{0}(x,y),\;\;(x,y)\in\overline{\Omega}.\end{cases}

Generalized to the one-dimensional case with
\Omega=(a,b)
being a interval in
\mathbb{R}
and three-dimensional case with
\Omega=(a,b)\times(c,d)\times(e,f)
being a cube in

are straightforward.
Choose the time-step

with

being a positive integer, and denote time steps

t_{n}=n\tau,\hskip 5.69046ptn=0,1,2,\cdots,N
, where
0<T<T_{\max}

with

being the maximal existing time of the solution;
let the mesh size
h_{x}:=\frac{b-a}{J}
and
h_{y}:=\frac{d-c}{K}
with
J,K
being two positive integers and denote
h:=h_{\max}=\max\{h_{x},h_{y}\},\,h_{\min}:=\min\{h_{x},h_{y}\}
, and
x_{j}:=a+jh_{x}
,
y_{k}:=c+kh_{y}
for
j=0,1,\ldots,J,\;k=0,1,\ldots,K
. We here assume that

.
Define the index sets as

\begin{split}\mathcal{T}_{h}&=\big\{(j,k)|j=1,2,\ldots,J-1;\,k=1,2,\ldots,K-1\big\},\\
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{0}&=\big\{(j,k)|j=0,1,2,\ldots,J;\,k=0,1,2,\ldots,K\big\},\quad\Gamma_{h}=\mathcal{T}_{h}^{0}\backslash\mathcal{T}_{h}.\end{split}

Assume that
u^{n}_{j,k}
is the approximation to the exact solution

for

and

. Denote by

the numerical solution vector at time

. The followings are the finite difference operators:

\begin{split}&\delta^{+}_{x}u^{n}_{j,k}=\frac{1}{h_{x}}({u^{n}_{j+1,k}-u^{n}_{j,k}}),\quad\delta^{+}_{y}u^{n}_{j,k}=\frac{1}{h_{y}}({u^{n}_{j,k+1}-u^{n}_{j,k}}),\quad\delta_{\nabla}^{+}{u}_{j,k}^{n}=(\delta_{x}^{+}u_{j,k}^{n},\delta_{y}^{+}u_{j,k}^{n})^{T},\\
&\delta^{2}_{x}u^{n}_{j,k}=\frac{1}{h_{x}^{2}}({u^{n}_{j+1,k}-2u^{n}_{j,k}+u^{n}_{j-1,k}}),\quad\delta^{2}_{y}u^{n}_{j,k}=\frac{1}{h_{y}^{2}}({u^{n}_{j,k+1}-2u^{n}_{j,k}+u^{n}_{j,k-1}}),\\
&\delta_{\nabla}^{2}u_{j,k}^{n}=\delta_{x}^{2}{u}_{j,k}^{n}+\delta_{y}^{2}{u}_{j,k}^{n},\quad\delta^{+}_{t}u^{n}_{j,k}=\frac{1}{\tau}({u^{n+1}_{j,k}-u^{n}_{j,k}}),\quad\delta^{-}_{t}u^{n+1}_{j,k}=\delta^{+}_{t}u^{n}_{j,k},\\
&D_{t}^{-}u^{n+1}_{j,k}=\frac{1}{2\tau}({3u^{n+1}_{j,k}-4u^{n}_{j,k}+u^{n-1}_{j,k}}),\quad u^{n+\frac{1}{2}}_{j,k}=\frac{1}{2}({u^{n}_{j,k}+u^{n+1}_{j,k}}).\end{split}

We introduce the space of grid functions
X_{h}^{0}
as

X_{h}^{0}=\big\{u=(u_{j,k})_{(j,k)\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{0}}|u_{j,k}=0\;{\rm{when}}\;(j,k)\in\Gamma_{h}\big\}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{(J+1)\times(K+1)}.

The inner product and discrete

\begin{split}&(u,v)_{h}=h_{x}h_{y}\sum\limits^{J-1}_{j=1}\sum\limits^{K-1}_{k=1}u_{j,k}\overline{v}_{j,k},\quad\langle\delta_{x}^{+}u,\delta_{x}^{+}v\rangle_{h}=h_{x}h_{y}\sum\limits^{J-1}_{j=0}\sum\limits^{K-1}_{k=0}\delta_{x}^{+}u_{j,k}\delta_{x}^{+}\overline{v}_{j,k},\\
&\langle\delta_{y}^{+}u,\delta_{y}^{+}v\rangle_{h}=h_{x}h_{y}\sum\limits^{J-1}_{j=0}\sum\limits^{K-1}_{k=0}\delta_{y}^{+}u_{j,k}\delta_{y}^{+}\overline{v}_{j,k},\quad\|u\|_{r}=(h_{x}h_{y}\sum\limits^{J-1}_{j=1}\sum\limits^{K-1}_{k=1}|u_{j,k}|^{r})^{\frac{1}{r}},\\

where

2.2.
Solvability

We now study the existence and uniqueness of the BDF1 scheme and the BDF2 scheme.

Lemma 2.1

(Unique solvability of the BDF1 scheme)
.

For any fixed
\tau,h_{x},h_{y}>0
and
0\leq n\leq N-1,
the discretised problem (
2.2
) has a unique solution
{u}^{n+1}\in X_{h}^{0}.

Proof.

From (
2.2

exists, then by using mathematical induction, we assume that

is known and try to prove that the solution

exists. In fact, for a given

solvability of the system of linear algebraic equations (

(2.4)

\begin{cases}\frac{{\rm i}}{\tau}u^{n+1}_{j,k}+\delta^{2}_{\nabla}u^{n+1}_{j,k}=0,\;\;(j,k)\in\mathcal{T}_{h},\\
u^{n+1}_{j,k}=0,\;\;(j,k)\in\Gamma_{h}\end{cases}

has a unique zero solution. To do this, we compute the inner production of (

and take the imaginary part to obtain

.
This ends the proof.
∎

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{1}=\{(j,k)\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{0}||v_{j,k}|\leq\epsilon,|u_{j,k}|\leq\epsilon\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{2}=\{(j,k)\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{0}||v_{j,k}|\leq\epsilon,|u_{j,k}|>\epsilon\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{3}=\{(j,k)\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{0}||u_{j,k}|\leq\epsilon,|v_{j,k}|>\epsilon\},\;\;

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{4}=\{(j,k)\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{0}||u_{j,k}|>\epsilon,|v_{j,k}|>\epsilon\}.

From (
2.9
), and when
(j,k)\in\mathcal{T}_{1}
, we have

\begin{split}h_{x}h_{y}\sum_{(j,k)\in\mathcal{T}_{1}}|f(u_{j,k})-f(v_{j,k})|^{2}&\leq h_{x}h_{y}\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}^{2}(\epsilon)\sum_{(j,k)\in\mathcal{T}_{1}}|u_{j,k}-v_{j,k}|^{2\alpha}=\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}^{2}(\epsilon)\|u-v\|_{{2\alpha}(\mathcal{T}_{1})}^{2\alpha}.\end{split}

Moreover, when
(j,k)\in\mathcal{T}_{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle h_{x}h_{y}\sum_{(j,k)\in\mathcal{T}_{2}}|f(u_{j,k})-f(v_{j,k})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq h_{x}h_{y}\sum_{(j,k)\in\mathcal{T}_{2}}(|\!\ln|u_{j,k}||+1)^{2}|u_{j,k}-v_{j,k}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{(j,k)\in\mathcal{T}_{2}}\{|\!\ln|u_{j,k}||+1\}^{2}h_{x}h_{y}\sum_{(j,k)\in\mathcal{T}_{2}}|u_{j,k}-v_{j,k}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\epsilon\leq\vartheta_{j,k}\leq\|u\|_{\infty(\mathcal{T}_{2})}}\{|\!\ln\vartheta_{j,k}|+1\}^{2}h_{x}h_{y}\sum_{(j,k)\in\mathcal{T}_{2}}|u_{j,k}-v_{j,k}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\big(\max_{\epsilon\leq\vartheta_{j,k}\leq\Lambda_{\infty}}\{|\!\ln\vartheta_{j,k}|+1\}\big)^{2}\|u-v\|^{2}_{(\mathcal{T}_{2})},

and similarly when
(j,k)\in\mathcal{T}_{3}
, we have

\displaystyle h_{x}h_{y}\sum_{(j,k)\in\mathcal{T}_{3}}|f(u_{j,k})-f(v_{j,k})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\big(\max_{\epsilon\leq\vartheta_{j,k}\leq\Lambda_{\infty}}\{|\!\ln\vartheta_{j,k}|+1\}\big)^{2}\|u-v\|^{2}_{(\mathcal{T}_{3})}.

At last, when
(j,k)\in\mathcal{T}_{4}
, we define
w_{j,k}:=\max\{|u_{j,k}|,|v_{j,k}|\}
, and note that

\epsilon\leq w_{j,k}\leq\max\{\|u\|_{\infty(\mathcal{T}_{4})},\|u\|_{\infty(\mathcal{T}_{4})}\}\leq\Lambda_{\infty},

we derive from (
2.10
) that

\begin{split}h_{x}h_{y}\sum_{(j,k)\in\mathcal{T}_{4}}|f(u_{j,k})-f(v_{j,k})|^{2}&\leq h_{x}h_{y}\sum_{(j,k)\in\mathcal{T}_{4}}(|\!\ln w_{j,k}|+1)^{2}|u_{j,k}-v_{j,k}|^{2}\\
&\leq\sup_{(j,k)\in\mathcal{T}_{4}}\{|\!\ln w_{j,k}|+1\}^{2}h_{x}h_{y}\sum_{(j,k)\in\mathcal{T}_{4}}|u_{j,k}-v_{j,k}|^{2}\\
&\leq\big(\max_{\epsilon\leq y\leq\Lambda_{\infty}}\{|\!\ln y|+1\}\big)^{2}\,\|u-v\|^{2}_{(\mathcal{T}_{4})}.\end{split}

Summing up the above “local” bounds and noticing Lemma
2.4
yield

\begin{split}\|f(u)-f(v)\|^{2}&=h_{x}h_{y}\sum_{i=1}^{4}\sum_{(j,k)\in\mathcal{T}_{i}}|f(u_{j,k})-f(v_{j,k})|^{2}\\
&\leq{\mathcal{H}}^{2}_{\alpha}(\epsilon)\|u-v\|^{2\alpha}_{{2\alpha}}+\Upsilon^{2}({\epsilon},\Lambda_{\infty})\,\|u-v\|^{2}\\
&\leq{\mathcal{H}}^{2}_{\alpha}(\epsilon)|\Omega|^{1-\alpha}\|u\|^{2\alpha}+\Upsilon^{2}({\epsilon},\Lambda_{\infty})\,\|u-v\|^{2},\end{split}

which leads to (
2.8
). It is clear that if
\Lambda_{\infty}\leq\epsilon
, then
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{0}=\mathcal{T}_{1},
i.e.,

\mathcal{T}_{i}=\emptyset
for
i=2,3,4
in (
2.12
). Therefore (
2.11
) is a direct consequence of
(
2.8
).
∎

, then under

3.1.
Proof of Theorem

on the BDF1 scheme (

)

To prove Theorem

, we first introduce and analyze the local truncation error of the BDF1 scheme.

Lemma 3.1
.

Under the assumption (

),
the local truncation error
\xi^{n}\in{X}_{h}^{0}
of the BDF1 scheme (

(3.5)

\xi^{n}_{j,k}:={\rm i}\delta^{+}_{t}U_{j,k}^{n}+\delta_{\nabla}^{2}{U}_{j,k}^{n+1}-2\lambda f(U_{j,k}^{n}),\quad(j,k)\in\mathcal{T}_{h},\;\;n=0,1,2,\ldots,N-1.

Then for all

(See Appendix

for the proof of Lemma

).

Now we are ready to establish the error estimates of the BDF1 scheme given in Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Subtracting (
2.2
) from (
3.5
), we get the following ‘error’ equation:

(3.7a)

\displaystyle{\rm i}\delta^{+}_{t}e^{n}_{j,k}+\delta_{\nabla}^{2}e^{n+1}_{j,k}=\xi^{n}_{j,k}+\lambda\mathcal{N}_{j,k}^{n},\quad{(j,k)}\in\mathcal{T}_{h},\;\;n=0,1,2,\dots,N-1,

where
\mathcal{N}^{n}\in X_{h}^{0}
with

\mathcal{N}_{j,k}^{n}:=2f(U_{j,k}^{n})-2f(u^{n}_{j,k}),\quad{(j,k)}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{0}
.

Now, we split our proof of Theorem
3.1
into the following two steps.

Step 1. (Estimate of
||e^{n}||
for the BDF1 scheme)
In this step, we aim to prove the error estimate in the discrete
L^{2}
norm given in (
3.2a
) and the bound of the numerical solution in the discrete
H^{1}
norm by mathematical induction.

Obviously,
||{e}^{0}||=0
and
||{u}^{0}||_{\infty}=||{U}^{0}||_{\infty}
is bounded. Assume that (
3.2a
) is valid and
||{u}^{n}||_{\infty}
is bounded by
1+||{U}^{n}||_{\infty}
for
0\leq n\leq m
with
m<N
, and we next prove that they are still hold for
n=m+1
.
Multiplying both sides of (
3.7a
) by
h_{1}h_{2}\bar{e}^{n+1}_{j,k}
and summing together for
(j,k)\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, then taking the imaginary part, we have

(3.8)

\begin{split}\frac{\|e^{n+1}\|^{2}-\|e^{n}\|^{2}}{2\tau}+\frac{\|{e}^{n+1}-e^{n}\|^{2}}{2\tau}=\lambda{\rm{Im}}(\mathcal{N}^{n},{e}^{n+1})_{h}+{\rm{Im}}(\xi^{n},{e}^{n+1})_{h},\;\;{0}\leq{n}<N.\end{split}

In order to estimate the first term on the right-hand side of (
3.8
),
we take
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
and apply Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Lemmas
2.4
,
2.5
,
2.7
for
0<\epsilon\leq\delta_{\alpha}
to obtain the following results:

(3.9)

\begin{split}&|\lambda{\rm{Im}}(\mathcal{N}^{n},{e}^{n+1})_{h}|\leq|\lambda|\cdot|{\rm{Im}}(\mathcal{N}^{n},e^{n})_{h}|+|\lambda|\cdot|{\rm{Im}}(\mathcal{N}^{n},{e}^{n+1}-{e}^{n})_{h}|\\
\leq&2|\lambda|\cdot\|{e}^{n}\|^{2}+\frac{|\lambda|^{2}\tau}{2}\|\mathcal{N}^{n}\|^{2}+\frac{\|{e}^{n+1}-{e}^{n}\|^{2}}{2\tau}\\
\leq&{{2}(b-a)^{1-\alpha}}|\lambda|^{2}{\mathcal{H}}^{2}_{\alpha}(\epsilon)\,\tau\,\|{e}^{n}\|^{2\alpha}+2|\lambda|\big(|\lambda|\Upsilon^{2}(\epsilon,{{\Lambda}_{\infty}})\,\tau+1\big)\,\|{e}^{n}\|^{2}+\frac{\|{e}^{n+1}-{e}^{n}\|^{2}}{2\tau},\end{split}

where
{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha}(\epsilon)
and
\Upsilon(\epsilon,{{\Lambda}_{\infty}})
are defined in (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) with

(3.10)

{{\Lambda}_{\infty}}=\max_{0\leq n\leq m}\big\{\|u^{n}\|_{\infty},\|U^{n}\|_{\infty}\big\}.

For the second term on the right-hand side of (
3.8
), deducing from Lemma
3.1
, we have

(3.11)

\begin{split}&|{\rm{Im}}(\xi^{n},e^{n+1})_{h}|\leq\frac{1}{4}\|{e}^{n+1}\|^{2}+\|\xi^{n}\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}\|{e}^{n+1}\|^{2}+C_{u}(\tau^{2}+h^{4}).\end{split}

Substituting (
3.9
)-(
3.11
) into (
3.8
) gives

\begin{split}\|{e}^{n+1}\|^{2}-\|{e}^{n}\|^{2}&\leq\frac{\tau}{2}\|{e}^{n+1}\|^{2}+4|\Omega|^{1-\alpha}|\lambda|^{2}\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\alpha}(\epsilon){\tau}^{2}\|{e}^{n}\|^{2\alpha}\\
&\quad+4|\lambda|\tau\big(|\lambda|\Upsilon^{2}(\epsilon,{{\Lambda}_{\infty}}){\tau}+1\big)\|{e}^{n}\|^{2}+{C_{u}}\,\tau({\tau}^{2}+{h}^{4}).\end{split}

Summing the above inequality for
n=0,1,2,\ldots,m
leads to

(3.12)

\begin{split}\|e^{m+1}\|^{2}&\leq\|e^{0}\|^{2}+\frac{\tau}{2}\|e^{m+1}\|^{2}+4|\Omega|^{1-\alpha}|\lambda|^{2}\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\alpha}(\epsilon)\tau^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{m}\|e^{n}\|^{2\alpha}\\
&\quad+\tau\Big(\frac{1}{2}+4|\lambda|+4|\lambda|^{2}\Upsilon^{2}(\epsilon,{{\Lambda}_{\infty}})\tau\Big)\sum_{n=0}^{m}\|e^{n}\|^{2}+{C_{u}}{T}(\tau^{2}+{{h^{4})}}.\end{split}

Then for
0<\tau<1
we derive from (
3.12
) that there is

(3.13)

\|e^{m+1}\|^{2}\leq c_{1}+c_{2}\tau\sum_{n=0}^{m}\|e^{n}\|^{2\alpha}+c_{3}\tau\sum_{n=0}^{m}\|e^{n}\|^{2},

where
\alpha\in[1/2,1)
and

(3.14)

\begin{split}c_{1}&=C_{u}\,\widehat{C}_{\tau}\,T(\tau^{2}+h^{4}),\quad c_{2}=4\widehat{C}_{\tau}\,|\Omega|^{1-\alpha}|\lambda|^{2}{\mathcal{H}}^{2}_{\alpha}(\epsilon)\tau,\\
c_{3}&=\widehat{C}_{\tau}\,\Big(\frac{1}{2}+4|\lambda|+4|\lambda|^{2}\Upsilon^{2}(\epsilon,{{\Lambda}_{\infty}})\tau\Big),\end{split}

with
\widehat{C}_{\tau}=1/(1-\tau/2).

By the nonlinear Grönwall’s inequality (
2.14
), we have

(3.15)

\begin{split}\|e^{m+1}\|^{2}&\leq c_{1}\big(1-\alpha^{-1}+\alpha^{-1}{(1+\alpha c_{1}^{\alpha-1}c_{2}{\tau}+c_{3}{\tau})^{m+1}}\big)\\
&\leq c_{1}\big(1-\alpha^{-1}+\alpha^{-1}{\rm exp}\big({{(m+1){\tau}}(\alpha c_{1}^{\alpha-1}c_{2}+c_{3})}\big)\big),\end{split}

where the inequality
(1+z)^{m+1}\leq e^{{(m+1)}z}
for
z\geq 0
was used.

For
0<\epsilon\leq\delta_{\alpha},
we can bound the factor in the exponential as follows

(3.16)

\begin{split}&G_{m}:={{(m+1)}{\tau}\big(\alpha{c_{2}}/{c_{1}^{1-\alpha}}+c_{3}\big)}={(m+1)}\tau\widehat{C}_{\tau}\big(1/2+4|\lambda|+4|\lambda|^{2}\Upsilon^{2}(\epsilon,{\Lambda_{\infty}})\tau\big)\\[6.0pt]
&\quad+\frac{4{(m+1)}\alpha\widehat{C}_{\tau}|\Omega|^{1-\alpha}|\lambda|^{2}\tau^{2\alpha}{\mathcal{H}}^{2}_{\alpha}(\epsilon)}{({C_{u}\widehat{C}_{\tau}}\,T)^{1-\alpha}(1+\tau^{-2}h^{4})^{1-\alpha}}\\[6.0pt]
&\leq\widehat{C}_{\tau}T\big(1/2+4|\lambda|+4|\lambda|^{2}\Upsilon^{2}(\epsilon,{{\Lambda}_{\infty}})\tau\big)+4\alpha C_{u}^{\alpha-1}(\widehat{C}_{\tau}T)^{\alpha}|\Omega|^{1-\alpha}|\lambda|^{2}\tau^{2\alpha-1}{\mathcal{H}}^{2}_{\alpha}(\epsilon),\end{split}

where we refer the inequality:
(1+z)^{\beta}\leq 1
for
z\geq 0
and
\beta=\alpha-1<0.

We now choose suitable values of
\epsilon
in the upper bound of
G_{m}
in (
3.16
), so as to obtain the best possible order of convergence.
Taking
\epsilon=\tau
in
{\mathcal{H}}^{2}_{\alpha}(\epsilon)
and
\Upsilon^{2}(\epsilon,{{\Lambda}_{\infty}})
, noticing from the definition (
2.10
) and (
3.10
) that for
0<\tau\leq\delta_{\alpha},
we get

(3.17)

\begin{split}&\tau^{2\alpha-1}{\mathcal{H}}^{2}_{\alpha}(\tau)\leq 4^{1-\alpha}\tau(|\!\ln\tau|+1)^{2},\;\;\;\tau\Upsilon^{2}(\tau,{{\Lambda}_{\infty}})\leq 2\tau\big\{|\!\ln\tau|^{2}+(\ln({\Lambda_{\infty}+1})+1)^{2}\big\}.\end{split}

For
0<\tau\leq\delta_{\alpha}<1
, there is

1\leq\widehat{C}_{\tau}\leq\widehat{C}_{\delta_{\alpha}}\leq\frac{1}{1-1/(2e)}<\frac{5}{4}.

Therefore, for the above results, we can rewrite (
3.16
) as

(3.18)

\begin{split}G_{m}\leq\frac{5}{4}T\Big(\frac{1}{2}+4|\lambda|\Big)+CT\big(1+(TC_{u})^{\alpha-1}\big)\tau|\!\ln\tau|^{2}+C(C_{u}^{\alpha-1}T^{\alpha}+{(\ln({\Lambda}_{\infty}+1))^{2}})\tau,\end{split}

where
C_{u}
is nonlocal and depend on the norms of
u
with regularity given in (
3.1
).
Then we deduce from (
3.14
)-(
3.18
) that, if
\frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha<1,\,0<\tau\leq\delta_{\alpha}
, there is

(3.19)

\begin{split}\|e^{m+1}\|\leq&\sqrt{c_{1}(1+\alpha^{-1}({\rm exp}(G_{m})-1))}\\
\leq&C_{u}(\tau+{h^{2}})\,{\rm exp}\big(CT\{1+(C_{u}T)^{\alpha-1}\tau|\!\ln\tau|^{2}\}+(C_{u}+C{(\ln({\Lambda}_{\infty}+1))^{2}})\tau\big).\end{split}

Noting that
\delta_{\alpha}={\rm exp}(\alpha/(\alpha-1))
is decreasing in
\alpha
and
\delta_{\alpha}\to 0
as
\alpha\to 1^{-}
, we then choose
\alpha=1/2
so that
\delta_{\alpha}
has the maximum value

Moreover, as the function

Besides, according to the assumption of induction, we obtain

{\ln(\Lambda_{\infty}+1)\leq C_{u}+\ln(1+\|U^{n}\|_{\infty}).}

Thus, we can conclude from (
3.19
) that

(3.20)

\|e^{m+1}\|\leq C_{u}(\tau+h^{2}).

Rewriting (
3.7a
) as follows

(3.21)

\delta^{2}_{\nabla}e^{m+1}_{j,k}=-{\rm i}\delta^{+}_{t}e^{m}_{j,k}+\xi^{m}_{j,k}+\lambda\mathcal{N}_{j,k}^{m},

and taking the discrete

\begin{split}|e^{m+1}|_{2}&\leq\tau^{-1}(\|e^{m}\|+\|e^{m+1}\|)+\|\xi^{m}\|+\lambda\|\mathcal{N}^{m}\|\\

&\quad+2|\lambda|\Upsilon(\epsilon,{{\Lambda}_{\infty}})\,\|e^{m}\|+C_{u}(\tau+h^{2}),\end{split}

then taking

in

and
\Upsilon(\epsilon,{{\Lambda}_{\infty}})
, we find from (

) that for
0<\tau\leq\delta_{\alpha}
, there is

(3.23)

\begin{split}&{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha}(\tau)\leq(2\tau)^{1-\alpha}(|\!\ln\tau|+1),\;\;\Upsilon(\tau,{{\Lambda}_{\infty}})\leq\sqrt{2}\big\{|\!\ln\tau|+(\ln({\Lambda_{\infty}+1})+1)\big\}.\end{split}

Then (
3.22
) can be bounded as

(3.24)

\begin{split}|e^{m+1}|_{2}&\leq\tau^{-1}(\|e^{m}\|+\|e^{m+1}\|)+{{2}^{2-\alpha}\tau^{2-\alpha}(|\!\ln\tau|+1)|\Omega|^{1-\alpha}}|\lambda|\,\|e^{m}\|^{\alpha}\\

By Lemma

\begin{split}\|e^{m+1}\|_{\infty}&\leq C\|e^{m+1}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}(|e^{m+1}|_{2}+\|e^{m+1}\|)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\

On the other hand, noticing

) gives that, without any constraint on the grid ratio, when

Step 2. (Estimate of

) with

, then take the real part to obtain

\displaystyle|{e}^{n+1}|_{1}^{2}=\frac{1}{\tau}{\rm{Im}}(e^{n+1},e^{n})_{h}-\lambda{\rm{Re}}(\mathcal{N}^{n},{e}^{n+1})_{h}-{\rm{Re}}(\xi^{n},{e}^{n+1})_{h},\quad{0}\leq{n}<N.

By using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and (

), (
3.2a
), (

) (

),and (

), we obtain

\displaystyle|{e}^{n+1}|_{1}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle{C}_{u}\tau^{-1}(\tau+h^{2})^{2},\quad{n}=0,1,2,\ldots,N-1,

Proof of Theorem

5.
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we propose the approximation of the solution to the LogSE by BDF1 and BDF2 schemes. We overcome the logarithmic nonlinear term bringing significant difficulties in the convergence analysis of the FDTD methods.
The convergence of two schemes are established by proving optimal/ almost optimal and unconditional order in the discrete
L^{2}
norm with some introduced inequalities. Moreover, we use the lifting technique to avoid the constrain of mesh conditions. Establishing the optimal (not almost optimal) error estimate of the two schemes in the discrete
H^{1}
norm will be the work we need to address in the near future.

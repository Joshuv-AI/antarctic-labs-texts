Approximation of generalized Wiener classes of functions of several variables in different metrics
Andrii Shydlich

Abstract
The paper presents new and known results on estimates of important linear and nonlinear approximation characteristics of generalized Wiener classes of functions of several variables in different metrics.

Introduction

Let
d
be a fixed positive integer
(d\in{\mathbb{N}})
, let
\mathbb{R}^{d}

and
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be the sets of ordered collections
{k}:=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})
of
d
real
and integer numbers correspondingly. Let also
\mathbb{T}^{d}\!\!:=[0,2\pi)^{d}

denote

-dimensional torus, and let

,
1\leq p\leq\infty,
be the space
of all Lebesgue-measurable on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

2\pi
-periodic in each variable functions

with finite norm

\|f\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L_{p}}}:=\|f\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}}=\left\{\begin{matrix}\Big((2\pi)^{-d}\displaystyle{\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}|f({x})|^{p}{\rm d}{x}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad\hfill&1\leq p<\infty,\\
\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{{x}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}}|f({x})|,\quad\hfill&p=\infty.\end{matrix}\right.

Set
({k,x}):=k_{1}x_{1}+k_{2}x_{2}+\ldots+k_{d}x_{d}
,
e_{k}(x):={\rm e}^{{\rm i}(k,x)}
and for any

,
we denote the Fourier coefficients of

by

\widehat{f}({k}):=(2\pi)^{-d}{\displaystyle\int}_{\!\!\!\!\mathbb{T}^{d}}f({x})\overline{e}_{k}(x){\rm d}{x}

{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},

Further, let

,

\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}|\widehat{f}({k})|,\quad\quad\quad p=\infty.\end{matrix}\right.

(1.1)

2
Approximative characteristics

Let
{\mathscr{X}}
be one of the spaces
L_{p}
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, or
{\mathcal{S}}^{p}
,
0<p\leq\infty
,
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
\gamma_{m}
be a collection of
m
different vectors of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
,
and let
f
be any function from
{\mathscr{X}}
. The quantity

E_{\gamma_{m}}(f)_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathscr{X}}}}=\inf_{c_{k}\in{\mathbb{C}}}\Big\|\,f-\sum\limits_{k\in{\gamma_{m}}}c_{k}\,e_{k}\Big\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathscr{X}}}}

(2.1)

is called the best approximation of the function
f
by
m
-term polynomials corresponding to the collection
\gamma_{m}
in the space
{\mathscr{X}}
.

Next, let
S_{\gamma_{m}}(f)=\sum_{k\in\gamma_{m}}\widehat{f}({k})e_{k}
be the Fourier sum
corresponding to the collection
\gamma_{m}
, and

{\mathscr{E}}_{\gamma_{m}}(f)_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathscr{X}}}}=\|\,f-S_{\gamma_{m}}(f)\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathscr{X}}}}

(2.2)

be the approximation of the function
f
by the Fourier sum
S_{\gamma_{m}}(f)
in
{\mathscr{X}}
.

If
{\mathfrak{N}}
is a subset of the space
{\mathscr{X}}
, then

E_{\gamma_{m}}(\mathfrak{N})_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathscr{X}}}}
and
{\mathscr{E}}_{\gamma_{m}}(\mathfrak{N})_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathscr{X}}}}
denote the exact upper bounds
of the quantities (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) over the set
{\mathfrak{N}}
, i.e.,

E_{\gamma_{m}}(\mathfrak{N})_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathscr{X}}}}=\sup\limits_{f\in\mathfrak{N}}E_{\gamma_{m}}(f)_{p}\qquad\mbox{\rm and}\qquad{\mathscr{E}}_{\gamma_{m}}(\mathfrak{N})_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathscr{X}}}}=\sup\limits_{f\in\,\mathfrak{N}}{\mathscr{E}}_{\gamma_{m}}(f)_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathscr{X}}}}.

(2.3)

Denote by
\Gamma_{m}
a set of all collections of
m
different vectors of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
. The quantities

{\mathscr{D}}_{m}({\mathfrak{N}})_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathscr{X}}}}=\inf\limits_{\gamma_{m}\in\Gamma_{m}}E_{\gamma_{m}}({\mathfrak{N}})_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathscr{X}}}}\qquad\mbox{\rm and}\qquad{\mathscr{D}}_{m}^{\perp}({\mathfrak{N}})_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathscr{X}}}}=\inf\limits_{\gamma_{m}\in\Gamma_{m}}{\mathscr{E}}_{\gamma_{m}}({\mathfrak{N}})_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathscr{X}}}}

(2.4)

are called the basis width and projection width (or Fourier-width) of order
m
of the set
{\mathfrak{N}}

in
{\mathscr{X}}
.

Further, for
f\in{\mathscr{X}}
, let
\{{k}_{l}\}_{l=1}^{\infty}=\{{k}_{l}(f)\}_{l=1}^{\infty}
denote a rearrangement of vectors of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
such that

|\widehat{f}({k}_{1})|\geq|\widehat{f}({k}_{2})|\geq\ldots.

(2.5)

In general case, this rearrangement is not unique. In such case, we take any rearrangement satisfying (
2.5
).

We define
\Sigma_{m}
to be the class of all complex trigonometric polynomials of the form

T\,{=}\sum_{{k}\in\gamma_{m}}c_{k}e_{k},

where
\gamma_{m}
is a collection from the set
\Gamma_{m}
.

In addition to (
2.1
)–(
2.4
),
consider the quantities

\|f-G_{m}(f)\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathscr{X}}}}=\Big\|f(\cdot)-\sum\limits_{l=1}^{m}\widehat{f}({k}_{l})e_{{k}_{l}}\Big\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathscr{X}}}},

(2.6)

\sigma_{m}^{\perp}(f)_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathscr{X}}}}=\inf\limits_{\gamma_{m}\in\Gamma_{m}}\Big\|f-\sum\limits_{{k}\in\gamma_{m}}\widehat{f}({k})e_{k}\Big\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathscr{X}}}}=\inf\limits_{\gamma_{m}\in\Gamma_{m}}{\mathscr{E}}_{\gamma_{m}}(f)_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathscr{X}}}},

(2.7)

and

\sigma_{m}(f)_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathscr{X}}}}=\inf\limits_{T\in\Sigma_{m}}\|f-T\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathscr{X}}}}=\inf\limits_{\gamma_{m}\in\Gamma_{m}}{E}_{\gamma_{m}}(f)_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathscr{X}}}}.

(2.8)

The quantities (
2.8
) and (
2.7
) are respectively called the best
m
-term trigonometric and the best
m
-term orthogonal trigonometric approximations of the function
f
in the space
{\mathscr{X}}
. The quantity (
2.6
) is called the approximation of the function
f
by
m
-term greedy polynomials in the space
{\mathscr{X}}
.

For a set
{\mathfrak{N}}\subset{\mathscr{X}}
, we put

\sigma_{m}^{\perp}({\mathfrak{N}})_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathscr{X}}}}=\sup\limits_{f\in{\mathfrak{N}}}\sigma_{m}^{\perp}(f)_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathscr{X}}}}\qquad\mbox{\rm and}\qquad\sigma_{m}({\mathfrak{N}})_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathscr{X}}}}=\sup\limits_{f\in{\mathfrak{N}}}\sigma_{m}(f)_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathscr{X}}}}.

(2.9)

In general case, the quantities (
2.6
) depend on the choice of the rearrangement satisfying (
2.5
). So, for the unique definition, we put

G_{m}({\mathfrak{N}})_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathscr{X}}}}=\sup\limits_{f\in{\mathfrak{N}}}\inf\limits_{\{k_{l}(f)\}_{l=1}^{\infty}}\Big\|f(\cdot)-\sum\limits_{l=1}^{m}\widehat{f}(k_{l}(f))e_{k_{l}(f)}\Big\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathscr{X}}}}.

(2.10)

In (
2.10
), for any function
f\in{\mathfrak{N}}
, we consider the infimum on all rearrangements, satisfying (
2.5
), but it should be noted that results, formulated in this paper, are also true for any other rearrangements, satisfying (

Research of the quantities of the form (
2.6
)–(
2.8
) goes back to
the paper of Stechkin
[
41
]
. Order estimates of these quantities on different
classes of functions of one and several variables were obtained by many authors.
In particular, the bibliography of papers with the similar results can be found in

[
8
,
54
,
55
,
28
,
11
]

It follows from (

\sigma_{m}(f)_{{}_{\scriptstyle L_{p}}}\leq\sigma_{m}^{\perp}(f)_{{}_{\scriptstyle L_{p}}}\leq\|f-G_{m}(f)\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle L_{p}}}\qquad\forall f\in L_{p},

(2.11)

\sigma_{m}(f)_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathcal{S}}^{p}}}=\sigma_{m}^{\perp}(f)_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathcal{S}}^{p}}}=\|f-G_{m}(f)\|_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathcal{S}}^{p}}}\qquad\forall f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{p},

(2.12)

and

(2.13)

3.1
Exact values of best approximations and basis widths

Let
\Psi=\{\Psi_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
be a sequence of complex numbers,
\Psi_{k}\not=0
, such that there exists a non-increasing rearrangement
\bar{\Psi}=\{\bar{\Psi}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
of the number system
\{|\Psi_{k}|\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
. It is clear that in this case the system
\{|\Psi_{k}|\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
is bounded, i.e.

|\Psi_{k}|\leq K\qquad\forall k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}.

(3.1)

Hereinafter,
K
,
c
,
K_{0},\ldots
are positive constants that do not depend on the corresponding variable (
k
,
t
, etc.).

A sufficient condition guaranteeing the existence of the rearrangement
\bar{\Psi}=\{\bar{\Psi}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is the condition

\lim\limits_{|k|\to\infty}|\Psi_{k}|=0,

(3.2)

however such rearrangement also exists, for example, when
\{|\Psi_{k}|\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
is a constant.

In the case where
{\mathfrak{N}}={\mathcal{F}}_{q}^{\Psi}
and
{\mathscr{X}}={\mathcal{S}}^{p}
,
the values of the characteristics (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) were found by
Stepanets
[
44
, Ch. XI]
,
[
45
]
for all
0<p,q<\infty
. In order to formulate this result,
for any collection
\gamma_{m}
of
m
different vectors of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
,
by
\Psi_{\gamma_{m}}=\{\Psi_{\gamma_{m}}(k)\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
denote a system of numbers such that

\Psi_{\gamma_{m}}(k)=\left\{\begin{matrix}0,\quad\hfill&k\in\gamma_{m},\\
\Psi_{k},\quad\hfill&k\overline{\in}\gamma_{m},\end{matrix}\right.

(3.3)

and by
\bar{\Psi}_{\gamma_{m}}=\{\bar{\Psi}_{\gamma_{m}}(j)\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
denote a non-increasing rearrangement
of the system
\{|\Psi_{\gamma_{m}}(k)|\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
.

Theorem A (​​
[
44
, Ch. XI]
,
[
45
]
).

Let
0<p,q<\infty
,
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\Psi=\{\Psi_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
be a sequence of complex numbers,
\Psi_{k}\not=0
,
such that there exists a non-increasing rearrangement
\bar{\Psi}=\{\bar{\Psi}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
of the number system
\{|\Psi_{k}|\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
.

(i)
In the case
0<p\leq q<\infty
,

E_{\gamma_{m}}({\mathcal{F}}_{q}^{\Psi})_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathcal{S}}^{p}}}={\mathscr{E}}_{\gamma_{m}}({\mathcal{F}}_{q}^{\Psi})_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathcal{S}}^{p}}}=\bar{\Psi}_{\gamma_{m}}(1)\qquad\forall\gamma_{m}\in\Gamma_{m},

(3.4)

and

{\mathscr{D}}_{m}({\mathcal{F}}_{q}^{\Psi})_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathcal{S}}^{p}}}={\mathscr{D}}_{m}^{\perp}({\mathcal{F}}_{q}^{\Psi})_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathcal{S}}^{p}}}=\bar{\Psi}_{m+1}.

(3.5)

(ii)
In the case when
0<q<p<\infty
and
\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}|\Psi_{k}|^{\frac{pq}{q-p}}<\infty
,

E_{\gamma_{m}}({\mathcal{F}}_{q}^{\Psi})_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathcal{S}}^{p}}}={\mathscr{E}}_{\gamma_{m}}({\mathcal{F}}_{q}^{\Psi})_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathcal{S}}^{p}}}=\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\bar{\Psi}_{\gamma_{m}}^{\frac{p\,q}{q-p}}(k)\Big)^{\frac{q-p}{p\,q}}\qquad\forall\gamma_{m}\in\Gamma_{m},

(3.6)

and

{\mathscr{D}}_{m}({\mathcal{F}}_{q}^{\Psi})_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathcal{S}}^{p}}}={\mathscr{D}}_{m}^{\perp}({\mathcal{F}}_{q}^{\Psi})_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathcal{S}}^{p}}}=\Big(\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}\bar{\Psi}_{k}^{\frac{p\,q}{q-p}}\Big)^{\frac{q-p}{p\,q}}.

(3.7)

Moreover, for any collection
\gamma_{m}^{*}=\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{m}\}
from the set
\Gamma_{m}
such that

\gamma_{m}^{*}=\{k_{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}:\quad|\Psi_{k_{j}}|=\bar{\Psi}_{j},\qquad j=1,2,\ldots,m\},

(3.8)

the following relation holds in both cases:

{\mathscr{D}}_{m}({\mathcal{F}}_{q}^{\Psi})_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathcal{S}}^{p}}}={\mathscr{D}}_{m}^{\perp}({\mathcal{F}}_{q}^{\Psi})_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathcal{S}}^{p}}}=E_{\gamma_{m}^{*}}({\mathcal{F}}_{q}^{\Psi})_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathcal{S}}^{p}}}={\mathscr{E}}_{\gamma_{m}^{*}}({\mathcal{F}}_{q}^{\Psi})_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathcal{S}}^{p}}}.

(3.9)

Next, we formulate a theorem that complements this statement for cases of infinite values of parameters
p
and
q
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\Psi=\{\Psi_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
be a sequence of complex numbers,
\Psi_{k}\not=0
,
such that there exists a non-increasing rearrangement
\bar{\Psi}=\{\bar{\Psi}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
of the system
\{|\Psi_{k}|\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
.

(i)
If
0<q<p=\infty
or
p=q=\infty
, then relations (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) hold.

(ii)
If

and the series

E_{\gamma_{m}}({\mathcal{F}}_{q}^{\Psi})_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathcal{S}}^{p}}}={\mathscr{E}}_{\gamma_{m}}({\mathcal{F}}_{q}^{\Psi})_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathcal{S}}^{p}}}=\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\bar{\Psi}_{\gamma_{m}}^{p}(k)\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\qquad\forall\gamma_{m}\in\Gamma_{m},

(3.10)

and

{\mathscr{D}}_{m}({\mathcal{F}}_{q}^{\Psi})_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathcal{S}}^{p}}}={\mathscr{D}}_{m}^{\perp}({\mathcal{F}}_{q}^{\Psi})_{{}_{\scriptstyle{\mathcal{S}}^{p}}}=\Big(\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}\bar{\Psi}_{k}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(3.11)

Moreover, for any collection
\gamma_{m}^{*}\in\Gamma_{m}
satisfying (
3.8
), relation (

3.2
.2.
Let us provide a few auxiliary facts and estimates regarding the structure of sequences of the form
(

).
It is clear that for any the sequence
\Psi
satisfying condition (
1.3
) with non-increasing positive function
\psi
, there exists a non-increasing rearrangement
\bar{\Psi}=\{\bar{\Psi}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
of the number system

\{|\Psi_{k}|\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
and it has the stepwise form:

\bar{\Psi}_{j}=\psi(s),\quad j\in(V_{s-1},V_{s}],\ \ s=0,1,2,\ldots,

(3.18)

where

V_{s}=V_{s}(d):=\#\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}:\|k\|_{\ell_{r}^{d}}\leq s\},\qquad s=0,1,\ldots,\qquad V_{-1}:=0.

(3.19)

Therefore, to study functionals that depend on such sequences
\Psi
,
it is necessary to have convenient estimates for the numbers
V_{s}
.

Let
B_{r}^{d}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\ \|x\|_{\ell_{r}^{d}}\leq 1\}
be the unit
\ell_{r}
-ball
of the space
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. It is known (see, e.g.,
[
62
]
) that the volume of
the ball
B_{r}^{d}
is calculated by the formula

M_{r,d}:=\operatorname{vol}(B_{r}^{d})={\textstyle\frac{\big(2\Gamma(1+\frac{1}{r})\big)^{d}}{\Gamma(1+\frac{d}{r})}},\qquad r\in(0,\infty],

(3.20)

and in particular,
M_{1,d}={\textstyle\frac{2^{d}}{d!}}
,
M_{2,d}={\textstyle\frac{\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2}+1)}}
and
M_{\infty,d}=2^{d}
.

For a real number
a
, we denote
(a)_{+}=\max\{0,a\}
. For positive sequences
a(s)
and
b(s)

(or functions
a(s)
and
b(s)
,
s\geq 1
), the expression “
a(s)\asymp b(s)
”
means that there are constants
0<K_{1}<K_{2}
such that for any
s\in\mathbb{N}
(or
s\geq 1
),

\ a(s)\leq K_{2}b(s)
(in this case, we write “
a(s)\ll b(s)
”) and
a(s)\geq K_{1}b(s)

(in this case, we write “
a(s)\gg b(s)
”).

The following lemma follows from known general results on asymptotic behavior of
the number of lattice points in scaled convex bodies (see, e.g.,
[
14
, Ch. 14]
).

Lemma 1
.

Let
r\in(0,\infty]
and
d\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then for any
s=0,1,2,\ldots
,

M_{r,d}\big((s-c_{r,d})_{+}\big)^{d}\leq V_{s}\leq M_{r,d}(s+c_{r,d})^{d},

(3.21)

where
c_{r,d}:={\textstyle\frac{d^{\frac{1}{r}}}{2}}
and

\nu_{s}:=V_{s}-V_{s-1}\asymp s^{d-1}.

(3.22)

Proof.
Relation (
3.22
) follows from (
3.21
).
Therefore, it suffices to prove (
3.21
).
For each
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, let
Q_{k}:=k+[-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}]^{d}
be the unit cube centered at
k
. The family
\{Q_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
forms a tiling of

\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\operatorname{vol}(Q_{k})=1
for all
k
. By definition, the values
V_{s}
can be expressed as the volume of the union of these cubes:

V_{s}=\operatorname{vol}\Big(\mathop{\bigcup}\limits_{\|k\|_{\ell_{r}^{d}}\leq s}Q_{k}\Big).

Note the value
c_{r,d}
is the maximum distance from the center to any point in the
unit cube
[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]^{d}
, i.e.,

c_{r,d}=\sup_{y\in[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]^{d}}\|y\|_{\ell_{r}^{d}}.

Upper bound.
Suppose
\|k\|_{\ell_{r}^{d}}\leq s
. For any point
x\in Q_{k}
, we have

x-k\in[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]^{d}
and by the triangle inequality

\|x\|_{\ell_{r}^{d}}\leq\|k\|_{\ell_{r}^{d}}+\|x-k\|_{\ell_{r}^{d}}\leq s+c_{r,d}.

This implies that the union of cubes is contained within the scaled ball of the radius
s+c_{r,d}
, i.e.,

\bigcup_{\|k\|_{\ell_{r}^{d}}\leq s}Q_{k}\subset(s+c_{r,d})B_{r}^{d}.

Taking the volume of both sides of this relation, we obtain

V_{s}\leq\operatorname{vol}((s+c_{r,d})B_{r}^{d})=M_{r,d}(s+c_{r,d})^{d}.

Lower bound.
It is sufficient to consider the case when
s>c_{r,d}
.
Let
x\in(s-c_{r,d})B_{r}^{d}
. Since the cubes
Q_{k}
tile
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

x
must belong to
Q_{k}
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. For this
k
, we have

\|k\|_{\ell_{r}^{d}}\leq\|x\|_{\ell_{r}^{d}}+\|k-x\|_{\ell_{r}^{d}}\leq(s-c_{r,d})+c_{r,d}=s.

This shows that every point in the smaller ball is covered by a cube whose center is in the set counted by
V_{s}
, i.e.,

(s-c_{r,d})B_{r}^{d}\subset\bigcup_{\|k\|_{\ell_{r}^{d}}\leq s}Q_{k}.

This similarly yields

M_{r,d}(s-c_{r,d})^{d}\leq V_{s}.

Remark 1
.

Denoting for a given positive integer
s
by

a number such that

V_{n_{s}-1}<s\leq V_{n_{s}},

(3.23)

we see that

\big((s/M_{r,d})^{\frac{1}{d}}-c_{r,d}\big)_{+}\leq n_{s}<(s/M_{r,d})^{\frac{1}{d}}+c_{r,d}+1.

1.
Introduction

For any prime
p\geq 3
,
B_{n}
is the
n
-th Bernoulli number. In 2007, Jianqiang Zhao
[
1
]
first established a curious congruence

(1.1)

\sum_{i+j+k=p\atop i,j,k>0}\frac{1}{ijk}\equiv-2B_{p-3}\pmod{p}.

In recent years, more and more researchers have generalized the formula above. For example, Xia Zhou and Tianxin Cai
[
2
]
used mathematical induction and the difference method and generalized the formula
(1.1). For any prime
p\geq 5
and
n\leq p-2
, they got

(1.2)

\sum_{l_{1}+l_{2}+...+l_{n}=p\atop l_{1},l_{2},...,l_{n}>0}\frac{1}{l_{1}l_{2}...l_{n}}\equiv\left\{\begin{aligned} -(n-1)!B_{p-n}\pmod{p},\quad\mbox{if}\quad 2\nmid n;\\
-\frac{n}{2(n+1)}n!B_{p-n-1}p\pmod{p^{2}},\quad\mbox{if}\quad 2\mid n.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

In addition, researchers have generalized formula (1.1) in various aspects. For example, in 2014, Liuquan Wang and Tianxin Cai
[
3
]
got a generalized curious congruence. For any prime
p\geq 3
and any positive integer
r
, they obtained

(1.3)

\sum_{i+j+k=p^{r}\atop i,j,k\in P_{p}}\frac{1}{ijk}\equiv-2p^{r-1}B_{p-3}\pmod{p^{r}},

where
P_{p}
denotes the positive integers which are coprime to
p
.

In 2015, Liuquan Wang
[
4
]
used the inclusion-exclusion principle and congruences of multiple harmonic sums and generalized the result, that is

(1.4)

\sum_{i_{1}+i_{2}+...+i_{5}=p^{r}\atop i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{5}\in P_{p}}\frac{1}{i_{1}i_{2}...i_{5}}\equiv-\frac{5!}{6}p^{r-1}B_{p-5}\pmod{p^{r}}.

Subsequently, in 2021, Liuquan Wang and Jianqiang Zhao
[
7
]
have generalized once again and got the following congruence

(1.5)

\sum_{l_{1}+...+l_{7}=p^{r}\atop l_{1},...,l_{7}\in P_{p}}\frac{1}{l_{1}...l_{7}}\equiv-\frac{7!}{10}p^{r-1}B_{p-7}\pmod{p^{r}}.

For any positive integer
n
, we define
Z(n)
as

(1.6)

Z(n):=\sum_{i+j+k=n\atop i,j,k\in P_{n}}\frac{1}{ijk},

where
P_{n}
denotes the positive integers which are coprime to
n
.

Although there have been numerous generalizations about formula (1.1), it remains many problems which have not yet to be conducted. In this paper, we shall further generalize the congruence (1.3) from

where
p_{1},...,p_{n}
are distinct odd primes and
r_{1},...,r_{n}

2.
Lemmas

In order to prove our theorems, we need the following lemmas.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let p be an odd prime and l, m, s be positive integers, then we have

\sum_{k=1\atop(k,p)=1}^{mp^{l}-1}\frac{1}{k^{s}}\equiv\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &0\pmod{p^{2l-1}},&&\text{{\rm for odd $s$ with $p-1\mid s+1$ and $p\nmid s$},}\\
&0\pmod{p^{2l}},&&\text{{\rm for odd $s$ with $p-1\nmid s+1$ or $p\mid s$},}\\
&0\pmod{p^{l-1}},&&\text{{\rm for even s with $p-1\mid s$},}\\
&0\pmod{p^{l}},&&\text{{\rm for even s with $p-1\nmid s$}.}\end{aligned}\end{cases}

Proof: By
[
10
]
, we have the following formulas

(2.1)

\sum_{k=1\atop(k,p)=1}^{p^{l}-1}\frac{1}{k^{s}}\equiv\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &0\pmod{p^{2l-1}},&&\text{{\rm for odd $s$ with $p-1\mid s+1$ and $p\nmid s$},}\\
&0\pmod{p^{2l}},&&\text{{\rm for odd $s$ with $p-1\nmid s+1$ or $p\mid s$},}\\
&0\pmod{p^{l-1}},&&\text{{\rm for even s with $p-1\mid s$},}\\
&0\pmod{p^{l}},&&\text{{\rm for even s with $p-1\nmid s$}.}\end{aligned}\end{cases}

Then for any positive integer n, we conclude the formula

\sum_{k=np^{l}+1\atop(k,p)=1}^{(n+1)p^{l}-1}\frac{1}{k^{s}}=\sum_{k=1\atop(k,p)=1}^{p^{l}-1}\frac{1}{(k+np^{l})^{s}}\equiv\sum_{k=1\atop(k,p)=1}^{p^{l}-1}\frac{1}{k^{s}}-snp^{l}\sum_{k=1\atop(k,p)=1}^{p^{l}-1}\frac{1}{k^{s+1}}\pmod{p^{2l}}.

When s is an odd integer which satisfies
p-1\mid s+1
and
p\nmid s
, from (2.1), we have

\sum_{k=1\atop(k,p)=1}^{p^{l}-1}\frac{1}{k^{s}}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2l-1}},\sum_{k=1\atop(k,p)=1}^{p^{l}-1}\frac{1}{k^{s+1}}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{l-1}}.

So we have,

(2.2)

\sum_{k=np^{l}+1\atop(k,p)=1}^{(n+1)p^{l}-1}\frac{1}{k^{s}}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2l-1}}.

Similarly, when s is an odd integer which satisfies
p-1\nmid s+1
or
p\mid s
, by (2.1), we have

(2.3)

\sum_{k=np^{l}+1\atop(k,p)=1}^{(n+1)p^{l}-1}\frac{1}{k^{s}}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2l}}.

When s is an even integer which satisfies
p-1\mid s
, we have,

(2.4)

\sum_{k=np^{l}+1\atop(k,p)=1}^{(n+1)p^{l}-1}\frac{1}{k^{s}}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{l-1}}.

When s is an even integer which satisfies
p-1\nmid s
, we have,

(2.5)

\sum_{k=np^{l}+1\atop(k,p)=1}^{(n+1)p^{l}-1}\frac{1}{k^{s}}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{l}}.

Therefore, we get

(2.6)

\sum_{k=np^{l}+1\atop(k,p)=1}^{(n+1)p^{l}-1}\frac{1}{k^{s}}\equiv\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &0\pmod{p^{2l-1}},&&\text{{\rm for odd $s$ with $p-1\mid s+1$ and $p\nmid s$},}\\
&0\pmod{p^{2l}},&&\text{{\rm for odd $s$ with $p-1\nmid s+1$ or $p\mid s$},}\\
&0\pmod{p^{l-1}},&&\text{{\rm for even s with $p-1\mid s$},}\\
&0\pmod{p^{l}},&&\text{{\rm for even s with $p-1\nmid s$}.}\end{aligned}\end{cases}

By the method of summation, we have finally got

\sum_{k=1\atop(k,p)=1}^{mp^{l}-1}\frac{1}{k^{s}}\equiv\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &0\pmod{p^{2l-1}},&&\text{{\rm for odd $s$ with $p-1\mid s+1$ and $p\nmid s$},}\\
&0\pmod{p^{2l}},&&\text{{\rm for odd $s$ with $p-1\nmid s+1$ or $p\mid s$},}\\
&0\pmod{p^{l-1}},&&\text{{\rm for even s with $p-1\mid s$},}\\
&0\pmod{p^{l}},&&\text{{\rm for even s with $p-1\nmid s$}.}\end{aligned}\end{cases}

Lemma 2.2
.

Let p be an odd prime, n and m be positive integers coprime to p, then we have

S(m,np;p)\equiv nm^{2}B_{p-3}\pmod{p}.

Proof: For any positive integer n and r, we have

(2.7)

\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}a^{r}=\frac{1}{r+1}\sum_{k=0}^{r}\binom{r+1}{k}B_{k}n^{r+1-k},

hence

\displaystyle\sum_{a=1\atop(a,p)=1}^{np-1}\frac{1}{a^{2}}\sum_{i=1\atop(i,p)=1}^{am-1}\frac{1}{i}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{a=1\atop(a,p)=1}^{np-1}\frac{1}{a^{2}}\sum_{i=1\atop(i,p)=1}^{am-1}i^{p-2}\equiv\sum_{a=1\atop(a,p)=1}^{np-1}\frac{1}{a^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{am-1}i^{p-2}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{a=1\atop(a,p)=1}^{np-1}\frac{1}{a^{2}}\frac{1}{p-1}\sum_{k=0}^{p-2}\binom{p-1}{k}B_{k}(am)^{p-1-k}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{m^{2}}{p-1}\sum_{k=0}^{p-2}\binom{p-1}{k}B_{k}\sum_{a=1\atop(a,p)=1}^{np-1}(am)^{p-3-k}\pmod{p}.

When
k\neq p-3
, we have

\sum_{a=1\atop(a,p)=1}^{np-1}(am)^{p-3-k}\equiv 0\pmod{p}.

So we have

S(n,np;p)\equiv\frac{m^{2}}{p-1}\binom{p-1}{p-3}B_{p-3}n(p-1)\equiv nm^{2}B_{p-3}\pmod{p}.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let p be an odd prime, n, m be positive integers coprime to p and
\alpha\geq 2
positive integer, then we have

(2.8)

S(m,np^{\alpha};p)\equiv np^{\alpha-1}m^{2}B_{p-3}\pmod{p^{\alpha}}

Proof: Let
a=s+np^{\alpha-1}t
,
1\leq s\leq np^{\alpha-1}-1
,
1\leq t\leq p-1
,
(s,p)=1
, then

\displaystyle S(np^{\alpha};p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{p-1}\sum_{s=1\atop(s,p)=1}^{np^{\alpha-1}-1}\frac{1}{(s+np^{\alpha-1}t)^{2}}\sum_{i=1\atop(i,p)=1}^{(s+np^{\alpha-1}t)m-1}\frac{1}{i}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{t=0}^{p-1}\sum_{s=1\atop(s,p)=1}^{np^{\alpha-1}-1}\frac{1}{s^{2}}(1-\frac{2np^{\alpha-1}t}{s})\sum_{i=1\atop(i,p)=1}^{sm-1}\frac{1}{i}

\displaystyle+\sum_{t=0}^{p-1}\sum_{s=1\atop(s,p)=1}^{np^{\alpha-1}-1}\frac{1}{s^{2}}(1-\frac{2np^{\alpha-1}t}{s})\sum_{i=sm\atop(i,p)=1}^{(s+np^{\alpha-1}t)m-1}\frac{1}{i}.

Trivially, we have

2np^{\alpha-1}\sum_{t=0}^{p-1}t\sum_{s=1\atop(s,p)=1}^{np^{\alpha-1}-1}\frac{1}{s^{3}}\sum_{i=1\atop(i,p)=1}^{sm-1}\frac{1}{i}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{\alpha}}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{p-1}\sum_{s=1\atop(s,p)=1}^{np^{\alpha-1}-1}\frac{1}{s^{2}}\left(1-\frac{2np^{\alpha-1}t}{s}\right)\sum_{i=sm\atop(i,p)=1}^{(s+np^{\alpha-1}t)m-1}\frac{1}{i}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{p-1}\sum_{s=1\atop(s,p)=1}^{np^{\alpha-1}-1}\frac{1}{s^{2}}(1-\frac{2np^{\alpha-1}t}{s})\sum_{i=sm\atop(i,p)=1}^{np^{\alpha-1}tm-1}\frac{1}{i}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{p-1}\sum_{s=1\atop(s,p)=1}^{np^{\alpha-1}-1}\frac{1}{s^{2}}(1-\frac{2np^{\alpha-1}t}{s})\sum_{i=np^{\alpha-1}tm+1\atop(i,p)=1}^{(s+np^{\alpha-1}t)m-1}\frac{1}{i}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{p-1}\sum_{s=1\atop(s,p)=1}^{np^{\alpha-1}-1}\frac{1}{s^{2}}(1-\frac{2np^{\alpha-1}t}{s})\sum_{i=sm\atop(i,p)=1}^{np^{\alpha-1}tm-1}\frac{1}{i}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{p-1}\sum_{s=1\atop(s,p)=1}^{np^{\alpha-1}-1}\frac{1}{s^{2}}(1-\frac{2np^{\alpha-1}t}{s})\sum_{i=1\atop(i,p)=1}^{sm-1}\frac{1}{i+np^{\alpha-1}tm}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{p-1}\sum_{s=1\atop(s,p)=1}^{np^{\alpha-1}-1}\frac{1}{s^{2}}(1-\frac{2np^{\alpha-1}t}{s})\sum_{i=sm\atop(i,p)=1}^{np^{\alpha-1}tm-1}\frac{1}{i}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{p-1}\sum_{s=1\atop(s,p)=1}^{np^{\alpha-1}-1}\frac{1}{s^{2}}(1-\frac{2np^{\alpha-1}t}{s})\sum_{i=1\atop(i,p)=1}^{sm-1}\frac{1}{i}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle np^{\alpha-1}m\sum_{t=0}^{p-1}t\sum_{s=1\atop(s,p)=1}^{np^{\alpha-1}-1}\frac{1}{s^{2}}(1-\frac{2np^{\alpha-1}t}{s})\sum_{i=1\atop(i,p)=1}^{sm-1}\frac{1}{i^{2}}

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{p-1}\sum_{s=1\atop(s,p)=1}^{np^{\alpha-1}-1}\frac{1}{s^{2}}(1-\frac{2np^{\alpha-1}t}{s})\sum_{i=1\atop(i,p)=1}^{np^{\alpha-1}tm-1}\frac{1}{i}\pmod{p^{\alpha}}.

.

Let p be an odd prime, n, m be positive integers coprime to p and
\alpha\geq 2
be a positive integer, then we have

T(m,np^{\alpha};p)\equiv\frac{n(m^{3}-m)}{3m}p^{\alpha-1}B_{p-3}\pmod{p^{\alpha}}.

1\leq s\leq np^{\alpha-1}-1

0\leq t\leq p-1

\displaystyle T(m,np^{\alpha};p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=1\atop(a,p)=1}^{np^{\alpha}-1}\frac{1}{a^{2}}\sum_{i=1\atop i\equiv am\pmod{p}}^{am-1}\frac{1}{i}=\sum_{t=0}^{p-1}\sum_{s=1\atop(s,p)=1}^{np^{\alpha-1}-1}\frac{1}{(s+np^{\alpha-1}t)^{2}}\sum_{i=1\atop i\equiv sm\pmod{p}}^{(s+np^{\alpha-1}t)m-1}\frac{1}{i}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=sm\atop i\equiv sm\pmod{p}}^{(s+np^{\alpha-1}t)m-1}\frac{1}{i}

3.
Proof of Theorems

In this part, we will give the proofs of Theorem 1 and Theorem 2.

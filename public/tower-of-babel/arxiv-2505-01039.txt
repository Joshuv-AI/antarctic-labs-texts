Regular expressions over countable words
Thomas Colcombet, A V Sreejith

Abstract
We investigate the expressive power of regular expressions for languages of countable words and establish their expressive equivalence with logical and algebraic characterizations. Our goal is to extend the classical theory of regular languages - defined over finite words and characterized by automata, monadic second-order logic, and regular expressions - to the setting of countable words. In this paper, we introduce and study five classes of expressions: marked star-free expressions , marked expressions , power-free expressions , scatter-free expressions , and scatter expressions . We show that these expression classes characterize natural fragments of logic over countable words and possess decidable algebraic characterizations. As part of our algebraic analysis, we provide a precise description of the relevant classes in terms of their 𝒥 \mathcal{J} -class structure. These results complete a triad of equivalences - between logic, algebra, and expressions - in this richer setting, thereby generalizing foundational results from classical formal language theory.

Introduction and contributions

This paper contributes to the theory of regular languages over
countable words
—that is, infinite words whose domains are countable linear orders, as opposed to
\omega
-words whose domains are isomorphic to the natural numbers. The foundational result in this direction is the decidability of satisfiability for monadic second-order logic (MSO) over countable words or chains
[
13
,
14
]
. Since then, the study of countable words has evolved into a rich theory that retain many foundational aspects of classical regular language theory over finite words. In particular, regular languages of finite words admit equivalent characterizations through several formal systems: automata (deterministic or nondeterministic), definability in MSO, recognizability by finite monoids, and regular expressions. Analogous equivalences have been established for languages of countable words; notably, MSO-definable languages over countable linear orders are known to coincide with those recognizable by
\circledast
-monoids

[
6
]
.

A striking branch of research concerning regular languages of finite words is the algebraic characterization of sub-logics of MSO. The seminal result in this direction is the celebrated Schützenberger–McNaughton–Papert theorem, that states that a language is recognized by an aperiodic monoid if and only if it is definable by a star-free expression, which is itself equivalent to being definable in first-order logic (FO). This theorem initiated a deep line of research in formal language theory.

In the setting of countable words,
[
7
]
establishes five correspondences between varieties of
\circledast
-monoids
and natural sub-logics of MSO. These sub-logics arise by restricting set quantification in various ways: to singletons (yielding FO), to Dedekind cuts, to finite sets, and so on.

This raises a natural question: can we identify natural classes of expressions that characterize these logics and algebraic varieties?

To better understand the expressive power of the different classes of expressions we study, we now present a few illustrative examples.

We denote

\displaystyle x<y\mid x\in X\mid a(x)\mid\phi_{1}\wedge\phi_{2}\mid\phi_{1}\vee\phi_{2}\mid\neg\phi\mid\exists x~\phi\mid\exists X~\phi\ ,

holds if there exists a

such that

The logic

is the extension of

3
Regular Expressions

In this section, we define the
regular operations
used in this paper. Our expressions build upon the usual operations over finite words – negation, union (denoted by
+
), intersection, concatenation (denoted by
.
), and
Kleene star
(denoted by
∗
) – and are extended by the
scatter operator
(denoted by
sc
).

We define
scatter expressions
(or simply
expressions
) over an alphabet
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Sigma}
by the following grammar:

S::=\emptyset\mid\sigma\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Sigma}\mid S+S\mid S\cap S\mid\overline{S}{}{}\mid SS\mid{S}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}*}}\mid{S}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}sc}}

We are also interested in a few sub classes of expressions. A
power-free expression
is an expression that does not use the operators
{S}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}*}}
or
{S}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}sc}}
. A
scatter-free expression
is an expression that does not use the operator
{S}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}sc}}
.

The semantics of expression
r
is given by the language
\mathcal{L}(r)\subseteq\Sigma^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\circledast}}
that is defined inductively.
The base cases are
\mathcal{L}(\emptyset)=\emptyset
and
\mathcal{L}(\sigma)=\{\sigma\}
.
The semantics of the boolean operators are natural.
The semantics of other operators are:
\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{r}_{1}\mathrm{r}_{2})=\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{r}_{1})\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{r}_{2})
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}({\mathrm{r}}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}*}})

\displaystyle=\{\prod_{i\in\alpha}u_{i}\mid\alpha\text{ is a finite ordering and }u_{i}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{r})\},\text{ and }

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}({\mathrm{r}}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}sc}})

\displaystyle=\{\prod_{i\in\alpha}u_{i}\mid\alpha\text{ is a {\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}scattered ordering} and }u_{i}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{r})\}.

A
left marked expression
is an expression of the form
\sigma\mathrm{r}
where
\sigma
is a letter and
r
is an expression.
We define
marked expressions
by the grammar:

M::=\emptyset\mid M+M\mid M\cap M\mid\overline{M}{}{}\mid M\sigma M\mid M{(\sigma M)}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}*}}

where
\sigma
is a letter. A
marked star-free expression
is a marked expression that does not contain the
Kleene star
. We say that
marked expressions
are closed under
marked concatenation
and
marked Kleene star
in contrast to
unrestricted concatenation
and
unrestricted Kleene star
for expressions in general.

Remark 5
.

Let
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
be
marked expressions
. Since the language
\{\epsilon\}
is definable by a marked expression (see
Section

3.1
.
Item

1
), we can define expressions like
{(\sigma H_{1})}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}*}}
within the marked expression syntax.

Moreover, the expression
{(\sigma_{1}H_{1}+\sigma_{2}H_{2})}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}*}}
is equivalent to the marked expression:

{(\sigma_{1}H_{1}{(\sigma_{2}H_{2})}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}*}})}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}*}}+{(\sigma_{2}H_{2}{(\sigma_{1}H_{1})}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}*}})}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}*}}.

Hence, marked expressions are closed under the
Kleene star
applied to unions of
left marked expressions
.

3.2

In this section, we describe constructions on languages that can be expressed using the operations defined in the previous section. We say that a language

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Sigma}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\circledast}}F{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Sigma}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\circledast}}\subseteq F

Lemma 6
.

Let

1.

The expressions
F{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Sigma}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Sigma}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\circledast}}

\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathtt{existsPrefix}}{}{}(F)::=\big\{w\mid\exists x\ w{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}|}_{(-\infty,x)}\in F\big\}\quad\text{and}\quad\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathtt{existsSuffix}}{}{}(F)::=\big\{w\mid\exists y\ w{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}|}_{(y,\infty)}\in F\big\}.

2.

The expressions
\overline{\overline{F}{}{}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Sigma}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Sigma}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\circledast}}}{}{}

\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathtt{forallPrefix}}{}{}(F)::=\ \big\{w\mid\forall x\ w{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}|}_{(-\infty,x)}\in F\big\}\quad\text{and}\quad\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathtt{forallSuffix}}{}{}(F)::=\ \big\{w\mid\forall y\ w{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}|}_{(y,\infty)}\in F\big\}.

3.

The expressions
\overline{({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Sigma}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\circledast}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Sigma})\overline{({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Sigma}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\circledast}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Sigma}F{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Sigma}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Sigma}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\circledast}})}{}{}}{}{}
and
\overline{(\overline{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Sigma}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\circledast}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Sigma}F{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Sigma}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Sigma}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\circledast}}}{}{})({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Sigma}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Sigma}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\circledast}})}{}{}

\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathtt{coFinal}}{}{}(F)::=\big\{w\mid\forall x\exists y\exists z\ x<y<z\wedge w{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}|}_{(y,z)}\in F\big\},\text{ and }

\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathtt{coInitial}}{}{}(F)::=\big\{w\mid\forall z\exists x\exists y\ x<y<z\wedge w{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}|}_{(x,y)}\in F\big\}.

4.

, then the set of words containing infinitely many non-overlapping factors from

(a)

\overline{\overline{F{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Sigma}F}{}{}\ {({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Sigma}\ \overline{F{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Sigma}F}{}{})}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}*}}}{}{}

(b)

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Sigma}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\circledast}}\big({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathtt{coFinal}}{}{}(F)+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathtt{coInitial}}{}{}(F)\big){\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Sigma}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\circledast}}

5.

Let

be

. The expression
\overline{{(\overline{FF}{}{})}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}sc}}}{}{}
defines the set of all words containing densely many non-overlapping factors from

.

Proof.

We prove each item separately:

1.

The expression

defines the set of words that have some prefix in

, followed by a letter. This is precisely the definition of

2.

Let
L::=\overline{F}{}{}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Sigma}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Sigma}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\circledast}}

3.

Suppose

4.

Let

, where

.

(
\Rightarrow
) Suppose

Then every finite factorization of

z_{i-1}x_{i}=z_{i-1}q\sigma p

in both the cases.

We conclude that
w\in H

5.

The proof for dense occurrence is similar. A word contains densely many

-factor. This is captured precisely by

In this section, we first recall the algebraic framework that was introduced in

4.1
\circledast
-monoids
,
\circledast
-algebras
,
recognizability

A
\circledast
-monoid

\mathbf{M}=(M,{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\pi}})
is a set
M
equipped with an operation
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\pi}:M^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\circledast}}\rightarrow M
, called the
product
, that satisfies
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\pi}(a)=a
for all
a\in M
, and the
generalized associativity
property: for every
words

u_{i}
over
M
with
i
ranging over a
countable linear ordering

\alpha

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\pi}\left(\prod_{i\in\alpha}u_{i}\right)={\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\pi}\left(\prod_{i\in\alpha}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\pi}(u_{i})\right)
.
We reserve the notation
1={\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\pi}(\varepsilon)
for the
identity element
.

An example of a

\circledast
-monoid
is the free
\circledast
-monoid

({\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Sigma}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\circledast}},\varepsilon,\prod)
over the
alphabet
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Sigma}
with the product being the
generalized concatenation
.
Now we discuss some natural algebraic notions.
A
morphism
from a
\circledast
-monoid

(M,{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\pi}})
to a
\circledast
-monoid

(M^{\prime},{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\pi}})
is a map

such that, for
every

,
h(\pi(w))=\pi^{\prime}(\bar{h}(w))
where
\bar{h}
is the pointwise extension of
h
to words.
We skip the notions sub-
\circledast
-monoid
and direct products since they are as expected.
We say
\mathbf{M}

divides

\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
if there exists a sub-
\circledast
-monoid

\mathbf{M}^{\prime\prime}
of
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
and a surjective morphism from
\mathbf{M}^{\prime\prime}
to
\mathbf{M}
.

A
\circledast
-monoid

(M,{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\pi}})

is said to be finite if
M
is so. But, even for a finite
\circledast
-monoid
,
the product operation
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\pi}
has an infinitary description. However,
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\pi}
can be captured using finitely presentable
derived operations
.
Corresponding to a
\circledast
-monoid

\circledast
-algebra

omega power
of
a

of

Let us fix a finite

\displaystyle a\mathrel{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\leqslant_{\mathcal{R}}}}b\ \text{if }a=b\mathbin{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\cdot}}{}{}x~\text{for $x\in M$},\ a\mathrel{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathcal{R}}}b\ \text{if }a\mathrel{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\leqslant_{\mathcal{R}}}}b\ \text{and}\ b\mathrel{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\leqslant_{\mathcal{R}}}}a,

3
3

3

If
\pi(w)\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\bar{A}}

Proof.

Assume

Let

The above closure follows from the assumption that no shuffle witness occurs in
w
.

We now invoke Zorn’s Lemma.

Claim 31

(Zorn’s Lemma)
.

The poset
(\mathcal{I},\subseteq)
has a maximal element.

Proof sketch.

We show that every chain in
\mathcal{I}
has an upper bound.

•

Finite chains: Since the
\mathcal{I}
is closed under finite union, the union of the finite chain is an upper bound.

•

Chains with common left endpoint: Let the chain be
I_{1},I_{2},\dots
. Consider
J_{k}=I_{k}\setminus I_{k-1}
for all
k>1
and
J_{1}=I_{1}
. Each of these
J_{k}
is in
\mathcal{I}
since
\pi(w{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}|}_{J_{k}})\mathrel{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\geqslant_{\mathcal{J}}}}\pi(w{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}|}_{I_{k}})
. Since
\mathcal{I}
is closed under omega unions, we have
\bigcup_{k\in\omega}J_{k}\in\mathcal{I}
. Hence union of
I_{k}
is an upper bound.

•

Chains with common right endpoint: This is symmetric to the case above.

•

General chains can be split into left- and right-coinciding subchains and handled via finite union closure.

Since every chain in
\mathcal{I}
has an upper bound, Zorn’s Lemma applies and we conclude that
\mathcal{I}
has a maximal element.
∎

Let
C\subseteq\mathcal{I}
be the set of maximal intervals obtained via Zorn’s Lemma. We show that
C=\{\alpha\}
:

•

If
|C|=1
, then
\pi(w)\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}A}{}{}
.

•

If some
C_{1},C_{2}\in C
intersect, their intersection contradicts maximality.

•

If
C_{1},C_{2}
are consecutive, then
C_{1}\cup C_{2}\in\mathcal{I}
, again contradicting maximality.

•

If
C
forms a dense linear order, then there is a domain
C^{\prime}\subseteq C
such that

\prod_{I\in C^{\prime}}w{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}|}_{I}
forms a shuffle word (see
[
14
]
), contradicting the absence of a shuffle witness.

Hence, only the first case is possible, and we conclude
\pi(w)\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}A}{}{}
.
∎

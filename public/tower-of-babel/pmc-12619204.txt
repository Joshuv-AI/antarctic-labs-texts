Electroacupuncture regulates Th17/Treg cell balance through SIRT1 mediated anti-inflammatory pathway to improve insulin resistance induced obesity
Authors: Wenyan Wang, Shurui Yang, Jing Liu, Shang Huang, Yong Li, Fengxia Liang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe prevalence of obesity and its associated complications poses a significant threat to human health. The application of electroacupuncture (EA) in the treatment of obesity has been extensively studied. This study aims to investigate the immunological mechanisms underlying the beneficial effects of EA on obesity-related inflammation.MethodsIn the present study, obesity was induced in rats through a high-fat diet (HFD), followed by the implementation of EA treatment for a duration of 8 weeks. Additionally, the obese rats were subjected to treatment with both an agonist (Resveratrol) and inhibitor (Sirtinol) of silent information regulator 1 (SIRT1). The body weight, fasting blood glucose, and 2-hour postprandial blood glucose levels were regularly monitored throughout the treatment of obese rats. Insulin sensitivity was evaluated through intraperitoneal insulin tolerance test and intraperitoneal glucose tolerance test upon completion of the treatment. The weight of perirenal adipose of obese rats was measured, and the size of adipocytes was observed using HE staining. The quantification of helper T 17 (Th17) cells and regulatory T (Treg) cells in peripheral blood and perirenal adipose tissue was performed using flow cytometry. The expression levels of SIRT1 and acetylated NF-κB in perirenal adipose tissue of rats were observed using immunofluorescence staining and Western blotting analysis.ResultsCompared to rats on a normal diet, HFD-induced obese rats exhibited insulin resistance (IR), pronounced accumulation of perirenal adipose, an imbalance between Th17 and Treg cells, as well as down-regulated expression of SIRT1. The administration of Resveratrol resulted in the alleviation of the aforementioned symptoms in obese rats. The EA treatment also ameliorated body weight gain, adipose accumulation, IR, and adipose tissue inflammation in rats. Additionally, it was found to enhance SIRT1 expression and reduce acetylation of NF-κB protein. However, the effects of EA treatment on HFD-induced obese rats were partially attenuated by Sirtinol.ConclusionEA exhibits a favorable therapeutic effect on IR and adipose inflammation in obese rats. The underlying mechanism is associated with the promotion of the SIRT1-mediated anti-inflammatory pathway, which regulates the balance between Th17 and Treg cells.

Introduction

Obesity is a chronic, progressive and relapsing disease caused by a variety of factors and has emerged as a significant public health challenge [1]. The prevalence of obesity is rapidly increasing on a global scale, thereby elevating the risk of various complications such as cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, and obstructive sleep apnea [2]. The prevalence of obesity in China has become a significant burden on the country’s healthcare system. Over the past four decades, changes in dietary patterns and a lack of physical activity, along with other individual-level risk factors such as genetic susceptibility and psychological influences, have contributed to a rapid increase in the number of overweight and obese individuals [3]. The health burden associated with obesity is substantial and projected to increase sharply in the future [4]. Therefore, it is of paramount importance to prioritize the prevention and treatment of obesity.

The adipose tissue serves not only as a reservoir of energy, but also as the primary organ for secretion of adipokines and cytokines [5, 6]. Adipokines play a crucial role as signaling factors in endocrine, metabolic, and immune responses. They are intricately involved in maintaining energy homeostasis in healthy individuals but become dysregulated in conditions such as obesity, metabolic syndrome, type 2 diabetes, and other related disorders [7]. In the obese state, macrophages constitute 40% of adipose tissue cells and undergo activation to secrete a diverse array of cytokines and chemokines, thereby precipitating the onset of inflammation [8]. Therefore, obesity is considered an inflammatory disease [9]. Adipose tissue inflammation plays a pivotal role in the accumulation of fat, which subsequently leads to lipotoxicity and triggers systemic inflammation. Over time, chronic low-grade systemic inflammation hinders the effectiveness of insulin within the insulin signaling pathway, ultimately resulting in insulin resistance (IR) [10]. Therefore, modulating immune cells in adipose tissue to restore immune homeostasis has emerged as a promising strategy for the prevention and treatment of obesity.

The helper T 17 (Th17) cells and regulatory T (Treg) cells, both belonging to the CD4+ T cell subset, play a crucial role in maintaining immune system homeostasis. The balance between Th17 and Treg cells appears to be associated with the maintenance of stable blood sugar levels and blood pressure control. However, obesity disrupts this delicate equilibrium [11]. It has been demonstrated that an imbalance between Th17 and Treg cells contributes to the development of IR [12]. Therefore, regulation of the balance between Th17 and Treg cells is crucial in effectively managing IR associated with obesity. The nuclear factor-κB (NF-κB) is a widely distributed family of proteins that govern gene transcription and participate in various cellular reactions [13]. Especially in the cellular immune response, the activation of NF-κB is involved in the activation and function regulation of T lymphocytes [14, 15]. Therefore, strict control of the activation of NF-κB signaling pathway to regulate the balance of Th17/Treg cells is crucial for inflammatory diseases [16, 17]. Silent information regulator 1 (SIRT1) is a highly conserved evolution of NAD+-dependent histone acetylation enzyme, which can deacetylate the p65 subunit and inhibit the nuclear translocation of NF-κB, thereby reducing inflammatory response [18, 19]. However, the regulation of SIRT1 to enhance NF-κB deacetylation and subsequently modulate the Th17/Treg balance in the study of obesity related rarely reported.

Electroacupuncture (EA) applies electrical stimulation to acupoints via an acupuncture needle connected to a device that generates electric pulses. It shares similarities with traditional acupuncture therapy, yet EA offers the advantages of a larger stimulating area, enhanced repeatability, and increased objectivity [20]. EA is widely utilized in the treatment of obesity, with its principle rooted in traditional Chinese medicine’s meridian system. Studies have demonstrated that EA therapy can effectively reduce blood lipid levels, inhibit the accumulation of white adipose tissue while promoting its browning, modulate the intestinal microbiota, improve the function of the enteric nervous system, and ultimately contribute to the management of obesity [21–23]. Moreover, EA treatment has the potential to attenuate inflammation associated with obesity [24, 25]. Additionally, EA has the ability to activate SIRT1 in adipose tissue of obese rats, resulting in the deacetylation of NF-κB and subsequently regulating IR [26]. However, the uncertainty remains regarding whether EA can modulate the Th17/Treg cell balance through SIRT1 activation to mitigate obesity-related inflammation.

Therefore, in this study, EA was employed to treat high-fat diet (HFD)-induced obesity in rats by targeting specific “Biao-Ben acupoints matching “. The objective was to investigate the impacts of EA on body weight, inflammation, insulin sensitivity, and Th17/Treg cell balance in obese rats. This study aimed to elucidate the immunological mechanism through which EA improves obesity by regulating Th17/Treg cell balance via the SIRT1- mediated anti-inflammatory pathway.

Materials and methods

Animals

Male SPF Wistar rats aged 8 weeks were obtained from Hubei Provincial Laboratory Animal Research Center. The rats were housed under controlled conditions with a temperature range of 22–26 ℃, relative humidity maintained at 50%-60%, and a light-dark cycle of 12 h. Experimental treatments were initiated following a period of adaptive feeding lasting for one week. The animal experiments procedures complied with the Laboratory Animal-Guideline for ethical review of animal welfare issued by the Ministry of Science and Technology of China, and received approval from the Ethics Committee of Wuhan Myhalic Biotechnology Co., Ltd (Approval number: HLK-20221031-001). In addition, the animal experiment reports in this study were conducted in accordance with the ARRIVE guidelines.

Animal models and grouping

The normal diet (ND) group consisted of five randomly selected rats that were fed a standard diet (3.8 kcal/g) containing 70% carbohydrates, 20% protein, and 10% fat. The remaining rats were subjected to a high-fat diet (5.4 kcal/g, consisting of 38.5% carbohydrates, 15% protein, and 46.5% fat) for a duration of 8 weeks. Upon achieving a body weight that was 20% higher than the ND group rats, the experimental obesity rat model was deemed successfully established [27]. Rats exhibiting an increase of less than 20% were excluded from the study. The rats that successfully constructed the model were randomly assigned to the following groups using the random number table method: HFD group (n = 20), HFD + EA group (n = 10), HFD + sham EA group (n = 5), HFD + EA + Sirtinol group (n = 5), and HFD + Resveratrol (Res) group (n = 5).

Animals treatment

The ND and HFD groups did not undergo any intervention, while the rats in the HFD + EA group received EA treatment at Zhongwan (CV 12), Guanyuan (CV 4), Zusanli (ST 36) and Fenglong (ST 40). The filiform needle, measuring 0.3 × 15 mm, is inserted straight into the Zusanli (ST 36) and Fenglong (ST 40) at a depth of 3 ~ 5 mm. The filiform needle was then inserted obliquely for 3 ~ 5 mm at Guanyuan (CV 4) towards the xiphoid process and Zhongwan (CV 12) towards the pubic bone, respectively. The HANS LH202H EA apparatus was utilized, with the parameters set at 2 Hz and 1 mA, employing a connected wave. The Guanyuan (CV 4) and Zhongwan (CV 12) acupoints were connected to two electrodes with one output, while the Zusanli (ST 36) and Fenglong (ST 40) acupoints on the same side were also connected to two electrodes with other output. The two sides were alternated during application. Each session of EA lasted for 10 min, conducted three times per week over a duration of 8 weeks. The sham EA group received shallow insertion of a filiform needle into the subcutaneous tissue near the acupoints Zhongwan (CV 12), Guanyuan (CV 4), Fenglong (ST 40), and Zusanli (ST 36), followed by electrode connection without electrical stimulation. Based on EA treatment, the rats in HFD + EA + Sirtinol group were received tail vein injections of the SIRT1 inhibitor Sirtinol (1 mg/kg; MedChemExpress, Monmouth Junction, NJ, USA), three times a week for 8 weeks [28]. Resveratrol (MedChemExpress, Monmouth Junction, NJ, USA) was administered via gavage at a dosage of 200 mg/kg, three times a week for 8 weeks. Drug injections were administered by experimenters who were blinded to the specific group intervention protocols. The timeline of animal handling procedures is presented in Fig. 1A.

The body weight, fasting blood glucose (FBG), and 2-hour postprandial blood glucose levels were assessed biweekly throughout the treatment period. Specifically, following an overnight fasting period or the intragastric administration of a glucose solution (2 g/kg) for 2 h in rats, blood samples were collected from the tail vein using a blood glucose meter test strip, and the corresponding blood glucose levels were measured to determine the FBG or 2-hour postprandial blood glucose levels in rats. The blood samples were collected from the abdominal aorta of rats under 3% pentobarbital sodium (30 mg/kg) anesthesia. Following this, rats were euthanized via intraperitoneal injection of 100 mg/kg sodium pentobarbital. The perirenal adipose tissue was carefully dissected and weighed. Subsequently, a portion of the fresh tissue samples was utilized for flow cytometry analysis, another portion was fixed for histological examination, and the remaining samples were stored at low temperature for subsequent use.

Insulin sensitivity

After a 8-week treatment period, intraperitoneal insulin tolerance test (IPITT) and intraperitoneal glucose tolerance test (IPGTT) were conducted. The rats were intraperitoneally injected with insulin solution (1 U/kg; Aladdin, Shanghai, China) or 50% glucose solution (2 g/kg; Aladdin, Shanghai, China), respectively. Subsequently, blood samples were collected from the tail vein at 0, 30, 60, 90, and 120 min post-injection for measurement of blood glucose levels. The blood glucose change curve was plotted, and the area under the curve (AUC) was calculated to assess insulin sensitivity in rats [29].

ELISA analysis

Rat blood samples were centrifuged at 3000 r/min for 10 min to obtain serum. The experimental procedures were conducted in accordance with the instructions provided by the ELISA kits (Bioswamp, Wuhan, China), and the concentrations of fasting insulin (FINS, RA20092), glycated hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c, RA20762), interleukin 17 A (IL-17 A, RA20117), and C-reactive protein (CRP, RA20041) in rat serum were quantified. The Homeostasis Model Assessment-IR (HOMA-IR) index was calculated using the formula HOMA-IR index = FBG × FINS / 22.5 to evaluate IR.

Morphological analysis

The perirenal adipose tissue of rat was isolated and weighed. A portion of the adipose tissue was then immersed in a 4% paraformaldehyde solution for fixation over a period of 48 h before undergoing paraffin embedding and sectioning. The paraffin sections containing adipose tissue were deparaffinized to water, stained using hematoxylin and eosin (Solarbio, Beijing, China), sealed with a neutral resin (Servicebio, Wuhan, China), observed under a microscope, and images were captured. Five non-overlapping fields of view were randomly selected from each sample for imaging. The boundaries of adipocytes within each field of view were delineated using Image J software. Measurement parameters were calibrated according to the scale bar, and the cross-sectional area of individual cells was quantified. The mean cell area was calculated and used as an indicator of adipocyte size in the respective sample.

Flow cytometry analysis

After rinsing with physiological saline, the perirenal adipose tissue in the rats is dissected and ground to obtain a single cell suspension. The peripheral blood was collected from rats, and erythrocytes were eliminated by the addition of red blood cell lysate. Subsequently, centrifugation at 2000 r/min for 5 min was performed to obtain lymphocytes. The cells were resuspended in phosphate buffer saline (PBS; Solarbio, Beijing, China), and the cell density was adjusted. A total of 1 × 106 cells were utilized for the assay.

For the detection of Th17 cell subsets, 0.5 µL of CD4- fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC) antibody (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) was added and incubated at 4 °C in the dark for 30 min. Following cell membrane rupture treatment, 0.625 µL of IL-17 A-phycoerythrin (PE) antibody (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) was added and incubated at 4 °C to avoid light exposure for 45 min. After centrifugation at a speed of 1000 r/min for 5 min, cells were resuspended by adding 200 µL of PBS and placed on a flow cytometer for detection.

For the detection of Treg cell subsets, 0.5 µL of CD4-FITC antibody and 0.625 µL of CD25-PE antibody (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) were added and incubated at 4 °C in the dark for 30 min. Following cell membrane rupture treatment, 5 µL of Forkhead box protein P3 (FoxP3)-allophycocyanin (APC) antibody (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) was added and incubated at 4 °C without exposure to light for 45 min. After centrifugation at a speed of 1000 r/min for 5 min, cells were resuspended by adding 200 µL of PBS and subjected to flow cytometer analysis.

Immunofluorescence staining

The paraffin-embedded sections of rat perirenal adipose tissue were deparaffinized to water, followed by antigen retrieval using Tris-ethylene diamine tetraacetic acid buffer. Subsequently, the slices were incubated in 3% H2O2 for 10 min and then in 10% goat serum for 30 min. The SIRT1 antibody (diluted 1:200; Abcam, Cambridge, MA, USA) and the Acetyl-NF-κB (Ac-NF-κB) antibody (diluted 1:100; Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) were added dropwise and incubated overnight at a temperature of 4 °C. After the sections were washed, secondary antibodies (Proteintech, Wuhan, China) were added and incubated at room temperature for 1 h. The nuclei were stained with dihydrochloride (DAPI) staining solution. Finally, the slices were sealed using an anti-fluorescence quenching sealing solution (Solarbio, Beijing, China), and the expression levels of SIRT1 and Ac-NF-κB in adipose tissue were observed under a fluorescence microscope. Five non-overlapping fields of view were randomly selected from each sample, and the integrated optical density (IOD) values of the positively stained regions corresponding to the target protein were quantitatively analyzed using Image-Pro Plus 6.0 software.

Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) analysis

The perirenal adipose tissue of rats was collected, and the total RNA in the tissue was extracted using FastPure Cell/Tissue Total RNA Isolation Kit (Vazyme, Nanjing, China). The RNA is synthesized into cDNA through reverse transcription using Hifair® III 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis SuperMix for qPCR (YEASEN, Shanghai, China). Subsequently, Hieff® qPCR SYBR® Green Master Mix (YEASEN, Shanghai, China) was used for PCR amplification of the cDNA. The PCR procedures consist of 40 cycles with the following steps: 95 ℃ for 5 s, 56 ℃ for 10 s, 72 ℃ for 25 s. The primer sequences were as follows: SIRT1 forward 5’-AGGGAACCTCTGCCTCAT CTAC-3’, reverse 5’-GGCATACTCGCCACCTAACC-3’; GAPDH forward 5’-GGCATACTCGCCACCTAACC-3’, reverse 5’-CCAGTAGACTCCACGACAT-3’.

Western blotting analysis

The perirenal adipose tissue of rats was collected, and then homogenized in RIPA lysis buffer (Solarbio, Beijing, China) for protein extraction. The protein concentration was determined using the BCA method, and 20 µg of protein samples were subjected to sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis. The proteins were transferred from the gel onto polyvinylidene fluoride (Millipore Corp., Billerica, MA, USA) membranes and subsequently blocked with 5% skim milk powder for a duration of 2 h at room temperature. Primary antibodies (diluted 1:100; mouse anti-SIRT1, Abcam, Cambridge, UK; rabbit anti-Ac-NF-κB, Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA; rabbit anti-NF-κB, rabbit anti-IL-17 A, rabbit anti- FoxP3, rabbit anti-IL-10, and rabbit anti-GAPDH, Bioswamp, Wuhan, China) were added to the membrane and incubated overnight at 4 ° C. Then, goat anti-rabbit or anti-mouse IgG was added and incubated for 1 h at room temperature. Apply chemiluminescence reagent onto the membrane, followed by analysis of protein bands using TANON GIS software.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis and graphing were conducted using SPSS 22.0 software and Graph Pad 8.0 software, respectively. The independent samples t-test was employed for comparing two groups, while one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by LSD test was used for comparing multiple groups. A significance level of P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

HFD-induced obese rats exhibited reduced insulin sensitivity, an imbalance in Th17/Treg cells, and decreased expression of SIRT1

The successful establishment of experimental obese rats was confirmed by inducing a weight increase of more than 20% in rats fed with HFD compared to ND group (Fig. 1B). The FINS and HOMA-IR levels in HFD-induced rats were significantly elevated, indicating the development of severe IR (Fig. 1C and D). Additionally, the IPITT and IPGTT revealed higher blood glucose levels in HFD group compared to ND group, along with a significant increase in AUC (Fig. 1E), indicating reduced insulin sensitivity in HFD-induced rats. The perirenal adipose tissue of the rats was dissected, and it was observed that the HFD group exhibited significantly higher weight in their perirenal adipose tissue compared to the ND group (Fig. 1F), along with increased adipocyte size (Fig. 1G). Strikingly, there was a notable increase in Th17 cells and a decrease in Treg cells observed in both the peripheral blood and perirenal adipose tissue of rats in HFD group (Fig. 1H). Moreover, the expression levels of SIRT1 mRNA and protein in perirenal adipose tissue were significantly downregulated in HFD group (Fig. 1I and J). Therefore, we postulated that the presence of IR and an imbalance between Th17 and Treg cells in rats fed a HFD may be associated with the downregulation of SIRT1 expression.

Activation of SIRT1 ameliorated obesity, enhanced insulin sensitivity, and restored the Th17/Treg cells balance

To validate our hypothesis, we administered Resveratrol, a SIRT1 agonist, to HFD-induced obese rats for a duration of 8 weeks. This intervention resulted in a significant upregulation of both mRNA and protein expression levels of SIRT1 in the perirenal adipose tissue of the rats (Fig. 2A and B). Furthermore, Resveratrol treatment induced weight reduction (Fig. 2C), diminished perirenal adiposity (Fig. 2D), and decreased the size of adipocytes in the perirenal adipose tissue of obese rats (Fig. 2E). Additionally, the administration of Resveratrol for a duration of 8 weeks demonstrated a significant regulatory effect on the levels of FBG, FINS and HOMA-IR index in obese rats (Fig. 2F-H) and significantly enhanced insulin sensitivity upon completion of the treatment (Fig. 2I). The administration of Resveratrol effectively regulated the Th17/Treg cells imbalance in both peripheral blood and perirenal adipose tissue of obese rats, leading to a reduction in the number of Th17 cells and an increase in the number of Treg cells (Fig. 2J). The findings of this study provide evidence to support our hypothesis that activation of SIRT1 can effectively regulate body weight, IR, and the imbalance between Th17 and Treg cells in HFD-induced obese rats.

EA upregulated expression of SIRT1 protein, downregulated acetylation level of NF-κB, and corrected the balance of Th17/Treg cells in HFD-induced rats

The effects of EA on HFD-induced obese rats were investigated by administering EA at Zhongwan (CV 12), Guanyuan (CV 4), Zusanli (ST 36), and Fenglong (ST 40) for a duration of 8 weeks. According to the results, EA treatment induced significant reductions in weight (Fig. 3A), FBG and 2-hour postprandial blood glucose of rats (Fig. 3B and C), as well as decreasing the levels of FINS, HbA1c, and HOMA-IR index (Fig. 3D and E), while significantly increasing insulin sensitivity (Fig. 3F). Additionally, EA treatment reduced perirenal adipose and adipocyte size in obese rats (Fig. 3G and H). These findings suggest that EA may effectively reduce body weight and alleviate IR in obese rats.

Additionally, our findings demonstrate that EA treatment enhances SIRT1 protein expression while reducing Ac-NF-κB level in the perirenal adipose tissue of HFD-induced obese rats (Fig. 4A and B). EA treatment markedly suppressed the expression of IL-17 A protein in the perirenal adipose tissue of obese rats, while promoting the expression of FoxP3 and IL-10 proteins (Fig. 4C). In addition, EA administration led to a reduction in the serum levels of both IL-17 A and CRP in obese rats (Fig. 4D). Furthermore, the frequency of Th17 cells in peripheral blood and perirenal adipose was reduced, while there was an increase in Treg cells population (Fig. 4E). The findings suggest that treatment with EA promotes the expression of SIRT1, reduces NF-κB acetylation, and modulates the balance between Th17 and Treg cells in order to alleviate adipose inflammation in HFD-induced obese rats.

SIRT1 inhibitor partially suppressed the effects of EA on obesity, insulin sensitivity, and the balance of Th17/Treg cells in HFD-induced rats

In order to investigate the potential relationship between the mechanism of EA against HFD-induced obesity and the upregulation of SIRT1 expression, we employed Sirtinol, a SIRT1 inhibitor, to decrease the protein expression level of SIRT1 in perirenal adipose tissues of rats, thereby enhancing NF-κB acetylation levels (Fig. 5A and B). The co-administration of Sirtinol effectively counteracted the suppressive impact of EA on weight gain and perirenal adipose tissue accumulation in obese rats (Fig. 5C-E). Importantly, the levels of FBG, HbA1c, and FINS and HOMA-IR index exhibited a significant increase (Fig. 6A-C), whereas insulin sensitivity demonstrated a significant decrease in rats treated with Sirtinol combined with EA compared to the HFD + EA group (Fig. 6D). Additionally, adipose inflammation was found to be more severe (Fig. 6E and F), accompanied by a more pronounced imbalance between Th17 and Treg cells (Fig. 6G). Therefore, these data suggest that the beneficial effects of EA on glycemic control and insulin sensitivity as well as its inhibitory effects on adipose inflammation in HFD-induced obese rats, were partially attenuated by Sirtinol.

Discussion

Obesity is a global epidemic, and obesity-related diseases are becoming a major threat to human health. The role of EA in weight loss has been investigated, and this study specifically examined the immune mechanism through which EA ameliorates inflammation in obesity. The obese rats that were fed a high-fat diet exhibited severe adipose inflammation and an imbalance between Th17 and Treg cells. This study provides the first evidence that electroacupuncture effectively ameliorates adipose inflammation in HFD-induced rats by modulating the balance of Th17 and Treg cells, thereby contributing to weight loss in obese rats.

The regulation of T lymphocytes in adipose tissue establishes a connection between IR and inflammation. The accumulation of dendritic cells in the adipose tissue of obese mice has been observed to induce differentiation of Th17 cells, thereby stimulating the immune response [30]. The findings of Chen et al. also validate an increase in the population of dendritic cells exhibiting an immature phenotype and Th17 cells within adipose tissue. Furthermore, it was experimentally confirmed that dendritic cells promote adipose inflammation by facilitating the generation of Th17 cells [31]. Studies have revealed that Treg cells exhibit a higher concentration in the abdominal adipose of normal mice; however, in IR obese mouse models, there is a reduction in Treg cells within the abdominal adipose. This decline contributes to adipose tissue inflammation observed in obese mice. Furthermore, Treg cells in adipose tissue exert an influence on the synthesis of inflammatory mediators and glucose uptake in adipocytes in vitro [32]. The insulin receptor can be expressed by Treg cells, and elevated levels of insulin can inhibit IL-10 secretion in Treg cells, thereby attenuating their anti-inflammatory capacity and promoting the progression of obesity-related inflammation [33]. In the IR rat model, there was a significant upregulation in the expression levels of Th17 cell markers IL-17 and RORγt, accompanied by a downregulation in the expression levels of Treg cell marker FoxP3 and IL-10, as well as a decrease in the number of CD4+CD25+Treg cells [34]. The findings suggest that IR leads to an imbalance in the Th17/Treg cell ratio, while the dysregulation of inflammatory cytokines secreted by T cells exacerbates adipose tissue inflammation. In the present study, HFD-induced obese rats exhibited an elevation in Th17 cells and a reduction in Treg cells both in peripheral blood and adipose tissue, indicating an imbalance of Th17/Treg cell populations. Furthermore, there was a down-regulation of SIRT1 expression observed in the perirenal adipose tissue of the experimental rat model. The role of SIRT1 in regulating the imbalance of Th17/Treg cells has been extensively investigated, highlighting its potential as an anti- inflammatory factor [35–37]. Our findings provide further evidence that up-regulation of SIRT1 can effectively mitigate insulin resistance, reduce body weight, and restore the equilibrium between Th17/Treg cells in obese rats. The research demonstrates that activation of SIRT1 can enhance the deacetylation of NF-κB p65 protein in the cell, thereby inhibiting the transcriptional activation of NF-κB induced by proinflammatory factors [38]. Additionally, reducing the nuclear translocation of NF-κB p65 leads to a decrease in the expression and secretion of proinflammatory cytokines dependent on NF-κB, thus alleviating hypertrophic adipocyte dysfunction [39]. The NF-κB signaling pathway serves as a pivotal regulator of immune response, exerting significant influence on T lymphocyte differentiation [40]. Consequently, modulating SIRT1 to maintain Th17/Treg cell balance may represent a promising strategy for achieving weight loss.

According to the theory of traditional Chinese medicine, it is believed that “the presence of positive qi prevents the drying up of pathogenic qi” and “when pathogenic qi combines, the overall qi becomes deficient”. This highlights the crucial role of positive qi in both the occurrence and development of diseases from a dual perspective. Therefore, maintaining and protecting positive qi is essential for disease prevention and treatment. “Biao-Ben acupoint matching” is a significant acupuncture point selection method, which involves the combined use of “Biao acupoints” and “Ben acupoints”. The so-called “Biao acupoints” refer to acupuncture points that exert a regulatory influence on the underlying essence or fundamental pathogenesis of a disease, whereas “Ben acupoints” are those that take effect on the surface-level symptoms or acute manifestations of a disease. Therefore, according to traditional Chinese medicine theory, the “Biao-Ben acupoint matching” approach involves selecting “Biao acupoints” based on underlying essence to regulate the meridians and the flow of qi, thereby facilitating the expulsion of malevolent qi. Subsequently, “Ben acupoints” that support both the innate and acquired qi are chosen to activate the body’s righteous qi and enhance its resistance to pathogenic influences. In this study, Guanyuan (CV4) and Zusanli (ST36) were selected as the “Ben acupoints” to enhance the innate kidney qi and improve the function of the acquired spleen and stomach, while Fenglong (ST 40) and Zhongwan (CV12) were chosen as the “Biao acupoints” to eliminate phlegm and dampness. These four acupoints, Guanyuan (CV4), Zusanli (ST36), Zhongwan (CV12), and Fenglong (ST 40) are commonly selected for the treatment of obesity [41–43]. The Zhongwan (CV12) acupoint plays a role in regulating the functions of the spleen and stomach and is implicated in the metabolism of lipids and carbohydrates [44, 45]. The Guanyuan (CV4) acupoint is involved in modulating the functions of the kidneys and small intestine, regulating the hypothalamic-pituitary- adrenal axis, and balancing the secretion of glucocorticoids, which aids in blood sugar regulation [46]. The Zusanli (ST36) acupoint plays a regulatory role in the functions of the spleen and stomach. By modulating the autonomic nervous system, it activates the cholinergic pathway, enhances insulin secretion, and suppresses inflammatory responses, thereby ameliorating IR [47]. The Fenglong (ST40) acupoint regulates the functions of the stomach and large intestine, facilitates the removal of phlegm and dampness, and contributes to the modulation of liver and gallbladder metabolism as well as immune system balance [48, 49]. The four acupoints—Zhongwan, Guanyuan, Zusanli, and Fenglong—act synergistically to enhance the body’s glucose regulation and energy homeostasis through modulation of gastrointestinal functions, the neuro-endocrine-immune network, and energy metabolism pathways [28]. This multi-target regulatory mechanism not only ameliorates insulin resistance but also facilitates glucose utilization and energy metabolism balance by regulating the composition and function of the intestinal microbiota and promoting the production of short-chain fatty acids [41]. Our experimental results further validate the effectiveness of EA at these acupoints in alleviating weight gain and improving IR in rats fed with HFD.

The role of EA as a “promoter” of SIRT1 is acknowledged, leading to the activation of SIRT1 and subsequent deacetylation of acetylated NF-κB. Consequently, EA plays a crucial role in regulating metabolic inflammation in obese rats [26]. Similarly, our previous study employed EA to activate the SIRT1 signaling pathway in the intestines at four specific acupoints (Zhongwan, Guanyuan, Zusanli, and Fenglong) in obese rats, which effectively alleviated intestinal inflammation and consequently improved insulin sensitivity in rats [28]. In this study, the research focus was redirected from intestinal inflammation to perirenal adipose tissue and systemic immune regulation, thereby offering a more comprehensive understanding of the mechanism underlying electroacupuncture in the treatment of obesity. Our findings demonstrate that EA treatment restored the Th17/Treg cell balance in peripheral blood and adipose tissue, while also upregulating SIRT1 expression in adipose tissue and downregulating acetylated NF-κB expression in obese rats. Hence, we postulate that the mechanism underlying EA’s improvement of obese rat symptoms is associated with the anti-inflammatory effect mediated by SIRT1 through regulation of Th17/Treg cell balance. Furthermore, partial reversal of the effects after EA treatment was observed upon introduction of Sirtinol, a SIRT1 inhibitor, providing further support for our hypothesis.

Obesity is characterized by a chronic, low-grade inflammatory state. Research has demonstrated that EA stimulation activates the vagus nerve-adrenal anti-inflammatory axis, thereby exerting anti-inflammatory effects [50]. EA enhances vagal nerve activity and promotes the release of acetylcholine, which suppresses the inflammatory response in obese rats and contributes to weight reduction [47]. Additional study has also indicated that EA can augment sympathetic nerve activity, stimulate thermogenesis in white adipose tissue, and ultimately modulate immune homeostasis in obese rats [51]. Collectively, these findings suggest that EA significantly influences autonomic nervous system activity and plays a regulatory role in obesity-associated inflammatory processes. However, this study has not yet elucidated the specific regions of the autonomic nervous system through which EA modulates Th17/Treg cell balance in obese rats, which is worthy of further in-depth investigation. Moreover, this study has several limitations that warrant consideration. First, the sample size is relatively small; thus, future in-depth investigations should aim to increase the sample size to improve statistical power and enhance the generalizability of the findings. Second, Sirtinol is not a specific inhibitor of SIRT1 and may exert off-target effects. Although the current results support a pivotal role of SIRT1 in electroacupuncture-mediated treatment of obesity, further studies employing more selective inhibitors are needed to confirm this conclusion. It is also important to note that electroacupuncture may lead to mild adverse events, such as pain, bleeding, or subcutaneous hematoma. In rare instances, skin infections may occur due to inadequate needle sterilization or improper procedural techniques. Therefore, strict adherence to standardized operational protocols is essential to minimize potential risks.

Conclusion

In conclusion, EA has a positive impact on obese rats by reducing adipose accumulation, improving IR, and decreasing adipose inflammation. Furthermore, our study reveals a novel finding that EA treatment can modulate the Th17/Treg cell balance in obese rats for the first time. The underlying molecular mechanism involves activation of the SIRT1-mediated anti-inflammatory pathway (Fig. 7). The findings of this study demonstrate the immunological mechanism underlying EA in the treatment of obesity and establish a theoretical foundation for the clinical application of EA in managing obesity.

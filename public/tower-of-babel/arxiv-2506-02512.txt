Extendability of the B 2 B_{2} -arrangement
Torsten Hoge, Shota Maehara, Sven Wiesner

Abstract
. Let ( 𝒜 , m ) ({\mathscr{A}},m) be a free multiarrangement, and let ℰ {\mathscr{E}} be an extension of ( 𝒜 , m ) ({\mathscr{A}},m) . It is well known that if 𝒜 {\mathscr{A}} is the Coxeter arrangement of type A 2 A_{2} , then a free extension of ( 𝒜 , m ) ({\mathscr{A}},m) always exists. In this work, we demonstrate that if 𝒜 {\mathscr{A}} is the Coxeter arrangement of type B 2 B_{2} , there exist infinitely many multiplicities for which no free extension of ( 𝒜 , m ) ({\mathscr{A}},m) exists. This result has immediate consequences for the existence of free extensions in higher rank.

52C35, 32S22, 51F15

1.
Introduction

For a given hyperplane arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
, Saito introduced the module of logarithmic vector fields
D({\mathscr{A}})
in
[
S80
]
. A famous and still open conjecture by Terao asserts that the freeness of
D({\mathscr{A}})
depends solely on the combinatorial structure of
{\mathscr{A}}
. It is known that if
D({\mathscr{A}})
is free, then the degrees of a homogeneous basis of
D({\mathscr{A}})
are uniquely determined, although the generators themselves need not be. These degrees are in one-to-one correspondence with the roots of the characteristic polynomial
\chi({\mathscr{A}};t)
, whose definition depends only on the combinatorial properties of
{\mathscr{A}}
. Ziegler introduced the notion of a multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},m)
in
[
Z86
]
, where
{\mathscr{A}}
is a hyperplane arrangement and
m:{\mathscr{A}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
is a multiplicity function. He generalized the module of logarithmic vector fields from
D({\mathscr{A}})
to
D({\mathscr{A}},m)
, and showed that if
D({\mathscr{A}})
is free, then there exists a canonical multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}}^{H},m^{H})
such that
D({\mathscr{A}}^{H},m^{H})
is a free module. The multiplicity function
m^{H}
is defined in a purely combinatorial manner. Moreover, the degrees of a homogeneous basis of
D({\mathscr{A}})
coincide with the degrees of a homogeneous basis of
D({\mathscr{A}}^{H},m^{H})
. This holds despite the fact that, for an arbitrary multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},m)
, the degrees of a minimal homogeneous set of generators for
D({\mathscr{A}},m)
are not determined by the combinatorial data of
{\mathscr{A}}
. Subsequently, Yoshinaga posed the question in
[
Y10
]
of whether, for every free multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},m)
, there exists a free simple arrangement
{\mathscr{E}}
such that
({\mathscr{E}}^{H},m^{H})=({\mathscr{A}},m)
for some
H\in{\mathscr{E}}
. If such an arrangement
{\mathscr{E}}
exists, then we call
{\mathscr{E}}
a
free extension
of
({\mathscr{A}},m)
. It is known that if
{\mathscr{A}}
is the Coxeter arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}(A_{2})
of type
A_{2}
, and
m
is an arbitrary multiplicity on
{\mathscr{A}}
, then a free extension of
({\mathscr{A}},m)
always exists (
[
Y10
, Thm. 2.5]
and
[
MRW24
, Lem. 5.8]
).
Building on this, one might expect that a free extension exists for every free multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},m)
. Given an arbitrary free multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},m)
and a corresponding free extension
{\mathscr{E}}
, this would provide a means to determine the degrees of a homogeneous set of generators of
D({\mathscr{A}},m)
by analyzing the combinatorial structure of
D({\mathscr{E}})
. In general, determining these degrees for
D({\mathscr{A}},m)
is a highly nontrivial problem; even among Coxeter arrangements of rank two, a complete classification is currently only known for the Coxeter arrangement of type
A_{2}
. In this paper, we focus on the Coxeter arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}(B_{2})
of type
B_{2}
and demonstrate that there exist infinitely many multiplicities
m_{k}
for which no free extension of the corresponding multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}}(B_{2}),m_{k})
exists. The main result of this article is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}(B_{2})
be the Coxeter arrangement of type
B_{2}
with
Q({\mathscr{A}})=xy(x-y)(x+y)
. For
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
define the Coxeter multiarrangement

Q({\mathscr{A}},m_{k})=x^{2}y^{k}(x-y)^{1}(x+y)^{k}.

If
k\geq 4
, then there does not exist a free extension of
({\mathscr{A}},m_{k})
.

As noted in Remark
3.7
, the values of the multiplicity
m_{k}
may be permuted among certain hyperplanes of
{\mathscr{A}}(B_{2})
without affecting the validity of the result. We prove Theorem
1.1
by employing results of Abe and Yoshinaga, along with a classification of the degrees of basis elements for certain multiplicities on
{\mathscr{A}}(B_{2})
. As a straightforward corollary of Theorem
1.1
, we further obtain infinitely many free multiplicities on the Coxeter arrangement of type
B_{n}
(for
n\geq 3
) that admit no free extensions.

Theorem 1.2

(Corollary
6.2
)
.

Let
({\mathscr{A}},m)=({\mathscr{A}}(B_{n}),m)
be the Coxeter multiarrangement of type
B_{n}
. If there exists a
X\in L({\mathscr{A}})
, with
\operatorname{rank}(X)=2
and
({\mathscr{A}}_{X},m_{X})=({\mathscr{A}}(B_{2}),m_{k})
(where
k\geq 4
and
({\mathscr{A}}(B_{2}),m_{k})
as in Theorem
1.1
), then no free extension of
({\mathscr{A}},m)
exists.

In our final result, we present an application of Theorem
1.2
, which yields an infinite family of inductively free multiplicities
n_{k}
on the Coxeter arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}(B_{3})
of type
B_{3}
, such that there does not exist a free extension of any of the pairs
({\mathscr{A}}(B_{3}),n_{k})
.

Theorem 1.3

(Corollary
6.4
)
.

Let

Q({\mathscr{A}}(B_{3}),n_{k})=x^{2}y^{k}(x-y)^{1}(x+y)^{k}z^{e}(x-z)^{f}(x+z)^{g}(y-z)^{h}(y+z)^{i},(k\geq 4),

be the Coxeter multiarrangement of type
B_{3}
. Fix the supersolvable filtration

\{\ker(x)\}\subset\{\ker(x),\ker(y),\ker(x-y),\ker(x+y)\}\subset{\mathscr{A}}(B_{3}).

Then
({\mathscr{A}}(B_{3}),n_{k})
satisfies the conditions of Theorem
6.3
if and only if
e=f=g=h=i=1
. Moreover, each
({\mathscr{A}}(B_{3}),n_{k})
is inductively free with exponents
\exp({\mathscr{A}}(B_{3}),n_{k})=(5,k+1,k+2)
. However, no free extension of
({\mathscr{A}}(B_{3}),n_{k})
exists.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we recall some definitions and elementary results. For this, we closely follow
[
OT92
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
{\mathbb{K}}
be a field, and let
V
be a vector space of dimension
\ell
over
{\mathbb{K}}
. A
hyperplane

H
in
V
is an affine subspace of dimension
\ell-1
. A
hyperplane arrangement
(or simply an
arrangement
) is a pair
{\mathscr{A}}=({\mathscr{A}},V)
, where
{\mathscr{A}}
is a finite set of hyperplanes in
V
.

We refer to
{\mathscr{A}}
as an
\ell
-arrangement to indicate that
\dim V=\ell
.

Definition 2.2
.

1. We call a hyperplane arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}

central
if
\cap_{H\in{\mathscr{A}}}H\neq\emptyset
. Therefore if
{\mathscr{A}}
is central, we will assume that each hyperplane contains the origin and is thus a subspace.

2. Let
V^{*}
be the dual space of
V
and
S=S(V^{*})
be the symmetric algebra of
V^{*}
. If
x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}
is a basis of
V^{*}
, then we identify
S
with the polynomial algebra
S=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}]
.

All arrangements investigated in this paper are central.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be a hyperplane arrangement. Each hyperplane
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
is the kernel of a polynomial
\alpha_{H}
of degree 1, defined up to a constant. A function
m:{\mathscr{A}}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
is called a
multiplicity
on
{\mathscr{A}}
and the pair
({\mathscr{A}},m)
is called a
multiarrangement
. We define the
cardinality

|m|
of
({\mathscr{A}},m)
as
|m|:=\sum_{H\in{\mathscr{A}}}m(H)
. The product

Q({\mathscr{A}},m)=\prod_{H\in{\mathscr{A}}}\alpha_{H}^{m(H)}

is called the
defining polynomial of
({\mathscr{A}},m)
. If
m\equiv 1
, we call
({\mathscr{A}},m)
a
simple arrangement
and denote it by just
{\mathscr{A}}
. If
{\mathscr{A}}
is central, then
\alpha_{H}
is a linear form for all
H
and
Q({\mathscr{A}})
is a homogeneous polynomial.

We now introduce the
lattice of intersections

L({\mathscr{A}})
of an arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
, which will play a fundamental role in the proof of our main result.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
L({\mathscr{A}})
be the set of all possible intersections of hyperplanes of
{\mathscr{A}}
:

L({\mathscr{A}})=\big\{\textstyle\bigcap_{H\in{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}}H\penalty\ |\penalty\ {\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathscr{A}}\big\}.

We define
\operatorname{rank}(X)=\operatorname{codim}(X),\operatorname{rank}({\mathscr{A}})=\operatorname{rank}(\cap_{H\in{\mathscr{A}}}H)
, and

L_{k}({\mathscr{A}}):=\{X\in L({\mathscr{A}})\penalty\ |\penalty\ \operatorname{rank}(X)=k\}.

By defining an ordering on
L({\mathscr{A}})
by reverse inclusion (
X\leq Y\Leftrightarrow Y\subseteq X
) the poset
L({\mathscr{A}})
becomes a geometric lattice
[
OT92
, Lem. 2.3]
which is isomorphic to the lattice of flats of its underlying matroid.

We can associate a certain polynomial
\chi({\mathscr{A}};t)
to
{\mathscr{A}}
, that only depends on its lattice of intersections
L({\mathscr{A}})
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be an arrangement with intersection poset
L({\mathscr{A}})
and Möbius function
\mu

[
OT92
, Def. 2.42]
. Define the
characteristic polynomial of
{\mathscr{A}}
as

\chi({\mathscr{A}};t)=\sum_{X\in L({\mathscr{A}})}\mu(X)t^{\dim X}.

The following operations on
L({\mathscr{A}})
are essential.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be a hyperplane arrangement. For
X\in L({\mathscr{A}})
define the

(1)

localization

{\mathscr{A}}_{X}
of
{\mathscr{A}}
at
X
by

{\mathscr{A}}_{X}=\{H\in{\mathscr{A}}\penalty\ |\penalty\ X\subseteq H\},\text{ and}

(2)

restriction

{\mathscr{A}}^{X}
of
{\mathscr{A}}
to
X
by

{\mathscr{A}}^{X}=\{X\cap H\penalty\ |\penalty\ H\in{\mathscr{A}}\backslash{\mathscr{A}}_{X}\penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ X\cap H\not=\emptyset\}

where
{\mathscr{A}}^{X}=({\mathscr{A}}^{X},X)
is an arrangement in
X
.

The module of logarithmic vector fields for a simple arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
was originally introduced by Saito in
[
S80
]
. The following definition is its generalization to multiarrangements introduced by Ziegler in
[
Z86
]
.

Definition 2.7
.

1. A
\mathbb{K}
-linear map
\theta:S\rightarrow S
is a
derivation
if for
f,g\in S
:

\theta(f\cdot g)=f\cdot\theta(g)+g\cdot\theta(f)

Let
\emph{Der}_{\mathbb{K}}(S)
be the
S
-module of derivations of
S
graded by polynomial degree
[
OT92
, Def. 4.1]
.

2. Define the
S
-submodule
D({\mathscr{A}},m)
of
\text{Der}_{\mathbb{K}}(S)
, called the
module of logarithmic vector fields along
({\mathscr{A}},m)
(or the module of
({\mathscr{A}},m)
-derivations), by

D({\mathscr{A}},m)=\{\theta\in\text{Der}_{\mathbb{K}}(S)\penalty\ |\penalty\ \theta(\alpha_{H})\in\alpha_{H}^{m(H)}\cdot S\penalty\ \text{for all}\penalty\ H\in{\mathscr{A}}\}.

The multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},m)
is called
free
if
D({\mathscr{A}},m)
is a free
S
-module.

3. For
X\in L({\mathscr{A}})
define the
localization

({\mathscr{A}}_{X},m_{X})
of
({\mathscr{A}},m)
at
X
, where
m_{X}=m|_{{\mathscr{A}}_{X}}
.

One of the most interesting questions about the freeness of an arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
is to what extent the freeness of
{\mathscr{A}}
and the combinatorial structure of
{\mathscr{A}}
are related.

Remark 2.8
.

1. If
D({\mathscr{A}},m)
is a free
S
-module, then there always exists a homogeneous basis
\{\theta_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq\ell\}
of
D({\mathscr{A}},m)
and we call the multiset
\exp({\mathscr{A}},m)=\{\deg(\theta_{i})\mid 1\leq i\leq\ell\}
the exponents of
({\mathscr{A}},m)
. It is shown by Ziegler in
[
Z86
, Cor. 7]
that if
\operatorname{rank}({\mathscr{A}})=2
, then
({\mathscr{A}},m)
is always free.

2. If
{\mathscr{A}}
is a simple free
\ell
-arrangement with
{\mathscr{A}}\neq\emptyset
, then
1\in\exp({\mathscr{A}})

[
OT92
, Prop. 4.27]
and if
\exp({\mathscr{A}})=(1,d_{2},\dots,d_{\ell})
, then
\chi({\mathscr{A}};t)=(t-1)(t-d_{2})\cdots(t-d_{\ell})
, as shown by Terao in
[
T81
]
. So
\exp({\mathscr{A}})
is determined by
L({\mathscr{A}})
. If
m\not\equiv 1
, then
\exp({\mathscr{A}},m)
is not determined by
L({\mathscr{A}})

[
Z86
, Prop. 10]
.

Starting at a simple arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
and fixing one hyperplane
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
, Ziegler defined the following multiplicity
m^{H}
on
{\mathscr{A}}^{H}
, which only depends on the intersection lattice
L({\mathscr{A}})
.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be an arrangement and
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
. Define a multiplicity
m^{H}
on
{\mathscr{A}}^{H}
by

m^{H}(X)=|{\mathscr{A}}_{X}|-1,\quad X\in{\mathscr{A}}^{H}

and call the pair
({\mathscr{A}}^{H},m^{H})
the
Ziegler restriction
of
{\mathscr{A}}
to
H
.

Ziegler showed the following connection between the freeness of
{\mathscr{A}}
and the freeness of any of its Ziegler restrictions.

Theorem 2.10

(
[
Z86
, Thm. 11]
)
.

Suppose
{\mathscr{A}}
is free with exponents
(1,d_{2},\dots,d_{\ell})
. Let
H
be a hyperplane in
{\mathscr{A}}
, then
({\mathscr{A}}^{H},m^{H})
is free with exponents
(d_{2},\dots,d_{\ell})
.

Yoshinaga started in
[
Y10
]
to investigate the question of
extendability
, that is, which free multiarrangements
({\mathscr{A}},m)
can be obtained from a free simple arrangement
{\mathscr{E}}
by using Theorem
2.10
.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
({\mathscr{A}},m)
be a free
\ell
-multiarrangement. We call a simple
(\ell+1)
-arrangement
{\mathscr{E}}
an
extension of
({\mathscr{A}},m)
if there exists a hyperplane
H\in{\mathscr{E}}
such that
({\mathscr{E}}^{H},m^{H})=({\mathscr{A}},m)
. If
{\mathscr{E}}
is a free arrangement, then we call it a
free extension of
({\mathscr{A}},m)
.

In
[
Y10
, Ex. 1.4]
, Yoshinaga provides an example of a free rank two multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},m)
that does not admit a free extension. Note that in Yoshinaga’s example, we have
|m|=9
. Consequently, Theorem
1.1
constitutes the first result establishing the existence of infinitely many free multiplicities on a fixed free arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
that do not admit any free extensions.

Furthermore, Yoshinaga showed in
[
Y05
]
that if
({\mathscr{A}}^{H},m^{H})
is a
2
-arrangement and the multiset of exponents
\exp({\mathscr{A}}^{H},m^{H})
is known, then the freeness of
{\mathscr{A}}
- and hence the question of whether
{\mathscr{A}}
is a free extension of
({\mathscr{A}}^{H},m^{H})
- depends solely on
L({\mathscr{A}})
.

Theorem 2.12

(
[
Y05
, Cor. 3.3]
)
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be a
3
-arrangement. Set

\chi({\mathscr{A}};t)=(t-1)(t^{2}-b_{1}t+b_{2})=t^{3}-(b_{1}+1)t^{2}+(b_{1}+b_{2})t-b_{2}

and
\exp({\mathscr{A}}^{H},m^{H})=(d_{1},d_{2})
. Then

(1)

b_{2}\geq d_{1}d_{2}
.

(2)

If
b_{2}=d_{1}d_{2}
, then
{\mathscr{A}}
is free with exponents
(1,d_{1},d_{2})
.

It should be noted that our version of Theorem
2.12
differs slightly from the original statement in
[
Y05
]
and can be found in (
[
Y14
, Thm. 1.39]
). Theorem
2.12
plays a fundamental role in the construction of free extensions of multiarrangements.

Remark 2.13
.

With notation as in Theorem
2.12
let
{\mathscr{A}}
be a central rank three arrangement and
\chi({\mathscr{A}};t)=(t-1)(t^{2}-b_{1}t+b_{2})=t^{3}-(b_{1}+1)t^{2}+(b_{1}+b_{2})t-b_{2}
. Let
X\in L_{2}({\mathscr{A}})
, then
\mu(X)=|{\mathscr{A}}_{X}|-1
. By definition of
\chi({\mathscr{A}};t)
, we have

b_{1}+b_{2}=\sum_{X\in L_{2}({\mathscr{A}})}\mu(X)=\sum_{X\in L_{2}({\mathscr{A}})}(|{\mathscr{A}}_{X}|-1).

By definition of
\chi({\mathscr{A}};t)
, the leading coefficient of
\chi({\mathscr{A}};t)
is always equal to
1
, the coefficient of
t^{2}
equals
-|{\mathscr{A}}|
and the constant term is given by the negative sum of the remaining coefficients of
\chi({\mathscr{A}};t)
. Consequently, the only coefficient that can get altered is the one in front of
t
, which can be achieved by moving hyperplanes and thereby changing
L({\mathscr{A}})
in the process. If
{\mathscr{A}}
is free, then by Theorem
2.12
, the constant term of
\chi({\mathscr{A}};t)
is maximized. In order to achieve this maximum, the coefficient of
t
must be minimized. By definition of
\chi({\mathscr{A}};t)
this coefficient is given by
\sum_{(X\in L_{2}({\mathscr{A}}))}(|{\mathscr{A}}_{X}|-1)
.

Define
\mathcal{P}_{i}:=|\{X\in L_{2}({\mathscr{A}})\mid|{\mathscr{A}}_{X}|=i\}|
, then we have

\mathcal{P}_{2}=\binom{|{\mathscr{A}}|}{2}-\sum_{i>2}\binom{i}{2}\cdot\mathcal{P}_{i}.

Also

\sum_{X\in L_{2}({\mathscr{A}})}(|{\mathscr{A}}_{X}|-1)=\sum_{i\geq 2}(i-1)\cdot\mathcal{P}_{i}.

Suppose we move a hyperplane in such a way that
\mathcal{P}_{3}
increases by one, then
\mathcal{P}_{2}
decreases by
\binom{3}{2}=3
. Since in this process, three distinct rank two flats, each containing two elements, are merged into a single rank two flat containing three elements. Therefore, instead of contributing 1 three times (once for each flat with two hyperplanes), the new configuration contributes 2 once (for the flat with three hyperplanes), shrinking the coefficient of
t
by
1
. Consequently, if
({\mathscr{A}}(A_{2}),m)
is the Coxeter multiarrangement of type
A_{2}
, then constructing a free extension
{\mathscr{A}}
of
({\mathscr{A}}(A_{2}),m)
is equivalent to maximizing
\mathcal{P}_{3}
for
{\mathscr{A}}
.

Given an arbitrary multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},m)
, Yoshinaga also defined a canonical extension
{\mathscr{E}}({\mathscr{A}},m)
of
({\mathscr{A}},m)
; however, this extension is not free in general.

Definition 2.14
.

Let
({\mathscr{A}},m)
be a
\ell
-arrangement. Define the
Yoshinaga-Extension
in
{\mathbb{K}}^{\ell+1}

with
(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{\ell},z)
as a coordinate system by

{\mathscr{E}}({\mathscr{A}},m)=\{\ker(z)\}\cup\left\{\ker(\alpha_{H}-kz)\ \middle|\ k\in\mathbb{Z},-\frac{m(H)-1}{2}\leq k\leq\frac{m(H)}{2}\right\}.

Note that for
H=\ker(z)
we have
({\mathscr{E}}({\mathscr{A}},m)^{H},m^{H})=({\mathscr{A}},m)
.

3.
Exponents for the Coxeter multiarrangement of type
B_{2}

For a general multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},m)
, determining wheter it is free and, if so, identifying its exponents is, in general, a difficult problem. Even in the case where
{\mathscr{A}}
is a Coxeter arrangement of rank two, a complete classification of the exponents
\exp({\mathscr{A}},m)
for arbitrary multiplicities
m
is currently available exclusively for the Coxeter arrangement of type
A_{2}
, as established by Wakamiko in
[
W07
]
. In this section, we present the latest results concerning the classification of
\exp({\mathscr{A}}(B_{2}),m)
, where
{\mathscr{A}}(B_{2})
denotes the Coxeter arrangement of type
B_{2}
equipped with an arbitrary multiplicity
m
. Understanding the exponents of
({\mathscr{A}}(B_{2}),m)
is essential for applying Theorem
2.12
.

Definition 3.1
.

Fixing a multiplicity
m=(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},m_{4})
, the
Coxeter multiarrangement
{\mathscr{A}}(B_{2})
of type
B_{2}
is defined by

Q({\mathscr{A}}(B_{2}),m)=x^{m_{1}}y^{m_{2}}(x-y)^{m_{3}}(x+y)^{m_{4}}.

We will denote the underlying simple arrangement by
{\mathscr{A}}(B_{2})
for the rest of this article.

Definition 3.2
.

The multiplicity
m=(m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n})
on an arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
of
n
hyperplanes is called
balanced
if
m
satisfies
m_{i}<\frac{|m|}{2}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
, where
|m|:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}
. Otherwise call
m

unbalanced
.

Remark 3.3
.

Wakefield and Yuzvinsky
[
WY07
]
determined the exponents of multiarrangements
({\mathscr{A}},m)
with unbalanced multiplicities in the case
\operatorname{rank}({\mathscr{A}})=2
. They showed that if
m_{\max}=\max\{m(H)\mid H\in{\mathscr{A}}\}
satisfies
m_{\max}\geq\frac{|m|}{2}
, then the exponents are given by
\exp({\mathscr{A}},m)=(|m|-m_{\max},m_{\max})
.
Consequently, in order to complete the classification of
\exp({\mathscr{A}}(B_{2}),m)
, it remains to consider the case where
m
is a balanced multiplicity.

The following theorem is instrumental in constraining the possible values of
\exp({\mathscr{A}},m)
when analyzing the exponents of a central
2
-arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
with a balanced multiplicity
m
.

Theorem 3.4

(
[
A13
, Thm. 1.6]
)
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be a central
2
-arrangement over a field of characteristic zero with
|{\mathscr{A}}|>2
. If
m:{\mathscr{A}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
is balanced and
\exp({\mathscr{A}},m)=(d_{1},d_{2})
, then
\Delta(m):=|d_{1}-d_{2}|\leq|{\mathscr{A}}|-2
.

Remark 3.5
.

(1)

With notation as in Theorem
3.4
the multiplicity
m
is called a
peak point
of
{\mathscr{A}}
if
|d_{1}-d_{2}|=|{\mathscr{A}}|-2
. Additional information on the combinatorial importance of peak points can be found in
[
AN12
]
.

(2)

Recall that the Coxeter multiarrangement
{\mathscr{A}}(A_{2})
of type
A_{2}
is defined by

Q({\mathscr{A}}(A_{2}),m)=x^{m_{1}}y^{m_{2}}(x-y)^{m_{3}}.

Therefore
|{\mathscr{A}}|=3
and if
m
is balanced, then Theorem
3.4
shows that

\exp({\mathscr{A}}(A_{2}),m)=(\frac{|m|}{2},\frac{|m|}{2})\text{ if }|m|\text{ is even or }\exp({\mathscr{A}}(A_{2}),m)=(\lfloor\frac{|m|}{2}\rfloor,\lceil\frac{|m|}{2}\rceil)\text{ if }|m|\text{ is odd.}

By combining this with Remark
3.3
in the case where
m
is unbalanced, we recover the full classification of
\exp({\mathscr{A}}(A_{2}),m)
for arbitrary multiplicities
m
, originally established by Wakamiko in
[
W07
]
.

Let
m
be a multiplicity on
{\mathscr{A}}(B_{2})
. Theorem
3.4
enables us to constrain the possible values of
\exp({\mathscr{A}}(B_{2}),m)
as follows.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
m
be a balanced multiplicity on
{\mathscr{A}}(B_{2})
. Then we have

(1)

|m|
is odd if and only if
\Delta({\mathscr{A}}(B_{2}),m)=1
.

(2)

|m|
is even if and only if
\Delta({\mathscr{A}}(B_{2}),m)\in\{0,2\}
.

It is sufficient to classify
\exp({\mathscr{A}}(B_{2}),m)
up to some permutations.

Remark 3.7
.

For a multiplicity
m=(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},m_{4})
on
{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}(B_{2})
,
one can show that the multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},m)
is isomorphic to the multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},m_{perm})
, where

m_{perm}\in\{(m_{1},m_{2},m_{4},m_{3}),(m_{2},m_{1},m_{3},m_{4}),(m_{3},m_{4},m_{1},m_{2})\}.

In particular, this implies that
\exp({\mathscr{A}},m)=\exp({\mathscr{A}},m_{perm})
. Therefore, when classifying the exponents of
({\mathscr{A}}(B_{2}),m)
for an arbitrary multiplicity
m
, it is sufficient to assume, without loss of generality, that
m_{2}-m_{1}\geq m_{4}-m_{3}\geq 0
.

The following corollary can be established using results by Maehara and Numata
[
MN25
]
, as well as Feigin, Wang and Yoshinaga
[
FWY25
]
. Since its proof is independent of the main development of this article, we defer it to the
Appendix
, where the relevant results are also reviewed.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
({\mathscr{A}},m)=({\mathscr{A}}(B_{2}),(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},m_{4}))
with
m
balanced. Let
\min\{m_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq 4\}=m_{4}=1
. Then
m
is a peak point if and only if
m_{1}=m_{2}
,
m_{3}\in 2{\mathbb{Z}}+1
and
|m|\in 4{\mathbb{Z}}
.

We illustrate the implications of this corollary in the following example.

Example 3.9
.

Consider the multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}}(B_{2}),m_{k})=({\mathscr{A}}(B_{2}),(2,k,1,k))
and let
k=3
. Fix an arbitrary hyperplane
H\in{\mathscr{A}}(B_{2})\backslash\{\ker(x-y)\}
. Applying Corollary
3.8
, we obtain
\exp({\mathscr{A}}(B_{2}),(2,2,1,3))=(3,5)
, whereas
\exp({\mathscr{A}}(B_{2}),(1,3,1,3))=\exp({\mathscr{A}}(B_{2}),(2,3,1,2))=(4,4)
.
Now consider the case
k\geq 4
. In this situation, reducing the multiplicity of any hyperplane
H\in{\mathscr{A}}(B_{2})\backslash\{\ker(x-y)\}
by
1
will not yield a peak point for
{\mathscr{A}}(B_{2})
, since the condition
m_{1}=m_{2}
from Corollary
3.8
is not satisfied.

4.
Reformulating Yoshinaga’s Theorem

In this section, we present the central tool utilized in the proof of Theorem
1.1
and illustrate its application through several examples. For a multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},m)
, the concepts of locally and globally mixed products were introduced by Abe, Terao, and Wakefield in
[
ATW07
]
. We adapt these definitions to suit the context of our work as follows.

Definition 4.1
.

1. For any
X\in L_{2}({\mathscr{A}})
, the localization
({\mathscr{A}}_{X},m_{X})
is free with exponents
\exp({\mathscr{A}}_{X},m_{X})=(d_{1}^{X},d_{2}^{X})
. The
locally mixed product
of
({\mathscr{A}},m)
is then defined by

\LMP({\mathscr{A}},m)=\sum_{X\in L_{2}({\mathscr{A}})}d_{1}^{X}d_{2}^{X}.

2. If
({\mathscr{A}},m)
is free with exponents
\exp({\mathscr{A}},m)=(d_{1},\dots,d_{\ell})
, the
globally mixed product
is defined by

\GMP({\mathscr{A}},m)=\sum d_{i_{1}}d_{i_{2}},

where the sum is taken over all unordered pairs
\{d_{i_{1}},d_{i_{2}}\}\subset\exp({\mathscr{A}},m)
.

If
m\equiv 1
(i.e.
{\mathscr{A}}
is a simple arrangement), we abbreviate by writing
\LMP({\mathscr{A}})
and
\GMP({\mathscr{A}})
instead of
\LMP({\mathscr{A}},m)
and
\GMP({\mathscr{A}},m)
, respectively.

Since
\GMP({\mathscr{A}},m)
is defined only for free arrangements, we introduce the following generalization.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be a simple arrangement of rank three and let
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
. Then the Ziegler restriction
({\mathscr{A}}^{H},m^{H})
is free. If
\exp({\mathscr{A}}^{H},m^{H})=(d_{1},d_{2})
, we define the
virtually globally mixed product
of
{\mathscr{A}}
with respect to
H
by

\VGMP({\mathscr{A}},H)=d_{1}+d_{2}+d_{1}d_{2}.

Note that if
{\mathscr{A}}
is free, then by Theorem
2.10
and Theorem
2.12
we have
\GMP({\mathscr{A}})=\VGMP({\mathscr{A}},H)
for all
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
.

Using this terminology, we may reformulate Theorem
2.12
as follows.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be a central
3
-arrangement, and let
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
. Then

\LMP({\mathscr{A}})\geq\VGMP({\mathscr{A}},H).

In particular,
{\mathscr{A}}
is free if and only if
\LMP({\mathscr{A}})=\VGMP({\mathscr{A}},H)
for all
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
.

Proof.

Let
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
and suppose
\exp({\mathscr{A}}^{H},m^{H})=(d_{1},d_{2})
. Since the localization
{\mathscr{A}}_{X}
is free for all
X\in L_{2}({\mathscr{A}})
, the locally mixed product
\LMP({\mathscr{A}})
is always defined. As
{\mathscr{A}}
is a simple arrangement, it holds that if
|{\mathscr{A}}_{X}|=d
, then
\exp({\mathscr{A}}_{X})=(1,d-1)
. Therefore,

\LMP({\mathscr{A}})=\sum_{X\in L_{2}({\mathscr{A}})}(|{\mathscr{A}}_{X}|-1)=\sum_{X\in L_{2}({\mathscr{A}})}\mu(X).

This sum corresponds to the coefficient of
t
in
\chi({\mathscr{A}};t)
. We now adapt the notation of
\chi({\mathscr{A}};t)
as in Theorem
2.12
, and compare coefficients. From the definition of
\chi({\mathscr{A}};t)
and the fact that
{\mathscr{A}}
is a simple arrangement with
\operatorname{rank}({\mathscr{A}})=3
, the coefficient of
t^{2}
is
b_{1}=|{\mathscr{A}}|-1
. On the other hand Theorem
2.12
implies that

|{\mathscr{A}}|=1+d_{1}+d_{2}\implies b_{1}=d_{1}+d_{2}.

Moreover, since
b_{1}+b_{2}=\LMP({\mathscr{A}})
, and Theorem
2.12
gives the inequality
b_{2}\geq d_{1}d_{2}
, we obtain

\LMP({\mathscr{A}})=b_{1}+b_{2}\geq d_{1}+d_{2}+d_{1}d_{2}=\VGMP({\mathscr{A}},H).

Equality holds if and only if
b_{2}=d_{1}d_{2}
, which, by Theorem
2.12
, is equivalent to the freeness of
{\mathscr{A}}
.
∎

The proof of Lemma
4.3
gives rise to the following Corollary.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be a central
3
-arrangement. Then its characteristic polynomial is given by

\chi({\mathscr{A}};t)=t^{3}-|{\mathscr{A}}|t^{2}+\LMP({\mathscr{A}})t-b_{2}.

We now recall the definition of the
deletion

({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},m^{\prime})
of a multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},m)
.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
({\mathscr{A}},m)
and let
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
. The
deletion of
H
is defined as the pair
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},m^{\prime})
, where:

•

if
m(H)>1
, define
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}:={\mathscr{A}}
and
m^{\prime}(H):=m(H)-1,m^{\prime}(H^{\prime}):=m(H^{\prime})
for all
H^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{A}}\backslash\{H\}
.

•

if
m(H)=1
, define
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}:={\mathscr{A}}\backslash\{H\}
and
m^{\prime}(H^{\prime}):=m(H^{\prime})
for all
H^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{A}}\backslash\{H\}
.

The following observations simplify the proof of Corollary
4.7
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
({\mathscr{A}},m)
be a multiarrangement with balanced multiplicity
m
, and suppose that
\exp({\mathscr{A}},m)=(e_{1},e_{2})
. Assume there exists a hyperplane
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
such that the deletion
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},m^{\prime})
of
H_{0}
yields an unbalanced multiplicity
m^{\prime}
. Then the following statements hold:

(1)

|m|
is odd.

(2)

If
({\mathscr{A}},m)=({\mathscr{A}}(B_{2}),m)
, then
\exp({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},m^{\prime})=(\frac{|m|-1}{2},\frac{|m|-1}{2})
.

Proof.

First let
({\mathscr{A}},m)
with
m=(m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{k})
and without loss of generality assume
m_{1}=1+\sum_{2\leq i\leq k}m_{i}
. Then
|m|=\sum_{1\leq i\leq k}m_{i}=1+2\cdot(\sum_{2\leq i\leq k}m_{i})
which is an odd number.

Now let
({\mathscr{A}},m)=({\mathscr{A}}(B_{2}),m)
such that
m
is a balanced multiplicity and there exists a hyperplane
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
such that for the deletion
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},m^{\prime})
of
H_{0}
the multiplicity
m^{\prime}
is unbalanced. Let
m^{\prime}_{\max}:=\max\{m^{\prime}(H)\mid H\in{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}\}
, then thanks to Remark
3.3

m^{\prime}_{\max}\in\exp({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},m^{\prime})
.

Applying Lemma
4.6
(1) shows that
|m|
is odd and since
m
is balanced use Corollary
3.6
to derive
\exp({\mathscr{A}},m)=(\frac{|m|-1}{2},\frac{|m|+1}{2})
. Since
m^{\prime}_{\max}\in\exp({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},m^{\prime})
we require
\exp({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},m^{\prime})=(\frac{|m|-1}{2},\frac{|m|-1}{2})
, otherwise we get a contradiction to the assumptions on
m
or
m^{\prime}
as follows. If
\exp({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},m^{\prime})=(\frac{|m|-3}{2},\frac{|m|+1}{2})
and
m^{\prime}_{\max}=\frac{|m|-3}{2}<\frac{|m|-1}{2}=\frac{|m^{\prime}|}{2}
, then
m^{\prime}
is not unbalanced by definition. If
m^{\prime}_{\max}=\frac{|m|+1}{2}
, then
m
was already unbalanced.
∎

Given a free multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},m)
with
\exp({\mathscr{A}},m)=(d_{1},d_{2})
, we extend the notation
\Delta(m):=|d_{1}-d_{2}|
, originally introduced in Theorem
3.4
for balanced multiplicities, to also include the case of non balanced multiplicities. We derive the following tool from Lemma
4.3
and Lemma
4.6
.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
{\mathscr{E}}
be a free extension of
({\mathscr{A}},m)=({\mathscr{A}}(B_{2}),m)
for a balanced multiplicity
m
such that
({\mathscr{E}}^{H_{0}},m^{H_{0}})=({\mathscr{A}},m)
. Fix
H\in{\mathscr{E}}\setminus{H_{0}}
and let
\exp({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},m^{\prime})
be the deletion of
(H\cap H_{0})
from
({\mathscr{A}},m)
and
{\mathscr{E}}^{\prime}={\mathscr{E}}\backslash\{H\}
. Then we have

(1)

If
|m|
is odd, then

(a)

|{\mathscr{E}}^{H}|\leq\frac{r+3}{2}
(if
\Delta(m^{\prime})=2
) or

(b)

|{\mathscr{E}}^{H}|\leq\frac{r+1}{2}
(if
\Delta(m^{\prime})=0
).

(2)

If
|m|
is even, then

(a)

|{\mathscr{E}}^{H}|\leq\frac{r}{2}
(if
\Delta(m)=2
) or

(b)

|{\mathscr{E}}^{H}|\leq\frac{r+2}{2}
(if
\Delta(m)=0
).

In particular, the deletion
{\mathscr{E}}^{\prime}
of
H
is free if and only if equality holds.

Proof.

Assume first that
m^{\prime}
is unbalanced. Then, by Lemma
4.6
,
|m|
is odd and
\Delta(m^{\prime})=0
. Thus, this case falls under
(1)(b)
of Corollary
4.7
. All statements concerning exponents are based on Remark
3.3
and Corollary
3.6
.

Let
({\mathscr{E}}^{H_{0}},m^{H_{0}})=({\mathscr{A}},m)
and suppose first that
r:=|m|
is odd. Since
{\mathscr{E}}
is free, we have

\exp({\mathscr{E}})=\left(1,\frac{r-1}{2},\frac{r+1}{2}\right),\text{ and by Lemma \ref{Lemma: LMP>=GMP for extensions}, }\LMP({\mathscr{E}})=\frac{r^{2}-1}{4}+r.

Now fix
H\in{\mathscr{E}}\backslash\{H_{0}\}
, and note that
\exp({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},m^{\prime})\in\{(\frac{r-1}{2},\frac{r-1}{2}),(\frac{r-3}{2},\frac{r+1}{2})\},
depending on whether
\Delta(m^{\prime})=0
or
\Delta(m^{\prime})=2
. By Lemma
4.3
, we obtain:

\LMP({\mathscr{E}}^{\prime})\geq\VGMP({\mathscr{E}}^{\prime},H_{0})\in\left\{\frac{r^{2}-3}{4}+\frac{r}{2},\frac{r^{2}-7}{4}+\frac{r}{2}\right\}.

Since
{\mathscr{E}}
is central, we have

\LMP({\mathscr{E}})=\sum_{X\in L_{2}({\mathscr{E}})}(|{\mathscr{E}}_{X}|-1)

from which it follows that

\LMP({\mathscr{E}}^{\prime})=\LMP({\mathscr{E}})-|{\mathscr{E}}^{H}|.

Substituting into the previous inequality yields:

\frac{r^{2}-1}{4}+r-|{\mathscr{E}}^{H}|=\LMP({\mathscr{E}}^{\prime})\geq\VGMP({\mathscr{E}}^{\prime},H_{0})=\frac{r^{2}-3}{4}+\frac{r}{2},

or respectively,

\frac{r^{2}-1}{4}+r-|{\mathscr{E}}^{H}|=\LMP({\mathscr{E}}^{\prime})\geq\VGMP({\mathscr{E}}^{\prime},H_{0})=\frac{r^{2}-7}{4}+\frac{r}{2}.

This implies:

|{\mathscr{E}}^{H}|\leq\frac{r+1}{2}\text{ or (if }\Delta(m^{\prime})=2)\mbox{ }|{\mathscr{E}}^{H}|\leq\frac{r+3}{2}.

with equality being equivalent to freeness for
{\mathscr{E}}^{\prime}
, again thanks to Lemma
4.3
.

Now suppose that
r
is even. Then

\exp({\mathscr{A}}(B_{2}),m)\in\left\{(\frac{r}{2},\frac{r}{2}),(\frac{r}{2}-1,\frac{r}{2}+1)\right\},

and by Lemma
4.3
, it follows that

\LMP({\mathscr{E}})\in\left\{\frac{r^{2}}{4}+r,\frac{r^{2}}{4}+r-1\right\}.

Now delete
(H_{0}\cap H)
from
({\mathscr{A}}(B_{2}),m)
. Since the resulting multiplicity
m^{\prime}
remains balanced, we obtain

\exp({\mathscr{A}}(B_{2})^{\prime},m^{\prime})=\left(\frac{r-2}{2},\frac{r}{2}\right)\text{ and }\VGMP({\mathscr{E}}^{\prime},H_{0})=\frac{r^{2}}{4}+\frac{r}{2}-1.

We now consider the two possible cases for
\LMP({\mathscr{E}})
:

\frac{r^{2}}{4}+r-|{\mathscr{E}}^{H}|=\LMP({\mathscr{E}}^{\prime})\geq\VGMP({\mathscr{E}}^{\prime},H_{0})=\frac{r^{2}}{4}+\frac{r}{2}-1

or

\frac{r^{2}}{4}+r-1-|{\mathscr{E}}^{H}|=\LMP({\mathscr{E}}^{\prime})\geq\VGMP({\mathscr{E}}^{\prime},H_{0})=\frac{r^{2}}{4}+\frac{r}{2}-1.

In the first case (i.e. when
\Delta(m)=0
), this implies
|{\mathscr{E}}^{H}|\leq\frac{r+2}{2},
and in the second case (i.e. when
\Delta(m)=2
),
|{\mathscr{E}}^{H}|\leq\frac{r}{2}.
Moreover, by Lemma
4.3
, equality holds if and only if
{\mathscr{E}}^{\prime}
is free.
∎

Lemma 5.1

Let

Proof.

Let
{\mathscr{E}}

Remark 6.5
.

It is well known that for every Coxeter multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}}(A_{2}),m)
of type
A_{2}
, a free extension always exists. In
[
Y10
]
, Yoshinaga provided a canonical free extension of a multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},m)
of rank greater than two, under the condition that
{\mathscr{A}}
is locally
A_{2}
, along with other constraints on both
{\mathscr{A}}
and
m
. This result raises the natural expectation that similar canonical free extensions might be constructed for other rank two arrangements.

However, our main result indicates that this is not generally the case: even when moving from type
A_{2}
to type
B_{2}
, the construction of free extensions may fail in many instances. As demonstrated in Corollary
6.2
, this obstruction extends to arrangements of higher rank, where the existence of a single non-extendable localization can prevent the existence of any free extension.

We begin by establishing the following for use in the proof.

Proposition 7.3
.

Under the definitions
\theta_{a,b,c}
and
I(a,b,c)
,

(1)

\theta_{a,b,c}(x+y)\in(x+y)S
if and only if
I(a,b,c)=0
.

(2)

I(a,b,c)=0
if and only if
a=b
and
a+b+c\in 2{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proof.

(1)
Let
\theta_{a,b,c}(x+y)\in(x+y)S
. Substituting
x=1
and
y=-1
, it follows that
I(a,b,c)=0
. Conversely, assume that
I(a,b,c)=0
. Since
p(x,y):=\theta_{a,b,c}(x+y)
is a homogeneous polynomial of some degree
n
, it can be written in the form

p(x,y)=\theta_{a,b,c}(x+y)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\lambda_{i}(x+y)^{i}x^{n-i},

for some constants
\lambda_{0},\ldots,\lambda_{n}
. Evaluating
p
at
(1,-1)
yields

p(1,-1)=\lambda_{0}\cdot 1=0,

which implies
\lambda_{0}=0
, and hence
\theta_{a,b,c}(x+y)\in(x+y)S
.

(2)
Assume without loss of generality that
a\leq b
, and set
n=b-a
. The case
b<a
can be treated analogously. We compute:

\displaystyle I(a,b,c)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}t^{c}(t-1)^{b}(t+1)^{a}\,dt+\int_{0}^{-1}t^{c}(t-1)^{b}(t+1)^{a}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\underbrace{t^{c}(t-1)^{a}(t+1)^{a}}_{=:f(t)}\underbrace{\left((t-1)^{n}-(-1)^{a+b+c}(t+1)^{n}\right)}_{=:g(t)}\,dt.

The function
f(t)
does not change sign on the interval
(0,1)
. The same is true for
g(t)
, since for
t\in(0,1)
and
n>0
, we have
(t+1)^{n}>(t-1)^{n}
. Therefore,
I(a,b,c)=0
if and only if
g(t)=0
for all
t\in(0,1)
. This is only the case when
n=0
and
a+b+c\in 2\mathbb{Z}
. In particular,
n=0
implies
a=b
.
∎

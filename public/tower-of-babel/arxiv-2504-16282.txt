An integral analogue of Fontaine’s crystalline functor
Naoki Imai (1), Hiroki Kato (2), Alex Youcis (3)

Abstract
. For a smooth formal scheme 𝔛 \mathfrak{X} over the Witt vectors W W of a perfect field k k , we construct a functor 𝔻 crys \mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{crys}} from the category of prismatic F F -crystals ( ℰ , φ ℰ ) (\mathcal{E},\varphi_{\mathcal{E}}) (or prismatic F F -gauges) on 𝔛 \mathfrak{X} to the category of filtered F F -crystals on 𝔛 \mathfrak{X} . We show that 𝔻 crys ​ ( ℰ , φ ℰ ) \mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{crys}}(\mathcal{E},\varphi_{\mathcal{E}}) enjoys strong properties when ( ℰ , φ ℰ ) (\mathcal{E},\varphi_{\mathcal{E}}) is what we call locally filtered free (lff) . Most significantly, we show that 𝔻 crys \mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{crys}} actually induces an equivalence between the category of prismatic F F -gauges on 𝔛 \mathfrak{X} with Hodge–Tate weights in [ 0 , p − 2 ] [0,p-2] and the category of Fontaine–Laffaille modules on 𝔛 \mathfrak{X} . Finally, we use our functor 𝔻 crys \mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{crys}} to enhance the study of prismatic Dieudonné theory of p p -divisible groups (as initiated by Anschütz–Le Bras) allowing one to recover the filtered crystalline Dieudonné crystal from the prismatic Dieudonné crystal. This in turn allows us to clarify the relationship between prismatic Dieudonné theory and the work of Kim on classifying p p -divisible groups using Breuil–Kisin modules.

But, for arithmetic applications, for example to the study of Shimura varieties (e.g., see

Crystalline approach (

Prismatic approach (

Syntomic approach (

The goal of this paper is to complete the comparisons of these three approaches by relating the crystalline approach to the prismatic and syntomic ones by defining

Applications to
p
-divisible groups

We finally describe the ways in which the functor
\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{crys}}
can be used to clarify the relationship between
p
-divisible groups and various types of Dieudonné theory when
p>2
. To this end, let us fix
\mathfrak{X}
to be a base formal
W
-scheme (see
[
IKY26
, §1.1.5]
).

Denote by
\mathbf{BT}_{p}(\mathfrak{X})
the category of
p
-divisible groups over
\mathfrak{X}
. There are then several approaches to use
p
-adic Hodge theory to classify objects of
\mathbf{BT}_{p}(\mathfrak{X})
via various ‘Dieudonné theories’:

(1)

the
filtered crystalline Dieudonné functor
of Grothendieck–Messing

\mathbb{D}\colon\mathbf{BT}_{p}(\mathfrak{X})\to\mathbf{VectF}^{\varphi,\mathrm{sd}}_{[0,1]}(\mathfrak{X}_{\mathrm{crys}}),

which is an (anti-)equivalence by results of Grothendieck, Messing, and de Jong (see
[
dJ95
]
),

(2)

the
Breuil–Kisin–Kim Dieudonné functor
(see
[
Kim15
]
)

\mathfrak{M}\colon\mathbf{BT}_{p}(\mathfrak{X})\to\mathbf{Vect}^{\varphi}_{[0,1]}(\mathfrak{S}_{R},\nabla^{0})

when
\mathfrak{X}=\Spf(R)
for a formally framed
W
-algebra
R
(see
[
IKY26
, §1.1.5]
for the definition of framed algebra, and Definition
4.11
for the definition of the target category), which is an (anti-)equivalence by
[
Kim15
]
,

(3)

the
prismatic Dieudonné functor
of Anschütz–Le Bras

\mathcal{M}_{{{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}}}\colon\mathbf{BT}_{p}(\mathfrak{X})\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.59442pt}{\smash{$\sim$}}}\mathbf{Vect}_{[0,1]}^{\varphi}(\mathfrak{X}_{{{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}}}),

which is an (anti-)equivalence by
[
ALB23
]
(cf. Theorem
4.2
).

The relationship between these various Dieudonné theories is important to establish the connection between historical results and modern methods (e.g., as is needed in the study of Shimura varieties in
[
IKY23
]
). That said, this relationship is far from clear given the different ways in which these functors are constructed.

In
[
ALB23
, Theorem 4.44]
, it is demonstrated that there is a canonical identification

\underline{\mathbb{D}}(H)\simeq\underline{\mathbb{D}}_{\mathrm{crys}}(\mathcal{M}_{{{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}}}(H)),

in
\mathbf{Vect}^{\varphi}(\mathfrak{X}_{\mathrm{crys}})
, for an object
H
of
\mathbf{BT}_{p}(\mathfrak{X})
. Here, recall,
\underline{\mathbb{D}}_{\mathrm{crys}}(\mathcal{M}_{{{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}}}(H))
is the underlying
F
-crystal of
\mathbb{D}_{\mathrm{crys}}(\mathcal{M}_{{{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}}}(H))
. We further denote by
\underline{\mathbb{D}}(H)
the underlying
F
-crystal of
\mathbb{D}(H)
. In particular, this identification ignores filtrations, which is equivalent (by Grothendieck–Messing theory) to only remembering the special fiber
H_{k}
of the
p

. Thus, it is desirable to upgrade this to a filtered identification.

(\mathcal{F}[\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}],\varphi_{\mathcal{F}},\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{F}}[\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}])=(\mathcal{F}[\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}],\varphi_{\mathcal{F}},\Fil^{\bullet}_{F})
.

2.1.3.
The category
\mathbf{VectF}^{\nabla}(\mathfrak{X})

We next discuss some results of Tsuji which allow one to understand the filtrations of some naive filtered
F
-crystals via the crystalline site.

Consider a pair
(\mathcal{E},\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{E}})
, where
\mathcal{E}
is an object of
\mathbf{Vect}(\mathfrak{X}_{\mathrm{crys}})
, and
\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{E}}\subseteq\mathcal{E}
is a filtration by locally quasi-coherent (see
[
SP
,
Tag 07IS
]
)
\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{crys}}
-submodules. We call
(\mathcal{E},\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{E}})
a
filtered crystal
if

(1)

(\mathcal{E},\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{E}})(x)
is a filtered module over
(A,\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{PD}})
for all
x=(i\colon A\twoheadrightarrow B,\gamma)
in
(\mathfrak{X}/W)_{\mathrm{crys}}
,

(2)

for a morphism
x=(i\colon A\to B,\gamma)\to(i^{\prime}\colon A^{\prime}\to B^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})=y
in
(\mathfrak{X}/W)_{\mathrm{crys}}
, the natural map
(\mathcal{E},\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{E}})(x)\otimes_{(A,\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{PD}}(A))}(A^{\prime},\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{PD}}(A^{\prime}))\to(\mathcal{E},\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{E}})(y)
is an isomorphism.

We will mostly be interested in the case when
(\mathcal{E},\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{E}})
is locally free over
(\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{crys}},\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{PD}})
in the sense of
[
IKY23
, Definition 1.1]
, and we denote the category of such by
\mathbf{VectF}(\mathfrak{X}_{\mathrm{crys}})
.

Now, define
\mathbf{VectF}^{\nabla}(\mathfrak{X})
to consist of triples
(\mathcal{V},\nabla_{\mathcal{V}},\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{V}})
where
(\mathcal{V},\nabla_{\mathcal{V}})
is an object of
\mathbf{Vect}^{\nabla}(\mathfrak{X})
and
\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{V}}
is a locally split filtration on
\mathcal{V}
which satisfies Griffiths transversality.

Proposition 2.3

(cf.
[
Tsu20
, Theorem 29]
)
.

The functor

\mathbf{VectF}(\mathfrak{X}_{\mathrm{crys}})\to\mathbf{VectF}^{\nabla}(\mathfrak{X}),\qquad\mathcal{E}\mapsto(\mathcal{E}|_{\mathfrak{X}_{\mathrm{Zar}}},\nabla_{\mathcal{E}},(\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{E}})|_{\mathfrak{X}_{\mathrm{Zar}}}),

is an equivalence.

Remark 2.4
.

By definition,
\mathcal{E}|_{\mathfrak{X}_{\mathrm{Zar}}}(\mathfrak{U})=\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{id}\colon\mathfrak{U}\to\mathfrak{U},\gamma)
. But while
\mathcal{E}(\mathrm{id}\colon\mathfrak{U}\to\mathfrak{U},\gamma)
agrees with
\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{U}_{0}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{U},\gamma)
, this is not true for
\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{E}}
as it is a
filtered
crystal and the PD filtrations for
(\mathrm{id}\colon\mathfrak{U}\to\mathfrak{U},\gamma)
and
(\mathfrak{U}_{0}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{U},\gamma)
.

Note that, by definition, we have

\mathbf{VectF}^{\varphi}(\mathfrak{X}_{\mathrm{crys}})=\mathbf{Vect}^{\varphi}(\mathfrak{X}_{\mathrm{crys}})\times_{\mathbf{Vect}^{\nabla}(\mathfrak{X})}\mathbf{VectF}^{\nabla}(\mathfrak{X}).

Thus, by Proposition
2.3
, we obtain an equivalence of categories

\mathbf{VectF}^{\varphi}(\mathfrak{X}_{\mathrm{crys}})\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.59442pt}{\smash{$\sim$}}}\mathbf{Vect}^{\varphi}(\mathfrak{X}_{\mathrm{crys}})\times_{\mathbf{Vect}(\mathfrak{X}_{\mathrm{crys}})}\mathbf{VectF}(\mathfrak{X}_{\mathrm{crys}}).

For this reason, for an object
(\mathcal{F},\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{F}})
of
\mathbf{Vect}^{\varphi}(\mathfrak{X}_{\mathrm{crys}})
we may consider the evaluation of
\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{F}}
at objects of
(\mathfrak{X}/W)_{\mathrm{crys}}
, by which we mean the evaluation of the associated filtered crystal.

2.1.4.
Divisibility and strong divisibility

Let us fix a base formal
W
-algebra
R
and a framing
w
, inducing the Frobenius lift
\phi_{w}
on
R
. Then, for an
F
-crystal
(\mathcal{F},\varphi_{\mathcal{F}})
on
(R/W)_{\mathrm{crys}}
we define the
Nygaard filtration
on
\phi_{w}^{\ast}\mathcal{F}(R)
, where we write
\mathcal{F}(R)
as shorthand for
\mathcal{F}_{\Spf(R)}(R)
, by:

\mathrm{Fil}^{r}_{\mathrm{Nyg},\mathcal{F}}=\bigg\{x\in\phi_{w}^{\ast}\mathcal{F}(R):\varphi_{\mathcal{F}}(x)\in p^{r}\mathcal{F}(R)\bigg\},

leaving
\varphi_{\mathcal{F}}
,
R
, and
\phi_{w}
implicit in the notation.

Remark 2.5
.

There is a small discrepancy between the Nygaard filtration here and the one in the sense of
[
IKY23
, Definition 1.23]
for the crystalline prism
(R,(p))
, which we briefly call the
prismatic Nygaard filtration
. Namely, when
(\mathcal{F},\varphi_{\mathcal{F}})
is considered as a prismatic
F
-crystal on
R/p
as in §
1.1.1
there is an introduced Frobenius twist (see Proposition
1.1
)
and so the prismatic Nygaard filtration actually induces a filtration on
\mathcal{F}(R)
, not
\phi_{w}^{\ast}\mathcal{F}(R)
. That said, the Nygaard filtration introduced here is precisely the pullback under
\phi_{w}
of the prismatic Nygaard filtration. Or, equivalently, it agrees with the prismatic Nygaard filtration but with respect to
(R,(p))
thought of as an object of
R_{{{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}}}
via the structure map
R\xrightarrow{\phi_{w}}R\to R/p
, where the last map is the natural map.

Definition 2.6
.

With notation as above, we say a naive filtered
F
-crystal
(\mathcal{F},\varphi_{\mathcal{F}},\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{F}})
is:

(1)

divisible with respect to
w
if for all
r\in\mathbb{Z}
one has
\phi_{w}^{\ast}\Fil^{r}_{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq\Fil^{r}_{\mathrm{Nyg},\mathcal{F}}
,

(2)

strongly divisible with respect to
w
if for all
r\in\mathbb{Z}
one has:

\varphi_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}p^{-i}\phi_{w}^{\ast}\Fil^{i}_{\mathcal{F}}\right)=\mathcal{F}(R).

We say that
(\mathcal{F},\varphi_{\mathcal{F}},\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{F}})
is
(strongly) divisible
if it is (strongly) divisible with respect to all choices of
w
.

To help better understand these definitions, we consider the following two lattices in
\phi_{w}^{\ast}\mathcal{F}(R)[\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}]
:

\displaystyle L_{\mathrm{Hdg}}\vcentcolon=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}p^{-i}\phi_{w}^{\ast}\Fil^{i}_{\mathcal{F}},\qquad L_{\mathrm{Nyg}}\vcentcolon=\varphi_{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(\mathcal{F}(R))=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}p^{-i}\Fil^{i}_{\mathrm{Nyg},\mathcal{F}},

leaving the various dependencies implicit in the notation. From this perspective, it is clear that
(\mathcal{F},\varphi_{\mathcal{F}},\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{F}})
being strongly divisible with respect to
w
is equivalent to the equality
L_{\mathrm{Hdg}}=L_{\mathrm{Nyg}}
, and it is simple to check that divisibility with respect to
w
is equivalent to
L_{\mathrm{Hdg}}\subseteq L_{\mathrm{Nyg}}
.

Next, we observe that when our naive filtered
F
-crystal is gtf, strong divisibility can be phrased in terms of filtered tensor products.

Proposition 2.7
.

Suppose that
(\mathcal{F},\varphi_{\mathcal{F}},\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{F}})
is a gtf naive filtered
F
-crystal. Then, it is strongly divisible with respect to
w
if and only if:

(\phi_{w}^{\ast}\mathcal{F}(R),\phi_{w}^{\ast}\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{F}})\otimes_{(R,\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{triv}})}(R,\Fil^{\bullet}_{p})=(\phi_{w}^{\ast}\mathcal{F}(R),\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{Nyg},\mathcal{F}}),

i.e., for all
r\in\mathbb{Z}
one has the equality

\phi_{w}^{\ast}\Fil^{r}_{\mathcal{F},p}\vcentcolon=\sum_{i\leqslant r}p^{r-i}\phi_{w}^{\ast}\Fil^{i}_{\mathcal{F}}=\Fil^{r}_{\mathrm{Nyg},\mathcal{F}}.

Proof.

Observe that if
N
is a finite-rank free
R
-module and
L,L^{\prime}\subseteq N[\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}]
are
R
-lattices, then
L=L^{\prime}
if and only if
N\cap p^{r}L=N\cap p^{r}L^{\prime}
for all
r
. In fact, one only needs to check for some
r
such that
p^{r}L
and
p^{r}L^{\prime}
are contained in
N
.

From this, it suffices to verify the two equalities

(2.1.1)

\phi^{\ast}_{w}\mathcal{F}(R)\cap p^{r}L_{\mathrm{Hdg}}=\sum_{i\leqslant r}p^{r-i}\phi_{w}^{\ast}\Fil^{i}_{\mathcal{F}},\qquad\phi^{\ast}_{w}\mathcal{F}(R)\cap p^{r}L_{\mathrm{Nyg}}=\Fil^{r}_{\mathrm{Nyg},\mathcal{F}}.

The second equality is by definition. To verify the first equality, observe that the right-hand side is clearly included in the left-hand side. For the other inclusion, an element of the left-hand side is of the form
x=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}p^{r-i}x_{i}
where
x_{i}\in\phi_{w}^{\ast}\Fil^{i}_{\mathcal{F}}
.
As
x\in\phi_{w}^{\ast}\mathcal{F}(R)
and
\sum_{i\leqslant r}p^{r-i}x_{i}\in\phi_{w}^{\ast}\mathcal{F}(R)
one also has that
b=\sum_{i>r}p^{r-i}x_{i}\in\phi_{w}^{\ast}\mathcal{F}(R)
. Let
m>r
be maximal with respect to
x_{m}\neq 0
. Then,
p^{m-r}b\in\phi_{w}^{\ast}\Fil^{r}_{\mathcal{F}}
, and thus by our gtf assumption,
b\in\phi_{w}^{\ast}\Fil^{r}_{\mathcal{F}}
. Thus,
x=b+\sum_{i\leqslant r}p^{r-i}x_{i}
is in
\sum_{i\leqslant r}p^{r-i}\phi_{w}^{\ast}\Fil^{i}_{\mathcal{F}}
as desired.
∎

Finally, we globalize the above definitions.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a base formal
W
-scheme. We say that a naive filtered
F
-crystal
(\mathcal{F},\varphi_{\mathcal{F}},\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{F}})
is
(
strongly
)
divisible
if it is after restriction to every affine open
\Spf(R)\subseteq\mathfrak{X}
.

We write
\mathbf{VectNF}^{\varphi,\mathrm{sd}}(\mathfrak{X}_{\mathrm{crys}})
for the category of strongly divisible naive filtered
F
-crystals. More generally, for any of the previously defined subcategories of naive filtered
F
-crystals, we use a superscript
\mathrm{sd}
to denote those which are additionally strongly divisible.

2.1.5.
PD-divisibility and strong PD-divisibility

In practice strong divisibility is too strong, and so we now aim to weaken this condition. We continue to fix a base formal
W
-algebra
R
and a framing
w
, inducing the Frobenius lift
\phi_{w}
on
R
.

Notation 2.9
.

For an integer
r\geqslant 0
, write
p^{[r]}\vcentcolon=\tfrac{p^{r}}{r!}
, and write
\langle r\rangle
for the unique integer such that

p^{\langle r\rangle}\mathbb{Z}_{p}=\bigcup_{i\geqslant r}p^{[i]}\mathbb{Z}_{p},

i.e.,
\langle r\rangle=\min\{v_{p}(p^{[i]}):i\geqslant r\}
. Finally, we can consider the filtered ring
(R,\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{PD}})
where

\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{PD}}=\begin{cases}p^{\langle r\rangle}R&\mbox{if}\quad r\geqslant 0\\
R&\mbox{if}\quad r<0.\end{cases}

Definition 2.10
.

We say a naive filtered
F
-crystal
(\mathcal{F},\varphi_{\mathcal{F}},\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{F}})
of
\mathbf{VectNF}^{\varphi}(R_{\mathrm{crys}})
is:

(1)

PD-divisible with respect to
w
if for all
r\in\mathbb{Z}
one has the inclusion

\phi_{w}^{\ast}\Fil^{r}_{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq\sum_{i\leqslant r}p^{[r-i]}\Fil^{i}_{\mathrm{Nyg},\mathcal{F}}=:\mathrm{Fil}^{r}_{\mathrm{Nyg},\mathcal{F},\mathrm{PD}}.

(2)

strongly PD-divisible with respect to
w
if it is PD-divisible with respect to
w
and if, for all
r\in\mathbb{Z}
, the above inclusion induces the equality

\phi_{w}^{\ast}\Fil^{r}_{\mathcal{F},\mathrm{PD}}\vcentcolon=\sum_{i\leqslant r}p^{[r-i]}\phi_{w}^{\ast}\Fil^{i}_{\mathcal{F}}=\mathrm{Fil}^{r}_{\mathrm{Nyg},\mathcal{F},\mathrm{PD}}.

We say that
(\mathcal{F},\varphi_{\mathcal{F}},\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{F}})
is
(strongly) PD-divisible
if it is (strongly) PD-divisible with respect to all choices of
w
.

We write
\mathbf{VectNF}^{\varphi,\mathrm{sPDd}}(\mathfrak{X}_{\mathrm{crys}})
for the category of strongly PD-divisible naive filtered
F
-crystals on
\mathfrak{X}
. More generally, for any other subcategory of naive filtered
F
-crystals, we use the superscript
\mathrm{sPDd}
to denote the subcategory consisting of strongly PD-divisible objects.

We first observe that (strong) PD-divisibility can be phrased in terms of filtered tensor products. The following result is simple, and left to the reader.

Lemma 2.11
.

A naive filtered
F
-crystal
(\mathcal{F},\varphi_{\mathcal{F}},\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{F}})
is PD-divisible
(
resp. strongly PD-divisible
)
with respect to
w
if and only if the identity map induces a morphism
(
resp. isomorphism
)
from
(\phi_{w}^{\ast}\mathcal{F}(R),\phi_{w}^{\ast}\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{F}})
to
(\phi_{w}^{\ast}\mathcal{F}(R),\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{Nyg},\mathcal{F}})
after filtered base change along
(R,\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{triv}})\to(R,\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{PD}})
.

Remark 2.12
.

When
(\mathcal{F},\varphi_{\mathcal{F}},\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{F}})
belongs to
\mathbf{VectNF}^{\varphi,\mathrm{gtf}}_{[0,p-1]}(\mathfrak{X}_{\mathrm{crys}})
(or more generally in
\mathbf{VectNF}_{[a,b]}^{\varphi,\mathrm{gtf}}(\mathfrak{X}_{\mathrm{crys}})
with
b-a\leqslant p-1
), PD-divisibility (resp. strong PD-divisibility) is equivalent to divisibility (resp. strong divisibility). As we only require the implication that strong PD-divisibility implies strong divisibility in this range, we content ourselves with an explanation of this.

To do this, we observe that, by definition of strong PD-divisibility, one has that
\phi_{w}^{\ast}\Fil^{p-1}_{\mathcal{F},\mathrm{PD}}=\Fil^{p-1}_{\mathrm{Nyg},\mathcal{F},\mathrm{PD}}
. Thus, if we can show that

p^{p-1}L_{\mathrm{Hdg}}=\phi_{w}^{\ast}\mathrm{Fil}^{p-1}_{\mathcal{F},p}=\phi_{w}^{\ast}\mathrm{Fil}^{p-1}_{\mathcal{F},\mathrm{PD}},\qquad p^{p-1}L_{\mathrm{Nyg}}=\mathrm{Fil}^{p-1}_{\mathrm{Nyg},\mathcal{F}}=\mathrm{Fil}^{p-1}_{\mathrm{Nyg},\mathcal{F},\mathrm{PD}},

then we deduce that
p^{p-1}L_{\mathrm{Hdg}}=p^{p-1}L_{\mathrm{Nyg}}
and thus
L_{\mathrm{Hdg}}=L_{\mathrm{Nyg}}
, and thus strong divisibility would follow.

We first show that
p^{p-1}L_{\mathrm{Hdg}}=\phi_{w}^{\ast}\Fil^{p-1}_{\mathcal{F},p}
. As
(\mathcal{F},\varphi_{\mathcal{F}},\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{F}})
is gtf, we know from Equation (
2.1.1
), that
\phi_{w}^{\ast}\Fil^{p-1}_{\mathcal{F},p}=p^{p-1}L_{\mathrm{Hdg}}\cap\phi_{w}^{\ast}\mathcal{F}(R)
. That said, as
\Fil^{i}_{\mathcal{F}}=0
for
i>p-1
, the intersection on the right-hand side of this equality is superfluous, and so we arrive at our desired equality.

To show that
\phi_{w}^{\ast}\Fil^{p-1}_{\mathcal{F},p}=\phi_{w}^{\ast}\Fil^{p-1}_{\mathcal{F},\mathrm{PD}}
means showing that

\sum_{i\leqslant p-1}p^{p-1-i}\phi^{\ast}_{w}\Fil^{i}_{\mathcal{F}}=\sum_{i\leqslant p-1}p^{[p-1-i]}\phi_{w}^{\ast}\Fil^{i}_{\mathcal{F}}.

Note that as
\Fil^{i}_{\mathcal{F}}=\mathcal{F}(R)
for
i\leqslant 0
, the fact that

\sum_{k\geqslant 0}p^{p-1+k}\phi_{w}^{\ast}\mathcal{F}(R)\subseteq p^{p-1}\phi_{w}^{\ast}\mathcal{F}(R),\qquad\sum_{k\geqslant 0}p^{[p-1+k]}\phi_{w}^{\ast}\mathcal{F}(R)\subseteq p^{[p-1]}\phi_{w}^{\ast}\mathcal{F}(R),

implies that we must only show

\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}p^{p-1-i}\phi_{w}^{\ast}\mathcal{F}(R)=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}p^{[p-1-i]}\phi_{w}^{\ast}\mathcal{F}(R).

But, this is clear as
v_{p}(p^{p-1-i})=v_{p}(p^{[p-1-i]})
for
i\in[0,p-1]
. A similar argument gives the equality
\Fil^{p-1}_{\mathrm{Nyg},\mathcal{F}}=\Fil^{p-1}_{\mathrm{Nyg},\mathcal{F},\mathrm{PD}}
.

Finally, we show that
p^{p-1}L_{\mathrm{Nyg}}=\Fil^{p-1}_{\mathrm{Nyg},\mathcal{F}}
.
By Equation (
2.1.1
), it suffices to show that
p^{p}L_{\mathrm{Nyg}}\subset p\phi_{w}^{*}\mathcal{F}(R)
, which follows from
p^{p}L_{\mathrm{Nyg}}\cap\phi_{w}^{*}\mathcal{F}(R)\subset p\phi_{w}^{*}\mathcal{F}(R)
because if there is
x\in p^{p}L_{\mathrm{Nyg}}\setminus p\phi_{w}^{*}\mathcal{F}(R)
then there is
n\geq 0
such that
p^{n}x\in(p^{p}L_{\mathrm{Nyg}}\cap\phi_{w}^{*}\mathcal{F}(R))\setminus p\phi_{w}^{*}\mathcal{F}(R)
. Then the claim follows from

p^{p}L_{\mathrm{Nyg}}\cap\phi_{w}^{*}\mathcal{F}(R)=\Fil^{p}_{\mathrm{Nyg},\mathcal{F}}\subset\Fil^{p}_{\mathrm{Nyg},\mathcal{F},\mathrm{PD}}=\Fil^{p}_{\mathcal{F},\mathrm{PD}}\subset p\phi_{w}^{*}\mathcal{F}(R)
, where we use Equation (
2.1.1
), the assumption of strong PD-divisibility and
\mathrm{Fil}^{i}_{\mathcal{F}}=0
for
i>p-1
.

Next we observe that, in fact, (strong) PD-divisibility with respect to
w
actually implies (strong) PD-divisibility if we assume that
(\mathcal{F},\varphi_{\mathcal{F}},\Fil^{\bullet})
is a filtered
F
-crystal.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
(\mathcal{F},\varphi_{\mathcal{F}},\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{F}})
be a filtered
F
-crystal. Then, if
(\mathcal{F},\varphi_{\mathcal{F}},\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{F}})
is
(
strongly
)
PD-divisible with respect to some
w
, then it is
(
strongly
)
PD-divisible.

Proof.

This follows from Lemma
2.11
and Proposition
2.3
. Indeed, the filtered module

(\phi_{w}^{\ast}\mathcal{F}(R),\phi_{w}^{\ast}\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{F}})\otimes_{(R,\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{triv}})}(R,\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{PD}}),

is equal to the evaluation of
\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{F}}
(through the equivalence in Proposition
2.3
) at the PD-thickening
R\to R/p
with the structural map being the composite
R\xrightarrow{\mathrm{nat}}R/p\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Frob}}R/p
. In particular, it is independent of the choice of
w
.
∎

2.1.6.
Comparison to the category of Faltings

Fix a naive filtered
F
-crystal
(\mathcal{F},\varphi_{\mathcal{F}},\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{F}})
on
\Spf(R)
, where
R
is a base
W
-algebra with formal framing
w
. Consider the following module as in
[
Fal89
, II.c), p. 30]
:

(2.1.2)

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathfrak{X}}\vcentcolon=\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font colim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font colim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font colim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font colim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits\bigg(\cdots\xrightarrow{}\Fil^{r+1}_{\mathcal{F}}\xleftarrow{\cdot p}\Fil^{r+1}_{\mathcal{F}}\xrightarrow{}\Fil^{r}_{\mathcal{F}}\xleftarrow{\cdot p}\Fil^{r}_{\mathcal{F}}\xrightarrow{}\Fil^{r-1}_{\mathcal{F}}\xleftarrow{}\cdots\bigg).

The maps
\Fil^{r}_{\mathcal{F}}\to\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{X}}[\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}]
sending
x
to
p^{-r}x
induce a natural map
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathfrak{X}}\to\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{X}}[\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}]
whose image is the sum
\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}}p^{-r}\Fil^{r}_{\mathcal{F}}
. We then have the following
Faltings morphism

(2.1.3)

\phi_{w}^{\ast}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathfrak{X}}\to\phi_{w}^{\ast}\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{X}}[\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}]\xrightarrow{\varphi_{\mathcal{F}}}\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{X}}[\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}].

Observe that if
(\mathcal{F},\varphi_{\mathcal{F}},\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{F}})
is gtf then the map
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathfrak{X}}\to\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{X}}[\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}]
is injective. Thus, as

is flat, we see that if
(\mathcal{F},\varphi_{\mathcal{F}},\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{F}})
is gtf and strongly divisible, then the Faltings morphism induces an isomorphism
\phi_{w}^{\ast}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathfrak{X}}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.59442pt}{\smash{$\sim$}}}\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{X}}
.

Observe that

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathfrak{X}}/p\cong\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font colim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font colim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font colim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font colim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits\bigg(\cdots\xrightarrow{}\Fil^{r+1}_{\mathcal{F}}/p\xleftarrow{0}\Fil^{r+1}_{\mathcal{F}}/p\xrightarrow{}\Fil^{r}_{\mathcal{F}}/p\xleftarrow{0}\Fil^{r}_{\mathcal{F}}/p\xrightarrow{}\Fil^{r-1}_{\mathcal{F}}/p\xleftarrow{}\cdots\bigg),

and so is isomorphic to
\bigoplus_{r}\Gr^{r}(\Fil_{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet})/p
. Leveraging this, we show the following.

Proposition 2.14

(cf.
[
Fal89
, Theorem 2.1]
,
[
LMP24
, Theorem 2.9]
)
.

Let
(\mathcal{F},\varphi_{\mathcal{F}},\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{F}})
be a gtf strongly divisible naive filtered
F
-crystal. Then, the filtration
\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{X}}
is locally split. If
(\mathcal{F},\varphi_{\mathcal{F}},\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{F}})
is further a weakly filtered
F
-crystal, then it is a strongly divisible filtered
F
-crystal.

Proof.

We may assume that
\mathfrak{X}=\Spf(R)
, where
R
is a base
W
-algebra with formal framing
w
.

Let us first verify that the filtration
\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{F}}
is locally split. In other words, we must show that
\mathrm{Gr}^{r}(\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{F}})
is a locally free
R
-module for all
r
. As
(\mathcal{F},\varphi_{\mathcal{F}},\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{F}})
is gtf and strongly divisible, the Faltings morphism is an isomorphism. This implies that
\phi_{w}^{\ast}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(R)
is a projective
R
-module. But, as observed above, this implies that
\phi_{w}^{\ast}\Gr^{r}(\Fil^{r}_{\mathcal{F}}(R))/p
is a projective
R/p
-module for all
r
, and since
\phi_{w}
is faithfully flat this implies that
\mathrm{Gr}^{r}(\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{F}}(R))/p
is a locally free
R/p
-module for all
r
. This implies that
\mathrm{Gr}^{r}(\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{F}}(R))
is a projective
R
-module by the following simple lemma.

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
A
be a Noetherian
p
-adically complete ring and
Q
a finitely generated
p
-torsion-free
A
-module such that

is a projective
A/p
-module. Then,
Q

4.3.2.
The Breuil–Kisin–Kim Dieudonné functor

We now recall the definition of the Breuil–Kisin–Kim Dieudonné functor.

Definition 4.13

(cf.
[
Kim15

)
.

A
(
minuscule
)
Kisin module
over
R
is a triple
(\mathfrak{M},\varphi_{\mathfrak{M}},\nabla^{0}_{\mathfrak{M}})
where:

•

(\mathfrak{M},\varphi_{\mathfrak{M}})
is an object of the category
\mathbf{Vect}^{\varphi}_{[0,1]}(\mathfrak{S}_{R},(E))
,

•

\nabla^{0}_{\mathfrak{M}}
is a topologically quasi-nilpotent integrable connection on
\mathfrak{M}_{0}\vcentcolon=\phi^{\ast}\mathfrak{M}/u
.

We denote the category of such objects by
\mathbf{Vect}_{[0,1]}^{\varphi}(\mathfrak{S}_{R},\nabla^{0})
.

There is a functor

\mathbf{Vect}^{\varphi}_{[0,1]}(\mathfrak{S}_{R},\nabla^{0})\to\mathbf{VectF}^{\varphi}_{[0,1]}(S_{R},\nabla^{0}),

where
(\mathfrak{M},\varphi_{\mathfrak{M}},\nabla^{0}_{\mathfrak{M}_{0}})

(\phi^{\ast}\mathfrak{M},\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{Nyg}})\otimes_{(\mathfrak{S}_{R},\Fil^{\bullet}_{E})}(S_{R},\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{PD}})

\nabla^{0}_{\mathfrak{M}}

R

[

Enhanced dissipation for the N-Dimensional elastic FENE dumbbell model near an equilibrium
Zheng-an Yao, Ruijia Yu ∗

Abstract
This paper studies the N N -dimensional FENE dumbbell model without velocity dissipation, focusing on the stability and decay of perturbations near the steady solution ( 0 , ψ ∞ ) (0,\psi_{\infty}) . Due to the lack of velocity dissipation, the above problems are highly challenging. In fact, without coupling, the corresponding N N -dimensional Euler equation is well known to be unstable. To overcome this difficulty, we investigate two types of enhanced dissipation associated with the system governing the perturbations near this steady state, one due to the equilibrium and one due to the coupling and interaction. These enhanced dissipation mechanisms enable us to establish the global stability in the H s H^{s} -type Sobolev norms. Also, we highlight the critical role of enhanced dissipation in the decay estimates of the elastic FENE dumbbell model. By combining this property with the Fourier splitting method, we derive the decay rate which is identical to that of the general FENE dumbbell with velocity dissipation.



keywords
FENE model; global existence; enhanced dissipation; decay estimates

1.
Introduction

Fluid-polymer coupled systems are fundamental in physics, chemistry, and biology, drawing significant research interest
[
4
,
14
]
. One of these models is the finite extensible nonlinear elastic (FENE) dumbbell model.
In this model, a polymer is depicted as an ”elastic dumbbell,” consisting of two ”beads” linked by a spring, which is represented by a vector

R
(see
Bird et al. 1987a
,
Bird et al. 1987b
,
Doi et al. 1988
,
Öttinger 2012
).
At the fluid level, the FENE dumbbell model combines the Navier-Stokes equations describing fluid velocity with a Fokker-Planck equation governing the dynamics of polymer distribution in the liquid medium.
The micro-macro FENE dumbbell model reads as follows

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+(u\cdot\nabla)u=\mu\Delta u-\nabla p+\text{div}\,\tau,\quad\text{div}\,u=0,\\
\partial_{t}\Psi+u\cdot\nabla\Psi=\nu\Delta\Psi+\text{div}_{R}\left[-\nabla u\cdot R\Psi+\beta\nabla_{R}\Psi+\nabla_{R}\,\mathcal{U}\Psi\right],\\
\tau_{l,m}=\int_{B}(R_{l}\partial_{R_{m}}\mathcal{U})\Psi(x,R,t)\,\mathrm{d}R,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0},\quad\Psi(x,R,0)=\Psi_{0},\\
(\nabla_{R}\Psi+\nabla_{R}\,\mathcal{U}\Psi)\cdot n=0\quad\text{on}\ \partial B(0,R_{0}).\end{cases}

(1)

In (
1
),
u(x,t)
denotes the velocity of the polymeric liquid, and
\Psi(x,R,t)
denotes the distribution function of the internal configuration, where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 2
. The polymer elongation
R
is bounded in a ball
B(0,R_{0})
, which indicates that the extensibility of the polymers is finite.
Also, the potential
\mathcal{U}
is given by
\mathcal{U}(R)=-k\log(1-\frac{|R|^{2}}{R_{0}^{2}})
for some
k>0
.
In addition,
\nu
is the center-of-mass diffusion rate of the polymer, and
\beta
relates to the Boltzmann constant and temperature.

As in
[
14
]
, to ensure the conservation of
\Psi
, we add an additional boundary condition, namely

(-\nabla u\cdot R\Psi+\beta\nabla_{R}\Psi+\nabla_{R}\,\mathcal{U}\Psi)\cdot n=0\quad\text{ on }\partial B(0,R_{0}).

This boundary condition implies that
\Psi=0
on
\partial B(0,R_{0})
, and if
\int_{B}\Psi_{0}(x,R)\,\mathrm{d}R=1
, then for all
(x,t)
, we have
\int_{B}\Psi(x,R,t)\,\mathrm{d}R=1
.

In this paper, we will set
\beta=1
and
R_{0}=1
.
Notice that
u=0
and

\psi_{\infty}(R)=\frac{e^{-\mathcal{U}(R)}}{\int_{B}e^{-\mathcal{U}(R)}\,\mathrm{d}R}=\frac{(1-|R|^{2})^{k}}{\int_{B}(1-|R|^{2})^{k}\,\mathrm{d}R}

is a trivial solution of (
1
), and we denote

\mathcal{L}\Psi=\text{div}_{R}(\nabla_{R}\Psi+\nabla_{R}\,\mathcal{U}\Psi)=\text{div}_{R}\left(\psi_{\infty}\nabla_{R}\frac{\Psi}{\psi_{\infty}}\right).

There are plenty of mathematical results about the FENE dumbbell model.
If
\nu=0
and
\mu>0
,
Renardy 1991
demonstrated the local well-posedness in Sobolev space for potentials of the form
\mathcal{U}(R)=(1-|R|^{2})^{1-\sigma}
with some
\sigma>1
.
Later,
Li et al. 2004
proved the local existence when
R
is taken in the whole space and under certain growth conditions on the potential.
Also,
Jourdain et al. 2004
analyzed the local
existence of a stochastic differential equation with potential
\mathcal{U}(R)=-k\log(1-|R|^{2})
and established local existence for Couette flow, under the assumption
b=2k>6
.

Zhang and Zhang 2006
proved the local well-posedness of the three-dimensional FENE model in weighted Sobolev spaces for
b=2k>76
.

Masmoudi 2008
developed some novel Hardy-type inequalities to handle the singular term
\text{div}\,\tau
, and proved local well-posedness and global well-posedness under small assumption near the equilibrium for the FENE model when
b=2k>0
.
Also near the equilibrium,
Lin et al. 2007
proved global existence under certain constraints on the potential.
Regarding the global existence of weak solutions in
L^{2}
,
Masmoudi 2013
derived results based on some entropy conditions.
Later, Luo and Yin
[
11
,
12
]
overcame the Liouville problem and established the
L^{2}
decay for the FENE model.

On the other hand, the center-of-mass diffusion of polymers is a well-established physical phenomenon and has been extensively studied.
In this case, namely
\mu>0
and
\nu>0
, the global existence of weak solution of the FENE model incorporating center-of-mass diffusion was demonstrated in
[
3
]
.
Also,
Barrett and Süli 2008
introduced a “microscopic” cut-off function in the drag term of the Fokker-Planck equation and proved the existence of global-in-time weak solutions to a mollification model with a general class of spring-force potentials, including the FENE potential.
Subsequently,
Barrett and Süli 2007
removed the cut-off in
[
2
]
and extended the results to the case of bead-spring chain models.

Regarding the decay of the FENE dumbbell model near the steady state, numerous important results have been obtained when the velocity equation exhibits full dissipation.
In 2006,
Schonbek 2009
investigated the
L^{2}
decay of the velocity for the co-rotation FENE dumbbell model, and obtained the decay rate
(1+t)^{-\frac{N}{4}+\frac{1}{2}}
.
Luo and Yin
[
11
,
12
]
enhanced the
L^{2}
decay results developed in
[
18
]
by Fourier methods, and obtained the optimal decay rates for the co-rotation case.
Lately,
Luo et al. 2024
refined the decay result in
[
11
]
for the general case and obtained the optimal decay rates.

In this paper, we explore the enhanced dissipation of the elastic FENE dumbbell model generated by the steady-state
(0,\psi_{\infty})
and the coupling and interaction. For this purpose, we set
\mu=0
and
\nu>0
. Also, we denote
\psi=\Psi-\psi_{\infty}
.
Since
\nabla_{x}\psi_{\infty}=0
, we have

\text{div}\,\tau=\text{div}\,\int_{B}(R\otimes\nabla_{R}\mathcal{U})\Psi\,\mathrm{d}R=\text{div}\,\int_{B}(R\otimes\nabla_{R}\mathcal{U})\psi\,\mathrm{d}R.

Hence, we may assume that

\tau=\int_{B}(R\otimes\nabla_{R}\mathcal{U})\psi\,\mathrm{d}R.

Now, we write
\psi_{0}:=\Psi_{0}-\psi_{\infty}
, and the equation governing the perturbation
(u,\psi)
of
N
-dimensional elastic FENE dumbbell model reads as follows

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+(u\cdot\nabla)u=-\nabla p+\text{div}\,\tau,\quad\text{div}\,u=0,\\
\partial_{t}\psi+u\cdot\nabla\psi=\nu\Delta\psi+\text{div}_{R}\left[-\nabla u\cdot R(\psi+\psi_{\infty})\right]+\mathcal{L}\psi,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0},\quad\psi(x,R,0)=\psi_{0},\\
\psi_{\infty}\nabla_{R}\frac{\psi}{\psi_{\infty}}\cdot n=0\quad\text{on}\ \partial B(0,1).\end{cases}

(2)

This paper comprehensively investigates (
2
), focusing on the small data global existence and stability, and the decay estimates. The first main result reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
k>1
,
s>\frac{N}{2}+1
, there exists a small constant
\varepsilon>0
such that for
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
\text{div}\,u_{0}=0
and
\psi_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathcal{L}^{2})
, if

||u_{0}||_{s}+||\psi_{0}||_{s,\mathcal{L}^{2}}\leq\varepsilon,

(3)

then (
2
) has a unique global solution
(u,\psi)
.
In addition, for all
t>0
, the following estimates hold:

\displaystyle||u(t)||_{s}^{2}+||\psi(t)||_{s,\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\Big\{||\nabla u(s)||_{s-1}^{2}+\nu||\nabla\psi(s)||_{s,\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}+||\psi(s)||_{s,{{\dot{\mathcal{H}}}^{1}}}^{2}\Big\}\,\mathrm{d}s\leq C\varepsilon^{2}.

Remark 1.1
.

Without the coupling of the polymer flows, (
2
) reduces the incompressible Euler equations, which is not stable even in 2-dimensions
[
8
]
. Hence, the enhanced dissipation induced by the equilibrium and by the coupling and interaction plays a crucial role in ensuring the stability.

Next, we demonstrate the importance of the enhanced dissipation in the study of decay estimates of (
2
).

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
hold, with
\varepsilon
chosen to be smaller if necessary.
Let the initial data
(u_{0},\psi_{0})
also satisfy

||u_{0}||_{L^{1}}+||\psi_{0}||_{L^{1}({{\mathcal{L}}^{2}})}\leq\varepsilon.

Then the following decay estimates hold:

\displaystyle||u(t)||_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon C(1+t)^{-\frac{N}{4}},

(4)

\displaystyle||\psi(t)||_{1,{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon C(1+t)^{-\frac{N+2}{4}}.

Remark 1.2
.

The obtained velocity decay rate aligns with that of the viscoelastic FENE dumbbell models, namely (
1
) with
\mu>0
and
\nu=0

[
13
]
.
Also, due to the enhanced dissipation, the velocity governed by this forced incompressible Euler equation exhibits the same decay rates as the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations.
We suggest that this decay rate is optimal, as it coincides with that of the heat equation.

1.1.
Main difficulties and strategies

In this part, we explain the main difficulties we encountered during our proofs and our main strategies.

1.1.1.
Global existence and stability

In this part, a major obstacle arises from the absence of the velocity dissipation. This greatly complicates the analysis and makes the issues of global existence and stability non-trivial.

Without coupling the equation of polymer density, (
2
) becomes the Euler equation:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+(u\cdot\nabla)u=-\nabla p,\\
\text{div}\,u=0,\end{cases}

(5)

and it is well-known that the above system is not stable for
N\geq 2
.
For this reason, to obtain the stability result of the elastic FENE dumbbell model, we do not expect the energy estimate could be closed only by the following conventional
H^{s}
energy structure

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{1}(t)=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0\leq s\leq t}||u(s)||_{s}^{2}+\sup\limits_{0\leq s\leq t}||\psi(s)||_{s,{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\Big\{\nu||\nabla\psi(s)||_{s,{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}}}^{2}+||\psi(s)||_{s,{{\dot{\mathcal{H}}}^{1}}}^{2}\Big\}\,\mathrm{d}s,

but also need to explore the enhanced dissipation of (
2
) and discover the additional regularity of
u
.

The enhanced dissipation of (
2
) comes from the equilibrium, and the coupling and interaction. To better illustrate the wave structure of (
2
), we consider the two-dimensional case.
Let
\mathbb{P}=I-\nabla\Delta^{-1}\text{div}\,
denote the Leray projection onto divergence free vector fields. By applying the Leray projection
\mathbb{P}
to the first equation of (
2
) to eliminate the pressure term, we obtain

\partial_{t}u=\mathbb{P}\text{div}\,\tau+\mathcal{N}_{1},\quad\mathcal{N}_{1}=-\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla u).

(6)

Now, by integrating the second equation of (
2
) with
R\otimes\nabla_{R}\mathcal{U}
, and applying
\mathbb{P}\text{div}\,
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbb{P}\text{div}\,\tau=

\displaystyle\nu\Delta\mathbb{P}\text{div}\,\tau+\mathbb{P}\text{div}\,\int_{B}\mathcal{L}\psi R\otimes\nabla_{R}\mathcal{U}\,\mathrm{d}R

(7)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}\text{div}\,\int_{B}\text{div}_{R}(-\nabla u\cdot R\psi_{\infty})R\otimes\nabla_{R}\mathcal{U}\,\mathrm{d}R+\mathcal{N}_{2},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{2}=

\displaystyle-\mathbb{P}\text{div}\,\left\{u\cdot\nabla\int_{B}\psi R\otimes\nabla_{R}\mathcal{U}\,\mathrm{d}R\right\}

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}\text{div}\,\int_{B}\text{div}_{R}(-\nabla u\cdot R\psi)R\otimes\nabla_{R}\mathcal{U}\,\mathrm{d}R.

Using the axisymmetric of
\psi_{\infty}
and
\text{div}\,u=0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\text{div}\,\int_{B}\text{div}_{R}(-\nabla u\cdot R\psi_{\infty})R\otimes\nabla_{R}\mathcal{U}\,\mathrm{d}R

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\begin{pmatrix}c_{1}(k)\partial_{1}^{2}u_{1}+2c_{2}(k)\partial_{1}\partial_{2}u_{2}+c_{2}(k)\partial_{2}^{2}u_{2}\\
c_{2}(k)\partial_{1}^{2}u_{2}+2c_{2}(k)\partial_{1}\partial_{2}u_{1}+c_{1}(k)\partial_{2}^{2}u_{2}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\begin{pmatrix}c_{1}(k)\partial_{1}^{2}u_{1}-2c_{2}(k)\partial_{1}^{2}u_{1}+c_{2}(k)\partial_{2}^{2}u_{2}\\
c_{2}(k)\partial_{1}^{2}u_{2}-2c_{2}(k)\partial_{2}^{2}u_{2}+c_{1}(k)\partial_{2}^{2}u_{2}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{2}(k)\Delta u,

where

c_{1}(k)=-2k\int_{B}\frac{R_{1}^{3}\partial_{R_{1}}\psi_{\infty}}{1-|R|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}R,\quad c_{2}(k)=-2k\int_{B}\frac{R_{1}^{2}R_{2}\partial_{R_{2}}\psi_{\infty}}{1-|R|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}R,

and
c_{1}(k)
,
c_{2}(k)
are integrable if
k>1
, and satisfy

c_{1}(k)=3c_{2}(k)>0
.

Hence, by differentiating (
6
) and (
7
) in time and making several substitutions, with notation
\Upsilon=\mathbb{P}\text{div}\,\tau
, we obtain that

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}u-\nu\partial_{t}\Delta u-c_{2}(k)\Delta u+\mathbb{P}\text{div}\,\int_{B}\mathcal{L}\psi R\otimes\nabla_{R}\mathcal{U}\,\mathrm{d}R=\mathcal{N}_{3},\\
\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}\Upsilon-\nu\partial_{t}\Delta\Upsilon-c_{2}(k)\Delta\Upsilon-\partial_{t}\mathbb{P}\text{div}\,\int_{B}\mathcal{L}\psi R\otimes\nabla_{R}\mathcal{U}\,\mathrm{d}R=\mathcal{N}_{4},\end{cases}

(8)

where

\mathcal{N}_{3}=(\partial_{t}-\nu\Delta)\mathcal{N}_{1}+\mathcal{N}_{2},\quad\mathcal{N}_{4}=c_{2}(k)\Delta\mathcal{N}_{1}+\partial_{t}\mathcal{N}_{2}.

In particular, (
8
) brings in the enhanced dissipation of
u
, which seems to be missing in the original (
2
). More precisely, thanks to the Laplacian in the wave structure (
8
), we can extract the extra regularity of time integral of
\nabla u
, this makes it promising to give the uniform bound of the following energy structure:

\mathcal{E}_{2}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}||\nabla u(s)||_{s-1}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s.

In addition to understanding the enhanced dissipation of
u
from the wave structure, there is another simple way to derive (
1.1.1
) without introducing the singularity.
In fact, by integrating the second equation of (
2
) with
R_{k}R_{l}
, where
k,l=1,\cdots,N
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B}\partial_{t}\psi R_{k}R_{l}\,\mathrm{d}R+\int_{B}u\cdot\nabla\psi R_{k}R_{l}\,\mathrm{d}R

(9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu\int_{B}\Delta\psi R_{k}R_{l}\,\mathrm{d}R+\int_{B}\text{div}_{R}(\psi_{\infty}\nabla_{R}\frac{\psi}{\psi_{\infty}})R_{k}R_{l}\,\mathrm{d}R

\displaystyle+\int_{B}\text{div}_{R}(-\nabla u\cdot R\psi)R_{k}R_{l}\,\mathrm{d}R

\displaystyle+\int_{B}\text{div}_{R}(-\nabla u\cdot R\psi_{\infty})R_{k}R_{l}\,\mathrm{d}R.

Due to the symmetry of
\psi_{\infty}
, we deduce that the last term of (
9
) is equivalent to a constant
\mathcal{C}
times
\mathbb{D}u
:

\displaystyle\int_{B}\text{div}_{R}(-\nabla u\cdot R\psi_{\infty})R_{k}R_{l}\,\mathrm{d}R=\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{N}\int_{B}\partial_{j}u_{i}R_{j}\psi_{\infty}\partial_{R_{i}}(R_{k}R_{l})\,\mathrm{d}R

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\int_{B}\left\{\partial_{j}u_{k}R_{j}\psi_{\infty}R_{l}+\partial_{j}u_{l}R_{j}\psi_{\infty}R_{k}\right\}\,\mathrm{d}R=\Big(2\int_{B}R_{1}^{2}\psi_{\infty}\,\mathrm{d}R\Big)[\mathbb{D}u]_{k,l},

where
[\mathbb{D}u]_{k,l}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{k}u_{l}+\partial_{l}u_{k})
.
By plugging the above equation into (
9
), we find

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}[\mathbb{D}u]_{j,k}=

\displaystyle\int_{B}\partial_{t}\psi R_{k}R_{l}\,\mathrm{d}R+\int_{B}u\cdot\nabla\psi R_{k}R_{l}\,\mathrm{d}R

(10)

\displaystyle-\nu\int_{B}\Delta\psi R_{j}R_{k}\,\mathrm{d}R-\int_{B}\text{div}_{R}(\psi_{\infty}\nabla_{R}\frac{\psi}{\psi_{\infty}})R_{j}R_{k}\,\mathrm{d}R

\displaystyle-\int_{B}\text{div}_{R}(-\nabla u\cdot R\psi)R_{j}R_{k}\,\mathrm{d}R.

Since
\text{div}\,u=0
, we have
||\mathbb{D}u||_{s}=||\nabla u||_{s}
.
Hence, the time integrability of
\nabla u
can be estimated by the terms on the right-hand side of (
10
). The reasoning above explains our strategy on how to prevent the growth of the Sobolev norms of velocity by exploiting the stabilizing effect of
\psi
on the fluid.

1.1.2.
Decay

The absence of velocity dissipation also causes significant difficulty when considering the decay rate, making it impossible to directly apply the Fourier splitting method.
To overcome this difficulty, we take advantage of the enhanced dissipation of the velocity, and combine (
10
) with the
L^{2}
-estimates.
However, the involvement of (
10
) induces linear terms with time-derivative, namely

\partial_{t}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\!\times\!{B}}(\psi R\otimes R):\mathbb{D}u\,\mathrm{d}R\,\mathrm{d}x.

To handle the above linear term, we innovatively introduce the
\dot{H}^{1}
-terms
and construct a two-layer estimate (see (
20
)). By combining (
20
) with Fourier splitting estimates, we obtain the decay rate that is identical to the heat equation.

3.
Global Existence

In this section, we Investigate the small data global existence and stability of the N-dimensional FENE dumbbell without velocity dissipation. Thanks to the enhanced dissipation of (
2
), we can discover the additional time integrability of
u
. Recall that we define the energy functionals as follows

\mathcal{E}_{1}(t)=\sup\limits_{0\leq s\leq t}||u(s)||_{s}^{2}+\sup\limits_{0\leq s\leq t}||\psi(s)||_{s,\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}+\nu\int_{0}^{t}\Big\{||\nabla\psi(s)||_{s,\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}+||\psi(s)||_{s,{{\dot{\mathcal{H}}}^{1}}}^{2}\Big\}\,\mathrm{d}s,

\mathcal{E}_{2}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}||\nabla u(s)||_{s-1}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s.

proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By the bootstrap argument (see e.g. P.21 of
[
19
]
), it suffices to prove the following two inequalities:

\mathcal{E}_{1}(t)\leq\mathcal{E}_{1}(0)+C\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\frac{3}{2}}(t)+C\mathcal{E}_{2}^{\frac{3}{2}}(t),

(11)

and

\mathcal{E}_{2}(t)\leq C\mathcal{E}_{1}(0)+C\mathcal{E}_{1}(t)+C\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\frac{3}{2}}(t)+C\mathcal{E}_{2}^{\frac{3}{2}}(t).

(12)

We begin with (
11
). By standard
L^{2}
energy method, we deduce from integrating by parts that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}(||u||^{2}+||\psi||_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}}^{2})+\nu||\nabla\psi||_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}+||\psi||_{{{\dot{\mathcal{H}}}^{1}}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

) in frequency space:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\hat{u}+\mathcal{F}(u\cdot\nabla u)+i\xi\hat{P}=i\xi\cdot\hat{\tau},\\
\partial_{t}\hat{\psi}+\mathcal{F}(u\cdot\nabla\psi)+\nu|\xi|^{2}\hat{\psi}+\mathcal{L}\hat{\psi}=\text{div}_{R}[-\mathcal{F}(\nabla u\cdot R(\psi+\psi_{\infty}))],\\
i\xi\cdot\hat{u}=0.\end{cases}

(22)

By integrating (
22
) with
(\bar{\hat{u}},\bar{\hat{\psi}})
and subtracting the real parts, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\big\{|\hat{u}|^{2}+|\hat{\psi}|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}\big\}+\nu|\xi|^{2}|\hat{\psi}|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}+|\hat{\psi}|_{{{\dot{\mathcal{H}}}^{1}}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathcal{R}e[\mathcal{F}(u\cdot\nabla u)\cdot\bar{\hat{u}}]-\mathcal{R}e\int_{B}\mathcal{F}(u\cdot\nabla\psi)\bar{\hat{\psi}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}R}{\psi_{\infty}}-\mathcal{R}e\int_{B}\text{div}_{R}\mathcal{F}(\nabla u\cdot R\psi)\bar{\hat{\psi}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}R}{\psi_{\infty}}.

By H
\ddot{\text{o}}
lder’s inequality,

\displaystyle-\mathcal{R}e\int_{B}\mathcal{F}(u\cdot\nabla\psi)\bar{\hat{\psi}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}R}{\psi_{\infty}}-\mathcal{R}e\int_{B}\text{div}_{R}\mathcal{F}(\nabla u\cdot R\psi)\bar{\hat{\psi}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}R}{\psi_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}|\hat{\psi}|_{{{\dot{\mathcal{H}}}^{1}}}^{2}+C\int_{B}|\mathcal{F}(u\cdot\nabla\psi)|^{2}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}R}{\psi_{\infty}}+C\int_{B}|\mathcal{F}(\nabla u\cdot R\psi)|^{2}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}R}{\psi_{\infty}},

which implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\big\{|\hat{u}|^{2}+|\hat{\psi}|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}\big\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\mathcal{R}e(\mathcal{F}(u\cdot\nabla u)\cdot\bar{\hat{u}})|+C\int_{B}|\mathcal{F}(u\cdot\nabla\psi)|^{2}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}R}{\psi_{\infty}}+C\int_{B}|\mathcal{F}(\nabla u\cdot R\psi)|^{2}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}R}{\psi_{\infty}}.

Integrating the above inequality in
S(t)
and in time yields

\displaystyle\int_{S(t)}|\hat{u}(t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi+\int_{S(t)}|\hat{\psi}(t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

(23)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{S(t)}|\hat{u}(0)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi+\int_{S(t)}|\hat{\psi}(0)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi+K_{1}+K_{2}+K_{3},

where

\displaystyle K_{1}:=

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{S(t)}\int_{B}|\mathcal{F}(u\cdot\nabla\psi)|^{2}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}R}{\psi_{\infty}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle K_{2}:=

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{S(t)}\int_{B}|\mathcal{F}(\nabla u\cdot R\psi)|^{2}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}R}{\psi_{\infty}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle K_{3}:=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{S(t)}|\mathcal{R}e(\mathcal{F}(u\cdot\nabla u)\cdot\bar{\hat{u}})|\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}s.

For the initial data, we take advantage of the definition of
S(t)
to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{S(t)}|\hat{u}(0)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi+\int_{S(t)}|\hat{\psi}(0)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{S(t)}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\big\{||u(0)||_{L^{1}}^{2}+||\psi(0)||_{L^{1}({{\mathcal{L}}^{2}})}^{2}\big\}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\frac{2s}{a\mathcal{C}}(\eta+t)}}r^{N-1}\,\mathrm{d}r\big\{||u(0)||_{L^{1}}^{2}+||\psi(0)||_{L^{1}({{\mathcal{L}}^{2}})}^{2}\big\}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(\eta+t)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\big\{||u(0)||_{L^{1}}^{2}+||\psi(0)||_{L^{1}({{\mathcal{L}}^{2}})}^{2}\big\}.

For
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
, we use Theorem
1.1
to obtain

\displaystyle K_{1}\leq

\displaystyle\int_{S(t)}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\int_{0}^{t}||u\cdot\nabla\psi||_{L^{1}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}R}{\psi_{\infty}}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{S(t)}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\int_{0}^{t}||u||^{2}||\nabla\psi||_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\lesssim\varepsilon^{4}(\eta+t)^{-\frac{N}{2}},

\displaystyle K_{2}\leq

\displaystyle\int_{S(t)}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\int_{0}^{t}||\nabla u\cdot R\psi||_{L^{1}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}R}{\psi_{\infty}}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{S(t)}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\int_{0}^{t}||\nabla u||^{2}||\psi||_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\lesssim\varepsilon^{4}(\eta+t)^{-\frac{N}{2}}.

For
K_{3}
, we use Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality and Theorem
1.1
to obtain

\displaystyle K_{3}\leq 2\int_{0}^{t}||\Lambda^{-1}(u\cdot\nabla u)||\,||\nabla u||\,\mathrm{d}s\leq C\int_{0}^{t}||u\cdot\nabla u||_{L^{6/5}}||\nabla u||\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\varepsilon Ct||\nabla u||^{2}.

By substituting the above estimates into (
23
), we find

\int_{S(t)}|\hat{u}(t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi+\int_{S(t)}|\hat{\psi}(t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\leq C\varepsilon^{4}(\eta+t)^{-\frac{N}{2}}+\varepsilon Ct||\nabla u||^{2}.

Plugging the above inequality into (
21
) and choosing
\varepsilon\leq\frac{a\mathcal{C}}{2C}
yields

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\big\{d(t)f(t)\big\}\leq\varepsilon^{4}Cd^{\prime}(t)(\eta+t)^{-\frac{N}{2}}.

Since

f\sim||u||_{1}^{2}+||\psi||_{1,{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}}}^{2}
,
by integrating the above inequality in time, we obtain that

||u||_{1}^{2}+||\psi||_{1,{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}}}^{2}\leq\varepsilon^{2}C(\eta+t)^{-\frac{N}{2}}.

Next, we take advantage of the higher-order decay to improve the decay rate of
||\psi||_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}}
.
By the argument parallel to the
L^{2}
case, it is not difficult to deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\Big\{d(t)\Big\{||\nabla u||_{1}^{2}+||\nabla\psi||_{1,{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}}}^{2}-2a\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\!\times\!{B}}\nabla(\psi R\otimes R):\nabla\mathbb{D}u\,\mathrm{d}R\,\mathrm{d}x\Big\}\Big\}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle d^{\prime}(t)\int_{S(t)}|\xi|^{2}|\hat{u}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}d^{\prime}(t)(\eta+t)^{-1}(\eta+t)^{-\frac{N}{2}}.

Integrating the above inequality yields that

||\nabla u||_{1}^{2}+||\nabla\psi||_{1,{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}}}^{2}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}(\eta+t)^{-\frac{N}{2}-1}.

(24)

By the standard
L^{2}
-estimate of
\psi
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}||\psi||_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}+\nu||\nabla\psi||_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}+||\psi||_{{{\dot{\mathcal{H}}}^{1}}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\!\times\!{B}}\text{div}_{R}(-\nabla u\cdot R\psi)\psi\,\frac{\mathrm{d}R}{\psi_{\infty}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\!\times\!{B}}\text{div}_{R}(-\nabla u\cdot R\psi_{\infty})\psi\,\frac{\mathrm{d}R}{\psi_{\infty}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|||\nabla u|||\,||\psi||_{{{\dot{\mathcal{H}}}^{1}}}^{2}+C||\nabla u||^{2}+\frac{1}{4}||\psi||_{{{\dot{\mathcal{H}}}^{1}}}^{2}.

Combining the above estimates with Theorem
1.1
and (
24
) yields that

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}||\psi||_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}+||\psi||_{{{\dot{\mathcal{H}}}^{1}}}^{2}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}(\eta+t)^{-\frac{N}{2}-1},

which implies

||\psi||_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq\varepsilon^{2}C(\eta+t)^{-\frac{N}{2}-1}.

∎

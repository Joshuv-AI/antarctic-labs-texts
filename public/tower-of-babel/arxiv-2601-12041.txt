Rafał Filipów, Małgorzata Kowalczuk, Hubert Ksia̧żek, Adam Kwela, Grzegorz Ucal

Abstract
. We study ideal-based refinements of sequential compactness arising from the class FinBW ⁡ ( ℐ ) \FinBW(\mathcal{I}) , consisting of topological spaces in which every sequence admits a convergent subsequence indexed by a set outside a given ideal ℐ \mathcal{I} . A central theme of this work is the existence of critical ideals whose position in the Katětov order determines the relationship between a fixed class of spaces and the corresponding FinBW ⁡ ( ℐ ) \FinBW(\mathcal{I}) classes. Building on earlier results characterizing several classical topological classes via such ideals, we extend this theory to a broader framework based on partition regular functions, which unifies ordinary convergence with other non-classical convergence notions such as IP- and Ramsey-type convergence. Furthermore, we investigate the existence of critical ideals associated with function classes motivated by Mazurkiewicz’s theorem on uniformly convergent subsequences.

1.
Introduction

See Section
2
for notions and notations used in the introduction.

In recent years, various ideal-based refinements of classical convergence and topological properties have been intensively studied. One such notion is the class
\FinBW(\mathcal{I})
(associated to an ideal
\mathcal{I}
on
\omega
) which consists of all topological spaces
X
having the property that for every sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\omega}
in
X
there exists
A\notin\mathcal{I}
such that the subsequence
(x_{n})_{n\in A}
is convergent in
X
(
[
14
, Definition 1.1]
).
For instance,
\FinBW(\mathsf{Fin})
coincides with the class of all sequentially compact spaces, where
\mathsf{Fin}
is the ideal of all finite subsets of
\omega
.

A natural problem in this area is to determine whether a given class of topological spaces can be characterized as a
\FinBW(\mathcal{I})
for a suitable ideal
\mathcal{I}
.

A particularly interesting question concerns the existence of a
critical ideal

\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{C}}
for a given class
\mathcal{C}
of topological spaces with the following property:
the position of a given ideal
\mathcal{J}
relative to
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{C}}
in the Katětov order determines the relationship between the classes
\mathcal{C}
and
\FinBW(\mathcal{J})
.
Problems of this type have already been investigated in the literature, see for instance

[
4
]
,
[
14
]
and

[
16
]
.
Some of these results are as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

•

\mathsf{Fin}\otimes\mathsf{Fin}\leq_{K}\mathcal{I}

\iff

\FinBW(\mathcal{I})
coincides with the class of all finite spaces.

•

The following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

\mathsf{BI}\leq_{K}\mathcal{I}
and
\mathsf{Fin}\otimes\mathsf{Fin}\not\leq_{K}\mathcal{I}
.

(2)

\FinBW(\mathcal{I})
coincides with the class of all boring spaces.

•

\mathsf{conv}\not\leq_{K}\mathcal{I}

\iff

\FinBW(\mathcal{I})
coincides with the class of all compact metric spaces in the realm of metric spaces.

The definition of
\FinBW(\mathcal{I})
is based on the notion of ordinary convergence. However, there exist other types of convergence that fall outside this classical framework. Examples include convergences arising in ergodic theory and other areas, for instance,
IP-convergence
(introduced by Furstenberg and Weiss
[
9
]
)
and

R-convergence
(considered in
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
and
[
10
]
).
Such convergences have been employed, for example, in
[
12
]
,
[
11
]
and
[
13
]
to define some classes of topological spaces, namely
Hindman spaces
and
Ramsey spaces
.
In general, these forms of convergence cannot be adequately described using the language of ordinary convergence alone.

More recently, in the paper
[
5
]
, the authors introduced a unifying framework (
partition regular functions
) which, in one formulation, can be interpreted in terms of ordinary convergence, while simultaneously encompassing these other, non-ordinary types of convergence.

Motivated by these developments, the aim of the present paper is to generalize the results of
Theorem
1.1
for partition regular functions. This task is addressed in Sections
3
,
4

and
6
.

In the final section, we address the problem of identifying the critical ideal for the class of functions possessing the property motivated by Mazurkiewicz’s theorem
[
15
]
, which states that every uniformly bounded sequence of continuous functions has a uniformly convergent subsequence once restricted to some perfect set.

2.
Preliminaries

All topological spaces considered in the paper are assumed to be Hausdorff.

Recall that an ordinal number
\alpha
is equal to the set of all ordinal numbers less than
\alpha
.
In particular, the smallest infinite ordinal number
\omega=\{0,1,\dots\}
is equal to the set of all natural numbers
\mathbb{N}
, and each natural number
n=\{0,\dots,n-1\}
is equal to the set of all natural numbers less than

Using this identification, we can, for instance, write
n\in k
instead of
n<k
,
n<\omega
instead of
n\in\omega
, or
A\cap n
instead of
A\cap\{0,1,\dots,n-1\}
.

We write

[A]^{<\omega}
to denote the family of all finite subsets of
A
,

[A]^{\omega}
to denote the family of all infinite countable subsets of
A

and

\mathcal{P}(A)
to denote the family of all subsets of
A
.
We say that
\mathcal{A}\subseteq[\omega]^{\omega}

is an
almost
disjoint family on
\omega
if

is finite for any distinct
A,B\in\mathcal{A}
.

2.1.
Ideals and partition regular functions

An
ideal
on a nonempty set
X
is a family
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X)
that satisfies the following properties:

(1)

\emptyset\in\mathcal{I}
and
X\not\in\mathcal{I}
;

(2)

if
A,B\in\mathcal{I}
then
A\cup B\in\mathcal{I}
;

(3)

if
A\subseteq B
and
B\in\mathcal{I}
then
A\in\mathcal{I}
;

(4)

\mathcal{I}
contains all finite subsets of
X
.

For an ideal
\mathcal{I}
, we write
\mathcal{I}^{+}=\{A\subseteq X:A\notin\mathcal{I}\}
and call it the
coideal of
\mathcal{I}
.
Sets belonging to
\mathcal{I}^{+}
will be called
\mathcal{I}
-positive
sets.
An ideal
\mathcal{I}
is
tall
(also called
dense
) if for every infinite
A\subseteq X
there is an infinite
B\in\mathcal{I}
such that
B\subseteq A
.

Example 2.1
.

(1)

\mathsf{Fin}
is the ideal of all finite subsets of
\omega
.

(2)

\mathsf{Fin}^{2}=\mathsf{Fin}\otimes\mathsf{Fin}
is the ideal on
\omega^{2}=\omega\times\omega
defined by

A\in\mathsf{Fin}^{2}\iff\exists i_{0}\in\omega\,\forall i\geq i_{0}\,(|\{j\in\omega:(i,j)\in A\}|<\omega).

(3)

\mathsf{BI}
is the ideal on
\omega^{3}=\omega\times\omega\times\omega

introduced in
[
14
, Definition 4.1]

and defined by

\begin{split}A\in\mathsf{BI}\iff\exists i_{0}\in\omega\,\left[\right.&\forall i<i_{0}\,(\,\{(j,k)\in\omega^{2}:(i,j,k)\in A\}\in\mathsf{Fin}^{2})\ \land\\
&\left.\hskip-3.0pt\forall i\geq i_{0}\,(|\{(j,k)\in\omega^{2}:(i,j,k)\in A\}|<\omega)\right].\end{split}

The ideal
\mathsf{BI}
is isomorphic to the ideal
\mathcal{WT}
introduced in
[
3
]
, where the authors showed its connection with weakly tight almost disjoint families.

(4)

\mathsf{conv}
is the ideal on

consisting of all subsets of
\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]
which can be covered by ranges of finitely many sequences in
\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]
which are convergent in
[0,1]
.

Definition 2.2

(
[
5
]
)
.

Let
\Lambda
and
\Omega
be countable infinite sets.
Let
\mathcal{F}
be a nonempty family of infinite subsets of
\Omega
such that
F\setminus K\in\mathcal{F}
for every
F\in\mathcal{F}
and every finite set
K\subseteq\Omega
.
We say that a function
\rho\colon\mathcal{F}\to[\Lambda]^{\omega}

is
partition regular
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(M):

\forall E,F\in\mathcal{F}\,\left(E\subseteq F\implies\rho(E)\subseteq\rho(F)\right)
;

(R):

\forall F\in\mathcal{F}\,\forall A,B\subseteq\Lambda\,(\rho(F)=A\cup B\implies\exists E\in\mathcal{F}\,(\rho(E)\subseteq A\lor\rho(E)\subseteq B));

(S):

\forall E\in\mathcal{F}\,\exists F\in\mathcal{F}\,(F\subseteq E\land\forall a\in\rho(F)\,\exists K\in[\Omega]^{<\omega}\,(a\notin\rho(F\setminus K)))
.

The following proposition reveals relationships between partition
regular functions and ideals.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
5
, Proposition 3.3]
)
.

(1)

If
\rho\colon\mathcal{F}\to[\Lambda]^{\omega}
is partition regular then

\mathcal{I}_{\rho}=\{A\subseteq\Lambda:\forall F\in\mathcal{F}\,(\rho(F)\not\subseteq A)\}

is an ideal on
\Lambda
.

(2)

If
\mathcal{I}
is an ideal on
\Lambda
then
the function

\rho_{\mathcal{I}}\colon\mathcal{I}^{+}\to[\Lambda]^{\omega}
given by
\rho_{\mathcal{I}}(A)=A

is partition regular and
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}_{\rho_{\mathcal{I}}}.

The above proposition shows that every ideal can be coded using partition regular functions, namely, for an ideal
\mathcal{I}
we have a partition regular function
\rho=\rho_{\mathcal{I}}
with
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}_{\rho}
. On the other hand, not every partition regular function can be coded using ideals.
For instance,
\FS\neq\rho_{\mathcal{I}}
for any ideal
\mathcal{I}
(
[
5
, Propositions 6.5 and 6.7]
), where
the partition regular function

\FS:[\omega]^{\omega}\to[\omega]^{\omega}
is given by

\FS(D)=\{d_{0}+\dots+d_{n}:n\in\omega,d_{0},\dots,d_{n}\in D,d_{i}\neq d_{j}\text{for }i\neq j\},

and the ideal

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{I}_{\FS}=\{A\subseteq\omega:\forall D\in[\omega]^{\omega}\,(\FS(D)\not\subseteq A)\}

is called
the Hindman ideal

[
8
, p. 109]
(see also
[
12
]
).

A set
F\in\mathcal{F}
has
small accretions
if

\rho(F)\setminus\rho(F\setminus K)\in\mathcal{I}_{\rho}
for every finite set
K
. We say that
\rho
has
small accretions
if for every
E\in\mathcal{F}
, there is
F\in\mathcal{F}
with
F\subseteq E
such that
F
has small accretions.

2.3.
Katětov order

Let
\mathcal{I}_{1}
and
\mathcal{I}_{2}
be ideals on
X
and
Y
, respectively.
We say that
\mathcal{I}_{1}
is above
\mathcal{I}_{2}
in the Katětov order
(in short:
\mathcal{I}_{2}\leq_{K}\mathcal{I}_{1}
) if
there exists a function
f\colon X\to Y

such that
f[A]\in\mathcal{I}_{2}^{+}
for each
A\in\mathcal{I}_{1}^{+}
.

Let
\rho_{i}\colon\mathcal{F}_{i}\to[\Lambda_{i}]^{\omega}

be partition regular with
\mathcal{F}_{i}\subseteq[\Omega_{i}]^{\omega}
for each
i=1,2
.
We say that
\rho_{1}
is above
\rho_{2}
in the Katětov order

(in short:
\rho_{2}\leq_{K}\rho_{1}
)
if there is a function
f\colon\Lambda_{1}\to\Lambda_{2}

such that

\forall F_{1}\in\mathcal{F}_{1}\,\exists F_{2}\in\mathcal{F}_{2}\,\forall K_{1}\in[\Omega_{1}]^{<\omega}\,\exists K_{2}\in[\Omega_{2}]^{<\omega}\,(\rho_{2}(F_{2}\setminus K_{2})\subseteq f[\rho_{1}(F_{1}\setminus K_{1})]).

We write
\rho_{2}\approx_{K}\rho_{1}
if both
\rho_{2}\leq_{K}\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{1}\leq_{K}\rho_{2}
hold.

2.5.
Some classes of topological spaces

A sequentially compact space

(
[

such that each one-to-one sequence in

For an ideal
\mathcal{I}
on

to denote the class of all topological spaces

such that for every sequence

such that

We say that an ideal

.

For a partition regular function
\rho\colon\mathcal{F}\to[\Lambda]^{\omega}
,
we define

to be the class of all topological spaces

such that for every sequence

there exists

such that

\rho
-converges

.

7.
Ideal version of Mazurkiewicz theorem

Mazurkiewicz’s theorem
[
15
]
states that every uniformly bounded sequence of continuous functions has a uniformly convergent subsequence once restricted to some perfect set.
The question whether the subsequence can be enumerated by
\mathcal{I}
-positive set for a given ideal
\mathcal{I}
, was investigated in
[
7
]
where the authors proved that the answer is positive for
F_{\sigma}
-ideals.
The following theorem shows that this result can be extended to every ideal with the
\FinBW
property which in turn means that the ideal
\mathsf{conv}
is critical for the ideal version of Mazurkiewicz’s theorem.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an ideal on
\omega

\mathcal{I}

\FinBW

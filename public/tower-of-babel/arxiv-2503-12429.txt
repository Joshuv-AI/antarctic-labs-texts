Abstract
. Let n n be a non-negative integer. Motivated by the universal property of the stable category of Frobenius categories, the authors in [ 3 ] generalized the stabilization of Frobenius categories to n n -Frobenius categories, defining the phantom stable category. For an n n -Frobenius category 𝒞 \mathscr{C} , this consists of a pair ( 𝒞 𝒫 , T ) (\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}},T) , where 𝒞 𝒫 \mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}} is an additive category having the same objects as 𝒞 \mathscr{C} and T : 𝒞 → 𝒞 𝒫 T:\mathscr{C}\rightarrow\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}} an additive covariant functor that vanishes on n n - 𝖤𝗑𝗍 \operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}} -phantom morphisms and sends n n - 𝖤𝗑𝗍 \operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}} -invertible morphisms to isomorphisms, and T T has the universal property with respect to these conditions. The existence of the phantom stable category ( 𝒞 𝒫 , T ) (\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}},T) and its several interesting properties have appeared in [ 3 ] . In this paper, we show that the syzygy functor Ω \mathsf{\Omega} , constructed from n n -projectives, from 𝒞 \mathscr{C} to 𝒞 𝒫 \mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}} is not only an additive functor, but also it induces an auto-equivalence functor 𝝮 \operatorname{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\Omega}}} on 𝒞 𝒫 \mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}} . Then, as the main result, it is proved that phantom stable category ( 𝒞 𝒫 , T ) (\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}},T) is triangulated, with 𝝮 \operatorname{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\Omega}}} serving as its shift functor.

1.
Introduction

The significance of Frobenius categories lies in their profound connection to triangulated categories. A fundamental result, due to Happel
[
17
]
, establishes that the stable category of a Frobenius category carries a natural triangulated structure, and in particular, most of the triangulated categories arise in this way, see for example
[
10
,
17
]
. Following Keller
[
28
]
, such categories are termed “algebraic triangulated” categories and are distinguished by their admitting a natural dg-enhancement in the sense of Bondal and Kapranov
[
9
]
. This makes them a more tractable and conceptually rich class than general triangulated categories.

These facts encouraged us to study the notion of
n
-Frobenius categories and make an attempt to extend the concept of the stabilization of Frobenius categories to
n
-Frobenius categories. Our principal focus in this direction is to consider the universal property for the stable category of a Frobenius category. In order to explain our idea more explicitly, we need to recall some notions. Assume that
\mathcal{A}
is an abelian category and
\mathscr{C}
is a full additive extension-closed subcategory of
\mathcal{A}
. The exact category
\mathscr{C}
is said to be an
n
-Frobenius category, with
n
a non-negative integer, if
\mathscr{C}
has enough
n
-projective and
n
-injective objects and these two classes of objects coincide, where
n
-projectivity and
n
-injectivity are defined via the vanishing of
(n+1)
-th Yoneda
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}
. Thus Frobenius categories are indeed 0-Frobenius categories in our sense. It is known that any abelian category with non-zero
n
-projective objects admits a non-trivial
n
-Frobenius subcategory, see
[
3
, Theorem 2.15]
.

Assume that
\mathscr{C}
is an
n
-Frobenius category. A given morphism
f:M\rightarrow N
in
\mathscr{C}
, is said to be an
n
-
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}
-phantom (resp. invertible) morphism, provided that the induced natural transformation
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{n+1}(-,f):\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}_{\mathscr{C}}^{n+1}(-,M)\longrightarrow\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{n+1}_{\mathscr{C}}(-,N)
vanishes (resp. is an equivalence of functors). Evidently, the class of all
n
-
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}
-phantom morphisms is an ideal of
\mathscr{C}
. It is established that if
\mathscr{C}
is a Frobenius category, then
0
-
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}
-phantom morphisms are precisely those morphisms that factor through projective objects, see Proposition
2.11
. Considering this fact together
[
26
, Theorem 1]
, we may revisit the stabilization of Frobenius categories, as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a Frobenius category. An additive category
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
equipped with a covariant additive functor
T:\mathscr{C}\rightarrow\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
is said to be the stable category of
\mathscr{C}
, if:

(1) for any
0
-
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}
-phantom morphism
f
in
\mathscr{C}
,
T(f)=0
in
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
;

(2) for any additive covariant functor
T^{\prime}:\mathscr{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
that satisfies the first assertion, there exists a unique additive covariant functor
F:\mathscr{C}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
such that
FT=T^{\prime}
.

By the idea of the above universal property, the authors in
[
3
]
have been able to extend the stabilization of Frobenius categories to
n
-Frobenius categories, which is called
the phantom stable category
. Precisely, we have the following.

Definition 1.2
.

(
[
3
, Definition 9.2]
) Assume that
\mathscr{C}
is an
n
-Frobenius category. A couple
(\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}},T)
, with
\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}}
an additive category and
T:\mathscr{C}\longrightarrow\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}}
an additive covariant functor, is said to be the
phantom stable category of
\mathscr{C}
, if:

(1) for any
n
-
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}
-phantom morphism
\varphi
,
T(\varphi)=0
in
\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}}
;

(2)
T(s)
is an isomorphism in
\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}}
, for any
n
-
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}
-invertible morphism
s
;

(3) any additive covariant functor
T^{\prime}:\mathscr{C}\longrightarrow\mathbb{D}
satisfying the conditions (1) and (2), factors in a unique way through
T
.

The existence of the phantom stable category
(\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}},T)
of an
n
-Frobenius category
\mathscr{C}
, has appeared in
[
3
, Theorem 9.5]
. Indeed,
\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}}
is an additive category with the same object of
\mathscr{C}
, and for every two objects
M,N\in\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}}
, the hom-set
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}}}(M,N)=(\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{n}_{\mathscr{C}}(M,\mathsf{\Omega}^{n}N)/{\mathcal{P}},\delta_{N})
modulo an equivalence relation, where
\delta_{N}
is a unit conflation of
N
of length
n
. Moreover, for each two objects
M,N\in\mathscr{C}
,

\mathcal{P}(M,N)
is the additive subgroup of
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{n}_{\mathscr{C}}(M,N)
consisting of all conflations
\operatorname{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
of length
n
, arising from a pull-back along a morphism
M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\rightarrow}}P
with
P

n
-projective. Furthermore, the addition of morphisms is defined via the Baer sum operation, while the composition is defined in terms of pull-back (or equivalently, push-out) diagrams. In addition,
T:\mathscr{C}\rightarrow\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}}
is an additive functor which is identical on objects and for a morphism
f:M\rightarrow N
in
\mathscr{C}
,
T(f)=(\overline{\delta_{N}f},\delta_{N})
(see Section 2 for the notations and the detailed construction of
\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}}
).

Since the stable category of a Frobenius category is triangulated, where the shift functor is given by the syzygy functor, a natural question arises: does the phantom stable category
(\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}},T)
of an
n
-Frobenius category
\mathscr{C}
admit a triangulated structure? The main goal of this paper is to answer this question affirmatively. Precisely,
we show that the syzygy functor
\mathsf{\Omega}
, defined using
n
-projective objects from
\mathscr{C}
to
\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}}
, provides an additive covariant functor that sends each
n
-
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}
-phantom (resp. invertible) morphism to zero (an isomorphism) in
\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}}
, see Theorem
3.3
. This, together with the universal property of the phantom stable category, induces an auto-equivalence functor
\operatorname{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\Omega}}}
on
\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}}
, which is again called the syzygy funcor, see Theorems
3.4
and
3.7
. As the main result of the paper, we prove that the phantom stable category
(\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}},T)
is a triangulated category, where the shift functor is given by
\operatorname{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\Omega}}}
. Moreover, the exact triangles are given up to isomorphism of triangles by
\operatorname{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\Omega}}}N\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T(g)}}{{\longrightarrow}}C\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T(h)}}{{\longrightarrow}}M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T(f)}}{{\longrightarrow}}N
, that arises from the pull-back of a syzygy sequence
\mathsf{\Omega}N\rightarrowtail Q\twoheadrightarrow N
with
Q\in n
-
\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\mathscr{C}
, along a morphism
f:M\rightarrow N
in
\mathscr{C}
, see Theorem
5.12
. At the end of the paper, we show that if
\mathscr{C}
is a Frobenius category, then its stabilization as a 0-Frobenius category,
(\operatorname{\underline{\mathscr{C}}},F)
, is triangle equivalent to the stabilization
(\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}},T)
of
\mathscr{C}
, viewed as an
n
-Frobeius category, where
n\geq 1
is an integer, see Theorem
5.14
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall the definition of
n
-Frobenius categories, provide several examples, and briefly outline the construction of the phantom stable category from
[
3
]
. Section 3 is devoted to defining the shift functor of the triangulated structure. Indeed, we show that the syzygy functor
\mathsf{\Omega}
, using
n
-projective objects, from
\mathscr{C}
to
\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}}
, induces an auto-equivalence functor
\operatorname{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{\Omega}}}
on
\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}}
. To verify that the phantom stable category is triangulated, we need to describe commutative diagrams in
\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}}
in terms of commutative diagrams in
\mathscr{C}
. This is carries out in Section 4. Finally, in Section 5, we prove that the phantom stable category
(\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}},T)
admits a triangulated structure.

Throughout the paper,
\mathcal{A}
is an abelian category and
\mathscr{C}
a full additive extension-closed
subcategory of
\mathcal{A}
. It is known that
\mathscr{C}
is an exact category, where conflations are precisely short exact sequences in
\mathcal{A}
whose terms are in
\mathscr{C}
, see
[
11
, Lemma 10.20]
. The integer
n\geq 0
is fixed.

2.
Phantom stable categories

This section is devoted to giving a brief description of the structure of the phantom stable category of an
n
-Frobenius category. First, we recall the definition of an
n
-Frobenius category and some of its properties from
[
3
]
. Also, several examples of
n
-Frobenius categories are presented.

2.1
.

Assume that
t\geq 1
is an integer. An extension
0\rightarrow N\rightarrow X_{t-1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow X_{0}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0
in
\mathcal{A}
, is said to be a conflation (of length
t
) in
\mathscr{C}
, if it is obtained by splicing
t
conflations in
\mathscr{C}
, and it will be denoted by
N\rightarrowtail X_{t-1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow X_{0}\twoheadrightarrow M
. So conflations are exactly conflations of length 1. For a given conflation
N\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\rightarrowtail}}Z\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\twoheadrightarrow}}M
in
\mathscr{C}
,
f
(resp.
g
) is called an inflation (resp. a deflation), see
[
28
]
. The set of all equivalence classes of conflations (of length
t
) of the form
N\rightarrowtail X_{t-1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow X_{0}\twoheadrightarrow M
, is denoted by
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{t}_{\mathscr{C}}(M,N)
, see for detail
[
34
, Chapter VII]
. We also set
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{0}_{\mathscr{C}}(-,-)=\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{\mathscr{C}}(-,-)
. For the sake of simplicity, in
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{t}_{\mathscr{C}}(-,-)
, the subscript
\mathscr{C}
will be suppressed. Furthermore, if there is no ambguity, we often write ‘a conflation’ instead of ‘a conflation of length
t
’.

Definition 2.2
.

Assume that
n
is a non-negative integer.

(1) A given object
P\in\mathscr{C}
(resp.
I\in\mathscr{C}
) is said to be an
n
-projective
(resp.
n
-injective
) object of
\mathscr{C}
, if
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{i}(P,X)=0
(resp.
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{i}(X,I)=0
) for all integers
i>n
and all objects
X\in\mathscr{C}
. The class of all
n
-projective (resp.
n
-injective) objects will be denoted by
n
-
\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\mathscr{C}
(resp.
n
-
\operatorname{\mathsf{inj}}\mathscr{C}
).

(2) The exact category
\mathscr{C}
is said to have enough
n
-projectives, provided that each object
M
in
\mathscr{C}
fits into a deflation
P\twoheadrightarrow M
where
P
is
n
-projective. Dually one has the notion of having enough
n
-injectives.

(3) The exact category
\mathscr{C}
is called
n
-Frobenius, if
it has enough
n
-projectives and
n
-injectives and
n
-
\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\mathscr{C}
coincides with
n
-
\operatorname{\mathsf{inj}}\mathscr{C}
.

It follows from the definition that 0-Frobenius categories are indeed Frobenius categories in the usual sense. Moreover, any
n
-Frobenius category is a
k
-Frobenius category, for all
k>n
.

Remark 2.3
.

It should be remarked that there are also other notions related to the Frobenius categories and their stable categories in
[
27
]
and
[
29
]
, where the notions of Frobenius
n
-exact categories and Frobenius
n
-exangulated categories, for each positive integer
n
, have been defined and studied. We point out that, if the base category is exact and
n=1
, then these notions recover the classical notion of a Frobenius exact category. So, in this case, these concepts coincide with the 0-Frobenius category in our sense.

We should emphasize that although the aforementioned three concepts are related to the classical notion of Frobenius exact categories, their ideas are totally different.
Indeed,
n
-exangulated categories defined in
[
21
,
22
]
, as a higher dimensional analog of extriangulated categories which were introduced by Nakaoka and Palu
[
36
]
by extracting those properties of
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{1}
on exact categories and on triangulated categories that seem relevant from the perspective of cotorsion pairs. However, our motivation for studying
n
-Frobenius categories and their stabilizations lies in the fact that there exist categories that have enough
n
-projectives with no non-zero projective objects. For example, there are no projective objects in the category of quasi-coherent sheaves over the projective line
\mathbf{P^{1}}(R)
, where
R
is a commutative ring with identity, see
[
12
, Corollary 2.3]
and also
[
18
, Exercise III 6.2(b)]
. However, a result of Serre indicates that the category of coherent sheaves over a projective scheme has enough locally free sheaves, see
[
18
, Corollary 5.18]
. More generally, as mentioned in
[
3
]
, the argument given in the proof of
[
38
, Lemma 1.12]
reveals that for a semi-separated noetherian scheme
\mathsf{X}
of finite Krull dimension, there exists a non-negative integer
n
such that locally free sheaves of finite rank, are
n
-projective objects in the category of coherent sheaves,
\operatorname{\mathsf{coh}}(\mathsf{X})
, and in particular, the subcategory of
n
-projective objects of
\operatorname{\mathsf{coh}}(X)
, is non-trivial.

In what follows, we provide some examples of
n
-Frobenius categories.

Example 2.4
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a
d
-dimensional commutative noetherian local ring.

(1) The category of all (finitely generated)
R
-modules of finite Gorenstein projective dimension is a
d
-Frobenius category, and moreover, its
d
-projective objects are all (finitely generated) modules with finite projective dimension. Particularly, if
R
is Gorenstein, then the category of all (finitely generated)
R
-modules, is a
d
-Frobenius category, see
[
3
, Example 2.16]
.

(2) The class of all Gorenstein flat
R
-modules,
\mathsf{GF}(R)
, is a
d
-Frobenius category such that its
d
-projective objects are flat modules. To see this, first one should note that since
R
is noetherian,
\mathsf{GF}(R)
is closed under extensions, see
[
24
, Theorem 3.7]
. So it will be an exact subcategory of
\mathsf{Mod}(R)
, the category of all
R
-modules. Now assume that
M\in\mathsf{GF}(R)
and
\mathbf{F}_{\bullet}
is its complete flat resolution. In view of
[
35
, Theorem 4.18]
,
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(\mathbf{F}_{\bullet},C)
is an acyclic complex, for all cotorsion flat modules
C
, implying that
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{i}_{R}(M,C)=0
for any
i\geq 1
. Furthermore, as
R
is
d
-dimensional, the projective (and so, cotorsion flat) dimension of any flat module is at most
d
, see
[
40
, Theorem 4.2.8]
and
[
25
, Theorem 3.4]
. Consequently,
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}_{R}^{i}(M,F)=0=\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{i}_{R}(F,M)
for all flat modules

and

[
3
, Proposition 2.18]
yields that
d
-projective (also
d
-injective) objects of
\mathsf{GF}(R)
are exactly flat modules, and so, the claim follows.

(3) Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a commutative noetherian Gorenstein local ring and
\mathcal{Q}
a finite acyclic quiver. Then the path algebra
R\mathcal{Q}
is a Gorenstein order in the sense of Auslander
[
1
]
. Assume that
\mathcal{G}
is the category consisting of all lattices over

, that is, those

-modules

which are finitely generated Gorenstein projective over
R
.
Since the category
\mathsf{Gp}(R)
of Gorenstein projective

. Thus, it will be an exact subcategory of
\mathsf{Mod}(R\mathcal{Q})
. In addition,
according to the second short exact sequence appeared in
[
14
, Page 4429]
and its dual, any

which is projective over

would not be a Frobenius category, whenever

has at least two vertices.

Recall that a left module
M
over an associtive ring

\mathbf{F}_{\bullet}:\cdots\rightarrow F_{1}\rightarrow F_{0}\rightarrow F_{-1}\rightarrow\cdots
such that

and for any left projective module

(resp.

\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{\Lambda}(-,Q)
, (resp.

) leaves the complex

[

]
.

In the remainder of the paper, unless stated otherwise, we assume that
\mathscr{C}
is an
n
-Frobenius category.

2.5
.

(1) Assume that
N
is an object of
\mathscr{C}
. For any integer

,
N
fits into conflations of length
k
,
\mathsf{\Omega}^{k}N\rightarrowtail P_{k-1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow P_{0}\twoheadrightarrow N
and

(resp. beginning with

\mathcal{U}_{k}(N)
(resp.
\mathcal{U}^{k}(N)
). Unit conflations usually will be depicted by

.

(2) Assume that
\delta,\delta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}^{n}(N)
(resp.
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\in\mathcal{U}_{n}(N)
). Then there exist deflations (resp. inflations)

with

-projective kernels (resp. cokernels) such that
\delta a=\delta^{\prime}a^{\prime}
(resp.

(resp.

(resp.
\delta_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a}}{{\longrightarrow}}\delta^{\prime\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a^{\prime}}}{{\longleftarrow}}\delta_{2}
), where
\delta^{\prime\prime}=\delta a
(resp.
\delta^{\prime\prime}=a\delta_{1}
).

(3) Suppose that
\operatorname{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\in\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{n}(M,\mathsf{\Omega}^{n}N)
is given, where

is an integer. It is known that there exist unit conflations
\delta\in\mathcal{U}^{n}(\mathsf{\Omega}^{n}N)
,
\delta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}_{n}(M)
and morphisms

in
\mathscr{C}

and

, see

, respectively, and are abbreviated by

.

(4) Assume that

is a morphism in
\mathscr{C}
and
\delta_{N}\in\mathcal{U}_{1}(N)
. So taking an
\operatorname{\mathsf{LUF}}
of

As mentioned in the introduction, modifying the stable category of a Frobenius category, we define the stabilization of an
n
-Frobenius category
\mathscr{C}
, which is called the phantom stable category. In order to give its structure, we need to recall some notions.

2.6
.

\mathcal{P}
-subfunctor.
For each two objects
X,Y
in
\mathscr{C}
,
\mathcal{P}(X,Y)
is the additive subgroup of
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{n}(X,Y)
consisting of all conflations
\operatorname{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
that arise as a pull-back along a morphism
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\rightarrow}}P
with
P

n
-projective. Equivalently, it arises from a push-out along a morphism
Q\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f^{\prime}}}{{\rightarrow}}Y
with
Q

n
-projective, see
[
3
, Proposition 4.3]
. Conflations of these types are referred to as
\mathcal{P}
-conflations. If there is no ambiguity, we denote
\mathcal{P}(-,-)
by
\mathcal{P}
. One should note that as being a
\mathcal{P}
-conflation is preserved under pull-back and push-out,
\mathcal{P}
will be a subfunctor of
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{n}
, see
[
2
,
15
]
. It should be pointed out that, since
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{0}(-,-)=\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{\mathscr{C}}(-,-)
, over Frobenius categories (or equivalently, 0-Frobenius categories), the push-out as well as the pull-back of a conflation along a morphism can be interpreted as composition of morphisms in
\mathscr{C}
. Particularly, in this situation,

\mathcal{P}
-conflations are exactly those morphisms that factor through a projective object.

Assume that
M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\rightarrow}}N
is a morphism in

5.

This section contains the main result of the paper, which states that the phantom stable category

possesses a triangulated structure with the shift functor

. Triangulated categories, first introduced by Verdier

in the mid 1960s, are important structures lying at the confluence of several exciting areas of mathematics. We will begin by recalling the definition of a triangulated category.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an additive category and
\mathcal{T}:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
an auto-equivalence, which is called a shift (or translation) functor. Let

be a collection of sequences of morphisms in

having the form

which are called

(TR1)

For any morphism

in
\mathcal{A}

. Moreover, for any object

\mathcal{T}X\rightarrow 0\rightarrow X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathsf{id}}}{{\rightarrow}}X

(TR2)

(Rotation axiom)
For a given exact triangle

For any commutative diagram of the form

Assume that
\mathscr{C}
is a Frobenius category. As mentioned before, it can be considered as an
n
-Frobenius category, for each integer
n\geq 1
. Suppose that
\operatorname{\underline{\mathscr{C}}}
is the stable category of
\mathscr{C}
modulo projectives and
F:\mathscr{C}\rightarrow\operatorname{\underline{\mathscr{C}}}
is the additive quotient functor. According to Remark
2.15
,
(\operatorname{\underline{\mathscr{C}}},F)
is the stabilization of
\mathscr{C}
as a 0-Frobenius category, which is known to be triangulated. On the other hand, assume that
(\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}},T)
is the stabilization of
\mathscr{C}
, considered as an
n
-Frobenius category. The following result asserts that these two triangulated structures are equivalent. First we include a lemma.

Lemma 5.13
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a Frobenius category. Then for any object
N\in\mathscr{C}
and any integer
n\geq 1
, the subfunctor
\mathcal{P}
of
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{n}(-,\mathsf{\Omega}^{n}N)
vanishes. In particular,
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{n}(-,\mathsf{\Omega}^{n}N)/\mathcal{P}\cong\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{n}(-,\mathsf{\Omega}^{n}N)
.

Proof.

Take an arbitrary object
M\in\mathscr{C}
. We would like to show that a given
\mathcal{P}
-conflation
\operatorname{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\in\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{n}(M,\mathsf{\Omega}^{n}N)
is zero. By the hypothesis, there exist a conflation
\operatorname{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\in\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{n}(P,\mathsf{\Omega}^{n}N)
with
P\in n
-
\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\mathscr{C}
, and a morphism
f:M\rightarrow P
in
\mathscr{C}
such that
\operatorname{\boldsymbol{\theta}}=\operatorname{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}f
. Since
P\in n
-
\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\mathscr{C}
, it is easily seen that
P
is a projective object of
\mathscr{C}
, as well. Considering
\operatorname{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
as the conflation
\mathsf{\Omega}^{n}N\rightarrowtail L_{n-1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow L_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\twoheadrightarrow}}P
, we infer that
\pi
is a split epimorphism. This, in turn, implies that
\operatorname{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
is zero as an object of
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{n}(P,\mathsf{\Omega}^{n}N)
, and so,
\operatorname{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
will be a zero object of
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{n}(M,\mathsf{\Omega}^{n}N)
. Thus the proof is finished.

Theorem 5.14
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a Frobenius category. Then two triangulated categories
(\operatorname{\underline{\mathscr{C}}},F)
and
(\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}},T)
are equivalent.

Proof.

Take a morphism
f
in
\mathscr{C}
that factors through a projective object. Since each projective object is
n
-projective, we have that
T(f)=0
in
\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}}
. Universal property of
(\operatorname{\underline{\mathscr{C}}},F)
gives us a unique additive covariant functor
F^{\prime}:\operatorname{\underline{\mathscr{C}}}\rightarrow\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}}
such that

. Assume that
f:M\rightarrow N
is an
n
-
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}
-phantom morphism in
\mathscr{C}
. So, for a given unit conflation
\delta_{N}\in\mathcal{U}_{n}(N)
,
\delta_{N}f
is a
\mathcal{P}
-conflation, see
[
3
, Proposition 6.2]
. Hence, invoking Lemma
5.13
, enables us to infer that
f
factors through an
n
-projective (and so, projective) object, implying that
F(f)=0
in
\operatorname{\underline{\mathscr{C}}}
. Finally, assume that
f
is an
n
-
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}
-invertible morphism. We shall prove that
F(f)
is an isomorphism. To this end, as
f
is an
n
-
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}
-invertible morphism, by
[
3
, Lemma 3.3]
, there exists a conflation
M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle[f~~l]^{t}}}{{\rightarrowtail}}N\oplus P\twoheadrightarrow Q
in
\mathscr{C}
, where
P,Q\in n
-
\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\mathscr{C}
. Since each
n
-projective object is indeed a projective object, this sequence is split, and then, we get the isomorphism
M\oplus Q\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{{\tiny{\left[\begin{array}[]{ll}f&g_{1}\\
l&g_{2}\end{array}\right]}}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}N\oplus P
in
\mathscr{C}
. This would imply that
F(f)
is an isomorphism. Thus the universal property of the phantom stable category
(\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}},T)
gives rise to the existence of a unique additive covariant functor
T^{\prime}:\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}}\rightarrow\operatorname{\underline{\mathscr{C}}}
with
T^{\prime}T=F
. Since by the universal property, the functors
F^{\prime}
and
T^{\prime}
are uniquely determined up to natural isomorphism, we have
T^{\prime}F^{\prime}\cong 1_{\operatorname{\underline{\mathscr{C}}}}
and
F^{\prime}T^{\prime}\cong 1_{\mathscr{C}_{\mathcal{P}}}
. It only remains to show that the functors
F^{\prime}
and
T^{\prime}
are triangulated. As these functors are identity on objects and
n
-projectives and projectives are the same, one may deduce that the functors
F^{\prime}
and
T^{\prime}
commute with the shift functors.
Now we show that these functors send standard triangles to standard triangles. To this end, for a given object

, take a unit conflation

. By the construction of

, we have

, one has

. Next assume that

is a morphism in
\operatorname{\underline{\mathscr{C}}}
. Consider the following pull-back diagram in
\mathscr{C}
:

\textstyle{\mathsf{\Omega}N~\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g}
\textstyle{T_{f}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{h}
\textstyle{M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{P~\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}

This gives us the standard triangle

in
\operatorname{\underline{\mathscr{C}}}

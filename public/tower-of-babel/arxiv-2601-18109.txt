1.
Introduction

A
Sierpiński carpet
, or
carpet
for short, is a homeomorphic image of the standard Sierpiński carpet fractal.
Due to
[
Wh
]
, a subset
S
of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is a Sierpiński carpet if and only if it has empty interior and can be written as

S=\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\backslash\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}D_{k},

where
D_{k},\,k\in\mathbb{N}
, are pairwise disjoint Jordan domains with

\partial D_{k}\cap\partial D_{l}=\emptyset
for
k\neq l
, and
\mathrm{diam}(D_{k})\to 0
as
k\to\infty
. The boundaries
\partial D_{k}
of
D_{k},\ k\in\mathbb{N}
, are called
peripheral circles
. A carpet
S
is called a
round Sierpiński carpet
, or
round carpet
, if the domains
D_{k},\,k\in\mathbb{N}
, are open geometric disks.

A rational map
f
is called
postcritically finite
if each critical point of
f
has a finite forward orbit.
The following result is Theorem 1.4 from
[
BLM
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
BLM
]
)
.

Let
f
and
g
be two postcritically finite rational maps and suppose that the corresponding Julia sets
\mathcal{J}(f)
and
\mathcal{J}(g)
are Sierpiński carpets.
If
\xi
is a quasisymmetric homeomorphism of
\mathcal{J}(f)
onto
\mathcal{J}(g)
,
then it is the restriction to
\mathcal{J}(f)
of a Mobius transformation on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.

Our first main result extends this theorem to quasiregular maps.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
f
and
g
be postcritically finite rational maps whose Julia sets
\mathcal{J}(f),\mathcal{J}(g)
, respectively, are Sierpiński carpets.
If
\xi\colon\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is a quasiregular map such that

\xi^{-1}(\mathcal{J}(g))=\mathcal{J}(f)
,
then
\xi|_{\mathcal{J}(f)}
has a unique extension to a rational map
\widehat{\xi}\colon\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
satisfying

(1)

G^{n+k}\circ\widehat{\xi}=G^{n}\circ\widehat{\xi}\circ F^{k},

for all
k,n\in\mathbb{N},\ n\geq l
, for some
l\in\mathbb{N}
, where
F
and
G
are some iterates of
f
and
g
, respectively.

Moreover, when
f=g
, we have the following result.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
f
be a postcritically finite rational map whose Julia set is a Sierpiński carpet, let
\xi\colon\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be a quasiregular map such that

\xi^{-1}(\mathcal{J}(g))=\mathcal{J}(f)
, and let
\widehat{\xi}
be the unique rational extension of
\xi|_{\mathcal{J}(f)}
from Theorem
1.2
. If
\mathrm{deg}(\widehat{\xi})>1
, then
\widehat{\xi}
is postcritically finite, the Julia set
\mathcal{J}(\widehat{\xi})
of
\widehat{\xi}
equals
\mathcal{J}(f)
,
and there exist
k,l\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(2)

f^{k}\circ(\widehat{\xi})^{l}=f^{2k}.

2.
Quasiregular maps and related concepts

A non-constant mapping
f\colon U\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
on a domain
U\subseteq\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is called
K
-quasiregular
,
K\geq 1
, if
f
is in the Sobolev space
W_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1,2}
and for almost every
z\in U
,

\left\lVert Df(z)\right\rVert^{2}\leq K\mathrm{det}(Df(z)).

If
f
is
K
-quasiregular for some
K\geq 1
, then
f
is called
quasiregular
. An equivalent condition for
f\colon U\to\mathbb{C}
to be quasiregular is that
f\in W_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1,2}
and
f
satisfies the
Beltrami equation

\frac{\partial f}{\partial\bar{z}}=\mu(z)\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}

for some complex valued Lebesgue measurable function
\mu
with

\esssup|\mu|<1.

If a
K
-quasiregular map
f
is a homeomorphism onto its image in addition, then
f
is called a
K
-
quasiconformal
map. A 1-quasiconformal map is called
conformal
.

We note here a useful factorization of quasiregular maps by S. Stoïlow
[
St
]
(see also
[
AIM
, Section 5.5]
and
[
LP2
]
):

Theorem 2.1

(Stoïlow’s Factorization)
.

If
f\colon U\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is a quasiregular map, then there exists a homeomorphism
h\colon U\to V
which satisfies the same Beltrami
equation as
f
. Moreover, for every such
h
there exists a holomorphic map
g\colon V\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
such that

f=g\circ h.

It follows easily from this theorem that a quasiregular map
f\colon U\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is a local homeomorphism at all but a discrete set of points.
We call a point
p\in U

critical
if
f
is not a local homeomorphism in any neighborhood of
p
. The set of critical points of
f
will be denoted by
{\rm crit}(f)
.

We will also need the following quasiregular version of Montel’s theorem
[
AIM
, Corollary 5.5.7]
as well as a compactness property of quasisymmetric maps below.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
AIM
]
)
.

Suppose
z_{0},z_{1}\in\mathbb{C}
and let
h_{k}\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{C},k=1,2,\dots
, be a
sequence of
K
-quasiregular mappings defined on a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
, each omitting
these two values. Then there is a subsequence converging locally uniformly on
\Omega

to a mapping
h
,
and
h
is either a
K
-quasiregular mapping or a constant.

Finally, recall that if
(X,d_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
are two metric spaces, a homeomorphism
f\colon X\to Y
is said to be
\eta
-
quasisymmetric
if there is an increasing homeomorphism
\eta\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that for any triple
x,y,z
of distinct points in
X
, we have

\displaystyle{\frac{d_{Y}(f(x),f(y))}{d_{Y}(f(x),f(z))}}\leq\eta\left({\frac{d_{X}(x,y)}{d_{X}(x,z)}}\right).

3.
Schottky maps and their local rigidity

A
relative Schottky set

S
in a domain
D\subseteq\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is a set of the form

S=D\backslash\cup_{i\in I}B_{i},

where each
B_{i},\,i\in I
, is an open geometric disk with the closure
\overline{B_{i}}
contained in
D
, and
\overline{B_{i}}\cap\overline{B_{j}}=\emptyset
for
i\neq j
.
The boundary
\partial B_{i}
of a disk
B_{i},\,i\in I
, is called a
peripheral circle
. If
D=\mathbb{C}
or
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
,
S
is called a
Schottky set
. The study of Schottky sets was initiated in
[
BKM
]
, and relative Schottky sets and maps in
[
Me1
,
Me2
]
.

Let
S\subseteq\mathbb{C}
be an arbitrary subset that has an accumulation point
p\in S
, and let
U
be a neighborhood of
p
in
\mathbb{C}
.
Suppose that
f\colon S\cap U\to\mathbb{C}
is a continuous function. We say that
f
is
conformal
at
p
if

(3)

\lim_{q\in S,\,q\to p}\frac{f(q)-f(p)}{q-p}

exists and is non-zero. The limit above is called the
derivative
of
f
at
p
and is denoted by
f^{\prime}(p)
.

Suppose that
S=D\backslash\cup_{i\in I}B_{i}
and
\widetilde{S}=\widetilde{D}\backslash\cup_{j\in J}\widetilde{B_{i}}
are relative Schottky sets.
Let
U
be an open subset of
D
and
f\colon S\cap U\to\widetilde{S}
is a local homeomorphism.
It follows that
p\in S\cap U\cap\partial B_{i}
for some
i\in I
if and only if

f(p)\in\partial\widetilde{B_{j}}
for some
j\in J
. Such a map
f\colon S\cap U\to\widetilde{S}
is called a
Schottky map
if it is conformal at every point of
S\cap U
, and the derivative
f^{\prime}
is a continuous function on
S\cap U
. We use standard modifications when either
p
of
f(p)
is
\infty
in Equation (
3
) above.

The notion of Schottky maps is closely related to that of quasiconformal maps. In fact, we will use the following criterion
[
Me3
, Lemma 2.2]
for maps to be Schottky maps.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
Me3
]
)
.

Let
S\subseteq\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be a Schottky set of measure zero.
Suppose
U\subseteq\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is open and
f\colon U\rightarrow\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is a quasiconformal map with
f^{-1}(S)=S\cap U
.
Then
f\colon S\cap U\to S
is a Schottky map.
In particular, if
f\colon\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is a quasiregular map with
f^{-1}(S)=S
, then

f\colon S\setminus\mathrm{crit}(f)\to S
is a Schottky map.

An important property of Schottky maps is their
local\;rigidity
, i.e., they are uniquely determined by their local behavior near one point. We need the following notion to state related results, Theorem
3.2

[
Me2
, Corollary 4.2]
and Theorem
3.3

[
Me2
, Theorem 5.2]
below. A relative Schottky set
S=D\setminus\cup_{i\in I}B_{i}
is called
locally porous at

p\in S
if there exist a neighborhood
U
of
p
and constants
r_{0}>0,\,C\geq 1
, such that for every
q\in S\cap U
and each
r
with
0<r\leq r_{0}
, there exists
i\in I
with
B(q,r)\cap B_{i}\neq\emptyset
and

r/C\leq{\rm diam}(B_{i})\leq Cr,

where
B(q,r)
denotes the open disk of radius
r
centered at
q
, and
{\rm diam}(X)
denotes the diameter of a set
X\subseteq\mathbb{C}
. A relative Schottky set
S
is called
locally porous
if it is locally porous at every
p\in S
. Every locally porous relative Schottky set has measure zero since it cannot have Lebesgue density points.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
Me2
]
)
.

Let
S
be a locally porous relative Schottky set in
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}
, and suppose that
U\subseteq D
is an open set such that
S\cap U
is connected.
Let
f
and
g\colon S\cap U\to\widetilde{S}
be Schottky maps into a relative Schottky set
\widetilde{S}
in a domain
\widetilde{D}
, and consider

E=\{p\in S\cap U\colon f(p)=g(p)\}.

Then
E=S\cap U
, provided
E
has an accumulation point in
U
.

We also record another theorem
[
Me2
, Theorem 5.2]
on the rigidity of Schottky maps. Roughly speaking, it says that, under a mild assumption that is satisfied for any subhyperbolic dynamical system, a sequence
f_{k}
of locally uniformly converging Schottky maps will become constant when
k
is large enough.

Theorem 3.3

(
[
Me2
]
)
.

Let
S
be a locally porous relative Schottky set in a domain
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}
, and
p\in S
be an arbitrary point.
Suppose that
U\subseteq D
is an open neighborhood of
p
such that
S\cap U
is connected. We assume that there exists a Schottky map

g\colon S\cap U\to S
with
g(p)=p
and
g^{\prime}(p)\neq 1
.
Let
\widetilde{S}
be a relative Schottky set in a domain
\widetilde{D}
and let
(f_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of Schottky maps
f_{k}\colon S\cap U\to\widetilde{S}
. We assume that for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
there exists an open set
\widetilde{U}_{k}
so that the map
f_{k}\colon S\cap U\to\widetilde{S}\cap\widetilde{U}_{k}
is a homeomorphism, and the sequence
(f_{k})

converges locally uniformly to a homeomorphism
f\colon S\cap U\to\widetilde{S}\cap\widetilde{U}
, where
\widetilde{U}
is an open set.
Then there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
f_{k}=f
in
S\cap U
for all
k\geq N
.

4.
Conformal elevator

In
[
BLM
, Section 4]
, the authors introduced the technique of
conformal elevator
for subhyperbolic rational maps. Here we discuss this technique for postcritically finite maps
only, and we will apply it to the proof of Proposition
5.1
in Section
5
.

Assume that
f
is a postcritically finite rational map with

\mathcal{J}(f)\subseteq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{D}\quad and\quad f^{-1}(\mathbb{D})\subseteq\mathbb{D}.

For a map
f
, say with an attracting fixed point, these are easy to achieve by conjugating
f
with an appropriate Möbius transformation.
In this subsection we use Euclidean metric on
\mathbb{C}
.
Fix
\epsilon_{0}>0
so small that
\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{J}(f))>2\epsilon_{0}
, and so that every disk
B^{\prime}=B(q,r^{\prime})
centered at a point
q\in\mathcal{J}(f)
with radius

r^{\prime}\leq 8\epsilon_{0}
is contained in
\mathbb{D}
, and contains at most one point in the postcritical set of
f
, i.e., in
\mathrm{post}(f)=\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f^{n}({\rm crit}(f))
.
Let
B=B(p,r)
be a small disk centered at a point
p\in\mathcal{J}(f)
and of radius
r<\epsilon_{0}
. We know that
\mathcal{J}(f)\subseteq f^{n}(B)
for sufficiently large

n
(see
[
Be
, Theorem 4.2.5 (ii)]
), thus
\mathrm{diam}(f^{n}(B))>2\epsilon_{0}
.
In other words, there exists a maximal number
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathrm{diam}(f^{n}(B))\leq 2\epsilon_{0}
.
By uniform continuity of
f
near the compact set
\mathcal{J}(f)
, there exists
\delta_{0}>0
only depending on
f
such that

\mathrm{diam}(f^{n}(B))\geq\delta_{0}.

Moreover, we have a lower bound for diameters of image sets
f^{n}(A)
, where
A\subseteq B
is connected (
[
BLM
, Lemma 4.1]
):

Lemma 4.1

(
[
BLM
]
)
.

There exist constants
\gamma>0
and
C>0
only depending on
f
and independent of
B=B(p,r)
, such that for any connected set
A\subseteq B
,

\mathrm{diam}(f^{n}(A))^{\gamma}\geq C\frac{\mathrm{diam}(A)}{\mathrm{diam}(B)}.

Proof
.

We sketch the proof here. First, by our choice of
\epsilon_{0}
,
we find a disk
B^{\prime}=B(q,r^{\prime})
with
q\in\mathcal{J}(f)
and
r^{\prime}\leq 8\epsilon_{0}
, such that

f^{n}(B)\subseteq\frac{1}{2}B^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{D}\quad{\rm and}\quad B^{\prime}\cap\mathrm{post}(f)\subseteq\{q\}.

Let
\Omega\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be the unique component of
f^{-n}(B^{\prime})
that contains
B
.

Second, there exist conformal maps
\varphi\colon B^{\prime}\to\mathbb{D}
and
\psi\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{D}
such that

(\varphi\circ f^{n}\circ\psi^{-1})(z)=z^{k}

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, where
k\leq N=N(f)
is uniformly bounded only by
f
and independent of
B
.
Third,
by Koebe distortion theorem we have

|u-v|\approx\left\lvert(\psi^{-1})^{\prime}(0)\right\rvert\cdot\left\lvert\varphi(u)-\varphi(v)\right\rvert,

whenever
u,v\in B
, and where we use
a\approx b
to indicate that there is a constant
C\geq 1
independent of the disk
B
such that
a/C\leq b\leq Cb
. In particular,

\mathrm{diam}(B)\approx\left\lvert(\psi^{-1})^{\prime}(0)\right\rvert\cdot\mathrm{diam}(f^{n}(B)).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle 1\approx\mathrm{diam}(f^{n}(B))

\displaystyle\approx\mathrm{diam}(\varphi(f^{n}(B)))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{diam}(P_{k}(\psi(B)))\leq C\cdot\mathrm{diam}(\psi(B))\leq 2,

where
C>0
is a constant independent of
B
.
Hence
\mathrm{diam}(\psi(B))\approx 1
, and so
\mathrm{diam}(B)\approx\left\lvert(\psi^{-1})^{\prime}(0)\right\rvert
.
This implies that

\frac{|u-v|}{\mathrm{diam}(B)}\approx\left\lvert\varphi(u)-\varphi(v)\right\rvert,

whenever
u,v\in B
. Hence

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{diam}(A)}{\mathrm{diam}(B)}\approx\mathrm{diam}(\psi(A))

\displaystyle\leq C\cdot\mathrm{diam}(P_{k}(\psi(A)))^{1/N}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{diam}(\varphi(f^{n}(A)))^{1/N}\approx\mathrm{diam}(f^{n}(A))^{1/N},

for another constant
C>0
independent of
B
.

5.
Local quasiconformal maps between carpet Julia sets

In this section,
f
and
g
are postcritically finite rational maps, and the corresponding Julia sets
\mathcal{J}(f)
and
\mathcal{J}(g)
are Sierpiński carpets.
In
[
BLM
, Theorem 1.4]
, the authors proved that a quasisymmetric map
\xi\colon\mathcal{J}(f)\to\mathcal{J}(g)
is the restriction to
\mathcal{J}(f)
of a Möbius transformation.
Many of the arguments in that paper are local and it makes sense to investigate whether they carry over to quasiregular maps
\xi
that restrict to maps between
\mathcal{J}(f)
and
\mathcal{J}(g)
. Note that a quasisymmetric map
\xi\colon\mathcal{J}(f)\to\mathcal{J}(g)
extends to a global quasiconformal map of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
. One of the main ingredients of
[
BLM
]
is the functional equation
[
BLM
, Equation (1.2)]

g^{m^{\prime}}\circ\xi=g^{m}\circ\xi\circ f^{n},

established on all of
\mathcal{J}(f)
in
[
BLM
, Section 8, Step IV]
.
Since quasiregular maps are locally quasiconformal away from critical points,
we obtain such a functional equation in the local setting; see Proposition
5.1
below, and then conclude that it holds globally on
\mathcal{J}(f)
for quasiregular maps in Theorem
5.2
.
We outline the proofs with these modifications for readers’ convenience.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
f,g\colon\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be postcritically finite rational maps, and suppose that the corresponding Julia sets
\mathcal{J}(f)
and
\mathcal{J}(g)
are Sierpiński carpets.
If
U
and
V
are open sets non-trivially intersecting
\mathcal{J}(f)
and
\mathcal{J}(g)
, respectively, and
\xi\colon U\rightarrow V
is a quasiconformal map with
\xi^{-1}(\mathcal{J}(g)\cap V)=\mathcal{J}(f)\cap U
, then there exist an open set
W\subseteq U
with
\mathcal{J}(f)\cap W\neq\emptyset
, and
n,m,m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
,
such that on
\mathcal{J}(f)\cap W
one has

(4)

g^{m^{\prime}}\circ\xi=g^{m}\circ\xi\circ f^{n}.

Proof
.

By conjugating
g
(hence
\mathcal{J}(g)
), we may assume that
\mathcal{J}(g)\subset\mathbb{D}
. Below we use the Euclidean metric.
Since repelling periodic points of
f
are dense in
\mathcal{J}(f)
(see

[
Be
, p. 148, Theorem 6.9.2]
), we can find a repelling periodic point

p\in\mathcal{J}(f)\cap U
of
f
of period
n
and
\rho>0
such that
U_{0}=B(p,\rho)\subseteq U
is disjoint from
\mathrm{post}(f)
.
Denote
n\cdot k
by
n_{k}
, and the connected component of
f^{-n_{k}}(U_{0})
containing
p
by
U_{k}
.
By possibly taking a smaller
\rho
, we may assume that
U_{0}\supseteq U_{1}\supseteq U_{2}\supseteq...
and
\mathrm{diam}(U_{k})\to 0
as
k\to\infty
.
We take the branch
f^{-n_{k}}
on
U_{0}
to be the inverse map of

f^{n_{k}}|_{U_{k}}\colon U_{k}\to U_{0}
. Then the choice of these inverse branches is consistent in the sense that we have

(5)

f^{n_{k+1}-n_{k}}\circ f^{-n_{k+1}}=f^{-n_{k}}

for all
k
.
Let
r_{k}>0
be the smallest number such that
\xi(U_{k})\subseteq B(\xi(p),r_{k})
. Since
\xi(p)\in\xi(U_{k})
,
\mathrm{diam}(\xi(U_{k}))\approx r_{k}
, where, similar to above, the symbol
\approx
between two quantities means that their quotient is bounded above and below by a constant independent of
k
.
By applying Lemma
4.1
to
A=\xi(U_{k})
and
B=B(\xi(p),r_{k})
, we can find iterates

g^{m_{k}}
such that
\mathrm{diam}\left(g^{m_{k}}(\xi(U_{k}))\right)\approx 1
. Since
g^{m_{k}}(\xi(U_{k}))
meets
\mathcal{J}(g)
at
g^{m_{k}}(\xi(p))
, it is contained in a
bounded region of
\mathbb{C}
.

Now we consider the composition

h_{k}=g^{m_{k}}\circ\xi\circ f^{-n_{k}}

defined on
B(p,\rho)
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Since

into a compact subset of

implies that a subsequence of

subconverges locally uniformly to a map

is also quasiregular or a constant. The latter possibility is ruled out since

. For simplicity of notations we assume that the whole sequence

converges; though

is no longer

in this case. The map

has at most countably many critical points, and so there exists a non-critical point

and a small neighborhood

of

is injective on

In what follows, we replace

by this

We know by

, Theorem 1.10]
that the peripheral circles of a Sierpiński carpet Julia set

of a postcritically finite rational map

satisfy nice geometric properties. Namely, they are uniformly relatively separated uniform quasicircles and appear at all locations and scales; see
[

]
for definitions. In particular,

, there exists a quasisymmetric map

such that

is locally porous.

From Lemma

we know that any iterate

f_{\beta}^{n}=\beta\circ f^{n}\circ\beta^{-1}\colon S\backslash\mathrm{crit}(f_{\beta}^{n})\to S

is a
Schottky map.
Moreover, since

by the same lemma, the quasiconformal maps

are Schottky maps on

It is easy to see (

, Lemma 2.1]

with

such that

is connected.
Since repelling periodic points are dense in

, we can find
a point

and

such that

implies that

Finally, we pre-compose

where

Theorem 5.2
.

If

and

are postcritically finite rational maps whose respective Julia sets

are Sierpiński carpets, and

such that

then Equation (

Since

has the same properties as

[

there exists a quasisymmetric map

\widehat{\mathbb{C}}

Using the same notations and arguments as in Proposition

6.
Rational maps sharing a measure of maximal entropy

It is an interesting problem to study rational maps that share a non-trivial Julia set, or have the same measure of maximal entropy.
Here we briefly summarize relevant notions and basic properties.
The reader can refer to
[
DS
]
for details.

If
f
is a rational map of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
of degree
d
, then the
measure of maximal entropy

\mu_{f}
of
f
is the unique
pull-back invariant probability measure with support equal to the Julia set
\mathcal{J}(f)
of
f
, achieving maximal entropy
\mathrm{log}\,d
.
Pull-back invariant

means that
\frac{1}{d}f^{*}\mu_{f}=\mu_{f}
, or
\mu_{f}(f(A))=d\cdot\mu_{f}(A)
, whenever
f
is injective on a Borel set
A
. Note that this implies a weaker notion of invariance that

f_{*}\mu_{f}=\mu_{f}
, or
\mu_{f}(f^{-1}(A))=\mu_{f}(A)
for all Borel sets
A\subseteq\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.

For any probability measure
\mu
on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
and a rational map
f
,
the
Jacobian

J_{\mu}(f)
of
f
is defined as the Radon-Nikodym derivative of
f^{*}(\mu)
with respect to
\mu
. Namely,
for each Borel measurable set
A
such that
f
is injective on
A
,

\mu(f(A))=\int_{A}J_{\mu}(f)(x)\>\mathrm{d}\mu(x),\quad\mathrm{whenever}\;f|_{A}\;\mathrm{is}\;\mathrm{injective}.

If
\mu=\mu_{f}
is the
measure of maximal entropy of a rational map
f
of degree
\mathrm{deg}(f)
at least 2, then
J_{\mu_{f}}(f)
is constant equal to
\mathrm{deg}(f)
.
In fact, the converse is generally true (
[
DS
, Theorem 1.118]
):

Theorem 6.1

(
[
DS
]
)
.

Let
f
be a ratinal map of degree
d
. If
\mu
is a pull-back invariant probability measure of constant Jacobian
d
,
then
\mu=\mu_{f}
.

Proof
.

The measure

\mu_{f}
is the unique probability measure such that the entropy
h_{\mu}(f)
attains the maximum value
\mathrm{log}\,d
. The result now follows from the inequality
[
Pa
]

h_{\nu}(f)\geq\int\mathrm{log}J_{\nu}(f)\;\mathrm{d}\nu,

satisfied for any probability measure
\nu
invariant under
f
, i.e., such that
f_{*}\nu=\nu
. Indeed, by setting
\nu=\mu
from the statement of the theorem we get
h_{\mu}(f)\geq\mathrm{log}\,d
, and so
h_{\mu}(f)
attains the maximum. Therefore
\mu=\mu_{f}
.

It is well known that
\mu_{f}=\mu_{f^{n}}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and commuting rational maps have the same measure of maximal entropy.
The following theorem is contained in
[
Le
,
Le2
,
LP
]
; see also
[
Ye
, Theorem 1.7]
.

Theorem 6.2

(
[
Le
,
Le2
,
LP
,
Ye
]
)
.

Let
f,g\colon\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be non-exceptional rational maps of degrees at least 2.
Then
\mu_{f}=\mu_{g}
if and only if for some
k,l\in\mathbb{N}
,

f^{k}\circ g^{l}=f^{2k}.

Here, a rational map is called
exceptional
if it is conformally conjugate to either a power map, or a Chebyshev polynomial, or a Lattès map. In particular, a rational map whose Julia set is a Sierpiński carpet is non-exceptional.

For general rational maps
f
and
g
sharing the same Julia set, they need not have common iterates up to Möbius transformations (
[
Ye
, Theorem 1.1]
).
In general, holomorphic maps of the form
f^{-k}\circ f^{k}
need not be Möbius, as the following example shows.

Example
.

Consider
f(z)=z^{2}-\frac{1}{16z^{2}}
. It is postcritically finite and has a Sierpiński carpet Julia set. A priori multi-valued map
f^{-1}\circ f(z)
must be either
\pm z
or
\pm\frac{i}{4z}
, i.e., a Möbius transformation.
However, when
k\geq 2
,
f^{-k}\circ f^{k}
first defined locally and then extended analytically may not be a Möbius transformation. For example, by solving for the coefficients of a Möbius transformation
\xi
such that

(6)

f^{2}\circ\xi=f^{2},

one can show that
\xi
belongs to the group generated by
\rho(z)=\mathrm{i}z
and
\iota(z)=\frac{1}{4z}
.
Indeed, if
\xi(z)={(az+b)}/{(cz+d)}
satisfies (
6
), we may assume
a\neq 0
, otherwise we may consider
\iota\circ\xi
. Then

\lim_{z\to\infty}f^{2}(\xi(z))=\lim_{z\to\infty}f^{2}(z)=\infty,

while
\lim_{z\to\infty}\xi(z)=\frac{a}{c}
. Hence
f^{2}(\frac{a}{c})=\infty
, implying
c=0
. We may assume
d=1
, and thus
\xi(z)=az+b
. But
f^{2}(z)=z^{4}+O(1)
and so
f^{2}(az+b)\thicksim(az+b)^{4}+O(1)
as
z\to\infty
. These two functions being identical, it implies that
a^{4}=1
and
b=0
and the claim follows. In particular, the group of Möbius transformation satisfuing (
6
) has eight elements. On the other hand, the map
f^{-2}
has 16 branches in a neighborhood of a noncritical value for
f^{2}
, so there must be some local
holomorphic maps of the form
f^{-2}\circ f^{2}
which are not Möbius.

Lemma 7.1

Let
\phi\colon\partial\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\partial\mathbb{D}
be an orientation preserving branched covering, and suppose that there exist numbers
k,l,n\in\mathbb{N}
,

, such that

(P_{l}\circ\phi)(z)=(P_{n}\circ\phi\circ P_{k})(z)\quad\text{for $z\in\partial\mathbb{D}$},

where

for all

Then

and there exists

, such that
\phi(z)=bz^{m}
for all
z\in\partial\mathbb{D}
.

Proof
.

By counting topological degrees of both sides, one immediately sees that

l=n\cdot k
.
Let
m
be the degree of

. Then there exists a continuous function
\alpha\colon\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

By (

\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}l\alpha(t)}=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}n\alpha(kt)}

for
t\in\mathbb{R}
. This implies that there exists a constant

such that

\alpha(t)=\frac{n}{l}\alpha(kt)+c=\frac{1}{k}\alpha(kt)+c\quad\text{for $t\in\mathbb{R}$}.

Using induction on

, one obtains

\alpha\left(t+\frac{2\pi}{k^{s}}\right)=\frac{1}{k}\alpha\left(kt+\frac{2\pi}{k^{s-1}}\right)+c=\frac{1}{k}\alpha(kt)+\frac{2\pi}{k^{s}}m+c=\alpha(t)+\frac{2\pi}{k^{s}}m,

for all

Continuity of
\alpha

f
be a postcritically finite rational maps whose Julia set
\mathcal{J}(f)
is a Siepiński carpet, and let
\widehat{\xi}
be a Möbius transformation preserving
\mathcal{J}(f)
, and such that
f^{2}\circ\widehat{\xi}=f\circ\widehat{\xi}\circ f
. Does it imply that
F\circ\widehat{\xi}=F
, where
F
is an iterate of
f
?

In fact, it is unclear whether such relations hold for specific maps such as
f(z)=z^{2}-\frac{1}{16z^{2}}
. However, we suspect that the following question has a positive answer.

Question 7.2
.

Let
f(z)=z^{2}-\frac{1}{16z^{2}}
, and
\rho,\iota
be as in the example of Section
6
, i.e.,
\rho(z)=\mathrm{i}z
and
\iota(z)=\frac{1}{4z}
.
Is it true that the group of all Möbius transformations preserving
\mathcal{J}(f)
is
\left\langle\rho,\iota\right\rangle
?

8.
Quasiregular maps between other topological types of Julia sets

One can ask whether similar statements to those in Theorems
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
hold for other topological types of non-exceptional postcritically finite rational maps. Here, again, by exceptional maps we mean, up to conjugation, power maps, Chebyshev polynomials, or Lattès maps.
In this section we discuss two examples: the basilica and the Apollonian gasket cases. In contrast to the Siepiński carpet case, we demonstrate that a quasiregular map of the basilica or the gasket Julia sets need not be related to the dynamics of the corresponding map, contrasting the Sierpiński carpet case of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
.

Example
.

The basilica; see
[
LM
]
for a description of the quasisymmetry group of the basilica and the discussion of the Thompson group
T
, and
[
BF
]
for more general statements and results.
The basilica is the filled Julia set
\mathcal{K}(f)
of the quadratic polynomial
f(z)=z^{2}-1
.
This polynomial has a superattracting cycle
\gamma=\{0,1\}
. By definition, the
immediate basin
of
\gamma
is the union of two components
U_{0}

and
U_{-1}
of the Fatou set containing 0 and -1, respectively. These components are Jordan disks, and they have a unique intersection point

\alpha=\frac{1-\sqrt{5}}{2}
, which is a fixed point of
f
.

Let
U_{\infty}
denote the basin at infinity, i.e., the unbounded Fatou component of
f
, and let
\phi_{\infty}
be the Böttcher coordinate
of
U_{\infty}
. Namely,
\phi_{\infty}
is the conformal map of
U_{\infty}
onto
\mathbb{C}\backslash\overline{\mathbb{D}}
conjugating
f
to
g(z)=z^{2}
.
It is known that the Julia set of a hyperbolic map
f
is locally connected, so
\psi_{\infty}=\phi_{\infty}^{-1}\colon\mathbb{C}\backslash\overline{\mathbb{D}}\to U_{\infty}
exteneds continuously to
the boundary, and so induces a continuous boundary map
\psi
of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
onto the Julia set
\mathcal{J}(f)
of
f
satisfying
\psi\circ g=f\circ\psi
.
Since
\alpha
is a fixed point of
f
, it can be calculated that

\psi^{-1}(\alpha)=\{\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2}{3}\pi\mathrm{i}},\mathrm{e}^{\frac{4}{3}\pi\mathrm{i}}\}
.

Since the interior of
\mathcal{K}(f)
for
f(z)=z^{2}-1
equals the (full, not just immediate) basin of the cycle
\gamma
, every bounded Fatou component
U
of
f
eventually, i.e., under a certain iterate of
f
, lands in the cycle
\{U_{0},U_{-1}\}
.
In fact, for each such bounded Fatou component
U\neq U_{0}
there exists a unique
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
f^{n}\colon U\to U_{0}
is a conformal map. Let
L(U)
denote the limb of
U
, namely the closure of the union of
U
and all the components of the interior of
\mathcal{K}(f)
separated by
U
from
U_{0}
. Note here that the basilica has a tree-like structure. Then
\psi^{-1}(\partial L(U))
maps under
g^{n}
onto

[{e}^{-\frac{2}{3}\pi{i}},{e}^{\frac{2}{3}\pi{i}}]\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{1}
. The two endpoints
{e}^{\pm\frac{2}{3}\pi{i}}
are mapped to the same point under
\psi
, called the
root
of
L(U)
.

In
[
LM
, Lemma 5.4]
, it is shown that the Thompson group
T
acts by quasisymmetries on
\mathcal{J}(f)
, where
f(z)=z^{2}-1
, leaving
\partial U_{0}
invariant. In fact, such an action is defined piecewise using forward iterates of
f^{2}
and branches of
f^{-2n},\ n\in\mathbb{N}
. Thompson group elements shuffle the limbs rooted at
\partial U_{0}
. Moreover, these quasisymmetries extend to global quasiconformal maps of
\mathbb{C}
. It is immediate that if one takes a non-trivial element of the Thompson group acting by a quasisymmetry
\xi
on
\mathcal{J}(f)
, say that fixes all the limbs rooted on a certain non-trivial arc of
\partial U_{0}
, then this
\xi
does not relate globally to the dynamics of
f
, say as in Equation (
2
). Such a map
\xi
has degree one however. Post-composing
\xi
with
f
produces a quasiregular map of degree two that does not satisfy Equation (
2
) on
\mathcal{J}(f)
.

Example
.

The Apollonian gasket; see
[
LLMM
]
for the discussion of the quasisymmetry group of the Apollonian gasket Julia set, and also
[
LMMN
]
for more general statements.

We need the following quasiregular extension lemma; see, e.g.,
[
LN
, Lemma 2.2]
for similar results.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
f
be a continuous orientation preserving branched covering map of the unit circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, and for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
I_{1},I_{2},\dots,I_{n}
, be closed arcs of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
with disjoint interiors and whose union is
\mathbb{S}^{1}
.
Assume that
f|_{I_{j}}
extends in a neighborhood of
I_{j}
to a holomorphic function with non-vanishing derivative for each
j=1,2,\dots,n
.
Then
f
can be extended to a quasiregular map
\widehat{f}\colon\overline{\mathbb{D}}\to\overline{\mathbb{D}}
.

Proof
.

Let
e^{i\theta_{j}},\ j=1,2,\dots,n
, be the points on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
such that
e^{i\theta_{j}}\in I_{j}\cap I_{j+1},\ j=1,2,\dots n-1
, and
e^{i\theta_{n}}\in I_{n}\cap I_{1}
. We define
\widehat{f}(z)=|z|f(\frac{z}{|z|})
on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}\setminus\{0\}
, and
\widehat{f}(0)=0
. It is a continuous map extending
f
. If
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
{\rm arg}(z)\notin\{\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{n}\}
, where
{\rm arg}(z)
denotes the argument of
z
, we have

\partial_{\bar{z}}\widehat{f}=\frac{z}{2|z|}f\left(\frac{z}{|z|}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{z}{|z|}\right)^{2}f^{\prime}\left(\frac{z}{|z|}\right),

\partial_{z}\widehat{f}=\frac{\bar{z}}{2|z|}f\left(\frac{z}{|z|}\right)+\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime}\left(\frac{z}{|z|}\right).

Since
f
maps
\mathbb{S}^{1}
to itself and preserves the orientation,
we get that
\mathrm{arg}(f^{\prime}(z))+\mathrm{arg}(z)\equiv\mathrm{arg}(f(z))\;\mathrm{mod}\;2\pi
for each
z\in\mathbb{S}^{1},\ {\rm arg}(z)\notin\{\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{n}\}
.
Therefore, for
z\in\mathbb{D},\ {\rm arg}(z)\notin\{\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{n}\}
,

\left\lvert\partial_{\bar{z}}\widehat{f}\right\rvert=\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert 1-\left\lvert f^{\prime}\left(\frac{z}{|z|}\right)\right\rvert\right\rvert,\quad\left\lvert\partial_{z}\widehat{f}\right\rvert=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\left\lvert f^{\prime}\left(\frac{z}{|z|}\right)\right\rvert\right).

By the assumption, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
|f^{\prime}(z)|\geq\varepsilon
for any
z\in\mathbb{S}^{1},\ {\rm arg}(z)\notin\{\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{n}\}
, and hence

\left\lvert\frac{\partial_{\bar{z}}\widehat{f}}{\partial_{z}\widehat{f}}\right\rvert=\frac{\left\lvert 1-\left\lvert f^{\prime}\left(\frac{z}{|z|}\right)\right\rvert\right\rvert}{1+\left\lvert f^{\prime}\left(\frac{z}{|z|}\right)\right\rvert}\leq k<1,

for some
k
independent of
z
. From the assumptions and the extension formula it is immediate that
\widehat{f}
is absolutely continuous on lines, and hence
\widehat{f}
is a quasiregular extension of
f
.

Now we are able to prove the following result.

Theorem 8.2
.

There exists a postcritically finite rational map
f\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
with gasket Julia set
\mathcal{J}(f)
,
and a global quasiregular map
\xi\colon\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
with
\xi^{-1}(\mathcal{J}(f))=\mathcal{J}(f)
,
such that
\xi|_{\mathcal{J}(f)}
does not have a rational extension to
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.

Proof
.

Let
f(z)=\frac{3z^{2}}{2z^{3}+1}
considered in
[
LLMM
]
.
Its critical points are
0,1,\omega
and
\omega^{2}
, where
\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}
, with orbits
0\mapsto 0
,
1\mapsto 1
, and
\omega\mapsto\omega^{2}\mapsto\omega
. In particular,
f
is postcritically finite. Note that
\bar{f}
, where the bar denotes the complex conjugation, is critically fixed, i.e., all its critical points are fixed. The Julia set
\mathcal{J}(f)=\mathcal{J}(\bar{f})
is the Apollonian gasket depicted in Figure
1
.

Figure 1
.
The Julia set of

f(z)=\frac{3z^{2}}{2z^{3}+1}
.

Denote the central closed triangular region in Figure
1
by
\Delta ABC
, with three edge
AB,BC,CA
being arcs on the boundaries of the disks
D_{1},D_{2},D_{3}
, respectively. The map
f^{2}
fixes the points
A,B,C
, and
D_{1},D_{2},D_{3}
, are its fixed Fatou components.
Now we take

\xi(z)=\begin{cases}f^{2}(z),\quad z\in\Delta ABC\\
z,\quad z\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\backslash\left((\Delta ABC)\cup\sqcup_{i=1}^{3}D_{i}\right).\end{cases}

The map
\xi
can be extended to a quasiregular map of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
. Indeed, the boundaries of
D_{1},D_{2},D_{3}
being quasicircles, it is enough to show that
\xi
can be extended as a quasiregular map in each of these domains.
We extend it to
D_{1}
and the other two are done in the same way.
Let
\phi\colon\overline{D_{1}}\to\overline{\mathbb{D}}
be the Böttcher coordinate of
\overline{D_{1}}
.
Counting topological degrees, for example, we find that

\phi\circ f^{2}=P_{4}\circ\phi\quad\mathrm{on}\quad\overline{D_{1}},

where
P_{d}(z)=z^{d}
as above. Therefore,
\xi\colon AB\to\partial D_{1}
is conjugated to

P_{4}(z)=z^{4}\colon\phi(AB)\to\mathbb{S}^{1}.

Applying Lemma
8.1
to the conjugate of
\xi
by
\phi
, we obtain a quasiregular extension into the unit disk. Conjugating it back by
\phi
, which is a conformal map, we obtain a quasiregular extension of
\xi
into
D_{1}
, and hence a quasiregular extension of
\xi
to
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
, still denoted by
\xi
.

Finally,
\xi|_{\mathcal{J}(f)}
cannot be extended to a global rational map. Indeed, if it extended to a rational map of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
, then, by uniqueness for analytic maps, it would have to equal to
f^{2}
in
\Delta ABC
and to the identity in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\backslash\left((\Delta ABC)\cup\sqcup_{i=1}^{3}D_{i}\right)
. This is a contradiction since
f^{2}
is not equal to the identity.

Dominating Manifolds by Radial Balls
samuel bronstein

Abstract
. This paper is devoted to a kind of rearrangement of functions on noncollapsed RCD ⁡ ( k , n ) \rm{RCD}(k,n) -spaces, which satisfy a Polyà–Szegö type inequality. This allows to prove functional inequalities on a wide class of manifolds, that we describe. As a consequence, we give a characterization, among Manifolds with lower bounded Ricci curvature, of those admitting a Moser–Trudinger type inequality.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we consider the Moser–Trudinger inequality on noncollapsed
\rm{RCD}(k,n)
-spaces,
a class of metric measure spaces generalizing manifolds with lower bounded Ricci curvature.

First proven on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
by Trudinger in
[
Tru67
]
, the Moser–Trudinger inequality
has since been extended by Moser
[
Mos71
]
to n-dimensional spheres,
and more recently to a wide variety of compact and noncompact manifolds see
[
Li01
,
Li05
,
MS10
,
Yan12
,
MST13
,
Kri19
,
LL21
]
. It always amount to, under some conditions on the
L^{n}
-norm of the gradient
of a function, to get a bound on the integral of
e^{u}-P_{n}(u)
,
where
P_{n}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}
is the first n terms of the decomposition of the exponential into its power series.
Here, in the spirit of Manicini–Sandeep–Tintarev’s inequality on hyperbolic spaces,
we prove the following Moser–Trudinger inequality:

Theorem 1.1

(A)
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be an infinite volume noncollapsed
\rm{RCD}(k,n)
-space,
with nonzero Cheeger isoperimetric constant.
Then there is a constant
C>0
and a maximal value
\beta>0
such that for any
\alpha\leq\beta
,
and
u\in W^{1,n}(X)
such that
\|\nabla u\|_{n}\leq 1
, we have

(1.1)

\int_{X}\exp\big(\alpha u^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\big)-P_{n}(\alpha u^{\frac{n}{n-1}})\leq C

Furthermore
\beta
admits the following explicit expression:

(1.2)

\beta=n(\theta_{\infty}\omega_{n-1})^{\frac{1}{n-1}},\text{ with }\theta_{\infty}=\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}\inf_{x\in X}\frac{\text{Vol}(B(x,r))}{\omega_{n}r^{n}}\,.

See Section 4 for the proof of this theorem.
This theorem characterizes the manifolds admitting that Moser–Trudinger inequality among
manifolds with Ricci curvature bounded below. The presented proof relies on a rearrangement method,
allowing us to compare with the model case of the hyperbolic space
with a conical singularity,
that can be handled as a consequence of
[
MST13
]
. Note that this theorem extends the results of Yang obtained in
[
Yan12
]
for asymptotically negatively curved manifolds.

The proof presented by rearrangements is also adapted to the study of compact manifolds,
or the study of compact noncollapsed RDC(k,n)-spaces. In this setup, we prove a Moser–Trudinger type inequality, specifically for zero-median functions:

Theorem 1.2

(B)
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a compact
\rm{ncRCD}(k,n)
space.
For
u\in W^{1,n}(X)
, denote by
c
its median.
Then for any
\alpha\leq n(\theta_{\infty}\omega_{n-1})^{\frac{1}{n-1}}
,
there is a constant
C
such that

(1.3)

\|\nabla u\|_{n}\leq 1\,\Rightarrow\,\int_{X}e^{\alpha(u-c)^{\frac{n}{n-1}}}-P_{n}(\alpha(u-c)^{\frac{n}{n-1}})\leq C\,.

The constant
C(\alpha)
can be replaced by a constant depending only on
the range of the curvature, the injectivity radius and the Cheeger constant of
M
.

And another one, in the same spirit, for zero-average functions:

Theorem 1.3

(C)
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a compact ncRCD(k,n)-space whose volume is larger than
V_{0}>0

Denote by
\theta_{\infty}
its asymptotic volume ratio,
Then for any
\alpha\leq n(\theta_{\infty})^{\frac{1}{n-1}}
,
there is a constant
C(M)
such that, for any
u\in W^{1,n}_{0}(M)
,
we have

\|\nabla u\|_{n}\leq 1\,\Rightarrow\,\int_{X}e^{\alpha u^{\frac{n}{n-1}}}-P_{n}(\alpha u^{\frac{n}{n-1}})\leq C(M)\,.

The constant
C(X)
can be replaced by a constant depending only on
V_{0}
,
the range of the curvature, the injectivity radius, the Cheeger constant and the
n
-th
spectral gap of
M
.

See Section 5 for the proof of both these theorems.
As far as the author is concerned, the dependency of the constant involved here is new, especially
the fact that it can be made Volume independent. It seems in general to be a hard problem to
find the explicit value of
C
appearing here, a question raised by Dolbeault–Esteban–Jankowiak in
[
DEJ14
]
. The proof presented here relying on rearrangements, we don’t expect the constant
C
found to be sharp.
However it allows to control
C
in large volumes, and the author used a version of that fact for hyperbolic surfaces in
[
Bro23
]
to study minimal surfaces in the hyperbolic

4
-space, and construct examples of hyperbolic structures on nontrivial disc bundles over
a surface.

2.
Context, geometric preliminaries

Here we introduce the geometric notions needed for rearrangements and for the study of the Moser–Trudinger inequality.

2.1.
ncRCD(k,n)-spaces and manifolds

Introduced by Gigli in
[
Gig15
]
, RCD(k,n)-spaces are a family of metric measure spaces
satisfying a curvature condition, and infinitesimally Hilbertian.
This family contains smooth
n
-dimensional manifolds with Ricci curvature larger than
k

and compact manifolds with conical singularities. See also
[
AGS14
]
for the class
\rm{RCD}(k,\infty)
. We refer to Antonelli et. al.
[
APPS24
]
for a proper introduction to these spaces,
as we mainly use their results which justify that non-collapsed
\rm{RCD}(k,n)
are a good family in which to study isoperimetric problems.

Definition 2.1
.

A
\rm{RCD}(k,n)
space
(X,d,\mathcal{H}^{n})
is said to be
non-collapsed

if there is
v_{0}>0
such that for any
x\in X
,

(2.1)

\text{Vol}(B_{x}(1))\geq v_{0}\,.

We say
\rm{ncRCD}(k,n)
-space short for non-collapsed
\rm{RCD}(k,n)
-space.

An important theorem when doing analysis in a
\rm{RCD}(k,n)
-space is the Bishop–Gromov
comparison theorem.

Theorem 2.2

(Bishop–Gromov)
.

Let
X
be a
\rm{ncRCD}(k,n)
-space. Let
v(n,k,r)
denote the volume of the ball of radius
r

in the simply connected Riemannian manifold of constant sectional curvature
k
and dimension
n
,
and by
s(n,k,r)
the surface of the sphere of radius
r
in it.
Then for any
x\in X
, the function
\frac{\text{Vol}(B_{r}(x))}{v(n,k,r)}
is non-increasing in
r
,
and for almost every
r\leq R
,

(2.2)

\frac{\rm{Per}(B_{R}(x))}{s(n,k,R)}\leq\frac{\rm{Per}(B_{r}(x))}{s(n,k,r)}\,.

Moreover, for any
r>0
,

(2.3)

\frac{\rm{Per}(B_{r}(x))}{s(n,k,r)}\leq\frac{\text{Vol}(B_{r}(x))}{v(n,k,r)}\,.

We refer to Villani
[
Vil09
]
for a proof of Bishop–Gromov comparison theorem in CD(k,n)-spaces,
Theorem 30.11.

2.2.
The Cheeger constant and Buser-type inequalities

Let
(X,d,\mathcal{H}^{n})
be a ncRCD(k,n)-space. Its Cheeger constant is defined in the following way:

Definition 2.3

(Cheeger constant)
.

The
Cheeger constant
of
X
, denoted
h(X)
,
is the following constant:

(2.4)

h(X)=\inf\{\frac{\rm{Per}(A)}{\text{Vol}(A)},\,A\subset X,\overline{A}\text{ compact},\,2\text{Vol}(A)\leq\text{Vol}(X)\}

The Cheeger quotient is heavily related to the
p
-th spectral gap,
which we define here for
p>1
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(X,d,\mathcal{H}^{n})
be a connected
\rm{RCD}(k,n)
-space.
If
\text{Vol}(X)=\infty
, we call
p
-th spectral gap the quantity

(2.5)

\lambda_{p}=\inf\big\{\frac{\int_{X}|\nabla f|^{p}}{\int_{X}|f|^{p}},\,f\in W^{1,p}(X)\big\}

If
\text{Vol}(X)<\infty
, we call
p
-th spectral gap the quantity

(2.6)

\lambda_{p}=\inf\big\{\frac{\int_{X}|\nabla f|^{p}}{\inf_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{X}|f-c|^{p}},\,g\in W^{1,p}(X)\big\}\,.

Note that Sobolev spaces are well-defined in the realm of metric measure spaces,
see for instance Hajlasz
[
Haj96
]
.

Note that
\lambda_{2}
is the usual spectral gap of a manifold. In general,

\lambda_{p}
is a synthetic spectral gap for the
p
-Laplacian operator
on
W^{1,p}(M)
.
For manifolds with Ricci curvature bounded below, Matei
[
Mat00
]
, generalizing on the arguments of Buser
[
Bus82
]
proved an upper bound on the
p
-th
spectral gap from the Cheeger constant.

Theorem 2.5
.

Assume
(M,g)
is a noncollapsed
manifold with Ricci curvature bounded below.
Then there is a constant
C(k,n,p)
such that

(2.7)

\lambda_{p}\leq c(n,k,p)(h+h^{p})\,.

To the best of our knowledge, this inequality has not been proved in
the general setup of
\rm{RCD}(k,n)
-spaces yet, apart from the case
p=2
,
see De Ponti–Mondino
[
DPM21
]
.

A control on the Cheeger constant will allow us to control the Moser–Trudinger inequality on the large scale. At small scale, a quantity that will play
a critical role will be the asymptotic growth of balls.

2.3.
The asymptotic growth ratio

Definition 2.6
.

Let
(X,d,\mathcal{H}^{n})
be an
\rm{RCD}(k,n)
-space.
Then its asymptotic growth ratio is the quantity
\theta_{\infty}
:

(2.8)

\theta_{\infty}=\liminf_{r\rightarrow 0}\inf_{x\in X}\frac{\text{Vol}(B(x,r))}{\omega_{n}r^{n}}\,.

Note that on a manifold, this quantity is always less than
1
.
Standard considerations on the volume growth of balls shows that
\theta_{\infty}=1
for any smooth compact manifold, or on a manifold with bounded sectional curvature and lower bounded injectivity radius.
Antonelli et.al.
[
APPS24
]
have studied this ratio, and shown that it is nonzero for noncollapsed
\rm{RCD}(k,n)
-spaces.

3.
Domination by radial balls

3.1.
Balls with radial metric and the isoperimetric profile

Let
n\geq 2
and
\mathbb{B}^{n}
denote the unit ball of dimension
n
.

Definition 3.1
.

A metric
\sigma
on
\mathbb{B}^{n}
is said to be
radial
if there is a continuous
function
g:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\ast}
such that
\sigma
can be written, in polar coordinates:

(3.1)

\sigma=dr^{2}+g(r)^{2}d\theta^{2}\,,

where
d\theta^{2}
denotes the round metric on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.

Note that if we want to avoid a cone-type singularity at zero,
the function
g
must satisfy
\frac{g(r)}{r}\rightarrow 1
when
r
goes to zero.

A convenient notion is that of the radial profile of such a ball:

Definition 3.2
.

Let
(\mathbb{B}^{n},\sigma)
be a radial ball. Then its
radial profile

\varphi_{\sigma}
is the function which
to the centered ball of volume
t
gives its perimeter. More precisely,
denoting by
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

the
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure on
(\mathbb{B}^{n},\sigma)
,
the radial profile is the function

(3.2)

\varphi_{\sigma}:\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}[0,\text{Vol}(B^{n},\sigma)]&\mapsto\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\ast}\\
t&\rightarrow\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S(0,T))\end{array}\right.

where
T
is the unique
T>0
such that
\text{Vol}(B(0,T))=t
.

Note that
T
is well-defined since we assumed
g>0
. Also, the function
T\mapsto\text{Vol}(B(0,T)

is
C^{1}
-regular, and an implicit function theorem shows that
\varphi_{\sigma}
is continuous,
strictly increasing if
g
is.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
. We call
hyperbolic trumpet of cone angle
\alpha
,
denoted
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{\alpha}
, the ball with the radial metric

\mathbb{B}^{n},dr^{2}+\alpha^{\frac{2}{n-1}}\sinh^{2}(r)d\theta^{2}
.

Remark
.

The hyperbolic trumpet of cone angle
\alpha
is the pullback of the hyperbolic metric on
\mathbb{B}^{n}
by the map
(r,\theta)\mapsto(r^{\alpha},\theta)
, hence the cone angle singularity at zero.
Note that it is not locally hyperbolic, even if its large scale geometry is similar.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
be a continuous function vanishing only at zero,
and such that
\frac{f(t)}{t^{1-\frac{1}{n}}}\rightarrow\alpha>0
at zero.
Then there is a ball with radial metric
dr^{2}+g(r)^{2}d\theta
whose radial profile
is
f
. It admits a cone singularity of angle
\frac{\alpha^{n}}{n^{n-1}\omega_{n-1}}
at zero.

If moreover, there is
h>0
and
t_{0}>0
such that
f(t)=ht
for
t\geq t_{0}
,
then the metric is radially bilipschitz conformal to a hyperbolic plane with cone singularity
\frac{\alpha^{n}}{n^{n-1}\omega_{n-1}}
.

Proof.

For
g
a
C^{0}
-regular function,
f
is the radial profile of
dr^{2}+g(r)^{2}d\theta^{2}

if and only if:

\forall s>0,\,G^{\prime}(s)=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}f(\omega_{n-1}G(s))\,,\,G(s)=\int_{0}^{s}g(u)^{n-1}du\,.

In order to solve it locally, introduce the function
F(s)=G(s)^{\frac{1}{n}}
.
Then it must satisfy the ODE:

F^{\prime}(s)=\frac{1}{n\omega_{n-1}}\frac{f(\omega_{n-1}G(s))}{G(s)^{1-\frac{1}{n}}}=\frac{1}{n\omega_{n-1}}\frac{f(\omega_{n-1}F(s)^{n})}{F(s)^{n-1}}\,.

From the asymptotics of
f
at zero, we can see that there is a unique local
solution
F
to this ODE such that

F(0)=0,\,F^{\prime}(0)=\frac{\alpha}{n\omega_{n-1}^{\frac{1}{n}}}>0\,.

Since
f
is positive continuous, it is clear that this solution can be extended uniquely to a solution defined on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, nondecreasing, vanishing only at zero.

This leads to corresponding functions
G
and
g
, satisfying the asymptotics

\frac{g(s)}{s}\underset{s\rightarrow 0}{\rightarrow}\frac{\alpha^{\frac{n}{n-1}}}{n\omega_{n-1}^{\frac{1}{n-1}}}\,.

Hence the metric is nonsingular at zero if and only if
\alpha=n^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\omega_{n-1}^{\frac{1}{n}}
, and otherwise it admits a cone type singularity, of total angle
\frac{\alpha^{n}}{n^{n-1}\omega_{n-1}}
.

Assume now that there exists
h>0
such that
f(t)=ht
for
t\geq t_{0}
.
That means that for
t\geq t_{0}
we have

F(t)=F(t_{0})e^{\frac{h}{n}(t-t_{0})},\,G(t)=G(t_{0})e^{h(t-t_{0})},\,g(t)=g(t_{0})e^{\frac{h}{n-1}(t-t_{0})}\,.

In particular the identity map is a Bilipschitz conformal transform
between
\mathbb{B}^{n},dr^{2}+g(r)^{2}d\theta^{2}
and
\mathbb{B}^{n},\big(\frac{\alpha^{\frac{n}{n-1}}}{hn\omega_{n-1}^{\frac{1}{n-1}}}\big)^{2}\sinh(hr)^{2}d\theta^{2}
. Composing with the radial map
h\mapsto hr
, we get a Bilipschitz
conformal transform to a hyperbolic space with cone angle
\frac{\alpha^{n}}{n^{n-1}\omega_{n-1}}
, as desired.
∎

Given a Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
, we denote again by
Vol
its volume form and by
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

its
n-1
-dimensional Hausdorff measure. We then consider its isoperimetric profile:

Definition 3.5
.

The isoperimetric profile
\Phi
of
(M,g)
is defined for
0\leq t\leq\text{Vol}(M)
:

(3.3)

\Phi(t)=\inf\{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial A),A\subset M,\text{Vol}(A)=t,\overline{A}\text{ compact }\}

The isoperimetric profile has been the object of a lot of research, specifically in the field
of isoperimetric inequalities, see for instance
[
Gal88
,
BM82
]
.Here we will say that our manifold is dominated if its isoperimetric profile is larger
than the radial profile of a ball of same dimension.

Definition 3.6
.

We say that
(M,g)
is
dominated
by the radial ball
(\mathbb{B}^{n},\sigma)
if its isoperimetric profile
satisfies
\text{Vol}(\mathbb{B}^{n})\geq\frac{\text{Vol}(M)}{2}
and

(3.4)

\forall t\leq\frac{\text{Vol}(M)}{2},\,\Phi(t)\geq\varphi_{\sigma}(t)\,.

Note that the definition makes sense when
\text{Vol}(M)=\infty
as well, in which case the
requirement
\text{Vol}(\mathbb{B}^{n})=\infty
is equivalent to the completeness of
\sigma
.
That definition extends verbatim to ncRCD(K,N)-spaces.

We finish this subsection with the notion of ”well-behaved” radial ball, in which isoperimetric and
radial profile coïncide:

Definition 3.7
.

We say that the radial ball
(\mathbb{B}^{n},dr^{2}+g(r)^{2}d\theta^{2})
is
well-behaved

if its isoperimetric profile equals its radial profile, i.e. it is well-behaved if it
is self-dominated.

Lemma 3.8
.

For any
\alpha\leq 1
the hyperbolic trumpet with cone angle
\alpha
is well-behaved.

Proof.

It is well-known that balls are solution to the isoperimetric problem in hyperbolic space,
see Schmidt
[
Sch43
]
. For a hyperbolic trumpet of cone angle
\alpha
, pick
D
a domain,
denote by
\text{Vol}(D)
its volume and
\text{Vol}_{hyp}(D)
its volume for the hyperbolic metric.
Similarly, consider
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(D)
and
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{hyp}(D)
its perimeters.
Than a quick computation shows that

\text{Vol}(D)=\alpha\text{Vol}_{hyp}(D),\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(D)\geq\alpha\mathcal{H}_{hyp}^{n-1}(D)

with equality when
D
is a centered ball.
This proves that the centered balls are solutions to the isoperimetric problem
in hyperbolic trumpets, hence their isoperimetric profile equals their radial profile.
∎

3.2.
Rearrangement to a dominating ball

Here we fix
(M,g,\mathcal{H}^{n})
a ncRCD(K,N)-space
dominated by a radial ball
(\mathbb{B}^{n},\sigma)
.
We want to build a rearrangement, that is a map

W^{1,p}(M)_{>0}\rightarrow W^{1,p}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
, mapping
f
to
f^{\ast}
, satisfying

(3.5)

\|\nabla f^{\ast}\|_{p}\leq\|\nabla f\|_{p},\,\text{Vol}(f^{\ast}\geq t)=\text{Vol}(f\geq t)\,\forall t\in\mathbb{R}.

In order for this to be possible, a first condition is that
\text{Vol}(M)=\infty
.
We will need a different appropriate rearrangement in the finite volume case,
considering only positive functions whose support ihas volume less than half of
\text{Vol}(M)
.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
(X,d,\mathcal{H}^{n})
be an infinite volume
ncRCD(K,n)-space
dominated by a radial ball
(\mathbb{B}^{n},\sigma)
.
Consider the radial rearrangement, from
W^{1,p}(X)_{\geq 0}
to
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{B}^{n},\sigma)

which to a nonnegative
u\in W^{1,p}(X)
associates
\hat{u}\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{B}^{n})

the unique radial decreasing left-continuous function satisfying

(3.6)

\forall t>0,\,\text{Vol}(u>t)=\mathcal{H}^{n}(\hat{u}>t)\,.

Then it satisfies

(3.7)

\|\nabla\hat{u}\|_{p}\leq\|\nabla u\|_{p}\,.

Proof.

Let
u\in W^{1,p}(X)
be a positive function with bounded variation.
First assume that it is smooth and compactly supported.
Applying the co-area formula (see for instance Huang
[
Hua24
]
, Miranda
[
Mir03
]
), we have for every
t
in the range of
u
:

A(t)=\int_{s\geq t}\int_{u=s}\frac{1}{|\nabla u|}d\nu\,.

,
with the convention
\frac{1}{|\nabla u|}=0
if
|\nabla u|=0
. See
Mondino–Semola
[
MS20
]
, Lemma 3.10., for a justification of that choice.
In particular,
A
is differentiable almost everywhere, and satisfies:

-A^{\prime}(t)=\int_{\{u=t\}}\frac{1}{|\nabla u|}d\nu\,.

Also, denoting
l(t)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\{u=t\})
, it is almost everywhere finite,

l(t)=\int_{\{u=t\}}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq(\int_{\{u=t\}}|\nabla u|^{p-1})^{\frac{1}{p}}(\int_{\{u=t\}}\frac{1}{|\nabla u|})^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\,.

From which we deduce that
(-A^{\prime})^{1-p}l^{p}
is integrable, with the bound:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}(-A^{\prime}(s))^{1-p}l(s)^{p}ds\leq\|\nabla u\|_{p}^{p}\,.

Now consider the radial rearrangement
\hat{u}
, and the corresponding functions
\hat{A}
,

\hat{l}
defined by

\hat{A}(t)=\text{Vol}(\{\hat{u}>t\})=A(t)\,,\,\hat{l}(t)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\{\hat{u}=t\})\,.

By construction of the radial profile, we have

\hat{l}(t)=\varphi(A(t))\,.

And since
\hat{u}
is radial, the Hölder inequality used above becomes an equality,
and we obtain

\|\nabla\hat{u}\|_{p}^{p}=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}(-A^{\prime}(s))^{1-p}\hat{l}(s)^{p}ds=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}(-A^{\prime}(s)^{1-p}\varphi(A(s))^{p}ds\,.

Finally, because of the domination assumption, we know that

\varphi(A(s))\leq\Phi(A(s))\leq l(s)\,.

Hence we have shown that

\|\nabla\hat{u}\|_{p}\leq\|\nabla u\|_{p}\,,

as desired.

Let now
u\in W^{1,p}(X)
a positive function, not necessarily of bounded variation.
Consider
u_{k}\rightarrow u
a regularizing sequence, converging towards
u
in
W^{1,p}(M)
.
From the discussion above, the sequence
\hat{u}_{k}
is bounded in
W^{1,p}_{rad}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
,
hence there is
v
such that
\hat{u}_{k}\rightarrow v
weakly, and since they’re radial we
can assume they converge uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{B}^{n}-\{0\}
.
We claim that
v=\hat{u}
. The proof is standard, and can be found in
Brothers–Ziemer
[
BZ88
]
. We reproduce it here for the reader’s convenience.
Let
t>0
be a value such that
\text{Vol}(\{u=t\})=0
. This is true for all but at most countably

t>0
, since
u^{p}
is integrable. Define the sets

E_{t}^{k}=\{u_{k}>t\},\,E_{t}=\{u>t\},\,\hat{E}_{t}^{k}=\{\hat{u}_{k}>t\},E_{t}^{v}=\{v>t\}\,.

From the convergence of
u_{k}
, it can be seen that the symmetric difference satisfies:

\text{Vol}(E_{t}^{k}\Delta E_{t}^{u})\rightarrow 0\,.

Indeed, for any
\eta>0
we can control the volume

\text{Vol}(E_{t}^{k}\Delta E_{t}^{u})\leq\text{Vol}(\{|u-u_{k}|\geq\eta\})+\text{Vol}(t-\eta\leq u\leq t+\eta)\,

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Since
\text{Vol}(\{u=t\}=0)
, there is
\eta>0
such that

\text{Vol}(|u-t|\leq\eta)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\,.

Also, by Markov’s inequality

\text{Vol}(\{|u-u_{k}|\geq\eta\})\leq\frac{\|u-u_{k}\|_{p}^{p}}{\eta^{p}}

which is smaller than
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
for
k
large enough.
Hence we have proven the convergence, for all but countably many values of
t
:

\text{Vol}(\{u_{k}>t\})\rightarrow\text{Vol}(\{u>t\})

Similarly, the uniform convergence on compact sets implies that for all but countably
many values of
t
,

\text{Vol}(\{\hat{u}_{k}>t\}\rightarrow\text{Vol}(\{v>t\})\,.

All in all, for almost every
t
,

\text{Vol}(v>t)=\text{Vol}(u>t)

and
v
is radially symmetric decreasing. Hence
v=\hat{u}
, at all but countably many values. Since at those values we can still characterize the volume

\text{Vol}(v>t)=\lim_{\eta\rightarrow 0}\text{Vol}(v>t+\eta)

we obtain that
v=\hat{u}
, as claimed.
Finally, since
v\in W^{1,p}
and we have the weak convergence
in
W^{1,p}
of
\hat{u}_{k}
towards
v
, we deduce that

\|\nabla\hat{u}\|_{p}\leq\liminf\|\nabla\hat{u}_{k}\|_{p}\leq\liminf\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{p}=\|\nabla u\|_{p}\,.

So
\|\nabla\hat{u}\|_{p}\leq\|\nabla u\|_{p}
, as desired.
∎

Remark
.

This rather technical process is necessary since the rearrangement is not continuous.
See Almgren–Lieb
[
AL89
]
for a discussion of the noncontinuity of rearrangement.
It seems, from the works of Brothers and Ziemer, that one could extend the proof
to include the case of
W^{1,1}(M)
, however our main interest is in
W^{1,n}(M)
,
so we won’t do it here.

Since the proof only used the fact that
u_{k}
converges towards
u
weakly, we have the following
corollary

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a
\rm{ncRCD}(k,n)
-space dominated by a radial ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
,
and
p>1
.
Then the radial decreasing rearrangement from
W^{1,p}(X)
to
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{B}^{n})

which to
u
associates
\widehat{|u|}
is weakly continuous.

3.3.
Characterization of dominated manifolds

Being dominated by a nonsingular radial ball implies strong properties of the isoperimetric profile.
Here we characterize dominated manifolds among noncollapsed manifolds with Ricci curvature bounded below.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be an
\rm{RCD}(k,n)
-space.
Then it is dominated by a nonsingular radial ball if and only if

(3.8)

\underset{x\in M}{\inf}\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(B_{x}(r))}{w_{n}r^{n}}\underset{r\rightarrow 0}{\rightarrow}1\,.

Proof.

Assume first that
(X,d,\mu)
is dominated by a nonsingular radial ball.
Then there is a function
f(t)
, going to
0
at zero, such that the isoperimetric profile satisfies,
on a neighborhood of zero,

\Phi(t)\geq f(t)t^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\,.

We claim that it implies
(X,d,\mu)
to be noncollapsed:
Indeed, let
\eta>0
such that the isoperimetric profile
\Phi
of
X
satisfies:

\Phi(t)\geq Ct^{1-\frac{1}{n}},\,\forall t\in[0,\eta]

for some positive constant
C>0
. Such an
\eta
exists since it exists for the radial profile
of the dominating radial ball. Now, if
(X,d,\mu)
is collapsed, for any
j>0

there is an
x_{j}\in X
such that

\text{Vol}(B_{\eta}(x_{j}))\leq\frac{1}{j}\,.

In particular, there must be some
r_{j}\in[\frac{\eta}{2},\eta]
such that

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{r_{j}}(x_{j}))\leq 2\text{Vol}(B_{\eta}(x_{j}))

Now by the Bishop–Gromov comparison theorem,
we can control the volume of the balls of radius
r_{j}
in the following way:

\text{Vol}(B_{r_{j}}(x_{j}))\geq\frac{b(k,r_{j})}{b(k,\eta)}\text{Vol}(B_{\eta}(x_{j}))\,.

Combining this controls, and by definition of the isoperimetric profile, we obtain, for
j
large enough:

2\text{Vol}(B_{\eta}(x_{j}))\geq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{r_{j}}(x_{j}))\geq\Phi(\text{Vol}(B_{r_{j}}(x_{j})))\geq C^{\prime}\text{Vol}(B_{\eta}(x_{j}))^{1-\frac{1}{n}}\,.

for some constant
C^{\prime}>0
independent of
j
.
Such an inequality is absurd as
j
goes to infinity, hence
(X,d,\mu)
is noncollapsed.

In this setup, the isoperimetric profile has been studied by Antonelli et al
[
APPS24
]
,
they prove that the ratio
\frac{\Phi(t)}{t^{1-\frac{1}{n}}}
converges at zero to some

\alpha>0
, where
\alpha
happens to be the asymptotic ratio of balls:

\alpha=\underset{r\rightarrow 0}{\lim}\big(\underset{x\in M}{\inf}\frac{\text{Vol}(B_{x}(r))}{\omega_{n}r^{n}}\big)\,.

Since the isoperimetric profile dominates the radial profile of a ball, we deduce that
\alpha=1
, as desired.

Conversely, assume there is such a function
f
. Then it is clear that the space
X

is noncollapsed, so the isoperimetric profile is a continuous function cf
[
MFN19
,
FN14
]
,
and it is nonzero on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\ast}
, as a consequence of the differential inequality it
satisfies.Hence it is larger than some smooth function
\varphi
, positive on
\mathbb{R}_{\ast}^{+}
,
and such that
\frac{\varphi(t)}{\omega_{n}t^{\frac{n-1}{n}}}
goes to one at zero.
Such a function is always the radial profile of a unit ball, proving our claim.
∎

If the space
(X,\mu)
has a nonzero spectral gap, it is possible to assume the same about a dominating radial ball:

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a noncollapsed
\rm{RCD}(k,n)
-space.
Assume it has a positive Cheeger constant
h>0
.
Denote by
\alpha
the asymptotic growth ratio of balls:

(3.9)

\alpha=\underset{r\rightarrow 0}{\lim}\big(\underset{x\in X}{\inf}\frac{\text{Vol}(B_{x}(r))}{\omega_{n}r^{n}}\big)\,.

Then there is a radial ball dominating
(X,\mu)
,
which is radially bilipschitz conformal to a hyperbolic trumpet of cone
angle
\alpha
.

Proof.

From
[
APPS24
]
, we know that
\alpha
appears in the asymptotics of the isoperimetric
profile of
(X,\mu)
:

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{\Phi(t)}{\alpha t^{\frac{n-1}{n}}}=1\,.

In particular,
\Phi
is greater than a continuous function
\varphi
satisfying

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{\varphi(t)}{\alpha t^{\frac{n-1}{n}}}=1\,,

and such that
\varphi(t)=ht
for
t\geq t_{0}
.
We have seen that such a function
\varphi
is the radial profile of
a radial ball radially bilipschitz conformal to a hyperbolic trumpet of cone angle
\alpha
,
which dominates
(X,\mu)
by construction.
∎

4.
Application to the Moser–Trudinger inequality

In this section, we consider the following Moser–Trudinger type inequality:

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a
\rm{RCD}(k,n)
-space of infinite volume, and
\alpha>0
.
We say that
(X,\mu)
satisfies the Moser–Trudinger inequality
(MT)_{\alpha}
if
there is a constant
C>0
such that, for any
u\in W^{1,n}(X)
:

(4.1)

\int|\nabla u|^{n}\leq 1\,\Rightarrow\int\exp\big(\alpha u^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\big)-P_{n}(\alpha u^{\frac{n}{n-1}})\leq C\,.

where
P_{n}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}
.

Remark
.

Since
X
has infinite volume, in general there is no embedding
W^{1,n}(X)\rightarrow L^{p}(X)

for
p<n
, hence the necessity of the polynomial correction term.
In this whole section, the notation
u^{r}
is used to denote
|u|^{r}
, unless otherwise specified.

Satisfying such an inequality implies a nice geometry at small and large scale of the space.
That geometry will be encapsulated by two quantities, the Cheeger constant and the asymptotic growth ratio, see definitions
2.3
,
2.6
.
Denote also by
\lambda_{p}
the spectral gap of the
p
-laplacian. Recall that by results
of Buser, Matei,
\lambda_{p}
is controlled by the bound on the curvature and the Cheeger
constant for manifolds.
We will prove here the following characterization:

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be an
\rm{RCD}(k,n)
-space of infinite volume.
Assume its Cheeger constant is nonzero, and let
\alpha
satisfy:

(4.2)

\alpha\leq n(\theta_{\infty}\omega_{n-1})^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\,.

Then
(X,\mu)
satisfies the Moser–Trudinger inequality
(MT)_{\alpha}
.

In the case of manifolds, this is actually a characterization of manifolds satisfying the Moser–Trudinger inequality among manifolds with Ricci curvature bounded below.

Theorem 4.3
.

A complete Riemannian
n
-dimensional manifold
(M,g)
with Ricci curvature bounded below
satisfies the Moser–Trudinger inequality
(MT)_{\alpha}
if and only if
its Cheeger constant is nonzero and
\alpha
satisfies

(4.3)

\alpha\leq n(\theta_{\infty}\omega_{n-1})^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\,.

4.1.
Counterexamples to the Moser–Trudinger inequality

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a collapsed
\rm{RCD}(k,n)
-space,
i.e. such that

(4.4)

\inf_{x\in X}\text{Vol}(B_{x}(1))=0\,.

Then for any
\alpha>0
, there is a sequence
u_{n}\in W^{1,n}(X)
such that

(4.5)

\int\|\nabla u\|^{n}\leq 1\quad\int_{X}\exp\big(\alpha u^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\big)-P_{n}(\alpha u^{\frac{n}{n-1}})\rightarrow+\infty\,.

Proof.

From the Bishop–Gromov comparison theorem, there is a constant
C(k)>0

such that, for any
x\in X
:

\text{Vol}(B_{x}(2))\leq C(k)\text{Vol}(B_{x}(1))\,.

Consider
r_{m}\rightarrow 0
, and consider a sequence
x_{m}\in X
such that

\text{Vol}(B_{x_{m}}(1))=r_{m}\,.

Consider the function
u_{m}\in W^{1,n}(X)
defined by

u_{m}(y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}T_{m}&\text{ if }d(x_{m},y)\leq 1\\
T_{m}(2-d(x,y))&\text{ if }d(x_{m},y)\in[1,2]\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

for some
T_{m}>0
to be specified later.

Then we have the following estimates:

\int\|\nabla u_{m}\|^{n}\leq T_{m}^{n}\text{Vol}(B_{x_{m}}(2))-\text{Vol}(B_{x_{m}}(1))\leq T_{m}^{n}(C(k)-1)r_{m}\,.

Choosing then

T_{m}^{n}=\frac{1}{(C(k)-1)r_{m}}\,

we obtain

\|\nabla u_{m}\|_{n}\leq 1\,,

while

\int\exp\big(\alpha u_{m}^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\big)-P_{n}(\alpha u^{\frac{n}{n-1}})\geq r_{m}\bigg[\exp\big(\frac{\alpha}{r_{m}^{\frac{1}{n-1}}(C(k)-1)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}}\big)-P_{n}(\frac{\alpha}{r_{m}^{\frac{1}{n-1}}(C(k)-1)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}})\bigg]\,.

which is arbitrarily large when
r_{m}\rightarrow 0
, as desired.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold with Ricci curvature bounded below.
Assume its Cheeger constant
h
is zero.
Then it doesn’t satisfy any
(MT)_{\alpha}
for any
\alpha>0
.

Proof.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be with Ricci curvature bounded below, and zero Cheeger constant.
The generalized Buser inequality, Theorem
2.5
, shows that for any
C>0
,
there is a function
u\in W^{1,n}(M)
satisfying

\|\nabla u\|_{n}\leq 1\text{ and }\|u\|_{n}\geq C\,.

In particular,
(M,g)
does not satisfy the Moser–Trudinger inequality
(MT)_{\alpha}

for any
\alpha>0
.
∎

Remark
.

The same proof shows that any
\rm{RCD}(k,n)
-space with zero
n
-th spectral gap
cannot satisfy
(MT)_{\alpha}
for any
\alpha>0
. What’s missing in general
is that a priori there could exist an
\rm{RCD}(k,n)
-space with zero Cheeger
constant, yet nonzero
n
-th spectral gap. De Ponti–Mondino
[
DPM21
]
showed it doesn’t exist in the case
n=2
, relying on Heat flow arguments.

For noncollapsed
\rm{RCD}(k,n)
-spaces, we first give an upper bound on the validity of the Moser–Trudinger inequality:

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a noncollapsed
\rm{RCD}(k,n)
-space. Denote
by
\theta_{\infty}
the asymptotic growth of balls

(4.6)

\theta_{\infty}=\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}\inf_{x\in M}\frac{\text{Vol}(B_{x}(r))}{\omega_{n}r^{n}}\,.

Then for any
\alpha>n(\theta_{\infty}\omega_{n-1})^{\frac{1}{n-1}}
,
there is a sequence
(u_{m})\in W^{1,p}(X)
such that

(4.7)

\|\nabla u_{m}\|_{n}\leq 1\text{ and }\int_{X}e^{\alpha u_{m}^{\frac{n}{n-1}}}-P_{n}(\alpha u_{m}^{\frac{n}{n-1}})\rightarrow+\infty\,.

Proof.

It mainly comes from the Bishop–Gromov comparison theorem. Fix
\theta>\theta_{\infty}
,

r>0
and
x_{r}\in X
such that

\text{Vol}(B_{x}(r))=\theta b(k,r)\,.

From the Bishop–Gromov comparison theorem, we deduce that for any
s>r

\text{Vol}(B_{x}(s))\leq\theta b(k,s)

Hence

\frac{\text{Vol}(B_{x}(s))-\text{Vol}(B_{x}(r))}{s-r}\leq\frac{\theta b(k,s)-\theta b(k,r)}{s-r}

from which we deduce

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{x}(r))\leq\theta s(k,r)\,.

And again, from the Bishop–Gromov comparison theorem, for any
s\geq r
,

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S_{x}(s))\leq\theta s(k,s)\,.

To use the counterexamples used by Moser in
[
Mos71
]
, we work at a scale
so that the curvature doesn’t play too much of a role. Let
R>0
and
\eta>0

be so that

\forall s\in(0,R),\,1-\eta\leq\frac{b(k,s)}{b(0,s)}\leq 1+\eta\,.

Let
u:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be radially symmetric around
x
.
Then one has, using
r
as the radial geodesic coordinate around
x
,
and introduce
t
the variable such that
e^{-t}=\frac{r^{n}}{R^{n}}
:

\displaystyle\int_{X}|\nabla u|^{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S(0,r))|\partial_{r}u|^{n}dr

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}n^{n-1}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S(0,e^{-\frac{t}{n}}))|\partial_{t}u|^{n}e^{\frac{n-1}{n}t}dt

Consider now the function
u
radial around
x_{r}
given by the following, and denote

t_{0}=-n\ln(\frac{r}{R})
.

u(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}Ct&\text{ if }0\leq t\leq t_{0}\\
Ct_{0}&\text{ if }t_{0}\leq t\end{array}\right.

whose support is
B(x_{r},R)
. Then we can compute the norm of its gradient

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{n}^{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t_{0}}n^{n-1}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S(0,e^{-\frac{t}{n}}))C^{n}e^{\frac{n-1}{n}t}dt

\displaystyle\leq C^{n}n^{n-1}\theta(1+\eta)\omega_{n-1}t_{0}

While we have the lower bound

\displaystyle I(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\exp\big(\alpha u^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\big)-P_{n}(\alpha u^{\frac{n}{n-1}})\geq\text{Vol}(B(x,r))\big[\exp(\alpha C^{\frac{n}{n-1}}t_{0}^{\frac{n}{n-1}})-P_{n}(\alpha C^{\frac{n}{n-1}}t_{0}^{\frac{n}{n-1}})\big]

\displaystyle\geq\theta(1-\eta)\omega_{n-1}\frac{r^{n}}{n}\big[\exp(\alpha C^{\frac{n}{n-1}}t_{0}^{\frac{n}{n-1}})-P_{n}(\alpha C^{\frac{n}{n-1}}t_{0}^{\frac{n}{n-1}})\big]

\displaystyle\geq\theta(1-\eta)\omega_{n-1}\frac{r^{n}}{n}\big[\exp(\alpha C^{\frac{n}{n-1}}n^{\frac{n}{n-1}}|\ln(\frac{r}{R})|^{\frac{n}{n-1}})-P_{n}(\alpha C^{\frac{n}{n-1}}n^{\frac{n}{n-1}}|\ln(\frac{r}{R})|^{\frac{n}{n-1}})\big]

Choosing for
C
the value

C=\frac{1}{n\theta^{\frac{1}{n}}(1+\eta)^{\frac{1}{n}}\omega_{n-1}^{\frac{1}{n}}|\ln(\frac{r}{R})|^{\frac{1}{n}}}

we obtain the controls

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{n}

\displaystyle\leq 1

\displaystyle I(u)

\displaystyle\geq\theta(1-\eta)\omega_{n-1}\frac{r^{n}}{n}\big[\exp(\frac{\alpha}{(\theta\omega_{n-1}(1+\eta))^{\frac{1}{n-1}}}|\ln(\frac{r}{R})|)-P_{n}(\frac{\alpha}{(\theta\omega_{n-1}(1+\eta))^{\frac{1}{n-1}}}|\ln(\frac{r}{R})|)\big]\,.

When
r\rightarrow 0
, the quantity
I(u)
diverges as soon as

n<\frac{\alpha}{(\theta\omega_{n-1}(1+\eta))^{\frac{1}{n-1}}}\,.

Since
\theta
can be chosen arbitrarily close to
\theta_{\infty}
and
\eta
arbitrarily close
to
1
, this shows that for any
\alpha
satisfying

\alpha>n(\theta_{\infty}\omega_{n-1})^{\frac{1}{n-1}},

there is a sequence
(u_{m})\in W^{1,n}(X)
such that

\|\nabla u_{m}\|_{n}\leq 1\text{ and }\int_{M}\exp(\alpha u_{m}^{\frac{n}{n-1}})-P_{n}(\alpha u^{\frac{n}{n-1}})\rightarrow+\infty\,,

as claimed.
∎

4.2.
Positive results with regard to the Moser–Trudinger inequality

We will deduce all our results from the study in the hyperbolic space,
which was covered in Mancini–Sandeep
[
MS10
]
for the plane,
and Mancini–Sandeep–Tintarev
[
MST13
]
in the higher-dimensional case.

Theorem 4.7

(Mancini–Sandeep, Mancini–Sandeep–Tintarev)
.

Consider
\mathbb{H}^{n}
the
n
-dimensional hyperbolic space.
There is a constant
C(n)>0
such that, for any
u\in W^{1,n}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, the
following implication is true:

(4.8)

\|\nabla u\|_{n}\leq 1\,\Rightarrow\,\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\exp(\alpha_{n}u^{\frac{n}{n-1}})-P_{n}(\alpha u^{\frac{n-1}{n}})\leq C(n)\,.

where
\alpha=\alpha_{n}=n\omega_{n-1}^{\frac{1}{n-1}}
.

A first corollary concerns the hyperbolic trumpets with cone singularity at zero.

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
\beta\in(0,1)
and
let
\mathbb{B}^{n},dr^{2}+\beta^{\frac{2}{n-1}}\sinh(r)^{2}d\theta^{2}
be the hyperbolic trumpet of cone angle
\beta
.
Then for any
\alpha\leq n(\beta\omega_{n-1})^{\frac{1}{n-1}}
,
the hyperbolic trumpet satisfies
(MT)_{\alpha}

Proof.

Fix
\alpha\leq n\beta\omega_{n-1}^{\frac{1}{n-1}}
.
First, since
\beta<1
the hyperbolic trumpet of cone angle
\beta
is well-behaved by Lemma
3.8
.
Hence the rearrangement map
u\mapsto\hat{u}
on itself satisfies:

\|\nabla\hat{u}\|_{n}\leq\|\nabla u\|_{n}\,\text{and}\,\int e^{\alpha u^{\frac{n}{n-1}}}-P_{n}(\alpha u^{\frac{n}{n-1}})=\int e^{\alpha\hat{u}^{\frac{n}{n-1}}}-P_{n}(\alpha\hat{u}^{\frac{n}{n-1}})\,.

In other words, it is enough to prove the Moser–Trudinger inequality on radial functions
on the hyperbolic trumpet.
Now consider the identity map

F:\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}W^{1,n}_{rad}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{\beta})&\rightarrow W^{1,n}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\\
u&\mapsto u\end{array}\right.

A quick computation shows that

\|\nabla u\|_{n}=\beta^{\frac{1}{n}}\|\nabla F(u)\|_{n}\text{ and }\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{\beta}}e^{\alpha u^{\frac{n}{n-1}}}-P_{n}(\alpha u^{\frac{n}{n-1}})=\beta\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}e^{\alpha F(u)^{\frac{n}{n-1}}}-P_{n}(\alpha F(u)^{\frac{n}{n-1}})

Assume that
u\in W^{1,n}_{rad}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{\beta})
satisfies
\|\nabla u\|_{n}\leq 1
.
Denoting
v=\beta^{\frac{1}{n}}F(u)\in W^{1,n}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, it satisfies

\|\nabla v\|_{n}\leq 1\,.

Also, by assumption

\alpha\beta^{\frac{-1}{n-1}}\leq n\omega_{n-1}^{\frac{1}{n-1}}=\alpha_{n}\,.

By the Mancini–Sandeep–Tintarev Theorem, there is a constant
C(n)

such that

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}e^{\alpha\beta^{\frac{-1}{n-1}}v^{\frac{n}{n-1}}}-P_{n}(\alpha\beta^{\frac{-1}{n-1}}v)\leq C(n)

i.e.

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{\beta}}e^{\alpha u^{\frac{n}{n-1}}}-P_{n}(\alpha u^{\frac{n}{n-1}})\leq C(n)\,.

So the hyperbolic trumpet
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{\beta}
satisfies
(MT)_{\alpha}
for

\alpha\leq n(\beta\omega_{n-1})^{\frac{1}{n-1}}
, as claimed.
∎

Satisfying the Moser–Trudinger inequality is invariant under Bilipschitz conformal
transforms, as the following lemma states:

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
(M_{1},g_{1})
and
(M_{2},g_{2})
be complete Riemannian manifolds,
and assume that there exists a bilipschitz conformal homeomorphism
f:M_{1}\rightarrow M_{2}
.
Then for
\alpha>0
,
(M_{1},g_{1})
satisfies
(MT)_{\alpha}

if and only if
(M_{2},g_{2})
satisfies
(MT)_{\alpha}
.

Proof.

Since the statement is symmetric, it is enough to assume that
(M_{1},g_{1})

satisfies
(MT)_{\alpha}
and to prove that
(M_{2},g_{2})
satisfies it too.
Let
C>0
be such that, for any
u\in W^{1,n}(M_{1})
:

\|\nabla u\|_{n}^{n}\leq 1\,\Rightarrow\,\int e^{\alpha u^{\frac{n}{n-1}}}-P_{n}(\alpha u^{\frac{n}{n-1}})\leq C\,.

Let
v\in W^{1,n}(M_{2})
such that
\|\nabla v\|_{n}\leq 1
.
Since
f
is conformal, we have
\|\nabla(v\circ f)\|_{n}=\|\nabla v\|_{n}\leq 1
.
And since it is bilipschitz conformal, we can control the integral

\int_{M_{2}}e^{\alpha v^{\frac{n}{n-1}}}-P_{n}(\alpha v^{\frac{n}{n-1}})\leq\rm{Lip}(f)\int_{M_{1}}e^{\alpha(v\circ f)^{\frac{n}{n-1}}}-P_{n}(\alpha(v\circ f)^{\frac{n}{n-1}})\leq\rm{Lip}(f)C\,.

Hence
(M_{2},g_{2})
satisfies
(MT)_{\alpha}
, as claimed.
∎

Finally, we are able to conclude with the proofs of Theorems
4.2
,
4.3
.

Proof of Theorems
4.2
,
4.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be an infinite volume
complete riemannian manifold with Ricci curvature bounded below by
k\in\mathbb{R}
.
We have already seen that if it is collapsed, then
(MT)_{\alpha}
does not hold
for any
\alpha>0
. Similarly, if its Cheeger constant is zero, then it cannot
satisfy any Moser–Trudinger type inequality.

Let now
(X,\mu)
be an
\rm{RCD}(k,n)
-space of infinite volume.
Assume it has nonzero Cheeger constant and noncollapsed volume.
Denoting by
\theta_{\infty}
its asymptotic volume ratio.
We know from Proposition
3.12
that
(X,\mu)
is dominated
by a radial ball bilipschitz conformal to a hyperbolic trumpet of cone angle

\theta_{\infty}
. By Lemma
4.9
and Corollary
4.8
,
that dominating ball satisfies the Moser–Trudinger inequality
(MT)_{\alpha}

for any
\alpha
satisfying

\alpha\leq n(\theta_{\infty}\omega_{n-1})^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\,.

Since this ball dominates
(M,g)
, the same is true for the space
(X,\mu)
,
as desired.
For
\alpha>n(\theta_{\infty}\omega_{n-1})^{\frac{1}{n-1}}
, it does not satisfy
(MT)_{\alpha}

from Lemma
4.6
, hence the characterization among manifolds is proven.
∎

4.3.
Dependency of the Moser–Trudinger constant

The method used here allows to give a uniform Moser–Trudinger inequality for families of
spaces
(X,\mu)
dominating a common ball bilipschitzly conformal to a hyperbolic trumpet.
In particular it allows the specific case:

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\delta>0
and
h_{0}>0

There is a constant
C=C(n,a,b,\delta,h_{0})
such that
for any
n
-dimensional infinite volume manifold satisfying

(4.9)

a\leq\rm{Sect}(M)\leq b,\,\rm{inj}(M)\geq\delta,\,h(M)\geq h_{0}\,,

then for
\alpha\leq n\omega_{n-1}^{\frac{1}{n-1}}
and
u\in W^{1,n}(M)
one has

(4.10)

\|\nabla u\|_{n}\leq 1\,\Rightarrow\int_{M}\exp\big(\alpha u^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\big)-P_{n}(\alpha u^{\frac{n}{n-1}})\leq C\,.

Proof.

The controls on the sectional curvature are enough, by the Bishop–Gunther and Bishop–Gromov comparison theorems, to control the isoperimetric profile of the manifolds uniformly. Hence at fixed
a,b,\delta,h_{0}
, there is a common radial ball dominating all
the manifolds satisfying the assumptions, so there is a uniform constant
valid for all the inequalities
(MT)_{\alpha}
involved.
∎

5.
Double rearrangement for compact manifolds

In this section we consider compact
\rm{RCD}(k,n)
-spaces,
and introduce a double rearrangement
specifically adapted to functions with zero average.
As a consequence, we are able to show that
all compact manifolds admit a Moser–Trudinger type
inequality, and we discuss its sharpness and
dependency in the geometry of the manifold.

5.1.
Double rearrangement for manifolds

We already introduced a notion of domination adapted to infinite volume manifolds.
Here we use this notion in order to introduce a double rearrangement for functions on a finite volume
\rm{RCD}(k,n)
-space.
We will make use of a particular value called the median of
u
:

Definition 5.1
.

Let
u:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be integrable.
We denote by
c(u)
or
c
when no confusion is possible,
a value
c\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying:

(5.1)

\text{Vol}(u<c)\leq\frac{\text{Vol}(M)}{2}\,\text{and}\,\text{Vol}(u>c)\leq\frac{\text{Vol}(M)}{2}\,.

Remark
.

Both these inequalities are equalities as soon as
\text{Vol}(u=c)=0
.
While
c
may not be uniquely defined, the set of medians of
u
is always an interval,
so one can choose any particular point in that interval, as it won’t play a role
in the forthcoming discussion.

For
u\in L^{p}(X)
, its median and average are closely related by the Markov’s inequality

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
u\in L^{p}(X)
, with median
c
. Denote by
\overline{u}
its average.
Then we have

(5.2)

|c-\overline{u}|\leq(\frac{2}{\text{Vol}(X)})^{\frac{1}{p}}\|u-\overline{u}\|_{p}\,.

Proof.

From Markov’s inequality, for any
t>0
, we have

\text{Vol}(|u-\overline{u}|\geq t)\leq\frac{\|u-\overline{u}\|_{p}^{p}}{t^{p}}\,.

Fix

t=\frac{2}{\text{Vol}(M)}^{\frac{1}{p}}\|u-\overline{u}\|_{p}\,.

Then from Markov’s inequality, we obtain

\text{Vol}(u<t+\overline{u})\geq\frac{\text{Vol}(X)}{2}\text{ and }\text{Vol}(u>\overline{u}-t)\geq\frac{\text{Vol}(X)}{2}\,.

From which we deduce, by construction of
c
,

|c-\overline{u}|\leq t\,,

as claimed.
∎

We will also need a lower estimate on the gradient of some functions, relying on
the spectral gap of the
n
-Laplacian of the space.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\beta<1
, and consider
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{\beta}
the hyperbolic trumpet with cone angle
\beta
.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}_{\beta}
a bounded domain.
Let
c>0
and
u:\mathbb{H}^{n}_{\beta}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a function such that
u-c
is supported in
\Omega

and satisfies

(5.3)

u\leq c\text{ and }\int_{\Omega}u\leq-2c\text{Vol}(\Omega)\,.

Then it satisfies

(5.4)

\|\nabla u\|^{n}_{n}\geq\beta^{\frac{1}{n-1}}(3(1-\frac{1}{n}))^{n}c^{n}\text{Vol}(\Omega)\,.

Proof.

First apply the Hölder inequality to obtain, for any
1\leq k\leq n
:

|\int_{\Omega}u|\leq\|u|_{\Omega}\|_{k}^{k}\text{Vol}(\Omega)^{\frac{k-1}{k}}\,.

Hence we deduce

2^{k}c^{k}\text{Vol}(\Omega)\leq\|u|_{\Omega}\|_{k}^{k}

We estimate now the norm of
u-c
:

\|u-c\|_{n}^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\|u|_{\Omega}\|_{k}^{k}c^{n-k}\geq\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}2^{k}c^{n}\text{Vol}(\Omega)\geq 3^{n}c^{n}\text{Vol}(\Omega)\,.

Note that we used the assumption
u\leq c
here.
Finally, since
u-c
is compactly supported, we obtain from Buser’s inequality

\|\nabla u\|_{n}^{n}\geq\mu_{n}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{\beta})3^{n}c^{n}\text{Vol}(\Omega)

It remains to estimate
\mu_{n}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{\beta})
.
From the variational expression

\mu_{n}(M)=\inf\big\{\frac{\int_{M}\|\nabla f\|^{n}}{\int_{M}f^{n}},f\in W^{1,n}(M)\big\}\,,

we derive that

\mu_{n}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{\beta})\geq\beta^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\mu_{n}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\geq\beta^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\frac{(n-1)^{n}}{n^{n}}\,.

Where we used the estimate for
\mu_{n}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
from Lima–Montenegro–Santos
[
LMS10
]
.
We have proven that

\|\nabla u\|^{n}_{n}\geq\beta^{\frac{1}{n-1}}(3(1-\frac{1}{n}))^{n}c^{n}\text{Vol}(\Omega)\,,

as claimed.
∎

For
u\in W^{1,p}(X)
, and
c\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\text{Vol}(u>c)\leq\frac{\text{Vol}(X)}{2}
and

\text{Vol}(u<c)\leq\frac{\text{Vol}(X)}{2}
, we consider
u_{+}
and
u_{-}
the rearrangements
of
(u-c)\mathbf{1}_{u>c}
and of its negative part, in the following way

Definition 5.4
.

Let
X
be a compact
\rm{RCD}(k,n)
-space dominated by a radial ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
,
and
p>1
.
For
u\in W^{1,p}(X)
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
its median, we consider
u_{+},u_{-}\in L^{p}(X)

the radially symmetric decreasing functions satisfying:

(5.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\text{Vol}(u_{+}>t)=\text{Vol}(u-c>t)&\forall t>0\,\inf u_{+}=0\\
\text{Vol}(u_{-}>t)=\text{Vol}(c-u>t)&\forall t>0\,\inf u_{-}=0\,.\end{array}\right.

The worth of this rearrangement is in the following properties:

Proposition 5.5
.

For
u\in W^{1,p}(X)
with median
c
, consider
u_{-},u_{+}
its rearrangements.
Let
F:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a measurable function, such that
F(c)=0
. Then we have

(5.6)

\int_{X}F(u)=\int_{\mathbb{B}}F(u_{+}+c)+\int_{\mathbb{B}}F(c-u_{-})\,.

As with the following Polya–Szegö type inequality:

(5.7)

\|\nabla u_{+}\|^{p}_{p}+\|\nabla u_{-}\|_{p}^{p}\leq\|\nabla u\|_{p}^{p}\,.

Proof.

Let
\mu(t)=\max(t,0)
.
Then decompose
u
in the following way:

u=\mu(u-c,0)+c-\mu(c-u,0)\,.

From that decomposition, we recognize
that
u_{+}
is the rearrangement of
\mu(u-c,0)
while
u_{-}
is the rearrangement
of
\mu(c-u,0)
.
Since
F(c)=0
, we can write the integral:

\displaystyle\int_{X}F(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}F(\mu(u-c,0)+c)+\int_{X}F(c-\mu(c-u,0))

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{B}}F(u_{+}+c)+\int_{\mathbb{B}}F(c-u_{-})\,.

From the same decomposition. we can write the gradient as a sum:

\nabla u=\nabla\mu(u-c,0)+\nabla\mu(c-u,0)\,.

Applying the Polya-Szegö inequality, valid since we have the required domination property,
we obtain

\|\nabla u_{+}\|_{p}^{p}+\|\nabla u_{-}\|_{p}^{p}\leq\int_{X}|\nabla\mu(u-c,0)|^{p}+|\nabla\mu(c-u,0)|^{p}\leq\|\nabla u\|_{p}^{p}\,,

as claimed.
∎

5.2.
Moser–Trudinger inequality for compact metric measure spaces

In the case of compact
\rm{RCD}(k,n)
-spaces, we are able to prove the
following Moser–Trudinger type inequality:

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a compact ncRCD(k,n)-space whose volume is larger than
V_{0}>0

Denote by
\theta_{\infty}
its asymptotic growth ratio,
and by
\varphi
the radial profile of a ball dominating
X
, radially biLipschitz
conformal to
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{\beta}
, with
\omega_{n-1}\beta^{n-1}=\theta_{\infty}
.
Then for any
\alpha\leq n(\theta_{\infty})^{\frac{1}{n-1}}
,
there is a constant
C(\alpha,V_{0},\varphi)
such that, for any
u\in W^{1,n}_{0}(X)
,
we have

\|\nabla u\|_{n}\leq 1\,\Rightarrow\,\int_{X}e^{\alpha u^{\frac{n}{n-1}}}-P_{n}(\alpha u^{\frac{n}{n-1}})\leq C(\alpha,V_{0},\varphi)\,.

We will deduce this Theorem from the noncompact case, via double rearrangement.
Remark that a compact
\rm{RCD}(k,n)
is always dominated by a radial ball which is
radially bilipschitz conformal to the hyperbolic plane.

The first step of the proof is to replace
W^{1,n}(X)
with the space of zero median
functions, more adapted to double rearrangements.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a compact
\rm{ncRCD}(k,n)
space.
For
u\in W^{1,n}(X)
, denote by
c
its median.
Then for any
\alpha\leq n(\theta_{\infty}\omega_{n-1})^{\frac{1}{n-1}}
,
there is a constant
C(\alpha)
such that

(5.8)

\|\nabla u\|_{n}\leq 1\,\Rightarrow\,\int_{X}e^{\alpha(u-c)^{\frac{n}{n-1}}}-P_{n}(\alpha(u-c)^{\frac{n}{n-1}})\leq C(\alpha)\,.

The constant
C(\alpha)
can be replaced by a constant depending only on
the isoperimetric profile of
(X,\mu)
.

Proof.

From Proposition
3.12
we know that
(X,\mu)
is dominated by a radial ball bilipschitz
conformal to a hyperbolic trumpet of cone angle
\theta_{\infty}
.
Let
\alpha\leq n(\theta_{\infty}\omega_{n-1})^{\frac{1}{n-1}}
.
We know that there is a constant
C>0
such that, for
u\in W^{1,n}(\mathbb{B})
,

\|\nabla u\|_{n}\leq 1\,\Rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{B}}e^{\alpha u^{\frac{n}{n-1}}}-P_{n}(\alpha u^{\frac{n}{n-1}})\leq C\,.

For
u\in W^{1,n}(X)
with
\|\nabla u\|_{n}\leq 1
and median
c
,
consider its double rearrangement
u_{-}
,
u_{+}
. From Proposition
5.5
,
we know that
u_{-},u_{+}\in W^{1,n}(\mathbb{B})
, with
\|\nabla u_{-}\|_{n}^{n}+\|\nabla u_{+}\|_{n}^{n}\leq 1
.
Applying the Moser–Trudinger inequality to both of these, we obtain

\int_{X}e^{\alpha(u-c)^{\frac{n}{n-1}}}-P_{n}(\alpha(u-c)^{\frac{n}{n-1}})\leq 2C\,.

as wished.
∎

To replace the median by the average in the Moser–Trudinger inequality, the proof is a bit
more involved. Let’s introduce the notations first

Notation 5.8
.

We will make use of the following notations:

(1)

(X,\mu)
is a finite volume ncRCD(k,n)-space dominated by a ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
with radial profile
\varphi

.

V_{0}>0
.

(2)

u
is a function on

c>0
,
such that

.

(3)

u_{-},u_{+}
are the rearrangements of
u
on
\mathbb{B}^{n}
. We denote by
\Omega\subset\mathbb{B}^{n}
the radial ball of volume half the volume of
X
.

(4)

F
denotes the following function:

(5.9)

F(t)=e^{|t|}-\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}\frac{|t|^{j}}{j!}\,.

(5)

\theta_{\infty}
is the asymptotic volume ratio of
X
,

\alpha

\alpha\leq n(\theta_{\infty}\omega_{n-1})^{\frac{1}{n-1}}

Step 1:The Moser Trudinger inequality for
u_{-}

Proposition 5.9
.

With the notations introduced, there is a constant
C_{3}(\varphi,\alpha,\lambda_{n},V_{0})

such that
u_{-}
satisfies

(5.10)

\int_{\Omega}F(\alpha(c-u_{-})^{\frac{n}{n-1}})\leq C_{3}(\varphi,\alpha,\lambda_{n},V_{0})\,.

If the space
(X,\mu)
is a manifold, we can drop the dependency in
\lambda_{n}
.

Proof.

First, from the Moser–Trudinger inequality for zero-median functions,
we get
C_{1}>0
such that

\int_{X}F(\alpha(u-c)^{\frac{n}{n-1}})\leq C_{1}\,.

From the properties of the double rearrangements, as
G=F(\alpha(\cdot-c)^{\frac{n}{n-1}})

vanishes at
c
, we have

\int_{X}F(\alpha(u-c)^{\frac{n}{n-1}})=\int_{\mathbb{B}}^{n}F(\alpha u_{+}^{\frac{n}{n-1}})+\int_{\mathbb{B}}^{n}F(\alpha u_{-}^{\frac{n}{n-1}})\,.

So in particular,

\int_{\mathbb{B}}^{n}F(\alpha u_{-}^{\frac{n}{n-1}})\leq C_{1}\,.

By construction,
u_{-}
is supported in
\Omega
and
0\leq u_{-}\leq c
.
Hence we obtain

0\leq(c-u_{-})^{\frac{n}{n-1}}+c^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\,.

By parity of the exponential map,

\int_{\Omega}\exp(\alpha(c-u_{-})^{\frac{n}{n-1}})\leq e^{\alpha c^{\frac{n}{n-1}}}(C_{1}+\int_{\Omega}\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}\frac{\alpha^{j}u_{-}^{\frac{jn}{n-1}}}{j!})\,.

In particular, there is a constant
C_{2}(\alpha,V_{0},c)

such that

\int_{\Omega}F(\alpha(c-u_{-})^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\leq C_{2}(\alpha,V_{0},c)\,.

Note that
c
is controlled by
\|u\|_{n}
and
V_{0}
by the Markov inequality,
which is in turned controlled by
\lambda_{n}
and
V_{0}
. Hence this yields

C_{3}(\alpha,\varphi,\lambda_{n})
such that

\int_{\Omega}F(c-u_{-})\leq C_{3}(\alpha,V_{0},\varphi,\lambda_{n})\,,

as claimed.
If the space
(X,\mu)
is a manifold, we can drop the dependency in
\lambda_{n}

to obtain

\int_{\Omega}F(c-u_{-})\leq C_{4}(\alpha,V_{0},\varphi)\,,

as desired.
∎

Step 2: get an improved bound on
\nabla u_{+}

Proposition 5.10
.

With the notations introduced, there is a constant
C_{5}=C_{5}(\beta,\varphi)

such that
u_{+}

\|\nabla u_{+}\|_{n}^{n}\leq 1-C_{5}c^{n}\text{Vol}(\Omega)\,.

Proof.

Let
\Psi:\mathbb{H}^{n}_{\beta}\rightarrow\mathbb{B}^{n}
be a radially biLipschitz conformal map.
The function

satisfies:

\rm{Supp}(c-u_{-})\subset\Omega\text{ and }w\leq c

Also, by construction

0=\int_{M}(u-c)+c\text{Vol}(M)=\int_{\Omega}u_{+}+\int_{\Omega}-u_{-}+c\text{Vol}(M)

Hence

\int_{M}u_{-}=\int_{\Omega}u_{+}+c\text{Vol}(M)\geq 3c\text{Vol}(\Omega)\,.

and regarding

,

\int_{\Omega}c-u_{-}\leq-2c\text{Vol}(\Omega)\,.

Since
\Psi
is bilipschitz conformal, we can apply Lemma

and we obtain that there is a constant

such that

\|\nabla u_{-}\|_{n}^{n}\geq C(\Psi,\beta)c^{n}\text{Vol}(\Omega)\,.

The control on

\|\nabla u_{+}\|_{n}^{n}+\|\nabla u_{-}\|_{n}^{n}\leq 1\,.

∎

Step 3: controling

Proposition 5.11

With the notations introduced, there is a constant

Proof.

Let

Genetic inference and single cell expression analysis of potential targets in heart failure and breast cancer
Authors: Yue Li, Ying Huang, Ning An, Xiaomiao Guan, Bing Liu, Huiying Li, Tingting Jiang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundBreast cancer and heart failure are major public health issues globally, characterized by significant genetic heterogeneity and complex molecular mechanisms. This study aims to explore potential genetic targets in heart failure and breast cancer through combining genetic inference and single-cell expression analysis, and to assess the causal relationships of these targets using Mendelian randomization methods.MethodsWe first identified genetic variants associated with heart failure and breast cancer using genome-wide association study (GWAS) data. Subsequently, we analyzed the expression patterns of these genetic variants in different cell types through single-cell RNA sequencing technology. Furthermore, we utilized in vitro experiment to assess the effects of ITM2B on MCF7 using immunofluorescence.ResultsOur study identified multiple genetic variants associated with heart failure and breast cancer, showing specific expression patterns in heart and breast cells. The ITM2B gene is highly expressed in breast cancer. ITM2B shows the highest expression in T cells and the lowest expression in proliferating cells. Silencing the ITM2B gene can promote apoptosis and inhibit proliferation and migration of MCF7 breast cancer cells.ConclusionBy integrating genetic inference and single-cell expression analysis, this study revealed potential genetic targets in heart failure and breast cancer, and validated the causal effects of these targets using Mendelian randomization methods.

Introduction

Breast cancer and heart failure are two major diseases that seriously threaten human health globally(Arun et al. 2024; Frazer et al. 2024; Ouyang et al. 2024). Breast cancer ranks first among malignant tumors in women, while heart failure is a complex clinical syndrome, typically the end stage of various heart diseases. Some treatment methods for breast cancer, such as anthracycline drugs (such as doxorubicin) and HER2 targeted therapy (such as trastuzumab), may lead to cardiac toxicity, thus increasing the risk of heart failure(Akhter and Hibler 2024; Phoebe et al. 2024; Hursoy et al. 2024). Heart failure and breast cancer share some common risk factors, such as hypertension, obesity and diabetes, which may increase the risk of both diseases. These diseases not only have a high degree of genetic heterogeneity but also involve complex molecular pathways in their pathogenesis, making them a focus of medical research. Gene therapy - Cell therapy is one of the research directions for the treatment methods for these two diseases, among which novel genes and cell groups can be targeted to develop new drugs. However, people are working on other ways to treat resistance and increase early correct diagnosis as well as mitigating side effects of treatment.

Our understanding of heart failure and breast cancer has further evolved with the advancement in genomics and molecular biology. However, a large number of genetic variations with the association to these diseases have been identified through genome-wide association studies (GWAS), which is useful information for the use in genetic study of the diseases. The discovery of genetic variants alone however is not adequate to fully comprehend the intricacies underlying these diseases; systematic examination of those variants’ expression profiles and biological functions in a cellular context are required for translational research on genomic data(Leite et al. 2024; Faridah et al. 2024; Su et al. 2024).

Recently, the advance in single-cell RNA sequencing technology (scRNA-seq) enable us to analyze gene expressions at a resolution of individual cells and uncovered dynamic changes in cellular heterogeneity and states(Hong et al. 2024; Marot-Lassauzaie et al. 2024; Yin et al. 2024). The researchers say that this technology will bring a new outlook to the ways in which they understood the cellular molecular mechanisms of heart failure and breast cancer.Mendelian randomization (MR) uses genetic variants as instrumental variables for causal inference. This approach has the potential to address confounding and reverse causality common in standard observational studies and is a powerful tool for assessing causality between genetic variation and disease (Chen et al. 2024; Zhang et al. 2024; Zhou et al. 2024).

In this study, researchers use single-cell RNA sequencing to investigate the cell-types in the heart and breast tissues where these genetic variants are expressed. We detect genetic variations for these two diseases with GWAS data, then assess the expression patterns of these variations across different cell types using scRNA-seq technology. We also employ Mendelian randomization (MR) to investigate the possible causal effects of these genetic variants on disease risk.

We predict that this study would offer new molecular insights on heart failure and breast cancer research, ultimately offering potential targets for the generation of innovative therapeutic approaches. Through our cancer and heart and circulatory disease studies, we will look to build on new discoveries that can help prevent, diagnose and treat these two diseases.

Methods

Study design

In the field of clinical practice, there may be limitations in conducting randomized controlled trials (RCTs). However, Mendelian randomization (MR) provides a viable alternative for detecting and quantifying causal relationships. MR is a form of instrumental variable (IV) analysis that utilizes genetic variation as an instrumental variable. Notably, the chronology is clearly established as the genetic variation precedes the emergence of the corresponding trait. This connection between MR and the concept of ‘natural RCT’ has been acknowledged by some scholars(Liu et al. 2024).

GWAS database

The two data sets used in this MR analysis were entirely derived from publicly available genome-wide association study (GWAS) summary data. These datasets are open to the public and no conflicts of interest are involved. All datasets related to exposed populations were obtained from the UK Biobank (gwas.mrcieu.ac.uk/datasets). Heart failure data from European ancestry (ebi-a-GCST009541, ncase = 47,309;ncontrol = 930,014). The data for breast cancer (finngen R11 CD2 INSITU BREAST LOBULAR, ncase = 184;ncontrol = 87,530) data were used to establish a Meta fixed model for analysis.

Single-cell analysis

Essentially these checks measure the RNA counts and confirm they are within an acceptable range satisfying analytic standards. The data is preprocessed and undergoes preprocessing operations prior to in-depth analysis. This process may entail cell quality filtering, subtraction of unwanted batch effects (ambient RNA etc.) and normalizing gene expression levels to adjust for sequencing depth and cellular content. The data undergoes preprocessing and PCA to dissect the patterns of biological similarity that distinguish different cell types or states. This is followed by creating PCA scatter plots where samples are projected onto the first two principal components (PC1 and PC2) that summarize the largest sources of variation in data. These plots indicate how the tumor samples spread across principal components. Following this, the t-SNE (t-distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding) algorithm is employed for cell clustering to distinguish different cell populations, and t-SNE clustering plots are created to illustrate cell diversity and potential subgroups. Cell-cell interaction network diagrams are developed to analyze interaction frequencies among myeloid cells, T cells, NK cells, endothelial cells, fibroblasts, proliferating cells, dendritic cells, and B cells, revealing the crucial cellular roles in signal transduction(Li et al. 2024; Rylaarsdam et al. 2024).

In data visualization, heatmaps and violin plots are utilized to demonstrate expression differences of various genes across different cell types. Coupled with cell-cell interaction strength network diagrams, the roles of MIF and GALECTIN signaling pathways in the tumor microenvironment are thoroughly analyzed. Moreover, bioinformatics analysis integrates gene expression data and cell type information to pinpoint potential biomarkers and therapeutic targets, employing data mining and machine learning techniques to delve deeper into the intricate communication networks between cells. By leveraging these methodologies, this study uncovers the heterogeneity of thyroid cancer cells and elucidates their potential biological mechanisms, laying a significant foundation for future research and treatment.

GO/KEGG analysis

To obtain a list of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) from the analysis of differentially expressed genes (using packages like limma, DESeq2, etc. in R), make sure the gene list includes gene identifiers (such as Entrez ID or Gene Symbol) and their corresponding statistical significance values (like log2 fold change and p-value). Load the clusterProfiler package and use the ego function for Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis. You can specify the GO categories to analyze (Biological Process, Molecular Function, or Cellular Component). Use the enrichPathway function for Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis(Nguyen et al. 2024).

The dUTP gap end labeling method mediated by terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase

Terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase-mediated dUTP nick end labeling (TUNEL) assay is performed according to the instructions of the TUNEL assay kit. In brief, cells are fixed, followed by TdT and Click-iT reactions. After color development using DAB reaction, images are captured using Aperio S2 Leica Biosystems microscope (Leica, Wetzlar, Germany). Image-Pro Plus (Meyer Instruments, TX, USA) is introduced for the quantification analysis of TUNEL-positive cells(Behdarvandian et al. 2023; Mitsui et al. 2023; Ray 2024).

Cell invade and proliferation ability were detected

1 × 10^4 MCF7 cells were seeded into the upper chamber of a Transwell culture plate with an 8.0 μm pore size (Corning). No matrix glue was used for the invade experiments, whereas in the invasion experiments, matrix glue and EdU (BD Biosciences) was applied beforehand. The cells on the outer side were then fixed with a 4% formaldehyde solution paraformaldehyde, and stained with 1 g/L crystal violet. Transfection of ITM2B silencing genes and overexpression genes.

Discussion

Heart failure and breast cancer are important global public health problems require improvement in treatment and prevention strategies(Assis 2024; Myhre et al. 2024). Here, we develop a multi-dimensional approach using genetic inference, single-cell expression analysis and Mendelian randomization methods to investigate candidate genetic targets and molecular mechanisms underlying these two diseases. Our findings contribute to the understanding of the etiology and potential risk prediction of heart failure and breast cancer, respectively, by identifying genetic variants associated with these diseases using Mendelian randomization analysis, which could be used as biomarkers for screening high-risk populations for susceptibility to heart failure or breast cancer. Identification of these targets could lead to personalized medicine aimed at more specific interventions based on the genetic background of the patient. Using single-cell transcriptomic technology, we can identify the complex gene expression representation of CEs in heart and breast cells. A fine-grained analysis at the cellular level peels back layers of complexity, shedding new light on where throughout the diversity of our proteomes genes drive disease and revealing novel molecular classification and treatment implications for subtypes. Thus, the evidence for heart failure and breast cancer revealed novel patters of genetic variation expression relating to each disease, highlighting that the gene-expression profiles in terms of these identified polymorphic loci may be meritorious strategies for explaining susceptibility to particular diseases. These are especially suggestive that these could be important in disease, which open up new avenues for early diagnosis and treatment of diseases.

These results are important as they serve as a foundation for therapeutic strategies. For example, elucidating its function in immune cells and tumor immune escape, may allow the development of treatments that trigger antitumor immunity. These results suggest that the ELOVL1 function in fatty acid metabolism should be addressed to eradicate the energy supply of tumor cells. The identification of GRM2 and MAP3K2 as candidate targets supports the idea that increased survival is achievablepre-clinically if we better understand tumor-environment cross-talk. These results are also concordant with the existing scientific background as they mechanistically underlie such a behavior of tumor cells and their immune system interaction. The presence of the identified genes, and their products, along with the signaling pathways that direct them may provide new biomarkers for tumor progression and response to therapy. Additional studies are needed to confirm these results and determine potential clinical translations. The findings suggest a number of ways both to better understand the biology of breast cancer and to more effectively target cellular processes and signaling pathways with novel therapeutic strategies. This will possibly lead to more individualized, and therefore more effective treatments based on the molecular characteristics of a given patient’s tumor enhancing the survival of cancer patients(Reza et al. 2024; Qin et al. 2024). Likewise, it might be participated in the tumoral cells invasion and metastasis, since alterations on cell membranes could alter cells adhesivity and mobilization. GRM2 is a cell signaling molecule that belongs to the class of G protein-coupled receptors and facilitates signaling pathways, such as those important for cell proliferation differentiation and migration. In tumor cell, GRM2 activation is associated with tumorigenesis and treatment response. Furthermore, PNUTS could also act as a therapeutic target be cultivating the microenvironment signaling of tumor development (Kalnins et al. 2020; Lyon et al. 2011). ITM2B is a calcium signaling-regulating transmembrane protein. Obviously, ITM2B might function as potential tumor suppressor gene and plays critical roles in cell cycle regulation and apoptosis, thereby modulating tumor cells proliferation and survival. This is also associated with the immune escape of MM, which may influence the recognition and killing function of immune cells(Xian et al. 2024; Zhou et al. 2020). MAP3K2 is a mitogen-activated protein kinase kinase kinase that functions in various signaling pathways, including the MAPK/ERK signaling pathway. In tumors, MAP3K2 may affect tumor development through regulating cell stress responses, cell cycle control, and apoptosis. Its activity may be related to the aggressiveness and metastatic potential of tumors, as it can influence extracellular matrix remodeling and cell interactions(Li et al. 2022; Zhang et al. 2022).

The t-SNE cell type plot further annotates and distinguishes different cell types based on t-SNE results, including fibroblasts, epithelial, T − cells, proliferating cells, endothelial. Breast cancer classification is based on the epithelial characteristics of tumor cells, such as histological grade, receptor status (estrogen receptor ER, progesterone receptor PR, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 HER2), etc. Epithelial-Mesenchymal Transition (EMT) is an important phenomenon in the development of breast cancer, involving the conversion of epithelial cells to a mesenchymal cell phenotype, which is related to enhanced tumor invasion and metastatic ability. The interaction between epithelial cells of breast cancer and other cell types in the tumor microenvironment (such as immune cells, fibroblasts, vascular cells, etc.) influences tumor growth, invasion, and response to treatment. Targeted therapies against specific epithelial cell markers, such as HER2-targeted therapy, have become an important approach in the treatment of breast cancer.

This study also explores the interaction between genetic variants and immune cell infiltration in the tumor microenvironment. These findings help in understanding how tumors evade immune surveillance and how they promote or inhibit disease progression through regulating immune responses. Future research can further explore the functional changes of these genetic variants in different disease states and how they affect the treatment response of diseases. Additionally, studying the distribution and effects of these variations in different populations will help achieve precision medicine on a global scale.

Both heart failure and breast cancer exhibit significant genetic heterogeneity, and the genetic variants identified may not capture the full spectrum of genetic influences on these diseases.The study’s conclusions are based on the available datasets, which may not be fully representative of all populations. A more diverse and larger sample size could provide a more comprehensive understanding of the genetic factors involved.Single-cell RNA sequencing technology, while powerful, is not without its limitations, including potential biases in library preparation, sequencing depth, and data processing.By acknowledging these limitations, future research can build upon the current study’s findings, addressing these gaps, and further advancing our understanding of the genetic basis of heart failure and breast cancer.

Conclusion

This study, through the comprehensive application of various bioinformatics methods, reveals potential genetic targets of heart failure and breast cancer and assesses the causal effects of these targets. These findings not only enhance our understanding of the molecular mechanisms of diseases but also provide a scientific basis for developing new treatment strategies.

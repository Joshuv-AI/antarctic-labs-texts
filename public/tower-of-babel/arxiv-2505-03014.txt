On the smallest eigenvalues of 3 3 -colorable graphs
Zilin Jiang, Zhiyu Wang

Abstract
We prove that the set of the smallest eigenvalues attained by 3 3 -colorable graphs is dense in ( − ∞ , − λ ∗ ) (-\infty,-\lambda^{*}) , where λ ∗ = ρ 1 / 2 + ρ − 1 / 2 ≈ 2.01980 \lambda^{*}=\rho^{1/2}+\rho^{-1/2}\approx 2.01980 and ρ \rho is the positive real root of x 3 = x + 1 x^{3}=x+1 . As a consequence, in the context of spherical two-distance sets, our result precludes any further refinement of the forbidden-subgraph method through the chromatic number of signed graphs.

Introduction

Motivated by the problem of estimating the maximum size of spherical two-distance sets with prescribed inner products, Jiang and Polyanskii
[
6
]
studied certain families of signed graphs within the class
\mathcal{G}_{p}
of
p
-colorable signed graphs. Here, a
signed graph
is a graph
G
whose edges are each labeled by
+
or
-
, and a signed graph
G
is
p
-colorable
if there exists a
p
-coloring of
V(G)
such that the endpoints of every negative edge receive identical colors, and the endpoints of every positive edge receive different colors.

Remark
.

Our coloring notion is the sign-reversed version of Cartwright–Harary
[
2
]
, where negative edges must join vertices of
different
colors and positive edges of
identical
colors. We choose the current definition because we need to discuss the (unsigned) graph coloring as well, and the current definition is a generalization of that. By flipping the signs of all edges, one can go back and forth between the two different definitions, as we will do when restating various results from
[
6
,
8
]
.

We now introduce the key families of signed graphs. Given
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, let
\mathcal{G}_{p}(\lambda)
denote the family of
p
-colorable signed graphs whose smallest eigenvalues are at least
-\lambda
. The
smallest eigenvalue
of a signed graph
G
, denoted by
\lambda_{1}(G)
, is the smallest eigenvalue of its signed adjacency matrix.

The Cauchy interlacing theorem implies that
\mathcal{G}_{p}(\lambda)
is closed under taking induced subgraphs. Jiang and Polyanskii asked in
[
6
]
whether it is possible to define each of these families by a
finite
set of forbidden subgraphs.

Definition 1
.

Given a class
\mathcal{G}
of signed graphs, and a family
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
that is closed under taking induced subgraphs, a set
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
of signed graphs is a
forbidden subgraph characterization
of
\mathcal{H}
within
\mathcal{G}
if
\mathcal{H}
consists exactly of signed graphs in
\mathcal{G}
that do not contain any member of
\mathcal{F}
as an induced subgraph.

Problem 2

(Problem 5.3 of Jiang and Polyanskii
[
6
]
)
.

For every
p\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, determine the set of
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
for which
\mathcal{G}_{p}(\lambda)
has a finite forbidden subgraph characterization within
\mathcal{G}_{p}
.

For

,

is essentially resolved by
[

, Theorem 1]
. In this paper, we completely resolve the rest of

as follows.

Theorem 3
.

For every

\mathcal{G}_{p}
of
p
-colorable signed graphs, the family

To reduce the case where

Corollary 6
.

For
p\in\left\{1,2\right\}
, within the class
\mathcal{G}_{p}
of
p
-colorable signed graphs, the family
\mathcal{G}_{p}(\lambda)

Proof.

Notice that for every signed graph

, it is
1
-colorable if and only if
-G
is unsigned, and

if and only if the largest eigenvalue of

is at most

and the class of unsigned graphs, the family

has a finite forbidden subgraph characterization within

if and only if the family of graphs with spectral radius at most

has a finite forbidden subgraph characterization. Therefore, the case where

5
.

By restricting signed graphs in
\mathcal{G}_{2}
to
\mathcal{G}_{1}
, we observe that if

has a finite forbidden subgraph characterization within
\mathcal{G}_{2}
, then so does
\mathcal{G}_{1}(\lambda)
within
\mathcal{G}_{1}
. Conversely, suppose that
\mathcal{G}_{1}(\lambda)
has a finite forbidden subgraph characterization

within
\mathcal{G}_{1}
. Let

5
Spherical two-distance sets

A
spherical two-distance set
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a collection of unit vectors whose pairwise inner products lie in
\left\{\alpha,\beta\right\}
. There was some partial success specifically in the regime where
-1\leq\beta<0\leq\alpha<1
. For fixed
\alpha
and
\beta
, denote by
N_{\alpha,\beta}(d)
the maximum size of such a set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

When
\beta=-\alpha
, the problem specializes to the famous question of
equiangular lines
with fixed angles, whose high-dimensional behavior is now understood thanks to the breakthrough of Jiang, Tidor, Yao, Zhang and Zhao
[
7
]
. Outside the equiangular setting, the landscape is much richer and remain largely open.

Recently, the limit
\lim_{d\to\infty}N_{\alpha,\beta}(d)/d
was determined in
[
6
,
8
]
when
p\leq 2
or
\lambda<\lambda^{*}
, where the parameters are defined by

p=\left\lfloor{-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\right\rfloor+1\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda=\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha-\beta}.

In
[
8
]
, a spherical two-distance set with prescribed inner products
\alpha
and
\beta
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
was constructed, and it was conjectured to provide the optimal
N_{\alpha,\beta}(d)
up to an additive error of
o(d)
. The limit
\lim_{d\to\infty}N_{\alpha,\beta}(d)/d
is believed to depend on the following spectral graph theoretic quantity:

k_{p}(\lambda)=\inf\left\{\frac{\lvert G\rvert}{\mathrm{mult}(-\lambda,G)}\colon G\in\mathcal{G}_{p}(\lambda)\right\},

where
\mathrm{mult}(-\lambda,G)
denotes the multiplicity of
-\lambda
as an eigenvalue of
G
.

Conjecture 20

(Conjecture 1.11 of Jiang et al.
[
8
]
)
.

Fix
-1\leq\beta<0\leq\alpha<1
. Set
\lambda=(1-\alpha)/(\alpha-\beta)
and
p=\lfloor-\alpha/\beta\rfloor+1
. Then

N_{\alpha,\beta}(d)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{k_{p}(\lambda)d}{k_{p}(\lambda)-1}+o(d)&\text{if }k_{p}(\lambda)<\infty,\\
d+o(d)&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

To prove
20
, a forbidden-subgraph approach was discovered in
[
8
]
that reduces the estimation of
N_{\alpha,\beta}(d)
to that of the following quantity. See
[
8
, Section 5]
for the reduction.

Definition 21

(Definition 5.2 of Jiang et al.
[
8
]
)
.

Given
p\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
and a family
\mathcal{F}
of signed graphs, let
M_{p,\mathcal{F}}(\lambda,N)
be the maximum possible value of
\mathrm{mult}(-\lambda,G)
over all
p
-colorable signed graphs
G
on at most
N
vertices that do not contain any member of
\mathcal{F}
as an induced subgraph and satisfy
\lambda_{p+1}(G)\geq-\lambda
. Here
\lambda_{p+1}(G)
denotes the
(p+1)
-th smallest eigenvalue of
G
.

Conjecture 22

(Conjecture 5.4 of Jiang et al.
[
8
]
)
.

For every
\lambda>0
and
p\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, there exists a finite family
\mathcal{F}
of signed graphs with
\lambda_{1}(F)<-\lambda
for each
F\in\mathcal{F}
such that

M_{p,\mathcal{F}}(\lambda,N)\leq\begin{cases}N/k_{p}(\lambda)+o(N)&\text{if }k_{p}(\lambda)<\infty,\\
o(N)&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

When
\lambda<\lambda^{*}
, according to
Theorem

7
, the family
\mathcal{F}
in
[
6
]
was taken as a finite forbidden subgraph characterization of the family of signed graphs with smallest eigenvalue at least
-\lambda
(within the class of signed graphs). In view of
Definition

21
, for this specific
\mathcal{F}
, the quantity simplifies to

M_{p,\mathcal{F}}(\lambda,N)=\max\left\{\mathrm{mult}(-\lambda,G)\colon\lvert G\rvert\leq N\text{ and }G\in\mathcal{G}_{p}(\lambda)\right\},

which in turn confirms
22
through a simple argument. We refer the readers to
[
6
, Section 4]
for more details.

Since a
p
-colorable graph
G
never contains a subgraph
F
that is not
p
-colorable, it is more economical to choose
\mathcal{F}
to be a forbidden subgraph characterization of
\mathcal{G}_{p}(\lambda)
within
\mathcal{G}_{p}
. This is the motivation behind
2
. Our
Theorem

4
therefore shows that restricting to
p
-colorable signed graphs yields no new finite forbidden subgraph characterizations once
p\geq 3
.

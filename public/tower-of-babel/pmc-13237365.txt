Oral Postbiotic (VMK223) Supplementation Is Associated with Improved Instrumental Skin Appearance Parameters in Healthy Women Aged 40–55 Years: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial
Authors: Caterina Pipino, Fausta Costabile, Sandra Martín Peláez, George Tzortzis, Jelena Vulevic, Adele Costabile
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionSkin aging in midlife women is influenced by intrinsic aging processes, hormonal transitions, and systemic factors that affect skin structure, hydration, and elasticity. Increasing interest has emerged in oral interventions targeting the gut–skin axis as a complementary strategy to topical skincare. However, clinical evidence linking postbiotic supplementation to objective changes in skin appearance remains limited.MethodsIn this randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, 34 healthy women aged 40–55 years were assigned to receive either an oral postbiotic supplement (VMK223, 500 mg/day) or placebo for 12 weeks. Objective skin appearance parameters, including pore appearance, melanin levels, acne severity, wrinkle depth, hydration, and elasticity, were assessed at baseline and at weeks 4, 8, and 12 using a standardized, noninvasive skin analysis device. A composite skin quality score integrating pores, melanin, acne, and wrinkles was developed as an exploratory outcome.ResultsOverall, 29 participants completed the study (VMK223: n = 16; placebo: n = 13). Compared with placebo, VMK223 supplementation was associated with significantly greater improvements across multiple skin parameters over time. At week 12, the VMK223 group showed greater reductions in wrinkle depth (28.0% versus 4.4%, p < 0.001), pore appearance (22.0% versus 8.2%, p < 0.001), acne severity (15.9% versus 7.0%, p < 0.01), and melanin levels (11.5% versus 0.9%, p < 0.05), alongside greater increases in skin hydration (28.3% versus 11.0%, p < 0.001), and elasticity (26.8% versus 5.8%, p < 0.001). The composite skin quality score improved by 20.9% in the VMK223 group compared with 5.6% in the placebo group (p < 0.001). Improvements were progressive and most pronounced after 8–12 weeks. Oral postbiotic supplementation for 12 weeks was associated with significant improvements in multiple objective skin appearance parameters in healthy women aged 40–55 years.ConclusionsThese findings support the potential role of postbiotics as a systemic, adjunctive approach for improving visible skin quality. Introduction

Skin aging is a multifactorial process characterized by progressive changes in skin structure, hydration, elasticity, pigmentation, and surface texture [1–3]. In women, the transition through midlife is often accompanied by accelerated visible skin changes, including increased wrinkle formation, dryness, reduced elasticity, and uneven pigmentation [4, 5]. These changes reflect not only intrinsic aging but also broader systemic influences that affect skin homeostasis [1, 6].

Traditionally, interventions targeting skin aging have focused on topical formulations designed to act directly on the epidermis and dermis [1, 3]. While topical approaches remain central to dermatological practice, growing attention has been directed toward systemic strategies that may influence skin appearance from within [7, 8]. In this context, the gut–skin axis has emerged as a conceptual framework linking gastrointestinal function, immune signaling, and skin physiology [9, 10].

Postbiotics, defined as nonviable microbial components or metabolites that confer health benefits to the host, represent a promising class of oral interventions [11]. Unlike probiotics, postbiotics do not rely on microbial survival or colonization and may offer a more predictable and standardized approach [11, 12]. Experimental and clinical research suggests that postbiotics can influence host signaling pathways relevant to barrier function, immune regulation, and tissue homeostasis [13, 14]. Postbiotics may also act through the gut–skin axis by influencing intestinal barrier integrity, microbial metabolite production, and systemic inflammatory signaling, which have been associated with processes relevant to skin aging, including dermal remodeling, oxidative stress, and pigmentation changes [10, 15, 16]. However, these pathways are based on prior experimental and clinical observations and were not directly assessed in the present study.

Beyond these conceptual links, emerging work has begun to describe a microbiome–ageing–wrinkles axis, in which age-related changes in host physiology and microbial communities are associated with wrinkle formation through mechanisms such as chronic low-grade inflammation, oxidative stress, and matrix metalloproteinase activation [15, 16]. In line with this, microbiome-targeted strategies, including probiotics, postbiotics, and prebiotic-containing interventions, have been associated with improvements in skin-related outcomes such as elasticity, hydration, and wrinkle depth in recent clinical and review literature [7, 17]. These findings support the rationale for investigating systemic approaches targeting host–microbe interactions in skin aging.

VMK223 is a proprietary formulation derived from Bifidobacterium breve BB091109 and composed of defined exopolysaccharide and cell wall polysaccharide fractions. Previous clinical research has shown that VMK223 supplementation can influence systemic biomarkers related to inflammation and endocrine regulation in healthy women aged 40–55 years [18]. While these findings provide biological plausibility for potential skin-related effects, direct assessment of skin appearance outcomes is necessary to establish clinical relevance in dermatology.

The present study aimed to evaluate whether oral supplementation with VMK223 is associated with measurable improvements in objective skin appearance parameters in healthy women aged 40–55 years. Using standardized, noninvasive instrumental assessments, we investigated changes in skin texture, pigmentation, hydration, elasticity, and acne-related parameters over a 12-week intervention period.

Methods

Study Design and Setting

This single-center, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled, parallel-group trial was conducted to assess the effects of oral postbiotic supplementation on skin quality and appearance parameters. Participants were randomly allocated to receive either VMK223 supplement or placebo using an Excel-based covariate adaptive randomization program, stratified by age and baseline hormonal status. Randomization codes were generated by an independent statistician and implemented using sequentially numbered, identical containers prepared off site, ensuring allocation concealment. Participants, site staff, and investigators performing instrumental skin assessments and data analysis were blinded to group allocation until completion of data cleaning and database lock.

The study comprised a 12-week treatment period preceded by a 2-week run-in phase, during which participants refrained from consuming probiotic- or prebiotic-containing foods or beverages. A 12-week intervention period was chosen in line with previous oral skincare and microbiome-targeted trials, which indicate that 8–12 weeks are typically required for systemic interventions to translate into measurable changes in skin structure and appearance [23].

The protocol complied with the Declaration of Helsinki and received approval from the University of Roehampton Research Ethics Committee (ethics reference no. LSC 18/274). The trial was registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (ID: NCT04267731). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to enrollment.

Participants

A total of 34 healthy women aged 40–55 years were recruited for this study. Inclusion criteria were: (1) generally healthy status without significant medical conditions (e.g., hypercholesterolemia, diabetes, gastrointestinal disorders), (2) interest in improving skin health, and (3) willingness to adhere to the study protocol (Fig. 1)

The study flow diagram

Exclusion criteria included: (1) use of medications or supplements affecting mineral or glucose metabolism, or skin health within 4 weeks prior to or during the study, (2) pregnancy, breastfeeding, or planned pregnancy during the study, (3) hormone replacement therapy, (4) known allergies or intolerances to study materials or related compounds, (5) significant weight loss (> 5% body weight) in the previous year, (6) abnormal eating behavior, and (7) history of skin disorders (e.g., eczema, psoriasis).

Participants were instructed to maintain their usual lifestyle, diet, and skincare routines throughout the study. During the 2-week run-in period, participants completed a 4-day food diary (including one weekend day) to assess habitual dietary intake.

Intervention

The oral postbiotic supplement consisted of 500 mg VMK223, a proprietary blend of postbiotic exopolysaccharides and cell wall polysaccharides from Bifidobacterium breve BB091109 (veMico Limited, UK), encapsulated in hydroxypropyl methylcellulose (HPMC) capsules (size 0). The placebo contained microcrystalline cellulose (Alfa Aesar, UK) and was identical in appearance, taste, and packaging. Both products were inert with respect to colonic fermentation and packaged in identical containers, providing a 30-day supply.

The daily dose of 500 mg VMK223 was selected on the basis of previous clinical research in a comparable cohort of women aged 40–55 years [18], in which this dose was well tolerated and associated with changes in systemic inflammatory and endocrine-related biomarkers, and was therefore considered appropriate for use in the present study.

Participants consumed one capsule daily for 12 weeks. Compliance was monitored through daily questionnaires and by counting returned unused capsules at study visits.

Skin Appearance Assessment

Skin quality and appearance were objectively assessed using the API-100 Skin Analysis Device (Aram Huvis, South Korea), a portable, high-resolution, noninvasive diagnostic tool designed to evaluate key skin parameters: moisture, sebum, pore condition, melanin (pigmentation), wrinkle depth, and skin elasticity. The API-100 (Aram Huvis, Republic of Korea) combines high-resolution digital imaging with optical and sensor-based measurements to assess multiple skin parameters. High-resolution imaging approaches have been applied in dermatological research to assess skin parameters under standardized conditions [19].

To enhance reproducibility, all measurements were performed under controlled environmental conditions, the device was calibrated prior to each session according to manufacturer instructions, and three replicate measurements per parameter were obtained at each time point, with mean values used for analysis.

The device is equipped with a 1/4-in. color CMOS sensor (1624 × 1212 pixels) and employs fixed-focus magnification lenses (×30-NL, ×30-PL, and ×30-UV) with integrated standard and ultraviolet (UV) light-emitting diodes (LEDs). Measurements were performed on the left cheek under standardized conditions (temperature: 20–22°C, relative humidity: 45–55%) at baseline (week 0), week 4, week 8, and week 12. Participants cleansed their faces with a mild cleanser and refrained from applying skincare products for at least 12 h before each assessment session. Composite Skin Quality Score Development

The composite score was prespecified as an exploratory outcome intended to provide an integrated overview of skin appearance changes rather than a validated clinical endpoint.

The score was developed to integrate four key parameters (pores, melanin, acne, and wrinkles) into a single metric and was calculated as a weighted average using the following formula:

Composite Score = [(0.25 × Pores) + (0.25 × Melanin) + (0.16 × Acne) + (0.34 × Wrinkles)]

Each parameter was first normalized to a 0–100 scale on the basis of device reference values, with lower scores indicating improved skin appearance. Weighting coefficients were defined a priori to reflect the relative contribution of each parameter to overall skin appearance, informed by published literature on skin aging-related changes [20]. Wrinkles were assigned the highest weighting, reflecting their recognized importance in skin ageing-related changes, followed by pores and melanin, while acne was assigned a lower weighting but retained as a relevant parameter in this age group. The composite score showed a strong correlation (r = 0.82, p < 0.01) with subjective ratings on a standardized Skin Quality Index, supporting its use as an exploratory integrative measure in this study.

Statistical Analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using Python (version 3.10) with the statsmodels [21], SciPy [22], and seaborn libraries [23]. Data were assessed for normality using the Shapiro–Wilk test, and for homogeneity of variances, Levene’s test was used [24, 25].

Continuous outcome variables were expressed as means ± standard deviations (SD). For the primary analysis of time-dependent changes in skin parameters, a two-way mixed-effects linear model was employed, with group (VMK223 versus placebo) as a between-subject factor and time (baseline, week 4, week 8, week 12) as a within-subject factor. Participant ID was included as a random effect to account for repeated measurements. The group × time interaction term was used to assess differential treatment effects over time.

For each parameter, percentage change from baseline to week 12 was calculated and compared between groups using independent samples t-tests. Pearson correlation coefficients were computed to evaluate associations between percentage changes in skin outcomes.

For secondary analyses involving multiple pairwise comparisons, Bonferroni correction was applied to control the family-wise error rate. In addition to p-values, 95% confidence intervals and effect size estimates (Cohen’s d) were calculated for key between-group differences to aid interpretation of the results. All tests were two-tailed, with statistical significance set at p < 0.05.

The planned sample size was determined a priori to detect medium effect sizes in primary instrumental skin parameters with 80% power at a two-sided alpha of 0.05, assuming moderate within-subject correlation in repeated measures. As an exploratory study, no formal adjustment for multiple endpoints was used in the sample size estimation. The final cohort of 29 completers was therefore considered sufficient for detecting moderate-to-large effects, but may limit the precision of effect estimates.

Effects of VMK223 on Skin Parameters

Overall Skin Composite Score

The composite skin quality score was significantly improved in the VMK223 group compared with placebo at all time points (p < 0.001). At week 4, VMK223 supplementation was associated with a 7.76% ± 2.93% improvement from baseline, compared with a 2.86% ± 2.23% improvement in the placebo group. This difference increased at week 8 (15.40% ± 3.85% versus 3.37% ± 2.05%) and reached 20.93% ± 4.81% by week 12 in the VMK223 group, compared with 5.56% ± 3.89% in the placebo group (Fig. 2A). The between-group difference at week 12 was 15.37%, corresponding to an absolute difference of −6.66 units (95% confidence interval [CI −9.0, −4.3]; Cohen’s d = 2.06), indicating a large effect size.

Individual Skin Parameters

Pore appearance: Significant reductions in pore visibility were observed in the VMK223 group at all time points. At week 12, VMK223 supplementation was associated with a 21.99% ± 5.76% improvement in pore appearance, compared with an 8.19% ± 8.55% improvement in the placebo group (p < 0.001) (Fig. 2B). The between-group difference at week 12 was 13.80%, corresponding to an absolute difference of −4.97 units (95% CI [−8.8, −1.1]; Cohen’s d = 0.86).

Melanin levels: While changes at weeks 4 and 8 were not statistically significant compared with placebo, by week 12, VMK223 supplementation was associated with a significantly greater reduction in melanin levels (11.48% ± 11.88%) compared with placebo (0.91% ± 10.14%) (p < 0.05) (Fig. 2C). The between-group difference at week 12 was 10.57%, corresponding to an absolute difference of −3.88 units (95% CI [−9.0, 1.2]; Cohen’s d = 0.58).

Acne severity: Statistically significant between-group differences emerged at week 8, with VMK223 supplementation associated with a 12.93% ± 6.59% reduction compared with 5.23% ± 6.63% in the placebo group (p < 0.01). These differences were maintained at week 12 (15.88% ± 6.59% versus 6.98% ± 8.09%, respectively; p < 0.01) (Fig. 2D). The between-group difference at week 12 was 8.90%, corresponding to an absolute difference of −6.50 units (95% CI [−14.4, 1.4]; Cohen’s d = 0.63).

Wrinkle depth: Among all measured parameters, wrinkle depth showed the largest between-group differences. At week 12, VMK223 supplementation was associated with a 28.04% ± 8.81% reduction in wrinkle depth, compared with a 4.44% ± 6.84% reduction in the placebo group (p < 0.001) (Fig.3A). The between-group difference at week 12 was 23.60%, corresponding to an absolute difference of −10.03 units (95% CI [−13.9, −6.2]; Cohen’s d = 2.01).

Time-Dependent Effects

A consistent, time-dependent pattern of improvement was observed across all measured skin parameters in the VMK223 group. While modest changes were evident at week 4, most parameters showed larger improvements by week 8, with the most pronounced differences observed at week 12. This pattern is consistent with progressive changes with continued VMK223 supplementation (Supplementary Tables 2–4).

Correlation Analysis

Correlation analysis identified significant positive associations between changes in several skin parameters, suggesting coordinated changes across related aspects of skin appearance (Fig. 4). The strongest associations were observed between improvements in hydration and elasticity (r = 0.74, p < 0.001), and between reductions in wrinkle depth and improvements in pore appearance (r = 0.68, p < 0.001).

Discussion

This randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial demonstrates that 12 weeks of oral postbiotic supplementation with VMK223 is associated with significant improvements in multiple objective skin appearance parameters in healthy women aged 40–55 years. Compared with placebo, VMK223 supplementation was associated with greater reductions in wrinkle depth, pore appearance, acne severity, and melanin levels, alongside meaningful increases in skin hydration and elasticity. These changes were progressive over time, with the most pronounced effects observed between weeks 8 and 12.

The magnitude and consistency of the observed effects across several independent skin parameters suggest a coordinated improvement in overall skin appearance rather than isolated changes in single metrics. Wrinkle depth, hydration, and elasticity, which are closely linked to skin aging and dermal integrity, showed the largest between-group differences [1]. The observed time-dependent pattern is consistent with systemic interventions that influence skin physiology indirectly rather than producing immediate surface effects [7].

While the present study was not designed to investigate biological mechanisms, the findings can be considered in the context of emerging concepts surrounding the gut–skin axis and systemic contributors to skin health [10, 15]. Postbiotics may influence host physiology through multiple pathways, including enhancement of intestinal barrier integrity and modulation of microbial metabolite production, such as short-chain fatty acids, which may attenuate low-grade systemic inflammation [15]. These signals may in turn influence skin homeostasis.

In addition, microbial metabolites and postbiotic components have been reported to influence keratinocyte differentiation, epidermal tight junction integrity, and dermal fibroblast function, which are processes relevant to barrier function, collagen and elastin turnover, and skin hydration [10, 26, 27]. However, these mechanisms were not directly assessed in this trial and should be considered as biologically plausible pathways rather than demonstrated effects.

Future studies should incorporate circulating inflammatory markers (e.g., IL-6, TNF-α), skin barrier-related proteins (e.g., filaggrin, claudins), endocrine parameters, and microbiome profiling to better characterize the pathways linking postbiotic supplementation to skin outcomes.

These findings also align with recent descriptions of a microbiome–aging–wrinkles axis, in which age-related changes in host physiology and microbial communities have been associated with wrinkle formation and loss of elasticity through mechanisms such as low-grade inflammation, oxidative stress, and matrix metalloproteinase activation [15, 16]. Within this framework, microbiome-targeted interventions, including oral probiotics, postbiotics, and prebiotic-containing formulations, have been associated with improvements in skin-related outcomes such as elasticity, hydration, and wrinkle appearance in clinical and review literature [7, 17, 28]. Although the present study did not include microbiome or mechanistic biomarker measurements, the observed improvements in skin parameters are consistent with these emerging concepts and support further investigation in studies integrating microbial, molecular, and clinical outcomes.

Previous clinical research has shown that VMK223 supplementation can influence inflammatory and endocrine-related biomarkers in a comparable population [18]. These earlier findings provide biological context for the present results but should not be interpreted as mechanistic evidence within this trial. Importantly, no inflammatory, hormonal, or microbiome-related markers were assessed in the current study, and causal pathways cannot be inferred.

The magnitude and time course of improvements observed with VMK223 are broadly consistent with previous trials of oral microbiome-targeted or nutricosmetic interventions reporting improvements in wrinkle depth, hydration, and barrier-related outcomes over 8–12 weeks, although direct comparisons are limited by differences in populations, formulations, and outcome measures [7, 8, 28]. Notably, VMK223 represents a postbiotic formulation and therefore differs mechanistically from live probiotic or synbiotic preparations [11], offering a potentially more standardized and host-targeted approach.

The composite skin quality score, developed as an exploratory measure integrating pores, melanin, acne, and wrinkles, showed a significantly greater improvement in the VMK223 group compared with placebo. Its strong correlation with subjective skin assessments suggests that the instrumental changes observed may be perceptible and relevant to individuals, although subjective outcomes were not formally assessed in this trial [29].

The clinical relevance of these findings lies in the potential role of postbiotics as an adjunctive, systemic approach to skin health. Oral interventions may complement topical skincare by addressing broader physiological factors that influence skin appearance, particularly in women undergoing midlife changes [28, 30]. The present results support further investigation of postbiotics within dermatological research, including studies that integrate biological markers, longer intervention periods, and diverse populations.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. The sample size was modest, and although the study was designed to detect medium effect sizes in primary instrumental skin parameters, the final cohort of 29 completers may limit the precision of the estimates and the generalizability of the findings, particularly to other populations, including men, different age groups, or individuals with dermatological conditions. The intervention period was 12 weeks, and longer-term effects remain unknown. Most importantly, the absence of concurrent biological measurements (e.g., circulating cytokines, skin barrier markers, and microbiome composition) precludes mechanistic interpretation. Future studies should incorporate systemic biomarkers, skin barrier assessments, and patient-reported outcomes to provide a more comprehensive understanding of the pathways involved.

Conclusions

This randomized, placebo-controlled trial shows that 12 weeks of oral postbiotic supplementation with VMK223 is associated with significant improvements in multiple objective skin appearance parameters in healthy women aged 40–55 years. The observed improvements in wrinkle depth, hydration, elasticity, and overall skin quality support the potential of postbiotics as a systemic, adjunctive strategy for enhancing visible skin health. These findings provide a basis for further dermatological research integrating biological and patient-centered outcomes to clarify underlying mechanisms and clinical applications.

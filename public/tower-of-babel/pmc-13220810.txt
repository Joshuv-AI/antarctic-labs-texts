Risk prediction models for venous thromboembolism among patients with multiple myeloma: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Jin Yang, You Pu, Yuqin Li, Xuelian Li, Xia Jiang, Liping Zou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundRisk prediction models help identify multiple myeloma (MM) patients at high risk of venous thromboembolism (VTE) and guide clinical decisions. However, their applicability and accuracy remain unclear. This study aims to systematically review existing VTE risk models in MM patients.MethodsWe systematically searched PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, and Web of Science for studies on VTE risk prediction models in patients with MM, up to March 31, 2026. Two investigators independently screened the literature, extracted data, and assessed the risk of bias and applicability of the included studies using the PROBAST tool. Data analysis was performed using the “meta” and “metafor” packages in R software.ResultsA total of 14 studies on VTE risk prediction models in MM patients were included, involving the development and/or validation of seven risk assessment tools. Meta-analysis showed that the overall VTE incidence in MM patients was 7.9% (95% CI [6.2–10.1%]). The combined area under the curve (AUCs) of the seven tools ranged from 0.57 to 0.68, with the IMPEDE VTE and IMPEDED VTE scores showing the best performance. Only two studies used the Hosmer-Lemeshow test for model calibration, and all studies presented the models in formula form. All included studies were at high risk of bias, mainly in the outcome and analysis domains.ConclusionExisting VTE risk models for MM patients show low predictive performance (pooled AUC < 0.7) and limited clinical use. Future research should focus on model updating and external validation to improve accuracy and applicability. PROSPERO Registration number ID: CRD420251024346.

Introduction

Multiple myeloma (MM) is a hematologic malignancy caused by clonal abnormal proliferation of plasma cells, which accounts for approximately 2% of all malignancies and 13% of all hematologic malignancies (McCurdy et al., 2023; Elbahoty, Papineni & Samant, 2024). According to the global epidemiological data in 2022, there will be about 188,000 new cases of MM and 121,000 deaths (Mafra et al., 2024). Patients with MM are often accompanied by highly disabling complications, such as bone destruction, renal insufficiency, and anemia, which seriously affect the quality of life and survival rate of patients (Long et al., 2025).

Novel therapeutic agents such as proteasome inhibitors, immunomodulatory drugs (IMiDs), and anti-CD38 monoclonal antibodies have significantly improved survival outcomes in MM patients. Nevertheless, certain treatment regimens increase the risk of venous thromboembolism (VTE), with IMiDs being a major contributing factor, while proteasome inhibitors are typically associated with lower thrombotic risk (Fotiou, Dimopoulos & Kastritis, 2022). Recent prospective studies report that the incidence of VTE in MM patients receiving IMiD therapy has exceeded 10% (Bradbury et al., 2020; Sborov et al., 2022). Even after anticoagulant therapy (including aspirin, prophylactic low molecular weight heparin, and warfarin), the VTE incidence remains above 4% (Palumbo et al., 2011; Chalayer et al., 2022), far exceeding that of the general population (0.1–0.3%) (Beckman et al., 2010). VTE not only leads to chemotherapy interruption, it also increases the risk of anticoagulation-related bleeding as well as the risk of death (Schoen et al., 2020; Khorana et al., 2022; Escobar et al., 2022). Studies have shown that the risk of death within 1 year in MM patients with VTE is three times higher than that in patients without VTE (Kristinsson et al., 2012). Therefore, accurately identifying patients with a high risk of VTE and optimizing prevention strategies are crucial to improve the clinical outcome of patients with MM.

The National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) (Streiff et al., 2021) emphasized that risk assessment models play a key role in disease diagnosis, prevention, and prognostic evaluation. In VTE management, risk prediction models can be used for individualized risk stratification to assist clinicians in accurately assessing patients’ thrombosis risk, thereby optimizing prevention strategies and balancing the benefits of anticoagulation therapy with potential bleeding risks (Horner et al., 2024; Stefano et al., 2022). Many scholars have studied VTE risk prediction models in MM patients (Li et al., 2023; Chakraborty et al., 2022; Sanfilippo et al., 2019; Li et al., 2019), however, due to factors such as study populations, treatment modalities, and differences in sample sizes, there is significant heterogeneity in the predictive efficacy of the current mainstream models (e.g., IMPEDE-VTE, SAVED, IMWG guideline, etc.), and their applicability and accuracy in clinical practice remain controversial. Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate the predictive performance and methodological quality of the existing models through systematic review and meta-analysis of all published VTE prediction models in MM patients, to provide a reference for clinical medical staff to select or develop more practical prognostic risk assessment models.

Methods

We designed this systematic review according to the checklist provided by the preferred reporting items for systematic reviews and meta-analyses guidelines (Supplemental Material). The study protocol has been registered on PROSPERO (registration number: CRD420251024346).

Search strategy

Due to the project’s tight timeline, formal screening of search results against eligibility criteria began before submitting to PROSPERO. This early screening was intended to streamline the review process and meet deadlines. All steps followed pre-specified criteria, and PROSPERO registration was submitted once the process was completed.

We conducted a systematic search of four databases (PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, and Web of Science) using the following keywords: “Multiple myeloma”, “Plasma cell myeloma*”, “MM”; “Venous thromboembolism”, “Venous thrombosis”, “VTE”, “Deep vein thrombosis”, “DVT”, “Pulmonary embolism”, “PE”; “Risk assessment”, “Risk factors”, “Risk prediction”, “Risk score”, “Prognostic model”, “Predictive Model”, “Nomogram”, “Algorithm”, with the search updated through March 31, 2026. In the case of Pubmed, the detailed search strategy is shown in the Supplemental Material. Furthermore, we manually reviewed the reference lists of both original studies and review articles to identify additional relevant studies. All English-language predictive model studies meeting our inclusion criteria (PICOTS) were included as follows:

P (Population): Patients diagnosed with MM

I (Intervention model): All risk prediction models used to predict VTE in MM patients

C (Comparator): Not applicable

O(Outcome): Objectively diagnosed VTE (including, but not limited to, deep vein thrombosis types, pulmonary embolism, and catheter-related venous thrombosis)

T (Timing): Outcomes were measured across the entire projection period without restrictions

S (Setting): Applicable across diverse healthcare environments.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Two searchers independently screened the literature based on the following criteria. The inclusion criteria included: (a) studies on MM patients aged ≥18 years; (b) studies on the construction and/or validation of VTE prediction models for MM patients; (c) study type of cross-sectional, case-control, or cohort studies; and (d) in cases where studies on the same topic were repeated, preference was given to more recent publications or those with more detailed information. The following exclusion criteria were applied: (a) articles that were not published, conference abstracts, or review articles; (b) studies where the full text was unavailable or data was incomplete. Two independent researchers initially performed the literature screening, and any disagreements were resolved by a third reviewer. The basic details and data extracted from the studies were cross-verified by the two researchers.

Study selection and data extraction

Two researchers (JY, YP) independently screened the literature according to the inclusion and exclusion criteria, deleted duplicates using NoteExpress software, first screened the titles and abstracts of the literature, and then re-screened the literature by reading the full text, and finally included the literature that met the criteria. When research choices were inconsistent, the third reviewer (LPZ) had talks to reach an agreement.

Based on the critical appraisal and data extraction for systematic reviews of prediction modeling studies (CHARMS) proposed by Moons et al. (2014). A data extraction form was developed. Two investigators extracted data independently, including Author(s)/year, Country, Study design, Participants, Treatment plan, Follow-up time, VTE (median time), VTE type, Sample size/VTE cases, Missing data handling, Variable selection, Final predictors, Model development method, Validation method, Model performance, Model presentation, and so on. Any differences of opinion among the authors were settled through discussion or by consulting a third author for arbitration (LPZ). For studies involving multiple predictive models, we extracted the relevant data for each model separately. For models that were externally validated in multiple studies, we included only externally validated studies based on non-overlapping populations to avoid replication effects.

Quality assessment

In this study, two investigators assessed the risk of bias and applicability of the included prediction models using PROBAST (prediction model risk of bias assessment tool) (Moons et al., 2019). When assessing the risk of bias, the analysis is mainly carried out from four aspects: study subjects, predictors, outcomes, and analysis; The evaluation of applicability focuses on the suitability of study subjects, predictors, and outcomes. The assessment results are divided into three categories: “low risk”, “high risk” and “unclear”. During the evaluation process, the two researchers independently evaluated the quality, and when they encountered differences, they reached an agreement through discussion and invited third-party experts to make a ruling if necessary.

Statistical analysis

In this study, R version 4.4. 0 software was used for data analysis (R Core Team, 2024), and meta-analysis was completed using the “meta” and “metafor” software packages. When no less than two original studies were included, a fixed-effect or random-effect model was used to perform a Meta-analysis of the incidence of VTE in MM patients and the discriminant efficacy of the risk prediction model. For the pooled incidence of VTE, if there is high heterogeneity and the number of studies is not less than two, further subgroup analyses will be conducted to explore the source of heterogeneity. For external validation studies of predictive models, if two or more studies were included, the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) was pooled and analyzed to assess the overall predictive performance of the models and their applicability in different clinical settings. AUC = 0.5 indicated no predictive ability, AUC > 0.5 and ≤0.7 indicated weak predictive ability, AUC > 0.7 and ≤0.9 indicated good predictive ability, and AUC = 1 indicates perfect predictive performance (de Hond, Steyerberg & van Calster, 2022). Heterogeneity was assessed by the I2 statistic, where I2 = 25%, 50%, and 75% represent low, moderate, and high levels of heterogeneity, respectively (Huedo-Medina et al., 2006). If ≥10 studies are included, funnel plots and Egger’s test can be used to assess publication bias; p > 0.05 indicates no significant publication bias (Egger et al., 1997). In addition, to ensure the robustness of the results, sensitivity analyses were conducted by excluding studies one at a time to assess their impact on the overall findings.

Results

Literature screening

A total of 2,578 relevant studies were retrieved from the database, and two articles were included in the manual search. After removing duplicates (n = 156) and excluding irrelevant titles and abstracts (n = 2,357), 67 studies were selected for full-text review, of which 53 were excluded due to study subject in compliance, non-predictive models, conference abstracts, duplicates, and finally 14 studies were included. Basic information about the studies

A total of 14 studies were included, published between 2019 and 2025. Of these, 13 were retrospective cohort studies (Chalayer et al., 2022; Li et al., 2023; Chakraborty et al., 2022; Sanfilippo et al., 2019; Li et al., 2019; Gursoy et al., 2025; Fernandez-Vargas et al., 2024; Bao et al., 2024; da Costa et al., 2023; Dima et al., 2023; Sanfilippo et al., 2022; Bravo-Perez et al., 2021; Covut et al., 2021), one was a retrospective case-control study (Sanfilippo et al., 2023), and two data (Li et al., 2023; Gursoy et al., 2025) were derived from multicenters. Studies were mainly from Europe and the United States, including the United States (n = 7) (Chakraborty et al., 2022; Sanfilippo et al., 2019; Li et al., 2019; Dima et al., 2023; Sanfilippo et al., 2022; Covut et al., 2021; Sanfilippo et al., 2023), China (n = 2) (Li et al., 2023; Bao et al., 2024), and one each from Brazil (da Costa et al., 2023), France (Chalayer et al., 2022), Mexico (Fernandez-Vargas et al., 2024), Spain (Bravo-Perez et al., 2021), and Turkey (Gursoy et al., 2025). Sample sizes ranged from 131 to 4,446 cases, with four studies (Li et al., 2023; Sanfilippo et al., 2019; Li et al., 2019; Sanfilippo et al., 2022) having sample sizes of more than 1,000 cases. The duration of follow-up varied across studies, with VTE follow-up dates ranging from 1 month to 12 months, and six studies (Chalayer et al., 2022; Chakraborty et al., 2022; Li et al., 2019; Gursoy et al., 2025; Bravo-Perez et al., 2021; Sanfilippo et al., 2023) described a median time to VTE appearance, ranging from 1.7 to 4.8 months. A total of 13 studies (Chalayer et al., 2022; Li et al., 2023; Chakraborty et al., 2022; Sanfilippo et al., 2019; Li et al., 2019; Gursoy et al., 2025; Fernandez-Vargas et al., 2024; Bao et al., 2024; da Costa et al., 2023; Dima et al., 2023; Sanfilippo et al., 2022; Bravo-Perez et al., 2021; Covut et al., 2021) reported on the percentage of IMiD use, covering a range of 14.3% to 100%, with five studies (Chalayer et al., 2022; Li et al., 2023, 2019; da Costa et al., 2023; Dima et al., 2023) reaching 100% IMiD use (see Table 1 for details).

Incidence of VTE in patients with MM

One case-control study was excluded, and 13 cohort studies were finally included for meta-analysis of VTE incidence. The incidence of VTE reported in each study ranged from 4.8% to 16.4%. A pooled analysis using a random effects model showed a combined VTE incidence of 7.9% (95% CI [6.2–10.1%]; I2 = 93.1%, p < 0.001) (Fig. 2). Sensitivity analysis was performed by sequentially excluding each individual study (Fig. 3). The pooled incidence of VTE remained stable, ranging from 7.4% to 8.3%, and the heterogeneity remained high (I2 = 89.8~93.6%). Further subgroup analysis by follow-up time (6 months vs. 12 months) showed a combined incidence rate of 6.6% (95% CI [4.8–8.9%]; I2 = 90%, p < 0.001) in the 6-month follow-up group (Chalayer et al., 2022; Sanfilippo et al., 2019; Gursoy et al., 2025; Bao et al., 2024; Sanfilippo et al., 2022; Covut et al., 2021); The combined incidence in the 12-month follow-up group (Li et al., 2023; Chakraborty et al., 2022; Li et al., 2019; da Costa et al., 2023; Dima et al., 2023) was 9.5% (95% CI [6.6–13.5%]; I² = 91.1%, p < 0.001). The results of the random effects model analysis showed that there was no statistically significant difference in the incidence of VTE between groups at different follow-up times (χ² = 2.33, p = 0.127). The results of the publication bias assessment showed that the overall funnel plot distribution was approximately symmetrical, with no obvious asymmetry (Fig. 4). The Egger test also did not indicate significant publication bias (t = 1.28, p = 0.228).

Basic information of the models

Fourteen studies reported clinical practice with one or more of seven risk assessment tools, including IMPEDE VTE score, SAVED score, IMWG guideline, PRISM score, IMPEDED VTE score, RAM score, and Khorana score. In terms of missing data processing, seven studies (Chakraborty et al., 2022; Sanfilippo et al., 2019; Gursoy et al., 2025; Fernandez-Vargas et al., 2024; da Costa et al., 2023; Sanfilippo et al., 2022; Covut et al., 2021) excluded patients with missing data, two studies (Li et al., 2023; Dima et al., 2023) used complete datasets, and the remaining five studies (Chalayer et al., 2022; Li et al., 2019; Bao et al., 2024; Bravo-Perez et al., 2021; Sanfilippo et al., 2023) mentioned missing data processing methods. In terms of predictor selection, five studies (Li et al., 2023; Chakraborty et al., 2022; Sanfilippo et al., 2019; Li et al., 2019; Sanfilippo et al., 2023) employed univariate analysis to screen candidate variables during the model construction phase. For model development and validation, four studies (Li et al., 2023; Chakraborty et al., 2022; Sanfilippo et al., 2019; Li et al., 2019) conducted model development and adopted both internal validation (Bootstrapping) and external validation, nine studies (Chalayer et al., 2022; Gursoy et al., 2025; Fernandez-Vargas et al., 2024; Bao et al., 2024; da Costa et al., 2023; Dima et al., 2023; Sanfilippo et al., 2022; Bravo-Perez et al., 2021; Covut et al., 2021) conducted independent external validation, and one study (Sanfilippo et al., 2023) did not report validation methods. During model development, the most commonly used methods were Cox regression (n = 4) (Li et al., 2023; Chakraborty et al., 2022; Sanfilippo et al., 2019; Li et al., 2019) and logistic regression (n = 1) (Sanfilippo et al., 2023) (See Table 2 for details).

Model performance index and presentation

We performed a pooled analysis of AUC for seven risk assessment tools (IMPEDE VTE score, SAVED score, IMWG guideline, PRISM score, IMPEDED VTE score, RAM score, Khorana score). The pooled AUC values ranged from 0.57 to 0.68. Estimates for I2 range from 0% to 93%. The best predictive performance was achieved with the IMPEDE VTE score and the IMPEDED VTE score. The predictive efficacy of the IMPEDE VTE score was evaluated in nine studies (Chalayer et al., 2022; Sanfilippo et al., 2019; Gursoy et al., 2025; Fernandez-Vargas et al., 2024; Bao et al., 2024; da Costa et al., 2023; Bravo-Perez et al., 2021; Covut et al., 2021; Sanfilippo et al., 2023), of which data from seven studies (Gursoy et al., 2025; Fernandez-Vargas et al., 2024; Bao et al., 2024; da Costa et al., 2023; Bravo-Perez et al., 2021; Covut et al., 2021; Sanfilippo et al., 2023) were available for pooled analysis with a pooled AUC of 0.68 (95% CI [0.63–0.72]; I2 = 28.7%, p = 0.163). The IMPEDED VTE score was assessed in two studies (Gursoy et al., 2025; Sanfilippo et al., 2023) with a combined AUC of 0.68 (95% CI [0.62–0.74]; I2 = 0.0%, p = 0.445). RAM score (Li et al., 2023) (AUC = 0.62) and Khorana score (Sanfilippo et al., 2022) (AUC = 0.53) were assessed in only one study, and no pooled analysis was performed. In terms of calibration analysis, only two studies (Sanfilippo et al., 2019, 2023) used the Hosmer-Lemeshow test to assess model calibration, and meta-analyses could not be performed due to inadequate reporting in external validation studies. All models are presented as formulas. For the models that can be analyzed in combination, their AUC results are summarized and presented in a single multi-panel plot to visually compare the prediction performance of each model (Table 3, Fig. 5). A limited number of studies (2–7) were included in each AUC pooled analysis, so no formal publication bias assessment was performed.

Risk of bias assessment

The quality assessment was performed according to the PROBAST tool, and the 14 studies included all showed a high risk of bias, mainly focused on the results domain and the data analysis domain. Involving the following: In the predictor domain, two studies (Fernandez-Vargas et al., 2024; da Costa et al., 2023) did not report whether the measurement method of the predictor was uniform, and one study (Sanfilippo et al., 2023) assessed the predictor with knowledge of the outcome due to study design issues. In the outcome domain, seven studies (Chalayer et al., 2022; Chakraborty et al., 2022; Gursoy et al., 2025; Fernandez-Vargas et al., 2024; da Costa et al., 2023; Sanfilippo et al., 2022; Covut et al., 2021) had non-standardized outcome definitions, and three studies (Chalayer et al., 2022; Gursoy et al., 2025; Sanfilippo et al., 2023) failed to exclude factors overlapping with predictors. In addition, one study (Sanfilippo et al., 2023) did not provide information on the blinded assessment of outcome-predictor relationships and failed to clarify whether there was an appropriate time interval between predictor assessment and outcome determination. In the area of data analysis, three studies (Fernandez-Vargas et al., 2024; da Costa et al., 2023; Sanfilippo et al., 2023) failed to meet the recommendation of “events per variable” (EPV) exceeding 20, and seven studies (Chakraborty et al., 2022; Sanfilippo et al., 2019; Gursoy et al., 2025; Fernandez-Vargas et al., 2024; da Costa et al., 2023; Sanfilippo et al., 2022; Covut et al., 2021) directly eliminated missing data, and five studies (Li et al., 2023; Chakraborty et al., 2022; Sanfilippo et al., 2019; Li et al., 2019; Sanfilippo et al., 2023) used univariate analysis to screen variables. Only two studies (Sanfilippo et al., 2019; Sanfilippo et al., 2023) evaluated the discrimination and calibration of the model simultaneously, and none of them evaluated the clinical utility. In terms of applicability risk assessment, six studies (Chalayer et al., 2022; Gursoy et al., 2025; Fernandez-Vargas et al., 2024; da Costa et al., 2023; Sanfilippo et al., 2022, 2023) were rated as at high risk of bias, mainly because the definition of predictors was not clearly stated in the text, and some studies used subjectively defined outcome measures (See Table 4 for details).

Discussion

This systematic review provides a comprehensive analysis of the predictive performance of existing VTE risk prediction models in patients with MM, involving the development and/or validation of seven risk assessment tools. The results showed that the overall predictive efficacy of the existing VTE risk prediction models for MM patients was weak, and the comprehensive AUC values were less than 0.7. In addition, all included studies had a high risk of bias. Therefore, this study highlights the need to optimize and update VTE prediction models in MM patients to improve their clinical applicability and predictive accuracy.

In this study, the combined incidence of VTE in MM patients was 7.9% (95% CI [6.2–10.1%]), with significant heterogeneity in the included studies (I² = 93.1%), and the heterogeneity could not be reduced by subgroup analyses (6-month vs. 12-month follow-up). This could be attributed to inconsistencies in population characteristics, treatment modalities, or outcome definitions. Regarding the study population, some studies included only patients with newly diagnosed MM, whereas others included patients with both relapsed and refractory MM, and the heterogeneity of the study population may have been exacerbated by differences in disease severity, treatment options, and risk of complications by disease stage. In addition, the proportion of IMiDs used varied greatly (14.3–100%). Although IMiDs have antitumor activity, their antiangiogenic effects also significantly increase the risk of thrombosis (Epstein & Morrison, 2022; Raghupathy et al., 2017). Differences in anticoagulation prevention strategies are also one of the influencing factors, as in Chalayer et al. (2022), more than 90% of patients received VTE prophylaxis (mainly low molecular heparin), whereas the studies by Fernandez-Vargas et al. (2024) and Bao et al. (2024) excluded patients who received anticoagulation. The definition of outcome measures is also inconsistent. Most studies have deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism as the primary endpoints, while some studies (Sanfilippo et al., 2019; Gursoy et al., 2025; Fernandez-Vargas et al., 2024; Bao et al., 2024; Bravo-Perez et al., 2021; Sanfilippo et al., 2023) include superficial venous thrombosis and central venous thrombosis. Studies by Bao et al. (2024) and Sanfilippo et al. (2023) both found that the placement of central venous catheters significantly increased the risk of VTE, and the incidence rate was nearly three times higher than that of patients without catheterization (21.2% vs. 6.0%; 32% vs. 10%).

The literature included in this study covers the development and/or validation of seven risk assessment tools including IMPEDE VTE score, SAVED score, IMWG guidelines, PRISM score, IMPEDED VTE score, RAM score, and Khorana score. Due to the strong heterogeneity among the included studies, the AUC of all models was not analyzed overall, but five of the models (IMPEDE VTE score (Gursoy et al., 2025; Fernandez-Vargas et al., 2024; Bao et al., 2024; da Costa et al., 2023; Bravo-Perez et al., 2021; Covut et al., 2021; Sanfilippo et al., 2023), SAVED score (Li et al., 2023, 2019; Gursoy et al., 2025; Fernandez-Vargas et al., 2024; da Costa et al., 2023; Dima et al., 2023), IMWG guidelines (Li et al., 2023; da Costa et al., 2023), PRISM score (Chakraborty et al., 2022; Fernandez-Vargas et al., 2024), and IMPEDED VTE score (Gursoy et al., 2025; Sanfilippo et al., 2023) were analyzed separately. The results showed that the combined AUC of these models ranged from 0.57 to 0.68, all of which were lower than 0.7, suggesting their limited discrimination ability. Furthermore, for the RAM score (Li et al., 2023) and Khorana score (Sanfilippo et al., 2022), which had a small number of original studies, the reported AUC alone was also less than 0.7, indicating that its predictive power was also weak. Therefore, existing VTE risk prediction tools for MM patients should be used with caution in clinical practice.

We compared the performance of multiple prediction models and found that IMPEDED VTE score and IMPEDE VTE score had the best predictive performance, with pooled AUCs of AUC = 0.68 (95% CI [0.62–0.74]) and AUC = 0.68 (95% CI [0.63–0.72]), respectively, and the heterogeneity of the two models across studies was low (I² < 30%), suggesting their stability in different populations. The IMPEDED VTE score was constructed based on the IMPEDE VTE score by integrating D-dimer metrics, and the predictive power of the model (AUC = 0.65 vs. AUC = 0.61) was improved (Sanfilippo et al., 2023). D-dimer has a high sensitivity in the diagnosis of suspected VTE, normal levels help to exclude VTE and guide anticoagulation therapy, and its dynamic changes better reflect the evolution of thrombotic risk (Yang et al., 2020; Posch et al., 2020). Posch et al. (2020) demonstrated that for every doubling of the trajectory level of D-dimer, the risk of VTE was increased by 2.8 times (95% CI [1.69–4.58]). Gibson et al. (2017) also found that the inclusion of D-dimer in the IMPROVE score improved the ability to discriminate VTE risk in medical patients (Δ AUC: 0.06 (95% CI [0.02–0.09]), p = 0.0006). In addition to D-dimer, other novel biomarkers, such as platelet activation markers (e.g., P-selectin), coagulation factor levels, and inflammatory markers (e.g., IL-6, fibrinogen), also show potential predictive value and can provide supplementary information for future VTE risk models (Joy, Venkatachalam & Binesh, 2026; Riondino et al., 2019). Combining these biomarkers for longitudinal monitoring and integrating dynamic indicators with clinical features is expected to further optimize the performance of existing predictive models. Additionally, using machine learning or multi-omics methods to construct joint multi-marker prediction models may improve the ability to identify individualized risk for different patient groups. It should be noted that the IMPEDED VTE score has been validated only in a small-sample retrospective study (Gursoy et al., 2025), and its broad applicability remains to be confirmed. Future studies should verify the model’s performance in large samples and across different populations, and explore the gain effect of novel biomarkers in dynamic monitoring and model optimization, to improve the accuracy and clinical applicability of VTE risk prediction.

PROBAST tool evaluation showed that all included studies had a high risk of bias, mainly reflected in insufficient sample size, improper handling of missing data, non-standard independent variable screening methods, and incomplete evaluation of model performance. Although da Costa et al. (2023) reported a high AUC value (0.80), their small sample size (n = 130) may have led to the overfitting of the model in the training set, thus limiting its ability to generalize to a wider population (Wynants et al., 2015). PROBAST recommends multiple imputations for handling missing data. However, most studies in this study directly excluded missing values (n = 7), which may lead to information loss and selective bias (Xin et al., 2025). Although Sanfilippo et al. (2019) and Li et al. (2019) had relatively large sample sizes, variable screening relied solely on univariate analysis and failed to fully consider interactions between variables, potentially missing key predictors and affecting model stability and predictive performance (Chowdhury & Turin, 2020). It is suggested that more advanced variable selection methods such as LASSO regression (McNeish, 2015) should be adopted in future research to effectively deal with multicollinearity problems and improve the stability and generalization ability of the model.

Currently, the traditional Cox regression (Li et al., 2023; Chakraborty et al., 2022; Sanfilippo et al., 2019; Li et al., 2019) and logistic regression (Sanfilippo et al., 2023) are still used for model construction, which has limitations in dealing with nonlinear relationships and confounding factors and may limit the improvement of model performance (Dalicandro et al., 2021). In the future, diversified modeling techniques such as machine learning and deep learning can be considered to enhance the accuracy and adaptability of prediction models (Koppe, Meyer-Lindenberg & Durstewitz, 2021). Furthermore, although all studies reported the discriminative ability of the models (e.g., AUC), only two (Sanfilippo et al., 2019, 2023) further evaluated their calibration performance. The degree of calibration measures the consistency between the predicted probability and the actual incidence rate and is an important indicator to evaluate the clinical practicability of the model, which is usually presented by a calibration plot (Steyerberg & Vergouwe, 2014). The lack of systematic calibration assessment may lead to one-sided interpretations of model efficacy and increase the risk of reporting bias. Another key issue is that none of the available studies conducted clinical utility assessments. Although statistical indicators such as discrimination and calibration can reflect model performance, they cannot directly evaluate their value in clinical decision-making. Future research should introduce methods such as decision curve analysis to clarify whether the model truly “benefits outweigh disadvantages” in practical application.

Although the IMPEDE score has been recommended by the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) (Streiff et al., 2021) to assess the risk of VTE in patients with MM, where those with a score ≥8 are classified as high risk and recommended for anticoagulation prophylaxis, this systematic review shows that this score failed to effectively identify high-risk patients in some studies, suggesting that it may have limitations in specific patient groups. This result is consistent with the International Myeloma Working Group (IMWG) and European Multiple Myeloma Network (EMN) guidelines: the guidelines recommend standard risk stratification for MM patients receiving IMiDs, emphasize that high-risk patients should implement anticoagulation prophylaxis, and point out that existing scoring tools still need to be externally validated in different patient populations (Yang et al., 2023). These findings suggest that existing risk scores may not capture all high-risk patients; therefore, external validation through large-sample, multicenter, prospective studies is still required before routine clinical application. In the future, optimizing risk assessment models in combination with dynamic clinical features or novel biomarkers may improve their applicability across different clinical settings and patient populations.

This study still has the following limitations: (a) Most of the included studies are based on European and American populations, which may limit the generalization of the study results in other populations; (b) only English literature is included, which may omit important studies in other languages and affect the comprehensiveness of the results; (c) all included studies are retrospective cohort or case-control studies, which are susceptible to selection bias and information bias, and the completeness and accuracy of data collection are difficult to guarantee, which may reduce the reliability and external validity of the results; (d) since 95% CI was not reported for AUC in some studies, this study was excluded, which may cause some bias to the quantitative synthesis results.

Conclusion

A total of 14 studies on risk prediction models for VTE in MM patients were included in this systematic review, involving the development and/or validation of seven risk assessment tools. Among them, the IMPEDE VTE score and IMPEDED VTE score had the best predictive performance, but both lacked the effective identification ability of high-risk VTE patients (pooled AUC < 0.7). In addition, all included studies had a high risk of bias as assessed by PROBAST. Therefore, it is recommended that future studies strictly follow the TRIPOD statement for study design and standardized reporting, and optimize or update the existing models in multicenter, large-sample prospective cohorts by optimizing variable selection, improving modeling methods, and introducing new biomarkers to improve their predictive efficacy and clinical applicability.

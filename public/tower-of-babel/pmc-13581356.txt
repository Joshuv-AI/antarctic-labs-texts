Evolution and Hotspots in Autologous Ear Reconstruction: A Bibliometric Analysis of the 100 Most-cited Studies
Authors: Charbel G. Saad, Laurentia Fidella Averina Setia Santoso, Rola Mahdi, Soumiya Nadar, Thenuli Tamasha Jayamaha Hitihamilage, Nancy Emmanuel, Juan Pablo Menendez, Gregory Nicolas
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Autologous auricular reconstruction has evolved from cartilage-carving techniques that established modern microtia repair to an expanding regenerative and biofabrication literature. However, the field has not been mapped bibliometrically using an autologous-only lens that separates the clinical literature from emerging tissue-engineering hotspots.Methods:A Scopus search in January 2026 identified publications on autologous auricular reconstruction. Records were sorted by citation count, screened for eligibility, and the 100 most-cited studies were included. Data extracted included citations, citations per year, year, journal, country, and evidence category. Studies were classified into 4 domains: surgical technique, tissue engineering/basic science, complications/management, and outcomes/quality of life. VOSviewer was used for coauthorship and keyword co-occurrence mapping. Temporal shifts were assessed by comparing the older versus newer halves.Results:The 100 most-cited studies (1971–2023) accrued a mean of 82.97 citations (range, 27–510) with a median of 3.46 citations per year. Most studies were clinical (71%), followed by preclinical (19%) and evidence synthesis (10%). Surgical technique predominated overall (53%), but decreased from 66% in the older half to 40% in the newer half, whereas tissue engineering increased from 16% to 28%. The most-cited article was the description by Nagata of a 2-stage, 3-dimensional costal cartilage framework (1993, Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery). Keyword clustering revealed a temporally newer regenerative cluster centered on chondrocytes, tissue engineering, and biomechanics.Conclusions:The citation canon of autologous auricular reconstruction is built on the late 20th-century technique classics, whereas contemporary influence is increasingly driven by regenerative medicine and biofabrication. Future high-impact work will likely be defined by durable, shape-stable engineered constructs validated in clinically relevant models.

Takeaways

Question: How has the most influential autologous auricular reconstruction literature evolved, and what emerging hotspots are shaping the field?

Findings: In a Scopus-based bibliometric analysis of the 100 most-cited autologous auricular reconstruction studies (1971–2023), surgical technique dominated early citation classics, whereas newer influential work increasingly clustered around tissue engineering, biofabrication, and biomechanics.

Meaning: The citation canon of the field is built on technique-defining surgical lineages, but contemporary influence is shifting toward regenerative strategies that aim to match or surpass rib cartilage reliability.

INTRODUCTION

Congenital and acquired auricular deformities remain among the most technically demanding problems in reconstructive surgery. The external ear is a highly visible 3-dimensional structure with fine subunit anatomy and limited local tissue redundancy, making the consistent restoration of projection and definition challenging. Autologous costal cartilage reconstruction has therefore remained a cornerstone approach for microtia and select complex acquired defects because it offers durable, long-term structural stability.1–3

The foundation of autologous auricular reconstruction was established through successive technique-defining series of cartilage carving, inset, and staged reconstruction, including the influential technique lineages developed by Brent and Nagata.1,2,4–15 In parallel, advances in skin expansion and vascularized fascial coverage, particularly temporoparietal and postauricular fascia–based strategies, were developed to improve contour fidelity, enhance auriculocephalic projection, and reduce the risk of framework exposure.16–23

Bibliometric analyses offer a complementary method to quantify influence and identify thematic inflection points by using citations as a proxy for collective scholarly attention. However, the field has not been mapped in a manner that specifically characterizes the most influential autologous auricular reconstruction literature while concurrently visualizing the emergence of regenerative hotspots. Accordingly, the aim of this study is to identify the 100 most-cited publications in autologous auricular reconstruction; characterize their contributing authors, institutions, and countries; and use bibliometric mapping to visualize collaboration networks and detect evolving research hotspots across technique, complications, outcomes, and regenerative domains.

METHODS

Study Design

This study was designed as a bibliometric analysis of the 100 most-cited publications in autologous auricular (external ear) reconstruction. The objective was to map the field’s intellectual foundations, collaboration structure, and thematic evolution from classic costal cartilage framework surgery to emerging regenerative and tissue-engineering strategies.

Data Source

A comprehensive bibliometric search was performed in January 2026 using the Scopus database. Scopus was selected because it offers broad coverage of surgical and multidisciplinary journals and provides robust citation and metadata fields required for bibliometric and network analyses.

Search Strategy

The search was applied to the “article title,” “abstract,” and “keywords” fields using the following Boolean query: (“ear reconstruction” OR “auricular reconstruction” OR “microtia repair” OR “microtia reconstruction”) AND (“autologous” OR “rib cartilage” OR “costal cartilage” OR “autogenous” OR “cartilage framework” OR “chondrocostal” OR “cartilage graft”) AND NOT (“Medpor” OR “polyethylene” OR “alloplastic” OR “prosthetic” OR “osseointegrated” OR “otoplasty”). No publication date restrictions were applied to capture the historical evolution of autologous auricular reconstruction. The search was limited to English-language publications.

Study Selection

The search results were sorted by citation count in descending order. The top 150 records were exported to enable manual screening while capturing the most-cited body of work. Two reviewers independently screened titles and abstracts and, when required, assessed full-text records to determine eligibility and derive the final set of the 100 most-cited included studies. Disagreements were resolved by consensus; if consensus could not be reached, a third reviewer adjudicated.

Eligibility Criteria

Studies were included if they primarily focused on clinical autologous costal cartilage framework reconstruction (including classic techniques and their modifications) or on auricular cartilage engineering/regeneration using autologous chondrocytes, stem or progenitor populations, and related construct development. Benchmark studies that characterized native human auricular cartilage were included when their stated aim was to establish reference properties for engineered or replacement auricular constructs. Accordingly, articles related to biomechanical characterization that were explicitly framed as having “implications for tissue engineering” were retained within the regenerative/basic science domain. Studies were excluded if the reconstructive framework was primarily alloplastic (eg, porous polyethylene/Medpor or permanent polymer frameworks), if they focused on prosthetic reconstruction, if they addressed middle-ear procedures as the primary topic, if they were limited to subunit-only repairs or minor otoplasty/cosmetic reshaping, or if they were purely educational simulation studies without reconstruction or biological construct development as the central aim. Although letters, editorials, and correspondence were removed, highly cited, field-defining review articles focusing explicitly on autologous reconstruction were retained. These reviews were categorized separately from original data-driven studies to preserve analytical distinctness.

Data Extraction

For each included publication, title, first author, senior author, publication year, journal, author affiliations, and country of origin were reviewed. Total citations were recorded as reported by Scopus at the time of export. Citations per year were calculated by dividing the total citations by the number of years since publication, using January 2026 as the reference date.

Topic Domain Classification

Each study was assigned to 1 primary topic domain based on its main focus: surgical technique, tissue engineering/basic science, complications and management, or outcomes and quality of life. Topic classification was performed independently by 2 reviewers, with disagreements resolved by consensus.

Evidence Category Classification

Each study was also categorized by evidence category as clinical, preclinical, or evidence synthesis. Clinical studies were defined as patient-based investigations, including technique descriptions, case series, outcomes studies, and complication management. Preclinical studies included in vitro, animal, cadaveric, biomechanical, or tissue-engineering construct studies. Evidence synthesis included narrative reviews, systematic reviews, and related synthesis articles.

Bibliometric Analysis and Science Mapping

Descriptive analyses were performed to summarize publication chronology, citation performance, and distributions by journal, country, and topic domain. Comparative analyses between the older and newer halves were performed in R software (version 2026.01.0 + 392). Continuous variables were compared using the Mann-Whitney U test and categorical variables using the Pearson χ2 test (or the Fisher exact test when expected cell counts were <5). Two-sided P values of less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Science mapping and network visualization were conducted using VOSviewer (version 1.6.19; Leiden University, the Netherlands). Coauthorship analyses were performed at the author and country levels to evaluate collaboration structures and identify research hubs. For the author-level coauthorship map, the minimum number of documents per author was set to 2. Link strength was defined as the total strength of coauthorship links with other authors, and authors with the greatest total link strength were prioritized for visualization. Network interpretation was based on the largest connected component, which comprised 18 connected authors (reflecting that not all eligible authors formed a single connected network).

Keyword co-occurrence analysis was conducted to identify thematic clusters and research hotspots. Keywords were analyzed using VOSviewer co-occurrence mapping, with the minimum number of occurrences per keyword set to 5. Where necessary, obvious keyword variants were harmonized to improve clustering while preserving distinct technical concepts.

Temporal Trend Analysis

To evaluate thematic evolution over time, studies were sorted by publication year and divided into 2 equal groups (older half = first 50; newer half = last 50). This stratification was specified after final cohort selection as an exploratory analytic framework. The purpose of this comparison was to contrast the highly cited citation canon with the lower ranked but often more contemporary literature within the same top-100 corpus. The distribution of topic domains and keyword clusters was compared between these periods.

RESULTS

The initial search yielded 1060 records. After sorting by citation count in descending order and applying predefined eligibility criteria, the top 100 most-cited articles were included in the final analysis (Fig. 1). The 100 citation classics spanned publications from 1971 to 2023 and were published across 31 journals. The mean total citation count per article was 82.97, with a range of 27–510 citations. The median citations per year was 3.46 (interquartile range, 1.88–5.41). The median total citations was 45.50 (interquartile range, 35.00–95.00). Among the included studies, 71 were clinical studies, 19 were preclinical studies, and 10 were evidence synthesis studies (Table 1). A total of 21 countries were represented based on corresponding author affiliations. Regarding thematic classification, 53 studies were categorized under surgical technique, 22 under tissue engineering/regenerative medicine, 15 under outcomes and quality of life, and 10 under complications and management.

The most-cited study in the surgical technique domain was the 1993 article by Nagata,2 “A New Method of Total Reconstruction of the Auricle for Microtia”, with 510 total citations and 15.45 citations per year. Meanwhile, the study by Brent et al11 “Auricular Repair With Autogenous Rib Cartilage Grafts,” ranked second with 383 citations.

In complication-focused studies, “Chest Wall Deformities and Thoracic Scoliosis After Costal Cartilage Graft Harvesting” by Ohara et al24 was the most cited, with 200 total citations. In the tissue engineering/regenerative domain, the study by Shieh et al25 was the most cited, with 160 total citations.

Analysis of authorship demonstrated a concentration of influence among foundational reconstructive surgeons. Among the top 10 most-cited studies, Nagata contributed 40% and Brent contributed 50%, with the top 5 positions occupied exclusively by these 2 authors (Table 2). Across the entire cohort of 100 studies, Brent was the most prolific first author (n = 9), whereas Nagata was the most frequent senior author (n = 8), reflecting their sustained influence in shaping the autologous reconstruction canon. The majority of top-cited articles were published in Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery (n = 42), followed by the Journal of Plastic, Reconstructive and Aesthetic Surgery (n = 12). Other contributing journals included Clinics in Plastic Surgery and the International Journal of Pediatric Otorhinolaryngology. Geographically, the United States contributed the highest number of corresponding authors (n = 25), followed by China (n = 18) and Japan (n = 13).

To assess thematic evolution, the dataset was stratified chronologically into earlier (n = 50) and more recent (n = 50) halves (Table 3). As expected, the earlier cohort demonstrated a higher mean total citation count (117.140 ± 108.242 versus 48.800 ± 21.759, P < 0.001), reflecting longer time available for citation accumulation. However, the more recent cohort exhibited a significantly higher mean citations per year (4.238 ± 2.122 versus 3.848 ± 3.483, P = 0.028), indicating accelerated citation velocity among contemporary publications. The newer cohort also demonstrated a significantly higher mean number of authors per article (5.700 ± 3.382 versus 2.540 ± 2.002, P < 0.001), suggesting increasing collaborative and multidisciplinary engagement in contemporary auricular reconstruction research. Although multinational collaboration increased from 10% in the earlier cohort to 22% in the newer cohort, this difference did not reach statistical significance (P = 0.173). With respect to topic domain distribution, surgical technique articles decreased from 66% in the earlier cohort to 40% in the newer cohort, whereas tissue engineering studies increased from 16% to 28%, outcomes studies from 12% to 18%, and complication-focused studies from 6% to 14%. Although this redistribution did not achieve statistical significance (P = 0.071), it demonstrates a clear directional shift in thematic emphasis over time.

Overlay visualization based on average publication year (Fig. 4) demonstrated temporal stratification within the semantic network. DISCUSSION

This bibliometric analysis shows that the intellectual base of autologous auricular reconstruction remains anchored in technique-defining clinical series, with the most-cited works disproportionately originating from eras when core operative problems—framework design, staging, projection, and soft-tissue coverage—were being standardized. The concentration of influence among Nagata and Brent in the highest ranks reflects how these articles function as enduring operative reference standards that continue to be cited for principles that remain clinically operative. The 2-stage, highly detailed costal cartilage framework concept described by Nagata2 became the most-cited technical cornerstone, whereas large-volume clinical experiences and refinements consolidated the role of Brent lineage in codifying autologous reconstruction as a durable reconstructive solution.1,4,5,9,11 Similarly, the series of staged modifications by Nagata, tailored to lobule-, concha-, and small-concha types, further formalized phenotype-specific framework logic.12–14 Compared with the broader bibliometric analysis of microtia publications from 2006 to 2020 by Wang et al,27 our study focuses on the 100 most-cited articles in autologous auricular reconstruction and highlights the shift of the field from costal cartilage framework techniques toward cartilage engineering and biofabrication.

Classic technique articles accumulate citations slowly but persistently because they are repeatedly invoked for training, justification of staging, and comparisons across operative lineages. In contrast, newer translational studies tend to exhibit higher citation velocity—consistent with the finding that the more recent half of the dataset has higher citations per year—reflecting rapid expansion of multidisciplinary attention to regenerative approaches. This may partly reflect broader publication growth and faster dissemination, but the clustering of highly cited work around biofabrication, stem cell biology, cartilage engineering, and scaffold design suggests a true shift toward regenerative strategies aimed at reducing donor-site morbidity and technical burden.

The temporal redistribution of topic domains—declining predominance of pure surgical technique alongside rising proportions of regenerative, outcomes, and complication-focused work—captures a clear directional shift in the field’s center of gravity. In the clinical domain, refinements in envelope creation and vascularized coverage reduced exposure risk and improved definition and projection. Tissue expansion approaches were advanced early,16 later scaled in large series,17,19 and paired with fascia-based strategies that improved contour fidelity and safety.18,22,23 Flap-based contributions from Park20 and Park et al21,28 further illustrate the field’s emphasis on reliable soft-tissue coverage and framework preservation, including 2-flap methods and temporoparietal fascial flap experience.

As surgical practice matured, attention broadened to donor-site morbidity and complication prevention. Long-term donor-site sequelae, such as chest wall deformity and scoliosis, were documented in foundational complication articles,24,29 stimulating iterative mitigation strategies, such as donor-site contour reduction and reconstitution30,31 and quantitative imaging-based assessment of residual deformity despite meticulous techniques.32 In parallel, complication-management work increasingly emphasized algorithmic salvage to protect framework integrity and final form.33

Concurrently, regenerative medicine emerged as a distinct and increasingly cohesive hotspot. Early tissue-engineering models established feasibility for auricular-shaped constructs and scaffold-based chondrogenesis.25,34–36 Subsequent work advanced clinically relevant cell-source strategies and full-scale constructs, including microtia chondrocyte–bone marrow–derived mesenchymal stem cell strategies to improve cell availability and stability37 and human auricular chondrocyte–mesenchymal stem cell co-implantation to generate patient-based ear constructs.38 A major translational inflection is the shift toward immunocompetent large-animal validation, which directly addresses the inflammatory and mechanical constraints that often undermine engineered cartilage in subcutaneous environments.39,40

Critically, the definition of success in the field evolved from cartilage formation toward functional equivalence. Autologous rib cartilage reconstruction continues to dominate clinical practice because it is durable and supported by decades of refined, high-volume clinical experience.1–3,9 Yet the most recent hotspot constellation increasingly frames rib-cartilage reconstruction as a benchmark to surpass, focusing on donor-site morbidity, technical complexity, and scalable patient-specific fabrication.41–45

The translational pathway remains constrained by recurring barriers evident across the engineering corpus: maintaining complex ear geometry over time, achieving elastic-cartilage composition, preventing contraction or distortion, ensuring stability in immunocompetent environments, and meeting regulatory/manufacturing requirements for clinical-grade constructs. The studies with the strongest translational plausibility in the top-cited set are those that directly address these barriers,39,40 and platform approaches that integrate biofabrication with clinically relevant cell sources and construct design.43,45 Thus, the bibliometric hotspot map should be interpreted as a shift in research attention and problem-solving.

Geographically, the United States and Japan dominated foundational technique articles, paralleling the Brent and Nagata lineages, whereas China contributed increasingly to later high-volume clinical and expander-based refinements.2,4,5,12,17,19,23,46 The higher number of authors in recent articles likely reflects the multidisciplinary structure of tissue engineering and biofabrication.25,39,43 Practically, these findings reinforce costal cartilage reconstruction as the current clinical benchmark while emphasizing that regenerative approaches must demonstrate durable shape, contour, projection, safety, and reduced donor-site morbidity before changing the practice.

Limitations

Several limitations should frame these interpretations. First, citation-based analyses are time-dependent: older articles have longer to accrue citations, inflating absolute counts compared with newer works; citations per year partially mitigates this but does not remove cohort effects. Second, Scopus-based retrieval may introduce database coverage bias, including underrepresentation of nonindexed journals and non-English literature. This is particularly relevant in auricular reconstruction, where internationally influential surgeons shaped the global practice not only through indexed publications but also through technical dissemination, teaching, and operative refinement. Therefore, citation rank should be interpreted as a measure of indexed academic influence rather than as a complete measure of historical or technical importance. Third, citation count is an imperfect proxy for quality or clinical effectiveness; highly cited studies may reflect historical importance, controversy, or educational utility rather than comparative outcomes. Fourth, assigning each publication to a single primary domain may oversimplify multidimensional studies.

CONCLUSIONS

In summary, the citation canon of autologous auricular reconstruction is built on technique-defining surgical classics that standardized costal cartilage framework design, staging, and soft-tissue coverage. However, the contemporary research frontier has shifted toward regenerative medicine and biofabrication, with accelerating attention to cell sources, scaffold design, native-tissue benchmarking, and translational validation in immunocompetent models. Future high-impact studies will likely be those that bridge the benchtop-to-operating room gap by delivering durable, shape-stable, functionally equivalent auricular constructs that match or surpass rib cartilage reliability while reducing donor-site morbidity and technical burden.

Finite Elements with weighted bases for the fractional Laplacian
Félix del Teso, Stefano Fronzoni, David Gómez-Castro

Abstract
This work presents a numerical study of the Dirichlet problem for the fractional Laplacian ( − Δ ) s (-\Delta)^{s} with s ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) s\in(0,1) using Finite Element methods with non-standard bases. Classical approaches based on piece-wise linear basis yield h 1 2 h^{\frac{1}{2}} convergence rates in the Sobolev–Slobodeckij norm H s H^{s} due to the limited boundary regularity of the solution u ⁡ ( x ) u(x) , which behaves like dist ​ ( x , ℝ d ∖ Ω ) s \textup{dist}(x,\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega)^{s} , where h h is the diameter of the mesh elements. To overcome this limitation, we propose a novel Finite Element basis of the form δ s × ( CLOSE \delta^{s}~\times~( piece-wise linear functions ) ) , where δ \delta is any suitably smooth approximation of dist ​ ( x , ℝ d ∖ Ω ) \textup{dist}(x,\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega) . This exploits the improved regularity of u / δ s u/\delta^{s} , achieving higher convergence rates. Under standard smoothness assumptions the method attains an order h 2 − s h^{2-s} on quasi-uniform meshes, improving the rates with the piece-wise linear basis. We provide a rigorous theoretical error analysis with explicit rates and validate it through numerical experiments.

1
Introduction and main results

The objective of this paper is to perform a numerical study, using finite element methods with non-standard bases, of the following Dirichlet problem associated with the fractional Laplacian:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta)^{s}u(x)&=f(x),&&x\in\Omega,\\
u(x)&=0,&&x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

with
\Omega
a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
(-\Delta)^{s}
denoting the fractional Laplacian of order
s\in(0,1)
and given by

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=C_{d,s}\textrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\mathrm{d}y,\quad\textup{with }C_{d,s}=\frac{4^{s}\Gamma(d/2+s)}{\pi^{d/2}|\Gamma(-s)|}.

(1.2)

Finite element methods for problem (
1.1
) have been studied using piece-wise linear basis functions on polygonal domains (see, for instance, Acosta & Borthagaray
[
4
,
5
]
). The reported convergence rates for these methods, in
s
-fractional Sobolev-Slobodeckij norm, are at best approximately
h^{\frac{1}{2}}
, where
h
is the diameter of the mesh triangles.
The sublinear convergence rates are due to the regularity of the solution
u
, which is at most
u\in H^{s+\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}(\Omega)
for small
\varepsilon>0
(we refer the reader to Proposition 3.6 and Proposition 3.11 in
[
5
]
). This reduced regularity arises from the fact that, even in smooth domains, the solution
u
behaves like
d_{\Omega}^{s}
in a neighbourhood of the boundary
\partial\Omega
, where
d_{\Omega}(x)=\mathrm{dist}(x,\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega)
. We refer to
[
2
]
for a recent result of this type and Section 2.7 in
[
33
]
for a summary of the related regularity results. The low regularity of
u
makes higher convergence rates difficult to obtain using a standard piece-wise linear basis that is normally used for solutions that are at least weakly differentiable once.
In
[
9
]
, the authors point out that when
u
is sufficiently regular and linear up to the boundary (e.g.,
u(x)=(1-|x|^{2})_{+}
with
\Omega=B_{1}
), the higher-order convergence rate
h^{\frac{3}{2}-s}
in the
s
-fractional Sobolev–Slobodeckij norm can be achieved using piecewise linear basis functions. It is worth noting that such functions
u
correspond to solutions of problems with a sign-changing right-hand side
f
(see further discussion in
Section

3
).

Our approach builds on the observation that, intuitively, if
\delta
denotes a smooth approximation of the distance function
d_{\Omega}
, then the quotient
u/\delta^{s}
exhibits significantly better regularity up to the boundary, provided that both
f
and the domain
\Omega
are sufficiently smooth. This regularity theory has been developed through a series of contributions

[
52
,
39
,
50
,
51
,
49
,
2
,
3
]
(see
[
33
]
for a good survey on the matter).
Motivated by this fact, we construct a tailored finite element basis that captures the improved regularity of
u/\delta^{s}
, thereby achieving higher-order convergence rates than those available in the existing literature.
The idea, that will be detailed in (
1.11
), is to use a basis of the form
\delta^{s}\times(
piece-wise linear functions
)
.
One of the key advantages of the finite element method is its effectiveness in approximating smooth functions and our method takes advantage of this well-known fact in order to approximate
u/\delta^{s}
and subsequently, knowing
\delta^{s}
, the solution
u
itself. This motivates our strategy of approximating
u/\delta^{s}
rather than
u
directly.
The advantage of this approach is that we can obtain improved convergence rates, without requiring higher regularity of
u
, but rather
f
and
\Omega
sufficiently smooth, which is often the case in applications. Assuming enough regularity on the right-hand side
f
and the domain
\Omega
, we obtain convergence rates of order
h^{2-s}
in
s
-fractional Sobolev-Slobodeckij norm, improving all the previously reported results in the literature.
Using Aubin-Nitsche duality, we obtain
L^{2}
-rates of higher than our
H^{s}
-rates.
At the end of the paper we provide numerical experiments that precisely match the
H^{s}
-rate of order
h^{2-s}
, and that suggest the
L^{2}
-rate is of order
h^{2}
.

1.1
Preliminaries and functional setting

In this section we introduce the functional spaces, the weak formulation of the fractional Dirichlet problem, as well as the finite element formulation of our numerical scheme with a weighted basis.

Sobolev and Hölder spaces.

We denote by
L^{p}(\Omega)
, for
p\in[1,\infty]
, the standard Lebesgue spaces endowed with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
.
We recall the fractional Sobolev-Slobodeckij spaces of order
s\in(0,1)
, where we will denote the bilinear form

a(u,v)\coloneq\frac{C_{d,s}}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y,

(1.3)

and the associated seminorm
[w]_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\coloneq a(w,w)^{1/2}
. Accordingly, for
s\in(0,1)
we define

H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\coloneq\left\{w\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):[w]_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty\right\},

endowed with the norm

\|w\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\coloneq\|w\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+[w]_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
.
We also introduce the corresponding energy space

V\coloneq\{w\in H^{s}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})\text{ such that }w=0\text{ in }{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\setminus\Omega\},

which is sometimes denoted by
\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
in the literature.

Remark 1.1
.

It is standard to define the trace spaces for
s\in(0,1)
as
H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)\coloneq\overline{C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{H^{s}}
.
When
\partial\Omega\in C^{0,1}
and
s\neq\tfrac{1}{2}
, we have the alternative characterization
V=H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
(see, e.g.,
[
26
]
).
In the case
s=\tfrac{1}{2}
, the space
V
coincides with the so-called Lions–Magenes space
H^{1/2}_{00}(\Omega)
.

The well-known fractional Poincaré inequality

\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C[u]_{H^{s}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})},\qquad\forall u\in V,

(1.4)

ensures that the seminorm
[\cdot]_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
is in fact an equivalent norm on
V
. For further details, we refer the reader to
[
26
,
13
]
.
Finally, for
\alpha>0
we denote by
W^{\alpha,\infty}(\Omega)
the Hölder space of order
\alpha

W^{\alpha,\infty}(\Omega)\coloneq C^{\lceil\alpha\rceil-1,\alpha-\lceil\alpha\rceil+1}(\overline{\Omega}).

We recall that Morrey’s inequality guarantees for all
\alpha>0
we have
H^{\alpha+\frac{d}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset W^{\alpha,\infty}_{\text{loc}}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
.

Weak formulation of the fractional Dirichlet problem.

We point out that we have the integration by parts formula

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(x)(-\Delta)^{s}\psi(x)\mathrm{d}x=a(\phi,\psi)\qquad\forall\phi,\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}^{d}}).

Problem (
1.1
) can be written in weak form as follows:

\text{Find }u\in V\text{ such that }a(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}f(x)v(x)\mathrm{d}x\quad\forall v\in V.

(1.5)

Regularity of the domain and regularized distance functions.

Given the distance function
d_{\Omega}(x)=\mathrm{dist}(x,\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega)
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we consider modified distance functions
\delta
satisfying for some
c,c_{j}>0
:

\displaystyle\delta\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap W^{\sigma,\infty}(\Omega)\textup{ with }\tfrac{1}{c}d_{\Omega}\leq\delta\leq c\,d_{\Omega}\textup{ in }{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\textup{ and}

(
\textup{A}_{\delta}^{\sigma}
)

\displaystyle\textup{$|D^{j}\delta|\leq c_{j}\delta^{\sigma-|j|}$ in $\Omega$ for all multi-indices $j$ with $|j|>\sigma$}.

Remark 1.2
.

The existence of a function
\delta
satisfying (

A
δ

σ

) can be ensured under regularity assumptions on the domain
\Omega
. More precisely,
a natural choice in general domains can be found in Lemma A.2 in
[

under the assumption
\sigma>1
and

and
\partial\Omega\in C^{k,t}
with

,

and

; with these hypotheses the function

is shown to be obtained as the solution of

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta\delta(x)&=1,&&x\in\Omega,\\
\delta(x)&=0,&&x\in\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.6)

In some scenarios, there are simpler ways of obtaining a suitable function

Generic regularity of the solution to the fractional Dirichlet problem.

Using super-solution arguments, it was shown (Lemma 2.6

We also mention

1.3
Comments on related literature

We briefly review previous finite element methods proposed for problem (
1.1
). The main challenges associated with this problem stem from the limited regularity of its solution and the singularity of the kernel in (
1.2
). Several recent works have addressed these issues, aiming to achieve both fast convergence and computational efficiency.

From a theoretical perspective, Acosta and Borthagaray first established in
[
5
]
that for a Lipschitz domain
\Omega
, the piece-wise linear finite element approximation
u_{h}
of the weak solution
u
to problem (
1.1
) satisfies the following estimate (see Theorem 4.6 therein):

\displaystyle\|u-u_{h}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\begin{cases}C\,h^{1/2}\lvert\log h\rvert\,\|f\|_{C^{\frac{1}{2}-s}(\overline{\Omega})},&\text{if }s\in(0,1/2),\\[4.0pt]

Therefore, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\delta(x)^{s}-\delta(y)^{s}|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\min\{\delta(x),\delta(y)\}^{s-1}|x-y|^{-d-s+1}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=2C\int_{\Omega}d_{\Omega}(x)^{s-1}\int_{\{y\in\Omega:d_{\Omega}(x)<d_{\Omega}(y)\}}|x-y|^{-d-s+1}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x.

[u-u_{h}]_{H^{s}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})}=\inf_{v_{h}\in V_{h}}[u-v_{h}]_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq[u-J_{h}u]_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

and hence the desired error estimate (
1.13
).
∎

Proof of
Corollary

1.10
.

Let us first notice that, by Proposition 1.1 in
[
52
]
we have
u\in C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

\|u\|_{W^{s,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{\Omega,s}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},

(2.9)

where
C_{\Omega,s}
is a positive constant depending only on the domain
\Omega
and
s
.
Applying the results of
[
2
]
, summarised in (
1.9
), we have
u/\delta^{s}\in W^{\gamma,\infty}(\Omega)
with the inequality

\|u/\delta^{s}\|_{W^{\gamma,\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\Omega,d,s,\gamma}(\|f\|_{W^{\gamma-s,\infty}(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}),

for a positive constant
C_{\Omega,d,s,\gamma}
depending only on
\Omega,d,s
and
\gamma
. Inequality (
2.9
) therefore implies

\|u/\delta^{s}\|_{W^{\gamma,\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\Omega,d,s,\gamma}\|f\|_{W^{\gamma-s,\infty}(\Omega)}.

(2.10)

We can hence apply
Theorem

1.9
with
\mu=\min\{\gamma,2\}
, combining (
1.14
) together with (
2.10
) to get the final result (
1.14
).
∎

2.2
Proof of convergence rates in
L^{2}

The standard approach to translate this
H^{s}
-rate to an improved
L^{p}
-rate is to use an Aubin-Nitsche duality argument.
The idea is to consider
e_{h}=u-u_{h}
and note that for any
w_{h}\in V_{h}
we have that

a(e_{h},w_{h})=a(u,w_{h})-a(u_{h},w_{h})=\int_{\Omega}f(x)w_{h}(x)\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}f(x)w_{h}(x)\mathrm{d}x=0.

Thus, given any
\phi_{h}
, we consider the problem

(-\Delta)^{s}\psi_{h}=\phi_{h}\text{ in }\Omega,\qquad\psi_{h}=0\text{ in }{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\setminus\Omega,

(2.11)

and note that for any
w_{h}\in V_{h}
, using the weak formulation of (
2.11
), we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}e_{h}(x)\phi_{h}(x)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=a(e_{h},\psi_{h})=a(e_{h},\psi_{h}-w_{h})\leq[e_{h}]_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}[\psi_{h}-w_{h}]_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Therefore, we get the following result

\int_{\Omega}e_{h}(x)\phi_{h}(x)\mathrm{d}x\leq[e_{h}]_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\inf_{w_{h}\in V_{h}}[\psi_{h}-w_{h}]_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(2.12)

Due to Céa’s lemma (
Lemma

2.2
) the infimum on the right-hand side is attained at the numerical solution of (
2.11
).

Remark 2.6
.

An interesting open question is whether
Corollary

1.10
could be improved for
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
to a result analogous to the classical case
s=1
, i.e.,

[u-u_{h}]_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq Ch^{s}\lvert\log h\rvert^{\kappa}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

In the spirit of
Theorem

1.9
, this would mean that
u/\delta^{s}
has “
2s
derivatives”.
If this was the case we could prove, using
\phi_{h}=e_{h}
and (
2.12
), an improved
L^{2}
-error estimate of order
h^{2}
, which is consistent with the rates suggested by the numerical experiments below.

Proof of
Theorem

1.11
.

Let
e_{h}=u-u_{h}
. Using the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}
, let us consider

\phi_{h}\coloneqq\mathcal{F}^{-1}\!\big[(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-t}\,\mathcal{F}[e_{h}]\big].

This corresponds to applying the operator
(I-\Delta)^{-t}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to
e_{h}
. Let us also consider the following equivalent norm in
H^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for any
r\in\mathbb{R}
, given by

\left|\!\left|\!\left|v\right|\!\right|\!\right|_{H^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\coloneqq\|(1+|\xi|^{2})^{r/2}\,\mathcal{F}[v]\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

We deduce, by Plancherel’s theorem, that
\phi_{h}\in H^{s+2t}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\left|\!\left|\!\left|\phi_{h}\right|\!\right|\!\right|_{H^{s+2t}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\left|\!\left|\!\left|e_{h}\right|\!\right|\!\right|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
. Thus, by
Morrey’s inequality, we have that
\phi_{h}\in W^{s+2t-\frac{d}{2},\infty}(\Omega)

provided that
s+2t-\tfrac{d}{2}>0
. In particular

\|\phi_{h}\|_{W^{s+2t-\frac{d}{2},\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\left|\!\left|\!\left|\phi_{h}\right|\!\right|\!\right|_{H^{s+2t}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=C\left|\!\left|\!\left|e_{h}\right|\!\right|\!\right|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad\textup{for all }t>\frac{d-2s}{4}.

Let us now consider WFEM solution corresponding to (
2.11
), that is

\text{Find }\Psi_{h}\in V_{h}\text{ such that }a(\Psi_{h},v_{h})=\int_{\Omega}\phi_{h}(x)v_{h}(x)\mathrm{d}x\text{ for all }v_{h}\in V_{h}.

We use now
Corollary

1.10
with
\overline{\gamma}=2s+2t-\frac{d}{2}
, provided
t
is such that
\overline{\gamma},\overline{\gamma}\pm s\not\in\mathbb{N}
, and
0<\overline{\gamma}-s<2-s
to get

\|\psi_{h}-\Psi_{h}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq Ch^{\overline{\gamma}-s}\lvert\log h\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\phi_{h}\|_{W^{\overline{\gamma}-s,\infty}}\leq Ch^{\overline{\gamma}-s}\lvert\log h\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\|e_{h}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

where have used that
\overline{\gamma}-s=s+2t-\frac{d}{2}
. Note that we have used here the hypothesis
\partial\Omega\in C^{2,1}
.
On the other hand, we can use Parceval’s identity and proceed as in (
2.12
) to conclude that

\displaystyle\left|\!\left|\!\left|e_{h}\right|\!\right|\!\right|_{H^{-t}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}e_{h}(x)\phi_{h}(x)\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}e_{h}(x)\phi_{h}(x)\mathrm{d}x=a(e_{h},\psi_{h})=a(e_{h},\psi_{h}-\Psi_{h})

\displaystyle\leq\|e_{h}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\psi_{h}-\Psi_{h}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{\overline{\gamma}-s}\lvert\log h\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\|e_{h}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

Finally, let us note that with our chosen norms we have the following interpolation inequality if
-t\theta+s(1-\theta)=0
(i.e.
\theta=\frac{s}{s+t}
):

\displaystyle\|e_{h}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\!\left|\!\left|e_{h}\right|\!\right|\!\right|_{H^{-t}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})}^{\theta}\left|\!\left|\!\left|e_{h}\right|\!\right|\!\right|_{H^{s}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})}^{1-\theta}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{\frac{\theta}{2}(\overline{\gamma}-s)}\lvert\log h\rvert^{\frac{\theta}{4}}\|e_{h}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Summarizing, we have obtained that

\|e_{h}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})}\leq Ch^{\frac{1}{2}\frac{s}{s+t}(\overline{\gamma}-s)}\lvert\log h\rvert^{\frac{1}{4}\frac{s}{s+t}}\|e_{h}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

with
\overline{\gamma}=2s+2t-\frac{d}{2}
provided
t
is such that
\overline{\gamma},\overline{\gamma}\pm s\not\in\mathbb{N}
, and
0<\overline{\gamma}-s<2-s
. Note that, in particular, we have

\|e_{h}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})}\leq Ch^{\frac{1}{2}\frac{s}{s+t}(s+2t-\frac{d}{2})}\lvert\log h\rvert^{\frac{1}{4}\frac{s}{s+t}}\|e_{h}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

for almost every
t\in\left(\frac{d-2s}{4},\frac{d-2s+2}{4}\right)
. This implies

\|e_{h}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})}\leq Ch^{\alpha}\lvert\log h\rvert^{\frac{1}{4}}\|e_{h}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

for almost every
\alpha\in\left(0,s\frac{4-2s}{4+d}\right)
. Applying the estimate in
Corollary

1.10
we get the result.
∎

3
Extensions and open problems

In this section, we discuss several natural extensions of this work and highlight open questions whose resolution could improve the results presented in this paper.

Parabolic problems.

Some authors have studied higher regularity of
u
and
u/\delta^{s}
to the parabolic problem
\partial_{t}u+(-\Delta)^{s}u=f
see
[
32
,
34
]
.
Our approach has a standard generalisation to this context, see e.g.,
[
48
]
.

Higher-order basis.

Since the regularity of
u/\delta^{s}
can be arbitrarily high (see (
1.9
))
a possibility in these case would be to use higher order elements to achieve higher order convergence rates. A close inspection to the proofs reveals that for a basis of elements of order
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the order of convergence in the
s
-Sobolev norm can be
h^{k+1-s}
.

Generalised fractional Laplacians.

The approach of this paper relies on the observation that solutions of (
1.1
) satisfy that the quotient
u/\delta^{s}
is highly regular. This feature also appears in problems where the fractional Laplacian is replaced by more general nonlocal operators of the form

L\phi(y)=\textup{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(\phi(x)-\phi(y)\big)K(x-y)\,\mathrm{d}x,

under suitable structural conditions on the kernel
K
(see
[
2
,
50
,
51
,
49
]
). Consequently, weighted finite element methods, such as the one proposed here, are naturally extendable to this broader class of operators.

Singular kernels and related operators

Another interesting family of problems is

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)+V(x)u(x)=f(x),

where the singularity of the potential
V
at the boundary may alter the boundary behaviour of
u
.

On the one hand, for
s>1/2
if we consider the so-called “killing” potential

\kappa(x)=C_{d,s}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\setminus\Omega}|x-y|^{-d-2s}\mathrm{d}y\asymp d_{\Omega}(x)^{-2s}

then
V(x)=-\kappa(x)
gives the so-called Censored Fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)_{\textup{CFL}}^{s}\coloneq(-\Delta)^{s}+V
, which can equivalently be written as

(-\Delta)_{\textup{CFL}}^{s}u(x)=C_{d,s}\textup{P.V.}\int_{\Omega}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\mathrm{d}y.

In this setting it is known that for
f
non-negative and bounded we have
u\asymp d_{\Omega}^{2s-1}
. The theory of regularity for
u/d_{\Omega}^{2s-1}
is available in
[
29
]
,
and the natural weighted basis for a finite elements theory like the one presented in this paper is
\delta^{2s-1}\mathrm{PL}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
.

On the other hand, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,s)
we can find
C(\varepsilon)>0
such that, if
V\geq C(\varepsilon)d_{\Omega}^{-2s}
, then
|u|\leq Cd_{\Omega}^{s+\varepsilon}
near
\partial\Omega
(see
[
27
]
).
In this setting it would be interesting to see whether using the suitably weighted basis gives any improvements.

Semi-linear problems.

Another interesting family of problems is
(-\Delta)^{s}u=f(u)
.
Depending on the behaviour of
f
at
u=0
we have different behaviour at the boundary (see
[
1
]
).

Fractional
p
-Laplacian.

The scheme proposed in this paper naturally extends to problems involving the fractional
p
-Laplacian,

(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}u(x)=C_{d,s,p}\,\operatorname{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\mathrm{d}y.

Nevertheless, convergence rates cannot be established due to the absence of higher-order regularity results for
u/\delta^{s}
comparable to those available for the case
p=2
.
Several authors have addressed regularity in this setting (see, e.g.,
[
14
,
15
]
and
[
40
]
for a survey).

C^{\alpha}
regularity for
u/\delta^{s}
can be found in
[
42
,
41
]
.

C^{1,\alpha}
regularity for local solutions
u
with
f=0
has appeared as a preprint

[
37
]
.
We also note that a finite elements method with piece-wise linear basis was studied in
[
12
,
47
]
.

Improved regularity gains.

An interesting open question is whether the regularity gain in (
1.9
) can be improved from
s
to
2s
derivatives. Such an improvement would yield the optimal
H^{s}
-convergence rate of order
2-s
(see
Corollary

1.10
) under relaxed regularity requirements on the right-hand side
f
.

Another open question, discussed in
Remark

2.6
, concerns the regularity of
u/\delta^{s}
in the
H^{\sigma}
scale for data
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Note that, in this setting, the standard interpolant
I_{h}
cannot be employed in high dimensions, and quasi-interpolants must be used instead.

Less regular domains.

Throughout the paper we work with domains
\Omega
that are, at least,
C^{1,1}
. This regularity is used to apply the Hölder regularity theory of
u/\delta^{s}
, to prove
\delta^{s}\in V
(in particular to show
\delta^{s-1}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
), and for the existence suitable extension operator in
W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
.
The latter technical facts probably hold under less regularity of
\Omega
, and hence
Theorem

1.9
can be sharpened. However, to weaken the regularity assumption of
\Omega
in
Corollary

1.10
would require improving the regularity theory of
u/\delta^{s}
.

Céa’s lemma in Hölder spaces.

The results in this paper rely on
C^{\alpha}
regularity estimates to derive
H^{s}
-type convergence rates. This approach appears to be suboptimal, as it requires passing from
C^{\alpha}
to
H^{\alpha-\varepsilon}
(and vice versa via Morrey’s embedding) incurring a loss of regularity.

It would be of interest to establish a Céa-type lemma in Hölder spaces
C^{s}(\Omega)
. Such a result would provide a natural framework for
C^{s}
and
L^{\infty}
-error estimates. This kind of result is known in the classical case
s=1
(see
[
28
]
).

Functional inequalities

Lemma A.1
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
\alpha\in(s,1]
. Let
v\in W^{\alpha,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\operatorname{supp}v\subset B_{R}
for some
R
. Then
v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

[v]_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{d,s,R}\left(\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha-s}}[v]_{W^{\alpha,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right).

Proof.

We will use the same name for different constants, just keep tracking of their dependence.
To short the notation, let us write
g(x,y)=\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}
. Then,

\displaystyle[v]_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\iint\limits_{B_{R}\times B_{R}}g(x,y)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y+2\iint\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}B_{R}\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{R})\\
|x-y|\leq 1\end{subarray}}g(x,y)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y+2\iint\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}B_{R}\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{R})\\
|x-y|>1\end{subarray}}g(x,y)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\coloneq I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

Let us estimate each term above. First,

\displaystyle I_{1}=[v]_{W^{\alpha,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\iint\limits_{B_{R}\times B_{R}}\frac{|x-y|^{2\alpha}}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\leq C_{d,R}[v]_{W^{\alpha,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\int_{0}^{2R}r^{2\alpha-1-2sdr}\leq\frac{C_{d,R}}{\alpha-s}[v]_{W^{\alpha,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

Similarly, we have that

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq 2\iint\limits_{B_{R+1}\times B_{R+1}}g(x,y)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\leq\frac{C_{d,R}}{\alpha-s}[v]_{W^{\alpha,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

Finally, we deduce

\displaystyle I_{3}\leq 4\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\int_{B_{R}}\int_{|x-y|>1}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y=C_{d,s,R}\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

The proof is completed by observing that
(a^{2}+b^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq|a|^{\frac{1}{2}}+|b|^{\frac{1}{2}}
for all
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
.
∎

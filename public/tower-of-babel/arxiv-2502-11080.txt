Boundedness of toric foliations
Chih-Wei Chang, Yen-An Chen

Abstract
. We discuss boundedness of toric Fano foliations and connectedness of its dicritical and singular loci. Moreover, we show the set of interpolated δ \delta -lcts for the toric foliations satisfies the descending chain condition.



1.
Introduction

In recent years, significant advancements have been made in the minimal model program for foliations, allowing for the extension of results from classical birational geometry to the realm of the foliations, particularly within the framework of the minimal model program.
Unlike the classical approach, which utilizes the canonical divisor
K_{X}
of the ambient variety, the theory of foliations employs the canonical divisor
K_{\mathcal{F}}
associated with the foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
to investigate its geometric properties for both ambient variety and the foliation itself.
This method constitutes a generalization, as when
{\mathcal{F}}={\mathcal{T}}_{X}
, it reverts the theory of foliations to the classical context.

Motivated by the Borisov-Alexeev-Borisov Conjecture, which is proved by Birkar, we propose the following question regarding the similar phenomena within the context of foliations:

Question 1.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a Fano foliation with mild singularities on a normal projective variety
X
.
Is the set
\{(X,{\mathcal{F}})\}
bounded?

[
CJV24
, Example 3.9]
provides a negative answer when both
X
and
{\mathcal{F}}
have at worst log canonical singularities.
Also, Fano foliations of rank one on surfaces must contain a dicritical point, which is log canonical or worse.
Consequently, the condition of having
\varepsilon
-log canonical singularities will render the set empty.

However, inspired by the works of
[
PS19
]
,
[
Che21
]
,
[
SS23
]
, and
[
CHL+24
]
, it is natural to propose the following modified question:

Question 1.2
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{F}},\Delta,t)
be an adjoint foliated structure with at worst
\delta
-log canonical singularities for
\delta\in(0,1]
.
Suppose
-K_{(X,{\mathcal{F}},\Delta,t)}
is ample.
Is the set
\{(X,{\mathcal{F}})\}
bounded?

We show it holds when
(X,{\mathcal{F}},\Delta,t)
is toric:

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
4.5
)
.

Fix a positive integer
n
, a non-negative integer
r\leq n
, a positive real number
\delta
, and two real numbers
t_{i}\in[0,1)
for
i=1
,
2
.
Let
{\mathcal{F}}={\mathcal{F}}_{W}
be a toric foliation of rank
r
on a complete toric variety
X=X_{\Sigma}
of dimension
n
,
\Delta
be an effective torus-invariant divisor on
X_{\Sigma}
, and
K_{t_{i}}=t_{i}K_{\mathcal{F}}+(1-t_{i})K_{X}+\Delta
for
i=1
,
2
.
Suppose
-K_{t_{1}}
is ample and the adjoint foliated structure
(X,{\mathcal{F}},\Delta,t_{2})
is
\delta
-lc.
Then we have the following:

(1)

There is an effective divisor

such that

is

-Cartier.

(2)

We then study the dicritical and singular loci for Fano foliations.

Proposition 1.4

(Proposition

)

Let

(Theorem

2.
Preliminaries

We work over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.

2.1.
Foliations

In this subsection, most of the definitions follow from
[
CS21
]
and
[
Dru21
]
.
Let
X
be a normal variety of dimension
n
.
A
foliation
is a coherent subsheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
of the tangent sheaf
{\mathcal{T}}_{X}
such that

(1)

{\mathcal{F}}
is saturated, that is
{\mathcal{T}}_{X}/{\mathcal{F}}
is torsion-free, and

(2)

{\mathcal{F}}
is closed under the Lie bracket.

Let
r=\operatorname{rank}({\mathcal{F}})
be the
rank
of the foliation and
c=n-r
be the
corank
of the foliation.
The
canonical divisor

K_{\mathcal{F}}
is a Weil divisor on
X
such that
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-K_{\mathcal{F}})\cong\det{\mathcal{F}}
.

Let
\pi\colon Y\dashrightarrow X
be a dominant rational map between normal varieties and
{\mathcal{F}}
be a foliation on
X
.
We denote by
\pi^{-1}{\mathcal{F}}
the
pullback foliation
on
Y
(see, for example,
[
Dru21
, Section 3.2]
).
If
f\colon X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
is birational, then
f_{*}\mathcal{F}
represents the transformed foliation on
X^{\prime}
induced by
f^{-1}
.

Let
X^{\circ}
be the open subset of
X
such that
{\mathcal{F}}|_{X^{\circ}}
is a subbundle of
T_{X^{\circ}}
.
A
leaf

L
is a maximal connected and immersed holomorphic submanifold
L\subseteq X^{\circ}
such that
T_{L}={\mathcal{F}}|_{L}
.

A foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
is called
algebraically integrable
if its leaves are algebraic.
Equivalently, an algebraically integrable foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
on
X
is induced from a dominant rational map
f\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
for some normal variety
Y
(see, for example,
[
Dru21
, Sections 3.2 and 3.6]
).

Definition 2.1

(Singular locus)
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a foliation of rank
r
on a normal variety
X
.
We obtain a morphism
\phi\colon(\Omega_{X}^{r})^{**}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(K_{\mathcal{F}})
by taking the double dual of the
r
-th wedge product of
\Omega_{X}^{**}\to{\mathcal{F}}^{*}
, which is induced by the inclusion
{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}_{X}
.
We define the
singular locus
of
{\mathcal{F}}
, denoted by
\operatorname{Sing}({\mathcal{F}})
, as the co-support of the image of
\phi^{\prime}\colon(\Omega_{X}^{r}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-K_{\mathcal{F}}))^{**}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
.

Definition 2.2

(Invariance)
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a foliation of rank
r
on a normal variety
X
.

(1)

We say that a subvariety
S\subseteq X
is
{\mathcal{F}}
-
invariant
if for any subset
U\subseteq X
and any section
\partial\in H^{0}(U,{\mathcal{F}})
, we have

\partial({\mathcal{I}}_{S\cap U})\subseteq{\mathcal{I}}_{S\cap U}
where
{\mathcal{I}}_{S\cap U}
is the ideal sheaf of
S\cap U
.

(2)

For any prime divisor
D\subseteq Y\xrightarrow{\pi}X
over
X
where
\pi
is a birational morphism, we define
\iota(D)=0
if
D
is
\pi^{-1}{\mathcal{F}}
-invariant and
\iota(D)=1
if
D
is non-
\pi^{-1}\mathcal{F}
-invariant.
Note that
\iota(D)
is independent of the choice of the birational morphism
\pi
that extracts
D
.

Definition 2.3

(Dicriticality,
[
CC23
, Definition 4.6]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be foliation of rank
r
on a normal variety
X
of dimension
n
.
We say
{\mathcal{F}}
is
dicritical
if there is an exceptional divisor
E
over
X
which is not foliation invariant and whose center on
X
has dimension at most
n-r-1
.

We say
{\mathcal{F}}
is
non-dicritical
if it is not dicritical.

2.2.
Toric varieties

In this paper, every toric variety is assumed to be normal. Our notations closely follow
[
CLS11
]
.
We recall some notations that are frequently used in this paper.

Let
N\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
be a lattice of rank
n
and
M:=\operatorname{Hom}(N,\mathbb{Z})
be its dual lattice. We write
N_{\mathbb{R}}:=N\otimes\mathbb{R}
and
N_{\mathbb{C}}:=N\otimes\mathbb{C}
.
A
fan

\Sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
is a finite collection of rational, strongly convex, polyhedral cones
\sigma\subseteq N_{\mathbb{R}}
, such that each face
\tau
of a cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
belongs to
\Sigma
and the intersection of any two cones in
\Sigma
is a face of each.
The
support
of
\Sigma
is defined as
|\Sigma|=\bigcup_{\tau\in\Sigma}\tau
.
For any
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
, denote the set of all
k
-dimensional cones in
\Sigma
by
\Sigma(k)
, and denote the set of all
k
-dimensional faces of
\sigma\in\Sigma
by
\sigma(k)
.
We write
\tau\preceq\sigma
when
\tau
is a face of
\sigma
.

For each cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
, the affine toric variety associated with
\sigma
is
U_{\sigma,N}=\operatorname{Spec}{\mathbb{C}}[\sigma^{\vee}\cap M]=\operatorname{Spec}{\mathbb{C}}[\chi^{m}\mid m\in\sigma^{\vee}\cap M]
where
\sigma^{\vee}
is the dual cone of
\sigma
.
A cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
is said to be
smooth with respect to
N
if the primitive generators of the rays in
\sigma(1)
form part of a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis for
N
(or equivalently,
U_{\sigma,N}
is smooth).
If
\tau\preceq\sigma
are two cones in
\Sigma
, there is an open immersion
U_{\tau,N}\hookrightarrow U_{\sigma,N}
.
The toric variety
X_{\Sigma,N}
of the fan
\Sigma
is constructed by gluing all
U_{\sigma,N}
together via
\Sigma
.
The dense torus
U_{\{0\},N}=\operatorname{Spec}{\mathbb{C}}[M]\subseteq X_{\Sigma,N}
is denoted by
T_{N}
.
The action of
T_{N}
on itself can be extended to an action on
X_{\Sigma,N}
.
We will omit
N
in the subscript when
N
is clear.

For each
\sigma\in\Sigma
,
\operatorname{Relint}(\sigma)
denotes the relative interior of
\sigma
,
O_{\sigma}
denotes the
T
-orbit of the distinguished point
x_{\sigma}
, and
V_{\sigma}
denotes the closure of

in
X_{\Sigma}
(see
[
CLS11
, Chapter 3]
for further details).
If

is a ray, then

is a divisor and will also be denoted by

.

Lemma-Definition 2.4
.

Let
D=\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}a_{\rho}D_{\rho}
be a
{\mathbb{R}}
-Cartier torus-invariant divisor on

where

for all

.
Then there exists a function

of

When

is Cartier, it is
[

.
In general, we can write

are Cartier torus-invariant divisors and

.
Let
\phi_{i}\colon|\Sigma|\to{\mathbb{R}}

.
Then we define

It is clear that

\phi_{D}(v_{\rho})=-a_{\rho}

Note that such function

2.3.
Toric foliations

We recall the definition of toric foliations and their properties, for more details, see
[
CC23
]
.
Let
X=X_{\Sigma}
be the toric variety defined by a fan
\Sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
.
A subsheaf
{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}_{X}
is called

-

or

if for any

t^{*}\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}
as subsheaves under the natural isomorphism
t^{*}{\mathcal{T}}_{X}\simeq{\mathcal{T}}_{X}
.
A foliation
{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}_{X}
is called a
toric foliation
if
{\mathcal{F}}
is
T
-invariant.

We recall the following description and properties for toric foliations:

Proposition 2.6

(
[
CC23
, Proposition 3.1]
or
[
Pan15
, Lemma 2.1.5]
)
.

Let
\Sigma
be a fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
and
X_{\Sigma,N}
be the toric variety defined by
\Sigma
.
Then there is a one-to-one correspondence between the set of toric foliations on
X_{\Sigma,N}
and the set of complex vector subspaces
W\subseteq N_{\mathbb{C}}
.

We will use
{\mathcal{F}}_{W,\Sigma,N}
to denote the toric foliation on
X_{\Sigma,N}
corresponding to the complex vector subspace

.
If we have another fan
\Sigma^{\prime}
in the same

, the transformed foliation on

is nothing but

to denote the transformed foliation on any birational model obtained by modifying the defining fan.

(
[

)

Let

be a toric foliation on a toric variety

.
For any

(

3.
Adjoint foliated structure

As
[
CJV24
, Example 3.9]
provides an unbounded example for the Fano foliations when both
X
and
{\mathcal{F}}
have at worst log canonical singularities.
Then we would like to impose
\varepsilon
-log canonicity on foliation singularities.
However, when
{\mathcal{F}}
is algebraically integrable or toric, the set consisting of
\varepsilon
-log canonical Fano foliations is empty.
It is therefore natural to consider the adjoint foliated structure.

Definition 3.1

(
[
CHL+24
]
)
.

We say
(X,{\mathcal{F}},\Delta,t)
is an
adjoint foliated structure
if the following conditions hold:

(1)

X
is a normal projective variety,

(2)

{\mathcal{F}}
is a foliation on
X
,

(3)

\Delta
is an effective
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisor,

(4)

t\in[0,1]
, and

(5)

the adjoint canonical divisor
K_{(X,{\mathcal{F}},\Delta,t)}=tK_{\mathcal{F}}+(1-t)K_{X}+\Delta
is
{\mathbb{R}}
-Cartier.

If
\Delta=0
, then we will denote the foliated structure
(X,{\mathcal{F}},0,t)
as the triple
(X,{\mathcal{F}},t)
.

Let
\pi\colon X^{\prime}\to X
be a birational morphism and
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}=\pi^{-1}{\mathcal{F}}
.
We define
\Delta^{\prime}
as the unique
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisor such that

K_{(X^{\prime},{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime},t)}=\pi^{*}K_{(X,{\mathcal{F}},\Delta,t)}.

For any prime divisor
E
on
X^{\prime}
, we define the
adjoint discrepancy
to be
-\operatorname{mult}_{E}\Delta^{\prime}
and the
adjoint log discrepancy
to be
a(E,{\mathcal{F}},\Delta,t)=-\operatorname{mult}_{E}\Delta^{\prime}+t\iota(E)+(1-t)
.
For
\delta\in[0,1]
, we say
(X,{\mathcal{F}},\Delta,t)
is
\delta
-log canonical
or
\delta
-lc
if
a(E,{\mathcal{F}},\Delta,t)\geq(t\iota(E)+(1-t))\delta
for any divisor
E
over
X
.

Remark 3.2
.

For
t\in(0,1]
, we note that
(X,{\mathcal{F}},\Delta,t)
is
\delta
-lc if and only
(X,{\mathcal{F}},B)
is
(\varepsilon=\frac{1-t}{t},\delta)
-adjoint lc in the sense of
[
SS23
, Definition 2.12]
where
B=\Delta_{\textnormal{n-inv}}+\frac{1}{1-t}\Delta_{\textnormal{inv}}
for
t\neq 1
and
B=\Delta_{\textnormal{n-inv}}
for
t=1
.

The following Proposition provides an evidence that the definition of adjoint log discrepancy is canonical:

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}={\mathcal{F}}_{W}
be a toric foliation on a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial toric variery
X=X_{\Sigma}
, where
\Sigma
is a fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
and
W\subset N_{\mathbb{C}}
is a linear subspace.
Let
\Delta
be a torus-invariant divisor on
X
and
\Sigma^{\prime}
be a refinement of
\Sigma
and
\pi\colon X_{\Sigma^{\prime}}\to X
be the corresponding toric birational morphism.
Then for any
\rho\in\Sigma^{\prime}(1)\setminus\Sigma(1)
, the adjoint log discrepancy
a(D_{\rho},{\mathcal{F}}_{W},\Delta,t)=\phi_{K_{(X,{\mathcal{F}}_{W},\Delta,t)}}(v_{\rho})
where
v_{\rho}
is the primitive generator of
\rho
.

Proof.

Let
\Delta^{\prime}
be an
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisor on
X^{\prime}
such that
K_{(X^{\prime},{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime},t)}=\pi^{*}K_{(X,{\mathcal{F}},\Delta,t)}
where
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}=\pi^{-1}{\mathcal{F}}
.
Note that
\operatorname{mult}_{D_{\rho}}K_{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}}=\iota(D_{\rho})
and
\operatorname{mult}_{D_{\rho}}K_{X^{\prime}}=1
.
Then

\displaystyle a(D_{\rho},{\mathcal{F}}_{W},\Delta,t)

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{mult}_{D_{\rho}}\Delta^{\prime}+t\iota(D_{\rho})+(1-t)

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{mult}_{D_{\rho}}(\pi^{*}K_{(X,{\mathcal{F}},\Delta,t)}-tK_{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}}-(1-t)K_{X^{\prime}})+t\iota(D_{\rho})+(1-t)

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{mult}_{D_{\rho}}(\pi^{*}K_{(X,{\mathcal{F}},\Delta,t)})

\displaystyle=\phi_{(K_{X},{\mathcal{F}},\Delta,t)}(v_{\rho}).

∎

The following lemma gives a combinatorial criterion of
\delta

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

, where

Then the adjoint foliated structure

\delta

\phi_{K_{(X,{\mathcal{F}},\Delta,t)}}(v)\geq\begin{cases}(1-t)\delta&\mbox{if }v\notin W\\
\delta&\mbox{if }v\in W\end{cases}

for all primitive elements

.

Proof.

This is a special case of Lemma
A.2
.

Even if we want to give a direct proof for the toric case, it is inevitable to consider the toroidal case in the appendix as we need to check the adjoint log discrepancies for all exceptional divisors, no matter whether they are torus-invariant or not.

Boundedness of Fano ajoint foliated structures

In this section, we will prove the main Theorem
4.5
.
Before we state and prove the main Theorem, we provide some examples to demonstrate some properties of Fano foliations.

Example 4.1

(Fano foliations which are not algebraically integrable)
.

Let
N={\mathbb{Z}}e_{1}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}e_{2}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}e_{3}

\Sigma

Example 4.2

(Fano foliations on non-Fano smooth varieties)
.

Let

,
u_{1}=(0,s,1)
,
u_{2}=(0,s,-1)
,
u_{3}=(-1,1,0)

u_{4}=(1,0,0)

Let
\rho_{i}={\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}u_{i}

W=yz

\Sigma(3)=\{\sigma_{134},\sigma_{234},\sigma_{135},\sigma_{145},\sigma_{235},\sigma_{245}\}

\sigma_{ijk}=\operatorname{Cone}(u_{i},u_{j},u_{k})

Note that there are only two primitive collections,
\{\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\}
and
\{\rho_{3},\rho_{4},\rho_{5}\}
.

Let
\phi_{\mathcal{F}}:=\phi_{-K_{\mathcal{F}}}
and
\phi_{X}:=\phi_{-K_{X}}
.
Note that
\varphi_{X}(u_{i})=-1
for all

\varphi_{\mathcal{F}}(u_{1})=\varphi_{\mathcal{F}}(u_{2})=\varphi_{\mathcal{F}}(u_{5})=-1\mbox{ and }\varphi_{\mathcal{F}}(u_{3})=\varphi_{\mathcal{F}}(u_{4})=0.

•

The following lemma shows that there is no
\delta
-lc Fano toric foliations:

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}:={\mathcal{F}}_{W}
be a Fano toric foliation on a complete
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
.
If
{\mathcal{F}}\neq{\mathcal{T}}_{X}
, then there is a non-foliation-invariant exceptional divisor
E
with foliated log discrepancy zero.

Proof.

Let
W^{\prime}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}\{\rho\in\Sigma(1)\mid\rho\subseteq W\}
and
\phi\colon N_{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be the support function of
-K_{\mathcal{F}}
.
Note that
\phi(u)\leq 0
for all
u\in N_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Moreover,
\phi
is strictly convex with respect to
\Sigma
as
-K_{\mathcal{F}}
is ample.

Since
{\mathcal{F}}_{W}
is Fano, we have
{\mathcal{F}}_{W}\neq 0
and thus there is a ray
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
such that
\rho\subseteq W
.
Hence
r^{\prime}:=\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}W^{\prime}\geq 1
.
As
{\mathcal{F}}\neq{\mathcal{T}}_{X}
, we have
r^{\prime}\leq\dim_{\mathbb{C}}W<n
.
Let
\pi\colon N_{\mathbb{Q}}\to N_{\mathbb{Q}}/W^{\prime}
be the canonical linear projection.
Note that
|\Sigma|=N_{\mathbb{R}}
as
X_{\Sigma}
is complete.
Since
r^{\prime}<n
, we have
\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}N_{\mathbb{Q}}/W^{\prime}=n-r^{\prime}\geq 1
and thus the set

S:=\{\rho\in\Sigma(1)\mid\pi(v_{\rho})\neq 0\}=\{\rho\in\Sigma(1)\mid\rho\nsubseteq W\}

is not empty.
Then
N_{\mathbb{Q}}/W^{\prime}=\sum_{\rho\in S}{\mathbb{Q}}_{\geq 0}\pi(v_{\rho})
where
v_{\rho}
is the primitive generator of
\rho
.
Hence
\#S\geq\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}N_{\mathbb{Q}}/W^{\prime}+1\geq 2
.
Therefore, there are
a_{\rho}\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{>0}
for
\rho\in S
such that
\sum_{\rho\in S}a_{\rho}\pi(v_{\rho})=0
.
Let
v_{0}:=\sum_{\rho\in S}a_{\rho}v_{\rho}\in N_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Since
\pi(v_{0})=0
, we have
v_{0}\in W^{\prime}\cap N_{\mathbb{Q}}\subseteq W\cap N_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Moreover, we note that

0\geq\phi(v_{0})=\phi(\sum_{\rho\in S}a_{\rho}v_{\rho})\geq\sum_{\rho\in S}a_{\rho}\phi(v_{\rho})=0

where the first inequality holds since
\phi\leq 0
, the second inequality holds because of the strict convexity of
\phi
, and the last equality follows as
\rho\nsubseteq W
.
Hence, we have
\phi(\sum_{\rho\in S}a_{\rho}v_{\rho})=\sum_{\rho\in S}a_{\rho}\phi(v_{\rho})
and therefore, all
v_{\rho}
, for
\rho\in S
, are in the same maximal cone because
\phi
is strictly convex.
Since all cones in
\Sigma
are strictly convex,
v_{0}=\sum_{\rho\in S}a_{\rho}v_{\rho}\neq 0
and the ray generated by
v_{0}
is not in
\Sigma(1)
.
So the star subdivision along
v_{0}
introduces an exceptional divisor which is not foliation-invariant and has log discrepancy zero.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}_{W}
be a Fano toric foliation on a complete
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
.
If the adjoint foliated structure
(X,{\mathcal{F}},1)
is
\delta
-lc for some
\delta>0
, then
{\mathcal{F}}={\mathcal{T}}_{X}
.

Proof.

Suppose
{\mathcal{F}}\neq{\mathcal{T}}_{X}
, then by Lemma
4.3
, there is a non-foliation-invariant exceptional divisor
E
such that
a(E,{\mathcal{F}})=0
.
Then the adjoint log discrepancy
a(E,X,{\mathcal{F}},1)=a(E,{\mathcal{F}})=0
and thus
(X,{\mathcal{F}},1)
is not
\delta
-lc, which is a contradiction.
Therefore,
{\mathcal{F}}={\mathcal{T}}_{X}
.
∎

Theorem 4.5

(Main Theorem)
.

Fix a positive integer
n
, a non-negative integer
r\leq n
, a positive real number
\delta
, and two real numbers
t_{i}\in[0,1)
for
i=1
,
2
.
Let
{\mathcal{F}}={\mathcal{F}}_{W}
be a toric foliation of rank
r
on a complete toric variety
X=X_{\Sigma}
of dimension
n
,
\Delta
be an effective torus-invariant divisor on
X_{\Sigma}
, and
K_{t_{i}}=t_{i}K_{\mathcal{F}}+(1-t_{i})K_{X}+\Delta
for
i=1
,
2
.
Suppose
-K_{t_{1}}
is ample and the adjoint foliated structure
(X,{\mathcal{F}},\Delta,t_{2})
is
\delta
-lc.
Then we have the following:

(1)

There is an effective divisor
\Delta^{\prime}
such that
K_{X}+\Delta^{\prime}
is
{\mathbb{R}}
-Cartier.

(2)

(X,\Delta^{\prime})
is
(1-t_{2})\delta
-lc. In particular,
X
is potentially klt.

(3)

The number of isomorphism classes of such
X
is finite, which depends only on
t
,
\delta
, and
n
.

(4)

The set consisting of
(X,{\mathcal{F}})
is bounded.

Proof.

(1)

We recall that
K_{X}=-\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}D_{\rho}
.
We write
K_{t_{2}}=-\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}c_{\rho}D_{\rho}
where
c_{\rho}\in[0,1]
.
Let
\Delta^{\prime}=K_{t_{2}}-K_{X}
.
Note that
\Delta^{\prime}
is effective as
1-c_{\rho}\geq 0
.

(2)

Note that
\phi_{K_{X}+\Delta^{\prime}}(v)=\phi_{K}(v)\geq\phi_{K_{t_{2}}}(v)\geq(1-t_{2})\delta
for all
v\in N
and thus
(X,\Delta^{\prime})
is
(1-t_{2})\delta
-lc.

(3)

Let
v_{\rho}
be the primitive generator of
\rho
for
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
.
Let
P=\operatorname{conv}(v_{\rho}\mid\rho\in\Sigma(1))
and
\widetilde{P}=\operatorname{conv}((-\operatorname{mult}_{D_{\rho}}K_{t_{1}})v_{\rho}\mid\rho\in\Sigma(1))\subseteq\operatorname{conv}((1-t_{1})v_{\rho},v_{\rho^{\prime}}\mid\rho\not\subseteq W,\rho^{\prime}\subseteq W)
.

We define
\iota(\rho)=1
if
\rho\subseteq W
and
\iota(\rho)=0
otherwise.

Since
(X,{\mathcal{F}},\Delta,t_{2})
is
\delta
-lc, by Lemma
3.4
, we have

\displaystyle t_{2}\iota(\rho)+(1-t_{2})-\operatorname{mult}_{D_{\rho}}\Delta

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{mult}_{D_{\rho}}K_{t_{2}}

\displaystyle=\phi_{K_{t_{2}}}(v_{\rho})

\displaystyle\geq(1-t_{2})\delta

and thus,
\operatorname{mult}_{D_{\rho}}\Delta\leq t_{2}\iota(\rho)-(1-t_{2})(1-\delta)
.
Hence,

\displaystyle-\operatorname{mult}_{D_{\rho}}K_{t_{1}}

\displaystyle=t_{1}\iota(\rho)+(1-t_{1})-\operatorname{mult}_{D_{\rho}}\Delta

\displaystyle\geq(t_{1}-t_{2})\iota(\rho)+(1-t_{1})+(1-t_{2})\delta

\displaystyle\geq\min\{(1-t_{1})+(1-t_{2})\delta,(1-t_{2})(1+\delta)\}=:\lambda.

Therefore,
\lambda P\subseteq\widetilde{P}
as
0\in P\cup\widetilde{P}
.
For each maximal cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
, we define
P^{\sigma}=\operatorname{conv}(0,(-\operatorname{mult}_{D_{\rho}}K_{t_{1}})v_{\rho}\mid\rho\in\sigma(1))
and thus

\lambda P\cap\sigma\subseteq\widetilde{P}\cap\sigma\subseteq P^{\sigma}

where the second inclusion follows from ampleness of
-K_{t_{1}}
.
Since
(X,\Delta^{\prime})
is
(1-t_{2})\delta
-lc, we have that
\lambda(1-t_{2})\delta P^{\sigma}
contains no lattice point other than the origin for any
\sigma\in\Sigma(n)
.
Thus

\lambda(1-t_{1})(1-t_{2})\delta P\cap\sigma=\lambda(1-t_{2})\delta((1-t_{1})P\cap\sigma)\subseteq\lambda(1-t_{2})\delta P^{\sigma}

contains no lattice point other than the origin for any
\sigma\in\Sigma(n)
.
Hence,
\lambda(1-t_{1})(1-t_{2})\delta P
contains no lattice point other than the origin.
Therefore, by
[
BB92
, Proposition 5]
, there are finitely many
P
up to the action of
\operatorname{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{Z}})
.

Note that
P
contains all primitive generators of
\rho
for
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
.
So there are only finitely many possible
\Sigma(1)
and thus there are only finitely many possible
\Sigma
.

(4)

For each
X
, the toric foliation on
X
is parametrized by
\operatorname{Gr}(r,n)
. Since there are only finitely many such
X
, the set consisting of
(X,{\mathcal{F}})
is bounded.

∎

5.
Dicritical locus

In this section, we study the locus where
{\mathcal{F}}
is dicritical and show that it is connected when
{\mathcal{F}}
is Fano.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a foliation of rank
r
on a normal variety
X
of dimension
n
.
We define the
dicritical locus
,
\operatorname{dicrit}({\mathcal{F}})
to be the union of subvarieties
Z
of dimension at most
n-r-1
such that there is a non-
{\mathcal{G}}
-invariant divisor
E
over
Z
where
{\mathcal{G}}
is the transformed foliation.

Definition 5.2
.

(\tau,W)
is dicritical if
\operatorname{Relint}(\tau)\cap W\cap N\neq\emptyset
and
\tau\nsubseteq W
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\Sigma
be a complete simplicial fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
, and let
\phi\colon N_{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a non-positive function which is strictly convex and piecewise linear with respect to
\Sigma
.
Then there exists a cone
\tau_{0}\in\Sigma
such that
\phi(v)=0
if and only if
v\in\tau_{0}
.

Proof.

Note that if there exists
v\in\operatorname{Relint}(\tau)
for some
\tau\in\Sigma
such that
\phi(v)=0
, then
\phi|_{\tau}=0
by the linearity of
\phi
.
Let
S=\{\tau\in\Sigma:\phi|_{\tau}=0\}
.
Note that
S
is not empty as the zero cone
\{0\}
belongs to
S
.
Then by Zorn’s lemma, we have a cone
\tau_{0}\in\Sigma
, a maximal element in
S
with respect to the inclusion.

Suppose there is an element
v
such that
\phi(v)=0
but
v\notin\tau_{0}
.
Then
v\in\operatorname{Relint}(\sigma)
for some
\sigma\in\Sigma
.
So
\sigma\in S
.
Since
\tau_{0}
is maximal,
\operatorname{Cone}(\sigma,\tau_{0})\notin\Sigma
.
Fix en element
u\in\operatorname{Relint}(\tau_{0})
.
Note that
0\geq\phi(u+v)>\phi(u)+\phi(v)=0
, which is impossible.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}_{W}
be a toric foliation on a toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
of dimension
n
.
Then

\operatorname{dicrit}(\mathcal{F}_{W})=\begin{cases}\bigcup_{\tau\in\Sigma,\,(\tau,W)\ \textnormal{is dicritical}}\mathcal{O}_{\tau}&\mbox{if }\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{F}\geq n-1\\
\bigcup_{\tau\in\Sigma,\,(\tau,W)\ \textnormal{is dicritical}}V_{\tau}&\mbox{if }\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{F}<n-1.\end{cases}

We use the convention that the union over an empty index set is an empty set.

It is clear when
{\mathcal{F}}=0
.
If
\operatorname{rank}{\mathcal{F}}=n
, then
{\mathcal{F}}={\mathcal{T}}_{X}
is non-dicritical and
(\tau,W)
is not dicritical for any
\tau\in\Sigma
.
For the proof when
1\leq\operatorname{rank}{\mathcal{F}}\leq n-1
, it involves the language of the toroidal structures.
So we leave the proof in the appendix.

Using Lemma
5.4
, we show the following properties for Fano toric foliations:

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}_{W}
be a Fano toric foliation on a complete
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
of a fan
\Sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
.

(1)

\operatorname{dicrit}({\mathcal{F}}_{W})
is locally closed and any two irreducible components of
\overline{\operatorname{dicrit}({\mathcal{F}}_{W})}
have non-empty intersection contained in
\operatorname{dicrit}({\mathcal{F}}_{W})
.
In particular,
\operatorname{dicrit}({\mathcal{F}}_{W})
is connected.

(2)

Any two irreducible components of
\operatorname{Sing}({\mathcal{F}}_{W})
intersect. In particular,
\operatorname{Sing}(\mathcal{F}_{W})
is connected.

Proof.

(1)

The local closedness of
\operatorname{dicrit}({\mathcal{F}}_{W})
follows immediately from Lemma
5.4
.

To show any two irreducible components of
\overline{\operatorname{dicrit}({\mathcal{F}}_{W})}
have non-empty intersection contained in
\operatorname{dicrit}({\mathcal{F}}_{W})
, it suffices to show that if
V_{\tau_{1}}
and
V_{\tau_{2}}
are two irreducible components of
\overline{\operatorname{dicrit}({\mathcal{F}}_{W})}
, then there exists
{\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{\tau}}\subseteq\operatorname{dicrit}({\mathcal{F}}_{W})
such that
\tau_{1}
,
\tau_{2}\preceq\widetilde{\tau}
.

Let
\phi=\phi_{-K_{\mathcal{F}_{W}}}
be the support function for
-K_{{\mathcal{F}}_{W}}
and

\tau_{1}
,
\tau_{2}\in\{\tau\in\Sigma\mid(\tau,W)\ \text{is dicritical}\}
be two minimal elements.

Claim
.

W\cap\tau_{i}
does not contain any ray in
\tau_{i}(1)
for
i=1
,
2
.

Proof of Claim.
Suppose not, then there is a ray
\rho_{1}\in\tau_{1}(1)
such that
\rho_{1}\subseteq W
.
Let
\underline{\tau_{1}}=\operatorname{Cone}(\rho\mid\rho\in\tau_{1}(1)\setminus\{\rho_{1}\})
.
Note that
\underline{\tau_{1}}\preceq\tau_{1}
and
(\underline{\tau_{1}},W)
is dicritical.
This contradicts to the minimality of
\tau_{1}
.
So there is no ray
\rho\in\tau_{1}(1)
contained in
W
.
Similarly for
\tau_{2}
.
This completes the proof of the claim.

Thus, we have
\phi|_{\tau_{i}}=0
for
i=1
,
2
.
By Lemma
5.3
, there is a cone
\tau_{0}\in\Sigma
such that
\phi(v)=0
if and only if
v\in\tau_{0}
.
Thus, we have
\tau_{i}\preceq\tau_{0}
for
i=1
,
2
.
Since
X_{\Sigma}
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial, we have
\tau_{0}
is simplicial and thus
\widetilde{\tau}:=\operatorname{Cone}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\in\Sigma
because
\Sigma
is a fan.

Claim
.

(\widetilde{\tau},W)
is dicritical.

Proof of Claim.
As
(\tau_{i},W)
is dicritical, there are
v_{i}\in\operatorname{Relint}(\tau_{i})\cap W\cap N
.
Then
v_{1}+v_{2}\in\operatorname{Relint}(\widetilde{\tau})\cap W\cap N
.
Moreover, we have
\widetilde{\tau}\nsubseteq W
as
\tau_{i}\nsubseteq W
for
i=1
,
2
.
Therefore,
(\widetilde{\tau},W)
is dicritical.
This completes the proof of the claim.
\quad\blacksquare

(2)

Recall
[
CC23
, Proposition 3.9]
that
\operatorname{Sing}({\mathcal{F}}_{W})=\bigcup_{\tau\in\Sigma,\,(\tau,W)\textnormal{ is singular}}V_{\tau}
where
(\tau,W)
is singular if
W\cap{\mathbb{C}}\tau\neq\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}(S)
for any
S\subseteq\tau(1)
.
Let
\tau_{1}
,
\tau_{2}\in\{\tau\in\Sigma\mid(\tau,W)\ \text{is singular}\}
be two minimal elements.

Claim
.

W\cap\tau_{i}
does not contain any ray in
\tau_{i}(1)
for
i=1
,
2
.

Proof of Claim.
We write
\tau_{1}={\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}v_{1}+\cdots+{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}v_{\ell}
where
\ell=\dim\tau_{1}
and each
v_{j}\in N
is primitive.
Since
(\tau_{i},W)
is singular, we have
W\cap({\mathbb{C}}v_{1}+\cdots+{\mathbb{C}}v_{\ell})\neq\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}(S)
for any
S\subseteq\{v_{1}\ldots,v_{\ell}\}
.
Suppose on the contrary that
v_{1}\in W
. Then we have
W\cap(\mathbb{C}v_{2}+\cdots+\mathbb{C}v_{\ell})\neq\operatorname{Span}(S)
for any
S\subseteq\{v_{2}\ldots,v_{\ell}\}
, which means
(\operatorname{Cone}(v_{2},\ldots,v_{\ell}),W)
is singular, contradicting the minimality of
\tau
.
Similarly we have
W\cap\tau_{2}
does not contain any ray in
\tau_{2}(1)
.
This completes the proof of the claim.
\quad\blacksquare

As a result, we have
\phi|_{\tau_{i}}=0
.
By Lemma
5.3
, there is a cone
\tau_{0}\in\Sigma
such that
\phi(v)=0
if and only if
v\in\tau_{0}
.
Thus, we have
\tau_{i}\preceq\tau_{0}
for
i=1
,
2
.
Since
X_{\Sigma}
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial, we have
\tau_{0}
is simplicial and thus
\widetilde{\tau}:=\operatorname{Cone}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\in\Sigma
because
\Sigma
is a fan.

Moreover,
W\cap\widetilde{\tau}
does not contain any ray in
\widetilde{\tau}(1)
.
Hence
W\cap{\mathbb{C}}\widetilde{\tau}\neq\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}(S)
for any
S\subseteq\widetilde{\tau}(1)
.
Therefore,
(\widetilde{\tau},W)
is singular.

∎

Appendix B
Dicritical locus of Fano foliation of rank one

In this section, we show the irreducibility of the dicritical loci and singular loci for Fano foliations, which are not necessarily toric, of rank one on normal projective varieties.

Proposition B.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a Fano foliation of rank one on a normal projective variety
X
.
Then
\operatorname{dicrit}({\mathcal{F}})=\operatorname{Sing}({\mathcal{F}})
which is irreducible and thus connected.

Proof.

Since
K_{\mathcal{F}}
is not pseudo-effective, by
[
LLM23
, Theorem 3.1]
(see also
[
CP19
, Theorem 1.1]
), there exists a non-zero algebraically integrable sub-foliation of
{\mathcal{F}}
.
Since
\operatorname{rank}{\mathcal{F}}=1
,
{\mathcal{F}}
itself is algebraically integrable.

Let
\pi\colon Y\to X
be a Property
(*)
-modification (see
[
ACSS21
, Definition 3.8 and Theorem 3.10]
) such that
{\mathcal{G}}:=\pi^{*}{\mathcal{F}}
is induced by an equi-dimensional morphism
f\colon Y\to Z
.
Moreover, we write
\pi^{*}K_{\mathcal{F}}=K_{\mathcal{G}}+E+G
where
E=\sum\iota(E_{i})E_{i}
and
G
is effective.

Note that the general fiber
F
of
f
is
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
and

0>K_{\mathcal{F}}\cdot\pi(F)=(K_{\mathcal{G}}+E+G)\cdot F\geq-2+\sum\iota(E_{i}).

Thus, there is at most one
E_{i}
with
\iota(E_{i})=1
.
Moreover, by
[
CJV24
, Proposition 5.8]
, there exists a non-foliation invariant exceptional divisor and thus, there is exactly one non-foliation invariant exceptional divisor, say
E_{0}
.
So the dicritical locus of
{\mathcal{F}}
is
\pi(E_{0})
, the center of
E_{0}
, which is irreducible.

Note that all fibers of
f
are irreducible as
{\mathcal{F}}
is Fano.
Also there is no foliation singularity for
{\mathcal{G}}
.
Thus,
\operatorname{Sing}{\mathcal{F}}=\pi(E_{0})
, which is irreducible.
∎

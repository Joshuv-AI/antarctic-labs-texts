Theoretical and numerical indirect stabilization of coupled wave equations with a single time-delayed damping
Alhabib Moumni

Abstract
The focal point of this paper is to theoretically investigate and numerically validate the effect of time delay on the exponential stabilization of a class of coupled hyperbolic systems with delayed and non-delayed dampings. The class in question consists of two strongly coupled wave equations featuring a delayed and non-delayed damping terms on the first wave equation. Through a standard change of variables and semi-group theory, we address the well-posedness of the considered coupled system. Thereon, based on some observability inequalities, we derive sufficient conditions guaranteeing the exponential decay of a suitable energy. On the other hand, from the numerical point of view, we validate the theoretical results in 1 ​ D 1D domains based on a suitable numerical approximation obtained through the Finite Difference Method. More precisely, we construct a discrete numerical scheme which preserves the energy decay property of its continuous counterpart. Our theoretical analysis and implementation of our developed numerical scheme assert the effect of the time-delayed damping on the exponential stability of strongly coupled wave equations.



Keywords: Stabilization, Hyperbolic systems, Delay feedbacks, Numerical Analysis

\displaystyle+\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\int_{\Omega}a(x)u_{t}^{2}(x,t)\,dx-\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\int_{\Omega}a(x)u_{t}^{2}(x,t-\tau)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mu_{1}\int_{\Omega}a(x)u_{t}^{2}(x,t)\,dx-\mu_{2}\int_{\Omega}a(x)u_{t}(x,t)u_{t}(x,t-\tau)\,dx+\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\int_{\Omega}a(x)u_{t}^{2}(x,t)\,dx

\displaystyle-\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\int_{\Omega}a(x)u_{t}^{2}(x,t-\tau)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\left(-\mu_{1}+\frac{\mu_{2}}{2}+\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\right)\int_{\Omega}a(x)u_{t}^{2}(x,t)\,dx+\left(\frac{\mu_{2}}{2}-\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\right)\int_{\Omega}a(x)u_{t}^{2}(x,t-\tau)\,dx.

The two conditions (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) guarantee that

-\mu_{1}+\frac{\mu_{2}}{2}+\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\leq 0,\quad\frac{\mu_{2}}{2}-\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\leq 0.

Then, the energy
E
is nonincreasing.
∎

Now that we have established the conditions under which the energy
E
is nonincreasing, we proceed to address the question of determining the rates at which its decay occurs. To this end, we consider the following system:

\begin{cases}w_{tt}-\Delta w+b\theta_{t}=0,&\text{in }\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
\theta_{tt}-\Delta\theta-bw_{t}=0,&\text{in }\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
w=\theta=0,&\text{on }\partial\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
w(x,0)=w_{0}(x),\quad w_{t}(x,0)=w_{1}(x),&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\theta(x,0)=\theta_{0}(x),\quad\theta_{t}(x,0)=\theta_{1}(x),&\text{in }\Omega.\end{cases}

(3.5)

with initial data
\left(w_{0},w_{1},\theta_{0},\theta_{1}\right)\in\left(H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)\right)^{2}
.

Furthermore, we denote by
E_{S}(.)
the standard energy corresponding to strongly coupled wave equations

E_{S}(t):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left\{w_{t}^{2}(x,t)+|\nabla w(x,t)|^{2}+\theta_{t}^{2}(x,t)+|\nabla\theta(x,t)|^{2}\right\}dx.

(3.6)

It is well-known (see
[
14
]
) that Problem (
3.5
) is conservative. Moreover, for any function
b,c\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
and non empty open sets
\omega_{c^{+}}
and
\omega_{b,0}
satisfying

\overline{\omega_{b,0}}\subset\left\{x\in\Omega:\quad b(x)\neq 0\right\}

(LH1)

\overline{\omega_{c^{+}}}\subset\left\{x\in\Omega:\quad c(x)>0\right\},

(LH2)

and

\omega_{b,0}\subset\omega_{c^{+}}

satisfying the GCC,
the following observability inequality holds if the observability time is sufficiently large.

Theorem 3.1
.

There exists a time
T_{0}>0
such that for any time
T>T_{0}
there exists a positive constant
C
(depending on
T
) for which

E_{S}(0)\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}c(x)w_{t}^{2}(x,t)dxdt

for any weak solution to Problem (
3.5
).

With the aforementioned observability inequality at hand, we can prove the following exponential stability result for the Problem (
1.1
).

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume that Conditions (
1.2
), (
1.4
), (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) hold and
\omega
satisfies the GCC. Then, there exist two positive constants
K,\tilde{\mu}
such that

E(t)\leq Ke^{-\tilde{\mu}t}E(0),\quad t>0,

(3.7)

for any solution to Problem (
1.1
).

Proof.

In the spirit of
[
25
]
and
[
21
]
, we can decompose the solution
(u,y)
of (
1.1
) as
u=w+\tilde{w}
and
y=\theta+\tilde{\theta},

where
(w,\theta)
is the solution to the problem

\begin{cases}w_{tt}(x,t)-\Delta w(x,t)+b\theta_{t}(x,t)=0,&\text{in }\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
\theta_{tt}(x,t)-\Delta\theta(x,t)-bw_{t}(x,t)=0,&\text{in }\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
w(x,t)=\theta(x,t)=0,&\text{on }\partial\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
w(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad w_{t}(x,0)=u_{1}(x),&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\theta(x,0)=y_{0}(x),\quad\theta_{t}(x,0)=y_{1}(x),&\text{in }\Omega,\end{cases}

(3.8)

and
(\tilde{w},\tilde{\theta})
solution to the problem

\begin{cases}\tilde{w}_{tt}(x,t)-\Delta\tilde{w}(x,t)+b\tilde{\theta}_{t}(x,t)=-a\left[\mu_{1}u_{t}(x,t)+\mu_{2}u_{t}(x,t-\tau)\right],&\text{in }\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
\tilde{\theta}_{tt}(x,t)-\Delta\tilde{\theta}(x,t)-b\tilde{w}_{t}(x,t)=0,&\text{in }\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
\tilde{w}(x,t)=\tilde{\theta}(x,t)=0,&\text{on }\partial\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
\tilde{w}(x,0)=0,\quad\tilde{w}_{t}(x,0)=0,&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\tilde{\theta}(x,0)=0,\quad\tilde{\theta}_{t}(x,0)=0,&\text{in }\Omega.\end{cases}

(3.9)

By (
3.1
) and (
3.6
), we obtain that

E(0)=E_{S}(0)+\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\int_{\Omega}a(x)\int_{-\tau}^{0}u_{t}^{2}(x,s)\,ds\,dx=E_{S}(0)+\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\int_{\Omega}a(x)\int_{0}^{\tau}u_{t}^{2}(x,t-\tau)\,dxdt.

Now, since
\omega_{b}
is non empty, then there exist
x_{0}\in\omega_{b}.
Moreover, since
\omega_{b}
is an open subset, then there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
B(x_{0},2\varepsilon)\subset\omega_{b}\subset\omega.
Taking
\omega_{b,0}=\omega_{b}\cap B(x_{0},\frac{\varepsilon}{2}),
it is clear that
\omega_{b,0}
satisfies Condition (
LH1
). On the other hand by Urysohn’s lemma, there exists a smooth function
0\leq c\leq 1
such that

c(x)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{ if }\quad x\in B(x_{0},\varepsilon),\\
0,&\text{ if }\quad x\notin B(x_{0},2\varepsilon).\end{cases}

(3.10)

Now, we set
\omega_{c^{+}}=B(x_{0},\frac{\varepsilon}{2}).
Thereby, we can see that
\overline{\omega_{c^{+}}}\subset B(x_{0},\varepsilon)
and hence the function
c
satisfies Condition (
LH2
) and
\omega_{b,0}\subset\omega_{c^{+}}.

Thus, we can apply the Theorem
3.1
, to obtain that for every
T>T_{0}

\displaystyle E_{S}(0)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}c(x)w_{t}^{2}(x,t)\,dx\,dt\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B(x_{0},2\varepsilon)}w_{t}^{2}(x,t)\,dx\,dt\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\omega}w_{t}^{2}(x,t)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{a_{0}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\omega}a_{0}w_{t}^{2}(x,t)\,dx\,dt\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\omega}a(x)w_{t}^{2}(x,t)\,dx\,dt\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}a(x)w_{t}^{2}(x,t)\,dx\,dt.

Hence, we can deduce that if
T>\max\{T_{0},\tau\}
, it holds that

\displaystyle E(0)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}a(x)w_{t}^{2}(x,t)dxdt+\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\int_{\Omega}a(x)\int_{0}^{T}u_{t}^{2}(x,t-\tau)dtdx

(3.11)

\displaystyle\leq 2C\int_{\Omega}a(x)\int_{0}^{T}\left(u_{t}^{2}(x,t)+\tilde{w}_{t}^{2}(x,t)\right)dtdx+\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\int_{\Omega}a(x)\int_{0}^{T}u_{t}^{2}(x,t-\tau)dtdx.

Now, observe that from (
3.9
), it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left\{\tilde{w}_{t}^{2}(x,t)+|\nabla\tilde{w}(x,t)|^{2}+\tilde{\theta}_{t}^{2}(x,t)+|\nabla\tilde{\theta}(x,t)|^{2}\right\}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left\{\tilde{w}_{t}(x,t)\tilde{w}_{tt}(x,t)+\nabla\tilde{w}(x,t)\nabla\tilde{w}_{t}(x,t)+\tilde{\theta}_{t}(x,t)\tilde{\theta}_{tt}(x,t)+\nabla\tilde{\theta}(x,t)\nabla\tilde{\theta}_{t}(x,t)\right\}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\tilde{w}_{t}\left[-\mu_{1}a(x)u_{t}(x,t)-\mu_{2}a(x)u_{t}(x,t-\tau)\right]dx.

Integrating with respect to time on
[0,t]
, for
t\in(0,T]
, and using the fact that the initial data in (
3.9
) is set to zero, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left\{\tilde{w}_{t}^{2}(x,t)+|\nabla\tilde{w}(x,t)|^{2}+\tilde{\theta}_{t}^{2}(x,t)+|\nabla\tilde{\theta}(x,t)|^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle dx=-\mu_{1}\int_{\Omega}a(x)\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{w}_{t}(x,t)u_{t}(x,t)dtdx

\displaystyle-\mu_{2}\int_{\Omega}a(x)\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{w}_{t}(x,t)u_{t}(x,t-\tau)dtdx.

By integrating with respect to time, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\tilde{w}_{t}^{2}(x,t)dxdt\leq

\displaystyle-2\mu_{1}T\int_{\Omega}a(x)\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{w}_{t}(x,t)u_{t}(x,t)dxdt

\displaystyle-2\mu_{2}T\int_{\Omega}a(x)\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{w}_{t}(x,t)u_{t}(x,t-\tau)dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{T}\tilde{w}_{t}^{2}(x,t)dxdt+4T^{2}\mu_{1}^{2}\|a\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}a(x)\int_{0}^{T}u_{t}^{2}(x,t)dxdt

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{T}\tilde{w}_{t}^{2}(x,t)dxdt+4T^{2}\mu_{2}^{2}\|a\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}a(x)\int_{0}^{T}u_{t}^{2}(x,t-\tau)dxdt,

which implies that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\tilde{w}_{t}^{2}(x,t)dxdt\leq

\displaystyle 8T^{2}\mu_{1}^{2}\|a\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}a(x)\int_{0}^{T}u_{t}^{2}(x,t)dxdt

\displaystyle+8T^{2}\mu_{2}^{2}\|a\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}a(x)\int_{0}^{T}u_{t}^{2}(x,t-\tau)dxdt.

We can conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a(x)\int_{0}^{T}\tilde{w}_{t}^{2}(x,t)dtdx\leq

\displaystyle\|a\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\tilde{w}_{t}^{2}(x,t)dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 8T^{2}\mu_{1}^{2}\|a\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\int_{\Omega}a(x)\int_{0}^{T}u_{t}^{2}(x,t)dxdt

\displaystyle+8T^{2}\mu_{2}^{2}\|a\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\int_{\Omega}a(x)\int_{0}^{T}u_{t}^{2}(x,t-\tau)dxdt.

This combined with (
3.11
) yield that

E(0)\leq C_{0}\int_{\Omega}a(x)\int_{0}^{T}\left(u_{t}^{2}(x,t)+u_{t}^{2}(x,t-\tau)\right)\,dt\,dx,

where
C_{0}
is a positive constant.

From (
3.4
), we have

E(T)-E(0)\leq-C\int_{\Omega}a(x)\int_{0}^{T}\left\{u_{t}^{2}(x,t)+u_{t}^{2}(x,t-\tau)\right\}dtdx.

Hence, we obtain

E(T)\leq E(0)\leq C_{0}\int_{\Omega}a(x)\int_{0}^{T}\left(u_{t}^{2}(x,t)+u_{t}^{2}(x,t-\tau)\right)\,dt\,dx\leq C_{0}C^{-1}(E(0)-E(T)),

then

E(T)\leq\tilde{C}E(0)

with
\tilde{C}<1
.

This easily implies the stability estimate (
3.7
), since our System (
1.1
) is invariant by translation and the energy
E
is decreasing.
∎

4
Numerical analysis in the case of one dimensional spatial domains

When it comes to the numerical validation of the theoretical results pertaining to the stabilization of partial differential equations, one needs to first make sure that the numerical solution, generated by the chosen approximating numerical method, preservers most of the physical properties of its continuous counterpart. In our case, the most important physical property that we shall focus on pertains to the inequality given by (
3.4
), which asserts that, for any given initial state
U_{0}=\left(u_{0},u_{1},y_{0},y_{1},f_{0}\right)\in\mathcal{H}
, if (
3.2
) holds and
\xi
satisfies (
3.3
), then the continuous energy of System (
1.1
) is non-increasing.

To proceed further, we consider a one dimensional spatial domain given by
\Omega=(0,1)
. Needless to say, in this case,
\Omega
is of class
C^{2}
and System (
1.1
) reduces to the following one:

\begin{cases}u_{tt}(x,t)-u_{xx}(x,t)+b(x)y_{t}(x,t)+a(x)\left[\mu_{1}u_{t}(x,t)+\mu_{2}u_{t}(x,t-\tau)\right]=0,&\text{in }(0,1)\times(0,\infty),\\
y_{tt}(x,t)-y_{xx}(x,t)-b(x)u_{t}(x,t)=0,&\text{in }(0,1)\times(0,\infty),\\
u(0,t)=y(0,t)=0,&\text{on }(0,\infty),\\
u(1,t)=y(1,t)=0,&\text{on }(0,\infty),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{t}(x,0)=u_{1}(x),&\text{in }(0,1),\\
y(x,0)=y_{0}(x),\quad y_{t}(x,0)=y_{1}(x),&\text{in }(0,1),\\
u_{t}(x,t)=f_{0}(x,t),&\text{in }(0,1)\times(-\tau,0).\end{cases}

(4.1)

On the other hand, the energy given by (
3.1
) becomes

\displaystyle E(t)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\left[\int_{0}^{1}\left\{u_{t}^{2}(x,t)+u_{x}^{2}(x,t)+y_{t}^{2}(x,t)+|y_{x}^{2}(x,t)\right\}\,dx\right]+\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\int_{0}^{1}a(x)\int_{t-\tau}^{t}u_{t}^{2}(x,s)dsdx,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\forall t\geq 0.

The numerical analysis of the stabilization problem of System (
4.1
) features the following two main parts:

1.

Numerical approximation of the continuous system:
In this step, we aim to develop by means of the Finite Difference Method a numerical scheme of System (
4.1
) which generates a stable converging discrete solution. In this regard, we point out that based on the approximations of the operators
\Psi\mapsto\Psi_{tt}
,
\Psi\mapsto\Psi_{xx}
and
\Psi\mapsto\Psi_{x}
, various numerical schemes can be constructed. In our case, we choose a three-point approximation of the operator
\Psi\mapsto\Psi_{xx}
at the current time step, a three-point approximation of the operator
\Psi\mapsto\Psi_{tt}
and lastly, a centered difference approximation of the operator
\Psi\mapsto\Psi_{x}
at the next time step. Let us point out that such choices guarantee that the discrete solution is stable and converging to the continuous smooth solution corresponding to System (
4.1
). This can be achieved by classical arguments based on the standard Von Neumann stability analysis for coupled partial differential equations.

2.

Numerical approximation of the continuous energy:
Contrarily to the previous part, the numerical approximation of the integrals featuring in the definition of the energy must be carefully selected in order to assure that the generated discrete energy preserves the decay property given by (
3.4
). In our case, we begin by approximating the operator
\Psi\mapsto\Psi_{t}^{2}
showing in the non-delayed integrands of the energy by a forward approximation. Then, the integral with respect to the spatial variable is approximated by a first-order forward approximation. On the other hand, we use a modified approximation of the operator
\Psi\mapsto\Psi_{x}^{2}
featuring both current and next time steps and then use a first-order forward approximation to approximate the integral with respect to the spatial variable. As for the last term featuring time delay, we use a second-ordered centered difference approximation of the operator
\Psi\mapsto\Psi_{t}^{2}
, then, as always, we use a forward approximation to approximate the integral with respect to time and space. As we will explore in the upcoming subsections, the justification of the aforementioned choices of approximations is purely computational in order to assure that the decaying property of the discrete energy is satisfied.

4.1
Numerical approximation of the continuous system

Let
N
be a non negative integer. Consider the subdivision of
[0,1]
given by

0=x_{0}<x_{1}<\ldots<x_{N}<x_{N+1}=1,\quad\text{ i.e. }x_{j}=j\Delta x,j=0,\ldots,N+1.

Set
t^{n+1}-t^{n}=\Delta t
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. For
j=0,\ldots,N+1
, we denote
b_{j}=b\left(x_{j}\right),a_{j}=a\left(x_{j}\right),

u_{j}^{n}=u(x_{j},t^{n})
and
y_{j}^{n}=y(x_{j},t^{n})
.
Let
\tau=I\Delta t,
then
t^{n}-\tau=(n-I)\Delta t,
then we can see that for
n\leq I
the delayed term in (
4.1
) is defined by the initial function
f_{0}.
The explicit finite-difference discretization of System (
4.1
) is thus, for
n=0,1,\ldots,I
and
j=1,\ldots,N
:

\begin{cases}\frac{u_{j}^{n+1}-2u_{j}^{n}+u_{j}^{n-1}}{\Delta t^{2}}-\frac{u_{j+1}^{n}-2u_{j}^{n}+u_{j-1}^{n}}{\Delta x^{2}}+b_{j}\frac{y_{j}^{n+1}-y_{j}^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}+a_{j}\mu_{1}\frac{u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}+a_{j}\mu_{2}f_{0}(x_{j},(n-I)\Delta t)=0,\\
\frac{y_{j}^{n+1}-2y_{j}^{n}+y_{j}^{n-1}}{\Delta t^{2}}-\frac{y_{j+1}^{n}-2y_{j}^{n}+y_{j-1}^{n}}{\Delta x^{2}}-b_{j}\frac{u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}=0,\\
u_{0}^{n}=u_{N+1}^{n}=0,\\
y_{0}^{n}=y_{N+1}^{n}=0.&\end{cases}

(4.3)

For
n>I,
the explicit finite-difference discretization of System (
4.1
) reads

\begin{cases}\frac{u_{j}^{n+1}-2u_{j}^{n}+u_{j}^{n-1}}{\Delta t^{2}}-\frac{u_{j+1}^{n}-2u_{j}^{n}+u_{j-1}^{n}}{\Delta x^{2}}+b_{j}\frac{y_{j}^{n+1}-y_{j}^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}+a_{j}\mu_{1}\frac{u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}+a_{j}\mu_{2}\frac{u_{j}^{n-I+1}-u_{j}^{n-I-1}}{2\Delta t}=0,\\
\frac{y_{j}^{n+1}-2y_{j}^{n}+y_{j}^{n-1}}{\Delta t^{2}}-\frac{y_{j+1}^{n}-2y_{j}^{n}+y_{j-1}^{n}}{\Delta x^{2}}-b_{j}\frac{u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}=0,\\
u_{0}^{n}=u_{N+1}^{n}=0,\\
y_{0}^{n}=y_{N+1}^{n}=0.&\end{cases}

(4.4)

According to the initial conditions, for
j=1,\ldots,N

u_{j}^{0}=u_{0}(x_{j})\quad\text{ and }\quad y_{j}^{0}=y_{0}(x_{j}).

As in
[
34
]
we can use the second initial conditions to find the values of
u
and
y
at time
t^{1}=\Delta t
by employing a ”ghost” time-boundary (i.e.
t^{-1}=-\Delta t
) and the second-order centered difference formula for
j=1,\ldots,N
:

u_{1}\left(x_{j}\right)=\left.\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}\right|_{x_{j},0}=\frac{u_{j}^{1}-u_{j}^{-1}}{2\Delta t}+O\left(\Delta t^{2}\right).

Thus we have for
j=1,\ldots,N
:

u_{j}^{-1}=u_{j}^{1}-2\Delta tu_{1}\left(x_{j}\right).

We use the same discrete form of the initial conditions for
y
to obtain, for
j=1,\ldots,N
, that:

y_{j}^{-1}=y_{j}^{1}-2\Delta ty_{1}\left(x_{j}\right).

Setting
n=0
, in the numerical scheme (
4.3
), the two preceding equalities permit us to compute
\left(u_{j}^{1},y_{j}^{1}\right)_{j=0,N}
as follows

u_{j}^{1}=(1-\lambda)u_{j}^{0}+\Delta tu_{1}(x_{j})+\frac{\lambda}{2}(u_{j+1}^{0}+u_{j-1}^{0})-b_{j}\frac{\Delta t^{2}}{2}y_{1}(x_{j})-a_{j}\frac{\mu_{1}\Delta t^{2}}{2}u_{1}(x_{j})-a_{j}\frac{\mu_{2}\Delta t^{2}}{2}f_{0}(x_{j},-I\Delta t)

and

y_{j}^{1}=(1-\lambda)y_{j}^{0}+\Delta ty_{1}(x_{j})+\frac{\lambda}{2}(y_{j+1}^{0}+y_{j-1}^{0})+b_{j}\frac{\Delta t^{2}}{2}u_{1}(x_{j}),

where
\lambda=\frac{\Delta t^{2}}{\Delta x^{2}}.

Finally, the solution
(u,y)
can be computed at any time
t^{n}
. Indeed, using the same argument as in
[
34
]
we have,
for
n=1,\ldots,I

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} u_{j}^{n+1}=(1-\lambda)\alpha_{j}u_{j}^{n}&+\lambda\beta_{j}\left(u_{j+1}^{n}+u_{j-1}^{n}\right)+\gamma_{j}u_{j}^{n-1}-(1-\lambda)\varrho_{j}y_{j}^{n}\\
&-\lambda\zeta_{j}\left(y_{j+1}^{n}+y_{j-1}^{n}\right)+\kappa_{j}y_{j}^{n-1}-r_{j}f_{0}(x_{j},(n-I)\Delta t)\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} y_{j}^{n+1}=(1-\lambda)\widetilde{\alpha}_{j}y_{j}^{n}&+\lambda\widetilde{\beta}_{j}\left(y_{j+1}^{n}+y_{j-1}^{n}\right)+\widetilde{\gamma}_{j}y_{j}^{n-1}+(1-\lambda)\widetilde{\varrho}_{j}u_{j}^{n}\\
&+\lambda\tilde{\xi}_{j}\left(u_{j+1}^{n}+u_{j-1}^{n}\right)+\widetilde{\kappa}_{j}u_{j}^{n-1}-\widetilde{r}_{j}f_{0}(x_{j},(n-I)\Delta t)\end{aligned}

and for
n>I

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} u_{j}^{n+1}=(1-\lambda)\alpha_{j}u_{j}^{n}&+\lambda\beta_{j}\left(u_{j+1}^{n}+u_{j-1}^{n}\right)+\gamma_{j}u_{j}^{n-1}-(1-\lambda)\varrho_{j}y_{j}^{n}\\
&-\lambda\zeta_{j}\left(y_{j+1}^{n}+y_{j-1}^{n}\right)+\kappa_{j}y_{j}^{n-1}-r_{j}\left(\frac{u_{j}^{n-I+1}-u_{j}^{n-I-1}}{2\Delta t}\right)\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} y_{j}^{n+1}=(1-\lambda)\widetilde{\alpha}_{j}y_{j}^{n}&+\lambda\widetilde{\beta}_{j}\left(y_{j+1}^{n}+y_{j-1}^{n}\right)+\widetilde{\gamma}_{j}y_{j}^{n-1}+(1-\lambda)\widetilde{\varrho}_{j}u_{j}^{n}\\
&+\lambda\tilde{\zeta}_{j}\left(u_{j+1}^{n}+u_{j-1}^{n}\right)+\widetilde{\kappa}_{j}u_{j}^{n-1}-\widetilde{r}_{j}\left(\frac{u_{j}^{n-I+1}-u_{j}^{n-I-1}}{2\Delta t}\right)\end{aligned}

where we have set:

\displaystyle\alpha_{j}=\frac{2}{c_{j}},

\displaystyle\beta_{j}=\frac{1}{c_{j}},

\displaystyle\gamma_{j}=\frac{c_{j}-2}{c_{j}},

\displaystyle\varrho_{j}=\frac{b_{j}\Delta t}{c_{j}},

\displaystyle\zeta_{j}=\frac{b_{j}\Delta t}{2c_{j}},

\displaystyle\kappa_{j}=\frac{\mu_{1}a_{j}}{c_{j}},

\displaystyle r_{j}=\frac{\mu_{2}a_{j}\Delta t^{2}}{c_{j}},

\displaystyle\widetilde{\alpha}_{j}=2-\frac{(b_{j}\Delta t)^{2}}{2c_{j}},

\displaystyle\widetilde{\beta}_{j}=1-\frac{(b_{j}\Delta t)^{2}}{4c_{j}},

\displaystyle\widetilde{\gamma}_{j}=\frac{(b_{j}\Delta t)^{2}}{2c_{j}}-1,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\varrho}_{j}=\frac{b_{j}\Delta t}{c_{j}},

\displaystyle\widetilde{\zeta}_{j}=\frac{b_{j}\Delta t}{2c_{j}},

\displaystyle\widetilde{\kappa}_{j}=\frac{b_{j}\Delta t}{2}(\frac{c_{j}-2}{c_{j}}-1),

\displaystyle\widetilde{r}_{j}=\frac{\mu_{2}a_{j}b_{j}\Delta t^{3}}{2c_{j}},

where
c_{j}=1+\frac{\mu_{1}a_{j}}{2}\Delta t+\left(\frac{b_{j}\Delta t}{2}\right)^{2}.

4.2
Numerical approximation of the continuous energy

We begin by discretizing the four leading terms of the energy (
3.1
) by employing the first procedure used in
[
34
]
to obtain:

•

The discrete kinetic energy for
u
as:
E_{k,u}^{n}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\frac{u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n}}{\Delta t}\right)^{2}.

•

The discrete potential energy for
u
as:
E_{p,u}^{n}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{N}\left(\frac{u_{j+1}^{n}-u_{j}^{n}}{\Delta x}\right)\left(\frac{u_{j+1}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n+1}}{\Delta x}\right).

•

The discrete kinetic energy for
y
as:
E_{k,y}^{n}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\frac{y_{j}^{n+1}-y_{j}^{n}}{\Delta t}\right)^{2}.

•

The discrete potential energy for
u
as:
E_{y,u}^{n}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{N}\left(\frac{y_{j+1}^{n}-y_{j}^{n}}{\Delta x}\right)\left(\frac{y_{j+1}^{n+1}-y_{j}^{n+1}}{\Delta x}\right).

We approximate the last term in the definition of the energy (
3.1
) as follows:

E_{d,u}^{n}=\begin{cases}\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\left(\sum_{k=n+1}^{I}\Delta tf_{0}^{2}(x_{j},(k-I)\Delta t)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Delta t\left(\frac{u_{j}^{k+1}-u_{j}^{k-1}}{2\Delta t}\right)^{2}\right),&\text{ if }n<I,\\
\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\Delta t\sum_{k=n-I+1}^{n}\left(\frac{u_{j}^{k+1}-u_{j}^{k-1}}{2\Delta t}\right)^{2},&\text{ if }n\geq I.\end{cases}

The total discrete energy is then defined as

E^{n}=E_{k,u}^{n}+E_{p,u}^{n}+E_{k,y}^{n}+E_{p,y}^{n}+E_{d,u}^{n}.

Let us prove now that this definition of the energy fulfills the dissipaing property stated above. To this end, for
n\leq I,

we multiply the first equation of (
4.3
) by
\left(u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n-1}\right)
and we sum up over
j=1,\ldots,N
.
We obtain:

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{u_{j}^{n+1}-2u_{j}^{n}+u_{j}^{n-1}}{\Delta t^{2}}\left(u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n-1}\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{u_{j+1}^{n}-2u_{j}^{n}+u_{j-1}^{n}}{\Delta x^{2}}\left(u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n-1}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{N}b_{j}\frac{y_{j}^{n+1}-y_{j}^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}\left(u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n-1}\right)+\mu_{1}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\frac{\left(u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n-1}\right)^{2}}{2\Delta t}

\displaystyle+\mu_{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}f_{0}(x_{j},(n-I)\Delta t)\left(u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n-1}\right)=0.

For the three first leading terms, we reason by the same technique used in
[
34
]
to establish that

\displaystyle 2\left(E_{k,u}^{n}+E_{p,u}^{n}-E_{k,u}^{n-1}-E_{p,u}^{n-1}\right)+2\Delta t\mu_{1}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\left(\frac{u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}\right)^{2}

(4.5)

\displaystyle+2\Delta t\mu_{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}f_{0}(x_{j},(n-I)\Delta t)\left(\frac{u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{N}b_{j}\frac{y_{j}^{n+1}-y_{j}^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}\left(u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n-1}\right)=0.

Similarly, by multiplying the second equation of (
4.3
) by
\left(y_{j}^{n+1}-y_{j}^{n-1}\right)
, and using the same
algebraic tricks, we arrive at

2\left(E_{k,y}^{n}+E_{p,y}^{n}-E_{k,y}^{n-1}-E_{p,y}^{n-1}\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{N}b_{j}\frac{u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}\left(y_{j}^{n+1}-y_{j}^{n-1}\right)=0.

(4.6)

Now, we devote our interest to the last term of the energy (
3.1
). For
n<I
, we obtain

\displaystyle E^{n}_{d,u}-E^{n-1}_{d,u}=

\displaystyle\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\left(\sum_{k=n+1}^{I}\Delta tf_{0}^{2}(x_{j},(k-I)\Delta t)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Delta t\left(\frac{u_{j}^{k+1}-u_{j}^{k-1}}{2\Delta t}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\left(\sum_{k=n}^{I}\Delta tf_{0}^{2}(x_{j},(k-I)\Delta t)+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\Delta t\left(\frac{u_{j}^{k+1}-u_{j}^{k-1}}{2\Delta t}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\Delta t\left(\frac{u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}\right)^{2}-\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\Delta tf_{0}^{2}(x_{j},(n-I)\Delta t).

For
n=I,
we have

\displaystyle E^{I}_{d,u}-E^{I-1}_{d,u}=

\displaystyle\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\Delta t\sum_{k=1}^{I}\left(\frac{u_{j}^{k+1}-u_{j}^{k-1}}{2\Delta t}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\left(\sum_{k=I}^{I}\Delta tf_{0}^{2}(x_{j},(k-I)\Delta t)+\sum_{k=1}^{I-1}\Delta t\left(\frac{u_{j}^{k+1}-u_{j}^{k-1}}{2\Delta t}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\Delta t\left(\frac{u_{j}^{I+1}-u_{j}^{I-1}}{2\Delta t}\right)^{2}-\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\Delta tf_{0}^{2}(x_{j},0).

Then, for
n\leq I
, we have

\displaystyle E^{n}_{d,u}-E^{n-1}_{d,u}=\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\Delta t\left(\frac{u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}\right)^{2}-\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\Delta tf_{0}^{2}(x_{j},(n-I)\Delta t).

(4.7)

Now, by combining (
4.5
), (
4.6
) and (
4.7
), it holds that

\displaystyle E^{n}-E^{n-1}=

\displaystyle-\Delta t\mu_{1}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\left(\frac{u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}\right)^{2}-\Delta t\mu_{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}f_{0}(x_{j},(n-I)\Delta t)\left(\frac{u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\Delta t\left(\frac{u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}\right)^{2}-\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\Delta tf_{0}^{2}(x_{j},(n-I)\Delta t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Delta t\left(-\mu_{1}+\frac{\mu_{2}}{2}+\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\left(\frac{u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\Delta t\left(\frac{\mu_{2}}{2}-\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}f_{0}^{2}(x_{j},(n-I)\Delta t).

Under the two conditions (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), the dissipating property of the energy (
3.1
), in the case
n\leq I
, holds.

We consider now the case where
n>I
. We multiply the first equation of (
4.4
) by
\left(u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n-1}\right)
, sum up over
j=1,\ldots,N
and multiply the second equation of (
4.4
) by
\left(y_{j}^{n+1}-y_{j}^{n-1}\right)
, and sum up over
j=1,\ldots,N
. Consequently, similarly to the case where
n\leq I
, we obtain that

\displaystyle 2\left(E_{k,u}^{n}+E_{p,u}^{n}+E_{k,y}^{n}+E_{p,y}^{n}-E_{k,u}^{n-1}-E_{p,u}^{n-1}-E_{k,y}^{n-1}-E_{k,y}^{n-1}\right)=-2\Delta t\mu_{1}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\left(\frac{u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}\right)^{2}

(4.8)

\displaystyle-2\Delta t\mu_{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\left(\frac{u_{j}^{n-I+1}-u_{j}^{n-I-1}}{2\Delta t}\right)\left(\frac{u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}\right)

For the last term corresponding to the energy, we obtain

\displaystyle E^{n}_{d,u}-E^{n-1}_{d,u}=

\displaystyle\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\Delta t\sum_{k=n-I+1}^{n}\left(\frac{u_{j}^{k+1}-u_{j}^{k-1}}{2\Delta t}\right)^{2}-\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\Delta t\sum_{k=n-I}^{n-1}\left(\frac{u_{j}^{k+1}-u_{j}^{k-1}}{2\Delta t}\right)^{2}

(4.9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\Delta t\left(\frac{u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}\right)^{2}-\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\Delta t\left(\frac{u_{j}^{n-I+1}-u_{j}^{n-I-1}}{2\Delta t}\right)^{2}.

Now, combining (
4.8
) and (
4.9
), it follows that

\displaystyle E^{n}-E^{n-1}=

\displaystyle-\Delta t\mu_{1}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\left(\frac{u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}\right)^{2}-\Delta t\mu_{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\left(\frac{u_{j}^{n-I+1}-u_{j}^{n-I-1}}{2\Delta t}\right)\left(\frac{u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\Delta t\left(\frac{u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}\right)^{2}-\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\Delta t\left(\frac{u_{j}^{n-I+1}-u_{j}^{n-I-1}}{2\Delta t}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Delta t\left(-\mu_{1}+\frac{\mu_{2}}{2}+\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\left(\frac{u_{j}^{n+1}-u_{j}^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\Delta t\left(\frac{\mu_{2}}{2}-\frac{\xi\tau^{-1}}{2}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\left(\frac{u_{j}^{n-I+1}-u_{j}^{n-I-1}}{2\Delta t}\right)^{2}.

Thus, under the two conditions (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), we have the dissipating property of the energy (
3.1
) holds in the case where
n>I.

5
Validation of the theoretical results by numerical simulations

Based on the results of the preceding section, we conduct various numerical simulations in the aim of establishing the theoretical results. To this end, throughout this section, we set

u_{0}(x)=x(x-1),\quad u_{1}(x)=x(x-1),\quad y_{0}(x)=-x(x-1),\quad y_{1}(x)=-x(x-1),

and

f_{0}(x,t)=x(x-1)e^{-t}.

The mesh size is chosen as
N=10
so that
\Delta x=0.1.

In
[
34
]
the authors described in detail the effect of Condition (
1.4
) in the case of strongly coupled wave equations without time delay. That being said, we devote our interest to illustrating the effect of the delayed term. To this end, we set

b=\mathrm{1}_{[0.1,0.2]}(x)\text{ and }a=\mathrm{1}_{[0.1,0.2]\cup[0.8,0.9]}(x).

We also choose
\tau=2
and discretize the interval
[0,\tau]
by taking a step size
\Delta t=0.01
into
I=200
sub-intervals. On the other hand, the final time
T
is chosen as
T=500
and
\xi=\mu_{1}\tau
so that Condition (
3.3
) is satisfied.

We recall that in
[
21
]
, the author proved that the single wave equation with time delay (
1.3
) is exponentially stable if and only if Condition (
3.2
) is satisfied. In order to validate the different theoretical results, we will treat two cases. The first case is when Condition (
3.2
) is satisfied. In this case, we illustrate the influence of the value of
\mu_{2}
corresponding to the delayed damping term. In the second case, we will illustrate that unstabillity occurs when
\mu_{2}\geq\mu_{1}.

The interpretation of the obtained numerical results is distinguished according to the following cases:

1.

The case
\mu_{2}<\mu_{1}.
Exponential stability.

In the case where
\mu_{2}<\mu_{1}
, Fig.
1
illustrates the dynamics of
u
and
y
versus time
t
for large time, where we have chosen
\mu_{1}=1
and
\mu_{2}=0.5
. This choice verifies the assumption
\mu_{2}<\mu_{1}
. It can be observed that the dynamics exponentially converge to the equilibrium point. Figure
2
shows the evolution of the energy
E
, as defined by (
3.1
), with respect to time
t
for different values of
\mu_{2}
. It can be seen that the energy converges exponentially faster as the values of
\mu_{2}
decrease from
\mu_{2}=0.75
to
\mu_{2}=0
. We notice that the case
\mu_{2}=0
is the case when our problem is without delay. In this case, the energy exhibits the fastest exponential decay rate. These results are in line with the theoretical findings established in Theorem
3.2
.

2.

The case
\mu_{2}\geq\mu_{1}.
Unstability results.
In the case where
\mu_{2}\geq\mu_{1}
, Fig.
3

presents the dynamics of
y
and
u
and Fig.
4
illustrates the evolution of the energy
E
with respect to time
t
when
\mu_{1}=1
and
\mu_{2}=1.2
. These figures indicate that the dynamics diverge. Therefore, we infer that the presence of a time delay
\tau=2
with a dominant coefficient
\mu_{2}\geq\mu_{1}
destabilizes the dynamics when
\mu_{2}<\mu_{1}
. This result is in accordance with the theoretical results given in
[
21
]
for the single wave equation (
1.3
).

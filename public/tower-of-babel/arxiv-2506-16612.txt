The K \displaystyle K -Theory of the Sphere with the Antipodal Involution
Jeffrey L. Boersema

Abstract
. This is a thorough investigation on the real K \displaystyle K -theory of the sphere S d \displaystyle S^{d} associated with the antipodal involution. We calculate the algebraic structure of real K \displaystyle K -theory and united K \displaystyle K -theory for all d \displaystyle d , we write down explicit unitaries representing the generators of all the non-trivial K \displaystyle K -theory groups for d ≤ 4 \displaystyle d\leq 4 , and we describe a recipe for generating such unitaries for all d \displaystyle d .

Introduction

It is a familiar adage that theory of commutative unital
\displaystyle C^{*}
-algebras is equivalent to the theory of compact Hausdorff spaces; and that the theory commutative non-unital
\displaystyle C^{*}
-algebras is equivalent to the theory of locally compact Hausdorff spaces.
Furthermore under this equivalence, the topological
\displaystyle K
-theory corresponds to the operator algebra
\displaystyle K
-theory. More precisely, there is a natural isomorphism

{K}^{i}(X)\cong K_{i}(C(X))

between graded topological
\displaystyle K
-theory groups and the operator
\displaystyle K
-theory groups, where
\displaystyle X
is a compact Hausdorff space.

Less-familiar perhaps is the idea that the theory of commutative unital real
\displaystyle C^{*}
-algebras is equivalent to the theory of compact Hausdorff spaces with involution; and that the theory commutative non-unital real
\displaystyle C^{*}
-algebras is equivalent to the theory of locally compact Hausdorff spaces with involution. Indeed every real commutative unital
\displaystyle C^{*}
-algebra is isomorphic to

C(X,\tau)=\{f\in C(X,{\mathbb{R}})\mid f(x^{\tau})=\overline{f(x)}\}

for some compact space with continuous involution
\displaystyle(X,\tau)
and every real commutative non-unital
\displaystyle C^{*}
-algebra is isomorphic to

\displaystyle C_{0}(X,\tau)
for some locally compact space
\displaystyle X
with involution (see Theorem 2.7.2 of
[
14
]

Furthermore, there is a natural isomorphism

KR^{i}(X,\tau)\cong KO_{i}(C(X,\tau))\;.

On the left side,

is the invariant introduced by Atiyah in
[

On the right side,

is the operator algebra

\displaystyle C^{*}
-algebra
\displaystyle C(X,\tau)

\displaystyle K^{\scriptscriptstyle{\it CRT}}({\mathbb{R}}),K^{\scriptscriptstyle{\it CRT}}({\mathbb{C}}),

\displaystyle K^{\scriptscriptstyle{\it CRT}}(T)

In the case
\displaystyle d=2

\displaystyle\mathcal{CRT}
-modules in Table

\displaystyle K^{\scriptscriptstyle{\it CRT}}(\mathfrak{S}^{2})=\Sigma^{a}K^{\scriptscriptstyle{\it CRT}}({\mathbb{R}})\oplus\Sigma^{b}K^{\scriptscriptstyle{\it CRT}}({\mathbb{R}})

\displaystyle a,b

Consider the map
\displaystyle\pi\colon\mathfrak{S}^{2}\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}^{1}

\displaystyle\pi

\displaystyle f(-x)=-f(x)

\displaystyle\ker\pi\cong S^{2}{\mathbb{C}}

0\rightarrow S^{2}{\mathbb{C}}\xrightarrow{i}\mathfrak{S}^{2}\xrightarrow{\pi}\mathfrak{S}^{1}\rightarrow 0\;.

Using the fact that
\displaystyle KO_{*}(S^{2}{\mathbb{C}})=({\mathbb{Z}},0)

\displaystyle K
-theory is

\displaystyle\partial_{1}=0
and
\displaystyle KO_{0}(\mathfrak{S}^{2})={\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
and
\displaystyle KO_{1}(\mathfrak{S}^{2})={\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
. An identical analysis, at the portion of the long exact sequence starting with
\displaystyle KO_{5}(S^{2}{\mathbb{C}})=0

\displaystyle KO_{4}(\mathfrak{S}^{2})={\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
and
\displaystyle KO_{5}(\mathfrak{S}^{2})={\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
. From this information, in light of the possibilities discussed in the previous paragraph, it follows that
\displaystyle K^{\scriptscriptstyle{\it CRT}}(\mathfrak{S}^{2})=\Sigma^{a}K^{\scriptscriptstyle{\it CRT}}({\mathbb{R}})\oplus\Sigma^{b}K^{\scriptscriptstyle{\it CRT}}({\mathbb{R}})
where
\displaystyle a=0
and
\displaystyle b=-4

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{1}=\pi\varepsilon_{2}

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{1}

\displaystyle KO_{i}({\mathbb{R}})\rightarrow KO_{i}(\mathfrak{S}^{1})

\displaystyle i=0,1

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{2}

\displaystyle KO_{i}({\mathbb{R}})\rightarrow KO_{i}(\mathfrak{S}^{2})

\displaystyle i=0,1

\displaystyle K^{\scriptscriptstyle{\it CRT}}(\mathfrak{S}^{2})

\displaystyle(\varepsilon_{2})_{*}
is injective, carrying
\displaystyle K^{\scriptscriptstyle{\it CRT}}({\mathbb{R}})

\displaystyle K^{\scriptscriptstyle{\it CRT}}(\mathfrak{S}^{2})

\displaystyle d=2
.

\displaystyle\pi\colon\mathfrak{S}^{d}\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}^{1}

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{1}=\pi\varepsilon_{d}

\displaystyle(\varepsilon_{d})^{*}

\displaystyle d\geq 2

\displaystyle K^{\scriptscriptstyle{\it CRT}}({\mathbb{R}})

\displaystyle K^{\scriptscriptstyle{\it CRT}}(\mathfrak{S}^{d})

\displaystyle K^{\scriptscriptstyle{\it CRT}}(\mathfrak{S}^{d})

\displaystyle\mathcal{CRT}

\displaystyle(\varepsilon_{d})_{*}\colon K^{\scriptscriptstyle{\it CRT}}({\mathbb{R}})\rightarrow K^{\scriptscriptstyle{\it CRT}}(\mathfrak{S}^{d})

\displaystyle K^{\scriptscriptstyle{\it CRT}}(\mathfrak{S}^{d})
has a direct summand isomorphic to
\displaystyle K^{\scriptscriptstyle{\it CRT}}({\mathbb{R}})
and a complementary summand, which we call
\displaystyle\widetilde{K}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\it CRT}}(\mathfrak{S}^{d})

must also be a free
\displaystyle\mathcal{CRT}
-module. We have

KU_{*}(\mathfrak{S}^{d})=KU_{*}(C(S^{d},{\mathbb{C}}))=\begin{cases}({\mathbb{Z}}^{2},0)&\text{if $\displaystyle d$ is even }\\
({\mathbb{Z}},{\mathbb{Z}})&\text{if $\displaystyle d$ is odd.}\end{cases}

so the complex part of
\displaystyle\widetilde{K}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\it CRT}}(\mathfrak{S}^{n})

(\widetilde{K}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\it CRT}}(\mathfrak{S}^{d})^{\scriptscriptstyle U}=\begin{cases}({\mathbb{Z}},0)&\text{if $\displaystyle d$ is even }\\
(0,{\mathbb{Z}})&\text{if $\displaystyle d$ is odd.}\end{cases}\;.

From this it follows that

\displaystyle\widetilde{K}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\it CRT}}(\mathfrak{S}^{d})=\Sigma^{a}K^{\scriptscriptstyle{\it CRT}}({\mathbb{R}})
where
\displaystyle a
has the same parity as
\displaystyle d
.

\displaystyle a=-d-2

Now, as in the
\displaystyle d=2

0\rightarrow S^{d}{\mathbb{C}}\xrightarrow{i}\mathfrak{S}^{d}\xrightarrow{\pi}\mathfrak{S}^{d-1}\rightarrow 0\;

\displaystyle K^{\scriptscriptstyle{\it CRT}}(\mathfrak{S}^{d})

\displaystyle K^{\scriptscriptstyle{\it CRT}}(\mathfrak{S}^{d-1})

\displaystyle K^{\scriptscriptstyle{\it CRT}}({\mathbb{R}})

Lemma 5.1
.

\displaystyle w=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\

we have

(2)

(3)

\displaystyle x^{\sharp}=wx^{\rm{Tr}}w^{*}

(4)

\displaystyle x^{\rm{Tr}}=w^{*}x^{\sharp}w

\displaystyle w^{2}=-1_{2}

\displaystyle M_{2}({\mathbb{C}})

\displaystyle\displaystyle x^{\sharp}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=w^{2}x^{\sharp}(w^{*})^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=w^{2}(w^{*}x^{\rm{Tr}}w)(w^{*})^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=wx^{\rm{Tr}}w^{*}\;.

Statements (2) and (4) follow immediate from Statements (1) and (3) by conjugation.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\displaystyle(A,\tau)
be a
\displaystyle C^{*}
-algebra with antimultiplicative involution. Let
\displaystyle w=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\

.
Then a unitary
\displaystyle u\in M_{2}(A)
satisfies

\displaystyle u^{\rm{Tr}\otimes\tau}=u
if and only if
\displaystyle v=uw
satisfies
\displaystyle v^{\sharp\otimes\tau}=-v
.
Furthermore, a unitary
\displaystyle u\in M_{2}(A)
satisfies

\displaystyle u^{\rm{Tr}\otimes\tau}=-u
if and only if
\displaystyle v=uw
satisfies
\displaystyle v^{\sharp\otimes\tau}=v
.

We also note that the same statements of Lemma
5.2
remain true if we instead use
\displaystyle w=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)

Proof.

Suppose that
\displaystyle u
satisfies
\displaystyle u^{\rm{Tr}\otimes\tau}=u
. Then

\displaystyle\displaystyle v^{\sharp\otimes\tau}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=(uw)^{\sharp\otimes\tau}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=w^{\sharp}u^{\sharp\otimes\tau}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-w(w^{*}u^{\rm{Tr}\otimes\tau}w)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-u^{\rm{Tr}\otimes\tau}w

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-v\;.

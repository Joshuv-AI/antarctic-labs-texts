Distinct cytokines regulate gene expression and anti-tumor activity in regenerated CD8+ T cells from induced pluripotent stem cells
Authors: Kenta Kondo, Nobuhito Nitta, Koji Terada, Yusuke Sano, Mina Kumode, Seiji Nagano, Kyoko Masuda, Daisuke Okuzaki, Toru Miyake, Masaji Tani, Hiroshi Kawamoto, Yasutoshi Agata
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryAdoptive T cell therapy can induce tumor regression in cancer patients. Tumor-specific CD8+ T cells regenerated from induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs), termed regenerated cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs), are promising resources for adoptive T cell therapy. However, little is known about the cytokines that enhance anti-tumor activity of regenerated CTLs. In this study, we examined effects of exogenous cytokines on regenerated CTLs. We found that IL-15 and IL-21 treatment enhanced the anti-tumor activity of regenerated CTLs, and these cells showed distinct gene expression profiles. IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs exhibited early-effector-like characteristics, whereas IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs exhibited both naive- and effector-like characteristics. Furthermore, we investigated effects of cytokine transduction on regenerated CTLs. IL-2, IL-7, or IL-15 transduction, but not IL-21 transduction, enhanced the survival and cytotoxic activity of regenerated CTLs. Importantly, IL-7 transduction improved the anti-tumor activity of regenerated CTLs in vivo. These findings provide insights for the clinical application of regenerated CTLs.Subject areas: immunology, stem cells research, cancer

Introduction

Adoptive transfer of tumor-specific T cells represents a significant advancement in cancer immunotherapy. For instance, CAR-T cells have been proven to be clinically effective in treating B cell lymphoma and leukemia.1,2,3 Differentiation state of transferred T cells is a major factor determining the effectiveness of immune responses.4,5,6 T cells are generally classified as naive T cells, stem cell memory T cells, central memory T cells, effector memory T cells, and terminal effector T cells based on the expression of cell surface markers.7 T cell differentiation induced by antigen stimulation and specific cytokines increases cytotoxic activity and production of inflammatory cytokines such as IFN-γ, but decreases proliferative and survival capacity of T cells.7,8 Less-differentiated T cells, such as stem cell memory T cells and central memory T cells, have superior anti-tumor activity owing to their greater proliferative and survival capacity compared with more-differentiated T cells, such as effector memory T cells and terminal effector T cells.4,5,9,10

Cytokines affect CD8+ T cell development, differentiation, and homeostasis. IL-2, IL-7, IL-15, and IL-21 are cytokines belonging to the family with the common gamma chain (γc) as the shared receptor. IL-2 strongly induces the proliferation of CD8+ T cells and their differentiation into effector T cells with high cytotoxic activity and IFN-γ production in vitro.11 However, in adoptive immunotherapy, IL-2 negatively impacts the anti-tumor activity of CD8+ T cells because of its low proliferative ability in vivo.4,11,12 In contrast, IL-7 contributes to the homeostasis of naive and memory T cells than T cell proliferation.13 IL-15 promotes the differentiation of CD8+ T cells into memory T cell states with low cytotoxicity but high proliferative and survival potential in vivo, thereby providing superior anti-tumor activity to CD8+ T cells.4,12 IL-21 also confers superior anti-tumor activity on CD8+ T cells by inducing their differentiation into memory T cell states.11,12,14 Furthermore, recent studies have demonstrated that transduction of cytokine genes can improve the anti-tumor activity of CAR-T cells.15,16,17,18,19,20,21 Thus, cytokines appear to play critical roles in enhancing the anti-tumor activity of CD8+ T cells.

T cells derived from cancer patients sometimes become dysfunctional for various reasons, such as exhaustion and expression of immune inhibitory receptors, leading to difficulty preparing tumor-reactive T cells for adoptive immunotherapy. Therefore, CD8+ T cells derived from induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC) technologies termed regenerated cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs) are promising resources for adoptive immunotherapy.22,23 Storing frozen stocks of regenerated CTLs enables patients to receive the required number of cells when needed. To apply regenerated CTLs in cancer immunotherapy, it is necessary to rapidly expand cryopreserved regenerated CTLs in vitro and then transplant them into cancer patients. However, the specific cytokines that can induce the rapid proliferation of cryopreserved regenerated CTLs remain undetermined. Additionally, although several studies have shown that memory T cells differentiated from primary T cells by IL-15 or IL-21 have high anti-tumor activity, the association between cytokines and anti-tumor activity in regenerated CTLs remains unclear. Furthermore, little is known about the characteristics of regenerated CTLs treated with either cytokine.

Thus, in this study, we investigated how each cytokine affects the proliferation and anti-tumor activity of regenerated CTLs. Although regenerated CTLs treated with either IL-15 or IL-21 showed high anti-tumor activity in vivo, the features of the regenerated CTLs were distinct. IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs exhibited early effector-like T cell characteristics, whereas IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs showed both naive- and effector-like T cell characteristics. Moreover, we also evaluated the effects of cytokine transduction on regenerated CTLs and confirmed that only IL-7-transduced regenerated CTLs exhibited high anti-tumor activity in vivo.

Results

IL-2, IL-15, and IL-21 induce the proliferation of regenerated CTLs in vitro

In adoptive immunotherapy for cancer using regenerated CTLs, a large number of cells are required for transfer into cancer patients. To increase the number of regenerated CTLs rapidly and vigorously in vitro, we first evaluated the cytokines that induce their proliferation in vitro. For this purpose, we utilized WT1-specific regenerated CTLs (clone: WT1#3-3), which were differentiated from iPSCs derived from WT1-specific CD8+ T cell clone,24 and autologous lymphoblastoid cell lines (LCLs) as antigen-presenting cells, which were established by Epstein-Barr virus transformation of B cells. We thawed the regenerated CTLs and co-cultured them with WT1235 peptide-pulsed or unpulsed irradiated LCLs in the presence of the indicated cytokines and then analyzed the proliferation of regenerated CTLs (Figure 1A). After 7 days, cytokine-free condition, IL-7, and IL-12 barely increased the frequency of rapidly proliferating cells in co-culture with unpulsed LCLs and WT1235 peptide-pulsed LCLs (Figures 1B–1D). In contrast, IL-15 and IL-21 increased the frequency of rapidly proliferating cells in co-culture with WT1235 peptide-pulsed LCLs (Figures 1B–1D). IL-2-treated regenerated CTLs also showed an increased frequency of rapidly proliferating cells, but the difference was not statistically significant (Figures 1B–1D). Although IL-7 did not induce the proliferation of regenerated CTLs, IL-7 tended to increase the frequency of live cells compared with cytokine-free condition, but the difference was not statistically significant (Figures S1A and S1B), implying that IL-7 supports the cell viability of regenerated CTLs. Taken together, our results indicated that IL-2, IL-15, and IL-21 can induce the proliferation of regenerated CTLs, suggesting that we can utilize these cytokines to expand cryopreserved regenerated CTLs before adoptive immunotherapy.

IL-15- or IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs suppress tumor growth in vivo

Several studies have reported that regenerated CTLs exhibit anti-tumor activity in xenograft models.24,25,26,27,28 However, which cytokines confer superior anti-tumor activity on regenerated CTLs in vivo remain unknown. To evaluate the anti-tumor activity of regenerated CTLs treated with the respective cytokines, we first established WT1235 peptide-overexpressing HLA-A∗24:02+ K562 cells (hereafter referred to as WT1235-K562 cells) (Figures S2A and S2B). WT1235-specific regenerated CTLs efficiently killed the WT1235-K562 cells in an HLA-A∗24:02-dependent manner (Figure S2C), suggesting that the established WT1235-K562 cells are useful for evaluating the anti-tumor activity of regenerated CTLs.

Next, we injected 5 × 105 WT1235-K562 cells subcutaneously into the abdomen of immunodeficient NOD-scid IL2rγnull (NSG) mice on day 0 and then transferred 5 × 106 regenerated CTLs cultured with IL-2, IL-15, or IL-21 intravenously on day 7 (Figure 2A). Because the viability of transferred T cells in vivo positively correlates with anti-tumor activity,4,10,29,30 we examined the frequency of transferred regenerated CTLs in the peripheral blood of tumor-bearing NSG mice. We found that the frequency of IL-15- and IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs was higher than that of IL-2-treated cells (Figures 2B and 2C), suggesting that treatment with IL-15 or IL-21 promotes the viability of regenerated CTLs in vivo. Consistent with their superior viability, IL-15- and IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs effectively suppressed tumor growth in vivo (Figures 2D and 2E). The NSG mice transferred with IL-15- or IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs, but not IL-2-treated regenerated CTLs, displayed improved overall survival compared with the NSG mice injected with PBS (Figure 2F). These results demonstrated that IL-15- and IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs have high viability and can suppress tumor growth in vivo.

IL-15- and IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs show distinct gene expression profiles

To investigate the molecular mechanisms underlying the superior anti-tumor activity of IL-15- and IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs, we performed their transcriptome analysis using RNA sequencing. Principal-component analysis revealed that the gene expression profiles were distinct among IL-2-, IL-15-, and IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs (Figure 3A). Unsupervised hierarchical clustering classified IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs as a distinct subcluster (Figure 3B). We identified upregulated and downregulated genes in IL-15- and IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs compared with IL-2-treated regenerated CTLs (Figure 3C). Most upregulated and downregulated genes did not overlap between IL-15- and IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs (Figure 3C). Collectively, these results demonstrated that IL-15- and IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs have distinct gene expression profiles, implying that their mechanism of suppressing tumor growth is different.

IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs have early-effector signatures

Next, we performed gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) to determine the features of regenerated CTLs cultured with IL-15 or IL-21. GSEA revealed that early effector-like signatures, a set of upregulated genes in antigen-specific CD8+ T cells on day 6 versus day 10 effector after Listeria monocytogenes infection, were enriched in IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs (Figures 3D and S3A).31,32 Huang et al. reported that the IL-7Rlow CXCR3high CD8+ T cell population, termed intermediated T cells (TINT cells) possesses early effector-like signatures as shown by IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs, and has greater capacities for both effector and memory T cell responses.32 Therefore, we reperformed GSEA using gene sets that are uniquely expressed among TINT cells, terminal effector T cells marked as KLRG1high CXCR3low IL-7Rlow and memory precursor T cells marked as KLRG1low CXCR3high IL-7Rhigh.32 As expected, the gene set of TINT cells was markedly enriched in IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs, whereas the gene sets of terminal effector and memory precursor T cells were not enriched in IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs (Figure S3C). Consistent with the early-effector-like signatures enriched in IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs, the expression of genes associated with early activation, such as IRF4, BATF3, IL-2RA, CD69, and TNFRSF9 (4-1BB) was increased in IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs compared with IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs (Figure 3E middle). Flow cytometric analysis also confirmed that CD25 (IL-2RA) expression levels were increased in IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs compared with other cytokine-treated regenerated CTLs (Figure 3F). TNFRSF9 (4-1BB) expression levels were decreased in IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs but not IL-15-treated CTLs (Figure 3F). These results suggested that IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs exhibit more activated state than IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs.

T cell activation by TCR stimulation is necessary for anti-tumor activity, but excessive activation induces terminal effector T cell differentiation, resulting in the loss of proliferative and viability capacity.7 Differentiation of terminal effector T cell is induced by the expression of the transcription factors, PRDM1 and ZEB2.33,34,35,36 The expression levels of PRDM1 and ZEB2 in IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs were equivalent to those in IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs (Figure 3E middle), implying that IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs did not differentiate into the terminal effector state despite being more activated state.

Furthermore, consistent with the superior survival capacity, stem-cell-memory and central-memory signatures were partially enriched in IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs (Figure 3D). Memory T cells express a higher level of the anti-apoptotic molecule, BCL2 than naive T cells.30,37 Consistent with stem-cell-memory and central-memory signatures enriched in IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs, IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs expressed a higher level of BCL2 than IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs (Figure 3E left). These results indicated that IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs have both early effector-like and memory-like phenotypes without differentiating into the terminal effector state.

IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs have both naive-like and effector-memory-like T cell signatures

IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs exhibited early effector-like signatures, whereas IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs showed naive and effector-memory signatures (Figures 3D and S3B). Consistent with the enriched naive signatures in IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs, IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs expressed high levels of naive/memory-associated genes, such as SELL (CD62L), IL7R, CD27, CD28, FOXO1, TCF7, and BCL6 than IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs (Figure 3E left). Flow cytometric analysis confirmed that the expression of CD28 and CD62L was higher in IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs than in IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs (Figure 3F). Furthermore, consistent with the slightly enriched effector-memory signatures in IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs, these cells expressed high levels of cytotoxicity-associated genes, such as GZMA, GZMB, GZMH, GZMK, GZMM, and PRF1 compared with IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs (Figure 3E middle). In conventional murine and human T cells, expression levels of memory-associated genes are typically downregulated during effector T cell differentiation, whereas expression levels of effector-associated genes are upregulated.38 However, in IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs, both memory-like (CD28+) and effector-like (CD28−) populations exhibited higher granzyme B (GZMB) expression than other cytokine-treated regenerated CTLs (Figures 4A and 4B), implying that IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs exhibit both naive- and effector-like features within the same cells. Taken together, these results indicated that IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs display both naive-like and effector-memory-like phenotypes.

IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs have superior cytotoxic activity

Because IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs expressed high levels of cytotoxicity-associated genes (Figure 3E middle; Figure 4), we examined whether IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs efficiently kill WT1235-K562 cells. However, the cytotoxic activity of IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs against WT1235-K562 cells was lower than that of IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs (Figure 5A). IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs also expressed high levels of the immune inhibitory receptors PD-1 and LAG-3 (Figure 3E right; Figure 5B). Engagement of PD-1 by its ligands PD-L1 or PD-L2 leads to inhibition of TCR activation signaling via the recruitment of SHP-2 protein tyrosine phosphatase.39 In contrast, LAG-3 selectively interacts with stable peptide-MHC class II complexes that inhibit TCR activation signals.40,41 WT1235-K562 cells moderately expressed PD-L1 but not PD-L2 or MHC class II (Figure 5C). Thus, to investigate whether PD-1/PD-L1 interactions attenuate the cytotoxic activity of IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs, we co-cultured cytokine-treated regenerated CTLs with WT1235-K562 cells in the presence of either anti-PD-1 mAb or isotype control mAb. Treatment of anti-PD-1 mAb did not rescue the cytotoxic activity of IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs (Figure 5D). The cytotoxic activity was comparable between isotype control mAb- and anti-PD-1 mAb-treated regenerated CTLs cultured with IL-21 (Figure S4). In contrast, in both conditions, IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs exhibited higher cytotoxic activity than IL-2- and IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs (Figure 5D). Thus, we did not obtain evidence that PD-1/PD-L1 interactions contribute to the low cytotoxic activity of IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs. These results indicated that IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs have superior cytotoxic activity against WT1235 peptide-overexpressing HLA∗A2402+ K562 cells.

Cell cycle and mTORC1 signaling are enhanced in IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs

GSEA revealed that IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs exhibit early effector-like signatures, whereas IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs exhibit naive-like signatures. These findings imply that IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs are in an activated state, whereas IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs are in a dormant state. Consistent with these observations, hallmark pathway analysis revealed that cell cycle-related pathways such as E2F targets and G2M checkpoint signatures were enriched in IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs (Figure 6A). IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs also exhibited mTORC1 signaling signatures (Figure 6A), which promotes cell growth and metabolism.42,43 Consistent with these enriched gene sets, phosphorylation of the TORC1 target, S6 kinase, was increased in IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs compared with other cytokine-treated regenerated CTLs (Figures 6B and 6C). Previous studies reported that enhanced mTORC1 signaling increases cell size.44,45 As expected, the size of IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs was larger than that of other cytokine-treated regenerated CTLs (Figures 6D and 6E). Furthermore, IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs exhibited glycolytic and oxidative phosphorylation signatures (Figure 5A). Consistent with these signatures, glucose uptake and mitochondrial mass were higher in IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs than in other cytokine-treated regenerated CTLs (Figures 6F–6I). These results indicated that IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs have early-effector-likes phenotypes with enhanced cell-cycle status and mTORC1 signaling.

Establishment of regenerated CTLs with transgenic cytokine expression

We showed that cytokine treatment affects the proliferation and anti-tumor activity of regenerated CTLs. Previous studies have reported that transgenic expression of IL-7, IL-15, and IL-21 in T cells or CAR-T cells enhances their anti-tumor activity.15,16,17,18,19,20,21 Thus, we hypothesized that sustained cytokine expression would enhance anti-tumor activity of regenerated CTLs. Thus, we constructed retroviral plasmids, in which IL-2, IL-7, IL-15, or IL-21 was introduced along with EGFP (Figure S5A). Regenerated CTLs were transduced with retroviruses according to the methods described in our previous study,46 and GFP expression was monitored by flow cytometry (Figure S5B). The frequency of GFP+ cells in mock-, IL-2-, IL-7-, and IL-15-transduced regenerated CTLs remained constant during culture in the presence of IL-7 and IL-21 (Figure S5B). However, the frequency of GFP+ cells was decreased in IL-21-transduced regenerated CTLs, suggesting that IL-21 transduction is disadvantageous for the proliferation and/or survival of regenerated CTLs (Figure S5B). Therefore, we used IL-2-, IL-7-, and IL-15-transduced regenerated CTLs in further experiments. To confirm transgenic expression of the respective cytokines, we sorted GFP+ cells in IL-2-, IL-7-, and IL-21-transduced regenerated CTLs, except for mock-transduced regenerated CTLs (Figure S5B), because we could not expand mock-transduced regenerated CTLs after sorting. Mock-transduced regenerated CTLs hardly expressed IL2, IL7, and IL21 mRNA, implying that mock-retroviral transduction did not alter cytokine gene expression in regenerated CTLs (Figure S5C). In contrast, IL-2-, IL-7, and IL-21-transduced regenerated CTLs expressed high levels of their respective cytokine mRNA (Figure S5C).

To estimate cytokine production by transduced regenerated CTLs based on cell numbers, we co-cultured cytokine-transduced regenerated CTLs with WT1235 peptide-pulsed irradiated LCLs in the absence of exogenous cytokines for 7 days. As controls, mock-transduced regenerated CTLs were co-cultured with WT1235 peptide-pulsed irradiated LCLs in medium containing titrated concentration of exogenous cytokines (Figure S5D). Subsequently, we calculated the number of regenerated CTLs to estimate the concentration of cytokines produced by cytokine-transduced regenerated CTLs. The numbers of mock-transduced regenerated CTLs were increased in a cytokine-dose dependent manner (Figure S5D), supporting the use of cell numbers as a surrogate metric for cytokine concentration. The estimated concentrations were as follows: IL-2-transduced regenerated CTLs, >10 ng/mL; IL-7-transduced regenerated CTLs, 1 ng/mL to 10 ng/mL; and IL-15-transduced regenerated CTLs, 10 pg/mL to 100 pg/mL (Figure S4D).

Cytokine transduction improve expansion and survival of regenerated CTLs in vitro and in vivo

We next evaluated whether cytokine transduction improves the expansion of regenerated CTLs in vitro in the absence of exogenous cytokines. Transduced regenerated CTLs were expanded by co-culture with WT1235 peptide-pulsed irradiated LCLs in the presence of IL-7 and IL-21, which support survival and proliferation of the cells (Figures 1 and S1), according to previous report (Figure 7A),24 and then the expanded transduced regenerated CTLs were co-cultured with unpulsed or WT1235 peptide-pulsed irradiated LCLs in a cytokine-free medium (Figure 7B). On day 5 after co-culture, the number of IL-7-transduced regenerated CTLs was higher than that of mock-transduced regenerated CTLs in co-culture with WT1235 peptide-pulsed LCLs, although the difference did not reach statistical significance (Figure 7B). These results indicated that the cytokines secreted by cytokine-transduced regenerated CTLs increased the number of regenerated CTLs in vitro.

To investigate the viability of cytokine-transduced regenerated CTLs in vivo, we transferred 5 × 106 regenerated CTLs into NSG mice intravenously on day 0, and then evaluated the frequency of regenerated CTLs in their peripheral blood on days 4 and 7, and in the spleen on day 7 (Figures 7C–7E). The frequency of cytokine-transduced regenerated CTLs in peripheral blood was significantly higher than that of mock-transduced regenerated CTLs (Figures 7D and 7E). In addition, the number of cytokine-transduced regenerated CTLs in the spleen was significantly higher than that of the mock-transduced regenerated CTLs (Figures 7D and 7E). These results demonstrated that cytokine-transduced regenerated CTLs have high viability in vivo. Previous studies reported that IL-2, IL-7, and IL-15 activate the JAK/STAT5 pathway in CD8+ T cells, and STAT5 activation subsequently enhances anti-apoptotic gene BCL2 expression.47,48 Consistent with the enhanced viability and previous findings, the mRNA level of BCL2 was high in cytokine-transduced regenerated CTLs compared with mock-transduced regenerated CTLs (Figure 7F). Therefore, cytokines secreted by cytokine-transduced regenerated CTLs may enhance BCL2 expression and contribute to improved survival in vivo.

Cytokine-transduced regenerated CTLs have high cytotoxic activity in vitro

Next, we evaluated the cytotoxic activity of cytokine-transduced regenerated CTLs in vitro. When co-cultured with WT1235-K562 cells in a cytokine-free medium, cytokine-transduced regenerated CTLs killed WT1235-K562 cells more efficiently than mock-transduced regenerated CTLs (Figure 8A left). To confirm that cytokine-transduced regenerated CTLs have high cytotoxic activity against another cancer cell lines, we generated WT1235 peptide-overexpressing MIAPaCa-2 cells which are human pancreatic cell line and positive for HLA-A∗24:02.49 Cytokine-transduced regenerated CTLs also killed WT1235-MIAPaCa-2 cells more efficiently than mock-transduced regenerated CTLs (Figure 8A right). However, the mRNA levels of GZMA, GZMB, and PRF1 in cytokine-transduced regenerated CTLs were comparable to those in mock-transduced regenerated CTLs (Figure 8B). Thus, despite similar mRNA expression levels of GZMA, GZMB, and PRF1, the high cytotoxic activity of cytokine-transduced regenerated CTLs may be reflect sustained cancer cell killing by the improved viability of cytokine-transduced regenerated CTLs.

Anti-tumor activity of cytokine-transduced regenerated CTLs

IL-7-transduced regenerated CTLs suppress tumor growth in mice

To investigate the anti-tumor activity of cytokine-transduced regenerated CTLs in vivo, we injected 5 × 105 WT1235-K562 cells to NSG mice subcutaneously on day 0 and then transferred 1 × 107 transduced regenerated CTLs into tumor-bearing NSG mice intravenously on days 7 and 10 (Figure 8C). Consistent with the results of naive NSG mice, the frequencies of cytokine-transduced regenerated CTLs in the blood of tumor-bearing NSG mice on day 14 was also significantly higher than that of mock-transduced regenerated CTLs (Figure 8D). Adoptive transfer of mock-transduced regenerated CTLs suppressed tumor growth compared with the PBS group (Figure 8E). Of note, tumor volumes in NSG mice transferred with IL-7-, but not IL-2- or IL-15-, transduced regenerated CTLs were significantly smaller than those of mock-transduced regenerated CTLs (Figure 8E). In addition, overall survival was significantly prolonged in mice receiving IL-7-transduced regenerated CTLs compared with mock-transduced regenerated CTLs (Figure 8F). Finally, to investigate underlying mechanisms of the improved anti-tumor activity of IL-7-transduced regenerated CTLs, we examined dynamics of transduced regenerated CTLs in tumor-bearing NSG mice. Transduced regenerated CTLs were transferred into tumor-bearing NSG mice, and donor cells were analyzed in the spleen and tumor on day 3 after adoptive transfer (Figure S6A). IL-7-transduced regenerated CTLs were clearly detected in the spleen and tumor compared with mock-transduced regenerated CTLs (Figure S6B), and the frequencies of GFP+ donor cells in spleen and tumor were significantly higher than before adoptive transfer (Figures S6B and S6C). Notably, the total number of IL-7-transduced regenerated CTLs was significantly increased in the tumor (Figure S6D). IL-7 transduction increased both GFP+ transduced and GFP− untransduced regenerated CTLs within the tumor (Figure S6D), implying that IL-7 secreted from transduced regenerated CTLs supports the viability and/or proliferation of surrounding untransduced regenerated CTLs. Taken together these results, IL-7-transduced regenerated CTLs have higher anti-tumor activity in vivo than mock-, IL-2-, and IL-15-transduced regenerated CTLs.

Discussion

Several studies have reported that regenerated CTLs from iPSCs can suppress tumor growth in xenograft models.24,25,26,27,28,50 The phenotypes and anti-tumor activity of CD8+ T cells treated with each cytokine in vitro have been defined in mice and humans. However, the characteristics of regenerated CTLs treated with each cytokine in vitro remain unclear. In the present study, we demonstrated that IL-15- and IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs exhibited prolonged persistence in vivo and suppressed tumor growth in a xenograft model, compared with IL-2-treated regenerated CTLs. Although both IL-15- and IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs showed the superior anti-tumor activity, these cells had distinct gene expression profiles. IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs exhibited early effector-like phenotypes, whereas IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs exhibited naive-like and effector-memory-like phenotypes. These findings suggested that IL-15 and IL-21 promote the anti-tumor activity of regenerated CTLs via different mechanisms by altering their gene expression profiles.

Several studies have reported that IL-15 or IL-21-treated CD8+ T cells in vitro possess superior survival capacity and anti-tumor activity in vivo compared with IL-2-treated CD8+ T cells.4,12,14 IL-15-treated CD8+ T cells show increased expression of naive and memory T cell-associated genes, such as SELL, CCR7, CD27, and TCF74,12 and exhibit memory T cell-like metabolic activities, such as reduction of mTORC1 signaling and enhancement of oxidative phosphorylation.12,51,52 IL-21-treated CD8+ T cells also show increased expression of naive and memory T cell-associated genes and preferentially use oxidative phosphorylation rather than glycolysis.11,14,53,54,55,56 Thus, IL-15 and IL-21 treatment have been thought to enhance the anti-tumor activity of CD8+ T cells by promoting the differentiation of memory T cells in mice and humans.

Similar to mouse and human CD8+ T cells, we demonstrated that IL-15- and IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs have the superior anti-tumor activity compared with IL-2-treated regenerated CTLs. However, IL-15- and IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs displayed different phenotypes from mouse and human CD8+ T cells. IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs exhibited early effector-like signatures, enhanced mTORC1 signaling, glycolysis, and oxidative phosphorylation. In addition, IL-15 barely induced the expression of memory T cell-associated genes, such as SELL, CCR7, and CD28. In contrast, IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs exhibited naive-like signatures and expressed high levels of naive/memory T cell-associated genes, similar to mouse and human CD8+ T cells treated with IL-21.11,14 As expected from the naive-like signatures, IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs exhibited lower mitochondrial mass than IL2-and IL-15-regenerated CTLs, which is consistent with a previous report showing that naive CD8+ T cells have lower mitochondrial mass than memory CD8+ T cells.57 Thus, the less-differentiated-like phenotype may contribute to the anti-tumor activity of regenerated CTLs. Interestingly, despite naive-like signatures, IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs highly express the cytotoxicity- and exhaustion-associated genes. In general, naive CD8+ T cells hardly express cytotoxicity- and exhaustion-associated genes. Antigen stimulation induces naive CD8+ T cells to lose the expression of naive/memory T cells-associated genes and to gain the expression of cytotoxicity- and exhaustion-associated genes.7,10 Therefore, the gene expression profiles of IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs are unique, suggesting that the characteristics of mouse and human CD8+ T cells cannot be completely applied to regenerated CTLs.

The initial method for generating regenerated CTLs from iPSCs was associated with the issue that most of these cells exhibited characteristics resembling innate-like T cells expressing CD8αα. Consequently, Maeda et al. demonstrated an improved method to generate CD8αβ-expressing regenerated CTLs by sorting CD4+ CD8α+ (DP) cells from iPSCs.24 On the other hand, Kawai et al. also reported that IL-21 can improve the generation of CD8αβ-expressing regenerated CTLs without sorting DP cells.28 The regenerated CTLs in the presence of IL-21 express high levels of naive-associated surface molecules, such as CD27, CD28, CD62L, and CCR7.28 The present study also found that IL-21 induces CD28 and CD62L expressions on the cell surface of established regenerated CTLs from sorted DP cells (Figure 3F). Although IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs expressed these naive-associated molecules on their cell surface, they also expressed cytotoxicity-associated genes such as GZMA and PRF1 (Figure 3E), suggesting that IL-21-treated regenerated CTLs are not functionally naive T cells. In contrast, we demonstrated that IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs expressed the typical terminal effector-like cell surface markers, CD62Llow, IL-7Rlow, CD27low, CD28low, IL2Rahigh, and HAVCR2 (TIM-3)high. However, their intrinsic gene expression profiles and phenotypes differed from those of terminal effector T cells. Indeed, IL-15-treated regenerated CTLs exhibited early effector-like signatures, high viability, and high anti-tumor activity. These findings suggested that phenotypes of regenerated CTLs cannot be classified using only surface molecules.

Based on the results of cytokine-treated regenerated CTLs, we retrovirally transduced regenerated CTLs with IL-2, IL-7, IL-15, or IL-21 to improve their anti-tumor activity. However, because IL-21 transduction significantly reduced the viability of regenerated CTLs, we did not use IL-21-transduced regenerated CTLs in further experiments. In contrast, in human CAR-T cells, IL-21 transduction enhanced their anti-tumor activity in a mouse xenograft model without impairing the viability and/or proliferation in vitro.15 Thus, phenotypes of IL-21-transduced cells differed markedly between regenerated CTLs and conventional human CTLs. We demonstrated that IL-2, IL-7, and IL-15 transduction enhanced the viability of regenerated CTLs and promoted the cytotoxic activity of regenerated CTLs. However, only IL-7-transduced regenerated CTLs suppressed tumor growth in vivo, but the reason behind this phenomenon is unclear. In addition, we cultured transduced regenerated CTLs with exogenous IL-7 and IL-21 to support survival and proliferation. The combinational effects between exogenous cytokines and those produced by the transduced cells were not evaluated.

Contrary to our results, previous studies have reported that CAR-T cells secreting IL-15 have high anti-tumor activity in vivo.15,16,18,58 Differences in responsiveness to cytokines may account for this discrepancy between IL15-transduced regenerated CTLs and CAR-T cells. In regenerated CTLs, IL-15 treatment increases mTORC1 signaling and enriched early-effector-like signatures, whereas in CAR-T cells IL-15 treatment decreases mTORC1 signaling and promotes differentiation into memory T cell state.12 Although mTORC1 signaling is required for T cell activation, sustained mTORC1 signaling promotes T cell exhaustion.12 Thus, one possible explanation for the lack of anti-tumor activity in vivo observed in IL-15-transduced regenerated CTLs might be exhaustion caused by sustained mTORC1 signaling.

Our results revealed that the effects of cytokines on the anti-tumor activity differ between cytokine-treated and cytokine-transduced regenerated CTLs. Exogenous IL-15 and IL-21 treatment, but not IL-15- or IL-21 transduction, enhanced the anti-tumor activity of regenerated CTLs. A recent study reported that IL-2-transduced CD8+ T cells and IL-2-treated CD8+ T cells exhibit different phenotypes.59 IL-2-transduced CD8+ T cells showed suppressed STAT5 phosphorylation upon exposure to exogenous IL-2, and displayed memory T cell-like gene expression profiles, whereas untransduced CD8+ T cells were induced to phosphorylate STAT5 upon exposure to exogenous IL-2, and displayed effector T cell-like gene expression profiles. Although the mechanism by which transgenic expression of IL-2 alters the phenotype of CD8+ T cells remains unknown,59 the properties of IL-15- and IL-21-transduced regenerated CTLs may differ from those of regenerated CTLs treated with exogenous IL-15 or IL-21.

In conclusion, our study provides the first evidence that IL-15 and IL-21 can confer superior anti-tumor activity on regenerated CTLs by altering their gene expression profiles. Compared with conventional mouse and human T cells, regenerated CTLs exhibit unique gene expression profiles after cytokine treatment. Thus, efficient methods to assess the subsets and functions of regenerated CTLs need to be developed. Additionally, IL-7 transduction improved the anti-tumor activity of regenerated CTLs. These results highlight that the properties of regenerated CTLs differ between the treatment with exogenous cytokine and transgenic expression of cytokines. Our findings provide valuable insights for the clinical application of regenerated CTLs in the future.

Limitations of the study

In this study, we showed that cytokine-treated regenerated CTLs exhibit phenotypes and anti-tumor activity that differ from those previous reported for cytokine-treated conventional CD8+ T cells. However, the relationship between phenotypes and anti-tumor activity of cytokine-treated regenerated CTLs remains to be determined. Although IL-15 treatment promotes mTORC1 signaling, we did not directly perturb the mTORC1 pathway by genetic deletion or pharmacological inhibition. In addition, systematic comparisons of cytokine-receptor expression and downstream signaling between regenerated CTLs and conventional CD8+ T cells were not performed, which may contribute to differences in cytokine responsiveness.

We also evaluated the anti-tumor activity of cytokine-transduced regenerated CTLs. Unexpectedly, cytokine transduction did not uniformly enhanced anti-tumor activity. Only IL-7 transduction improved the anti-tumor activity of regenerated CTLs, but not IL-15 transduction. Moreover, although IL-21 treatment promoted proliferation of regenerated CTLs, IL-21 transduction reduced their proliferation and/or survival. The intracellular synthesis and secretion of retrovirally expressed cytokines may modulate the state of regenerated CTLs. Pharmacological inhibition of these pathway may help elucidate the different outcomes between cytokine-treated and cytokine-transduced regenerated CTLs. Furthermore, following to a previous study,24 we cultured cytokine-transduced regenerated CTLs with exogenous IL-7 and IL-21. Although these exogenous cytokines support survival and proliferation of regenerated CTLs, we did not systematically evaluate the combinatorial effects between exogenous cytokines and those produced by transduced regenerated CTLs. Future studies are needed to determine how exogenous and transgenically produced cytokines interact in regenerated CTLs.

Method details

Construction of retroviral plasmids

To construct the pMXs-EGFP-p2A-luciferase plasmid, EGFP and luciferase genes were amplified by PCR and assembled into the XhoI and SalI sites of pMXs-IRES-EGFP using the Gibson Assembly Master Mix (New England Biolabs, E2621S). Subsequently, the WT1235 peptide sequence (CYTWNQMNL) was ligated into the EcoRI and XhoI sites of the pMXs-EGFP-p2A-luciferase plasmid to construct the pMXs-WT1235 peptide-EGFP-p2A-luciferase plasmid.

To construct cytokine (hIL-2, hIL-7, hIL-15 and hIL-21)-encoding retroviral plasmids, each cytokine gene amplified by PCR was fused with the human immunoglobulin kappa leader sequence (IgkL) and Flag-tag, and then inserted into the pENTR1A plasmid (Thermo Fisher Scientific) using the Gibson Assembly Master Mix. The IgkL-Flag-cytokine gene blocks were transferred to pMXs-RfA-IRES-EGFP using the Gateway LR reaction (Thermo Fisher Scientific, 11791-020). The resulting plasmid, pMXs-IgkL-Flag-cytokine-IRES-EGFP, was used for retrovirus production.

Retroviral production and transduction

To produce retrovirus for the establishment of WT1235 peptide-overexpressing cell lines, the pMXs-WT1235 peptide-EGFP-p2A-luciferase plasmid, was transfected into Plat-E cells, following the methods described in a previous report.46 The culture supernatants collected 60 hours after transfection were used to infect K562 cells and MIAPaCa-2 cells in the presence of polybrene (4 μg/ml; Sigma, H9268) at 1,220 × g at 32°C for 90 min. After infection, the GFP+ K562 and MIAPaCa-2 cells were sorted using FACSAriaTM Fusion, and then the sorted GFP+ cells were used for analysis.

To produce retrovirus for the generation of cytokine-transduced regenerated CTLs, pMXs-IgkL-Flag-cytokine-IRES-EGFP was transfected into Plat-E cells. Culture supernatants collected 72 h after transfection were used to infect PG13 cells in the presence of polybrene. The supernatants of PG13 cells collected 14–21 days after infection were used to transduce regenerated CTLs, following the methods described in a previous report.46 After 7 days of transduction, GFP+ regenerated CTLs were sorted using FACSAriaTM Fusion. Then, the sorted GFP+ cells were expanded by stimulation with WT1235 peptide-pulsed irradiated LCLs in the presence of hIL-7 (5 ng/mL), hIL-21 (10 ng/mL), and ascorbic acid (100 μM) for further analysis.

In vitro proliferation assay

Regenerated CTLs were labeled with 1.5 μM CellTrace violet (Invitrogen). Labeled regenerated CTLs (1×105 cells for Figure 1, 2×105 cells for Figure 7B) were cultured with WT1235 peptide-pulsed or un-pulsed irradiated LCLs (0.5×105 cells for Figure 1, 2×105 cells for Figure 7B) in the presence of the indicated cytokines for 5 or 7 days. The cultured regenerated CTLs were stained with monoclonal antibodies (mAbs), and then analyzed using flow cytometry.

The cell numbers were determined using an automated cell counter (TC20TM, Bio-Rad) or flow cytometry. For cell counting by flow cytometry, 1×104 EG7 cells were added to the cultured regenerated CTLs. These cells were stained with anti-hCD8α and anti-CD45 mAb and then analyzed by flow cytometry. The number of regenerated CTLs (hCD8α+) were calculated as follows: the number of hCD8α+ cells / the number of mCD45+ cells × 1×104.

In vitro cytotoxicity assay using the luciferase assay

Regenerated CTLs and luciferase-expressing target cells (1 × 104) were seeded in 96-well plates at an indicated effector to target (E:T) ratio and cultured for 16-20 hours in the absence of recombinant cytokines. For PD-1 blocking, regenerated CTLs and luciferase-expressing target cells (1 × 104) were co-cultured in the presence of 10 μg/mL anti-PD1 mAb (EH12.2H7) or isotype mAb (MOPC-21) at an indicated effector to target (E:T) ratio for 16 hours. The luciferase activity was measured using the Bright-Glo™ Luciferase Assay System (Promega, E2610) and expressed as relative light units (RLU). Target cells alone were cultured alone to measure the maximal luciferase activity (RLUmax). The percentage of lysis was calculated as follows: % lysis = (1 − (RLU)/(RLUmax)) × 100.

In vivo anti-tumor activity assay for cytokine-treated regenerated CTLs

On day 0, 5×105 WT1235-K562 cells were subcutaneously injected into the lateral abdomen of the mice. On days 7, tumor engraftment was confirmed by measuring the tumor size, and mice were uniformly allocated to the control (PBS) or cytokine-treated regenerated CTLs group based on the tumor size. Thereafter, the mice were intravenously injected with PBS or 5×106 cytokine-treated regenerated CTLs. For ethical considerations, tumor-bearing mice were sacrificed when the tumor reached 20 mm in size. Tumor size was measured every 2 days and tumor volume was calculated using the formula ab2/2 (a, width; b, length) as described previously.46

In vivo analysis of cytokine-transduced regenerated CTLs

To investigate the viability of cytokine-transduced regenerated CTLs in vivo, 5×106 transduced regenerated CTLs were intravenously injected into NSG mice on day 0. On days 4 and 7, peripheral blood cells and spleen cells were analyzed using flow cytometry. The number of regenerated CTLs per spleen was calculated from the total live cell count and the percentage of hCD45+ hCD8α+ PI- (propidium iodide, FUJIFILM) cells.

To evaluate the anti-tumor activity of cytokine-transduced regenerated CTLs in vivo, 5×105 WT1235-K562 cells were suspended in 50 μL of Matrigel (Corning, 356237) plus 50 μL of PBS and were injected subcutaneously into the lateral abdomen of mice on day 0. On days 7 and 10, mice were injected intravenously with PBS or 1×107 transduced regenerated CTLs. The tumor volume was calculated every 2 days. To analyze the transferred regenerated CTLs in the tumor-bearing mice, their tumors and spleens were cut into small pieces, and treated with ACK lysis buffer. The cells were stained with mAbs and analyzed by flow cytometry.

Flow cytometry

The mAbs used for flow cytometric analyses are listed in key resources table. PI and Fixable Viability Stain 620 was used to distinguish between live and dead cells. For analyzing surface molecules, a single cell suspension was prepared in 1% FBS/PBS, followed by staining with mAbs for 60 min at 4°C. For pS6 staining, cells stained with surface molecules were fixed and permeabilized with 2% paraformaldehyde and ice-cold 90% methanol. The permeabilized cells were then incubated with anti-p6S antibody for 60 min at 4°C. For GZMB staining, cells stained with surface molecules were fixed with 2% PFA for 20 min at 4°C, and then permeabilized with 0.1 % saponin buffer. The permeabilized cells were stained with anti-GZMB Ab for 60 min at 4°C. To evaluate glucose uptake, cells were incubated with 50 μmol/L 2-NBDG (Peptide Institute) in 20%FBS/αMEM for 30 min at 37°C. To measure mitochondrial mass, cells were incubated with 100nmol/L MitoTracker™ Green (Thermo Fisher Scientific) in 20% FBS/αMEM for 30 min at 37°C. Data were acquired on a FACSAriaFusion, a LSRFortessa X-20, or a FACSCant II (BD Biosciences) and were analyzed using the FlowJo software (Tree Star).

Real-time quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR)

GFP+ transduced regenerated CTLs (excluding mock-transduced CTLs) were sorted. The sorted cells were expanded by stimulation with WT1235 peptide-pulsed irradiated LCLs in the presence of IL-7 and IL-21 for 7 days. Total RNA of the cells was isolated using TRIzol reagent, and cDNA was synthesized using ReverTra Ace qPCR RT Master Mix with gDNA Remover (TOYOBO). Then, RT-qPCR was performed using the QuantiTect SYBR Green RT-PCR Master Mix (QIAGEN). Data were normalized to ACTB expression. Primer sequences are listed in key resources table.

RNA sequencing analysis

Biological triplicates of regenerated CTLs treated with IL-2, IL-15, or IL-21 were used for RNA-sequencing analysis. Total RNA was isolated using the TRIzol reagent. Library preparation was performed using a TruSeq stranded mRNA sample prep kit (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Sequencing was performed on an Illumina HiSeq 2500 platform in a 75-base single-end mode. The Illumina Casava1.8.2 software was used for base calling. The sequenced reads were mapped to the human reference genome sequences (hg19) using TopHat version 2.0.13 in combination with Bowtie2 version 2.2.3 and SAMtools version 0.1.19. The fragments per kilobase of exon per million mapped fragments (FPKMs) were calculated using Cufflinks version 2.2.1. RNA-seq data are available at the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) with accession number GEO: GSE265947.

For further analysis, FPKMs were imported into iDEP.95,61 and principal component analysis (PCA) and unsupervised hierarchical clustering were performed. In addition, differential expressed genes (DEGs) from two group comparisons were obtained using the DESeq2 package with a threshold of false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05 and fold-change > 2. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was performed using the GSEA v4.1.0 software (Broad Institute).

Quantification and statistical analysis

All the data with error bars are presented as mean ± SEM. Comparisons were assessed by one-way ANOVA, two-way ANOVA, or log-rank test using Prism 7 and 9 software (GraphPad). Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05. Sample sizes (biological or technical replicates) are provided in each figure legend. ∗p < 0.05, ∗∗p < 0.01, ∗∗∗ p < 0.001, and ∗∗∗∗ p < 0.0001.

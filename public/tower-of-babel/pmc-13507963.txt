Protonation-induced local electronic structure regulation enables confinement-conversion synergy for long-life zinc–iodine batteries
Authors: Wenxiao Bi, Likuan Teng, Ju Duan, Xinzeyu Zhang, Linchu Xu, He Liu, Yifei Zhou, Kexiang Wang, Yaozu Liao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Aqueous zinc–iodine batteries (AZIBs) have attracted increasing attention due to their high safety, high theoretical capacity and fast iodine redox kinetics. However, the dissolution and shuttle of polyiodides (I3− and I5−) in aqueous electrolytes still lead to poor cycling stability and slow iodine conversion kinetics. In this study, we synthesized a protonated porphyrin-based conjugated microporous polymer (p-por-CMP) and utilized it as an iodine-hosting cathode material (I2@p-por-CMP) for AZIBs. The protonation strategy regulates the local electronic structure and induces charge redistribution within the framework, enabling simultaneous polyiodide immobilization and reversible iodine conversion. The I2@p-por-CMP cathode delivers a reversible specific capacity of 229 mAh g−1 at 0.3 A g−1. Even at a high current density of 5 A g−1, it still exhibits an initial specific capacity of 152.9 mAh g−1, with a capacity decay rate of 0.0036% per cycle over 10 000 cycles. Additionally, the assembled pouch cell exhibits good cycling stability along with a capacity of 117 mAh g−1 at 0.2 A g−1 after 50 cycles. This work provides a new research approach for the design of high-performance AZIBs iodine-hosting materials.

Introduction

Recently, aqueous zinc–iodine batteries (AZIBs) have attracted attention due to their high safety, high theoretical capacity and abundant reserves.1,2 These advantages make AZIBs promising for large-scale energy-storage applications.3 However, the further development of AZIBs is still severely restricted by the dissolution and shuttle of polyiodide species (I3− and I5−) in aqueous electrolytes. During cycling, the continuous loss of active iodine species not only causes severe capacity fading and poor cycling stability, but also disrupts the reversible iodine conversion process.4,5

Carbon-based materials,6 including carbon cloth,7 carbon nanotubes8 and porous carbons,9 have been widely investigated as iodine hosts due to the van der Waals interactions between hosts and iodine species.10–12 However, such weak interactions are insufficient to ensure long-term cycling stability in AZIBs.13 Although heteroatom doping and pore engineering can improve iodine adsorption capability, the limited interfacial charge-transfer capability and sluggish iodine conversion kinetics still restrict their electrochemical performance.14,15 To enhance the interaction with polyiodides, recent studies have introduced linear cationic polymers as iodine-regulating hosts. These materials can immobilize I3− and I5− through electrostatic attraction, thereby alleviating the shuttle effect. However, such linear polymer systems lack interconnected porous frameworks and their adsorption sites are mainly distributed along flexible chains or terminal groups.16

To address these limitations, conjugated microporous polymers (CMPs) have been proposed as more effective iodine hosts. In contrast to linear polymers, CMPs possess extended π-conjugated frameworks and permanent microporous structures, which facilitate electron delocalization and interfacial charge transport.17,18 Moreover, their framework structure can be molecularly tailored to regulate local electronic environments, enabling simultaneous control over iodine adsorption and redox behaviour. Among CMPs building blocks, porphyrin units possess highly delocalized π-conjugated structures, excellent redox activity and abundant nitrogen active sites. These characteristics are beneficial for electron transport and polyiodide conversion.19 However, most reported porphyrin-based iodine hosts rely on metal centers or pore engineering to regulate iodine adsorption and conversion, while the intrinsic proton-responsive behaviour of metal-free porphyrin inner-ring nitrogen atoms has rarely been explored.20–22

Herein, a porphyrin-based CMP (por-CMP) was first synthesized through a Buchwald–Hartwig coupling reaction and then protonated to obtain a p-por-CMP as an iodine host for AZIBs (Scheme 1). Unlike permanently ionized porous frameworks or electrolyte-additive strategies, the intrinsic porphyrin nitrogen sites of por-CMP are directly protonated to generate localized positive-potential regions without introducing additional ionic additives or metal species. After protonation, the positively polarized nitrogen sites induce charge redistribution and regulate the local electronic structure of the conjugated framework with a more positive zeta potential (30.1 mV) and a narrower HOMO–LUMO bandgap (ΔEg = 0.24 eV). As a result, the positively charged framework strengthens electrostatic interactions with polyiodides and promotes charge-transfer complexation. These interactions effectively immobilize polyiodide intermediates and suppress shuttle reactions. Meanwhile, the narrower bandgap facilitates charge transport and lowers the charge-transfer resistance. Consequently, the p-por-CMP, as a cathodic iodine host material, exhibits faster iodine redox kinetics (5.87 × 10−3 mol s−1 m−2) and superior electrochemical performance. The I2@p-por-CMP cathode delivers a specific capacity of 229 mAh g−1 at 0.3 A g−1 with a high coulombic efficiency (CE) of 98.2%. Even at a high current density of 5 A g−1, I2@p-por-CMP retains an outstanding initial specific capacity of 152.9 mAh g−1 with an ultralow capacity decay of only 0.0036% per cycle over 10 000 cycles. Combined with in situ and ex situ characterization techniques and density functional theory (DFT) calculations, the underlying mechanism was systematically elucidated. This work clarifies the role of protonation-induced electronic regulation in coupling polyiodide confinement with iodine redox conversion, offering a feasible strategy for designing high-performance iodine hosts for AZIBs.

Results and discussion

Based on the B–H coupling reaction, a porphyrin-based conjugated microporous polymer (por-CMP) was constructed using 5,10,15,20-tetrakis(4-bromophenyl) porphyrin (TBP) and N,N,N′,N′-tetrakis(4-aminophenyl)-p-phenylenediamine (TPDA) as the monomers. Subsequent treatment with concentrated hydrochloric acid (12 M HCl) yielded a protonated por-CMP (p-por-CMP) with positively charged N sites (Fig. 1a and Scheme S1). The chemical structures of p-por-CMP and por-CMP were verified by Fourier transform infrared (FT-IR) spectroscopy, solid-state 13C cross polarization magic angle spinning nuclear magnetic resonance (13C CP/MAS NMR) and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS). After protonation, the intensity ratio between the benzenoid and quinoid rings (I1596/I1496) increases from 0.40 to 0.64, indicating increased quinonoid character in the p-por-CMP.23 This result suggests enhanced electron delocalization after protonation. Meanwhile, the C–N peak becomes broadened with a shoulder feature and a slight shift (1301 cm−1) in the p-por-CMP, confirming the successful introduction of protonation.24 As shown in Fig. S1a, solid-state 13C CP/MAS NMR spectra of the por-CMP and p-por-CMP exhibit three distinct main resonances at ∼139 ppm (substituted aromatic carbons), ∼127 ppm (unsubstituted aromatic carbons in the framework) and ∼119 ppm (methine carbons of the porphyrin units).25 In addition, the peaks at ∼145.2 ppm and ∼115.2 ppm can be assigned to the quaternary carbon of the organic framework and β-pyrrolic carbon24 (Fig. S1b). In comparison, the p-por-CMP retains similar peak positions and spectral profiles within the same chemical-shift region, indicating that the protonation process predominantly occurs at the nitrogen active sites without disrupting the conjugated framework structure.26 Consistent evidence is provided by the high-resolution X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (HR-XPS) N 1s spectra (Fig. 1c). For the por-CMP, the peaks at 398.3 and 400.5 eV are attributable to –NC– and –NH– in the porphyrin ring, while the peak at 399.8 is attributable to the C–N bond formed by the B–H reaction.27,28 In contrast, the p-por-CMP exhibits two additional peaks located at 401.9 (–NH+–) and 402.3 eV (NH+–), corroborating the protonated nitrogen sites.25,29 Consistently, zeta-potential measurements reveal a more positive zeta potential for the p-por-CMP (30.1 mV) than for the por-CMP (11.3 mV) (Fig. 1d). The increased zeta potential confirms the formation of positively charged sites, providing favorable electrostatic interactions toward polyiodides.30 To confirm the stability of the protonated structure under practical conditions, the p-por-CMP was immersed in solutions with different pH values and subsequently characterized by N 1s XPS (Fig. S2). The protonated nitrogen sites remain clearly detectable at pH < 6.5, a range wider than that reported for porphyrin protonation systems.31

The effect of protonation on the porous and electronic characteristics was then examined. Nitrogen adsorption–desorption measurements at 77 K further reveal hierarchical porosity in both samples (Fig. S3). At low relative pressure, both materials display type-I adsorption behavior, confirming abundant microporosity, whereas the type-IV feature at higher pressure suggests the coexistence of mesopores.32 The calculated Brunauer–Emmett–Teller (BET) specific surface area decreases from 586 m2 g−1 for the por-CMP to 498 m2 g−1 for the p-por-CMP, which can be attributed to partial pore occupation or slight framework contraction induced by protonation. As shown in Fig. 1e, the pore size distribution of the two CMPs was analyzed using the nonlocal density functional theory (NLDFT) model. After protonation, the mesopores (2–5 nm) in the p-por-CMP are significantly reduced, while more micropores are generated, especially pores smaller than 1 nm. The increased fraction of micropores in the p-por-CMP favors size-selective confinement of I3−and I5−.33 To distinguish the contribution of electrostatic interactions from possible structural changes, a deprotonated sample (d-por-CMP) was prepared by immersing the p-por-CMP in a pH 10 buffer solution. As shown in Fig. S4, the d-por-CMP exhibits a comparable BET surface area (492 m2 g−1) to that of the p-por-CMP, with a slight decrease in the micropore fraction. Powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) patterns also support this interpretation (Fig. S5). The por-CMP shows a broad peak between 10° and 30°, characteristic of an amorphous skeleton. After protonation, the weakened broad peak after protonation points to a slight decrease in structural ordering. Tauc plots derived from Kubelka–Munk-transformed solid-state ultraviolet-visible (UV-vis) spectra show that the optical bandgap decreases from 1.64 eV for the por-CMP to 1.32 eV for the p-por-CMP (Fig. S6a and b), indicating that protonation effectively regulates the local electronic structure and facilitates electron transport. The above conclusion was further verified using DFT calculations. The molecular electrostatic potential (MESP) images of the por-CMP and p-por-CMP are shown in Fig. S7 where the blue regions represent electron-deficient areas and the red regions correspond to electron-rich areas. After protonation, the p-por-CMP exhibits more pronounced positive electrostatic potential around the nitrogen sites, consistent with enhanced positive polarization around the protonated nitrogen sites. The pronounced positive electrostatic potential around the protonated N sites strengthens interfacial coupling with polyiodides (I3− and I5−) and promotes charge transfer during iodine conversion. Moreover, the p-por-CMP exhibits a much narrower HOMO–LUMO gap (ΔEg = 0.24 eV) than the por-CMP (ΔEg = 2.33 eV), suggesting facilitated iodine (I−/I2) conversion kinetics (Fig. 1f).34,35

To evaluate the ability of the two CMP frameworks to confine iodine, iodine adsorption–desorption measurements were first performed (Fig. S8). Both materials exhibited high iodine adsorption capacities under iodine vapor, reaching saturated uptakes of 3.8 g g−1 for the p-por-CMP and 3.5 g g−1 for the por-CMP, which are calculated using eqn (S1). Based on eqn (S2), after natural desorption under ambient conditions for 24 h, 92.1% and 91.4% of the adsorbed iodine remained in the p-por-CMP and por-CMP. These results suggest that the majority of the adsorbed iodine is retained by the CMP frameworks after removal of weakly adsorbed surface iodine. The increased iodine retention ratio indicates that the enhanced positive surface charge strengthens the charge-transfer complexation between the p-por-CMP framework and anionic iodine species.36 The scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) elemental mapping of both the p-por-CMP and por-CMP after iodine loading (I2@p-por-CMP and I2@por-CMP) reveals that C, N and I are uniformly distributed throughout the framework (Fig. 2a and S9). Compared with the pristine CMPs, two additional peaks (at 619 and 630 eV) were detected in the XPS spectra of I2@p-por-CMP and I2@por-CMP, confirming the effective loading of iodine species in both frameworks (Fig. 2b). The HR-XPS I 3d spectrum of I2@p-por-CMP and I2@por-CMP reveals the existence of I3− (618.7 and 630.3 eV) and I5− (619.8 and 631.3 eV) in the framework (Fig. 2c).37 Raman spectra of pure I2 and the two CMPs after loading iodine were collected to further probe the confined iodine species. As shown in Fig. S10, the elemental iodine at 180.8 cm−1 disappears, while new peaks at 106.8 and 142.3 cm−1 assigned to I3− and a peak at 167.4 cm−1 corresponding to I5− emerge, confirming that iodine species are present within the framework in the form of polyiodide ions.38 To verify their suitability for use in aqueous batteries, UV-vis spectroscopy was conducted using a diluted 0.1 mM I3− solution after adsorption for 30 min (Fig. 2d). The characteristic absorption peaks of I3− show a more pronounced decrease in the presence of p-por-CMP than in the presence of por-CMP. Meanwhile, the enhanced I− signal implies facilitated I−/I3− conversion in the protonated framework. Fig. 2e presents the time-dependent color evolution of I3− solutions after the addition of equal amounts of p-por-CMP and por-CMP. After 12 h, the I3− solution containing the p-por-CMP became noticeably lighter than that containing the por-CMP, indicating stronger polyiodide immobilization by the protonated framework. This observation is consistent with the UV-vis results.

To evaluate the advantage of introducing protonated sites into the framework, I2@p-por-CMP and I2@por-CMP were assembled in coin cells to evaluate the electrochemical performance as cathodes for AZIBs within a voltage window of 0.5–1.6 V. Consistent with literature reports, the conversion of I2 to I− follows a two-step reaction pathway, namely, 3I2 + 2e− ↔ 2I3− and I3− + 2e−↔3I−.39 As shown in the cyclic voltammetry (CV) curve of I2@por-CMP at 0.2 mV s−1 (Fig. 3a), the major redox peak at 1.36/1.26 V is assigned to the I−/I3−conversion, while a slight redox peak at 1.24/1.17 V corresponds to the I3−/I2 conversion. In contrast, these two redox couples merge into a broader peak in the I2@p-por-CMP cathode, with lower oxidation and reduction peak potentials (1.29/1.23 V). This behavior suggests reduced electrochemical polarization during the sequential iodine conversion process. The positively charged nitrogen sites introduced by protonation strengthen the electrostatic interaction with I3− and I5−, suppressing polyiodide diffusion while promoting their reversible conversion within the cathode framework.40–42 To clarify the influence of iodine loading on electronic transport, the electrical conductivities (σe) of the p-por-CMP and I2@p-por-CMP were compared. Based on eqn (S3), the σe increases from 0.46 × 10−11 S m−1 for the p-por-CMP to 0.65 × 10−7 S m−1 for I2@p-por-CMP (Fig. S11a). The conductivity of p-por-CMP increases markedly after iodine loading, suggesting that the confined iodine species participate in charge transport within the conjugated framework. It is noteworthy that the conductivities of the por-CMP and I2@por-CMP are 0.5 × 10−13 S m−1 and 0.23 × 10−7 S m−1, both lower than that of the protonated materials (Fig. S11b). The higher conductivity of the protonated framework highlights the role of local electronic structure regulation in facilitating charge transport, which is more conducive to enhancing electrochemical performance. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was conducted to further probe the interfacial and diffusion kinetics of the two samples (Fig. S12a). The Nyquist curve of the I2@p-por-CMP cathode exhibits a smaller semicircle diameter in the high-frequency range and a steeper slope in the low-frequency range. Furthermore, DRT analysis was used to calculate the relevant impedances, including contact resistance (Rb), interfacial resistance (RCEI) and charge transfer resistance (Rct) (Fig. S12b).43,44 The results indicate that I2@p-por-CMP exhibits lower impedance across all characteristic processes (Rb = 1.4 Ω, RCEI = 54 Ω and Rct = 29 Ω) than I2@por-CMP (Rb = 1.7 Ω, RCEI = 71.2 Ω and Rct = 37 Ω) (Table S1). The reduced impedance indicates that protonation improves interfacial charge-transfer kinetics and facilitates ion transport. Notably, compared with the single broad RCEI peak of I2@por-CMP, two partially resolved peaks are observed for I2@p-por-CMP. This peak splitting can be attributed to the strengthened electrostatic confinement after protonation, which reduces the repeated exchange of polyiodides between the framework and the electrolyte. As a result, the interfacial responses exhibit better-separated characteristic relaxation times and can be resolved as two partially resolved peaks in the RCEI region.45,46

The cycling stability of the two cathodes was further assessed at 0.3 A g−1 (Fig. S13). After 100 cycles, I2@por-CMP exhibits a lower capacity of 194 mAh g−1 (calculated based on the mass of iodine loading) due to the partial dissolution and shuttle of polyiodides in the electrolyte. In contrast, I2@p-por-CMP delivers a specific capacity of 229 mAh g−1 with a high CE of 98.2%. The improved capacity and high CE originate from the synergistic effect of enhanced electronic conductivity and effective polyiodide immobilization induced by protonation. Electrostatic interactions effectively immobilize polyiodides and suppress their shuttle, while charge-transfer complexation facilitates reversible iodine conversion.47 The galvanostatic charge–discharge (GCD) curves at 0.3 A g−1 further show that the I2@p-por-CMP cathode delivers a lower overpotential (0.09 V) than I2@por-CMP (0.14 V), indicating reduced reaction polarization and faster redox kinetics (Fig. 3b).48 The rate performances were further evaluated at different current densities from 0.3 to 5 A g−1. As shown in Fig. 3c, the I2@p-por-CMP cathode delivers capacities of 212, 200, 187, 162 and 146 mAh g−1 at 0.3, 0.5, 1, 3 and 5 A g−1, higher than those of I2@por-CMP (184, 174, 163, 144 and 132 mAh g−1). The excellent rate performance can be attributed to the confinement-conversion synergistic interface, which provides confined reaction environments (–NH+– and NH+–) and accelerated interfacial charge-transfer kinetics at high current densities.49 Moreover, compared with previously reported systems,9,21,50–56 I2@p-por-CMP exhibits outstanding electrochemical rate performance (Fig. 3d and Table S2). Long-term cycling tests of the two CMPs were carried out at 5 A g−1 (Fig. 3e). The I2@p-por-CMP cathode exhibits a higher initial capacity of 152.9 mAh g−1 than I2@por-CMP (120 mAh g−1). After 10 000 cycles, a reversible capacity of 98.2 mAh g−1 is retained with a low-capacity decay of 0.0036% per cycle. To further evaluate the electrochemical contribution of protonation, the I2@d-por-CMP cathode was investigated under identical conditions. Despite retaining a similar porous framework, I2@d-por-CMP delivers an initial capacity of only 68.7 mAh g−1 at 5 A g−1 (Fig. S14), which is substantially lower than that of I2@p-por-CMP. Therefore, the excellent cycling stability can be attributed to the electrostatic confinement of polyiodides and the accelerated iodine conversion induced by protonation. Relative to previously reported systems,9,52,53,56–59 I2@p-por-CMP thus delivers a longer cycling lifespan at a higher current density (Fig. S15 and Table S3). To assess the practical feasibility of the I2@p-por-CMP cathode, a pouch cell was further assembled as shown in Fig. 3f with a loading of 1.25 mg cm−2. Notably, the pouch cell can continuously power electronic devices, highlighting its potential for practical energy-storage applications (Fig. 3g and Video S1–S3). The cycling stability was further evaluated. The pouch cell delivers an initial open-circuit voltage of 1.35 V and retains a specific capacity of 117 mAh g−1 at a current density of 0.2 A g−1 after 50 cycles (Fig. 3h).

Electrochemical kinetic analyses were further performed to investigate the iodine-storage behavior and ion diffusion kinetics. The CV curves of both cathodes were collected at scan rates from 0.2 to 1.0 mV s−1 to investigate the iodine-storage behavior (Fig. 4a and S16a). Both cathodes exhibit well-defined redox peaks at ∼1.25/1.35 V. Notably, in the CV curves of I2@p-por-CMP, even at high scan rates, the redox peaks remain highly symmetric with minimal polarization, suggesting a fast and reversible I−/I2 conversion process.60 The ion storage characteristics were further analyzed by fitting the relationship between peak current and scan rate based on eqn (S4). The b values of the I2@por-CMP cathode are 0.82 and 0.91, both of which are significantly higher than 0.5, indicating dominant pseudocapacitive-controlled charge storage behavior (Fig. S16b).61,62 However, the b values of I2@p-por-CMP are higher with 0.86 and 0.99 (Fig. 4b) due to the regulated local electronic environment after protonation. Such pseudocapacitive-dominated behavior is beneficial for fast charge transfer and reduced diffusion limitation under high-rate conditions. Based on eqn (S5), the pseudocapacitive contributions were calculated at different scan rates. As the scan rate increased, the pseudocapacitive contribution of I2@por-CMP gradually increased to 81.5%, 86.3%, 87.2%, 89.4% and 89.4% (Fig. S16c). In comparison, I2@p-por-CMP exhibits a higher pseudocapacitive contribution, at the corresponding scan rates, which is 84.4%, 89.3%, 90.6%, 93.2% and 96.3% respectively (Fig. 4c). The higher pseudocapacitive contribution can be attributed to the confinement-conversion synergistic interface, which promotes rapid interfacial charge transfer and reversible iodine conversion.

The galvanostatic intermittent titration technique (GITT) was performed to further evaluate ion diffusion kinetics after protonation (Fig. 4d). As shown in Fig. 4e, the calculated Dions values based on eqn (S6) of I2@por-CMP and I2@p-por-CMP are ∼8.6 × 10−10 and ∼1.1 × 10−9 cm2 s−1. This improvement originates from protonation-induced charge redistribution, which strengthens interfacial coupling with polyiodides and facilitates ion diffusion during reversible iodine conversion. Furthermore, based on Faraday's law (eqn (S7)–(S10)),63,64 the calculated reaction rate (ν) increases from 4.87 × 10−3 mol s−1 m−2 (I2@por-CMP) to 5.87 × 10−3 mol s−1 m−2 (I2@p-por-CMP) (Fig. 4f). These results suggest that protonation facilitates coupled ion and electron transport, thereby accelerating reversible iodine conversion kinetics. This kinetic advantage is consistent with the superior capacity, rate capability and cycling stability of I2@p-por-CMP.

Further analysis of the mechanism of iodine species transformation of I2@p-por-CMP cathode was conducted through in situ Raman spectroscopy and ex situ XPS. As shown in Fig. 4g and h, during the charge and discharge process three characteristic peaks located at ∼106, ∼142 and ∼167 cm−1 corresponding to I3− and I5− were observed, which are highly consistent with the observations from the I2@p-por-CMP powder. The distinct I3− signal indicates that the protonated p-por-CMP framework is effective in confining the reactivity of the reaction intermediates. Meanwhile, the I5− signal appears only at high charging voltages and gradually diminishes during discharge, indicating that it forms reversibly in a highly oxidized state. Similar results can also be observed in ex situ HR-XPS I 3d spectra (Fig. 4i). Two peaks at ∼619.6 eV and ∼631.2 eV can be observed, corresponding to the I 3d5/2 and I 3d3/2 orbitals. Furthermore, these two peaks can be deconvoluted into I− (∼619.8/631.1 eV) and I2 (∼620.9/632.5 eV).65 Compared with the initial cathode, both peaks in the charge state show a positive shift in binding energy with an increased I2/I− ratio from 0.03 to 0.82, corresponding to the oxidation of I− to I2. Upon discharge, the peaks return to lower binding energies with a decreased I2/I− ratio (0.39), demonstrating the reduction of I2. These results demonstrate that the protonation strategy constructs confinement-conversion synergistic interfaces, which suppress polyiodide migration while accelerating reversible iodine conversion through interfacial charge redistribution.66

To further clarify the role of protonation in polyiodide immobilization and iodine conversion, density functional theory (DFT) calculations were performed. As shown in Fig. 5a, the binding energies of iodine species (I2, I−, I3− and I5−) on the por-CMP and p-por-CMP were calculated.67,68 Compared with the por-CMP (−0.78, −1.54, −1.23 and −1.29 eV), the p-por-CMP exhibits much stronger binding toward I2, I−, I3− and I5− (−0.97, −16.1, −14.8 and −14.8 eV), indicating stronger interfacial coupling between the protonated framework and iodine species. Weak interaction analysis based on the independent gradient model with Hirshfeld partition (IGMH) further confirmed this conclusion (Fig. S17 and S18). Subsequently, the iodine reduction reaction (IRR) pathway was further evaluated by Gibbs free energy (ΔG) analysis (Fig. 5b). Notably, the ΔG values of the IRR process on the p-por-CMP are consistently lower than those on the por-CMP throughout the conversion process, indicating a more thermodynamically favorable iodine conversion on the protonated framework. These results demonstrate that protonation enhances the thermodynamic driving force for polyiodide conversion, thereby facilitating the reversible redox process of iodine species. Electron density difference analysis was then conducted to reveal the charge-transfer behavior at the host–iodine interface (Fig. 5c). Compared with the por-CMP, the p-por-CMP shows more pronounced charge redistribution after adsorption of iodine species. The Atomic Dipole Moment Corrected Hirshfeld (ADCH) population analysis further confirms enhanced charge transfer between the p-por-CMP and iodine species.69 These results indicate that the protonation strategy regulates the local electronic structure of the CMP framework and promotes interfacial charge transfer, which is favorable for reversible iodine conversion.

Conclusions

In summary, a protonation-induced local electronic structure regulation strategy was developed. Protonation simultaneously strengthens polyiodide immobilization and accelerates iodine conversion through interfacial charge redistribution. As a result, the I2@p-por-CMP cathode delivers a high specific capacity of 229 mAh g−1 at 0.3 A g−1, excellent rate capability (retained 146 mAh g−1 at 5 A g−1) and outstanding cycling stability (152.9 mAh g−1) with an ultralow capacity decay of only 0.0036% per cycle over 10 000 cycles. The superior electrochemical performance originates from protonation-induced confinement-conversion synergy. Electrostatic interactions provide effective polyiodide immobilization, while charge-transfer complexation and interfacial charge redistribution accelerate reversible iodine conversion. In addition, the assembled pouch cell demonstrates the practical application potential of p-por-CMP as a cathode material for AZIBs. In situ/ex situ characterization and DFT calculations further validated the role of the protonation strategy in enhancing the confinement ability of polyiodides and revealed the mechanism of iodine transformation. This work demonstrates that protonation-induced local electronic structure regulation is an effective strategy for constructing confinement-conversion synergistic interfaces toward high-performance AZIBs.

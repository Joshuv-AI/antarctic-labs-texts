Geometric rigidity of simple modules for algebraic groups
Michael Bate, David I. Stewart

Abstract
. Let k k be a field, let G G be an affine algebraic k k -group and V V a finite-dimensional G G -module. We say V V is rigid if the socle series and radical series coincide for the action of G G on each indecomposable summand of V V ; say V V is geometrically rigid (resp. absolutely rigid ) if V V is rigid after base change of G G and V V to k ¯ {\overline{k}} (resp. any field extension of k k ). We show that all simple G G -modules are geometrically rigid, though not in general absolutely rigid. More precisely, we show that if V V is a simple G G -module, then there is a finite purely inseparable extension k V / k k_{V}/k naturally attached to V V such that V k V V_{k_{V}} is absolutely rigid as a G k V G_{k_{V}} -module. The proof turns on an investigation of algebras of the form K ⊗ k E K\otimes_{k}E where K K and E E are field extensions of k k ; we give an example of such an algebra which is not rigid as a module over itself. We establish the existence of the purely inseparable field extension k V / k k_{V}/k through an analogous version for artinian algebras. In the second half of the paper we apply recent results on the structure and representation theory of pseudo-reductive groups to give a concrete description of k V k_{V} when G G is smooth and connected. Namely, we combine the main structure theorem of the Conrad–Prasad classification of pseudo-reductive G G together with our previous high weight theory. For a simple G G -module V V , we calculate the minimal field of definition of the geometric Jacobson radical of End G ​ ( V ) \mathrm{End}_{G}(V) in terms of the high weight of V V and the Conrad–Prasad classification data; this gives a concrete construction of the field k V k_{V} as a subextension of the minimal field of definition of the geometric unipotent radical of G G . We also observe that the Conrad–Prasad classification can be used to hone the dimension formula for V V we had previously established; we also use it to give a description of End G ​ ( V ) \mathrm{End}_{G}(V) which includes a dimension formula.

Let

\Soc_{R}(M)

We say the socle series (resp. radical series) has length

(resp.
\Rad^{\ell}(M)=0

They must have a common length

[

, §9.4]

If

We say an

is a

Following

The following collects some basic observations about rigidity, whose proofs are obvious:

Lemma I.1.4
.

Suppose that

(ii)

It was shown in

that one may characterise rigidity of a commutative Gorenstein algebra of finite dimension over a field

using its Hilbert function, so we explain this now. Take any filtration
\{M_{i}:=\mathscr{F}_{i}(M)\}

, where the

are all submodules of

H_{\mathscr{F}}(M)(x):=H_{\mathscr{F},0}+H_{\mathscr{F},1}x+H_{\mathscr{F},1}x^{2}+\dots

Let

the radical filtration

are symmetric about the middle.

coincide if and only if there is an isomorphism

, where

denotes the associated graded algebra of

]
.

I.1.4. Base change

Let
k
be a field and
G
an affine algebraic
k
-group; let
A
be a finite-dimensional
k
-algebra. We record two results about the behaviour of
G
-modules and
A
-modules under base change.
Let
V
be a finite-dimensional
A
-module (so
V
is finite-dimensional with respect to the
k
-vector space structure inherited from
A
).
Then obviously for any field extension
E/k
, we have
V_{E}:=V\otimes_{k}E
is an
A_{E}:=A\otimes_{k}E
-module in a natural way. If
W
is another
A
-module then, analogously with
Eq.

1
, the finite-dimensionality of
V
and the flatness of
E/k
implies

\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(V,W)_{E}\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{A_{E}}(V_{E},W_{E}).

Now we may state

Lemma I.1.6
.

Suppose that
E/k
is an extension of fields and
V
is a finite-dimensional
G
-module (resp.
A
-module).
If
V_{E}
is semisimple as a
G_{E}
-module (resp.
A_{E}
-module), then
V
is semisimple as a
G
-module (resp.
A
-module).

Proof.

We consider the case of
G
-modules; the proof for
A
-modules is identical. We prove the contrapositive.
Suppose that
V
is not semisimple.
Then there is a non-split short exact sequence
0\to M\to V\to U\to 0
with
U
simple.
Since
U
is simple, every element of
\mathrm{Hom}_{G}(U,V)
must actually have image in the submodule
M
; that is

\mathrm{Hom}_{G}(U,V)=\mathrm{Hom}_{G}(U,M)
.
Tensoring with
E
, we get from (
1
) that

\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{E}}(U_{E},V_{E})=\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{E}}(U_{E},M_{E})
.
In other words, the short exact sequence
0\to M_{E}\to V_{E}\to U_{E}\to 0
also has no splitting, so
V_{E}
is not semisimple.
∎

The first part of the next result provides a partial converse to
Lemma

I.1.6
; it is well-known, but we include a proof for completeness.

Lemma I.1.7
.

Suppose that
E/k
is a separable algebraic field extension, and
V
is a finite-dimensional
G
-module or
A
-module.

(i)

V
is semisimple if and only if
V_{E}
is semisimple.

(ii)

\Soc^{i}(V_{E})=(\Soc^{i}(V))_{E}
and
\Rad^{i}(V_{E})=(\Rad^{i}(V))_{E}
.

Proof.

Again we only give the proof for
G
-modules, with the proof for
A
-modules being completely analogous.
Suppose first that
E
is separably closed and
V
is simple.
Then
\Soc_{G_{E}}(V_{E})
is a non-trivial
G_{E}
-submodule of
V_{E}
which is stable under the Galois group of
E/k
,
and so has a
k
-form.
That is, there is a
G
-submodule
U
of
V
with
U_{E}=\Soc(V_{E})
.
Since
V
is simple and
\Soc_{G_{E}}(V_{E})
is non-trivial, we have
U=V
.
Using
Lemma

I.1.6
, we can now deduce for all separable extensions
E/k
and all finite-dimensional
G
-modules
V
that we have
\Soc_{G_{E}}(V_{E})=\Soc_{G}(V)_{E}
.
This is enough to deduce (i) and the first part of (ii).
The second part of (ii) follows by repeating the argument with the radical in place of the socle.
∎

I.1.5. Minimal fields of definition for Jacobson radicals

Definition I.1.8
.

Let
A
be a finite-dimensional
k
-algebra,
K/k
be a field extension and
M
an
A_{K}
-module. An intermediate field
K/E/k
is a
field of definition
for
M
if there is an
A_{E}
-module
N
such that
N_{K}
and
M
are isomorphic
A_{K}
-modules. If
E
is a field of definition for
M
admitting no proper subfield of definition, then
E
is a
minimal field of definition
for
M
.

Similarly, if
M
is an
A
-module and
M^{\prime}
is an
A_{K}
-submodule of
M_{K}
, then
E
is a field of definition for
M^{\prime}
if there is a submodule
N
of
M_{E}
such that
N_{K}=M^{\prime}
, and it is minimal if
E
admits no proper subfield of definition.

Remark I.1.9
.

In general there is no guarantee of a minimal field of definition: in
[
6
, Sec. 6]
one can find an example of a two-dimensional module for the quaternion algebra
A:=\mathbb{Q}\{x,y\}/(x^{2}=y^{2}=-1,\ xy=-yx)
that is defined over the field
K=\mathbb{Q}(a,b)/(a^{2}+b^{2}+1)
, and whenever it is defined over some subfield
E
of
K
, it is also defined over some proper subfield of
E
.

In the special case
A=k
, so that
M
is a
K
-vector space, one sees that
M\cong N_{K}
if
N
is a
k
-vector space with a basis of the same cardinality as that of
M
so that its minimal field of definition exists and is
k
. When
V
is any
k
-vector space and
W
a
K
-subspace for some field extension
K/k
, it is explained in
[
12
, Rk. 1.1.7]
how to construct the unique minimal field of definition
E
for
W
: one takes a basis
\{e_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of
V
, with a subset
\{e_{j}\}_{j\in J}
that maps to a basis
\{\overline{e_{j}}\}_{j\in J}
of
(V\otimes K)/W
; then one takes for
E
the subfield of
K
spanned by the coefficients of the remaining
\{\overline{e_{i}}\}_{i\in I\setminus J}
when expressed as linear combinations of the
\overline{e_{j}}
. See also
[
17
, Sec. 4.8]
for a discussion of fields of definition.

For an artinian
k
-algebra
A
, we will be interested in the field of definition of the Jacobson radical
\Jac(A_{{\overline{k}}})
as a submodule of
A_{{\overline{k}}}
—the construction above implies its existence.

Lemma I.1.10
.

Let
k
be a field and let
A
be a finite-dimensional algebra over
k
.
Let
\mathcal{J}:=\Jac(A_{{\overline{k}}})
denote the Jacobson radical of the base change
A_{{\overline{k}}}=A\otimes_{k}{\overline{k}}
.

(i)

If
A=K
is a purely inseparable field extension of
k
, then as a
\mathcal{J}
has minimal field of definition
K
.

(ii)

More generally, the minimal field of definition of
\mathcal{J}
is a finite purely inseparable extension
K/k

(and, in particular, it exists).

Let
J\subseteq A_{K}
be such that
\mathcal{J}=J_{\overline{k}}
.

(iii)

For any extension
E/K
we have
\Jac(A_{E})=J_{E}
.

(iv)

The module
A_{K}
is rigid if and only if
A_{E}
is rigid for some field extension
E/K
if and only if
A_{E}
is rigid for all field extensions
E/K
.

Proof.

(i). Because
K
is purely inseparable, for any algebraic extension
E/k
the algebra
K\otimes_{k}E
is local, since there is precisely one embedding
K\hookrightarrow\overline{E}={\overline{k}}
and so there is just one possible quotient field: the compositum of
K
and
E
.
Seen as an
E
-algebra this quotient field is
1
-dimensional precisely when
K\subseteq E
, hence
\dim\Jac(K\otimes_{k}{\overline{k}})=[K:k]-1
and
K
is the required minimal field of definition.

(ii). Since
A
is finite-dimensional, by the Artin–Wedderburn theorem we have

A_{\text{ss}}:=A_{k_{s}}/\Jac(A_{k_{s}})\cong\prod_{i=1}^{r}\Mat_{n_{i}}(k_{i})

for
n_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
and each
k_{i}
a finite purely inseparable field extension of
k_{s}
. By (i) we have that
k_{i}
is the minimal field of definition for
\Jac(\Mat_{n_{i}}(k_{i})_{\overline{k}})=\Mat_{n_{i}}(\Jac((k_{i})_{\overline{k}}))
and we claim that the compositum
L
of the
k_{i}
is the finite purely inseparable extension of

which is the minimal field of definition for
\Jac(A_{\text{ss}})_{\overline{k}}
. Indeed we have
(A_{\text{ss}})_{L}/\Jac((A_{\text{ss}})_{L})\cong\prod\Mat_{n_{i}}(L)
, which is an absolutely semisimple
L
-algebra expressed as a quotient of

by a nilpotent ideal. Hence
\mathcal{J}
is defined over
L
. Conversely, if
E
does not contain some
k_{i}
, then there is some quotient algebra
\Mat_{n_{i}}(k_{i})_{E}
of
A_{E}
whose Jacobson radical is
\Mat_{n_{i}}(\Jac(k_{i}\otimes_{k}E))
; by (i),
\Jac(k_{i}\otimes_{k}E)_{\overline{k}}
is a strict subalgebra of
\Jac(k_{i}\otimes_{k}{\overline{k}})
and so
\mathcal{J}
is not defined over
E
.

Since
{\overline{k}}\cong k_{\mathbf{p}}\otimes k_{s}
where
k_{\mathbf{p}}=k^{p^{-\infty}}
, there is a purely inseparable field extension
K^{\prime}/k
such that
K^{\prime}\otimes_{k}k_{s}
is an extension of
k_{s}
containing
L
; by Galois descent this implies that the minimal field of definition
K
over
k
of
\Jac(A_{L})
(hence
\mathcal{J}
) is purely inseparable.

(iii). Keeping the notation already established, we now claim that
L=K\otimes_{k}k_{s}
.
First note that
K\otimes_{k}k_{s}
is a field, since
K/k
is purely inseparable and
k_{s}/k
is separable.
Since
\mathcal{J}
is
K
-defined, it is
(K\otimes_{k}k_{s})
-defined, and hence
L\subseteq K\otimes_{k}k_{s}
(we established that
L
is the minimal field of definition of
\mathcal{J}
over
k_{s}
).
But
L/k
is an extension over which
\mathcal{J}
is defined, so
K\subseteq L
, and the claim is proved.

Now we have observed that
A_{L}/J_{L}=(A_{K}/J_{K})_{L}
is absolutely semisimple.
If
E/K
is any extension, we may identify all the fields so far encountered in an algebraic closure
\overline{E}
of
E
.
The compositum
EL
in
\overline{E}
is generated by elements of the separable algebraic extension
L/K
, and hence
EL/E
is separable.
Since
A_{EL}/J_{EL}=(A_{L}/J_{L})_{EL}
is semisimple, so is
A_{E}/J_{E}
, by an application of
Lemma

I.1.6
.

(iv). Since any artinian algebra is a direct product of local artinian algebras whose factors are the indecomposable summands of
A
as a left
A
-module, we may assume it is local, since rigidity of a module is predicated on its indecomposable summands. Let
E/K
be any field extension.
Since
A_{E}
is artinian, the radical series for
A_{E}
is formed by taking the powers of
\Jac(A_{E})
, and the socle series by taking the annihilators of those powers, see
Section

I.1.3
.
Part (iii) says that
\Jac(A_{E})=J_{E}
, so the powers of
\Jac(A_{E})
are the base changes to
E
of the powers of
J
.
Then the annihilators of the powers of
\Jac(A_{E})
are the base changes to
E
of the annihilators of the powers of
J
as well: certainly we have an inclusion
\Ann(J^{i})_{E}\subseteq\Ann(J_{E}^{i})
, and then a consideration of dimension gives equality.
Hence, if the socle and radical series coincide over

, they already coincide over

, and vice versa.
∎

Remark I.1.11
.

Let us underline the aspect of the construction of

in the proof which shows that the minimal field of definition of

Suppose

is such that

.
If

is some separable extension,

On the other hand, if

over

.

I.2. Two Morita equivalences

Recall that
k
is a commutative unital ring. Denote the comultiplication on
k[G]
by
\Delta_{G}
and the surjective algebra map
\epsilon_{G}:k[G]\to k
that corresponds to ‘evaluation at the identity point’. From this, one can define an algebra structure on
k[G]^{*}:=\mathrm{Hom}_{k}(k[G],k)
as follows. For
\mu,\nu\in k[G]^{*}
, we define
\mu\cdot\nu
as
(\mu\otimes\nu)\circ\Delta_{G}
; more explicitly, if
\Delta_{G}(f)=\sum g_{i}\otimes h_{i}
then

(\mu\cdot\nu)(f)=\sum\mu(g_{i})\otimes\nu(h_{i}).

Then one checks from the Hopf algebra axioms that this makes
k[G]^{*}
into an associative
k
-algebra with
\epsilon_{G}
its unit—see
[
21
, I.7.7]
. Furthermore, a
G
-module
M
becomes naturally a
k[G]^{*}
-module: if
\Delta_{M}
denotes the comodule map, then
\mu
acts on
M
by
(1\otimes\mu)\circ\Delta_{M}
, or more explicitly, if
\Delta_{M}(m)=\sum m_{i}\otimes f_{i}
, then
\mu(m):=\sum m_{i}\mu(f_{i})
; see
[
21
, I.7.11]
for more details.

Now suppose that
k[G]
is a flat and projective
k
-module—immediate when
k
is a field. Since
k[G]=k\cdot 1\oplus I_{1}
for
I_{1}
the functions vanishing at the identity point, we have that
I_{1}
is also flat and projective and
k[G]^{*}=k\cdot\epsilon_{G}\oplus I_{1}^{*}
. Under these hypotheses every
G
-module is locally finite and in fact
k[G]^{*}\cdot m=kGm
, where
kGm
denotes the smallest
G
-submodule of
M
containing
m
; see
[
27
, Prop. 3]
and its proof. Also, by projectivity, we may apply the dual basis lemma,
[
22
, 2.9]
to find an indexing set
\mathbb{I}
and a family of pairs
\{(f_{i},\mu_{i})\mid i\in\mathbb{I}\}\subset I_{1}\times I_{1}^{*}
, such that for any
f\in I_{1}
,
\mu_{i}(f)\neq 0
for only finitely many
i
and

(3)

f=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\mu_{i}(f)f_{i}.

For convenience let us add a new element
0
to
\mathbb{I}
with
f_{0}=1
and
\mu_{0}=\epsilon_{G}
. Then
Eq.

3
holds with
f\in k[G]
. (Clearly
\{f_{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{I}\}
is now a generating set of
k[G]
as a
k
-module.) Let
\mathscr{M}
be the subalgebra of
k[G]^{*}
generated by the
\mu_{i}
.

Lemma I.2.1
.

With the above hypotheses, suppose
M
is a
G
-module and
m\in M
. Then
\mathscr{M}m=kGm
. Hence a
k
-submodule
N
of
M
is an
\mathscr{M}
-submodule if and only if it is a
G
-submodule.

Proof.

It is convenient to first show how to deduce the second sentence given the first.
First, if
N
is a
G
-submodule of
M
then the flatness of
G
implies
\Delta_{M}(N)\subseteq N\otimes k[G]\subseteq M\otimes k[G]
, and so
\mu(N)\subseteq N\otimes\mu(k[G])\subseteq N
for any
\mu\in k[G]^{*}
, showing that
N
is an
\mathscr{M}
-submodule
(note that this does not rely on the first sentence of the statement).
Conversely, if
N
is an
\mathscr{M}
-submodule then the first sentence of the statement implies

N=\sum_{m\in N}\mathscr{M}\cdot m=\sum_{m\in N}kGm,

and the latter is a sum of
G
-submodules, hence a
G
-submodule.

For the first statement, it is shown in
[
21
, I.2.13]
that
\Delta_{M}(m)\in kGm\otimes k[G]
and hence we may write
\Delta_{M}(m)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}m_{j}\otimes g_{j}
for some finite indexing set
\mathbb{J}
and with each
m_{j}\in kGm
.
When we do this, we have
kGm=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}km_{j}
, again by
[
21
, I.2.13]
.
Since for each
g_{j}
, there are only finitely many
\mu_{i}
which do not vanish on
g_{j}
, and since
\mathbb{J}
is finite, we can find a finite subset
\mathbb{I}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{I}
containing
0
such that
\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}^{\prime}}f_{i}\mu_{i}
is the identity map on the
k
-submodule of
k[G]
generated by the

.

Let
M^{\prime}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}^{\prime}}k\mu_{i}(m)
and claim
M^{\prime}=kGm
.
Since
kGm
is a
G
-submodule containing
m
, it is an
\mathscr{M}
-submodule by the start of the proof; in particular,
\mu_{i}(m)\in kGm
for each

, so

.

For the other inclusion, we first show that
\Delta_{m}(m)\in M^{\prime}\otimes k[G]
.
For this, note that for each

we have
\mu_{i}(m)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\mu_{i}(g_{j})m_{j}
and thus

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}m_{j}\mu_{i}(g_{j})\otimes 1=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}m_{j}\otimes\mu_{i}(g_{j})

is in

Multiplying by

we get

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}m_{j}\otimes f_{i}\mu_{i}(g_{i})\in M^{\prime}\otimes k[G]
, and now summing up over

we get

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}m_{j}\otimes\left(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}^{\prime}}f_{i}\mu_{i}(g_{j})\right)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}m_{j}\otimes g_{j}\in M^{\prime}\otimes k[G],

as required.

Thus, we may write
\Delta_{M}(m)=\sum n_{i}\otimes h_{i}
for some
n_{i}\in M^{\prime}
and
h_{i}\in k[G]
.
If we let
N=\sum kn_{i}
, then it follows from
[

, I.2.13(1)]
that

.
But then we have

and we are done.
∎

If
\{(f_{i},\mu_{i})\}
is a dual basis in the sense described above, then it remains so after flat base change. Hence the construction of
\mathscr{M}
from

commutes with flat base change.
Therefore we may replace
(G,M,\mathscr{M})
by
(G_{E},M_{E},\mathscr{M}_{E})
in the theorem and we get the following:

.

Suppose
M
is a
G
-module and
E
a flat

-algebra. Then the

-submodules of

are just the

-submodules of
M_{E}
. Moreover if

denotes the image of

in

, then the

-submodules of

are just the

-submodules of
M_{E}
.

We apply the corollary to the case where

is a simple

-module which is projective over

. By local finiteness, we have

is finitely generated and so

is too
[
22
, 2.11]
. Furthermore, as
\mathrm{End}_{k}(M)\cong M^{*}\otimes_{k}M

[

so also
\mathrm{End}_{k}(M)
is finitely generated and projective. Now Schur’s Lemma

tells us that
\mathrm{End}_{G}(M)\cong\mathrm{End}_{R}(M)=:D
is a division algebra over

, and it is finitely-generated as a

is left primitive (i.e. has a faithful left module), it must be simple—​​
[

, 11.7]
.

An

is a faithful functor from the category of left

[

for

.
As

thus

is a progenerator of

We aim to show:

Theorem I.2.10
.

Let

(i)

(ii)

-splitting field

The existence of a field extension which

For the second, note that we have

I.3. On the rigidity of finite-dimensional algebras

Theorem I.3.1
.

Suppose that
R
is a finite-dimensional simple
k
-algebra (though not necessarily central simple).
Let
k^{\prime}/k
be the minimal field of definition of
\Jac(R_{{\overline{k}}})
. Then
R_{k^{\prime}}
is absolutely rigid.

Note that the proof of this theorem immediately reduces to the case that
R
is a field:
since
R
is simple, the Artin-Wedderburn Theorem says that
R\cong M_{n}(D)
for some
D
, where
D
is a finite-dimensional division
k
-algebra.
For any field extension
E/k
, we have
R_{E}\cong M_{n}(D_{E})
, and the ideal structure of
R_{E}
is therefore identical with that of
D_{E}
—
[
23
, 3.1]
.
But now we may replace
D
with its centre

, as in
Corollary

I.2.4
.
Since
R
is finite-dimensional,

is some finite extension of
k
.
We can also see that the minimal fields of definition of
\Jac(R_{\overline{k}})
,
\Jac(D_{\overline{k}})
and
\Jac(Z_{\overline{k}})
all coincide.

I.3.1. Generalities on tensor products of fields

The literature already contains a number of results about the tensor product
A:=K\otimes_{k}E
of general field extensions
K/k
and
E/k
.
For example, it is a result of Grothendieck, in generalised form by Sharp
[
28
]
that
\dim(K\otimes_{k}E)=\min(\mathrm{tr.deg}(K/k),\mathrm{tr.deg}(E/k))
.
Furthermore Grothendieck proved in
[
17
, Lem. 6.7.1.1]
that if one of the extensions has finite type, then
A
is Cohen-Macauley. This has been generalised in at least two directions: in
[
9
, Lem. 2.2]
weakening the hypotheses to demanding
A
be noetherian; and in
[
32
, I.Thm. 2]
strengthening the conclusion to saying
A
is Gorenstein.

A
simply truncated polynomial algebra (STP algebra)
over a field
k
is an algebra of the form

A=k[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]/(X_{1}^{a_{1}},\ldots,X_{n}^{a_{n}})

with
a_{1}\geq a_{2}\geq\cdots\geq a_{n}

[
26
, Ch. 1]
.
Let
x_{i}
denote the image of
X_{i}
in
A
.
Then
A
is a local ring, with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
generated by the
x_{i}
.
It is clear that if
A
is an STP algebra then, for any field extension
E/k
,
A_{E}=A\otimes_{k}E
is an STP algebra over
E
.

Lemma I.3.2
.

An STP algebra
A
is a rigid local Gorenstein algebra, and hence has a symmetric Hilbert function.

Proof.

Since
A
is local, it is indecomposable and thus we need to check (
2
).
For a tuple
\beta=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})
of non-negative integers, we let
x^{\beta}:=\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{b_{i}}
,
and for another such tuple
\beta^{\prime}=(b_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,b_{n}^{\prime})
we say
\beta\leq\beta^{\prime}
when
b_{i}\leq b_{i}^{\prime}
for every
i
.
Note that for any
\beta=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})
, we have
x^{\beta}=0
if and only if there exists some
i
with
b_{i}\geq a_{i}
.
Let
\gamma=(a_{1}-1,\ldots,a_{n}-1)
, and let
n=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}(a_{j}-1)\right)+1
.
Since
x^{\gamma}\in\mathfrak{m}^{n-1}
we have
\mathfrak{m}^{n}=\{0\}
but
\mathfrak{m}^{n-1}\neq\{0\}
.
We need to show that
\Ann(\mathfrak{m}^{i})=\mathfrak{m}^{n-i}
for each
1\leq i\leq n-1
.
It is clear that for any
1\leq i\leq n-1
,
\mathfrak{m}^{n-i}
annihilates
\mathfrak{m}^{i}
.
On the other hand, given
1\leq i\leq n-1
, the ideal
\mathfrak{m}^{n-i-1}
is generated by the
x^{\alpha}
with
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\leq\gamma
and
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}=n-i-1
.
Given any such
\alpha
, let
\beta=\gamma-\alpha
.
Then
x^{\alpha}x^{\beta}=x^{\gamma}\neq 0
, and
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\beta_{j}=i
, so
x^{\alpha}\not\in\Ann(\mathfrak{m}^{i})
.
Thus
\Ann(\mathfrak{m}^{i})=\mathfrak{m}^{n-i}
, and we see that
A
is rigid.

The final power
\mathfrak{m}^{n-1}=\Ann(\mathfrak{m})=\Soc(A)
is generated by the element
x^{\gamma}
, and so is simple as an
A
-module, so
A
is Gorenstein.
The symmetry of the Hilbert function of
A
follows from
[
24
, §70]
, as explained in Remark
I.1.5
.
∎

Now suppose
Z/k
is some finite extension, and let
M\subseteq Z
be the separable part of the extension.
From
[
26
, Ch. 2, Thm. 6]
we learn that there is some finite (normal) extension

such that
Z\otimes_{k}E
is a sum of STP
E
-algebras, and the summands are all isomorphic (via Galois automorphisms) to
Z\otimes_{M}E
.
In particular,
Z\otimes_{k}{\overline{k}}
is a sum of STP
{\overline{k}}
-algebras, and all the summands are isomorphic as rings.

Proposition I.3.3
.

Let
k^{\prime}
be the minimal field of definition of
\Jac(Z_{\overline{k}})
.
Then
Z_{k^{\prime}}:=Z\otimes_{k}k^{\prime}
is absolutely rigid.
Thus for all extensions

, the Hilbert functions
H(Z_{E})
coincide, and are symmetric about the middle degree term.

Proof.

By the results of
[
26
]
above,
Z_{\overline{k}}
is a sum of isomorphic copies of the STP
{\overline{k}}
-algebra
Z\otimes_{M}{\overline{k}}
;
these will be the blocks of
Z_{\overline{k}}
.
Since by
Lemma

I.3.2
each of these is rigid, so is
Z_{\overline{k}}
.
In fact, since the blocks are all isomorphic as rings, we are in the sitiuation where the socle/radical series of
Z_{\overline{k}}
is given by powers of its Jacobson radical.
In any case, we can now apply

Lemma

I.1.10
(iii) with

to deduce rigidity of

for all extensions

.

The final statement about the Hilbert functions follows since the socle and radical series for any
Z_{E}
are just the base changes of those for

, and
so all the Hilbert series coincide.
The dimensions of quotients for that series can therefore be calculated over
{\overline{k}}
, and since
Z_{\overline{k}}
is the sum of isomorphic copies of an STP,
Lemma

I.3.2
gives us the symmetry result.
∎

The proposition above completes the proof of

We give an example of a tensor product of two finite purely inseparable field extensions

Example I.3.4

Let

Remark I.3.5
.

While Example
I.3.4
proves that tensor products of field extensions are not generally rigid, it can often happen “by accident”, even when

does not contain

. For example, if
K
and
E
are linearly disjoint over
k
, then

is a field; this happens for example if

is separable and

is purely inseparable.

Or, suppose

is a purely inseparable simple extension. Then if we let

be minimal such that

, the maximal ideal

is the principal ideal generated by the element

, and rigidity follows easily.

The following example helps to motivate our chosen definition of rigidity and shows how base change of a field along a (non-normal) field extension can give rise to an algebra whose regular module has indecomposable modules of different Loewy lengths.

Example I.3.6
.

Suppose that
p=3
and let
k=\mathbb{F}_{3}(t,u)
be the field of rational functions in

and
u
over the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{3}
.
Let

.
Then

has degree
6
over
k
, and is made up of a Galois extension
E/k
of degree
2
, where
E=k(t^{1/2})
, together with a purely inseparable extension

of degree
3
.
The field
E
has another purely inseparable extension of degree
3
which is abstractly isomorphic to
F
, namely
F^{*}=k(-t^{1/6}+u^{1/3})
.
Let
L=k(t^{1/6},u^{1/3})
be the compositum of
F
and
F^{*}
, so

has degree
18
.
The nontrivial Galois automorphism
\gamma:t^{1/2}\mapsto-t^{1/2}
of

extends in an obvious way to an automorphism of

which swaps the subfields
F
and
F^{*}
; we denote this automorphism by
\gamma
as well.

We claim that the regular module for

has indecomposable summands of different Loewy lengths.
There are two maximal ideals
\mathfrak{m}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{m}_{2}
in
A
: we can realise
\mathfrak{m}_{1}
as the kernel of the map
x\otimes y\mapsto xy
with image

, and
\mathfrak{m}_{2}
as the kernel of the map

with image

.
Then

Both summands are rigid, however the first one has Loewy length
3
and the second is isomorphic to the field

, so has Loewy length
1
.

Some further analysis in this example shows that the minimal field of definition of
\Jac(F_{\overline{k}})
is the field

, the subfield of
\gamma
-fixed points in

.
Certainly
\Jac(F_{\overline{k}})
is

-defined, so the minimal field of definition of
\Jac(F_{\overline{k}})
is contained in the maximal purely inseparable subextension of

, which is

.
Now any proper subfield of

of degree

over

can be written as

with

.
Then since

is separable of degree
2
and

is purely inseparable, we have

.
This allows us to write

F\otimes_{k}k(a)\cong F\otimes_{E}(E\otimes_{k}k(a))\cong F\otimes_{E}E(a).

Since

and

are primitive purely inseparable extensions of

of degree

, either

or

is linearly disjoint from

over

.
In either case we see that

is a local ring, and hence

cannot be

is not local).
Hence

is the minimal field of definition for

Finally, note that we also showed in this case that

Example II.1.3

The classification of pseudo-reductive groups in

A.1 Standard groups

Let
G
be a
pseudo-reductive
group over
k
, that is to say it is a smooth connected affine
k
-group scheme with
k
-unipotent radical
\mathscr{R}_{u,k}(G)=1
. A

group is a smooth connected affine
k
-group defined by having no non-trivial smooth connected normal subgroup; this is of course a special type of pseudo-reductive group. Analogously with reductive groups, one wishes to proceed by reducing the pseudo-reductive case to the pseudo-simple and classifying the latter.

The headline result
[
12
, Thm. 1.5.1]
is a first pass at this which works as least as long as
\Char k=p>3
; it is called the
the standard construction

[
12
, §1.3–1.5]
. Since the standard construction is a necessary concept also in the full classification as given in
[
13
]
, we record the platonic ideal first. For elegance among other things, one takes
k^{\prime}
to be a non-zero finite reduced
k
-algebra; so there is an isomorphism with a product of fields
k^{\prime}\cong k_{1}^{\prime}\times\dots\times k_{r}^{\prime}
, with each
k_{i}^{\prime}/k
a finite field extension.
Then a (reductive)
k^{\prime}
-group

is isomorphic to a product

, where each
G^{\prime}_{i}
is a (reductive)

-group and

is the fibre of
G^{\prime}
over
k_{i}^{\prime}

, for a maximal torus
T^{\prime}
, and for the Weil restriction
\mathrm{R}_{k^{\prime}/k}(G^{\prime})
.
Assuming that
G^{\prime}
is reductive, the Weil restriction
\mathrm{R}_{k^{\prime}/k}(G^{\prime})
is always pseudo-reductive and has Cartan subgroup

[

, Prop. 1.1.10, Prop. A.5.15(3)]

and

are both reductive, it follows that

and

The standard construction swaps out the Cartan subgroup
\mathrm{R}_{k^{\prime}/k}(T^{\prime})
of
\mathrm{R}_{k^{\prime}/k}(G^{\prime})
for any commutative pseudo-reductive group

that sits in a factorisation

. We start by making
\mathrm{R}_{k^{\prime}/k}(G^{\prime})

. The group thus formed, namely

As advertised, most of the time (e.g. away from characteristics

and

) this gives

pseudo-reductive groups.
The way this goes is to show that

is standard if and only if its derived subgroup

is
[

is standard

,

.

A major step is to show that any pseudo-split pseudo-reductive group has a Levi subgroup

Theorem

(Structure Theorem)
.

Let
G
be a pseudo-reductive group over a field

Then

We therefore need to describe the generalised standard construction.
First suppose that

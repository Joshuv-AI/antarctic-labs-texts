Poincare field theory for massive particles
B. Sazdović

Abstract
There is ambitious pretension formulated by Weinberg [ 1 ] that any relativistic quantum theory will look at sufficiently low energy like a quantum field theory. It is based on the observation that for formulation of quantum field theory … much better starting point is Wigner’s definition of particles as representations of inhomogeneous Lorentz group [ 2 , 3 ] . To achieve that Ref. [ 1 ] starts with particles and get to the field equations later. Here we propose a complementary approach and directly introduce field equations as Casimir eigenvalue problem. Note that Casimir invariants commute with all group elements and therefore commute between each other. So, they have common eigenvalues (for Poincare group mass and spin ) and common eigenstates (here irreducible representation of Poincare group). We use derivatives as standard representation for momenta P a → i ∂ a P_{a}\to i\partial_{a} and introduce representation for arbitrary spin operator S a ​ b S_{ab} with the help of recurrence relations. To solve eigenvalue problem for Casimir operators we will go to the formulation with standard momentum, where differential equations turn to algebraic ones. Then for arbitrary field Ψ A \Psi^{A} we construct projection operators on particular spins. The irreducible representations, have a role of equations of motion. For fermions we can go to linear form of equations of motion and obtain Dirac equation.

Introduction

Quantum field theory
[
1
,
4
,
5
]
combines quantum theory
[
6
]
with principle of special relativity. In contrast to traditional approach
[
4
,
5
]
which start with Maxwell and Dirac equations
we will prefer Weinberg approach
[
1
]
who uses Wigner’s definition of particles as irreducible representations of Poincare group
[
2
,
3
]
.

Unlike Ref.
[
1
]
which starts with particles and get to the field equations later we will start with
principle field equations
for arbitrary spin
and show that all known equations for free fields follow from our principal equations. We offer general prescription which can be used to construct field theory based on symmetry of some given group.
Here we apply general prescription to the Poincare group which explains the title of the present article
Poincare field theory
.

In this article we start with special theory of relativity as a theory in four dimensional Minkowski space-time with three space directions
\mathbf{x}
and one time direction
t
.
Poincare transformations are all four dimensional rotations (space rotations and boosts) and translations which preserve line element
dx^{2}=dt^{2}-d\mathbf{x}^{2}
.
They are symmetries of Minkowski space-time.
Generators of these transformations, translations
P_{a}
and rotations
M_{ab}
, form a Poincare algebra.

2
Representations of the Poincare algebra

We will need representations of the Poincare algebra as a first step in order to find representations of the Poincare Casimir invariants.

2.1
Poincare group

Lie algebra of Poincare group has a form

\displaystyle[P_{a},P_{b}]=0\,,\qquad[P_{c},M_{ab}]=-i\Big(\eta_{bc}P_{a}-\eta_{ac}P_{b}\Big)\,,

(1)

\displaystyle[M_{ab},M_{cd}]=i\Big(\eta_{ad}M_{bc}+\eta_{bc}M_{ad}-\eta_{ac}M_{bd}-\eta_{bd}M_{ac}\Big)\,,

(2)

where
P_{a}
are translation generators and

M_{ab}=L_{ab}+S_{ab}
are four dimensional rotations generators. They consist of orbital part

L_{ab}=x_{a}P_{b}-x_{b}P_{a}
and spin part
S_{ab}
.

2.2
Poincare Casimir operators

Casimir operators commute with all group generators and allow us to label the irreducible representations.
Here we will introduce Casimir operators for massive Poincare group and find theirs representations for arbitrary spins.
In the case of
P^{2}>0
there are two Casimir operators

\displaystyle P^{2}=P^{a}P_{a}\,,\qquad W^{2}=-\frac{P^{2}}{2}M_{ab}M^{ab}\,,

(3)

where

\displaystyle W_{a}=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{abcd}M^{bc}P^{d}\,,

(4)

is Pauli-Lubanski vector. Note that Pauli-Lubanski vector does not depend on orbital part
L_{ab}
because

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{abcd}L^{bc}P^{d}=\varepsilon_{abcd}(x^{b}P^{c}-x^{c}P^{b})P^{d}=0\,.

(5)

Therefore, we can rewrite above equations in the form

\displaystyle P^{2}=P^{a}P_{a}\,,\qquad W^{2}=-\frac{P^{2}}{2}S_{ab}S^{ab}\,,

(6)

where now

\displaystyle W_{a}=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{abcd}S^{bc}P^{d}\,,

(7)

and
S_{ab}
is spin part of of Lorentz generators
M_{ab}
.

Let us stress that from Casimir operator’s point of view just generators
P_{a}
and
S_{ab}
are relevant. They satisfy relations

\displaystyle[P_{a},P_{b}]=0\,,\qquad[P_{c},S_{ab}]=0\,,

(8)

\displaystyle[S_{ab},S_{cd}]=i\Big(\eta_{ad}S_{bc}+\eta_{bc}S_{ad}-\eta_{ac}S_{bd}-\eta_{bd}S_{ac}\Big)\,.

(9)

Note that generators
P_{a}
and
S_{ab}
commute and so Casimir operators are defined without problem of order unambiguity.

2.3
Representation of Casimir operators

Representations of Poincare group are labeled by the eigenvalues of Casimir invariants, that we can assign to a physical state.
For
P^{2}>0
eigenvalues of Casimir operators are mass
m
and spin
s

\displaystyle P^{2}=m^{2}\,,\qquad W^{2}=-m^{2}s(s+1)\,.

(10)

To find representation of Casimir operators we need representation of Poincare algebra generators, momentum
P_{a}
and spin
S_{ab}
.
Representation of momentum
P_{a}
is well known from quantum mechanics
(P_{a})^{A}{}_{B}\to i\delta^{A}_{B}\partial_{a}
and it is spins independent.

We can obtain representation of spin generators
(S_{ab})^{A}{}_{B}
for arbitrary field from corresponding expressions with smaller spins and initial expression for fermions.
Starting from infinitesimal Lorentz transformation

\displaystyle\Psi_{\omega}^{A}(x)=\Psi^{A}(x)-\frac{i}{2}\omega^{ab}(S_{ab})^{A}{}_{B}\Psi^{B}(x)\,,

(11)

we can find infinitesimal transformation of product
\Psi_{1}^{A}\Psi_{2}^{B}
. First, multiplying expressions (
11
) for infinitesimal
\omega^{ab}
we obtain

\displaystyle\Psi^{A}_{1\omega}(x)\Psi^{B}_{2\omega}(x)=\Psi^{A}_{1}(x)\Psi^{B}_{2}(x)-\frac{i}{2}\omega^{ab}\Big[(S_{ab})^{A}{}_{C}\Psi^{C}_{1}(x)\Psi^{B}_{2}(x)+\Psi^{A}_{1}(x)(S_{ab})^{B}{}_{D}\Psi^{D}_{2}(x)\Big]\,.\qquad

(12)

Second, by definition of Lorentz transformation we have

\displaystyle\Psi^{A}_{1\omega}(x)\Psi^{B}_{2\omega}(x)=\Psi^{A}_{1}(x)\Psi^{B}_{2}(x)-\frac{i}{2}\omega^{ab}(S_{ab})^{AB}{}_{CD}\Big(\Psi_{1}^{C}(x)\Psi_{2}^{D}(x)\Big)\,.

(13)

Comparing these results we can conclude that spin generators
(S_{ab})^{A}{}_{B}
act as derivatives, where its form depend on the fields on the right

\displaystyle(S_{ab})^{AB}{}_{CD}=(S_{ab})^{A}{}_{C}\delta_{D}^{B}+\delta^{A}_{C}(S_{ab})^{B}{}_{D}\,.

(14)

It is easy to check that

\displaystyle[S_{ab},S_{cd}]^{AB}{}_{CD}=[S_{ab},S_{cd}]^{A}{}_{C}\delta_{D}^{B}+\delta^{A}_{C}[S_{ab},S_{cd}]^{B}{}_{D}\,,

(15)

so that
(S_{ab})^{AB}{}_{CD}
is solution of (
9
) because
(S_{ab})^{A}{}_{B}
is solution of this relation.

The initial expression for Dirac spinor is

\displaystyle(S_{ab})^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}=\frac{i}{4}[\gamma_{a},\gamma_{b}]^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}\,,

(16)

and we can find representations for all other fields using recurrence relation (
14
).
For example, using expression for vectors in terms of spinors
V^{a}(x)={\bar{\psi}}(x)\gamma^{a}\psi(x)
we can find the same expression for vector spin generators as that obtained by direct calculation

\displaystyle(S_{ab})^{c}{}_{d}=i\Big(\delta^{c}_{a}\eta_{bd}-\delta^{c}_{b}\eta_{ad}\Big)\,.

(17)

3
Principle field equations

The fields are functions defined in Minkowski space-time which transforms with some representation of Poincare group. They can be marked with arbitrary number of vector and spinor indices. We will
introduce notation
\Psi^{A}(x)
where
A
contains the set of corresponding indices.

In order to separate states which describe definite particles we should impose some constraints on the field
\Psi^{A}(x)
.
Since in field theory particles are defined by mass and spin it is natural to use just operators whose eigenvalues are mass and spin.
Consequently,
we will postulate principle field equations for arbitrary spin as representation of relations (
10
)

\displaystyle(P^{2})^{A}{}_{B}\Psi^{B}(x)=m\Psi^{A}(x)\,,\qquad{\cal S}^{A}{}_{B}\Psi^{B}(x)=s(s+1)\Psi^{A}(x)\,.

(1)

In fact they are Casimir eigenvalue equations. These equations are Poincare covariant because Casimir operators commute with all Poincare generators.
In particular, Casimir operators commute mutually. Since commuting observables have a complete set of common eigenfunctions we are able to impose both Casimir operators to the same field
\Psi^{A}(x)
.

In our basic equations (
1
)
(P^{2})^{A}{}_{B}
and
{\cal S}^{A}{}_{B}
are representation of Casimir operators.
The eigenvalues of the operators
(P^{2})^{A}{}_{B}
and
{\cal S}^{A}{}_{B}
are
m
and
s(s+1)
and they define the mass
m
and the spins
s
. The eigenfunctions
\Psi^{A}(x)
are irreducible representations of the Poincare group. So irreducible representations are labeled by the mass
m
and the spin
s
.

Using representations for momentum and spin operators we obtain the set of differential equations

\displaystyle\Big(\partial^{2}+m^{2}\Big)\Psi^{A}(x)=0\,,\qquad{\cal S}^{A}{}_{B}\Psi^{B}(x)=s(s+1)\Psi^{A}(x)\,,

(2)

where

\displaystyle{\cal S}^{A}{}_{B}\equiv-\frac{(W^{2})^{A}{}_{B}}{m^{2}}=-\frac{1}{m^{2}}(S^{a}{}_{c})^{A}{}_{C}(S^{cb})^{C}{}_{B}\partial_{a}\partial_{b}+\frac{1}{2}(S_{ab})^{A}{}_{C}(S^{ab})^{C}{}_{B}\,.

(3)

3.1
Principle field equations for standard momentum

So far we have obtained constraints on the fields
\Psi^{A}(x)
as eigenfunctions of Casimir invariants.
The next step is to find projectors on irreducible representations of Poincare group, which correspond to well known field equations.
In order to to achieve that it is useful to go to standard momentum.

Lorentz invariant functions of momentum
p^{a}
are square
p^{2}=\eta_{ab}p^{a}p^{b}
and sign of
p^{0}
. So, for
p^{2}
and sign of
p^{0}
defined in advance we can chose
standard momentum

k^{a}
and
express any momentum
p^{a}
as Lorentz transformation of
k^{a}

\displaystyle p^{a}=L^{a}{}_{b}(p)k^{b}\,.

(4)

For massive case where
p^{2}=m^{2}
we can chose rest frame momentum
k^{a}=(m,0,0,0)
as standard momentum.
With this choice the first equation (
2
) is solved.

For standard momentum second differential equation (
2
) becomes algebraic one, which makes the calculation much easier.
After solving algebraic equation we can go back to
p^{a}
dependent solutions using (
4
) and then to solution in coordinate representation.

The spin equation for standard momentum takes a form

\displaystyle{\cal S}^{A}{}_{B}\Psi^{B}(k)=s(s+1)\Psi^{A}(k)\,,

(5)

where

\displaystyle{\cal S}^{A}{}_{B}=(S^{2}_{i})^{A}{}_{B}\,,\qquad(S_{i})^{A}{}_{B}=\frac{1}{2}\,\varepsilon_{ijk}(S_{jk})^{A}{}_{B}\,.

(6)

Note that instead of
6
components of spin operator
S_{ab}
in the frame of standard momentum we have left with
3
components
S_{i}
, which are generators of space rotations.
They form a subgroup known as
little group
for massive Poinacare case.

The next step is to solve the eigenvalue problem of operator
{\cal S}^{A}{}_{B}
(
5
).
To have nontrivial solution for function
\Psi^{A}
the characteristic polynomial must vanish

\displaystyle\det\Big({\cal S}^{A}{}_{B}-\lambda\delta^{A}_{B}\Big)=0\,,\qquad\lambda\equiv s(s+1)\,.

(7)

Generally, it may have multiple solutions,
\lambda_{i}
where
i=1,2,\cdots,n
counts irreducible representations.
The values
s_{i}
, corresponding to the eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}
, are spins of irreducible representations.

The representations of eigenfunctions
\Psi_{i}^{A}
with definite spin have a form

\displaystyle\Psi_{i}^{A}=(P_{i})^{A}{}_{B}\Psi^{B}\,,\qquad(P_{i})^{A}{}_{B}=\frac{\Big[\prod_{j\neq i}^{n}\Big({\cal S}-\lambda_{j}\Big)\Big]^{A}{}_{B}}{\prod_{j\neq i}^{n}\Big(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\Big)}\,,\qquad i=\{1,2,\cdots,n\}\,,

(8)

where
(P_{i})^{A}{}_{B}
are corresponding projection operators.
In the case of degeneracy, we should chose basis in subspace
\Xi_{\lambda}
of eigenvalue
\lambda
.
The fields
\Psi_{i}^{A}(k)
are representations of Poincare group, but not necessary irreducible representations. To obtain irreducible representations we should separate fields

\Psi_{i}^{A}(k)
in several sets with well-defined symmetry properties.

According to (
8
) we will introduce the equation of motion for fixed spin
s_{i}

\displaystyle(P_{i})^{A}{}_{B}\Psi^{B}=0\,,\qquad i=\{1,2,\cdots,n\}\,.

(9)

We can rewrite equation (
7
) in the form

\displaystyle\det\Big({\cal S}^{A}{}_{B}-\lambda\delta^{A}_{B}\Big)=\prod_{i=0}^{n}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda)^{d_{i}}=0\,,\qquad

(10)

where
d_{i}
are dimensions of eigenpaces which correspond to projector
P_{i}
.
Note that
d_{i}
is number of degrees of freedom of component
\Psi_{i}^{A}(k)
.

The gauge transformations of field equation are generated by projection operators. Fields of different spins have different gauge transformations. Explicitly

\displaystyle\delta\Psi^{A}(x)=\sum_{j\neq i}(P_{j})^{A}{}_{B}\,\varepsilon^{B}_{j}\,,\qquad

(11)

is symmetry transformation of field equation (
9
) because projectors are orthogonal. Here,
\varepsilon^{B}_{j}
for
j\neq i
are parameters of gauge transformation.

4
Examples

We are going to confirm that above principle field equations for particular spins coincides with well known field equations. Here we will present three examples: Klein-Gordon equation for scalar fields (
s=0
),
Dirac equation for spinors (
s=\frac{1}{2}
) and equation for massive vector fields (
s_{0}=0
and
s_{1}=1
).

4.1
Scalar field

A scalar field has no indices
\Psi^{A}(x)\to\varphi(x)
, so that
(S_{ab})^{A}{}_{B}\to 0
. Equation (
7
) produces
\lambda=0
and consequently
s=0
.
This is one dimensional representation with one degree of freedom.
We are left with the Klein-Gordon equation

\displaystyle\Big(\partial^{2}+m^{2}\Big)\varphi(x)=0\,.

(1)

4.2
Dirac field

For Dirac field
\Psi^{A}(x)\to\psi^{\alpha}(x)
and representation of spin operator is

\displaystyle(S_{ab})^{A}{}_{B}\to(S_{ab})^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}=\frac{i}{4}[\gamma_{a},\gamma_{b}]^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}\,,

(2)

so that

\displaystyle S_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\,\varepsilon_{ijk}S_{jk}=\frac{i}{4}\varepsilon_{ijk}\gamma_{j}\gamma_{k}\,,\qquad{\cal S}^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}=[(S_{i})^{2}]^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}=\frac{3}{4}\delta^{\alpha}_{\beta}\,.

(3)

Then the spin equation (
5
) takes the form

\displaystyle{\cal S}^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}\psi^{\beta}(k)=\lambda\psi^{\alpha}(k)\,,\qquad\lambda\equiv s(s+1)

(4)

and since
{\cal S}^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}
is diagonal we obtain

\displaystyle\det({\cal S}-\lambda)^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}=\Big(\lambda-\frac{3}{4}\Big)^{4}=0\,.

(5)

Therefore,
\lambda=\frac{3}{4}
which produces spin
s=\frac{1}{2}
. According to exponent in last expression this is four dimensional representation,
which means that field
\psi^{\alpha}(k)
has four degrees of freedom.

There is only one trivial projection operator
P^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}=\delta^{\alpha}_{\beta}
.
Using (
4
) we can go from standard momentum
k^{a}
to arbitrary one
p^{a}
and then to coordinate representation. So, we obtain standard
Klein-Gordon equation for all components
(\partial^{2}+m^{2})\psi^{\alpha}(x)=0
.

We can linearize it in the form

\displaystyle(i\gamma^{a}\partial_{a}+m)\psi^{\alpha}(x)=0\,,

(6)

where
\gamma^{a}
are constant matrices. In fact, it produces Klein-Gordon equation if

\displaystyle\{\gamma^{a},\gamma^{b}\}=2\eta^{ab}\,.

(7)

Therefore, for fields with spin
s=\frac{1}{2}
in expression (
6
) we recognize Dirac equation and in (
7
) relation for
\gamma
- matrices.

4.3
Vector field

For vector field we have
\Psi^{A}\to V^{a}
, and from representation of spin operators (
17
) we obtain

\displaystyle(S_{i})^{a}{}_{b}=\frac{1}{2}\,\varepsilon_{ijk}(S_{jk})^{a}{}_{b}=i\,\varepsilon_{ijk}\delta^{a}_{j}\,\eta_{kb}\,,\qquad{\cal S}^{a}{}_{b}=(S^{2}_{i})^{a}{}_{b}=-2\delta_{jk}\delta^{a}_{j}\,\eta_{kb}\,.

(8)

Therefore, the spin equation in the rest frame takes the form

\displaystyle{\cal S}^{a}{}_{b}V^{b}(k)=\lambda V^{a}(k)\,.\qquad\lambda\equiv s(s+1)

(9)

The consistency condition produces

\displaystyle\det({\cal S}-\lambda)^{a}{}_{b}=-\lambda(2-\lambda)^{3}=0\,,

(10)

with solutions for eigenvalues

\lambda_{0}=0\,,\,\lambda_{1}=2
and for spins
s_{0}=0\,,\,s_{1}=1
.
Subspace with
s_{0}=0
is one dimensional while one with
s_{1}=1
is three dimensional.

Using expression (
8
) we obtain two projectors

\displaystyle(P_{0})^{a}{}_{b}(k)=\delta^{a}_{b}-\frac{{\cal S}^{a}{}_{b}}{2}=\delta^{a}_{0}\delta^{0}_{b}\,,\qquad(P_{1})^{a}{}_{b}(k)=\frac{{\cal S}^{a}{}_{b}}{2}=\delta^{a}_{b}-\delta^{a}_{0}\delta^{0}_{b}\,.

(11)

In order to obtain irreducible representations of vector fields in arbitrary frame we should boost corresponding equation of the rest frame. Then for projection operators we get

\displaystyle(P^{L})^{a}{}_{b}(p)={\cal B}^{a}{}_{c}(P_{0})^{c}{}_{d}(k)({\cal B}^{-1})^{d}{}_{b}={\cal B}^{a}{}_{0}({\cal B}^{-1})^{0}{}_{b}\,,

\displaystyle(P^{T})^{a}{}_{b}(p)={\cal B}^{a}{}_{c}(P_{1})^{c}{}_{d}(k)({\cal B}^{-1})^{d}{}_{b}=\delta^{a}_{b}-(P^{L})^{a}{}_{b}(p)\,.

(12)

Using expressions for boost components

\displaystyle{\cal B}^{a}{}_{0}=\frac{p^{a}}{m}\,,\qquad({\cal B}^{-1})^{0}{}_{a}=\frac{p_{a}}{m}\,,

(13)

and condition
p^{2}=m^{2}
we obtain standard form of longitudinal and transversal projection operators

\displaystyle(P^{L})^{a}{}_{b}(p)=\frac{p^{a}p_{b}}{p^{2}}\,,\qquad(P^{T})^{a}{}_{b}(p)=\delta^{a}_{b}-\frac{p^{a}p_{b}}{p^{2}}\,.

(14)

Therefore, solution of the spin equation produces two irreducible representations

\displaystyle s=0:\qquad V_{L}^{a}(p)=(P^{L})^{a}{}_{b}(p)V^{b}(p)=\frac{p^{a}p_{b}}{p^{2}}V^{b}(p)\,,\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle s=1:\qquad V_{T}^{a}(p)=(P^{T})^{a}{}_{b}(p)V^{b}(p)=V^{a}(p)-\frac{p^{a}p_{b}}{p^{2}}V^{b}(p)\,,

(15)

and in coordinate space using
p_{a}=i\partial_{a}

\displaystyle s=0:\quad V_{L}^{a}(x)=\frac{\partial^{a}\partial_{b}}{\partial^{2}}V^{b}(x)\,,\qquad s=1:\quad V_{T}^{a}(x)=V^{a}(x)-V_{L}^{a}(x)\,.

(16)

Equations of motion for massive vector fields, for spins
0
and
1
have a form

\displaystyle\Big(\partial^{2}+m^{2}\Big)V_{L}^{a}(x)=0\,,\qquad\Big(\partial^{2}+m^{2}\Big)V_{T}^{a}(x)=0\,.

(17)

Since the number of degrees of freedom is equal to dimensions of representations field
V_{L}^{a}(x)
has one degree of freedom and field
V_{T}^{a}(x)

has three degrees of freedom.

If we introduce notation
\varphi=\partial_{b}V^{b}
so that

\displaystyle V^{L}_{a}=\frac{\partial_{a}}{\partial^{2}}\varphi\,,\qquad V^{T}_{a}=U_{a}\,,

(18)

we can rewrite above equations as

\displaystyle\Big(\partial^{2}+m^{2}\Big)\varphi(x)=0\,,\qquad\Big(\partial^{2}+m^{2}\Big)U_{a}(x)=0\,,

(19)

where according to second relation (
15
)
U_{a}
satisfy condition
\partial_{a}U^{a}=0
. This condition reduces four components of the field
U_{a}
to three degrees of freedom. It provides positivity of vector field energy
[
4
]
.

The gauge transformations for spins
s=0
and
s=1
take a form

\displaystyle\delta V^{a}(x)=(P_{T})^{a}{}_{b}\varepsilon^{b}\,,\qquad\delta V^{a}(x)=(P_{L})^{a}{}_{b}\varepsilon^{b}\,.

(20)

which for scalar field means
\delta\varphi=0
. In fact, first gauge transformation (
20
) reduces four components of the field
V^{a}
to one degree of freedom.

5
Conclusion

We formulated general approach which allows us to obtain free field equations only on the basis of symmetry group.
In order to realize it we need to know Casimir invariants of the symmetry group. Our approach shows that symmetry group completely determine corresponding free field theory.

In the present article we started with well known fact that Poincare invariance is the fundamental symmetry in particle physics. Based on Poincare Casimir invariants,
we introduced principle field equations which produce all known equations for free massive fields with arbitrary spin.
In the present article we considered massive case and in the next one
[
7
]
we will consider massless case.

Therefore, we have developed systematic approach that supports Wigner’s idea
[
2
,
3
]
developed by Weinberg
[
1
]
that
particles are irreducible representations of Poincare group.

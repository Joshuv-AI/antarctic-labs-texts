Triangulated Categories Admitting Linear Generators
Marina Godinho and Dave Murphy

Abstract
. The main result of this paper is that there is an additive equivalence between 𝒞 ¯ n \overline{\mathcal{C}}_{n} , the Paquette-Yıldırım completion of the discrete cluster categories of Dynkin type A ∞ A_{\infty} , and the perfect derived category of a certain DG algebra. This additive equivalence preserves some of the triangulated structure: it commutes with the suspension functor and preserves triangles with at least two indecomposable terms. In the process, we introduce the notion of a linear generator G G in a Krull-Schmidt, Hom-finite triangulated category. It turns out that the existence of a linear generator affords a large amount of control over 𝒯 \mathcal{T} . For example, it allows us to describe all indecomposable objects in 𝒯 \mathcal{T} in terms of G G , to determine all triangles of 𝒯 \mathcal{T} with at least two indecomposable objects, and to show that the Rouquier dimension of 𝒯 \mathcal{T} is at most one. Moreover, we prove that there is an additive equivalence (which preserves some of the triangulated structure) between 𝒯 \mathcal{T} and the perfect derived category of a certain DG algebra. Finally, we show that any triangulated category with a linear generator is additively equivalent to a thick subcategory of 𝒞 ¯ n \overline{\mathcal{C}}_{n} .

With this question in mind, we introduce in

Conventions

Throughout, we let
k
be an algebraically closed field.
All modules will be considered as right modules, unless otherwise stated, and we will compose paths from left to right.
That is, if
a
and
b
are paths, then we write
ab
to mean first apply
a
then
b
.
However, morphisms will be composed right to left,
i.e.
if

and
g:Y\rightarrow Z
, then
gf:X\rightarrow Z
.
For a category
\mathcal{C}
and two objects

, we write

.

We will assume that the suspension functor
[1]_{\mathcal{T}}
for a triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
has a natural inverse,
[-1]_{\mathcal{T}}
, and we will simply denote the suspension functor by
[1]
if the category is clear from context.
We will always take triangle to mean a distinguished triangle in a triangulated category.

For a graded/differential graded algebra
A
, we denote the degree

component of
A
by
A^{m}
.

2.
Triangulated Categories with a Linear Generator

This section introduces the concept of triangulated categories with linear generators. It turns out that the existence of such generators imply very strong and interesting properties on the triangulated category and its morphisms. We are interested in these types of categories as the main examples of categories in this paper satisfy this setup and, moreover, the abstract properties of this setup that are proved in this section will play a key role in the remainder of the paper.

Throughout, we will be interested in triangulated categories satisfying the following setup.

Setup 2.1
.

Let
k
be a field and let
\mathcal{T}
be a
k
-linear Hom-finite, Krull-Schmidt triangulated category.

2.1.
Linear Generators

We begin by recalling some standard notation and terminology concerning generators of triangulated categories.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category and consider a subcategory
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathcal{T}
. Recall
\mathcal{S}
is
thick
if it is a triangulated subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
closed under direct summands. Moreover, given an object
X\in\mathcal{T}
, we are interested in the following subcategories associated to
X
.

(1)

The
thick closure

\langle X\rangle
of
X
is the smallest thick subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
containing
X
.

(2)

The category
\langle X\rangle_{1}
is the smallest additive full subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
which contains
X
, and is closed under direct summands and the suspension functor
[1]
of
\mathcal{T}
.

(3)

The category
\langle X\rangle_{m}
is defined inductively as the full subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
consisting of objects
A\in\langle X\rangle_{m}
which fit into a triangle

B\rightarrow A\rightarrow C\rightarrow B[1],

where
B\in\langle X\rangle_{1}
and
C\in\langle X\rangle_{m-1}
. In other words,
\langle X\rangle_{m}:=\langle X\rangle_{1}\star\langle X\rangle_{m-1}
.

A
classical generator
of
\mathcal{T}
is an object
G\in\mathcal{T}
such that
\langle G\rangle=\mathcal{T}
. A classical generator is, moreover, a
strong generator
if
\langle G\rangle_{m}=\mathcal{T}
for some integer
m\geq 1
. Given a strong generator
G
, let
n
be the smallest positive integer such that
\langle G\rangle_{n}=\mathcal{T}
. Then, the
generation time
of
G
is
n-1
.

This section investigates the properties of Krull-Schmidt, Hom-finite,
k
-linear, triangulated categories equipped with a classical generator
G
such that
\langle G\rangle_{1}
satisfies a certain property.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category satisfying
2.1
and suppose that
G\in\mathcal{T}
is a classical generator. Then, we say that
G
is a
linear generator
if it satisfies the properties:

(G1)

The set of classes of indecomposable objects in
\langle G\rangle_{1}
admits a total ordering defined as:

For indecomposable objects
Q,P\in\langle G\rangle_{1}
, then
Q\leq P
if and only if
\mathcal{T}(P,Q)\cong k
. Moreover, if
Q\not\cong P
, then
\mathcal{T}(Q,P)=0
.

(G2)

Given an indecomposable object
P\in\langle G\rangle_{1}
, then
P\leq P[1]
.

(G3)

For indecomposable objects
P,Q\in\langle G\rangle_{1}
, then
P\leq Q\leq P[1]
implies that
Q
is isomorphic to at least one of
P
and
P[1]
.

(G4)

For indecomposable objects
R\leq Q\leq P
in
\langle G\rangle_{1}
, then any morphism from
P
to
R
factors non-trivially through
Q
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category satisfying
2.1
and admitting a linear generator
G
. Suppose that
f\colon P\rightarrow Q
is a non-zero morphism between non-isomorphic, indecomposable objects in
\langle G\rangle_{1}
. Then,
\mathrm{cone}\,f
is indecomposable in
\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

We will prove the statement by proving that
\mathrm{cone\,}{f}
has a local endomorphism ring.

Given that
f
is non-zero and
G
is a linear generator, then it must be that
Q\leq P
by property
(G1)
in
Definition

2.2
. If
Q\cong P
, then, since
\mathcal{T}(P,Q)\cong k
, it follows that
f
is an isomorphism and we may conclude that
\mathrm{cone\,}{f}=0
. Hence, it suffices to consider the case where
P
and
Q
are not isomorphic.

Consider the triangle

P\xrightarrow{f}Q\xrightarrow{g}\mathrm{cone}\,f\rightarrow P[1].

Applying the covariant functor

induces the long exact sequence

\mathcal{T}(P[1],P)\xrightarrow{\mathcal{T}(P[1],f)}\mathcal{T}(P[1],Q)\rightarrow\mathcal{T}(P[1],\mathrm{cone}\,f)\rightarrow\mathcal{T}(P[1],P[1])\xrightarrow{\mathcal{T}(P[1],f[1])}\mathcal{T}(P[1],Q[1])

It follows from properties
(G1)
and
(G2)
in
Definition

2.2
that
\mathcal{T}(P[1],P)\cong k
and
\mathcal{T}(P[1],Q)\cong k
. Further, observe that
\mathcal{T}(P[1],f)
and
\mathcal{T}(P[1],f[1])
are not the zero map since
f
is nonzero, and therefore, by dimension arguments, they must be isomorphisms. As a result, we may conclude from the long exact sequence that
\mathcal{T}(P[1],\mathrm{cone\,}{f})=0
. Similarly, applying
\mathcal{T}(P,-)
to the triangle defining
\mathrm{cone\,}{f}
shows that
\mathcal{T}(P,\mathrm{cone\,}{f})
vanishes.

Moreover, since
P\not\cong Q
, then
\mathcal{T}(Q,P)=0
by
Definition

2.2

(G1)
. Therefore, applying the functor
\mathcal{T}(Q,-)
to the triangle defining

shows that
\mathcal{T}(Q,\mathrm{cone\,}{f})\cong k
.

Finally, the contravariant functor
\mathcal{T}(-,\mathrm{cone}\,f)
applied to the triangle defining
\mathrm{cone\,}{f}
induces the exact sequence

\mathcal{T}(P[1],\mathrm{cone}\,f)\rightarrow\mathcal{T}(\mathrm{cone}\,f,\mathrm{cone}\,f)\rightarrow\mathcal{T}(Q,\mathrm{cone}\,f)\rightarrow\mathcal{T}(P,\mathrm{cone}\,f).

Since
\mathcal{T}(P,\mathrm{cone\,}{f})
,
\mathcal{T}(P[1],\mathrm{cone\,}{f})
vanish, and
\mathcal{T}(Q,\mathrm{cone\,}{f})\cong k
, then it must be that
\mathcal{T}(\mathrm{cone\,}{f},\mathrm{cone\,}{f})\cong k
. That is,
\mathrm{cone\,}{f}
has a local endomorphism ring, whence
\mathrm{cone}\,f
is indecomposable.

2.2.
Morphism Spaces

Given a triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
as in
2.1

, this subsection aims to describe morphisms in
\mathcal{T}(X,Y)

Lemma 2.5
.

With notation as above, then
\mathcal{T}(X_{P,Q},R)\cong k
if and only if
Q[1]<R\leq P[1]
. Otherwise,
\mathcal{T}(X_{P,Q},R)=0
.

Proof.

Applying the functor
\mathcal{T}(-,R)
to the triangle

P\xrightarrow{f}Q\rightarrow X_{P,Q}\rightarrow P[1]

defining
X_{P,Q}
induces the long exact sequence

\mathcal{T}(Q[1],R)\rightarrow\mathcal{T}(P[1],R)\rightarrow\mathcal{T}(X_{P,Q},R)\rightarrow\mathcal{T}(Q,R)\rightarrow\mathcal{T}(P,R).

Observe that
Q\leq P
since
X_{P,Q}
is the cone of a non-zero morphism
P\to Q
by assumption.

Suppose first that
Q[1]<R\leq P[1]
, then we will show that
\mathcal{T}(X_{P,Q},R)\cong k
. Since
R\leq P[1]
,
\mathcal{T}(P[1],R)\cong k
. Moreover, since
Q\leq Q[1]<R
, then
\mathcal{T}(Q,R)
and
\mathcal{T}(Q[1],R)
vanish and so we may conclude that
\mathcal{T}(X_{P,Q},R)\cong k
.

For the converse, suppose that
Q[1]<R\leq P[1]
does not hold. Then, either
R=Q[1]
,
R<Q[1]
or
P[1]<R
. Consider the latter case where
Q\leq P\leq P[1]<R
. Then,
\mathcal{T}(P,R)
,
\mathcal{T}(Q,R)
, and
\mathcal{T}(P[1],R)
all vanish, which, by the long exact sequence, forces
\mathcal{T}(X_{P,Q},R)
to vanish and proves the the claim for the
P[1]<R

Assume next that
R<Q[1]\leq P[1]
. Then, the Hom spaces
\mathcal{T}(Q,R)
,
\mathcal{T}(P,R)
,
\mathcal{T}(Q[1],R)
, and
\mathcal{T}(P[1],R)
are all one-dimensional. Hence, if
\mathcal{T}(f,R)
and
\mathcal{T}(f[1],R)
are nonzero, then they are isomorphisms, and so it must be that
\mathcal{T}(X_{P,Q},R)=0
by the long exact sequence.

To see that
\mathcal{T}(f,R)
is non-zero, observe that since,
\mathcal{T}(P,R)
is one dimensional, then we may choose a non-zero morphism
\alpha_{P,R}\in\mathcal{T}(P,R)

(G4)
of

\beta\in\mathcal{T}(Q,R)
and
\gamma\in\mathcal{T}(P,Q)
such that
\alpha_{P,R}=\beta\cdot\gamma

is nonzero and
\mathcal{T}(P,Q)
is one-dimensional, there is a non-zero

such that

. Since composition is k-linear, this means that

\mathcal{T}(f,R)\left(\frac{1}{c}\beta\right)=\frac{1}{c}\beta\cdot f=\beta\cdot\gamma=\alpha_{P,R}\neq 0

as required. A similar proof works to show that
\mathcal{T}(f[1],R)\neq 0
.

. If

\mathcal{T}(Q,R)
vanishes, and
\mathcal{T}(P[1],R)
,
\mathcal{T}(Q[1],R)

. Thus, if

\mathcal{T}(P,R)

\mathcal{T}(Q[1],R)

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category satisfying

and equipped with a linear generator

. Suppose that

. Then, a morphism

Applying the functor

to the triangle

induces the exact sequence

\mathcal{T}(R,P)=\mathcal{T}(R,P[1])=0
, hence

is an isomorphism. Therefore, any morphism

factors through a morphism

. Moreover, because

, then property

in

2.2

(and thus

.

Proposition 2.10

\mathcal{T}

G
. Suppose that

\langle G\rangle_{1}

It suffices to show that any morphism

is such that
\mathrm{cone\,}{g}\cong A\oplus B

for all

if necessary, we may assume

. We may thus construct the following morphism of triangles.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}P_{i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{P,P_{1}}\oplus\bigoplus_{i=2}^{n}P_{i}}

.

\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category satisfying

and equipped with a linear generator

. Let

be indecomposable objects in

. Then,

(2)

.

) is a forwards morphism, and a morphism of type (

induces the long exact sequence

. Then, we will show that

2.6
, since

. Moreover, because

\mathcal{T}(P,X_{R,S})

that

, as required.

Finally, consider the case that

, the Hom spaces

Remark 2.14

As a corollary of

2.3.
Rouquier Dimension

This section uses the properties of linear generators established in the previous sections, in order to compute the Rouquier dimension of a triangulated category admitting a linear generator.

Lemma 2.15

Let

be a triangulated category satisfying

Since

are indecomposable objects in

.

By

Z_{i}\cong Q\in\langle G\rangle_{1}
or

if

Furthermore, for all

Theorem 2.16
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category satisfying
2.1
, and equipped with a linear generator
G
. Then, category
\langle G\rangle_{2}
is triangulated, and hence equivalent to
\mathcal{T}
. In other words, the generation time of
G
is
1
.

Proof.

To show that
\langle G\rangle_{2}
is triangulated, it suffices to show that
\langle G\rangle_{3}=\langle G\rangle_{2}
. That it is, it is enough to show that the cone of any morphism
f\colon P\rightarrow Z
with
P\in\langle G\rangle_{1}
and
Z\in\langle G\rangle_{2}
is in
\langle G\rangle_{2}
.
Since
\mathcal{T}
is Krull-Schmidt, we may write
P=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}P_{i}
, where
P_{i}
are indecomposable objects, and
f=(f_{i}\colon P_{i}\rightarrow Z)_{i=1}^{n}
. We will show that
\mathrm{cone\,}{f}\in\langle G\rangle_{2}
by induction on
n
. The
n=1
case is
Lemma

2.15
. Suppose by induction that
\mathrm{cone\,}{f^{\prime}}\in\langle G\rangle_{2}
for all

where
Q=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n-1}Q_{i}
for indecomposable objects
Q_{i}\in\langle G\rangle_{1}
.

By reindexing if necessary, assume
P_{i}\leq P_{1}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. By applying the octahedral axiom, it is possible to construct the following commutative diagram with exact rows and columns.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P/P_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P_{1}[1]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{cone}\,g}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P[1]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{cone}\,f}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{cone}\,h}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P_{1}[1]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P[1]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P/P_{1}[1]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P_{1}[2].}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(1,0,\ldots)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}

As an immediate corollary of
Theorem

2.16
, we are able to compute the Roquier dimension of certain triangulated categories equipped with a linear generator. Recall that the
Orlov spectrum

\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{S})
of a triangulated category
\mathcal{S}
is the collection of generation times across all classical generators in
\mathcal{S}

[
Orl09
]
. Further, the
Rouquier dimension
of a triangulated category is the infimum of
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{S})

[
Rou08
]
.

Corollary 2.17
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category satisfying
2.1
, and equipped with a linear generator
G
. Then,
\mathcal{T}
has Rouquier dimension 1 if and only if
G\not\cong G[1]
.

Proof.

Suppose that
G\not\cong G[1]
. In view of
Theorem

2.16
, it is suffices to show that the Rouquier dimension of
\mathcal{T}
is greater than zero. That is, it suffices to show that there does not exist an object
G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}
such that
\langle G^{\prime}\rangle_{1}=\mathcal{T}
.

Observe first that, since
\mathcal{T}
is Krull-Schmidt,
G^{\prime}
is a finite direct sum of indecomposable objects in
\mathcal{T}
, and so
\langle G^{\prime}\rangle_{1}
has finitely many indecomposable objects up to suspension. However, when
G\not\cong G[1]
, then we can fix
P\in\langle G\rangle_{1}
such that
P\not\cong P[1]
, which in turn, implies that
P[i-1]<P[i]
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. Thus, by
Corollary

2.7
the collection of objects
\{X_{P,P[i]}\}_{i<0}
is infinite. Again, by
Corollary

2.7
, since
P
is fixed, then
X_{P,P[i]}\not\cong X_{P,P[i^{\prime}]}[j]
, for all
i,i^{\prime}<0
and
j\in\mathbb{Z}
. Hence, the collection
\{X_{P,P[i]}\}_{i<0}
is an infinite collection of indecomposable objects which are not isomorphic and are not suspensions of each other. It thus follows that this collection is not in
\langle G^{\prime}\rangle_{1}
, from which we conclude that
\langle G^{\prime}\rangle_{1}\subsetneq\mathcal{T}
for all objects

, and so the Rouquier dimension of
\mathcal{T}
is greater than zero.

Now consider the case
G\cong G[1]
. Then,
\langle G\rangle_{1}
has finitely many isomorphism classes of indecomposable objects. Hence, it follows from
Proposition

2.10
and
Theorem

2.16
that
\mathcal{T}
has finitely many isomorphism classes of indecomposable objects
\{P_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
. Hence, by letting
G^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}P_{i}
, then
\mathcal{T}=\langle G^{\prime}\rangle_{1}
and we may conclude that
\mathcal{T}
has Rouquier dimension 0 if there exists a linear generator
G\cong G[1]
.
∎

\mathcal{T}(X_{P,Q},P^{\prime}\oplus X_{R,Q^{\prime}}[-1])
and
\mathcal{T}(X_{P,Q},P^{\prime}[1]\oplus X_{R,Q^{\prime}})
vanish, so that we may conclude that
\mathcal{T}(X_{P,Q},g)
is an isomorphism.

To prove that converse, assume that
Q[1]<Q^{\prime}\leq R\leq P[1]<P^{\prime}
does not hold. Observe that we may assume
\mathcal{T}(R,X_{P^{\prime},Q^{\prime}})\neq 0
,
\mathcal{T}(X_{P,Q},R)\neq 0
and
\mathcal{T}(X_{P,Q},X_{P^{\prime},Q^{\prime}})\neq 0
, since otherwise, the statement is clear. Thus, we may assume that
Q^{\prime}\leq R<P^{\prime}
,
\,Q[1]<R\leq P[1]
and that either (1)
\,Q^{\prime}\leq Q<P^{\prime}\leq P
or (2)
\,Q[1]<Q^{\prime}\leq P[1]<P^{\prime}
. Combining (2) with
Q^{\prime}\leq R\leq P[1]
implies that
Q[1]<Q^{\prime}\leq R\leq P[1]<P^{\prime}
, which is a contradiction since we assumed this does not hold. Hence, (1) must hold. Combining (1) and
Q[1]\leq R<P^{\prime}
implies that
Q^{\prime}\leq Q\leq Q[1]<R<P^{\prime}\leq P
holds. Similarly to the proof of
Lemma

2.22
, this implies that there is an exact sequence of vector spaces

k\xrightarrow{\mathcal{T}(X_{P,Q},g)}k\to k^{2}\to k

and so
\mathcal{T}(X_{P,Q},g)=0
. That is, the morphism
f
cannot factor through
g
.
∎

We may now complete our computations for the cone of a morphism between indecomposable objects.

Let

2.5.
Factorisation Properties, Composition and Triangles

In this section, we consolidate the results established thus far into a handful of propositions that are easier to track. Our first result summarises the factorisation properties of morphisms between indecomposable objects.

Proposition 2.26
.

Let
Y,Z\in\mathcal{T}
be non-isomorphic indecomposable objects such that
\mathcal{T}(Y,Z)\cong k
.
Then, any nonzero morphism
f:Y\rightarrow Z
factors through an indecomposable object
W
if and only if there exist non-zero morphisms
g:Y\rightarrow W
and
h:W\rightarrow Z
, such that

(1)

if
f
is a forward morphism, then
g
and
h
are both forward morphisms,

(2)

if
f
is a backwards morphism, then one of
g
or
h
is a backwards morphism and the other is a forwards morphism.

Proof.

There are eight cases for us to consider, where each of
Y,Z,W
are either of the form
R\in\langle G\rangle_{1}
or of the form
X_{P,Q}
. However, seven of these cases have been considered.

First, when
Y,Z,W\in\langle G\rangle_{1}
, then the statement follows from
Definition

2.2
and
Lemma

2.12
(
1
). Further, for
Y,W\in\langle G\rangle_{1}
and
Z=X_{P,Q}
, then the statement is a consequence of
Lemmas

2.8
and
2.12
(
1
). The third case, where
Y=X_{P,Q}
and
Z,W\in\langle G\rangle_{1}
, is implied by
Lemmas

2.9
and
2.12
(
2
). Next, when
Y\in\langle G\rangle_{1}
,
Z=X_{P,Q}
and
W=X_{P^{\prime},Q^{\prime}}
, then the statement is entailed by
Lemmas

2.12
,
2.13
and
2.22
. The fifth case, where
Y=X_{P,Q}
,
Z\in\langle G\rangle_{1}
and
W=X_{P^{\prime},Q^{\prime}}
, can be deduced by applying
Lemmas

2.12
,
2.13
and
2.23
. Additionally, the situation where
Y=X_{P,Q}
,
Z=X_{P^{\prime},Q^{\prime}}
and
W\in\langle G\rangle_{1}
results from applying
Lemmas

2.12
,
2.13
and
2.24
. The seventh case, where
Y,Z\in\langle G\rangle_{1}
and
W\cong X_{P,Q}
follows from
Lemmas

2.12
and
2.21
.

Consider the final case, where
Y\cong X_{P,Q}
,
Z\cong X_{P^{\prime},Q^{\prime}}
and
W\cong X_{R,S}
. If there does not exists a nonzero morphism
g\colon Y\to W
, then the statement is clear. Hence, we may assume that such a morphism exists. The functor
\mathcal{T}(-,Z)
induces the exact sequence

(2.D)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(Y[1],Z)\rightarrow\mathcal{T}(\mathrm{cone\,}{g},Z)\rightarrow\mathcal{T}(W,Z)\xrightarrow{\mathcal{T}(g,Z)}\mathcal{T}(Y,Z)\rightarrow\mathcal{T}(\mathrm{cone\,}{g}[-1],Z)\rightarrow\mathcal{T}(W[-1],Z)

We begin by showing that if either (
1
) or (
2
) hold, then
f
factors through
W
. Suppose first that (
1
) holds. That is,
f\colon Y\to Z
is a fowards morphism and there exist forwards morphisms
g\colon Y\to W
and
h\colon W\to Z
. We will show that
f
factors through
W
by proving that
\mathcal{T}(g,Z)
is an isomorphism. Due to
Lemma

2.13
, it must be that
Q^{\prime}\leq S\leq Q<P^{\prime}\leq R\leq P
. Moreover, by
Lemma

2.25
,
\mathrm{cone\,}{g}\cong X_{P,R}\oplus X_{Q,S}
. It thus follows from
Lemma

2.13
that
\mathcal{T}(\mathrm{cone\,}{g},Z)=0
, and so (
2.D
) implies that there is an exact sequence

0\rightarrow k\xrightarrow{\mathcal{T}(g,Z)}k

Hence, we may conclude that
\mathcal{T}(g,Z)
is an isomorphism, as required.

Next, assume that
f\colon Y\to Z
is a backwards morphism and that there exist a forwards morphism
g\colon Y\to W
and a backwards morphism
h\colon W\to Z
. Again, we will show that
f
factors through
W
by showing that
\mathcal{T}(g,Z)
is an isomorphism. Our assumptions imply that
S\leq Q[1]<Q^{\prime}\leq R[1]\leq P[1]<P^{\prime}
by
Lemma

2.13
and that
\mathrm{cone\,}{g}\cong X_{P,R}\oplus X_{Q,S}
by
Lemma

2.25
. As before, this implies that
\mathcal{T}(\mathrm{cone\,}{g},Z)=0
and we may deduce that
\mathcal{T}(g,Z)
is an isomorphism.

Finally, assume that
f\colon Y\to Z
is a backwards morphism and that there exist a backwards morphism
g\colon Y\to W
and a forwards morphism
h\colon W\to Z
. Then,
Q[1]<Q^{\prime}\leq S\leq P[1]<P^{\prime}\leq R
by
Lemma

2.13
and
\mathrm{cone\,}{g}\cong X_{R,Q}\oplus X_{P,S}
by
Lemma

2.25
. Therefore,
\mathcal{T}(\mathrm{cone\,}{g},Z)=0
and
\mathcal{T}(g,Z)
is an isomorphism. Consequently, if either (
1
) or (
2
) hold, then
f
factors through
W
.

To prove the converse, suppose that
f\colon Y\to Z
is such that
f=h\circ g
where
g\colon Y\to W
and
h\colon W\to Z
. Assume first that
f
is a forwards morphism. We will show that (
1
) holds by showing that all other cases lead to a contradiction. If
f
is forwards and both
g
and
h
are backwards, then
Lemma

2.13
implies that
S<Q^{\prime}\leq Q\leq Q[1]<S
, which is a contradiction. Whence,
g
and
h
cannot both be backwards.

On the other hand, if
f
is forwards,
g
is backwards and
h
is forwards, then
Q^{\prime}\leq Q[1]<S<P^{\prime}\leq P[1]<R
by
Lemma

2.13
. By the same lemma, (
2.D
) induces the exact sequence

\mathcal{T}(Y[1],Z)\rightarrow k^{2}\rightarrow k\xrightarrow{\mathcal{T}(g,Z)}k.

Since
\mathcal{T}(Y[1],Z)
is at most one dimensional, then
\mathcal{T}(g,Z)
cannot be an isomorphism, whence it is zero. We conclude that
f=h\circ g=0
, which is a contradiction.

By the same arguement, it cannot be that
f
is a forwards morphsm,
g
is a forwards morphism and
h
is a backwards morphism. We thus conclude that if
f
is a forwards morphism, then both
g
and
h
are forwards morphisms. That is, (
1
) holds.

Next, suppose that
f
is a backwards morphism. We will show that (
2
) holds by showing that the two other possible cases lead to contradictions. If both
g
and
h
are backwards, then by
Lemma

2.13
, (
2.D
) induces again the exact sequence (
2.5
). Therefore, it must be that
\mathcal{T}(g,Z)=0
and
f=h\circ g=0
, which is a contradiction. Furthermore, if
g
and
h
are both forwards, then
Lemma

2.13
implies that
S\leq Q\leq Q[1]<Q^{\prime}\leq S
, which is a contradiction. Consequently, if
f
is a backwards morphism, then (
2
) holds. ∎

.

Let

∎

2.6.
Functors Preserving Linear Generators

We aim to understand functors between triangulated categories satisfying
2.1
with linear generators, and what properties these functors may preserve under mild assumptions.

Recall that a functor
\mathscr{F}:\mathcal{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}
between triangulated categories commutes with the respective suspension functors if there exists a natural isomorphism
\phi\colon\mathscr{F}\circ[1]_{\mathcal{T}}\xrightarrow{\sim}[1]_{\mathcal{S}}\circ\mathscr{F}
. Moreover, the functor
\mathscr{F}
preserves a triangle

X\xlongrightarrow{f}Y\xlongrightarrow{g}Z\xlongrightarrow{h}X[1]_{\mathcal{T}},

in
\mathcal{T}
if

\mathscr{F}(X)\xrightarrow{\mathscr{F}(f)}\mathscr{F}(Y)\xrightarrow{\mathscr{F}(g)}\mathscr{F}(Z)\xrightarrow{\mathscr{F}(h)}\mathscr{F}(X[1]_{\mathcal{T}}),

is a triangle in
\mathcal{S}
.

Theorem 2.29
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{S}
be triangulated categories satisfying
2.1
and admitting linear generators
G
and
G^{\prime}
, respectively. If there is a fully faithful additive functor
F\colon\langle G\rangle_{1}\rightarrow\langle G^{\prime}\rangle_{1}
that commutes with the respective suspension functors, then there exists a fully faithful additive functor
\mathscr{F}:\mathcal{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}
which commutes with suspension and preserves triangles with at least two indecomposable terms.

Proof.

Let
\{\alpha^{Y,Z}\}_{Y,Z\in\mathrm{ind}\,\mathcal{T}}
be a choice of generators in as in
Proposition

2.27
. Then, by
Corollary

A.2
, the generators
\{F(\alpha^{Y,Z})\}_{Y,Z\in\langle G^{\prime}\rangle_{1}\,\mathcal{T}}
can be extended to a choice of generators
\{\beta^{Y,Z}\}_{Y,Z\in\mathcal{S}}
which is compatible with composition.

Let
P,Q\in\langle G\rangle_{1}
be non-isomorphic indecomposable objects. Then, by
Corollary

2.28
, any triangle with vertices
P\not\cong Q
in
\mathcal{T}
and any triangle with vertices
F(P),F(Q)\in\mathcal{S}
is isomorphic to

(2.I)

\displaystyle P\xrightarrow{\alpha^{P,Q}}Q\xrightarrow{\delta_{\mathcal{T}}\alpha^{Q,X_{P,Q}}}X_{P,Q}\xrightarrow{\alpha^{X_{P,Q},P[1]}}P[1]

(2.J)

\displaystyle F(P)\xrightarrow{\beta^{F(P),F(Q)}}F(Q)\xrightarrow{\delta_{\mathcal{S}}\beta^{F(Q),X_{F(P),F(Q)}}}X_{F(P),F(Q)}\xrightarrow{\beta^{X_{F(P),F(Q)},F(P)[1]}}F(P)[1]

respectively. Here,
\delta_{\mathcal{T}},\delta_{\mathcal{S}}\in k^{\times}
are scalars that only depend on the generators for Hom spaces that we have chosen in
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{S}
and not on the objects
P,Q
.

We may "change basis"
\{\beta^{X,Y}\}
in
\mathcal{S}
in the following way. We first let
\gamma^{P^{\prime},Q^{\prime}}=\beta^{P^{\prime},Q^{\prime}}
for all indecomposable objects
P^{\prime},Q^{\prime}\in\langle G^{\prime}\rangle_{1}
. Then, for indecomposable objects
F(Q)\in\langle G^{\prime}\rangle_{1}
and
Z=X_{F(P),F(Q)}\not\in\langle G^{\prime}\rangle_{1}
, let
\gamma^{F(Q),Z}=\delta_{\mathcal{S}}\delta_{\mathcal{T}}^{-1}\beta^{F(Q),Z}
. Moreover, for indecomposable objects
Q^{\prime}\in\langle G^{\prime}\rangle_{1}
and
Z^{\prime}=X_{P^{\prime},Q^{\prime}}\not\in\langle G^{\prime}\rangle_{1}
which are not in the essential image of
F
, let
\gamma^{Q^{\prime},Z}=\beta^{Q^{\prime},Z}
. Then, we use
Proposition

2.27
to complete this choice of generators to
\{\gamma^{Y,Z}\}_{Y,Z\in\mathrm{ind}\,\mathcal{S}}
. By the proof of
Proposition

2.27
, it is straightforward to check that
\gamma^{Z,F(P)[1]}=\beta^{Z,F(P)[1]}
. Under this choice of basis, we may rewrite the triangles (
2.I
) to

(2.K)

\displaystyle P\xrightarrow{\alpha^{P,Q}}Q\xrightarrow{\delta_{\mathcal{T}}\alpha^{Q,X_{P,Q}}}X_{P,Q}\xrightarrow{\alpha^{X_{P,Q},P[1]}}P[1]

(2.L)

\displaystyle F(P)\xrightarrow{\gamma^{F(P),F(Q)}}F(Q)\xrightarrow{\delta_{\mathcal{T}}\gamma^{F(Q),X_{F(P),F(Q)}}}X_{F(P),F(Q)}\xrightarrow{\gamma^{X_{F(P),F(Q)},F(P)[1]}}F(P)[1].

Let us now define the functor
\mathscr{F}\colon\mathcal{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}
. Note that, since
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{T}
are Krull-Schmidt, it suffices to define
\mathscr{F}
on indecomposable objects. For
Y\in\mathcal{T}
indecomposable, if

then we let
\mathscr{F}(Y)=F(Y)
. Otherwise, if

, then

for some

and so we let

. For a morphism

, we let
\mathscr{F}(\alpha^{Y,Z})=\gamma^{Y,Z}
and extend linearly over
k
.

Given indecomposable objects
Y,Z\in\mathcal{T}
, the linear map
\mathscr{F}\colon\mathcal{T}(Y,Z)\to\mathcal{S}(\mathscr{F}(Y),\mathscr{F}(Z))
is bijective since it maps basis to basis. Since our categories are Krull-Schmidt, this is enough to conclude that
\mathscr{F}
is fully faithful.

Moreover,
\mathscr{F}
commutes with the suspension functor. To see this, let
P,Q\in\langle G\rangle_{1}
. Then, there is a natural isomorphism

\mathscr{F}(P[1])=F(P[1])\cong F(P)[1]=\mathscr{F}(P)[1]

by assumption on
F
. On the other hand, for
Y=X_{P^{\prime},Q^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}
and

, recall that by
Remark

2.14
, there are canonical isomorphisms

and

, so that we may identify

and

. Therefore,

\mathscr{F}(Z[1])=\mathscr{F}(X_{P[1],Q[1]})=X_{F(P[1]),F(Q[1])}\cong X_{F(P)[1],F(Q)[1]}=X_{F(P),F(Q)}[1]=\mathscr{F}(Z)[1].

where the isomorphisms involved are natural by assumption on
F
. Again, since our categories are Krull-Schmidt, this is enough to conclude that
\mathscr{F}
commutes with suspension.

Finally, given the definition of
\mathscr{F}
, then

2.28
implies that
\mathscr{F}
preserves all triangles with three indecomposable objects. Hence, it remains to show that
\mathscr{F}
preserves triangles with exactly two indecomposable terms. Well, the image of a triangle in
\mathcal{T}
with two indecomposable summands under
\mathscr{F}
is

\mathscr{F}(X)\xrightarrow{\begin{pmatrix}\gamma^{\mathscr{F}(X),\mathscr{F}(Z)}\\
\gamma^{\mathscr{F}(X),\mathscr{F}(Z^{\prime})}\end{pmatrix}}\mathscr{F}(Z)\oplus\mathscr{F}(Z^{\prime})\xrightarrow{(\gamma^{\mathscr{F}(Z),\mathscr{F}(Y)},\gamma^{\mathscr{F}(Z^{\prime}),\mathscr{F}(Y)})}\mathscr{F}(Y)\xrightarrow{\delta_{\mathcal{S}}\gamma^{\mathscr{F}(Y),\mathscr{F}(X)[1]}}\mathscr{F}(X)[1]

which, by

2.28
and our choice of

, is a triangle in
\mathcal{S}
.
∎

We find an immediate corollary to Theorem
Theorem

2.29
.

.

Let

\mathcal{T}

3.
Graded Endomorphism Algebras of Linear Generators

3.1.
The Algebra
\Lambda^{G}

In this subsection, we are interested in computing the graded endomorphism algebra of a
minimal
linear generator
G
. of
\mathcal{T}
.
Recall from
[
Mur25
]
that a generator is minimal if there exists no proper direct summand
H
of
G
, such that
H
is a generator of
\mathcal{T}
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category. Recall that the
graded endomorphism ring
of an object
Y\in\mathcal{T}
is the graded ring
\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(Y)
which, at degree
i
, is given by

\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{i}(Y)=\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{T}}^{i}(Y,Y)=\mathcal{T}(Y,Y[i]).

For
f\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{i}(Y)
and
g\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{j}(Y)
, multiplication is given by the rule

g\cdot f:=g[i]\circ f\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{i+j}(M).

Notice that if
\mathcal{T}
is Krull-Schmidt and
Y=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}Y_{i}
, then
\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(Y)
can be written as a graded generalised
m\times m
-matrix ring

\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(Y)=\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(Y_{1})&\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(Y_{2},Y_{1})&\dotsb&\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(Y_{m},Y_{1})\\
\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(Y_{1},Y_{2})&\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(Y_{2})&\dotsb&\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(Y_{m},Y_{2})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(Y_{1},Y_{m})&\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(Y_{2},Y_{m})&\dotsb&\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(Y_{m})\end{pmatrix}

where
\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(Y_{i})
are seen as graded rings and
\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(Y_{i},Y_{j})
are seen as graded
\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(Y_{j})
-
\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(Y_{i})
-bimodules.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category satisfying
2.1
and admitting a minimal linear generator
G=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}G_{i}
with
m
indecomposable summands. Then,
\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(G)
is isomorphic to the upper triangular
m\times m
-matrix algebra
\chi^{G}
, with

\chi^{G}_{i,j}:=\begin{cases}k[x]&\text{if $i=j$ and $G_{i}<G_{i}[1]$}\\
0&\text{if $j<i$}\\
k[x^{\pm}]&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

where
x
is concentrated in degree
-1
.

Proof.

Let
\{\alpha^{Y,Z}\}_{Y,Z\in\mathrm{ind}\,\mathcal{T}}
be a choice of generators of Hom spaces as in
Proposition

2.27
. Moreover, by reordering if necessary, we assume that
G_{j}<G_{i}
for all
j<i
. By the definition of a linear generator, these assumptions imply that
G_{j}[t]<G_{i}
for all
t\in\mathbb{Z}
. To simplify notation, let
\Lambda:=\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(G)
. Throughout the proof,
k[x]
and
k[x^{\pm}]
are graded rings with grading defined by considering
x
as a homogeneous element of degree
-1
.

We begin by constructing isomorphisms
\Lambda_{i,j}\cong\chi^{G}_{i,j}
that repect grading. Observe first that the definition of a linear generator implies that

\Lambda_{i,j}^{t}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(G_{j},G_{i}[t])=k\cdot\alpha^{G_{j},G_{i}[t]}

for all
i,j,t\in\mathbb{Z}
, and where
\alpha^{G_{j},G_{i}[t]}
might possibly be zero.

If
j<i
, then
G_{j}<G_{i}[t]
for all
t\in\mathbb{Z}
and so

\alpha^{G_{j},G_{i}[t]}=0
by the definition of a linear generator. Hence,
\Lambda_{i,j}=0=\chi^{G}_{i,j}
.

Consider next the case
j=i
and
G_{i}<G_{i}[1]
. If
0<t
, then
G_{i}<G_{i}[t]
so that
\Lambda_{i,i}^{t}=0
. If, however,
t\leq 0
, then
\Lambda_{i,i}^{t}\neq 0
. In fact, For
t,s\leq 0
, observe that

\alpha^{G_{i},G_{i}[t]}\cdot\alpha^{G_{i},G_{i}[s]}=\alpha^{G_{i},G_{i}[t]}[s]\circ\alpha^{G_{i},G_{i}[s]}=\alpha^{G_{i},G_{i}[t+s]},

and we may construct a graded algebra isomorphism

\Lambda_{i,i}\to k[x]\colon\alpha^{G_{i},G_{i}[t]}\mapsto x^{t}

for all
t\leq 0
.

On the other hand, if
j=i
and
G_{i}\cong G_{i}[1]
, then
\Lambda_{i,j}^{t}\neq 0
for all
t
and so there is an algebra isomorphism

\Lambda_{i,i}\to k[x^{\pm}]\colon\alpha^{G_{i},G_{i}[t]}\mapsto x^{t}

for all
t\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Next, suppose
i<j
, so that
G_{i}<G_{j}
and
G_{i}[t]<G_{j}
for all
t\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let
s,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
. If
s\leq 0
or
G_{i}=G_{i}[1]
, and if
\ell\leq 0
or
G_{j}=G_{i}[1]
, then

\displaystyle\alpha^{G_{i},G_{i}[s]}\cdot\alpha^{G_{j},G_{i}[t]}=\alpha^{G_{i}[t],G_{i}[s+t]}\circ\alpha^{G_{j},G_{i}[t]}=\alpha^{G_{j},G_{i}[s+t]}

\displaystyle\alpha^{G_{j},G_{i}[t]}\cdot\alpha^{G_{j},G_{j}[\ell]}=\alpha^{G_{j}[\ell],G_{i}[\ell+t]}\circ\alpha^{G_{j},G_{j}[\ell]}=\alpha^{G_{j},G_{i}[\ell+t]}.

Hence, under the identification
\Lambda_{i,i}\cong k[x]
or
k[x^{\pm}]
and
\Lambda_{j,j}\cong k[x]
or
k[x^{\pm}]
, then there is a graded
\Lambda_{i,i}
-
\Lambda_{j,j}
-bimodule isomorphism

\Lambda_{i,j}\to k[x^{\pm}]\colon\alpha^{G_{j},G_{i}[t]}\mapsto x^{t}

for all
t\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Finally, we let
\phi\colon\Lambda\to\chi^{G}
be the graded ring morphism specified by

[\phi([\alpha^{G_{i},G_{j}[t]}]_{i,j})]_{i,j}:=\begin{cases}x^{t}&\text{if $i=j$, $G_{i}<G_{i}[1]$ and $t\leq 0$}\\
0&\text{if $i=j$, $G_{i}<G_{i}[1]$ and $0<t$}\\
0&\text{if $j<i$}\\
x^{t}&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

It is straightforward to check that this is indeed a graded ring isomorphism.
∎

Let
G\in\mathcal{T}
be a linear generator of a triangulated category satisfying
2.1
.
Then we define
\Lambda^{G}
to be the differential graded algebra with underlying graded algebra
\chi^{G}
and with a trivial differential,
d=0
. Our goal for the remainder of this section is to show that
\Lambda^{G}
is a linear generator of the triangulated category
\perf(\Lambda^{G})
, so that we may construct an equivalence
\mathcal{T}\xrightarrow{\sim}\perf(\Lambda^{G})
. We first recall a well-known lemma that will be useful to us.

Lemma 3.3

(See e.g.
[
Kra15
, Prop. 2.3]
or
[
JY22
, A.1 (iv)]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an additive idempotent complete category and consider objects
X,Y,Z\in\mathcal{A}
. If
Y\in\add_{\mathcal{A}}X
, then functor
\mathcal{A}{X}{-}
induces an isomorphism

\mathcal{A}({Y},{Z})\to\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{A}}(X)}(\mathcal{A}({X},{Y}),\mathcal{A}({X},{Z})).

which is natural in
Y
and
Z
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
G\in\mathcal{T}
be a minimal linear generator of a triangulated category satisfying
2.1
. Then, the functor

\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(G,-)\colon\langle G\rangle_{1}\to\langle\Lambda^{G}\rangle_{1}

is an equivalence.

Proof.

Let
G=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}G_{i}
. We begin by showing that the functor
\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(G,-)
is fully faithful. This amounts to showing that the assignment

\mathcal{T}(G_{i},G_{j}[t])\to\mathrm{Hom}_{\Lambda^{G}}(\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(G,G_{i}),\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(G,G_{j}[t]))

sending
f\colon G_{i}\to G_{j}[t]
to
f\circ(-)
is bijective. This is essentially a consequence
Lemma

3.3
, as we will explain below.

Observe first that morphism
f\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\Lambda^{G}}(\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(G,G_{i}),\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(G,G_{j}[t]))
is equivalent to the data

(f^{s}\colon\mathcal{T}(G,G_{i}[s])\to\mathcal{T}(G,G_{j}[s+t]))_{s\in\mathbb{Z}},

where for all for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{T}(G,G_{i}[s])
and
\lambda\in(\Lambda^{G})^{\ell}
,
f^{s}(\alpha)\cdot\lambda=f^{s+\ell}(\alpha\cdot\lambda)
.

We claim that
f
is uniquely determined by
f^{0}
. To see this, note that, under the graded algebra isomorphism of
Proposition

3.2
, then
\alpha\in\mathcal{T}(G,G_{i}[s])
can be written as
\alpha=(a_{\ell}x^{s})_{\ell=1}^{m}
with
a_{\ell}\in k
for all
1\leq\ell\leq m
. Let
I_{-s}\in\Lambda^{G}
denote the
m\times m
diagonal matrix with entries
x^{-s}
along the diagonal. Then,

f^{s}(\alpha)\cdot I_{-s}=f^{0}((\alpha_{\ell}x^{s})_{\ell=1}^{m}I_{-s})=f^{0}((\alpha_{\ell})_{\ell=1}^{m}),

and so

f^{s}(\alpha)=f^{0}((\alpha_{\ell})_{\ell=1}^{m})I_{s}.

Hence, the morphisms
f^{s}
are all uniquely determined by
f^{0}
. That is,
f
is determined by its component
f^{0}
.

Furthermore, morphisms
f^{0}
are precisely the morphisms in
\mathrm{Hom}_{(\Lambda^{G})^{0}}(\mathcal{T}(G,G_{i}),\mathcal{T}(G,G_{j}[t]))
. By definition,
(\Lambda^{G})^{0}=\mathcal{T}(G,G)
and
G_{i}\in\add_{\mathcal{T}}G
, so that by
Lemma

3.3
, there are isomorphisms

\mathcal{T}(G_{i},G_{j}[t])\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{Hom}_{(\Lambda^{G})^{0}}(\mathcal{T}(G,G_{i}),\mathcal{T}(G,G_{j}[t]))

sending
h\colon G_{i}\to G_{j}[t]
to
h\circ(-)
.

Since morphisms
f\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\Lambda^{G}}(\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(G,G_{i}),\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(G,G_{j}[t]))
are determined by the component
f^{0}
, this is enough to conclude that the assignment

\mathcal{T}(G_{i},G_{j}[t])\to\mathrm{Hom}_{\Lambda^{G}}(\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(G,G_{i}),\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(G,G_{j}[t]))

sending
f\colon G_{i}\to G_{j}[t]
to
f\circ(-)
is bijective.

To conclude that
\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(G,-)\colon\langle G\rangle_{1}\to\langle\Lambda^{G}\rangle_{1}
is an equivalence, it remains to show that it is essentially surjective. This is essentially the same arguement as in the proof of
[
Kra15
, 2.3]
. An object
Y\in\langle\Lambda^{G}\rangle_{1}
is a summand of
(\Lambda^{G})^{s}[t]
for some intergers
s,t
. Hence, there is an idempotent morphism
\bar{e}\colon(\Lambda^{G})^{s}\to(\Lambda^{G})^{s}
and morphisms
\bar{p}\colon G^{s}\to Y[-t]
,
\bar{i}\colon Y[-t]\to G^{s}
such that
\bar{i}\circ\bar{p}=\bar{e}
and
\bar{p}\circ\bar{i}=\mathrm{id}_{Y[-t]}
.

Since the functor
\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(G,-)
is fully faithful and additive, there is an idempotent
e\colon G^{s}\to G^{s}\in\mathcal{T}
such that
\bar{e}=e\circ(-)
. Because
\mathcal{T}
is idempotent complete (since it is Krull-Schmidt),
e
splits so that there are morphisms
p\colon G^{s}\to X
and
i\colon X\to G^{s}
with
i\circ p=e
and
p\circ i=\mathrm{id}_{X}
.

It is straightforward to check that the morphisms

\displaystyle(p\circ(-))\circ\bar{i}\colon Y[-t]\to\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(G,X),

\displaystyle(i\circ(-))\circ\bar{p}\colon\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(G,X)\to Y[-t]

are inverses. Hence, since
\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(G,-)
commutes with suspension,
\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(G,X[t])\cong Y
, as required.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
G\in\mathcal{T}
be a minimal linear generator of a triangulated category satisfying
2.1
. Then, there is an additive equivalence
\mathscr{F}\colon\mathcal{T}\to\perf(\Lambda^{G})
which commutes with the respective suspension functors and preserves triangles with at least two indecomposable terms.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of
Corollary

2.31
and
Proposition

3.4
.
∎

Recall that an
algebraic triangulated category
is a triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
such that there exists a Frobenius exact category
\mathcal{E}
and a triangulated equivalence
\underline{\mathcal{E}}\cong\mathcal{T}
. Here,
\underline{\mathcal{E}}
denotes the stable category of
\mathcal{E}
.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
G\in\mathcal{T}
be a linear generator of a triangulated category satisfying
2.1
. Suppose that
\mathcal{T}
is algebraic. If
G
is indecomposable, then there is a triangulated equivalence
\mathcal{T}\xrightarrow{\sim}\perf(\Lambda^{G})
.

Proof.

If
G
is indecomposable, then
\Lambda^{G}=k[x^{\pm}]
or
\Lambda^{G}=k[x]
, depending on whether or not

. If

, then
\Lambda^{G}=k[x^{\pm}]
and the morphisms in
\mathcal{T}
are isomorphisms. Hence, the only triangles in
\mathcal{T}
are triangles isomorphic to direct sums of shifts of the triangle

. Thus, it is straightforward to see that the equivalence
\mathscr{F}
of

3.5
preserves all triangles.

On the other hand, if

, then
\Lambda^{G}=k[x]
, with

in degree
-1
. It follows from
[

, Lemma 3.1]
that
\Lambda^{G}
is intrinsically formal, which, by definition, implies that
\Lambda^{G}
is quasi-isomorphic to
\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{DG}}(G)
. Hence, since
\mathcal{T}
is algebraic,
[
Kel94
, §4.3]
implies that there is a triangulated equivalence
\mathcal{T}\xrightarrow{\sim}\perf(\Lambda^{G})
.
∎

3.2.

With applications to Discrete Cluster Categories of type

, that

\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(P_{i},P_{j})=\begin{cases}k[x]&\text{if $i=j$ and $i$ odd,}\\

From now on, we shall denote

The algebra

Theorem 3.7
.

Let

be a minimal linear generator of
\mathcal{T}
, with
G\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{2n-1}P_{i}
, such that
P_{i}\cong P_{i}[1]
if and only if

.
Then there is an isomorphism of graded
k
-algebras,

\Lambda_{n}\xrightarrow{\sim}kQ/I.

To prove this theorem, we begin with some lemmas concerning submodules of

.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
a,b
be different vertices in
Q_{0}
, and let
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then
(\iota_{a}(kQ/I)\iota_{b})^{m}\cong(\iota_{a}(kQ/I)\iota_{b})^{0}
as
k
-vector spaces.
Moreover, the dimension of
(\iota_{a}(kQ/I)\iota_{b})^{m}
as a
k
-vector space is at most 1. In fact, if there is a nonzero path
\sigma\in(\iota_{a}(kQ/I)\iota_{b})^{m}
, then it is equivalent to

\sigma\equiv\begin{cases}\delta\cdots\delta\delta\alpha^{-m}&if $m\leq 0$,\\
\delta\cdots\delta\delta\beta^{m}&if $m\geq 0$ and $b\not\in B$,\\
\delta\cdots\delta\beta^{m}\delta&if $m\geq 0$ and $b\in B$.\end{cases}

Proof.

In order to avoid cluttered notation, given arrows
\delta_{a,b}
,
\alpha_{a}
and
\beta_{a}
, we may supress the indices
a
and
b
when there is no ambiguity.

Suppose
\sigma=\rho\delta\rho\delta\cdots\rho\delta\rho
is a path of degree
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, starting at
a
and ending at
b
where each
\rho
is some path consisting combinations of cycles
\alpha_{c}
,
\beta_{c}
, and
\iota_{c}
for a
c\in Q_{0}
.

By the relations in
I
, we know that
\alpha_{a}\delta_{ab}=\delta_{ab}\alpha_{b}
and
\alpha_{a}\beta_{a}=\beta_{a}\alpha_{a}
, and so we may "commute"
\alpha
with
\beta
and
\delta
. Hence

\sigma\equiv\sigma^{\prime}=\beta^{i_{1}}\delta\beta^{i_{2}}\delta\cdots\beta^{i_{p-1}}\delta\beta^{i_{p}}\alpha^{i},

where
m=\sum_{l=1}^{p}(i_{l})-i
.

Similarly, according to the relations in
I
, for a vertex
c\notin B
,
\beta_{c}\delta_{c,c+1}\delta_{c+1,c+2}=\delta_{c,c+1}\delta_{c+1,c+2}\beta_{c+2}
, and so we may "commute"
\beta
with paths starting and ending at vertices not in
B
. Hence, we have either

\sigma\equiv\sigma_{1}^{\prime\prime}=\delta\cdots\delta\delta\alpha^{i}\beta^{j},

or

\sigma\equiv\sigma_{2}^{\prime\prime}=\delta\cdots\delta\beta^{j}\delta\alpha^{i},

where, in both
\sigma_{1}^{\prime\prime}
and
\sigma_{2}^{\prime\prime}
, we must have

, as
\sigma
is in degree
m
and all paths
\delta
are in degree
0
.

Thus, one of the following is true;

\sigma\equiv\begin{cases}\delta\cdots\delta\delta\alpha^{-m}&if $m\leq 0$,\\
\delta\cdots\delta\delta\beta^{m}&if $m\geq 0$ and $b\not\in B$,\\
\delta\cdots\delta\beta^{m}\delta&if $m\geq 0$ and $b\in B$.\end{cases}

That is, any non-zero path from
a
to

, determined by

and
b
.
Indeed, such a path exists if and only if a path of degree

between

and
b
exists, as any path starting and ending at different vertices must pass through a vertex not in
B
.
Hence there is a
k
-vector space isomorphism
(\iota_{a}(kQ/I)\iota_{b})^{m}\cong(\iota_{a}(kQ/I)\iota_{b})^{0}
, and it follows that the dimension as a
k
-vector space is at most
1
as

is a tree.
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

There is an isomorphism of graded
k
-algebras,

\iota_{a}(kQ/I)\iota_{a}\cong e_{a}\Lambda_{n}e_{a}\cong\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(P_{a}),

for any

.

Proof.

By
Lemma

3.8
, there exists at most one non-zero equivalence class of paths of degree

starting and ending at vertex

: If
i<0
, then all nonzero paths are equivalent to
\alpha^{i}
, and so
(\iota_{a}(kQ/I)\iota_{a})^{i}=k\cdot\alpha^{i}
. On the other hand, if
i=0
, then all nonzero paths can be represented by
\iota_{a}
and so
(\iota_{a}(kQ/I)\iota_{a})^{i}=k\cdot\iota_{a}
. If
i>0
and
a\in B
, then there is no loop
\beta_{a}
at
a
and so there are no nonzero paths of degree
i
. However, if
i>0
and
a\notin B
, then all nonzero paths are equivalent to
\beta^{i}
, so that
(\iota_{a}(kQ/I)\iota_{a})^{i}=k\cdot\beta^{i}
..

Suppose first that
a\in B
, then
a
is odd and

by definition of the linear generator
G
. Hence, there is an isomorphism
\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(P_{a})\cong k[x]
, with
\lvert x\rvert=-1
by
Proposition

3.2
. Moreover, since

, then, by the above, there are no nonzero paths starting and ending at

of degree

. We may thus construct a graded

-linear map

sending

for

and sending
\iota_{a}\mapsto 1
. This is clearly a graded algebra isomorphism.

Next, consider the case
a\not\in B
. Then,
a
is even,
P_{a}\cong P_{a}[1]
and
\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(P_{a})\cong k[x^{\pm}]
, with
\lvert x\rvert=-1
by

. Thus, similarly to before, we may construct the
k
-linear map
\varphi_{a,a}:\iota_{a}(kQ/I)\iota_{a}\rightarrow k[x^{\pm 1}]
, taking
\alpha_{a}^{-i}\mapsto x^{-i}
for

,
\iota_{a}\mapsto 1
, and
\beta_{a}^{i}\mapsto x^{-i}
for

. Again, it is clear that this is an isomorphism of graded

-algebras given the relations
\alpha_{a}\beta_{a}-\iota_{a}
and
\beta_{a}\alpha_{a}-\iota_{a}
.
∎

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
a,b
be distinct vertices in
Q_{0}
.
Then there is an isomorphism of graded

-

-bimodules

\iota_{a}(kQ/I)\iota_{b}\cong e_{a}\Lambda_{n}e_{b}\cong\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(P_{a},P_{b}).

Proof.

If

, then there are no paths from

to

, and so

. Similarly, by definition of the linear generator

,

implies that

. Whence, we may assume

.

There are two cases to consider: the case where

and

or vice-versa. Let us first assume that
a\in B
and
b\notin B
. By
Proposition

3.2
and the of

, we know that there are algebra isomorphisms
\mathrm{End}^{\ast}(P_{a})\cong k[x]
,
\mathrm{End}^{\ast}(P_{b})\cong k[x^{\pm}]
. Moreover,
\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}(P_{a},P_{b})\cong k[x^{\pm}]
as a

-

-bimodule. Hence, it suffices to show that
\iota_{a}(kQ/I)\iota_{b}\cong k[x^{\pm}]

There is only one degree zero path from

to

, so that
(\iota_{a}(kQ/I)\iota_{b})^{0}=k\cdot\sigma

, any path

of degree

is equivalent to
\sigma\alpha^{-m}
, and any path

of degree

. Hence,
(\iota_{a}(kQ/I)\iota_{b})^{m}=k\cdot\sigma\alpha^{m}
and
(\iota_{a}(kQ/I)\iota_{b})^{j}=k\cdot\sigma\beta^{j}

\varphi_{a,b}\colon\iota_{a}(kQ/I)\iota_{b}\to k[x^{\pm}]

,

, and
\sigma\beta^{j}\mapsto x^{-j}

The case where

We are now in a position to prove Theorem

.

Proof of
Theorem

3.7
.

We combine

3.9
and
3.10
, and

3.2
to construct a
k
-linear isomorphism

sending a path
\sigma
between vertices

to
\varphi_{a,b}(\sigma)
. Here, we mildly abuse notation and denote the composition of

with the natural inclusion

as
\varphi_{a,b}\colon\iota_{a}kQ/I\iota_{b}\to\Lambda_{n}
.

All that is left to show is that

is an isomorphism of graded

-algebras. Let
\sigma\in kQ/I
be a path

of degree

and let
\tau\in kQ/I
be a path

. We would like to show that

.

A case by case analysis of

\displaystyle h(\sigma)=\varphi_{a,b}(\sigma)=i_{a,b}(x^{i})

\displaystyle h(\tau)=\varphi_{b,c}(\tau)=i_{b,c}(x^{j}).

Thus,

Moreover, since paths

In any of the cases above,

4.
Discrete Cluster Categories

Discrete cluster categories of Dynkin type
A_{\infty}
, labelled
\mathcal{C}_{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, were introduced by Igusa–Todorov
[
IT15
]
, as an example cluster categories arising from cyclic posets.
These categories may be thought of as an infinite rank version of the cluster categories of Dynkin type
A_{n}
, first studied independently in both
[
Bua+06
,
CCS05
]
.
Also, discrete cluster categories of Dynkin type
A_{\infty}
can be seen as generalisation of a category studied by Holm–Jørgensen
[
HJ10
]
, namely the perfect derived category of
k[x]
viewed as a differential graded algebra, with
x
in homogeneous degree
-1
and equipped with a trivial differential.
In the case
n=1
, there is a triangulated equivalence between
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\perf{k[x]}
.

The categories
\mathcal{C}_{n}
are Hom-finite,
k
-linear, Krull-Schmidt, 2-Calabi-Yau, triangulated categories.
Becuase these categories are well-behaved examples of cluster categories of infinite rank, they have been studied by many authors since their introduction
[
Fis14
,
GHJ19
,
GZ23
,
LP17
,
Mur25a
]
.
The category
\mathcal{C}_{n}
admits a combinatorial model, with a discrete set of infinitely many marked points on the boundary of a closed orientated disc, and
n
so-called accumulation points.
The indecomposable objects of
\mathcal{C}_{n}
are in bijection to isotopy classes of non-contractible curves between marked points, such that the curves are non-isotopic to the boundary.

Paquette–Yıldırım introduced a completion of
\mathcal{C}_{n}
in
[
PY21
]
by considering a Verdier localisation of
\mathcal{C}_{2n}
by a thick subcategory, labelled
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{n}
.
The category
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{n}
also admits the same combinatorial model as
\mathcal{C}_{n}
, however we now also consider isotopy classes of curves with endpoints in the set of accumulation points and marked points.

The categories
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{n}
have since been studied in their own right
[
Aug+23
,
Fra25a
,
Mur25
,
Mur25a
]
, and are our primary example of triangulated categories admitting a linear generator.

4.1.
Paquette-Yıldırım Completions of Discrete Cluster Categories of Dynkin Type
A_{\infty}

By adapting a construction found in
[
GHJ19
]
, we construct the category
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{n}
via a combinatorial model, namely the closed disk
D^{2}
with marked points on the boundary.
We will consider
D^{2}
to have an anti-clockwise orientation on the boundary
\partial D^{2}=S^{1}
.

Definition 4.1

(
[
GHJ19
]
)
.

A subset
\mathscr{M}
of

is called

if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

\mathscr{M}
has infinitely many elements,

(2)

\mathscr{M}\subset S^{1}
is a discrete subset,

(3)

A novel approach to design compact, wideband, and high-gain circularly polarized MIMO antenna using dual-polarized elements and hybrid couplers
Authors: H Tran, Phuong Kim-Thi, Nguyen Tran, Thao Hoang-Thi-Phuong, Yem Vu-Van
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper presents a novel design approach for a compact, circularly polarized (CP) multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) antenna that achieves wide bandwidth, high gain, and multi-port operation. Unlike conventional MIMO configurations, where each port feeds a single-polarized radiating element and gain enhancement relies on frequency-selective surfaces or T-dividers, the proposed design integrates dual-polarized CP radiating elements with hybrid couplers. Through this configuration, a four-port MIMO array is realized using only two radiating elements, significantly reducing the overall antenna size. The hybrid coupler enables simultaneous excitation of both elements, resulting in enhanced gain and improved radiation characteristics. A prototype is fabricated and experimentally verified. The measured results show that the antenna, with compact dimensions of 1.41 1.06 0.07, exhibits an operating bandwidth of 9.5% (5.0–5.5 GHz), port isolation better than 10 dB, and gain ranging from 7.8 to 8.8 dBi. Owing to its compact, wideband, and high-gain CP performance, the proposed antenna is well suited for modern wireless communication systems, including WLAN, Wi-Fi 6, C-band radar, and satellite IoT applications.Subject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering

Introduction

Multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) antennas have been widely used in modern communication systems due to their capability to enhance channel capacity without requiring additional spectrum1. Compared to linearly polarized (LP) antennas, circularly polarized (CP) antennas are more effective in mitigating multi-path effects. This work focuses on the design of compact CP MIMO antennas featuring multiple ports and high-gain performance.

Various MIMO CP antennas utilizing microstrip patch structure to achieve low-profile configuration have been reported in2–8. In such designs, one MIMO port is connected to one radiating element. Besides, additional decoupling networks are also necessary to suppress the mutual coupling between the MIMO elements. Consequently, this type of MIMO antenna generally possesses extremely large overall dimensions as a number of MIMO ports is similar to a number of radiators. In addition, low gain performance is also another drawback of such designs.

For gain enhancement, the conventional approach is a combination of single-polarized radiator and a frequency selective surface (FSS) layer9–14. Accordingly, high gain radiation can be achieved. However, this approach significantly enhances the antenna profile due to the FSS layers are positioned at a distance of at least quarter-wavelength from the radiators. Another method combines single-polarized radiator and T-junction power divider15–21. Here, one MIMO port is connected to at least two radiators by a T-divider. When the array elements are fed with equal magnitude and phase, high gain radiation can be achieved. Nonetheless, this method requires many radiators, which significantly enhance the antenna dimensions. Besides, the element spacing must keep around 0.5 to 0.7, which also increases the antenna size dramatically.

To conclude, designing MIMO antennas with multiple ports and high gain with compact size is very challenging. The conventional methods commonly used single-polarized radiators as MIMO elements and FSS layers/T-dividers for gain enhancement. This paper proposed a novel approach to overcome the limitations of current designs. Instead of using a single-polarized radiator, the proposed approach employs two dual-polarized radiators and two hybrid couplers. Accordingly, a 4-port MIMO antenna system with high gain and compact size can be realized. The measured data demonstrates that the antenna with compact overall dimensions of 1.41 1.06 0.07 can achieve good performance over an operating bandwidth from 5.0 to 5.5 GHz, equivalent to about 9.5%. Across this band, the gain varies in the range from 7.8 to 8.8 dBi.

Proposed solution

This paper focuses on the solution to design multi-port MIMO antennas with high gain radiation and compact size features. To improve antenna gain, the possible solution is to increase the radiating elements. For this purpose, a power divider is necessary as it can be used to excite multiple radiating elements simultaneously. Generally, the conventional designs15–21 utilize T-junction power divider and single-polarized radiator, designated as Solution-1 in Fig. 1. This configuration has the drawback of employing many radiating elements, resulting in large antenna dimensions. The other solution is to combine the T-divider with a dual-polarized radiator, as Solution-2. It can be seen obviously that this combination has the advantage of reducing the number of radiating elements. For example, to design a 2-port MIMO antenna, Solution-1 requires 4 radiating elements, while Solution-2 only needs 2 radiating elements. Now, let’s consider when the hybrid coupler is used as a replacement for the T-junction divider, as a proposed solution in Fig. 1. Obviously, a 4-port MIMO antenna can be realized with only 2 radiators, which has a larger number of ports than Solution-2 despite using similar radiating elements. This contributes to significantly reducing the overall antenna size when designing multiple ports high-gain MIMO antenna arrays.

There are two critical tasks that need to be considered in this paper. Firstly, a dual-CP antenna with high inter-port isolation and wideband operation as well has to be designed. Secondly, using the proposed dual-CP antennas to design MIMO array configuration with the aid of hybrid couplers.

Wideband dual-CP antenna

Principles for isolation enhancement

The most challenging task is to design dual-CP antenna with high isolation. Theoretically, the coupling of a right-hand CP (RHCP) and left-hand CP (LHCP) antenna can be shown to be minimum when their phase center is collocated (due to the orthogonality of RHCP and LHCP).

Consider a typical asymmetrical dual-CP antenna structure, which uses a dual-fed patch with truncated corners. When both RHCP and LHCP are excited in a patch that has an asymmetrical geometry, the isolation becomes very poor. The main reason is the strong mutual coupling between modes that share the same polarization (horizontal-to-horizontal and vertical-to-vertical), much like what occurs when two linearly polarized antennas are placed close together. This issue is consistently seen in many other dual-CP antenna designs with asymmetric configurations, where achieving high isolation is notably difficult22–24.

On the other hand, a symmetrical configuration inherently supports two orthogonal modes, which can be excited to generate RHCP and LHCP. When the phase centers coincide and the CP modes are excited correctly−meaning equal amplitude with a 90 phase difference−high isolation is achieved. As a result, the isolation in antennas with symmetric primary radiators depends heavily on polarization purity. Any deviation from ideal RHCP/LHCP purity (i.e., a non-ideal axial ratio) leads to leakage between the two senses. This behavior has been reported in numerous studies25–28, where different slot geometries are used to excite symmetrical patches or metasurfaces.

Four-port MIMO array

Antenna geometry

Figure 6 depicts the geometry of the proposed 4-port MIMO antennas. Here, two dual-CP radiators are employed as the MIMO elements. To excite these elements, two hybrid couples (Coupler-1 and Coupler-2) are utilized, and they are positioned underneath the ground plane to minimize their effects on the radiation characteristic of the antenna. These couplers are printed on a low-cost FR-4 substrate. Coupler-1 has two input ports namely Port-1 and Port-2 and its output ports are connected to P-1 and P-2 of the radiators. Meanwhile, Coupler-2 has Port-3 and Port-4 as input ports, and it excited the antenna at P-3 and P-4. Accordingly, LHCP waves can be radiated with either Port-1 or Port-2 excitation. On the other hand, RHCP radiation can be produced when either Port-3 or Port-4 of Coupler-2 is excited. The radiating element and the coupler are separated by a continuous ground plane, which acts as an effective isolation layer. As a result, the feeding network does not disturb the radiation characteristics or isolation performance of the antenna. Diversity performance

One of the most important parameters to evaluate the efficiency of the MIMO system is the envelop correlation coefficient (ECC). The ECC is a key metric used to evaluate how independently multiple antennas in a MIMO system operate. Low ECC means the antennas provide diverse, uncorrelated signals, which improves system performance. Figure 12 shows the ECC calculation based on S-parameter results for Port-1 and Port-4 operation. It can be seen obviously that over the band from 5.0 to 5.6 GHz, the ECC values are all lower than 0.05. This demonstrates that signals are mostly uncorrelated, and then good isolation and diversity performance.

Calculated ECC of the proposed antenna.

Figure 13 shows the calculated Total Active Reflection Coefficient (TARC) and MIMO efficiency of the proposed antenna. The TARC quantifies the overall reflected power of a multi-port antenna under simultaneous excitation, accounting for both mismatch and mutual coupling effects. Meanwhile, MIMO efficiency measures the real transmission effectiveness of a MIMO antenna under simultaneous multi-port excitation. According to the data shown in Fig. 13, it is obvious that in the operating frequency band from 5.0 to 5.5 GHz, the TARC is below − 10 dB, which meets the excellent criteria for MIMO performance. Besides, the efficiency is in the range from 70 to 75%, demonstrating good overall multi-port radiation capability.

Conclusion

A MIMO CP antenna with multi-port, compact size, and high gain radiation characteristics has been presented and investigated in this paper. These novel features can be achieved by combining the dual-polarized CP radiating elements and the hybrid couplers. As a result, 4-port MIMO antenna can be realized with only two radiators. This is different from the conventional approach, which employs multiple radiators and thus significantly increases the antenna size. Meanwhile, the use of hybrid coupler can excite both elements simultaneously, leading to high gain radiation. Measurement on the fabricated antenna prototype demonstrates that the antenna can achieve operating bandwidth of 9.5% with isolation of better than 10 dB. Additionally, the gain within this band is always higher than 8.4 dBi and maximum value of 9.2 dBi can be obtained at 5.3 GHz. With its compact, wideband, and high-gain CP performance, the design is highly suitable for WLAN (5.15–5.35 GHz), Wi-Fi 6/6E (5.17–5.85 GHz), C-band radar (5.0–5.4 GHz), and satellite IoT communication systems (5.25–5.35 GHz).

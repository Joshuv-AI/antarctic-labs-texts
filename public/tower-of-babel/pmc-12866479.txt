Construction and characterization of the ORF131 gene deletion strain of lumpy skin disease virus
Authors: Jiaqi Li, Weitao Huang, Qunhua Ke, Miaomiao Li, Xiangwei Wang, Qi Wang, Xiangping Yin, Yuefeng Sun
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractLumpy Skin Disease (LSD), caused by the Lumpy Skin Disease Virus (LSDV), is a legally reportable disease recognized by the World Organization for Animal Health (WOAH) and has resulted in significant economic losses for the global cattle industry. Although several commercial LSDV vaccines are currently available, safer and more effective gene-deleted versions remain lacking. Therefore, screening key functional genes and developing gene-deleted live-attenuated vaccine strains hold substantial research value. In this study, we focused on ORF131, a gene whose function remains unclear. We successfully constructed an rLSDV-ΔORF131-EGFP gene deletion strain utilizing a homologous recombination, followed by purification using limiting dilution and single-cell subcloning techniques. Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR) and Sanger sequencing validation confirmed that the deletion strain was successfully purified and free from wild-type virus contamination. Biological characterization indicated that the strain was genetically stable, with the optimal viral harvesting time in Madin-Darby Bovine Kidney (MDBK) cells being 72 h. Furthermore, RNA sequencing analysis of virus-infected cells revealed that rLSDV-ΔORF131-EGFP enhanced the immune and inflammatory responses of host cells compared to wild-type LSDV. This study not only provides a potential candidate strain for the development of an LSDV attenuated vaccine but also offers a theoretical foundation for the prevention and control strategies of LSD.Keywords: LSDV, ORF131, Homologous recombination, RNA-Seq

Introduction

Lumpy Skin Disease Virus (LSDV) belongs to the member of Poxviridae family, Chordopoxvirinae subfamily, and Capripoxvirus genus, The members of the same genus include Sheep Pox virus (SPPV) and Goat Poxvirus (GTPV), these three viruses exhibiting high genetic homology [1–4]. LSDV is elliptical or brick-shaped by electron microscopy, a length of 294 ± 20 nm and a width of 262 ± 22 nm, which has a lipid vesicle membrane, a double-stranded DNA virus and a genome size of approximately 151 kb, consists of a central coding region flanked by 2.4 kb of inverted terminal repeat sequences, and is predicted to contain 156 open reading frames [5, 6]. The LSDV genome has a large structure with complex immune evasion mechanisms, and a large number of genes that may be involved in immune evasion have not yet been deeply explored.

LSDV is mainly transmitted by arthropods or bloodsucking animals [7, 8], and causes Lumpy Skin Disease (LSD) in cattle, which leading to clinical signs such as enlarged lymph nodes as part of the host’s immune response, ocular and nasal discharge, and typical dermal nodules and other clinical signs leading to decreased milk yield to meat ratio, abortion and even death of affected animals [9, 10]. Currently there are LSD outbreaks in Africa [11], the Middle East [12, 13], Europe [14], and Asia [15], posing a serious threat to the global cattle industry. For LSD prevention and control, currently the main reliance is on live attenuated vaccines [16, 17], but live attenuated vaccines inherently have certain risks [18], including clinical side effects [19], virulence regression [20], or homologous recombination with other viruses of the same genus [21]. In recent years, genetic engineering technology has become a key tool in the field of poxvirus vaccine development, in which attenuated strains can be effectively obtained by targeting and knocking out virulence-related factors or immunoregulatory genes in the viral genome. This strategy has significantly improved the safety of poxvirus vaccines and is one of the main ways to construct genetically engineered attenuated vaccines [22, 23]. Gene deletion attenuated vaccines have the advantages of reduced vaccine virulence, improved immunogenicity, and the potential to discriminate between naturally infected and vaccinated animals [24–26].

Screening for LSDV virulence-related genes and immune escape genes is a crucial step in the development of gene-deficient attenuated vaccines. However, the complex nature of the LSDV genome, combined with the uncharacterized functions of many of its genes, has significantly hindered the advancement of attenuated vaccines. The ORF131 gene, whose function remains largely uncharacterized, requires urgent investigation to assess its potential immunomodulatory activity. In this study, we constructed an ORF131 gene deletion strain using homologous recombination techniques and systematically analyzed its biological properties. More importantly, we conducted a transcriptomic analysis to compare the differentially transcribed genes between the ORF131 deletion strain and wild-type LSDV-infected host cells, focusing on how ORF131 regulates the host immune response and inflammatory response. This study not only provides new insights into the biological functions of LSDV ORF131 but also lays a theoretical foundation for the design of LSDV live attenuated vaccines.

Materials and methods

Cells, viruses and plasmids

The MDBK, CSF, Vero, BHK-21 cell lines were preserved by our Laboratory in Lanzhou Veterinary Research Institute,Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences; pUC19T vector and LSDV/FJ/CHA/2021 virus strain (GenBank Sequence Number: OP752701.1) were isolated and preserved by our laboratory. All cell lines and viral stocks used in this study tested negative for mycoplasma contamination. All the experimental operations involving LSDV in this study were completed in the biosafety level 3 (BSL-3) laboratory of Lanzhou Veterinary Research Institute, which strictly followed the nationally promulgated biosafety practices and standard experimental procedures.

Reagents and enzymes

DMEM medium (No. E600003-0500) and E. coli DH5α competent cells (No. B528413-0100) were purchased from Shanghai Sangong Bioengineering Company Limited. EcoRI-HF® (No. R3101S) and BamHI-HF® (No. R3136S) were purchased from New England Biolabs Inc. Biolabs, USA. QuantiNova™ SYBR® Green PCR (No. 208052) was purchased from QIAGEN, Germany. Viral Genome DNA/RNA Extraction Kit (No. DP315) was purchased from TIANGEN, Beijing, China. Gel Recovery Kit (No. D2500) was purchased from OMEGA, Guangzhou, China.

Primers design and RT-qPCR quantification

Total RNA in the cells was extracted using the Trizol reagent. Subsequently, the RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA, and the primers design was done through the Primer-BLAST tool on the NCBI website, and the specific sequence information is shown in Table 1. As to RT-qPCR analysis, the QuantiNova SYBR Green PCR kit was selected for amplification reaction, and GAPDH was used as an internal control. GraphPad Prism software was used to process the experimental data statistically and generate the resultant graph.

Construction of gene deletion strains

Using wild-type LSDV genomic DNA as a template, specific primers were designed according to the ORF131 gene sequence (GenBank Sequence Number: OP752701.1) (Table 2). The right and left homology arms of ORF131 gene (ORF131-L and ORF131-R) were amplified using primer pairs Primer1/Primer2 and Primer3/Primer4, respectively. The EGFP reporter gene was also amplified by Primer5/Primer6 using pUC-19 T plasmid as a template. A two-step fusion PCR method was used, in which ORF131-L was first ligated to EGFP and subsequently EGFP was ligated to ORF131-R. The PCR products of each stage were separated by 1% agarose gel electrophoresis and purified. The complete ORF131-L-EGFP-ORF131-R recombinant fragment was finally obtained by secondary fusion. The fusion product was ligated with EcoRI/BamHI double-cleaved pUC19T vector and transformed into DH5α competent cells, and the plasmid was extracted from the positive clone after incubation at 37℃ for 16 h. The plasmid was successfully constructed and named pUC19T-LSDV-ΔORF131-EGFP by restriction enzyme digestion and sequencing analysis, the specific strategy for plasmid construction was as described previously [27, 28]. Subsequently, the plasmid was transfected into Vero cells and inoculated with wild-type LSDV virus 16–18 h after transfection. The cytopathic effect and EGFP expression were observed by fluorescence microscopy at 12, 24, 48 and 72 h post-infection, respectively, and finally selected to be collected around 72 h of infection, named rLSDV-ΔORF131-EGFP.

Primers used for PCR

Purification and characterization of gene deletion strains

The virus solution was inoculated with MDBK cells after tenfold dilution using the limited dilution method and purified in multiple rounds by monoclonal screening technique, specific methods were as described previously[27]. The viral genomic DNA was extracted after picking the monoclonal virus for amplification. the extracted DNA was used as a template for PCR amplification using specific primers (Table 2), and wild-type LSDV (LSDV-WT) and ddH2O were set up as the positive and negative controls, respectively. the PCR products were analyzed by 1% agarose gel electrophoresis, and the bands that were in line with the expected sizes were sent to Sangon Bioengineering Co. Ltd (Shanghai, China) for sequencing verification to ensure the correctness of the deleted viral sequence.

Determination of growth curves of gene deletion strains

To compare the proliferation of the ORF131 deletion virus with the wild-type strain and to determine the optimal time for virus collection. The rLSDV-ΔORF131-EGFP and LSDV wild type were inoculated into MDBK cells at the same MOI, respectively. Cell culture supernatants were collected at different time points, and the viral titer (TCID50/mL) at each time point was determined by the Reed-Muench method. The data were statistically analyzed by GraphPad Prism software and viral growth curves were plotted.

Suitable cell line screening for the proliferation of gene deletion strains

To determine the cell lines suitable for the growth of the rLSDV-ΔORF131-EGFP strain, CSF, Vero, MDBK and BHK-21 cells were inoculated with the rLSDV-ΔORF131-EGFP strain of the same MOI of 0.1, and fluorescent photographs were taken at the time point of the highest viral TCID50 post-infection, and fluorescence intensity was analyzed by Image J.

RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) sample preparation

MDBK cells grown to 90% confluence were infected with either wild-type LSDV or the purified ORF131-deletion strain at an MOI of 0.1. At 24 h post infection, Total RNA was extracted from collected cells using Trizol reagent, and three biological replicates per group were submitted to GENEWIZ (Suzhou) for RNA-seq.

Statistical analysis

All experimental data were statistically analyzed and visualized using GraphPad Prism 9 software, with results presented as mean ± standard deviation (Mean ± SD). Each experimental group included three replicates. Appropriate statistical methods were applied according to the analytical requirements of different datasets: Fig. 3B was analyzed using Student’s t-test; Fig. 3C-b and Fig. 5 employed one-way ANOVA; Fig. 3D utilized two-way ANOVA for statistical comparisons. The threshold for statistical significance was set at P < 0.05, with the following asterisk-based notation system used to denote varying significance levels: *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001, and ****P < 0.0001.

Results

Construction of gene deletion strains

As shown in Fig. 1A, the left and right arms of the ORF131 gene (815 bp of ORF131-L and 675 bp of ORF131-R) and the EGFP reporter gene (1151 bp) were amplified respectively (Fig. 1B-a). Through a two-step fusion PCR reaction, ORF131-L was first ligated to EGFP to produce a 1907 bp fragment, while EGFP was ligated to ORF131-R to obtain a 1768 bp fragment (Fig. 1B-b); these two fragments were then further fused to ultimately obtain a 2567 bp three-segment fusion product (ORF131-L-EGFP-ORF131-R) (Fig. 1B-c). All amplified fragments were of the expected size, confirming the success in obtaining the correct target gene fragment. The above fusion fragments were cloned into pUC19T vector to construct a transfer vector, which was verified by restriction endonucleases digestion (Fig. 1B-d), and the results showed that both the vector and insert fragments were correct size as expected. Following sequence verification, the correct construct was designated pUC19T-LSDV-ΔORF131-EGFP. The plasmid was transfected into Vero cells, and inoculated with wild-type LSDV virus 16–18 h after transfection. Through the homologous recombination mechanism, the EGFP expression cassette carried by the plasmid successfully replaced the ORF131 gene locus in the viral genome. During 72 h of infection, a continuous enhancement of specific green fluorescence could be observed by fluorescence microscopy (Fig. 1C), and the stable EGFP expression profile indicated that the gene deletion virus rLSDV-ΔORF131-EGFP was successfully constructed.

Purification and characterization of gene deletion strains

In order to obtain the purified deletion strain, the virus solution was firstly gradient diluted by the finite dilution method, followed by multi-generation purification by applying the monoclonal screening technique. When the specific green fluorescence was observed under fluorescence microscope and the cell lesions under white light were almost completely overlapped (Fig. 2A), the virus was verified by using three pairs of specific primers, the ORF131 gene locus was detected negatively (Fig. 2B-a); the EGFP reporter gene was detected positively (Fig. 2B-b); and the sizes of amplified bands of the target region were significantly different from that of the wild-type strain and the sizes were all as expected (Fig. 2B-c). The ORF131 locus was completely replaced by the EGFP sequence as verified by Sanger sequencing (Fig. 2C). In conclusion, the purified target gene deletion strain has been successfully obtained. All identification experiments were set up with wild-type LSDV as positive control and ddH2O as negative control to ensure the reliability of the results.

Biological characterization of the purified ORF131 gene deletion strain

To assess the genetic stability of the ORF131 gene deleted strain, rLSDV-ΔORF131-EGFP was detected by PCR (Primer 1/Primer 4) for consecutive passage for 20 generations, and the results showed that only a specific band of 2567 bp could be amplified (Fig. 3A), and no wild-type LSDV gene fragment appeared, which confirmed that the deletion strain was genetically stable and free of wild-type virus contamination within 20 generations.

To determine the growth curves of the gene deletion strain and LSDV-WT, the viral growth curves were measured using the TCID50 method and found that both the wild-type LSDV and the ORF131 deletion virus reached the peak of replication at 72 h post-infection (Fig. 3B). Based on this, 72 h was determined to be the optimal harvest time point for rLSDV-ΔORF131-EGFP.

To screen for sensitive cell lines suitable for the proliferation of rLSDV-ΔORF131-EGFP, CSF, Vero, MDBK and BHK-21 cells were inoculated with rLSDV-ΔORF131-EGFP strains of the same MOI, and the cytopathic effect with specific green fluorescent foci were observed at the optimal harvesting time point of the determined strains, after 72 h post-infection, and the photographic Fluorograms showed that rLSDV-ΔORF131-EGFP grew best in MDBK cells (Fig. 3C-a). The fluorescence intensity was then analyzed using Image J to further screen the sensitive cell lines suitable for the growth of the deletion strain, and the results showed that the MDBK cells had the highest fluorescence intensity and were significantly different from the other three cells (Fig. 3C-b), so the MDBK cells were the sensitive cell line suitable for the growth of the rLSDV-ΔORF131-EGFP.

To quantify the transcriptional level of IFN-β, during viral infection, MDBK cells were first inoculated with the same MOI of rLSDV-ΔORF131-EGFP or LSDV-WT. Cell samples were collected at different time points after infection (0, 12, 24, 48 h) and subjected to RT-qPCR assay. The results showed that compared to LSDV-WT, rLSDV-ΔORF131-EGFP significantly increased the transcriptional level of IFN-β mRNA in MDBK cells at 48 h post-infection (Fig. 3D).

Analysis of genes expression differences by RNA-Seq between rLSDV-ΔORF131-EGFP and LSDV-WT infection

To assess the modulation of host innate immune response mediated by different viruses, MDBK were infected with either rLSDV-ΔORF131-EGFP or wild-type LSDV strains of the same MOI, respectively. After 24 h post-infection, cells were harvested using Trizol reagent, three biological replicates were set for each group, and RNA-Seq were performed by Suzhou GENEWIZ Biotechnology Co., Ltd.

To ensure the reliability of the transcriptome data, inter-sample reproducibility was first evaluated. The Pearson correlation coefficient squared (R2) was used as the evaluation index, and the R2 value is usually required to be no less than 0.8. The analysis results showed that the R2 values among samples reached more than 0.96 (Fig. 4A), which indicated that the good reproducibility of the experiments, and the obtained data had a high degree of reliability. The results laid a reliable foundation for the subsequent differential gene analysis. Secondly, based on the normalized processed gene transcription data [log10(FPKM + 1)], as shown in Fig. 4B, these two groups showed significantly different clustering patterns. And the differential transcribed gene analysis showed that a total of 1122 differentially transcribed genes were generated after rLSDV-ΔORF131-EGFP infection of MDBK cells compared with wild-type LSDV infection. Upon statistical analysis, 496 of these genes were significantly up-regulated and 626 genes were significantly down-regulated, and the volcano plot visualized the differential transcriptional features (Fig. 4C).

After GO functional enrichment analysis of differentially transcribed genes, two visualizations were used to display GO enrichment-Pvalue histogram and GO enrichment-bar graph. The first GO enrichment-Pvalue histogram (Fig. 4D-a), whose vertical axis labels the significantly enriched GO entries, and the horizontal axis corresponds to the p-value of each entry after -log10 transformation. The second is the GO enrichment-histogram (Fig. 4D-b), with the vertical axis also showing GO entries and the horizontal axis reflecting the number of differential genes in each entry. Color coding was used to distinguish three major GO categories: biological process, molecular function, and cellular component. The results showed that in the cellular component category, differential genes were significantly enriched in the nucleus and cytoplasm related function entries.

KEGG pathway enrichment analysis was performed using two visualization methods to present the results: significantly enriched KEGG annotated classification bar graph and differential gene KEGG enrichment scatter plot. Significantly enriched KEGG annotated categorical bar graphs showed the number of differential genes in each pathway (Fig. 4E-a), whereas differential KEGG enrichment scatter plots reflected the importance of the pathways through the Rich factor and Q value (Fig. 4E-b). The analysis revealed that several signaling pathways closely related to immune regulation were significantly activated: 1) IL-17 signaling pathway with significant alterations in the transcription of genes such as chemokines (CXCL3/5/8) and apoptosis-associated molecules (CASP3/8); 2) C-type lectin receptor pathway involving several regulatory factors (PLK3, MDM2) and cytokines (IL6, IL12A), etc.; 3) MAPK signaling pathway contains transcriptional regulators (MYC) and stress proteins (HSPA1A/6), etc.; 4) TNF signaling pathway contains TNFAIP3 and MMP3/9, etc.; and 5) significant transcriptional changes of key receptors (IL6R, IFNAR2) and ligands (IL6), etc., in the cytokine network. Taken together, the results suggest that ORF131 gene deletion may alter the host response by affecting multiple immune-related pathways.

Further analysis revealed that the deletion of the ORF131 gene significantly upregulates the transcriptional levels of key inflammation-related genes (TRIF, NFKB1, IL1B, IL6). These genes have been confirmed as core molecules that initiate and amplify the body’s inflammatory response [29–32]), suggesting that ORF131 deletion may enhance the inflammatory response. Furthermore, as previously mentioned, ORF131 deletion promotes IFN-β transcription (Fig. 3D), and transcriptomic data further indicate that transcription of ISG15, an interferon-stimulated gene (ISG) downstream of the interferon pathway, is also activated. ISG15 possesses well-established antiviral activity, effectively inhibiting viral replication [33], which synergistically corroborates the role of ORF131 deletion in activating the interferon pathway. Concurrently, transcription levels of CASP3, a key executor of apoptosis, were significantly elevated [34], suggesting ORF131 may possess apoptosis-inhibitory functions. In summary, compared to wild-type LSDV, rLSDV-ΔORF131-EGFP induces a stronger innate immune response and exhibits favorable characteristics as a candidate attenuated vaccine strain.

Validation of differential genes by RT-qPCR

Based on transcriptome sequencing data, representative differential genes were selected for validation by using RT-qPCR analysis. As shown in Fig. 5A, coampared with LSDV-WT, the transcripts of pro-inflammatory factors (IL1B, IL6), interferon-stimulated genes (ISG15), natural immune signaling molecules (TRIF, NFKB1), and apoptosis-associated genes (CASP3) were significantly up-regulated in cells infected with rLSDV-ΔORF131-EGFP strain, whereas the transcripts of genes such as MAPK4, and TGB3 were significantly down-regulated (Fig. 5B). The validation results were highly consistent with the RNA-Seq data, confirming the reliability of the sequencing results.

Discussion

Since the first report in 1929, LSD has spread to most countries and regions in Africa, Europe and Asia, causing significant economic losses to the cattle industry [35]. Currently, LSD prevention and control mainly rely on vaccination, but traditional attenuated vaccines still pose safety concerns [18]. Constructing gene-deficient attenuated vaccines by targeting single or multiple virulent genes or immune evasion genes has become an important development strategy for poxvirus fourth-generation genetically engineered vaccines [36]. For example, engineered vaccines with single gene deletion of VACV A41L [37], double gene deletion of VACV B13R/B22R [38], and double gene deletion of ASFV MGF360/MGF505 [39] have demonstrated good homologous strain protection efficacy.

To elucidate the function of ORF131 and develop novel vaccine candidates, we successfully constructed an ORF131 deletion strain, rLSDV-ΔORF131-EGFP, which maintains good genetic stability even after 20 generations of passage, which is one of the most important indicators for evaluating viruses as vaccine candidates [40, 41]. In addition, MDBK cells was verified to be one of the sensitive cell lines for rLSDV-ΔORF131-EGFP propagation. It has been shown that poxviruses are usually strictly cytophilic [2], and that genetic deletions or mutations in poxviruses (especially in genes that determine the range of the host) may alter the cytophilicity of poxviruses [2, 42, 43], and the cell culture technique of the virus is in turn a critical step in the production of vaccines, which in turn is a central part of the production of vaccines [44, 45], making the evaluation of these properties crucial for subsequent applications.

RNA-Seq analysis is a powerful technique to demonstrate the gene expression profile, which is necessary for the next gene function study. Crucially, RT-qPCR validation of RNA-Seq results would ensure the accuracy. In this study, through RNA-Seq analysis, followed by RT-qPCR verification, we clearly showed that rLSDV-ΔORF131-EGFP infection mainly activated three key immune pathways compared with the wild-type LSDV strain: 1) IL-17 signaling pathway: which plays a central role in antiviral immunity by inducing pro-inflammatory factors [46, 47], whose activation inhibits the replication of Chikungunya virus [48] and African swine fever virus [49], and vice versa inhibition promotes the proliferation of Gammaherpesvirus [50]; 2) the C-type lectin receptor pathway: which can influence immune response by regulating cytokine production and antigen presentation [51, 52], and its activation antagonizes Middle East Respiratory Syndrome Coronavirus [53]; 3) MAPK signaling pathway: involved in the regulation of inflammatory response [54, 55], whose activation inhibits PEDV infection by inducing apoptosis [56]. Together, these data indicate that deleting ORF131 heightens the host immune response, underscoring the potential of rLSDV-ΔORF131-EGFP as a live-attenuated LSDV vaccine.

The k k -Total Bondage Number of a Graph Journal: Discrete Mathematics
Jean-Pierre Appel, Gabrielle Fischberg, Kyle Kelley, Nathan Shank, Eliel Sosis

Abstract
Let G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) be a connected, finite undirected graph. A set S ⊆ V S\subseteq V is said to be a total dominating set of G G if every vertex in V V is adjacent to some vertex in S S . The total domination number, γ t ​ ( G ) \gamma_{t}(G) , is the minimum cardinality of a total dominating set in G G . We define the k k -total bondage of G G to be the minimum number of edges to remove from G G so that the resulting graph has a total domination number at least k k more than γ t ​ ( G ) \gamma_{t}(G) . We establish general properties of k k -total bondage and find exact values for certain graph classes including paths, cycles, wheels, complete and complete bipartite graphs.

In this paper, we generalize previous concepts in bondage theory and apply them to some common graph classes.

Preliminary Properties

While in this paper we start with connected graphs, however, graphs often become disconnected when removing edges.
The following obvious lemma relates the total domination number of a graph’s components to the entire graph.

Lemma 1
.

For a disconnected graph
G
with no isolated vertices and
j
components,
H_{1},H_{2},\dots,H_{j}
, we have

\gamma_{t}(G)=\sum_{i=1}^{j}\gamma_{t}(H_{i}).

The removal of a single edge can only increase the total domination number by at most 2. This is shown in the following theorem. Note that we never remove pendant edges because this would create an isolated vertex.

Theorem 1
.

Given a graph
G
with no isolated vertices and any non-pendant edge
e
,

\gamma_{t}(G\setminus\{e\})\leq\gamma_{t}(G)+2.

Proof.

Let
G
be a graph with no isolated vertices and assume there exists a non-pendant edge
ab
.
Let
S
be a
\gamma_{t}\text{-set}
of
G
. Let
c
be adjacent to
a
and
d
be adjacent to
b
, where
c
and
d
are not necessarily unique and
c\neq b
and
d\neq a
. Note
c
and
d
will necessarily exist since
ab
is not a pendant edge. If
a,b\not\in S
, then
S
is a
\gamma_{t}\text{-set}
of
G\setminus\{ab\}
. If
a\in S
and
b\not\in S
, then
S\cup\{b,d\}
is a TD-set of
G\setminus\{ab\}
. If
a,b\in S
, then
S\cup\{c,d\}
is a TD-set of
G
. ∎

Using Lemma

Corollary 1

Let

be disjoint paths with

\gamma_{t}(P_{a}+P_{b})\leq\gamma_{t}(P_{a+b-2}+P_{2}).

Proof.

Let

and
P_{b}
be disjoint paths such that

and

. We can rewrite the target inequality as
\gamma_{t}(P_{a})+\gamma_{t}(P_{b})\leq\gamma_{t}(P_{a+b-2})+2
using Lemma

. We will split this into cases based on

and
b\pmod{4}
.

Case 1:

Let
a,b\equiv 0\pmod{4}
.
Then
\gamma_{t}(P_{a})+\gamma_{t}(P_{b})=\frac{a}{2}+\frac{b}{2}=\frac{a+b}{2}
,
meanwhile
\gamma_{t}(P_{a+b-2})+2=\frac{a+b-2}{2}+3=\frac{a+b+4}{2}
.
Thus
\gamma_{t}(P_{a}+P_{b})<\gamma_{t}(P_{a+b-2}+P_{2})
.

Case 2:

Let
a,b\equiv 2\pmod{4}
.
Then
\gamma_{t}(P_{a})+\gamma_{t}(P_{b})=\frac{a}{2}+\frac{b}{2}+2=\frac{a+b+4}{2}
, meanwhile
\gamma_{t}(P_{a+b-2})+2=\frac{a+b-2}{2}+3=\frac{a+b+4}{2}
.
Thus
\gamma_{t}(P_{a}+P_{b})=\gamma_{t}(P_{a+b-2}+P_{2})
.

The remaining cases follow similarly using Proposition
1
.
∎

This follows from Corollary

Thus for each

Theorem 6
.

For a wheel
W_{n}
where
n\geq 3k
,

b_{t}^{k-1}(W_{n})=k.

Proof.

First we show by construction that
b_{t}^{k-1}(W_{n})\leq k
. Label the vertices of

from
\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
such that
\deg(v_{0})=n
. Remove the set of

\{v_{0}v_{i}\mid i\equiv 0\pmod{3},1\leq i\leq 3k\}
. The total domination number would then be

, hence an increase of

Next we show that when we remove less than

edges, we can always find a TD-set of cardinality at most

.
First let

be in the TD-set.
For each vertex
v_{i}
where we have removed
v_{0}v_{i}
, we can choose a neighbor to add to the TD-set since
v_{i}
can not be isolated.
When possible, choose a neighbor of
v_{i}
that is adjacent to
v_{0}
so that
v_{0}
is adjacent to another vertex in the TD-set.
If along a sequence of vertices it is never possible to choose a sequence of neighbors to find one adjacent to
v_{0}
, that means we must have disconnected a component of the graph. Disconnecting this component would require removing two edges from
W_{n}\setminus\{v_{0}\}
, so we can choose an arbitrary vertex that is still connected to
v_{0}
to add to our TD-set. Using this strategy we have created a TD-set of cardinality at most

after removing less than

edges.

∎

Theorem 10

First, we consider when the graph stays connected after removing edges.

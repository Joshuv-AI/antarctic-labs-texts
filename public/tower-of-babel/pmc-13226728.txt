The effects of seaweed extract and amino acid fertilizers on growth and productivity of two grape (Vitis vinifera L.) cultivars
Authors: Yusra Mohammed Saleh, Badran S. Agha, Ayad T. Shayal Alalam, Ayad Hani Alalaf, Asmaa Mohammed Adil, Ali Farouq Al-Ma’athedi, Mohamed M. Mohamed, Waleed Fouad Abobatta, Waleed Mohammed El-hanafy Fekry, Ahmed Mohammed, Heidar Meftahizade
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The effects of two concentrations of seaweed extract (15, 30 ml L−1) and amino acid fertilizer (10, 20 ml L−1) were tested on some growth, productivity and sustainability traits of Thompson Seedless and Olivette Noire grape vines with five years old in a RCBD experiment with three replicates in Which treatments were evaluated independently to assess their individual effects on grapevine performance. No significant difference was observed between cultivars for studied traits. The fertilizer levels (seaweed and amino acid), especially 20 ml L−1 amino acid positively influenced the yield, chlorophyll content, and vegetative and reproductive attributes except number of leaves, 100 berry volume and weight, as well as TSS content. Application of 20 ml L−1 amino acid significantly were increased number of leaves vine, leaf area, vine leaf area, dry weight of leaves, number of clusters per vine, cluster length, cluster width, cluster weight, and number of berries in the cluster, as well as volume of 100 berries, total yield per vine, total chlorophyll in leaves, and TSS value in the Olivetti Noire cultivar as well as Thompson Seedless compared to control. PC analysis confirmed the results of mean comparisons. The results of correlation estimation and regression, revealed a tightly coordinated structure among vegetative and yield-related traits under the applied treatments. According to the results, the use of amino acid 20 ml L−1 is recommended as a superior treatment and a proper stimulant to improve the grape growth and fruit yield of these two grape cultivars. Overall, the results highlight amino acid application as the most effective factor under the conditions of this study.

Introduction

Grapes (Vitis vinifera L.), belong to the Vitaceae family, is a well-known fruit crops, widespread cultivated in many areas of the world for thousands of years with high nutritional and economic value1–3. Grapes are climbers and, in a few cases, grow in the form of shrubs and are characterized by their small fruits with high nutritional value, as they contain a variety of vitamins and minerals, especially vitamin C, as well as antioxidants4,5.Grapes consumes worldwide as a most popular fruits for their health benefits and has a market demand2,34. More than 8000 grape varieties cultivated in different parts of the world, vary greatly in terms of color, size, taste, ripening period, and uses, whether for fresh eating or for making juices, jams, wine, or raisins4,6,7.

Modern agriculture is witnessing an increasing trend towards adopting sustainable agricultural development concepts aimed at achieving high-quality production without harming the environment or depleting natural resources8–11. Among these alternatives are seaweed extracts and amino acids, due to their positive effects on fruit crop growth and increased productivity12,13. Soluble organic fertilizers containing some amino acids are among the most important fertilizers currently used in sustainable agriculture14–16. Amino acids are organic nitrogenous compounds play a pivotal role in plant physiology, extending far beyond their traditional function as protein building blocks17. This biostimulants influence the physiological processes of plant growth and development, as they affect enzymatic activity, in the plant through their role in enzyme formation and thus participate in enzymatic reactions in cells18–23. Amino acids are accountable for cell division and producing some natural growth hormones such as IAA and GA3 and consequently increasing the yield and improving the quality of fruits24. Amino acid Fertilizers, derived from organic waste materials or formulated as commercial products, have exposed significant potential to improve plant nutrient uptake, vegetative growth, yield, and stress tolerance and generally indorsing photosynthesis, phytohormone activity, and nutrient availability17,19,25. Therefore, foliar spraying with amino acids has contributed to improving the growth and yield of grapes, as reported by authors26–30.

Seaweed extract an environmental-friendly biostimulants, applying in sustainable agriculture31–34. Various reports showed appreciated effects of seaweed bioactive materials in horticultural35–38. These materials boost the efficacy of fertilizers and intensify the confrontation of plants to stressfully conditions38–41. Seaweed delivers substitute for chemical fertilizers and eradicate certain hostile effects accompanying with these fertilizers, like acidification, leaching of nutrients, and soil dilapidation and compaction42–45. Seaweed-derived compounds rehabilitees soil microbial communities and encouraging their beneficial interactions, resulting recovers soil structure and fertility31. In addition, seaweeds fertilizers comprise plant hormones such auxins, cytokinins, and gibberellins, nutritional elements such Ca, P, K, Mn, Zn, Cu, Mg B, and etc.31,46–48. Furthermore, they are containing various enzymes, vitamins, proteins, particularly secondary metabolite involved in stress resistance through regulating molecular, physiological, and biochemical mechanisms49,50.

This sustainable bioproducts used to improve the growth and production of fruit crops, due to their effective role in stimulating growth, increasing yields, and improving their quality31,34,51,52. They contain many nutritional elements, in addition growth-promoting substances such as cytokinins, auxins, gibberellins, vitamins, amino and organic acids, and polysaccharides53,54. Also, seaweed extract enhances root growth, stem thickness, and photosynthesis, resulting improves growth and yield43,55–58. Many studies have exposed the reputation of seaweed spraying in improving the quantitative and qualitative characteristics of grapes59–62.

Some authors stated that, integrating foliar spraying of amino acids and seaweed extracts into vineyard management is a sustainable technique to enhancing the quality of grapes and wines28,63,64. Despite extensive research on bio stimulants, limited information is available regarding the comparative effectiveness of amino acids and seaweed extracts on different grape cultivars under field conditions. Therefore, in current study, For the first times, we studied the effectiveness of fertilization with amino acids and seaweed extracts on growth, productivity and sustainability cultivation of Thompson Seedless and Olivette Noire grape varieties.

Materials and methods

Aim to study of influences different concentration of seaweed (SW) and amino acid (AA) PGRs on the growth and some quantitative and qualitative characteristics of Vitis vinifera L. grapes cultivars. The experiment was conducted using a randomized complete block design (RCBD) in which seaweed extract and amino acid treatments were evaluated as independent factors rather than as a fully crossed factorial combination in the vineyard of the College of Agriculture and Forestry at the University of Mosul during the 2025 season (36.3407° N, 43.1269° E; elevation 223 m above sea level). The foliar spraying of treatments was done on the Thompson Seedless and Olivette Noire varieties with five years old, which were planted in straight lines, with a distance of 2 m between vines and 3 m between lines. The vines were set on wires using the T-shaped method. Winter pruning was carried out in mid-February, leaving six canes, eight eyes, and four renewal spurs. For soil analysis, the soil samples were taken from the orchard at a depth of 0–40 cm. The physical and chemical properties of the soil were presented in Table 1. Based on results of soil analysis, soil fertilization of orchard was done with NPK 20:20:20 at a rate of 100 g per vine once, using a drip irrigation system, before foliar spraying. The Standard methods were followed for collecting and analyzing samples in the Environmental Laboratory of the Dept of Biology, College of Education, University of Mosul, Iraq65. The climatic data of experimental site were acquired from the Iraq Meteorological Organization and Seismology (http://www.meteoseism.gov.iq/), and presented in Fig. 1.

A total of 60 grapevines were used in the experiment, consisting of two cultivars (Thompson Seedless and Olivette Noire). The experiment included five treatments (control, SW15, SW30, AA10, and AA20), arranged in a randomized complete block design with three replicates. Each treatment within each cultivar consisted of three replicates, with two vines per replicate (n = 2 vines per replicate). Therefore, each cultivar included 30 vines (5 treatments × 3 replicates × 2 vines). The treatments were comprising seaweed extract at 0, 15, 30 mL L−1 (C, SW15, and SW30, respectively) (Heavy Alga) containing Ascophyllum nodosum from HEFE FERTILIZER, Rota – Cádiz, Spain, and amino acid fertilizer at 0, 10, 20 ml L−1 (C, AA10, and AA20, respectively) (Heavy Amino 12) HEFE FERTILIZER, Rota – Cádiz, Spain (www.hefefertilizer.com). The specifications of SW extract ad amino fertilizer were presented in Table 2. The same vines were sprayed with tow concentrations of SW (15 and 30 mL/L) and tow concentrations of AA (10 and 20 mL/L), separately. The spraying solution was prepared as follows: 150 mL of SW extract was dissolved in 10 L of water to obtain a 15 mL/L concentration, and 300 mL was dissolved in 10 L of water to obtain a 30 mL/L concentration. For the AA fertilizer, concentrations applied to the grapevines were 0, 10, and 20 mL/L. The solutions were prepared by dissolving 100 mL of AA extract in 10 L of water to obtain 10 mL/L, and 200 mL in 10 L of water to obtain 20 mL/L.

The fertilizer treatments were used three times during the growing season, the first when the new shoots reached a length of 15 cm, while the second spray was two weeks after the first spray, and the third spray was two weeks after the second spray. The vines were sprayed early in the morning, with three sprays per season for combined SW and AA simultaneously, using a 16-L backpack sprayer until complete leaf wetting. The schedule was: first spray when shoot length reached 15 cm, second spray at two weeks after the first, and third spray at two weeks after the second.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using a combination of univariate and multivariate statistical methods. The Analysis of Variance and Tukey’s HSD test (p ≤ 0.05, r = 3) were perfumed using IBM Statistical SPSS Software Ver. 27. The Principal Component Analysis, Pearson’s Correlations Estimation, Cluster Analysis, and Linear Regression Analysis were done using R software (Ver. 4.5).

Results

No significant differences were observed between the grape cultivars for any of the measured traits. However, significant differences among treatments were detected for average leaf area, total leaf area per vine, average leaf dry weight, average number of clusters per vine, cluster length, width and weight, berry number, berry weight, fruit yield, and leaf chlorophyll content. The genotype × treatment interaction was also significant for these variables. All treatments involving SW extract and AA enhanced leaf area and leaf dry weight in both cultivars.

The highest number of leaves (1548.3 leaves) was recorded in Olivetti Noire cultivar under the 20 ml/L AA treatment; however, this value was not significantly different those obtained from application of other treatments. The lowest number of leaves was observed in the control vines of Thompson Seedless (Fig. 2a). Average leaf area differed significantly among treatments (P ≤ 0.05). The largest leaf area (112.86 cm2) was obtained from Olivetti Noire treated with 20 ml/L AA, whereas the smallest value (94.63 cm2) was recorded in the control plants of the same genotype (Fig. 2b). Similarly, the highest total leaf area per vine (17.25 m2) was found in Olivetti Noire treated with 20 ml/L AA, while the lowest (8.53 m2) occurred in the control vines of Thompson Seedless (Fig. 2c). Leaf dry weight in both cultivars responded similarly to the 20 ml/L AA treatment, which produced the highest values. The maximum average leaf dry weight (1.89 g per leaf) was recorded in both cultivars under this treatment (Fig. 2d). The leaf dry weight of control plants of Olivetti Noire (1.17 g) was slightly lower than that of the other cultivar.

Cluster analysis

The cluster analysis revealed that treatments combining SW or AA applications at higher doses (20–30 mL L−1), particularly in both Olivetti Noire and Thompson Seedless, tend to cluster together and are characterized by above-average values for key traits such as total yield per vine, number of leaves per vine, dry leaf weight, cluster length, vine leaf area, chlorophyll content, and grain size. This indicates a strong positive response of grapevines to higher biostimulant. In contrast, the control treatments for both varieties form a distinct cluster marked by consistently lower values across most traits, suggesting comparatively weaker growth and yield performance. Lower-dose applications of SW (15 mL L−1) or AA (10 mL L−1) generally occupy an intermediate position, showing moderate improvements over controls but not reaching the levels observed with application of higher doses. Overall, the clustering pattern highlighted that treatment intensity rather than variety alone plays a dominant role in shaping grapevine performance, with high-dose SW and AA treatments producing the most favorable agronomic outcomes (Fig. 8).

Regression analysis

The model of stepwise regression (Fig. 9) revealed the significant positive associations for total fruit yield as independent variable with leaf number (TY/V ~ NL/V) (p = 0.0064**), average leaf area (LA; p = 0.019*), total vine leaf area (TVLA) (p = 0.003**), leaf dry weight (DWL) (p = 0.023*), number of clusters per vine (NC/V) (p = 0.00003**), and number of berries per vine (p = 0.01*). The fitted equation of linear regression between total fruit yield per vine (TY–V) and the number of leaves per vine (NL–V) (Fig. 9a) was Y = 7.0 x – 2900.2, which explains 63% of the variance in yield (R2 = 0.6). Regression between total yield (TY–V) and average leaf area (LA) (Fig. 9b) represents moderately strong relationship with the equation Y = 199.7x – 16363.8 and a coefficient of determination R2 = 0.5. The linear regression between total yield (TY–V) and total vine leaf area (TVLA) (Fig. 9c) with a linear equation as Y = 535.3x – 1529.1 and R2 = 0.68 (p < 0.01), representing a strong association. The equation of linear regression between total yield (TY–V) and leaf dry weight (DWL) (Fig. 9d) was Y = 5572.9x – 4259.0, while R2 was 0.6. Also, the regression between TY–V and the number of clusters per vine (NC–V) (Fig. 9e), with equation Y = 457.8x – 1888.4 demonstrated strong relations between dependent variable and predictor.

Discussion

Although grapevine is a perennial crop and long-term experiments are generally preferred to account for inter-annual variability, the present study was conducted as a single-season field experiment to evaluate short-term physiological and agronomic responses to SW extract and AA biostimulants. Previous studies have shown that such biostimulants can induce rapid changes in photosynthetic efficiency, nutrient uptake, vegetative growth, and yield components within one growing season, particularly when applied foliarly. Therefore, the observed improvements in growth and productivity parameters in this study likely reflect immediate metabolic and physiological responses rather than cumulative long-term effects. Nevertheless, it should be acknowledged that seasonal climatic conditions and potential carry-over effects between years may influence treatment performance in perennial crops. As a result, the findings of the present study should be interpreted as short-term responses under the specific environmental conditions of the experimental year. Multi-year field trials are required to confirm the consistency, stability, and long-term agronomic relevance of the observed effects. It should be noted that, although both seaweed extract and amino acid treatments were applied within the same experimental framework, the design does not allow for a strict evaluation of the synergistic interactions. Therefore, the observed responses are interpreted as individual treatment effects rather than true synergistic effects.

The grape cultivars were not exhibited significant differences for measured traits. While, all applied SW and AA levels influenced different vegetative and reproductive characteristics of grape cultivars. Application of 20 ml/L AA as the most effectiveness treatment caused increases by 69.34, 19.26, 101.64, 61.92, 129.13, 33.31, 27.51, 141.25, 20.28, 15.31, 15.50, 289.49, 16.43, and 6.25%, respectively in number of leaves vine, leaf area, vine leaf area, dry weight of leaves, number of clusters per vine, cluster length, cluster width, cluster weight, and number of berries in the cluster, as well as volume of 100 berries, weight of 100 berries, total yield per vine, total chlorophyll in leaves, and TSS value in Olivetti Noire cultivar. Similarly, in Thompson Seedless cultivar, this treatment caused notably improvements by 33.38, 12.59, 47.62, 24.95, 82.53, 25.77, 21.09, 101.93, 11.03, 7.14, 254.90, 17.05, and 6.12% respectively, in the values of number of leaves vine, leaf area, vine leaf area, dry weight of leaves, number of clusters per vine, cluster length, cluster width, cluster weight, and number of berries in the cluster, furthermore volume of 100 berries, total yield per vine, total chlorophyll in leaves, and TSS. While it significantly reduced weight of 100 berries by 54.90%.

The superiority of the treatment of 20 ml L−1 of AA to improving most growth and yield characteristics may be due to the role of AAs in increasing the activity of various physiological activities in the plant, directly or indirectly, through their importance in the formation of growth hormones and enzymes and their entry into the formation of the root system, cell division, the construction of cell membranes and carbohydrate materials, which is positively reflected in the efficiency of photosynthesis, thus improving the characteristics of vegetative growth24,67–69. Amino acids involve various advantages such as increased yield and yield components, increased nutrient assimilation and stress tolerance, and improved yield components and quality characteristics crops19. The results of a lot of studies on different grape cultivars are in line with present results and confirmed it. For example: In a study, two cultivars of grapevines (Arra 15 and Arra 20) which exposed to foliar spray of SW in 0.50 g/L + amino acid 0.25 g/L significantly producing the highest yield63.In Flame seedless grapevines, treated with amino acids, the values of shoot length, leaf area, chlorophyll content, total amino acids, and N, P, K and Mg content in the leaves, as well as yield per vine, cluster weight, berry weight, soluble solids content, total sugars, total anthocyanin and total phenols has increased, while total acidity in berries was reduced compared to control26. Application of 250 ppm from glutamic acid, glycine, and aspartic acid, solely or in combination, significantly improved yield per vine, bunch numbers and volume, shot numbers, and berry quality of Superior grapevines comparing with untreated vines27. When three grape cultivars including “Bez-El-Anza”, “Thompson Seedless” and “Red Roomy were sprayed with SW extract and AA, observed that all treatments had a significant impact in the respect of TSS %, acidity% and TSS/acid ratio, and Tyrosin at 500 ppm recorded the highest yield (9.47 kg/vine). Application of commercial SW and an amino acids-based biostimulant has altered the primary metabolites such as carbohydrates, organic acids (AcOrg), and free amino acids in the leaves and berries of Vitis vinifera L. cv. Tempranillo13. Results of a comparative transcriptomic analysis in strawberry, treated with soluble AA fertilizer, revealed significant expression changes in the genes involved in metabolisms of starch and sucrose, ascorbate and aldarate, carbon and nitrogen, glyoxylate and dicarboxylate, as well as upregulation in GLDH, SPS and β-GE genes and downregulation of APX, ICL and MS genes, additionally in the differential expression of PK, IDH, GDH and SPX which is consistent with the shift from C flux to N metabolism and the improvement in phosphorus accumulation16. These observations demonstrated that AA and SW improved plant metabolism through enhancing water and nutrient availability, resulting enhances photosynthesis, and biosynthesis of organic acids, proteins and carbohydrates. Results of an experiment showed that the growth characteristics, especially dry matter were significantly improved under foliar application of 50- and 100-mM L-phenylalanine in Dragonhead plants and 100/150 mM L-phenylalanine along with vermicompost produced the highest dry matter yield (481/531 g m−2)15.

Application of water-soluble AA fertilizer promoted the growth of tomatoes and improve the quality of fruits more effectively than chemical fertilizer18. In Cucumis plants, the application of 500 mg/plant glycine increased leaf area, total yield, and the economic life compared to control and gained the same or better records than NPK fertilization70. Application of three AA-based biostimulants, Pepton 85/16, Pepton Origin, and Nutriterra, on lettuce showed that all biostimulants improved shoot biomass, leaf area, and physiological performance, including photosynthetic rate and key N assimilation parameters71. The application of AA fertilizers and algal extracts positively affected the growth, quality, antioxidant activity, and flavor of Allium ramosum23.

Seaweed has proven significant improvements on growth, yield, and quality in a variety of fruit crops56,72. Many studies have shown that SW applications could prevent post-harvest berry drops in grapes and allow improvements in cluster and berry quality characteristics64. Seaweed extract at 3000 ppm has improved significantly fruit length, diameter, weight, and total yield of kiwifruit, fruit soluble solids contents and total sugars73. Seaweed extracts has enhanced the chlorophyll content, fruit size, and yield of Golden Delicious’ apple74. The scientific observations and mentioned reports confirmed our findings.

Conclusion

In conclusion, foliar application of SW extract and AA fertilizers positively influenced vegetative growth, physiological traits, and yield components of Thompson Seedless and Olivette Noire grape cultivars during the experimental season. Among the evaluated treatments, AA application at 20 ml L−1 exhibited the most pronounced short-term effects under the conditions of this study. However, as the experiment was conducted during a single growing season, the results should be considered preliminary. Further multi-year investigations are necessary to validate the long-term effectiveness, seasonal stability, and practical applicability of these biostimulants in perennial grapevine production systems. The improvements in berry number and vine yield suggest that biostimulant applications are a promising strategy to enhance grape production and productivity in both cultivars.

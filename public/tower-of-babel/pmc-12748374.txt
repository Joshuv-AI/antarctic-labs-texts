CCR2/CXCR6 enhances tumor infiltration and antitumor efficacy of MSLN CAR-T cells
Authors: Yan Zhang, Lianfeng Zhao, Jiaqi Liu, Xinhao Yang, Tianyu Chen, Zhengliang Chen, Wenying Li, Zhigang Hu, Feiyan Pan, Jiannan Chen, Lingfeng He, Zhigang Guo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAbstractChimeric antigen receptor T (CAR-T) cell therapy has shown promise in treating solid tumors, but the clinical success is often limited by insufficient tumor infiltration. In this study, we sought to engineered two MSLN-targeted CAR-T cell variants, Intra2 and Intra6, expressing CCR2 and CXCR6, respectively, to improve their migration toward the tumor microenvironment. Flow cytometry confirmed stable receptor expression. In vitro assays demonstrated that both Intra2 and Intra6 CAR-T cells exhibited significantly improved functional phenotype, migration and invasion, as well as persistence in killing target cells compared to conventional MSLN CAR-T cells. Notably, in vivo, Intra6 CAR-T cells displayed superior antitumor effects, showing enhanced tumor suppression and reduced exhaustion. RNA sequencing analysis revealed that CXCR6 expression upregulated genes related to immune activation, migration, adhesion, and cytoskeletal remodeling, such as LFA-1, PAK1, and FSCN1, suggesting improved migration and transendothelial infiltration. RT-qPCR and flow cytometry confirmed higher LFA-1 expression and enhanced migratory capacity in Intra6 CAR-T cells. Importantly, LFA-1 was crucial for CXCR6-driven migration. These results suggest that chemokine receptor modification of MSLN-targeted CAR-T cells can significantly improve their tumor infiltration and therapeutic efficacy, offering a potential strategy to optimize CAR-T cell therapy for mesothelin-expressing cancers. MethodsWe engineered two CAR-T cell variants, Intra2 and Intra6, by introducing chemokine receptors CCR2 and CXCR6, respectively. The migration and tumor infiltration capabilities of these modified cells were evaluated in vitro through migration assays and in vivo using tumor-bearing mouse models. The phenotypic characteristics of the CAR-T cells, including memory T cell subsets (stem cell memory and central memory), were analyzed by flow cytometry. Tumor growth inhibition was assessed, and markers of immune exhaustion and evasion were quantified.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00018-025-05972-7.Keywords: MSLN CAR-T cells, Tumor immunotherapy, Tumor microenvironment, Chemokine receptors, Tumor infiltration

Introduction

Immunotherapy, particularly chimeric antigen receptor T-cell (CAR-T) therapy, has shown significant success in treating hematological malignancies [1, 2]. However, its effectiveness in solid tumors remains limited due to challenges such as poor tumor infiltration, immune evasion, and the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (TME) [3, 4]. While MSLN-targeted CAR-T therapy has demonstrated promise in ovarian and breast cancers [5, 6], its clinical application is hindered by inefficient CAR-T cell migration and persistence within the tumor [7, 8]. Chemokines, often overexpressed in tumors, are key regulators of immune cell chemotaxis and tissue infiltration [9, 10]. For example, CXCL16, found in melanoma and colorectal cancer [11], binds to CXCR6 to recruit T cells, NK cells, and monocytes to tumors, whereas CCL2, overexpressed in glioma, breast, and ovarian cancers [12–14], attracts regulatory T cells (Tregs) and myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs), fostering an immunosuppressive environment [15]. However, chemokine-receptor mismatches in CAR-T cells limit their efficient infiltration into tumors [16].

Given these challenges, this study explores the co-expression of MSLN-specific CAR with chemokine receptors CCR2 and CXCR6 to enhance CAR-T cell migration and tumor infiltration. CCR2 promotes homing to CCL2-rich regions [17], while CXCR6 targets areas with high CXCL16 levels [18]. Notably, CXCR6 was selected for its potential to facilitate deeper penetration into tumor cores, an area where conventional CAR-T cells often fail to reach effectively. By improving CAR-T cell migration, tumor infiltration, and anti-tumor efficacy, this strategy aims to overcome current barriers in CAR-T therapy for solid tumors. This approach could be particularly impactful in ovarian cancer, where MSLN is highly expressed, offering a novel solution to enhance the therapeutic potential of CAR-T immunotherapy in solid tumors.

Materials and methods

Cells and culture conditions

HEK293 (human embryonic kidney), Ovcar3 (ovarian cancer), HCC1806 (breast squamous carcinoma), and Capan-2 (pancreatic cancer) cell lines were obtained from ATCC and cultured in DMEM, RPMI-1640–5 A with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS). PBMCs from healthy donors (TPCS#PB025C, Miles-bio, Shanghai) were isolated using Lymphoprep (Stemcell, Canada), and T cells were purified by negative selection with the EasySep™ human T Cell Isolation Kit (Stemcell, Canada). On day 0, T cells were activated with anti-human CD3 and CD28 beads (Miltenyi, Germany) and cultured in X-VIVO medium (Ronsa, Switzerland) supplemented with recombinant IL-2 (300 IU/mL). CAR-T cells were infected with lentivirus, and the cell phenotype and positive rate were assessed after 48 h.

Construction of target cell lines expressing luciferase and CXCL16

A plasmid co-expressing luciferase and CXCL16 was constructed, packaged using lentiviral vectors, and subsequently transfected into the 1806 cell line. Following transfection, puromycin was continuously added to select for cells expressing luciferase. After 14 days of selection, luminescence intensity was measured using a multimode plate reader to quantitatively assess luciferase expression levels. The Human CXCL16 Quantikine ELISA Kit (Cat. DCX160) was obtained from R&D Systems. Following 48 h of culture, the cell culture supernatants were collected and analyzed by ELISA to determine the concentration of CXCL16 in the supernatant.

Lentivirus production

Lentivirus for CAR-T cells was produced by co-transfecting HEK293T cells (ATCC) with lentiviral and packaging plasmids (pMDLg/pRRE, pRSV-Rev, pMD2.G). Supernatants were collected 48 and 72 h post-transfection, concentrated by ultracentrifugation, and stored at −80 °C.

Flow cytometry

Flow cytometry was performed on a Beckman Coulter machine, and data were analyzed using software CytExpert. Antibodies involved in this study included Percp-conjugated anti-CD3 antibody, FITC-conjugated anti-CD69 antibody, PE-conjugated anti-CD25 antibody, PE-conjugated anti-TIM antibody, APC-conjugated anti-PD-1 antibody, FITC-conjugated anti-LAG-3 antibody, APC-conjugated anti-CD45RO antibody, FITC-conjugated anti-CD62L antibody, APC-conjugated anti-CD192(CCR2) antibody, APC-conjugated anti-CD186(CXCR6)antibody, FITC-conjugated anti-human CD4 antibody, PerCP-conjugated anti-human CD8 antibody and PE-conjugated anti-MSLN antibody and PE-conjugated anti-MSLN antibody, all antibodies are derived from Biolegend Company. The cells were stained at room temperature for 25 min, washed twice with PBS and then tested on the machine. The detailed information of the antibodies were listed in Table S1.

CAR-T cytotoxicity assay

Short-term Killing: CAR-T cells were co-cultured with luciferase-expressing Ovcar-3, HCC1806, or Capan-2 cells at E: T ratios of 1:1, 1:2 for 8 h. After incubation, luminescence was measured, and cytotoxicity was calculated as [(background luminescence - sample luminescence)/background luminescence] × 100%.

Long-term killing: Tumor cells and CAR-T cells were co-incubated for 24 h at the specified effector-to-target ratio (1:1, 1:2, 1:4), and the lysis of target cells was measured. Then, new target cells were added to the effector cells at the same effector-to-target ratio, and four rounds of co-incubation and target cell lysis detection were repeated.

Real-time killing: Tumor cells were spread on a 96-well electrode plate coated with L-cysteine. After the tumor cells were fully covered, effector cells were added successively at the specified effector target ratios (2:1, 1:2, 1:4). Continuously collect data.

Transwell migration assay

The Transwell assay was used to evaluate the migration ability of CAR-T cells. 1 × 105 MockT cells, MSLN CAR-T cells, Intra2 CAR-T cells and Intra6 CAR-T cells were placed in small chambers coated with matrix gel. The chamber diameter was 6.5 mm and the pore diameter was 5 μm transwell (Corning). HCC1806 and Ovcar3 cells (each well was incubated at 8 × 105) were pre-coated in the lower chamber. The plate was incubated at 37 ° C with 5% CO2 for 17 h, and then fluorescence photography was taken to quantify the number of CAR-T cell migrations.

Cell proliferation detection

The proliferation cells was detected using the CellTrace™ far-infrared cell proliferation Kit (Invitrogen™). On the tenth day after the construction of CAR-T cells, MockT, MSLN CAR-T, Intra2 CAR-Tand Intra6 CAR-T cells were stained with FarRed for 15 min, washed twice with PBS, and the initial proliferation peak of CAR-T and the proliferation peak after 8 days of culture were detected by flow cytometry.

3D microsphere infiltration experiment

3D tumor microsphere models were constructed using Corning® 96-well transparent round-bottomed spherical microplates for HCC1806 cells and Huh7 cells with green fluorescent protein (GFP). After each well of 2E4 tumor cells formed into spheres and were cultured for 48 h, MockT, MSLN CAT-T, Intra2 CAR-T and Intra6 CAR-T cells were added to each well at the specified effector target ratios (2:1, 1:1). After 24 h, immunofluorescence photography showed the killing of 3D microspheres.

Mouse tumor model

Female B-NKG mice (6 weeks, Biocytogen, Beijing) were subcutaneously implanted with 2 × 106 HCC1806 cells. On day 10, mice were randomly divided into six groups (n = 6/group) and treated with PBS, 1 × 106 MSLN CAR-T, or Intra6-CAR-T cells. Tumor volumes were calculated using the formula: volume = (length × width²) × 0.5. Additionally, histological examination of tumors and major organs was performed at the endpoint to assess potential tissue damage or inflammation.

Cytokine measurement

Cell co-culture supernatant: MockT, MSLN CAR-T, Intra2 CAR-T, and Intra6 CAR-T cells were co-cultured with HCC1806 cells for 24 h to collect the supernatant. 200 µl of peripheral blood were collected from each group of mice, and then the samples were centrifuged at 3000×g for 10 min to separate the serum. IL-6, TNF-α and IFN-γ was determined by BD™ Cytometric Bead Array (CBA) Mouse/Rat Soluble Protein Master Buffer Kit (Cat:558266). Experiment were conducted as stated by the manufacturer. Data were processed and results generated using FCAP.GUI software.

Histopathological examination

On the tenth day after CAR-T cell reinfusion, one mouse in each group was randomly sacrificed to obtain tumor tissue samples. All mice were sacrificed on the 28th day after CAR-T reinfusion, and the heart, liver, spleen, lung, kidney and brain tissues were collected. The tissue specimens were fixed with 4% buffer solution formaldehyde. Paraffin sections were used for hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining and immunohistochemical analysis. Both the primary antibody and the secondary antibody were rabbit anti-human antibody CD3 antibody and enzyme-labeled goat anti-rabbit IgG H&L separate (Abcam). Then the cell nucleus was stained with DAPI (Abcam) Tumor staining of mice treated with PBS was used as the control. The infiltration quantity of IHC CD3+T cells was analyzed using ImageJ.

Reverse transcription-quantitative PCR (qRT-PCR) analysis

Total RNA was extracted from cells and tissues using TRIzol reagent, following the manufacturer’s guidelines, and quantified with a Thermo NanoDrop 2000. A total of 1 µg of RNA was reverse-transcribed into cDNA. Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) was carried out using a SYBR-based reaction mixture, in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions. All PCR amplifications were performed in triplicate across three independent experiments. The primers used for each gene are listed in Table S2.

RNA-seq analysis

CAR-T cells were prepared and expanded as described above. For transcriptomic profiling, 5 × 10⁶ cells per sample were harvested. Total RNA was extracted using the RNeasy Mini Kit (Qiagen), and RNA quality was assessed with an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer. Sequencing libraries were prepared using the NEBNext Ultra II RNA Library Prep Kit (NEB) and sequenced on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform to generate 150-bp paired-end reads. Raw reads were quality-checked with FastQC, trimmed with Trimmomatic, and aligned to the human reference genome (GRCh38) using STAR. Transcript abundance was quantified with RSEM, and differential expression analyses were conducted with DESeq2.

CRISPR/Cas9 knockout of the LFA-1 molecule

The LFA-1 molecule on the surface of CAR-T cells was knocked out using the CRISPR/Cas9 technology. A specific sgRNA (GTTCAACGTGACCTTCCGGC) targeting LFA-1 (ITGB2) was designed based on the LFA-1 gene sequence. The Cas9 protein and sgRNA were introduced into the constructed MSLN CAR-T cells and Intra6 CAR-T cells by electroporation to achieve gene editing. The knockdown efficiency of the LFA-1 gene was detected by flow cytometry using an APC-conjugated anti-human CD11a/CD18 (LFA-1) Antibody (Biolegend, Cat: 363409).

Statistical analysis

All experiments were performed at least three times. Data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism 10.0 software and presented as means ± SEM. Student’s t-test was used for two-group comparisons, and one-way or two-way ANOVA was used for multiple-group analysis. Statistical significance was defined as *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001, ns, not significant.

Results

Construction and phenotypic function verification of MSLN CAR-T cells

Although MSLN CAR-T cell therapy has shown promising results in solid tumors such as ovarian and breast cancers [19], its limited tumor infiltration remains a major barrier to therapeutic efficacy [20]. Poor intra-tumor penetration leads to uneven distribution, persistent reduction and functional exhaustion of CAR-T cells in the tumor microenvironment (TME), ultimately affecting anti-tumor activity [21]. Given that chemokines are abundantly expressed in most TMEs [22], we hypothesized that introducing chemokine receptors could enhance the tumor-infiltrating capacity of MSLN CAR-T cells. Using the GEPIA database, we identified CXCL16 and CCL2 as significantly upregulated chemokines in ovarian cancer tissues (Fig. 1A). As reported, their corresponding receptors are CXCR6 and CCR2, respectively. We therefore generated two modified CAR-T constructs co-expressing MSLN-CAR with either CXCR6 or CCR2, termed Intra6 CAR-T and Intra2 CAR-T, respectively (Fig. 1B). Flow cytometry confirmed stable expression of both chemokine receptors on the surface of engineered CAR-T cells (Fig. 1C–D). To identify suitable target cells, MSLN expression was examined across multiple tumor cell lines (Fig. 1E), and the MSLN-high HCC1806 line was selected for functional assays. Upon antigen stimulation, Intra2 and Intra6 CAR-T cells showed slightly reduced activation marker expression (CD25, CD69) compared to conventional MSLN CAR-T cells (p < 0.05) (Fig. 1F–G), but exhibited significantly lower levels of exhaustion markers PD-1, TIM-3, and LAG-3 (Fig. 1H– J). Furthermore, Intra2 and Intra6 CAR-T cells have superior proliferation capabilities (Fig. 1K). These results suggest that chemokine receptor co-expression improves the functional durability and persistence of MSLN CAR-T cells in vitro.

CCR2/CXCR6 receptor enhance the migration and tumor invasion abilities of MSLN CAR-T cells

To assess the impact of chemokine receptors CCR2 and CXCR6 on the migratory capacity of MSLN CAR-T cells, Transwell migration assays were performed using HCC1806 and Ovcar3 cell lines. Both Intra2 and Intra6 CAR-T cells exhibited significantly enhanced migration compared to MSLN CAR-T and MockT cells. Notably, the number of Intra6 CAR-T cells migrating toward Ovcar3 cells was more than fivefold higher than that of MSLN CAR-T cells (Fig. 2A–C). To further mimic the structural complexity of solid tumors and evaluate CAR-T cell infiltration and cytotoxicity, a three-dimensional (3D) tumor spheroid model was established. MockT, MSLN CAR-T, Intra2 CAR-T, and Intra6 CAR-T cells were co-cultured with spheroids at a defined effector-to-target ratio, and immunofluorescence imaging was used to assess tumor infiltration and cell lysis. While all CAR-T groups demonstrated spheroid penetration, as evidenced by reduced GFP fluorescence, Intra2 and particularly Intra6 CAR-T cells showed deeper infiltration into both the peripheral and core regions. Quantitative analysis confirmed the superior infiltration capacity of Intra6 CAR-T cells (Fig. 2D–G). Collectively, these results demonstrate that the introduction of CCR2 and CXCR6 enhances the chemotactic migration and tumor infiltration of MSLN CAR-T cells, with Intra6 CAR-T cells exhibiting the most robust intratumoral infiltration.

CCR2/CXCR6 receptor enhance the killing ability of MSLN CAR-T cells

To evaluate the cytotoxicity of MSLN CAR-T, Intra2 CAR-T, and Intra6 CAR-T cells, we performed standard cytotoxicity assays using HCC1806, Ovcar3, and Capan-2 as target cell lines, with MockT cells serving as negative controls. All CAR-T cell types demonstrated strong cytolytic activity compared to MockT, with Intra2 and particularly Intra6 CAR-T cells exhibiting significantly enhanced cytotoxicity. Cytolytic activity increased in a dose-dependent manner (Fig. 3A–B). To further validate these findings, real-time killing assays were conducted. Results confirmed that Intra2 and Intra6 CAR-T cells displayed superior killing efficiency, with Intra6 CAR-T cells inducing the fastest and most complete tumor cell lysis (Fig. 3C–K). Importantly, none of the CAR-T constructs, including Intra6, exhibited cytotoxicity against the non-target Huh7 cell line, demonstrating preserved antigen specificity (Fig. 3L–N). Collectively, these findings indicate that co-expression of chemokine receptors—particularly CXCR6—markedly enhances the cytotoxic function of MSLN CAR-T cells while maintaining target specificity.

CCR2/CXCR6 maintains the memory stemness of MSLN CAR-T cells and enhances the durable anti-tumor effect

To characterize the phenotypic and functional properties of CAR-T cells, flow cytometry was used to assess CD45RO and CD62L expression on MSLN, Intra2, and Intra6 CAR-T cells. Intra2 and Intra6 CAR-T cells exhibited significantly higher proportions of T stem cell memory (TSCM) and central memory T cells (TCM) compared to MSLN CAR-T cells, while showing lower proportions of effector memory (TEM) and terminally differentiated effector T cells (TTE) (Fig. 4A). In CD4+ and CD8+ cell subsets, Intra CAR-T cells, particularly Intra6 CAR-T, exhibit a pronounced reduction in exhaustion (Fig. S1a-S1b). Moreover, Intra6 displays higher proportions of TSCM and TCM and lower proportions of TEM and TTE compared with MSLN CAR-T cells (Fig. S1c-S1f). These results suggest that chemokine receptor expression helps maintain early memory phenotypes, potentially enhancing proliferative capacity and in vivo persistence. A multi-round killing assay (R1–R4) demonstrated that, while MSLN CAR-T cells had strong cytotoxicity in R1 and R2, their activity declined in R3 and R4. In contrast, Intra2 and Intra6 CAR-T cells maintained high cytotoxicity across all rounds, with Intra6 CAR-T cells showing the strongest tumor lysis in R3 and R4 (Fig. 4B–J). The CBA results showed that compared with the MSLN CAR-T, the Intra CAR-T cells, especially the Intra6 CAR-T cells, released higher levels of IL-6, TNF-α, and IFN-γ cytokines after co-incubation with tumor cells. This result was consistent with the enhanced killing effect of the Intra6 CAR-T cells (Fig. 4K–M). The aforementioned results demonstrate that CCR2/CXCR6 can promote the development of a memory phenotype in MSLN CAR-T cells, thereby enhancing their sustained cytotoxic activity and capacity for cytokine secretion.

Intra6 CAR-T cells showed stronger tumor suppressive effects and better immune infiltration ability in vivo

To evaluate the therapeutic effect of MSLN CAR-T cells with chemokine receptor introduction in vivo, we successfully constructed HCC 1806 cells that can secrete the chemokine CXCL16 in vitro (Fig. 5A–C).Then we injected NSG mice with HCC1806 cells secreting CXCL16 or regular HCC1806 cells (Fig. 5D). Ten days later, mice were treated with PBS, MSLN CAR-T, or Intra6 CAR-T cells (1 × 10⁶ cells per mouse). Tumor volume was measured every two days, and body weight was monitored. Peripheral blood was collected to assess CAR-T cell activation, exhaustion, and memory phenotypic distribution. Results showed no significant difference in CD69 expression between Intra6 and MSLN CAR-T cells (Fig. 5E), but PD-1 expression was significantly reduced in Intra6 CAR-T cells, indicating lower exhaustion (Fig. 5F). Additionally, the proportions of TSCM and TCM cells were significantly higher in the Intra6 CAR-T group compared to the MSLN CAR-T group, suggesting better memory retention (Fig. 5G). Tumor growth was significantly inhibited in the Intra6 CAR-T group, with enhanced tumor regression in the presence of CXCL16 (Fig. 5H–I). Immunohistochemical staining revealed higher tumor infiltration by Intra6 CAR-T cells compared to MSLN CAR-T cells, and CXCL16 further enhanced this infiltration (Fig. 5J–M). The results of peripheral blood supernatant CBA showed that, compared with MSLN CAR-T, the Intra6 CAR-T cells released a significantly higher level of IFN-γ in the mice (Fig. 5N). These results suggest that Intra6 CAR-T cells improve tumor infiltration, reduce exhaustion, and provide more durable tumor-killing effects in vivo.

Safety assessment of intra6 CAR-T cells

To assess the safety of MSLN CAR-T cells with chemokine introduction, we monitored body weight and tissue changes in mice treated with Intra6 CAR-T cells. No significant changes in body weight were observed (Fig. 6A–B). On day 28, all mice were euthanized, and tissues from the heart, liver, spleen, lung, kidney, and brain were collected for hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining. Histological analysis revealed normal tissue structures in all organs, with no signs of damage or dysfunction (Fig. 6C). These results confirm that Intra6 CAR-T cells demonstrate strong anti-tumor efficacy without compromising safety.

RNAseq analysis of differential gene expression of MSLN CAR-T cells after CXCR6 implantation

To explore how CXCR6 enhances the anti-tumor efficacy of CAR-T cells, we performed RNA sequencing on both Intra6 and MSLN CAR-T cells. The volcano plot reveals the differences in gene expression between MSLN CAR-T and Intra6 CAR-T. A large number of genes show significant differential expression (Fig. 7A). Gene ontology (GO) enrichment analysis confirmed these differences: compared to MSLN CAR-T cells, Intra6 CAR-T cells exhibited stronger enrichment in pathways related to immune activation, T cell migration/chemotaxis, and cytotoxicity (Fig. 7B). Further analysis revealed that Intra6 CAR-T cells exhibited upregulated gene expression related to T cell proliferation, maintenance of TSCM/TCM memory phenotype, inhibition of cell exhaustion and apoptosis, cytotoxicity and cell division (Fig. S2a-S2e). These results were consistent with the persistent anti-exhaustion and killing functions of Intra6 CAR-T cells in vitro.

Additionally, CXCR6 upregulates LFA-1, facilitating interaction with integrins on the vascular endothelium and promoting the migration-adhesion-extravasation process of T cells, thereby improving tumor infiltration and enhancing anti-tumor efficacy. (Data are present as means ± SEM from three different experiments. ns, not significant; *, p < 0.05; **, p < 0.01; ***, p < 0.001; two-way ANOVA.)

Based on the significant enhancement of the in vitro migration and invasion abilities as well as the in vivo tumor infiltration ability of Intra6 CAR-T cells, we conducted further analysis. Notably, chemotaxis-related genes were significantly upregulated in Intra6 CAR-T cells (Fig. 7C). Additionally, genes involved in migration, adhesion, and extravasation were also upregulated, suggesting improved functional persistence and tumor infiltration potential (Fig. 7D). GO pathway analyses further indicated that Intra6 CAR-T cells were enriched in cell morphology regulation pathways, suggesting enhanced migration and directed movement (Fig. 7E). Among the upregulated genes, LFA-1 [23], an adhesion molecule facilitating T cell binding to vascular endothelial cells via ICAM1 [24], showed increased expression in Intra6 CAR-T cells, suggesting stronger transendothelial migration abilities [25–27]. Additionally, PAK1 [28] and FSCN1, both involved in cytoskeleton remodeling, were upregulated, indicating enhanced motility and invasiveness [29, 30]. These results led to the hypothesis that CXCR6 promotes tumor infiltration by upregulating LFA-1, PAK1, and FSCN1, facilitating adhesion, migration, and extravasation of CAR-T cells. RT-qPCR confirmed significantly higher mRNA expression levels of PAK1, FSCN1, and LFA-1 in Intra6 CAR-T cells compared to MSLN CAR-T cells (Fig. 7F). Flow cytometry further verified the upregulation of LFA-1 expression on the surface of Intra6 CAR-T cells (Fig. 7G). The Transwell migration assays demonstrated significantly enhanced migration ability in Intra6 CAR-T cells, which was abolished in LFA-1-deficient CAR-T cells, emphasizing the critical role of LFA-1 in CXCR6-mediated migration (Fig. 7H-I). In conclusion, the introduction of CXCR6 enhances CAR-T cell tumor infiltration by upregulating LFA-1, PAK1, and FSCN1, promoting cytoskeleton remodeling, adhesion, and transendothelial migration, thereby improving anti-tumor efficacy (Fig. 7J).

Discussion

Solid tumors present a formidable challenge for CAR-T cell therapy, primarily due to an immunosuppressive microenvironment and inefficient T cell trafficking and infiltration [31–35]. In this study, we addressed the critical barrier of poor tumor infiltration by engineering MSLN-directed CAR-T cells to co-express the chemokine receptors CCR2 or CXCR6. Our findings demonstrate that this strategy, particularly the introduction of CXCR6, profoundly enhances the migratory capacity, tumor penetration, cytotoxicity, and functional persistence of CAR-T cells in vitro and in vivo, without compromising safety or antigen specificity. The superior anti-tumor efficacy of Intra6 CAR-T cells is associated with a less exhausted phenotype, a stem-like memory signature, and the transcriptional upregulation of a pro-migratory adhesion program.

A pivotal finding is the synergistic relationship between CXCR6 expression on CAR-T cells and CXCL16 secretion within the tumor milieu. Our in vivo data compellingly showed that the anti-tumor efficacy of Intra6 CAR-T cells was significantly potentiated in the presence of tumor-derived CXCL16 (Fig. 6H-I, L-M). This underscores a potential limitation of this monotherapy: its optimal efficacy is contingent upon sufficient ligand availability within the tumor microenvironment (TME) [34, 36]. Consequently, strategies designed to augment CXCL16 expression in target tissues represent a promising avenue for combinatorial regimens. Interrogation of public databases such as TCGA reveals that CXCL16 is frequently expressed in various MSLN-positive malignancies [37, 38]. It is notably upregulated in subsets of ovarian carcinomas [37, 39, 40] and pancreatic ductal adenocarcinomas [41, 42]—tumors characterized by high MSLN expression. In non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), CXCL16 expression correlates with immune infiltration and can be further induced by radiotherapy and certain chemotherapeutic agents [43, 44]. Thus, combining Intra6 CAR-T cells with standard therapies that induce CXCL16 secretion could constitute a highly effective strategy to augment homing and therapeutic efficacy.

RNA sequencing provided a mechanistic underpinning for the enhanced infiltration observed with Intra6 CAR-T cells, revealing significant upregulation of genes orchestrating adhesion, migration, and cytoskeletal dynamics, notably ITGAL (LFA-1), PAK1, and FSCN1. While we functionally validated the crucial role of LFA-1 via knockout in Transwell assays (Fig. 7H-I), we acknowledge that further validation is warranted. To unequivocally establish the contribution of these pathways, future studies should employ intravital imaging to dynamically visualize and quantify the extravasation and intratumoral motility of Intra6 CAR-T cells relative to controls. Furthermore, blocking assays utilizing monoclonal antibodies against LFA-1 or small-molecule inhibitors of PAK1 are essential to delineate the functional contribution of each pathway to the enhanced migration and cytotoxicity observed both in vitro and in vivo.

The translational potential of any potent CAR-T cell therapy necessitates rigorous safety considerations [34, 45]. First, the risk of cytokine release syndrome (CRS) is paramount [46, 47]. Although Intra6 CAR-T cells secreted elevated levels of inflammatory cytokines (e.g., IL-6, TNF-α, IFN-γ) upon tumor engagement (Figs. 4K-M and 6N)—correlating with enhanced efficacy—this also mandates vigilant clinical monitoring for CRS. Incorporating a safety switch, such as a caspase-9-based suicide gene, could provide a critical mitigative strategy [48–50]. Second, the risk of on-target, off-tumor toxicity remains governed by the MSLN-CAR’s specificity [51]. Our data confirmed that Intra6 CAR-T cells retained strict specificity and exhibited no cytotoxicity against MSLN-negative cells (Fig. 3L-N). However, enhanced migratory potential could theoretically facilitate infiltration into healthy tissues exhibiting low-level MSLN and cognate chemokine expression [52]. A comprehensive pathological survey of CXCL16 expression in vital organs is therefore imperative. Finally, our approach appears to mitigate T cell overstimulation and exhaustion, as Intra6 CAR-T cells sustained a more memory-like phenotype and lower expression of exhaustion markers (e.g., PD-1) both in vitro and in vivo (Figs. 1H-J and 6F). This suggests that improved infiltration may alleviate the chronic, suboptimal antigen stimulation that drives dysfunction in densely packed tumors.

The observed reduction in PD-1 expression on Intra6 CAR-T cells presents a compelling rationale for combination therapy with immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs). Although our cells exhibit reduced exhaustion, the immunosuppressive TME may still engage alternative inhibitory pathways. Combining Intra6 CAR-T with PD-1/PD-L1 blockade could preempt the eventual acquisition of exhaustion, thereby further sustaining anti-tumor activity. This combination has shown promise in other CAR-T models [53, 54] and should be evaluated in future studies using immunocompetent humanized mouse models bearing MSLN+/CXCL16+ tumors to comprehensively assess its synergistic potential and safety profile.

In conclusion, we have demonstrated that CXCR6 engineering confers robust tumor-homing and killing capabilities upon MSLN CAR-T cells. The mechanism involves both chemokine receptor-ligand guidance and the transcriptional orchestration of a multifaceted migratory program. Future efforts should focus on combinatorial strategies that modulate the TME to express cognate chemokines and counteract immune suppression, thereby maximizing the clinical potential of this promising therapeutic paradigm for solid tumors.

Conclusion

Enhancing tumor infiltration and antitumor efficacy of CAR-T cells by introducing chemokine receptors (CCR2 and CXCR6)

This study demonstrates that overexpression of chemokine receptors (CCR2 and CXCR6) significantly enhances the tumor infiltration capacity of MSLN CAR-T cells, concurrently improving their anti-exhaustion capability, promoting memory T cell phenotypes, and conferring sustained cytotoxicity. Furthermore, Intra6 CAR-T cells (engineered with these receptors) display favorable safety profiles. Mechanistically, the introduction of CXCR6 enhances CAR-T cell tumor infiltration by upregulating LFA-1, PAK1, and FSCN1, promoting cytoskeleton remodeling, adhesion, and transendothelial migration, thereby improving anti-tumor efficacy. These results highlight a promising strategy to enhance CAR-T cell tumor homing and antitumor efficacy via chemokine receptor engineering.

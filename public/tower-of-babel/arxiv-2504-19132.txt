1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{H}
denote the class of all analytic functions in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
and let
\mathcal{A}
denote the class of functions
f\in\mathcal{H}
of the form

\displaystyle f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}.

(1.1)

Further, let
\mathcal{S}
be the subclass of
\mathcal{A}
that are univalent (
i.e.
, one-to-one) in
\mathbb{D}
.
A domain
\Omega
is called starlike with respect to a point
z_{0}\in\Omega
if the line segment joining
z_{0}
to any point in
\Omega
lies in
\Omega
,
i.e.
,

(1-t)z_{0}+tz\in\Omega
for all
t\in[0,1]
and for all
z\in\Omega
. In particular, if
z_{0}=0
, then
\Omega
is simply called starlike. A function

f\in\mathcal{A}
is said to be starlike if
f(\mathbb{D})
is starlike with respect to the origin.
Let
\mathcal{S}^{*}
denote the class of starlike functions in
\mathbb{D}
. It is well-known that a function
f\in\mathcal{A}
is in
\mathcal{S}^{*}
if, and only if,
\text{Re}(zf^{\prime}(z)/f(z))>0
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
. A domain
\Omega
is called convex if it is starlike with respect to any point
in
\Omega
. In other words, convexity implies starlikeness, but the converse is not necessarily true. A domain can be starlike
without being convex. A function
f\in\mathcal{A}
is said to be convex if
f(\mathbb{D})
is convex. Let
\mathcal{C}
denote the class of convex functions in
\mathbb{D}
.
It is well-known that a function
f\in\mathcal{A}
is in
\mathcal{C}
if, and only if,
\text{Re}\left(1+zf^{\prime\prime}(z)/f^{\prime}(z)\right)>0
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Moreover, a function

f\in\mathcal{A}
is said to be
\alpha
-spirallike function if
\textrm{Re}(e^{-i\alpha}zf^{\prime}(z)/f(z))>0
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
, where
-\pi/2<\alpha<\pi/2
.
For more details about the aforementioned classes, we refer to
[
12
,
15
,
37
]
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be the class of all analytic functions
\omega:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
and
\mathcal{B}_{0}=\{\omega\in\mathcal{B}:\omega(0)=0\}
.
Functions in
\mathcal{B}_{0}
are called Schwarz function. According to Schwarz’s lemma, if
\omega\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
, then
|\omega(z)|\leq|z|
and
|\omega^{\prime}(0)|\leq 1
.
Strict inequality holds in both estimates unless
\omega(z)=e^{i\theta}z
,
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. A sharpened form of the Schwarz lemma, known as the Schwarz-Pick lemma,
gives the estimate
|\omega^{\prime}(z)|\leq(1-|\omega(z)|^{2})/(1-|z|^{2})
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
\omega\in\mathcal{B}
.

An analytic function
f
in
\mathbb{D}
is said to be subordinate to an analytic function
g
in
\mathbb{D}
, written as
f\prec g
, if there exists a function
\omega\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
such that
f(z)=g(\omega(z))
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Moreover, if
g
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}
,
then
f\prec g
if, and only if,
f(0)=g(0)
and
f(\mathbb{D})\subseteq g(\mathbb{D})
. For basic details and results on subordination classes, we refer
to
[
12
, Chapter 6]
.
Using the notion of subordination, Ma and Minda
[
27
]
have introduced more general subclasses of starlike and convex functions as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}:\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\prec\varphi(z)\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{C}(\varphi)=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}:1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\prec\varphi(z)\right\},

where the function
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
, called Ma-Minda function, is analytic and univalent in
\mathbb{D}
such that
\varphi(\mathbb{D})
has positive real
part, symmetric with respect to the real axis, starlike with respect to
\varphi(0)=1
and
\varphi^{\prime}(0)>0
. A Ma-Minda function has the Taylor series expansion
of the form
\varphi(z)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}

(a_{1}>0)
.
We call
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)
and
\mathcal{C}(\varphi)
the Ma-Minda type starlike and Ma-Minda type convex classes associated with
\varphi
, respectively. One
can easily prove the inclusion
relations
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)\subset\mathcal{S}^{*}
and
\mathcal{C}(\varphi)\subset\mathcal{C}
. It is known that
f\in\mathcal{C}(\varphi)
if, and only if,
zf^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)
.

For different choices of the function
\varphi
, the classes
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)
and
\mathcal{C}(\varphi)
generate several important subclasses of
\mathcal{S}^{*}

and
\mathcal{C}
, respectively.
For example, if
\varphi(z)=(1+z)/(1-z)
, then
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)=\mathcal{S}^{*}
and
\mathcal{C}(\varphi)=\mathcal{C}
. For
\varphi(z)=(1+(1-2\alpha)z)/(1-z),~0\leq\alpha<1
, we get the classes
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\alpha)
of starlike functions of
order
\alpha
and
\mathcal{C}(\alpha)
of convex functions of order
\alpha
. If
\varphi=((1+z)/(1-z))^{\alpha}
for
0<\alpha\leq 1
, then

\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)=\mathcal{S}\mathcal{S}^{*}(\alpha)
the class of strongly starlike functions of order
\alpha
and

\mathcal{C}(\varphi)=\mathcal{S}\mathcal{C}(\alpha)
the class of strongly convex functions of order
\alpha
(see
[
35
]
). Also for
\varphi=(1+Az)/(1+Bz),~-1\leq B<A\leq 1
, we have the classes
of Janowski starlike functions
\mathcal{S}^{*}[A,B]
and Janowski convex functions
\mathcal{C}[A,B]
(see
[
18
]
).
For
\varphi(z)=(1+2/\pi^{2}(\log(1-\sqrt{z})/(1+\sqrt{z}))^{2})
the class
\mathcal{C}(\varphi)
(resp.,
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)
) is the class
UCV
(resp.
UST
) of
normalized uniformly convex (resp. starlike) functions (see
[
34
,
16
,
17
,
33
]
). Ma and Minda
[
25
,
26
]
have studied the class
UCV

extensively. Cho
et al.

[
9
]
introduced the family
\mathcal{S}^{*}(1+\sin z)
and studied the radius of starlikeness and convexity. Kargar
et al.

[
19
]

have introduced the class
\mathcal{BS}^{*}(\alpha):=\mathcal{S}^{*}(1+z/(1-\alpha z^{2}))
, which is associated with the Booth lemniscate.
The class
\mathcal{S}^{*}(2/(1+e^{-z})
was introduced by Goel and Kumar
[
14
]
and studied several inclusion relations, radius problems as well as coefficient estimates.

In this paper, we consider two different classes of functions:
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{hyp}=\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi_{s})
with
\varphi_{s}(z)=1/(1-z)^{s}

(0<s\leq 1)
and
\mathcal{S}_{L}^{*}=\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)
with
\varphi(z)=(1+sz)^{2}
(
0<s\leq 1/\sqrt{2}
), where the branch of the logarithm is determined by
\varphi_{s}(0)=1
. More precisely,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{*}_{hyp}=\left\{f\in\mathcal{A}:\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\prec\frac{1}{(1-z)^{s}},~0<s\leq 1\right\}

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{*}_{L}=\left\{f\in\mathcal{A}:\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\prec(1+sz)^{2},0<s\leq 1/\sqrt{2}\right\}.

The function

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-z)^{s}}=\mathrm{exp}(-s\log(1-z))=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{s(s+1)\cdots(s+n-1)}{n!}z^{n}\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}),

where the branch of the logarithm is determined by
\log(1)=0
.
It is evident that the function
\varphi(z)=1/(1-z)^{s}
maps the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
onto a domain bounded by the right branch of the hyperbola

\displaystyle H(s):=\left\{re^{i\theta}:r=\frac{1}{(2\cos(\theta/s))^{s}},~|\theta|<\frac{\pi s}{2}\right\},

as illustrated in Figure
2
.
Moreover,
\varphi(\mathbb{D})
is symmetric respecting the real axis,
\varphi
is convex and hence starlike with
respect to
\varphi(0)=1
. It is evident that
\varphi^{\prime}(0)>0
and
\varphi
has positive real part in
\mathbb{D}
. Thus,
\varphi
satisfies the category of Ma-Minda functions.
A function
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}_{hyp}
if, and only if, there exists an analytic function
p
with
p(0)=1
and
p(z)\prec 1/(1-z)^{s}
in
\mathbb{D}
such that

\displaystyle f(z)=z\;\mathrm{exp}\left(\int_{0}^{z}\frac{p(t)-1}{t}dt\right).

(1.2)

If we choose,
p(t)=1/(1-t)^{s}
, then from (
1.2
), we obtain the function

\displaystyle f_{s,1}(z):=z\;\mathrm{exp}\left(\int_{0}^{z}\frac{(1-t)^{-s}-1}{t}dt\right)=z+sz^{2}+\frac{3s^{2}+s}{4}z^{3}+\frac{17s^{3}+15s^{2}+4s}{36}z^{4}+\cdots.

Image of
\mathbb{D}
under the mapping
1/(1-z)^{s}
for
s=1/3

Figure 2.
Image of
\mathbb{D}
under the mapping
(1+sz)^{2}
for
s=1/\sqrt{2}

The function
\varphi(z)=(1+sz)^{2}
maps the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
onto a domain bounded by a limaçon given by

\displaystyle\left\{u+iv\in\mathbb{C}:\left((u-1)^{2}+v^{2}-s^{4}\right)^{2}=4s^{2}\left(\left(u-1+s^{2}\right)^{2}+v^{2}\right)\right\},

which is symmetric about the real axis, as illustrated in Figure
2
. Note that for
0<s\leq 1/\sqrt{2}
,
\varphi(z)
satisfies the category of Ma-Minda functions.
A function
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}_{L}
if, and only if, there exists
an analytic function
p
with
p(0)=1
and
p\prec(1+sz)^{2}
in
\mathbb{D}
such that

\displaystyle f(z)=z\;\mathrm{exp}\left(\int_{0}^{z}\frac{p(t)-1}{t}dt\right).

(1.3)

If we choose
p(z)=(1+sz)^{2}
in (
1.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle f_{s,2}(z)=z\;\mathrm{exp}\left(\int_{0}^{z}\frac{(1+st)^{2}-1}{t}dt\right)=z+2sz^{2}+\frac{5}{2}s^{2}z^{3}+\cdots.

The functions
f_{s,1}(z)
and
f_{s,2}(z)
plays the role of extremal function for many extremal problems in the classes
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{hyp}
and
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{L}
, respectively.

It is important to note that
f\in\mathcal{C}_{hyp}
(resp.
\mathcal{C}_{L}
) if, and only if,
zf^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}^{*}_{hyp}
(resp.
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{L}
), where the classes
\mathcal{C}_{hyp}
and
\mathcal{C}_{L}
are defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{hyp}=\left\{f\in\mathcal{A}:1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\prec\frac{1}{(1-z)^{s}},~0<s\leq 1\right\}

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{L}=\left\{f\in\mathcal{A}:1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\prec(1+sz)^{2},~0<s\leq 1/\sqrt{2}\right\}.

It is evident that a function
f\in\mathcal{C}_{hyp}
if, and only if, there exists an analytic function
p
with
p(0)=1
and
p(z)\prec 1/(1-z)^{s}
in
\mathbb{D}
such that

\displaystyle f(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\left(\;\mathrm{exp}\left(\int_{0}^{u}\frac{p(t)-1}{t}dt\right)\right)du.

(1.4)

If we choose
p(z)=1/(1-z)^{s}
in (

), we obtain

\displaystyle f_{s,3}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\mathrm{exp}\left(\int_{0}^{u}\frac{(1-t)^{-s}-1}{t}dt\right)du\in\mathcal{C}_{hyp}.

Note that

.

. For a more in-depth results of these classes, we refer to

2.
Pre-Schwarzian Norm

An analytic function
f(z)
in a domain
\Omega
is said to be locally univalent if for each
z_{0}\in\Omega
, there exists a neighborhood
U
of
z_{0}
such that
f(z)
is univalent in
U
. It is well-known that the non-vanishing of the Jacobian is necessary and sufficient conditions for local univalence (see
[
12
, Chapter 1]
).
Let
\mathcal{LU}
denote the subclass of
\mathcal{H}
consisting of all locally univalent functions in
\mathbb{D}
,
i.e.
,
\mathcal{LU}:=\{f\in\mathcal{H}:f^{\prime}(z)\not=0~~\text{ for all }~~z\in\mathbb{D}\}
. For
f\in\mathcal{LU}
, the pre-Schwarzian derivative is defined by

\displaystyle P_{f}(z):=\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)},

and the pre-Schwarzian norm (the hyperbolic sup-norm) is defined by

\displaystyle\|P_{f}\|:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\;(1-|z|^{2})\left|P_{f}(z)\right|.

This norm plays an important rule in the theory of Teichmüller spaces. For a univalent function
f
in
\mathbb{D}
, it is well-known that
\|P_{f}\|\leq 6
and the equality
is attained for the Koebe function or its rotation. One of the most used univalence criterion for locally univalent analytic functions is the Becker’s univalence criterion
[
7
]
,
which states that if
f\in\mathcal{LU}
and
\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)\left|zP_{f}(z)\right|\leq 1
, then
f
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}
. In a subsequent study,
Becker and Pommerenke
[
8
]
prove that the constant
1
is sharp. In 1976, Yamashita
[
38
]

proved that
\|P_{f}\|<\infty
is finite if, and only if,
f
is uniformly
locally univalent in
\mathbb{D}
. Moreover, if
\|P_{f}\|<2
, then
f
is bounded in
\mathbb{D}
(see
[
23
]
).

In the field of univalent function theory, several researchers have studied the pre-Schwarzian norm for various subclasses of analytic and univalent functions.
In 1998, Sugawa
[
36
]
established the sharp estimate of the pre-Schwarzian norm for functions in the class of strongly starlike functions of order
\alpha
(
0<\alpha\leq 1
).
In
1999
, Yamashita
[
39
]
proved that
\|P_{f}\|\leq 6-4\alpha
for
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\alpha)
and
\|P_{f}\|\leq 4(1-\alpha)
for
f\in\mathcal{C}(\alpha)
,
where
0\leq\alpha<1
and both the estimates are sharp. In
2000
, Okuyama
[
29
]
established the sharp estimate of the pre-Schwarzian norm for
\alpha
-spirallike functions. Kim and Sugawa
[
24
]
established the sharp
estimate of the pre-Schwarzian norm
\|P_{f}\|\leq 2(A-B)/(1+\sqrt{1-B^{2}})
for
f\in\mathcal{C}[A,B]
(see also
[
31
]
). Ponnusamy and Sahoo
[
32
]

obtained the sharp estimates of the pre-Schwarzian norm for functions in the class

\mathcal{S}^{*}[\alpha,\beta]:=S^{*}\left(\left((1+(1-2\beta)z)/(1-z)\right)^{\alpha}\right)
, where
0<\alpha\leq 1
and
0\leq\beta<1
. In
2014
, Aghalary and Orouji
[
1
]
obtained the sharp estimate of the pre-Schwarzian norm for
\alpha
-spirallike function of order
\rho
, where
\alpha\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)
and
\rho\in[0,1)
. The pre-Schwarzian norm of certain integral transform of
f
for certain subclass of
f
has been also studied in the literature. For a detailed study on pre-Schwarzian norm, we refer to
[
22
,
30
,
31
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
11
]
and the references therein.

In this paper, we establish sharp estimates of the pre-Schwarzian norms for functions in the classes
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{hyp}
,
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{L}
,
\mathcal{C}_{hyp}
and
\mathcal{C}_{L}
.

3.
Main results

In the following result, we establish the sharp estimate of the pre-Schwarzian norm for functions
f
in the class
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{hyp}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}_{hyp}
. Then the pre-Schwarzian norm satisfies the following sharp inequality

\displaystyle\|P_{f}\|\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\dfrac{st_{s}(1+t_{s})+(1+t_{s})(1-t_{s})^{1-s}-(1-t_{s}^{2})}{t_{s}}&\text{for}~s\in(0,1)\\[5.69054pt]
4&\text{for}~s=1,\end{array}\right.

where
t_{s}\in(0,1)
is the unique root of the equation

\displaystyle\frac{(1-t)^{-s}\left(st^{2}(1-t)^{s}+t^{2}(1-t)^{s}+st^{2}+(1-t)^{s}+st-t^{2}-1\right)}{t^{2}}=0.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}_{hyp}
. By the definition of the class
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{hyp}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\prec\frac{1}{(1-z)^{s}}.

Thus, there exist a Schwarz function
\omega\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}=\frac{1}{(1-\omega(z))^{s}}.

Taking logarithmic derivative on both sides with respect to
z
, we obtain

\displaystyle P_{f}(z)=\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}=\frac{s\omega^{\prime}(z)}{1-\omega(z)}+\frac{1}{z}\left(\frac{1}{(1-\omega(z))^{s}}-1\right).

Since
|\omega(z)|\leq|z|<1
and the branch of the logarithm is determined by
\log(1)=0
, thus, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-\omega(z))^{s}}=\mathrm{exp}(-s\log(1-\omega(z)))=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{s(s+1)\cdots(s+n-1)}{n!}\omega^{n}(z)\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}).

In view of the Schwarz-Pick lemma, we have

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})|P_{f}(z)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})\left|\frac{s\omega^{\prime}(z)}{1-\omega(z)}+\frac{1}{z}\left(\frac{1}{(1-\omega(z))^{s}}-1\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq(1-|z|^{2})\left(\frac{s|\omega^{\prime}(z)|}{1-|\omega(z)|}+\frac{1}{|z|}\left(\frac{1}{(1-|\omega(z)|)^{s}}-1\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{s\left(1-|\omega(z)|^{2}\right)}{1-|\omega(z)|}+\frac{(1-|z|^{2})}{|z|}\left(\frac{1}{(1-|\omega(z)|)^{s}}-1\right).

For
0\leq t:=|\omega(z)|\leq|z|<1
, we have

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})|P_{f}(z)|\leq s(1+t)+\frac{(1-|z|^{2})}{|z|}\left(\frac{1}{(1-t)^{s}}-1\right).

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\|P_{f}\|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\;(1-|z|^{2})|P_{f}(z)|\leq\sup_{0\leq t\leq|z|<1}F_{1}(|z|,t),

(3.2)

where

\displaystyle F_{1}(r,t)=s(1+t)+\frac{(1-r^{2})}{r}\left(\frac{1}{(1-t)^{s}}-1\right)\quad\text{for}\quad r=|z|.

Now the objective is to determine the supremum of
F_{1}(r,t)
on
\Omega=\{(r,t):0<t\leq r<1\}
.
Differentiating partially
F_{1}(r,t)
with respect to
r
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial r}F_{1}(r,t)=-\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}}+1\right)\left(\frac{1}{(1-t)^{s}}-1\right)<0.

Therefore,
F_{1}(r,t)
is a monotonically decreasing function of
r\in[t,1)
and it follows that
F_{1}(r,t)\leq F_{1}(t,t)=F_{2}(t)
,
where

\displaystyle F_{2}(t)=s(1+t)+\frac{(1-t^{2})}{t}\left(\frac{1}{(1-t)^{s}}-1\right).

It is evident that
F_{2}(t)=2(1+t)
for
s=1
. Hence, we have
\|P_{f}\|\leq 4
. We consider the case, where
0<s<1
.
Differentiating
F_{2}(t)
with respect to
t
, we obtain

\displaystyle F_{2}^{\prime}(t)=\frac{(1-t)^{-s}\left(st^{2}(1-t)^{s}+t^{2}(1-t)^{s}+st^{2}+(1-t)^{s}+st-t^{2}-1\right)}{t^{2}}

and

\displaystyle F_{2}^{\prime\prime}(t)=\frac{s^{2}t^{3}+s^{2}t^{2}-st^{3}+st^{2}+2t(1-t)^{s}-2(1-t)^{s}-2st-2t+2}{(1-t)^{s+1}t^{3}}.

Let

\displaystyle F_{3}(t)=\frac{s^{2}t^{3}+s^{2}t^{2}-st^{3}+st^{2}+2t(1-t)^{s}-2(1-t)^{s}-2st-2t+2}{(1-t)^{s+1}}.

It is evident that
F_{2}^{\prime\prime}(t)=F_{3}(t)/t^{3}
and

\displaystyle F_{3}^{\prime}(t)=\frac{-s(1-s)t^{2}(st+s-2t+4)}{(1-t)^{s+2}}<0\quad\text{for}\quad 0<t<1,~0<s<1.

Therefore,
F_{3}(t)
is a monotonically decreasing function of
t\in(0,1)
and it follows that
F_{3}(t)\leq\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}F_{3}(t)=0
,
i.e.,

F_{2}^{\prime\prime}(t)\leq 0
for
0<t<1
.
Thus,
F_{2}^{\prime}(t)
is a monotonically decreasing function in
t
with
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}F_{2}^{\prime}(t)=s(s+3)/2
and

(3.3)

4&\text{for}~s=1,\end{array}\right.

where

In the following result, we obtain the estimate of the pre-Schwarzian norm for functions in the class
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{L}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}_{L}
. Then the pre-Schwarzian norm satisfies the following inequality

\displaystyle\|P_{f}\|\leq\frac{2s(1-t_{s}^{2})}{1-st_{s}}+\frac{(1-t_{s}^{2})\left((1+st_{s})^{2}-1\right)}{t_{s}},

where
t_{s}\in(0,1)
is the unique positive root of the equation

\displaystyle\frac{-3s^{4}t^{4}+2s^{3}t^{3}+\left(s^{4}+7s^{2}\right)t^{2}-\left(2s^{3}+8s\right)t+3s^{2}}{(1-st)^{2}}=0.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}_{L}
. By the definition of the class
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{L}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\prec(1+sz)^{2}.

Thus, there exists a Schwarz function
\omega(z)\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}=(1+s\omega(z))^{2}.

Taking logarithmic derivative on both sides with respect to
z
, we obtain

\displaystyle P_{f}(z)=\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}=\frac{2s\omega^{\prime}(z)}{1+s\omega(z)}+\frac{1}{z}\left((1+s\omega(z))^{2}-1\right),

In view of the Schwarz-Pick lemma, we have

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})|P_{f}(z)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})\left(\frac{2s|\omega^{\prime}(z)|}{|1+s\omega(z)|}+\frac{|(1+s\omega(z))^{2}-1|}{|z|}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})\left(\frac{2s|\omega^{\prime}(z)|}{1-s|\omega(z)|}+\frac{(1+s|\omega(z)|)^{2}-1}{|z|}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2s\left(1-|\omega(z)|^{2}\right)}{1-s|\omega(z)|}+\frac{(1-|z|^{2})\left((1+s|\omega(z)|)^{2}-1\right)}{|z|}.

For
0\leq t:=|\omega(z)|\leq|z|<1
, we obtain

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})|P_{f}(z)|\leq\frac{2s(1-t^{2})}{1-st}+\frac{(1-|z|^{2})\left((1+st)^{2}-1\right)}{|z|}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\|P_{f}\|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\;(1-|z|^{2})|P_{f}(z)|\leq\sup_{0\leq t\leq|z|<1}F_{4}(|z|,t),

(3.6)

where

\displaystyle F_{4}(r,t)=\frac{2s(1-t^{2})}{1-st}+\frac{(1-r^{2})\left((1+st)^{2}-1\right)}{r}\quad\text{for}\quad|z|=r.

Now our objective is to determine the supremum of
F_{4}(r,t)
on
\Omega=\{(r,t):0<t\leq r<1\}
.
Differentiating partially
F_{4}(r,t)
with respect to
r
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial r}F_{4}(r,t)=-\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}}+1\right)\left((1+st)^{2}-1\right)<0.

Therefore,
F_{4}(r,t)
is a monotonically decreasing function of
r\in[t,1)
and it follows that
F_{4}(r,t)\leq F_{4}(t,t)=F_{5}(t)
,
where

\displaystyle F_{5}(t)=\frac{2s(1-t^{2})}{1-st}+\frac{(1-t^{2})\left((1+st)^{2}-1\right)}{t}.

(3.7)

Differentiate
F_{5}(t)
twice with respect to
t
, we obtain

\displaystyle F_{5}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-3s^{4}t^{4}+2s^{3}t^{3}+\left(s^{4}+7s^{2}\right)t^{2}-\left(2s^{3}+8s\right)t+3s^{2}}{(1-st)^{2}},

\displaystyle F_{5}^{\prime\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\left(3s^{5}t^{4}-7s^{4}t^{3}+3s^{3}t^{2}+3s^{2}t+2s^{3}-4s\right)}{(1-st)^{3}}.

Let

\displaystyle F_{6}(t)=3s^{5}t^{4}-7s^{4}t^{3}+3s^{3}t^{2}+3s^{2}t+2s^{3}-4s.

Differentiating
F_{6}(t)
with respect to
t
, we obtain

\displaystyle F_{6}^{\prime}(t)=3s^{2}\left(4s^{3}t^{3}-7s^{2}t^{2}+2st+1\right)=3s^{2}(1-st)^{2}(1+4st)>0.

Therefore,
F_{6}(t)
is a monotonically increasing function of
t\in[0,1)
and it follows that

\displaystyle F_{6}(t)\leq F_{6}(1)=3s^{5}-7s^{4}+3s^{3}+3s^{2}+2s^{3}-4s=-s(1-s)\left(3s^{3}-4s^{2}+s+4\right)<0.

Therefore,
F_{5}^{\prime\prime}(t)<0
and hence, we have
F_{5}^{\prime}(t)
is a monotonically decreasing function of
t
with
F_{5}^{\prime}(0)=3s^{2}
and
\lim_{t\to 1^{-}}F_{5}^{\prime}(t)=(-2s^{4}+10s^{2}-8s)/(1-s)^{2}=2s\left(s^{2}+s-4\right)/(1-s)<0
.
This leads us to conclude that the equation
F_{5}^{\prime}(t)=0
has the unique root
t_{s}
in
(0,1)
. This shows that
F_{5}(t)
attains its maximum at
t_{s}
. From (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), we have

\displaystyle\|P_{f}\|\leq F_{5}(t_{s})=\frac{2s(1-t_{s}^{2})}{1-st_{s}}+\frac{(1-t_{s}^{2})\left((1+st_{s})^{2}-1\right)}{t_{s}},

where
t_{s}\in(0,1)
is the unique positive root of the equation

\displaystyle G_{s}(t):=\frac{-3s^{4}t^{4}+2s^{3}t^{3}+\left(s^{4}+7s^{2}\right)t^{2}-\left(2s^{3}+8s\right)t+3s^{2}}{(1-st)^{2}}=0.

(3.8)

This completes the proof.
∎

In the following result, we establish the sharp estimate of the pre-Schwarzian norm for the functions in the class
\mathcal{C}_{hyp}
.

Theorem 3.3
.

For any
g\in\mathcal{C}_{hyp}
, the pre-Schwarzian norm satisfies the following sharp inequality

\displaystyle\|P_{g}\|\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\dfrac{(1+r_{s})(1-r_{s})^{1-s}-(1-r_{s}^{2})}{r_{s}}&\text{for}~0<s<1\\[5.69054pt]
2&\text{for}~s=1,\end{array}\right.

where
r_{s}\in(0,1)
is the unique root of the equation

\displaystyle\frac{(1-r)^{-s}\left(r^{2}(1-r)^{s}+r^{2}s-r^{2}+(1-r)^{s}+rs-1\right)}{r^{2}}=0.

Proof.

Let
g\in\mathcal{C}_{hyp}
, then by the definition of the class
\mathcal{C}_{hyp}
, we have

\displaystyle 1+\frac{zg^{\prime\prime}(z)}{g^{\prime}(z)}\prec\frac{1}{(1-z)^{s}}.

Thus, there exist a function
\omega(z)\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
such that

\displaystyle 1+\frac{zg^{\prime\prime}(z)}{g^{\prime}(z)}=\frac{1}{(1-\omega(z))^{s}}.

By a simple calculation, we have

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})|P_{g}(z)|=(1-|z|^{2})\left|\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(z)}{g^{\prime}(z)}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})\left|\frac{1}{z}\left(\frac{1}{(1-\omega(z))^{s}}-1\right)\right|.

Since
|\omega(z)|\leq|z|<1
and the branch of the logarithm is determined by
\log(1)=0
, thus, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-\omega(z))^{s}}=\mathrm{exp}(-s\log(1-\omega(z)))=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{s(s+1)\cdots(s+n-1)}{n!}\omega^{n}(z)\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}).

Thus, we have

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})|P_{g}(z)|\leq(1-|z|^{2})\frac{1}{|z|}\left(\frac{1}{(1-|\omega(z)|)^{s}}-1\right)\leq(1-|z|^{2})\frac{1}{|z|}\left(\frac{1}{(1-|z|)^{s}}-1\right).

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\|P_{g}\|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})|P_{g}(z)|\leq\sup_{0\leq|z|<1}F_{6}(|z|),

(3.10)

where

\displaystyle F_{6}(r)=\frac{(1-r^{2})}{r}\left(\frac{1}{(1-r)^{s}}-1\right)=\frac{(1+r)(1-r)^{1-s}-(1-r^{2})}{r}\quad\text{for}\quad|z|=r.

It is easy to see that for
s=1
, we have
F_{6}(r)=1+r
. Hence, we have
\|P_{g}\|\leq 2
. Now, we consider the case, where
0<s<1
.
A simple calculation gives

\displaystyle F_{6}^{\prime}(r)=\frac{(1-r)^{-s}\left(r^{2}(1-r)^{s}+r^{2}s-r^{2}+(1-r)^{s}+rs-1\right)}{r^{2}}

and

\displaystyle F_{6}^{\prime\prime}(r)=\frac{r^{3}s^{2}-r^{3}s+r^{2}s^{2}+r^{2}s+2r(1-r)^{s}-2(1-r)^{s}-2rs-2r+2}{(1-r)^{s+1}r^{3}}.

Let

\displaystyle F_{7}(r)=(1-r)^{-s-1}\left(r^{3}s^{2}-r^{3}s+r^{2}s^{2}+r^{2}s+2r(1-r)^{s}-2(1-r)^{s}-2rs-2r+2\right).

It is evident that
F_{6}^{\prime\prime}(r)=F_{7}(r)/r^{3}
and

\displaystyle F_{7}^{\prime}(r)=\frac{r^{2}(s-1)s(rs-2r+s+4)}{(1-r)^{s+2}}\leq 0\quad\text{for}\quad 0<r<1,~0<s<1.

Therefore,
F_{7}(r)
is a monotonically decreasing function of
r\in(0,1)
and it follows that
F_{7}(r)\leq\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}F_{7}(r)=0
,
i.e.,

F_{6}^{\prime\prime}(r)\leq 0
for
0<r<1
.
Thus,
F_{6}^{\prime}(r)
is a monotonically decreasing function in
r
with
\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}F_{6}^{\prime}(r)=s(s+1)/2
and
\lim_{r\to 1^{-}}F_{6}^{\prime}(r)=-\infty
.
Therefore, the equation
F_{6}^{\prime}(r)=0
has the unique root
r_{s}
in
(0,1)
, as illustrated in Figure
5
. Thus,
F_{6}(r)
attains its maximum value at
r=r_{s}
.
From (
3.10
), we have

\displaystyle\|P_{g}\|\leq\dfrac{(1+r_{s})(1-r_{s})^{1-s}-(1-r_{s}^{2})}{r_{s}},

where
r_{s}\in(0,1)
is the unique root of the equation

\displaystyle h_{s}(r):=\dfrac{(1-r)^{-s}\left(r^{2}(1-r)^{s}+r^{2}s-r^{2}+(1-r)^{s}+rs-1\right)}{r^{2}}=0.

(3.11)

To show that the estimate is sharp, we consider the function
f_{3}
given by

\displaystyle f_{2}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\mathrm{exp}\left(\int_{0}^{u}\frac{(1-t)^{-s}-1}{t}dt\right)du.

The pre-Schwarzian norm of

\displaystyle\|P_{f_{2}}\|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})|P_{f_{2}}(z)|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})\left|\frac{(1-z)^{-s}-1}{z}\right|.

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq r<1}(1-r^{2})\frac{(1-r)^{-s}-1}{r}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\dfrac{(1+r_{s})(1-r_{s})^{1-s}-(1-r_{s}^{2})}{r_{s}},&\text{when}~0<s<1\\[5.69054pt]
2,&\text{when}~s=1,\end{array}\right.

where
r_{s}\in(0,1)
is the unique root of the equation (
3.11

\displaystyle\|P_{f_{2}}\|=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\dfrac{(1+r_{s})(1-r_{s})^{1-s}-(1-r_{s}^{2})}{r_{s}},&\text{when}~0<s<1\\[5.69054pt]

This completes the proof.

In the following result, we establish the sharp estimate of the pre-Schwarzian norm for the functions in the class
\mathcal{C}_{L}
.

Theorem 3.4
.

For any
g\in\mathcal{C}_{L}
, the pre-Schwarzian norm satisfies the following sharp inequality

\displaystyle\|P_{g}\|\leq\frac{2\left(\sqrt{3s^{2}+4}+4\right)\left(3s^{2}+2\sqrt{3s^{2}+4}-4\right)}{27s}.

Proof.

Let
g\in\mathcal{C}_{L}
, then by the definition of the class
\mathcal{C}_{L}
, we have

\displaystyle 1+\frac{zg^{\prime\prime}(z)}{g^{\prime}(z)}\prec(1+sz)^{2}.

Thus, there exist a function
\omega(z)\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
such that

\displaystyle 1+\frac{zg^{\prime\prime}(z)}{g^{\prime}(z)}=(1+s~\omega(z))^{2},\quad\text{\it i.e.,}\quad\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(z)}{g^{\prime}(z)}=\frac{(1+s~\omega(z))^{2}-1}{z}.

As
|\omega(z)|\leq|z|<1
, we have

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})|P_{g}|=(1-|z|^{2})\left|\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(z)}{g^{\prime}(z)}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})\left|\frac{(1+s~\omega(z))^{2}-1}{z}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})\left(\frac{(1+s~|\omega(z)|)^{2}-1}{|z|}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(1-|z|^{2})\left((1+s|z|)^{2}-1\right)}{|z|}.

Therefore, the pre-Schwarzian norm for the function
g\in\mathcal{C}_{L}
is

\displaystyle\|P_{g}\|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})|P_{g}(z)|\leq\sup_{0\leq|z|<1}F_{8}(|z|),

(3.14)

where

\displaystyle F_{8}(r)=\frac{(1-r^{2})\left((1+sr)^{2}-1\right)}{r}\quad\mathrm{for}~|z|=r.

Differentiate twice
F_{8}(r)
with respect to
r
, we obtain

\displaystyle F_{8}^{\prime}(r)=s^{2}(1-3r^{2})-4rs\quad\text{and}\quad\quad F_{8}^{\prime\prime}(r)=-2\left(3rs^{2}+2s\right)<0.

Thus,
F_{8}^{\prime}(r)
is a monotonically decreasing function in
r
with
F_{8}^{\prime}(0)=s^{2}
and
F_{8}^{\prime}(1)=-2s^{2}-4s
.
Thus, the equation
F_{8}^{\prime}(r)=0
has the unique root
r_{0}=(-2+\sqrt{3s^{2}+4})/(3s)
in
(0,1)
. Therefore,
F_{8}(r)
attains its maximum value at
r=r_{0}
.
From (
3.14
), we have

\displaystyle\|P_{g}\|\leq\frac{2\left(\sqrt{3s^{2}+4}+4\right)\left(3s^{2}+2\sqrt{3s^{2}+4}-4\right)}{27s}.

To show that the estimate is sharp, let us consider the function
f_{4}
defined by

\displaystyle f_{3}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\mathrm{exp}\left(2st+\frac{s^{2}}{2}t^{2}\right)dt.

The pre-Schwarzian norm of
f_{3}
is given by

\displaystyle\|P_{f_{3}}\|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})|P_{f_{3}}(z)|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left|\frac{(1-|z|^{2})((1+sz)^{2}-1)}{z}\right|.

On the positive real axis, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq r<1}\left(\frac{(1-r^{2})((1+sr)^{2}-1)}{r}\right)=\frac{2\left(\sqrt{3s^{2}+4}+4\right)\left(3s^{2}+2\sqrt{3s^{2}+4}-4\right)}{27s}.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\|P_{f_{3}}\|=\frac{2\left(\sqrt{3s^{2}+4}+4\right)\left(3s^{2}+2\sqrt{3s^{2}+4}-4\right)}{27s}.

This completes the proof.
∎

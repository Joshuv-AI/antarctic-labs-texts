Derivative of the truncated singular value and eigen decomposition
Jan Naumann

Abstract
Recently developed applications in the field of machine learning and computational physics rely on automatic differentiation techniques, that require stable and efficient linear algebra gradient computations. This technical note provides a comprehensive and detailed discussion of the derivative of the truncated singular and eigenvalue decomposition. It summarizes previous work and builds on them with an extensive description of how to derive the relevant terms. A main focus is correctly expressing the derivative in terms of the truncated part, despite lacking knowledge of the full decomposition.



I
Introduction

In recent years the usage of automatic differentiation, originally emerged in the context of machine learning, to solve problems in quantum physics lead to the requirement of stable and efficient gradients of linear algebra operations.
The example in mind is the framework of variational optimization of two dimensional tensor networks – for a comprehensive review of the method see Ref.
Naumann et al. 2024
and the citations therein.
In this technical note a comprehensive discussion of the derivative of the truncated singular and eigen value decompostition (tSVD/tEVD) is presented.
This work aims to summarize the results already presented in the literature in a self-contained and detailed way. The aim of this work is to formulate the results in a form useful for a technical implementation in the context of automatic differentation frameworks as JAX
Bradbury et al. 2018
.
The discussion is mainly based on the work from Ref.
Wan and Zhang 2019
;
Wilde et al. 2022
;
Francuz et al. 2025
;
der Aa et al. 2007
.
Previous works usually assume that one has access to the full singular/eigen decomposition including all singular/eigen values and vectors.
However, as Francuz et al.
Francuz et al. 2025
pointed out, a correction to the derivative is necessary when only a truncated decomposition is computed and the derivative is obtained from the the kept part.
This note is structured in two main parts, one for the truncated singular value decomposition (tSVD), one for the truncated eigen decomposition (tEVD).
For the tSVD a detailed comparison of the two cases where first a full SVD is calculated and then truncated and second a iterative solver is used to only calculate the kept part is provided.
For the eigen decomposition only the second case is discussed since the other one can be easily derived from it.

II
Truncated singular value decomposition (SVD)

II.1
General definition

The full singular value decomposition (SVD) of a matrix
A\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times m}
is expressed as

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=U_{f}S_{f}V_{f}^{\dagger},

(1)

with
S_{f}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}
diagonal and positive semi-definite and
U_{f}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times r}
as well as
V_{f}^{\dagger}\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times m}
for
r=\min(n,m)
.
U_{f}
and
V_{f}
are unitaries if
n=m
, otherwise one matrix is unitary (
U_{f}
for
n<m
or
V_{f}
for
n>m
) and the other is semi-unitary with only the property
U_{f}^{\dagger}U_{f}
or
V_{f}^{\dagger}V_{f}=\mathbb{1}_{r}
remaining. Here
\mathbb{1}_{r}
denotes the identity matrix of size
r\times r
.

To truncate the singular values down to
t<r
values, we split the elements into the truncated space and its orthogonal counterpart also known as the discarded space. This can be written as block matrices of the form

\displaystyle U_{f}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}U&U_{\perp}\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle S_{f}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}S&0\\
\hline\cr 0&S_{\perp}\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle V^{\dagger}_{f}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}V^{\dagger}\\
\hline\cr V^{\dagger}_{\perp}\end{array}\right),

with
U\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times t}
,
U_{\perp}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times(r-t)}
,
S\in\mathbb{R}^{t\times t}
,
S_{\perp}\in\mathbb{R}^{(r-t)\times(r-t)}
,
V^{\dagger}\in\mathbb{C}^{t\times m}
and
V^{\dagger}_{\perp}\in\mathbb{C}^{(r-t)\times m}
.
Applying these relations to Eq. (
1
) we obtain easily the adapted relation

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=USV^{\dagger}+U_{\perp}S_{\perp}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}.

(7)

From the (orthonormality) properties of the full matrices we can obtain some relations for the block matrices as well,

\displaystyle U^{\dagger}U=V^{\dagger}V

\displaystyle=\mathbb{1}_{t},

\displaystyle U_{\perp}^{\dagger}U_{\perp}=V_{\perp}^{\dagger}V_{\perp}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{1}_{r-t},

(8)

\displaystyle U_{\perp}^{\dagger}U

\displaystyle=\mathbb{0}_{(r-t)\times t},

\displaystyle U^{\dagger}U_{\perp}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{0}_{t\times(r-t)},

(9)

\displaystyle V_{\perp}^{\dagger}V

\displaystyle=\mathbb{0}_{(r-t)\times t},

\displaystyle V^{\dagger}V_{\perp}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{0}_{t\times(r-t)},

(10)

\displaystyle U^{\dagger}U\oplus\mathbb{0}_{r-t}+\mathbb{0}_{t}\oplus U_{\perp}^{\dagger}U_{\perp}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{1}_{r},

\displaystyle V^{\dagger}V\oplus\mathbb{0}_{r-t}+\mathbb{0}_{t}\oplus V_{\perp}^{\dagger}V_{\perp}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{1}_{r},

(11)

with
\oplus
indicating the matrix direct sum (
A\oplus B=\operatorname{blockdiag}(A,B)
),
\mathbb{0}_{t}
the square matrix of size
t\times t
consisting only of zeros and
\mathbb{0}_{x\times y}
the matrix of size
x\times y
consiting only of zeros.
The last line can be compactly written as
\overline{U_{\perp}^{\dagger}U_{\perp}}=\mathbb{1}_{r}-\overline{U^{\dagger}U}
and
\overline{V_{\perp}^{\dagger}V_{\perp}}=\mathbb{1}_{r}-\overline{V^{\dagger}V}
, where we extend the matrices to the bigger vector space
\mathbb{C}^{r\times r}
in the way
\overline{U^{\dagger}U}\equiv U^{\dagger}U\oplus\mathbb{0}_{r-t}
and
\overline{U_{\perp}^{\dagger}U_{\perp}}\equiv\mathbb{0}_{t}\oplus U_{\perp}^{\dagger}U_{\perp}
(the relations for
V
and
V_{\perp}
are defined analogously).

Square case

In this section we assume that the original matrix
A
is square and thus
r=m=n
. Then we can use the (unitarity) property,
U_{f}U_{f}^{\dagger}=V_{f}V_{f}^{\dagger}=\mathbb{1}_{r}
.
Applying this to our split matrices we obtain

\displaystyle UU^{\dagger}+U_{\perp}U_{\perp}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{1}_{r},

\displaystyle VV^{\dagger}+V_{\perp}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{1}_{r}.

(12)

Non-square case

If our input matrix
A\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times m}
is not square, then either
U_{f}
or
V_{f}
is not unitary anymore, but only a semi-unitary.
For the discussion, we assume that
n>m
and thus
U_{f}
is only a semi-unitary as well as
r=m
.
The results for the opposite case
n<m
can be found analogously.
For the derivative calculation below, it will be necessary to extend
U_{f}
to a full unitary in the
\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
space.
To do so, we introduce another matrix
U_{\top}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times(n-m)}
whose columns are orthonormal vectors orthogonal to the (left-)singular vectors of
U_{f}
.
A full unitary
U_{f}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
can be expressed by (horizontally) stacking both matrices
U^{\prime}_{f}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}U_{f}&U_{\top}\end{array}\right)
.

For the tSVD, this modifies our block matrices for a complete ansatz to the following form,

\displaystyle\widetilde{U_{f}}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c|c}U&U_{\perp}&U_{\top}\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle\widetilde{S_{f}}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c|c}S&0&0\\
\hline\cr 0&S_{\perp}&0\\
\hline\cr 0&0&0\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle\widetilde{V_{f}}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c|c}V&V_{\perp}&0\end{array}\right).

We want to note that the orthonormal extension matrix
U_{\top}
obviously fulfills the orthogonality property to the other two parts as well,

\displaystyle U_{\top}^{\dagger}U_{\top}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{1}_{n-m},

\displaystyle U_{\top}^{\dagger}U

\displaystyle=\mathbb{0}_{(n-m)\times t},

\displaystyle U_{\top}^{\dagger}U_{\perp}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{0}_{(n-m)\times(r-t)}.

(18)

Since we have two unitaries again, we can find two completeness relations similar to the square case,

\displaystyle UU^{\dagger}+U_{\perp}U_{\perp}^{\dagger}+U_{\top}U_{\top}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{1}_{n},

\displaystyle VV^{\dagger}+V_{\perp}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{1}_{m}.

(19)

This relation also allows us to easily express
U_{\top}U_{\top}^{\dagger}
in terms of known matrices,

\displaystyle U_{\top}U_{\top}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{1}_{n}-UU^{\dagger}-U_{\perp}U_{\perp}^{\dagger}.

(20)

II.2
General derivative

From Eq. (
7
) we can obtain the differential of the SVD relation for the truncated case,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}A

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}USV^{\dagger}+U\mathrm{d}SV^{\dagger}+US\mathrm{d}V^{\dagger}+\mathrm{d}U_{\perp}S_{\perp}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}+U_{\perp}\mathrm{d}S_{\perp}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}+U_{\perp}S_{\perp}\mathrm{d}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}.

(21)

The properties from Eqs. (
8
,
9
,
10
) can be used to find the following orthogonality-preserving differential conditions relations

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}U^{\dagger}U+U^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}U

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}V^{\dagger}V+V^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}V

\displaystyle=0,

(22)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}U_{\perp}^{\dagger}U+U_{\perp}^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}U

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}U^{\dagger}U_{\perp}+U^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}U_{\perp}

\displaystyle=0,

(23)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}V+V_{\perp}^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}V

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}V^{\dagger}V_{\perp}+V^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}V_{\perp}

\displaystyle=0.

(24)

In the discussion, we distinguish between the square and the non-square case for the sake of clarity, since the latter requires a more thorough analysis.

II.3
Derivative for square case

Since we are interested in how the differentials
\mathrm{d}U
,
\mathrm{d}S
and
\mathrm{d}V
are modified by the truncation, we take a look at the
\mathrm{d}A
terms split in the both spaces by using Eq. (
12
),

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}A

\displaystyle=UU^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}A+(\mathbb{1}_{r}-UU^{\dagger})\mathrm{d}A=UU^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}A+U_{\perp}U_{\perp}^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}A,

(25)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}A

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}AVV^{\dagger}+\mathrm{d}A(\mathbb{1}_{r}-VV^{\dagger})=\mathrm{d}AVV^{\dagger}+\mathrm{d}AV_{\perp}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}.

(26)

Notice that we are just projecting with
UU^{\dagger}

\left(VV^{\dagger}\right)
onto the truncated space spanned by the left (right) singular basis vectors, respectively. This also involves using their orthogonal counterpart,
U_{\perp}{U_{\perp}}^{\dagger}

\left(V_{\perp}{V_{\perp}}^{\dagger}\right)
, which instead projects to the discarded space spanned by the left (right) singular basis vectors, respectively. Combining both previous equations yields

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}A

\displaystyle=UU^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}AVV^{\dagger}

(27a)

\displaystyle+U_{\perp}U_{\perp}^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}AV_{\perp}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}

(27b)

\displaystyle+UU^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}AV_{\perp}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}

(27c)

\displaystyle+U_{\perp}U_{\perp}^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}AVV^{\dagger}.

(27d)

The first two lines (
27a
) and (
27b
) correspond to the parts of the differential that only live in the truncated and the discarded space, respectively. The last two lines (
27c
) and (
27d
) contain the cross terms. We can now insert Eq. (
21
) into Eqs. (
27a
-
27d
), whereby we use the orthogonality properties from Eqs. (
9
,
10
),

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}A

\displaystyle=U\mathrm{d}SV^{\dagger}+UU^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}USV^{\dagger}+US\mathrm{d}V^{\dagger}VV^{\dagger}

(28a)

\displaystyle+U_{\perp}\mathrm{d}S_{\perp}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}+U_{\perp}U_{\perp}^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}U_{\perp}S_{\perp}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}+U_{\perp}S_{\perp}\mathrm{d}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}V_{\perp}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}

(28b)

\displaystyle+US\mathrm{d}V^{\dagger}V_{\perp}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}+UU^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}U_{\perp}S_{\perp}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}

(28c)

\displaystyle+U_{\perp}U_{\perp}^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}USV^{\dagger}+U_{\perp}S_{\perp}\mathrm{d}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}VV^{\dagger}.

(28d)

Applying the orthogonality-preserving differential relations from Eqs. (
23
,
24
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}A

\displaystyle=U\mathrm{d}SV^{\dagger}+UU^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}USV^{\dagger}+US\mathrm{d}V^{\dagger}VV^{\dagger}

(29a)

\displaystyle+U_{\perp}\mathrm{d}S_{\perp}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}+U_{\perp}U_{\perp}^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}U_{\perp}S_{\perp}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}+U_{\perp}S_{\perp}\mathrm{d}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}V_{\perp}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}

(29b)

\displaystyle+US\mathrm{d}V^{\dagger}V_{\perp}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}-U\mathrm{d}U^{\dagger}U_{\perp}S_{\perp}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}

(29c)

\displaystyle+U_{\perp}U_{\perp}^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}USV^{\dagger}-U_{\perp}S_{\perp}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}VV^{\dagger}.

(29d)

For compactness, we define the following notation

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{1}

\displaystyle=U^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}U,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{2}

\displaystyle=U_{\perp}^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}U,

(30)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{1}

\displaystyle=V^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}V,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{2}

\displaystyle=V_{\perp}^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}V.

(31)

This notation is very convenient, as it allows us to split
\mathrm{d}U
/
\mathrm{d}V
in the same way as we did previously for
\mathrm{d}A
in Eqs. (
25
,
26
),

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}U

\displaystyle=UU^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}U+U_{\perp}U_{\perp}^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}U=U\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{1}+U_{\perp}\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{2},

(32)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}V

\displaystyle=VV^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}V+V_{\perp}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}V=V\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{1}+V_{\perp}\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{2}.

(33)

Rewriting our last expression for
\mathrm{d}A
, Eq. (
29
), in terms of the compact notation, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}A

\displaystyle=U\mathrm{d}SV^{\dagger}+U\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{1}SV^{\dagger}+US\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{1}^{\dagger}V^{\dagger}

(34a)

\displaystyle+U_{\perp}\mathrm{d}S_{\perp}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}+U_{\perp}U_{\perp}^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}U_{\perp}S_{\perp}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}+U_{\perp}S_{\perp}\mathrm{d}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}V_{\perp}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}

(34b)

\displaystyle+US\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{2}^{\dagger}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}-U\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{2}^{\dagger}S_{\perp}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}

(34c)

\displaystyle+U_{\perp}\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{2}SV^{\dagger}-U_{\perp}S_{\perp}\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{2}V^{\dagger}.

(34d)

Our goal is now to determine
\mathrm{d}S
,
\mathrm{d}U
and
\mathrm{d}V
. As shown in Eqs. (
32
,
33
), the latter two Eqs. (
34c
,
34d
) can be described by determining
\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{1}
/
\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{2}
and
\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{1}
/
\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{2}
.
To further simplify the previous expression for
\mathrm{d}A
, it is convenient to compute the joint action of the (semi-)unitaries from left and right over
\mathrm{d}A
further simplifying with the orthogonality properties, which yields

\displaystyle U^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}AV

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}S+\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{1}S+S\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{1}^{\dagger},

(35)

\displaystyle U_{\perp}^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}AV_{\perp}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}S_{\perp}+U_{\perp}^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}U_{\perp}S_{\perp}+S_{\perp}\mathrm{d}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}V_{\perp},

(36)

\displaystyle U^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}AV_{\perp}

\displaystyle=S\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{2}^{\dagger}-\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{2}^{\dagger}S_{\perp},

(37)

\displaystyle U_{\perp}^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}AV

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{2}S-S_{\perp}\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{2}.

(38)

The second equation (
36
) only contains terms living supported in the discarded space, and thus are neglected in the following.

Calculation of
\mathrm{d}S
,
\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{1}
,
\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{1}

To determine the relation for
\mathrm{d}S
, we want to note that from Eq. (
22
) follows that
\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{1}
and
\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{1}
are anti-Hermitian, and thus have a diagonal with purely imaginary entries. This property can be used to calculate
\mathrm{d}S
by adding Eq. (
35
) and its Hermitian conjugate (note that
\mathrm{d}S
has to be real and diagonal), which yields

\displaystyle U^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}AV+V^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}A^{\dagger}U

\displaystyle=2\mathrm{d}S+\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{1}S-S\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{1}+S\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{1}^{\dagger}-\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{1}^{\dagger}S.

(39)

Restricting ourselves solely to the diagonal entries, we can fully determine dS as follows

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}S

\displaystyle=\mathbb{1}_{t}\circ\frac{1}{2}\left(U^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}AV+V^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}A^{\dagger}U\right),

(40)

where
\circ
denotes the Hadamard or element-wise product of matrices, filtering out only the diagonal entries.
Considering now the off-diagonal parts
U^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}AV
leads to an expression for the calculation of the off-diagonal elements of
\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{1}
and
\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{1}
,

\displaystyle\left(U^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}AV+V^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}A^{\dagger}U\right)_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle=(\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{1})_{i,j}S_{j,j}-S_{i,i}(\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{1})_{i,j}+S_{i,i}(\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{1}^{\dagger})_{i,j}-(\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{1}^{\dagger})_{i,j}S_{j,j}.

(41)

By appropriately multiplying by
S
on Eq. (
41
) (for clearness marked by a bold
\boldsymbol{S}
and square symbol
2
), we can obtain the following relations for the off-diagonal elements of
\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{1}
and
\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{1}
,

\displaystyle\left(U^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}AV\boldsymbol{S}+\boldsymbol{S}V^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}A^{\dagger}U\right)_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle=(\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{1})_{i,j}S^{\boldsymbol{2}}_{j,j}-S^{\boldsymbol{2}}_{i,i}(\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{1})_{i,j},

(42)

\displaystyle\left(\boldsymbol{S}U^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}AV+V^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}A^{\dagger}U\boldsymbol{S}\right)_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle=S^{\boldsymbol{2}}_{i,i}(\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{1}^{\dagger})_{i,j}-(\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{1}^{\dagger})_{i,j}S^{\boldsymbol{2}}_{j,j},

(43)

where we have used the anti-Hermiticity of
\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{1}
, i.e.,
\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{1}^{\dagger}=-\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{1}
.
Assuming now that we have no degenerate singular values, we can define a matrix
F\in\mathbb{R}^{t\times t}
with
F_{i,j}=1/(S^{2}_{j,j}-S^{2}_{i,i})
for
i\neq j
and zero on the diagonal. Using this matrix, we can rewrite the result for the off-diagonal entries of our differentials as

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{1}}_{t}\circ\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{1}

\displaystyle=F\circ\left(U^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}AVS+SV^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}A^{\dagger}U\right),

(44)

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{1}}_{t}\circ\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{1}

\displaystyle=F\circ\left(SU^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}AV+V^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}A^{\dagger}US\right),

(45)

where
\bar{\mathbb{1}}_{t}
denotes the square matrix of size
t\times t
containing ones in all off-diagonal entries, and zeros along the diagonal.

Looking at the anti-Hermitian part of Eq. (
35
); i.e., subtracting its corresponding hermitian conjugate version from itself, yields

\displaystyle U^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}AV-V^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}A^{\dagger}U

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{1}S+S\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{1}+S\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{1}^{\dagger}+\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{1}^{\dagger}S.

(46)

Now we can solve for the diagonal part of
\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{1}+\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{1}
, obtaining

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\mathbb{1}_{t}\circ\left(\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{1}+\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{1}^{\dagger}\right)

\displaystyle=S^{-1}\circ\frac{1}{2}\left(U^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}AV-V^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}A^{\dagger}U\right).

(47)

As discussed in Ref.
Wilde et al. 2022
;
Francuz et al. 2025
, any solution of this sum is enough to reconstruct
\mathrm{d}A
, so we can just add the term to either of the differentials or split it between both. Wilde et al.
Wilde et al. 2022
suggested that splitting the term in half yield to smallest rounding error in their numerical analysis.

Calculation of
\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{2}
,
\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{2}

A similar approach as for the calculation of the off-diagonal elements above can be used to obtain
\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{2}
and
\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{2}
. Let us take a look at the sum of the hermitian conjugate of Eq. (
37
) and Eq. (
38
),

\displaystyle U_{\perp}^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}AV+V_{\perp}^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}A^{\dagger}U

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{2}S-S_{\perp}\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{2}+\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{2}S-S_{\perp}\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{2}.

(48)

Again, we can modify the equation by multiply
S
and
S_{\perp}
from the left and the right as indicted by bold symbols,

\displaystyle U_{\perp}^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}AV\boldsymbol{S}+\boldsymbol{S_{\perp}}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}A^{\dagger}U

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{2}S^{\boldsymbol{2}}-S^{\boldsymbol{2}}_{\perp}\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{2},

(49)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{S_{\perp}}U_{\perp}^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}AV+V_{\perp}^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}A^{\dagger}U\boldsymbol{S}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{2}S^{\boldsymbol{2}}-S^{\boldsymbol{2}}_{\perp}\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{2}.

(50)

Defining a matrix
G\in\mathbb{R}^{(r-t)\times t}
as
G_{i,j}=1/(S^{2}_{j,j}-S^{2}_{\perp_{i,i}})
, and zero elsewhere, both previous equations can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{2}

\displaystyle=G\circ\left(U_{\perp}^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}AVS+S_{\perp}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}A^{\dagger}U\right),

(51)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{2}

\displaystyle=G\circ\left(S_{\perp}U_{\perp}^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}AV+V_{\perp}^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}A^{\dagger}US\right).

(52)

Summary of the results for square case

To summarize the results for the square case, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}S

\displaystyle=\mathbb{1}_{t}\circ\frac{1}{2}\left(U^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}AV+V^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}A^{\dagger}U\right),

(53)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}U

\displaystyle=U\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{1}+U_{\perp}\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{2},

(54)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}V

\displaystyle=V\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{1}+V_{\perp}\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{2},

(55)

with the terms

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{1}

\displaystyle=F\circ\left(U^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}AVS+SV^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}A^{\dagger}U\right)+\frac{1}{2}S^{-1}\circ\frac{1}{2}\left(U^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}AV-V^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}A^{\dagger}U\right),

(56)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{d}V}_{1}

\displaystyle=F\circ\left(SU^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}AV+V^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}A^{\dagger}US\right)+\frac{1}{2}S^{-1}\circ\frac{1}{2}\left(U^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}AV-V^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}A^{\dagger}U\right),

(57)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{d}U}_{2}

\displaystyle=G\circ\left(U_{\perp}^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}AVS+S_{\perp}V_{\perp}^{\dagger}\mathrm{d}A^{\dagger}U\right),

(58)

III
Trunctated eigenvalue decomposition (EVD)

The full eigenvalue decomposition of a general square matrix
A\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
given in terms of a right eigenvector matrix
X\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
and its corresponding diagonal eigenvalue matrix
\Lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
can be stated as

\displaystyle AX

\displaystyle=X\Lambda.

(122)

We shall denote by
Y\equiv X^{-1}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
the inverse of the eigenvector matrix
X
for brevity. However, we occasionally write
X^{-1}
explicitly when we deem it necessary to highlight specific points.

For the truncation of the eigenvalue decomposition into
p
kept and
n-p
discarded values we split the eigenvalues and eigenvectors into two blocks
\lambda/x/y
and
\lambda_{\perp}/x_{\perp}/y_{\perp}
such that

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}x&x_{\perp}\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle\Lambda

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}\lambda&0\\
\hline\cr 0&\lambda_{\perp}\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}y\\
\hline\cr y_{\perp}\end{array}\right),

with
x\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times p}
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{p\times p}
diagonal,
y\in\mathbb{C}^{p\times n}
,
x_{\perp}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times(n-p)}
,
\lambda_{\perp}\in\mathbb{C}^{(n-p)\times(n-p)}
diagonal and
y_{\perp}\in\mathbb{C}^{(n-p)\times n}
.
Furthermore, we can find from the relation
XY=YX=\mathbb{1}_{n}
the following equations:

\displaystyle yx

\displaystyle=\mathbb{1}_{p},

\displaystyle y_{\perp}x_{\perp}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{1}_{n-p},

\displaystyle y_{\perp}x

\displaystyle=yx_{\perp}=0,

\displaystyle xy+x_{\perp}y_{\perp}=\mathbb{1}_{n},

(128)

and for the eigenvalue relation

\displaystyle Ax

\displaystyle=x\lambda,

\displaystyle Ax_{\perp}

\displaystyle=x_{\perp}\lambda_{\perp},

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=x\lambda y+x_{\perp}\lambda_{\perp}y_{\perp}.

(129)

III.1
Full derivative

The derivative of the full eigenvalue relation is

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}AX+A\mathrm{d}X

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}X\Lambda+X\mathrm{d}\Lambda.

(130)

Premultipling by
X^{-1}
and substitute
X^{-1}A=\Lambda X^{-1}
yields

\displaystyle X^{-1}\mathrm{d}AX-\mathrm{d}\Lambda

\displaystyle=X^{-1}\mathrm{d}X\Lambda-\Lambda X^{-1}\mathrm{d}X.

(131)

As discussed in Ref.
der Aa et al. 2007
, there is a gauge freedom in the eigenvalue decomposition such that the decomposition can be transformed by a gauge fixing matrix
\Gamma
. If we assume that we already have a set of eigenvectors
\bar{X}
(for example calculated by LAPACK), we can relate both sets of eigenvectors by
\Gamma
,

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=\bar{X}\Gamma,

\displaystyle\Lambda\Gamma

\displaystyle=\Gamma\Lambda.

(132)

With this relation we can write the derivative in terms of the known eigenvectors
\bar{X}
,

\displaystyle\Gamma^{-1}\bar{X}^{-1}\mathrm{d}A\bar{X}\Gamma-\mathrm{d}\Lambda

\displaystyle=\Gamma^{-1}\bar{X}^{-1}\mathrm{d}X\Lambda-\Lambda\Gamma^{-1}\bar{X}^{-1}\mathrm{d}X,

(133)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\bar{X}^{-1}\mathrm{d}A\bar{X}\Gamma-\Gamma\mathrm{d}\Lambda

\displaystyle=\bar{X}^{-1}\mathrm{d}X\Lambda-\Lambda\bar{X}^{-1}\mathrm{d}X.

(134)

In the following we want derive the derivative of the truncated eigenvalue decomposition from Eq. (
129
) starting from the general relation.

III.2
Derivative for truncated EVD with non-degenerate eigenvalues

In this section we assume that there are no degenerate eigenvalue and therefore the gauge-fixing matrix
\Gamma
to be a diagonal matrix.

Applying our notation of the split up parts for the derivative, we obtain the four equations

\displaystyle\bar{y}\mathrm{d}A\bar{x}\gamma-\gamma\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=\bar{y}\mathrm{d}x\lambda-\lambda\bar{y}\mathrm{d}x,

(135)

\displaystyle\bar{y}_{\perp}\mathrm{d}A\bar{x}\gamma

\displaystyle=\bar{y}_{\perp}\mathrm{d}x\lambda-\lambda_{\perp}\bar{y}_{\perp}\mathrm{d}x,

(136)

\displaystyle\bar{y}\mathrm{d}A\bar{x}_{\perp}\gamma_{\perp}

\displaystyle=\bar{y}\mathrm{d}x_{\perp}\lambda_{\perp}-\lambda\bar{y}\mathrm{d}x_{\perp},

(137)

\displaystyle\bar{y}_{\perp}\mathrm{d}A\bar{x}_{\perp}\gamma_{\perp}-\gamma_{\perp}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{\perp}

\displaystyle=\bar{y}_{\perp}\mathrm{d}x_{\perp}\lambda_{\perp}-\lambda_{\perp}\bar{y}_{\perp}\mathrm{d}x_{\perp}.

(138)

Since the last two equations are only related to the derivatives in the part we truncate, we are only interested in the first two equations. From the first equation we can directly obtain the result for the derivative of the eigenvalues, since this one is restricted to having only diagonal non-zero values. When we look at the diagonal elements of the first equation we observe that the right-hand side cancels out, thus finding

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=\mathbb{1}_{p}\circ(\bar{y}\mathrm{d}A\bar{x}).

(139)

The task at hand now is to determine
\mathrm{d}x
. As before for the truncated SVD, we split the problem up into two parts,
\mathrm{d}x=\bar{x}\bar{y}\mathrm{d}x+(\mathbb{1}_{n}-\bar{x}\bar{y})\mathrm{d}x=\bar{x}\widetilde{\mathrm{d}x}_{1}+\widetilde{\mathrm{d}x}_{2}
. The first part can be determined from Eq. (
135
), the second one from Eq. (
136
).
For the first part
\widetilde{\mathrm{d}x}_{1}=\bar{y}\mathrm{d}x
, we can obtain the off-diagonal elements from Eq. (
135
),

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{1}}_{p}\circ\widetilde{\mathrm{d}x}_{1}

\displaystyle=F\circ\left(\bar{y}\mathrm{d}A\bar{x}\gamma\right),

(140)

with
\bar{\mathbb{1}}
being the matrix consisting of ones everywhere except for the zero-valued diagonal and
F\in\mathbb{C}^{p\times p}
a matrix with elements
F_{i,j}=1/(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i})
for
i\neq j
and zero on the diagonal.
The diagonal elements are dependent on the gauge-fixing we choose. We assume that we set an
m
-th element of the
k
-th eigenvector to be
1
(with
m
not necessary the same for all eigenvectors) such that
\gamma_{k}=\frac{1}{x_{mk}}
. For details we want to point to Ref.
der Aa et al. 2007
where the reason how to select the values of
m
is discussed. Summarized, there are several methods such as choosing the element with largest magnitude of
x_{k}
(LAPACK default) or the largest product of the absolute values of
x
and
y
such that
|x_{mk}||y_{km}|=\max_{i}|x_{ik}||y_{ki}|

der Aa et al. 2007
. However, since we fix this value, we know that
\mathrm{d}x_{mk}=0
. This can be used to express the diagonal element
\widetilde{\mathrm{d}x}_{1_{kk}}
in terms of the off-diagonal ones,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}x_{mk}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l}\bar{x}_{ml}\widetilde{\mathrm{d}x}_{1_{lk}}+\widetilde{\mathrm{d}x}_{2_{mk}}=0,

(141)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\widetilde{\mathrm{d}x}_{1_{kk}}

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{1}{\bar{x}_{mk}}\sum_{l\neq k}\bar{x}_{ml}\widetilde{\mathrm{d}x}_{1_{lk}}+\widetilde{\mathrm{d}x}_{2_{mk}}\right)

(142)

To determine the second part, we have to remove the dependency on the
\perp
elements in Eq. (
136
). To this end, we can use, after multiplication with
\bar{x}_{\perp}
from the left side, the relations we found in Eqs. (
128
) and (
129
),

\displaystyle(\mathbb{1}_{n}-\bar{x}\bar{y})\mathrm{d}A\bar{x}\gamma

\displaystyle=(\mathbb{1}_{n}-\bar{x}\bar{y})\mathrm{d}x\lambda-(A-\bar{x}\lambda\bar{y})\mathrm{d}x.

(143)

With the relation
A\bar{x}\bar{y}=\bar{x}\lambda\bar{y}
we can obtain a Sylvester equation for
\widetilde{\mathrm{d}x}_{2}=(\mathbb{1}_{n}-\bar{x}\bar{y})\mathrm{d}x
,

\displaystyle(\mathbb{1}_{n}-\bar{x}\bar{y})\mathrm{d}A\bar{x}\gamma

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\mathrm{d}x}_{2}\lambda-A\widetilde{\mathrm{d}x}_{2}.

(144)

After solving this equation numerically, we have all components together for the full eigenvector derivative.

III.3
Derivative for degenerate eigenvalues

In Ref.
der Aa et al. 2007
the derivative for the case of degenerate eigenvalues has been discussed as well, but this requires the knowledge of the second differential
\mathrm{d}^{2}A
for a unique solution. Since this object is not generally available in numerical implementation of automatic differentiation frameworks, the discussion of this case is beyond scope of this work.

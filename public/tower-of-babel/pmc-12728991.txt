Upregulation of lncRNA SSTR5-AS1 promotes osteoblast differentiation and reduces apoptosis
Authors: Zhuan Zuo, Shiwen Li, Bo Gao, Chenchen Zhao, Yongsheng Ma
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveOsteoporotic fractures (OPF) and delayed fracture healing (DFH) pose major health challenges for the elderly. The lncRNA SSTR5-AS1 shows abnormal expression in people with low BMD. Understanding how SSTR5-AS1 regulates fracture healing could help address DFH.MethodsThis study detected the expression levels of SSTR5-AS1, miR-193b-3p, osteogenic markers (RUNX2, ALP, BSP, OCN, OPN) mRNA, and apoptosis-related genes (Bcl-2, Bax, Bad) in osteoblasts and serum samples from all participants using RT-qPCR. Logistic regression was used to identify the independent factors influencing fracture healing. Cell proliferation and apoptosis were assessed using CCK-8 assays and flow cytometry, respectively. The binding relationship between SSTR5-AS1 and miR-193b-3p was validated through dual-luciferase reporter assays.ResultsSSTR5-AS1 is significantly downregulated in patients with OPF and DFH and negatively regulates the expression level of miR-193b-3p. SSTR5-AS1 is a protective factor for fracture healing. Elevating SSTR5-AS1 levels significantly reduces miR-193b-3p expression, thereby decreasing osteoblast apoptosis, increasing Bcl-2 expression, and decreasing Bax and Bad levels. Furthermore, enhancing SSTR5-AS1 promotes osteoblast proliferation, upregulates RUNX2, ALP, BSP, OCN, and OPN expression, and facilitates osteoblast differentiation.ConclusionSSTR5-AS1 demonstrates significant diagnostic value for OPF and DFH, making it a promising biomarker for these conditions. When overexpressed, SSTR5-AS1 reduces miR-193b-3p levels, promoting osteoblast proliferation and differentiation. This may accelerate fracture healing.

Introduction

Osteoporotic fractures (OPF) are among the most severe complications of osteoporosis, commonly occurring in elderly populations, particularly postmenopausal women [1, 2]. As the population ages, OPF incidence is rising significantly, imposing a heavy burden on patients’ families and healthcare systems [3]. Patients with OPF not only face a significantly elevated fracture risk but also encounter impaired fracture healing (FH) capacity [4, 5]. A substantial proportion experience delayed healing or even nonunion [6], severely impacting quality of life and increasing healthcare costs. Effective clinical treatments for delayed OPF healing remain scarce. Traditional interventions primarily focus on osteoporosis medications and surgical fixation, yet their efficacy is limited. In recent years, researchers have focused on elucidating the molecular regulatory networks governing OPF healing [7, 8], which may enable targeted clinical therapies for delayed healing. Therefore, unraveling the molecular mechanisms underlying delayed OPF healing and identifying novel targets for early diagnosis and enhanced healing carries substantial clinical and scientific value.

Fracture healing represents a critical phase for patients with OPF. Numerous factors influence the rate of FH [9–11]. During FH, osteoblast differentiation is a critical process [12]. Several markers indicate stages of osteoblast differentiation: Runt-related transcription factor-2 (RUNX2) marks the initiation [13]; alkaline phosphatase (ALP) indicates early differentiation [14]; bone sialoprotein (BSP) and osteopontin (OPN) signify matrix maturation and the onset of mineralization [15]; while osteocalcin (OCN) marks the completion of mineralization and the maturation of bone tissue [16]. Recent studies indicate that long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs) can regulate the expression of markers such as RUNX2, ALP, OCN, BSP, and OPN through multiple mechanisms, including gene expression modulation and acting as competing endogenouse RNAs (ceRNAs) [17]. This further involves them in crucial biological processes like osteoblastic differentiation and bone remodeling during FH. The SSTR5-AS1 has been found to exhibit lower expression in individuals with low bone mineral density (BMD) [18]. However, its expression and mechanism of action in OPF remain unclear. Additionally, miRNAs also play a significant role in FH [19]. Notably, bioinformatics analysis suggests SSTR5-AS1 may interact with miR-193b-3p via sponge adsorption. miR-193b-3p has been demonstrated to be highly expressed in elderly osteoporosis patients [20]. The regulatory relationship between SSTR5-AS1 and miR-193b-3p during OPF healing requires further investigation.

Building on this research background, this study investigates the expression characteristics of SSTR5-AS1 in patients with OPF and its clinical diagnostic value. It also explores the regulatory function and molecular mechanisms of SSTR5-AS1 in the FH process. Through clinical sample analysis and cellular functional experiments, we evaluate the role of the SSTR5-AS1/miR-193b-3p axis in OPF occurrence and progression. This research provides novel molecular biomarkers for early OPF diagnosis and offers theoretical and experimental support for developing RNA-targeted therapeutic strategies to promote FH.

Materials and methods

Inclusion of patients

A total of 118 patients with osteoporotic fractures who visited the First Affiliated Hospital of Harbin Medical University between May 2023 and August 2024 were enrolled. Inclusion criteria: (a) met the diagnostic criteria for osteoporotic fractures and underwent internal fixation surgery; (b) had complete clinical data. Exclusion criteria: (a) Patients with concomitant bone metabolic disorders; (b) Patients with renal disease; (c) Patients with heart failure or autoimmune diseases. Fasting venous blood samples were collected from all patients at the time of admission for subsequent laboratory testing. Additionally, we enrolled 110 healthy individuals undergoing physical examinations at our hospital during the same period as controls. Clinical data were collected from patients and healthy participants. BMD is measured using dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry. Based on healing duration in Patients with OPF, they were categorized into the normal healing group (NFH) and the delayed healing group (DFH, healing duration exceeding 4 months).

All subjects and their families provided informed consent The Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Harbin Medical University. The experimental procedures complied with the regulations of the Ethics Committee and the Declaration of Helsinki.

Cell culture and transfected

hFOB 1.19 cells were purchased from the Chinese Academy of Sciences Cell Bank (Shanghai, China). Cells were cultured in DMEM medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% antibiotic–antimycotic solution at 37 °C and 5% CO2. Transfection was performed when cells reached logarithmic growth phase at 60–70% confluence.

RiboBio (Guangzhou, China) designed and constructed the oe-SSTR5-AS1 overexpression plasmid with its empty vector control (oe-NC), along with SSTR5-AS1-specific siRNA (si-SSTR5-AS1) and negative control siRNA (si-NC). hFOB 1.19 cells were seeded in 24-well plates. Following the Lipofectamine™ 3000 transfection reagent protocol, 500 ng of plasmid or siRNA at a final concentration of 50 nM was transfected into hFOB 1.19 cells. Additionally, mimics NC and miR-193b-3p mimics were co-transfected with oe-NC and oe-SSTR5-AS1. Cells were harvested 48 h post-transfection for subsequent analysis.

Real-time quantitative reverse transcription PCR (RT-qPCR)

Serum was collected from patient venous blood samples by centrifugation. Following transfection, hFOB 1.19 cells were harvested from each group. One milliliter of Trizol reagent was added to both serum and cell samples, which were then incubated at room temperature for 5 min to ensure complete lysis. Chloroform was added to induce phase separation. The colorless aqueous layer was transferred to a new Eppendorf tube and mixed with an equal volume of isopropanol. After inverting and mixing thoroughly, samples were left to stand for 10 min. The supernatant was discarded, leaving total RNA as the precipitate Genomic DNA was removed according to the reverse transcription kit instructions, with incubation at 42 °C for 2 min. The reverses transcription reaction mixture was then added directly to the tube, bringing the total volume to 20 μL. Reaction conditions: 37 °C for 15 min, followed by 85 °C for 5 s. The resulting cDNA was stored at − 20 °C for later use. Detection was performed using TB Green® Premix Ex Taq™ II (Tli RNaseH Plus) (TaKaRa, Japan) on a QuantStudio™ 5 Real-Time Fluorescent Quantitative PCR System (Applied Biosystems, USA). GAPDH was used for normalization of SSTR5-AS1 and all mRNAs, while U6 was used for normalization of miR-193b-3p. Each sample included three technical replicates. Relative gene expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method.

Cell proliferation assay

hFOB 1.19 cells in the logarithmic growth phase were digested with trypsin, and resuspended at a density of 1 × 105 cells/mL. Experimental groups were established based on cell transfection, with 5 replicate wells per group. The control group contained untransfected hFOB 1.19 cells, while the blank group received only 100 μL of complete medium without cells. Each experimental group was seeded into three 96-well plates. The plates were incubated at 37 °C for 0, 24, 48, and 72 h. The 0-h value represented the absorbance measured before incubation. After each incubation period, 10 μL of CCK-8 solution was added to each well, and the plates were returned to the incubator for 1 h. Absorbance at 450 nm was then measured using a microplate reader.

Cell apoptosis assay

Collect transfected cells into centrifuge tubes and centrifuge at 300 g for 5 min at 4 °C. Discard the supernatant and resuspend the cell pellet in pre-chilled PBS for washing. Centrifuge again under the same conditions and discard the supernatant. Resuspend the cells in 100 μL of 1 × Binding Buffer. Add 5 μL each of Annexin V-FITC and PI to the cell suspension, then gently mix. Incubate at room temperature in the dark for 15 min. After incubation, add 400 μL of 1 × Binding Buffer to dilute the sample. Perform instrument analysis within 1 h.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay (DLR)

The lncRNA SNP2 database predicted a binding site between SSTR5-AS1 and miR-193b-3p. Based on this predicted binding site, we constructed wild-type sequences (WT-SSTR5-AS1) and mutant sequences (MUT-SSTR5-AS1) containing the miR-193b-3p binding site. Transfection was performed using the Lipofectamine™ 3000 Transfection Reagent Kit (Thermofisher, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Recombinant plasmids were co-transfected into cells with NC mimic, NC inhibitor, miR-193b-3p mimic, and miR-193b-3p inhibitor, then incubated in a cell culture incubator. After 48 h, cells were lysed according to the DLR assay kit instructions, and luciferase activity was measured.

Bioinformatics analysis

We screened downstream target genes of miR-193b-3p using three databases: miRDB, miRWalk, and TargetScan. We selected target genes that overlapped across all three databases. These genes were then analyzed using Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) to identify their biological functions and signaling pathways. We selected the top ten results with P < 0.05 were selected for further analysis.

Statistical analysis

All experiments were independently repeated at least three times. Data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and analyzed using GraphPad Prism 9.0 statistical software. Comparisons between two groups were conducted using Student’s t test, while comparisons among multiple groups were performed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). ROC curve analysis evaluated the diagnostic value of SSTR5-AS1 and miR-193b-3p. Logistic regression analysis identified independent factors influencing fracture healing. Pearson correlation analysis examined the expression correlation between SSTR5-AS1 and miR-193b-3p. Data from the dual luciferase reporter assay were analyzed using two-way ANOVA to assess interactions between groups. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05, P < 0.01, and P < 0.001.

Factors affecting the rate of fracture healing

Patients were divided into NFH and DFH groups based on healing speed. Clinical data comparison revealed significant differences in SSTR5-AS1 expression, BMD, and T-scores between groups, while other aspects showed no significant differences (Table 2). Serum SSTR5-AS1 levels were notably lower in DFH patients compared to NFH patients (Fig. 2A). ROC analysis evaluated the diagnostic value of SSTR5-AS1 for DFH. showing an AUC of 0.856 (Fig. 2B). Logistic regression analysis revealed that SSTR5-AS1 (OR 0.071, 95%CI0.022–0.231), BMD (OR 3.060, 95%CI1.057–8.859), and T-score (OR 2.897, 95%CI1.065–7.881) were independent factors influencing fracture healing (Fig. 2C). Notably, the OR value for SSTR5-AS1 was less than 1, while those for BMD and T-score were both greater than 1. This indicates that SSTR5-AS1 is a protective factor for FH, whereas BMD and T-score are risk factors (Fig. 2C).

The SSTR5-AS1 level affects osteoblast growth and differentiation

Transfection with oe-SSTR5-AS1 significantly increased SSTR5-AS1 levels in cells. Conversely, transfection with si-SSTR5-AS1 reduced SSTR5-AS1 expression (Fig. 3A). Elevated SSTR5-AS1 expression decreased the apoptosis rate in osteoblasts and increased the anti-apoptotic gene Bcl-2, while the pro-apoptotic genes Bax and Bad showed reduced expression. SSTR5-AS1 knockdown increased the apoptosis rate and decreased Bcl-2 expression, while Bax and Bad exhibited the opposite trend (Fig. 3B–C). Elevating SSTR5-AS1 levels in cells notably enhanced proliferation capacity; conversely, reducing SSTR5-AS1 levels markedly decreased proliferation (Fig. 3D).

Our findings indicate that SSTR5-AS1 levels influence the expression of osteoblast differentiation markers. Elevated SSTR5-AS1 levels markedly increased the expression of RUNX2, ALP, BSP, OPN, and OCN, indicating that SSTR5-AS1 promotes osteoblast differentiation. Conversely, silencing SSTR5-AS1 reduced the levels of these markers in cells (Fig. 3E–F).

SSTR5-AS1 and miR-193b-3p jointly regulate osteoblast growth and differentiation

Transfection with oe-SSTR5-AS1 significantly reduced miR-193b-3p levels (Fig. 4A). This reduction markedly decreased the apoptosis rate markedly decreased (Fig. 4B) and significantly elevated levels of the anti-apoptotic protein Bcl-2, while levels of the pro-apoptotic proteins Bax and Bad were markedly reduced (Fig. 4C). Cellular proliferation capacity also increased with reduced miR-193b-3p expression (Fig. 4D). In addition, expression levels of RUNX2, ALP, BSP, OPN, and OCN were markedly elevated (Fig. 4E–F). However, transfection with a miR-193b-3p mimic significantly reversed these changes. These findings indicate that modulating SSTR5-AS1 levels can regulate miR-193b-3p level, thereby influencing osteoblast growth and differentiation capacity of osteoblasts.

Downstream target genes of miR-193b-3p and involved signaling pathways

We identified 108 overlapping downstream target genes of miR-193b-3p by screening three databases (Fig. 5A). GO database analysis showed that biological processes were enriched primarily in histone H3-K4 trimethylation, cellular components in glutamatergic synapse and cell–cell junction, and molecular functions in modification-dependent protein binding and cytoskeletal anchor activity (Fig. 5B). KEGG analysis showed that the PI3K-Akt and Ras signaling pathways were most enriched among these 108 target genes (Fig. 5C).

Discussion

Osteoporotic fractures (OPF) and their delayed healing represent significant health challenges for the elderly population [21]. Understanding their molecular regulatory mechanisms is crucial for developing novel diagnostic and therapeutic strategies. This study first reveals the expression profile of the lncRNA SSTR5-AS1 in patients with OPF and its potential role in the FH process. Our findings indicate that SSTR5-AS1 levels are significantly downregulated in the peripheral blood of patients with OPF. ROC curve analysis demonstrated that SSTR5-AS1 achieved a diagnostic AUC of 0.914 (95% CI 0.879–0.949) for OPF, with a sensitivity of 87.3% and specificity of 80.9%. This diagnostic performance surpasses the QCT value for OPF diagnosis reported by Yin et al. (AUC: 0.6717) [22]. These findings suggest that SSTR5-AS1 may serve as a novel circulating biomarker to address the current limitations in early OPF diagnosis.

LncRNAs influence fracture healing by regulating osteoblast differentiation [23]. For example, Chen et al. confirmed that lncRNA H19 affects osteoblast proliferation and differentiation [24], while Li et al. reported that LINC00339 overexpression reduces osteogenic marker mRNA levels [25]. Functional experiments revealed that SSTR5-AS1 modulates fracture healing by regulating key osteoblast differentiation markers. Following SSTR5-AS1 overexpression, expression of the early osteogenic marker ALP increased approximately twofold (p < 0.001), signaling the transition from cartilaginous callus to bony callus stage [26]. Concurrently, BSP, a key molecule initiating core formation during mineralization [27], also significantly increased with elevated SSTR5-AS1 expression levels. The late differentiation marker OCN showed marked upregulation as well. These findings align with Wu et al. discovery that lncRNA TUG1 promotes osteogenic differentiation of human periodontal ligament stem cells by regulating the Smad2/7 pathway [28]. Furthermore, our study revealed that elevated SSTR5-AS1 levels significantly increased the expression of the transcription factor RUNX2. As a “master regulator” of osteogenic differentiation, the upregulation of RUNX2 directly promotes the expression of mineralization-related genes such as OPN, accelerating the maturation and mineralization of the extracellular matrix [29]. These results indicate that elevating SSTR5-AS1 levels enhances osteoblast differentiation and mineralization, benefiting fracture healing.

Existing reports indicate that SSTR5-AS1 significantly influences cancer cell growth and apoptosis [30–32]. Overexpression of SSTR5-AS1 promotes malignant behavior in ovarian cancer cells [33]. Building on these findings, this study also investigated the effects of SSTR5-AS1 levels on osteoblast proliferation and apoptosis. Results showed that SSTR5-AS1 inhibits osteoblast apoptosis by regulating Bcl-2 family proteins. Overexpression of SSTR5-AS1 significantly reduced pro-apoptotic proteins Bax and Bad, while markedly increasing anti-apoptotic protein Bcl-2. In the osteoporotic microenvironment, excessive osteoblast apoptosis is a key factor leading to insufficient bone formation [34]. By balancing pro-apoptotic and anti-apoptotic proteins, SSTR5-AS1 provides a novel molecular basis for maintaining osteoblast survival.

Mechanistic studies indicate that SSTR5-AS1 may act as a ceRNA to sequester miR-193b-3p, thereby relieving its inhibitory effect on downstream target genes [35]. This aligns with findings by Wu et al., where lncRNA modulates delayed fracture healing by sponging miRNAs [36]. Dual luciferase reporter assays confirmed that the miR-193b-3p mimic significantly reduced luciferase activity in the WT-SSTR5-AS1 reporter vector, while having no effect on the mutant vector. This finding supports the classical ceRNA regulatory network model. Bioinformatics analysis predicted that miR-193b-3p may primarily interacts with the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway. Guo et al. confirmed the close association between the PI3K-Akt pathway and osteoporotic fractures, demonstrating its crucial role in osteogenic differentiation [37]. We hypothesize that SSTR5-AS1 regulates fracture healing in patients with OPF through the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway. However, this study has several limitations: First, the specific downstream signaling pathways of SSTR5-AS1 remain incompletely understood. Second, in vivo animal experimental evidence is needed. Third, the clinical sample size requires expansion to validate diagnostic efficacy. Furthermore, the role of SSTR5-AS1 in fracture healing among OPF patients has not been clinically validated. Future research will explore the application potential of SSTR5-AS1 in bone tissue engineering and develop its value as a therapeutic target.

In summary, this study is the first to systematically demonstrate the diagnostic value of SSTR5-AS1 in OPF and its dual role in promoting fracture healing through regulation of osteogenic differentiation and apoptosis. SSTR5-AS1 is significantly downregulated in patients with OPF and negatively regulates miR-193b-3p levels. Elevating SSTR5-AS1 reduces miR-193b-3p expression, which decreases osteoblast apoptosis while enhancing proliferation and differentiation capacity. Overexpression of SSTR5-AS1 may accelerate fracture healing. These findings provide novel circulating biomarkers for early OPF diagnosis and establish a theoretical foundation for developing lncRNA-targeted strategies to promote fracture healing.

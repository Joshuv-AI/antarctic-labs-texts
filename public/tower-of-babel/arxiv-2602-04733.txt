Denote by s K ​ ( x , y ) s_{K}(x,y) the triangular ratio metric in K K ; for x ≠ y x\neq y the value of s K ​ ( x , y ) s_{K}(x,y) equals the ratio of the Euclidean distance | x − y | |x-y| between x x , y ∈ K y\in K to the value sup z ∈ ∂ K ( | x − z | + | z − y | ) \sup_{z\in\partial K}(|x-z|+|z-y|) . Introduction

The hyperbolic metric plays an important role in the geometric function theory.
One of the main properties of the hyperbolic metric is its conformal invariance:
the hyperbolic distance keeps under conformal mapping of domains of hyperbolic type.
Also the hyperbolic metric does not increase under holomorphic mappings what is a corollary of the
Schwarz lemma. In a simply connected domain
D
of hyperbolic type,
the hyperbolic metric can be easily calculated if we know the
conformal mapping of the domain onto a canonic domain such as the
unit disc or the half-plane. If the mapping has a simple form, then
we can calculate the hyperbolic distance between points
sufficiently well. Otherwise, the problem is to estimate the
hyperbolic metric.

To do this, we can try to find another metric in
D
, equivalent
in some sense to the hyperbolic one, which can be simply calculated
in Euclidean terms. Many such metrics are known (see, e.g., the
monographs
[
7
,
8
]
). One of such metrics is the triangular
ratio
metric
s=s_{D}
. Is is defined in
D
as follows: if

x\neq y
, then

s_{D}(x,y)=\frac{|x-y|}{\sup_{z\in\partial D}(|x-z|+|z-y|)},

and if
x=y
, then
s_{D}(x,y)=0
. Here
|x-y|
denotes the Euclidean distance between
x
and
y
.

The properties of this metric for some classes of domains and its
applications in the theory of conformal and quasiconformal mappings
were studied in many works (see, e.g.,

[
5
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
).

Very important planar domains are polygonal ones, in particular rectangles, see, e.g.,
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
. In this paper, we investigate the hyperbolic metric in a square.

In
[
5
]
, the problem of finding the best constants in the
inequality

(1)

C_{1}\,s_{D}(x,y)\leq\math@th(\rho_{D}(x,y)/2)\leq C_{2}\,s_{D}(x,y),\quad x,y\in D,

is investigated for some convex polygonal planar domains. It is easy to show that
the maximal constant
C_{1}
in the inequality is equal to
1
. The equality is attained in the
limit when one of the points tends to the boundary of
D
. The problem of finding the
minimal constant
C_{2}
is more complicated. In
[
5
]
a more simple problem to estimate the value

\lim_{y\to x}\frac{\math@th(\rho_{D}(x,y)/2)}{s_{D}(x,y)}

for all
x\in D
was solved. It is easy to see that

\lim_{y\to x}\frac{\math@th(\rho_{D}(x,y)/2)}{s_{D}(x,y)}=\frac{2d_{D}(x)}{r_{D}(x)},

where
d_{D}(x)=\mbox{\rm dist}(x,\partial D)
is the distance from

x
to the boundary
\partial D
of
D
, and
r_{D}(x)
is the
conformal radius of
D
in the point
x
, i.e., the radius of disc

\{|z|<r=r_{D}(x)\}
, onto which we can map conformally the domain
D

by the function
f
with normalization
f(x)=0
,
f^{\prime}(x)=1
.

In
[
5
]
, the sharp estimates of the value

(2)

\frac{2d_{D}(x)}{r_{D}(x)},

were established for many often used convex polygonal domains
D
,
for example, rectangles, isosceles triangles, and regular

-gons.
Also the set, on which the maximum of the value (
2

attained, was described there.

Now we will describe some results from
[
5
]
obtained for rectangles of the form

,

. As it was shown in

Here

In particular, in the case of the square

\math@th\frac{\rho_{K}(w_{1},w_{2})}{2}=\math@th\frac{\rho_{U}(z_{1},z_{2})}{2}=\frac{|z_{1}-z_{2}|}{|1-z_{1}\overline{z_{2}}|},

where

are the preimages of

and

under the mapping

. Then,

|z_{1}-z_{2}|=\left|\int_{w_{1}}^{w_{2}}g^{\prime}(w)dw\right|.

In addition,
g^{\prime}(w)=C\sqrt{1+z^{4}}
, where
z=g(w)
, and

|g^{\prime}(w)-C|=C|\sqrt{1+z^{4}}-1|\leq CA(r).

Then,

|g^{\prime}(w)|\leq C(1+A(r)).

Thus,

|z_{1}-z_{2}|\leq C(1+A(r))|w_{1}-w_{2}|.

Now we will estimate the value
|1-z_{1}\overline{z_{2}}|
from below.
We have

|z_{k}-Cw_{k}|=\left|\int_{0}^{w_{k}}(g^{\prime}(w)-C)dw\right|\leq CA(r)|w_{k}|\leq A(r)B(r).

Since, by (
14
),

|w_{k}|=|f(z_{k})|=C^{-1}\left|\int_{0}^{z_{k}}\frac{d\zeta}{\sqrt{1+\zeta^{4}}}\right|\leq C^{-1}B(|z_{k}|)\leq C^{-1}B(r),

with the help of the triangle inequality we obtain

|1-z_{1}\overline{z_{2}}|=|1-[(z_{1}-Cw_{1})+Cw_{1}][(\overline{z_{2}}-C\overline{w_{2}})+C\overline{w_{2}}]|\\
\geq|1-C^{2}w_{1}\overline{w_{2}}|-2A(r)B^{2}(r)-A^{2}(r)B^{2}(r).

Therefore,

\frac{|z_{1}-z_{2}|}{|1-z_{1}\overline{z_{2}}|}\leq C\frac{(1+A(r))|w_{1}-w_{2}|}{|1-C^{2}w_{1}\overline{w_{2}}|-2A(r)B^{2}(r)-A^{2}(r)B^{2}(r)},

what follow the statement of the lemma.
∎

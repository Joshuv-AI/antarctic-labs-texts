1.
Introduction

1.1.
The thin obstacle problem

A solution to the the thin obstacle problem in
B_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a continuous function
u
, even with respect to the thin space
\{x_{n}=0\}
, that satisfies

\triangle u=0\quad\mbox{in}\quad B_{1}\setminus Z(u),\quad\quad Z(u):=\{x_{n}=0\}\cap\{u=0\},

and

(1.1)

u\geq 0\quad\text{and}\quad u_{n}\leq 0\quad\text{on}\quad\{x_{n}=0\},

where
u_{n}
denotes the one-sided directional derivative in the positive
x_{n}
direction.

Homogeneous solutions are of particular interest, since they appear in the blow-up analysis at points on the free boundary

\Gamma(u)=\partial_{\{x_{n}=0\}}\,Z(u).

The homogeneity
\mu>0
of a homogeneous solution is called its frequency.

Recall that, if
w
is an homogeneous function of degree
\mu
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then on the unit sphere

\triangle w=\,L_{\mu}w,\quad\quad\quad\quad L_{\mu}w:=\triangle_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}w+\mu(\mu+n-2)w.

Thus finding
\mu
-homogeneous solutions of (
1.1
) is equivalent to find solutions of the following problem on the upper hemisphere:

(1.2)

\begin{cases}L_{\mu}u=0&\text{ in }\{x_{n}>0\}\cap\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\\
u\geq 0&\text{ on }\{x_{n}=0\}\cap\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\\
u_{n}\leq 0&\text{ on }\{x_{n}=0\}\cap\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\\
u\cdot u_{n}=0&\text{ on }\{x_{n}=0\}\cap\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.\end{cases}

1.2.
Main results

Our convention for spherical coordinates in 3D is:

x=r\cos\theta\cos\varphi,\quad y=r\sin\theta\cos\varphi,\quad z=r\sin\varphi,

with
\theta\in\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
and
\varphi\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2].
The thin space is

1.3.
Comments

Our main theorems answer some open questions about the set of possible frequencies in the thin obstacle, that is the set

\mathcal{F}_{nD}:=\{\mu>0:\text{there is a nontrivial solution to \eqref{eq:TOPsphere}}\}.

The only known examples of homogeneous solutions are based on extensions of the explicit solutions in dimension
n=2
. For all
n\geq 2,
it holds

(1.3)

\mathcal{F}_{2D}=\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{2k+\tfrac{3}{2}:k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}\,\subseteq\,\mathcal{F}_{nD}.

Athanasopoulous–Caffarelli–Salsa in
[
2
]
classified the lowest frequencies and showed that for all
n\geq 2,

\mathcal{F}_{nD}\cap(0,2)=\{1,\tfrac{3}{2}\},

which in turn gives the optimal regularity of solutions.

By the work Garofalo–Petrosyan
[
10
]
, Colombo–Spolaor–Velichkov
[
3
]
, Figalli–Ros-Oton–Serra
[
6
]
, Savin–Yu
[
15
,
14
]
, when
\mu
is an integer, then homogeneous solutions are classified and

(1.4)

\mathcal{F}_{nD}\cap(m-c_{m,n},m+c_{m,n})=\{m\}\quad\text{for all}\quad m\in\mathbb{N},

for suitable numbers
c_{m,n}>0
.

A dimension reduction argument based on Almgren’s frequency, shows that the set
\mathcal{F}_{nD}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{2D},
has dimension at most
n-3
(see
[
8
]
).
Beside this, very little was known for
\mathcal{F}_{nD}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{2D},
not even if it was empty or not.
This is the main novelty of
Theorem

1
:

\mathcal{F}_{3D}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{2D}\neq\emptyset.

Additionally,
Theorem

1
shows that the numbers
c_{m,n}
appearing in (
1.4
) are necessarily infinitesimal as
m\to+\infty
with
n
fixed.

We also remark that, in all the known examples of homogeneous solutions, the boundary of the interior of the contact set was either empty or an
(n-2)
-dimensional linear space, in particular it was a flat cone.

Theorem

1
shows that this set is not a flat cone in general.

Beside its own interest in showing that the set
\mathcal{F}_{nD},
has large gaps,
Theorem

2
has an important consequence for the free boundary regularity in the
thick
Obstacle Problem, by the work of Figalli–Serra
[
7
]
(see also Savin–Yu
[
14
,
16
]
).

Specifically,
Theorem

2
implies that the top-dimensional stratum of the singular set (the set
\Sigma_{n-1}
, in the notation of
[
7
]
) is locally contained in a
C^{1,1}
hypersurface, rather than a
C^{1,\alpha_{\circ}}
one.
In other words, there are no “anomalous” points in
\Sigma_{n-1}
, see Remark 1.2 (2a) in
[
7
]
for more details.

1.4.
Connection with 2-valued harmonic functions and
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
eigenfunctions

Our examples also work for unsigned versions of the thin obstacle problem.

Denote by
\mathcal{A}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
the space of unordered pairs of real numbers. If
u\colon\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
is one of our examples constructed in
Theorem

1
for some
m
, then

U\colon x\mapsto\{+u(x)\,,\,-u(x)\}\ \in\ \mathcal{A}_{2}(\mathbb{R}),

is a
\bar{\mu}_{m}
-homogeneous, 2-valued,
C^{1,1/2}
, stationary harmonic function (see Krummel–Wickramasekera
[
13
]
).
These functions arise naturally as limits of stable minimal hypersurfaces, see Wickramasekera
[
19
]
.
We remark that
U
is not a Dir-minimizer in the sense of Almgren, having a too big singular set, see De Lellis–Spadaro
[
4
]
.

In a related framework, if we set

\mathbb{S}^{2}\cap\Gamma(u)=:\{z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{2m}\},

then such a
U
can be interpreted as an homogeneous example of an harmonic section of an appropriate real line bundle over
\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{2m}\},
which has nontrivial holonomy around each
z_{i}.
See Taubes–Wu
[
17
,
18
]
, Donaldson
[
5
]
and Haydys–Mazzeo–Takahashi
[
12
]
for some recent developments.

1.5.
Structure of the paper

In
Section

2
we construct the solutions of
Theorem

1
and prove
Theorem

2
. The construction is based on the study of eigenfunctions in spherical domains inside the region
0\leq\theta\leq\pi/m,
and it is short.

The proof of the asymptotics of
Theorem

1
is carried out in
Section

3
. This requires a more refined analysis, but it is necessary in order to justify rigorously why the new frequencies are different from the 2D frequencies, at least for large values of
m
. We remark that this fact can be
inferred numerically already in the first few cases when
m=3
,
m=5
or
m=7.

In
Section

4
we present a natural variation of our construction, which allows us to construct even more solutions.

In
Section

5
we give a self-contained proof of some auxiliary results concerning Legendre functions.

The spherical coordinates in 3D are

x=r\cos\theta\cos\varphi,\quad y=r\sin\theta\cos\varphi,\quad z=r\sin\varphi,

with
\theta\in\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}

\varphi\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2].
The thin space is
\{z=0\}=\{\varphi=0\}

We denote by

Let

\triangle_{\mathbb{S}}u:=u_{\varphi\varphi}-\tan\varphi\,u_{\varphi}+(\cos\varphi)^{-2}\,u_{\theta\theta}.

We denote with

In the sphere

L_{\mu}w=\rho^{-2}\triangle_{\mathbb{S}^{n-2}}w+w_{\varphi\varphi}-(n-2)\tan\varphi\,w_{\varphi}+\mu(\mu+n-2)w.

We often write, for readability,

\lambda=\lambda_{\mu}=\mu(\mu+n-2).

For every
\mu\geq 0
denote by

the unique solution to

(2.1)

L_{\mu}p_{\mu}=0,\qquad p_{\mu}(N)=1

which is also invariant by rotations around the

-axis. The function

depends only on the angle

, and it is uniquely determined by the final value problem

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}p_{\mu}^{\prime\prime}-(n-2)\tan\varphi\cdot p_{\mu}^{\prime}+\mu(\mu+n-2)p_{\mu}=0\quad\text{in}\quad[0,\tfrac{\pi}{2}],\\
p_{\mu}(\tfrac{\pi}{2})=1,\quad\quad p_{\mu}^{\prime}(\tfrac{\pi}{2})=0.\end{cases}

If
\mu
is an even (odd) integer,
p_{\mu}
is a
\mu
-homogeneous harmonic polynomial even (odd) in

and invariant by those rotations that fix
\{x_{n}=0\}
.

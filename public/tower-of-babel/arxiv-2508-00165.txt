Abstract
. We use the version of the Lyapunov–Perron method operating on individual solutions to investigate the existence of invariant manifolds for non-autonomous dynamical systems, focusing in particular on inertial and stable manifolds. We establish a characterization of both types of manifolds in terms of solutions exhibiting a common growth behavior, analogous to the classical characterization involving hyperbolicity. Furthermore, we introduce a unified formulation of the gap condition, from which known sharp versions are derived. Finally, we show that the constructed inertial manifolds have C 1 C^{1} regularity.

1.
Introduction

We consider the non-autonomous abstract parabolic problem governed by the equation

u_{t}=A(t)u+f(t,u),

defined on a possibly infinite-dimensional Banach space
X
, where
\{A(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is a time dependent family of linear operators, and
f(t,\cdot)
are Lipschitz functions. The equation defines a nonlinear process
\{T(t,\tau)\colon t\geqslant\tau\}
of mappings
T(t,\tau)\colon X\to X
which, for the initial value taken at time
\tau
, return the solution of the problem at time
t
.

An
invariant manifold
for this system is the time dependent family of sets

\mathcal{M}(t)=\{\phi+\Sigma(t,\phi)\colon\phi\in Q(t)X\},

where the functions
\Sigma(t,\cdot)
are Lipschitz with common Lipschitz constant,
\{Q(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is a family of projections related to operators
A(t)
, and we have the invariance relation
T(t,\tau)\mathcal{M}(\tau)=\mathcal{M}(t)
for
t\geqslant\tau
. If this manifold is, in addition, exponentially attracting, it is called an
inertial manifold
, see Definition
2.7
.
Once we have constructed an invariant manifold for a given problem we can reduce its dimensionality by restricting the dynamics to the manifold, the resulting system being invertible, and, if this manifold is inertial, all solutions starting outside of it are attracted towards it.

There are several methods used to construct inertial manifolds: two most widely used are the Hadamard’s graph transformation method and the Lyapunov–Perron method. The first one, more geometrical in nature, was applied to construct the inertial manifolds by Mallet-Paret and Sell
[
10
]
, and later was extended to the non-autonomous situation by Koksch and Siegmund
[
8
]
. The Lyapunov–Perron method, on the other hand, was used by D. Henry
[
7
]
to construct stable, unstable and center manifolds for parabolic problems, and, in the work of Foias, Sell, and Temam
[
6
]
, it was adapted for construction of inertial manifolds. We note that in
[
6
]
the concept of inertial manifolds was introduced, and their existence was proved for the first time. Since their introduction, the theory of invariant and inertial manifolds has greatly developed: a very good overview of known results, as of 2014, can be found in
[
13
]
.

Existence of invariant manifolds for a given problem requires a condition known as
spectral gap
which bounds from above the Lipschitz constant of
f(t,\cdot)
by a quantity dependent on the difference of two exponents coming from the
exponential splitting
(Definition
2.2
) of the linear evolution process related to the linear equation
u_{t}=A(t)u
. The natural question is to derive the sharp, or optimal spectral gap condition. This was done by Miklavčič
[
11
]
and Romanov
[
12
]
, who constructed inertial manifolds using the sharp gap condition and gave examples from which it follows that the condition cannot be further improved.

Recently, in the work
[
3
]
, which serves as a direct inspiration for the present article, the authors employed the Lyapunov–Perron method to construct non-autonomous Lipschitz invariant and inertial manifolds. A key idea in their approach is that exponential splitting for the linear process is preserved under nonlinear perturbations, an idea we also further develop here. Furthermore, by introducing the notion of a stable manifold associated with an invariant manifold,
[
3
]
establishes a link between the saddle-point property and invariant manifolds within the non-autonomous framework.

In the present work, we refine the gap condition from
[
3
]
used in the construction of Lipschitz invariant, inertial, and stable manifolds, and also obtain improved associated Lipschitz constants.
In addition, we establish new characterizations of these manifolds in terms of spaces of solutions that exhibit controlled growth, where the growth rate explicitly depends on the gap condition. This approach draws parallels with classical results concerning exponential dichotomies and the saddle-point property, see, for example
[
1
,
5
,
14
]
where such characterizations are established. This further supports the central message of
[
3
]
, emphasizing the strong interrelation among all these concepts.

In
[
3
]
, the gap condition is formulated in terms of the rate of the spectral gap and the size of the nonlinearity (denoted by
(\gamma-\rho)/\ell
in
[
3
]
). In the present work, we propose a more general framework for interpreting the gap condition; see Definition
2.9
. Building upon ideas from
[
4
,
9
]
, we refine the condition in
[
3
]
, obtaining a version that, when expressed in suitable norms, coincides with the sharp condition established in
[
11
,
12
]
; see Definition
2.9
and Remark
2.11
.

Following
[
4
,
9
]
, instead of considering the Lyapunov–Perron fixed point operator which transforms Lipschitz graphs to Lipschitz graphs, we study a version of this operator that acts on continuous functions of time having appropriate growth, at
-\infty
for the invariant manifold and at
+\infty
for the stable manifold. This differs from the approach of
[
3
]
as it operates on individual fibers rather than on the whole graphs. Although this method does not yield automatically the Lipschitzness of the constructed manifold (this property is additionally obtained using the
cone condition
and later even improved), the advantage of this approach is the possibility of obtaining the sharp gap. We note that in the non-autonomous case the same gap as ours has been obtained using the Hadamard’s method in
[
8
]
, and, moreover,
[
9
, Section 6]
discusses the extension of the method to the case of cocycles which entails the non-autonomous framework. Still, our exposition fits into the unified framework of
[
3
]
which allows for the extension to study the stable manifold of an invariant manifold which in turn leads to various applications in autonomous and non-autonomous frameworks such as, for example, the study of robustness of exponential dichotomies. The study of these topics under the assumption of our gap condition from Definition
2.9
will be presented in a continuation of this article. Here, in addition, we prove that under the same gap condition the constructed invariant and inertial manifolds are
C^{1}
smooth.
This smoothness of the manifold is important in further applications as it allows us to construct the tangent space to the manifold at every point, and moreover it implies that the reduced
vector field on the inertial manifold also inherits its smoothness.

The structure of the article is as follows: Section
2
contains the setup of the studied problem and presents and discusses the assumptions needed for the invariant manifold existence. The main result, Theorem
3.1
, on the existence of invariant manifolds is contained and proved in Section
3
. Section
4
is devoted to the construction of the stable manifold associated with the invariant manifold, see Theorem
4.1
. Finally, Sections
5
and
6
contain proofs that the constructed manifolds are differentiable and
C^{1}
(see Theorems
5.1
and
6.1
), respectively.

2.
Problem setup and assumptions

2.1.
Linear evolution process and its exponential splitting.

Let
(X,\left\|\cdot\right\|)
be a Banach space. We denote by
\mathcal{L}(X)
the space of linear and bounded operators from
X
to itself. For a possibly unbounded linear operator
A
in
X
we denote by
D(A)
its domain. Moreover, let
J=\{(s,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\colon s\geqslant t\}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A family of linear and bounded operators
\{L(t,\tau)\colon\ (t,\tau)\in J\}\subset\mathcal{L}(X)
is called a
linear evolution process
if

(i)

L(t,\tau)=L(t,s)L(s,\tau)
for every
t\geqslant s\geqslant\tau
;

(ii)

L(t,t)=I
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
;

(iii)

the function
J\ni(t,\tau)\mapsto L(t,\tau)\eta\in X
is continuous for each
\eta\in X
.

We consider a family of (possibly unbounded) linear operators
A(t)\colon X\supset D(A(t))\to X
defined for
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
We assume that the family
\{A(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
generates a linear evolution process
\{L(t,\tau)\colon\ (t,\tau)\in J\}
such that for each
(\tau,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}\times X
the function
u(t,\tau,\eta)=L(t,\tau)\eta
for
t\geqslant\tau
is a mild solution of the abstract Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}u_{t}=A(t)u\ \ \textrm{for}\ \ t>\tau,\\
u(\tau)=\eta\in X.\end{cases}

(2.1)

Definition 2.2
.

We say that the linear evolution process
\{L(t,\tau)\colon\ (t,\tau)\in J\}
has exponential splitting with a bound
M\geqslant 1
, exponents
\gamma,\rho\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\gamma>\rho
and a family of projections
\{Q(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}\subset\mathcal{L}(X)
if

(i)

for every
(t,\tau)\in J
we have

Q(t)L(t,\tau)=L(t,\tau)Q(\tau);

(ii)

L(t,\tau)|_{Q(\tau)X}\colon Q(\tau)X\to Q(t)X
is an isomorphism with the inverse denoted by

L(t,\tau)^{-1}=L(\tau,t)\colon Q(t)X\to Q(\tau)X\ \ \text{for every}\ \ (t,\tau)\in J;

(iii)

the following estimates hold

\displaystyle e^{\gamma(t-\tau)}\left\|L(t,\tau)(I-Q(\tau))\right\|\leqslant M\ \ \text{for}\ \ (t,\tau)\in J,

(2.2)

\displaystyle e^{\rho(t-\tau)}\left\|L(t,\tau)Q(\tau)\right\|\leqslant M\ \ \text{for}\ \ (\tau,t)\in J.

(2.3)

If
\gamma>0>\rho
, that is,
-\rho>0>-\gamma
, then
Q(t)
is a projection on the unstable
subspace of
X
at time
t
, and the complementary projection

I-Q(t)
is the projection on the stable subspace of
X
at time
t
. In the special case
\rho=-\gamma
we say that the linear evolution process has
exponential dichotomy
with constants
M\geqslant 1
and
\gamma>0
.

2.2.
Renorming of the space
X

For every
t\in\mathbb{R}
the space
X
is a sum of two linear subspaces
Q(t)X
and
(I-Q(t))X
. We introduce the following families of time dependent norms on these subspaces.

Definition 2.3
.

We define the following nonnegative valued functions:

\displaystyle|x|_{N(\tau)}=\sup_{t\leqslant\tau}e^{\rho(t-\tau)}\left\|L(t,\tau)x\right\|\ \ \text{for}\ \ x\in N(\tau)=Q(\tau)X,

(2.4)

\displaystyle|x|_{S(\tau)}=\sup_{t\geqslant\tau}e^{\gamma(t-\tau)}\left\|L(t,\tau)x\right\|\ \ \textrm{for}\ \ x\in S(\tau)=(I-Q(\tau))X.

(2.5)

The next result is a direct consequence of the above definition, see
[
2
, Lemma 6.8]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

If the process
\{L(t,\tau)\colon(t,\tau)\in J\}
has exponential splitting, then
the functions
|\cdot|_{N(\tau)}
and
|\cdot|_{S(\tau)}
are norms in
N(\tau)
and
S(\tau)
, respectively, which are equivalent to
\left\|\cdot\right\|
on each of the spaces. Moreover, we have

\left\|x\right\|\leqslant|x|_{N(\tau)}\leqslant M\left\|x\right\|\ \text{for}\ x\in N(\tau)\quad\text{and}\quad\left\|x\right\|\leqslant|x|_{S(\tau)}\leqslant M\left\|x\right\|\ \text{for}\ x\in S(\tau)

(2.6)

and

|Q(\tau)x|_{N(\tau)}\leqslant M\left\|x\right\|\ \text{and}\ |(I-Q(\tau))x|_{S(\tau)}\leqslant M\left\|x\right\|\ \text{for}\ x\in X.

(2.7)

Furthermore, we have

e^{\rho t}|L(t,\tau)x|_{N(t)}\leqslant e^{\rho\tau}|x|_{N(\tau)}\ \text{for}\ t\leqslant\tau\ \text{and}\ x\in N(\tau),

e^{\gamma t}|L(t,\tau)x|_{S(t)}\leqslant e^{\gamma\tau}|x|_{S(\tau)}\ \text{for}\ t\geqslant\tau\ \text{and}\ x\in S(\tau).

The last two inequalities mean that after renorming, the estimates (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) hold with
M=1
. Thus, the choice of the norms in a way adjusted to the operator of the problem allows us to avoid the constant
M
in the bounds of the exponential splitting.

We choose a norm
\Gamma(\cdot,\cdot)
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
that we will use to build the new norm on
X
from the norms
|\cdot|_{N(\tau)}
and
|\cdot|_{S(\tau)}
. This
\Gamma(\cdot,\cdot)
is assumed to satisfy the following property.

Definition 2.5
.

We say that a norm
\Gamma\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,\infty)
is admissible if for every
a,b\geqslant 0
functions
\Gamma(a,\cdot)
and
\Gamma(\cdot,b)
are strictly monotone on
[0,\infty)
.

Among admissible norms on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
we distinguish the norms
\left\|(a_{1},a_{2})\right\|_{p}=(|a_{1}|^{p}+|a_{2}|^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}
with
p\geqslant 1
or
\left\|(a_{1},a_{2})\right\|_{\infty}=\max\{|a_{1}|,|a_{2}|\}
. Due to the equivalence of norms, we introduce a constant
c_{\Gamma}>0
such that

|a_{1}|+|a_{2}|=\left\|(a_{1},a_{2})\right\|_{1}\leqslant c_{\Gamma}\Gamma(a_{1},a_{2})\ \text{for every}\ (a_{1},a_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

We have
c_{\Gamma}=2^{1-\frac{1}{p}}
if
\Gamma(a_{1},a_{2})=\left\|(a_{1},a_{2})\right\|_{p}
,
p\geqslant 1
, and
c_{\Gamma}=2
if
\Gamma(a_{1},a_{2})=\left\|(a_{1},a_{2})\right\|_{\infty}
.

The next result follows in a straightforward way from Lemma
2.4
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Given an admissible norm on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, for each
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
the function

\left\|x\right\|_{\tau}=\Gamma\left(|Q(\tau)x|_{N(\tau)},|(I-Q(\tau))x|_{S(\tau)}\right)\ \text{for}\ x\in X

(2.8)

is an equivalent norm in
X
. We have

\frac{1}{c_{\Gamma}}\left\|x\right\|\leqslant\left\|x\right\|_{\tau}\leqslant M\Gamma(1,1)\left\|x\right\|\text{ for }x\in X\ \text{and}\ \tau\in\mathbb{R}.

2.3.
Nonlinearity, nonlinear process, and the gap condition.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}\times X\to X
be a continuous function such that

f(t,0)=0\text{ for all }t\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.9)

We need the nonlinearity
f
to be Lipschitz with respect to its second argument. Specifically, it is convenient for us to make the Lipschitz assumption separately for
Q(t)
projection of
f
and for its complement. Namely, the standing assumption of this paper is that there exist
L_{1},L_{2}>0
such that for
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
u,v\in X
we have

\displaystyle|Q(t)(f(t,u)-f(t,v))|_{N(t)}\leqslant L_{1}\left\|u-v\right\|_{t},

(2.10)

\displaystyle|(I-Q(t))(f(t,u)-f(t,v))|_{S(t)}\leqslant L_{2}\left\|u-v\right\|_{t}.

(2.11)

Note that if
f
is uniformly globally Lipschitz in the second variable with the Lipschitz constant
\ell>0
, i.e.,

\left\|f(t,u)-f(t,v)\right\|\leqslant\ell\left\|u-v\right\|,\ u,v\in X,\ t\in\mathbb{R},

(2.12)

then assumptions (
2.10
), (
2.11
) hold with
L_{1}=L_{2}=M\ell c_{\Gamma}
.

We consider an abstract Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}u_{t}=A(t)u+f(t,u)\ \ \textrm{for}\ \ \ t>\tau,\\
u(\tau)=\eta\in X,\end{cases}

(2.13)

which generates a nonlinear evolution process
\{T(t,\tau)\colon(t,\tau)\in J\}
in
X
, that satisfies

T(t,\tau)\eta=L(t,\tau)\eta+\int_{\tau}^{t}L(t,s)f(s,T(s,\tau)\eta)ds\ \ \text{for}\ \ t\geqslant\tau,\ \eta\in X.

(2.14)

Thus
T(t,\tau)\colon X\to X
satisfies
T(t,t)=I
,
T(t,s)T(s,\tau)=T(t,\tau)
for
t\geqslant s\geqslant\tau
and
the function
[\tau,\infty)\ni t\mapsto T(t,\tau)\eta
is continuous for each
(\tau,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}\times X.

Moreover,
T(t,\tau)0=0
for
t\geqslant\tau
.

Assuming the existence of an exponential splitting for the linear process
\{L(t,\tau)\colon t\geqslant\tau\}
, we show that this property is, in a certain sense, preserved under nonlinear perturbations. This behavior leads to the existence of an invariant manifold, which we now recall.

Definition 2.7
.

A family
\{\mathcal{M}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}\subset X
is called an invariant manifold for the evolution process
\{T(t,\tau)\colon t\geqslant\tau\}
if

(i)

\{\mathcal{M}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is invariant under the process, i.e.,

T(t,\tau)\mathcal{M}(\tau)=\mathcal{M}(t)\text{ for all }t,\tau\in\mathbb{R}\text{ such that }t\geqslant\tau;

(ii)

\{\mathcal{M}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is
forward and pullback exponentially dominated
, i.e.,
there is
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
such that
given a bounded set
U\subset X
, there exist
t_{*}\geqslant 0
,
K>0
such that

\operatorname{dist}(T(t,\tau)U,\mathcal{M}(t))\leqslant Ke^{-\omega(t-\tau)},

for all
t,\tau\in\mathbb{R}
with
t-\tau\geqslant t_{*}
;

(iii)

\mathcal{M}(t)
is a Lipschitz graph for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

If additionally
\omega>0
, then
\{\mathcal{M}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}\subset X
is
exponentially attracting
(pullback and forward) and it is called an
inertial manifold
.

When studying invariant objects, complete trajectories that capture their behavior naturally arise. In
[
2
]
, these special solutions are referred to as global solutions.

Definition 2.8
.

We say that
\xi\colon\mathcal{T}\to X
is a solution of the evolution process
\{T(t,\tau)\colon(t,\tau)\in J\}
(or of (
2.14
), for short) on an interval
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathbb{R}
if

T(t,\tau)\xi(\tau)=\xi(t)\ \text{for}\ t\geqslant\tau,\ t,\tau\in\mathcal{T}.

We call it a global solution (or complete trajectory) if
\mathcal{T}=\mathbb{R}
.

Note that by the assumed continuity of the evolution process its solutions are continuous functions with values in
X
.

We assume the gap condition given by the following definition.

Definition 2.9
.

We say that the constants
\gamma>\rho
, and
L_{1},L_{2}>0
satisfy the gap condition with the admissible norm
\Gamma(\cdot,\cdot)
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
if there exists
\sigma\in(\rho,\gamma)
such that

\Gamma\left(\frac{L_{1}}{\sigma-\rho},\frac{L_{2}}{\gamma-\sigma}\right)<1.

(2.15)

Corollary 2.10
.

Assume that

\gamma-\rho>\Gamma(1,1)(L_{1}+L_{2}),

(2.16)

then the gap condition given in Definition
2.9
is satisfied with any
\sigma\in(\rho+\Gamma(1,1)L_{1},\gamma-\Gamma(1,1)L_{2})
.

Proof.

We can choose
\sigma
such that

\gamma-L_{2}\Gamma(1,1)>\sigma>\rho+L_{1}\Gamma(1,1),

which is equivalent to the fact that
\frac{L_{1}}{\sigma-\rho}<\frac{1}{\Gamma(1,1)}
and
\frac{L_{2}}{\gamma-\sigma}<\frac{1}{\Gamma(1,1)}
. By the monotonicity of
\Gamma
, we have

\Gamma\left(\frac{L_{1}}{\sigma-\rho},\frac{L_{2}}{\gamma-\sigma}\right)<1,

which ends the proof.
∎

Remark 2.11
.

Note that if (
2.12
) holds, and we have the condition

\gamma-\rho>4M\ell,

then, regardless of the chosen norm
\Gamma(\cdot)=\left\|\cdot\right\|_{p}
with
1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty
, the gap condition in Definition
2.9
holds with any
\sigma\in(\rho+2M\ell,\gamma-2M\ell)
. This improves the gap condition of
[
3
]
.

If we choose the norm
\|(a_{1},a_{2})\|_{\infty}
in the role of
\Gamma
, then the inequality (
2.15
) holds

if and only if

\sigma\in(\rho-L_{1},\gamma+L_{2})
. Thus, in this case, the gap condition in Definition
2.9
is
equivalent
to the optimal condition of
[
9
]
, which is exactly (
2.16
), namely

\gamma-\rho>L_{1}+L_{2}.

If additionally
L_{1}=L_{2}=L
, this reduces to the sharp gap condition

\gamma-\rho>2L.

(2.17)

Sharpness of condition (
2.17
) was first obtained in
[
11
,
12
]
, see also
[
4
,
9
,
13
]
, and it can be seen in the example of
[
4
]
, namely in the system

\begin{pmatrix}x\\
y\end{pmatrix}^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x\\
y\end{pmatrix}+\varepsilon\begin{pmatrix}-y\\
x\end{pmatrix},

where the term with
\varepsilon
is treated as nonlinearity. Then
\gamma=1
,
\rho=-1
, and
L=L_{1}=L_{2}=|\varepsilon|
. The gap condition (
2.17
) signifies that
|\varepsilon|<1
. The constant
1
in the right-hand side of the above formula cannot be increased, because if we take
\varepsilon
greater than one, we do not have the invariant set which is a graph over the
x
variable.

Note that in this norm, the Lipschitz conditions (
2.10
)–(
2.11
) on the nonlinearity
f
with
L_{1}=L_{2}=L
can be written as

\|f(\tau,u)-f(\tau,v)\|_{\tau}\leqslant L\|u-v\|_{\tau}\ \ \text{for}\ \ \tau\in\mathbb{R}.

In the case when the norm
\|(a_{1},a_{2})\|_{p}
is taken in the role of
\Gamma
, the condition (
2.15
) is equivalent to say that

\gamma-\rho>\left(L_{1}^{\frac{p}{p+1}}+L_{2}^{\frac{p}{p+1}}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{p}}.

In particular, if
L_{1}=L_{2}=L
, this takes the (non-sharp) form

\gamma-\rho>2^{1+\frac{1}{p}}L.

In particular, in the case of the norm
\|(a_{1},a_{2})\|_{1}
we obtain the condition

\gamma-\rho>4L,
and in the case of the norm
\|(a_{1},a_{2})\|_{2}
we get
\gamma-\rho>2\sqrt{2}L.

2.4.
Gronwall lemmas.

We conclude this section by recalling two integral versions of the Gronwall lemma that will be both used in the sequel.

Lemma 2.12

(Gronwall)
.

Let
-\infty\leqslant a<b<\infty
and let
y\colon(a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
be a locally Lebesgue integrable function. If a non-increasing function
\alpha\colon(a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
and
\beta>0
satisfy the inequality

y(t)\leqslant\alpha(t)+\beta\int_{t}^{b}y(s)ds,\ t\in(a,b],

then

y(t)\leqslant\alpha(t)e^{\beta(b-t)},\ t\in(a,b].

(2.18)

Proof.

We multiply by
\beta e^{\beta(t-b)}
and get

\beta y(t)e^{\beta(t-b)}\leqslant\alpha(t)\beta e^{\beta(t-b)}+\beta^{2}e^{\beta(t-b)}\int_{t}^{b}y(s)ds,\ t\in(a,b],

so

\frac{d}{dt}\Bigl(-\beta e^{\beta(t-b)}\int_{t}^{b}y(s)ds\Bigr)\leqslant\alpha(t)\beta e^{\beta(t-b)}.

Integrating on the interval
[t,b]
we obtain

\beta e^{\beta(t-b)}\int_{t}^{b}y(s)ds\leqslant\beta\int_{t}^{b}\alpha(s)e^{\beta(s-b)}ds\leqslant\beta\alpha(t)\int_{t}^{b}e^{\beta(s-b)}ds=\alpha(t)(1-e^{\beta(t-b)}),

which leads to (
2.18
).
∎

Lemma 2.13

(Gronwall)
.

Let
-\infty\leqslant a<b<\infty
and let
y\colon(a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha\colon(a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
be locally Lebesgue integrable functions. If for some
\beta>0
they satisfy the inequality

y(t)\leqslant\int_{t}^{b}(\alpha(s)+\beta y(s))ds,\ t\in(a,b],

then

y(t)\leqslant\int_{t}^{b}\alpha(s)e^{\beta(s-t)}ds,\ t\in(a,b].

Proof.

Defining

z(s)=\int_{s}^{b}(\alpha(r)+\beta y(r))dr,\ s\in(a,b],

we get

\frac{d}{ds}\bigl[-z(s)e^{\beta s}\bigr]=(-z^{\prime}(s)-\beta z(s))e^{\beta s}\leqslant\alpha(s)e^{\beta s},\ s\in(a,b].

Thus

z(t)e^{\beta t}\leqslant\int_{t}^{b}\alpha(s)e^{\beta s}ds,\ s\in(a,b],

which yields the claim.
∎

3.
Results on existence of Lipschitz invariant and inertial manifolds

3.1.
Main result.

We first formulate the main theorem of this paper, which will be proved in the following part of this section.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that the linear abstract Cauchy problem (
2.1
) generates a linear process
\{L(t,\tau)\colon t\geqslant\tau\}
on a Banach space
X
, which has the exponential splitting (Definition
2.2
) with projections
\{Q(\tau)\colon\tau\in\mathbb{R}\}
and constants
\gamma,\rho\in\mathbb{R}
,
\gamma>\rho
and
M\geqslant 1
.

Introducing the spaces
N(\tau)=Q(\tau)X
and
S(\tau)=(I-Q(\tau))X
for
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, endowed with the norms
|\cdot|_{N(\tau)}|
and
|\cdot|_{S(\tau)}
given by (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), respectively,
and equipping
X
with the equivalent norms
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\tau}
given in (
2.8
) for a chosen admissible norm
\Gamma
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
(Definition
2.5
), we further assume that a continuous function
f\colon\mathbb{R}\times X\to X
satisfies (
2.9
) and the uniform global Lipschitz conditions
(
2.10
) and (
2.11
) with constants
L_{1},L_{2}>0
.

Consider the perturbed abstract Cauchy problem (
2.13
) and assume that it generates an evolution process
\{T(t,\tau)\colon t\geqslant\tau\}
in
X
, which satisfies the variation of constants formula (
2.14
), that is,

T(t,\tau)\eta=L(t,\tau)\eta+\int_{\tau}^{t}L(t,s)f(s,T(s,\tau)\eta)ds\ \ \text{for}\ \ t\geqslant\tau\ \ \text{and}\ \ \eta\in X.

Finally, assume that the gap condition (
2.15
) given in Definition
2.9
holds, that is,

\Gamma\left(\frac{L_{1}}{\sigma-\rho},\frac{L_{2}}{\gamma-\sigma}\right)<1\ \ \text{for some}\ \ \sigma\in(\rho,\gamma).

Then there exists an invariant manifold
\{\mathcal{M}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
for
\{T(t,\tau)\colon t\geqslant\tau\}
given by

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}(t)=\Big\{\eta\in X\colon&\text{there exists a global solution}\ z\colon\mathbb{R}\to X\ \text{of}\ \eqref{e:VCF}\\
&\text{such that }z(t)=\eta,\ \sup_{r\leqslant t}\{e^{\sigma r}\|z(r)\|_{r}\}<+\infty\Big\},\end{split}

(3.1)

satisfying the following properties:

(I)

\{\mathcal{M}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is a Lipschitz graph:

\mathcal{M}(t)=P_{\Sigma}(t)X:=\{Q(t)\eta+\Sigma(t,Q(t)\eta)\colon\eta\in X\},

where
P_{\Sigma}(t)\eta:=Q(t)\eta+\Sigma(t,\eta)
,
(t,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}\times X
, for a function

\Sigma\colon\mathbb{R}\times X\to X
, (defined later in (
3.3
)) such that

\Sigma(\tau,\eta)=\Sigma(\tau,Q(\tau)\eta)=(I-Q(\tau))\Sigma(\tau,\eta)\ \text{for}\ \tau\in\mathbb{R}\ \text{and}\ \eta\in X,

\Sigma(\tau,0)=0
for
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, and

|\Sigma(\tau,\eta)-\Sigma(\tau,\tilde{\eta})|_{S(\tau)}\leqslant\kappa_{\Sigma}|Q(\tau)(\eta-\tilde{\eta})|_{N(\tau)}\ \text{for}\ \eta,\tilde{\eta}\in X\ \text{and}\ \tau\in\mathbb{R},

with the Lipschitz constant
0<\kappa_{\Sigma}<\kappa=\frac{L_{2}}{L_{1}}\frac{\sigma-\rho}{\gamma-\sigma}
(see Corollary
3.15
)
satisfying

\gamma-\rho=L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma})+L_{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa_{\Sigma}},1\right),

so that the value of
\kappa_{\Sigma}
depends only on
\gamma,\rho,L_{1},L_{2}
and the chosen admissible norm
\Gamma
.

(II)

\{\mathcal{M}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
satisfies the following refined controlled growth

\|z(t)\|_{t}\leqslant\frac{\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma})}{\Gamma(1,0)}e^{-(\rho+L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma}))(t-\tau)}\|\eta\|_{\tau}\ \text{for}\ t\leqslant\tau,

where
z\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
is a unique global solution of (
2.14
) through
z(\tau)=\eta\in\mathcal{M}(\tau)
,

(III)

\{\mathcal{M}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
satisfies the following property

|T(t,\tau)\eta-P_{\Sigma}(t)T(t,\tau)\eta|_{S(t)}\leqslant|\eta-P_{\Sigma}(\tau)\eta|_{S(\tau)}e^{-\omega(t-\tau)}\ \text{for}\ t\geqslant\tau,\ \eta\in X,

with

\omega=\gamma-\frac{(\gamma-\rho)L_{2}\Gamma(0,1)}{\gamma-\rho-L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma})}\in(\rho,\gamma),

(IV)

\{\mathcal{M}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
exponentially controls the evolution of any bounded set
G\subset X
in the following sense: for each bounded subset
G\subset X
there is
C_{G}>0
such that

\operatorname{dist}(T(t,\tau)G,\mathcal{M}(t))\leqslant C_{G}e^{-\omega(t-\tau)}\ \ \text{for}\ \ t\geqslant\tau,

where
\operatorname{dist}(A,B)=\sup_{a\in A}\inf_{b\in B}{\|a-b\|}
is the Hausdorff semi-distance between
A,B\subset X
.

If additionally
\omega>0
, then
\{\mathcal{M}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is a forward and pullback exponentially attracting invariant manifold, i.e., an inertial manifold for the evolution process
\{T(t,\tau)\colon t\geqslant\tau\}
.

We now briefly recall the definition of a pullback attractor, see
[
2
]
for more details on this object.
A
pullback attractor

\{\mathcal{A}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
for
\{T(t,\tau)\colon t\geqslant\tau\}
is a family of compact subsets of
X
such that
\{\mathcal{A}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is invariant, pullback attracts bounded subsets of
X
, i.e., for each bounded subset
B
of
X
, and
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\lim_{\tau\to-\infty}\operatorname{dist}(T(t,\tau)B,\mathcal{A}(t))=0,

and it is the minimal family of closed subsets which pullback attracts bounded subsets.

Remark 3.2
.

If
\{\mathcal{M}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is an inertial manifold (if
\omega>0
in Theorem
3.1
) and there exists pullback attractor
\{\mathcal{A}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
for
\{T(t,\tau)\colon t\geqslant\tau\}
, then minimality implies that
\mathcal{A}(t)\subset\mathcal{M}(t)
, for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

3.2.
Fixed point argument.

Let
z\colon(-\infty,\tau]\to X
be a solution of (
2.14
).
Then after representing
Q(t)z(t)=q(t)
and
(I-Q(t))z(t)=p(t)
, projecting (
2.14
) on
N(\tau)
and
S(t)
, the following variation of constants formulas hold

\displaystyle q(\tau)=L(\tau,t)q(t)+\int_{t}^{\tau}L(\tau,s)Q(s)f(s,z(s))ds\ \ \text{for}\ \ {-\infty<t\leqslant\tau},

\displaystyle p(t)=L(t,r)p(r)+\int_{r}^{t}L(t,s)(I-Q(s))f(s,z(s))ds\ \ \text{for}\ \ {-\infty<r\leqslant t\leqslant\tau.}

In the first of these equations we use the invertibility of
L(\tau,t)
, which yields

q(t)=L(t,\tau)q(\tau)-\int_{t}^{\tau}L(t,s)Q(s)f(s,z(s))ds\ \ \text{for}\ \ -\infty<t\leqslant\tau.

To deal with the second equation note that for
\sigma<\gamma
we have

|L(t,r)p(r)|_{S(t)}\leqslant e^{-\gamma(t-r)}|p(r)|_{S(r)}\leqslant\frac{e^{(\gamma-\sigma)r}}{\Gamma(0,1)}e^{-\gamma t}e^{\sigma\tau}\sup_{r\leqslant\tau}\left\{e^{\sigma(r-\tau)}\|z(r)\|_{r}\right\},

which tends to zero as
r\to-\infty
provided the last supremum is finite. Then this means that

z(t)=L(t,\tau)q(\tau)-\int_{t}^{\tau}L(t,s)Q(s)f(s,z(s))ds+\int_{-\infty}^{t}L(t,s)(I-Q(s))f(s,z(s))ds,\ {t\leqslant\tau.}

(3.2)

Above equation motivates the following fixed point problem.

Given
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
we consider the space

E_{\sigma,\tau}=\left\{z\in C((-\infty,\tau];X)\colon\left\|z\right\|_{E_{\sigma,\tau}}=\sup_{t\leqslant\tau}e^{\sigma(t-\tau)}\left\|z(t)\right\|_{t}<\infty\right\},

which is a Banach space with the given norm. Having fixed
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
,
\eta\in X
, we consider the mapping
H_{\tau,\eta}\colon E_{\sigma,\tau}\to C((-\infty,\tau];X)
given by the formula

H_{\tau,\eta}z(t)=L(t,\tau)Q(\tau)\eta-\int_{t}^{\tau}L(t,s)Q(s)f(s,z(s))ds+\int_{-\infty}^{t}L(t,s)(I-Q(s))f(s,z(s))ds,

for
-\infty<t\leqslant\tau
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume the gap condition (
2.15
). The mapping
H_{\tau,\eta}
is a contraction leading from
E_{\sigma,\tau}
to itself, and hence it has a unique fixed point
z_{\tau,\eta}\in E_{\sigma,\tau}
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\left\|H_{\tau,\eta}z(t)\right\|_{t}

\displaystyle=\Gamma\left(\left|L(t,\tau)Q(\tau)\eta-\int_{t}^{\tau}L(t,s)Q(s)f(s,z(s))ds\right|_{N(t)},\left|\int_{-\infty}^{t}L(t,s)(I-Q(s))f(s,z(s))ds\right|_{S(t)}\right).

We estimate both terms in the above norm as follows

\left|L(t,\tau)Q(\tau)\eta-\int_{t}^{\tau}L(t,s)Q(s)f(s,z(s))ds\right|_{N(t)}\leqslant e^{\rho(\tau-t)}|Q(\tau)\eta|_{N(\tau)}+L_{1}\int_{t}^{\tau}e^{\rho(s-t)}\left\|z(s)\right\|_{s}ds,

and

\left|\int_{-\infty}^{t}L(t,s)(I-Q(s))f(s,z(s))ds\right|_{S(t)}\leqslant L_{2}\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{\gamma(s-t)}\left\|z(s)\right\|_{s}ds.

Thus

\displaystyle e^{\sigma(t-\tau)}\left\|H_{\tau,\eta}z(t)\right\|_{t}

\displaystyle\ \ \leqslant\Gamma\left(e^{(\sigma-\rho)(t-\tau)}|Q(\tau)\eta|_{N(\tau)}+L_{1}\left\|z\right\|_{E_{\sigma,\tau}}\int_{t}^{\tau}e^{(\sigma-\rho)(t-s)}ds,L_{2}\left\|z\right\|_{E_{\sigma,\tau}}\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{(\gamma-\sigma)(s-t)}ds\right)

\displaystyle\ \ =\Gamma\left(e^{(\sigma-\rho)(t-\tau)}|Q(\tau)\eta|_{N(\tau)}+L_{1}\left\|z\right\|_{E_{\sigma,\tau}}\frac{1-e^{(\sigma-\rho)(t-\tau)}}{\sigma-\rho},L_{2}\left\|z\right\|_{E_{\sigma,\tau}}\frac{1}{\gamma-\sigma}\right)

\displaystyle\ \ \leqslant\Gamma\left(|Q(\tau)\eta|_{N(\tau)}+L_{1}\left\|z\right\|_{E_{\sigma,\tau}}\frac{1}{\sigma-\rho},L_{2}\left\|z\right\|_{E_{\sigma,\tau}}\frac{1}{\gamma-\sigma}\right).

Therefore, since
\gamma>\sigma>\rho
, we get

\left\|H_{\tau,\eta}z\right\|_{E_{\sigma,\tau}}\leqslant\Gamma\left(|Q(\tau)\eta|_{N(\tau)}+L_{1}\left\|z\right\|_{E_{\sigma,\tau}}\frac{1}{\sigma-\rho},L_{2}\left\|z\right\|_{E_{\sigma,\tau}}\frac{1}{\gamma-\sigma}\right)<\infty,

and
H_{\tau,\eta}
leads from
E_{\sigma,\tau}
to itself.

To prove that it is a contraction, estimating in a similar way, we obtain

\left\|H_{\tau,\eta}z_{1}-H_{\tau,\eta}z_{2}\right\|_{E_{\sigma,\tau}}\leqslant\left\|z_{1}-z_{2}\right\|_{E_{\sigma,\tau}}\Gamma\left(\frac{L_{1}}{\sigma-\rho},\frac{L_{2}}{\gamma-\sigma}\right)\ \ \textrm{for all}\ \ z_{1},z_{2}\in E_{\sigma,\tau},

which ends the proof by (
2.15
).
∎

Thus there exists a unique function
z_{\tau,\eta}\in E_{\sigma,\tau}
such that

z_{\tau,\eta}(t)=L(t,\tau)Q(\tau)\eta-\int_{t}^{\tau}L(t,s)Q(s)f(s,z_{\tau,\eta}(s))ds+\int_{-\infty}^{t}L(t,s)(I-Q(s))f(s,z_{\tau,\eta}(s))ds,

It is clear that
z_{\tau,\eta}
does not depend on
(I-Q(\tau))\eta
and hence
z_{\tau,\eta}=z_{\tau,Q(\tau)\eta}
for
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and
\eta\in X
. Moreover
z_{\tau,0}=0
for every
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
.

3.3.
Definition of the manifold and its invariance.

In this subsection we will use the notation

q(t)=Q(t)z(t)\quad\text{and}\quad p(t)=(I-Q(t))z(t)\ \ \text{for}\ \ z\in C((-\infty,\tau];X).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\eta\in X
and
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
be given. The function
z
is a fixed point of
H_{\tau,\eta}
in
E_{\sigma,\tau}
if and only if
z\in E_{\sigma,\tau}
is a solution of (
2.14
) and
Q(\tau)z(\tau)=Q(\tau)\eta
.

Proof.

The computation above Lemma
3.3
shows that a solution of (
2.14
) defined on the interval
(-\infty,\tau]
which belongs to
E_{\sigma,\tau}
and satisfies
Q(\tau)z(\tau)=Q(\tau)\eta
is a fixed point of
H_{\tau,\eta}
. For an opposite implication observe that for
-\infty<t\leqslant\theta\leqslant\tau
we have

\begin{split}q(t)&=L(t,\tau)Q(\tau)\eta-\int_{t}^{\tau}L(t,s)Q(s)f(s,z(s))ds\\
&=L(t,\theta)\left(L(\theta,\tau)Q(\tau)\eta-\int_{\theta}^{\tau}L(\theta,s)Q(s)f(s,z(s))ds\right)-\int_{t}^{\theta}L(t,s)Q(s)f(s,z(s))ds\\
&=L(t,\theta)q(\theta)-\int_{t}^{\theta}L(t,s)Q(s)f(s,z(s))ds.\end{split}

Inverting
L(t,\theta)
yields

q(\theta)=L(\theta,t)q(t)+\int_{t}^{\theta}L(\theta,s)Q(s)f(s,z(s))\,ds.

On the other hand, for
-\infty<t\leqslant\theta\leqslant\tau
we have

\begin{split}p(\theta)&=\int_{-\infty}^{\theta}L(\theta,s)(I-Q(s))f(s,z(s))ds\\
&=L(\theta,t)\int_{-\infty}^{t}L(t,s)(I-Q(s))f(s,z(s))ds+\int_{t}^{\theta}L(\theta,s)(I-Q(s))f(s,z(s))ds\\
&=L(\theta,t)p(t)+\int_{t}^{\theta}L(\theta,s)(I-Q(s))f(s,z(s))ds,\end{split}

and the proof is complete.
∎

Note that the uniqueness of the fixed point of
H_{\tau,\eta}
and the above lemma imply that for a given
\eta\in X
and
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
there exists a unique function
z\in E_{\sigma,\tau}
which is a solution of (
2.14
) such that
Q(\tau)z=Q(\tau)\eta
and at the same time the fixed point
z_{\tau,\eta}
of
H_{\tau,\eta}
. We are in a position to define the function
\Sigma\colon\mathbb{R}\times X\to X
, whose graph is a candidate for the manifold that we are constructing,

\Sigma(\tau,\eta)=(I-Q(\tau))z_{\tau,\eta}(\tau)=\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}L(\tau,s)(I-Q(s))f(s,z_{\tau,\eta}(s))ds\ \ \text{for}\ \ \tau\in\mathbb{R}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \eta\in X.

(3.3)

From its construction, we know that
\Sigma(\tau,\eta)=\Sigma(\tau,Q(\tau)\eta)=(I-Q(\tau))\Sigma(\tau,\eta)
for
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and
\eta\in X
, and
\Sigma(\tau,0)=0
for
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
.
Now, we define

\mathcal{M}(\tau)
as the graph of
\Sigma(\tau,\cdot)\colon Q(\tau)X\to(I-Q(\tau))X
, that is,

\mathcal{M}(\tau):=\{Q(\tau)\eta+\Sigma(\tau,Q(\tau)\eta)\colon\eta\in X\}=\{z_{\tau,\eta}(\tau)\colon\eta\in X\}.

(3.4)

In other words,

\{\mathcal{M}(\tau)\colon\tau\in\mathbb{R}\}

is a family of subsets of
X
given by
the image of the nonlinear projections,

P_{\Sigma}(\tau):=Q(\tau)+\Sigma(\tau,\cdot)\text{ for }\tau\in\mathbb{R}.

(3.5)

This family satisfies the following result.

Lemma 3.5
.

Fix
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
. We have
\eta\in\mathcal{M}(\tau)
if and only if there exists a solution
z\colon(-\infty,\tau]\to X
of (
2.14
) such that
z(\tau)=\eta
and
z\in E_{\sigma,\tau}
. In consequence, this means that (
3.1
) holds.

Proof.

Note that
\eta\in\mathcal{M}(\tau)
if and only if
\Sigma(\tau,\eta)=(I-Q(\tau))\eta
. For
\eta\in\mathcal{M}(\tau)
we consider the unique fixed point
z_{\tau,\eta}
of
H_{\tau,\eta}
. By Lemma
3.4
this function is a solution of (
2.14
), belongs for
E_{\sigma,\tau}
, and
Q(\tau)z_{\tau,\eta}(\tau)=Q(\tau)\eta
. Moreover, by definition of
\Sigma
, we have
(I-Q(\tau))z_{\tau,\eta}(\tau)=\Sigma(\tau,\eta)
and hence
z_{\tau,\eta}(\tau)=\eta
.

Now assume that
z\colon(-\infty,\tau]\to X
is a solution of (
2.14
) such that
z(\tau)=\eta
and
z\in E_{\sigma,\tau}
. Then, by Lemma
3.4
the function
z
is a unique fixed point of

H_{\tau,\eta}
, and it must be equal to
z_{\tau,\eta}
. Therefore, by definition,
\Sigma(\tau,\eta)=(I-Q(\tau))z(\tau)
. This means that
\eta=z(\tau)=Q(\tau)\eta+\Sigma(\tau,\eta)\in\mathcal{M}(\tau).

Extending
z(t)=T(t,\tau)\eta
for
t\geqslant\tau
, we get a global solution of (
2.14
) so that (
3.1
) holds.
∎

The above result allows us to easily deduce the invariance of constructed family of manifolds
\{\mathcal{M}(\tau)\colon\tau\in\mathbb{R}\}
.

Lemma 3.6
.

The family
\{\mathcal{M}(\tau)\colon\tau\in\mathbb{R}\}
is invariant, that is,
T(t,\tau)\mathcal{M}(\tau)=\mathcal{M}(t)
for every
(t,\tau)\in J
.

Proof.

Take
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
,
\eta\in\mathcal{M}(\tau)
, and
t>\tau
. By Lemma
3.5
there exists a function
z\in E_{\sigma,\tau}
, solution of (
2.14
) on interval
(-\infty,\tau]
such that
z(\tau)=\eta
. Define

v(s)=\begin{cases}z(s)\ \ &\text{for}\ \ s\in(-\infty,\tau],\\
T(s,\tau)\eta\ \ &\textrm{for}\ \ s\in(\tau,t].\end{cases}

This is a solution of (
2.14
) on
(-\infty,t]
belonging to
E_{\sigma,t}
. Hence
v(t)=T(t,\tau)\eta\in\mathcal{M}(t)
so we have the positive invariance.

To deduce the negative invariance take
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
\eta\in\mathcal{M}(t)
, and
\tau<t
. By Lemma
3.5
there exists
z\in E_{\sigma,t}
which solves (
2.14
) on the interval
(-\infty,t]
such that
z(t)=\eta
. Consider
z|_{(-\infty,\tau]}
. This function belongs to
E_{\sigma,\tau}
and solves
(
2.14
) on
(-\infty,\tau]
. Hence
\xi=z|_{(-\infty,\tau]}(\tau)\in\mathcal{M}(\tau)
. As
T(t,\tau)\xi=\eta
it follows that
\mathcal{M}(t)\subset T(t,\tau)\mathcal{M}(\tau)
and we have the negative invariance.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

Note that the results of this section imply that the invariant manifold is defined uniquely for a given family of projections
\{Q(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
for which we have the exponential splitting such that the gap condition (
2.15
) holds. Indeed, choosing the two exponents
\sigma_{1}<\sigma_{2}
for which the gap condition holds, we denote the two constructed manifolds by
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma_{1}}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma_{2}}
with the corresponding functions
\Sigma_{\sigma_{1}}
and
\Sigma_{\sigma_{2}}
. Pick
t\in\mathbb{R}
. By (
3.1
) we must have
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma_{1}}(t)\subset\mathcal{M}_{\sigma_{2}}(t)
. Suppose that
\eta\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma_{2}}(t)\setminus\mathcal{M}_{\sigma_{1}}(t)
. Consider
Q(t)\eta+\Sigma_{\sigma_{1}}(t,Q(t)\eta)
. This point belongs to
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma_{1}}(t)
and hence also to
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma_{2}}(t)
. But
\eta
is a unique point in
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma_{2}}(t)
whose projection
Q(t)
is equal to
Q(t)\eta
and hence
\eta=Q(t)\eta+\Sigma_{\sigma_{1}}(t,Q(t)\eta)\in\mathcal{M}_{\sigma_{1}}(t)
, a contradiction.

3.4.
Cone condition and Lipschitzness of
\Sigma

This subsection is devoted to the verification of the Lipschitzness of the function
\Sigma(t,\cdot)
that is used to define the manifold
\{\mathcal{M}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
. This will be done by means of the
cone condition
. Assume the gap condition (
2.15
) given in Definition
2.9
and let

\kappa=\frac{L_{2}}{L_{1}}\frac{\sigma-\rho}{\gamma-\sigma}.

(3.6)

We consider two solutions of (
2.14
), which we call
z,\tilde{z}
, defined on an interval
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathbb{R}
, which can be bounded or unbounded. We denote

q(t)=Q(t)z(t),\ p(t)=(I-Q(t))z(t),\ \tilde{q}(t)=Q(t)\tilde{z}(t),\ \tilde{p}(t)=(I-Q(t))\tilde{z}(t)\ \ \text{for}\ \ t\in\mathcal{T}.

Moreover, we set

u=q-\tilde{q},\ v=p-\tilde{p}.

Then
t\mapsto|u(t)|_{N(t)}
and
t\mapsto|v(t)|_{S(t)}
are continuous functions on
\mathcal{T}
.
Let

\zeta(t):=|v(t)|_{S(t)}-\kappa|u(t)|_{N(t)}\ \text{for}\ t\in\mathcal{T}.

(3.7)

We will study the behavior of the function
\zeta\colon\mathcal{T}\to\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 3.8
.

If the gap condition (
2.15
) holds then for any
r<\theta
,
r,\theta\in\mathcal{T}
if only
u(\theta)\neq 0
or
v(r)\neq 0
then

\zeta(t)\geqslant 0\ \ \text{ for }\ \ t\in[r,\theta]\ \ \text{ implies }\ \ \zeta(r)>0.

Proof.

Suppose contrary to the claim that
\zeta(r)=0
, that is,
|v(r)|_{S(r)}=\kappa|u(r)|_{N(r)}
. Subtracting the equations for
z
and
\tilde{z}
and applying the projection
Q(\theta)
, we obtain

u(t)=L(t,\theta)u(\theta)-\int_{t}^{\theta}L(t,s)Q(s)[f(s,z(s))-f(s,\tilde{z}(s))]ds\ \ \textrm{for}\ \ t\leqslant\theta,\ t,\theta\in\mathcal{T}.

Thus

|u(t)|_{N(t)}\leqslant e^{\rho(\theta-t)}|u(\theta)|_{N(\theta)}+L_{1}\int_{t}^{\theta}e^{\rho(s-t)}\Gamma(|u(s)|_{N(s)},|v(s)|_{S(s)})ds.

By assumption we obtain

|u(t)|_{N(t)}\leqslant e^{\rho(\theta-t)}|u(\theta)|_{N(\theta)}+L_{1}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa},1\right)\int_{t}^{\theta}e^{\rho(s-t)}|v(s)|_{S(s)}ds\ \ \text{for}\ \ t\in[r,\theta].

(3.8)

Applying the projection
I-Q(t)
to the difference of equations for
z
and
\tilde{z}
we get

v(t)=L(t,r)v(r)+\int_{r}^{t}L(t,s)(I-Q(s))[f(s,z(s))-f(s,\tilde{z}(s))]ds\ \ \text{for}\ \ t\in[r,\theta],

which by assumption leads to

|v(t)|_{S(t)}\leqslant e^{\gamma(r-t)}|v(r)|_{S(r)}+L_{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa},1\right)\int_{r}^{t}e^{\gamma(s-t)}|v(s)|_{S(s)}ds\ \ \text{for}\ \ t\in[r,\theta].

(3.9)

By the Gronwall inequality we obtain from the above bound

|v(t)|_{S(t)}\leqslant|v(r)|_{S(r)}e^{\left(\gamma-L_{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa},1\right)\right)(r-t)}\ \ \text{for}\ \ t\in[r,\theta].

(3.10)

Plugging (
3.10
) into (
3.8
) we derive

|u(t)|_{N(t)}\leqslant e^{\rho(\theta-t)}|u(\theta)|_{N(\theta)}+L_{1}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa},1\right)\int_{t}^{\theta}e^{\rho(s-t)}e^{\left(\gamma-L_{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa},1\right)\right)(r-s)}ds|v(r)|_{S(r)}\ \text{for}\ t\in[r,\theta].

Taking
t=r
we get

\displaystyle|u(r)|_{N(t)}\leqslant e^{\rho(\theta-r)}|u(\theta)|_{N(\theta)}+L_{1}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa},1\right)\int_{r}^{\theta}e^{\left(\gamma-\rho-L_{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa},1\right)\right)(r-s)}ds|v(r)|_{S(r)}

\displaystyle\ \ =e^{\rho(\theta-r)}|u(\theta)|_{N(\theta)}+\frac{L_{1}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa},1\right)}{\gamma-\rho-L_{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa},1\right)}\left(1-e^{\left(\gamma-\rho-L_{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa},1\right)\right)(r-\theta)}\right)|v(r)|_{S(r)}.

Since
|v(r)|_{S(r)}=\kappa|u(r)|_{N(r)}
, we obtain, by definition of
\kappa
,

\left(\frac{1}{\kappa}-\frac{L_{1}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa},1\right)}{\gamma-\rho-L_{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa},1\right)}\left(1-e^{\left(\gamma-\rho-L_{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa},1\right)\right)(r-\theta)}\right)\right)|v(r)|_{S(r)}\leqslant e^{\rho(\theta-r)}|u(\theta)|_{N(\theta)}.

(3.11)

It follows by (
3.10
) written for
t=\theta
that

\left(1-\frac{L_{1}\kappa\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa},1\right)}{\gamma-\rho-L_{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa},1\right)}\left(1-e^{\left(\gamma-\rho-L_{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa},1\right)\right)(r-\theta)}\right)\right)|v(\theta)|_{S(\theta)}\leqslant e^{\left(\gamma-\rho-L_{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa},1\right)\right)(r-\theta)}\kappa|u(\theta)|_{N(\theta)}.

(3.12)

Observe that

\gamma-\rho-L_{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa},1\right)=\gamma-\rho-(\gamma-\sigma)\Gamma\left(\frac{L_{1}}{\sigma-\rho},\frac{L_{2}}{\gamma-\sigma}\right)>0,

where the last inequality follows from the fact that
\rho<\sigma<\gamma
, the gap condition (
2.15
) and (
3.6
). Moreover, the same gap condition implies that

0<\frac{L_{1}\kappa\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa},1\right)}{\gamma-\rho-L_{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa},1\right)}=\frac{(\sigma-\rho)\Gamma\left(\frac{L_{1}}{\sigma-\rho},\frac{L_{2}}{\gamma-\sigma}\right)}{\gamma-\rho-(\gamma-\sigma)\Gamma\left(\frac{L_{1}}{\sigma-\rho},\frac{L_{2}}{\gamma-\sigma}\right)}<1.

We deduce that the parenthesis on the left-hand side of (
3.12
) is positive. Therefore, the fact that
\zeta(\theta)\geqslant 0
, i.e.,
|v(\theta)|_{S(\theta)}\geqslant\kappa|u(\theta)|_{N(\theta)}
, implies that

\left(1-\frac{L_{1}\kappa\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa},1\right)}{\gamma-\rho-L_{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa},1\right)}\left(1-e^{\left(\gamma-\rho-L_{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa},1\right)\right)(r-\theta)}\right)\right)\kappa|u(\theta)|_{N(\theta)}\leqslant e^{\left(\gamma-\rho-L_{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa},1\right)\right)(r-\theta)}\kappa|u(\theta)|_{N(\theta)}.

Hence

\left(1-\frac{L_{1}\kappa\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa},1\right)}{\gamma-\rho-L_{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa},1\right)}\right)\left(1-e^{\left(\gamma-\rho-L_{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa},1\right)\right)(r-\theta)}\right)\kappa|u(\theta)|_{N(\theta)}\leqslant 0.

This means that
u(\theta)=0
, and, by (
3.11
)
v(r)=0
, a contradiction.
∎

Second, an analogous result concerns the situation when

is nonpositive on a time interval. In such a case, it must be strictly negative at its end.

Lemma 3.9
.

If the gap condition (

if only

Proceeding analogously as in the proof of (

|u(t)|_{N(t)}\leqslant e^{\rho(\theta-t)}|u(\theta)|_{N(\theta)}+L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa)\int_{t}^{\theta}e^{\rho(s-t)}|u(s)|_{N(s)}ds\ \ \text{for}\ \ t\in[r,\theta].

which for

\displaystyle|v(\theta)|_{S(\theta)}\leqslant e^{\gamma(r-\theta)}|v(r)|_{S(r)}+L_{2}\Gamma(1,\kappa)\int_{r}^{\theta}e^{(\gamma-\rho-L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa))(s-\theta)}ds|u(\theta)|_{N(\theta)}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ =e^{\gamma(r-\theta)}|v(r)|_{S(r)}+\frac{L_{2}\Gamma(1,\kappa)}{\gamma-\rho-L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa)}\left(1-e^{(\gamma-\rho-L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa))(r-\theta)}\right)|u(\theta)|_{N(\theta)}.

.

∎

Proof.

The function

(i)

function

(ii)

function
\zeta
is positive in

(iii)

function
\zeta
has a unique zero

in

and
then
\zeta(t)>0
for

\zeta(t)<0

v(t)=L(t,r)v(r)+\int_{r}^{t}L(t,s)(I-Q(s))[f(s,z(s))-f(s,\tilde{z}(s))]ds\ \ \text{for}\ \ -\infty<r\leqslant t\leqslant t_{1},

As it is seen in the next result, the Lipschitz constant of the inertial manifold can be proved to be lower than it follows from the previous theorem.

Theorem 3.14
.

The function
\Sigma
defining the manifold
\{\mathcal{M}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is Lipschitz continuous,

|\Sigma(\tau,\eta)-\Sigma(\tau,\tilde{\eta})|_{S(\tau)}\leqslant\kappa_{1}|Q(\tau)(\eta-\tilde{\eta})|_{N(\tau)},\ \eta,\tilde{\eta}\in X,\ \tau\in\mathbb{R},

3.9

|u(t)|_{N(t)}\leqslant e^{\rho(\tau-t)}|Q(\tau)(\eta-\tilde{\eta})|_{N(\tau)}+L_{1}\int_{t}^{\tau}e^{\rho(s-t)}\left\|z(s)-\tilde{z}(s)\right\|_{s}ds\ \ \text{for}\ \ t\leqslant\tau.

Since by Lemma
3.5

z(s)
,
\tilde{z}(s)
are in the
\kappa
-Lipschitz manifold
\mathcal{M}(s)
and

, we get

|u(t)|_{N(t)}\leqslant e^{\rho(\tau-t)}|Q(\tau)(\eta-\tilde{\eta})|_{N(\tau)}+L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa)\int_{t}^{\tau}e^{\rho(s-t)}|u(s)|_{N(s)}ds\ \ \text{for}\ \ t\leqslant\tau

|u(t)|_{N(t)}\leqslant|Q(\tau)(\eta-\tilde{\eta})|_{N(\tau)}e^{(\rho+L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa))(\tau-t)}\ \ \text{for}\ \ t\leqslant\tau.

\displaystyle|\Sigma(\tau,\eta)-\Sigma(\tau,\tilde{\eta})|_{S(\tau)}\leqslant L_{2}\Gamma(1,\kappa)|Q(\tau)(\eta-\tilde{\eta})|_{N(\tau)}\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}e^{(\gamma-\rho-L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa))(s-\tau)}ds

\displaystyle\ \ =\frac{L_{2}\Gamma(1,\kappa)}{\gamma-\rho-L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa)}|Q(\tau)(\eta-\tilde{\eta})|_{N(\tau)}:=\kappa_{1}|Q(\tau)(\eta-\tilde{\eta})|_{N(\tau)}.

Corollary 3.15
.

The function
\Sigma
defining the manifold
\{\mathcal{M}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is Lipschitz continuous,

|\Sigma(\tau,\eta)-\Sigma(\tau,\tilde{\eta})|_{S(\tau)}\leqslant\kappa_{\Sigma}|Q(\tau)(\eta-\tilde{\eta})|_{N(\tau)},\ \eta,\tilde{\eta}\in X,\ \tau\in\mathbb{R},

∎

Observe that the equation (

\sigma
from the gap condition (

3.5.
Estimate of the distance to the manifold and its controlled growth.

\displaystyle q(t)=L(t,\tau)Q(\tau)\eta-\int_{t}^{\tau}L(t,s)Q(s)f(s,q(s)+\Sigma(s,q(s)))ds\ \ \text{for}\ \ t\in(-\infty,\tau],

(3.19)

\displaystyle\Sigma(t,q(t))=\int_{-\infty}^{t}L(t,s)(I-Q(s))f(s,q(s)+\Sigma(s,q(s)))ds\ \ \text{for}\ \ t\in\ \ (-\infty,\tau].

(3.20)

We continue by showing more properties of
\{\mathcal{M}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
defined in (
3.4
), stressing that the proofs of this subsection are largely analogous to those of
[
3
]
.

Given
(\tau,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}\times X
, we define the quantity

\chi(t)=T(t,\tau)\eta-P_{\Sigma}(t)T(t,\tau)\eta=(I-Q(t))T(t,\tau)\eta-\Sigma(t,Q(t)T(t,\tau)\eta)\in S(t)\ \ \text{for}\ \ t\geqslant\tau,

where
P_{\Sigma}(t)\colon X\to\mathcal{M}(t)
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
is defined in (
3.5
).

We carry out the fixed point procedure from
T(t,\tau)\eta
which yields the fixed point
z_{t,T(t,\tau)\eta}\in E_{\sigma,\tau}
.
For
t\geqslant\tau
we denote

q_{*}(s,t)=Q(s)z_{t,T(t,\tau)\eta}(s)\ \ \text{for}\ \ s\in(-\infty,t].

In particular, for
t=\tau
and
s\in(-\infty,\tau]
we have
q_{*}(s,\tau)=q(s)
for
s\in(-\infty,\tau]
. We start from deriving two auxiliary estimates that will be useful in the estimation of the distance of solutions to the invariant manifold.

Lemma 3.16
.

We have

\displaystyle|q_{*}(s,t)-Q(s)T(s,\tau)\eta|_{N(s)}\leqslant L_{1}\Gamma(0,1)\int_{s}^{t}|\chi(r)|_{S(r)}e^{(\rho+L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma}))(r-s)}dr\ \ \text{for}\ \ s\in[\tau,t],

(3.21)

\displaystyle|q_{*}(s,t)-q_{*}(s,\tau)|_{N(s)}\leqslant L_{1}\Gamma(0,1)\int_{\tau}^{t}|\chi(r)|_{S(r)}e^{(\rho+L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma}))(r-s)}dr\ \ \text{for}\ \ s\in(-\infty,\tau].

(3.22)

Proof.

The function
q_{*}(s,t)
defined for
s\in(-\infty,t]
satisfies the equation

\displaystyle q_{*}(s,t)=L(s,t)Q(t)T(t,\tau)\eta-\int_{s}^{t}L(s,r)Q(r)f(r,q_{*}(r,t)+\Sigma(r,q_{*}(r,t)))dr

\displaystyle=L(s,\tau)Q(\tau)\eta+\int_{\tau}^{t}L(s,r)Q(r)f(r,T(r,\tau)\eta)dr-\int_{s}^{t}L(s,r)Q(r)f(r,q_{*}(r,t)+\Sigma(r,q_{*}(r,t)))dr.

Therefore, for
s\in[\tau,t]
we have

q_{*}(s,t)-Q(s)T(s,\tau)\eta=\int_{s}^{t}L(s,r)Q(r)[f(r,T(r,\tau)\eta)-f(r,q_{*}(r,t)+\Sigma(r,q_{*}(r,t)))]dr.

We continue by estimating for
s\in[\tau,t]

\displaystyle e^{\rho s}|q_{*}(s,t)-Q(s)T(s,\tau)\eta|_{N(s)}

\displaystyle\ \ \leqslant L_{1}\int_{s}^{t}e^{\rho r}\left\|Q(r)T(r,\tau)\eta+(I-Q(r))T(r,\tau)\eta-q_{*}(r,t)-\Sigma(r,q_{*}(r,t)))\right\|_{r}dr

\displaystyle=L_{1}\int_{s}^{t}e^{\rho r}\Gamma(|Q(r)T(r,\tau)\eta-q_{*}(r,t)|_{N(r)},|(I-Q(r))T(r,\tau)\eta-\Sigma(r,q_{*}(r,t))|_{S(r)})\,dr.

But we have

\displaystyle|(I-Q(r))T(r,\tau)\eta-\Sigma(r,q_{*}(r,t))|_{S(r)}

\displaystyle\leqslant|(I-Q(r))T(r,\tau)\eta-\Sigma(r,Q(r)T(r,\tau)\eta)|_{S(r)}+|\Sigma(r,Q(r)T(r,\tau)\eta)-\Sigma(r,q_{*}(r,t))|_{S(r)}

\displaystyle\leqslant|\chi(r)|_{S(r)}+\kappa_{\Sigma}|Q(r)T(r,\tau)\eta-q_{*}(r,t)|_{N(r)}.

Hence we obtain

\displaystyle e^{\rho s}|q_{*}(s,t)-Q(s)T(s,\tau)\eta|_{N(s)}

\displaystyle\leqslant L_{1}\int_{s}^{t}e^{\rho r}\Gamma(|Q(r)T(r,\tau)\eta-q_{*}(r,t)|_{N(r)},|\chi(r)|_{S(r)}+\kappa_{\Sigma}|Q(r)T(r,\tau)\eta-q_{*}(r,t)|_{N(r)})dr

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{s}^{t}L_{1}\Gamma(0,1)e^{\rho r}|\chi(r)|_{S(r)}+L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma})e^{\rho r}|q_{*}(r,t)-Q(r)T(r,\tau)\eta|_{N(r)}dr.

By the Gronwall inequality (see Lemma
2.13
) we get (
3.21
). To prove (
3.22
) observe that for
s\in(-\infty,\tau]
we have

q_{*}(s,t)=L(s,\tau)q_{*}(\tau,t)-\int_{s}^{\tau}L(s,r)Q(r)f(r,q_{*}(r,t)+\Sigma(r,q_{*}(r,t)))dr,

and

\displaystyle q_{*}(s,t)-q_{*}(s,\tau)=L(s,\tau)[q_{*}(\tau,t)-Q(\tau)\eta]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ -\int_{s}^{\tau}L(s,r)Q(r)[f(r,q_{*}(r,t)+\Sigma(r,q_{*}(r,t)))-f(r,q_{*}(r,\tau)+\Sigma(r,q_{*}(r,\tau)))]dr.

Thus we get for
s\in(-\infty,\tau]

\displaystyle e^{\rho s}|q_{*}(s,t)-q_{*}(s,\tau)|_{N(s)}\leqslant e^{\rho\tau}|q_{*}(\tau,t)-Q(\tau)\eta|_{N(\tau)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+L_{1}\int_{s}^{\tau}e^{\rho r}\left\|q_{*}(r,t)+\Sigma(r,q_{*}(r,t))-q_{*}(r,\tau)-\Sigma(r,q_{*}(r,\tau))\right\|_{r}dr

\displaystyle\ =e^{\rho\tau}|q_{*}(\tau,t)-Q(\tau)\eta|_{N(\tau)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+L_{1}\int_{s}^{\tau}e^{\rho r}\Gamma(|q_{*}(r,t)-q_{*}(r,\tau)|_{N(r)},|\Sigma(r,q_{*}(r,t))-\Sigma(r,q_{*}(r,\tau))|_{S(r)})dr

\displaystyle\ \leqslant L_{1}\Gamma(0,1)\int_{\tau}^{t}e^{\rho r}|\chi(r)|_{S(r)}e^{L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma})(r-\tau)}dr+L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma})\int_{s}^{\tau}e^{\rho r}|q_{*}(r,t)-q_{*}(r,\tau)|_{N(r)}dr,

where, in the last estimate, we have used (
3.21
). By the Gronwall lemma we obtain (
3.22
).
∎

We are in position to derive the bound on the evolution of the error between the solution of the problem and its nonlinear projection
P_{\Sigma}
onto the invariant manifold.

Lemma 3.17
.

We have

|T(t,\tau)\eta-P_{\Sigma}(t)T(t,\tau)\eta|_{S(t)}\leqslant|\eta-P_{\Sigma}(\tau)\eta|_{S(\tau)}e^{\bigl(-\gamma+\frac{(\gamma-\rho)L_{2}\Gamma(0,1)}{\gamma-\rho-L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma})}\bigr)(t-\tau)}\ \ \text{for}\ \ t\geqslant\tau,\ \eta\in X.

(3.23)

Proof.

By (
3.20
) we have for
t\geqslant\tau

\displaystyle\chi(t)-L(t,\tau)\chi(\tau)=(I-Q(t))[T(t,\tau)\eta-L(t,\tau)\eta]+L(t,\tau)\Sigma(\tau,Q(\tau)\eta)-\Sigma(t,Q(t)T(t,\tau)\eta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\tau}^{t}L(t,s)(I-Q(s))f(s,T(s,\tau)\eta)ds+\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}L(t,s)(I-Q(s))f(s,q_{*}(s,\tau)+\Sigma(s,q_{*}(s,\tau))ds

\displaystyle\ \ -\int_{-\infty}^{t}L(t,s)(I-Q(s))f(s,q_{*}(s,t)+\Sigma(s,q_{*}(s,t)))ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\tau}^{t}L(t,s)(I-Q(s))[f(s,T(s,\tau)\eta)-f(s,q_{*}(s,t)+\Sigma(s,q_{*}(s,t)))]ds

\displaystyle\ \ +\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}L(t,s)(I-Q(s))[f(s,q_{*}(s,\tau)+\Sigma(s,q_{*}(s,\tau)))-f(s,q_{*}(s,t)+\Sigma(s,q_{*}(s,t)))]ds.

Estimating, we obtain

\displaystyle e^{\gamma t}|\chi(t)-L(t,\tau)\chi(\tau)|_{S(t)}

\displaystyle\ \ \leqslant L_{2}\int_{\tau}^{t}e^{\gamma s}\left\|T(s,\tau)\eta-q_{*}(s,t)-\Sigma(s,q_{*}(s,t))\right\|_{s}ds

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+L_{2}\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma})\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}e^{\gamma s}|q_{*}(s,t)-q_{*}(s,\tau)|_{N(s)}ds

\displaystyle\ \ \leqslant L_{2}\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma})\int_{\tau}^{t}e^{\gamma s}|Q(s)T(s,\tau)\eta-q_{*}(s,t)|_{N(s)}ds

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+L_{2}\Gamma(0,1)\int_{\tau}^{t}e^{\gamma s}|(I-Q(s))T(s,\tau)\eta-\Sigma(s,Q(s)T(s,\tau)\eta)|_{S(s)}ds

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+L_{2}\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma})\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}e^{\gamma s}|q_{*}(s,t)-q_{*}(s,\tau)|_{N(s)}ds.

Therefore we get by (
3.21
) and (
3.22
)

\displaystyle e^{\gamma t}|\chi(t)-L(t,\tau)\chi(\tau)|_{S(t)}

\displaystyle\leqslant L_{2}\Gamma(0,1)\int_{\tau}^{t}e^{\gamma s}|\chi(s)|_{S(s)}ds+L_{1}L_{2}\Gamma(0,1)\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma})\int_{\tau}^{t}\int_{s}^{t}e^{\gamma s}|\chi(r)|_{S(r)}e^{(\rho+L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma}))(r-s)}drds

\displaystyle\ \ +L_{1}L_{2}\Gamma(0,1)\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma})\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}\int_{\tau}^{t}e^{\gamma s}|\chi(r)|_{S(r)}e^{(\rho+L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma}))(r-s)}drds

\displaystyle=L_{2}\Gamma(0,1)\int_{\tau}^{t}e^{\gamma s}|\chi(s)|_{S(s)}ds

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ +L_{1}L_{2}\Gamma(0,1)\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma})\int_{\tau}^{t}|\chi(r)|_{S(r)}e^{(\rho+L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma}))r}\int_{-\infty}^{r}e^{(\gamma-\rho-L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma}))s}dsdr

\displaystyle=L_{2}\Gamma(0,1)\int_{\tau}^{t}e^{\gamma r}|\chi(r)|_{S(r)}dr+\frac{L_{1}L_{2}\Gamma(0,1)\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma})}{\gamma-\rho-L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma})}\int_{\tau}^{t}e^{\gamma r}|\chi(r)|_{S(r)}dr\ \ \text{for}\ \ t\geqslant\tau,

since
\gamma-\rho>L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma})
.
This yields

\displaystyle e^{\gamma t}|\chi(t)|_{S(t)}\leqslant e^{\gamma\tau}|\chi(\tau)|_{S(\tau)}+\left(L_{2}+\frac{L_{1}L_{2}\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma})}{\gamma-\rho-L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma})}\right)\Gamma(0,1)\int_{\tau}^{t}e^{\gamma r}|\chi(r)|_{S(r)}dr

\displaystyle=e^{\gamma\tau}|\chi(\tau)|_{S(\tau)}+\frac{(\gamma-\rho)L_{2}\Gamma(0,1)}{\gamma-\rho-L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma})}\int_{\tau}^{t}e^{\gamma r}|\chi(r)|_{S(r)}dr\ \ \text{for}\ t\geqslant\tau.

The Gronwall inequality yields

|\chi(t)|_{S(t)}\leqslant|\chi(\tau)|_{S(\tau)}e^{\bigl(-\gamma+\frac{(\gamma-\rho)L_{2}\Gamma(0,1)}{\gamma-\rho-L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma})}\bigr)(t-\tau)}\ \ \text{for}\ \ t\geqslant\tau,

and the proof is complete.
∎

The next result, in addition to providing the estimate on the distance of solutions to the manifold, yields the criterion under which it is attracting, that is, when this manifold is inertial.

Corollary 3.18
.

We have

|T(t,\tau)\eta-P_{\Sigma}(t)T(t,\tau)\eta|_{S(t)}\leqslant|\eta-P_{\Sigma}(\tau)\eta|_{S(\tau)}e^{-\omega(t-\tau)}\ \ \text{for}\ \ t\geqslant\tau,\ \eta\in X,

(3.24)

or, in the original norm of
X
,

\left\|T(t,\tau)\eta-P_{\Sigma}(t)T(t,\tau)\eta\right\|\leqslant M(1+\kappa_{\Sigma})\left\|\eta\right\|e^{-\omega(t-\tau)}\ \ \text{for}\ \ \ t\geqslant\tau,\ \eta\in X,

(3.25)

where

\omega=\gamma-\frac{(\gamma-\rho)L_{2}\Gamma(0,1)}{\gamma-\rho-L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma})}

(3.26)

with the Lipschitz constant
\kappa_{\Sigma}
of
\{\mathcal{M}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
. Moreover,
\omega
belongs to the interval
(\rho,\gamma)
.
In particular, given a bounded set
G\subset X
there exists
c_{G}>0
such that

\operatorname{dist}(T(t,\tau)G,\mathcal{M}(t))\leqslant c_{G}e^{-\omega(t-\tau)},\ t\geqslant\tau.

If
\omega>0
, then
\{\mathcal{M}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is an inertial manifold for the process.

Proof.

The bound (
3.24
) follows directly from Lemma
3.17
. The estimate (
3.25
) follows from (
3.24
), (
2.6
) and (
2.7
), since

|\eta-P_{\Sigma}(\tau)\eta|_{S(\tau)}\leqslant|(I-Q(\tau))\eta|_{S(\tau)}+|\Sigma(\tau,Q(\tau)\eta)-\Sigma(\tau,0)|_{S(\tau)}\leqslant(1+\kappa_{\Sigma})M\left\|\eta\right\|.

Recalling (
3.18
), we have

L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma})+L_{2}\Gamma(0,1)<L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma})+L_{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\kappa_{\Sigma}},1\right)=\gamma-\rho.

This means that

\omega=\gamma-\frac{(\gamma-\rho)L_{2}\Gamma(0,1)}{\gamma-\rho-L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma})}>\gamma-\frac{(\gamma-\rho)L_{2}\Gamma(0,1)}{L_{2}\Gamma(0,1)}=\rho,

and the assertion follows.
∎

The last property of
\{\mathcal{M}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is the control of the growth of the solutions that lie on it. To this end, let
\eta=Q(\tau)\eta+\Sigma(\tau,Q(\tau)\eta)\in\mathcal{M}(\tau)
. We define

z(s)=\begin{cases}T(s,\tau)\eta=Q(s)T(s,\tau)\eta+\Sigma(s,Q(s)T(s,\tau)\eta)&\ \text{if}\ s\geqslant\tau,\\
q_{\tau,\eta}(s)+\Sigma(s,q_{\tau,\eta}(s))=z_{\tau,\eta}(s)&\ \text{if}\ -\infty<s\leqslant\tau,\end{cases}

(3.27)

with
q_{\tau,\eta}(s)=Q(s)z_{\tau,\eta}(s)
for
s\leqslant\tau
. Note that the formula for
T(s,\tau)\eta
,
s\geqslant\tau
, is a consequence of
(
3.23
), since
\eta\in\mathcal{M}(\tau)
.

Lemma 3.19
.

The function
z\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
given in (
3.27
) is a unique global solution of (
2.14
) through
\eta\in\mathcal{M}(\tau)
within
\{\mathcal{M}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
, that is,

z(t)\in\mathcal{M}(t)
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
z(\tau)=\eta
and

T(t,s)z(s)=z(t)\ \ \text{for every}\ \ t\geqslant s.

Moreover,
z
has controlled growth in the past, that is, we have

\left\|z(s)\right\|_{s}\leqslant\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma})e^{(\rho+L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma}))(\tau-s)}|Q(\tau)\eta|_{N(\tau)}\ \ \text{for}\ \ s\in(-\infty,\tau],

(3.28)

or, in the original norm of
X
,

\left\|z(s)\right\|\leqslant Mc_{\Gamma}\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma})e^{(\rho+L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma}))(\tau-s)}\left\|\eta\right\|\ \ \text{for every}\ \ s\in(-\infty,\tau].

Proof.

Consider the interval
[s,t]
. If
\tau\leqslant s
, then

T(t,s)z(s)=T(t,s)T(s,\tau)\eta=T(t,\tau)\eta=z(t).

On the other hand, if
\tau\geqslant t
, by Lemma
3.4
we have

T(t,s)z(s)=z(t).

Finally, if
\tau\in(s,t)
, then we have

T(t,s)z(s)=T(t,\tau)T(\tau,s)z(s)=T(t,\tau)z(\tau)=T(t,\tau)\eta=z(t).

To prove the bound, we write down the projection of
z_{\tau,\eta}(s)
onto
N(s)
for
s\leqslant\tau
and estimate

e^{\rho s}|Q(s)z_{\tau,\eta}(s)|_{N(s)}\leqslant e^{\rho\tau}|Q(\tau)\eta|_{N(\tau)}+L_{1}\int_{s}^{\tau}e^{\rho r}\Gamma(|Q(r)z_{\tau,\eta}(r)|_{N(r)},|\Sigma(r,Q(r)z_{\tau,\eta}(r))|_{S(r)})dr.

Since
\Sigma
is
\kappa_{\Sigma}
-Lipschitz, by the Gronwall inequality we get

|Q(s)z_{\tau,\eta}(s)|_{N(s)}\leqslant e^{(\rho+L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma}))(\tau-s)}|Q(\tau)\eta|_{N(\tau)}\ \ \text{for}\ \ s\leqslant\tau.

We have for
s\leqslant\tau

\left\|z(s)\right\|_{s}=\Gamma(|Q(s)z_{\tau,\eta}(s)|_{N(s)},|\Sigma(s,Q(s)z_{\tau,\eta}(s))|_{S(s)})\leqslant\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma})|Q(s)z_{\tau,\eta}(s)|_{N(s)}.

Thus

\left\|z(s)\right\|_{s}\leqslant\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma})e^{(\rho+L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma}))(\tau-s)}|Q(\tau)\eta|_{N(\tau)}\ \ \text{for}\ \ s\in(-\infty,\tau],

and the assertion follows.
∎

Remark 3.20
.

Note that
\rho+L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma})<\rho+L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa)<\sigma
, and hence the above bound gives us better control than merely the fact that the fixed point belongs to the space

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Remark 3.21
.

If
\Gamma
is the maximum norm, the conditions and constants in Theorem

are getting simpler.

Remark 3.22
.

If
\Gamma(a_{1},a_{2})=|a_{1}|+|a_{2}|

If

, then this Lipschitz constant is equal to

\kappa_{\Sigma}=\frac{\frac{\gamma-\rho}{L}-2-\sqrt{\left(\frac{\gamma-\rho}{L}-2\right)^{2}-4}}{2}=\frac{2}{\frac{\gamma-\rho}{L}-2+\sqrt{\left(\frac{\gamma-\rho}{L}-2\right)^{2}-4}}<1.

4.
Stable manifold of the invariant Manifold

We now establish the existence of a stable manifold associated with the invariant manifold. This concept was introduced in
[
3
]
as the complementary manifold of the inertial. Here, we prove its existence based on the refined gap condition (

3.1

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that the linear abstract Cauchy problem (

Introducing the spaces

and

for
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, endowed with the norms

and
|\cdot|_{S(\tau)}
given by (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), respectively,
and equipping

with the equivalent norms
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\tau}
given in (
2.8
) for a chosen admissible norm
\Gamma
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
(Definition
2.5
), we further assume that a continuous function

satisfies (
2.9
) and the uniform global Lipschitz conditions
(
2.10
) and (
2.11
) with constants
L_{1},L_{2}>0
.

Consider the perturbed abstract Cauchy problem (
2.13
) and assume that it generates an evolution process
\{T(t,\tau)\colon t\geqslant\tau\}
in
X
, which satisfies the variation of constants formula (
2.14
).
Finally, assume that the gap condition (
2.15
) holds, that is,

\Gamma\left(\frac{L_{1}}{\sigma-\rho},\frac{L_{2}}{\gamma-\sigma}\right)<1\ \ \text{for some}\ \ \sigma\in(\rho,\gamma).

Then, there exists a family of sets
\{\mathcal{S}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
, the positively invariant stable manifold complementary to
\{\mathcal{M}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
, given by

\mathcal{S}(t)=\left\{\eta\in X\colon\sup_{r\geqslant{t}}\{e^{\sigma r}\|T(r,t)\eta\|_{r}\}<\infty\right\},\ {t\in\mathbb{R}}.

(4.1)

Moreover,
\mathcal{S}(t)=P_{\Theta}(t)X
,
where
P_{\Theta}(t)u=\Theta(t,u)+(I-Q(t))u
for every
(t,u)\in\mathbb{R}\times X
, and
\Theta\colon\mathbb{R}\times X\to X
is a function such that

(1)

\Theta(t,u)=\Theta(t,(I-Q(t))u)=Q(t)\Theta(t,u)
,
and
\Theta(t,0)=0
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

(2)

|\Theta(t,\eta)-\Theta(t,\tilde{\eta})|_{N(t)}\leqslant\kappa_{\Theta}|(I-Q(t))(\eta-{\tilde{\eta}})|_{S(t)}
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\eta,\tilde{\eta}\in X
with a constant
0<\kappa_{\Theta}<\frac{L_{1}}{L_{2}}\frac{\gamma-\sigma}{\sigma-\rho}
satisfying

\gamma-\rho=L_{1}\Gamma\left(1,\frac{1}{\kappa_{\Theta}}\right)+L_{2}\Gamma(\kappa_{\Theta},1).

(4.2)

In addition, we have the following controlled growth estimate
and

\|T(t,\tau)P_{\Theta}(\tau)u\|_{t}\leqslant\frac{\Gamma(\kappa_{\Theta},1)}{\Gamma(0,1)}e^{-(\gamma-L_{2}\Gamma(\kappa_{\Theta},1))(t-\tau)}\|P_{\Theta}(\tau)u\|_{\tau}\ \ \text{for every}\ \ t\geqslant\tau,\ u\in X.

(4.3)

Proof.

Existence and properties of
\Theta
are obtained using the argument which is complementary to the proof of Theorem
3.1
for existence of
\Sigma
.
First, choose
\sigma\in(\rho,\gamma)
such that the gap condition (
2.15
) holds. Suppose that
\eta\in X
and
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
are such that
\sup_{t\geqslant\tau}\{e^{\sigma t}\|T(t,\tau)\eta\|_{t}\}
is bounded.
Then, if
z(t)=T(t,\tau)\eta
we denote
q(t):=Q(t)z(t)
and
p(t):=(I-Q(t))z(t)
. Our aim is to construct the function
\Theta\colon\mathbb{R}\times X\to X
such that
q(t)=\Theta(t,p(t))
for all
t\geqslant\tau
. The function
q
satisfies the following Duhamel formula

q(r)=L(r,t)q(t)+\int_{t}^{r}L(r,s)Q(s)f(s,z(s))ds\ \ \text{for}\ \ r\geqslant t\geqslant\tau.

Applying the inverse operator
L(t,r)
to the above equation, we obtain

q(t)=L(t,r)q(r)-\int_{t}^{r}L(t,s)Q(s)f(s,z(s))ds\ \ \text{for}\ \ r\geqslant t\geqslant\tau.

Since
e^{\sigma r}\|z(r)\|_{r}
is bounded for
r\geqslant t
, we deduce that
e^{\rho r}\|z(r)\|_{r}\to 0
as
r\to\infty
, whence

q(t)=-\int_{t}^{\infty}L(t,s)Q(s)f(s,z(s))ds,\ t\geqslant\tau.

Therefore, if we are able to find a continuous function
z\colon[\tau,+\infty)\to X
satisfying

z(t)=-\int_{t}^{\infty}L(t,s)Q(s)f(s,z(s))ds+L(t,\tau)(I-Q(\tau))\eta+\int_{\tau}^{t}L(t,s)(I-Q(s))f(s,z(s))ds,

then, analogously to Lemma
3.4
,
z
is a solution of (
2.14
) on
[\tau,\infty)
with
(I-Q(\tau))z(\tau)={(I-Q(\tau))\eta}
, and the candidate for the sought function
\Theta
is given as
\Theta(\tau,\eta):=Q(\tau)z(\tau)
. Then, if we define the nonlinear projection as
P_{\Theta}(\tau)\eta=\Theta(\tau,\eta)+(I-Q(\tau))\eta
, the image of this projection
P_{\Theta}(\tau)X
is the complementary manifold of the invariant manifold
\mathcal{M}(\tau)
.
Indeed, immediately, we get

\Theta(\tau,{(I-Q(\tau))\eta})=\Theta(\tau,\eta)=Q(\tau)\Theta(\tau,\eta)
.

The function
z
is constructed using a fixed point argument, which follows the lines of the argument of Subsection
3.2
.
Given
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and
\sigma\in(\rho,\gamma)
such that (
2.15
) holds we define the space

F_{\sigma,\tau}=\{z\in C([\tau,{\infty});X)\colon\|z\|_{F_{\sigma,\tau}}=\sup_{t\geqslant\tau}e^{\sigma(t-\tau)}\|z(t)\|_{t}<\infty\},

which is a Banach space with
the norm
\|\cdot\|_{F_{\sigma,\tau}}
.
Choosing
\eta\in X
,
z\in F_{\sigma,\tau}
we define

(Hz)(t)=-\int_{t}^{\infty}L(t,s)Q(s)f(s,z(s))ds+L(t,\tau)(I-Q(\tau))\eta+\int_{\tau}^{t}L(t,s)(I-Q(s))f(s,z(s))ds

for
t\in[\tau,\infty)
. We prove that
H
is a contraction from
F_{\sigma,\tau}
to itself. To this end, we must estimate the quantity

\|(Hz)(t)\|_{t}=\Gamma(|Q(t)(Hz)(t)|_{N(t)},|(I-Q(t))(Hz)(t)|_{S(t)}),

where

Q(t)(Hz)(t)=-\int_{t}^{\infty}L(t,s)Q(s)f(s,z(s))ds,

and

(I-Q(t))(Hz)(t)=L(t,\tau)(I-Q(\tau))\eta+\int_{\tau}^{t}L(t,s)(I-Q(s))f(s,z(s))ds.

We have

\left|\int_{t}^{\infty}L(t,s)Q(s)f(s,z(s))ds\right|_{N(t)}\leqslant L_{1}\int_{t}^{{\infty}}e^{\rho(s-t)}\|z(s)\|_{s}\,ds\leqslant\|z\|_{F_{\sigma,\tau}}L_{1}\int_{t}^{{\infty}}e^{\rho(s-t)-\sigma(s-\tau)}\,ds,

and

\begin{split}|(I-Q(t))(Hz)(t)|_{S(t)}&\leqslant|L(t,\tau)(I-Q(\tau))\eta|_{S(t)}+\int_{\tau}^{t}|L(t,s)(I-Q(s))f(s,z(s))|_{S(t)}ds\\
&\leqslant e^{\gamma(\tau-t)}|(I-Q(\tau))\eta|_{S(t)}+L_{2}\int_{\tau}^{t}e^{\gamma(s-t)}\|z(s)\|_{s}\,ds\\
&\leqslant e^{\gamma(\tau-t)}|(I-Q(\tau))\eta|_{S(t)}+L_{2}\|z\|_{F_{\sigma,\tau}}\int_{\tau}^{t}e^{\gamma(s-t)-\sigma(s-\tau)}\,ds.\end{split}

Hence, for
t\geqslant\tau
,

\displaystyle e^{\sigma(t-\tau)}\|(Hz)(t)\|_{t}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\leqslant}\Gamma\bigg(\|z\|_{F_{\sigma,\tau}}\frac{L_{1}}{\sigma-\rho},e^{(t-\tau)(\sigma-\gamma)}|{(I-Q(\tau))\eta}|_{S(\tau)}+\|z\|_{F_{\sigma,\tau}}\frac{L_{2}(1-e^{(\sigma-\gamma)(t-\tau)})}{\gamma-\sigma}\bigg),

\displaystyle\leqslant\Gamma\bigg(\|z\|_{F_{\sigma,\tau}}\frac{L_{1}}{\sigma-\rho},|(I-Q(\tau))\eta|_{S(\tau)}+\|z\|_{F_{\sigma,\tau}}\frac{L_{2}}{\gamma-\sigma}\bigg).

Therefore, we have
Hz\in F_{\sigma,\tau}
.
Similar computations lead to

e^{\sigma(t-\tau)}\|(Hz_{1})(t)-(Hz_{2})(t)\|_{t}\leqslant\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{F_{\sigma,\tau}}\Gamma\bigg(\frac{L_{1}}{\sigma-\rho},\frac{L_{2}}{\gamma-\sigma}\bigg)

for
z_{1},z_{2}\in F_{\sigma,\tau}
, and from the gap condition (
2.15
), the operator
H
has a unique fixed point

.
Define

\Theta(\tau,\eta):=-\int_{\tau}^{\infty}L(\tau,s)Q(s)f(s,z^{*}(s))ds\ \ \text{for every}\ \ (\tau,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}\times X.

An argument that follows the lines of Lemma
3.5
implies that
P_{\Theta}(t)X=\mathcal{S}(t)
for every

with
\mathcal{S}(t)
given by (
4.1
), and the family
\{\mathcal{S}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is positively invariant.

To prove Lipschitzness, pick
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
,
\eta,\tilde{\eta}\in X
and consider solutions
z,\tilde{z}:[\tau,\infty)\to X
such that
z(\tau)=P_{\Theta}(\tau)\eta
and
\tilde{z}(\tau)=P_{\Theta}(\tau)\tilde{\eta}
. Picking
\kappa=\frac{L_{2}}{L_{1}}\frac{\sigma-\rho}{\gamma-\sigma}
as in (

)
we will
study the function

and we will prove that

, i.e.,

|\Theta(\tau,\eta)-\Theta(\tau,\tilde{\eta})|_{N(t)}\leqslant\frac{1}{\kappa}|(I-Q(\tau))(\eta-\tilde{\eta})|_{S(t)}.

Without loss of generality we may assume that
(I-Q(\tau))(\eta-\tilde{\eta})\neq 0

3.11
we only have to exclude the case where
\zeta(t)<0
for every
t\in[\tau,\infty)
. We skip the argument to obtain this assertion as it is based on the standard estimate for the equation

u(t)=L(t,s)u(s)-\int_{t}^{s}L(t,r)Q(r)(f(r,z(r))-f(r,\tilde{z}(r))\,dr\ \ \text{for}\ \ \tau\leqslant t\leqslant s<\infty

3.12
.

Following the lines of the argument of Theorem
3.14
and Corollary
3.15

can be made to satisfy

\frac{L_{1}\Gamma(\kappa_{\Theta},1)}{\gamma-\rho-L_{2}\Gamma(\kappa_{\Theta},1)}=\kappa_{\Theta}.

Finally, we prove (
4.3
). Let
u\in X
and let
\eta=P_{\Theta}(\tau)u\in\mathcal{S}(\tau)
.
Denoting
p(t)=(I-Q(t))T(t,\tau)\eta
for every

|p(t)|_{S(t)}\leqslant e^{\gamma(\tau-t)}|(I-Q(\tau))\eta|_{{S(\tau)}}+L_{2}\Gamma(\kappa_{\Theta},1)\int_{\tau}^{t}e^{\gamma(r-t)}|p(r)|_{S(r)}\,dr.

Applying the Gronwall inequality
to the last bound, we obtain

|p(t)|_{S(t)}\leqslant e^{-(\gamma-L_{2}\Gamma(\kappa_{\Theta},1))(t-\tau)}|(I-Q(\tau))\eta|_{S(\tau)},\ t\geqslant\tau.

Since
\|T(t,\tau)\eta\|_{t}=\|P_{\Theta}(t)p(t)\|_{t}=\Gamma(|\Theta(t,p(t))|_{N(t)},|p(t)|_{S(t)})\leqslant\Gamma(\kappa_{\Theta},1)|p(t)|_{S(t)}
,
we obtain (
4.3
) and the proof is complete.
∎

The above result establishes the existence of a stable manifold associated with the zero solution. However, as noted in
[
3
]
, it is also possible to prove the existence of stable manifolds associated with any global solution lying on the inertial manifold. More precisely, since
\mathcal{M}
can be characterized as the union of global solutions, one can construct, for each such solution, a corresponding stable manifold, as we describe in the following definition.

Definition 4.2
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
4.1
, there exists an invariant manifold
\{\mathcal{M}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
.
Let
\xi\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
be a global solution such that

\xi(t)\in\mathcal{M}(t)
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
The
stable manifold of the invariant manifold along
\xi
is a family
\{\mathcal{S}_{\xi}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
such that

(1)

\mathcal{S}_{\xi}(t)
is a Lipschitz manifold for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
;

(2)

\{\mathcal{S}_{\xi}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is positively invariant, i.e.,
T(t,\tau)\mathcal{S}_{\xi}(\tau)\subset\mathcal{S}_{\xi}(t)
, for
t\geqslant\tau
;

(3)

there exists
\delta\in(\rho,\gamma)
such that, for every
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and
\eta\in S_{\xi}(\tau)
, there exists
K=K(\tau,\eta)>0
such that

\|T(t,\tau)\eta-\xi(t)\|\leqslant Ke^{-\delta(t-\tau)},\ t\geqslant\tau.

As described in
[
3
, Remark 2.4]
, it is possible to use Theorem
4.1
to obtain the stable manifold along any global solution in the inertial manifold.
Indeed, for a given global solution
\xi\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
, define

\tilde{f}(t,v)=f(t,v+\xi(t))-f(t,\xi(t)),\ (t,v)\in\mathbb{R}\times X.

Then
\tilde{f}
is globally Lipschitz and
\tilde{f}(t,0)=0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and we apply Theorem
4.1
to obtain the stable manifold
\mathcal{S}_{0,\tilde{f}}
along zero solution for
\tilde{f}
.
Then the stable manifold
\mathcal{S}_{\xi,f}
along
\xi
for the problem with
f
is obtained by
\mathcal{S}_{\xi,f}(t)=\xi(t)+\mathcal{S}_{0,\tilde{f}}(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Remark 4.3
.

Continuing the considerations of Remark
3.21
when
\Gamma
is the maximum norm on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we see that

\kappa_{\Theta,i}=\frac{1}{\kappa_{\Sigma,i}}
,
i=1,2

), that is,

\gamma-\rho=L_{1}\Gamma\left(1,\frac{1}{\kappa_{\Theta,i}}\right)+L_{2}\Gamma\left(\kappa_{\Theta,i},1\right),\ i=1,2.

Since

, we deduce that
the Lipschitz constant in Theorem
4.1
is then equal to

\kappa_{\Theta}=\kappa_{\Theta,2}=\frac{L_{1}}{\gamma-\rho-L_{2}}<1.

Moreover, for any
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and
\eta\in\mathcal{S}(\tau)
the solution

for

belongs to

.

5.
Differentiable invariant and inertial manifolds

We maintain the assumptions of previous sections. In this setting we prove in this section that the function
\Sigma(t,\eta)
that defines the inertial manifold is differentiable with respect to the variable
\eta
provided that
f(t,\cdot)
is Fréchet differentiable.
To this end, we additionally assume that
f\colon\mathbb{R}\times X\to X
is Fréchet differentiable w.r.t. the second variable and we denote its Fréchet derivative
Df(t,u)\in\mathcal{L}(X,X)
,
(t,u)\in\mathbb{R}\times X
, that is,

f(t,u+h)-f(t,u)-Df(t,u)h=\varepsilon(t,u,h)\left\|h\right\|_{t}\ \ \text{for}\ \ \ t\in\mathbb{R},\ u,h\in X,

(5.1)

where
\varepsilon\colon\mathbb{R}\times X\times X\to X
satisfies

\varepsilon(t,u,h)\to\varepsilon(t,u,0)=0\text{ as }h\to 0\text{ for each }t\in\mathbb{R},\ u\in X.

(5.2)

The choice of the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{t}
on the right-hand side of (
5.1
) is for our convenience only as it is equivalent to
\left\|\cdot\right\|
by Lemma
2.6
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Under assumptions of Theorem
3.1
and (
5.1
), (
5.2
), the function
\Sigma\colon\mathbb{R}\times X\to X
, given in (

\{\mathcal{M}(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}
, is differentiable w.r.t. the second variable and

D_{\eta}\Sigma(\tau,\eta)=\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}L(\tau,s)(I-Q(s))Df(s,z_{\tau,\eta}(s))D_{\eta}[z_{\tau,\eta}(s)]ds,\ \tau\in\mathbb{R},\ \eta\in X.

(5.3)

The above result will be proved in the following part of this section.

We first observe that from (

), (
2.11
) and (
5.1
) it follows that for
t\in\mathbb{R}
and

we have

|Q(t)Df(t,u)w|_{N(t)}\leqslant L_{1}\left\|w\right\|_{t},

(5.4)

|(I-Q(t))Df(t,u)w|_{S(t)}\leqslant L_{2}\left\|w\right\|_{t}.

(5.5)

Indeed, writing (
5.1
) with

, we get by (

) and (

)

\lambda|Q(t)Df(t,u)w|_{N(t)}\leqslant\lambda L_{1}\left\|w\right\|_{t}+\lambda M\left\|w\right\|_{t}\left\|\varepsilon(t,u,\lambda w)\right\|.

Dividing by
\lambda
and passing with

5.4

5.2
). The proof of (
5.5
) is analogous. We also have

|Q(t)\varepsilon(t,u,h)|_{N(t)}\leqslant 2L_{1}\ \ \text{for}\ \ t\in\mathbb{R},\ u,h\in X,

|(I-Q(t))\varepsilon(t,u,h)|_{S(t)}\leqslant 2L_{2}\ \ \text{for}\ \ \ t\in\mathbb{R},\ u,h\in X.

Let us fix
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
. Given
\eta\in X
, under the conditions of Section
3
with
\sigma\in(\rho,\gamma)
satisfying the gap condition (
2.15

H_{\tau,\eta}\colon E_{\sigma,\tau}\to E_{\sigma,\tau}

\begin{split}H_{\tau,\eta}z(t)=L(t,\tau)Q(\tau)\eta&-\int_{t}^{\tau}L(t,s)Q(s)f(s,z(s))ds\\

In the sequel we will drop the subscript

Before we pass to the main result of this section we prove several preparatory estimates.

Lemma 5.2
.

We have the following estimates valid for

\displaystyle|Q(t)[\psi(\eta+h)(t)-\psi(\eta)(t)]|_{N(t)}\leqslant|Q(\tau)h|_{N(\tau)}e^{(\rho+L_{1}\Gamma(1,\kappa_{\Sigma}))(\tau-t)},

(5.6)

We consider the Banach space

F_{\sigma}=\left\{Z\in C((-\infty,\tau];\mathcal{L}(X,X))\colon\left\|Z\right\|_{F_{\sigma}}=\sup_{t\leqslant\tau}\sup_{\left\|w\right\|\leqslant 1}e^{\sigma(t-\tau)}\left\|Z(t)w\right\|_{t}=\sup_{\left\|w\right\|\leqslant 1}\left\|Z(\cdot)w\right\|_{E_{\sigma}}<\infty\right\}.

then

for

,

.

Given

\begin{split}(J_{\eta}Z)(t)&=L(t,\tau)Q(\tau)-\int_{t}^{\tau}L(t,s)Q(s)Df(s,z_{\eta}(s))Z(s)ds\\
&+\int_{-\infty}^{t}L(t,s)(I-Q(s))Df(s,z_{\eta}(s))Z(s)ds\ \ \text{for}\ \ Z\in F_{\sigma},\ t\leqslant\tau.\end{split}

\begin{split}|L(t,s)Q(s)Df(s,z_{\eta}(s))Z(s)w|_{N(t)}&\leqslant e^{\rho(s-t)}|Q(s)Df(s,z_{\eta}(s))Z(s)w|_{N(s)}\\
&\leqslant L_{1}e^{\rho(s-t)}\left\|Z(s)w\right\|_{s}\ \ \text{for}\ \ \ s\in[t,\tau],\ \left\|w\right\|\leqslant 1,\\
\end{split}

\begin{split}|L(t,s)(I-Q(s))Df(s,z_{\eta}(s))Z(s)w|_{S(t)}&\leqslant e^{\gamma(s-t)}|(I-Q(s))Df(s,z_{\eta}(s))Z(s)w|_{S(s)}\\
&\leqslant L_{2}e^{\gamma(s-t)}\left\|Z(s)w\right\|_{s}\ \ \text{for}\ \ s\leqslant t,\ \left\|w\right\|\leqslant 1,\end{split}

whence it follows that

\displaystyle e^{\sigma(t-\tau)}|Q(t)(J_{\eta}Z)(t)w|_{N(t)}\leqslant e^{(\sigma-\rho)(t-\tau)}|Q(\tau)w|_{N(\tau)}+L_{1}\left\|Z\right\|_{F_{\sigma}}\int_{t}^{\tau}e^{(\sigma-\rho)(t-s)}ds

\displaystyle\ \ \ =e^{(\sigma-\rho)(t-\tau)}|Q(\tau)w|_{N(\tau)}+L_{1}\left\|Z\right\|_{F_{\sigma}}\frac{1-e^{(\sigma-\rho)(t-\tau)}}{\sigma-\rho}\ \ \text{for}\ \ \left\|w\right\|\leqslant 1,

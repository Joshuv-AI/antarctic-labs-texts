Insufficiency of Pure-State Ensembles in Characterizing Transformations of Entangled States under LOCC
C. L. Liu, Baoqing Sun, D. L. Zhou

Abstract
The conditions for transforming pure entangled states under local operations and classical communication (LOCC) are well understood. A natural question then arises: Can we determine the transformation conditions for mixed entangled states under LOCC based on the properties of their pure-state ensembles? While much effort has been devoted to this issue, in this paper, we rule out this possibility. Our findings address several open questions, including: (i) The conditions E f c ​ r ​ ( ρ ) ≥ E f c ​ r ​ ( σ ) E_{f}^{cr}(\rho)\geq E_{f}^{cr}(\sigma) for all convex roof entanglement measures E f c ​ r E_{f}^{cr} is insufficient to guarantee the existence of an LOCC transformation Λ L ​ ( ⋅ ) \Lambda^{L}(\cdot) from ρ \rho to σ \sigma ; and (ii) The inequalities ∑ j p j ​ E ​ ( φ j ) ≥ ∑ l q l ​ E ​ ( ψ l ) \sum_{j}p_{j}E(\varphi_{j})\geq\sum_{l}q_{l}E(\psi_{l}) for all entanglement monotones E E are not sufficient to ensure the existence of an LOCC transformation from { p j , | φ j ⟩ } \{p_{j},|\varphi_{j}\rangle\} to { q l , | ψ l ⟩ } \{q_{l},|\psi_{l}\rangle\} .

II
Some Concepts in Entanglement Theory

To address the above issues clearly, it is helpful to review several related concepts, including separable states, entangled states, separable operations, and LOCC (local operations and classical communication). Separable States: A bipartite quantum state
\rho_{AB}
is called separable if it can be expressed as a convex combination of product states

\displaystyle\rho_{AB}=\sum_{\alpha}p_{\alpha}\rho_{\alpha}^{A}\otimes\rho_{\alpha}^{B},

(3)

where
\{p_{\alpha}\}
is a probability distribution and
\rho_{\alpha}^{A}
and
\rho_{\alpha}^{B}
are density operators acting on the Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
\mathcal{H}_{B}
, respectively
Werner
. A state is considered entangled if it cannot be represented as a separable state. Separable Operations: A separable operation
\Lambda^{S}(\cdot)
on a bipartite quantum system is a transformation of the form
Rains
;
Horodecki

\displaystyle\Lambda^{S}(\rho_{AB})=\sum_{n=1}^{N}K_{n}^{A}\otimes K_{n}^{B}\,\rho_{AB}\,{K_{n}^{A}}^{\dagger}\otimes{K_{n}^{B}}^{\dagger},

(4)

where
\rho_{AB}
is the initial density operator on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
. The Kraus operators
K_{n}^{A}\otimes K_{n}^{B}
are arbitrary product operators that satisfy the trace-preserving condition

\displaystyle\sum_{n}{K_{n}^{A}}^{\dagger}K_{n}^{A}\otimes{K_{n}^{B}}^{\dagger}K_{n}^{B}=\mathbb{I}_{A}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B},

(5)

where
\mathbb{I}_{A}
and
\mathbb{I}_{B}
are the identity operators on parties A and B, respectively. The extension to multipartite systems is straightforward; however, we will focus on the bipartite case here. Therefore, for simplicity, we will omit the indices
AB
in the following discussion.

LOCC describes all the transformations that two separated parties (party A and party B) can perform when they apply local operations and have access to classical communication. In general, local operations encompass measurements and operations involving additional local systems
Bennett1
. Classical communication allows the two parties to exchange information over a classical channel. Specifically, consider two subsystems, one located in party A’s laboratory and the other in party B’s. Suppose party A starts by performing a generalized measurement with outcomes corresponding to Kraus operators
K_{i_{1}}
. If the initial state of the system was
|\psi\rangle
and party A obtains outcome
i_{1}
, the state becomes
K_{i_{1}}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{B}|\psi\rangle
. Party A then communicates the outcome
i_{1}
to party B, who performs a measurement on his subsystem conditioned on
i_{1}
, described by Kraus operators
K_{i_{2}}^{(i_{1})}
. After obtaining outcome
i_{2}
, party B informs party A, who then performs another measurement with Kraus operators
K_{i_{3}}^{(i_{1},i_{2})}
. This process can continue for an arbitrary number of rounds. The probabilities of outcomes obtained at each stage must always sum to unity, leading to the conditions
\sum_{i_{n}}{K_{i_{n}}^{(S_{n})}}^{\dagger}K_{i_{n}}^{(S_{n})}=\mathbb{I}_{A},\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{i_{n}}{K_{i_{n}}^{(S_{n})}}^{\dagger}K_{i_{n}}^{(S_{n})}=\mathbb{I}_{B},

where
S_{n}
denotes the collection of indices
\{i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{n-1}\}
indicating all outcomes obtained in previous measurements.

It has been proved that LOCC is complex and challenging to characterize in its full mathematical detail
Chitambar2
. Furthermore, it is important to note that the set of LOCC operations is a proper subset of separable operations. This means that while all LOCC operations are separable operations, not all separable operations can be achieved through LOCC.

III
Characterizing Transformations of Entangled States via Pure-state Ensembles

With these concepts clarified, we are now ready to present the main result of this paper.

Lemma 1
.–Let
\rho
be any
M\otimes N
mixed state (without loss of generality, we assume
M\geq N
), and let
|\varphi\rangle
be any pure entangled state with Schmidt rank
r

\rho
to
|\varphi\rangle
using LOCC is that
M\geq 2r
and
N\geq r
. (See the Appendix I for the detailed proof.)

We emphasize that if the LOCC in the above lemma is replaced with separable operations, the necessary condition in the above lemma will no longer hold. This can be demonstrated by the following transformation: Consider the initial state

\displaystyle\rho=p|\varphi_{1}\rangle\langle\varphi_{1}|+(1-p)|\varphi_{2}\rangle\langle\varphi_{2}|,

(6)

where the two states
|\varphi_{1}\rangle
and
|\varphi_{2}\rangle
above are defined as

|\varphi_{1}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}\left(|01\rangle+|10\rangle+\sum_{j=3}^{r}|jj\rangle\right)
and

|\varphi_{2}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}\left(|02\rangle+|20\rangle+\sum_{j=-3}^{-r}|jj\rangle\right)
, respectively.
The final state is given by

\displaystyle|\varphi\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{2}{r}}\left(\sqrt{\mu}\nu|00\rangle+\sqrt{\mu}|11\rangle+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{j=3}^{r}|jj\rangle\right),

(7)

which has a Schmidt rank of
r
, where
\mu=\frac{15}{16}
and
\nu=\frac{1}{\sqrt{15}}
. Using Lemma 1, it is straightforward to verify that the transformation from
\rho
to
|\varphi\rangle
via LOCC is impossible, but a separable operation can achieve the transformation from
\rho
to
|\varphi\rangle
. (See the Appendix II for the specific form of the separable operation.)

Lemma 1 and the examples above can be seen as generalizations of the results presented in Ref.

, which compared the differences between using separable operations and LOCC to perform entanglement distillation on 3

Theorem 1.
–There exist two entangled states,

and

Proof
.–First, we consider the case where the initial state is a mixed state

. Let

We will show that if there exists a separable operation
\Lambda^{S}(\cdot)

\{|\varphi_{j}\rangle\}

\displaystyle\Lambda^{L}(\psi)=\varphi.

\displaystyle K_{n}^{A}\otimes K_{n}^{B}|\psi\rangle=K_{n}^{A}\otimes K_{n}^{B}\sum_{j}c_{j}|\varphi_{j}\rangle=\sum_{j}c_{j}\mu_{j}^{n}|\varphi\rangle.

Since

\displaystyle\Lambda^{S}(\psi)=\varphi.

(11)

By using a result from Ref.

\uplambda_{\psi}\prec\uplambda_{\varphi}.
Furthermore, from Nielsen’s theorem

\Lambda^{L}(\cdot)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{L}(\psi)=\varphi.

(12)

Next, let us demonstrate that if there exists a separable operation

Since

Theorem 1 tells us that treating a mixed state as a collection of pure states and equating the transformation of mixed states under LOCC to a global transformation between two sets of pure states is incorrect. Thus, to study the conditions for transforming mixed entangled states under LOCC, one must consider properties beyond those of pure-state ensembles. Additionally, Theorem 1 highlights that the operational differences between separable operations and LOCC provide valuable insights for addressing certain problems. In the following, we will use this result to analyze several previously introduced problems.

The first problem is related to the relationship between convex roof entanglement measures and the transformations of mixed entangled states under LOCC, as discussed in Refs.
Ghiu
;
Li
;
Jonathan
;
Li1
. Given that the transformations between pure entangled states and the transformations from pure entangled states to mixed entangled states using LOCC can be characterized via convex roof entanglement measures, some researchers conjecture that the transformations between mixed entangled states under LOCC can also be characterized via convex roof entanglement measures
Ghiu
;
Li
;
Jonathan
;
Li1
. However, as we will demonstrate, this is not the case.

To illustrate this, let us present the concepts of entanglement monotones and convex roof entanglement measures. For a bipartite pure state, assume its Schmidt form is
|\varphi\rangle=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sqrt{\uplambda_{i}}|ii\rangle
. For simplicity, we denote
\bm{\uplambda}_{\varphi}=(\uplambda_{0},\uplambda_{1},\ldots,\uplambda_{n-1})^{T}
as the Schmidt vector of
|\varphi\rangle
, where
0\leq\uplambda_{i}\leq\uplambda_{i+1}
. Each entanglement monotone corresponds to a function
f
that maps the simplex of probability vectors to real numbers, where
f
is both symmetric and concave
Vidal
. Such a function defines an entanglement monotone for pure states
|\varphi\rangle
, denoted as

\displaystyle E_{f}(\varphi)=f(\bm{\uplambda}_{\varphi}),

(15)

by evaluating
f
on the vector of Schmidt coefficients
\bm{\uplambda}_{\varphi}
of
|\varphi\rangle
. It has been proven that every entanglement monotone on pure states can be obtained in this manner. Given an entanglement monotone
E_{f}
defined on pure states as
E_{f}(\varphi)
, we can extend it to mixed states using the convex roof construction, also known as convex roof entanglement measures

\displaystyle E_{f}^{cr}(\rho)=\inf\sum_{i}p_{i}E_{f}(\varphi_{i}),

(16)

where the infimum is taken over all ensembles
\{p_{i},|\varphi_{i}\rangle\}
such that
\rho=\sum_{i}p_{i}|\varphi_{i}\rangle\langle\varphi_{i}|
. Some well-known entanglement measures, including the entanglement of formation
Bennett1
;
Wootters
, are defined in this manner. With these notions, we arrive at the following Theorem 2.

Theorem 2
.–The conditions

\displaystyle E_{f}^{cr}(\rho)\geq E_{f}^{cr}(\sigma)

(17)

for all convex roof entanglement measures
E_{f}^{cr}
are insufficient to guarantee the existence of an LOCC
\Lambda^{L}(\cdot)
from
\rho
to
\sigma
.

Proof.
–Let
\rho
be the state in Eq. (
6
) and
\sigma
be the state in Eq. (
14
). Suppose
\{p_{j},|\varphi_{j}\rangle\}
is an arbitrary pure-state ensemble that generates
\rho
. Based on the arguments in the Theorem 1, it is straightforward to verify that we can achieve the transformations from
|\varphi_{j}\rangle
to
|\varphi\rangle
in Eq. (
7
) for all

using LOCC. Furthermore, using the result that any ensemble decomposition of
\rho=\{q_{i},|e_{i}\rangle\}
can be obtained from
\{p_{j},|\varphi_{j}\rangle\}
through the relation
\sqrt{q_{i}}|e_{i}\rangle=\sum_{j}U_{ij}\sqrt{p_{j}}|\varphi_{j}\rangle
, where
U=[U_{ij}]
represents a unitary matrix, and applying the arguments in Theorem 1, it follows that the transformation from
|e_{i}\rangle
to
\varphi
can also be accomplished for all

using LOCC. Based on these results, we can deduce that for any convex roof entanglement measure
E_{f}^{cr}
, it follows that
E_{f}^{cr}(|\varphi_{j}\rangle)\geq E_{f}^{cr}(|\varphi\rangle)
for all
j
. Since we have
E_{f}^{cr}(|\varphi\rangle)=E_{f}^{cr}(\sigma)
for all
E_{f}^{cr}
, it follows that there are

\displaystyle\sum_{i}q_{i}E_{f}^{cr}(|e_{i}\rangle)\geq E_{f}^{cr}(\sigma)

(18)

for any pure-state ensemble
\{q_{i},|e_{i}\rangle\}
of
\rho
. This further implies that

\displaystyle E_{f}^{cr}(\rho)\geq E_{f}^{cr}(\sigma)

(19)

for all convex roof entanglement measures
E_{f}^{cr}
. Nevertheless, according to Theorem 1, it is impossible to achieve the transformation from
\rho
to
\sigma
using LOCC. Therefore, the condition
E_{f}^{cr}(\rho)\geq E_{f}^{cr}(\sigma)
for all convex roof entanglement measures
E_{f}^{cr}
are not sufficient to guarantee the existence of an LOCC
\Lambda^{L}(\cdot)
that transforms
\rho
to
\sigma
. This concludes the proof of the theorem.
\blacksquare

Based on Theorem 2 above, we obtain that we cannot determine the conditions for the transformation between mixed states under LOCC solely by using convex roof entanglement measures. It is noteworthy that this result is closely related to another research topic. This topic primarily investigates whether there exists a set of entanglement measures

that constitutes a complete set

;

satisfy the inequality

, then there exists an LOCC

that realizes the transformation from

to
\sigma
. Theorem 2 indicates that even if

includes all convex roof entanglement measures, it still does not satisfy completeness.

The second open question concerns the conditions for the transformation between two pure-state ensembles using LOCC. The transformation from one pure-state ensemble

to another
D_{2}:=\{q_{l},|\psi_{l}\rangle\}
is a probabilistic process in which Alice and Bob initially share

, and use LOCC to convert it into

. Specifically, for each ensemble element

in

, Alice and Bob apply a transformation

that outputs the state

with conditional probability

, such that the overall probability of obtaining

is

. This process is a valid LOCC transformation if a set of LOCC steps can achieve the conversion with the required probabilities. In Ref.

between two probability distributions

for all entanglement monotones

\displaystyle\sum_{j}p_{j}E(\varphi_{j})\geq\sum_{l}q_{l}E(\psi_{l})

\displaystyle|\varphi_{1}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}(|01\rangle+|10\rangle+\sum_{j=3}^{r}|jj\rangle)

\displaystyle|\varphi_{2}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}(|02\rangle+|20\rangle+\sum_{j=-3}^{-r}|jj\rangle),

\displaystyle|\psi_{1}\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{2}{r}}(\sqrt{\mu}\nu|00\rangle+\sqrt{\mu}|11\rangle+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{j=3}^{r}|jj\rangle),

\displaystyle|\psi_{2}\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{2}{r}}(\sqrt{\mu}\nu|rr\rangle+\sqrt{\mu}|r\text{+}1,r\text{+}1\rangle+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{j=3}^{r}|r\text{+}j,r\text{+}j\rangle).

|\psi_{2}\rangle
to
|\psi_{r}\rangle
can both be achieved using LOCC. This indicates that for all entanglement monotones, there are
E(|\varphi_{j}\rangle)\geq E(|\psi_{1}\rangle)
and
E(|\varphi_{j}\rangle)\geq E(|\psi_{2}\rangle)
for
j=1,2
.

On the other hand, if there is an LOCC, say
\Lambda^{L}(\cdot)
, achieving the transformation from the ensemble
\{p,|\varphi_{1}\rangle;1\text{-}p,|\varphi_{2}\rangle\}
to
\{q,|\psi_{1}\rangle;1\text{-}q,|\psi_{2}\rangle\}
, then according to the Theorem 1, there are
\Lambda^{L}(|\varphi_{j}\rangle)=|\psi_{1}\rangle
and
\Lambda^{L}(|\varphi_{j}\rangle)=|\psi_{2}\rangle
for all
j=1,2
. This further implies that
\sum_{j}p_{j}E(\varphi_{j})\geq\sum_{l}q_{l}E(\psi_{l})
for all entanglement monotones
E
. According to the assumption, we can thus transform
\rho=p|\varphi_{1}\rangle\langle\varphi_{1}|+(1\text{-}p)|\varphi_{2}\rangle\langle\varphi_{2}|
into
\sigma=q|\psi_{1}\rangle\langle\psi_{1}|+(1\text{-}q)|\psi_{2}\rangle\langle\psi_{2}|
using LOCC. However, this contradicts the result presented in Theorem 1, completing the proof of the theorem.
\blacksquare

IV
Conclusions

In summary, we have addressed the question: Can the transformation conditions of mixed entangled states under LOCC be determined based on the properties of their pure-state ensembles? Theorem 1 rules out this possibility, demonstrating that treating a mixed state as a collection of pure states and equating the transformation of mixed states under LOCC to a global transformation between two sets of pure states is incorrect. Building on this result, we resolve two conjectures, presented in Theorems 2 and 3. While we show that both conjectures are incorrect, these results are still significant in a positive way, as they reveal that the direction taken by previous approaches was not fruitful. To study the conditions for transforming mixed entangled states under LOCC, one must consider properties beyond those of pure-state ensembles.

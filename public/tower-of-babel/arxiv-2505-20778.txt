Abstract
. Let P P be a pseudogroup of local diffeomorphisms of an n n -dimensional smooth manifold M M . Following Losik we consider characteristic classes of the quotient M / P M/P as elements of the de Rham cohomology of the second order frame bundles over M / P M/P coming from the generators of the Gelfand-Fuchs cohomology. We provide explicit expressions for the classes that we call Godbillon-Vey-Losik class and the first Chern-Losik class. Reducing the frame bundles we construct bundles over M / P M/P such that the Godbillon-Vey-Losik class is represented by a volume form on a space of dimension 2 ​ n + 1 2n+1 , and the first Chern-Losik class is represented by a symplectic form on a space of dimension 2 ​ n 2n . Examples in dimension 2 are considered. Keywords : diffeomorphism pseudogroup; characteristic classes; Gelfand formal geometry; Gelfand-Fuchs cohomology; Godbillon-Vey-Losik class. AMS Mathematics Subject Classification: 57R30; 57R32.

A
D_{n}
-space
X
introduced by Losik
[
10
]
is a set with a smooth structure generalizing the smooth structure of an
n
-dimensional manifold. Typically
X
is the quotient
M/P
of an
n
-dimensional manifold
M
by a diffeomorphism pseudogroup
P
. An important example is the leaf space
N/\mathcal{F}
of a regular foliation
\mathcal{F}
on a manifold
N
, where
N/\mathcal{F}
may be considered as the quotient of a complete transversal
M
by the holonomy pseudogroup
P
. The characteristic classes
of a quotient
X
are elements of the de Rham cohomology of the second order frame bundles over
X
coming from the generators of the Gelfand-Fuchs cohomology. These classes may be mapped to the usual characteristic classes of diffeomorphism groups or foliations, but then one looses information. For example, the Godbillon-Vey class of any Reeb foliation
\mathcal{F}
on the 3-dimensional sphere
S^{3}
is trivial, while the corresponding characteristic class of the
D_{1}
-space
S^{3}/\mathcal{F}
may be non-trivial
[
1
]
.

For a quotient
M/P
we consider first of all the Godbillon-Vey-Losik (GVL) class and the first Chern-Losik class (CL class). The GVL and CL classes are the cohomology classes given by a
(2n+1)
-form and a
2
-form on the spaces
S_{2}(M/P)/\text{\rm O}(n)
and
S_{2}(M/P)/\text{\rm GL}(n,\mathbb{R})
, respectively. Here
S_{2}(M/P)
is the bundle of frames of second order over
M/P
. The dimension of the spaces
S_{2}(M/P)/\text{\rm O}(n)
and
S_{2}(M/P)/\text{\rm GL}(n,\mathbb{R})
are respectively
\frac{1}{2}n(n^{2}+2n+3)
and
\frac{1}{2}(n^{2}+n+2)
.
We find simplified versions of these classes and consider the GVL class as the cohomology class of a volume form on a
D_{2n+1}
-space
A(M/P)
, also we consider the CL class as the cohomology class of a symplectic form on a
D_{2n}
-space
B(M/P)
.

Let us give the explicit construction.
Let
U\subset M
be a coordinate neighborhood with the coordinates
y^{1},\dots,y^{n}
. Let
A(U)=U\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the bundle over
U
with the coordinates
y^{1},\dots,y^{n},x,y_{1},\dots y_{n}
.
Suppose that
V
is another coordinate neighborhood with the coordinates
\alpha^{1},\dots,\alpha^{n}
.
We extend a local diffeomorphism from
U
to
V
given by
\alpha^{i}=\alpha^{i}(y^{1},\dots,y^{n})
to a local diffeomorphism from
A(U)
to
A(V)
by setting

\alpha^{i}=\alpha^{i}(y^{1},\dots,y^{n}),\quad\beta=x+\ln\left|\det\left(\frac{\partial\alpha^{i}}{\partial y^{j}}\right)\right|,\quad\alpha_{k}=\frac{\partial y^{p}}{\partial\alpha^{j}}\frac{\partial^{2}\alpha^{j}}{\partial y^{p}\partial y^{q}}\frac{\partial y^{q}}{\partial\alpha^{k}}+\frac{\partial y^{j}}{\partial\alpha^{k}}y_{j}.

This defines a bundle
A(M)
over
M
.
For each
A(U)
, consider the differential form

-dx\wedge(dy_{i}\wedge dy^{i})^{n}\in\Omega^{2n+1}(A(U)).

The above coordinate transformations transform these forms to each other.
This gives a
(2n+1)
-form
gvl
on
A(M)
. Each diffeomorphism of
M
is extended to a diffeomorphism of
A(M)
in the obvious way.
The form
gvl
is invariant under all such diffeomorphisms, hence it defines a
(2n+1)
-form on
A(M/P)
, which we denote by the same symbol. We consider the cohomology class of
gvl
in
H^{2n+1}(A(M/P))
as a simplified version of the GVL class. This GVL class is trivial if and only if there exists a
P
-invariant
2n
-form
\omega
on
A(M)
such that
d\omega=gvl
.

As above, for each
U
consider the bundle
B(U)=U\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the coordinates
y^{1},\dots,y^{n},y_{1},\dots y_{n}
. We obtain the bundles
B(M)
and
B(M/P)
. The 2-form

dy_{i}\wedge dy^{i}

on
B(U)
defines a 2-form on
B(M/P)
. We consider the cohomology class of this form as a simplified version of the first CL class.

We define characteristic classes of a vector field
V
on
M
as the characteristic classes of the corresponding pseudogroup
P
. In Section
5
, we consider examples of vector fields on the open disc
D^{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Let
f
be a smooth even function on
(-1,1)
, non-vanishing on
(0,1)
. Then the function
f(r)
, where
r=\sqrt{(y^{1})^{2}+(y^{2})^{2}}
, is smooth on
D^{2}
. The first CL class of vector field
V_{1}
with the components
(f(r)y^{1},f(r)y^{2})
is non-trivial if and only if
f(0)=0
. This means that the triviality of the first CL class of
V_{1}
is equivalent to the non-degeneracy of the singular point
(0,0)
. The first
CL class of the vector field
V_{2}
with the components
(f(r)y^{1},-f(r)y^{2})
is trivial for each

D^{2}/V_{2}
is just the segment
[0,1)
. Thus the characteristic classes under consideration provide information about singularities.

1.
Definition of characteristic classes

Here we give a very short introduction to Losik’s ideas. The details may be found in
[
9
,
10
,
12
,
11
]
.
Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional smooth manifold. Denote by
S(M)
the space of frames of infinite order over
M
, see
[
3
]
for details.
Consider also the reduced bundles
S(M)/\text{\rm GL}(n,\mathbb{R})
and
S(M)/\text{\rm O}(n)
. Let
P
be a pseudogroup of local diffeomorphisms of
M
. Losik
[
10
]
introduced on
M/P
a
D_{n}
-structure generalizing the notion of the smooth structure of an
n
-dimensional smooth manifold. For simplicity we call
M/P
just a quotient and denote it by
X
. A morphism of two quotients is a morphism of the corresponding pseudogroups defined by Haefliger
[
6
]
.
The action of
P
on
M
is extended to an action of
P
on the bundles
S(M)
,
S(M)/\text{\rm GL}(n,\mathbb{R})
and
S(M)/\text{\rm O}(n)
. Let
S(M/P)=S(M)/P
and define the spaces

S(M/P)/\text{\rm GL}(n,\mathbb{R})
and
S(M/P)/\text{\rm O}(n)
in a similar way. A differential form on
S(M/P)
is a
P
-invariant differential form on
S(M)
.
Let
\text{\rm Diff}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the pseudogroup of all local diffeomorphisms of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
\mathop{\text{}}{\rm pt}\nolimits_{n}=\mathbb{R}^{n}/\text{\rm Diff}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
There is a unique morphism

X=M/P\to\mathop{\text{}}{\rm pt}\nolimits_{n}

inducing the morphisms

S(X)\to S(\mathop{\text{}}{\rm pt}\nolimits_{n}),\quad S(X)/\text{\rm GL}(n,\mathbb{R})\to S(\mathop{\text{}}{\rm pt}\nolimits_{n})/\text{\rm GL}(n,\mathbb{R}),\quad S(X)/\text{\rm O}(n)\to S(\mathop{\text{}}{\rm pt}\nolimits_{n})/\text{\rm O}(n).

There are the following isomorphism of the de Rham cohomology of the bundles over
\mathop{\text{}}{\rm pt}\nolimits_{n}
and Gelfand-Fuchs cohomology of the Lie algebra
W_{n}
of formal vector fields on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

(1)

H^{*}(S(\mathop{\text{}}{\rm pt}\nolimits_{n}))/G\cong H^{*}(W_{n},G),

where
G
is a trivial group or one of
\text{\rm GL}(n,\mathbb{R})
,
\text{\rm O}(n)
.
This gives the characteristic morphisms

(2)

\displaystyle H^{*}(W_{n})

\displaystyle\to H^{*}(S(X)),

(3)

\displaystyle H^{*}(W_{n},\text{\rm GL}(n,\mathbb{R}))

\displaystyle\to H^{*}(S(X)/\text{\rm GL}(n,\mathbb{R})),

(4)

\displaystyle H^{*}(W_{n},\text{\rm O}(n))

\displaystyle\to H^{*}(S(X)/\text{\rm O}(n)).

The images of the generators of the cohomology groups under these maps give Losik’s characteristic classes.

2.
Expressions for some characteristic classes

Now we describe the construction of the isomorphisms (
1
) following
[
12
]
. Recall the definition of the canonical Gelfand-Kazhdan form
[
3
]
.
Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open subset.
Let
\tau
be a tangent
vector at
s\in S(U)
and let
s(u)
be a curve on
S(U)
such that

\tau=\frac{ds}{du}(0)
. One can represent
s(u)
by a smooth family

k_{u}
of germs at
0
of regular at
0\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
maps
\mathbb{R}^{n}\to U
,
i.e.,
s(u)=j^{\infty}_{0}k_{u}
. Then put

\theta(\tau)=-j_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d}{du}(k_{0}^{-1}\circ k_{u})(0).

The form
\theta
is canonical, i.e., it is invariant under the infinite order frame bundle maps induced by the diffeomorphisms of open subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Consequently,
\theta
is a well-defined 1-form on
S(\mathop{\text{}}{\rm pt}\nolimits_{n})
with values in
W_{n}
.
Now, each continuous cochain
c\in C^{k}(W_{n})
defines
\theta_{c}\in\Omega^{k}(S(\mathop{\text{}}{\rm pt}\nolimits))
by the formula

(5)

\theta_{c}(V_{1},\dots,V_{k})=c(\theta(V_{1}),\dots,\theta(V_{k})),

where
V_{1},\dots,V_{k}
are vector fields on
S(\mathop{\text{}}{\rm pt}\nolimits_{n})
. The form
\theta
satisfies the Maurer-Cartan equation, this implies that the map
c\mapsto\theta_{c}
defines the homomorphism
C^{*}(W_{n})\to\Omega^{*}(S(\mathop{\text{}}{\rm pt}\nolimits_{n}))
of the complexes. This homomorphism induces homomorphisms of the relative complexes.
All these cochain homomorphisms are isomorphisms that induce (
1
).

Let us collect some information about Gelfand-Fuchs cohomology
[
5
]
following
[
12
]
.
The complex

C^{*}(W_{n})
is generated by the 1-forms
c^{i}_{j_{1},\dots,j_{r}}
,
r=0,1,2\dots
,
1\leqslant i,j_{1},\dots,j_{r}\leqslant n
,
where

c^{i}_{j_{1},\dots,j_{r}}(\xi)=\frac{\partial^{r}\xi^{i}}{\partial x^{j_{1}}\cdots\partial x^{j_{r}}}(0),\quad\xi=\xi^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}\in W_{n}.

Let

\gamma=\left(c^{i}_{j}\right),\quad\Psi^{i}_{j}=c^{i}_{jk}\wedge c^{k},\quad\Psi=\left(\Psi^{i}_{j}\right).

The cochains

\Psi_{p}=\text{tr}(\underbrace{\Psi\wedge\cdots\wedge\Psi}_{p\text{ times}}),\quad p=1,\dots,n,

are cocycles of
C^{*}(W_{n},\text{\rm GL}(n,\mathbb{R}))
, and the cohomology classes of these cocycles generate
H^{*}(W_{n},\text{\rm GL}(n,\mathbb{R}))
. The cohomology classes of
\Psi_{p}
are called formal Chern classes.

There exist cochaines
\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{n}
in
C^{*}(W_{n})
such that
d\Gamma_{i}=\Psi_{i}
. In particular,
\Gamma_{1}=c^{i}_{i}
. The cohomology
H^{*}(W_{n})
are isomorphic to the cohomology of the complex generated by
\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{n},\Psi_{1},\dots,\Psi_{n}
. Finally, there exist cochains
\Lambda_{2k+1}
(
1\leqslant 2k+1\leqslant n
) from
C^{*}(W_{n},\text{\rm O}(n))
such that
d\Lambda_{2k+1}=\Psi_{2k+1}
. The construction of these cochains may be found in
[
12
]
. The cohomology
H^{*}(W_{n},\text{\rm O}(n))
are isomorphic to the cohomology of the complex generated by the cochains
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{3},\dots,\Psi_{1},\dots,\Psi_{n}
.

The equality

\theta=\sum_{r\geqslant 0}\frac{1}{(j_{1}+\cdots+j_{r})!}\theta^{k}_{j_{1}\dots j_{r}}x^{j_{1}}\cdots x^{j_{r}}\partial_{x^{k}}

defines on
S(\mathop{\text{}}{\rm pt}\nolimits_{n})
canonical 1-forms
\theta^{k}_{j_{1}\cdots j_{r}}
. Applying (
5
), one gets

\theta^{k}_{j_{1}\dots j_{r}}=\theta_{c^{k}_{j_{1}\dots j_{r}}},

see
[
12
]
.
The explicit formulas for the canonical forms
\theta^{k}_{j_{1}\cdots j_{r}}
for
r=0,1,2
may be found in
[
8
]
.

Each cohomology class from
H^{*}(W_{n})
,
H^{*}(W_{n},\text{\rm GL}(n,\mathbb{R}))
, and
H^{*}(W_{n},\text{\rm O}(n))
may be represented by cocycles that depend on the second jets of the vector fields
[
5
]
. Together with the description of the form
\theta
this shows that the images of the characteristic maps (
2
), (
3
), (
4
) may be represented by forms on the spaces of frames of the second order
S_{2}(X)
,
S_{2}(X)/\text{\rm GL}(n,\mathbb{R})
, and
S_{2}(X)/\text{\rm O}(n)
, respectively. To simplify the exposition we will consider the characteristic classes with values in

H^{*}(S_{2}(X))
,
H^{*}(S_{2}(X)/\text{\rm GL}(n,\mathbb{R}))
, and
H^{*}(S_{2}(X)/\text{\rm O}(n))
.

Let
U,W\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open subsets such that
0\in W
.
Let
f:W\to U
,
z^{i}=f^{i}(t^{1},\dots,t^{n})
, be a smooth map regular at the point
0
. The corresponding element of
S_{2}(U)
has the
coordinates

(6)

z^{i}_{j_{1}\dots j_{r}}=\frac{\partial^{r}f^{i}}{\partial t^{j_{1}}\cdots\partial t^{j_{r}}}(0),\quad r=0,1,2.

It is convenient to consider the following coordinate system:

(7)

y^{i}=z^{i},\quad y^{i}_{j}=z^{i}_{j},\quad y^{i}_{jk}=v^{p}_{j}z^{i}_{pq}v^{q}_{k},

where
(v^{i}_{j})
is the inverse matrix to
(z^{i}_{j})
.

Suppose that we have
another open subset
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a regular map
h:U\to V
.
Denote by
\alpha^{k}_{j_{1}\dots j_{r}}
,
r=0,1,2
, the coordinates on
S_{2}(V)
. Then we get the
induced map
\tilde{h}:S_{2}(U)\to S_{2}(V)
given by

(8)

\alpha^{i}=\alpha^{i}(y^{1},\dots,y^{n}),\quad\alpha^{i}_{j}=\frac{\partial\alpha^{i}}{\partial y^{p}}y^{p}_{j},\quad\alpha^{i}_{jk}=\frac{\partial y^{p}}{\partial\alpha^{j}}\frac{\partial^{2}\alpha^{i}}{\partial y^{p}\partial y^{q}}\frac{\partial y^{q}}{\partial\alpha^{k}}+\frac{\partial y^{p}}{\partial\alpha^{j}}\frac{\partial\alpha^{i}}{\partial y^{s}}\frac{\partial y^{q}}{\partial\alpha^{k}}y^{s}_{pq}.

Using the results from
[
8
]
, we get

\theta^{i}_{j}=-v^{i}_{k}dy^{k}_{j}+y^{i}_{jk}dy^{k}.

Let

\Gamma_{1}=\theta^{i}_{i}.

Note that

d\Gamma_{1}=d\theta^{i}_{i}=dy^{i}_{ik}\wedge dy^{k}.

On the other hand, it is known
[
8
]
that

d\theta^{i}_{i}=\theta^{i}_{ik}\wedge\theta^{k}.

The action of the group
\text{\rm GL}(n,\mathbb{R})
on
S_{2}(U)
with respect to the coordinates (
7

(9)

A:(y^{i},y^{i}_{j},y^{i}_{jk})\mapsto(y^{i},y^{i}_{p}A^{p}_{j},y^{i}_{jk}),

where
A=(A^{i}_{j})\in\text{\rm GL}(n,\mathbb{R})
.
This shows that the functions
y^{i}
,
y^{i}_{jk}
may be considered as coordinates on
S_{2}(U)/\text{\rm GL}(n,\mathbb{R})
.

Definition 1
.

Let
X
be a quotient. The first Chern-Losik class (CL class) is the element of
H^{2}(S_{2}(X)/\text{\rm GL}(n,\mathbb{R}))
defined by the image of the class
[\Psi_{1}]\in H^{2}(W_{n},\text{\rm GL}(n,\mathbb{R}))
under the map (
3
).

From the above we obtain

Proposition 1
.

The first CL class is the cohomology class of the form

\theta^{i}_{ik}\wedge\theta^{k}
, which with respect to coordinates
y^{i}
,
y^{i}_{jk}
is given by the form
dy^{i}_{ik}\wedge dy^{k}
.

Consider the space
S_{2}(U)/\text{\rm O}(n)
. Let
y^{ij}
be the coordinates on the space of symmetric non-degenerate bilinear forms on
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{*}
. From (
9
) it follows that the functions
y^{i}
,
y^{ij}
,
y^{i}_{jk}
may be considered as the coordinates on
S_{2}(U)/\text{\rm O}(n)
.
The projection
p:S_{2}(U)\to S_{2}(U)/\text{\rm O}(n)
is given by

(y^{i},y^{i}_{j},y^{i}_{jk})\mapsto\left(y^{i},\sum_{l}y^{i}_{l}y^{k}_{l},y^{i}_{jk}\right).

A morphism

induces the map
\tilde{f}:S_{2}(U)/\text{\rm O}(n)\to S_{2}(V)/\text{\rm O}(n)
. Under
\tilde{f}
, the transformation of the coordinates
y^{i}
and
y^{i}_{jk}
is given by (
8
), and, moreover, it holds

(10)

\alpha^{ij}=\frac{\partial\alpha^{i}}{\partial y^{k}}y^{kl}\frac{\partial\alpha^{j}}{\partial y^{l}}.

Let
(y_{ij})
denote the inverse matrix to
(y^{ij})
. Consider the following 1-form on
S(U)/\text{\rm O}(n)
:

\Lambda_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}y_{ij}dy^{ij}+y^{j}_{jk}dy^{k}.

It is not hard to check

Lemma 1
.

The 1-form
\Lambda_{1}
is canonical, it satisfies
p^{*}\Lambda_{1}=\Gamma_{1}
, and
d\Lambda_{1}=d\Gamma_{1}
.

Now we define two generalizations of the classical Godbillon-Vey class.

Definition 2
.

Let
X
be a quotient. The Godbillon-Vey-Losik class of

with values in

is the cohomology class
[\Gamma_{1}\wedge(d\Gamma_{1})^{n}]\in H^{2n+1}(S_{2}(X))
.

Definition 3
.

Let

be a
\mathcal{D}_{n}
-space. The Godbillon-Vey-Losik class of

with values in

is the cohomology class
[\Lambda_{1}\wedge(d\Lambda_{1})^{n}]\in H^{2n+1}(S_{2}(X)/\text{\rm O}(n))
.

It is clear that the projection
S_{2}(X)\to S_{2}(X)/\text{\rm O}(n)
preserves the GVL classes.
From the above we obtain

Proposition 2
.

The GVL classes with values in
H^{2n+1}(S_{2}(X))
is the cohomology class represented by the
(2n+1)
-form that with respect to the coordinates
y^{i}
,
y^{i}_{j}
,
y^{i}_{jk}
may be written as

-v^{j}_{i}dy^{i}_{j}\wedge(dy^{k}_{kl}\wedge dy^{l})^{n}.

Proposition 3
.

The GVL classes with values in
H^{2n+1}(S_{2}(X)/\text{\rm O}(n))
is the cohomology class represented by the
(2n+1)
-form that with respect to the coordinates
y^{i}
,
y^{ij}
,
y^{i}_{jk}
may be written as

-\frac{1}{2}y_{ij}dy^{ij}\wedge(dy^{k}_{kl}\wedge dy^{l})^{n}.

3.
Characteristic classes associated to vector fields

Let
M
be a smooth manifold of dimension
n
. Let
\mathcal{V}
be a set of vector fields on
M
. Denote by
P
the pseudogroup of local diffeomorphisms of
M
generated by the pseudogroups of the elements of
\mathcal{V}
. We will denote the quotient
M/P
simply by
M/\mathcal{V}
. We define the characteristic classes of
\mathcal{V}
to be the characteristic classes of
M/P
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open subset. Let
V=V^{i}\partial_{y^{i}}
be a vector field on
U
. The local flow of
V
is extended to local flows on
S_{2}(U)
and induces a vector field there. The same holds for the other bundles. Define the functions

\tilde{V}^{i}=V^{i},\quad\tilde{V}^{i}_{j}=\frac{\partial V^{i}}{\partial y^{p}}y^{p}_{j},\quad\tilde{V}^{ij}=\frac{\partial V^{i}}{\partial y^{p}}y^{pj}+\frac{\partial V^{j}}{\partial y^{p}}y^{ip}\quad\tilde{V}^{i}_{jk}=\frac{\partial^{2}V^{i}}{\partial y^{j}\partial y^{k}}-\frac{\partial V^{p}}{\partial y^{j}}y^{i}_{pk}-\frac{\partial V^{p}}{\partial y^{k}}y^{i}_{jp}+\frac{\partial V^{i}}{\partial y^{p}}y^{p}_{jk}.

Using (
8
) it is easy to check that

Proposition 4
.

The components of the extension
\tilde{V}
of
V
to
S_{2}(U)
are
\tilde{V}^{i}
,
\tilde{V}^{i}_{j}
,

\tilde{V}^{i}_{jk}
.
The components of the extension
\tilde{V}
of
V
to
S_{2}(U)/\text{\rm GL}(n,\mathbb{R})
are
\tilde{V}^{i}
,

\tilde{V}^{i}_{jk}
. The components of the extension
\tilde{V}
of
V
to
S_{2}(U)/\text{\rm O}(n)
are
\tilde{V}^{i}
,
\tilde{V}^{ij}
,

\tilde{V}^{i}_{jk}
.

The following obvious proposition shows how to check the (non-)triviality of the characteristic classes.

Proposition 5
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a set of vector fields on an
n
-dimensional smooth manifold
M
. A cohomology class
[\alpha]\in H^{k}(S_{2}(M/\mathcal{V}))
is trivial if and only if there exists a form

\omega\in\Omega^{k-1}(S_{2}(M))
such that

d\omega=\alpha

and

L_{\tilde{V}}\omega=0

for all
V\in\mathcal{V}
. The same statements hold for the spaces
S_{2}(X)/\text{\rm GL}(n,\mathbb{R})
and
S_{2}(X)/\text{\rm O}(n)
.

For the first CL class, the triviality may be checked using the following proposition.

Proposition 6
.

The first CL class is trivial in
H^{2}(S_{2}(M/\mathcal{V})/\text{\rm GL}(n,\mathbb{R}))
if and only if there is a cover
\{U_{a}\}
of
M
by coordinate neighborhoods, and
functions

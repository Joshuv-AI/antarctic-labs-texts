Advances in semi-rational design of terpene synthases and their modifying enzymes
Authors: Xiqin Liang, Bengui Fan, Jun Wang, Yuan Qin, Tongle Yu, Pengpeng Xu, Guoli Wang, Tianyue An, Sixin Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAs research on natural terpene compounds advances, their pharmacological properties and commercial potential are increasingly recognized. Microbial cell factories offer an efficient means of producing high-value terpene natural products through biosynthesis. However, inherent limitations of native enzymes, such as low catalytic activity and poor stability, have constrained further improvements in production capacity. Recent progress in bioinformatics and the availability of detailed enzyme crystal structures have made semi-rational design a viable and powerful strategy for enhancing the catalytic performance of terpene synthases. This review summarizes semi-rational design strategies applied to key enzymes in the biosynthetic pathways of various terpenes, including mono-, sesqui-, di-, tri-, and tetraterpenes, as well as modifying enzymes such as cytochrome P450s, glycosyltransferases, acetyltransferases, ketolases and hydroxylases involved in terpene biosynthesis. By highlighting recent advances in the functional application of beneficial mutations, it offers a framework for optimizing the catalytic efficiency of enzymes within terpene biosynthesis.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13568-025-01984-5.Keywords: Semi-rational design, Terpene synthases, Cytochrome P450 enzymes, Glycosyltransferases protein engineering

Introduction

Terpenoid compounds possess exceptionally high value in the pharmaceutical and healthcare industries. However, conventional preparation methods are increasingly inadequate for fulfilling the current market demand for high-value terpenoid products. The implementation of synthetic biology strategies and the construction of microbial cell factories have emerged as effective avenues for the large-scale production of natural terpenoid products (Yao and Liu 2022; Xiao and Zhong 2016). The enhancement of microbial chassis for terpenoid biosynthesis is intrinsically dependent on the involvement of terpene synthases. Enzymes, which act as biological catalysts derived and purified from living cells, play a pivotal role in this context (Robinson 2015). Nevertheless, native biological enzymes are often constrained by suboptimal activity, limited catalytic efficiency, and inadequate thermostability, thereby significantly impeding the biosynthetic efficiency of terpenoid natural products (Feng et al. 2023; Zhou et al. 2022; Jiang et al. 2024a). Although numerous metabolic engineering strategies have been established to boost the microbial production of terpenoid compounds, the structural and functional modification of target enzymes remains an urgent challenge. Addressing this bottleneck is essential to optimizing rate-limiting properties and meeting the rigorous requirements of industrial-scale production (Yan et al. 2024; Nirantar 2021). Currently, natural biological enzymes refined via enzyme engineering techniques have been extensively utilized in the biosynthesis of food (Liu et al. 2023), pharmaceuticals (Miranda et al. 2023), and biofuels (Naeem et al. 2022; Tian et al. 2022).

Common strategies in enzyme engineering primarily encompass directed evolution, rational design, and semi-rational design (Silva et al. 2022). Among these approaches, directed evolution operates independently of prior knowledge regarding enzyme structure and function. Random mutagenesis libraries are constructed through techniques such as error-prone polymerase chain reaction or DNA shuffling, followed by extensive screening and analysis to identify desirable mutants (Fig. 1) (Quin and Schmidt-Dannert 2021; Radley et al. 2023). This methodology often demands considerable time investment, thereby significantly constraining the broader application of directed evolution strategies (Yu et al. 2022). In contrast, rational design necessitates detailed information concerning protein structure and function, combined with computational tools to precisely predict key residues for site-directed mutagenesis (Fig. 1) (Silva et al. 2022; Bilal and Iqbal 2019). Although this strategy circumvents the limitations of large-scale screening by generating more compact mutant libraries, its practical implementation remains limited due to the intricate nature of the structure–function relationship, which hinders a comprehensive understanding of the roles played by specific active-site residues. Semi-rational design integrates the strengths of both directed evolution and rational design and is categorized into structure-based and sequence-based methodologies (Ali et al. 2020). The structure-based variant employs homologous sequence alignment, computer-aided homology modeling, or substrate binding simulation based on the enzyme’s three-dimensional crystal structure (Fig. 1). This approach facilitates the identification of key residues within the active pocket or at other critical loci involved in enzyme–substrate interactions. Efficient mutant libraries are subsequently developed using methods such as site-directed mutagenesis or combinatorial active site saturation mutagenesis (O’Maille et al. 2002; Lutz 2010; Quin and Schmidt-Dannert 2021). Notably, structure-based semi-rational design has achieved over a 163-fold enhancement in the catalytic efficiency of nitrobenzyl esterase pnbA (Zhou et al. 2024a), and has demonstrated the capacity to modify catalytic activity, yielding novel enzymatic products (Xu and Dickschat 2023; Zhang et al. 2024; Wu et al. 2025). Meanwhile, sequence-based semi-rational design leverages the evolutionary conservation observed in homologous protein sequences. Through multiple sequence alignment, consensus sequences or residues that frequently recur in related proteins can be identified. From an evolutionary perspective, amino acids crucial to enzyme function tend to be conserved. Consequently, bioinformatics analysis enables the substitution of non-consensus residues with consensus ones, or the replacement of key amino acids with those more prevalent in homologous sequences, thereby enhancing the properties of the target enzymes (Ali et al. 2020; Porebski and Buckle 2016; Xu et al. 2020). Research on the copper-zinc superoxide dismutase RrCuZnSOD from Rosa roxburghii utilizing conserved sequence-based semi-rational design revealed that mutants generated via consensus sequence strategies could partially resolve the trade-off between enzymatic activity and stability, ultimately improving both characteristics (Zhao et al. 2024).

As investigations into natural terpene compounds have progressed, their pharmacological properties and commercial potential have been progressively unveiled. Nonetheless, the use of microbial cell factories for the biosynthesis of high-value terpene natural products necessitates the resolution of inherent limitations associated with native enzymes, such as limited activity and poor stability. In recent years, the increasing sophistication of bioinformatics tools and the detailed elucidation of enzyme three-dimensional crystal structures have rendered semi-rational design a practical and effective strategy for enhancing the catalytic functions of natural terpene synthases. This review provides an overview of the semi-rational design strategies applied to key enzymes across various terpene biosynthetic pathways, as well as recent advancements concerning the functional implementation of beneficial mutations in terpene biosynthesis, with the objective of offering a reference for optimizing the catalytic efficiency of related enzymes involved in terpene metabolic routes.

Semi-rational design of prenyltransferase

Enhancing precursor availability represents a widely adopted metabolic engineering strategy within the realm of synthetic biology. Geranyl diphosphate (GPP) functions as the immediate precursor for monoterpene biosynthesis. Through the semi-rational engineering of farnesyl diphosphate (FPP) synthase Erg20p in yeast, the competition posed by FPP for GPP can be diminished, thereby directing a greater proportion of carbon flux toward monoterpene biosynthetic pathways and improving monoterpene yields. K197 has been identified as a conserved residue within Erg20 (Plochocka et al. 2000; Fischer et al. 2011). Replacement of K197 with amino acids such as G, E, A, S, T, D, C, or L has been demonstrated to significantly enhance monoterpene production, achieving titers as high as 5 mg/L (Fischer et al. 2011). F96, located within the active site of Erg20 (Ignea et al. 2014), has also been a target of mutagenesis. In strains of Saccharomyces cerevisiae expressing the sabinene synthase SpSabS1 from Salvia pomifera, the introduction of the Erg20F96W variant resulted in a threefold increase in sabinene production relative to wild-type Erg20, reaching 0.18 ± 0.02 mg/g. Further evaluation of the steady-state kinetic parameters of Erg20 revealed that the F96W mutation reduced both the enzyme’s substrate affinity for GPP and its kcat, thereby increasing intracellular GPP availability. Building upon prior crystal structure analyses of the avian farnesyl diphosphate synthase dimer (Tarshis et al. 1994; Stanley Fernandez et al. 2000), the Erg20N127W mutant was subsequently co-expressed with SpSabS1 in S. cerevisiae, resulting in a sixfold enhancement in sabinene production to 0.312 ± 0.024 mg/L. The dual mutation Erg20F96W−N127W further amplified sabinene titers to 0.53 mg/L. When integrated with additional metabolic engineering interventions, including promoter engineering and the use of fusion proteins, sabinene production ultimately reached 17.5 ± 2.065 mg/L (Ignea et al. 2014).

When Erg20K197E and Erg20K197G were respectively employed in the biosynthesis of (S)-linalool and geraniol, a redistribution of GPP and FPP was achieved. In conjunction with additional regulatory strategies, the resulting yields of (S)-linalool and geraniol ultimately reached 101.55 μg/L and 36.04 mg/L, respectively (Deng et al. 2016; Liu et al. 2013).

Integration of the functional roles of upstream rate-limiting enzymes in terpenoid biosynthetic pathways is also essential for enhancing catalytic efficiency, as it enables the shortening of reaction cascades while mitigating excessive precursor consumption by competing pathways. Semi-rational design has been recognized as an effective approach in this context. Under acidic culture conditions, 8-hydroxycopalyl diphosphate synthase CcCLS from Cistus creticus was heterologously expressed in S. cerevisiae to facilitate sclareol production. To alleviate the limitations imposed by the modular enzymatic architecture of FPP and geranylgeranyl diphosphate (GGPP) synthesis steps on sclareol biosynthesis, and to augment GGPP availability, key active-site residues of Erg20 were mutated based on its structural configuration. Two high-efficiency mutants, F96C and Y95L/F96I, capable of concurrently catalyzing the synthesis of both FPP and GGPP, were subsequently identified. Expansion of the active site was found to enhance the binding efficiency between FPP and Erg20p, thereby preventing feedback inhibition resulting from FPP accumulation and minimizing its depletion through competing pathways. In comparison to strains harboring wild-type Erg20p, sclareol titers exhibited an increase exceeding 70-fold. Following the construction of a fusion protein comprising mutant F96C and CcCLS, alongside pathway optimization, sclareol production reached 42.7 mg/L (Ignea et al. 2015). This strategy is equally applicable to the biosynthesis of other diterpene compounds, offering new perspectives for the production of GGPP-derived isoprenoid molecules.

Carotenoids, categorized as tetraterpene-derived natural compounds exhibiting antioxidant bioactivity, typically encompass lycopene, β-carotene, and astaxanthin. GGPP, synthesized by geranylgeranyl diphosphate synthase (GGPPS) through the catalysis of three isopentenyl pyrophosphate molecules with one dimethylallyl pyrophosphate molecule, functions as the immediate precursor in carotenoid biosynthesis (Terao 2023; Tan and Norhaizan 2019). To alleviate the rate-limiting step within carotenoid biosynthetic pathways, the semi-rational engineering of GGPPS has been demonstrated as a viable methodology for augmenting catalytic efficacy. Based on results from homology modeling and molecular docking of OsGGPPS1, the variants V162A, M218S, and F227Y manifested elevated enzymatic activity relative to the wild-type. Additionally, mutations A22R and A26P, identified through ancestral sequence analysis of OsGGPPS1, similarly resulted in enhanced catalytic performance. The composite mutant d-OsGGPPS1 (A22R/A26P/V162A/M218S/F227Y), incorporating all aforementioned beneficial substitutions, exhibited concurrent improvement in both catalytic efficiency and thermal stability by reducing steric hindrance between side chains and substrates and reinforcing enzyme–substrate affinity. At 25 °C, the production of GPP, FPP, and GGPP was elevated by 9.8-fold, 6.4-fold, and 1.4-fold, respectively, while enzymatic activity remained markedly superior to the wild-type at 50 °C (Wang et al. 2024a). This methodological framework offers valuable perspectives on carotenoid biosynthetic enhancement and enzyme engineering and constitutes a technically feasible solution to the limited precursor availability in tetraterpene compound synthesis.

Semi-rational design of monoterpene synthases

A biosynthetic route for the monoterpene alcohol borneol was established via heterologous expression of Lippia dulcis truncated bornyl diphosphate synthase (LdtBPPS) in Escherichia coli (Lei et al. 2021). Homology modeling of LdtBPPS was conducted using the crystal structure of the SoBPPS–BPP complex (PDB: 1N24) (Whittington et al. 2002) as a structural reference. This was followed by site-directed mutagenesis of residues proximal to the active pocket, guided by molecular docking with bornyl diphosphate, to identify amino acid positions that influence catalytic efficiency. Relative to the wild-type LdtBPPS, the variants I382L, S488T, L526S, and F612Y yielded markedly enhanced borneol production, with the S488T mutant delivering a 2.4-fold increase in borneol output compared to the wild-type-expressing strain. When integrated with additional metabolic engineering strategies and fermentation optimization, the final concentration of borneol attained 87.20 mg/L. In vitro assays further revealed that the S488T variant exhibited elevated enzymatic activity along with improved soluble protein expression. Moreover, the substitution of F612 with A was found to compromise enzyme–substrate binding stability, thereby promoting the transformation of the expected borneol product into nerol. This enzymatic shift resulted in a nerol concentration of 68.77 mg/L, representing a 24% elevation over that achieved by strains expressing the wild-type nerol synthase from Glycine max (Lei et al. 2021).

A structure-guided semi-rational engineering approach was applied to linalool synthase t67OMcLISM, wherein site-directed mutagenesis targeted residue F447, the sole strongly hydrophobic amino acid in proximity to the substrate-binding pocket. Substitution of F447 with residues E, S, Q, A, M, and G was found to enhance the catalytic efficiency of t67OMcLISM toward GPP (Fig. 2A). Among these variants, the F447E mutation was particularly effective in decreasing steric hindrance and hydrophobicity within the binding pocket, thereby improving enzymatic performance and yielding a 2.2-fold elevation in linalool production (Zhou et al. 2023). Upon co-expression with Erg20F96W−N127W (Lei et al. 2021) to augment GPP availability, the strain expressing t67OMcLISMF447E exhibited an 80% enhancement in linalool output relative to wild-type t67OMcLISM. When further integrated with metabolic engineering interventions, including peroxisomal pathway optimization, a final linalool titer of 219.1 mg/L was achieved under shake flask fermentation conditions (Zhou et al. 2023).

Additionally, semi-rational engineering strategies have been employed to optimize the catalytic performance of other monoterpene synthases. For instance, a semi-rational design was applied to elucidate key active-site residues influencing both catalytic activity and structural stability in the nerol synthase PgfB from Penicillium griseofulvum (Li et al. 2024). In the case of (+)-limonene synthase ((+)-LS), structure-guided modifications of its active site revealed mechanistic relationships among its three alternative substrates: GPP, linalyl diphosphate (LPP), and neryl diphosphate (NPP). Although residues T446 and F484 do not act as direct catalytic bases, they modulated the enzyme’s product profile by altering substrate–catalytic base interactions. Variants T446A/C/V and F484A/L were found to produce novel non-natural monoterpenes, α-pinene, and sabinene, respectively (Schiff and Oprian 2023). These findings suggest novel pathways for structure-based semi-rational engineering of terpene synthases and contribute valuable perspectives for expanding monoterpene synthase product diversity. Semi-rational design of sesquiterpene synthase

AaBOS and AaADS, identified as α-bisabolol synthase and amorpha-4,11-diene synthase respectively, share high sequence similarity and were isolated from Artemisia annua, with AaADS functioning as a pivotal enzyme in the artemisinin biosynthetic pathway (Li et al. 2013; Bouwmeester et al. 1999). Based on the crystal structure of AaBOS and informed by structural insights, chimeric proteins were generated through a domain-swapping strategy to localize active sites responsible for the product specificity of AaBOS and AaADS. A BOS-specific motif consisting of V373, I395, N398, and L399 near the substrate-binding pocket was identified. The L399T mutation substantially altered AaBOS product specificity, resulting in γ-humulene production, a characteristic byproduct of AaADS, while the L381A variant further enhanced its yield. In the case of AaADS mutants, T399S significantly improved amorpha-4,11-diene production and exhibited greater thermal and pH stability than the wild-type AaADS (Li et al. 2013). Although domain-swapping differs from traditional semi-rational design, which directly targets residues near the active site, this approach offered a novel perspective for the simultaneous engineering of two homologous enzymes. Furthermore, to augment artemisinin biosynthetic capacity, a separate investigation employed a semi-rational design to re-optimize active-site residues of AaADS based on its reported three-dimensional model (Abdallah et al. 2016). Among the mutants identified, substitutions at H392 and H448 demonstrated enhanced catalytic activity, particularly H448A, which exhibited a fourfold increase in catalytic efficiency over the wild-type. Upon heterologous expression in E. coli, this variant achieved a threefold increase in amorpha-4,11-diene production. Residue T399, responsible for regioselective deprotonation, was confirmed to govern the specificity of amorpha-4,11-diene synthesis, consistent with previous findings by Li et al. (Abdallah et al. 2018; Li et al. 2013). The combined mutant T399S/H448A displayed further enhancement in catalytic activity. When expressed in E. coli, this dual mutant yielded a fourfold increase in amorpha-4,11-diene titer under shake flask fermentation conditions, reaching 2.7 mg/L (Abdallah et al. 2018), thereby representing a more efficient engineered biocatalyst for artemisinin biosynthesis.

Patchoulol is a naturally occurring sesquiterpene compound widely utilized in the pharmaceutical and fragrance industries (Fatima et al. 2023; Luo et al. 2023). To further enhance microbial patchoulol biosynthesis, a structure-based semi-rational design was applied to engineer patchoulol synthase (PTS). Homology modeling of PTS was conducted using the crystal structure of AaBOS as a reference, and site-directed mutagenesis targeting residues within the active site was implemented based on prior structural analyses, wherein residue 404 in PTS corresponds to residue 399 in AaBOS and AaADS (Liu et al. 2021; Li et al. 2013). It was demonstrated that the mutations C405G, T404S, H454A, and H454C improved patchoulol yield, with T404S notably enhancing catalytic efficiency while concurrently optimizing product selectivity and reducing byproduct accumulation. Expression of T404S in S. cerevisiae, in combination with additional optimization strategies, led to a patchoulol titer of 141.5 mg/L under shake flask fermentation conditions (Liu et al. 2021). In a separate study, Zhou et al. targeted residues near the Mg2⁺ binding site of PTS for mutagenesis, identifying H454A, K458A, and K458L as beneficial variants that increased enzymatic activity and raised patchoulol production in E. coli by 76.9%, 65.2%, and 76.1%, respectively (Zhou et al. 2021), with the result for H454A corroborating previous findings in S. cerevisiae (Liu et al. 2021). Furthermore, multiple sequence alignment revealed that replacement of C415 with the consensus residue F yielded mutant C415F, which both reduced byproduct levels and enhanced patchoulol titer by 95.8%. The construction of the double mutant C415F/H454A exhibited additive effects from both single-point mutations, resulting in a 2.9-fold increase in patchoulol production, reaching 338.6 mg/L in E. coli via shake flask fermentation (Zhou et al. 2021). To develop a PTS variant with higher catalytic efficiency, Zhou et al. introduced the G457E/S103N combination to construct PTS3, followed by structure-guided mutation H390A and sequence alignment-based mutations H108Y and V481A, which raised patchoulol titers by 89.1%, 63.5%, and 78.6%, respectively. When compared to H390A alone, the four-point mutant PTS3mut4 (V481A/H108Y/V504T/H390A) further elevated the patchoulol titer by 6.9%, ultimately achieving a production level 11-fold greater than that of C415F/H454A (Zhou et al. 2021). Following codon optimization and incorporation of regulatory strategies such as a glycolysis-control device mediated by pyruvate sensing, patchoulol production under shake flask conditions increased to 497.4 mg/L. Upon scale-up in a 5 L bioreactor, a final patchoulol titer of 1,675.1 mg/L was attained, with a volumetric productivity of 239.3 mg/L/d (Zhou et al. 2024b).

Two (+)-valencene synthases, CnVS and EgVS, originating from Callitropsis nootkatensis and Eryngium glaciale, respectively, catalyze the synthesis of (+)-valencene, a direct precursor to the high-value sesquiterpene compound (+)-nootkatone (Guo et al. 2022a; Ye et al. 2022; Meng et al. 2020). The structure of CnVS comprises an N-terminal domain and a C-terminal domain, with its catalytic active site for substrate reactions located within a hydrophobic pocket in the C-terminal domain. The entrance to this active site also contains two conserved aspartate-rich regions—DDIYD and DDVKD (Fig. 2B). Structural and functional analysis revealed that substitution of residue M560 with leucine within the substrate-binding pocket of CnVS—known to participate in C14 alkyl transfer—enhanced enzyme–substrate affinity, thereby increasing (+)-valencene yield per cell in yeast by 60%. In contrast, replacement with alanine led to a marked decline in production, attributed to disrupted enzyme–substrate interactions. Expression of the M560L variant in metabolically engineered S. cerevisiae resulted in (+)-nootkatone titers of 85.43 mg/L under shake flask fermentation and 804.96 mg/L in a 5 L bioreactor (Guo et al. 2022a). Additionally, structural modifications of EgVS improved its catalytic performance, with the EgVSI533V/R336K mutant substantially enhancing (+)-valencene biosynthesis. Building upon this, additional mutations—D176E, R306K, and K325E—were identified via sequence alignment and conservative residue substitution, each contributing positively to (+)-valencene production. Integration of these beneficial variants yielded a six-point mutant, EgVS mut6 (I533V, R336K, H196R, D176E, R306K, K325E), which demonstrated additive effects from single-point mutations and further elevated enzymatic efficiency. Compared to the wild-type EgVS, expression of EgVS mut6 in engineered S. cerevisiae resulted in a 3.15-fold increase in (+)-valencene production, achieving a final yield of 16.6 g/L under fed-batch fermentation (Ye et al. 2022). These enzyme engineering efforts for CnVS and EgVS indicated that conformational alterations in distal enzyme regions could indirectly modulate protein stability, enhancing the flexibility of the enzyme–substrate binding site and ultimately improving catalytic efficiency (Guo et al. 2022a; Ye et al. 2022).

ClSS and SanSyn, both identified as santalene synthases derived from Clausena lansium, have undergone yield enhancement and product selectivity optimization via semi-rational design approaches (Zhang et al. 2022a; Zha et al. 2022). Substitution of residue S533 with alanine in ClSS introduced two additional hydrogen bonds at the mutation site, thereby increasing enzyme stability. The α-santalene yield of the S533A mutant expressed in E. coli was slightly greater than that of S533Q and 1.7-fold higher than that of the wild-type ClSS-expressing strain. Upon integration with multiple optimization strategies, α-santalene production under shake flask fermentation reached 1,272 mg/L (Zhang et al. 2022a). The initially product-specific enzyme SanSyn was re-engineered through a semi-rational design targeting key residues within the active-site cavity, resulting in modified product specificity. The F441V mutation enlarged the cavity space and improved the conformational flexibility of catalytic intermediates, enabling the general base T298 to catalyze the formation of both α- and β-santalenes, thereby altering the product profile. Subsequent mutation of T298 to alanine eliminated its deprotonation function, which led to a pronounced reduction in the synthesis of both α- and β-santalenes. Expression of F441V in engineered S. cerevisiae, followed by fermentation optimization, yielded maximum titers of 842.7 mg/L santalenes and 1,075.7 mg/L santalols. Although an increase in CYP736A167 gene copy number elevated the metabolic burden in yeast and reduced the total yield to 704.2 mg/L, the Z-α-santalol to Z-β-santalol ratio met the quality standard established for Santalum album L. oil by the International Organization for Standardization (Zha et al. 2022).

Additionally, semi-rational design optimization of α-farnesene synthase CsAFS from Camellia sinensis and germacrene A synthase AvGAS from Anabaena variabilis, in conjunction with metabolic engineering strategies, enabled the successful construction of efficient S. cerevisiae cell factories for the biosynthesis of α-farnesene and germacrene A, respectively (Wang et al. 2023; Zhang et al. 2021). In the case of CsAFS, mutants generated by substituting non-consensus residues with consensus residues demonstrated enhanced performance. Specifically, variants W281C and C455M achieved 1.9-fold and 1.7-fold increases in α-farnesene production relative to the wild-type, whereas the double mutant W281C/C455M did not exhibit additive effects. As a result, CsAFSW281C was selected as the optimal variant for α-farnesene biosynthesis, yielding 2.8 g/L under shake flask fermentation and reaching 28.3 g/L in a 5 L bioreactor when combined with additional metabolic engineering modifications (Wang et al. 2023). In contrast, structure-based optimization of AvGAS yielded suboptimal outcomes. Therefore, a homologous sequence alignment-guided strategy was employed to identify activity-related residues for targeted mutagenesis. Among the resulting variants, F23W and F23V significantly improved catalytic efficiency. Compared to strains expressing wild-type AvGAS, S. cerevisiae strains expressing AvGASF23W and AvGASF23V exhibited 35.2% and 21.8% increases in germacrene A production, respectively (Zhang et al. 2021).

CrTPS18, a 5-epi-jinkoheremol synthase from C. roseus, has been structurally investigated through homology modeling and molecular docking, resulting in enhanced catalytic activity and improved enzyme stability. The T414S mutation was found to facilitate the dissociation of hydrophobic products and to expand the active-site pocket, leading to a 1.9-fold increase in 5-epi-jinkoheremol production relative to the wild-type TPS18. Moreover, the introduction of proline at position I21, located within a flexible region of the TPS18 structure, conferred greater structural rigidity and yielded a 1.3-fold enhancement in product formation compared to the T414S variant. By integrating both modifications, the double mutant I21P/T414S was expressed at multi-copy genomic sites in engineered yeast. Upon optimization of fermentation conditions, 5-epi-jinkoheremol production reached 875.25 mg/L under shake flask cultivation (Wang et al. 2024b).

Beyond the optimization of common enzymatic properties such as catalytic activity and structural stability, the semi-rational design of selina-4(15),7(11)-diene synthase (SdS) has enabled modification of its hydroxylation capability, allowing the biosynthesis of the specific hydroxylated product selin-7(11)-en-4-ol. Molecular docking and structural analyses revealed that key residues within the K-helix region of SdS could interact with water molecules in the active site. Although substitutions at W304 with serine or glutamic acid (W304S and W304E, respectively) increased the availability of water molecules in the catalytic pocket, these variants did not yield selin-7(11)-en-4-ol; instead, germacrene B emerged as the predominant product. In contrast, the mutation of residue G305 to glutamic acid repositioned water molecules closer to the reaction center, thereby facilitating carbocation quenching and promoting the formation of selin-7(11)-en-4-ol, with the yield reaching 48% at pH 6.0. Upon introduction of the mevalonate pathway into E. coli and heterologous expression of the G305E variant, selin-7(11)-en-4-ol production attained 10 mg/L under batch fermentation conditions (Srivastava et al. 2024).

Sesquiterpene synthases exhibit considerable structural and functional diversity, and the semi-rational design of such enzymes serves as a pivotal approach for elucidating the relationship between enzyme architecture and catalytic function. In one study, the sesquiterpene ether synthase SsLOS was identified from the medicinal plant Senecio scandens, and through semi-rational design coupled with mutational analysis of key active-site residues, hedycaryol was established as a catalytic intermediate (Wang et al. 2021). Additionally, enzyme engineering investigations of the (+)-δ-cadinol synthases Copu5 and Copu9 from Coniophora puteana, along with the cubebol synthase AcCop4 from Antrodia cinnamomea, demonstrated that the helix–turn–helix motif is integral to product profile modulation in class I terpene synthases. Furthermore, the contour of the active site was shown to be essential for directing the cyclization cascade (Chen et al. 2023a; Ringel et al. 2022). These findings offer valuable perspectives for future enzyme engineering and lay a robust foundation for the biosynthesis of corresponding sesquiterpenes.

Semi-rational design of diterpene synthases

Levopimaradiene (LP), the precursor of the diterpene resin acid levopimaric acid (LA) in plants, undergoes conversion to LA catalyzed by CYP720B1, conferring substantial industrial application value (Liu et al. 2018; Schepmann et al. 2001). To address the bottleneck in the downstream synthetic pathways of LA biosynthesis in E. coli, a semi-rational design strategy targeting levopimaradiene synthase (LPS) was employed, successfully enhancing its catalytic efficiency and minimizing byproduct formation. Guided by phylogeny-based mutation principles and the identification of amino acid residues within the substrate-binding pocket potentially influencing catalytic activity, various single-point mutants were generated and expressed in E. coli. Although the mutants M593I, C618N, L696Q, K723S, V731L, N838E, and I855L significantly elevated diterpene yields, the product distribution profile of the enzyme remained unchanged. Among these, M593I achieved the highest yield, exhibiting a 3.7-fold increase compared to wild-type LPS, consistent with the results observed in S. cerevisiae (Leonard et al. 2010; Liu et al. 2018). Mutation Y700H notably altered product distribution, and subsequent saturation mutagenesis at this locus revealed that Y700F, Y700M, and Y700W not only augmented diterpene production by approximately fivefold but also suppressed the generation of the byproduct abietadiene. Combinatorial mutation of M593I with Y700 variants demonstrated that M593I/Y700F led to a tenfold enhancement in diterpene yield, whereas M593I/Y700A and M593I/Y700C elevated the LP proportion in the product mixture to 97%, with yields increasing approximately 6.3-fold and 5.8-fold, respectively. In bioreactor-scale experiments, co-expression of the directed evolution mutant S239C/G295D of GGPPS with the semi-rationally engineered LPS mutant M593I/Y700F in metabolically engineered E. coli resulted in an LP yield of 700 mg/L, corresponding to an approximate 2,600-fold improvement over the original strain (Leonard et al. 2010). Additionally, expression of T79LPS—with a 79 amino acid N-terminal truncation—together with M593I and Y700F mutations in metabolically engineered S. cerevisiae produced an LP yield of 49.21 mg/L. Integration of cytochrome P450 reductases TcCPR and CYP720B1 from Taxus cuspidata into the yeast genome at multi-copy loci further elevated LA yield to 45.24 mg/L under shake flask fermentation. This value was subsequently enhanced to 400.31 mg/L following fed-batch fermentation in a 5 L bioreactor (Liu et al. 2018).

Bnd4 serves as the catalytic enzyme in the initial step of bacterial diterpenoid benditerpenoic acid biosynthesis, mediating the conversion of GGPP into the eunicellane-type diterpenoid benditerpe-2,6,15-triene, characterized by a 6,10-bicyclic skeleton. Notably, Bnd4 represents the first eunicellane synthase to be identified in nature (Zhu et al. 2021; Xu et al. 2021). The influence of aromatic residues within the active site on Bnd4 enzymatic function was explored through semi-rational design strategies. The F162A variant was found to interfere with the cyclization of GGPP, leading to the formation of additional products, namely geranylgeraniol and geranyllinalool, alongside benditerpe-2,6,15-triene. In contrast, the Y197A mutation resulted in a complete loss of benditerpe-2,6,15-triene synthesis, likely due to disrupted hydrophobic interactions at the mutated site, and instead produced geranylgeraniol, geranyllinalool, and β-springene. Substitution of W316 with Ala or His altered the binding conformation between GGPP and Bnd4, decreasing the spatial distance between C1 and C14 and ultimately facilitating the generation of various cembranoid-type diterpenes possessing 14-membered macrocyclic structures (Xu et al. 2022).

Cattleyene synthase CyS, a diterpene synthase derived from Streptomyces cattleya, has had its catalytic mechanism elucidated through the application of semi-rational design and isotope labeling approaches. Mutations F86A and W160A were observed to diminish the stability of catalytic intermediates, thereby altering the reaction trajectories. Structural analysis of the C59A mutant revealed that the interaction between this site and residue F86 was disrupted, which induced a rotational shift in F86 and resulted in an expanded active-site cavity. This spatial alteration facilitated substrate access to the catalytic pocket while simultaneously diminishing enzymatic selectivity, consequently yielding novel diterpene compounds (Xing et al. 2022). In parallel, the Y88W and T112W variants of the venezuelaene A synthase VenA, originating from Streptomyces venezuelae, were found to constrict the active-site pocket, thereby transforming the enzyme from a diterpene synthase into one that exclusively catalyzes the formation of monoterpene and sesquiterpene products (Li et al. 2023a).

Semi-rational design of triterpene synthases

Squalene epoxidase (SQE) functions as a pivotal enzyme within the triterpene biosynthetic pathway, catalyzing the transformation of squalene into 2,3-oxidosqualene, which is subsequently subjected to cyclization by oxidosqualene cyclase (OSC) to generate specific triterpene compounds (Hou et al. 2021). As a result, the engineering of both SQE and OSC has been extensively employed to enhance triterpene biosynthetic production.

Research has demonstrated that alleviating the rate-limiting constraint imposed by SQE on β-amyrin biosynthesis in S. cerevisiae enables efficient production of β-amyrin, prompting the selection of squalene epoxidase OsSQE52 from Oryza sativa for structure-based semi-rational design. Residues located within 5 Å of the squalene binding pocket in OsSQE52 were systematically screened through two iterative rounds of mutagenesis involving alanine scanning and saturation mutagenesis. Mutations at positions L256, M470, and G491 were identified to significantly influence β-amyrin biosynthesis, with the L256R variant forming proton transfer channels that contributed to a 1.69-fold increase in β-amyrin yield (Fig. 2C). Subsequent integration of the OsSQE52L256R mutant into the rDNA loci of the yeast genome—regions characterized by high-copy number and stable expression—combined with pathway optimization via metabolic engineering strategies, resulted in a β-amyrin titer of 66.97 mg/L under shake flask fermentation conditions (Li et al. 2023b).

Amyrin is a triterpene compound exhibiting antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and anticancer properties (Viet et al. 2021; Askari et al. 2018; Neto et al. 2021). The α-amyrin synthases ItOSC2, CsOSC3, and GsAS2, originating from Iris tectorum, Camellia sasanqua, and Gentiana straminea, respectively, have been shown to catalyze the formation of amyrin (Wu et al. 2020; Huang et al. 2022a; Liu et al. 2016). Semi-rational design analysis has elucidated the functional significance of key active sites and amino acid residues in these three OSCs with respect to catalytic performance, product specificity, and enzyme–substrate binding stability (Wu et al. 2020; Huang et al. 2022a; Liu et al. 2016). Additionally, structural refinement of α-amyrin synthase MdOSC1 was conducted through modeling analysis and sequence alignment, resulting in a substantial enhancement of its catalytic efficacy. Initially, single-point mutations targeting residues located in or proximal to the active pocket or catalytic site were introduced, leading to the identification of P373N/A/C, M373C, and L421F mutants, all of which significantly increased enzymatic activity and α-amyrin yield (Yu et al. 2020). Subsequently, considering the influence of distal conformational alterations on catalytic efficiency (Guo et al. 2022a; Ye et al. 2022), alanine substitution was applied to residues near specific motifs and within the N-terminal region. From these strategies, the optimal single-point mutants, P520A and N11A, exhibited 2.5-fold and fivefold higher catalytic activities than the wild-type MdOSC1, respectively (Yu et al. 2020). Furthermore, the combinatorial mutant N11T/P250H/P373A demonstrated a 1310% increase in α-amyrin production relative to the wild type. When expressed in metabolically engineered S. cerevisiae, the strain achieved a titer of 213.7 ± 12.4 mg/L α-amyrin in shake flask fermentation, and 1107.9 ± 76.8 mg/L under fed-batch conditions, representing the highest reported α-amyrin biosynthetic yield at that time (Yu et al. 2020). In parallel, expression of the multifunctional amyrin synthase CrMAS from Catharanthus roseus in Y. lipolytica enabled concurrent production of α-amyrin and β-amyrin. To further improve enzymatic performance, semi-rational design was employed targeting key residues within the substrate binding pocket. The resulting alanine substitutions were found to reduce steric hindrance within the substrate channel, thereby enhancing catalytic efficiency. Among the variants, L323A and L240A exhibited the highest production levels of α-amyrin and β-amyrin, respectively, with 12-fold and 11-fold improvements compared to the wild type. In engineered Y. lipolytica, L323A was selected for further production, and following optimization of fermentation conditions, the shake flask fermentation titer of amyrin reached 125 mg/L, comprising 103 mg/L α-amyrin and 22 mg/L β-amyrin (Kong et al. 2022).

Besides amyrin, numerous OSCs in nature remain to be explored and characterized, and semi-rational design has proven to be an effective strategy for elucidating structure–function relationships in enzymes. For instance, PFOSC4, PFOSC7, and PFOSC9, identified in the traditional Chinese medicinal plant Perilla frutescens, were subjected to semi-rational design, and the results demonstrated that functional interconversion could be induced through mutations at specific key residues (Huang et al. 2023). In another case, the mutant I725V/I726F/I727N of the malabaricane-type triterpene synthase AmOSC2 from Astragalus membranaceus was found to generate seven structurally distinct triterpenes, thereby broadening the diversity of the resulting compound profile (Song et al. 2023). Furthermore, EtOSC5 isolated from Euphorbia tirucalli was shown to simultaneously yield euphol and tirucallol, and mutational analyses of residues adjacent to the active site revealed that the hydrophobic microenvironment surrounding residue F261 played a determining role in euphol biosynthesis (Qiao et al. 2021), thereby confirming the cyclization mechanism of 2,3-oxidosqualene to euphol and tirucallol proposed in earlier studies (Morlacchi et al. 2009). In elucidating the biosynthetic pathway of celastrol, two multiproduct friedelin synthases, TwOSC1 and TwOSC3, involved in celastrol formation, were isolated from Tripterygium wilfordii, with friedelin—a shared product—identified as a biosynthetic precursor. Subsequently, by employing a semi-rational design approach guided by multiple sequence alignment and molecular docking, it was established that the K residue upstream of the DCTAE motif was indispensable for friedelin biosynthesis (Fig. 2D). Additionally, functional analysis of the optimal mutant TwOSC1T502E indicated that modifications within the product release channel environment significantly enhanced friedelin output. Upon expression of TwOSC1T502E in metabolically engineered yeast and optimization of fermentation conditions, the highest friedelin yield reached 37.07 mg/L, thereby achieving efficient production of friedelin in engineered yeast for the first time (Zhou et al. 2019).

Semi-rational design of tetraterpene synthase

Lycopaoctaene synthase (LOS), a tetraterpene synthase identified in Botryococcus braunii, exhibits homology and mechanistic similarity with squalene synthase. It functions as a pivotal enzyme in the lycopadiene biosynthetic pathway and is capable of utilizing FPP, GGPP, and phytyl diphosphate as substrates to produce six hydrocarbon products with C30, C35, and C40 carbon chain lengths (Thapa et al. 2016). To enable LOS-mediated production of hydrocarbons with defined chain lengths and to facilitate its industrial exploitation, key amino acid residues influencing substrate and product specificity were examined via semi-rational design strategies incorporating sequence and structural analyses. Experimental findings revealed that the single-point mutants S276Y and A288F both markedly decreased lycopaoctaene synthesis while concurrently enhancing C35H58 production. In contrast, the combined mutant S276Y/A288F led to pronounced suppression of both lycopaoctaene and C35H58 yields, yet resulted in over a threefold increase in squalene accumulation. This phenotypic shift was attributed to enhanced steric hindrance introduced upon GGPP binding to the catalytic pocket, thereby redirecting substrate preference from GGPP to FPP (Thapa et al. 2017). These findings highlight the functional relevance of residues S276 and A288 in mediating substrate selectivity in LOS, offering valuable perspectives for its industrial application.

In the β-carotene biosynthetic pathway, the accumulation of lycopene leads to substrate inhibition of lycopene cyclase, thereby impairing β-carotene production (Ma et al. 2022). Heterologous biosynthesis of β-carotene was achieved in Y. lipolytica through the introduction of phytoene desaturase CarB and the bifunctional phytoene synthase/lycopene cyclase CrtYB from Mucor circinelloides (Velayos et al. 2000). To alleviate substrate inhibition, the R domain of CrtYB—responsible for lycopene cyclase activity—was subjected to semi-rational optimization guided by evolutionary insights from homologous protein sequences. Among the resulting mutant variants, Y27R, V175W, and the combined mutant T31R-F92W significantly improved both β-carotene selectivity and yield, with Y27R entirely abolishing the inhibitory effect. The GGPPS variant GGPPxd from Xanthophyllomyces dendrorhous displayed the highest expression efficiency among all candidates tested. In a Y. lipolytica strain optimized through multiple strategies integrating GGPPxd and Y27R, a β-carotene yield of 39.5 g/L was obtained under 3 L fed-batch cultivation, with a productivity of 0.165 g/L/h, marking a 1,441-fold increase relative to the original strain (Ma et al. 2022).

Semi-rational design of CYPs

CYPs for sesquiterpene modification

In addition to the semi-rational engineering of terpene synthases, molecular modification of cytochrome P450 enzymes (CYPs) has also been shown to promote product accumulation. (+)-Valencene is converted to β-nootkatol through the coordinated action of cytochrome P450 monooxygenase and cytochrome P450 reductase, followed by subsequent oxidation via alcohol dehydrogenase to yield (+)-nootkatone (Meng et al. 2020). The biosynthetic efficiency of (+)-nootkatone can be further improved through semi-rational design-based optimization of CYPs.

Previous studies have demonstrated that the catalytic efficiency of converting (+)-valencene to (+)-nootkatone has been enhanced through semi-rational design of P450cam from Pseudomonas putida, P450BM-3 from Bacillus megaterium, and hyoscyamine 6β-hydroxylase (HPO) from Hyoscyamus muticus (Sowden et al. 2005; Takahashi et al. 2007; Liu et al. 2022). The P450cam mutant F87A/Y96F, generated via semi-rational design, enabled the otherwise inactive P450cam to acquire oxidative activity toward (+)-valencene, while the P450BM-3 mutant R47L/Y51F exhibited a significant improvement in product yield (Sowden et al. 2005). Moreover, semi-rational design targeting residues surrounding the HPO catalytic center identified V480, V482, and A484 as key positions influencing catalytic efficiency (Fig. 3A) (Takahashi et al. 2007; Liu et al. 2022). The catalytic activity of the V482A variant was found to be 2.03-fold higher than that of wild-type HPO. However, subsequent combinatorial mutation analysis indicated that simple aggregation of effective single-point mutations did not consistently result in superior performance. For instance, although the double mutant V482I/A484I displayed elevated kcat/KM values in in vitro assays, no corresponding increase in (+)-nootkatone production was observed in yeast expression systems. Combinatorial approaches targeting residues implicated in substrate access/egress or protein folding often achieved additional enhancement of catalytic function, as exemplified by the quadruple mutant HPO_M18 (G302G/V480A/V482A/A484A), which exhibited a 2.54-fold increase in catalytic activity compared to the wild-type enzyme. When HPO_M18 was expressed in engineered S. cerevisiae, (+)-nootkatone production reached 214.09 mg/L under shake flask fermentation, and following two-stage batch fermentation in a 3 L fermenter, the yield was elevated to 2.39 g/L (Liu et al. 2022).

CYPs for diterpene modification

The biosynthetic pathway of the high-value diterpene compound forskolin involves the participation of multiple CYPs, with the initial step—conversion of 13R-manoyl oxide to 11-oxo-13R-manoyl oxide—being catalyzed by CYP76AH15. Utilizing semi-rational design informed by homologous protein sequences and structural data, the A99I mutant was identified to enhance 11-oxo-13R-manoyl oxide production by 5.6-fold. Although the L366F and S235G/Y236F variants also exhibited elevated catalytic efficiency, reduced substrate specificity resulted in increased byproduct formation. Combinatorial mutants based on these substitutions produced additive enhancements, with A99I/L366F and A99I/S235G/Y236F yielding 6.2-fold and 6.5-fold improvements in 11-oxo-13R-manoyl oxide synthesis, respectively (Forman et al. 2018).

CYP76AH1 and CYP76AH3 in Salvia miltiorrhiza are CYPs exhibiting high sequence homology, both capable of sequentially catalyzing the formation of various diterpenoid compounds within the tanshinone biosynthetic pathway. Functional integration of these two CYPs has been reported to further enhance enzymatic catalytic efficiency. Homology modeling combined with molecular docking analyses identified putative active site residues potentially responsible for functional divergence between the two enzymes, which subsequently guided site-directed mutagenesis. Experimental results demonstrated that the D301E and V479F substitutions in CYP76AH1 reduced the spatial distance between the substrate and the heme-bound active oxygen, thereby increasing the likelihood of oxidation reactions. As a result, the catalytic efficiency of the CYP76AH1 double mutant D301E/V479F was significantly enhanced, while simultaneously integrating the C11 and C12 hydroxylation and C7 oxidation functionalities typically attributed to CYP76AH3 (Mao et al. 2020). The production level of 11-hydroxy sugiol in strain YJ14 expressing the D301E/V479F mutant was comparable to that observed in strain YJ51 co-expressing wild-type CYP76AH1 and CYP76AH3 (Zhou et al. 2012; Guo et al. 2016), whereas 11-hydroxy ferruginol accumulation in YJ14 markedly exceeded that in YJ51 (Mao et al. 2020). This strategy provides a theoretical foundation for the application of structure- and sequence-based semi-rational design to integrate functions of homologous genes, thereby streamlining biosynthetic pathways. The effectiveness of this approach was further supported by subsequent studies, which identified five CYPs (CYP71225–CYP71229) in Panicum virgatum capable of synergistically interacting with various diterpene synthases to generate furanoditerpenoid compounds. Among them, both the combinatorial mutant of CYP71227 and the single-point mutant S86F were found not only to catalyze all tested diterpenol substrates but also to yield a product profile closely resembling that of wild-type CYP71225. Modifications at key catalytic residues substantially influenced substrate specificity and enzymatic activity, rendering the two CYPs functionally more alike (Muchlinski et al. 2021).

CYPs for triterpene modification

PgCYP2 is a cytochrome P450 enzyme from Panax ginseng involved in ginsenoside biosynthesis, capable of catalyzing the conversion of protopanaxadiol (PPD) to protopanaxatriol (PPT), which is subsequently transformed into various PPT-type ginsenosides under the action of glycosyltransferase. Accordingly, enhancement of PgCYP2 catalytic efficiency through semi-rational design constitutes an effective strategy for promoting PPT biosynthesis. Key residues associated with PPD binding were targeted for mutagenesis using structural insights derived from homology modeling and molecular docking (Fig. 3B). Multiple amino acid substitutions at positions V272, S347, and Y450 were found to significantly increase PPT yield. Among these, the I117S variant exhibited improved catalytic performance by enhancing local polar interactions, resulting in a 31.2% increase in PPT production relative to strains expressing the wild-type enzyme. Building on this finding, the directed evolution-derived mutant T451A was combined with I117S, and the resulting I117S/T451A construct was overexpressed at multicopy loci within the engineered S. cerevisiae genome. Under shake flask cultivation conditions, this strain achieved a PPT titer of 249 mg/L (Li et al. 2021).

Semi-rational design of glycosyltransferase

Diterpene glycosyltransferases

Rebaudioside D (Reb D) and rebaudioside M (Reb M) are diterpene natural sweeteners characterized by enhanced sweetness and reduced bitterness (Prakash et al. 2014). These compounds are biosynthesized from steviol through a cascade of enzymatic reactions involving UGT85C2, UGT91D2, and UGT76G1, all derived from Stevia rebaudiana. Within this pathway, UGT76G1 serves a predominant catalytic function, facilitating the 1,3-β-D-glucosyltransferase reaction at either the C13 or C19 positions on various glycosylated substrates. This enzymatic activity ultimately leads to the formation of Reb D and Reb M alongside numerous undesirable byproducts (Richman et al. 2005; Olsson et al. 2016; Liu et al. 2019). In addition, UGTSL2 from Solanum lycopersicum and PgUGT from P. ginseng exhibit functional similarity to UGT91D2, as all three enzymes can convert rebaudioside A (Reb A) into Reb D through the addition of a glucose moiety to the glucose residue at the C19 position (Chen et al. 2020, 2022). Consequently, the refinement of glycosyltransferases (UGTs) specificity and activity is imperative for enhancing Reb D and Reb M production while minimizing the generation of ancillary metabolites.

Currently, numerous studies employing semi-rational design strategies for the optimization of UGT76G1 have been documented. Utilizing molecular docking models, non-conserved amino acid residues within the active site were identified and subjected to site-directed saturation mutagenesis. By modifying the spatial configuration, polarity, and hydrophobic character of the substrate-binding pocket, both substrate specificity and catalytic efficiency of the enzyme were enhanced. It was demonstrated that mutations such as L257G, I26W, S389F, T284G, T146G, and S283N markedly improved Reb D production while concurrently diminishing byproduct formation. Among these, T146G and S283N specifically suppressed the metabolic flux of Reb D toward the Reb M biosynthetic pathway. Moreover, H155L, K337P, and T55K were found to significantly enhance Reb M synthesis, with strains harboring K337P and TThr55K mutations achieving approximately 20% greater Reb M yields compared to those expressing wild-type UGT76G1 (Olsson et al. 2016). The T284S substitution notably enhanced the conversion rate of Reb D to Reb M, and the byproduct rebaudioside I was completely abolished when expressed in E. coli. Although the L379I variant exhibited substantial catalytic activity across all examined substrates and promoted the accumulation of Reb D and Reb M, it concomitantly increased the formation of undesirable byproducts (Liu et al. 2019). Consequently, to facilitate high-yield Reb M production, alanine scanning mutagenesis targeting residues proximal to the Reb D binding region was performed on the T284S mutant, guided by molecular docking analysis. This strategy aimed to identify variants exhibiting superior catalytic performance. As a result, M88A, I199A, and L200A were found to significantly augment the catalytic efficiency of T284S. Follow-up saturation mutagenesis generated the M88L and I199F variants, which further enhanced T284S activity by 1.67-fold and 1.63-fold, respectively, while no improvement was observed with alternative mutations at the L200 position. Furthermore, molecular docking and 200 ns of unconstrained molecular dynamics simulations revealed that the T284S/M88L/L200A triple mutant enhanced T284S activity by 2.38-fold. This improvement was attributed to a remodeled hydrophobic pocket that allowed better accommodation of Reb D and UDP-glucose, thereby reducing their distance from the catalytic center and stabilizing the active conformation. Ultimately, the optimized cascade enzymatic system achieved a Reb M yield of 90.5% (Guo et al. 2022b).

When Reb A was employed as the substrate for Reb D synthesis, UGTSL2 exhibited limited catalytic efficiency and concurrently produced the byproduct rebaudioside M2 (Reb M2). Through homology modeling and structural analysis, the N358 residue, located proximal to the substrate-binding region, was identified as a candidate for saturation mutagenesis. Mutants N358F, F358C, and F358M were all found to elevate Reb D yields. Although N358C led to a reduction in Reb M2 formation, its catalytic activity was inferior to that of the wild-type enzyme. Conversely, N358M exhibited enhanced activity. However, Reb M2 production was also increased. Among the variants, only N358F demonstrated a higher conversion rate of Reb A compared to wild-type UGTSL2 within 24 h and significantly suppressed Reb M2 formation. In a multi-enzyme system composed of UGT76G1 and Solanum tuberosum sucrose synthase StSUS1, the N358F mutant converted 20 g/L stevioside to 14.4 g/L Reb D within 24 h, representing a 1.6-fold increase relative to the wild type (Chen et al. 2020). In a separate study, UGT94E13 from Gardenia jasminoides was shown to glycosylate the C13 position of Reb M2 to produce rebaudioside M9. Mutations F169A, I185A, and E269A, located adjacent to the catalytic pocket, significantly enhanced enzyme activity. The F169A/I185A double mutant displayed a 12-fold increase in catalytic efficiency relative to wild-type UGT94E13, outperforming the simple combination of the three single-site mutations. Within the optimized cascade reaction, the yield of rebaudioside M9 reached 98.3% (Guo et al. 2025). Collectively, these findings, along with the semi-rational engineering of CsAFS and HPO, indicated that the additive effects of single-site forward mutations may be insufficient for achieving optimal catalytic efficiency. Instead, the identification of synergistic mutation combinations with cooperative functionality may yield superior enhancements, effectively producing a “1 + 1 > 2” outcome.

Meanwhile, the computer-aided semi-rational design yielded an optimized PgUGT variant with simultaneously enhanced catalytic activity and thermal stability, rendering it more suitable for the industrial production of Reb D. Structural models were generated through two independent approaches: homologous protein structures from the PDB database and the RoseTTAFold web-based tool (https://robetta.bakerlab.org/) (Baek et al. 2021). These models were subsequently analyzed using the FireProt platform (https://loschmidt.chemi.muni.cz/fireprot/) (Musil et al. 2017) to predict mutation sites potentially influencing enzymatic activity and thermal stability. A cross-comparison of the two predictive outputs identified 16 candidate single-point mutations for optimization. Using Reb A as the substrate, both the thermostability and enzymatic activity of the variants were independently evaluated. The results revealed that mutants A11L, F39Y, S55P, N109K, A250E, I279L, V304L, and T329I exhibited enhanced catalytic performance and improved thermal tolerance. These eight favorable mutations were subsequently integrated into a single variant, designated as Mut8, which demonstrated a 3.2-fold increase in enzymatic activity and a 2.14-fold enhancement in catalytic efficiency relative to the wild-type PgUGT. Regarding thermostability, Mut8 maintained over 93% residual activity after 2 h of incubation at 35 °C, whereas the wild-type retained only 38.5%. Furthermore, 59% residual activity was sustained after 2 h at 40 °C (Chen et al. 2022). The application of computer-guided mutational prediction followed by cross-validation enabled more robust site selection during semi-rational enzyme engineering, concurrently yielding an efficient and thermostable biocatalyst for Reb D biosynthesis.

Triterpene glycosyltransferases

The glycosylation reaction catalyzed by triterpene UGTs constitutes the primary rate-limiting step in the biosynthetic pathway of triterpene saponins. By employing semi-rational design and engineering strategies to optimize the selective catalytic efficiency of key triterpene UGTs, a substantial enhancement in triterpene saponin production can be achieved.

Ginsenoside Rg3 and ginsenoside Rh1 possess broad application prospects in the pharmaceutical field (Wang et al. 2022, 2024c; Zheng et al. 2023). Through a semi-rational design approach, the glycosyltransferase Pq3-O-UGT2, involved in the Rg3 biosynthetic pathway, was optimized via site-directed mutagenesis targeting key residues in the N-terminal domain associated with sugar receptor ginsenoside Rh2 binding. The resulting mutants S49R/I50M and H85Y markedly improved catalytic efficiency, with kcat values elevated by 1.90-fold and 1.65-fold, respectively. Molecular dynamics simulations of the triple mutant S49R/I50M/H85Y indicated that extension of the binding pocket and formation of new hydrogen bonds between residue R91 and Rh2 facilitated a more rapid and stable enzyme–substrate interaction, resulting in a kcat/KM value 1.30-fold higher than that of the wild-type enzyme. In conjunction with strategies such as suppression of competing pathways and overexpression of phenylalanine ammonia lyase, the S49R/I50M/H85Y variant was expressed in ginseng roots cultivated in shake flasks, leading to Rg3 accumulation of 7.0 mg/g dry weight—representing a 21.12-fold increase relative to wild-type expression (Yao et al. 2022). In Rh1 biosynthesis, the cooperative effect of M320H/P321Y within the PSPG motif of UGTBL1 from Bacillus licheniformis, together with I62R located adjacent to the active pocket, substantially enhanced 6-O regioselectivity by modulating substrate orientation and flexible loop conformation, respectively. Furthermore, the N170A mutation at the substrate channel entrance significantly increased catalytic efficiency by enlarging the substrate-binding channel. The quadruple mutant I62R/M320H/P321Y/N170A demonstrated a 6.8-fold enhancement in specific enzyme activity, with 6-O regioselectivity improved from 10.98% (wild type) to 96.26%. Ultimately, Rh1 production reached 20.48 g/L in a fed-batch cascade reaction during in vitro biosynthesis (Chu et al. 2024). Additionally, the improved catalytic efficiency of the UGT227 mutant I83A/F285M was likewise attributed to the expansion of the substrate-binding channel. When sucrose was utilized as the carbon source in a co-expression system with Arabidopsis thaliana sucrose synthase, Rh1 yield attained 99.99% (Deng et al. 2025).

Astragalosides, as bioactive constituents of the traditional Chinese medicine A. membranaceus (Su et al. 2021), demonstrate prominent pharmacological properties, including hepatoprotective (Hao et al. 2024), antiviral (Indu et al. 2021), and anti-inflammatory effects (Qi et al. 2017). To enable the synthesis of structurally diverse astragalosides, semi-rational design strategies have previously been applied to optimize the UGTs AmGT1 and AmGT8 from A. membranaceus, revealing the pivotal roles of residues 146 and 394 in determining sugar donor specificity and regioselectivity, respectively (Chen et al. 2023b; Zhang et al. 2022b). The AmGT1 mutant G146V, while losing activity toward UDP-glucose, exhibited markedly enhanced selectivity and catalytic activity for UDP-xylose, functioning as a specific 3-O-xylosyltransferase (Chen et al. 2023b). AmGT8, a multifunctional glycosyltransferase, catalyzes triterpene 3-/6-/2-O-glycosylation. Through enzyme engineering approaches, its regioselectivity was modulated, with mutants A394F, A394D, and T131V showing dominant 6-O, 3-O, and 2ⅱ-O glycosylation activity and specificity, respectively. The P192E variant significantly increased the conversion rate from 11.2% to 50.1% when UDP-xylose was used as the sugar donor, likely by altering the local conformation of the substrate-binding pocket. Ultimately, astragalosides III and IV were synthesized using AmGT8 variants P192E, A394F, and T131V (Zhang et al. 2022b). Moreover, the low catalytic efficiency of the triterpene glycosyltransferase UGT74AC1 from Siraitia grosvenorii was addressed through a combined strategy of directed evolution and semi-rational design. The M7 mutant (T79Y/L48M/R28H/L109I/S15A/M76L/H47R) retained regioselectivity for the C3 hydroxyl group while expanding compatibility with diverse substrates and sugar donors, resulting in a 4.17 × 104-fold enhancement in catalytic efficiency compared to the wild-type enzyme (Li et al. 2020). These investigations offer new perspectives on glycosyltransferase engineering to improve regioselectivity and sugar donor recognition.

Semi-rational design of acetyltransferase

Baccatin III, a precursor of paclitaxel—a high-value anticancer active compound (Jiang et al. 2024b)—is synthesized through an acetyl transfer reaction involving 10-deacetylbaccatin III, catalyzed by 10-deacetylbaccatin III 10-O-transferase (DBAT) (Fang and Ewald 2004). However, the limited thermal stability of DBAT constrains the efficiency of baccatin III biosynthesis. To establish a microbial cell factory with enhanced baccatin III production, a semi-rational design approach guided by computational prediction was employed to introduce mutations at hotspot residues located within the enzyme’s binding pocket and solvent channel. Among these, residue S189 was identified as a critical determinant of thermal stability. Molecular docking analysis revealed that the S189V variant exhibited reduced binding energy with the substrate and formed a greater number of hydrogen bonds, thereby improving both binding affinity and thermal resilience. Although the soluble expression level of S189V was slightly reduced compared to the wild-type enzyme, its catalytic efficiency increased by 3.81-fold, and its half-life at 25 °C was prolonged from 10 min (wild type) to 245 min. In E. coli, metabolic engineering strategies were implemented to augment acetyl-CoA availability and integrate the S189V mutant, resulting in a baccatin III yield of 28.8 mg/L following fermentation optimization (Huang et al. 2022b).

Semi-rational design of ketolase and hydroxylase

Astaxanthin is a carotenoid with potent antioxidative activity, ultimately synthesized through a cascade of intermediates generated via the co-catalysis of β-carotene by β-carotene hydroxylase and β-carotene ketolase, and holds considerable application potential in pharmaceutical, food, and related sectors (Zhu et al. 2023; Higuera-Ciapara et al. 2006; Stachowiak and Szulc 2021). Based on structural modeling and molecular docking analyses, semi-rational design was conducted on β-carotene ketolase HpBKT and β-carotene hydroxylase HpCrtZ from Haematococcus pluvialis. Although the HpBKTW194A mutant improved the enzyme–substrate binding efficiency, its astaxanthin yield remained lower than that of HpBKTV264D, as reported in earlier studies involving expression in Y. lipolytica (Abdullah et al. 2025; Zhou et al. 2017). Additionally, HpCrtZN183A and HpCrtZY219H both resulted in marked enhancements in astaxanthin production, with HpCrtZN183A demonstrating the greatest yield increase by simultaneously reinforcing enzyme rigidity and stabilizing the enzyme–substrate complex. Upon modular co-expression of HpBKTV264D and HpCrtZN183A in multi-optimized engineered Y. lipolytica, followed by fed-batch fermentation in a 5 L bioreactor, an astaxanthin yield of 2,820 mg/L was attained, with a productivity of 434 mg/L/d—representing the highest productivity reported in Y. lipolytica to date (Abdullah et al. 2025).

Prospects

Enzyme engineering constitutes a foundational technology for promoting the industrialization of terpenoid biosynthesis, with semi-rational design—widely adopted as an enzyme engineering strategy—offering the potential to further improve its efficiency and robustness in the current landscape of interdisciplinary technological integration. As genomic sequencing technologies continue to advance and associated costs decline, key amino acid residues influencing catalytic activity can be efficiently identified by mining and comparing an expanded set of homologous enzyme sequences, thereby increasing the effectiveness of semi-rational design in the discovery of highly active mutants. Concurrently, structural analysis techniques such as X-ray crystallography and cryo-electron microscopy facilitate the acquisition of more comprehensive three-dimensional enzyme conformational data, which not only serve as reliable templates for molecular-level structure–function analysis, but also provide broader structural references for structure-based semi-rational design.

It is noteworthy that the ongoing advancement of computer and artificial intelligence technologies has expanded their application potential in enzyme engineering. However, not all mutation sites identified during the initial stages of semi-rational design contribute to the optimization of enzyme performance. By integrating computer-assisted analysis with AI-driven predictive modeling to guide mutant selection, the experimental workload required for subsequent validation can be substantially reduced, thereby enhancing the overall efficiency of semi-rational design. In addition, machine learning models enable the mining of large-scale enzymatic datasets and allow for effective prediction of the impacts of mutation site combinations on complex traits such as enzyme thermostability and substrate selectivity. This approach significantly mitigates the trial-and-error nature of traditional screening methods and has facilitated the evolution of conventional enzyme engineering paradigms. The synergistic implementation of these technologies renders mutants obtained via semi-rational design more efficient and reliable, promotes innovation within the design framework, ensures progressive enhancement of terpene synthases and their associated modifying enzymes with respect to catalytic activity, thermal stability, and substrate specificity, and accelerates the industrialization of terpene compound biosynthesis.

Comparative Evaluation of Satranidazole Gel and Aloe Vera Gel as Adjuncts to Scaling and Root Planing in the Treatment of Chronic Periodontitis: A Randomized, Single-Blind, Parallel-Group Study
Authors: Aadarsh Vikram Dhan, Hiroj Bagde, Aniket Sharan, Shashank Rai, Preeti Paryani Sachdev
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:To evaluate the clinical effectiveness of locally administered Satranidazole gel and Aloe Vera gel as adjunctive treatments to scaling and root planing (SRP) in the management of mild-to-moderate chronic periodontitis.Materials and Methods:Forty-five participants were diagnosed with mild-to-moderate chronic periodontitis. Subjects were randomly assigned to one of three groups: SRP alone (control), SRP with Satranidazole gel, and SRP with Aloe Vera gel. Clinical parameters—plaque index, gingival index, sulcus bleeding index, and probing pocket depth—were assessed at baseline and one-month posttreatment. Statistical analysis used paired t-tests, one-way ANOVA.Results:All groups showed significant improvements in PI, GI, SBI, and PPD after one month (P < 0.05). The combination of SRP with Satranidazole and Aloe Vera resulted in greater reductions in PI and PPD compared to SRP alone (P < 0.05). The SRP with Satranidazole group also had a superior reduction in SBI and PPD compared to the control group (P < 0.05). No significant differences were found between the Satranidazole and Aloe Vera groups.Conclusion:The use of Satranidazole and Aloe Vera gels as adjuncts to SRP significantly enhanced clinical parameters in comparison with SRP alone in the short term. While Satranidazole offered slightly greater improvements in bleeding and pocket depth, Aloe Vera presented a natural and cost-effective option.

INTRODUCTION

Chronic periodontitis is a common inflammatory disease affecting surrounding structures of teeth, caused mainly due to dental plaque buildup. It leads to tissue destruction, deeper periodontal pockets, gum recession, and tooth mobility. Increasingly, it is linked to systemic health issues, like cardiovascular disease and diabetes, emphasizing the need for good oral hygiene. Traditional treatment includes scaling and root planing (SRP), which helps disrupt bacterial biofilms but may be less effective in deep pockets.[12]

Local drug delivery systems (LDD) improve outcomes in chronic periodontitis by delivering antimicrobial or anti-inflammatory agents directly into periodontal pockets. This method achieves high localized concentrations, targets pathogens, modulates inflammation, and minimizes systemic side effects, significantly enhancing tissue regeneration potential.[3]

MATERIALS AND METHODS

A randomized, single-blind, parallel-group clinical trial was conducted to assess adjunctive local therapies for chronic periodontitis. Adults with mild-to-moderate chronic periodontitis (PPD 4–6 mm) were included. Key exclusion criteria comprised recent systemic antibiotic or periodontal therapy (within six months) and pregnancy or lactation.

Participants were randomly assigned into groups:

Group A (Control): SRP alone.

Group A (Control): SRP alone.

Group B: SRP combined with Satranidazole (SZ) gel delivered intrapocket.

Group B: SRP combined with Satranidazole (SZ) gel delivered intrapocket.

Group C: SRP combined with Aloe Vera (AV) gel delivered intrapocket.

Group C: SRP combined with Aloe Vera (AV) gel delivered intrapocket.

SZ gel was formulated with the active ingredient, McIlvaine buffer, and sodium carboxymethyl cellulose, mixed until uniform. AV gel was obtained from external source. After SRP, 0.5 mL of the assigned gel was applied into each periodontal pocket using a blunt cannula, and sites were covered with periodontal dressing. Examinations occurred at baseline and four weeks posttreatment using a mouth mirror and UNC-15 probe. The parameters recorded were: PI, GI (score 0–3), SBI (score 0–5), and PPD (in mm). Four sites (mesial, distal, buccal, and lingual) were assessed, and averages were calculated for analysis.

Statistical analysis was carried out using SPSS IBM version 25.

RESULTS

The study included 45 patients (26 males, 19 females) equally divided into three groups: Group A (SRP alone), Group B (SRP + SZ gel), and Group C (SRP + AV gel), with comparable mean ages across groups.

All groups showed significant improvements in PI, GI, SBI, and PPD at one month (P < 0.0001). PI decreased notably: Group A (2.12 → 1.41), Group B (2.09 → 0.90), and Group C (2.09 → 1.01). Intergroup analysis revealed significantly greater PI reduction in Groups B and C compared to Group A (P = 0.0003 and P = 0.0037, respectively), with no difference between B and C (P = 0.6686). GI and SBI significantly decreased in all groups (baseline ~ 2.1-2.3 to ~ 1.07-1.26), with no significant intergroup differences. PPD was significantly reduced in Group A (4.93 → 3.87 mm), Group B (5.00 → 2.67 mm), and Group C (4.93 → 2.93 mm). Groups B and C showed greater PPD reduction than Group A (P = 0.0002 and P = 0.0044, respectively), with no significant difference between B and C (P = 0.6021) [Table 1].

DISCUSSION

This study reinforces that SRP is a key nonsurgical treatment for chronic periodontitis, significantly improving parameters, like PI, GI, SBI, and PPD. However, SRP alone may not fully eliminate subgingival pathogens in complex anatomical sites, which could affect long-term results.

The addition of SZ gel enhanced clinical outcomes, particularly in terms of plaque reduction and probing depth, due to its strong antimicrobial activity and ability to remain in the periodontal pocket. Prior studies by Pradeep et al.[4] and others also support the effectiveness of SZ when combined with SRP through its controlled release and immunomodulatory effects.[56]

AV gel similarly provided benefits as an adjunct to SRP, particularly in reducing plaque and probing depths due to its anti-inflammatory and wound-healing properties, aligning with research from Pradeep et al.[7] and recent work by Marella et al.[8]

Notably, while plaque and probing depth showed significant improvements, the gingival index did not differ markedly between groups, suggesting that mechanical debridement primarily drives reductions in gingival inflammation. This agrees with prior studies indicating limited extra benefit from adjunctive gels for this parameter.[4910]

The study’s single-blind design and one-month follow-up present limitations, including potential patient perception biases and restricted insights into long-term efficacy. The absence of microbiological analysis limits evaluation of treatment effects on subgingival biofilms. Further study should include larger sample sizes, longer follow-up periods, and microbial assessments. Both SZ and AV gels show promise as effective adjuncts to SRP, improving clinical outcomes beyond mechanical therapy alone.

SUMMARY AND CONCLUSION

This study concluded that the application of SZ gel and AV gel in conjunction with SRP significantly enhances clinical outcomes for chronic periodontitis when compared to SRP alone. SZ gel exhibited superior antimicrobial properties, whereas AV gel was more effective in anti-inflammatory and wound-healing aspects.

Purely coclosed G 2 \mathrm{G}_{2} -structures on nilmanifolds - II
Giovanni Bazzoni, Giorgia Petracci

Abstract
. This paper completes the classification of seven-dimensional nilpotent Lie groups endowed with a left-invariant purely coclosed G 2 \text{G}_{2} -structure, initiated in [ 3 ] . In [ 3 ] , the authors provided the classification of decomposable seven-dimensional nilpotent Lie groups and of the indecomposable ones up to step 4 4 of nilpotency. Here, we address the case of indecomposable 5 5 - and 6 6 -step nilpotent Lie groups.

1.
Introduction

A
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure on a
7
-dimensional manifold is a reduction of its frame bundle from
\mathrm{GL}(7,\mathbb{R})
to the exceptional Lie group
\mathrm{G}_{2}\subset\mathrm{SO}(7)
. As shown by Gray
[
17
]
, the existence of such a reduction is equivalent to the manifold being orientable and spin. Moreover, a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure is also tantamount to the existence of a positive
3
-form
\varphi
(see
[
6
]
for details). This form defines a Riemannian metric
g_{\varphi}
and an orientation
\mathrm{vol}_{\varphi}
on
M
, hence a Hodge operator
*_{\varphi}
. When
\varphi
is parallel
with respect to the Levi-Civita connection of
g_{\varphi}
, the identity component of its holonomy group is contained in
\mathrm{G}_{2}
; in fact,
\mathrm{G}_{2}
is one of the two exceptional Lie groups appearing in the celebrated Berger’s classification
[
5
]
. By a result of Fernández and Gray,
\varphi
is parallel if and only if
\varphi
is closed and coclosed, see
[
11
]
. In this case,
g_{\varphi}
is Ricci-flat. A
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure is called
closed
if
d\varphi=0
, and
coclosed
if
d*_{\varphi}\varphi=0
.

As it is the case for general
\mathrm{G}
-structures, the non-integrability of a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure is governed by its intrinsic torsion
\tau
, see
[
23
]
. In this particular case,

\tau
has four components
\tau_{i}\in\Omega^{i}(M)
,
i=0,1,2,3
, determined by the equations

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}d\varphi&=&\tau_{0}*_{\varphi}\varphi+3~\tau_{1}\wedge\varphi+*_{\varphi}\tau_{3}\\
d*_{\varphi}\varphi&=&4\tau_{1}\wedge*_{\varphi}\varphi+\tau_{2}\wedge\varphi\end{array}\right.\,;

(1)

see
[
6
, Proposition 1]
. Closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures are thus those for which
\tau_{0}=\tau_{1}=\tau_{3}
, while coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures are characterized by
\tau_{1}=\tau_{2}=0
. A
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure is of
pure type
if all the torsion components vanish, but one. Thus closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures are of pure type, while coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures are not.
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures with
\tau_{i}=0
,
i=0,2,3
, are called
locally conformally parallel
(see
[
19
]
).
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures with
\tau_{i}=0
,
i=1,2,3
are known as
nearly parallel
, see
[
15
]
. In this case
d\varphi=\tau_{0}*_{\varphi}\varphi
,
\tau_{0}
is a constant, and
g_{\varphi}
is Einstein with positive scalar curvature. Nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures are in particular coclosed.

This paper deals with the last pure class of
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures, namely
purely coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures
; these are
given by the conditions
\tau_{0}=\tau_{1}=\tau_{2}=0
, that is,
d\varphi=*_{\varphi}\tau_{3}
and
d*_{\varphi}\varphi=0
. Equivalently (see
[
9
]
), they can be characterized by

d*_{\varphi}\varphi=0\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\varphi\wedge d\varphi=0\,.

In particular, a purely coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure is coclosed. The second equation is an equality of
7
-forms, so it imposes a single extra condition. By a recent result of Crowley and Nordström, coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures exist on any closed, oriented spin manifold, since they satisfy an
h
-principle (see
[
8
, Theorem 1.8]
). It is not clear whether there is an
h
-principle for purely coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures.

We study left-invariant
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on nilmanifolds, compact quotients of connected, simply connected, nilpotent Lie groups by a lattice. Hence, we can restrict the attention to
7
-dimensional real nilpotent Lie algebras, which have been classified by Gong in
[
16
]
. As for pure classes, nilpotent Lie groups endowed with a left-invariant closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure have been classified by Conti and Fernández in
[
7
]
. It can be shown that nilmanifolds can not carry locally conformally parallel or left-invariant nearly parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures (see the discussion in
[
3
]
). As for left-invariant (purely) coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures, we report some known facts from the literature:

•

Bagaglini, Fernández and Fino determined in
[
2
]
which nilpotent Lie groups admit left-invariant coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures in two cases: when the Lie algebra is decomposable, and when it is
2
-step nilpotent.

•

In
[
14
]
, Freibert obtained all nilpotent almost-abelian Lie algebras with a coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure.

•

del Barco, Moroianu and Raffero classified
2
-step nilpotent Lie groups admitting left-invariant purely coclosed invariant
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures. Their approach uses the theory of 2-step nilpotent Lie algebras and does not rely on the classification of
7
-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebras (see
[
9
]
).

•

In
[
3
]
, the first author, together with Garvín and Muñoz, determined which decomposable and indecomposable Lie algebras of nilpotency step
\leq 4
admit purely coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures.

We refer the reader to Section
2
for the relevant details.

In this paper we follow the same approach as in
[
3
]
and determine which 7-dimensional indecomposable nilpotent Lie groups with nilpotency step
\geq 5
admit a left-invariant purely coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure. In the positive cases, we provide an explicit example of a left-invariant purely coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure. In the negative cases, we consider obstructions to the existence of coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures. The results are summarized in Theorems
5.1
and
5.2
. In particular, putting together our results with those of
[
3
]
and
[
9
]
, we have (see Theorem
6.1
):

Theorem
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a 7-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebra.

•

If
\mathfrak{g}
is decomposable, then it admits a coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure if and only if it admits a purely coclosed one, unless
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}_{3}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{4}
.

•

If
\mathfrak{g}
is indecomposable, then it admits a coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure if and only if it admits a purely coclosed one.

Here,
\mathfrak{h}_{3}
denotes the Heisenberg Lie algebra.

For the ease of the verification, we use SageMath worksheets
[
10
]
. For each of the Lie algebras there is a single
worksheet. When the Lie algebra admits a left-invariant purely coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure, we provide the
relevant forms, and the list of commands that verify that it is purely coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure. In the negative case,
we provide a commented worksheet with all the steps that show the obstructions. The SageMath worksheets can be found at
[
4
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

This work uses the same techniques introduced in
[
3
]
in order to finish the classification of 7-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebras with purely coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures. Nevertheless, we take the opportunity to include a short section with the necessary preliminaries, in order to make this paper as self-contained as possible.

2.1.
\mathrm{G}_{2}
- and
\mathrm{SU}(3)
-structures

In this paper we describe
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures using a positive 3-form. More precisely, a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-strucure on a 7-dimensional manifold
M
is a 3-form
\varphi\in\Omega^{3}(M)
for which at each point there exists a local coframe
\{e^{i}\}
such that

\varphi=e^{127}+e^{347}+e^{567}+e^{135}-e^{146}-e^{236}-e^{245}\,.

(2)

Such 3-forms are called
positive
and the space of positive 3-forms is denoted
\Omega^{3}_{+}(M)
. Then
\varphi
defines a Riemannian metric
g_{\varphi}
and a volume form
\mathrm{vol}_{\varphi}
by the formula

g_{\varphi}(X,Y)\,\mathrm{vol}_{\varphi}=\frac{1}{6}\bigl(\iota_{X}\varphi\bigr)\wedge\bigl(\iota_{Y}\varphi\bigr)\wedge\varphi\,,

for vector field
X,Y
on
M
. General references on
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-geometry are
[
6
,
20
]
. We are interested in
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures of
purely coclosed
type: with respect to (
1
), they are defined by the vanishing of all torsion forms but
\tau_{3}
. Equivalently, they can be defined as coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures, i.e.
d\ast_{\varphi}\varphi=0
, such that
\varphi\wedge d\varphi=0
.

We are interested in particular instances of
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on nilmanifolds, which are compact quotients of connected, simply connected, nilpotent Lie groups. Let
G
be a
7
-dimensional connected and simply connected Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. A
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure on
G
is
left-invariant
if the defining
3
-form is left-invariant. Thus, a left-invariant
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure on
G
is defined by a positive
3
-form
\varphi\in\Lambda^{3}{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
which can be written, in some orthonormal coframe
\{e^{1},\dotsc,e^{7}\}
of
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
, as (
2
). A
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure on
\mathfrak{g}
is
coclosed
if
\varphi
is coclosed, that is, if

d*_{\varphi}\varphi=0\,,

where
d
denotes the Chevalley-Eilenberg differential on
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
. A coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure on
\mathfrak{g}
is said to be
purely coclosed
if, in addition,

\varphi\wedge d\varphi=0\,.

\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on 7-dimensional manifolds are strictly related to
\mathrm{SU}(3)
-structures on
6
-manifolds. Indeed, both structures can be described uniformly in terms of a certain spinor, see
[
1
]
. On a 6-dimensional manifold
N
consider a pair
(\psi_{-},\omega)\in\Omega^{3}(N)\times\Omega^{2}(N)
, where
\psi_{-}
is stable in the sense of Hitchin and
\omega
is almost symplectic. There are two orbits of stable forms in
\Omega^{3}(N)
: the
positive
and the
negative
stable forms (see
[
18
, Proposition 2]
). If
\psi_{-}
is negative, one can use it to define an almost complex structure
J
on
N
. We call
(\psi_{-},\omega)
an
\mathrm{SU}(3)
-structure on
N
if, in addition,
\psi_{-}\wedge\omega=0
and
g(\cdot,\cdot)=\omega(\cdot,J\cdot)
defines a Riemannian metric on
N
. In this case, defining
\psi_{+}=-J^{*}\psi_{-}
, one obtains a complex volume form
\Psi=\psi_{+}+i\psi_{-}
on
N
.

Starting with an
\mathrm{SU}(3)
-structure
(\psi_{-},\omega)
on a 6-dimensional manifold
N
, we can construct a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
structure on
M=N\times\mathbb{R}
by setting
\varphi=\omega\wedge dt+\psi_{+}
, there
t
is a coordinate on
\mathbb{R}
.

As it was the case for
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures, one can make sense of left-invariant
\mathrm{SU}(3)
-structures on Lie groups.

2.2.
Nilmanifolds

Given a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, consider the series of ideals defined by

\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\{0\}\,,\qquad\mathfrak{g}_{k}=\{X\in\mathfrak{g}\mid[X,\mathfrak{g}]\subset\mathfrak{g}_{k-1}\}\quad\mathrm{for}\ k\geq 1\,.

\mathfrak{g}
is
nilpotent
if there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathfrak{g}_{n}=\mathfrak{g}
. Notice that if
\mathfrak{g}
is nilpotent then
\mathfrak{g}_{1}=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
, the center of
\mathfrak{g}
. Moreover,
\mathfrak{g}
is
n-step nilpotent
if
\mathfrak{g}_{n}=\mathfrak{g}
but
\mathfrak{g}_{n-1}\neq\mathfrak{g}
;
n
is then the
nilpotency step
of
\mathfrak{g}
. A connected Lie group
G
is
nilpotent
or
n-step nilpotent
if its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is.

A nilmanifold
M=\Gamma\backslash G
is the compact quotient of a connected, simply connected, nilpotent Lie group
G
by a lattice
\Gamma\subset\,G
. A lattice
\Gamma\subset\,G
exists if and only if the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
has a rational structure, by a result of Mal’cev
[
22
]
. Nilmanifolds are parallelizable, hence, by the result of Crowley and Nordström mentioned in the introduction, they admit coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures. In this paper, however, we are interested in
left-invariant
(purely) coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on nilmanifolds. By definition, these are left-invariant
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on the corresponding Lie group.

3.
Constructing purely coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures

In this section we review the construction of purely coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on 7-dimensional (nilpotent) Lie algebras presented in
[
3
]
, to which we refer for the details and the proofs.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a
7
-dimensional Lie algebra with non-trivial center
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
(recall that the center of a nilpotent Lie algebra is non-trivial). Let
V\subset\mathfrak{g}
be a codimension 1 subspace, cooriented by
X\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
; thus
X
is a central vector with non-zero projection to
\mathfrak{g}/V
. Let
\omega\in\Lambda^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and
\psi_{-}\in\Lambda^{3}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be such that

•

\imath_{X}\omega=0
;

•

\imath_{X}\psi_{-}=0
;

•

they define an
\mathrm{SU}(3)
-structure on
V
.

In particular, denoting by
\bar{\omega}
and
\bar{\psi}_{-}
the pull-back to
V
of the above tensors, we have an almost symplectic 2-form
\bar{\omega}
and a stable, negative 3-form
\bar{\psi}_{-}
with
\bar{\omega}\wedge\bar{\psi}_{-}=0
. This determines
\bar{\psi}_{+}\in\Lambda^{3}(V^{*})
. Extend
\bar{\psi}_{+}
to an element
\psi_{+}\in\Lambda^{3}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
by declaring
\imath_{X}\psi_{+}=0
. Finally, let
\eta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be such that

\eta(X)\neq 0
.

It follows that
\varphi=\omega\wedge\eta+\psi_{+}
is a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-form on
\mathfrak{g}
; moreover, if
h
denotes the induced
\mathrm{SU}(3)
-metric on
V
, then the
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-metric on
\mathfrak{g}
is
g=g_{\varphi}=h+\eta\otimes\eta
. Clearly,
*_{\varphi}\varphi=\frac{\omega^{2}}{2}+\psi_{-}\wedge\eta
. The following result, taken from
[
3
, Theorem 4.1]
, gives sufficient conditions on
(\omega,\psi_{-},\eta)
in order for the
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure
\varphi=\omega\wedge\eta+\psi_{+}
to be (purely) coclosed.

Theorem 3.1
.

In the above setting, the
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure is coclosed if

(1)

d\psi_{-}=0
;

(2)

\omega\wedge d\omega=\psi_{-}\wedge d\eta
.

Furthermore, the coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure is pure if

(3)

\omega^{2}\wedge d\eta=-2\psi_{+}\wedge d\omega
.

4.
Obstructions for coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures on nilpotent Lie algebras

In this section we present the obstructions we use to prove the non-existence of a coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure on a certain Lie algebra. The first three obstructions were already introduced in
[
3
]
, to which we refere for the further details and for the proofs. The fourth obstruction is new.

We take the opportunity to correct a minor inaccuracy in the first obstruction presented in
[
3
]
. Indeed, in
[
3
, Corollary 5.3]
one picks two vectors
X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}
with
Y\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
and considers the subspace
U\subset\Lambda^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
obtained by contracting a generic closed 4-form with
X
and
Y
. The claim there is that if
\Lambda^{2}U=0
, then
\mathfrak{g}
does not admit any coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure. In the proof it is claimed that if
\kappa\in\Lambda^{4}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is exact and
Y\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
then
\iota_{Y}\kappa=0
. This is not true, in general. The first obstruction has to be stated in a slightly different way, which is actually a mere reformulation of
[
3
, Lemma 5.2]
.

First obstruction
Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a seven-dimensional Lie algebra. Suppose that there exist linearly independent vector
X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}
such that, for every
\kappa\in\Lambda^{4}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
closed,
\iota_{X}\iota_{Y}\kappa\in U
for a subspace
U\subset\Lambda^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
such that
\Lambda^{2}U=0
. Then,
\mathfrak{g}
does not admit any coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure.

We checked all the Lie algebras handled in
[
3
]
, for which the existence of a coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure was excluded using the first obstruction. In all cases, we were able to find two vectors which satisfy the hypotheses of first obstruction above. Thus, the results of
[
3
]
are correct.

The second obstruction is taken from
[
3
, Corollary 5.5]
.

Second obstruction
Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a
7
-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebra and let
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{7}\}
be a nilpotent basis, with dual basis
\{e^{1},\ldots,e^{7}\}
. Take a list of generators of the space of closed
4
-forms
z_{\alpha}\in\Lambda^{4}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
. Suppose that
z_{\alpha}\in\langle e^{1},e^{2}\rangle\wedge\Lambda^{3}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
for all
\alpha
. Then
\mathfrak{g}
does not admit coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures.

The third obstruction appears in
[
3
, Proposition 5.6]
. Given a nilpotent basis
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{7}\}
of
\mathfrak{g}
, fix
X=e_{7}
. We compute a basis
\{z_{\alpha}\}
of the closed
3
-forms in
\mathfrak{h}^{*}=\langle e^{1},\ldots,e^{6}\rangle
. Therefore for a closed
3
-form, we can write
\tau=\sum a_{\alpha}z_{\alpha}
.

Third obstruction
Suppose we have elements
w_{1},\ldots,w_{\ell}\in\Lambda^{5}(\mathfrak{h}^{*})
, and let
W
be a subspace such that
\Lambda^{5}(\mathfrak{h}^{*})=W\oplus\langle w_{1},\ldots,w_{\ell}\rangle
. Suppose furthermore that for every closed
2
-form
\beta
and closed
3
-form
\tau
on
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, we have

\beta\wedge d\beta,\tau\wedge de^{j}\in W,\ j=1,\ldots,6\,.

Then define the linear subspace

H=\left\{(a_{\alpha})\,|\,\sum a_{\alpha}z_{\alpha}\wedge de^{7}\in W\right\}.

If
\lambda(\sum a_{\alpha}z_{\alpha})\geq 0
for all
(a_{\alpha})\in H
, then there is no coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure on
\mathfrak{g}
.

In the analysis carried out in this paper we bumped into two Lie algebras,
23457
B and
23457
F in Gong’s notation, which passed the three obstructions above, but for which the method used in
[
3
]
did not yield any result.

We thus develop a fourth obstruction, which allows us to prove that the above Lie algebras do not carry any coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure. The fourth obstruction is based on the technique described in
[
13
]
for computing the inner product determined by a positive
4
-form.

Let
\varphi
be a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-form, with associated metric
g_{\varphi}
, orientation
\mathrm{vol}_{\varphi}
and Hodge-star
\ast_{\varphi}
. At each point of the manifold,
\varphi
can be written as in (
2
). Recall that
\varphi
is stable, in the sense of Hitchin (see
[
18
]
). It turns out that the
4
-form
*_{\varphi}\varphi
is also stable and positive; at each point, its
\text{GL}(7,\mathbb{R})
-orbit
\Lambda_{+}^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{7})^{*}
is open in
\Lambda^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{7})^{*}
. The orbit
\Lambda_{+}^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{7})^{*}
is isomorphic to

\text{GL}(7,\mathbb{R})/\bigl\{\mathrm{G}_{2}\cup\bigl(\mathrm{G}_{2}\circ(-\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbb{R}^{7}})\bigr)\bigr\},

so the pointwise map

\Lambda_{+}^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{7})^{*}\longrightarrow\Lambda_{+}^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{7})^{*},\qquad\varphi\longmapsto*_{\varphi}\varphi,

is a double covering. Hence, a positive
4
-form defines an inner product but not an orientation. Consequently, in order to compute the inner product determined by a positive
4
-form, it is necessary to fix a background volume form. Once a volume form
\Omega
on
\mathbb{R}^{7}
is fixed, there is an isomorphism

\Lambda^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{7})^{*}\cong\Lambda^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{7})\otimes\Lambda^{7}(\mathbb{R}^{7})^{*}

that identifies
\rho\in\Lambda^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{7})^{*}
with the element

\hat{\rho}\otimes\Omega\in\Lambda^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{7})\otimes\Lambda^{7}(\mathbb{R}^{7})^{*}
such that
\iota_{\hat{\rho}}\Omega=\rho
.
One then obtains a symmetric bilinear map

\displaystyle\mathrm{B}_{\rho}:(\mathbb{R}^{7})^{*}\times(\mathbb{R}^{7})^{*}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\Lambda^{7}(\mathbb{R}^{7})\otimes\bigl(\Lambda^{7}(\mathbb{R}^{7})^{*}\bigr)^{\otimes 3}\cong\bigl(\Lambda^{7}(\mathbb{R}^{7})^{*}\bigr)^{\otimes 2}

(3)

\displaystyle(v,w)

\displaystyle\longmapsto\left(\frac{1}{6}\,\iota_{v}\hat{\rho}\wedge\iota_{w}\hat{\rho}\wedge\hat{\rho}\right)\otimes\Omega^{\otimes 3}.

(4)

The
4
-form
\rho
is stable if and only if

\bigl|\det(\mathrm{B}_{\rho})\bigr|^{\frac{1}{12}}\in\Lambda^{7}(\mathbb{R}^{7})^{*}

is non-zero.

Remark 4.1
.

The
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-metric induced by a definite
4
-form
\rho
is then defined as
g_{\rho}=|\det(\mathrm{B}_{\rho})|^{-\frac{1}{6}}\mathrm{B}_{\rho}
. As noticed in
[
13
]
, if
\varphi
is a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-form, the relation between
g_{\varphi}
and the metric
g_{\ast_{\varphi}\varphi}
induced by the
4
-form
\ast_{\varphi}\varphi
is given by
g_{\ast_{\varphi}\varphi}=g_{\varphi}^{-1}
.

The Lie algebras we deal with are described in terms of a (nilpotent) basis
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{7}\}
, hence
\Omega=e^{1234567}
is a natural choice for the background volume form. We can state the fourth obstruction:

Proposition 4.2

(Fourth obstruction)
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a seven-dimensional Lie algebra, and let
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{7}\}
be a basis of
\mathfrak{g}
. If, for every closed
4
-form
\rho\in\Lambda^{4}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, the matrix
B_{\rho}
defined in (
3
) fails to be positive definite, then
\mathfrak{g}
admits no coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure.

In order to check that
\mathrm{B}_{\rho}
is (not) positive definite, one can apply, for instance, the Sylvester theorem.

5.
Seven-dimensional nilpotent lie algebras with purely coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures

6.
Futher comments

Using results from
[
9
]
, it was proved in
[
3
, Theorem 6.2]
that every seven-dimensional decomposable nilpotent Lie algebra admitting a coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure also admits a purely coclosed one, except for
\mathfrak{h}_{3}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{4}
, the direct sum of the 3-dimensional Heisenberg algebra and
\mathbb{R}^{4}
(this is denoted
\mathfrak{n}_{2}
in
[
3
]
). In the indecomposable case, it was observed in
[
3
, Corollary 6.8]
that every seven-dimensional indecomposable nilpotent Lie algebra of nilpotency step
\leq 4
admitting a coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure also admits a purely coclosed one.

As a consequence of the results presented in the previous section, we can complete the picture in the indecomposable case, obtaining the following theorem.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a 7-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebra.

•

If
\mathfrak{g}
is decomposable, then it admits a coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure if and only if it admits a purely coclosed one, unless
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}_{3}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{4}
.

•

If
\mathfrak{g}
is indecomposable, then it admits a coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure if and only if it admits a purely coclosed one.

Remark 6.2
.

It seems difficult to deduce the existence of a purely coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure directly from the existence of a coclosed one, under the indecomposability assumption. Also, it seems hard to deduce directly the exceptionality of
\mathfrak{h}_{3}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{4}
in this context.

Remark 6.3
.

It is worth pointing out that all purely coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure have been constructed in a uniform way, using the
Ansatz
of Theorem
3.1
.

The class of coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure contains both the pure classes of nearly parallel and purely coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures. Clearly, the
\mathrm{G}_{2}
4-form is exact in the nearly parallel case. Using
SageMath
we checked that none of the purely coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
4-forms we constructed is exact. It is an open question whether the
\mathrm{G}_{2}
4-form of a purely coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure can be exact. A similar question exists in the context of closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
structures: it is not known whether the
\mathrm{G}_{2}
3-form of a closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure can be exact. Negative results on compact quotients of Lie groups have been obtained in
[
12
]
.

In
[
21
]
, the authors gave a positive answer as to whether there exist infinitely many non-isomorphic nilpotent Lie algebras in dimension 8 admitting complex structures. Recall that a nilpotent Lie algebra admits a Kähler structure if and only if it is abelian. Now in dimension 7, a nilpotent Lie algebra can not admit torsion-free, nearly parallel nor locally conformally parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures. Thus, a way to transfer the above question to the 7-dimensional world could be the following:

Question 6.4
.

Do there exist infinitely many non-isomorphic nilpotent Lie algebras in dimension 7 admitting closed or (purely) coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures?

Using results of
[
3
,
7
]
and from the above analysis, we are able to give an answer to this question:

•

in the closed setting, there are only finitely many non-isomorphic nilpotent Lie algebras which admit closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures; in particular, there exist only finitely many
real homotopy types
of 7-dimensional nilmanifolds admitting a closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure;

•

in the purely coclosed setting, for every nilpotency step
k\geq 3
there are infinitely many non-isomorphic nilpotent Lie algebras which admit purely closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures. Explicit examples are given by the following families:

–

147\textrm{E}(\lambda)
, with nilpotency step 3;

–

1357\textrm{M}(\lambda)
, with nilpotency step 4;

–

12457\textrm{N}(\lambda)
, with nilpotency step 5;

–

123457\textrm{I}(\lambda)
, with nilpotency step 6.

In particular, there exist infinitely many
real homotopy types
of 7-dimensional nilmanifolds admitting a purely coclosed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure.

It not known to the authors whether there exist infinitely many
rational
homotopy types of nilmanifolds admitting a closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure.

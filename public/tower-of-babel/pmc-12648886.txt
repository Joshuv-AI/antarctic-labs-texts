Correlation between preoperative PCI imaging, intraoperative PCI measurement, and overall survival in peritoneal carcinomatosis secondary to ovarian, tubal, and primary peritoneal carcinoma
Authors: Boonlert Viriyapak, Khemanat Khemworapong, Arithat Patkhunpisit, Thanongchai Siriapisith
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPeritoneal Cancer Index (PCI) is widely used to evaluate peritoneal carcinomatosis before surgery. This study aims to assess the correlation between preoperative and intraoperative PCI. The secondary objectives were determining whether PCI could predict surgical oncologic outcomes and overall survival.MethodWomen with advanced-stage epithelial ovarian cancer, primary peritoneal cancer, or fallopian tube cancer who underwent primary cytoreductive surgery or interval debulking were included. The preoperative computerized tomography (CT) scan findings and intraoperative measurement of the peritoneal carcinomatosis were evaluated using PCI, and their correlation was determined using the Spearman coefficient. The overall survival was calculated using the Kaplan-Meier method.Results52 patients were eligible and analyzed. Mean preoperative and intraoperative PCI were 5.04 and 7.27, orderly. Twenty-nine patients achieved optimal surgery (55.8%). A moderate correlation exists between the PCI obtained from the CT image and surgical findings (r = 0.510, P < 0.001). The significant cutoff values of preoperative PCI and intraoperative PCI to predict optimal surgical outcomes could be 7 and 8, respectively. In multivariate analysis, preoperative Cancer Antigen 125 (CA125) < 416 U/mL and intraoperative PCI < 8 were the only independent factors for optimal surgery. The overall survival was significantly improved in patients with an intraoperative PCI score of less than 8(14 to 36 months, p = 0.015).ConclusionThe preoperative PCI from practical imaging within 30 day before surgery would be beneficial as a predictive method to assess the possibility of optimal cytoreduction and the optimal time for surgery.

Background

Ovarian cancer is the eighth most common female cancer and the second most common gynecologic malignancy in Thailand, with an incidence of 6.2 per 100,000 population. However, it caused the highest mortality among gynecologic malignancies [1]. As a disease-causing generalized peritoneal carcinomatosis manifestation is a major challenge for complete resection.

The most important prognostic factor for survival in epithelial ovarian cancer is residual tumor after cytoreductive surgery [2, 3]. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy followed by interval debulking surgery has a role in enhancing complete tumor resection with a lower morbidity rate than primary cytoreductive surgery. The overall survival was no different in two groups [4–7].

Many studies tried to assess preoperative data to predict the severity of the disease, which would relate to its resectability and surgical outcomes [8, 9]. Peritoneal Cancer Index (PCI), described by Sugarbaker PH, is a peritoneal cancer measurement system, primarily intraoperative [10]. This scoring method has been widely used to assess peritoneal carcinomatosis. It became the standard assessment for intraperitoneal diseases in gastric and colonic carcinoma [11, 12]. In ovarian cancer, intraoperative PCI was also helpful in predicting optimal surgery and disease prognosis [13, 14]. The correlation between preoperative PCI and intraoperative PCI must be considered for precise preoperative evaluation, which would help to select suitable candidates for optimal surgery.

The main objective of this study is to evaluate the correlation between preoperative and intraoperative PCI. The secondary objective is to determine whether the PCI could predict the optimal surgical outcomes and overall survival.

Methods

This study was retrospective study. Siriraj Institute Review Board ethically approved. The informed consent is waived by the respective ethics committee. The patients with peritoneal carcinomatosis secondary to epithelial ovarian cancer, primary peritoneal cancer, or fallopian tube cancer were enrolled in this study. The patient was diagnosed preoperatively by clinical and radiological findings and finally diagnosed by pathological specimens. All patients who underwent primary cytoreductive surgery or interval debulking surgery following neoadjuvant chemotherapy were included. Patients were excluded if the post-operative diagnosis was benign ovarian diseases, metastases ovarian tumors, or nonepithelial ovarian cancer.

Preoperative PCI was the PCI data obtained from recently preoperative advanced imaging, usually Computed Tomography (CT) abdomen with contrast. An intraoperative PCI was the PCI obtained from intraoperative measurement using the PCI scheme proposed by Sugarbaker [10]. PCI was calculated by size, and localization of peritoneal carcinomatosis manifestations as shown in Fig. 1. The whole abdominal cavity was divided into 13 regions. Each region was evaluated with a Lesion size Score (LS), ranging from 0 to 3: LS 0 indicated no tumor in this region; LS 1 indicated tumor mass up to 0.5 cm; LS 2 indicated tumor mass up to 5 cm; and LS 3 indicated tumor mass greater than 5 cm or confluent tumor masses, which have caused the adherence of neighboring structures. A preoperative CT scan was reviewed and calculated preoperative PCI by an independent radiologist blinded to intraoperative details. Intraoperative PCI was assessed by gynecologic oncologists during the operation. All surgical efforts and procedures had been performed by gynecologic oncologists with or without incorporated general surgeons.Fig. 1PCI scoring system. PCI, Peritoneal Cancer Index; LS, Lesion size score. Modified diagram from PCI scoring system described by Sugarbaker et al. [10]

PCI scoring system. PCI, Peritoneal Cancer Index; LS, Lesion size score. Modified diagram from PCI scoring system described by Sugarbaker et al. [10]

Surgical outcomes were classified as optimal surgery, which referred to no residual tumor or residual tumor less than 1 cm, and suboptimal surgery, which referred to a residual tumor of more than 1 cm.

The study’s target sample size was 52 based on 80% power and type I error at 0.05, which would detect a correlation coefficient not less than 0.30, reflecting a significant correlation seen in a previous study [15]. The correlation between preoperative and intraoperative PCI was analyzed by Spearman rank correlation. The compare mean and t-test were applied to evaluate preoperative and intraoperative PCIs in the optimal/suboptimal group. A receiver operating characteristic (ROC) with Youden’s index analysis was used to calculate cutoff values for CA125, preoperative PCI, and intraoperative PCI, which could optimally (high specificity and sensitivity) predict the surgical outcome. Data were analyzed by descriptive statistics. Frequency and percentages were used to describe categorical variables, and data of median and interquartile range or mean and standard deviation were used for continuous variables. Associations between these factors and the probability of favorable optimal surgical outcomes were evaluated using Pearson’s chi-square test. Multiple logistic regression was applied to determine multivariate analysis. SPSS version 26 was used for all analyses.

Results

52 eligible patients were enrolled and analyzed. The mean age was 55.7 years, and the most primary tumor was epithelial ovarian cancer for 40 cases (76.9%). The majority of histology was high-grade serous carcinoma (57.8%). Half of the cases received neoadjuvant followed by interval debulking surgery. 29 of 52 patients (55.8%) had optimal surgical outcomes, while 16 of 29 patients (30.8%) had a complete resection. All patients were preoperatively imaged by CT abdomen within 45 days before the surgery; thus, 27 patients (51.9%) recently received their surgical treatment within 30 days after imaging, and the mean imaging to surgery interval was 25.3 days. An advanced multidetector computed tomography (MDCT) was used as preoperative imaging in 37 of 52 patients (71.2%), while the remaining had been performed standard CT. To postulate our cutoff point of preoperative CA125 used in an analysis of its effect on the optimal surgical outcome, we could identify the value of 416, dividing patients into lower and higher CA125 groups. The population with preoperative CA125 more than 416 U/mL resulted in a thirteenth-time increased risk of suboptimal surgical outcome (OR 13.4, 95%CI 3.2–56.0, p < 0.001), with 70% sensitivity and 88.5% specificity.

Concerning a group of 26 patients (50%) treated by neoadjuvant chemotherapy followed by interval debulking surgery, reduction of CA125 level after chemotherapy cycles would relate to a chance of optimal following surgery. We analyzed the CA125 pretreatment/preoperative ratio and revealed that the optimal cutoff CA125 ratio was 15 (p = 0.03).

In univariate analysis, the variables significantly associated with optimal surgery were ECOG-0, preoperative CA125 < 416 U/mL, preoperative PCI < 7, and intraoperative PCI < 8, as shown in Table 3. The influent factors were further analyzed in a multivariate model. Significantly, the lesser preoperative CA125 (not over 416 U/mL) and the lower intraoperative PCI (not over 8) were the only independent factors that impacted the probability of achieving optimal surgery.

Discussion

Since 1995, the Peritoneal Cancer Index has previously been described and validated as a quantitative measurement of peritoneal malignancy diseases [10]. The PCI was acceptable and widely used to evaluate peritoneal carcinomatosis and predict surgical resectability [13]. In our study, a significant moderate correlation (r = 0.510, p < 0.001) between preoperative PCI imaging and intraoperative PCI measurement was confirmed, being consistent with many previous retrospective studies [15, 17, 18]. Some single institutional studies showed better strong correlation [16, 19]. Of a difference in the measured imaging technique from our method, these studies used MDCT for all subjects, while it was only used for 71.2% of our studied cases. A longer interval between imaging and surgery seems to affect the PCI correlation since we found a lesser correlation in the group of patients who had an interval between imaging and surgery of more than 30 days (r = 0.262) than a strong correlation in those within 30 days (r = 0.810).

According to the analyzed PCIs in different abdominal areas, it was found that two main factors enhanced the correlation between preoperative PCI imaging and intraoperative PCI measurement in each region which were the increased frequency of peritoneal implantation and the larger size of lesions. In detail, regions 0 and 6 showed a strong correlation corresponding to the frequent presentations of peritoneal implantation and the existing larger size of lesions. On the other hand, there was less peritoneal seeding frequency and usually smaller tumors at both sides of paracolic areas (regions 4 and 8), leading to negative correlations, as shown in Tables 4 and 5. In addition, we found that the preoperative PCI would show a lower detection rate in small tumor volume images and also present lesser sensitivity in the small bowel and mesenteric regions (region 9–12). CT imaging is unreliable for detecting tiny tumors, consistent with the findings of many previous investigators [20–22]. Moreover, similar poor image detections in the small bowel and mesenteric areas were repeatedly reported [19, 23].

Our data demonstrated that preoperative and intraoperative PCI measurements were statistically related to the optimal surgical outcome. With the correlative results between both PCI measurements, the lesser preoperative PCI would predict the greater chance of optimal surgery.

In our study, the AUC of intraoperative and preoperative PCI by the optimal surgical outcome were 0.67 and 0.69, respectively, the same as the previous study [13, 24]. These findings showed acceptable association even by a simple PCI system which was not an objective measurement. According to the PCI scoring system, PCI measurement was still limited in advanced-stage ovarian cancer, especially in a communal area of the primary tumor and adjacent organs (region 6). After analyzing residual tumor sites, it was found that tumors isolatedly occupied in the mid-pelvis, including the bladder, rectum, or colon, and also at the diaphragm or liver frequently led to suboptimal surgical outcomes. The score obtained from these areas would be lower than the extent of organ involvement which would warrant a complete resection.

Furthermore, we found that the cutoff values for optimal resection by preoperative and intraoperative PCI were 7 and 8, respectively. To prove their predicting activities, we determined if each of them or any other clinical factor was a significantly influential factor associated with the surgical outcome using multivariate analysis. Only intraoperative PCI value and preoperative CA125 level were considered the independent factors of the optimal surgery. A higher intraoperative PCI than 8 reflected a fifth-time increased risk of suboptimal surgical outcome with 60.0% sensitivity and 74.1% specificity. Although the analysis could not demonstrate preoperative PCI as a significant factor related to the surgical outcome, it is a potentially associated factor resulting in univariate analysis. However, some previous studies reported their extremely high cutoff value of intraoperative PCI predicting optimal surgery. Muallem et al. conducted a retrospective study of 70 patients with advanced-stage ovarian cancer and found that the PCI cutoff value was as high as 20 with 63% sensitivity and 76.7% specificity [8]. In the same way, Ghisoni et al. reported that their intraoperative PCI cutoff value to the suboptimal surgical outcome was more than 16, with 62.5% sensitivity and 90.1% specificity [25]. The difference in these PCI cutoff values might be affected by an attempt at complete cytoreductive surgery performed among individual centers. Therefore, intraoperative decisions using PCI assessment should be based on their data or the result from a center with the same surgical achievement. Preoperative PCI’s predicting advantage was previously determined as a PCI cutoff value in some published studies [16–18]. Ahmed et al. reported a corresponding preoperative PCI cutoff of 20 which could offer an 83.3% chance of optimal surgery, 97% sensitivity, and 40% specificity [16]. Our preoperative PCI cutoff value of 7 showed a significant correlation to those of intraoperative PCI but also a slightly smaller value than its corresponding intraoperative measurement.

Preoperative PCI scores exceeding 7 were predominantly observed in the NACT group, reflecting the surgeon’s decision to administer CMT prior to surgery in initially inoperable cases.

Surprisingly, the underlined significant factor in predicting our optimal surgical outcome was preoperative CA125, whose cutoff value of 416 U/mL reflected a 14.8-fold increased risk of suboptimal surgery. These findings parallel two previously presented results which complied with different cutoffs [8, 25]. Within our subgroup of patients who received neoadjuvant chemotherapy followed by interval debulking surgery, we investigated their CA125 ratio (pretreatment/preoperative) to determine whether a reduction of CA125 level by course of chemotherapy could be the prognosis of surgical outcome, showing a cutoff value of 15. A high CA125 reduction through this ratio would suggest a reasonable probability of a favorable surgical outcome, also matched to a CA 125 ratio of 16.7 reported by Ghisoni et al. [25].

Using correlated preoperative PCI to facilitate the surgical treatment plan for a patient with peritoneal carcinomatosis is practical. For general practice, calculation of preoperative PCI in a proper timing should be considered to enhanced optimal surgery.

Employing correlated preoperative PCI as a guide for surgical planning in patients with peritoneal carcinomatosis is a practical approach. In general practice, calculation of preoperative PCI is recommended to optimize surgical decision-making.

This study had several limitations. First, the sample size was relatively small due to the inclusion criterion of complete intraoperative PCI calculation. Second, preoperative PCI was assessed by a single radiologist to ensure consistency, but this approach may have introduced potential bias. Third, the impact of neoadjuvant chemotherapy on PCI calculation cannot be entirely ruled out, as nearly half of the participants received this treatment. Finally, variations in the time interval between imaging and surgery could have influenced the accuracy of PCI measurements.

In conclusion, our correlation between preoperative PCI and intraoperative PCI is particularly measurable. With lesser PCI that would predict the optimal surgical outcome, the preoperative PCI from practical imaging would be applicable as a predictive method to assess the possibility of optimal cytoreduction and the optimal time of surgery. A further study was conducted to evaluate advanced imaging predicting the surgical outcome, the following cytoreductive surgery guided by the preoperative PCI, and the survival benefit would be facilitated.

Association of Echocardiographic Parameters and Health Status in Patients With Obstructive Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy: Insights From EXPLORER-HCM
Authors: Suzanne V. Arnold, Kensey L. Gosch, Chantal Dolan, Jennifer T. Fine, Ahmad Masri, Sara Saberi, Andrew Wang, Perry M. Elliott, Sheila M. Hegde, Jenny Lam, Amy J. Sehnert, Sharon Cresci, Richard G. Bach, John A. Spertus
Published: 2024

Patients with hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) can have substantial impairments in health status, including exercise intolerance, fatigue, dyspnea, and syncope. Mavacamten reduces ventricular contractility and improves myocardial energetics, resulting in improved echocardiographic parameters,1 exercise capacity,2 and health status.3 We sought to better understand the cross-sectional relationship between echocardiographic-derived measures of cardiac physiology and structure with health status, and the association of changes in echocardiographic parameters over time with concurrent changes in health status.

EXPLORER-HCM trial (A Randomized, Double Blind, Placebo Controlled Clinical Study to Evaluate Mavacamten [MYK-461] in Adults With Symptomatic Obstructive Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy; URL: https://www.clinicaltrials.gov; Unique identifier: NCT03470545) was a double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial of mavacamten versus placebo for 30 weeks in adults with symptomatic obstructive HCM.2 Echocardiograms at baseline and 30 weeks were reviewed by core laboratory personnel blinded to treatment (patients also blinded to echocardiogram results). Patient-reported health status was assessed using the Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire (KCCQ)4; a heart failure-specific measure validated in HCM.5 Physical limitations, symptoms, quality of life, and social limitations domains are combined into the KCCQ–overall summary score (KCCQ-OS); scores range 0 to 100, where a score is better if it’s higher. The protocol was approved by a central or site-specific Institutional Review Board; all patients provided written informed consent. The data that support the findings of this study are available from Bristol Myers Squibb upon reasonable request.

This analysis had 2 aims: (1) examine the cross-sectional associations of echocardiographic parameters with health status at baseline; and (2) examine the longitudinal associations of changes in echocardiographic parameters with changes in health status from baseline to 30 weeks. For cross-sectional analyses, we constructed multivariable linear regression models to explore the association of each echocardiographic parameter with KCCQ-OS, adjusted for age, sex, systolic blood pressure, and body mass index. For longitudinal analyses, models included only the baseline value for each echocardiographic parameter and baseline KCCQ-OS. For each echocardiographic parameter, a nonlinear association with KCCQ-OS was tested using restricted cubic splines. Splines were included for all other continuous variables for adjustment.

Between 2018 and 2019, 251 adults with symptomatic obstructive HCM from 68 clinics in 13 countries were enrolled in EXPLORER-HCM. The analytic cohort included 196 (78%) who completed baseline KCCQ (mean age, 57.9±12.2 y; 39% women; mean interventricular septum thickness, 16.9±2.6 mm; peak left ventricular outflow tract [LVOT] gradient, 51.5±29.9 mm Hg at rest, 73.5±31.5 mm Hg with Valsalva, and 85.0±35.2 mm Hg postexercise). The mean KCCQ-OS was 66.6±18.4 at baseline and 76.3±18.5 at 30 weeks.

In cross-sectional models, only interventricular septum wall thickness was associated with KCCQ-OS (1.2-point lower KCCQ-OS per mm wall thickness [95% CI –2.2 to –0.2]; P=0.015). In this mechanistic analysis from the EXPLORER-HCM trial, we found few echocardiographic factors were cross-sectionally associated with health status. This may be due to narrow distributions of baseline echocardiographic variables introduced by the trial inclusion criteria. Furthermore, while cardiac patients often have health status decrements, it can be challenging to identify anatomic or physiologic factors associated with worse or better health status cross-sectionally, because other patient-centered factors (eg, socioeconomic status, depression) may impact patients’ symptoms and quality of life. This is less of an issue when examining changes in anatomy or physiology over time, where patients serve as their own control. Notably, in longitudinal analyses, declines in the peak LVOT gradient at rest, with Valsalva, and postexercise were each associated with improvements in health status, with declines in resting gradients appearing to have the strongest association. This may be due to resting gradients being more representative than provocable gradients of patients’ routine activities. Additionally, decreases in interventricular septum wall thickness were associated with improvements in health status. Because of the complex relationship between wall thickness and dynamic LVOT gradients in obstructive HCM, the interplay between physiologic and anatomic changes and changes in health status is challenging to disentangle. Contrary to our original hypothesis, measures of diastolic function were not significantly associated with health status, either cross-sectionally or longitudinally. Future studies, with larger cohorts, should further examine these associations in patients with nonobstructive HCM.

Potential limitations to our study include the exclusion of treatment in the models, to avoid it serving as a marker of physiologic changes. This could be a confounder, however, if there are mechanisms of benefit in myosin inhibition beyond the effect on cardiac physiology and anatomy. Additionally, although the echocardiograms were performed using standardized protocols and analyzed at a core laboratory, all parameters have potential error, and some parameters may even change according to patients’ volume statuses. Both factors increase noise in the measurement, making it more challenging to identify associations between these parameters and health status.

In conclusion, in patients with obstructive HCM, the most important anatomic and physiologic changes associated with changes in patients’ health status are the magnitude of LVOT obstruction and septal hypertrophy. These data provide novel insights to inform future studies to define how such anatomic and physiologic measures may better support patient selection for treatment with cardiac myosin inhibitors.

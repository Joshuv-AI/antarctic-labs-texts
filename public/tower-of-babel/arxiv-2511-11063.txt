Uniform Bounds and Uncertainty for Asymptotics of Representations of p p -adic GL N \mathrm{GL}_{N}
Rahul Dalal, Mathilde Gerbelli-Gauthier, Simon Marshall

Abstract
. We prove two results on the growth of dimensions of fixed vectors of representations π \pi of p p -adic GL N \mathrm{GL}_{N} under principal congruence subgroups: First, a uniform bound on the growth of fixed vectors in terms of the GK-dimension π \pi , which we extend to a uniform bound on the Harish-Chandra–Howe coefficients. Second, for π \pi unitary, a quantitative relationship between the GK-dimension of π \pi and the rate of decay of its matrix coefficients. These results are independent of one another and proved in the framework of the Langlands and Zelevinsky classifications.

1.1.
Analytic invariants

We now describe the invariants in more detail.

1.1.1.
Fixed-Vector Growth

For a positive integer
\ell
, let
K_{\ell}
be the
\ell^{\text{th}}
principal congruence subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
:

K_{\ell}:=K_{\ell}^{N}:=\{g\in\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)\,:\,g\equiv\mathbf{I}_{N}\pmod{\varpi^{\ell}}\},

where
N
is omitted when it is clear from context. Our first invariant is the function
\ell\mapsto\dim(\pi^{K_{\ell}})
, which is known to grow polynomially in
q^{\ell}
, see (
1.1.1
). In the global context, this invariant is key to relating counts of automorphic forms (important in analytic applications and more easily accessible via the trace formula) to counts of automorphic representations (more natural from an arithmetic point of view).

We call the smallest integer
\ell(\pi)
such that
\pi^{K_{\ell(\pi)}}\neq 0

of
\pi

1.1.2.
Local Character Expansion

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie algebra of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
, and let
\mathcal{N}:=\mathcal{N}(N)
be the set of nilpotent coadjoint orbits in the dual Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. (Note that henceforth we shall identify
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
with
\mathfrak{g}
using a symmetric nondegenerate invariant bilinear form.) To

corresponds a
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
-invariant orbital integral measure
\mu_{O}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathfrak{g})^{*}
, which, together with its Fourier transform
\widehat{\mu}_{O}
, can be be interpreted as a distribution on
\mathfrak{g}
.

Let
\pi
be a smooth, admissible representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
with character
\Theta_{\pi}
. The following is a special case of a result known for general reductive

-adic groups:

Theorem 1.1.1

(Local Character Expansion,
[

,
18

For a suitable normalization of the

for

such that for small enough

, the trace character of

admits the expansion

\Theta_{\pi}(\exp X)=\sum_{O\in\mathcal{N}}c_{O}(\pi)\widehat{\mu}_{O}(X).

The set of maximal orbits (under the closure ordering) satisfying that
c_{O}(\pi)\neq 0

is known as the
wavefront set
of
\pi
. For
O
in the wavefront set,
c_{O}(\pi)
is equal to the dimension of a corresponding space of degenerate Whittaker models following
[
34
, II.2 Proposition]
in residual characteristic
p\neq 2
and
[
39
]
for
p=2
. The wavefront sets of local representations are connected to the vanishing of the various generalized Fourier coefficients of global automorphic representations (see
[
20
]
for an overview).

For
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
, the wavefront set is determined by
[
34
]
, although for general reductive groups its computation is still an open question and an active subject of research, see e.g.
[
38
,
6
]
.
Even more subtle, and not fully understood even for

[

The character expansion determines the asymptotic rate of growth of fixed vectors via the following notion:

Definition 1.1.2

The Gelfand-Kirillov dimension, or GK-dimension of

is

d_{GK}(\pi)=\frac{1}{2}\max\{\dim O:c_{O}(\pi)\neq 0\}.

Let
D:=D_{N}
be the set of possible GK-dimensions
\{1/2\dim O:O\in\mathcal{N}(N)\}
.

It is well-known

that

(1.1.1)

\dim(\pi^{K_{\ell}})\asymp q^{d_{GK}(\pi)\ell},

1.1.3.
Matrix Coefficient Decay

Finally, we let
G
be a reductive group over a real or
p
-adic field, we consider
\pi
an admissible representation of
G
on a Hilbert space, and introduce the
L^{p}
-integrability exponent
p(\pi)
of
\pi
. For any
v\in\pi
and
w\in\pi^{\vee}
, we can define the matrix coefficient on
G
given by

m(v,w):=\langle gv,w\rangle.

Next, if
\pi
has central character
\omega
, then for any matrix coefficient
m
of
\pi
,
m\omega^{-1}
is defined on the central quotient
G/Z_{G}
. Therefore, the integrals

\|m\|_{q,\omega}=\left(\int_{G/Z_{G}}|m\omega^{-1}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}

are well-defined (but possibly infinite). We say that
m\in L^{q}_{\omega}(G)
if
\|m\|_{q,\omega}<\infty
.

Definition 1.1.3
.

Given an admissible Hilbert space representation
\pi
of a real or
p
-adic reductive group
G
with central character
\omega
, let

p(\pi):=\inf\{q\geq 2\,:\,\text{ the $K$-finite matrix coefficients of }\pi\text{ are in }L^{q}_{\omega}(G)\}.

Representations such that
p(\pi)=2
are
tempered
. The naïve Ramanujan conjecture would give that
p(\pi)=2
for any local component of a cuspidal global automorphic representation. This is still expected to hold for global
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
, but
[
17
]
famously showed that it does not in general, and counterexamples also exist for groups such that
G(F)={\rm GL}_{N}(F)
, e.g.
[
35
]
. Therefore it is crucial in analytic applications to understand how frequently these
p(\pi)
can be large—see the Sarnak-Xue conjecture introduced in
[
36
]
.

We note that for
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
, tempered representations are generic (i.e. have maximal wavefront set), but that the converse does not hold.

1.2.
Results

We work with the explicit combinatorial classifications of smooth irreducible representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
in terms of supercuspidals, which we review in Section
2
. In Section
3
, we prove a uniform upper bound on the growth of dimensions of
K_{\ell}
-fixed vectors in terms of the GK-dimension, making the asymptotic (
1.1.1
) uniform:

Theorem 1.2.1

(Corollary
3.3.1
)
.

For all
\epsilon>0
there is a uniform constant
C_{\epsilon}:=C_{\epsilon,N,F}
such that for any smooth irreducible representation
\pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
, we have

\dim(\pi^{K_{\ell}})\leq C_{\epsilon}q^{\ell(d_{GK}(\pi)+\epsilon)}.

The proof is given in terms of the “Zelevinsky classification”, for which the intermediate building blocks are so-called Speh representations. For supercuspidal representations, a uniform upper bound was established in
[
27
, Lem A.1]
using the Whittaker model. The main contribution here is Theorem
3.2.1
extending the supercuspidal bound to Speh representations using the generalized Whittaker models of
[
34
]
.

In Section
4
, we use a homogeneity property of the distributions
\widehat{\mu}_{O}
appearing in the HCH-expansion of Theorem
1.1.1
to turn Theorem
1.2.1
into a uniform bound on the coefficients
c_{O}(\pi)
in terms of the GK-dimension and level:

Corollary 1.2.2

(Corollary
4.3.1
)
.

Let
\epsilon>0
. Then there is a uniform constant
C_{\epsilon}:=C_{\epsilon,N,F}
such that for all smooth irreducible representations
\pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
and all
O\in\mathcal{N}(N)
, we have

|c_{O}(\pi)|\leq C_{\epsilon}q^{\ell(\pi)(d_{GK}(\pi)-1/2\dim O+\epsilon)}.

In view of the results of
[
32
, §6]
, we believe that these bounds are sharp.

These results are an instance of the rather general principle that the Zelevinsky classification is well-adapted to computing analytic properties of representations near the identity. On the other hand, the decay of matrix coefficients can be computed from the “Langlands classification”, built from discrete Steinberg representations. In the latter half of the paper, we study of the relationship between the GK-dimension and decay of matrix coefficients by playing the two classifications against each other via Aubert-Zelevinsky duality.

Starting in Section
5
, we focus on unitarizable and Arthur-type representations. These are most relevant in global applications, and constitute a special case of the “ladder representations” of
[
23
]
for which the Aubert-Zelevinsky dual can be computed combinatorially. We first review the classification of the unitary dual and use it to prove a variant of Theorem
1.2.1
, Corollary
5.3.2
, which is more suited to global analytic applications.

Finally, in Section
6
, we give a method to compute the
p(\pi)
of Definition
1.1.3
using the notion of “exponents” from
[
14
,
5
]
. In the case of Arthur-type representations this reduces to an especially nice formula, Theorem
6.2.3
, which we use to quantitatively relate the non-genericity and non-temperedness of
\pi
.

Theorem 1.2.3

(Theorem
6.3.2
)
.

Define

g(\pi)=1-\frac{2d_{GK}(\pi)}{N(N-1)}.

Then for all Arthur-type irreducible representations
\pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
:

g(\pi)\leq 1-\frac{2}{p(\pi)}\leq g(\pi)^{1/2}.

For general unitarizable
\pi
, the same identity holds with the right-hand side replaced by
g(\pi)^{1/2}+2/N
.

When
p(\pi)=2
, this is simply the statement that Arthur-type representations are tempered if and only if they are generic: our new contribution is to measure the relationship between
p(\pi)
and
d_{GK}(\pi)
for more degenerate representations. As a corollary, we give bounds on fixed-vector growth and HCH-coefficients in terms of
p(\pi)
.

Remark 1.2.4
.

It is tempting to call Theorem
1.2.3
an uncertainty principle. Indeed, the fixed-vector growth and HCH-coefficients describe the behavior of
\pi
“around the identity” while matrix coefficient decay relates to the behavior of
\pi
“at infinity”. The numerical identity we establish describes a phenomenon likely known to experts: for unitarizable representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
, more singular analytic behavior of the character near the identity, characterized by a larger GK dimension, corresponds to more well-behaved (i.e. closer to square-integrable) behavior of the matrix coefficients at infinity, and vice versa.

1.3.
Generalizations

The focus of this paper is on
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
, but we expect both uniform bounds on fixed vectors and an uncertainty principle to hold for complex-valued representations of general reductive
p
-adic groups. For the uniform upper bounds, a crucial input is a bound on the support of certain matrix coefficients of supercuspidal representations, which is conjectured in
[
13
, §4]
to hold generally and is known in several cases.

Beyond this, our proofs rely heavily on the explicit combinatorics of the Langlands and Zelevinsky classification for
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
as detailed in
[
23
,
24
]
. It is therefore unlikely that our methods would carry through verbatim. For classical groups, we mention recent progress on the local Jiang’s conjecture, which aims to describe the wavefront set of representations inside of an Arthur packet
[
26
]
in terms of the attached
A
-parameter. This, combined with known computations of the
p(\pi)
for
A
-packets of classical groups in terms of the Arthur
SL_{2}

[
8
,
11
]
, could provide an approach to prove an uncertainty principle for classical
A
-packets.

As the most general possible conjecture for fixed vectors:

Conjecture 1.3.1
.

Let
G
be a reductive group over
p
-adic field
F
,
x
a point in its Bruhat-Tits building, and
K_{\ell}:=K_{G,x,\ell}
the
\ell
th Moy-Prasad subgroup at
x
. Then for all
\epsilon>0
, there is a uniform constant
C:=C_{\epsilon,G,F,x}
such that for any smooth irreducible representation
\pi
of
G(F)
:

\dim(\pi^{K_{\ell}})\leq C_{\epsilon}q^{\ell(d_{GK}(\pi)+\epsilon)}.

Note again that uniformity is the key difficulty here since the non-uniform variant holds by the HCH expansion.

2.
Background

2.1.
Nilpotent orbits in
\mathrm{GL}_{N}

Nilpotent orbits in
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
are parameterized by the set
\mathcal{P}(N)
of partitions of
N
via the Jacobson-Morozov theorem, see e.g.
[
7
]
. In this parameterization, the natural closure ordering on orbits corresponds to the partial ordering on
\mathcal{P}(N)
where
P_{1}\preceq P_{2}
if
P_{1}
refines
P_{2}
. For example, the trivial, minimal, and regular nilpotent orbit correspond respectively to

P_{0}=[1^{(N)}],\quad P_{\mathrm{min}}=[2,1^{(N-1)}],\quad P_{\text{max}}=[N].

The set
\mathcal{P}(N)
is equipped with an involution sending
P
to its dual partition

\hat{P}:=[\#{\text{ parts of }P\text{ of size}\geq j}]_{j}.

One derives a dimension formula for nilpotent orbits. If
\hat{P}=[\hat{N}_{1},...,\hat{N}_{r}]
, then

(2.1.1)

\dim O_{P}=N^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}\hat{N}^{2}_{i}.

2.2.
Bernstein-Zelevinsky Classifications

Bernstein–Zelevinsky give two classifications of the irreducible representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
. One is the Langlands classification, in which irreducibles are realized as quotients of parabolic inductions of tempered/Steinberg representations. The other is the Zelevinsky classification, in which irreducible representations appear as subrepresentations of parabolic inductions of anti-tempered/Speh representations.
We recall the combinatorics of these classifications: the original references are
[
2
,
40
]
, but we follow the summary in
[
24
, §2]
.

2.2.1.
Multisegments

Let
N_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq r
be such that
\sum_{i=1}^{r}N_{i}=N
. Given representations
\pi_{i}
of
\mathrm{GL}_{N_{i}}
, define the normalized parabolic induction

\pi_{1}\times\cdots\times\pi_{r}:=\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{GL}_{N_{1}}(F)\times\cdots\times\mathrm{GL}_{N_{r}}(F)}^{\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)}(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\pi_{r})

For a representation
\pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
, we denote by
|\cdot|^{x}\pi
the twist of
\pi
by the
x^{\text{th}}
power of the norm-of-determinant character.

Definition 2.2.1
.

Let
\rho
be a supercuspidal representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
, and
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
such that
b-a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. The corresponding
segment
is the set:

\langle a,b\rangle_{\rho}:=\{|\cdot|^{a}\rho,|\cdot|^{a+1}\rho,\dotsc,|\cdot|^{b}\rho\}.

Note that the triple
(a,b,\rho)
is not uniquely determined by the segment.

Two segments
\langle a,b\rangle_{\rho}
and
\langle a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\rangle_{\rho^{\prime}}
are said to be
linked
if their union is also a segment and neither is contained in the other. We say that
\langle a,b\rangle_{\rho}

precedes

\langle a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\rangle_{\rho^{\prime}}
if they are linked and
|\cdot|^{a^{\prime}}\rho^{\prime}=|\cdot|^{a+j}\rho
for some integer
j>0
.

Note
.

There is always a unique way to represent a segment as
\langle a,b\rangle_{\rho}
for
\rho
unitary. If
\rho,\rho^{\prime}
are unitary, then
\langle a,b\rangle_{\rho}
precedes
\langle a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\rangle_{\rho^{\prime}}
if and only if:

(1)

\rho=\rho^{\prime}
,

(2)

a^{\prime}-a\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(3)

a<a^{\prime},b<b^{\prime},a^{\prime}\leq b+1
.

Definition 2.2.2
.

A
multisegment
is a multiset of segments, denoted
[\langle a_{i},b_{i}\rangle_{\rho_{i}}]_{i}
. We say that
[\langle a_{i},b_{i}\rangle_{\rho_{i}}]_{i}
is
ordered
if for all
i<j
,
\langle a_{i},b_{i}\rangle_{\rho_{i}}
does not precede
\langle a_{j},b_{j}\rangle_{\rho_{j}}
.

Both the Langlands and Zelevinsky classifications of representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
are given in terms of multisegments.

2.2.2.
Langlands Classification

Theorem 2.2.3

(Langlands classification)
.

Every smooth, irreducible representation
\pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
corresponds to a unique multisegment
\check{M}(\pi)=[\langle a_{i},b_{i}\rangle_{\rho_{i}}]_{i=1}^{r}
as follows:

(1)

For each segment
m_{i}=\langle a_{i},b_{i}\rangle_{\rho_{i}}
, there is a unique irreducible quotient:

|\cdot|^{a_{i}}\rho_{i}\times\cdots\times|\cdot|^{b_{i}}\rho_{i}\twoheadrightarrow L(m_{i}),

which is necessarily square-integrable modulo the center.

(2)

\pi
is the unique irreducible quotient:

L(m_{1})\times\cdots\times L(m_{r})\twoheadrightarrow L(\check{M})=\pi

whenever the
m_{i}
are indexed so that
(m_{1},\dotsc,m_{r})
is ordered.

(3)

If none of the
m_{i}
are linked, then
L(m_{1})\times\cdots\times L(m_{r})
is irreducible.

(4)

If all the
\rho_{i}
are unitary,
\pi
is tempered modulo the center if and only if the
a_{i}+b_{i}
are all equal.

Representations constructed in (1) of the above theorem are known as (determinant twist of)
generalized Steinberg representations
. Accordingly, we will denote

L(\langle a,b\rangle_{\rho})=:|\cdot|^{(a+b)/2}\mathrm{St}(b-a+1,\rho).

Lastly, we introduce an invariant from which we will compute the rate of decay of matrix coefficients of
\pi
in §
6.2
.

Definition 2.2.4
.

Given a multisegment
\check{M}=[\langle a_{i},b_{i}\rangle_{\rho_{i}}]_{i=1}^{r}
, with supercuspidal representations
\rho_{i}
of
\mathrm{GL}_{N_{i}}(F)
, define its
character

\Xi(\check{M})
to be the multiset of cardinality
N
given by

\Xi(\check{M}):=\left[\left(\frac{a_{1}+b_{1}}{2}\right)^{(N_{1}(b_{1}-a_{1}+1))},\dotsc,\left(\frac{a_{r}+b_{r}}{2}\right)^{(N_{r}(b_{r}-a_{r}+1))}\right],

and set

\Xi(\pi):=\Xi(\check{M}(\pi)).

2.2.3.
Zelevinsky Classification

Theorem 2.2.5

(Zelevinsky classification)
.

Every smooth irreducible representation
\pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
corresponds to a unique multisegment
M(\pi)=[\langle a_{i},b_{i}\rangle_{\rho_{i}}]_{i=1}^{r}
as follows:

(1)

For each segment
m_{i}=\langle a_{i},b_{i}\rangle_{\rho_{i}}
, there is a unique irreducible subrepresentation:

Z(m_{i})\hookrightarrow|\cdot|^{a_{i}}\rho_{i}\times\cdots\times|\cdot|^{b_{i}}\rho_{i}.

(2)

\pi
is the unique irreducible subrepresentation:

\pi=Z(M)\hookrightarrow Z(m_{1})\times\cdots\times Z(m_{r})

whenever the
m_{i}
are indexed so that
(m_{1},\dotsc,m_{r})
is ordered.

(3)

If none of the
m_{i}
are linked, then
Z(m_{1})\times\cdots\times Z(m_{r})
is irreducible.

Similarly to the case of the Langlands classification, we will use the notation

(2.2.1)

Z(\langle a,b\rangle_{\rho})=:|\cdot|^{(a+b)/2}\Speh(b-a+1,\rho)

and call the corresponding representation a (determinant twist of a)
Speh representation
.

The following invariant will determine the wavefront set, and ultimately the GK-dimension, of a representation
\pi
in the Zelevinsky classification:

Definition 2.2.6
.

Given a multisegment
M=[\langle a_{i},b_{i}\rangle_{\rho_{i}}]_{i=1}^{r}
, with supercuspidals
\rho_{i}
of
\mathrm{GL}_{N_{i}}(F)
, we define its
partition

P(M):=[(a_{1}-b_{1}+1)^{(N_{1})},\dots,(a_{r}-b_{r}+1)^{(N_{r})}]

and write

P(\pi):=P(M(\pi)).

2.2.4.
Duality

The space of smooth irreducible representations of a reductive
p
-adic group
G
is equipped with the
Aubert-Zelevinsky
involution
\pi\mapsto\check{\pi}
. For
G=GL_{N}
, this involution has the property:

Theorem 2.2.7

(
[
24
, §A.5]
)
.

Let
\pi
be a smooth irreducible representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
. Then
M(\pi)=\check{M}(\check{\pi})
.

Moeglin–Waldspurger
[
33
]
give an algorithm to compute
M(\check{\pi})
from
M(\pi)
and vice versa.

2.3.
Degenerate Whittaker Models

We next introduce degenerate Whittaker models, which arise in
[
34
]
in the proof of Theorem
2.4.2
. They will play a central role in our uniform upper bounds.

Let
U\subseteq\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
be the upper-triangular unipotent matrices. Given a partition
P=[d_{1},\dotsc,d_{r}]
of
N
, let

S=\{d_{1},d_{1}+d_{2},\cdots,d_{1}+\cdots+d_{r-1}\}\subseteq\{1,\dotsc,N-1\}

and define a character
\psi_{P}
of
U
by

\psi_{P}:g\mapsto\psi_{F}\left(\sum_{i\notin S}g_{i,i+1}\right),

where the
g_{i,i+1}
are the above-the-diagonal entries of
g
and
\psi_{F}
is a fixed additive character of
F
with conductor
\mathcal{O}
.

Definition 2.3.1
.

Given a smooth representation
\pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
, a
P
-Whittaker model
for
\pi
is an embedding

(2.3.1)

\pi\hookrightarrow W_{P}:=\{f\in C^{\infty}(\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F),\mathbb{C}):f(ug)=\psi_{P}(u)f(g)\,\forall u\in U\},

where
W_{P}
is a
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
-representation under right-translation. The
W_{P}
for different choices of
\psi_{F}
and indexings of the
d_{i}
can be identified. When
P\neq[N]
, a
P
-Whittaker model for
\pi
is said to be
degenerate
. A representation is
generic
if it admits an
[N]
-Whittaker model, which we simply call a
Whittaker model
.

Theorem 2.3.2

(
[
34
, §II.2]
using
[
40
]
)
.

Let
\pi
be a smooth irreducible representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
and let

be as in Definition

. Then
\pi
admits a unique
\hat{P}(\pi)
-Whittaker model up to scalar. Furthermore,
\pi
admits no
Q
-Whittaker model for
Q
that doesn’t refine
\hat{P}(\pi)
.

Proof.

Note that
[
34
, §II.2]
simply summarizes the parts of
[
40
]
explaining which degenerate Whittaker models
\pi
admits and that this argument works in all residue characteristics.
∎

2.3.1.
Restrictions of Whittaker Functions

We recall a property of degenerate Whittaker models of Speh representations:

Lemma 2.3.3
.

Let
\pi
be a Speh representation with degenerate
\hat{P}(\pi)
-Whittaker model
\mathcal{W}(\pi)
. Consider the subgroup
\mathrm{GL}_{N-1}(F)\subset\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
embedded in the upper-left corner. Then the map

\mathcal{W}(\pi)\to C^{\infty}(\mathrm{GL}_{N-1}(F))

given by restriction of functions is injective.

Proof.

Let
P_{N}\subset\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
be the mirabolic subgroup, defined by the property that the bottom row of
p\in P_{N}
is
(0,\dotsc,0,1)
. Observe that

(2.3.2)

P_{N}=U\cdot\mathrm{GL}_{N-1}.

Let
f\in\mathcal{W}(\pi)
. It follows directly from (
2.3.2
) and the equivariance property (
2.3.1
) that if
f
vanishes identically on
\mathrm{GL}_{N-1}(F)
, then it also does so on
P_{N}
.

This reduces the Lemma to showing injectivity of the restriction map

\iota:\mathcal{W}(\pi)\to C^{\infty}(P_{N}).

By Remark 3.6 in
[
40
]
, the restriction of representations
\pi|_{P_{N}}
is irreducible, a property which extends to its Whittaker model

\mathcal{W}(\pi)\simeq\pi
. Then since
\ker\iota
is a
P_{N}
-subrepresentation of
\mathcal{W}(\pi)
, injectivity is equivalent to
\ker\iota\neq\mathcal{W}(\pi)
.

To show this, let
f\in\mathcal{W}(\pi)
be a non-zero function and let
f(g)\neq 0
. Let
a\in\mathrm{GL}_{N}
be such that
ga^{-1}\in P_{N}
. Then
(af)(ga^{-1})\neq 0
so
af\not\in\ker\iota
and
\ker\iota\neq\mathcal{W}(\pi)
.
∎

2.4.
Wavefront Sets

Definition 2.4.1
.

The wavefront set
WF(\pi)
of a smooth irreducible representation
\pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
is the set of maximal elements of

under the closure ordering; equivalently, it is the set of maximal elements of

under the refinement ordering.

Moeglin–Waldspurger compute

from the Zelevinsky classification by relating them to the degenerate Whittaker models that
\pi
admits:

Theorem 2.4.2

(
[
34
]
,
[
39
]
)
.

Let
\pi
be a smooth irreducible representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
. Then

WF(\pi)=\left\{\widehat{P(\pi)}\right\}.

Proof.

This follows from Theorem
2.3.2
together with the main result of
[
34
]
(resp.
[
39
]
in residual characteristic
2
).
∎

Together with (
2.1.1
), we can then compute GK-dimensions:

Corollary 2.4.3
.

If
\pi
is a smooth irreducible representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
with
M(\pi)=[\langle a_{i},b_{i}\rangle_{\rho_{i}}]_{i=1}^{r}
for
\rho_{i}
on

Example 2.4.4
.

If

, the Speh representation
\mathrm{Sp}(d,\rho)=Z(\langle\frac{1-d}{2},\frac{d-1}{2}\rangle_{\rho})
has wavefront set and GK-dimension

3.
Fixed Vector Bounds

3.1.
Supercuspidals and Inductions

We first recall a uniform bound for fixed vectors in supercuspidal representations proved in
[
27
]
:

Lemma 3.1.1

(
[
27
, Lem A.1]
)
.

Fix
N
and
\epsilon>0
. Then there is a uniform constant
C_{\epsilon}=C_{\epsilon,N}
such that if
\pi
is a supercuspidal of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
, then

\dim(\pi^{K_{\ell}})\leq C_{\epsilon}q^{\ell\left(\frac{N(N-1)}{2}+\epsilon\right)}.

Proof.

Sketching the argument in
[
27
]
, the main result of
[
25
]
gives that for any supercuspidal
\pi
, all functions in
\mathcal{W}(\pi)^{K_{\ell}}
are supported on some uniform set
S_{\ell}
depending only on
\ell
. Then, Lemma
2.3.3
bounds the dimension of
\pi^{K_{\ell}}
by the dimension of the space of
K_{\ell}
-invariant Whittaker functions supported on
\mathrm{GL}_{N-1}\cap S_{\ell}
which can be explicitly computed.
∎

Note that the exponent
N(N-1)/2
is the GK-dimension of the supercuspidal (and hence generic) representation
\pi
.

For later use, we also recall a bound on fixed vectors in parabolic inductions:

Lemma 3.1.2
.

For all
N
, there are uniform
C_{1},C_{2}
such that for all

\pi=\rho_{1}\times\cdots\times\rho_{r}

with each
\rho_{i}
a smooth representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{N_{i}}(F)
and
\sum_{i=1}^{r}N_{i}=N
:

C_{1}\dim(\pi^{K_{\ell}})\leq q^{\frac{1}{2}(N^{2}-\sum_{i}N_{i}^{2})}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\dim(\rho_{i}^{K_{\ell}})\leq C_{2}\dim(\pi^{K_{\ell}}).

Proof.

This is standard; see e.g. the proof of
[
27
, Lem 5.2]
.
∎

3.2.
Speh Representations

We now move to the intermediate and technically most complex step: bounding fixed vectors in Speh representations.

Theorem 3.2.1
.

Let
N,k>0
. There is a uniform constant
C:=C_{N,k}
such that for all supercuspidal representations
\rho
of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
we have

\dim(\Speh(k,\rho)^{K_{\ell}})\leq Cq^{\frac{1}{2}\ell k(k-1)N(N-1)}\left(\dim(\rho^{K_{\ell}})\right)^{k}.

Proof.

By induction, it suffices to find a uniform
C:=C_{N,k}
such that:

\dim(\Speh(k,\rho)^{K_{\ell}})\leq Cq^{\ell kN(N-1)}\dim(\Speh(k-1,\rho)^{K_{\ell}})\dim(\rho^{K_{\ell}}).

Let
\pi=\Speh(k,\rho)
and
\pi_{0}=\Speh(k-1,\rho)
. Define the following subgroups of
\mathrm{GL}_{Nk}
:

•

\mathrm{GL}_{Nk-1}
,
\mathrm{GL}_{N(k-1)}
in the upper-left corner,

•

\mathrm{GL}_{N}
in the lower-right corner,

•

\mathrm{GL}_{N-1}
in the upper-left corner of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
,

•

P
the
(N(k-1),N-1)
-upper parabolic of
\mathrm{GL}_{Nk-1}
and
V
its unipotent radical.

By Theorem
2.3.2
,
\pi
has a unique
[N^{(k)}]
-Whittaker model
\mathcal{W}(\pi)
; likewise
\pi_{0}
has a unique
[N^{(k-1)}]
-Whittaker model
\mathcal{W}(\pi_{0})
. Lastly, let
\mathcal{W}(\rho)
be the Whittaker model of
\rho
.

Let
\Theta
be the space
\mathcal{W}(\pi)^{K^{Nk}_{\ell}}|_{\mathrm{GL}_{Nk-1}}
of functions on
\mathrm{GL}_{Nk-1}
. By Lemma
2.3.3
,
\dim\pi^{K_{\ell}^{Nk}}\leq\dim\Theta
. Our goal is to bound
\dim\Theta
, which we will do by restriction to
P
.

By
[
22
, (3a)]
, there is unramified character
\chi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{N(k-1)}\times\mathrm{GL}_{N}
such that restriction of functions gives a containment:

(3.2.1)

\mathcal{W}(\pi)|_{\mathrm{GL}_{N(k-1)}\times\mathrm{GL}_{N}}\subseteq\chi\otimes(\mathcal{W}(\pi_{0})\boxtimes\mathcal{W}(\rho)).

Let
\Psi
be the space
\mathcal{W}(\rho)^{K_{\ell}^{N}}\mid_{GL_{N-1}}
given by restricting functions. Let
\Phi
be the space of functions on
P
that are left-invariant under
V
and whose restriction to
\mathrm{GL}_{N(k-1)}\times\mathrm{GL}_{N-1}
lies in
\chi\otimes\mathcal{W}(\pi_{0})^{K_{\ell}^{N(k-1)}}\otimes\Psi
. It follows from (
3.2.1
) that if
f\in\mathcal{W}(\pi)^{K_{\ell}^{Nk}}
then
f|_{P}\in\Phi
.

If we let
\gamma_{i}
be a set of double coset representatives for

(K^{Nk-1}\cap P)\backslash K^{Nk-1}/K^{Nk-1}_{\ell},

then from the Iwasawa decomposition for
\mathrm{GL}_{Nk-1}
we have that

\mathrm{GL}_{Nk-1}=\bigcup_{i}P\gamma_{i}K^{Nk-1}_{\ell}.

If
f\in\Theta
, then the function
p\mapsto f(p\gamma_{i})
lies in
\Phi
for all
i
. It follows that

\dim\Theta\leq\#\left((K^{Nk-1}\cap P)\backslash K^{Nk-1}/K^{Nk-1}_{\ell}\right)\dim\Phi.

Next,

\#(K^{Nk-1}\cap P)\backslash K^{Nk-1}/K^{Nk-1}_{\ell}=\#P(\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{\ell})\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{Nk-1}(\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{\ell})\leq Cq^{\ell(N-1)N(k-1)}

for some constant
C
, by a dimension count and the formula for the order of a general linear group over a finite field. Computing

\dim\Phi=\dim\mathcal{W}(\pi_{0})^{K^{N(k-1)}_{\ell}}\dim\Psi=\dim\pi_{0}^{K^{N(k-1)}_{\ell}}\dim\rho^{K^{N}_{\ell}}

then completes the proof.
∎

Substituting in Lemma
3.1.1
:

Corollary 3.2.2
.

Let
N,k>0
. Then for any
\epsilon>0
, there is a uniform constant
C_{\epsilon}
such that for all supercuspidal representations
\rho
of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
,

\dim(\Speh(k,\rho)^{K_{\ell}})\leq C_{\epsilon}q^{\ell(\frac{1}{2}k^{2}N(N-1)+\epsilon)}.

It is also useful to sometimes use the bound

(3.2.2)

\dim(\Speh(k,\rho)^{K_{\ell}})\leq C_{\epsilon}q^{\ell(\frac{1}{2}(k^{2}-1)N(N-1)+\epsilon)}\dim(\rho^{K_{\ell}})

in the same notation as Theorem
3.2.1
and Corollary
3.2.2
.

3.3.
General Representations

Combining the Speh bound of Corollary
3.2.2
with the Zelevinsky classification gives the general bound:

Corollary 3.3.1
.

Let
N>0
and fix
N_{i},k_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq r
such that
\sum_{i}N_{i}k_{i}=N
. Then for any
\epsilon>0
, there is a uniform constant
C_{\epsilon}
such that for all smooth admissible irreducible representations
\pi
with
M(\pi)=(\langle a_{i},b_{i}\rangle_{\rho_{i}})_{i=1}^{r}
with
b_{i}-a_{i}+1=k_{i}
and
\rho_{i}
a supercuspidal representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{N_{i}}(F)
,

\dim(\pi^{K_{\ell}})\leq C_{\epsilon}q^{\frac{1}{2}\ell(N^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}N_{i}k_{i}^{2}+\epsilon)}.

Proof.

Let
x_{i}=(a_{i}+b_{i})/2
. Then
\pi
is a subrepresentation of the parabolic induction

\sigma:=|\cdot|^{x_{i}}\Speh(k_{i},\rho_{i})\times\cdots\times|\cdot|^{x_{r}}\Speh(k_{r},\rho_{r}).

Therefore, for some constant
C
,

\dim(\pi^{K_{\ell}})\leq\dim(\sigma^{K_{\ell}})\leq Cq^{\frac{1}{2}(N^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}N_{i}^{2}k_{i}^{2})}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\dim((|\cdot|^{x_{i}}\Speh(k_{i},\rho_{i}))^{K_{\ell}})

by Lemma
3.1.2
. Finally, by Corollary
3.2.2
, there is
C_{\epsilon}
such that

(3.3.1)

\dim((|\cdot|^{x_{i}}\Speh(k_{i},\rho_{i}))^{K_{\ell}})=\dim(\Speh(k_{i},\rho_{i})^{K_{\ell}})\leq C_{\epsilon}q^{\frac{1}{2}\ell(N_{i}^{2}k_{i}^{2}-N_{i}k_{i}^{2}+\epsilon)},

from which the result follows.
∎

In the notation of Corollary
3.3.1
, Corollary
2.4.3
gives

d_{GK}(\pi)=N^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}N_{i}k_{i}^{2}

which depends only on the
k_{i}
and
N_{i}
and is exactly the exponent on
q^{\ell}
in Corollary
3.3.1
. Maximizing constants over the finitely many choices of
N_{i},k_{i}
gives Theorem
1.2.1
.

If we input (
3.2.2
) or Theorem
3.2.1
instead of Corollary
3.2.2
in the intermediate equation (
3.3.1
), we get alternate uniform bounds

(3.3.2)

\displaystyle\dim(\pi^{K_{\ell}})

\displaystyle\leq C_{\epsilon}q^{\ell\left(d_{GK}(\pi)-\sum_{i=1}^{r}d_{GK}(\rho_{i})+\epsilon\right)}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\dim(\rho_{i}^{K_{\ell}})

(3.3.3)

\displaystyle\dim(\pi^{K_{\ell}})

\displaystyle\leq C_{\epsilon}q^{\ell\left(d_{GK}(\pi)-\sum_{i=1}^{r}k_{i}d_{GK}(\rho_{i})+\epsilon\right)}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(\dim(\rho_{i}^{K_{\ell}})\right)^{k_{i}}

in the same notation as Corollary
3.3.1
.

4.
Homogeneity and Harish-Chandra-Howe Coefficients

We introduce a notion of scaling families of functions that will allow us to translate the upper bounds on fixed vectors into bounds on the Harish-Chandra-Howe coefficients of a representation. We use the notation of Section
1.1.2
.

Definition 4.0.1
.

Let
f
be a function on
\mathfrak{g}
. For each
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
, define

(4.0.1)

f^{(\ell)}(X):=q^{\ell\dim\mathfrak{g}}f(\varpi^{-\ell}X).

For
\ell
large enough such that the exponential map is a bijection from
\varpi^{\ell}\Supp(f)
, also define

\varphi^{(\ell)}(\exp X)=f^{(\ell)}(X).

We call such sequences
f^{(\ell)}
on
\mathfrak{g}
or
\varphi^{(\ell)}
on
G

scaling families
.

Example 4.0.2
.

The notion of scaling family is a direct generalization of the prototypical example

f^{(\ell)}=\mu(L_{\ell})^{-1}\mathbf{1}_{L_{\ell}},

for
L_{\ell}=\varpi^{\ell}M_{n}(\mathcal{O})
. These functions satisfy the defining property (
4.0.1
) for
\ell\geq 1
. The corresponding family on
G
is
\varphi^{(\ell)}=\bar{\mathbf{1}}_{K_{\ell}}
, the normalized indicator functions of the subgroups
K_{\ell}
.

4.1.
Homogeneity bounds from Character Expansions

The next proposition is a variant for scaling families of a result that is certainly known to the experts in the case of
\varphi^{(\ell)}=\bar{\mathbf{1}}_{K_{\ell}}
, in which case it implies the identity

\dim(\pi^{K_{\ell}})\asymp q^{\ell d_{GK}(\pi)}.

Lemma 4.1.1
.

Let
f^{(\ell)}
be a scaling family. For any
O\in\mathcal{N}
we have

\hat{\mu}_{O}(f^{(\ell)})=\hat{\mu}_{O}(f)q^{\ell\cdot\dim O/2}.

Proof.

This follows directly from the homogeneity of the distributions
\mu_{O}
established in
[
15
]
. Let
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathfrak{g})
and recall that by definition
\hat{\mu}(\phi):=\mu(\hat{\phi})
.
The Fourier transform on
\mathfrak{g}
is defined as

\hat{\phi}(Y)=\int_{\mathfrak{g}}\phi(X)\psi(B(X,Y))dX,

where
\psi
is a fixed character of
F
and
B
is a nondegenerate symmetric invariant bilinear form. The usual property of the Fourier transform holds in our setup:

(4.1.1)

\widehat{\phi(aX)}=\frac{1}{|a|^{\dim\mathfrak{g}}}\hat{\phi}(X/a).

We apply this to
f^{(\ell)}(X)=q^{\ell\cdot\dim\mathfrak{g}}f(\varpi^{-\ell}X)
and get
\widehat{f^{(\ell)}}(X)=\hat{f}(\varpi^{\ell}X)
.

The result of
[
15
, §3.1]
shows that, given a family of functions
\phi_{t}(X):=\phi(tX)
, the nilpotent orbital integrals satisfy

\mu_{O}(\phi_{t})=|t^{-1}|^{\dim O/2}\mu_{O}(\phi).

By applying this to
\phi=\hat{f}
and
t=\varpi^{\ell}
, we see that

\mu_{O}(\widehat{f^{(\ell)}})=|\varpi^{-\ell}|^{\dim O/2}\mu_{O}(\hat{f})=q^{\ell\cdot\dim O/2}\mu_{O}(\hat{f}).

It folows that for each orbit
O
we have

(4.1.2)

\hat{\mu}_{O}(f^{(\ell)})=\hat{\mu}_{O}(f)q^{\ell\cdot\dim O/2},

as claimed.
∎

Remark 4.1.2
.

Using the Harish-Chandra–Howe expansion and applying Lemma
4.1.1
, the scaling family
\varphi^{(\ell)}=\bar{\mathbf{1}}_{K_{\ell}}
of Example
4.0.2
gives that for each smooth irreducible representation
\pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
:

\tr_{\pi}(\bar{\mathbf{1}}_{K_{\ell}})=\dim(\pi^{K_{\ell}})\asymp q^{\ell d_{GK}(\pi)},

where the implied constants in the
\asymp
are non-uniform depending on
\pi
. Here we used that
\widehat{\mu}_{O}(\bar{\mathbf{1}}_{K_{\ell}})>0
since
\bar{\mathbf{1}}_{K_{\ell}}
has positive Fourier transform.

A similar asymptotic with non-uniform constants holds for arbitrary
|\tr_{\pi}(\varphi^{(\ell)})|
provided that
\sum_{O\in WF(\pi)}\widehat{\mu}_{O}(f)\neq 0
, where
\varphi^{(\ell)}
is the scaling family associated to
f
(the sum is a singleton in our
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
case).

4.2.
Homegeneity bounds from Fixed Vectors

The fixed vector bound of Theorem
1.2.1
can be upgraded to a bound on traces against scaling families. In contrast with the above remark, note that our goal here is uniform constants.

Proposition 4.2.1
.

Let
\varphi^{(\ell)}
be a scaling family defined from
f:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{C}
. Let
\pi
be a smooth irreducible representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
. Then for any
\epsilon>0
, there is a constant
C_{\epsilon,f}:=C_{\epsilon,N,f,F}
independent of
\pi
such that for large enough
\ell
, we have

|\tr_{\pi}(\varphi^{(\ell)})|\leq C_{\epsilon,f}q^{\ell(d_{GK}(\pi)+\epsilon)}.

Proof.

Recall
L_{\ell}=\varpi^{\ell}M_{n}(\mathcal{O})
. Since the
L_{\ell}
form a basis at the identity for the topology on
\mathfrak{g}
, there exists
r
such that for some fixed constants
c_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
, and
a_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}
:

f=\sum_{i}c_{i}\bar{\mathbf{1}}_{a_{i}+L_{r}}\implies f^{(\ell)}=\sum_{i}c_{i}\bar{\mathbf{1}}_{\varpi^{\ell}a_{i}+L_{r+\ell}}.

Since for large
\ell
,
\exp(L_{\ell})=K_{\ell}
, the Zassenhaus formula gives that
\exp(\varpi^{\ell}a_{i}+L_{r+\ell})=\exp(\varpi^{\ell}a_{i})K_{r+\ell}
for
\ell
larger than some threshold
\ell_{0}
depending only on
a_{i},N,F
. In total, for
\ell\geq\ell_{0}

\varphi^{(\ell)}=\sum_{i}c_{i}\bar{\mathbf{1}}_{\exp(\varpi^{\ell}a_{i})K_{r+\ell}}.

Next,
\bar{\mathbf{1}}_{\exp(\varpi^{\ell}a_{i})K_{r+\ell}}
acts on
\pi
by projecting to
\pi^{K_{r+\ell}}
and then acting by
\exp(\varpi^{\ell}a_{i})
. We can without loss of generality choose
\ell_{0}
so that all the
\exp(\varpi^{\ell}a_{i})
are also contained in some compact subgroup and therefore act unitarily with respect to some Hermitian form. This gives the bound

|\tr_{\pi}(\bar{\mathbf{1}}_{\exp(\varpi^{\ell}a_{i})K_{r+\ell}})|\leq\dim\pi^{K_{r+\ell}}.

In total

|\tr_{\pi}(\varphi^{(\ell)})|\leq\sum_{i}|c_{i}||\tr_{\pi}(\bar{\mathbf{1}}_{\exp(\varpi^{\ell}a_{i})K_{r+\ell}})|\leq C_{f}\dim\pi^{K_{r+\ell}}

for some constant
C_{f}
depending only on
f
. The result then follows by Theorem
1.2.1
.
∎

4.3.
Harish-Chandra-Howe Coefficients

Proposition
4.2.1
and the homogeneity result of Lemma
4.1.1
let us give a uniform bound on the coefficients
c_{O}(\pi)
in terms of the level of
\pi
and its GK-dimension.

Corollary 4.3.1
.

Let
\epsilon>0
. Then there is a uniform constant
C_{\epsilon}:=C_{\epsilon,N,F}
such that for all smooth irreducible representations
\pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
and all
O\in\mathcal{N}(N)
, we have

|c_{O}(\pi)|\leq C_{\epsilon}q^{\ell(\pi)(d_{GK}(\pi)-1/2\dim O+\epsilon)}.

Proof.

There is a function
f_{O}
on
\mathfrak{g}
such that for all
O^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}(N)
,
\widehat{\mu}_{O^{\prime}}(f_{O})=\mathbf{1}_{O=O^{\prime}}
(see
[
21
, §6.4]
for the corresponding function for
\mu_{O}
and take Fourier transforms). Define the corresponding scaling family
\varphi_{O}^{(\ell)}
for all
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
, where
\ell_{0}
is the threshold appearing in the proof of Proposition
4.2.1
and depends only on
f_{O}
.

By Proposition
4.2.1
, for all
\epsilon>0
, there is
C_{\epsilon,O}
such that for any
\pi
and any
\ell>\ell_{0}

|\tr_{\pi}(\varphi_{O}^{(\ell)})|\leq C_{\epsilon,O}q^{\ell(d_{GK}(\pi)+\epsilon)}.

On the other hand, the main result of
[
10
]
gives that for
\ell(\pi)\geq\ell_{0}
,

\tr_{\pi}(\varphi_{O}^{(\ell(\pi)+1)})=\sum_{O^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}(N)}c_{O^{\prime}}(\pi)\widehat{\mu}_{O^{\prime}}(f^{(\ell(\pi)+1)}_{O})=c_{O}(\pi)q^{1/2(\ell(\pi)+1)\dim O},

where the last equality follows from the homogeneity property of Lemma
4.1.1
and
\widehat{\mu}_{O}(f_{O})=1
.

Combining the two equations and maximizing the constants over
O
we have

|c_{O}(\pi)|\leq q^{d_{GK}(\pi)-1/2\dim O}C_{\epsilon}q^{\ell(\pi)(d_{GK}(\pi)-1/2\dim O+\epsilon)}

whenever
\ell(\pi)\geq\ell_{0}
. We can without loss of generality change the constant to handle all
\pi
by using an alternate bound by
q^{\ell_{0}(d_{GK}(\pi)-1/2\dim O+\epsilon)}
for small
\ell
.
∎

5.
Arthur-type and Unitarizable Representations

5.1.
Classification

In global applications, we mostly care about unitarizable irreducible representations. These have a more refined classification with particularly nice properties which we now recall.

For
1\leq i\leq r
, let
\rho_{i}
be a supercuspidal on
\mathrm{GL}_{N_{i}}(F)
and let
a_{i},d_{i}
be positive integers. We denote

(5.1.1)

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}|\cdot|^{x_{i}}\rho_{i}[a_{i}][d_{i}]:=L(\check{M})

where we recall that
L(\check{M})
denotes a Langlands quotient, and
\check{M}
is the multisegment

(5.1.2)

\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{r}\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{d_{i}}\left[\left\langle x_{i}+\frac{d_{i}-2j+1}{2}+\frac{1-a_{i}}{2},x_{i}+\frac{d_{i}-2j+1}{2}+\frac{a_{i}-1}{2}\right\rangle_{\rho_{i}}\right].

Definition 5.1.1
.

The representation
\pi
is
Arthur-type
if

\pi=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\rho_{i}[a_{i}][d_{i}],

i.e. if all the
x_{i}
in (
5.1.1
) are
0
.

Remark 5.1.2
.

This definition is motivated by Arthur’s classification: if
\pi
is Arthur-type
and
\rho_{i}

The dependence on the Arthur-

5.3.
A Variant Fixed-Vector Bound

For global applications, it is convenient to have a fixed vector bound analogous to (
3.2.2
) in terms of the Langlands, rather than Zelevinsky, classification. We begin by proving a lower bound on fixed vectors in Steinberg representations:

Lemma 5.3.1
.

Choose
N,k
. Then there are uniform constants

such that for
\rho
a supercuspidal on
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
and

:

\dim(\mathrm{St}(k,\rho)^{K_{\ell}})\geq C_{2}q^{\frac{1}{2}\ell N^{2}k(k-1)}\left(\dim(\rho^{K_{\ell}})\right)^{k}.

Proof.

By
[
40
, §9.1]
, the Zelevinsky data of
\mathrm{St}(k,\rho)
is
[|\cdot|^{\frac{k-1}{2}}\rho,\ldots,|\cdot|^{\frac{-k+1}{2}}\rho]
, so that it is a subrepresentation of

\mathrm{St}(k,\rho)\subseteq\sigma:=|\cdot|^{\frac{k-1}{2}}\rho\times|\cdot|^{\frac{k-3}{2}}\rho\times\cdots\times|\cdot|^{\frac{-k+1}{2}}\rho.

Moreover,
\mathrm{St}(k,\rho)
occurs with multiplicity 1 in
\sigma
by
[
40
, Thm 7.1]
. By Lemma
3.1.2
, there is a uniform constant
C
such that

\dim(\sigma^{K_{\ell}})\geq Cq^{\frac{1}{2}\ell N^{2}k(k-1)}\left(\dim(\rho^{K_{\ell}})\right)^{k}.

Consider the non-Steinberg irreducible subquotients
\pi
of
\sigma
, which satisfy:

\dim(\mathrm{St}(k,\rho)^{K_{\ell}})=\dim(\sigma^{K_{\ell}})-\sum_{\pi\neq\mathrm{St}(k,\rho)}\dim(\pi^{K_{\ell}}).

We give an upper bound on this rightmost sum.

Let
\pi\neq\mathrm{St}(k,\rho)
be one of the other subquotients. By uniqueness of supercuspidal supports,
we have
M(\pi)=[m_{1},\dotsc,m_{r}]
with
r<k
and

\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}=\left\langle\frac{1-k}{2},\frac{k-1}{2}\right\rangle_{\rho}.

In particular, Corollary
2.4.3
implies that
d_{GK}(\pi)<\frac{1}{2}Nk(Nk-1)
, so that (
3.3.3
) gives

\dim(\pi^{K_{\ell}})\leq C^{\prime}q^{\frac{1}{2}\ell(N^{2}k(k-1)-a)}\left(\dim(\rho^{K_{\ell}})\right)^{k}

for some

and uniform

. Finally,
\pi
decomposes into
2^{k-1}
irreducible subquotients by
[
40
, Cor 2.3]
.
∎

Combining this with (
3.3.3
) gives the following bound for representations associated to generalized Arthur parameters:

Corollary 5.3.2
.

For any
\epsilon>0
, there is a uniform constant
C_{\epsilon}:=C_{\epsilon,N,F}
such that the following holds. If
\psi
is a generalized
2
2

2

i.e, in Arthur’s set

By Corollary
5.2.3
and Theorem

Remark 5.3.3
.

The above result is motivated by the problem of counting non-tempered automorphic forms on unitary groups as the split level varies. If

are cuspidal automorphic representations of

are tempered.)

In

, the first two authors make a conjecture about an optimal upper bound for these counts in terms of the global Arthur-

combined with the arguments of

9
]
or

]
is enough to prove this conjecture. It therefore implies an upper bound with the expected optimal exponent on the growth of counts of automorphic forms on unitary groups with fixed cohomological component at infinity—see
[
9
, Thm 11.4.2]
and the discussion afterwards. This will be explained in more detail in a future work.

Matrix Coefficient Decay and Uncertainty

In this final section, we show that the matrix coefficient decay of a unitarizable representation

\mathrm{SL}_{2}

6.1.
Exponents

Following Casselman
[
5
]
, the matrix coefficient decay of a representation is determined by a key set of invariants called

6.1.1.
Definition and Properties

Definition 6.1.1

(
[
5
,
14
]
)
.

Fix a minimal parabolic
P_{0}
of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
. Let
\pi
be a smooth irreducible representation of
G=\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
. An
exponent

\omega
of

is a central character of an irreducible subquotient of the normalized Jacquet functor
\mathcal{R}^{G}_{M}(\pi)
associated to the Levi factor
M
of a standard parabolic
P

The character

M=\prod_{j}\mathrm{GL}_{N_{j}}(F)
which we can decompose as

\omega=\boxtimes_{j}\omega_{j},\quad\omega_{j}=\phi_{j}|\det|^{\alpha_{j}}:Z_{\mathrm{GL}_{N_{j}}(F)}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}

for
\phi_{j}
unitary and
\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
. Since

determines an ordering of the blocks of
M
, we abuse notation and also denote by
\omega
the ordered
N

\omega=\left(\alpha_{j}^{(N_{j})}\right)_{j}.

This is normalized so that the unramified character on the torus determined by this list of numbers restricts on
Z_{M}
to the “unramified part” of
\omega
as above.

In order to compute with exponents, we introduce some combinatorial notation.

Definition 6.1.2
.

Given a sequence
a=(a_{1},\dotsc,a_{n})
of real numbers:

•

Let
\sigma_{i}(a)=\sum_{j\leq i}a_{j}
(where out-of-bounds elements are treated as

for indexing purposes).

•

Given another sequence

, we say that

is dominated by
a^{\prime}
, or
a\preceq a^{\prime}
, if for all

\sigma_{i}(a)\leq\sigma_{i}(a^{\prime})
.

Given a multiset
\Xi
of real numbers, we can abuse notation and treat it as a sequence in non-increasing order and similarly define the above notions.

6.1.2.
Minimal Exponent

When introducing the Langlands classification in Definition

, we defined a so-called character
\Xi(\pi)

Lemma 6.1.3
.

Let
\pi
be an Arthur-type irreducible representation of

p(\pi)
introduced in Definition
1.1.3
.

Proposition 6.2.1
.

Let
\pi
be a unitarizable irreducible representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
. Then

\frac{2}{p(\pi)}=1-\max_{1\leq i\leq N-1}\frac{2\sigma_{i}(\Xi(\pi))}{i(N-i)}.

Proof.

We prove this in the framework of
[
5
, §4]
and apply a variant (which goes through mutatis mutandis) of Theorem 4.4.6 therein, where
p=2
is replaced by
p>2
.

We recall the bijection between standard parabolic subgroups of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
and subsets
\Theta\subset\Delta
of the simple roots. Let
A
be the diagonal subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
. Given
\Theta\subset\Delta
, we let
A_{\Theta}\subset A
be the common kernel of all
\alpha\in\Theta
,
M_{\Theta}
be the centralizer of
A_{\Theta}
in
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
, and
P_{\Theta}
be the standard parabolic with Levi
M_{\Theta}
. We also define

A_{\Theta}^{-}=\{x\in A_{\Theta}:|\alpha(x)|\leq 1\text{ for all }\alpha\in\Delta\setminus\Theta\},

and let
A^{-}=A_{\emptyset}^{-}
. Theorem 4.4.6 of
[
5
]
then states that the matrix coefficients of
\pi
belong to
L^{p+\epsilon}
for all
\epsilon>0
if and only if for any
\Theta\subset\Delta
and any exponent
\chi
associated to
P_{\Theta}
and viewed as a character of
A_{\Theta}
, we have

(6.2.1)

\left|\chi\delta^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}(x)\right|\leq 1\quad\text{for all }x\in A_{\Theta}^{-}.

Note that this differs from Casselman’s statement by
\delta^{1/2}
since we are working with normalized parabolic induction.

We next introduce some notation to distinguish between exponents viewed as ordered
N
-tuples and as characters. If
E
is an exponent of
\pi
associated to the Levi
M_{\Theta}
, and viewed as an ordered
N
-tuple, we let
\chi_{E}
be the corresponding character of
A_{\Theta}
. (Note that
\chi_{E}
is not uniquely determined by
E
, but
|\chi_{E}|
is.) We let
\widetilde{\chi}_{E}
denote the unramified positive character of
A
corresponding to the
N
-tuple
E
, which has the property that
\widetilde{\chi}_{E}=|\chi_{E}|
on
A_{\Theta}
.

If
E
and
E^{\prime}
are exponents of
\pi
, the definition of
\prec
in Definition
6.1.2
implies that

E^{\prime}\preceq E\iff\widetilde{\chi}_{E}(x)\leq\widetilde{\chi}_{E^{\prime}}(x)\text{ for all }x\in A^{-}.

We therefore conclude from Lemma
6.1.7
that all exponents
E
of
\pi
satisfy

\widetilde{\chi}_{E}(x)\leq\widetilde{\chi}_{-\Xi(\pi)}(x)\text{ for all }x\in A^{-}.

Let
p^{\prime}
be defined by the condition

(6.2.2)

\frac{2}{p^{\prime}}=1-\max_{1\leq i\leq N-1}\frac{2\sigma_{i}(\Xi(\pi))}{i(N-i)}.

We first show that
p(\pi)\leq p^{\prime}
, which means showing that (
6.2.1
) holds with
p=p^{\prime}
. Let
E
be an exponent of
\pi
associated to the Levi
M_{\Theta}
, and let
\chi_{E}
and
\widetilde{\chi}_{E}
be as above. To show that
\left|\chi_{E}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}(x)\right|\leq 1
on
A_{\Theta}^{-}
, it suffices to show that
\widetilde{\chi}_{-\Xi(\pi)}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}(x)\leq 1
on
A^{-}
. Moreover, it suffices to check this at the elements

x_{i}=(\overbrace{\varpi,...,\varpi}^{i},1,...,1)\in A^{-},\quad 1\leq i\leq N-1

corresponding to the fundamental weights, as these generate the semigroup
A^{-}/A(\mathcal{O})Z
. With the notation
\sigma_{i}
introduced in Definition
6.1.2
, we have

(6.2.3)

\log_{q}\widetilde{\chi}_{-\Xi(\pi)}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}(x_{i})=\sigma_{i}(\Xi(\pi))-\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\right)i(N-i).

Rearranging, the definition of
p^{\prime}
implies that the right hand side is nonpositive, as required.

To show that
p(\pi)\geq p^{\prime}
, we will show that (
6.2.1
) doesn’t hold when
p<p^{\prime}
and
E=-\Xi(\pi)
. If
-\Xi(\pi)
corresponds to the Levi
M_{\Theta}
, it suffices to show that there is
x_{i}\in A_{\Theta}^{-}
such that
|\chi_{-\Xi(\pi)}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}(x_{i})|=1
. By (
6.2.3
), this will follow if the maximum in (
6.2.2
) is realized at an
i
such that
x_{i}\in A_{\Theta}^{-}
.

We do this using Lemma
6.2.2
below. To apply it, write
\Xi(\pi)
(considered as an ordered list) as

(d_{1}^{(a_{1})},\dotsc,d_{r}^{(a_{r})},0^{(b)},-d_{r}^{(a_{r})},\dotsc,-d_{1}^{(a_{1})})

with
d_{i}
distinct. If
M_{\Sigma}
is the Levi corresponding to the partition
(a_{1},...,a_{r},b,a_{r},...,a_{1})
, then because
\Xi(\pi)
is constant on the blocks of
M_{\Theta}
, we must have
M_{\Theta}\subset M_{\Sigma}
and
A_{\Sigma}\subset A_{\Theta}
. It therefore suffices to show that the maximum in (
6.2.2
) is realized for
i
such that
x_{i}\in A_{\Sigma}^{-}
, or equivalently (because we may assume without loss of generality that
i\leq N/2
), of the form
a_{1}+\ldots+a_{j}
for some
j
, and this is established by Lemma
6.2.2
.

∎

Lemma 6.2.2
.

Define an ordered list of length
n
and indices
s_{j}
by:

\Xi=(d_{1}^{(a_{1})},\dotsc,d_{r}^{(a_{r})},0^{(b)},-d_{r}^{(a_{r})},\dotsc,-d_{1}^{(a_{1})}),\qquad s_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{j}a_{i},

where
d_{1}>\cdots>d_{r}
. Then the maximum value

\max_{i\leq N/2}\frac{\sigma_{i}(\Xi)}{i(N-i)}

can only be achieved at
i=s_{j}
for some
j
.

Proof.

Let
\rho=(N-1,N-3,\dotsc,-N+3,-N+1)
. Any maximizing index must be at most
s_{r}
, and if it is not equal to
s_{r}
we may write it as
s_{j}+x
where
j<r
is as large as possible and
0\leq x<a_{j+1}
. Assume the statement is false so that
x\neq 0
. If
y
and
z
are the averages of
\rho_{i}
over
s_{j}+1\leq i\leq s_{j}+x
and
s_{j}+1\leq i\leq s_{j+1}
respectively, then we have
y>z>0
since
\rho
is decreasing and
s_{j+1}\leq N/2
, and

Q_{s_{j}+x}=\frac{\sigma_{s_{j}}(\Xi)+xd_{j+1}}{\sigma_{s_{j}}(\rho)+xy},\quad Q_{s_{j+1}}=\frac{\sigma_{s_{j}}(\Xi)+a_{j+1}d_{j+1}}{\sigma_{s_{j}}(\rho)+a_{j+1}z}.

Since

\frac{\sigma_{s_{j}}(\Xi)+xd_{j+1}}{\sigma_{s_{j}}(\rho)+xy}=Q_{s_{j}+x}\geq Q_{s_{j}}=\frac{\sigma_{s_{j}}(\Xi)}{\sigma_{s_{j}}(\rho)},

we know that

\alpha\mapsto\frac{\sigma_{s_{j}}(\Xi)+\alpha d_{j+1}}{\sigma_{s_{j}}(\rho)+\alpha y}

is non-decreasing in
\alpha>0
. Combined with
y>z
, this gives

Q_{s_{j+1}}=\frac{\sigma_{s_{j}}(\Xi)+a_{j+1}d_{j+1}}{\sigma_{s_{j}}(\rho)+a_{j+1}z}>\frac{\sigma_{s_{j}}(\Xi)+a_{j+1}d_{j+1}}{\sigma_{s_{j}}(\rho)+a_{j+1}y}\geq\frac{\sigma_{s_{j}}(\Xi)+xd_{j+1}}{\sigma_{s_{j}}(\rho)+xy}=Q_{s_{j}+x}

which is a contradiction.
∎

We have the following simplification of Proposition
6.2.1
for Arthur-type representations:

Corollary 6.2.3
.

Let
\pi
be an Arthur-type irreducible representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
such that

A(\pi)=(d_{1}^{(a_{1})},\dotsc,d_{r}^{(a_{r})})

with
d_{1}>\cdots>d_{r}\geq 1
and
a_{i}>0
. Then

\frac{2}{p(\pi)}=1-\frac{d_{1}-1}{N-a_{1}}.

This follows from Proposition
6.2.1
, and the following combinatorial lemma:

Lemma 6.2.4
.

Let
A
be a sequence of the form
(d_{1}^{(a_{1})},\dotsc,d_{r}^{(a_{r})})
with
d_{1}>\cdots>d_{r}\geq 1
and
a_{i}>0
. Let
\Xi
be the sequence defined as the non-increasing rearrangement of

\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{r}\left[\left(\frac{d_{i}-1}{2}\right)^{(a_{i})},\left(\frac{d_{i}-3}{2}\right)^{(a_{i})},\dotsc,\left(\frac{1-d_{i}}{2}\right)^{(a_{i})}\right],

and let
N=\sum_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}a_{i}
be the length of
\Xi
. Then

\max_{i}\frac{2\sigma_{i}(\Xi)}{i(N-i)}=\frac{d_{1}-1}{N-a_{1}}.

Proof.

It is equivalent to show that the desired maximum occurs at
i=a_{1}
. We prove this by induction on
d_{1}
. The case
d_{1}=1
is trivial, and
d_{1}=2
follows immediately from Lemma
6.2.2
. We may therefore assume that
d_{1}\geq 3
.

We write
2\Xi
as

2\Xi=((d_{1}-1)^{(m_{1})},(d_{1}-2)^{(m_{2})},\dotsc,(1-d_{1})^{(m_{2d_{1}-1})}),

possibly with some
m_{j}=0
, and let
s_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}m_{j}
. We define
A^{\prime}
to be the sequence obtained from
A
by replacing
d_{1}^{(a_{1})}
with
(d_{1}-2)^{(a_{1})}
and taking the non-increasing rearrangement, and let
\Xi^{\prime}
be obtained from
A^{\prime}
in the same way as
\Xi
is from
A
. It follows that

2\Xi^{\prime}=((d_{1}-2)^{(m_{2})},\dotsc,(2-d_{1})^{(m_{2d_{1}-2})}).

We let
N^{\prime}=N-2m_{1}
be the length of
\Xi^{\prime}
, and define
s^{\prime}_{k}=\sum_{j=2}^{k+1}m_{j}
for
k\geq 1
, so that
s^{\prime}_{k}
are associated to
\Xi^{\prime}
in the same way as
s_{k}
are to
\Xi
.

By Lemma
6.2.2
, the maximum is realized at
i=s_{k}
for some
1\leq k\leq d_{1}-1
, and we may assume without loss of generality that
s_{k}>m_{1}
. We wish to show that

\frac{2\sigma_{s_{k}}(\Xi)}{s_{k}(N-s_{k})}\leq\frac{d_{1}-1}{N-a_{1}}.

Breaking
\sigma_{s_{k}}(\Xi)
into its first
m_{1}
and last
s_{k}-m_{1}
terms gives

\displaystyle\frac{2\sigma_{s_{k}}(\Xi)}{s_{k}(N-s_{k})}

\displaystyle=\frac{(d_{1}-1)m_{1}+\sum_{j=2}^{k}(d_{1}-j)m_{j}}{(N-m_{1})m_{1}+(N-s_{k}-m_{1})(s_{k}-m_{1})}

\displaystyle=\frac{(d_{1}-1)m_{1}+2\sigma_{s^{\prime}_{k-1}}(\Xi^{\prime})}{(N-m_{1})m_{1}+(N^{\prime}-s^{\prime}_{k-1})s^{\prime}_{k-1}}.

By the inequality

(6.2.4)

a,c\geq 0,\quad b,d>0,\quad\frac{a}{b}\geq\frac{c}{d}\implies\frac{a}{b}\geq\frac{a+c}{b+d}\geq\frac{c}{d},

it suffices to show that

\frac{2\sigma_{s^{\prime}_{k-1}}(\Xi^{\prime})}{(N^{\prime}-s^{\prime}_{k-1})s^{\prime}_{k-1}}\leq\frac{d_{1}-1}{N-a_{1}}.

If
m_{2}>0
, then the first term of
\Xi^{\prime}
is
d_{1}-2
, and our induction hypothesis gives that

\frac{2\sigma_{s^{\prime}_{k-1}}(\Xi^{\prime})}{(N^{\prime}-s^{\prime}_{k-1})s^{\prime}_{k-1}}\leq\frac{d_{1}-2}{N^{\prime}-m_{2}}=\frac{d_{1}-2}{N-2m_{1}-m_{2}}.

We must therefore show that

\frac{d_{1}-2}{N-2m_{1}-m_{2}}\leq\frac{d_{1}-1}{N-a_{1}}.

This simplifies to

N\geq m_{1}d_{1}+m_{2}(d_{1}-1)=a_{1}d_{1}+a_{2}d_{2},

which follows from the definition of
N
.

On the other hand, if
m_{2}=0
we must have
m_{3}>0
, so in this case our induction hypothesis gives

\frac{2\sigma_{s^{\prime}_{k-1}}(\Xi^{\prime})}{(N^{\prime}-s^{\prime}_{k-1})s^{\prime}_{k-1}}\leq\frac{d_{1}-3}{N^{\prime}-m_{3}}=\frac{d_{1}-2}{N-2m_{1}-m_{3}}.

It therefore suffices to show that

\frac{d_{1}-2}{N-2m_{1}-m_{3}}\leq\frac{d_{1}-1}{N-a_{1}},

and this simplifies to
2N\geq m_{1}(d_{1}+1)+m_{3}(d_{1}-1)
. This again follows from the definition of
N
, and the observation that if
m_{3}>m_{1}
then
m_{3}=m_{1}+a_{2}
and
d_{2}=d_{1}-2
.
∎

6.3.
Uncertainty Principle

In Corollary
5.2.3
and Section
6.2
respectively, we established that the GK-dimension and matrix coefficient decay of unitarizable
\pi
are both computable in terms of the augmented Arthur-
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
. This lets us compare the two invariants by some elementary inequality work.

Definition 6.3.1
.

Choose
N
and let
d_{\max}=\frac{1}{2}N(N-1)
. Define a normalized GK-parameter

g(\pi):=1-d_{GK}(\pi)/d_{\max}

for irreducible representations
\pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
.

The parameter
g(\pi)
ranges between
0
when
\pi
is generic and
1
when
\pi
is a character.

Theorem 6.3.2

(“Uncertainty Principle”)
.

For all Arthur-type irreducible representations
\pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
:

g(\pi)\leq 1-\frac{2}{p(\pi)}\leq g(\pi)^{1/2}.

Proof.

Let
A(\pi)=[d_{1},\dotsc,d_{r}]
with
d_{1}
the largest entry. By Corollary
5.2.3
and some algebra,

g(\pi)=\frac{1}{N(N-1)}\sum_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}(d_{i}-1).

For the lower bound, Proposition
6.2.1
gives

1-\frac{2}{p(\pi)}\geq\frac{d_{1}-1}{N-1},

since
(d_{1}-1)/2
is the largest element of
\Xi(\pi)
. On the other hand,

g(\pi)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{d_{i}}{N}\frac{d_{i}-1}{N-1}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{d_{i}}{N}\frac{d_{1}-1}{N-1}=\frac{d_{1}-1}{N-1}.

For the upper bound, assume there are
m
many
d_{1}
’s in
A(\pi)
. Then

g(\pi)\geq\frac{md_{1}(d_{1}-1)}{N(N-1)}.

Corollary
6.2.3
gives that

1-\frac{2}{p(\pi)}=\frac{d_{1}-1}{N-m},

and the inequality

\left(\frac{d_{1}-1}{N-m}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{md_{1}(d_{1}-1)}{N(N-1)}

always holds under the condition
m\leq N/d_{1}
.
∎

plots
2d_{\max}/p(\pi)
against
d_{GK}(\pi)
for all possible
A(\pi)
when
N=50
together with the bounds from Theorem
6.3.2
. As can be inspected, both bounds (particularly the square-root one) are quite tight.

N=50

Unitarizable representations satisfy a slightly weaker upper bound:

Theorem 6.3.3
.

For all unitarizable irreducible representations
\pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
,

g(\pi)\leq 1-\frac{2}{p(\pi)}\leq g(\pi)^{1/2}+\frac{2}{N}.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.1.3
, a unitarizable representation is of the form

\pi=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r_{1}}\rho_{i}[a_{i}][d_{i}]\oplus\bigoplus_{i=r_{1}+1}^{r_{2}}(|\cdot|^{y_{i}}\rho_{i}[a_{i}][d_{i}]\oplus|\cdot|^{-y_{i}}\rho_{i}[a_{i}][d_{i}])

for
0<y_{i}<1/2
. Define the corresponding Arthur-type representation

\pi^{-}:=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r_{1}}\rho_{i}[a_{i}][d_{i}]\oplus\bigoplus_{i=r_{1}+1}^{r_{2}}(\rho_{i}[a_{i}][d_{i}]\oplus\rho_{i}[a_{i}][d_{i}]).

Corollary
5.2.3
gives
g(\pi)=g(\pi^{-})
.

For the lower bound, the multiset of elements in
\Xi(\pi)
is symmetric around
0
. Therefore, Proposition
6.2.1
can be reduced to

1-\frac{2}{p(\pi)}=\max_{1\leq i\leq N/2}\frac{2\sigma_{i}(\Xi(\pi))}{i(N-i)}\geq\frac{d_{1}-1}{N-a_{1}}\geq 1-\frac{2}{p(\pi^{-})}.

For the upper bound,

1-\frac{2}{p(\pi)}<\max_{1\leq i\leq N/2}\frac{2\sigma_{i}(\Xi(\pi^{-}))+i}{i(N-i)}\leq 1-\frac{2}{p(\pi^{-})}+\frac{2}{N}

into which we substitute the upper bound in Theorem
6.3.2
.
∎

Remark 6.3.4
.

The “worst case” in the upper bound of Theorem
6.3.3
is when all
d_{i}=1
and the
x_{i}
approach the boundary
x_{i}=\pm 1/2
. At this boundary
g(\pi)=0
and
1-2/p(\pi)=2/N
.

Combining the results of this section with Theorem
1.2.1
and Corollary
4.3.1
respectively gives bounds on fixed-vector growth and HCH-coefficients in terms of matrix coefficient decay:

Corollary 6.3.5
.

For all
\epsilon>0
, there is a uniform constant
C_{\epsilon}:=C_{\epsilon,N,F}
such that for any smooth irreducible representation
\pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
with
p(\pi)\geq p_{0}
:

\dim(\pi^{K_{\ell}})\leq C_{\epsilon}q^{\ell\left(N(N-1)\left(1-\left(1-\frac{2}{p_{0}}\right)^{2}\right)+\epsilon\right)}

if
\pi
is Arthur-type and

\dim(\pi^{K_{\ell}})\leq C_{\epsilon}q^{\ell\left(N(N-1)\left(1-\left(1-\frac{2}{p_{0}}-\frac{2}{N}\right)^{2}\right)+\epsilon\right)}

if
\pi
is only unitarizable.

Corollary 6.3.6
.

For all
\epsilon>0
, there is a uniform constant
C_{\epsilon}:=C_{\epsilon,N,F}
such that the Harish-Chandra–Howe coefficients of any smooth irreducible representation
\pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
with
p(\pi)\geq p_{0}

are bounded by

|c_{O}(\pi)|\leq C_{\epsilon}q^{\ell(\pi)\left(N(N-1)\left(1-\left(1-\frac{2}{p_{0}}\right)^{2}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\dim O+\epsilon\right)}

if
\pi
is Arthur-type and

|c_{O}(\pi)|\leq C_{\epsilon}q^{\ell(\pi)\left(N(N-1)\left(1-\left(1-\frac{2}{p_{0}}-\frac{2}{N}\right)^{2}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\dim O+\epsilon\right)}

if
\pi
is only unitarizable.

KnitLoRA: bridging low-rank adaptation as interwoven layers for deeper semantic reasoning
Authors: Hongjie Qiu, Youyou Ning, Jinqiang Li, Xiangrong Zhu, Yifan Gong, Liqi Yan, Fangli Guan, Jianhui Zhang, Fuli Feng, Zenglin Xu, Qifan Wang, Pan Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractLow-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is a parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) method that facilitates the lightweight adaptation of large language models (LLMs) by introducing low-rank update matrices, and has since motivated the development of various extensions. However, LoRA and its most variants overlook the issue of information loss in deep LoRA layers, where signals or gradients passing through multiple LoRA blocks may gradually vanish before reaching the final layers of the network. This limitation hampers convergence speed during fine-tuning. To address these challenges, we propose KnitLoRA, an innovative dense connection low-rank adaptation framework. First, we introduce dense connections between each LoRA block and multiple, or even all preceding blocks to facilitate feature reuse and fusion. Second, these connections improve gradient flow and mitigate the vanishing gradient problem. Third, KnitLoRA accelerates model convergence, further enhancing its efficiency in fine-tuning. Importantly, KnitLoRA eliminates dense connection paths during inference, incurring no extra computational overhead. Our experiments show that KnitLoRA outperforms LoRA and its variants without adding any additional trainable parameters, achieving stronger performance and faster, lower loss reduction. Introduction

Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable performance, but also come with substantial training costs. LLMs focus on broad general knowledge during initial training, but may lack sufficient accuracy in specific domains or tasks. Therefore, they are often fine-tuned for downstream tasks. However, Full Fine-Tuning (FFT) requires enormous resource consumption, leading to the development of Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT)1,2, which reduces computational overhead while improving fine-tuning efficiency. Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)3, as a form of PEFT, aims to minimize the parameters that need to be adjusted, achieving efficient fine-tuning of LLMs by adding two low-rank matrices next to the frozen linear layers of the original model and fine-tuning these two matrices. Building on LoRA, researchers have proposed several variants4,5 to optimize the performance-resource trade-off across different task, expanding the scope of the application of LoRA.

Although LoRA has demonstrated excellent performance in domain adaptation, the gradient must pass sequentially through each LoRA block, which can lead to exponential decay and vanishing gradients in very deep networks. There are two types of LoRA variants. The first category, parameter-efficient LoRA methods (e.g., LoKr6, AdaLoRA5), focus on parameter reduction but neglect gradient propagation issues. The second, structure-efficient LoRA methods (e.g., ResLoRA4), address this via structural adjustments like residual connections between LoRA blocks. However, all of these approaches still face limitations. First, overly sparse connections result in insufficient multi-level feature fusion for deeper domain adaptation. This constraint limits the model’s capacity to capture hierarchical representations essential for effective fine-tuning on complex downstream tasks. Second, connecting multiple entire LoRA blocks increases training overhead and overlooks gradient propagation issues within blocks. This inefficiency can hinder stable optimization during large-scale model fine-tuning, especially when adapting to diverse downstream domains. The lack of multi-level information exchange between LoRA blocks could affect the domain knowledge transferring. Consequently, this localized approach requires more training iterations to capture sufficient global features, and we believe that there is still considerable room for improvement in the convergence speed of many LoRA structures.

To address those issues, we propose KnitLoRA, a dense connectivity–enhanced low-rank adaptation framework that restructures the network for lightweight fine-tuning of LLMs. This methodology establishes dense connections between each LoRA block and multiple preceding blocks for deeper fine-tuning of reasoning. Considering the influence of different connection patterns, we develop three distinct KnitLoRA structures. For the no-plain structure (where additional connections modify the forward computation path), we implement parameter merging strategies that enable seamless conversion to standard LoRA during inference, eliminating computational overhead from auxiliary pathways. Additionally, we construct the HSCoT benchmark by extending HellaSwag8 with Chain-of-Thought annotations generated via DeepSeek-R1-671B9, specifically designed to assess logical reasoning capabilities. Comparative experiments against LoRA and its variants demonstrate that KnitLoRA achieves superior inference performance and accelerated loss convergence. Furthermore, our text-to-image experimental results demonstrate KnitLoRA’s exceptional advantages in image generation, further highlighting the multimodal learning capabilities of our architecture beyond traditional language processing. Figure 1 depicts the architectural framework of KnitLoRA alongside our experimental tasks and findings, collectively demonstrating the efficacy of our proposed approach. Finally, the parameter merging methods ensures compatibility with existing LoRA variants, facilitating adoption across diverse LoRA frameworks.

Related works

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT)

Certain PEFT strategies effectively minimize trainable parameters through the integration of trainable modules or compact layers1. Others selectively optimize specific model parameters while maintaining others in a frozen state10. For example, the FineDiffusion11 method performs fine-grained image generation by fine-tuning only the extended embedding layers, bias terms, and normalization parameters, achieving competitive or superior performance compared to full-parameter fine-tuning while training only 1.77% of the parameters and attaining a 56% speedup. Approaches such as LoRA3, utilize low-rank reparameterization to decrease adaptation complexity, while Prefix Tuning2 achieves adaptation to downstream tasks by introducing trainable prefix vectors. Despite their demonstrated benefits, these approaches face inherent limitations in adaptation efficiency and architectural complexity, motivating subsequent structural innovations to improve optimization effectiveness.

LoRA’s variants

The original LoRA framework3 exhibits performance constraints arising from low rank configurations, frequently necessitating extended training iterations to attain optimal results. Recent studies have investigated adaptive rank determination strategies, with LoKr6 proposing Kronecker-based decomposition for more flexible parameter allocation. Alternative approaches emphasize hybrid architectures: HydraLoRA12 combines Mixture of Experts with LoRA for multi-task learning efficiency, while ResLoRA4 incorporates residual connectivity mechanisms into LoRA modules to accelerate convergence dynamics. Despite these advancements in parameter optimization and architectural integration, existing LoRA variants systematically neglect critical challenges in deep adaptation stacks–particularly the degradation of cross-block information flow and limited feature reutilization across sequential LoRA layers. To address these limitations, we propose integrating dense connectivity patterns from DenseNet13 into the LoRA framework, enabling comprehensive feature interaction while preserving parameter efficiency.

Densely connected neural network

Research in related domains has explored similar challenges, particularly in image feature extraction. Notably, DenseNet13, as a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), improves gradient propagation and information flow through dense inter-layer connectivity, alleviating vanishing gradients and enabling efficient feature reuse. It achieves higher accuracy than ResNet with fewer parameters. Owing to its compact and effective design, DenseNet has been extended to diverse applications: MDU-Net14 enhances biomedical segmentation, DCCT15 integrates dense connections into Transformer architectures for image dehazing, and16 introduces a tensor network-based DenseNet with a tensor regression layer to reduce memory consumption while maintaining high classification accuracy. Furthermore, DenseAttNet17 incorporates a self-attention mechanism into a 3D DenseNet for multi-label emotion recognition, demonstrating its potential for high-dimensional spatiotemporal modeling. However, these methods require fully training dense feature extraction architectures in both the pre-training and fine-tuning stages, exhibiting limited flexibility for structural adjustment during LLM fine-tuning. In contrast, KnitLoRA restructures the network to enable lightweight adaptation of LLMs across diverse downstream tasks.

Methodology

This section details implementation of KnitLoRA framework, which comprises two phases: During the training phase (section 3.2), we construct KnitLoRA blocks through three distinct dense connectivity structures, each designed to optimize specific adaptation characteristics. In the inference phase (section 3.3), we implement parameter merging techniques to eliminate dense connectivity paths in the no-plain structure variant, thereby preserving computational efficiency while maintaining performance gains achieved during training.

Input-based dense connection structure implements dense connectivity by integrating input signals from all preceding LoRA blocks into the current block’s input processing. HSCoT benchmark construction

In GenKP20, structured triples from a knowledge graph are generated via in‑context learning, then weighted, filtered, and injected into a large language model; experimental results demonstrate that this approach yields higher accuracy across multiple NLP tasks compared to traditional template‑based or pure‑text prompting. ReLMKG21, by contrast, utilizes a graph neural network to perform multi‑hop explicit reasoning over the knowledge graph, tightly integrating textual representations with graph structure, and likewise significantly outperforms methods relying solely on text retrieval. Together, these studies demonstrate that embedding explicit structured reasoning within large language models far surpasses the “example–fill‑in‑the‑blank” style of pure‑text prompting, and have catalyzed the development of Chain‑of‑Thought (CoT) reasoning techniques–whereby a step‑by‑step reasoning chain is articulated within the prompt to furnish the model with fine‑grained thought pathways, thereby further enhancing performance on complex reasoning tasks.

Given that KnitLoRA demonstrated significant advantages on the HellaSwag dataset, we sought to further evaluate its reasoning capabilities by introducing HSCoT benchmark. HSCoT consists of 1.5K samples, among which 1 K training samples are taken from the HellaSwag training set and augmented with CoT annotations, as depicted in Figure 3. The CoT annotations were initially generated by DeepSeek-R1-671B9 through sample-specific prompts and subsequently validated by annotators, the prompt is as shown in Figure 4. The 500 test samples are directly retained from HellaSwag’s evaluation set. By measuring model performance on HSCoT after fine-tuning, we quantitatively assess CoT reasoning acquisition capabilities. As shown in Table 1, only KnitLoRA achieved meaningful improvements over baseline methods, with statistically significant margins confirming the architectural advantages in capturing structured reasoning patterns.

Merge approach

While our parameter merging strategy offers computational advantages during inference, it remains critical to evaluate its impact on model accuracy. As demonstrated in Table 7, direct deployment of unmerged post-training weights fails to generate valid outputs due to structural inconsistencies introduced by dense connectivity paths, which create inconsistencies between training and inference phases. This confirms the necessity of the merging procedure. Between the two proposed methods, DP-Merge achieves superior accuracy compared to DI-Merge, even outperforming unmerged inference with dense paths by 0.34%, demonstrating that proper merging preserves or enhances model performance. DI-Merge estimates the scaling factor by computing the ratio between the Frobenius norm of the input tensor and the averaged Frobenius norm of the summed inputs from preceding layers. This approach implicitly assumes a stable linear proportionality between them. However, when the magnitude of the input tensor varies substantially, the averaged norm may fail to accurately capture the dynamic fluctuations of the input, thereby introducing approximation errors. For example, in long-tailed distribution scenarios, the input magnitudes of low-frequency samples (e.g., rare medical terms, archaic legal jargon, rare dialects, or specific technical nomenclature) differ significantly from those of high-frequency samples. Consequently, the computed by DI-Merge based on a global average norm cannot precisely adapt to the features of these low-frequency samples. In contrast, DP-Merge computes the scaling factor based on weight norms, which are independent of input fluctuations, resulting in more stable performance on long-tailed samples compared to DI-Merge. These quantitative analyses validate the effectiveness of our merging strategies in maintaining accuracy while eliminating inference overhead.

Impact of different KnitLoRA structures on efficiency

Beyond the raw efficiency metrics reported in Table 9, the observed differences among KnitLoRA structures can be further explained by their architectural characteristics. KnitLoRA introduces dense connections only at the input aggregation stage, which requires minimal additional tensor operations and does not increase the number of trainable parameters. Consequently, its GPU memory consumption and throughput remain close to those of standard LoRA. This design provides an effective trade-off between computational overhead and representation enhancement, making KnitLoRA particularly suitable for large-scale training scenarios where efficiency is a critical constraint. In contrast, KnitLoRA and KnitLoRA perform dense aggregation directly on the low-rank matrices across multiple LoRA blocks. While this design slightly increases intermediate tensor operations and memory usage during training, it substantially improves cross-layer feature interaction and parameter expressiveness. In particular, the B-Dense structure enables the final projection matrix to integrate information from multiple historical LoRA blocks, effectively increasing the expressive capacity of the low-rank adaptation without increasing the rank. This explains why KnitLoRA and KnitLoRA often achieve higher accuracy and faster convergence in complex reasoning tasks despite their higher training cost. More importantly, all KnitLoRA variants maintain the core efficiency advantage of LoRA during inference. Through the proposed parameter merging strategies described in Section 3.3, the dense connections introduced during training can be eliminated and converted into standard LoRA weight updates. As a result, KnitLoRA incurs no additional inference latency or memory overhead compared with the original LoRA during inference. Therefore, KnitLoRA achieves a desirable balance: richer cross-layer feature utilization during training and identical deployment efficiency during inference.

Conclusion and future work

In this study, our work reveals the limitations of the traditional LoRA method using a single-path structure and highlights the advantages brought by dense connections. To this end, we propose a novel low-rank adaptation framework–KnitLoRA, consisting of three structures that introduce dense connections during training and remove these paths during inference through merging strategies. We also propose HSCoT, a 1.5K-sample benchmark drawn from HellaSwag and enhanced with Chain-of-Thought annotations, which can effectively evaluate a model’s ability to perform multi-step reasoning under the CoT paradigm. We conducted experiments on various tasks and models, which confirmed the effectiveness of our approach. Meanwhile, our architecture also demonstrated powerful multimodal learning capabilities in experiments. The advantages of our method can be summarized as follows: KnitLoRA offers significantly enhanced semantic reasoning capabilities for models with fewer training steps and no additional trainable parameters compared to baseline methods. Future work will focus on improving the robustness of the merging strategy and optimizing the training efficiency of dense connectivity, particularly under long-tailed data distributions where rare samples and highly variable input magnitudes may amplify approximation errors during merging and increase optimization instability.

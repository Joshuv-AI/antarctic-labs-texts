Curved paths: A comprehensive review and case insights into root canal anatomy
Authors: Hetvi Parikh, Rajendra Bharatiya, Urvashi Ujariya, Anjali Kothari
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Endodontic therapy conserves the natural tooth by treating diseased pulp and promoting periapical healing. Curved root canals pose challenges in transportation, ledges, perforations, and instrument separation, impacting treatment results. Effective assessment and management are essential. Methodologies such as Schneider’s and Weine’s techniques, along with cone-beam computed tomography, refine the accuracy of curvature measurements. Proper techniques, involving anti-curvature filling and tactile controlled activation, are pivotal for successful treatment. Understanding root canal curvature and employing applicable approaches are vital for minimizing the complications and refining treatment success. An acclimatized approach grounded on curvature characteristics enhances endodontic sequels. This article reviews the cases involving patients with curved root canals, emphasizing treatment avenues and outcomes.

INTRODUCTION

Root canal instrumentation is one of the most essential therapeutic procedures in endodontic treatment as it determines the efficacy of all subsequent procedures, it not only provides mechanical debridement, but also helps in the creation of space for medicament delivery and optimized canal geometries for adequate obturation.[1]

Constricted canals and curvatures can augment the threat of procedural breaches during root canal preparation such as transportation, ledges (58.4%), perforations (7.8%), instrument separation, asymmetrical dentine removal, and alteration in internal anatomy.[2]

This paper presents the case studies of patients with curved root canals, outlining the treatment process and outcomes achieved.

CASE REPORTS

Case-1 and case-2

A 65-year-old male patient reported to the department of conservative dentistry and endodontics, with a chief complaint of pain concerning the lower right and left back tooth region for 1 month. Clinical examination revealed deep proximal caries in respect to (i.r.t) 37 and 47. The teeth were tender on percussion. Medical history was noncontributory. Teeth gave a negative response on the pulp sensibility test. The radiographic examination revealed deep caries approximating pulp with signs of periapical pathosis. A diagnosis of symptomatic apical periodontitis was established i.r.t 37 and 47 and the endodontic treatment was planned. Both teeth showed curved (Bayonet/S-shaped) roots [Figure 1a and d]. A cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) was advised to gain detailed information on curvatures [Figure 1b and e].

(a) Preoperative radiographic image of Case 1, (b) Sagittal section of cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT), (c) Postoperative radiographic image, (d) Preoperative radiographic image of Case 2, (e) Sagittal section of CBCT, (f) Postoperative radiographic image, (g) Preoperative radiographic image Case 3, (h) Schneider’s angle 47°, (i) Postoperative radiographic image

With informed consent, local anesthesia (2% lignocaine and 1:200,000 adrenaline) was administered, and endodontic therapy was initiated under rubber dam isolation. Access cavity was prepared in both teeth using endo access bur # 2 (Dentsply Maillefer). The initial instrumentation was done using no. 6 and 8 stainless steel K–files (Mani, Inc., Japan). The working length was determined by an electronic apex locator and confirmed by a radiograph. The glide path was established up to the radiographic working length. Coronal flaring of the canals was done with an orifice opener of Hyflex CM rotary files (Coltene–Whaledent, Switzerland). For verifying the patency of the root canals, no. 10 stainless steel K–files (Mani, Inc., Japan) were used. Calcium hydroxide was used as an intracanal medicament for 2 weeks.

Irrigation was done with 5.25% sodium hypochlorite after the usage of each file. Cleaning and shaping were carried out using the tactile controlled activation (TCA) technique with Hyflex CM rotary files (Coltene–Whaledent, Switzerland) up to 4% of 20 size of the instrument, maintaining the bayonet shape of the curvature.

In the second visit, the canals were irrigated with 5.25% sodium hypochlorite followed by 17% ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid and finally with saline and dried with paper points. A master cone radiograph was taken and obturation was done with 20 size 4% gutta-percha along with Bioceramic Sealer (Safe Endo) where the bayonet shape of the curvature was maintained. The access cavity was restored with composite (Ivoclar Vivadent-Tetric N Ceram) [Figure 1c and f].

Case-3

A 27-year-old female patient reported to the department of conservative dentistry and endodontics with complaints of pain for 1 month and swelling for 2 days in the right lower back tooth region. Oral examination revealed deep proximal caries and swelling i.r.t 46. The tooth was sensitive to palpation and tender on percussion. Radiographic examination showed radiolucency extending to the pulp with signs of periapical pathosis, radiograph also showed the presence of root curvature [Figure 1g]. A diagnosis of symptomatic apical periodontitis i.r.t 46 was made. Root canal treatment was planned i.r.t 46.

The curvature was measured with Schneider’s method showing 36° in mesiobuccal canal and 47° distal canal.

The treatment protocol followed was the same as in case one mentioned above [Figure 1h]. Cleaning and shaping were carried out using the TCA technique with Trunatomy rotary files (Dentsply Maillefer) up to 4% 26 size of the instrument maintaining the curvature.

In the second visit, obturation was done with 26 size 4% gutta-percha and Bioceramic Sealer (Safe Endo) where the curvature was maintained. The access cavity was restored with composite resin (Ivoclar vivadent-Tetric N Ceram) [Figure 1i].

DISCUSSION

The maximum number of teeth show some kind of curvature and multiple planes of deflection along the root canal length.[2] Tomes defined the term root canal curvatures as dilaceration if the direction of the curved root is 90° or more to the long axis of the tooth.[3] Constricted and obliterated canals, slender and lengthy roots, curved canals of more than 27°, and S-shaped (multicurve) canals with smaller than 5 mm radius of curvature are regarded challenging for all instrumentation systems available currently.[4]

CONCLUSION

A thorough understanding of tooth and root canal morphology during preoperative assessment is highly important in curved canals, i.e. angle of curvature, radius, length, height, and distance of curvature. Proper exploring of the root canals and making preparations with the proper sequence of techniques (TCA, anticurvature filling) are very necessary to prevent procedural errors. A customized treatment plan based on the degree of curvature will assist manage curved canals, reduce complexities, and upgrade the quality of the treatment.

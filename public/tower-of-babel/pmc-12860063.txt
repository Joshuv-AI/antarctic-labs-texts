Comparison of the efficacy between radiotherapy alone and surgery plus radiotherapy for ipsilateral supraclavicular lymph node metastasis in breast cancer based on propensity score matching analysis: a retrospective study with 8-year follow-up
Authors: Chao Wei, Shuhua Zhou, Longyu Zhu, Jie Kong, Zimeng Gao, Danyang Wang, Deyou Kong, Andu Zhang, Shuo Pan, Kaiye Du, Dongxing Shen, Jun Zhang, Zhikun Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo evaluate the efficacy of supraclavicular involved lymph node dissection combined with radiotherapy compared to radiotherapy alone in breast cancer patients with synchronous ipsilateral supraclavicular lymph node metastasis (sISLM).MethodThe clinical data of 326 breast cancer patients with sISLM admitted to the Fourth Hospital of Hebei Medical University from January 2010 to December 2021 were retrospectively analyzed. Patients were divided into two groups based on different local treatment patterns for sISLM: 88 cases (27.0%) in supraclavicular surgery plus radiotherapy group and 238 cases (73.0%) in radiotherapy alone group. 1:2 propensity score matching (PSM) was used to balance intergroup differences, resulting in 83 patients in the surgery plus RT group and 162 patients in the radiotherapy alone group. The Kaplan-Meier method and multivariate Cox proportional hazards model were employed to calculate the Localregion relapse-free survival (LRFS), supraclavicular recurrence-free survival (SCRFS), and overall survival (OS) for both groups.ResultThe median follow-up duration in the study was 67 months (Q1: 44, Q3: 94). No statistically significant differences were observed between the surgery combined with radiotherapy group and the radiotherapy alone group in terms of age, menstrual status, tumor location, hormone receptor status, molecular subtype, and clinical stage.Univariate survival analysis indicated that, the 5-year and 8-year survival for the surgery plus radiotherapy group and the radiotherapy alone group were 84.5%, 78.0%, 88.3% and 86.0%, respectively (P = 0.191). The SCRFS were 88.2%, 83.7%, 94.7% and 92.3%, respectively (P = 0.062). The OS were 67.0%, 58.8%, 75.1% and 63.9%, respectively (P = 0.564). Multivariate survival analysis revealed that Ki67＞30% were independent prognostic factors for poor LRFS(P = 0.032) and SCRFS(P = 0.026). Treatment period from 2010 to 2016, Hormone receptor positive/endocrine therapy negative(HR+/ET-), HR- and the ratio of involved axillary lymph node (ALNR) were independent prognostic factors for poor OS(all P ＜0.05). Subgroup analysis revealed that, irrespective of whether the supraclavicular lymph nodes achieved complete remission (CR) following neoadjuvant chemotherapy, there was no significant difference in prognosis between the two groups (P >0.05). Among patients who received radiotherapy alone, those treated with 50-60Gy to the supraclavicular region exhibited superior 5-year and 8-year LRFS and SCRFS compared to those >60Gy (P = 0.049, P = 0.034, respectively). Notably, in patients with non-CR supraclavicular involved lymph nodes, the 5-year and 8-year LRFS was significantly better in the 50-60 Gy group than >60 Gy (P = 0.015).ConclusionBoth radiotherapy alone and supraclavicular surgery combined with radiotherapy were considered local treatment strategies for patients with sISLM breast cancer involving supraclavicular lymph nodes. Within the framework of contemporary treatment, the exploration of lower-dose radiation for supraclavicular lymph nodes will proceed, irrespective of whether CR was achieved following neoadjuvant chemotherapy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-025-15424-x.Keywords: Breast cancer, Ipsilateral supraclavicular lymph node metastasis, Supraclavicular involed lymph node dissection, Radiotherapy, Prognosis

Introduction

Breast cancer had the highest incidence among malignant tumors in women in 2022, accounting for 11.6% of new cancer cases worldwide, and ranked fourth among the causes of death from malignant tumors according to global cancer statistics [1]. Breast cancer with ipsilateral supraclavicular lymph node metastasis (ISLM) without distant metastasis is classified as locally advanced breast cancer which incidence is relatively low, accounting for approximately 1% to 4% of breast cancer cases [2]. The occurrence of ISLM generally indicates a poor prognosis, with most ISLM breast cancer patients experiencing distant metastasis within one year [3, 4]. ISLM can be categorized based on its timing into synchronous and metachronous types. Synchronous ipsilateral supraclavicular lymph node metastasis (sISLM) refers to the presence of ISLM at the initial diagnosis of breast cancer, while metachronous ipsilateral supraclavicular lymph node metastasis (mISLM) occurs after definitive treatment for breast cancer. The staging of ISLM breast cancer has been downgraded from advanced (M1) to locally advanced (N3c). Several studies have indicated that the prognosis of ISLM breast cancer patients receiving aggressive comprehensive treatment is comparable to that of patients with stage IIIB breast cancer, and significantly superior to that of patients with established distant organ or tissue metastases [5–7].

ISLM breast cancer has been considered a potentially curable form of locally advanced breast cancer through comprehensive systemic treatments, including chemotherapy, surgery, radiotherapy, endocrine therapy, and targeted therapy. In recent years, The prognosis of ISLM breast cancer has been improved accouting for advancements in systemic treatments. However, controversy over the local management of supraclavicular lymph node metastases on the basis of these systemic treatments still remains. Some investigators have found that radiotherapy to the supraclavicular region can provide survival benefits. Therefore, exploring the optimal radiation dose to the supraclavicular region will be beneficial for individualized clinical decision-making. Given the complex anatomical location of the supraclavicular region, surgical dissection is challenging and accouting for the risk of brachial plexus injury and lymphedema. Consequently, the necessity of supraclavicular lymph node dissection for ISLM breast cancer patients is still debated. The efficacy of supraclavicular lymph node dissection combined with radiotherapy versus radiotherapy alone in patients with sISLM breast cancer were retrospectively analyzed in our study, to explore the optimal local treatment strategy for sISLM breast cancer and to provide clinical reference for selecting local treatment modalities for these patients.

Materials and methods

Eligibility and exclusion criteria

The study was designed in accordance with the requirements of medical ethics and adhered to the principles of the Helsinki Declaration. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Fourth Hospital of Hebei Medical University. The inclusion criteria were: (1) Female patients; (2) unilateral breast cancer; (3) Pathological or imaging diagnosis at initial presentation indicating ipsilateral supraclavicular lymph node metastasis. Pathological diagnosis included fine needle aspiration or surgical biopsy. Imaging diagnosis included ultrasound, computed tomography (CT), or positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET/CT), with supraclavicular lymph node imaging findings meeting the following criteria: short axis > 1 cm, absence of a hilum, hypervascularity, indistinct margins, cortical thickening, necrosis within the lymph node, or high metabolic uptake on PET/CT; 5) Complete comprehensive treatment including chemotherapy, surgery, radiotherapy, etc.; 6) Absence of severe cardiopulmonary comorbidities.; The exclusion criteria were: (1) Male breast cancer; (2) Bilateral breast cancer; (3) Non-epithelial malignancies of the breast, such as sarcoma or lymphoma; (4) Patients who did not receive surgery or adjuvant radiotherapy for the primary tumor; (5) History of other malignant diseases; (6) Missing follow-up data. Clinical data of 326 breast cancer patients with sISLM at the Fourth Hospital of Hebei Medical University between January 2010 to December 2021 were analyzed. The patients were divided into two groups based on different local treatment modalities for supraclavicular involved lymph nodes: the radiotherapy alone group with 238 cases, and the surgery plus radiotherapy group with 88 cases.

Treatment

Systemic therapy

The chemotherapy regimens included AC-T (doxorubicin 90–100 mg/m² plus cyclophosphamide 600 mg/m² d1 Q21d, followed by docetaxel 80–100 mg/m² d1 Q21d), TCbHP (docetaxel 75 mg/m² plus carboplatin AUC = 6 plus trastuzumab initial dose 8 mg/kg, followed by 6 mg/kg plus pertuzumab initial dose 840 mg, followed by 420 mg d1 Q21d), weekly T (paclitaxel 80 mg/m² d1 Q7d), AT (doxorubicin 50 mg/m² plus docetaxel 75 mg/m² d1 Q21d), TAC (docetaxel 75 mg/m² plus doxorubicin 50 mg/m² plus cyclophosphamide 500 mg/m² d1 Q21d), FEC-T (fluorouracil 500 mg/m² plus epirubicin 100 mg/m² plus cyclophosphamide 500 mg/m² d1 Q21d, followed by docetaxel 80–100 mg/m² d1 Q21d), and TC (docetaxel 75 mg/m² plus cyclophosphamide 600 mg/m² d1 Q21d). Tamoxifen, toremifene, letrozole, anastrozole, and exemestane were included into ET. Additionally, ovarian function suppression(OFS) with goserelin or leuprorelin was considered for premenopausal HR-positive patients based on their menopausal status. The trastuzumab and pertuzumab were administered included into TT. In the surgery-combined radiotherapy group, all patients underwent supraclavicular lymph node dissection; therefore, pathological lymph node negativity was defined as complete response (CR). For patients in the radiotherapy-only group, a comprehensive evaluation was required by integrating imaging diagnoses with clinical palpation, including specifically ultrasound, computed tomography (CT) scan, and positron emission tomography-computed tomography (PET-CT) scan. CR is defined as no supraclavicular lymph nodes were identified on imaging examinations and palpable during clinical examination.

Surgery therapy

Surgical procedures included mastectomy and breast-conserving surgery. All patients underwent axillary lymph node dissection (ALND), which was classified into level I and level II; some patients underwent dissection up to level III. The necessity of supraclavicular lymph node dissection (SCLND) requires multidisciplinary treatment (MDT) involving surgeons, medical oncologists, pathologists, and radiologists. Generally, MDT determines that patients require SCLND if they meet the following criteria: heavy tumor burden in supraclavicular lymph nodes, chemotherapy-resistant metastatic supraclavicular lymph nodes, or palpable and significantly enlarged supraclavicular lymph nodes with compressive symptoms on physical examination. The surgical extent of SCLND typically covers a triangular region, with the inferior boundary defined as the superior border of the clavicle, the medial boundary as the medial border of the internal jugular vein, and the lateral-superior boundary as the inferior belly of the omohyoid muscle.

Radiotherapy

All patients received postoperative adjuvant radiotherapy, utilizing three-dimensional conformal intensity-modulated radiation therapy (IMRT). The primary radiation fields included the chest wall or whole breast (with a prophylactic dose of 50 Gy) and the supraclavicular and infraclavicular lymphatic drainage regions (with a prophylactic dose of 50 Gy). The contouring of the target area for the supraclavicular and infraclavicular lymphatic drainage regions encompassed the supraclavicular area, levels II and III of the axilla, and the posterior cervical triangle. For patients with residual enlarged lymph nodes in the supraclavicular area, an additional localized dose of 10–16 Gy was administered. The dose constraints for organs at risk refer to Table S1.

Study endpoints

LRFS is calculated at the time of relapse in the ipsilateral breast or chest wall, ipsilateral axilla, ipsilateral supraclavicular or infraclavicular region, or ipsilateral internal mammary region, or the time of the last follow-up or death. SCRFS is calculated at the time of recurrence in the ipsilateral supraclavicular region, or to the time of the last follow-up or death. OS is calculated at the time of death from any cause or the last follow-up.

Statistical analysis

SPSS 25.0 and R 4.2.2 software were performed for Statistical analyses. The Chi-square test and Fisher’s exact test were employed to compare baseline characteristics between the two groups. PSM was used to balance baseline characteristics. Univariate survival analysis was conducted using the Kaplan-Meier method, and differences between groups were assessed using the Log-Rank test. Multivariate survival analysis was conducted using the Cox proportional hazards model which analyzes the local treatment modalities for supraclavicular involed lymph nodes and variables from the univariate analysis with p-values less than 0.1. A p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Result

Baseline characteristics of the surgery plus radiotherapy and radiotherapy alone groups before and after PSM

The follow-up period extended until October 31, 2023, with a median follow-up duration of 67 months (Q1: 44, Q3: 94), and a follow-up rate of 100%. Among the entire cohort, 255 patients had pathologically confirmed supraclavicular involved lymph node, while 71 patients had only imaging-supported supraclavicular involved lymph node due to the absence of supraclavicular involed lymph node aspiration or biopsy at initial diagnosis. The status of the supraclavicular involed lymph nodes was assessed following NAC: 104 patients achieved CR, 192 patients had a non-complete remission (non-CR), and the status of 7 patients remained unknown. The overall cohort experienced 24 cases (7.4%) of supraclavicular recurrence, 39 cases (12.0%) of local-regional recurrence, 120 cases (36.8%) of distant metastasis, 34 cases (10.4%) of concurrent local-regional recurrence and distant metastasis, and 106 deaths (32.5%). In the radiotherapy alone group, there were 12 cases (5.0%) of supraclavicular recurrence, 23 cases (9.7%) of local-regional recurrence, 82 cases (34.5%) of distant metastasis, 20 cases (8.4%) of concurrent local-regional recurrence and distant metastasis, and 66 deaths (27.7%). In the surgery plus radiotherapy group, there were 12 cases (13.6%) of supraclavicular recurrence, 16 cases (18.2%) of local-regional recurrence, 38 cases (43.2%) of distant metastasis, 14 cases (15.9%) of concurrent local-regional recurrence and distant metastasis, and 40 deaths (45.5%). 1:2 PSM was conducted between the surgery plus radiotherapy group and the radiotherapy alone group with a caliper value of 0.2 to reduce bias acounting for 83 patients in the surgery plus radiotherapy group and 162 patients in the radiotherapy alone group. Clinicopathological factors affecting LRFS in patients

The 5-year and 8-year LRFS for the Surgery plus radiotherapy and the radiotherapy alone group were 84.5%, 78.0%, 88.3% and 86.0%, respectively (P = 0.191). The treatment era, HR/ET, and Ki−67 showed significant differences in LRFS in univariate analysis (P < 0.05). Further multivariate analysis revealed that Ki67>30% were independent prognostic factors for poor LRFS(HR: 2.316, 95%CI: 1.076–4.986, P = 0.032). See Table S2 and Fig. 1-A for details.

Clinical characteristics with a significant effect on SCRFS of patients

The 5-year and 8-year SCRFS were 88.2%, 83.7%, 94.7% and 92.3% between the surgery plus radiotherapy and the radiotherapy alone group, respectively (P = 0.062). The treatment era and Ki−67 showed significant differences in SCRFS in univariate analysis (P = 0.030, P = 0.018, respectively). The authors found that Ki67>30% were independent prognostic factors for poor SCRFS by multivariate analysis (HR: 3.476, 95%CI: 1.162–10.404, P = 0.026). See Table S3 and Fig. 1-B for details.

Analysis results of clinical features affecting OS

The 5-year and 8-year OS were 67.0%, 58.8%, 75.1% and 63.9% between the two groups, respectively (P = 0.564). The resuilts observed by authors showed clinical T stage, LVI, ALN, and ALNR were significant differences in OS in univariate analysis(P < 0.05). Further multivariate analysis revealed that The treatment era (HR: 0.633, 95%CI: 0.406–0.988, P = 0.044), HR/ET (P = 0.001, P = 0.018, respectively), and ALNR (HR: 2.004, 95%CI: 1.170–3.433, P = 0.011) were independent prognostic factors for poor OS. The authors observed patients with HR-positive status who did not receive endocrine therapy and HR-negative had 2.7 and 1.8-fold increase in mortality risk compared to those with HR-positive status who received endocrine therapy, respectively. Toxicity

In the present study, all patients exhibited favorable tolerance to acute and long-term radiotherapy-related toxicities. In the radiotherapy-alone group, 211 (88.7%) patients developed RTOG grade 1–2 radiation dermatitis, including 186 (78.2%) cases of grade 1 and 25 (10.5%) cases of grade 2; grade 3 radiation dermatitis occurred in 27 (11.3%) patients. In the surgery plus radiotherapy group, 79 (89.7%) patients experienced RTOG grade 1–2 radiation dermatitis, with 75 (85.2%) cases of grade 1 and 4 (4.5%) cases of grade 2; grade 3 radiation dermatitis was observed in 9 (10.3%) patients. The incidence of radiation dermatitis was comparable between the two groups (P > 0.05), and no RTOG grade 4 radiation dermatitis was detected in either group. Regarding myelosuppression, 173 (72.7%) patients in the radiotherapy-alone group developed grade I-II myelosuppression, consisting of 105 (44.1%) cases of grade I and 68 (28.6%) cases of grade II; grade III myelosuppression occurred in 6 (2.5%) patients. In the surgery plus radiotherapy group, 71 (80.7%) patients had grade I-II myelosuppression, including 47 (53.4%) cases of grade I and 24 (27.3%) cases of grade II; grade III myelosuppression was noted in 6 (6.8%) patients. The incidence of myelosuppression was similar between the two groups (P > 0.05), and no grade IV myelosuppression was observed. No patients discontinued radiotherapy due to severe radiotherapy-related adverse events. Furthermore, no significant long-term toxicities were identified in this study, including radiation-induced cardiovascular events, radiation pneumonitis, pulmonary fibrosis, skin fibrosis, and brachial plexus injury.

Discussion

sISLM breast cancer was considered a locally advanced breast cancer with relatively poor prognosis [8, 9]. Previous research showned that the 5-year LRFS ranges from 73.1% to 79.2%, SCRFS from 83% to 85.5%, and OS from 46.2% to 67.9% for surgery plus radiotherapy. The 5-year LRFS ranges from 79.1% to 88.9%, SCRFS from 91.7% to 95.0%, and OS from 37.5% to 80.2% for radiotherapy alone [10–16]. In our study, the 5-year LRFS, SCRFS and OS were 84.2%, 87.7%, and 64.1%, respectively for the surgery plus radiotherapy group. The 5-year LRFS, SCRFS and OS were 90.0%, 95.1% and 77.3% respectively for the radiotherapy alone group. These results were consistent with previous findings and indicate that the 5-year prognosis for both groups was essentially similar, regardless of whether sISLM were treated with surgery plus radiotherapy or with radiotherapy alone. Reviewed of relevant literature, few studies were reported follow-up results beyond 5 years for these patients. The 8-year follow-up results reported by authors indicateed that, the 8-year LRFS, SCRFS and OS were 77.9%, 83.3%, and 56.6% respectively for the surgery plus radiotherapy group. While those were 88.5%, 93.6% and 69.3% respectively for the radiotherapy alone group. We achieved longer follow-up results, providing more comprehensive data for clinical reference.

The National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines recommend multimodal therapy for patients with sISLM breast cancer [17]. Most studies suggested that radiotherapy to the supraclavicular region may offer a survival benefit [18–22]. Nevertheless, the efficacy of supraclavicular lymph node dissection remains a subject of significant debate [10, 12–14, 23–29]. Jung [29] found that the 5-year OS was superior in the 73 patients who received surgery plus radiotherapy compared to the 38 patients who received radiotherapy alone (70.9% vs. 49.3%, P = 0.036). However, the heterogeneity between the two groups in above study was significant, which greatly interfered with the results. Our hospital conducted a retrospective analysis of 128 sISLM breast cancer in previous research. 60 patients received radiotherapy alone, while 68 patients underwent surgery plus radiotherapy. There was no significant difference between the two groups in terms of 5-year LRFS, DMFS, PFS, and OS (P >0.05) [14]. Song [10] conducted a multicenter retrospective study including 160 patients of our hospital, which analyzed 293 sISLM breast cancer patients, with 85 patients received surgery plus radiotherapy and 208 patients received radiotherapy alone. There were no significant differences between the two groups in 5-year SCRFS, LRFS, DMFS, DFS, and OS (P >0.05). However, some baseline characteristic biases were observed between the two groups, which may have affected the accuracy of the results to some extent. We conducted 1:2 PSM to reduce the basis observed in our research between the two groups, which resulted in 83 patients in the surgery plus radiotherapy group and 162 patients in the radiotherapy alone group. The analysis revealed no significant differences between the two groups in 5-year and 8-year LRFS, SCRFS, OS. Therefore, radiotherapy alone may be a available option for treating sISLM. Although the superiority of any local treatment strategy did not be conclusively determined by our study. Potential biases arising from the retrospective nature of the data were minimized by the the authors as much as possible. Our reasearch was one of the larger-scale analyses and employing multiple analytical methods comparing local treatment strategies for sISLM, has not yet identified a clear advantage between surgery plus radiotherapy and radiotherapy alone. Therefore, individualized treatment neededs to be considered essentially in clinical practice and further large-scale prospective randomized studies are needed to clarify these findings.

There remains controversy regarding the optimal radiation dose for the supraclavicular region in patients with sISLM breast cancer. Generally, non-CR indicates that tumor cells are not sensitive to treatment. Therefore, higher radiation doses were often administered to patients with sISLM following non-CR to improve local control rates(LCR). Diao [11] found that a radiation dose of ≥ 60 Gy improved OS compare to dose < 60 Gy, although it did not enhance SCRFS or LRFS; Li [14] showed that the 5-year OS in patients receiving >50 Gy radiation to the supraclavicular area during radiotherapy alone was better than in the 50 Gy group (P = 0.047). Wang [27] demonstrated that administering a 66 Gy dose to the supraclavicular invoved lymph nodes in non-CR patients resulted in improving LCR and DFS. In our study, when analyzing the radiotherapy alone group, For patients of sISLM with non-CR, the 5-year LRFS in the 50–60 Gy group was superior to the >60 Gy group (P = 0.015). Escalating the radiotherapy dose to the supraclavicular region did not markedly improve prognosis in non-CR patients. This phenomenon may be attributed to the inherent resistance to radiotherapy in non-CR patients with sISLM, the advanced tumor stage, high tumor burden and the progressive intensification of systemic therapeutic interventions.

With the ongoing advancements in systemic therapies, the potential for reducing radiotherapy doses to the supraclavicular region in these patients have garnered considerable interest among researchers. In our reasearch, when analyzing the radiotherapy alone group, for the entire patients, the 50–60 Gy group had better 5-year and 8-year LRFS and SCRFS than the >60 Gy group (P = 0.049, P = 0.034, respectively). For patients with non-CR of sISLM, the 5-year LRFS in the 50–60 Gy group was superior to the >60 Gy group (P = 0.015). Some researchers have achieved similar conclusions that high-dose radiotherapy did not improve prognosis. Jung [29] indicated that there was no significant difference in 5-year LRFS whether the supraclavicular region was irradiated with ≥ 50.4 Gy or < 50.4 Gy (P = 0.427). Park [21] suggested that there were no significant differences in 5-year SCRFS and DFS between doses of ≥ 55.8 Gy and ≤ 50.4 Gy to the supraclavicular region (P = 0.91, P = 0.95, respectively). Huang [30] showed no significant differences in 5-year LRFS and DFS between doses of ≥ 60 Gy and < 60 Gy (P >0.05). A definitive conclusion cannot be drawn from the limited existing studies. We found that administering a dose of 50–60 Gy to the supraclavicular region was not inferior to doses more than 60 Gy during radiotherapy alone. We can believe that 50–60 Gy dose to the supraclavicular region is a safe and effective approach for patients with sISLM breast cancer undergoing radiotherapy alone within current systemic treatment.

Admittedly, our study had limitations. First, being a retrospective study conducted at a single center, it is prone to recall bias and selection bias. Second, the study spans a long period, during which systemic treatments evolved, potentially affecting patient outcomes. Earlier patients received less intensive systemic therapy and were more likely to undergo supraclavicular lymph node dissection. Finally, Early and late treatment toxicities did not be comprehensively assessed by the authors, such as radiation dermatitis, brachial plexopathy, and lymphedema.

Our reasearch is one of the largest to samples, both domestically and internationally, to evaluate the efficacy of different local treatments and to explore optimal radiation doses for the supraclavicular region in sISLM breast cancer patients, with an 8-year follow-up. Additionally, 78% patients had pathologically confirmed sISLM. The findings reported by the authors provide valuable insights for clinical practice and were necessarily verified through prospective studies.

Conclusion

Both radiotherapy alone and supraclavicular surgery combined with radiotherapy were considered local treatment strategies for patients with sISLM breast cancer involving supraclavicular lymph nodes. Within the framework of contemporary treatment, the exploration of lower-dose radiation for supraclavicular lymph nodes will proceed, irrespective of whether CR was achieved following neoadjuvant chemotherapy.

Potential of di-Higgs observation via a calibratable jet-free 𝑯 ​ 𝑯 → 𝟒 ​ 𝒃 HH\to 4b framework Preprint: APS/123-QED
Tianyi Yang, Congqiao Li

Abstract
We present a calibratable, jet-free framework that enhances the search significance of the flagship LHC channel H ​ H → 4 ​ b HH\to 4b by more than a factor of five compared to existing approaches. The method employs a mass-decorrelated discriminant to identify h 1 ​ h 2 → 4 ​ b h_{1}h_{2}\to 4b with variable h 1 , 2 h_{1,2} masses and a simultaneous estimator of ( m h 1 , m h 2 ) (m_{h_{1}},m_{h_{2}}) , both derived from multiclass classification on all-particle inputs. The H ​ H HH signal response can be calibrated using Z ​ Z → 4 ​ b ZZ\to 4b . Using a highly realistic simulation framework validated through multiple tests, we demonstrate the method’s robustness and identify two prerequisites essential for achieving this level of sensitivity. Results indicate that with LHC Run 2 and 3 data, observation-level sensitivity to H ​ H HH appears within reach, enabling constraints on κ λ \kappa_{\lambda} comparable to HL-LHC projections and offering an accelerated path to precision measurements of the Higgs trilinear coupling.



Introduction.—

The observation of the Higgs boson by ATLAS and CMS in 2012
Aad et al. 2012
;
Chatrchyan et al. 2012
completed the particle content of the Standard Model (SM), yet left the shape of the Brout–Englert–Higgs potential largely untested. Expanding the scalar potential about its minimum through
V(\phi)\to V(v+h)
yields

V=V_{0}+\frac{1}{2}m_{h}^{2}h^{2}+\frac{m_{h}^{2}}{2v^{2}}vh^{3}+\frac{1}{4}\frac{m_{h}^{2}}{2v^{2}}h^{4},

(1)

where the cubic term encodes the trilinear self-coupling
\lambda_{HHH}=m_{h}^{2}/2v^{2}
. This relation makes Higgs-boson-pair production,
pp\!\to\!HH
, a unique probe of the Higgs potential and the mechanism of electroweak symmetry breaking
Degrassi et al. 2012
;
McCullough 2014
.

However, the most precise calculations predict SM cross sections of only
\sigma_{\text{SM}}(pp\to HH)=31.1^{+2.1}_{-7.2}\,\text{fb}
for gluon-gluon fusion (
gg\text{F}
) and
1.73\pm 0.04\,\text{fb}
for vector-boson fusion (VBF) at
\sqrt{s}=13\,\text{TeV}

Dawson et al. 1998
;
Borowka et al. 2016
;
Baglio et al. 2019
;
de Florian and Mazzitelli 2013
;
Shao et al. 2013
;
de Florian and Mazzitelli 2015
;
Grazzini et al. 2018
;
Baglio et al. 2021
;
Dreyer and Karlberg 2018
, making inclusive
HH
production exceptionally rare yet a key physics target for the HL-LHC era. The destructive interference between the triangle diagram (proportional to
\lambda_{HHH}
) and box diagram contributions in
gg\text{F}
further suppresses the total rate while creating strong sensitivity to deviations from the SM prediction, parameterized by the coupling modifier
\kappa_{\lambda}=\lambda_{HHH}/\lambda_{HHH}^{\text{SM}}
.

With the largest branching ratio,
HH\to b\overline{b}b\overline{b}
(denoted
4b
) is the LHC’s flagship channel for di-Higgs studies. Recent ATLAS and CMS searches in the
HH\to 4b
final state using
\approx
140
\mathrm{fb^{-1}}
highlight both challenges and opportunities. The “resolved-channel” analysis sets a 95% CL upper limit of 5.4 and
3.9\,\sigma_{HH}^{\rm SM}
, constraining
\kappa_{\lambda}\in[-3.9,\,11.3]
and
[-2.3,\,9.4]
, respectively
Aad et al. 2023
;
Tumasyan et al. 2022
. CMS also reports a “boosted-channel” analysis in the
gg\text{F}
channel, excluding
\kappa_{2V}=0
at
6.3\,\sigma
while achieving a compatible
\kappa_{\lambda}
constraint:
[-9.9,\,16.9]
(expected
[-5.1,\,12.2]
), despite targeting largely different phase space
Tumasyan et al. 2023
. The power of the boosted regime is largely attributed to advanced deep learning-based
H\to b\overline{b}
jet tagging using jet constituent particles as input
CMS Collaboration 2020
;
Qu and Gouskos 2020
, which dramatically suppresses multijet backgrounds
Tumasyan et al. 2023
. This raises an intriguing question: can the superior background-rejection capability of the boosted channel be extended across the broader resolved-channel phase space?

In this Letter, we extend advanced deep-learning techniques from boosted
HH
searches to the full kinematic regime through a
jet-free

HH\to 4b
framework. Using a highly realistic simulation setup, we input all pileup-mitigated reconstructed particles into a transformer network to distinguish generic
h_{1}h_{2}\to 4b
signals from multijet backgrounds in the resolved-channel phase space. The flat mass prior of the signal sample permits calibration of the
HH
response with
ZZ\to 4b
events. Robustness is established through multiple validation tests, which also illuminate the origin of performance gains over conventional approaches. For a projected 450
\mathrm{fb^{-1}}
dataset (Run 2+3), the method attains an expected
3.3\,\sigma
sensitivity under realistic systematic uncertainties—exceeding existing resolved analyses by a factor of
>
5 and approaching observation-level sensitivity. For comparison, the latest ATLAS–CMS HL-LHC combination at
3\,\mathrm{ab^{-1}}
quotes
2.8\,\sigma
in
HH\to 4b

ATLAS and CMS Collaborations 2025
, with earlier projections of
1.0\,\sigma
in 2019
Dainese et al. 2019
;
ATLAS and CMS Collaborations 2019
. This work thus represents a major methodological advance, offering a fast-track path to precision measurements of the trilinear Higgs coupling years ahead of schedule.

Methodology.—

Building on these foundations, we develop a calibratable, full–event strategy based on a dedicated ParT network (
\approx
9\times 10^{6}
trainable parameters) that ingests all pileup‑mitigated reconstructed particles and performs direct signal–background discrimination at the event level. The implementation is a 138‑class classifier. Of these, 136 signal classes correspond to the process
h_{3}\to h_{1}h_{2}
(produced within a two-Higgs-doublet model
Branco et al. 2012
setup), discretizing
(m_{h_{1}},\,m_{h_{2}})
over the range 40–200
GeV
in 10
GeV
increments, with
(m_{h_{1}},\,m_{h_{2}})
and
(m_{h_{2}},\,m_{h_{1}})
identified as the same class. The remaining two classes model the QCD multijet and
t\overline{t}
backgrounds. Training such a network is intrinsically challenging because the per‑event token multiplicity can reach
O(300)
.

This tailored multiclass design provides two main advantages. (i) The aggregate of the 136 signal classes constitutes a flat prior in
(m_{h_{1}},\,m_{h_{2}})
, resulting in a mass‑decorrelated discriminant that is simultaneously sensitive to any
h_{1}h_{2}\to 4b
resonance configuration in that plane, including the SM benchmarks
ZZ/HH\to 4b
. (ii) By the Neyman–Pearson lemma
Neyman and Pearson 1933
, the vector of per‑class signal scores serves as a discrete estimator of the underlying probability density ratio
f(m_{h_{1}},m_{h_{2}})
for the hypothesis that an event arises from
h_{1}h_{2}\to 4b
with masses
(m_{h_{1}},\,m_{h_{2}})
, from which a localized fit extracts the most probable point
(m_{h_{1}}^{0},\,m_{h_{2}}^{0})
. The classifier output thus provides both a powerful background-rejection discriminant,
D_{h_{1}h_{2}\to 4b}
, and an event-level mass prediction point in the
(m_{h_{1}},\,m_{h_{2}})
plane (up to exchange symmetry).

Validation and calibration.—

It is crucial to verify that the significant sensitivity improvements arise from genuine methodological advances rather than artifacts of the simulation. We perform two validation studies. First, Fig.
1
shows that the signal and background yields obtained with the resolved-trigger strategy with our method (red curve) are approximately a rescaling of those from the CMS-implemented boosted strategy (purple curve). This motivates an explicit test that the classifier maintains comparable discriminating power for signal versus background across different event Lorentz boosts. Figure
3
(left) confirms this: the background sample is constructed from QCD events containing exactly four
b
hadrons at generator level, allowing us to form
b
-hadron pairs and use their
p_{\text{T}}
as a proxy for the event’s Lorentz boost. As can be found, the classifier performance is stable across this boost proxy. Second, in analogy with the validation procedures used when developing jet taggers at LHC
Sirunyan et al. 2020b
;
Aad et al. 2025a
, we assess the robustness of the model to hadronization modeling by comparing three parton-shower generators:
Pythia
8.3,
Herwig
7.2
Bellm et al. 2020
, and
Vincia

Fischer et al. 2016
. As shown in Fig.
3
(right), tighter classifier working points amplify discrepancies, most notably between
Herwig
7.2 and the others. Nevertheless, no significant efficiency loss is observed in
Herwig
7.2 or
Vincia
, indicating that the classifier largely captures genuine physics features of the
4b
final state.

Calibration of the classifier performance on signal events is a central concern for experimental implementation.
Using
Z\to b\overline{b}
as a calibration “candle” for
H\to b\overline{b}
is standard in boosted Higgs analyses
Aad et al. 2024
;
ATLAS Collaboration 2021
;
CMS Collaboration 2022
and can be applied analogously in our approach: the ratio of simulation-to-data efficiencies (scale factors) for
ZZ\to 4b
events is obtained in the vicinity of the
ZZ

2

HH

3

ZZ
to
HH

ZZ
and
HH

Z

ZZ\to 4b
. Finally, for background estimation, data-driven methods using multiple control regions can be employed. A detailed experimental implementation of these strategies is beyond the scope of this work.

Conclusion.—

We have demonstrated that the
HH\to 4b
channel possesses far greater discovery potential than previously recognized. By exploiting the full set of particles in collision events, our approach achieves unprecedented background suppression and delivers an expected
3.3\,\sigma
sensitivity with 450
\mathrm{fb^{-1}}
under realistic systematics—already competitive with HL-LHC projections based on conventional strategies
ATLAS and CMS Collaborations 2025
;
Dainese et al. 2019
;
ATLAS and CMS Collaborations 2019
. The method constrains
\kappa_{\lambda}\in[-1.1,8.8]
at 95% CL, thereby enabling precision probes of the Higgs self-coupling years ahead of schedule.

Although the direct use of low-level particle information may be considered too aggressive for immediate experimental adoption, a key contribution of this work lies in the development of a complete and calibratable analysis strategy, constructed with reference to several real LHC analyses. In brief, the framework features a mass-decorrelated
h_{1}h_{2}\to 4b
“event tagger”, enabling
HH
signal calibration through the
ZZ
process, and an estimator for
(m_{h_{1}},m_{h_{2}})
to extract peak-like structures in the mass spectrum. Extensive validation studies further confirm the reliability of the method.

Our results, particularly those shown in Fig.
1
, demonstrate that the significant performance gain relies critically on (i) moving beyond jet-based analyses and (ii) the use of sufficiently large training datasets supported by dedicated deep learning engineering. While several alternative proposals have explored sensitivity improvements in
HH\to 4b

Amacker et al. 2020
;
Chiang et al. 2024
;
Li et al. 2024b
;
Wu et al. 2025
, to our knowledge none have uncovered the level of gain realized here, which we attribute to these two key ingredients.

Looking ahead, this approach opens new opportunities for precision Higgs physics and beyond. The demonstrated sensitivity suggests that discovery-level evidence for SM Higgs pair production may already be within reach with existing LHC datasets, particularly given the advances in trigger performance since Run 3
Aad et al. 2025b
;
CMS Collaboration 2023
;
CMS Collaboration 2025
. The precision on
\kappa_{\lambda}
reaches levels once thought attainable only at the HL-LHC, opening the door to studies previously out of reach, such as tri-Higgs production
Abouabid et al. 2024
. More broadly, the particle-level framework offers a scalable template for next-generation searches, where full-event analyses, empowered by advances in artificial intelligence techniques—larger models, richer training samples, and greater computing power—promise to access previously unexplored regions of parameter space.

A.1
Sophon
model for
bb

The name of
Sophon
represents a novel experimental methodology called “Signature-Oriented Pre-training for Heavy-resonance ObservatioN”, proposed in Ref.
Li et al. 2024a
. Pre-trained on the comprehensive
JetClass-II
dataset as a large-scale classifier, the
Sophon
model can distinguish 188 different jet signatures, covering an extensive range of boosted final states including both resonant jets (2, 3, and 4 prongs) and QCD background jets.

The model architecture is built on the ParT (ParT)
Qu et al. 2022
backbone with 6 particle attention blocks and 2 class attention blocks, featuring a 128-dimensional embedding space and 8 attention heads. The fully connected multilayer perceptron is expanded to two layers with dimensions (512, 188), where the 128-dimensional neuron values before the final MLP serve as latent features for transfer learning applications. The complete model contains approximately
2.3\times 10^{6}
parameters.

For resonant jet classification,
Sophon
processes jets initiated from generic spin-0 resonances
X
decaying to various final states. Two-prong signatures include diparton decays (
bb
,
cc
,
ss
,
qq
,
bc
,
cs
, etc.) and dilepton decays (
ee
,
\mu\mu
,
\tau_{h}\tau_{e}
, etc.). Three- and four-prong signatures arise from cascade decays
X\to Y^{(*)}Y^{(*)}
with subsequent diparton/dilepton decays of the secondary resonances. QCD background jets are subdivided into 27 classes based on the number and flavors of quarks within the jet.

Regarding
X\to bb
tagging, the optimal discriminant for distinguishing
X\to bb
signals from QCD backgrounds is constructed using the likelihood-ratio properties of multiclass classifiers,

\text{discr}(X\to bb\text{ vs QCD})=\frac{g_{X\to bb}}{g_{X\to bb}+\sum_{l=1}^{27}g_{\text{QCD}_{l}}},

(3)

where
g_{X\to bb}
corresponds to the
X\to bb
output score and
g_{\text{QCD}_{l}}
represents the scores of the 27 QCD classes. This construction leverages the comprehensive classification capability while maintaining optimal discrimination power.

B.1.1

The resolved channel analysis follows a systematic approach to identify and reconstruct Higgs boson pairs from four
b

|\eta|<2.5

SophonAK4

>0.187

The four highest
b
-tagged jets (ranked by
SophonAK4
B-score) are selected for Higgs reconstruction. Given four
b
-jets, three possible pairings exist for forming two Higgs candidates:

Pairing A:

\displaystyle H_{1}=j_{1}+j_{2},\quad H_{2}=j_{3}+j_{4}

(6)

Pairing B:

\displaystyle H_{1}=j_{1}+j_{3},\quad H_{2}=j_{2}+j_{4}

(7)

Pairing C:

\displaystyle H_{1}=j_{1}+j_{4},\quad H_{2}=j_{2}+j_{3}

(8)

Optimal pairing algorithm:

The selection employs a two-stage algorithm designed to minimize combinatorial background. In Stage 1, we calculate the diagonal distance for each pairing

d_{HH}=\frac{|m_{H_{1}}-\alpha\cdot m_{H_{2}}|}{\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}}

(9)

where
\alpha=1.04
accounts for the slight asymmetry in Higgs mass reconstruction due to detector effects. When the difference between minimum and second-minimum diagonal distances is small (
\Delta d_{HH}<30
GeV), Stage 2 applies an additional criterion based on the leading Higgs
p_{\text{T}}
in the di-Higgs center-of-mass frame,

p_{\text{T}}^{\text{CM}}(H_{1})=||\vec{p}_{T}(H_{1})+\beta\vec{p}_{T}(HH)||

(10)

where
\beta
is the boost factor to the di-Higgs rest frame, and the pairing yielding higher
p_{\text{T}}^{\text{CM}}(H_{1})
is selected.

Signal region definition:

Following CMS resolved-analysis strategies
Tumasyan et al. 2022
, we define the Analysis Signal Region (ASR) as a circular region centered at
(m_{H_{1}},m_{H_{2}})=(110,105)
GeV with radius 25 GeV. Unlike the CMS analysis which centers the signal region near the Higgs boson mass of 125 GeV, our analysis does not implement
b
-jet energy regression corrections. Without these regression techniques, the reconstructed Higgs masses exhibit a systematic downward shift, leading to signal event concentration at lower invariant masses. The ASR center is therefore optimized to maximize signal acceptance in the region of highest signal density, accounting for jet energy scale effects and the natural asymmetry in the leading/subleading Higgs mass distribution,

\sqrt{(m_{H_{1}}-110)^{2}+(m_{H_{2}}-105)^{2}}<25\text{ GeV}

(11)

This cut-based selection strategy yields 197 signal events and 302,778 background events at 450 fb
-1
integrated luminosity. These event counts serve as a baseline reference point for evaluating the performance improvements achieved by our machine learning approach. The substantial background contamination in the traditional cut-based analysis motivates the development of more sophisticated discrimination techniques, as demonstrated by our ParT architecture which achieves significantly enhanced signal purity through learned feature representations.

Appendix D
Supplementary details on the localized fitting strategies

The signal extraction strategy leverages the complete 136-dimensional mass probability distribution predicted by the event-level ParT to reconstruct Higgs boson masses through sophisticated fitting procedures. This approach transforms the discrete classification outputs into continuous mass measurements, enabling precise kinematic reconstruction without traditional jet clustering limitations.

Event selection and probability distribution construction:

Each event must first pass the CMS resolved channel HLT trigger requirements, specifically the “4j3b” selection criteria (four jets with
p_{\text{T}}>[75,\,60,\,45,\,40]
GeV,
H_{\text{T}}>330
GeV, and three
b
-tagged jets). For qualifying events, the model’s first 136 classification scores represent probability densities across different
(m_{h_{1}},m_{h_{2}})
mass regions, forming a triangular distribution in the upper-diagonal mass space due to the exchange symmetry constraint
m_{h_{1}}\leq m_{h_{2}}
.

To enable comprehensive two-dimensional analysis, we transform this triangular probability distribution into a symmetric 256-bin histogram through diagonal reflection. The symmetrization procedure applies the transformation
(H+H^{T})/2
, where
H
represents the original triangular histogram and
H^{T}
its transpose, creating a complete mass distribution symmetric about the diagonal
m_{h_{1}}=m_{h_{2}}
that preserves the original probability information while enabling standard two-dimensional fitting techniques.

Two-stage Double Crystal Ball fitting strategy:

The fitting procedure employs a sophisticated two-stage approach designed to identify multiple resonance peaks while maintaining robust background modeling. The core methodology combines symmetric two-dimensional Double Crystal Ball (DCB) functions with flexible quadratic background models, both constructed to respect the diagonal symmetry of the Higgs pair system.

The two-dimensional DCB function extends the standard one-dimensional Crystal Ball formulation through rotational coupling:

\displaystyle\text{DCB}_{\text{2D}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=A\cdot\text{DCB}_{\text{1D}}(x_{\text{rot}})\cdot\text{DCB}_{\text{1D}}(y_{\text{rot}})

(16)

where
(x_{\text{rot}},y_{\text{rot}})
represent coordinates rotated by angle
\theta
, and each one-dimensional DCB component features asymmetric power-law tails characterized by parameters
(\alpha_{\text{low}},n_{\text{low}},\alpha_{\text{high}},n_{\text{high}})
for modeling the characteristic Breit-Wigner line shapes with detector resolution effects.

The background model employs a flexible functional form combining linear and logarithmic terms:

\displaystyle B(x,y)

\displaystyle=A_{\text{bg}}\left[1+s_{x}(x-x_{c})+s_{y}(y-y_{c})+l_{x}\ln(x/x_{c})+l_{y}\ln(y/y_{c})\right]

(17)

designed to capture the smooth, non-resonant behavior across the [40, 200]
GeV

2
mass window while maintaining positivity constraints and diagonal symmetry through the combination
B(x,y)+B(y,x)
.

Peak identification and parameter optimization:

The first fitting stage combines a primary DCB peak pair (respecting exchange symmetry) with the symmetric background model, floating all parameters simultaneously to identify the dominant resonance structure. The optimization procedure employs robust curve fitting with bounded parameters: DCB amplitudes remain positive, mass positions span the physical range [40, 200] GeV, width parameters are constrained to [2.5, 50] GeV, and tail parameters follow empirically determined bounds ensuring numerical stability.

Following successful primary peak identification, the algorithm calculates residuals by subtracting the fitted model from the original histogram. A sophisticated masking procedure excludes regions within
2\sigma
of the primary peak and its symmetric partner, enabling unbiased secondary peak detection through local maximum identification using morphological filtering with adaptive thresholds.

The second fitting stage applies identical DCB methodology to the masked residual, identifying secondary resonance structures while maintaining the same parameter constraints and symmetry requirements. Multiple initialization strategies with varying width parameters ensure robust convergence across diverse kinematic configurations.

Peak exchange and refinement logic:

A critical algorithmic component addresses potential peak misidentification through systematic exchange logic. If the primary peak falls outside the physical mass window [40, 200]
GeV

2
while the secondary peak remains within bounds, the algorithm automatically exchanges peak assignments and performs localized parameter refinement. This procedure fixes the geometric parameters of the newly promoted primary peak while optimizing only its amplitude and the background parameters, followed by complete re-fitting of the secondary peak using the updated residual distribution.

This exchange mechanism proves essential for maintaining physical consistency, particularly in events where initial fitting converges to spurious local minima or where detector effects shift apparent peak positions beyond the intended mass range.

Diagonal smearing and mass window selection:

Fitted peak positions near the diagonal
m_{h_{1}}=m_{h_{2}}
exhibit systematic bias toward the symmetry line due to the flattened loss function landscape in this region. F.1
Threshold selection and event yield optimization

The signal extraction procedure employs a systematic optimization of the discriminant threshold to maximize the expected discovery significance. This process requires careful treatment of the ROC curve tail region, where statistical fluctuations can introduce artificial features that compromise the optimization procedure.

ROC curve smoothing strategy:

The raw ROC curve obtained from the
\text{score}_{\text{HH vs both}}
discriminant exhibits significant statistical noise in the high-purity tail region due to limited background statistics surviving stringent selections. To enable robust threshold optimization, we implement a sophisticated smoothing procedure that preserves the underlying physics while removing statistical artifacts.

The smoothing algorithm employs a three-stage approach: (i) smart data selection using logarithmic spacing to preserve curve shape while reducing point density, (ii) conversion to log-space coordinates to linearize the exponential behavior in the tail region, and (iii) application of a Savitzky-Golay filter with heavy smoothing parameters to suppress statistical fluctuations.

Specifically, the procedure reduces the original ROC data points through intelligent subsampling that maintains curve fidelity, targeting approximately 400 points distributed logarithmically across the signal efficiency range. The selected points undergo coordinate transformation to
(\log_{10}(\text{signal events}),\log_{10}(\text{background events}))
space, where the exponential tail behavior becomes approximately linear, enabling effective polynomial filtering.

The Savitzky-Golay filter applies a third-order polynomial fit within a sliding window encompassing approximately 25% of the data points, providing substantial smoothing while preserving the overall ROC structure. This “Savgol Heavy” configuration effectively eliminates statistical noise in the tail region without introducing systematic biases in the bulk ROC behavior.

Significance optimization:

Following ROC smoothing, we perform a comprehensive scan over discriminant thresholds to identify the optimal working point. For each threshold value, we extract the corresponding signal and background event yields from the smoothed ROC curve and calculate the expected significance using the Asimov approximation

Z_{A}=\sqrt{2\left\{(s+b)\ln\left[\frac{(s+b)(b+\sigma_{b}^{2})}{b^{2}+(s+b)\sigma_{b}^{2}}\right]-\frac{b^{2}}{\sigma_{b}^{2}}\ln\left[1+\frac{\sigma_{b}^{2}s}{b(b+\sigma_{b}^{2})}\right]\right\}}

(20)

where
s
and
b
represent the signal and background yields, respectively, and
\sigma_{b}
denotes the relative background uncertainty.

The optimization scan reveals a well-defined maximum at discriminant threshold
\text{score}_{\text{HH vs both}}=0.999903
, corresponding to signal and background yields of
s=20.463
and
b=15.176

-1

Likelihood scan procedure:

The profile likelihood scan over
\kappa_{\lambda}
employs a grid-based approach with 100 evaluation points spanning the range
\kappa_{\lambda}\in[-5,+15]
. At each grid point, the likelihood is maximized with respect to all nuisance parameters while keeping
\kappa_{\lambda}
fixed. The other coupling parameters (
\kappa_{t}
,
C_{V}
,
C_{2V}
) are held at their SM values to isolate the self-coupling dependence.

The statistical model incorporates systematic uncertainties through log-normal nuisance parameters affecting both signal and background normalizations. Four uncertainty scenarios are considered: (i) statistical-only (0% systematic uncertainties), (ii) optimistic (10% background uncertainty), (iii) realistic (20% background uncertainty), and (iv) conservative (50% background, 20% signal uncertainties).

For each scenario, the profile likelihood ratio
\Lambda(\kappa_{\lambda})=-2\ln[\mathcal{L}(\kappa_{\lambda})/\mathcal{L}(\hat{\kappa}_{\lambda})]
is computed, where
\hat{\kappa}_{\lambda}
denotes the best-fit value. The 95% confidence intervals are determined by the condition
\Lambda(\kappa_{\lambda})=3.84
, corresponding to the critical value for one degree of freedom.

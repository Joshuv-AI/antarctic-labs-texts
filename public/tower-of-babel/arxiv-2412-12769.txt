A note on complex interpolation of quasi-Banach function spaces
Moritz Egert and Benjamin W. Kosmala

Abstract
. Kalton and Mitrea characterized complex interpolation spaces of quasi-Banach function spaces as Calderón products if both interpolants are separable. We show that one separability assumption may be omitted and establish a Wolff-reiteration result with one non-separable endpoint space.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
46B70, 46E30

1.
Introduction

Recent literature uses Kalton and Mitrea’s complex interpolation theory for quasi-Banach spaces
[
15
]
to prove estimates for solutions to e.g. elliptic boundary value problems in tent spaces
[
3
,
2
]
. Typically, these spaces are characterized by an integrability parameter
p\in(0,\infty]
such that
p\in(0,1)
yields quasi-Banach spaces,
p\in[1,\infty]
Banach spaces and separable spaces are obtained if and only if
p\in(0,\infty)
.

Amongst others, the authors of
[
2
]
establish interpolation identities for the whole range
p\in(0,\infty]
, using so-called Wolff-reiteration
[
21
, Thm. 2]
as follows: They show the desired interpolation identity for parameters in
(0,\infty)
, use a duality argument on
[1,\infty)
to cover parameters in
(1,\infty]
and finally ’glue’
(0,\infty)
and
(1,\infty]
together using Wolff-reiteration.
Regarding this final gluing procedure,
[
2
, Thm. 2.12]
contains a subtle gap, because Wolff-reiteration for the complex method is not known for general quasi-Banach spaces.

The author of
[
10
]
derives such identities differently, by characterizing interpolation spaces elegantly as appropriate products and then mainly argues on the product side.
In their case, for instance in
[
10
, Thm. 4.3]
, coincidence of products and interpolation spaces is simply taken for granted.

In the setting of quasi-Banach function spaces, the purpose of this paper is thus to establish the identification of complex interpolation spaces as appropriate products and to derive from this the desired Wolff-type result.

We first expand on Kalton and Mitrea’s pioneering work
[
15
, Thm. 3.4]
by identifying complex interpolation spaces as Calderón products if only one interpolant is non-separable. Prior to our note, this was only known for sequence spaces
[
14
, Sect. 7]
. Our (standard) notation is explained in Sections
2
and
3
.

Theorem 1.1

(Calderón formula)
.

Let
X_{0},X_{1}
be
A
-convex quasi-Banach function spaces over a separable and
\sigma
-finite measure space
\Omega
, one of which is separable.
Then
X_{0}+X_{1}
is
A
-convex and for every
\theta\in(0,1)
the spaces
[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}
and
X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}
are separable and agree up to equivalence of quasi-norms.

We then apply a reiteration result for Calderón products due to Gomez and Milman
[
9
, Thm. 4.13]
, which provides the desired Wolff-reiteration for quasi-Banach function spaces.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X_{0},X_{1},X_{2},X_{3}
be
A
-convex quasi-Banach function spaces over a separable and
\sigma
-finite measure space
\Omega
, where either
X_{0}
or
X_{3}
is separable and
X_{0}\cap X_{3}
is dense in
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
.
Further, let
\theta,\eta,\lambda,\mu\in[0,1]
be parameters subject to the following conditions:

(1)

0<\theta<\eta<1
,

(2)

\theta=\lambda\eta
,

(3)

\eta=(1-\mu)\theta+\mu
.

If
X_{1}=[X_{0},X_{2}]_{\lambda}
and
X_{2}=[X_{1},X_{3}]_{\mu}
, then
X_{1}=[X_{0},X_{3}]_{\theta}
and
X_{2}=[X_{0},X_{3}]_{\eta}
hold up to equivalence of quasi-norms.

Remark 1.3
.

It remains an interesting open problem to prove Theorem
1.2
for more general
A
-convex quasi-Banach spaces.

We will recall the complex interpolation method for general quasi-Banach spaces in Section
2
and then focus on quasi-Banach function spaces from Section
3
onward, while also establishing the main tools for the proofs of the Calderón formula and the reiteration result given in Sections
4
and
5
.

Concerning notation, all vector spaces are taken over
\mathbb{C}
,
\mathds{D}
is the open unit disc in
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{S}\coloneqq\{z\in\mathbb{C}\,|\,\mathrm{Re}(z)\in(0,1)\}
.
Quasi-norms on quasi-Banach spaces
X
will be denoted as
\left\|\,\cdot\,\right\|_{X}
, measures of measureable sets
E
will be denoted as
\left|E\right|
and we let
\theta\in(0,1)
.

2.
Complex interpolation of quasi-Banach spaces

Let
(X_{0},X_{1})
be an interpolation couple, namely
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
are quasi-Banach spaces that continuously embed in some common Hausdorff topological vector space
Z
.
Following
[
14
,
15
]
, a function
F:\mathbb{S}\to X_{0}+X_{1}
is called
analytic
if for every
z_{0}\in\mathbb{S}
there exists an
r\in(0,\text{dist}(z_{0},\partial\mathbb{S}))
such that
F
has a power series representation
F(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f_{k}(z-z_{0})^{k}
with convergence in
X_{0}+X_{1}
for every
z\in B_{r}(z_{0})
.
The space of
admissible functions
, denoted as
\mathcal{F}
, contains all bounded and analytic functions, which can be continuously extended to
\overline{\mathbb{S}}
such that for
j=0,1
the traces
t\mapsto F(j+\text{i}t)
are continuous and bounded functions into
X_{j}
.
This space is endowed with the quasi-norm

\displaystyle\left\|F\right\|_{\mathcal{F}}\coloneqq\max\left\{\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\|F(\text{i}t)\right\|_{X_{0}},\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left\|F(1+\text{i}t)\right\|_{X_{1}},\sup_{z\in\mathbb{S}}\left\|F(z)\right\|_{X_{0}+X_{1}}\right\}.

We define the
complex interpolation space
as
[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}\coloneqq\{F(\theta)\,|\,F\in\mathcal{F}\}
and equip it with the quotient quasi-norm
\left\|f\right\|_{[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}}\coloneqq\inf\{\left\|F\right\|_{\mathcal{F}}\,|\,F(\theta)=f,F\in\mathcal{F}\}
.

Both
\mathcal{F}
and
[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}
are again quasi-Banach spaces, which agree with Calderóns method when the
X_{j}
are Banach spaces
[
4
, §2, §3]
.
There are other complex interpolation methods for quasi-Banach spaces, such as
[
5
, Ch. 3]
and
[
9
]
, which come with their own partly undesired properties (the former does not agree with Calderóns method in the Banach setting
[
20
, Ch. 1.6.7, Rem. 3]
and the latter does in general not produce subspaces of
X_{0}+X_{1}
, see
[
7
]
). They are thus not used in this paper.

Remark 2.1
.

In comparison to
[
15
]
, we made two harmless but noteworthy changes:

(1)

We dropped the assumption that
X_{0}\cap X_{1}
is dense in both
X_{j}
, as it was neither needed in our arguments nor in other works in the field
[
22
,
23
]
. In particular, already
[
14
,
15
]
contain results, for which this density assumption cannot hold. Density is typically used to extend operators
T
, which act on
X_{0}\cap X_{1}
, first to both
X_{j}
and then to
[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}
. However, if
T
is already defined on both
X_{j}
, then it can be extended to
X_{0}+X_{1}
simply by setting
T(x_{0}+x_{1})\coloneqq T_{0}(x_{1})+T_{1}(x_{1})
.

(2)

We dropped the requirement of locally uniform convergence of the series expansion of admissible functions on
B_{r}(z_{0})
, because convergence of quasi-Banach valued power series inside an open disc implies absolute convergence inside the same open disc, from which locally uniform convergence easily follows
[
17
, Lem. 5.10]
.

3.
Quasi-Banach function spaces

From now on, let
\Omega
be a measure space. We denote with
\mathrm{L}^{0}(\Omega)
the space of
\mathbb{C}
-valued measureable functions on
\Omega
, where two functions are identified if they agree almost everywhere.

In order to do function spaces right, we introduce them as in
[
19
, Ch. 2]
.

Definition 3.1
.

A quasi-Banach space
X\subseteq\mathrm{L}^{0}(\Omega)
is called a
quasi-Banach function space
or
quasi-Banach function space over
\Omega
, if:

(1)

X
contains a
weak order unit
, i.e. there exists
f\in X
such that
f>0
a.e.,

(2)

X
satisfies the
lattice property
, i.e. if
f\in X
,
g\in\mathrm{L}^{0}(\Omega)
with
\left|g\right|\leq\left|f\right|
a.e., then
g\in X
with
\left\|g\right\|_{X}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{X}
.

Let
(X_{0},X_{1})
be a couple of quasi-Banach function spaces. It is then admissible for complex interpolation, because the sum
X_{0}+X_{1}
is well-defined in
\mathrm{L}^{0}(\Omega)
and is again a quasi-Banach function space, into which
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
continuously embed. Also the complex interpolation space
[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}
is again a quasi-Banach function space: As weak order unit we take the minimum of the weak order units in the
X_{j}
and if
f\in[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}
is obtained as
f=F(\theta)
for some
F\in\mathcal{F}
, then every
g\in\mathrm{L}^{0}(\Omega)
with
\left|g\right|\leq\left|f\right|
is obtained as
g=G(\theta)
for
G\coloneqq\nicefrac{{g}}{{f}}F
satisfying
\|G\|_{\mathcal{F}}\leq\|F\|_{\mathcal{F}}
.

Having pointwise products available will allow us to characterize interpolation spaces via

Definition 3.2
.

The
Calderón product

X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}
of quasi-Banach function spaces
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
over
\Omega
is defined as the set of all
f\in\mathrm{L}^{0}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}}\coloneqq\inf\left\{\left\|f_{0}\right\|_{X_{0}}^{1-\theta}\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{X_{1}}^{\theta}\,|\,\left|f\right|\leq\left|f_{0}\right|^{1-\theta}\left|f_{1}\right|^{\theta}\text{ a.e.},\ f_{j}\in X_{j}\right\}

is finite.

With slight adaptations regarding the quasi-triangle inequality, completeness of Calderón products works as in
[
4
, §13.5]
, making them quasi-Banach function spaces that appear naturally in the context of interpolation.

In order to show that interpolation spaces and Calderón products agree in the Banach case, Calderón used an argument based on approximation via truncations
[
4
, §13.6 (i)]
, which will also be our strategy for the quasi-Banach setting.
This requires that truncations give rise to admissible functions and that some sort of pointwise a.e. convergence should be compatible with quasi-norm convergence.

To identify limits of truncations in
X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}
and
[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}
in the common ambient space
X_{0}+X_{1}
later on, we will need

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
X
be a quasi-Banach function space over
\Omega
.
If
x_{n}\to x
in
X
, then
x_{n}\to x
locally in measure.
In particular, convergent sequences in
X
admit pointwise a.e. converging subsequences and, consequently, limits in
X
are determined by their pointwise a.e. limits.

The proof for the Banach case can be found in
[
4
, Thm. 13.2]
and only needs slight modifications to also handle the quasi-triangle inequality.

Now, we can provide the classical construction of admissible functions with prescribed boundary data on
\mathbb{S}
.
The result seems to be well known and we only include an elementary proof for completeness, since in the quasi-Banach case we cannot rely on difference quotients
[
11
, p. 275]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
be quasi-Banach function spaces over
\Omega
and
f_{j}\in X_{j}
such that for some
M>1
their nonzero absolute values are contained in
[M^{-1},M]
almost everywhere.
Then
F:\mathbb{S}\to X_{0}+X_{1},z\mapsto\left|f_{0}\right|^{1-z}\left|f_{1}\right|^{z}
is an admissible function.

Proof.

The proof comes in four steps.

Step 1:
F
is well-defined.

Let
z\in\mathbb{S}
. Since powers of
0
are not unanimously defined, we need to be careful when defining
F
. We set
\left|f_{0}\right|^{1-z}\coloneqq g_{z}\circ\left|f_{0}\right|
, where

\displaystyle g_{z}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{C},\qquad w\mapsto\begin{cases}w^{1-z},&\text{if\ }w\neq 0,\\
0,&\text{else}.\end{cases}

Then
g_{z}
is measurable and using a similar definition for
\left|f_{1}\right|^{z}
, defining
F(z)\coloneqq\left|f_{0}\right|^{1-z}\left|f_{1}\right|^{z}
yields a measurable function. By Young’s inequality, we have that

\displaystyle\left|\left|f_{0}\right|^{1-z}\left|f_{1}\right|^{z}\right|=\left|f_{0}\right|^{\mathrm{Re}(1-z)}\left|f_{1}\right|^{\mathrm{Re}(z)}\leq\mathrm{Re}(1-z)\left|f_{0}\right|+\mathrm{Re}(z)\left|f_{1}\right|.

Thus,
F(z)\in X_{0}+X_{1}
by the lattice property of
X_{0}+X_{1}
.

Step 2:
F
is bounded on
\mathbb{S}
.

The pointwise estimate for
F
on
\mathbb{S}
directly translates into a quasi-norm estimate:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\left\|F(z)\right\|_{X_{0}+X_{1}}\leq\left\|\mathrm{Re}(1-z)\left|f_{0}\right|+\mathrm{Re}(z)\left|f_{1}\right|\right\|_{X_{0}+X_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|f_{0}\right\|_{X_{0}}+\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{X_{1}}.

Step 3:
F
extends continuously to
\overline{\mathbb{S}}
such that the traces are bounded into
X_{j}
.

To define an extension, we only need to observe that the definition of
g_{z}
can be extended to
z\in\overline{\mathbb{S}}
and still yields a measurable function. Abusing notation, we denote this extension of
F
to
\overline{\mathbb{S}}
again as
F
. Then
\left|F(j+\text{i}t)\right|=\left|f_{j}\right|
and we obtain that
t\mapsto F(j+\text{i}t)
is a bounded map into
X_{j}
.

For continuity on
\overline{\mathbb{S}}
, let
z,z^{\prime}\in\overline{\mathbb{S}}
and denote with
\overline{zz^{\prime}}
the line segment connecting
z
and
z^{\prime}
. On
A\coloneqq\text{supp}(f_{0})\cap\text{supp}(f_{1})
, we obtain pointwise a.e. the estimate

\displaystyle|F(z)-F(z^{\prime})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\left|f_{0}\right|^{1-z}\left|f_{1}\right|^{z}-\left|f_{0}\right|^{1-z^{\prime}}\left|f_{1}\right|^{z^{\prime}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\overline{zz^{\prime}}}\log(\left|f_{1}\right|/\left|f_{0}\right|)\left|f_{0}\right|^{1-\xi}\left|f_{1}\right|^{\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|z-z^{\prime}\right|\log(\left|f_{1}\right|/\left|f_{0}\right|)\sup_{\xi\in\overline{zz^{\prime}}}\left|f_{0}\right|^{1-\mathrm{Re}(\xi)}\left|f_{1}\right|^{\mathrm{Re}(\xi)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|z-z^{\prime}\right|\log(M^{2})M.

The same estimate holds true on
\Omega\setminus A
. As
\boldsymbol{1}_{A}\leq M^{-1}\left|f_{j}\right|
, the lattice property of
X_{0}+X_{1}
yields
\boldsymbol{1}_{A}\in X_{0}+X_{1}
with

\displaystyle\left\|F(z)-F(z^{\prime})\right\|_{X_{0}+X_{1}}\leq\left|z-z^{\prime}\right|\log(M^{2})M\left\|\boldsymbol{1}_{A}\right\|_{X_{0}+X_{1}},

showing continuity of
F
up to
\overline{\mathbb{S}}
. The continuity of the traces works similarly by taking
X_{j}
-norms when
z,z^{\prime}\in j+\text{i}\mathbb{R}
.

Step 4:
F
is analytic.

Let
z_{0}\in\mathbb{S}
. By the series expansion of the exponential, we obtain for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
pointwise a.e. on
A
that

\displaystyle F(z)

\displaystyle=\left|f_{0}\right|^{1-z}\left|f_{1}\right|^{z}

\displaystyle=\left|f_{0}\right|\mathrm{e}^{z_{0}\log\left(\left|\frac{f_{1}}{f_{0}}\right|\right)}\mathrm{e}^{(z-z_{0})\log\left(\left|\frac{f_{1}}{f_{0}}\right|\right)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left|f_{0}\right|}{k!}\mathrm{e}^{z_{0}\log\left(\left|\frac{f_{1}}{f_{0}}\right|\right)}\log^{k}\left(\left|\frac{f_{1}}{f_{0}}\right|\right)(z-z_{0})^{k}

\displaystyle\eqqcolon\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}g_{k}(z-z_{0})^{k}.

We again denote the extension of
g_{k}
by 0 to
\Omega
as
g_{k}
. This yields measurable functions and
g_{k}\in X_{0}+X_{1}
, as
k!\left|g_{k}\right|\leq\left|f_{0}\right|M^{2}[\log(M^{2})]^{k}
a.e. and the latter is in
X_{0}
.
By Proposition
3.3
, this is the only candidate for a series expansion in
X_{0}+X_{1}
and we need to check that the series converges in
X_{0}+X_{1}
.
If
z\in\mathbb{S}
, then

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{k=n}^{m}g_{k}(z-z_{0})^{k}\right\|_{X_{0}+X_{1}}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{k=n}^{m}\frac{\left|f_{0}\right|}{k!}M^{2}\log^{k}(M^{2})\left|z-z_{0}\right|^{k}\right\|_{X_{0}+X_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M\left\|\boldsymbol{1}_{A}\right\|_{X_{0}+X_{1}}M^{2}\sum_{k=n}^{m}\frac{\log^{k}(M^{2})}{k!}\left|z-z_{0}\right|^{k}

shows that the series converges indeed in
X_{0}+X_{1}
for every
z\in B_{\text{dist}(z_{0},\partial\mathbb{S})}(z_{0})
.
∎

To relate functions to their truncations, we use the following concept.

Definition 3.5
.

A quasi-Banach function space
X
over
\Omega
is
order continuous
, if for every nonnegative sequence
(f_{n})_{n}
in
X
with
f_{n}\searrow 0
a.e. it follows that
f_{n}\to 0
in
X
.

We have access to order continuity if
\Omega
is ‘reasonably separable’.

Definition 3.6

(
[
19
, p. 12]
)
.

The measure space
\Omega
is called
separable
, if there exists a sequence of measurable sets
(A_{n})_{n}
in
\Omega
such that for every measurable set
E\subseteq\Omega
with
\left|E\right|<\infty
and every
\varepsilon>0
there exists an
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\left|A_{n}\Delta E\right|<\varepsilon
.

In particular, if
\Omega
is a separable metric space, which is endowed with its Borel
\sigma
-algebra and a regular measure,
\Omega
is separable in the above sense. (Take
(A_{n})_{n}
as an enumeration of finite unions of balls with rational radius centered around a dense sequence in
\Omega
.)

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
X
be a quasi-Banach space over a separable and
\sigma
-finite measure space
\Omega
. Then
X
is separable if and only if
X
is order continuous.

Showing that order continuity implies separability is done similarly to the
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-case by making use of the weak order unit, see
[
19
, Prop. 3.13]
.
The other direction is more involved: Arguing by contraposition boils down to constructing a discrete copy of
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})
in
X
as in
[
1
, Cor. 14.5]
, which can be adapted to the quasi-Banach setting.

Our generalization of the Calderón formula is based on

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
be quasi-Banach function spaces over
\Omega
. If either of them is order continuous, then so is
X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}
.

The proof in
[
8
, Thm. 1.29]
relies only on pointwise a.e. estimates and the lattice property and thus works
verbatim
in the quasi-Banach setting.

By Theorem
3.7
, the space
X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}
is order continuous (and therefore truncation arguments work) if
X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}
is separable, which is the case if for instance
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
are separable, as is easily seen from the definition of
\left\|\,\cdot\,\right\|_{X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}}
. By Proposition
3.8
and Theorem
3.7
, we can reduce to a single separability assumption.

We will need further properties that a quasi-Banach function space may have. The first one is a weak version of the maximum principle.

Definition 3.9
.

A quasi-Banach function space
X
over
\Omega
is called
A-convex
or
analytically convex
, if there exists
C\geq 1
such that for all polynomials
P:\overline{\mathds{D}}\to X
it follows that
\left\|P(0)\right\|_{X}\leq C\sup_{\left|z\right|=1}\left\|P(z)\right\|_{X}
.

Remark 3.10
.

Tent spaces as in
[
2
,
10
,
6
]
are covered by our definitions of quasi-Banach function spaces and
A
-convexity, see e.g. the proof of
[
10
, Thm. 4.3]
. Additionally, general Banach spaces are
A
-convex
[
14
, p. 21]
.

The second one will yield a different perspective on the proof of the Calderón formula later on.

Definition 3.11
.

A quasi-Banach function space
X
over
\Omega
has the
weak Fatou property
if there exists
C\geq 1
such that for all sequences of non-negative functions
(f_{n})_{n}
in
X
that satisfy
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left\|f_{n}\right\|_{X}<\infty
, it follows that
\liminf_{n\to\infty}f_{n}\in X
with
\left\|\liminf_{n\to\infty}f_{n}\right\|_{X}\leq C\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left\|f_{n}\right\|_{X}
.

Among the different versions of the Fatou property
[
24
, Ch. 15, §65]
we choose this one, because it is most reminiscent of Fatou’s lemma from integration theory.

4.
Proof of the Calderón formula

We recall the Aoki-Rolewicz Theorem
[
16
, Thm. 1.3]
, which asserts that for every quasi-Banach space
X
there is an
r\in(0,1]
and an equivalent quasi-norm, which is
s
-subadditive and continuous for all
s\in(0,r]
.
In particular,
\left\|\sum_{k=0}^{n}x_{k}\right\|_{X}^{s}\leq C^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left\|x_{k}\right\|_{X}^{s}
holds whenever
x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}\in X
and
s\in(0,r]
, where
C\geq 1
is the constant from the quasi-norm equivalence.

For the following proof, we may assume that the equivalent quasi-norms on either of
[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}
and
X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}
are
s
-subadditive for the same value
r\in(0,1]
and switching to them from the original norm will come at the cost of a constant
C\geq 1
or
D\geq 1
, respectively. As only the equivalent quasi-norm on
X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}
shows up explicitly, this one will be denoted as
\left\|\,\cdot\,\right\|
. We also denote the constant from the quasi-triangle inequality of
[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}
as
C_{\square}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We use ideas from
[
4
, §13.6 (i)]
to show that the inclusion
X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}\subseteq[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}
holds, that it is bounded and that both spaces are separable.

That
X_{0}+X_{1}
is
A
-convex and that there is a bounded inclusion
[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}\subseteq X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}
was proven in
[
15
, Thm. 3.4]
without the assumption on separability. To convince the reader that these results indeed do not rely on separability, an overview on their proofs can be found in Section
5
. We set
K\coloneqq 2D^{2}
and work with the following auxiliary set

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}\coloneqq\Bigl\{f\in X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}\,|\,

\displaystyle\exists\,M>1,\text{ s.t. }\left|f\right|=K\left\|f\right\|_{X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}}\left|f_{0}\right|^{1-\theta}\left|f_{1}\right|^{\theta},

\displaystyle f_{j}\in X_{j},\,\left\|f_{j}\right\|_{X_{j}}\leq 1,\ M^{-1}\leq\left|f_{j}\right|\leq M\text{ a.e.\ on }\text{supp}(f_{j})\Bigr\}.

The actual argument comes in three steps.

Step 1: The bounded inclusion
\mathcal{D}\subseteq[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}
.

If
f\in\mathcal{D}
, then

\displaystyle F:\mathbb{S}\to X_{0}+X_{1},\qquad z\mapsto\frac{f}{\left|f\right|}\left|f_{0}\right|^{1-z}\left|f_{1}\right|^{z}

is admissible by Lemma
3.4
.
In view of (
3.1
), the bound
\left\|F\right\|_{\mathcal{F}}\leq 2
is immediate and as
F(\theta)=f(K\left\|f\right\|_{X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}})^{-1}
, we obtain
f\in[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}
and

(4.1)

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}}\leq\left\|F\right\|_{\mathcal{F}}K\left\|f\right\|_{X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}}\leq 2K\left\|f\right\|_{X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}}.

Step 2:
\mathcal{D}
is dense in
X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}
.

By assumption, either
X_{0}
or
X_{1}
is separable, so that one is order continuous by Theorem
3.7
.
Using Proposition
3.8
, we obtain order continuity of
X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}
and then separability, again by Theorem
3.7
.

Let
f\in X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}
. Of course, we can assume
f\neq 0
.
Then there exist
f_{j}\in X_{j}\setminus\{0\}
such that

\displaystyle\left|f\right|\leq\left|f_{0}\right|^{1-\theta}\left|f_{1}\right|^{\theta},\quad\left\|f_{0}\right\|_{X_{0}}^{1-\theta}\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{X_{1}}^{\theta}\leq\frac{3}{2}\left\|f\right\|_{X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}}.

Replacing
f_{0}
by
\left|\nicefrac{{f}}{{\left|f_{1}\right|^{\theta}}}\right|^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{1-\theta}}}\leq\left|f_{0}\right|
, we may assume that
\left|f\right|=\left|f_{0}\right|^{1-\theta}\left|f_{1}\right|^{\theta}
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
we introduce
E_{n}\coloneqq\{\omega\in\Omega\,|\,\nicefrac{{1}}{{n}}\leq\left|f_{0}(\omega)\right|,\left|f_{1}(\omega)\right|\leq n\}
and define truncations via
g_{n}\coloneqq f\boldsymbol{1}_{E_{n}}
.
Then
g_{n}\in X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}
and order continuity yields
g_{n}\to f
in
X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}
due to
\left|f-g_{n}\right|\searrow 0
.

In order to show
g_{n}\in\mathcal{D}
, we observe that
g_{n}\to f
in
X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}
implies
\left\|g_{n}\right\|\to\left\|f\right\|
by continuity of
\left\|\,\cdot\,\right\|
and so we have
\left\|f\right\|\leq\nicefrac{{4}}{{3}}\left\|g_{n}\right\|
for all large enough
n
. Making
n
even larger, we can also assume
\left\|f_{j}\boldsymbol{1}_{E_{n}}\right\|_{X_{j}}>0
and define
h_{i,n}\coloneqq f_{i}\boldsymbol{1}_{E_{n}}\|f_{i}\boldsymbol{1}_{E_{n}}\|^{-1}
for those
n
. We have
h_{i,n}\in X_{i}
and obtain

\displaystyle\left|g_{n}\right|

\displaystyle=\left\|f_{0}\boldsymbol{1}_{E_{n}}\right\|_{X_{0}}^{1-\theta}\left\|f_{1}\boldsymbol{1}_{E_{n}}\right\|_{X_{1}}^{\theta}\left|h_{0,n}\right|^{\theta}\left|h_{1,n}\right|^{1-\theta}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{2}\left\|f\right\|_{X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}}\left|h_{0,n}\right|^{1-\theta}\left|h_{1,n}\right|^{\theta}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{2}\,D\left\|f\right\|\left|h_{0,n}\right|^{1-\theta}\left|h_{1,n}\right|^{\theta}

\displaystyle\leq 2D\left\|g_{n}\right\|\left|h_{0,n}\right|^{1-\theta}\left|h_{1,n}\right|^{\theta}

\displaystyle\leq 2D^{2}\left\|g_{n}\right\|_{X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}}\left|h_{0,n}\right|^{1-\theta}\left|h_{1,n}\right|^{\theta}

\displaystyle=K\left\|g_{n}\right\|_{X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}}\left|h_{0,n}\right|^{1-\theta}\left|h_{1,n}\right|^{\theta}.

Replacing
h_{0,n}
by
|g_{n}(K\left\|f_{n}\right\|_{X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}}\left|h_{1,n}\right|^{\theta})^{-1}|^{1/(1-\theta)}\leq\left|h_{0,n}\right|
achieves equality and we see that every
g_{n}
belongs to
\mathcal{D}
by making a certain choice of
M>1
.

Step 3:
X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}\subseteq[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}
follows by density.

This is not immediate, as
\mathcal{D}
might not be a linear subspace. So, let
f\in X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}
.
Inductively, we shall construct a sequence
(g_{n})_{n}
in
\mathcal{D}
such that

(4.2)

\displaystyle\Bigg\|f-\sum_{k=0}^{n}f_{k}\Bigg\|^{r}

\displaystyle\leq\rho^{n+1}\left\|f\right\|^{r},

(4.3)

\displaystyle\left\|f_{n}\right\|^{r}

\displaystyle\leq\rho^{n+1}\left\|f\right\|^{r}

hold for some
\rho\in(0,1)
.

For
n=0
, let
c,\varepsilon>0
be parameters to be chosen momentarily.
By the previous step, there is an
f_{0}\in\mathcal{D}
such that
\left\|cf-f_{0}\right\|^{r}\leq\varepsilon\left\|f\right\|^{r}
.
This function also satisfies

\displaystyle\left\|f-f_{0}\right\|^{r}\leq\left\|cf-f_{0}\right\|^{r}+\left\|(1-c)f\right\|^{r}\leq(\varepsilon+(1-c)^{r})\left\|f\right\|^{r}

and

\displaystyle\left\|f_{0}\right\|^{r}\leq\left\|f_{0}-cf\right\|^{r}+\left\|cf\right\|^{r}\leq(\varepsilon+c^{r})\left\|f\right\|^{r}.

Fixing
c=\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
and
\varepsilon
so that
\rho\coloneqq\varepsilon+c^{r}<1
yields the claim for
n=0
.
The induction over
n
is done similarly by replacing
f
with
f-\sum_{k=0}^{n}f_{k}
and
f_{0}
with
f_{n+1}
.

Since
\rho\in(0,1)
, estimate (
4.2
) implies that
\sum_{k=0}^{n}f_{k}\overset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}f
in
X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}
.
By (
4.1
) and (
4.3
) we also obtain

\displaystyle\left\|f_{n}\right\|^{r}_{[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq(2K)^{r}\left\|f_{n}\right\|^{r}_{X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq(2K)^{r}D^{r}\left\|f_{n}\right\|^{r}

\displaystyle\leq(2K)^{r}D^{r}\rho^{n+1}\left\|f\right\|^{r}

\displaystyle\leq(2K)^{r}D^{2r}\rho^{n+1}\left\|f\right\|^{r}_{X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}},

which we use to show that
(\sum_{k=0}^{n}f_{k})_{n}
is a Cauchy sequence in
[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}
:

\displaystyle\Bigg\|\sum_{k=m}^{n}f_{k}\Bigg\|^{r}_{[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}}\leq

\displaystyle C^{2r}\sum_{k=m}^{n}\left\|f_{k}\right\|^{r}_{[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{2r}(2K)^{r}D^{2r}\sum_{k=m}^{n}\rho^{k+1}\left\|f\right\|^{r}_{X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}}.

Thus, the limit
\widetilde{f}
in
[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}
satisfies

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{f}\|_{[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}}\leq

\displaystyle C_{\square}\Bigg(\Bigg\|\widetilde{f}-\sum_{k=0}^{n}f_{k}\Bigg\|_{[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}}+\Bigg\|\sum_{k=0}^{n}f_{k}\Bigg\|_{[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\square}\Bigg(\Bigg\|\widetilde{f}-\sum_{k=0}^{n}f_{k}\Bigg\|_{[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}}+C^{2}(2K)D^{2}\Bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\rho^{k+1}\Bigg)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{r}}}\left\|f\right\|_{X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}}\Bigg)

and so by passing to the limit, we obtain

(4.4)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{f}\|_{[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}}\leq C_{\square}C^{2}(2K)D^{2}\left(\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}\right)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{r}}}\left\|f\right\|_{X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}}.

We have shown that in the limit as
n\to\infty
, the partial sums above converge to
f
in
X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}
and to
\widetilde{f}
in
[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}
. Since both spaces continuously embed into
X_{0}+X_{1}
(by definition and Young’s inequality, respectively), the limits can be identified by virtue of Proposition
3.3
.
Thus, (
4.4
) yields the claim.∎

Our second main result follows immediately:

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

If
X_{0}
is separable, then by Theorem
1.1
also
X_{1}
is separable with
X_{1}=[X_{0},X_{2}]_{\lambda}=X_{0}^{1-\lambda}X_{2}^{\lambda}
.
Since
X_{2}=[X_{1},X_{3}]_{\mu}
, separability of
X_{1}
yields
X_{2}=X_{1}^{1-\mu}X_{3}^{\mu}
.
The same conclusion can be achieved when
X_{3}
is separable.
Now,
[
9
, Thm. 4.13]
applies and yields
X_{1}=X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{3}^{\theta}
and
X_{2}=X_{0}^{1-\eta}X_{3}^{\eta}
. Using Theorem
1.1
again to convert Calderón products back to interpolation spaces, we finally obtain
X_{1}=[X_{0},X_{3}]_{\theta}
and
X_{2}=[X_{0},X_{3}]_{\eta}
.
∎

Our proof of the inclusion
X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}\subseteq[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}
uses separability of the Calderón product in a crucial way. We can simplify the argument, whenever more structure of the interpolation space is known a priori.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
X_{0},X_{1}
be
A
-convex quasi-Banach function spaces over a
\sigma
-finite measure space
\Omega
and let
[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}
satisfy the weak Fatou property.
Then
X_{0}+X_{1}
is
A
-convex and the spaces
[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}
and
X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}
agree up to equivalence of quasi-norms.

Proof.

We took inspiration from the proof of the corollary in
[
18
, p. 242]
. In our proof of
X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}\subseteq[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}
above, separability is only used to prove that truncations also converge in
X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}
. If
[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}
satisfies the Fatou property, we can manage this inclusion differently.

As a preliminary result, we note that
f\in\mathrm{L}^{0}(\Omega)
belongs to some quasi-Banach function space
X
if and only if
\left|f\right|\in X
and in this case we have
\left\|f\right\|_{X}=\left\|\,\left|f\right|\,\right\|_{X}
. This follows by the lattice property, since
\left|f\right|\leq\left|\,\left|f\right|\,\right|
and
\left|\,\left|f\right|\,\right|\leq\left|f\right|
. Thus, to show that
X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}\subseteq[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}
and that the corresponding quasi-norm estimates hold, it suffices to consider absolute values.

Let
f\in X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}
. Again, we may assume
f\neq 0
. Then there exist
f_{j}\in X_{j}\setminus\{0\}
such that

\displaystyle\left|f\right|\leq\left|f_{0}\right|^{1-\theta}\left|f_{1}\right|^{\theta},\quad\left\|f_{0}\right\|_{X_{0}}^{1-\theta}\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{X_{1}}^{\theta}\leq 2\left\|f\right\|_{X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}}

and as in Step 2 of the proof of Theorem
1.1
we may assume that
\left|f\right|=\left|f_{0}\right|^{1-\theta}\left|f_{1}\right|^{\theta}
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
set
E_{n}\coloneqq\{\omega\in\Omega\,|\,\nicefrac{{1}}{{n}}\leq\left|f_{0}(\omega)\right|,\left|f_{1}(\omega)\right|\leq n\}
and define
g_{n}\coloneqq f\boldsymbol{1}_{E_{n}}
. The maps

\displaystyle F_{n}:\mathbb{S}\to X_{0}+X_{1},\qquad z\mapsto\left|\frac{f_{0}\boldsymbol{1}_{E_{n}}}{\left\|f_{0}\boldsymbol{1}_{E_{n}}\right\|_{X_{0}}}\right|^{1-z}\left|\frac{f_{1}\boldsymbol{1}_{E_{n}}}{\left\|f_{1}\boldsymbol{1}_{E_{n}}\right\|_{X_{1}}}\right|^{z}

are well-defined for large enough
n
and admissible by Lemma
3.4
and so
\left|g_{n}\right|\in[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}
with

\displaystyle\left\|\,\left|g_{n}\right|\,\right\|_{[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|F_{n}\right\|_{\mathcal{F}}\left\|f_{0}\boldsymbol{1}_{E_{n}}\right\|_{X_{0}}^{1-\theta}\left\|f_{1}\boldsymbol{1}_{E_{n}}\right\|_{X_{1}}^{\theta}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|f_{0}\right\|_{X_{0}}^{1-\theta}\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{X_{1}}^{\theta}

\displaystyle\leq 4\left\|f\right\|_{X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}},

where we have used (
3.1
) and the lattice property in the second step.
Since
\left|f\right|=\liminf_{n}\left|g_{n}\right|
holds with
\liminf_{n}\left\|\,\left|g_{n}\right|\,\right\|_{[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}}<\infty
, the weak Fatou property of
[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}
now implies that
\left|f\right|\in[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}
with

\displaystyle\left\|\,\left|f\right|\,\right\|_{[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}}\leq C\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left\|\,\left|g_{n}\right|\,\right\|_{[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}}\leq 4C\left\|f\right\|_{X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}},

where
C\geq 1
is the constant from the weak Fatou property of
[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}
. By the preliminary remark, we see that
f\in[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}
with
\left\|f\right\|_{[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}}\leq 4C\left\|f\right\|_{X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}}
.

The remainder of this proof now works as indicated in Section
5
below.
∎

5.
A glimpse at the other inclusion of the Calderón formula

In order to fully justify why separability is only used for one direction of Theorem
1.1
, we give a short overview on the ingredients of the proof of the inclusion
[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}\subseteq X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}
in
[
15
]
.

A motivation for the Banach case can be found in
[
18
, Chap. 4]
, where it is argued that admissible functions
F
are determined by their boundary values through the Poisson formula

(5.1)

\displaystyle F(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}F(\text{i}t)P_{0}(z,\text{i}t)\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\mathbb{R}}F(1+\text{i}t)P_{1}(z,1+\text{i}t)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\eqqcolon(1-\text{Re}(z))x_{0}+\text{Re}(z)x_{1},

where
P_{0}
and
P_{1}
denote the components of the Poisson kernel for
\overline{\mathbb{S}}
, while
x_{0}
and
x_{1}
define elements in
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
respectively.
The above equality can be turned into a product estimate of the form
\left|F(\theta)\right|\leq x_{0}^{1-\theta}x_{1}^{\theta}
a.e., showing
[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}\subseteq X_{0}^{1-\theta}X_{1}^{\theta}
with continuous inclusion due to the validity of the triangle inequality for
X_{j}
-valued integrals.

As a preliminary result in the quasi-Banach case, Kalton showed in
[
13
, Thm. 4.4]
and
[
12
, Thm. 2.2]
that for a quasi-Banach function space
X
, the property of
A
-convexity is equivalent to so-called
p
-convexity
, which means that there is
p\in(0,1]
and
C\geq 1
depending only on
p
such that for all
g_{0},\ldots,g_{n}\in X
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left|g_{k}\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\right\|_{X}\leq C\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left\|g_{k}\right\|_{X}^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

In
[
12
, Thm. 2.2]
, it is also shown that
X
is
p
-convex if and only if
X
is
q
-convex for all
q\in(0,p]
.

Straightforward estimates then show that
X_{0}+X_{1}
is
p
-convex
[
15
, Thm. 3.4]
.
In particular, we may assume
X_{0}
,
X_{1}
and
X_{0}+X_{1}
to be
p
-convex for the same range of parameters
q\in(0,p]
.

First, in
[
15
, Thm. 3.4]
it is used that
p
-convex quasi-Banach function spaces
X
admit the
p
-convexification

X^{p}\coloneqq\{f\in\mathrm{L}^{0}(\Omega)\,|\,\left|f\right|^{1/p}\in X\}
, which is endowed with
\left\|f\right\|_{X^{p}}\coloneqq\|\left|f\right|^{1/p}\|_{X}^{p}
and after renorming satisfies a triangle inequality
[
17
, Prop. 3.23]
, is a Banach function space.
The idea of the proof is then to mimic equation (
5.1
) for
\left|F\right|^{q}
, which – and this is a remarkable observation – can be turned back into an estimate for
\left|F\right|
via a limiting process as
q\to 0
.

Indeed, if
f=F(\theta)\in[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}
for some
F\in\mathcal{F}
, then
\left|F\right|^{q}
is Banach-valued for all
q\in(0,p]
but not analytic in general.
Using scalar-valued theory, it is shown in
[
15
, Thm. 3.4]
that
\left|F\right|^{q}
satisfies the sub-mean value formula

\displaystyle\left|F(z)(\,\cdot\,)\right|^{q}\leq\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|F(z+r\mathrm{e}^{\text{i}t})(\,\cdot\,)\right|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}t=\left[\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|F(z+r\mathrm{e}^{\text{i}t})\right|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}t\right](\,\cdot\,),

whenever
B_{r}(z)
is compactly contained in
\mathbb{S}
.
We stress that the above is an a.e. estimate on
\Omega
and that the right-hand side is a vector-valued Riemann integral. Interchanging the inner variable and the integral was a non-trivial consequence of analyticity of
F
, compare with
[
17
, Lem 7.5]
.

Applying a positive functional
\varphi
(of which there are plenty, because the dual space of
(X_{0}+X_{1})^{q}
contains a saturated Banach function space by virtue of the weak order unit, see
[
24
, Ch. 71, Thm. 4(a)]
and
[
19
, Prop. 2.5]
), yields a scalar-valued subharmonic function
\varphi(\left|F(\,\cdot\,)\right|^{q})
. Using the Poisson formula for subharmonic functions on
\overline{\mathbb{S}}
, it is shown in
[
15
, Thm. 3.4]
that

\displaystyle\varphi(\left|F(\theta)\right|^{q})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}P_{0}(\theta,\text{i}t)\,\varphi(\left|F(\text{i}t)\right|^{q})\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\mathbb{R}}P_{1}(\theta,1+\text{i}t)\,\varphi(\left|F(1+\text{i}t)\right|^{q})\,\mathrm{d}t.

Pulling
\varphi
out of the integrals by continuity and using positivity, yields

\displaystyle\left|F(\theta)\right|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}P_{0}(\theta,\text{i}t)\left|F(\text{i}t)\right|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\mathbb{R}}P_{1}(\theta,1+\text{i}t)\left|F(1+\text{i}t)\right|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\eqqcolon(1-\theta)f_{0}(q)^{q}+\theta f_{1}(q)^{q},

where the functions
f_{j}(q)^{q}
are defined as
X_{j}^{q}
-valued Riemann integrals. Interchanging inner variables and integrals is again a tedious task (see the proof of
[
17
, Thm. 7.1, Ch. 7.3]
for details), but it works and Jensen’s inequality yields
\left|F(\theta)\right|^{q}\leq(1-\theta)f_{0}(p)^{q}+\theta f_{1}(p)^{q}
almost everywhere.
By letting
q\to 0
, it is argued in
[
15
, Thm. 3.4]
that
\left|F(\theta)\right|\leq f_{0}(p)^{1-\theta}f_{1}(p)^{\theta}
almost everywhere. Finally, the triangle inequality for
X_{j}^{p}
-valued integrals (after renorming) yields

\displaystyle\left\|f_{j}(p)\right\|_{X_{j}}

\displaystyle=\left(\left\|\frac{1}{1-\theta}\int_{\mathbb{R}}P_{j}(\theta,j+\text{i}t)\left|F(j+\text{i}t)\right|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t\right\|_{(X_{j})^{p}}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{1-\theta}\int_{\mathbb{R}}P_{j}(\theta,j+\text{i}t)\left\|\left|F(j+\text{i}t)\right|^{p}\right\|_{(X_{j})^{p}}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|F\right\|_{\mathcal{F}}.

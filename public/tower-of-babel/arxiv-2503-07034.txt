Abstract




Abstract:
This paper is devoted to an optimal control problem of fully coupled forward-backward stochastic differential equations driven by sub-diffusion, whose solutions are not Markov processes. The stochastic maximum principle is obtained, where the control domain may not be convex and the diffusion term is independent of the control variable. Additionally, problem with state constraint is researched by using Ekeland’s variational principle. The theoretical results obtained are applied to a cash management optimization problem in bear market, and the optimal strategy is derived.

Keywords:
Forward-backward stochastic differential equation, stochastic optimal control, sub-diffusion, stochastic maximum principle

Introduction

In modern control theory, it is well known that Pontryagin’s maximum principle establishes a necessary condition for optimal controls (
[
4
]
). Since Pontryagin’s initial proof of the maximum principle in the deterministic situation, researchers have shown sustained interest in its stochastic counterpart. Significant advancements have been achieved in cases where the control domain is convex and the diffusion term is control-dependent, or in cases where the control domain may be non-convex, but the diffusion term is independent of control
[
13
,
9
,
3
,
1
]
. Notably, Bismut
[
3
]
introduced linear
backward stochastic differential equations
(BSDEs) by applying duality techniques. However, for non-convex control domains where the diffusion term is dependent of control, a general outcome remains unsolved due to the difference in order between Itô integral
\int_{E_{\varepsilon}}\sigma dB
(
\sqrt{\varepsilon}
order) and Lebesgue integral
\int_{E_{\varepsilon}}\sigma ds
(
\varepsilon
order). In 1990, Peng
[
18
]
used a second-order variation technique, introducing two adjoint equations to derive a general stochastic maximum principle.

In 1990, Pardoux and Peng
[
17
]
introduced the following nonlinear BSDE:

-dY(t)=f(t,Y(t),Z(t))dt-Z(t)dB_{t},\quad Y(T)=\xi.

Unlike forward
stochastic differential equations
(SDEs), BSDEs specify terminal conditions, with solutions in pairs
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot))
.
[
17
]
proved the existence and uniqueness of solutions under linear growth condition and Lipschitz condition. Since then, mathematicians and economists recognized BSDEs’ theoretical and practical significance in fields such as stochastic analysis, stochastic control, and financial mathematics (
[
6
,
19
,
8
,
5
]
).

A controlled
forward-backward stochastic differential equation
(FBSDE) consists of a forward equation and a backward equation:

\left\{\begin{aligned} dX(t)&=b(t,X(t),Y(t),Z(t),v(t))dt+\sigma(t,X(t),Y(t),Z(t),v(t))dB_{t},\\
dY(t)&=-f(t,X(t),Y(t),Z(t),v(t))dt+Z(t)dB_{t},\\
X(0)&=x_{0},\quad Y(T)=\phi(X(T)),\end{aligned}\right.

with solution triple
(X(\cdot),Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot))
, where
v(\cdot)
is the control. Researchers have extensively studied the stochastic maximum principle for controlled FBSDEs. Peng first derived a stochastic maximum principle for partially coupled FBSDEs under convex control domains
[
19
]
. Xu
[
26
]
extended this result to partially coupled FBSDEs with general control domains provided that the diffusion term does not include the control variable. Wu
[
25
]
considered general maximum principle for partially coupled FBSDEs, by treating
Z
as one of control variables. Hu
[
10
]
introduced a new variation technique to deal with
z
, to obtained a general stochastic maximum principle with recursive utilities. Hu and Peng
[
12
]
and Peng and Wu
[
20
]
introduced monotonicity conditions to establish the existence and uniqueness of fully coupled FBSDEs. Based on these results, Wu
[
24
]
demonstrated a stochastic maximum principle for fully coupled FBSDEs under convex control domains. Shi and Wu
[
22
]
extended the maximum principle to general control domains where the forward equation’s diffusion term is independent of control. Yong
[
27
]
obtained a general optimality variational principle for controlled fully coupled FBSDEs with mixed initial-terminal conditions. Hu, Ji and Xue
[
11
]
identified a new variation term in
Z
,
\langle p(t),\delta\sigma(t)\rangle I_{E_{\varepsilon}}(t)
, enabling the derivation of a general stochastic maximum principle for fully coupled FBSDEs. Scholars have also studied stochastic maximum principles under various conditions, such as state constraints, partial information and partial observation
[
21
,
15
,
23
,
14
]
.

As Zhang and Chen
[
30
]
pointed out that, compared to Brownian motion, anomalous sub-diffusion more clearly and accurately describes the characteristics of inactive market trading.
Suppose that
S_{t}
is a subordinator with drift
\kappa>0
and Lévy measure
\nu
; that is,
S_{t}=\kappa t+S_{t}^{0}
, where
S_{t}^{0}
is a driftless subordinator with Lévy measure
\nu
.
Let
L_{t}:=\inf\{r>0:S_{r}>t\}
,
t\geq 0
, be the inverse of
S
. The inverse subordinator
L_{t}
is continuous in
t
but stays constant during infinitely many time periods which result from the infinitely many jumps by the subordinator
S_{t}
when its Lévy measure is non-trivial.
B_{t}
is the standard Brownian motion on
\mathbb{R}
, and is independent of
B_{t}
. When the Lévy measure
\nu
of the subordinator
S_{t}
is infinite,

S_{t}
has infinitely many small jumps in any given time interval, and for any
\epsilon>0
, the jumps of
S_{t}
of size larger than
\epsilon
occur according to a Poisson process with parameter
\nu(\epsilon,\infty)
.
In this case, during any fixed time intervals,
L_{t}
has infinitely many small time periods but only finite many time periods larger than a fixed length during which it stays constants. Thus the sub-diffusion
B_{L_{t}}
matches well the phenomena that the market constantly has small corrections but big corrections or bear market occurs only sporadically.

Research on the Pontryagin’s maximum principle of stochastic optimal problems driven by sub-diffusion remains limited, mainly due to the non-Markovian nature of this process.
Zhang and Chen
[
30
]
established the existence and uniqueness of solutions for BSDEs driven by sub-diffusion. Using convex and spike variation methods, they derived stochastic maximum principles for both convex and non-convex control domains and sufficient ones for general and convex control domains. Additionally, they get the existence and uniqueness for the solution of fully coupled FBSDEs driven by sub-diffusion and the stochastic maximum principle with convex control domain
[
29
,
31
]
.

In this paper, we study the general stochastic maximum principle for fully coupled FBSDEs driven by sub-diffusion for which the diffusion term is independent of control.
In Section 2, we begin by outlining the fundamental properties of the sub-diffusion and proceed to introduce the stochastic optimal control problem driven by sub-diffusion. In Section 3, we obtain the stochastic maximum principle for FBSDEs driven by sub-diffusion, which is one of the main results in this paper. In Section 4, we consider the state constraints and obtain the stochastic maximum principle by Ekeland’s variational principle. In Section 5, theoretical results are used to model cash management optimization in bear markets and the optimal strategy are obtained. Some concluding remarks are given in Section 6.

2.2
Existence and uniqueness for solutions of FBSDEs driven by sub-diffusion

Considering the following fully coupled FBSDE driven by sub-diffusion:

\left\{\begin{aligned} dx(t)&=b(t,x(t),y(t))dt+\delta(t,x(t),y(t),z(t))d{L_{(t-a)+}}+\sigma(t,x(t),y(t),z(t))dB_{L_{(t-a)+}},\\
-dy(t)&=f(t,x(t),y(t))dt+h(t,x(t),y(t),z(t))d{L_{(t-a)+}}-z(t)dB_{L_{(t-a)+}},\\
x(0)&=x_{0},\quad y(T)=\phi(x(T)),\end{aligned}\right.

(2.4)

whose solution triple is
(x(\cdot),y(\cdot),z(\cdot))\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
. Without loss of generality, let
0\leqslant a<T
.
b,\delta,\sigma,f
and
h
are all
\mathcal{F}_{t}^{\prime}
-adapted stochastic processes.
\phi
is a square-integrable
\mathcal{F}_{T}^{\prime}
-measurable random variable. We need the following hypothesis.

Assumption 2.1
.

i)

are all uniformly Lipschitz continuous over their domains, and

\mathbb{E}\left[b^{2}(0)+\sigma^{2}(0)+f^{2}(0)+\delta^{2}(0)+h^{2}(0)+\phi^{2}(0)\right]<\infty.

ii) Monotonicity condition: There exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
t>0
,
x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},

y_{2},z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\big(b(t,x_{1},y_{1})-b(t,x_{2},y_{2})\big)(y_{1}-y_{2})-\big(f(t,x_{1},y_{1})-f(t,x_{2},y_{2})\big)(x_{1}-x_{2})

\displaystyle\leqslant-C\big(|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{2}+|y_{1}-y_{2}|^{2}\big),

and

\displaystyle\big(\sigma(t,x_{1},y_{1},z_{1})-\sigma(t,x_{2},y_{2},z_{2})\big)(z_{1}-z_{2})+\big(\delta(t,x_{1},y_{1},z_{1})-\delta(t,x_{2},y_{2},z_{2})\big)(y_{1}-y_{2})

\displaystyle-\big(h(t,x_{1},y_{1},z_{1})-h(t,x_{2},y_{2},z_{2})\big)(x_{1}-x_{2})

\displaystyle\leqslant-C\big(|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{2}+|y_{1}-y_{2}|^{2}+|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2}\big).

iii)
\phi(\cdot)
is a non-decreasing function.

Conditions ii) and iii) can be replaced by the following:

ii’) Monotonicity condition: There exists a constant

\displaystyle\big(b(t,x_{1},y_{1})-b(t,x_{2},y_{2})\big)(y_{1}-y_{2})-\big(f(t,x_{1},y_{1})-f(t,x_{2},y_{2})\big)(x_{1}-x_{2})

\displaystyle\geqslant C\big(|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{2}+|y_{1}-y_{2}|^{2}\big),

and

\displaystyle\big(\sigma(t,x_{1},y_{1},z_{1})-\sigma(t,x_{2},y_{2},z_{2})\big)(z_{1}-z_{2})+\big(\delta(t,x_{1},y_{1},z_{1})-\delta(t,x_{2},y_{2},z_{2})\big)(y_{1}-y_{2})

\displaystyle\geqslant C\big(|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{2}+|y_{1}-y_{2}|^{2}+|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2}\big).

Definition 2.1

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{a}[0,T]

\displaystyle:=\Big\{(x(\cdot),y(\cdot),z(\cdot)):(x(\cdot),y(\cdot),z(\cdot))\text{ is an adapted process defined}

2.3
Stochastic optimal control problem for FBSDEs driven by sub-diffusion

Let
U
be a set on
\mathbb{R}
. We denote the quintuple
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},X,v)
as
\mathcal{U}_{a}[0,T]
if it satisfies:

i)
\widetilde{X}_{\cdot}
(defined in Lemma
2.2
) is a Markov process with initial value
\widetilde{X}_{0}=(0,a)
.

ii)
v(t,\omega)
is a
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-progressively measurable stochastic process and
\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}v(t)^{2}\,dt<\infty
. If
v(t,\omega)
is a
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{t}
-progressively measurable stochastic process, we denote
v(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}_{a}^{\prime}[0,T]
. Since
\mathcal{F}_{t}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{F}_{t}
, it follows that
\mathcal{U}_{a}^{\prime}[0,T]\subset\mathcal{U}_{a}[0,T]
. We call
\mathcal{U}_{a}^{\prime}[0,T]
the admissible control set.

Given
v(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}_{a}^{\prime}[0,T]
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, consider the controlled FBSDE driven by sub-diffusion:

\left\{\begin{aligned} dx^{v}(t)&=b(t,x^{v}(t),y^{v}(t),v(t))+\sigma(t,x^{v}(t),y^{v}(t),z^{v}(t))dB_{L_{(t-a)+}},\\
-dy^{v}(t)&=f(t,x^{v}(t),y^{v}(t),v(t))dt-z^{v}(t)dB_{L_{(t-a)+}},\\
x^{v}(0)&=x_{0},\quad y^{v}(T)=\phi(x^{v}(T)),\end{aligned}\right.

(2.5)

where

(x^{v}(\cdot),y^{v}(\cdot),z^{v}(\cdot))\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R},\quad x_{0}\in\mathbb{R},

b:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times U\to\mathbb{R},

\sigma:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R},\quad g:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times U\to\mathbb{R},\quad\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}.

The cost functional defined on
\mathcal{U}_{a}^{\prime}[0,T]
is

J(v(\cdot))=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}g(t,x^{v}(t),y^{v}(t),v(t))dt+h(x^{v}(T))+\gamma(y^{v}(0))\right],

(2.6)

where

g:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times U\to\mathbb{R},\quad h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R},\quad\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}.

The goal of the problem is to find an admissible control
u(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}_{a}^{\prime}[0,T]
such that

J(u(\cdot))=\inf_{v(\cdot)\in\,\mathcal{U}_{a}^{\prime}[0,T]}J(v(\cdot)).

We need the following assumption.

Assumption 2.2
.

i)
b,\sigma,f,g,h,\gamma,\phi
are continuously differentiable with respect to their variables, their partial derivatives are bounded and Lipschitz continuous. The minimum values of these Lipschitz constants and bounds are denoted by some constant
L
.

ii)
b,\sigma,f
satisfy the monotonicity condition of Assumption
2.1
ii).

iii)
\phi
is a non-decreasing function.

From Theorem
2.1
, it follows that for all
v(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}_{a}^{\prime}[0,T]
, the FBSDE (
2.5
) admits a unique adapted solution
(x^{v}(\cdot),y^{v}(\cdot),z^{v}(\cdot))\in\mathcal{M}_{a}[0,T]
.

We assume that the control domain
U
can be non-convex, so
\mathcal{U}_{a}^{\prime}[0,T]
can also be non-convex. To address this, we adopt a spike variation method (
[
18

(x(\cdot),y(\cdot),z(\cdot))\equiv(x^{u}(\cdot),y^{u}(\cdot),z^{u}(\cdot))

u^{\varepsilon}(t):={u}(t)1_{E_{\varepsilon}^{c}}(t)+v(t)1_{E_{\varepsilon}}(t),\quad\text{for }t\in[0,T].

Here,

u^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}_{a}^{\prime}[0,T]

Now we introduce the variational equation:

\left\{\begin{aligned} dx_{1}(t)&=\left[b_{x}x_{1}(t)+b_{y}y_{1}(t)+b(u^{\varepsilon}(t))-b(u(t))\right]dt\\
&\quad+\left[\sigma_{x}x_{1}(t)+\sigma_{y}y_{1}(t)+\sigma_{z}z_{1}(t)\right]dB_{L_{(t-a)+}},\\
-dy_{1}(t)&=\left[f_{x}x_{1}(t)+f_{y}y_{1}(t)+f(u^{\varepsilon}(t))-f(u(t))\right]dt-z_{1}(t)dB_{L_{(t-a)+}},\\
x_{1}(0)&=0,\quad y_{1}(T)=\phi_{x}(x(T))x_{1}(T),\end{aligned}\right.

where

\displaystyle b(u^{\varepsilon}(t))

\displaystyle\equiv b(t,x^{\varepsilon}(t),y^{\varepsilon}(t),v^{\varepsilon}(t)),\quad b(u(t))\equiv b(t,x(t),y(t),u(t)),

\displaystyle b_{x}

\displaystyle\equiv b_{x}(t,x(t),y(t),u(t)),\quad b_{y}\equiv b_{y}(t,x(t),y(t),u(t)),\quad\text{for }b=b,f,g,

Given

\xi_{2}\nabla b(t,x,y)\cdot(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})-\xi_{1}\nabla f(t,x,y)\cdot(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\leqslant-C(\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}).

Proof.

\displaystyle x_{1}(T)y_{1}(T)=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}x_{1}\Big\{\left[f_{x}x_{1}+f_{y}y_{1}+f(u^{\varepsilon})-f(u)\right]dt-z_{1}dB_{L_{(t-a)+}}\Big\}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}z_{1}\left(\sigma_{x}x_{1}+\sigma_{y}y_{1}+\sigma_{z}z_{1}\right)dL_{(t-a)+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left[y_{1}\left(b_{x}x_{1}+b_{y}y_{1}\right)-x_{1}\left(f_{x}x_{1}+f_{y}y_{1}\right)\right]dt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\left[y_{1}\left(b(u^{\varepsilon})-b(u)\right)-x_{1}\left(f(u^{\varepsilon})-f(u)\right)\right]dt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}z_{1}\left[{\sigma}_{x}x_{1}+\sigma_{y}y_{1}+\sigma_{z}z_{1}\right]dL_{(t-a)+}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\left[y_{1}\left(\sigma_{x}x_{1}+\sigma_{y}y_{1}+\sigma_{z}z_{1}\right)+x_{1}\left(f_{x}x_{1}+f_{y}y_{1}\right)\right]dB_{L_{(t-a)+}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle-C\int_{0}^{T}\left[x_{1}^{2}dt+y_{1}^{2}dt+z_{1}^{2}dL_{(t-a)+}\right]

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\left[y_{1}\left(b(u^{\varepsilon})-b(u)\right)-x_{1}\left(f(u^{\varepsilon})-f(u)\right)\right]dt.

Taking expectations on both sides, considering the martingale property of sub-diffusion, we obtain

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\left(x_{1}^{2}+y_{1}^{2}\right)dt+z_{1}dL_{(t-a)+}\Big]+\mathbb{E}\left[\phi_{x}\left(x(T)\right)x_{1}(T)^{2}\right]

(3.4)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left[y_{1}\left(b(u^{\varepsilon})-b(u)\right)-x_{1}\left(f(u^{\varepsilon})-f(u)\right)\right]dt

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left[\frac{1}{C}\left(b(u^{\varepsilon})-b(u)\right)^{2}+\frac{C}{4}y_{1}^{2}\right]dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left[\frac{1}{C}\left(f(u^{\varepsilon})-f(u)\right)^{2}+\frac{C}{4}x_{1}^{2}\right]dt.

The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Proof.

Integrating the BSDE in (

) over the interval

, we get

\displaystyle y_{1}(t)+\int_{t}^{T}z_{1}(s)dB_{L_{(s-a)+}}=\phi_{x}(x(T))x_{1}(T)+\int_{t}^{T}\left(f_{x}x_{1}+f_{y}y_{1}+f(u^{\epsilon})-f(u)\right)ds.

(3.5)

Noting that

\mathbb{E}\left[y_{1}(t)\int_{t}^{T}z_{1}(s)dB_{L_{(s-a)+}}\right]=0,\quad\text{for any }t\in[0,T].

Taking expectations after squaring both sides of (

3.2
, by Itô’s isometry, there exists some constant

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|y_{1}(t)|^{2}+\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\left(z_{1}(s)\right)^{2}dL_{(s-a)+}

\displaystyle\leqslant 4\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\phi_{x}(x(T))x_{1}(T)\right)^{2}+\int_{t}^{T}\Big[(Lx_{1})^{2}+(Ly_{1})^{2}+(f(u^{\epsilon})-f(u))^{2}\Big]ds\right]

Integrating the forward SDE in (

\displaystyle y_{1}(t)^{2}

\displaystyle+4\left(\int_{0}^{t}z_{1}dB_{L_{(s-a)+}}\right)^{2}+4\left(\int_{0}^{T}z_{1}dB_{L_{(s-a)+}}\right)^{2}.

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}y_{1}(t)^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 4\mathbb{E}\left(\phi_{x}(x(T))x_{1}(T)\right)^{2}+C_{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left(x_{1}^{2}+y_{1}^{2}+\left(f(u^{\epsilon})-f(u)\right)^{2}\right)ds

Lemma 3.5

Proposition 3.1

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left[g_{x}x_{1}(t)+g_{y}y_{1}(t)+g(u^{\varepsilon}(t))-g(u(t))\right]dt

\displaystyle 0\leqslant

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left[g(t,x+x_{1},y+y_{1},u^{\varepsilon})-g(u)\right]dt+\mathbb{E}\left[h(x(T)+x_{1}(T))-h(x(T))\right]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left[g(t,x+x_{1},y+y_{1},u)-g(u)\right]dt

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left[g_{x}x_{1}(t)+g_{y}y_{1}(t)+g(u^{\varepsilon})-g(u)\right]dt

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left[(g_{x}(u^{\varepsilon})-g_{x})x_{1}+(g_{y}(u^{\varepsilon})-g_{y})y_{1}\right]dt

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left[h_{x}(x(T))x_{1}(T)\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\gamma_{y}(y(0))y_{1}(0)\right]+o(\varepsilon),

where we have denoted
g_{x}(u^{\varepsilon}):=g_{x}(t,x,y,u^{\varepsilon})
,
g_{y}(u^{\varepsilon}):=g_{y}(t,x,y,u^{\varepsilon})
.

Since

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left[(g_{x}(u^{\varepsilon})-g_{x})x_{1}+(g_{y}(u^{\varepsilon})-g_{y})y_{1}\right]dt

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{T}(g_{x}(u^{\varepsilon})-g_{x})dt\right)^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\left|x_{1}(t)\right|^{2}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{T}(g_{y}(u^{\varepsilon})-g_{y})dt\right)^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\left|y_{1}(t)\right|^{2}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}=o(\varepsilon).

we have

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left[g_{x}x_{1}+g_{y}y_{1}+g(u^{\varepsilon})-g(u)\right]dt+\mathbb{E}\left[h_{x}(x(T))x_{1}(T)\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\gamma_{y}(y(0))y_{1}(0)\right]\geqslant o(\varepsilon).

Thus, (
3.6
) holds.
∎

To establish the stochastic maximum principle, we introduce the following adjoint equation:

\left\{\begin{aligned} dp(t)&=\left[f_{y}p(t)-b_{y}q(t)-g_{y}\right]dt-\sigma_{y}k(t)dL_{(t-a)+}-\sigma_{z}k(t)dB_{L_{(t-a)+}},\\
-dq(t)&=\left[-f_{x}p(t)+b_{x}q(t)+g_{x}\right]dt+\sigma_{x}k(t)dL_{(t-a)+}-k(t)B_{L_{(t-a)+}},\\
p(0)&=-\gamma_{y}(y(0)),\quad q(T)=-\phi_{x}(x(T))p(T)+h_{x}(x(T)).\end{aligned}\right.

(3.7)

From Theorem
2.1
, there exists a unique adapted solution
(p(\cdot),q(\cdot),k(\cdot))\in\mathcal{M}_{a}[0,T]
to (
3.7
). Define the Hamiltonian function as follows:

H(t,x,y,z,v,p,q):=q(t)b(t,x,y,v)-p(t)f(t,x,y,v)+g(t,x,y,v).

The next theorem provides the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
u(\cdot)
be an optimal control,
(x(\cdot),y(\cdot),z(\cdot))
the corresponding solution to the FBSDE with sub-diffusion (
2.5
), and
(p(\cdot),q(\cdot),k(\cdot))
the solution to the adjoint equation (
3.7
). Then for any
v(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}_{a}^{\prime}[0,T]
, we have

H(t,x(t),y(t),z(t),v(t),p(t),q(t))\geqslant H(t,x(t),y(t),z(t),u(t),p(t),q(t)),\ a.e.\ t\in[0,T],\ a.s..

(3.8)

Proof.

By applying Ito’s formula to
p(\cdot)y_{1}(\cdot)+q(\cdot)x_{1}(\cdot)
over the interval
[0,T]
, we get

\displaystyle p(T)y_{1}(T)+q(T)x_{1}(T)-p(0)y_{1}(0)-q(0)x_{1}(0)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\Big\{-p\left[f_{x}x_{1}+f_{y}y_{1}+f(u^{\varepsilon})-f(u)\right]dt+\left[f_{y}p-b_{y}q-g_{y}\right]y_{1}dt

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\left[k\sigma_{y}y_{1}+k\sigma_{z}z_{1}\right]dL_{(t-a)+}\Big\}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{T}\Big\{\left[b_{x}x_{1}+b_{y}y_{1}+b(u^{\varepsilon})-b(u)\right]qdt+\left[f_{x}p-b_{x}q-g_{x}\right]x_{1}dt

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\left[k\sigma_{y}y_{1}+k\sigma_{z}z_{1}\right]dL_{(t-a)+}\Big\}+\text{martingale}.

By substituting the adjoint equation (
3.7
) and taking expectations on both sides:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[h_{x}(x(T))x_{1}(T)+\gamma_{y}(y(0))y_{1}(0)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big\{-p\left[f(u^{\varepsilon})-f(u)\right]+q\left[b(u^{\varepsilon})-b(u)\right]-(g_{x}x_{1}+g_{y}y_{1})\Big\}dt.

By Proposition
3.1
, we have

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big\{-p\left[f(u^{\varepsilon})-f(u)\right]+q\left[b(u^{\varepsilon})-b(u)\right]+g(u^{\varepsilon})-g(u)\Big\}dt\geqslant o(\varepsilon).

Combining this with the definition of the Hamiltonian, and letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, (
3.8
) holds.
∎

4
Stochastic maximum principle for FBSDEs driven by sub-diffusion with state constraints

In practical problems, it is common to encounter situations with state constraints. This section is concerned with the stochastic maximum principle for FBSDEs driven by sub-diffusion with state constraints.

For the readers’ convenience, we first introduce the following Ekeland’s variational principle, which can be seen, for example, in Yong and Zhou
[
28
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
V
be a complete metric space, and let
f
be a lower semi-continuous function defined on
V
, i.e., for any
v\in V
,
\varliminf\limits_{v\to v_{0}}f(v)\geqslant f(v_{0})
. Suppose
\inf\limits_{v\in V}f(v)>-\infty
. For any given
\varepsilon\geqslant 0
, there exists
u\in V
such that
f(u)\leqslant\inf\limits_{v\in V}f(v)+\varepsilon
, then there exists, for any
\lambda\geqslant 0
, a point
u^{\varepsilon}\in V
such that:

\displaystyle\mathrm{i})\quad f(u^{\varepsilon})\leqslant f(u),

\displaystyle\mathrm{ii})\quad d(u^{\varepsilon},u)\leqslant\lambda,

\displaystyle\mathrm{iii})\quad f(u^{\varepsilon})\leqslant f(w)+\frac{\varepsilon}{\lambda}d(w,u^{\varepsilon}),\quad\forall w\in V.

Now, we introduce a stochastic optimal control problem of FBSDEs with sub-diffusion with state constraints. Considering the following controlled forward-backward stochastic system:

\left\{\begin{aligned} dx^{v}(t)&=b(t,x^{v}(t),y^{v}(t),v(t))dt+\sigma(t,x^{v}(t),y^{v}(t),z^{v}(t))dB_{L_{(t-a)+}},\\
-dy^{v}(t)&=f(t,x^{v}(t),y^{v}(t),v(t))dt-z^{v}(t)dB_{L_{(t-a)+}},\\
x^{v}(0)&=x_{0},\quad y^{v}(T)=\phi(x^{v}(T)),\end{aligned}\right.

(4.1)

where

(x^{v}(\cdot),y^{v}(\cdot),z^{v}(\cdot))\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R},\quad x_{0}\in\mathbb{R},

b:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times U\to\mathbb{R},\quad\sigma:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R},

g:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times U\to\mathbb{R},\quad\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}.

Unlike the previous section, here we assume that
\{v(t)\mid t\in[0,T],v(t)\in\mathcal{U}_{a}^{\prime}[0,T]\}
is a bounded closed set, and the state variables
x^{v}(T)
and
y^{v}(0)
satisfy the following constraints:

\mathbb{E}[G_{1}(x^{v}(T))]=0,\quad\mathbb{E}[G_{0}(y^{v}(0))]=0,

(4.2)

where
G_{1}
and
G_{0}
are continuously differentiable functions from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{R}
with bounded derivatives.

The cost functional defined on
\mathcal{U}_{a}^{\prime}[0,T]
is

J(v(\cdot))=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}g\big(t,x^{v}(t),y^{v}(t),v(t)\big)dt+h\big(x^{v}(T)\big)+\gamma\big(y^{v}(0)\big)\right],

where
g:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times U\to\mathbb{R}
,
h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, and
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
.

The goal in this section is to find an admissible control
u(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}_{a}^{\prime}[0,T]
such that

J(u(\cdot))=\inf_{v(\cdot)\in\,\mathcal{U}_{a}^{\prime}[0,T]}J(v(\cdot)).

In order to apply Lemma
4.1
, we define a metric on the control domain
\mathcal{U}_{a}^{\prime}[0,T]
as follows:

d(v_{1}(\cdot),v_{2}(\cdot)):=\mathbb{E}\left[\mu\{t\in[0,T]\mid v_{1}(t)\neq v_{2}(t)\}\right],\quad\forall v_{1}(\cdot),v_{2}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}_{a}^{\prime}[0,T],

where
\mu
denotes the Lebesgue measure. It can be shown that under this metric,
\mathcal{U}_{a}^{\prime}[0,T]
is complete.

Let
u(\cdot)
be an optimal control, and
(x(\cdot),y(\cdot),z(\cdot))\equiv(x^{u}(\cdot),y^{u}(\cdot),z^{u}(\cdot))\in\mathcal{M}_{a}[0,T]
be the corresponding solution to (
4.1
). For any
v(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}_{a}^{\prime}[0,T]
, define

J_{\rho}(v(\cdot)):=\left\{|\mathbb{E}[G_{1}(x^{v}(T))]|^{2}+|\mathbb{E}[G_{0}(y^{v}(0))]|^{2}+[J(v(\cdot))-J(u(\cdot))+\rho]^{2}\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Obviously

J_{\rho}(u(\cdot))\leqslant\inf_{v(\cdot)\in\,\mathcal{U}_{a}^{\prime}[0,T]}J_{\rho}(v(\cdot))+\rho.

Thus, by Lemma
4.1
, we obtain the existence of
u_{\rho}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}_{a}^{\prime}[0,T]
such that:

\displaystyle\mathrm{i})\quad J_{\rho}(u_{\rho}(\cdot))\leqslant J_{\rho}(u(\cdot))=\rho,

\displaystyle\mathrm{ii})\quad d(u_{\rho}(\cdot),u(\cdot))\leqslant\sqrt{\rho},

\displaystyle\mathrm{iii})\quad J_{\rho}(u_{\rho}(\cdot))\leqslant J_{\rho}(w(\cdot))+\sqrt{\rho}\,d(u_{\rho}(\cdot),w(\cdot)),\quad\forall w(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}_{a}^{\prime}[0,T].

We denote the solution to (
4.1
) with respect to
u_{\rho}(\cdot)
as
(x_{\rho}(\cdot),y_{\rho}(\cdot),z_{\rho}(\cdot))
.
Considering a spike variation
u_{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
of
u_{\rho}(\cdot)
defined by

u_{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(t):=u_{\rho}(t)1_{E_{\varepsilon}^{c}}(t)+v(t)1_{E_{\varepsilon}}(t),\quad t\in[0,T],

and let
(x_{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot),y_{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot),z_{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot))
be the solution to (
4.1
) with respect to
u_{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
. Then we obtain from the above that

J_{\rho}(u_{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot))-J_{\rho}(u_{\rho}(\cdot))+\sqrt{\rho}\varepsilon\geqslant 0.

Now we introduce the variational equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} dx_{\rho}^{1}(t)&=\left[b_{x,\rho}x_{\rho}^{1}(t)+b_{y,\rho}y_{\rho}^{1}(t)+b(u_{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(t))-b(u_{\rho}(t))\right]dt\\
&\quad+\left[\sigma_{x,\rho}x_{\rho}^{1}(t)+\sigma_{y,\rho}y_{\rho}^{1}(t)+\sigma_{z,\rho}z_{\rho}^{1}(t)\right]dB_{L_{(t-a)+}},\\
-dy_{\rho}^{1}(t)&=\left[f_{x,\rho}x_{\rho}^{1}(t)+f_{y,\rho}y_{\rho}^{1}(t)+f(u_{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(t))-f(u_{\rho}(t))\right]dt-z_{\rho}^{1}(t)dB_{L_{(t-a)+}},\\
x_{\rho}^{1}(0)&=0,\quad y_{\rho}^{1}(t)=\phi_{x}(x_{\rho}(T))x_{\rho}^{1}(T),\end{aligned}\right.

(4.3)

where

\displaystyle b(u_{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(t))

\displaystyle\equiv b(t,x_{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(t),y_{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(t),v_{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(t)),\quad b(u_{\rho}(t))\equiv b(t,x_{\rho}(t),y_{\rho}(t),u_{\rho}(t)),

\displaystyle b_{x,\rho}

\displaystyle\equiv b_{x}(t,x_{\rho}(t),y_{\rho}(t),u_{\rho}(t)),\quad b_{y,\rho}\equiv b_{y}(t,x_{\rho}(t),y_{\rho}(t),u_{\rho}(t)),\quad\text{for }b=b,f,g,

and

\sigma_{x,\rho}\equiv\sigma_{x}(t,x_{\rho}(t),y_{\rho}(t),z_{\rho}(t)),\quad\sigma_{y,\rho}\equiv\sigma_{y}(t,x_{\rho}(t),y_{\rho}(t),z_{\rho}(t)),\quad\sigma_{z,\rho}\equiv\sigma_{z}(t,x_{\rho}(t),y_{\rho}(t),z_{\rho}(t)).

It can similarly be shown that (
4.3
) satisfies Assumption
2.2
, hence it has a unique adapted solution
(x_{\rho}^{1}(\cdot),y_{\rho}^{1}(\cdot),z_{\rho}^{1}(\cdot))\in\mathcal{M}_{a}[0,T]
. Using the same methods from Lemmas
3.2
to
3.5
in Section 3, we can ontain the following result.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let Assumption
2.2
hold. Then the following inequalities hold:

\begin{gathered}\sup\limits_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\left[\mathbb{E}\left|x^{\varepsilon}_{\rho}(t)-x_{\rho}(t)-x^{1}_{\rho}(t)\right|^{2}\right]\leqslant C_{\varepsilon}\varepsilon^{2},\\
\sup\limits_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\left[\mathbb{E}\left|y^{\varepsilon}_{\rho}(t)-y_{\rho}(t)-y^{1}_{\rho}(t)\right|^{2}\right]\leqslant C_{\varepsilon}\varepsilon^{2},\\
\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|z^{\varepsilon}_{\rho}(t)-z_{\rho}(t)-z_{\rho}^{1}(t)|^{2}dL_{(t-a)+}\leqslant C_{\varepsilon}\varepsilon^{2},\end{gathered}

where
\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}C_{\varepsilon}=0
.

From this result, we can derive the following variational inequality.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let Assumption
2.2
hold. Then there exist non-zero constants
\psi_{1}
,
\psi_{2}
, and
\psi_{3}
, such that the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\psi_{1}\mathbb{E}\left[G_{1x}(x(T))x_{1}(T)\right]+\psi_{2}\mathbb{E}\left[G_{0y}(y(0))y_{1}(0)\right]

(4.4)

\displaystyle+\psi_{3}\left(\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left[g_{x}x_{1}+g_{y}y_{1}+g(u^{\varepsilon})-g(u)\right]dt\right.

\displaystyle+\ \mathbb{E}[h_{x}(x(T))x_{1}(T)]+\mathbb{E}[\gamma_{y}(y(0))y_{1}(0)]\bigg)\geqslant o(\varepsilon).

where
(x_{1}(\cdot),y_{1}(\cdot),z_{2}(\cdot))
satisfies (
3.1
).

Proof.

Since

J_{\rho}(u_{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot))-J_{\rho}(u_{\rho}(\cdot))+\sqrt{\rho}\varepsilon\geqslant 0,

we have

\frac{J_{\rho}^{2}(u_{\rho}^{\epsilon}(\cdot))-J_{\rho}^{2}(u_{\rho}(\cdot))}{J_{\rho}(u_{\rho}^{\epsilon}(\cdot))+J_{\rho}(u_{\rho}(\cdot))}+\sqrt{\rho}\varepsilon\geqslant 0.

According to Proposition
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle J_{\rho}^{2}(u_{\rho}^{\epsilon}(\cdot))-J_{\rho}^{2}(u_{\rho}(\cdot))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{|\mathbb{E}[G_{1}(x_{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(T))]|^{2}-|\mathbb{E}[G_{1}(x_{\rho}(T))]|^{2}\right\}+\left\{|\mathbb{E}[G_{0}(y_{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(0))]|^{2}-|\mathbb{E}[G_{0}(y_{\rho}(0))]|^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle+\left\{[J(u_{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot))-J(u(\cdot))+\rho]^{2}-[J(u_{\rho}(\cdot))-J(u(\cdot))+\rho]^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}\left[G_{1}(x_{\rho}(T))\right]\mathbb{E}\left[G_{1x}(x_{\rho}(T))x_{\rho}^{1}(T)\right]+2\mathbb{E}\left[G_{0}(y_{\rho}(0))\right]\mathbb{E}\left[G_{0y}(y_{\rho}(0))y_{\rho}^{1}(0)\right]

\displaystyle+2[J(u_{\rho}(\cdot))-J(u(\cdot))+\rho]\left\{\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left[g_{x,\rho}x_{\rho}^{1}(t)+g_{y,\rho}y_{\rho}^{1}(t)+g(u_{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(t))-g(u_{\rho}(t))\right]dt\right.

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left[h_{x}(x_{\rho}(T))x_{\rho}^{1}(T)\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\gamma_{y}(y_{\rho}(0))y_{\rho}^{1}(0)\right]\bigg\}+o(\varepsilon).

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{J_{\rho}^{2}(u_{\rho}^{\epsilon}(\cdot))-J_{\rho}^{2}(u_{\rho}(\cdot))}{J_{\rho}(u_{\rho}^{\epsilon}(\cdot))+J_{\rho}(u_{\rho}(\cdot))}=

\displaystyle\psi^{\varepsilon}_{\rho 1}\mathbb{E}\left[G_{1x}(x_{\rho}(T))x_{\rho}^{1}(T)\right]+\psi^{\varepsilon}_{\rho 2}\mathbb{E}\left[G_{0y}(y_{\rho}(0))y_{\rho}^{1}(0)\right]

(4.5)

\displaystyle+\psi^{\varepsilon}_{\rho 3}\left\{\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left[g_{x,\rho}x_{\rho}^{1}(t)+g_{y,\rho}y_{\rho}^{1}(t)+g(u_{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(t))-g(u_{\rho}(t))\right]dt\right.

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left[h_{x}(x_{\rho}(T))x_{\rho}^{1}(T)\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\gamma_{y}(y_{\rho}(0))y_{\rho}^{1}(0)\right]\bigg\},

where

\psi_{\rho 1}^{\varepsilon}:=\frac{2\mathbb{E}[G_{1}(x_{\rho}(T))]}{J_{\rho}(u_{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot))+J_{\rho}(u_{\rho}(\cdot))},\ \psi_{\rho 2}^{\varepsilon}:=\frac{2\mathbb{E}[G_{0}(y_{\rho}(0))]}{J_{\rho}(u_{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot))+J_{\rho}(u_{\rho}(\cdot))},\ \psi_{\rho 3}^{\varepsilon}:=\frac{2\left[J(u_{\rho}(\cdot))-J(u(\cdot))+\rho\right]}{J_{\rho}(u_{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot))+J_{\rho}(u_{\rho}(\cdot))}.

Since

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}J_{\rho}(u^{\varepsilon}_{\rho}(\cdot))=J_{\rho}(u_{\rho}(\cdot)),

then

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left(|\psi_{\rho 1}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+|\psi_{\rho 2}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+|\psi_{\rho 3}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)=1.

Therefore, there exists a convergent subsequence (still denoted as)
(\psi_{\rho 1}^{\varepsilon},\psi_{\rho 2}^{\varepsilon},\psi_{\rho 3}^{\varepsilon})\to(\psi_{\rho 1},\psi_{\rho 2},\\
\psi_{\rho 3})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, such that

|\psi_{\rho 1}|^{2}+|\psi_{\rho 2}|^{2}+|\psi_{\rho 3}|^{2}=1.

Similarly, there exists a convergent subsequence (still denoted as)
(\psi_{\rho 1},\psi_{\rho 2},\psi_{\rho 3})\to(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3})
as
\rho\to 0
,
satisfying

|\psi_{1}|^{2}+|\psi_{2}|^{2}+|\psi_{3}|^{2}=1.

Since
u_{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)\to u^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
and
u_{\rho}\to u(\cdot)
as
\rho\to 0
, by the continuous dependence of parameters of the solution to FBSDE with sub-diffusion (Lemma 4.2 in Zhang and Chen
[
31
]
), we have

x_{\rho}^{1}(\cdot)\to x_{1}(\cdot),\quad y_{\rho}^{1}(\cdot)\to y_{1}(\cdot),\quad z_{\rho}^{1}(\cdot)\to z_{1}(\cdot),

x_{\rho}(\cdot)\to x(\cdot),\quad y_{\rho}(\cdot)\to y(\cdot),\quad z_{\rho}(\cdot)\to z(\cdot),\quad\text{in }\mathcal{M}_{a}[0,T].

Substituting these results into (
4.5
) and letting
\rho\to 0
yields

\displaystyle\frac{J_{\rho}^{2}(u_{\rho}^{\epsilon}(\cdot))-J_{\rho}^{2}(u_{\rho}(\cdot))}{J_{\rho}(u_{\rho}^{\epsilon}(\cdot))+J_{\rho}(u_{\rho}(\cdot))}=

\displaystyle\psi_{1}\mathbb{E}[G_{1x}(x(T))x_{1}(T)]+\psi_{2}\mathbb{E}[G_{0y}(y(0))y_{1}(0)]

\displaystyle+\psi_{3}\left\{\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left[g_{x}x_{1}+g_{y}y_{1}(t)+g(u^{\varepsilon})-g(u)\right]dt\right.

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}[h_{x}(x(T))x_{1}(T)]+\mathbb{E}[\gamma_{y}(y(0))y_{1}(0)]\bigg\}\geqslant o(\varepsilon).

Thus, (
4.4
) holds.
∎

Next, we introduce the adjoint equation:

\left\{\begin{aligned} dp(t)&=[f_{y}p(t)-b_{y}q(t)-\psi_{3}g_{y}]dt-\sigma_{y}k(t)d{L_{(t-a)+}}-\sigma_{z}k(t)dB_{L_{(t-a)+}},\\
-dq(t)&=[-f_{x}p(t)+b_{x}q(t)+\psi_{3}g_{x}]dt+\sigma_{x}k(t)d{L_{(t-a)+}}-k(t)dB_{L_{(t-a)+}},\\
p(0)&=-G_{0y}(y(0))\psi_{2}-\gamma_{y}y(0)\psi_{3},\\
q(T)&=G_{1x}(x(T))\psi_{1}-\phi_{x}(x(T))p(T)+h_{x}(x(T))\psi_{3}.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.6)

Similarly, by Theorem
2.1
, there exists a unique adapted solution
(p(\cdot),q(\cdot),k(\cdot))\in\mathcal{M}_{a}[0,T]
to (
4.6
). Define the Hamiltonian:

H(t,x,y,z,v,p,q):=q(t)b(t,x,y,v)-p(t)f(t,x,y,v)+g(t,x,y,v).

Now, we present the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\ u(\cdot)
be an optimal control and
(x(\cdot),y(\cdot),z(\cdot))
the corresponding solution to the FBSDE with sub-diffusion (
4.1
). Then there exist three constants
\psi_{1}
,
\psi_{2}
,
\psi_{3}
, not all zero, such that
(p(\cdot),q(\cdot),k(\cdot))
is the solution to the adjoint equation (
4.6
). Moreover, for any
v(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}_{a}^{\prime}[0,T]
, the following inequality holds:

H(t,x(t),y(t),z(t),v(t),p(t),q(t))\geqslant H(t,x(t),y(t),z(t),u(t),p(t),q(t)),\ a.e.\ t\in[0,T],\ a.s..

(4.7)

Proof.

Applying Ito’s formula to
p(\cdot)y_{1}(\cdot)+q(\cdot)x_{1}(\cdot)
on the interval
[0,T]
, we have

\displaystyle p(T)y_{1}(T)+q(T)x_{1}(T)-p(0)y_{1}(0)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\Big\{-p\left[f_{x}x_{1}+f_{y}y_{1}+f(u^{\varepsilon})-f(u)\right]\Big\}dt+\int_{0}^{T}\left[f_{y}p-b_{y}q-\psi_{3}g_{y}\right]y_{1}dt

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\left[k(t)\sigma_{y}y_{1}+k(t)\sigma_{z}z_{1}\right]d{L_{(t-a)+}}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\left[b_{x}x^{1}+b_{y}y^{1}+b(u^{\varepsilon})-b(u)\right]qdt+\int_{0}^{T}\left[f_{x}p-b_{x}q-\psi_{3}g_{x}\right]x_{1}dt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}[k\sigma_{y}y_{1}+k\sigma_{z}z_{1}]d{L_{(t-a)+}}+\text{martingale}.

Substituting into the adjoint equation (
4.6
) and taking expectations on both sides, it yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[y_{1}(0)G_{0y}(y(0))\psi_{2}+y_{1}(0)\gamma_{y}y(0)\psi_{3}+x_{1}(T)G_{1x}(x(T))\psi_{1}+x_{1}(T)h_{x}(x(T))\psi_{3}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big\{-p\left[f(u^{\varepsilon})-f(u)\right]+q\left[b(u^{\varepsilon})-b(u)\right]-\psi_{3}(g_{x}x^{1}+g_{y}y^{1})\Big\}dt.

Using the variational inequality (
4.4
), we get

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big\{-p\left[f(u^{\varepsilon})-f(u)\right]+q\left[b(u^{\varepsilon})-b(u)\right]+g(u^{\varepsilon})-g(u)\Big\}dt\geqslant o(\varepsilon).

By the definition of the Hamiltonian, let
\varepsilon\to 0
, (
4.7
) holds.
∎

In particular, when
G_{1}=G_{2}=0,\psi_{1}=\psi_{2}=0
and
\psi_{3}=1
, the result of Theorem
4.1
reduces to that of Theorem
3.1
.

5
Applications to cash management problem in bear markets

In this section, we apply preceding theoretical results of stochastic maximum principles of FBSDEs driven by sub-diffusion, to model cash management optimization in bear markets. While FBSDEs have been widely applied to cash management optimization in active markets (for example, Bensoussan et al.
[
2
]
), research on their applications to bear markets remains limited. In fact, in bear markets with infrequent trading, traditional stochastic modeling methods cannot accurately represent market dynamics. To address this, we use FBSDEs driven by sub-diffusion to model bear market conditions.

Consider the following FBSDE driven by sub-diffusion:

\left\{\begin{aligned} dx^{v}(t)&=\left(-m_{1}x^{v}(t)-n_{1}y^{v}(t)+c_{1}v(t)\right)dt-n_{2}z^{v}(t)dB_{L_{(t-a)+}},\\
-dy^{v}(t)&=\left(-m_{1}y^{v}(t)+m_{2}x^{v}(t)+c_{2}v(t)\right)dt-z^{v}(t)dB_{L_{(t-a)+}},\\
x^{v}(0)&=x_{0},\quad y^{v}(T)=ax^{v}(T).\end{aligned}\right.

(5.1)

In this model,
x^{v}(t)
represents the agent’s cash flow at time
t
,
v(t)
represents the agent’s investment and withdrawal decisions at time
t
,
y^{v}(t)
represents the recursive utility of
v(t)
at time
t
, and
z^{v}(t)
represents the volatility of the utility. Due to the significant mutual influence between cash flow and recursive utility, this FBSDE (
5.1
) is fully coupled, and
x^{v}(\cdot)
and
y^{v}(\cdot)
exhibit a negative correlation. The cost functional is

J(v(\cdot)):=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}(v(t)-l(t))^{2}dt-y^{v}(0)\right],

(5.2)

where
l(\cdot)
is a deterministic bounded function.

The objective is to find
u(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}_{a}^{\prime}[0,T]
such that

J(u(\cdot))=\inf_{v(\cdot)\in\,\mathcal{U}_{a}^{\prime}[0,T]}J(v(\cdot)).

The cost functional indicates that the agent aims to maximize recursive utility while minimizing investment risk to achieve a given investment objective.

Here,
a\geqslant 0,m_{i}\geqslant 0,n_{i}\geqslant 0\,(i=1,2)
. Based on Assumption
2.2
and Theorem
2.1
, for any
v(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}_{a}^{\prime}[0,T]
, equation (
5.1
) admits a unique adapted solution
(x^{v}(\cdot),y^{v}(\cdot)),z^{v}(\cdot))\in\mathcal{M}_{a}[0,T]
.

We apply the stochastic maximum principle obtained in Section 3 (Theorem
3.1
), to solve this problem. In this case,

b(t,x,y,v)=-m_{1}x-n_{1}y+c_{1}v,\quad\sigma(t,x,y,z)=-n_{2}z,\quad f(t,x,y,v)=-m_{1}y+m_{2}x+c_{2}v,

\phi(x)=ax,\quad g(t,x,y,v)=\frac{(v-l(t))^{2}}{2},\quad\gamma(y)=-y,\quad h(x)=0.

Thus, the adjoint equation (
3.7
) writes

\left\{\begin{aligned} dp(t)&=[-m_{1}p(t)+n_{1}q(t)]dt+n_{2}k(t)dB_{L_{(t-a)+}},\\
-dq(t)&=[-m_{2}p(t)-m_{1}q(t)]dt-k(t)B_{L_{(t-a)+}},\\
p(0)&=1,\quad q(T)=-ap(T),\end{aligned}\right.

(5.3)

and the Hamiltonian is

H(t,x,y,z,v,p,q)=q(-m_{1}x-n_{1}y+c_{1}v)-p(-m_{1}y+m_{2}x+c_{2}v)+\frac{(v-l(t))^{2}}{2}.

(5.4)

By (
3.8
), the optimal control
u(\cdot)
satisfies

u(t)=-c_{1}q(t)+c_{2}p(t)+l(t),\ a.e.\ t\in[0,T],\ a.s..

(5.5)

Since Theorem
2.1
gives necessary conditions for the optimal control, but not sufficient ones, we cannot directly conclude that
u(\cdot)
is really optimal. Next, we verify that
u(t)
of (
5.5
) is indeed the optimal strategy. Let
v(\cdot)
be any strategy in
\mathcal{U}_{a}^{\prime}[0,T]
, and as before, denote the solution to (
5.1
) associated with
v(\cdot)
as
(x^{v}(\cdot),y^{v}(\cdot),z^{v}(\cdot))
and the solution associated with
u(\cdot)
as
(x(\cdot),y(\cdot),z(\cdot))
. We define

\tilde{x}(t):=x^{v}(t)-x(t),\quad\tilde{y}(t):=y^{v}(t)-y(t),\quad\tilde{z}(t):=z^{v}(t)-z(t),\quad\tilde{v}(t):=v(t)-u(t).

Applying Itô’s formula to
\tilde{x}(\cdot)q(\cdot)+\tilde{y}(\cdot)p(\cdot)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\tilde{y}(0)]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big\{-p(t)[-m_{1}\tilde{y}(t)+m_{2}\tilde{x}(t)+c_{2}\tilde{v}(t)]+q(t)[-m_{1}\tilde{x}(t)-n_{1}\tilde{y}(t)+c_{1}\tilde{v}(t)]

\displaystyle+\tilde{y}(t)[-m_{1}p(t)+n_{1}q(t)]-\tilde{x}(t)[-m_{2}p(t)-m_{1}q(t)]\Big\}dt

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}[-c_{2}p(t)+c_{1}q(t)]\tilde{v}(t)dt.

Noticing that

\mathbb{E}[\tilde{y}(0)]=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}(l(t)-u(t))(u(t)-v(t))dt.

Thus

\displaystyle J(v(\cdot))-J(u(\cdot))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\Big[(v(t)-l(t))^{2}-(u(t)-l(t))^{2}\Big]dt-\widetilde{y}(0)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\frac{(v(t)-l(t))^{2}-(u(t)-l(t))^{2}}{2}-(l(t)-u(t))(u(t)-v(t))\Big]dt\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\frac{(v(t)-u(t))^{2}}{2}{d}t\right]\geqslant 0.

Therefore,
u(\cdot)
is indeed the optimal strategy.

6
Concluding remarks

In this paper, optimal control problems of FBSDEs driven by sub-diffusion are studied. The stochastic maximum principle is obtained, where the diffusion coefficient
\sigma
is independent of control variable and the control domain may not be convex. Furthermore, the case with initial and terminal constraints state constraints is also addressed, by employing Ekeland’s variational principle. Theoretical results are applied to a cash management problem, where the optimal strategy is derived explicitly.

When the diffusion coefficient
\sigma
depends on the control, but the control domain is required to be a convex set, the stochastic maximum principle has been established in
[
31
]
through convex variation. However, for the case where the diffusion coefficient
\sigma
is dependent of control and the control domain may not be convex, the corresponding maximum principle still need to be researched. It can be anticipated that its proof would require the introduction of second-order variational equations and two adjoint equations (See
[
18
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
for standard diffusions’ cases). Furthermore, the results can be extended to mean-field type systems, systems with random jumps, partial information, and differential games. For constrained cases, the constraint conditions could also be modified by incorporating integral type constraints. We will consider these topic in the near future.

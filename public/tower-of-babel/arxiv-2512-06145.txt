2.2.
Primitive isometries into
\mathbf{L}
(see
[
16
]
)

Consider a non-degenerate even lattice
N
and extend the bilinear form to a

\mathbb{Q}
-valued form on
N\otimes\mathbb{Q}
.
The dual group
N^{\vee}
can be identified with
the subgroup

N^{\vee}=\bigl\{x\in N\otimes\mathbb{Q}\bigm|\text{$x\cdot y\in\mathbb{Z}$ for all $y\in N$}\bigr\}\supset N,

and the
discriminant group

(denoted
q_{N}
in
[
16
]
) is defined as the finite quotient

\mathop{\operator@font discr}\nolimits N:=N^{\vee}\!/N.

This group is equipped with a
(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
-valued symmetric bilinear
form and its
(\mathbb{Q}/2\mathbb{Z})
-valued quadratic extension:

(x\bmod N)\otimes(y\bmod N)\mapsto x\cdot y\bmod\mathbb{Z},\quad x\bmod N\mapsto x^{2}\bmod 2\mathbb{Z}.

We often abbreviate

\mathcal{N}:=\mathop{\operator@font discr}\nolimits N\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{N}_{p}:=\mathop{\operator@font discr}\nolimits_{p}N:=\mathcal{N}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}\ \text{for a prime~$p$}

and use the shorthand

[\![\xi]\!]:=\xi\bmod N\in\mathcal{N}

for the discriminant class of a vector
\xi\in N^{\vee}
.
We also use the notation
\langle x\rangle\subset\mathcal{N}
for the subgroup generated by

x\in\mathcal{N}
, and we abbreviate
\langle\xi\rangle:=\langle[\![\xi]\!]\rangle
for
\xi\in N^{\vee}
.

Obviously,
\mathcal{N}=\bigoplus_{p}\mathcal{N}_{p}
over all primes
p
.
The discriminant form on
\mathcal{N}
is non-degenerate in
the sense that the map

\mathcal{N}\to\mathop{\operator@font Hom}\nolimits(\mathcal{N},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}),\quad x\mapsto(y\mapsto x\cdot y\bmod\mathbb{Z}),

is an isomorphism.
Besides, one has
\mathopen{|}\mathop{\operator@font discr}\nolimits N\mathclose{|}=\mathopen{|}\det N\mathclose{|}
.

Remark 2.5
.

To find the group
\mathop{\operator@font discr}\nolimits_{p}N
, we
indicate a subset
therein,
whereupon it suffices to
compute the Gram matrix
G
and check that

\nu_{p}(\det G\cdot\det N)=0

(

the non-degeneracy would imply that the set is a basis.

For

, we distinguish between

(
i.e.

for each element

of order

) and

(otherwise) forms. In other

\mathcal{N}_{2}
is odd if and only if it has an element of square

\pm\frac{1}{2}\bmod 2\mathbb{Z}
(necessarily of order

and necessarily an orthogonal

The
length

\ell(\mathcal{N})
of a finite abelian group

is the minimal
number of generators. We usually describe a discriminant form by its

Gram matrix

in a minimal basis

are defined modulo

, whereas all

\mathbb{Z}
.

If

or

and
\mathcal{N}_{2}
is even, one can speak about the

determinant

\det\mathcal{N}_{p}
:

\mathopen{|}\mathcal{N}_{p}\mathclose{|}\det\mathcal{N}_{p}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times\!},\quad\text{well defined modulo $(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times\!})^{2}$}.

In
[
16
]
, this is defined as the determinant (divided by
\mathopen{|}\mathcal{N}_{p}\mathclose{|}^{2}
,
which is irrelevant modulo squares) of the only

-lattice

such that

\mathop{\operator@font rk}\nolimits K_{p}=\ell(\mathcal{N}_{p}),\quad\mathop{\operator@font discr}\nolimits K_{p}\simeq\mathcal{N}_{p}.

(Such a lattice
exists and,
unless

and
\mathcal{N}_{2}
is odd,
is unique up to isomorphism.)
Alternatively, this is just the determinant of the
Gram matrix in a carefully chosen minimal basis for
\mathcal{N}_{2}
:
e.g.

suffices to require that any two basis elements of distinct orders must be
orthogonal. Indeed, such a basis immediately gives us

: we merely change

,

, in the matrix.

If

is odd, then

is only defined modulo

making it redundant in

The determinant is multiplicative,

\det(\mathcal{N}\oplus\mathcal{M})=(\det\mathcal{N})(\det\mathcal{M})
.
Each non-degenerate finite
quadratic form splits into orthogonal direct sum of the following elementary
blocks: for a prime

\mathcal{W}=\biggl[\frac{2m}{p^{k}}\biggr],\quad\text{$\mathop{\operator@font g.c.d.}\nolimits(m,p)=1$ and only
$p^{k}\det\mathcal{W}=\biggl(\frac{2m}{p}\biggr)\in\{\pm 1\}$ matters},

and for

\mathcal{U}_{2^{k}}:=\begin{bmatrix}0&1/2^{k}\\
1/2^{k}&0\end{bmatrix},\qquad\mathcal{V}_{2^{k}}:=\begin{bmatrix}1/2^{k-1}&1/2^{k}\\
1/2^{k}&1/2^{k-1}\end{bmatrix};

we have

\phantom{-}3\bmod 8,&\text{if $\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{V}_{2^{k}}$}.\end{cases}.

Lemma 2.12

Let

Pick a parabolic component

.

2.4.
Degree

-surfaces

A

{2n}

-surface is a pair
(X,h)
, where

is a
K3
-surface and

is a very ample divisor,

;

cf.

[
3
,
20
,
17
]
.
Usually, the polarization

is considered part of the structure and omitted
from the notation.

We imitate
[
3
,
20
]
(
cf.
also
[
11
,
21
]
)
and define the Fano graph of smooth rational degree

set

\operatorname{Fn}^{d}(X,h):=\{\text{smooth rational curves $C\subset X$ with $C\cdot h=d$}\},

where two vertices
C,C^{\prime}
are connected by an edge of multiplicity

C\cdot C^{\prime}
.
We focus on curves of a fixed degree
d
;
hence, contrary to

[
11
,
20
]
, we treat

as a plain graph;
in other words, we assign a fixed color

to all vertices.

We say that the Néron–Severi lattice

is

spanned by degree

if it is
a finite index extension
of its sublattice generated by the classes of

smooth rational
degree
d
curves on
X

and the polarization

.

Torelli theorem for
K3
-surfaces and the theory of lattice-polarized
K3
-surfaces immediately yield the following version of

[
11
, Theorem 3.9]

Theorem 2.18
.

Let
d>0
and

n>4
be fixed integers. A graph
\Gamma
is geometric in
degree
{2n}
with respect to
d
if and only if

\Gamma\simeq\operatorname{Fn}^{d}(X,h)
for a
degree
{2n}
polarized
K3
-surface
(X,h)

such that
\operatorname{NS}(X)
is spanned by
degree
d
curves.
∎

Remark 2.19
.

If
d>1
,
the realizability criterion for a graph of degree
d
curves

must
appeal to curves of lower degree. For example, famous Schur’s
quartic
[
22
]
has (obviously admissible) Néron–Severi lattice
spanned by conics, but most of these conics are reducible,
see
[
9
]
.
In the parabolic context, see
4.22
below.

In particular, to lift the condition that
\operatorname{NS}(X)
should be spanned by

-curves, one would have to consider the full Fano graph of smooth rational
curves of degree up to

,
cf.

2.8
and
(

) below.
Clearly, the requirement that the graph should be

-geometric is necessary for its realizability in any case.

Theorem 2.18
justifies the fact
that, once

and

have been fixed, one can
identify geometric graphs
with configurations of smooth rational degree

K3
-surfaces. In
particular, when we speak of a

(resp. its

),
we mean a parabolic
graph (resp. connected parabolic subgraph).

4.1.

In general,

•

•

\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl O}}\nolimits_{\hbar}\bigl(\mathfrak{G}_{{2n}}^{d}(\Gamma)\bigr)=\mathop{\operator@font stab}\nolimits\hbar\subset\mathop{\operator@font\text{\sl O}}\nolimits_{h}\bigl(\mathfrak{F}_{{2n}}^{d}(\Gamma)\bigr);

the orthogonal complements

2.15
) and exceptional classes/orbits (

2.16

The splitting (

)

) reads

\mathop{\operator@font discr}\nolimits_{p}\mathfrak{F}_{{2n}}^{d}(\Gamma)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}\langle\alpha_{i}\rangle\oplus\mathcal{N}_{p},\quad\alpha_{i}^{2}=\frac{p-1}{p}\bmod 2\mathbb{Z}.

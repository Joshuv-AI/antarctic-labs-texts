Temporal Trends in Hearing Aid Utilization in Korean Adults: A KNHANES-Based Study (2010–2022)
Authors: Sang-Yoon Han, Hee Won Seo, Seung Hwan Lee, Jae Ho Chung
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHearing loss (HL) is a prevalent public health issue that affects communication and quality of life. Hearing aids (HAs) are an effective rehabilitation tool, yet adoption rates remain low. To improve accessibility, South Korea revised its HA financial support policy in 2015. This study evaluates changes in HA adoption rates over the past decade and examines the impact of these policy modifications.MethodsThis study analyzed data from 9,994 (2020–2022) and 10,019 (2010–2012) individuals aged ≥ 40 years from the Korea National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey. HL was categorized based on pure-tone average thresholds: mild (26–39 dB HL), unsupported disabling (≥ 40 dB HL but not meeting financial aid criteria), lowest-grade hearing disability (≥ 40 dB HL in the better ear and ≥ 80 dB HL in the worse ear), and supported disabling (≥ 60 dB HL, eligible for financial aid). HA adoption rates were compared between the survey periods.ResultsThe overall HA adoption rate among individuals with HL significantly increased from 2.12% (2010–2012) to 4.73% (2020–2022) (P < 0.001). This increase was observed across all HL severities (mild: 0.30% → 0.64%; unsupported disabling: 4.23% → 9.42%; lowest-grade hearing disability group: 4.24% → 16.29%; supported disabling: 23.06% → 53.52%; all P < 0.001). The highest HA adoption rates were seen in individuals in their 70s, and the supported disabling HL group had adoption rates 5–6 times higher than the unsupported group. Multivariable analysis confirmed a significant association between increased HA adoption and survey period (adjusted odds ratio, 3.70, P < 0.001).ConclusionHA adoption rates have nearly doubled over the past decade, with the greatest increases observed among financially supported individuals and those in their 70s. Expanding financial support eligibility may further enhance HA adoption, particularly among those with disabling but unsupported HL, ensuring broader access to hearing rehabilitation.Keywords: Hearing Loss, Hearing Aids, Government Subsidy, Aged

INTRODUCTION

Hearing loss (HL) is a common age-related sensory impairment that impacts overall health and quality of life. It has been linked to various functional impairments, including cognitive decline, depression, social isolation, reduced physical activity, and nutritional deficiencies.1,2,3,4,5,6

Recent studies have postulated a causal relationship between HL and these comorbidities, highlighting the importance of timely intervention.3,4,5,7 Hearing rehabilitation plays a crucial role in addressing the negative consequences of HL. Evidence suggests that the use of hearing aids (HAs) can improve emotional well-being, social engagement, and overall health outcomes.1,3,4,5,8 Furthermore, studies have demonstrated an association between HA use and improved physical activity levels and nutritional status.4,8,9,10 Recently, Lin et al.5 reported that HA use may help prevent cognitive decline associated with untreated HL. These findings underscore the necessity of effective hearing rehabilitation strategies.

HAs are a non-invasive and widely accessible intervention for HL.2 However, unlike eyeglasses, HA adoption rates remain consistently low worldwide.11 Barriers to HA use include insufficient auditory benefit, discomfort, high cost, and social stigma.12,13,14 Moreover, coexisting otologic symptoms (e.g., tinnitus, dizziness) and demographic factors influence HA adoption and continued use.15

To increase HA adoption rates and compensate for functional impairments, financial support policies have been implemented in various countries, including South Korea.11 In 1997, South Korea introduced an HA subsidy of approximately $200, which was later increased to $300 in 2005. In 2015, the subsidy was further raised to $1,000, covering the cost of basic HAs for eligible individuals.11 Despite these policy changes, limited research has evaluated their effectiveness in increasing HA adoption rates.

The present study aimed to compare HA adoption rates before and after the 2015 financial support policy revision, using survey data from the general South Korean population. Furthermore, it aimed analyze factors associated with HA use, including demographic characteristics and otologic symptoms, to provide a comprehensive understanding of the impact of financial assistance on HA adoption.

METHODS

Database

The Korea National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (KNHANES) is a nationally representative survey conducted by the Korean Disease Control and Prevention Agency (KDCA) since 1998 to assess the health and nutritional status of the non-institutionalized South Korean population. Since 2007, the survey has been conducted annually, incorporating audiological assessments in selected cycles. This study utilized data from the fifth KNHANES (2010–2012) and the eighth and ninth KNHANES (2020–2022), in which hearing evaluations were included. The sampling method differed across survey cycles. In the fifth KNHANES, a two-stage stratified sampling method was employed, with initial stratification based on provinces, followed by classification by gender, age, house price, and residential area. In the eighth and ninth KNHANES, the first stratification was based on administrative divisions and housing type, followed by internal stratification considering sex, age, residential floor area ratio, and educational level of household head.

Inclusion of subjects

This study included data from the eighth and ninth KNHANES (2020–2022) and the fifth KNHANES (2010–2012). Among 11,365 participants aged ≥ 40 years in 2020–2022, 9,994 individuals with complete data on age, sex, household income (quintile), HA usage, and air-conduction hearing thresholds, and with a normal tympanic membranes and type A tympanograms, and without a history of cochlear implantation, were included in the analysis (Fig. 1).

KNHANES = Korea National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey, TM = tympanic membrane.

Similarly, among 11,729 individuals aged ≥ 40 years in 2010–2012, 10,019 participants were selected based on the availability of household income, HA use, and air-conduction hearing thresholds at 0.5, 1, 2, and 4 kHz. Participants included in the study had normal tympanic membranes confirmed by endoscopic examination (Fig. 1).

Otological and audiological evaluation in the eighth, ninth, and fifth KNHANES

In the fifth KNHANES (2010–2012), endoscopic examination by trained otorhinolaryngology residents was used to assess tympanic membranes. Pure-tone audiometry (PTA) was conducted using an automatic audiometer (GSI SA-203; Entomed Diagnostics AB, Svedala, Sweden), measuring hearing thresholds at 0.5, 1, 2, 4, and 6 kHz. In the eighth and ninth KNHANES (2020–2022), middle ear status and tympanic membrane condition were assessed using tympanometry with an Titan IMP440 screener (Interacoustics, Middelfart, Denmark). PTA was conducted in a double-walled soundproof booth using an AD629 audiometer (Interacoustics), which complies with International Organization for Standardization standards. Hearing thresholds were measured at 0.5, 1, 2, 4, and 8 kHz.16

Participants with missing audiometric data at 0.5, 1, 2, or 4 kHz were excluded from the study. Cases with scale-out values or incomplete audiometry were also excluded. Otologic symptoms, including self-perceived HL (rated on a 1–4 Likert scale, with 1 indicating no discomfort and 4 indicating inability to hear), tinnitus, and dizziness, were assessed through surveys. Participants who responded “cannot remember” to otologic symptom history were classified as having no history of otologic symptoms.

Analysis of hearing-aid-associated otologic factors

Since a previous study showed that increased hearing threshold, severe perceived HL, bothersome tinnitus, and balance problems were associated with HA usage.15 Given that audiometric thresholds were measured using different methods in the fifth and eighth/ninth KNHANES, this study compared perceived HL severity, tinnitus severity, and dizziness prevalence as potential predictors of HA adoption (Fig. 1).

A multivariable analysis was conducted to adjust for these factors when evaluating differences in HA adoption rates between the two time periods.

Age-binned analysis of HA adoption

To assess trends in HA adoption over time, participant ages were stratified into 10-year groups (e.g., 40s, 50s, 60s, 70+). HA adoption rates each age group in 2020–2022 and 2010–2012, were compared as a function of HL severity.

Classification of HL

HL severity was classified based on the World Health Organization criteria and South Korea’s hearing disability grading system, which determines eligibility for social support, including financial aid for HAs.11,17 In South Korea, individuals with a bilateral hearing threshold of ≥ 60 dB HL qualify for government financial support. Based on these criteria, HL was categorized into three groups: mild HL, defined as a better-ear PTA of 26–39 dB HL; unsupported disabling HL, which included individuals with PTA ≥ 40 dB HL but who did not meet either the 60 dB threshold in the better ear or the 80 dB threshold in the worse ear required for financial support; and supported disabling HL, referring to individuals with a PTA of ≥ 60 dB HL, who are eligible for financial assistance. The changes in HA usage rates over time were analyzed within these HL categories to evaluate the impact of financial support policies on HA adoption.

In addition, individuals who had a hearing threshold ≥ 40 dB in the better ear and ≥ 80 dB in the worse ear but exhibited less severe HL in the better ear compared to the supported disabling HL group were eligible for financial support. These individuals were classified as the lowest-grade hearing disability group and were analyzed only for overall changes over the 10 years due to the limited number of subjects in each age bin.

Statistical analysis

Categorical and continuous variables were compared between the 2020–2022 and 2010–2012 cohorts using the χ2 test and t-test, respectively. Variables showing significant differences were further analyzed using logistic regression models to adjust for potential confounders. To estimate annual HA adoption rates, sampling weights were applied to the three-year datasets (eighth/ninth KNHANES and fifth KNHANES), dividing participant weights by three. All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics 25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Ethics statement

The eighth, ninth, and fifth KNHANES were performed in accordance with the guidelines provided by the KDCA and its previous form, the Korean Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (KCDC), respectively. The eighth, ninth, and fifth KNHANES were conducted after receiving approval from the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of KDCA or KCDC (IRB No. 2018-01-03-4C-A, 2018-01-03-2C-A, 2018-01-03-5C-A, 2012-01EXP-01-2C, 2011-02CON-06-C, 2010-02CON-21-C). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants before their participation in the survey and audiological evaluations.

RESULTS

Demographic and otologic characteristics

The mean age of the subjects was 59.91 ± 11.72 in 2020–2022, significantly older than the mean age in 2010–2012 (57.91 ± 11.33, P < 0.001). The proportion of males was 42.64% (n = 4,262) and that of females was 57.35% (n = 5,732) in 2020–2022, which was not significantly different from 2010–2012 (males: 43.27%, n = 4,335; females: 56.73%, n = 5,684) (P = 0.374). The frequencies of tinnitus and dizziness differed between the decades (P < 0.001 for all) (Table 1). They are representative of 23,308,019 individuals over 40 (male: 11,148,760, female: 12,159,259) in 2020 to 2022 and 15,655,626 (male: 7,591,630, female: 8,063,996) in 2010 to 2012.

Changes in HA adoption rates, and factors associated with HA use

The unweighted HA adoption rate among HL patients was 5.41% in 2020–2022, significantly higher than in 2010–2012 (2.76%; odds ratio [OR], 2.01; P < 0.001).

The association between HA usage and demographic or otologic factors was analyzed for the periods 2020–2022 and 2010–2012. Age (P < 0.001 for both), sex (P < 0.001 for 2020–2022, P = 0.019 for 2010–2012), household income (P < 0.001 for both), subjective HL (P < 0.001 for both), and tinnitus (P < 0.001 for both) were all associated with HA usage in both periods, while dizziness was linked to HA usage only in 2020–2022 (P = 0.030 for 2020–2022, P = 0.066 for 2010–2012) (Table 2).

Changes in the factors significantly associated with HA adoption rate were also analyzed. The subjective degree of HL was 1.19 ± 0.47 (Likert scale) in 2020–2022, significantly lower than in 2010–2012 (mean Likert scale, 1.20 ± 0.48; P < 0.001). The prevalence of tinnitus in 2020–2022 (10.38%) was again lower than in 2010–2012 (23.42%, P < 0.001), and the prevalence of dizziness was 30.61% in 2020–2022, also higher than in 2010–2012 (20.45%, P < 0.001) (Table 1).

Binary logistic regression analysis was conducted to evaluate differences in HA adoption rates between 2020–2022 and 2010–2012 after adjusting significantly different covariates. Subjective degree of HL was excluded from the analysis since worsening hearing thresholds lead to increases in perceived HL. In the multivariable analysis, age (adjusted OR [aOR], 1.04; P = 0.001), hearing thresholds (aOR, 1.11; P < 0.001), tinnitus (aOR, 2.18; P < 0.001), and years of survey (aOR, 3.70; P < 0.001) were significantly associated with HA adoption rate after adjusting for age, sex, household income, hearing threshold, tinnitus, dizziness, and survey period (Table 3).

The same analysis was conducted for patients with HL and those with disabling HL. After adjustment, hearing threshold, tinnitus, and survey period were strongly linked to the HA adoption rate in both the HL group (hearing thresholds: aOR, 1.10; P < 0.001, tinnitus: aOR, 1.84; P < 0.001, year of survey: aOR, 3.57; P < 0.001) and the disabling HL group (hearing thresholds: aOR, 1.07; P < 0.001, tinnitus: aOR, 1.74; P = 0.002, year of survey: aOR, 3.34; P < 0.001), while age was only associated with HA adoption rate in patients with HL (aOR, 1.03; P = 0.021) (Table 3).

Comparison of weighted HA use before and after revision of the HA provision policy

To assess and represent the one-year HA adoption rates of the general Korean middle-aged and older population, participant weights were averaged by dividing them by three and applied in the statistical analysis, as detailed in the "statistical analysis" section of the Methods. The HA adoption rates in the middle-aged to older populations, as well as among individuals, were analyzed in each HL category and age group. Individuals with mild HL, those with unsupported disabling HL, the lowest-grade hearing disability group, and those with supported disabling HL exhibited higher adoption rates across all age groups (mild HL: 0.64%, unsupported disabling HL: 9.42%, lowest-grade hearing disability group: 16.29%, supported disabling HL: 53.52%, overall HL: 4.73% in middle-aged to older individuals) in 2020–2022 compared to those in 2010–2012 (mild HL: 0.30%; OR, 2.09; P < 0.001, unsupported disabling HL: 4.23%; OR, 2.36; P < 0.001, lowest-grade hearing disability group: 4.24%; OR, 4.39; P < 0.001, supported disabling HL: 23.06%; OR, 3.84; P < 0.001, overall HL: 2.12%; OR, 2.29 in middle-aged to older individuals) (Table 4, Fig. 2A-C). These tendencies was consistent across the 40–50s, 60s, and 70s (Table 4, Fig. 2). The highest HA adoption rate was observed among individuals with HL in their 70s, with an increase from 2.58% in 2010–2012 to 6.64% in 2020–2022. Across all age groups, HA adoption rates increased proportionately with HL severity, demonstrating a clear pattern of greater uptake among those with more severe hearing impairment (Table 4, Fig. 2).

DISCUSSION

This study reveals changes in hearing adoption rates following the expansion of political support for HAs and estimates the hearing adoption rates of the general Korean middle-aged and elderly population based as a function of age and severity of HL. The adoption rate of HAs was significantly higher in 2020–2022 than in 2010–2012 even after adjusting covariates. The HA adoption rates of overall HL were highest in individuals in their 70s both in 2020 to 2022 and 2010 to 2012. Furthermore, the HA adoption rate increased with the severity of HL.

The implementation of expanded insurance coverage for HAs in 2015 significantly improved accessibility to hearing rehabilitation in South Korea. Previous studies have reported a higher HA adoption rate following these policy changes.11,18 However, earlier research primarily focused on individuals eligible for financial support, such as those officially registered as hearing disabled.11,18 In contrast, the present study provides a comprehensive analysis of HA adoption rates in the entire population with HL, including those both eligible and ineligible for financial assistance. Like earlier findings, our results highlight the crucial role of government subsidies in increasing HA usage. The data further demonstrate that individuals in the unsupported disabling HL group, who have moderate or greater HL but do not meet the eligibility criteria for financial aid, exhibited an HA adoption rate five to six times lower than that of financially supported individuals. Given that HAs are typically recommended for individuals with at least moderate HL,19,20 this difference suggests that financial barriers remain a key determinant of HA adoption. Practical policy measures, such as subsidy expansion, should significantly enhance HA adoption among this underserved group.

The increased life expectancy and better social function among the elderly in 2020 - 2022 may also have contributed to the higher adoption rate of HAs.21,22 Average life expectancy in South Korea has increased, reaching about 80 years for men and 86 years for women,21 accompanying better health with social function. Better physical and social functioning may help reduce psychological issues and tinnitus,23,24 while dizziness may become more prevalent due to the increased occurrence of vestibular dysfunction with age,25 as shown in this study. Furthermore, as aging populations maintain more active lifestyles, the demand for hearing rehabilitation is likely to increase.12,21,26 We found that HA adoption rates peaked in individuals in their 70s, reflecting the combined effects of age-related hearing decline and increased awareness of HL.26,27 Given the ongoing demographic shift, more comprehensive support should be considered to address the rising demand for HL rehabilitation in the elderly population.

The present study demonstrated that age, hearing thresholds, and tinnitus were significantly associated with HA use after adjusting for other factors. Since increased hearing thresholds lead to increased demand for hearing rehabilitation, and HAs are considered an effective treatment strategy for tinnitus, HA adoption rates were no doubt influenced by other factors, some of which differed significantly between 2020–2022 and 2010–2012. Thus, for example, the mean age of participants increased over time, and the prevalence of tinnitus was lower in 2020–2022. However, despite the potentially negative influence of decreased tinnitus prevalence on HA adoption, the overall HA adoption rate increased in 2020–2022, suggesting that the effects of expanded financial support and an aging population outweighed other contributing factors.

In the binary logistic regression analysis, the HA adoption rate showed a positive association with age. However, this association was lost when patients had hearing thresholds exceeding the disabling HL level. Considering the social stigma associated with wearing HAs, middle-aged individuals are less likely to wear them than older individuals.12 Nevertheless, when hearing thresholds exceed 40 dB, the necessity for HAs may outweigh these influences. Therefore, it is essential for individuals with disabling HL who do not meet the criteria for hearing disability grades to receive subsidies for HAs in South Korea.

This study has some limitations. First, data on the financial sources used to obtain HAs were not available, making it difficult to assess the direct impact of financial support on HA acquisition. Future studies should include detailed financial data to evaluate the role of government subsidies more precisely. Second, the cross-sectional design of this study limits its ability to establish causal relationships between HA use and associated factors. While it demonstrates significant associations between HA adoption and factors such as tinnitus, dizziness, and subjective HL, further longitudinal research is needed to determine whether these variables directly influence HA usage over time. Third, cognitive impairment and psychological conditions, which may influence HA adoption, could not be evaluated due to the absence of relevant data. Finally, the present study excluded patients with abnormal tympanic membranes due to limitations in middle ear assessment in the eighth KNHANES and the fluctuating nature of conductive or mixed HL caused by chronic otitis media. HA use may also be limited in cases with active otorrhea,28 which could not be assessed. Further studies should explore HA adoption in this group.

Following the expansion of financial support policies, HA adoption rates have nearly doubled over the past decade, with the greatest increases observed among individuals in their 70s. These findings underscore the importance of financial assistance in improving HA accessibility. Given that individuals with disabling HL who do not meet the criteria for financial aid exhibit significantly lower adoption rates, further policy expansion—including broader subsidy coverage—may be necessary to bridge the gap and enhance hearing rehabilitation outcomes. Moreover, targeted public awareness campaigns, family support programs, and behavioral interventions could help increase long-term HA usage, ultimately leading to better health outcomes and economic benefits.

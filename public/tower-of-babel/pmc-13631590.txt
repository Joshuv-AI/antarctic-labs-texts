PI3K/AKT-associated fibroblast activation by native Argiope anasuja spidroin for cutaneous wound healing: An in vitro approach
Authors: Sri Raagavee Sivakumar, Kamarajan Rajagopalan, Rajendran Venkatesh, Jackson Durairaj Selvan Christyraj, P Susheela, Shobana Sampath, Douglas Law, Gholamreza Abdi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSpidroins, the main structural protein constituents of spider silk, show excellent mechanical strength, durability and biocompatibility; therefore, they are potentially useful biomaterials for medical uses. Nevertheless, the molecular mechanisms that underlie their wound-healing activity are not well understood. The present study has been designed to investigate the in vitro wound-healing potential of the Native Argiope anasuja orb-web spidroin protein in NIH/3T3 fibroblasts and the underlying molecular mechanisms. Cell viability was measured by MTT assay, and fibroblast migration and re-epithelialization were determined by scratch wound assay under 0% FBS, 10% FBS, 1× PBS vehicle control and 5% spidroin treatment. In the spidroin-treated group, fibroblast migration was enhanced, and wound closure was complete within 36 h when compared to the control groups. Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) analysis demonstrated an increased in VEGFR2, PI3K and AKT gene expression in spidroin-treated cells, indicating the activation of angiogenic and proliferative signalling pathways. Western blot analysis also verified activation of the PI3K/AKT signalling pathway, as indicated by an 8.5-fold increase in the expression of PI3K, a 1.2-fold increase in the expression of AKT, and a 2.8-fold increase in the expression of phosphorylated AKT (p-AKT) compared with controls. Immunofluorescence studies also revealed increased fluorescence intensity of VEGFR2, PI3K and AKT in the treated cells. In total, these results indicate that native Argiope anasuja spidroin induces fibroblast proliferation and migration through involvement of VEGFR2-associated PI3K/AKT signalling pathways, which illustrates its therapeutic potential in wound healing and tissue regeneration.Keywords: Spidroin, Argiope anasuja, Wound healing, NIH/3T3 cells, PI3K, Fibroblast

Introduction

Wound healing is a multifaceted process that includes restoration of tissue integrity and maintenance of the role of the skin as the main defense on environmental factors and invasion by microbes. Effective wound healing depends on interactions among various cell types, cytokines, enzymes, and the extracellular matrix (ECM).1 Growth-factor signalling pathways are mediated, in part, by receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs), which regulate cellular processes such as cell growth, migration, and proliferation.2 Growth factors including PDGF, EGF, VEGF and FGF regulate these mechanisms during wound healing, contributing to fibroblast recruitment, proliferation and re-epithelialization3. Vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) is a highly important signalling molecule in angiogenesis and tissue regeneration. VEGF allows nutrients to be delivered and stimulates the growth of new blood vessels from existing ones. Apart from VEGF, platelet-derived growth factor, epidermal growth factor, and fibroblast growth factor receptors, as well as numerous RTK fibroblasts that have a key effector role in dermal regeneration by contributing to proliferation, ECM remodelling associated with growth-factor signalling regulates intracellular pathways, including the PI3K/Akt pathway, which is involved in wound-healing processes such as migration, proliferation, and survival.4, 5, 6 Disruption of these pathways results in delayed healing and chronic wounds, such as pressure sores and diabetic ulcers, that are difficult to treat7, 8.

The basic structural units of spider silk, spidroins, have extraordinary mechanical properties, biodegradability and natural biocompatibility, which makes them suitable for the development of bio-scaffolds.9, 10, 11, 12 Spidroins have remarkable tensile strength, flexibility, and biological stability because of the presence of glycine- and proline-rich repetitive sequences.

Spiders also have naturally occurred cell-adhesive properties that facilitate the adhesion and proliferation of fibroblasts, and these are deemed vital for the wound-healing process, in addition to mechanical advantages. Most of the previous studies were performed on the recombinant spidroins of Araneus diadematus and Nephila clavipes, emphasizing its structural support and mechanical strength.9, 13, 14 Argiope species is an orb weaver spider that builds unique web designs such as stabilimentum and has complex functional adaptations.15 In Argiope anasuja, the native spidroin is structurally intact, with its original biochemical composition and bioactivity preserved even after post-translational modifications. Compared with recombinant silk, this spidroin provides a scaffold for support and triggers cellular signalling pathways important for wound healing and serves a biomaterial in regenerative medicine.16, 17 Our study demonstrates the role of spidroin extracted from Argiope anasuja as a functional biomaterial and modulator in fibroblast-driven proliferation and angiogenesis, offering a dual therapeutic approach by combining bioactive PI3K-signalling pathway association and mechanical structural support.

Methods

Spider silk collection and spidroin extraction

Argiope anasuja specimens were identified using the visual search method at Nilgiris Biosphere Nature Park (NBNP), Anaikatty, Tamil Nadu, India. Spider silk was extracted with sterile forceps, placed in a sterile container for storage, and transported to our laboratory. Using sterile forceps, Native Argiope anasuja spider silk was extracted and placed in a sterile container for storage. To remove all dust and debris, 1 g of Native A. anasuja spider silk was weighed and rinsed 3 times with distilled water. The silk was dissolved in 100 mL of 99% hexafluoro-2-propanol at 25 °C using a magnetic stirrer. Then, the solvent was removed using a rotary evaporator, and the residue was reconstituted with 100 mL of 1× phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). The samples were centrifuged for 30 min at 2800g, and the supernatant was collected, centrifuged again for 30 min, and then stored in the refrigerator at −4 °C. To remove contaminants and low-molecular-weight proteins, spidroin extract was isolated by dialysis against 1× PBS (pH 7.4) using a cellulose membrane (molecular weight cut-off 12–14 kDa) at 4 °C. The dialysis membrane was treated with 70% ethanol, washed with distilled water, and soaked in PBS before adding the protein sample. Dialysis was performed with gentle agitation, and the buffer was replaced 3 times at 3 h, 6 h, and 12 h to efficiently remove low-molecular-weight impurities. The protein concentrations were determined using Bradford assay. The protein preparation was further characterized by SDS-PAGE and LC-MS-based amino-acid composition analysis. SDS-PAGE was used to assess the molecular-weight profile of the native spidroin, while LC-MS-based amino-acid analysis was used to obtain amino-acid composition assessment.

Cell culture maintenance

NIH/3T3 mouse embryonic fibroblast cells were acquired from the National Centre for Cell Science, Pune, India. The NIH/3 T3 cells were cultured in T-flasks with DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% of each of the following antibiotics: penicillin (HiMedia; Thane, India. Catalogue: SD028); streptomycin (IBI Scientific; Dubuque, Iowa. Catalogue: IB02180); and amphotericin B (Thermo Fisher Scientific; Waltham, Massachusetts. Catalogue: 15290018). The cells were maintained in a CO2 incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO2 concentration.18

Cell proliferation assay

The proliferation effect of Native A. anasuja spidroin extract on NIH/3 T3 cells was assessed using the 3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide (MTT) assay. Stock 3 T3 cells were seeded at a concentration of 1 × 105 cells and a density of 7000 cells per well in a 96-well plate. The cells were incubated in the CO2 incubator for 24 h in DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS. Then, the cells were washed 3 times with DMEM supplemented with 0% FBS under serum-free conditions. Spidroin concentrations ranging from 5% to 100% (loading control, 10% FBS + DMEM medium) were added, and the cells were incubated at 37 °C in the CO2 incubator. After 24 h of incubation, 10 μL of MTT was added to each well, and the 96-well plate was incubated in the dark for 2 h. Then, the medium was carefully removed, and DMSO was added to the plate to elicit purple-colour development (Rajagopalan et al., 2024)18. The 96-well plate was assessed for optical density at 570 nm using a microplate reader (BioTek Epoch Microplate Spectrophotometer; Agilent Technologies, California, USA). Wound healing assessment

The migration ability of NIH/3T3 fibroblasts was evaluated using a scratch wound assay. The cells were seeded (3 × 105 cells/mL) into 6-well tissue culture plates containing DMEM medium supplemented with 10% FBS until monolayers were formed. A linear scratch wound was created in the monolayer by using a sterile 200 μL micropipette tip and a ruler. Cellular debris was removed by rinsing the plates with serum-free DMEM +0% FBS; then, 5% Native A. anasuja spidroin extract was incorporated, with DMEM +10% FBS as the loading control, DMEM +0% FBS as the negative control and 1× PBS as the vehicle control. The plates were incubated at 37 °C in the CO2 incubator for 24 h. The migratory ability of 3T3 cells was systematically monitored and photographed at 0 and 24 h using an EVOS microscope (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc.).

Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) analysis

NIH/3 T3 fibroblast cells were cultured in Dulbecco's Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% antibiotic-antimycotic solution at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO₂. The cells were seeded in 6-well plates and allowed to reach 70–80% confluency before treatment with 5% Native A. anasuja spidroin extract for 12 h, while untreated cells served as the control group. After treatment, the culture medium used for culture was aspirated, and cells were washed twice with sterile 1× PBS. Total RNA was obtained with the TRIzol reagent following the manufacturer's instructions. Cells were lysed with TRIzol reagent for thorough homogenization and dissolution of nucleoprotein complexes; chloroform was added, and the mixture was centrifuged at 12,000 ×g for 15 min at 4 °C to divide the aqueous and organic phases. The upper aqueous phase with RNA was thoroughly collected and precipitated with isopropanol. The RNA pellet obtained after centrifugation was washed with 75% ethanol, air-dried, and dissolved in nuclease-free water. RNA concentration and purity were determined spectrophotometrically at 260/280 nm. Complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesised from isolated RNA using the iScript™ cDNA Synthesis Kit (Bio-Rad). The cDNA reaction mixture was prepared by combining RNA template, 5× iScript reaction mix, reverse transcriptase enzyme, and nuclease-free water to a final volume of 20 μL. Reverse transcription was carried out in a thermal cycler under the following conditions: priming at 25 °C for 5 min, reverse transcription at 46 °C for 20 min, and enzyme inactivation at 95 °C for 1 min. The synthesised cDNA was stored at −20 °C until further use.

Quantitative real-time PCR was performed using SYBR Green chemistry on a Bio-Rad CFX96 Real-Time PCR Detection System to analyse the expression levels of the VEGFR2, PI3K, and AKT genes, with β-actin as the internal housekeeping gene. The PCR reaction mixture (10 μL) consisted of 5 μL of 2× SYBR Green master mix, 1 μL of forward primer, 1 μL of reverse primer, 2 μL of cDNA template (containing 1.5 ng of RNA equivalent) and 1 μL of nuclease-free water. Amplification conditions were initial denaturation at 95 °C for 3 min, then forty cycles of denaturation at 95 °C for 10 s, optimized a primer-specific temperature for 30 s, and standard extension at 72 °C for 30 s. To ensure specificity of amplification, melt curve evaluation was performed. Relative gene expression values were calculated with the 2^-ΔΔCt method, and all reactions were performed in duplicate.

Western blotting

Western blot analysis was carried out to evaluate the expression level of proteins involved in angiogenesis, cell proliferation, and wound healing. NIH/3 T3 cells were treated using 10% FBS, 0% DMEM medium and 5% Spidroin protein extract from Native A. anasuja orb web. After treatment, cells were lysed using RIPA buffer along with a protease inhibitor. 80 μg protein of each sample was loaded onto 12% SDS-PAGE gel and run on 60 V electrophoresis. The proteins were transferred to PVDF membranes by the semi-dry transfer method after electrophoresis. The membranes were blocked with 5% BSA in TBST for 1 h at ambient temperature to preclude non-specific binding. The membranes were then incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies directed against Anti-VEGFR2 antibody (Ab194806), Anti-PI3K antibody (Abcam, A19742), Anti-AKT antibody (Abcam, ab18675), Anti-P-AKT antibody (Abcam, AP1453) and Anti-β-actin (Abcam, ab8227) at a dilution of 1:5000. Following the initial incubation period, membranes were subjected to washing thrice using 1× TBST, subsequently undergoing incubation with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibody (anti-rabbit IgG, Sigma A0545) at a dilution of 1:10,000 for a duration of two hours at ambient temperature. After washing, an enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) substrate was used to facilitate the detection of protein bands. The resultant luminescence signals were visualised using the Bio-Rad ChemiDoc XRS+ imaging apparatus.

Immunofluorescence assay

NIH/3T3 cells were seeded into 6-well plates and treated with the positive control (10% FBS) and 5% Native A. anasuja spidroin extract. The cells were washed 3 times with ice-cold 1× PBS after reaching full confluence. The cells were fixed using 4% paraformaldehyde in 1% PBS, followed by permeabilisation with 0.5% Triton-X-100 for 10 min on ice. The blocking process was performed with 1% bovine serum albumin (BSA) for 30 min, followed by overnight incubation at 4 °C with the primary antibodies Anti-Alpha Tubulin (Abcam, ab52623), Anti-PI3K (Abcam, A19742), Anti-AKT (Abcam, ab18675), and Anti-VEGFR2 antibody (Ab194806). The cells were rinsed with 1× TBST buffer and subsequently incubated with the secondary antibody anti-rabbit IgG-HRP (Sigma, AS053) for 2 h at ambient temperature in the dark. Subsequently, the cells were rinsed 3 times with 1× TBST buffer, counterstained with DAPI, mounted with DPX, and examined using the EVOS fluorescence microscope.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism version 8.0.1. The results were presented as mean ± standard error of the mean. Each experiment was conducted in triplicate. For comparisons in multiple experimental groups, One-way ANOVA followed by Tukey's multiple comparison test was used and for the time-dependent scratch assay involving treatment and time as factors, Two-way ANOVA was used. A p-value <0.05 is considered statistically significant. The specific test used for each experiment is indicated in the corresponding figure legend.

Results

Optimising the effective dose of extracted spidroin via the MTT assay

Based on the Bradford protein estimation assay, the stock solution of Native Argiope anasuja spidroin contained 52.42 mg/mL protein, corresponding to a 5.242% w/v concentration. Working concentrations from this stock were determined by volume-to-volume (v/v) dilution to experimental use. The protein amounts for the final volume of 100 mL at each concentration were determined as follows: 262.1 mg at 5% v/v, 1310.5 mg at 25% v/v, 2621 mg at 50% v/v, 3931.5 mg at 75% v/v and 5242 mg at 100% (the undiluted stock solution).

To demonstrate the proliferative effect caused by spidroin on NIH/3 T3 cells, different concentrations of spidroin (5%, 25%, 50%, 75% and 100%) were used. The data indicated that Native A. anasuja spidroin extract had a proliferative effect, with increased growth observed at 5% concentration and a gradual decrease at 25% concentration compared with the 20% serum control. However, a gradual decrease in cell viability was observed when 50%, 75%, and 100% concentrations were used (Fig. 1). The cytotoxicity of 5% spidroin extract was lower than that of 100% spidroin extract, and the IC50 value was found to be 57.354 μM. MTT analysis showed that Native A. anasuja spidroin maintained high NIH/3T3 cell viability, with viability higher than the 10% FBS control. However, One-way ANOVA indicated that the differences among the tested concentrations were not statistically significant (p = 0.657). Based on its favourable observed viability, 5% spidroin was selected as treatment for further experiments.

Spidroin enhances wound closure in NIH/3T3 cells

The re-epithelialization and migratory potential of NIH/3 T3 fibroblast cells were assessed using a scratch wound assay under four conditions: DMEM with 0% FBS (negative control), DMEM with 10% FBS (positive control), 1× PBS (vehicle control), and 5% native A. anasuja spidroin protein extract (Fig. 2). At 0 h, all groups showed 0% wound closure, verifying the equal width of the scratches for all the treatments. At 18 h, the rate of wound closure was 28% in the 0% FBS group, 52% in the 10% FBS group, 34% in the 1× PBS vehicle control, and 49% in the 5% spidroin-assisted group, demonstrating improved early migration in both serum and spidroin-treated conditions as compared to controls. At 24 h, wound closure elevated to 41% (0% FBS), 78% (10% FBS), 60% (1× PBS), and 83% (5% spidroin), indicating increased cell migration in the spidroin-treated group, even over the serum control. Notably, at 36 h, the 5% spidroin-treated group achieved complete wound closure (100%), whereas the 10% FBS group showed near-complete closure at 96%, the 1× PBS group exhibited 63% closure, and the 0% FBS group reached only 55%. These findings clearly indicate that spidroin promotes wound closure, potentially through fibroblast migration and proliferation in a time-dependent manner, ultimately achieving complete re-epithelialization, and highlight its superior efficacy compared to both vehicle and serum controls. Two-way ANOVA showed a significant difference in wound closure at both 18 h (p = 0.031) and 24 h (p < 0.008). 5% native A. anasuja spidroin significantly increased wound closure compared to 1× PBS and showed a comparable response to the 10% FBS control.

qPCR analysis of PI3K/AKT signalling pathway

The relative mRNA expression levels of VEGFR2, PI3K, and AKT were analysed by quantitative PCR to assess activation of the PI3K/AKT signalling pathway following spidroin treatment, as shown in Fig. 3. Three experimental groups were included in the study: Group 1 (1× PBS control), Group 2 (10% FBS-treated cells), and Group 3 (spidroin-treated cells). The expression levels were calculated as fold change difference (FCD) relative to the control group. The qPCR results showed a significant up-regulation of PI3K with a relative fold expression of 1.07 in Group 1, 6.60 in Group 2, and 13.98 in Group 3. The high expression of PI3K in the spidroin-treated cells suggests that the intracellular proliferative and survival pathways of signalling pertinent to wound healing are robustly activated.

Similarly, the treatment groups showed a gradual increase in VEGFR2 expression. Group 1 showed basal fold expression of 1.16, and Group 2 showed a mild increase of 1.69-fold. Meanwhile, Group 3 showed a significant upregulation (fold change of 19.07, highest in all analysed genes). The greater VEGFR2 expression in the spidroin-treated group indicates that there is an increase in angiogenic activity that may lead to an increase in vascularization and tissue regeneration during wound healing. After treatment, AKT also displayed an increased transcriptional activation pattern. Group 1 had a fold expression of 1.15, while Group 2 and Group 3 had increased levels of 2.71 and 2.89, respectively. While the increase in AKT expression was relatively modest when compared with PI3K and VEGFR2, the constant upregulation in the spidroin-treated group indicates the activation of downstream signalling components that regulate cell proliferation, migration and survival. In conclusion, the qPCR results clearly showed that the treatment of spidroin significantly increased the expression of VEGFR2, PI3K and AKT genes in comparison to the control PBS and MFBS-treated groups. The PI3K/AKT-mediated signalling pathway is activated by upregulating these genes, stimulating cellular proliferation, angiogenesis and wound-healing responses. VEGFR2 expression showed a statistically significant difference (p = 0.0101), indicating significant modulation of VEGFR2 transcript levels after spidroin treatment. PI3K showed increased expression in the spidroin-treated group, though the difference is not statistically significant (p = 0.2333). AKT expression also did not differ statistically among the treatment groups (p = 0.4786). The results indicate the transcriptional response was increased in VEGFR2, but the changes seen in PI3K and AKT at the mRNA level were not statistically significant.

Spidroin regulates the PI3K/Akt pathway for efficient wound healing

Western blot analysis was performed to elucidate the involvement of the PI3K/AKT signalling pathway in NIH/3T3 fibroblast cells following treatment with 5% Native A. anasuja spidroin extract for 36 h (Fig. 4). Total protein was extracted using RIPA buffer, and the expression levels of VEGFR2, PI3K, total AKT, and phosphorylated AKT (p-AKT) were analysed, with β-actin serving as the internal loading control. VEGFR2 expression was increased in 5% native A. anasuja spidroin protein compared with control. Densiometric analysis using ImageJ showed a 2.9-fold increase, reflecting enhanced associated signalling after spidroin treatment. The results clearly demonstrated that PI3K expression was absent or negligible in Groups 1–3 (0% FBS, 10% FBS, and 1× PBS controls) and was distinctly expressed only in Group 4 with 8.5-fold (5% Sp1-treated group), indicating the downstream signalling of PI3K component. In contrast, total AKT expression was consistently observed across all groups, with only minor variation (1.2-fold), suggesting that basal AKT protein levels remain relatively stable irrespective of treatment. However, a significant 2.8-fold increase in phosphorylated AKT (p-AKT) expression was observed in the Spidroin-treated group compared to the control groups, supporting enhanced activation of the downstream signalling cascade. The selective expression of PI3K in the Spidroin-treated group, along with the marked upregulation of p-AKT, indicates activation of the PI3K/AKT signalling pathway. This pathway is critically involved in regulating fibroblast survival, proliferation, and migration, key processes required for effective wound healing. The increased phosphorylation of AKT further confirms that Spidroin not only initiates upstream signalling via PI3K but also promotes downstream signal transduction, thereby enhancing cellular responses associated with tissue regeneration. Increased VEGFR2 expression along with enhanced PI3K expression and AKT phosphorylation after spidroin exposure supports the involvement of VEGFR2-associated PI3K/AKT signalling axis in the response of NIH/3 T3 fibroblast cell lines. One-way ANOVA followed by Tukey's multiple comparison showed significant differences among the experimental groups for VEGFR2, PI3K, and p-AKT expression p = 0.001 and AKT with p = 0.899. Overall, these findings suggest that native A. anasuja spidroin exerts its wound-healing effects through targeted activation of the VEGFR2-mediated PI3K/AKT pathway.

Enhanced cytoplasmic localisation of PI3K/AKT in spidroin-treated samples

During the wound-healing process, immunofluorescence analysis of alpha-tubulin revealed different cytoskeletal configurations in NIH/3 T3 fibroblasts treated with spidroin (Fig. 5). The mean intensity of green fluorescence was higher in the spidroin-treated cells (125.95) than in the control (77.66). Alpha-tubulin labelling revealed a normal radial microtubule network originating from the microtubule-organising centre in the control cells, which showed negligible remodelling near the wound edge. These cells had not been treated with any chemicals or other agents. In contrast, the cells treated with spidroin exhibited increased microtubule polymerisation and reorientation towards the wound borders. Cell migration was activated, as evidenced by a significant increase in alpha-tubulin fluorescence at the outermost ends and perinuclear regions of proliferating cells.

Discussion

Spidroins extracted from spider silk possess extraordinary mechanical properties and biocompatibility, and they have remarkable applications in the biomedical field.9, 19 A previous study20 demonstrated that silk fibroin enhanced the growth of keratinocyte and fibroblast cell lines, which are essential for skin regeneration during wound healing. In our study, higher metabolic activity was observed in the 5% spidroin extract-treated group than in the control groups, indicating a proliferative effect, as assessed by live mitochondrial function necessary for cell viability. Moreover, no cytotoxicity was observed at 5% spidroin concentration. In particular, the decrease in cell viability in the 20% FBS + DMEM medium may be due to excessive nutritional stress, so 10% FBS + DMEM medium would be ideal for optimal growth of NIH/3 T3 cell lines. Cell viability was adversely affected by 75% spidroin extract; however, 5% spidroin extract increased cell viability, suggesting its potential therapeutic efficacy in wound healing applications, which warrants further investigation. Therefore, the therapeutic uses of native A. anasuja spidroin extract need to be analysed in future studies, in which a wider concentration range and extended exposure times should be considered.

Cell movement during the proliferative phase in the wound area is a crucial step in wound healing. The 5% spidroin-treated group showed improved healing due to increased cellular movement; 90% wound closure was observed in NIH/3T3 cells treated with 5% spidroin extract, substantially exceeding that observed in cells treated with the 10% FBS + DMEM medium control. The enhanced cellular motility may be due to spidroin's effect on cytoskeletal reorganisation during ECM remodelling. These surface-receptor interactions can activate the proliferation signalling pathways necessary for cell migration and wound healing.21

The present qPCR analysis demonstrated that spidroin treatment markedly increased the expression of key genes in the PI3K/AKT signalling pathway, including VEGFR2, PI3K, and AKT, indicating its potential to promote cellular proliferation, migration, and angiogenesis during wound healing. Among the genes evaluated, VEGFR2 exhibited the highest upregulation in the spidroin-treated group, followed by PI3K and AKT, indicating that spidroin might strongly induce angiogenic and proliferative responses in fibroblast cells. VEGFR2 is a major mediator of angiogenesis and serves an important role in wound repair by stimulating neovascularisation, nutrient supply and tissue regeneration.22, 23 The substantial rise in VEGFR2 expression seen in the spidroin-treated group suggests increased vascular endothelial activation and enhanced healing potential. Similar results have been reported by24 Bao et al. (2009), showing that VEGFR2 signalling activation enhances vascular development to promote tissue granulation and wound closure. Therefore, the high VEGFR2 expression detected in this work suggests that spidroin may be involved in the rapid tissue repair through the stimulation of angiogenic mechanisms. The upregulation of PI3K expression further confirms the involvement of proliferative signalling pathways in response to spidroin treatment. PI3K is an intracellular signalling molecule that has an important role in cell survival, proliferation, migration and protein synthesis during tissue regeneration.25 Activation of the PI3K pathway has been demonstrated to promote fibroblast migration and cellular proliferation during wound healing.26, 27 The increased PI3K expression in Group 4 in the present study suggests that spidroin stimulates intracellular signalling cascades that are related to fibroblast growth and wound contraction. Furthermore, the treated groups showed increased expression of AKT, a downstream effector of PI3K signalling. AKT signalling is known to regulate cell survival and anti-apoptotic activity and migration of fibroblasts and keratinocytes in tissue repair.28 The fold increase of AKT was modest, but its sustained upregulation confirms activation of the PI3K/AKT signalling axis. Similar observations were also reported by Fang et al. (2025)29 who demonstrated that AKT signalling activation facilitates wound closure and extracellular matrix remodelling. Thus, the simultaneous upregulation of PI3K and AKT in the current study indicates increased proliferative and regenerative activity by spidroin treatment. The overall qPCR results indicate that native spidroin isolated from A. anasuja has the potential to activate the PI3K/AKT signalling pathway and the angiogenic responses involved in wound healing. The combined upregulation of VEGFR2, PI3K, and AKT suggests the bioactivity of spidroin to promote fibroblast activation, cell proliferation, and tissue regeneration.

To target the proliferative phase of wound healing, 5% spidroin extract was used, and Western blot analysis showed that PI3K expression was negligible in control groups, but was significantly upregulated in spidroin-treated cells, which suggests that spidroin functions as a strong upstream activator of intracellular signalling cascades. Total AKT levels were not significantly changed between experimental conditions, indicating that spidroin does not affect AKT protein synthesis, but rather its activation status. Notably, we observed a significant increase in phosphorylated AKT (p-AKT) exclusively in the treated group, thereby suggesting activation of the PI3K/AKT pathway through post-translational modification, not protein overexpression. This distinction is important as AKT phosphorylation is the functional activation phase leading to downstream signalling events related to cell survival and proliferation. These findings are in agreement with the research of30 Yang et al. (2017), who observed that lucidone treatment markedly increased the levels of p-PI3K and p-AKT in keratinocytes through Western blot analysis. Interestingly, their study also showed no changes in total AKT levels, which further supports the idea that the PI3K/AKT pathway is activated by phosphorylation rather than changes in protein expression. Furthermore, PI3K inhibition by LY294002 effectively inhibited p-AKT expression and suppressed cell proliferation and migration, confirming the essential function of this pathway in wound healing. The congruence of the present findings with the lucidone study suggests a conserved biological mechanism whereby activation of PI3K results in phosphorylation of AKT, which then modulates downstream targets that contribute to cell cycle progression, migration and survival. In the lucidone framework, activation of the PI3K/AKT pathway further modulated the β-catenin and NF-κB signalling pathways, which ultimately promoted wound closure via the coordinated regulation of proliferation, inflammation and extracellular matrix remodelling.30

Moreover, the current findings are consistent with other pathway-based studies, which have represented the involvement of the PI3K/AKT pathway in wound healing. These studies, taken together highlight that AKT phosphorylation constitutes a crucial molecular switch that determines the shift from a quiescent to a proliferative and migratory active cellular state. Mechanistically, the activation of the PI3K/AKT pathway identified in this study can account for the improved wound closure and fibroblast migration. PI3K activation produces phosphatidylinositol (3,4,5)-trisphosphate (PIP3) that recruits AKT to the plasma membrane for phosphorylation and activation. Activated AKT then controls multiple downstream targets involved in, for example, cell cycle progression (e.g. PCNA), cytoskeletal remodelling and anti-apoptotic signalling, which promotes tissue regeneration.

Furthermore, the Western blot results from this research offer direct molecular evidence for the link between spidroin treatment and intracellular signalling activation, rather than only phenotypic observations. The induction of PI3K and up-regulation of p-AKT, along with steady total AKT levels, strongly suggest that spidroin acts as a biologically active signalling modulator and not as a passive scaffold. Increased expression of VEGFR2, a receptor tyrosine kinase can activate downstream intracellular pathways including PI3K/AKT. Activation of PI3K promotes downstream AKT phosphorylation, and activated AKT regulates cellular processes associated with survival, proliferation and migration.31 The overall Western blot results indicate that A. anasuja spidroin influences wound healing through a involvement of the VEGFR2 mediated PI3K – AKT – p-AKT signalling axis, aligning with mechanisms reported earlier for other bioactive compounds such as lucidone. These findings are consistent with the literature and add to the solidity of spidroin as a potential biomaterial for regenerative uses.

Alpha-tubulin was analysed via immunofluorescence. The microtubules produced by alpha-tubulin are essential for cell division and intracellular transport.32, 33 During mitotic division, microtubules cause morphological changes and cell polarisation during the proliferative phase of wound healing.34 The normal shape of microtubules in NIH/3 T3 cells was maintained in 10% FBS + DMEM medium; however, the microtubule arrangement was altered in spidroin-treated cells, possibly due to biochemical and mechanical signalling that affect cytoskeletal dynamics. Therefore, the altered microtubule arrangement enhanced fibroblast migration, indicating the bioactivity of spidroin in influencing the wound-healing process. The increased VEGFR2 immunofluorescence intensity seen in spidroin treated cells supports activation of primary receptor. In parallel, high intensity observed in PI3K and AKT fluorescence intensities confirm activation of both upstream and downstream signalling. Previous study by Jiang et al., 202035 showed that integrin mediated activation of PI3K/AKT signalling is essential for cellular migration and tissue repair. Moreover, activation of PI3K/AKT pathway is being associated with enhanced fibroblast migration, collagen deposition and regenerative response in wound-healing models.36 A limitation of the present study is the use of NIH/3 T3 fibroblast cell line as an established in vitro model. Although NIH/3 T3 cells provide a reproducible system for evaluating fibroblast proliferation, migration and intracellular signalling, future studies with primary fibroblast models from larger experimental animals would provide additional translational validation of the effects of native Argiope anasuja spidroin. Overall, the findings establish that A. anasuja spidroin enhances fibroblast wound closure and is associated with VEGFR2-mediated PI3K/AKT signalling in wound healing.

Conclusion

In this study, NIH/3T3 fibroblast cells were used to demonstrate the in vitro wound-healing potential of Argiope anasuja spidroin extract through involvement of the VEGFR2-mediated PI3K/AKT signalling pathway. The scratch wound assay revealed that spidroin-treated cells exhibited significantly accelerated wound gap closure and achieved complete wound healing within 36 h compared with the control groups. Molecular analyses further demonstrated increased expression of VEGFR2, PI3K, and AKT at both the gene and protein levels, along with increased AKT phosphorylation, indicating activation of intracellular signalling pathways associated with fibroblast proliferation, migration, and survival. Immunofluorescence studies additionally confirmed increased cellular localisation and expression of VEGFR2, PI3K, and AKT in spidroin-treated cells. These findings support the crucial role of fibroblast activation in dermal wound healing, in which enhanced migration and activation of signalling pathways contribute to effective tissue repair and extracellular matrix remodelling. In conclusion, this study highlights the therapeutic promise of native A. anasuja spidroin as an attractive natural biomaterial used for wound healing and tissue regeneration. Further in vivo studies are needed to confirm these findings and to assess the clinical feasibility of spidroin-based conventional therapies for the management of cutaneous wounds.

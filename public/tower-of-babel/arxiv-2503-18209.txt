Hyers-Ulam stability of homomorphisms and ∗ \ast -homomorphisms on Hilbert C ∗ C^{*} -modules
Sajjad Khan, Choonkil Park

Abstract
In this paper, we introduce the idea of ∗ \ast -homomorphism on a Hilbert C ∗ C^{*} -module. Furthermore, we prove the Hyers-Ulam stability of homomorphisms and ∗ \ast -homomorphisms on Hilbert C ∗ C^{*} -modules using the fixed point method.

Introduction and preliminaries

The stability problem of functional equations was arose from a question posed by Ulam
[
28
]

in 1940.
Specifically, the question was: Let
(G,\ast)
be a group and
(G^{\diamond},\diamond,d^{\diamond})
be a metric group with the metric
d^{\diamond}
. For any
\epsilon>0
, does there exist a
\delta>0
such that if
f:G\rightarrow G^{\diamond}
satisfies
d(f(x\ast y),f(x)\diamond f(y))<\delta
for all
x,y\in G
, then a homomorphism
h:G\rightarrow G^{\diamond}
exists with
d^{\diamond}(f(x),h(x))<\epsilon
for all
x\in G
? If such
h
exists, then the functional equation
h(x\ast y)=h(x)\diamond h(y)
is stable. In 1941, Hyers
[
7
]
provided the first affirmative answer for approximately additive mappings in the context of Banach spaces. In 1978, Rassias
[
24
]
generalized the Hyers’ result for linear mappings. Gǎvruta
[
6
]
introduced a general control function instead of the Cauchy difference introduced in
[
24
]
, and further extended the Rassias’ results. Rassias
[
25
]
posed the question in 1990 about whether a similar theorem could be established for

p\geq 1
. Gajda
[
5
]
provided an affirmative answer to this question for

p>1
, following the same approach as in
[
24
]
by Rassias. Gajda
[
5
]
, along with Rassias and Šemrl
[
26
]
, showed that the Rassias’ results cannot be established for
p=1
.

In 1991, Baker
[
1
]
used the fixed point method for the first time to study the stability of functional equations. Then, Radu
[
23
]
and Cadariu and Radu
[
2
]
studied the stability of functional equations via fixed point method. In 1996, Isac and Rassias
[
8
]
were the first to apply the stability theory of functional equations to establish new fixed point theorems and their applications. Using fixed point methods, many authors have extensively investigated the stability problem of various functional equations (see, for example
[
3
,
17
,
21
]
).

Park
[
19
,
20
]
introduced additive
\rho
-functional inequalities and established the Hyers-Ulam stability of these inequalities in both Banach spaces and non-Archimedean Banach spaces. The stability problems of various functional equations, functional inequalities, and differential equations have been thoroughly studied by numerous researchers (see
[
10
,
13
,
22
]
).

In mathematics, the notion of a Hilbert
C^{*}
-module generalizes the concept of a Hilbert space by replacing the field of complex numbers with a general
C^{*}
-algebra. This concept was first introduced by Kaplansky in
[
9
]
. The stability of various mappings on Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules has been investigated by several mathematicians (see
[
4
,
16
]
).

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra and
\Xi
be a complex linear space equipped with a compatible left
\mathcal{A}
-module action (i.e.,
\lambda(ax)=(\lambda a)x=a(\lambda x)
for
x\in\Xi
,
a\in\mathcal{A}
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
). The space
\Xi
is called a (left) pre-Hilbert
\mathcal{A}
-module or (left) inner product
\mathcal{A}
-module if there exists a mapping
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\Xi\times\Xi\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
, satisfying the following conditions: For all
x,y,z\in\Xi,\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C},a\in\mathcal{A},

(i)

\langle\alpha x+\beta y,z\rangle=\alpha\langle x,z\rangle+\beta\langle y,z\rangle
;

(ii)

\langle ax,y\rangle=a\langle x,y\rangle
;

(iii)

\langle x,y\rangle^{*}=\langle y,x\rangle
;

(iv)

\langle x,x\rangle\geq 0
and
\langle x,x\rangle=0
if and only if
x=0.

The (left) pre-Hilbert space
\Xi
is considered a (left) Hilbert
\mathcal{A}
-module or a (left) Hilbert
C^{*}
-module over the
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
if it is complete with the induced norm
\|x\|:=\|\langle x,x\rangle\|^{\frac{1}{2}}

[
14
]
. Right Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules can be defined in a similar way. In this paper, we will simply refer to a (left) Hilbert
C^{*}
-module over the
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
as a Hilbert
C^{*}
-module. As an example, it is easy to see that every complex Hilbert space
\Xi
is a Hilbert
C^{*}
-module over the
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathbb{C}
, with its inner product. Similarly, every
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
can be regarded as a Hilbert
C^{*}
-module where,

\langle x,y\rangle=xy^{*}
, for
x,y\in\mathcal{A}
.

Khan
et al.

[
11
]
introduced the idea of fuzzy Hilbert
C^{*}
-module homomorphisms and fuzzy Hilbert
C^{*}
-module derivations and studied their Hyers-Ulam stability while in
[
12
]
they introduced Jordan homomorphisms and Jordan
\ast
-homomorphisms in Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules and discussed their Hyers-Ulam stability.

In this paper, we introduce Hilbert
C^{*}
-module
\ast
-homomorphisms and prove the Hyers-Ulam stability of the Hilbert
C^{*}
-module homomorphisms and Hilbert
C^{*}
-module
\ast
-homomorphisms. It is observed that the definition of a Hilbert
C^{*}
-module homomorphism first appeared in
[
27
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

[
27
]

Let
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
be
C^{*}
-algebras,
\Xi
be a Hilbert
\mathcal{A}
-module and
\nabla
be a Hilbert
\mathcal{B}
-module. A mapping
H:\Xi\rightarrow\nabla
is called a Hilbert
C^{*}
-module homomorphism if
H
is
\mathbb{C}
-linear and satisfies

H\left(\langle x,y\rangle z\right)=\langle H(x),H(y)\rangle H(z)

(1.1)

for all
x,y,z\in\Xi
.

Definition 1.2
.

A Hilbert
C^{*}
-module homomorphism
H:\Xi\rightarrow\nabla
is called a Hilbert
C^{*}
-module
\ast
-homomorphism if

H\left(\langle x,y\rangle^{*}z\right)=\langle H(x),H(y)\rangle^{*}H(z)

(1.2)

for all
x,y,z\in\Xi
.

We recall a fundamental result in the fixed point theory.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
2
]

Let (X,d) be a complete generalized metric space
and
J:X\rightarrow X
be a strictly contractive mapping with
Lipschitz constant
L<1
. Then, for all
x\in X
, either

d(J^{n}x,J^{n+1}x)=\infty

for all nonnegative integers
n
or there exists a positive
integer
n_{0}
such that

(1)

d(J^{n}x,J^{n+1}x)<\infty
for all
n\geq n_{0}
;

(2)
the sequence
\{J^{n}x\}
converges to a fixed point
y^{*}
of

J
;

(3)

y^{*}
is the unique fixed point of
J
in the set
Y=\{y\in X:d(J^{n_{0}}x,y)<\infty\}
;

(4)

d(y,y^{*})\leq\frac{1}{1-L}d(y,Jy)
for all
y\in Y
.

Throughout the paper, we will denote
\Xi
as a Hilbert
\mathcal{A}
-module and
\nabla
as a Hilbert
\mathcal{B}
-module. The paper is organized as follows: In Section 2, we prove the Hyers-Ulam stability of Hilbert
C^{*}
-module homomorphisms. In Section 3, we establish the same results for Hilbert
C^{*}
-module
\ast
-homomorphisms.

2
Stability of Hilbert
C^{*}
-module homomorphisms

We will need the following lemma for our main results.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
18
]

Let
X
and
Y
be linear spaces and
f:X\rightarrow Y
be an additive mapping such that
f(\mu x)=\mu f(x)
for all
x\in X
and any
\mu\in\mathbb{T}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}
. Then, the mapping f is
\mathbb{C}
-linear.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
f:\Xi\rightarrow\nabla
be a mapping for which there exists a function
\varphi:\Xi^{3}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
such that

||f(\mu x+y)-\mu f(x)-f(y)||\leq\varphi(x,y,0),

(2.1)

\|f(\langle x,y\rangle z)-\langle f(x),f(y)\rangle f(z)\|\leq\varphi(x,y,z)

(2.2)

for all
x,y,z\in\Xi
and all
\mu\in\mathbb{T}
. If there exists
0\leq L<1
such that
\varphi(x,y,z)\leq 2L\varphi(\frac{x}{2},\frac{y}{2},\frac{z}{2})
for all
x,y,z\in\Xi
, then there exists a unique Hilbert
C^{*}
-module homomorphism
H:\Xi\rightarrow\nabla
such that

\|f(x)-H(x)\|\leq\dfrac{1}{2-2L}\varphi(x,x,0)

(2.3)

for all
x\in\Xi
.

Proof.

Consider
\mathcal{S}=\{g:\Xi\rightarrow\nabla\}
, and define the generalized metric on
\mathcal{S}
:

d(g,h):=\text{inf}\{c\in(0,\infty):\|g(x)-h(x)\|\leq c\varphi(x,x,0),\forall x\in\Xi\}.

(2.4)

It is easy to show that
(\mathcal{S},d)
is a complete metric space (see
[
15
]
).

Now, consider the linear mapping
J:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}

(Jg)(x):=\dfrac{1}{2}g(2x)

for all
x\in\Xi
.

By
[
2
, Theorem 3.1]
,
J
is a strictly contractive mapping.

Letting
x=y
,
\mu=1
in (
2.1
), we obtain

\left\|f(2x)-2f(x)\right\|\leq\varphi(x,x,0)

(2.5)

for all
x\in\Xi
. This implies that

\left\|f(x)-\dfrac{1}{2}f(2x)\right\|\leq\frac{1}{2}\varphi(x,x,0)

for all
x\in\Xi
. Thus
d(f,Jf)\leq\frac{1}{2}
.

By Theorem
1.3
, there exists a mapping
H:\Xi\rightarrow\nabla
which is a fixed point of
J
such that

H(2x)=2H(x)

(2.6)

for all
x\in\Xi
and
\displaystyle{\lim_{n\to\infty}}d(J^{n}f,H)=0
. This implies that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{f(2^{n}x)}{2^{n}}=H(x)

(2.7)

for all
x\in\Xi.
Notice that
H
is the unique fixed point of
J
in the set

U=\{g\in\mathcal{S}:d(g,f)<\infty\}.

This implies that
H
is the unique fixed point satisfying (
2.6
) such that there exists
c\in(0,\infty)
satisfying

\|f(x)-H(x)\|\leq c\varphi(x,x,0)

for all
x\in\Xi
.

Applying Theorem
1.3
once again, we obtain
d(f,H)\leq\frac{1}{1-L}d(f,Jf)\leq\frac{1}{2-2L}.
This implies

\|f(x)-H(x)\|\leq\frac{1}{2-2L}\varphi(x,x,0)

for all
x\in\Xi
and (
2.3
) holds.

Now we show that
H
is
\mathbb{C}
-linear. From the assumption
\varphi(x,y,z)\leq 2L\varphi(\frac{x}{2},\frac{y}{2},\frac{z}{2})
for all
x,y,z\in\Xi
, it follows that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n}}\varphi(2^{n}x,2^{n}y,2^{n}z)\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{2^{n}L^{n}}{2^{n}}\varphi(x,y,z)=0.

(2.8)

In (
2.1
), replacing
x
by
2^{n}x
and
y
by
2^{n}y
, respectively, we have

\|f(\mu 2^{n}x+2^{n}y)-\mu f(2^{n}x)-f(2^{n}y)\|\leq\varphi(2^{n}x,2^{n}y,0).

Taking the limit as
n\rightarrow\infty
, using (
2.7
) and (
2.8
), we obtain

H(\mu x+y)=\mu H(x)+H(y)

(2.9)

for all
\mu\in\mathbb{T}
and all
x,y\in\Xi.
The case
\mu=1
in (
2.9
) implies that
H
is additive and so
H(0)=0
, together with these, Lemma
2.1
implies that
H
is
\mathbb{C}
-linear.

Putting
x,y,z
as
2^{n}x,2^{n}y,2^{n}z
, respectively, in (
2.2
), we obtain

\|f(\left\langle 2^{n}x,2^{n}y\right\rangle 2^{n}z)-\left\langle f(2^{n}x),f(2^{n}y)\right\rangle f(2^{n}z)\|\leq\varphi(2^{n}x,2^{n}y,2^{n}z).

This further implies that

\displaystyle\left\|2^{-3n}f(2^{3n}\left\langle x,y\right\rangle z)-\left\langle\frac{f(2^{n}x)}{2^{n}},\frac{f(2^{n}y)}{2^{n}}\right\rangle\frac{f(2^{n}z)}{2^{n}}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-3n}\varphi(2^{n}x,2^{n}y,2^{n}z)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-3n}8^{n}L^{n}\varphi(x,y,z)

for all
x,y,z\in\Xi
, since
\varphi(x,y,z)\leq 2L\varphi(\frac{x}{2},\frac{y}{2},\frac{z}{2})\leq 8L\varphi(\frac{x}{2},\frac{y}{2},\frac{z}{2})
.

Taking the limit as
n\rightarrow\infty
, applying (
2.7
) and (
2.8
), we get

H(\left\langle x,y\right\rangle z)=\left\langle H(x),H(y)\right\rangle H(z)

for all
x,y,z\in\Xi
. Thus,
H
is a Hilbert
C^{*}
-module homomorphism.
∎

The following corollary gives us the Hyers-Ulam-Rassias stability of homomorphisms in
\Xi
.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
p\in[0,1)
,
\theta
be a nonnegative real number and
f:\Xi\rightarrow\nabla
be a mapping such that

\|f(\mu x+y)-\mu f(x)-f(y)\|\leq\theta\left(\|x\|^{p}+\|y\|^{p}\right),

(2.10)

\|f(\left\langle x,y\right\rangle z)-\left\langle f(x),f(y)\right\rangle f(z)\|\leq\theta\left(\|x\|^{p}+\|y\|^{p}+\|z\|^{p}\right)

(2.11)

for all
x,y,z\in\Xi
and all
\mu\in\mathbb{T}
. Then there exists a unique Hilbert
C^{*}
-module homomorphism
H:\Xi\rightarrow\nabla
such that

\|f(x)-H(x)\|\leq\frac{2\theta}{2-2^{p}}\|x\|^{p}

(2.12)

for all
x\in\Xi
.

Proof.

Take
\varphi(x,y,z):=\theta\left(\|x\|^{p}+\|y\|^{p}+\|z\|^{p}\right)
and
L=2^{p-1}
. Then the proof follows from Theorem
2.2
.
∎

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
f:\Xi\rightarrow\nabla
be a mapping for which there exists a function
\varphi:\Xi^{3}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
satisfying (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) for all
x,y,z\in\Xi
and all
\mu\in\mathbb{T}
.
If there exists
0\leq L<1
such that
\varphi(x,y,z)\leq\frac{L}{8}\varphi(2x,2y,2z)
for all
x,y,z\in\Xi
, then there exists a unique Hilbert
C^{*}
-module homomorphism
H:\Xi\rightarrow\nabla
such that

\|f(x)-H(x)\|\leq\dfrac{L}{8-8L}\varphi(x,x,0)

(2.13)

for all
x\in\Xi
.

Proof.

Consider the linear mapping
J:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}

Jg\left(x\right):=2g\left(\frac{1}{2}x\right)

for all
x\in\Xi
.

It follows from (
2.5
) that

\left\|f(2x)-2f\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)\right\|\leq\varphi\left(\frac{x}{2},\frac{x}{2},0\right)\leq\frac{L}{8}\varphi(x,x,0)

for all
x\in\Xi
. By (
2.4
), we have
d(f,Jf)\leq\frac{L}{8}
.

By Theorem
1.3
, there exists a mapping
H:\Xi\rightarrow\nabla
which is a fixed point of
J
such that

H\left(\frac{1}{2}x\right)=\frac{1}{2}H(x)

(2.14)

for all
x\in\Xi
. Also
\displaystyle{\lim_{n\to\infty}}d(J^{n}f,H)=0
. This implies that

\lim_{n\to\infty}2^{n}f\left(\frac{x}{2^{n}}\right)=H\left(x\right)

(2.15)

for all
x\in\Xi.
Notice that
H
is the unique fixed point of
J
in the set

U=\{g\in\mathcal{S}:d(g,f)<\infty\}.

This implies that
H
is the unique fixed point satisfying (
2.14
) such that there exists
c\in(0,\infty)
satisfying

\|f(x)-H(x)\|\leq c\varphi(x,x,0)

(2.16)

for all
x\in\Xi
.

Applying Theorem
1.3
once again, we obtain
d(f,H)\leq\frac{1}{1-L}d(f,Jf)\leq\frac{L}{8-8L}.
This implies

\|f(x)-H(x)\|\leq\frac{L}{8-8L}\varphi(x,x,0)

for all
x\in\Xi
and (
2.13
) holds.

From the assumption
\varphi(x,y,z)\leq\frac{L}{8}\varphi(2x,2y,2z)\leq\frac{L}{2}\varphi(2x,2y,2z)
for all
x,y,z\in\Xi
, it follows that

\lim_{n\to\infty}2^{n}\varphi\left(\frac{x}{2^{n}},\frac{y}{2^{n}},\frac{z}{2^{n}}\right)\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{2^{n}L^{n}}{2^{n}}\varphi(x,y,z)=0.

(2.17)

In (
2.1
), replacing
x
by
\frac{x}{2^{n}}
,
y
by
\frac{y}{2^{n}}
, multiplying both side by
2^{n}
, letting
n\rightarrow\infty
, using (
2.15
) and (
2.17
), we obtain

H(\mu x+y)=\mu H(x)+H(y)

(2.18)

for all
x,y\in\Xi
and all
\mu\in\mathbb{T}
. The case
\mu=1
in (
2.18
) implies that
H
is additive and by Lemma
2.1
,
H
is
\mathbb{C}
-linear.

Replacing
x,y,z
with
\frac{x}{2^{n}},\frac{y}{2^{n}},\frac{z}{2^{n}}
, respectively, in (
2.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|2^{3n}f\left(\left\langle\frac{x}{2^{n}},\frac{y}{2^{n}}\right\rangle\frac{z}{2^{n}}\right)-\left\langle 2^{n}f\left(\frac{x}{2^{n}}\right),2^{n}f\left(\frac{y}{2^{n}}\right)\right\rangle 2^{n}f\left(\frac{z}{2^{n}}\right)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{3n}\varphi\left(\frac{x}{2^{n}},\frac{y}{2^{n}},\frac{z}{2^{n}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{3n}2^{-3n}L^{n}\varphi\left(x,y,z\right)

for all
x,y,x\in\Xi
. Taking
n\rightarrow\infty
, using (
2.15
) and (
2.17
), we obtain

H(\left\langle x,y\right\rangle z)=\left\langle H(x),H(y)\right\rangle H(z)

for all
x,y,z\in\Xi
. Thus
H
is a Hilbert
C^{*}
-module homomorphism.
∎

.

p>3
,

be a mapping satisfying (

) and (

.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f:\Xi\rightarrow\nabla
be a mapping for which there exists a function
\varphi:\Xi^{3}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
satisfying (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) and

\|f(\left\langle x,y\right\rangle^{*}z)-\left\langle f(x),f(y)\right\rangle^{*}f(z)\|\leq\varphi(x,y,z)

(3.1)

for all
x,y,z\in\Xi
. If there exists
0\leq L<1
such that
\varphi(x,y,z)\leq 2L\varphi(\frac{x}{2},\frac{y}{2},\frac{z}{2})
for all
x,y,z\in\Xi
, then there exists a unique Hilbert
C^{*}
-module
*
-homomorphism
H:\Xi\rightarrow\nabla
such that

\|f(x)-H(x)\|\leq\dfrac{1}{2-2L}\varphi(x,x,0)

(3.2)

for all
x\in\Xi
.

Proof.

From Theorem
2.2
, there exists a unique Hilbert
C^{*}
-module homomorphism
H
satisfying (
3.2
) and defined as

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{f(2^{n}x)}{2^{n}}=H\left(x\right)

(3.3)

for all

.

Replacing

by

, respectively, in (

), we obtain

\|f(\left\langle 2^{n}x,2^{n}y\right\rangle^{*}2^{n}z)-\left\langle f(2^{n}x),f(2^{n}y)\right\rangle^{*}f(2^{n}z)\|\leq\varphi(2^{n}x,2^{n}y,2^{n}z).

This further implies that

\displaystyle\left\|2^{-3n}f(2^{3n}\left\langle x,y\right\rangle^{*}z)-\left\langle\frac{f(2^{n}x)}{2^{n}},\frac{f(2^{n}y)}{2^{n}}\right\rangle^{*}\frac{f(2^{n}z)}{2^{n}}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-3n}\varphi(2^{n}x,2^{n}y,2^{n}z)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-3n}2^{3n}L^{n}\varphi(x,y,z)

for all
x,y,z\in\Xi
. Taking the limit as
n\rightarrow\infty
, using (
2.15
) and (
2.17
), we obtain

H(\left\langle x,y\right\rangle^{*}z)=\left\langle H(x),H(y)\right\rangle^{*}H(z)

for all
x,y,z\in\Xi
. So

is a Hilbert
C^{*}
-module
\ast
-homomorphism.
∎

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
p\in[0,1)
,
\theta
be a nonnegative real number and
f:\Xi\rightarrow\nabla
be a mapping satisfying (
2.10
), (
2.11
) and

\|f(\left\langle x,y\right\rangle^{*}z)-\left\langle f(x),f(y)\right\rangle^{*}f(z)\|\leq\theta(\|x\|^{p}+\|y\|^{p}+\|z\|^{p})

(3.4)

for all
x,y,z\in\Xi
. Then there exists a unique Hilbert
C^{*}
-module
\ast
-homomorphism
H:\Xi\rightarrow\nabla
such that

\|f(x)-H(x)\|\leq\frac{2\theta}{2-2^{p}}\|x\|^{p}

for all
x\in\Xi
.

Proof.

The proof follows from Theorem
3.1
by taking
\varphi(x,y,z)=\theta(\|x\|^{p}+\|y\|^{p}+\|z\|^{p})
and
L=2^{p-1}
.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
f:\Xi\rightarrow\nabla
be a mapping for which there exists a function
\varphi:\Xi^{3}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
satisfying (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) and (
3.1
). If there exists
0\leq L<1
such that
\varphi(x,y,z)\leq\frac{L}{8}\varphi(2x,2y,2z)
for all
x,y,z\in\Xi
, then there exists a unique Hilbert
C^{*}
-module
\ast
-homomorphism
H:\Xi\rightarrow\nabla
such that

\|f(x)-H(x)\|\leq\dfrac{L}{8-8L}\varphi(x,x,0)

(3.5)

for all
x\in\Xi
.

Proof.

By Theorems
2.4
, there exists a unique Hilbert
C^{*}
-module homomorphism
H:\Xi\rightarrow\nabla
satisfying (
3.5
) and defined as

\lim_{n\to\infty}2^{n}f\left(\frac{x}{2^{n}}\right)=H\left(x\right)

(3.6)

for all
x\in\Xi
.

Replacing
x,y,z
by
\frac{x}{2^{n}},\frac{y}{2^{n}},\frac{z}{2^{n}}
, respectively, in (
3.1
), we obtain

\left\|2^{3n}f\left(\left\langle\frac{x}{2^{n}},\frac{y}{2^{n}}\right\rangle^{*}\frac{z}{2^{n}}\right)-\left\langle 2^{n}f\left(\frac{x}{2^{n}}\right),2^{n}f\left(\frac{y}{2^{n}}\right)\right\rangle^{*}2^{n}f\left(\frac{z}{2^{n}}\right)\right\|\leq 2^{3n}\varphi\left(\frac{x}{2^{n}},\frac{y}{2^{n}},\frac{z}{2^{n}}\right)

for all
x,y,z\in\Xi
. Taking the limit as
n\rightarrow\infty
. using (
2.15
) and (
2.17
), we obtain

H(\left\langle x,y\right\rangle^{*}z)=\left\langle H(x),H(y)\right\rangle^{*}H(z)

for all
x,y,z\in\Xi
. So
H
is a Hilbert
C^{*}
-module
\ast
-homomorphism.
∎

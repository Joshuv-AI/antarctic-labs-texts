On the uniqueness of the smallest embedding cover
Dan Haran

Abstract
We prove the uniqueness of a smallest embedding cover of a profinite group.



Introduction

The purpose of this note is to
show the uniqueness of the smallest embedding cover
(
[
FJ
, Definition 27.4.3]
;
Definition
2.1
here).
Chatzidakis
[
Ch
]
proves this using model theoretic methods.
It is an open problem
(
[
FJ
, Problem 36.2.25]

to prove the uniqueness
using only group theoretic methods.

Before we briefly explain
how this is done here,
we introduce some notation for the whole paper
(which will also serve us for this explanation).

We use the word ‘family’ for indexed family.

We use
\twoheadrightarrow
to denote an epimorphism of profinite groups.
An epimorphism
\eta\colon H\twoheadrightarrow G

is sometimes called

cover
(of
G
)
and sometimes

extension
(of
G
).
Another epimorphism

\pi\colon E\twoheadrightarrow G

is
dominated by
\eta

(or
\eta
dominates
\pi
),
denoted
\pi\preccurlyeq\eta
,
if there is

\psi\colon H\twoheadrightarrow E

such that

\eta=\pi\circ\psi
;
if such
\psi
is an isomorphism of profinite groups,
we say that

\pi

is
isomorphic to
\eta
,
denoted
\pi\cong_{G}\eta
.

Now,
Definition
2.1

defines an embedding cover,
and then it defines an I-cover
as a cover dominated by an embedding cover;
this is needed to define
a smallest embedding cover.
However, this definition of an I-cover
is non-intrinsic, and therefore hard to work with.
We define intrinsically
(Definition
2.4
)

basic covers

that are, to begin with,
simple examples of I-covers,
but later we show that
they play a central role in the characterization
of arbitrary I-covers.

We heavily rely on our recent work

[
H
]

in which we characterize general epimorphisms of profinite groups.
More precisely,

[
H
]

presents an epimorphism
H\twoheadrightarrow G

as the inverse limit of a unique sequence of

fundaments

\textstyle{H\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\quad\cdots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{3}}
\textstyle{G_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{2}}
\textstyle{G_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{1}}
\textstyle{G_{0}\hbox to0.0pt{ $=G$\hss}}

that are (generalized) fiber products of indecomposable epimorphisms.
This presentation allows us to reduce the study of I-covers
to fundamental I-covers.
And it turns out
(Corollary
4.5
)
that fundamental I-covers with finite kernels
are precisesly basic covers.
This allows us to give a precise characterization
(Theorem
4.6
)
of fundamental I-covers
in terms of the invariants introduced in

[
H
]
,
and thus to prove
the desired uniqueness.

Definition 1.6
.

Let
\pi\colon H\twoheadrightarrow G
be an epimorphism of profinite groups.
The
fundament kernel
of
\pi
,
denoted
\mathcal{M}(\pi)
,
is the intersection of all

N\triangleleft H

contained in
\Ker\pi

such that
the map
H/N\twoheadrightarrow G

induced from
\pi

is indecomposable.
Clearly
\mathcal{M}(\pi)\triangleleft H
and
\mathcal{M}(\pi)\leq\Ker\pi
.
An epimorphism

\bar{\pi}\colon{\bar{H}}\twoheadrightarrow G

is the
fundament
of
\pi
,
if it is isomorphic to the quotient map

\bar{\pi}\colon H/\mathcal{M}(\pi)\twoheadrightarrow G
,
that is,
if there is

\rho\colon H\twoheadrightarrow{\bar{H}}

such that

\bar{\pi}\circ\rho=\pi

and

\Ker\rho=\mathcal{M}(\pi)
;
we then also say that

\bar{\pi}
is

the fundament of
\pi
by
\rho
.
We say that
\pi
is
fundamental
,
if
\bar{\pi}=\pi
, that is,
if
\mathcal{M}(\pi)=1
.
Notice that the fundament of an epimorphism
is fundamental.

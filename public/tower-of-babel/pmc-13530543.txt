Poster 128. The Effects of Venting and Capsulotomy on Traction Force and Distraction Distance in Post-Based and Post-Less Hip Arthroscopy
Authors: Travis G. Maak, Nathan Barber, Hansel E. Ihn, Dylan Quintana, Devin Eddington, Angela P. Presson
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:Hip arthroscopy is a common treatment modality in the management of femoroacetabular impingement syndrome (FAIS). However, access to the central compartment requires traction, which has traditionally been accomplished using a perineal post, but may predispose to soft tissue injury and neuropraxia. To reduce the risk of injury, post-less distraction systems have been developed, but there are concerns about their effectiveness in distracting the hip joint compared to post-based systems. Intraoperative techniques like venting and capsulotomy have been suggested as potential strategies to mitigate those concerns with post-less distraction by decreasing traction force requirements. This study aims to compare traction force and hip distraction distance between post-based and post-less distraction systems under three conditions: traction alone, with venting, and with capsulotomy. We hypothesized that (1) post-less distraction would require lower traction force and (2) that venting and capsulotomy would not significantly increase the distraction distance compared to traction alone.Methods:This was a single-surgeon retrospective cohort study using prospectively collected patient data of patients undergoing hip arthroscopy for FAIS. Patients were excluded if they: (1) had a history of intraarticular dysplasia, (2) history of prior surgery on the affected hip, or (3) had a simultaneous extraarticular injury. Load cell-based traction force measurements were collected along with demographic and clinical data. The load cell captured continuous traction force measurements beginning with the initial traction application and ending approximately 5 minutes after completion of the capsulotomy. Fluoroscopy-based distraction distances were measured retrospectively using a best-fit circle technique. Comparison groups were constructed based on the use of a post-based or post-less system. Demographics, clinical characteristics, traction force, and distraction distance were compared, with further subgroup analysis assessing the impact on traction force and distraction distance made by traction alone, with venting, and with capsulotomy. Statistical analysis included inferential testing with Kruskal-Wallis, Chi-squared, Fisher's Exact Test, superiority analysis, and regression analysis of demographic and clinical factors. A superiority margin of 1 mm was chosen based on a review of prior literature and principal investigator expertise. Statistical evaluation was performed by our institution's Study Design and Biostatistics Center.Results:Sixty-nine post-based distraction patients and 76 post-less distraction patients were compared. Minimal differences existed among demographic and clinical values between the post-based and post-less groups (Table 1). The post-based group had higher mean traction time (50.3 minutes vs. 44.2 minutes, p < 0.001), lower Hamstring Test scores (34.6 vs. 51.7, p < 0.001), and higher Modified Thomas Test scores (109.2 vs. 106.2, p < 0.012) when compared to the post-less group (Table 1). Mean traction force was significantly lower in the post-less group than the post-based group (p < 0.001) (Table 2). Venting and capsulotomy reduced traction force for both groups (p < 0.001) (Table 3). The mean distraction distance associated with the post-based group was generally greater than the post-less group (p < 0.01) (Table 4). The distraction distance associated with each group serially increased with venting and capsulotomy compared to the traction-only condition (p < 0.001) (Table 4). In superiority analysis with a superiority margin of 1 mm distraction distance, only capsulotomy was shown to provide statistically superior distraction (p < 0.001).Conclusions:Venting, capsulotomy, and post-based distraction are potential strategies to enhance distraction in a hip joint that is difficult to access with traction alone. Venting and capsulotomy can be used with post-less distraction to reduce the traction force applied to a distracted hip. Capsulotomy may be utilized with post-less distraction to improve central compartment access in hips with difficult distraction. While the lower traction forces in post-less distraction resulted in a smaller distraction distance than that obtained with post-based distraction, the difference is likely clinically insignificant since it was enough to complete the procedure.

Abstract

A symmetric
\phi^{4}
-
\phi^{2}|\phi|
-
\phi^{2}
model has recently attracted a lot of
attention due to its usefulness in studying tunable phase transitions. We analyze the
behavior of this model for the entire range of parameters and obtain its kink and pulse
solutions. For completeness, we also present several periodic solutions of this model.
Furthermore, we present a generalized symmetric
\phi^{4n}
-
\phi^{2n}|\phi|
-
\phi^{2}

model where
n=1,2,3,...
and obtain its kink and pulse solutions for arbitrary
n
.

1
Introduction

Recently, a symmetric
\phi^{4}
-
\phi^{2}|\phi|
-
\phi^{2}
triple well model containing a third
order term has attracted considerable attention in the literature
[
1
,
2
]
.
Specifically, a weakly first-order phase transition character in certain superfluids and
superconductors can be captured by such a model
[
1
]
. This model also
serves as a testbed to study topological defect formation in phase transitions with
tunable order
[
2
]
within the context of the Kibble-Zurek mechanism

[
3
,
4
]
.

However, to our surprise, there does not seem to exist a detailed analysis of the
various possible phases as well as topological and non-topological defects admitted
by this model. This is our main motivation and one of the tasks that we have undertaken
in this paper. In particular, we discuss in detail the behavior of the model

V(\phi)=\frac{a}{2}\phi^{2}-\frac{|c|}{3}\phi^{2}|\phi|+\frac{b}{4}\phi^{4}+d\,,

(1)

where
d
is a constant which one can choose as required, and in general
a
,
b
,

c
can be positive or negative. For
c=0
and
b>0
it reduces to the standard
double well
\phi^{4}
model (
\phi^{4}
-
\phi^{2}
) for second order transitions. The value
of
c
as compared to
a
for
b>0
determines the strength of the first order character
of the transition. Thus, this model is suitable to study tunable order phase transitions
[
2
]
.
Note that, this is another model for first order transitions in addition to the standard
triple well
\phi^{6}
model (
\phi^{6}
-
\phi^{4}
-
\phi^{2}
)
[
5
]
and the asymmetric
double well
\phi^{4}
model
[
6
,
7
]
. The latter arises when a third order nonlinearity
is symmetry allowed (e.g.,
\phi^{4}
-
\phi^{3}
-
\phi^{2}
)
[
6
,
7
]
. Note that there
is no modulus in the third order term in this case unlike the model considered in this paper.

The plan of the paper is as follows. In Sec. II we discuss the behavior of
this model for various possible values of the parameters
a,b,c
. In
Sec. III we obtain several novel hyperbolic pulse and kink solutions of the
model in the most interesting case of
a,b,c>0
. In Sec. IV we generalize
the symmetric
\phi^{4}
-
\phi^{2}|\phi|
-
\phi^{2}
model by presenting a
one-parameter family of symmetric triple well
\phi^{4n}
-
\phi^{2n}|\phi|
-
\phi^{2}

models, where
n=1,2,3,...
, and

V(\phi)=\frac{a}{2}\phi^{2}-\frac{|c|}{(2n+1)}\phi^{2n}|\phi|+\frac{b}{4n}\phi^{4n}+d\,.

(2)

We then discuss the behavior of the generalized model for various possible values
of the parameters
a,b,c
. Further, for an arbitrary integer
n
, we obtain its hyperbolic
kink and pulse solutions in the most interesting case of
a,b,c>0
. In Sec. V we
summarize the new results obtained in this paper and point out some of the open
problems. For completeness, in the Appendix we present a few representative periodic
solutions of the above symmetric
\phi^{4}
-
\phi^{2}|\phi|
-
\phi^{2}
model.

2
Analysis of the Symmetric Triple Well Model

Consider the symmetric triple well model characterized by the potential
(
1
). Let us discuss the behavior of this model for different possible
values of the parameters
a,b,c
.

Case I:
a,b>0

Let us first consider the most interesting case of
a,b>0
. On
differentiating Eq. (
1
) we obtain

\frac{dV}{d\phi}=\phi(a-|c||\phi|+b|\phi|^{2})\,.

(3)

Thus the extrema of the potential are at

\phi=0\,,~~|\phi|=\frac{|c|\pm\sqrt{c^{2}-4ab}}{2b}\,.

(4)

Therefore, if
c^{2}<4ab
then the potential has minima only at
\phi=0
. On the
other hand, if
4ab<c^{2}<(9/2)ab
,
\phi=0
is still an absolute minimum
while
|\phi|=|\phi_{-}|=\frac{|c|+\sqrt{c^{2}-4ab}}{2b}
is a local minimum.
The two minima at
\phi=0
and
|\phi|=|\phi_{-}|
are degenerate with
each other provided
2c^{2}=9ab
. Note that since it is
|\phi_{-}|
,
one actually has three degenerate minima at
\phi=0
and
\phi=\pm|\phi_{-}|
.
Finally, for
2c^{2}>9ab
,
\phi=0
is a local minimum and
|\phi|=|\phi_{-}|

is an absolute minimum. Note also that for
2c^{2}>9ab
, one has a maximum
at
\phi=|\phi_{+}|=\frac{|c|-\sqrt{c^{2}-4ab}}{2b}
. As we will see below,
the local minimum at
\phi=0
disappears at
a=0
and so also the two
maxima, and
\phi=0
is now a point of inflection while
\phi=|\phi_{-}|

continues to be an absolute minimum. Finally, for
a<0
, now
\phi=0
is
a maximum while
|\phi_{-}|
continues to be a minimum. Notice that this
picture is very similar to that of the
a\phi^{2}
-
c\phi^{4}
-
b\phi^{6}
model

[
5
]
.

Case II:
b,a<0

In this case the extrema of the potential are at

\phi=0\,,~~|\phi|=|\phi_{-}|=\frac{|c|+\sqrt{c^{2}+4|a|b}}{2b}\,.

(5)

Out of these while
\phi=0
is a maximum,
|\phi|=\phi_{-}
is a
minimum.

Case III:
b>0,a=0

In this case the extrema of the potential are at

\phi=0\,,~~|\phi|=|\phi_{-}|=\frac{|c|}{b}\,.

(6)

Out of these while
\phi=0
is a point of inflection,
|\phi|=|c|/b

is an absolute minimum. Thus, there must be a kink solution from

\phi=-c/b
to
\phi=+c/b
.

Case IV:
a>0,b=0

In this case while
\phi=0
is an absolute minimum,
|\phi|=a/|c|

corresponds to maxima. Hence there should be a pulse solution around

\phi=0
going from
\phi=-a/c
to
\phi=+a/c
.

Case V:
a,b,c<0

Note, instead of
|c|
, we will consider
-|c|
. Thus, the extrema of the
potential are at

\phi=0\,,~~|\phi_{\pm}|=\frac{|c|\pm\sqrt{c^{2}-4|a||b|}}{2|b|}\,.

(7)

It is easy to see that while the potential goes to
-\infty
as
\phi\rightarrow\pm\infty
, for finite
\phi
one has maxima at
\phi=0
and

\phi=\phi_{+}
while a minimum is at
\phi=\phi_{-}
. Thus, one
could have a pulse solution around
\phi=0
.

3
Kink and Pulse Solutions in Case
a,b>0

For the remaining part of the paper we mostly confine ourselves to the most
interesting case of
a,b>0
.
For simplicity, we now do a dimensional analysis so that the physics will
essentially depend on one parameter
\mu={ab}/{c^{2}}
. If we write the
Ginzburg-Landau type free energy, it is given by

F_{T}=(G/2)\left(\frac{d\phi}{dx}\right)^{2}+(a/2)\phi^{2}-(|c|/3)\phi^{2}|\phi|+(b/4)\phi^{4}\,,

(8)

where
G
is the gradient coefficient that sets the scale for the kink or pulse
width. On rescaling, that is considering

F_{R}=\frac{c^{4}}{b^{3}}\,,~~\eta=\frac{b\phi}{|c|}\,,

(9)

one finds that

\frac{F_{T}}{F_{R}}=g\left(\frac{d\eta}{dx}\right)^{2}+(\mu/2)\eta^{2}-(1/3)\eta^{2}|\eta|+(1/4)\eta^{4}\,,

(10)

where
\mu={ab}{/c^{2}}
. From now onwards we consider various
solutions of the field equation (where for simplicity we revert to denoting
\phi

instead of
\eta
)

\phi_{xx}=\phi^{3}-\phi|\phi|+\mu\phi\,.

(11)

In this case, at the point of three degenerate minima (that is, first order
transition point), i.e.
\mu=2/9
, one expects two (half) kink and two (half)
antikink solutions. On the other hand, below the transition point, i.e. when

\mu<2/9
, there are two degenerate minima and one expects a kink (and
the corresponding antikink) solution. Besides, we also expect pulse solutions
in the cases of both above and below the first order transition point, i.e. for

2/9<\mu<1/4
as well as when
\mu<2/9
. Note, when
2/9<\mu<1/4
,
there are two local minima and
\phi=0
is an absolute minimum. In Fig. 1,
a plot of the potential
V(\phi)
vs
\phi
, Eq. (
11
), is given for various
representative values of
\mu
(note
\mu={ab}/{c^{2}}
).

It is worth pointing out that the symmetric model which we are discussing here
is very different from the asymmetric
\phi^{4}
model
[
6
,
7
]
for
which instead of the field Eq. (
11
), we have the equation

\phi_{xx}=\phi^{3}-\phi^{2}+\mu\phi\,.

(12)

The corresponding potential has an absolute minimum at
\phi=0
and a local minimum
at
\phi=\phi_{+}=\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4\mu}}{2}
in case
2/9<\mu<1/4
while
\phi=0

is a local minimum and
\phi=\phi_{+}
is an absolute minimum in case

\mu<2/9
; and
\phi=0
and
\phi=\phi_{+}
are degenerate at

\mu=2/9
. As a result, the physics described by the symmetric and the
asymmetric
\phi^{4}
models is very different. Note that asymmetric double well
potentials are of significant physical interest in the study of quantum tunneling
and localization
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
.

. (b) Just above

3.1
Kink and Antikink Solutions at
\mu=2/9

We now show that at the point
\mu=2/9
where there are three
degenerate minima at
\phi=0
and
\phi=\pm\phi_{-}=\frac{|c|+\sqrt{c^{2}-4ab}}{2b}
, there are several exact solutions
including the kink and the periodic kink solutions. While we discuss the
kink solution below, the corresponding periodic kink solution is discussed in
the Appendix.

Instead of the rescaled field Eq. (
11
), we consider the following
field equation

\phi_{xx}=\phi^{3}-\phi^{2}+\mu\phi

(13)

and obtain those solutions for which
\phi=|\phi|
, so that they are
automatically also the solutions of the field Eq. (
11
).

Kink Solutions at
\mu=2/9

It is easy to check that

\phi=A+B\tanh(\beta x)\,,

(14)

is an exact solution of Eq. (
13
) provided

A=\frac{1}{3}\,,~~\beta^{2}=\frac{1}{18}\,,~~B=\pm\frac{1}{3}\,.

(15)

Clearly for this solution
\phi=|\phi|
and hence Eq. (
14
) is also an
exact solution of Eq. (
11
). Observe that since
B=\pm A
, hence
we have both the kink and the antikink solutions for
\phi>0
as well as for

\phi<0
. For
\phi>0
we have

\phi(x)=\frac{1}{3}[1\pm\tanh(\beta x)]\,,

(16)

while for
\phi<0
we have

\phi(x)=-\frac{1}{3}[1\pm\tanh(\beta x)]\,,

(17)

where
\beta=\sqrt{{1}/3\sqrt{2}}
. In Fig. 2 a plot of the kink and the
corresponding antikink solutions
\phi(x)
vs
x
, Eqs. (
16
) and
(
17
), is given at the transition temperature
T_{c}
corresponding to

\mu=2/9
.

\phi(x)
vs
x

)
and Eq. (

), at the transition temperature
T_{c}
corresponding to
the potential
V(\phi)
(left panel) with three degenerate minima at

3.2
Kink and Antikink Solutions for
\mu<2/9

For
\mu<2/9
, we have two degenerate absolute minima at
\phi=\pm\phi_{-}
and hence there should be a kink and an antikink solution. In this
case the potential can be reexpressed as

V(\phi)=\frac{1}{4}(\delta-|\phi|)^{2}\left[|\phi|^{2}+2\left(\delta-\frac{2}{3}\right)|\phi|+\delta\left(\delta-\frac{2}{3}\right)\right]\,,

(18)

where

\delta=\phi_{-}=\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4\mu}}{2}\,.

(19)

In order to get the kink solution, we need to solve the first order
self-dual equation

\frac{d\phi}{dx}=\sqrt{2V(\phi)}\,,

(20)

which in our case takes the form

\int\frac{d\phi}{\sqrt{|\phi|^{2}+2(\delta-\frac{2}{3})|\phi|+\delta(\delta-\frac{2c}{3b})}~(\delta-|\phi|)}=\frac{x}{\sqrt{2}}\,.

(21)

We can breakup the left hand side into two parts depending on whether
\phi>0

or
\phi<0
. Thus we have to solve the following first order equations
depending on if
\phi>0
or
\phi<0
:

\int\frac{d\phi}{\sqrt{\phi^{2}+2(\delta-\frac{2}{3})\phi+\delta(\delta-\frac{2}{3})}~(\delta-\phi)}=\frac{x}{\sqrt{2}}\,,~~~\phi>0\,,

(22)

\int\frac{d\phi}{\sqrt{\phi^{2}-2(\delta-\frac{2}{3})\phi+\delta(\delta-\frac{2}{3})}~(\delta+\phi)}=\frac{x}{\sqrt{2}}\,,~~~\phi<0\,.

(23)

The integrals in Eqs. (
22
) and (
23
) are easily done using

\int\frac{dx}{x\sqrt{Cx^{2}+Bx+A}}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{A}}\tanh^{-1}\left[\frac{2A+Bx}{2\sqrt{A}\sqrt{A+Bx+Cx^{2}}}\right]\,,

(24)

provided
A>0
. For
\phi>0
, on making the substitution
u=\delta-\phi
,
the integral (
22
) takes the form

-\int\frac{du}{u\sqrt{u^{2}-4(\delta-\frac{1}{3})u+2\delta(2\delta-1)}}=\frac{x}{\sqrt{2}}\,.

(25)

On using the integral (
25
) we then obtain for
\phi>0

\displaystyle\tanh\left[\sqrt{\delta(2\delta-1)}x\right]=\frac{2\delta(2\delta-1)-2(\delta-\frac{1}{3})u}{\sqrt{2\delta(2\delta-1)}\sqrt{u^{2}-4(\delta-\frac{1}{3})u+2\delta(2\delta-1)}}\,.

(26)

Note that we have used
u=\delta-\phi
. From here it immediately follows
that

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\phi=\delta\,.

(27)

On the other hand if
\phi=0
, then
x>0
since in that case

\tanh\left[\sqrt{\delta(2\delta-1)}x_{0}\right]=\frac{[\sqrt{1-4\mu}-\frac{1}{3}]^{1/2}}{\sqrt{1-4\mu}}>0\,,

(28)

since
\mu<2/9
.

Proceeding in the same way, for
\phi<0
we find that

\displaystyle\tanh\left[\sqrt{\delta(2\delta-1)}x\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\delta(2\delta-1)-2(\delta-\frac{1}{3})u}{\sqrt{2\delta(2\delta-1)}\sqrt{u^{2}-4(\delta-\frac{1}{3})u+2\delta(2\delta-1)}}\,.

(29)

Note that here
u=\delta+\phi
. It then follows that

\lim_{x\rightarrow-\infty}\phi=-\delta\,.

(30)

On the other hand if
\phi=0
, then
x<0
since in that case

\tanh\left[\sqrt{\delta(2\delta-1)}x_{0}\right]=-\frac{[\sqrt{1-4\mu}-\frac{1}{3}]^{1/2}}{\sqrt{1-4\mu}}<0\,,

(31)

Note that in general

\phi(-\infty)=-\phi(\infty)
. Thus, while for
\phi>0
,
\phi
goes from

\phi=\delta
to
\phi=0
in case
x
goes from
\infty
to
x_{0}
as given
by Eq. (
26
), while for
\phi<0
,
\phi
goes from
\phi=-\delta
to

\phi=0
in case
x
goes from
-\infty
to
-x_{0}
as given by
Eq. (
3.2
). In Fig. 3, a plot of the kink solution
\phi(x)
as a function
of
x
is given corresponding to
T<T_{c}
for a representative value of

\mu=0.0979
.

From the above analysis as well as from the figure it is clear that the region

-x_{0}
to
x_{0}
is not accessible to the kink solution. One possible
explanation is that the kink profile is like the trajectory of a ball that hits
the wall at
x_{0}
and bounces because of the
|\phi|
term. A similar ball on the
other side of
-x_{0}
does the same bounce. Thus the two pieces from
-\infty

to
-x_{0}
and
x_{0}
to
\infty
can be considered as the kink solution.
Clearly one needs to have a better understanding of this kink solution.

3.3
Pulse Solution For
\mu<2/9

The symmetric
\phi^{4}
triple well Eq. (
13
) admits a hyperbolic pulse solution

\phi=\frac{A}{B+\cosh^{2}(\beta x)}\,,~~B>0\,,

(32)

provided

\mu=4\beta^{2}\,,~~\frac{9\mu}{2}=\frac{4B(B+1)}{(2B+1)^{2}}<1\,,~~A=\frac{3(2B+1)\mu}{2}\,.

(33)

Notice that this solution is only valid if
\mu<2/9
, i.e. when the potential
has two degenerate minima. Further, for this
solution
\phi=|\phi|
and hence Eq. (
32
) is also an exact solution of
Eq. (
11
).

On using the well known identity
[
12
]

\frac{1}{B+\cosh^{2}(y)}=\frac{\tanh(y+\Delta)-\tanh(y-\Delta)}{\sinh(2\Delta)}\,,~~B=\sinh^{2}(\Delta)\,,

(34)

we can reexpress the solution of Eq. (
32
) as a
superposition of a kink-antikink-like solution, i.e.

\phi=\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{2}}[\tanh(\beta x+\Delta)-\tanh(\beta x-\Delta)]\,,

(35)

where
B=\sinh^{2}(\Delta)
.
A plot of the pulse solution
\phi(x)
vs
x
, Eq. (
32
) , is given in Fig. 4 for

T<T_{c}
at a representative value of
\mu
.

3.4
Pulse Solution For
2/9<\mu<1/4

The symmetric
\phi^{4}
triple well Eq. (
13
) also admits another
hyperbolic pulse solution

\phi=D-\frac{A}{B+\cosh^{2}(\beta x)}\,,~~B>0\,,

(36)

provided

D=\mu+D^{2}\,,

(37)

A^{2}=8B(B+1)\beta^{2}\,,

(38)

4\beta^{2}=D^{2}-\mu\,,

(39)

(3D-1)A=6(2B+1)\beta^{2}\,.

(40)

On solving Eqs. (
37
) and (
39
) we obtain

D=\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4\mu}}{2}\,,~~\beta^{2}=\frac{\sqrt{1-4\mu}[1+\sqrt{1-4\mu}]}{8}\,.

(41)

Further, on solving Eqs. (
37
) to (
40
) one obtains a quadratic
equation for the ratio
A/D=y
in terms of
B
. In particular, we obtain

2y^{2}-3(2B+1)y+4B(B+1)=0\,,

(42)

which on solving gives

y=\frac{A}{D}=\frac{3(2B+1)\pm\sqrt{(2B+1)^{2}+8}}{4}\,.

(43)

Thus, solution (
36
) can be reexpressed as

\phi=\frac{D[(B-y)+\cosh^{2}(\beta x)]}{B+\cosh^{2}(\beta x)}\,.

(44)

We must demand that
\phi=|\phi|
which is ensured if
B+1-y>0

since
\cosh^{2}(\beta y)\geq 1
. Clearly, this is ensured if we
choose the lower root of
y
in Eq. (
43
). Thus, we must choose

y=\frac{A}{D}=\frac{3(2B+1)-\sqrt{(2B+1)^{2}+8}}{4}\,.

(45)

One can now show that

\frac{1}{\mu}=\frac{2y[(2B+1)-y]^{2}}{B(B+1)[3(2B+1)-4y]}\,,

(46)

where
y
is given in terms of
B
by Eq. (
45
). It is then
easily shown that for this solution
2/9<\mu<1/4
.
Since for this solution
\phi=|\phi|
, hence Eq. (
36
) is also
an exact solution of Eq. (
11
). Thus, this solution is valid when
there are two local minima and an absolute minimum at
\phi=0
.

On using the identity of Eq. (
34
)
one can reexpress the solution (
36
) as a superposition of
kink-antikink-like solution over a background, i.e.

\phi=D-\sqrt{2}\beta[\tanh(\beta x+\Delta)-\tanh(\beta x-\Delta)]\,,

(47)

where
B=\sinh^{2}(\Delta)
while
D
and
\beta
are given by Eq. (
41
).
A plot of the pulse solution
\phi(x)
vs
x
, Eq. (
36
), is given in
Fig. 5 for
T>T_{c}
at a representative value of
2/9<\mu<1/4
.

4
One-Parameter Family of Generalized Symmetric Triple Well Models

In this paper we have discussed the symmetric
\phi^{4}
-
\phi^{2}|\phi|
-
\phi^{2}
triple
well model characterized by Eq. (
1
). One interesting question is if one can generalize it
to arbitrary even power (i.e.,
\phi^{4n}
-
\phi^{2n}|\phi|
-
\phi^{2}
) symmetric triple well
models. The answer to the question turns out to be in the affirmative. We now discuss such
a model and its interesting properties. In particular, we consider a symmetric model
as given by Eq. (
2
).

4.1
Analysis of the Generalized Model

Consider the symmetric model characterized by the potential
(
2
). Let us discuss the behavior of this model for different possible
positive values of the parameters
a,b
.

On differentiating Eq. (
2
) we obtain

\frac{dV}{d\phi}=\phi\left(a-|c||\phi|^{2n-1}+b|\phi|^{4n-2}\right)\,.

(48)

Therefore, the extrema of the potential are at

\phi=0\,,~~|\phi|^{2n-1}=\frac{|c|\pm\sqrt{c^{2}-4ab}}{2b}\,.

(49)

Thus, if
c^{2}<4ab
then the potential has minima only at
\phi=0
. On the
other hand, if
4ab<c^{2}<\frac{(2n+1)^{2}ab}{2n}
,
\phi=0
is an
absolute minimum while
|\phi|=|\phi_{-}|=[\frac{|c|+\sqrt{c^{2}-4ab}}{2b}]^{1/(2n-1)}
is a local minimum and
|\phi|=|\phi_{+}|=[\frac{|c|-\sqrt{c^{2}-4ab}}{2b}]^{1/(2n-1)}
is the maximum in between the two
minima. The two minima at
\phi=0
and
|\phi|=|\phi_{-}|
are degenerate
with each other provided
c^{2}=\frac{(2n+1)^{2}ab}{2n}
. Note that since it is

|\phi_{-}|
, one actually has three degenerate minima at
\phi=0

and
\phi=\pm|\phi_{-}|
. Finally, for
c^{2}>\frac{(2n+1)^{2}ab}{2n}
,

\phi=0
is a local minimum and
|\phi|=|\phi_{-}|
is an absolute minimum
and one has a maximum at
\phi=|\phi_{+}|=[\frac{|c|-\sqrt{c^{2}-4ab}}{2b}]^{1/(2n+1)}
.
Notice that this picture is very similar to that of the
\phi^{2}
-
\phi^{2n+2}
-
\phi^{4n+2}

triple well model
[
11
]
.

For simplicity, we now carry out a dimensional analysis so that the physics will
essentially depend on one parameter
\mu={ab}/{c^{2}}
. If we write the free
energy, it is given by

F_{T}=(G/2)\left(\frac{d\phi}{dx}\right)^{2}+(a/2)\phi^{2}-\left(|c|/(2n+1)\right)\phi^{2n}|\phi|+(b/4n)\phi^{4n}\,,~~a,b,c>0\,.

(50)

On rescaling, that is considering

F_{R}=\frac{c^{4n/(2n-1)}}{b^{(2n+1)/(2n-1)}}\,,~~\eta=\left(\frac{b}{|c|}\right)^{1/(2n-1)}\phi\,,

(51)

one finds that

\frac{F_{T}}{F_{R}}=g\left(\frac{d\eta}{dx}\right)^{2}+(\mu/2)\eta^{2}-\left(1/(2n+1)\right)\eta^{2n}|\eta|+(1/4n)\eta^{4n}\,,

(52)

where
\mu={ab}/{c^{2}}
and
g=G/2F_{R}
. Thus, the rescaled potential is given by
Eq. (
52
) (where we relabel
\eta
as
\phi
)

V(\phi)=\frac{\mu}{2}\phi^{2}-\frac{1}{2n+1}\phi^{2n}|\phi|+\frac{1}{4n}\phi^{4n}\,.

(53)

In this case, at the point of three degenerate minima, i.e.
\mu=\frac{2n}{(2n+1)^{2}}
,
one expects two (half) kink and two (half) antikink solutions. On the
other hand, when
\mu<\frac{2n}{(2n+1)^{2}}
, there are two degenerate minima
and one expects a kink (and a corresponding antikink) solution. Besides, we also
expect pulse solutions.

4.2
Kink and Antikink Solutions at
\mu=\frac{2n}{(2n+1)^{2}}

We now show that at the point
\mu=\frac{2n}{(2n+1)^{2}}
, where there are three
degenerate minima at
\phi=0
and
\phi=\pm\phi_{-}=[\frac{|c|+\sqrt{c^{2}-4ab}}{2b}]^{1/(2n-1)}
, there are two (half) kink and
two (half) antikink solutions.

We thus need to consider the field equation

\phi_{xx}=\phi^{4n-1}-\phi^{2n-1}|\phi|+\mu\phi\,.

(54)

We instead consider the following field equation

\phi_{xx}=\phi^{4n-1}-\phi^{2n}+\mu\phi

(55)

and obtain those solutions for which
\phi=|\phi|
so that they are
automatically also the solutions of the field Eq. (
54
).

It is easy to check that

\phi=A[1\pm\tanh(\beta x)]^{1/(2n-1)}\,,

(56)

is an exact solution of Eq. (
55
) provided

A^{2}=\left[\frac{n^{2}}{(2n+1)^{2}}\right]^{1/(2n-1)}\,,~~\beta^{2}=\frac{n(2n-1)^{2}}{2(2n+1)^{2}}\,,~~\mu=\frac{2n}{(2n+1)^{2}}\,.

(57)

Clearly, for this solution
\phi=|\phi|
and hence Eq. (
56
) is also an
exact solution of Eq. (
54
). Observe that
we have both kink and antikink solutions for
\phi>0
as well as for

\phi<0
. For
\phi>0
we have

\phi=\left[\frac{n}{(2n+1)}\right]^{1/(2n-1)}[1\pm\tanh(\beta x)]^{1/(2n-1)}\,,

(58)

while for
\phi<0
we have

\phi=-\left[\frac{n}{2n+1}\right]^{1/(2n-1)}[1\pm\tanh(\beta x)]^{1/(2n-1)}\,,

(59)

where
\beta=\sqrt{\frac{n(2n-1)^{2}}{2(2n+1)^{2}}}
.

4.3
Pulse Solution I

The symmetric
\phi^{4n}
triple well Eq. (
55
) also admits a hyperbolic pulse
solution

\phi=\frac{A}{[B+\cosh^{2}(\beta x)]^{1/(2n-1)}}\,,~~B>0\,,

(60)

provided

\displaystyle\mu=\frac{4\beta^{2}}{(2n-1)^{2}}\,,~~A=\left[\frac{(2B+1)(2n+1)\mu}{2}\right]^{1/(2n-1)}\,,

\displaystyle\frac{(2n+1)^{2}\mu}{2n}=\frac{4B(B+1)}{(2B+1)^{2}}<1\,.

(61)

Thus, this solution is valid only if
\mu<\frac{2n}{(2n+1)^{2}}
. Notice that
for this solution
\phi=|\phi|
and hence Eq. (
60
) is also an exact
solution of Eq. (
54
).

On using the well known hyperbolic identity (
34
)
[
12
]

we can reexpress the solution (
60
) as a
superposition of a kink-antikink-like solution, i.e.

\phi=\frac{n\mu}{{2}^{1/(2n-1)}}[\tanh(\beta x+\Delta)-\tanh(\beta x-\Delta)]^{1/(2n-1)}\,,

(62)

where
B=\sinh^{2}(\Delta)
while
A
is as given by Eq. (
4.3
).

4.4
Pulse Solution II

The symmetric
\phi^{4n}
triple well Eq. (
55
) also admits another hyperbolic
pulse solution

\phi=\left[\frac{D\cosh^{2}(\beta x)+DB-A}{B+\cosh^{2}(\beta x)}\right]^{1/(2n-1)}\,,~~B,D>0\,,~~n=1,2,3,...\,.

(63)

In this case one finds that

\displaystyle\phi_{xx}=\frac{2\beta^{2}}{(2n-1)^{2}}\Big[-2D(2n-1)\cosh^{6}(\beta x)+[3(2n-1)D+2A]\cosh^{4}(\beta x)

\displaystyle+[2(2n-1)DB(1+B)-2(2n-1)AB-(2n+1)A]\cosh^{2}(\beta x)

\displaystyle-(2n-1)B(BD-A)\Big]\,.

(64)

On substituting Eqs. (
63
) and (
4.4
) in Eq. (
55
) and
comparing coefficients of different powers of
\cosh^{8,6,4,2,0}(\beta x)

on both sides of the equation yields

\mu=D-D^{2}\,,~~2D^{2}-D=\frac{4\beta^{2}}{(2n-1)}\,,

(65)

A^{2}=\frac{8B(B+1)n\beta^{2}}{(2n-1)^{2}}\,,

(66)

4B(B+1)ny=4B(B+1)(2n+1)A-(2n+1)(2B+1)Ay\,,

(67)

4B(B+1)n=6(2B+1)(2n-1)A-2(10n-7)Ay\,,

(68)

where
A=Dy
. On using Eqs. (
67
) and (
68
) one finds that
y

satisfies the quadratic equation

2(10n-7)y^{2}-(14n-5)(2B+1)y+4B(B+1)(2n+1)=0\,,

(69)

so that

y=\frac{(14n-5)(2B+1)\pm\sqrt{(2B+1)^{2}(14n-5)^{2}-32B(B+1)(2n+1)(10n-7)}}{4(10n-7)}\,.

(70)

In order for the solution to be real, it is necessary that
B>y
(note

A=Dy
) which is ensured if we choose the lower root for
y
so that in
our case
y
is given by

y=\frac{(14n-5)(2B+1)-\sqrt{(2B+1)^{2}(14n-5)^{2}-32B(B+1)(2n+1)(10n-7)}}{4(10n-7)}\,.

(71)

From Eq. (
65
) it follows that

D=\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4\mu}}{2}\,,

(72)

so that
\mu<1/4
, as otherwise
D
is not real. We now show that

\mu>\frac{2n}{(2n+1)^{2}}
. On solving Eqs. (
67
) and (
68
) one
can show that

D=\frac{2B(B+1)n[2(10n-7)y-(2n+1)(2B+1)]}{(2n+1)y[16B(B+1)(n-1)-3(2n-1)]}\,.

(73)

Now in case
\mu=\frac{2n}{(2n+1)^{2}}
, it follows from Eq. (
72
) that

D=2n
. On using
D=2n
it easily follows that this is impossible. Note
that for
n=1
we have already shown that
\mu>2/9
. Notice that
for this solution
\phi=|\phi|
and hence Eq. (
63
) is also an exact
solution of Eq. (
54
).

5
Conclusion

In this paper we have analyzed the symmetric
\phi^{4}
-
\phi^{2}|\phi|
-
\phi^{2}
triple well
model given by Eq. (
1
) for various ranges of parameters. This model is relevant to
condensed matter systems
[
1
]
as well as for studying tunable phase
transitions
[
2
]
. We then specialized it to the most interesting case when
a,b

are positive and obtained kink solutions in the case when there are three degenerate minima
as well as when there are two degenerate minima. We also obtained two pulse solutions,
one of which is when there are two degenerate absolute minima while the other one when
there are two local minima and one absolute minimum. In the Appendix we have given
a few representative periodic solutions of the symmetric triple well model. Although the
analytic solutions are different from the standard
\phi^{6}
model solutions
[
5
]
,
the overall behavior is similar, albeit with a weaker nonlinearity (i.e.,
\phi^{4}
). A double
well in a field (
\phi^{4}
-
\phi^{2}
-
\phi
) and the asymmetric double well in a field are
alternative models of first order phase transitions depending on the physical situation

[
6
,
7
]
.

Additionally, in Sec. IV we have generalized the symmetric
\phi^{4}
-
\phi^{2}|\phi|
-
\phi^{2}

model and discussed a one-parameter family of symmetric
\phi^{4n}
-
\phi^{2n}|\phi|
-
\phi^{2}

triple well models for any positive integer
n
and analyzed the behavior of this model
for various ranges of parameters. In addition, we obtained kink solutions when there
are three degenerate minima and two pulse solutions, one when there are two
degenerate absolute minima while the other in case there are two local minima
and one absolute minimum. These findings are similar to those for the

\phi^{4n+2}
-
\phi^{4n}
-
\phi^{2}
model
[
11
]
.

This paper raises several open problems. Some of which are:

1.

For the symmetric
\phi^{4}
-
\phi^{2}|\phi|
-
\phi^{2}
model as given by Eq.
(
1
), while we have obtained a kink solution when there are two degenerate minima,
it is not in a satisfactory form. Is it possible to obtain the kink solution in a better form?

2.

Are there more hyperbolic and periodic solutions to the symmetric triple
well model? For example (in the Appendix) we have been able to obtain the
periodic solutions only in case
\mu=2/9
. It would be desirable to obtain
periodic solutions even when
\mu<2/9
.

3.

For the generalized
\phi^{4n}
-
\phi^{2n}|\phi|
-
\phi^{2}
triple well model
as given by Eq. (
2
), can one obtain an analytic form for the kink solution when
there are two degenerate minima? Moreover, can one obtain some periodic solutions
for arbitrary
n
?

4.

Finally, can one find physical relevance, possibly in condensed matter or field
theory, of at least some of the generalized
\phi^{4n}
-
\phi^{2n}|\phi|
-
\phi^{2}

models for one or two values of
n
?

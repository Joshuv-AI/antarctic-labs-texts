Acute respiratory distress syndrome associated with e-cigarette or vaping product use in Bogotá, Colombia: a lung injury epidemic
Authors: Isabel Hernández-Linares, María Cristina Martínez-Avila, Alieth Lucia Acosta-Cardozo, Olga Milena García
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundE-cigarette or vaping product use-associated lung injury (EVALI) emerged as a serious public health concern following outbreaks in North America, but remains underreported in Latin America. This study aims to describe the clinical, radiologic, and histopathologic features of EVALI in a tertiary care center in Bogotá, Colombia.MethodsWe conducted a retrospective case series of patients hospitalized with suspected EVALI between January 2020 and July 2024. Background

The global use of electronic cigarettes (e-cigarettes) and vaping devices has increased significantly since their introduction, particularly among adolescents and young adults. Promoted as safer alternatives to combustible cigarettes and potential tools for smoking cessation, these devices may deliver aerosolized mixtures of nicotine, tetrahydrocannabinol (THC), flavoring agents, and other chemicals [1, 2]. However, emerging evidence has linked vaping to a range of adverse health outcomes, most notably a form of acute respiratory illness now known as e-cigarette or vaping-associated lung injury (EVALI) [3–5].

EVALI was first recognized during a 2019 outbreak in the United States, with patients typically presenting with non-specific respiratory and gastrointestinal symptoms, bilateral ground-glass opacities on chest imaging, and recent vaping history [6]. The U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) defines EVALI as a diagnosis of exclusion, requiring radiographic abnormalities, recent e-cigarette use within 90 days, and the absence of alternative diagnoses such as infectious or autoimmune etiologies [7].

Although the precise pathophysiological mechanisms underlying EVALI remain under investigation, proposed contributors include direct epithelial toxicity, oxidative stress, lipid-laden macrophage activation, and inflammatory responses triggered by substances such as vitamin E acetate, THC oils, and flavoring agents [8–10]. Histopathologic findings in EVALI cases have included organizing pneumonia, diffuse alveolar damage, and eosinophilic pneumonitis [11, 12]. While most patients recover with corticosteroid therapy and supportive care, a substantial proportion require hospitalization or intensive care, particularly when diagnosis is delayed.

Most epidemiologic data on EVALI originates from North America and Europe, with limited case series reported in Latin America. However, recent data suggest that vaping prevalence is increasing in the region. According to the 2022 National Survey on Psychoactive Substance Use in Colombia, e-cigarettes are now the second most consumed psychoactive substance among school-aged children, with a lifetime prevalence of 22.7% and a mean initiation age of 14.1 years [13]. In this context, isolated case reports have emerged from Colombian hospitals describing EVALI with systemic complications, including myocardial infarction and acute kidney injury [14].

This study aims to contribute to the growing international literature on EVALI by presenting a retrospective case series of 25 patients hospitalized with EVALI in a tertiary care hospital in Bogotá, Colombia. We describe the clinical, radiological, and histopathological features of these cases, highlighting the need for heightened clinical suspicion and public health awareness in low- and middle-income countries.

Methods

This retrospective case series was conducted at Fundación Santa Fe de Bogotá, a tertiary care academic hospital in Bogotá, Colombia. We included all patients admitted between January 2020 and July 2024 with suspected e-cigarette or vaping product use-associated lung injury (EVALI). The study was approved by the institution’s research ethics committee, and all data were anonymized prior to analysis. Clinical trial number: not applicable.

Inclusion criteria were based on the case definition provided by the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) for EVALI, and included: (1) use of electronic cigarette or vaping products within 90 days prior to symptom onset; (2) presence of pulmonary opacities, particularly ground-glass opacities, on chest radiograph or computed tomography (CT); and (3) exclusion of alternative plausible diagnoses such as bacterial or viral pneumonia, cardiac disease, or rheumatologic conditions.

Patients were identified through a retrospective chart review of hospital admissions flagged by pulmonary consultations or ICD-10 discharge codes related to acute lung injury (e.g., J80, J68.9). Each case was independently reviewed by two board-certified pulmonologists to determine whether CDC criteria for EVALI were met and to exclude alternative diagnoses. Cases that did not fulfill all inclusion criteria were excluded.

We reviewed the medical records of all eligible patients to extract relevant demographic, clinical, laboratory, and imaging data. Specific variables collected included age, sex, history and type of vaping product used (nicotine- or THC-based), presenting symptoms (respiratory and gastrointestinal), laboratory inflammatory markers, radiographic findings, and treatment received. The need for intensive care unit (ICU) admission, mechanical ventilation, and corticosteroid therapy was also recorded, along with length of hospital stay and follow-up imaging outcomes.

Infectious etiologies were excluded through a combination of clinical evaluation, laboratory testing (including respiratory viral panels, influenza and HIV screening), sputum or tracheal aspirate cultures, and bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) when performed. For patients who did not undergo BAL, infections were ruled out using clinical assessment, respiratory viral PCR testing, blood cultures, sputum cultures (when available), and HIV serology. A subset of patients underwent fiberoptic bronchoscopy with BAL and/or lung biopsy for diagnostic clarification. Histopathologic reports were reviewed to identify patterns consistent with EVALI, such as organizing pneumonia or interstitial inflammation.

Descriptive statistics were used to summarize patient characteristics and clinical outcomes. Due to the nature of the study, no formal hypothesis testing or comparative analysis was performed.

Results

We identified 25 patients who met CDC criteria for EVALI and were admitted to Fundación Santa Fe de Bogotá between January 2020 and July 2024. The mean age was 30 years (range: 18–45), and 80% of the patients were male. Baseline demographic, clinical, and diagnostic characteristics are summarized in Table 1. The median duration of symptoms prior to hospital presentation was 6 days (IQR: 4–9). Most patients (72%, n = 18) reported exclusive use of e-cigarettes without concurrent combustible tobacco, while 28% (n = 7) had a history of dual use. Among those using vaping products, 84% (n = 21) reported nicotine-based liquids, and 16% (n = 4) reported products containing THC. Seven patients (28%) obtained their cartridges from informal street vendors, often lacking clear labeling or composition data. Demographic, Clinical, Imaging, and treatment characteristics of patients with EVALI

Summary of clinical symptoms, respiratory support, vaping product characteristics, and hospital course among 25 patients diagnosed with EVALI at Fundación Santa Fe de Bogotá, a tertiary academic hospital in Colombia, between January 2020 and July 2024. Data were collected retrospectively. Categories are grouped into four sections: symptoms (blue), respiratory support required (orange), vaping product characteristics (purple), and hospital course and invasive procedures (green). Percentages reflect the proportion of the total cohort. This figure is designed to be interpreted independently and provides a compact summary of patient characteristics and clinical course

The most commonly reported symptoms were cough (88%, n = 22), dyspnea (80%, n = 20), and fever (76%, n = 19), with a median recorded temperature of 38.3 °C. Gastrointestinal symptoms were present in 48% (n = 12), including nausea (36%, n = 9), vomiting (28%, n = 7), and diarrhea (12%, n = 3). Chest pain was reported in 24% (n = 6), and fatigue in 40% (n = 10). Oxygen desaturation on presentation (SpO₂ < 92% on room air) was noted in 68% (n = 17). Laboratory findings revealed leukocytosis (WBC > 11,000/mm³) in 68% of patients, with a median white blood cell count of 13,200/mm³. C-reactive protein was elevated (> 10 mg/L) in 84% (n = 21), with a median value of 78 mg/L (IQR: 54–120). Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) was elevated in 60% (n = 15), and 3 patients had transient elevation of liver transaminases. HIV and influenza testing were negative in all cases; two patients had low-positive cytomegalovirus (CMV) PCR titers but without organ involvement.

Chest CT imaging revealed bilateral ground-glass opacities in 88% of patients (n = 22), with peripheral distribution and subpleural sparing observed in 72% (n = 18). Additional findings included interlobular septal thickening (44%, n = 11), consolidation (28%, n = 7), and mild pleural effusion in 12% (n = 3). No pulmonary emboli were identified. In one patient, a CT pulmonary angiogram was performed due to suspected embolism and was negative.

Oxygen support was required in 100% of cases. Fifteen patients (60%) were managed with nasal cannula (median FiO₂ 30%), 8 patients (32%) required non-invasive ventilation (NIV), and 2 patients (8%) progressed to invasive mechanical ventilation. High-flow nasal cannula (HFNC) was used in 4 cases (16%) as an intermediate step. The median oxygenation index (PaO₂/FiO₂) among patients requiring ventilatory support was 190 (IQR: 140–230). The frequency of presenting symptoms and respiratory support modalities is shown in Fig. 1.

Bronchoscopy with bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) was performed in 12 patients (48%), yielding negative results for bacterial, fungal, and mycobacterial cultures. Cytology of BAL fluid demonstrated lipid-laden macrophages in 5 patients (42% of those sampled). Seven patients (28%) underwent lung biopsy: four via transbronchial biopsy and three via surgical wedge resection. Histopathological analysis revealed organizing pneumonia (n = 4), diffuse alveolar damage (n = 2), and eosinophilic pneumonitis (n = 1).

All patients received systemic corticosteroids, initiated with intravenous methylprednisolone (median initial dose: 1 mg/kg/day), followed by tapering oral prednisone. Duration of steroid therapy ranged from 7 to 21 days. Empiric antibiotics were initiated in 68% (n = 17), but discontinued in 12 patients after exclusion of infection. No patients required extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO).

Nineteen patients (76%) were admitted to the ICU, with a median ICU stay of 4 days (IQR: 2–6). The total median length of hospital stay was 8 days (IQR: 6–11). All patients survived to hospital discharge. On follow-up imaging at 1–3 months, radiologic improvement was observed in 98% of patients (n = 24), including complete resolution in 80% (n = 20), and persistent mild ground-glass opacities without functional impairment in 20% (n = 5). One patient was lost to imaging follow-up.

Discussion

This case series provides one of the first comprehensive clinical characterizations of e-cigarette or vaping product use-associated lung injury (EVALI) in Colombia, contributing to the limited but growing body of evidence on this emerging public health threat in Latin America. Our findings highlight the need for increased awareness among clinicians, policymakers, and the general public regarding the potential harms associated with vaping, especially in low- and middle-income countries where regulatory frameworks and surveillance systems are still evolving.

The demographic and clinical profile of our patients mirrors previously reported cases from North America and Europe: predominantly young adults, mostly male, presenting with non-specific respiratory and gastrointestinal symptoms, and characteristic bilateral ground-glass opacities on imaging [1, 2]. Importantly, nearly one-third of the patients in our cohort acquired vaping cartridges from informal street vendors, which has been identified in prior studies as a significant risk factor for toxic exposure, including contaminants like vitamin E acetate and synthetic cannabinoids [3–5]. Although only 16% of patients reported using THC-containing products, underreporting is likely, especially in a context where legal and social stigma may discourage disclosure. Urine toxicology screening was not routinely performed, which limits our ability to objectively confirm THC exposure.

The high rate of ICU admissions (76%) and the need for non-invasive or invasive mechanical ventilation in 40% of our cohort underscores the potential severity of EVALI. These findings are consistent with those reported during the 2019 outbreak in the United States, where delayed recognition of the condition contributed to worse outcomes [6]. In this series, clinical improvement was observed in all patients, the majority of whom received systemic corticosteroids. While this finding aligns with prior reports suggesting potential benefit [7–9], the role of corticosteroids in recovery cannot be definitively established from our retrospective data.

From a radiological perspective, the predominance of bilateral ground-glass opacities and subpleural sparing observed in our patients is consistent with previously described imaging patterns of EVALI [10, 11]. Histopathological confirmation of organizing pneumonia or acute interstitial inflammation in biopsy specimens further supports the diagnosis, as seen in prior studies [12–14].

While no fatalities occurred in our cohort, the need for hospitalization and intensive care indicates a substantial burden on healthcare systems. Moreover, the young age of affected individuals raises concerns about long-term pulmonary sequelae and underscores the importance of preventive strategies.

Given the increasing popularity of e-cigarettes among adolescents and young adults in Colombia—especially as documented by national surveys reporting rising lifetime use and decreasing age of initiation—public health interventions are urgently needed [15]. These should include regulation of product content and labeling, restriction of informal sales, educational campaigns, and integration of vaping-related respiratory illness into national surveillance systems.

This study is limited by its retrospective nature and single-center design, which may limit generalizability. Additionally, chemical analysis of the vaping products used was not available, preventing confirmation of specific causative agents. Pulmonary function tests were not consistently performed during follow-up, limiting our ability to assess long-term functional impairment. Nonetheless, the consistency of clinical, radiologic, and histopathologic findings supports the diagnosis of EVALI across cases.

Conclusion

This retrospective case series highlights the clinical, radiological, and histopathological features of EVALI in a low- and middle-income country context. Despite increasing global awareness, our findings emphasize that EVALI remains underdiagnosed outside North America, particularly in regions where e-cigarette use is rising and regulatory oversight is limited.

The majority of patients in our series were young adults without comorbidities, many of whom required ICU-level care. Imaging patterns were consistent with previous descriptions, and the identification of organizing pneumonia in biopsy samples supports proposed immunologic and toxicologic injury mechanisms. Notably, the use of informally purchased cartridges raises concerns about unregulated contents and reinforces the importance of public education and policy enforcement.

All patients experienced clinical improvement, most of whom received corticosteroids, suggesting a potential therapeutic benefit; nonetheless, a causal relationship cannot be established. However, the need for mechanical ventilation in a subset of cases also illustrates the potentially severe course of EVALI when diagnosis is delayed or clinical suspicion is low.

These findings advocate for greater clinical vigilance, broader access to diagnostic tools, and the development of national reporting systems for EVALI in Latin America. Furthermore, targeted regulatory actions and educational campaigns are urgently needed to mitigate the health risks associated with vaping products among youth and vulnerable populations.

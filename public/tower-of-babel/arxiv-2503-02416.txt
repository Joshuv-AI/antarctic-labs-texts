Non-split superstrings of dimension ( 1 | 2 ) (1|2)
Dimitry Leites a,∗ , Alexander S. Tikhomirov b

Abstract
. Any supermanifold diffeomorphic to one whose structure sheaf is the sheaf of sections of a vector bundle over the underlying manifold is called split. Gawȩdzki (1977) and Batchelor (1979) were the first to prove that any smooth supermanifold is split. In 1981, P. Green, and Palamodov, found examples of non-split analytic supermanifolds and described obstructions to splitness that were further studied by Manin (resp. Onishchik with his students) following Palamodov’s (resp. Green’s) approach. Following Palamodov, Donagi and Witten demonstrated that some of the moduli supervarieties of superstring theories are non-split. None of the above-mentioned authors considered odd parameters of supervarieties of obstructions to non-splitness. Here, using Palamodov’s approach, we classify and describe the even (degree-2) and the odd (degree-1) obstructions to splitness of ( 1 | 2 ) (1|2) -dimensional superstrings. In particular, we correct calculations of degree-2 obstructions due to Bunegina and Onishchik and confirm Manin’s answer.

Introduction

Let
M
be a real manifold,
\textbf{E}\longrightarrow M
a vector bundle with fiber
V_{x}
over
x\in M
. Let
{\mathcal{L}}_{E(\textbf{E})}
be the sheaf of sections of the exterior algebra of the bundle
E
whose fiber at
x\in M
is the exterior algebra
E(V_{x})
of
V_{x}
. Any supermanifold diffeomorphic to one of the form
{\mathcal{M}}=(M,{\mathcal{L}}_{E(\textbf{E})})
is called
split
. In the category of smooth supermanifolds, every object is split; for a transparent proof of this fact, see
[
MaG
, Subsection 4.1.3]
, which is more instructive than the first publications
[
Ga
,
Bat
]
.

To any supermanifold

{\mathcal{M}}=(M,{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{M}})
, where
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{M}}
is a filtered sheaf of supercommutative superalgebras, there corresponds a supermanifold with the graded sheaf constructed as follows. Consider the filtration

(1)

{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{M}}:={\mathcal{J}}^{0}\supset{\mathcal{J}}^{1}\supset{\mathcal{J}}^{2}\supset\cdots

of
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{M}}
by the powers of the subsheaf of ideals
{\mathcal{J}}:={\mathcal{J}}^{1}
generated by the odd elements that span the fiber of a bundle
E
. The associated graded sheaf

\mathop{\mathrm{gr}}\nolimits{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{M}}=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\mathop{\mathrm{gr}}\nolimits^{k}{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{M}},

where
\mathop{\mathrm{gr}}\nolimits^{k}{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{M}}={\mathcal{J}}^{k}/{\mathcal{J}}^{k+1}
,
gives rise to the supermanifold
(M,\mathop{\mathrm{gr}}\nolimits{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{M}})
, which is split (or is a singular version of a split supervariety, if the stalk of
\mathop{\mathrm{gr}}\nolimits{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{M}}
is not a free Grassmann algebra, but a quotient thereof). The supermanifold
(M,\mathop{\mathrm{gr}}\nolimits{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{M}})
is
called the
retract
of
{\mathcal{M}}
.

In
[
Gr
]
, Green showed that in the category of complex-analytic supermanifolds there exist non-split supermanifolds and gave the first such example.

[

]
. Palamodov’s description of obstructions to splitness looks totally different from Green’s whom Palamodov does not cite in
[
Pa
]
submitted on November 23, 1981, obviously being unaware of
[
Gr
]
submitted on September 24, 1981.

Later on, Manin described non-split supermanifolds with the underlying manifold
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
, in particular, he classified degree-2 obstructions to splitness of supermanifolds whose retract is the split superstring
{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}^{1|2}
, a projective supermanifold. Manin’s way of describing non-split supermanifolds is the same as Palamodov’s, see
[

]

[

, Ch. 4]
,
[

; however, Manin did not mention either Palamodov or Green whose way of describing non-splitness is unclear to us, unlike Palamodov’s.

Manin’s classification of supermanifolds whose retract is the projective superspace
{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{P}}^{1|2}
, a.k.a. superstring, differs from that given by Bunegina and Onishchik, who followed the method outlined by Green. Since the answers are different, at least one of them is wrong; our description of degree-2 invariants coincides with Manin’s. We cannot pinpoint the exact erroneous spot in

None of the above-mentioned authors, and none of the authors of the collection
[
LB
]
and references therein, considered odd parameters of supervarieties of obstructions to splitness.
To consider such obstructions, recall, see
[
L
]
, that functorially, for any (say, finitely generated, over the same ground field) supercommutative superalgebra
C
, the changes of coordinates in a chart of a superdomain
{\mathcal{U}}
with even coordinates
u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{m})
and odd coordinates
\xi=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})
are given by the parity-preserving
C
-linear automorphisms of the form

(2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\varphi^{*}(u_{i})&=\;\varphi_{i}(u,\xi):=\varphi^{0}_{i}(u)+\framebox{$\sum\limits_{r\geq 1}\;\sum\limits_{i_{1}<\dots<i_{r}}\varphi_{i}^{i_{1}\dots i_{r}}(u)\xi_{i_{1}}\cdots\xi_{i_{r}}$}\text{~~for all $i$},\\
\varphi^{*}(\xi_{j})&=\;\psi_{j}(u,\xi):=\sum\limits_{r\geq 0}\;\sum\limits_{j_{1}<\dots<j_{2r+1}}\psi_{j}^{j_{1}\dots j_{2r+1}}(u)\xi_{j_{1}}\cdots\xi_{j_{2r+1}}\\
&+\framebox{$\psi^{0}_{j}(u)+\sum\limits_{r\geq 1}\;\sum\limits_{j_{1}<\dots<j_{2r}}\psi_{j}^{j_{1}\dots j_{2r}}(u)\xi_{j_{1}}\dots\xi_{j_{2r}}$}\text{~~for all $j$},\\
\end{array}\right.

where, for all
r
, the even and the odd parameters are as follows

\begin{array}[]{l}\varphi^{0}_{i}(u),\ \ \varphi_{i}^{i_{1}\dots i_{2r}}(u),\ \ \psi_{j}^{j_{1}\dots j_{2r+1}}(u)\in C_{\bar{0}}\ \ (\text{the even parameters}),\\
\psi^{0}_{j}(u),\ \ \psi_{j}^{j_{1}\dots j_{2r}}(u),\ \ \varphi_{i}^{i_{1}\dots i_{2r+1}}(u)\in C_{\bar{1}}\ \ (\text{the odd parameters}).\end{array}

These are parameters
of the infinite-dimensional supergroup of automorphisms of
C^{\infty}({\mathcal{U}})
or, equivalently, of diffeomorphisms of
{\mathcal{U}}
; infinitesimally: of the Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{vect}}(m|n)
.

Note that the boxed summands in
\varphi^{*}(u_{i})
are precisely what the prefix “super” brings to the non-super (“classical”) Differential Geometry: these summands, meaningless in the non-super setting even for the even parameters
\varphi_{i}^{i_{1}\dots i_{2r}}(u)
, acquire meaning, as explained above, even for the odd parameters
\varphi_{i}^{i_{1}\dots i_{2r+1}}(u)
. The split supermanifolds are those for which there exists an atlas without these boxed summands in
\varphi^{*}(u_{i})
.

For every non-zero

Lemma
.

The set

is a homogeneous space under the action of
\mathop{\mathrm{{G\hskip-1.29167ptL}}}\nolimits(V)
.

Proof

of the lemma. Since
\dim(W\setminus K)=\dim\mathop{\mathrm{{G\hskip-1.29167ptL}}}\nolimits(V)=4
, to describe the stationary subgroup of a point it suffices to prove that the stabilizer of a vector
w\in W\setminus K
is

(15)

\text{St}_{\mathop{\mathrm{{G\hskip-0.90417ptL}}}\nolimits(V)}w=\{\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits\}~~\text{for any $w\in W\setminus K$}.

Having selected a basis of
V
we get an isomorphism

\mathop{\mathrm{{G\hskip-1.29167ptL}}}\nolimits(V)\longrightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{{G\hskip-1.29167ptL}}}\nolimits(2),\ \ g\mapsto A_{g}.

Accordingly, fixing the basis
\{e_{ij}:=v_{i}\otimes u_{j}\mid 1\leq i,j\leq 2\}
of

\mathop{\mathrm{{G\hskip-1.29167ptL}}}\nolimits(V)\longrightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{{G\hskip-1.29167ptL}}}\nolimits(4)\simeq\mathop{\mathrm{{G\hskip-1.29167ptL}}}\nolimits(W),\ \ g\mapsto\widetilde{A}_{g}:=\begin{pmatrix}A_{g}&0\\
0&A_{g}\end{pmatrix}.

Let now
w:=(w_{11},\dots,w_{22})^{t}
and
g\in\text{Stab}_{\mathop{\mathrm{{G\hskip-0.90417ptL}}}\nolimits(V)}w
. Then, by (
16

\widetilde{A}_{g}w=w\Longleftrightarrow A_{g}(w_{11},w_{12})^{t}=(w_{11},w_{12})^{t}\ \ \text{and}\ \ A_{g}(w_{21},w_{22})^{t}=(w_{21},w_{22})^{t},

hence

w_{1}:=(w_{11},w_{12})^{t}\ \ \text{and}\ \ w_{2}:=(w_{21},w_{22})^{t}\ \ \text{belong to $\mathop{\mathrm{Ker}}\nolimits(A_{g}-1_{2})$,}

where

w_{1}
and

contrary to our assumption that

Most topological orders forbid sign-problem-free quantum Monte Carlo: Nonpositive Gauss sum as an indicator
Donghae Seo, Minyoung You, Hee-Cheol Kim, Gil Young Cho

Abstract
Quantum Monte Carlo is a powerful tool for studying quantum many-body physics, yet its efficacy is often curtailed by the notorious sign problem. In this Letter, we introduce a novel criterion for the “intrinsic” sign problem in two-dimensional bosonic topological orders, which cannot be resolved by local basis transformations or adiabatic deformations of the Hamiltonian. Specifically, we show that a nonpositive higher Gauss sum for a given topological order indicates the presence of an intrinsic sign problem. This condition not only aligns with prior findings but significantly broadens their scope. Using this new criterion, we examine the Gauss sums of all 405 bosonic topological orders classified up to rank 12, and strikingly find that 398 of them exhibit intrinsic sign problems. We also uncover intriguing links between the intrinsic sign problem, gappability of boundary theories, and time-reversal symmetry, suggesting that sign-problem-free quantum Monte Carlo may fundamentally rely on both time-reversal symmetry and gapped boundaries. These results highlight the deep connection between the intrinsic sign problem and fundamental properties of topological phases, offering valuable insights into their classical simulability.



Studying quantum many-body systems poses a significant challenge due to the exponential growth of their Hilbert space dimension, making only a few special models tractable. Quantum Monte Carlo (QMC) methods
Sandvik and Kurkijärvi 1991
, which employ random sampling, offer a scalable way to analyze such systems, frequently providing more accurate estimates of physical observables than density functional theory or mean-field theory
Foulkes et al. 2001
;
Jones 2015
;
Altland and Simons 2010
. However, these algorithms become much less efficient when the sign problem arises
Pan and Meng 2024
, namely when the partition function cannot be expressed solely as a sum of classical probability weights. Although the sign problem can occasionally be remedied by a suitable basis transformation, finding a universal approach is widely believed to be computationally intractable
Marvian et al. 2019
. This naturally leads to the question of whether certain systems possess a sign problem so robust that it cannot be alleviated by any finite-depth local unitary circuit—an ‘intrinsic sign problem’
Hastings 2015
. Intriguingly, several topological orders are argued to exhibit this irreducible obstacle
Ringel and Kovrizhin 2017
;
Smith et al. 2020
;
Golan et al. 2020
;
Kim et al. 2022
.

In this Letter, we introduce a rigorous and efficient criterion for detecting the intrinsic sign problem in two-dimensional bosonic topological orders. This criterion is formulated in terms of the higher Gauss sum defined for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\displaystyle\tau_{n}=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}d_{a}^{2}\theta_{a}^{n},

(1)

which has been studied intensively both in pure mathematics and physics literature
Ng et al. 2019
;
Kaidi et al. 2022
;
Ng et al. 2022
;
Geiko and Moore 2023
;
Kobayashi et al. 2024
;
You 2024
.
Here,
\mathcal{A}
is the finite set of anyon labels in a topological order, and
d_{a}
and
\theta_{a}
are the quantum dimension and the topological spin of the corresponding anyon
a\in\mathcal{A}
. In general,
\tau_{n}
takes its values in the complex numbers, more specifically in the cyclotomic field
Ng and Schauenburg 2010
, and is not necessarily positive. In this manuscript, we will demonstrate that if
\tau_{n}
is nonpositive for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, the corresponding topological order possesses the intrinsic sign problem and thus cannot be simulated efficiently via QMC algorithms, irrespective of detailed forms of microscopic Hamiltonians. Our results not only align with previous findings
Hastings 2015
;
Ringel and Kovrizhin 2017
;
Smith et al. 2020
;
Golan et al. 2020
;
Kim et al. 2022
but also significantly extend their scope. To highlight the power of this condition, we analyze the Gauss sums of 405 different topological orders classified up to rank 12
Ng et al. 2023
, which also include the complete classification up to rank 11, and find that 398 suffer from the intrinsic sign problem. Additionally, we uncover intriguing connections between the intrinsic sign problem, gappability of the boundary theory, and time-reversal symmetry.

QMC methods and their associated sign problem have a venerable history in the study of quantum many-body systems, attracting sustained interest in the literature
Foulkes et al. 2001
;
Carlson et al. 2015
. Other popular approaches, such as exact diagonalization and tensor network methods, face inherent limitations when dealing with large or highly entangled systems: the former is constrained by the exponential growth of the Hilbert space, while the latter becomes less effective for states with substantial entanglement entropy and is particularly challenging to implement in two-dimensional settings
Stoudenmire and White 2012
. Although QMC often provides a promising alternative, determining whether a given many-body problem can be efficiently simulated—i.e., is free of the sign problem

—remains a nontrivial challenge. Moreover, the precise physical criteria dictating the onset or absence of the sign problem are not well understood, underscoring its significance not only in condensed matter physics but also in the broader domain of quantum complexity theory
Troyer and Wiese 2005
;
Bravyi 2015
;
Marvian et al. 2019
;
Ellison et al. 2021
.

Two-dimensional bosonic topological order is an ideal system for advancing our understanding of the sign problem. First, fermionic systems are already recognized as a known challenge for QMC
Loh et al. 1990
;
Troyer and Wiese 2005
. Second, the physics of two-dimensional topological orders is relatively well understood, with known classifying data represented by a small set of discrete numbers such as modular data, fusion coefficients, and central charge
Kitaev 2006
;
Wen 2015
. This makes it easier to pinpoint the sources of the sign problem compared to more general quantum many-body systems. Additionally, these states are not only of mathematical interest but are also actively studied in quantum simulations and condensed matter physics
Georgescu et al. 2014
;
Wen 2017
. Indeed, there has been substantial research effort in identifying the origins of the unavoidable sign problem, known as the intrinsic sign problem, in bosonic topological orders
Hastings 2015
;
Ringel and Kovrizhin 2017
;
Smith et al. 2020
;
Golan et al. 2020
;
Kim et al. 2022
. Building upon these results, we present a new source of the intrinsic sign problem—namely, a nonpositive Gauss sum Eq. (
1
)—in bosonic topological orders.

1. Some backgrounds.
To begin, we briefly introduce several key concepts such as QMC, the sign problem, and stoquastic Hamiltonians
Sandvik and Kurkijärvi 1991
;
Pan and Meng 2024
;
Bravyi et al. 2008
, which we will use extensively in this Letter.

QMC is a family of algorithms designed to simulate quantum many-body systems by sampling the configuration space of a given model
Sandvik and Kurkijärvi 1991
. Efficient implementation of a QMC algorithm requires decomposing the quantum partition function into a sum of terms interpretable as as probability weights, which must be nonnegative real numbers. If such a decomposition is not known, the system is said to suffer from the sign problem; otherwise, it is said to be sign-problem-free, or sign-free in short
Pan and Meng 2024
. Crucially, a system is sign-free if the Hamiltonian is stoquastic in some basis
Bravyi 2015
. A Hamiltonian
H
is defined as stoquastic with respect to a basis
\mathcal{B}
if and only if the matrix representation of
H
in
\mathcal{B}
has off-diagonal elements that are all nonpositive real numbers
Bravyi et al. 2008
.

Being stoquastic is a basis-dependent property. In some cases, nonstoquastic Hamiltonians can be transformed into stoquastic ones through an appropriate basis transformation, meaning the sign problem in such systems can be effectively ‘cured’
Marvian et al. 2019
. Consequently, being nonstoquastic in a given basis does not necessarily indicate the presence of sign problem. However, certain nonstoquastic Hamiltonians cannot be transformed into stoquastic ones using any finite-depth local unitary quantum circuit
Hastings 2015
. Such systems are said to exhibit an intrinsic sign problem, which is the central focus of our work.

2. Derivation.
We will demonstrate that a nonpositive Gauss sum Eq. (
1
) cannot be compatible with stoquastic Hamiltonians and therefore results in an intrinsic sign problem. Our derivation closely follows and generalizes the approach of Ref.
Golan et al. 2020
. We consider a quantum lattice system defined on a cylinder, which is much longer than the correlation length. At each lattice site, a qudit degree of freedom is located, as dipicted in Fig.
1
. The Hilbert space is then given by a tensor product
\mathcal{H}=\bigotimes_{i\in\Lambda}\mathcal{H}_{i}
where
\Lambda
is the set of lattice sites and
\mathcal{H}_{i}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{d}
for all
i\in\Lambda
with a fixed positive integer
d
. We assume that the Hamiltonian
H
, whose ground state exhibits the target topological order, is stoquastic with respect to the onsite basis
\mathcal{B}=\left\{|\bm{k}\rangle=\bigotimes_{i\in\Lambda}|k_{i}\rangle|k_{i}=0,\cdots,d-1\right\}
. Without loss of generality, we also assume that the diagonal elements of
H
in the basis are all nonpositive. We denote the ground state by
|G_{a}\rangle
where
a
labels the superselection sector of the topological order (anyon label).

Next, we perform a partial rotation
T_{R}(\varphi)
by the angle
\varphi=2\pi/n
for a positive integer
n
on the right half of the system as illustrated in the right panel of Fig.
1
and compute the resulting quantum amplitude
\langle G_{a}|T_{R}(2\pi/n)|G_{a}\rangle
in two distinct ways: one using the property of stoquastic Hamiltonians and the other through topological quantum field theory (TQFT)
Kobayashi et al. 2024
. By comparing these two results, we derive a condition for the topological order to be sign-free.

i. TQFT.
It was shown in Ref.
Kobayashi et al. 2024
that
\langle G_{a}|T_{R}(2\pi/n)|G_{a}\rangle
is given by the (twisted)
n
-th higher Gauss sum
\tau_{a,n}=\sum_{b\in\mathcal{A}}S_{ab}d_{b}\theta_{b}^{n}
, where
S_{ab}
is the modular
S
-matrix of the topological order
Wen 2015
. More precisely, the expectation value is given by

\mathcal{T}_{a,n}\equiv\langle G_{a}|T_{R}\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)|G_{a}\rangle\propto e^{\frac{2\pi i}{n}h_{a}-2\pi i(\frac{2}{n}+n)\frac{c_{-}}{24}}\tau_{a,n}

where
\propto
means being proportional up to a positive real number,
h_{a}
is the conformal weight of a state in the conformal family associated to the superselection sector
a\in\mathcal{A}
, and
c_{-}
is the chiral central charge of the topological order. This formula has been used to numerically extract the higher central charges of bosonic topological orders, e.g., the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
toric code order
Kobayashi et al. 2024
.

ii. Stoquastic Hamiltonian.
We show that
\mathcal{T}_{a,n}
is positive if
H
is stoquastic in the onsite basis. The fact that a Hamiltonian is stoquastic also determines some structure of its ground state wavefunciton. Precisely speaking, the Frobenius-Perron theorem for nonnegative matrices implies that the ground state of a stoquastic Hamiltonian
H
has only nonnegative real amplitudes in the basis in which the Hamiltonian is stoquastic. Hence, all the coefficients
\langle\bm{k}|G_{a}\rangle
in the onsite basis
|\bm{k}\rangle\in\mathcal{B}
are real and nonnegative. Furthermore,
T_{R}
in the onsite basis is represented as a permutation matrix, which has only nonnegative elements. By combining the above two, we can now evaluate
\mathcal{T}_{a,n}
as

\mathcal{T}_{a,n}=\sum_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}\langle G_{a}|\bm{k}\rangle\langle\bm{k}|T_{R}\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)|\bm{k}^{\prime}\rangle\langle\bm{k}^{\prime}|G_{a}\rangle

(2)

where
\bm{k}
and
\bm{k}^{\prime}
label the onsite basis states in
\mathcal{B}
. Since every summand on the right-hand side of Eq. (
2
) is given by a product of nonnegative real numbers,
\mathcal{T}_{a,n}
is real and nonnegative. In fact, we can further show that
\mathcal{T}_{a,n}
is nonzero
sup
, leading to the conclusion
\mathcal{T}_{a,n}>0
.

Comparing the two results, we find

e^{\frac{2\pi i}{n}h_{a}-2\pi i(\frac{2}{n}+n)\frac{c_{-}}{24}}\tau_{a,n}>0

(3)

for some superselection sector
a\in\mathcal{A}
and for all
n
. This results in several nontrivial consequences on each of the topological data appaering in the above equation.

First, the anyon label
a
should be the trivial superselection sector
\mathds{1}
. To see this, we note that
\tau_{a,n}
vanishes for
n=N_{FS}
unless
a=\mathds{1}
, where
N_{FS}
is the Frobenius-Schur exponent
Ng and Schauenburg 2010
, since
\tau_{a,N_{FS}}=D\sum_{b\in\mathcal{A}}S_{ab}S_{b\mathds{1}}^{*}=D\delta_{a,\mathds{1}}
. Thus, if a topological order is realized through a stoquastic local Hamiltonian on a cylinder, the ground state is in the trivial sector. Further, we assume
c_{-}=0
since the nonzero chiral central charge is a known source of the intrinsic sign problem
Ringel and Kovrizhin 2017
;
Kim et al. 2022
. With these, the sign-free condition Eq. (
3
) is reduced to

e^{\frac{2\pi i}{n}h_{\mathds{1}}}\tau_{n}>0.

(4)

From this, we conclude
h_{\mathds{1}}=0
and
\tau_{n}>0
. This is because
\tau_{n}
lies in the cyclotomic field
\mathbb{Q}(e^{2\pi i/N_{FS}})

Ng and Schauenburg 2010
. Meanwhile, the phase factor
e^{\frac{2\pi i}{n}h_{\mathds{1}}}
would not be in the same field for a sufficiently large
n
, unless
h_{\mathds{1}}=0

sup
. Hence, Eq. (
4
) requires
h_{\mathds{1}}=0
. If we plug this back to Eq. (
4
), we finally find
\tau_{n}>0
to be sign-free.

A few clarifying notes are in order. First, although
\tau_{n}
is defined for any integer
n
, it exhibits periodicity with respect to the Frobenius-Schur exponent
N_{FS}
, satisfying
\tau_{n}=\tau_{n+N_{FS}}
. Consequently, for a given topological order, determining the presence of the intrinsic sign problem requires computing only a finite number of
\tau_{n}
values. Second, while our derivation assumes that the stoquastic Hamiltonian produces a ground state within a single superselection sector, the results can be readily generalized to a superposition of multiple superselection sectors. For details, please refer to Supplementary Information
sup
. Third, while we have focused on the case where the basis
\mathcal{B}
corresponds to the tensor product of the Hilbert space at each microscopic lattice site, it is straightforward to generalize this to include a local but non-onsite basis by clustering multiple sites into a superlattice site. Similarly, if the dimensions of Hilbert spaces per each lattice site differ between sites, one can apply a generalized local unitary transformation
Chen et al. 2010
, which preserves the topological order, to ensure that all lattice sites share the same local Hilbert space dimension. Since the sign-free condition depends solely on the topological data, such transformations do not affect the conclusion.

3. Consistency with previous results.
Our sign-free condition,
\tau_{n}>0
, demonstrates nontrivial consistency with the previous results on the intrinsic sign problem
Hastings 2015
;
Ringel and Kovrizhin 2017
;
Smith et al. 2020
;
Golan et al. 2020
;
Kim et al. 2022
, providing strong support for the validity of our findings.

First, our result correctly identifies the intrinsic sign problem of the double semion topological order
Hastings 2015
as the topological order has a nonpositive higher Gauss sum
\tau_{2}=0
. Second, our derivation of the sign-free condition
\tau_{n}>0
relies on the results of
Ringel and Kovrizhin 2017
;
Kim et al. 2022
which showed that a nonvanshining chiral central charge signals the intrinsic sign problem. Our sign-free condition is self-consistent with this. The first Gauss sum
\tau_{1}
determines the chiral central charge
c_{-}
modulo
8
through the Gauss-Milgram formula
Wang 2010

\frac{\tau_{1}}{|\tau_{1}|}=e^{2\pi ic_{-}/8}.

Thus, the positivity of
\tau_{1}
implies
c_{-}=0
mod
8
, which includes
c_{-}=0
. Notably, even without assuming
a priori
that
c_{-}=0
, our derivation allows us to prove that every sign-free topological order must satisfy
c_{-}=0
mod
12

sup
, which includes
c_{-}=0
. This further reinforces the validity of our approach. Furthermore, our classification of the intrinsic sign problem of topological orders
sup
is consistent with that of Ref.
Golan et al. 2020
, by identifying the intrinsic sign problem of bosonic Laughlin states, Kitaev’s spin liquid, and the Fibonacci anyon model.

Lastly, the necessary condition for a bosonic Abelian topological order to be sign-free
Smith et al. 2020
, which states that the topological spins must form a disjoint union of complete sets of roots of unity, is consistent with our result as well. This can be seen as follows. Note that the
n
-th higher Gauss sum of an Abelian topological order is given by
\tau_{n}=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\theta_{a}^{n}
because
d_{a}=1
for all
a\in\mathcal{A}
in an Abelian topological order. For each complete set of roots of unity, the sum of
n
-th powers of its elements would either vanish or be a positive real number. Importantly, there is always a trivial complete set that contains a single element
\{\theta_{\mathds{1}}=1\}
as otherwise the first Gauss sum would vanish and the Gauss-Milgram formula would be ill-defined. Note that the sum of
n
-th powers of the elements of a complete set of roots of unity is either positive or equal to zero, while for the trivial set
\{\theta_{\mathds{1}}\}
, the sum is trivially real and positive. Therefore, the existence of the trivial complete set implies that
\tau_{n}>0
for all positive integers
n
.

4. Physical consequences of intrinsic sign problem.
The fact that our sign-free condition is expressed entirely in terms of topological data suggests that the intrinsic sign problem is indeed a fundamental property of topological phases of matter, being consistent with, for example, Ref.
Hastings 2015
. Recall that the intrinsic sign problem refers to the sign problem that cannot be cured by any local basis transformation or, equivalently, by any finite-depth local unitary circuit. This immediately implies that the intrinsic sign problem is preserved throughout the entire region of the corresponding topological phase in the phase diagram, as any two gapped Hamiltonians within the same phase are related by a finite-depth local quantum circuit. Consequently, it is natural to expect that the intrinsic sign problem in a topological phase should be formulated solely in terms of topological data, which remain invariant under finite-depth local unitary circuits. Our result aligns well with this expectation.

Notably, our sign-free condition has direct implications on the compatibility with gapped boundary conditions
Kaidi et al. 2022
and time-reversal symmetries
Geiko and Moore 2023
. First, any sign-free Abelian topological order must admit a gapped boundary. It was proven in Ref.
Kaidi et al. 2022
that any Abelian topological order whose higher Gauss sums satisfy
\tau_{n}>0
for
n
such that
\gcd(n,\frac{N_{FS}}{\gcd(n,N_{FS})})=1
admits a gapped boundary. Since our condition states that sign-free topological orders satisfy
\tau_{n}>0
for all
n
, our proposition follows directly. For non-Abelian topological orders, the precise relationship between higher Gauss sums and boundary gappability is only partially understood. Specifically, a non-Abelian topological order admits a gapped boundary only if
\tau_{n}>0
for
n
such that
\gcd(n,N_{FS})=1

Kaidi et al. 2022
. Nevertheless, our sign-free condition suggests that any sign-free non-Abelian topological order satisfies the necessary condition for admitting a gapped boundary. Second, any sign-free Abelian topological order admits a time-reversal symmetry. Reference
Geiko and Moore 2023
proves for Abelian topological orders and conjectures for non-Abelian topological orders that a topological order admits a time-reversal symmetry if and only if its higher Gauss sums are all real. Since our sign-free condition
\tau_{n}>0
directly implies the reality of
\tau_{n}
, any sign-free Abelian topological order must be compatible with the time-reversal symmetry. The same proposition for non-Abelian topological orders is subject to the conjecture in Ref.
Geiko and Moore 2023
.

5. Classification of topological orders with intrinsic sign problems.
Our sign-free condition is powerful enough to identify the intrinsic sign problems of almost all bosonic topological orders up to rank 12
Ng et al. 2023
, which includes the complete list of topological orders up to rank 11. There are in total 405 distinct bosonic topological orders up to rank 12. Among these, we find that the majority, 398 out of 405, fails to satisfy our sign-free condition, indicating that they exhibit the intrinsic sign problem. Of the remaining seven, only 3 topological orders, i.e., the toric code, the
S_{3}
gauge theory, and the
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
gauge theory, can be shown to be sign-free, as their quantum double realizations
Hu et al. 2013
yield stoquastic Hamiltonians. Notably, this aligns with the findings of Ref.
Isakov et al. 2011
, where the toric code topological order was successfully analyzed using QMC. For the other four topological orders—namely, the double Fibonacci, the double Ising, the twisted double Ising, and the Drinfeld center of
\tilde{R}_{\mathrm{PSU}(2)_{5}}
—we cannot diagnose their intrinsic sign problem, as their stoquastic realizations are unknown and they do not violate our sign-free condition simultaneously. The data of full classification of topological orders with the intrinsic sign problem is avaliable in Supplementary Information
sup
.

6. Conclusions.
We present a novel condition for identifying the intrinsic sign problems in bosonic topological orders, formulated in terms of the Gauss sums Eq. (
1
). Specifically, we show that a nonpositive higher Gauss sum for a given topological order indicates the presence of the intrinsic sign problem. This seemingly simple condition is not only consistent with previously established criteria
Hastings 2015
;
Ringel and Kovrizhin 2017
;
Smith et al. 2020
;
Golan et al. 2020
;
Kim et al. 2022
, but also extends their applicability. To demonstrate the power of this condition, we analyzed the classification data of bosonic topological orders up to rank 12
Ng et al. 2023
and found that an overwhelming majority—398 out of 405—exhibit the intrinsic sign problem. Moreover, we uncover intriguing connections between the intrinsic sign problem, gappability of boundary theories
Kaidi et al. 2022
, and time-reversal symmetry
Geiko and Moore 2023
: sign-free topological orders not only permit gapped boundaries but are also compatible with time-reversal symmetry (for non-Abelian orders, this statement is subject to the conjecture in Ref.
Geiko and Moore 2023
).

Our results open the door to several intriguing future research directions. First, further refinement of our condition for the intrinsic sign problem would be valuable. While we have shown that nearly all bosonic topological orders exhibit intrinsic sign problems, four cases remain undetermined. Developing more precise criteria to resolve these cases would be an interesting direction for future research.
Second, it is worth exploring whether certain topological orders face fundamental obstructions using other numerical methods, such as tensor network techniques
Dubail and Read 2015
. If such obstructions exist, identifying the specific topological data that give rise to these limitations would be a valuable contribution. Third, extending our investigation to higher-dimensional topological orders could provide new insights into whether intrinsic sign problems persist or manifest differently in higher dimensions.

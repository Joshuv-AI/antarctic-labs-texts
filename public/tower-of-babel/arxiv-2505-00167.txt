Uniqueness of asymptotically conical Kähler-Ricci flow
Longteng Chen

Abstract
. We study the uniqueness problem for the Kähler-Ricci flow with a conical initial condition. Given a complete gradient expanding Kähler-Ricci soliton on a non compact manifold with quadratic curvature decay, including its derivatives, we establish that any complete solution to the Kähler-Ricci flow emerging from the soliton’s tangent cone at infinity—appearing as a Kähler cone—must coincide with the forward self-similar Kähler-Ricci flow associated with the soliton, provided certain conditions hold. Specifically, if its Kähler form remains in the same cohomology class as that of the soliton’s self-similar Kähler-Ricci flow, its full Riemann curvature operator is bounded for each fixed positive time, its Ricci curvature is bounded from above by A t \frac{A}{t} , its scalar curvature is bounded from below by − A t -\frac{A}{t} , and it shares a same Killing vector field with the soliton metric. This paper gives a partial answer to a question in [ FIK03 ] , and generalizes the earlier work of [ CD20 ] and the work of [ CDS24 ] .



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview

A smooth family
g(t)_{t\in(0,T)}
of Riemannian metrics on
M
as a smooth manifold is a solution to Ricci flow if

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g(t)=-2\operatorname{Ric}(g(t)),\quad t\in(0,T).

(1.1)

The Ricci flow was introduced by R. Hamilton in
[
Ham82
]
and has since become a powerful tool in differential geometry. However, since the Ricci flow equation is a non-linear degenerated parabolic equation, the question of uniqueness has garnered significant interest among mathematicians.

For compact manifolds, R. Hamilton established the uniqueness property in
[
Ham82
]
. However, for non-compact manifolds, the situation is more complicated. Assuming that the initial metric is smooth and complete with uniformly bounded curvature, W.-X. Shi
[
Shi89
]
constructed a complete solution to the Ricci flow with bounded curvature that evolves from this initial metric. Moreover, B.-L. Chen and X.-P. Zhu
[
CZ06
]
proved that if
g_{1}(t)_{t\in[0,T]}
and
g_{2}(t)_{t\in[0,T]}
are two complete solutions to the Ricci flow with uniformly bounded curvature and satisfy
g_{1}(0)=g_{2}(0)
, then they must be identical, i.e.,
g_{1}(t)\equiv g_{2}(t)
. Reciprocally, B. Kotschwar
[
Kot10
]
showed the backward uniqueness property for the Ricci flow with bounded curvature, i.e. assume that
g_{1}(t)_{t\in[0,T]}
and
g_{2}(t)_{t\in[0,T]}
are two complete solutions to the Ricci flow with uniformly bounded curvature such that
g_{1}(T)=g_{2}(T)
, then
g_{1}(t)=g_{2}(t)
for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Instead of assuming bounded curvature along the Ricci flow, B. Kotschwar
[
Kot14
]
used en energy method to establish a uniqueness result under different curvature bounds and metric equivalence conditions along the flow, assuming that the smooth complete initial metric satisfies a volume growth condition. B. Kotschwar
[
Kot17
]
also proved that if two complete solutions of the Ricci flow share the same smooth initial metric and satisfy the condition that the full Riemann curvature operator tensor of one solution and the other’s Ricci curvature tensor are bounded by
\frac{C}{t^{\delta}}
for some
\delta\in[0,1)
, then the two solutions must be identical.

However, his method does not apply to the case where the curvature is bounded by
\frac{C}{t}
. This condition plays a crucial role in the theory of Ricci flow, as it is invariant under scaling. Specifically, if
g(t)_{t\in(0,T)}
is a solution to the Ricci flow and its Riemann curvature is bounded by
\frac{C}{t}
, then for any
\lambda>0
, the
dilated
Ricci flow defined by

g_{\lambda}(t):=\lambda g(\lambda^{-1}t),\quad t\in(0,\lambda T),

is also a solution to the Ricci flow. Moreover, its curvature remains bounded by
\frac{C}{t}
.

The most optimal result to date is probably the work of M.-C. Lee
[
Lee25
]
, who proved that two complete solutions to the Ricci flow with the same smooth initial metric and curvatures bounded by
\frac{C}{t}
must coincide.

Consider a smooth manifold
M
of dimension 3, which admits a smooth and complete Riemannian metric
g
with a bounded non negative curvature operator (
0\leq\operatorname{Rm}(g)\leq K_{0}
), B.-L. Chen
[
Che09
]
showed that two complete solutions of Ricci flow living on
[0,T]
with
g
as their common initial metric must coincide on
[0,\min\{T,\frac{1}{4K_{0}}\}]
. In particular, the only complete solution of the Ricci flow starting from the Euclidean metric as initial metric in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
must be constant in time.

On the non-uniqueness side, people seek counterexamples to the uniqueness of the Ricci flow. In their paper
[
AK22
]
, S. Angenent and D. Knopf demonstrated that uniqueness should not be expected to hold for weak solutions of the Ricci flow in dimension
n\geq 5
if the flow is allowed to continue past singularities.

Specifically, for any integers
p_{1},p_{2}\geq 2
satisfying
p_{1}+p_{2}\leq 8
, and for any
K\in\mathbb{N}
, they constructed a complete shrinking soliton metric
g_{K}
on
\mathbb{S}^{p_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{p_{2}+1}
. The associated backward Ricci flow
g_{K}(t)
starts at
t=-1
and converges to a conical metric on
\mathbb{S}^{p_{1}}\times\mathbb{S}^{p_{2}}\times(0,+\infty)
as
t\to 0^{-}
. Moreover, they showed that there exist at least
K
distinct, non-isometric forward desingularizations by Ricci flow expanding solitons on
\mathbb{S}^{p_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{p_{2}+1}
, as well as at least
K
distinct, non-isometric forward desingularizations by expanding solitons on
\mathbb{R}^{p_{1}+1}\times\mathbb{S}^{p_{2}}
. However, in the latter case, we need to note that the topology of the manifold changes.

It is important to note that a counterexample to the uniqueness of the Ricci flow in a smooth space-time setting remains an open question.

The Ricci flow also plays a significant role in Kähler geometry. Given a complex manifold
(M,J)
with complex structure
J
, a smooth family of Riemannian metrics
g(t)
for
t\in(0,T)
is said to be a solution to the Kähler-Ricci flow if and only if
g(t)
remains a Kähler metric with respect to
J
for all

, and it satisfies the evolution equation

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g(t)=-\operatorname{Ric}(g(t)),\quad\forall t\in(0,T).

(1.2)

We view equation (
1.2
) as a system of partial differential equations. From the dynamical systems perspective, it is important to study self-similar solutions to the Kähler–Ricci flow, as they correspond to fixed points in the moduli space of solutions. Every self-similar solution is given by a
Kähler-Ricci soliton
metric.

A

is a triple

is a complex manifold with complex structure

and a complete Kähler metric

and a complete real-holomorphic vector field

satisfying the equation

\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{X}g=\operatorname{Ric}(g)-\lambda g

(1.3)

for some

. If

for some real-valued smooth function

on

,
then we say that

is
gradient
. In this case, the soliton equation (

)
reduces to

\operatorname{Hess}_{g}(f)=\operatorname{Ric}(g)-\lambda g,

(1.4)

if

is the Kähler form of
g

\rho_{\omega}
is the Ricci form of
\omega

i\partial\bar{\partial}f=\rho_{\omega}-\lambda\omega.

(1.5)

For Kähler-Ricci solitons

is called the

soliton vector field
. Its completeness is guaranteed by the completeness of

[
Zha09
]

A Kähler-Ricci soliton are called

Every Kähler-Ricci soliton is related to a self-similar solution to Kähler-Ricci flow, for example, if
(M,g,X)
is a complete expanding Kähler-Ricci soliton, we use
(\Phi^{X}_{\tau},\tau\in\mathbb{R})
to denote the flow of the complete, real-holomorphic vector field

. We define the biholomorphism
\Phi_{t}:M\mapsto M
such that
\Phi_{t}=\Phi^{X}_{-\frac{1}{2}\log t}
for all

. Then
g(t):=t\Phi_{t}^{*}g
is a solution to Kähler-Ricci flow, which means

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g(t)=-\operatorname{Ric}(g(t)),\quad\forall t>0.

(1.6)

In their work
[
FIK03
]
, for any real number
p>0
, M. Feldman, T. Ilmanen, and D. Knopf constructed a complete expanding Kähler-Ricci soliton

on the total space of the line bundle
\mathcal{O}(-k)
over
\mathbb{C}P^{n-1}
for

. Furthermore, they observed that the associated self-similar forward Kähler-Ricci flow

converges, as

, to a conical metric away from the zero section. This limiting space is exactly the Kähler cone
(\mathbb{C}^{n}/\mathbb{Z}^{k},i\partial\bar{\partial}\left(\frac{|\cdot|^{2p}}{p}\right))
for given
p>0
.

In their paper, they have also asked the following question:

Question 1.1
.

[
FIK03
, Question 4]

Does the Ricci flow evolve uniquely after a singularity? If not, is there a selection principle for the best flow?

Later, R.J.Conlon and A.Deruelle’s paper
[
CD20
]
partially answered this question for a Kähler cone. They gave necessary and suﬃcient conditions for a Kähler equivariant resolution of a Kähler cone, with the resolution satisfying one of a number of auxiliary conditions, to admit a
unique
asymptotically conical (AC) expanding gradient Kähler-Ricci soliton.

In the paper
[
CDS24
]
, R.J.Conlon, A.Deruelle and S. Sun generalized the result of Conlon-Deruelle
[
CD20
]
. They showed that an expanding gradient Kähler-Ricci soliton with quadratic curvature decay (including all derivatives), which desingularizes a Kähler cone, exists and is unique if and only if the Kähler cone admits a smooth canonical model.

1.2.
Main result

We regard (
1.2
) as a PDE system with singular initial data, in this paper, we give a partial answer to Question

for a Kähler cone with a smooth canonical model. Our main theorem states as follows:

Theorem 1.2

(Uniqueness theorem)
.

Let
(C_{0},g_{0})
be a Kähler cone of complex dimension

which admits a smooth canonical model
M
. Let
\pi:M\mapsto C_{0}
be a smooth Kähler resolution with exceptional set

is
\pi-

Let
(M,g,X)
be the unique (up to pullback by biholomorphism) complete expanding gradient Kähler-Ricci soliton whose curvature

satisfies

A_{k}(g):=\sup_{x\in M}|(\nabla^{g})^{k}\operatorname{Rm}(g)|_{g}(x)d_{g}(p,\,x)^{2+k}<\infty\quad\textrm{for all $k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}$},

where
d_{g}(p,\,\cdot)
denotes the distance to a fixed point

with respect to
g
, with tangent cone at infinity
(C_{0},g_{0})
. Let

Assume that

(i)

(Conical condition)

\pi_{*}g_{\varphi}(t)
converges to

locally smoothly when

goes to 0.

(ii)

(Cohomology condition)
There exists a smooth real-valued function

(iii)

(Killing condition)
The Reeb vector field

(iv)

(Curvature condition)

.

1.3.
Strategy of proof

Recall that
g_{\varphi}(t)_{t\in(0,T)}
and
g(t)_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
are two solutions to the Kähler-Ricci flow starting with the same conical initial data. The strategy of this paper is to analyze the
normalized Kähler-Ricci flow
and investigate whether it is a static flow.

The normalized Kähler-Ricci flow
g_{\psi}(\tau)_{\tau\in(-\infty,\log T)}
is defined through the relation

e^{\tau}\Phi_{e^{\tau}}^{*}g_{\psi}(\tau)=g_{\varphi}(e^{\tau}).

The evolution equation for
g_{\psi}(\tau)
then becomes

\partial_{\tau}g_{\psi}(\tau)=\mathcal{L}_{\frac{X}{2}}g_{\psi}(\tau)-\mathrm{Ric}(g_{\psi}(\tau))-g_{\psi}(\tau).

This geometric flow equation can be further reduced to a
complex Monge-Ampère equation
for a scalar potential function
\psi(\tau)
, given by:

\partial_{\tau}\psi(\tau)=\log\left(\frac{w_{\psi}(\tau)^{n}}{w^{n}}\right)+\frac{X}{2}\cdot\psi(\tau)-\psi(\tau),

where
w_{\psi}(\tau)^{n}
(resp.
w^{n}
) denotes the volume form associated with the Kähler metric
g_{\psi}(\tau)
(resp
g
).

Taking the time derivative
\dot{\psi}:=\partial_{\tau}\psi
, we obtain the following linear parabolic evolution equation:

\left(\partial_{\tau}-\Delta_{w_{\psi}}-\frac{X}{2}\right)\dot{\psi}=-\dot{\psi}.

From this, if
f
denotes the normalized soliton potential with respect to Kähler-Ricci soliton
g
, we construct a crucial energy functional:

A(\tau):=\int_{M}\left(\dot{\psi}(\tau)\right)^{2k}e^{f+\frac{X}{2}\cdot\psi(\tau)}w_{\psi}(\tau)^{n},

for an integer
k
sufficiently large. The central result is to show that
A(\tau)\equiv 0
, from which we conclude that the normalized Kähler-Ricci flow is a static flow.

The main technical challenge lies in establishing the
well-definedness
and
boundedness
of the energy
A(\tau)
.

In A. Deruelle and F. Schulze’s paper
[
DS20
, Theorem 1.1]
, they proved that for two asymptotically conical soliton metrics
g_{1}
,
g_{2}
whose soliton vector fields coincide outside of a compact set, then
\lim_{r\to+\infty}r^{n}e^{\frac{r^{2}}{4}}(g_{1}-g_{2})
exists and is in
L^{2}_{loc}(C(S))
topology, where
C(S)
is the asymptotic Riemannian cone with radial function
r
. Thus, here we have the reason to believe that
|\partial\bar{\partial}\psi|_{g_{0}}=O(e^{-f}f^{-n})
. To ensure
A(\tau)
is well-defined, we analyze the asymptotic behavior of
\dot{\psi}
. The evolution equation implies that

\dot{\psi}=O\left(e^{-f}f^{-n-1}\right)

at spatial infinity, with the decay exponent
n+1
being nearly optimal. This decay ensures that, for sufficiently large
k
, the integrand in
A(\tau)
decays rapidly enough to guarantee convergence outside of a sub-level set of
f
. Thus, the energy
A(\tau)
is well-defined.

The remaining difficulty is to obtain uniform estimates for
\dot{\psi}
and the volume form
w_{\psi}(\tau)^{n}
on this sub-level set, where the decay at infinity no longer helps the analysis. To overcome this, we employ the maximum principle, using carefully chosen auxiliary functions and boundary data to control the behavior of
\dot{\psi}
and the weighted volume ratio
e^{\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\psi}\frac{w_{\psi}(\tau)^{n}}{w^{n}}
in this compact set, it turns out that the weighted volume ratio is a subsolution of the backward drift Laplacian
\Delta_{w_{\psi}}-\frac{X}{2}
. This allows us to extend the boundedness of the energy to the entire manifold, completing the proof that
A(\tau)\equiv 0
, and hence demonstrating the triviality of the normalized Kähler-Ricci flow.

There exists a constant
\varepsilon>0
such that
R_{w}\geq-n+\varepsilon
on
M
.

Proof.

We use
\Delta_{g}
to denote the real Laplacian-Beltrami operator. Then due to Bochner’s formula

\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{g}|\nabla f|_{g}^{2}=|\nabla^{g,2}f|^{2}_{g}+\operatorname{Ric}(g)(\nabla^{g}f,\nabla^{g}f)+g(\nabla^{g}\Delta_{g}f,\nabla^{g}f).

By Lemma
2.2

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{g}|\nabla f|_{g}^{2}=\Delta_{g}|\partial f|^{2}_{g}=\Delta_{g}(f-R_{w});\\
&\operatorname{Ric}(g)(\nabla^{g}f,\nabla^{g}f)=-X\cdot R_{w};\\
&g(\nabla^{g}\Delta_{g}f,\nabla^{g}f)=2X\cdot R_{w},\end{split}

and
\nabla^{g,2}f=g+\operatorname{Ric}(g)
, hence we have

|\operatorname{Ric}(g)+g|^{2}_{g}+X\cdot R_{w}=\Delta_{g}(f-R_{w})=n+R_{w}-\Delta_{g}R_{w}.

Thus we have established an elliptic equation for
R_{w}+n
:

\Delta_{g}(R_{w}+n)+X\cdot(R_{w}+n)=R_{w}+n-|\operatorname{Ric}(g)+g|^{2}_{g}.

Since
R_{w}
decays quadratically at infinity, the weak maximum principle implies that
R_{w}+n\geq 0
on
M
. Moreover, as
\Delta_{g}(R_{w}+n)+X\cdot(R_{w}+n)-R_{w}+n\leq 0
, Hopf’s maximum principle
[
Aub82
, Theorem 3.71]
ensures that if there exists
x_{0}\in M
such that
R_{w}(x_{0})+n=0
, it immediately yields
R_{w}\equiv-n
, and therefore
\operatorname{Ric}(g)+g\equiv 0
, this gives us a contradiction since
|\operatorname{Rm}(g)|_{g}
tends to
0
at spacial infinity by assumption. Hence
R_{w}+n>0
on
M
.

As
R_{w}
decays to 0 at spatial infinity, there exists a constant
\varepsilon>0
such that
R_{w}\geq-n+\varepsilon
.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

The normalized Hamiltonian potential
f
is a strictly subharmonic function on
M
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
and
2.3
,
\Delta_{w}f=R_{w}+n\geq\varepsilon>0
, which implies that
f
is a strictly subharmonic function on
M
as expected.
∎

3.
Ricci flow coming out of Riemannian cone

Let
(C(S),g_{C})
be a Riemannian cone over a closed smooth Riemannian manifold
(S,g_{S})
such that
C(S)=\mathbb{R}_{+}\times S,g_{C}=dr^{2}+r^{2}g_{S}
, where
r
is the radial function of cone
C(S)
.

Definition 3.1

(Ricci flow coming out of cone)
.

Assume that
M
is an
n-
dimensional smooth non compact manifold, and
(g(t))_{t\in(0,T)}
is a family of smooth complete Riemannian metrics on
M
, we say that
g(t)
is a Ricci flow coming out of the Riemannian cone
(C(S),g_{C})
if the following conditions hold:

(i)

The family
(g(t))_{t\in(0,T)}
satisfies the Ricci flow equation:
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g(t)=-2\operatorname{Ric}(g(t)),\quad t\in(0,T)
;

(ii)

there exists a diffeomorphism
\pi:M\backslash K\mapsto C(S)\backslash\{o\}
, where
K
is a compact subset of
M
,
\{o\}
is the vertex of
C(S)
;

(iii)

\pi_{*}g(t)
converges locally smoothly to
g_{C}
away form the vertex
o
when
t
converges to
0
;

(iv)

for each
t\in(0,T)
the Riemannian curvature of
g(t)
is bounded on
M
.

For convenience, we identify
M\backslash K
with
C(S)-\{o\}
via the diffeomorphism
\pi
.
For a Ricci flow coming out of the Riemannian cone, we have quadratic decays for the full Riemann curvature tensor and its derivatives.

Theorem 3.2
.

[
Sie13
, Theorem 3.2.3]

Let
(M^{n},g(t))_{t\in(0,T)}
be a complete Ricci flow coming out of Riemannian cone
(C(S),g_{C})
. Then
\operatorname{Rm}(g(t))
decays quadratically, i.e. there exist constants
\{C_{k}>0\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}},\lambda>0
independent of time such that

|(\nabla^{g(t)})^{k}\operatorname{Rm}(g(t))|_{g(t)}(x)\leq\frac{C_{k}}{r(x)^{2+k}},\quad\textrm{for all $k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}$},

(3.1)

for all
(x,t)\in M\times(0,T)
such that
r(x)^{2}>\lambda t
.

Proof.

We only provide a sketch of proof here, for detailed proof, see
[
Sie13
, Theorem 3.2.3]
.

First, since
(C(S),g_{C})
is smooth outside the vertex, there exists a uniform constant
\frac{1}{2}>\delta>0
such that

|\operatorname{Rm}(g_{C})|_{g_{C}}(x)\leq\frac{1}{(2\delta r(x_{0}))^{2}},\quad\forall x\in B_{g_{C}}(x_{0},2\delta r(x_{0})),

and

\operatorname{Vol}_{g_{C}}(B_{g_{C}}(x_{0},2\delta r(x_{0}))\geq(1-\varepsilon)w_{n}(2\delta r(x_{0}))^{n},\quad\forall x_{0}\in C(S)-\{o\},

where
w_{n}
denotes the volume of unit ball in
n
-dimensional Euclidean space and
\varepsilon
denotes the universal constant given by Perelman’s pseudolocality theorem (
[
Per02
, Theorem 10.1]
in the compact case,
[
CTY11
, Theorem 8.1]
in the non compact case).

Now pick any point
x_{0}\in C(S)-\{o\}
since
g(t)
converges to
g_{C}
locally smoothly, hence there exists a
0<s_{0}<T
(which potentially depends on the choice of
x_{0}
) such that

|\operatorname{Rm}(g(s))|_{g(s)}(x)\leq\frac{1}{(\delta r(x_{0}))^{2}},\quad\forall x\in B_{g(s)}(x_{0},\delta r(x_{0})),\quad s\leq s_{0}.

And

\operatorname{Vol}_{g(s)}\left(B_{g(s)}(x_{0},\delta r(x_{0}))\right)\geq(1-\varepsilon)w_{n}(\delta r(x_{0}))^{n}.

Then by applying Perelman’s pseudolocality theorem on
g(s_{0}+t)_{t\geq 0}
, it follows that

|\operatorname{Rm}(g(s_{0}+t))|_{g(s_{0}+t)}(x)\leq\frac{1}{(\delta\varepsilon r(x_{0})^{2})},

for all
x\in B_{g(s_{0}+t)}(x_{0},\delta\varepsilon r(x_{0}))
,
t\in[0,\min\{T-s_{0},(\delta\varepsilon r(x_{0})^{2})\}]
, in particular

|\operatorname{Rm}(g(s_{0}+t))|_{g(s_{0}+t)}(x_{0})\leq\frac{1}{(\delta\varepsilon r(x_{0})^{2})},

for
t\in[0,\min\{T-s_{0},(\delta\varepsilon r(x_{0})^{2})\}]
. By the definition of
s_{0}
, there exists
\lambda>0
independent of
x_{0}
such that

|\operatorname{Rm}(g(t))|_{g(t)}(x_{0})\leq\frac{1}{(\delta\varepsilon r(x_{0})^{2})},

for
(x_{0},t)\in M\times(0,T)
such that
r(x_{0})^{2}>\lambda t
. Estimates for higher covariant derivatives of the curvature
follow from
[
Top10
, Lemma A.4]
.
∎

Define
\Omega_{\lambda}:=\{(x,t)\in M\backslash K\times(0,T)\ |\ r^{2}(x)>\lambda t\}
as a parabolic set of space-time.

Given the above decay of the full Riemann curvature tensor, the first observation is about equivalence of metrics.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(M^{n},g(t))_{t\in(0,T)}
be a complete Ricci flow coming out of Riemannian cone
(C(S),g_{C})
. There exists constants
\lambda>0

A_{0}>1
independent of time such that for all
(x,t)\in\Omega_{\lambda}

A_{0}^{-1}g_{C}(x)\leq g(x,t)\leq A_{0}g_{C}(x).

(3.2)

Proof.

For any
(x,t)\in\Omega_{\lambda}
,
(x,s)\in\Omega_{\lambda}
for
0<s\leq t
. Take a vector
V\in T_{x}M
such that
g_{C}(x)(V,V)=1
, by (
3.1
)

\displaystyle\log g(x,t)(V,V)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{-2\operatorname{Ric}(g(s))(V,V)(x)}{g(x,s)(V,V)}ds

(3.3)

\displaystyle\leq C(n)C_{0}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{r(x)^{2}}ds=C(n)C_{0}\frac{t}{r(x)^{2}}\leq C(n)C_{0}\frac{1}{\lambda}.

Take
A_{0}=\exp{\{C(n)C_{0}\frac{1}{\lambda}}\}
and we have
A_{0}^{-1}g_{C}(x)\leq g(x,t)\leq A_{0}g_{C}(x)
as required.
∎

The equivalence of metrics would help us to get a rough estimate of Taylor’s expansion of
g(t)
:

Proposition 3.4

(Taylor’s expansion formula)
.

Let
(M^{n},g(t))_{t\in(0,T)}
be a complete Ricci flow coming out of Riemannian cone
(C(S),g_{C})
. There exist constants
\{B_{k}>0\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}},\lambda>0
independent of time such that for all
(x,t)\in\Omega_{\lambda}

\left|g(t)-\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\left(\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial t^{j}}g(t)\bigg|_{t=0}\right)\right|_{g_{C}}(x)\leq B_{k+1}\frac{t^{k+1}}{r(x)^{2+2k}},\quad\textrm{for all $k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}$},

(3.4)

\left|\nabla^{g_{C}}\left(g(t)-\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\bigg(\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial t^{j}}g(t)\bigg|_{t=0}\bigg)\right)\right|_{g_{C}}(x)\leq B_{k+1}\frac{t^{k+1}}{r(x)^{2+2k+1}},\quad\textrm{for all $k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}$}.

(3.5)

To prove this proposition, we need a lemma related to higher order time derivatives of metric along Ricci flow.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
(M,g(t))_{t\in(0,T)}
be a smooth solution to Ricci flow. Then for
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, the following equality holds point-wisely

\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial t^{k}}g(t)=\sum_{p+q=0}^{k}\sum_{\sum_{j=1}^{p}i_{j}=2q}(\nabla^{g(t)})^{i_{1}}\operatorname{Rm}(g(t))*\cdot\cdot\cdot*(\nabla^{g(t)})^{i_{p}}\operatorname{Rm}(g(t))*\operatorname{Rm}(g(t))^{k-p-q}.

(3.6)

Here we use the notation
T*S
for the linear combination of contractions of tensors, and
\underbrace{T*\cdot\cdot\cdot*T}_{n}:=T^{n}
for tensor
T
.

Proof.

This proof is based on induction and is based on the evolution equation of
\nabla^{g(t),k}\operatorname{Rm}
. For convenience, we omit all dependence related to
g(t)
.

When
k=1
, (
3.6
) holds automatically by the Ricci flow equation. Assume that (
3.6
) is true for
k\geq 1
.

\frac{\partial^{k+1}}{\partial t^{k+1}}g(t)=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\sum_{p+q=0}^{k}\sum_{\sum_{j=1}^{p}i_{j}=2q}\nabla^{i_{1}}\operatorname{Rm}*\cdot\cdot\cdot*\nabla^{i_{p}}\operatorname{Rm}*\operatorname{Rm}^{k-p-q}\right).

Recall that (see
[
Der
, Lemma 3.7]
)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\nabla^{k}\operatorname{Rm})=\Delta(\nabla^{k}\operatorname{Rm})+\sum_{l=0}^{k}\nabla^{l}\operatorname{Rm}*\nabla^{k-l}\operatorname{Rm}.

Hence for any
1\leq j\leq p

\displaystyle\nabla^{i_{1}}\operatorname{Rm}*\cdot\cdot\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\nabla^{i_{j}}\operatorname{Rm}*\cdot\cdot\cdot*\nabla^{i_{p}}\operatorname{Rm}*\operatorname{Rm}^{k-p-q}

\displaystyle=\nabla^{i_{1}}\operatorname{Rm}*\cdot\cdot\cdot(\Delta(\nabla^{k}\operatorname{Rm})+\sum_{l=0}^{i_{j}}\nabla^{l}\operatorname{Rm}*\nabla^{i_{j}-l}\operatorname{Rm})*\cdot\cdot\cdot*\nabla^{i_{p}}\operatorname{Rm}*\operatorname{Rm}^{k-p-q}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{i_{j}}\nabla^{i_{1}}\operatorname{Rm}*\cdot\cdot\cdot\nabla^{l}\operatorname{Rm}*\nabla^{i_{j}-l}\operatorname{Rm}*\cdot\cdot\cdot*\nabla^{i_{p}}\operatorname{Rm}*\operatorname{Rm}^{k-p-q}

\displaystyle+\nabla^{i_{1}}\operatorname{Rm}*\cdot\cdot\cdot*\nabla^{i_{j}+2}\operatorname{Rm}*\cdot\cdot\cdot*\nabla^{i_{p}}\operatorname{Rm}*\operatorname{Rm}^{k-p-q}.

If
k-p-q\neq 0

\displaystyle\nabla^{i_{1}}\operatorname{Rm}*\cdot\cdot\cdot*\nabla^{i_{p}}\operatorname{Rm}*\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\operatorname{Rm}^{k-p-q})

\displaystyle=\nabla^{i_{1}}\operatorname{Rm}*\cdot\cdot\cdot*\nabla^{i_{p}}\operatorname{Rm}*\operatorname{Rm}^{k-p-q-1}*(\Delta\operatorname{Rm}+\operatorname{Rm}*\operatorname{Rm})

\displaystyle=\nabla^{i_{1}}\operatorname{Rm}*\cdot\cdot\cdot*\nabla^{i_{p}}\operatorname{Rm}*\operatorname{Rm}^{k+1-p-q}+\nabla^{i_{1}}\operatorname{Rm}*\cdot\cdot\cdot*\nabla^{i_{p}}\operatorname{Rm}*\nabla^{2}\operatorname{Rm}*\operatorname{Rm}^{k-p-q-1}.

All of these terms above belong to
\sum_{p+q=0}^{k+1}\sum_{\sum_{j=1}^{p}i_{j}=2q}\nabla^{i_{1}}\operatorname{Rm}*\cdot\cdot\cdot*\nabla^{i_{p}}\operatorname{Rm}*\operatorname{Rm}^{k+1-p-q}
, thus (
3.6
) holds for
k+1
.

By induction, (
3.6
) holds for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
∎

With (
3.6
), we prove Lemma
3.4
directly.

Proof of Proposition
3.4
.

In this proof
\{B_{k}>0\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
denotes constants independent of time but they may vary from line to line.

Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, performing a Taylor expansion with integral remainder,

\displaystyle\left|g(t)-\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\left(\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial t^{j}}g(t)\bigg|_{t=0}\right)\right|_{g_{C}}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{k!}\int_{0}^{t}\left|\frac{\partial^{k+1}}{\partial s^{k+1}}g(s)\right|_{g_{C}}(x)(t-s)^{k}ds

\displaystyle\leq C(n)\frac{A_{0}}{k!}\int_{0}^{t}\left|\frac{\partial^{k+1}}{\partial s^{k+1}}g(s)\right|_{g(s)}(x)(t-s)^{k}ds.

By (
3.1
) and (
3.6
), there exists a constant
B_{k+1}>0
such that

\left|\frac{\partial^{k+1}}{\partial s^{k+1}}g(s)\right|_{g(s)}(x)\leq C(n)\sum_{p+q=0}^{k+1}\sum_{\sum_{j=1}^{p}i_{j}=2q}|\nabla^{i_{1}}\operatorname{Rm}|\cdot\cdot\cdot|\nabla^{i_{p}}\operatorname{Rm}||\operatorname{Rm}|^{k+1-p-q}(x)\leq B_{k+1}\frac{1}{r(x)^{2+2k}}.

Hence (
3.4
) holds for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

To obtain (
3.5
), we first need to estimate
h(x,t):=\nabla^{g_{C}}(g(t)-g_{C})(x)
for
(x,t)\in\Omega_{\lambda}
. Fix
(x,t)\in\Omega_{\lambda}
and define
y(s):=|h(x,s)|_{g_{C}}^{2}
for
0<s\leq t
. Since
(x,s)\in\Omega_{\lambda}
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}y(s)

\displaystyle\leq 4|\nabla^{g_{C}}\operatorname{Ric}{(g(s))}|_{g_{C}}(x)\sqrt{y(s)}

\displaystyle\leq 4\left(|(\nabla^{g_{C}}-\nabla^{g(s)})\operatorname{Ric}(g(s))|_{g_{C}}(x)+|\nabla^{g(s)}\operatorname{Ric}(g(s))|_{g_{C}}(x)\right)\sqrt{y(s)}.

Notice that

\displaystyle(\nabla^{g_{C}}-\nabla^{g(s)})\operatorname{Ric}(g(s))

\displaystyle=g(s)^{-1}*h(x,s)*\operatorname{Ric}(g(s)),

\displaystyle|(\nabla^{g_{C}}-\nabla^{g(s)})\operatorname{Ric}(g(s))|_{g_{C}}

\displaystyle\leq C(n)|g(s)^{-1}|_{g_{C}}(x)|\operatorname{Ric}(g(s))|_{g_{C}}(x)\sqrt{y(s)}

\displaystyle\leq C(n)A_{0}|\operatorname{Ric}(g(s))|_{g_{C}}(x)\sqrt{y(s)}

\displaystyle\leq C(n)A_{0}^{2}|\operatorname{Ric}(g(s))|_{g(s)}(x)\sqrt{y(s)}

\displaystyle\leq C(n)C_{0}A_{0}^{2}\frac{1}{r(x)^{2}}\sqrt{y(s)},

and by (
3.1
),

|\nabla^{g(s)}\operatorname{Ric}(g(s))|_{g_{C}}(x)\leq C(n)A_{0}|\nabla^{g(s)}\operatorname{Ric}(g(s))|_{g(s)}(x)\leq C(n)A_{0}C_{1}\frac{1}{r(x)^{3}}.

Hence by Cauchy-Schwarz

\frac{d}{ds}y(s)\leq\left(C(n)A_{0}C_{1}\frac{1}{r(x)^{3}}+C(n)C_{0}A_{0}^{2}\frac{1}{r(x)^{2}}\sqrt{y(s)}\right)\sqrt{y(s)}\leq B_{0}\left(\frac{y(s)}{r(x)^{2}}+\frac{1}{r(x)^{4}}\right).

By integration and initial condition
y(0)=0
, we have
y(s)\leq\frac{1}{r(x)^{2}}e^{B_{0}\frac{s}{r(x)^{2}}}\leq B_{0}\frac{1}{r(x)^{2}}
as
s\leq t\leq\lambda^{-1}r(x)^{2}
.

Moreover,

\displaystyle|h(x,t)|_{g_{C}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{t}|\nabla^{g_{C}}\operatorname{Ric}(g(s))|_{g_{C}}(x)ds

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{t}\left(|(\nabla^{g_{C}}-\nabla^{g(s)})\operatorname{Ric}(g(s))|_{g_{C}}(x)+|\nabla^{g(s)}\operatorname{Ric}(g(s))|_{g_{C}}(x)\right)ds

\displaystyle\leq B_{0}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{1}{r(x)^{2}}\sqrt{y(s)}+\frac{1}{r(x)^{3}}\right)ds

\displaystyle\leq B_{0}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{r(x)^{3}}ds=B_{0}\frac{t}{r(x)^{3}}.

For higher order expansion, performing a Taylor expansion with integral remainder:

\left|\nabla^{g_{C}}\left(g(t)-\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\bigg(\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial t^{j}}g(t)\bigg|_{t=0}\bigg)\right)\right|_{g_{C}}(x)\leq\frac{1}{k!}\int_{0}^{t}\left|\nabla^{g_{C}}\left(\frac{\partial^{k+1}}{\partial s^{k+1}}g(s)\right)\right|_{g_{C}}(x)(t-s)^{k}ds.

Notice that,

\displaystyle\nabla^{g_{C}}\left(\frac{\partial^{k+1}}{\partial s^{k+1}}g(s)\right)(x)

\displaystyle=(\nabla^{g_{C}}-\nabla^{g(s)})\left(\frac{\partial^{k+1}}{\partial s^{k+1}}g(s)\right)(x)+\nabla^{g(s)}\left(\frac{\partial^{k+1}}{\partial s^{k+1}}g(s)\right)(x)

\displaystyle=g(s)^{-1}*h(x,s)*\left(\frac{\partial^{k+1}}{\partial s^{k+1}}g(s)\right)(x)+\nabla^{g(s)}\left(\frac{\partial^{k+1}}{\partial s^{k+1}}g(s)\right)(x).

Hence,

\left|\nabla^{g_{C}}\left(\frac{\partial^{k+1}}{\partial s^{k+1}}g(s)\right)\right|_{g_{C}}(x)\leq C(n)A_{0}\left(|h(x,s)|_{g_{C}}\left|\frac{\partial^{k+1}}{\partial s^{k+1}}g(s)\right|_{g_{C}}(x)+\left|\nabla^{g(s)}\left(\frac{\partial^{k+1}}{\partial s^{k+1}}g(s)\right)\right|_{g_{C}}(x)\right),

for each term by (
3.1
) and (
3.6
):

\left|\frac{\partial^{k+1}}{\partial s^{k+1}}g(s)\right|_{g_{C}}(x)\leq C(n)A_{0}\left|\frac{\partial^{k+1}}{\partial s^{k+1}}g(s)\right|_{g(s)}(x)\leq C(n)A_{0}B_{k+1}\frac{1}{r(x)^{2+2k}}=B_{k+1}\frac{1}{r(x)^{2+2k}},

\left|\nabla^{g(s)}\left(\frac{\partial^{k+1}}{\partial s^{k+1}}g(s)\right)\right|_{g_{C}}(x)\leq C(n)A_{0}\left|\nabla^{g(s)}\left(\frac{\partial^{k+1}}{\partial s^{k+1}}g(s)\right)\right|_{g(s)}(x)\leq B_{k+1}\frac{1}{r(x)^{2+2k+1}}.

Thus,

\left|\nabla^{g_{C}}\left(\frac{\partial^{k+1}}{\partial s^{k+1}}g(s)\right)\right|_{g_{C}}(x)\leq B_{k+1}\frac{1}{r(x)^{2+2k+1}}.

As a consequence, we have

\left|\nabla^{g_{C}}\left(g(t)-\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\bigg(\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial t^{j}}g(t)\bigg|_{t=0}\bigg)\right)\right|_{g_{C}}(x)\leq\int_{0}^{t}B_{k+1}\frac{1}{r(x)^{2+2k+1}}(t-s)^{k}ds=B_{k+1}\frac{t^{k+1}}{r(x)^{2+2k+1}}.

Thus (
3.5
) holds for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
∎

4.
Set up of a complex Monge-Ampère equation

Let
(M,g,J,X)
be a gradient expanding Kähler-Ricci soliton as in Theorem
1.2
,
(C_{0},g_{0})
be the Kähler cone as in Theorem
1.2
, and let
g(t)
be the forward self-similar Kähler-Ricci flow associated to
g
. Suppose that
f\in C^{\infty}(M;\mathbb{R})
is the normalized Hamiltonian potential of vector field
X
such that
\nabla^{g}f=X
and
|\partial f|^{2}_{g}+R_{w}=f
.

4.1.
Symmetries of the Kähler potential

Lemma 4.1
.

The Reeb vector field
JX
is a Killing vector field for
g(t)
for all
t\in(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

The Reeb vector field
JX
is a Killing vector field for
g
.

For any two vector fields
Y,Z
, we have

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{JX}g)(Y,Z)

\displaystyle=g(\nabla^{g}_{Y}JX,Z)+g(Y,\nabla^{g}_{Z}JX)

\displaystyle=g(\nabla^{g}_{JY}X,Z)-g(JY,\nabla^{g}_{Z}X)

\displaystyle=\nabla^{g,2}f(JY,Z)-\nabla^{g,2}f(Z,JY)

\displaystyle=0.

As
X
is a real-holomorphic vector field,
[JX,X]=J([X,X])=0
, this implies that
\Phi_{t}^{*}(JX)=JX
for any
t\in(0,\infty)
. Since
g(t)=t\Phi_{t}^{*}g
, and
\mathcal{L}_{JX}g=0
, we have
\mathcal{L}_{JX}g(t)=0
as expected.
∎

In Theorem
1.2
, we assume that
JX
is Killing for
g_{\varphi}(t_{0})
for some
t_{0}\in(0,T)
. The following lemma tells us
JX
is Killing for
g_{\varphi}(t)
for all
t\in(0,T).

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
g_{\varphi}(t)_{t\in(0,T)}
be the solution to the Kähler-Ricci flow in Theorem
1.2
, then
\mathcal{L}_{JX}g_{\varphi}(t)=0
for all
t\in(0,T)
.

Proof.

From the Killing condition of Theorem
1.2
, there exists
t_{0}\in(0,T)
such that
\mathcal{L}_{JX}g_{\varphi}(t_{0})=0
. From the curvature condition and conical condition,
g_{\varphi}(t)
is a Ricci flow coming out of Kähler cone
(C_{0},g_{0})
(see Definition
3.1
). Thanks to (
3.1
), there exist constants
\lambda>0
and
C_{0}>0
independent of time such that

|\operatorname{Rm}(g_{\varphi}(t))|_{g_{\varphi}(t)}(x)\leq C_{0}r(\pi(x))^{-2}

for all
(x,t)
such that
r(\pi(x))^{2}\geq\lambda t
.

Fix
t_{0}<t_{1}<T
, choose
x\in M
such that
r(\pi(x))^{2}\geq\lambda t_{1}
, hence for all
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]

|\operatorname{Rm}(g_{\varphi}(t))|_{g_{\varphi}(t)}(x)\leq C_{0}r(\pi(x))^{-2}\leq C_{0}(\lambda t_{1})^{-1}.

We deduce that there exists a constant
K_{1}>0
such that for all
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
,

\sup_{M}|\operatorname{Rm}(g_{\varphi}(t))|_{g_{\varphi}(t)}\leq K_{1}.

As a consequence of the uniqueness result of
[
CZ06
, Theorem 1.1]
, since
JX
is a Killing vector field for
g_{\varphi}(t_{0})
,
JX
is a Killing vector field for
g_{\varphi}(t_{1})
.

Now fix
0<t_{2}<t_{0}
, choose
x\in M
such that
r(\pi(x))^{2}\geq\lambda t_{0}
, hence for all
t\in[t_{2},t_{0}]

|\operatorname{Rm}(g_{\varphi}(t))|_{g_{\varphi}(t)}(x)\leq C_{0}r(\pi(x))^{-2}\leq C_{0}(\lambda t_{0})^{-1}.

We deduce that there exists a constant
K_{2}>0
such that for all
t\in[t_{2},t_{0}]
,

\sup_{M}|\operatorname{Rm}(g_{\varphi}(t))|_{g_{\varphi}(t)}\leq K_{2}.

As a consequence of the backward uniqueness theorem due to
[
Kot10
, Theorem 1.1]
, since
JX
is a Killing vector field for
g_{\varphi}(t_{0})
,
JX
is a Killing vector field for
g_{\varphi}(t_{2})
.

Hence
JX
is a Killing vector field for
g_{\varphi}(t)
for all
t\in(0,T)
.
∎

We have shown that
JX
is a Killing vector field for
g_{\varphi}(t)
for all
t\in(0,T)
, the following lemma implies that the Kähler potential
\varphi
could admit more symmetries thanks to the Killing vector field
JX
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
g_{\varphi}(t)
be the solution to Kähler-Ricci flow in Theorem
1.2
. There exists a smooth real-valued function
\tilde{\varphi}\in C^{\infty}(M\times(0,T))
such that
JX\cdot\tilde{\varphi}=0
and
\partial\bar{\partial}\tilde{\varphi}=\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi
.

Proof.

Consider the isometry group of
(M,\,g)
that fixes
E
endowed with the topology induced by uniform convergence on compact
subsets of
M
. By the Arzelà-Ascoli theorem, this is a compact Lie group. And
[
CDS24
]
has proved that the flows of
X
and
JX
preserve
E
(see
[
CDS24
, Lemma 2.6]
). Thus in particular, the flow of
JX
lies in such isometry group, taking the closure of the flow of
JX
in this group therefore yields the holomorphic isometric action of a torus
T
on
(M,\,J,\,g)
.

Since
JX
is Killing with respect to
g_{\varphi}
by Lemma
4.2
, all elements in
T
fix
g_{\varphi}
. Let
\mu_{T}
be the normalized Haar measure of
T
, and we define

\tilde{\varphi}:=\int_{a\in T}a^{*}\varphi d\mu_{T}(a).

(4.1)

By construction,
\tilde{\varphi}
is invariant under the flow of
JX
, so it yields that
JX\cdot\tilde{\varphi}=0
. In addition, Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
imply that
0=\mathcal{L}_{JX}(w_{\varphi})=\mathcal{L}_{JX}(i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)=i\partial\bar{\partial}(JX\cdot\varphi)
. By (
4.1
) we have

\partial\bar{\partial}\tilde{\varphi}=\int_{a\in T}a^{*}(\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi)d\mu_{T}(a)=\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi,

as expected.
∎

Since for all
t\in(0,T)

w_{\varphi}(t)=w(t)+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(t)=w(t)+i\partial\bar{\partial}\tilde{\varphi}(t)
. From now on, we choose to use
g_{\tilde{\varphi}}(w_{\tilde{\varphi}})
instead of
g_{\varphi}(w_{\varphi})
. For convenience, we keep the notation
\varphi
for the Kähler potential.

4.2.
Reduction to a complex Monge-Ampère equation

The symmetries of the Kähler potential help us to reduce the Kähler-Ricci flow equation to a complex Monge-Ampère equation.

Proposition 4.4

(Complex Monge-Ampère equation)
.

Let
g_{\varphi}(t)_{t\in(0,T)}
be the solution to Kähler-Ricci flow in Theorem
1.2
satisfying

\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g_{\varphi}(t)&=-\operatorname{Ric}(g_{\varphi}(t)),\\
JX\cdot\varphi(t)&=0\quad t\in(0,T).\end{split}

(4.2)

Then there exists a smooth real-valued function
\bar{\varphi}\in C^{\infty}(M\times(0,T))
such that
\partial\bar{\partial}\bar{\varphi}=\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi
and

\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\bar{\varphi}(t)&=\log\frac{w_{\bar{\varphi}}(t)^{n}}{w(t)^{n}},\\
JX\cdot\bar{\varphi}(t)&=0\quad t\in(0,T),\end{split}

(4.3)

with
\pi_{*}\bar{\varphi}(t)
converges to
0
locally smoothly when
t
tends to
0
. Here
w_{\bar{\varphi}}(t)=w(t)+i\partial\bar{\partial}\bar{\varphi}(t)
for all
t\in(0,T).

Proof.

We compute the difference of (
1.6
) and (
4.2
), we get

i\partial\bar{\partial}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi(t)=\rho_{w(t)}-\rho_{w_{\varphi}(t)}.

In Kähler manifold
M

\rho_{w(t)}-\rho_{w_{\varphi}(t)}=i\partial\bar{\partial}\log\frac{w_{\varphi}(t)^{n}}{w(t)^{n}}.

This yields that

i\partial\bar{\partial}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi(t)-\log\frac{w_{\varphi}(t)^{n}}{w(t)^{n}}\right)=0.

(4.4)

Let
\kappa(t):=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi(t)-\log\frac{w_{\varphi}(t)^{n}}{w(t)^{n}}
. We have
i\partial\bar{\partial}\kappa(t)=0
and
JX\cdot\kappa(t)=0
thanks to Lemma
4.2
and Lemma
4.3
. If we denote
X^{1,0}
to be the holomorphic part of
X
, then
JX\cdot\kappa(t)=0
is equivalent to say that
X^{1,0}\cdot\kappa(t)
is real. Since
i\partial\bar{\partial}\kappa(t)=0
, it follows that

\bar{\partial}(X^{1,0}\cdot\kappa(t))=0
. This means
X^{1,0}\cdot\kappa(t)
is a real-valued holomorphic function on
M
, this results in that
X^{1,0}\cdot\kappa(t)
is constant for each time
t
. Therefore
X\cdot\kappa(t)
is a constant denoted by
c
.

Since
d\pi(X)=r\partial_{r}
, equation
r\partial_{r}\pi_{*}\kappa(t)=c
means
\pi_{*}\kappa(t)=c\log r+\bar{\kappa}(t)
, where
\bar{\kappa}
denotes a smooth function that is only defined on the link
S
of
C_{0}
. Moreover, as
i\partial\bar{\partial}(\pi_{*}\kappa(t))=0
, in particular
\Delta_{g_{0}}\pi_{*}\kappa(t)=0
,
n\geq 2
, so that,

0=\Delta_{g_{0}}\pi_{*}\kappa(t)=cr^{-2}(2n-2)+r^{-2}\Delta_{g_{S}}\bar{\kappa}.

This implies that
c=0
and
\bar{\kappa}
is a constant. Since
M\backslash E
is dense on
M
,
\kappa(t)
is a constant in space denoted by
c(t).
Hence

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi(t)-\log\frac{w_{\varphi}(t)^{n}}{w(t)^{n}}=c(t).

(4.5)

Take
C(t)
as a primitive of
c(t)
such that
C^{\prime}(t)=c(t)
, and let
\tilde{\varphi}(t)=\varphi(t)+C(t)
, we have

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\tilde{\varphi}(t)-\log\frac{w_{\tilde{\varphi}}(t)^{n}}{w(t)^{n}}=0.

Local convergence (conical condition stated in Theorem
1.2
) of
\pi_{*}w_{\varphi}(t),\pi_{*}w(t)
and (
4.5
) imply that one can extend
\pi_{*}\tilde{\varphi}(t)
to a smooth function at time 0, which we denote by
\varphi_{0}
.

In particular,
\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{0}=0
in
C_{0}-\{\textrm{vertex}\}
and
J_{0}X\cdot\varphi_{0}=0
. By the same reason as above
\varphi_{0}
is a constant
b
. Let
\bar{\varphi}(t)=\tilde{\varphi}(t)-b
, we have
\partial\bar{\partial}\bar{\varphi}=\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi
,
JX\cdot\bar{\varphi}=JX\cdot\varphi=0
and

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\bar{\varphi}(t)=\log\frac{w_{\bar{\varphi}}(t)^{n}}{w(t)^{n}},\quad t\in(0,T)

with
\pi_{*}\bar{\varphi}
converging to 0 when
t
tends to 0 as desired.
∎

Since
\partial\bar{\partial}\bar{\varphi}=\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi
and
JX\cdot\bar{\varphi}=JX\cdot\varphi=0
, from now on, we choose to use
g_{\bar{\varphi}}
(resp.
w_{\bar{\varphi}}
) instead of
g_{\varphi}
(resp.
w_{\varphi}
). For convenience, we keep the notation
\varphi
for the Kähler potential.

5.
A priori estimates at spatial infinity

We adapt the notation
\Omega_{\lambda}
mentioned in section
3
, and we identify
M\backslash E
with
C_{0}\backslash\{o\}
as in Theorem
1.2
through the biholomorphism
\pi
.

In this section, we aim to derive precise estimates in this parabolic set
\Omega_{\lambda}
. Building on the expansion formula developed (Proposition
3.4
), we first establish polynomial decay in
\Omega_{\lambda}
. Then, by applying the maximum principle, we obtain a nearly optimal estimate, which ultimately leads to exponential decay.

Recall the setting of
\varphi
as in Proposition
4.4
:

\begin{split}&\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi(t)=\log\frac{w_{\varphi}(t)^{n}}{w(t)^{n}},\\
&JX\cdot\varphi(t)=0,\quad\forall t\in(0,T),\end{split}

with
\pi_{*}\varphi(t)
converges to

locally smoothly when

.

5.1.
Polynomial decay

From Section
3
, both
g(t)
and
g_{\varphi}(t)
as in Theorem
1.2
are Ricci flows coming out of the same Kähler cone
(C_{0},g_{0})
and share the same Taylor expansion formula.

As a consequence of Proposition
3.4
applied to
g_{\varphi}(t)_{t\in(0,T)}
and
g(t)_{t\in(0,\infty)}
, we have the following proposition:

Proposition 5.1
.

There exist constants
\{B_{k}>0\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and
\lambda>0
independent of time such that

\begin{split}&|\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(x,t)|_{g_{0}}\leq B_{k}\frac{t^{k}}{r(x)^{2k}},\quad\textrm{for all $(x,t)\in\Omega_{\lambda}$, $k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}$},\\
&|\nabla^{g_{0}}(\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(x,t))|_{g_{0}}\leq B_{k}\frac{t^{k}}{r(x)^{2k+1}},\quad\textrm{for all $(x,t)\in\Omega_{\lambda}$, $k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}$},\\
\end{split}

(5.1)

Thanks to the complex Monge-Ampère equation (
4.3
), we establish polynomial decay of
\varphi,X\cdot\varphi
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi:=\dot{\varphi}
.

Proposition 5.2

(Polynomial decay)
.

For
\lambda>0
as in Proposition
5.1
, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, there exists a constant
C_{k}>0
independent of time, such that for all
(x,t)\in\Omega_{\lambda}
:

\begin{split}&|\dot{\varphi}(x,t)|\leq C_{k}\frac{t^{k}}{r(x)^{2k}},\\
&|X\cdot\dot{\varphi}(x,t)|\leq C_{k}\frac{t^{k}}{r(x)^{2k}},\\
&|X\cdot\varphi(x,t)|\leq C_{k}\frac{t^{k+1}}{r(x)^{2k}},\\
&|\varphi(x,t)|\leq C_{k}\frac{t^{k+1}}{r(x)^{2k}}.\end{split}

(5.2)

Proof.

For
(x,t)\in\Omega_{\lambda}
,
the complex Monge-Ampère equation (
4.3
) implies that

\begin{split}&\dot{\varphi}(x,t)\leq\Delta_{w(t)}\varphi(x,t),\\
&\dot{\varphi}(x,t)\geq\Delta_{w_{\varphi}(t)}\varphi(x,t).\\
\end{split}

Since
|\Delta_{w(t)}\varphi(x,t)|\leq C(n)|g(t)^{-1}|_{g_{0}}|i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(x,t)|_{g_{0}}\leq C(n)A_{0}B_{k}\frac{t^{k}}{r(x)^{k}}
. Take
C_{k}:=C(n)A_{0}B_{k}
such that
\dot{\varphi}(x,t)\leq C_{k}\frac{t^{k}}{r(x)^{2k}}
. From now on, we use
C_{k}>0
to denote a constant independent of time and itself would vary from line to line.

Moreover,
|\Delta_{w_{\varphi}(t)}\varphi(x,t)|\leq C(n)|g_{\varphi}(t)^{-1}|_{g_{0}}|i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(x,t)|_{g_{0}}\leq C_{k}\frac{t^{k}}{r(x)^{k}}
, hence
|\dot{\varphi}(x,t)|\leq C_{k}\frac{t^{k}}{r(x)^{2k}}
. By integration and
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\varphi(x,t)=0
, hence
|\varphi(x,t)|\leq C_{k}\frac{t^{k+1}}{r(x)^{2k}}
follows immediately.

Recall that
X\cdot\dot{\varphi}(t)=X\cdot\log\frac{w_{\varphi}(t)^{n}}{w(t)^{n}}=\operatorname{tr}_{w_{\varphi}(t)}(\mathcal{L}_{X}w_{\varphi}(t))-\operatorname{tr}_{w(t)}(\mathcal{L}_{X}w(t))
.

On the one hand

|\operatorname{tr}_{w_{\varphi}(t)}(\mathcal{L}_{X}w_{\varphi}(t))-\operatorname{tr}_{w(t)}(\mathcal{L}_{X}w(t))|\leq|\operatorname{tr}_{w_{\varphi}(t)}(\mathcal{L}_{X}w(t))-\operatorname{tr}_{w(t)}(\mathcal{L}_{X}w(t))|_{g_{0}}+|\operatorname{tr}_{w_{\varphi}(t)}\left(\mathcal{L}_{X}(\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(t))\right)|_{g_{0}}.

On the other hand

\begin{split}\operatorname{tr}_{w_{\varphi}(t)}(\mathcal{L}_{X}w(t))-\operatorname{tr}_{w(t)}(\mathcal{L}_{X}w(t))&=\nabla^{g(t)}X*(g_{\varphi}(t)^{-1}-g(t)^{-1})=\nabla^{g(t)}X*\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(t)*g_{\varphi}(t)^{-1}*g(t)^{-1},\\
\operatorname{tr}_{w_{\varphi}(t)}\left(\mathcal{L}_{X}(\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(t))\right)&=g_{\varphi}(t)^{-1}*\nabla^{g_{0}}X*\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(t)+g_{\varphi}(s)^{-1}*X*\nabla^{g_{0}}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(t).\end{split}

For
(x,t)\in\Omega_{\lambda}
, by Proposition
5.1
we have

|\operatorname{tr}_{w_{\varphi}(t)}(\mathcal{L}_{X}w(t))-\operatorname{tr}_{w(t)}(\mathcal{L}_{X}w(t))|_{g_{0}}(x)\leq C(n)|\nabla^{g(t)}X|_{g_{0}}|g_{\varphi}(t)|_{g_{0}}|g(t)|_{g_{0}}|\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(t)|_{g_{0}}(x)\leq C_{k}\frac{t^{k}}{r(x)^{2k}},

and,

\displaystyle|\operatorname{tr}_{w_{\varphi}(t)}\left(\mathcal{L}_{X}(\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(t))\right)|_{g_{0}}(x)

\displaystyle\leq C(n)|g_{\varphi}(t)^{-1}|_{g_{0}}\left(|\nabla^{g_{0}}X|_{g_{0}}|\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(t)|_{g_{0}}+|\nabla^{g_{0}}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(t)|_{g_{0}}|X|_{g_{0}})(x)\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{k}\frac{t^{k}}{r(x)^{2k}}.

Since
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}X\cdot\varphi(x,t)=0
, by integration we have
X\cdot\varphi(x,t)\leq C_{k}\frac{t^{k+1}}{r(x)^{2k}}
as required.

∎

5.2.
Obstruction tensor

Recall that the self-similar solution to Kähler-Ricci flow
g(t)
satisfies

\mathcal{L}_{\frac{X}{2}}g(t)-t\operatorname{Ric}(g(t))-g(t)=0,\quad\textrm{for all $t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}$}.

(5.3)

Hence the obstruction tensor
T(t)
to evaluate if
g_{\varphi}(t)
coincides with
g(t)
must be

T(t)=\mathcal{L}_{\frac{X}{2}}g_{\varphi}(t)-t\operatorname{Ric}(g_{\varphi}(t))-g_{\varphi}(t),\quad\textrm{for all $t\in(0,T)$}.

(5.4)

Combining it with (
5.3
), we get

\displaystyle T(t)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\frac{X}{2}}g_{\varphi}(t)-\mathcal{L}_{\frac{X}{2}}g(t)+t\operatorname{Ric}(g(t))-t\operatorname{Ric}(g_{\varphi}(t))+g(t)-g_{\varphi}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\frac{X}{2}}(\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(t))+t\partial\bar{\partial}\log\frac{w_{\varphi}(t)^{n}}{w(t)^{n}}-\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(t)

\displaystyle=\partial\bar{\partial}\left(\frac{X}{2}\cdot\varphi(t)+t\dot{\varphi}(t)-\varphi(t)\right).

The obstruction tensor is very important in Riemannian geometry and Kähler geometry. In Riemannian geometry, analysis of this obstruction tensor is more complicated. In Kähler geometry, from the above calculation,
T
is a (1,1) form given by a scalar function. Analysis of this scalar function may help us better understand the obstruction tensor.

Define
u(x,t):=t\dot{\varphi}(x,t)+\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\varphi(x,t)-\varphi(x,t)
for
(x,t)\in M\times(0,T)
.

In Section
6
, the function
u
is related to
\partial_{\tau}\psi
in self-similar variables (pulled back by the flow of
X
).

Proposition 5.3

(Solution to Heat equation)
.

The function
u
is a solution to the heat equation along Ricci flow, i.e.

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{w_{\varphi}(t)}\right)u(x,t)=0.

(5.5)

Proof.

On the one hand

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u

\displaystyle=\dot{\varphi}+t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\dot{\varphi}+\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\dot{\varphi}-\dot{\varphi}

\displaystyle=t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\dot{\varphi}+\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\dot{\varphi}.

On the other hand

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\dot{\varphi}=\Delta_{w_{\varphi}(t)}\dot{\varphi}+\operatorname{tr}_{w_{\varphi}(t)}(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}w(t))-\operatorname{tr}_{w(t)}(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}w(t)).

And

\displaystyle X\cdot\dot{\varphi}

\displaystyle=X\cdot\log\frac{w_{\varphi}(t)^{n}}{w(t)^{n}}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr}_{w_{\varphi}(t)}(\mathcal{L}_{X}w_{\varphi}(t))-\operatorname{tr}_{w(t)}(\mathcal{L}_{X}w(t))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr}_{w_{\varphi}(t)}(\mathcal{L}_{X}w(t))-\operatorname{tr}_{w(t)}(\mathcal{L}_{X}w(t))+\Delta_{w_{\varphi}(t)}(X\cdot\varphi).

By (
5.3
) and
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}w(t)=-\rho_{w(t)}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u

\displaystyle=\Delta_{w_{\varphi}(t)}(u+\varphi)+\operatorname{tr}_{w_{\varphi}(t)}(w(t))-\operatorname{tr}_{w(t)}(w(t))

\displaystyle=\Delta_{w_{\varphi}(t)}(u+\varphi)-\Delta_{w_{\varphi}(t)}\varphi=\Delta_{w_{\varphi}(t)}u

as desired.
∎

The obstruction tensor is exactly
\partial\bar{\partial}u
. Moreover,
u
is a solution to the Heat equation along the Kähler-Ricci flow

We immediately get a rough estimates for
u,X\cdot u
and
\partial\bar{\partial}u
thanks to (
5.2
).

Corollary 5.4
.

There exists a constant
N>0
independent of time, such that for all
(x,t)\in\Omega_{\lambda}
,

\begin{split}&|u(x,t)|\leq N\frac{t^{2}}{r(x)^{2}},\\
&|X\cdot u(x,t)|\leq N\frac{t^{2}}{r(x)^{2}},\\
&|\partial\bar{\partial}u(x,t)|_{g_{0}}\leq N\frac{t}{r(x)^{2}}.\\
\end{split}

(5.6)

Proof.

For convenience,
N>0
is a constant independent of time that may vary from line to line.

The estimate of
u
follows immediately by (
5.2
). Due to (
5.2
), there exists a constant
N
such that
|X\cdot\dot{\varphi}|\leq N\frac{t}{r(x)^{2}}
.

Moreover, since
JX\cdot\varphi=0,JX\cdot(X\cdot\varphi)=0
and
X
is real holomorphic, we have

\partial\bar{\partial}{\varphi}(X,X)=2X^{i}X^{\bar{j}}\partial_{i}\bar{\partial}_{\bar{j}}\varphi=2X^{i}\partial_{i}(X^{\bar{j}}\bar{\partial}_{\bar{j}}\varphi)=X^{i}\partial_{i}(X\cdot\varphi)=\frac{1}{2}X\cdot(X\cdot\varphi).

Combining it with (
5.1
) and (
5.2
), there is a constant
N>0
such that
|X\cdot u(x,t)|\leq N\frac{t^{2}}{r(x)^{2}}
.

Since
g_{\varphi}(t)
and
g(t)
are two solutions coming out of cone, there exists
N>0
such that

|\operatorname{Ric}(g_{\varphi}(t))(x)|_{g_{0}}+|\operatorname{Ric}(g(t))(x)|_{g_{0}}\leq\frac{N}{r(x)^{2}}

for
(x,t)\in\Omega_{\lambda}
. Recall that

\displaystyle\partial\bar{\partial}u(x,t)

\displaystyle=t\partial\bar{\partial}\log\frac{w_{\varphi}(t)}{w(t)}+\partial\bar{\partial}\left(\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\varphi(t)\right)-\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(t)

\displaystyle=t\operatorname{Ric}(g(t))(x)-t\operatorname{Ric}(g_{\varphi}(t))(x)+\mathcal{L}_{\frac{X}{2}}(\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(t))-\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(t).

By (
5.1
) and the proof of (
5.2
), we have
|\partial\bar{\partial}u(x,t)|_{g_{0}}\leq N\frac{t}{r(x)^{2}}
as required.
∎

5.3.
Hamiltonian potentials of
X

Let
f\in C^{\infty}(M;\mathbb{R})
be the normalized Hamiltonian potential of
X
with respect to the Kähler-Ricci soliton metric
g
, then for any
t\in(0,T)
, function
t\Phi_{t}^{*}f
is a Hamiltonian potential of
X
with respect to
g(t)
.

Lemma 5.5
.

For
t\in(0,T)
,
t\Phi_{t}^{*}f+\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\varphi(t)
is a Hamiltonian potential of
X
with respect to
g_{\varphi}(t)
.

Proof.

Take local holomorphic coordinate of
M
, since
X
is real holomorphic and
JX\cdot\varphi=0
,
X^{1,0}\cdot\varphi=\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\varphi
, we have

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}_{\bar{j}}(t\Phi_{t}^{*}f+\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\varphi(t))

\displaystyle=\bar{\partial}_{\bar{j}}(t\Phi_{t}^{*}f)+\bar{\partial}_{\bar{j}}(X^{1,0}\cdot\varphi(t))

\displaystyle=g(t)_{i\bar{j}}X^{i}+(X^{1,0})^{i}\partial_{i}\bar{\partial}_{\bar{j}}\varphi(t)

\displaystyle=g(t)_{i\bar{j}}X^{i}+X^{i}\partial_{i}\bar{\partial}_{\bar{j}}\varphi(t)

\displaystyle=g_{\varphi}(t)_{i\bar{j}}X^{i}.

Hence
t\Phi_{t}^{*}f+\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\varphi(t)
is a Hamiltonian potential of
X
with respect to
g_{\varphi}(t)
.
∎

For convenience we define
f_{t}(x,t):=\Phi_{t}^{*}f(x),f_{\varphi}(x,t):=\Phi_{t}^{*}f(x)+\frac{1}{2t}X\cdot\varphi(x,t)
for
(x,t)\in M\times(0,T)
.

Corollary 5.6
.

For
\lambda>0
as in Proposition
5.1
, there exists a constant
D>0
independent of time such that for all
(x,t)\in\Omega_{\lambda}
:

\begin{split}&\left|f_{t}(x,t)-\frac{r(x)^{2}}{2t}\right|\leq D,\\
&\left|f_{\varphi}(x,t)-\frac{r(x)^{2}}{2t}\right|\leq D.\\
\end{split}

(5.7)

Proof.

In this proof
D
is a constant independent of time that would vary from line to line.

From Lemma
2.2
, we deduce that

\Phi_{t}^{*}f=\Phi^{*}_{t}(|\partial f|^{2}_{g}+R_{w})=|\partial\Phi_{t}^{*}f|^{2}_{\Phi_{t}^{*}g}+\Phi^{*}_{t}R_{w}=t^{-1}|\partial t\Phi_{t}^{*}f|^{2}_{g(t)}+\Phi^{*}_{t}R_{w}=t^{-1}\frac{1}{2}|X|^{2}_{g(t)}+\Phi^{*}_{t}R_{w}.

Since
R_{w}
is globally bounded, we bound it by
D
, and we notice that in
\Omega_{\lambda}
,

\displaystyle\left||X|^{2}_{g(t)}-|X|^{2}_{g_{0}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{t}|\operatorname{Ric}(g(s))|_{g_{0}}|X|^{2}_{g_{0}}ds\leq Dt.

Hence we get
|\Phi_{t}^{*}f(x)-\frac{r(x)^{2}}{2t}|\leq D
, and the second inequality comes from the polynomial decay of
t^{-1}X\cdot\varphi
stated in (
5.2
).
∎

Proposition 5.7
.

5.4.
Maximum principle

Since

. We also denote by
g_{\psi}(\tau)
the Riemannian metric induced by
w_{\psi}(\tau)
.

We call this equation (
6.1
) the
normalized Kähler-Ricci flow
.

Lemma 6.1

(Correspondences)
.

One has the following correspondences for quantities of normalized Kähler-Ricci flow and Kähler-Ricci flow: for any
(x,\tau)\in M\times(-\infty,\log T)
,

(i)

e^{\tau}\Phi_{e^{\tau}}^{*}\psi(x,\tau)=\varphi(x,e^{\tau})
,

(ii)

e^{\tau}\Phi_{e^{\tau}}^{*}(X\cdot\psi)(x,\tau)=X\cdot\varphi(x,e^{\tau})
,

(iii)

e^{\tau}\Phi_{e^{\tau}}^{*}\dot{\psi}(x,\tau)=u(x,e^{\tau})
, here
\dot{\psi}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\psi
,

(iv)

e^{\tau}\Phi_{e^{\tau}}^{*}(X\cdot\dot{\psi})(x,\tau)=X\cdot u(x,e^{\tau})
,

(v)

e^{\tau}\Phi_{e^{\tau}}^{*}(\partial\bar{\partial}\dot{\psi})(x,\tau)=\partial\bar{\partial}u(x,e^{\tau})
,

(vi)

a Hamiltonian potential of
X
with respect to
w_{\psi}(\tau)
is given by
f_{\psi}(x,\tau):=f(x)+\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\psi(x,\tau)
.

Proof.

(i) and (ii) follow by definition. By recalling the definition of
u
and (
6.1
),

\begin{split}&u(x,e^{\tau})=e^{\tau}\log\frac{w_{\varphi}(e^{\tau})^{n}}{w(e^{\tau})^{n}}(x)+\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\varphi(x,e^{\tau})-\varphi(x,e^{\tau}),\\
&\dot{\psi}(x,\tau)=\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\psi(x)=\log\frac{w_{\psi}(\tau)^{n}}{w^{n}}(x,\tau)+\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\psi(x,\tau)-\psi(x,\tau),\end{split}

we conclude that
e^{\tau}\Phi_{e^{\tau}}^{*}\dot{\psi}(x,\tau)=u(x,e^{\tau})
. Therefore (iv) and (v) follow immediately.

By the definition of
w_{\psi}(\tau)
, a Hamiltonian potential of
X
with respect to
w_{\varphi}(e^{\tau})
is given by
e^{\tau}\Phi_{e^{\tau}}^{*}f+\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\varphi(e^{\tau})
, hence a Hamiltonian potential of
X
with respect to
w_{\psi}(\tau)
is given by
f_{\psi}(x,\tau):=f(x)+\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\psi(x,\tau)
.
∎

Proposition 6.3

(Boundedness of
X\cdot\psi
)
.

There exists a constant
C_{f}>0
independent of time such that
|X\cdot\psi|\leq C_{f}
on
\{f\leq\lambda\}\times(-\infty,\log T)
.

Proof.

For all
\tau\in(-\infty,\log T)
,
we observe that

X=\nabla^{g_{\psi}(\tau)}\left(f+\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\psi(\tau)\right)=\nabla^{g}f,

holds on
M
. Assume that at
x_{0}
(resp.
y_{0}
)
\in\{f\leq\lambda\}
,
f+\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\psi(\tau)
achieves its maximum (resp. minimum) on
\{f\leq\lambda\}
.

If
x_{0}\in\{f<\lambda\}
, which means that
x_{0}
lies in the interior of
\{f\leq\lambda\}
, then we have
0=\nabla^{g_{\psi}(\tau)}(f+\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\psi)(x_{0},\tau)=X(x_{0})
, hence
(f+\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\psi)(x_{0},\tau)=f(x_{0})\leq\lambda.
If
x_{0}\in\{f=\lambda\}
, then by the estimate above (Lemma
6.2
) we have
(f+\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\psi)(x_{0},\tau)\leq\lambda+C_{0}
. Therefore for any
x\in\{f\leq\lambda\}

\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\psi(x,\tau)=\left(f+\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\psi\right)(x,\tau)-f(x)\leq\max_{f\leq\lambda}\left(f+\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\psi(\tau)\right)-\min_{f\leq\lambda}f:=C_{f}.

If
y_{0}\in\{f<\lambda\}
, which means
y_{0}
is in the interior of
\{f\leq\lambda\}
, then we have
0=\nabla^{g_{\psi}(\tau)}(f+\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\psi)(y_{0},\tau)=X(y_{0})

(f+\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\psi)(y_{0},\tau)=f(y_{0})\geq\min_{f\leq\lambda}f.
If

, then by the estimate above (Lemma

) we have
(f+\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\psi)(x_{0},\tau)\geq\inf_{M}f-C_{0}
. Therefore for any

, we have

\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\psi(x,\tau)=\left(f+\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\psi\right)(x,\tau)-f(x)\geq\min_{f\leq\lambda}\left(f+\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\psi(\tau)\right)-\max_{f\leq\lambda}f:=-C_{f}.

Since this constant does not depend on
\tau
, on
\{f\leq\lambda\}\times(-\infty,\log T)
we have

|X\cdot\psi|\leq C_{f},

as expected.
∎

Proposition 6.4

(Lower bound of
\psi+\dot{\psi}
)
.

There exists a constant
C>0
independent of time such that
\dot{\psi}+\psi\geq-C
on
\{f\leq\lambda\}\times(-\infty,\log T)
.

Proof.

For
\tau\in(-\infty,\log T)
, since
g
is an expanding soliton,
\mathcal{L}_{\frac{X}{2}}g-\operatorname{Ric}(g)-g=0
, and by (
6.1
),

\displaystyle\partial\bar{\partial}\dot{\psi}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Ric}(g)-\operatorname{Ric}(g_{\psi}(\tau))+\mathcal{L}_{\frac{X}{2}}(g_{\psi}(\tau)-g)+g-g_{\psi}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\frac{X}{2}}(g_{\psi}(\tau))-\operatorname{Ric}(g_{\psi}(\tau))-g_{\psi}(\tau).

Thus, by tracing the previous identity with respect to metric
w_{\psi}(\tau)
, we get

\Delta_{w_{\psi}(\tau)}\dot{\psi}=\operatorname{tr}_{w_{\psi}(\tau)}(\mathcal{L}_{\frac{X}{2}}w_{\psi}(\tau))-R_{w_{\psi}(\tau)}-n.

Moreover, observe that,

\Delta_{w_{\psi}(\tau)}\psi=\operatorname{tr}_{w_{\psi}(\tau)}(w_{\psi}(\tau)-w)=n-\operatorname{tr}_{w_{\psi}(\tau)}w.

By (
6.1
),

\frac{X}{2}\cdot\dot{\psi}=\operatorname{tr}_{w_{\psi}(\tau)}(\mathcal{L}_{\frac{X}{2}}w_{\psi}(\tau))-\operatorname{tr}_{w}(\mathcal{L}_{\frac{X}{2}}w)+\frac{X}{2}\cdot\left(\frac{X}{2}\cdot\psi\right)-\frac{X}{2}\cdot\psi.

Since
X
is real-holomorphic and
JX\cdot\psi=0
,
\frac{X}{2}\cdot(\frac{X}{2}\cdot\psi)=\frac{1}{2}g_{\psi}(\tau)(X,X)-\frac{1}{2}g(X,X)\geq-|\partial f|^{2}_{g}
, which combined once with soliton equation (
1.3
) and Proposition
6.3
gives:

\displaystyle\Delta_{w_{\psi}(\tau)}(\dot{\psi}+\psi)-\frac{X}{2}(\dot{\psi}+\psi)

\displaystyle\leq|\partial f|^{2}_{g}+\operatorname{tr}_{w}(\mathcal{L}_{\frac{X}{2}}w)-R_{w_{\psi}(\tau)}-\operatorname{tr}_{w_{\psi}(\tau)}w

\displaystyle\leq|\partial f|^{2}_{g}+n+R_{w}-R_{w_{\psi}(\tau)}-n\left(\frac{w^{n}}{w_{\psi}(\tau)^{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle=|\partial f|^{2}_{g}+n+R_{w}-R_{w_{\psi}(\tau)}-ne^{-\frac{1}{n}(\dot{\psi}+\psi-\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\psi)}

\displaystyle=f+n-R_{w_{\psi}(\tau)}-ne^{-\frac{1}{n}(\dot{\psi}+\psi-\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\psi)}.

where the second line is ensured by the arithmetic geometric inequality. The third line comes from (
6.1
), the fourth line comes from Lemma
2.2
. Thanks to the curvature condition (Proposition
6.2
), on
\{f\leq\lambda\}\times(-\infty,\log T)
, we have

\begin{split}\Delta_{w_{\psi}(\tau)}(\dot{\psi}+\psi)-\frac{X}{2}(\dot{\psi}+\psi)&\leq f+n-R_{w_{\psi}(\tau)}-ne^{-\frac{1}{n}(\dot{\psi}+\psi-\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\psi)}\\
&\leq\lambda+n+A-ne^{-\frac{1}{n}(\dot{\psi}+\psi-\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\psi)}.\end{split}

Assume that at
x_{0}
, the function
\dot{\psi}+\psi
achieves its minimum. If
x_{0}\in\{f<\lambda\}
, the minimum principle implies that

0\leq\left(\Delta_{w_{\psi}}(\dot{\psi}+\psi)-\frac{X}{2}(\dot{\psi}+\psi)\right)(x_{0})\leq\lambda+n+A-ne^{-\frac{1}{n}(\dot{\psi}+\psi-\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\psi)}(x_{0}).

This gives
(\dot{\psi}+\psi-\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\psi)(x_{0})\geq-C
for some constant which does not depend on
\tau
or
x_{0}
. Moreover, thanks to Proposition
6.3
, we conclude that
(\dot{\psi}+\psi)\geq-C
for some constant
C>0
independent of time and
x_{0}
.

If
x_{0}\in\{f=\lambda\}
, we use Proposition
6.2
to get such time independent constant
C
.
∎

Thanks to the lower bound of
\psi+\dot{\psi}

\psi

Proposition 6.5

Proposition 6.6
.

There exists a time independent constant

|\psi|+|\dot{\psi}|\leq C

Proof.

Thanks to Corollary

\Delta_{w}f\geq\varepsilon>0,\quad\textrm{on $M$}.

(6.3)

Notice that for

Claim 6.8
.

(6.5)

Claim 6.9

6.9
, by Stokes divergence theorem, we have,

\int_{\{f_{\psi}\leq R\}}\dot{\psi}^{2k}\Delta_{w_{\psi},X}\dot{\psi}e^{f_{\psi}}w_{\psi}^{n}+2k\int_{\{f_{\psi}\leq R\}}|\partial\dot{\psi}|_{w_{\psi}}^{2}\dot{\psi}^{2k-1}e^{f_{\psi}}w_{\psi}^{n}=\int_{\{f_{\psi}=R\}}\dot{\psi}^{2k}\frac{\partial\dot{\psi}}{\partial\nu}e^{f_{\psi}}d\sigma,

and

\lim_{R\to+\infty}\int_{\{f_{\psi}=R\}}\dot{\psi}^{2k}\frac{\partial\dot{\psi}}{\partial\nu}e^{f_{\psi}}d\sigma=0.

Since
2k-1\geq|\dot{\psi}|
holds uniformly in time, we get

\int_{\{f_{\psi}\leq R\}}\dot{\psi}^{2k}\Delta_{w_{\psi},X}\dot{\psi}e^{f_{\psi}}w_{\psi}^{n}\leq\int_{\{f_{\psi}=R\}}\dot{\psi}^{2k}\frac{\partial\dot{\psi}}{\partial\nu}e^{f_{\psi}}d\sigma+2k(2k-1)\int_{\{f_{\psi}\leq R\}}|\partial\dot{\psi}|_{w_{\psi}}^{2}\dot{\psi}^{2k-2}e^{f_{\psi}}w_{\psi}^{n},

Due to Claim
6.9
,
|\partial\dot{\psi}|_{w_{\psi}}^{2}\dot{\psi}^{2k-2}e^{f_{\psi}}
is Lebesgue integrable with respect to
w_{\psi}^{n}
. By letting
R
tend to
+\infty
, we conclude that

\int_{M}\dot{\psi}^{2k}\Delta_{w_{\psi},X}\dot{\psi}e^{f_{\psi}}w_{\psi}^{n}\leq 2k(2k-1)\int_{M}|\partial\dot{\psi}|_{w_{\psi}}^{2}\dot{\psi}^{2k-2}e^{f_{\psi}}w_{\psi}^{n}.

This implies that

\partial_{\tau}A(\tau)\leq-2kA(\tau).

and that
e^{2k\tau}A(\tau)
is non increasing. For all
\rho\leq\tau
, by integration, we have

e^{2k\tau}A(\tau)\leq e^{2k\rho}A(\rho)\leq Be^{2k\rho}.

By letting
\rho
tend to
-\infty
, we get
A(\tau)=0
for any
\tau
, thus
\dot{\psi}
is identically 0.
∎

Once we have
\dot{\psi}=0
, we deduce
\psi=0
.

Claim 6.11
.

The function
\psi
is identically 0. Hence
g_{\varphi}(t)=g(t)
for all
t\in(0,T)
.

Proof of Claim
6.11
.

Since
\dot{\psi}
is identically 0, (
6.1
) reduces to

\log\frac{w_{\psi}^{n}}{w^{n}}+\frac{X}{2}\cdot\psi-\psi=0.

Fix
\tau\in(-\infty,\log T)
, by Lemma
6.2
,
|\psi(\tau)|
tends to 0 at spatial infinity. If
\sup_{M}\psi(\tau)>0
, there exists
x_{0}\in M
such that
\psi(x_{0},\tau)=\sup_{M}\psi(\tau)>0
. Due to the maximum principle, at the point
x_{0}
:

0\geq\log\frac{w_{\psi}(\tau)^{n}}{w^{n}}(x_{0})+\frac{X}{2}\cdot\psi(x_{0})=\psi(x_{0}).

This gives a contradiction. Hence
\sup_{M}\psi(\tau)\leq 0
. For the same reason, we prove that
\inf_{M}\psi(\tau)\geq 0
, we have
\psi(\tau)=0
as desired.
∎

7.
Open questions

This section consists of the open questions that the author finds helpful for further study.

Question 7.1
.

Does the Ricci flow evolve uniquely after a singularity? If not, is
there a selection principle for the best flow?

This is still the question asked in
[
FIK03
]
. This paper did not provide a full answer to this question. To get a full answer to this question, we need to think about the following questions.

Question 7.2
.

Let
g_{1}(t)_{t\in(0,T)}
be a smooth complete solution to Ricci flow, such that
\pi_{*}g_{1}(t)
converges to
g_{0}
locally smoothly when
t
tends to 0, is it true that
g(t)
is Kähler with respect to
J
for all
t\in(0,T)
?

S. Huang and L. Tam
[
HT18
]
have shown that a smooth, complete solution
g(t)
to the Ricci flow on the interval
t\in[0,T]
, with initial metric
g(0)
Kähler, remains Kähler for all
t\in[0,T]
provided the full Riemann curvature operator is bounded by
\frac{C}{t}
. But for a solution to Ricci flow with sigular initial data, the preservation of Kählerity is still an open question.

As a Kähler cone may be desingularied by non-biholomorphic complex manifolds (but diffeomorphic), so Question
7.2
is challenging but delicate.

Question 7.3
.

Let
g_{1}(t)_{t\in(0,T)}
be a smooth complete solution to Kähler-Ricci flow, such that
\pi_{*}g_{1}(t)
converges to
g_{0}
locally smoothly when
t
tends to 0, is it true that
JX
is a Killing vector field for
g(t)
for any
t\in(0,T)
?

In Theorem
1.2
, we assume that
JX
is a Killing vector field with respect to
g_{\varphi}(t_{0})
for some
t_{0}\in(0,T)
. Given that
J_{0}X
is Killing with respect to
g_{0}
, we aim to determine whether
JX
remains Killing for
g_{1}(t)
for all
t\in(0,T)
.

Question 7.4
.

Let
g_{1}(t)_{t\in(0,T)}
be a smooth complete solution to Kähler-Ricci flow, such that
\pi_{*}g_{1}(t)
converges to
g_{0}
locally smoothly when
t
tends to 0. Is there any algebraic obstruction of the exceptional set
E
to ensure that
w_{1}(t)-w(t)
is globally exact on
M
?

In Remark
1.3
, we have noticed that
\pi_{*}(w_{1}(t)-w(t))
is exact on
C_{0}-\{o\}
. But if we want get that
w_{1}(t)-w(t)
is globally exactly on
M
, we will need more information of this exceptional set
E
. We want to know if there is any algebraic obstruction of
E
to ensure that
w_{1}(t)-w(t)
is exact on
M
.

Question 7.5
.

Is the curvature condition sharp in Theorem
1.2
?

This question is about the non-uniqueness problem of Kähler-Ricci flow. Here one want to know whether there exists an asymptotical conical solution to Kähler-Ricci flow different form
g(t)
with unbounded Ricci curvature or scalar curvature.

We also consider these questions in a more general setting, namely, for solutions to the Ricci flow on Riemannian manifolds that are asymptotically conical. A trivial example of a asymptotically conical expanding Ricci soliton is the Gauss soliton
(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{eucl},\frac{1}{2}r\partial_{r})
, the self-similar solution to the Ricci flow is
g(t)=g_{eucl}
.

Another example is the Bryant soliton
[
Bry
]
, a one-parameter family of expanding gradient Ricci solitons
(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{c},\nabla^{g_{c}}f_{c})_{c\in(0,1)}
, which are
SO(n-1)
-invariant and satisfy
\operatorname{Rm}(g_{c})>0
. These self-similar solutions are asymptotic to the cone
(C(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}),\,dr^{2}+(cr)^{2}g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}},\,\tfrac{1}{2}r\partial_{r})
.

In
[
Der17
]
, it is shown that the limit
\lim_{f\to+\infty}(f+\mu(g))^{\frac{n}{2}-1}e^{f+\mu(g)}\operatorname{Ric}(g)
exists for a normalized asymptotically Ricci flat expanding gradient soliton
(M^{n},g,\nabla^{g}f)
. Here
\mu(g)
denotes the entropy. In
[
Sie13
]
, it is proved that
\operatorname{Ric}(g(t))=O(t^{-1}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4t}}(\frac{r^{2}}{4t})^{\kappa+1})
with some
\kappa>0
for a solution to Ricci flow which is asymptotic to Ricci flat cone
(C,g_{C})
with radial function
r
.

Question 7.6
.

What is the optimal rate of convergence of
\operatorname{Ric}(g(t))
for a solution
g(t)_{t\in(0,T)}
to Ricci flow which is asymptotic to a Ricci flat cone? i.e. what is the optimal
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
such that on some
\Omega_{\lambda}
we have

\operatorname{Ric}(g(t))=O\left(t^{-1}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4t}}\left(\frac{r^{2}}{4t}\right)^{\tau}\right),\quad\forall t\in(0,T).

One has the conjecture that the optimal rate should be
-\frac{n}{2}+1
. If we assume that there exists a complete expanding gradient Ricci soliton which desingularies this Ricci flat cone, then with the maximum principle in Section
5
, one can prove that
\operatorname{Ric}(g(t))=O\left(t^{-1}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4t}}\left(\frac{r^{2}}{4t}\right)^{\tau}\right)
for any Ricci flow
(g(t))_{t\in(0,T)}
that smooths out this cone.

In
[
FIK03
]
, besides the expanding Kähler-Ricci soliton, they constructed shrinking and steady Kähler-Ricci soliton whose pointed Gromov-Hausdorff limit is the Kähler cone
\mathbb{C}^{n}/\mathbb{Z}^{k}
with
k\leq n
.

Question 7.7
.

Is there a solution to Ricci flow
g(t)_{t\in(0,T)}
on a smooth manifold
M
such that the pointed Gromov-Hausdorff limit of
(M,g(t))
, when
t
tends to 0, is
(\mathbb{C}^{n}/\mathbb{Z}^{k},\partial\bar{\partial}\left(\frac{|\cdot|^{2p}}{p}\right))
with
k\leq n
and some
p>0
?

In
[
OL24
]
, Ozuch and Lopez proved that there cannot be any ALE expanding soliton on the minimal resolution of
\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma
for
\Gamma\subset SU(2)
. However, this result cannot provide a negative answer to Question
7.6
in complex dimension 2, we notice that the action of
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
is in
U(2)\backslash SU(2)
.

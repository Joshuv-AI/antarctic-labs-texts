Introduction

In this paper, we consider weak solutions of non-homogeneous
{\textbf{p}}(x)
-Laplace equations of the form

(1.1)

-\Delta_{{\textbf{p}}(x)}\,u+|u|^{p_{M}(x)-2}u=f(x,u,Du)\ \ \ \mbox{in}\ \Omega,

where
\Omega
be a bounded domain of

(

) with smooth boundary,
{\textbf{p}}:\overline{\Omega}\to\mbox{{\sl I}}\!\mbox{{\sl R}}^{n}

{\textbf{p}}(x)=(p_{1}(x),p_{2}(x),\ldots,p_{n}(x)),\quad p_{i}\in C(\overline{\Omega}),\,p_{i}(x)>1\text{ in }\overline{\Omega},\,\,i=1,2,\ldots,n,

the variable exponent

is

p_{M}(x):=\max\{p_{1}(x),...,p_{n}(x)\}\quad x\in\Omega,

and

{\textbf{p}}(x)
-Laplace operator defined as

(1.2)

-\Delta_{{\textbf{p}}(x)}\,u:=-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{x_{i}}(|\partial_{x_{i}}u|^{p_{i}(x)-2}\partial_{x_{i}}u).

Recently, the study of partial differential equations with variable exponents has been motivated by the description of models in electrorheological and thermorheological fluids, image processing, or robotics (see for instance
[
6
]
for image restoration and the book
[
7
]
for further applications and references).

Existence and multiplicity of weak solutions to problems involving the
{\textbf{p}}(x)
-Laplace operator have been considered in several works. In
[
3
]
, variational methods have been applied to non-homogeneous problems with a non linearity depending on lower order terms. Also, the critical point theory has been used in
[
19
]
for more general operators of anisotropic type, and in
[
16
]
for eigenvalue problems with both Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions. In these works, the Ambrosetti-Rabinowitz assumption on the right-hand side is the key to stablish the mountain pass geometry of the energy functional associated to the differential equation. More recently, anisotropic problems which can be critical in some region of the domain, have been considered in
[
21
]
, and in
[
1
]
. In this latter case, the right-hand side has low regularity (it belongs for instance to
L^{1}
). We also mention that the concentration compactness principle of Lions to treat critical problems has been extended to the anisotropic case in the reference
[
8
]
. Finally, the relation between different notions of weak solutions and their regularity has been studied recently in
[
20
]
for non-homogeneous equations with the
{\textbf{p}}(x)
-Laplacian operator.

On the other hand, topological degree theory can be one of the most effective tools for demonstrating the existence of solutions in nonlinear equations, even without the Ambrosetti-Rabinowitz condition. As a measure of the algebraic number of solutions of the functional equation
F(x)=h
for a fixed
h
, the degree has special properties such as existence, normalization, additivity, and homotopy invariance. The most powerful is that the value of the degree does not vary under appropriate perturbations (homotopies), which plays an important role in the study of differential and integral equations.

Brouwer
[
4
]
initiated the construction of a topological degree for continuous mappings on a bounded domain of
\mbox{{\sl I}}\!\mbox{{\sl R}}^{n}
. Afterwards, Leray and Schauder
[
22
]
developed the degree theory for compact perturbations of the identity operator in infinite-dimensional Banach spaces. Brouwer
[
5
]
introduced a topological degree for nonlinear operators of monotone type in reflexive Banach spaces, where the Galerkin method is used to apply Brouwer’s degree. Berkovits
[
2
]
provided a new construction of the Browder degree, based on the Leray-Schauder degree. From this perspective, a recent extension of the Leray-Schauder degree for operators of generalized monotone type was studied in
[
2
]
and for possible unbounded operators in
[
17
]
. One application of the Degree Theory to double phase problems with variable exponents can be seen in
[
10
]
for degenerate equations. We refer the reader to the book
[
24
]
for a comprehensive introduction to the matter.

In this paper, we investigate the existence of weak solution for a Dirichlet boundary value problem involving the anisotropic

-Laplacian of the following form

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{{\textbf{p}}(x)}u+|u|^{p_{M}(x)-2}u=f(x,u,Du)\quad\text{in }\Omega\\

Under suitable nonstandard growth conditions for the function

Variable exponent spaces

In this section we introduce basic definitions and preliminary results concerning variable exponent spaces.

In what follows, all the scalar variable exponents
p=p(x):\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
are in the space

L^{\infty}_{+}(\Omega):=\left\{p:\Omega\to[1,\infty):p\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\right\}.

For

, we define

p^{+}:=\sup_{x\in\Omega}p(x)\quad\text{ and }\quad p^{-}:=\inf_{x\in\Omega}p(x).

2.1.
Scalar variable exponent spaces

We define the Lebesgue variable exponent space as

L^{p(x)}(\Omega):=\left\{u:\Omega\to\mbox{{\sl I}}\!\mbox{{\sl R}}:u\ \mbox{is measurable and}\ \displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p(x)}dx<\infty\right\},

equipped with the Luxemburg norm

|u|_{p(x)}:=\inf\bigg\{\lambda>0:\int_{\Omega}\bigg|\dfrac{u(x)}{\lambda}\bigg|^{p(x)}\,dx\leq 1\bigg\}.

It turns out that
L^{p(x)}(\Omega)
is a Banach space, which is also separable. Assuming
p^{-}>1
, the dual space of
L^{p(x)}(\Omega)
is
L^{p^{\prime}(x)}(\Omega)
, where

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p(x)}+\displaystyle\frac{1}{p^{\prime}(x)}=1,\quad\text{for all }x\in\Omega,

and the Lebesgue space

is reflexive. For these and the next results, we refer the reader to
[
7
]
.

Theorem 2.1

\Big|\int_{\Omega}uv\,dx\Big|\leq\left(\frac{1}{p^{-}}+\frac{1}{(p^{\prime})^{-}}\right)|u|_{p(x)}|v|_{p^{\prime}(x)}.

The next proposition states the relation between norms and modulars.

2.2.
Anisotropic variable exponent spaces

Next, we turn our attention to anisotropic Sobolev spaces. Let
{\textbf{p}}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
denote the vector field

{\textbf{p}}(x)=(p_{1}(x),...,p_{n}(x)),\quad x\in\Omega,

where

p_{i}\in L^{\infty}_{+}(\Omega),\quad\text{for all }i.

We define

p_{M}(x):=\sup\left\{p_{1}(x),...,p_{n}(x)\right\}.

Then,
p_{M}\in L^{\infty}_{+}(\Omega).
The anisotropic variable exponent Sobolev space
W^{1,{\textbf{p}}(x)}(\Omega)
is defined as

W^{1,{\textbf{p}}(x)}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in L^{p_{M}(x)}(\Omega):\partial_{x_{i}}u\in L^{p_{i}(x)}(\Omega),\,i=1,...,n\right\},

endowed with the norm

|u|_{{\textbf{p}}(x)}:=|u|_{p_{M}(x)}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\partial_{x_{i}}u|_{p_{i}(x)}.

Observe that the space
W^{1,{\textbf{p}}(x)}(\Omega)
may be defined equivalently by

W^{1,{\textbf{p}}(x)}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega):u\in L^{p_{i}(x)}(\Omega),\,\partial_{x_{i}}u\in L^{p_{i}(x)}(\Omega),\,i=1,...,n\right\}.

Without any further assumption on
p
,
W^{1,{\textbf{p}}(x)}(\Omega)
is a Banach space (
[
11
, Theorem 2.1]
). Moreover, if
p_{i}^{-}>1
, then by
[
11
, Theorem 2.3]
, the space
W^{1,{\textbf{p}}(x)}(\Omega)
is reflexive. Also, to consider zero boundary condition, define
W_{0}^{1,{\textbf{p}}(x)}(\Omega)
as the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with respect to the norm in
W^{1,{\textbf{p}}(x)}(\Omega)
.

Remark 2.4
.

Zero boundary conditions may be considered in the trace sense. Indeed, let
\Omega
be a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary and for
u\in W^{1,{\textbf{p}}(x)}(\Omega)\subset W^{1,1}(\Omega)
, we let
u|_{\partial\Omega}
the boundary trace of

in
W^{1,1}(\Omega)
. Then, we can define

W=\left\{u\in W^{1,{\textbf{p}}(x)}(\Omega):u|_{\partial\Omega}=0\right\}.

In the variable exponent case, we can have
W\neq W_{0}^{1,{\textbf{p}}(x)}(\Omega)
. However, when for all

,
p_{i}
satisfies the log-Hölder continuity, that is, there exists

such that

|p_{i}(x)-p_{i}(y)|\leq C\frac{1}{|\log|x-y||},\quad\text{for all }\;x,y\in\Omega,\;x\neq y,\,|x-y|\leq 1/2,

then by
[
11
, Theorem 2.4]
, the set
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is dense in
W^{1,{\textbf{p}}(x)}(\Omega)
and so
W=W_{0}^{1,{\textbf{p}}(x)}(\Omega)
.

We end the section by quoting the following embedding result from
[
11
, Theorem 2.5]
:

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be any bounded domain,
p_{i},q\in L^{\infty}_{+}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{C}(\overline{\Omega})
for all

where

(2.1)

\overline{p}^{*}(x):=\begin{cases}\dfrac{n\overline{p}(x)}{n-\overline{p}(x)},\,if\,\,\overline{p}(x)<n,\\
\infty,\,if\,\,\overline{p}(x)\geq n\end{cases}

and

(2.2)

\overline{p}(x):=\dfrac{n}{\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{1}{p_{i}(x)}},

then we have that the embedding

W_{0}^{1,{\textbf{p}}(x)}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{q(x)}(\Omega)

is compact.

Therefore, Problem (
1.4

\overline{p}^{*}(x)\leq p_{M}(x).

Observe that this is never the case when

is a scalar function.

Some further anisotropic Sobolev embedding results in the case

for all

[

and the reference therein.

Definition 3.1

Let

(2)

(3)

Definition 3.2

A mapping

is said to be

(1)

of class
(S_{+})

(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\Omega
with
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
and
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\langle Fu_{n},u_{n}-u\rangle\leqslant 0
we have
u_{n}\to u
.

(2)

(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\Omega
with
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
we have
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\langle Fu_{n},u_{n}-u\rangle\geqslant 0
.

Observe that if

Let

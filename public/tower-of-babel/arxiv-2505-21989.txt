Extending Recent Congruence Results on ( ℓ , μ ) (\ell,\mu) -regular overpartitions
Bishnu Paudel, James A. Sellers, Haiyang Wang

Abstract
. Recently, Alanazi, Munagi, and Saikia employed the theory of modular forms to investigate the arithmetic properties of the function R ℓ , μ ¯ ​ ( n ) \overline{R_{\ell,\mu}}(n) , which enumerates the overpartitions of n n where no part is divisible by either ℓ \ell or μ \mu , for various integer pairs ( ℓ , μ ) (\ell,\mu) . In this paper, we substantially extend several of their results and establish infinitely many families of new congruences. Our proofs are entirely elementary, relying solely on classical q q -series manipulations and dissection formulas.



1.
Introduction

A
partition
of a non-negative integer
n
is a sequence of positive integers
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{r}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}=n
. Each integer
\lambda_{i}
in this sequence is referred to as a
part
of the partition.

An
overpartition
of
n
extends the notion of ordinary partitions by allowing the first occurrence of each
part size to be overlined. To illustrate, the fourteen overpartitions of
4
are:

(4),(\overline{4}),(3,1),(\overline{3},1),(3,\overline{1}),(\overline{3},\overline{1}),(2,2),(\overline{2},2),

(2,1,1),(\overline{2},1,1),(2,\overline{1},1),(\overline{2},\overline{1},1),(1,1,1,1),(\overline{1},1,1,1).

The number of overpartitions of
n
is denoted by
\overline{p}(n)
, with
\overline{p}(0):=1
. The study of overpartitions dates back to MacMahon
[
17
]
, and was later revisited by Corteel and Lovejoy
[
7
]
, sparking renewed interest in the subject. To describe the generating function for overpartitions, recall the
q
-Pochhammer symbol

(a;q)_{\infty}:=\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}(1-aq^{i}),

and use the shorthand notation

f_{k}:=(q^{k};q^{k})_{\infty}.

Corteel and Lovejoy
[
7
]
noted that the generating function for
\overline{p}(n)
is given by

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\overline{p}(n)q^{n}=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1+q^{n}}{1-q^{n}}=\frac{f_{2}}{f_{1}^{2}}.

For the arithmetic properties of
\overline{p}(n)
, we refer the reader to
[
6
,
10
,
13
,
18
,
23
,
24
]
.

An overpartition is called
\ell
-regular
if none of its parts is divisible by
\ell
. The number of
\ell
-regular overpartitions of
n
, denoted by
\overline{A}_{\ell}(n)
, has the generating function

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\overline{A}_{\ell}(n)q^{n}=\frac{f_{\ell}^{2}f_{2}}{f_{1}^{2}f_{2\ell}}.

Lovejoy
[
16
]
studied this family of functions in detail,
and the arithmetic properties of
\overline{A}_{\ell}(n)
have been explored by several authors; see
[
3
,
4
,
20
,
22
]
.

A natural extension arises by considering overpartitions that are simultaneously
\ell
-regular and
\mu
-regular for coprime integers
\ell,\mu>1
. Such overpartitions, called
(\ell,\mu)
-regular
, contain no part divisible by
\ell
or
\mu
. The number of
(\ell,\mu)
-regular overpartitions of
n
is denoted by
\overline{R_{\ell,\mu}}(n)
with generating function

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\overline{R_{\ell,\mu}}(n)q^{n}=\frac{f_{2}f_{\ell}^{2}f_{\mu}^{2}f_{2\mu\ell}}{f_{1}^{2}f_{2\ell}f_{2\mu}f_{\mu\ell}^{2}}.

In their work
[
19
]
, Nadji, Ahmia, and Ramírez investigated the arithmetic properties of
\overline{R_{\ell,\mu}}(n)
for the pairs

(\ell,\mu)\in\{(4,3),(4,9),(8,3),(8,9)\},

employing dissection formulas and Smoot’s implementation of Radu’s algorithm. Alanazi, Munagi, and Saikia
[
2
]
established numerous congruences for the following combinations:

(\ell,\mu)\in\{(2,3),(2,5),(3,5),(4,3),(4,9),(8,27),(16,81)\}.

Their approach utilized modular forms and Radu’s algorithm. In the concluding remarks of
[
2
]
, the authors suggested that it is desirable to find elementary proofs for their congruences. Recently, Ghoshal and Jana
[
11
]
provided such proofs for several of these congruences and also introduced additional divisibility relations.

This article contributes further to the topic by extending various congruences from
[
2
]
using elementary
q
-series identities. We also supply elementary proofs for several results from
[
2
]
that were not
addressed in
[
11
]
and
[
19
]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
k,s,t>0
be integers and let
G(q)
be a
q
-series. If
G(q)\equiv q^{s}G(q^{t})\pmod{k}
, then
G(q)\equiv 0\pmod{k}
.

Proof.

We write

G(q)=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}q^{n}.

We use induction to show that
a_{n}\equiv 0\pmod{k}
for all
n
. Since
G(q)\equiv q^{s}G(q^{t})\pmod{k}
, we have

(32)

\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}q^{n}\equiv\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}q^{tn+s}\pmod{k}.

Clearly,
a_{0}\equiv 0\pmod{k}
. Suppose
a_{n}\equiv 0\pmod{k}
for
0\leq n\leq m
. Then, from (
32
), we get

\sum_{n\geq m+1}a_{n}q^{n}\equiv\sum_{n\geq m+1}a_{n}q^{tn+s}\pmod{k},

from which we can conclude that
a_{m+1}\equiv 0\pmod{k}
.
∎

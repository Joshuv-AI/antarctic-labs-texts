On Core Quandles
Filippo Spaggiari, Marco Bonatto

Abstract
We characterize several properties of core quandles in terms of the properties of their underlying groups. Specifically, we characterize connected cores providing an answer to an open question in [ 26 ] and present a standard homogeneous representation for them, which allows us to prove that simple core quandles are primitive.



Introduction

Quandles are binary algebras introduced in the development of the theory of knots as a way to construct knot invariants
[
20
,
24
]
. The origins of quandle theory trace back to self-distributive quasigroups, or what are now referred to as latin quandles, which have been extensively studied in relation to modern theory.

Later on, they have proven to be deeply connected to various other mathematical structures, such as solutions to the set-theoretical quantum Yang-Baxter equation
[
11
,
10
]
and pointed Hopf algebras
[
1
]
. Historical examples also stem from group conjugation, which ultimately led to the development of the knot quandle by Joyce and Matveev. Another line of research was inspired by the abstract properties of reflections on differentiable manifolds, giving rise to what is now known as involutory quandles, that is the class of structures where the identity
x*(x*y)=y
holds
[
22
]
.

Core quandles represent a particular class of involutory quandles constructed from a group
G
, defined by the operation
x*y=xy^{-1}x
for all
x,y\in G
. The study of cores as binary algebraic structures has its roots in the analysis of quasigroups and loops (see
[
3
]
), which served as a foundation for the development of modern core theory.

From the perspective of universal algebra, core quandles can be regarded as reducts of groups, inheriting numerous structural properties from the operation of
G
. Indeed, several properties of the quandle
\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)
can be derived directly from those of the underlying group
G
. The paper was motivated by an open question in
[
26
]
, in which a characterization of connected quandles was asked. Previous investigations have addressed properties such as latinness
[
21
]
, faithfulness
[
28
]
, right self-distributivity
[
2
]
, and mediality (an unpublished result by B. Roszkowska). This paper contributes to this ongoing research on core quandles in purely group-theoretic terms by providing a characterization of other properties, among which connectedness, simplicity, primitivity, abelianness and nilpotency in the sense of
[
14
]
. To ensure comprehensiveness, we also revisit existing results, rephrasing them in the modern framework of quandle theory and presenting independent, complete proofs.

The structure of the paper is as follows. In Section
1
, we introduce the foundational concepts and notations of quandle theory.

Section
2
examines the homogeneous representations of core quandles and the properties of their associated groups of automorphisms. In particular, we use these homogeneous representations to characterize simple and primitive core quandles. In Section
3
, we focus on the displacement group and we establish the characterization of connected core quandles.

In Section
4
, we investigate the above mentioned characterization theorems for several properties of core quandles in purely group theoretical terms.

In Sections
5
, we employ tools from universal algebra to examine the notions of abelianness and nilpotence in core quandles (in the sense of
[
14
]
).

In Section
6
we address the problem of embedding into core quandles, also investigated in
[
4
]
. Using a categorical language we obtained a characterization of quandles that can be embedded into core quandles.
Finally, in Section
7
we focus on knot colorings by core quandles.

The research presented in this work has been facilitated by the computational tools RIG
[
25
]
and GAP
[
16
]
.

Notation

Let us collect the group-theoretical notation we are using in the paper. Let
G
be a group. We denote by
G^{op}
the
opposite
of
G
, that is the group with operation
x\cdot^{op}y=yx
for every
x,y\in G
. The map
\iota:x\mapsto x^{-1}
is an isomorphism between
G
and
G^{op}
. Let
G
be acting on a set
X
. We denote by
G_{x}
the
stabilizer
in
G
of
x\in X
, and by
x^{G}
the orbit of
x
under the action of
G
. We say that (the action of)
G
is
semiregular
if
G_{x}=1
for every
x\in G
and
regular
if is it transitive and semiregular. We will employ the following standard notation for the canonical left and right actions of a group
G
on itself:

\displaystyle\lambda:G\to\operatorname{\mathsf{Sym}}(G),

\displaystyle g\mapsto(\lambda_{g}:x\mapsto gx),

\displaystyle\rho:G^{op}\to\operatorname{\mathsf{Sym}}(G),

\displaystyle g\mapsto(\rho_{g}:x\mapsto xg).

Note that both
\lambda
and
\rho
are regular actions and that
[\lambda_{g},\rho_{h}]=1
for every
g,h\in G
. We also denote by
\widehat{g}
the inner automorphism by
G
, mapping
x
to
\lambda_{g}\rho_{g}^{-1}(x)=gxg^{-1}
for every
x\in G
.
We define the
squaring map
as
s:G\to G
such that
s(x)=x^{2}
for every
x\in G
. The image
s(G)=\{x^{2}\colon x\in G\}
is the
square
of the group
G
.

1
Core quandles

Let
Q=(Q,*)
be a set with a binary operation. We say that
Q
is a
rack
if all left translations

L_{x}:Q\rightarrow Q,\quad y\mapsto x*y

are automorphisms of
Q
. Equivalently, we have that
L_{x}
is bijective and

x*(y*z)=(x*y)*(x*z)

holds for every
x,y,z\in Q
.
We have introduced quandles as algebras with a single binary operation, but since
L_{x}^{-1}L_{x}(y)=y
, it is also common to read
L_{x}^{-1}(y)
as a second binary operation, the left division of the quandle, denoted
x\backslash y
. In the finite case the algebraic structures
(Q,*)
and
(Q,*,\backslash)
are equivalent, so we can study finite racks avoiding the division without loss of generality. If a rack
Q
is idempotent, that is, if
x*x=x
for all
x\in Q
, then
Q
is called
quandle
. In particular we can define the
left multiplication group
of
Q
as

\operatorname{\mathsf{LMlt}}(Q)=\left\langle L_{x}:x\in Q\right\rangle

and the
displacement group
of
Q
as

\operatorname{\mathsf{Dis}}(Q)=\left\langle L_{x}L_{y}^{-1}:x,y\in Q\right\rangle.

It is known that
\operatorname{\mathsf{LMlt}}(Q)
is a normal subgroup of the automorphism group
\operatorname{\mathsf{Aut}}(Q)
.

A rack
Q
is said to be:

(i)

homogeneous
(resp.
connected
) if the natural action of
\operatorname{\mathsf{Aut}}(Q)
(resp.
\operatorname{\mathsf{LMlt}}(Q)
) is transitive on
Q
.

(ii)

projection
if
x*y=y
for all
x,y\in Q
.

(iii)

a
crossed set
if it is a quandle and for all
x,y\in Q
we have
x\ast y=y
if and only if
y\ast x=x
.

Quandles can be constructed using groups and their automorphisms in several ways.

Example 1.1
.

(i)

Let
G
be a group. It is easy to see that
G
endowed with the binary operation

\displaystyle x*y

\displaystyle=xyx^{-1}

is a quandle, called
conjugation quandle
over
G
and denoted
\operatorname{\mathsf{Conj}}(G)
.

(ii)

Let
G
be a group,
f\in\operatorname{\mathsf{Aut}}(G)
and
H\leq\operatorname{\mathsf{Fix}}(f)=\{x\in G\,\colon f(x)=x\}
. The set
G/H
endowed with the operation

xH*yH=xf(x^{-1}y)H

for every
x,y\in G
is a quandle. We denote such quandle as
\mathcal{Q}(G,H,f)
and we refer to it as
coset quandle
. If
G
is abelian and
H=1
, we say that it is
affine
. In this case, we use the notation
\operatorname{\mathsf{Aff}}(G,f)
.

It can be proven that homogeneous quandles are exactly those that can be built using the above coset construction (see, for instance
[
17
]
). Note that the statement of
[
17
, Proposition 3.5]
holds even under slightly weaker assumptions, by using the same proof.

Proposition 1.2

(
[
17
]
, Proposition 3.5)
.

Let
Q
be a quandle and let
x\in Q
. Let
G\leq\operatorname{\mathsf{Aut}}(Q)
be acting transitively on
Q
and such that
\widehat{L_{x}}(G)=G
. Then
Q\cong\mathcal{Q}(G,G_{x},\widehat{L_{x}})
.

The displacement group plays a very important role for quandles, for instance it can be used to build connected quandles with the coset representation (indeed the orbits of the displacement group and the left multiplication group coincide).

Proposition 1.3

(
[
17
]
, Theorem 4.1, and
[
20
]
)
.

Let
Q
be a connected quandle and
x\in Q
. Then
Q\cong\mathcal{Q}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Dis}}(Q),\operatorname{\mathsf{Dis}}(Q)_{x},\widehat{L_{x}})
.

Let
G
be a group. The binary operation

\displaystyle x*y

\displaystyle=xy^{-1}x

endows
G
with a quandle structure called
core
of
G
, and denoted by
\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)
. It is easy to prove that
\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)
is an
involutory
quandle, that is
L_{x}^{2}=1
for all
x\in Q
.

Example 1.4
.

(i)

Let
A
be an abelian group. Then
\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(A)=\operatorname{\mathsf{Aff}}(A,-1)
.

(ii)

It is easy to prove that
\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)
is projection if and only if
G
is elementary
2
-abelian.

Core quandles are
reducts
of groups, so we have the following.

Remark 1.5
.

(i)

Every homomorphism of groups
G\to H
gives rise to a homomorphism of core quandles
\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)\to\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(H)
. The converse does not hold, indeed
\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(\mathbbm{Z}_{3}^{2}\rtimes\mathbbm{Z}_{3})
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(\mathbbm{Z}_{3}^{3})
are isomorphic as quandles (but their undelying groups are not).

(ii)

If
G
is a group and
H
is a normal subgroup of
G
then the canonical map
G\longrightarrow G/H
is a onto quandle homomorphism from
\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)
onto
\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G/H)
.

We start by analyzing the closure properties of the class of core quandles from a universal algebraic point of view.

Proposition 1.6
.

The class of core quandles is closed under direct products.

Proof.

It is easy to check that
\prod_{i\in I}\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G_{i})=\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(\prod_{i\in I}G_{i})
for an arbitrary set of groups
\{G_{i}\,\colon i\in I\}
.
∎

Example 1.7
.

(i)

The class of core quandles is not closed under subalgebras. In fact, the quandle
\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(\mathsf{A}_{4})
contains a copy of a projection quandle of size
3
, which is not the core quandle of
\mathbbm{Z}_{3}
(the only group of size 3).

(ii)

The class of core quandles is not closed under quotients (hence, under homomorphic images). Indeed, the core quandle
Q=\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(\mathsf{A}_{4})
has a congruence
\theta
such that
Q/\theta
is the connected quandle
SmallQuandle(6,1)
of the RIG database.
None of the quandles
\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)
is isomorphic to
Q/\theta
, for
G
running over all the groups of order
6
.

2
Homogeneous representations

These canonical actions of
G
on itself and the action by conjugation of
G
on
G
are actions by automorphisms of
\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)
. Indeed

\displaystyle xy*xz=xyz^{-1}x^{-1}xz=x(yz^{-1}y)=x(y*z),

\displaystyle yx*zx=yxx^{-1}z^{-1}yx=(yz^{-1}y)x=(y*z)x,

for every
x,y,z\in Q
.
Therefore core quandles are homogeneous. In this section we provide a standard homogeneous representation obtained by using the canonical left and right actions of the underlying group.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
Q=\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)
be a quandle, and let
K=\{(x,x^{-1})\,\colon x\in\mathsf{Z}(G)\}
. Then

\varphi:G\times G^{op}\to\operatorname{\mathsf{Aut}}{(Q)}\quad(x,y)\mapsto\lambda_{x}\rho_{y}

is a group homomorphism and
\operatorname{\mathsf{ker}}{\varphi}=K\cong\mathsf{Z}(G)
. Consequently,
(G\times G^{op})/K\cong\lambda(G)\rho(G)
.

Proof.

Let
x,y,z,t\in G
. Then

\varphi(x,y)\varphi(z,t)=\lambda_{x}\rho_{y}\lambda_{z}\rho_{t}=\lambda_{x}\lambda_{z}\rho_{y}\rho_{t}=\lambda_{xz}\rho_{ty}=\varphi(xz,ty)=\varphi((x,y)(z,t)).

If
(x,y)\in\operatorname{\mathsf{ker}}{\varphi}
then
\lambda_{x}\rho_{y}(z)=xzy=z
for every
z\in G
. If
z=1
we have that
y=x^{-1}
. Then
xzx^{-1}=z
for every
z\in G
, thus
x\in\mathsf{Z}(G)
.
∎

We can actually provide two different homogeneous representations that will be used later on. Let
G
be a group,
t\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\theta\in\operatorname{\mathsf{Aut}}(G)
. We denote by
\theta_{t}
the map

G^{t}\to G^{t},\quad(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})\mapsto(\theta(x_{t}),x_{1},\ldots,x_{t-1})

for every
x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}\in G
. Note that
\theta_{t}
is an automorphism of
G^{t}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
Q=\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)
. Then:

(i)

Q\cong\mathcal{Q}(G\times G^{op},H,\hat{\iota})
, where
H=\{(x,x^{-1})\,\colon x\in G\}
.

(ii)

Q\cong\mathcal{Q}(G^{2},D,1_{2})
, where
D=\{(x,x)\,\colon x\in G\}
and
1_{2}(x,y)=(y,x)
for every
x,y\in G
.

Proof.

(i) Identify the group
G\times G^{op}
with
\rho(G)\lambda(G)
. The group
G\times G^{op}
is transitive on
Q
and it is
\hat{\iota}
-invariant, since for all
x,y\in G
we have
\iota\lambda_{x}\rho_{y}\iota=\rho_{x^{-1}}\lambda_{y^{-1}}=\lambda_{y^{-1}}\rho_{x^{-1}}
. Thus, according to Proposition
1.2
we have that
Q\cong\mathcal{Q}(G\times G^{op},H,\hat{\iota})
, where
H
is the stabilizer of
1
. In particular we have that
(x,y)\in(G\times G^{op})_{1}
if and only if
xy=1
, that is
H=\{(x,x^{-1})\,\colon x\in G\}
.

(ii) The map
(x,y)\mapsto(x,y^{-1})
from
G\times G^{op}
to
s(G)
provides and isomorphism of quandles between
\mathcal{Q}(G\times G^{op},H,\iota)
and
\mathcal{Q}(G^{2},D,1_{2})
.
∎

The first consequence of the homogeneous representation we obtained above is the following characterization of core quandles among involutory quandles in general.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
Q
be an involutory quandle and
x_{0}\in Q
. The following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

Q\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)
.

(ii)

There exist regular actions
\lambda\colon G\to\operatorname{\mathsf{Aut}}(Q)
and
\rho\colon G^{op}\to\operatorname{\mathsf{Aut}}(Q)
such that
[\lambda_{g},\rho_{h}]=1
,
L_{x_{0}}\lambda_{g}L_{x_{0}}=\rho_{g}^{-1}
and
\lambda_{g}(x_{0})=\rho_{h}(x_{0})
implies
h=g
for all
g,h\in G
.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) If
Q\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)
, it is enough to take the canonical left and right actions and
x_{0}=1
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) Conversely, define
Q^{\prime}=\mathcal{Q}(\lambda(G)\times\rho(G^{op}),H,f)
where
H=\{(\lambda_{g},\rho_{g}^{-1})\,\colon g\in G\}
and
f(\lambda_{g},\rho_{h})=L_{x_{0}}(\lambda_{g},\rho_{h})L_{x_{0}}=(\lambda_{h}^{-1},\rho_{g}^{-1})
for every
g,h\in G
. The map
(\lambda_{g},\rho_{h})H\mapsto\lambda_{g}\rho_{h}(x_{0})
is an isomorphism of quandle. On the other hand the map, let
K=\{(g,g^{-1})\,\colon g\in G\}\leq G\times G^{op}
and define the map
(\lambda_{g},\rho_{h})H\mapsto(g,h)K\in(G\times G^{op})/K
. Such map provides and isomorphism between
Q^{\prime}
and
Q^{\prime\prime}=\mathcal{Q}(G\times G^{op},K,f)
where
f(g,h)=(h^{-1},g^{-1})
for every
g,h\in G
. According to Proposition
2.2

Q^{\prime\prime}
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)
.
∎

We can also use the homogeneous representation obtained in Proposition
2.2
to characterize simple and primitive core quandles. Recall that a quandle is
simple
if the congruence lattice of
Q
has only two elements, and it is
primitive
if the natural action of
\operatorname{\mathsf{LMlt}}(Q)
on
Q
is primitive.

In
[
6
]
we investigated primitive and simple quandles and we denoted the quandle
\mathcal{Q}(G^{t},Fix(\theta_{t}),\theta_{t})
just by
(G,t,\theta)
. For our purposes note that the quandle
(G,2,1)
is just
\mathcal{Q}(G^{2},D,1_{2})
where
D=\{(x,x)\,\colon x\in G\}
as defined in Proposition
2.2
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
Q=\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)
. The following are equivalent.

(i)

Q
is primitive.

(ii)

Q
is simple.

(iii)

G
is simple.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) This implication is true in general.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii) Every normal subgroup of
G
would give rise to a quandle congruence of
Q
(see Remark
1.5
), so if
\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)
is simple, then also
G
must be simple.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(i) Let
G
be simple. Then
\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)\cong\mathcal{Q}(G^{2},D,1_{2})
where
D=\{(x,x)\,\colon x\in G\}
(see Proposition
2.2
). Using the notation introduced in
[
6
]
then
\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)\cong(G,2,1)
, so according to
[
6
, Theorem 4.10]

Q
is primitive.
∎

3
The displacement group of core quandles

Let
Q=\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)
. Let us first focus on the action of
\operatorname{\mathsf{LMlt}}(Q)
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{Dis}}(Q)
. Note that
L_{1}(x)=\iota(x)=x^{-1}
and that
L_{x}L_{1}(y)=xyx
for every
x,y\in G
. We denote the map
L_{x}L_{1}
by
\delta_{x}
for every
x\in G
.

It is known that the displacement group of a quandle
Q
is generated by
\{L_{x}L_{x_{0}}^{-1}\,\colon x\in Q\}
, therefore we can write
\operatorname{\mathsf{LMlt}}(Q)=\operatorname{\mathsf{Dis}}(Q)\langle L_{x_{0}}\rangle
for every
x_{0}\in Q

[
7
, Lemma 1.4]
. With this property, if
Q=\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(Q)
we have that

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{Dis}}(Q)

\displaystyle=\langle\delta_{x}\,\colon x\in Q\rangle,

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{LMlt}}(Q)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\iota,\delta_{z}\,\colon\,z\in G\right\rangle.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
Q=\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)
. The following are equivalent

(i)

\delta_{x_{1}}\cdots\delta_{x_{t}}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{Dis}}(Q)_{1}
.

(ii)

x_{1}\cdots x_{t}=(x_{t}\cdots x_{1})^{-1}
.

In particular,
\operatorname{\mathsf{Dis}}(Q)_{1}=\operatorname{\mathsf{Dis}}(Q)\cap\operatorname{\mathsf{LMlt}}(G)
.

Proof.

Note that
\delta_{x_{1}}\cdots\delta_{x_{t}}(1)=x_{1}\cdots x_{t}x_{t}\cdots x_{1}=1
if and only if
x_{t}\cdots x_{1}=(x_{1}\cdots x_{1})^{-1}
. Thus (i) and (ii) are equivalent.

So, if
\delta_{x_{1}}\cdots\delta_{x_{t}}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{Dis}}(Q)_{1}
then
\delta_{x_{1}}\cdots\delta_{x_{t}}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{LMlt}}(G)
. On the other hand,
\operatorname{\mathsf{LMlt}}(G)_{1}\leq\operatorname{\mathsf{Aut}}{Q}_{1}
. Therefore
\operatorname{\mathsf{Dis}}(Q)_{1}=\operatorname{\mathsf{Dis}}(Q)\cap\operatorname{\mathsf{LMlt}}(G)
.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
Q=\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)
be a non-projection quandle. Then
\operatorname{\mathsf{LMlt}}(Q)\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{Dis}}(Q)\rtimes\mathbbm{Z}_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
I=\langle\iota\rangle\cong\mathbbm{Z}_{2}
. We know that
\operatorname{\mathsf{Dis}}(Q)
is normal in
\operatorname{\mathsf{LMlt}}(Q)
and that
\operatorname{\mathsf{Dis}}(Q)I=\operatorname{\mathsf{LMlt}}(Q)
. Assume that
I\cap\operatorname{\mathsf{Dis}}(Q)\neq 1
, so we must necessarily have that
I\subseteq\operatorname{\mathsf{Dis}}(Q)
. In particular
\iota\in\operatorname{\mathsf{Dis}}(Q)_{1}
, and so according to Lemma
3.1
, the map
\iota:x\mapsto x^{-1}
is an inner automorphism of
G
. Therefore
G
is abelian. Hence,
\operatorname{\mathsf{LMlt}}(G)=1
and so
\iota(x)=x^{-1}=x
for every
x\in G
. Therefore
G
is a
2
-elementary abelian group and
Q
is projection.

Thus, if
Q
is not projection then
\operatorname{\mathsf{Dis}}(Q)\cap I=1
and so
\operatorname{\mathsf{LMlt}}(Q)=\operatorname{\mathsf{Dis}}(Q)\rtimes I
.
∎

Let us now investigate the relationship between the group
G
and the displacement group of the corresponding core quandle.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
Q=\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)
. The following are equivalent

(i)

\delta_{x_{1}}\cdots\delta_{x_{t}}=1
.

(ii)

x_{1}\cdots x_{t}=(x_{t}\cdots x_{1})^{-1}\in\mathsf{Z}(G)
.

Proof.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) If
x_{1}\cdots x_{t}=(x_{t}\cdots x_{1})^{-1}\in\mathsf{Z}(G)
then

\displaystyle\delta_{x_{1}}\cdots\delta_{x_{t}}(a)=x_{1}\cdots x_{t}ax_{t}\cdots x_{1}=x_{1}\cdots x_{t}a(x_{1}\cdots x_{t})^{-1}=a

for every
a\in G
. Thus,
\delta_{x_{1}}\cdots\delta_{x_{t}}=1
.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) Assume that
\delta_{x_{1}}\cdots\delta_{x_{t}}=1
. Then

\displaystyle\delta_{x_{1}}\cdots\delta_{x_{t}}=x_{1}\cdots x_{t}x_{t}\cdots x_{1}=1

and so
x_{1}\cdots x_{t}=(x_{t}\cdots x_{1})^{-1}
. Then

\displaystyle\delta_{x_{1}}\cdots\delta_{x_{t}}(a)=x_{1}\cdots x_{t}ax_{t}\cdots x_{1}=x_{1}\cdots x_{t}a(x_{1}\cdots x_{t})^{-1}=a

for every
a\in G
. Therefore
x_{1}\cdots x_{t}\in\mathsf{Z}(G)
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
Q=\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)
be a quandle, and let
K=\{(x,x^{-1})\,\colon x\in\mathsf{Z}(G)\}
. Then

\psi:\operatorname{\mathsf{Dis}}(Q)\to\left(G\times G^{op}\right)/K,\quad\delta_{x_{1}}\cdots\delta_{x_{t}}\mapsto(x_{1}\cdots x_{t},x_{t}\cdots x_{1})K

is an injective group homomorphism.

Proof.

The map
\psi
is a group homomorphism, indeed, on the generators of
\operatorname{\mathsf{Dis}}(Q)
we have

\displaystyle\psi(\delta_{x_{1}}\delta_{x_{2}})=(x_{1}x_{2},x_{2}x_{1})K=(x_{1},x_{1})K(x_{2},x_{2})K=\psi(\delta_{x_{1}})\psi(\delta_{x_{2}}).

Let
g,h\in\operatorname{\mathsf{Dis}}(Q)
. According to Lemma
3.3
,
\psi(g)=\psi(h)
if and only if
g=h
. Then
\psi
is a well defined injective group homomorphism.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
Q=\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)
. Then
G/\mathsf{Z}(G)
is a quotient of
\operatorname{\mathsf{Dis}}(Q)
.

Proof.

The map

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathsf{Dis}}(Q)\to G\times G^{op}/K\to\left(G/\mathsf{Z}(G)\right)^{2}\to G/\mathsf{Z}(G)

mapping

Proposition 3.6

4
Group theoretical characterizations of core quandle properties

We are now prepared to present the characterization theorems concerning the properties of cores in terms of those of the underlying groups. We start by restating a result first seen in
[
28
]
. The
Cayley kernel
of a quandle
Q
is the congruence relation
\lambda_{Q}
defined by setting
x\,\lambda_{Q}\,y
if and only if
L_{x}=L_{y}
. Recall that a quandle
Q
is
faithful
if and only if
\lambda_{Q}
is the identity relation.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
G
be a group,
Q=\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)
and
H=\{x\in\mathsf{Z}(G)\,\colon x^{2}=1\}
. Then:

(i)

L_{x}=L_{y}
if and only if
x^{-1}y\in H
.

(ii)

Q/\lambda_{Q}=\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G/H)
.

(iii)

Q
is faithful if and only if
\mathsf{Z}(G)
contains no involutions.

Proof.

(i) Let
y=xa
for
a\in H
then

L_{y}(z)=xaz^{-1}xa=xz^{-1}xa^{2}=xz^{-1}x=L_{x}(z).

Conversely, if
L_{x}=L_{y}
then
L_{x}(1)=x^{2}=L_{y}(1)=y^{2}
, that is
x^{-1}y=xy^{-1}
. Since for all
z\in G

L_{x}(z)=xz^{-1}x=yz^{-1}y=L_{y}(z)
. Using the equality above we have

z^{-1}=y^{-1}xz^{-1}xy^{-1}=y^{-1}xz^{-1}x^{-1}y=(y^{-1}x)z^{-1}(y^{-1}x)^{-1},

so
y^{-1}x\in\mathsf{Z}(G)
. Since
y^{-1}x\in\mathsf{Z}(G)
, we have

(y^{-1}x)^{2}=y^{-1}xy^{-1}x=y^{-1}y^{-1}xx=1.

(ii) Using the statement above, it is enough to observe that

x\,\lambda_{Q}\,y\iff x^{-1}y\in H.

Thus, the kernel of the canonical map
\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)\to\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G/H)
is
\lambda_{Q}
.

(iii) Note that
\lambda_{Q}
is the identity relation if and only if
H=1
, that is there are no involutions in
\mathsf{Z}(G)
.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group. Then
\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)
is faithful if and only if
|\mathsf{Z}(G)|
is odd.

We proceed with a characterization of superfaithful core quandles. Recall that a quandle
Q
is
superfaithful
if every subquandle is faithful, or equivalently, if
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fix}}(L_{x})=\{x\}
for every
x\in Q

[
8
, Lemma 1.9]
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
Q=\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)
and
x,y\in Q
. Then

(i)

x*y=y
if and only if
|x^{-1}y|=2
.

(ii)

Q
is a crossed set.

(ii)

Q
is superfaithful if and only if
G
has no involutions.

Proof.

(i) Note that
x*y=xy^{-1}x=y
if and only if
x^{-1}y=y^{-1}x=(x^{-1}y)^{-1}
, i.e.
|x^{-1}y|=2
.

(ii) Note that
|y^{-1}x|=|(y^{-1}x)^{-1}|=|x^{-1}y|
. Thus
x*y=y
if and only if
y*x=x
. Thus,
\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)
is a crossed set.

(iii) Let
x,y\in Q
be such that
y\in Fix(L_{x})
, i.e.
x\ast y=y
. Thus, according to (i)
|x^{-1}y|=2
. So,
Q
is superfaithful if and only if
G
has no involutions.
∎

A quandle
Q
is
latin
if also all right translations (defined by
R_{x}:y\mapsto y*x
for every
x,y\in Q
) are bijective maps. Recall that a group
G
is said to be
uniquely 2-divisible
if the squaring map

is bijective.

Proposition 4.4

(
[
21
]
)
.

\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)
is latin if and only if
G
is uniquely 2-divisible.

Proof.

The quandle
\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)
is homogeneous, so
Q
is latin if and only if
R_{1}
is bijective.
Note that for a core quandle
Q=\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)
, we have
R_{1}(x)=x*1=x^{2}=s(x)
. So,
Q
is latin if and only if it is uniquely 2-divisible.
∎

A finite involutory quandle

is latin if and only if
Q
is superfaithful
[
8
]
. So we have the following.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
Q=\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)
be finite. The following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

Q
is superfaithful;

(ii)

Q
is latin;

(iii)

|G|
is odd.

Note that the equivalence of Corollary

does not hold for infinite core quandles. Consider
\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(\mathbb{Z})
as a counterexample.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
G
be a finite nilpotent group and
Q=\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)
. Then

is connected if and only if
Q
is latin.

Proof.

Latin quandles are connected. Assume that

is connected and
K
be the
2
-Sylow of

. The group

is the direct product of its Sylow subgroups. So we have that the canonical projection onto

is a quandle morphism from
\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(K)
. Let
\Phi(K)
be the Frattini subgroup of

. The quandle
\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(K/\Phi(K))
is a factor of
\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(K)
. Since

is a
2
-elementary abelian group the
\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(K/\Phi(K))
is projection. Thus
\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(K/\Phi(K))
is trivial and accordingly

is trivial (the Frattini subgroup of a nilpotent group is proper). Hence

is odd and according to Corollary
4.5

is latin.
∎

A quandle

is

if

(x*y)*z=(x*z)*(y*z)

for every

, or equivalently, if right translation maps are quandle endomorphisms. Right-distributivity for core quandles can be characterized as follows.

Theorem 4.7

(
[

]
)
.

Let

. The following are equivalent

(i)

is right-distributive.

(ii)

is a

-Engel group, that is

for every

(iii)

Proof.

(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii) The quandle

is homogeneous, so it is enough to prove

for all

\displaystyle(x*y)*1

\displaystyle=xy^{-1}xxy^{-1}x

\displaystyle(x*1)*(y*1)

\displaystyle=x^{2}y^{-2}x^{2}

Replacing

We conclude this section by reproving a known theorem about mediality, specifically, an unpublished result by Roszkowska that characterize medial cores. Recall that a quandle

(x*y)*(z*t)=(x*z)*(y*t)

for every

. It is known that a quandle is medial if and only if its displacement group is abelian

Theorem 4.8

(B. Roszkowska)
.

\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)
is medial if and only if

is 2-nilpotent.

Proof.

If

is medial then
\operatorname{\mathsf{Dis}}(Q)
is abelian and so

is abelian, according to Corollary

. On the other hand, if

is

step nilpotent then
[x,y]\in\mathsf{Z}(G)
and so

\displaystyle[\delta_{x},\delta_{y}](z)

\displaystyle=x^{-1}y^{-1}xyzyxy^{-1}x^{-1}

\displaystyle=[x,y]zyxy^{-1}x^{-1}

\displaystyle=zyx[x,y]y^{-1}x^{-1}

\displaystyle=zyxx^{-1}y^{-1}xyy^{-1}x^{-1}=z

for every

. Thus
\operatorname{\mathsf{Dis}}(Q)
is abelian and then

is medial.
∎

Medial quandles are right distributive. The converse does not hold even in the class of core quandles.

Commutator theory

In
[

we adapted commutator theory in the sense of

Corollary 5.1
.

Let

is also solvable (resp. nilpotent).

On the other hand

embeds into a factor of

with respect to a central subgroup. So, if

is solvable (resp. nilpotent) then also
\operatorname{\mathsf{Dis}}(Q)
is solvable (resp. nilpotent).
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let

be a group and let

be connected. The following are equivalent

(i)

\operatorname{\mathsf{Dis}}(Q)
is semiregular.

(ii)

.

The quandle

\operatorname{\mathsf{Dis}}(Q)_{1}
(the stabilizers are conjugate). According to Lemma

,

(i)
\implies
(ii) Assume that

(ii)
\implies

Proposition 5.3
.

Let

6
The Functor Core

The assignment
G\mapsto\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)
gives rise to a functor from the category of groups to the category of involutory quandles. It is easy to construct the right adjoint of such functor. Let
Q
be a quandle, we define the
\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}
-Adjoint group
of

by generators and relations as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(Q)=\langle e_{x},\,x\in Q\,\colon\,e_{x*y}=e_{x}e_{y}^{-1}e_{x},\,x,y\in Q\rangle.

(1)

It is clear that the map

\displaystyle i:Q\to\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(\mathcal{H}(Q)),\quad x\mapsto e_{x}

(2)

is a quandle homomorphism.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
Q
be an involutory quandle and

be a group. If
f:Q\to\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(G)
is a quandle homomorphism then there exists a group homomorphism
\widetilde{f}:\mathcal{H}(Q)\to G
such that
f=\widetilde{f}\circ i
.

Proof.

Assume that

is a quandle homomorphism. Then we have that

. So, by definition of
\mathcal{H}(Q)
(see (
1
)) there exists a group homomorphism
\widetilde{f}
mapping
e_{x}
to
f(x)
. Hence
\widetilde{f}\circ i=f
.
∎

Let
f:Q_{1}\to Q_{2}
a morphism of quandles, then
i_{2}\circ f
is a quandle morphisms into
\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(\mathcal{H}(Q_{2}))=
. Thus, according to Proposition
6.1
tha map

H(f)=\widetilde{i_{2}\circ f}:\mathcal{H}(Q_{1})\to\mathcal{H}(Q_{1}),\quad e_{x}\to e_{f(x)}

is a well defined group morphism. So
H(-)
defines a functor from the category of quandles to the category of groups.

Moreover Proposition
6.1
shows that the functor
\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(-)
is right adjoint to
\mathcal{H}(-)
. Therefore, the pair of functors
(\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(-),\mathcal{H}(-))
provides an adjunction between the category of groups and the category of involutory quandles (see
[

for further details).

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
Q
be an involutory quandle. Then:

(i)

\operatorname{\mathsf{ker}}{i}\leq\lambda_{Q}
.

(ii)

The assignment

\mathcal{H}(Q)\to\operatorname{\mathsf{LMlt}}(Q),\quad e_{x}\to L_{x}

can be extended to a well-defined surjective group homomorphism.

Proof.

The map:
f:x\mapsto L_{x}
from

\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{LMlt}}(Q))
is a quandle morphism and the kernel is

, i.e
\operatorname{\mathsf{ker}}{i}\leq\lambda_{Q}
.
∎

7
Coloring by core quandles

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a knot and
Q
a quandle. A
quandle coloring
of
\mathcal{K}
by
Q
is an assignment of elements of
Q
to the arcs of
\mathcal{K}
such that the
crossing relation
as shown in Figure
1
holds in
Q
(see
[
12
]
and
[
13
]
for a complete presentation of the theory of knots and colorings).

Figure 1
:
Crossing relation.

These crossing relations ensure that the number of colorings by a given quandle is invariant under the Reidemeister moves, making it a topological invariant of the knot. A
trivial coloring
of a knot diagram using a quandle
Q
is a quandle coloring in which the same element of
Q
is assigned to all arcs of the diagram. We say that a knot is
colorable
by a quandle
Q
if the knot admits a non-trivial coloring by
Q
.

Given a diagram of a knot
\mathcal{K}
, its knot quandle
\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{K})
is the free quandle on the arc-set of
\mathcal{K}
modulo the equivalence relations generated by the crossing relations. A coloring
c
of a knot
\mathcal{K}
using a quandle
Q
can be seen as a quandle homomorphism
c:\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{K})\to Q
.

Let
Q
be a quandle. Following
[
5
, Section 2.2]
we define

\displaystyle L_{0}(Q),\qquad L_{n+1}(Q)=L_{n}(Q)/\lambda_{L_{n}(Q)},\quad\text{ for every }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
Q
be a finite connected quandle. Then there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
L_{n}(Q)
is faithful and
|L_{n}(Q)|>1
.

Proof.

Assume that
m
is the smallest integer such that
|L_{m}(Q)|=1
. Then
L_{m-1}(Q)
is connected and projection factor of
Q
, so
|L_{m-1}(Q)|=1
, contradiction. Therefore
|L_{n}(Q)|>1
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
|L_{k+1}(Q)|\leq|L_{k}(Q)|
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
|L_{n}(Q)|=|L_{n+1}(Q)|
, that is
L_{n}(Q)
is faithful.
∎

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a knot and
Q
be a quandle. If
\mathcal{K}
is colorable by
Q
then
\mathcal{K}
is colorable by
L_{n}(Q)
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

It is enough to prove the statement for
n=1
(because
L_{n+1}(Q)=L_{1}(L_{n}(Q))
).

Let
Q_{\mathcal{K}}
be the quandle knot of
\mathcal{K}
. Assume that
c:Q_{\mathcal{K}}\to Q
is a coloring. The map

c^{\prime}:Q_{\mathcal{K}}\to Q\to Q/\lambda_{Q},\quad x\mapsto[c(x)]_{\lambda_{Q}}

is a quandle coloring. Assume that the image is trivial, that is
x\,\lambda_{Q}\,y
for every
x,y\in\operatorname{\mathsf{Im}}(c)
. Then
\operatorname{\mathsf{Im}}(c)
is a projection and connected quandle. Hence
|\!\operatorname{\mathsf{Im}}(c)|=1
, contradiction.
∎

Corollary 7.3
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a knot. If
\mathcal{K}
can be colored by a finite quandle, then
\mathcal{K}
can be colored by a faithful quandle.

Proof.

Put together Lemma
7.1
and Lemma
7.2
.
∎

Corollary 7.4
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a knot. If
\mathcal{K}
can be colored by a finite involutory quandle, then
\mathcal{K}
can be colored by a core quandle.

Proof.

Let
Q
be an involutory quandle and
c:Q_{\mathcal{K}}\to Q
be a coloring. Then we have a non-trivial coloring by a faithful quandle
Q^{\prime}
that embeds into
\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{LMlt}}(Q^{\prime}))
. Hence we have a coloring of
\mathcal{K}
by
\operatorname{\mathsf{Core}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{LMlt}}(Q^{\prime}))
.
∎

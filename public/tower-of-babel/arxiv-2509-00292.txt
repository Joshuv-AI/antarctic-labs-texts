Boundary Value Problems for the Magnetic Laplacian in Semiclassical Analysis
Zhongwei Shen

Abstract
This paper is concerned with the magnetic Laplacian P h ​ ( 𝐀 ) = ( h ​ D + 𝐀 ) 2 P^{h}(\mathbf{A})=(hD+\mathbf{A})^{2} in semiclassical analysis, where h h is a semiclassical parameter. We study the L 2 L^{2} Neumann and Dirichlet problems for the equation P h ​ ( 𝐀 ) ​ u = 0 P^{h}(\mathbf{A})u=0 in a bounded Lipschitz domain Ω \Omega . Under the assumption that the magnetic field ∇ × 𝐀 \nabla\times\mathbf{A} is of finite type on Ω ¯ \overline{\Omega} , we establish the nontangential maximal function estimates for ( h ​ D + 𝐀 ) ​ u (hD+\mathbf{A})u , which are uniform for 0 < h < h 0 0<h<h_{0} . This extends a well-known result due to D. Jerison and C. Kenig for the Laplacian in Lipschitz domains to the magnetic Laplacian in the semiclassical setting. Our results are new even for smooth domains. Keywords . Schrödinger Operator; Magnetic Field; Semiclassical Analysis; Boundary Value Problem; Lipschitz Domain. Introduction

Let
D=-i\nabla
and
\mathbf{A}=(A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{d})\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Consider the Schrödinger operator with a magnetic potential
in semiclassical analysis,

P^{h}(\mathbf{A})=(hD+\mathbf{A})^{2},

(1.1)

also called the magnetic Laplacian,
where
h\in(0,1)
is a semiclassical parameter. In this paper, we are interested in boundary regularities of
P^{h}(\mathbf{A})
that are
uniform in
h
.
More precisely, consider the Neumann problem in a bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega
,

\left\{\begin{aligned} P^{h}(\mathbf{A})u&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
n\cdot(hD+\mathbf{A})u&=g&\quad&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

where
n=(n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{d})
denotes the outward unit normal to
\partial\Omega
, and the Dirichlet problem,

\left\{\begin{aligned} P^{h}(\mathbf{A})u&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u&=f&\quad&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.3)

Let
\mathbf{B}=\nabla\times\mathbf{A}
denote the magnetic field. In the main results of this paper,
we shall assume that
\mathbf{B}
is of finite type on
\overline{\Omega}
; i.e.,
there exist an integer
\kappa\geq 0
and
c_{0}>0
such that

\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\kappa}|\partial^{\alpha}\mathbf{B}(x)|\ \geq c_{0}

(1.4)

for any
x\in\overline{\Omega}
.
Recall that the nontangential maximal function is defined by

(u)^{*}(x)=\sup\big\{|u(y)|:\,y\in\Omega\text{ and }\ |y-x|<M_{0}\,\text{\rm dist}(y,\partial\Omega)\big\}

(1.5)

for
x\in\partial\Omega
,
where
M_{0}>1
is a large (fixed) constant depending on
\Omega
.
The following two theorems are the main results of this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
.
Let
\mathbf{A}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Suppose
\mathbf{B}
is of finite type on
\overline{\Omega}
.
Then for any
g\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega,\mathbb{C})
, a solution
u\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})
to the Neumann problem (
1.2
)
satisfies the nontangential maximal function estimate,

\|(v_{h})^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

(1.6)

for
h\in(0,h_{0})
, where
v_{h}(x)=|(hD+\mathbf{A})u(x)|+h\,m(x,h^{-1}\mathbf{B})|u(x)|
.
Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}m(x,h^{-1}\mathbf{B})|v_{h}(x)|^{2}dx\leq C\int_{\partial\Omega}|g|^{2}.

(1.7)

The constants
C
in (
1.6
)-(
1.7
) and
h_{0}
depend only
on
\Omega
,
(\kappa,c_{0})
in (
1.4
), and
\|\mathbf{B}\|_{C^{\kappa+1}(\overline{\Omega})}
.

Let
T^{h}=(T^{h}_{jk})
, where
1\leq j,k\leq d
and

T^{h}_{jk}=n_{j}(hD_{k}+A_{k})-n_{k}(hD_{j}+A_{j})

is a (tangential) differential operator on
\partial\Omega
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that
\Omega
and
\mathbf{B}
satisfy the same conditions as in Theorem
1.1
.
Then for any
f\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{C})
, a solution
u\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})

to the Dirichlet problem (
1.3
) satisfies the estimate,

\displaystyle\|(v_{h})^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\Big\{\|T^{h}f\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+h\|m(x,h^{-1}\mathbf{B})f\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\Big\}

(1.8)

for
h\in(0,h_{0})
, where
v_{h}(x)=|(hD+\mathbf{A})u(x)|+h\,m(x,h^{-1}\mathbf{B})|u(x)|
.
Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}m(x,h^{-1}\mathbf{B})|v_{h}(x)|^{2}dx\leq C\Big\{\|T^{h}f\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{2}+h^{2}\|m(x,h^{-1}\mathbf{B})f\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{2}\Big\}.

(1.9)

The constants
C
in (
1.8
)-(
1.9
) and
h_{0}

depend only on
\Omega
,
(\kappa,c_{0})
in (
1.4
), and
\|\mathbf{B}\|_{C^{\kappa+1}(\overline{\Omega})}
.

A few remarks are in order.

Remark 1.3
.

In the case
\mathbf{A}\equiv 0
in
\Omega
and
h=1
, the Neumann and Dirichlet problems (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) reduce to

\Delta u=0\quad\text{ in }\Omega\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}=g\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

(1.10)

and

\Delta u=0\quad\text{ in }\Omega\quad\text{ and }\quad u=f\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

(1.11)

respectively.
In this case, it was proved by D. Jerison and C. Kenig
[
4
,
5
]
that the weak solutions in
H^{1}(\Omega)

to (
1.10
) and (
1.11
)
satisfy the nontangential maximal function estimates,

\|(\nabla u)^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\quad\text{ and }\quad\|(\nabla u)^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)},

respectively, assuming that
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain.
Our main results extend this classical work to the magnetic Laplacian
P^{h}(\mathbf{A})

in the semiclassical setting, where the estimates are uniform in
h\in(0,h_{0})
.
To the best of the author’s knowledge,
Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
are new
even for smooth domains.

Remark 1.4
.

The auxiliary function
m(x,\mathbf{B})
in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
is defined by

\frac{1}{m(x,\mathbf{B})}=\sup\Big\{r>0:\sup_{\mathcal{B}(x,r)}|\mathbf{B}|\leq\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Big\},

(1.12)

where
\mathcal{B}(x,r)
denotes the ball centered at
x
with radius
r
.
This function, which was introduced by the present author in
[
9
,
10
]
, plays the role of a
critical scaling in the study of Schrödinger operators with electrical and magnetic potentials.
See earlier work in
[
2
,
12
,
14
]
as well as references for more recent work in
[
7
,
8
]
.
Note that if
\mathbf{B}
is a (matrix-valued) polynomial of degree
\kappa
, then

\sup_{\mathcal{B}(x,r)}|\mathbf{B}|\approx\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\kappa}|\partial^{\alpha}\mathbf{B}(x)|r^{|\alpha|}.

It follows that

c\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\kappa}|\partial^{\alpha}\mathbf{B}(x)|^{\frac{1}{|\alpha|+2}}\leq m(x,\mathbf{B})\leq C\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\kappa}|\partial^{\alpha}\mathbf{B}(x)|^{\frac{1}{|\alpha|+2}},

where
C,c>0
depend only on
d
and
\kappa
.
Under the finite-type condition on
\mathbf{B}
in Theorem
1.1
, we have

m(x,h^{-1}\mathbf{B})\geq c\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\kappa}h^{-\frac{1}{|\alpha|+2}}|\partial^{\alpha}\mathbf{B}(x)|^{\frac{1}{|\alpha|+2}}

for
h\in(0,h_{0})
. See Remark
10.2
.

Remark 1.5
.

Consider the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map associated with the operator
P^{h}(\mathbf{A})
,

\Lambda^{h}:f\to n\cdot(hD+\mathbf{A})u,

where
u
is the solution of the Dirichlet problem (
1.3
).
It follows from Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
that

\|\Lambda^{h}f\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\approx\|T^{h}f\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+h\|m(x,h^{-1}\mathbf{B})f\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

(1.13)

for
h\in(0,h_{0})
. Note that
T^{h}f
represents the tangential component of
(hD+\mathbf{A})u
on
\partial\Omega
.
In fact, our proof yields the weighted estimates,

\|m(x,h^{-1}\mathbf{B})^{\ell}\Lambda^{h}f\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\approx\|m(x,h^{-1}\mathbf{B})^{\ell}T^{h}f\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+h\|m(x,h^{-1}\mathbf{B})^{\ell+1}f\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

for any
\ell\in\mathbb{R}
(the bounding constants depend on
\ell
).
See Theorem
7.9
.

Remark 1.6
.

Let
u
be a solution of the Dirichlet problem (
1.3
) with
f\in C(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{C})
.
Then

\|(u)^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

(1.14)

This follows readily from the maximum principle for subharmonic functions.
No addition condition beyond
\mathbf{A}\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d})
is not needed.
See Theorem
2.5
.

Remark 1.7
.

Suppose
\mathbf{B}
is of finite type on
\partial\Omega
; i.e., the inequality (
1.4
) holds
for any
x\in\partial\Omega
.
By compactness it follows that (
1.4
) holds in a neighborhood of
\partial\Omega
.
In this case, the estimates
(
1.6
) and (
1.8
) hold if one modifies the definition of the nontangential maximal function
(u)^{*}

by considering only points near
\partial\Omega
,

(u)^{*}(x)=\sup\big\{|u(y)|:\ y\in\Omega,\ |y-x|<M_{0}\text{\rm dist}(y,\partial\Omega)\text{ and }\text{\rm dist}(y,\partial\Omega)<c\big\}.

This follows directly from the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

We now describe the main ingredients in the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.
First, we rewrite the equation
{P}^{h}(\mathbf{A})u=0
as

(D+\beta\mathbf{A})^{2}u=0
, where
\beta=h^{-1}>1
.
By a localization argument, we reduce the problem to the equation

(D+\mathbf{A})^{2}u=0
in a bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega
, assuming that
\Omega\subset\mathcal{B}(0,R_{0})
for some
R_{0}>0

and the magnetic field
\mathbf{B}
satisfies the following conditions.
There exists
C_{0}>0
such that

\sup_{\mathcal{B}(x,r)}|\nabla\mathbf{B}|\leq\frac{C_{0}}{r}\fint_{\mathcal{B}(x,r)}|\mathbf{B}|

(1.15)

for any
\mathcal{B}(x,r)\subset\mathcal{B}(0,4R_{0})
, and

\sup_{\mathcal{B}(x_{0},R_{0})}|\mathbf{B}|\geq 4R_{0}^{-2}

(1.16)

for any
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
.
Note that when
\mathbf{B}
is replaced by
\beta\mathbf{B}
,
the condition (
1.15
) remains invariant, while (
1.16
) is satisfied for
\beta
sufficiently large.
Moreover, under the conditions (
1.15
)-(
1.16
), it is proved in
[
8
]
that

\int_{\Omega}m(x,\mathbf{B})^{2}|\psi|^{2}dx\leq C\int_{\Omega}|(D+\mathbf{A})\psi|^{2}dx

(1.17)

for any
\psi\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{C})
.

Next, let
u\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})
be a weak solution of
(D+\mathbf{A})^{2}u=0
in
\Omega
.
By an approximation argument, we may assume that
\Omega
and thus
u
are smooth.
To relate the
L^{2}
norm of the normal component of
(D+\mathbf{A})u
on
\partial\Omega

to the
L^{2}
norm of its tangential components, we use two Rellich-type identities,
adapted to the operator
(D+\mathbf{A})^{2}
.
See Lemmas
7.1
and
7.2
.
As a result, the proof for (
1.13
) with
h=1
is reduced to the estimates,

\left\{\begin{aligned} \int_{\Omega}m(x,\mathbf{B})^{3}|u|^{2}\,&dx\leq C\int_{\partial\Omega}m(x,\mathbf{B})^{2}|u|^{2}\,dx,\\
\int_{\Omega}m(x,\mathbf{B})^{3}|u|^{2}\,&dx\leq C\int_{\partial\Omega}|n\cdot(D+\mathbf{A})u|^{2}\,dx.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.18)

To establish (
1.18
), we study the Green function
G_{\mathbf{A}}(x,y)

and the Neumann function
N_{\mathbf{A}}(x,y)
for the operator
(D+\mathbf{A})^{2}

in
\Omega
. Suppose
d\geq 3
(the case
d=2
is handled by the method of descending).
Using (
1.17
), we are able to show that under the assumptions (
1.15
)-(
1.16
),

\left\{\begin{aligned} |G_{\mathbf{A}}(x,y)|&\leq\frac{C_{\ell}}{\{1+|x-y|m(x,\mathbf{B})\}^{\ell}}\cdot\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d-2}},\\
|N_{\mathbf{A}}(x,y)|&\leq\frac{C_{\ell}}{\{1+|x-y|m(x,\mathbf{B})\}^{\ell}}\cdot\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d-2}}\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(1.19)

for any
\ell\geq 0
and
x,y\in\Omega
.
See Sections
5
and
6
.
The estimates in (
1.18
) follow from (
1.19
) by using the Rellich identities mentioned above and a duality argument.

Finally, with the Rellich estimates (
1.13
) at our disposal, we establish the nontangential maximal function
estimates (
1.6
) and (
1.8
) with the help of (
1.14
).
A key observation here is that although the commutator
[(D+\mathbf{A})^{2},D_{k}+A_{k}]
may not be zero in
\Omega
,
the function
v(x)=|(D+\mathbf{A})u(x)|+m(x,\mathbf{B})|u(x)|
nevertheless satisfies
the interior estimate,

v(x)\leq C\fint_{\mathcal{B}(x,r)}v,

if
\mathcal{B}(x,r)\subset\Omega
. See Section
4
.
This allows us to use the techniques developed for (
1.18
) to control the additional error terms.

Preliminaries

Throughout the section we assume that
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain and
\mathbf{A}\in C^{1}({\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
u_{\varepsilon}=\sqrt{|u|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}
. Then for
u\in C^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})
,

|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|\leq|(\nabla+i\mathbf{A})u|,

(2.1)

and for
u\in C^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})
,

u_{\varepsilon}\Delta u_{\varepsilon}\geq\Re\left\{(\nabla+i\mathbf{A})^{2}u\cdot\overline{u}\right\}.

(2.2)

Proof.

By differentiating
u_{\varepsilon}^{2}=|u|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}
, we obtain

u_{\varepsilon}\partial_{j}u_{\varepsilon}=\Re\{(\partial_{j}+iA_{j})u\cdot\overline{u}\}

(2.3)

for
1\leq j\leq d
.
Hence,
u_{\varepsilon}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|\leq|(\nabla+i\mathbf{A})u||u|
.
Since
|u|\leq u_{\varepsilon}
, this yields (
2.1
).

Next, by differentiating (
2.3
), we see that

|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+u_{\varepsilon}\Delta u_{\varepsilon}=\Re\{(\nabla+i\mathbf{A})^{2}u\cdot\overline{u}\}+|(\nabla+i\mathbf{A})u|^{2},

(2.4)

which, together with (
2.1
) and
|u|\leq u_{\varepsilon}
, leads to (
2.2
).
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})
. Then

\left(\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p_{d}}\right)^{1/p_{d}}\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}|(D+\mathbf{A})u|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

(2.5)

where
p_{d}=\frac{2d}{d-2}
for
d\geq 3
,

2<p_{d}<\infty
for
d=2
, and
C
depends only on
\Omega
and
p_{d}
.
Furthermore, if
u\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})
, then

\left(\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p_{d}}\right)^{1/p_{d}}\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}|(D+\mathbf{A})u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+C\left(\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

(2.6)

and

\int_{\partial\Omega}|u|^{2}\leq C\int_{\Omega}|(D+\mathbf{A})u|^{2}+C\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}.

(2.7)

Proof.

Let
v_{\varepsilon}=\sqrt{|u|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}-\varepsilon
, where
\varepsilon>0
.
It follows from (
2.1
) that

|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|\leq|(D+\mathbf{A})u|
.
Since
v_{\varepsilon}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
, by Sobolev inequality,

\left(\int_{\Omega}|v_{\varepsilon}|^{p_{d}}\right)^{1/p_{d}}\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}|(D+\mathbf{A})u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

By letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, we obtain (
2.5
) for
u\in C_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{C})
.
A density argument gives (
2.5
) for
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})
.
The proofs for (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) are similar.
∎

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
u\in C^{2}(\mathcal{B}(x_{0},2r);\mathbb{C})
be a solution of
(D+\mathbf{A})^{2}u=F
in
\mathcal{B}(x_{0},2r)
.
Then

\left(\fint_{\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)}|u|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leq C\fint_{\mathcal{B}(x_{0},2r)}|u|+Cr^{2}\left(\fint_{\mathcal{B}(x_{0},2r)}|F|^{p}\right)^{1/p},

(2.8)

where
2\leq p<q\leq\infty
and
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}<\frac{2}{d}
.
The constant
C
depends only on
d
,
p
and
q
,

Proof.

We give the proof for
d\geq 3
.
The case
d=2
is similar.
Let
u_{\varepsilon}=\sqrt{|u|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}
, where
\varepsilon>0
.
By (
2.2
), we have
\Delta u_{\varepsilon}\geq-|F|
in
\mathcal{B}(x_{0},2r)
.
By differentiating

\fint_{\partial\mathcal{B}(y,t)}u_{\varepsilon}

in
t
, as in the proof of the mean value property for harmonic functions,
it follows that if
\mathcal{B}(y,t)\subset\mathcal{B}(x_{0},2r)
,

u_{\varepsilon}(y)\leq\fint_{\partial\mathcal{B}(y,t)}u_{\varepsilon}+C\int_{\mathcal{B}(y,t)}\frac{|F(z)|}{|z-y|^{d-2}}dz.

Hence, for
y\in\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)
,

u_{\varepsilon}(y)\leq\fint_{\mathcal{B}(y,r)}u_{\varepsilon}+C\int_{\mathcal{B}(y,r)}\frac{|F(z)|}{|z-y|^{d-2}}dz.

By letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, we obtain

|u(y)|\leq\fint_{B(y,r)}|u|+C\int_{\mathcal{B}(y,r)}\frac{|F(z)|}{|z-y|^{d-2}}dz

for any
y\in\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)
.
This yields (
2.8
) by using Young’s inequality.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
u\in C^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})\cap C(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{C})
be a solution of the Dirichlet problem,

(D+\mathbf{A})^{2}u=0\quad\text{ in }\Omega\quad\text{ and }\quad u=f\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

(2.9)

where
f\in C(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{C})
. Then
|u|\leq v
in
\Omega
, where
v
is harmonic in
\Omega
and

v=|f|
on
\partial\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
w_{\varepsilon}=u_{\varepsilon}-v
, where
u_{\varepsilon}=\sqrt{|u|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}
.
It follows from (
2.2
) that
\Delta w_{\varepsilon}=\Delta u_{\varepsilon}\geq 0
in
\Omega
.
Thus,
w_{\varepsilon}
is subharmonic in
\Omega
.
Note that

w_{\varepsilon}=\sqrt{|f|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}-|f|\leq\varepsilon\quad\text{ in }\partial\Omega.

By the maximum principle for subharmonic functions, this implies that

\max_{\overline{\Omega}}w_{\varepsilon}=\max_{\partial\Omega}w_{\varepsilon}\leq\varepsilon.

Hence,
u_{\varepsilon}\leq v+\varepsilon
in
\Omega
.
By letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, we obtain
|u|\leq v
in
\Omega
.
∎

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain and
\mathbf{A}\in C^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})

Let

for

, where

\Omega
.
The constant

in (

) depends only on

,

, the Lipschitz character of

.
If

, the estimate (

) holds for

.

Proof.

By Lemma

, we see that
(u)^{*}\leq(v)^{*}
on
\partial\Omega
, where

is a harmonic function in

\Omega
such that

.
As a consequence, the estimate (

domains
[

]
.
∎

3
The function
m(x,\mathbf{B})

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
. It follows that there exist
r_{0}>0
and
M_{0}>0
such that
for any
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
,

\Omega\cap\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r_{0})=\left\{(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\text{ and }x_{d}>\phi(x^{\prime})\right\}\cap\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r_{0})

(3.1)

in a new coordinate system, obtained from the standard one through translation and rotation,
where
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Lipschitz function with
\|\nabla\phi\|_{\infty}\leq M_{0}
and
\phi(x_{0})=0
.
A constant
C
is said to depend on the Lipschitz character of
\Omega
if
C
depends on
M_{0}
and the number of balls

\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r_{0})
, centered on
\partial\Omega
, which are needed to cover
\partial\Omega
.
By translation, without the loss of generality, we assume that
\Omega\subset\mathcal{B}(0,R_{0})
for some
R_{0}=Cr_{0}>0
,
where
C
depends on the Lipschitz character of
\Omega
.

We will impose the following conditions on

Proof.

\displaystyle\geq c\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}-C(\mathbf{A})\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}

(5.6)

).

6

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and
0<r<r_{0}
.
Let
u\in C^{2}(\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega;\mathbb{C})\cap C^{1}(\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)\cap\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{C})
be a solution of

\left\{\begin{aligned} (D+\mathbf{A})^{2}u&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\ \mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega,\\
n\cdot(D+\mathbf{A})u&=0&\quad&\text{ on }\ \mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)\cap\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(6.1)

where
n
denotes the outward unit normal to
\partial\Omega
.
Then

\sup_{\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r/2)\cap\Omega}|u|\leq C\fint_{\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega}|u|,

(6.2)

where
C
depends only on
\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
u_{\varepsilon}=\sqrt{|u|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}
.
Since
(D+\mathbf{A})^{2}u=0
in
\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega

2.2
),
we have
\Delta u_{\varepsilon}\geq 0
in
\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega

Using
n\cdot(D+\mathbf{A})u=0
on
\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)\cap\partial\Omega
and (
2.3
),
we see that
n\cdot\nabla u_{\varepsilon}=0
on
\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)\cap\partial\Omega
.
By the even reflection, one can show that
u_{\varepsilon}
is a nonnegative sub-solution of
a second-order elliptic equation in divergence form with bounded measurable coefficients in

\mathcal{B}(x_{0},cr)
.
More precisely, without the loss of generality, assume that

\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r_{0})\cap\Omega=\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r_{0})\cap\{(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:x_{d}>\phi(x^{\prime})\},

where
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Lipschitz continuous function with
\phi(x_{0})=0
and
\|\nabla\phi\|_{\infty}\leq M_{0}
. Let
\Phi
be the map

(x^{\prime},x_{d})\to(x^{\prime},2\phi(x^{\prime})-x_{d})

\mathbb{R}^{d}
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}

Note that
\Phi^{-1}=\Phi
.
For
x\in\mathcal{B}(x_{0},cr)

&u_{\varepsilon}(\Phi(x))&\quad&\text{ if }x_{d}<\phi(x^{\prime}).\end{aligned}\right.

Then

and

\int_{\mathcal{B}(x_{0},cr)}E\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\varphi\leq 0

for any nonnegative
\varphi\in C_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(x_{0},cr);\mathbb{R})
, where

E=\left\{\begin{aligned} &I&\quad&\text{ if }x_{d}\geq\phi(x^{\prime}),\\
&\left(\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial x}\right)\left(\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial x}\right)^{T}(\Phi^{-1}(x))&\quad&\text{ if }x_{d}<\phi(x^{\prime}).\end{aligned}\right.

is symmetric.
The Lipschitz condition

is bounded measurable and uniformly elliptic.
As a result, by the De Giorgi-Nash-Moser estimates, we obtain

\sup_{B(x_{0},cr/2)\cap\Omega}u_{\varepsilon}\leq C\fint_{B(x_{0},cr)\cap\Omega}u_{\varepsilon},

depends only on the Lipschitz character of
\Omega

from which and the interior estimate (
4.1

6.2

∎

.

\int_{\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)\cap\partial\Omega}|v|^{2}\leq\frac{C}{r}\int_{\mathcal{B}(x_{0},2r)\cap\Omega}|v|^{2}+Cr\int_{\mathcal{B}(x_{0},2r)\cap\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}

\displaystyle+C\int_{\Omega\cap\mathcal{B}(x_{0},2r)}|(D+\mathbf{A})u|^{2}m(x,\mathbf{B})^{\ell+d-2}.

By integrating the inequality above in

over

Throughout this section, we assume that

Lemma 8.1
.

Let

and
w(x)=u(x)m(x,\mathbf{B})
.
Let
\mathcal{M}(w)
be defined by (
4.13

Then

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}|\mathcal{M}(w)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\partial\Omega}|w|^{2}+C\int_{\Omega}|(D+\mathbf{A})u(x)|^{2}m(x,\mathbf{B})\,dx

(8.1)

\displaystyle+C\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{2}m(x,\mathbf{B})^{3}\,dx,

where
C
depends on
\Omega
and
C_{0}
in (
3.2
).

Proof.

Fix
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
.
By a change of the coordinate system, we may assume that
x_{0}=0
and

\Omega\cap\mathcal{B}(0,r_{0})=\left\{(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\text{ and }x_{d}>\phi(x^{\prime})\right\}\cap\mathcal{B}(0,r_{0}),

where

is a Lipchitz function with
\phi(0)=0
and
\|\nabla\phi\|_{\infty}\leq M_{0}

For

It is not hard to see that

\mathcal{M}(w)(x)\leq C\mathcal{M}_{\partial\Omega}\big(\mathcal{M}_{1}(w)\big)(x),

where
\mathcal{M}_{\partial\Omega}
denotes the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator on
\partial\Omega

\mathcal{M}_{\partial\Omega}(g)(x)=\sup\Big\{\fint_{\mathcal{B}(x,r)\cap\partial\Omega}|g|:0<r<cr_{0}\Big\}

for

. As a result, by the

\int_{\mathcal{B}(0,cr_{0})\cap\partial\Omega}|\mathcal{M}(w)|^{2}\leq C\int_{\mathcal{B}(0,c_{1}r_{0})\cap\partial\Omega}|\mathcal{M}_{1}(w)|^{2},

where

.

Next, note that for

By a covering argument, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}|\mathcal{M}(w)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\partial\Omega}|w|^{2}+C\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w||w|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\partial\Omega}|w|^{2}+C\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}m(x,\mathbf{B})^{-1}+C\int_{\Omega}|w|^{2}m(x,\mathbf{B})

for any
w\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{C})
, where we have used the Cauchy inequality for the last step.
We now apply (
8.3
) to the function

w_{\varepsilon}=\sqrt{|w|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}
and then let
\varepsilon\to 0
.
Using

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}|\mathcal{M}(w)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\partial\Omega}|w|^{2}+C\int_{\Omega}|(D+\mathbf{A})w|^{2}m(x,\mathbf{B})^{-1}+C\int_{\Omega}|w|^{2}m(x,\mathbf{B}).

Finally, we apply (

, where
\widetilde{m}
is a function in

]

It follows that

This gives (

∎

\Gamma
denotes the fundamental solution for

-\Delta
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Note that
w\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C})
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla w(\Lambda_{\ell}(x))-\nabla w(x)|^{2}dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}|w(\Lambda_{\ell}(x))-w(x)|^{2}dx

(9.2)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\ell}}\left\{|\nabla^{2}w|^{2}+|\nabla w|^{2}\right\}\to 0,

as
\ell\to\infty
.
Also observe that
v\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})
is harmonic in
\Omega
and
\frac{\partial v}{\partial n}=\widetilde{g}-n\cdot\nabla w\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{C})
.
As a result,
v
and
\nabla v
have nontangential limits a.e. on
\partial\Omega
and

(v)^{*}+(\nabla v)^{*}\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)

[
4
,
5
]
.
By the dominated convergence theorem,

\int_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla v(\Lambda_{\ell}(x))-\nabla v(x)|^{2}dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}|v(\Lambda_{\ell}(x))-v(x)|^{2}dx\to 0

as
\ell\to\infty
, which, together with (
9.2
), gives (
9.1
).
∎

Theorem 9.3
.

Let
u\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})
be a solution of the Neumann problem (
8.6
)
for some
g\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{C})
.
Let
v(x)=|(D+\mathbf{A})u(x)|+m(x,\mathbf{B})|u(x)|
. Then

\|(v)^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\|m(x,\mathbf{B})^{1/2}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)},

(9.3)

where
C
depends on
C_{0}
in (
3.3
),
r_{0}
and the Lipschitz character of
\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
u\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})
be a weak solution of (
8.6
) with
g\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{C})
.
Let
\{\Omega_{\ell}\}
be a sequence of smooth domains, given by Lemma
9.1
.
Since
\overline{\Omega}_{\ell}\subset\Omega
and
\mathbf{A}\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
we have
u\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega}_{\ell};\mathbb{C})
.
This allows us to apply Theorem
8.2
and (
7.20
) to obtain

\|(v)^{*}_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{\ell})}+\|m(x,\mathbf{B})^{1/2}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\ell})}\leq C\|n\cdot(D+\mathbf{A})u\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{\ell})},

(9.4)

where we have used
(v)^{*}_{\ell}
to denote the nontangential maximal function of

v
for the domain
\Omega_{\ell}
.
Thanks to Lemma
9.1
,
the constant
C
in (
9.4
) depends only on
r_{0}
, the Lipschitz character of
\Omega
, and
C_{0}
in (
3.2
).
In particular, the estimate (
9.4
) is uniform with respect to
\ell
.
Using the observation
\|(v)^{*}_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{\ell})}\leq C\|(v)^{*}_{m}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{m})}
for
\ell<m
,
we see that

\|(v)^{*}_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{\ell})}+\|m(x,\mathbf{B})^{1/2}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\ell})}\leq C\|n\cdot(D+\mathbf{A})u\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{m})}

for
\ell<m
.
We now let
m\to\infty
in the inequality above.
Note that by Lemma
9.2
,

\|n\cdot(D+\mathbf{A})u\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{m})}\to\|g\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

Thus,

\|(v)^{*}_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{\ell})}+\|m(x,\mathbf{B})^{1/2}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\ell})}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

By letting
\ell\to\infty
in the inequality above, we obtain (
9.3
).
∎

Theorem 9.4
.

Let
u\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})
be a solution of the Dirichlet problem (
8.12
)
for some

.
Let

. Then

\|(v)^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\|m(x,\mathbf{B})^{1/2}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\left\{\|Tf\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\|m(x,\mathbf{B})f\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\right\},

(9.5)

where
C
depends on
C_{0}
in (
3.3
),

and the Lipschitz character of

.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Theorem

, using Theorem

in the place of Theorem

.
∎

10
Proof of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2

In this section, with Theorems
9.3
and
9.4
at our disposal,
we use a localization argument to complete the proofs
of our main results.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
.
Assume
\mathbf{A}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

\mathbf{B}=\nabla\times\mathbf{A}
is of finite type on
\overline{\Omega}
.
It follows that there exist an integer
\kappa\geq 0
and
c_{0}>0
such that

\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\kappa}|\partial^{\alpha}\mathbf{B}(x)|\geq c_{0}

(10.1)

for any
x\in\overline{\Omega}
.
By extension we may assume
\mathbf{B}\in C^{\kappa+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and

\|\mathbf{B}\|_{C^{\kappa+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|\mathbf{B}\|_{C^{\kappa+1}(\overline{\Omega})}
(there is no need to extend
\mathbf{A}
).
By compactness we may further assume that (
10.1
) holds for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with dist
(x,\Omega)<2r_{0}
.

Let
\widetilde{\Omega}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\text{\rm dist}(x,\Omega)<r_{0}\}
.

Lemma 10.1
.

Suppose (
10.1
) holds for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\text{\rm dist}(x,\Omega)<2r_{0}
. Then

\sup_{\mathcal{B}(x,r)}|\partial^{\alpha}\mathbf{B}|\leq\frac{C_{1}}{r^{|\alpha|}}\fint_{\mathcal{B}(x,r)}|\mathbf{B}|

(10.2)

for any
\mathcal{B}(x,r)\subset\widetilde{\Omega}
and
any
\alpha
with
|\alpha|\leq\kappa
.
Moreover,

\sup_{\mathcal{B}(x,r)}|\mathbf{B}|\geq c_{1}r^{\kappa}

(10.3)

for any
x\in\overline{\Omega}
and
0<r<r_{0}
.
The constants
C_{1},c_{1}>0
depend on
\Omega
,
(\kappa,c_{0})
in (
10.1
) and
\|\mathbf{B}\|_{C^{\kappa+1}(\overline{\Omega})}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}(y,r)\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\text{\rm dist}(x,\Omega)<2r_{0}\}
.
Let
\mathbf{P}_{y}
denote the
\kappa^{th}
(matrix-valued) Taylor polynomial of
\mathbf{B}
at
y
.
Then, if
|\alpha|\leq\kappa
,

|\partial^{\alpha}\mathbf{P}_{y}(x)-\partial^{\alpha}\mathbf{B}(x)|\leq C|x-y|^{\kappa+1-|\alpha|}

for any
x\in\mathcal{B}(y,r)
, where
C
depends on
\kappa
and
\|\mathbf{B}\|_{C^{\kappa+1}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})}
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathcal{B}(y,r)}|\partial^{\alpha}\mathbf{B}|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\mathcal{B}(y,r)}|\partial^{\alpha}\mathbf{P}_{y}|+Cr^{\kappa+1-|\alpha|}

(10.4)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{r^{|\alpha|}}\fint_{\mathcal{B}(y,r)}|\mathbf{P}_{y}|+Cr^{\kappa+1-|\alpha|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{r^{|\alpha|}}\fint_{\mathcal{B}(y,r)}|\mathbf{B}|+Cr^{\kappa+1-|\alpha|}

for any
\alpha
with
|\alpha|\leq\kappa
.
Also note that

\displaystyle\fint_{\mathcal{B}(y,r)}|\mathbf{B}|

\displaystyle\geq\fint_{\mathcal{B}(y,r)}|\mathbf{P}_{y}|-Cr^{\kappa+1}

(10.5)

\displaystyle\geq c\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\kappa}|\partial^{\alpha}\mathbf{B}(y)|r^{|\alpha|}-Cr^{\kappa+1}

\displaystyle\geq cr^{\kappa}-Cr^{\kappa+1},

where we have used (
10.1
) for the last step.
As a result, we see that

\fint_{\mathcal{B}(y,r)}|\mathbf{B}|\geq cr^{\kappa}

(10.6)

if
0<r<c
and
c
is sufficiently small.
This gives (
10.3
).
Together with (
10.4
), it also yields (
10.2
) under the additional condition
0<r<c
.
Note that the inequality (
10.2
) with
\alpha=0
and
0<r<c
implies the doubling condition,

\int_{\mathcal{B}(x,2r)}|\mathbf{B}|\leq C\int_{\mathcal{B}(x,r)}|\mathbf{B}|

for any
\mathcal{B}(x,4r)\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\text{\rm dist}(x,\Omega)<2r_{0}\}
.
The general case for (
10.2
) follows by a covering argument.
∎

Remark 10.2
.

Suppose (
10.1
) holds for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with dist
(x,\partial\Omega)<2r_{0}
.
Also assume that

\sup_{\mathcal{B}(x,r_{0})}|\mathbf{B}|\geq 2r_{0}^{-2}\qquad\text{ for any }x\in\overline{\Omega}.

(10.7)

It follows by definition that
m(x,\mathbf{B})>r_{0}^{-1}
for any
x\in\overline{\Omega}
.
By letting
r=m(x,\mathbf{B})^{-1}<r_{0}
in (
10.2
), we see that

|\partial^{\alpha}\mathbf{B}(x)|\leq Cm(x,\mathbf{B})^{|\alpha|+2}

for any
x\in\overline{\Omega}
and
|\alpha|\leq\kappa
.
As a result, we obtain

m(x,\mathbf{B})\geq c\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\kappa}|\partial^{\alpha}\mathbf{B}(x)|^{\frac{1}{|\alpha|+2}}

(10.8)

for any
x\in\overline{\Omega}
.
By definition it is not hard to see that
m(x,\mathbf{B})\leq C\|\mathbf{B}\|^{1/2}_{L^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega})}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We first consider the case
d\geq 3
.
Rewrite (
1.2
) as

(D+\beta\mathbf{A})^{2}u=0\quad\text{ in }\Omega\quad\text{ and }\quad n\cdot(D+\beta\mathbf{A})u=\beta g\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

where
\beta=h^{-1}
.
By Lemma
10.1
, the magnetic field
\mathbf{B}
satisfies the condition

\sup_{\mathcal{B}(x,r)}|\nabla\mathbf{B}|\leq\frac{C}{r}\fint_{\mathcal{B}(x,r)}|\mathbf{B}|

for any
\mathcal{B}(x,r)\subset\widetilde{\Omega}
.
It follows that
\beta\mathbf{B}
satisfies the same condition. Moreover,
by the proof of Lemma
10.1
, there exists
c>0
, depending only on
\Omega
,
(\kappa,c_{0})
in (
10.1
) and

\|\mathbf{B}\|_{C^{\kappa+1}(\overline{\Omega})}
such that

\fint_{\mathcal{B}(x,r)}|\mathbf{B}|\geq cr^{\kappa}

for any
x\in\overline{\Omega}
and
0<r<r_{0}
. This implies that

\sup_{\mathcal{B}(x,r)}|\beta\mathbf{B}|\geq c\beta r^{\kappa}.

(10.9)

for any
x\in\overline{\Omega}
and
0<r<r_{0}
, where
c>0
depends only on
\Omega
,
(\kappa,c_{0})
in (
10.1
) and

\|\mathbf{B}\|_{C^{\kappa+1}(\overline{\Omega})}
.

Next, we fix
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and consider a family of Lipschitz domains
\mathcal{O}_{t}=\Omega\cap\mathcal{B}(x_{0},tr_{0})
,
where
t\in(c,2c)
and
c>0
is sufficiently small.
Let
R_{0}=2cr_{0}
.
Note that
\mathcal{O}_{t}\subset\mathcal{B}(x_{0},R_{0})
,

\beta\mathbf{B}
satisfies (
3.2
) for any
\mathcal{B}(x,r)\subset\mathcal{B}(x_{0},4R_{0})
.
Moreover, by (
10.9
),

\sup_{\mathcal{B}(x,R_{0}/2)}|\beta\mathbf{B}|\geq c\beta(R_{0}/2)^{\kappa}>4R_{0}^{-2},

if
\beta>\beta_{0}=CR_{0}^{-\kappa-2}
and
C
is sufficiently large.
This allows us to apply Theorem
9.3
to obtain

\|(v)^{*}_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\mathcal{O}_{t})}+\|m(x,\beta\mathbf{B})^{1/2}v\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{t})}\leq C\|n\cdot(D+\beta\mathbf{A})u\|_{L^{2}(\partial\mathcal{O}_{t})},

(10.10)

where
v(x)=|(D+\beta\mathbf{A})u(x)|+m(x,\beta\mathbf{B})|u(x)|
and
(v)^{*}_{t}
denotes the nontangential maximal function of

v
with respect to the domain
\mathcal{O}_{t}
.
We point out that the constant
C
in (
10.10
) does not depend on
t
,
as the family of Lipschitz domains
\{\mathcal{O}_{t}:t\in(c,2c)\}

possesses uniform Lipschitz characters.
It follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{B}(x_{0},cr_{0})\cap\partial\Omega}|\mathcal{N}(v)|^{2}+\int_{\mathcal{B}(x_{0},cr_{0})\cap\Omega}m(x,\beta\mathbf{B})|v|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r_{0})\cap\partial\Omega}|\beta g|^{2}+C\int_{\Omega\cap\partial\mathcal{B}(x_{0},tr_{0})}|(D+\beta\mathbf{A})u|^{2}

for
t\in(c,2c)
, where

\mathcal{N}(v)(x)=\sup\big\{|v(y)|:y\in\Omega,|y-x|<M_{0}\text{\rm dist}(y,\partial\Omega)\text{ and }\text{\rm dist}(y,\partial\Omega)<cr_{0}\big\}

for
x\in\partial\Omega
. By integrating the inequality above in
t\in(c,2c)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{B}(x_{0},cr_{0})\cap\partial\Omega}|\mathcal{N}(v)|^{2}+\int_{\mathcal{B}(x_{0},cr_{0})\cap\Omega}m(x,\beta\mathbf{B})|v|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r_{0})\cap\partial\Omega}|\beta g|^{2}+C\int_{\Omega\cap\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r_{0})}|(D+\beta\mathbf{A})u|^{2}.

By a covering argument, this gives

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}|\mathcal{N}(v)|^{2}+\int_{\Omega_{cr_{0}}}m(x,\beta\mathbf{B})|v|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\partial\Omega}|\beta g|^{2}+C\int_{\Omega}|(D+\beta\mathbf{A})u|^{2},

(10.11)

where
\Omega_{s}=\{x\in\Omega:\text{\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)<s\}
.

To bound
v(x)
for
x
away from
\partial\Omega
, observe that if
\mathcal{B}(x,cr_{0})\subset\Omega
,

\displaystyle|v(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\fint_{\mathcal{B}(x,cr_{0}/2)}|v|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\fint_{\mathcal{B}(x,cr_{0}/2)}|(D+\beta\mathbf{A})u|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

where we have used the interior estimate (
4.14
)
for the first inequality and (
3.12
) for the second.
It follows that

\int_{\partial\Omega}|(v)^{*}|^{2}\leq C\int_{\partial\Omega}|\mathcal{N}(v)|^{2}+C\int_{\Omega}|(D+\beta\mathbf{A})u|^{2}.

This, together with (
10.11
), leads to

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}|(v)^{*}|^{2}+\int_{\Omega_{cr_{0}}}m(x,\beta\mathbf{B})|v|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\partial\Omega}|\beta g|^{2}+C\int_{\Omega}|(D+\beta\mathbf{A})u|^{2}.

(10.12)

To bound
m(x,\beta\mathbf{B})|v|^{2}
away from
\partial\Omega
, we let
\mathcal{B}(x_{0},2cr_{0})\subset\Omega
and apply
Theorem
9.3
to the domain
\mathcal{O}_{t}=\mathcal{B}(x_{0},tr_{0})
, where

t\in(c,2c)
.
This yields

\int_{\mathcal{B}(x_{0},cr_{0})}m(x,\beta\mathbf{B})|v|^{2}\leq C\int_{\partial\mathcal{B}(x_{0},tr_{0})}|(D+\beta\mathbf{A})u|^{2}.

By integrating the inequality above in
t\in(c,2c)
, we obtain

\int_{\mathcal{B}(x_{0},cr_{0})}m(x,\beta\mathbf{B})|v|^{2}\leq C\int_{\mathcal{B}(x_{0},2cr_{0})}|(D+\beta\mathbf{A})u|^{2}.

Thus, by a covering argument,

\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{cr_{0}}}m(x,\beta\mathbf{B})|v|^{2}\leq C\int_{\Omega}|(D+\beta\mathbf{A})u|^{2}.

In view of (
10.12
), we have proved that

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}|(v)^{*}|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}m(x,\beta\mathbf{B})|v|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\partial\Omega}|\beta g|^{2}+C\int_{\Omega}|(D+\beta\mathbf{A})u|^{2}

(10.13)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\partial\Omega}|\beta g|^{2},

where we have used the energy estimate for the last step.

Finally, note that

\beta^{-1}v=|(hD+\mathbf{A})u(x)|+hm(x,h^{-1}\mathbf{B})|u(x)|=v_{h},

where

h=\beta^{-1}
.
Let
h_{0}=\beta_{0}^{-1}
.
The desired estimates (
1.6
)-(
1.7
) for
h\in(0,h_{0})
now follow from (
10.13
).
Note that by Remark
10.2
, if
h\in(0,h_{0})
,

c\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\kappa}h^{-\frac{1}{|\alpha|+2}}|\partial^{\alpha}\mathbf{B}(x)|^{\frac{1}{|\alpha|+2}}\leq m(x,h^{-1}\mathbf{B})\leq Ch^{-\frac{1}{2}},

where
C,c>0
depend only on
\Omega
,
(\kappa,c_{0})
in (
10.1
) and

\|\mathbf{B}\|_{C^{\kappa+1}(\overline{\Omega})}
.

The case
d=2
can be handled by the method of descending.
Indeed, let
u\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})
be a weak solution of
the Neumann problem (
1.2
) with boundary data
g\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{C})

in a two-dimensional Lipschitz domain

\Omega
.
Define

\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}=(A_{1}(x_{1},x_{2}),A_{2}(x_{1},x_{2}),0)
and
v(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=u(x_{1},x_{2})
.
Then

(hD+\widetilde{\mathbf{A}})^{2}v=0
in
\mathcal{O}
, where

\mathcal{O}=\Omega\times(0,r_{0})
is a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
Note that
n\cdot(hD+\widetilde{\mathbf{A}})v=0
on
\Omega\times\{0,r_{0}\}
, and that

n\cdot(hD+\widetilde{\mathbf{A}})v=(n_{1},n_{2})\cdot(hD+\mathbf{A})u
on
\partial\Omega\times(0,r_{0})
.
It follows from the case
d=3
that

\displaystyle\|((hD+\mathbf{A})u)^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|((hD+\widetilde{\mathbf{A}})v)^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\mathcal{O})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|n\cdot(hD+\widetilde{\mathbf{A}})v\|_{L^{2}(\partial\mathcal{O})}=C\|g\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

Observe that if
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=(x^{\prime},x_{3})
, then

m(x,\nabla\times\widetilde{\mathbf{A}})\approx m(x^{\prime},\nabla\times\mathbf{A})
.
The other estimates in Theorem
1.1
may be obtained in the same manner.
We omit the details.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

The proof for the case
d\geq 3
is similar to that of Theorem
1.2
, using Theorem
9.4
.
The case
d=2
can also be treated by the method of descending.
Let
u\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})
be a weak solution of the Dirichlet problem (
1.3
) with boundary data
f\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{C})

in a two-dimensional
Lipschitz domain
\Omega
.
Let
\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}
,
v
and
\mathcal{O}
be defined as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Then
v\in H^{1}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{C})
is a weak solution of
(hD+\widetilde{\mathbf{A}})^{2}v=0
in
\mathcal{O}
with
Dirichlet data
F
, where
F(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=u(x_{1},x_{2})
on
\Omega\times\{0,r_{0}\}
,
and
F(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=f(x_{1},x_{2})
for
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\partial\Omega\times(0,r_{0})
.
It is not hard to check that

\displaystyle\|T^{h}(F)\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega\times(0,r_{0}))}+h\|m(x,h^{-1}\nabla\times\widetilde{\mathbf{A}})F\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega\times(0,r_{0}))}

\displaystyle=\|T^{h}(f)\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+h\|m(x^{\prime},h^{-1}\nabla\times\mathbf{A})f\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)},

and

\displaystyle\|T^{h}(F)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\{0,r_{0}\})}+h\|m(x,h^{-1}\nabla\times\widetilde{\mathbf{A}})F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\{0,r_{0}\})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(hD+\mathbf{A})u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+Ch\|m(x^{\prime},h^{-1}\nabla\times\mathbf{A})u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(hD+\mathbf{A})u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

where we have used (
3.12
). Moreover,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|(hD+\mathbf{A})u|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq h\int_{\partial\Omega}|n\cdot(hD+\mathbf{A})u||u|

\displaystyle\leq C\|(hD+\mathbf{A})u\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\|hm(x,h^{-1}\mathbf{B})u\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\|(hD+\mathbf{A})u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+C\varepsilon^{-1}\|hm(x,h^{-1}\mathbf{B})f\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{2},

where we have used the fact
m(x,h^{-1}\mathbf{B})\geq r_{0}^{-1}
for any
x\in\overline{\Omega}
, if
h\in(0,h_{0})
.
As a result, we have proved that

\displaystyle\|((hD+\mathbf{A})u)^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+h\|(m(x,h^{-1}\mathbf{B})u)^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|T^{h}f\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+C(1+\varepsilon^{-1})h\|m(x,h^{-1}\mathbf{B})f\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+C\varepsilon\|(hD+\mathbf{A})u\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

By choosing
\varepsilon
small so that
C\varepsilon\leq(1/2)
, we obtain (
1.8
).
The estimate (
1.9
) follows in a similar manner.

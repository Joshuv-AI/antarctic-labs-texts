Mirror Descent Methods with Weighting Scheme for Outputs for Constrained Variational Inequality Problems
Mohammad S. Alkousa, Belal A. Alashqar, Fedor S. Stonyakin, Tarek Nabhani, Seydamet S. Ablaev

Abstract
. This paper is devoted to the variational inequality problems. We consider two classes of problems, the first is classical constrained variational inequality, and the second is the same problem with functional (inequality type) constraints. To solve these problems, we propose mirror descent-type methods with a weighting scheme for the generated points in each iteration of the algorithms. This scheme assigns smaller weights to the initial points and larger weights to the most recent points, thus it improves the convergence rate of the proposed methods. For the variational inequality problem with functional constraints, the proposed method switches between productive and non-productive steps in the dependence on the values of the functional constraints at iterations. We analyze the proposed methods for the time-varying step sizes and prove the optimal convergence rate for variational inequality problems with bounded and monotone operators. The results of numerical experiments of the proposed methods for classical constrained variational inequality problems show a significant improvement over the modified projection method.

2.
Fundamentals

Let
(\mathbf{E},\|\cdot\|)
be a normed finite-dimensional vector space, with an arbitrary norm
\|\cdot\|
, and
\mathbf{E}^{*}
be the conjugate space of
\mathbf{E}
with the following norm

\|y\|_{*}=\max\limits_{x\in\mathbf{E}}\{\langle y,x\rangle:\|x\|\leq 1\},

where
\langle y,x\rangle
is the value of the continuous linear functional
y\in\mathbf{E}^{*}
at
x\in\mathbf{E}
.

Let
Q\subset\mathbf{E}^{n}
be a convex compact set with a diameter
D>0
, i.e.,
\max_{x,y\in Q}\|x-y\|\leq D
, and
\psi:Q\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a proper closed differentiable and
\sigma
-strongly convex (called prox-function or distance generating function). The corresponding Bregman divergence is defined as

V_{\psi}(x,y)=\psi(x)-\psi(y)-\langle\nabla\psi(y),x-y\rangle,\quad\forall x,y\in Q.

For the Bregmann divergence, it holds the following inequality

(2.1)

V_{\psi}(x,y)\geq\frac{\sigma}{2}\|y-x\|^{2},\quad\forall x,y\in Q.

Definition 2.1
.

(
\delta
-monotone operator).
Let
\delta>0
. The operator
F:Q\longrightarrow\textbf{E}^{*}
is called
\delta
-monotone, if it holds

(2.2)

\langle F(y)-F(x),y-x\rangle\geq-\delta,\quad\forall x,y\in Q.

For example, we can consider
F=\nabla_{\delta}f
for
\delta
-subgradient
\nabla_{\delta}f(x)
of convex function
f
at a point
x\in Q
:
f(y)-f(x)\geq\langle\nabla_{\delta}f(x),y-x\rangle-\delta
for each
y\in Q
(see e.g., Chapter 5 in
[
53
]
).

When
\delta=0
, then the operator
F
is called monotone, i.e.,

(2.3)

\langle F(x)-F(y),x-y\rangle\geq 0,\quad\forall x,y\in Q.

We say that the operator
F
is bounded on
Q
, if there exist
L_{F}>0
such that

(2.4)

\|F(x)\|_{*}\leq L_{F},\quad\forall x\in Q.

The following identity, known as the three points identity, is essential in analyzing the mirror descent method.

Lemma 2.2
.

(Three points identity
[
17
]
)
Suppose that
\psi:\mathbf{E}\longrightarrow(-\infty,\infty]
is a proper
closed, convex, and differentiable function over
\operatorname{dom}(\partial\psi)
. Let
a,b\in\operatorname{dom}(\partial(\psi))
and
c\in\operatorname{dom}(\psi)
. Then it holds

(2.5)

\left\langle\nabla\psi(b)-\nabla\psi(a),c-a\right\rangle=V_{\psi}(c,a)+V_{\psi}(a,b)-V_{\psi}(c,b).

Fenchel-Young inequality
(
[
12
]
). For any
b\in\mathbf{E},a\in\mathbf{E}^{*}
, it holds the following inequality

(2.6)

\langle a,b\rangle\leq\frac{\|a\|_{*}^{2}}{2\lambda}+\frac{\lambda\|b\|^{2}}{2},\quad\forall\lambda>0.

3.
Mirror descent method for constrained variational inequality problem

In this section, we consider the following variational inequality problem

(3.1)

\text{Find}\quad x^{*}\in Q:\quad\langle F(x),x^{*}-x\rangle\leq 0\quad\forall x\in Q,

where
F:Q\longrightarrow\textbf{E}^{*}
is a continuous, bounded (i.e., (
2.4
) holds), and
\delta
-monotone operator (i.e., (
2.2
) holds).

Under the assumption of continuity and monotonicity (i.e.,
\delta=0
) of the operator
F
, the problem (
3.1
) is equivalent to a Stampacchia
[
23
]
(or strong
[
51
]
) variational inequality, in which the goal is to find
x^{*}\in Q
such that

(3.2)

\langle F(x^{*}),x^{*}-x\rangle\leq 0,\quad\forall x\in Q.

To emphasize the extensiveness of the problem (
3.1
) (or (
3.2
)), we mention three common special cases for VIs.

Example 3.1

(Minimization problem)
.

Let us consider the minimization problem

(3.3)

\min_{x\in Q}f(x),

and assume that
F(x)=\nabla f(x)
, where
\nabla f(x)
denotes the (sub)gradient of
f
at
x
. Then, if
f
is convex, it can be proved that
x^{*}\in Q
is a solution to (
3.2
) if and only if
x^{*}\in Q
is a solution to (
3.3
).

Example 3.2

(Saddle point problem)
.

Let us consider the saddle point problem

(3.4)

\min_{u\in Q_{u}}\max_{v\in Q_{v}}f(u,v),

and assume that
F(x):=F(u,v)=\left(\nabla_{u}f(u,v),-\nabla_{v}f(u,v)\right)^{\top}
, where
Q=Q_{u}\times Q_{v}
with
Q_{u}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{u}},Q_{v}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{v}}
. Then if
f
is convex in
u
and concave in
v
, it can be proved that
x^{*}\in Q
is a solution to (
3.2
) if and only if
x^{*}=(u^{*},v^{*})\in Q
is a solution to (
3.4
).

Example 3.3

(Fixed point problem)
.

Let us consider the fixed point
problem

(3.5)

\text{find}\;\;x^{*}\in Q\;\;\text{such that}\quad T(x^{*})=x^{*},

where
T:\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an operator. By taking
F(x)=x-T(x)
, it can be proved that
x^{*}\in Q=\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a solution to (
3.2
) if
F(x^{*})=\textbf{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, i.e.,
x^{*}
is a solution to (
3.5
).

Definition 3.4
.

For some
\varepsilon>0
, we call a point
\widehat{x}\in Q
an
\varepsilon
-solution of the problem (
3.1
), if

(3.6)

\left\langle F(x),\widehat{x}-x\right\rangle\leq\varepsilon,\quad\forall x\in Q.

Following
[
51
]
, to assess the quality of a candidate solution
\widehat{x}
, we use the following restricted gap (or merit) function

(3.7)

\operatorname{Gap}(\widehat{x})=\max_{u\in Q}\langle F(u),\widehat{x}-u\rangle.

Thus, our goal is to find an approximate solution to the problem (
3.1
), that is, a point
\widehat{x}\in Q
such that the following inequality holds

(3.8)

\operatorname{Gap}(\widehat{x})=\max_{u\in Q}\langle F(u),\widehat{x}-u\rangle\leq\varepsilon,

for some
\varepsilon>0
.

For problem (
3.1
), we propose an Algorithm
1
, under consideration

(3.9)

V_{\psi}(x,y)\leq R<\infty\quad\forall x,y\in Q,

for some
R>0
.

Algorithm 1

Mirror descent method for constrained variational inequality problem.

0:

step sizes
\{\gamma_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
, initial point
x^{1}\in Q
, number of iterations
N
.

1:

for

k=1,2,\ldots,N

do

2:

x^{k+1}=\arg\min\limits_{x\in Q}\left\{\langle x,F(x^{k})\rangle+\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}V_{\psi}(x,x^{k})\right\}
.

3:

end

for

For Algorithm
1
, we have the following result.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
F:Q\longrightarrow\textbf{E}^{*}
be a continuous, bounded, and
\delta
-monotone operator. Then for problem (
3.1
), by Algorithm
1
, with a positive non-increasing sequence of step sizes
\{\gamma_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
, for any fixed
m\geq-1
, it satisfies the following inequality

(3.10)

\operatorname{Gap}(\widehat{x})\leq\frac{1}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}^{-m}}\left(\frac{R}{\gamma_{N}^{m+1}}+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\|F(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{\gamma_{k}^{m-1}}\right)+\delta,

where

\widehat{x}=\frac{1}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}^{-m}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}^{-m}x^{k}.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{f}(x):=\left\langle x,F(x^{k})\right\rangle+\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}V_{\psi}(x,x^{k})
. From Algorithm
1
, we have
x^{k+1}=\arg\min\limits_{x\in Q}\widetilde{f}(x)
. By the optimality condition, we get

\left\langle\nabla\widetilde{f}(x^{k+1}),x-x^{k+1}\right\rangle\geq 0,\quad\forall x\in Q.

Thus,

\left\langle\gamma_{k}F(x^{k})+\nabla\psi(x^{k+1})-\nabla\psi(x^{k}),x-x^{k+1}\right\rangle\geq 0,\quad\forall x\in Q.

i.e.,

\left\langle\gamma_{k}F(x^{k}),x^{k+1}-x\right\rangle\leq-\left\langle\nabla\psi(x^{k})-\nabla\psi(x^{k+1}),x-x^{k+1}\right\rangle,\quad\forall x\in Q.

By Lemma
2.2
, for any
x\in Q
, we have

\displaystyle\quad-\left\langle\nabla\psi(x^{k})-\nabla\psi(x^{k+1}),x-x^{k+1}\right\rangle,

\displaystyle=-\left(V_{\psi}(x,x^{k+1})+V_{\psi}(x^{k+1},x^{k})-V_{\psi}(x,x^{k})\right)

\displaystyle=V_{\psi}(x,x^{k})-V_{\psi}(x,x^{k+1})-V_{\psi}(x^{k+1},x^{k}).

Thus, we get

\left\langle\gamma_{k}F(x^{k}),x^{k+1}-x\right\rangle\leq V_{\psi}(x,x^{k})-V_{\psi}(x,x^{k+1})-V_{\psi}(x^{k+1},x^{k}),\quad\forall x\in Q.

This means that for any
x\in Q
, we have

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}\left\langle F(x^{k}),x^{k}-x\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq V_{\psi}(x,x^{k})-V_{\psi}(x,x^{k+1})-V_{\psi}(x^{k+1},x^{k})

\displaystyle\quad+\left\langle\gamma_{k}F(x^{k}),x^{k}-x^{k+1}\right\rangle.

By the Fenchel-Young inequality (
2.6
), with
\lambda=\sigma>0
, we find

\left\langle\gamma_{k}F(x^{k}),x^{k}-x^{k+1}\right\rangle\leq\frac{\gamma_{k}^{2}}{2\sigma}\|F(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}+\frac{\sigma}{2}\|x^{k}-x^{k+1}\|^{2}.

Therefore, for any
x\in Q
, we get

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}\left\langle F(x^{k}),x^{k}-x\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq V_{\psi}(x,x^{k})-V_{\psi}(x,x^{k+1})-V_{\psi}(x^{k+1},x^{k})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\gamma_{k}^{2}}{2\sigma}\|F(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}+\frac{\sigma}{2}\|x^{k}-x^{k+1}\|^{2}.

But from (
2.1
), we have

V_{\psi}(x^{k+1},x^{k})\geq\frac{\sigma}{2}\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}.

Thus, for any
x\in Q
, we get the following inequality

(3.11)

\left\langle F(x^{k}),x^{k}-x\right\rangle\leq\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}\left(V_{\psi}(x,x^{k})-V_{\psi}(x,x^{k+1})\right)+\frac{\gamma_{k}}{2\sigma}\|F(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}.

Since
F
is
\delta
-monotone operator, we get

\left\langle F(x),x^{k}-x\right\rangle-\delta\leq\left\langle F(x^{k}),x^{k}-x\right\rangle,\quad\forall x\in Q.

Therefore, for any
x\in Q
, we have

(3.12)

\left\langle F(x),x^{k}-x\right\rangle\leq\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}\left(V_{\psi}(x,x^{k})-V_{\psi}(x,x^{k+1})\right)+\frac{\gamma_{k}}{2\sigma}\|F(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}+\delta.

By multiplying both sides of (
3.12
) by
\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{m}}
, and taking the sum from
1
to
N
, for any
x\in Q
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{m}}\left\langle F(x),x^{k}-x\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{m+1}}\left(V_{\psi}(x,x^{k})-V_{\psi}(x,x^{k+1})\right)

(3.13)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\|F(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{\gamma_{k}^{m-1}}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\delta}{\gamma_{k}^{m}}.

But,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{m}}\left\langle F(x),x^{k}-x\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}^{-m}\right)\left\langle F(x),\frac{1}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}^{-m}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}^{-m}x^{k}-x\right\rangle

(3.14)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}^{-m}\right)\left\langle F(x),\widehat{x}-x\right\rangle.

For any
x\in Q
, we have

\displaystyle\quad\;\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{m+1}}\left(V_{\psi}(x,x^{k})-V_{\psi}(x,x^{k+1})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}^{m+1}}\left(V_{\psi}(x,x^{1})-V_{\psi}(x,x^{2})\right)+\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{m+1}}\left(V_{\psi}(x,x^{k})-V_{\psi}(x,x^{k+1})\right)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\frac{1}{\gamma_{N}^{m+1}}\left(V_{\psi}(x,x^{N})-V_{\psi}(x,x^{N+1})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}^{m+1}}V_{\psi}(x,x^{1})+\frac{1}{\gamma_{N}^{m+1}}V_{\psi}(x,x^{N})+\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{m+1}}V_{\psi}(x,x^{k})

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}^{m+1}}V_{\psi}(x,x^{2})-\sum_{k=2}^{N-1}\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{m+1}}V_{\psi}(x,x^{k+1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}^{m+1}}V_{\psi}(x,x^{1})+\sum_{k=2}^{N}\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{m+1}}V_{\psi}(x,x^{k})-\sum_{k=2}^{N}\frac{1}{\gamma_{k-1}^{m+1}}V_{\psi}(x,x^{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}^{m+1}}V_{\psi}(x,x^{1})+\sum_{k=2}^{N}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{m+1}}-\frac{1}{\gamma_{k-1}^{m+1}}\right)V_{\psi}(x,x^{k})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{R}{\gamma_{1}^{m+1}}+R\sum_{k=2}^{N}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{m+1}}-\frac{1}{\gamma_{k-1}^{m+1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{R}{\gamma_{1}^{m+1}}+R\left(-\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}^{m+1}}+\frac{1}{\gamma_{N}^{m+1}}\right)=\frac{R}{\gamma_{N}^{m+1}}.

Therefore, from (
3.13
), (
3.14
), and the last inequality, we get

\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}^{-m}\right)\max_{x\in Q}\left\langle F(x),\widehat{x}-x\right\rangle\leq\frac{R}{\gamma_{N}^{m+1}}+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\|F(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{\gamma_{k}^{m-1}}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\delta}{\gamma_{k}^{m}}.

By dividing by
\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}^{-m}
, we get the desired inequality

\max_{x\in Q}\left\langle F(x),\widehat{x}-x\right\rangle\leq\frac{1}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}^{-m}}\left(\frac{R}{\gamma_{N}^{m+1}}+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\|F(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{\gamma_{k}^{m-1}}\right)+\delta.

∎

Now, let us see the convergence rate of Algorithm
1
with the following (non-adaptive) time-varying step size rule

(3.15)

\gamma_{k}=\frac{\sqrt{2\sigma}}{L_{F}\sqrt{k}},\quad k=1,2,\ldots,N,

and different values of the parameter
m\geq-1
.

We mention here that for the following adaptive step size scheme

(3.16)

\gamma_{k}=\frac{\sqrt{2\sigma}}{\|F(x^{k})\|_{*}\sqrt{k}}\quad k=1,\ldots N,

there is no guarantee that the sequence
\{\gamma_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
is non-increasing. As a result, this step size does not satisfy one of the conditions of Theorem
3.5
. However, Algorithm
1
performs well in practice, and the convergence rate remains the same as the step size defined in (
3.15
). Therefore, we need to consider restructuring the scheme of step sizes so that it becomes adaptive and allows us to obtain the same results as previously obtained for the non-adaptive ones.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
F:Q\longrightarrow\textbf{E}^{*}
be a continuous, bounded, and
\delta
-monotone operator. Then for problem (
3.1
), by Algorithm

, with

, and the time-varying step size given in (

), it satisfies the following

(3.17)

\operatorname{Gap}(\widetilde{x})\leq\frac{L_{F}\left(1+R+\log(N)\right)}{\sqrt{\sigma}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}+\delta=O\left(\frac{\log(N)}{\sqrt{N}}\right)+\delta,

where
\widetilde{x}=\frac{1}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}x^{k}
.

Proof.

By setting
m=-1
in (
3.10
), we get the following inequality

(3.18)

\operatorname{Gap}(\widetilde{x})\leq\frac{1}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}}\left(R+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}^{2}\|F(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}\right)+\delta,

where
\widetilde{x}=\frac{1}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}x^{k}
.

When
\gamma_{k}=\frac{\sqrt{2\sigma}}{L_{F}\sqrt{k}},\,k=1,2,\ldots,N
, and since
\|F(x^{k})\|_{*}\leq L_{F}
, then by substitution in (
3.18
) we find

\operatorname{Gap}(\widetilde{x})=\max_{x\in Q}\left\langle F(x),\widetilde{x}-x\right\rangle\leq\frac{L_{F}}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\cdot\frac{R+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{k}}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}}+\delta,

where
\widetilde{x}=\frac{1}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}x^{k}
. But

\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{k}\leq 1+\log(N),\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\geq 2\sqrt{N+1}-2,\quad\forall N\geq 1.

Therefore,

\operatorname{Gap}(\widetilde{x})\leq\frac{L_{F}}{\sqrt{2\sigma}}\cdot\frac{1+R+\log(N)}{2\sqrt{N+1}-2}+\delta\leq\frac{L_{F}}{\sqrt{\sigma}}\cdot\frac{1+R+\log(N)}{\sqrt{N}}+\delta.

In the last inequality, we used the fact
2\sqrt{2}\left(\sqrt{N+1}-1\right)\geq\sqrt{N},\,\forall N\geq 1
.
∎

Note that the convergence rate in (
3.17
) is suboptimal for the bounded monotone operators (i.e., when
\delta=0
in (
2.2
)).

From Theorem
3.5

m

of Algorithm

For this, we have the following result.

\overline{x}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}x^{k}

Proof.

By setting

(3.20)

\operatorname{Gap}(\overline{x})\leq\frac{1}{N}\left(\frac{R}{\gamma_{N}}+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}\|F(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}\right)+\delta,

4.
Mirror-Descent Method for Variational Inequality Problem with Functional Constraints

Consider a set of convex subdifferentiable functionals
g_{i}:Q\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
i=1,2,\ldots,p
. Also we assume that all functionals
g_{i}
are Lipschitz-continuous with some constant
M_{g_{i}}>0
, i.e.,

(4.1)

\left|g_{i}(x)-g_{i}(y)\right|\leq M_{g_{i}}\|x-y\|,\quad\forall\;x,y\in Q\;\;\text{and}\;\;i=1,\ldots,p.

This means that at every point
x\in Q
and for any
i=1,\ldots,p
there is a subgradient
\nabla g_{i}(x)
, such that
\|\nabla g_{i}(x)\|_{*}\leq M_{g_{i}}
.

In this section, we consider the following variational inequality problem

(4.2)

\displaystyle\text{Find}\quad x^{*}\in Q

\displaystyle:\quad\langle F(x),x^{*}-x\rangle\leq 0\quad\forall x\in Q,

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad g_{i}(x)\leq 0,\quad\forall i=1,2\ldots,p,

where
F:Q\longrightarrow\textbf{E}^{*}
is a continuous, bounded (i.e., (
2.4
) holds), and
\delta
-monotone operator (i.e., (
2.2
) holds).

It is clear that instead of a set of Lipschitz-continuous functions
\{g_{i}(\cdot)\}_{i=1}^{p}
we can see one Lipschitz-continuous functional constraint
g:Q\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
, such that

(4.3)

g(x)=\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq p}\{g_{i}(x)\},\;\;\text{and}\;\;|g(x)-g(y)|\leq M_{g}\|x-y\|\;\quad\forall\;x,y\in Q,

where
M_{g}=\max_{1\leq i\leq p}\{M_{g_{i}}\}
. Thus, the problem (
4.2
), will be equivalent to the following problem

(4.4)

\text{Find}\quad x^{*}\in Q:\quad\langle F(x),x^{*}-x\rangle\leq 0,\;\;\text{and}\;\;g(x)\leq 0,\quad\forall x\in Q.

Definition 4.1
.

For some
\varepsilon>0
, we call a point
\widehat{x}\in Q
an
\varepsilon
-solution of the problem (
4.4
), if

(4.5)

\left\langle F(x),\widehat{x}-x\right\rangle\;\leq\varepsilon\quad\forall x\in Q,\quad\text{and}\quad g(\widehat{x})\leq\varepsilon.

To solve the problem (
4.2
) (or its equivalent (
4.4
)), we propose a mirror-decent type method, listed as Algorithm
2
below.

As can be seen from the items of Algorithm
2
, the needed point (i.e., the output, see (
4.7
)) is selected among the points
x^{i}
for which
g(x^{i})\leq\varepsilon
. Therefore, we will call step
i

productive
if
g(x^{i})\leq\varepsilon
. If the reverse inequality
g(x^{i})>\varepsilon
holds, then the step
i
will be called
non-productive
.

Let
I
and
J
denote the set of indices of productive and non-productive steps, respectively.
|A|
denotes the cardinality of the set
A
. Let us also set
\gamma_{k}:=\gamma_{k}^{F}
if
k\in I
,
\gamma_{k}:=\gamma_{k}^{g}
if
k\in J
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
F:Q\longrightarrow\textbf{E}^{*}
be a continuous, bounded, and
\delta
-monotone operator. Let
g(x)=\max_{1\leq i\leq p}\{g_{i}(x)\}
be an
M_{g}
-Lipschitz convex function, where
g_{i}:Q\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},\;\forall i=1,2,\ldots,p
are
M_{g_{i}}
-Lipschitz convex functions, and
M_{g}=\max_{1\leq i\leq p}\{M_{g_{i}}\}
. Then for problem (
4.4
), by Algorithm
2
, with a positive non-increasing sequence of step sizes
\{\gamma_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
, for any fixed
m\geq-1
, after
N\geq 1
iterations, it satisfies the following inequality

\displaystyle\operatorname{Gap}(\widehat{x})<\frac{1}{\sum_{k\in I}(\gamma_{k}^{F})^{-m}}

\displaystyle\Bigg(\frac{R}{\gamma_{N}^{m+1}}+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k\in I}\frac{\|F(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{(\gamma_{k}^{F})^{m-1}}+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k\in J}\frac{\|\nabla g(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{(\gamma_{k}^{g})^{m-1}}

(4.6)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-(\varepsilon-M_{g}D)\sum_{k\in J}(\gamma_{k}^{g})^{-m}\Bigg)+\delta,

where
D>0
is the diameter of
Q
, and

(4.7)

\widehat{x}=\frac{1}{\sum_{k\in I}(\gamma_{k}^{F})^{-m}}\sum_{k\in I}(\gamma_{k}^{F})^{-m}x^{k}.

Proof.

Similar to what was done in the proof of Theorem
3.5
, we find that for any
k\in I
and
x\in Q
(see (
3.11
)),

\left\langle F(x^{k}),x^{k}-x\right\rangle\leq\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{F}}\left(V_{\psi}(x,x^{k})-V_{\psi}(x,x^{k+1})\right)+\frac{\gamma_{k}^{F}}{2\sigma}\|F(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}.

By multiplying both sides of the previous inequality with
\frac{1}{(\gamma_{k}^{F})^{m}}
, and since
F
is
\delta
-monotone, i.e.,

\left\langle F(x^{k}),x^{k}-x\right\rangle\geq\left\langle F(x),x^{k}-x\right\rangle-\delta,\quad\forall x\in Q,

we get (for any
k\in I
and
x\in Q
)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\gamma_{k}^{F})^{m}}\left\langle F(x),x^{k}-x\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(\gamma_{k}^{F})^{m+1}}\left(V_{\psi}(x,x^{k})-V_{\psi}(x,x^{k+1})\right)

(4.8)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\|F(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{2\sigma(\gamma_{k}^{F})^{m-1}}+\frac{\delta}{(\gamma_{k}^{F})^{m}}.

Also, for any
k\in J
and
x\in Q
, we have

(4.9)

\frac{g(x^{k})-g(x)}{(\gamma_{k}^{g})^{m}}\leq\frac{1}{(\gamma_{k}^{g})^{m+1}}\left(V_{\psi}(x,x^{k})-V_{\psi}(x,x^{k+1})\right)+\frac{\|\nabla g(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{2\sigma(\gamma_{k}^{g})^{m-1}}.

By taking the summation, in each side of (
4.8
) and (
4.9
), over productive and non-productive steps, with
\gamma_{k}=\gamma_{k}^{F}
if
k\in I
and
\gamma_{k}=\gamma_{k}^{g}
if
k\in J
, we get the following (for any
x\in Q
)

\displaystyle\quad\;\sum_{k\in I}(\gamma_{k}^{F})^{-m}\langle F(x),x^{k}-x\rangle+\sum_{k\in J}(\gamma_{k}^{g})^{-m}(g(x^{k})-g(x))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{m+1}}\left((V_{\psi}(x,x^{k})-V_{\psi}(x,x^{k+1})\right)+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k\in I}\frac{\|F(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{(\gamma_{k}^{F})^{m-1}}

(4.10)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k\in J}\frac{\|\nabla g(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{(\gamma_{k}^{g})^{m-1}}+\sum_{k\in I}\frac{\delta}{(\gamma_{k}^{F})^{m}}.

But for any
x\in Q
, we have

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{k\in I}(\gamma_{k}^{F})^{-m}\left\langle F(x),x^{k}-x\right\rangle=\left\langle F(x),\sum_{k\in I}(\gamma_{k}^{F})^{-m}x^{k}-\sum_{k\in I}(\gamma_{k}^{F})^{-m}x\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k\in I}(\gamma_{k}^{F})^{-m}\right)\left\langle F(x),\frac{1}{\sum_{k\in I}(\gamma_{k}^{F})^{-m}}\sum_{k\in I}(\gamma_{k}^{F})^{-m}x^{k}-x\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k\in I}(\gamma_{k}^{F})^{-m}\right)\left\langle F(x),\widehat{x}-x\right\rangle.

Thus, from the last inequality and (
4.10
), for any
x\in Q
, we get

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k\in I}(\gamma_{k}^{F})^{-m}\right)\left\langle F(x),\widehat{x}-x\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{m+1}}\left((V_{\psi}(x,x^{k})-V_{\psi}(x,x^{k+1})\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k\in I}\frac{\|F(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{(\gamma_{k}^{F})^{m-1}}+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k\in J}\frac{\|\nabla g(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{(\gamma_{k}^{g})^{m-1}}

(4.11)

\displaystyle\quad+\delta\sum_{k\in I}(\gamma_{k}^{F})^{-m}-\sum_{k\in J}(\gamma_{k}^{g})^{-m}(g(x^{k})-g(x)).

Since, for any
k\in J
, we have

(4.12)

g(x^{k})-g(x^{*})\geq g(x^{k})>\varepsilon>0.

Then by the convexity of the function
g
, for any
x\in Q
, we have

\displaystyle\quad\,-\sum_{k\in J}(\gamma_{k}^{g})^{-m}(g(x^{k})-g(x))

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k\in J}(\gamma_{k}^{g})^{-m}(g(x^{k})-g(x^{*}))+\sum_{k\in J}(\gamma_{k}^{g})^{-m}(g(x)-g(x^{*}))

\displaystyle<-\varepsilon\sum_{k\in J}(\gamma_{k}^{g})^{-m}+\sum_{k\in J}(\gamma_{k}^{g})^{-m}\left\langle\nabla g(x),x-x^{*}\right\rangle

(4.13)

\displaystyle\leq-\varepsilon\sum_{k\in J}(\gamma_{k}^{g})^{-m}+M_{g}D\sum_{k\in J}(\gamma_{k}^{g})^{-m},

where in the last inequality, we used the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and the fact that
\|\nabla g(x)\|_{*}\leq M_{g},\,\forall x\in Q
, and
Q
is bounded with a diameter
D>0
.

For any
x\in Q
, we have

(4.14)

\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}^{m+1}}\left(V_{\psi}(x,x^{k})-V_{\psi}(x,x^{k+1})\right)\leq\frac{R}{\gamma_{N}^{m+1}}.

Therefore, by combining (
4.13
) and (
4.14
) with (
4.11
), for any
x\in Q
, we get the following

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k\in I}(\gamma_{k}^{F})^{-m}\right)\left\langle F(x),\widehat{x}-x\right\rangle

\displaystyle<\frac{R}{\gamma_{N}^{m+1}}+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k\in I}\frac{\|F(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{(\gamma_{k}^{F})^{m-1}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{k\in J}\frac{\|\nabla g(x^{k})\|_{*}^{2}}{(\gamma_{k}^{g})^{m-1}}+\delta\sum_{k\in I}(\gamma_{k}^{F})^{-m}

\displaystyle\quad-(\varepsilon-M_{g}D)\sum_{k\in J}(\gamma_{k}^{g})^{-m}.

By dividing both sides of the last inequality by
\left(\sum_{k\in I}(\gamma_{k}^{F})^{-m}\right)
, we get the desired inequality (
4.6
).
∎

Remark 4.3

(Stopping rule for Algorithm
2
)
.

From Theorem
4.2
, with

\widehat{x}=\frac{1}{\sum_{i\in I}(\gamma_{i}^{F})^{-m}}\sum_{i\in I}(\gamma_{i}^{F})^{-m}x^{i}
, for any
k\geq 1
, we find

\displaystyle\quad\left(\sum_{i\in I}\left(\gamma_{i}^{F}\right)^{-m}\right)\operatorname{Gap}(\widehat{x})

\displaystyle<\frac{R}{\gamma_{k}^{m+1}}+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\|F(x^{i})\|_{*}^{2}}{(\gamma_{i}^{F})^{m-1}}+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{j\in J}\frac{\|\nabla g(x^{j})\|_{*}^{2}}{(\gamma_{j}^{g})^{m-1}}-(\varepsilon-M_{g}D)\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\gamma_{i})^{-m}

\displaystyle\quad+(\varepsilon-M_{g}D)\sum_{i\in I}(\gamma_{i}^{F})^{-m}+\delta\sum_{i\in I}\left(\gamma_{i}^{F}\right)^{-m}

\displaystyle=\left(\delta+\varepsilon\right)\sum_{i\in I}\left(\gamma_{i}^{F}\right)^{-m}-\Bigg((\varepsilon-M_{g}D)\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\gamma_{i})^{-m}+M_{g}D\sum_{i\in I}\left(\gamma_{i}^{F}\right)^{-m}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\frac{R}{\gamma_{k}^{m+1}}-\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\|F(x^{i})\|_{*}^{2}}{(\gamma_{i}^{F})^{m-1}}-\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{j\in J}\frac{\|\nabla g(x^{j})\|_{*}^{2}}{(\gamma_{j}^{g})^{m-1}}\Bigg).

From this, without relying on prior knowledge of the number of iterations

\displaystyle M_{g}D\sum_{i\in I}\left(\gamma_{i}^{F}\right)^{-m}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{R}{\gamma_{k}^{m+1}}+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\|F(x^{i})\|_{*}^{2}}{(\gamma_{i}^{F})^{m-1}}+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{j\in J}\frac{\|\nabla g(x^{j})\|_{*}^{2}}{(\gamma_{j}^{g})^{m-1}}

(4.15)

\displaystyle\quad+(M_{g}D-\varepsilon)\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\gamma_{i})^{-m}

as a stopping rule of Algorithm

. As a result, we conclude

\left(\sum_{i\in I}\left(\gamma_{i}^{F}\right)^{-m}\right)\max_{x\in Q}\left\langle F(x),\widehat{x}-x\right\rangle<(\delta+\varepsilon)\sum_{i\in I}\left(\gamma_{i}^{F}\right)^{-m}.

Thus,

\max_{x\in Q}\left\langle F(x),\widehat{x}-x\right\rangle<\delta+\varepsilon.

Note that for all
i\in I
it holds that
g(x^{i})\leq\varepsilon
, and since

g(\widehat{x})\leq\frac{1}{\sum_{i\in I}\left(\gamma_{i}^{F}\right)^{-m}}\sum_{i\in I}\left(\gamma_{i}^{f}\right)^{-m}g(x^{i})\leq\varepsilon.

Thus after the stopping rule (

\operatorname{Gap}(\widehat{x})=\max_{x\in Q}\left\langle F(x),\widehat{x}-x\right\rangle<\delta+\varepsilon,\quad\text{and}\quad g(\widehat{x})\leq\varepsilon.

Now, let us take the following time-varying step size rule

(4.16)

\gamma_{k}=\begin{cases}\gamma_{k}^{F}:=\frac{\sqrt{2\sigma}}{L_{F}\sqrt{k}},\quad\text{if}\;k\in I,\\

)) that

Remark 4.4
.

, by Algorithm
2
with (
4.16
), we have at least one productive step for any

and
R>0
.

Now let us analyze the convergence of Algorithm
2
, by taking the time-varying step size rule (
4.16
).

Let
M:=\max\{L_{F},M_{g}\}
. By using (

6.
Conclusion

In this paper, we studied two classes of variational inequality problems. The first is classical constrained (i.e., without functional constraints) variational inequality and the second is the same problem with functional constrained (inequality type constraints). To solve such problems, we proposed mirror descent-type methods with a weighting scheme for the generated points in each iteration of the algorithms. For the second class of problems, we proposed a mirror descent method by switching between productive and non-productive steps. We analyzed the proposed methods for the time-varying step sizes and proved the optimal convergence rate of the proposed algorithm concerning the classical variational inequality problems with bounded and
\delta
-monotone operators. We conducted some numerical experiments, which illustrate the advantages of the presented weighting scheme for some examples of the classical variational inequality problem, with a comparison with the modified projection method. As a future work, there are many directions connecting with the problems under consideration, such as the results for the Lipschitz monotone and strongly monotone operators, as well as for the stochastic setting of the problem.

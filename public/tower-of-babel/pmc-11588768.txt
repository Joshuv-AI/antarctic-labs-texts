Therapeutic effect of total glucosides of paeony on IgA vasculitis nephritis: progress and prospects
Authors: Zhifeng Wang, Jiao Yang, Pengfen He, Junfeng Lan, Ting Shi, Shuangfeng Xu, Zhihui Hao, Yujiang Xi, Jian Wang, Ping He
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
IgA vasculitis nephritis (IgAVN), a type of immune system disease characterized by the deposition of IgA in the mesangial area of the glomerulus, is most common in children. Corticosteroids and immunosuppressants agents are commonly prescribed for the clinical management of IgAVN; however, these therapies are associated with numerous adverse reactions. This necessitates the discovery of alternative, potential therapeutic agents that can offer a safer treatment option. Natural plants contain abundant total glucosides of paeony (TGP), which represent one of the most prevalent secondary metabolites found within these organisms. TGP has been proven to be a safe and desirable natural medicine, with the most central ingredient being polyphenolic. TGP, a naturally occurring and cost-effective compound, exerts its therapeutic influence on IgAVN via diverse pathways and targets, with minimal side effects. Its substantial clinical potential underscores the importance of delving deeper into its pharmacological mechanisms, which hold great promise for novel drug development. This review examines the multifaceted therapeutic mechanisms of TGP in IgAVN encompassing modulation of Wnt/β-catenin pathways and programmed cell death pathways, antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects, and enhancement of vascular repair. Additionally, we provide an overview of recent advancements in enhancing the bioavailability of TGP and highlight crucial considerations that may inform future research endeavors.

Materials and methods

We searched through the Chinese database of CNKI and three English databases, PubMed, Web of Science, and Google Scholar, from January 1, 2000, to July 1, 2024. We used both subject keyword search and multi-author search to improve the completeness rate and reduce the bias of selection. We only counted English and Chinese literature in the search, which may have missed the research results of non-Chinese and non-English researchers, so there is a certain error. The English search terms were: “IgA vasculitis nephritis; Henoch Schonlein Purpura Nephritis; Total Glucosides of Paeony; paeoniflorin; mechanisms of treatment; bio-availability; Wnt/β-catenin Signaling Pathway; Oxidative Stress; Cell Death; Inflammation; Vascular Repair”. The Chinese search terms correspond to the English search terms. Introduction

IgAVN, which refers to kidney damage that occurs concurrent with IgA vasculitis (IgAV), is one of the most common secondary glomerular diseases in children [1]. Hematuria and (or) albuminuria are the main clinical manifestations, and glomerular mesangial changes are the main pathological change in IgAVN [2]. IgAVN often occurs in children under 10 years old—ranking the most frequently occurring secondary nephritis in children, with an incidence rate of 10% to 100% [3]—and about 15% of affected children go on to develop renal failure [4]. However, the etiology and pathogenesis of IgAVN are not clear. An increasing number of studies indicate that IgAVN is mediated by immune factors related to systemic diverticulitis, abnormal by glycosylation IgA1 immune complex causes, other immune mechanisms involving cytokines and complement, or autoimmune disease. Early prevention and treatment of patients with IgAVN and effective control of inflammation are key to improving patient prognosis. In current clinical practice, oral drugs for the treatment of IgAVN mainly include glucocorticoids and immunosuppressants, in addition to symptomatic and supportive treatment. Although corticosteroids and immunosuppressants can reduce inflammation-mediated damage in glomeruli, these treatments are hampered by poor curative effects and adverse reactions [5], IgAVN recurrence, and side effects such as hormone dependence, and steroid resistance. Therefore, innovative drugs and therapies to slow down the progression of IgAVN are still needed. In recent years, there has been a surge of interest in natural remedies, with total glucosides of paeony (TGP) emerging as a prominent research focus [6]. TGP, a secondary metabolite found abundantly in flora, is therapeutically significant among plant-derived compound. Its main ingredients include paeoniflorin (PF), hydroxyl paeoniflorin, paeonin, and albiflorin; among them, the content of PF, a polyphenolic compound, accounts for more than 90% of the total glucoside [7]. The effects of TGP include antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, anti-tumor, anti-fibrotic, and immune regulatory effects [8]. Further, TGP has also been proven to be a safe and ideal natural drug, which regulates IgAVN via multiple pathways and in a multi-targeted manner, with few side effects, low cost, and good clinical application value [9]. However, PF, one of the main pharmacological components of TGP, has a bioavailability of only 3% to 4%, which indicates that the proportion of PF absorbed by the body into the blood circulation is relatively low after oral administration, thus limiting its application in medicine [10]. The solubility of TGP can be enhanced through structural modifications, while nanotechnology offers the potential to precisely target TGP's role, thereby augmenting its biological activity [11].

This comprehensive review outlines the cutting-edge advancements in research aimed at harnessing the therapeutic potential of TGP for IgAVN management. Additionally, it explores the utilization of TGP derivatives and innovative delivery systems to optimize its bioavailability, offering valuable insights and guidance for the therapeutic application of this natural compound.

TGP’s clinical trials/animal studies in IgAVN and other kidney diseases

TGP regulates the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway to treat IgAVN

The mechanism of kidney injury in IgAVN is not completely clear, and its pathological features, include glomerular mesangial cell proliferation, extracellular matrix deposition, and renal interstitial fibrosis, remain poorly understood [12]. Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway is involved in the functional differentiation, proliferation, apoptosis and migration of cells, which are consistent with the pathological characteristics of IgAVN [13–15]. Studies have shown that this signaling pathway plays an important role in the regeneration and repair of renal tubules during acute tubular injury as well as the occurrence and development of renal tumors, and is closely related to the formation of renal fibrosis [16]. Studies have found that abnormal expression of Wnt4, β-catenin, and E-cadherin in the kidney tissue of children with IgAVN. Compared with that in the normal control group, the expression of Wnt4 and β-catenin in kidney tissue of children with IgAVN was significantly increased, while that of E-cadherin was significantly decreased [17]. The Wnt signaling pathway is widely found in various organisms and participates in the regulation of cell differentiation, cell polarity, cell migration, and cell proliferation. The Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway plays an important role in the development of renal units, including Wnt—family, Wnt4 and Wnt9b is most important, Wnt4 gene mutation will lead to renal dysplasia, Wnt9b for induction signal of the development of the kidney When Wnt9b gene mutations or missing ectomesenchymal cells can make kidney stagnation of differentiation, also can cause renal dysplasia [18, 19]. β-catenin plays an important role in kidney development, which affects cell proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis by regulating the transcription of related genes, mainly through the Wnt signaling pathway [20]. In normal human mature kidneys, Wnt signals are “silent”, and β-catenin and E-cadherin form complexes, preventing cell migration to maintain complete organizational structure and avoid kidney damage [21]. Furthermore, studies have found that TGP can inhibit Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway activation and ameliorate kidney damage in diabetic rats [22]. These findings suggest that TGP can modulate the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway to treat kidney disease (Fig. 1A).

TGP interferes with programmed cell death

Programmed cell death (PCD) is widespread in the process of biological development, decided by gene Cell active, orderly and predetermined manner of Death, and mainly including apoptosis, autophagy, ferroptosis and disulfidptosis. TGP can activate caspase family proteins and regulate the expression of Bcl-2 family proteins [23]. The intricate apoptosis cascade is tightly orchestrated by the Bcl-2 family of proteins, which comprises both pro-apoptotic members like Bax and anti-apoptotic counterparts such as Bcl-2. Caspase-3 is a member of the cysteine protease family and a key executor of the apoptosis cascade. It through the original caspase3 cracking is activated, and then cracking of caspase 3 mediated apoptosis of mitochondrial pathways [24]. These processes are particularly important in the treatment of IgAVN because the induction of apoptosis of diseased cells can reduce kidney inflammation and protect renal function. Autophagy is a cellular degradation process, through the formation of autophagy body to remove damaged or organelles and protein, no longer need to maintain the stability of the environment in the cell. Various common stimuli can trigger a combination of autophagy and apoptosis, which is associated with the expression of Bcl-2 and Beclin 1, leading to impaired mitochondrial function [25]. TGP has anti-inflammatory effects, and its main component PF has been shown to regulate Th1/Th2 imbalance [26]. Therefore, similar to the inflammatory response [27], autophagy can also be regulated by PF [28]. Therefore, autophagy may be involved in the protection and repair of renal cells in IgAVN. When the kidney is injured, autophagy can remove damaged organelles and proteins, reduce the inflammatory response, and promote the repair and regeneration of kidney cells [29]. Hence, delving into the intricate interplay between TGP (or PF) and the biological processes of apoptosis, inflammation, and autophagy could potentially unveil novel therapeutic avenues for addressing IgAVN, offering a fresh perspective on the management of this disease.

Ferroptosis represents a novel form of programmed cell death, distinguished by its reliance on iron-mediated lipid peroxidation as a pivotal mechanism leading to cell death [30]. In the pathogenesis of IgAVN, renal cells may cause iron overload due to oxidative stress and other factors, and then trigger ferroptosis [31]. TGP has stronger ability to resist oxidative stress, remove free radicals in the body, reduce oxidative stress to cells in the kidney damage, and reduce the risk of renal cell iron overload, which inhibits ferroptosis [32]. Another mode of cell death, similar to ferroptosis, is disulfidptosis, a newly discovered type of cell death caused by disulfide stress due to excessive cystine accumulation. In this process, cystine transporter SLC7A11 plays a key role [33]. Although there is no direct evidence that there is a direct causal relationship between disulfidptosis and IgAVN, kidney injury in IgAVN involves pathological changes such as glomerular mesangial proliferation and crescent formation, which are closely related to cell death [34]. It has been found that [35] SLC7A11 is a component of the cystine/glutamate transporter and its high expression is associated with cell death in kidney disease. In renal injury models, oxidative stress and cell death in the kidney can be reduced by inhibiting the activity or expression of SLC7A11. It has been shown [36] that PF can reduce acute kidney injury induced by ischemia–reperfusion by inhibiting SLC7A11, suggesting that SLC7A11 may be involved in the occurrence of disulfidptosis in renal injury. PF may inhibit disulfidptosis and promote renal repair (Fig. 1B).

The role of TGP in reactive oxygen species

Oxidative stress arises when the body is subjected to various harmful stimuli, resulting in an overproduction of highly reactive molecules within the body such as reactive oxygen species (ROS) and reactive nitrogen free radicals (RNS). This excess production overwhelms the body’s ability to neutralize them, leading to an imbalance between the oxidation and antioxidation systems, ultimately causing tissue damage [37]. In kidney diseases, inflammatory mediators and various substances in the blood may trigger the oxidative stress response, potentially damaging vascular tissues and further affecting blood circulation and renal function [38]. Furthermore, excessive production of ROS may directly damage the cells in the kidney, leading to glomerular and renal tubular dysfunction. Oxidative stress may also affect the kidneys, specifically the antioxidant system, resulting in the loss of ROS removal ability and exacerbating renal damage [39]. A study shows [40] that TGP can inhibit inflammatory cells’ release of ROS, thus reducing ROS accumulation in the kidney tissue, decreasing ROS-mediated direct damage to kidney cells and protecting renal function. Furthermore, TGP has the ability to augment the activities of vital antioxidant enzymes in kidney tissue, notably superoxide dismutase (SOD) and catalase (CAT), thereby bolstering the body’s defenses against oxidative stress. These antioxidant enzymes remove ROS from the body, improve the antioxidant capacity of kidney tissue, and combat ROS-mediated damage. During the course of IgAVN, there is an infiltration of inflammatory cells, an increase in the generation of free radicals, which in turn triggers a chain reaction of free radicals, leading to enhanced lipid peroxidation. This process amplifies the inflammatory response, leading to thrombosis, allergic reactions, or direct effects, and ultimately may cause vascular spasm, thrombosis, or even vascular occlusion, worsening the condition of IgAVN. In related studies [41], TGP pretreatment has been shown to significantly increase the levels of SOD and CAT and reduce the content of MDA (malondialdehyde), indicating that it can enhance the antioxidant capacity of the body. Secondly, TGP can regulate the signaling pathway related to oxidative stress—namely, the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway—increase antioxidant stress gene expression, and strengthen the body’s antioxidant ability. Immunomodulatory effects of TGP can inhibit the excessive activation of immune cells, reduce IL-1 beta levels, and dampen the release of TNF alpha [8], reducing inflammation and oxidative stress and thus protecting the kidney from further damage. Also, some studies suggest [42] TGP can exert direct effects on kidney cells, reducing ROS damage to these cells and promoting their repair and regeneration. To sum up, TGP regulates oxidative stress through various channels to ameliorate kidney damage.

TGP regulates inflammation

In IgAVN, oxidative stress and the inflammatory response have a mutually promoting effect [2]. ROS and other free radicals produced by oxidative stress can activate immune cells and promote the release of inflammatory factors. Inflammatory factors can also affect the activity of antioxidant enzymes and aggravate oxidative stress, which makes the inflammatory response more difficult to control. Subsequently, activated immune cells release a lot of inflammatory cytokines and antibody, further aggravate the inflammatory response and damage to the kidneys. Furthermore, the inflammatory cytokines may also affect the function of the immune system, forming a vicious cycle [43]. TGP has been proven to have good anti-inflammatory effects. Studies have shown [44] that TGP has significant efficacy in the treatment of IgAVN, reducing the renal inflammatory response and protecting the kidneys by inhibiting the proliferation of Tfh cells and downregulating the expression of IL-21 and IL-4. In addition, PF, as a major component in TGP, it can inhibit renal inflammation by promoting macrophage polarization from M1 to M2 as well as inducing mitochondrial autophagy by regulating KLF4 [45].

Another key pathway in vivo through which inflammation is closely related to oxidative stress and immune regulation is the Janus kinase 1/signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (JAK1/STAT3) pathway. Treg/Th17 immune balance is an important component of cellular immune function. Treg releases anti-inflammatory factors such as IL-10 under the induction of Foxp3 to slow down the progression of inflammatory stress. However, Th17 specifically activates the transcription factor retinoid-related orphan receptor γt (RORγt) to promote the synthesis and secretion of proinflammatory factors such as IL-17 [46]. Nevertheless, there are few reports on the role of this pathway and Treg/Th17 in IgAVN. TGP, the main active ingredient in the traditional Chinese medicine Paeony, has anti-inflammatory, analgesic, and immunomodulatory effects. Shao et al. [44] showed that TGP treatment for IgAVN had significant effects and fewer adverse reactions. Wang et al. [47] also showed that the combination of leukotriene receptor antagonist Montelukast and Paeoniae Radix extract could improve vascular permeability and alleviate inflammatory reactions in mild IgAVN, but they did not deeply reveal the mechanism of action of the drugs. Dolff et al. [48] pointed out that the balance of Treg/Th17 is crucial for maintaining renal immune homeostasis. In autoimmune nephropathy, excessive activation of Th17 cells and relative deficiency of Treg cells often lead to persistent inflammatory damage to renal tissues. Deng et al. [49] showed that increasing the level of serum Treg and its related factors in children with anaphylactic purpura can significantly improve clinical symptoms and enhance autoimmune function. Yang et al. [50] showed that Treg/Th17 imbalance is involved in the pathogenesis of IgAVN, and that increased expression of Th17 and its related cytokine IL-17 is a key mechanism leading to a variety of complications in children. In summary, TGP may mediate inflammatory stress and immune regulation through JAK1/STAT3 and Treg/Th17, thereby providing a new approach for the treatment of IgAVN.

In addition, results have also shown that TGP inhibits dendritic cell maturation and function by selectively blocking Toll-like receptor 4/5(TLR4/5) activation in vivo, which in turn reduces immune-mediated inflammation in vivo [51]. Furthermore, PF, the main component of TGP, inhibits inflammatory factor levels and NLRP3 inflammasome activation to reduce tissue damage [52], representing a new mechanism and therapeutic agent for the treatment of IgAVN and other inflammatory and autoimmune diseases. Although there are fewer studies on the modulation of inflammation by TGP to reduce renal injury, and the mechanism has not been fully revealed, the strength of the anti-inflammatory effects of TGP also indirectly confirms its feasibility in treating IgAVN (Fig. 1C).

The role of TGP in vascular repair

In IgAVN, capillary damage is the major pathological manifestation, and is often accompanied by chronic inflammatory injury of glomerular capillaries and blood vessels as well as micro thrombosis, and complex deposition. These pathological changes further affect the blood circulation and vascular status of the kidney, leading to ischemia and hypoxia in various systems of the kidney and thus aggravating kidney damage [53]. TGP, as an immune regulator, can modulate the proportion and the function of T cell subgroups, inhibit excessive immune response, promote vascular repair, and protect renal function [8]. In kidney repair after vascular injury, endothelial cells play a key role in restoring the function of blood vessels. Studies have shown that [54] TGP can ameliorate the inflammatory response and oxidative stress-mediated damage to the vascular wall through its anti-inflammatory and anti-oxidation effects, and provide a favorable microenvironment for the repair of endothelial cells. Furthermore, TGP may promote endothelial cell proliferation and migration mechanisms, and thus be directly involved in vascular repair. Lv et al. [55] believe that flow in the TGP can increase vascular smooth muscle cell calcium levels, promote platelet aggregation and adhesion, and enhance the capacity for deformation of red blood cells, so as to improve blood flow and permeability. This helps to increase the blood supply and nutrient delivery to the injured vascular area, providing the necessary material support for vascular repair. Furthermore, TGP has anti-platelet aggregation and anti-thrombosis effects, which can inhibit the formation and development of blood clots, thus protecting blood vessels from further damage and creating conditions for the repair process (Fig. 1D).

Other mechanisms

TGP can also ameliorate nephropathy and weight loss in rats, reduce serum UA and XOD, MCP-1, TNF alpha, Cr and BUN, decrease kidney URAT1 and GLUT9 levels, increase renal OAT1, and improve pathological changes in the thymus, spleen, and kidney tissue [56]. TGP can also prevent tubulointerstitial injury by inhibiting increased macrophage infiltration and TLR2 expression [57]. It has also been shown [58] that treatment with PF, the main component of TGP, significantly ameliorated renal interstitial fibrotic lesions in mice by attenuating collagen and plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 (PAI-1) synthesis; furthermore, PF has also been found to inhibit epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) through downregulation of TGF-β1 expression and maintenance of BMP-7 mRNA expression, and suppress UUO-induced fibrotic renal Smad2/3 activation. TNF-α can induce vascular endothelial cells to produce TGF-β (transforming growth factor-β); high concentrations of Tgf-β1 can promote the proliferation of mesangial cells and induce extracellular matrix deposition, thereby promoting glomerulosclerosis and renal fibrosis [59]. TGP can regulate the Th/Ts cell ratio and promote the release of PGE2, which regulates the secretion of TNF alpha, from the peritoneal macrophages of mice [60]. In summary, TGP is instrumental in the management of IgAVN, primarily by modulating relevant signaling pathways and diverse programmed cell death processes, exerting antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects, and promoting vascular repair.

Methods to improve the bioavailability of TGP

Although the therapeutic potential of TGP in IgAVN has been rigorously investigated, its clinical translation is hindered by inherent challenges such as unique structural properties, poor aqueous solubility, swift in vivo metabolism, short half-life, and low bioavailability [10]. Aiming to explore strategies to overcome these limitations, this review delves into nano-self-microemulsion delivery systems tailored for TGP, encompassing innovative techniques such as the synergistic integration of nobiletin and TGP, the employment of microemulsion formulations, and the elaboration of TGP liposomes, all aimed at enhancing the bioavailability of this promising natural compound.

The solubility and bioavailability of TGP can be effectively improved by using nano-self-microemulsion delivery systems (consisting of propylene glycol monoctanoate, polyoxyethylene castor oil, and polyethylene glycol 400; or propylene glycol monoctanoate, polyoxyethylene castor oil, and propylene glycol monethyl ether). Nobiletin can inhibit the efflux transport of PF, thereby increasing its permeability. When nobiletin was used in combination with TGP, the solubility and bioavailability of TGP were significantly increased in a microemulsion formulation prepared by a nanoemulsification drug delivery system. Such systems achieve nanoscale dispersion of drugs by controlling the ratio of oil phase, surfactant, and cosurfactant, thereby improving drug stability and bioavailability [61]. Pharmacokinetic data show that [62], compared with TGP, this microemulsion preparation can improve the bioavailability of PF to as high as 435.04%. Microemulsion, as a new drug carrier method [63], can increase the stability of drugs, improve their bioavailability, and ensure a slow release effect. The experimental results for PF microemulsion preparation in a rat model have shown that [64] the preparation could significantly improve the pharmacokinetic parameters of the drug in vivo, such as increasing the peak concentration and the area under the drug-time curve, thereby prolonging the retention time of the drug in the body and reducing the clearance rate.

In addition, the preparation of TGP liposomes can also be optimized by the Box–Behnken design-response surface optimization method (BBD-RSM), such as the mass ratio of cholesterol to phospholipids, TGP to lipid, and the amount of hydration medium, to achieve high drug encapsulation efficiency and in vitro release rate [65]. Second, TGP can also be structurally modified, e.g., via esterification, acylation, and glycosylation, which can change its physical and chemical properties, such as solubility, stability and permeability, to improve its bioavailability [66]. Nonetheless, the current research landscape in this domain remains relatively sparse, necessitating further exploration. It is anticipated that combining optimized, modified TGP derivatives with innovative, intelligent drug delivery systems will hold significant promise for the development of cost-effective, novel therapeutics that harness the therapeutic potential of TGP in regulating IgAVN.

Future prospects and conclusion

Over the past decades, numerous studies have revealed a complex network of mechanisms underlying the ability of the polyphenolic compound TGP to regulate the pathogenesis of IgAVN. This review delves into the multifaceted ways in which TGP exerts its influence in this context, revealing the multiple pathways involved and its multi-target efficacy. However, despite these advancements, the current research landscape harbors certain limitations. Notably, the regulatory mechanisms governed by TGP, which encompass signaling pathways, programmed cell death pathways, antioxidant defense, anti-inflammatory actions, and vascular restoration, are intricately interconnected rather than isolated entities. This intricate regulatory network, while crucial for IgAVN modulation, remains largely unexplored in its entirety, with most studies focusing on a limited number of targets or approaches. Furthermore, the clinical translation of TGP faces a significant hurdle in the form of low bioavailability. This review compiles the latest advancements in enhancing TGP's bioavailability, offering theoretical insights that could pave the way for the cost-effective and efficient clinical utilization of this natural product.

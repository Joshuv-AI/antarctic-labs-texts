Research on a lightweight model for laser-cut diamond defect detection based on multi-module collaborative optimization
Authors: Anfu Zhu, Qinghua Jiang, Heng Guo, Yinbing Chen, Yaning Yang, Yi Yang, Yueyong Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAs a high-end abrasive material, diamond is prone to defects such as cracks and ablation during laser cutting due to complex physical interactions. Achieving efficient and accurate detection of laser-cut diamond defects is crucial for enhancing product quality and production efficiency. Traditional deep learning object detection algorithms generally face challenges like large parameter magnitudes and heavy computational burdens, making them difficult to apply in potential scenarios of resource-restricted equipment such as handheld inspection devices. In response to this problem, this research develops FAS-YOLO, a lightweight defect detection model for laser-cut diamonds based on the YOLOv11n framework. Initially, the FDConv (Frequency Domain Convolution) module is integrated to reconstruct the C3k2 feature extraction component, enhancing the capture of core defect features.Second, the ADown (Adaptive Downsampling) module is employed to refine the downsampling layer, resolving parameter redundancy. The SEAM attention mechanism is integrated to enhance the detection head module. By learning the importance of different channels and fusing channel information, the model is guided to focus more precisely on defect region features while suppressing background interference such as metal reflections. Experimental results demonstrate that the FAS-YOLO model achieves 92% precision, 80.4% recall, and an mAP50 value of 82.6%. Introduction

As the hardest natural material, diamond is widely used in the field of ultra-hard processing. Its forms include polycrystalline diamond, coated diamond and single crystal diamond tools1. Diamond cutting tools are widely used in high-precision tasks, but common diamond materials struggle to balance high hardness and fracture toughness2. As a typically brittle material, its tightly packed atomic structure and extremely high chemical bond energy render it susceptible to various defects during laser cutting due to complex physical interactions3. The high-density energy released during laser cutting can instantly raise the temperature of the cutting area to several thousand degrees Celsius. The drastic temperature change will cause a significant thermal stress gradient within the diamond. When the stress exceeds the material’s own fracture strength, it will trigger defects such as cracks4 and depressions5. Moreover, the intense thermal cycling at room temperature to high temperatures will further cause diamond to crack or react with surrounding substances, thereby exacerbating the defects6. These defects degrade diamond’s mechanical, optical, and electrical properties, harming product usability and safety7. Therefore, realizing efficient and precise identification of defects in laser-cut diamonds plays a pivotal role in improving product quality and boosting production efficiency.

Traditional diamond defect detection methods primarily rely on manual visual inspection, optical microscopy, and ultrasonic testing8. While these techniques can effectively identify surface defects, the inspection process heavily depends on the operator’s experience, which not only reduces detection efficiency but also struggles to meet the demands of modern industrial production. In recent years, machine learning-based object detection methods have been applied to defect detection tasks across multiple scenarios. These approaches primarily utilize Gaussian mixture models for precise defect classification9 and support vector machines with wavelet features to analyze time-series defect data for classification10. However, machine learning methods have limitations: they depend on precise defect model construction and lack adaptability to complex scenarios. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have enabled multi-object recognition in complex environments, with wide industrial applications: Magdy A, Moustafa M S, Ebied H M, et al.11 proposed a two-stage compression method, through awareness training and post-training pruning and quantization, to achieve a lightweight R-CNN for satellite images. The model size was reduced by 25.6%, the parameters were decreased by 56.6%, while maintaining mAP. Zhao C et al.12 utilized a cross-peer-to-peer network optimized using generative adversarial networks (GANs) addresses the shortage of training samples required for deep learning in diamond tool defect detection.

Above-mentioned deep learning solutions have promoted the development of industrial defect detection, there is still a gap in the research field of lightweight technology in the laser-cut diamond environments. The YOLO algorithm, leveraging its single-stage detection advantage, offers a novel solution to this challenge13. Wang H et al.14 proposed the RDLK-YOLO model, which combines illumination compensation, multiple innovative modules and a hybrid loss function, achieving excellent detection performance in pipeline defect detection under uneven lighting conditions. Wang T, Li Y, Shen M et al.15 proposed a PCBA-YOLO method, which is based on the YOLOv5 model and can effectively improve the accuracy of PCBA defect detection. Feng Z et al.16 proposed a model specifically designed to identify the adsorption state of diamond particles on pinholes. The improved system reduces the detection time for diamond abrasive layers from 6.35 milliseconds to 5.06 milliseconds. Feng Y et al.17 proposed a lightweight YOLO-MFD model, which overcomes the challenges of small target accuracy and potential deployment in steel surface defect detection through multi-module optimization. On the NEU-DET dataset, the mAP reached 83.8%, and the parameters were reduced by 27%, achieving a balance between accuracy and lightweight design. Zhu et al.18 developed the MFF-YOLO model, which enabled accurate tunnel defect detection through the construction of a multi-scale feature fusion network and the adoption of a reweighted filtering strategy. Hou C et al.19 proposed the SD-YOLO model, which overcomes the challenges of detecting small surface defects in lithium batteries through multi-module optimization. Compared to YOLOv11, it improves accuracy, recall rate, and mAP, and has better detection speed and small target detection capabilities. Huang H et al.20 proposed the SO-YOLO model, which improves the performance of detecting small defects on chips by expanding the integration of shallow features, optimizing the anchor boxes, and removing redundant branches, outperforming models such as YOLOv4.

Despite the outstanding performance of the YOLO series algorithms in object detection, applying them to lightweight technology research for laser-cut diamond scenarios still faces the following challenges: (1) Insufficient capture of edge and detail features for different diamond defects during feature extraction. (2) If lightweighting is achieved by reducing feature map resolution, critical semantic information about defects may be lost. Meanwhile, the original model has a large number of parameters and low lightweighting degree, which is unfavorable for deployment. (3) The detection heads of traditional YOLO systems are susceptible to multiple interference factors such as flame traces and metal reflections, thereby reducing the accuracy of defect detection. To tackle the aforementioned problems, this study proposes FAS-YOLO—a lightweight defect detection model for laser-cut diamonds built on the YOLOv11n architecture. Algorithm improvement

YOLOv11n model

YOLOv11n’s core strength resides in striking an optimal balance between object detection precision and inference speed while maintaining a compact network scale21. Its greatest strength lies in the C3k2 module, which supports flexible selection of custom convolutional kernel sizes, balancing feature extraction efficiency with model configuration flexibility. In addition, a novel C2PSA module is incorporated subsequent to the SPPF layer. The network architecture of YOLOv11n is illustrated in Fig. 1.

YOLOv11n network architecture diagram.

FAS-YOLO model

Although YOLOv11n has achieved satisfactory results in object detection, it still exhibits limitations in capturing edge features of diamond defects during laser cutting defect detection. The model suffers from large parameter sizes and low lightweighting efficiency, while the detection head remains susceptible to environmental interference in laser cutting scenarios. To address this, this paper designs the FAS-YOLO model based on YOLOv11n.

For feature extraction, FDConv is adopted to reconstruct the C3k2 layer. FDConv dynamically groups features into high-frequency, mid-frequency, and low-frequency categories, dynamically adjusting the weight parameters of convolutional kernels based on the frequency distribution of input features. This method enhances the expression capability of high-frequency detail features such as diamond cracks and fractures while suppressing low-frequency background noise from laser reflections, thereby optimizing the processing of target details against complex backgrounds. Subsequently, replacing the downsampling convolutional layers in the backbone network’s P3 layer and subsequent layers with the existing ADown module effectively prevents detail loss caused by excessive downsampling. This enhances overall object detection performance while reducing the number of parameters. Finally, the SEAM attention mechanism is adopted to strengthen the detection head module’s performance. This module employs dual-channel and spatial attention filtering through a dynamic attention mechanism to enhance generalization capabilities in complex scenes. It accurately captures key features of diamond defects such as cracks and fractures while focusing on target areas and suppressing irrelevant information like cutting flame marks, metallic reflections, and complex backgrounds on the diamond surface. This improves the extraction efficiency of effective defect features and boosts detection accuracy. The improved network architecture is shown in Fig. 2.

Rework the C3k2 module with FDConv

Traditional C3k2 layers capture features at different scales through convolution operations. However, the fixed convolution kernels and sampling grid struggle to effectively capture irregular, multi-directional defects such as diamond cracks and ablation. To enhance feature extraction while suppressing laser reflection noise, the FDConv22 module is adopted to reconstruct C3k2.

FDConv consists of three clear, collaborative sub-modules: Fourier Disjoint Weights (FDW), Kernel Space Modulation (KSM)23 and Frequency Band Modulation (FBM). FDW constructs diverse convolution kernels in the frequency domain; KSM finely tunes each kernel weight element; FBM decomposes kernels into multiple frequency bands and applies spatial-variant modulation. Together, they achieve strong adaptability to defect features and anti-interference ability. The structure of the FDConv module is illustrated in Fig. 3.

ADown downsampling module

In deep learning models, downsampling serves as a critical operation that effectively reduces the spatial dimensions of feature maps, enhancing computational efficiency while expanding the receptive field. The feature extraction process in traditional deep learning models exhibits static characteristics in both weights and computational logic, unable to dynamically adjust the focus range based on input image content. Diamond defect features, however, are characterized by minute scales and intricate details. Traditional static subsampling approaches directly result in critical feature loss, thereby reducing detection accuracy. This study introduces the ADown subsampling module, derived from YOLOv9, to replace conventional subsampling operations. This lightweight module reduces computational complexity while preserving feature integrity24. The core workflow is illustrated in Fig. 4.

SEAMHead detection head module

In the scenario of laser cutting diamond defect detection, the detection head part of the YOLOv11 model has two major problems. First, laser cutting diamond defects are often interfered by complex backgrounds such as metal reflections and cutting flame marks, and the detection head is prone to misjudgment background noise as defect features. Secondly, the detection head lacks targeted filtering of the channel and spatial information in the feature map, which hinders its ability to focus on the defect core areas. Consequently, the insufficient efficiency of feature utilization results in low detection precision. Therefore, in this paper, the SEAM attention mechanism25 is integrated into the detection head module of the model to construct SEAMHead. The SEAM attention mechanism achieves the refined selection of feature maps through the synergistic effect of channel attention branches and spatial attention branches. The parallel design of the two ensures the comprehensiveness of feature extraction while It also avoids the limitations of single-dimensional attention. The structural mechanism by which the model’s diamond defect detection ability in complex environments is enhanced is shown in Fig. 5.

SEAM module structure diagram.

The SEAM attention mechanism is the core component enabling SEAMHead to effectively select key features for defect detection. Its implementation comprises four steps: multi-scale feature fusion, subsampling, nonlinear transformation, and attention weight generation. First, the three sets of patch-scale features output by CSMM undergo additive fusion, integrating information to capture defect textures and contours and establishing the feature foundation. The fused features undergo subsampling via average pooling to reduce redundancy and focus on core semantic information. Subsequently, a fully connected network combined with a channel expansion module performs nonlinear transformation to learn the channel-spatial differences between defects and noise. Finally, attention weights are generated to enhance defect features while suppressing background noise.

Among these, multi-scale patch feature extraction is performed by the CSMM module, whose architecture forms the core of SEAM’s multi-scale feature perception. This module efficiently extracts features from patches of varying sizes through the workflow: “patch embedding → separable convolutions → residual connections → point convolutions.” First, the patch embedding layer divides input features into patch units of different sizes, completing initial feature encoding. Subsequently, the GELU activation function and batch normalization layer perform nonlinear feature transformation and normalization. Deep separable convolutions extract spatial dimensions within each patch, while residual connections enhance feature flow. Finally, point convolutions adjust the channel dimension, and after GELU and batch normalization, multiscale patch features are output. The module architecture is illustrated in Fig. 6.

Evaluation indicators

To comprehensively assess the performance of the improved model in laser-cutting diamond defect detection, this study chose precision (P), recall (R), mean average precision (mAP), parameter count, and gigaflops per second (GFLOPs) as the core evaluation metrics. Precision (P) quantifies the proportion of correctly predicted positive cases among all predicted positives, which reflects the reliability of the model’s predictions. Recall (R) measures the proportion of correctly predicted positive instances among all actual positives, reflecting the model’s coverage capability. Mean Average Precision (mAP) evaluates the model’s overall detection precision in multi-class object detection. The calculation of this metric relies on Average Precision (AP), which represents the area beneath the P-R curve and assesses the detection performance for a single class. Additionally, the number of parameters serves as a crucial metric for evaluating model performance.

Discussion

Analysis of module complementarity mechanism and synergistic enhancement mechanism

In the FAS-YOLO model, the three modules achieve efficiency enhancement through differentiated functions and feature link collaboration: FDConv compensates for the shortcomings of traditional convolution in capturing irregular features by performing frequency-domain dynamic grouping. Consistent with Yin Y et al.41 who enhanced model performance by optimizing local feature extraction, this study confirmed the effectiveness of frequency-domain dynamic convolution in enhancing feature representation and improving model efficiency. ADown, which uses a dual-branch adaptive dimension reduction to solve the information loss problem of traditional downsampling, is in line with the research results of Cai S et al.42. SEAMHead uses a dual attention mechanism to distinguish defects from background noise. It forms a complete complementary link of “feature extraction - feature dimension reduction - feature selection”. Tang W et al.43 used this method to solve the sensitivity problem of traffic signal detection algorithms to changes in lighting and vehicle speed.

In summary, the scientific innovation presented in this paper does not simply involve stacking existing modules. Instead, addressing the challenges of weak defect patterns and complex backgrounds in the industrial scenario of laser-cut diamond defect detection, we have tailored a combination of these three modules to create a multi-module, collaboratively optimized detection framework. Systematic experiments demonstrate the suitabilityand practicality of this framework for the target tasks.

Analysis of the main reasons for the slight decrease in precision and the mitigation training strategies

FAS-YOLO achieved a detection precision of 92%, slightly lower than baseline YOLOv11n—an inherent trade-off between lightweighting and feature completeness in multi-module collaborative optimization. Specifically, ADown’s dual-branch dimensionality reduction compressed parameters but caused minor fine-grained feature loss for low-contrast defects; FDConv’s frequency-domain grouping focused on core defect features, yet its high-frequency branch weight allocation was susceptible to input feature complexity, leading to insufficient low-quality sample edge feature capture. This resulted in a marginal precision drop for partial edge defects, which is well justified by the model’s 37.4% parameter reduction.

To mitigate this precision decline, targeted training strategies can be adopted while preserving FAS-YOLO’s lightweight merit: introducing a knowledge distillation framework with YOLOv11 as the teacher model and KL divergence loss to enhance low-contrast sample detection by learning complex defect feature discrimination logic; optimizing data augmentation via frequency-domain noise suppression and defect area local enhancement to balance feature distribution in complex scenarios. These measures can effectively compensate for the current precision loss, providing stronger performance support for FAS-YOLO’s industrial application in laser cutting diamond defect detection.

The limitations of this study and the directions for future research

Although FAS-YOLO has demonstrated effectiveness in laser-cut diamond defect detection, the research still has limitations: model training was based solely on data from conventional laser-cutting environments, and its detection accuracy under extreme conditions such as sudden changes in illumination remains unverified. Furthermore, the dataset’s limited coverage of defect types may result in insufficient generalization capabilities for detecting specific defects.

To enhance the robustness of defect detection in complex laser cutting scenarios, future plans include introducing visible-light and infrared image multimodal fusion technology: Visible-light images clearly reveal the geometric and textural details of diamond surface defects but are susceptible to interference from flames and reflections, leading to the loss of low-contrast features. Infrared images, relying on thermal radiation differences to distinguish defects, exhibit strong anti-interference capabilities and can accurately capture temperature anomalies such as microcracks caused by thermal stress. This approach overcomes the limitations of single-modality detection, effectively enhancing the robustness of defect detection models for laser-cut diamonds.

To adapt for edge potential deployment, a combined strategy of pruning and quantization with knowledge distillation is proposed. This approach significantly reduces model size while maintaining accuracy. Pruning eliminates redundant convolutional kernels and connection layers to minimize redundant computations, while quantization converts floating-point parameters to low-precision integers, thereby reducing memory consumption and shortening inference latency. Simultaneously, a high-precision baseline model serves as the teacher network, transferring defect feature discrimination logic through knowledge distillation to compensate for accuracy loss caused by pruning and quantization. This dual-compression strategy reduces both model parameters and computational load, enabling the optimized model to efficiently adapt to edge devices for laser-cut diamond defect detection and meet real-time online inspection engineering requirements.

Conclusion

Object detection techniques based on deep learning often exhibit strong scene dependency and task-specificity across various industrial scenarios. In this study, addressing the specific application of defect detection in laser-cut diamonds, we propose the FAS-YOLO model, which is based on the efficient integration of existing modules. Experimental results demonstrate that, compared to baseline models, the proposed model achieves significantly reduced complexity and parameter count while still delivering competitive detection performance, thereby laying the foundation for future research on deployment on edge devices.

Although the dataset in this paper covers four typical defect categories, it does not encompass all possible defect types. Furthermore, due to the difficulty of collecting defect samples in industrial environments, the dataset size is relatively limited, resulting in certain practical limitations for the proposed method.

In future research, we will further explore effective deployment on edge devices to facilitate practical applications. We will investigate how to achieve an optimal balance between detection accuracy and inference speed under limited computational resources, and strive to translate this algorithm from theoretical research into practical industrial inspection applications.

Cycling along Euler road
Dylan Wyrzykowski

Abstract
. We introduce the notion of P λ P_{\lambda} points, which canonically parametrize points on the Euler line. This allows us to show that the Euler line of any d d -dimensional inscribed polygon in Euclidean space arises from the Euler lines of its sub-polygons, beginning from the Euler line of a point in the plane. Furthermore, we situate P λ P_{\lambda} points in the literature of modern triangle centers.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{n}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}(O,1)

to the multidimensional polygon

(4)

the lines

A_{1}A_{2}\ldots A_{n}

Proof.

For point 1, we compute the distance between
P_{\lambda}^{n}
and each
P_{\lambda,i}^{n-1}

\begin{split}|p_{\lambda}^{n}-p_{\lambda,i}^{n-1}|&=\left|\lambda\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}-\left(\lambda\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}-\lambda a_{i}\right)\right|\\
&=|\lambda a_{i}|=|\lambda|,\end{split}

(2.2)

since
A_{i}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}(O,1)
. This proves that all points
P_{\lambda,i}^{n-1}
lie on the hypersphere
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}(P_{\lambda}^{n},|\lambda|)

P_{\lambda}^{n}
lies at distance

from each

\mathbb{S}^{d-1}(P_{\lambda,i}^{n-1},|\lambda|)
. For point 3, consider the homothety
\Phi_{\mathbf{T}}^{-\lambda}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
defined as follows:

\Phi_{\mathbf{T}}^{-\lambda}:x\mapsto-\lambda(x-\mathbf{t})+\mathbf{t},\quad\text{where}\quad\mathbf{t}=\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}.

(2.3)

Thus, for any
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
we have:

\begin{split}\Phi^{-\lambda}_{\mathbf{T}}(a_{i})&=-\lambda\left(a_{i}-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\right)+\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\\
&=-\lambda a_{i}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\lambda+1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}+\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\\
&=-\lambda a_{i}+\lambda\left(\frac{\lambda+1}{\lambda+1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\right)\\
&=\lambda\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}-\lambda a_{i}=p_{\lambda,i}^{n-1}.\end{split}

(2.4)

Proving point 3. For point 4, since

since

Lemma 3.2

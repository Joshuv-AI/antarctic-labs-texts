General quantum backflow in realistic wave packets
Tomasz Paterek, Arseni Goussev

Abstract
Quantum backflow is a counterintuitive phenomenon in which the probability density of a quantum particle propagates opposite to its momentum. Experimental observation of backflow has remained elusive due to two main challenges: (i) the effect is intrinsically small, with less than 4% of the probability able to flow backward, and (ii) it requires wave packets with a well-defined momentum direction, which are difficult both to prepare and to verify under realistic, noisy conditions. Here, we overcome these challenges by introducing a general formulation of quantum backflow applicable to arbitrary momentum distributions. The framework recovers the standard backflow limit for unidirectional states and identifies general backflow as probability flow exceeding that predicted by the particle’s momentum distribution alone. We show that this excess can reach nearly 13%, surpassing the standard backflow bound by more than a factor of three. Furthermore, we extend the framework to the closely related phenomenon of quantum reentry, provide explicit examples of quantum states exhibiting large general backflow and reentry, and discuss the foundational implications of these nonclassical effects. Our results open a pathway toward the experimental observation of quantum backflow in realistic settings.



Introduction.
Interference of matter waves—evidenced by the appearance of characteristic density fringes obtained through repeated position measurements of a particle at a given time—has been a cornerstone of quantum mechanics since its early days. Only relatively recently, however, has it been recognized that performing position measurements at multiple distinct times can reveal nonclassical aspects of probability transport even for a free nonrelativistic particle. Perhaps the most well-known example is quantum backflow
Allcock 1969
;
Kijowski 1974
;
Werner 1988
;
Bracken and Melloy 1994
;
Bracken 2021
, but other notable effects include quantum reentry
Goussev 2019
, Tsirelson’s precession
Tsirelson
;
Zaw et al. 2022
;
Zaw et al. 2025
;
Zaw and Scarani 2025
;
Vaartjes et al. 2025
, and quantum advantage in various transportation tasks
Hofmann 2017
;
Hofmann 2021
;
Ono et al. 2023
;
Trillo et al. 2023
. Although these effects appeal to the very core of quantum mechanics---much like interference and diffraction---even quantum backflow has not yet been experimentally observed.

Two main obstacles have hindered the experimental observation of quantum backflow. The first arises from the intrinsic weakness of the effect. Bracken and Melloy

Bracken and Melloy 1994
;

;
Yearsley et al. 2012

;

The second obstacle is the strict requirement of a fixed momentum direction. Such states are difficult to engineer and verify because realistic, noisy measurements inevitably produce spurious outcomes that can indicate an incorrect momentum direction even for properly prepared initial states. Moreover, in some systems—such as ballistic electrons in mesoscopic conductors—preparing a strictly unidirectional wave packet is fundamentally impossible
Goussev and Morozov 2025
.
While Bracken has generalized the standard backflow formulation to systems with any momentum cutoff
Bracken 2021
, these considerations motivate the development of a broader framework capable of describing quantum backflow for arbitrary momentum distributions.

Here, we develop such a general formulation, applicable to both quantum backflow and the closely related phenomenon of quantum reentry. The latter describes the nonzero probability that a free quantum particle reenters a spatial region it has previously left—a behavior forbidden to classical free particles, whose motion is unidirectional in the absence of external forces. Backflow emerges as a special case of reentry
Goussev 2020
. We show that, when extended to arbitrary momentum distributions, quantum reentry can be tested by examining only a finite region of the experimental interferogram, making this approach particularly suitable for experimental implementation.

Our approach is founded on the observation that quantum interference enables a particle to reach spatial regions inaccessible to classical particles. We express the classical limits on probability transport as two inequalities—one for backflow and one for reentry—that make no assumptions about momentum direction or spatial localization. Quantum mechanics can violate these inequalities, with up to
0.1281
of the particle’s probability flowing opposite to the classical expectation. The optimal state yielding this maximal violation differs from the standard backflow-maximizing state. We also investigate general backflow in experimentally relevant settings using superpositions of Gaussian wave packets. While Gaussian states have been considered in prior studies of standard backflow
Yearsley et al. 2012
, only the present work provides a rigorous treatment that accounts for their infinite momentum support.

Classical limits.
We begin by characterizing the absence of backflow in classical systems. Consider a free classical particle of mass
m
moving along the
x
axis, with an unrestricted phase-space distribution
f(x,p,t)=f(x-pt/m,p,0)
. The central quantity in backflow-like problems is the probability of finding the particle to the left of the origin
x=0
at time
t
, denoted as
P_{-}(t)=\operatorname{Prob}(x<0,t)
. This probability can change between times
t_{1}
and
t_{2}=t_{1}+T
in two distinct ways: (i) particles with positive momentum (
p
> 0) initially located in the interval
x\in(-pT/m,0)
will leave the region, while (ii) particles with negative momentum (
p<0
) initially positioned in
x\in(0,-pT/m)
will enter it. Since in general backflow we are interested in the increment of this probability, we upper-bound the probability gain by the second contribution only:

\hskip-8.5359ptP_{-}(t_{2})-P_{-}(t_{1})\leq\int_{-\infty}^{0}\!\!dp\int_{0}^{-\frac{pT}{m}}\!\!dx\,f(x,p,t_{1})\leq\tilde{P}_{-}\,.

(1)

Here,
\tilde{P}_{-}=\operatorname{Prob}(p<0)
is the probability of negative momentum, which remains time independent under free evolution. The second inequality follows by extending the
x
-integration limits to the entire real line and using the non-negativity of the probability density
f
.

Inequality (
1
) is expressed solely in terms of measurable position and momentum marginals, making it directly comparable to quantum results. For
\tilde{P}_{-}=0
, it reduces to the familiar statement that the probability of finding a right-moving particle to the left of the origin cannot increase with time. In its general form, however, the inequality makes no assumptions about the marginals and simply bounds the position-probability gain by the probability of negative momentum. Any violation of this bound—shown below to be possible for quantum wave packets—therefore signals a nonclassical transport effect where the particle moves backward more than is permitted by its momentum distribution.

The momentum probability in (
1
) can be traded for a position probability to produce a generalization of the reentry problem
Goussev 2019
. Consider a finite interval along the
x
axis,
r=(-s,0)
for concreteness, and define
P_{r}(t)=\operatorname{Prob}(x\in r,t)
as the probability for the particle to be inside
r
at time
t
. The difference
P_{r}(t_{2})-P_{r}(t_{1})
quantifies the fraction of particles that have entered
r
between times
t_{1}
and
t_{2}>t_{1}
. Since classical particles can only enter from outside the region, this increase cannot exceed the total fraction of particles that were outside
r
at any earlier time
t_{0}<t_{1}
, namely
1-P_{r}(t_{0})
. This leads to the following inequality characterizing the impossibility of reentry for classical particles:

P_{r}(t_{2})-P_{r}(t_{1})\leq 1-P_{r}(t_{0})\,,

(2)

which holds for any
t_{0}<t_{1}<t_{2}
.

Simple example.
We now demonstrate that the classical bounds expressed in inequalities (
1
) and (
2
) can be violated by quantum particles. Consider a wave function consisting of a superposition of two Gaussian wave packets,
\Psi(x,t)=\sum_{n=1,2}c_{n}g_{n}(x,t)
. This type of superposition was previously analyzed in Ref.
Yearsley et al. 2012
for real coefficients
c_{n}
and Gaussians
g_{n}(x,t)
with identical widths, yielding quantum backflow of
0.0061
. Although strictly nonzero, the corresponding probability of negative momentum was found to be small,
\tilde{P}_{-}\approx 10^{-10}
, and was therefore neglected.

To systematically study the effect of negative momentum, we define

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathrm{QB}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{-}(t_{2})-P_{-}(t_{1})-\tilde{P}_{-}\,,

(3)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathrm{RE}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{-}(t_{2})-P_{-}(t_{1})+P_{-}(t_{0})-1\,,

(4)

which, according to Eqs. (
1
) and (
2
), cannot exceed zero in classical mechanics. Details of maximizing these quantities over the set of superposed Gaussian states are provided in the Supplemental Material (SM). Using the same assumptions as in Ref.
Yearsley et al. 2012
, we find that the generalized quantum backflow reaches
\Delta_{\mathrm{QB}}=0.0106
. Relaxing the assumptions on the Gaussian superposition increases the maximum backflow to
\Delta_{\mathrm{QB}}=0.0120
. The same enhancements are observed for quantum reentry,
\Delta_{\mathrm{RE}}
. While these values remain modest, they clearly demonstrate that incorporating negative-momentum components allows substantially larger violations of classical probability bounds.

Large backflow example.
The previous illustration showed that general quantum backflow can exceed the magnitude observed in the standard scenario. Remarkably, general backflow can even significantly surpass the Bracken–Melloy constant,
c_{\text{BM}}=0.0384506
. Inspired by Ref.
Halliwell et al. 2013
, we construct an explicit example using the momentum-space wave function
\tilde{\psi}(p)=\Theta(p)a_{1}(b_{1}-p)e^{-c_{1}^{2}p^{2}}+\Theta(-p)a_{2}(b_{2}-p)e^{-c_{2}^{2}p^{2}}
, where
a
’s,
b
’s, and
c
’s are constants, and
\Theta(\cdot)
is the Heaviside step function. With a suitable choice of parameters, this state achieves a general backflow of
\Delta_{\mathrm{QB}}=0.0624
. There also exists a corresponding state exhibiting the same value of
\Delta_{\mathrm{RE}}
. Detailed derivations and parameter choices are provided in the SM.

Unified approach.
We now demonstrate that the expressions for
\Delta_{\mathrm{QB}}
and
\Delta_{\mathrm{RE}}
with the range
r=(-\infty,0)
can be cast in the same mathematical form. For general backflow, we write the time-dependent wave function of a free particle as

\Psi(x,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\hbar}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dp\,\tilde{\psi}(p)\exp\left(-\frac{it}{2m\hbar}p^{2}+\frac{ix}{\hbar}p\right),

(5)

where
\tilde{\psi}(p)
is the normalized momentum-space wave function at
t=0
. The relevant probabilities in Eq. (
3
) are then

P_{-}(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{0}dx\,|\Psi(x,t)|^{2}\,,\quad\tilde{P}_{-}=\int_{-\infty}^{0}dp\,\big|\tilde{\psi}(p)\big|^{2}\,.

(6)

Following the approach of Ref.
Bracken and Melloy 1994
, detailed for convenience in the SM, we find that
\Delta_{\mathrm{QB}}=\Delta
, where

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du^{\prime}\,\varphi^{*}(u)K(u,u^{\prime})\varphi(u^{\prime})\,,

(7)

\displaystyle K(u,u^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\sin\left(u^{2}-{u^{\prime}}^{2}\right)}{\pi(u-u^{\prime})}-\Theta(-u)\delta(u-u^{\prime})\,,

(8)

and

\varphi(u)=\left(\frac{4\hbar m}{t_{2}-t_{1}}\right)^{\!\!1/4}\exp\!\left(-i\frac{t_{2}+t_{1}}{t_{2}-t_{1}}u^{2}\right)\tilde{\psi}\!\left(u\sqrt{\frac{4\hbar m}{t_{2}-t_{1}}}\right)

(9)

Here,
\delta(\cdot)
denotes the Dirac delta function, and
\varphi(u)
is a rescaled momentum-space wave function normalized as
\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du\,|\varphi(u)|^{2}=1
.

For quantum reentry with
r=(-\infty,0)
, we express
\Psi(x,t)
via the free-particle propagator

\Psi(x,t)=\sqrt{\frac{m}{2\pi i\hbar\tau}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx^{\prime}\,\exp\!\left(i\frac{m(x-x^{\prime})^{2}}{2\hbar\tau}\right)\psi(x^{\prime})\,,

(10)

where
\tau=t-t_{0}
, and
\psi(x)
is the position-space wave function at
t=t_{0}
. Following the method of Ref.
Goussev 2019
, detailed in the SM,
\Delta_{\mathrm{RE}}
can be expressed in the same form,
\Delta_{\mathrm{RE}}=\Delta
, with
\varphi(u)
now representing a rescaled position-space wave function:

\displaystyle\varphi(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{4\hbar\tau_{2}\tau_{1}}{m(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})}\right)^{\!\!1/4}\exp\!\left(i\frac{\tau_{2}+\tau_{1}}{\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}}u^{2}\right)

(11)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\psi\!\left(-u\sqrt{\frac{4\hbar\tau_{2}\tau_{1}}{m(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})}}\right)

where
\tau_{1}=t_{1}-t_{0}
and
\tau_{2}=t_{2}-t_{0}
.

Thus,
\Delta
captures the probability transfer in both the general backflow and reentry scenarios. Compared with the standard backflow
Bracken and Melloy 1994
and reentry
Goussev 2019
formulations, two key differences appear: the integrals over
u
and
u^{\prime}
extend over the entire real line, and the kernel
K(u,u^{\prime})
includes an additional term,
-\Theta(-u)\delta(u-u^{\prime})
. These modifications account for the larger values of
\Delta
observed in the general setting. In the SM we also give a perturbative argument, based on modifications of the state maximizing standard positive-momentum backflow, showing that
\Delta
can exceed the Bracken–Melloy limit.

Supremum of
\Delta
.
The maximal quantum violation of the classical inequalities (
1
) and (
2
) is obtained by optimizing
\Delta
over all normalized functions
\varphi(u)
. Following Ref.
Bracken and Melloy 1994
, this is achieved by performing an unconstrained optimization of
\Delta+\lambda\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du\,|\varphi(u)|^{2}
, where
\lambda
is a Lagrange multiplier. The resulting Euler-Lagrange equation reads

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du^{\prime}\,K(u,u^{\prime})\varphi(u^{\prime})=\lambda\,\varphi(u).

(12)

so that the supremum of
\Delta
corresponds to the largest eigenvalue of the integral operator:
\sup_{\varphi}\Delta=\sup\{\lambda\}
.

To evaluate
\sup_{\varphi}\Delta
numerically, we first truncate the integration domain in Eq. (
12
) to
(-L,L)
, giving eigenvalues
\lambda_{L}
such that
\lambda=\lim_{L\to\infty}\lambda_{L}
. We then discretize
(-L,L)
into
2N
intervals, replacing the integral with a finite sum, thereby converting the integral eigenproblem into a matrix eigenproblem with eigenvalues
\lambda_{L,N}
, which satisfy
\lim_{N\to\infty}\lambda_{L,N}=\lambda_{L}
.

In our calculations,
L
was varied from
10
to
40
in steps of
5
, and for each
L
,
N
was increased from hundreds to thousands to determine the maximal
\lambda_{L,N}
. For fixed
L
, the maximal
\lambda_{L,N}
scales approximately linearly with
1/N
; we therefore estimate the limit
N\to\infty
and obtain
\lambda_{L}
by fitting a linear function to the computed eigenvalues. The optimal fit parameters and associated uncertainties are obtained via standard linear regression
Bevington and Robinson 2003
. Similarly, the maximal
\lambda_{L}
scales approximately linearly with
1/L
, allowing us to estimate the limit
L\to\infty
and its uncertainty. The resulting value, with one standard deviation error, is

\sup\Delta=0.128100\pm 0.000002\,.

(13)

Comprehensive details of the numerical analysis are provided in the SM.

Analytical bounds.
Analytical lower and upper bounds on
\sup\Delta
can be inferred from the results of Refs.
Trillo et al. 2023
;
Zaw et al. 2022
;
Zaw and Scarani 2025
. Let us express
\tilde{P}_{-}=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\,\Theta(-\hat{p}))
, where
\rho
is the initial density matrix of the particle and
\hat{p}
is the momentum operator. The probability of finding the particle at negative positions at time
t
is then
P_{-}(t)=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{t}\,\Theta(-\hat{x}))=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\,\Theta(-\hat{x}-\hat{p}t/m))
, where
\hat{x}
is the position operator,
\rho_{t}=U\rho U^{\dagger}
, and
U=\exp(-i t \hat{p}^2 / 2 \hbar m)
is the free-evolution operator.
For the case
m=1
,
t_{1}=0
, and
t_{2}=1
, Eq. (
3
) can be written as
\Delta_{\mathrm{QB}}=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\,\Omega)
, with
\Omega=\Theta(-\hat{x}-\hat{p})-\Theta(-\hat{x})-\Theta(-\hat{p})
. The operator
\Omega
was introduced in Ref.
Trillo et al. 2023
to estimate the transport efficiency of quantum projectiles with the same position and momentum marginals as their classical counterparts. (These assumptions are not present in our analysis.)

The best known analytical bounds on the maximal eigenvalue of
\Omega
were established by connecting it to Tsirelson’s precession problem
Tsirelson
, and applying very recent results of Ref.
Zaw et al. 2022
;
Zaw and Scarani 2025
. This shows that
0.128092\leq\sup\Delta\leq 0.192466
, exceeding the Bracken-Melloy bound
c_{\text{BM}}\simeq 0.0384506
by more than a factor of three, and with the lower bound coming remarkably close to our estimate, given by Eq. (
13
).

Discussion.
We conclude with several remarks on our results and their foundational implications. The central outcome of this work is a general characterization of quantum backflow and reentry for arbitrary wave functions, without imposing constraints on their momentum or position marginals.

In the general backflow formulation, we considered the probability of locating the particle to the left of the origin. Naturally, the origin can be replaced by any point
b
on the
x
axis. Quantum backflow occurs whenever
\Delta_{\text{QB}}=\operatorname{Prob}(x<b,t_{2})-\operatorname{Prob}(x<b,t_{1})-\tilde{P}_{-}
[c.f. Eq. (
3
)] becomes positive. Similarly, one can define quantum “forwardflow”, which occurs when
\Delta_{\text{QF}}=\operatorname{Prob}(x>f,t_{2})-\operatorname{Prob}(x>f,t_{1})-\tilde{P}_{+}
is positive, with
f
an arbitrary point and
\tilde{P}_{+}=1-\tilde{P}_{-}
the probability of positive momentum. This raises a question: Can a quantum particle simultaneously exhibit backflow and forwardflow? In other words, does there exist a wave function for which
\Delta_{\text{QB}}>0
and
\Delta_{\text{QF}}>0
for some choice of parameters? The answer appears to be negative. It can be shown (see the SM for details) that
\Delta_{\mathrm{QB}}+\Delta_{\mathrm{QF}}\leq 0
for all choices of parameters, revealing a trade-off and mutual incompatibility between these two effects.

The Bracken–Melloy constant
c_{\text{BM}}
characterizes maximal standard (positive-momentum) backflow and is independent of the particle’s mass, the backflow duration, and Planck’s constant. It was proposed to reflect the mathematical structure of quantum free-particle evolution
Bracken and Melloy 1994
. The same properties hold for the general backflow constant
\sup\Delta
, given by Eq. (
13
), suggesting that quantum free evolution is characterized by at least two fundamental dimensionless constants. Understanding the relation between them presents a promising direction for future research.

Finally, we comment on the foundational implications of quantum backflow and reentry. The classical limits, Eqs. (
1
) and (
2
), rely on two assumptions: (i) the existence of a joint probability density
f(x,p,t)
, and (ii) the validity of classical equations of motion for a free particle,
x_{t}=x_{0}+pt/m
and
p_{t}=\text{const}
. Quantum backflow or reentry violates at least one of these assumptions and accordingly can be interpreted in different ways. Relaxing (i) while keeping (ii) leads to pseudo-probability frameworks, such as the Wigner function formalism, in which backflow emerges as negative probability transfer of particles in straight line motion
Bracken and Melloy 1994
;
Yearsley et al. 2012
. Conversely, retaining (i) and relaxing (ii) leads to Bohmian mechanics, where even free particles follow curved trajectories due to an effective nonlocal potential
Muga and Leavens 2000
;
Penz et al. 2006
.

In summary, the methods developed here provide a realistic route toward experimental observation of quantum backflow and reentry, while raising several fundamental and practical questions for further exploration.

Rescaling of general backflow

\Psi(x,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\hbar}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dp\,\tilde{\psi}(p)\exp\!\left(-\frac{it}{2m\hbar}p^{2}+\frac{ix}{\hbar}p\right)\,,

where
\tilde{\psi}(p)
is the momentum-space wave function at time
t=0
. The latter satisfies the normalization condition

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dp\,\big|\tilde{\psi}(p)\big|^{2}=1\,.

The associated probability current through
x=0
at time
t
is given by

\displaystyle j(0,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar}{m}\left.\operatorname{Im}\!\left\{\Psi^{*}(x,t)\frac{\partial\Psi(x,t)}{\partial x}\right\}\right|_{x=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi\hbar m}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dp\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dp^{\prime}\,\left(p+p^{\prime}\right)\tilde{\psi}(p)^{*}\tilde{\psi}\left(p^{\prime}\right)\exp\!\left[\frac{it}{2\hbar m}\left(p^{2}-{p^{\prime}}^{2}\right)\right]\,.

The probability transfer into the region
x<0
during a time interval
(t_{1},t_{2})
, with
t_{1}<t_{2}
, is

\displaystyle P_{-}(t_{2})-P_{-}(t_{1})

\displaystyle=-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}dt\,j(0,t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4\pi\hbar m}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dp\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dp^{\prime}\,\left(p+p^{\prime}\right)\tilde{\psi}(p)^{*}\tilde{\psi}\left(p^{\prime}\right)\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}dt\,\exp\!\left[\frac{it}{2\hbar m}\left(p^{2}-{p^{\prime}}^{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dp\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dp^{\prime}\,\left(p+p^{\prime}\right)\tilde{\psi}(p)^{*}\tilde{\psi}\left(p^{\prime}\right)\frac{\exp\!\left[\frac{it_{2}}{2\hbar m}(p^{2}-{p^{\prime}}^{2})\right]-\exp\!\left[\frac{it_{1}}{2\hbar m}(p^{2}-{p^{\prime}}^{2})\right]}{p^{2}-p^{\prime 2}}\,.

u=p\sqrt{\frac{T}{4\hbar m}}\,,\quad u^{\prime}=p^{\prime}\sqrt{\frac{T}{4\hbar m}}\,,

we get

P_{-}(t_{2})-P_{-}(t_{1})=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du^{\prime}\,\sqrt{\frac{4\hbar m}{T}}\,\tilde{\psi}\!\left(u\sqrt{\frac{4\hbar m}{T}}\right)^{\!\!*}\tilde{\psi}\!\left(u^{\prime}\sqrt{\frac{4\hbar m}{T}}\right)\exp\!\left[i\frac{2\bar{t}}{T}\!\left(u^{2}-u^{\prime 2}\right)\right]\frac{\sin(u^2 - u'^2)}{u-u^{\prime}}\,.

We now introduce the rescaled momentum-space wave function

\displaystyle\varphi(u)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{4\hbar m}{T}\right)^{\!\!1/4}\exp\!\left(-i\frac{2\bar{t}}{T}u^{2}\right)\tilde{\psi}\!\left(u\sqrt{\frac{4\hbar m}{T}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{4\hbar m}{t_{2}-t_{1}}\right)^{\!\!1/4}\exp\!\left(-i\frac{t_{2}+t_{1}}{t_{2}-t_{1}}u^{2}\right)\tilde{\psi}\!\left(u\sqrt{\frac{4\hbar m}{t_{2}-t_{1}}}\right)\,.

Then,

P_{-}(t_{2})-P_{-}(t_{1})=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du^{\prime}\,\varphi(u)^{*}\varphi(u^{\prime})\frac{\sin(u^2 - u'^2)}{u-u^{\prime}}\,.

The normalization condition now reads

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du\,|\varphi(u)|^{2}=1\,,

and the probability of negative momentum becomes

\tilde{P}_{-}=\int_{-\infty}^{0}du\,|\varphi(u)|^{2}\,.

Combining the results, we find

\displaystyle\Delta_{\text{QB}}

\displaystyle=P_{-}(t_{2})-P_{-}(t_{1})-\tilde{P}_{-}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du^{\prime}\,\varphi(u)^{*}\varphi(u^{\prime})\frac{\sin(u^2 - u'^2)}{u-u^{\prime}}-\int_{-\infty}^{0}du\,|\varphi(u)|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du^{\prime}\,\varphi(u)^{*}\varphi(u^{\prime})\frac{\sin(u^2 - u'^2)}{u-u^{\prime}}-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du^{\prime}\,\varphi(u)^{*}\varphi(u^{\prime})\Theta(-u)\delta(u-u^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du^{\prime}\,\varphi(u)^{*}K(u,u^{\prime})\varphi(u^{\prime})\,,

where

K(u,u^{\prime})=-\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\sin(u^2 - u'^2)}{u-u^{\prime}}-\Theta(-u)\delta(u-u^{\prime})\,.

I.3
Rescaling of general reentry

The time-dependent wave function of a free particle can also be expressed via the free-particle propagator:

\Psi(x,t)=\sqrt{\frac{m}{2\pi i\hbar\tau}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx^{\prime}\,\exp\!\left(i\frac{m(x-x^{\prime})^{2}}{2\hbar\tau}\right)\psi(x^{\prime})\,,

where
\tau=t-t_{0}
, and
\psi(x)
is the position-space wave function at
t=t_{0}
that satisfies the normalization condition

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx\,|\psi(x)|^{2}=1\,.

In this representation, the probability current through
x=0
at time
t
becomes

\displaystyle j(0,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar}{m}\left.\operatorname{Im}\!\left\{\Psi^{*}(x,t)\frac{\partial\Psi(x,t)}{\partial x}\right\}\right|_{x=0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{m}{4\pi\hbar\tau^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx^{\prime}\,(x+x^{\prime})\exp\!\left[-i\frac{m\left(x^{2}-x^{\prime 2}\right)}{2\hbar\tau}\right]\psi(x)^{*}\psi(x^{\prime})\,.

The probability transfer into the region
x<0
during a time interval
(t_{1},t_{2})
, with
t_{1}<t_{2}
, is

\displaystyle P_{-}(t_{2})-P_{-}(t_{1})

\displaystyle=-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}dt\,j(0,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{m}{4\pi\hbar}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx^{\prime}\,(x+x^{\prime})\psi(x)^{*}\psi(x^{\prime})\int_{t_{1}-t_{0}}^{t_{2}-t_{0}}\frac{d\tau}{\tau^{2}}\exp\!\left[-i\frac{m\left(x^{2}-x^{\prime 2}\right)}{2\hbar\tau}\right]\,.

Making the substitution
z=1/\tau
, we write

\displaystyle P_{-}(t_{2})-P_{-}(t_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{m}{4\pi\hbar}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx^{\prime}\,(x+x^{\prime})\psi(x)^{*}\psi(x^{\prime})\int_{1/(t_{2}-t_{0})}^{1/(t_{1}-t_{0})}dz\,\exp\!\left[-i\frac{m\left(x^{2}-x^{\prime 2}\right)}{2\hbar}z\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx^{\prime}\,(x+x^{\prime})\psi(x)^{*}\psi(x^{\prime})\frac{\exp\!\left[-i\frac{m\left(x^{2}-x^{\prime 2}\right)}{2\hbar(t_{1}-t_{0})}\right]-\exp\!\left[-i\frac{m\left(x^{2}-x^{\prime 2}\right)}{2\hbar(t_{2}-t_{0})}\right]}{x^{2}-x^{\prime 2}}\,.

Introducing
\nu
and
\omega
such that

\frac{1}{t_{2}-t_{0}}=\nu-\frac{\omega}{2}\,,\quad\frac{1}{t_{1}-t_{0}}=\nu+\frac{\omega}{2}\,,

and, consequently,

\nu=\frac{t_{2}+t_{1}-2t_{0}}{2(t_{2}-t_{0})(t_{1}-t_{0})}\,,\quad\omega=\frac{t_{2}-t_{1}}{(t_{2}-t_{0})(t_{1}-t_{0})}\,,

we get

\displaystyle P_{-}(t_{2})-P_{-}(t_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx^{\prime}\,\psi(x)^{*}\psi(x^{\prime})\exp\!\left[-i\frac{m\left(x^{2}-x^{\prime 2}\right)}{2\hbar}\nu\right]\frac{\exp\!\left[-i\frac{m\left(x^{2}-x^{\prime 2}\right)}{4\hbar}\omega\right]-\exp\!\left[i\frac{m\left(x^{2}-x^{\prime 2}\right)}{4\hbar}\omega\right]}{x-x^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx^{\prime}\,\psi(x)^{*}\psi(x^{\prime})\exp\!\left[-i\frac{m\left(x^{2}-x^{\prime 2}\right)}{2\hbar}\nu\right]\frac{\sin\!\left[\frac{m\left(x^{2}-x^{\prime 2}\right)}{4\hbar}\omega\right]}{x-x^{\prime}}\,.

We then change the integration variables as

\displaystyle u=-x\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{4\hbar}}\,,\quad u^{\prime}=-x^{\prime}\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{4\hbar}}\,.

This yields

P_{-}(t_{2})-P_{-}(t_{1})=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du^{\prime}\,\sqrt{\frac{4\hbar}{m\omega}}\psi\!\left(-u\sqrt{\frac{4\hbar}{m\omega}}\right)^{\!\!*}\psi\!\left(-u^{\prime}\sqrt{\frac{4\hbar}{m\omega}}\right)\exp\!\left[-i\frac{2\nu}{\omega}\left(u^{2}-u^{\prime 2}\right)\right]\frac{\sin(u^2 - u'^2)}{u-u^{\prime}}\,.

Now, defining

\displaystyle\varphi(u)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{4\hbar}{m\omega}\right)^{\!\!1/4}\exp\!\left(i\frac{2\nu}{\omega}u^{2}\right)\psi\!\left(-u\sqrt{\frac{4\hbar}{m\omega}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{4\hbar(t_{2}-t_{0})(t_{1}-t_{0})}{m(t_{2}-t_{1})}\right)^{\!\!1/4}\exp\!\left(i\frac{t_{2}+t_{1}-2t_{0}}{t_{2}-t_{1}}u^{2}\right)\psi\!\left(-u\sqrt{\frac{4\hbar(t_{2}-t_{0})(t_{1}-t_{0})}{m(t_{2}-t_{1})}}\right)\,,

we obtain

P_{-}(t_{2})-P_{-}(t_{1})=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du^{\prime}\,\varphi(u)^{*}\varphi(u^{\prime})\frac{\sin(u^2 - u'^2)}{u-u^{\prime}}\,.

In view of the normalization of
\psi(x)
, the rescaled wave function
\varphi(u)
is normalized to unity,

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du\,|\varphi(u)|^{2}=1\,.

Finally, since

\displaystyle 1-P_{-}(t_{0})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}dx\,|\psi(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{0}du\,|\varphi(u)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du^{\prime}\,\Theta(-u)\delta(u-u^{\prime})\varphi(u)^{*}\varphi(u^{\prime})\,,

we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta_{\text{RE}}

\displaystyle=P_{-}(t_{2})-P_{-}(t_{1})-\big(1-P_{-}(t_{0})\big)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du^{\prime}\,\varphi(u)^{*}K(u,u^{\prime})\varphi(u^{\prime})\,,

where

K(u,u^{\prime})=-\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\sin(u^2 - u'^2)}{u-u^{\prime}}-\Theta(-u)\delta(u-u^{\prime})\,.

I.4
Superposition of two Gaussians

Figure 3:
Approximation to probability current in standard backflow. The plot shows the probability current, label
J
, and the simple approximation given by Eq. (
14
), label
J_{s}
, for the optimal parameters of Ref.

. The backflow interval is marked with light blue area. The two curves are practically indistinguishable up to

For comparison with the standard backflow, we first derive a simple approximation to the probability current.
Consider the following quantum state at time
t
:

\displaystyle\psi(x,t)=c_{1}g_{1}(x,t)+c_{2}g_{2}(x,t)\,,

where
|c_{1}|^{2}+|c_{2}|^{2}=1
, and the free-particle Gaussian wave functions read

g_{n}(x,t)=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi\sigma_{n}^{2}(1+i\omega_{n}t)^{2}}\right)^{1/4}\exp\left(-\frac{(x-p_{n}t/m)^{2}}{4\sigma_{n}^{2}(1+i\omega_{n}t)}\right)\exp\left(\frac{i}{\hbar}p_{n}x\right)\exp\left(-\frac{it}{\hbar}\frac{p_{n}^{2}}{2m}\right)\,,\qquad\omega_{n}=\frac{\hbar}{2m\sigma_{n}^{2}}\,.

Here,
p_{n}
is the mean momentum of the
n
th Gaussian, and
\sigma_{n}
is its standard deviation.
The probability current at
x=0
and time
t
can be written as

j(0,t)=j_{1}(0,t)+j_{2}(0,t)+j_{\crossproduct}(0,t)\,,

with the contributions from the individual Gaussians given by

j_{n}(0,t)=\frac{p_{n}}{m}|c_{n}|^{2}|g_{n}(0,t)|^{2}\frac{1}{1+\omega_{n}^{2}t^{2}}\,,

and the cross-term:

\displaystyle j_{\crossproduct}(0,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|z|}{m}\cos\varphi\left(\frac{p_{2}}{1+\omega_{2}^{2}t^{2}}+\frac{p_{1}}{1+\omega_{1}^{2}t^{2}}\right)+\frac{|z|}{m}\sin\varphi\left(\frac{p_{2}\omega_{2}t}{1+\omega_{2}^{2}t^{2}}-\frac{p_{1}\omega_{1}t}{1+\omega_{1}^{2}t^{2}}\right),

where
|z|e^{i\varphi}=c_{1}^{*}c_{2}g_{1}(0,t)^{*}g_{2}(0,t)
.

For comparison with Ref.
Yearsley et al. 2012
we assume that
\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}\equiv\sigma
, hence
\omega_{1}=\omega_{2}\equiv\omega
, and that coefficients
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are purely real.
In this case,

\displaystyle|z|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{1}c_{2}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi\sigma^{2}(1+\omega^{2}t^{2})}\right)^{1/2}\exp\left(-\frac{(p_{1}t/m)^{2}+(p_{2}t/m)^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}(1+\omega^{2}t^{2})}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(p_{1}^{2}-p_{2}^{2})t}{2\hbar m(1+\omega^{2}t^{2})}\,.

The current admits a simple form under further assumption that
\omega t\ll 1
. It turns out that this is not restrictive and includes maximal standard backflow identified in
Yearsley et al. 2012
, as shown in Fig.
3
. The current reads:

\displaystyle j_{s}=c_{1}^{2}\,\frac{p_{1}}{m}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi\sigma^{2}}\right)^{1/2}\exp\left(-\frac{(p_{1}t/m)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)+c_{2}^{2}\,\frac{p_{2}}{m}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi\sigma^{2}}\right)^{1/2}\exp\left(-\frac{(p_{2}t/m)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle+c_{1}c_{2}\,\frac{p_{1}+p_{2}}{m}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi\sigma^{2}}\right)^{1/2}\exp\left(-\frac{(p_{2}t/m)^{2}+(p_{1}t/m)^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}\right)\cos\left(\frac{(p_{1}^{2}-p_{2}^{2})t}{2m}\right)\,.

(14)

Using these assumptions on the parameters, we numerically explored the general backflow quantity
\Delta_{\mathrm{QB}}=-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}dt\,j(0,t)-\tilde{P}_{-}
and found the quoted value of
0.0106
attained for
c_{1}=1.8
,
c_{2}=1
,
\sigma=1.6
,
p_{1}=1
,
p_{2}=5.7
,
t_{1}=0.152246
, and
t_{2}=0.246813
.

For the full optimization of
\Delta_{\mathrm{QB}}
, as well as of
\Delta_{\mathrm{RE}}
, over the most general superposition of two Gaussian packets (without the assumption considered above), we write the problem in the dimensionless form:

\Delta=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du^{\prime}\,\varphi(u)^{*}\frac{\sin(u^2 - u'^2)}{u-u^{\prime}}\varphi(u^{\prime})-\int_{-\infty}^{0}du\,|\varphi(u)|^{2}

with

\varphi(u)=C\Big(e^{-a_{1}(u-b_{1})^{2}}\cos\alpha+e^{-a_{2}(u-b_{2})^{2}}e^{i\beta}\sin\alpha\Big)\,.

Here,
a_{1},b_{1},a_{2},b_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
are parameters, and the constant
C
, given by

\displaystyle C^{-2}

\displaystyle=\cos^{2}\alpha\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2\operatorname{Re}a_{1}}}\exp\left[\frac{2(\operatorname{Re}a_{1}b_{1})^{2}}{\operatorname{Re}a_{1}}-2(\operatorname{Re}a_{1}b_{1}^{2})\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\sin^{2}\alpha\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2\operatorname{Re}a_{2}}}\exp\left[\frac{2(\operatorname{Re}a_{2}b_{2})^{2}}{\operatorname{Re}a_{2}}-2(\operatorname{Re}a_{2}b_{2}^{2})\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\sin 2\alpha\operatorname{Re}\left\{\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{a_{1}+a_{2}^{*}}}\exp\left[\frac{(a_{1}b_{1}+a_{2}^{*}b_{2}^{*})^{2}}{a_{1}+a_{2}^{*}}-a_{1}b_{1}^{2}-(a_{2}b_{2}^{2})^{*}-i\beta\right]\right\}\,,

ensures the normalization condition
\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du\,|\varphi(u)|^{2}=1
. The function
\varphi(u)
represents the rescaled momentum-space wave function of the particle in the case of general backflow, and the rescaled position-space wave function in the case of general reentry. Performing a numerical optimization over the space of parameters
a_{1},b_{1},a_{2},b_{2},\alpha,\beta
, we find a (local) maximum with
\Delta=0.012011
, attained for
a_{1}=28.95
,
b_{1}=0.8479
,
a_{2}=83.28
,
b_{2}=0.1637
,
\alpha=1.271
, and
\beta=\pi
.

I.5
Perturbative argument

Here we construct a state
\varphi(u)
that satisfies the normalization condition

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du\,|\varphi(u)|^{2}=1

and is accompanied by

\Delta=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\,\varphi^{*}(u)\frac{\sin(u^2 - v^2)}{u-v}\varphi(v)-\int_{-\infty}^{0}du\,|\varphi(u)|^{2}

larger than the Bracken-Melloy constant
c_{\text{BM}}\simeq 0.03845
.

Consider two real-valued functions
f,g:[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
, and let
\varphi(u)
be given by

\varphi(u)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f(u)&\text{if }u>0\\[2.84544pt]
g(-u)&\text{if }u<0\end{array}\right.

The normalization condition takes the form

\int_{0}^{\infty}du\,f(u)^{2}+\int_{0}^{\infty}du\,g(u)^{2}=1\,,

(15)

and
\Delta
reads

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}du\int_{0}^{\infty}dv\,f(u)\frac{\sin(u^2 - v^2)}{u-v}f(v)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}du\int_{0}^{\infty}dv\,g(u)\frac{\sin(u^2 - v^2)}{u-v}g(v)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}du\int_{0}^{\infty}dv\,f(u)\frac{\sin(u^2 - v^2)}{u+v}g(v)-\int_{0}^{\infty}du\,g(u)^{2}\,.

(16)

Consider
F(u)
to be the backflow-maximizing state in the standard Bracken-Melloy problem:

-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}du\int_{0}^{\infty}dv\,F(u)\frac{\sin(u^2 - v^2)}{u-v}F(v)=c_{\text{BM}}\,,\qquad\int_{0}^{\infty}du\,F(u)^{2}=1\,.

We superpose it with yet-to-be-specified function
G(u)
such that

\int_{0}^{\infty}du\,G(u)^{2}=1\,,

as follows:

f(u)=\sqrt{1-\epsilon^{2}}F(u)\,,\qquad g(u)=\epsilon G(u)\,,

where
0\leq\epsilon\ll 1
. Notice that the normalization condition, Eq. (
15
), is automatically fulfilled. Equation (
16
) now reads

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle=(1-\epsilon^{2})c_{\text{BM}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}du\int_{0}^{\infty}dv\,G(u)\frac{\sin(u^2 - v^2)}{u-v}G(v)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{2\epsilon\sqrt{1-\epsilon^{2}}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}du\int_{0}^{\infty}dv\,F(u)\frac{\sin(u^2 - v^2)}{u+v}G(v)-\epsilon^{2}\,,

or

\Delta=c_{\text{BM}}+\epsilon I+O(\epsilon^{2})\,,

where

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}dvH(v)G(v)\qquad\text{with}\qquad H(v)\equiv-\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}du\,F(u)\frac{\sin(u^2 - v^2)}{u+v}\,.

The function
G(u)
can be always chosen such that
I>0
. Subsequently, the positivity of
I
implies that
\Delta>c_{\text{BM}}
for a sufficiently small
\epsilon
.

I.6
Example of large general backflow

Here we present a state that admits general backflow (or reentry) exceeding the Bracken-Melloy constant. We employ the unified framework in which both
\Delta_{\text{QB}}
and
\Delta_{\text{RE}}
are represented by

\Delta=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du^{\prime}\,\varphi(u)^{*}\frac{\sin(u^2 - u'^2)}{u-u^{\prime}}\varphi(u^{\prime})-\int_{-\infty}^{0}du\,|\varphi(u)|^{2}\,,

where
\varphi(u)
denotes the rescaled momentum-space wave function of the particle in the case of general backflow, and the rescaled position-space wave function in the case of general reentry.

Motivated by Ref.
Halliwell et al. 2013
, we consider the state

\varphi(u)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C\cos\alpha\,(\beta_{1}-u)e^{-\gamma_{1}^{2}u^{2}}&\text{if}\quad u>0\\[5.69046pt]
C\sin\alpha\,(\beta_{2}-u)e^{-\gamma_{2}^{2}u^{2}}&\text{if}\quad u<0\end{array}\right.

where
\alpha,\beta_{1},\gamma_{1},\beta_{2},\gamma_{2}
are real parameters, and
C
is the normalization constant ensuring
\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du\,|\varphi(u)|^{2}=1
. By computing both
C
and
\Delta
numerically, we perform a numerical optimization of
\Delta
over the parameter space and find a (local) maximum of
\Delta=0.0624188
, attained for
\alpha=0.1654
,
\beta_{1}=0.5641
,
\gamma_{1}=1.265
,
\beta_{2}=0.03529
, and
\gamma_{2}=0.6365
.

I.7
Backflow and forwardflow

Recall the parameter characterizing general backflow for any point
b
on the
x
axis:

\Delta_{\text{QB}}=\mathrm{Prob}(x<b,t_{2})-\mathrm{Prob}(x<b,t_{1})-\tilde{P}_{-}\,.

(17)

Similarly, we introduced the parameter quantifying forwardflow by

\Delta_{\text{QF}}=\mathrm{Prob}(x>f,t_{2})-\mathrm{Prob}(x>f,t_{1})-\tilde{P}_{+}\,,

(18)

where
f
is an arbitrary point and
\tilde{P}_{+}=1-\tilde{P}_{-}
is the probability of positive momentum.
We now show that both of these quantities cannot be simultaneously positive and, in fact, the following trade-off relation holds:

\Delta_{\mathrm{QB}}+\Delta_{\mathrm{QF}}\leq 0\,.

Indeed, summing up Eqs. (
17
) and (
18
), we note that the momentum probabilities add to
-1
, and the remaining terms are, at any time
t
, of the form:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Prob}(x<b,t)+\mathrm{Prob}(x>f,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\mathrm{Prob}[x\in(b,f),t]\quad\textrm{if}\quad b\leq f\,,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Prob}(x<b,t)+\mathrm{Prob}(x>f,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\mathrm{Prob}[x\in(b,f),t]\quad\textrm{if}\quad f<b\,.

Therefore,

\Delta_{\mathrm{QB}}+\Delta_{\mathrm{QF}}=\pm\mathrm{Prob}[x\in(b,f),t_{2}]\mp\mathrm{Prob}[x\in(b,f),t_{1}]-1\leq 0\,,

because each probability satisfies
0\leq\mathrm{Prob}[x\in(b,f),t]\leq 1
.

I.8
Numerical evaluation of
\sup\Delta

Here, we present details of the numerical estimation of
\sup\{\lambda\}
(
=\sup\Delta
) in the eigenproblem

-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\,(u+v)\operatorname{sinc}(u^{2}-v^{2})\varphi(v)-\Theta(-u)\varphi(u)=\lambda\,\varphi(u)\,,

(19)

where
\operatorname{sinc}(z)\equiv\sin(z)/z
. Truncating the integral, we obtain a new eigenproblem,

-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-L}^{L}dv\,(u+v)\operatorname{sinc}(u^{2}-v^{2})f(v)-\Theta(-u)f(u)=\lambda_{L}f(u)\,.

Subsequently, discretizing the integral yields yet another eigenproblem,

\sum_{m=-N}^{N}K_{nm}g_{m}=\lambda_{L,N}g_{n}

(20)

with

K_{nm}=-\frac{L^{2}(n+m)}{\pi N^{2}}\operatorname{sinc}\frac{L^{2}(n^{2}-m^{2})}{N^{2}}-\Theta(-n)\delta_{nm}\,.

\sup\{\lambda\}
is based on the assumption that

\sup\{\lambda\}=\lim_{L\to\infty}\sup\{\lambda_{L}\}=\lim_{L\to\infty}\lim_{N\to\infty}\max\{\lambda_{L,N}\}\,.

(21)

\max\{\lambda_{L,N}\}

L
, we perform an extrapolation as
N\to\infty
to evaluate the corresponding value of
\sup\{\lambda_{L}\}
. All data in intermediate calculations are presented with full precision (of 16 significant figures), and rounding is applied only to the final result.

We now fit
\sup\{\lambda_{L}\}
to a straight line in
1/L
:

\sup\{\lambda_{L}\}=a+\frac{b}{L}\,.

The optimal fitting parameters are given by

a=\frac{\left(\sum\frac{x_{j}^{2}}{\sigma_{j}^{2}}\right)\left(\sum\frac{y_{j}}{\sigma_{j}^{2}}\right)-\left(\sum\frac{x_{j}}{\sigma_{j}^{2}}\right)\left(\sum\frac{x_{j}y_{j}}{\sigma_{j}^{2}}\right)}{\left(\sum\frac{1}{\sigma_{j}^{2}}\right)\left(\sum\frac{x_{j}^{2}}{\sigma_{j}^{2}}\right)-\left(\sum\frac{x_{j}}{\sigma_{j}^{2}}\right)^{2}}\,,\qquad b=\frac{\left(\sum\frac{1}{\sigma_{j}^{2}}\right)\left(\sum\frac{x_{j}y_{j}}{\sigma_{j}^{2}}\right)-\left(\sum\frac{x_{j}}{\sigma_{j}^{2}}\right)\left(\sum\frac{y_{j}}{\sigma_{j}^{2}}\right)}{\left(\sum\frac{1}{\sigma_{j}^{2}}\right)\left(\sum\frac{x_{j}^{2}}{\sigma_{j}^{2}}\right)-\left(\sum\frac{x_{j}}{\sigma_{j}^{2}}\right)^{2}}\,,

where, for brevity,
x_{j}=L_{j}
,
y_{j}=\sup\{\lambda_{L}\}_{j}
, and
\sigma_{j}=\left(\sigma_{\sup\{\lambda_{L}\}}\right)_{j}
(see Table
8
). In the present case, these formulas yield

a=0.1280997589328653\,,\qquad b=-9.050752023369246\times 10^{-2}\,.

DC-LA: Difference-of-Convex Langevin Algorithm
Hoang Phuc Hau Luu, Zhongjian Wang

Abstract
We study a sampling problem whose target distribution is π ∝ exp ⁡ ( − f − r ) \pi\propto\exp(-f-r) where the data fidelity term f f is Lipschitz smooth while the regularizer term r = r 1 − r 2 r=r_{1}-r_{2} is a non-smooth difference-of-convex (DC) function, i.e., r 1 , r 2 r_{1},r_{2} are convex. By leveraging the DC structure of r r , we can smooth out r r by applying Moreau envelopes to r 1 r_{1} and r 2 r_{2} separately. In line of DC programming, we then redistribute the concave part of the regularizer to the data fidelity and study its corresponding proximal Langevin algorithm (termed DC-LA). We establish convergence of DC-LA to the target distribution π \pi , up to discretization and smoothing errors, in the q q -Wasserstein distance for all q ∈ ℕ ∗ q\in\mathbb{N}^{*} , under the assumption that V V is distant dissipative. Our results improve previous work on non-log-concave sampling in terms of a more general framework and assumptions. Numerical experiments show that DC-LA produces accurate distributions in synthetic settings and reliably provides uncertainty quantification in a real-world Computed Tomography application.

2.1
Lipschitz and distant dissipativity

A function
f
is called Lipschitz continuous (or
M
-Lipschitz for some
M>0
) if
|f(x)-f(y)|\leq M\|x-y\|
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

is called Lipschitz-smooth (or
L_{f}
-smooth for some
L_{f}>0
) if

\displaystyle\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\|\leq L_{f}\|x-y\|,~\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

while it is said to have (
\theta
,
M
)-Hölder continuous gradient if

\displaystyle\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\|\leq M\|x-y\|^{\theta},~\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Given a drift
b:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we say that
b
is distant dissipative (or weak dissipative)
(
38
;
11
)
if there exist
R\geq 0
and
m>0
such that
\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying
\|x-y\|\geq R
,

\displaystyle\langle b(x)-b(y),x-y\rangle\geq m\|x-y\|^{2}.

(5)

b
is also called
(m,R)
-distant dissipative. If
b=\nabla V
for some
V
, by abusing of terminology, we also say that
V
is distant dissipative. In our work, as
V
is not differentiable, we may extend the concept to some generalized notion of gradients (Section
3
). Note that a strongly convex function is dissipative, i.e., (
5
) holds for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

2.2
Moreau envelope and proximal operator

Given a convex function
g
and
\lambda>0
, we denote the Moreau envelope
g^{\lambda}
of
g
as

\displaystyle g^{\lambda}(x)=\inf_{y}\left\{g(y)+\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\|x-y\|^{2}\right\}

and the proximal operator of
g
as

\displaystyle\prox_{\lambda g}(x)=\argmin_{y}\left\{g(y)+\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\|x-y\|^{2}\right\}.

Note that, in this paper, we only work with Moreau envelopes and proximal operators of convex functions. In the following lemma, we summarize some known important properties of the Moreau envelope and the proximal operator
(
5
)
that are used throughout this paper.

Lemma 1
.

Let
g
be a convex function and
\lambda>0
. The following properties hold

(i)

g^{\lambda}
is convex,
\frac{1}{\lambda}
-smooth, and is a lower bound of
g
, i.e.,
g^{\lambda}(x)\leq g(x)
,
\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Furthermore, if
g
is
G
-Lipschitz,
g(x)\leq g^{\lambda}(x)+\frac{G^{2}\lambda}{2}
,
\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

(ii)

\nabla g^{\lambda}(x)=\frac{1}{\lambda}(x-\prox_{\lambda g}(x))
.

(iii)

\prox_{\lambda g}
is nonexpansive, i.e., for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\|\prox_{\lambda g}(x)-\prox_{\lambda g}(y)\|\leq\|x-y\|
.

(iv)

\nabla g^{\lambda}(x)\in\partial g(\prox_{\lambda g}(x))
where
\partial g
denotes the usual convex subdifferential of
g
.

In the definition of the ULA, one may, in practice, replace the exact gradient

\displaystyle X_{k+1}=X_{k}-\gamma b(X_{k})+\sqrt{2\gamma}Z_{k+1}.

Stability results are to establish (quantitative) bounds on the distance (e.g. Wasserstein) between the invariant distribution of ULA and the invariant distribution of (

Assumption 1

Difference of Moreau envelopes

Now we want to apply a forward-backward Langevin-type algorithm to
V
. Thanks to the DC structure of
r
, the principle of DC programming and DC algorithm
(
40
)
suggests that the forward step should apply to
f-r_{2}
while the backward step should apply to
r_{1}
for tractability and robustness. To retain a degree of smoothness in the forward step, we replace
r_{2}
with its Moreau envelope
r_{2}^{\lambda}
. This modification is necessary, as the concavity of
-r_{2}
and its associated tangent inequality are hardly sufficient to guarantee the stability results and to enable two-sided comparisons of general drifts (Subsection
4.1
). On the other hand, the analysis in
(
42
)
suggests that, if the backward step is applied to
r_{1}
,
r_{1}
should be smooth on the set
\prox_{\gamma r_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\{\prox_{\gamma r_{1}}(x):x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
, where
\gamma
is the step size. This requirement arises in order to prevent the Lipschitz smoothness constant of
r_{1}^{\gamma}
to explode as
\gamma\to 0
. In our setting,
\prox_{\gamma r_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\mathbb{R}^{d}

(
22
, Fact 2)
, and
r_{1}
remains nonsmooth on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, so this requirement cannot be satisfied. To circumvent this issue, we further replace
r_{1}
with its Moreau envelope
r_{1}^{\lambda}
. These considerations motivate the following augmented potential and distribution

\displaystyle V_{\lambda}=\left(f-r_{2}^{\lambda}\right)+r_{1}^{\lambda},\quad\pi_{\lambda}\propto e^{-V_{\lambda}},

(8)

which results in the DC-LA (
3

.

The difference-of-convex structure of

In practice, there is also a class of sparsity promoting DC regularizers where

Assumption 3

There exists

\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ \forall z\in\partial r_{1}(x)

To be noted, Assumption

Conclusion

We propose a mathematically justified sampler DC-LA for nonsmooth DC regularizers, extending beyond log-concave settings and ensuring convergence under distant dissipativity. Our work overcomes the limitations of the assumptions in
(
42
)
while considering DC regularizers that are not necessarily weakly convex. DC-LA is able to produce sensible variance images in a computed tomography application with DICNN priors—an aspect largely overlooked by existing work in this domain, which typically focuses solely on MAP estimation. Future work will explore the practical use of these variance images in downstream applications, e.g.,
(
26
)
.

Lemma 10
.

Let

be a convex function and
\lambda,\gamma>0
, it holds

\displaystyle\prox_{\gamma g^{\lambda}}(x)=\dfrac{1}{\gamma+\lambda}\left(\gamma\prox_{(\gamma+\lambda)g}(x)+\lambda x\right).

p_{X_{k+1}}=(\prox_{\gamma r_{1}^{\lambda}})_{\#}p_{Y_{k+1}}
. Let
\nu_{\lambda,\gamma}=(\prox_{\gamma r_{1}^{\lambda}})_{\#}\pi_{\lambda,\gamma}
and
(X,Y)
be the optimal coupling of
(\pi_{\lambda,\gamma},p_{Y_{k+1}})
,

\displaystyle W_{1}(p_{X_{k+1}},\nu_{\lambda,\gamma})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\|\prox_{\gamma r_{1}^{\lambda}}(X)-\prox_{\gamma r_{1}^{\lambda}}(Y)\|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\|X-Y\|

\displaystyle=W_{1}(\pi_{\lambda,\gamma},p_{Y_{k+1}}).

Case 2:
q\geq 2

According to the proof of Theorem 5 in
[
42
]
and Lemma 18 therein: there exist
D_{q}(\lambda)>0,\rho_{\lambda}\in(0,1)
:

\displaystyle W_{q}(\mu Q^{k},\nu Q^{k})^{q}\leq 2^{q-1}\|\mu Q^{k}-\nu Q^{k}\|_{V_{q}}\leq D_{q}(\lambda)\rho_{\lambda}^{k\gamma}(\mu(V_{q}^{2})+\nu(V_{q}^{2})).

where
V_{q}=1+\|\cdot\|^{q}
. In particular, with
\nu=p_{\lambda,\gamma}
, it holds

\displaystyle W_{q}(\mu Q^{k},p_{\lambda,\gamma})^{q}\leq D_{q}(\lambda)\rho_{\lambda}^{k\gamma}(\mu(V_{q}^{2})+p_{\lambda,\gamma}(V^{2}_{q})).

(42)

Thanks to Lemma
12
,
p_{\lambda,\gamma}(V_{q}^{2})<+\infty
, the convergence is exponentially fast given that
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. From (
42
), we will further show the following uniform bound in the later part of the text: there exist
C_{1,q}(\lambda)>0
:

\displaystyle W_{q}(p_{Y_{k+1}},p_{\lambda,\gamma})\leq C_{1,q}(\lambda)\rho_{\lambda}^{k\gamma/q}.

(43)

On another hand, from
[
42
, Theorem 1]
, there exists
C_{2,q}(\lambda)>0

\displaystyle W_{q}(p_{\lambda,\gamma},\bar{p}_{\lambda,\gamma})\leq C_{2,q}(\lambda)\left(L_{f}+\dfrac{1}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}(G_{1}\gamma)^{\frac{1}{q}}

and from
[
42
, Theorem 2]
, there exists
C_{3,q}(\lambda)>0
:

\displaystyle W_{q}(\bar{p}_{\lambda,\gamma},\pi_{\lambda,\gamma})\leq C_{3,q}(\lambda)\gamma^{\frac{1}{2q}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle W_{q}(p_{Y_{k+1}},\pi_{\lambda,\gamma})\leq C_{1,q}(\lambda)\rho_{\lambda}^{k\gamma/q}+C_{2,q}(\lambda)\left(L_{f}+\dfrac{1}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}G_{1}^{\frac{1}{q}}\gamma^{\frac{1}{q}}+C_{3,q}(\lambda)\gamma^{\frac{1}{2q}}.

Note that the convergence rate in the theorem is then
\rho_{\lambda}^{1/q}\in(0,1)
. Here, by abuse of notation, we still denote this rate by
\rho_{\lambda}
.

Finally, we show the uniform bound (
43
) which boils down to a uniform bound for
p_{\lambda,\gamma}(V_{2q})
. We have

\displaystyle W_{2q}(p_{\lambda,\gamma},\pi_{\lambda,\gamma})\leq W_{2q}(p_{\lambda,\gamma},\bar{p}_{\lambda,\gamma})+W_{2q}(\bar{p}_{\lambda,\gamma},\pi_{\lambda,\gamma}).

(44)

Similar to the arguments of Case 1, the RHS of (
44
) can be bounded uniformly by some
E(\lambda)
. By Hölder inequality,

\displaystyle\|x\|^{2q}\leq 2^{2q-1}(\|y\|^{2q}+\|x-y\|^{2q}).

Let
(X,Y)
be the optimal coupling w.r.t. the cost
d(x,y)=\|x-y\|^{2q}
of
p_{\lambda,\gamma}
and
\pi_{\lambda,\gamma}
,

\displaystyle\int{\|x\|^{2q}dp_{\lambda,\gamma}(x)}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\|X\|^{2q}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2q-1}\left(\mathbb{E}\|Y\|^{2q}+\mathbb{E}\|X-Y\|^{2q}\right)

\displaystyle=2^{2q-1}\left(\mathbb{E}\|Y\|^{2q}+W_{2q}(p_{\lambda,\gamma},\pi_{\lambda,\gamma})^{2q}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2q-1}\left(\int{\|y\|^{2q}}d\pi_{\lambda,\gamma}(y)+E(\lambda)^{2q}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2q-1}\left(e^{\frac{G_{1}^{2}\gamma_{0}}{2}}\int{\|y\|^{2q}}d\pi_{\lambda}(y)+E(\lambda)^{2q}\right)

where the last inequality follows (
41
).

Lastly, for the sequence
\{X_{k}\}_{k}

\displaystyle W_{q}(p_{X_{k+1}},\nu_{\lambda,\gamma})\leq W_{q}(p_{Y_{k+1}},\pi_{\lambda,\gamma}).

Retrieving constants from

Bound
W_{q}(\pi_{\lambda},\pi)

We follow the proof template in
[

, Appendix G.3]
where we additionally handle the part
-r^{\lambda}
.

Case 1: Assumption
4
(i):

Since
r_{i}
is
G_{i}
-Lipschitz (
i\in\{1,2\}
), it holds
r_{i}(x)-r_{i}^{\lambda}(x)\leq\frac{\lambda G_{i}^{2}}{2}
for all
x
. Combining with the fact that the Moreau envelope of a function is a lower bound of that function, we get

\displaystyle-\dfrac{\lambda G_{2}^{2}}{2}\leq r_{1}-r_{2}-(r_{1}^{\lambda}-r_{2}^{\lambda})\leq\dfrac{\lambda G_{1}^{2}}{2}.

(45)

Let
\beta:=\frac{\lambda G_{2}^{2}}{2}
. Given a function
\mathcal{V}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[1,\infty)
(that induces the
\mathcal{V}
-norm), we write

\displaystyle\|\pi_{\lambda}-\pi\|_{\mathcal{V}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{|\phi|\leq\mathcal{V}}\int{\phi(x)(\pi_{\lambda}(x)-\pi(x))}dx

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{|\phi|\leq\mathcal{V}}\int{|\phi(x)||\pi_{\lambda}(x)-\pi(x)|}dx

\displaystyle\leq\int{\mathcal{V}(x)|\pi_{\lambda}(x)-\pi(x)|}dx

\displaystyle=\int{\mathcal{V}(x)\pi(x)\left|1-\dfrac{\pi_{\lambda}(x)}{\pi(x)}\right|}dx

\displaystyle=\int{\mathcal{V}(x)\pi(x)\left|1-e^{r_{2}^{\lambda}(x)-r_{1}^{\lambda}(x)-r_{2}(x)+r_{1}(x)}\dfrac{\int{e^{-f-r_{1}+r_{2}}}}{\int{e^{-f-r_{1}^{\lambda}+r_{2}^{\lambda}}}}\right|}dx

\displaystyle=\int{\mathcal{V}(x)\pi(x)\left|1-e^{r_{2}^{\lambda}(x)-r_{1}^{\lambda}(x)-r_{2}(x)+r_{1}(x)+\beta}\cdot e^{-\beta}\dfrac{\int{e^{-f-r_{1}+r_{2}}}}{\int{e^{-f-r_{1}^{\lambda}+r_{2}^{\lambda}}}}\right|}dx

\displaystyle\leq\int{\mathcal{V}(x)\pi(x)\left|1-e^{r_{2}^{\lambda}(x)-r_{1}^{\lambda}(x)-r_{2}(x)+r_{1}(x)+\beta}\right|}dx

\displaystyle\quad+\int{\mathcal{V}(x)\pi(x)e^{r_{2}^{\lambda}(x)-r_{1}^{\lambda}(x)-r_{2}(x)+r_{1}(x)+\beta}\left|1-e^{-\beta}\dfrac{\int{e^{-f-r_{1}+r_{2}}}}{\int{e^{-f-r_{1}^{\lambda}+r_{2}^{\lambda}}}}\right|}dx.

Since

\displaystyle r_{2}^{\lambda}(x)-r_{1}^{\lambda}(x)-r_{2}(x)+r_{1}(x)+\beta\geq 0

(46)

it follows
e^{r_{2}^{\lambda}(x)-r_{1}^{\lambda}(x)-r_{2}(x)+r_{1}(x)+\beta}\geq 1
and

\displaystyle e^{-\beta}\dfrac{\int{e^{-f-r_{1}+r_{2}}}}{\int{e^{-f-r_{1}^{\lambda}+r_{2}^{\lambda}}}}\leq 1,

we have

\displaystyle\|\pi_{\lambda}-\pi\|_{\mathcal{V}}

\displaystyle\leq\int{\mathcal{V}(x)\pi(x)\left(e^{r_{2}^{\lambda}(x)-r_{1}^{\lambda}(x)-r_{2}(x)+r_{1}(x)+\beta}-1\right)}dx

\displaystyle\quad+\int{\mathcal{V}(x)\pi(x)e^{r_{2}^{\lambda}(x)-r_{1}^{\lambda}(x)-r_{2}(x)+r_{1}(x)+\beta}\left(1-e^{-\beta}\dfrac{\int{e^{-f-r_{1}+r_{2}}}}{\int{e^{-f-r_{1}^{\lambda}+r_{2}^{\lambda}}}}\right)}dx

Now using the evaluation (
45
), we derive

\displaystyle\|\pi_{\lambda}-\pi\|_{\mathcal{V}}

\displaystyle\leq\int{\mathcal{V}(x)\pi(x)\left(e^{\frac{\lambda(G_{1}^{2}+G_{2}^{2})}{2}}-1\right)}dx

\displaystyle\quad+\int{\mathcal{V}(x)\pi(x)e^{\frac{\lambda(G_{1}^{2}+G_{2}^{2})}{2}}\left(1-e^{-\beta}\dfrac{\int{e^{-f-r_{1}+r_{2}}}}{\int{e^{-f-r_{1}^{\lambda}+r_{2}^{\lambda}}}}\right)}dx

It also follows from (
45
) that

\displaystyle\dfrac{\int{e^{-f-r_{1}+r_{2}}}}{\int{e^{-f-r_{1}^{\lambda}+r_{2}^{\lambda}}}}\geq e^{-\frac{\lambda G_{1}^{2}}{2}}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\|\pi_{\lambda}-\pi\|_{\mathcal{V}}\leq 2\left(e^{\frac{\lambda(G_{1}^{2}+G_{2}^{2})}{2}}-1\right)\int{\mathcal{V}(x)\pi(x)}dx=O(\lambda).

It then follows from
[
42
, Lemma 18]
that (by using
\mathcal{V}=1+\|\cdot\|^{q}
)

\displaystyle W_{q}(\pi_{\lambda},\pi)=O(\lambda^{1/q}).

Case 2: Assumption
4
(ii):

It is a standard result that

\displaystyle r_{2}(x)-r_{2}^{\lambda}(x)\leq\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\|\nabla r_{2}(x)\|^{2},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Indeed, a short proof is as follows

\displaystyle r_{2}(x)-r_{2}^{\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=r_{2}(x)-\inf_{y}\left\{r_{2}(y)+\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\|x-y\|^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{y}\left\{r_{2}(x)-r_{2}(y)-\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\|x-y\|^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{y}\left\{\langle\nabla r_{2}(x),x-y\rangle-\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\|x-y\|^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{y}\left\{-\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\|y-x+\lambda\nabla r_{2}(x)\|^{2}+\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\|\nabla r_{2}(x)\|^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\|\nabla r_{2}(x)\|^{2}.

Under Assumption
4
(ii):

\displaystyle\|\nabla r_{2}(x)\|^{2}\leq 2(\|\nabla r_{2}(0)\|^{2}+M^{2}\|x\|^{2\kappa}).

Therefore

\displaystyle r_{2}(x)-r^{\lambda}_{2}(x)\leq\lambda(\|\nabla r_{2}(0)\|^{2}+M^{2}\|x\|^{2\kappa})

and

\displaystyle|r_{1}(x)-r_{2}(x)-r_{1}^{\lambda}(x)+r_{2}^{\lambda}(x)|\leq\dfrac{\lambda G_{1}^{2}}{2}+\lambda\|\nabla r_{2}(0)\|^{2}+\lambda M^{2}\|x\|^{2\kappa}.

By applying

\displaystyle|e^{t}-1|\leq|t|e^{|t|},\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R},

we have

\displaystyle\|\pi_{\lambda}-\pi\|_{\mathcal{V}_{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\int{\mathcal{V}_{q}(x)\pi(x)\left|1-e^{r_{2}^{\lambda}(x)-r_{1}^{\lambda}(x)-r_{2}(x)+r_{1}(x)}\dfrac{\int{e^{-f-r_{1}+r_{2}}}}{\int{e^{-f-r_{1}^{\lambda}+r_{2}^{\lambda}}}}\right|}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int{\mathcal{V}_{q}(x)\pi(x)\left|1-e^{r_{2}^{\lambda}(x)-r_{1}^{\lambda}(x)-r_{2}(x)+r_{1}(x)}\right|}dx

\displaystyle+\int{\mathcal{V}_{q}(x)\pi(x)e^{r_{2}^{\lambda}(x)-r_{1}^{\lambda}(x)-r_{2}(x)+r_{1}(x)}}dx\left|1-\dfrac{\int{e^{-f-r_{1}+r_{2}}}}{\int{e^{-f-r_{1}^{\lambda}+r_{2}^{\lambda}}}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int{\mathcal{V}_{q}(x)\pi(x)|r_{2}^{\lambda}(x)-r_{1}^{\lambda}(x)-r_{2}(x)+r_{1}(x)|}e^{|r_{2}^{\lambda}(x)-r_{1}^{\lambda}(x)-r_{2}(x)+r_{1}(x)|}dx

\displaystyle+e^{\lambda G_{1}^{2}/2}\pi(\mathcal{V}_{q})\left|1-\dfrac{\int{e^{-f-r_{1}+r_{2}}}}{\int{e^{-f-r_{1}^{\lambda}+r_{2}^{\lambda}}}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\underbrace{\int{\mathcal{V}_{q}(x)\pi(x)\left(\dfrac{\lambda G_{1}^{2}}{2}+\lambda\|\nabla r_{2}(0)\|^{2}+\lambda M^{2}\|x\|^{2\kappa}\right)e^{\frac{\lambda G_{1}^{2}}{2}+\lambda\|\nabla r_{2}(0)\|^{2}+\lambda M^{2}\|x\|^{2\kappa}}}dx}_{I}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{e^{\lambda G_{1}^{2}/2}\pi(\mathcal{V}_{q})\left|1-\dfrac{\int{e^{-f-r_{1}+r_{2}}}}{\int{e^{-f-r_{1}^{\lambda}+r_{2}^{\lambda}}}}\right|}_{II}.

Since
\pi
has sub-Gaussian tails (Appendix
A
),

\displaystyle\int{\mathcal{V}_{q}(x)\|x\|^{2\kappa}e^{\lambda M^{2}\|x\|^{2\kappa}}\pi(x)}<+\infty

(47)

for all
\kappa\in(0,1)
. To see this, recall
\pi\propto e^{-V}
and there exist
a,b>0
:

\displaystyle V(x)\geq a\|x\|^{2}-b.

Let
\bar{a}=a/(\lambda M^{2}\kappa)
. By Young’s inequality

\displaystyle\|x\|^{2\kappa}\leq\dfrac{\kappa\bar{a}}{2}\|x\|^{2}+\left(\dfrac{2}{\bar{a}}\right)^{\frac{\kappa}{1-\kappa}}(1-\kappa).

Therefore,

\displaystyle V(x)-\lambda M^{2}\|x\|^{2\kappa}\geq\dfrac{a}{2}\|x\|^{2}-c

for some
c
. So
e^{-V(x)+\lambda M^{2}\|x\|^{2\kappa}}
also has sub-Gaussian tails, which guarantees the finiteness of (
47
). As a result,
I=O(\lambda)
.

Now that

\displaystyle\int e^{-\lambda\bigl(\|\nabla r_{2}(0)\|^{2}+M^{2}\|x\|^{2\kappa}\bigr)}\,\pi(x)\,dx\;\leq\;\frac{\int e^{-f(x)-r_{1}^{\lambda}(x)-r_{2}^{\lambda}(x)}\,dx}{\int e^{-f(x)-r_{1}(x)+r_{2}(x)}\,dx}\;\leq\;e^{\lambda G_{1}^{2}/2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left|1-\dfrac{\int{e^{-f-r_{1}+r_{2}}}}{\int{e^{-f-r_{1}^{\lambda}+r_{2}^{\lambda}}}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|1-e^{-\lambda G_{1}^{2}/2}\right|+\left|1-\dfrac{e^{\lambda\|\nabla r_{2}(0)\|^{2}}}{\int{e^{-\lambda M^{2}\|x\|^{2\kappa}}\pi(x)}dx}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\dfrac{G_{1}^{2}}{2}e^{\lambda G_{1}^{2}/2}+\dfrac{|1-e^{\lambda\|\nabla r_{2}(0)\|^{2}}|+|1-\int{e^{-\lambda M^{2}\|x\|^{2\kappa}}\pi(x)}dx|}{\int{e^{-\lambda M^{2}\|x\|^{2\kappa}}\pi(x)}dx}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\dfrac{G_{1}^{2}}{2}e^{\lambda G_{1}^{2}/2}+\dfrac{\lambda\|\nabla r_{2}(0)\|^{2}e^{\lambda\|\nabla r_{2}(0)\|^{2}}+\int{|1-e^{-\lambda M^{2}\|x\|^{2\kappa}}|\pi(x)}dx}{\int{e^{-\lambda M^{2}\|x\|^{2\kappa}}\pi(x)}dx}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\dfrac{G_{1}^{2}}{2}e^{\lambda G_{1}^{2}/2}+\dfrac{\lambda\|\nabla r_{2}(0)\|^{2}e^{\lambda\|\nabla r_{2}(0)\|^{2}}+\lambda M^{2}\int{\|x\|^{2\kappa}e^{\lambda M^{2}\|x\|^{2\kappa}}\pi(x)}dx}{\int{e^{-\lambda M^{2}\|x\|^{2\kappa}}\pi(x)}dx}

\displaystyle=O(\lambda)

thanks to
\pi
having sub-Gaussian tails.

I.8
Analysis of DC-LA-S

We recall DC-LA-S

\displaystyle Y_{k+1}

\displaystyle=X_{k}-\gamma\nabla f(X_{k})+\gamma\nabla r_{2}(X_{k})+\sqrt{2\gamma}Z_{k+1}

\displaystyle X_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\prox_{\gamma r_{1}^{\lambda}}(Y_{k+1}).

Similar to Section
4.1
,
Y_{k+1}
is formulated as

\displaystyle Y_{k+1}=Y_{k}-\gamma b^{\gamma}_{\lambda}(Y_{k})+\sqrt{2\gamma}Z_{k+1}

(48)

where the drift is

\displaystyle b^{\gamma}_{\lambda}(y):=\nabla r_{1}^{\lambda}(\prox_{\gamma r_{1}^{\lambda}}(y))+\nabla f(\prox_{\gamma r_{1}^{\lambda}}(y))-\nabla r_{2}(\prox_{\gamma r_{1}^{\lambda}}(y)).

(49)

b_{\lambda}^{\gamma}
is
(L_{f}+\frac{1}{\lambda}+L_{r_{2}})
-smooth. Similar to the evaluations in
I.3
, for
\gamma\leq\gamma_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle b_{\lambda}^{\gamma}(x)-b_{\lambda}^{\gamma}(y),x-y\rangle\geq\mu\|x-y\|^{2}-2G_{1}(2+L_{f}\gamma_{0}+L_{r_{2}})\|x-y\|

for
\|x-y\|\geq R_{0}
. Therefore,
b_{\lambda}^{\gamma}
is
(\frac{\mu}{2},R)
-distant dissipative where

\displaystyle R:=\max\left\{R_{0},\dfrac{4G_{1}(2+L_{f}\gamma_{0}+L_{r_{2}})}{\mu}\right\}.

We next define another drift

\displaystyle\bar{b}^{\gamma}_{\lambda}(y)=\nabla r_{1}^{\lambda}(\prox_{\gamma r_{1}^{\lambda}}(y))+\nabla f(y)-\nabla r_{2}(y),

(50)

and the corresponding general ULA:

\displaystyle\bar{Y}_{k+1}=\bar{Y}_{k}-\gamma\bar{b}_{\lambda}^{\gamma}(\bar{Y_{k}})+\sqrt{2\gamma}\bar{Z}_{k+1}.

(51)

We also denote

\displaystyle\pi_{\lambda,\gamma}(x)\propto\exp(-f(x)-(r_{1}^{\lambda})^{\gamma}(x)+r_{2}(x)),

then it follows that
-\nabla\log\pi_{\lambda,\gamma}=\bar{b}_{\lambda}^{\gamma}.

Similarly, we can show that: for all
\gamma>0
,
\bar{b}_{\lambda}^{\gamma}
is
(\frac{1}{\lambda}+L_{f}+L_{r_{2}})
-smooth and is
(\frac{\mu}{2},\bar{R})
-distant dissipative where

\displaystyle\bar{R}=\max\left\{R_{0},\dfrac{8G_{1}}{\mu}\right\}.

Let
Q,\bar{Q}
be transition kernels of
\{Y_{k}\}_{k}
and
\{\bar{Y}_{k}\}_{k}
, respectively. According to Lemma
12
,
Q,\bar{Q}
admit invariant distributions called
p_{\lambda,\gamma},\bar{p}_{\lambda,\gamma}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Furthermore, if
q\geq 2
, Lemma
12
guaranties
p_{\lambda,\gamma},\bar{p}_{\lambda,\gamma}\in\mathcal{P}_{2q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Similar to the arguments in Section
I.5
, there exist
C_{1,q}(\lambda),C_{2,q}(\lambda),C_{3,q}(\lambda)>0
and
\rho_{q,\lambda}\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle W_{q}(p_{Y_{k+1}},\pi_{\lambda,\gamma})\leq C_{1,q}(\lambda)\rho_{q,\lambda}^{k\gamma/q}+C_{2,q}(\lambda)\left(L_{f}+L_{r_{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}G_{1}^{\frac{1}{q}}\gamma^{\frac{1}{q}}+C_{3,q}(\lambda)\gamma^{\frac{1}{2q}}.

Finally, we can apply
[
42

\displaystyle W_{q}(\pi_{\lambda,\gamma},\pi_{\lambda})=O(\gamma^{1/q})

\displaystyle W_{q}(\pi_{\lambda},\pi)=O(\lambda^{1/q}).

Standard proximal operators

It is well-known that the proximal operators of

\displaystyle\prox_{\gamma\ell_{1}}(x)

\displaystyle=\sign(x)\odot\max(|x|-\gamma,0),

Recently,

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{\epsilon\gamma}{\|\prox_{\gamma\ell_{1}}(x)\|_{2}}\right)\prox_{\gamma\ell_{1}}(x),&\text{if }\|x\|_{\infty}>\gamma,\\[11.99998pt]
\operatorname{sign}(x_{i^{\star}})\big(\|x\|_{\infty}+(\epsilon-1)\gamma\big)e_{i^{\star}},&\text{if }(1-\epsilon)\gamma<\|x\|_{\infty}\leq\gamma,i^{\star}=\arg\max_{i}|x_{i}|.\end{cases}

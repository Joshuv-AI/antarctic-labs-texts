Hearing the Unusual: A Rare Otological Case of IgG4‐Related Disease Treated With Steroids and Inebiluzmab
Authors: Yuanzun Peng, Muhammad Zaheer
Published: 2026

Introduction

IgG4‐related disease (IgG4‐RD) is a systemic fibroinflammatory condition that frequently involves multiple organs, with classic presentations affecting the pancreas, biliary tree, salivary glands, and retroperitoneum. Histopathology demonstrating a dense lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate rich in IgG4‐positive plasma cells along with storiform fibrosis and obliterative phlebitis is a hallmark of the disease and central to its diagnosis. 1 Rarely, IgG4‐RD can present solely with otologic involvement, manifesting as otitis media and hearing loss; only a few dozen cases have been reported in the literature, most originating from Asia. 2 To increase awareness of IgG4‐RD and its atypical presentations, we report the case of a patient with recurrent otitis media and associated conductive hearing loss who was ultimately diagnosed with biopsy‐proven IgG4‐RD and showed improvement following systemic glucocorticoid therapy and treatment with a monoclonal anti‐CD19 antibody in the United States.

Case report

A 56‐year‐old woman with a history of asthma and chronic rhinosinusitis was referred to the rheumatology clinic by otolaryngology (ENT) for recurrent otitis media with conductive hearing loss ongoing for five years. The patient had been followed in the ENT clinic for decades before rheumatology evaluation, initially for chronic rhinosinusitis and later for recurrent otitis media and associated hearing loss. She had undergone bilateral sinus surgery a decade ago, followed by bilateral sphenoidotomy two years before for poorly controlled chronic rhinosinusitis. Since then, she had been followed in an allergy clinic for asthma and chronic rhinosinusitis with associated hypereosinophilia. Her asthma and rhinosinusitis had been controlled on budenosnide/formoterol, dupilumab, and montelukast.

For recurrent otitis media, she underwent multiple procedures, including bilateral myringotomy, placement of tympanostomy tubes, and right‐sided tympanomastoidectomy, with only minimal improvement in symptoms. Follow‐up temporal bone computed tomography showed progression of bilateral mastoid and middle ear effusions, with essentially complete opacification of the mastoid air cells and middle ear cavity. ENT subsequently performed bilateral cochlear Osia bone‐conduction implants and bilateral cartilage‐embedded tube placement with only mild improvement in her symptoms.

Differential diagnosis

At the rheumatology clinic presentation, she denied skin rashes, Raynaud phenomenon, history of inflammatory eye disease, oral ulcers, or joint pain, as well as tobacco, alcohol, or illicit drug use. ENT examination before the rheumatology visit revealed thick inflammatory fibrous tissue filling the middle ear; otherwise, she had no signs of synovitis, inflammatory arthritis, cutaneous rashes, or focal neurologic deficits. Laboratory evaluation for otologic symptoms revealed leukocytosis of 12.4 × 109/L with an absolute eosinophil count of 5.0 × 109/L. Plasma IgG was 11.1 g/L. Rheumatologic serologies, including antinuclear antibody, extractable nuclear antigen antibodies, and antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibodies, were negative. Histopathologic examination of the fibrous tissue demonstrated an increased number of IgG4‐positive plasma cells (IgG4/IgG ratio >70%) with dense lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate, consistent with IgG4‐related disease (IgG4‐RD) (Figure 1A–C). The patient was diagnosed with IgG4‐RD.

Management and outcome

A follow‐up positron emission tomographic scan to assess disease extent showed fluorodeoxyglucose uptake in the mastoid air cells with no other focal abnormal FDG uptake, suggesting localized IgG4‐RD. She was initiated on prednisone 60 mg daily, with subsequent improvement in hearing. Her otologic symptoms worsened after an attempt to taper prednisone, and she was restarted on prednisone 40 mg daily with addition of inebilizumab.

At her most recent follow‐up visit, she had been receiving inebilizumab 300 mg intravenously every 6 months, with prednisone successfully tapered off, and reported approximately a “20% improvement” in hearing, with no ongoing symptoms of recurrent otitis media or rhinosinusitis. She continues to coordinate care with otolaryngology regarding her Osia implant and hearing aids.

Discussion

IgG4‐RD is an uncommon fibroinflammatory condition that requires a high index of clinical suspicion for diagnosis. Only a few dozen cases worldwide have described its association with otologic involvement. The presenting symptoms of otologic IgG4‐RD—such as progressive hearing loss, tinnitus, vertigo, recurrent mastoiditis, and otitis media—often mimic those of infectious and autoimmune disorders, complicating timely recognition. Histopathology remains the gold standard for diagnosis, characterized by IgG4‐positive plasma cell infiltration, storiform fibrosis, lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate, and obliterative phlebitis. However, in otologic IgG4‐RD, dense fibrosis may predominate, obscuring these classic histopathological features. Particularly in the head and neck region, storiform fibrosis and obliterative phlebitis may not always be evident, as seen in our case. 3

Given the rarity of otologic manifestations in this already uncommon disease, diagnosis is frequently delayed, with patients often undergoing multiple empirical treatments and surgical procedures before accurate identification. In our case, the patient underwent repeated tympanostomy tube placements and several other interventions without sustained improvement before being referred to rheumatology for further evaluation. This highlights the importance of clinician awareness regarding rare manifestations of IgG4‐RD as part of the differential diagnosis. Broader recognition can facilitate earlier initiation of targeted therapy and potentially reduce the need for repeated surgical interventions.

Another notable aspect of our case is the patient's history of refractory chronic rhinosinusitis requiring multiple sinus surgeries. Previous studies have demonstrated a potential association between IgG4‐RD and chronic or allergic rhinosinusitis, with sinonasal disease sometimes serving as a precursor or component of systemic IgG4‐RD. 4 Notably, the current ACR/EULAR IgG4‐RD classification criteria do not include nasal cavity or otologic involvement, a limitation that may reduce the criteria's sensitivity. 5 Our findings contribute to the growing body of evidence supporting the need to revise these criteria to reflect head and neck disease involvement.

Although no randomized controlled trials have specifically evaluated optimal therapy for otologic IgG4‐RD, case reports consistently describe favorable responses to glucocorticoids as first‐line treatment. 6 Furthermore, recent randomized trials in systemic IgG4‐RD have demonstrated the efficacy of inebilizumab, which has now received US Food and Drug Administration approval. 6 Our patient's robust response to combined steroid and inebilizumab therapy suggests these agents are also effective in isolated otologic manifestations of IgG4‐RD.

Conclusions

We present a unique case of IgG4‐related disease (IgG4‐RD) in which the patient exhibited isolated otologic symptoms and showed improvement with steroid therapy and the monoclonal anti‐CD19 antibody, inebilizumab. This case highlights a rare manifestation of IgG4‐RD and provides anecdotal evidence supporting the efficacy of CD19‐targeted therapy in treating IgG4‐RD, including cases with single‐organ involvement.

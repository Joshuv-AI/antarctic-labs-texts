Nucci based soliton structures and nonlinear dynamics of the (3+1) dimensional hyperbolic Schrödinger equation
Authors: Afifa Shahbaz, Muhammad Abbas, M. Higazy, Muhammad Zain Yousaf, Meltem Ogrenmis, Asnake Birhanu, Y. S. Hamed
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The Hyperbolic (3+1)-dimensional nonlinear Schrödinger equation, a basic model for multidimensional nonlinear wave propagation, is examined in this work. Exact analytical solutions covering bright and dark solitons, periodic waveforms, and M- and W-shaped soliton solutions were obtained by using Nucci’s reduction approach. The spatial-temporal features and stability of these solutions are further demonstrated by means of contour graphs, 2D graphs, and 3D surface plots. Bifurcation analysis was used to investigate the system’s dynamical changes beyond precise solutions, highlighting characteristics including multi-stability, chaotic development, and sensitivity towards initial conditions. The system’s considerable dependence on initial states and control parameters, which determine whether steady soliton propagation, oscillatory modes, or chaotic behaviors emerge, is particularly highlighted by the sensitivity analysis. The physical interpretations highlight the significance of these findings in fields such as fluid dynamics, Bose-Einstein condensates, plasma physics, and nonlinear optics, where structured multi-lobed solitons, phase defects, oscillatory states, and localized energy transfer are important. This study combines analytical soliton solutions obtained through Nucci’s reduction method with bifurcation, chaotic, multi-stability, and sensitivity analyses to investigate the dynamical behavior of the hyperbolic (3+1)-dimensional nonlinear Schrödinger equation. These results lay down the foundation for future theoretical, computational, and experimental studies of multidimensional nonlinear wave dynamics and enhance the collection of analytical standards for higher-dimensional nonlinear systems.

Introduction

The mathematical basis of chaotic models, which are essential for modeling a variety of experimental results in fields including biology, nonlinear optics, economics, and modern physics1,2, is provided by nonlinear partial differential equations(NLPDEs). In many scientific fields, precise solutions to nonlinear partial differential equations are essential to interpreting natural occurrences. Their greatest significance has thus compelled modern physical research to step up its hunt for these responses. Numerous branches of science and technology deal with hyperbolic partial differential problems. In order to represent complex dynamic behaviors in higher dimensions, this equation contains hyperbolic-type components extending the basic nonlinear Schrödinger equation. In contrast to the classical NLSE, the hyperbolic nonlinear Schrödinger equation has unique mathematical and physical properties that allow it to describe richer dynamics in three spatial dimensions plus time. This is primarily due to the addition of the second-order time derivative term, which has a negative sign. This equation demonstrates how dispersion and nonlinearity interact in systems like deep sea wave dynamics and nonlinear optical fibers, where waves display a variety of complex characteristics such as modulation instability, stability, and soliton propagation. To describe multidimensional wave evolution, stability, and the creation of unstable wave patterns in domains like fluid dynamics and optics, the HNSE extends classical wave models through the inclusion of a second-order time derivative with a hyperbolic character.

Understanding how multidimensional dispersion, transverse effects, and external perturbations affect wave propagation has become a major focus of recent research on higher-dimensional nonlinear Schrödinger-type equations. Although these models have been investigated using a variety of analytical and numerical techniques, the majority of previous studies either take into account simplified lower-dimensional reductions or concentrate on unique nonlinearities which allow easy integration. The literature is still quite limited for the hyperbolic (3+1)-dimensional NLSE, especially when it comes to analytical solution structures and the dynamical behavior that goes along with them. The results that are currently available frequently only deal with particular kinds of solutions or depend on numerical studies without demonstrating a clear analytical-dynamical relationship. This gap encourages more research on the qualitative dynamics of explicit solution families under physically relevant parameter conditions.

Bifurcation analysis

It identifies important points, often referred to as bifurcation points, in which a small adjustment to a system variable results in a major shift in the system’s long-term characteristics. In nonlinear dynamical systems, these changes may appear as shifts from periodic to chaotic behavior or from stable fixed points to periodic oscillations. Chaotic patterns

The bifurcation analysis predicts transitions that are similar to the chaotic behavior of the (3 + 1)-dimensional HNSE. As the system shifts from periodic to chaotic phases in response to parameter changes, it displays predictable paths to chaos. The sensitivity and complexity of the system has been demonstrated by these patterns, which improve understanding about the development of bifurcation structures as system parameters change. Additionally, this coherence shows that the identical range of parameters has multi-stability. To investigate the development of quasi-periodic and chaotic behaviors, we support Eq. Multi-stability

The recognition of multi-stability in the Hyperbolic (3+1)-dimensional NLS framework under bifurcation and sensitivity analysis is the major outcome of this investigation. The coexistence of several stable states for the same set of system characteristics, when the ultimate state is highly dependent on starting conditions or disturbances, is known as multi-stability. Depending on the initial configuration selected, the system can develop toward several dynamical phases in this study, such as periodic oscillations, chaotic behaviors, and stable soliton states, as shown by the bifurcation diagrams and sensitivity analysis. Because it shows how nonlinear wave propagation in real-world systems may be controlled by carefully adjusting initial inputs or external factors, this phenomena has important physical applications. While multi-stability may be involved in the stability and transition between coexisting nonlinear wave modes in plasma physics and Bose-Einstein condensates, it may also be used in nonlinear optics for optical switching and memory systems. Thus, the existence of multi-stability highlights the complexity of the dynamics that the hyperbolic NLS equation supports and the value of analytical and numerical methods like bifurcation analysis and Nucci’s approach in revealing these complex behaviors.

Sensitivity analysis

The Hyperbolic (3+1)-dimensional NLS system is highly dependent on its initial conditions and system parameters, as shown by the sensitivity analysis carried out in this paper. Dynamical behaviors vary significantly, from periodic oscillations to chaotic regions to steady soliton propagation, depending on small perturbations in the starting state or small changes in control parameters. This sensitivity, which reflects the unstable but controlled character of higher-dimensional wave dynamics, is an essential sign of nonlinear and chaotic systems. In applications like optical communication systems, where stability and robustness are critical, such sensitivity is physically significant. Not only does having knowledge of this sensitivity helps in long-term growth of systems prediction, but it also offers insight into parameter ranges where mechanisms for modifying or stabilizing nonlinear wave propagation may be developed.

Physical importance of proposed study

Figure 1 represents dark solution for real values for Eq. (13), the dark soliton solutions are of great physical relevance in the case of nonlinear wave propagation. Because dark solitons are localized intensity dips placed in a continuous wave backdrop, they are especially important in systems with a steady background field. They convert into phase-modulated light pulses in nonlinear optics that may propagate robustly and with little distortion, providing uses in fiber communication systems, signal processing, and optical switching. Whereas dark solitons in Bose-Einstein condensates simulate phase defects or density notches that are essential to comprehending condensate stability and coherence, in plasma physics they can characterize localized density depressions that propagate without dispersing. The study’s graphical analysis highlights the dark soliton structures’ potential as basic nonlinear modes in higher-dimensional physical systems by confirming their stability and persistence under the hyperbolic (3+1)-dimensional NLS framework. Localized intensity dips on a continuous background are associated with dark solitons. They appear when there is a defocusing nonlinearity in the medium, which causes a low-intensity notch to form and a localized phase shift. Dark solitons are phase defects moving through a uniform background field in physical systems like Bose-Einstein condensates or nonlinear optical fibers.

Figure 2 illustrates periodic solution showing real values for Eq. (14), periodic modulation with dispersion and nonlinearity coexist in higher dimensions, as demonstrated by the periodic solutions found in this study, which demonstrate the system’s capacity to maintain persistent nonlinear oscillations. Physically, these solutions are equivalent to wave packs or nonlinear lattices, which can manifest as nonlinear interference patterns or optical lattice states in Bose-Einstein condensates, as periodic modulations of the refractive index in nonlinear optics, and as nonlinear surface or internal waves in fluid dynamics. Nonlinear wave propagation’s repeated spatial-temporal pattern is captured by these solutions, which also show how energy may be regularly dispersed across the medium without loss. The oscillating character of the solutions is obvious from the contour and 3D representations in this work, highlighting their significance in modeling recurrent nonlinear waveforms in multidimensional systems. Wave patterns with recurring peaks and troughs are described by the periodic solutions. These occur when a balance between the dispersive and nonlinear effects results in oscillatory structures instead of isolated pulses. Physically, these solutions can be found in surface water-wave patterns, modulated plasma waves, or layered optical media.

Self-trapped light beams or pulses in optical fibers and wave guides are described by such solutions in nonlinear optics, which are essential for reliable long-distance communication and pulse transmission. While bright solitons explain localized matter-wave structures with concentrated particle density in Bose-Einstein condensates, they can also reflect localized density increases traveling across a uniform plasma medium without distortion in plasma physics. The bright soliton is a basic mode for stable energy transfer in multidimensional nonlinear media, and the graphical representations here demonstrate its localization and adaptability in the (3+1)-dimensional hyperbolic NLS framework. The localized pulses that concentrate energy in space and time are represented by the bright soliton solutions. These profiles physically correspond to self-focusing environments where a stable traveling pulse can form because the dispersive spreading is balanced by the nonlinear effect. These solutions describe the propagation of intense localized beams or density spikes in optical or plasma settings.

These solutions reveal basic wave features including bright solitons, dark solitons, and periodic waveforms. Due to their correspondence to localized energy transport, phase defects on continuous backgrounds, and stable nonlinear oscillations phenomena that arise in fluid dynamics, Bose-Einstein condensates, plasma physics, and nonlinear optics, these solutions are not only mathematically appealing but also physically significant. As a result, the work provides a strong analytical and visual platform for investigating multidimensional nonlinear wave phenomena, providing information that might direct future theoretical advancements as well as experimental realizations.

Conclusion

The (3+1)-dimensional Hyperbolic nonlinear Schrödinger equation was studied in this study utilizing Nucci’s reduction approach, which produced precise analytical solutions in the form of periodic waves, bright and dark solitons, and structured M- and W-shaped soliton profiles. 3D, 2D, and contour plots were used to further illustrate the analytical results and give a clear picture of the stability and spatial-temporal evolution of the resulting waveforms. Physical interpretations confirmed its applicability in disciplines including plasma physical science, Bose-Einstein condensates, fluid mechanics, and nonlinear optics, where such constrained and oscillatory structures are significant. Bifurcation analysis supported the analytical study by demonstrating the system’s ability to switch between several dynamical structures, such as periodic oscillations, chaotic attractors, and stable soliton states, therefore validating the existence of multi-stability. Sensitivity analysis further highlighted the system’s significant dependence on control settings and initial conditions, indicating that it can exhibit both predictable and stable behaviors. This combined method confirms Nucci’s reduction method as a useful tool for identifying precise solutions alongside complicated nonlinear dynamics and highlights the complexity of the hyperbolic higher-dimensional NLS framework.All symbolic computations and analytical solutions, along with their corresponding graphical representations, were obtained using Wolfram Mathematica. MATLAB was employed for the numerical simulations and for generating the bifurcation diagrams and graphical results associated with the chaotic and sensitivity analyses.

This study presented analytical soliton solutions together with bifurcation, chaotic, multi-stability, and sensitivity analyses, providing a comprehensive investigation of the dynamical behavior of the hyperbolic (3+1)-dimensional nonlinear Schrödinger equation. To increase the applicability of these findings to real-world multidimensional nonlinear systems, future studies could expand this methodology to other generalized nonlinear evolution equations, examine stability under external perturbations, and look into parameter systems useful to experimental observations.

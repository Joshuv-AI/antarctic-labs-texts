Legendrian barriers in prequantization bundles
Emmanuel Opshtein

Abstract
. In [ OS24 ] we proved that the contact sphere S 3 S^{3} has Legendrian barriers, that intersect any sufficiently long embedded Reeb cylinders over Legendrian knots. We generalize here this result to contact prequantization bundles.



1.
Introduction

Symplectic manifolds have several flavors of size, measured by so-called symplectic capacities of different natures. Dynamical capacities, like Hofer-Zehnder’s, Eckeland-Hofer’s, Viterbo’s, or Hutchings’ ECH, just to cite a few, detect homologically visible periodic orbits of Hamiltonian systems subject to various constraints and normalizations, and evaluate their symplectic actions
[
HZ94
,
EH90
,
Vit92
,
Hut11
]
. Others, more geometric, measure the maximal sizes of natural objects that fit in the manifold. The objects used to probe the manifold can either be of the same dimension (the embedding capacities, including Gromov’s width), or Lagrangian submanifolds (Cieliebak-Mohnke).
In
[
Bir01
]
, Biran discovered that removing some Lagrangian CW-complexes from closed Kähler manifolds with rational symplectic classes decreases their Gromov’s width, up to arbitrarily small values. He called these Lagrangian submanifolds
barriers
.
The Kähler assumption was soon removed by works of Donaldson and Giroux
[
Don96
,
Gir02
]
. With Felix Schlenk, we proposed an extension of Biran’s framework to the open case, and studied it in the simplest example of the
4
-dimensional bidisc
[
OS24
]
. The result was that in this context, the removal of some explicit isotropic CW-complexes decreases
all
symplectic capacities.

By comparison, the quantitative aspects of contact geometry are much more subtle. For instance, the group of contactomorphisms allows for arbitrary contraction of a Darboux ball, but there still exists a contact non-squeezing phenomenon
[
EKP06
,
San11
,
FSZ25
,
Can24
]
. In parallel with our work on the rigidity of Lagrangian skeleta, a Legendrian barrier phenomenon in the contact
3
-sphere was shown:

Theorem
.

[
OS24
]

Let
\alpha_{0}
be the contact form on
S^{3}
obtained by restricting the form
R_{1}d\theta_{1}+R_{2}d\theta_{2}
to
S^{3}(1)\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}
. There exists a Legendrian CW-complex
\Lambda_{k}
such that, for any contact form
\alpha\leq\alpha_{0}
, any closed Legendrian submanifold
\Lambda\subset S^{3}
whose Reeb flow remains in
S^{3}\backslash\Lambda_{k}
a time
\frac{1}{k}
has an
\alpha
-Reeb chord of length
\leq\frac{1}{k}
.

The aim of the present paper is to generalize this statement to contact prequantization bundles. Before we state our main result, we need to introduce the notation and some concepts. Let
(N,\tau)
be a symplectic manifold with integral symplectic class (so
[\tau]\in H_{2}(N,\mathbb{Z})
). In the present paper, we call
polarization
of
(N,\tau)
of degree
k
a tuple
(\Sigma,\lambda,\varphi)
of a symplectic hypersurface Poincaré-Dual to
k[\tau]
in
N
and a Liouville form on
N\backslash\Sigma
whose Liouville vector field is gradient-like for some Morse function
\varphi
that tends to
-\infty
at
\Sigma
. In other terms,
(N\backslash\Sigma,\lambda,\varphi)
is a Weinstein manifold. A polarization comes with a
skeleton
defined as the maximal compact invariant subset of
X_{\lambda}
. This is the isotropic CW-complex of all the stable manifolds of the critical points of
\varphi
under the flow of
X_{\lambda}
, and is known to be non-subcritical isotropic when
k\geq 2

[
GSZ21
]
. Biran’s Lagrangian barriers are such skeleta. Moreover, Donaldson-Giroux’s theory guarantees that all symplectic manifolds with integral symplectic class admit polarizations
[
Don96
,
Gir02
,
Gir17
]
.

We now define the
contact prequantization bundles
associated to an integral symplectic manifold
(N,\tau)
. Let
\mathcal{P}\to N
be the
S^{1}
-bundle over
N
with Euler-class
-[\tau]
and
\alpha
a connection form for this bundle with curvature
\tau
. This means that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\alpha_{|S^{1}}=d\theta\\
d\alpha=\pi^{*}\tau\end{array}\right.

Although
\alpha
is not completely determined by the above conditions, different choices of connections lead to strict contactomorphic forms. The contact prequantization associated to
(N,\tau)
is the contact manifold
\mathcal{P}(N,\tau):=(\mathcal{P},\alpha)
, and we insist that it is endowed with a contact form, not just a contact structure.

Recall finally that in a contact manifold
(M,\xi=\ker\alpha)
, any function

H\in\mathbb{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(M\times[0,1])
gives rise to a contact Hamiltonian vector field, defined by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}X_{H}=HR_{\alpha}+Z,\hskip 8.5359ptZ\in\xi,\\
d\alpha(Z,\cdot)=-dH_{|\xi}.\end{array}\right.

The map
\Phi_{H}^{t}:=\Phi_{X_{H}}^{[0,t]}
is a contact diffeomorphism, meaning that it preserves the contact distribution
\xi
.
Given a time-dependent function
H
and two subsets
X,Y\subset M
, we say that
H
has a chord of length
\ell
between
X
and
Y
if there exists a point
x\in X
such that
\Phi^{\ell}_{H}(x)\in Y
. Notice that by convention, the trajectory is required to starts on
X
at time
0
. Such a trajectory is called a
H
-chord between
X
and
Y
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
(N^{2n},\tau)
be a closed symplectic manifold with integral symplectic class and
(\mathcal{P}^{2n+1},\alpha)\overset{\pi}{\to}(N,\tau)
be its prequantization bundle. Let
\Gamma_{k}\subset N
be an isotropic skeleton associated to a polarization of degree
k\geq 2
of
(N,\tau)
. Then,

\bullet

A minimal Legendrian lift
\Lambda_{k}
of
\Gamma_{k}
to
\mathcal{P}
is a compact subset of
\mathcal{P}
, that intersects each circle
\pi^{-1}(p)
,
p\in\Gamma_{k}
in exactly
k
(equireparted) points,

\bullet

For any closed Legendrian submanifold
\Lambda\subset(M,\alpha)
and any smooth contact Hamiltonian
H:M\times[0,1]\to[1,+\infty)
, either there exists an
H
-chord from
\Lambda
to
\Lambda_{k}
of length
\leq\frac{1}{k}
, or the set

\bigcup_{t\in[0,\frac{1}{k}]}\Phi^{t}_{H}(\Lambda)

is not embedded.

When
H
is autonomous, the second bullet can be stated alternatively:

\bullet

There exists an
H
-chord from
\Lambda
to
\Lambda\cup\Lambda_{k}
of length
\leq\frac{1}{k}
.

In other terms, like several results on the symplectic rigidity of the lifts of natural rational Lagrangian manifolds,
e.g.

[
DRS24a
,
Kil25
]
, we get:

The Legendrian lift of a Lagrangian barrier is a Legendrian barrier.

Indeed its removal obstructs the existence of some objects that exist in
\mathcal{P}(N,\tau)
. For instance:

Corollary 2
.

There exists no contact form
\alpha^{\prime}\leq\alpha
on
(\mathcal{P}(N,\tau),\xi=\ker\alpha)
with a strong contact embedding

(\overline{D({\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}})}^{n}\times S^{1},\lambda_{\textnormal{st}}+d\theta)\hookrightarrow(\mathcal{P}(N,\tau)\backslash\Lambda_{k},\alpha^{\prime}).

The first bullet of theorem
1
relies on the fact that, although not exact, Lagrangian skeleta are rational: the areas of the discs they bound are multiples of
{\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}
. This fact is probably well-known to the experts, but we provide a precise statement and proof (see proposition
3.5
). Let us now comment on the actual content of theorem
1
, its second bullet.

In
[
EP23
]
, Entov and Polterovich introduced a notion of interlinkedness. Two Legendrian submanifolds
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}\subset(M,\xi=\ker\alpha)
are
\delta
-interlinked for
\alpha
if the contact Hamiltonian vector fields associated to smooth function that assume values larger than
1
have a chord from
\Lambda_{1}
to
\Lambda_{2}
of length at most
\delta
. Although interlinkedness does not depend on the contact form
\alpha
, the constant of interlinkedness
\delta
does: expanding
\alpha
by a factor
C
shrinks
\delta
by the same factor. This notion has been studied for instance in
[
AA25
,
DRS24b
]
In some sense, the Legendrian barriers
\Lambda_{k}
of theorem
1
are
universal interlinkers
. Let us dedicate some lines to propose a proper definition of this notion. We would like to say that a subset
X\subset(M,\xi=\ker\alpha)
is a universal
\delta
-interlinker if
(X,\Lambda)
is
\delta
-interlinked for all Legendrian submanifold
\Lambda
. Thinking a bit shows the lack of relevance of such a definition. Since there exist local Legendrian knots in arbitrary neighbourhoods of points of
M
, a universal interlinker
X
would have to intersect
all
Reeb orbits (associated to the Hamiltonian
H\equiv 1
), hence would need to be of codimension
1
, and the property would in fact have no relation with the Legendrian nature of
\Lambda
. Still, if we force the test Legendrian submanifolds to be non-local, or more precisely not localized near a Hamiltonian trajectory, the property has a chance to hold. As in
[
OS24
]
, we characterize the non-locality of a Legendrian submanifold by requiring that it has no short self-chord.

Definition 1.1
.

A subset
X
of a contact manifold
(M,\xi=\ker\alpha)
is a
(\delta,\delta^{\prime})
-universal interlinker with respect to
\alpha
if for any Legendrian submanifold
\Lambda\subset M
and smooth function
H\in\mathbb{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(M\times[0,1])
with
H\geq 1
,

\bullet

either there exists an
H
-chord from
\Lambda
to
X
of length less than
\delta
,

\bullet

or there exists
t_{0}\in[0,\delta^{\prime}]
and an
H
-chord from
\Lambda_{t_{0}}:=\Phi_{H}^{t_{0}}(\Lambda)
to itself starting at
t_{0}
and getting back to
\Lambda_{t_{0}}
before the time
\delta^{\prime}
.

The second bullet can be restated by saying that the
\delta^{\prime}
-sweep of
\Lambda
along
H
(starting at time
0
), defined by

\bigcup_{t\in[0,\delta^{\prime}]}\Phi^{t}_{H}(\Lambda)

is not embedded. When
H
is autonomous, it even simplifies to:

\bullet

there exists an
H
-chord from
\Lambda
to itself of length
\leq\delta^{\prime}
.

As above, the
notion
of universal interlinkedness depends only on the contact structure but the exact values
(\delta,\delta^{\prime})
depend on the contact form. Testing this definition with autonomous Hamiltonian is already interesting. In this case, the Hamiltonian flow is exactly the Reeb flow of the form
\alpha^{\prime}:=H^{-1}\alpha
, the function
H^{-1}
taking values in
(0,1]
. Moreover, the class
\mathcal{L}(\alpha^{\prime},\delta^{\prime})
of Legendrian submanifolds that have no self
\alpha^{\prime}
-chord of length less than
\delta^{\prime}
is invariant under the
\alpha^{\prime}
-Reeb flow. Thus,
X
being a
(\delta,\delta^{\prime})
-universal interlinker for the form
\alpha
guarantees in particular the following: for any contact form
\alpha^{\prime}:=f\alpha
with
0<f\leq 1
, for any
\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}(\alpha^{\prime},\delta^{\prime})
, there exists an
\alpha^{\prime}
-Reeb chord from
\Lambda
to
X
of length
\leq\delta
.
With this terminology, the content of theorem
1
is that
\Lambda_{k}
is a
(\frac{1}{k},\frac{1}{k})
-universal interlinker,. It might be interesting to understand the dependency between the constants
\delta
,
\delta^{\prime}
of the definition: does there exist
(\delta,\delta^{\prime})
-universal interlinkers with sharp constants
\delta\neq\delta^{\prime}
?

Some hard tools based on generating functions or Legendrian Contact Homology may detect Reeb chords. The latter has even been computed for examples of Legendrian lifts in prequantizations
[
DRG23
]
. The non-smoothness of
\Lambda_{k}
puts it however a bit outside the usual scope of these techniques. Indeed, although Legendrian lifts of Lagrangian immersions may well be embedded, the lifts of our CW-complexes are not. This already shows up for the Legendrian lift of the skeleton of
S^{2}
consisting of half great circles that cut
S^{2}
into
k
equal discs
[
OS24
]
.
Our proof consists on an embedding construction à la Mohnke that reduces the statement to a problem on estimating the Lagrangian area class of some Lagrangian embedding associated to
\Lambda
. As in Biran’s initial work, the embedding takes place in a space that somehow forgets about
\Lambda_{k}
, so that the hard part of the proof avoids the investigation of the latter. Still, recent results on detecting rigidity properties of the non-embedded isotropic skeleta
via
symplectic homology tools
[
TV23
]
give hope that these Legendrian barriers can be studied by other means than the one developped in this paper, leading to further rigidity properties. For instance, our theorem gives a bound on a ”contact Gromov width” of
\mathcal{P}(N,\tau)\backslash\Lambda_{k}
: if the latter contains a subset contactomorphic to
B^{2n}(\delta)\times S^{1}
, then
\delta\leq\frac{1}{k}
. But, as for Lagrangian skeleta, the complements of the
\Lambda_{k}
may get small whatever the contact invariant is used for measure. Let us ask a precise question in this direction. In
[
CS15
]
, V. Colin and S. Sandon introduce the Legendrian discriminant length between isotopic Legendrian submanifolds, defined as follows. Say first that a Legendrian isotopy
(\Lambda_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}\subset(M,\xi)
is embedded if

\bigcup_{t\in[0,1]}\Lambda_{t}

is a submanifold of
M
. Then define the discriminant length of a Legendrian isotopy as the minimal number
n
for which
(\Lambda_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
may be written as a concatenation of
n
embedded isotopies. And finally define the discriminant distance between two isotopic Legendrian submanifolds as the shortest length of a Legendrian isotopy between them. Then theorem
1
shows that any isotopy of a Legendrian
\Lambda
that remains in the complement of
\Lambda_{k}
through the Hamiltonian flow of a function larger than
1
has length at least
k
. Hence the following question:

Question
.

Does there exist a sequence of Legendrian pairs
(\Lambda_{1}^{k},\Lambda_{2}^{k})
in
\mathcal{P}(N,\alpha)\backslash\Lambda_{k}
whose discriminant distance in
\mathcal{P}(N,\tau)\backslash\Lambda_{k}
grows much faster than in
\mathcal{P}(N,\tau)
?

Let us finally state a rigidity result on Lagrangian skeleta:

Theorem 3
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a symplectic manifold with rational symplectic class and
(\Sigma,\lambda,L_{k})
a smooth polarization of degree
k
,
L_{k}
being its skeleton. Then any immersion of a closed Lagrangian submanifold
L
in
M\backslash L_{k}
with transverse self-intersections bounds a symplectic disc in
M\backslash L_{k}
of area
<{\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}
. In terms of Cieliebak-Mohnke’s capacity:

c_{\textnormal{Lag}}(M\backslash L_{k})\leq\frac{1}{k}.

The proof is rather straightforward after Biran’s original paper and Cieliebak-Mohnke’s. We take advantage of the present paper to provide a full proof, the road for theorem
1
passing very nearby.

Organization of the paper.

The whole paper aims at proving theomem
1
. In section
2
, we explain it in a very particular case, and try to give the general idea of the actual proof. In section
3
we gather several general statements that may be found scattered throughout the litterature. For the sake of readability and in view of a relative self-containedness, we state these results in a form that is the most suitable to our proof of theorem
1
and provide at least brief proofs. We also take a short detour to prove theorem
3
. In section
4
we set the framework of our proof, discussing symplectic disc and projective bundles and their relations with prequantizations. This section is mainly meant for setting the notation, save for the more original paragraph
4.4
. The sections
5
,
6
and
7
are the technical (and mostly computational) core of the paper.

Notation and conventions.

\bullet

The image of the Hurewicz morphism
\pi_{2}(M)\to H_{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
is denoted
H_{2}^{\textnormal{Hur}}(M,\mathbb{Z})
.

\bullet

Given a subset
S
of a manifold
M
,
\textnormal{Op}\,(S,M)
stands for an arbitrarily small neighbourhood of
S
in
M
. We sometimes write
\textnormal{Op}\,(S)
if
M
is understood from the context. We sometimes need pointed neighbourhoods , that we denote
\textnormal{Op}\,(S)^{*}:=\textnormal{Op}\,(S)\backslash S
.

\bullet

For a symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
, which may be a symplectic submanifold of an ambiant one, we write
\mathcal{A}_{\omega}:H_{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})\to\mathbb{R}
for the evaluation of
[\omega]
.

\bullet

Similarly, when
L\subset(M,\omega)
is a Lagrangian submanifold, we call
\mathcal{A}_{\omega}^{L}:H_{2}(M,L,\mathbb{Z})\to\mathbb{R}
its area morphism. When
\textnormal{Im}\,\mathcal{A}_{\omega}^{L}
is a discrete subgroup of
(\mathbb{R},+)
, we call its positive generator the area class of
L
and denote it
\mathcal{A}_{\textnormal{min}}(L)
.

\bullet

Our norms are always written with simple bars
|\cdot|
. No vector space is endowed with more than one norm, so we never dsitinguish our norms in their notation.

2.
Idea of proof

The case
H=1
of theorem
1
is already non-trivial but can be explained easily, until a certain point at least, by an argument very similar to the one exposed in
[
OS24
, Section 8]
. In this case, the Hamiltonian vector field is the Reeb vector field on
(\mathcal{P},\alpha)
, which generates the
S^{1}
-action of the circle bundle
\mathcal{P}\overset{\pi}{\longrightarrow}N
. Saying that a Legendrian
\Lambda
has no Reeb chord to
\Lambda_{k}
of length
\leq{\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}
amounts to saying that it does not intersect the
-{\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}
-sweep of
\Lambda_{k}
under the Reeb flow, defined by

\bigcup_{t\in[-{\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}},0]}\Phi_{R_{\alpha}}^{t}(\Lambda_{k}).

Since
\Lambda_{k}
consists of exactly
k
equireparted points in each fiber circle over the points of
L_{k}
, this
-{\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}
-sweep is exactly
\pi^{-1}(L_{k})
, so the absence of Reeb chord from
\Lambda
to
\Lambda_{k}
of length
\leq{\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}
means exactly that
L:=\pi(\Lambda)
lies in
N\backslash L_{k}
. In dimension
3
,
N
is a surface that
L_{k}
cuts into discs of area
{\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}
. Thus
L
is a Lagrangian immersion into a
2
-disc of area
{\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}
. It therefore bounds a disc of area
<{\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}
, and any Legendrian lift of the boundary of this disc starting at a point in
\Lambda
provides a path whose endpoints are on the same fiber, thus a Reeb self-chord of
\Lambda
. The length of this Reeb-chord is the area of the disc, hence the result. In higher dimension, theorem
3
provides a disc of area
<{\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}
bounded by
L
that can be used exactly as in dimension
3
to produce the short Reeb chord we are seeking. Of course, when
H
is not constant, the Reeb flow is not periodic any more, and the above argument breaks down.

Let us now describe briefly the actual proof of theorem
1
. In the general framework of a time dependent contact Hamiltonian
H
on
(\mathcal{P},\alpha)=\mathcal{P}(N^{2n},\tau)
, instead of projecting the picture down from
(\mathcal{P},\alpha)
to
(N,\tau)
, we rather lift it to the symplectization
S\mathcal{P}(N,\tau):=(\mathcal{P}\times\mathbb{R}_{*}^{+},R\alpha)
and use Mohnke’s trick. The latter associates to an embedded
T
-sweep of
\Lambda
in
\mathcal{P}\backslash\Lambda_{k}
a Lagrangian submanifold
L
in
S\mathcal{P}(N,\tau)\backslash(\Lambda_{k}\times\mathbb{R})
with
\mathcal{A}_{\textnormal{min}}(L)=T
. The assumption
H\geq 1
guarantees that
L\subset S^{-}\mathcal{P}(N,\tau):=\mathcal{P}\times(0,1)
. The latter is symplectomorphic to the symplectic disc bundle
\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)\to(N,\tau)
. The technical core of the paper consists in computing a polarization
(\Sigma_{k},\lambda_{k})
of degree
k
on
\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)
whose skeleton is precisely
\Lambda_{k}\times[0,1)
. Since
L
does not intersect this skeleton, Biran’s decomposition theorem shows that it lies in the symplectic disc bundle over
\Sigma_{k}
of radius
{\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}
, which compactifies to a ruled symplectic manifold of width
<{\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}
. The Lagrangian capacity of the latter can be shown to be bounded by
{\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}
. Thus,

T=\mathcal{A}_{\textnormal{min}}(L)<{\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}},

so no
H
-sweep of length larger than
{\nicefrac{{1}}{{k}}}
can be embedded into
\mathcal{P}\backslash\Lambda_{k}
.

Besides the main difficulty of uncovering a polarization with computable skeleton, the secondary difficulty in this line of argument is to get a correct notion of polarization of
\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)
, which is an open symplectic manifold. The concept of Liouville polarization introduced in
[
OS24
]
is not relevant here. Our approach consists in compactifying
\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)
to a sphere bundle, which is unfortunately not symplectic. Biran’s decomposition theorem must then be adapted to this context, and the analysis requires
k\geq 2
. This last assumption is not of technical nature. The result does not hold for
(N,\tau)=(\mathbb{P}^{n},\omega_{\text{FS}}(1))
and
k=1
. My guess would be that the result is never true when
k=1
.

3.
Technical preliminaries

We collect in this section a certain number of technical results, that may be considered folklore.

3.1.
Lagrangian capacity of ruled symplectic manifolds.

Theorem
1
is based on the following rigidity result. We recall that
H_{2}^{\textnormal{Hur}}(Y)
denotes the image of the Hurewitz morphism
\pi_{2}(Y)\to H_{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(S^{2},\omega_{{\text{FS}}}(a))\hookrightarrow(M^{2n},\omega)\overset{\pi}{\longrightarrow}Y^{2n-2}
be a closed symplectic manifold with the structure of a symplectic sphere bundle, the parameter
a\in\mathbb{R}
being the area of the fibers. Assume the existence of two disjoint sections
Y\overset{\sigma_{0,\infty}}{\longrightarrow}\Sigma_{0,\infty}\subset(M^{2n},\omega)
such that
\sigma_{\infty}^{*}\omega
is a symplectic form, and denote
X:=M\backslash\Sigma_{\infty}
. If

a<\textnormal{Im}\,(\mathcal{A}_{\sigma_{0}^{*}\omega}:H^{\textnormal{Hur}}_{2}(Y)\to\mathbb{R})\cap\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+},

then any closed Lagrangian sumanifold
L\subset(X,\omega)
has an attached symplectic disc

u:(D,\partial D)\longrightarrow(X,L)

of area
\displaystyle\int_{D}u^{*}\omega\leq a
.

This result follows from the usual SFT-type argument, as used for instance in
[
CM18
]
, and is based on the following even more classical lemma:

Lemma 3.2
.

Under the assumptions of theorem
3.1
, let
V^{\prime}\Subset V
be two neighbourhoods of
\Sigma_{\infty}
and
p\in M\backslash V
. For any
\omega
-compatible almost complex structure
J
on
M
, there exists an arbitrarily close perturbation
J^{\prime}
of
J
in the
\mathbb{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
-topology that coincides with
J
on
(M\backslash V)\cup V^{\prime}
and a pseudo-holomorphic sphere

u:(S^{2},j)\longrightarrow(M,J^{\prime})

that verifies
[u]=[\pi^{-1}(\{\star\})]=:F\in H_{2}(M)
and
p\in\textnormal{Im}\,(u)
.

This lemma directly follows from Gromov’s theory of pseudo-holomorphic curves
[
Gro85
,
MS12
]
. A full proof under slightly different assumptions can be consulted for instance in
[
Bir01
, Theorem 5.3.A]
. The difference with our context is a trading between Biran’s assumptions
(L)
and ours on the existence of the symplectic sections
\sigma_{0},\sigma_{\infty}
and the area estimate of the fibers. Both aim at forcing the compactness of the moduli space of
J
-holomorphic spheres in the class of the fiber, that we call
F
. In our context, if
u_{n}:S^{2}\to M
is a sequence of
J
-holomorphic spheres with
[u_{n}]=F
, Gromov’s compactness theorem gives a limit of a subsequence that converges in Gromov’s sense to a bunch of
J
-holomorphic spheres
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}:S^{2}\to M
, with
\sum[v_{i}]=F
. Decomposing

H_{2}(M,\mathbb{R})=\langle F\rangle+\sigma_{0}(H_{2}(Y,\mathbb{R})),

write
[v_{i}]=\alpha_{i}F+B_{i}
. Since
\Sigma_{0}\cap\Sigma_{\infty}=\emptyset
,
B_{i}\cdot[\Sigma_{\infty}]=0
, so
[v_{i}]\cdot[\Sigma_{\infty}]=\alpha_{i}
. Since
\Sigma_{\infty}
is
J
-holomorphic, positivity of intersections implies that all the
\alpha_{i}
but one vanish, say
\alpha_{1}=1
,
\alpha_{j}=0

\forall j>1
. Since the classes in
\sigma_{0}(H_{2}^{\textnormal{Hur}}(Y))
have symplectic area larger than the area
a
of the fiber by assumption, no such class can appear as a bubble, so
j=1
and the Gromov limit consists of one component exactly. This shows the compactness, and the rest of the proof goes as in

3.2.
Lagrangian lift of the contact flow of a Legendrian submanifold.

The following proposition is a reformulation of Mohnke’s trick
[
Moh01
]
, that we already used in a slightly weaker form in
[
OS24
]
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
(M,\alpha)
be a contact manifold,
\Lambda\subset M
be a closed Legendrian submanifold and
H\in\mathbb{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(M\times[0,T])
a smooth positive time-dependent contact Hamiltonian such that the map

\begin{array}[]{rcccl}\Psi&:&\Lambda\times[0,T]&\longrightarrow&M\\
&&(x,t)&\longmapsto&\Phi^{t}_{H}(x)\end{array}

is an embedding.

Then there exists a Lagrangian embedding of
\Lambda\times S^{1}
in the symplectization
SM:=(M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*},d(R\alpha))
with the following properties:

\bullet

if
\pi:SM\to M
denotes the obvious projection,
\pi(L)
is the sweep of
\Lambda
under the flow of
H
:

\pi(L)=\{\Phi_{H}^{s}(x),\;x\in\Lambda,s\in[0,T]\}.

In particular, if
H
has no chord between
\Lambda
and some closed subset
X
, then
L\cap(X\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*})=\emptyset
.

\bullet

\mathcal{A}_{{\textnormal{min}}}(L)=T
.

\bullet

If
H\geq 1
,
L\subset S^{-}M:=M\times(0,1]
.

Proof:
We recall that the contact isotopy associated to
(H,\alpha)
is the flow of the time-dependent vector field
X_{s}
defined by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\alpha(X_{s})=H_{s},\\
d_{x}\alpha(X_{s},\cdot)=R_{\alpha}\cdot H_{s}(x)\alpha-dH_{s}.\end{array}\right.

Since
\Lambda
and
X
are closed and
H
is smooth,
H
can be extended to
M\times[0,T+\varepsilon]
for some small
\varepsilon>0
, still with no Reeb-chords from
\Lambda
to itself or to
X
.
Define the map

\begin{array}[]{rcccl}\Phi&:&\Lambda\times[0,T+\varepsilon]\times(0,1]&\longrightarrow&M\times(0,+\infty)\\
&&(x,s,t)&\longmapsto&(\Phi_{H}^{s}(x),{\nicefrac{{t}}{{H_{s}(x)}}}),\end{array}

which is an embedding because of the injectivity of
\Psi
.
Then

\begin{array}[]{lll}&\Phi^{*}\alpha\big(\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\big)&=\alpha(X_{s})=H_{s}(x),\\
&\Phi^{*}\alpha\big(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\big)&=0,\\
\forall u\in T\Lambda,&\Phi^{*}\alpha(u)&=\alpha(\Phi_{*}u)\\
&&=\alpha(\Phi_{H*}^{s}u)\\
&&=0.\end{array}

The vanishing of the last expression holds because
\Phi_{H}^{s}
is a contact diffeomorphism and
u\in T\Lambda\subset\ker\alpha
, so
\Phi_{H*}^{s}u
remains in the contact distribution
\ker\alpha
for all
s
. Thus

\Phi^{*}\alpha=H_{s}(x)ds

and

\Phi^{*}(R\alpha)=\frac{t}{H_{s}(x)}\Phi^{*}\alpha=tds.

Let now
\gamma=(s(\theta),t(\theta)):S^{1}\to[0,T+\varepsilon]\times(0,1]
be an embedded loop that encloses an area
T
(for the form
dt\wedge ds
) and

L:=\Phi(\Lambda\times\textnormal{Im}\,\gamma)=\{(\Phi_{H}^{s(\theta)}(x),\frac{t(\theta)}{H_{s(\theta)}(x)}),x\in\Lambda,\theta\in S^{1}\}.

By the above computation,
L
is an embedded Lagrangian submanifold, that obviously projects to the
H
-sweep of
\Lambda
. If
H\geq 1
,
L\subset\{R\leq 1\}=S^{-}M
. Finally, since the symplectic form on
SM
is
d(R\alpha)
,
\mathcal{A}_{{\textnormal{min}}}(L)
is simply the minimal positive
R\alpha
-action of the loops in
L
. Since
H_{1}(L)\overset{\Phi_{*}}{\simeq}H_{1}(\Lambda)\oplus\langle[\gamma]\rangle
and
\Phi^{*}(R\alpha)=tds
we see that the area morphism vanishes on
H_{1}(\Lambda)
and equals
T
on
[\gamma]
. Thus indeed,

\mathcal{A}_{{\textnormal{min}}}(L)=T
.
\square

3.3.
On the area class of some skeleta.

We introduce here the most basic notion of polarization and prove the first bullet of theorem
1
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a closed symplectic manifold with integral cohomology class. A polarization of degree
k
is a s
tuple
(\Sigma,\lambda,\varphi)
composed of a symplectic hypersurface Poincaré-dual to
k\omega
, a Liouville form
\lambda
on
X\backslash\Sigma
and a Morse function
\varphi
on
X\backslash\Sigma
that tends to
-\infty
at
\Sigma
, such that the Liouville vector field
X_{\lambda}
is gradient-like for
\varphi
.

The skeleton of
(\Sigma,\lambda)
is defined as the set of points that do not reach
\Sigma
under the Liouville flow of
\lambda
and is denoted
\textnormal{Skel}(\Sigma,\lambda)
. It is an isotropic CW-complex, composed of the stable manifolds of the critical points of
\varphi
.

Since the skeleton of a polarization is isotropic, we can define an area morphism

\begin{array}[]{rcccl}\mathcal{A}&:&H_{2}(X,\textnormal{Skel}(\Sigma,\lambda),\mathbb{R}))&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{R}\\
&&\alpha&\longmapsto&\int_{\alpha}\omega,\end{array}

where the integral is meant on any representative of the class
\alpha
by a
2
-chain in
X
with boundary in
\textnormal{Skel}(\Sigma,\lambda)
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be an integral symplectic manifold with a polarization
(\Sigma,\lambda)
of degree
k
. Then the image of the area morphism of
\textnormal{Skel}(\Sigma,\lambda)
lies in
\frac{1}{k}\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof:
The Liouville form
\lambda
has residue
-\frac{1}{k}
along
\Sigma
, in the sense that its integral along a loop that turns once and positively around
\Sigma
is very close to
-\frac{1}{k}
when this loop is
\mathbb{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
-close to a constant in
\Sigma
. This can be shown by considering any
2
-cycle
S
in
\Sigma
, perturbing it to a
2
-cycle
\widetilde{S}
in
M
that intersects
\Sigma
transversally, and computing its area in two different ways, one using the Poincaré-Duality condition of
\Sigma
and one by using Stokes theorem on the complement of very small discs around the intersections of
\widetilde{S}
with
\Sigma
(see for instance
[
Ops15
]
for a complete proof).

Let now
S
denote a
2
-cycle of
M
with boundary on
\textnormal{Skel}(W,\lambda)
, and
\partial S
the boundary cycle in
\textnormal{Skel}(W,\lambda)
.
Since
\textnormal{Skel}(W,\lambda)
is the disjoint union of the stable manifolds of the critical points of
\varphi
, we can write

\partial S=\cup_{x\in\text{Crit}\,\varphi}\gamma_{x},

with
\gamma_{x}:=\partial S\cap W^{s}(x)
. Under the positive Liouville flow (that leaves
\textnormal{Skel}(W,\lambda)
invariant),
\gamma_{x}
deforms to a union of Liouville trajectories that link

to other critical points in the closure of

. We therefore get a new representative

of

made of a union of orbits of the Liouville flow. Since

is isotropic and

is a Liouville form, the

, on which

\int_{\Gamma}\lambda=0.

In view of the preliminary remark on the residue of

, we therefore see that

\mathcal{A}_{\omega}(S)=\frac{1}{k}[S]\cdot[\Sigma]\in\frac{1}{k}\mathbb{Z}

(in this equality, the intersection means homological intersections between a

-cycle relative to
\textnormal{Skel}(W,\lambda)
and a

\square

As a consequence, we get the first assertion of theorem

3.4.
On quasi-holomorphic sections.

In our context, quasi-holomorphic sections were introduced by Giroux in
[

,
Gir17
]
. We recall now their definition and the relevant properties.
Let
(V,\omega,J,g)
be a linear symplectic space with an
\omega
-compatible linear complex structure
J
, that altogether define the scalar product
g=\omega(\cdot,J\cdot)
. The metric
g
provides a dual norm on
V^{*}
,
\omega
an isomorphism
X_{\centerdot}:V^{*}\to V
and the complex structure an isomorphism
{}^{c}:V^{*}\to V^{*}
given by
\alpha^{c}=\alpha(J\cdot)
(it is an anti-involution). In fact,
the dual norm is euclidean,
X_{\centerdot}
is an isometry and
\alpha^{c}(X_{\alpha})=|\alpha|^{2}
.
Indeed,

|\alpha|=\max_{|X|=1}\alpha(X)=\max_{|X|=1}\omega(X_{\alpha},X)=\max_{|X|=1}g(X_{\alpha},JX)=|X_{\alpha}|.

One checks without difficulty that
X_{\alpha^{c}}=-JX_{\alpha}
. For a complex-valued linear form
\Lambda\in V^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}:=V^{*}\otimes\mathbb{C}
, we define

\begin{array}[]{l}\alpha^{(0,1)}:=\frac{1}{2}(\alpha+i\alpha^{c})\\
\alpha^{(1,0)}:=\frac{1}{2}(\alpha-i\alpha^{c}),\end{array}

so that

\alpha^{(0,1)}(Jv)=-i\alpha^{(0,1)}v,\hskip 8.5359pt\alpha^{(1,0)}(Jv)=i\alpha^{(1,0)}v,\hskip 8.5359pt\alpha=\alpha^{(0,1)}+\alpha^{(1,0)}.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\Lambda=\alpha+i\beta\in V^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}
,
\alpha,\beta\in V^{*}
. Assume that there exists
\kappa<1
such that

|\Lambda^{(0,1)}|\leq\kappa|\Lambda^{(1,0)}|

(we say that
\Lambda
is
\kappa
-quasi-holomorphic). Then

\omega(X_{\beta},X_{\alpha})\geq\frac{1-\kappa^{2}}{1+\kappa^{2}}\frac{|\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2}}{2}.

As a result, there exists a function
\kappa^{\prime}(\kappa)<1
that tends to
0
when
\kappa\to 0
such that

|X_{\alpha}-JX_{\beta}|\leq\kappa^{\prime}{\textnormal{min}}(|X_{\alpha}|,|X_{\beta}|).

Proof:

Notice first that given
\alpha\in V^{*}
,

|\alpha+i\alpha^{c}|=\sqrt{2}|\alpha|.

One inequality is obvious, and the other is obtained by feeding
\alpha+i\alpha^{c}
with the vector
v+Jv
, where
\alpha(v)=|\alpha|
,
|v|=1
(such a vector automatically verifies
\alpha(Jv)=0
because
v\perp Jv
). Now

\begin{array}[]{l}2\Lambda^{(1,0)}=\Lambda-i\Lambda^{c}=\alpha+\beta^{c}+i(\alpha+\beta^{c})^{c}\\
2\Lambda^{(0,1)}=\Lambda+i\Lambda^{c}=\alpha-\beta^{c}+i(\alpha-\beta^{c})^{c}\\
\end{array}

Thus the
\kappa
-quasi-holomorphicity of
\Lambda
gives

|\alpha-\beta^{c}|\leq\kappa|\alpha+\beta^{c}|.

Squaring the last inequality and developping gives

g(\alpha,\beta^{c})\geq\frac{1-\kappa^{2}}{1+\kappa^{2}}\frac{|\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2}}{2}.

Putting
f(\kappa)=\frac{1-\kappa^{2}}{1+\kappa^{2}}\in(0,1)
,

\omega(X_{\beta},X_{\alpha})=g(X_{\alpha},-JX_{\beta})\geq f(\kappa)\frac{|X_{\alpha}|^{2}+|JX_{\beta}|^{2}}{2}\geq f(\kappa)|X_{\alpha}||JX_{\beta}|.

Thus, the unoriented angle
\theta
between
X_{\alpha}
and
JX_{\beta}
verifies

\theta\leq\arccos(f(\kappa)),

which tends to
0
when
\kappa\to 0
. Finally, since
g(X_{\alpha},-JX_{\beta})\leq|X_{\alpha}||JX_{\beta}|
, the last inequality gives

\frac{|X_{\alpha}|^{2}+|JX_{\beta}|^{2}}{2}\geq|X_{\alpha}||X_{\beta}|\geq f(\kappa)\frac{|X_{\alpha}|^{2}+|JX_{\beta}|^{2}}{2}.

So when
\kappa\approx 0
, we almost have equality in the above inequalities, so the norms of
X_{\alpha}
and
X_{\beta}
are very close.
\square
​​

Given now a symplectic manifold endowed with an almost complex structure
(M,\omega,J,g)
and a Hermitian line bundle
\mathcal{L}\to M
, the definitions above obviously apply pointwise, and provide a map
X_{\centerdot}:\Omega^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})=\Gamma(T^{*}M)\to\Gamma(TM)
, dual norms on
TM^{*}\otimes\mathcal{L}
, and fiberwise isomorphisms
{}^{c}:T^{*}M\otimes\mathcal{L}\to T^{*}M\otimes\mathcal{L}
. Given a Hermitian connection
\nabla
on
\mathcal{L}
, we can define its linear and anti-linear parts:

\nabla^{\prime}:=\nabla^{(1,0)}\hskip 14.22636pt\nabla^{\prime\prime}:=\nabla^{(0,1)}.

A section
s:M\to\mathcal{L}
is said
\kappa
-quasi-holomorphic if

\forall x\in M,\hskip 8.5359pt|\nabla^{\prime\prime}s(x)|\leq\kappa|\nabla^{\prime}s(x)|.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
(M,\omega,J,g)
be a symplectic manifold with a compatible almost complex structure,
\mathcal{L}\to M
a Hermitian line bundle over
M
and
s:M\to\mathcal{L}
a
\kappa
-quasi-holomorphic section, with
\kappa<1
. On
M\backslash\{s=0\}
, write
\nabla s=(\alpha-i\beta)s
,
\alpha,\beta\in\Omega^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
. Then,

\bullet

\alpha=d\ln|s|
,

\bullet

\omega(X_{\beta},X_{\alpha})=X_{\beta}\cdot(-\ln|s|)\geq\frac{1-\kappa^{2}}{1+\kappa^{2}}\frac{|\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2}}{2}
,

\bullet

\nabla s(p)=0\Longleftrightarrow d\ln|s|(p)=0
,

\bullet

On
\{s\neq 0\}
,
\nabla s(p)
either vanishes or is surjective.

Proof:
Let
s
be our quasi-holomorphic section. In
M\backslash\{s=0\}
, for any smooth complex-valued function
f
, we can write

\nabla f\frac{s}{|s|}=(df+f\vartheta)\frac{s}{|s|},

and
\vartheta
is a connection
1
-form. Since the connection is Hermitian,
\vartheta=-i\beta
,
\beta\in\Omega^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
. Applying the formula for
f=|s|
further gives

\nabla s=(d|s|-i|s|\beta)\frac{s}{|s|}=(d\ln|s|-i\beta)s.

This concludes the first bullet.
The remaining assertions just follow by applying lemma
3.7
pointwise.
\square
​​

3.5.
Connections on powers of line bundles

Along this paper, sections of complex line bundles of the form
\mathcal{L}^{\otimes k}
(denoted for short
\mathcal{L}^{k}
) will be used intensively. These power line bundles are naturally equipped with connections as long as
\mathcal{L}
is, and the connections we consider throughout the whole paper are of this kind. This is very standard, but we recall it for self-containedness, highlighting the points of further use.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
(\mathcal{L}\overset{\pi}{\longrightarrow}M,\nabla)
be a hermitian line bundle equipped with a unitary connection. There is a natural unitary connection
\nabla^{k}
on
\mathcal{L}^{\otimes k}
defined in the following way:

\bullet

On an open set
U\subset M
above which
\mathcal{L}
is trivial, pick a non-vanishing section
e:U\to\mathcal{L}
and write
\nabla e=i\theta e
,
\theta\in\Omega^{1}(M)
(the form is real-valued because
\nabla
is unitary),

\bullet

Define
\nabla^{k}e^{\otimes k}:=k\theta e^{\otimes k}
,

\bullet

Extend
\nabla^{k}
on all sections above
U
by the Leibniz rule.

This connection has the following properties:

(1)

If
s:[0,1]\to\mathcal{L}
is a
\nabla
-parallel section along
\gamma:[0,1]\to M
then
s^{\otimes k}
is
\nabla^{k}
-parallel along
\gamma
.

(2)

If
M
is equipped with an almost complex structure
J
and
s:M\to\mathcal{L}
is
\kappa
-quasi-holomorphic, so is
s^{\otimes k}
.

(3)

If
\nabla
has curvature
\vartheta
,
\nabla^{\otimes k}
has curvature
k\vartheta
.

Proof:
The three consequences are completely straightforward and we leave them to the reader. Checking that
\nabla^{k}
indeed provides a well-defined connection on
\mathcal{L}^{k}
is equivalent to the fact that it is natural in the sense that it does not depend on the section
e
we pick in this definition. We prove this point. Let
(e,\theta,\nabla^{k})
be defined on
U
as proposed in the statement. Let
e^{\prime}
be another non-vanishing section above
U
and
\theta^{\prime}\in\Omega^{1}(U)
its associated
1
-form, meaning that
\nabla e^{\prime}=\theta^{\prime}e^{\prime}
. Then
e^{\prime}=fe
for some smooth function
f:U\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
and
\nabla e^{\prime}=(\frac{df}{f}+\theta)e^{\prime}
, so
\theta^{\prime}=\theta+\frac{df}{f}
. On the other hand,
e^{\prime}{}^{\otimes k}=f^{k}e^{\otimes k}
so

\nabla^{k}e^{\prime}{}^{\otimes k}=(df^{k}+k\theta)e^{\otimes k}=k(\frac{df}{f}+\theta)e^{\prime\otimes k}=k\theta^{\prime}e^{\prime}{}^{\otimes k}.

This shows indeed that this definition of
\nabla^{k}
does not depend on the chosen section
e
.
\square
​​

Remark 3.10
.

The three properties listed above have implications of different nature. The point (
3
) tells that these connections are compatible with the symplectic category, in the sense that if
\nabla
has curvature
-i\omega
,
\nabla^{k}
has curvature
-ik\omega
. The point (
2
) shows that these connections are compatible with the complex category, allowing to compute examples in the Kähler setting, for instance the holomorphic
\nabla^{k}
sections on
\mathcal{O}(k)
are holomorphic in the usual sense provided the connection
\nabla
on
\mathcal{O}(1)
is the most natural connection. Finally (
1
) happens to be technically very usefull in our proof.

4.
Negative symplectization of prequantization bundles.

In this section we introduce symplectic disc bundles and discuss their relations with prequantization bundles. Most of it is very classical and already explained in Biran’s orginal paper, where symplectic disc bundles first appear
[
Bir01
]
. Sections
4.2
and
4.3
are a bit more original, describing a rather obvious but non-symplectic compactification of these objects. Leaving the purely symplectic world has a price in terms of the attention we need to pay, but provides the sharpest possible results. Degeneracy is dealt with an unusual almost-complex structure, introduced in section
4.1
.

4.1.
Symplectic disc bundles.

Let
(N,\tau)
be a symplectic manifold with integral symplectic class and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. Let
\mathcal{L}\overset{\pi}{\to}(N,\tau)
be a line bundle with
c_{1}(\mathcal{L})=\ell[\tau]

equipped with a radial coordinate
R
and a connexion
1
-form
\Theta
that satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\Theta|_{\mathcal{L}_{p}}=d\theta,\\
d\Theta=-\ell\pi^{*}\tau.\end{array}\right.

The closed two-form

\omega_{0}:=\pi^{*}\tau+d(R\Theta)=(1-\ell R)\pi^{*}\tau+dR\wedge\Theta

(4.1)

is symplectic on
\{R<\frac{1}{\ell}\}
. This subset of
\mathcal{L}
is the symplectic disc bundle over
(N,\tau)
associated to
\mathcal{L}
:

\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau,\ell):=\big(\{R<\frac{1}{\ell}\}\subset\mathcal{L},\omega_{0}\big).

The
1
-form

\lambda_{0}:=(R-\frac{1}{\ell})\Theta,

defined on the complement of the zero section
N_{0}:=0_{\mathcal{L}}
, is a Liouville form for
\omega_{0}
with radial Liouville vector field

X_{0}:=(R-\frac{1}{\ell})\frac{\partial}{\partial R}.

One main feature of these symplectic disc bundles is that they provide explicit local models for some symplectic divisors:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a symplectic manifold and
\Sigma\subset M
a smooth symplectic divisor. If
[\Sigma]=\textnormal{\small PD}(\ell[\omega])
, or if the latter condition holds locally, in the sense that the symplectic normal bundle to
\Sigma
has first Chern class
\ell[\omega_{|\Sigma}]
, then
\textnormal{Op}\,(\Sigma,M)
can be symplectically identified with

\textnormal{\small SDB}_{\varepsilon}(\Sigma,\omega_{|\Sigma},\ell):=\{R<\varepsilon\}\subset\textnormal{\small SDB}(\Sigma,\omega_{|\Sigma},\ell)

for some
\varepsilon\ll 1
.

In most of the present paper, we will only need to consider

\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau):=\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau,\ell=1).

Given a
\tau
-compatible almost complex structure
j
on
N
, one may lift
j
to
\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)
by pulling back the natural almost complex structure on
\mathcal{L}
by the radial map

(z,R,\theta)\longmapsto(z,\frac{R}{1-R},\theta),

that takes the disc bundle to the whole of
\mathcal{L}
. On checks that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}J(T\mathcal{L}_{p})=T\mathcal{L}_{p}\text{ and }J(\ker\Theta)=\ker\Theta,\\
J\frac{\partial}{\partial R}=\frac{1}{2R(1-R)}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta},\\
J(U)=\pi^{*}j\pi_{*}U\hskip 8.5359pt\forall U\in\ker\Theta\end{array}\right.

(in the last line and throughout the paper,
\pi^{*}
denotes the inverse of the restricted isomorphism
\pi_{*|\ker\Theta}:\ker\Theta\to TN
).

4.2.
Blow-up of the zero-section, contact compactification.

The negative part of the symplectization of the prequantization bundle
(\mathcal{P},\alpha)\to(N,\tau)
, denoted
S^{\leq 0}\mathcal{P}
is defined by

S^{\leq 0}\mathcal{P}:=(\mathcal{P}\times(0,1],d(R\alpha)).

It is an open symplectic manifold, with two ends, for
R\in\{0,1\}
. The end
\{R=1\}
is a contact type boundary, with periodic Reeb vector field, so it can be compactified by standard reduction procedure. This compactification leads to the symplectic disc bundle over
(N,\tau)
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(N,\tau)
be a symplectic manifold with integral symplectic class,
(\mathcal{P},\alpha)
its prequantization bundle and
\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)
its symplectic disc bundle. The map

\begin{array}[]{rcccl}\Phi&:&\big(\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)\backslash N_{0},\lambda_{0}\big)&\longrightarrow&S^{<0}\mathcal{P}(N,\tau):=(\mathcal{P}\times(0,1),R\alpha)\\
&&(z,R,\theta)&\longmapsto&(z,1-R,-\theta)\end{array}

is a well-defined exact symplectomorphism (
\Phi^{*}(R\alpha)=\lambda_{0}
).

Proof:
The map
\Phi
is well-defined because
S\mathcal{P}
is a complex line bundle over
N
of Chern-class equals to the Euler class of
\mathcal{P}\to N
, which is
[\tau]
, hence opposite to the Chern-class of
\mathcal{L}
. Obviously,

\Phi^{*}(R\alpha)=(1-R)\Phi^{*}\alpha
and since
\alpha
is a connection
1
-form on
\mathcal{P}
with curvature
\tau
,
\Phi^{*}\alpha
is a
1
-form on
\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)
, invariant under radial transformations, and

\begin{array}[]{l}\Phi^{*}\alpha|_{F}=-d\theta\\
\Phi^{*}d\alpha=\pi^{*}\tau.\end{array}

Thus
-\Phi^{*}\alpha
is a connection form on
\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)\backslash N_{0}
with curvature
-\pi^{*}\tau
, so we can assume that it coincides with
\Theta
. Thus,

\Phi^{*}(R\alpha)=(1-R)\Phi^{*}\alpha_{0}=(R-1)\Theta=\lambda_{0}.\hskip 56.9055pt\square

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
(\mathcal{P},\alpha)\to(N,\tau)
be a contact prequantization bundle. Let
(\Sigma,\lambda)
be a polarization of
N
of degree
k
and
\Gamma
its skeleton (see definition
3.4
). Let
\Lambda
be the
k
-fold Legendrian lift of
\Gamma
to
\mathcal{P}
described in corollary
3.6
and
\Phi:\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)\backslash N_{0}\to S^{<0}\mathcal{P}
the symplectic identification provided by lemma
4.2
. Then

\Phi^{-1}(\Lambda\times(0,1))

is the unique
\omega_{0}
-isotropic CW-complex that projects under
\pi
to
\Gamma
, and such that the fibers of this projection are
k
radial rays. This uniqueness is meant up to global fibered rotation of fixed angles, i.e. maps of the form
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}:(z,R,\theta)\mapsto(z,R,\theta+\alpha)
.

Proof:
The subset
\Phi^{-1}(\Lambda\times(0,1))
is indeed an isotropic CW-complex and its projection to
N
is

\pi\circ\Phi^{-1}(\Lambda\times(0,1))=\pi(\Lambda\times(0,1))=\pi(\Lambda)=\Gamma.

Since
\pi_{|\Lambda}
is a
k
-fold cover of
\Lambda
and since
\Phi^{-1}(\{\star\}\times(0,1))
is a radial ray, the fibers of
\pi
in
\Phi^{-1}(\Lambda\times(0,1))
are indeed
k
radial rays.
Further, any global rotation of this subset (by a map
R_{\alpha}:(z,R,\theta)\mapsto(z,R,\theta+\alpha)
where
\alpha
is a constant) is of the form

\Phi^{-1}\big(\Lambda^{\prime}\times(0,1)\big)
, where
\Lambda^{\prime}
is obtained from
\Lambda
by a fibered rotation in
\mathcal{P}
with constant angle. Thus
\Lambda^{\prime}
is a Legendrian
k
-fold cover of
\Gamma
, so
\pi(R_{\alpha}(\varphi^{-1}(\Lambda\times(0,1))))=\Gamma
as well. It remains to check the uniqueness part of the statement.

Let
L
be an isotropic radially invariant CW-complex of
\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)\backslash N_{0}
whose projection to
N
is a
k
-fold cover of
\Gamma
(in the sense of the lemma). Then
\Phi(L)
is isotropic and radially invariant in
\mathcal{P}
, so is of the form
\widetilde{\Lambda}\times(0,1)
for some Legendrian CW-complex
\widetilde{\Lambda}
. Since moreover

\Gamma=\pi(L)=\pi(\widetilde{\Lambda}\times(0,1))=\pi(\widetilde{\Lambda}),

\widetilde{\Lambda}
is indeed a Legendrian
k
-fold lift of
\Gamma
.
\square
​​

4.3.
Projectivization of symplectic disc bundles.

We now aim at compactifying the end
\{R=0\}
of the negative symplectization of
\mathcal{P}
, which can be obtained equivalently as a compactification of
\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)
at the boundary
\{R=1\}
of the disc bundle by lemma
4.2
. In
[
Bir99
]
, Biran used a compactification of
\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)
obtained in the following way:

\bullet

Remove a neighbourhood of the boundary, thus considering
\{R<{1-\delta}\}\subset\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)
,

\bullet

Consider
\delta\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau,-1):=\big(\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau,-1),\delta\omega_{0}\big)
.

\bullet

Observe that
\{0<R<\delta\}\subset\delta\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau,-1)
is exact symplectomorphic to
\{1-2\delta<R<1-\delta\}\subset\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)
,
via
the most simple map
\psi:(z,R,\theta)\mapsto(z,1-\delta-R,-\theta)
.

\bullet

Define therefore

\textnormal{\small SPB}(N,\tau,\delta):=\{R<1-\delta\}\subset{\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)}\underset{\psi}{\bigcup}\{R<\delta\}\subset{\delta\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau,-1))}.

The result of this compactification is a symplectic sphere bundle over
N
(hence a ruled symplectic manifold), the
0
and
\infty
-sections being symplectic, namely
(N,\tau)
and
(N,\delta\tau)
, respectively. The area of the fibers is
1-\delta
, and this compactification contains
\textnormal{\small SDB}_{1-\delta}(N,\tau)
.
Unfortunately, this symplectic manifold has symplectic class in
\frac{1}{N(\delta)}\mathbb{Z}
, with
N(\delta)\to+\infty
when
\delta\to 0
, so has polarizations only in very large degrees when
\delta
becomes small, which makes it useless in this paper.

Instead, we describe now a non-symplectic compactification of
\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)
into a sphere bundle with a smooth closed
2
-form that degenerates along the section at infinity. As we have already seen,
\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)
lies in a hermitian line bundle
\mathcal{L}
, on which we have a smooth
2
-form

\omega_{0}=\pi^{*}\tau+d(R\Theta)=(1-R)\pi^{*}\tau+dR\wedge\Theta

which is symplectic on
\{R<1\}\subset\mathcal{L}
and degenerates on
\{R=1\}
although it remains symplectic fiberwise. Let
\textnormal{\small SPB}(N,\tau):=\{R\leq 1\}_{/\sim}
where
(z,1,\theta)\sim(z,1,\theta^{\prime})
for all
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in S^{1}
. As a smooth manifold, it is the projectivization of
\mathcal{L}
, so we call it a
symplectic projective bundle
. Since
\Theta
is
S^{1}
-invariant,
\omega_{0}
extends smoothly to
\textnormal{\small SPB}(N,\tau)
, although not to a symplectic form on the whole space: it degenerates along the ”section at infinity”
N_{\infty}:=\{R=1\}_{/\sim}
. Nonetheless, this not really symplectic pair
(\textnormal{\small SPB}(N,\tau),\omega_{0})
will be convenient for our purpose. We still denote
N_{0}:=\{R=0\}\subset\textnormal{\small SPB}(N,\tau)
the zero-section. Notice that

\textnormal{\small PD}([\omega_{0}])=[N_{0}].

Also, the almost complex structure
J
on
\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)
defined in §
4.1
extends smoothly to an almost complex structure on
\textnormal{\small SPB}(N,\tau)
, still denoted
J
. Indeed, the restriction of the almost complex structure on the fibers were defined to be the pull-back of the complex structure on the fibers of
\mathcal{L}
, which extends to the the complex structure on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Moreover, the horizontal planes
\ker\Theta
, preserved by
J
by definition, tend to
TN_{\infty}
, so
J
extends smoothly to
N_{\infty}
, making it a holomorphic submanifold, the map
\pi:(N_{\infty},J)\to(N,j)
being a biholomorphism.

To conclude, given an integral symplectic manifold
(N,\tau)
, we have defined a sphere bundle
\pi_{N}:M:=\textnormal{\small SPB}(N,\tau)\to N
, which comes along with several structures, namely:

\bullet

a closed
2
-from
\omega_{0}
with
[\omega_{0}]\in H^{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
.

\bullet

a smooth horizontal distribution
H^{N}
on
M
associated to a connexion form
\Theta
defined on
M\backslash(N_{0}\cup N_{\infty})
,

\bullet

an almost complex structure
J
that preserves the vertical and horizontal distributions,
N_{0}
and
N_{\infty}
.

\bullet

a radial coordinate
R
on
M
, that vanishes on the zero section
N_{0}
and equals
1
at
N_{\infty}
,

\bullet

we also have two vector fields
\frac{\partial}{\partial R}
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}
on
M\backslash(N_{0}\cup N_{\infty})
and
M
, respectively, tangent to the fibers of
M
.

4.4.
Line bundles over projective bundles.

Let
M:=\textnormal{\small SPB}(N,\tau)
. Since
[\omega_{0}]\in H^{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
, there exists a complex line bundle
\mathcal{L}\overset{\pi}{\to}M
with
c_{1}(\mathcal{L})=[\omega_{0}]
and a connexion
\nabla
with curvature
-i\omega_{0}
that extends the connection
\nabla
on
\mathcal{L}\to N_{0}
, seen as
\mathcal{L}\to N
. Indeed, starting with any connection
\nabla^{\prime}
on
M
with curvature
-i\omega_{0}
,
\nabla^{\prime}_{|N}-\nabla
is a closed
1
-form
A
(because
\nabla^{\prime}_{|N}
and
\nabla
have the same curvature) and
\nabla:=\nabla^{\prime}-\pi_{N}^{*}A
is another connection on the whole of
M
with the same curvature
-i\omega_{0}
that coincides with
\nabla
on
N_{0}
. This specific connection gives rise to connections
\nabla^{k}
on
\mathcal{L}^{k}\to M
, as defined in proposition
3.9
, that also restrict to the product connection
\nabla^{k}
on
\mathcal{L}^{k}\to N_{0}\approx N
. Since there can be no ambiguity, we drop the subscript
k
from the notation
\nabla^{k}
in order to lighten the notation.
As explained in section
3.4
, the almost complex structure
J
provides a splitting of the connexion into its
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
parts, defined by

\nabla^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla-i\nabla_{J\cdot})\hskip 14.22636pt\text{and}\hskip 14.22636pt\nabla^{\prime\prime}=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla+i\nabla_{J\cdot}).

This splitting allows to define holomorphic or quasi-holomorphic sections (those that satisfy
\nabla^{\prime\prime}s=0
or
|\nabla^{\prime\prime}s|<\kappa|\nabla^{\prime}s|
, respectively).

The aim of this paragraph is to give the basic properties of this bundle and its sections. We start with some more or less obvious observations, that we will use several times in our computations.

Remarks 4.4
.

(i)

Given any vector field
u\in\Gamma^{\infty}(TN)
, the vector field
U=\pi_{N}^{*}u\in\Gamma^{\infty}(TM)
uniquely defined
by
U\in H^{N}
and
\pi_{N*}U=u
commutes with
\frac{\partial}{\partial R}
(in the sense of the Lie bracket).
Indeed, since the connection defined by
\Theta
is linear, radial dilations preserve
H^{N}
.
In particular, any vector
u\in T_{p}N
can be extended to a smooth horizontal vector field on
M

that commutes with
\frac{\partial}{\partial R}
. As a result,
any horizontal vector
U(p)\in T_{p}M
can be extended to a smooth horizontal vector field
that commutes with
\frac{\partial}{\partial R}
and whose value at
\pi_{N}(p)
is

Claim 5.1

Let us introduce the function

We see from this expansion that, as soon as

. As a result,

\begin{array}[]{ll}-d^{2}|s_{\varepsilon}|^{2}=&-\frac{k(k-2)\varepsilon^{2}R^{k-3}}{2(1-R)}dR^{2}\\
&+\varepsilon^{2}R^{k-1}(1-R)d\psi^{2}\\
&-2\varepsilon^{2}R^{k-2}(1-R)d^{2}\ln|\sigma|.\end{array}

By claim
5.2
,
T^{*}_{p_{0}}M=\langle dR\rangle\oplus\langle d\psi(p_{0})\rangle\oplus H_{N}^{*}(p_{0})
, so this decomposition shows that the index of
-|s_{\varepsilon}|^{2}
at
p_{0}
is one more than that of
-\ln|\sigma|
at
z_{0}
.
\square

6.
A Liouville vector field on
\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)\backslash Z(s_{\varepsilon})
.

This paragraph starts when the previous ends, within the same framework and we keep the same notation. We have constructed a section
s_{\varepsilon}
of
\mathcal{L}^{k}\to M=\textnormal{\small SPB}(N,\tau)
out of a section
\sigma
of the restriction of this bundle to
N_{0}
, and we deduce now a Liouville form on
\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)\backslash Z(s_{\varepsilon})
with good properties. As in
[
Gir02
]
, define
\lambda_{\varepsilon}
on
M\backslash Z(s_{\varepsilon})
by

\nabla(f\frac{s_{\varepsilon}}{|s_{\varepsilon}|})=(df-ik\lambda_{\varepsilon}f)\frac{s_{\varepsilon}}{|s_{\varepsilon}|},

or equivalently by

\nabla s_{\varepsilon}=(d\ln|s_{\varepsilon}|-ik\lambda_{\varepsilon})s_{\varepsilon}=(-d\varphi_{\varepsilon}-ik\lambda_{\varepsilon})s_{\varepsilon},

where we have defined

\varphi_{\varepsilon}:=-\ln|s_{\varepsilon}|.

Since
\nabla
is a smooth connection on
M
,
\lambda_{\varepsilon}
is smooth on
M\backslash Z(s_{\varepsilon})
, and since
\nabla
has curvature
-ik\omega_{0}
,
d\lambda_{\varepsilon}=\omega_{0}
. On
M\backslash(Z(s_{\varepsilon})\cup N_{\infty})=\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)\backslash Z(s_{\varepsilon})
, on which
\omega_{0}
is non-degenerate, we have a well-defined associated Liouville vector field
X_{\varepsilon}
. The aim of this paragraph is to explain Biran’s decomposition in the degenrate context at hand. More precisely, we aim at describing the basin of attraction of
Z(s_{\varepsilon})
under the flow of
X_{\varepsilon}
.

When
\sigma
is holomorphic, so is
s_{\varepsilon}
and it is well-known that
X_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{k}\nabla\varphi_{\varepsilon}
(it also follows from lemma
3.8
). In any case, still as shown by lemma
3.8
,
X_{\varepsilon}
is a gradient-like vector field for the Morse function
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
(meaning that
X_{\varepsilon}\cdot\varphi_{\varepsilon}\geq 0
, with equality only at critical points of
\varphi
, where
X_{\varepsilon}
vanishes).

Notice that when we restrict
s_{\varepsilon}
to
N_{0}
, we get

\nabla\sigma=(d\ln|\sigma|-ik\lambda_{\varepsilon})\sigma,

so
\lambda:=\lambda_{\varepsilon|N_{0}}
is the Liouville form naturally associated to the section
\sigma
.

Lemma 6.1
.

\pi_{N}^{*}\lambda=-\frac{1}{k}d\psi-\Theta.

As a result, the function
\psi
remains constant along the trajectories of
\pi_{N}^{*}X_{\lambda}
(the horizontal vector field that projects to
X_{\lambda}
).

Proof:
We recall that
\lambda=\lambda_{\varepsilon|N_{0}}
verifies
\nabla\sigma=(d\ln|\sigma|-ik\lambda)\sigma
.
On one hand, lemma
4.5
gives

\begin{array}[]{ll}\nabla s_{0}&=\big(-\frac{k}{2(1-R)}dR-ikR\Theta)s_{0}+R^{\frac{k}{2}}P^{R}\nabla_{\pi_{N*\centerdot}}\sigma\\
&=\big(-\frac{k}{2(1-R)}dR-ikR\Theta)s_{0}+R^{\frac{k}{2}}P^{R}(\pi_{N}^{*}d\ln|\sigma|-ik\pi_{N}^{*}\lambda)\sigma\\
&=\big(-\frac{k}{2(1-R)}dR-ikR\Theta)s_{0}+(d\ln|\sigma\circ\pi_{N}|-ik\pi^{*}_{N}\lambda)s_{0}\\
\end{array}

so

-\textnormal{Im}\,\frac{\nabla s_{0}}{ks_{0}}=\pi^{*}_{N}\lambda+R\Theta.

On the other hand, writing
s_{0}=\varepsilon xe^{i\psi}s_{\infty}
and using lemma
4.5
again, we get:

\nabla s_{0}=(\frac{k}{2R}dR+d\ln x+i(d\psi+k(1-R)\Theta))s_{0}

so

-\textnormal{Im}\,\frac{\nabla s_{0}}{ks_{0}}=-\frac{d\psi}{k}-(1-R)\Theta.

Comparing these two expressions, we deduce that

\pi_{N}^{*}\lambda+R\Theta=-\frac{d\psi}{k}-(1-R)\Theta,

and so
\pi_{N}^{*}\lambda=-\frac{1}{k}d\psi-\Theta
. As a result, since
X_{\pi_{N}^{*}\lambda}=\pi_{N}^{*}X_{\lambda}
,

0=\pi_{N}^{*}\lambda(\pi_{N}^{*}X_{\lambda})=-\frac{1}{k}d\psi(\pi_{N}^{*}X_{\lambda})-\Theta(\pi_{N}^{*}X_{\lambda})=\frac{1}{k}d\psi(\pi_{N}^{*}X_{\lambda}),

we get
X_{\pi_{N}^{*}\lambda}\cdot\psi=0
.
\square
​​

Lemma 6.2
.

\lambda_{\varepsilon}=\frac{x^{2}+x\cos\psi}{|1+xe^{i\psi}|^{2}}\pi_{N}^{*}\lambda+\frac{Rx^{2}+(2R-1)x\cos\psi-1+R}{|1+xe^{i\psi}|^{2}}\Theta-\frac{\sin\psi}{k|1+xe^{i\psi}|^{2}}dx

Proof:
The same computations as in the previous proof using lemma
4.5
give

\begin{array}[]{ll}\nabla s_{\varepsilon}&=\nabla s_{0}+\varepsilon\nabla s_{\infty}\\
&=\big(d\ln|s_{0}|-ik(\pi_{N}^{*}\lambda+R\Theta)\big)s_{0}+\varepsilon\big(d\ln|s_{\infty}|+ik(1-R)\Theta\big)s_{\infty}.\end{array}

Since
s_{0}=\varepsilon xe^{i\psi}s_{\infty}
,

s_{\infty}=\frac{s_{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon(1+xe^{i\psi})},\hskip 14.22636pts_{0}=\frac{xe^{i\psi}}{1+xe^{i\psi}}s_{\varepsilon}.

Thus,

\begin{array}[]{ll}|1+xe^{i\psi}|^{2}\frac{\nabla s_{\varepsilon}}{s_{\varepsilon}}=&\big(d\ln|s_{0}|-i(k\pi_{N}^{*}\lambda+R\Theta)\big)(x^{2}+xe^{i\psi})+\big(d\ln|s_{\infty}|+\\
&+ik(1-R)\Theta\big)(1+xe^{-i\psi})\end{array}

and

\begin{array}[]{rl}|1+xe^{i\psi}|^{2}\textnormal{Im}\,\frac{\nabla s_{\varepsilon}}{s_{\varepsilon}}=&x\sin\psi d\ln\left(\frac{|s_{0}|}{|s_{\infty}|}\right)-k(x^{2}+x\cos\psi)(R\Theta+\pi_{N}^{*}\lambda)+\\
&+k(1-R)(1+x\cos\psi)\Theta\\
=&x\sin\psi d\ln|x|-k\big(Rx^{2}+(1-2R)x\cos\psi+1-R\big)\Theta-\\
&-k(x^{2}+x\cos\psi)\pi_{N}^{*}\lambda.\end{array}

This concludes the proof, since
\lambda_{\varepsilon}=-\textnormal{Im}\,\frac{\nabla s_{\varepsilon}}{ks_{\varepsilon}}
.
\square
​​

Corollary 6.3
.

The stable manifold of
X_{\varepsilon}
at a critical point
p=(z_{0},R_{0},\theta_{0})\in\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)\backslash Z(s_{\varepsilon})
of
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
is given by:

W^{s}\big(X_{\varepsilon},p\big)=\{(z,R,\theta)\;|\;z\in W^{s}(X_{\lambda},z_{0}),\cos\psi(z,\theta)=1\}.

Proof:
Lemma
6.2
, together with the obvious identities
X_{\pi_{N}^{*}\lambda}=\pi_{N}^{*}X_{\lambda}
,
X_{\Theta}=\frac{\partial}{\partial R}
allows to compute explicitely
X_{\varepsilon}
. Denoting as usual
X_{x}
the Hamiltonian vector field associated to the function
x
, we have

X_{\varepsilon}=\frac{x^{2}+x\cos\psi}{|1+xe^{i\psi}|^{2}}\pi_{N}^{*}X_{\lambda}+\frac{Rx^{2}+(2R-1)x\cos\psi-1+R}{|1+xe^{i\psi}|^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial R}-\frac{\sin\psi}{k|1+xe^{i\psi}|^{2}}X_{x}.

Let
p_{0}:=(z_{0},R_{0},\theta_{0})
be a critical point of
|s_{\varepsilon}|^{2}
in
\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)\backslash Z(s_{\varepsilon})
. We recall that it verifies:

z_{0}\in\text{Crit}\,\ln|\sigma|,\hskip 14.22636pte^{i\psi}=1,\hskip 14.22636ptx=\frac{1-R}{R}.

Let

W:=\{(z,R,\theta)\;|\;z\in W^{s}(X_{\lambda},z_{0}),\cos\psi(z,\theta)=1\}.

We claim that
W\subset W^{s}(X_{\varepsilon},p_{0})
. Let indeed
p=(z,R,\theta)\in W
and define
p(t):=(z(t),R(t),\theta(t))
by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}(z(0),R(0),\theta(0))=(z,R,\theta),\\
\dot{z}(t)=\frac{x^{2}+x}{|1+x|^{2}}X_{\lambda}(z(t))\\
\dot{R}(t)=\frac{Rx^{2}+(2R-1)x-1+R}{|1+x|^{2}}=\frac{(1+x)(Rx-1+R)}{|1+x^{2}}\\
\dot{\psi}(t)=-k\dot{\theta}(t)+\lambda(\dot{z}(t))=0.\end{array}\right.

Notice that the second and third lines do not involve the coordinate
\theta
, so define a differential equation on
(R,z)
. Once it is solved,
z(t)
and
R(t)
are fixed, and the last line fixes the derivative of
\theta(t)
, hence
\theta
itself, and forces
\cos\psi(p(t))=1
along the trajectory. Notice also that this last line could equivalently be written
\cos\psi(p(t))=1
, making no reference to the coordinate
\theta
, so this differential equation is well-defined on the whole of
M
, not only on a trivialization chart of
\textnormal{\small SPB}(N,\tau)\to N
. Now
p(t)
is obviously a solution of the differential equation
\dot{p}(t)=X_{\varepsilon}(p(t))
,
\cos\psi(z(t),\theta(t))=1
,
z(t)
converges to
z_{0}
and
R
increases or decreases, depending on whether
x<\frac{1-R}{R}
or
x>\frac{1-R}{R}
, respectively. As a result
p(t)\to p_{0}
, so
p\in W^{s}(X_{\varepsilon},p_{0})
, hence the claimed inclusion
W\subset W^{s}(X_{\varepsilon},p_{0})
. Now it is clear that
W
is a submanifold of dimension one more than the index of
-\ln|\sigma|
at
z_{0}
, which is the Morse index of
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
at
p_{0}
. Since
X_{\varepsilon}
is gradient-like for
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
,
W
is the stable manifold of
X_{\varepsilon}
at
p_{0}
.
\square
​​

Let us call the skeleton of
|s_{\varepsilon}|
the union of the stable manifolds of the singular points of its Liouville flow:

\textnormal{Skel}(s_{\varepsilon}):=\bigcup_{p\in\text{Crit}\,|s_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\backslash(N_{\infty}\cup N_{0})}W^{s}(X_{\varepsilon},p).

The reason we do not consider the critical points in
N_{0}
or
N_{\infty}
in this definition of skeleton will be clear very soon.
The previous corollary
6.3
tells precisely what this set is. This skeleton can be presented in a different way:

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\Gamma_{k}\subset N
be the isotropic skeleton associated to the section
\sigma:N\to\mathcal{L}^{k}
, namely

\Gamma_{k}:=\bigcup_{z\in\text{Crit}\,|\sigma|\backslash Z(\sigma)}W^{s}(X_{\lambda},z).

Then there exists a
k
-fold Legendrian lift
\Lambda_{k}
of
\Gamma_{k}
in the prequantization
\mathcal{P}(N,\tau)
such that

\textnormal{Skel}(s_{\varepsilon})=\Phi^{-1}(\Lambda_{k}\times(0,1)),

where
\Phi
is the identification between
\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)\backslash N_{0}
and
S^{<0}\mathcal{P}
provided by lemma
4.2
.

Proof:
We need to show that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\textnormal{Skel}(s_{\varepsilon})\backslash N_{0}&=\bigcup_{p\in\text{Crit}\,|s_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\backslash(N_{0}\cup N_{\infty})}W^{s}(X_{\varepsilon},p)\\
&=\left\{p\in M\;|\;\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\pi_{N}(p)\in\cup_{z\in\text{Crit}\,|\sigma|^{2}\backslash Z(\sigma)}W^{s}(X,z),\\
\cos\psi(p)=0\end{array}\right.\right\}\end{array}

is of the form
\Phi^{-1}(\Lambda_{k}\times(0,1))
for some
k
-fold isotropic lift

of

. By
[
Gir17
]
, this skeleton is known to be isotropic in
\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)
because it is the skeleton of the quasi-holomorphic section

. The formula on the right shows that it is also radially invariant and that it projects to
\Gamma_{k}
. Finally, since
\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\theta}(p)=-k
, the fiber above a point in
\Gamma_{k}
is a union of

-equidistributed rays. The uniqueness part of lemma
4.3
therefore guarantees that

\textnormal{Skel}(s_{\varepsilon})=\Phi^{-1}(\Lambda_{k}\times(0,1)),

where
\Lambda_{k}
is a
k
-fold isotropic cover of

.
\square
​​

Lemma 6.5
.

The submanifold
N_{0}
is stable under the flow of
X_{\varepsilon}
. If
k\geq 3
,
X_{\varepsilon}
points outwards
N_{\infty}
in a neighbourhood of
N_{\infty}
. If
k=2
and
\varepsilon\gg 1
, the same conclusion holds.

Proof:
Since
s_{\varepsilon}
is smooth on
\textnormal{\small SPB}(N,\tau)
and
\omega_{0}
is well-defined and smooth on
\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)
, the Liouville vector field
X_{\varepsilon}
is smooth on
\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)
. Lemma
6.2
gives for
X_{\varepsilon}
the formula:

X_{\varepsilon}=\frac{x^{2}+x\cos\psi}{|1+xe^{i\psi}|^{2}}\pi_{N}^{*}X_{\lambda}+\frac{Rx^{2}+(2R-1)x\cos\psi-1+R}{|1+xe^{i\psi}|^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial R}-\frac{\sin\psi}{k|1+xe^{i\psi}|^{2}}X_{x},

(where
\pi_{N}^{*}X_{\lambda}\in H^{N}
as usual). Since the function
x
has the form
f(R)\ln|\sigma|
, its Hamiltonian vector field is tangent to the level sets of
R
, so the radial component of
X_{\varepsilon}
is

\frac{Rx^{2}+(2R-1)x\cos\psi-1+R}{|1+xe^{i\psi}|^{2}}=\frac{R+(2R-1)x^{-1}\cos\psi-(1-R)x^{-2}}{|1+x^{-1}e^{-i\psi}|^{2}}.

The function
x^{-1}
is smooth on
\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)\backslash\{\sigma\circ\pi_{N}=0\}
, and equals
0
on
N_{0}
. As a result, the radial component of
X_{\varepsilon}
vanishes on
N_{0}\backslash Z(\sigma)
, so
X_{\varepsilon}
is tangent to
N_{0}
on
N_{0}\backslash Z(\sigma)
. Since
\sigma
vanishes transversally by assumption, its vanishing set is nowhere dense so
X_{\varepsilon}
is tangent to
N_{0}
.

If
k\geq 3
,
x\underset{R\approx 1}{\lesssim}(1-R)^{\frac{k}{2}}\lesssim(1-R)^{\frac{3}{2}}
, so the left version of the formula of the radial component of
X_{\varepsilon}
shows that it is equivalent to
-(1-R)<0
when
R
is close to
1
, so points outwards
N_{\infty}
. Finally, when
k=2
, one has to be slightly more precise. The radial component of
X_{\varepsilon}
is then, up to a positive factor that tends to
1
when
R
goes to
1
:

-(1-R)\big(1-\frac{(2R-1)|\sigma|\cos\psi}{\varepsilon R}-\frac{(1-R)|\sigma|^{2}}{R\varepsilon^{2}}\big).

Thus, when
\varepsilon>2\max|\sigma|
, this component is again negative for
R\approx 1
.
\square
​​

In summary, after maybe chosing
\varepsilon
large enough when
k=2
, the Liouville vector field
X_{\varepsilon}
, defined on
\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)\backslash Z(s_{\varepsilon})
, points outwards
N_{\infty}
, is tangent to
N_{0}
, and is
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
-gradient-like. The classical arguments of Morse theory apply to show that the trajectories are of two kinds: those that tend to a critical point of
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
, and those that approach
Z(s_{\varepsilon})
. The formers precisely describe
\textnormal{Skel}(s_{\varepsilon})
, while the latters reach
Z(s_{\varepsilon})
in positive time.

7.
Taming the Liouville form.

We finally aim at describing the basin of attraction of
Z(s_{\varepsilon})
under the flow of
X_{\varepsilon}
. Towards this goal, we introduce the notion of tameness of Liouville forms, as proposed in
[
Ops15
,
OS24
]
:

Definition 7.1
.

Let
(M^{2n},\omega)
be a symplectic manifold,
\Sigma^{2n-2}\subset M
a symplectic hypersurface Poincaré-dual to a multiple
\ell[\omega_{|\Sigma}]
of the symplectic class, at least locally near
\Sigma
. Use the symplectic neighbourhood theorem to present a neighbourhood of
\Sigma
in
M
as a neighbourhood of the zero section in
\textnormal{\small SDB}(\Sigma,\omega_{|\Sigma},\ell)
, on which we have the connection form
\Theta
(see section
4.1
). We say that a
1
-form
\lambda
defined on
M\backslash\Sigma
is tame along
\Sigma
if there exists a constant
a\in\mathbb{R}
and a bounded
1
-form
\nu
in
M\backslash\Sigma
such that
\lambda=a\Theta+\nu
. The value
a
is called the residue of
\lambda
around
\Sigma
.

The form
\lambda_{0}
defined in section
4.1
provides a Liouville form in
\textnormal{Op}\,(Z(s_{\varepsilon}))^{*}
that is tame along
Z(s_{\varepsilon})
, with residue
-\frac{1}{\ell}
(recall our notation
\textnormal{Op}\,(X)^{*}=\textnormal{Op}\,(X)\backslash X
). On the other hand, the form
\lambda_{\varepsilon}
, defined on the larger set
\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)\backslash Z(s_{\varepsilon})
may not be. In the Kähler setting,
\lambda_{\varepsilon}
is known to be
-\frac{1}{k}d^{c}\ln|s_{\varepsilon}|
, which can be proved tame, but the proof I am aware of does not seem to pass easily to the quasi-holomorphic setting. The next two lemma allow to modify
\lambda_{\varepsilon}
locally near
Z(s_{\varepsilon})
to a tame Liouville form, keeping its skeleton fixed.
We recall that
s_{\varepsilon}
vanishes transversally, not on
N_{\infty}
, so
Z(s_{\varepsilon})\subset\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)
is a smooth symplectic submanifold.
As a zero-set of a section of
\mathcal{L}^{k}\to\textnormal{\small SPB}(N,\tau)
, it is Poincaré-dual to
k[\omega_{0}]
, so it has a neighbourhood that can be identified with a neighbourhood of the zero-section of a symplectic disc bundle, on which we have a radial coordinate
P
and a Liouville form
\lambda_{0}^{\varepsilon}
whose vector field is negatively proportional to
\frac{\partial}{\partial P}
.

Lemma 7.2
.

For
P
small enough,
d|s_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\big(\frac{\partial}{\partial P}\big)>0
.

Proof:
Since
s_{\varepsilon}
vanishes transversally in the case at hand,
|s_{\varepsilon}|^{2}
is Morse-Bott along
Z(s_{\varepsilon})
with definite positive transverse Hessian. Thus, in local coordinates
(z,P,\theta)
provided by the symplectic disc bundle model, and denoting
P=\rho^{2}
, we have

\frac{\partial^{2}|s_{\varepsilon}|^{2}}{\partial\rho^{2}}(z,0,\theta)>0\hskip 28.45274pt\forall z,\forall\theta,

so this second derivative is positive also for all
\rho\ll 1
. Since the first derivative in the radial direction has to vanish at
\rho=0
because
Z(s_{\varepsilon})
is a minimum of the function
|s_{\varepsilon}|^{2}
, this first radial derivative is positive on a small enough pointed neighbourhood of
Z(s_{\varepsilon})
.

\square
​​

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a possibly open symplectic manifold,
\Sigma
a closed smooth symplectic divisor locally Poincaré-dual to a positive multiple of the symplectic class, and
f:\textnormal{Op}\,(\Sigma)\to[0,1]
any smooth function whose zero-set is
\Sigma
. Let
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}
be two Liouville forms defined in
\textnormal{Op}\,(\Sigma)^{*}
that satisfy
X_{\lambda}\cdot f>0
and
X_{\lambda^{\prime}}\cdot f>0
. Then for all
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon^{\prime}\ll 1
, there exists a Liouville vector field
\lambda^{\prime\prime}
in
\textnormal{Op}\,(\Sigma)^{*}
that coincides with
\lambda+\pi^{*}\vartheta
for some closed
1
-form on
\Sigma
in
\{f<\varepsilon\}
and with
\lambda^{\prime}
in
\{f>\varepsilon^{\prime}\}
, and such that
X_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}\cdot f>0
.

Proof:

\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}
is a closed
1
-form on
\textnormal{Op}\,(\Sigma)^{*}
, that we see as a pointed neighbourhood of a symplectic disc bundle. Let
\gamma(\theta):=(z_{0},R_{0},\theta)\in\textnormal{Op}\,(\Sigma)^{*}
be a small loop in a fiber. Since
H_{1}(\textnormal{Op}\,(\Sigma)^{*})=H_{1}(\Sigma)+\langle\gamma\rangle
, we can write

\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda=\pi^{*}\vartheta+\nu,

where
\vartheta
is a smooth closed
1
-form on
\Sigma
and
\nu
is a closed
1
-form that vanishes on
H_{1}(\Sigma)
. Consider a
2
-cycle
S\subset\Sigma
, deformed to
S^{\prime}\subset\textnormal{Op}\,(\Sigma)
that intersects
\Sigma
transversally. By Stokes theorem,

-c_{1}(\mathcal{N}\Sigma,[S])[\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda](\gamma)=\int_{S^{\prime}}\omega-\omega=0,

so
\nu
is in fact exact. Thus we can write

\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda=\pi^{*}\vartheta+dh,

for some smooth function
h:\textnormal{Op}\,(\Sigma)^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
. Let now
\varepsilon<\varepsilon^{\prime}
be such that
\{f<\varepsilon^{\prime}\}\subset\textnormal{Op}\,(\Sigma)
,
\chi:[\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}]\to[0,1]
be a smooth function that equals
0
near
\varepsilon
and
1
near
\varepsilon^{\prime}
. Define

\lambda^{\prime\prime}:=\lambda+\pi^{*}\vartheta+d(\chi(f)h)=\chi(f)\lambda^{\prime}+(1-\chi(f))\lambda+hd\chi\circ f.

The first formula shows that
\lambda^{\prime\prime}
is again a Liouville form, while the second shows that the associated Liouville vector field is
a positive barycenter of
X_{\lambda}
and
X_{\lambda}^{\prime}
modulo a vector field
Z
which is tangent to the levels of
f
. This implies that

X_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}\cdot f>0
, as claimed.
\square
​​

Let us now come back to our framework:
\lambda_{\varepsilon}
is a Liouville form in the complement of the smooth divisor
Z(s_{\varepsilon})\subset\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)
, this divisor being Poincaré-dual to
k[\omega_{0}]
in
M=\textnormal{\small SPB}(N,\tau)
, hence locally around itself. Endow a neighbourhood of
Z(s_{\varepsilon})
with a structure of a symplectic disc bundle, hence in particular with a radial coordinate
P
and a local tame Liouville form
\lambda_{0}^{\varepsilon}
. By lemma
7.2
,
X_{\lambda_{0}^{\varepsilon}}\cdot(-|s_{\varepsilon}|^{2})>0
in
\textnormal{Op}\,(Z(s_{\varepsilon}))^{*}
and the same positivity holds for
X_{\varepsilon}
by the quasi-holomorphicity of
s_{\varepsilon}
(lemma
3.8
). By lemma
7.3
, there exists a Liouville form
\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}
on
\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)\backslash Z(s_{\varepsilon})
that coincides with
\lambda_{\varepsilon}
outside a neighbourhood of
Z(s_{\varepsilon})
and with
\lambda_{0}^{\varepsilon}+\vartheta
on
\textnormal{Op}\,(Z(s_{\varepsilon}))^{*}
, where
\vartheta
is a smooth
1
-form on
\textnormal{Op}\,(Z(s_{\varepsilon}))
. The latter form is tame along
Z(s_{\varepsilon})
, so
\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}
is tame. Moreover, this same lemma guarantees that its associated Liouville vector field
X_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}
is still gradient-like for
-\ln|s_{\varepsilon}|
. Since moreover
Z(s_{\varepsilon})
does not meet
N_{\infty}
,
\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{\varepsilon}
and
\lambda_{\varepsilon}
coincide near
\partial\textnormal{\small SDB}(N,\tau)
, so
X_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}
points outside
N_{\infty}
, just the same as
\lambda_{\varepsilon}
.

In conclusion of this paragraph, we have obtained the following (we drop the comas and the subscript from
\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{\varepsilon}
):

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
(\mathcal{P},\alpha)\to(N,\tau)
be a prequantization bundle. Let
(\mathcal{L}^{k},\nabla)\to(N,\tau)
be a Hermitian bundle endowed with a connection of curvature

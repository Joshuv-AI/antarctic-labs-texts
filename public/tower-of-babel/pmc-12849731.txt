The impact of National Volume-Based Procurement (NVBP) on healthcare expenditures for breast cancer outpatients: an interrupted time series analysis in Wuhan, China
Authors: Jinyi Tao, Shihong Li, Shiwei Gong, Yong Han, Fang Zeng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIn January 2019, the Chinese government launched a pilot National Volume-Based Procurement (NVBP) program, which was then gradually extended to the whole country to alleviate the financial burden of healthcare expenditure. This study aims to investigate the impact of the NVBP policy on healthcare expenditure.MethodBy extracting outpatient payment records of breast cancer patients in a tertiary hospital information system from May 2020 to April 2023, we performed an interrupted time series (ITS) to assess the impact of the NVBP policy on healthcare expenditure.ResultsITS analyses showed that the NVBP policy mitigated the persistent increase in total average monthly per-visit expenditure, shifting it from an upward trend (9.68 CNY (p < 0.01)) to a flat trend (-2.33 CNY (p = 0.673)). The momentum of the continuous increase in the average monthly per-visit drug expenditure of patients has been effectively curbed; the expenditure has turned from an upward trend (15.10 CNY (p < 0.01)) to a downward trend (-11.19 CNY (p < 0.01)). Although the trend of continuous increase in average monthly per-visit expenditures for chemotherapy drugs (p < 0.01), oral endocrine drugs (p < 0.01) and Traditional Chinese Medicines (TCM) (p < 0.01) were effectively mitigated, especially for TCM; however, the expenditure of injectable endocrine drugs (p = 0.038), examination (p = 0.015) and laboratory (p = 0.029) were the persistent increases.ConclusionsOur study suggested that the NVBP policy effectively prevented the continued growth in drug expenditure for breast cancer outpatients, but did not substantially improve total expenditure. In addition, the policy has not only affected the expenditures of medicines, but has also changed the structure of expenditures.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12913-025-13841-z.Keywords: National Volume-Based Procurement (NVBP), Policy intervention, Interrupted time series, Health expenditure, Breast cancer

Introduction

The latest report from the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) indicates that by 2020, there will be over 2.2 million new cases of breast cancer globally each year, with over half of these occurring in low- and middle-income countries [1, 2]. In China, the number of new cases of breast cancer diagnosed annually exceeds 350,000, representing the second-highest incidence and the fifth-highest mortality rate from female cancers [3]. As the survival rate of female breast cancer in China has exceeded 82% [4], and even higher in some regions [5]; breast cancer is characterized by a high cure rate and a long survival time, which has been gradually incorporated into the chronic disease management for oncology [6]. A multicenter study involving 37 hospitals in 13 provinces in China showed that the average medical expenditure for breast cancer patients was $ 7,527, accounting for nearly 90% [7] of total patient expenditure, with out-of-pocket payments accounting for 55% of average household non-food expenditure [8]. Although the Chinese government has implemented a series of healthcare policies over the past two decades to curb the rapid growth of drug spending, the average per capita healthcare expenditure for breast cancer patients has maintained an upward trend [9].

The challenge of controlling pharmaceutical expenditures while ensuring access is a global concern. Various countries have implemented innovative policies to address this. For instance, in the United States, the 340B Drug Pricing Program mandates drug manufacturers to provide outpatient drugs at discounted prices to eligible safety-net hospitals and clinics, with the goal of enabling these entities to stretch scarce federal resources [10]. Meanwhile, in order to restrict the continuous growth of China’s drug expenditure [11] and alleviate the heavy financial burden of patients [12], the National Healthcare Security Administration launched a pilot national volume-based procurement (NVBP) program in January 2019 [13] and gradually extended it to the whole country. While the mechanisms differ—the 340B program is a discount program for specific providers, whereas NVBP is a centralized procurement model—both aimed to alleviate the financial burden of drugs on the healthcare system. Evaluating the effects and unintended consequences of such major policy interventions, including potential changes in provider billing behavior, is of critical importance globally.

By March 2024, a total of nine batches of approximately 500 products will be included in the NVBP list [14]. These include more than 30 commonly prescribed medicines for oncology patients, particularly for breast cancer patients, such as docetaxel injections, decitabine injections, letrozole tablets, anastrozole tablets and paclitaxel injections. Significant price reductions have been achieved for these medicines. Despite the reduction in drug prices following the implementation of the zero mark-up policy, research has indicated that the average per-visit expenditure of breast cancer outpatients has increased [15]. It therefore remains to be examined whether the implementation of the NVBP policy can effectively alleviate the financial burden of breast cancer patients.

The implementation of the NVBP policy has resulted in a notable reduction in the prices of medicines, thereby enhancing their affordability [16]. However, a small proportion of patients have not yet perceived a tangible decrease in their medical costs [17]. It has been demonstrated that the implementation of NVBP policy has resulted in a notable reduction in the overall expenditure on pharmaceuticals, including antihypertensives [18], antidepressants [19], and anti-hepatitis B virus drugs [20]. However, it is important to note that the data presented in these studies are based on the procurement records of the drugs, rather than the actual prescription data of the patients. Despite the robust correlation between purchase records and prescription data, there is a temporal lag between the two, which may lead to biased study results, especially for oncology patients requiring multiple or long-term medications.

At present, only two studies have employed real prescribing data to examine the economic consequences after the implementation of the NVBP policy [21, 22]. However, the changes in expenditure on different types of drugs and the possible reasons behind them have not been examined in detail. Although there have been a few studies that have estimated the impact of the NVBP policy on health expenditures for diseases by using real prescription records, they have not explored in detail the impact of the policy on different types of drugs’ expenditure for a particular disease, let alone breast cancer. Accordingly, the study analyzed the outpatient payment records at the Union Hospital of Tongji Medical College of Huazhong University of Science and Technology from May 2020 to April 2023 to evaluate the impact of the NVBP policy on the medical expenditures of breast cancer outpatients.

Methods

Study design

We propose to implement an interrupted time series (ITS) design to evaluate the impact of NVBP policy on the economic outcomes of breast cancer outpatients. The ITS is a quasi-experimental method that is applied at the population level to study the longitudinal effects of the evaluation of policy interventions [23]. The methodology compares trends in outcome indicators before and after the implementation of the intervention, based on a comprehensive consideration of the original trend of the outcome event [24]. Ultimately, the impact of the intervention on the outcome indicators will be scientifically assessed and quantified and presented in a visual chart. After the NVBP policy was implemented in the sample hospital in September 2021, many prescription drugs for breast cancer were switched to products from the NVBP catalog. Therefore, the ITS method was adopted for this study to compare the changes in trends in each type of healthcare expenditures before and after the policy.

Setting and data sources

The NVBP policy was implemented in the hospital involved in our study in September 2021. The data for this study was extracted from the hospital information system (HIS) of Union Hospital affiliated to Huazhong University of Science and Technology, which is a teaching hospital with 6,700,000 outpatients and 300,000 inpatients annually located in the central part of China [25]. According to the International Classification of Diseases (ICD-10), hospital payment records for outpatients diagnosed with breast cancer and treated in the Union Hospital between 1 May 2020 and 30 April 2023 were extracted for this study. The period from May 2020 to August 2021 is the pre-policy implementation study series, while the period from September 2021 to April 2023 is the post-policy implementation study series. The extracted data includes a detail of each payment for each drug, examination and laboratory.

Data collation and outcome indicators

To ensure the quality of the data, researchers developed a drug classification catalog for indirectly obtained outcome indicators. The two researchers separately classified and logically proofread the extracted raw data; in case of disagreement between the two, a group discussion was held to form a unified opinion. This study categorizes all the raw payment records, then allows each piece of payment record to be adapted to the appropriate type, and finally creates a data table that can be analyzed. The collated data are shown in Additional file 1.

The primary outcome indicator set for this study was the average monthly per-visit expenditures of breast cancer outpatients (calculated as follows), including average monthly per-visit total expenditures, drug expenditures and non-drug expenditures. The study will further investigate the average monthly per-visit expenditures for different types of medicines, such as chemotherapy drugs, injectable endocrine drugs, oral injectable drugs, and Traditional Chinese Medicines (TCM); it will also explore the average monthly per-visit expenditures for two non-drugs, including examination and laboratory expenditures.

Calculation formula

Average monthly per-visit expenditures = Total outpatient medical expenses for the month / Total number of outpatient visits for the month.

Statistical analysis

This study designed a segmented ITS to assess changes in breast cancer outpatients’ expenditure on various types of expenses affected by the NVBP policy. We analyzed data from May 2020 to April 2023, with the time of implementation of NVBP policy in the sample hospitals (September 2021) considered as the intervention time point for the ITS analysis. All analyses were performed using Stata/MP version 18.0.

Comparative analysis of healthcare expenditure

A total of 88,040 individuals who met the criteria were included in this study. Of these, the sample size before the policy intervention was 30,767 and the sample size after the policy intervention was 57,273. There were significant changes in the different types of expenditure before and after the policy (Table 1). The top three expenses before the policy were, in descending order: TCM, examination and oral endocrine; the ranking after the policy changed to: examination, chemotherapy drug and TCM. The data for each type of expense are expressed as mean values, some of which conformed to normal distribution and were comparatively analyzed using the t-test [29]; other data were comparatively analyzed using the Mann-Whitney test [30], as shown in Table 1. After the implementation of the NVBP policy, we found that the expenditure of oral endocrine drugs and TCM for breast cancer outpatients was significantly reduced (p < 0.01), by 50.46 CNY and 177.87 CNY respectively; however, the expenditures of chemotherapy drugs, injectable endocrine drugs and contrast agents was increased (p < 0.01), by 132.73 CNY, 33.00 CNY and 16.99 CNY respectively. The average per-visit drug expenditure decreased by 62.06 CNY, but this change was not statistically significant (p = 0.054). In contrast, after the implementation of the policy, both examination and laboratory expenses were significantly elevated (p < 0.01), increasing by 201.00 CNY, 35.64 CNY, respectively. These changes resulted in a significant increase (p < 0.01) in average per-visit total expenditure of breast cancer outpatient, with an increase of CNY186.57.

ITS analysis of the average per-visit expenditure for breast cancer outpatients

Before the implementation of the NVBP policy, the average monthly per-visit drug expenditure for breast cancer outpatients showed a significant upward trend, increasing by 15.10 CNY per month (p < 0.01); after the policy intervention, the expenditure on drugs immediately decreased by 84.12 CNY in the same month (p = 0.279), a statistically non-significant change; however, the drug expenditure shifted from an upward trend to a downward trend, with a monthly decrease of 11.19 CNY (β1 + β3) (p < 0.01). In other words, the implementation of the NVBP policy curbed the continuous increase in the average monthly per-visit drug expenditure for breast cancer outpatients. Immediately after the implementation of the policy, the non-drug costs increased by 204.73 CNY (p < 0.01) and maintained the trend of increasing by 8.86 CNY (β1 + β3) (p < 0.01) per month. Therefore, the average monthly per-visit non-drug expenditure for breast cancer outpatients changed significantly after the implementation of the NVBP policy and showed a trend of continuous increase. The ITS analysis showed that prior to the NVBP policy, the average monthly per-visit total expenditure for breast cancer outpatients showed a significant upward trend, increasing by 9.68 CNY per month (p < 0.01), although in the first month after the policy intervention, the total expenditure immediately increased by 126.38 CNY (p = 0.109) and showed a downward trend − 2.33 CNY (β1 + β3) (p = 0.673), but not statistically significant. The NVBP policy alleviated the continuous increase in the total average monthly per-visit expenditure for breast cancer outpatients. ITS analysis of average monthly per-visit expenditure on each type of medication

The NVBP policy had a significant impact on the expenditures of all types of medicines for breast cancer outpatients. Before the policy was implemented, the average monthly per-visit expenditure of chemotherapy drugs (p < 0.01), oral endocrine drugs (p < 0.01) and TCM (p = 0.048) showed an upward trend; and the average monthly per-visit expenditure of contrast agents showed a downward trend (p < 0.01). In the first month after the policy intervention, the average monthly per-visit expenditure of chemotherapy drugs and contrast agents increased by 99.25 CNY (p < 0.01) and 31.54 CNY (p < 0.01), respectively; however, the average monthly per-visit expenditure of oral endocrine drugs decreased by 74.69 CNY (p < 0.01) immediately. After the implementation of the NVBP policy, the continuously increasing trend of the average monthly per-visit expenditure of chemotherapy drugs (p < 0.01), oral endocrine drugs (p < 0.01) and TCM (p < 0.01) has been effectively mitigated; in particular, the average monthly per-visit expenditure of TCM has been changed from an increasing trend to a decreasing trend with 12.22 CNY (p < 0.01) per month. However, the average monthly per-visit expenditure of injectable endocrine drugs showed an increasing trend of 2.63 CNY per month (p = 0.038). Discussion

This study investigates the impact of the NVBP policy on expenditures of breast cancer outpatients. Our study indicates that after the implementation of the NVBP policy, the average monthly per-visit drug expenditure for breast cancer outpatients changed from an increasing trend to a decreasing trend (p < 0.01), with the largest decreases in the average monthly per-visit expenditure of TCM and oral endocrine drugs, which decreased by 46.77% (p < 0.01) and 31.50% (p < 0.01), respectively. The results are reasonable and consistent with previous studies, as the NVBP policy eases the financial burden on patients by significantly reducing the price of medicines [16, 21, 22], with a wide range of oral endocrine medicines and TCM included in the procurement catalog [31]. The drug with the largest price reduction was letrozole tablets, which reached 60% [32]. Nevertheless, our study also found an increase of 135.49% (p < 0.01), 21.34% (p < 0.01), and 49.85% (p < 0.01) in the average monthly per-visit expenditure of chemotherapy drugs, injectable endocrine drugs, and contrast agents respectively, and the change in these drug expenditures has not been reported. The possible reasons we believe are: (1) Endocrine injections were not included in the NVBP catalog and the prices of such medicines remained the same. (2) Although the significant price reductions associated with the inclusion of many types of chemotherapy drugs in the NVBP catalog, the proportion of outpatient daytime chemotherapy continues to increase [33], a change in accordance with China’s policy requirements [34] and is consistent with the remainder of the world [35]. (3) Based on previous reports, patients’ and physicians’ distrust in the efficacy of domestically produced NVBP medications [17, 36] may result in patients combining other medications or increasing the frequency of examination for fear of worsening their condition. In order to further alleviate the healthcare burden, we propose to: (1) further expand the scope of NVBP and include more drugs in the catalog; (2) further study the efficacy of domestically-produced drugs to alleviate the concerns of doctors and patients.

We found that after the implementation of the policy, the examination and laboratory expenses for breast cancer outpatients increased by 67.04% (p < 0.01) and 25.55% (p < 0.01), respectively, and both showed a continuous upward trend (p < 0.05). These unexpected changes result in a non-significant reduction in the total average monthly per-visit expenditure. The results of this study are similar to the results of the impact of the zero mark-up policy on breast cancer patients [15], but the impact of the NVBP policy on the examination and laboratory expenses of outpatients has indeed not been reported. We have analyzed the reasons for the significant increase in examination and laboratory expenses as follows: 1) This phenomenon can be reasonably explained through the classic substitution effect. The policy has led to a sharp decline in the relative prices of drugs within treatment combinations. In response, rational clinical decision-makers—including physicians and patients—may reallocate the resulting financial resources toward other healthcare services, such as more comprehensive diagnostic tests. These services are perceived as providing additional diagnostic certainty or value [37]. 2)Seriously ill patients taking domestically produced medicines may ask their doctors to increase the frequency and intensity of their examinations if they are concerned that the treatment is not as effective as expected [17]. Similarly, research on the U.S. 340B program indicates that hospitals participating in the program and receiving substantial drug discounts influence physicians’ prescribing practices for breast cancer patients [38]. 3) The dramatic increase in the proportion of outpatient daytime chemotherapy, however, chemotherapy is prone to lead to the occurrence of adverse reactions in patients [39], which require several examinations and laboratory tests for follow-up. Thus, the NVBP policy not only directly and significantly reduces the price of medicines, but may also have an impact on the composition of healthcare expenditures for a certain disease. We therefore advocate a comprehensive study to assess the impact of the NVBP policy on healthcare expenditure.

Strengths and limitations

Our study has some strengths. Firstly, our study used patient-level real prescribing data to assess the impact of NVBP policies on the medical expenditure of breast cancer outpatients. To our knowledge, this is the first time that real prescribing data have been used to evaluate the policy’s impact on expenditure for breast cancer patients; it is also the first time that the policy’s impact on expenditure on different types of drugs has been examined. In addition to investigating the overall impact of the policy on drug expenditures, this study also pioneered the study of the impact of the policy from the perspective of expenditures on different types of drugs for a particular disease. The results of the study not only visualize the overall economic effects of the policy, but also reveal the impact of the policy on the structure of expenditure on a particular disease. Second, this study used a research design of segmented regression analyses using the ITS model, which is suitable for examining the impact of interventions in terms of changing outcomes both immediately and over time. ITS has the advantage of controlling and eliminating the influence of other factors on the long-term trend changes caused by the observed indices, as well as correctly assessing the actual impact of the intervention on the results and presenting a visual graphical representation of the results [23, 40].

Our study also has some limitations. Firstly, the baseline characteristics of the sample sizes of the two groups before and after were not collected and compared, which may introduce some bias in the results. However, oncology outpatients are different from inpatients in that the continuity of oncology outpatients treated at a specific hospital is reliable and the baseline error is relatively small because China’s critical care insurance policy requires outpatients to choose a specific hospital for treatment. Secondly, the data were collected from a single healthcare organization, which has some limitations on the generalization of the findings. Although it is one of the most famous hospitals in Central China, it still has some limitations. Therefore, caution should be exercised when extrapolating these results to a national level, and future multi-center studies are warranted. If more real prescribing data from public hospitals could be included in the study, the results would be more informative. Finally, our study is limited by the relatively short post-policy follow-up period (1.5 years). While this time frame is sufficient to capture the initial, direct impact of the NVBP policy on cost structures, it may not fully reflect the long-term dynamics. Sustained cost trends, potential shifts in drug utilization patterns over a longer horizon, and the ultimate impact on long-term patient outcomes require continued monitoring. Future studies with extended longitudinal data are essential to validate the sustainability of the effects observed in our study and to comprehensively assess the policy’s long-term implications for cancer care.

Conclusions

This study assessed the impact of the NVBP policy on breast cancer outpatients’ healthcare expenditures and their expenditure structure. Our study suggested that the implementation of the NVBP policy effectively prevented the continued growth in drug expenditure for breast cancer outpatients, but non-drug expenditures maintained their upward trend, resulting in total expenditures that did not achieve significant improvements. In addition, the implementation of the NVBP policy had different impacts on different types of expenditures for breast cancer outpatients. Thus, the implementation of the NVBP policy has not only had a significant impact on the financial expenditures of breast cancer outpatients, but has also had a significant influence on the structure of patient expenditures. Further study should assess the long-term impact of NVBP policies on healthcare expenditures and the structure of expense.

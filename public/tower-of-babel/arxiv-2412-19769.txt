Canonical bundle formula and a conjecture on certain algebraic fiber spaces by Schnell Thanks: The author was partially supported by NSF grant DMS-2301463 and the Simons Collaboration grant Moduli of Varieties
Hyunsuk Kim

Abstract
. We observe what the canonical bundle formula gives towards a conjecture of Schnell on algebraic fiber spaces, a question concerning the equivalence between the non-vanishing conjecture and the Campana–Peternell conjecture. As a result, we give a partial result on Schnell’s conjecture under two independent assumptions. One weakens Schnell’s assumption of the pseudo-effectivity of the canonical bundle of the base by adding some effective divisor supported on the ramification locus. The other is analogous to results on algebraic fiber spaces where the existence of good minimal models of a general fiber is assumed, but we use a priori a weaker assumption. More precisely, we prove Schnell’s conjecture when the canonical class of the general fiber is represented by a rigid current .

1.
Introduction

In
[
20
]
, Schnell attempts to prove an equivalence between two conjectures, namely the non-vanishing conjecture and the Campana–Peternell conjecture (on Kodaira dimension, see
[
5
]
*page 43). Both conjectures are special cases of the abundance conjecture. The non-vanishing conjecture predicts that if
X
is a smooth projective variety whose canonical bundle
K_{X}
is pseudo-effective, then a positive multiple of
K_{X}
is effective. The Campana–Peternell conjecture says the following.

Conjecture 1.1

(Campana–Peternell)
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety and let
D
be an effective divisor. Suppose that
m_{0}K_{X}-D
is pseudo-effective for some
m_{0}>0
. Then
\kappa(X)\geq\kappa(X,D)
.

In particular, if we put
D=0
in the conjecture above, we get the non-vanishing conjecture. In this sense, the Campana–Peternell conjecture seems to be a priori stronger than non-vanishing. In
[
20
]
, it is shown that assuming non-vanishing, the Campana–Peternell conjecture reduces to proving the following problem on algebraic fiber spaces.

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be an algebraic fiber space between smooth projective varieties with
\kappa(F)=0
, where
F
is the general fiber. If there is an ample divisor
H
on
Y
such that
m_{0}K_{X}-f^{*}H
is pseudo-effective, then
\kappa(X)=\dim Y
.

This formulation is slightly different than
[
20
]
*Conjecture 10.1. However, one can reduce to the case above by following the lines of
[
20
]
*§9. We prefer this formulation so that the canonical bundle formula naturally comes into the picture. Schnell proves his conjecture when
K_{Y}
is pseudo-effective, using the result of Păun–Takayama
[
18
]
on semi-positive metrics on relative pluri-adjoint line bundles.

The aim of this article is to observe what canonical bundle formula gives toward Conjecture
1.2
. As a result, we are able to weaken Schnell’s assumption on the pseudo-effectivity of
K_{Y}
, and also observe that
rigid currents
in the sense of
[
15
]
naturally plays a role in this conjecture.

1.1.
Canonical bundle formula

We introduce the canonical bundle formula, following
[
11
,
8
,
13
]
, in the set-up of Conjecture
1.2
. We also refer to
[
12
]
for analytic aspects of the canonical bundle formula using fiberwise integrals, which is related to the content appearing in the later part of this paper.

First, following
[
20
]
*§5, we note that this conjecture is insensitive upon taking birational models of
X
and
Y
. By taking suitable resolutions, we may and will assume that we have an SNC divisor
Q=\sum_{l\in L}Q_{l}
on
Y
such that
f
is smooth over
Y_{0}=Y\setminus Q
, and
f^{-1}(Q)_{\mathrm{red}}=\sum_{j\in J}P_{j}
is an SNC divisor on
X
. We can write
K_{X}
in the following way:

(1)

K_{X}\sim_{\mathbf{Q}}f^{*}(K_{Y}+B_{Y}+M_{Y})+\Delta,

so that

(1)

f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(\left\lfloor i\Delta^{+}\right\rfloor)=\mathcal{O}_{Y}
for all
i>0
,

(2)

\codim_{Y}f(\supp\Delta^{-})\geq 2
, and

(3)

H^{0}(X,mK_{X})=H^{0}(Y,m(K_{Y}+B_{Y}+M_{Y}))
for sufficiently divisible
m
(see
[
8
]
*Theorem 4.5).

Here,
\Delta^{+}
and
\Delta^{-}
are the effective divisors sharing no irreducible components and satisfying
\Delta=\Delta^{+}-\Delta^{-}
. Roughly speaking, the divisor
B_{Y}
measures the singularity of the fibers of
f
, and
M_{Y}
measures the Hodge-theoretic variation of the fibers. In particular,
B_{Y}
is always effective and is supported in the discriminant locus of
f
, and
M_{Y}
is nef. For a precise description of the divisors
B_{Y}
and
\Delta
, we refer to §
2.2
.

We return to the setup of Conjecture
1.2
. Suppose we have some
m_{0}>0
such that
m_{0}K_{X}-f^{*}H
is pseudo-effective. Hence
K_{X}-\epsilon f^{*}H
is pseudo-effective for
\epsilon>0
small enough. By taking birational models of both
X
and
Y
, we assume that
f\colon X\to Y
satisfies the SNC assumption as above. In order to show that
\kappa(X)=\dim Y
, it is equivalent to showing that
K_{Y}+B_{Y}+M_{Y}
is big, as the pluricanonical sections of
X
descend to
Y
. Hence, it is enough to show that
K_{Y}+B_{Y}+M_{Y}-\epsilon H
is pseudo-effective for some
\epsilon>0
. Rewriting the canonical bundle formula, we have

(K_{X}+\Delta^{-}-\epsilon f^{*}H)-\Delta^{+}\sim_{\mathbf{Q}}f^{*}(K_{Y}+B_{Y}+M_{Y}-\epsilon H).

Note that
K_{X}+\Delta^{-}-\epsilon f^{*}H
has a semi-positively curved singular hermitian metric on
X
. Hence, we see that
\Delta^{+}
is the only obstacle for descending the metric positivity of
K_{X}+\Delta^{-}-\epsilon f^{*}H
down to
Y
. In our main result, we give two separate ways to deal with this
\Delta^{+}
.

1.2.
Main results

We state the main results.

Theorem 1.3
.

As in the setup of Conjecture
1.2
and §
1.1
, suppose that one of the two following conditions holds:

(1)

K_{Y}+(1-\epsilon)B_{Y}
is pseudo-effective for some
\epsilon>0
, or

(2)

for the general fiber
F
, the class
\{K_{F}\}
is rigid.

Then
\kappa(X)=\dim Y
.

For a compact Kähler manifold
X
, a class
\alpha\in H^{1,1}(X)(\simeq H_{\mathrm{BC}}^{1,1}(X))
is
rigid
if it contains exactly one closed positive (1,1)-current
T
such that
\{T\}\in\alpha
. For varieties of Kodaira dimension zero, the rigidity of the canonical class is tightly related to the existence of good minimal models. For a systematic treatment of rigid currents in terms of birational geometry, we refer to
[
15
]
. Here, we mention the easy direction that if
F
admits a good minimal model, then the cohomology class
\{K_{F}\}
is rigid
[
15
]
*Lemma 3.9.

We point out that the proofs for the two cases are independent. Also, due to technical reasons concerning the volume asymptotics, we were not able to handle the case when
\epsilon=0
for the first condition.

Comparison with known results

We compare Theorem
1.3
with previously known results. The first result can be understood as a strengthening of

*Theorem 12.1, where he assumes that
K_{Y}
is pseudo-effective. Indeed, adding an effective divisor

[

]
*Question 4.1,

,

Our second result is somewhat analogous to results on algebraic fiber spaces assuming that the general fibers have good minimal models. However, we emphasize that the rigidity of the canonical class is a property
purely in terms of hermitian metrics
. For example, a numerically trivial line bundle is rigid, even if it is non-torsion. This condition is weaker than the existence of good minimal models as mentioned in the section above, but still stronger than the Campana–Peternell conjecture. Indeed, given a smooth projective variety
X
whose canonical class is rigid (which implies
\kappa(X)=0
), if one writes
K_{X}=D+E
where
D
is an effective
\mathbf{Q}
-divisor and
E
is pseudo-effective, the rigidity of the canonical class forces
\kappa(X,D)=0
. In this vein, we would like to formulate the following two questions:

Question 1.5
.

Is there an example of a smooth projective variety
X
with zero Kodaira dimension, where we know whose canonical class
\{K_{X}\}
is rigid, but the existence of good minimal models is not known?

Question 1.6
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety with zero Kodaira dimension. Suppose that if we write
K_{X}=D+E
as a sum of an effective divisor
D
and a pseudo-effective divisor
E
, we have
\kappa(X,D)=0
. In this case, is
K_{X}
rigid?

We would also like to compare our second result with the inductive approach by
[

]
*Proposition 2.6. Let
X
be a smooth projective
n

D
be an effective divisor such that

is pseudo-effective for some

. Assuming the Campana–Peternell conjecture in dimension

, by applying the easy addition (see

*Corollary 1.7) to the Iitaka fibration of

, they show that the Campana–Peternell conjecture holds in dimension

except for the two following situations:

(1)

\kappa(X)=0
and
\kappa(X,D)>0
, or

(2)

Assuming the non-vanishing conjecture in dimension

Corollary 1.7
.

Rigidity of the canonical class for smooth projective varieties with Kodaira dimension zero in dimension
n-1
and non-vanishing in dimension
n
imply the Campana–Peternell conjecture in dimension
n
.

Proof.

By
[
20
]
*§9, the Campana–Peternell conjecture on dimension
n
reduces to non-vanishing in dimension
\leq n
and Conjecture
1.2
for
\dim X=n
. Theorem
1.3
(2) shows that Conjecture
1.2
is true under the assumption that the general fiber has a rigid canonical class.
∎

We also give an unconditional result for fourfolds.

Corollary 1.8
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective fourfold. Let

\kappa_{\mathrm{CP}}(X)=\sup\{\kappa(X,D):mK_{X}-D\text{ is pseudo-effective for some }m>0\}.

Then
\kappa(X)=\kappa_{\mathrm{CP}}(X)
unless
(\kappa(X),\kappa_{\mathrm{CP}}(X))=(-\infty,0)
.

Proof.

It is clear that
\kappa_{\mathrm{CP}}(X)\geq\kappa(X)
, hence we assume that
\kappa_{\mathrm{CP}}(X)\geq 1
. Let

be a divisor on

such that

such that

for an ample divisor

(2).
∎

Remark 1.9
.

We remark that Corollary
1.8
is slightly stronger than the inductive formulation in Corollary
1.7
since we didn’t assume the non-vanishing conjecture in dimension 4, but rather treated it as an ‘exceptional case’. We point out that Corollary
1.8
can already be achieved using the method by Taji that is explained above.

Finally, we mention that the Campana–Peternell conjecture is closely related to the ‘birational stability conjecture’ of Campana (see
[
5
]
*page 43 line 3). For a smooth projective variety
X
of dimension
n
, we define

\kappa_{+}(X)=\sup\{\kappa(\det\mathcal{F}):\mathcal{F}\subset\Omega_{X}^{p}\text{ for some }p>0\}.

The conjecture predicts that
\kappa_{+}(X)=\kappa(X)
if
K_{X}
is pseudo-effective. This is actually a special case of the Campana–Peternell conjecture as mentioned in
[
5
]
. Indeed, using
[

]
*Theorem 1.3 or
[
5
]
*Theorem 1.8,
L=\det\mathcal{F}
embeds to

for some

and we see that

\det\left(\otimes^{m}\Omega_{X}^{1}/L\right)\simeq\omega_{X}^{\otimes m\cdot n^{m-1}}\otimes L^{-1}

is pseudo-effective (one can assume that
L
is saturated in

). Therefore, the Campana–Peternell conjecture tells
\kappa(X)\geq\kappa(L)
. The other inequality
\kappa(X)\leq\kappa_{+}(X)
is clear.

2.
The proof

2.1.
Strategy of the proof

We give an outline of the proof of Theorem
1.3
. The main strategy is to use the Siu decomposition
[
21
]
to deal with the divisorial part of closed positive (1,1)-currents. It says that for a closed positive (1,1)-current
T
on a complex manifold
X
,
T
can be written as

T=\sum_{W}\nu(T,W)[W]+R,

where
W
runs through all codimension 1 analytic subsets of
X
,
\nu(T,W)
is the generic Lelong number of
T
along
W
, and
R
is a closed positive (1,1)-current such that
\nu(R,W)=0
for all codimension 1 analytic subsets of
X
. Also, the sum in the formula above is a countable sum. Lelong numbers could be understood as analytic analogues of multiplicities and we refer to
[
7
]
*Chapter 2 for the basic definition and properties of Lelong numbers. The generic Lelong number is defined as

\nu(T,W)=\inf_{x\in W}\nu(T,x)

and the equality holds for a very general member
x\in W
due to the analyticity of the super-level sets of Lelong numbers (see
[
21
]
or
[
7
]
*14.3).

The way we use the Siu decomposition is the following. Let
L
be a pseudo-effective divisor class on
X
, hence equipped with a singular metric
h_{L}
with semi-positive curvature. Denote by
T_{h_{L}}
the curvature current of
h_{L}
. Then for a prime divisor
W
, the divisor class
L-\nu(T_{h_{L}},W)[W]
is again pseudo-effective. Hence, we can subtract certain effective divisors from pseudo-effective classes and still remain pseudo-effective.

Of course, we have to start with the singular metric on the line bundle
L_{0}=m_{0}K_{X}-f^{*}H
, where we have a priori no control on the singularity of the metric. Under the assumption (1) in Theorem
1.3
, we use the Bergman-kernel metric on

L_{1}=m_{1}K_{X/Y}+(m_{0}K_{X}-f^{*}H)

due to
[
18
]
. The point in
[
20
]
is that the singularities of the metric improve via considering the Bergman-kernel metric on
L_{1}
. We want to stress that when the Kodaira dimension of the general fiber is zero, the description of the Bergman-kernel metric is even more explicit. However, due to the presence of the
relative
pluricanonical bundle on
L_{1}
, Schnell has to assume that
K_{Y}
is pseudo-effective in order to prove
[
20
]
*Theorem 12.1. Similarly, we cannot avoid this issue as well, but we get a weaker assumption in this case by explicitly analyzing the singularity of the Bergman-kernel metric over the codimension 1 locus of
Y
. As a result, we gain an extra effective factor of
(1-\epsilon)B_{Y}
.

Under the second assumption, we observe that the rigidity of the canonical class of the fibers gives a strict constraint on the behavior of a positively curved metric
h_{L_{0}}
on
L_{0}=m_{0}K_{X}-f^{*}H
, since the restrictions of
h_{L_{0}}
to the fibers are essentially unique up to scaling.

2.2.
Coefficients of
\Delta
and
B_{Y}
in the canonical bundle formula

We describe some coefficients of
\Delta
appearing in the canonical bundle formula (
1
) in §
1.1
. Let
E
be a horizontal divisor on
X
. Then we have

r\ord_{E}\Delta=\ord_{E|_{F}}\sigma

where
\sigma\in H^{0}(F,rK_{F})
is the unique (up to constant) section. Here we take a sufficiently divisible
r>0
.

We describe the coefficients of the vertical divisors lying over codimension 1 points of
Y
. Consider
Q_{l}\subset Y
and let

J_{l}:=\{j\in J:f(P_{j})=Q_{l}\}.

We consider a local non-vanishing section
\alpha
of
f_{*}\omega_{X/Y}^{\otimes r}
in a neighborhood
\Omega
of a general point of
Q_{l}
. By fixing a local volume form
dt
on
\Omega
, we obtain an
r
-pluricanonical form
\alpha(dt^{\otimes r})
on
f^{-1}(\Omega)
. For
j\in J_{l}
, the order of vanishing of
\alpha(dt^{\otimes r})
along
P_{j}
equals to
r\cdot\ord_{P_{j}}\Delta
. One can also see that for all
l\in L
, we have
\ord_{P_{j}}\Delta\geq 0
for
j\in J_{l}
, and there exists at least one
j\in J_{l}
such that
\ord_{P_{j}}\Delta=0
. We put
a_{j}=\ord_{P_{j}}\Delta
.

We now explain how the coefficients of
B_{Y}
are determined. Let
B_{Y}=\sum b_{l}Q_{l}
. Then,

1-b_{l}=\sup\{b\in\mathbf{Q}:(X,-\Delta+b\cdot f^{*}Q_{l})\text{ is lc over }\eta_{Q_{l}}\}

where
\eta_{Q_{l}}
is the generic point of
Q_{l}
. These numbers can be described more explicitly as follows. Put
\delta_{j}=\ord_{P_{j}}f^{*}Q_{l}
for
j\in J_{l}
. Then we can easily see that

b_{l}=\max_{j\in J_{l}}\left(1-\frac{a_{j}+1}{\delta_{j}}\right)

and that
b_{l}
lies in the interval
[0,1[
. We also refer to
[
13
]
for a nice exposition on the canonical bundle formula.

2.3.
The proof for the second case

We give a proof of Theorem
1.3
under the second assumption. We recall that
L_{0}=m_{0}K_{X}-f^{*}H
is pseudo-effective, hence carries a semi-positively curved metric
h_{0}
. Throughout the paper, we will always take
m_{0}
to be divisible enough. We first show that we can subtract the horizontal part of
\Delta
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that the canonical class of the general fiber
K_{F}
is rigid, as in the second assumption of the setup of Theorem
1.3
. Then,
m_{0}(K_{X}-\Delta^{h})-f^{*}H
is pseudo-effective.

Proof.

Let
Y_{\mathrm{rig}}
be the set of points
y\in Y_{0}
such that the canonical class of the fiber
K_{X_{y}}
is rigid. By assumption,
Y_{\mathrm{rig}}
contains a Zariski open subset of
Y_{0}
. Note that
L_{0}|_{X_{y}}=m_{0}K_{X_{y}}
. Hence, for each
y\in Y_{\mathrm{rig}}
, the restricted metric
h_{0}|_{X_{y}}
is either constantly
+\infty
, or its curvature current is
m_{0}[\Delta^{h}|_{X_{y}}]
. We denote by
T_{h_{0}}
the curvature current of
h_{0}
. Consider an open ball
\Omega\subset Y_{\mathrm{rig}}
and consider a section
\sigma
on
H^{0}(f^{-1}(\Omega),m_{0}K_{X}-f^{*}H)
which vanishes along
m_{0}\Delta^{h}
. We see that
|\sigma|_{h_{0}}\colon f^{-1}(\Omega)\to\mathbf{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is constant along the fibers.

Let
E
be one of the components
\Delta^{h}
and let
\alpha_{E}=\ord_{E}\Delta^{h}
. Then
\sigma
vanishes along
E
with order
m_{0}\alpha_{E}
. Consider an open subset
\Omega^{\prime}\subset f^{-1}(\Omega)
intersecting
E
. We may assume that
\Omega^{\prime}
does not intersect other components of
\Delta^{h}
. Suppose that
E
is given by the equation
u=0
. Then
\tau\coloneqq\sigma/u^{m_{0}\alpha_{E}}
is a local non-vanishing section of
m_{0}K_{X}-f^{*}H
on
\Omega^{\prime}
. By the rigidity assumption, we have

|\tau|_{h_{0}}^{2}=e^{-\varphi(y)}|u|^{-2m_{0}\alpha_{E}}.

Note that
\varphi
only depends on
y\in\Omega
and it is psh on
\Omega
. In particular, it is bounded above. This shows that
\nu(T_{h_{0}},E)\geq m_{0}\alpha_{E}
since
E\cap\Omega^{\prime}
is Zariski dense in
E
. Using the Siu decomposition, we get the pseudo-effectivity of
m_{0}(K_{X}-\Delta^{h})-f^{*}H
.
∎

Now we give the proof of the theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
(2).

Let
L=K_{Y}+B_{Y}+M_{Y}
. Note that over
Y_{0}
, we have

m_{0}(K_{X}-\Delta^{h})-f^{*}H\sim f^{*}(m_{0}L-H).

For a local non-vanishing section
\sigma\in H^{0}(\Omega,m_{0}L-H)
for
\Omega\subset Y_{0}
, we see that

|\sigma|_{\widetilde{h}_{0},x}^{2}:X_{y}\to\mathbf{R}_{\geq 0}\cup\{+\infty\}

has to be a constant function since the only global psh functions on a compact space are constants. We define
|\sigma|_{h,y}:=|\sigma|_{\widetilde{h}_{0},x}
for
y=f(x)
. This gives a positively curved singular metric on
m_{0}L-H
over
Y_{0}
. Therefore, it remains to show that the local potentials are bounded above outside of a codimension 2 subset of
Y
. Then this metric automatically extends across
Y\setminus Y_{0}
and therefore,
m_{0}L-H
is pseudo-effective.

We recall from §
1.1
for the set-up of the canonical bundle formula and §
2.2
for the description of the coefficients appearing therein. Consider an open neighborhood
\Omega
of a point of
Q_{l}
that avoids
f(\supp\Delta^{-})
and other components of
Q
. Let
\sigma
be a local non-vanishing section of
m_{0}L-H
on
\Omega
, which we can also view as a section on
m_{0}(K_{X}-\Delta)-f^{*}H
on
f^{-1}(\Omega)
, vanishing along the divisor
\sum_{j\in J_{l}}m_{0}a_{j}P_{j}
. Note that there exists some
j\in J_{l}
such that
a_{j}=0
. Then we have an open set
\Omega^{\prime}
of
f^{-1}(\Omega)
intersecting
P_{j}
that surjects to
\Omega
. Hence, we can assume that
\sigma
does not vanish on
\Omega^{\prime}
. Since
|\sigma|_{h_{0},x}^{2}
is bounded below by a positive constant on
\Omega^{\prime}
,
|\sigma|_{\widetilde{h}_{0},y}^{2}
is also bounded below by a positive constant on
\Omega
. This shows that the local potentials for the metric
\widetilde{h}_{0}
are bounded above. This concludes the proof of the second case.
∎

The rest of the paper is devoted to the proof of the first case of Theorem
1.3
.

2.4.
Bergman-kernel metric

We briefly describe the construction of the relative Bergman-kernel metric, constructed in
[
18
]
, adapted to our setup. The upshot is that for a given metric
h_{0}
on
L_{0}=m_{0}K_{X}-f^{*}H
, we can induce a positively curved hermitian metric on
L_{1}=m_{1}K_{X/Y}+L_{0}
with ‘better properties’, when
m_{1}
is big enough. We point out that the situation is substantially simpler than
[
18
]
due to the fact that the Kodaira dimension of the general fiber is zero. We assume that
m_{0}
and
m_{1}
are sufficiently divisible and
m_{1}\gg m_{0}
. We let
f^{-1}(Y_{0})=X_{0}
and describe the singular hermitian metric of
h_{1}
on
L_{1}=m_{1}K_{X/Y}+L_{0}
on
X_{0}
. For
y\in Y_{0}
and
\sigma_{y}\in H^{0}(X_{y},m_{1}K_{X_{y}}+L_{0,y})
, we define

l(\sigma_{y}):=\left(\int_{X_{y}}|\sigma_{y}|^{2/m_{1}}_{h_{0}}\right)^{m_{1}/2}.

For the right-hand side, we can view
\sigma_{y}
as an
m_{1}
-pluricanonical form with values in
(L_{0,y},h_{0,y})
on
X_{y}
, and therefore,
|\sigma_{y}|^{2/m_{1}}_{h_{0}}
is a well-defined volume form on
X_{y}
. We simply define
l(\sigma_{y})=\infty
when
h_{0,y}\equiv+\infty
. For a fixed point
x\in X_{0}
such that
f(x)=y
and a local section
s
of
L_{1}
at
x
, we have

|s|_{h_{1},x}=l(\sigma_{y}),

where
\sigma_{y}(x)=s(x)
. This is due to the fact that the dimension of
H^{0}(X_{y},m_{1}K_{X_{y}}+L_{0,y})
is 1, so the supremum in the formula goes away. We follow the convention that if
\sigma_{y}
vanishes at
x
, then
|s|_{h_{1},x}=+\infty
. The main result in
[
18
]
is that the local potentials of this metric are upper semi-continuous and plurisubharmonic, and bounded above towards
X\setminus X_{0}
. This allows us to extend the metric on
L_{1}|_{X_{0}}
uniquely to a semi-positively curved singular metric on
L_{1}
. We note that since
m_{1}\gg m_{0}
, the set
\{y\in Y_{0}:l(\sigma_{y})<+\infty\}
is non-empty, and in fact, of full measure. We denote by
T_{1}
the curvature current associated to the metric
h_{1}
on
L_{1}
.

Remark 2.2
.

Following the notation in
[
18
]
, one technical issue in the construction of the Bergman-kernel metric is that they have to define the set

Y_{m_{1},\mathrm{ext}}=\{y\in Y_{0}:h^{0}(X_{y},m_{1}K_{X_{y}}+L_{0,y})=\rank f_{*}(m_{1}K_{X/Y}+L)\}

and construct the metric on
X_{m_{1},\mathrm{ext}}=f^{-1}(Y_{m_{1},\mathrm{ext}})
, ignoring the behavior of the metric on
X_{0}\setminus X_{m_{1},\mathrm{ext}}
. In our situation, we have
Y_{0}=Y_{m_{1},\mathrm{ext}}
due to the invariance of plurigenera
[
22
,
19
]
.

2.5.
Lelong numbers along the horizontal divisors

We first estimate the Lelong numbers on the horizontal divisors. Let
E
be a component of
\Delta^{h}
and let
\alpha=\ord_{E}\Delta^{h}
. Consider an open set
\Omega\subset Y_{0}
and fix a local non-vanishing section
\sigma\in f_{*}(m_{1}K_{X/Y}+L_{0})
. We may assume that
1\leq l(\sigma_{y})<+\infty
for
y\in\Omega
by shrinking
\Omega
and rescaling
\sigma
. Let
s
be a local non-vanishing section of
m_{1}K_{X/Y}+L_{0}
on a neighborhood of
E\cap f^{-1}(\Omega)
and suppose that
E
is given by the equation
u=0
. Then we can assume that
\sigma=u^{\alpha(m_{1}+m_{0})}s
, where we consider
\sigma
as an element in
H^{0}(f^{-1}(\Omega),m_{1}K_{X/Y}+L_{0})
. We denote by
|s|_{h_{1}}^{2}=e^{-\varphi}
and we get

e^{-\varphi}=|u|^{-2\alpha(m_{0}+m_{1})}l(\sigma_{f(x)})^{2}\geq|u|^{-2\alpha(m_{0}+m_{1})}.

This implies that
\nu(\varphi,x)\geq\alpha(m_{1}+m_{0})
for
x\in E\cap f^{-1}(\Omega)
. Therefore, we have
\nu(T_{1},E)\geq\alpha(m_{1}+m_{0})
since
E\cap f^{-1}(\Omega)
is Zariski dense in
E
.

2.6.
Lelong numbers along the vertical divisors

Here, we fix
Q_{l}
and
P_{j}
such that
j\in J_{l}
. We recall the notations for the setup from §
2.2
. Fix a general point
x_{0}\in P_{j}
so that
x_{0}
is not contained in other divisors in the support of
\Delta
. We fix local coordinates
t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}
on
Y
centered at
f(x_{0})
and
z_{1},\ldots,z_{q}
on
X
centered at
x_{0}
such that

•

P_{j}
is defined by the equation
z_{1}=0
,

•

Q_{l}
is defined by the equation
t_{1}=0
, and

•

The local expression of
f:X\to Y
is
t_{1}=z_{1}^{\delta_{j}}
, and
t_{j}=z_{j}
for
j=2,\ldots,p
.

Let
\sigma
be a local non-vanishing section of
f_{*}(m_{1}K_{X/Y}+L_{0})
on a neighborhood of
f(x_{0})
, and let
s
be a non-vanishing section of
m_{1}K_{X/Y}+L_{0}
on a neighborhood of
x_{0}
. Then by the discussion in §
2.2
, we may assume that

\sigma=z_{1}^{(m_{1}+m_{0})a_{j}}s

and therefore

|s|_{h_{1},x}^{2}=|z_{1}|^{-2(m_{1}+m_{0})a_{j}}l(\sigma_{f(x)})^{2}.

Using direct calculation, we will show separately in §
3
that for fixed
\epsilon>0
, we have

l(\sigma_{f(x)})^{2}\gtrsim|t_{1}|^{-2m_{1}(1-\epsilon)b_{l}},

if
m_{1}
is sufficiently large. The proof of the volume asymptotics will be omitted at this point in order to simplify the arguments and stress the key ideas. Since
t_{1}=z_{1}^{\delta_{j}}
, we have that

\nu(T_{1},P_{j})\geq(m_{1}+m_{0})a_{j}+(1-\epsilon)b_{l}\delta_{j}m_{1}.

2.7.
Conclusion

From the computation of the generic Lelong numbers of the metric on
L_{1}
, we see that

L_{1}^{\prime}=m_{1}K_{X/Y}+(m_{0}K_{X}-f^{*}H)-(m_{1}+m_{0})\left(\Delta^{h}+\sum_{l\in L}\sum_{j\in J_{l}}a_{j}P_{j}\right)-m_{1}\sum_{l\in L}\sum_{j\in J_{l}}(1-\epsilon)b_{l}\delta_{j}P_{j}

is pseudo-effective. Over a complement of a codimension 2 subset of
Y
, we see that this is equivalent to

f^{*}\Big((m_{1}+m_{0})(K_{Y}+B_{Y}+M_{Y})-m_{1}(K_{Y}+(1-\epsilon)B_{Y})-H\Big).

Hence, the singular hermitian metric on
L_{1}^{\prime}
descends to a semi-positively curved singular hermitian metric on

(m_{1}+m_{0})(K_{Y}+B_{Y}+M_{Y})-m_{1}(K_{Y}+(1-\epsilon)B_{Y})-H,

over a complement of a codimension 2 subset of
Y
. Therefore, the metric extends to the whole space
Y
. Since we assumed that
K_{Y}+(1-\epsilon)B_{Y}
is pseudo-effective,
m_{1}(K_{Y}+(1-\epsilon)B_{Y})+H
is big, and therefore,
K_{Y}+B_{Y}+M_{Y}
is big. This concludes the first case of Theorem
1.3
.
\square

Remark 2.3
.

The argument unconditionally implies that
M_{Y}+\delta(K_{Y}+B_{Y})+\epsilon B_{Y}
is big for some
0<\delta\ll\epsilon\ll 1
.

Question 2.4
.

Even though
K_{Y}+(1-\epsilon)B_{Y}
is not pseudo-effective in general, we predict that
K_{Y}+Q
is big. We already know that
M_{Y}+\delta(K_{Y}+B_{Y})+\epsilon B_{Y}
is big for some
0<\delta\ll\epsilon\ll 1
. Indeed, if one manages to show that
M_{Y}
is big, then
K_{Y}+Q
is big.

Let’s assume that
M_{Y}
is big. Note from
[
13
]
that we have a variation of Hodge structures
\mathbb{V}
on
Y_{0}
and after a finite cover
\pi\colon\widetilde{Y}\to Y
, the pull-back of
M_{Y}
coincides with the canonical extension of the lowest piece of
\pi^{*}\mathbb{V}
and we can also assume that
\pi^{*}\mathbb{V}
has unipotent local monodromies. Then one can calculate the intersection number
(\pi^{*}M_{Y})^{\dim Y}
by integrating the curvature induced by the polarization of
\mathbb{V}
, and this being strictly positive implies that the period map is immersive at one point (see
[
1
]
*Lemma 6.15 and 6.17 for instance). This shows that the period map on
Y_{0}
is immersive at least at one point. Then the result of
[
3
]
says that
K_{Y}+Q
is big.

In this vein, we would like to ask the question if one can unconditionally show that
K_{Y}+Q
is big in the set-up of Theorem
1.3
. A posteriori, this should be the case since we have
\kappa(X)=\dim Y
and this implies the bigness of
K_{Y}+Q
by
[
17
]
*Theorem 1.9.

3.
Volume asymptotics

Here, we give the proof of the volume asymptotics in §
2.6
.

Lemma 3.1
.

As in the setup of §
2.6
, we have the following volume asymptotics:

l(\sigma_{f(x)})^{2}\gtrsim|t_{1}|^{-2m_{1}(1-\epsilon)b_{l}},

if
m_{1}
is sufficiently big.

Proof.

First, we point out that we can ignore the case when
b_{l}=0
since
l(\sigma_{f(x)})
is bounded below by a positive constant following the proof of
[
18
]
*Theorem 4.2.7. Therefore, we assume that
b_{l}>0
.

Let
\Omega
be a neighborhood on a general point of
Q_{l}
and write the coordinate functions as
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})
and identify
\Omega
with the polydisk of radius 1. Assume that
Q_{l}
is defined by the equation
t_{1}=0
. Let

f^{-1}(\Omega)\supset\bigcup_{j\in J_{l}}\Omega^{(j)},

where
\Omega^{(j)}
are disjoint open sets where
\Omega^{(j)}
only intersects with
P_{j}
. Fix coordinate charts
z_{1}^{(j)},\ldots,z_{q}^{(j)}
of
\Omega^{(j)}
and assume that
P_{j}
is defined by the equation
z_{1}^{(j)}
and the local expression for
f
is

f(z_{1}^{(j)},\ldots,z_{q}^{(j)})=((z_{1}^{(j)})^{\delta_{j}},z_{2}^{(j)},\ldots,z_{p}^{(j)}).

Identify
\Omega^{(j)}
with the polydisk of radius 1 as well. Let
\sigma
be a local non-vanishing section of
f_{*}(m_{1}K_{X/Y}+L_{0})
on
\Omega
and
s_{j}
be local non-vanishing sections of
m_{1}K_{X/Y}+L_{0}
on
\Omega^{(j)}
. We can assume that

\sigma=(z_{1}^{(j)})^{(m_{1}+m_{0})a_{j}}s_{j}

and

s_{j}(dt^{\otimes m_{1}})=(dz_{1}^{(j)}\wedge\ldots\wedge dz_{q}^{(j)})^{\otimes m_{1}}\otimes e_{j},

where
e_{j}
is a non-vanishing section of
L_{0}
on
\Omega^{(j)}
. Here, we identify
m_{1}K_{X/Y}+L_{0}
with the sheaf

\SheafHom_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(f^{*}(m_{1}K_{Y}),m_{1}K_{X}+L_{0}).

We let
|e_{j}|_{h_{0}}^{2}=e^{-\varphi_{j}}
, where
\varphi_{j}:\Omega^{(j)}\to\mathbf{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
are the local weight functions for the metric on
L_{0}
. By shrinking the domains if necessary, one can assume that
\varphi_{j}\leq C_{0}
for some constant. For
y=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})\in\Omega
, we have

l(\sigma_{y})^{2/m_{1}}=\int_{X_{y}}|\sigma_{y}|_{h_{0}}^{2/m_{1}}\geq\sum_{j\in J_{l}}\int_{\Omega_{y}^{(j)}}|\sigma_{y}|_{h_{0}}^{2/m_{1}},

where
\Omega_{y}^{(j)}=X_{y}\cap f^{-1}(\Omega)
. We note that

\frac{dz_{1}^{(j)}}{z_{1}^{(j)}}\wedge dz_{2}^{(j)}\wedge\ldots\wedge dz_{p}^{(j)}=\frac{1}{\delta_{j}}f^{*}\left(\frac{dt_{1}}{t_{1}}\wedge dt_{2}\wedge\ldots\wedge dt_{p}\right).

By definition,
\sigma_{y}
is the section on
m_{1}K_{X_{y}}+L_{0,y}
satisfying

\sigma(dt^{\otimes m_{1}})|_{X_{y}}=\sigma_{y}\otimes f^{*}(dt^{\otimes m_{1}}).

Therefore, the local expression for
\sigma_{y}
is

\sigma_{y}=\frac{1}{\delta_{j}^{m_{1}}}(z_{1}^{(j)})^{(m_{1}+m_{0})a_{j}+(1-\delta_{j})m_{1}}\cdot(dz_{p+1}^{(j)}\wedge\ldots\wedge dz_{q}^{(j)})^{\otimes m_{1}}\otimes s_{j}.

This shows that

\int_{\Omega_{j}^{(j)}}|\sigma_{y}|_{h_{0}}^{2/m_{1}}\geq C_{1}\cdot|t_{1}|^{2\cdot\frac{(m_{1}+m_{0})a_{j}+(1-\delta_{j})m_{1}}{\delta_{j}m_{1}}},

where
C_{1}>0
is some constant, using
t_{1}=(z_{1}^{(j)})^{\delta_{j}}
.
Therefore, we can see that

\int_{X_{y}}|\sigma_{y}|_{h_{0}}^{2/m_{1}}\gtrsim|t_{1}|^{-2b_{l}^{\prime}},

where

-b_{l}^{\prime}=\min_{j\in J_{l}}\frac{(m_{1}+m_{0})a_{j}+(1-\delta_{j})m_{1}}{m_{1}\delta_{j}}\geq\min_{j\in J_{l}}\frac{m_{1}a_{j}+(1-\delta_{j})m_{1}}{m_{1}\delta_{j}}=-b_{l}.

Moreover, we see that

|b_{l}-b_{l}^{\prime}|\leq\frac{m_{0}}{m_{1}}\max_{j\in J_{l}}\frac{a_{j}}{\delta_{j}}

and we can make the right-hand side to be less than
\epsilon b_{l}
, by taking
m_{1}
sufficiently big, since
b_{l}>0
. This shows that

l(\sigma_{y})^{2}\gtrsim|t_{1}|^{-2m_{1}(1-\epsilon)b_{l}}.

∎

Remark 3.2
.

If one uses the locus on the intersection of
P_{j}
’s, it is possible to get an extra log term for the volume asymptotics, following the computation of
[
2
]
or
[
24
]
. However, we do not write it down since we don’t need this.

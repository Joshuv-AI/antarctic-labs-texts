Our approach is based on the methods of cut-elimination developed by the author in

Here we develop the corresponding ordinal notations (without much reference to the cut-elimination techniques that motivate them). We first present a (more or less) standard presentations of the so-called Buchholz ordinal (the proof-theoretic ordinal of
\Pi^{1}_{1}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{CA}_{0}

The notation we present, as is typical, uses ordinal notations

\Omega_{1}
is understood as being larger than any notation representing a countable ordinal term

Even more formally, our ordinal notation is equipped, at least implicitly, with a system of fundamental sequences. An ordinal of “countable formal cofinality” is one in which the associated fundamental sequence of smaller ordinals is indexed by
\mathbb{N}

\mathbb{N}

As a stepping stone, we give a second presentation of the same ordinal using an approach we call “polymorphic”—we have a single notation

3.
Buchholz’s Ordinal, Polymorphically

3.1.
Ordinal Terms

The presence of terms with variables invites us to consider the term

Definition 3.3

3.2.
Well-Foundedness

Since
\Omega^{(0)}>\Omega^{(-1)}>\cdots
, the ordinal terms are certainly not well-founded on their face. This is because our notation looks at ordinal terms “from above”, fixing
\Omega^{(0)}
to be the highest cardinality. We should instead compare “from below”, demanding the ordinals share the same ground.

Definition 3.6
.

For any
\alpha
, we define
\mathbb{G}(\alpha)=\min\mathbb{FC}(\alpha)
or
\{-\infty\}
if
\mathbb{FC}(\alpha)=\emptyset
.

We call
\mathbb{G}(\alpha)
the
ground
of
\alpha
—it is the lowest cardinality appearing free, so we think of it as the “base level” of the ordinal. We would like to compare ordinals by positioning them to share the same ground.

Given an ordinal
\alpha
, we write
\alpha^{*}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\alpha&\text{if }\overline{\mathbb{FC}}(\alpha)=-\infty\\
\alpha^{\geq 0}_{-\overline{\mathbb{FC}}(\alpha)}&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.
. We may think of this as a normalized version of
\alpha
, in the sense that we have arranged pushed the cardinalities up as high as we are allowed to; in particular,
\overline{\mathbb{FC}}(\alpha^{*})\in\{-\infty,0\}
.

We could think of ordinal terms with
\overline{\mathbb{FC}}(\alpha)=0
and
\mathbb{G}(\alpha)\geq n
as being the terms of cardinality
\Omega_{n+1}
by interpreting
\Omega^{(i)}
as
\Omega_{n+i+1}
.

We will define two collections of sets
\mathrm{Acc}_{n}\subseteq M_{n}
for
n\in\{-\infty\}\cup\mathbb{N}
.

Definition 3.7
.

We let
M_{-\infty}
be all ordinal terms of formal cardinality
-\infty
. Given
M_{n}
, we let
\mathrm{Acc}_{n}
be the well-founded part of
M_{n}
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, given
\mathrm{Acc}_{i}
for
i<n
, we set
M_{n}
to be the set of ordinal terms
\alpha
with
\overline{\mathbb{FC}}(\alpha)=0
,
\mathbb{G}(\alpha)\geq-n
, and
\{\beta^{*}\mid\beta\in K^{<0}\alpha\}\subseteq\bigcup_{i<n}\mathrm{Acc}_{i}
.

Note that when
\beta\in K^{<0}\alpha
, we always have
\overline{\mathbb{FC}}(\beta)<\overline{\mathbb{FC}}(\alpha)
and therefore
\mathbb{G}(\beta^{*})>\mathbb{G}(\alpha)
.

The construction of
\mathrm{Acc}_{n}
requires an application of
\Pi^{1}_{1}
comprehension using
\bigcup_{i<n}\mathrm{Acc}_{n}
as a parameter. In particular, contructing the whole collection of sets

for all

cannot be done in
\Pi^{1}_{1}\mathchar 45\relax\mathrm{CA}_{0}
.

We proceed first by induction on

\alpha

So let

then either
\gamma\leq\alpha^{\geq 0}_{-(n-m)}

since

is. If

In the remaining case,

If

3.4.
The Path Not Taken

Before proceeding, we briefly discuss an alternative route one might take to develop the syntax of
\mathrm{OT}^{poly}_{\Omega_{\omega}}
, since it sometimes provides useful intuition.

In the alternate version, we avoid indices, having only a single cardinal term
\Omega
. We instead include variables from the beginning, and we adopt the rule that if
\alpha
is an ordinal with free variables
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
then
\vartheta\alpha\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{n}
is an ordinal. The interpretation we have in mind is that this term represents
(\vartheta\alpha)[v_{i}\mapsto\beta_{i}]
.

Since we don’t actually carry out the substitutions, we no longer need to worry about variable clashes—in the term
({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\vartheta}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\Omega}\#v)(\Omega)
, there is no danger of thinking the black
\Omega
is bound, since it is not even inside
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\vartheta}
.

We lose uniqueness, since we should have, for instance,
(\vartheta v)(\alpha\#\omega^{\beta})=(\vartheta v\#\omega^{w})(\alpha,\beta)
, but could easily recover this by adding some appropriate normal form.

If we followed through on this, we would run into the following obstacle—in order to compare
\vartheta\alpha\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{n}
with
\vartheta\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}_{1}\cdots\beta^{\prime}_{n^{\prime}}
, we would like to compare their
uncollapsed
versions. But the uncollapsed version of
\vartheta\alpha\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{n}
cannot be
\alpha[v_{i}\mapsto\beta_{i}]
—this loses the distinction between
\Omega
’s at different levels. Rather, we want
\alpha
to be something like
\lambda v_{1}\cdots v_{n}.\alpha
. But this means, for instance, that
(\lambda v.v)\Omega
is fundamentally different that
\Omega
(because the former collapses to something like
(\vartheta v)\Omega
while the latter collapses to
\vartheta\Omega
). We could accept such issues, but it seems that the solution would end up replicating the counting and shifting we had to deal with above instead of rescuing us from it.

4.
Functions as Cardinals

4.1.

We next describe a system introducing our new device, replacing

with a cardinal-like term

—that is, we will have new terms of the form

. Like

for

\displaystyle-\infty<1<2<\cdots<n<

\displaystyle\cdots<(J-1,0)

\displaystyle<(J-1,1)<

\displaystyle\cdots<(J-1,n)<\cdots<(J-1,\infty)<(J,0)<

\displaystyle\cdots<(J,n)<\cdots<(0,\infty).

Here
-\infty
is the smallest cardinality, for countable ordinals.
n
is the cardinality of
\Omega_{n}
.
(J,0)
is the cardinality of
\Xi^{(J)}
and
(J,n)
is the cardinality of
\Omega^{(J)}_{\Omega+n}
. Nothing sits at the cardinality
(J,\infty)
—rather, it denotes the state in which
\Xi^{(J+1)}
is bound, but no
\Omega_{n}^{(J)}
has been bound yet.

Definition 5.2
.

We define the
formal cardinalities
to the

\{-\infty\}\cup\{n\mid n\in\mathbb{N},n>0\}\cup\{(J,m)\mid J\leq 0,m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}\}

with the ordering described above.

We call a formal cardinality
large
if it has the form
(J,m)
.

We need to define a few operations on formal cardinalities we will use regularly.

Definition 5.3
.

We define
(J^{\prime},n)-J=(J^{\prime}-J,\infty)
,
\min\{(J,m),n\}=(J,\min\{m,n\})
, and
J^{\prime}-(J,n)=J^{\prime}-J
.

Definition 5.4
.

Let
c
be a large formal cardinality and
J
an integer. We define
\alpha^{<c}_{\pm J}
by:

•

\#\{\alpha_{i}\}^{<c}_{\pm J}=\#\{(\alpha_{i})^{<c}_{\pm J}\}
,

•

(\omega^{\alpha})^{<c}_{\pm J}=\omega^{\alpha^{<c}_{\pm J}}
,

•

(\Omega_{n})^{<c}_{\pm J}=\Omega_{n}
,

•

(\Omega^{(J^{\prime})}_{n})^{<c}_{\pm J}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Omega^{(J^{\prime}+J)}_{n}&\text{if }(J^{\prime},n)<c\\
\Omega^{(J^{\prime})}_{n}&\text{if }(J^{\prime},n)\geq c\\
\end{array}\right.
,

•

(\Xi^{(J^{\prime})}(\alpha))^{<c}_{\pm J}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Xi^{(J^{\prime}+J)}(\alpha)&\text{if }(J^{\prime},0)<c\\
\Xi^{(J^{\prime})}(\alpha^{<c-J^{\prime}}_{\pm J})&\text{if }(J^{\prime},0)\geq c\\
\end{array}\right.
,

•

(\vartheta_{\Omega_{n}}\alpha)^{<c}_{\pm J}=\vartheta_{\Omega_{n}}\alpha
,

•

(\vartheta_{\Omega_{\Omega+n}}\alpha)^{<c}_{\pm J}=\vartheta_{\Omega_{\Omega+n}}(\alpha^{<\min\{c,n\}}_{\pm J})
,

•

(\vartheta_{\Xi}\alpha)^{<c}_{\pm J}=\vartheta_{\Xi}(\alpha^{<c-1}_{\pm J})
,

•

(v^{(J^{\prime})})^{<c}_{\pm J}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}v^{(J^{\prime}+J)}&\text{if }(J^{\prime},0)<c\\
v^{(J^{\prime})}&\text{if }(J^{\prime},0)\geq c\\
\end{array}\right.
.

Note that, for this purpose,
v^{(J)}
sits in the same cardinality as
\Xi^{(J)}
.

Next we can define how substituting for the variables
v^{(J)}
works.

Definition 5.5
.

Let
J\leq 0
. We define
\alpha[v\mapsto^{J}\beta]
by induction on
\alpha
:

•

\#\{\alpha_{i}\}[v\mapsto^{J}\beta]=\#\{\alpha_{i}[v\mapsto^{J}\beta]\}
,

•

\omega^{\alpha}[v\mapsto^{J}\beta]=\omega^{\alpha[v\mapsto^{J}\beta]}
,

•

\Omega_{n}[v\mapsto^{J}\beta]=\Omega_{n}
,

•

\Omega^{(J^{\prime})}_{\Omega+n}[v\mapsto^{J}\beta]=\Omega^{(J^{\prime})}_{n}
,

•

\Xi^{(J^{\prime})}(\alpha)[v\mapsto^{J}\beta]=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Xi^{(J^{\prime})}(\alpha[v\mapsto^{J-J^{\prime}}\beta])&\text{if }J\leq J^{\prime}\\
\Xi^{(J^{\prime})}(\alpha)&\text{if }J>J^{\prime}\\
\end{array}\right.

•

(\vartheta_{\Omega_{n}}\alpha)[v\mapsto^{J}\beta]=\vartheta_{\Omega_{n}}\alpha
,

•

(\vartheta_{\Omega_{\Omega+n}}\alpha)[v\mapsto^{J}\beta]=\vartheta_{\Omega_{\Omega+n}}\alpha[v\mapsto^{J}\beta]
,

•

(\vartheta_{\Xi}\alpha)[v\mapsto^{J}\beta]=\vartheta_{\Xi}(\alpha[v\mapsto^{J-1}]\beta)
,

•

w^{(J^{\prime})}[v\mapsto^{J}\beta]=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\beta^{<(0,\infty)}_{+J}&\text{if }v=w\\
w&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.
.

As before, we only want to substitute into
v
when it is at the right level at the moment of substitution.

Definition 5.6
.

We define when
v
is
J
-substitutable in
\alpha
by:

•

v
is
J
-substitutable in
\#\{\alpha_{i}\}
if
v
is
J
-substitutable in each
\alpha_{i}
,

•

v
is
J
-substitutable in
\omega^{\alpha}
if
v
is
J
-substitutable in
\alpha
,

•

v
is always
J
-substitutable in
\Omega_{n}
,

•

v
is always
J
-substitutable in
\Omega^{(J^{\prime})}_{\Omega+n}
,

•

v
is
J
-substitutable in
\Xi^{(J^{\prime})}(\alpha)
if either
v
does not appear in
\alpha
or
J\leq J^{\prime}
and
v
is
J-J^{\prime}
-substitutable in
\alpha
,

•

v
is
J
-substitutable in
(\vartheta_{\Omega_{n}}\alpha)
,

•

v
is
J
-substitutable in
(\vartheta_{\Omega_{\Omega+n}}\alpha)
if
v
is
J
-substitutable in
\alpha
,

•

v
is
J
-substitutable in
(\vartheta_{\Xi}\alpha)
if
v
is
J-1
-substitutable in
\alpha
,

•

v
is
c

w^{(J^{\prime})}
if
v\neq w
or
J=J^{\prime}
.

We next collect up the cardinalities present in an ordinal term.

Definition 5.7
.

Let
c
be a large formal cardinality. We define
\mathbb{FC}^{<c}(\alpha)
inductively by:

•

\mathbb{FC}^{<c}(\#\{\alpha_{i}\})=\bigcup_{i}\mathbb{FC}^{<c}(\alpha_{i})
,

•

\mathbb{FC}^{<c}(\omega^{\alpha})=\mathbb{FC}^{<c}(\alpha)
,

•

\mathbb{FC}^{<c}(\Omega_{n})=\{n\}
,

•

\mathbb{FC}^{<c}(\Omega^{(J^{\prime})}_{\Omega+n})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\{(J^{\prime}-c,n)\}&\text{if }(J^{\prime},n)<c\\
\emptyset&\text{if }(J^{\prime},n)\geq c\end{array}\right.

•

\mathbb{FC}^{<c}(\Xi^{(J^{\prime})}(\alpha))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\{(J^{\prime}-c,0)\}\cup\mathbb{FC}^{<c-J^{\prime}}(\alpha)&\text{if }(J^{\prime},0)<c\\
\mathbb{FC}^{<c-J^{\prime}}(\alpha)&\text{if }(J^{\prime},0)\geq c\end{array}\right.
,

•

\mathbb{FC}^{<c}(\vartheta_{\Omega_{n}}\alpha)=\mathbb{FC}^{<c}\alpha
,

•

\mathbb{FC}^{<c}(\vartheta_{\Omega_{\Omega+n}}\alpha)=\mathbb{FC}^{<\min\{c,n\}}\alpha
,

•

\mathbb{FC}^{<c}(\vartheta_{\Xi}\alpha)=\mathbb{FC}^{<c-1}\alpha
,

•

\mathbb{FC}^{<c}(v^{(J^{\prime})})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\{(J^{\prime}-c,0)\}&\text{if }(J^{\prime},0)<c\\
\emptyset&\text{if }(J^{\prime},0)\geq c\end{array}\right.
.

We define
\overline{\mathbb{FC}}^{<c}(\alpha)=\sup\mathbb{FC}^{<c}(\alpha)
(where
\sup\emptyset=-\infty
).

Definition 5.8
.

We now define the critical subterms for each type of cardinal.

•

K_{n}\#\{\alpha_{i}\}=\bigcup_{i}K_{n}\alpha_{i}
,

•

K_{n}\omega^{\alpha}=K_{n}\alpha
,

•

K_{n}\Omega_{m}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\{\Omega_{m}\}&\text{if }m<n\\
\emptyset&\text{if }m\geq n\end{array}\right.
,

•

K_{n}\Omega^{(J^{\prime})}_{m}=\emptyset
,

•

K_{n}\Xi^{(J^{\prime})}(\alpha)=\emptyset
,

•

K_{n}\vartheta_{\Omega_{m}}\alpha=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\{\vartheta_{\Omega_{m}}\alpha\}&\text{if }m\leq n\\
K_{n}\alpha&\text{if }m>n\\
\end{array}\right.
,

•

K_{n}\vartheta_{\Omega_{\Omega+m}}\alpha=K_{n}\alpha
,

•

K_{n}\vartheta_{\Xi}\alpha=K_{n}\alpha
,

•

K^{<c}_{\Omega+n}v^{(J^{\prime})}=\emptyset
.

•

K^{<c}_{\Omega+n}\#\{\alpha_{i}\}=\bigcup_{i}K^{<c}_{\Omega+n}\alpha_{i}
,

•

K^{<c}_{\Omega+n}\omega^{\alpha}=K^{<c}_{\Omega+n}\alpha
,

•

K^{<c}_{\Omega+n}\Omega_{m}=\{\Omega_{m}\}
,

•

K^{<c}_{\Omega+n}\Omega^{(J^{\prime})}_{m}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\{\Omega^{(J^{\prime}-c)}_{m}\}&\text{if }(J^{\prime},m)<c\\
\emptyset&\text{if }(J^{\prime},m)\geq c\end{array}\right.
,

•

K^{<c}_{\Omega+n}\Xi^{(J^{\prime})}(\alpha)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\{\Xi^{(J^{\prime}-c)}(\alpha)\}&\text{if }(J^{\prime},0)<c\\
K^{<c-J^{\prime}}_{\Omega+n}\alpha&\text{if }(J^{\prime},0)\geq c\end{array}\right.
,

•

K^{<c}_{\Omega+n}\vartheta_{\Omega_{m}}\alpha=\vartheta_{m}\alpha
,

•

K^{<c}_{\Omega+n}\vartheta_{\Omega_{\Omega+m}}\alpha=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\vartheta_{\Omega_{\Omega+m}}\alpha)^{<(0,\infty)}_{+0-c}&\text{if }\overline{\mathbb{FC}}^{<(0,\infty)}(\vartheta_{\Omega_{\Omega+m}}\alpha)<\min\{c,n\}\\
K^{<\min\{c,n\}}_{\Omega+n}\alpha&\text{if }\overline{\mathbb{FC}}^{<(0,\infty)}(\vartheta_{\Omega_{\Omega+m}}\alpha)\geq\min\{c,n\}\end{array}\right.
,

•

K^{<c}_{\Omega+n}\vartheta_{\Xi}\alpha=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\vartheta_{\Xi}\alpha)^{<(0,\infty)}_{+0-c}&\text{if }\overline{\mathbb{FC}}^{<(0,\infty)}(\vartheta_{\Xi}\alpha)<\min\{c,n\}\\
K^{<c-1}_{\Omega}\alpha&\text{if }\overline{\mathbb{FC}}^{<(0,\infty)}(\vartheta_{\Xi}\alpha)\geq\min\{c,n\}\end{array}\right.
,

•

K^{<c}_{\Omega+n}v^{(J^{\prime})}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\{v^{(J^{\prime}-c)}\}&\text{if }(J^{\prime},0)<\min\{c,n\}\\
\emptyset&\text{if }(J^{\prime},0)>\min\{c,n\}\end{array}\right.
.

•

K^{<c}_{\Xi}\#\{\alpha_{i}\}=\bigcup_{i}K^{<c}_{\Xi}\alpha_{i}
,

•

K^{<c}_{\Xi}\omega^{\alpha}=K^{<c}_{\Xi}\alpha
,

•

K^{<c}_{\Xi}\Omega_{n}=\{\Omega_{n}\}
,

•

K^{<c}_{\Xi}\Omega_{m}^{(J^{\prime})}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\{\Omega^{(J^{\prime}-c)}_{m}\}&\text{if }(J^{\prime},m)<c\\
\emptyset&\text{if }(J^{\prime},m)\geq c\end{array}\right.
,

•

K^{<c}_{\Xi}\Xi^{(J^{\prime})}(\alpha)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\{(\Xi^{J^{\prime}}(\alpha))^{<(0,\infty)}_{+0-c}\}&\text{if }(J^{\prime},0)<c\\
K^{<c-J^{\prime}}_{\Xi}\alpha&\text{if }(J^{\prime},0)\geq c\end{array}\right.
,

•

K^{<c}_{\Xi}\vartheta_{\Omega_{n}}\alpha=\{\vartheta_{\Omega_{n}}\alpha\}
,

•

K^{<c}_{\Xi}\vartheta_{\Omega_{\Omega+n}}\alpha=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\vartheta_{\Omega_{\Omega+n}}\alpha)^{<(0,\infty)}_{+0-(c-1)}&\text{if }\overline{\mathbb{FC}}^{<(0,\infty)}(\vartheta_{\Omega_{\Omega+n}}\alpha)<c-1\\
K_{\Xi}^{<\min\{c,n\}}\alpha&\text{if }\overline{\mathbb{FC}}^{<(0,\infty)}(\vartheta_{\Omega_{\Omega+n}}\alpha)\geq c-1\end{array}\right.
,

•

K^{<c}_{\Xi}\vartheta_{\Xi}\alpha=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\overline{(\vartheta_{\Xi}\alpha)^{<(0,\infty)}_{+1}}\right)^{<(0,\infty)}_{+0-c}&\text{if }\overline{\mathbb{FC}}^{<(0,\infty)}(\vartheta_{\Xi}\alpha)<c\\
K_{\Xi}^{<c-1}\alpha&\text{if }\overline{\mathbb{FC}}^{<(0,\infty)}(\vartheta_{\Xi}\alpha)\geq c\end{array}\right.
,

•

K^{<c}_{\Xi}v^{(J^{\prime})}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\{v^{(J^{\prime}-c)}\}&\text{if }(J^{\prime},0)<c\\
\emptyset&\text{if }(J^{\prime},0)\geq c\end{array}\right.
.

Definition 5.9
.

We define the ordering
\alpha<\beta
:

•

\#\{\alpha_{i}\}<\#\{\beta_{j}\}
if there is some
\beta_{j_{0}}\in\{\beta_{j}\}\setminus\{\alpha_{i}\}
such that, for all
\alpha_{i}\in\{\alpha_{i}\}\setminus\{\beta_{j}\}
,
\alpha_{i}<\beta_{j_{0}}
,

•

if
\beta\in\mathbb{H}
then:

–

\beta<_{\kappa}\#\{\alpha_{i}\}
if there is some
i
with
\beta\leq\alpha_{i}
,

–

\#\{\alpha_{i}\}<\beta
if, for all
i
,
\alpha_{i}<\beta
,

•

\omega^{\alpha}<\omega^{\beta}
if
\alpha<\beta
,

•

if
\beta\in\mathbb{SC}
then:

–

\beta<\omega^{\alpha}
if
\beta<\alpha
,

–

\omega^{\alpha}<\beta
if
\alpha\leq\beta
,

•

\Omega_{m}<\Omega^{(J)}_{\Omega+n}
,

•

\Omega_{m}<\Xi^{(J)}(\alpha)
,

•

\Omega^{(J)}_{\Omega+m}<\Omega^{(J^{\prime})}_{\Omega+n}
if
J<J^{\prime}
or
J=J^{\prime}
and
m<n
,

•

\Xi^{(J)}(\alpha)<\Omega^{(J^{\prime})}
if
J\leq J^{\prime}
,

•

\Omega^{(J)}_{\Omega+n}<\Xi^{(J^{\prime})}(\alpha)
if
J<J^{\prime}
,

•

\Xi^{(J)}(\alpha)<\Xi^{(J^{\prime})}(\beta)
if
J<J^{\prime}
or
J=J^{\prime}
and
\alpha<\beta
,

•

if
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
then
\alpha<\vartheta_{\Omega_{n}}\beta
if there is a
\gamma\in K_{n}\beta
with
\alpha\leq\gamma
,

•

if
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
then
\alpha<\vartheta_{\Omega_{\Omega+n}}\beta
if there is a
\gamma\in K^{<(0,n)}_{\Omega+n}\beta
with
\alpha\leq\gamma
,

•

if
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
then
\alpha<\vartheta_{\Xi}\beta
if there is a
\gamma\in K^{<(0,0)}_{\Xi}\beta
with
\alpha\leq\gamma
,

•

to compare
\vartheta_{c}\alpha
and
\vartheta_{c^{\prime}}\beta
we first define the critical sets
C,D
:

–

if
c\neq\Xi
then
C=K^{<(0,c)}_{c}\alpha
,

–

if
c^{\prime}\neq\Xi
then
D=K^{<(0,c)}_{c^{\prime}}\beta
,

–

if
c=\Xi
then
C
is the set of
\gamma[v\mapsto\beta^{\prime}]
such that
\gamma\in K^{<(0,0)}_{\Xi}\alpha
and
\beta^{\prime}
is a parameter of
\beta
,

–

if
c^{\prime}=\Xi
then
D
is the set of
\gamma[v\mapsto\alpha^{\prime}]
such that
\gamma\in K^{<(0,0)}_{\Xi}\beta
and
\alpha^{\prime}
is a parameter of
\alpha
.

we then set
\vartheta_{c}\alpha<\vartheta_{c^{\prime}}\beta
if either

–

there is a
\delta\in D
with
\vartheta_{c}\alpha\leq\delta
,

–

there is no
\delta\in C

\vartheta_{c}\beta\leq\delta
and either
c=c^{\prime}
and
\alpha<\beta
, or
c<c^{\prime}
.

We can now introduce variables at any cardinality—ordinal variables for any
\Omega_{n}
or
\Omega_{\Omega+n}
, or function variables for

Solving nonconvex optimization problems via a second order dynamical system with unbounded damping

Abstract.

In this paper we study a second order dynamical system with variable coefficients in connection to the minimization problem of a smooth nonconvex function. The convergence of the trajectories generated by the dynamical system to a critical point of the objective function is assured, provided a regularization of the objective function satisfies the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz property. We also provide convergence rates for the trajectories generated by the dynamical system, formulated in terms of the Łojasiewicz exponent, and we show that the unbounded damping considered in our dynamical system significantly improves the convergence rates known so far in the literature, that is, instead of linear rates we obtain superlinear rates.

Keywords.

second order dynamical system; unbounded damping; nonconvex optimization; Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz inequality; convergence rate; superlinear rate.

1.
Introduction

Consider the second order dynamical system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{t^{2}}\ddot{x}(t)+\frac{\gamma}{t}\dot{x}(t)+\nabla g(x(t))=0\\
\\
\displaystyle x(t_{0})=u_{0},\,\dot{x}(t_{0})=v_{0},\end{array}\right.

(DS)

in connection to the optimization problem

\ \inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x),

(P)

where
t_{0}>0,

g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a possibly nonconvex Fréchet differentiable function with
L_{g}
-Lipschitz continuous gradient,
u_{0},v_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\gamma,\lambda\in(0,+\infty).

Note that the governing differential equation of (
DS
) can be written as
\ddot{x}(t)+\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}t\dot{x}(t)+\frac{t^{2}}{\lambda}\nabla g(x(t))=0
where the damping
\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}t
is indeed unbounded. Of course, instead of (
DS
) one can consider the dynamical system
\lambda(t)\ddot{x}(t)+\gamma(t)\dot{x}(t)+\nabla g(x(t))=0,\,\displaystyle x(t_{0})=u_{0},\,\dot{x}(t_{0})=v_{0}
provided
\lambda(t)=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{t^{2}}\right)
and
\gamma(t)=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)
as
t\to+\infty
and the analysis presented in the paper remains valid. We emphasize that a particular instance of the latter system can be obtained from the dynamical system studied in
[
9
]
, that is,

\ddot{y}(s)+\gamma\dot{y}(s)+{\nabla}g(y(s))=0,\,y(s_{0})=u_{0},\,\dot{y}(s_{0})=v_{0},\,b>0,

(BBJ)

by using the time rescaling
s=t^{2},\,x(t)=y(s).

Indeed, after some easy computations we obtain

\frac{1}{4t^{2}}\ddot{x}(t)+\left(\frac{\gamma}{2t}-\frac{1}{4t^{3}}\right)\dot{x}(t)+{\nabla}g(x(t))=0,

therefore in some sense (
DS
) and (
BBJ
) seem to be equivalent. However, according to
[
9
]
, (see also
[
14
]
), the trajectories generated by (
BBJ
) converge to a critical point of
g
, provided
g
is of class
C^{2}
and satisfies the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz property, further the desingularizing function
\varphi
satisfies

\varphi(s)\geq c\sqrt{s},\,s\in(0,\eta),

where
c,\eta>0
.

In contrast, in this paper we obtain the convergence of a trajectory generated by (
DS
) to a critical point of
g
, provided the regularization of
g
,

H:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},\,H(u,v)=g(u)+\frac{1}{2}\|u-v\|^{2}

satisfies the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz inequality. Moreover, we will obtain some convergence rates that are better than those obtained in
[
9
]
or
[
14
]
. In particular, in case of strongly convex objective functions, we will obtain superlinear rates instead of linear rates.

Another motivation for the study of the dynamical system (
DS
) arises in the following setting.
Consider the perturbed dynamical system studied in
[
15
]
, that is,

\ddot{x}(t)+\left(\frac{\alpha}{t}+\gamma\right)\dot{x}(t)+\nabla g(x(t))=0,\,x(t_{0})=u_{0},\,\dot{x}(t_{0})=v_{0},

(BCL)

where
u_{0},v_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\alpha,\gamma\in(0,+\infty).
Indeed, (
BCL
) is a perturbed version of the dynamical system considered by Su, Boyd and Candès in
[
25
]
, the latter one can be obtained by taking
\gamma=0
in (
BCL
). However the objective function in
[
25
]
is assumed to be convex, and in this context the dynamical system studied in
[
25
]
can be seen as the continuous counterpart of the celebrated Nesterov accelerated convex gradient method
[
22
]
.

By using the time rescaling
t=s^{2}
and by denoting
y(s)=x\left(s^{2}\right),
the system (
BCL
) can be rewritten as

\frac{1}{4s^{2}}\ddot{y}(s)+\left(\frac{\gamma}{2s}-\frac{2\alpha-1}{4s^{3}}\right)\dot{y}(s)+\nabla g(y(s))=0,\,y(s_{0})=u_{0},\,\dot{y}(s_{0})=2s_{0}v_{0},

where
s_{0}=\sqrt{t_{0}}.
Consequently the dynamical system (
DS
) is also similar to the dynamical system (
BCL
).

According to
[
15
]
, the trajectories generated by (
BCL
) converge to a critical point of
g
, provided the function
g(u)+\frac{1}{2}\|u-v\|^{2},\,u,v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz inequality.

Note that in case
g
is a strongly convex function then according to
[
9
]
and
[
15
]
one has

lim_{t\to+\infty}x(t)=\overline{x}

where
\overline{x}
is a critical point of
g.
Further, it holds the linear rate

\|x(t)-\overline{x}\|=\mathcal{O}(e^{-At})
as
t\to+\infty,
where
A>0.

In contrast, for strongly convex objective functions, in this paper we will obtain the superlinear rate

\|x(t)-\overline{x}\|=\mathcal{O}(e^{-At^{2}})\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty.

The paper is organized as follows. After presenting some preliminary notion and results that we need in order to carry out our analysis, in section 3 we show the existence and uniqueness of the trajectories of the dynamical system (
DS
). We also show that the third order derivative exists almost everywhere. In section 4 we show the convergence of the trajectories generated by the dynamical system (
DS
) to a critical point of our objective function
g
. We also discuss the cases when
g
is semialgebraic or
g
is strongly convex. In section 5 we obtain convergence rates in terms of the Łojasiewicz exponent of the objective function. In particular we show that for strongly convex objective functions the trajectories generated by the dynamical system (
DS
) converge in superlinear rate to a critical point of
g.

2.
Preliminaries

The finite-dimensional spaces considered in the manuscript are endowed with the Euclidean norm topology.
The Fermat rule in this setting reads as: if
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a local minimizer of
g
then
0\in\nabla g(x)
. We denote by

\crit(g)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:0\in{\nabla}g(x)\}

the set of
critical points
of
g
.

Definition 2.1
.

(see, for instance,
[
7
,
1
]
) A function
x:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be locally absolutely continuous, if is absolutely continuous on every interval
[0,T],\,T>0
, that is, one of the
following equivalent properties holds:

(i) there exists an integrable function
y:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

x(t)=x(0)+\int_{0}^{t}y(s)ds\ \ \forall t\in[0,T];

(ii)
x
is continuous and its distributional derivative is Lebesgue integrable on
[0,T]
;

(iii) for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\eta>0
such that for any finite family of intervals
I_{k}=(a_{k},b_{k})\subseteq[0,T]
we have the implication

\left(I_{k}\cap I_{j}=\emptyset\mbox{ and }\sum_{k}|b_{k}-a_{k}|<\eta\right)\Longrightarrow\sum_{k}\|x(b_{k})-x(a_{k})\|<\varepsilon.

Remark 2.2
.

(a) It follows from the definition that an absolutely continuous function is differentiable almost
everywhere, its derivative coincides with its distributional derivative almost everywhere and one can recover the function from its derivative
\dot{x}=y
by the integration formula (i).

(b) If
x:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
(where
T>0
) is absolutely continuous and
B:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
L
-Lipschitz continuous
(where
L\geq 0
), then the function
z=B\circ x
is absolutely continuous, too. This can be easily seen by using the characterization of absolute continuity in
Definition
2.1
(iii). Moreover,
z
is almost everywhere differentiable and the inequality
\|\dot{z}(\cdot)\|\leq L\|\dot{x}(\cdot)\|
holds almost everywhere.

We recall two technical lemmas which will play an essential role in our analysis, (see
[
1
]
Lemma 5.1 and Lemma 5.2, respectively).

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
F:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is locally absolutely continuous and bounded below and that
there exists
G\in L^{1}([0,+\infty))
such that for almost every
t\in[0,+\infty)

\frac{d}{dt}F(t)\leq G(t).

Then there exists
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}F(t)\in\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
1\leq p<\infty
,
1\leq r\leq\infty
,
F:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
is
locally absolutely continuous,
F\in L^{p}([0,+\infty))
,
G:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
G\in L^{r}([0,+\infty))
and
for almost every
t\in[0,+\infty)

\frac{d}{dt}F(t)\leq G(t),

then
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}F(t)=0
.

The convergence of the trajectories generated by the dynamical system (
DS
) will be shown in the framework of the functions satisfying
the
Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz property
. For
\eta\in(0,+\infty]
, we denote by
\Theta_{\eta}

the class of concave and continuous functions
\varphi:[0,\eta)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
such that
\varphi(0)=0
,
\varphi
is
continuously differentiable on
(0,\eta)
, continuous at
0
and
\varphi^{\prime}(s)>0
for all

s\in(0,\eta)
.

Definition 2.5
.

(
Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz property
) Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a differentiable function. We say that
f
satisfies the
Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz (KL) property
at

\overline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

if there exist
\eta\in(0,+\infty]
, a neighborhood
U
of
\overline{x}
and a function
\varphi\in\Theta_{\eta}
such that for all
x
in the
intersection

U\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:f(\overline{x})<f(x)<f(\overline{x})+\eta\}

the following inequality holds

\varphi^{\prime}(f(x)-f(\overline{x}))\|{\nabla}f(x))\|\geq 1.

If
f
satisfies the KL property at each point in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then
f
is called a
KL function
.

The function in the above definition is called a desingularizing function
[
9
]
. The origins of this notion go back to the pioneering work of Łojasiewicz
[
21
]
, where it is proved that for a
real-analytic function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and a critical point
\overline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
(that is
\nabla f(\overline{x})=0
), there exists
\theta\in[1/2,1)
such that the function

|f-f(\overline{x})|^{\theta}\|\nabla f\|^{-1}
is bounded around
\overline{x}
. This corresponds to the situation when

\varphi(s)=C(1-\theta)^{-1}s^{1-\theta}
. The result of Łojasiewicz allows the interpretation of the KL property as a re-parametrization of the function values in order to avoid flatness around the
critical points. Kurdyka
[
17
]
extended this property to differentiable functions definable in an o-minimal structure.
Further extensions to the nonsmooth setting can be found in
[
11
,
5
,
12
,
13
]
.

One of the remarkable properties of the KL functions is their ubiquity in applications, see
[
10
,
15
,
2
,
18
,
19
]
. To the class of KL functions belong semi-algebraic, real sub-analytic, semiconvex, uniformly convex and
convex functions satisfying a growth condition. We refer the reader to

[
11
,
5
,
13
,
10
,
12
,
6
,
4
]
and the references therein for more details regarding all the classes mentioned above and illustrating examples.

An important role in our convergence analysis will be played by the following uniformized KL property given in
[
10
, Lemma 6]
.
We recall that the
distance function

to a set is defined for
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
as
\dist(x,A)=\inf_{y\in A}\|x-y\|
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a compact set and let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a differentiable function. Assume that
f
is constant on
\Omega
and
f
satisfies the KL property at each point of
\Omega
.
Then there exist
\varepsilon,\eta>0
and
\varphi\in\Theta_{\eta}
such that for all
\overline{x}\in\Omega
and for all
x
in the intersection

\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\dist(x,\Omega)<\varepsilon\}\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:f(\overline{x})<f(x)<f(\overline{x})+\eta\}

the following inequality holds

\varphi^{\prime}(f(x)-f(\overline{x}))\|{\nabla}f(x)\|\geq 1.

A related notion that we need is the notion of a Łojasiewicz exponent, which is defined as follows, see
[
5
,
6
,
20
]
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a differentiable function. The function
f
is said to fulfill the Łojasiewicz property, if for every
\overline{x}\in\crit{f}
there exist
K,\epsilon>0
and
\theta\in(0,1)
such that

|f(x)-f(\overline{x})|^{\theta}\leq K\|{\nabla}f(x)\|\mbox{ for every }x\mbox{ fulfilling }\|x-\overline{x}\|<\epsilon.

The number
\theta
is called the Łojasiewicz exponent of
f
at the critical point
\overline{x}.

3.
Existence and uniqueness

Let us specifying which type of solutions are we considering in the analysis of the dynamical system (
DS
).

Definition 3.1
.

We say that
x:[t_{0},+\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a strong global solution of (
DS
), if the
following properties are satisfied:

(i)
x,\dot{x},\ddot{x}:[t_{0},+\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
are locally absolutely continuous, in other words, absolutely continuous on each interval
[0,b]
for
0<b<+\infty
;

(ii)
\frac{\lambda}{t^{2}}\ddot{x}(t)+\frac{\gamma}{t}\dot{x}(t)+\nabla g(x(t))=0
for almost every
t\geq t_{0}
;

(iii)
x(t_{0})=u_{0}
and
\dot{x}(t_{0})=v_{0}
.

For proving existence and uniqueness of the strong global solutions of (
DS
), we use the Cauchy-Lipschitz-Picard Theorem for absolutely continues trajectories (see for example

[
16
, Proposition 6.2.1]
,
[
24
, Theorem 54]
). The key argument is that one can rewrite (
DS
) as a particular first order dynamical system in a suitably chosen product space (see also
[
3
]
).

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume that
\lambda,\gamma>0
. Then, for every starting points
u_{0},v_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
there exists a unique strong global solution
x
of the dynamical system (
DS
).

Proof.

By making use of the notation
X(t)=(x(t),\dot{x}(t))
the system (
DS
) can be rewritten as a first order dynamical system.

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dot{X}(t)=F(t,X(t))\\
X(s_{0})=(u_{0},v_{0}),\end{array}\right.

(3.1)

where
F:[t_{0},+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\,F(t,u,v)=\left(v,-\frac{\gamma t}{\lambda}v-\frac{t^{2}}{\lambda}\nabla g(u)\big)\right).

First we show that
F(t,\cdot,\cdot)
is Lipschitz continuous with a Lipschitz constant
L(t)\in L^{1}_{loc}([t_{0},+\infty))
, for every
t\geq t_{0}
.

Indeed,

\|F(t,u,v)-F(t,\overline{u},\overline{v})\|=\sqrt{\|v-\overline{v}\|^{2}+\left\|\frac{\gamma t}{\lambda}(\overline{v}-v)+\frac{t^{2}}{\lambda}(\nabla g(\overline{u})-\nabla g(u))\right\|^{2}}\leq

\sqrt{\left(1+2\frac{\gamma^{2}t^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\right)\|v-\overline{v}\|^{2}+\frac{2L_{g}^{2}t^{4}}{\lambda^{2}}\|u-\overline{u}\|^{2}}\leq

\sqrt{1+2\frac{\gamma^{2}t^{2}+L_{g}^{2}t^{4}}{\lambda^{2}}}\sqrt{\|v-\overline{v}\|^{2}+\|u-\overline{u}\|^{2}}=

\sqrt{1+2\frac{\gamma^{2}t^{2}+L_{g}^{2}t^{4}}{\lambda^{2}}}\|(u,v)-(\overline{u},\overline{v})\|.

Obviously, the Lipschitz constant
L(t)=\sqrt{1+2\frac{\gamma^{2}t^{2}+L_{g}^{2}t^{4}}{\lambda^{2}}}
is continuous, hence integrable on
[t_{0},b]
for all
0<b<+\infty
, consequently
L(t)\in L^{1}_{loc}([t_{0},+\infty))
.

Next we show that
F(\cdot,u,v)\in L^{1}_{loc}([t_{0},+\infty),{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{n}})
for all
u,v\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}.

Indeed, for
0<b<+\infty
and
u,v\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
fixed one has

\int_{t_{0}}^{b}\|F(t,u,v)\|ds=\int_{t_{0}}^{b}\sqrt{\|v\|^{2}+\left\|\frac{\gamma t}{\lambda}v-\frac{t^{2}}{\lambda}\nabla g(u)\right\|^{2}}dt\leq

\int_{t_{0}}^{b}\sqrt{\left(1+2\frac{\gamma^{2}t^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\right)\|v\|^{2}+2\frac{t^{4}}{\lambda^{2}}\|\nabla g(u)\|^{2}}dt\leq

\sqrt{\|v\|^{2}+\|\nabla g(u)\|^{2}}\int_{t_{0}}^{b}\sqrt{1+2\frac{\gamma^{2}t^{2}+t^{4}}{\lambda^{2}}}dt,

and the conclusion follows by the continuity of
\sqrt{1+2\frac{\gamma^{2}t^{2}+t^{4}}{\lambda^{2}}}.

The conclusion of the theorem follows by applying the Cauchy-Lipschitz-Picard theorem to the first order dynamical system (
3.1
).
∎

Considering again the setting of the proof of Theorem
3.2
, from Remark
2.2
(b)
it follows that
\ddot{X}
exists almost everywhere on
[t_{0},+\infty).
We have the following result.

Lemma 3.3
.

Consider
u_{0},v_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
x
be the unique strong global solution of (
DS
). Then, for almost every
t\in[t_{0},+\infty)
one has:

\displaystyle\|x^{(3)}(t)\|\leq\left(3\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}+\frac{L_{g}t^{2}}{\lambda}\right)\|\dot{x}(t)\|+\left(\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}t+\frac{2}{t}\right)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|.

(3.2)

Proof.

By using the 1-norm of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have,

\|\dot{X}(t+h)-\dot{X}(t)\|=\|F(t+h,X(t+h))-F(t,X(t))\|=

\left\|(\dot{x}(t+h)-\dot{x}(t),-\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}(t+h)\dot{x}(t+h)+\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}t\dot{x}(t)-\frac{(t+h)^{2}}{\lambda}\nabla g(x(t+h))+\frac{t^{2}}{\lambda}\nabla g(x(t)))\right\|=

\|\dot{x}(t+h)-\dot{x}(t)\|+\left\|-\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}(t+h)\dot{x}(t+h)+\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}t\dot{x}(t)-\frac{(t+h)^{2}}{\lambda}\nabla g(x(t+h))+\frac{t^{2}}{\lambda}\nabla g(x(t))\right\|\leq

\|\dot{x}(t+h)-\dot{x}(t)\|+\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}\left\|(t+h)\dot{x}(t+h)-t\dot{x}(t)\right\|+\frac{1}{\lambda}\left\|(t+h)^{2}\nabla g(x(t+h))-t^{2}\nabla g(x(t))\right\|\leq

\|\dot{x}(t+h)-\dot{x}(t)\|+\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}\left\|(t+h)\dot{x}(t+h)-t\dot{x}(t)\right\|+\frac{1}{\lambda}\left\|((t+h)^{2}-t^{2})\nabla g(x(t+h))\right\|+

\frac{t^{2}}{\lambda}\left\|\nabla g(x(t+h))-\nabla g(x(t))\right\|\leq

\|\dot{x}(t+h)-\dot{x}(t)\|+\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}\left\|(t+h)\dot{x}(t+h)-t\dot{x}(t)\right\|+\frac{1}{\lambda}\left\|((t+h)^{2}-t^{2})\nabla g(x(t+h))\right\|+

\frac{L_{g}t^{2}}{\lambda}\left\|x(t+h)-x(t)\right\|.

Hence,

\left\|\frac{\dot{X}(t+h)-\dot{X}(t)}{h}\right\|\leq

\left\|\frac{\dot{x}(t+h)-\dot{x}(t)}{h}\right\|+\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}\left\|\frac{(t+h)\dot{x}(t+h)-t\dot{x}(t)}{h}\right\|+\frac{1}{\lambda}\left\|\frac{(t+h)^{2}-t^{2}}{h}\nabla g(x(t+h))\right\|+

\frac{L_{g}t^{2}}{\lambda}\left\|\frac{x(t+h)-x(t)}{h}\right\|.

By taking the limit
h\longrightarrow 0
we obtain

\|\ddot{X}(t)\|\leq\|\ddot{x}(t)\|+\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}\left\|\left(t\dot{x}(t)\right)^{\prime}\right\|+\frac{2t}{\lambda}\|\nabla g(x(t))\|+\frac{L_{g}t^{2}}{\lambda}\|\dot{x}(t)\|.

Now
\|\ddot{X}(t)\|=\|x^{(3)}(t)\|+\|\ddot{x}(t)\|
and by using (
DS
) we get
\nabla g(x(t))=-\frac{\lambda}{t^{2}}\ddot{x}(t)-\frac{\gamma}{t}\dot{x}(t)
, consequently

\|x^{(3)}(t)\|\leq\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}\left\|\dot{x}(t)+t\ddot{x}(t)\right\|+\frac{2t}{\lambda}\left\|\frac{\lambda}{t^{2}}\ddot{x}(t)+\frac{\gamma}{t}\dot{x}(t)\right\|+\frac{L_{g}t^{2}}{\lambda}\|\dot{x}(t)\|.

Hence,

\|x^{(3)}(t)\|\leq\left(3\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}+\frac{L_{g}t^{2}}{\lambda}\right)\|\dot{x}(t)\|+\left(\frac{2}{t}+\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}t\right)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|.

∎

4.
Convergence of trajectories

In this section we study thee convergence of the trajectories of the dynamical system (
DS
).

The set of limit points of the trajectory
x,
which we denote by
\omega(x)
, is defined as

\omega(x):=\{\overline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\exists t_{k}\longrightarrow\infty\mbox{ such that }x(t_{k})\longrightarrow\overline{x},\,k\longrightarrow+\infty\}.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that
g
is bounded from below and for
u_{0},v_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
let
x
be the unique strong global solution of the dynamical system (
DS
).

Then

(i)

\frac{1}{\sqrt{t^{3}}}\ddot{x}(t),\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\dot{x}(t)\in L^{2}([t_{0},+\infty),\mathbb{R}^{n})
;

(ii)

there exists and is finite the limit
\lim_{t\longrightarrow+\infty}g(x(t))
;

(iii)

\lim_{t\longrightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{t^{2}}\ddot{x}(t)=0,
and
\lim_{t\longrightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\dot{x}(t)=0
;

(iv)

\omega(x)\subseteq\crit(g).

Proof.

Let
T>0.
Since
x,\dot{x},\ddot{x}
are locally absolutely continuous we have
x,\dot{x},\ddot{x}\in L^{2}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{n}).
Further, by the
L_{g}
-Lipschitz property of
{\nabla}g
we have
{\nabla}(g(x))\in L^{2}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Consider
\beta
such that
0<\beta<\min\left(\frac{2\gamma}{3L_{g}},\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda}{L_{g}}}\right).

By using the
L_{g}
Lipschitz continuity of
{\nabla}g
we evaluate:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}g\left(\frac{\beta}{t}\dot{x}(t)+x(t)\right)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{\beta}{t}\ddot{x}(t)+\left(1-\frac{\beta}{t^{2}}\right)\dot{x}(t),{\nabla}g\left(\frac{\beta}{t}\dot{x}(t)+x(t)\right)-{\nabla}g(x(t))\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\langle\frac{\beta}{t}\ddot{x}(t)+\left(1-\frac{\beta}{t^{2}}\right)\dot{x}(t),-\frac{\lambda}{t^{2}}\ddot{x}(s)-\frac{\gamma}{t}\dot{x}(t)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\beta\lambda}{t^{3}}\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2}-\left(\frac{\beta\gamma+\lambda}{t^{2}}-\frac{\beta\lambda}{t^{4}}\right)\langle\ddot{x}(t),\dot{x}(t)\rangle-\left(\frac{\gamma}{t}-\frac{\gamma\beta}{t^{3}}\right)\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle+L_{g}\left\|\frac{\beta}{t}\ddot{x}(t)+\left(1-\frac{\beta}{t^{2}}\right)\dot{x}(t)\right\|\left\|\frac{\beta}{t}\dot{x}(t)\right\|.

Further by using the inequality
\|\dot{x}(t)\|\|\ddot{x}(t)\|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}+\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2}\right)
we have:

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\beta}{t}\ddot{x}(t)+\left(1-\frac{\beta}{t^{2}}\right)\dot{x}(t)\right\|\left\|\frac{\beta}{t}\dot{x}(t)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{\beta}{t}\ddot{x}(t)\right\|\left\|\frac{\beta}{t}\dot{x}(t)\right\|+\left|1-\frac{\beta}{t^{2}}\right|\frac{\beta}{t}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sqrt{\left(\frac{\beta}{t}\right)^{3}}\ddot{x}(t)\right\|\left\|\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{t}}\dot{x}(t)\right\|+\left|1-\frac{\beta}{t^{2}}\right|\frac{\beta}{t}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\left(\frac{\beta}{t}\right)^{3}\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{\beta}{t}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}\right)+\left|1-\frac{\beta}{t^{2}}\right|\frac{\beta}{t}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}.

On the other hand

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{\beta\gamma+\lambda}{t^{2}}-\frac{\beta\lambda}{t^{4}}\right)\langle\ddot{x}(t),\dot{x}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\left(\frac{\beta\gamma+\lambda}{t^{2}}-\frac{\beta\lambda}{t^{4}}\right)\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{\lambda+\beta\gamma}{t^{3}}-\frac{2\beta\lambda}{t^{5}}\right)\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}.

Consequently, one has:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(g\left(\frac{\beta}{t}\dot{x}(t)+x(t)\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\beta\gamma+\lambda}{t^{2}}-\frac{\beta\lambda}{t^{4}}\right)\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}\right)\leq

(4.1)

\left(-\frac{\beta\lambda}{t^{3}}+\frac{L_{g}\beta^{3}}{2t^{3}}\right)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2}+\left(-\frac{\gamma}{t}+\frac{L_{g}\beta}{2t}+L_{g}\left|1-\frac{\beta}{t^{2}}\right|\frac{\beta}{t}-\frac{\lambda}{t^{3}}+\frac{2\beta\lambda}{t^{5}}\right)\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}.

Let us denote

A(t)=-\frac{\beta\lambda}{t^{3}}+\frac{L_{g}\beta^{3}}{2t^{3}}

and

B(t)=-\frac{\gamma}{t}+\frac{L_{g}\beta}{2t}+L_{g}\left|1-\frac{\beta}{t^{2}}\right|\frac{\beta}{t}-\frac{\lambda}{t^{3}}+\frac{2\beta\lambda}{t^{5}}.

Since
0<\beta<\min\left(\frac{2\gamma}{3L_{g}},\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda}{L_{g}}}\right)
we have that there exists
t_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
such that for all
t>t_{1}
one has

\frac{\beta\gamma+\lambda}{t^{2}}-\frac{\beta\lambda}{t^{4}}>0,\,A(t)<0\mbox{ and }B(t)<0.

Now by integrating (
4.1
) on
[t_{1},T]
one obtains:

\displaystyle g\left(\frac{\beta}{T}\dot{x}(T)+x(T)\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\beta\gamma+\lambda}{T^{2}}-\frac{\beta\lambda}{T^{4}}\right)\|\dot{x}(T)\|^{2}+\int_{t_{1}}^{T}-A(t)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2}dt+\int_{t_{1}}^{T}-B(t)\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}dt\leq

(4.2)

g\left(\frac{\beta}{t_{1}}\dot{x}(t_{1})+x(t_{1})\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\beta\gamma+\lambda}{t_{1}^{2}}-\frac{\beta\lambda}{t_{1}^{4}}\right)\|\dot{x}(t_{1})\|^{2}.

Taking into account that
g
is bounded from bellow, by taking the limit as
T\longrightarrow+\infty
we obtain, that

\int_{t_{1}}^{\infty}-A(t)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2}dt<+\infty

and

\int_{t_{1}}^{\infty}-B(t)\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}dt<+\infty.

Consequently

-A(t)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2},\,-B(t)\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}\in L^{1}([t_{0},+\infty),\mathbb{R})
, that is

\sqrt{|A(t)|}\ddot{x}(t),\,\sqrt{|B(t)|}\dot{x}(t)\in L^{2}([t_{0},+\infty),\mathbb{R}^{n}).

In other words,

\frac{1}{\sqrt{t^{3}}}\ddot{x}(t),\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\dot{x}(s)\in L^{2}([t_{0},+\infty),\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Further, (
4.1
) and Lemma
2.3
assure that there exists and is finite the limit

\displaystyle\lim_{t\longrightarrow+\infty}\left(g\left(\frac{\beta}{t}\dot{x}(t)+x(t)\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\beta\gamma+\lambda}{t^{2}}-\frac{\beta\lambda}{t^{4}}\right)\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}\right).

(4.3)

We show next that
\lim_{t\longrightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\dot{x}(t)=0.

We have:

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{t^{2}}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}\right)=-\frac{2}{t^{3}}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{2}{t^{2}}\langle\ddot{x}(t),\dot{x}(t)\rangle=-\frac{2}{t^{3}}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}+2\left\langle\frac{1}{\sqrt{t^{3}}}\ddot{x}(t),\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\dot{x}(t)\right\rangle\leq

-\frac{2}{t^{3}}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{t^{3}}\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{t}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}\in L^{1}([t_{0},+\infty)).

The conclusion follows by Lemma
2.4
.

Similarly, we show that
\lim_{t\longrightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{t^{2}}\ddot{x}(t)=0.

We have:

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{t^{4}}\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2}\right)=-\frac{4}{t^{5}}\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{2}{t^{4}}\langle{x}^{(3)}(t),\ddot{x}(t)\rangle\leq-\frac{4}{t^{5}}\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{2}{t^{4}}\|x^{(3)}(t)\|\|\ddot{x}(t)\|.

Now according to Lemma
3.3
we have

\|x^{(3)}(t)\|\leq\left(3\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}+\frac{L_{g}t^{2}}{\lambda}\right)\|\dot{x}(t)\|+\left(\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}t+\frac{2}{t}\right)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|,

hence,

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{t^{4}}\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2}\right)\leq-\frac{4}{t^{5}}\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2}+\left(6\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}\frac{1}{t^{4}}+\frac{2L_{g}}{\lambda}\frac{1}{t^{2}}\right)\|\dot{x}(t)\|\|\ddot{x}(t)\|+\left(\frac{2\gamma}{\lambda}\frac{1}{t^{3}}+\frac{4}{t^{5}}\right)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2}\leq

\frac{2\gamma}{\lambda}\frac{1}{t^{3}}\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2}+3\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}\frac{1}{t^{4}}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}+3\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}\frac{1}{t^{4}}\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{2L_{g}}{\lambda}\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\dot{x}(t)\right\|\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{t^{3}}}\ddot{x}(t)\right\|\leq

\frac{2\gamma}{\lambda}\frac{1}{t^{3}}\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2}+3\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}\frac{1}{t^{4}}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}+3\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}\frac{1}{t^{4}}\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{L_{g}}{\lambda}\left(\frac{1}{t}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{t^{3}}\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2}\right)\in L^{1}([t_{0},+\infty)).

The conclusion follows by Lemma
2.4
.

Finally, by using the continuity of
g
and the fact that
\lim_{t\longrightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\dot{x}(t)=0,
(
4.3
) becomes:

\exists\lim_{t\longrightarrow+\infty}\left(g\left(\frac{\beta}{t}\dot{x}(t)+x(t)\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\beta\gamma+\lambda}{t^{2}}-\frac{\beta\lambda}{t^{4}}\right)\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}\right)=\lim_{t\longrightarrow+\infty}g(x(t))\in\mathbb{R}.

(4.4)

Let
\overline{x}\in\omega(x).
Then, there exists a sequence
t_{k}\longrightarrow+\infty,\,k\longrightarrow+\infty
such that
x(t_{k})\longrightarrow\overline{x},\,k\longrightarrow+\infty.

Hence, by using the continuity of
{\nabla}g
and the dynamical system (
DS
), we have

0=\lim_{k\longrightarrow+\infty}\left(\frac{\lambda}{t_{k}^{2}}\ddot{x}(t_{k})+\frac{\gamma}{t_{k}}\dot{x}(t_{k})+{\nabla}g(x(t_{k}))\right)={\nabla}g(\overline{x}),

which shows that
\overline{x}\in\crit(g).

∎

The following result is an easy consequence of Theorem
4.1
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that
g
is bounded from below and for
u_{0},v_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
let
x
be the unique strong global solution of the dynamical system (
DS
). Consider the function

H:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},\,H(x,y)=g(x)+\frac{1}{2}\|x-y\|^{2}.

Consider
0<\beta<\min\left(\frac{2\gamma}{3L_{g}},\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda}{L_{g}}}\right).
For simplicity, for
t\geq t_{1}
, where
t_{1}
was defined in Theorem
4.1
, let us denote

u(t)=\frac{\beta}{t}\dot{x}(t)+x(t)\mbox{ and }v(t)=\left(\sqrt{\frac{\beta\gamma+\lambda}{t^{2}}-\frac{\beta\lambda}{t^{4}}}+\frac{\beta}{t}\right)\dot{x}(t)+x(t).

Then

(i)

\omega(u)=\omega(v)=\omega(x);

(ii)

\frac{d}{dt}H(u(t),v(t))\leq A(t)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2}+B(t)\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}\leq 0,\,\mbox{ for all }t\geq t_{1};

(iii)

there exists the limit
\lim_{t\longrightarrow+\infty}H(u(t),v(t))=\lim_{t\longrightarrow+\infty}g(x(t))\in\mathbb{R};

(iv)

H
is finite and constant on
\omega(u,v)=\{(\overline{x},\overline{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:\overline{x}\in\omega(x)\};

(v)

\|\nabla H(u(t),v(t))\|\leq q(t)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|+p(t)\|\dot{x}(t)\|,\,\,\mbox{ for all }t\geq t_{1};

(vi)

\omega(u,v)\subseteq\crit(H),

where

A(t)=-\frac{\beta\lambda}{t^{3}}+\frac{L_{g}\beta^{3}}{2t^{3}},\,B(t)=-\frac{\gamma}{t}+\frac{L_{g}\beta}{2t}+L_{g}\left|1-\frac{\beta}{t^{2}}\right|\frac{\beta}{t}-\frac{\lambda}{t^{3}}+\frac{2\beta\lambda}{t^{5}},

q(t)=\frac{\lambda}{t^{2}}\mbox{ and }p(t)=\frac{L_{g}\beta}{t}+\frac{\gamma}{t}+2\sqrt{\frac{\beta\gamma+\lambda}{t^{2}}-\frac{\beta\lambda}{t^{4}}}.

Assume further that
x
is bounded. Then

(vii)

\omega(u,v)
is nonempty and compact;

(viii)

\lim_{t\longrightarrow+\infty}\dist((u(t),v(t)),\omega(u,v))=0.

Proof.

(i) According to Theorem
4.1
(i)

\lim_{t\longrightarrow+\infty}\frac{\beta}{t}\dot{x}(t)=\lim_{t\longrightarrow+\infty}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\beta\gamma+\lambda}{t^{2}}-\frac{\beta\lambda}{t^{4}}}+\frac{\beta}{t}\right)\dot{x}(t)=0,

hence

\omega(u)=\omega(v)=\omega(x).

(ii) and (iii) are nothing else than (
4.1
) and (
4.4
).

(iv) Consider
(\overline{x},\overline{y})\in\omega(u,v)
. Then, there exists a sequence
t_{k}\longrightarrow+\infty,\,k\longrightarrow+\infty
such that
u(t_{k})\longrightarrow\overline{x},\,k\longrightarrow+\infty\mbox{ and }v(t_{k})\longrightarrow\overline{y},\,k\longrightarrow+\infty.
But according to Theorem
4.1
(i) and the form of
u
and
v
we get that
\overline{x}=\overline{y}
and
\overline{x}\in\omega(x).
Consequently,
\omega(u,v)=\{(\overline{x},\overline{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:\overline{x}\in\omega(x)\}.

Now, let
(\overline{x},\overline{x})\in\omega(u,v)
. Then, there exists a sequence
t_{k}\longrightarrow+\infty,\,k\longrightarrow+\infty
such that
u(t_{k})\longrightarrow\overline{x},\,k\longrightarrow+\infty\mbox{ and }v(t_{k})\longrightarrow\overline{x},\,k\longrightarrow+\infty.

But according to (iii)
\lim_{t\longrightarrow+\infty}H(u(t),v(t))=l\in\mathbb{R}
, hence
H(\overline{x},\overline{x})=\lim_{t_{k}\longrightarrow+\infty}H(u(t_{k}),v(t_{k}))=\lim_{t\longrightarrow+\infty}H(u(t),v(t))=l.
Consequently,
H
is constant on
\omega(u,v).

(v) Since
\nabla H(x,y)=(\nabla g(x)+x-y,y-x)
by using (
DS
), we have

\|\nabla H(u(t),v(t))\|\leq\|\nabla g(u(t))\|+2\|u(t)-v(t)\|\leq\|\nabla g(u(t))-\nabla g(x(t))\|+\|\nabla g(x(t))\|+2\|u(t)-v(t)\|\leq

\frac{L_{g}\beta}{t}\|\dot{x}(t)\|+\left\|-\frac{\lambda}{t^{2}}\ddot{x}(t)-\frac{\gamma}{t}\dot{x}(t)\right\|+2\|u(t)-v(t)\|\leq

\frac{\lambda}{t^{2}}\|\ddot{x}(t)\|+\left(\frac{L_{g}\beta}{t}+\frac{\gamma}{t}+2\sqrt{\frac{\beta\gamma+\lambda}{t^{2}}-\frac{\beta\lambda}{t^{4}}}\right)\|\dot{x}(t)=

q(t)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|+p(t)\|\dot{x}(t)\|.

(vi) Since
\crit{H}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:(0,0)\in{\nabla}H(x,y)\}=\{(\overline{x},\overline{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:\overline{x}\in\crit(g)\},
and
according to (i)
\omega(u,v)=\{(\overline{x},\overline{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:\overline{x}\in\omega(x)\}
by Theorem
4.1
(iv), one has:

\omega(u,v)\subseteq\crit(H).

(vii) Since
x
is bounded and
\frac{1}{t}\dot{x}(t)\longrightarrow 0,\,t\longrightarrow+\infty
we obtain that
u
and
v
are bounded, respectively. Hence from Bolzano-Weierstrass Theorem one obtains that the set of limit points
\omega(u,v)
is nonempty. Moreover

\omega(u,v)=\{(\overline{x},\overline{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:\overline{x}\in\omega(x)\}
, and
\omega(x)
is bounded, thus it is enough to show that
\omega(x)
is closed.

Consider
(\overline{x}_{n})_{n\geq 1}\subseteq\omega(x)
and assume that
\lim_{n\longrightarrow+\infty}\overline{x}_{n}=x^{*}.
We show that
x^{*}\in\omega(x).

Obviously, for every
n\geq 1
there exits the sequence
t_{k}^{n}\longrightarrow+\infty,\,k\longrightarrow+\infty
such that

\lim_{k\longrightarrow+\infty}x(t_{k}^{n})=\overline{x}_{n}.

Further,
\lim_{n\longrightarrow+\infty}\overline{x}_{n}=x^{*},
hence for every
\epsilon>0
there exists
N_{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n\geq N_{\epsilon}
one has

\|\overline{x}_{n}-x^{*}\|<\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

Let us fix
\epsilon>0
and let
n\geq 1
fixed. Since
\lim_{k\longrightarrow+\infty}x(t_{k}^{n})=\overline{x}_{n},
there exists
k(n,\epsilon)\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
k\geq k(n,\epsilon)
one has

\|x(t_{k}^{n})-\overline{x}_{n}\|<\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

The last two relations lead to

\|x(t_{k}^{n})-x^{*}\|<\epsilon,\,\forall n\geq N_{\epsilon},\,k\geq k(n,\epsilon).

Since
t_{k}^{n}\longrightarrow+\infty,\,k\longrightarrow+\infty
for all
n\geq 1
, we obtain that for every
n\geq 1
there exists
N_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
t_{k}^{n}>n
for all
k\geq N_{n}.
For every
n\geq 1
consider now
k_{n}>\max\{n,N_{n},k(n,\epsilon)\}
and the sequence
s_{n}=t_{k_{n}}^{n}.
Obviously
s_{n}\longrightarrow+\infty,\,n\longrightarrow+\infty
and

\|x(s_{n})-x^{*}\|<\epsilon,\,\forall n\geq N_{\epsilon}.
Hence

\lim_{n\longrightarrow+\infty}x(s_{n})=x^{*}\mbox{ and }s_{n}\longrightarrow+\infty,\,n\longrightarrow+\infty,

in other words
x^{*}\in\omega(x).

(viii) We have

0\leq\lim_{t\longrightarrow+\infty}\dist((u(t),v(t)),\omega(u,v))\leq\lim_{k\longrightarrow+\infty}\dist((u(t_{k}),v(t_{k})),\omega(u,v))=0.

∎

Remark 4.3
.

Combining (iii) and (iv) in Lemma
4.2
, one obtains that for every
\overline{x}\in\omega(x)
and
t_{k}\longrightarrow+\infty
such that
x(t_{k})\longrightarrow\overline{x}
as
k\longrightarrow+\infty
we have

\lim_{k\longrightarrow+\infty}H(u(t_{k}),v(t_{k}))=H(\overline{x},\overline{x}).

The following result is the main result of the paper.

Theorem 4.4
.

Assume that
g
is bounded from below and for
u_{0},v_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
x
be the unique strong global solution of (
DS
). Consider the function

H:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},\,H(x,y)=g(x)+\frac{1}{2}\|x-y\|^{2}.

Suppose that
x
is bounded and
H
is a KL function. Then the following statements are true

(a)

\dot{x}(t)\in L^{1}([t_{0},+\infty),\mathbb{R}^{n});

(b)

\frac{1}{t}\ddot{x}(t)\in L^{1}([t_{0},+\infty),\mathbb{R}^{n});

(c)

there exists
\overline{x}\in\crit(g)
such that
\lim_{t\longrightarrow+\infty}x(t)=\overline{x}.

Proof.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
4.2
, consider
(\overline{x},\overline{x})\in\omega(u,v).
Then

\lim_{t\longrightarrow+\infty}H(u(t),v(t))=H(\overline{x},\overline{x}).

Case I.
There exists
\overline{t}\geq t_{1}
such that
H(u(\overline{t}),v(\overline{t}))=H(\overline{x},\overline{x}).
From Lemma
4.2
(ii) we have

\frac{d}{dt}H(u(t),v(t))\leq A(t)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2}+B(t)\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}\leq 0,\,\mbox{ for all }t\geq t_{1},

hence,

H(u(t),v(t))\leq H(\overline{x},\overline{x}),\,\mbox{ for all }t\geq\overline{t}.

On the other hand

H(u(t),v(t))\geq\lim_{t\longrightarrow+\infty}H(u(t),v(t))=H(\overline{x},\overline{x}),\,\mbox{ for all }t\geq t_{1},

hence

H(u(t),v(t))=H(\overline{x},\overline{x}),\,\mbox{ for all }t\geq\overline{t}.

But then,
H(u(t),v(t))
is constant, hence
\frac{d}{dt}H(u(t),v(t))=0,\,\mbox{ for all }t\geq\overline{t},
which leads to

0\leq A(t)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2}+B(t)\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}\leq 0,\,\mbox{ for all }t\geq\overline{t}.

Since
A(t)<0
and
B(t)<0
for every
t\geq t_{1}
, the latter inequality can hold only if

\dot{x}(t)=\ddot{x}(t)=0,\mbox{ for all }t\geq\overline{t}.

Consequently,
\dot{x},\ddot{x}\in L^{1}([t_{0},+\infty),\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
x(t)=\overline{x}
is constant on
[\overline{t},+\infty).

Case II.
For every
t\geq t_{1}
one has that
H(u(t),v(t))>H(\overline{x},\overline{x}).

Let
\Omega=\omega(u,v).
Then according to Lemma
4.2
,
\Omega
is nonempty and compact and
H
is constant on
\Omega.

Since
H
is KL, according to Lemma
2.6
there exist
\varepsilon,\eta>0
and
\varphi\in\Theta_{\eta}
such that for all
(\overline{x},\overline{x})\in\Omega
and for all
(z,w)
in the intersection

\{(z,w)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:\dist((z,w),\Omega)<\varepsilon\}\cap\{(z,w)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:H(\overline{x},\overline{x})<H(z,w)<H(\overline{x},\overline{x})+\eta\}

one has

\varphi^{\prime}(H(z,w)-H(\overline{x},\overline{x}))\|{\nabla}H(z,w)\|\geq 1.

According to Lemma
4.2
(viii),
\lim_{t\longrightarrow+\infty}\dist((u(t),v(t)),\Omega)=0
, hence there exists
t_{2}\geq 0
such that

\dist((u(t),v(t)),\Omega)<\epsilon,\,\forall t\geq t_{2}.

Since

\lim_{t\longrightarrow+\infty}\left(H(u(t),v(t))\right)=H(\overline{x},\overline{x})

and

H(u(t),v(t))>H(\overline{x},\overline{x}),

there exists
t_{3}>t_{1}
such that

H(\overline{x},\overline{x})<H(u(t),v(t))<H(\overline{x},\overline{x})+\eta,\,\forall t\geq t_{3}.

Hence, for all
t\geq T=\max(t_{2},t_{3})
we have

\varphi^{\prime}(H(u(t),v(t))-H(\overline{x},\overline{x}))\cdot\|{\nabla}H(u(t),v(t))\|\geq 1.

According to Lemma
4.2
(ii) and (v), we have
\frac{d}{dt}H(u(t),v(t))\leq A(t)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2}+B(t)\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}\leq 0

and
\|\nabla H(u(t),v(t))\|\leq q(t)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|+p(t)\|\dot{x}(t)\|,
hence,

\frac{d}{dt}\varphi(H(u(t),v(t))-H(\overline{x},\overline{x}))=\varphi^{\prime}(H(u(t),v(t))-H(\overline{x},\overline{x}))\frac{d}{dt}H(u(t),v(t))\leq\frac{A(t)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2}+B(t)\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}}{q(t)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|+p(t)\|\dot{x}(t)\|}

for all
t\in[T,+\infty).

By integrating on the interval
[T,\overline{t}],\,\overline{t}>T
we obtain

\varphi(H(u(\overline{t}),v(\overline{t}))-H(\overline{x},\overline{x}))-\int_{T}^{\overline{t}}\frac{A(s)\|\ddot{x}(s)\|^{2}+B(s)\|\dot{x}(s)\|^{2}}{q(s)\|\ddot{x}(s)\|+p(s)\|\dot{x}(s)\|}ds\leq\varphi(H(u(T),v(T))-H(\overline{x},\overline{x})).

Since
\varphi
is bounded from below,
A(s)<0,B(s)<0,p(s)>0,q(s)>0
for all
s\geq T
and
\overline{t}
was arbitrary chosen, we obtain that

0\leq\int_{T}^{+\infty}\frac{-A(s)\|\ddot{x}(s)\|^{2}-B(s)\|\dot{x}(s)\|^{2}}{q(s)\|\ddot{x}(s)\|+p(s)\|\dot{x}(s)\|}ds\leq\varphi(H(u(T),v(T))-H(\overline{x},\overline{x}))

which leads to

\frac{\frac{1}{t^{3}}\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2}}{q(t)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|+p(t)\|\dot{x}(t)\|},\frac{\frac{1}{t}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}}{q(t)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|+p(t)\|\dot{x}(t)\|}\in L^{1}([t_{0},+\infty),\mathbb{R}^{n}).

By using the arithmetical-geometrical mean inequality we have

\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\frac{1}{t^{3}}\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2}}{q(t)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|+p(t)\|\dot{x}(t)\|}+\frac{\frac{1}{t}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}}{q(t)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|+p(t)\|\dot{x}(t)\|}\right)\geq\frac{\frac{1}{t^{2}}\|\ddot{x}(t)\|\|\dot{x}(t)\|}{q(t)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|+p(t)\|\dot{x}(t)\|},

hence,

\frac{\frac{1}{t^{2}}\|\ddot{x}(t)\|\|\dot{x}(t)\|}{q(t)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|+p(t)\|\dot{x}(t)\|}\in L^{1}([t_{0},+\infty),\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Since
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{q(t)}{t}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{t^{3}}\right),\,\mathcal{O}(p(t))=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{t}\right),
and

\mathcal{O}\left(q(t)+\frac{p(t)}{t}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{t^{2}}\right),
one has

\frac{\frac{q(t)}{t}\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2}}{q(t)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|+p(t)\|\dot{x}(t)\|}+\frac{p(t)\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}}{q(t)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|+p(t)\|\dot{x}(t)\|}+\frac{\left(q(t)+\frac{p(t)}{t}\right)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|\|\dot{x}(t)\|}{q(t)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|+p(t)\|\dot{x}(t)\|}=

\|\dot{x}(t)\|+\frac{1}{t}\|\ddot{x}(t)\|,

which shows that

\dot{x}(t),\frac{1}{t}\ddot{x}(t)\in L^{1}([t_{0},+\infty),\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Now, since
\dot{x}(t)\in L^{1}([t_{0},+\infty),\mathbb{R}^{n})
, it follows that there exists and is finite the limit
\lim_{t\longrightarrow+\infty}x(t).
But, obviously, then one must have

\lim_{t\longrightarrow+\infty}x(t)=\overline{x}.

∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Assume that
g
is semi-algebraic and bounded from below and for
u_{0},v_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
x
be the unique strong global solution of (
DS
). If
x
is bounded then all the conclusions of Theorem
4.4
hold.

Proof.

We need only to prove that
H
is a KL function. Since the class of semi-algebraic functions is closed under addition (see for example
[
10
]
) and

(x,y)\mapsto\frac{1}{2}\|x-y\|^{2}
is semi-algebraic, we obtain that the function

H:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\},\,H(x,y)=g(x)+\frac{1}{2}\|y-x\|^{2}

is semi-algebraic. Consequently,
H
is a KL function.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

Note that in the hypotheses of Theorem
4.4
one need to assume that the trajectory
x
generated by (
DS
) is bounded and
g
is bounded from below.

In order to validate these assumptions it is enough to assume that
g
is coercive,
that is

\lim_{\|u\|\rightarrow+\infty}(g)(u)=+\infty.

Indeed, notice that
g
is bounded from below, being a continuous and coercive function (see for example
[
23
]
). From (
4.2
) it follows that
\frac{\beta}{T}\dot{x}(T)+x(T)
is contained for every
T\geq t_{1}
in a lower level set
of
g
, which is a bounded set due to the coercivity assumption. But, according to Theorem
4.1
,
\frac{\beta}{t}\dot{x}(t)\longrightarrow 0,\,t\longrightarrow+\infty
, hence one can easily deduce that
x
is bounded.

5.
Convergence rates

In the context of optimization problems involving KL functions, it is known (see
[
21
,
11
,
4
]
)
that convergence rates of the trajectory can be formulated in terms of the Łojasiewicz exponent.

In the following theorem we obtain convergence rates for the trajectory generated by (
DS
).

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume that
g
is bounded from below and for
u_{0},v_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
x
be the unique strong global solution of (
DS
). Consider the function

H:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},\,H(x,y)=g(x)+\frac{1}{2}\|x-y\|^{2}.

Suppose that
x
is bounded and let
\overline{x}\in\crit(g)
be such that
\lim_{t\longrightarrow+\infty}x(t)=\overline{x}
and
H
fulfills the Łojasiewicz property at
(\overline{x},\overline{x})\in\crit H
with Łojasiewicz exponent
\theta
.

Then, there exist
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}>0
and
t^{\prime}>0
such that for every
t\in[t^{\prime},+\infty)
the following statements are true

(a)

if
\theta\in(0,\frac{1}{2}),
then
x
converges in finite time;

(b)

if
\theta=\frac{1}{2},
then
\|x(t)-\overline{x}\|\leq a_{1}e^{-a_{2}t^{2}}
;

(c)

if
\theta\in(\frac{1}{2},1),
then
\|x(t)-\overline{x}\|\leq(a_{3}t^{2}+a_{4})^{-\frac{1-\theta}{2\theta-1}}.

Proof.

We define for every
t\in[t_{1},+\infty)
, (
t_{1}
is defined in the proof of Theorem
4.1
),

\sigma(t):=\int_{t}^{+\infty}\left(\|\dot{x}(s)\|+\frac{1}{s}\|\ddot{x}(s)\|\right)ds.

Let
t\in[t_{1},+\infty)
be fixed. For
T\geq t
we have

\|x(t)-\overline{x}\|=\left\|x(T)-\overline{x}-\int_{t}^{T}\dot{x}(s)ds\right\|\leq\|x(T)-\overline{x}\|+\int_{t}^{T}\|\dot{x}(s)\|ds.

By taking the limit as
T\longrightarrow+\infty
we obtain

\|x(t)-\overline{x}\|\leq\int_{t}^{+\infty}\|\dot{x}(s)\|ds\leq\sigma(t).

(5.1)

We show next, that there exists
m<0
such that

\frac{A(t)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2}+B(t)\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}}{q(t)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|+p(t)\|\dot{x}(t)\|}\leq m\left(\|\dot{x}(t)\|+\frac{1}{t}\|\ddot{x}(t)\|\right).

(5.2)

Indeed,

(q(t)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|+p(t)\|\dot{x}(t)\|)\left(\|\dot{x}(t)\|+\frac{1}{t}\|\ddot{x}(t)\|\right)=\frac{q(t)}{t}\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2}+\left(q(t)+\frac{p(t)}{t}\right)\|\dot{x}(t)\|\|\ddot{x}(t)\|+p(t)\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}=

\frac{q(t)}{t}\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2}+\left(tq(t)+p(t)\right)\|\dot{x}(t)\|\left\|\frac{1}{t}\ddot{x}(t)\right\|+p(t)\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}\leq

\left(\frac{q(t)}{t}+\frac{tq(t)+p(t)}{2t^{2}}\right)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2}+\left(p(t)+\frac{tq(t)+p(t)}{2}\right)\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}=

\left(\frac{3}{2}\frac{q(t)}{t}+\frac{p(t)}{2t^{2}}\right)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2}+\left(\frac{3}{2}p(t)+\frac{tq(t)}{2}\right)\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}\leq

\frac{A(t)}{m}\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{B(t)}{m}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2},

where

m=\max\left(\max_{t\geq t_{1}}\frac{A(t)}{\frac{3}{2}\frac{q(t)}{t}+\frac{p(t)}{2t^{2}}},\max_{t\geq t_{1}}\frac{B(t)}{\frac{3}{2}p(t)+\frac{tq(t)}{2}}\right).

It is an easy verification that
m<0.

As we have seen in the proof of Theorem
4.4
if there exists
\overline{t}\geq t_{1}
such that
H(u(\overline{t}),v(\overline{t}))=H(\overline{x},\overline{x})
then
x
is constant on
[\overline{t},+\infty),
therefore the conclusion follows.

On the other hand, if for every
t\geq t_{1}
one has that
H(u(t),v(t))>H(\overline{x},\overline{x})
then according to the proof of Theorem
4.1
(i) and the fact that
\lim_{t\longrightarrow+\infty}x(t)=\overline{x}
, there exists
\epsilon>0
and
t^{\prime}\geq t_{1}
such that

\|(u(t),v(t))-(\overline{x},\overline{x})\|<\epsilon.

Further, according to the proof of Theorem
4.4
one has

K\frac{d}{dt}(H(u(t),v(t))-H(\overline{x},\overline{x}))^{1-\theta}\leq\frac{A(t)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|^{2}+B(t)\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}}{q(t)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|+p(t)\|\dot{x}(t)\|},\mbox{ for all }t\geq t^{\prime}.

Now by using (
5.2
) we obtain that

M\left(\|\dot{x}(t)\|+\frac{1}{t}\|\ddot{x}(t)\|\right)+\frac{d}{dt}(H(u(t),v(t))-H(\overline{x},\overline{x}))^{1-\theta}\leq 0,\mbox{ for all }t\geq t^{\prime},

where
M=-\frac{m}{K}>0.

We integrate the last relation on the interval
[t,T],\,T>t\geq t^{\prime}
and we obtain

M\int_{t}^{T}\|\dot{x}(s)\|+\frac{1}{s}\|\ddot{x}(s)\|ds+(H(u(T),v(T))-H(\overline{x},\overline{x}))^{1-\theta}\leq(H(u(t),v(t))-H(\overline{x},\overline{x}))^{1-\theta}.

Consequently, for
T\longrightarrow+\infty
we have

M\sigma(t)\leq(H(u(t),v(t))-H(\overline{x},\overline{x}))^{1-\theta},\mbox{ for all }t\geq t^{\prime}.

But,

(H(u(t),v(t))-H(\overline{x},\overline{x}))^{\theta}\leq K\|{\nabla}H(u(t),v(t)\|

and, according to Lemma
4.2
(v),

\|{\nabla}H(u(t),v(t)\|\leq q(t)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|+p(t)\|\dot{x}(t)\|,\mbox{ for all }t\geq t^{\prime},

hence

M\sigma(t)\leq K^{\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}}(q(t)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|+p(t)\|\dot{x}(t))^{\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}},\mbox{ for all }t\geq t^{\prime}.

Easily can be checked that there exists
a>0
such that

q(t)\|\ddot{x}(t)\|+p(t)\|\dot{x}(t)\leq\frac{a}{t}\left(\dot{x}(t)\|+\frac{1}{t}\|\ddot{x}(t)\|\right),\mbox{ for all }t\geq t^{\prime},

hence

M\sigma(t)\leq\left(\frac{aK}{t}\right)^{\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}}\left(\dot{x}(t)\|+\frac{1}{t}\|\ddot{x}(t)\|\right)^{\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}},\mbox{ for all }t\geq t^{\prime}.

But
\dot{x}(t)\|+\frac{1}{t}\|\ddot{x}(t)\|=-\dot{\sigma}(t)
, consequently

-\alpha t\sigma^{\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}}(t)\geq\dot{\sigma}(t),\mbox{ for all }t\geq t^{\prime},

(5.3)

where
\alpha=\frac{M^{\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}}}{aK}>0.

If
\theta=\frac{1}{2}
then (
5.3
) becomes
\alpha t\sigma(t)+\dot{\sigma}(t)\leq 0,\mbox{ for all }t\geq t^{\prime}.
By multiplying with
e^{\frac{\alpha t^{2}}{2}}
then integrating on
[t^{\prime},t]
we get that there exists
a_{1},a_{2}>0
such that

\sigma(t)\leq a_{1}e^{-a_{2}t^{2}},\mbox{ for all }t\geq t^{\prime}.

Now using (
5.1
) we obtain

\|x(t)-\overline{x}\|\leq a_{1}e^{-a_{2}t^{2}},\mbox{ for all }t\geq t^{\prime},

which proves (b).

Assume now that
0<\theta<\frac{1}{2}.
We prove (a) by contradiction. So, assume that for all
t\geq t^{\prime}
one has
\sigma(t)\neq 0.

By using (
5.3
) again we obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\sigma^{\frac{1-2\theta}{1-\theta}}(t)=\frac{1-2\theta}{1-\theta}\sigma^{\frac{-\theta}{1-\theta}}(t)\dot{\sigma}(t)\leq-\alpha\frac{1-2\theta}{1-\theta}t,\mbox{ for all }t\geq t^{\prime}.

By integrating on
[t^{\prime},t]
we obtain

\sigma^{\frac{1-2\theta}{1-\theta}}(t)\leq-\overline{\alpha}t^{2}+\overline{\beta},\mbox{ for all }t\geq t^{\prime},

where
\overline{\alpha}>0.
Hence,
\sigma(t)\longrightarrow-\infty
as
t\longrightarrow+\infty,
which is in contradiction with
\sigma(t)\geq 0
for all
t\geq t^{\prime}.

It is thus deduced by contradiction that there exists
T\geq t^{\prime}
such that
\sigma(T)=0.
Since, according to (
5.3
),
\sigma
is
nonnegative and nonincreasing, one has
\sigma(t)=0
for all
t\geq T,
therefore (
5.1
) implies that
x
is constant on
[T,+\infty)
and (a) follows.

Assume now that
\frac{1}{2}<\theta<1.
By using (
5.3
) we obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\sigma^{\frac{1-2\theta}{1-\theta}}(t)=\frac{1-2\theta}{1-\theta}\sigma^{\frac{-\theta}{1-\theta}}(t)\dot{\sigma}(t)\geq\alpha\frac{2\theta-1}{1-\theta}t,\mbox{ for all }t\geq t^{\prime}.

By integrating on
[t^{\prime},t]
we obtain

\sigma^{\frac{1-2\theta}{1-\theta}}(t)\geq a_{3}t^{2}+a_{4},\mbox{ for all }t\geq t^{\prime},

where
a_{3},a_{4}>0,
or equivalently,

\sigma(t)\leq(a_{3}t^{2}+a_{4})^{-\frac{1-\theta}{2\theta-1}},\mbox{ for all }t\geq t^{\prime}.

Now using (
5.1
) we obtain

\|x(t)-\overline{x}\|\leq(a_{3}t^{2}+a_{4})^{-\frac{1-\theta}{2\theta-1}},\mbox{ for all }t\geq t^{\prime}

and this proves (c).
∎

According to Theorem 3.6
[
20
]
, if
g
has the KL property with KL exponent
\theta\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right)
at
\overline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m},
then the function
H:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\},\,H(x,y)=g(x)+\frac{1}{2}\|y-x\|^{2}
has the KL property at
(\overline{x},\overline{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
with the same KL exponent
\theta.
This result allows us to reformulate Theorem
5.1
.

Corollary 5.2
.

Assume that
g
is bounded from below and for
u_{0},v_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
x
be the unique strong global solution of (
DS
).
Suppose that
x
is bounded and let
\overline{x}\in\crit(g)
be such that
\lim_{t\longrightarrow+\infty}x(t)=\overline{x}
and
g
fulfills the Łojasiewicz property at
\overline{x}\in\crit g
with Łojasiewicz exponent
\theta\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right)
. If
\theta=\frac{1}{2}
then the convergence rates stated at
Theorem
5.1
(b)
hold, if
\theta\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right)
then the convergence rates stated at
Theorem
5.1
(c)
hold.

Proof.

Indeed, according to Theorem 3.6
[
20
]

H
has the Łojasiewicz property at
(\overline{x},\overline{x})\in\crit H
with the Łojasiewicz exponent
\theta\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right)
. Hence, Theorem
5.1
can be applied.
∎

In case we assume that the function
g
is strongly convex, then Theorem
5.1
assures superlinear convergence rates for the trajectories generated by (
DS
). The following result holds.

Theorem 5.3
.

Assume that the objective function
g
is strongly convex and let
x^{*}
be the unique minimizer of
g.
For
u_{0},v_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
x
be the unique strong global solution of (
DS
).

Then, there exist
a_{1},a_{2}>0
and
t^{\prime}>0
such that for every
t\in[t^{\prime},+\infty)
one has

\|x(t)-x^{*}\|\leq a_{1}e^{-a_{2}t^{2}}.

Proof.

We emphasize that the strongly convex function
g
is coercive, see
[
8
]
. According to
[
23
]
the function
g
is bounded from bellow. Hence,
\omega(x)=\{x^{*}\}
and
x(t)\longrightarrow x^{*},\,t\longrightarrow+\infty.
According to
[
4
]
,
g
satisfies the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz property at
x^{*}
with the Łojasiewicz exponent
\theta=\frac{1}{2}.
The conclusion now follows from Corollary
5.2
.
∎

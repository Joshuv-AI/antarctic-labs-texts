Abstract
. Let G G be a graph and let cl ⁡ ( G ) \cl(G) be the number of distinct induced cycle lengths in G G . We show that for c , t ∈ ℕ c,t\in\mathbb{N} , every graph G G that does not contain an induced subgraph isomorphic to K t + 1 K_{t+1} or K t , t K_{t,t} and satisfies cl ⁡ ( G ) ≤ c \cl(G)\leq c has bounded treewidth. As a consequence, we obtain a polynomial-time algorithm for deciding whether a graph G G contains induced cycles of at least three distinct lengths.

Berger, Seymour, and Spirkl

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

that decides whether

As a generalized version and a core subroutine of this algorithm, we also show that we can decide if

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

be constants and let

be a graph with

. There exists an algorithm with running time

that decides whether

1.2.
Proof outline and organization

We start by outlining the proof of Theorem
1.3
. In Section
3
, we show that if a graph
G
contains one of the basic obstructions (see Section
3
for a precise statement) or a large dome, then
\cl(G)\geq c
. In Section
4
, we apply this result to reduce Theorem
1.3
to Theorem
4.1
, which states that a graph containing a large dense banana contains a large dome. We then prove Theorem
4.1
in Sections
5
,
6
and
7
.

In Section
5
, we analyze the structure of rigid bananas. We show how to obtain either a theta
\Theta_{x,y}
where each vertex in

has degree two in
G
, or a large dome. In Section
6
, we apply this result to obtain many tailed thetas, which together form a kite. Then, we show how to either make this kite clean or obtain a large dome. In Section
7
, we recursively obtain a sequence of kites, which we use to obtain a large dome
(S,P)
. The last kite in this sequence provides the path
P
, and all the previous kites provide the paths of the subdivided star
S
. This completes the proof of Theorem
4.1
, and thus, also Theorem
1.3
.

Then, in Section
8
we describe our “key subroutine”, Theorem
1.2
. The input of this algorithm is a graph

with bounded treewidth. The algorithm finds a tree decomposition
(T,\chi)
of
G
and, using dynamic programming over

, either finds
c
induced cycles of distinct lengths in
G
, or determines that
\cl(G)<c
.

Finally, in Section
9
, we prove Theorem
1.1
. Suppose that
G
contains
K_{3}
or
K_{2,2}
. Then, we can build on the algorithm that finds a non-shortest

path from
[
3
]
to decide whether

. Thus, we may assume that

-free. In this case, we apply Theorem

to reduce the problem to the case where

. This proves Theorem

.

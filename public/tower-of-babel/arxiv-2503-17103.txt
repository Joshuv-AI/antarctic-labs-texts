Martingale property and moment explosions in signature volatility models
Eduardo Abi Jaber, Paul Gassiat, Dimitri Sotnikov

Abstract
We study the martingale property and moment explosions of a signature volatility model, where the volatility process of the log-price is given by a linear form of the signature of a time-extended Brownian motion. Excluding trivial cases, we demonstrate that the price process is a true martingale if and only if the order of the linear form is odd and a correlation parameter is negative. The proof involves a fine analysis of the explosion time of a signature stochastic differential equation. This result is of key practical relevance, as it highlights that, when used for approximation purposes, the linear combination of signature elements must be taken of odd order to preserve the martingale property. Once martingality is established, we also characterize the existence of higher moments of the price process in terms of a condition on a correlation parameter.



1
Introduction

The martingale property and moment explosions of the asset price process are fundamental considerations in stochastic volatility modeling, particularly due to their implications for derivative pricing and numerical methods. The martingale property is a key criterion for assessing the validity of a stochastic volatility model, as emphasized by
Sepp and Rakhmonov (2023)
. In particular, strict local martingale models exhibit structural deficiencies, such as the failure of put-call parity and deviations in the asymptotic behavior of implied volatility wings
(
Cox and Hobson, 2005
;
Heston, Loewenstein, and Willard, 2007
;
Jacquier and Keller-Ressel, 2018
)
. Similarly, the existence of moments for the price process plays a crucial role in both theoretical and computational aspects of option pricing. In particular, the existence of finite moments is crucial for ensuring the numerical stability and convergence of Monte Carlo methods used to compute derivative prices and their sensitivities. Moments also play a key role in the proof of asymptotic formulae, allowing one to transition from large deviations of the price process to call price asymptotics, see for instance
(
Friz et al., 2021
, Section 4.2)
and
Lee (2004)
.

Both of these problems have been extensively studied in the literature, dating back to the works of
Girsanov (1960)
and
Novikov (1973)
. However, Novikov’s criterion is often not applicable to stochastic volatility models. An alternative approach consists in using a change-of-measure argument to transform the problem into the study of SDE explosion; see, for instance,
McKean (1969, Chapter 3.7)
. This technique was applied to stochastic volatility modeling by
Sin (1998)
, who observed that in certain models—such as the Hull–White model—the stock price can be a strict martingale for certain parameter values. In this context,
Lions and Musiela (2007)
present general sufficient conditions for martingality and strict local martingality when the volatility process follows a stochastic differential equation (SDE), and also address the problem of moment existence. Subsequently,
Mijatović and Urusov (2012)
provide a more general criterion for martingality in SDE-based models, while
Bernard, Cui, and McLeish (2017)
extend this work to the general spot-volatility correlation case
-1\leq\rho\leq 1
, offering a complete classification of convergence properties for general one-factor Markovian stochastic volatility models.

Most of these works, however, focus on Markovian models where both the asset price and volatility processes are solutions of SDEs. For example,
Jourdain (2004)
proposes a martingality criterion, along with integrability conditions for prices in SABR-like models. Similarly,
Andersen and Piterbarg (2007)
address the moment problem and provide sufficient martingality conditions for SABR-like and Heston-like models.

For the non-Markovian setting, we refer to the works of
Criens (2020)
and
Criens and Glau (2018)
, which establish general results on the martingale property of stochastic exponential martingales for Itô processes. However, deriving such results for specific path-dependent models often requires careful, case-by-case analysis. A notable class of such models considers volatility processes governed by stochastic Volterra equations. For the rough Bergomi model,
Gassiat (2018)
establishes conditions for both martingality and non-martingality, as well as a sufficient condition for moment explosions.
Gerhold, Pachschwöll, and Ruf (2024)
propose an alternative approach to verifying higher-order moment explosions by examining the integrability of the price supremum and apply this method to the rough Bergomi model.
In the context of the Volterra Heston model,
Abi Jaber, Larsson, and Pulido (2019)
establish conditions for the martingale property, while
Gerhold, Gerstenecker, and Pinter (2018)
study the problem of moment explosions. In the class of so-called path-dependent volatility (PDV) models
(
Guyon and Lekeufack, 2023
)
,
Nutz and Riveros Valdevenito (2024)
analyze the martingale property in a two-factor PDV model. Additionally,
Ruf (2015)
provides a general martingality criterion for stochastic exponentials defined as non-anticipative path-dependent functionals of solutions to stochastic differential equations. Despite its generality, this result cannot be directly applied to stochastic volatility models and requires a thorough analysis to verify the condition for each particular model.

Recently, signature-based models have gained popularity in non-Markovian modeling. Such models were first studied by
Arribas, Salvi, and Szpruch (2020)
and further developed by
Cuchiero, Gazzani, and Svaluto-Ferro (2023)
;
Cuchiero, Gazzani, Möller, and Svaluto-Ferro (2025)
, and
Abi Jaber and Gérard (2025)
. Their distinguishing feature is that the volatility process is represented as a (possibly infinite) linear combination of signature elements. These models can serve both for the approximation, see
Cuchiero et al. (2023)
, and the exaxt representation, see
Abi Jaber and Gérard (2025)
, of a wide range of models, though in both cases, the linear combination of signature elements must be truncated for practical use. As we will demonstrate, the truncation order plays a crucial role in determining whether the price process is a true martingale. However, no results on either martingality or moment explosions have been established for these models so far. The aim of this paper is to fill this gap by providing necessary and sufficient conditions for the martingality of the price process and by studying the finiteness of its moments in signature volatility models. We adopt the multiplicative framework of
Abi Jaber and Gérard (2025)
;
Cuchiero et al. (2025)
, in which the price process is given by a stochastic exponential, making the question of martingality non-trivial.

The Markovian setting provides key insights into the martingality problem. In particular, for truncated exponential Ornstein-Uhlenbeck models with negative spot-volatility correlation, obtained by truncating the power series expansion of the exponential, the price process is a martingale if and only if the truncation order is odd. A similar pattern appears in polynomial volatility models, where volatility is expressed as a polynomial function of a Brownian motion or an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process, as in the Quintic model of
Abi Jaber, Illand, and Li (2023)
. These results can be established using techniques similar to those in
Lions and Musiela (2007, Theorem 2.4(i))
. Since path signatures play an analogous role to polynomials on path space, we expect similar characterizations to hold beyond the Markovian framework.

In this work, we show that the order of the linear combination of signature elements plays the same role as the order of a polynomial and that a similar result holds for signature volatility models. Specifically, given two Brownian motions,

The martingale property.
Our main result, Theorem

under the assumption that the leading coefficient
\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}

\rho\neq 0

the price process

is a true martingale if and only if

is odd and
\rho\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}\leq 0

The standard technique for verifying the martingale property reduces to studying the explosion time of a certain stochastic differential equation (SDE) following a change of probability measure. In our case, this yields a signature SDE whose explosion time is intricate to analyze, see Proposition
4.1
and Theorem
4.2
. To address this, we establish two key bounds for linear combinations of signature elements in Lemmas
5.1
and
5.2
. These bounds exploit the fact that the shuffle algebra forms a polynomial algebra over the
Lyndon (1954)
words, thanks to the
Radford (1979)
Theorem. Using these bounds, we then prove the finiteness or explosion of the solution of the signature SDE, depending on the parity of the order
N
and the sign of
\rho
, leading to Theorem
3.1
. We stress that such result is of key practical relevance, as it highlights that, when used for approximation purposes, the linear combination of signature elements must be taken of odd order to preserve the martingale property. The non-positivity of
\rho\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}
does not imply that the spot-volatility correlation is necessarily non-positive, see Remark
3.1.4
.

Additionally, we extend the martingale characterization to a volatility process constructed from the signature of a multidimensional Brownian motion, as presented in Theorem
3.5
.

Moment explosions.
Assuming the conditions for
S
to be a martingale, we prove in Theorem
3.2
that

the moment
\mathbb{E}[S_{T}^{m}]
of order
m
is finite for any
T>0
if
|\rho|>\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{m}}
.

Conversely, if
|\rho|<\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{m}}
, then
\mathbb{E}[S_{T}^{m}]=+\infty
for all
T>0
.

This result aligns well with those obtained in the Markovian case, such as
(
Lions and Musiela, 2007
, Theorem 2.3)
and
(
Jourdain, 2004
, Proposition 6)
. The same condition for moment explosions in the non-Markovian rough Bergomi model was proved in
Gassiat (2018)
, see also
Gulisashvili (2020)
. To prove Theorem

, we rely on the
Boué and Dupuis (1998)
formula, which reduces the moment existence problem to the finiteness of an optimal control problem value. In the case of infinite moments, we explicitly construct a sequence of controls to show that the problem value is infinite, whereas in the case of finite moments, we establish the finiteness of the value function using the bounds on linear combinations derived earlier. For the critical case
|\rho|=\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{m}}
, we show in Proposition
3.4
that the finiteness of the
m
-th moment may or may not hold depending on the other coefficients of
\bm{\sigma}
and the time horizon
T
.

S_{t}=S_{0}\exp\left\{-\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{s}^{2}\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{s}\,dB_{s}\right\},\quad t\geq 0.

(3.5)

We note that the volatility dynamics in the model (
3.2
) are driven by the signature
\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}
of a one-dimensional Brownian motion. We will first characterize the martingale property in Subsection
3.1
, then study moment explosions in Subsection
3.2
, before extending the characterization of the martingale property to volatility dynamics driven by a multidimensional Brownian motion in Subsection
3.3
.

3.1
Martingale property

Our main result in this section provides a necessary and sufficient condition for the price process
S
, defined by (
3.2
), to be a martingale. This condition is given in terms of the parity of the signature level
N
and the correlation parameter
\rho
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
S
be given by (
3.2
).
Suppose that the leading coefficient
\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}\not=0
.

(i)

If
\rho=0
or
N=1
, then
S
is a martingale.

(ii)

If
\rho\not=0
and
N\geq 2
, then
S
is a martingale if and only if the order
N
is odd and
\rho\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}\leq 0.

Proof.

(i)
and sufficiency in
(ii)
are proved in Section
6
and necessity in
(ii)
is proved in Subsection
7.1
.
∎

Remark 3.1.1
.

Part (i) of the theorem has already been well understood and is covered in the existing literature. Indeed, when
\rho=0
, the volatility process is independent of the Brownian motion
B
driving the price, and a conditioning argument (see, for example,
Karatzas and Kardaras (2007, Lemma 4.8)
) immediately yields the martingale property. In the case
N=1
, the volatility process reduces to a Brownian motion with drift, and martingality follows (see, for instance,
Criens and Glau (2018, Corollary 6.1)
). We nevertheless include this part of the statement for completeness and to illustrate that these particular cases are also naturally covered by our approach.

\blacksquare

The results of Theorem
3.1
can be illustrated numerically on implied volatility surfaces. Namely, if the spot price
S
is a strict local martingale, the put-call parity is not verified, and hence, put-option implied volatility is different from the call-option implied volatility which may be not even defined, as shown by
Jacquier and Keller-Ressel (2018)
. More precisely, recall that the Black–Scholes Call and Put option prices
C_{\mathrm{BS}}(T,K,\sigma)
and
P_{\mathrm{BS}}(T,K,\sigma)
satisfy the put-call parity

C_{\mathrm{BS}}(T,K,\sigma)-P_{\mathrm{BS}}(T,K,\sigma)=S_{0}-K,

while the model Call and Put option prices
C(T,K)
and
P(T,K)
satisfy only

C(T,K)-P(T,K)=\mathbb{E}[S_{T}]-K.

Thus, if
P(T,K)=P_{\mathrm{BS}}(T,K,\sigma_{\mathrm{BS}}^{P})
, one obtains

C(T,K)=C_{\mathrm{BS}}(T,K,\sigma_{\mathrm{BS}}^{P})+\mathbb{E}[S_{T}]-S_{0}=C_{\mathrm{BS}}(T,K,\sigma_{\mathrm{BS}}^{C}).

(3.6)

Hence, the call and put implied volatilities coincide if and only if the put-call parity is satisfied, which holds if and only if
\mathbb{E}[S_{T}]=S_{0}
. In turn, this is equivalent to the martingality of
S
, since
S
is a positive local martingale and therefore a supermartingale.

We compute the put and call implied volatility smiles via the Monte Carlo method using
10^{6}
trajectories of the spot price given by (
3.2
) for even (
N=4
) and odd (
N=5
) orders and for positive (
\rho=0.9
) and negative (
\rho=-0.9
) values of the correlation parameter. The maturity is taken equal to
T=1
. The coefficients
\bm{\sigma}
are chosen randomly (same random numbers were used in all cases) from the uniform distribution on
[-0.5,\,0.5]
, except for the coefficient
\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}
fixed equal to
1
.

Simulation results are provided in Figure

.
As expected, the only case where call and put implied volatilities coincide corresponds to the odd order
N=5
and negative correlation
\rho=-0.9
.

Remark 3.1.2
.

The characterization of Theorem
3.1
still holds if, instead of
\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}
, we consider the signature of a time-extended Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process
\widehat{Y}_{t}=(t,Y_{t})
for the volatility process, where
Y
satisfies

dY_{t}=aY_{t}dt+bdW_{t},\quad t\geq 0,

for some
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
. Although slight modifications in the proof of Lemma
5.2
are needed, the proof of this extension follows the same ideas presented later in this paper. The case of the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process is important for practical applications as it includes the Quintic model of
Abi Jaber, Illand, and Li (2023)
and can be seen as a particular case of the multi-factor signature volatility model proposed by
Cuchiero, Gazzani, Möller, and Svaluto-Ferro (2025)
.

Remark 3.1.3
.

Theorem
3.1
provides useful insights into the practical use of the signature volatility model (
3.2
). Specifically, when performing a linear regression of the volatility process against the driving Brownian motion, using its exact signature representation, or calibrating the model, a suitable truncation order must be chosen. Our result offers clear and simple guidelines for selecting hyperparameters:
N
should be chosen to be odd, while the condition on
\rho\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}
can be easily incorporated as a constraint in the optimization problem. Failing to do so could have dramatic consequences for martingality, as illustrated in Figure
1
.

Remark 3.1.4
.

We note that the condition
\rho\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}\leq 0
does not imply that the spot-volatility correlation must be negative. Indeed, as shown in
(
Abi Jaber and Gérard, 2025
, Subsection 3.2)
, the spot-volatility correlation is given by

\frac{d[\log S,|\sigma|]_{t}}{\sqrt{d[\log S]_{t}}\sqrt{d[|\sigma|]_{t}}}=\rho\cdot\textnormal{sign}\left(\left\langle\bm{\sigma}|_{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}\right\rangle\right),

where
\bm{\sigma}|_{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}}=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\sum\limits_{|{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{v}}}|\leq n}\bm{\sigma}^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{v2}}}}{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{v}}}
. Since the sign depends on the other coefficients of
\bm{\sigma}
, the spot-volatility correlation can be positive while still ensuring the price martingality. In this way, the model can be applied to markets with a positive implied volatility skew, such as FX or commodity markets.

3.2
Moments

In this subsection, we present our main result concerning the moment explosions of the process
S
in the signature volatility model (
3.2
). Under the assumptions ensuring true martingality stated in Theorem
3.1
, we identify the threshold value of
m
for which
\mathbb{E}[S_{T}^{m}]
is finite.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
S
be given by (
3.2
).
Suppose that the order
N\geq 2
is odd and
\rho\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}<0
.

(i)

If
|\rho|<\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{m}}
, then
\mathbb{E}[S_{T}^{m}]=+\infty
for any
T>0
.

(ii)

If
|\rho|>\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{m}}
, then
\mathbb{E}[S_{T}^{m}]<+\infty
for any
T>0
.

Proof.

The proof is given in Section
8
.
∎

Theorem
3.2
provides the threshold
\bar{m}
for the finiteness of the price process moments, which determines the asymptotic behavior of the implied volatility smile as
K\to+\infty
in the signature volatility model (
3.2
), using the celebrated formula by
Lee (2004)
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
S
be given by (
3.2
).
Suppose that the order
N\geq 2
is odd and
\rho\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}<0
. For a maturity
T>0
and a strike
K\in\mathbb{R}
we denote by
\sigma_{\mathrm{BS}}^{2}(T,k)
the implied volatility of vanilla (call or put) options with
k=\log\left(\frac{K}{S_{0}}\right)
the log-moneyness.
Then, the following asymptotic behavior of the implied volatility holds

\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\dfrac{\sigma_{\mathrm{BS}}^{2}(T,k)T}{k}=\beta_{R}=2\dfrac{1-|\rho|}{1+|\rho|}.

(3.7)

Proof.

An application of the
Lee (2004, Theorem 3.2)
formula yields the asymptotic behavior (
3.7
) with
\beta_{R}=2-4\left(\sqrt{\bar{p}^{2}+\bar{p}}-\bar{p}\right)
, where
\bar{p}=\sup\left\{p\colon\ \mathbb{E}\left[S_{T}^{1+p}\right]<\infty\right\}
. Under the assumptions of Theorem
3.2
, a straightforward computation gives

\bar{p}=\dfrac{\rho^{2}}{1-\rho^{2}},\qquad\beta_{R}=2\dfrac{1-|\rho|}{1+|\rho|}.

∎

. The simulated total variance smiles

.

.

The proof of Theorem

crucially relies on the presence of a dominant term that determines the outcome of the moment explosion problem. The sign of this term is that of
\tfrac{\bar{\rho}^{2}m^{2}-m}{2}
. In the critical case where

.

.

.

was stated for the signature volatility model (

. Consider now a model

\displaystyle\dfrac{dS_{t}}{S_{t}}

(3.8)

\displaystyle\sigma_{t}

,

.

Let

). Suppose that the leading coefficient

.

If the order

(ii)

If the order

Proof.

(i)
and sufficiency in

.
∎

Remark 3.5.1
.

The price model (

) is indeed a special case of the model (

), since the elements of the signature of

can be written as a linear combination of elements of the signature of

Although Theorem

4
A general martingality criterion

In this section, we derive a general criterion to establish the martingality of the price process in signature volatility models. Similarly to the Markovian setting, lack of martingality is seen by a Girsanov transform to be equivalent to the explosion of a certain SDE of signature drift form.

We let
S
be given by (
3.8
), i.e. the volatility process is given by

\sigma_{t}=\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}_{t}\right\rangle,\quad t\geq 0

(4.1)

where
\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}_{t}
is the signature of the process
\widehat{Y}_{t}=(t,B_{t},Z^{1}_{t},\ldots,Z^{d}_{t})
, and
\bm{\sigma}\in T^{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d+2})
for some
N\geq 1
.

The criterion will be stated, in Theorem
4.2
, in terms of the solution to the SDE

X_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X}_{s}\right\rangle ds+B_{t},

(4.2)

where
\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X}_{t}
denotes the signature of
\widehat{Y}^{X}_{t}=(t,X_{t},Z^{1}_{t},\ldots,Z^{d}_{t})
.

4.1
Signature SDE

In this subsection, we show the signature SDE (
4.2
)
is well-defined (up to an explosion time) for arbitrary
\bm{\sigma}
in the truncated tensor algebra
T^{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d+2})
.

We note that
X
may explode in finite time, so the solution is understood as a process satisfying equation (
4.2
) on
[[0,\tau^{X}_{\infty}[[
, where the explosion time
\tau^{X}_{\infty}
is a stopping time defined by

\tau^{X}_{\infty}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau_{n}^{X},\quad\tau_{n}^{X}:=\inf\{t\geq 0\colon\,|X_{t}|=n\},

(4.3)

such that, on the event
\{\tau^{X}_{\infty}<\infty\}
, we have
\lim\limits_{t\to\tau^{X}_{\infty}}|X_{t}|=+\infty
a.s.

Proposition 4.1
.

The SDE (
4.2
) admits a unique strong solution. In addition, it holds that

\tau^{X}_{\infty}=\inf\left\{t\geq 0,\;\;\int_{0}^{t}\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X}_{s}\rangle^{2}ds=+\infty\right\}.

(4.4)

Proof.

For the well-posedness of the SDE, we note that it can be equivalently written as a system of classical (Markovian) SDEs. Indeed, setting
\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X,{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}}_{t}=\langle{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X}_{t}\rangle
, the signature SDE (
4.2
) is equivalent to
X=\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X,{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}}
, where the
(\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X,{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}})_{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}:\,|{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}|\leq N}
satisfy
\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X,{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\textup{{\o{}}}}}\equiv 1
and for
|{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}}|<N
,

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X,{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}}{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{1}}}}_{t}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X,{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}}}_{s}ds,

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X,{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}}{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}}_{t}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X,{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}}}_{s}\left(\sum_{|{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}|\leq N}\bm{\sigma}^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}}\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X,{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}}_{s}\right)ds+\int_{0}^{t}\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X,{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}}}_{s}\circ dB_{s},

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X,{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}}{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{i}}}}_{t}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X,{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}}}_{s}{\circ}dZ^{i-2}_{s},\;\;i=3,\ldots,d+2.

This is a Stratonovich SDE with locally Lipschitz coefficients, for which existence and uniqueness up to an explosion time are standard.

It remains to prove (
4.4
).
Since on the event
\{\tau^{X}_{\infty}<\infty\}
, we have
\int_{0}^{\tau^{X}_{\infty}}\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X}_{s}\rangle\,ds=X_{\tau^{X}_{\infty}}-B_{\tau^{X}_{\infty}}=\pm\infty
, it follows that

\int_{0}^{\tau^{X}_{\infty}}\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X}_{s}\rangle^{2}ds\geq\left(\int_{0}^{\tau^{X}_{\infty}}\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X}_{s}\rangle\,ds\right)^{2}=+\infty,

we have
\int_{0}^{\tau^{X}_{\infty}}\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X}_{s}\rangle^{2}ds=+\infty
on
\{\tau^{X}_{\infty}<\infty\}
. Conversely, by Lemma
5.2
(stated in Section
5
below), we can show that, for each word
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\tau_{n}^{X}\wedge T}\langle{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X}_{t}\rangle^{2}dt\right]\leq{C_{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}},T}n^{2|{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}|+1}}<\infty.

Since
\tau^{X}_{\infty}=\sup\limits_{n\geq 1}\tau^{X}_{n}
, this implies that
\int_{0}^{t}\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X}_{s}\rangle^{2}ds<\infty
for each
t<\tau^{X}_{\infty}
.
∎

4.2
Martingality and explosion

By the results of the previous subsection, equation (
4.2
) admits a unique strong solution up to an explosion time
\tau^{X}_{\infty}
, satisfying (
4.4
).

Theorem 4.2
.

For each
T>0
, for
S
defined by (
3.8
), it holds that

S\mbox{ is a martingale on $[0,T]$}\;\Leftrightarrow\mathbb{P}(\tau^{X}_{\infty}<T)=0,

where
\tau^{X}_{\infty}
is the explosion time of
X
defined by (
4.2
).

Proof.

Since
S
is a nonnegative local martingale, it is a supermartingale by Fatou’s Lemma, so that
\mathbb{E}[S_{T}]\leq S_{0}
for all
T\geq 0
. Hence, for a given
T>0
, it is a martingale on
[0,T]
if and only if
\mathbb{E}\left[S_{T}\right]=S_{0}
. Let

\tau_{n}=\inf\left\{t>0\colon\,\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}_{s}\right\rangle^{2}ds=n\right\}.

As the stopped process
\left(\int_{0}^{t\land\tau_{n}}\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}_{s}\right\rangle^{2}ds\right)_{t\geq 0}
is bounded by
n
, the stopped price process
S^{\tau_{n}}=(S_{t\land\tau_{n}})_{t\geq 0}
is an exponential martingale by Novikov’s criterion and it follows that

S_{0}=\mathbb{E}\left[S^{\tau_{n}}_{T}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[S_{T\land\tau_{n}}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[S_{T}\mathds{1}_{\left\{T\leq\tau_{n}\right\}}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[S_{\tau_{n}}\mathds{1}_{\left\{\tau_{n}<T\right\}}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[S_{T}\mathds{1}_{\left\{T\leq\tau_{n}\right\}}\right]+S_{0}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{n}\left({\tau_{n}<T}\right),

where
\dfrac{d\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{n}}{d\mathbb{P}}=\dfrac{S_{T\land\tau_{n}}}{S_{0}}
.

By the monotone convergence theorem, the term
\mathbb{E}\left[S_{T}\mathds{1}_{\left\{T\leq\tau_{n}\right\}}\right]
converges to
\mathbb{E}\left[S_{T}\right]
as
n\to\infty
. Thus,

S_{0}-\mathbb{E}\left[S_{T}\right]=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[S_{\tau_{n}}\mathds{1}_{\left\{\tau_{n}<T\right\}}\right]=\lim_{n\to\infty}S_{0}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{n}\left({\tau_{n}<T}\right).

On the other hand, by Girsanov theorem, for each
n
, it holds that for each
t\leq T
,

B_{t}=\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}_{s}\right\rangle ds+\widehat{B}_{t},

where
\widehat{B}
is a Brownian motion under
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{n}
. In particular, the Girsanov change-of-measure argument implies the uniqueness of the weak solution to the signature SDE (satisfied by
B
under
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{n}
):

X_{t}^{n}=\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X^{n}}_{s}\right\rangle\mathds{1}_{\left\{\int_{0}^{s}\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X^{n}}_{r}\right\rangle^{2}dr\leq n\right\}}ds+B_{t}.

(4.5)

Indeed, by applying the Girsanov theorem as above, and noting that the corresponding Radon–Nikodym derivative is a true martingale, one verifies that the law of
X^{n}
can be expressed in terms of Wiener measure and is therefore uniquely determined. It remains to observe that (
4.5
) is also satisfied by

X_{t}^{n}=X_{t\land\tilde{\tau}_{n}}+\mathds{1}_{\left\{t\geq\tilde{\tau}_{n}\right\}}(B_{t}-B_{\tilde{\tau}_{n}}),

where
X
denotes the strong solution to (
4.2
) given by Proposition
4.1
, so that

\tilde{\tau}_{n}=\inf\left\{t>0:\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X}_{s}\right\rangle^{2}ds=n\right\}=\inf\left\{t>0:\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X^{n}}_{s}\right\rangle^{2}ds=n\right\}.

Hence, weak uniqueness implies that

\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{n}\left(\tau_{n}<T\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\tilde{\tau}_{n}<T\right),

and therefore,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{n}\left(\tau_{n}<T\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\tilde{\tau}_{n}<T\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\tau^{X}_{\infty}<T\right),

where we have used that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\tilde{\tau}_{n}=\tau^{X}_{\infty}
.

∎

Corollary 4.3
.

The same proof applied to the one-dimensional model implies that
S
given by (
3.2
) is a martingale on
[0,T]
if and only if
\mathbb{P}(\tau^{X}_{\infty}<T){=0}
, where
\tau^{X}_{\infty}
is the explosion time of
X
defined by

X_{t}=\rho\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}_{s}\right\rangle ds+W_{t},

(4.6)

and
\widehat{\mathbb{X}}_{t}
denotes the signature of
\widehat{X}_{t}=(t,X_{t})
, for
t\in[0,T]
.

Remark 4.3.1
.

Theorem
4.2
is closely related to the result proved in
(
Kazamaki, 2006
, Lemma 1.5)
rewritten in terms of new probability measures. A more general criterion was proposed in
(
Ruf, 2015
, Theorem 3.3)
. It states that the price process is a martingale if and only if

\widehat{\mathbb{P}}\left(\int_{0}^{T\land\vartheta}\sigma_{s}^{2}ds<\infty\right)=1,\quad T\geq 0,

(4.7)

where
\vartheta

\widehat{\mathbb{P}}
denotes the extension of

which is constructed in
(
Ruf, 2015
, Proposition 3.1)
. However, this approach assumes a specific structure of the underlying probability space as highlighted in
(
Ruf, 2015

. For this reason, we prefer to work with Theorem

5.1
Polynomial representations in the shuffle algebra

In this subsection, we recall the notion of
Lyndon (1954)
words which will help us prove the bounds given by Lemma
5.1
and Lemma
5.2
.
The extended tensor algebra
T((\mathbb{R}^{d}))
can be considered as an algebra endowed with a shuffle product. We define a shuffle multivariate polynomial
P^{\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.2mu\sqcup}}
of
n
arguments as

P^{\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.2mu\sqcup}}:T((\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{n}\to T((\mathbb{R}^{d})),\quad({{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{v_{1}}}},\ldots,{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{v_{n}}}})\mapsto\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\end{subarray}}c_{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}}\,{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{v_{1}}}}^{\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.2mu\sqcup}p_{1}}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.2mu\sqcup}{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{v_{2}}}}^{\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.2mu\sqcup}p_{2}}\ldots\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.2mu\sqcup}{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{v_{n}}}}^{\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.2mu\sqcup}p_{n}},

where the sum is finite and taken over some set of positive natural numbers
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
.

The Lyndon words defined below are useful to construct the polynomial representations of the shuffle algebra elements.

Definition 5.3
.

A Lyndon word in an alphabet
{A}_{d}
is a non-empty word that is strictly lexicographically greater than any of its non-trivial cyclic rotations. A set of all Lyndon words in the alphabet
{A}_{d}
will be denoted by
\mathcal{L}({A}_{d})
, and
\mathcal{L}_{N}({A}_{d})
will denote the Lyndon words of length not greater than
N
.

Example 5.3.1
.

The first Lyndon words in the alphabets
{A}_{2}
and

are given by

•

\bm{\ell}\in T(\mathbb{R}^{d})
can be represented by a shuffle polynomial

(5.3)

\bm{\ell}\in T^{N}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{v_{1}}}},\ldots,{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{v_{n_{\bm{\ell}}}}}}\in\mathcal{L}_{N}

where
\epsilon_{j}>0

. We then conclude by the induction hypothesis applied to each

In the case where

{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w_{k,1}}}}

{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}}{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{1}}}

{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}}{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{j}}}

Again, it is enough to bound separately the integrals of each of the three terms. The second one follows exactly as in the case
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w_{k,1}}}}={{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}}{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{1}}}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\tau}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X}_{s}\right\rangle dZ^{j-2}_{s}\right|^{q}dt\right]

\displaystyle\leq T\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leq t\leq\tau}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X}_{s}\right\rangle dZ^{j-2}_{s}\right|^{q}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C_{T,q}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}\left\langle{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X}_{s}\right\rangle^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}_{T,q}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\tau}\left|\left\langle{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X}_{s}\right\rangle\right|^{q}ds\right]

Using Hölder’s inequality, recalling that

, and applying the induction hypothesis to
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}}
since
|{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}}|=n-1
, we establish (
5.2
) for the word
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}
, thereby completing the proof.

6
Proof of martingality in Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.5

As noted in Remark
3.5.1
, Theorem
3.1
follows immediately from Theorem
3.5
when
\rho\neq 0
.
The case
\rho=0
is trivial, as
X
, given by (
4.6
), is a Brownian motion. Consequently, we have
\tau_{\infty}^{X}=+\infty
almost surely, and
S
is a martingale by Corollary
4.3
.
Hence, we turn directly to the more general model (
3.8
) and proceed with the proof of Theorem
3.5
.

If
N=1
, the equation (
4.2
) is a linear SDE, which admits a unique strong solution on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
. This ensures that
\tau_{\infty}^{X}=+\infty
almost surely. We conclude that
S
is a martingale by Theorem
4.2
. This establishes
(i)
of Theorem
3.5
.

We now consider the non-trivial case
N\geq 2
, corresponding to point
(ii)
of Theorem
3.5
.
By Theorem
4.2
, we need to prove that the solution
X
to (
4.2
) does not explode in finite time. Specifically, defining

\tau^{X}_{n}=\inf\left\{t\geq 0{\colon}\,|X_{t}|=n\right\},

we will show that

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\tau^{X}_{n}<T)=\mathbb{P}(\tau^{X}_{\infty}<T)=0.

The probabilities on the left-hand side are controlled by

\mathbb{P}(\tau^{X}_{n}\leq T)\leq\dfrac{1}{n^{2N}}\mathbb{E}\left[X_{T\wedge\tau_{n}^{X}}^{2N}\right],

since

\mathbb{E}\left[X_{T\wedge\tau_{n}^{X}}^{2N}\right]\geq\mathbb{E}\left[n^{2N}\mathds{1}_{\left\{\tau_{n}^{X}\leq T\right\}}\right]=n^{2N}\mathbb{P}(\tau^{X}_{n}\leq T),

so that it is enough to obtain a uniform bound (in
n
) for
\mathbb{E}\left[X_{T\wedge\tau_{n}^{X}}^{2N}\right]
.

By Itô’s formula, we have on
t\in[[0,\tau_{n}^{X}]]

dX_{t}^{2N}=2NX_{t}^{2N-1}dX_{t}+N(2N-1)X_{t}^{2N-2}dt=2NX_{t}^{2N-1}\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\mathbb{\widehat{Y}^{X}}_{t}\right\rangle dt+2NX_{t}^{2N-1}dB_{t}+N(2N-1)X_{t}^{2N-2}dt

(6.1)

Integration from
0
to
T\wedge\tau_{n}^{X}
, taking the expectation and denoting
\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}=\bm{\sigma}-\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[X_{T\wedge\tau_{n}^{X}}^{2N}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau_{n}^{X}}\left(2NX_{s}^{2N-1}\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\mathbb{\widehat{Y}^{X}}_{s}\right\rangle+N(2N-1)X_{s}^{2N-2}\right)\,ds\right]

(6.2)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau_{n}^{X}}\left(\dfrac{2N}{N!}\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}X_{s}^{3N-1}+\dfrac{2N}{N!}X_{s}^{2N-1}\left\langle\tilde{\bm{\sigma}},\mathbb{\widehat{Y}^{X}}_{s}\right\rangle+N(2N-1)X_{s}^{2N-2}\right)\,ds\right]

(6.3)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau_{n}^{X}}\left(\dfrac{2N}{N!}\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}X_{s}^{3N-1}+\delta X_{s}^{3N-1}+C(N,\delta)\left\langle\tilde{\bm{\sigma}},\mathbb{\widehat{Y}^{X}}_{s}\right\rangle^{\frac{3N-1}{N}}+N(2N-1)X_{s}^{2N-2}\right]\right)\,ds

(6.4)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau_{n}^{X}}\left({\left(\dfrac{2N}{N!}\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}+\delta\right)}X_{s}^{3N-1}+\tilde{C}(N,\delta,T)\left(|X_{s}|^{(3N-1)\frac{N-1}{N}+\epsilon}+1\right)+N(2N-1)X_{s}^{2N-2}\right)\,ds\right],

(6.5)

where we have applied Young’s inequality to obtain the first inequality and Lemma
5.2
for the second one. Choosing
\delta\in\left(0,-\dfrac{2N}{N!}\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}\right)
and
\epsilon
such that
{(3N-1)\frac{N-1}{N}+\epsilon}<3N-1
, we ensure that the integrand is bounded from above uniformly in
n
since
3N-1
is even and
\left(\dfrac{2N}{N!}+\delta\right)\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}<0
. This finishes the proof.

7
Proofs of strict local martingality

In this section, we prove the necessity in Theorem
3.1

(ii)
and Theorem
3.5

(ii)
. Unlike the “one-shot” proof of the martingale property in Section
6
, we first prove strict local martingality in the simpler one-dimensional case and then extend it to the multidimensional setting.

7.1
Proof of necessity in Theorem
3.1

(ii)

The proof of the theorem follows the ideas used by
Lions and Musiela (2007)
in the Markovian setting.

Fix
T>0
. According to Corollary
4.3
, it is enough to prove than
\mathbb{P}(\tau_{\infty}^{X}<T)>0
, where

\tau_{\infty}^{X}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau_{n}^{X},\quad\tau_{n}^{X}:=\inf\{t\geq 0\colon|X_{t}|=n\},

(7.1)

and
X=(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
satisfies

dX_{t}=dW_{t}+\left\langle\rho\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\right\rangle dt,\quad t\in[[0,\tau_{\infty}^{X}]],\quad X_{0}=0.

(7.2)

For the linear form of signature, the following inequality is a direct consequence of Lemma
5.1
:

\sup_{t\in[0,\,T]}\left|\left\langle\bm{\sigma}-\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N},\mathbb{\widehat{X}}_{t}\right\rangle\right|\leq C_{\bm{\sigma},T}\left(1+\sup_{t\in[0,\,T]}\left|X_{t}\right|^{N-1}\right)\quad a.s.

(7.3)

for some positive
C_{\bm{\sigma},T}>0
depending only on
T
and the coefficients
\bm{\sigma}
. This implies

\dfrac{\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}}{N!}X_{t}^{N}-C_{\bm{\sigma},T}\left(1+\sup_{s\in[0,\,t]}\left|X_{s}\right|^{N-1}\right)\leq\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\mathbb{\widehat{X}}_{t}\right\rangle\leq\dfrac{\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}}{N!}X_{t}^{N}+C_{\bm{\sigma},T}\left(1+\sup_{s\in[0,\,t]}\left|X_{s}\right|^{N-1}\right)\quad a.s.

(7.4)

for all
t\in[0,\,\tau^{X}_{\infty}]
.

In particular, it follows that
\lim\limits_{t\to\tau_{\infty}^{X}}\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\right\rangle^{2}=+\infty
on
\{\tau_{\infty}^{X}<\infty\}
, so it is sufficient to show that
\mathbb{P}(\tau_{\infty}^{X}<T)>0
.

For an arbitrary
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, define the event

{\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}=\left\{\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|W_{t}-\lambda t\right|\leq 1\right\},\quad\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}_{\lambda})>0.}

(7.5)

If
\left\langle\bm{\sigma}_{t},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\right\rangle
was a deterministic function of
X_{t}
, so that
X_{t}
was Markovian, we would write on
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}

X_{t}\geq\lambda t-1+\rho\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}_{s}\right\rangle\,ds

(7.6)

and apply the comparison theorem for ODEs to establish the explosion of
X_{t}
.
However, in general, it is not true, and the equation becomes path-dependent. Our strategy consists of isolating the term
\frac{\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}}{N!}X_{t}^{N}

\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\right\rangle=\dfrac{\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}}{N!}X_{t}^{N}+\left\langle\bm{\sigma}-\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\right\rangle

(7.7)

and bounding the remainder using (
7.4
). Then, we compare the solutions on
[\tau_{n}^{X},\tau_{n+1}^{X})
, bounding
\max\limits_{s\in[0,t]}|X_{s}|^{N-1}
by
(n+1)^{N-1}
, and apply the comparison principle for ODEs on this interval. This leads to a bound on
(\tau_{n+1}^{X}-\tau_{n}^{X})
, which is used in its turn to bound
\tau_{\infty}^{X}
on
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}
.

Assume first that
\rho\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}>0
.
By (
7.4
), for
t\in[\tau_{n}^{X},\tau_{n+1}^{X})
, we have on
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}

\displaystyle X_{t}

\displaystyle\geq X_{\tau_{n}^{X}}+(W_{t}-W_{\tau_{n}^{X}})+\dfrac{\rho\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}}{N!}\int_{\tau_{n}^{X}}^{t}X_{s}^{N}\,ds-|\rho|C_{\bm{\sigma},T}\int_{\tau_{n}^{X}}^{t}\left(1+\sup_{u\in[0,\,s]}\left|X_{u}\right|^{N-1}\right)\,ds

(7.8)

\displaystyle\geq n+\lambda(t-\tau_{n}^{X})-2+\dfrac{\rho\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}}{N!}\int_{\tau_{n}^{X}}^{t}X_{s}^{N}\,ds-|\rho|C_{\bm{\sigma},T}\left(1+(n+1)^{N-1}\right)(t-\tau_{n}^{X}).

(7.9)

This allows us to apply the comparison theorem for ODEs, so that
X_{t}\geq Y_{n}(t)
on
[\tau_{n}^{X},\tau_{n+1}^{X})
, where
Y_{n}(t)
satisfies

\dot{Y}_{n}(t)=F_{n}(Y_{n}(t)),\quad Y_{n}(\tau_{n}^{X})=n-2,

(7.10)

where

F_{n}(x):=\lambda-|\rho|C_{\bm{\sigma},T}\left(1+(n+1)^{N-1}\right)+\dfrac{\rho\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}}{N!}x^{N}

At the left endpoints of the intervals
[\tau_{n}^{X},\tau_{n+1}^{X})
, the derivatives

\dot{Y}_{n}(\tau_{n}^{X})=F_{n}(n-2):=\lambda-|\rho|C_{\bm{\sigma},T}\left(1+(n+1)^{N-1}\right)+\dfrac{\rho\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}}{N!}(n-2)^{N}

are uniformly bounded from below in
n
, since
\rho\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}>0
.
Hence, one can choose
\lambda
sufficiently large so that
\dot{Y}_{n}(\tau_{n}^{X})\geq 0
for all
n\geq 0
.

Moreover, as each
F_{n}(\cdot)
is increasing and
F_{n}(Y_{n}(\tau_{n}^{X}))>0
, it follows that
F_{n}(Y_{n}(t))>0
for all
t\geq\tau_{n}^{X}
.
Consequently, the solutions
(Y_{n}(t))_{t\geq\tau_{n}^{X}}
remain increasing and satisfy
Y_{n}(t)\geq n-2
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\in[\tau_{n}^{X},\tau_{n+1}^{X})
.
Therefore,

Y_{n}(t)\geq n-2+\left(\lambda+\dfrac{\rho\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}}{N!}(n-2)_{+}^{N}-|\rho|C_{\bm{\sigma},T}\left(1+(n+1)^{N-1}\right)\right)(t-\tau_{n}^{X}).

(7.11)

Hence, on
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}
, the following estimate holds

\tau_{n+1}^{X}-\tau_{n}^{X}\leq\tau_{n+1}^{Y}-\tau_{n}^{X}\leq\dfrac{3}{\lambda+\frac{\rho\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}}{N!}(n-2)_{+}^{N}-|\rho|C_{\bm{\sigma},T}\left(1+(n+1)^{N-1}\right)}

(7.12)

and

\tau_{\infty}^{X}=\sum_{n\geq 0}(\tau_{n+1}^{X}-\tau_{n}^{X})\leq\sum_{n\geq 0}\dfrac{3}{\lambda+\frac{\rho\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}}{N!}(n-2)_{+}^{N}-|\rho|C_{\bm{\sigma},T}\left(1+(n+1)^{N-1}\right)}.

(7.13)

The series converges since
N\geq 2
and the its sum can be made arbitrary small by choosing
\lambda
large enough.

For the remaining case of even
N
and
\rho\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}<0
, we take
\lambda<0
large enough, so that
X_{\tau_{n}^{X}}=-n
on
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}
, and we have on
[\tau_{n}^{X},\tau_{n+1}^{X})

\displaystyle X_{t}

\displaystyle\leq X_{\tau_{n}^{X}}+(W_{t}-W_{\tau_{n}^{X}})+\dfrac{\rho\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}}{N!}\int_{\tau_{n}^{X}}^{t}X_{s}^{N}\,ds+|\rho|C_{\bm{\sigma},T}\int_{\tau_{n}^{X}}^{t}\left(1+\sup_{u\in[0,\,s]}\left|X_{u}\right|^{N-1}\right)\,ds

(7.14)

\displaystyle\leq-n+\lambda(t-\tau_{n}^{X})+2+\dfrac{\rho\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}}{N!}\int_{\tau_{n}^{X}}^{t}X_{s}^{N}\,ds+|\rho|C_{\bm{\sigma},T}\left(1+(n+1)^{N-1}\right)(t-\tau_{n}^{X}).

(7.15)

As
\dfrac{\rho\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}}{N!}X_{s}^{N}<0
, using the same argument as before, we can choose
\lambda(T)
in order to ensure that
X_{t}
blows up to
-\infty
on
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}
before
T
for any
T>0
.

7.2
Proof of necessity in Theorem
3.5

(ii)

We prove the result for the case where
\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}>0
showing that
X
blows up to
+\infty
with positive probability. The case where
\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}<0
and

is even is treated similarly by showing that
X
blows up to
-\infty
.

The idea of this proof is to extend the proof of strict local martingality in Section
7
by finding a suitable event
\mathcal{A}
of positive probability such that the solution of (
4.2
) blows up before
T>0
on
\mathcal{A}
. We achieve this by keeping the elements of the signature containing
Z
(i.e., those corresponding to words containing the letters
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{3}}},\ldots,{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{d+2}}}
) small, so that the solution of the SDE remains close to the one considered in the one-dimensional case. More precisely, we define

\tau_{n}^{X}=\inf\{t\geq t_{0}\colon X_{t}=n\},

and we fix
t_{0}>0
,
M\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\epsilon
such that
N\epsilon<1
. We require that on
\mathcal{A}
, the following inequalities hold:

\displaystyle X_{t_{0}}

\displaystyle\geq M,

(7.16)

\displaystyle|\langle{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X}_{t_{0}}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq 1,\quad\text{for all ${{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}$ containing at least one of the letters ${{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{3}}},\ldots,{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{d+2}}}$;}

(7.17)

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[\tau_{n}^{X},\tau_{n+1}^{X}{\land\tilde{\tau}_{n+1}^{X}]}}|\langle{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X}_{s}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq n^{N_{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}}({{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}})+\epsilon|{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}|},\quad\text{for $n\geq M$ and for all words ${{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}$ such that $|{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}|\leq N$;}

(7.18)

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[\tau_{n}^{X},\tau_{n+1}^{X}{\land\tilde{\tau}_{n+1}^{X}]}}|B_{s}-B_{\tau_{n}}|

\displaystyle\leq n^{-N/2+\epsilon},\quad\text{for $n\geq M$,}

(7.19)

where
\tilde{\tau}_{n+1}^{X}:=\tau_{n}^{X}+Cn^{-N}
for some positive constant
C=C_{\bm{\sigma},N}>0
to be determined later.

Step 1: Proving the explosion on
\mathcal{A}
.

Conditional on this event, we can use ODE comparison on the dynamics of
X
to choose
C>0
such that

\tau_{n+1}^{X}-\tau_{n}^{X}\leq Cn^{-N},\quad n\geq M,

(7.20)

so that
\tau_{n+1}^{X}\land\tilde{\tau}_{n+1}^{X}=\tau_{n+1}^{X}
on
\mathcal{A}
for
n\geq M
.
Indeed, for
t\in[\tau_{n}^{X},\tau_{n}^{X}+Cn^{-N}]

\displaystyle X_{t}

\displaystyle=X_{\tau_{n}^{X}}+\int_{\tau_{n}^{X}}^{t}\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\mathbb{\widehat{Y}^{X}}_{s}\right\rangle\,ds+B_{t}-B_{\tau_{n}^{X}}

\displaystyle\geq X_{\tau_{n}^{X}}+\frac{\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}}{N!}\int_{\tau_{n}^{X}}^{t}X_{s}^{N}ds-C_{\bm{\sigma}}n^{(N-1)+\epsilon N}(t-\tau_{n}^{X})-n^{-N/2+\epsilon}

\displaystyle\geq n-\left(C_{\bm{\sigma}}Cn^{N\epsilon-1}+n^{-N/2+\epsilon}\right)+\frac{\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}}{N!}\int_{\tau_{n}^{X}}^{t}X_{s}^{N}ds,

By the comparison theorem for ODEs, this implies that
\tau_{n+1}^{X}-\tau_{n}^{X}\leq{\tau}_{(n-1)\to(n+1)}^{x}
, where
{\tau}_{(n-1)\to(n+1)}^{x}
is the time needed for the ODE

\dot{x}(t)=\frac{\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}}{N!}x^{N}(t),

to go from
(n-1)
to
(n+1)
. Since the coefficient
\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}
is positive, a straightforward computation gives

{\tau}_{(n-1)\to(n+1)}^{x}=\left(\frac{N!}{(N-1)\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}}\right)\left((n-1)^{1-N}-(n+1)^{1-N}\right)\leq Cn^{-N}

(7.21)

for a suitable choice of
C>0
. It then follows that, on the event
\mathcal{A}
, we have

\tau_{\infty}\leq t_{0}+C\sum\limits_{n\geq M}n^{-N}.

Since
t_{0}
and
M
in (
7.16
)–(
7.17
) are arbitrary, they can be chosen so that
\tau_{\infty}<T
on
\mathcal{A}
.
It remains to show that
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A})>0
.

Step 2: Showing that
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A})>0
.

First, we note that

\mathcal{A}=A_{M}\cap\bigcap_{n\geq M}\left(A_{n}^{B}\cap\bigcap_{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}:\,|{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}|\leq N}A_{n}^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}}\right),

where the event
A_{M}
corresponds to the inequalities (
7.16
)–(
7.17
),
A_{n}^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}}
corresponds to (
7.18
) for fixed
n
and
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}
, and
A_{n}^{B}
corresponds to (
7.19
) for a given
n
.

We first claim that
\mathbb{P}\left(A_{M}\right)>0
. By the support theorem of
Stroock and Varadhan (1972, Theorem 5.1)
, it suffices to show that, for a suitable choice of
(w,z^{1},\ldots,z^{d})
(deterministic smooth functions on
[0,T]
), the corresponding deterministic dynamics (i.e. where
W
,
Z^{i}
are replaced by
w,z^{i}
in (
4.2
)) satisfy the conditions. Taking
z^{1}\equiv\cdots\equiv z^{d}\equiv 0
clearly leads to (
7.17
). Further taking
w_{t}=\lambda t
leads to (
7.16
) by the analysis of Section
7.1
. It follows that
\mathbb{P}\left(A_{M}\right)>0
.

We define

A_{n+1}:=A_{n}\cap\left(\bigcap_{|{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}|\leq N}A^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}}_{n+1}\right)\cap A^{B}_{n+1},\quad n\geq M.

It is enough to prove that, if
M
is large enough, for each
n\geq M
, we have

\mathbb{P}(A_{n+1}^{c}|A_{n})\leq ce^{-cn^{\delta}},

(7.22)

for some
c,\delta>0
. Indeed, since
A_{n+1}\subset A_{n}
and
\mathcal{A}=\bigcap\limits_{n\geq M}A_{n}
, we write

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A})=\mathbb{P}(A_{M})\prod_{n\geq M}\mathbb{P}(A_{n+1}|A_{n}),

(7.23)

so that, if (
7.22
) is verified, we have

\log(\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}))=\log(\mathbb{P}(A_{M}))+\sum_{n\geq M}\log(\mathbb{P}(A_{n+1}|A_{n}))\geq\log(\mathbb{P}(A_{M}))+\sum_{n\geq M}\log(1-ce^{-cn^{\delta}}).

As
n\to\infty
,
\log(1-ce^{-cn^{\delta}})\sim ce^{-cn^{\delta}}
, and the series converges. Thus, we obtain
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A})>0
.

Note that since

\mathbb{P}(A_{n+1}^{c}|A_{n})=\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcup_{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}:\,|{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}|\leq N}(A_{n+1}^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}})^{c}\cup(A_{n+1}^{B})^{c}\Bigg|\,A_{n}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}((A_{n+1}^{B})^{c}|A_{n})+\sum_{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}:\,|{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}|\leq N}\mathbb{P}((A_{n+1}^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}})^{c}|A_{n}),

the condition (
7.22
) is stable under finite intersections, and it suffices to prove (
7.22
) for
\mathbb{P}((A_{n+1}^{B})^{c}|A_{n})
and for each of the
\mathbb{P}((A_{n+1}^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}})^{c}|A_{n})
.

The bound (
7.22
) for
\mathbb{P}(A_{n+1}^{B}|A_{n})
follows from Gaussian scaling and Gaussian tail estimates for the Brownian motion
B
.
Thus, it remains to establish (
7.22
) for the events
A_{n+1}^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}}
, which we proceed to do via induction on the length of the word
|{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}|
.

For
|{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}|=1
, we consider three cases. If
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}={{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{1}}}
, (
7.18
) holds for sufficiently large
n
due to the bound (
7.20
) on
\tau_{n}^{X}
. If
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}={{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}
, (
7.18
) follows directly from the definition of
\tau_{n}^{X}
for large
n
. In the remaining case, where
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}\in\{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{3}}},\ldots,{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{d+2}}}\}
, (
7.22
) follows from Gaussian tail estimates, as was previously done for the probability
\mathbb{P}(A_{n+1}^{B}|A_{n})
.

Now, suppose that
|{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}|>1
and that (
7.22
) holds for all words
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}}
such that
|{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}}|<|{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}|
.

By Proposition
5.4
, we can write
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}
as a linear combination of shuffle products and we consider three cases.

The first case consists of words of the form
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}={{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w_{1}}}}\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.2mu\sqcup}\ldots\mathrel{\sqcup\mkern-3.2mu\sqcup}{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w_{k}}}}
with
k\geq 2
. The result then follows from the induction hypothesis for
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w_{j}}}}
, since on
\bigcap\limits_{j=1}^{k}A_{n+1}^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w_{j}}}}}
we have

\sup_{s\in[\tau_{n}^{X},\tau_{n+1}^{X}{\land\tilde{\tau}_{n+1}^{X}}]}|\langle{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X}_{s}\rangle|\leq\prod_{j=1}^{k}\sup_{s\in[\tau_{n}^{X},\tau_{n+1}^{X}{\land\tilde{\tau}_{n+1}^{X}}]}|\langle{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w_{j}}}},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X}_{s}\rangle|\leq\prod_{j=1}^{k}n^{N_{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}}({{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w_{j}}}})+\epsilon|{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w_{j}}}}|}=n^{N_{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}}({{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}})+\epsilon|{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}|}.

This implies that
A_{n+1}^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}}\subset\bigcap\limits_{j=1}^{k}A_{n+1}^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w_{j}}}}}
.

The second case concerns words of the form
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}}{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{1}}}
, which is again handled by the induction hypothesis for
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}}
. Indeed, conditional on
A_{n}
and
A_{n+1}^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}}}
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[\tau_{n}^{X},\tau_{n+1}^{X}{\land\tilde{\tau}_{n+1}^{X}}]}|\langle{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X}_{s}\rangle|

\displaystyle=\sup_{s\in[\tau_{n}^{X},\tau_{n+1}^{X}{\land\tilde{\tau}_{n+1}^{X}}]}\left|\langle{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X}_{\tau_{n}^{X}}\rangle+\int_{\tau_{n}^{X}}^{s}\langle{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X}_{s}\rangle ds\right|

\displaystyle\leq(n-1)^{N_{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}}({{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}})+\epsilon|{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}|}+({\tilde{\tau}_{n+1}^{X}}-\tau_{n}^{X})n^{N_{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}}({{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}})+\epsilon|{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}}|}

\displaystyle\leq(n-1)^{N_{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}}({{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}})+\epsilon|{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}|}+Cn^{-N+N_{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}}({{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}})+\epsilon|{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}}|}

\displaystyle\leq(n-1)^{N_{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}}({{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}})+\epsilon|{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}|}+Cn^{-1+\epsilon(N-1)},

which is bounded by
n^{N_{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}}({{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}})+\epsilon|{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}|}
for sufficiently large
n
(recall that
M
can be chosen large enough).

The last case corresponds to
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}={{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}}{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{i}}}
for
i\in\{3,\ldots,d+2\}
.
We write

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}((A_{n+1}^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}})^{c}|A_{n})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}((A_{n+1}^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}})^{c},(A_{n+1}^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}}})^{c}|A_{n})+\mathbb{P}((A_{n+1}^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}})^{c},A_{n+1}^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}}}|A_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}((A_{n+1}^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}}})^{c}|A_{n})+\mathbb{P}((A_{n+1}^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}})^{c},A_{n+1}^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}}}|A_{n}),

and note that (
7.22
) holds for the first term by the induction hypothesis for
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}}
.
For
s\geq\tau_{n}
, we have

\langle{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X}_{s}\rangle=\langle{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X}_{\tau_{n}^{X}}\rangle+\int_{\tau_{n}}^{s}\langle{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X}_{s}\rangle{\circ}dZ^{i-2}_{s}.

The first term and the Stratonovich correction are bounded on
A_{n}
and
A_{n+1}^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}}}
as in the previous case. Thus, it suffices to prove the bound for the conditional probability of the event

\left\{\sup_{t\in[\tau_{n}^{X},\tau_{n+1}^{X}{\land\tilde{\tau}_{n+1}^{X}}]}\int_{\tau_{n}^{X}}^{t}\langle{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X}_{s}\rangle dZ^{i-2}_{s}\geq n^{N_{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}}({{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}})+\epsilon|{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}|-1}\right\}.

We use the exponential inequality

\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[\tau_{1},\tau_{2}]}\left|\int_{\tau_{1}}^{t}\gamma_{s}dW_{s}\right|\geq\lambda,\;\sup_{t\in[\tau_{1},\tau_{2}]}|\gamma_{t}|\leq\eta\right)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2\eta^{2}h}\right),

(7.24)

which holds for all stopping times
\tau_{1}\leq\tau_{2}\leq\tau_{1}+h
, any
h>0
, and any progressively measurable
\gamma
. Indeed, it follows from

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[\tau_{1},\tau_{2}]}\left|\int_{\tau_{1}}^{t}\gamma_{s}dW_{s}\right|\geq\lambda,\;\sup_{t\in[\tau_{1},\tau_{2}]}|\gamma_{t}|\leq\eta\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[\tau_{1},\tau_{2}]}\left|\int_{\tau_{1}}^{t}\gamma_{s}dW_{s}\right|\geq\lambda,\;\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}|\gamma_{s}|^{2}\,ds\leq\eta^{2}h\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\geq 0}M_{t}\geq\lambda,\;\langle M\rangle_{\infty}\leq\eta^{2}h\right)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2\eta^{2}h}\right),

where we applied the exponential Bernstein inequality (cf.
Revuz and Yor (1999, IV, 3.16)
) to the martingale

M_{t}:=\int_{\tau_{1}}^{(\tau_{1}+t)\land\tau_{2}}\gamma_{s}\,dW_{s},\ t\geq 0.

Applying the exponential inequality (
7.24
) with
h=Cn^{-N}
, we then obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\left(\sup_{t\in[\tau_{n}^{X},\tau_{n+1}^{X}{\land\tilde{\tau}_{n+1}^{X}}]}\int_{\tau_{n}}^{t}\langle{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X}_{s}\rangle dZ^{i}_{s}\geq n^{N_{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}}({{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}})+\epsilon|{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}|-1},A^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}}}_{n+1}\Big|A_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[\tau_{n}^{X},\tau_{n}^{X}{\land\tilde{\tau}_{n+1}^{X}}]}\int_{\tau_{n}}^{t}\langle{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X}_{s}\rangle{\circ}dZ^{i}_{s}\geq n^{N_{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}}({{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}})+\epsilon|{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}|-1},\;\sup_{t\in[\tau_{n}^{X},\tau_{n}^{X}{\land\tilde{\tau}_{n+1}^{X}]}}\left|\langle{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u}}},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{X}_{t}\rangle\right|\leq n^{N_{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}}({{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}})+\epsilon(|{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}|-1)}\Big|A_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\frac{n^{N-2(1-\epsilon)}}{2{C}}\right).

Thus, (
7.22
) holds for
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}}
. This completes the proof.

8
Proof of Theorem
3.2
: Moments explosion

Without loss of generality, we will assume that
N
is odd and

\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}>0\mbox{ and }\rho<0.

The proof for the remaining case
\sigma^{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}^{\otimes N}}<0
and
\rho>0
is analogous.

By Itô’s formula,

\displaystyle S_{T}^{m}

\displaystyle=S_{0}^{m}\exp\left(m\int_{0}^{T}\sigma_{s}\,dB_{s}-\dfrac{m}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\sigma_{s}^{2}\,ds\right)

(8.1)

\displaystyle=S_{0}^{m}\exp\left(\rho m\int_{0}^{T}\sigma_{s}\,dW_{s}+\bar{\rho}m\int_{0}^{T}\sigma_{s}\,dW_{s}^{\perp}-\dfrac{m}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\sigma_{s}^{2}\,ds\right),

(8.2)

As
\sigma_{t}=\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{t}\right\rangle
is adapted to the filtration generated by
W
, conditioning with respect to
W
implies

\displaystyle S_{0}^{-m}\mathbb{E}\left[S_{T}^{m}\right]

\displaystyle=\;\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\rho m\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s}\right\rangle\,dW_{s}+\frac{\bar{\rho}^{2}m^{2}-m}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s}\right\rangle^{2}\,ds\right)\right].

We then proceed as in
Gassiat (2018)
and apply the
Boué and Dupuis (1998)
formula (more precisely, a variant allowing unbounded functionals, see Appendix
A
)
to obtain

\displaystyle\ln{\mathbb{E}}\left[S_{T}^{m}/S^{m}_{0}\right]

\displaystyle=\sup_{(u_{t})_{t\geq 0}\in\mathcal{U}_{q}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(-\frac{u_{s}^{2}}{2}+\rho m\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}^{u}_{s}\right\rangle u_{s}+\frac{\bar{\rho}^{2}m^{2}-m}{2}\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}^{u}_{s}\right\rangle^{2}\right)ds\right]

(8.3)

\displaystyle=\sup_{(u_{t})_{t\geq 0}\in\mathcal{U}_{q}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(\frac{m^{2}-m}{2}\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}^{u}_{s}\right\rangle^{2}-\frac{\left(u_{s}-\rho m\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}^{u}_{s}\right\rangle\right)^{2}}{2}\right)ds\right]

\displaystyle=:\sup_{u\in\mathcal{U}_{q}}\mathcal{J}(u;\rho,m,T)\;=:\mathcal{V}(\rho,m,T),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{q}=\left\{(u_{t})_{t\geq 0}\mbox{ progressively measurable with }{\mathbb{E}}\left[\int_{0}^{T}|u_{t}|^{q}dt\right]<+\infty\right\},

(8.4)

for some fixed
q\geq 2N
,
and
\widehat{\mathbb{X}}^{u}_{t}
denotes the signature of
\widehat{X}^{u}_{t}=(t,X^{u}_{t})
with
X^{u}_{t}
defined by

X^{u}_{t}=W_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}u_{s}ds.

The finiteness of the moments is therefore equivalent to the control problem having a finite value. We now proceed to prove that, depending on the position of
\rho
relative to
-\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{m}}
, the value will either be finite or infinite.

8.1
Proof of Theorem
3.2
(i): negative result

In this subsection we assume that

-\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{m}}<\rho<0,

and we will show that
\mathcal{V}(\rho,m,T)=+\infty
.
The idea is simple: we take
u
of feedback form

u_{t}=\lambda\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}^{u}_{t}\right\rangle

for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.

In that case, we denote by
X^{\lambda}
the solution of the signature SDE

dX^{\lambda}_{t}=dW_{t}+\lambda\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}^{\lambda}_{t}\right\rangle dt,

and we denote its signature by
\widehat{\mathbb{X}}^{\lambda}_{t}
. This solution exists (up to a possible explosion time) by Proposition
4.1
,
the value (
8.3
) corresponding to the control is simply

P(\lambda)\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}^{\lambda}_{s}\right\rangle^{2}ds\right],

with

P(\lambda)=\frac{m^{2}-m}{2}-\frac{(\lambda-\rho m)^{2}}{2}.

The assumption on
\rho
is exactly equivalent to
P(0)>0
, which guarantees that there exists
\lambda
s.t.

\lambda>0\mbox{ and }P(\lambda)>0.

(8.5)

As shown in the proof in Section
7
, the first inequality
\lambda>0
will imply that
X^{\lambda}
blows up with positive probability, which with the help of the second one implies that the problem value (
8.3
) is infinite.

The above argument is not completely rigorous, since the feedback control is only defined up to the explosion time and clearly not in
\mathcal{U}_{q}
. This can be dealt with by a simple truncation argument : for
R>0
, let

\tau_{R,\lambda}=\inf\{t\geq 0:|X_{t}^{\lambda}|=R\}

and take

u^{\lambda,R}_{t}=\begin{cases}\lambda\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}^{\lambda}_{t}\right\rangle,&t<\tau_{R,\lambda}\\
0,&t\geq\tau_{R,\lambda}\end{cases}

Then, by Lemma
5.2
,
u^{\lambda,R}
is in
\mathcal{U}_{q}
(for any
q\geq 2
), and

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}(\rho,m,T)\geq\mathcal{J}(u^{\lambda,R};\rho,m,T)

\displaystyle=P(\lambda)\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau_{R,\lambda}}\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}^{\lambda}_{s}\right\rangle^{2}ds\right]+P(0)\mathbb{E}\left[\int^{T}_{T\wedge\tau_{R,\lambda}}\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}_{s}^{u^{\lambda,R}}\right\rangle^{2}ds\right].

Taking the supremum over
R
, we obtain

\mathcal{V}(\rho,m,T)\geq P(\lambda){\mathbb{E}}\left[\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau_{\infty,\lambda}}\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}^{\lambda}_{s}\right\rangle^{2}ds\right]

where
\tau_{\infty,\lambda}
is the explosion time of
X^{\lambda}
. However, the r.h.s is infinite since the event
\{\tau_{\infty,\lambda}<T\}
has positive probability (by the results of Section
7.1
), and on this event
\int_{0}^{\tau_{\infty,\lambda}}\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}^{\lambda}_{s}\right\rangle^{2}ds=+\infty
(by Proposition
4.1
).

8.2
Proof of Theorem
3.2
(ii): positive result

We assume now that the condition

\rho<-\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{m}},

(8.6)

is verified and fix
(u_{t})_{t\geq 0}\in\mathcal{U}_{q}
. We want to bound

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(-\frac{u_{s}^{2}}{2}+\rho m\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}^{u}_{s}\right\rangle u_{s}+\frac{\bar{\rho}^{2}m^{2}-m}{2}\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}^{u}_{s}\right\rangle^{2}\right)ds\right].

(8.7)

Define

U_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}u_{s}\,ds.

(8.8)

We denote by
\widehat{Y}^{U}_{t}=(t,U_{t},W_{t})
and we denote its signature by
\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{U}_{t}
. Since this is a
3
-dimensional process, this signature is in the dual of the tensor algebra over words with letters
{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{1}}},{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}},{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{3}}}
. Since
X=U+W
, we can rewrite the linear forms in terms of
\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{U}_{t}
. Namely, there exists
\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}\in T^{N}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, such that
\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}^{u}_{t}\right\rangle=\left\langle\tilde{\bm{\sigma}},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{U}_{t}\right\rangle
.

In the expectation (
8.7
), the term with the highest degree in
U
is

Note that

We return to the value in (

Step 1. Bounding the third term.

Using the same techniques as in the proof of Theorem

, we can bound the third term

from above by

For the second term

For the second term, we claim that it holds, for any

{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{j}}}

\mathbb{E}[|U_{T}|^{N_{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}}({{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}})+\epsilon}]+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u_{i}}}},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{U}_{t}\right\rangle dt\right|^{\frac{N_{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}}({{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}})+\epsilon}{N_{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}}({{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{u_{i}}}})+\epsilon_{1,i}}}\right]+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{v_{j}}}},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{U}_{t}\right\rangle\circ dW_{t}\right|^{\frac{N_{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}}({{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}})+\epsilon}{N_{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}}({{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{v_{j}}}})+\epsilon_{2,j}}}\right],

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{v_{j}}}},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{U}_{t}\right\rangle dW_{t}\right|^{\frac{N_{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}}({{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{w}}})+\epsilon}{N_{{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{2}}}}({{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{v_{j}}}})+\epsilon_{2,j}}}\right]

To conclude, combining (

), (

), and (

Proof of Proposition
3.4

Without loss of generality, we assume that
\alpha>0
and

\rho=-\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{m}}<0.

In this case, equation (
8.3
) becomes

\displaystyle\ln\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{S_{T}^{m}}{S_{0}^{m}}\right]

\displaystyle=\sup_{(u_{t})_{t\geq 0}\in\mathcal{U}_{q}}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(-\frac{u_{t}^{2}}{2}-\kappa\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}^{u}_{t}\right\rangle u_{t}\right)dt\right],

where
\kappa:=\rho m=\sqrt{m^{2}-m}
.

In the case
\bm{\sigma}=\alpha{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{222}}}+\beta{{\color[rgb]{0.06,0.46,1}\mathbf{221}}}
, which we consider here, the objective functional can be expressed more explicitly as

\mathbb{E}\left[-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}u_{t}^{2}\,dt-\frac{\alpha\kappa}{6}\int_{0}^{T}(B_{t}+U_{t})^{3}\,dU_{t}-\frac{\beta\kappa}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t}(B_{s}+U_{s})^{2}\,ds\,dU_{t}\right].

(8.19)

By Itô’s formula, the expectation of the middle term equals

\mathbb{E}\left[-\frac{\alpha\kappa}{6}\int_{0}^{T}(B_{t}+U_{t})^{3}\,dU_{t}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[-\frac{\alpha\kappa}{24}(B_{T}+U_{T})^{4}+\frac{\alpha\kappa}{4}\int_{0}^{T}(B_{t}+U_{t})^{2}\,dt\right],

so that the objective functional becomes

\mathbb{E}\left[-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}u_{t}^{2}\,dt-\frac{\alpha\kappa}{24}(B_{T}+U_{T})^{4}+\frac{\alpha\kappa}{4}\int_{0}^{T}(B_{t}+U_{t})^{2}\,dt-\frac{\beta\kappa}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t}(B_{s}+U_{s})^{2}\,ds\,dU_{t}\right].

(8.20)

Case
\beta=0
.

To show the finiteness of moments, we take
u\in\mathcal{U}_{q}
and decompose it in the following way

u_{t}=\dfrac{1}{T}U_{T}+\tilde{u}_{t},\quad U_{t}=\dfrac{t}{T}U_{T}+\tilde{U}_{t},

where
\tilde{u}_{t}:=u_{t}-\dfrac{1}{T}U_{T}
verifies
\tilde{U}_{T}=\int_{0}^{T}\tilde{u}_{t}\,dt=0
. Using the inequality

(a+b)^{2}\leq(1+\epsilon)a^{2}+(1+\epsilon^{-1})b^{2},\quad\epsilon>0,

(8.21)

we can bound the third term in (
8.20
) as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\alpha\kappa}{4}\int_{0}^{T}(B_{t}+U_{t})^{2}\,dt\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\alpha\kappa}{4}\int_{0}^{T}\left(B_{t}+\dfrac{t}{T}U_{T}+\tilde{U}_{t}\right)^{2}\,dt\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\alpha\kappa}{4}\int_{0}^{T}\left((1+\epsilon^{-1})\left(B_{t}+\dfrac{t}{T}U_{T}\right)^{2}+(1+\epsilon)\tilde{U}_{t}^{2}\right)\,dt\right]

\displaystyle\leq(1+\epsilon^{-1})\frac{\alpha\kappa}{4}\mathbb{E}\left[{T^{2}}+\dfrac{2}{3}TU_{T}^{2}\right]+(1+\epsilon)\frac{\alpha\kappa}{4}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\tilde{U}_{t}^{2}\,dt\right].

Since
\tilde{u}_{t}=\tilde{U}_{t}^{\prime}
and
\tilde{U}_{0}=\tilde{U}_{T}=0
, we can apply the Wirtinger’s inequality
(
Hardy et al., 1952
, Chapter 7.7, 257)

\int_{0}^{T}\tilde{U}_{t}^{2}\,dt\leq\frac{T^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}\tilde{u}_{t}^{2}\,dt,

so that the third term is controlled by

\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\alpha\kappa}{4}\int_{0}^{T}(B_{t}+U_{t})^{2}\,dt\right]\leq C+C_{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}[U_{T}^{2}]+(1+\epsilon)\frac{\alpha\kappa T^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\tilde{u}_{t}^{2}\,dt\right],

(8.22)

for some
C,C_{\epsilon}>0
.

Similarly, one can control the first term in (
8.20
) by

-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}u_{t}^{2}\,dt\right]\leq\left(-1+\epsilon\right)\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\tilde{u}_{t}^{2}\,dt\right]+\dfrac{1}{2}\left(-1+\epsilon^{-1}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\dfrac{1}{T}U_{T}^{2}\right].

(8.23)

Combining (
8.23
) and (
8.22
), and noting that

\mathbb{E}[U_{T}^{2}]
is dominated by the second term in (
8.20
), we conclude that
the objective functional (
8.20
) is bounded by

C_{0,\epsilon}+\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left(\!(-1+\epsilon)\frac{1}{2}+(1+\epsilon)\frac{\alpha\kappa T^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\!\right)\int_{0}^{T}\tilde{u}_{t}^{2}\,dt\right],

for some constant
C_{0,\epsilon}>0
.
The coefficient in front of
\int_{0}^{T}\tilde{u}_{t}^{2}\,dt
is negative if

T^{2}<\left(\dfrac{1-\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}\right)\dfrac{2\pi^{2}}{\alpha\kappa},

which holds for sufficiently small
\epsilon
whenever

T^{2}<\dfrac{2\pi^{2}}{\alpha\kappa}.

This establishes the finiteness of the moments.

To show the explosion, consider a deterministic control

U^{\lambda}_{t}=\lambda\psi(t),

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
\psi\in C^{1}([0,\,T])
satisfies

\psi(0)=\psi(T)=0
and

\int_{0}^{T}\psi(t)^{2}\,dt=\frac{T^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}\psi^{\prime}(t)^{2}\,dt>0.

These conditions are verified, for instance, by
\psi(t)=\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi t}{T}\right)
. Hence, the objective functional (
8.20
) becomes

\mathbb{E}\left[-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\psi^{\prime}(t)^{2}\,dt-\frac{\alpha\kappa}{24}B_{T}^{4}+\frac{\alpha\kappa}{4}\int_{0}^{T}(B_{t}+\lambda\psi(t))^{2}\,dt\right].

This expression is quadratic in
\lambda
, with leading coefficient

\left(\dfrac{\alpha\kappa T^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)\int_{0}^{T}\psi^{\prime}(t)^{2}\,dt.

Letting
\lambda\to\infty
, we conclude that the value function diverges to infinity whenever

T^{2}>\dfrac{2\pi^{2}}{\alpha\kappa}.

Case
\beta\neq 0
.

It remains to show that the value function is infinite when
\beta\neq 0
.
Consider the deterministic control

U^{\lambda}_{t}=\lambda\phi(t),

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
\phi\in C^{1}([0,\,T])
satisfies
\phi(0)=\phi(T)=0
and

\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\phi(s)^{2}\,ds\right)\phi^{\prime}(t)\,dt\neq 0.

By integration by parts, the latter condition is equivalent to

\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\phi(s)^{2}\,ds\right)\phi^{\prime}(t)\,dt=\phi(T)\int_{0}^{T}\phi(s)^{2}\,ds-\int_{0}^{T}\phi(t)\,d\!\left(\int_{0}^{t}\phi(s)^{2}\,ds\right)=-\int_{0}^{T}\phi(s)^{3}\,ds\neq 0.

Substituting this control into (
8.20
), we see that the objective functional becomes

\mathbb{E}\left[-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\phi^{\prime}(t)^{2}\,dt-\frac{\alpha\kappa}{24}B_{T}^{4}+\frac{\alpha\kappa}{4}\int_{0}^{T}(B_{t}+\lambda\phi(t))^{2}\,dt-\frac{\beta\kappa}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{0}^{t}(B_{s}+\lambda\phi(s))^{2}\,ds\right)\lambda\phi^{\prime}(t)\,dt\right].

This expression is a cubic polynomial in
\lambda
, whose leading coefficient is

-\dfrac{\beta\kappa}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\phi(s)^{2}\,ds\right)\phi^{\prime}(t)\,dt\neq 0,

and is therefore unbounded from above as
\lambda\to\pm\infty
.
This completes the proof.

Proposition A.1

F:C([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
be (Borel-)measurable and such that

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|F\left(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}u\right)\right|\right]<\infty,\quad u\in\mathcal{U}_{q}.

(A.1)

Then it holds that

\ln\mathbb{E}\left[e^{F(W)}\right]=\sup_{u\in\mathcal{U}_{q}}\mathbb{E}\left[F\left(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}u\right)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}u_{s}^{2}ds\right].

Proof.

We proceed as in
(
Hariya and Watanabe, 2022
, Remark 1.2 (1))
by a truncation argument. For
n\geq 0
, let
F_{n}=F\wedge n
, then it holds that
\mathbb{E}[|F_{n}(W)|]
and
\mathbb{E}[e^{F_{n}(W)}]
are finite, so that we can apply the results of
Hariya and Watanabe (2022)
to obtain

\ln\mathbb{E}\left[e^{F_{n}(W)}\right]=\sup_{u\in\mathcal{U}_{q}}\mathbb{E}\left[F_{n}\left(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}u\right)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}u_{s}^{2}ds\right]

(They prove that the above formula holds for the supremum taken over both
\mathcal{U}_{2}
and the set of bounded controls
\mathcal{U}_{b}
, see Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 2.11 therein. This implies the case of arbitrary
q\geq 2
since
\mathcal{U}_{b}\subset\mathcal{U}_{q}\subset\mathcal{U}_{2}
).

We then have by monotone convergence that

\displaystyle\ln\mathbb{E}\left[e^{F(W)}\right]

\displaystyle=\sup_{n}\ln\mathbb{E}\left[e^{F_{n}(W)}\right]

\displaystyle=\sup_{n\geq 0}\sup_{u\in\mathcal{U}_{q}}\mathbb{E}\left[F_{n}\left(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}u\right)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}u_{s}^{2}ds\right]

\displaystyle=\sup_{u\in\mathcal{U}_{q}}\sup_{n\geq 0}\mathbb{E}\left[F_{n}\left(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}u\right)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}u_{s}^{2}ds\right]

\displaystyle=\sup_{u\in\mathcal{U}_{q}}\mathbb{E}\left[F\left(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}u\right)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}u_{s}^{2}ds\right]

where the last equality follows from (
A.1
) and dominated convergence.
∎

Proof of (
8.3
).

We let

F(W)=\rho m\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s}\right\rangle\,dW_{s}+\frac{\bar{\rho}^{2}m^{2}-m}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{W}}_{s}\right\rangle^{2}\,ds

and check that it satisfies (
A.1
) for
q\geq 2N
.
For
u\in\mathcal{U}_{q}
, letting
\widehat{\mathbb{X}}^{u}_{t}
denote the signature of
\widehat{X}^{u}_{t}=(t,X^{u}_{t})
with
X^{u}_{t}
defined by

X^{u}_{t}=W_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}u_{s}ds,

one has that

F\left(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}u\right)=\rho m\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}^{u}_{s}\right\rangle\,(dW_{s}+u_{s}ds)+\frac{\bar{\rho}^{2}m^{2}-m}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}^{u}_{s}\right\rangle^{2}\,ds

As in Section 10.2, there exists
\tilde{\bm{\sigma}}\in T^{N}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, such that
\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}^{u}_{t}\right\rangle=\left\langle\tilde{\bm{\sigma}},\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{U}_{t}\right\rangle
where
\widehat{\mathbb{Y}}^{U}_{t}
is the signature of
Y^{U}_{t}=(t,\int_{0}^{t}u_{s}ds,W_{t})
. By Lemma
5.2
and Hölder’s inequality, this implies that

\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}^{u}_{s}\right\rangle^{2}\,ds\right]\leq C\left(1+\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}|u_{s}|^{2N}ds\right]\right)<\infty

(A.2)

since
q\geq 2N
. Since it holds that

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|F\left(W+\int_{0}^{\cdot}u\right)\right|\right]\leq C\left(1+\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}u_{s}^{2}ds\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}^{u}_{s}\right\rangle^{2}ds\right]\right)

(using Itô isometry for the term in
dW
and Young’s inequality for the term in
u_{s}ds
), we see that (
A.1
) holds.

We then use Proposition
A.1
to obtain that

\displaystyle\ln\mathbb{E}\left[e^{F(W)}\right]

\displaystyle=\sup_{u\in\mathcal{U}_{q}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[-\int_{0}^{T}\frac{u_{s}^{2}}{2}ds+\int_{0}^{T}\rho m\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}^{u}_{s}\right\rangle(dW_{s}+u_{s}ds)+\frac{\bar{\rho}^{2}m^{2}-m}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}^{u}_{s}\right\rangle^{2}ds\right]

\displaystyle=\sup_{u\in\mathcal{U}_{q}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[-\int_{0}^{T}\frac{u_{s}^{2}}{2}ds+\int_{0}^{T}\rho m\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}^{u}_{s}\right\rangle u_{s}ds+\frac{\bar{\rho}^{2}m^{2}-m}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\bm{\sigma},\widehat{\mathbb{X}}^{u}_{s}\right\rangle^{2}ds\right],

where we have used that the stochastic integral has zero expectation by (
A.2
).
∎

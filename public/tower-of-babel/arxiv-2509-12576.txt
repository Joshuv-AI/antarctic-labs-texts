Taking trace does not preserve reflexivity
Haydee Lindo, Sarasij Maitra, William Zhang

Abstract
. In this note, we address a question raised in [ DMS23 ] regarding the preservation of reflexivity under taking trace. We answer this question negatively. We also study a few cases where the question has a positive answer in a one dimensional, analytically unramified Cohen-Macaulay local ring.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 13C05, 13C13, 13B02, 13B22, 13F30, 13Gxx, 13H10

1.
Introduction

Over a commutative ring
R
, a module
M
is called reflexive if the natural map
M\to M^{**}
is an isomorphism where
M^{**}
denotes the double dual
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,R),R)
. Over a field, all finite dimensional vector spaces are reflexive modules. Similarly, over a general ring, all finite free modules are the simplest examples of reflexive modules. Also, it can be shown that any reflexive module occurs as a second syzygy module
[
BH93
, 1.4.20]
, thereby lending some connection to the theory of resolutions as well. The first formal treatment of reflexive modules seems to be present in
[
Bou65
]
, though there were some discussions in the works of
[
D+58
]
,
[
Mor58
]
and
[
Bas60
]
(where the term “reflexive” was first used). In fact, over Gorenstein rings, a detailed analysis can be found in
[
Vas68
]
. Using
[
BH93
, Proposition 1.4.1]
, the study of reflexivity mainly boils down to analyzing the case when
R
is a one dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring and in recent years, various researchers have been actively exploring this avenue; see for instance
[
KV21
]
,
[
DMS23
]
,
[
IK24
]
,
[
DL24
]
,
[
EG24
]
, among other recent sources.

In such studies, the usage of trace ideal of a module is quite common and crucial. The trace ideal of a module is defined to be the sum of all homomorphic images of
M
inside
R
. So in a way, it helps understand the properties of the module by looking at its
total image
as an ideal in
R
. Many insightful and extremely useful results on trace ideals can be found in various sources such as
[
Lin17
]
,
[
KT19
]
,
[
GIK20
]
,
[
LP22
]
,
[
LM24
]
amongst others. In fact, various necessary and sufficient criteria for an ideal to be the trace ideal of a reflexive module were explored in
[
Lin17
]
paving the way for subsequent work.

In this article, our main aim was answering the following question raised in the recent works of Dao et. al.

Question 1.1
.

[
DMS23
, Question 7.17]
Let
R
be a one dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring. If
I
is a reflexive ideal, is
\operatorname{tr}_{R}(I)
also reflexive?

We settle this question negatively by providing a counterexample, namely we prove the following theorem (
Theorem

4.2
).

Theorem 1.2
.

There exists an analytically unramified one dimensional local domain where trace of a reflexive ideal need not be reflexive.

In the course of this investigation we realized that
1.1
can have affirmative answers based on the co-length,
\ell(R/\mathcal{C})
, where
\mathcal{C}
is the conductor ideal of the integral closure
\overline{R}
in
R
. Firstly, if this colength is small enough, then any regular trace ideal is reflexive (see
Theorem

3.10
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
R
be a one dimensional analytically unramified non-regular Cohen-Macaulay local ring with infinite residue field. Let
J
be a proper regular trace ideal of
R
. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

If
\ell(R/\mathcal{C})\leq 3
, then
J
is reflexive.

(2)

If
\ell(R/\mathcal{C})=4
and
R
has minimal multiplicity, then
J
is reflexive.

Secondly, using the theory of partial trace ideals developed in
[
Mai22
,
Mai24
]
, we were able to push the study further under some more constraints on
R
(see
Theorem

3.7
).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
R
be an analytically unramified one dimensional non-regular local ring with infinite residue field such that
\overline{R}
is a DVR . Let
I
be a reflexive regular ideal of
R
. Then
\operatorname{tr}_{R}(I)
is reflexive if any one of the following conditions holds.

(1)

\ell(R/\mathcal{C})=4
,

(2)

\ell(R/\mathcal{C})=5
and
R
is of minimal multiplicity.

In fact, our counterexample to
1.1
occurs when we remove the minimal multiplicity assumption in
Theorem

1.4
, thereby making it a minimal counterexample in this context.

The article is structured as follows. In
Section

2
, we collect the various background results that we use throughout the paper. In
Section

3
, we prove both
Theorem

1.3
and
Theorem

1.4
. In the course of proving these, we recollect and prove various useful results concerning the relationship between trace, integral closure of an ideal and reflexivity (see
3.2
,
3.5
). This naturally leads to
Section

4
where we prove
Theorem

1.2
thereby settling
1.1
negatively. Finally, in
Section

5
, we provide a collection of statements that provide further insights into double duals of trace ideals of finitely generated modules (see
5.4
) and their connections with birational extensions in the form of endomorphism rings and their centers. This discussion is in the spirit of
[
Lin17
]
,
[
GIK20
]
and
[
EG24
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this article
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
will be a one dimensional commutative local Noetherian ring,
R
, with unique maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
and residue field
k
, which we assume to be infinite. A regular ideal will refer to an ideal
I
containing a non-zero-divisor. The total ring of fractions of
R
will be denoted by
Q
and let
\overline{R}
be the integral closure of
R
in
Q
. All modules considered will be finitely generated (left)
R
-modules. For an
R
-module
M
, we use
\ell(M)
, respectively
\mu(M)
, to denote the length of the module, respectively minimal number of generators of the module. By a fractional ideal, we mean an
R
-submodule of
Q
. If
I,J
are fractional ideals of
R
, then

I:J:=\{\alpha\in Q\mid\alpha J\subseteq I\}.

We use
I:_{R}J
to denote the case where we restrict
\alpha
to be in
R
. This is called a colon ideal of
R
.

A module
M
is said to be maximal Cohen-Macaulay over
R
if the depth of
M
and the (Krull) dimension of
R
are equal, where

0pt(M):=\min\{i\mid\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(k,M)\neq 0\}.

The ring
R
is said to be Cohen-Macaulay if
R
is a maximal Cohen-Macaulay module over itself.
We refer the interested reader to
[
BH93
, Chap 1, 2]
for further details regarding the notions of depth and the Cohen-Macaulay property. We shall always assume that
\mu(\mathfrak{m})\geq 2
, that is,
R
is not a regular local ring.

Let
\widehat{R}
be the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic completion of
R
. The ring
R
is said to be analytically unramified if
\widehat{R}
is a reduced ring, which implies
R
is also reduced. In this case,
\overline{R}
is a finitely generated module over
R

[
HS06
, Corollary 4.6.2]
.

Below we collect the necessary background tools that will be used in the subsequent discussion.

2.1.
Integral closure of Ideals and reductions

Given an ideal
I
, the integral closure of
I
, denoted
\overline{I}
, is defined to be the collection of all elements
r\in R
such that
r
satisfies a monic polynomial

f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}x^{n-i},

where
a_{i}\in I^{i},i\geq 1
. The integral closure of
I
,
\overline{I}
, is an ideal of
R
and with
I\subseteq\overline{I}
. If
I=\overline{I}
,
I
is said to be integrally closed. An element
x\in R
(or the ideal
(x)
generated by such an element) is called a principal (alternatively, minimal) reduction of
I
if
xI^{n}=I^{n+1}
for some
n
. Under the assumption that the residue field
k
is infinite, every nonzero regular ideal has a minimal reduction generated by a regular element
[
HS06
, Proposition 8.3.7, Corollary 8.3.9]
(c.f.
[
DGH01
, Sec 2]
). It is helpful to use minimal reductions as
\overline{xR}=\overline{I}
(for a more general statement, see
[
HS06
, Corollary 1.2.5]
).

2.2.
Conductor Ideal

The conductor ideal of
\overline{R}
in
R
is defined to be
\mathcal{C}:=R:\overline{R}
. This is the largest ideal shared by both
R
and
\overline{R}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{C}\overline{R}=\mathcal{C}
and any ideal
I
such that
I\overline{R}=I
necessarily means that
I\subseteq\mathcal{C}
(see, for instance
[
HS06
, Exercise 2.11]
). More generally, if
S
is a
birational extension
of
R
, i.e., a ring extension
R\subseteq S\subseteq Q
, then we define
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{R}(S):=R:S
and call it the conductor of
S
in
R
. Notice that
\mathcal{C}_{R}(S)
is regular if and only if
S
is a finitely generated
R
-module: let
0\neq\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{R}(S)
. This implies that
\alpha S\subseteq R\implies S\subseteq\frac{1}{\alpha}R
. Hence,
S
must be a submodule of the finitely generated module
\frac{1}{\alpha}R
, thus must itself be finitely generated. Conversely, if
S
is finitely generated as an
R
-module by the elements
\alpha_{i}\in Q
, then the least common multiple of the denominators of
\alpha_{i}
’s give rise to a regular element in
\mathcal{C}_{S}(R)
.

In particular, we will be interested in the case when
\overline{R}
is finitely generated as an
R
-module (this is guaranteed, for instance when
R=\widehat{R}
or
R
is analytically unramified, as discussed above).

2.3.
Reflexive Modules and Ideals

For any
R
-module
M
, there exists a natural
R
-linear map
\displaystyle\Phi:M\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,R),R),~m\mapsto\phi_{m}
, where
\phi_{m}(f)=f(m)
for any
f\in\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,R)
. The module
M
is said to be
reflexive
if
\Phi
is an isomorphism of
R
-modules. It is standard practice in the literature to write
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,R)
as
M^{*}
and we follow this convention in the rest of this paper.

We collect some important well-known observations in the following lemma which will be useful in the subsequent discussion.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
R
be a one dimensional analytically unramified local Cohen-Macaulay ring. Let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module and let
I
be fractional ideal of
R
containing a regular element
x
of
R
. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

[
Fab20
, Proposition 2.2]
The module
M^{*}
is reflexive.

(2)

[
KT19
, Proposition 2.4(1)]
[
HS06
, Lemma 2.4.3]
Let
J
be a fractinal ideal of
R
. The module
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(I,J)
is isomorphic to
J:I
. Further, both the above modules are isomorphic to
\frac{1}{x}(xJ:_{R}I)
.

(3)

[
KT19
, Proposition 2.4(4)]
The fractional ideal
I
is reflexive if and only if
\displaystyle I=R:(R:I)
.

(4)

[
DMS23
, Corollary 3.2]
The ideals
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathfrak{m}
are reflexive.

Corollary 2.2
.

For any ideal
I
with a regular element
x
, the ideal
xR:_{R}I
is reflexive.

Proof.

Observe that
xR:_{R}I\cong\frac{1}{x}(xR:_{R}I)
and the latter can be identified with
I^{*}
using
2.1
(2). Now
2.1
(1) finishes the proof.
∎

In the context of
2.1
(2), any isomoprhism between two fractional ideal is given by multiplication by ana element of
Q

[
Mai22
, Remark 2.2]
.

2.4.
Trace Ideal of a Module

Given a module
M
, we define the trace ideal of
M
as

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}_{R}(M):=\sum_{f\in M^{*}}f(M).

An ideal
I
is said to be a trace ideal if
I=\operatorname{tr}_{R}(M)
for some module
M
. Notice that if
M\cong N
, then
\operatorname{tr}_{R}(M)=\operatorname{tr}_{R}(N)
, i.e., trace is invariant under isomorphisms. We record some important properties of trace ideals that we will use in this article.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
R
be a one dimensional analytically unramified local Cohen-Macaulay ring and let the conductor ideal be
\mathcal{C}
. Let
M
be a non-zero finitely generated
R
-module. The following statements hold.

(1)

[
Lin17
, Proposition 2.8 (iii)]
The equality
\operatorname{tr}_{R}(M)=R
occurs if and only if
\displaystyle M
has
R
a direct summand. In particular, if
M
is an ideal, then
\operatorname{tr}_{R}(M)=R
if and only if
M
is a principal ideal.

(2)

[
Lin17
, Proposition 2.8 (v)]
The inclusion
\operatorname{tr}_{R}(M)\subseteq\operatorname{tr}_{R}(M^{*})
holds and equality holds if and only if
M
is reflexive.

(3)

[
KT19
, Proposition 2.4(2)]
If
M
is a non-zero fractional ideal of
R
containing a regular element of
R
, then
\operatorname{tr}_{R}(M)=(R:M)M
.

(4)

[
Lin17
, Proposition 2.8 (iv)]
If
M
is a regular ideal of
R
, then
M\subseteq\operatorname{tr}_{R}(M)
with equality if and only if
M
is a trace ideal.

(5)

[
DMS23
, Corollary 3.6]
If
M
is an ideal with a principal reduction, then
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\operatorname{tr}_{R}(M)
.

(6)

[
DMS23
, Lemma 3.7]
If
M
is an ideal with a regular element
x
, then
xR:_{R}M\subseteq\operatorname{tr}_{R}(M)
.

2.5.
Partial Trace Ideals

Given a module
M
over a one dimensional analytically unramified local domain
R
, we let
\operatorname{h}(M):=\min\{\ell(R/I)\mid f(M)=I\text{~for some ~}f\in M^{*}\}.
Any such ideal
I
which achieves the value
\operatorname{h}(M)
is called a partial trace ideal of
M
(see
[
Mai22
,
Mai24
]
). Notice that any such partial trace ideal of
M
is of the form
f(M)
for some
f\in M^{*}
and is therefore contained within
\operatorname{tr}_{R}(M)
. Also, if
J
is a partial trace ideal of
M
, then
J
is a partial trace ideal of itself by definition
[
Mai22
, Remark 2.3]
. Hence, it is often enough to restrict our attention to ideals. Recall that if
R
is a domain with field of fractions
Q
, then the rank of a module
M
is defined to be
\dim_{Q}(Q\otimes_{R}M)

[
BH93
, Definition 1.4.2]
. Below are the main results that will be useful to us.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
R
be an analytically unramified one dimensional local domain with integral closure
\overline{R}
.

(1)

Let
J
be any non-zero ideal of
R
. Consider the following statements.

(a)

\operatorname{h}(J)=\ell(R/J)
,

(b)

R:J\subseteq\overline{R}
.

Then
(b)
implies
(a)
. Further if
\overline{R}
is a
DVR
, then
(a)
implies
(b)
.

(2)

Let
J
be a non-zero fractional ideal of
R
. Then for any partial trace ideal
I
of
J
,
\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}_{R}(I)=\operatorname{tr}_{R}(J)
.

(3)

If
\overline{R}
is a
DVR
, then for any partial trace ideal
I
of any module
M
,
\overline{\operatorname{tr}_{R}(I)}=\overline{I}
.

Proof.

Statement (1) is directly stating
[
Mai22
, Theorem 2.5]
. Statement (2) follows
[
Mai22
, Proposition 3.5]
with the added observation that all non-zero fractional ideals have rank one. Statement (3) appears as part of the proof of
[
Mai24
, Proposition 3.8]
and we repeat the proof here for convenience: by statement (1), we have
R:I\subseteq\overline{R}
. Thus,
(R:I)I\subseteq I\overline{R}\subseteq\overline{I\overline{R}}
. Thus
(R:I)I\subseteq\overline{I\overline{R}}\cap R
. Hence, using
2.3
(3), (4) and
[
HS06
, Proposition 1.6.1]
, we obtain that
I\subseteq\operatorname{tr}_{R}(I)\subseteq\overline{I}
. Taking integral closure throughout now finishes the proof.
∎

3.
Main results

Our primary goal in this section is to study some partial cases where we can guarantee that the trace ideal of a reflexive ideal is reflexive, in the situation when
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
is a one dimensional analytically unramified Cohen-Macaulay local ring. We first recall some known cases and also provide the proofs for convenience of the reader.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
R
be an analytically unramified one dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring with infinite residue field. Then for any regular ideal
I
,
\overline{I}=I\overline{R}\cap R
. In particular, any regular ideal shared by both
R
and
\overline{R}
is integrally closed.

Proof.

By
[
HS06
, Proposition 1.6.1]
, it is enough to show that
\overline{I\overline{R}}=I\overline{R}
. Since the residue field is infinite, there exists a minimal reduction
x
of
I
. Since
x\in I
, we get that
x\overline{R}\cap R\subseteq I\overline{R}\cap R\subseteq\overline{I\overline{R}}\cap R=\overline{I}
. However, since
\overline{R}
is integrally closed, we get that
x\overline{R}=\overline{x\overline{R}}

[
HS06
, Proposition 1.5.2]
. Now the fact that
\overline{xR}=\overline{I}
, finishes the first part of the proof. For the last statement, we use the fact that
I\overline{R}=I
whenever
I
is shared by both
R
and
\overline{R}
.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
R
be an analytically unramified one dimensional Cohen-Macaulay ring with conductor ideal
\mathcal{C}
and infinite residue field. Then any integrally closed ideal containing
\mathcal{C}
is a reflexive trace ideal.

Proof.

Since
R
is analytically unramified, we know that
\mathcal{C}
is a proper regular ideal. The proof now follows from
3.1
and
[
DMS23
, Proposition 3.9]
by taking
S=\overline{R}
and
I
to be any integrally closed ideal containing
\mathcal{C}
.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
R
be an analytically unramified one dimensional Cohen-Macaulay ring with infinite residue field. Let
J
be a regular trace ideal of
R
. Then the integral closure
\overline{J}
is a reflexive trace ideal and
\operatorname{tr}_{R}(\overline{J}^{*})=\overline{J}
.

Proof.

Notice that
J
contains the conductor ideal by
2.3
(5). The proof now follows immediately from
3.2
and
2.3

(2),(4)
.
∎

We should also mention that
[
DL24
, Theorem 6.2]
provides a strong result that the integral closure of a trace ideal is again a trace ideal.

Recall that a Cohen-Macaulay local ring
R
of dimension
\dim(R)
is of
minimal multiplicity
if
e(R)=\mu(\mathfrak{m})+\dim(R)-1
where
e(R)
denotes the Hilbert Samuel multiplicity of
R
(for further details, we refer the reader to
[
HS06
, Chapter 11]
,
[
Abh67
, 1]
). We shall use the following characterization of minimal multiplicity: if the residue field
k
is infinite, then
R
is of minimal multiplicity if and only if there exists a minimal reduction
x
of
\mathfrak{m}
such that
\mathfrak{m}^{2}=x\mathfrak{m}

[
Sal77
, Theorem 1]
. The following lemma is well known in the literature. We provide a proof here. Recall that we are always assuming that
R
is non-regular, i.e.,
\mu(\mathfrak{m})\geq 2
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a one dimensional non-regular Cohen-Macaulay ring with infinite residue field. Then
R
is of minimal multiplicity if and only if
xR:_{R}\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{m}
for some minimal reduction
x
of
\mathfrak{m}
.

Proof.

Observe that
\operatorname{tr}_{R}(\mathfrak{m})=\mathfrak{m}
as otherwise
\operatorname{tr}_{R}(\mathfrak{m})=R
and
2.3
(1) shows that
\mathfrak{m}
must be principal, a contradiction to the regularity assumption. Now observe that
R
is of minimal multiplicity if and only if
\mathfrak{m}^{2}=x\mathfrak{m}
for some minimal reduction
x
of
\mathfrak{m}

[
Sal77
, Theorem 1]
. If
\mathfrak{m}^{2}=x\mathfrak{m}
, then
[
DMS23
, Corollary 3.8]
shows that
\mathfrak{m}=xR:_{R}\mathfrak{m}
. Conversely, assume that
xR:_{R}\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{m}
. This implies that
\mathfrak{m}^{2}\subseteq xR
. Now
[
HS06
, Proposition 8.3.3]
shows that
\mathfrak{m}^{2}=xR\cap\mathfrak{m}^{2}=x\mathfrak{m}
since
xR
is a minimal reduction of
\mathfrak{m}
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Recall that colons satisfy the following nice inclusion reversing property: if
J\subseteq K
, then
I:_{R}K\subseteq I:_{R}J
. We use this to establish the next result.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
R
be an analytically unramified one dimensional non-regular Cohen-Macaulay ring of minimal multiplicity and infinite residue field. If
I
is a proper regular trace ideal such that
\overline{I}=\mathfrak{m}
, then
I=\mathfrak{m}
and hence reflexive.

Proof.

Since
\overline{I}=\mathfrak{m}
and
I\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
, we can find a common minimal reduction
xR
of
I
and
\mathfrak{m}
. Since
R
is of minimal multiplicity, by
3.4
,
xR:_{R}\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{m}
. Since
I\subseteq\overline{I}
, the conclusion now follows from
2.1
(4) and the following chain of ideals:

\mathfrak{m}=xR:_{R}\mathfrak{m}=xR:_{R}\overline{I}\subseteq xR:_{R}I\subseteq I\subseteq\mathfrak{m}.

∎

Remark 3.6
.

Observe that we used the case,
\mathfrak{m}^{2}=x\mathfrak{m}
in the above discussion and also the fact that
\operatorname{tr}_{R}(\mathfrak{m})=\mathfrak{m}
. More generally, under the same hypothesis on the ring as above, if
I
is a regular stable ideal, i.e.,
I\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(I,I)
, then we know that in this case,
I^{2}=xI
for some non-zero divisor
x
, and hence
\operatorname{tr}_{R}(I)=x:_{R}I\cong I^{*}

[
DL24
, Proposition 3.2, Proposition 3.10]
. Reflexivity now follows from
2.1
(1).

We now provide a series of statements based on the co-length of the conductor ideal, which enables us to conclude reflexivity under taking trace in some cases.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
R
be a one dimensional analytically unramified non-regular Cohen-Macaulay local ring with infinite residue field. Let
J
be a proper regular trace ideal of
R
. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

If
\ell(R/\mathcal{C})\leq 3
, then
J
is reflexive.

(2)

If
\ell(R/\mathcal{C})=4
and
R
has minimal multiplicity, then
J
is reflexive.

Proof.

Since
R
is analytically unramified,
\overline{R}
is finitely generated over
R
and hence
\mathcal{C}=R:\overline{R}
is a non-zero regular ideal of
R
. Since the residue field is infinite, we know that every proper regular ideal has a principal reduction (see
Section

2.1
). By
2.3
(5),
\mathcal{C}\subseteq J
for any regular trace ideal
J
of
R
.

If
\ell(R/\mathcal{C})\leq 2
, then
the only ideals containing
\mathcal{C}
are
\mathcal{C}
,
\mathfrak{m}
and
R
. Hence, any proper regular trace ideal is reflexive by
2.1
(4).

Now assume
\ell(R/\mathcal{C})=3
. Since the residue field is infinite, we can take
xR
to be a minimal reduction of
J
and
\overline{J}
. By
[
DMS23
, Theorem 4.6(1), Corollary 4.10]
, we know that
J\mathcal{C}=x\mathcal{C}\subseteq xR
and
\overline{J}\mathcal{C}=x\mathcal{C}
. Hence using
2.3
(6), we get that
\mathcal{C}\subseteq xR:_{R}J\subseteq\operatorname{tr}_{R}(J)=J\subseteq\mathfrak{m}\subseteq R
. Since
\ell(R/\mathcal{C})=3
, there are three possibilities:
xR:_{R}J=\mathcal{C}
or
xR:_{R}J=J
or
J=\mathfrak{m}
. In the latter two cases, we get the conclusion from
2.2
and
2.1
(4). Thus, assume that
xR:_{R}J=\mathcal{C}
.

Since we also have
\overline{J}\mathcal{C}=x\mathcal{C}
, we obtain that
\mathcal{C}\subseteq xR:_{R}\overline{J}\subseteq xR:_{R}J.
Thus, we get that
\mathcal{C}=xR:_{R}\overline{J}\cong\overline{J}^{*}
. Taking trace on both sides and using
3.3
, we obtain that
\mathcal{C}=\overline{J}
. Hence
\mathcal{C}=J=\overline{J}
and thus
J
is reflexive, finishing the proof of
(1)
.

For
(2)
, observe again that we have the chain
\mathcal{C}\subseteq xR:_{R}J\subseteq J\subseteq\overline{J}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
. If
\mathcal{C}=xR:_{R}J
, then the same argument as above shows that
J=\mathcal{C}
and hence reflexive. We can also assume that
xR:_{R}J\subsetneq J\subsetneq\overline{J}
as otherwise our conclusion holds from
2.2
and
3.3
. Thus, we have the chain
\mathcal{C}\subsetneq xR:_{R}J\subsetneq J\subsetneq\overline{J}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
. So the only possibility that remains is
\overline{J}=\mathfrak{m}
and we are done in this case by
3.5
.
∎

The following example shows that the minimal multiplicity assumption in
Theorem

3.7
(2) is essential.

Example 3.8
.

[
DMS23
, Example 7.12]

Let
R=k[[t^{5},t^{6},t^{7}]]
. Let
I=(t^{5},t^{7})
. Then
I
is a non-reflexive trace ideal.

We now shift our focus on starting with a reflexive ideal and studying its trace. We can constrain our hypothesis a bit more to increase our colength study further. The following lemma will be important in the subsequent theorem.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
R
be an analytically unramified one dimensional non-regular local ring with infinite residue field such that
\overline{R}
is a DVR. For any finitely generated non-zero fractional ideal
M
, let
I
be a partial trace ideal of
M
and let
xR
be a minimal reduction of
I
. Then the following chain of ideals exists:

(3.1)

\mathcal{C}\subseteq xR:_{R}\overline{I}=xR:_{R}\overline{\operatorname{tr}_{R}(M)}\subseteq xR:_{R}\operatorname{tr}_{R}(M)\subseteq xR:_{R}I\subseteq\operatorname{tr}_{R}(M)\subseteq\overline{\operatorname{tr}_{R}(M)}=\overline{I}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}.

Proof.

Since
\overline{R}
is a
DVR
,
R
is a Cohen-Macaulay local domain. Let
L=\operatorname{tr}_{R}(M)
. By
2.4
(2) and (3), we know that
\operatorname{tr}_{R}(I)=L\subseteq\overline{L}=\overline{I}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
. Since
I\subseteq\operatorname{tr}_{R}(I)
, we can choose
xR
to be a common minimal reduction of
I,L
and
\overline{I}
. The same argument as in the proof of
Theorem

3.7
gives us that
\mathcal{C}\subseteq xR:_{R}\overline{I}=xR:_{R}\overline{L}
. Since
I\subseteq L\subseteq\overline{L}
, we get
xR:_{R}\overline{L}\subseteq xR:_{R}L\subseteq xR:_{R}I\subseteq\operatorname{tr}_{R}(I)=L
where the last inclusion follows from
2.3
(6). Now connecting these chains finishes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
R
be an analytically unramified one dimensional non-regular local ring with infinite residue field such that
\overline{R}
is a DVR . Let
I
be a reflexive regular ideal of
R
. Then
\operatorname{tr}_{R}(I)
is reflexive if any one of the following conditions holds.

(1)

\ell(R/\mathcal{C})=4
,

(2)

\ell(R/\mathcal{C})=5
and
R
is of minimal multiplicity.

Proof.

Let
L=\operatorname{tr}_{R}(I)
. Since
\operatorname{tr}_{R}(I)
and reflexivity are invariant under the isomorphism class of
I
, we can replace
I
to be a partial trace ideal of itself. By
2.4
(1), we get that
R:I\subseteq\overline{R}
and since
I
is reflexive, using
2.1
(3), we get that
\mathcal{C}=R:\overline{R}\subseteq R:(R:I)=I
. Hence, by
3.2
,
\overline{I}
is a reflexive trace ideal. Moreover, from
2.4
(3), we get that
L=\operatorname{tr}_{R}(I)\subseteq\overline{L}=\overline{I}
.

For
(1)
, start with the following chain from
Equation

3.1
:
\mathcal{C}\subseteq xR:_{R}\overline{I}\subseteq xR:_{R}I\subseteq L\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
. Since
\ell(\mathfrak{m}/\mathcal{C})=3
, we get that at least one of the inclusions is an equality. If
\mathcal{C}=xR:_{R}\overline{I}
, then
\mathcal{C}\cong\overline{I}^{*}
. Taking trace, we conclude that
\mathcal{C}=\overline{I}
using
3.2
and
2.3
(2). But this shows that
\mathcal{C}=I=L
and hence
L
is reflexive. Next, let
xR:_{R}\overline{I}=xR:_{R}I
. By
[
DMS23
, Remark 2.4]
, we get that
x\overline{I}^{*}=xI^{*}
, but this means that
\overline{I}=I
since
I
and
\overline{I}
are both reflexive. But now from
I\subseteq L\subseteq\overline{I}
, we conclude that
L=I=\overline{I}
and hence
L
is reflexive. If
L=\mathfrak{m}
, we are done by
2.1
(4).
Finally, if
xR:_{R}{I}=L
, then
L\cong{I}^{*}
and hence is reflexive by
2.1
(1).
This finishes the proof of
(1)
.

For
(2)
, from
Equation

3.1
we now start with

(3.2)

\mathcal{C}\subseteq xR:_{R}\overline{I}\subseteq xR:_{R}I\subseteq L\subseteq\overline{L}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}.

Since
\ell(\mathfrak{m}/\mathcal{C})=4
, we need at least one inclusion to be an equality. From the arguments in the previous paragraph, we may assume that
L=\overline{L}
or
\overline{L}=\mathfrak{m}
. In the first case,
3.3
settles the problem. In the latter case, we obtain that
L=\mathfrak{m}
by
3.5
. This completes the proof.
∎

4.
A Counterexample to
1.1

In this section, we provide an example to show the necessity of minimal multiplicity in
Theorem

3.10
. More importantly, this provides a counterexample to
1.1
. Our counterexample arises in the context of studying complete numerical semigroup rings which are one dimensional local domains of the form
k[[t^{a_{1}},t^{a_{2}},\ldots,t^{a_{n}}]]
where
k
is a field and
a_{1}<\ldots<a_{n},a_{i}\in\mathbb{N}.
In this case,
\overline{R}=k[[t]]
which is a
DVR
and the conductor ideal
\mathcal{C}
can be described as the collection of all
t^{c+i},i\geq 0
where
c
is such that
t^{c-1}\notin R
but
t^{c+i}\in R
for all
i\geq 0
. The value group of
R
, denoted
v(R)
is the collection of all integers
r
such that
t^{r}\in R
. More generally, for a fractional ideal
I
, the value group of
I
is defined to be
v(I):=\{r\in\mathbb{Z}\mid t^{r}\in I\}
. Notice that
v(\mathfrak{m})=v(R)
.

A minimal reduction of the maximal ideal
(t^{a_{1}},\ldots,t^{a_{n}})
is given by
t^{a_{1}}

[
MM23
, Remark 2.1]
. The advantage of working on these rings is that a lot of information about ideals is encoded in the associated valuation semigroup of the ring. Thus, often the study boils down to studying the semigroup generated by the positive integers
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
. We refer the reader to the discussion in
[
LM24
, Sec 3.3]
; most importantly, every homogeneous element is essentially of the form
t^{h}
and hence to detect the presence of such an element in a desired subset of elements of
R
, it is enough to detect the presence of the integer
h
in the corresponding suitable semigroup of integers associated with the subset.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
R
be an integral domain with fraction field
Q
. For any non-zero
\alpha\in Q
and any fractional ideal
L
,
\displaystyle\alpha(R:L)=R:\frac{1}{\alpha}L.

Proof.

Notice that
x\in\alpha(R:L)\iff\frac{x}{\alpha}L\subseteq R\iff\frac{x}{\alpha}\in R:L\iff x\in\alpha(R:L)
.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\displaystyle R=k[[t^{7},t^{8},t^{9},t^{11}]]
where
k
is infinite. Let
x_{1}=t^{7},x_{2}=t^{8},x_{3}=t^{9}
and
x_{4}=t^{11}
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be the conductor ideal. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

R
is not of minimal multiplicity and
\ell(R/\mathcal{C})=5
.

(2)

The ideal
I=(x_{2},x_{3},x_{1}^{3})
is reflexive.

(3)

\operatorname{tr}_{R}(I)=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
.

(4)

\operatorname{tr}_{R}(I)
is not a reflexive ideal.

In particular,
1.1
is false in general.

Proof.

Notice that
x_{1}R:_{R}\mathfrak{m}
is given by all elements of the form
t^{h}\in R
such that
t^{h}\mathfrak{m}\subseteq x_{1}R
. In terms of valuations, this is the same as studying whether
h+r-7\in v(\mathfrak{m})
for all
r\in v(\mathfrak{m})
. However, observe that
x_{2}\in x_{1}R:_{R}\mathfrak{m}
if and only if
8+r-7\in v(\mathfrak{m})
for all
r
, which is a contradiction since
r=9\in v(\mathfrak{m})
but
10\notin v(\mathfrak{m})
. Thus,
x_{2}\notin x_{1}R:_{R}\mathfrak{m}
which shows that
x_{1}R:_{R}\mathfrak{m}\subsetneq\mathfrak{m}
and hence
R
is not of minimal multiplicity by
3.4
.

Now notice that
t^{13}\notin R
but
t^{14+i}\in R
for all
i\geq 0
. Hence
v(\mathcal{C})=\{14,15,\longrightarrow\}
. Moreover,
v(R)=\{0,7,8,9,11,14,15,\longrightarrow\}
. Thus,
\ell(R/\mathcal{C})=5
, being given by the valuations
\{0,7,8,9,11\}

[
MM23
, Proposition 2.2]
(c.f.
[
HK71
, Proposition 2.9]
). This finishes the proof of
(1)
.

For (2), we begin by showing that
I
can be realized as the double dual
J^{**}
where
J=(x_{2},x_{3})=(t^{8},t^{9})
. Since
J=x_{2}R+x_{3}R
, we get that
J^{*}=R:J=(R:x_{2}R)\cap(R:x_{3}R)=\left(\frac{1}{x_{2}}(R:R)\right)\cap\left(\frac{1}{x_{3}}(R:R)\right)
where the last equality follows from
4.1
. Hence,
v(J^{*})
is given by
v(t^{-8}R)\cap v(t^{-9}R)
. Since we know
v(R)
, we get that
v(t^{-8}R)=\{r-8\mid r\in v(R)\}
and
v(t^{-9}R)=\{r-9\mid r\in v(R)\}
. The intersection is easily seen to be
\{-1,0,6,7,8,\longrightarrow\}
. Notice that using
\{t^{-1},t^{0},t^{12}\}
as
R
-module generators, all other elements of the form
t^{j}
, with
j
in the above intersection, can be generated. Hence,
J^{*}=t^{-1}R+R+t^{12}R
.

Next, we compute
J^{**}=R:J^{*}=(R:t^{-1}R)\cap(R:R)\cap(R:t^{12}R)=(t(R:R))\cap R\cap(t^{-12}(R:R))=tR\cap R\cap t^{-12}R
, where the second to last inequality again follows from
4.1
. This is the same as looking for the valuations in
v(tR)\cap v(R)\cap v(t^{-12}R)
. Now observe that
v(tR)=\{1,8,9,10,12,15,\longrightarrow\}
and
v(t^{-12}R)=\{-12,-5,-4,-3,-1,2,\longrightarrow\}
. Thus,
v(J^{**})=v(tR)\cap v(R)\cap v(t^{12}R)=\{8,9,15,\longrightarrow\}
. Finally, observe that
\{t^{8},t^{9},t^{21}\}
as
R
-module generators, generates all the remaining elements whose valuations are in the intersection. Thus,
J^{**}=(t^{8},t^{9},t^{21})=(x_{2},x_{3},x_{1}^{3})
as desired. This shows that
I
is a reflexive ideal, finishing the proof of (2).

To see (3), we first observe that
(x_{2},x_{3})\subseteq\operatorname{tr}_{R}(I)
by
2.3
(4). Further notice that
x_{1}x_{3}=t^{16}=x_{2}^{2}\in x_{2}R
and
x_{1}x_{1}^{3}=t^{28}=x_{2}x_{3}x_{4}\in x_{2}R
. Since
x_{2}R:_{R}I=(x_{2}R:_{R}x_{2}R)\cap(x_{2}R:_{R}x_{3}R)\cap(x_{2}R:_{R}x_{1}^{3}R)=(x_{2}R:_{R}x_{3}R)\cap(x_{2}R:_{R}x_{1}^{3}R)
, we conclude that
x_{1}\in x_{2}R:_{R}I\subseteq\operatorname{tr}_{R}(I)
where the last inclusion is from
2.3
(6). Thus, we get
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\subseteq\operatorname{tr}_{R}(I)
. Next notice that
\{t^{14},t^{15},\longrightarrow\}\subseteq(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
. So,
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=\operatorname{tr}_{R}(I)
if and only if
x_{4}\notin\operatorname{tr}_{R}(I)
.

From
2.3
(3), we know that
\operatorname{tr}_{R}(I)=I^{*}I
. We first compute
I^{*}
using the procedure as in the proof of (2):
I^{*}=(R:x_{2}R)\cap(R:x_{3}R)\cap(R:x_{1}^{3}R)=t^{-8}R\cap t^{-9}R\cap t^{-21}R
. Since
v(t^{-21}R)=\{-21,-14,-13,-12,-10,-7,\longrightarrow\}
, we find that the intersection of the valuations is given by
\{-1,0,6,7,\longrightarrow\}
. Hence
I^{*}=t^{-1}R+R+t^{12}R
using the same argument as before. So,
\operatorname{tr}_{R}(I)=I^{*}I=(t^{-1}R+R+t^{12}R)(t^{8}R+t^{9}R+t^{21}R)
and none of the combinations give rise to the valuation
11
since
t^{12}\notin R
. Thus
x_{4}\notin\operatorname{tr}_{R}(I)
proving (3).

Finally, we claim that
x_{4}\in\operatorname{tr}_{R}(I)^{**}
.
Let
L=\operatorname{tr}_{R}(I)=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
. Then
L^{*}=x_{1}^{-1}R\cap x_{2}^{-1}R\cap x_{3}^{-1}R
. Again, observing the valuations, we obtain that
L^{*}=R+\sum_{i\geq 7}t^{i}R
. Looking at the generators, we conclude that
x_{4}L=t^{11}L\subseteq R
, hence
x_{4}\in L^{**}
. But
x_{4}\not\in L
. Thus,
\operatorname{tr}_{R}(I)
is not reflexive by
2.1
(3), and this finishes the proof of (4).
∎

The previous example shows that without the minimal multiplicity assumption,
1.1
is false. However, our tools do not seem to answer the case when
R
is of minimal multiplicity. As such, we end this section with the following question.

Question 4.3
.

Let
R
be a one dimensional Cohen Macaulay ring of minimal multiplicity. If
I\subseteq R
is a reflexive ideal, is
\operatorname{tr}_{R}(I)
reflexive?

5.
Miscellaneous Results

In this section, we provide some miscellaneous results that provide further tools to study the relationship between a trace ideal and its double dual. We start by providing a more general version of
2.1
(1) by relaxing the hypothesis. Recall that a Noetherian ring
R
satisfies Serre’s criterion
(S_{k})
if for all prime ideals
P
in
R
, the depth of
R_{P}
is at least
\min\{k,\operatorname{height}P\}
(see
[
HS06
, Sec 4.5]
) for further details). Also, recall that a module
M
is called totally reflexive if
M
is reflexive and
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(M,R)=\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(M^{*},R)=0
for all
i>0
(refer to
[
KV21
]
for instance).

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
R
be a reduced local Cohen-Macaulay ring or a ring satisfying
(S_{1})
. Let
M
be a finitely generated module over
R
. If
R
only satisfies
(S_{1})
then further assume that
M
and
M^{*}
are locally totally reflexive on all the minimal primes of
R
. Then
\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}_{R}(M^{*})=\operatorname{tr}_{R}(M^{**})
.

Proof.

Under the given hypotheses,
M^{*}
is reflexive (see
[
DMS23
, Lemma 2.5]
and
[
Fab20
, Proposition 2.2]
). Now the conclusion follows from
[
Lin17
, Proposition 2.8]
.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
S
be a finite birational extension of
R
where
R
is a reduced local Cohen-Macaulay ring. Then

\operatorname{tr}_{R}(S)=\operatorname{tr}_{R}(S^{*})=\operatorname{tr}_{R}(S^{**})=S^{*}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{R}(S)=R:S=S^{*}
denote the conductor ideal of
S
in
R
. Since it is the largest common ideal between
R
and
S
, notice that
\operatorname{tr}_{R}(S)=S^{*}S=\mathcal{C}_{R}(S)=S^{*}
. This shows that
\operatorname{tr}_{R}(S)=S^{*}
. Now applying
\operatorname{tr}_{R}(\cdot)
again to this equality and using
5.1
, we get that
\operatorname{tr}_{R}(S)=\operatorname{tr}_{R}(\operatorname{tr}_{R}(S))=\operatorname{tr}_{R}(S^{*})=\operatorname{tr}_{R}(S^{**})
which finishes the proof.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated module over a reduced local Cohen-Macaulay ring such that
\operatorname{tr}_{R}(M)
is a regular ideal. Then
\operatorname{tr}_{R}(M)^{**}=\operatorname{tr}_{R}(\operatorname{End}_{R}(\operatorname{tr}_{R}(M)))
.

Proof.

Let
J=\operatorname{tr}_{R}(M)
and consider the birational extension
S=\operatorname{End}_{R}(J)
. Then
J^{*}=R:J=J:J=S
since
J
is a trace ideal
[
KT19
, Proposition 2.4]
. Thus
J^{**}=S^{*}=\operatorname{tr}_{R}(S)
by
5.2
, thereby finishing the proof.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
R
be a reduced local Cohen-Macaulay ring. Then the reflexive hull of a regular trace ideal is again a trace ideal.

Proof.

The proof is immediate from
5.3
.
∎

In the following, we denote the center of the endomorphism ring of a module
M
, by
Z(\operatorname{End}_{R}(M))
.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
R
be a reduced Cohen-Macaulay local ring and let
M
be a finitely generated reflexive
R
-module with regular trace ideal. Then
\operatorname{tr}_{R}(M)
is reflexive if and only if
\operatorname{tr}_{R}(M)=\operatorname{tr}_{R}(Z(\operatorname{End}_{R}(M)))
.

Proof.

By
[
Lin17
, Corollary 3.11]
, we know that
\operatorname{tr}_{R}(\operatorname{End}_{R}(\operatorname{tr}_{R}(M)))=\operatorname{tr}_{R}(Z(\operatorname{End}_{R}(M)))
. The result now follows immediately from
5.3
.
∎

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
R
be a reduced local Cohen-Macaulay ring. If
I
is a reflexive ideal of
R
, then
\operatorname{tr}_{R}(I)
is reflexive if and only if
\operatorname{tr}_{R}(I)=\operatorname{tr}_{R}(\operatorname{End}_{R}(I)).

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{End}_{R}(I)=I:I
is a commutative ring, we get that
Z(\operatorname{End}_{R}(I))=\operatorname{End}_{R}(I)
. The proof now follows immediately from
5.5
.
∎

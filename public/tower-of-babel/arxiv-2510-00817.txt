Semantic Bridges Between First Order c c -Representations and Cost-Based Semantics: An Initial Perspective
Nicholas Leisegang, Giovanni Casini, Thomas Meyer

Abstract
Weighted-knowledge bases and cost-based semantics represent a recent formalism introduced by Bienvenu et al. for Ontology Mediated Data Querying in the case where a given knowledge base is inconsistent. This is done by adding a weight to each statement in the knowledge base (KB), and then giving each DL interpretation a cost based on how often it breaks rules in the KB. In this paper we compare this approach with c c -representations , a form of non-monotonic reasoning originally introduced by Kern-Isberner. c c -Representations describe a means to interpret defeasible concept inclusions of the form C ⊏ ∼ D C\>\raisebox{1.93747pt}{$\sqsubset$}\hskip-7.3194pt\raisebox{-2.58334pt}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\sim$}}\>D (read “instances of C C are usually instances of D D ”) in the first-order case. This is done by assigning a numerical ranking to each interpretations via penalties for each violated conditional. We compare these two approaches on a semantic level. In particular, we show that under certain conditions a weighted knowledge base and a set of defeasible conditionals can generate the same ordering on interpretations, and therefore an equivalence of semantic structures up to relative cost. Moreover, we compare entailment described in both cases, where certain notions are equivalently expressible in both formalisms. Our results have the potential to benefit further work on both cost-based semantics and c-representations.

Introduction

Description logics (DLs)
Baader et al. 2007
provide the logical foundation for formal ontologies of the OWL family
OWL 2 Web Ontology Language Document Overview 2009
.
Many extensions of classical DLs have been proposed to enrich the representational capabilities of DLs, especially to support reasoning under forms of uncertainty. Two aspects of uncertain reasoning that have attracted the attention of the community are defeasible reasoning
Baader and Hollunder 1995
;
Bonatti et al. 2015
;
Bonatti et al. 2009
;
Casini and Straccia 2013
;
Donini et al. 2002
;
Giordano et al. 2013
;
Giordano et al. 2015
and inconsistency handling
Lembo et al. 2010
;
Bienvenu et al. 2014
;
Bienvenu et al. 2016
;
Peñaloza 2020
.

In this paper we consider two specific semantic constructions, each connected to one of these two areas. On the side of inconsistency handling, we take under consideration a recent proposal by Bienvenu et al.
Bienvenu et al. 2024
, formulated for the DL
\mathcal{ALCO}
. In the area of defeasible reasoning, we will consider
c
-representations
Kern-Isberner 2001
, a semantic framework based on ranking functions, and we refer to its formulation for First Order Logic (FOL)
Hahn et al. 2024
, that we constrain to the expressivity of
\mathcal{ALCO}
. Both these semantic constructions are based on a similar idea: ranking the interpretations according to a numeric value that is determined by the amount of information that each interpretation violates.

We present a formal comparison between these two frameworks. The main contribution of the paper is to show that, under certain conditions, both semantic structures are equivalent up to relative cost. That is, on one hand we prove that for any ranking function defining a
c
-representation of a defeasible
\mathcal{ALCO}
knowledge base
\mathcal{K}
, we can construct a weighted knowledge base
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
that induces the same ordering of interpretations, according to the methods in
Bienvenu et al. 2024
. On the other hand, we provide necessary and sufficient conditions that a weighted knowledge base must satisfy for the converse to hold. We also show how certain entailment relations defined in each semantic framework can be equivalently expressed in terms of the other.

The paper is organised as follows: Sections
2.2
and
2.3
present the two formal frameworks we refer to:
cost-based semantics
and
c-representations
, respectively; in Section
3
we present the focus of the paper, making a first comparison between the semantic structures and the forms of reasoning that we can model in these two structures; finally, in Sections
4
and
5
we mention related and future work.

Background

2.1
Description Logic
\mathcal{ALCO}

In this section we provide a brief introduction to the description logic
\mathcal{ALCO}
Baader et al. 2007
.
Let
\mathcal{N}_{C},\mathcal{N}_{R},\mathcal{N}_{I}
be finite, disjoint sets of symbols. These symbols will be our
concept names
,
role names
, and
individual names
,
respectively, that together define a
vocabulary
.
Based on this we define the concept language
\mathcal{L}_{C}
of
\mathcal{ALCO}
, where a concept
C\in\mathcal{L}_{C}
iff

C::=A\mid\{o\}\mid\neg C\mid C\sqcap D\mid C\sqcup D\mid\exists r.C\mid\forall r.C\mid\bot\mid\top

for
A\in\mathcal{N}_{C},r\in\mathcal{N}_{R},o\in\mathcal{N}_{I}
, and
C,D\in\mathcal{L}_{C}
.
An ABox
\mathcal{A}
is a finite set of
assertions
of the form
a
:
A
and
(a,b)
:
r
where
a,b\in\mathcal{N}_{I}
,
A\in\mathcal{N}_{C}
and
r\in\mathcal{N}_{R}
. These are called concept and role assertions respectively.
A TBox
\mathcal{T}
is a finite set of
concept inclusions
(GCIs) of the form
C\sqsubseteq D
for
C,D\in\mathcal{L}_{C}
. An
\mathcal{ALCO}

knowledge base
is a pair
\mathcal{K}=(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A})
where
\mathcal{T}
is a TBox and
\mathcal{A}
is an ABox.

Interpretations give a semantics to assertions and
concept inclusions.
An interpretation is a tuple
\mathcal{I}=\langle\Delta^{\mathcal{I}},\cdot^{\mathcal{I}}\rangle
where
\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}
is a non-empty set called the
domain
and
\cdot^{\mathcal{I}}
is a function that maps every
a\in\mathcal{N}_{I}
to an individual
a^{\mathcal{I}}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}
, every

r\in\mathcal{N}_{R}
to a set of ordered pairs
r^{\mathcal{I}}\subseteq\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}\times\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}
, and every
A\in\mathcal{N}_{C}
to a set
A^{\mathcal{I}}\subseteq\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}
.
Interpretations are lifted to complex concepts by:

\displaystyle\bot^{\mathcal{I}}=\emptyset,\qquad\quad\thickspace\top^{\mathcal{I}}=\Delta^{\mathcal{I}},\qquad\quad\{o\}^{\mathcal{I}}=o^{\mathcal{I}},\qquad\quad(\neg C)^{\mathcal{I}}=\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}\setminus C^{\mathcal{I}},

\displaystyle(C\sqcap D)^{\mathcal{I}}=C^{\mathcal{I}}\cap D^{\mathcal{I}},\qquad\quad(C\sqcup D)^{\mathcal{I}}=C^{\mathcal{I}}\cup D^{\mathcal{I}},

\displaystyle(\exists r.C)^{\mathcal{I}}=\{x\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}\mid\text{there is an $(x,y)\in r^{\mathcal{I}}$ \text{ s.t. }$y\in C^{\mathcal{I}}$}\},

\displaystyle(\forall r.C)^{\mathcal{I}}=\{x\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}\mid y\in C^{\mathcal{I}}\text{ for all }(x,y)\in r^{\mathcal{I}}\}.

An interpretation
\mathcal{I}
is a
model
of a concept inclusion
C\sqsubseteq D
if
C^{\mathcal{I}}\subseteq D^{\mathcal{I}}
.

\mathcal{I}
is a model of the assertion
a
:
A
if
a^{\mathcal{I}}\in A^{\mathcal{I}}
, and
\mathcal{I}
is a model of
(a,b)
:
r
if
(a^{\mathcal{I}},b^{\mathcal{I}})\in r^{\mathcal{I}}
. This is denoted
\mathcal{I}\Vdash\tau
for any assertion or GCI
\tau
.
\mathcal{I}
is a model of the knowledge base
\mathcal{K}=(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A})
(denoted
\mathcal{I}\Vdash\mathcal{K}
) if
\mathcal{I}\Vdash\tau
for all
\tau\in\mathcal{T}\cup\mathcal{A}
.
A knowledge base
\mathcal{K}

entails
a concept inclusion or assertion
\tau
if
\mathcal{I}\Vdash\mathcal{K}
implies
\mathcal{I}\Vdash\tau
.

Concept inclusions
C\sqsubseteq D
, read as “Every
C
is a
D
”, represent the main element of a DL knowledge base, but they do not allow for exceptions. To do so,
defeasible concept inclusions
(DCIs)
C\>\raisebox{1.93747pt}{$\sqsubset$}\hskip-7.3194pt\raisebox{-2.58334pt}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\sim$}}\>D
have been introduced in various forms
Britz et al. 2021
;
Giordano et al. 2015
;
Bonatti et al. 2015
and associated to different semantics, but always with the same intended reading:
C\>\raisebox{1.93747pt}{$\sqsubset$}\hskip-7.3194pt\raisebox{-2.58334pt}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\sim$}}\>D
stands for “Typically, an element of
C
is also an element of
D
”. In Section
2.3
we provide an interpretation of DCIs by adapting to
\mathcal{ALCO}
a semantic approach defined for defeasible conditionals in FOL.

2.2
Cost-Based Semantics

Cost-based semantics in DLs were introduced by Bienvenu et al.
Bienvenu et al. 2024
in order to facilitate query answering over inconsistent DL knowledge bases. Intuitively, they assign a numerical “penalty” to each element of an ABox and TBox in the knowledge base. Then an interpretation
\mathcal{I}
is assigned a weight based on how many elements of
\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}
“break” rules of the knowledge base. This is defined as follows.

Definition 1

(
Bienvenu et al. 2024
)
.

A
weighted knowledge base (WKB)
is a pair
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}=((\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}),\omega)
where
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A})
is a classical DL KB and
\omega:\mathcal{T}\cup\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
is a cost function.

This cost function is then migrated from the knowledge base to an interpretation by tracking the
violations
of each rule in the interpretation.

Definition 2

(
Bienvenu et al. 2024
)
.

The set of
violations of a GCI
,
C\sqsubseteq D
, is given by,

\textit{vio}_{C\sqsubseteq D}(\mathcal{I})=(C\sqcap\neg D)^{\mathcal{I}}

while the
violations of an ABox

\mathcal{A}
in
\mathcal{I}
are given by

\textit{vio}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{I})=\{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}\mid\mathcal{I}\nvDash\alpha\}

Definition 3

(
Bienvenu et al. 2024
)
.

Given a WKB
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}=((\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}),\omega)
, the
cost
of an interpretation
\mathcal{I}
with regards to
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
is given by,

\textit{cost}_{\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}(\mathcal{I})=\sum\limits_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}}\omega(\tau)|\textit{vio}_{\tau}(\mathcal{I})|+\sum\limits_{\alpha\in\textit{vio}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{I})}\omega(\alpha),

where
|\textit{vio}_{\tau}(\mathcal{I})|
is the cardinality of the set
\textit{vio}_{\tau}(\mathcal{I})
. We will, in some cases, omit the subscript from the function
\textit{cost}_{\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}
. For each WKB
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}=((\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}),\omega)
we can define the
optimal cost
of
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
as

\textit{optc}(\mathcal{K}_{\omega})=\min_{\mathcal{I}}(\textit{cost}_{\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}(\mathcal{I}))
.

The intuition is that an interpretation with a higher cost is “worse” in the sense that it contradicts more axioms with a greater cost. In particular, if some TBox or ABox axiom has a cost of
\infty
, for any interpretation
\mathcal{I}
breaking it we have
\textit{cost}(\mathcal{I})=\infty
. From this semantics, there are several different notions of entailment which are defined
Bienvenu et al. 2024
. These are originally defined for Boolean Conjunctive Queries, but it is straightforward to extend them to general
\mathcal{ALCO}
statements.

Definition 4

(
Bienvenu et al. 2024
)
.

For any GCI or assertion
\tau
in
\mathcal{ALCO}
, any weighted knowledge base
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
, and any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the following entailment relations:

•

\mathcal{K}_{\omega}\vDash^{k}_{c}\tau
if
\mathcal{I}\Vdash\tau
for all interpretations
\mathcal{I}
with
\textit{cost}_{\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}(\mathcal{I})\leq k
.

•

\mathcal{K}_{\omega}\vDash^{k}_{p}\tau
if
\mathcal{I}\Vdash\tau
for some interpretation
\mathcal{I}
with
\textit{cost}_{\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}(\mathcal{I})\leq k
.

•

\mathcal{K}_{\omega}\vDash^{opt}_{c}\tau
if
\mathcal{I}\Vdash\tau
for all interpretations
\mathcal{I}
with
\textit{cost}_{\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}(\mathcal{I})=optc(\mathcal{K}_{\omega})
.

•

\mathcal{K}_{\omega}\vDash^{opt}_{p}\tau
if
\mathcal{I}\Vdash\tau
for some interpretation
\mathcal{I}
with
\textit{cost}_{\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}(\mathcal{I})=optc(\mathcal{K}_{\omega})
.

Note that if the knowledge base
\mathcal{K}
is classically consistent, then the optimal cost for
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
is
0
. In this case, we have that
\vDash^{opt}_{c}
coincides with classical reasoning
Bienvenu et al. 2024
. Another preliminary result of particular interest in our context concerns the monotonicity of these entailment relations. For that, we first need to define monotonicity in the context of WKBs.

Definition 5
.

Let
\vDash
be an entailment relation defined with respect to WKBs. Then
\vDash
is
monotonic
if, for any WKB
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
, we have

\mathcal{K}_{\omega}\vDash\tau\implies\mathcal{K}^{\prime}_{\omega^{\prime}}\vDash\tau

where
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
and
\omega^{\prime}(\tau^{\prime})=\omega(\tau^{\prime})
for all
\tau^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}
.

This characterization of monotonicity is fairly straightforward: the knowledge base
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
extends the information contained in
\mathcal{K}
. New information and its associated costs can be added to the TBox or ABox, while the existing information and its costs are preserved. Under this framework, only one of the four mentioned entailment relations is monotonic.

Proposition 1
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the entailment relation
\vDash^{k}_{c}
is monotonic, while
\vDash^{k}_{p}
,
\vDash^{opt}_{c}
and
\vDash^{opt}_{p}
are non-monotonic.

2.3
Ranking Semantics and C-Representations – From FOL to
\mathcal{ALCO}

In this section, we present a semantic framework aimed at modeling non-monotonic reasoning:
c-representations

Kern-Isberner 2001
. They are a subclass of
ranking functions
, also known as
Ordinal Conditional Functions
(OCFs)
Spohn 2012
. Both frameworks were originally introduced for propositional logic, but extensions to a conditional language in FOL have been proposed first for ranking functions
Kern-Isberner and Thimm 2012
, and subsequently tailored for c-representations
Hahn et al. 2024
. Since DLs correspond to specific fragments of FOL, these characterizations can be applied to DLs as well. Therefore, we introduce ranking functions and c-representations directly for the DL
\mathcal{ALCO}
: the following definitions are those presented in
Hahn et al. 2024
for FOL, here reformulated to match the expressivity of
defeasible

\mathcal{ALCO}
. A
defeasible

\mathcal{ALCO}
KB is a triple
\mathcal{K}=(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{D},\mathcal{A})
, where
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{A}
are, respectively, a TBox containing GCIs
C\sqsubseteq D
, and an ABox containing assertions
a
:
C
and
(a,b)
:
r
, while
\mathcal{D}=\{C_{1}\>\raisebox{1.93747pt}{$\sqsubset$}\hskip-7.3194pt\raisebox{-2.58334pt}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\sim$}}\>D_{1},\ldots,C_{n}\>\raisebox{1.93747pt}{$\sqsubset$}\hskip-7.3194pt\raisebox{-2.58334pt}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\sim$}}\>D_{n}\}
is a finite set of DCIs
Britz et al. 2021
.

Remark.

In order to adapt the definition for conditional FOL to defeasible
\mathcal{ALCO}
, we rely on the translation of
\mathcal{ALCO}
statements in FOL as presented in
Hahn et al. 2024
:

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mbox{$a$:\hskip 2.84544pt$C$}&\rightsquigarrow&C(a)\\
\mbox{$(a,b)$:\hskip 2.84544pt$r$}&\rightsquigarrow&r(a,b)\\
C\sqsubseteq D&\rightsquigarrow&\forall x(C(x)\Rightarrow D(x))\\
C&\rightsquigarrow&C(x)\\
C\>\raisebox{1.93747pt}{$\sqsubset$}\hskip-7.3194pt\raisebox{-2.58334pt}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\sim$}}\>D&\rightsquigarrow&(D(x)|C(x))\\
\end{array}

In particular, a defeasible inclusion is interpreted as an open conditional. We refer the reader to
Hahn et al. 2024
to check the details and notation used for conditional FOL. Here, we also define the additional symbol
\>\raisebox{1.93747pt}{$\sqsubset$}\hskip-7.3194pt\raisebox{-2.58334pt}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\sim$}}\>^{\forall}
in order to refer to a universally quantified defeasible conditional, as introduced in
Kern-Isberner and Thimm 2012
. That is:

C\>\raisebox{1.93747pt}{$\sqsubset$}\hskip-7.3194pt\raisebox{-2.58334pt}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\sim$}}\>^{\forall}D\rightsquigarrow\forall x(D(x)|C(x)).

Intuitively, a
quantified DCI

C\>\raisebox{1.93747pt}{$\sqsubset$}\hskip-7.3194pt\raisebox{-2.58334pt}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\sim$}}\>^{\forall}D
expresses that, in the most typical situations,
a\in C^{\mathcal{I}}
implies
a\in D^{\mathcal{I}}
for
every

a\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}
, whereas
C\>\raisebox{1.93747pt}{$\sqsubset$}\hskip-7.3194pt\raisebox{-2.58334pt}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\sim$}}\>D
expresses that, in the most typical situations,
a\in C^{\mathcal{I}}
implies
a\in D^{\mathcal{I}}
for
certain
elements
a\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}
: the most preferred (or typical) members of
C^{\mathcal{I}}
. In what follows, the technical differences between various forms of defeasible concept inclusions become apparent.

Another aspect we adapt from the FOL formulation is Herbrand semantics, a well-known form of first-order interpretations, that in
Kern-Isberner and Thimm 2012
are used to define ranking semantics for defeasible reasoning. In the present setting, we fix a finite set of individual names
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{N}_{I}
, referred to as the
Herbrand Universe
. That is,
\mathcal{U}
corresponds to the set
\mathcal{N}_{I}
in the vocabulary, hence it includes all the individual names appearing in the KB and possibly others. An
\mathcal{ALCO}
interpretation
\mathcal{I}
is a
Herbrand interpretation
if
\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}=\mathcal{U}
and
a^{\mathcal{I}}=a
for all
a\in\mathcal{U}
. The set of all Herbrand interpretations for a given
\mathcal{ALCO}
vocabulary is denoted by
\Omega_{\mathcal{N}}
. In the following, we assume that all interpretations are Herbrand interpretations defined over some vocabulary. The following definition are all reformulations of notions presented for conditional FOL in
Kern-Isberner and Thimm 2012
;
Hahn et al. 2024
.

Definition 6
.

A
ranking function

\kappa
is a mapping
\kappa:\Omega_{\mathcal{N}}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, such that
\kappa^{-1}(0)\neq\emptyset
.

Ranking functions are generally interpreted as representing the expectations of an agent: the lower the rank associated to an interpretation, the more plausible the represented situation is considered by the agent. The interpretations with rank
0
describe what the agent expects to hold.

Although these ranking functions are initially defined on interpretations, they can be extended to
\mathcal{ALCO}
statements.

Definition 7
.

Let
\kappa
be a ranking function.

•

for every assertion
a
:
C
,
\kappa(\mbox{$a$:\hskip 2.84544pt$C$})=\min_{\mathcal{I}\Vdash a:C}\kappa(\mathcal{I})
;

•

for every assertion
(a,b)
:
r
,
\kappa(\mbox{$(a,b)$:\hskip 2.84544pt$r$})=\min_{\mathcal{I}\Vdash(a,b):r}\kappa(\mathcal{I})
;

•

for every TBox statement
C\sqsubseteq D
,
\kappa(C\sqsubseteq D)=\min_{\mathcal{I}\Vdash C\sqsubseteq D}\kappa(\mathcal{I})
;

•

for every concept
C
,
\kappa(C)=\min_{a\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}}\kappa(\mbox{$a$:\hskip 2.84544pt$C$})
;

•

for every DBox statement
C\>\raisebox{1.93747pt}{$\sqsubset$}\hskip-7.3194pt\raisebox{-2.58334pt}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\sim$}}\>D
,
\kappa(C\>\raisebox{1.93747pt}{$\sqsubset$}\hskip-7.3194pt\raisebox{-2.58334pt}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\sim$}}\>D)=\min_{a\in\Delta^{\mathcal{I}}}(\kappa(\mbox{$a$:\hskip 2.84544pt$C\sqcap D$})-\kappa(\mbox{$a$:\hskip 2.84544pt$C$}))
.

Based on such ranking functions, we can define the satisfaction relation of classical
\mathcal{ALCO}
statements and
\>\raisebox{1.93747pt}{$\sqsubset$}\hskip-7.3194pt\raisebox{-2.58334pt}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\sim$}}\>^{\forall}
-inclusions.

Definition 8
.

Given a ranking function
\kappa
we define satisfaction of
\mathcal{ALCO}
statements as follows:

•

\kappa\Vdash\mbox{$a$:\hskip 2.84544pt$C$}
iff
\mathcal{I}\Vdash\mbox{$a$:\hskip 2.84544pt$C$}
for all
\mathcal{I}\in\kappa^{-1}(0)
.

•

\kappa\Vdash\mbox{$(a,b)$:\hskip 2.84544pt$r$}
iff
\mathcal{I}\Vdash\mbox{$(a,b)$:\hskip 2.84544pt$r$}
for all
\mathcal{I}\in\kappa^{-1}(0)
.

•

\kappa\Vdash C\sqsubseteq D
iff
\mathcal{I}\Vdash C\sqsubseteq D
for all
\mathcal{I}\in\kappa^{-1}(0)
.

•

\kappa\Vdash C\>\raisebox{1.93747pt}{$\sqsubset$}\hskip-7.3194pt\raisebox{-2.58334pt}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\sim$}}\>^{\forall}D
iff
\kappa(\mbox{$a$:\hskip 2.84544pt$C\sqcap D$})<\kappa(\mbox{$a$:\hskip 2.84544pt$C\sqcap\neg D$})
for all
a\in\mathcal{U}
.

More elaborate is the definition of acceptance for DCIs.

Definition 9
.

Let
C\>\raisebox{1.93747pt}{$\sqsubset$}\hskip-7.3194pt\raisebox{-2.58334pt}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\sim$}}\>D
be any defeasible subsumption. We say that
a\in\mathcal{U}
is a
weak representative
for
C\>\raisebox{1.93747pt}{$\sqsubset$}\hskip-7.3194pt\raisebox{-2.58334pt}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\sim$}}\>D
if the following conditions hold:

\kappa(\mbox{$a$:\hskip 2.84544pt$C\sqcap D$})=\kappa(C\sqcap D)

(1)

\kappa(\mbox{$a$:\hskip 2.84544pt$C\sqcap D$})<\kappa(\mbox{$a$:\hskip 2.84544pt$C\sqcap\neg{D}$}).

(2)

and denote the set of weak representatives by
WRep(C\>\raisebox{1.93747pt}{$\sqsubset$}\hskip-7.3194pt\raisebox{-2.58334pt}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\sim$}}\>D)
. A
strong representative
of
C\>\raisebox{1.93747pt}{$\sqsubset$}\hskip-7.3194pt\raisebox{-2.58334pt}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\sim$}}\>D
is defined as a weak representative of
C\>\raisebox{1.93747pt}{$\sqsubset$}\hskip-7.3194pt\raisebox{-2.58334pt}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\sim$}}\>D
such that

\kappa(\mbox{$a$:\hskip 2.84544pt$C\sqcap\neg{D}$})=\min_{b\in wrep(C\scriptsize\>\raisebox{1.35623pt}{$\sqsubset$}\hskip-5.12358pt\raisebox{-1.80835pt}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\sim$}}\>\normalsize D)}\kappa(\mbox{$b$:\hskip 2.84544pt$C\sqcap\neg{D}$})

(3)

We denote the set of strong representatives by
Rep(C\>\raisebox{1.93747pt}{$\sqsubset$}\hskip-7.3194pt\raisebox{-2.58334pt}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\sim$}}\>D)
.

The conditions above for representatives may appear somewhat technical. However, the intuition behind each condition can be described as follows. If
a
is a (strong) representative for the rule
C\>\raisebox{1.93747pt}{$\sqsubset$}\hskip-7.3194pt\raisebox{-2.58334pt}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\sim$}}\>D
, then
a
is a “maximally typical” instance of both
C
and
D
(as per condition (1)). Moreover, it is more “typical” for
a
to satisfy the defeasible inclusion than to violate it (according to condition (2)), and condition (3) specifies that when
a
does violate
C\>\raisebox{1.93747pt}{$\sqsubset$}\hskip-7.3194pt\raisebox{-2.58334pt}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\sim$}}\>D
, it does so for the “most common” reasons, compared to other groundings that satisfy (1) and (2). This intermediary definition enables us to define acceptance for open conditionals, that is, defeasible inclusions.

Definition 10
.

Let
\kappa
be a ranking function and
C\>\raisebox{1.93747pt}{$\sqsubset$}\hskip-7.3194pt\raisebox{-2.58334pt}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\sim$}}\>D
be a defeasible inclusion. Then we say that

5
Conclusions

In this paper, we provide a comparison between the frameworks of cost-based semantics for inconsistency-tolerant query answering in the DL
\mathcal{ALCO}
, and the ranking function based semantics of
c
-representations for defeasible reasoning in First-Order Logic. In doing so, we highlight the similarities between both frameworks and suggest ways in which certain aspects can be expressed by the other under certain conditions. In particular, we show that for a given
c
-representation
\kappa
for a defeasible knowledge base
\mathcal{K}
, we can define a weighted knowledge based
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
such that
cost_{\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}(\mathcal{I})<cost_{\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}(\mathcal{I}^{\prime})
iff.
\kappa(\mathcal{I})<\kappa(\mathcal{I}^{\prime})
, and show that when certain conditions are satisfied, the converse is possible. Moreover, we consider the resulting links between entailment in each framework and show that certain entailment relations which are defined for one framework can be expressed in terms of entailment relations defined in the other. Overall, the goal of this paper is to provide a technically precise means to understand one framework in terms of the other, and provide a starting point for unifying both approaches from the query-answering and defeasible reasoning communities, where desirable. This has the potential to benefit both frameworks, since under certain conditions they stand to inherit methodologies and results from the other framework, as well as increase the expressivity and scope of applicability for both frameworks.

As such, immediate future work to consider is to what extent results which hold in one framework can be applied to the other. In particular, it is worth investigating the complexity results given in cost-based semantics
Bienvenu et al. 2024
as a means to determine complexity bounds for defeasible reasoning in DLs. On the other hand, it would be worth investigating methods used for determining relevant ranking functions within defeasible reasoning, such as the Constraint Satisfaction problems considered in
Beierle et al. 2018
, as a means to algorithmically determine cost functions within weighted knowledge bases, rather than requiring such cost functions to be declared. In order to further unify the field, a more detailed comparison between cost-based semantics and other semantics for defeasible descriptions logics, such as the more widely used preferential interpretations
Britz et al. 2021
, could be considered.

Proposition
7
.
For any WKB
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
, we have that

cost_{\mathcal{K}_{\omega}}(\mathcal{I})=cost_{\mathcal{K}^{\prime}_{\omega^{\prime}}}(\mathcal{I})

for all

Proof.

\mathcal{K}_{\omega}=((\mathcal{T},\mathcal{A}),\omega)

Then note that, since
\omega(\tau)=\omega^{\prime}(\tau)
for all
\tau\in\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\cup\mathcal{T}
. We have that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}\omega^{\prime}(\tau)|\textit{vio}_{\tau}(\mathcal{I})|+\sum\limits_{\alpha\in\textit{vio}_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{I})}\omega^{\prime}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}}\omega(\tau)|\textit{vio}_{\tau}(\mathcal{I})|+\sum\limits_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\setminus\mathcal{T}}\omega^{\prime}(\tau)|\textit{vio}_{\tau}(\mathcal{I})|+\sum\limits_{\alpha\in\textit{vio}_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{I})}\omega^{\prime}(\alpha).

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}}\omega(\tau)|\textit{vio}_{\tau}(\mathcal{I})|+\sum\limits_{\alpha\in\textit{vio}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{I})}\omega(\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}}\omega(\tau)|\textit{vio}_{\tau}(\mathcal{I})|+\sum\limits_{\alpha\in\textit{vio}_{\mathcal{A}\setminus\mathcal{A}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{I})}\omega(\alpha)+\sum\limits_{\alpha\in\textit{vio}_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{I})}\omega^{\prime}(\alpha).

. But then for any

\mathcal{I}
for every

Köhler’s Conjecture on Hecke Theta Series of Weight One
Mahima Kumar, Gabor Wiese

Abstract
In this article, we prove a conjecture of Günter Köhler on the ambiguity of the quadratic field in the definition of Hecke theta series by deriving it from a similar statement on two-dimensional Galois representations induced from characters of quadratic fields. MSC Classification: 11F80 (Galois representations), 11F11 (Holomorphic modular forms of integral weight), 11R37 (Class field theory)

Introduction

By definition, a Hecke theta series is associated with a Hecke character of a quadratic field.
It is natural to ask under which conditions the quadratic field is uniquely determined by the theta series.
This question is addressed in a conjecture by Günter Köhler in his book (
[
Köh11
, Conjecture 5.5]
) and, in a more precise form, in the the subsequent article
[
Köh18
]
.

In this paper we prove a slightly modified version of Köhler’s conjecture (see Theorem
4.1
).
We derive it from a completely analogous question on
2
-dimensional representations of a group that are inductions of a character of an index-
2
subgroup (see Proposition
2.1
). This group theoretic result is well known (e.g.
[
Roh19a
, Propositions 9, 10]
,
[
ST18
, Proposition 2.3]
, where the representations are called triply monomial and triply imprimitive, respectively).
Our presentation is independent, very explicit with concrete matrix realisations and follows the structure of Köhler’s conjecture.

We also determine all possible images of
2
-dimensional group representations that can be induced by characters of two different index-
2
subgroups (see Proposition
2.2
). This provides a different perspective on the list of almost abelian
2
-groups with a faithful
2
-dimensional representation from
[
Roh19a
, Proposition 18]
.

Theta series of weight one and their Galois representations

In this section, we collect statements about Hecke and Dirichlet characters, their associated theta series and their Galois counterparts, which we will need to pass from the representation theoretic result to a proof of Köhler’s conjecture. 3.3
Galois representations attached to theta series of weight one

In this section, we collect the statements from class field theory, which we need to pass between Dirichlet and Galois characters, and construct the Galois representation attached to the theta series from Theorem
3.4
.
We continue to work with a quadratic extension
K
of
\mathbb{Q}
. By
\Frob_{p}
we denote any choice of Frobenius element at
p
in the Galois group of any Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
and by
\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}}
a Frobenius element at a prime
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
in the Galois group of any Galois extension of
K
.

Theorem 3.5

(Artin Reciprocity Law)
.

(
[
Neu11
, III.9.2]
) For an integral ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
K
, there exists an abelian extension
K^{\mathfrak{m}}/K
, called the
narrow ray class field of modulus
\mathfrak{m}
over
K
, such that there is an isomorphism

\left(\frac{\left.K^{\mathfrak{m}}\middle|K\right.}{\cdot}\right)\colon J(\mathfrak{m})/P(\mathfrak{m})\xrightarrow{\sim}\Gal(K^{\mathfrak{m}}/K);\mathfrak{a}\mapsto\left(\frac{\left.K^{\mathfrak{m}}\middle|K\right.}{\mathfrak{a}}\right)

where
\left(\frac{\left.K^{\mathfrak{m}}\middle|K\right.}{\mathfrak{p}}\right)=\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}}
for all prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}
of
K
coprime to
\mathfrak{m}
.
Here
\left(\frac{\left.K^{\mathfrak{m}}\middle|K\right.}{\mathfrak{a}}\right)
is the Artin symbol.

The Artin reciprocity law thus allows us to obtain from any Dirichlet character
\xi
of modulus
\mathfrak{m}
for
K
a character
\chi
of
\Gal(L/K)
for any Galois extension
L/K
containing
K^{\mathfrak{m}}
via

\chi\colon\Gal(L/K)\twoheadrightarrow\Gal(K^{\mathfrak{m}}/K)\xrightarrow{\left(\frac{\left.K^{\mathfrak{m}}\middle|K\right.}{\cdot}\right)^{-1}}J(\mathfrak{m})/P(\mathfrak{m})\xrightarrow{\xi}\mathbb{C}^{\times}\textnormal{ and }\chi(\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}})=\xi(\mathfrak{p})

(3.5)

for any prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
K
.

Moreover, any character of the Galois group of a Galois extension
L
of
K
can be obtained from some Dirichlet character of
K
in this way because any abelian extension of
K
is contained in some
K^{\mathfrak{m}}
by a generalisation of the theorem of Kronecker–Weber (
[
Neu11
, III.7.9]
).

If
K
is totally real and
L
totally imaginary, then
\Gal(L/K)
contains a complex conjugation
c_{\tau}
for each embedding
\tau:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}
(which is uniquely defined up to conjugation in
\Gal(L/K)
and consequently unique in any abelian quotient of
\Gal(L/K)
). Via the inverse Artin reciprocity map,
c_{\tau}
corresponds to an ideal class, which we denote
\mathfrak{c}_{\tau}
.
We have
\xi(\mathfrak{c}_{\tau})=\chi(c_{\tau})=(-1)^{p_{\tau}}
. This can, for instance, be seen by viewing the Dirichlet character as a character of the idèle class group (
[
Neu92
, VII.6.14]
) and using the definition of the Artin reciprocity map for infinite places (
[
Neu11
, §II.5]
).

The following well-known theorem establishes the existence of a Galois representation attached to a theta series of weight one.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\Theta_{1}(K,\xi,z)
be a theta series of weight one coming from Theorem
3.4
.
Let
L/\mathbb{Q}
be any Galois extension containing
K^{\mathfrak{m}}
and let
\chi
be the corresponding character of
\Gal(L/K)
obtained from
\xi
via the Artin reciprocity map.

Then the Galois representation

\rho={\rm Ind}_{\Gal(L/K)}^{\Gal(L/\mathbb{Q})}(\chi)\colon\Gal(L/\mathbb{Q})\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})

is attached to
\Theta_{1}(K,\xi,z)
in the sense that
\tr(\rho(\Frob_{p}))=a_{p}
for all primes
p
coprime to
|D|N(\mathfrak{m})
.
Moreover, the representation
\rho
is odd, i.e. the determinant of complex conjugation equals
-1
.

Proof.

The assumptions on
\xi
imply that
\chi
is different from its conjugate, when conjugated by any
\sigma\in\Gal(L/\mathbb{Q})\setminus\Gal(L/K)
. Consequently, by Mackey’s criterion,
\rho
is irreducible.
By Chebotarev’s Density Theorem,
\rho
is uniquely determined by the equality
\tr(\rho(\Frob_{p}))=a_{p}
for all primes
p
coprime to
|D|N(\mathfrak{m})
. We notice that such
p
are either split or inert in
K
, and handle these two cases one by one.

Suppose that
p
is inert in
K
. Then by Lemma
3.3
, we find
a_{p}=0
. Under the same assumption,
\Frob_{p}\in\Gal(L/\mathbb{Q})\setminus\Gal(L/K)
, whence (
2.1
) yields
\tr(\rho(\Frob_{p}))=0
.

Now consider that
p
splits in
K
, more precisely,
p\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathfrak{p}_{1}\mathfrak{p}_{2}
. Then by Lemma
3.3
, we find
a_{p}=\xi(\mathfrak{p}_{1})+\xi(\mathfrak{p}_{2})
. On the Galois side, we have
\Frob_{p}\in\Gal(L/K)
and we can assume without loss of generality
\Frob_{p}=\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}
, whence
\sigma^{-1}\circ\Frob_{p}\circ\sigma=\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}
. We thus find from (
2.1
) and (
3.5
)

\tr(\rho(\Frob_{p}))=\chi(\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}})+\chi(\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}})=\xi(\mathfrak{p}_{1})+\xi(\mathfrak{p}_{2}).

If
K
is imaginary, then complex conjugation does not fix it; consequently, the conjugacy class of complex conjugation
c\in\Gal(L/\mathbb{Q})
does not lie in the subgroup
\Gal(L/K)
, whence it is an involution of trace
0
, implying that its determinant is
-1
.
If
K
is real, then for each embedding
\tau:K\to\mathbb{R}
, we have a complex conjugation
c_{\tau}\in\Gal(L/K)
.
Moreover,
\sigma^{-1}\circ c_{\tau}\circ\sigma
is a complex conjugation
c_{\tau\sigma}
for the other embedding
\tau\sigma
, whence without loss of generality
\chi(c)=\chi(c_{\tau})=\xi(\mathfrak{c}_{\tau})=(-1)^{p_{\tau}}
and
\chi^{\sigma}(c_{\tau})=\chi(c_{\tau\sigma})=(-1)^{p_{\tau\sigma}}
. This finally yields
\det(\rho(c))=(-1)^{p_{\tau}+p_{\tau\sigma}}=-1
by the condition on the
p_{\tau}
.
∎

4

In this section, we prove a slightly modified version of Köhler’s conjecture (

The character

(2)

Remark 4.2
.

(a)

We chose to follow Köhler’s original formulation quite closely in Theorem
4.1
but made some notational changes (e.g. Dirichlet characters vs. Hecke characters) and suppressed the explicit mentions of the moduli of the characters and the discriminants of the quadratic fields.

(b)

In Köhler’s original formulation, there was an additional item (1) in (
B
), which we removed as it need not always hold because our computation of the images of Galois representations shows that the characters can have arbitrarily large order.

(c)

If
\xi
is not primitive, the implication (

)
\Rightarrow

To construct a counter example, let

be a primitive Dirichlet character satisfying both conditions of (

\tilde{\xi}^{\prime\prime}

for

(e)

In the case of

(f)

In the case of (

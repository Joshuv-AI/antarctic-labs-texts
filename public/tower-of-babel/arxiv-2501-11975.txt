Matched pairs and Yang-Baxter operators
Yunnan Li

Abstract
. Recently, Ferri and Sciandra introduced two equivalent algebraic structures, matched pair of actions on an arbitrary Hopf algebra and Yetter-Drinfeld brace. In fact, they equivalently produce braiding operators on Hopf algebras satisfying the braid equation, thus generalize the construction of Yang-Baxter operators by Lu, Yan and Zhu from braiding operators on groups, and also by Angiono, Galindo and Vendramin from cocommutative Hopf braces. In this paper, we provide equivalence conditions for such kind of Yang-Baxter operators to be involutive. Particularly, we give a positive answer for an open problem raised by Ferri and Sciandra, namely, a matched pair of actions on a Hopf algebra H H induces an involutive Yang-Baxter operator if and only if its intrinsic Hopf algebra H ⇀ H_{\rightharpoonup} in the category of Yetter-Drinfeld modules over H H is braided commutative. Also, we show that the double cross product H ⋈ H H\bowtie H is a Hopf algebra with a projection and H ⇀ H_{\rightharpoonup} serves as its subalgebra of coinvariants. As an illustration, we use a simplified characterization to classify matched pairs of actions on the 8-dimensional non-semisimple Hopf algebra A C 2 × C 2 A_{C_{2}\times C_{2}} and analyze the associated Yang-Baxter operators to find that they are all involutive.



1.
Introduction

The Yang-Baxter equation is one of the fundamental equations in mathematical physics,
initially appearing in both quantum and statistical mechanics, and introducing the theory of quantum group. How to systematically construct and classify its solutions is an extremely challenging open problem.

Given a vector space
V
, let
R:V\otimes V\to V\otimes V
be a linear automorphism satisfying the Yang-Baxter equation, namely

R_{12}R_{13}R_{23}=R_{23}R_{13}R_{12}.

Denote
\tau
the usual flip map, i.e.
\tau(u\otimes v)=v\otimes u
. Then
r=\tau R
is a solution of the braid equation

(r\otimes{\rm id})({\rm id}\otimes r)(r\otimes{\rm id})=({\rm id}\otimes r)(r\otimes{\rm id})({\rm id}\otimes r),

and called a
Yang-Baxter operator
. It gives rise to a representation of the braid group
B_{n}
on the tensor space
V^{\otimes n}
for any
n
. In particular, if
r
is involutive, i.e.
r^{2}={\rm id}^{\otimes 2}
, then it becomes a representation of the symmetric group
S_{n}
on
V^{\otimes n}
.

In 1992, Drinfeld proposed in
[
4
]
the study of set-theoretic solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation. In the same decade, Gateva-Ivanova and Van den Bergh
[
8
]
, Etingof, Schedler and Soloviev
[
6
]
, as well as Lu, Yan and Zhu
[
14
]
, gave their seminal work independently. Specifically, Lu, Yan and Zhu
[
14
]
introduced a general construction method for set-theoretic solutions to the Yang-Baxter equation using braiding operators on groups. Afterwards, Takeuchi noticed in
[
26
]
that the LYZ construction of braided group is equivalent to certain matched pair of groups with compatible actions.

So far the study of set-theoretic solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation has got explosive development. An effective and fruitful approach
is to identify and study the underlying algebraic structures. More precisely,
one can focus on e.g. the (semi)group and ring theoretical aspects that occur, and study them for specific classes of set-theoretic solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation. For example, the milestone research
[
22
]
of Rump introduced braces as a tool to classify all non-degenerate involutive set-theoretic solutions, and one decade later Guarnieri and Vendramin in
[
9
]
introduced the notion of skew brace to produce non-degenerate solutions in general, and pointed out that skew left brace is equivalent to LYZ’s braided group. Recently, one more equivalent notion, namely post-(Lie) group, was also introduced in
[
3
]
as the integration of post-Lie algebras.

On the other hand, matched pair of Hopf algebras was introduced by Takeuchi in
[
25
]
, and presented by Majid with the modern definition; see
[
16
,
17
]
.
Generalizing the above framework from groups to Hopf algebras, Angiono, Galindo, and Vendramin in
[
1
]
introduced the notion of Hopf brace, and demonstrated that cocommutative Hopf braces are equivalent to matched pairs on cocommutative Hopf algebras with compatible actions, and produce Yang-Baxter operators.
Recently, the construction of Yang-Baxter operators in
[
1
]
from Hopf braces was reformulated in
[
13
]
from the perspective of cocommutative post-Hopf algebras, with the universal enveloping algebra of a post-Lie algebra
[
20
,
5
]
as a basic model.

Also, Guccione, Guccione and Vendramin in
[
10
]
extended the ideas in
[
1
]
to the general setting of non-degenerate solutions in symmetric tensor categories, and proposed braiding operators on cocommutative Hopf algebras. This approach was further generalized to arbitrary Hopf algebras by Guccione, Guccione and Valqui in
[
11
]
, where they established a more general framework, so-called weak braiding operator and Hopf
q
-brace, relating to left non-degenerate solutions of the braid equation.

As another approach to removing the cocommutativity hypothesis in
[
1
]
, Ferri and Sciandra
[
7
]
recently introduced the notion of matched pair of actions as matched pair on a Hopf algebra with compatible actions, and innovatively discovered its equivalent structure, so-called Yetter-Drinfeld brace. Roughly speaking,
Yetter-Drinfeld brace replaces the first ordinary Hopf algebra in a Hopf brace by a Hopf algebra in the Yetter-Drinfeld module category over the second one.
Relating to Majid’s transmutation theory, the authors in
[
7
]
also pointed out that coquasitriangular structures produce matched pairs of actions and provided concrete examples to illustrate this result. During our study about the work
[
7
]
, we accidentally found that Guccione, Guccione and Valqui have recognized the equivalence between matched pairs of actions on Hopf algebras and braiding operators on Hopf algebras satisfying the braid relation, and pointed it out in their updated version
[
12
]
.
In addition, Sciandra further obtained one more equivalent structure, so-called Yetter-Drinfeld post-Hopf algebra, in
[
23
]
, generalizing the equivalence between cocommutative Hopf braces and cocommutative post-Hopf algebras.

In summary, the aforementioned algebraic structures have the following connection.

•

According to
[
1
,
10
,
13
]
, the following four categories are all isomorphic:

(i)

matched pairs of actions on cocommutative Hopf algebras

(ii)

braiding operators on cocommutative Hopf algebras

(iii)

cocommutative Hopf braces

(iv)

cocommutative post-Hopf algebras

•

In
[
7
,
12
,
23
]
the above isomorphisms were generalized to four larger categories:

(i)

matched pairs of actions on Hopf algebras

(ii)

braiding operators on Hopf algebras

(iii)

Yetter-Drinfeld braces

(iv)

Yetter-Drinfeld post-Hopf algebras

In order to simplify the terminology, we only focus on matched pair of actions, braiding operator and Yetter-Drinfeld brace here, while the post-Hopf counterpart will not be discussed.

In this paper, we first revisit the equivalence between matched pairs of actions on an arbitrary Hopf algebra and braiding operators on it which satisfy the braid relation, so it is meaningful to study matched pairs of actions as a rich source for Yang-Baxter operators.
Then as the main result, we provide several equivalence conditions for such kind of braiding operators to be involutive. Particularly, we solve a problem left in
[
7
]
by confirming that if a matched pair of actions on a Hopf algebra
H
induces an involutive braiding operator, then its intrinsic Hopf algebra in the Yetter-Drinfeld
H
-module category is braided commutative, and vice versa. Namely, the role of braces inside the family of skew braces has an extension in the context of Yetter-Drinfeld braces.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall the main notions, namely matched pair of actions on a Hopf algebra and Yetter-Drinfeld brace.
In Section
3
, we first reprove that a matched pair of actions on a Hopf algebra
H
is equivalent to a braiding operator
r
on
H
satisfying the braid relation, especially a Yang-Baxter operator (Theorem
3.2
) and give some concise formulas involving
r
and the antipode of
H
(Proposition
3.4
).
Next we provide several equivalence conditions for the Yang-Baxter operator
r
to be involutive (Theorem
3.5
), and then justify them by given the explicit formulas for the adjoint actions of the intrinsic braided Hopf algebra
H_{\rightharpoonup}
from a matched pair of actions on
H
(Proposition
3.8
). Also, we find a Hopf algebra isomorphism between the double cross product
H\bowtie H
and the bosonization
H_{\rightharpoonup}\#H
(Theorem
3.11
).
In Section
4
, we use a simplified characterization of matched pairs of actions on Hopf algebras (Theorem
4.1
) to classify matched pairs of actions on an
8
-dimensional non-semisimple Hopf algebra
A_{C_{2}\times C_{2}}
(Example
4.3
), and then analyze their associated Yang-Baxter operators (Theorem
4.4
) as an illustration.

Convention.

In this paper, we fix an algebraically closed ground field
{\mathbf{k}}
of characteristic 0.
All the objects under discussion, including vector spaces, algebras and tensor products, are taken over
{\mathbf{k}}
by default.

For any unital algebra
(A,m,u)
with multiplication
m
and the unit map
u:{\mathbf{k}}\to A
, let
m^{(0)}={\rm id}
and for

we write

For any coalgebra
(C,\Delta,\varepsilon)
, we compress the Sweedler notation of the comultiplication
\Delta
as

\Delta(x)=x_{1}\otimes x_{2}

for simplicity.
Furthermore, let
\Delta^{(0)}={\rm id}
and for
n\geq 1
we write

\Delta^{(n)}(x)=(\Delta\otimes{\rm id}^{\otimes(n-1)})\cdots(\Delta\otimes{\rm id})\Delta(x)=x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{n+1}.

By convention, a Hopf algebra is denoted by
H=(H,\cdot\,,1,\Delta,\varepsilon,S)
.
Denote by
G(H)
the set of group-like elements in
H
, which is a group. Denote by
P_{g,h}(H)
the subspace of
(g,h)
-primitive elements in

for
g,h\in G(H)
.
For other basic notions of Hopf algebras, we follow the textbooks
[
19
]
.

2.
Matched pairs of actions and Yetter-Drinfeld braces

First we recall the notion of matched pair of Hopf algebras
in Majid’s book
[
17
]
.

Definition 2.1

(
[
17
, § 7.2]
)
.

A
matched pair of Hopf algebras
is a quadruple
(H,K,\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup)
, where
H
and
K

are Hopf algebras,
\rightharpoonup
is a left
H
-module coalgebra action on
K
,
\leftharpoonup
is a right
K
-module coalgebra action on
H
such that

(1)

\displaystyle x\rightharpoonup ab

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{1}\rightharpoonup a_{1})((x_{2}\leftharpoonup a_{2})\rightharpoonup b),

(2)

\displaystyle x\rightharpoonup 1_{K}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{H}(x)1_{K},

(3)

\displaystyle xy\leftharpoonup a

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x\leftharpoonup(y_{1}\rightharpoonup a_{1}))(y_{2}\leftharpoonup a_{2}),

(4)

\displaystyle 1_{H}\leftharpoonup a

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{K}(a)1_{H},

(5)

\displaystyle(x_{1}\rightharpoonup a_{1})\otimes(x_{2}\leftharpoonup a_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{2}\rightharpoonup a_{2})\otimes(x_{1}\leftharpoonup a_{1})

for
x,y\in H
and
a,b\in K
.

For a matched pair of Hopf algebras
(H,K,\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup)
, the
double cross product

K\bowtie H
is a Hopf algebra structure on
K\otimes H
equipped with the product

(6)

\displaystyle(a\otimes x)(b\otimes y)

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle a(x_{1}\rightharpoonup b_{1})\otimes(x_{2}\leftharpoonup b_{2})y,\quad\forall x,y\in H,\ a,b\in K,

and the usual tensor coproduct. The antipode of
K\bowtie H
is given by

S(a\otimes x)=(1\otimes S(x))(S(a)\otimes 1)=(S(x_{1})\rightharpoonup S(a_{1}))\otimes(S(x_{2})\leftharpoonup S(a_{2})).

Recently Ferri and Sciandra further considered a certain subclass of matched pairs of Hopf algebras, then introduced two equivalent notions, matched pair of actions on a Hopf algebra and Yetter-Drinfeld brace.

First we recall the notion of matched pair of actions, generalizing the braided group construction of Lu, Yan and Zhu in
[
14
]
.

Definition 2.2

(
[
7
]
)
.

A matched pair
(H,H,\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup)
of Hopf algebras
is called a
matched pair of actions
on
H
, when

(7)

\displaystyle xy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{1}\rightharpoonup y_{1})(x_{2}\leftharpoonup y_{2}),\quad\forall x,y\in H.

A matched pair of actions on
H
will be abbreviated as
(H,\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(H,\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup)
be a matched pair of actions on a Hopf algebra
H
. For any
x,y\in H
, we have

(8)

\displaystyle S(x\rightharpoonup y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x\leftharpoonup y_{1})\rightharpoonup S(y_{2}),

(9)

\displaystyle S(x\leftharpoonup y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(x_{1})\leftharpoonup(x_{2}\rightharpoonup y).

Equivalently,

(10)

\displaystyle S(x_{1}\leftharpoonup y_{1})\rightharpoonup S(x_{2}\rightharpoonup y_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon(x)S(y),

(11)

\displaystyle S(x_{1}\leftharpoonup y_{1})\leftharpoonup S(x_{2}\rightharpoonup y_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon(y)S(x).

Proof.

The result of the following convolution product implies Eq. (
8
).

\displaystyle((x_{1}\leftharpoonup y_{1})\rightharpoonup S(y_{2}))(x_{2}\rightharpoonup y_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((x_{1}\leftharpoonup y_{1})\rightharpoonup S(y_{4}))((x_{2}\leftharpoonup y_{2}S(y_{3}))\rightharpoonup y_{5})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((x_{1}\leftharpoonup y_{1})\rightharpoonup S(y_{4}))(((x_{2}\leftharpoonup y_{2})\leftharpoonup S(y_{3}))\rightharpoonup y_{5})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:MP1}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle(x\leftharpoonup y_{1})\rightharpoonup S(y_{2})y_{3}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:MP2}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\varepsilon(x\leftharpoonup y)1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon(x)\varepsilon(y)1,

and then

\displaystyle S(x_{1}\leftharpoonup y_{1})\rightharpoonup S(x_{2}\rightharpoonup y_{2})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:mp-antipode-1}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle S(x_{1}\leftharpoonup y_{1})\rightharpoonup((x_{2}\leftharpoonup y_{2})\rightharpoonup S(y_{3}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(x_{1}\leftharpoonup y_{1})(x_{2}\leftharpoonup y_{2})\rightharpoonup S(y_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon(x)\varepsilon(y_{1})S(y_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon(x)S(y),

so Eq. (
10
) holds. Eqs. (
9
) and (
11
) can be proven similarly.
∎

Next we turn to the notion of Yetter-Drinfeld brace, generalizing cocommutative Hopf brace studied in
[
1
]
. Recall that a (left-left)
Yetter-Drinfeld module
over a Hopf algebra
H
is a left
H
-module
M
which is also a left
H
-comodule satisfying

\rho(x\cdot m)=x_{1}m_{-1}S(x_{3})\otimes(x_{2}\cdot m_{0}),\quad x\in H,\ m\in M,

where
\rho:M\to H\otimes M
is the coaction map under the notation
\rho(m)=m_{-1}\otimes m_{0}
.
A morphism of Yetter-Drinfeld modules is a left
H
-module and also left
H
-comodule map. Denote by
{{}_{H}^{H}}{\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}}
the braided tensor category of Yetter-Drinfeld modules over
H
.

Definition 2.4

(
[
7
]
)
.

A
Yetter-Drinfeld brace

(H,\cdot,\circ,1,\Delta,\varepsilon,S,T)
is the datum of
a Hopf algebra
H_{\circ}=(H,\circ,1,\Delta,\varepsilon,T)
and a second multiplication
\cdot
on
H
and a linear map
S:H\to H
such that

(i)

(H,\cdot,1,\Delta,\varepsilon,S)
is a Hopf algebra in the category
{{}_{H_{\circ}}^{H_{\circ}}}{\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}}
via the action
\rightharpoonup
and the coadjoint coaction
{\rm Coad}_{L}
respectively defined by

(12)

\displaystyle x\rightharpoonup y

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle S(x_{1})\cdot(x_{2}\circ y),

(13)

\displaystyle{\rm Coad}_{L}(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle x_{1}\circ T(x_{3})\otimes x_{2}

for any
x,y\in H
.

(ii)

Define linear map
\leftharpoonup:H\otimes H\to H
by
x\leftharpoonup y=T(x_{1}\rightharpoonup y_{1})\circ x_{2}\circ y_{2}
, then the pair
(\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup)
satisfies condition (
5
).

(iii)

The following Hopf brace compatibility holds.

(14)

\displaystyle x\circ(y\cdot z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{1}\circ y)\cdot S(x_{2})\cdot(x_{3}\circ z),\quad\forall x,y,z\in H.

According to
[
7
, Corollary 3.18, Theorem 3.24]
, we know that matched pair of actions on a Hopf algebra and Yetter-Drinfeld brace are two equivalent algebraic structures.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
7
]
)
.

Let
(H,\cdot,1,\Delta,\varepsilon,S)
be a Hopf algebra with a matched pair of actions
(H,\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup)
on it. Then the tuple

(H,\bullet_{\rightharpoonup},\cdot,1,\Delta,\varepsilon,S_{\rightharpoonup},S)

is a Yetter-Drinfeld brace, with linear maps
\bullet_{\rightharpoonup}:H\otimes H\to H
and
S_{\rightharpoonup}:H\to H
respectively defined by

(15)

\displaystyle x\bullet_{\rightharpoonup}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{1}(S(x_{2})\rightharpoonup y),

(16)

\displaystyle S_{\rightharpoonup}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{1}\rightharpoonup S(x_{2}).

In particular, there is a braided Hopf algebra

H_{\rightharpoonup}=(H,\bullet_{\rightharpoonup},1,\Delta,\varepsilon,S_{\rightharpoonup})

in the category
{{}_{H}^{H}}{\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}}
via the action
\rightharpoonup
and the coadjoint coaction
{\rm Coad}_{L}
, and with its pre-braiding
c_{H_{\rightharpoonup},H_{\rightharpoonup}}
given by

(17)

\displaystyle c_{H_{\rightharpoonup},H_{\rightharpoonup}}(x\otimes y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{1}S(x_{3})\rightharpoonup y)\otimes x_{2},\quad\forall x,y\in H.

Namely, all the structure maps of

are morphisms in
{{}_{H}^{H}}{\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}}
.

Conversely, if
(H,\cdot,\circ,1,\Delta,\varepsilon,S,T)
is a Yetter-Drinfeld brace, then under the notation in Definition

(H_{\circ},\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup)

.

Remark 2.6
.

According to
[
12
, Theorem 6.34]
, Yetter-Drinfeld brace is also equivalent to Hopf skew brace introduced by Guccione, Guccione and Valqui. For any Yetter-Drinfeld brace
(H,\cdot,\circ,1,\Delta,\varepsilon,S,T)

.

3.
Matched pairs of actions and Yang-Baxter operators

Guccione, Guccione and Vendramin introduced the notion of braiding operator on cocommutative Hopf algebras in
[
10
, Definition 5.9]
, and showed that it provides Yang-Baxter operators
[
10
, Theorem 5.11]
.
This notion was further applied for arbitrary Hopf algebras in
[
12
]
.

Definition 3.1

(
[
12
]
)
.

Given a Hopf algebra
H
, a linear operator

r
of
H\otimes H
is called a
braiding operator
on
H
if it is a coalgebra homomorphism satisfying

(i)

mr=m
,

(ii)

r(m\otimes{\rm id})=({\rm id}\otimes m)(r\otimes{\rm id})({\rm id}\otimes r)
,

(iii)

r({\rm id}\otimes m)=(m\otimes{\rm id})({\rm id}\otimes r)(r\otimes{\rm id})
,

(iv)

r(u\otimes{\rm id})={\rm id}\otimes u
,

(v)

r({\rm id}\otimes u)=u\otimes{\rm id}
,

where
m
is the multiplication of
H
and
u
is the unit map of
H
.

Referring to
[
12
, Theorem 5.27, Theorem 6.34]
, we know that a matched pair of actions
(H,\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup)
on a Hopf algebra
H
is equivalent to a braiding operator
r
on
H
satisfying the braid relation. This equivalence generalizes the original group version in
[
14
, Theorem 1, Theorem 2]
and the cocommutative Hopf algebra case in
[
1
, Corollary 2.4]
and
[
10
, Theorem 5.11]
.
Here we reorganize the result as the following theorem and do not claim any originality.

Theorem 3.2
.

A matched pair of actions
(H,\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup)
on a Hopf algebra
H
is equivalent to a braiding operator
r
on
H
, where
r
is defined by

(18)

\displaystyle r(x\otimes y)

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle(x_{1}\rightharpoonup y_{1})\otimes(x_{2}\leftharpoonup y_{2}),\quad\forall x,y\in H,

and
(H,\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup)
is conversely recovered from
r
by letting

(19)

\rightharpoonup\,\coloneqq({\rm id}\otimes\varepsilon)r,\quad\leftharpoonup\,\coloneqq(\varepsilon\otimes{\rm id})r.

Moreover, the braiding operator
r
on
H
is a Yang-Baxter operator with its inverse given by

(20)

\displaystyle r^{-1}(x\otimes y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(y_{1}\leftharpoonup(S(y_{2})\rightharpoonup S(x_{1})))\otimes((S(y_{3})\leftharpoonup S(x_{2}))\rightharpoonup x_{3}),\quad\forall x,y\in H.

Proof.

For the sake of completeness, we reprove the theorem as follows.

First suppose that
(H,\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup)
is a matched pair of actions and
r
is the linear map defined by Eq. (
18
). Condition (
5
) equivalently means that
r
is a coalgebra homomorphism.
According to
[
7
, Lemma 2.4]
, conditions (
1
)–(
4
) hold for
\rightharpoonup
and
\leftharpoonup
if and only if the braiding operator conditions
ii
–
v
hold for
r
, while condition (
7
) is an equivalent form of
i
. So
r
is a coalgebra homomorphism satisfying all braiding operator conditions
i
–
v
.

Conversely, if
r
is a braiding operator on
H
, then the linear maps
\rightharpoonup
and
\leftharpoonup
defined by (
19
) are clearly coalgebra homomorphisms. By
[
7
, Lemma 2.7]
, they are also left and right
H
-module actions respectively. Then by the previous discussion,
\rightharpoonup
and
\leftharpoonup
are module coalgebra actions satisfying compatibility conditions (
1
)–(
7
), so
(H,\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup)
is a matched pair of actions.

To check that the braiding operator
r
satisfies the braid relation, one only need to mimic the proof of
[
10
, Theorem 5.11]
based on the equality

m^{(2)}(r\otimes{\rm id})({\rm id}\otimes r)(r\otimes{\rm id})=m^{(2)}({\rm id}\otimes r)(r\otimes{\rm id})({\rm id}\otimes r)=m^{(2)}.

See also the proof of
[
12
, Theorem 5.27]
.

At last, we check that the braiding operator
r
has the inverse given by Eq. (
20
). Let
t:H\otimes H\to H\otimes H
be the linear operator defined by (
20
), and we have

\displaystyle t(r(x\otimes y))

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:pyb},\,\eqref{eq:MP5}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle t((x_{2}\rightharpoonup y_{2})\otimes(x_{1}\leftharpoonup y_{1}))

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:pyb-left-inv}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\big((x_{1}\leftharpoonup y_{1})\leftharpoonup(S(x_{2}\leftharpoonup y_{2})\rightharpoonup S(x_{4}\rightharpoonup y_{4}))\big)

.

(i)

(ii)

(22)

\displaystyle(x_{1}\rightharpoonup y_{1})\rightharpoonup(x_{2}\leftharpoonup y_{2})=\varepsilon(y)x,\quad(x_{1}\rightharpoonup y_{1})\leftharpoonup(x_{2}\leftharpoonup y_{2})=\varepsilon(x)y.

(iii)

For any
x,y\in H
,

(23)

\displaystyle x\leftharpoonup y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(x_{1}\rightharpoonup y)\rightharpoonup x_{2}.

(iv)

The multiplication

of

\displaystyle r^{2}(x\otimes y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((x_{1}\rightharpoonup y_{1})\rightharpoonup(x_{3}\leftharpoonup y_{3}))\otimes((x_{2}\rightharpoonup y_{2})\leftharpoonup(x_{4}\leftharpoonup y_{4}))

\displaystyle(x_{1}\rightharpoonup y_{1})\rightharpoonup(x_{2}\leftharpoonup y_{2}),

\displaystyle(\varepsilon\otimes{\rm id})r^{2}(x\otimes y)

\displaystyle(x_{1}\rightharpoonup y_{1})\leftharpoonup(x_{2}\leftharpoonup y_{2}).

).

and

are equivalent. If

is true, then

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(x_{1}\rightharpoonup y_{1})(x_{2}\rightharpoonup y_{2})\rightharpoonup(x_{3}\leftharpoonup y_{3})

\displaystyle S(x_{1}\rightharpoonup y)\rightharpoonup x_{2}.

\displaystyle(x_{1}\rightharpoonup y_{1})S(x_{2}\rightharpoonup y_{2})\rightharpoonup x

\displaystyle\varepsilon(y)x,

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:in3}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle S((x_{1}\rightharpoonup y_{1})\rightharpoonup(x_{3}\leftharpoonup y_{3}))\rightharpoonup(x_{2}\rightharpoonup y_{2})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:MP5}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:ydbraid}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle x_{1}S(x_{2})y\ =\ \varepsilon(x)y,

\displaystyle{\rm ad}_{R,y}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{1}\rightharpoonup(S(x_{4})\rightharpoonup y_{1}))\rightharpoonup(x_{2}\leftharpoonup(S(x_{3})\rightharpoonup y_{2}))

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:in2}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle x_{1}\varepsilon(S(x_{2})\rightharpoonup y)\ =\ x\varepsilon(y).

Definition 3.10

(
[
21
,
15
]
)
.

Given a braided Hopf algebra
R
in the category
{{}_{H}^{H}}{\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}}
of Yetter-Drinfeld modules over a Hopf algebra
H
, there is an ordinary Hopf algebra structure on the tensor product
R\otimes H
, called the
biproduct
or
bosonization
of
R
by
H
.
It is the smash product Hopf algebra
R\#H
equipped with the multiplication

(24)

\displaystyle(a\otimes x)(b\otimes y)

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle a(x_{1}\cdot b)\otimes x_{2}y,\quad\forall a,b\in R,\,x,y\in H,

and the following coproduct

(25)

\displaystyle\Delta(a\otimes x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle(a^{1}\otimes(a^{2})_{-1}x_{1})\otimes((a^{2})_{0}\otimes x_{2}),

where the coproduct of
R
is written as
\Delta_{R}(a)=a^{1}\otimes a^{2}
.

In the end of this section, we identify the double cross product
H\bowtie H
with the bosonization of
H_{\rightharpoonup}
by
H
for a matched pair of actions
(H,\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup)
. Namely,
H\bowtie H
becomes a Hopf algebra with a projection and
H_{\rightharpoonup}
serves as its subalgebra of coinvariants.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
(H,\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup)
be a matched pair of actions on a Hopf algebra
H
. The following linear map

\Phi:H\bowtie H\to H_{\rightharpoonup}\#H,\ x\otimes y\mapsto x_{1}\otimes x_{2}y

gives an isomorphism of Hopf algebras between the double cross poduct
H\bowtie H
and the bosonization
H_{\rightharpoonup}\#H
.

Proof.

It is clear that
\Phi
has the inverse
\Phi^{-1}:x\otimes y\mapsto x_{1}\otimes S(x_{2})y
.
For any
x,y,z,w\in H
,

\displaystyle\Phi((x\otimes y)(z\otimes w))

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:dcp}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\Phi(x(y_{1}\rightharpoonup z_{1})\otimes(y_{2}\leftharpoonup z_{2})w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{1}(y_{1}\rightharpoonup z_{1})\otimes x_{2}(y_{2}\rightharpoonup z_{2})(y_{3}\leftharpoonup z_{3})w

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:MP*}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle x_{1}(y_{1}\rightharpoonup z_{1})\otimes x_{2}y_{2}z_{2}w.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\Phi(x\otimes y)\Phi(z\otimes w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{1}\otimes x_{2}y)(z_{1}\otimes z_{2}w)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:bp_mul}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle x_{1}\bullet_{\rightharpoonup}(x_{2}y_{1}\rightharpoonup z_{1})\otimes x_{3}y_{2}z_{2}w

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:mp-ydb-1}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle x_{1}(S(x_{2})\rightharpoonup(x_{3}y_{1}\rightharpoonup z_{1}))\otimes x_{4}y_{2}z_{2}w

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{1}(S(x_{2})x_{3}y_{1}\rightharpoonup z_{1})\otimes x_{4}y_{2}z_{2}w

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{1}(y_{1}\rightharpoonup z_{1})\otimes x_{2}y_{2}z_{2}w,

so
\Phi
is an algebra homomorphism. Meanwhile,

\displaystyle\Delta(\Phi(x\otimes y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta(x_{1}\otimes x_{2}y)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:bp_co},\,\eqref{eq:ydb-codaction}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle(x_{1}\otimes(x_{2}S(x_{4}))(x_{5}y_{1}))\otimes(x_{3}\otimes x_{6}y_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{1}\otimes x_{2}y_{1})\otimes(x_{3}\otimes x_{4}y_{2}).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle(\Phi\otimes\Phi)(\Delta(x\otimes y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi(x_{1}\otimes y_{1})\otimes\Phi(x_{2}\otimes y_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{1}\otimes x_{2}y_{1})\otimes(x_{3}\otimes x_{4}y_{2}).

Namely,
\Phi
is also a coalgebra homomorphism, thus an isomorphism of Hopf algebras.
∎

An illustration to classify matched pairs of actions

According to
[
12
, Theorem 5.27, Theorem 6.34]
, we know that a matched pair of actions on a Hopf algebra
H
is just a pair of module coalgebra actions
(\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup)
satisfying compatibility condition (
7
), namely conditions (
1
)–(
5
) are redundant. In other words, we have the following simplified characterization of matched pairs of actions on Hopf algebras for the purpose of classification.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra, and
\rightharpoonup
be a left
H
-module coalgebra action on itself. Define linear map
\leftharpoonup:H\otimes H\to H
by

(26)

\displaystyle x\leftharpoonup y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(x_{1}\rightharpoonup y_{1})x_{2}y_{2},\quad\forall x,y\in H.

If
\leftharpoonup
is a right
H
-module coalgebra action,
then
(H,\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup)

is a matched pair of actions on
H
.

Proof.

For the sake of completeness, we give the following sketched proof. Suppose that
\leftharpoonup
defined by (
26
) is a right
H
-module coalgebra action.
The defining formula (
26
) equivalently implies condition (
7
). Also,
\varepsilon(x\leftharpoonup y)=\varepsilon(x)\varepsilon(y)
for any
x,y\in H
, since
\rightharpoonup
is a left
H
-module coalgebra action. According to
[
7
, Lemma 3.6]
,
\leftharpoonup
is a coalgebra homomorphism if and only if condition (
5
) holds.

Next we show that
\rightharpoonup
is a left “twisted” module algebra action satisfying conditions (
1
) and (
2
).
For any
x,y,z\in H
,

\displaystyle xyz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x(yz)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:MP*}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle(x_{1}\rightharpoonup y_{1}z_{1})(x_{2}\leftharpoonup y_{2}z_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{1}\rightharpoonup y_{1}z_{1})((x_{2}\leftharpoonup y_{2})\leftharpoonup z_{2}).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle xyz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(xy)z

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:MP*}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle(x_{1}\rightharpoonup y_{1})(x_{2}\leftharpoonup y_{2})z

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:MP*}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle(x_{1}\rightharpoonup y_{1})((x_{2}\leftharpoonup y_{2})\rightharpoonup z_{1})((x_{3}\leftharpoonup y_{3})\leftharpoonup z_{2}),

so we see that condition (
1
) holds by cancelling factors via convolution product.

Meanwhile, we have

\displaystyle\varepsilon(x_{1})1x_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x1

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:MP*}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle(x_{1}\rightharpoonup 1)(x_{2}\leftharpoonup 1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{1}\rightharpoonup 1)x_{2}.

Hence, condition (
2
) also holds by cancelling factors via convolution product.

It remains to check that
\leftharpoonup
is a right “twisted” module algebra action satisfying conditions (
3
), (
4
). Indeed, for any
x,y,z\in H
,

\displaystyle 1\leftharpoonup x

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:r-action}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle S(1\rightharpoonup x_{1})x_{2}\ =\ S(x_{1})x_{2}\ =\ \varepsilon(x)1

\displaystyle(xy)\leftharpoonup z

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:r-action}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle S(x_{1}y_{1}\rightharpoonup z_{1})x_{2}y_{2}z_{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:MP*}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle S(x_{1}\rightharpoonup(y_{1}\rightharpoonup z_{1}))x_{2}(y_{2}\rightharpoonup z_{2})(y_{3}\leftharpoonup z_{3})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:r-action}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle(x\leftharpoonup(y_{1}\rightharpoonup z_{1}))(y_{2}\leftharpoonup z_{2}).

In summary,
(H,\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup)

is a matched pair of actions on
H
.
∎

Relating to Majid’s transmutation theory, the authors in
[
7
]
pointed out that coquasitriangular structures on Hopf algebras induce matched pairs of actions, i.e. Yetter-Drinfeld braces, and applied this result to several examples, such as
Sweedler’s Hopf algebra
H_{4}
and its generalization
E_{n}\ (n\geq 3)
, and also quantum group
{\rm SL}_{q}(2)
.

Recall that a
coquasitriangular Hopf algebra
is a Hopf algebra
H
with a convolution-invertible bilinear map
{\mathcal{R}}:H\otimes H\to{\mathbf{k}}
such that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}(a_{1}\otimes b_{1})a_{2}b_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b_{1}a_{1}{\mathcal{R}}(a_{2}\otimes b_{2}),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}(a\otimes bc)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}(a_{1}\otimes c){\mathcal{R}}(a_{2}\otimes b),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}(ab\otimes c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}(a\otimes c_{1}){\mathcal{R}}(b\otimes c_{2})

for any
a,b,c\in H
. In particular, if
{\mathcal{R}}^{-1}={\mathcal{R}}^{\rm op}
, it is called
cotriangular

[
17
, § 2.2]
.

Theorem 4.2

(
[
7
]
)
.

Any coquasitriangular structure
{\mathcal{R}}
on a Hopf algebra
H
naturally induces a matched pair of actions
(H,\rightharpoonup,\leftharpoonup)
defined by

\displaystyle a\rightharpoonup b

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}^{-1}(a_{1}\otimes b_{1})b_{2}{\mathcal{R}}(a_{2}\otimes b_{3}),

\displaystyle a\leftharpoonup b

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}^{-1}(a_{1}\otimes b_{1})a_{2}{\mathcal{R}}(a_{3}\otimes b_{2}).

The associated braiding operator
r
as in Eq. (
18
) is given by

(27)

\displaystyle r(a\otimes b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}^{-1}(a_{1}\otimes b_{1})b_{2}\otimes a_{2}{\mathcal{R}}(a_{3}\otimes b_{3}).

Here as an illustration, we use the characterization in Theorem

to classify matched pairs of actions on the

intensively studied in
[

. After that, we analyze their associated Yang-Baxter operators.

Example 4.3
.

The Hopf algebra
A_{C_{2}\times C_{2}}
is generated by 3 elements
g
,
h
,
x
, with the relations

g^{2}=h^{2}=1,\quad gh=hg,\quad x^{2}=0,\quad gx=-xg,\quad hx=-xh,

A linear basis for
A_{C_{2}\times C_{2}}
is given by
\{g^{i}h^{j}x^{k}\,|\,i,j,k=0,1\}
. The coalgebra structure and the antipode of
A_{C_{2}\times C_{2}}
are defined by

\Delta(g)=g\otimes g,\quad\Delta(h)=h\otimes h,\quad\Delta(x)=x\otimes 1+g\otimes x,

\varepsilon(g)=\varepsilon(h)=1,\quad\varepsilon(x)=0,\quad S(g)=g,\quad S(h)=h,\quad S(x)=xg,

so it contains
H_{4}
as a Hopf subalgebra. In particular, the group of group-like elements in
A_{C_{2}\times C_{2}}

G(A_{C_{2}\times C_{2}})=\{1,g,h,gh\},

isomorphic to the Klein 4-group, and all the nonzero subspaces of skew primitive elements in
A_{C_{2}\times C_{2}}
are as follows.

\displaystyle P_{1,h}(A_{C_{2}\times C_{2}})=P_{h,1}(A_{C_{2}\times C_{2}})={\mathbf{k}}(1-h),

\displaystyle P_{1,gh}(A_{C_{2}\times C_{2}})=P_{gh,1}(A_{C_{2}\times C_{2}})={\mathbf{k}}(1-gh),

\displaystyle P_{g,h}(A_{C_{2}\times C_{2}})=P_{h,g}(A_{C_{2}\times C_{2}})={\mathbf{k}}(g-h),

\displaystyle P_{g,gh}(A_{C_{2}\times C_{2}})=P_{gh,g}(A_{C_{2}\times C_{2}})={\mathbf{k}}(g-gh),

\displaystyle P_{1,g}(A_{C_{2}\times C_{2}})={\mathbf{k}}(1-g)\oplus{\mathbf{k}}x,

\displaystyle P_{g,1}(A_{C_{2}\times C_{2}})={\mathbf{k}}(1-g)\oplus{\mathbf{k}}gx,

\displaystyle P_{h,gh}(A_{C_{2}\times C_{2}})={\mathbf{k}}(h-gh)\oplus{\mathbf{k}}hx,

\displaystyle P_{gh,h}(A_{C_{2}\times C_{2}})={\mathbf{k}}(h-gh)\oplus{\mathbf{k}}ghx.

Suppose that there is a left module coalgebra action
\rightharpoonup
on
A_{C_{2}\times C_{2}}
,
and the linear map
\leftharpoonup
defined by Eq. (
26
) is a right module coalgebra action on
A_{C_{2}\times C_{2}}
. Then

\displaystyle g\leftharpoonup g=g\rightharpoonup g,

\displaystyle g\leftharpoonup h=(g\rightharpoonup h)gh,

\displaystyle h\leftharpoonup g=(h\rightharpoonup g)gh,

\displaystyle h\leftharpoonup h=h\rightharpoonup h

are all non-trivial group-like elements by Eqs. (
2
), (
4
). Otherwise,
let
g\leftharpoonup g=1
for instance, then
g=g\leftharpoonup g^{2}=(g\leftharpoonup g)\leftharpoonup g=1\leftharpoonup g=1
, which is a contradiction.

First note that

g\rightharpoonup x\in P_{1,g\rightharpoonup g}(A_{C_{2}\times C_{2}})\setminus{\mathbf{k}}G(A_{C_{2}\times C_{2}})
, as
g\rightharpoonup(g\rightharpoonup x)=x
.
According to the situation of skew primitive elements of
A_{C_{2}\times C_{2}}
, we have
g\rightharpoonup g=g\leftharpoonup g=g
. Then it implies that
x\rightharpoonup g
,
g\leftharpoonup x\in P_{g,g}(A_{C_{2}\times C_{2}})=\{0\}
, so
x\rightharpoonup g=g\leftharpoonup x=0
.
Meanwhile, for
g\rightharpoonup x\in P_{1,g}(A_{C_{2}\times C_{2}})={\mathbf{k}}(1-g)\oplus{\mathbf{k}}x
, we know that it is a scalar multiple of
x
, and then

g\leftharpoonup x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:r-action}}}{{=}}S(g\rightharpoonup x)g+S(g\rightharpoonup g)gx=(g\rightharpoonup x)g^{2}+g^{2}x=g\rightharpoonup x+x=0.

So
g\rightharpoonup x=-x
. On the other hand,
x\leftharpoonup g\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:r-action}}}{{=}}S(x\rightharpoonup g)g+S(g\rightharpoonup g)xg=gxg=-x
.

By a similar argument, we consider

h\rightharpoonup x\in P_{1,h\rightharpoonup g}(A_{C_{2}\times C_{2}})
to obtain that
h\rightharpoonup g=g
, and then
h\leftharpoonup g=h
. It implies that
h\leftharpoonup x\in P_{h,h}(A_{C_{2}\times C_{2}})=0

h\leftharpoonup x=0
.
For
h\rightharpoonup x\in P_{1,g}(A_{C_{2}\times C_{2}})={\mathbf{k}}(1-g)\oplus{\mathbf{k}}x

x

so
h\rightharpoonup x=-x
. By symmetry, we have
g\rightharpoonup h=h
,
g\leftharpoonup h=g
,
x\rightharpoonup h=0

x\leftharpoonup h=-x

\displaystyle\Delta(x\rightharpoonup x)

\displaystyle\quad+(x\rightharpoonup g)\otimes(1\rightharpoonup x)+(g\rightharpoonup g)\otimes(x\rightharpoonup x)

.

(ii)

\displaystyle g\rightharpoonup(-g(x\rightharpoonup x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\alpha(g\rightharpoonup g(1-g))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha(1-g).

\displaystyle(-1)^{(j+1)l}\alpha(1-g)h^{l}

for any
i,j,k,l=0,1
. Take for example the LHS of Eq. (
23
) to be

\displaystyle g^{i}h^{j}x\leftharpoonup g^{k}h^{l}x

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:r-action}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle S(g^{i}h^{j}x\rightharpoonup g^{k}h^{l}x)g^{i}h^{j}g^{k}h^{l}+S(g^{i+1}h^{j}\rightharpoonup g^{k}h^{l}x)g^{i}h^{j}xg^{k}h^{l}

\displaystyle\quad+S(g^{i}h^{j}x\rightharpoonup g^{k+1}h^{l})g^{i}h^{j}g^{k}h^{l}x+S(g^{i+1}h^{j}\rightharpoonup g^{k+1}h^{l})g^{i}h^{j}xg^{k}h^{l}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{(j+1)l}\alpha(1-g)h^{l}g^{i}h^{j}g^{k}h^{l}+(-1)^{i+j(l+1)}xg^{k+jl+1}h^{l}g^{i}h^{j}xg^{k}h^{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{i+k+(j+1)l}\alpha(1-g)h^{j}.

Correspondingly, the RHS of Eq. (
23
) equals to

\displaystyle S(g^{i}h^{j}x\rightharpoonup g^{k}h^{l}x)\rightharpoonup g^{i}h^{j}+S(g^{i+1}h^{j}\rightharpoonup g^{k}h^{l}x)\rightharpoonup g^{i}h^{j}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{(j+1)l}\alpha(1-g)h^{l}\rightharpoonup g^{i}h^{j}+(-1)^{i+1+j(l+1)}xg^{k+jl+1}h^{l}\rightharpoonup g^{i}h^{j}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{i+j+k+l}g^{k+jl+1}h^{l}x\rightharpoonup g^{i}h^{j}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{i+k+(j+1)l}\alpha(1-g)h^{j}.

So they coincide with each other.
∎

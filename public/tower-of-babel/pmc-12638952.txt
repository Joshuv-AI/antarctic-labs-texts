Study on strength and ultrasonic pulse velocity of cemented aeolian sand backfill under multiple factors using response surface method
Authors: Yingxi Li, Shushuai Wang, Gang Li, Qinghe Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAeolian sand (AS) has been increasingly used to prepare cemented AS backfill (CASB) for coal mining. Although strength is an important determinant of the CASB performance, existing research on the CASB has not addressed the effects of multi-factor interaction and coupling on the uniaxial compressive strength (UCS) and splitting tensile strength (STS). In addition, ultrasonic pulse velocity (UPV) testing, has not been used on the CASB. In the present study, ordinary Portland cement (PO) to fly ash (FA) ratio (PO/FA, x1), fine particles (total mass of PO and FA) to AS ratio (fine particles/AS, x2) and solid content (x3) were considered as factors. The response surface method (RSM) was used for assessing their influence on the UCS, STS and UPV of the CASB, and for analyzing the relationships between the UCS, STS and UPV. The RSM results showed that the UCS, STS and UPV increased with these factors. The interaction terms x1x2 and x1x3 significantly affected the UPV, and x2x3 significantly affected the UCS and STS. The influence surface of the two-factor interaction and a three-dimensional scatter plot of the three–factor coupling were established. The effects of above factors on the strength and UPV were revealed from three levels of "single factor–interaction terms of double factors–coupling effect of three factors". There existed a linear growth relationship between the STS and UCS, and the STS values were primarily distributed within 0.09–0.105 of the UCS values. The strength increased quadratically with the UPV. In conclusion, this study determined the influence rules of the PO/FA, fine particles/AS and solid content on the UPV, UCS and STS, and the relationship among the UCS, STS and UPV.Keyword: Cemented aeolian sand backfill (CASB), Response surface method (RSM), Uniaxial compressive strength (UCS), Splitting tensile strength (STS), Ultrasonic pulse velocity (UPV)Subject terms: Engineering, Materials science

Introduction

Coal mining aims to extract coal resources from underground coal seams for social, economic and industrial uses1–3. When coal is extracted, many underground goafs and protective pillars are left behind, which causes problems such as surface subsidence, loss of coal resources, and environmental pollution4,5. Cemented filling mining is the most widely used filling technology in coal mines to controll surface subsidence, improve the coal resource recovery rates, and reduce coal-based solid waste stacking6–8.

Gangue is the most widely used filling material, however, gangue production in some mining areas cannot meet the needs of mine filling9,10. Considerable aeolian sand (AS) is stored in the Ordos region of China11,12. Using AS as the filling material can help overcome the need for gangue, and help to coordinate green mining and AS control. Compared with gangue, AS has fine and uniform particle size, good gradation, smooth surface and no water retention. The cemented backfilling slurry prepared from AS has good homogeneity and fluidity. Cemented AS backfill (CASB) can achieve a higher mass concentration, and the internal structure will be more compact.

Backfill strength is extremely important13,14. Sun et al.15,16 analyzed the effect of material properties on the strength of CASB at different curing times and selected an appropriate proportion for the Ershike Coal Mine. Shao et al.17,18 analyzed the rheological properties of an AS-FA based filling slurry and the UCS of backfill with different fly ash (FA) content,the rheological parameter and UCS both increased with increasing FA content. Zhou et al.19,20 studied the effect of FA content, cement content, lime–slag content, and concentration on the mechanical properties of CASB, the results showed that mechanical properties increased as each of these factors increased. Xin et al.21 and Yang et al.22 analyzed the effects of activator types and dosage on the mechanical properties and early hydration characteristics of the cemented AS-modified gasification slag backfill.

Ultrasonic pulse velocity (UPV) testing, a non-destructive and easily applicable method under on-site and laboratory conditions, is typically used to estimate the strength of concrete and rock23,24. Galaa et al.25 and Yilmaz et al.26 applied the UPV technology to evaluate the mechanical properties of cemented paste backfill (CPB), and observed that the UCS increased linearly with the UPV, which could be used to predict the CPB strength. Subsequently, scholars applied the UPV technology to different types of backfills. Wu et al.27 investigated the influence of the solid content and FA dosage on the UPV and UCS of cemented gangue backfill (CGB). The UCS and UPV increased with the two factors, the UCS and UPV exhibited an exponential relationship. Zhao et al.28 experimentally determined that the UPV of a smaller CGB exhibited a rapid increase in the early stage and decrease in the later stage, and the strength decreased with increasing specimen size. Guo et al.29 and Feng et al.30 revealed that the UCS and UPV of a CGB column (CGBC) exhibited an exponential growth relationship. Wu et al.31,32 analysed the influence of the dosage and aggregate gradation on the UCS and UPV of cemented waste rock backfill (CWRB), the results showed that the UCS and UPV positively correlated with the cementitious material dosage, and the UCS and UPV exhibited cubic polynomial dependences on the aggregates’ Talbot index. An exponential relationship was observed between the UCS and UPV. Cao et al.33 experimentally investigated the linear relationship between the UCS and UPV for cemented coarse-tailing backfill (CCTB). Jiang et al.34 and Qiu et al.35 studied the UCS and UPV of a cemented tailing backfill (CTB). The UPV and UCS, which were linearly related, first decreased and then remained constant or slightly increased as the fineness of tailings increased. Wang et al.36 also studied the UCS and UPV of a CTB, and reported consistent test results. Jiang et al.37 study the relationship between UPV and UCS for CPB with alkali-activated slag, and reported a exponential relationship between the UCS and UPV. Tang et al.38 studied the UCS and UPV of cemented aeolian sand-red mud-fly ash paste backfill, the result showed that there also was an exponential relationship between the UCS and UPV.

The UPV and UCS of backfills with different aggregates exhibited different relationships. The particle size distribution and composition of the AS differed from those of the aforementioned aggregates; therefore, the previously obtained UPV-UCS relationship cannot be directly applied to CASB. Currently, UPV testing has not been employed to evaluate the strength of CASB to establish a correlation between the strength and UPV of CASB.

Current, studies on the strength of CASB have mainly focused on the UCS, however, research on the splitting tensile strength (STS) is insufficient. The single-factor analysis method fails to well accurately capture the effect of multi-factor interactions on strength. Response surface methodology (RSM) can capture the interaction effects of multiple factors on performance indicators with high prediction accuracy, according to a small number of experimental groups39–41. In the backfill mining, Guo et al.42 used the RSM to analyze the fluidity, setting time, strength and impermeability of low-cost geopolymer grouting material, and obtained the optimal ratio. Liu et al.43 used the added quantities of cement, lime and gypsum as RSM influence factors to analyze the influence of these factors on the UCS of backfill, and obtained an optimal material ratio. Wu et al.44 carried out multi-objective optimization of flocculation effect based on response surface method, Box-Behnken design and expectation function (RSM-BBD-DF), the optimal flocculation conditions were obtained. Wang et al.45 analyzed the influence of the cement content, FA content and concentration on the strength of CGB and optimized the corresponding material ratios. The RSM enables the analysis and optimization of the performance and ratio of cemented fillers.

The present study utilized the RSM and microstructure testing to analyze the response laws of the UPV, UCS, and STS of the CASB for the curing time of 90 d from three aspects: "single-factor influence–two-factor interaction influence–three-factor coupling effect", as well as the mechanism of factors. Functional expressions for the variation of strength with each factor and the functional relationship between the strength and UPV were established. For any combination ratio within the value range of the three factors, the strength of the backfill was predicted using the strength prediction model. The strength can be predicted via non-destructive ultrasonic testing by utilizing the functional relationship between the strength and UPV. This study provides a novel method for predicting the mechanical properties and offers theoretical and engineering guidance for CASB filling.

Materials and methods

Materials and characterization

The CASB preparation included AS as aggregate, fly ash (FA) as auxiliary material, cement as the binder, and water. The FA was taken from a power plant near the coal mine and was classified as first-grade FA. The cement was 42.5 ordinary Portland cement (PO). XRF-1800 spectrum analyzer, SmartLab RagiKU 9 diffractometer, and laser diffraction particle size analyzer (NKT5200-H, China) were used to analyze the chemical composition (XRF), mineral composition (XRD) and particle size distribution (PSD) of AS, FA, and PO. The analysis results are shown in Fig. 1.

Chemical and Mineral composition, and PSD of raw materials. (a) AS, (b) FA, and (c) PO.

The chemical compositions of AS, FA, and PO were mainly SiO2, CaO, Al2O3 and Fe2O3, accounting for 91.6%, 92.67% and 92.86% respectively. The AS minerals were mainly quartz, potash feldspar, muscovite, and calcite; The FA minerals were mainly illite, quartz, and muscovite; The PO minerals were mainly C3S, C2S, C4AF, C3A.

The maximum particle size of the AS was 459 μm, and ranged from 80–400 μm, accounting for more than 98%. The particle size of FA ranged 2–70 μm, and the maximum is < 120 μm. The particle size of PO ranged from 5–40 μm, with a maximum of 54 μm.

Sample preparation

Thirteen experimental groups were designed by RSM–BBD (Box–Behnken design). To obtain a uniform mixture, first, PO, FA and AS were mixed with a blender for 5 min to form an evenly mixed dry mixture. Then, the water and dry mixture were stirred again for 5 min to obtain a uniform slurry. After even mixing, the slurry was poured into φ50 mm × 100 mm (for the UPV and UCS tests) and φ50 mm × 25 mm (for the STS tests) cylindrical molds pre–applied with a release agent. The molds with the slurry were vibrated for 2 min to eliminate vacuoles and optimize specimen uniformity, and then cured in the curing chamber (temperature: 21 ± 1 ℃; relative humidity: more than > 95%.) for 24 h for demolding. The demolded samples were placed in the curing chamber. The UPV, UCS and STS tests were performed on the CASB samples cured for 90 d.

Test content and method

UPV test

The UPV of the CASB samples was measured using an RSM-SY5(T) non-metallic ultrasonic detector. The transducers (transmitter and receiver) were 50 mm in diameter with 50 kHz. Before the measurements, the end surfaces of the CASB samples were made smooth and flat to provide good coupling between the transducer face and sample surface and maximise the accuracy of the transit time measurement. An ultrasonic flaw detection coupling agent was applied to the surface of the transducers and sample to ensure full contact and eliminate air pockets between the transducers and test medium. XRD, SEM and MIP tests

XRD, scanning electron microscope (SEM) and mercury intrusion porosimetry (MIP) tests were conducted to determine the influence mechanism of three factors. The 10 mg CASB powder with particle size less than 75 μm was screened for XRD test. The scanning angle range of this test was between 10–70° undertaken by SmartLab RagiKU 9 diffractometer. The JSM-IT2000 SEM instrument was used to observe micro-structural characteristics and mineral composition distribution of the CASB blocks. The scanning samples were collected from the backfill specimen and fabricated to approximately 3 × 8 × 10 mm. The autopore 9600–9510 MIP instrument was employed to test the pore structure of the CASB blocks. During the test, 10 mm × 10 mm × 10 mm cube samples were used.

Response surface method (RSM)

In this study, instead of directly considering the PO and FA contents as the analytical factors, the PO to FA ratio (PO/FA, x1) and the fine particles (PO and FA) to AS ratio (fine particles/AS, x2) were utilized. The PO and FA contents can be calculated through the PO/FA and fine particles/AS. PO content increased with the PO/FA and fine particles/AS. Under the same fine particles/AS, the addition of FA reduced the consumption of PO. The initial PO/FA was close to 0.75 and the high proportion of PO increased costs. To reduce the amount of PO (costs) and increase FA consumption, the PO/FA was taken as an analytical factor and assigned values of 0.4–0.72. The fine particles/AS directly affected the interaction between the hydration products and aggregate particles and was approximately 0.6 in the initial mix. To analyze the effect of changes in fine particles/AS, values were set at 0.4–0.8. The solid content (x3) is an important factor for controlling the various properties of the CASB. Based on the analysis of the previous tests and considering the conveying performance of the filling slurry and the mechanical characteristics of the backfill in the project, solid content was set at 80wt%-84wt%.

In this study, the RSM was adopted to analyze the effect of the three factors, their interaction terms and coupling effect on the UPV, UCS, and STS of the CASB, and to establish relationships among them. According to the design scheme, PO/FA (x1), fine particles/AS (x2), and solid content(x3) adopted three change values, among which x1 were 0.4, 0.56, and 0.72, x2 were 0.4, 0.6, and 0.8, and x3 were 80wt%, 82wt%, and 84wt%. Thirteen experimental groups were designed using the RSM–BBD (Box–Behnken design), and three samples were considered for each group (labeled S1, S2, and S3). The RSM design scheme and test results are summarized in Tables 1 and 2, respectively. In Table 2, AVG represented the average value and SD represented the standard deviation. The standard deviations were all within 10% of the average, and the datas were reliable. The test process and content are shown in Fig. 2.

The XRD and SEM results of group #6–#10 samples are shown in Fig. 5.

C–S–H was the most abundant hydration product, which was the main source of backfill strength. UCS was positively correlated with C–S–H content (except #6),#6 and #8 indicated that the UCS was not only related to hydration products, but also to the pore structure of the backfill.

MIP results

The MIP results of group #6–#9 samples are shown in Fig. 6.

Pore structure of experimental groups. relationship between the UCS and STS. (a, b) the pore distribution curve of #6–#9, (c) the pore content and UCS of #6–#10.

It can be seen from the pore distribution curve in Fig. 6 (a–b) that the pore size distribution range of the samples is 0.005 ~ 366 μm. The pore curve shifted to the left as the PO/FA (#7 vs. #8), fine particles/AS (#9 vs. #10) and solid content (#6 vs. #8) increased, indicating that the pore diameter decreased, and the inner structure of the CASB became denser. Figure 6 (c) showed micropores (< 0.1 μm) content (the volume ratio of micropores to all pores), porosity and UCS of different groups. Micropores content increased obviously as the PO/FA, fine particles/AS and solid content increased, the porosity showed the opposite trend with the three factors. The effect of solid content on micropores content and porosity was the strongest, followed by the particles/AS and PO/FA. Compared with #6 and #10, PO/FA influence on micropores content greater than fine particles/AS, while fine particles/AS influence on porosity greater. The volume of hydration products was small, which can better fill micropores and increased micropores number. FA has micro-agglomeration effect, FA content increased with fine particles/AS, which can reduce the backfill porosity on the whole. The UCS was positively correlated with the micropores content while negatively correlated with the porosity.

Combined with XRD, SEM and MIP results, PO/FA and fine particles/AS effected significantly on hydration products and pore structure, while solid content mainly affected pore structure.

Influence of single factor on the UPV, UCS and STS

The effects of x1, x2, and x3 on the UPV, UCS and STS are shown in Fig. 7. The effects of x1, x2, and x3 are shown in Fig. 7 (a) (x2 = 0.6 and x3 = 82wt%), Fig. 7 (b) (x1 = 0.56 and x3 = 82wt%), and Fig. 7 (c) (x1 = 0.56 and x2 = 0.6), respectively.

Influence of single factor on the UPV, UCS and STS. (a) PO/FA, (b) ine particles/AS, (c) solid content.

Figure 7 (a)–(c) showed that the UPV, UCS, and STS increased with x1, x2, and x3.

PO was the main source of the hydration products. When x2 and x3 remained unchanged, the hydration products inside the CASB increased with x1 and overlapped with solid particles and wrapped solid particles, thereby increasing the strength and UPV of the CASB15,20.

The effect of FA depended on micro-aggregation and pozzolanic activity. When x1 and x3 remained unchanged, both the PO and FA content increased with x2. FA acted as a micro-aggregate to fill the voids, and its pozzolanic effect caused it to hydrate and generate the hydration products, combined with the hydration products caused by PO, which had a bonding effect on the aggregate. The hydration products fully wrapped the surfaces of the AS particles because the AS content was reduced. The micro-aggregate and pozzolanic effects together reduced the internal porosity of the CASB and improved its strength and UPV46.

When x1 and x2 remained unchanged, the number of solid particles inside the CASB increased with x3, while the number of pores and pore size inside the CASB both decreased. The hydration products overlapped and wrapped particles better, thus increasing the strength and UPV47.

Influence of the interaction terms on UPV, UCS and STS

According to Table 3, x1x2 and x1x3 significantly influenced the UPV, and x2x3 had a considerable influence on the UCS and STS. The effects of the interaction terms x1x2 (x3 = 82 wt%), x1x3 (x2 = 0.6) and x2x3 (x1 = 0.56) are shown in Fig. 8. As can be seen from Fig. 8 (a)–(d), response surfaces of the UPV, UCS and STS to interaction terms (x1x2, x1x3 and x2x3) all conformed to Ploy2D-type function (z = z0 + ax + by + cx22 + dy22 + fxy) surfaces, and the expressions of each response function are shown in Fig. 8, with the goodness of fit reaching 1.

Influence of interaction terms on the UPV, UCS and STS. (a) x1x2 and (b) x1x3 on UPV, x2x3 on (c) UCS and (d) STS.

The effects of x1x2 and x1x3 on the UPV of the CASB are shown in Fig. 8 (a) and (b),respectively. When the values of x1 were 0.4 and 0.72, the UPV increased by 0.473 km·s-1 and 0.175 km·s-1, respectively, as x2 increased from 0.4 to 0.8. When the x2 values were 0.4 and 0.8, the UPV increased by 0.371 km·s-1 and 0.073 km·s-1, respectively, as x1 increases from 0.4 to 0.72. The data showed that the effects of x1 and x2 on the UPV gradually weakened with another factor. Similarly, x1x3 has a similar influence on the UPV.

The influences of x2x3 on the UCS and STS of the CASB are presented in Fig. 8 (c) and (d), respectively, which showed that the UCS and STS increased with x2 and x3. When the values of x2 were 0.4 and 0.8, the UCS increased by 1.65 and 6.74 MPa, and the STS increased by 0.13 and 0.71 MPa, respectively, as x3 increased from 80 wt% to 84 wt%. When the x3 values were 80wt% and 84wt%, the UCS increased by 1.76 and 6.85 MPa, and the STS increased by 0.2 and 0.78 MPa, respectively, as x2 increases from 0.4 to 0.8. The data indicated that the influence of x2 and x3 on the strength gradually increased with another factor.

Coupling effect of three factors on the UPV, UCS and STS

The coupling effect revealed the influence of the three factors on the UPV and strength when they were changed simultaneously. The RSM is advantageous because the effect of any value within the variation range of each factor on the response index can be accurately predicted. Therefore, five change values of each factor within its variation range (namely, x1 in the range of 0.4–0.72, x2 in the range of 0.4–0.8 range, andx3 in the range of 80–84 wt.%) were selected for predicting the values of the UPV, UCS, and STS. A total of 125 predicted RSM data points were obtained to create three-dimensional spatial scatter plots of the coupling effect of the three factors on the UPV, UCS, and STS, as shown in Fig. 9 (a)-(c),respectively. The scatter points were mapped by a “rainbow balance” palette in “Origin 2018”. The colors and sizes of each scatter point in Fig. 9 indicated that the UPV, UCS, and STS increased with x1, x2, and x.

The changes in the scatter values (colors) of the four coordinate axes corresponding to each factor showed that under different limiting conditions (values of the two factors), the effects of the third factor on the UPV and strength were of varying degrees. The Increment in the UPV and strength values of coordinate axes.

The increment in the UPV and strength on the coordinate axis in Fig. 10 intuitively displayed the degree of effect of the third factor on the UPV and strength when the two factors were at different values. As shown in Fig. 10 (a), the degree of effect of x1 on the UPV significantly varied with the change in x2 and x3 values; the degree of effect of x2 and x3 on the UPV significantly varied with the change in x1, However, significant variations were not observed with changes in x3 or x2, indicating that x1x2 and x1x3 strongly affected on the UPV. In addition, an increase x1, x2, or x3 weakened the effect of other factors on the UPV. Similarly, Fig. 10 (b)–(c) showed that the degree of effect of x1 on the UCS and STS didn’t significantly vary with changes in x2 and x3. The effect degree of x2 and x3 on the UCS and STS significantly varied with the change in x3 and x2, indicating that x2x3 strongly affected the UCS and STS.

In combination with sections "Influence of single factor on the UPV, UCS and STS"–"Coupling effect of three factors on the UPV, UCS and STS", the remaining two factors remained unchanged during the analysis of a single factor. During the analysis of the interaction terms, the remaining factor remained unchanged, whereas the three factors changed together during the analysis of the coupling effect. Therefore, this study revealed the effect of three factors on the UPV, UCS and STS of the CASB from the three levels of "single factor analysis–interaction terms of double factors–coupling effect of three factors".

PO was the primary source of hydration products, and FA exhibited pozzolanic activity. The increase in x1 and x2, hydration products (C–S–H, C–H, and AFt) of PO and FA, and the microaggregate effect of FA improved the filling of the internal pores and compactness of the backfill. The increase in x3 further enhanced the compactness of the backfill. The increase in UPV was mainly attributed to the densification of the microstructure. Because the UPV was higher in solid phases than in pores or air voids, a denser microstructure with fewer imperfections provided a continuous path for wave transmission, and consequently increased the UPV. The strength of the backfill was determined the hydration products. An increase in x1 and x2 increased the hydration products, which completely encapsulated solid particles and formed a flocculation structure. When x3 increased, the porosity of the solid particles decreased, and the flocculation structure overlapped better to form a network structure, thereby enhancing the strength of the backfill.

Limitations and discussion

The curing conditions of the specimens and the scale of the laboratory affected the performance of the backfill and the statistical results of the tests. The curing temperature significantly effected the mechanical properties of the backfill. Generally, the compactness, hydration degree and strength of the backfill increase with the curing temperature. The mechanical properties improve with increasing curing time. Typically, the strength increases rapidly in the early stage of curing, however, it increases slowly or remains stable in the later stage of curing. In this study, the curing temperature was 21 ± 1 ℃, and the curing time was 90 days. Future studies may consider the influence of curing temperature and time. In particular, by establishing a functional relationship between the mechanical properties of the backfill and curing temperature and time, the mechanical properties under other curing conditions can be predicted based on the limited curing temperature and time.

The laboratory scale including the quantity and size of the samples, etc. Increasing the number of samples in a single group further enhances the accuracy and reliability of the data statistics results. Because the mechanical properties of the backfill exhibite a significant size effect, a change in the sample size will affect the mechanical properties of the backfill. Future studies should consider the size effect of the samples.

Because the raw material particle sizes of the CASB are relatively small, the results of this study can be applied to similar fine-grained backfills. The nondestructive monitoring methods and the influence laws and mechanisms of multiple factors in this study can be used to analyze the mechanical properties of construction materials. The statistics and analysis based on RSM and the analysis of the multilevel influence of factors can be used as a reference for studies on the response law of chemical soil properties to various factors.

Although this study successfully established predictive models for CASB properties under laboratory conditions, their applicability in field settings requires further verification. On-site borehole sampling and in situ testing are necessary to verify the reliability of the functional relationship between the performance indicators and factors, as well as the UCS-UPV and STS-UPV relationship models. Accordingly, the necessary corrections were made to the model. The above model can be integrated into an artificial intelligence-based real-time monitoring system for backfill quality to obtain a nondestructive and rapid assessment of mechanical properties, thereby providing technical support for intelligent backfilling and mining safety.

Conclusion

To determine the effect of multiple factors on the UPV, UCS and STS of CASB, and the relationships among them, the RSM was used to analyze the effects of PO/FA (x1), fine particles/AS (x2), solid content (x3), and their interaction terms and coupling on the UPV, UCS and STS values, as well as the relationships among the UCS, STS and UPV. The major conclusions are as follows:

(1) The RSM results revealed that single factor strongly affected the UPV, UCS, and STS, and their values increased with the different factors. The response surfaces of the UPV, UCS, and STS to the interaction terms conformed to the Ploy2D-type function (z = z0 + ax + by + cx2 + dy2 + fxy) surfaces. Three–dimensional scatter plots of the UPV, UCS and STS were obtained under the coupling effect of these three factors. The effects of the three factors on the UPV, UCS and STS were revealed from three levels of "single factor analysis–interaction terms of double factors–coupling effect of three factors".

(2) The XRD, SEM, and MIP results revealed that CASB primarily consisted of flocculent gelation C–S–H, slab C–H, fine needle-shaped AFt, and Quartz. C–S–H was the most abundant hydration product, whose content increased with the increase of PO/FA and fine particles/AS, had little change with solid content. The micropores content increased, whereas the porosity decreased as the three factors increased. The strength and UPV were positively correlated with the C–S–H and micropore content, but negatively correlated with the porosity.

(3) The RSM results revealed a linear growth relationship between the STS and UCS, with the STS values primarily in the 0.09–0.105 range of the UCS values. The strength and UPV exhibited a good quadratic relationship, and UPV can be used to predict strength.

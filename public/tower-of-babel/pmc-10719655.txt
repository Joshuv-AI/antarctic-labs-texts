Nomogram based on multiparametric analysis of early‐stage breast cancer: Prediction of high burden metastatic axillary lymph nodes
Authors: Ling Li, Jing Zhao, Yu Zhang, Zhanyu Pan, Jin Zhang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe Z0011 and AMAROS trials found that axillary lymph node dissection (ALND) was no longer mandatory for early‐stage breast cancer patients who had one or two metastatic axillary lymph nodes (mALNs). The aim of our study was to establish a nomogram which could be used to quantitatively predict the individual likelihood of high burden mALN (≥3 mALN).MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed 564 women with early breast cancer who had all undergone both ultrasound (US) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) to examine axillary lymph nodes before radical surgery. All the patients were divided into training (n = 452) and validation (n = 112) cohorts by computer‐generated random numbers. Their clinicopathological features and preoperative imaging associated with high burden mALNs were evaluated by logistic regression analysis to develop a nomogram for predicting the probability of high burden mALNs.ResultsMultivariate analysis showed that high burden mALNs were significantly associated with replaced hilum and the shortest diameter >10 mm on MRI, with cortex thickness >3 mm on US (p < 0.05 each). These imaging criteria plus higher grade (grades II and III) and quadrant of breast tumor were used to develop a nomogram calculating the probability of high burden mALNs. The AUC of the nomogram was 0.853 (95% CI: 0.790–0.908) for the training set and 0.783 (95% CI: 0.638–0.929) for the validation set. Both internal and external validation evaluated the accuracy of nomogram to be good.ConclusionA well‐discriminated nomogram was developed to predict the high burden mALN in early‐stage breast patients, which may assist the breast surgeon in choosing the appropriate surgical approach.Keywords: axillary lymph node, breast cancer, clinicopathological features, magnetic resonance imaging, ultrasound

INTRODUCTION

Breast cancer is the most common malignant cancer in females. 1 Approximately 20%–30% of patients with breast cancer are found to have metastatic axillary lymph nodes (mALNs) at the time of diagnosis. 2 , 3 The presence of mALNs is an important prognostic factor and a determining factor for subsequent treatment decisions. 4 With the publication of data from the Z0011 and AMAROS trials, axillary lymph node dissection (ALND) is no longer mandatory for early‐stage breast cancer patients, who even have one or two mALN. 5 , 6 The advent of the de‐escalation era has enabled 84% of breast cancer patients to avoid postoperative complications associated with ALND. 3 Therefore, what is more important is to not only stage the ALN but also to be able to predict accurate quantification of high burden mALN (>2 mALN) preoperatively.

The conventional diagnostic method for determining whether a patient has high burden mALN is intraoperative pathological evaluation of sentinel lymph nodes (SLNs). The advancement of preoperative imaging has further propelled noninvasive management of high burden ALNs. The study by Ahn et al. found ultrasound (US) had a sensitivity of 66.7% and a specificity of 88.4% in the assessment of high burden mALN, while chest computed tomography (CT) had a sensitivity of 55.2% and specificity of 92.8%, positron emission tomography (PET)‐CT had a sensitivity of 70.7% and specificity of 74.5%. 7

Our study aimed to identify any preoperative imaging of US and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), and clinicopathological factors associated with high burden mALNs. We also aimed to establish a nomogram base on predictive factors, which could be used to quantitatively predict the probability of high burden mALNs in early‐stage breast cancer patient.

METHODS

Patients

Our study included 564 women with early breast cancer (cT1‐2 N0) who had undergone US and MRI to evaluate ALNs before proceeding to invasive procedures at the Cancer Institute and Hospital of Tianjin Medical University between January 2015 and May 2019. Patients' clinicopathological features included age, menopause status, family history of breast cancer, clinical tumor size (cT size), location and quadrant, pathological type, tumor grade, subtypes, ALN status and the expression of ER, PR, HER‐2 and Ki67. According to ACOSOG Z0011 trials, high burden mALN is defined as the presence of three or more mALNs. According to the 2022 edition Chinese Society of Clinical Oncology (CSCO) guide, the weak positive of ER is defined as the expression of 1%–10% positive cells. The power positive of ER is defined as the expression of >10% positive cells.

US and MRI examination

All patients underwent MRI (Discovery 750, GE Medical Systems), US (LOGIQ E9, LOGIQ 7 and SuperSonic Imaging Aixplorer Color Doppler Ultrasound System, GE, probe frequency: 6.0–15.0 MHz) and radical mastectomy in our hospital. All MRI and US parameters were reviewed by a breast imaging specialist. MRI parameters were collected by sagittal and axial T1 and T2 weighted sequences using a GE 1.5 T magnet. A suspicious ALN on US is generally an ALN with the shortest diameter ≥ 10 mm, L/T (long/short axis of lymph nodes)<2, cortex thickness >3 mm, abnormal shape, heterogeneous hyperechoic cortex, rich blood flow and replaced hilum. MRI criteria for a suspicious ALN included features: the shortest diameter ≥ 10 mm, L/T <2, cortex thickness >3 mm and replaced hilum. Relevant parameters were collected and analyzed.

Study population

All patients were divided into training (n = 452) and validation (n = 112) cohorts by computer‐generated random numbers. The clinicopathological, US and MR imaging features of patients in the training and validation cohorts are shown in Table 1. They were compared using the χ2 or Fisher's exact tests. No significant differences were found between them (p > 0.05). In the training cohort there were 60 patients (13.27%) with high burden mALN, and 17 patients (15.18%) in the validation cohort.

Statistical analysis

Risk factors (clinicopathological characteristics, MRI parameters or ultrasound parameters of abnormal ALN) of high burden mALNs were identified by univariate analysis also based on the χ2 or Fisher's exact tests, and significant variables (p < 0.05) were included by multivariate logistic regression to the training set. Consequently, a nomogram predicting the probability of high burden mALNs was developed based on the results of the multivariate analysis.

To evaluate the discriminative power and consensus of the established prediction model, a receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was drawn and the area under the ROC curve (AUC) was used. The larger the AUC value, the higher the prediction accuracy. The cutoff point was applied to evaluate nomogram performance and divided all patients into high‐ and low‐risk groups for predicting high burden mALNs. While the performance of the nomogram was further assessed by the calibration curve, which plotted the predicted probability of the nomogram against the observed probability. Furthermore, to determine the clinical utility of the prediction model, decision curve analysis (DCA) was performed to evaluate the clinical value of the models independently by calculating the net benefit for patients at each risk threshold.

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 19.0 (SPSS Inc.) and R software version 3.4.1 (http://www.r-project.org/) with the “foreign”, “rms”, “pROC”, “rmda”, “ggplot2” and “ggDCA”. Two‐sided p‐values less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

Risk factors of high burden mALNs in the training cohort

In the training cohorts, risk factors were determined by univariate analysis with the chi‐squared or Fisher's exact test for different clinicopathological and imaging features. These variables comprised age (<35 years; ≥35 years), menopausal status (postmenopausal, premenopausal), family history of breast cancer (yes; no), cT size (≤2 cm; >2 cm, ≤5 cm), location (left breast; right breast), quadrant (inner upper; inner down; outer down; outer upper; central), pathological type (invasive ductal carcinoma; carcinoma in situ; special type of carcinoma), tumor grade (I; II; III), subtypes (luminal A; luminal B; HER2 enriched; basal‐like), and the expression of ER (negative; power positive; weak positive), PR (negative; positive), HER‐2 (negative; positive), Ki67 (<20%; ≥20%), US imaging features (the shortest diameter, L/T, shape, cortex thickness, blood flow and hilum) and MRI features (the shortest diameter, L/T, cortex thickness and hilum), shown in Table 2. Among the patients having high burden mALNs, it was significantly associated with quadrant (p = 0.005) and tumor grade (p = 0.007). It also showed that having high burden mALN was significantly associated with US imaging features (the shortest diameter, L/T, cortex thickness, rich blood flow and hilum) and MR imaging features (the shortest diameter, cortex thickness and hilum), all p < 0.001.

Performance of the nomogram

We assigned a matching point to each variable, and then summed to yield the total number of points (Figure 1). We assigned risk scores for each risk factor based on the regression coefficients from the final model for predicting high burden mALNs (Table 4). The overall risk scores of all patients ranged between 0–209, and we chose 160 as the cutoff score for predicting high burden mALN rates. Then, patients were divided into high‐ and low‐risk groups according to the cutoff point. In the low‐risk group, 72.90% had benign pathology (304/417), while 21.58% (90/417) had 1–2 mALN. Of the 417 patients who were predicted to have ≤2 mALN, only 23 (5.5%) showed false‐negative results. Thus, this cutoff could be effectively used to define a subset of patients who have ≤2 mALNs or >3 mALNs. The decision curve analysis simultaneously showed that using the nomogram to predict high burden mALN provided a better net benefit than either the treat‐all or treat‐none scheme in predicting high burden mALNs, with a potential threshold, as shown in Figure 4.

DISCUSSION

In the post‐ACOSOG Z0011 and AMAROS trials era, the identification of high burden mALNs has become crucial. However, the number of positive ALN detected in the above trials relies on sentinel lymph node biopsy (SLNB) rather than imaging examination. Riedel et al. even suggests that US performance is insufficient to accurately identify mALN. 8 However, the SLNB is an intraoperative procedure which also carries postoperative complications, although its severity and incidence are lower than ALND. 9

With the rapid development of imaging diagnosis technologies, researchers have begun to explore nonsurgical methods for assessing high burden mALNs. 10 , 11 , 12 , 13 Ahn et al. found that although it was difficult to distinguish between benign and malignant ALN on imaging examination, assessing high burden mALNs may be feasible. They attempted to develop a nomogram incorporating age, US and CT findings for predicting the probability of high burden mALNs by analyzing preoperative imaging of ALNs. The AUC of the nomogram was 0.852. The sensitivity was 66.7%, and specificity was 88.4%. 7 This is consistent with our research results. In our study, the AUC of the nomogram was 0.853. The sensitivity of the developed nomogram was 76.7% with a specificity of 80.6%.

US and MRI, as two noninvasive, nonradioactive, preoperative imaging methods, are already widely used for the assessment of ALNs. 14 , 15 , 16 Therefore, we are striving to develop a nomogram which incorporates these methods into predicting the probability of high burden mALNs.

In comparison with other technical studies, US is recommended by many researchers because of its real‐time performance, noninvasiveness, ease of implementation, lower cost, and high detection rate. 17 , 18 , 19 , 20 mALN has certain ultrasound features which can be evidence of suspicious ALN involvement. L/T, disappearance of lymph node hilum, thickened lymph node cortex and change of vascular pattern of the lymph node scored by sonographers, are suggestive characters for diagnosing mALN. 21 In our univariate analysis, the shortest diameter ≥ 10 mm, L/T<2, cortex thickness >3 mm, rich blood flow and replaced hilum were associated with high burden mALNs. In multivariable analysis, cortex thickness >3 mm was an independent factor. The AUC of US for high burden mALNs was 0.713.

MRI usually depends on the morphological criteria of ALN. Previous studies have detected the associated morphological features with MR images, such as the shape and size of ALN, fatty hilum, whole‐breast vascularity, and cortex thickness, detected and assessed by MRI diagnostic radiographers. 22 MRI can provide an instantaneous global view of the axilla and allowes the observation of lymph nodes on both sides, with less dependence on the operator compared to ultrasound. 23 However, MRI employs a breast coil centered around the breast. As you move farther away from the breast, the signal‐to‐noise ratio (SNR) of the image decreases, and the quality of MRI can be affected by heartbeat. Because ALNs may be located at the edge of the coil, MRI scanning may only be suitable for assess in the ALNs partially near the thoracic and mammary glands. What is more, the high false‐positive rate of MRI results in more patients undergoing increased preoperative biopsies and unnecessary surgical interventions. 24 In our study, high burden mALNs were significantly associated with MRI features of the shortest diameter ≥ 10 mm, cortex thickness >3 mm and replaced hilum. The AUC for high burden mALNs produced by MRI was 0.724.

Patients with high burden mALNs re significantly associated with higher grade (grades II and III) of breast tumor, in line with the study by Yip et al. 25 It is interesting to note we observed that patients with grade II breast tumor were more prone to mALN compared with grade III, although there was no statistical significance between them. The same results have also been found in other studies. 25 , 26 Other studies found that a breast tumor occurring in the upper outer quadrant was more likely to have mALN. 27 However, in our study, we did not find the same result. We only found that a breast tumor occurring in the upper inner quadrant was least likely to have high burden mALNs, which is consistent with the study by Chen et al. 26

The aim of our study was also to build a nomogram model for predicting high burden mALNs based on the demonstrated independently correlated imaging features and clinicopathological characteristics. Gao et al. and Zhang et al. also established a nomogram based on MRI or US radiomic analysis of mALN images combined with clinicopathological features. 28 , 29 The AUC of the models were also 0.846 and 0.820 separately, similar to our model. However, the manual ROI segmentation of each lesion is time consuming and complicated, and a reliable segmentation technique with reproducibility is needed.

In our study, we faced the common problem of identifying ALNs node‐by‐node through radiological‐pathological correlation in a retrospective study. The selection bias of a retrospective study is inevitable. The established nomogram for predicting the high burden mALNs in early‐stage breast patients is based on data extracted from a single‐center. Therefore, more multicenter cases are needed as an external validation to establish the practicality of the nomogram.

In conclusion, we revealed that patients with high burden mALNs can be identified using a nomogram incorporating preoperative imaging of US and MRI, and clinicopathological factors. The developed nomogram may be useful to breast surgeons when choosing a surgical approach.

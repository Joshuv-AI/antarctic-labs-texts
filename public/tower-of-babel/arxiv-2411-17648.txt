2.  Review of calibrated geometry

The purpose of this second section is to recall the four main examples of calibrated geometries and to collect different characterizations of their calibrated submanifolds.
See, for example,
[
HL82
,
Joy07
,
Lot22
]
for more details, or
[
KM05
]
for a quick overview.

Calibrated submanifolds are a special case of minimal submanifolds of a Riemannian manifold.
They offer two key advantages over general minimal submanifolds:
First, they are characterized by a nonlinear partial differential equation of only first-order on the immersion map. Second, they are volume-minimizing in their homology class.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
and
1\leq k\leq n-1
.
An
oriented tangent
k
-plane
on
M
is an oriented
k
-dimensional vector subspace
V
of some tangent space
T_{p}M
to
M
. Given such a
V
,
g|_{V}
together with the orientation on
V
gives a natural volume form on
V
, which we denote by
\text{vol}_{V}\in\Lambda^{k}(V^{*})
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
k
-calibration
on
M
is a closed
k
-form
\varphi\in\Omega^{k}(M)
which satisfies
\varphi|_{V}\leq\text{vol}_{V}
for all oriented tangent
k
-planes
V
on
M
.
We call a
k
-dimensional oriented immersed submanifold
N
of
M

calibrated
by
\varphi
if
\varphi|_{T_{p}N}=\text{vol}_{T_{p}N}
for all
p\in N
.

If
M
is the total space of a vector bundle over a base
X
and a calibrated submanifold
N
is also the total space of a subbundle, we call
N
a
calibrated subbundle
of
M
.
In this context, a
subbundle
of
M\rightarrow X
means a vector bundle
N\rightarrow L
over a submanifold
L
of
X
, whose fibers are subspaces of the corresponding fibers of
M
.

Due to the
holonomy principle
, manifolds of reduced holonomy are likely to possess calibrations with good prospects of calibrated submanifolds (see
[
Joy07
, Prop. 2.5.2, Ch. 4.2]
for details). This approach leads us to the following four main examples:

(I)

A
Kähler manifold
is a Riemannian manifold
(M^{2n},g)
with holonomy in
\text{U}(n)
. Equivalently, it is a complex manifold
(M,J)
with Hermitian metric
g
and Kähler form
\omega
. Given such a manifold,
\omega^{k}/k!
defines a calibration whose calibrated submanifolds are the complex
k
-dimensional submanifolds, i.e., those submanifolds
N\subset M
satisfying
J(T_{p}N)=T_{p}N
for every
p\in N
.

(II)

A
Calabi–Yau manifold
is a Riemannian manifold
(M^{2n},g)
with holonomy in
\text{SU}(n)
. Equivalently, it is a Ricci-flat Kähler manifold
(M,J,g,\omega)
equipped with a holomorphic volume form
\Omega
. In this case,
\text{Re}(\exp(-i\theta)\Omega)
is a calibration for every
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. The corresponding calibrated submanifolds are the
special Lagrangian
submanifolds with phase
\exp(i\theta)
, which are defined as the oriented real
n
-dimensional submanifolds
N
of
M
that satisfy

\displaystyle\omega|_{N}=0\text{ ({Lagrangian})}\quad\text{ and }\quad\text{Im}\bigl(\exp(-i\theta)\Omega\bigr)|_{N}=0\text{ ({special} Lagrangian)}.

(III)

A
\text{G}_{2}
-manifold
is a Riemannian manifold
(M^{7},g)
with holonomy in
\text{G}_{2}
.
This is equivalent to the existence of a parallel
\text{G}_{2}
-structure
\varphi\in\Omega^{3}(M)
on
M
.
Both the
associative
3
-form

\varphi\in\Omega^{3}(M)
and the
coassociative
4
-form

\psi=*\varphi\in\Omega^{4}(M)
are calibrations and we call the corresponding calibrated submanifolds
associative
3
-folds
and
coassociative
4
-folds
, respectively.

(IV)

A
\text{Spin}(7)
-manifold
is a Riemannian manifold
(M^{8},g)
with holonomy in
\text{Spin}(7)
.
This is equivalent to the existence of a parallel
\text{Spin}(7)
-structure
\Phi\in\Omega^{4}(M)
on
M
.
The
Cayley
4
-form

\Phi\in\Omega^{4}(M)
defines a calibration and its calibrated submanifolds are known as
Cayley
4
-folds
.

There are different characterizations of the calibrated submanifolds in examples
(III)
and
(IV)
, and we compile some of them in the remainder of this section.
Starting with example
(III)
, recall that there exists a (not necessarily parallel)
\text{G}_{2}
-structure
\varphi\in\Omega^{3}(M)
on
(M^{7},g)
if and only if its tangent spaces can be identified with the purely imaginary octonions
\text{Im}\,\mathbb{O}
in a smoothly varying way.
Due to this, a
\text{G}_{2}
-manifold
(M^{7},g,\varphi)
inherits the associator
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]
and coassociator
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]
from
\text{Im}\,\mathbb{O}
.
Additionally,
\varphi
defines a natural two-fold cross product via
(u\times v)^{\flat}=v\lrcorner u\lrcorner\varphi
, where
\lrcorner
denotes the interior product.

As established in
[
HL82
, Sec. IV.1]
and
[
KM05
, Prop. 2.3]
, an oriented submanifold
E^{3}
is associative in
(M^{7},g,\varphi)
, that is,
\varphi|_{E}=\text{vol}_{E}
, if and only if one of the following equivalent conditions (up to a change of orientation) is satisfied:

(A1)

The tangent space
TE\subset TM
of
E
is preserved by the two-fold cross product.

(A2)

The associator
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]
vanishes on
E
.

(A3)

At every point
p\in E
, we have
u\lrcorner v\lrcorner w\lrcorner\psi=0
for some basis
\{u,v,w\}
of
T_{p}E
.

Furthermore,
[
HL82
, Sec. IV.1]
demonstrated that an oriented submanifold
F^{4}
is coassociative in
(M^{7},g,\varphi)
, that is,
\psi|_{F}=\text{vol}_{F}
, if and only if it fulfills one of the following equivalent conditions (up to a change of orientation):

(B1)

The two-fold cross product
u\times v
is orthogonal to
T_{p}F
for all
u,v\in T_{p}F,\,p\in F
.

(B2)

The coassociator
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]
vanishes on
F
.

(B3)

\varphi|_{F}=0
.

Lastly, we turn to example
(IV)
.
Similarly to the
\text{G}_{2}
case,
(M^{8},g)
admits a (not necessarily parallel)
\text{Spin}(7)
-structure
\Phi\in\Omega^{4}(M)
if and only if its tangent spaces can be smoothly identified with the octonions
\mathbb{O}
.
Thus, a
\text{Spin}(7)
-manifold
(M^{8},g,\Phi)
acquires a four-fold cross product
\cdot\times\cdot\times\cdot\times\cdot
, and its tangent vectors split into real and imaginary parts.
Furthermore,
\Phi
defines a three-fold cross product
X
via
X(u,v,w)^{\flat}=(-u\times v\times w)^{\flat}=w\lrcorner v\lrcorner u\lrcorner\Phi
, where we introduce a sign to match the convention used in
[
KM05
]
.

By
[
HL82
, Sec. IV.1]
and
[
KM05
, Prop. 2.5]
,
an oriented submanifold
F^{4}
is Cayley in
(M^{8},g,\Phi)
, that is,
\Phi|_{F}=\text{vol}_{F}
, if and only if one of the following equivalent conditions (up to a change of orientation) is satisfied:

(C1)

The tangent space
TF\subset TM
of
F
is preserved by the three-fold cross product
X
.

(C2)

At every point
p\in F
, we have
\text{Im}(u\times v\times w\times y)=0
for some basis
\{u,v,w,y\}
of
T_{p}F
.

(C3)

At every point
p\in F
, the rank
7
bundle valued
4
-form
\eta
on
M
defined by

\displaystyle\hskip-15.0pt\eta(u,v,w,y)

\displaystyle=u^{\flat}\wedge X(v,w,y)^{\flat}+v^{\flat}\wedge X(w,u,y)^{\flat}+w^{\flat}\wedge X(u,v,y)^{\flat}+y^{\flat}\wedge X(v,u,w)^{\flat}

\displaystyle\quad+u\lrcorner X(v,w,y)\lrcorner\Phi+v\lrcorner X(w,u,y)\lrcorner\Phi+w\lrcorner X(u,v,y)\lrcorner\Phi+y\lrcorner X(v,u,w)\lrcorner\Phi

vanishes for some basis
\{u,v,w,y\}
of
T_{p}F
.

Remark 2.2
.

For a brief overview of the alternating multilinear brackets and cross products on
\mathbb{O}
, see
[
Mer24
]
. Further details can be found in
[
HL82
, Sec. IV.1, IV.B]
and
[
SW17
]
.

3.  The second fundamental form

Let
(X^{n},g=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
be a real
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold and consider some oriented immersed submanifold
L^{q}
with immersion
x\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}L\rightarrow X
.
We write
(\,)^{T}
and
(\,)^{N}
for the orthogonal projections from
x^{*}(TX)
onto the tangent bundle
dx(TL)\cong TL
and normal bundle
NL
of
L
in
X
, respectively.
Throughout this paper,
\nabla
always denotes the Levi-Civita connection on the tangent bundle
TX
of the ambient manifold
X
, unless stated otherwise.

In the following sections, we examine whether certain subbundles
N\rightarrow L
of a bundle
M\rightarrow X
are calibrated by a given calibration on
M
. Since this is a pointwise algebraic condition on the tangent space, we have the freedom of working in coordinates specially adapted to each point in
N
. We now describe the local setup for a fixed point
p^{*}\in L
.

By parallel transporting orthonormal bases of
(d_{p^{*}}x)(T_{p^{*}}L)
and
N_{p^{*}}L
via the induced connections on
dx(TL)
and
NL
, respectively, we obtain a local orthonormal frame
e_{1},\dots,e_{q},\nu_{q+1},\dots,\nu_{n}
for
x^{*}(TX)
that satisfies

(3.1)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{e_{i}}e_{j})|_{p^{*}}^{T}=0\qquad\text{ and }\qquad(\nabla_{e_{i}}\nu_{k})|_{p^{*}}^{N}=0,

and which we refer to as a
normal frame
at
p^{*}
.
Given such a frame, we choose geodesic normal coordinates
u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{q})
for
L
centered at
p^{*}
such that
\partial_{u_{i}}({p^{*}})=(d_{p^{*}}x)^{-1}(e_{i}(p^{*}))\in T_{p^{*}}L
for
i=1,\dots,q
. In that case, the coordinates of the fixed point are
p^{*}=(0,\dots,0)
, and the directional derivatives of the immersion
x
at this point are given by
\partial x/\partial u_{i}(p^{*})=(d_{p^{*}}x)(\partial_{u_{i}}(p^{*}))=e_{i}(p^{*})\in(d_{p^{*}}x)(T_{p^{*}}L)
.

From now on, we identify
dx(TL)
with
TL
, while keeping in mind that the former is technically a subbundle of
x^{*}(TX)
, and is spanned by
(d_{p^{*}}x)(T_{p^{*}}L)=\text{span}\{e_{i}(p^{*})=\partial x/\partial u_{i}(p^{*})\mid i=1,\dots,q\}\subset(x^{*}(TX))_{p^{*}}=T_{x(p^{*})}X
at the point
p^{*}
. Moreover, we write
TX|_{L}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}x^{*}(TX)

\int^{B}
.

Let the
second fundamental form

A
of the immersion
L^{q}\subset X^{n}
be defined as the bilinear operator

\displaystyle A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Gamma(NL)\times\Gamma(TL)\rightarrow\Gamma(TL),\quad(\nu,w)\mapsto A^{\nu}(w)=(\nabla_{w}\nu)^{T}.

It is easy to check that for any normal vector field
\nu
,
A^{\nu}
is a symmetric linear operator and, hence, diagonalizable
[
IKM05
, Sec. 2]
. We use the following abbreviations:

\displaystyle A^{\nu}_{ij}=\langle A^{\nu}(e_{i}),e_{j}\rangle=A^{\nu}_{ji}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad A^{k}_{ij}=A^{\nu_{k}}_{ij}.

Remark 3.1
.

There are different ways to define the second fundamental form. Above, we stated the definition used in
[
HL82
]
and
[
IKM05
]
, which relates to the definition

\displaystyle A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Gamma(TL)\times\Gamma(TL)\rightarrow\Gamma(NL),\quad(u,w)\mapsto A(u,w)=(\nabla_{w}u)^{N}

via
\langle A(u,w),\nu\rangle=-\langle u,A^{\nu}(w)\rangle

for every
\nu\in\Gamma(NL)
.
As the sign does not affect the results, we stick to the sign conventions and terms used in
[
IKM05
]
and continue to refer to
A^{\nu}
as the second fundamental form in the direction of
\nu
.

Remark 3.2
.

Recall that
L
is minimal in
X
if and only if
\text{Tr}\,A=0
, which is equivalent to demanding
\text{Tr}\,A^{k}=0
for all
k=q+1,\dots,n
in our notation.

Finally, using a normal frame (
3.1
) and the second fundamental form
A
, we obtain the identities

(3.2)

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{i}}e^{j}=-\sum_{k=q+1}^{n}A^{k}_{ij}\nu^{k}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\nabla_{e_{i}}\nu^{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{q}A^{k}_{ij}e^{j}

at
p^{*}
, where
e^{1},\dots,e^{q}
and
\nu^{q+1},\dots,\nu^{n}
are the dual coframes
[
IKM05
, (2.3)]
.

4.  Special Lagrangians in
T^{*}S^{n}
with the Stenzel metric

We begin by examining special Lagrangians in the cotangent space
T^{*}S^{n}
of the standard round sphere.
To do so, we first endow
T^{*}S^{n}
with a Calabi–Yau structure following
[
Sző91
,
Ste93
,
Anc07
,
KM05
]
: We identify
T^{*}S^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\oplus(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})^{*}
with the complex quadric

\displaystyle Q=\Bigl\{(z_{0},\dots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\ \Big|\ \sum_{k=0}^{n}z_{k}^{2}=1\Bigr\}

via the diffeomorphism

(4.1)

\displaystyle\Psi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}T^{*}S^{n}\rightarrow Q,\quad(x,\xi)\mapsto x\cosh\lvert\xi\rvert+i\frac{\xi}{\lvert\xi\rvert}\sinh\lvert\xi\rvert,

where the term
\frac{\xi}{\lvert\xi\rvert}\sinh\lvert\xi\rvert
is to be interpreted as
0
when
|\xi|=0
, and we identify
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}=\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\oplus i(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})^{*}
.
From this,
T^{*}S^{n}
inherits a natural complex structure, a nowhere vanishing holomorphic
(n,0)
-form
\Omega
and the Stenzel metric, turning it into a Calabi–Yau manifold.
By
[
Anc07
, Lemma 2.1]
, the corresponding Kähler form is given by

\displaystyle\omega_{\text{St}}=\frac{i}{2}\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}a_{jk}dz_{j}\wedge d\bar{z}_{k}

with

(4.2)

\displaystyle a_{jk}=\left(\delta_{jk}+\frac{z_{j}\bar{z}_{k}}{\lvert z_{0}\rvert^{2}}\right)v^{\prime}+2\,\text{Re}\left(\overline{z}_{j}z_{k}-\frac{\bar{z}_{0}}{z_{0}}z_{j}z_{k}\right)v^{\prime\prime}

in a neighborhood of a point where
z_{0}\neq 0
.
Above,
v
is a function of
r=\lvert z\rvert
, defined by a certain differential equation which ensures that the metric is Ricci-flat. For our purposes, it suffices to know that
v^{\prime}(r),v^{\prime\prime}(r)>0
for
r>0

[
Ste93
, Prop. 6]
.
By construction, the Kähler potential only depends on
r
, which implies that
\omega_{\text{St}}
is
\text{O}_{n+1}(\mathbb{C})
-invariant.

Let
L^{q}\subset S^{n}
be an oriented immersed submanifold and

be a

\displaystyle X_{\mu}=\bigl\{(p,\xi+\mu(p))\in T^{*}S^{n}|_{L}\,\big|\,p\in L,\,\xi\in N^{*}_{p}L\bigr\},

”

This is a

of the conormal bundle

\mu({p})\in T^{*}_{p}L
.
Our goal is to find conditions on

. We start with the conditions for it to be Lagrangian.

Theorem 4.1
.

The submanifold
N^{*}L+\mu
is Lagrangian in
T^{*}S^{n}
if and only if
\mu=0
.

Proof.

The submanifold
N^{*}L+\mu
is Lagrangian in
T^{*}S^{n}
if and only if its tangent space at every point is a Lagrangian subspace of the corresponding tangent space to
T^{*}S^{n}
. We will show that for this to be the case at a point
(p,\xi+\mu(p))\in(N^{*}L\setminus L)+\mu
, the 1-form
\mu
has to vanish at
p
. The other implication then follows from the proof of
[
KM05
, Thm. 3.1]
.

To do so, we fix a point
(p^{*},\xi^{*}+\mu({p^{*}}))\in X_{\mu}
with
\xi^{*}\neq 0
, and let
(e^{1},\dots,e^{q},\nu^{q+1},\dots,\nu^{n})
be a local orthonormal adapted coframe along
L
whose dual frame is normal at
p^{*}
(see (
3.1
)). Moreover, we let
u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{q})
be the coordinates for
L
described in
Section 3
, and
t=(t_{q+1},\dots,t_{n})
be the coordinates on the fiber with respect to the chosen coframe.
Then our fixed point
(p^{*},\xi^{*}+\mu({p^{*}}))
has the coordinates
(0,t^{*})
for some
t^{*}=(t_{q+1}^{*},\dots,t_{n}^{*})\neq 0
.

The immersion of
N^{*}L+\mu
into
T^{*}S^{n}
,
\Phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X_{\mu}\rightarrow T^{*}S^{n}
, is locally given by

\displaystyle\Phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(u,t)\mapsto\Bigl(x(u),\sum_{k=q+1}^{n}t_{k}\nu^{k}(u)+\mu(u)\Bigr)=\Bigl(x(u),\sum_{k=q+1}^{n}t_{k}\nu^{k}(u)+\sum_{l=1}^{q}a_{l}(u)e^{l}(u)\Bigr),

where
x
is the immersion of
L
into
S^{n}
, and
a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{q})
are the coordinates of
\mu
with respect to the local trivialization
T^{*}L\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{loc}}}{{=}}\text{span}\{e^{1},\dots,e^{q}\}
\int^{B}
.

Define
\hat{\nu}(u,t)=\sum_{k=q+1}^{n}t_{k}\nu^{k}(u)
and
y(u,t)=\lvert\hat{\nu}(u,t)+\mu(u)\rvert^{2}
.
Since
e^{1},\dots,e^{q},\allowbreak\nu^{q+1},\dots,\nu^{n}
are orthonormal, we get

\displaystyle y(u,t)

\displaystyle=\Big\lvert\sum_{k=q+1}^{n}t_{k}\nu^{k}(u)+\sum_{l=1}^{q}a_{l}(u)e^{l}(u)\Big\rvert^{2}=\sum_{k=q+1}^{n}\lvert t_{k}\rvert^{2}+\sum_{l=1}^{q}\lvert a_{l}(u)\rvert^{2}

(4.3)

\displaystyle=\lvert t\rvert^{2}+\lvert a(u)\rvert^{2}.

In particular,
y
vanishes nowhere in a sufficiently small neighborhood of
(0,t^{*})
because
t^{*}\neq 0
.
Restricting the diffeomorphism
\Psi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}T^{*}S^{n}\rightarrow Q
(see (
4.1
)) to
\Phi(X_{\mu})\subset T^{*}S^{n}
gives

(4.4)

\displaystyle\Psi(x,\hat{\nu}+\mu)=x\cosh\sqrt{y}+i\,\frac{\hat{\nu}+\mu}{\sqrt{y}}\sinh\sqrt{y}.

We want to determine when the Kähler form
\omega_{\text{St}}
vanishes on the tangent space to
(\Psi\circ\Phi)(X_{\mu})
at
z^{*}=(\Psi\circ\Phi)(0,t^{*})\in Q\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
.
Let
(\mathbf{e}_{0},\dots,\mathbf{e}_{n})
denote the orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with dual basis
(\mathbf{e}^{0},\dots,\mathbf{e}^{n})
defined by
(x,e^{1},\dots,e^{q},\nu^{q+1},\dots,\nu^{n})|_{u=0}=(\mathbf{e}_{0},\mathbf{e}^{1},\dots,\mathbf{e}^{n})
.
Then the point
z^{*}
takes the form

\displaystyle z^{*}

\displaystyle=\cosh\sqrt{y(0,t^{*})}\,\mathbf{e}_{0}+i\,\frac{\sum_{k=q+1}^{n}t_{k}^{*}\mathbf{e}^{k}+\sum_{l=1}^{q}a_{l}(0)\mathbf{e}^{l}}{\sqrt{y(0,t^{*})}}\sinh\sqrt{y(0,t^{*})},

which simplifies to

\displaystyle z^{*}=\cosh\sqrt{y}\,\mathbf{e}_{0}+i\sum_{l=1}^{q}\biggl(\frac{\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}a_{l}\biggr)\mathbf{e}^{l}+i\sum_{k=q+1}^{n}\biggl(\frac{\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}t_{k}^{*}\biggr)\mathbf{e}^{k},

omitting the dependence on
(0,t^{*})
.

The invariance of
\omega_{\text{St}}
under
\text{O}_{n+1}(\mathbb{C})
allows us to transform the standard coordinates on
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
via (complex) orthogonal transformations without changing the form’s local expression (as long as the coefficients
a_{jk}
remain well-defined). Explicitly, we can use
(\mathbf{e}_{0},\dots,\mathbf{e}_{n})
as the underlying orthonormal basis for
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with dual basis
(\mathbf{e}^{0},\dots,\mathbf{e}^{n})
. The corresponding coordinates for
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}=\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\oplus i(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})^{*}
are then given by
z_{k}=\langle\text{Re}\,\cdot,\mathbf{e}_{k}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}+i\langle\text{Im}\,\cdot,\mathbf{e}^{k}\rangle_{(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})^{*}}
for
k=0,\dots,n
, and are related to the standard coordinates on
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
via a (real) orthogonal transformation. As these functions are linear, they satisfy
dz_{k}=z_{k}
for all
k=0,\dots,n
.

As a result, the point
z^{*}
has the coordinates

\displaystyle z_{0}=\cosh\sqrt{y},\quad z_{l}=i\,\frac{\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}a_{l}\quad(l=1,\dots,q),\quad z_{k}=i\,\frac{\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}t^{*}_{k}\quad(k=q+1,\dots,n).

Therefore, the coefficients of the Kähler form
\omega_{\text{St}}
corresponding to the Stenzel metric (see (
4.2
)) are given by

\displaystyle a_{kl}=a_{lk}=\begin{cases}\biggl(\delta_{kl}+\frac{a_{k}a_{l}}{y}\tanh^{2}\sqrt{y}\biggr)v^{\prime}+\frac{4a_{k}a_{l}}{y}\sinh^{2}\sqrt{y}\,v^{\prime\prime},&k\leq l\leq q,\\
\frac{a_{k}t_{l}^{*}}{y}\tanh^{2}\sqrt{y}\,v^{\prime}+\frac{4a_{k}t^{*}_{l}}{y}\sinh^{2}\sqrt{y}\,v^{\prime\prime},&k\leq q<l,\\
\biggl(\delta_{kl}+\frac{t^{*}_{k}t^{*}_{l}}{y}\tanh^{2}\sqrt{y}\biggr)v^{\prime}+\frac{4t^{*}_{k}t^{*}_{l}}{y}\sinh^{2}\sqrt{y}\,v^{\prime\prime},&q<k\leq l\end{cases}

at the point
z^{*}
.
Their symmetry allows us to write
\omega_{\text{St}}
as

(4.5)

\displaystyle\omega_{\text{St}}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{kk}(dz_{k}\wedge d\bar{z}_{k})+\frac{i}{2}\sum_{1\leq k<l\leq n}a_{kl}(dz_{k}\wedge d\bar{z}_{l}+dz_{l}\wedge d\bar{z}_{k}).

The tangent space
T_{z^{*}}((\Psi\circ\Phi)(X_{\mu}))
is spanned by
E_{i}=(\Psi\circ\Phi)_{*}(\partial_{u_{i}})
and
F_{j}=(\Psi\circ\Phi)_{*}(\partial_{t_{j}})
for
i=1,\dots,q
and
j=q+1,\dots,n
. Specifically,
E_{i}
is given by

\displaystyle E_{i}

\displaystyle=\Bigg[\frac{\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}\biggl(\sum_{l=1}^{q}a_{l}\frac{\partial a_{l}}{\partial{u_{i}}}\biggr)x+\cosh\sqrt{y}\,\frac{\partial x}{\partial{u_{i}}}\Bigg.

\displaystyle\qquad+i\,\frac{1}{y}\biggl(\sum_{l=1}^{q}a_{l}\frac{\partial a_{l}}{\partial{u_{i}}}\biggr)\biggl(\cosh\sqrt{y}-\frac{\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}\biggr)(\hat{\nu}+\mu)\Bigg.+i\,\frac{\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}\left(\nabla_{e_{i}}\hat{\nu}+\nabla_{e_{i}}\mu\right)\Bigg]\Bigg|_{(0,t^{*})}

for all
i=1,\dots,q
.
As we are working with a normal frame, we can use (
3.2
) to compute

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{i}}\hat{\nu}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{e_{i}}\Bigl(\sum_{k=q+1}^{n}t_{k}\nu^{k}\Bigr)=\sum_{k=q+1}^{n}t_{k}(\nabla_{e_{i}}\nu^{k})=\sum_{k=q+1}^{n}t_{k}\Bigl(\sum_{l=1}^{q}A^{k}_{il}e^{l}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{q}\Bigl(\sum_{k=q+1}^{n}t_{k}A^{k}_{il}\Bigr)e^{l}=\sum_{l=1}^{q}A^{\hat{\nu}}_{il}e^{l}=\sum_{l=1}^{q}A^{\hat{\nu}}_{il}\mathbf{e}^{l}

and

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{i}}\mu

\displaystyle=\nabla_{e_{i}}\Bigl(\sum_{l=1}^{q}a_{l}e^{l}\Bigr)=\sum_{l=1}^{q}\biggl(\frac{\partial a_{l}}{\partial u_{i}}e^{l}+a_{l}\nabla_{e_{i}}e^{l}\biggr)=\sum_{l=1}^{q}\biggl(\frac{\partial a_{l}}{\partial u_{i}}e^{l}-\sum_{k=q+1}^{n}a_{l}A^{k}_{il}\nu^{k}\biggr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{q}\frac{\partial a_{l}}{\partial u_{i}}e^{l}-\sum_{k=q+1}^{n}\Bigl(\sum_{l=1}^{q}a_{l}A^{k}_{il}\Bigr)\nu^{k}=\sum_{l=1}^{q}\frac{\partial a_{l}}{\partial u_{i}}\mathbf{e}^{l}-\sum_{k=q+1}^{n}\Bigl(\sum_{l=1}^{q}a_{l}A^{k}_{il}\Bigr)\mathbf{e}^{k}

at
(0,t^{*})
.
Thus, every
E_{i}
takes the form

\displaystyle E_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}\biggl(\sum_{l=1}^{q}a_{l}\frac{\partial a_{l}}{\partial{u_{i}}}\biggr)\mathbf{e}_{0}+\cosh\sqrt{y}\,\mathbf{e}_{i}

\displaystyle\quad+i\,\frac{1}{y}\biggl(\sum_{l=1}^{q}a_{l}\frac{\partial a_{l}}{\partial{u_{i}}}\biggr)\biggl(\cosh\sqrt{y}-\frac{\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}\biggr)\Bigl(\sum_{l=1}^{q}a_{l}\mathbf{e}^{l}+\sum_{k=q+1}^{n}t_{k}^{*}\mathbf{e}^{k}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+i\,\frac{\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}\Biggl(\sum_{l=1}^{q}\biggl(A^{\hat{\nu}}_{il}+\frac{\partial a_{l}}{\partial u_{i}}\biggr)\mathbf{e}^{l}-\sum_{k=q+1}^{n}\Bigl(\sum_{l=1}^{q}a_{l}A^{k}_{il}\Bigr)\mathbf{e}^{k}\Biggr).

On the other hand,
F_{j}=(\Psi\circ\Phi)_{*}(\partial_{t_{j}})
is given by

\displaystyle F_{j}

\displaystyle=\Bigg[t_{j}\frac{\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}x+i\,\frac{t_{j}}{y}\biggl(\cosh\sqrt{y}-\frac{\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}\biggr)(\hat{\nu}+\mu)+i\,\frac{\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}\nu^{j}\Bigg]\Bigg|_{(0,t^{*})}

\displaystyle=t_{j}^{*}\frac{\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}\mathbf{e}_{0}+i\,\frac{t_{j}^{*}}{y}\biggl(\cosh\sqrt{y}-\frac{\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}\biggr)\Bigl(\sum_{l=1}^{q}a_{l}\mathbf{e}^{l}+\sum_{k=q+1}^{n}t_{k}^{*}\mathbf{e}^{k}\Bigr)+i\,\frac{\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}\mathbf{e}^{j}

for all
j=q+1,\dots,n
.

Our goal is to compute
\omega_{\text{St}}
of any pair of vectors in the basis
E_{1},\dots,E_{q},\allowbreak F_{q+1},\dots,F_{n}
.
To do so, we first need to determine
dz_{k}
and
d\bar{z}_{k}
of every basis vector for all
k=1,\dots,n
.
(Recall that
dz_{k}=z_{k}=\langle\text{Re}\,\cdot,\mathbf{e}_{k}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}+i\langle\text{Im}\,\cdot,\mathbf{e}^{k}\rangle_{(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})^{*}}
for all
k
.)
For every
i=1,\dots,q
, we find

\displaystyle dz_{k}(E_{i})

\displaystyle=\overline{d\bar{z}_{k}(E_{i})}

\displaystyle=\ \delta_{ik}\cosh\sqrt{y}

\displaystyle\quad+i\,\Biggl(\frac{a_{k}}{y}\biggl(\sum_{l=1}^{q}a_{l}\frac{\partial a_{l}}{\partial{u_{i}}}\biggr)\biggl(\cosh\sqrt{y}-\frac{\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}\biggr)+\biggl(A^{\hat{\nu}}_{ik}+\frac{\partial a_{k}}{\partial u_{i}}\biggr)\frac{\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}\Biggr)

for
k\leq q
, and

\displaystyle dz_{k}(E_{i})

\displaystyle=-d\bar{z}_{k}(E_{i})

\displaystyle=i\,\Biggl(\frac{t^{*}_{k}}{y}\biggl(\sum_{l=1}^{q}a_{l}\frac{\partial a_{l}}{\partial{u_{i}}}\biggr)\biggl(\cosh\sqrt{y}-\frac{\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}\biggr)-\Bigl(\sum_{l=1}^{q}a_{l}A^{k}_{il}\Bigr)\frac{\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}\Biggr)

for
k\geq q+1
. The remaining basis vectors satisfy

\displaystyle dz_{k}(F_{j})

\displaystyle=-d\bar{z}_{k}(F_{j})

\displaystyle=i\,\frac{a_{k}t_{j}^{*}}{y}\biggl(\cosh\sqrt{y}-\frac{\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}\biggr)

for
k\leq q
, and

\displaystyle dz_{k}(F_{j})

\displaystyle=-d\bar{z}_{k}(F_{j})

\displaystyle=i\,\frac{t^{*}_{k}t_{j}^{*}}{y}\biggl(\cosh\sqrt{y}-\frac{\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}\biggr)+i\delta_{jk}\frac{\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}

for
k\geq q+1
, where
j=q+1,\dots,n
.

We start with the pair
(F_{j},E_{i})
for
i=1,\dots,q
and
j=q+1,\dots,n
. Using the above formulas, we compute

\displaystyle(dz_{k}\wedge d\bar{z}_{l}+dz_{l}\wedge d\bar{z}_{k})(F_{j},E_{i})

\displaystyle\qquad=dz_{k}(F_{j})\bigl(d\bar{z}_{l}(E_{i})+dz_{l}(E_{i})\bigr)+dz_{l}(F_{j})\bigl(dz_{k}(E_{i})+d\bar{z}_{k}(E_{i})\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad=2\Bigl(dz_{k}(F_{j})\,\text{Re}\bigl(dz_{l}(E_{i})\bigr)+dz_{l}(F_{j})\,\text{Re}\bigl(dz_{k}(E_{i})\bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad=2i\,\frac{t_{j}^{*}\cosh\sqrt{y}}{y}\biggl(\cosh\sqrt{y}-\frac{\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}\biggr)(a_{k}\delta_{li}+a_{l}\delta_{ki})

for
k\leq l\leq q
,

\displaystyle(dz_{k}\wedge d\bar{z}_{l}+dz_{l}\wedge d\bar{z}_{k})(F_{j},E_{i})

\displaystyle\qquad=2\Bigl(dz_{k}(F_{j})\,\text{Re}\bigl(dz_{l}(E_{i})\bigr)+dz_{l}(F_{j})\,\text{Re}\bigl(dz_{k}(E_{i})\bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad=2i\,\delta_{ki}\cosh\sqrt{y}\,\Biggl(\frac{t^{*}_{l}t_{j}^{*}}{y}\biggl(\cosh\sqrt{y}-\frac{\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}\biggr)+\delta_{jl}\frac{\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}\Biggr)

\displaystyle\qquad=2i\,\delta_{ki}\frac{t^{*}_{l}t_{j}^{*}\cosh\sqrt{y}}{y}\biggl(\cosh\sqrt{y}-\frac{\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}\biggr)+2i\,\delta_{ki}\delta_{jl}\frac{\cosh\sqrt{y}\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}

for

, and

\displaystyle(dz_{k}\wedge d\bar{z}_{l}+dz_{l}\wedge d\bar{z}_{k})(F_{j},E_{i})

\displaystyle\qquad=2\Bigl(dz_{k}(F_{j})\,\text{Re}\bigl(dz_{l}(E_{i})\bigr)+dz_{l}(F_{j})\,\text{Re}\bigl(dz_{k}(E_{i})\bigr)\Bigr)=0

for

.
Substituting these formulas into (

) yields

\displaystyle\omega_{\text{St}}(E_{i},F_{j})

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{q}a_{kk}(dz_{k}\wedge d\bar{z}_{k})(F_{j},E_{i})

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{i}{2}\sum_{1\leq k<l\leq q}a_{kl}(dz_{k}\wedge d\bar{z}_{l}+dz_{l}\wedge d\bar{z}_{k})(F_{j},E_{i})

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{i}{2}\sum_{1\leq k\leq q<l\leq n}a_{kl}(dz_{k}\wedge d\bar{z}_{l}+dz_{l}\wedge d\bar{z}_{k})(F_{j},E_{i})

\displaystyle=\frac{t_{j}^{*}\cosh\sqrt{y}}{y}\biggl(\cosh\sqrt{y}-\frac{\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}\biggr)

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{q}a_{kk}a_{k}\delta_{ki}+\sum_{1\leq k<l\leq q}a_{kl}(a_{k}\delta_{li}+a_{l}\delta_{ki})+\sum_{1\leq k\leq q<l\leq n}a_{kl}\delta_{ki}t^{*}_{l}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\cosh\sqrt{y}\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}\sum_{1\leq k\leq q<l\leq n}a_{kl}\delta_{ki}\delta_{jl}

\displaystyle=\frac{t_{j}^{*}\cosh\sqrt{y}}{y}\biggl(\cosh\sqrt{y}-\frac{\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}\biggr)\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{q}a_{ki}a_{k}+\sum_{l=q+1}^{n}a_{il}t^{*}_{l}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\cosh\sqrt{y}\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}a_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{t_{j}^{*}\cosh\sqrt{y}}{y}\biggl(\cosh\sqrt{y}-\frac{\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}\biggr)

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\Biggl[\sum_{k=1}^{q}\biggl(\biggl(\delta_{ki}a_{k}+\frac{a_{k}^{2}a_{i}}{y}\tanh^{2}\sqrt{y}\biggr)v^{\prime}+\frac{4a_{k}^{2}a_{i}}{y}\sinh^{2}\sqrt{y}\,v^{\prime\prime}\biggr)

\displaystyle\qquad+\biggl(\frac{\lvert t^{*}\rvert^{2}}{y}\tanh^{2}\sqrt{y}\,v^{\prime}+\frac{4\lvert t^{*}\rvert^{2}}{y}\sinh^{2}\sqrt{y}\,v^{\prime\prime}\biggr)\Biggr]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{a_{i}t_{j}^{*}\cosh\sqrt{y}}{y}\frac{\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}\bigl(\tanh^{2}\sqrt{y}\,v^{\prime}+4\sinh^{2}\sqrt{y}\,v^{\prime\prime}\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{a_{i}t_{j}^{*}\cosh\sqrt{y}}{y}\frac{\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}\bigl(\tanh^{2}\sqrt{y}\,v^{\prime}+4\sinh^{2}\sqrt{y}\,v^{\prime\prime}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{i}t_{j}^{*}\cosh\sqrt{y}}{y}\biggl(\cosh\sqrt{y}\,\bigl(v^{\prime}+\tanh^{2}\sqrt{y}\,v^{\prime}+4\sinh^{2}\sqrt{y}\,v^{\prime\prime}\bigr)-\frac{\sinh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}v^{\prime}\biggr)

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{i}t_{j}^{*}\cosh^{2}\sqrt{y}}{y}\biggl(\biggl(1-\frac{\tanh\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt{y}}+\tanh^{2}\sqrt{y}\biggr)v^{\prime}+4\sinh^{2}\sqrt{y}\,v^{\prime\prime}\biggr)

for

and

.
Since

is nonzero by assumption,

. Consequently,

.
In other words, we have

∎

7.  Some explicit examples in the
\text{G}_{2}

In this section, we use

on the round sphere

To do so, we examine two isometric immersions of 2-spheres into

and the Veronese immersion of the sphere

of radius

As we will show below, they are negative and positive superminimal, respectively, and in particular minimal.

In order to apply the first part of
5.3

\int^{B^{B}}
F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{loc}}}{{=}}\{f^{2},f^{3}\}
\int^{B}
over

with respect to the holomorphic structure

Unlike in the proof, we may work with an oriented local orthonormal adapted frame

which does not satisfy (

and

Since

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{j}}f^{1}

\displaystyle=(\Gamma_{j4}^{1}-\Gamma_{j3}^{2})f^{2}+(-\Gamma_{j3}^{1}-\Gamma_{j4}^{2})f^{3},

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{j}}f^{2}

\displaystyle=(\Gamma_{j3}^{2}-\Gamma_{j4}^{1})f^{1}+(\Gamma_{j2}^{1}-\Gamma_{j4}^{3})f^{3},

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{j}}f^{3}

\displaystyle=(\Gamma_{j3}^{1}+\Gamma_{j4}^{2})f^{1}+(\Gamma_{j4}^{3}-\Gamma_{j2}^{1})f^{2}

Using this, we compute

\displaystyle\nabla^{F}_{e_{1}+ie_{2}}\sigma

\displaystyle=\nabla^{F}_{e_{1}}\sigma+J_{F}(\nabla^{F}_{e_{2}}\sigma)

\displaystyle=\pi_{F}(\nabla_{e_{1}}\sigma)+J_{F}\bigl(\pi_{F}(\nabla_{e_{2}}\sigma)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\pi_{F}(a\nabla_{e_{1}}f^{2}+b\nabla_{e_{1}}f^{3}+a_{1}f^{2}+b_{1}f^{3})

\displaystyle\quad+J_{F}\bigl(\pi_{F}(a\nabla_{e_{2}}f^{2}+b\nabla_{e_{2}}f^{3}+a_{2}f^{2}+b_{2}f^{3})\bigr)

\displaystyle=a(\Gamma_{12}^{1}-\Gamma_{14}^{3})f^{3}+b(\Gamma_{14}^{3}-\Gamma_{12}^{1})f^{2}+a_{1}f^{2}+b_{1}f^{3}

\displaystyle\quad+J_{F}\bigl(a(\Gamma_{22}^{1}-\Gamma_{24}^{3})f^{3}+b(\Gamma_{24}^{3}-\Gamma_{22}^{1})f^{2}+a_{2}f^{2}+b_{2}f^{3}\bigr)

\displaystyle=(a_{1}-b_{2})f^{2}+(a_{2}+b_{1})f^{3}

\displaystyle\quad+\bigl(-a(\Gamma_{22}^{1}-\Gamma_{24}^{3})+b(\Gamma_{14}^{3}-\Gamma_{12}^{1})\bigr)f^{2}+\bigl(a(\Gamma_{12}^{1}-\Gamma_{14}^{3})+b(\Gamma_{24}^{3}-\Gamma_{22}^{1})\bigr)f^{3},

\displaystyle a_{1}-b_{2}=(\Gamma_{22}^{1}-\Gamma_{24}^{3})\,a-(\Gamma_{14}^{3}-\Gamma_{12}^{1})\,b\quad\text{ and }\quad a_{2}+b_{1}=(\Gamma_{14}^{3}-\Gamma_{12}^{1})\,a+(\Gamma_{22}^{1}-\Gamma_{24}^{3})\,b

or, equivalently,

\displaystyle G_{1}+i\,G_{2}=(\Gamma_{22}^{1}-\Gamma_{24}^{3})\,G+i\,(\Gamma_{14}^{3}-\Gamma_{12}^{1})\,G

G_{j}=dG(e_{j})

A necessary condition for this is that

is uniformly bounded because

with oriented local orthonormal adapted frames, determine how the coefficients of

We will show that, while the only globally defined holomorphic section for

The first part of

As for the coassociative case, the second part of

The Veronese immersion, on the other hand, is positive instead of negative superminimal. However, since the antipodal map

In the remainder of this section, we show that the equatorial sphere and the Veronese immersion are indeed negative and positive superminimal, respectively, and discuss the corresponding holomorphic sections in more detail.

7.1
The equatorial sphere

The equatorial embedding of
S^{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
into
S^{4}\subset\mathbb{R}^{5}
is given by

\displaystyle\iota\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}S^{2}\rightarrow S^{4},\quad(x,y,z)\mapsto(x,y,z,0,0).

Since this is naturally totally geodesic, the second fundamental form vanishes identically, which in particular implies that
S^{2}
is negative superminimal in
S^{4}
.

Using stereographic projection from
N=(1,0,0)\in S^{2}
onto
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the immersion
\iota|_{S^{2}\setminus\{N\}}
can be written as

\displaystyle\iota\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow S^{4},\quad(u_{1},u_{2})\mapsto\biggl(\frac{\lvert u\rvert^{2}-1}{\lvert u\rvert^{2}+1},\frac{2u_{1}}{\lvert u\rvert^{2}+1},\frac{2u_{2}}{\lvert u\rvert^{2}+1},0,0\biggr).

This leads to the oriented local orthonormal adapted frame

\displaystyle e_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lvert u\rvert^{2}+1}{2}\frac{\partial\iota}{\partial u_{1}}=\biggl(\frac{2u_{1}}{\lvert u\rvert^{2}+1},\frac{1-u_{1}^{2}+u_{2}^{2}}{\lvert u\rvert^{2}+1},-\frac{2u_{1}u_{2}}{\lvert u\rvert^{2}+1},0,0\biggr),

\displaystyle\nu_{3}=(0,0,0,1,0),

\displaystyle e_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lvert u\rvert^{2}+1}{2}\frac{\partial\iota}{\partial u_{2}}=\biggl(\frac{2u_{2}}{\lvert u\rvert^{2}+1},-\frac{2u_{1}u_{2}}{\lvert u\rvert^{2}+1},\frac{1+u_{1}^{2}-u_{2}^{2}}{\lvert u\rvert^{2}+1},0,0\biggr),

\displaystyle\nu_{4}=(0,0,0,0,-1),

where we omit the dependence on
u
for simplicity.

As the Levi-Civita connection on
S^{4}
is the tangential part of the directional derivative in
\mathbb{R}^{5}
, the connection coefficients with respect to the given frame can be computed via

\displaystyle\Gamma_{jk}^{l}=\frac{\lvert u\rvert^{2}+1}{2}\biggl\langle\frac{\partial e_{k}}{\partial u_{j}},e_{l}\biggr\rangle\qquad\text{ for }j,k,l=1,2,

and analogously for

\displaystyle\Gamma_{11}^{2}=-\Gamma_{12}^{1}=u_{2}\quad\text{ and }\quad-\Gamma_{21}^{2}=\Gamma_{22}^{1}=u_{1}.

Since

the condition (

\displaystyle\frac{\lvert u\rvert^{2}+1}{2}(\partial_{u_{1}}G+i\,\partial_{u_{2}}G)=(u_{1}+iu_{2})\,G\iff(z\bar{z}+1)\,\partial_{\bar{z}}G=zG\iff\partial_{\bar{z}}\frac{G}{z\bar{z}+1}=0

If

Similarly to before, we have

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1k}^{l}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\biggl\langle\frac{\partial e_{k}}{\partial\varphi},e_{l}\biggr\rangle,\qquad\Gamma_{2k}^{l}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}\sin\varphi}\biggl\langle\frac{\partial e_{k}}{\partial\theta},e_{l}\biggr\rangle,\qquad\text{ for }k,l=1,2,

and analogous formulas for
k,l=3,4
. Using
(Y_{1})^{\prime}=2Y_{3},\,(Y_{2})^{\prime}=Y_{4},\,(Y_{3})^{\prime}=-2Y_{1}
and
(Y_{4})^{\prime}=-Y_{2}
, we find that the only nonzero coefficients
\Gamma_{jk}^{l}
with
j\in\{1,2\}
are

\displaystyle\Gamma_{11}^{4}=-\Gamma_{12}^{3}=\Gamma_{13}^{2}=-\Gamma_{14}^{1}=-\Gamma_{21}^{3}=-\Gamma_{22}^{4}=\Gamma_{23}^{1}=\Gamma_{24}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}},

\displaystyle 2\Gamma_{21}^{2}=-2\Gamma_{22}^{1}=\Gamma_{23}^{4}=-\Gamma_{24}^{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\cot\varphi}{\sqrt{3}}.

Consequently, the second fundamental form is given by

\displaystyle A^{3}=\begin{pmatrix}\Gamma_{13}^{1}&\Gamma_{13}^{2}\\
\Gamma_{23}^{1}&\Gamma_{23}^{2}\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad A^{4}=\begin{pmatrix}\Gamma_{14}^{1}&\Gamma_{14}^{2}\\
\Gamma_{24}^{1}&\Gamma_{24}^{2}\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},

and satisfies
A^{4}=+J_{T}A^{3}
. In other words,
U\subset S^{2}(\sqrt{3})
is positive superminimal.

On the other hand, we can write
\iota|_{\hat{U}}
as

\displaystyle\iota(\hat{\varphi},\hat{\theta})=\sqrt{3}\sin\hat{\varphi}\cos\hat{\varphi}\,\hat{Y}_{1}(\hat{\theta})+\sin^{2}\hat{\varphi}\,\hat{Y}_{2}(\hat{\theta})+\cos^{2}\hat{\varphi}\,\biggl(-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\mathbf{e}_{4}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{e}_{5}\biggr),

where

\displaystyle\hat{Y}_{1}(\hat{\theta})

\displaystyle=\sin\hat{\theta}\,\mathbf{e}_{1}+\cos\hat{\theta}\,\mathbf{e}_{3},

\displaystyle\hat{Y}_{2}(\hat{\theta})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{3}\sin\hat{\theta}\cos\hat{\theta}\,\mathbf{e}_{2}+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\sin^{2}\hat{\theta}\,\mathbf{e}_{4}+\frac{1}{2}(1-3\cos^{2}\hat{\theta})\,\mathbf{e}_{5}.

In a similar manner as before, we find the oriented orthonormal frame

\displaystyle\hat{e}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\frac{\partial\iota}{\partial\hat{\varphi}}=\cos 2\hat{\varphi}\,\hat{Y}_{1}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\sin 2\hat{\varphi}\,\hat{Y}_{2}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\sin 2\hat{\varphi}\,\biggl(-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\mathbf{e}_{4}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{e}_{5}\biggr),

\displaystyle\hat{e}_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}\sin\hat{\varphi}}\frac{\partial\iota}{\partial\hat{\theta}}=\cos\hat{\varphi}\,\hat{Y}_{3}+\sin\hat{\varphi}\,\hat{Y}_{4},

\displaystyle\hat{\nu}_{3}

\displaystyle=\sin\hat{\varphi}\,\hat{Y}_{3}-\cos\hat{\varphi}\,\hat{Y}_{4},

\displaystyle\hat{\nu}_{4}

\displaystyle=-\sin\hat{\varphi}\cos\hat{\varphi}\,\hat{Y}_{1}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(1+\cos^{2}\hat{\varphi})\,\hat{Y}_{2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(1+\sin^{2}\hat{\varphi})\,\biggl(-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\mathbf{e}_{4}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{e}_{5}\biggr),

where

\displaystyle\hat{Y}_{3}(\hat{\theta})

\displaystyle=(\hat{Y}_{1})^{\prime}(\hat{\theta})=\cos\hat{\theta}\,\mathbf{e}_{1}-\sin\hat{\theta}\,\mathbf{e}_{3},

\displaystyle\hat{Y}_{4}(\hat{\theta})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(\hat{Y}_{2})^{\prime}(\hat{\theta})=\cos 2\hat{\theta}\,\mathbf{e}_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sin 2\hat{\theta}\,\mathbf{e}_{4}+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\sin 2\hat{\theta}\,\mathbf{e}_{5}.

As before,
\hat{Y}_{1},\dots,\hat{Y}_{4}
are orthonormal, but this time
\langle\hat{Y}_{j},-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\mathbf{e}_{4}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{e}_{5}\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}\delta_{2j}
.
Using
(\hat{Y}_{1})^{\prime}=\hat{Y}_{3}
,
(\hat{Y}_{2})^{\prime}=\sqrt{3}\hat{Y}_{4},\,(\hat{Y}_{3})^{\prime}=-\hat{Y}_{1}
and
(\hat{Y}_{4})^{\prime}=-\frac{4}{\sqrt{3}}\hat{Y}_{2}-\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}(-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\mathbf{e}_{4}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{e}_{5})
, we find that the connection coefficients
\hat{\Gamma}_{jk}^{l}
with respect to
(\hat{e}_{1},\hat{e}_{2},\hat{\nu}_{3},\hat{\nu}_{4})
are the same as
\Gamma_{jk}^{l}
for
j\in\{1,2\}
, but with
\hat{\varphi}
in place of
\varphi
.
Thus, we also have
\hat{A}^{4}=+J_{T}\hat{A}^{3}
, which proves that the Veronese immersion of
S^{2}(\sqrt{3})
is positive superminimal in
S^{4}
.

As
G_{1}=dG(e_{1})=\partial_{\varphi}G/\sqrt{3}
and
G_{2}=dG(e_{2})=\partial_{\theta}G/(\sqrt{3}\sin\varphi)
,
the condition (
7.1
) with respect to (
e_{1},e_{2},\nu_{3},\nu_{4}
) is

(7.2)

\displaystyle\sin\varphi\,\partial_{\varphi}G+i\,\partial_{\theta}G=\cos\varphi\,G.

The method of characteristics yields the solution

\displaystyle G(\varphi,\theta)=\sin\varphi\cdot H\Biggl(\theta-i\ln\biggl(\tan\frac{\varphi}{2}\biggr)\Biggr)

for any holomorphic function
H\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid\text{Re}\,z\in(0,2\pi)\}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
.
The necessary condition
\lvert\sigma\rvert^{2}=2(a^{2}+b^{2})=2\lvert G\rvert^{2}\leq M<\infty
holds, for example, when
H
is constant, in which case the section takes the form
\sigma=C\sin\varphi\,f^{2}+D\sin\varphi\,f^{3}
for
C,D\in\mathbb{R}
.

Under the transformation

(\sin{\varphi}\cos{\theta},\sin{\varphi}\sin{\theta},\cos{\varphi})=(\sin{\hat{\varphi}}\sin{\hat{\theta}},\cos{\hat{\varphi}},\sin{\hat{\varphi}}\cos{\hat{\theta}})
,
the vectors
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{4}
become

\displaystyle\sin^{2}\varphi\,Y_{1}

\displaystyle=2\sin{\hat{\varphi}}\cos{\hat{\varphi}}\sin{\hat{\theta}}\,\mathbf{e}_{1}+(\sin^{2}{\hat{\varphi}}\sin^{2}{\hat{\theta}}-\cos^{2}{\hat{\varphi}})\,\mathbf{e}_{4},

\displaystyle\sin\varphi\,Y_{2}

\displaystyle=\sin{\hat{\varphi}}\sin{\hat{\theta}}\,\mathbf{e}_{2}+\cos{\hat{\varphi}}\,\mathbf{e}_{3},

\displaystyle\sin^{2}\varphi\,Y_{3}

\displaystyle=(\sin^{2}{\hat{\varphi}}\sin^{2}{\hat{\theta}}-\cos^{2}{\hat{\varphi}})\,\mathbf{e}_{1}-2\sin{\hat{\varphi}}\cos{\hat{\varphi}}\sin{\hat{\theta}}\,\mathbf{e}_{4},

\displaystyle\sin\varphi\,Y_{4}

\displaystyle=-\cos{\hat{\varphi}}\,\mathbf{e}_{2}+\sin{\hat{\varphi}}\sin{\hat{\theta}}\,\mathbf{e}_{3},

where
\sin\varphi=\sqrt{1-\sin^{2}\hat{\varphi}\cos^{2}\hat{\theta}}
.
Substituting these expressions into

\displaystyle\sin\varphi\,e_{1}

\displaystyle=\cos\varphi\,(\sin^{2}\varphi\,Y_{1})+(\cos^{2}\varphi-\sin^{2}\varphi)(\sin\varphi\,Y_{2})+\sqrt{3}\sin^{2}\varphi\cos\varphi\,\mathbf{e}_{5}

\displaystyle=\sin\hat{\varphi}\cos\hat{\theta}\,(\sin^{2}\varphi\,Y_{1})+(2\sin^{2}\hat{\varphi}\cos^{2}\hat{\theta}-1)(\sin\varphi\,Y_{2})

\displaystyle\quad+\sqrt{3}\sin\hat{\varphi}\cos\hat{\theta}\,(1-\sin^{2}\hat{\varphi}\cos^{2}\hat{\theta})\,\mathbf{e}_{5},

\displaystyle\sin\varphi\,e_{2}

\displaystyle=(\sin^{2}\varphi\,Y_{3})+\cos\varphi\,(\sin\varphi\,Y_{4})=(\sin^{2}\varphi\,Y_{3})+\sin{\hat{\varphi}}\cos{\hat{\theta}}\,(\sin\varphi\,Y_{4}),

\displaystyle\sin^{2}\varphi\,\nu_{3}

\displaystyle=-\cos\varphi\,(\sin^{2}\varphi\,Y_{3})+\sin^{2}\varphi\,(\sin\varphi\,Y_{4})

\displaystyle=-\sin{\hat{\varphi}}\cos{\hat{\theta}}\,(\sin^{2}\varphi\,Y_{3})+(1-\sin^{2}\hat{\varphi}\cos^{2}\hat{\theta})(\sin\varphi\,Y_{4}),

\displaystyle\sin^{2}\varphi\,\nu_{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\bigl((1+\cos^{2}\varphi)(\sin^{2}\varphi\,Y_{1})-2\sin^{2}\varphi\cos\varphi\,(\sin\varphi\,Y_{2})-\sqrt{3}\sin^{4}\varphi\,\mathbf{e}_{5}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\bigl((1+\sin^{2}{\hat{\varphi}}\cos^{2}{\hat{\theta}})(\sin^{2}\varphi\,Y_{1})-2(1-\sin^{2}\hat{\varphi}\cos^{2}\hat{\theta})\sin{\hat{\varphi}}\cos{\hat{\theta}}\,(\sin\varphi\,Y_{2})

\displaystyle\qquad-\sqrt{3}(1-\sin^{2}\hat{\varphi}\cos^{2}\hat{\theta})^{2}\mathbf{e}_{5}\bigr),

and projecting the latter onto the orthonormal frame vectors
\hat{e}_{1},\hat{e}_{2},\hat{\nu}_{3},\hat{\nu}_{4}
gives

\displaystyle\sin\varphi\,e_{1}

\displaystyle=-\cos\hat{\varphi}\cos\hat{\theta}\,\hat{e}_{1}+\sin\hat{\theta}\,\hat{e}_{2},

\displaystyle\sin\varphi\,e_{2}

\displaystyle=-\sin\hat{\theta}\,\hat{e}_{1}-\cos\hat{\varphi}\cos\hat{\theta}\,\hat{e}_{2},

\displaystyle\sin^{2}\varphi\,\nu_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}(-1+3\cos 2\hat{\theta}+2\cos^{2}\hat{\theta}\cos 2\hat{\varphi})\,\hat{\nu}_{3}-\cos\hat{\varphi}\sin 2\hat{\theta}\,\hat{\nu}_{4},

\displaystyle\sin^{2}\varphi\,\nu_{4}

\displaystyle=\cos\hat{\varphi}\sin 2\hat{\theta}\,\hat{\nu}_{3}+\frac{1}{4}(-1+3\cos 2\hat{\theta}+2\cos^{2}\hat{\theta}\cos 2\hat{\varphi})\,\hat{\nu}_{4}.

Consequently,
f^{2}
and

transform as

\displaystyle\sin^{3}\varphi\,f^{2}

\displaystyle=(\sin\varphi\,e^{1})\wedge(\sin^{2}\varphi\,\nu^{3})-(\sin^{2}\varphi\,\nu^{4})\wedge(\sin\varphi\,e^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\cos\hat{\varphi}\cos\hat{\theta}\,(1-3\cos 2\hat{\theta}-2\cos^{2}\hat{\theta}\cos 2\hat{\varphi}-8\sin^{2}\hat{\theta})\,\hat{f}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{4}\sin\hat{\theta}\,(8\cos^{2}\hat{\varphi}\cos^{2}\hat{\theta}+1-3\cos 2\hat{\theta}-2\cos^{2}\hat{\theta}\cos 2\hat{\varphi})\,\hat{f}^{3}

\displaystyle=\sin^{2}\varphi\,(-\cos\hat{\varphi}\cos\hat{\theta}\,\hat{f}^{2}+\sin\hat{\theta}\,\hat{f}^{3}),

\displaystyle\sin^{3}\varphi\,f^{3}

\displaystyle=(\sin\varphi\,e^{1})\wedge(\sin^{2}\varphi\,\nu^{4})-(\sin\varphi\,e^{2})\wedge(\sin^{2}\varphi\,\nu^{3})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\sin\hat{\theta}\,(-8\cos^{2}\hat{\varphi}\cos^{2}\hat{\theta}-1+3\cos 2\hat{\theta}+2\cos^{2}\hat{\theta}\cos 2\hat{\varphi})\,\hat{f}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sin^{2}\varphi\,(-\sin\hat{\theta}\,\hat{f}^{2}-\cos\hat{\varphi}\cos\hat{\theta}\,\hat{f}^{3}),

where
\sin\varphi=\sqrt{1-\sin^{2}\hat{\varphi}\cos^{2}\hat{\theta}}
.

Applying this to the section

\displaystyle\sigma=\hat{a}\hat{f}^{2}+\hat{b}\hat{f}^{3}=(-C\cos\hat{\varphi}\cos\hat{\theta}-D\sin\hat{\theta})\hat{f}^{2}+(C\sin\hat{\theta}-D\cos\hat{\varphi}\cos\hat{\theta})\hat{f}^{3},

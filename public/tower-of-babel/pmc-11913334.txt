Chromoblastomycosis is curable with DAT therapy (debulking, intralesional amphotericin B, oral terbinafine); case series of 16 patients
Authors: Ranthilaka R. Ranawaka, Viharatennegedara Abeywickrama
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Once incurable and chronic devastating diseases of chromomycosis is now curable with, debulking, intralesional amphotericin B and oral terbinafine (DAT). Debulking methods ranged from electrocautery to total surgical excision according to the size and the site of the lesion; a diluted solution of 1 mg/mL of amphotericin B (AMB) was injected weekly at the edge of the lesion; and simultaneous treatment with daily 500 mg oral terbinafine. Voriconazole 200 mg twice daily was added in one patient who had infection spread along the right lower limb for more than 20 years. DAT therapy was continued until complete clinical clearance where 14 out of 16 (87.5%) were cured using intralesional AMB 4–8 weeks (mean 5.8, mode 7) and oral terbinafine 6–12 weeks (mean 9.6, mode 12). Two patients who had lesions for 10 years and 20 years had to continue treatments for 14 weeks and 34 weeks, respectively, leaving scarring, chronic lymphedema, or depigmentation to a lesser degree. Early initiation of treatment gives an optimal outcome in a shorter period of time without residual sequelae.

Introduction

Chromoblastomycosis is a chronic occupational disease caused by dematiaceous fungi (usually Fonsecaea pedrosoi, Phialophora verrucosa, Cladosporium carrionii, or Fonsecaea compacta). It is caused by traumatic inoculation and is commonly found in farmers, manual labourers, and barefoot walkers. Its recalcitrant and polymorphic nature makes its diagnosis and management difficult. In this case series, we report 16 patients whom were successfully cured in two centers in Sri Lanka during 1-year period from 2022 to 2023.

Methods

These 16 patients, 14 men and 2 women, were diagnosed clinically and were confirmed histopathologically many years back and were on the various treatment modalities for 6 months–20-year duration distressing both patients and the physicians. They were on itraconazole, terbinafine, liquid nitrogen cryotherapy, debulking surgery, either monotherapy or combination of above [Table 1].

The initial management of our patients included surgical debulking of the lesion. Debulking methods ranged from electrocautery, curettage, and total surgical excision of the lesion, or combination of above and was decided according to the size and the site of the lesion. In some of the patients’ cautery and curettage was done in several occasions. Intralesional amphotericin B (AMB, Gufic Stridden Biopharma Pvt Ltd, India) was administered on the same day and was infiltrated every 3rd day for the 1st week and then continued weekly. Intralesional AMB solution was prepared by dissolving 50 mg of AMB in 40 mL of sterilized water. Ten milliliters of 2% lidocaine solution was added making 1 mg/mL of AMB. In large lesions, intralesional AMB was injected at the edge of the wound using 0.25–0.5 mL of AMB solution per cm2 of the lesion. Simultaneously, oral terbinafine 500 mg daily was added.

Treatment duration varied from 6 weeks to 8 months according to the clinical response. The absence of active lesions was the hallmark to discontinue intralesional AMB while oral terbinafine was continued for few more weeks to ensure complete eradication of the subcutaneous fungi. In one patient with extensive lesion extending along the right leg from upper thigh to the ankle was prescribed 12 weeks of voriconazole 200 mg twice daily in addition to 25 weeks of oral terbinafine [Figures 1 and 2]. Liver transaminases and full blood count were monitored monthly.

Results

All 16 patients achieved complete cure without any side effects to drugs. Fourteen patients (87.5%) were given intralesional AMB for 4–8 (mean 5.8, mode 7) weeks and oral terbinafine for 6–12 (mean 9.6, mode 12) weeks [Table 1]. Two patients who had lesions for more than 10 years and 20 years had to continue treatments for 14 weeks and 34 weeks respectively. However, the patients who had sequelae such as scarring, chronic lymphedema, or depigmentation before initiation of DAT therapy persisted to a lesser degree even after complete cure while the patients who were started DAT therapy early were cured without any residual effects.

Discussion

Chromoblastomycosis is frequently found in tropics such as India, Sri Lanka, Central and South America, Cuba, and Jamaica. Lack of protective garments, barefoot walking, and poor hygiene are the identified risk factors. It is notoriously difficult to cure distressing both patients and physicians. Several reports have shown a good response of chromoblastomycosis to itraconazole or terbinafine as a single agent, but a long period of treatment 18–30 months was needed. Weekly intralesional AMB for 8 weeks as monotherapy had been tried in 5 mg/mL and 2.5 mg/mL concentrations showing 78% and 56% complete response respectively.[1] Physical interventions such as surgical excision and cryotherapy were curative on smaller lesions. A combination of intralesional AMB and oral terbinafine 500 mg daily was proven curative against melanized fungi in vitro and in vivo showing shorter duration of therapy without side effects.[234] Ranawaka RR in 2020 used this combination on a 75-year-old farmer who had the lesion fully covering the right foot, and surgical debulking added to the treatment regimen in view to enhance the efficacy systemic therapy.[5]

In this case series, 1 mg/mL of AMB diluted dose was used once weekly to reduce the anticipated side effects such as local necrosis. However, higher concentrations of AMB may be used safely as proven by Goswami et al.[1] and may show faster response. Electrocautery was the preferred debulking method as it was easy to perform in an outpatient clinic, cheaper, did not need prior patient preparations or postoperative care, and successfully removed even bigger nodules and plaques. Nevertheless, electrocautery was avoided in vulnerable sites such as over the superficial nerves and vessels, while the larger and ulcerated lesions were excised surgically. Patients’ response to therapy was assessed clinically and total clearance of the lesion was the guide to stop intralesional AMB. However, oral terbinafine was continued 2–4 weeks more depending on the severity of the lesion to ensure clearing of undetectable subcutaneous fungi. Oral terbinafine was proven to be a safe drug in higher dose (500 mg daily) and prolong therapy, 3–4 months.

In conclusion, this case series prove successful cure of chromoblastomycosis with DAT therapy, i.e., debulking, intralesional amphotericin B, and oral terbinafine. Early initiation of treatment gives an optimal outcome in a shorter period of time without residual effects.

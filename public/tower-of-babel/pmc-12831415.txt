Solitary giant abscess extending from the left gluteal to thigh due to Nocardia cyriacigeorgica in an immunocompetent patient: a case report
Authors: Wei Li, Zhouyang Wang, Yang Li, Feng Zhang, Quankui Zhuang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundNocardia cyriacigeorgica, a rare aerobic actinomycete, primarily infects immunocompromised hosts but is increasingly reported in immunocompetent individuals. Pulmonary involvement is its predominant manifestation; solitary giant abscesses are exceptionally rare in all patients.Case presentationA 92-year-old immunocompetent woman with coronary artery disease presented with acute left gluteofemoral swelling, pain, and fever (39.5 °C) persisting for 10 days. Symptoms progressed despite empirical antibiotics. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) revealed a multiloculated abscess spanning the left gluteal, hip, and proximal thigh compartments. Routine pus cultures were negative. Metagenomic next-generation sequencing (mNGS) of drainage fluid identified Nocardia cyriacigeorgica at 48 h, guiding a targeted culture that confirmed the diagnosis within a further 72 h. Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing demonstrated susceptibility to trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX). Ultrasound-guided drainage and TMP-SMX therapy achieved complete clinical and radiological resolution within 6 weeks.ConclusionThis is the first reported case of an immunocompetent patient with a solitary giant Nocardia cyriacigeorgica abscess in the gluteofemoral region. mNGS facilitated rapid pathogen identification, guiding targeted culture and susceptibility testing. This approach establishes a diagnostic-therapeutic paradigm for early recognition and precision management of similar infections.Clinical trialNot applicable.Keywords: Nocardia cyriacigeorgica, Immunocompetent patient, Abscess, Geriatrics

Introduction

Nocardia species are Gram-positive aerobic actinomycetes typically causing opportunistic infections in immunocompromised hosts [1]. First isolated in 2001, Nocardia cyriacigeorgica is increasingly recognized as a human pathogen associated with pulmonary and disseminated disease [2]. Solitary giant abscesses in immunocompetent hosts remain scarcely documented, with only one fatal case describing multiregional soft tissue involvement [3]. We present a rare case of a solitary giant Nocardia cyriacigeorgica abscess spanning the gluteofemoral region in an immunocompetent patient, highlighting diagnostic challenges of this atypical presentation.

Case presentation

A 92-year-old woman with coronary artery disease, who was independent in all activities of daily living, presented with 10 days of left lower limb swelling, pain, and fever (39.5 °C). Empirical antibiotics at multiple institutions failed, and symptoms progressed. Initial treatment with cefoxitin(2 g every 12 h) in general surgery yielded no improvement, prompting transfer to orthopedics.

On admission, vital signs were stable except for fever. Physical examination revealed significant swelling, erythema, and tenderness over the left gluteal and proximal thigh region. Laboratory investigations revealed: White blood cell (WBC) count 12.74 × 10⁹/L (normal range: 3.5–9.5 × 10⁹/L) with neutrophilia (95.6%), C-reactive protein (CRP) 176.7 mg/L (normal: <5 mg/L), Serum amyloid protein A (SAA) 215.3 mg/L (normal: <10 mg/L), Erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) 58 mm/hr (normal: 0–20 mm/hr), and Procalcitonin (PCT) 0.057 ng/mL (normal: <0.05 ng/mL). the left hip and thigh MRI demonstrated a large, multiloculated abscess with septations, spanning the left gluteal muscles, hip region, and extending into the proximal thigh compartments (Fig. 1A-D).

Diagnostic procedures

An initial diagnostic aspiration was performed on day 2 after transfer to the orthopedics department, which yielded purulent material; however, routine bacterial culture remained negative after 72 h. Subsequently, on the same day, ultrasound-guided drainage with catheter placement was performed(Fig. 1E).

mNGS: Metagenomic DNA was extracted from the purulent drainage fluid and subjected to the PACEseq mNGS technology (Hugo Biotech, China) for comprehensive pathogen detection. Sequencing was performed with a SE75 (Single-End 75 base pairs) read length. Bioinformatic analysis identified Nocardia cyriacigeorgica as the dominant pathogen with high confidence, supported by 20 specific sequencing reads and a relative abundance of 91.56%. No other significant pathogenic bacteria, fungi, DNA viruses, or parasites were detected. The data quality control for the sequencing run was qualified.Although the absolute number of sequencing reads for N. cyriacigeorgica was relatively low, its overwhelmingly high relative abundance (91.56%) in the absence of any other significant pathogens strongly supports its role as the true causative agent rather than background contamination or colonization.

Microbiological Culture and Identification: Based on the mNGS result, the drainage fluid was specifically cultured for aerobic actinomycetes on blood agar at 37 °C in a CO2-enriched environment. Characteristic chalk-white, wrinkled colonies were observed after 72 h (Fig. 1F). The isolate was confirmed as N. cyriacigeorgica using VITEK® MS MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry.Gram stain revealed branching Gram-positive filaments (Fig. 1G), and weak acid-fast staining confirmed partial acid-fast properties (Fig. 1H).

Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing (AST): AST was performed using the broth microdilution method according to the Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute (CLSI) guidelines M24-A2. The minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) values were interpreted using CLSI breakpoints for Nocardia species. The results are summarized in Table 1.

Treatment

Empirical piperacillin-tazobactam (4.5 g every 8 h) was initiated upon transfer. After pathogen confirmation, therapy was transitioned to oral TMP-SMX (TMP 160 mg/SMX 800 mg, one tablet every 12 h) based on susceptibility for 6 weeks.

Results

Significant symptomatic improvement followed drainage and targeted antibiotic therapy. At one-week follow-up, laboratory parameters showed marked improvement: White blood cell count normalized to 6.54 × 10⁹/L, C-reactive protein decreased to 28 mg/L, serum amyloid protein A to 12 mg/L, and erythrocyte sedimentation rate to 33 mm/h. By one month, all inflammatory parameters had normalized, and repeat ultrasound confirmed complete abscess resolution. A detailed timeline of key clinical events is provided in Table 2.

Discussion

To our knowledge, this represents the first reported case of a solitary giant gluteofemoral abscess caused by Nocardia cyriacigeorgica in a patient without any classic immunosuppressive conditions. The classification of our patient as immunocompetent is based on the standard clinical definition, which denotes the absence of recognized conditions predisposing to immunodeficiency, such as HIV/AIDS, hematological malignancy, solid organ transplantation, chronic immunosuppressive therapy, or congenital immune disorders [4–5]. While advanced age is associated with immunosenescence, it is distinct from a state of clinical immunosuppression. Our patient had no such classic risk factors, and her robust systemic inflammatory response further demonstrates a functional immune system.

The uniqueness of our case is underscored by a systematic review of the literature (summarized in Table 3) [3, 6–14]. Previous reports of N. cyriacigeorgica soft tissue infections are predominantly documented in immunocompromised hosts or, in the rare immunocompetent cases, typically involve superficial, injection-associated, or disseminated lesions. In contrast, our patient presented with a solitary, massive abscess spanning multiple anatomical compartments without any history of trauma or dissemination, a presentation that remains undocumented.

The diagnostic approach in this case highlights an evolving paradigm. Conventional cultures frequently yield false negatives due to the fastidious growth of Nocardia [15]. Here, metagenomic next-generation sequencing (mNGS) enabled rapid pathogen identification within 48 h, which directly guided targeted culture and appropriate susceptibility testing. This diagnostic sequence—mNGS leading to confirmatory culture and AST—proved critical for the precision management of this culture-negative infection.

Current evidence indicates a significant mortality risk in nocardiosis, with reported fatality rates up to 50% [16]. A fatal 2010 case involved multiregional abscesses despite aggressive therapy [3]. In contrast, our case achieved clinical cure through ultrasound-guided drainage combined with targeted antibiotic therapy.

Conclusion

This first-reported solitary giant Nocardia cyriacigeorgica abscess in an immunocompetent patient highlights the role of mNGS in overcoming diagnostic hurdles. The established pathway—rapid molecular identification guiding targeted culture and susceptibility testing—provides a paradigm for managing similar rare infections.

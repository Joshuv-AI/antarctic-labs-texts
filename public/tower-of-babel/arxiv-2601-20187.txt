Weil conjectures and affine hypersurfaces
Dingxin Zhang

Abstract
. We give yet another proof of the Riemann hypothesis for smooth projective varieties over a finite field, by reducing to the case of a hypersurface [ Kat15 ] via deformation. The main tool is Artin’s vanishing theorem together with a few basic facts about perverse sheaves.

Primary: 11G25; Secondary: 14G15

1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper, we work over a finite base field

\mathbf{F}_{q}
. Our notation is as follows: capital Latin letters
with a subscript
0
, such as
X_{0},S_{0}
, denote separated schemes
of finite type defined over
\mathbf{F}_{q}
. Removing the subscript
(e.g.,
X,S
) indicates the base change of these schemes to a fixed
algebraic closure
\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{q}
.

We fix a prime number
\ell
which is invertible in

\mathbf{F}_{q}
, and throughout, we consider only

\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell}
-sheaves. Script letters with
subscript
0
, like
\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathcal{G}_{0}
, refer to
sheaves on
X_{0},S_{0}
. Omitting the subscript denotes their pullback
to the corresponding scheme over
\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{q}
.

Let
X_{0}
be a separated scheme of finite type over
\mathbf{F}_{q}
.
We define the zeta function of
X_{0}
as

Z(X_{0}/\mathbf{F}_{q},t)=\exp\left\{\sum_{e\geqslant 1}\#X_{0}(\mathbf{F}_{q^{e}})\frac{t^{e}}{e}\right\},

which is an element of
1+t\mathbf{Z}[\![t]\!]
.
Grothendieck’s trace formula expresses the zeta function in terms of the action of the geometric Frobenius on the
\ell
-adic cohomology of
X
:

Z(X_{0}/\mathbf{F}_{q},t)=\prod_{i=0}^{2\dim X_{0}}\det\left(1-tF,\mathrm{H}^{i}_{c}(X;\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})\right)^{(-1)^{i+1}}.

Recall that
F
, the
geometric
Frobenius, is the inverse of
the arithmetic Frobenius automorphism
a\mapsto a^{q}
in

\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{q}/\mathbf{F}_{q})
, acting on the

\ell
-adic cohomology groups by transport of structure.

Our purpose is to provide an alternative proof of the following
celebrated theorem of Deligne, known as the
Riemann Hypothesis

for smooth proper varieties defined over a finite field:

Theorem 1.1

(Deligne
[
Del74
]
)
.

Assume
X_{0}
is smooth and proper over
\mathbf{F}_{q}
.
Suppose
\alpha
is an eigenvalue of the geometric Frobenius
F

acting on

\mathrm{H}^{i}_{c}(X;\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})=\mathrm{H}^{i}(X;\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})
.
Then for any isomorphism

\iota\colon\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell}\to\mathbf{C}
, we have

|\iota(\alpha)|=q^{\frac{i}{2}}
.

In terms of the definition below, for a smooth proper variety

X_{0}
over
\mathbf{F}_{q}
, the Riemann Hypothesis asserts that
every eigenvalue of the geometric Frobenius acting on

\mathrm{H}^{i}(X;\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})
has
q
-weight
exactly equal to
i
.

Definition 1.2

(Weight)
.

Let
\iota\colon\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell}\to\mathbf{C}
be an
isomorphism of fields. Let
q>0
be a real number, and let

\alpha\in\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell}
. We say that
\alpha

has
q
-
weight
(or simply
weight
when the number
q

is understood from the context)
\leqslant w
(with respect to
the isomorphism
\iota
), if

|\iota(\alpha)|\leqslant q^{w/2}.

We say
\alpha
has
q
-weight equal to
w
, if

|\iota(\alpha)|=q^{w/2}

Katz
[
Kat15
]

provides an (arguably) elementary and accessible proof of the Riemann
Hypothesis for smooth projective hypersurfaces in a projective space.
We shall reduce Theorem
1.1

to this case.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
X_{0}\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{n}
be a smooth hypersurface. Then
the Riemann Hypothesis holds for
X_{0}
.

Katz’s proof of Theorem
1.3
is in effect a
specialization argument. Building on Deligne’s interpretation of
Rankin’s method, Katz showed that to establish the Riemann Hypothesis
for
all
smooth hypersurfaces of degree
d
in

\mathbf{P}^{n}
, it is enough to find just
one
smooth
hypersurface of degree
d
in
\mathbf{P}^{n}
for which the Riemann
Hypothesis holds. Depending on whether the degree
d
is coprime to
the characteristic of the base field, we can construct the following
examples:

•

If
d
is coprime to the characteristic of
\mathbf{F}_{q}
,
Weil
[
Wei49
]
proved, using only basic
properties of Gauss sums, that the so-called
diagonal hypersurfaces

a_{0}T_{0}^{d}+\cdots+a_{n}T_{n}^{d}=0,\qquad(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathbf{F}_{q}^{\ast})

satisfy the Riemann Hypothesis.

•

If
d
is divisible by the characteristic, Katz then used
properties of Gauss sums to verify the Riemann Hypothesis for the
so-called
Gabber hypersurface
:

T_{0}^{d}+T_{0}T_{1}^{d-1}+\cdots+T_{n-1}T_{n}^{d-1}=0.

In this paper, we will use a degeneration argument to reduce the
general case of the Riemann Hypothesis for smooth proper varieties
to the hypersurface case covered by Theorem
1.3
.
Earlier, Scholl
[
Sch11
]

developed a reduction method relying on alterations and the weight
spectral sequence of Steenbrink–Rapoport–Zink. In contrast, the
engine of our approach is Artin’s vanishing
theorem
[
SGA“,4
, Exposé XIV, 3.1]
and an elementary “perverse
degeneration lemma” (
[
Kat03
, Proposition 9]
,

[
WZ25a
, Lemma 3.1]
).
Since Artin’s vanishing theorem is closely related to the perverse
t-structure, we will also use some properties of perverse sheaves in a
superficial way.

In fact, we will prove a slightly more general assertion, which is a
direct consequence of the main theorem of Weil
II
[
Del80
]
:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
X_{0}
be a separated scheme of finite type over

\mathbf{F}_{q}
. Then for every integer
i
, all eigenvalues of Frobenius acting on

\mathrm{H}^{i}_{c}(X;\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})
have

q
-weight
\leqslant i
.

Proof that Theorem
1.4

\Rightarrow
Theorem
1.1
.

Let
0\leqslant i\leqslant 2d
. Then by Theorem
1.4
, any
Frobenius eigenvalue
\alpha
of

\mathrm{H}^{i}(X;\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})
satisfies the
inequality

|\iota(\alpha)|\leqslant q^{\frac{i}{2}}.

On the other hand, Poincaré
duality gives an isomorphism

\mathrm{H}^{i}(X;\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})\simeq\mathrm{H}^{2d-i}(X;\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})^{\vee}\otimes\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell}(-d).

Therefore,
q^{d}/\alpha
is also a Frobenius eigenvalue of
\mathrm{H}^{2d-i}(X;\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})
.
Hence

\left|\iota\left(\frac{q^{d}}{\alpha}\right)\right|\leqslant q^{d-\frac{i}{2}},

or equivalently
|\iota(\alpha)|\geqslant q^{\frac{i}{2}}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

The paper is organized as follows. In
Section
2
, we give a detailed review of the
perverse t-structure on a curve and present the “perverse
degeneration lemma”, which is the main technical tool to our proof.
Section
3
summarizes key properties of perverse sheaves
on general varieties that will be used in our approach. In
Section
4
, we discuss Deligne’s sheaf-theoretic
perspective on trivial bounds of character sums, and explain how these
lead to the lower semicontinuity of weights. While the results of
Section
4
are already clearly stated in Weil
II
[
Del80
]
, we include complete proofs here both for
clarity and to emphasize their fundamental role in the proof.
Finally, in Section
5
, we assemble these ingredients to
prove Theorem
1.4
.

2.
The perverse degeneration lemma

The goal of this section is to explain the proof of the perverse
degeneration lemma
2.6
. In order
to set up notation and terminology, we first recall the perverse
t-structure on a curve. Throught the rest of the paper, for a
separated scheme
X
of finite type over an algebriacally closed
field or a finite field
k
, we denote by

\mathrm{D}^{b}_{c}(X)
the bounded derived
category of constructible complexes of

\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell}
-sheaves on
X
.

Setup 2.1
.

Let
B
be a smooth connected curve over an algebraically closed
field
k
. Let
\Sigma
be a finite closed subscheme of
B

with complement
U
. Denote by
j\colon U\hookrightarrow B
and

\iota\colon\Sigma\hookrightarrow B
the open and closed
immersions, respectively. We call the pair
(U,\Sigma)
a

partition
of
B
.

Definition 2.2
.

We say
\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{D}^{b}_{c}(B)
is

constructible
with respect to a partition
(U,\Sigma)
of

B
, if
\mathcal{H}^{e}(j^{\ast}\mathcal{F})
is a local system
for all
e\in\mathbf{Z}
. The full subcategory of

\mathrm{D}^{b}_{c}(B)
consisting of all sheaves constructible with
respect to
(U,\Sigma)
is denoted by

\mathrm{D}^{b}_{c}(U,\Sigma)
. Note that

\mathrm{D}^{b}_{c}(B)=\bigcup_{(U,\Sigma)}\mathrm{D}^{b}_{c}(U,\Sigma),

where the union runs over all possible partitions
(U,\Sigma)
with

U
a nonempty open subset of
B
and
\Sigma=B\mathbin{\rule[1.99997pt]{6.69998pt}{1.19995pt}}U
.

Definition 2.3
.

Suppose
\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{D}^{b}_{c}(U,\Sigma)
. We say

\mathcal{F}
is
perverse connective
if the following
conditions hold:

(1)

\iota^{\ast}\mathcal{F}
is connective,
that is, for any
s\in\Sigma
, and any
e\geqslant 1
, we have

\mathrm{H}^{e}(\mathcal{F}_{s})=0
, and

(2)

j^{\ast}\mathcal{F}[-1]
is connective, that is,

\mathcal{H}^{e}(j^{\ast}\mathcal{F})=0
for any
e\geqslant 0
.

The full subcategory of
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{c}(U,\Sigma)
of all perverse
connective sheaves is denoted by

{}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{D}_{c}^{\leqslant 0}(U,\Sigma)
.

We say
\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{D}^{b}_{c}(B)
is
perverse
connective
, if there exits a partition
(U,\Sigma)
of

B
, such that
\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{D}^{b}_{c}(U,\Sigma)
. The
full subcategory of
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{c}(B)
consisting of all perverse
connective sheaves on
B
is denoted by

{}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{D}^{\leqslant 0}_{c}(B)
.

Definition 2.4
.

Suppose
\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{D}^{b}_{c}(U,\Sigma)
.
We say
\mathcal{F}
is
perverse coconnective
,
if the following conditions hold:

(1)

\mathrm{R}\iota^{!}\mathcal{F}
is coconnective,
that is, for any
e\leqslant-1
, we have

\mathcal{H}^{e}(\mathrm{R}\iota^{!}\mathcal{F})=0
, and

(2)

j^{\ast}\mathcal{F}[-1]
is coconnective, that is,

\mathcal{H}^{e}(j^{\ast}\mathcal{F})=0
for any
e\leqslant-2
.

The full subcategory of
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{c}(U,\Sigma)
of all
perverse coconnective sheaves is denoted by

{}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{D}_{c}^{\geqslant 0}(U,\Sigma)
.

We say
\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{D}^{b}_{c}(B)
is
perverse
coconnective
if there exists a partition
(U,\Sigma)
of
B
,
such that

\mathcal{F}\in{}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{D}_{c}^{\geqslant 0}(U,\Sigma)
.
The full subcategory of
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{c}(B)
consisting of all
perverse coconnective sheaves is denoted by

{}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{D}_{c}^{\geqslant 0}(B)
.

Remark 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{D}^{b}_{c}(U,\Sigma)
, and let

U^{\prime}\subseteq U
be an open subset. Define the corresponding
closed subset
\Sigma^{\prime}=B\mathbin{\rule[1.99997pt]{6.69998pt}{1.19995pt}}U^{\prime}
, so that

(U^{\prime},\Sigma^{\prime})
gives a refinement of the partition

(U,\Sigma)
. It is straightforward to verify that

{}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{D}^{\leqslant 0}_{c}(U,\Sigma)\subseteq{}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{D}^{\leqslant 0}_{c}(U^{\prime},\Sigma^{\prime})\qquad\text{and}\qquad{}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{D}^{\geqslant 0}_{c}(U,\Sigma)\subseteq{}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{D}^{\geqslant 0}_{c}(U^{\prime},\Sigma^{\prime}).

Therefore, if
\mathcal{F}
is perverse connective (respectively,
coconnective) for some partition
(U,\Sigma)
, it remains perverse
connective (respectively, coconnective) for any finer partition.

Now we can state and prove the perverse degeneration lemma.

Lemma 2.6

(cf.
[
Kat03
, Proposition 9]
,
[
WZ25a
, Lemma 3.1]
)
.

In Situation
2.1
, let

\mathcal{F}\in{}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{D}^{\geqslant 0}_{c}(U,\Sigma)
be perverse coconnective with
respect to
(U,\Sigma)
. Then for any
s\in\Sigma
, there is a
natural injective map

\mathrm{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{F}_{s})\hookrightarrow\bigl(\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{\ast}\mathcal{H}^{-1}(j^{\ast}\mathcal{F})\bigr)_{s}.

In particular, the dimension of
\mathrm{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{F}_{s})
does not exceed the rank of the local system
\mathcal{H}^{-1}(j^{\ast}\mathcal{F})
on
U
.

Proof.

Fix
s\in\Sigma
.
Without loss of generality, we may reduce to the case
\Sigma=\{s\}
.
Consider the distinguished triangle

\iota_{\ast}\mathrm{R}\iota^{!}\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}\to\mathrm{R}j_{\ast}j^{\ast}\mathcal{F}\to\iota_{\ast}\mathrm{R}\iota^{!}\mathcal{F}[1],

Applying the exact functor
\iota^{\ast}
, we
obtain an exact triangle in
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{c}(\{s\})

\mathrm{R}\iota^{!}\mathcal{F}\to\iota^{\ast}\mathcal{F}\to\iota^{\ast}\mathrm{R}j_{\ast}j^{\ast}\mathcal{F}\to\mathrm{R}\iota^{!}\mathcal{F}[1].

Passing to cohomology, this induces a long exact sequence:

\cdots\to\mathrm{H}^{-1}(\mathrm{R}\iota^{!}\mathcal{F})\to\mathrm{H}^{-1}(\iota^{\ast}\mathcal{F})\to\mathrm{H}^{-1}(\iota^{\ast}\mathrm{R}j_{\ast}j^{\ast}\mathcal{F})\to\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathrm{R}\iota^{!}\mathcal{F})\to\cdots

Thanks to Property (
2.4
.
1
), we have

\mathrm{H}^{e}(\mathrm{R}\iota^{!}\mathcal{F})=0
for all
e\leqslant-1
.
In particular,
\mathrm{H}^{-1}(\mathrm{R}\iota^{!}\mathcal{F})=0
. Thus,
from the long exact sequence above, we obtain an injective map

(2.6.1)

\mathrm{H}^{-1}(\iota^{\ast}\mathcal{F})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{-1}(\iota^{\ast}\mathrm{R}j_{\ast}j^{\ast}\mathcal{F}).

Since
\iota^{\ast}
is exact,

\mathrm{H}^{-1}(\iota^{\ast}\mathrm{R}j_{\ast}j^{\ast}\mathcal{F})
coincides with the
stalk at
s
of the sheaf
\mathrm{R}^{-1}j_{\ast}j^{\ast}\mathcal{F}
.

We now analyze
\mathrm{R}^{-1}j_{\ast}j^{\ast}\mathcal{F}
via the spectral sequence

E_{2}^{a,b}=\mathrm{R}^{a}j_{\ast}\,\mathcal{H}^{b}(j^{\ast}\mathcal{F})\implies\mathrm{R}^{a+b}j_{\ast}j^{\ast}\mathcal{F}.

Thanks to Property (
2.4
.
2
), we have

\mathcal{H}^{b}(j^{*}\mathcal{F})=0
when
b\leqslant-2
.
On the other hand, for any local system
\mathcal{E}
on
U
,
one has
\mathrm{R}^{a}j_{*}\mathcal{E}=0
unless
a\geqslant 0
.
Since
\mathcal{H}^{b}j^{\ast}\mathcal{F}
is a local system for any
b
,
we see that
E_{2}^{a,b}=0
whenever
a+b\leqslant-2
,
and the only nontrivial contribution to
\mathrm{R}^{-1}j_{*}j^{*}\mathcal{F}

arises from
a=0
,
b=-1
. That is,

\mathrm{R}^{-1}j_{\ast}j^{\ast}\mathcal{F}=\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{\ast}\mathcal{H}^{-1}(j^{\ast}\mathcal{F}).

Therefore,

(2.6.2)

\mathrm{H}^{-1}\bigl(\iota^{\ast}\mathrm{R}j_{\ast}j^{\ast}\mathcal{F}\bigr)=\left(\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{\ast}\mathcal{H}^{-1}(j^{\ast}\mathcal{F})\right)_{s}.

The proof is complete by combining the injective map (
2.6.1
) with (
2.6.2
).
∎

3.
A few facts about the perverse t-structure

Let
X
be a variety (i.e., separated scheme of finite type) over an
algebraically closed field
k
. Then the constructible derived
category
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{c}(X)
admits a
perverse
t-structure

({}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{D}^{\leqslant 0}_{c}(X),{}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{D}_{c}^{\geqslant 0}(X)),

see
[
BBDG
, §§1.3, 1.4, and 2.2.10]
or
[
KW01
, Lemma-Definition III 1.1]
.

Definition 3.1
.

An object
\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{D}^{b}_{c}(X)
is in
{}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{D}^{\leqslant 0}_{c}(X)
if and only if

\dim\operatorname{Supp}\mathcal{H}^{i}(\mathcal{F})\leqslant-i

for all
i\in\mathbf{Z}
. Let

\mathbf{D}_{X}\colon\mathrm{D}^{b}_{c}(X)^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{D}^{b}_{c}(X)

be the duality functor

\mathbf{D}_{X}(\mathcal{F})=R\mathcal{H}\mathit{om}(\mathcal{F},a^{!}\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})

where
a\colon X\to\operatorname{Spec}k
is the structural morphism.
Then we define

{}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{D}^{\geqslant 0}_{c}(X)=\mathbf{D}_{X}({}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{D}^{\leqslant 0}_{c}(X)).

Objects in
{}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{D}^{\leqslant 0}_{c}(X)

(resp.
{}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{D}^{\geqslant 0}_{c}(X)
) are called

perverse connective
(resp.
perverse coconnective
). Objects in the heart

{}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{D}^{\leqslant 0}_{c}(X)\cap{}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{D}_{c}^{\geqslant 0}(X)

of this t-structure are called
perverse sheaves
.

It is straightforward to verify that, for the case where
X
is a smooth curve, the above definition of the perverse t-structure recovers the same notion as in Section
3
.

Below, we summarize several facts about the perverse t-structure that
will be used later. To keep the exposition clear, we will not include
detailed proofs here, but will provide references for each result.
The proofs are conceptually straightforward, with the exception of the
Artin’s vanishing theorem
(Theorem
3.4
), whose proof is
substantially more subtle.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
X
be a variety of dimension
\leqslant n
. Then for any plain sheaf

\mathcal{F}
on
X
,
\mathcal{F}[n]
is perverse connective. In
particular, if
X
is smooth of pure dimension
n
, and

\mathcal{F}
is a local system, then
\mathcal{F}[n]
is a
perverse sheaf.

Proof.

The first assertion follows straightforwardly from the definition of a
perverse connective sheaf. For the second assertion, we use the fact
that for a local system
\mathcal{F}
on a smooth variety, we have

\mathbf{D}_{X}(\mathcal{F}[n])=\mathcal{F}^{\vee}[n](n)
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X
be a variety and let
\mathcal{F}\in{}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{D}^{\geqslant 0}_{c}(X)
be perverse coconnective.
Suppose
V

is a closed subscheme of
X
locally cut out by
r
regular
functions. Then
\mathcal{F}[-r]|_{V}
is perverse
coconnective.

Proof.

See
[
WZ25a
, Lemma 2.1.1]
.
∎

If follows immediately that
X
is smooth of pure dimension
n
,
and
V\subseteq X
is locally cut out by
r
equations, and

\dim V=n-r
, then
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell,V}[n-r]
is a
perverse sheaf on
V
.

We next state Artin’s vanishing theorem which underlies the various
cohomological Lefschetz hyperplane theorems in algebraic geometry, and
is also a key tool of this paper.

Theorem 3.4

(Artin’s vanishing theorem)
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to S
be an affine morphism of varieties. Let

\mathcal{F}\in{}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{D}_{c}^{\geqslant 0}(X)
be perverse coconnective on

X
. Then
\mathrm{R}\pi_{!}\mathcal{F}
is perverse coconnective
on
S
.

Proof.

See
[
SGA“,4
, Exposé XIV, 3.1]
or
[
BBDG
, Théorème 4.1.1]
.
∎

Here is another useful result not easily found in standard references
on étale cohomology or perverse sheaves. This statement is a
consequence of Deligne’s perverse weak Lefschetz
theorem
[
Kat93
, Appendix]
, which
in turn is based on the Artin vanishing theorem
together with Deligne’s generic base change
theorem
[
Del77
]
.

Lemma 3.5

(Perverse weak Lefschetz theorem)
.

Let
X
be an algebraic variety over an algebraically closed field

k
, and let
\iota\colon X\to\mathbf{P}^{N}
be a quasi-finite
morphism. Suppose

\mathcal{F}\in{}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{D}^{\leqslant 0}_{c}(X)
is
perverse connective. Then, for a general hyperplane

B\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{N}
, the Gysin map

\mathrm{H}^{i-2}_{c}\left(X\times_{\mathbf{P}^{N}}B;\mathcal{F}(-1)\right)\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{i}_{c}(X;\mathcal{F})

is surjective for
i=1
, and is an isomorphism for all
i\geqslant 2
.

Sketch of proof.

Set
\mathcal{G}=\mathbf{D}\mathcal{F}
. Then

\mathcal{G}\in{}^{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{D}^{\geqslant 0}_{c}(X)
. By
Deligne’s perverse weak Lefschetz
theorem
[
Kat93
, Corollary A.5]

(while the statement was for perverse sheaves, the proof only uses
that the complex is coconnective in order to apply Artin’s vanishing
theorem), for a general hyperplane
B
, the restriction map

(3.5.1)

\mathrm{H}^{i}(X;\mathcal{G})\to\mathrm{H}^{i}(X\times_{\mathbf{P}^{N}}B;\mathcal{G}|_{B})

is injective if
i=-1
, and bijective if
i\leqslant-2
. Using
Deligne’s generic base change
(
[
Del77
, Corollarie 2.9]
), one can show that for
B

general, the morphism
\iota\colon X\times_{\mathbf{P}^{N}}B\to X

also satisfies

(3.5.2)

\mathrm{R}\iota^{!}\mathcal{F}\simeq\iota^{\ast}\mathcal{F}[-2](-1)

(see, e.g.,
[
WZ25
, Lemma 3.2]
for details).
The lemma follows from applying the biduality theorem
to (
3.5.1
)
then using (
3.5.2
).
∎

4.
Trivial bounds and semicontinuity of weights

To prove Theorem
1.4
, we need two preliminary results,
both of which can be found in
[
Del80
]
. Before stating
these results, we recall the geometric Frobenius action on a

\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell}
-sheaf on a variety and the formalism
for L-functions.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
X_{0}
be a separated scheme of finite type over

\mathbf{F}_{q}
. Let
\mathcal{F}_{0}
be a

\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell}
-sheaf on
X_{0}
. For any point

x\in X_{0}(\mathbf{F}_{q^{e}})
, we get a morphism

\overline{x}\colon\operatorname{Spec}\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{q}\to\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{F}_{q^{e}}\xrightarrow{x}X_{0},

Then the
inverse
of the Frobenius automorphism of

\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{q}/\mathbf{F}_{q^{e}})
, called
the
geometric Frobenius
, acts
on the stalk of
\mathcal{F}
at
x
. We denote this action by

F_{x}\colon\mathcal{F}_{0\overline{x}}\to\mathcal{F}_{0\overline{x}}.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
X_{0}
be a separated scheme of finite type over
\mathbf{F}_{q}
.
Let
\mathcal{F}_{0}
be a
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell}
-sheaf on
X_{0}
.
Then the L-function of
\mathcal{F}_{0}
is

L(\mathcal{F}_{0},t)=\exp\left(\sum_{e\geqslant 1}\left(\sum_{x\in X_{0}(\mathbf{F}_{q^{e}})}\operatorname{Tr}(F_{x},\mathcal{F}_{0\overline{x}})\right)\frac{t^{e}}{e}\right).

L(\mathcal{F}_{0},t)=\prod_{i}\det(1-tF,\mathrm{H}^{i}_{c}(X;\mathcal{F}))^{(-1)^{i-1}}.

Definition 4.3

(Pointwise weight of a sheaf)
.

In the situation above, we say

is of

\leqslant w
, if for any
e\geqslant 1
, any point

x\in X_{0}(\mathbf{F}_{q^{e}})
, the eigenvalues of

F_{x}\colon\mathcal{F}_{0\overline{x}}\to\mathcal{F}_{0\overline{x}}
have
q^{e}
-weight
\leqslant w
.

Lemma 4.5

(Trivial bounds
[
Del80
, Proposition 1.4.6]
)
.

Let
X_{0}
be a separated scheme of finite type over

\mathbf{F}_{q}
of dimension
\leqslant d
. Suppose

\mathcal{F}_{0}
is a
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell}
-sheaf on

X_{0}
of pointwise weight
\leqslant w
. Then for any isomorphism

\iota\colon\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbf{C}
,
the L-function
\iota L(\mathcal{F}_{0},t)
is convergent for all

|t|<q^{-w/2-d}
, and has no zeros nor poles in this region.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can assume
X_{0}
is affine,
geometrically reduced, geometrically irreducible, and has dimension
equal to
d
. After passing to a finite extension, we can find a
finite morphism
X_{0}\to\mathbf{A}^{d}_{\mathbf{F}_{q}}
. Then
there is a constant
C
independent of
e
, such that

\#X(\mathbf{F}_{q^{e}})\leqslant C(q^{e})^{d},\quad e=1,2,\ldots.

Let

r=\max\{\dim_{\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell}}\mathcal{F}_{\overline{x}}\}

where
\overline{x}
runs through all geometric points of
X_{0}
.
Then for any closed point
x
of
X_{0}
, we have

|\iota\mathrm{Tr}(F_{x})|\leqslant r\cdot(q^{\deg x})^{\frac{w}{2}}

Therefore, the series

\iota\left(\frac{L^{\prime}(\mathcal{F}_{0},t)}{L(\mathcal{F}_{0},t)}\right)=\sum_{e\geqslant 1}\sum_{x\in X_{0}(\mathbf{F}_{q^{e}})}\iota\mathrm{Tr}(F_{x})\cdot t^{e-1}

has a majorant

rC\cdot\sum_{e\geqslant 1}(q^{d+\frac{w}{2}})^{e}\cdot t^{e-1}
. Hence,

\iota\left(L^{\prime}(\mathcal{F}_{0},t)/L(\mathcal{F}_{0},t)\right)
is convergent on the disk
|t|<q^{-w/2-d}
, so is

\log L(\mathcal{F}_{0},t)
. The lemma then follows after taking
exponentiation.
∎

Lemma 4.6

(Semicontinuity of weight
[
Del80
, Lemme 1.8.1]
)
.

Let
C_{0}
be a nonsingular geometrically connected curve. Let

\mathcal{E}_{0}
be a local system on a Zariski open subset

j\colon U_{0}\to C_{0}
. Suppose
\mathcal{E}_{0}
has
pointwise weight
\leqslant w
. Then
\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{\ast}\mathcal{E}_{0}

has pointwise weight
\leqslant w
as well.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can assume that
C_{0}
is affine and

C_{0}\mathbin{\rule[1.99997pt]{6.69998pt}{1.19995pt}}U_{0}
is comprised of
\mathbf{F}_{q}
-rational
points. In this case, we have

\mathrm{H}^{0}_{c}(C;\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{\ast}\mathcal{E})=0
. This can be
seen, for example, from Artin’s vanishing theorem
(Theorem
3.4
, with

S=\operatorname{Spec}\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{q}
) and the perversity
of
\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{\ast}\mathcal{E}[1]
.

We have an exact sequence

0\to j_{!}\mathcal{E}_{0}\to\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{\ast}\mathcal{E}_{0}\to\mathcal{G}_{0}\to 0

where
\mathcal{G}_{0}
is a sheaf supported on the finite set

C_{0}\mathbin{\rule[1.99997pt]{6.69998pt}{1.19995pt}}U_{0}
. For any point
x
in

C_{0}\mathbin{\rule[1.99997pt]{6.69998pt}{1.19995pt}}U_{0}
, we have

\mathcal{G}_{0\overline{x}}=(\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{\ast}\mathcal{E}_{0})_{\overline{x}}
.

The Grothendieck trace formula then takes the following form:

\displaystyle L(\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{\ast}\mathcal{E}_{0},t)

\displaystyle=L(\mathcal{E}_{0},t)\cdot\prod_{x\in C_{0}\mathbin{\rule[1.63885pt]{5.49028pt}{0.98329pt}}U_{0}}\det(1-tF_{x},(\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{\ast}\mathcal{E}_{0})_{\overline{x}})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\det(1-tF,\mathrm{H}_{c}^{1}(C;\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{\ast}\mathcal{E}))}{\det(1-tF,\mathrm{H}_{c}^{2}(C;\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{\ast}\mathcal{E}))}.

Taking compactly supported cohomology, using that

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{c}(C;\mathcal{G})=\mathrm{H}^{2}_{c}(C;\mathcal{G})=0
, we find that

\mathrm{H}^{2}_{c}(C;\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{\ast}\mathcal{E})\simeq\mathrm{H}^{2}_{c}(U;\mathcal{E}).

Therefore, the L-functions
L(\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{\ast}\mathcal{E}_{0},t)
and

L(\mathcal{E}_{0},t)
have the same denominator, equal to

\det(1-tF,\mathrm{H}^{2}_{c}(U;\mathcal{E}))
. It follows
that for any
x\in C_{0}\mathbin{\rule[1.99997pt]{6.69998pt}{1.19995pt}}U_{0}
, the local factor

\det(1-tF_{x},(\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{\ast}\mathcal{E}_{0})_{\overline{x}})^{-1}

must be cancelled by zeros of
L(\mathcal{E}_{0},t)
. By
Lemma
4.5
above,
L(\mathcal{E}_{0},t)
has no zero in
the range
|t|<q^{-(w/2+1)}
. Hence, the Frobenius eigenvalues

\alpha
of
(\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{\ast}\mathcal{E}_{0})_{\overline{x}}
must
satisfy
|\iota\alpha|\leqslant q^{\frac{w+2}{2}}
. We can repeat the
same argument with
\mathcal{E}_{0}
replaced by

\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\otimes k}
. Since

\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{\ast}(\mathcal{E}^{\otimes k}_{0})
contains

(\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{\ast}\mathcal{E})^{\otimes k}_{0}
as a subsheaf, and the
Frobenius eigenvalues of the latter are the
k
th

powers of those of
\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{\ast}\mathcal{E}
, we find the Frobenius
eigenvalues
\alpha
of
\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{\ast}\mathcal{E}
satisfy

|\iota\alpha^{k}|\leqslant q^{\frac{wk+2}{2}},\quad\text{or}\quad|\iota\alpha|<q^{\frac{w}{2}+\frac{2}{k}}.

Letting
k\to\infty
concludes the proof.
∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

We proceed by induction on the dimension
n
of
X_{0}
. We also
remark that the theorem is insensitive to the choice of base field, so
we may replace
\mathbf{F}_{q}
by a finite extension as needed in the
argument.

5.1. Reduction to the case of an affine hypersurface.

After replacing
\mathbf{F}_{q}
by a suitable finite extension, we
may assume that all the geometric irreducible components of
X_{0}

are defined over
\mathbf{F}_{q}
. By an induction on the number of
geometric irreducible components, we may assume that
X_{0}
is
itself geometrically irreducible of pure dimension
n
.
Furthermore, we can find an open dense subscheme

U_{0}\subseteq X_{0}
which is isomorphic to an open dense subscheme
of a hypersurface
Y_{0}
in affine space

\mathbf{A}^{n+1}_{\mathbf{F}_{q}}
. Consider the exact sequence

\mathrm{H}^{i}_{c}(U;\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})\to\mathrm{H}^{i}_{c}(X;\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})\to\mathrm{H}^{i}_{c}(X\mathbin{\rule[1.99997pt]{6.69998pt}{1.19995pt}}U;\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell}),

and observe that the complement
X\mathbin{\rule[1.99997pt]{6.69998pt}{1.19995pt}}U
has dimension at
most
n-1
. By the induction hypothesis, it suffices to prove the
theorem for
U_{0}
. Let
Z_{0}=Y_{0}\mathbin{\rule[1.99997pt]{6.69998pt}{1.19995pt}}U_{0}
;
clearly,
\dim Z_{0}\leqslant n-1
. The following exact sequence,

\mathrm{H}^{i-1}_{c}(Z;\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})\to\mathrm{H}^{i}_{c}(U;\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})\to\mathrm{H}^{i}_{c}(Y;\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell}),

shows that it is further enough to prove the theorem for the affine
hypersurfaces
Y_{0}
. Thus, after these reductions, we may assume

X_{0}
is a hypersurface in
\mathbf{A}^{n+1}_{\mathbf{F}_{q}}
,
defined by a single polynomial

f\in\mathbf{F}_{q}[T_{1},\ldots,T_{n+1}]
.

If the projective closure
of
X_{0}
is smooth, then Theorem
1.4
follows from
Katz’s theorem (Theorem
1.3
) directly:

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume that Theorem
1.4
holds for all separated schemes
of finite type over
\mathbf{F}_{q}
of dimension at most
n-1
.
Let
X_{0}\subseteq\mathbf{A}^{n+1}_{\mathbf{F}_{q}}
be a hypersurface
such that its projective closure is a smooth hypersurface in

\mathbf{P}^{n+1}_{\mathbf{F}_{q}}
. Then Theorem
1.4

holds for
X_{0}
.

Proof.

Observe that the complement
\overline{X}_{0}\mathbin{\rule[1.99997pt]{6.69998pt}{1.19995pt}}X_{0}
has
dimension at most
n-1
. By the inductive hypothesis,
Theorem
1.3
, together with the exact sequence

\mathrm{H}^{n-2}(\overline{X}\mathbin{\rule[1.99997pt]{6.69998pt}{1.19995pt}}X;\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})\to\mathrm{H}^{n-1}_{c}(X;\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})\to\mathrm{H}^{n-1}(\overline{X};\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell}),

we see Theorem
1.4
holds for
X_{0}
.
∎

In our reduction of Theorem
1.4
to the case of affine
hypersurfaces, we have no control over the singularities of the
resulting hypersurface of its projective closure. As a result, to
complete the proof, we require an additional degeneration argument to
adequately address these potential singularities.

5.3. Singular affine hypersurface: weight of middle cohomology.

For an arbitrary
n
-dimensional hypersurface

X_{0}\subseteq\mathbf{A}^{n+1}
, we now prove that the
Frobenius eigenvalues of

\mathrm{H}^{n}_{c}(X;\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})
are
of
q
-weight at most
n
.

After possibly extending the base field, we can find a polynomial

g\in\mathbf{F}_{q}[T_{1},\ldots,T_{n+1}]
such that:

•

the affine hypersurface
W_{0}
defined by
g
is smooth; and

•

the projective closure
\overline{W}_{0}
of
W_{0}
is
itself a smooth hypersurface in
\mathbf{P}^{n+1}_{\mathbf{F}_{q}}
.

Next, we introduce a family which interpolates between our original
hypersurface
X_{0}
and this auxiliary smooth hypersurface
W_{0}
.
Consider

{X}_{0}\subseteq\mathbf{A}^{1}_{\mathbf{F}_{q}}\times\mathbf{A}^{n+1}_{\mathbf{F}_{q}}

defined by the equation

T_{0}g(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n+1})+(1-T_{0})f(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n+1})=0.

Since

\mathbf{A}^{1}_{\mathbf{F}_{q}}\times\mathbf{A}^{n+1}_{\mathbf{F}_{q}}

is smooth, Lemma
3.2

shows that the shifted constant sheaf

\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell,\mathbf{A}^{1}_{\mathbf{F}_{q}}\times\mathbf{A}^{n+1}_{\mathbf{F}_{q}}}[n+2]

is perverse. Hence, by
Lemma
3.3
, we see
that
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell,{X}_{0}}[n+1]
is perverse
coconnective, as
{X}_{0}
is defined locally by a single
regular function.

Consider the projection

\pi\colon{X}_{0}\to\mathbf{A}^{1}_{\mathbf{F}_{q}}
. This is an
affine morphism. So, by Artin’s vanishing theorem
(Theorem
3.4
), the complex

\mathrm{R}\pi_{!}(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell,{X}_{0}}[n+1])
is
perverse coconnective on
\mathbf{A}^{1}_{\mathbf{F}_{q}}
.

Choose an open embedding

j\colon V_{0}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{A}^{1}_{\mathbf{F}_{q}}

of a dense Zariski open subset such that

•

for all
m
,
the cohomology sheaves
\mathrm{R}^{m}\pi_{!}(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell,{X}_{0}})
of

\mathrm{R}\pi_{!}(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell,{X}_{0}})
are local systems on

V_{0}
, and

•

for every geometric point
t\in V_{0}
, the
hypersurface given by
tg+(1-t)f=0
has smooth projective
closure.

By Lemma
5.2
and the proper base change
theorem, the sheaf

\mathrm{R}^{n}\pi_{!}(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell,{X}_{0}})|_{V_{0}}
is of
pointwise weight
\leqslant n
, that is, for
all closed points
x
of
V_{0}
, the eigenvalues of Frobenius

F_{x}
acting on the stalks are of
\#\kappa(x)
-weight at most

n
.

Now, by the semicontinuity of weights
(Lemma
4.6
), the stalk at
0
of the sheaf

\mathrm{R}^{0}j_{*}(\mathrm{R}^{n}\pi_{!}\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})
has
q
-weight

\leqslant n
. Using the perverse degeneration lemma
(Lemma
2.6
), we deduce that the
compactly supported cohomology group

\mathrm{H}^{n}_{c}(X;\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})
also has

q
-weight at most
n
.

5.4. Singular affine hypersurface: other cohomology degrees.

Finally, we address the remaining cohomology degrees. Since
X
is
defined by one polynomial in
\mathbf{A}^{n+1}
,

\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell,X}[n]
is a perverse sheaf on
X
by
Lemma
3.2
and
Lemma
3.3
. We need
to show that for each integer
i\neq 0
, the eigenvalues of
Frobenius acting on

\mathrm{H}^{n+i}_{c}(X;\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})
are of

q
-weight at most
n+i
. If
i\leqslant-1
, Artin’s vanishing
theorem guarantees that

\mathrm{H}^{n+i}_{c}(X;\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})=0
, so there
is nothing to prove. For
i\geqslant 1
, the weak Lefschetz theorem
(Lemma
3.5
) provides a surjection

\mathrm{H}^{n-i}_{c}(X\cap L^{(i)};\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell}(-i))\to\mathrm{H}^{n+i}_{c}(X;\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell}),

where
L_{0}^{(i)}
is a sufficiently general codimension
i
linear
subspace of
\mathbf{A}^{n+1}_{\mathbf{F}_{q}}
(again, we may pass to
a finite field extension in order to find such a subspace). Since

L^{(i)}
is chosen in a general fashion,

\dim X\cap L^{(i)}=n-i
is a hypersurface in an
n-i
-dimensional affine space.
By the previous step, the
Frobenius eigenvalues of the group

\mathrm{H}^{n-i}_{c}(X\cap L^{(i)};\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})

are of
q
-weight at most
n-i
; so taking account of the Tate
twist, the Frobenius eigenvalues of

\mathrm{H}^{n+i}_{c}(X;\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell})
are of

q
-weight at most
n+i
. This completes the proof.∎

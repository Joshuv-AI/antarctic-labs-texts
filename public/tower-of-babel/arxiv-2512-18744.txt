Higher-Rank Mathieu Opers, Toda Chain, and Analytic Langlands Correspondence
Jonah Baerman, Giovanni Ravazzini, Jörg Teschner

Abstract




Abstract

We study the Riemann-Hilbert problem associated to
flat sections of oper connections
of arbitrary rank on the twice-punctured Riemann sphere with irregular singularities of the mildest type.
We construct the solutions in terms of the solutions to a single non-linear integral equation.
It follows from this construction
that the generating function of the submanifold of opers coincides with the Yang-Yang function of the quantum Toda chain, proving a conjecture by Nekrasov, Rosly and Shatashvili.
In this way we may furthermore reformulate
the quantization conditions of the Toda chain in terms of the connection problem, for which we also provide a solution.
We finally interpret our results as a variant of the Analytic Langlands Correspondence for the real version
of the Hitchin system corresponding to the Toda chain.

Introduction

Our work describes connections between certain differential operators called opers
and the spectrum of the closed Toda chain, a paradigmatic example for an integrable many-particle problem.
These connections are predicted by the relations to
{\mathcal{N}}=2
,
d=4
supersymmetric quantum field
theories discussed in
[
48
]
and
[
46
]
.
We are going to propose that one may regard these relations from a mathematical perspective
as a variant of the analytic Langlands correspondence

[
6
]
. We will provide mathematical proofs of the conjectured relations, and use
them to offer a construction of the solutions to the Riemann-Hilbert type problems naturally arising in this context.

1.1
Riemann-Hilbert Correspondence for Some Higher Rank Irregular Opers

We will, on the one hand, consider
differential equations of the form

\left(\partial_{z}^{N}-u_{2}(z)\partial_{z}^{N-2}-\dots-u_{N}(z)\right)\chi(z)=0\,.

(1.1)

In order to have analyticity on
C_{0,2}=\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{0,\infty\}
with
irregular singularities of the mildest possible type at
0
and
\infty

one must have

u_{j}(z)=\mathsf{u}_{j}z^{-j}\,,\qquad j=2,\dots,N-1\,,\qquad u_{N}(z)=\frac{\Lambda^{N}}{z^{N+1}}+\frac{\mathsf{u}_{N}}{z^{N}}+\frac{\Lambda^{N}}{z^{N-1}}\,.

(1.2)

Such differential equations are known to be closely related to a class of holomorphic
\operatorname{SL}(N,{\mathbb{C}})
-connections on

C_{0,2}
called
opers.

A generalization of the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence associates differential equations of the
form (
1.1
) to the generalized monodromy data, which include Stokes data
in the presence of irregular singularities. In the particular case of interest
we are going to demonstrate that the Stokes data are completely determined by the eigenvalues of
the monodromy of the simple closed curve
\gamma
separating
0
and
\infty
.
One may therefore formulate a variant of the Riemann-Hilbert
problem naturally associated to the Riemann surfaces
C_{0,2}
:

(RH)
For any given tuple of
N
complex numbers
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{N}
satisfying

\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sigma_{k}=0
, find differential operators of the form

\partial_{z}^{N}-u_{2}(z)\partial_{z}^{N-2}-\dots-u_{N}(z)
having coefficients
u_{j}(z)
which are
holomorphic on
C_{0,2}
, for
j=2,\dots,N
,
irregular singularities of mildest possible type at

0
and
\infty
, and monodromy eigenvalues
e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\sigma_{k}}
,
k=1,\dots,N
.

We are going to show that the solutions to the Riemann-Hilbert (RH) type problem can be constructed from
the solutions to a single non-linear integral equation.

We’d like to note that there exists
another approach to the solution of this RH type problem using non-linear integral equations proposed
in
[
17
]
based on
[
14
,
15
]
, see
[
29
]
for a
recent application to the case
N=2
of the RH problem above. The
integral equations that appear in this approach are
coupled systems
of non-linear integral
equations.
We hope that the new solution to this RH problem in terms of a single integral equation presented in this paper
can be useful both for numerical computations, and for more detailed investigations
of the analytic properties of the solutions.

1.2
Relation to the Closed Toda Chain

Inspiration for the solution to the Riemann-Hilbert type problem (RH) discussed in this paper
comes from the relation between
the differential equation (
1.1
) and the spectrum of the closed Toda chain,
a paradigmatic quantum integrable model that has been studied intensively. It has been understood
how to translate the quantization conditions of the Toda chain into a simple set of
analytic conditions that solutions to the Baxter equation

t(\lambda)q(\lambda)=\Lambda^{N}\left(\mathrm{i}^{N}q(\lambda+\mathrm{i}\hbar)+\mathrm{i}^{-N}q(\lambda-\mathrm{i}\hbar)\right),\qquad t(\lambda)=\lambda^{N}+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\lambda^{k}E_{N-k}\,,

(1.3)

have to satisfy in order for the coefficients
E_{k}
appearing in the expansion of
t(\lambda)

to be eigenvalues of the
commuting conserved quantities of the closed Toda chain. In order to describe the
resulting conditions more explicitly, one may construct two linearly independent
solutions
Q^{\pm}
to (
1.3
) having
N
free parameters
\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{N}
,
and thereby translate the quantization
conditions into conditions on these parameters which are as computable as the solutions

Q^{\pm}
are. The construction of
Q^{\pm}
given in
[
40
]
is based on
the solution of a non-linear integral equation. It turns out that the sequences

(\chi_{j,n})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
with
\chi_{j,n}=Q^{\pm}(\delta_{j}-\mathrm{i}\hbar n)

are the Laurent-coefficients of solutions to (
1.1
) having
monodromy around
z=0
represented by multiplication with
e^{-\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\delta_{j}}
.

1.3
Yang-Yang Function as Generating Functions of Opers

The relations between
{\cal N}=2
,
d=4
supersymmetric quantum field theories and
integrable models discussed in
[
48

suggest that
the effective twisted
superpotentials
\mathcal{W}
in
\mathcal{N}=2
,

supersymmetric QFTs with partial Omega-deformation

{\mathcal{Y}}
of the corresponding quantum integrable models, allowing

\frac{\partial}{\partial\delta_{k}}{\mathcal{Y}}(\bm{\delta},\Lambda)=2\pi\mathrm{i}\,n_{k}\in 2\pi\mathrm{i}\,{\mathbb{Z}}\,,\qquad k=1,\dots,N\,.

(1.4)

The terminology Yang’s function for
{\mathcal{Y}}
is motivated by the analogy to the potential
for Bethe ansatz equation introduced by C.N. Yang and C.P. Yang in 1969. The instanton calculus
for
\mathcal{N}=2
,
d=4
supersymmetric QFTs leads in the case of pure
\mathcal{N}=2
SYM
to an expression for
{\mathcal{W}}

in terms of the solution to the same nonlinear integral equation as considered in our paper
[
48
,
43
]
.
A direct proof that the known quantization conditions of the closed Toda chain can be reformulated in
the form (
1.4
) with
{\mathcal{Y}}
directly related to
{\mathcal{W}}
was given in
[
40
]
.

The subsequent work
[

proposed a geometric description
of the twisted
superpotentials

S
theories of higher rank in

32

34

, emphasizing the role of abelianization
[

framework for the definition of the coordinates that are relevant in this context.
The relations between

By combining the conjectures of

[

In the particular case of pure
\mathcal{N}=2

a function
{\mathcal{S}}

satisfying

\frac{\partial}{\partial\sigma_{j}}\mathcal{S}(\bm{\sigma},\Lambda)=2\pi\mathrm{i}\,\eta_{j}(\bm{\sigma},\Lambda),

(1.5)

with

We are going to show that

\mathcal{S}(\bm{\sigma},\Lambda)={\mathcal{Y}}(\bm{\delta},\Lambda)\Big|_{\bm{\delta}=-\mathrm{i}\hbar\bm{\sigma}},

(1.6)

with
{\mathcal{Y}}
being the Yang’s function of the closed Toda chain. This
proves the above-mentioned consequence of
the conjectures of
[
48
]
and
[

]

directly, without any reference to quantum field theory.

It has furthermore been suggested in
[

]
that
the above-mentioned conjectures are related to the

see
[

for a review. For the case of our interest one may note that

as studied in
[

Imposing, in addition, single-valuedness and normalizability of the eigenfunctions leads to the

1.4
Interpretation as a Variant of the Analytic Langlands Correspondence

The analytic Langlands correspondence (ALC)
[
6
]
provides a geometric description
of the spectra of
the quantized Hitchin systems in terms of opers on Riemann surfaces.

Hitchin’s integrable systems
\mathcal{M}_{\rm Hit}(C,G)
are classified
by pairs
(C,G)
, with
C
being a Riemann surface and
G
being a complex semi-simple Lie group.
Quantization of the Hitchin system yields a large family of quantum integrable models containing
several known models such as the Toda chain as special cases.
Of basic interest is the spectral problem for the
commuting Hamiltonians characterizing the integrable structures of these models.

Oper connections are classified by pairs
(C,G^{\prime})
, with
C
being the Riemann surface they are
defined on, and
G^{\prime}
being the Lie group used to define of oper connections on
C
.
The opers that correspond to the eigenstates of the quantized Hitchin system
\mathcal{M}_{\rm Hit}(C,G)

according to the ALC are defined on the same Riemann surface
C
as used to define the Hitchin system of interest,
but the Lie group
G^{\prime}
defining the opers corresponding to eigenstates of
\mathcal{M}_{\rm Hit}(C,G)

according to the ALC is the Langlands dual
Lie group
G^{\prime}={}^{L}G
of
G
.

In practice one encounters two types of quantum spectral problems, differing by the choices of scalar products defining
the underlying Hilbert spaces. For generic
C

one may consider scalar products realizing the Hamiltonians as normal operators
[
6
]
.
If
C
admits involutions allowing one to define real forms of the Hitchin systems, one may
alternatively consider a second type of scalar product, defined by integrating over
real
Lagrangian subspaces.

So far most of the work on the analytic Langlands correspondence has considered the first type of
spectral problems.
The analytic Langlands correspondence for this type of problems,
in the following referred to as (ALC)
C
, predicts a one-to-one correspondence between
eigenstates and real opers, opers having monodromy conjugate to a real form
{}^{L}G_{\mathbb{R}}
of
{}^{L}G

[
53
,
6
]
.

However, it can also be interesting to study spectral problems of the second type,
associated to real slices of the phase space
from this point of view. The closed Toda chain is arguably the
simplest example for integrable models of this type. It is a real form of the Hitchin system
associated to the Riemann surface
C_{0,2}=\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{0,\infty\}

with irregular singularities of the mildest possible type at
0
and
\infty
.

The Hitchin moduli space
is represented by Higgs fields of the same form as the right hand side of eq. (
3.6
) below.

We are going to prove:

(ALC)
R

C_{0,2}
having
canonical bases associated to the two irregular singularities related by
parallel transport matrices proportional to the identity.

As the solutions to the spectral
problem are thereby classified by opers with simple conditions on the monodromy data,
one may regard the assertion above, referred to as
(ALC)
R

analytic Langlands correspondence for spectral problems of the second
type.

It seems quite remarkable that in both cases it is possible to express the quantization conditions in terms of

of the subspace of opers,

\displaystyle\text{(ALC)${}_{\mathbb{C}}$ :}\quad\quad\mathrm{Re}\bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial\sigma_{j}}\mathcal{S}(\bm{\sigma},\Lambda)\bigg)=2\pi\mathrm{i}\,n_{j}\,,\quad\mathrm{Re}(\sigma_{j})=2\pi\mathrm{i}\,m_{j}\,,

(1.7)

\displaystyle\text{(ALC)${}_{\mathbb{R}}$ :}\quad\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial\sigma_{j}}\mathcal{S}(\bm{\sigma},\Lambda)=2\pi\mathrm{i}\,n_{j}\,,

One may note, on the other hand, that the conditions implied by (ALC)

The analytic Langlands correspondence appears to exchange reality conditions in a subtle way.

Further support for the proposal that (ALC)

1.5
Further Connections

There exists an interesting variant of the relations between supersymmetric partition
functions and quantization conditions of integrable models. It expresses the quantization conditions
of various spectral problems in terms of the isomonodromic tau-functions, which
in turn are related to the instanton partition functions of the corresponding
\mathcal{N}=2
SUSY QFTs by
the correspondence discovered by Gamayun, Iorgov and Lisovyy
[
18
,
19
]
.
Background from theoretical physics for these relations
is provided by the relations between
topological string theory and supersymmetric gauge theories

[
51
,
26
,
25
,
24
]
.
For the case of
N=2
one thereby obtains
a description of the spectrum of the Mathieu operator that is equivalent to the one discussed in this
paper, see
[
2
]
for a proof.

A more recent variant of these relations extends the scope of these methods from the quantization
conditions to the eigenfunctions
[
12
,
11
]
. This leads to
exact expressions for the solutions to the Baxter equation (
1.3
) of the closed Toda chain
in terms of known building blocks from topological string theory. More general
quantization conditions can be considered in this context.
It would be very interesting to compare these results with our approach.

We’d furthermore like to note that the relation between the Baxter equation and opers is a
special case of a phenomenon known as spectral duality in the literature

[
44
,
45
]
, relating the canonical quantizations of the spectral
curves of (in general) different integrable systems. Providing the exact dictionary between
the conditions that have to be satisfied by the solutions to the respective equations, as done here,
can be regarded as an analytic version of the spectral duality. It would be interesting
to describe the quantization conditions of other pairs of quantum integrable models related
by spectral duality in an analogous fashion.

2
Spectral Problem of the Closed Toda Chain

The
N
-particle, periodic Toda chain is defined by the Hamiltonian

{\mathsf{H}}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\frac{p_{j}^{2}}{2}+\Lambda^{2}e^{x_{j}-x_{j+1}}\right),\qquad x_{N+1}\equiv x_{1}\,,

(2.1)

defining a differential operator on
L^{2}\left({\mathbb{R}}^{N}\right)
by
setting
p_{j}=\frac{\hbar}{\mathrm{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}
. Complete quantum integrability of the periodic Toda chain is reflected by the existence of a set
\{{\mathsf{H}}_{1},\dots,{\mathsf{H}}_{N}\}
of
N
algebraically independent commuting operators containing
{\mathsf{H}}
.
In order to fully establish complete integrability of the quantum Toda chain it is useful to introduce the Baxter Q-operator
{\mathsf{Q}}(\lambda)

[
20
]
related to the conserved charges by the Baxter equation

{\mathsf{T}}(\lambda){\mathsf{Q}}(\lambda)=\Lambda^{N}\left(\mathrm{i}^{N}{\mathsf{Q}}(\lambda+\mathrm{i}\hbar)+\mathrm{i}^{-N}{\mathsf{Q}}(\lambda-\mathrm{i}\hbar)\right),\qquad{\mathsf{T}}(\lambda)=\lambda^{N}+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\lambda^{k}{\mathsf{H}}_{N-k}\,.

(2.2)

The operators in
\{\mathsf{Q}(\lambda)\,|\,\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
are mutually commuting.
One may therefore refine the spectral problem of
{\mathsf{H}}
to finding the simultaneous
spectral decomposition of the operators
{\mathsf{Q}}(\lambda)
for all
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Eigenstates of
{\mathsf{Q}}(\lambda)

are simultaneously eigenstates of
{\mathsf{T}}(\lambda)
. Notations for the eigenvalues are defined through the expansions

t(\lambda)=\lambda^{N}+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\lambda^{k}E_{N-k}=\prod_{k=1}^{N}(\lambda-\tau_{k})\,,

(2.3)

where
E_{k}
is the eigenvalue of
{\mathsf{H}}_{k}
, and reality of
t(\lambda)
for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
requires
\{\tau_{k}\}=\{\bar{\tau}_{k}\}
.
It can be shown that
{\mathsf{H}}_{1}=-P
and
{\mathsf{H}}_{2}=P^{2}/2-{\mathsf{H}}
, where
P
is the total momentum.
We will assume throughout that
E_{1}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\tau_{k}=0
, such that
-E_{2}
is the eigenvalue of the Hamiltonian
{\mathsf{H}}
.

With the help of the Separation of Variables (SoV) it has been shown that

eigenstates of the quantum Toda chain are in a one-to-one correspondence with
the solutions of the Baxter equation

t(\lambda)q(\lambda)=\Lambda^{N}\left(\mathrm{i}^{N}q(\lambda+\mathrm{i}\hbar)+\mathrm{i}^{-N}q(\lambda-\mathrm{i}\hbar)\right),

(2.4)

which are (i) entire, and (ii)
have asymptotic behavior

\lvert q^{\pm}(\lambda)\rvert=O\Big(e^{-\tfrac{N\pi}{2\hbar}\lvert\operatorname{Re}\lambda\rvert}\lvert\lambda\rvert^{\tfrac{N}{2\hbar}(2\lvert\operatorname{Im}\lambda\rvert-\hbar)}\Big)\,,

(2.5)

uniformly in a strip of width
\hbar
centered on the real line.

As the conditions (i) and (ii) are necessary and sufficient for existence of a square-integrable eigenfunction
\Psi_{\mathbf{E}}
of
{\mathsf{H}}_{1},\dots,{\mathsf{H}}_{N}
having eigenvalues
E_{1},\dots,E_{N}
, we will refer to them as quantization conditions.

The characterization of the spectrum above is the result of a long series of works including
[
27
,
28
,
20
,
38
,
1
]
.
In order to show that to each solution to the conditions
(i) and (ii) there corresponds an eigenfunction one can use Sklyanin’s Separation of Variables method (SoV),
see
[
38
]
and references therein. The SoV method defines a unitary integral transformation mapping
products of solutions to the conditions (i) and (ii) to eigenstates
[
39
]
.
The fact that the
all
eigenstates are obtained from solutions
of conditions (i) and (ii) in this way has been proven in
[
1
]
.

In order to describe the quantization conditions more explicitly, one may start by constructing
families of solutions to the Baxter equation (
2.4
) depending on
N
parameters,
and deriving a finite set of equations that have to be satisfied by the parameters in order to satisfy the quantization conditions
(i), (ii).
In the following two subsections we will briefly review the two known constructions of solutions to the quantization conditions.

2.1
Solutions to the Baxter Equations from Infinite Determinants

The first construction of two linearly independent solutions
Q^{\pm}_{\bm{\tau}}
of the Baxter equation (
2.4
)
was given in
[
27
,
28
]
.
In this construction,
Q^{\pm}_{\bm{\tau}}
take the roots
\boldsymbol{\tau}\coloneqq(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{N})
of the transfer matrix
t(\lambda)
(
2.3
) appearing in the Baxter equation as input data: explicitly, they are given by

\displaystyle Q^{+}_{\bm{\tau}}(\lambda)=\left(\frac{\hbar}{\Lambda}\right)^{\frac{\mathrm{i}N\lambda}{\hbar}}\frac{e^{-N\pi\lambda/\hbar}K_{+}(\lambda)}{\prod_{k=1}^{N}\Gamma(1-\mathrm{i}(\lambda-\tau_{k})/\hbar)}\,,

(2.6a)

\displaystyle Q^{-}_{\bm{\tau}}(\lambda)=\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\hbar}\right)^{\frac{\mathrm{i}N\lambda}{\hbar}}\frac{e^{-N\pi\lambda/\hbar}K_{-}(\lambda)}{\prod_{k=1}^{N}\Gamma(1+\mathrm{i}(\lambda-\tau_{k})/\hbar)}\,,

(2.6b)

where
K_{+}(\lambda)

\displaystyle K_{+}(\lambda-\mathrm{i}\hbar)=K_{+}(\lambda)-\frac{\Lambda^{2N}K_{+}(\lambda+\mathrm{i}\hbar)}{t(\lambda)t(\lambda+\mathrm{i}\hbar)}\,,

(2.7a)

\displaystyle K_{-}(\lambda+\mathrm{i}\hbar)=K_{-}(\lambda)-\frac{\Lambda^{2N}K_{-}(\lambda-\mathrm{i}\hbar)}{t(\lambda)t(\lambda-\mathrm{i}\hbar)}\,,

(2.7b)

that tend to

K_{+}(\lambda,\Lambda)=\det\begin{pmatrix}1&\frac{1}{t(\lambda+\mathrm{i}\hbar)}&&\lx@intercol\hfil\qquad\;\mathbf{0}\hfil\lx@intercol\\
\frac{\Lambda^{2N}}{t(\lambda+2\mathrm{i}\hbar)}&1&\frac{1}{t(\lambda+2\mathrm{i}\hbar)}\\
&\frac{\Lambda^{2N}}{t(\lambda+3\mathrm{i}\hbar)}&1&\frac{1}{t(\lambda+3\mathrm{i}\hbar)}\\
\mathbf{0}&&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots\end{pmatrix},

(2.8)

and
K_{-}(\lambda,\Lambda)=\widebar{K}_{+}(\widebar{\lambda},\widebar{\Lambda})

While
Q^{\pm}_{\bm{\tau}}(\lambda)
are entire, they have exponential growth in one direction along the real

q(\lambda)=\frac{Q^{+}_{\bm{\tau}}(\lambda)-\zeta Q^{-}_{\bm{\tau}}(\lambda)}{\prod_{j=1}^{N}e^{-\tfrac{\pi\lambda}{\hbar}}\sinh\tfrac{\pi}{\hbar}(\lambda-\delta_{j})}\,,\qquad\delta_{j}\in{\mathbb{C}}\,,\quad j=1,\dots,N\,,

(2.9)

ensuring validity of (ii), but sacrificing (i) for generic choice of
\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{N}

\delta_{j}

\mathfrak{q}_{\bm{\tau}}^{\pm}(\lambda)=\big(Q_{\bm{\tau}}^{\pm}(\lambda+\mathrm{i}\hbar n)\big)_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{{\mathbb{Z}}}\,.

It can be shown that for any given polynomial

of the form (
2.3
), and any

there exist
N

\delta_{1}(\bm{\tau},\Lambda),\dots,\delta_{N}(\bm{\tau},\Lambda)

\lambda

\mathfrak{q}_{\bm{\tau}}^{+}(\lambda)

is proportional to
\mathfrak{q}_{\bm{\tau}}^{-}(\lambda)

In order to see this, one may consider the

W[Q^{+}_{\bm{\tau}},Q^{-}_{\bm{\tau}}](\lambda)\coloneqq Q^{+}_{\bm{\tau}}(\lambda)Q^{-}_{\bm{\tau}}(\lambda+\mathrm{i}\hbar)-Q^{-}_{\bm{\tau}}(\lambda)Q^{+}_{\bm{\tau}}(\lambda+\mathrm{i}\hbar)\,.

(2.10)

Vanishing of the quantum Wronskian for a given value
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
implies that
\mathfrak{q}_{\bm{\tau}}^{+}(\lambda)

Lemma 4

5
Floquet Bases and Riemann-Hilbert Problem

In this section we construct explicit Floquet solutions to the oper equation, by making precise the relation between the Toda chain and opers on

In particular, this allows us to solve the direct monodromy problem, as well as the Riemann-Hilbert problem outlined in Section

5.1

In order to construct Floquet solutions, one may use an ansatz of the form

6.2
Analytic Spectral Duality

As noted in Section
3

).
Generically, this cannot be extended to an analytic Fourier transform of solutions to these equations

Validity of the quantization conditions will turn out to be sufficient, and probably also necessary
for the possibility to relate solutions of the Baxter equation to solutions of the oper equation
by a Fourier-transformation. We may prove, on the one hand, the following result.

The unique (up to normalization) maximally decaying solution

Consider the following pair of integrals

away from their poles and a strip around

Concerning a possible converse to this statement, let us make the following observations.
Solutions

6.3

In this appendix we provide additional details on some analytic properties of the Floquet solutions (

In Section

\mathfrak{sl}(N,{\mathbb{C}})

More precisely, they are solutions to the differential equation

t(-\mathrm{i}\hbar w\partial_{w})\chi(w)=(\mathrm{i}\hbar)^{N}w\chi(w)\,.

(B.1)

A large class of solutions is given by Meijer

(B.3)

\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{q}\end{matrix}\,\middle|\,w\right)

\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{q}\end{matrix}\,\middle|\,w\right),

(B.4a)

\displaystyle{}_{p}F_{q}\left(\begin{matrix}\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p}\\
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{q}\end{matrix}\,\middle|\,w\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\alpha_{1})_{n}\dots(\alpha_{p})_{n}}{(\beta_{1})_{n}\dots(\beta_{q})_{n}}\frac{w^{n}}{n!}\,,

Maximally Decaying Solutions

A crucial step in the discussion of the quantization conditions is the identification of the maximally decaying solution.

Lemma 7

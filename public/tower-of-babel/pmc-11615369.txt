Stereolithography 3D printing gyroid triply periodic minimal surface vitrified bond diamond grinding wheel
Authors: Zhaoqi Chen, Kehan Li, Ping Han, Yuetang Pan, Guoju Bai, Zijing Xia, Na Xiao, Pengyu Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe pores of vitrified bond diamond grinding wheel play a key role in the grinding process. However, uneven pore distribution and low porosity affect the grinding performance of the wheel significantly. Stereolithography based additive manufacturing provides an effective method to fabricate vitrified bond diamond grinding wheels with a uniform distribution and an interconnected pore structure. The key to high-performance grinding wheel via stereolithography 3D printing lies in the preparation of the slurry with high solid loading, low viscosity and uniform stability. In this study, the dispersion and stability of vitrified bond and diamond slurries were investigated systematically. The effects of resin monomers, surface modifiers, and solid loading on the dispersion, rheological behavior and stability of slurries were studied in detail. Finally, an optimal vitrified bond and diamond slurry for stereolithography based additive manufacturing was obtained, and complex-shaped gyroid triply periodic minimal surface grinding wheel were fabricated. By grinding the SiC ceramics, the material removal rate, grinding temperature, and surface roughness were compared to those achieved using a conventional solid structure grinding wheel. The results show that the gyroid porous grinding wheel can achieve better surface roughness and lower the grinding temperature.Keywords: Vitrified diamond composite, Stereolithography, Gyroid, Triply periodic minimal surface, Grinding performanceSubject terms: Engineering, Materials science

Introduction

The vitrified bond diamond grinding wheel is an ultra-hard machining tool known for its high sharpness, precision, and excellent heat resistance1–3. Compared with resin bond abrasive tools, it is not easy to block the pores, and not easy to burn the workpiece. Compared with metal bond abrasive tools, it is easy to dress and the porosity is controllable. They are widely used in hard and brittle materials, optical glass, semiconductor materials aircraft engines, and other processing areas4–7. The traditional preparation of vitrified bond diamond grinding wheel is formed by cold pressing of steel mold8. The diamond abrasive and vitrified bond were mixed proportionately, and the temporary binder was added. The mixture was injected into the mold and formed by cold pressing9. The shape of the grinding wheel manufactured by this method depends on the mold, which makes it difficult to manufacture the grinding wheel with a uniform distribution and an interconnected pore structure.

The pores in vitrified bond diamond grinding wheels play a key role in the grinding process10. They not only effectively dissipate heat and prevent surface burns of the workpiece, but also accommodate chips, avoid blockages, and maintain the self-sharpening of the grinding wheel11–14. The pore-forming methods of traditional abrasive tools manufacturing include the sintering method, space-occupying method, and foaming method. Even without adding a pore-forming agent, the vitrified bond will shrink and produce pores during the sintering process. The pore size is related to the particle size of the green body powder and the sintering temperature. The porosity and the distribution of pores in this method are difficult to control; The space-occupying method is based on walnut peel, soluble substances (such as inorganic salts, sugars, etc.), hollow balls, and polymer materials (such as polymethacrylate) as pore-forming agents. They will occupy a certain space during the cold pressing process of the abrasive tool. Under high temperature conditions, these pore-forming agents will carbonize, burn, shrink, or volatilize, leaving space in the original position and generating pores in the grinding wheel9,15,16. The size, shape, and distribution of pores in the grinding wheel are heavily dependent on the pore-forming agent. The foaming method is to add some substances that are decomposed by heat to produce gas in the abrasive tool. During the sintering process, bubbles are generated in the melting state of the vitrified bond, and they remain after cooling and become pores16–18 The pore morphology generated by the bubbling method is usually irregular and uncontrollable. While advancements have been made in traditional tool manufacturing technologies4,19, they still face challenges in producing tools with complex internal and external structures, high precision, and personalization20.

Based on the above problems, triply periodic minimal surface (TPMS) can be applied to vitrified bond diamond grinding wheels. The TPMS is defined and controlled by the mathematical expression of the implicit function, and the structure can be controlled by modifying the parameters in the expression21,22. The average curvature of this structure is zero, and the surface is very smooth. Compared with the pillar-based lattice structure, the structure does not have sharp edges or nodes, which effectively reduces the stress concentration23,24. TPMS structure can meet the mechanical requirements of grinding wheel grinding and can realize the uniform distribution and interconnection of pores, which can solve the difficulties in the preparation of traditional grinding wheels. Additive Manufacturing (AM), also known as 3D printing, is an advanced manufacturing technology that manufactures three-dimensional objects by adding materials layer by layer25. It is different from the traditional subtractive manufacturing technology. The traditional method usually achieves the desired shape by removing the material, while additive manufacturing is to build the model layer by layer, which can manufacture the complex TPMS porous structure26. Compared with other 3D printing technologies, the stereolithography 3D printing technology used in this study is widely used in a large number of scientific explorations because of its high printing precision, fast molding speed, and low cost27-28. This technology holds significant potential for the field of abrasive tool manufacturing, offering the possibility to develop ultra-hard abrasive tools with highly complex structures and precise processing capabilities. It is expected to overcome the limitations of traditional abrasive tool production methods. Currently, it has been reported that 3D printing technology is directly or indirectly applied to vitrified bond diamond tools: direct ink writing29, and 3D printed mold combined with sol-casting technology30. Their limitation is that they do not realize the interconnection of pores.

In the grinding process, a vitrified bond diamond grinding wheel must have high strength to ensure processing stability and accuracy1. The grinding wheel will be subjected to significant thermal stress and mechanical load in high-speed operation2. High-strength vitrified bond can effectively resist the structural damage caused by temperature rise and grinding force, maintain the stability of grinding wheel shape, and ensure the accuracy and surface quality of machined parts. Secondly, the bond with sufficient strength can stably maintain the diamond abrasive particles, reduce the premature shedding or breaking of the abrasive particles, prolong the service life of the grinding wheel, and improve the grinding efficiency5,6. In order to ensure the high strength of the grinding wheel, the slurry used in the process of stereolithography 3D printing body needs to meet the requirements of low viscosity, good stability, and high solid content. The low viscosity of the slurry indicates that the vitrified bond and diamond particles can be well dispersed in the photosensitive resin. If the dispersion is poor, the particles will attract each other through van der Waals force, electrostatic force, and so on to form aggregates. Compared with the uniformly dispersed single particles, the agglomerates occupy a larger volume and form a larger obstacle in the slurry, and the viscosity of the slurry will become larger, which is not conducive to the drawing of the printing platform31; the stability of the slurry is good, indicating that the particle settling velocity is slow. This means that the slurry can maintain its uniformity during the standing or printing process. If the stability of the slurry is poor, the particles will settle at the bottom in a short time, and the settlement of the particles will affect the transmittance of ultraviolet light in the slurry, resulting in uneven photocuring process. This will lead to the bonding between the layers not being strong, thus affecting the integrity of the overall structure32; the solid content of the slurry should be as high as possible, and the high solid content means that the body has more solid particles and less organic matter or solvent during the sintering process. If the solid content of the slurry is low, the volume shrinkage of the material will be relatively large during the degreasing and sintering process, which will greatly increase the possibility of grinding wheel cracking, thereby reducing the strength of the grinding wheel33.

In this study, the dispersion and stability of vitrified bond and diamond slurries were investigated systematically. The effects of resin monomers, surface modifiers, and solid loading on the dispersion, rheological behavior, and stability of slurries were studied in detail. By grinding the SiC ceramics, the material removal rate, grinding temperature, and surface roughness were compared to those achieved using a conventional solid-structure grinding wheel. The purpose of this study is to develop a vitrified bond diamond grinding wheel with uniform pore distribution and interconnection.

Materials and methods

Materials

The granular raw materials used in this study are: commercial vitrified bond VB-1 (thermal expansion coefficient is 8.76 × 10−6/°C; the softening temperature is 653 °C). The fluidity reached 128% at 680 °C in 120 min. The density was 2.42 g cm−3. D50 was 12 ± 5 μm; SanTai Materials Technology Co., Ltd., Henan, China); diamond abrasive (purity of 99.6%; the density was 3.52 g cm−3. D50 was 10 ± 5 μm; Yellow River Whirlwind Co., Ltd., Henan, China).

Photosensitive resin system: The bifunctional 1,6-hexanediol diacrylate (HDDA) is used as a diluent (purity 99%; density 1.01 g cm−3; viscosity at room temperature 5–10 mPa s; Youming Chemical Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China); the trifunctional ethoxylated trimethylolpropane triacrylate (TMP3EOTA) serves as the photopolymerizable monomer (purity 99%; density 1.12 g cm−3; viscosity at room temperature 40–80 mPa s; Youming Chemical Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China); 2,4,6-trimethylbenzoyl-diphenylphosphine oxide (TPO) is used as the photoinitiator (purity 99%; density 1.17 g cm−3; Youming Chemical Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China); polyethylene glycol (PEG) 200 (purity 99%; density 1.27 g cm−3; Aladdin Biochemical Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) is used as a dispersant.

Silane coupling agents KH550 (purity 99%; density 0.95 g cm−3), KH560 (purity 99%; density 1.07 g cm− 3), and KH570 (purity 99%; density 1.05 g cm− 3) were used as surface modifiers for the particles, all purchased from Aladdin Biochemical Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China32,33.

According to our previous research, the vitrified bond slurry exhibited optimal performance when 5 wt% PEG (relative to the powder mass) was used as a dispersant and 5 wt% TPO (relative to the resin mass) was added as a photoinitiator34. This study investigates the impact of resin monomer ratios, the surface modification effects of silane coupling agents, and solid content on the dispersion, rheological properties, and stability of the slurry. By grinding the SiC ceramics, the material removal rate, grinding temperature, and surface roughness were compared to those achieved using a conventional solid structure grinding wheel.

Vitrified bond slurry, sample preparation and sedimentation observation

Figure 1 is a flow chart of the slurry preparation and sedimentation observation process. As shown in the diagram, the vitrified bond particles are first subjected to ball milling modification. In this process, smaller diameter zirconia balls (3 mm: 5 mm: 7 mm ratio of 7: 2: 1) are selected as the grinding media, with a ball-to-material ratio of 8:135–37. The ball was milled for 4 h at a speed of 400 r/min. The modified vitrified bond powder was passed through a 200 mesh sieve to avoid agglomeration. Under the conditions of a constant temperature water bath at 40 °C, HDDA and TMP3EOTA were mixed in different proportions using magnetic stirring for 30 min38,39. Then, 5 wt% (relative to the total mass of the resin) of PEG and TPO photoinitiator were added to the mixture34. Finally, the modified ceramic binder is mixed with photosensitive resin at 400 r/min for 4 h in a ball mill to obtain the ceramic binder slurry. Once the optimal system is determined, diamond abrasives can be added in the same manner to create a slurry system composed of a ceramic binder mixed with diamonds.

The prepared slurry was loaded into a 10 ml test tube for sedimentation observation experiments to test the stability of the slurry. The height of the supernatant (Hl) and the initial slurry height (H0) were recorded every 2 h, and the value of Hl/H0 was calculated. The smaller the value, the higher the stability of the slurry31–35.

In this work, Digital light processing (DLP) 3D printer (Prism Ceramic; shenzhen Sankaiyou Technology Co., Ltd., Shenzhen, China). The three-dimensional model of TPMS porous structure was established by using nTopology 3.5.4 software. In the CHITUBOX 1.9.0 slicing software, the bottom exposure time was set to 40 s, the exposure time of each layer was 3 s, and the layer thickness was 50 μm. Import the sliced files into the printer to start printing (more details can be found in our previous studies22).

Characterization

Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy IR Prestige-21 (Shimadzu, Japan) was used to analyze the group changes on the surface of the particles before and after modification. A rotational rheometer MCR-301 (Anton Paar, Austria) was used to measure the relationship between viscosity and shear rate of different slurry systems. Differential scanning calorimetry DSC-200F3 (Germany Netzsch) was used to observe the burnout of photosensitive resin during heating. The fracture morphology of diamond grinding wheel samples was observed by scanning electron microscope FEI INSPCET F50 (FEI, USA). The solidified layer thickness was measured by a spiral micrometer DL321025S (China Deli Group).

The mechanical properties were tested using an electronic universal testing machine (WDW-50, Jinan, China) with a loading rate of 0.5 mm/min. The three-point bending strength of the sample was tested five times in each group. The Vickers microhardness of the samples was evaluated using a microhardness tester (FM700, FUTURE-TECH, Japan). Finally, the grinding performance of the grinding wheel was tested by using the numerical control cylindrical grinder MK1320C (Wuxi, China) with a speed of 2500 r/min and distilled water as the coolant. The surface roughness of SiC ceramics was measured using a white light interferometer (Zegege, ZYGO, USA).

Results and discussion

Effect of resin monomer on vitrified bond slurry

Slurries with a solid content of 40 wt% were prepared. In order to minimize the viscosity of the mixtures while ensuring the quality of the green body, a preliminary set of 2a–2g, 8 groups of vitrified bond slurries with different resin monomers were formulated as shown in Table 1. Figure 2 shows the effect of the proportion of different resin monomers on the viscosity of the vitrified bond slurry and the thickness of the cured layer. It was found that the viscosity of the slurry decreased with the increase of the proportion of HDDA, and the viscosity of the slurry system was minimized to 1.36 Pa s at a shear rate of 30 s− 1 with a proportion of 100 wt% of HDDA, and the viscosity of the slurry system was maximized to 2.56 Pa s with a proportion of 100 wt% of TMP3EOTA. This trend can be attributed to the fact that monomers with more functional groups have higher molecular weights, which increases viscosity and reduces the dilution effect, making the slurry less suitable for printing40. Additionally, a higher number of functional groups results in more free radicals participating in the polymerization reaction, accelerating the photocuring process. The thickness of the cured layer was found to increase with a higher proportion of TMP3EOTA. When TMP3EOTA was 100 wt%, the cured layer thickness reached 85 μm. In contrast, with 100 wt% HDDA, the cured layer thickness was 48 μm, which is less than the desired thickness of 50 μm, potentially compromising green body quality. Closer examination of the data reveals that when the HDDA ratio is between 60 and 70 wt%, the slurry viscosity decreases most rapidly, and the cured layer thickness also shows the fastest reduction. Therefore, it was preliminarily concluded that the optimal HDDA proportion likely falls within the 60–70 wt% range.

Effect of surface modifier on vitrified bond slurry

To investigate the influence of silane coupling agents as modifiers on vitrified bond slurry, three agents—KH550, KH560, and KH570—were selected for study32,42 For clarity in the results, a slurry with a solid content of 60 wt% was prepared, with the viscosity of the unmodified slurry at this solid content being 2.72 Pa s34. Figure 4a illustrates the effect of varying the content of these silane coupling agents (relative to the total weight of the powder) on the viscosity of the slurry. It was observed that as the modifier content increased, the viscosity initially decreased before rising again. All three silane coupling agents effectively reduced the viscosity of the vitrified bond slurry. The most significant reduction was observed with the addition of 3.0 wt% KH560, where the viscosity dropped to 0.95 Pa s, marking a 65% reduction. KH550, when added at 2.5 wt%, resulted in the lowest viscosity of 1.25 Pa s, a 54% reduction. KH570, at 2.0 wt%, achieved a viscosity of 1.12 Pa s, reducing it by 59%. This viscosity reduction was attributed to the molecular structure of the silane coupling agents, which enhance the interaction between vitrified bond particles and the prepolymer. Upon hydrolysis, the silane coupling agents form silanol (–Si–OH) groups. These groups react with the –OH groups on the surface of the particles through hydrogen bonding, followed by a dehydration condensation reaction to form covalent –Si–O–X bonds. These covalent bonds anchor the particles, facilitating their dispersion in the slurry. The Y group at the other end of the silane molecule was chemically bonded or compatible with epoxy acrylate, serving as a molecular bridge that adds hydrophobic functional groups to the particle surface. This modification ensures uniform dispersion of vitrified bond particles in the prepolymer, thereby effectively reducing the slurry’s viscosity32,43. The modification behavior of vitrified bond powder was further analyzed using Fourier Transform Infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy to identify the hydrophobic structures formed on the surface of the powder through chemical adsorption. In Fig. 4b–d, the black curve represents the FTIR spectrum of the unmodified vitrified bond raw material, where the main vibration peaks of the –OH group are observed at wave numbers 1628 cm–1 and 3448 cm–1. The spectra of the pure modifier and its corresponding modified samples were displayed in red and green curves, respectively. For KH550, which contains primary groups such as Si–O and –NH2, wave numbers were found around 1000 cm–1 and 1700 cm–1 in the infrared spectrum. New peaks at 1008 cm–1 and 1684 cm–1 in the modified vitrified bond particles confirm that KH550 has been adsorbed onto the particle surfaces. Figure 4b shows the infrared spectrum of the KH550-modified vitrified bond. Similarly, KH560 exhibits a Si–O group in the 1000–1100 cm–1 range, and a new peak at 1081 cm–1 in the modified particles indicates the adsorption of KH560 on the surface, as illustrated in Fig. 4c. For KH570, in addition to the C–H stretching vibration absorption peaks at 2848 cm–1 and 2948 cm–1, a new absorption peak at 1730 cm–1—representing the bending vibration absorption peak of C=O within the KH570 molecule—emerges in the modified particles. Furthermore, the bending vibration absorption peak of Si–O–Si at 822 cm–1 suggests that KH570 has successfully coated the surface of the vitrified bond particles, as shown in Fig. 4d. The clear detection of absorption peaks corresponding to all three modifiers in the modified vitrified bond particles demonstrates that silane coupling agents KH550, KH560, and KH570 were successfully adsorbed onto the particle surfaces.

Effect of surface modifier on slurry stability

The stability of the slurry is an important indicator of particle dispersion, with better stability signifying reduced agglomeration and less sedimentation of particles within the slurry32,42–45. Figure 7 presents the sedimentation behavior of the slurry after standing for 12 h. From Fig. 6a–d, the slurries were modified using 2.5 wt% KH550, 3.0 wt% KH560, 2.0 wt% KH570, and a sample without surface modification, respectively. The study found that particle modification significantly enhances the stability of the slurry, maintaining its stability even during extended periods of sedimentation. Among the samples, the slurry modified with KH560 demonstrated the best stability. This superior performance can be attributed to the epoxy group in KH560, which reacts with various functional groups (such as hydroxyl and amino groups) in the photosensitive resin to form stable chemical bonds. The reactivity and versatility of the epoxy group prevent particle aggregation and promote strong interfacial bonding42. Consequently, KH560-modified particles maintain better dispersion and stability in the photosensitive resin. The Hl/H0 values shown in Fig. 7e indicate that the slurry without surface modification exhibits the highest Hl/H0 ratio, signifying severe sedimentation over time. The slurry began to stabilize only after standing for 6 h, indicating poor overall stability. In contrast, slurries with surface modifications stabilized more quickly: those modified with KH570 and KH550 reached stability after 4 h, while the slurry modified with KH560 reached a stable state in just 2 h, evidenced by the smallest Hl/H0 value. This demonstrates that KH560 is the most effective modifier in achieving rapid and lasting slurry stability.

Effect of solid content of slurry on rheological properties

Figure 8 illustrates the rheological behavior of KH560-modified vitrified bond at different solid contents. The data show that the slurry’s viscosity gradually decreases as the shear rate increases from 1 to 200 s–1. Regardless of the solid content, the slurry exhibits shear-thinning behavior and maintains good dispersion. At a shear rate of 30 s–1, the viscosity values for slurries with solid contents of 60, 65, 70, 72, 74, and 76 wt% are 0.95, 1.7, 2.64, 3.52, 4.54, and 7.72 Pa s, respectively. A notable increase in viscosity is observed when the solid content rises from 74 to 76 wt%, resulting in a 60% increase in viscosity. Excessive viscosity adversely affects printing and forming, making 74 wt% (approximately 55 vol%) the most suitable solid content. Compared to the unmodified slurry, KH560 surface modification allowed an increase in solid content from 65 to 74 wt% (a 13.85% rise), while the viscosity only increased slightly from 3.82 to 4.54 Pa s, a modest increase of 0.72 Pa s34.

Performance analysis of vitrified bond diamond grinding wheel

To meet the requirements of stereolithography 3D printing, extensive experimental exploration was conducted. The optimal formula for the vitrified bond diamond grinding wheel was determined to be 30 wt% diamond and 70 wt% vitrified bond34. Stereolithography 3D printing was employed to manufacture the samples. Table 4 presents the post-sintering shrinkage rate of the grinding wheel. Consistent with the conclusions mentioned above, the shrinkage rate in the thickness direction of the green body is the highest. The grinding wheel was mounted on the substrate and operated at a rotational speed of 2500 r/min (linear velocity of approximately 15 m/s). The feed rate of the grinding wheel was calculated to be approximately 5 μm/s. The heat treatment results of the green body are shown in Fig. 10a. The green body begins to decompose at approximately 100 °C, due to the presence of adsorbed water on its surface. An endothermic peak appears around 150 °C, corresponding to the decomposition of the resin TMP3EOTA (boiling point 157 °C). The most rapid thermal decomposition occurs between 300 and 500 °C, with the pyrolysis temperatures of HDDA and PEG being around 290 °C and 300 °C, respectively31–34. Above 500 °C, the resin within the green body is completely burned off, and the rate of mass change slows. When the temperature exceeds 700 °C, no further mass change occurs, indicating that sintering is complete. Slow debinding and sintering curves were developed based on DSC data. After extensive experimentation, the optimal performance of the grinding wheel was achieved at a sintering temperature of 680 °C34.

Overall, the performance of the vitrified bond diamond grinding wheel fabricated via stereolithography 3D printing is similar to that of the traditionally prepared grinding wheel46–48.

Prior to conducting the grinding experiments, the parallelism between the ground surface of the workpiece and the traverse direction of the grinding wheel was carefully verified. A precision leveling instrument was utilized to ensure accurate alignment, thereby eliminating any potential influence of surface unevenness or misalignment on the grinding process. Figure 10f presents the grinding temperature profile for machining SiC ceramics. Prior to grinding, at 500 s, the solid-structure grinding wheel lacked sufficient porosity, with only a few micropores formed through sintering, which hindered the circulation of coolant both within the grinding wheel and on the workpiece surface. As a result, the sintering temperature reached its peak, increasing the likelihood of burning the workpiece. In contrast, the gyroid TPMS porous structure, with its interconnected channels, facilitated better coolant flow, resulting in a lower grinding temperature compared to the solid-structure grinding wheel. As grinding time progressed, the grinding temperatures of all four groups of wheels increased. This rise in temperature was attributed to the increasing contact area between the grinding wheel and the workpiece, leading to greater frictional energy generation3. At 500 s of grinding, the temperature difference between the two wheels was most pronounced, with the gyroid porous grinding wheel demonstrating a 27% lower temperature than the solid-structure wheel, significantly reducing the risk of workpiece burning.

Conclusion

In this study, a vitrified bond and diamond slurry system with high solid content, low viscosity and good stability was studied and prepared for the preparation of grinding wheels. Finally, the grinding performance of the traditional grinding wheel and the 3D printed gyroid porous structure grinding wheel was compared. The main conclusions are as follows:

The viscosity of vitrified bond slurry decreases with the increase of HDDA ratio, and the curing layer thickness increases with the increase of TMP3EOTA content. When the content of HDDA is 67 wt%, the content of TMP3EOTA is 33 wt%, that is, TMP3EOTA is 67: 33, the performance of vitrified bond slurry is the best.

The viscosity of vitrified bond slurry decreases with the increase of HDDA ratio, and the curing layer thickness increases with the increase of TMP3EOTA content. When the content of HDDA is 67 wt%, the content of TMP3EOTA is 33 wt%, that is, TMP3EOTA is 67: 33, the performance of vitrified bond slurry is the best.

The effects of silane coupling agents KH550, KH560 and KH570 as modifiers on the slurry were explored. When KH560 is used as a modifier, the viscosity of the slurry is significantly lower than that of KH550 and KH570. When the addition amount of KH560 was 3.0 wt%, a high solid content of 74 wt% (about 55 vol%) was successfully prepared. FTIR spectra showed that the modifier was successfully attached to the vitrified bond particles.

Comparison of the effect of eight weeks of Rhythmic aerobic training and CX-WORX on cholesterol transport and inflammatory factors in obese women
Authors: Samira Sabetrasekh, Allahyar Arabmomeni, Jamshid Banaei Borojeni
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Obesity is associated with a wide range of complications and an increased risk of premature occurrence of physical problems, mental disorders, and some diseases. Doing sports activities along with other treatment strategies can be an effective solution to overcome this problem. Therefore, this study aimed to compare the effect of balanced aerobic exercise and CX-WORK on the expression of growth genes, metabolic biomarkers, and indicators of reverse cholesterol transport in obese women.MATERIALS AND METHODS:In this semi-experimental study, with a pre-test-post-test design and a control group, 45 individuals with an average age of 25.47 years and a BMI between 30 and 35 kg/m2 were purposefully selected among the obese women of Isfahan City and were randomly divided to three groups of 15 individuals; Balanced aerobic exercises, CX-WORK and control exercises. Rhythmic aerobic training protocol including eight weeks, rhythmic movements accompanied by music, and three sessions of 40 to 50 min per week was implemented incrementally. The CX-WORK exercise program was performed for eight weeks, three sessions a week and each session lasted 30 min. This program was done in six stages warming up, strength and endurance movements related to central muscles 1, standing endurance movements 1, standing endurance movements 2, strength and endurance movements related to central muscles 2, and strength and endurance movements related to central muscles 3. Blood sampling was conducted to estimate the study variables and to measure height, weight, and BMI index in two stages pre-test and post-test. To test the significance of group mean differences, one-way analysis of variance and Tukey’s post hoc test were used using SPSS software version 26 at a significance level of 0.05.RESULTS:The results of the study showed that in both experimental groups; The expression of GH, IGF-1, Follistatin, ABCG1, ABCG5, and ABCG8 genes increased significantly (P ≥ 0.05). In addition, a significant decrease was observed in both experimental groups in myostatin, IL-6, and CRP gene expression in obese women (P ≥ 0.05). In addition, although there was a difference between the effects of the two types of exercise on these variables, this difference was not significant (P ≤ 0.05).CONCLUSION:These results showed the beneficial effects of balanced aerobic exercise and CX-WORK on the expression of growth genes, metabolic biomarkers, and indicators of reverse cholesterol transport in obese women. Therefore, the use of these training methods is recommended as a suitable prevention and treatment method for obesity and improving the aforementioned indicators.

Introduction

Obesity is associated with a varied range of problems and an increased risk of premature onset of physical complications, mental disorders, and some diseases, leading to increased health problems in numerous physiological processes.[1] These problems can include various kinds of cardiovascular diseases, diabetes, high blood pressure, high cholesterol, liver disease, sleep apnea, and some cancers.[2] According to the World Health Organization (WHO), more than 1.9 billion adults are overweight or obese, of which about 300 million are obese.[3] In Iran, 28.6% of people are overweight, 10.8% are obese, and 3.4% are morbidly obese, and these statistics are still increasing.[4]

Wrong lifestyle habits are one of the most important causes of this condition. Consequently, it is essential to change lifestyle and find methods that prevent overweight and obesity. Although obesity is a changeable disorder through lifestyle changes, it is important to notice that early interventions are crucial to prevent the pathological changes observed in obese individuals.[5] Therefore, lifestyle changes such as physical activity should be considered as a primary intervention to minimize the synergistic effect of obesity on age-related conditions and other diseases.

Among the factors that improve in obese individuals with physical activity are reverse cholesterol transport indices and inflammatory factors. Lipid profile imbalance and increased cholesterol levels are some of the most important complications reported in obese people.[6] Reverse cholesterol transport (RCT) is a process in which extra cholesterol from peripheral tissues is returned to the liver for breakdown and excretion. This process is mediated by box-like membrane protein transporters ABC (ATP binding cassette transporter A1), including ABCG proteins (ABCG1, ABCG2, ABCG4, ABCG5, ABCG8).[7] These transport proteins, which use ATP energy to transport various substances across the membrane, play a prominent role in the RCT mechanism. These transporters are found in the membranes of most cells, including cells in the brain, lung, skeletal muscle, heart, testes, liver, spleen, thymus, ovary, stomach, kidney, adrenal gland, mitochondria, immune system, pancreas, peroxisome, placenta, liver, and intestine, and are responsible for transporting a variety of macromolecules across the membrane.[8] They reduce the absorption of herbal sterols and cholesterol by secreting them from enterocytes into the intestinal tract and excreting them from hepatocytes into bile, thereby contributing to the removal of extra cholesterol from the walls of coronary arteries and reducing blood cholesterol levels, thereby reducing the risk of atherosclerosis and heart attack. Therefore, they have been considered both as a new therapeutic target and for the development of drugs designed to remove cholesterol from arterial macrophages.[9] In this view, Patel et al. (2018),[10] in a review article, stated that the ABCG5 and ABCG8 genes reduce blood cholesterol by activating the reverse cholesterol process and play an important role in reducing serum cholesterol.

Studies have shown that exercise training has a positive effect on these protein transporters. For example, Bagheri et al. (2020),[11] examining the effect of resistance training and aerobic training on the expression of ABCG4, ABCG5, and ABCG8 genes in female athletes, stated that both training protocols significantly increased the expression of ABCG4, ABCG5, and ABCG8. Also, Ghanbari-Niaki et al. (2013),[12] in a study examining ABCG5 gene expression in response to exercise in rats, reported increased ABCG5 gene expression in the liver, small intestine, and kidney tissue. In addition, some studies have reported a link between obesity and inflammatory factors.[1314] Among the Inflammatory markers are Interlukine 6 (IL6) and C-reactive Protein (CRP). IL6 is a stimulator of acute phase inflammatory proteins, an activator of the hypothalamic-pituitary axis, a proinflammatory cytokine, and a stimulator of some diseases, especially cardiovascular and metabolic diseases. CRP has been studied in many studies as the most sensitive inflammatory index and independent predictor of cardiovascular diseases. These studies have reported that reducing these factors leads to increased health.[1516] The researches show that exercise reduces levels of inflammatory factors.[1718] Soheily et al.(2016)[19] in his study stated that resistance training reduces inflammatory factors such as IL6 and reduces fat mass in inactive, overweight women. Similar studies have reported that aerobic and resistance training reduces CRP levels in overweight individuals.[20] However, Molanouri Shamsi et al. (2011)[21] reported that resistance training causes an increase or no change in the levels of inflammatory factors. Although the importance and physiological mechanism of many of these responses is not yet well understood, the fact that these variables respond to various exercises and sports activities is of great importance. According to researchers, regular physical activity can be effective as a non-pharmacological and healthy treatment method in preventing and treating obesity and related disorders. In recent years, many training programs, including compound exercises, aerobic training programs, low and high-intensity interval training, Crossfit training, weight lifting, aerobic training, and Body pump training, have been presented by sports science researchers, but less attention has been paid to balanced aerobic exercises and CX-WORX. Balanced aerobic exercise improves the inflammatory, antioxidant, and lipid status of obese individuals. These exercises are a type of aerobic exercise with musical rhythm (up to 140 beats per minute) that is performed in preliminary chains of 4. In these movements, the pressure of the foot strike force is controlled because one foot is always on the ground. In addition to improving physical fitness, these exercises also increase energy and cheerfulness because of the accompaniment of music. It seems that these exercises are useful in reducing obesity and overweight by increasing the efficiency of the cardiorespiratory system, reducing inflammatory factors, increasing some factors affecting the lipolysis system. CX-WORX exercise is also one of the latest training methods that include strength movements such as crunches, planks, squats, etc., and is used to strengthen the core muscles of the body. These exercises are used to maximize strength and endurance, increase stability and stability, and reduce injury. They are also effective in burning fat and losing weight.[22] However, the effectiveness of this type of exercise on physiological and inflammatory indicators in obese individuals has not been fully established.

Therefore, with an emphasis on non-invasive therapeutic methods such as the use of physical activities to prevent and treat obesity, prevent inflammation and metabolic damaging factors, and considering the limitations and contradictions in the results of studies conducted on the effect of rhythmic aerobic exercises and CX-WORX on factors affecting reverse cholesterol transport and inflammatory factors in obese women, the aim of this was to compare the effect of rhythmic aerobic exercises and CX-WORX on the expression of genes effective in reverse cholesterol transport and inflammatory biomarkers in young obese women.

Material and Methods

Study design and setting

The study is a quasi-experimental and applied research that was conducted in 2024 on obese women aged 20 to 30 with a body mass index between 30 and 35 kg/m2 in Isfahan City who volunteered to participate in the study through a call on social media.

Study participants and sampling

From the statistical population, 45 people who met the inclusion criteria were purposefully selected and were divided into 3 groups of 15 people (balanced aerobic exercise, CX-WORX exercise, and control group) by systematic randomization. The statistical sample size was calculated based on G*Power software (version 3.1.9.2). The minimum sample size was 36 individuals (twelve people in each group), assuming an alpha of 5%, a beta of 80%, and an effect size of 0.30.[23] Because of the possibility of sample dropout at different stages of the research, 15 people were considered in each group. Obese participants were identified by calculating BMI and cholesterol levels. Women with a BMI between 30 and 35 kg/m2 and cholesterol levels above 200 mg/dL were selected for the study [Figure 1].

The conceptual model of the research

Inclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria included: obese women aged 20–30 years, with a body mass index between 30 and 35 kg/m2, cholesterol levels above 200 mg/dL, willingness to participate in the study based on ethical standards in research, having cognitive, visual, and hearing health, absence of acute and advanced neuromuscular, orthopedic, neurological, rheumatological, psychological diseases, diabetes, limb fractures, structural defects, and cardiovascular diseases that would prevent the exercise training considered in this study, and absence of use of dietary supplements and obesity control medications.

Exclusion criteria

These included: the absence of one of the characteristics mentioned in the acceptance criteria section, unwillingness to continue cooperating in the research, occurrence of injury during the research, use of sports supplements and medications that affect research variables, simultaneous participation in other sports activities, and absence from three consecutive sessions or four non-consecutive sessions during the research.

Data collection tools and techniques

One week before the start of the training protocols, all programs, potential benefits and risks, and the correct method of performing the exercises were explained to the participants in an explanatory session. During the research, all subjects were monitored by a doctor to prevent any possible risks. After completing the consent form for research participation, the steps for implementing the tests and practice protocols were communicated to all subjects. The examiner presented each test once to familiarize the subjects, and then all subjects participated in the pre-test phase. The results of the pre-test were recorded to determine the initial level of physical fitness of obese women. The subjects were examined by a doctor before starting the exercises and their information was recorded in the file. Then, the subjects were randomly assigned to two experimental groups and a control group. The subjects were fully satisfied with the tests and exercise program and were encouraged to perform the movements completely. In addition, in the first session, they were consulted by a nutritionist, and their diet plan was controlled and standardized to prevent diet interference in the research results. In addition, the control group was asked to maintain their usual activities and treatment methods during the study. Blood sampling, height, weight, body fat percentage, and BMI were measured in two stages: pre-test and post-test. The subjects were forbidden from performing any physical activity 48 h before the test, and their meal (breakfast) was the same as before the test. Thus, information related to the test results was collected and statistically analyzed on two different occasions, at the beginning and end of the training period.

Method for measuring height, weight, fat percentage, and body mass index (BMI)

A German height gauge, SECA model 210, with an accuracy of 3 mm was used to measure height. The subject stood with his bare feet against a height measuring tape attached to the wall in such a way that, first, her body weight was distributed equally on both feet, and second, her head, torso, and legs were in line, and the backs of his legs, buttocks, and head touched the wall. Then, using a ruler placed on the subject’s head, the subject’s height was measured and recorded in centimeters while exhaling. In addition, a digital scale, KEEP FIT Model 6657, made in China, was used to measure weight. Subjects were placed on a medical scale wearing light clothing and without shoes, with their weight distributed evenly across both feet. Then, the individual’s body weight was recorded with an accuracy of 0.1 kg.

To calculate body fat percentage, first, the subcutaneous fat thickness at three points on the subjects’ buttocks, thighs, and upper back was measured using calipers (Baseline, made in the United States), and then the body fat percentage was calculated using the formula. In addition, the fat percentage of each point was rotationally measured three times.[24]

Also, the subjects’ BMI was determined based on height and weight measurements, and BMI was calculated by dividing weight in kilograms by the square of height in meters squared.

Blood sampling method

Three days before the start of the research project, nutritional and behavioral tips for blood sampling in the pre-test and post-test (48 h after the last training session) were explained. Blood sampling was performed by a laboratory scientist between 8 and 9 AM and approximately 10 cc of blood was taken from the subjects’ left brachial vein.

Exercise protocols

A. Balanced Aerobic Exercise Protocol

The balanced aerobic exercise protocol included eight weeks of rhythmic movements accompanied by music in three 40- to 50-min sessions per week, with three warm-up sections (10 min), progressive aerobic movements in a standing position (30 min), and final movements of returning to the starting position in a sitting position (10 min). Exercise intensity was estimated by calculating the maximum heart rate using a Polar heart rate monitor (age-220 maximum heart rate formula and target heart rate percentage per session from the Karvonen formula). Training began at 50% to 60% of maximum heart rate in the first week and reached 75% to 85% of maximum heart rate in the final sessions.[25]

B. CX-WORX Training Protocol

The CX-WORX exercise program protocol was performed for 8 weeks, three sessions per week, for 30 min per session. Following previous studies, a 10-min warm-up exercise program was performed before each training session. Then, the training program was carried out with the following steps, and at the end of each training session, a cool-down training program was performed.

The polymerase chain reaction was performed using a BIO-RAD (C1000 TM Thermal Cycler) in 96-well plates. The researcher followed a time-temperature schedule of the PCR steps. The Pfaffle method was used to measure the number of target and reference gene copies.

To analyze the data, descriptive statistics methods were used to calculate central and dispersion indices, and the data were reported based on the mean and standard deviation. Ethical consideration

In addition, subjects were assured that their personal information would be kept confidential by the researchers and would ultimately be reported in general terms. They were also given the option to withdraw at any stage of the training if they did not wish to continue their cooperation. In addition, at the end of this session, all subjects were given the relevant forms to consciously and voluntarily declare their willingness to participate in the study. All research steps were approved by the Ethics Committee of Islamic Azad University, Najafabad Branch, with the ethics code IR.IAU.NAJAFABAD.REC.1402.216.

Discussion

The results of the present study showed that in both experimental groups (balanced aerobic exercise and CX-WORK); the expression of ABCG1, ABCG5, and ABCG8 genes increased significantly (P ≥ 0.05). Also, a significant decrease in the expression of IL-6 and CRP genes was observed in both experimental groups in obese women (P ≤ 0.05). In addition, although there was a difference between the effects of the two types of exercise on these variables, this difference was not significant (P ≥ 0.05). Consistent with the results of this study, most studies conducted in this field have reported increased expression of ABCG1, ABCG5, and ABCG8 genes following exercise training. For example; Moosavi et al. (2021),[27] Rahmati-Ahmadabad (2019),[28] Hoang et al. (2009),[29] Butcher et al. (2008)[30] confirmed these finding. In addition, consistent with these results, Sadeghi-Fazel et al. (2022),[31] Bagheri et al. (2020),[11] and Ghanbari-Niaki et al. (2014)[32] reported similar results. However, these results were not consistent with the results of the studies of Meissner et al. (2010)[33] and Ngo Sock et al. (2014).[34] The reason for the lack of similarity in the results of these studies was the type of subject, methodology, and exercise protocol. Bagheri et al. (2020)[11] studied the effect of high-intensity resistance training and aerobic exercise on the expression of ABCG4, ABCG5, and ABCG8 genes in female athletes. The results of this study showed that both exercise protocols significantly increased the expression of ABCG4, ABCG5, and ABCG8 in the subjects, and no significant difference was observed in the expression of these genes between the experimental groups. In addition, Jalali et al. (2019),[35] in a study investigating the effect of high-intensity interval training (HIT) and continuous training on the expression of ABCG5 and ABCG8 genes in Wistar rats after a high-fat diet, reported an increase in the expression of ABCG5 and ABCG8 genes after training. In this regard, Rashidlamir (2012) investigated the effect of two types of single-session training (aerobic and resistance training) on the expression of the ABCG1 gene in peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMN) in female athletes. This researcher showed that the expression of PBMN and ABCG1m-RNA increased significantly following a single-session training session in both aerobic and resistance training groups, but the increase was greater in the resistance training group than in the endurance training group.[36] Furthermore, Patel et al. (2018),[10] in a review article, stated that the ABCG5 and ABCG8 genes play an important and fundamental role in reducing cholesterol. These two genes reduce blood cholesterol by activating the reverse cholesterol process and can be important factors in reducing diseases caused by increased blood lipids, such as hyperlipidemia and cardiovascular disease.

Based on the findings of this study and the results of the aforementioned studies, it can be concluded that various exercise exercises can improve the function of reverse cholesterol transport by increasing the expression of genes affecting reverse cholesterol transport (ABCG1, ABCG5, and ABCG8) in the body’s cells, thereby promoting health, reducing fat, and improving the cardiovascular system.

One of the most important complications of obesity is increased cholesterol. Reduced expression and impaired function of cholesterol reverse transporter factors are some of the main causes of this problem, so it is necessary to directly target the mechanisms that impair ABC transporters or affect the actual steps in cellular pathways.[37] ABCGs play an important role in this process (reverse cholesterol transport). The useful effects of these transporters on cholesterol homeostasis have made them important therapeutic targets for the prevention of obesity.[38] The results of most studies have shown that exercise increases the expression of cholesterol transporters, which can therefore affect reducing blood cholesterol.[32] In addition to the positive role of physical activity in reducing cholesterol, triglycerides, and VLDL-C, and various lipoprotein characteristics such as the size, number, and chemical composition of lipoproteins, the most important role of exercise is to affect HDL-C and LDL-C levels. The importance of HDL-C is due to the role it plays in reverse cholesterol transport. HDL-C levels are largely dependent on the transporter ABCG1, which plays a key role in increasing the HDL-C molecule. ABCG1 appears to play a dual role in the reverse cholesterol transport process. First, in macrophages, it causes cholesterol to be released from the cells of the vascular wall, and second, in the liver and intestine, ABCG1 plays a key role in the formation of HDL.[39] Studies have shown that exercise increases ABCG1 expression. For example, Hoang et al. (2009)[29] investigated the effect of lifestyle on ABCG1 gene expression and reported that people who lead active lives had higher ABCG1 gene expression than sedentary people. Butcher et al. (2008)[30] also studied the effect of low-intensity walking on ABCG1 gene expression. The results showed a 3.46-fold increase in ABCG1 gene expression in lymphocytes. The mechanism by which exercise increases the expression of ABCG genes in leukocytes and macrophages remains unknown to this day, but the regulatory properties of fatty acids are probably exerted by Peroxisome Proliferator-Activated Receptors (PPARs). It has been found that PPARs, such as Liver X Receptor (LXR) and Retinoid X Receptor (RXR), are nuclear receptors that regulate the expression of genes controlling lipid and glucose metabolism. Three isoforms of PPARs (α, β, γ) are expressed in metabolic tissues including the heart, liver, skeletal muscle, and kidney, as well as arterial wall cells such as monocytes and macrophages.[2728] PPARα activity reduces cholesterol by stimulating genes involved in lipid metabolism, including increasing HDL-C levels, accelerating cholesterol flow from peripheral cells, increasing its uptake from the liver, and increasing the expression of hepatic LXR/RXR receptors.[9] Numerous studies have examined the effect of exercise on the stimulation and expression of the above factors and have reported the positive effect of exercise training on these factors.[4041] However, Fatone et al. (2010)[42] reported that combined training (aerobic exercise at 55%–70% of maximal oxygen consumption and circuit resistance training at an intensity of -6-80% of maximal power) significantly increased PPAR-α after six and twelve months. Butcher et al. (2008)[30] also reported that activating the PPARγ ligand leads to the initial activation of LXR, and eventually, LXR upregulates and increases the expression of the ABCG1 transporter gene, all of which leads to an increase in reverse cholesterol transport. It has also been reported that exercise, by increasing LDL-c oxidation, leads to activation and upregulation of transcription factors PPAR-γ and LXRα, thereby increasing the genes that transport cholesterol to HDL-c.[43] Additionally, in another study, Hosseini et al. (2017),[44] by examining the effect of two types of aerobic exercise and high-intensity exercise on the expression of ABCG5, ABCG4, and ABCG8 genes in female athletes, showed that one session of high-intensity aerobic exercise caused the expression of genes involved in the cholesterol transport process compared to the control group. The role of increased stimulation of these receptors and cholesterol reduction by the ABCG family has been investigated in many articles.[4546]

Therefore, the balanced aerobic exercises and CX-WORK performed in this study, consistent with the results of the above studies, increase the expression of genes affecting RCT, including ABCG8/5/1, by improving the lipid profile increasing HDL levels and decreasing LDL.

In addition, the results of the present study showed that eight weeks of aerobic exercise and CX-WORK significantly reduced the expression of IL-6 and CRP genes in obese women. Moreover, the effects of these two training methods on these two genes were similar. These findings were consistent with the results of studies by Malandish et al. (2023),[47] Nash et al. (2023),[48] Espinoza-Salinas et al. (2022),[49] Pahlevani et al. (2023),[50] and Shakiba et al. (2018),[51] but were inconsistent with the results of studies by Collins et al. (2023),[52] Kouchaksaraei, et al. (2022),[53] and Nicklas et al. (2004).[54] Differences in factors such as training methods, samples, methodology, and measurement methods were among the reasons for the inconsistency of the results of this study with other studies.

Malandish et al. (2023),[47] in a review article examining the effects of various types of exercise training, including endurance, resistance, and combined training, on inflammatory factors, reported that exercise-related changes improve markers of inflammation and vascular adhesion. Espinoza-Salinas et al. (2022),[49] in a study examining the effect of aerobic exercise on inflammatory and metabolic parameters in obese individuals, showed that four types of aerobic exercise reduce leptin, insulin resistance, IL6, and TNFa.[49] Liu et al. (2022),[55] in a meta-analysis article examining the effect of resistance training on IL6 and CRP levels in middle-aged and elderly individuals, reported that resistance training reduces IL6 levels, but has little effect on CRP. In addition, Pandit (2022)[56] showed in a study that 20 weeks of exercise training reduced body mass index, insulin resistance, and serum CRP levels. In the same context, Kemalasari et al. (2022),[57] in a review article, reported that reducing C-reactive protein causes a decrease in fat mass. In addition, Khalafi et al. (2021),[58] in a review study of 540 articles, showed that resistance training reduces hs-CRP levels by affecting cellular pathways and the expression of various gene factors. They also reported that one of the factors that causes the decrease in hs-CRP levels after resistance training is the decrease in fat mass. Shakiba et al. (2018)[51] showed a decrease in plasma levels of IL-6, TNFa, and CRP following eight weeks of continuous and intermittent exercise training. Kazemi et al. (2015)[59] also reported a decrease in TNF-a and IL-6 levels in a study investigating the effect of HIT on plasma levels of TNF-a, IL-6, and lipid profile of overweight children. However, Collins et al.[52] (2023), in an article examining the effect of continuous and interval training with low and high intensities on inflammatory factors, reported that endurance cycling exercises for 30 minutes at an intensity of 60% of maximum oxygen consumption and interval training exercises at an intensity of 90% of maximum oxygen consumption increased IL-1a levels, but had no effect on plasma levels of IL-6 and TNFa. IL-6 is an immune system factor that is released from the muscle during exercise and causes inflammatory, pro-inflammatory, and in some cases, anti-inflammatory responses, and this range of changes varies depending on the type, volume, and intensity of exercise.[60] Research has shown that IL-6 increases more than other cytokines due to exercise, but its biological effects are sometimes not the same.[61] Increased IL-6 levels lead to the expression of inflammatory and catabolic factors in various cells of the body, which inhibit the proliferation and differentiation of skeletal and cardiac muscle cells and are considered one of the signs of atrophy and are considered a negative point for the body, especially in obese people. Therefore, in inflammatory conditions of obesity and disease, IL-6 increases in parallel with fat, indicating an increase in inflammatory factors in the body.[62] However, serum levels of IL-6 and CRP may decrease with weight and fat mass reduction.[49] One of the reasons for the decrease in IL6 levels following exercise can be attributed to energy adaptations. Because IL6 acts as an energy sensor, energy availability during exercise, especially endurance exercise, results in a lack of stimulation of IL6 activity.[63] Similarly, as IL-6 levels decrease, CRP levels also decrease. In addition, a possible mediator of the decrease in C-reactive protein levels could be a decrease in fat mass and an increase in muscle mass.[50]

Balanced aerobic exercises are beneficial in reducing obesity and overweight by increasing the activity of the cardiorespiratory system, reducing inflammatory factors, and increasing some factors affecting the lipolysis system, and improving the inflammatory, antioxidant, and lipid status of obese individuals. CX-WORX exercises have also been suggested as an effective method for improving core muscle strength and endurance and upper extremity function. Performing these exercises, which engage the core area of the body, leads to a reduction in fat in this area and ultimately a reduction in abdominal obesity. Additionally, aerobic exercise and CX-WORX also reduce body fat stores by increasing the stimulation of lipolytic enzymes. This reduction in body fat likely contributes to the reduction in IL-6 and CRP levels.

Limitations and recommendations

The subjects of this study were all young obese women, so caution should be exercised in generalizing the results to other genders and ages. In addition, this study examined some genes affecting reverse cholesterol transport and inflammation in obese women, and it was not possible for researchers to examine other factors such as TNFa, LXR, and RXR. While the study duration was 8 weeks, longer training periods may be more effective. It is recommended that obese women, doctors, nurses, health centers, and fitness club trainers use balanced aerobic exercise and CX-WORX to prevent and treat obesity and improve reverse cholesterol transport and inflammatory factors. It is also suggested that future research should study other exercise protocols in obese women and that further studies be conducted on other factors and proteins involved in reverse cholesterol transport processes and inflammatory factors.

Conclusion

The results of this study showed that in both experimental groups (balanced aerobic exercise and CX-WORX); the expression of ABCG1, ABCG5, and ABCG8 genes increased significantly (P ≤ 0.05). In addition, a significant decrease in the expression of IL-6 and CRP genes was observed in both experimental groups in obese women (P ≤ 0.05). In addition, although there was a difference between the effects of the two types of exercise on these variables, this difference was not significant (P ≥ 0.05). These two training protocols reduced fat mass, increased muscle mass, increased stimulation of lipolytic enzymes, and had a positive effect on HDL-C and LDL-C levels, which play an important role in reverse cholesterol transport. They increased the expression and improved the function of reverse cholesterol transport factors (ABCG1, ABCG5, and ABCG8), and reduced inflammatory factors in obese women. Therefore, balanced aerobic exercise and CX-WORX can play an effective role in preventing, treating, and reducing complications of obesity by creating positive changes in inflammatory biomarkers and genes effective in reverse cholesterol transport. Therefore, changing lifestyles by institutionalizing physical activity and implementing regular exercise such as aerobic exercise and CX-WORX, which are popular with the public, especially women, can play a very important role in reducing overweight and obesity and ultimately the general health and hygiene of the community.

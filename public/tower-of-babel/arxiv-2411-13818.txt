Proof of Merca’s stronger conjecture on truncated Jacobi triple product series
Xiangyu Ding

Abstract




Abstract.
In the study of theta series and partition functions, Andrews and Merca, Guo and Zeng independently conjectured that a truncated Jacobi triple product series has nonnegative coefficients. This conjecture was proved analytically by Mao and combinatorially by Yee. In 2021, Merca proposed a stronger version of the conjecture, that is, for positive integers
1\leq S<R
with
k\geq 1
, the coefficient of
q^{n}
in
the theta series

\frac{(-1)^{k}\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}q^{Rj(j+1)/2}\left(q^{-Sj}-q^{(j+1)S}\right)}{\left(q^{S},q^{R-S};q^{R}\right)_{\infty}}

is nonnegative. Recently, some very special cases of this conjecture have been proved and studied. For any given
R,S
and
k
, we take
s=S/(S,R),r=R/(S,R)
which are coprime, equivalently. In this paper, we confirm Merca’s stronger conjecture for sufficiently large
n
. Furthermore, for given
r,s
and
k
, we provide a systematic method to determine an integer
N(r,s,k)
such that Merca’s stronger conjecture holds for
n\geq N(r,s,k)
. More precisely, we decompose the infinite product in the denominator of the above theta series into two parts, one of which can be interpreted as the generating function of partitions with certain restricted parts and the other is a nonmodular infinite product.
We derive the general upper and lower bounds for the coefficients of these two parts by using the partition theoretical method and the circle method, respectively. Further multiplying the partition part by the numerator of the theta series and considering the convolution with the nonmodular infinite product, we obtain the constant
N(r,s,k)
and confirm Merca’s stronger conjecture when
n\geq N(r,s,k)
. Moreover, we also show that when
k
is sufficiently large, this conjecture holds directly for any
n\geq 0
.

Keywords:
truncated theta series; partition function; Jacobi triple product series; nonmodular infinite product; circle method

AMS Classification:
05A17, 33D15, 11P55, 11P82

Introduction

In 2012, Andrews and Merca investigated a truncated form of Euler’s pentagonal number theorem and obtained the following result
[
3
, Lemma 1.2]
:

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{j=-k}^{k-1}(-1)^{j}q^{j(3j+1)/2}=(-1)^{k-1}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{\binom{k}{2}+(k+1)n}}{(q;q)_{n}}{n-1\brack k-1}.

(1.1)

The truncated sum on the left hand side can be written in the form of

\sum_{j=-k-\tau}^{k}(-1)^{j}q^{aj^{2}+bj}

by replacing
k
with
k+1
, where
a\in\mathbb{Q}^{+},b\in\mathbb{Q}
and
\tau\in\{0,1\}
. Such truncated sums can be traced back to Shanks’ version
[
35
, (2)]
of Euler’s pentagonal number theorem,

\sum_{j=-k}^{k}(-1)^{j}q^{j(3j+1)/2}=\sum_{n=0}^{k}\frac{(-1)^{n}(q;q)_{k}q^{\binom{n}{2}+(k+1)n}}{(q;q)_{n}}.

Warnaar also studied the case of
\sum_{j=-k-\tau}^{k}(-1)^{j}q^{j(5j+c)/2}
in
[
38
, Theorem 1.1]
with
c\in\{1,3\}
, for which Chapman
[
11
]
provided a combinatorial proof.

One can see that the coefficient of
q^{n}
on the right hand side of (
1.1
) is nonnegative, for which Andrews and Merca also gave a corresponding partition interpretation, which also leads to

\displaystyle(-1)^{k-1}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{j}\big(p(n-j(3j+1)/2)-p(n-j(3j+5)/2-1)\big)\geq 0.

Later, Guo and Zeng studied a class of truncated forms of Gauss’ triangular and square numbers series in
[
20
]
, from which they also derive the recurrence relations for partition functions. Since then the truncated forms for theta series have attracted more and more attentions.

In 2018, Andrews and Merca
[
4
]
obtained the unified truncated forms for Euler’s pentagonal number theorem, Gauss’s theta series for triangular and square numbers, which are further generalized by Xia, Yee and Zhao in 2022
[
39
]
. We can easily notice that these three classical theta series all arise from the Jacobi triple product series. Actually, Andrews and Merca
[
3
]
, and Guo and Zeng
[
20
]
proposed the following conjecture for a truncated Jacobi triple product series, independently.

Conjecture 1.1
.

For positive integers
k,R,S
with
1\leq S<R/2
, the coefficient of
q^{n}
with
n\geq 1
in

\displaystyle(-1)^{k-1}\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{j}q^{Rj(j+1)/2-Sj}\left(1-q^{(2j+1)S}\right)}{\left(q^{S},q^{R-S},q^{R};q^{R}\right)_{\infty}}

(1.2)

is nonnegative.

The conjecture was proved analytically by Mao
[
28
]
and combinatorially by Yee
[
41
]
in 2015. By applying the theory of Bailey pairs and Liu’s expansion formula
[
26
]
for
q
-series, Wang and Yee
[
37
]
reproved this conjecture.
Recently, by utilizing the iteration of Bailey’s methods, we
[
16
]
obtained a general expression of (
1.2
) in the form of multiple sums and the results can reduce to Andrews-Gordon and Bressoud type identities.

In 2021, Merca
[
29
,
30
]
proposed a stronger version of Conjecture
1.1
as follows.

Conjecture 1.2
.

For positive integers
1\leq S<R
with
k\geq 1
, the theta series

\displaystyle(-1)^{k}\frac{\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}q^{Rj(j+1)/2}\left(q^{-Sj}-q^{(j+1)S}\right)}{\left(q^{S},q^{R-S};q^{R}\right)_{\infty}}

(1.3)

has nonnegative coefficients.

Ballantine and Feigon
[
7
]
proved combinatorially for the cases when
k\in\{1,2,3\}
.
We also confirmed the cases when
R=3S
for
S\geq 1
in
[
16
]
by using the partition theoretical method. For the related works, see also Yao
[
40
]
, Ballantine and Feigon
[
7
]
, and Zhou
[
42
]
.

Notice that
S
and
R-S
are symmetric in our method. So without losing of generality, by replacing
q^{(R,S)}
with
q
, we can assume that
1\leq S<R/2
and
R,\ S
are coprime. In addition, the case of
R=2S
is simpler and it will be proved as Corollary
4.4
in Section
4
. Then Conjecture
1.2
can be restated equivalently as follows.

Conjecture 1.3
.

For positive integers
1\leq s<r/2
,
(r,s)=1
and
k\geq 1
, the coefficient of
q^{n}
in

\displaystyle(-1)^{k}\frac{\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}q^{rj(j+1)/2}\left(q^{-sj}-q^{(j+1)s}\right)}{\left(q^{s},q^{r-s};q^{r}\right)_{\infty}}

(1.4)

is nonnegative.

In this paper, we are mainly devoted to determine a nonnegative integer constant
N(r,s,k)
respected to
r,s,k
such that for such given
r,s
and
k
, Merca’s stronger conjecture holds for
n\geq N(r,s,k)
.

Theorem 1.4
.

For positive coprime integers
r
and
s
with
1\leq s<r/2
and
k\geq 1
, there exists a constant
N(r,s,k)
relative to
r,s
and
k
, such that when
n\geq N(r,s,k)
, the coefficient of
q^{n}
in

\displaystyle(-1)^{k}\frac{\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}q^{rj(j+1)/2}\left(q^{-sj}-q^{(j+1)s}\right)}{\left(q^{s},q^{r-s};q^{r}\right)_{\infty}}

(1.5)

is nonnegative.

Actually, we not only certify the existence of such constant
N
, but also provide a systematic method to calculate it precisely for given
r,s
and
k
.

To do so, we first decompose the denominator of the above theta series into two parts as follows,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(q^{s};q^{r})_{\alpha}(q^{r-s};q^{r})_{\beta}}\cdot\frac{1}{\left(q^{\alpha r+s},q^{(\beta+1)r-s};q^{r}\right)_{\infty}},

(1.6)

where
(\alpha,\beta)
is chosen to be one of the elements in
\{(2,2),(1,2),(2,1)\}
under different conditions. The denominator of the second part is a nonmodular infinite product.
Firstly, by using the residue theorem and the involutions between partitions, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 1.5
.

For positive integers
1\leq s<r/2
,
(s,r-s,r+s,2r-s)=1
and
k\geq 1
, the coefficient of
q^{n}
in

(-1)^{k}\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}q^{r(j^{2}+(2k+1)j)/2-sj}(1-q^{2js+(2k+1)s})}{(1-q^{s})(1-q^{r-s})(1-q^{r+s})(1-q^{2r-s})},

(1.7)

is positive when

\displaystyle n\geq\left(2r^{2}\sqrt[3]{{s}/{k}}+k\right)\left(4r^{3}\sqrt[3]{{s}/{k}}-r+2s\right).

(1.8)

Consequently, when
k
is large enough to make the right hand side of (
1.8
) negative, that is to say (
1.7
) has nonnegative coefficient for
n\geq 0
, which directly deduce to Conjecture
1.3
holding for
n\geq 0
. This leads to the following result.

Corollary 1.6
.

When
(s,r-s,r+s,2r-s)=1
,
1\leq s<r/2
and

\displaystyle k\geq\frac{64r^{9}s}{(r-2s)^{3}},

the theta series (
1.7
) has nonnegative coefficients for
n\geq 0
, and thereby Conjecture
1.3
holds for such
k
.

When
(s,r-s,r+s,2r-s)\neq 1
, we obtain the following two results.

Theorem 1.7
.

If
2\mid s
,
1\leq s<r/2
and
k\geq 1
, then the coefficient of
q^{n}
in

\displaystyle(-1)^{k}\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}q^{r(j^{2}+(2k+1)j)/2-sj}(1-q^{2js+(2k+1)s})}{(1-q^{s})(1-q^{r-s})(1-q^{r+s})}

is positive if
n\geq(z_{0}+k)(2z_{0}r-r+2s)
, where
z_{0}
is the maximum zero of certain polynomials in
p
as given in Section
4
.

Theorem 1.8
.

If
2\nmid s
,
1\leq s<r/2
and
k\geq 1
, then the coefficient of
q^{n}
in

\displaystyle(-1)^{k}\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}q^{r(j^{2}+(2k+1)j)/2-sj}(1-q^{2js+(2k+1)s})}{(1-q^{s})(1-q^{r-s})(1-q^{2r-s})}

is positive if
n\geq(z_{0}+k)(2z_{0}r-r+2s)
, where
z_{0}
is the maximum zero of certain polynomials in
p
as given in Section
4
.

Similar to Corollary
1.6
, from Theorem
1.7
and Theorem
1.8
we also derive the following results, the details can be seen in Section
6
.

Corollary 1.9
.

When
2\mid s
,
1\leq s<r/2
and

\displaystyle k>\frac{4r^{3}s-2s(r-2s)}{r(r-2s)},

the theta series in Theorem
1.7
has nonnegative coefficients for
n\geq 0
.

When
2\nmid s
,
1\leq s<r/2
and

\displaystyle k>\frac{8r^{3}s-2s(r-2s)}{r(r-2s)},

the theta series in Theorem
1.8
has nonnegative coefficients for
n\geq 0
. Moreover, Conjecture
1.3
holds for the above
k
’s.

Secondly, we observe that the second part on the right hand side of (
1.6
) is a nonmodular infinite product. The work on the asymptotic properties of nonmodular infinite products was first utilized by Lehner
[
25
]
and Livingood

]
and developed by Grosswald
[

For positive integers

and

\displaystyle G_{a,M}(q)=\frac{1}{(q^{2M+a},q^{3M-a};q^{M})_{\infty}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}g_{a,M}(n)q

\displaystyle\Phi_{a,M}=\log\frac{1}{(q^{a};q^{M})_{\infty}},\quad\Psi_{a,M}=\log\frac{1}{(q^{2M+a};q^{M})_{\infty}}.

Theorem 1.10

.

Let

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(q^{a},q^{M-a};q^{M})_{\infty}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}g_{1}(n)q^{n}.

When

is sufficiently large,

\displaystyle g_{1}(n)\sim\frac{1}{4\sin\frac{a\pi}{M}}\left(3M\right)^{-\frac{1}{4}}n^{-\frac{3}{4}}\mathrm{exp}\left(2\pi\sqrt{\frac{n}{3M}}\right).

By using the circle method, we further extend Chern’s idea to derive the following expression for the nonmodular infinite product
\Psi_{a,M}(q)+\Psi_{M-a,M}(q)
.

.

Let

be a sufficiently large positive number, and

Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, we adopt standard notations and terminologies for
q
-series, see, for example,
[
18
]
. We assume that
|q|<1
.
The
q
-shifted factorial is denoted by

(a)_{n}=(a;q)_{n}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{\it if $n=0$},\\[5.69054pt]
(1-a)(1-aq)\cdots(1-aq^{n-1}),&\text{\it if $n\geq 1$}.\end{cases}

We also use the notation

(a)_{\infty}=(a;q)_{\infty}=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}(1-aq^{n}).

There are more compact notations for the multiple
q
-shifted factorials

\displaystyle(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{m};q)_{n}

\displaystyle=(a_{1};q)_{n}(a_{2};q)_{n}\cdots(a_{m};q)_{n},

\displaystyle(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{m};q)_{\infty}

\displaystyle=(a_{1};q)_{\infty}(a_{2};q)_{\infty}\cdots(a_{m};q)_{\infty}.

The well-known Jacobi triple product identity is given by
[
18
, II.28]

\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}q^{k\choose 2}a^{k}=(a,q/a,q;q)_{\infty}.

(2.1)

Truncated theta series are closely related to integer partitions. Recall that a partition of a nonnegative integer
n
is a finite nonincreasing sequence of positive integers
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{k}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}=n
, see, for example,
[
2
]
. Let
p(n)
denote the number of integer partitions of
n
with
p(0)=1
, which has the generating function

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p(n)q^{n}=\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}}.

We then reveal the following important result given by Polya and Szegö.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
32
, Problem 27.1]
)
.

Let
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{\ell}
be positive integers with
(a_{i},a_{j})=1
when
i\neq j
, then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-q^{a_{1}})(1-q^{a_{2}})\cdots(1-q^{a_{\ell}})}=R\Big(\frac{1}{1-q}\Big)+\frac{S(q)}{1-q^{a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{\ell}}},

(2.2)

where
R(x)
and
S(x)
are polynomials of
x
with degree
\ell-1
and smaller than
a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{\ell}
, respectively.

Let
p_{\ell}(n)
be the number of partitions with coprime numbers
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{\ell}
as parts, then the generating function of
p_{\ell}(n)
can be stated as follows

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p_{\ell}(n)=\frac{1}{(1-q^{a_{1}})(1-q^{a_{2}})\cdots(1-q^{a_{\ell}})}.

(2.3)

By employing Lemma
2.1
with
\ell=2
, it leads to the following result.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
32
, Problem 26]
)
.

We have

p_{2}(n)=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{a_{1}a_{2}}\right\rfloor\text{ or }\ p_{2}(n)=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{a_{1}a_{2}}\right\rfloor+1,

when
\lfloor a\rfloor
denotes the greatest integer no more than

7
Concluding remarks

For the partition functions as given in Theorem
1.5
, we can further consider the following more general form

\displaystyle(-1)^{k}\frac{\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}q^{rj(j+1)/2}\left(q^{-sj}-q^{(j+1)s}\right)}{\left(q^{s};q^{r})_{\ell_{1}}(q^{r-s};q^{r}\right)_{\ell_{2}}},

(7.1)

where
r
and
s
are coprime positive integers such that
1\leq s<r/2
,
k\geq 1,\ \ell_{1}\geq 0,\ \ell_{2}\geq 0
and denote
\ell=\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}
. Then by dividing these
\ell
integers
\{ir+s\}_{0\leq i<\ell_{1}}
and
\{(j+1)r-s\}_{0\leq j<\ell_{2}}
into groups of coprime numbers and following the similar discussions based on Lemma
2.1
and the circle method, one can try to show the nonnegativity of the coefficient of
q^{n}
.

Moreover, employing our method, one may study the following truncated quintuple product identity, which was also investigate by Chan, Ho and Mao in
[
10
]
:

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{j=-k}^{k}(-1)^{j}q^{Rj(3j+1)}\left(q^{3jS}-q^{-(3j+1)S}\right)}{\left(q^{-S},q^{R+S},q^{R};q^{R}\right)_{\infty}\left(q^{R-2S},q^{R+2S};q^{2R}\right)_{\infty}}.

At last, we proposed the following generalized truncated version for Conjecture
1.1
.

Conjecture 7.1
.

For positive integers
1\leq S<R
with
k>\ell\geq 1
and
k\geq 4
, there exists an integer
N
such that the theta series

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k-1}\left(\sum_{j=-k+1}^{k}-\sum_{j=-\ell+1}^{\ell}\right)(-1)^{j}q^{Rj(j+1)/2+Sj}}{\left(q^{S},q^{R-S},q^{R};q^{R}\right)_{\infty}}

(7.2)

has nonnegative coefficients when
n\geq N
.

The above conjecture is equivalent to the following inequality for the partition function
P_{R,S}(n)
, where
P_{R,S}(n)
denotes the number of partition of
n
with parts congruent to
\ \pm S
or
R
modulo
R
.

Conjecture 7.2
.

Under the same conditions as given in Conjecture
7.1
, we have that for
n\geq N

\displaystyle(-1)^{k-1}\bigg(\sum_{j=-k+1}^{-\ell}+\sum_{j=\ell+1}^{k}\bigg)(-1)^{j}P_{R,S}\Big(n-\left(Rj(j+1)/2+Sj\right)\Big)\geq 0.

For Conjecture
7.1
, if
k-\ell\equiv 1\ (\bmod\ 2)
, then
N=0
. At this time, the conjecture is a direct corollary of Conjecture
1.1
and we have the following theorem.

Theorem 7.3
.

When
k-\ell\equiv 1\ (\bmod\ 2)
, we have

\displaystyle(-1)^{\ell}\bigg(\sum_{j=-k+1}^{-\ell+1}+\sum_{j=\ell}^{k}\bigg)(-1)^{j}P_{R,S}\big(n-(Rj(j+1)/2+Sj)\big)\geq 0.

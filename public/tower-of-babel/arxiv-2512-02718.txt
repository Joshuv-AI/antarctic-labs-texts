Irreduciblity of certain 𝔰 ​ l ^ 2 \widehat{\mathfrak{s}l}_{2} –modules of Wakimoto type
Dražen Adamović, Veronika Pedić Tomić

Abstract
. We investigate the irreducible smooth 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ^ 2 \widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2} –modules recently constructed in [ 13 ] , and demonstrate that these modules admit a Wakimoto-type realization at both critical and non-critical levels. In the critical level case, we identify simple quotients of these modules with the Wakimoto modules whose irreducibility was already established in [ 1 , 2 ] . We also generalize some Wakimoto modules constructed in [ 4 ] and identify them as generalized Whittaker modules.

The Wakimoto modules are representations of affine Kac–Moody Lie algebras.

Wakimoto modules were also used for a construction of positive energy modules (cf.

2.1.
Affine vertex algebra

Let us first recall some background on affine Lie algebras and vertex algebras following

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra over
\mathbb{C}
, and let
(\cdot,\cdot)

{\mathfrak{g}}

We fix a triangular decomposition

{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{n}}_{-}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}_{+}.

The affine Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
associated with
{\mathfrak{g}}
is defined by

\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]\oplus\mathbb{C}K\oplus\mathbb{C}d,

where

is the canonical central element
[
17
]

The Lie bracket on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
is given by

\displaystyle[x\otimes t^{n},\,y\otimes t^{m}]

\displaystyle=[x,y]\otimes t^{n+m}+n(x,y)\,\delta_{n+m,0}\,K,

(2.1)

\displaystyle[d,\,x\otimes t^{n}]

\displaystyle=n\,x\otimes t^{n},

(2.2)

for
x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
and
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
We write
x(n)
for
x\otimes t^{n}
.

The Cartan subalgebra
{\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}}_{0}
and subalgebras
{\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\pm}
of
{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}
are defined by

{\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}}_{0}={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus\mathbb{C}K\oplus\mathbb{C}d,\qquad{\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\pm}=\mathfrak{g}\otimes t^{\pm}\mathbb{C}[t^{\pm}]+{\mathfrak{n}}_{\pm}\otimes\mathbb{C}.

Thus, we obtain the triangular decomposition

\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{+}\oplus\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}\oplus\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{-}.

Let
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}=[\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}},\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}]=\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{+}\oplus\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}\oplus\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{-}
,
where
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus\mathbb{C}K
and
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\pm}=\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{\pm}
.

Let

P={\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\mathbb{C}[t]\oplus\mathbb{C}K.

For each
\kappa\in\mathbb{C}
, let
\mathbb{C}v_{\kappa}
be the one-dimensional
P
–module on which

{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\mathbb{C}[t]
acts trivially and
K
acts as multiplication by
\kappa
.
Define the generalized Verma module

V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g})=U(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})\otimes_{U(P)}\mathbb{C}v_{\kappa}.

Then
V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g})
carries a natural structure of a vertex algebra generated by the fields

x(z)=Y(x(-1)\mathbf{1},z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}x(n)z^{-n-1},\qquad(x\in\mathfrak{g}),

where
\mathbf{1}=1\otimes v_{\kappa}
is the vacuum vector.

A
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
–module

is called

(or

and

x(z)w\in\mathbb{C}((z)).

A restricted
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
–module of level
\kappa
naturally acquires the structure of a
V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g})
–module.

From now on, we specialize to
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
with standard generators
e,f,h
,
and let
{\mathfrak{b}}=\mathbb{C}h+\mathbb{C}e
.
Recall that

[e,f]=h,\quad[h,e]=2e,\quad[h,f]=-2f,\quad(e,f)=1,\quad(h,h)=2.

Hence
\mathfrak{h}=\mathbb{C}h
,
\mathfrak{n}_{+}=\mathbb{C}e
, and
\mathfrak{n}_{-}=\mathbb{C}f
.
Let
\hat{\mathfrak{b}}
denote the subalgebra of
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
generated by

.

Assume first that
\kappa\neq-2

Define the canonical Sugawara Virasoro vector in the vertex algebra

\omega=\frac{1}{2(\kappa+2)}\bigl(e(-1)f(-1)+f(-1)e(-1)+\tfrac{1}{2}h(-1)^{2}\bigr)\mathbf{1}.

Then the corresponding field

Y(\omega,z)=L(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}L(n)z^{-n-2}

c_{\kappa}=\frac{3\kappa}{\kappa+2}.

Thus, every

Moreover,

[L(n),x(m)]=-m\,x(n+m),\qquad x\in\{e,f,h\}.

(2.3)

In particular,

[L(n),x(0)]=0,\qquad x\in\{e,f,h\}.

Now let
\kappa=-2
.
Set

[

]

\mathbb{C}[\,T(-n)\mid n\geq 0\,]

2.2.
Weyl vertex algebra and its Whittaker modules

We now recall the definition of the Weyl vertex algebra, which will serve as one of our main objects of study.
To define this vertex algebra, we first review the notion of the Weyl algebra
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be the infinite-dimensional Lie algebra with generators

K,\quad a(n),\quad a^{*}(n),\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z},

such that
K
is central and the only nontrivial commutation relations are

[a(n),a^{*}(m)]=\delta_{n+m,0}\,K,\qquad n,m\in\mathbb{Z}.

The corresponding Weyl algebra
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}

\widehat{\mathcal{A}}=\frac{U(\mathcal{L})}{\langle K-1\rangle},

\langle K-1\rangle

\widehat{\mathcal{A}}

To construct the Weyl vertex algebra, we consider the simple

a(n)\mathbf{1}=a^{*}(n+1)\mathbf{1}=0,\qquad n\geq 0.

Thus,

W=\mathbb{C}[\,a(-n),a^{*}(-m)\mid n>0,\,m\geq 0\,].

By the generating fields theorem, there exists a unique vertex algebra
(W,Y,\mathbf{1})

such that the vertex operator map

Y:W\longrightarrow\operatorname{End}(W)[[z,z^{-1}]]

is determined by

Y(a(-1)\mathbf{1},z)=a(z),\qquad Y(a^{*}(0)\mathbf{1},z)=a^{*}(z),

where

a(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}a(n)z^{-n-1},\qquad a^{*}(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}a^{*}(n)z^{-n}.

We choose the following conformal vector (cf.
[
20
]
) of central charge

\omega=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(a(-1)a^{*}(-1)\bigr)\mathbf{1}.

Then

\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
–graded vertex operator algebra.

Following
[
5
]
,
we define the Whittaker module for

to be the quotient

\lambda_{n}=0

\mu_{n}=0

n>>0

I=\big\langle\{a(i)-\lambda_{i},a^{*}(j)-\mu_{j}\ |\ i,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},j>0\}\ \big\rangle.

Let

be the subalgebra of
\mathcal{L}
generated by
a(n),a^{*}(n+1)

Then

.

5

is an
W\otimes\pi^{\kappa+2}
–module, and therefore a

V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
–module.

Assume now that
\kappa\neq-2
.
Then we have a natural action of the Virasoro algebra generated by the Sugawara vector

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2(\kappa+2)}\big(e(-1)f(-1)+f(-1)e(-1)+\tfrac{1}{2}h(-1)^{2}\big){\bf 1}

\displaystyle=a(-1)a^{*}(-1)+\frac{1}{4(\kappa+2)}\big(b(-1)^{2}-2b(-2)\big).

Let

L(z)=Y(\omega,z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}L(n)z^{-n-2}.

For further details, see
[
15
]
.

5.
Wakimoto realization of modules
\widehat{M}(\varphi)
: the case
\kappa\neq-2

Let
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\geq 0}={\mathfrak{h}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[t]
. Let
\bm{\eta}\in(\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\geq 0})^{*}
such that

\bm{\eta}(b(n))=0\quad(n>>0).

Let
N_{1}(\bm{\eta})
be the standard Whittaker module for the Heisenberg Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}
of level
\kappa+2
with the Whittaker function
\bm{\eta}
. This module is an irreducible module for the Heisenberg vertex algebra
\pi^{\kappa+2}
(for details see
[
5
]
and references therein).

Now we shall prove the first main theorem in our paper:

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume that
N>0
such that
\bm{\eta}(b(N))\neq 0
and
\bm{\eta}(b(j))=0
for
j>N
.
Then
W\otimes N_{1}(\bm{\eta})
is an irreducible
V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
–module. Moreover,

W\otimes N_{1}(\bm{\eta})\cong\widehat{M}(\varphi),

where
\varphi:S_{-1,N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is the Lie functional uniquely determined by

\varphi(e(n))=\varphi(f(N+1+n))=0,\ \varphi(h(n))=\bm{\eta}(b(n))\quad(n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}).

Proof.

Identify
v_{\bm{\eta}}
with
{\bf 1}\otimes v_{\bm{\eta}}
.
We first claim that

(1)

v_{\bm{\eta}}
is a cyclic vector for the
V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
–action, i.e.

V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}).v_{\bm{\eta}}=W\otimes N_{1}(\bm{\eta}).

Let us prove claim (1). The PBW basis of
W\otimes N_{1}(\bm{\eta})
as
W\otimes\pi^{\kappa+2}
–module is

a(-n_{1})\cdots a(-n_{r})a^{*}(-m_{1})\cdots a^{*}(-m_{s})b(-j_{1})\cdots b(-j_{t})v_{\bm{\eta}},

where
r,s,t\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
,
n_{1},\dots,n_{r},j_{1},\dots,j_{t}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
,
m_{1},\dots,m_{s}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
.
Define

\displaystyle\deg(a(-n_{1})\cdots a(-n_{r})a^{*}(-m_{1})\cdots a^{*}(-m_{s})b(-j_{1})\cdots b(-j_{t}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r}+m_{1}+\cdots+m_{s}+j_{1}+\cdots j_{t}.

Then

W\otimes N_{1}(\bm{\eta})=\bigoplus_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}}(W\otimes N_{1}(\bm{\chi}))_{m},

w\in(W\otimes N_{1}(\bm{\chi}))_{m}\iff\deg(w)=m.

We shall prove by induction on
m
that
(W\otimes N_{1}(\bm{\eta}))_{m}\subset V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}).v_{\bm{\eta}}
.

Basis,
m=0
. Then each
w\in(W\otimes N_{1}(\bm{\eta}))_{0}
is a linear combination of vectors

a^{*}(0)^{p}v_{\bm{\eta}}
.

Using the action of
f(N)
on
W\otimes N_{1}(\bm{\eta})
we easily get

f(N)^{p}v_{\bm{\eta}}=\nu a^{*}(0)^{p}v_{\bm{\eta}},\quad(\nu\neq 0),

which implies that
a^{*}(0)^{p}v_{\bm{\eta}}\in V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}).v_{\bm{\eta}}
for each
p\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
. Therefore

(W\otimes N_{1}(\bm{\eta}))_{0}\subset V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}).v_{\bm{\chi}}
. The basis of induction holds.

Assume now that there is
M\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
such that

(W\otimes N_{1}(\bm{\eta}))_{m}\subset V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}).v_{\bm{\eta}}\quad\mbox{for}\ m<M.

Let
w\in(W\otimes N_{1}(\bm{\eta}))_{M}
. It suffices to consider case when
w
is written in PBW basis. So assume that

\displaystyle w=a(-n_{1})\cdots a(-n_{r})a^{*}(-m_{1})\cdots a^{*}(-m_{s})b(-j_{1})\cdots b(-j_{t})v_{\bm{\eta}},

(5.1)

M=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r}+m_{1}+\cdots+m_{s}+j_{1}+\cdots j_{t}.

If
r>0
, then we have
w=e(-n_{1})w^{\prime}
for
\deg(w^{\prime})<M
. By the inductive assumption we have that
w^{\prime}\in V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}).v_{\bm{\eta}}
, which implies that
w\in V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}).v_{\bm{\eta}}
.
So it suffices to prove the statement in the case
r=0
. Therefore, let

w=a^{*}(-m_{1})\cdots a^{*}(-m_{s})b(-j_{1})\cdots b(-j_{t})v_{\bm{\eta}},

M=m_{1}+\cdots+m_{s}+j_{1}+\cdots j_{t}.

Assume that
s>0
. Then

f(N-m_{1})a^{*}(-m_{2})\cdots a^{*}(-m_{s})b(-j_{1})\cdots b(-j_{t})v_{\bm{\eta}}=\nu_{1}w+w^{\prime},

for certain
\nu_{1}\neq 0
and
\deg(w^{\prime})<M
. Using the inductive assumption again, we get that
w\in V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}).v_{\bm{\eta}}
.

Finally, we consider the case
r=s=0
, and
w
of the form

w=b(-j_{1})\cdots b(-j_{t})v_{\bm{\lambda}},

M=j_{1}+\cdots j_{t}.

Let
t>0
. Then

h(-j_{1})b(-j_{2})\cdots b(-j_{t})v_{\bm{\chi}}=w+w_{1},

where
\deg(w_{1})=M
and
w_{1}
is a linear combination of basis elements of the form (
5.1
) where
r>0
. We have already showed that in these cases
w_{1}\in V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}).v_{\bm{\chi}}
. This proves that
w\in V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}).v_{\bm{\eta}}
. Therefore the claim (1) holds.

So
v_{\bm{\eta}}
is cyclic vector in
W\otimes N_{1}({\bm{\eta}})
. Since

e(n)v_{\bm{\eta}}=f(N+1+n)v_{\bm{\eta}}=0,\ h(n)v_{\bm{\eta}}={\bm{\eta}}(b(n))v_{\bm{\eta}},

for
n\geq 0
,
we conclude that
W\otimes N_{1}({\bm{\eta}})
is isomorphic to a quotient of the universal module
\widehat{M}(\varphi)
, where

\varphi:\mathcal{S}_{-1,N}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
is the Lie functional defined by

\varphi(e(n))=\varphi(f(N+1+n))=0,\ \varphi(h(n))={\bm{\chi}}(b(n)),\quad(n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}).

Since
\varphi(h(N))\neq 0
, Theorem
3.1
implies that
\widehat{M}(\varphi)
is irreducible. Therefore

\widehat{M}(\varphi)=W\otimes N_{1}({\bm{\lambda}}),

and thus
W\otimes N_{1}({\bm{\eta}})
is an irreducible
V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
–module.
∎

6.
The explicit realization of simple quotients of
\widehat{M}(\varphi)
at the critical level

Now we consider critical level case
\kappa=-2
. Therefore
\pi^{\kappa+2}=\pi^{0}
is a commutative vertex algebra generated by the field

b(z)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}b(n)z^{-n-1},\quad b(n)=0\ \mbox{for}\ n\geq 0.

Let

\bm{\chi}(z)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\chi_{n}z^{-n-1}\in{\mathbb{C}}((z)).

Let
L(\chi)
be the one dimensional
\pi^{0}
–module such that
b(n)
acts as a multiplication with
\chi_{n}
. Since
\pi^{0}
is commutative, all irreducible modules are
1
–dimensional and isomorphic to
L(\bm{\chi})
for certain
\bm{\chi}(z)\in{\mathbb{C}}((z))
.

Note that
L(\bm{\chi})
is a simple quotient of the Whittaker
\pi^{0}
–module
N_{1}(\bm{\eta})
where

\bm{\eta}\in(\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\geq 0})^{*}
is given by

\bm{\eta}(b(i))=\chi_{i}\quad(i\geq 0).

Now we consider the Wakimoto module

W_{\bm{\chi}}=W\otimes L(\bm{\chi}).

The irreducibility result for modules
W_{\chi}
was obtained in
[
1
,
2
]
.
In order to present the irreducibility criterion, we need to
recall the definition of Schur polynomials.

Define the Schur polynomials
S_{r}(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots)

in variables
x_{1},x_{2},\cdots
by the following equation:

\displaystyle\exp\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{x_{n}}{n}y^{n}\right)=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}S_{r}(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots)y^{r}.

(6.1)

We shall also use the following formula for Schur polynomials:

\displaystyle S_{r}(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r!}

\displaystyle\det\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}x_{1}&x_{2}&\cdots&&x_{r}\\
-r+1&x_{1}&x_{2}&\cdots&x_{r-1}\\
0&-r+2&x_{1}&\cdots&x_{r-2}\\
0&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\\
0&\cdots&0&-1&x_{1}\\
\end{array}\right)

Theorem 6.1
.

[
1
,
2
]

Assume that
\bm{\chi}\in{\mathbb{C}}((z))
. Then the Wakimoto module

W_{-\bm{\chi}}
is an irreducible
V^{-2}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
–module if and only
if
\bm{\chi}
satisfies one of the following conditions:

There is
p\in{{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}}
,
p\geq 1
such that

\bm{\chi}(z)=\sum_{n=-p}^{\infty}{\chi}_{-n}z^{n-1}\in{\mathbb{C}}((z))\quad\mbox{and}\quad\chi_{p}\neq 0.

\bm{\chi}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\chi}_{-n}z^{n-1}\in{\mathbb{C}}((z))\quad\mbox{and}\quad\chi_{0}\in\{1\}\cup({\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbb{Z}}).

There is
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\bm{\chi}(z)=\frac{\ell+1}{z}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\chi}_{-n}z^{n-1}\in{\mathbb{C}}((z))

and
S_{\ell}(-\chi)\neq 0
,
where
S_{\ell}(-\chi)=S_{\ell}(-\chi_{-1},-\chi_{-2},\dots)

is a Schur polynomial.

Let us now explicitly compare modules
\overline{M}(\varphi,\theta)
from
[
13
]
, which we recalled in Section
3
),
with the Wakimoto modules considered in
[
1
,
2
]
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Assume that
N=p\in{{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}}
and

\bm{\chi}(z)=\sum_{n=-p}^{\infty}{\chi}_{-n}z^{n-1}\in{\mathbb{C}}((z)).

Let
\varphi:S_{-1,N}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
be the Lie functional such that

\varphi(h(i))=-\chi_{i},\varphi(e(i))=\varphi(f(i+N+1))=0\quad\mbox{for}\ i\geq 0.

Assume that
\bm{\chi}\in{\mathbb{C}}((z))
satisfies the condition (i), (ii) or (iii) of Theorem
6.1
. Then
W_{-\chi}
is a simple quotient of
\widehat{M}(\varphi)
.

If
p\geq 1
and
\chi_{p}\neq 0
, then

\overline{M}(\varphi,\theta)\cong W_{-\bm{\chi}}
, where

\theta(z)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\bm{\chi}(z)^{2}+2\frac{d}{dz}\bm{\chi}(z)\right)=\sum_{i\geq 0}\theta_{2p+i}z^{-2p-i-2}.

Proof.

By construction
W_{-\bm{\chi}}
is generated by a cyclic vector
w
satisfying conditions

e(i)w=f(i+N+1)w=0,\ h(i)w=-\chi_{i}w\quad(i\geq 0).

Therefore it is isomorphic to a quotient of
\widehat{M}(\varphi)
.

Moreover, whenever one of the conditions (i), (ii), or (iii) of Theorem
6.1
is satisfied, the module
W_{-\bm{\chi}}
is simple. Consequently, in this case
W_{-\bm{\chi}}
is a simple quotient of
\widehat{M}(\varphi)
.

Assume next that the conidition (i) holds, i.e.
p\geq 1
and
\chi_{p}\neq 0
. Since we know that
\overline{M}(\varphi,\theta)
is a quotient of
\widehat{M}(\varphi)
by a submodule generated by singular vectors

(T(i)-\theta_{i})u,\ i<N_{0}=N,

and
T(z)=\theta(z)
on
W_{-\chi}
, we get that
W_{-\chi}
is a quotient of
\overline{M}(\varphi,\theta)
.

For
N\geq 1
, the module
\overline{M}(\varphi,\theta)
is irreducible by Theorem
3.2
, so
\overline{M}(\varphi,\theta)\cong W_{-\bm{\chi}}
.
∎

Let us now consider the case
N=0
. The simple quotients in the case
N=0
were not described in
[
13
]
. So our result in the case
N=0
is a refinement of the main result of
[
13
]
.
Moreover, we shall see below that in this case
\overline{M}(\varphi,\theta)
need not to be irreducible.
Applying previous theorem and
[
2
, Corollary 5.2, Remark 5.1]
we get:

Proposition 6.3
.

Assume that

\bm{\chi}(z)=\frac{\ell+1}{z}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\chi}_{-n}z^{n-1}\in{\mathbb{C}}((z))

is such that

\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>1}
and
S_{\ell}(-\chi)=0
or
\ell<0
.
Then
\overline{M}(\varphi,\theta)
is reducible and contains an irreducible submodule isomorphic to
W_{-\chi}^{int_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}}
, which is the maximal integrable
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
–submodule of
W_{-\chi}
.

7.
Wakimoto realization of generalized Whittaker modules

Let
\mathcal{P}_{q,r}
, where
q,r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
,
q<r
, be the Lie subalgebra of
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
generated by
K
and the elements

\{\,e(n),h(m),f(p),\mid n\geq 0,\;m\geq q,\;p\geq r\}.

Let
\psi:\mathcal{P}_{q,r}\to\mathbb{C}
be a homomorphism of the Lie algebra such that
\kappa=\psi(K)
.

Denote by
\mathbb{C}v
the one-dimensional
\mathcal{P}_{q,r}
–module on which
x\in\mathcal{P}_{q,r}
acts as

\displaystyle x\cdot v=\psi(x)\,v.

(7.1)

We then form the induced
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
–module

\widehat{R}(\psi)=\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathcal{P}_{q,r}}^{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}\mathbb{C}v.

The module
\widehat{R}(\psi)
is a restricted (i.e. smooth)
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
–module of level
\kappa
.
Hence,
\widehat{R}(\psi)
is a weak
V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{g})
–module. This module is a standard (universal) generalized Whittaker module with the Whittaker function
\psi
.

Definition 7.1
.

Assume that
Z
is any
V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
–module and
v\in Z
. We say that
v
is a
\psi
–Whittaker vector if it satisfies condition (
7.1
).

We shall consider
W\otimes\pi^{\kappa+2}
–modules
M_{1}(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu})\otimes N_{1}(\bm{\eta})
from Subsection
2.2
. Assume that

\lambda_{N}\neq 0,\mu_{M}\neq 0\quad\mbox{and}\ \lambda_{N+i}=\mu_{M+i}=0\ \mbox{for}\ i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}.

As before for
\bm{\eta}\in(\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{\geq 0})^{*}
, set
\eta_{n}=\bm{\eta}(b(n))
. Assume that

\eta_{P}\neq 0,\ \eta_{P+i}=0\quad\mbox{for}\ i>0.

Using the Frenkel-Feigin homomorphism, we can consider
M_{1}(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu})\otimes N_{1}(\bm{\eta})
as a
V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
–module.
Set
w=v_{\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu}}\otimes v_{\bm{\eta}}
.
Let

q=\mbox{max}\{M,N+1\},\ \ r=\mbox{max}\{M+N+1,2M,P+1\}.

By a direct calculation we see that

\displaystyle e(i)w=a_{i}w,\ h(q+i)w=b_{i}w\ f(r+i)w=c_{i}w\quad(i\geq 0),

(7.2)

where

\displaystyle\ a_{i}=\lambda_{i},b_{i}=-2(\lambda_{i}\mu_{M}+\lambda_{i+1}\mu_{M-1}+\cdots+\lambda_{N}\mu_{M-N+i})+\eta_{q+i},

\displaystyle\ c_{i}=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\,k_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}\\
k_{3}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\\
k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=r+i\end{subarray}}\mu_{k_{1}}\mu_{k_{2}}\lambda_{k_{3}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}\\
j_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\\
j_{1}+j_{2}=r+i\end{subarray}}\mu_{j_{1}}\eta_{j_{2}}-k(r+i)\,\mu_{r+i}.

(7.3)

Therefore
w
is a
\psi
–Whittaker vector with Whittaker function
\psi:\mathcal{P}_{q,r}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
defined by

\displaystyle\psi(e(i))=a_{i},\ \psi(h(q+i))=b_{i},\ \ \psi(f(r+i))=c_{i}\quad i\geq 0,

(7.4)

where
a_{i},b_{i},c_{i}
are given by (
7.3
).

In this way we have proved that:

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
k\in{\mathbb{C}}
be arbitrary. Then
U(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}})w
is a generalized Whittaker module with the Whittaker function
\psi
satisfying the condition satisfying (
7.4
).

We have the following general conjecture:

Conjecture 7.3
.

Assume that
\mu_{M}\neq 0
,
{\lambda}_{N}\neq 0
,
\bm{\eta}\neq 0
and that
\psi
is a Whittaker function satisfying (
7.4
).
Assume that
k\neq-2
. Then

\widehat{R}(\psi)
is an irreducible
V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
–module and

\widehat{R}(\psi)\cong M_{1}(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu})\otimes N_{1}(\bm{\eta}).

Similarly as in Theorem
3.2
one shows that
\widehat{R}(\psi)
is reducible at the critical level. But we shall identify its irreducible subquotients following approaches from
[
2
,
4
]
.

Let

\bm{\chi}(z)=\sum_{n=-P}^{\infty}\chi_{-n}z^{n-1}\in{\mathbb{C}}((z))\quad\chi_{P}\neq 0,

such that

\chi_{i}=\eta_{i}\ \mbox{for}\ i\geq 0,

and let
L(\chi)
be the simple
1
–dimensional
\pi^{0}
–module as in Section
6
. Clearly,
L(\bm{\chi})
is a simple quotient of
N_{1}(\bm{\eta})
.
We have the following result.

Theorem 7.4
.

Assume that
k=-2
. Then
M_{1}(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu})\otimes L(\bm{\chi})
is a simple quotient of
\widehat{R}(\psi)
, where
\psi
is a Whittaker function determined by (
7.4
).

Proof.

First we noticed that Theorem was already proved in
[
4
, Theorem 10.4]
in the special case
\bm{\lambda}=(\lambda_{0},0,0,\dots),\bm{\mu}=(\mu_{1},0,0,\dots)
,
\lambda_{0}\neq 0
and
\bm{\chi}
arbitrary. Our situation is slightly more general, but the same proof applies here. Let us just present the basic idea for the reader convenience.

Let
h_{1}=-2:aa^{*}:
,
{\mathfrak{b}}_{1}={\mathbb{C}}e+{\mathbb{C}}h_{1}
be a two dimensional Borel Lie algebra with bracket

[h_{1},e]=2e
and

\widehat{{\mathfrak{b}}}_{1}={\mathfrak{b}}_{1}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[t,t^{-1}]+{\mathbb{C}}K
its affinization. Using the same arguments as in the proof of
[
4
, Proposition 10.2]
we get

•

M_{1}(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu})
is an irreducible
\widehat{{\mathfrak{b}}}_{1}
–module.

Next we consider the Borel Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{b}}={\mathbb{C}}e+{\mathbb{C}}h
, where
h=h_{1}+b
, and its affinization
\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}={\mathfrak{b}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[t,t^{-1}]+{\mathbb{C}}K\subset\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}
. Then we consider
M_{1}(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu})\otimes L(\bm{\chi})
as an
\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}
–module. The irreducibility of
M_{1}(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu})
as a
\widehat{{\mathfrak{b}}}_{1}
–module is not affected if we twist the action of
h_{1}(z)
by
\bm{\chi}(z)
. Thus, we get:

•

M_{1}(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu})\otimes L(\bm{\chi})
is an irreducible
\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}
–module.

Since
\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}
is a Lie subalgebra of
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}
we conclude that
M_{1}(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu})\otimes L(\bm{\chi})
is irreducible
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}
–module and therefore irreducible
V^{\kappa}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
–module. The proof follows.

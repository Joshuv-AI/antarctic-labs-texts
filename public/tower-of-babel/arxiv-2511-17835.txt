Kicked-Ising Quantum Battery
Sebastián V. Romero, Xi Chen, Yue Ban

Abstract
Quantum batteries (QBs) have emerged as promising candidates capable of outperforming classical counterparts by utilizing entangled operators. Spin chains, in particular, exhibit unique charging properties across diverse settings. Here, we introduce the kicked-Ising model as a QB and analytically characterize its charging dynamics within the self-dual operator regime, valid for arbitrary system sizes and Floquet cycles. Using Clifford quantum cellular automata and momentum-space Floquet analysis with the Cayley-Hamilton theorem, we obtain exact expressions for energy injection, uncovering the influence of boundary conditions and spin-chain parity on charging performance. The kicked-Ising QB achieves maximal charging while exhibiting remarkable robustness against disorder. We further propose an intensified protocol within a fixed time window that enables faster and more efficient energy injection, while non-uniform kick schedules enhance experimental flexibility. Spin correlators analysis further shows that low-frequency driving boosts energy injection, highlighting a clear connection between charging, scrambling, and kick-induced delocalization. Our theoretical framework are supported by tensor-network simulations and finally verified on IBM quantum hardware. Accounting for platform-specific constraints, we demonstrate that the kicked-Ising QB offers a scalable, disorder-resilient protocol and testbed to assess quantum platforms.

The kicked-Ising QB remains stable against parameter and timing deviations, achieving maximal energy injection under specified conditions. \displaystyle U(1,0)

\displaystyle=e^{-iH_{I}\Delta t_{m}}\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}e^{-iH_{K}\Delta t_{i}}e^{-iH_{I}\Delta t_{i}},

Spin localization in time domain.
—Next, the scrambling nature of the charging protocol under a butterfly effect perspective can be analyzed by means of spin correlators
Colmenarez and Luitz 2020
. Hereinafter, we use
H^{zz}_{1}
as charger, but similar results can be obtained with

. The spin correlator is defined as

C_{ij}(t)=[\sigma^{z}_{i}(t),\sigma^{z}_{j}]=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(it)^{k}}{k!}[[H(t),\sigma^{z}_{i}]_{k},\sigma^{z}_{j}],

(6)

where we used the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula,
[H(t),\sigma^{z}_{i}]_{k}=[[H(t),\sigma^{z}_{i}]_{k-1},\sigma^{z}_{i}]
and
[H(t),\sigma^{z}_{i}]_{0}=\sigma^{z}_{i}
with the Heisenberg picture of
\sigma^{z}_{i}(t)\coloneqq U^{\dagger}(t,0)\sigma^{z}_{i}U(t,0)
. This provides a direct probe of how delocalization spreads across a chain. Importantly, when we choose
\sigma^{z}_{i}
as local operator, locality is preserved during evolution under the Ising interaction
H^{zz}_{I}
, since
[e^{iH^{zz}_{I}t}\sigma^{z}_{i}e^{-iH^{zz}_{I}t},\sigma^{z}_{j}]=[\sigma^{z}_{i},\sigma^{z}_{j}]=0
, and hence delocalization is solely induced by the kicks.

We compute the light cones generated by a local operator initially placed at the chain center, tracking
\left\lVert C_{ij}(t)\right\rVert
for all
j\in[1,N]
, where
\left\lVert A\right\rVert
denotes the largest singular value. For a KIC with
N=13
up to
m=4N
kicks as shown in Fig.

, and observe that spreading pattern, with red vertical lines indicating the kicks. At specific kicks

and

and

Conclusion.
—We have proposed the kicked-Ising model as an efficient and robust QB, provide an analytical description of its charging dynamics in the self-dual operator regime. The injected energy scales with both Floquet cycles and system size, while entanglement growth emerges as a key performance indicator. The model is stable against parameter fluctuations and timing imperfections, with more intense kick sequences enabling faster and more efficient energy injection compared to continuously driven schemes.
Non-uniform kicking protocols further enhance charging efficiency and offer additional experimental flexibility.
Our spin correlator analysis have revealed that low-frequency kicks improve energy delocalization and entanglement spreading. Given the feasibility of spin chains, our results demonstrated that kicked-Ising QBs pose a compelling candidate for realizing in existing platforms such as trapped ions, ultracold atoms, and superconducting transmons. Proof-of-principle simulations on IBM quantum hardware have already confirmed its potential as a programmable testbed, supporting current experimental realization. Looking ahead, extending our model to current quantum hardware architectures with denser connectivity among spins and optimized kick schedules will provide a natural pathway toward scalable and high-performance QBs.

Exact charging dynamics of KIC.
—Here, we summarize two independent analytical methods to obtain the charging dynamics of KIC, see equations (
2
) and (
3
), for arbitrary system sizes and number of kicks. These provide key tools to unveil the charging dynamics in our KIC QB proposal.

The first method considered is based on Clifford quantum cellular automata (CQCA), using the fact that any Clifford gate acting on a Pauli operator returns another Pauli operator, which holds for the Floquet operator [equation (
4
)] under the self-dual operator regime, thereby establishing a connection between the kicked-Ising model and CQCA. As a result, this allows to track how each quantum cell of
H_{0}
evolves after any number of kicks, thereby reconstructing the dynamics of energy injection.
The procedure is as follows:

1.

Under the self-dual operator regime, the Floquet operator becomes a Clifford unitary, allowing for an analytic description of the dynamics.

2.

Using
U_{\{I,K\}}=\exp(-iH_{\{I,K\}})
, evolve each quantum cell
\sigma^{\alpha}_{i}(m)\coloneqq(U^{\dagger}_{I}U^{\dagger}_{K})^{m}\sigma^{\alpha}_{i}(U_{K}U_{I})^{m}
after
m
kicks by applying known conjugation rules.

3.

Evaluate the energy
E_{N}(m)=\braket{\psi(m)|H_{0}|\psi(m)}=\braket{\psi(0)|(U^{\dagger}_{I}U^{\dagger}_{K})^{m}H_{0}(U_{K}U_{I})^{m}|\psi(0)}
.

To obtain the exact dynamics for arbitrary coupling strengths, we derive the charging dynamics from momentum space. The steps to consider are (see Fig.
7
):

1.

Apply the Jordan-Wigner transformation
Jordan and Wigner 1928
to work under a spinless fermion basis.

2.

Depending on the parity of the initial state and number of spins, apply a Fourier transform consistent with boundary conditions
Dziarmaga 2005
;
Damski and Rams 2013
.

3.

Apply the Bogoliubov transformation and integrate the Bogoliubov-de Gennes equations to obtain the Floquet operator
U_{k}(1,0)
per mode.

4.

Since
U_{k}(m,0)=U^{m}_{k}(1,0)
after
m
kicks, compute the
m
th power of the Floquet operator relying on the Cayley-Hamilton theorem
Griffiths and Steinke 2001
;
Das and Dutta 2025
.

5.

Evolve the initial state
\ket{\psi(0)}=\prod_{k}\ket{\psi_{k}(0)}
as
\ket{\psi(m)}=\prod_{k}U^{m}_{k}(1,0)\ket{\psi_{k}(0)}
to obtain
E_{N}(m)
.

For a thorough step-by-step derivation using both methods, see Supplementary Section I B and I C.

One of the quantities to monitor along the evolution is the injected energy
E_{N}(\tau)=(\omega_{0}/2)\sum_{i=1}^{N}\braket{\sigma^{\alpha}_{i}}_{\tau}
. In order to measure Pauli expectation values, we can sample our circuit after rotating into their corresponding basis. While for Pauli-Z values no rotation is needed, for Pauli-Y the gate product
\text{RZ}(\frac{\pi}{2})\text{RX}(\frac{\pi}{2})
is required right before measuring. To estimate the energy uncertainty from the sampled bitstrings, we use the propagation of uncertainties as a function of the Pauli expectation values. Each bit position can be treated as a Bernoulli variable, where if a bit
b
has a probability
p
of being 1, its variance is given by
\mathbb{V}(b)=p(1-p)
. For KIC, its variance reads as

\mathbb{V}[E_{N}]=\frac{\omega_{0}^{2}}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Big[\mathbb{V}[\braket{\sigma^{\alpha}_{i}}_{\tau}]+\sum_{i\neq j}\cov[\braket{\sigma^{\alpha}_{i}}_{\tau},\braket{\sigma^{\alpha}_{j}}_{\tau}]\Big],

(9)

where we considered
100000
samples per circuit. For the non-uniform KIC, the variance yields

\mathbb{V}[E_{N}]=\frac{1}{n_{\text{d}}}\sum_{d=1}^{n_{\text{d}}}\left[\mathbb{V}[E_{N,d}]+\left(\mathbb{E}[E_{N,d}]-\bar{E}_{N}\right)^{2}\right]

(10)

with
E_{N,d}
the energy injected at the
d
th disorder realization and
\bar{E}_{N}\coloneqq(1/n_{\text{d}})\sum_{d=1}^{n_{\text{d}}}E_{N,d}
, where we considered
n_{\text{d}}=10
and
1000
samples per circuit. We have included covariances because, although
E_{N}
is a sum of local Pauli expectation values, both the quantum state (e.g., via entanglement) and hardware effects (e.g., readout cross-talk) can induce interqubit correlations, thus ignoring covariance would miss these contributions.

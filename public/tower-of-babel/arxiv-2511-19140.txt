2.1
Problem statement

Let
M={\mathbb{R}}^{3}_{x,y,z}
be the Heisenberg group with the product rule

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\\
y_{1}\\
z_{1}\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}x_{2}\\
y_{2}\\
z_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}+x_{2}\\
y_{1}+y_{2}\\
z_{1}+z_{2}+(x_{1}y_{2}-x_{2}y_{1})/2\end{pmatrix}.

The vector fields

X_{1}=\frac{\partial\,}{\partial\,x}-\frac{y}{2}\frac{\partial\,}{\partial\,z},\qquad X_{2}=\frac{\partial\,}{\partial\,y}+\frac{x}{2}\frac{\partial\,}{\partial\,z},\qquad X_{3}=\frac{\partial\,}{\partial\,z}

form a left-invariant frame on

M
. The one-forms

\omega_{1}=dx,\qquad\omega_{2}=dy,\qquad\omega_{3}=\frac{y}{2}dx-\frac{x}{2}dy+dz

form the dual left-invariant coframe on
M
.

Fix a parameter
\varepsilon>0
and consider the Lorentzian form
\displaystyle g=-\omega_{1}^{2}+\omega_{2}^{2}+\frac{\omega_{3}^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}
. An orthonormal frame for this form is given by the vector fields
X_{1}
,
X_{2}
,
\varepsilon X_{3}
. Lorentzian length maximizers for the form
g
are solutions to the following optimal control problem:

\displaystyle\dot{q}=u_{1}X_{1}+u_{2}X_{2}+\varepsilon u_{3}X_{3},\qquad q\in M,\quad u\in U=\left\{u_{1}\geq\sqrt{u_{2}^{2}+u_{3}^{2}}\right\},

(1)

\displaystyle q(0)=q_{0},\qquad q(t_{1})=q_{1},

(2)

\displaystyle l=\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\sqrt{u_{1}^{2}-u_{2}^{2}-u_{3}^{2}}dt\to\max.

(3)

The problem is left-invariant, thus we will assume that
q_{0}=\operatorname{Id}\nolimits=(0,0,0)
.

The aim of this section is to study the Lorentzian problem
(\ref{pr1})
–
(\ref{pr3})
for any
\varepsilon>0
and, as a consequence, to study its limit behaviour for
\varepsilon\to 0
. It is natural to expect that essential objects of the Lorentzian problem (attainable set, extremal trajectories, spheres) tend to the corresponding objects of the sub-Lorentzian problem with the orthonormal frame
\{X_{1},X_{2}\}
(this sub-Lorentzian problem was studied in papers
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
.

2.2
Invariant set

Denote by
\mathcal{A}
(resp. by
\mathcal{A}(t_{1})
) the attainable set of system
(\ref{pr1})
from the point
q_{0}
for arbitrary nonnegative time (resp. for time

Lemma 1
.

There holds the inclusion

\mathcal{A}\subset\{q=(x,y,z)\in M\mid x\geq|y|\},

thus system
(\ref{pr1})
,
(\ref{pr2})
is not globally controllable.

Proof.

It follows from system
(\ref{pr1})
that

\dot{x}=y_{1},\qquad\dot{y}=u_{2},\qquad u_{1}\geq u_{2},

whence
|\frac{dy}{dx}|=\frac{|u_{2}|}{u_{1}}\leq 1
, so
x(t)\geq|y(t)|
.
∎

In order to construct an invariant set (which will turn out to be the attainable set
\mathcal{A}

Let

be the cotangent bundle of the group
M
. Denote the linear on leaves of this bundle Hamiltonians
h_{i}(\lambda)=\langle\lambda,X_{i}\rangle
,
i=1,2,3.
Let

x=-h_{1}t,\qquad y=h_{2}t,\qquad z=0.

(12)

If

\displaystyle h_{1}=h_{1}^{0}\cosh\tau-h_{2}^{0}\sinh\tau,\qquad h_{2}=h_{2}^{0}\cosh\tau-h_{1}^{0}\sinh\tau,\qquad\tau=h_{3}t,

2.4
Conjugate points

Proposition 3
.

Problem
(\ref{pr1})
–
(\ref{pr3})
has no conjugate points in the domain
\{h_{3}\neq 0\}
.

Proof.

In the domain
\left\{h_{3}=\frac{\sinh\theta\sin\varphi}{\varepsilon}\neq 0\right\}
we have

J=\frac{\partial(x,y,z)}{\partial(t,\theta,\varphi)}=\frac{\varepsilon^{4}}{\sin^{3}\varphi\sinh^{2}\theta}\left(\tau\sin\tau+\frac{2-\cosh\tau+\tau\sinh\tau}{\sin^{2}\varphi\sinh^{2}\theta}\right).

When
\tau\neq 0
the function
2-\cosh\tau+\tau\sinh\tau
is positive, thus
J\neq 0
for
h_{3}\neq 0
. Consequently, there are no conjugate points for
h_{3}\neq 0
.
∎

Corollary 1
.

For
h_{3}\neq 0
extremal trajectories
\operatorname{Exp}(\theta,\varphi,t)
are locally optimal.

2.5
Diffeomorphic property of the exponential mapping

Denote a domain in the preimage of the exponential mapping

N=\{(\theta,\varphi,\tau)\mid\theta>0,\ \varphi\in(0,\pi),\ \tau>0\},

and a subset of the invariant set

D_{+}=\left\{q=(x,y,z)\in M\mid x>y,\ 0<z<\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}(\sinh\tau+\tau),\right.\\
\left.\tau=\operatorname{arcosh}\left(\frac{x^{2}-y^{2}}{2\varepsilon^{2}}+1\right)\right\}.

Proposition 4
.

(1)

\operatorname{Exp}(N)\subset D_{+}
.

(2)

The mapping
\operatorname{Exp}\,:\,N\to D+
is a diffeomorphism.

Proof.

(1) The set
D
is invariant, thus
\operatorname{Exp}(N)\subset D
.

It follows from equality
(\ref{zt})
that
z>0
for
\tau>0
. Let us prove that
\operatorname{Exp}(N)\cap\partial D\neq 0
. By contradiction, suppose that there exists a point
q\in\operatorname{Exp}(N)\cap\partial D
. Then
q=\operatorname{Exp}(\theta,\varphi,\tau)
for some
\nu=(\theta,\varphi,\tau)\in N
. By virtue of Proposition
3
, the mapping
\operatorname{Exp}
is a local diffeomorphism at the point
\nu
, thus
\operatorname{Exp}(N)
contains a neighborhood of the point
q
. Thus
\operatorname{Exp}(N)\not\subset D
, a contradiction.

So
\operatorname{Exp}(N)\subset\operatorname{int}\nolimits D\cap\{z>0\}
, thus
\operatorname{Exp}(N)\subset D_{+}
.

(2) Let us apply Hadamard’s global diffeomorphism theorem
[
8
]
: if
F\,:\,X\to Y
is a smooth nondegenerate proper mapping between connected simply connected smooth manifolds of the same dimension, then
F
is a diffeomorphism. Let us apply this theorem to the mapping
\operatorname{Exp}\,:\,N\to D_{+}
.

It is obvious that
N
and
D_{+}
are 3D smooth connected simply connected manifolds.

The mapping
\operatorname{Exp}\,:\,N\to D_{+}
is nondegenerate by Proposition
3
.

Let us prove that the mapping
\operatorname{Exp}\,:\,N\to D_{+}
is proper, i.e., for any compact
K\subset D_{+}
the preimage
\operatorname{Exp}^{-1}(K)\subset N
is compact. In order to prove this we show that if a sequence
\nu_{n}=(\theta_{n},\varphi_{n},\tau_{n})\to\partial N
, then its image
\operatorname{Exp}(\nu_{n})\to\partial D_{+}
.

If
\nu_{n}=(\theta_{n},\varphi_{n},\tau_{n})\to\partial N
, then there exists a subsequence on which one of the following conditions hold:

1)
\theta\to 0
,

2)
\theta\to\infty
,

3)
\varphi\to 0
,

4)
\varphi\to\pi
,

5)
\tau\to 0
,

6)
\tau\to+\infty
.

Let us consider these possibilities case by case and show that in each of them
q=(x,y,z)=\operatorname{Exp}(\theta,\varphi,\tau)\to\partial D_{+}
.

1) Let
\theta\to 0
, then
h_{3}\to 0
.

1.1) Let
\tau\to\bar{\tau}\in(0,+\infty)
. Then
z\to+\infty
.

1.2) Let
\tau\to+\infty
. Then
z\to+\infty
.

1.3) Let
\tau\to 0
. Then
\sinh\tau-\tau\sim\frac{\tau^{3}}{6}
.

1.3.1) Let
\frac{\tau^{3}}{h_{3}^{2}}\to 0
, then
z\to 0
.

1.3.2) Let
\frac{\tau^{3}}{h_{3}^{2}}\to+\infty
, then
z\to+\infty
.

1.3.3) Let
\frac{\tau^{3}}{h_{3}^{2}}\to C\in(0,+\infty)
, then
x\to\infty
.

2) Let
\theta\to+\infty
.

2.1) Let
\tau\to 0
.

2.1.1) Let
h_{3}\to 0
, then
z\to 0
.

2.1.2) Let
h_{3}\to\bar{h}_{3}\in(0.+\infty)
, then
z\to 0
.

2.1.3) Let
h_{3}\to 0
.

2.1.3.1) Let
\frac{\tau^{3}}{h_{3}^{2}}\to 0
, then
z\to 0
.

2.1.3.2) Let
\frac{\tau^{3}}{h_{3}^{2}}\to\infty
, then
z\to+\infty
.

2.1.3.3) Let
\frac{\tau^{3}}{h_{3}^{2}}\to C\in(0,+\infty)

x\to+\infty

2.2) Let
\tau\to+\infty
, then
z\to+\infty

2.3) Let
\tau\to\bar{\tau}\in(0,+\infty)
.

2.3.1) Let
h_{3}\to 0
, then
x\to+\infty
.

2.3.2) Let
h_{3}\to+\infty
.

2.3.2.1) Let
\frac{h_{1}}{h_{3}}\to+\infty
, then
x\to+\infty

2.3.2.2) Let
\frac{h_{1}}{h_{3}}\to C\in(0,+\infty)

Attainable sets

Theorem 2
.

There holds the identity
\mathcal{A}=D
.

Proof.

In Proposition
2

\operatorname{int}\nolimits D\subset\mathcal{A}

D\subset\mathcal{A}

Let us parametrise the surface

\displaystyle\theta\in{\mathbb{R}},\quad\varphi\in{\mathbb{R}}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z}).

Normal extremal trajectories
(\ref{xt2})
–
(\ref{zt2})
define the exponential mapping

\operatorname{Exp}\,:\,(T_{q_{0}}^{*}M\cap E)\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to M,\qquad(\theta,\varphi,\tau)\mapsto(x,y,z).

Conjugate points are zeros of the Jacobian
J=\frac{\partial(x,y,z)}{\partial(\theta,\varphi,\tau)}

Let

We say that a subspace

L\setminus\{0\}\subset\operatorname{int}\nolimits(\tilde{C}\cup\tilde{C}^{-}).

\tilde{g}

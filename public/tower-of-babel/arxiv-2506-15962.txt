Nucleon Tomography with 0-jettiness
Shen Fang, Shuo Lin, Ding Yu Shao, Jian Zhou

Abstract
We propose a novel strategy to systematically isolate the nucleon’s intrinsic non-perturbative three-dimensional structure by employing 0-jettiness to suppress initial-state radiation in transverse momentum-dependent (TMD) observables. Applying this method to transverse single spin asymmetries (SSAs) in W ± W^{\pm} and Z 0 Z^{0} boson production at RHIC, we demonstrate a substantial enhancement of the asymmetry signal (e.g., by 83% for Z 0 Z^{0} SSA at q ⟂ = 5 q_{\perp}=5 GeV), enabling a more definitive test of the predicted sign change of the Sivers function–a key prediction of TMD factorization. We further explore its applicability to spin-dependent measurements at the Electron-Ion Collider. Our analysis is formulated within a joint resummation framework that systematically resums large logarithms associated with both the veto scale and the gauge boson’s transverse momentum.

TMDs with 0-jettiness
— As TMD evolution is predominantly driven by soft gluon radiation, observables that suppresses radiation in the central rapidity region are particularly effective for enhancing sensitivity to non-perturbative effects. Among the various event shape observables, beam thrust, also referred to as 0-jettiness
Stewart et al. 2010c
;
Stewart et al. 2010a
;
Kang et al. 2012
;
Jouttenus et al. 2013
;
Kang et al. 2013a
;
Gaunt et al. 2014
, stands out as a powerful tool for this purpose. For electroweak Drell-Yan processes, the 0-jettiness variable is defined as

\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{2}{Q^{2}}\sum_{i}\min\{p_{a}\cdot l_{i},p_{b}\cdot l_{i}\}=\sum_{i}\frac{|\vec{l}_{\perp,i}|}{Q}e^{|y_{i}-y|}\,,

(1)

where
Q
and
y
are the mass and the rapidity of the produced gauge boson. The sum runs over all particles
i
(excluding the gauge boson) with momentum
l_{i}
, transverse momentum
\vec{l}_{\perp,i}
and rapidity
y_{i}
in the final state. The momenta of the incoming partons are defined as
p_{a}^{\mu}=Qe^{+y}n_{a}^{\mu}/2
and
p_{b}^{\mu}=Qe^{-y}n_{b}^{\mu}/2
, where
n_{a,b}^{\mu}=(1,0,0,\pm 1)
are light-like reference vectors.

This observable is designed so that energetic particles collimated along the beam directions contribute minimally to
\tau
, while central emissions contribute significantly. Imposing a veto
\tau<\tau_{0}
therefore strongly suppresses central gluon radiation and effectively constrains ISR. Small values of
\tau_{0}
enhance the sensitivity to the intrinsic non-perturbative structure of TMDs.

While previous studies have explored the joint resummation of TMDs with 0-jettiness or jet veto
Jain et al. 2012
;
Procura et al. 2015
;
Monni et al. 2020
;
Lustermans et al. 2019
;
Makris et al. 2021
, we present a novel derivation that is both transparent and heuristic, aiming to enhance accessibility to a broader audience. Our formulation unifies the various soft-collinear effective theory (SCET)
Bauer et al. 2001
;
Bauer and Stewart 2001
;
Bauer et al. 2002a
;
Bauer et al. 2002b
;
Beneke et al. 2002
regimes (SCET
{}_{\text{I}}
, SCET
{}_{\text{II}}
, and SCET
+
) and is applicable in a more comprehensive kinematic region. The resulting expression is particularly suitable for phenomenological applications, as it can be readily implemented in numerical analyses. We examine the transverse momentum spectrum of the gauge boson across a wider range of scales, where the perturbative expansion is spoiled by two distinct types of large logarithms:
\ln(Q^{2}/q_{\perp}^{2})
and
\ln(1/\tau_{0})
, thereby going beyond the standard TMD resummation framework, which accounts only for the former.

To establish the framework that resums both types of logarithms simultaneously, we begin by recalling the standard TMD resummation formula for unpolarized cross section at next-to-leading logarithmic (NLL) accuracy. This serves as the baseline upon which the joint resummation formalism is constructed:

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}\sigma_{UU}}{{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}^{2}\vec{q}_{\perp}}=\sigma_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{b\,{\rm d}b}{2\pi}J_{0}(b\,q_{\perp}),

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\times\sum\limits_{q,q^{\prime}}|V_{qq^{\prime}}|^{2}f_{q}(x_{a},\mu_{b})f_{q^{\prime}}(x_{b},\mu_{b})e^{-S_{\rm P}(b)}\,,

(2)

where
\sigma_{0}
denotes the Born cross section and
|V_{qq^{\prime}}|^{2}
is the squared CKM matrix element. The parton momentum fractions are given by
x_{a}=Qe^{y}/\sqrt{s}
and
x_{b}=Qe^{-y}/\sqrt{s}
, with
\sqrt{s}
the hadronic center-of-mass energy.

To elucidate the impact of the 0-jettiness veto on the Sudakov factor
e^{-S_{\rm P}(b)}
, we begin by reviewing the standard TMD resummation framework. For clarity, we consider gluon radiation in hemisphere ‘a’ (transverse to the beam axis), focusing on the central rapidity region
y=0
, which implies
l^{-}<l^{+}
where
l^{-}\equiv l\cdot n_{a}/\sqrt{2}
and
l^{+}\equiv l\cdot n_{b}/\sqrt{2}
. This configuration imposes the kinematic constraint
l_{\perp}/\sqrt{2}<l^{+}<p_{a}^{+}=Q/\sqrt{2}
with
l_{\perp}=|\vec{l}_{\perp}|
. The real-emission correction includes collinear and soft contributions, governed by the unregularized DGLAP splitting kernel,
\int_{0}^{1-l_{\perp}\!/Q}\!{\rm d}z\,(1+z^{2})/(1-z)
, with
1-z=l^{+}/p_{a}^{+}
. Notably, this kinematic constraint provides a natural regularization of the light-cone divergence as
z\rightarrow 1
. The full real-emission correction can be decomposed as

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{2\pi^{2}}\!\!\int_{0}^{Q^{2}}\!\frac{{\rm d}^{2}\vec{l}_{\perp}}{l^{2}_{\perp}}\!\left\{\int_{0}^{1}{\rm d}z\left[\frac{1+z^{2}}{(1-z)_{+}}+\frac{3}{2}\delta(1-z)\right]\right.\

\displaystyle\left.\ \ \ -\frac{3}{2}+\int_{0}^{1-l_{\perp}/Q}{\rm d}z\,\frac{2}{1-z}\right\}\,,

(3)

where the terms on the first line, combined with the corresponding virtual corrections, drive the DGLAP evolution of PDFs, while the second line generates the leading double and single logarithms characteristic of TMD factorization. According to the standard Collins-Soper-Sterman (CSS) formalism
Collins 2011
, these logarithmic contributions exponentiate, together with the virtual corrections, into the perturbative Sudakov factor in impact parameter
b
space:

\displaystyle S_{\text{P}}(b)\!=\!\frac{C_{F}}{\pi}\int_{\mu_{b}^{2}}^{Q^{2}}\!\!\frac{{\rm d}\mu^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\left(\ln\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\!-\!\frac{3}{2}\right)\alpha_{s}(\mu)\,,

(4)

where
\mu_{b}\equiv 2\,e^{-\gamma_{E}}/b
.

Imposing a veto with
\tau_{0}\ll 1
restricts the phase space available for ISRs, significantly modifying the logarithmic structure of the cross section. Specifically, this constraint modifies the minimal value of
l^{+}
to
l^{2}_{\perp}Q/(\sqrt{2}t)
, where
t=\tau_{0}Q^{2}
, and bounds the integration of radiated gluon transverse momentum by
t
. Under these constraints, the leading double-logarithmic contribution from gluon radiation in hemisphere ‘a’ takes the following modified form:

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{\pi^{2}}\!\!\int_{0}^{t}\!\frac{{\rm d}^{2}\vec{l}_{\perp}}{l^{2}_{\perp}}\!\int^{p_{a}^{+}}_{\frac{l^{2}_{\perp}Q}{\sqrt{2}t}}\frac{{\rm d}l^{+}}{l^{+}}\left(e^{i\vec{b}\cdot\vec{l}_{\perp}}-1\right)\approx-\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{2\pi}\ln^{2}\!\frac{t}{\mu_{b}^{2}}.

(5)

However, when
l^{+}<l_{\perp}/\sqrt{2}
, the gluon is emitted into the opposite hemisphere. Requiring consistency with
l_{\perp}>l^{2}_{\perp}Q/t
leads to the condition
l^{2}_{\perp}<\tau_{0}t
. To avoid double-counting in this phase space, we subtract the following contribution:

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{\pi^{2}}\!\!\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}t}\!\!\frac{{\rm d}^{2}\vec{l}_{\perp}}{l^{2}_{\perp}}\!\!\int^{\frac{l_{\perp}}{\sqrt{2}}}_{\frac{l^{2}_{\perp}Q}{\sqrt{2}t}}\frac{{\rm d}l^{+}}{l^{+}}\left(e^{i\vec{b}\cdot\vec{l}_{\perp}}-1\right)\approx\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{4\pi}\ln^{2}\!\frac{\tau_{0}t}{\mu_{b}^{2}}.

(6)

Following the procedure outlined in Eq. (
3
), once the TMD is matched onto the PDF at its natural scale
t
, a single logarithm remains to be resummed,

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{\pi^{2}}\frac{3}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\!\frac{{\rm d}^{2}\vec{l}_{\perp}}{l^{2}_{\perp}}\left(e^{i\vec{b}\cdot\vec{l}_{\perp}}-1\right)\approx-\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{\pi}\frac{3}{2}\ln\frac{t}{\mu_{b}^{2}}\,.

(7)

The virtual corrections are unaffected by the 0-jettiness. In addition to the components paired with the real emission, there are further contributions from the two phase space regions. In the region
\tau_{0}t<l^{2}_{\perp}<t
with
l_{\perp}/\sqrt{2}<l^{+}<l^{2}_{\perp}Q/(\sqrt{2}t)
, the real emissions are vetoed while the virtual corrections remain:

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{\pi^{2}}\int_{\tau_{0}t}^{t}\frac{{\rm d}^{2}\vec{l}_{\perp}}{l^{2}_{\perp}}\int_{\frac{l_{\perp}}{\sqrt{2}}}^{\frac{l^{2}_{\perp}Q}{\sqrt{2}t}}\frac{{\rm d}l^{+}}{l^{+}}=\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{4\pi}\ln^{2}\frac{1}{\tau_{0}}\,.

(8)

The virtual contribution above the scale
t
to single-logarithmic accuracy is given by:

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{2\pi}\left[\frac{1}{2}\ln^{2}\frac{Q^{2}}{t}-\frac{3}{2}\ln\frac{Q}{t}\right]\,,

(9)

yielding a global suppression factor after carrying out all order resummation.

Combining the contributions from two hemispheres and collecting all pieces together, one eventually ends up with the following Sudakov factor for the isolated Drell-Yan like process,

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!S_{\text{P}}(b)\!=\!\frac{C_{F}}{\pi}\!\left[\int_{\mu_{b}^{2}}^{t}\!\!\frac{{\rm d}\mu^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\left(2\ln\frac{t}{\mu^{2}}-\frac{3}{2}\right)-\int_{\mu_{b}^{2}}^{\tau_{0}t}\!\!\frac{{\rm d}\mu^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\ln\frac{\tau_{0}t}{\mu^{2}}\right.\

\displaystyle\!\!\left.\ \!\!+\!\int_{t}^{Q^{2}}\!\!\frac{{\rm d}\mu^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\left(\ln\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\!-\!\frac{3}{2}\right)+\!\int^{t}_{\tau_{0}t}\!\!\frac{{\rm d}\mu^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\ln\frac{\mu^{2}}{\tau_{0}t}\right]\!\!\alpha_{s}(\mu)\,,

(10)

where the Heaviside
\theta
functions
\theta(t-\mu_{b}^{2})
and
\theta(\tau_{0}t-\mu_{b}^{2})
in the first term and second term are suppressed for simplicity, respectively. A rigorous formulation of this joint resummation within the SCET formalism is provided in the Supplemental Material.

Impact of 0-jettiness on spin asymmetries
— We now examine the phenomenological implications of this approach for probing the spin structure of the nucleon. As an example, we focus on the SSAs for

Accordingly, the analysis presented in the previous section extends directly to the polarization-dependent case, yielding the same perturbative Sudakov factor for the Sivers distribution. Conclusions and outlook
—
In this Letter, we establish a novel method for probing the nucleon’s spin structure by employing a 0-jettiness veto to suppress TMD evolution effects. This approach directly addresses the long-standing challenge of disentangling non-perturbative dynamics from perturbative radiations. To this end, we derive a
k_{t}
-resummation formalism for exclusive 0-jet events and demonstrate its phenomenological impact by analyzing single-spin asymmetries (SSAs) in
W^{\pm}/Z^{0}
production at RHIC. We find that the veto leads to a remarkable enhancement of the asymmetry, enabling a more definitive test of the predicted sign change of the Sivers function.

This approach broadens the potential of TMD studies across a variety of experimental settings. The ability to systematically tune the veto scale, or to employ other veto observables like jet transverse momentum or vector angularities
Bijl et al. 2024
, provides a powerful new lever to isolate intrinsic parton motion and constrain the non-perturbative Sudakov factor
Collins and Rogers 2015
. The framework’s versatility extends to studying TMDs in di-jet and electron-jet production at the EIC, while offering particular advantages for TMD measurements at the LHC, where evolution effects are more pronounced. These developments pave the way for a new era of precision in mapping the nucleon’s 3D structure.

SCET
+
:
\tau_{0}^{2}Q^{2}\ll q_{\perp}^{2}\ll\tau_{0}Q^{2}\ll Q^{2}
;

•

SCET
II
:
\tau_{0}^{2}Q^{2}\sim q_{\perp}^{2}\ll\tau_{0}Q^{2}\ll Q^{2}
;

These regimes correspond to different momentum scalings of the hard, collinear, soft and collinear-soft modes. Fig.
5
provides a schematic summary of the mode structure in each regime.

SCET
I
Regime

In the SCET
I
regime, defined by the hierarchy
\tau_{0}^{2}Q^{2}\ll q_{\perp}^{2}\sim\tau_{0}Q^{2}\ll Q^{2}
, the factorization formula for the differential cross-section is given by:

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}\sigma_{\rm I}(\tau_{0})}{{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}^{2}\vec{q}_{\perp}}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{0}\,H(Q,\mu)\sum_{a,b}C_{ab}\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}Q}\!{\rm d}{\cal T}\int\!{\rm d}t_{a}\int\!{\rm d}^{2}\vec{k}_{a}\,{\mathcal{B}}_{a/p}(t_{a},x_{a},\vec{k}_{a},\mu)\int\!{\rm d}t_{b}\int\!{\rm d}^{2}\vec{k}_{b}\,{\mathcal{B}}_{b/p}(t_{b},x_{b},\vec{k}_{b},\mu)

\displaystyle\quad\times\int\!{\rm d}k\,{\mathcal{S}}(k,\mu)\,\delta^{(2)}\left({\vec{q}}_{\perp}-\vec{k}_{a}-\vec{k}_{b}\right)\delta\left({\cal T}-\frac{t_{a}}{Q}-\frac{t_{b}}{Q}-k\right)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{0}\,H(Q,\mu)\sum_{a,b}C_{ab}\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}Q}\!{\rm d}{\cal T}\int\!\frac{e^{s{\cal T}}{\rm d}s}{2\pi i}\int\!\frac{b\,{\rm d}b}{2\pi}J_{0}(b\,q_{\perp})\,{\mathcal{B}}_{a/p}(s/Q,x_{a},b,\mu)\,{\mathcal{B}}_{b/p}(s/Q,x_{b},b,\mu)\,{\mathcal{S}}(s,\mu)\,,

(S-1)

where
\sigma_{0}=\sqrt{2}\pi G_{F}Q^{2}/(sN_{c})
, and
C_{ab}=|V_{ab}|^{2}
for
W^{\pm}
production and
C_{ab}=(V_{a}^{2}+A_{a}^{2})\delta_{a,-b}
for
Z^{0}
production. Here,
H(Q,\mu)
is the hard function, encoding the short-distance partonic scattering at the hard scale
Q
. The function
{\mathcal{B}}_{a/p}(s/Q,x_{a},b,\mu)
denotes the double-differential beam function for parton
a
originating from proton
p
; it incorporates the effects of initial-state radiation and is expressed in
b
-space (Fourier conjugate to the transverse momentum
\vec{k}_{a}
) and
s
-space (Laplace conjugate to the
0
-jettiness variable
t_{a}/Q
).
{\mathcal{S}}(s,\mu)
is the soft function, which accounts for soft gluon emissions contributing to the
0
-jettiness measurement. The factorization formula is first presented in momentum space, and then in
b
- and
s
-space, where the convolutions over transverse momenta and
0
-jettiness are replaced by simple products. The Bessel function
J_{0}(b\,q_{\perp})
arises from the Fourier transform between
b
-space and transverse momentum space.

The evolution of these functions with the renormalization scale
\mu
is governed by the following renormalization group (RG) equations in
b
-space and
s
-space:

\displaystyle\mu\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\mu}{\mathcal{B}}_{q/p}(s/Q,x_{a},b,\mu)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{\mathcal{B}}^{q}(s/Q,\mu){\mathcal{B}}_{q/p}(s/Q,x_{a},b,\mu)\,,

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathcal{B}}^{q}(s/Q,\mu)

\displaystyle=-2\Gamma_{\rm cusp}^{q}(\alpha_{s})\ln\frac{Q}{\mu^{2}se^{\gamma_{E}}}+\gamma_{\mathcal{B}}^{q}(\alpha_{s})\,,

(S-2)

\displaystyle\mu\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\mu}{\mathcal{S}}(s,\mu)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{\mathcal{S}}^{q}(s,\mu){\mathcal{S}}(s,\mu)\,,

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathcal{S}}^{q}(s,\mu)

\displaystyle=2\Gamma_{\rm cusp}^{q}(\alpha_{s})\ln\frac{1}{\mu^{2}s^{2}e^{2\gamma_{E}}}+\gamma_{\mathcal{S}}^{q}(\alpha_{s})\,,

(S-3)

\displaystyle\mu\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\mu}H(Q,\mu)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{H}^{q}(Q,\mu)H(Q,\mu)\,,

\displaystyle\gamma_{H}^{q}(Q,\mu)

\displaystyle=2\Gamma_{\rm cusp}^{q}(\alpha_{s})\ln\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}}+2\gamma_{V}^{q}(\alpha_{s})\,.

(S-4)

These equations describe how the double-differential beam functions (
{\mathcal{B}}_{q/p}
), the beam thrust soft function (
{\mathcal{S}}
), and the hard function (
H
) change with the renormalization scale
\mu
, as dictated by their respective anomalous dimensions
\gamma_{\mathcal{B}}^{q}
,
\gamma_{\mathcal{S}}^{q}
, and 2
\gamma_{V}^{q}
. The cusp anomalous dimension
\Gamma_{\rm cusp}^{q}
and non-cusp anomalous dimensions
\gamma_{\mathcal{B}}^{q}(\alpha_{s})
,
\gamma_{\mathcal{S}}^{q}(\alpha_{s})
,
2\gamma_{V}^{q}(\alpha_{s})
appear in these expressions.
All the corresponding anomalous dimensions in this section are shown in the following section. The canonical choices for the renormalization scales associated with each function in this regime are set to minimize large logarithms in perturbative calculations:

\displaystyle\mu_{H}^{\rm I}\sim Q\,,\qquad\mu_{\mathcal{B}}^{\rm I}\sim\sqrt{\tau_{0}}Q\,,\qquad\mu_{\mathcal{S}}^{\rm I}\sim\tau_{0}Q\,.

(S-5)

These scales reflect the characteristic momentum scales of the hard, collinear (beam), and soft interactions, respectively. After implementing the one-loop
anomalous dimensions and integrating
s
and
\tau
, we define the perturbative Sudakov factor
S_{\text{P}}(b)
as

\displaystyle S_{\text{P}}(b)\!

\displaystyle=\!\frac{C_{F}}{\pi}\!\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}Q}\!{\rm d}{\cal T}\int\!\frac{e^{s{\cal T}}{\rm d}s}{2\pi i}\left[\int_{\tau_{0}Q^{2}}^{\mu_{b}^{2}}\!\!\frac{{\rm d}\mu^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\left(2\ln\frac{Q}{\mu^{2}se^{\gamma_{E}}}-\frac{3}{2}\right)-\int_{\tau_{0}^{2}Q^{2}}^{\mu_{b}^{2}}\!\!\frac{{\rm d}\mu^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\ln\frac{1}{\mu^{2}s^{2}e^{2\gamma_{E}}}+\!\int_{\mu_{b}^{2}}^{Q^{2}}\!\!\frac{{\rm d}\mu^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\left(\ln\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\!-\!\frac{3}{2}\right)\right]\!\!\alpha_{s}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\!\frac{C_{F}}{\pi}\!\left[\int_{\tau_{0}Q^{2}}^{\mu_{b}^{2}}\!\!\frac{{\rm d}\mu^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\left(2\ln\frac{\tau_{0}Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}}-\frac{3}{2}\right)-\int_{\tau_{0}^{2}Q^{2}}^{\mu_{b}^{2}}\!\!\frac{{\rm d}\mu^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\ln\frac{\tau_{0}^{2}Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}}+\!\int_{\mu_{b}^{2}}^{Q^{2}}\!\!\frac{{\rm d}\mu^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\left(\ln\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\!-\!\frac{3}{2}\right)\right]\!\!\alpha_{s}(\mu)\,.

(S-6)

This factor exponentiates the dominant logarithmic contributions arising from the evolution of the functions from their canonical scales (Eq. (
S-5
)) to a common scale
\mu_{b}=b_{0}/b
with
b_{0}\equiv 2e^{-\gamma_{\rm E}}
.

SCET
II
Regime

Next, we consider the SCET
II
regime, characterized by the scale hierarchy
\tau_{0}^{2}Q^{2}\sim q_{\perp}^{2}\ll\tau_{0}Q^{2}\ll Q^{2}
. In this regime, the factorization formula takes the form:

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}\sigma_{\rm II}(\tau_{0})}{{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}^{2}\vec{q}_{\perp}}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{0}\,H(Q,\mu)\sum_{a,b}C_{ab}\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}Q}\!{\rm d}{\cal T}\int\!{\rm d}^{2}\vec{k}_{a}\,{B}_{a/p}(x_{a},\vec{k}_{a},\mu,\nu/\omega_{a})\int\!{\rm d}^{2}\vec{k}_{b}\,{B}_{b/p}(x_{b},\vec{k}_{b},\mu,\nu/\omega_{b})

(S-7)

\displaystyle\quad\times\int\!{\rm d}k\,S(k,\vec{k}_{s},\mu,\nu)\,\delta^{(2)}\left({\vec{q}}_{\perp}-\vec{k}_{a}-\vec{k}_{b}-\vec{k}_{s}\right)\delta\left({\cal T}-k\right)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{0}\,H(Q,\mu)\sum_{a,b}C_{ab}\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}Q}\!{\rm d}{\cal T}\int\!\frac{e^{s{\cal T}}{\rm d}s}{2\pi i}\int\!\frac{b\,{\rm d}b}{2\pi}J_{0}(b\,q_{\perp})\,{B}_{a/p}(x_{a},b,\mu,\nu/\omega_{a})\,{B}_{b/p}(x_{b},b,\mu,\nu/\omega_{b})\,S(s,b,\mu,\nu)\,,

with
\omega_{a}=Qe^{+y}
and
\omega_{b}=Qe^{-y}
.
In this expression,
H(Q,\mu)
is the hard function.
{B}_{a/p}(x_{a},b,\mu,\nu/\omega_{a})
represents the standard TMD PDF, which depends on both the renormalization scale
\mu
and the rapidity scale
\nu
.
S(s,b,\mu,\nu)
is the double-differential soft function, also depending on
\mu
and
\nu
.

The RG and rapidity-RG (RRG) equations for the functions in this regime, describing their evolution with respect to both
\mu
and
\nu
, are:

\displaystyle\mu\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\mu}{B}_{q/p}(x,b,\mu,\nu/\omega)

\displaystyle={\gamma}_{B}^{q}(\mu,\nu/\omega){B}_{q/p}(x,b,\mu,\nu/\omega)\,,

\displaystyle\nu\frac{{\rm d}}{d\nu}{B}_{q/p}(x,b,\mu,\nu/\omega)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}{\gamma}_{\nu}^{q}(b,\mu){B}_{q/p}(x,b,\mu,\nu/\omega)\,,

\displaystyle\gamma_{B}^{q}(\mu,\nu/\omega)

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\rm cusp}^{q}(\alpha_{s})\ln\frac{\nu^{2}}{\omega^{2}}+\gamma_{B}^{q}(\alpha_{s})\,,

\displaystyle{\gamma}_{\nu}^{q}(b,\mu)

\displaystyle=2\left[\int_{\mu^{2}}^{\mu_{b}^{2}}\frac{{\rm d}\mu^{\prime 2}}{\mu^{\prime 2}}\Gamma_{\rm cusp}^{q}(\alpha_{s})+\gamma_{r}^{q}(\alpha_{s})\right]\,,

(S-8)

\displaystyle\mu\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\mu}S(s,b,\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{S}^{q}(\mu,\nu)S(s,b,\mu,\nu)\,,

\displaystyle\nu\frac{{\rm d}}{d\nu}S(s,b,\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle={\gamma}_{\nu}^{q}(b,\mu)S(s,b,\mu,\nu)\,,

\displaystyle\gamma_{S}^{q}(\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle=2\Gamma_{\rm cusp}^{q}(\alpha_{s})\ln\frac{\mu^{2}}{\nu^{2}}+\gamma_{S}^{q}(\alpha_{s})\,,

(S-9)

\displaystyle\mu\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\mu}H(Q,\mu)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{H}^{q}(Q,\mu)H(Q,\mu)\,,

\displaystyle\gamma_{H}^{q}(Q,\mu)

\displaystyle=2\Gamma_{\rm cusp}^{q}(\alpha_{s})\ln\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}}+2\gamma_{V}^{q}(\alpha_{s})\,,

(S-10)

These equations detail the scale dependence of the TMD beam functions (
B_{q/p}
), the double-differential soft function (
S
), and the hard function (
H
). The TMD functions evolve with both the renormalization scale
\mu
(governed by anomalous dimensions
\gamma_{B}^{q},\gamma_{S}^{q}
) and the rapidity scale
\nu
(governed by the rapidity anomalous dimension
\gamma_{\nu}^{q}
).
The canonical scales for the functions in the SCET
II
regime are:

\displaystyle\mu_{H}^{\rm II}\sim Q\,,\qquad

\displaystyle\mu_{B}^{\rm II}\sim\mu_{b}\,,\qquad\mu_{S}^{\rm II}\sim\mu_{b}\,,

\displaystyle\nu_{B}^{\rm II}\sim Q\,,\qquad\ \nu_{S}^{\rm II}\sim\mu_{b}\,.

(S-11)

Here,
\mu_{b}
is the scale associated with the transverse momentum
q_{\perp}
.

Therefore, the perturbative Sudakov factor for the SCET
II
regime, which resums logarithms associated with the evolution from canonical scales, is:

\displaystyle S_{\text{P}}(b)\!

\displaystyle=\!\frac{C_{F}}{\pi}\!\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}Q}\!{\rm d}{\cal T}\int\!\frac{e^{s{\cal T}}{\rm d}s}{2\pi i}\left[\!\int_{\mu_{b}^{2}}^{Q^{2}}\!\!\frac{{\rm d}\mu^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\left(\ln\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\!-\!\frac{3}{2}\right)\right]\!\!\alpha_{s}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\!\frac{C_{F}}{\pi}\!\left[\!\int_{\mu_{b}^{2}}^{Q^{2}}\!\!\frac{{\rm d}\mu^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\left(\ln\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\!-\!\frac{3}{2}\right)\right]\!\!\alpha_{s}(\mu)\,.

(S-12)

This factor primarily accounts for the evolution of the hard function and the
\mu
-evolution part of the TMD functions from
\mu_{b}
to
Q
. This regime corresponds to standard TMD evolution.

SCET
+
Regime

The third regime, SCET
+
, is defined by the hierarchy
\tau_{0}^{2}Q^{2}\ll q_{\perp}^{2}\ll\tau_{0}Q^{2}
. The factorization formula in this intermediate regime is:

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}\sigma_{\rm+}(\tau_{0})}{{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}^{2}\vec{q}_{\perp}}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{0}\,H(Q,\mu)\sum_{a,b}C_{ab}\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}Q}\!{\rm d}{\cal T}\int\!{\rm d}^{2}\vec{k}_{a}\,{B}_{a/p}(x_{a},\vec{k}_{a},\mu,\nu/\omega_{a})\int\!{\rm d}^{2}\vec{k}_{b}\,{B}_{b/p}(x_{b},\vec{k}_{b},\mu,\nu/\omega_{b})

\displaystyle\quad\times\int\!{\rm d}l_{a}^{+}\int\!{\rm d}^{2}\vec{l}_{a}\,{\tilde{\cal S}}_{a}(l_{a}^{+},\vec{l}_{a},\mu,\nu)\int\!{\rm d}l_{b}^{-}\int\!{\rm d}^{2}\vec{l}_{b}\,{\tilde{\cal S}}_{b}(l_{b}^{-},\vec{l}_{b},\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle\quad\times\int\!{\rm d}k\,{\mathcal{S}}(k,\mu)\,\delta^{(2)}\left({\vec{q}}_{\perp}-\vec{k}_{a}-\vec{k}_{b}-\vec{l}_{a}-\vec{l}_{b}\right)\delta\left(\tau-l_{a}^{+}-l_{b}^{-}-k\right)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{0}\,H(Q,\mu)\sum_{a,b}C_{ab}\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}Q}\!{\rm d}{\cal T}\int\!\frac{e^{s{\cal T}}{\rm d}s}{2\pi i}\int\!\frac{b\,{\rm d}b}{2\pi}J_{0}(b\,q_{\perp})\,{B}_{a/p}(x_{a},b,\mu,\nu/\omega_{a})\,{B}_{b/p}(x_{b},b,\mu,\nu/\omega_{b})

\displaystyle\quad\times{\tilde{\cal S}}_{a}(s,b,\mu,\nu)\,{\tilde{\cal S}}_{b}(s,b,\mu,\nu)\,{\mathcal{S}}(s,\mu)\,,

(S-13)

Here,
H(Q)
is the hard function, and
{B}_{a/p}(x_{a},b)
is the standard TMD beam function, similar to SCET
II
. A new element is
{\tilde{\cal S}}_{a(b)}(s,b)
, the double-differential collinear-soft function, which describes collinear-soft emissions sensitive to both 0-jettiness (via
s
) and the transverse momentum (via
b
), respectively. Finally,
{\mathcal{S}}(s)
is the beam thrust soft function, similar to that in SCET
I
. The formula is presented in momentum space and its
b
-space Fourier transform.

The RG equations in
b
-space governing the evolution of these functions are:

\displaystyle\mu\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\mu}{B}_{q/p}(x,b,\mu,\nu/\omega)

\displaystyle={\gamma}_{B}^{q}(\mu,\nu/\omega){B}_{q/p}(x,b,\mu,\nu/\omega)\,,

\displaystyle\nu\frac{{\rm d}}{d\nu}{B}_{q/p}(x,b,\mu,\nu/\omega)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}{\gamma}_{\nu}^{q}(b,\mu){B}_{q/p}(x,b,\mu,\nu/\omega)\,,

\displaystyle\gamma_{B}^{q}(\mu,\nu/\omega)

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\rm cusp}^{q}(\alpha_{s})\ln\frac{\nu^{2}}{\omega^{2}}+\gamma_{B}^{q}(\alpha_{s})\,,

\displaystyle{\gamma}_{\nu}^{q}(b,\mu)

\displaystyle=2\left[\int_{\mu^{2}}^{\mu_{b}^{2}}\frac{{\rm d}\mu^{\prime 2}}{\mu^{\prime 2}}\Gamma_{\rm cusp}^{q}(\alpha_{s})+\gamma_{r}^{q}(\alpha_{s})\right]\,,

(S-14)

\displaystyle\mu\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\mu}{\tilde{\cal S}}(s,b,\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle={\tilde{\gamma}}_{\cal S}^{q}(s,\mu,\nu){\tilde{\cal S}}(s,b,\mu,\nu)\,,

\displaystyle\nu\frac{{\rm d}}{d\nu}{\tilde{\cal S}}(s,b,\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}{\gamma}_{\nu}^{q}(b,\mu){\tilde{\cal S}}(s,b,\mu,\nu)\,,

\displaystyle{\tilde{\gamma}}_{\cal S}^{q}(s,\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle=-2\Gamma_{\rm cusp}^{q}(\alpha_{s})\ln\frac{\nu}{\mu^{2}se^{\gamma_{E}}}+{\tilde{\gamma}}_{\cal S}^{q}(\alpha_{s})\,,

(S-15)

\displaystyle\mu\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\mu}{\mathcal{S}}(s,\mu)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{\mathcal{S}}^{q}(s,\mu){\mathcal{S}}(s,\mu)\,,

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathcal{S}}^{q}(s,\mu)

\displaystyle=2\Gamma_{\rm cusp}^{q}(\alpha_{s})\ln\frac{1}{\mu^{2}s^{2}e^{2\gamma_{E}}}+\gamma_{\mathcal{S}}^{q}(\alpha_{s})\,,

(S-16)

\displaystyle\mu\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\mu}H(Q,\mu)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{H}^{q}(Q,\mu)H(Q,\mu)\,,

\displaystyle\gamma_{H}^{q}(Q,\mu)

\displaystyle=2\Gamma_{\rm cusp}^{q}(\alpha_{s})\ln\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}}+2\gamma_{V}^{q}(\alpha_{s})\,.

(S-17)

The canonical scales for the functions in the SCET
+
regime are set as follows:

\displaystyle\mu_{H}^{\rm+}\sim Q\,,\qquad

\displaystyle\mu_{B}^{\rm+}\sim\mu_{b}\,,\qquad\mu_{\tilde{\cal S}}^{\rm+}\sim\mu_{b}\,,\qquad\mu_{\mathcal{S}}^{\rm+}\sim\tau_{0}Q\,,

\displaystyle\nu_{B}^{\rm+}\sim Q\,,\qquad\ \nu_{\tilde{\cal S}}^{\rm+}\sim\frac{\mu_{b}^{2}}{\tau_{0}Q}\,.

(S-18)

The perturbative Sudakov factor for the SCET
+
regime is:

\displaystyle S_{\text{P}}(b)\!

\displaystyle=\!\frac{C_{F}}{\pi}\!\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}Q}\!{\rm d}{\cal T}\int\!\frac{e^{s{\cal T}}{\rm d}s}{2\pi i}\left[-\int_{\tau_{0}^{2}Q^{2}}^{\mu_{b}^{2}}\!\!\frac{{\rm d}\mu^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\ln\frac{1}{\mu^{2}s^{2}e^{2\gamma_{E}}}+\!\int_{\mu_{b}^{2}}^{Q^{2}}\!\!\frac{{\rm d}\mu^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\left(\ln\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\!-\!\frac{3}{2}\right)\right]\!\!\alpha_{s}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\!\frac{C_{F}}{\pi}\!\left[-\int_{\tau_{0}^{2}Q^{2}}^{\mu_{b}^{2}}\!\!\frac{{\rm d}\mu^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\ln\frac{\tau_{0}^{2}Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}}+\!\int_{\mu_{b}^{2}}^{Q^{2}}\!\!\frac{{\rm d}\mu^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\left(\ln\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\!-\!\frac{3}{2}\right)\right]\!\!\alpha_{s}(\mu)\,,

(S-19)

This factor resums the large logarithms arising from the evolution of the various functions from their respective canonical scales (Eq. (
SCET
+
Regime
)) to the common scales
\mu_{b}
.

Anomalous Dimensions and Combined Perturbative Sudakov Factor

The cusp and non-cusp anomalous dimensions are expanded as

\Gamma^{q}_{\rm cusp}({\alpha_{s}})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Gamma^{q}_{n}\Bigl(\frac{{\alpha_{s}}}{4\pi}\Bigr)^{n+1}\,,\qquad\gamma^{q}({\alpha_{s}})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{q}_{n}\Bigl(\frac{{\alpha_{s}}}{4\pi}\Bigr)^{n+1}\,.

(S-20)

All the one-loop anomalous dimensions present in the previous section are given by

\displaystyle\Gamma^{q}_{0}

\displaystyle=4C_{F}\,,

(S-21)

\displaystyle\gamma^{q}_{{\cal B}\,0}

\displaystyle=\gamma^{q}_{{B}\,0}=6C_{F}\,,

(S-22)

\displaystyle\gamma^{q}_{{\cal S}\,0}

\displaystyle=\gamma^{q}_{{S}\,0}={\tilde{\gamma}}^{q}_{{\cal S}\,0}=\gamma^{q}_{{r}\,0}=0\,,

(S-23)

\displaystyle\gamma^{q}_{V\,0}

\displaystyle=-6C_{F}\,,

(S-24)

To obtain a unified description across the different kinematic regions defined by the scale
\mu_{b}\sim 1/b
, we combine the results from the three SCET regimes. Specifically:

•

When
\mu_{b}^{2}>\tau_{0}Q^{2}
(large
b
, meaning
q_{\perp}
is small relative to
\sqrt{\tau_{0}}Q
), the DGLAP evolution of PDFs is frozen and instead replaced by scale evolution of beam functions. Hence the SCET
I
formulation is appropriate, which is consistent with Ref.
Stewart et al. 2010c
.

•

When
\tau_{0}^{2}Q^{2}<\mu_{b}^{2}<\tau_{0}Q^{2}
(intermediate
b
), the SCET
+
formulation is used.

•

When
\mu_{b}^{2}<\tau_{0}^{2}Q^{2}
(small
b
, meaning
q_{\perp}
is large relative to
\tau_{0}Q
), the SCET
II
formulation, which corresponds to standard TMD evolution, is applied.

We implement the transitions between these regimes using Heaviside
\theta
functions, resulting in the final combined perturbative Sudakov factor:

\displaystyle S_{\text{P}}(b)\!

\displaystyle=\!\frac{C_{F}}{\pi}\!\left[\int_{\tau_{0}Q^{2}}^{\mu_{b}^{2}}\!\!\frac{{\rm d}\mu^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\left(2\ln\frac{\tau_{0}Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}}-\frac{3}{2}\right)\,\theta(\mu_{b}^{2}-\tau_{0}Q^{2})-\int_{\tau_{0}^{2}Q^{2}}^{\mu_{b}^{2}}\!\!\frac{{\rm d}\mu^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\ln\frac{\tau_{0}^{2}Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\,\theta(\mu_{b}^{2}-\tau_{0}^{2}Q^{2})\right.\

\displaystyle\quad\!\!\left.\ +\!\int_{\mu_{b}^{2}}^{Q^{2}}\!\!\frac{{\rm d}\mu^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\left(\ln\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\!-\!\frac{3}{2}\right)\right]\!\!\alpha_{s}(\mu)\,.

(S-25)

which is consistent with the Sudakov factor given by Eq. (
Nucleon Tomography with 0-jettiness
) in the main text.

Neoadjuvant Hepatic Arterial Infusion Chemotherapy with FOLFOX Plus Tislelizumab for Resectable Hepatocellular Carcinoma Beyond the Milan Criteria: Efficacy, Safety and Biomarker Exploration
Authors: Quanwei Dai, Huiting Deng, Jun Wang, Jingxiang Shi, Jianmin Ding, Yanying Gao, Wei Sun, Yunzhi Shen, Ximo Wang, Cheng Lou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThis study aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of hepatic arterial infusion chemotherapy of oxaliplatin, fluorouracil, and leucovorin (FOLFOX-HAIC) combined with tislelizumab as a neoadjuvant regimen in patients with resectable hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) beyond the Milan criteria, and to explore predictive biomarkers of treatment response.Results26 patients completed neoadjuvant therapy, with a median tumor size of 6.35cm. The objective response rate according to mRECIST criteria reached 57.7%, and the disease control rate was 96.2%. 22 patients underwent radical surgery, and 10 patients (10/22, 45.5%) achieved major pathological response (residual viable tumor ≤10%). Six patients (27.3%) achieved complete pathological response, and 25 patients (96.2%) experienced at least one treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) The most common TRAEs were elevated transaminases (76.9%), HAIC-related pain (34.6%). Transcriptomic analysis revealed that differential genes between responding and non-responding tumors primarily involved pathways related to bile acid secretion and fatty acid metabolism. Metabolomic analysis showed elevated serum chenodeoxycholic acid (CDCA) in non-responders and elevated tumor glycocholic acid (GCA). Microbiome analysis further confirmed increased abundance of bile acid metabolism-related bacteria such as bacteroides in non-responders. Serum interleukin-6 (IL-6) levels after neoadjuvant therapy were correlated with treatment response.ConclusionFOLFOX-HAIC combined with tislelizumab as neoadjuvant therapy for HCC beyond the Milan criteria demonstrates favorable anti-tumor efficacy and controllable toxicity. The level of GCA in tumor, peripheral blood CDCA, IL-6, and fecal bacteroides may hold the potential to serve as a composite biomarker panel to predict pathological response and treatment sensitivity.Keywords: hepatocellular carcinoma, neoadjuvant therapy, major pathological response, hepatic arterial infusion chemotherapy, immune checkpoint inhibitors

Introduction

Hepatectomy remains the primary curative treatment for early and intermediate-stage HCC, however, the high postoperative recurrence rate and low sensitivity to adjuvant therapies significantly limit the long-term survival benefits for patients. Currently recognized high-risk factors for HCC recurrence include tumor size >5 cm, presence of >3 tumors, vascular invasion, and poor tumor differentiation.1,2 Several large Phase III adjuvant therapy trials for HCC such as STORM3 and IMbrave0504 have failed to demonstrate efficacy. The mechanism of action of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) suggests that advancing immunotherapy to the preoperative setting may offer greater advantages. Multiple studies have begun exploring the use of ICIs for neoadjuvant therapy in high-risk recurrent HCC, with favorable outcomes reported for both monotherapy and combination regimens.5–7 These studies support the feasibility of neoadjuvant therapy for HCC. The advantage of local regional treatment for HCC is to directly kill the tumor, shrink tumor volume, and may lead to the release of neoantigens, thereby enhancing the efficacy of ICIs. The combination of these two approaches theoretically has an advantage in neoadjuvant treatment for HCC, but there is insufficient evidence for its practical application. Patients receiving hepatic arterial infusion chemotherapy with fluorouracil, leucovorin, and oxaliplatin (FOLFOX-HAIC) compared to transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) as initial treatment for locally advanced HCC, demonstrated a higher objective response rate, longer median overall survival (OS) and prolonged median progression-free survival (PFS).8 However, there is a lack of relevant research reports on the efficacy and safety of FOLFOX-HAIC combined with ICI as a neoadjuvant therapy in patients with HCC. Tislelizumab achieved the first positive results in a Phase III study9 of immune monotherapy as first-line treatment for advanced HCC, providing context for its involvement in neoadjuvant therapy. Additionally, neoadjuvant strategies offer a unique opportunity to test the sensitivity of tumors to treatment in vivo, which may contribute to the development of predictive biomarkers. In this context, this single-arm clinical trial aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of FOLFOX-HAIC combined with tislelizumab as a neoadjuvant treatment for patients with resectable HCC beyond the Milan criteria. Through multi-omics analysis of tumor samples, blood samples, and stool samples (collected at baseline and before surgery), predictive biomarkers of treatment efficacy will be identified.

Methods

Study Design and Participants

This is a single-center, single-arm, Phase II prospective study initiated by Central Hospital, Tianjin University (Tianjin Third Central Hospital). The study protocol has been approved in November 2024 by the Ethics Committee of Tianjin Third Central Hospital (IRB2024-068-01). All procedures adhere to the ethical principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki, and all participants provided written informed consent prior to enrollment. The privacy rights of human subjects have been observed and that informed consent was obtained for experimentation with human subjects. The registration details of this trial have been published on the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry under the registration number ChiCTR2500098290.

Patients aged 18 years or older must meet all of the following criteria to be enrolled in this study. The inclusion criteria are as follows: (i) HCC confirmed by imaging or pathology, with tumors evaluated as beyond the Milan criteria; (ii) indications for surgical resection; (iii) no prior local or systemic treatment for HCC before this study; (iv) at least one measurable target lesion according to the response evaluation criteria in solid tumors (RECIST) criteria (V.1.1); (v) Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS) of 0–1 and Child-Pugh score of 7 or below (Child-Pugh class A). Exclusion criteria included (i) intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (ICC) or combined hepatocellular-cholangiocarcinoma (cHCC-CC), sarcomatoid hepatocellular carcinoma, or fibrolamellar hepatocellular carcinoma, or having other active malignant tumors besides HCC; (ii) prior radiotherapy, chemotherapy, local therapy, or systemic therapy for HCC (iii) planning to undergo or having previously received organ or allogeneic bone marrow transplantation (iv) presence of active autoimmune diseases requiring systemic treatment, use of immunosuppressive agents, or systemic or absorbable local hormone therapy for immunosuppressive purposes (dose >10 mg/day prednisone or equivalent), and continued use within 2 weeks prior to enrollment (v) clinically symptomatic ascites or pleural effusion requiring therapeutic puncture or drainage.

Study Endpoints and Assessments

The neoadjuvant therapy regimen consisted of FOLFOX-HAIC (Oxaliplatin 85 mg/m2, administered over 4 hours; Folic acid 200 mg/m2, administered over 2 hours; 5-Fluorouracil: First, administer 400 mg/m2 as an intravenous bolus over 15 minutes, followed by a continuous infusion of 1200 mg/m2 over 24 hours) combined with tislelizumab administered concurrently for a total of two cycles, with each cycle spaced 21 days apart. Surgery was scheduled at least three weeks after the last treatment. The primary endpoint of the study was Major Pathological Response (MPR), defined as the proportion of viable tumor cells in the resected tissue being ≤10%. Secondary endpoints included objective response rate (ORR) according to the modified response evaluation criteria in solid tumors (mRECIST) criteria and treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) throughout the study.

The therapeutic response was assessed using enhanced computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) more than 3 weeks after completion of the last neoadjuvant therapy, with the degree of tumor necrosis evaluated by two abdominal radiologists according to the mRECIST criteria. The assessment of tumor necrosis in pathological examinations was determined by consensus among two specialized liver pathologists. Adverse events (AEs) were systematically recorded and graded according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (NCI-CTCAE) version 5.0. Exploratory endpoints included differential genes, metabolites, and gut microbiota between treatment-responders and non-responders.

Bulk RNA Sequencing

Some patients had tumor samples collected by biopsy at baseline, and all patients undergoing surgery had samples collected from resected tumors and adjacent liver tissue. Total RNA was extracted using Trizol reagent (Thermo Fisher, 15596018). The total RNA quantity and purity were analyzed using Thermo Fisher Qubit3.0 (Thermo Fisher, Q33216) and Agilent 5300 Fragment Analyzer (Agilent, CA, USA, M5311AA), high-quality RNA samples with RIN number > 7.0 were used to construct sequencing library. At last, we performed the 2×150 bp paired-end sequencing (PE150) on an Illumina Novaseq™ X Plus (LC-Bio Technology CO. Ltd, Hangzhou, China) following the vendor’s recommended protocol. Genes differential expression analysis was performed by DESeq2 software between two different groups (and by edge R between two samples). The genes with the parameter of false discovery rate (FDR) below 0.05 and absolute fold change ≥ 2 were considered differentially expressed genes. Differentially expressed genes were then subjected to enrichment analysis of GO functions and KEGG pathways.

Targeted Bile Acid Metabolomics

The separation and quantification of target compounds were performed using an Thermo Fisher ScientificVanquish ultra performance liquid chromatograph coupled with a Thermo Fisher Scientific TSQ Altis triplequadrupole mass spectrometer. Collect fresh plasma samples and tumor tissues to measure bile acids (BAs). Weigh 50 mg of tumor tissue sample (or 100 μL plasma sample), add 500 μL of 80% methanol water, add steel bead milling, centrifuge (4°C, 20000 rcf) for 15 minutes, take the supernatant into a 1.5 mL EP tube, Centrifuge again. Take 60 μL sample extraction solution, combined with 20 μL 3-NPH, 20 μL EDC and 20 μL internal standard mixed solution, vortexed and then sonicated for 15 min, and take a 30°C water bath for 1 h. Centrifuge at 4 °C, 20000 rcf for 15 min, and take the supernatant for UPLC-MS/MS analysis. Qualitative analysis was performed by comparing the retention time and MRM fragment ions with the standard methodology, and quantitative analysis was conducted using the internal standard method. Calibration curves were constructed with the concentration of the standard as the abscissa and the ratio of the peak area of the standard to that of the internal standard as the ordinate. In this experiment, partial samples were taken and mixed to form QC samples, which were interspersed throughout the entire determination process to monitor system stability. The filled data were subjected to principal component analysis, metabolite correlation analysis and hierarchical cluster analysis. Student’s t-test was performed on the metabolite measurements of the two groups of samples, and metabolites with p-values less than 0.05 were identified as differential metabolites. Finally, KEGG enrichment analysis and network enrichment analysis were made for these metabolic differentials.

16SrRNA Gene Sequencing

DNA extraction was performed using the HiPure Stool DNA Kit. DNA concentration was measured with the Qubit® dsDNA HS Assay Kit. The sequencing library was constructed using a MetaVX Library Preparation Kit. Finally, the PCR product library was analyzed by 1.5% agarose gel electrophoresis to confirm the target fragment size at 600bp. Next generation sequencing was conducted on an Illumina Miseq/Novaseq Platform (Illumina, San Diego, USA) at laboratory. Metastats gap by comparing the differences between the analysis group rank values and rank within the group differences between whether group has significance.

Plasma Cytokine Assay

Collect fasting venous blood from untreated and completed neoadjuvant-treated HCC patients in the morning using EDTA anticoagulant tubes, centrifuge immediately at low temperature (1200g,4℃) for 10 minutes to separate plasma. Apply the LEGENDplexTM kit (Biolegend, USA) strictly following the manufacturer’s instructions, and read the results via flow cytometry. The flow cytometer model used was BD FACSDiva 8.0.1 (Becton Dickinson, USA).

Statistical Analysis

Demographics, clinical characteristics, and tumor characteristics were described using medians (Interquartile Range [IQR]) or percentages. Continuous variables were compared between groups using the Mann–Whitney U-test, with a two-tailed p value <0.05 considered statistically significant. Spearman correlation analysis was used to assess the relationship between pathological evaluation of tumor necrosis and assessment of tumor regression diameter, with both p values and correlation coefficients reported.

Results

Patient Characteristics

Between November 2024 to December 2025, 31 patients were screened for eligibility, with 27 enrolling in the trial after providing informed consent (Figure 1a). Fifteen patients underwent biopsy. As of the data cutoff in February 2026, all patients had completed post-neoadjuvant therapy evaluations. Among these, one patient with a postoperative pathological diagnosis of intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma withdrew from subsequent follow-up. Three patients had stable disease (SD) upon liver cancer assessment but declined further radical surgery due to personal and financial reasons. One patient developed lung metastasis, losing the opportunity for surgery. Twenty-three patients underwent radical hepatectomy, all achieving negative surgical margins. Demographic and clinical characteristics are summarized in Table 1. The median age was 65.5 years, with 20 male patients (76.9%). 19 patients (73.1%) had hepatitis B or hepatitis C virus. 69.2% had BCLC stage A disease and 2 patients had portal vein tumor thrombus, classified as BCLC stage C. The median maximum tumor diameter was 6.35 cm, and 11 patients (42.31%) had an initial alpha-fetoprotein level exceeding 400 ng/mL.

Efficacy Outcomes

Radiological assessment was conducted on 26 cases of HCC that completed neoadjuvant therapy. According to the mRECIST criteria, the overall response rate (ORR) was 57.7%, with one case (3.9%) achieving complete response (CR), and the disease control rate (DCR) was 96.2% (Table 2). In contrast, according to the RECIST v1.1 criteria, the waterfall plot (Figure 1b) shows stable disease in most of the tumors and the ORR was only 30.8%. Among the 22 HCC patients who underwent surgical resection, pathological evaluation revealed that 10 patients (45.5%) achieved MPR, and 6 patients (27.3%) achieved complete pathological response (pCR). According to mRECIST, all patients who achieved a MPR got a PR (Figure 1c). According to RECIST V.1.1, the reduction rate in tumor diameter is closely related to the pathological assessment of tumor necrosis rate (Figure 1d, r=0.54, p=0.005). In Figure 2a and 2b, we present the clinical data of one non-responder patient and one patient who achieved a pathological complete response (pCR). Patient a achieved stable disease (SD) according to mRECIST criteria, with a baseline AFP level of 500 ng/mL. Following neoadjuvant therapy, the AFP level decreased by 63.4%, and pathological assessment revealed 99.5% tumor viability and 0.5% tumor stroma. Patient b showed a reduction in the maximum tumor diameter from 6.2 cm to 4.2 cm, with an mRECIST score of CR. After neoadjuvant therapy, the AFP level decreased by 98.9%, and no viable tumor cells were detected in the surgically resected specimen, achieving pathological complete response.

Safety Profile

25 patients (96.2%) experienced at least one TRAE. The most common grade 1–2 TRAEs included drug-induced liver injury (76.9%), HAIC-related pain (34.6%), thrombocytopenia (34.6%), leukopenia (23.1%), and vomiting (23.1%). Only one patient experienced grade 3–4 TRAEs (Table 3), which required hospitalization due to persistent fever. The patient’s persistent fever may be associated with an intra-abdominal infection following interventional therapy for HCC. After one week of treatment with cefoperazone-sulbactam, the patient’s body temperature and inflammatory markers returned to normal. No adverse events necessitated corticosteroid treatment. No grade 5 TRAEs were observed. No patients progressed from Child-Pugh class A to class B after neoadjuvant HAIC, and no patients experienced delays in surgery due to adverse reactions. All surgical patients were discharged without significant complications.

Exploratory Analysis

Transcriptomic Analysis of the Tumor Samples

Transcriptomic analysis of baseline biopsy tissues and post-treatment surgical samples revealed that in the non-responder group (non-MPR) compared to the responder group with effective treatment (MPR), a total of 2890 differentially expressed genes were identified, with 2185 upregulated and 705 downregulated (Figure 3a). Gene Ontology (GO) analysis showed a significant enrichment of upregulated genes in lipid metabolism processes, while downregulated genes were notably more abundant in immune responses (Figure 3b). Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis of post-treatment responsive tumors compared to non-responsive tumors highlighted differential gene functional enrichments in bile secretion, fatty acid degradation, and the PPAR signaling pathway (Figure 3c). Post-treatment assessment indicated that responsive tumors exhibited downregulation of 3433 genes and upregulation of 2549 genes (Figure 3d). In biological processes (BPs), compared to the baseline state, post-treatment responsive tumors displayed a significant increase in upregulated genes involved in immune responses, while genes involved in lipid metabolism were more prominently downregulated (Figure 3e). Differential gene functional enrichment in responsive tumors and their paired pre-treatment biopsy tissues also primarily involved in bile secretion, fatty acid degradation, and the PPAR signaling pathway (Figure 3f).

Association Between Bile Acids and Therapeutic Response

Targeted bile acid metabolomics was employed to analyze the bile acid metabolic profiles in the serum and tumor tissues of responders and non-responders. Compared to the responsive tumors, glycocholic acid (GCA) was determined to be the prominently increased bile acid in non-responsive tumor tissues (Figure 4a and b). Further pathway enrichment analysis revealed that the differential bile acid metabolism in tumor tissues between responders and non-responders was significantly enriched in the bile secretion, primary bile acid biosynthesis, and cholesterol metabolism pathways (Figure 4c). While chenodeoxycholic acid (CDCA) was identified as the most significantly differential bile acid in the serum of both responders and non-responders (Figure 4d and e). Functional enrichment of differential bile acids in the serum was associated with bile secretion and primary bile acid biosynthesis (Figure 4f).

Changes in Intestinal Flora After Neoadjuvant Therapy

16S rRNA gene sequencing analysis showed that the treatment did not alter the patients’ microbial diversity (Figure 5a and b). At the genus level, Bacteroidota remained dominant both before and after treatment (Figure 5c). The heatmap also showed that at baseline the Prevotella abundance in the non-responder group was relatively lower than in the responder group, while Phascolarctobacterium abundance was relatively higher (Figure 5d). Likewise, there was no difference in gut microbiota α-diversity between the two groups after treatment, and β-diversity also exhibited homogeneity (Figure 5e and f). After neoadjuvant therapy, Bacteroides and Barnesiella were the species with markedly increased abundance in non-responders (Figure 5g and h).

Discussion

In HCC, small-sample neoadjuvant clinical trials10–12 have also shown significant benefits, demonstrating that the preoperative application of ICIs or combination therapies can enhance anti-tumor immune responses and eliminate micro-metastatic tumors while the primary tumor remains in situ. The primary risk of performing neoadjuvant therapy before surgery is that the patient may be insensitive to the treatment, leading to tumor progression, increased surgical difficulty, and even loss of the opportunity for curative resection. The ORR of targeted therapy combined with immunotherapy and FOLFOX-HAIC for unresectable HCC is 44%-60.4%.13,14 For neoadjuvant therapy, the tumor is already resectable, and the focus of treatment is not primarily on shrinking the tumor, therefore, we designed the neoadjuvant treatment plan of “hepatic arterial infusion chemotherapy combined with tislelizumab,” without opting for a more aggressive combination therapy approach.

Currently, HCC uses 2–3 cycles of neoadjuvant therapy with two combination treatment regimens.15–17 The number of neoadjuvant treatments in our study was also set at two, administered at intervals of 21 days. After two cycles of combined FOLFOX-HAIC and tislelizumab therapy, radiological assessments based on mRECIST criteria showed an ORR of 57.7% and a DCR of 96.2%, indicating that this neoadjuvant treatment intensity is sufficient. In addition to tumor volume reduction, pathological response can more directly reflect the degree of tumor tissue’s response to neoadjuvant therapy. Currently, there is inconsistency in the MPR assessment criteria following neoadjuvant therapy for HCC. A study involving 111 HCC patients who underwent hepatectomy after receiving neoadjuvant ICI therapy showed that a tumor regression rate of 90% is the optimal tumor regression cutoff value for predicting improved relapse-free survival.18 Drawing on the MPR standards for non-small cell lung cancer19 and renal cell carcinoma,20 this study defines the MPR as a residual viable tumor cell ratio of ≤10% in the tumor bed. The MPR after treatment with FOLFOX-HAIC combined with tislelizumab reached 45.5%, higher than the 33.3% from the nivolumab plus ipilimumab dual immunotherapy combination,21 surpassing the 17.6% from the camrelizumab combined with apatinib targeted immunotherapy approach,15 and approaching the 48.1% of transarterial therapy combined with bevacizumab plus ICI.22 This fully demonstrates the advantage of HAIC combined with ICIs as a neoadjuvant regimen, where the use of a dual-agent approach ensures treatment intensity while reducing the treatment burden and toxic side effects, offering a more effective and promising method. Not all patients will benefit from this single neoadjuvant regimen, so accurately identifying the sensitive population rather than increasing the number of neoadjuvant treatment cycles is more valuable.

In this study, comprehensive transcriptomic analyses were conducted using samples from pre-treatment biopsies and subsequent surgical resections. Responsing tumors post-treatment, when compared to non-responsing tumors, exhibit differential genes that intersect with those genes that are differentially expressed in responsing tumors before and after treatment. These intersections reveal that gene functional enrichments are all involved in bile secretion, fatty acid degradation, and PPAR signaling pathways. Similar to our findings, non-responsive HCCs treated with neoadjuvant nivolumab show upregulation of bile acid metabolism and PPARG expression.23 Previous studies have shown that high levels of bile acids in the tumor microenvironment of cholangiocarcinoma24 and hepatocellular carcinoma25 enhance immunosuppression and lead to resistance to immunotherapy. Therefore, we measured the levels of bile acids in plasma and tumor tissues of responders and non-responders by mass spectrometry and found that GCA levels were significantly elevated within non-responding tumor tissues. There was a report that conjugated bile acids such as GCA are significantly elevated in HCC tissues and are associated with functional suppression of tumor-specific CD8+ T cells.26 However, in serum-targeted bile acid metabolomics analysis we found that CDCA, rather than GCA, was elevated in the serum of non-responders. Gaoping et al27 found that CDCA is degraded to iso-lithocholic acid (iso-LCA), and the accumulated iso-LCA impairs the antitumor activity of hepatic NK cells. In this study, we found the abundance of Bacteroides and Barnesiella increased in non-responding patients after treatment. In colorectal cancer,28 enrichment of Bacteroides producing bile salt hydrolase (BSH) enhances bile acid deconjugation, increasing CDCA. This suggests that levels of conjugated bile acids such as GCA rise in non‑responding tumors, and through microbiota‑mediated bile acid deconjugation, primary bile acids like CDCA undergo enterohepatic circulation leading to increased serum CDCA. We examined changes in serum cytokines before and after neoadjuvant therapy and found that IL-6 was higher after treatment in responders than in non-responders. Research has found that CDCA can inhibit immune cells from releasing proinflammatory factors such as IL-6 by activating the farnesoid X receptor (FXR);29 this is strong evidence, and in this study CDCA levels increased and IL-6 levels decreased in the serum of non-responders after neoadjuvant therapy.

In terms of safety, this regimen provides good short-term control of HCC: among 26 patients, only one lost the opportunity for surgery, and the surgical resection rate was higher than previously reported.30,31 We also paid close attention to whether neoadjuvant therapy would affect subsequent surgical procedures. Although almost all patients (96.2%) experienced TRAEs, the observed adverse events were mainly grade 1–2 and were primarily related to HAIC treatment, including elevations in ALT and AST after HAIC (76.9%), pain (34.6%), thrombocytopenia (34.6%), fever (23.1%) and leukopenia (23.1%), consistent with the adverse reactions caused by HAIC when used as neoadjuvant therapy for HCC.32 Grade 3–4 TRAEs accounted for only 3.9%, lower than results reported in previous neoadjuvant clinical trials.33 This demonstrates controllable perioperative safety under strict monitoring and standardized management.

As a single-center study with a limited sample size (n=26), this somewhat restricts the statistical power and the generalizability of our conclusions. Firstly, the sample size is relatively small, and the participants were enrolled from a single center, which may overestimate the efficacy of the regimen. Secondly, as a single-arm phase II clinical trial, this study lacks a control group, making it difficult to accurately evaluate efficacy and adverse reactions. Additionally, the study included HCC beyond the Milan criteria, covering a wide range and exhibiting significant differences in baseline tumor burden levels. Finally, whether pathological response can translate into long-term survival benefits requires longer-term follow-up.

In summary, based on the data from this study, the “local + systemic immunotherapy” combined regimen of FOLFOX-HAIC with tirelizumab as neoadjuvant therapy for HCC demonstrated good efficacy and acceptable adverse reactions. Increased serum CDCA, fecal microbiota Bacteroides elevated GCA levels in tumor tissue, and decreased serum IL-6 appeared to be associated with poorer treatment response, suggesting these markers hold the potential to serve as a composite biomarker panel, but require validation in large-scale prospective cohorts. Currently, the understanding of the “gut microbiota-bile acid-tumor immunity” axis is deepening,34–36 and the multi-omics results of this study further demonstrate the value of this pathway, shedding new light on elucidating the mechanism of action of “FOLFOX-HAIC combined with tislelizumab” in treating HCC.

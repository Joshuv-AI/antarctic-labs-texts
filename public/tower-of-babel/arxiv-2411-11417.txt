The Kernel of the Gysin Homomorphism for Positive characteristic
by Claudia Schoemann, Skylar Werner

Abstract
Let k k be an uncountable algebraically closed field of positive characteristic and let S 0 S_{0} be a connected smooth projective surface over k k . We extend the theorem on the Gysin kernel from [ 28 , Theorem 5.1] to also be true over k k , where it was proved over ℂ \mathbb{C} . This is done by showing that almost all results still hold true over k k via the same argument or by using étale base arguments and then use a lift with the Comparison theorems [ 22 , Theorem 21.1 & 20.5] as needed.

2
A Brief History

This section goes over a very brief history of this problem and thus can be skipped without any consequence to the rest of the paper.

It has been classically known that in the theory of algebraic curves there is a map
Sym^{n}(C)\to J(C)
from the
n
-th Symmetric product
of the curve
C
into the
Jacobian Variety
where the fibers are projective spaces, i.e. representing the linear systems of degree
n

). As usual in mathematics, when adding on an extra dimension, i.e. algebraic surfaces, the whole system becomes a lot more difficult. Naturally we have the analogous map
Sym^{n}(S)\to Alb(S)

Alb(S)
is the
Albanese variety
. The fibers are irreducible and regular when
n
is large enough (see
[
18
]
). However, in the 1960’s, a long open problem was if the fibers are rational. One of the first leading researchers on the problem was Severi
[
34
]
. After Severi, Arthur Mattuck proved the theorem

Theorem
.

Let
S
be a complete nonsingular surface in characteristic zero, and let
q=\dim(Alb(S))
. If for some
n>q
the general fiber of the morphism
Sym^{n}(S)\to Alb(S)
is a rational variety, then
V
is a ruled surface.

Proof.

see
[
18
]

∎

Very similarly and independently, David Mumford in the paper
[
24
]
has the theorem

Theorem
.

Let
S
be a non-singular complex algebraic surface. For all
f:S\to Sym^{n}(S)
such that all the
0
-cycles

, are rationally equivalent, it follows that
\eta_{f}=0
where
\eta_{f}
is the
2
-form in
\Gamma(S,\Omega_{S}^{2})
.

As we can see in Mumford’s theorem, differential
2
-forms were used. Similarly in the proof of Mattuck’s theorem, differential
2
-forms were also used. At a quick glance at this paper, we do not use differentials at all. From Mumford’s paper, he has lemma 3 which states that
Sym^{n,n}(S)
contains a countable set of closed subvarieties

such that if

, then

. From this, A. A. Roĭtman produced two papers generalizing this subject over any uncountable algebraically closed field of characteristic

(see

]
and

]

Let

be the Albanese variety of a nonsingular irreducible projective variety

induces an epimorphism on the points of finite order:

.

Let

Proof.

see
[
20
]

∎

From here, Spencer Bloch started investigating the relation between algebraic cycles and higher

]
). This has been a very successful route of research, but for what we focus on, we will not talk about

Let

Except for the few generalizations, most of these results are over

]
). Since the method of Hodge theory only works over the complex numbers, in the recent paper of K. Banerjee and V. Guletskiĭ

The Jacobian Variety

We will quickly define the Jacobian variety over a general field
k
and note theorems that we require. In particular, the Jacobian of a complete non-singular projective curve is the
Albanese variety

Alb(C)
(i.e. the universal abelian pointed variety) and is also the Chow group
CH_{0}(C)_{\deg=0}
of
0
-cycles of degree zero.

Note that in
[
28
]
, the authors worked with the
Intermediate Jacobian

[
28

\mathbb{C}
, which classically looks like a complex torus and has a Hodge structure. Since we are working over
k

Recall that the

Assume that

\sum_{i}n_{i}[k(P_{i}):k]

[
10

). It is known that for every invertible sheaf
\mathcal{L}\in Pic(C)
is of the form
\mathcal{L}(D)
for some divisor

is uniquely determined up to linear equivalence. Thus giving us
\deg(\mathcal{L})=\deg(D)
which implies that
\deg(\mathcal{L}^{n})=\deg(nD)=n\deg(D)

). We write

for the group of isomorphism classes of invertible sheaves of degree zero on

Let

be a connected scheme over

and let

be an invertible sheaf on

. From the Seesaw Theorem
[

, we have that for a sheaf

on

where

is the second projection and

is the fiber over

and in particular it has degree

. Let

That is,

are functors from the category of schemes over

There exists an abelian variety

7.2
Irredundant countable union.

A countable union
V=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}V_{n}
of algebraic varieties will be called irredundant if
V_{n}
is irreducible for each
n
and
V_{m}\not\subset V_{n}
for
m\neq n
. If
V
is a irredundant decomposition, then the sets
V_{n}
are called c-components of
V
.

7.3
.

Let
V
be a countable union of algebraic varieties over an uncountable algebraically closed ground field
k
of arbitrary characteristic. Then
V
admits an irredundant decomposition, and such an irredundant decomposition is unique.

Proof.

Let
V=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}V_{n}^{\prime}
be a countable union of algebraic varieties over
k
. For each
n
let
V_{n}^{\prime}=V_{n,1}^{\prime}\cup...\cup V_{n,r}^{\prime}
be the irreducible components of
V_{n}^{\prime}
. Ignoring all components
V_{m,i}^{\prime}
with
V_{m,i}^{\prime}\subset V_{n,j}^{\prime}
for some
n
and
j
we end up with an irredundant decomposition. Having two irredundant decompositions
V=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
V=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}W_{n}
, suppose there exists
V_{m}
such that
V_{m}
is not contained in
W_{n}
for any
n
. Then
V_{m}
is the union of the closed subsets
V_{m}\cap W_{n}
, each of which is not
V_{m}
. This contradicts to
7.1
. Therefore, any
V_{m}
is contained in some
W_{n}
. By symmetry, any
W_{n}
is in
V_{l}
for some
l
. Then
V_{m}\subset V_{l}
. By irredundancy,
l=m
and
V_{m}=W_{n}
.
∎

7.4
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety over an uncountable field
k
of arbitrary characteristic, and let
K
be a subgroup which can be represented as a countable union of Zariski closed subsets in
A
. Then the irredundant decomposition of
K
contains a unique irreducible component passing through
0
, and this component is an abelian subvariety in
A
.

Proof.

Let
K=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}K_{n}
be the irredundant decomposition of
K
, which exists by
7.3
. Since
0\in K
, there exists at least one component in the irredundant decomposition, which contains
0
. Suppose there are
s
components
K_{1},...,K_{s}
containing
0
and
s>1
. The summation in
K
gives the regular morphism from the product
K_{1}\times...\times K_{s}
into
A
. By
7.1
, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
K_{1}+...+K_{s}\subset K_{n}
and so
K_{i}\subset K_{n}
for each
1\leq i\leq n
. By irredundancy,
s=1
, which contradicts to the assumption
s>1
.

After renumbering of the components, we may assume that
0\in K_{0}
. If
K_{0}=\{0\}
, then
K_{0}
is trivially an abelian variety. Suppose
K_{0}\neq\{0\}
and take a non-trivial element
x
in
K_{0}
. Since
-x+K_{0}
is irreducible (since translation is an isomorphism, see
[
21
, page 8]
), it is contained in some
K_{n}
by
7.1
. As
0\in-x+K_{0}
it follows that
0\in K_{n}
and so
n=0
. It follows that
-x\in K_{0}
. Similarly, since
K_{0}+K_{0}
is irreducible and contains
K_{0}
, we see that
K_{0}+K_{0}=K_{0}
. Being a Zariski closed abelian subgroup in
A
, the set
K_{0}
is an abelian subvariety in
A
.
∎

Lefschetz Pencils and The Monodromy Argument

This section is to quickly give us an overview of basic definitions and properties of Lefschetz pencils and the monodromy argument. This is purely for the act to prove part (b) of a theorem on the Gysin kernel.

9.1
Lefschetz Pencils

9.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective algebraic variety of positive dimension over a field
k
. If
k
is infinite, there is an everywhere defined birational morphism

\pi:\tilde{X}\to X

from a smooth projective variety
\tilde{X}
and a mapping

that is singular at no more than finitely many points. Each fiber

contains at most one singular point, and any such point occurring is an ordinary double point.

Proof.

see
[
9
, Chapter 3 §1 Proposition 1.10]

∎

9.2

For any line

in

satisfying the conditions in the proposition above, the family of curves
(X\cap H)_{H\in D}
is called a Lefschetz pencil of
X
. In other words, the fibers have singularities that are at most ordinary double points.

9.3
Zariski’s theorem.

Let
D\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a Lefschetz pencil and let
A
be the set of points where

is singular, i.e. for every

there lies exactly one singular point in the fiber

and it is an ordinary double point.

Proof.

see
[
9
, A I.16]

∎

In particular, in our setting, we will always be able to force ourselves to be working with a Lefschetz pencil.

For more details, see

9.4
Covering space.

A scheme

is a covering space if

is étale and a finite mapping.

Let

be the group of automorphisms of

over

9.5
Galois.

We say that a covering

Since the covering spaces of

form a full subcategory of the category

A pointed covering space (over the base point

It is a known fact that if we have two pointed covering spaces

The fundamental group of

(Y,\alpha)\end{subarray}}G(Y/X).

The fundamental character is the map

is also a countable union of Zariski closed subsets in the abelian variety
J_{t_{0}}
.

Now since
alb_{C_{t_{0}}}(G_{t_{0}})
is a countable union of algebraic varieties over an uncountable field,
alb_{C_{t_{0}}}
admits a unique irredundant decomposition inside the abelian variety
J_{t_{0}}
(see
7.3
). Using the isomorphism
alb_{C_{t_{0}}}
, we can identify
alb_{C_{t_{0}}}(G_{t_{0}})
with
G_{t_{0}}
and write
G_{t_{0}}=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(G_{t_{0}})_{n}
for the irredundant decomposition of
G_{t_{0}}
in
J_{t_{0}}\cong CH_{0}(C_{t_{0}})_{\deg=0}
.

On the other hand, note that by definition
G_{t_{0}}
is a subgroup in
CH_{0}(C_{t_{0}})_{\deg=0}
, hence its image
alb_{C_{t_{0}}}(G_{t_{0}})
in
J_{t_{0}}
via
alb_{C_{t_{0}}}
is also a subgroup in
J_{t_{0}}
.

As
J_{t_{0}}
is an abelian variety and
G_{t_{0}}\subset J_{t_{0}}
a subgroup which can be represented as a countable union of Zariski closed subsets in
J_{t_{0}}
, the irredundant decomposition of
G_{t_{0}}
contains a unique irreducible component passing through
0
which is an abelian subvariety of
J_{t_{0}}
(see
7.4
). After renumbering the components, we may assume that this component is
(G_{t_{0}})_{0}
.

It is clear that for any
x\in G_{t_{0}}
, the set
x+(G_{t_{0}})_{0}
is an irreducible Zariski closed subset (just translation of a Zariski closed subset) in
G_{t_{0}}
, and that we can write
G_{t_{0}}=\bigcup_{x\in G_{t_{0}}}(x+(G_{t_{0}})_{0})
.

Ignoring each set
x+(G_{t_{0}})_{0}
inside
y+(G_{t_{0}})_{0}
, for
x,y\in G_{t_{0}}
, we get a subset
\Xi\subset G_{t_{0}}
such that
G_{t_{0}}=\bigcup_{x\in\Xi}(x+(G_{t_{0}})_{0})
which is an irredundant decomposition of
G_{t_{0}}
.

Now we claim that
\Xi
is countable. Indeed, for any
x,y\in\Xi,x+(G_{t_{0}})_{0}
and
y+(G_{t_{0}})_{0}
are irreducible and not contained one in another. Now observe that since
x+(G_{t_{0}})_{0}
is irreducible and
G_{t_{0}}
is a subgroup, then
x+(G_{t_{0}})_{0}\subset(G_{t_{0}})_{n}
for some
n
, because otherwise if
x+(G_{t_{0}})_{0}
is not contained in
(G_{t_{0}})_{n}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then
x+(G_{t_{0}})_{0}
would be the union of the closed subsets of the form
(x+(G_{t_{0}})_{0})\cap(G_{t_{0}})_{n}
each of which is not
x+(G_{t_{0}})_{0}
, contradicting
7.1
. It follows that
(G_{t_{0}})_{0}\subset-x+(G_{t_{0}})_{n}
. Similarly, we can prove that
-x+(G_{t_{0}})_{n}
is contained in
(G_{t_{0}})_{l}
for some
l\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
(G_{t_{0}})_{0}=(G_{t_{0}})_{l}
, that is,
(G_{t_{0}})_{0}\subset-x+(G_{t_{0}})_{n}\subset(G_{t_{0}})_{0}
, so
-x+(G_{t_{0}})_{n}=(G_{t_{0}})_{0}
, i.e.
x+(G_{t_{0}})_{0}=(G_{t_{0}})_{n}
for each
x\in\Xi
. It means that
\Xi
is countable.

Taking
A_{t_{0}}=(G_{t_{0}})_{0}
, until now, we have proved that there is an Abelian variety
A_{t_{0}}\subset J_{t_{0}}
such that
G_{t_{0}}=\bigcup_{x\in\Xi}(x+A_{t_{0}})
, where
\Xi\subset G_{t_{0}}
is a countable subset. Equivalently, we can write as follows: there is an Abelian variety
A_{t_{0}}\subset J_{t_{0}}
such that
G_{t_{0}}=\bigcup_{\text{countable}}\text{ translates of }A_{t_{0}}
.

To complete the proof, we next show that
A_{t_{0}}
is contained in
B_{t_{0}}
.

In Paucar/Schoemann’s paper, they showed via Hodge theory that
H^{1}(A_{t,\mathbb{C}},\mathbb{Z})\subset H^{1}(B_{t,\mathbb{C}},\mathbb{Z})
. By the comparison theorem, we can translate this into étale cohomology and then Lefschetz’s principle says that we get
H_{et}^{1}(A_{t_{\overline{\eta}}},\mathbb{Z}_{p})\subset H_{et}^{1}(B_{t_{\overline{\eta}}},\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. Since we have a lift, using the lift comparison theorem, we get
H_{et}^{1}(A_{t_{0}},\mathbb{Z}_{p})\subset H_{et}^{1}(B_{t_{0}},\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. Thus
A_{t_{0}}\subset B_{t_{0}}
.
∎

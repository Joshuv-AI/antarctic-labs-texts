Bimodular Gravity: Unimodularising Bimetric Scalar–Tensor Gravity
James Hallam, João Magueijo

Abstract
It is the object of the present paper to unimodularise a disformal bimetric scalar–tensor theory, thereby defining what we call bimodular gravity. We impose one unimodular constraint per metric via multipliers λ 1 , 2 \lambda_{1,2} and show that two natural implementations—a dual fixed-determinant (BUG) and a dual diffeomorphism-invariant (BHT/BDUG) formulation—are classically inequivalent. In BUG the relative volume element ν = 1 − 2 ​ B ​ X \nu=\sqrt{1-2BX} is fixed, enforcing a kinematic constraint on the biscalar and we derive the “bimodular cosmological constant” Λ = λ 1 + ν ​ λ 2 \Lambda=\lambda_{1}+\nu\lambda_{2} . In BHT/BDUG, λ 1 , 2 \lambda_{1,2} are individually constant but ν \nu (hence B ​ X BX ) remains dynamical. Recasting the theory in an Einstein-frame form, we derive the biscalar sound speed and identify a subluminal domain 1 + B ⁡ ( V + λ 2 ) > 0 1+B(V+\lambda_{2})>0 . At the background level, BUG admits constant-roll solutions governed by first-order flow, whereas BHT supports solutions with time-dependent roll. These structural differences yield distinct, in-principle testable predictions for the expansion history, the dark-energy equation of state, and the propagation of biscalar perturbations. Finally, we present a diffeomorphism-invariant completion that correlates the two HT volume forms, reproducing the Λ \Lambda of BUG on shell whilst maintaining full covariance.

II.1
Unimodular Gravity

Unimodular gravity has long been known to yield field equations classically equivalent to
\mathrm{GR}\!+\!\Lambda
, with the cosmological constant emerging as an integration constant rather than a coupling in the action. The differences amongst the standard formulations of unimodular gravity lie only in how the constancy of the Lagrange multiplier is enforced, and hence in their off-shell symmetry content.

In the original fixed-determinant formulation of unimodular gravity (UG), one constrains
\sqrt{-g}
to a constant scalar density
\varepsilon_{0}
by means of a Lagrange multiplier,

S_{\text{UG}}=\int d^{4}x\left[\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{-g}R-\lambda\left(\sqrt{-g}-\varepsilon_{0}\right)\right].

(1)

Variation with respect to
\lambda
imposes the constraint, whilst variation with respect to
g_{\mu\nu}
yields the traceless Einstein equations. The Bianchi identity implies
\nabla_{\mu}\lambda=0
, so that
\lambda
is constant on shell and can be identified with
\Lambda

9
;
10
;
29
;
24
.

The covariant formulation of Henneaux and Teitelboim (HT)
17
restores full diffeomorphism invariance by introducing an auxiliary vector density
\tau^{\mu}
,

S_{\text{HT}}=\int d^{4}x\left[\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{-g}R-\lambda\left(\sqrt{-g}-\partial_{\mu}\tau^{\mu}\right)\right].

(2)

Here, variation with respect to
\lambda
enforces
\sqrt{-g}=\partial_{\mu}\tau^{\mu}
, and variation with respect to
\tau^{\mu}
gives
\partial_{\mu}\lambda=0
, so the constancy of
\lambda
is enforced directly in the action
18
.

A closely related fully diffeomorphism-invariant formalism (DUG) trades the metric constraint for a divergence condition on an auxiliary vector
V^{\mu}
,

S_{\text{DUG}}=\int d^{4}x\left[\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{-g}R-\sqrt{-g}\left(\lambda+V^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\lambda\right)\right],

(3)

which is equivalent to HT up to a boundary term. Variation of
V^{\mu}
directly yields
\partial_{\mu}\lambda=0
, and variation with respect to
\lambda
imposes
\nabla_{\mu}V^{\mu}=1

11
.

In all three cases, the classical field equations are on-shell equivalent to those of GR with a cosmological constant. Their differences are purely off shell, specifically in how the unimodular condition is implemented and how the constancy of
\lambda
is imposed—derived via the Bianchi identity (UG) or assumed in the action (HT/DUG). These distinctions are classically inconsequential in the single-metric setting but will become become decisive for the failure of the classical equivalence once unimodularity is applied in a bimetric framework. To that end, the next section introduces the disformal bimetric theory that will subsequently be unimodularised.

II.2
Bimetric Gravity

In the bimetric gravity theory considered here, gravity is described by an Einstein metric
g_{\mu\nu}
and a matter metric
\hat{g}_{\mu\nu}
, disformally related through a biscalar field
\phi
via

\hat{g}_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}+B(\phi)\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi,

(4)

with the
disformal coupling

B(\phi)>0
of dimension
[\text{length}]^{2}
. Invertibility of the disformal map and preservation of a Lorentzian signature require
1-2BX>0
, in which case, from the Sherman–Morrison formula and matrix determinant lemma, the inverse and determinant follow as

\displaystyle\hat{g}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=g^{\mu\nu}-\frac{B}{1-2BX}\partial^{\mu}\phi\partial^{\nu}\phi,

(5)

\displaystyle\sqrt{-\hat{g}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{-g}\sqrt{1-2BX},

(6)

so that the
relative volume element
is

\nu\coloneqq\frac{\sqrt{-\hat{g}}}{\sqrt{-g}}=\sqrt{1-2BX}.

(7)

These are precisely the objects that will be subjected to unimodular constraints in Sec.
III
. It may already be obvious how the different unimodular formalisms will affect
\nu
, and subsequently the biscalar dynamics.

For the biscalar to be dynamical, a kinetic term must be chosen. One may place
\phi
in the Einstein frame, where it is canonical relative to
g_{\mu\nu}
and luminal with respect to that frame, or in the matter frame, where conservation laws take their standard form
22
. For present purposes, it is more insightful to adopt the latter so that the gravitational sector alone sees two metrics, with the bimetric action

S=\int d^{4}x\left[\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{-g}R+\sqrt{-\hat{g}}\left(\hat{X}-V(\phi)\right)\right],

(8)

where
\hat{X}=X/\nu^{2}
. Here, the matter stress tensor is conserved in the matter frame,
\hat{\nabla}_{\mu}\hat{T}^{\mu\nu}=0
, and small fluctuations of
\phi
propagate luminally relative to
\hat{g}_{\mu\nu}
.

It is the action in (
8
) that will serve as the basis for the unimodularisation procedure. In Sec.
III
, we impose the different unimodular constraints simultaneously on both the Einstein and matter metrics, thereby introducing the general theoretical framework of bimodular gravity.

III
Bimodular Gravity Theory

To unimodularise a disformal bimetric setup in full generality, we impose one unimodular-type condition per metric using two independent Lagrange multipliers,
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
, and two constraint functionals
\mathcal{C}_{1}[g]
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}[\hat{g}]
. The general bimodular gravity (BMG) action is then

\displaystyle S_{\text{BMG}}=

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\left[\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{-g}R[g]+\sqrt{-\hat{g}}\Big(\hat{X}-V(\phi)\Big)\right]

\displaystyle-\int d^{4}x\left[\lambda_{1}\mathcal{C}_{1}[g]+\lambda_{2}\mathcal{C}_{2}[\hat{g}]\right].

(9)

The choice of constraint functionals specifies the appropriate unimodular formalism and thus the bimodular formalism, which we now present for the three unimodular formalisms discussed above.

III.1
BUG Theory

In the fixed-determinant UG formulation, the constraint functionals take the form

\mathcal{C}_{i}[h]=\sqrt{-h}-\varepsilon_{i},

(10)

with
\varepsilon_{i}
a constant density for an arbitrary metric
h
. The constraint sector in the bimodular action (
9
) under the dual UG constraints (BUG) then reads

S_{BUG}\supset\int d^{4}x\left[\lambda_{1}\left(\sqrt{-g}-\varepsilon_{1}\right)+\lambda_{2}\left(\sqrt{-\hat{g}}-\varepsilon_{2}\right)\right].

(11)

Variation with respect to
\lambda_{1,2}
enforces

\sqrt{-g}=\varepsilon_{1},\qquad\sqrt{-\hat{g}}=\varepsilon_{2},

(12)

whence the relative volume element reduces to

\nu=\frac{\varepsilon_{2}}{\varepsilon_{1}}=\sqrt{1-2BX}.

(13)

Since
\varepsilon_{i}
are constants, the relative volume element
\nu
is found to be constant throughout the spacetime continuum. Because
\nu
is related to the biscalar kinetic density via (
7
), its constancy consequently imposes

\nabla_{\mu}\nu=-\frac{1}{\nu}\nabla_{\mu}(BX)=0\quad\implies\quad BX=\text{const}.

(14)

This constitutes a physical
kinematic constraint
upon the biscalar, which implies that the gradient of
X
in parallel with the gradient of
\phi
,

\nabla_{\mu}X=-\frac{B_{\phi}X}{B}\nabla_{\mu}\phi,

(15)

where subscript donates partial derivative with respect to
\phi
. For the remainder of this work,
B
shall be treated as a constant, so that the BUG kinematic constraint yields
\nabla_{\mu}X=0
.

It should be emphasised that this kinematic constraint arises specifically from imposing two independent fixed-determinant constraints. If only one metric were constrained (i.e., unimodularising either
g_{\mu\nu}
or
\hat{g}_{\mu\nu}
alone), the disformal coupling (
7
) would allow the determinant of the unconstrained metric to absorb variations in
X
, thus preserving complete dynamical freedom even in the fixed-volume formalism. This underlies the initial decision of placing the biscalar kinetic term in the matter frame.

But of course, no condition has yet been imposed upon
\lambda_{1,2}
.
In single-metric UG, constancy of the multiplier is derived via the Bianchi identities, which then allows it to be identified with the cosmological constant. Therefore, in order to obtain the bimodular cosmological constant, the analogous relation must be derived through explicit calculation of the BUG Bianchi identity. This is addressed at the end of this section, where it is found that instead of individual constancy, it is a linear sum of the two
\lambda_{1,2}
that is conserved. By contrast, the fully diffeomorphism-invariant theories assume constancy of their corresponding multipliers individually, as we now demonstrate.

III.2
BHT Theory

In the case of the HT formulation, the diffeomorphism-invariant bimodular constraints read

\mathcal{C}_{i}[h]=\sqrt{-h}-\partial_{\mu}\tau_{i}^{\mu},

(16)

where
\tau_{i}^{\mu}
are auxiliary vector densities. The constraint sector of the bimodular action (
9
) under the dual HT constraints (BHT) then becomes

\displaystyle S_{BHT}\supset-\int d^{4}x\Bigg[

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\left(\sqrt{-g}-\partial_{\mu}\tau_{1}^{\mu}\right)

\displaystyle+\lambda_{2}\left(\sqrt{-\hat{g}}-\partial_{\mu}\tau_{2}^{\mu}\right)\Bigg].

(17)

Variation with respect to the auxiliary densities enforces the spacetime constancy of the multipliers,

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\lambda_{1}=0,\qquad\partial_{\mu}\lambda_{2}=0,

(18)

so that
\lambda_{1,2}
are individually integration constants. Variation with respect to
\lambda_{1,2}
imposes the corresponding bimodular constraints

\sqrt{-g}=\partial_{\mu}\tau_{1}^{\mu},\qquad\sqrt{-\hat{g}}=\partial_{\mu}\tau_{2}^{\mu}.

(19)

Consequently, the relative volume element becomes

\nu=\frac{\partial_{\mu}\tau_{2}^{\mu}}{\partial_{\mu}\tau_{1}^{\mu}}=\sqrt{1-2BX},

(20)

which, unlike BUG, remains fully dynamical because
\partial_{\mu}\tau_{i}^{\mu}
need not be constant. Consequently,
X
is untouched, and the biscalar may keep its full dynamical freedom.

Thus, the promotion of bimodular gravity to a diffeomorphism-invariant theory via the HT formalism introduces on-shell inequivalence to its volume-preserving counterpart, BUG. The kinematic freedom of the biscalar in BHT arises because the implementation assumes the multipliers to be constant at the level of the action. In analogy with unimodular gravity, these constants enter the field equations as vacuum/potential contributions in their respective frames, but their physical interpretation of course differs from that of the standard cosmological constant. They are not necessarily associated with a fixed equation of state
w=-1
. Accordingly, the BHT theory carries two unimodular integration constants—one per metric—and the background cosmological behaviour still depends on the fully dynamical biscalar. We will now show that a similar result is obtained when the DUG formulation is implemented.

III.3
BDUG Theory

Alternatively, still under the wish to preserve full diffeomorphism invariance in the bimodular framework, one may instead employ the DUG formalism, in which case the constraint functionals become (noting the slight abuse of notation)

\lambda_{i}\mathcal{C}_{i}[h]=\sqrt{-h}(\lambda_{i}+V_{i}\nabla_{\mu}\lambda_{i}),

(21)

where
V_{i}^{\mu}
are auxiliary vector fields. The bimodular constraint sector under the DUG formalism (BDUG) is therefore

\displaystyle S_{BDUG}\supset-\int d^{4}x\Bigg[

\displaystyle\sqrt{-g}\left(\lambda_{1}+V_{1}\nabla_{\mu}\lambda_{1}\right)

\displaystyle+\sqrt{-\hat{g}}\left(\lambda_{2}+V_{2}\hat{\nabla}_{\mu}\lambda_{2}\right)\Bigg],

(22)

where
\nabla_{\mu}
and
\hat{\nabla}_{\mu}
denote the covariant derivatives compatible with
g_{\mu\nu}
and
\hat{g}_{\mu\nu}
, respectively. Variation with respect to the auxiliary vectors enforces spacetime constancy of the multipliers,

\nabla_{\mu}\lambda_{1}=0,\qquad\hat{\nabla}_{\mu}\lambda_{2}=0,

(23)

so that, just as in BHT,
\lambda_{1,2}
play the role of strict integration constants. Varying with respect to
\lambda_{1,2}
yields the divergence conditions

\nabla_{\mu}V^{\mu}_{1}=1,\qquad\hat{\nabla}_{\mu}V^{\mu}_{2}=1,

(24)

which entirely replace the fixed–determinant constraints by normalisation conditions on the auxiliary vectors. As a consequence, the relative volume element remains fully dynamical.

The shared imposition of constant
\lambda_{1,2}
and fully dynamical
BX
means that that the BHT and BDUG theories are equivalent on shell. Hence, for all intents and purposes of this work, we treat them as identical and focus on the BHT formulation for notational convenience, with its classical dynamics taken to represent those of BDUG.

It was noted above that within the BUG theory, the multipliers
\lambda_{1,2}
remain individually dynamical, and only a specific linear combination of them is constrained to be constant. This is by no means a trivial result. Deriving it requires computing the BUG Bianchi identities, which in turn necessitates deriving the corresponding Einstein and biscalar equations so that the divergence of the former can be evaluated using the latter (the full proof of which is presented in
16
). We therefore now present the Einstein equations of bimodular gravity.

III.4
The Modified Einstein Equations

Since the constraints
\mathcal{C}_{i}
themselves are metric-independent, the Einstein equations can be derived from the BMG action without specifying the explicit form of bimodularity. The resulting expressions will thus hold across all bimodular formalisms, differing only in how the actual fields are interpreted in accordance with above.

Varying the general bimodular action (

g^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle G_{\mu\nu}+(\lambda_{1}+\nu\lambda_{2})g_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=T^{(\phi)}_{\mu\nu},

(25)

with
1

1

\displaystyle T^{(\phi)}_{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{X}{\nu_{2}}-V\right)g_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1-BX}{\nu^{4}}+\frac{B(\lambda_{2}+V)}{\nu^{2}}\right)\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi.

(26)

As noted above, the Einstein equations share the same algebraic structure in both BUG and BHT formulations. The physical distinction between them, therefore, does not lie in their formal appearance, but in the interpretation of the variables and constraints specific to each framework. To reiterate, the on-shell gravitational dynamics of bimodular gravity depend on the particular unimodular formalism adopted, a difference that will become more explicit once some cosmological examples are presented (see Sec.
V

As a direct consequence of this inequivalence, the nature of the bimodular cosmological constant is subject to the implementation of bimodularity. In BHT, the interpretation is fairly straight forward. There now emerge two integration constants acting as vacuum/potential offsets. Yet in BUG the constant plays a subtler and arguably more interesting role, as we now show.

III.5
The Bimodular Cosmological Constant

Recall that within the BUG theory, the relative volume element is constant. Making use of the biscalar equation of motion derived in the next section rexpressed in the Einstein frame, we show in
16
that the biscalar stress-energy tensor is indeed conserved,

\nabla_{\mu}T_{(\phi)}^{\mu\nu}=0.

(27)

Taking the divergence of (
25

27
) and the Bianchi identities
\nabla_{\mu}G^{\mu\nu}
, implies

\nabla_{\mu}\left(\lambda_{1}+\sqrt{1-2BX}\lambda_{2}\right)=0,

(28)

presenting a
balance law
between the multipliers. This may naturally be interpreted as the generalised
bimodular cosmological constant
for BUG,

\Lambda\coloneqq\lambda_{1}+\sqrt{1-2BX}\lambda_{2}.

(29)

Individually,
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
need not be constant. Rather, their variations are kinematically restricted through the constant factor
\nu
so as to keep
\Lambda

\Lambda
is a scalar, its conservation is metric–independent:
\hat{\nabla}_{\mu}\Lambda=\partial_{\mu}\Lambda=\nabla_{\mu}\Lambda=0

\Lambda
is constant in both the Einstein and matter frames, even though its constituents
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
need not be. Thus the Einstein equations of BUG take the algebraic form of
\mathrm{GR}\!+\!\Lambda
with a biscalar-induced source.

By contrast, in BHT the auxiliary fields impose
\nabla_{\mu}\lambda_{1,2}=0
independently. Because
\nu
is not constant here, the BUG balance law (

\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
, rather than a single conserved linear combination. With this significant distinction established, we now analyse the biscalar sector, where the distinct interpretations of the cosmological constant in BUG and BHT lead to vastly different dynamics.

Biscalar Dynamics

Since the biscalar appears only in the biscalar kinetic term and in the matter–metric constraint term, those are the only sectors that contribute upon variation with respect to

\displaystyle\left[B(\hat{X}+V+\lambda_{2})+1\right]\hat{\Box}\phi=

In BUG, (

By contrast, in the BHT formulation, the multiplier

IV

For linear scalar perturbations about a homogenous background, a broad class of minimally coupled single-field theories with Lagrangian density

(33)

Absence of ghosts and of gradient instabilities requires

K_{X}>0,\qquad K_{X}+2XK_{XX}>0,

Equations (

IV.1

Rewriting the bimodular action (

\displaystyle S_{\text{BMG}}=

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{1}{2}R+\frac{X}{\nu}-\nu(V(\phi)+\lambda_{2})-\lambda_{1}\right]

\displaystyle+\int d^{4}x\left[\lambda_{1}\mathcal{C}_{1}+\lambda_{2}\mathcal{C}_{2}\right].

It should be stressed that this is a mere change of presentation, not of physics. Observables still remain defined in the matter sector, whilst the Einstein-frame rewrite simply organises the biscalar kinetics into a form from which one can read off the effective kinetic function,

\displaystyle K(\phi,X;\lambda_{2})=\frac{X}{\sqrt{1-2BX}}-\sqrt{1-2BX}(V(\phi)+\lambda_{2}).

A small-

expansion gives

c_{s}^{2}=1-\frac{4+2\alpha}{1+\alpha}x+\mathcal{O}\big(x^{2}\big),

(41)

which is consistently positive and subluminal for
\alpha>-1
(the same condition ensuring
K_{X}>0
). More generally, the denominator of (
40
) is positive for all
x\in(0,1/2)
if and only if
1+\alpha>0
. Thus, on
0<x<1/2
,

K_{X}>0,\qquad K_{X}+2XK_{XX}>0,\qquad 0<c_{s}^{2}<1,

(42)

are simultaneously satisfied whenever
\alpha>-1
. Hence, we propose that bimodular gravity is consistently subluminal
for
B>0
,
0<BX<1/2
, and
1+B(V+\lambda_{2})>0
, with the following immediate properties:

\displaystyle\text{(i) Canonical limit }BX\to 0:\quad

\displaystyle c_{s}^{2}\to 1.

\displaystyle\text{(ii) Relativistic DBI limit }BX\to\tfrac{1}{2}:\quad

\displaystyle c_{s}^{2}\to 0.

\displaystyle\text{(iii) Healthy domain }0<BX<\tfrac{1}{2}:\quad

\displaystyle 0<c_{s}^{2}<1.

In BUG, the kinematic constraint implies that
X
is a fixed constant and any time variation of
c_{s}^{2}
arises through only
\alpha=B(V+\lambda_{2})
, i.e. through the potential evolution and the allowed dynamics of
\lambda_{2}
. By contrast, in BHT the multipliers are spacetime constants, and so variation in
c_{s}^{2}
is driven by the standard, genuinely dynamical biscalar. In both cases, the healthy, subluminal regime is characterised by the statements above.

V
Bimodular Cosmology

Having established the general field equations and the distinct dynamical structure of each bimodular formalism, we now turn to their cosmological realisations. In this section, we impose the FLRW ansatz within the bimetric setting and examine the corresponding bimodular constraints, deriving the resulting background field equations and their reduction to a coupled system of ordinary differential equations. This will allow us to identify the conditions under which each theory admits de Sitter solutions and to clarify how the BUG and BHT formalisms differ in their cosmological dynamics.

V.1
Bimetric FLRW Ansatz

We specialise to a spatially flat, homogeneous, and isotropic cosmology for both geometries, displaying explicit FLRW forms for the Einstein metric and its disformally related counterpart
23
. In the Einstein frame,

ds^{2}=-N(t)^{2}dt^{2}+a(t)^{2}(dx^{2}+dy^{2}+dz^{2}),

(43)

and the biscalar is homogeneous,
\phi=\phi(t)
, so
\partial_{\mu}\phi=(\dot{\phi},0,0,0)
(where an overdot
\dot{}
denotes
d/dt
). Using the disformal relation (
4
), the components of the matter-frame metric are

\hat{g}_{00}=-N(t)^{2}+B(\phi)\dot{\phi}^{2},\qquad\hat{g}_{ij}=a(t)^{2}\delta_{ij}.

(44)

Hence, the the matter-frame line element takes the form

\displaystyle d\hat{s}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\hat{N}(t)^{2}dt^{2}+\hat{a}(t)^{2}(dx^{2}+dy^{2}+dz^{2}),

(45)

where

\hat{N}(t)^{2}\coloneqq N(t)^{2}-B(\phi)\dot{\phi}^{2},\quad\hat{a}(t)\equiv a(t).

(46)

Reality of the lapse requires
\hat{N}^{2}>0
, i.e.
N^{2}>B\dot{\phi}^{2}
, corresponding to the healthy domain
1-2BX>0
. The relative volume element is then

\displaystyle\nu=\frac{\sqrt{-\hat{g}}}{\sqrt{-g}}=\frac{\hat{N}}{N}=\sqrt{1-2BX}.

(47)

In BUG, the constraints (
12
) imply, for the FLRW ansatz,

\displaystyle Na^{3}=\varepsilon_{1},\qquad\hat{N}a^{3}=\varepsilon_{2}.

(48)

These relations fix the time gauge in both the Einstein and matter frames. Given
a(t)
, the lapses are determined by (
48
). Thus, the volume-preserving unimodular condition removes the freedom to impose
N=1
or
\hat{N}
by hand. But of course, this is a gauge fixing rather than a dynamical restriction on
a(t)
, so cosmological expansion is very much still allowed. The corresponding proper times,

d\tau\coloneqq Ndt=\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{a^{3}}dt,\qquad d\hat{\tau}\coloneqq\hat{N}dt=\frac{\varepsilon_{2}}{a^{3}}dt,

(49)

are related by
d\hat{\tau}/d\tau=\varepsilon_{2}/\varepsilon_{1}=\text{const}
. Combining (
46
) with (
48
) yields

\dot{\phi}^{2}=\frac{\varepsilon_{1}^{2}-\varepsilon_{2}^{2}}{Ba^{6}},\quad\implies\quad\frac{\dot{\phi}}{\hat{N}}=\pm\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_{1}^{2}-\varepsilon_{2}^{2}}{B\varepsilon_{2}^{2}}},

(50)

so that reality requires
\varepsilon_{1}^{2}>\varepsilon_{2}^{2}
. From (
49
), the quantity
\dot{\phi}/\hat{N}=d\phi/d\hat{\tau}
is the biscalar proper speed as measured in the matter frame. In BUG, this quantity is constant,

C_{0}\coloneqq\frac{d\phi}{d\hat{\tau}}=\text{const},

(51)

which in turn implies
X=\dot{\phi}^{2}/2N^{2}=\text{const}
, thereby realising the kinematic constraint in cosmology. Hence, within an FLRW background, BUG enforces a constant proper biscalar speed.

As for the BHT theory, the bimodular constraints in homogeneous minisuperspace (
19
) with
\tau_{i}^{\mu}=\tau_{i}^{\mu}(t)
, become

\displaystyle Na^{3}=\dot{\tau}_{1}(t),\qquad\hat{N}a^{3}=\dot{\tau}_{2}(t).

(52)

Using (
46
) then yields

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}^{2}=\frac{\dot{\tau}_{1}^{2}-\dot{\tau}_{2}^{2}}{Ba^{6}}.

(53)

Unlike BUG, however,
\dot{\tau}_{1}(t)
and
\dot{\tau}_{2}(t)
are dynamical, so neither
\dot{\phi}/\hat{N}
nor
X
is fixed a priori. Equivalently, the relative volume element
\nu=\hat{N}/N=\dot{\tau}_{2}/\dot{\tau}_{1}
is itself dynamical, preserving the standard time dependence along
\ddot{\phi}
of the biscalar sector—the very freedom that the BUG constraint removes.

Having specified the disformally related FLRW metrics and their respective bimodular constraints, we now substitute these ansatze into the general action to obtain the minisuperspace reduction and rederive the corresponding field equations.

V.2
Cosmological Field Equations

Reducing the bimodular action (
9
) to the spatially flat FLRW ansatz yields the bimodular minisuperspace action

\displaystyle S_{\text{BMG}}=\int dt\Bigg[

\displaystyle\left(-3\frac{a\dot{a}^{2}}{N}\right)+a^{3}\left(\frac{1}{2}\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{\hat{N}}-\hat{N}V(\phi)\right)

\displaystyle-\lambda_{1}\left(Na^{3}-\mathcal{C}_{1}\right)-\lambda_{2}\left(\hat{N}a^{3}-\mathcal{C}_{2}\right)\Bigg].

(54)

It is this action from which bimodular cosmological dynamics may be derived.

Variation with respect to
\lambda_{1,2}
reproduces the minisuperspace constraints

Na^{3}=\mathcal{C}_{1},\qquad\hat{N}a^{3}=\mathcal{C}_{2},

(55)

whose specific forms depend on the chosen bimodular formalism, as discussed above. Defining the Einstein-frame Hubble parameter
H\coloneqq\dot{a}/aN
and the matter-frame Hubble parameter
\hat{H}=H/\nu
, variation with respect to
N
gives the modified Friedmann equation

3H^{2}=\lambda_{1}+\dfrac{1}{\nu}\left(\hat{X}+V(\phi)+\lambda_{2}\right).

(56)

Varying with respect to

and eliminating
\lambda_{1}
using (

\frac{d\hat{H}}{d\hat{\tau}}=\frac{B\hat{H}}{\nu^{2}}\frac{dX}{d\hat{\tau}}+\frac{\hat{X}}{\nu}\left[B(\hat{X}-V-\lambda_{2})-\frac{1}{\nu^{2}}\right],

(57)

whilst variation with with respect to
\phi

\frac{d}{dt}\left[a^{3}\frac{\dot{\phi}}{\hat{N}}\left(1+B(\hat{X}+V+\lambda_{2})\right)\right]=-a^{3}\hat{N}V_{\phi}.

(58)

Eqs. (
57
)-(
58

Before investigating their dynamics, however, the next section considers their source terms utilising the single–metric presentation of Sec.
IV.1
, so that the biscalar sector can be packaged into an effective fluid. This fluid interpretation elucidates the distinct roles played by the bimodular cosmological constants within the different formalisms.

V.3
Bimodular Dark Energy

In the single–metric
k
-essence representation of bimodular gravity, (
36
), the biscalar contribution in (
56
) is naturally packaged as an effective perfect fluid carried by
\phi
together with the potential offset
\lambda_{2}
. We define

\displaystyle\rho_{\phi}

\displaystyle\coloneqq K=\frac{\hat{X}+V(\phi)+\lambda_{2}}{\nu},

(59)

\displaystyle p_{\phi}

\displaystyle\coloneqq 2XK_{X}-K=\nu\big(\hat{X}-V(\phi)-\lambda_{2}\big),

(60)

from
3
so that the corresponding equation-of-state parameter is

w_{\phi}\equiv\frac{p_{\phi}}{\rho_{\phi}}=\nu^{2}\frac{\hat{X}-\big(V(\phi)+\lambda_{2}\big)}{\hat{X}+\big(V(\phi)+\lambda_{2}\big)}.

(61)

In the canonical limit
BX\to 0
(hence
\nu\to 1
) and with
\lambda_{2}\to 0
, (
61
) reduces to the familiar quintessence expression
w_{\phi}=(X-V)/(X+V)
.

The quantity
\lambda_{2}
thus acts as a potential offset within the biscalar sector, rather than as a separate fluid. By contrast,
\lambda_{1}
enters the Einstein equations as an additive vacuum offset. If
\lambda_{1}
is constant (as in BHT/BDUG, or in BUG on solutions with
\dot{\lambda}_{1}=0
), one may consistently regard a separate vacuum component with

\rho_{\lambda_{1}}=\lambda_{1},\qquad p_{\lambda_{1}}=-\lambda_{1},\qquad w_{\lambda_{1}}=-1.

(62)

In general BUG backgrounds, however, only
\Lambda=\lambda_{1}+\nu\lambda_{2}
is conserved, so
\lambda_{1,2}
need not be individually constant. It is then most precise to view
\lambda_{1}
as an Einstein–frame vacuum offset (not a stand-alone conserved fluid), whilst
\lambda_{2}
remains part of the biscalar sector as a potential shift inside
\rho_{\phi}
and
p_{\phi}
in (
59
)-(
60
). Having clarified the status of the bimodular cosmological constants, we now proceed to investigate the dynamical behaviour that emerges within each formulation, beginning with BUG.

V.4
BUG: Coupled Background ODEs

In BUG cosmology, kinematic constraint is equal to
\hat{X}=\frac{1}{2}\phi^{\prime 2}=\tfrac{1}{2}C_{0}^{2}
, where
′
donates
d/d\hat{\tau}
. Equivalently, the would-be scalar acceleration
\phi^{\prime\prime}
ceases to be an independent degree of freedom. This constitutes the main structural difference from BHT. In other words, since
\phi^{\prime}=C_{0}
is fixed, the homogeneous biscalar equation of motion no longer provides a genuine second-order Klein–Gordon equation for
\phi
. Instead, it reduces to an algebraic (or first-order) consistency relation for the matter-frame Hubble rate.

Evaluating the derivative in (
58
) in BUG, we obtain

\hat{H}=-\frac{B}{3\big[1+B(\hat{X}+V+\lambda_{2})\big]}\left(\frac{1+BC_{0}^{2}}{BC_{0}^{2}}C_{0}V_{\phi}+\lambda_{2}^{\prime}\right),

(63)

which makes explicit that
\hat{H}
depends on the slope of the potential
V_{\phi}
and the time evolution of
\lambda_{2}
. Thus (
63
) plays the role of the BUG biscalar field equation, enforcing consistency between the biscalar sector and the cosmic expansion rate, rather than evolving
\phi
dynamically through a second-order
\phi^{\prime\prime}
term.

The remaining background equations in BUG are first-order in the matter–frame proper time
\hat{\tau}
. Combining (
63
) with the BUG Raychaudhuri equation (
57
) (which simplifies here because
dX/d\hat{\tau}=0
) and the balance law for
\lambda_{2}
, the homogeneous background reduces to the autonomous system

\displaystyle\frac{da}{d\hat{\tau}}

\displaystyle=a\hat{H},\qquad\frac{d\phi}{d\hat{\tau}}=C_{0},

(64)

\displaystyle\frac{d\hat{H}}{d\hat{\tau}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hat{X}}{\nu}\left[B\left(\hat{X}-V-\lambda_{2}\right)-\frac{1}{\nu^{2}}\right],

(65)

\displaystyle\frac{d\lambda_{2}}{d\hat{\tau}}

\displaystyle=-3\hat{H}\left[\frac{1}{B}+(\hat{X}+V+\lambda_{2})\right]-\frac{1}{1-\nu^{2}}C_{0}V_{\phi}.

(66)

Once a potential
V(\phi)
is specified, (
64
)–(
66

(a(\hat{\tau}),\hat{H}(\hat{\tau}),\lambda_{2}(\hat{\tau}))
, with
\phi(\hat{\tau})=\phi_{0}+C_{0}\hat{\tau}
determined trivially. Consequently, BUG cosmology possesses no independent second-order scalar degree of freedom at the background level. The biscalar does not accelerate freely—it simply coasts with (
64
). The entire background is therefore governed by coupled first-order flow in of
(\hat{H},\lambda_{2})
, whilst
\phi
serves merely as a clock-like variable, encoding the passage of
\hat{\tau}
.

V.5
Exact de Sitter in BUG

Having established in Sec.
V.4
that BUG removes the independent acceleration of the biscalar
\phi^{\prime\prime}
and reduces the background to a coupled first-order flow for
(\hat{H},\lambda_{2})
, we now ask under what conditions BUG admits an exact de Sitter (dS) phase. By “exact dS” we mean a matter-frame Hubble rate

\hat{H}(\hat{\tau})=\hat{H}_{0}=\text{const}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\frac{d\hat{H}}{d\hat{\tau}}=0.

(67)

Since
H=\nu\hat{H}
and
\nu
is constant in BUG, a constant
\hat{H}
automatically implies a constant
H
. Both frames are therefore simultaneously de Sitter.

Imposing
d\hat{H}/d\hat{\tau}=0
in the BUG Raychaudhuri equation (
65
) does not generate a differential condition but instead yields an algebraic constraint. One then finds

V^{*}\coloneqq V(\phi)+\lambda_{2}=\frac{1}{\nu^{2}}\left(X-\frac{1}{B}\right)=\text{const}.

(68)

Hence, on a BUG de Sitter background the combination
V+\lambda_{2}
is locked to a constant value determined by the constants
B
and
X
. Substituting this relation into (
61
) yields
w_{\phi}=-1
, as expected for an exact cosmological constant. Furthermore, recalling that the BUG sound speed (
33
) depends only on the constant pair
(x,\alpha)
, with
x=BX
and
\alpha=BV^{*}
, it follows that
c_{s}^{2}
remains time-independent along this branch.

Differentiating (
68
) with respect to matter-frame proper time
\hat{\tau}
gives a direct relation between the multiplier
\lambda_{2}(\hat{\tau})
and the potential slope,

\frac{d\lambda_{2}}{d\hat{\tau}}=-C_{0}V_{\phi}.

(69)

Meanwhilst,
\lambda_{2}
is not a free variable in BUG, for it obeys its own first-order balance law (
66
). On the de Sitter branch defined by (
68
), that balance law simplifies considerably. Substituting (
68
) to eliminate
V+\lambda_{2}
in (
66
), the source term proportional to
\hat{H}
cancels identically. Consistency with (
69
) then requires

\frac{d\lambda_{2}}{d\hat{\tau}}=0,\qquad V_{\phi}=0\quad(\text{for }C_{0}\neq 0).

(70)

This shows that an exact de Sitter configuration in BUG forces
\lambda_{2}
to be constant and the potential to be locally flat along the rolling trajectory. The biscalar continues to evolve at fixed proper speed
C_{0}
, so
\hat{X}
and
\nu
remain constant, but it must do so on a flat plateau of
V(\phi)
.

The BUG balance law (
28
) then implies that
\lambda_{1}
is constant as well. Hence the bimodular cosmological constant (
29
) is trivially conserved on this de Sitter branch—it is literally a sum of constants. The background is therefore indistinguishable at the homogeneous level from a pure-
\Lambda
spacetime, albeit accompanied by a non-trivial disformal adjustment
\nu\neq 1
and a constantly rolling biscalar.

Importantly, (
70
) also makes clear that this dS phase is not automatic in BUG. Away from this branch,
\lambda_{2}(\hat{\tau})
evolves according to (
66
), and the matter-frame Hubble rate
\hat{H}(\hat{\tau})
follows that evolution through (
65

\lambda_{2}^{\prime}
and
V_{\phi}
vanish—typically marking the transition where their rates of change reverse sign. Thus, although
\Lambda=\lambda_{1}+\nu\lambda_{2}
is conserved in BUG, dS occurs only at the fixed point where the
(\lambda_{2},V)
flow halts; away from it, BUG departs from dS even with a constant
\Lambda
.

In contrast, in BHT the multipliers
\lambda_{1,2}
are constant by construction, the disformal factor
\nu(\hat{\tau})
is dynamical, and the biscalar obeys a genuine second-order equation
\phi^{\prime\prime}\neq 0
. As a result, BHT can sustain exact dS even with a time-dependent roll rate
\phi^{\prime}(\hat{\tau})
, provided the potential slope
V_{\phi}
satisfies the self-tuning relation, as we show in the following sections.

V.6
BHT: Coupled Background ODEs

In the BHT formulation, the multipliers
\lambda_{1,2}
are individually conserved,
\nabla_{\mu}\lambda_{1,2}=0
, so each acts as a standard unimodular integration constant in its respective frame. Unlike in BUG, however, no kinematic constraint is imposed on the relative volume factor
\nu
.
Consequently,
\phi^{\prime}
need not be fixed. This reintroduces genuine biscalar dynamics and, as we now show, leads to a second-order equation of motion for
\phi
.

Starting from the minisuperspace biscalar equation (
58
), the background biscalar equation in BHT reads

\frac{d}{d\hat{\tau}}\left[a^{3}\phi^{\prime}\Big(1+B(\hat{X}+V+\lambda_{2})\Big)\right]=-a^{3}V_{\phi},

(71)

where we have used the fact that
\lambda_{2}
is constant in BHT, so
\lambda_{2}^{\prime}=0
.

Expanding (
71
) and dividing by
a^{3}
gives

\Big[1+B(\hat{X}+V+\lambda_{2})\Big](\phi^{\prime\prime}+3\hat{H}\phi^{\prime})=\big(1+2B\hat{X}\big)V_{\phi}-B\hat{X}^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}.

(72)

By definition of
X
, one has
\hat{X}^{\prime}=\phi^{\prime}\phi^{\prime\prime}
. Substituting this into (
72
) and solving explicitly for
\phi^{\prime\prime}
yields

\phi^{\prime\prime}=\frac{\big(1+2B\hat{X}\big)V_{\phi}-3\hat{H}\Big[1+B(\hat{X}+V+\lambda_{2})\Big]\phi^{\prime}}{1+B\big(3\hat{X}+V+\lambda_{2}\big)}.

(73)

This is a genuine second-order evolution equation for the biscalar
\phi(\hat{\tau})
in the matter frame. It has no BUG analogue. There, the dual fixed-determinant constraints enforce the kinematic constraint, reducing the biscalar dynamics to a first-order kinematic relation rather than a sourced second-order equation of motion.

The full homogeneous BHT background is therefore governed by the coupled system

\displaystyle\frac{da}{d\hat{\tau}}

\displaystyle=a\hat{H},\qquad\frac{d\phi}{d\hat{\tau}}=\phi^{\prime},

(74)

\displaystyle\frac{d\hat{H}}{d\hat{\tau}}

\displaystyle=\frac{B\hat{H}}{1+2B\hat{X}}\frac{d\hat{X}}{d\hat{\tau}}+\frac{\hat{X}}{\nu}\left[B(\hat{X}-V-\lambda_{2})-\frac{1}{\nu^{2}}\right],

(75)

\displaystyle\frac{d\hat{X}}{d\hat{\tau}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\big(1+2B\hat{X}\big)V_{\phi}\phi^{\prime}-3\hat{H}\Big[1+B(\hat{X}+V+\lambda_{2})\Big]\phi^{\prime 2}}{1+B\big(3\hat{X}+V+\lambda_{2}\big)}.

(76)

Together, (
74
)-(
76
) form an autonomous first-order system in the variables
(a,\hat{H},\hat{X},\phi,\phi^{\prime})
, with
\lambda_{1,2}
treated as constants in BHT. The biscalar thus retains full dynamical freedom, in contrast to the algebraically locked BUG case, and its behaviour will determine contrasting dS configurations, as we show next.

V.7
Exact de Sitter in BHT

We now illustrate how the additional dynamical freedom of BHT manifests in cosmology by revisiting exact de Sitter. Imposing
\hat{H}(\hat{\tau})=\hat{H}_{0}=\text{const}
, we ask under what conditions such a solution may be sustained in BHT.

Using the autonomous system of the previous section, the condition
d\hat{H}/d\hat{\tau}=0
in the matter-frame Raychaudhuri equation (
75

\phi^{\prime\prime}=-\frac{1+2B\hat{X}}{B\hat{H}_{0}\phi^{\prime}}\frac{\hat{X}}{\nu}\left[B(\hat{X}-V-\lambda_{2})-\frac{1}{\nu^{2}}\right].

(77)

Independently, the biscalar equation of motion (
73
) specialised to
\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{0}
gives

\phi^{\prime\prime}=\frac{(1+2B\hat{X})V_{\phi}-3\hat{H}_{0}\Big[1+B(\hat{X}+V+\lambda_{2})\Big]\phi^{\prime}}{1+B\big(3\hat{X}+V+\lambda_{2}\big)}.

(78)

An exact matter–frame dS solution in BHT must satisfy (
77
) and (
78
) simultaneously. This already demonstrates a qualitative difference from BUG, as exemplified by the following cases.

Assume the additional ansatz
\phi^{\prime}=\phi^{\prime}_{0}\neq 0
, so
\hat{X}=\tfrac{1}{2}\phi_{0}^{\prime 2}
and
\nu
are constant, hence
\phi^{\prime\prime}=0
. Setting
\phi^{\prime\prime}=0
in (
77

B(\hat{X}-V-\lambda_{2})-\frac{1}{\nu^{2}}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad V=-\hat{X}-\lambda_{2}-\frac{1}{B}.

(79)

Thus the potential takes a constant value fixed by
B
,
\lambda_{2}
, and
\hat{X}
. Substituting
\phi^{\prime\prime}=0
and
V_{\phi}=0
into (
78

1+B(\hat{X}+V+\lambda_{2})=0,

(80)

which is immediately satisfied by (
79

V.5
: a constant-roll biscalar, a constant relative volume element
\nu
, and an effective vacuum energy set by the integration constants
\lambda_{1,2}
. However, the ways one can depart from exact dS under
\phi^{\prime\prime}=0
differ across the two formalisms. In BUG,
\lambda_{2}
may vary (unlocking
V+\lambda_{2}
) and thus drive deviations from dS even with
\phi^{\prime}=\text{const}
. Whereas in BHT,
\lambda_{2}
is constant, so within the constant-roll ansatz, one cannot leave dS without abandoning the condition
\phi^{\prime\prime}=0

BHT does indeed admit the distinct possibility, absent in BUG, of dS with time-dependent
\phi^{\prime}
. Equating (
77

78
) and solving for the potential slope yields

\displaystyle V_{\phi}=\frac{1}{1+2B\hat{X}}\Big[

\displaystyle\big(1+B(3\hat{X}+V+\lambda_{2})\big)\phi^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle+3\hat{H}_{0}\big(1+B(\hat{X}+V+\lambda_{2})\big)\phi^{\prime}\Big],

(81)

with
\phi^{\prime\prime}
governed by (
77

using (
77

\displaystyle V_{\phi}=\frac{\phi^{\prime}}{1+2B\hat{X}}\Bigg[3\hat{H}_{0}\big(1+B(\hat{X}+V+\lambda_{2})\big)

\displaystyle-\frac{1+B(3\hat{X}+V+\lambda_{2})}{2B\hat{H}_{0}\nu}\left(B(\hat{X}-V-\lambda_{2})-\frac{1}{\nu^{2}}\right)\Bigg].

(82)

Either form makes the key point evident. That being, unlike BUG, exact dS in BHT does not require
V_{\phi}=0
. Instead,
V_{\phi}
is fixed dynamically by the rolling rate
\phi^{\prime}
and the other related quantities. The dS state can thus be sustained whilst the scalar keeps evolving (
self-tuning
).

We have therefore shown that BHT supports two qualitatively different dS realisations, with a BUG-like constant-roll branch where
V
is constant, and a genuinely dynamical, self-tuned branch with
\phi^{\prime}(\hat{\tau})
varying in time and
V_{\phi}\neq 0
. The latter has no BUG analogue.

VI
Diffeomorphism-Invariant Reformulation

The demonstrated inequivalence between BUG and BHT—now exemplified with cosmological examples—prompts a natural question that we address in this section: can we restore diffeomorphism invariance without forfeiting the BUG balance law
\nabla_{\mu}(\lambda_{1}+\nu\lambda_{2})=0
and its kinematic constraint on
BX
?

VI.1
Motivation

For quantisation and for coupling to matter sectors in a standard covariant path integral, it is generally preferred to work with a formulation that is fully diffeomorphism invariant at the level of the action
11
. In the single-metric case, one may pass from UG to HT (or DUG) without concern, for they are classically equivalent and reproduce the same field equations as
\mathrm{GR}\!+\!\Lambda
.

In the present disformal bimetric setting, however, this equivalence is broken. There are distinct bimetric generalisations of the unimodular integration constant, and these lead to different on-shell relations, different field equations, and, consequently, different physical dynamics.

A naive attempt to covariantise bimodular gravity by importing the HT/DUG formalism separately for each metric enforces both
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
to be strict integration constants. Conversely, in BUG only a particular linear combination is conserved, and the individual multipliers remain dynamically correlated through the balance law. Treating the two HT sectors independently therefore overconstrains the multipliers—freezing both
\lambda_{1,2}
—and simultaneously underconstrains the relative volume element, leaving
X
as a fully dynamical variable. The biscalar then behaves as a standard, freely propagating degree of freedom with a genuine second-order evolution equation.

Hence, two formalisms that are equivalent in the single-metric case become on-shell inequivalent once two disformally related metrics are present. This motivates the search for a diffeomorphism-invariant reformulation that is tailored specifically to the bimodular structure which:

1.

realises full diffeomorphism invariance off shell;

2.

enforces the balance relation
\nabla_{\mu}(\lambda_{1}+\nu\lambda_{2})=0
on shell;

3.

reproduces the BUG kinematic constraint on

4.

reduces to BUG in an appropriate gauge, and hence is classically equivalent to BUG.

In what follows we present precisely such an action. One may regard it as the diffeomorphism-invariant completion of unimodularised bimetric scalar-tensor gravity, whereby full covariance is restored and the physical content of BUG is retained on shell.

VI.2
A Diffeomorphism-Invariant BUG

A diffeomorphism-invariant completion that reproduces the on-shell content of BUG can be obtained by correlating the two HT axillary vectors, rather than introducing two completely independent sectors. Specifically, start from the bimetric HT-like construction, (
17
), and specialise to

\tau^{\mu}_{1}\mapsto\tau^{\mu},\qquad\tau^{\mu}_{2}\mapsto\nu\tau^{\mu},

(83)

where
\tau^{\mu}
is a single auxiliary vector density, and
\nu
is a constant scalar. The two auxiliary vectors are now related by a common
\tau^{\mu}
, up to a fixed proportionality factor
\nu
. With this identification, the constraint sector of bimodular gravity becomes

\displaystyle S_{BMG}\supset-\int d^{4}x\Bigg[

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\big(\sqrt{-g}-\partial_{\mu}\tau^{\mu}\big)

\displaystyle+\lambda_{2}\big(\sqrt{-\hat{g}}-\nu\partial_{\mu}\tau^{\mu}\big)\Bigg].

(84)

Varying (
84
) with respect to
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
imposes

\sqrt{-g}=\partial_{\mu}\tau^{\mu},\qquad\sqrt{-\hat{g}}=\nu\partial_{\mu}\tau^{\mu}.

(85)

Taking the ratio immediately gives

\nu=\frac{\sqrt{-\hat{g}}}{\sqrt{-g}}=\sqrt{1-2BX},\quad\implies\quad BX=\text{const},

(86)

i.e. the relative volume element is restricted to the constant
\nu
by the Euler-Lagrange equations, dynamically enforcing the BUG kinematic constraint of (
14
). This is precisely the fixed-volume element condition of BUG, now realised within a diffeomorphism-invariant action.

Variation of (
84
) with respect to
\tau^{\mu}
yields

\partial_{\mu}\big(\lambda_{1}+\nu\lambda_{2}\big)=0,

(87)

which, once (
86
) is imposed, is exactly the BUG balance law leading to the conservation of a single bimodular cosmological constant. Most importantly, the proposed action does not force
\partial_{\mu}\lambda_{1}=0
and
\partial_{\mu}\lambda_{2}=0
separately. The multipliers are not individually constant; only the linear combination
\lambda_{1}+\nu\lambda_{2}
is conserved. This avoids the overconstraint of naively applying HT/DUG to each metric independently. At the same time, by fixing
\nu
via the correlated volume forms, the action prevents the underconstraint that would otherwise arise from treating the two volume elements as unrelated.

The gravitational and biscalar sectors of the theory are otherwise untouched. Consequently, the metric and biscalar field equations obtained from (
84

and

\leftrightarrow

\varepsilon\leftrightarrow\partial_{\mu}\tau^{\mu}

VII
Conclusions

In this paper we have addressed the nature of the analogous unimodular cosmological constant in a theory where matter and gravity couple to two distinct, disformally related metrics. We constructed a new framework that we termed bimodular gravity: a disformal bimetric scalar-tensor theory in which both
g_{\mu\nu}
and
\hat{g}_{\mu\nu}
are unimodularised simultaneously. We then specialised this bimodular construction to the direct analogue of fixed-determinant unimodular gravity, and to the diffeomorphism-invariant HT/DUG-type implementations. Although these formalisms are classically equivalent in the ordinary single-metric setting, we have shown that in the disformal bimetric case they become inequivalent already at the classical level.

This inequivalence directly impacts the resultant cosmologies. In BUG,
\phi^{\prime}=C_{0}
is fixed by the bimodular constraints, but
w_{\phi}(\hat{\tau})
can nevertheless evolve without invoking an independently accelerating scalar degree of freedom. The evolution arises instead from energy exchange between
V(\phi)
and
\lambda_{2}
under the BUG balance law keeping
\Lambda=\lambda_{1}+\nu\lambda_{2}
constant. BUG therefore realises a time-varying effective dark-energy equation of state even though
\phi^{\prime\prime}
never becomes a free dynamical variable. By contrast, in BHT/BDUG,
\lambda_{2}
is a strict constant but
\phi^{\prime\prime}
is truly dynamical, so
w_{\phi}
evolves in the more standard way: the scalar actually accelerates,
\nu
changes in time, and the effective fluid behaves in closer analogy with ordinary scalar-induced dynamical dark energy.

We illustrated these structural differences by analysing exact dS solutions. In BUG, consistency of the BUG background flow demanded that
\lambda_{2}^{\prime}=V_{\phi}=0
. Along this branch, one indeed finds
w_{\phi}=-1
, thus mimicking a cosmological-constant-like dark energy. In BHT, because
\phi^{\prime\prime}
is not constrained away, a genuinely dynamical-roll dS phase is permitted, provided the potential slope
V_{\phi}
satisfies an appropriate self-tuning relation.

We further recast the bimodular theory in the Einstein-frame and derived the biscalar sound speed and identified that within
0<BX<1/2
the biscalar is free of ghosts, free of gradient instabilities, and has subliminal propagation
0<c_{s}^{2}<1
if and only if
1+B(\hat{X}+V)>0
. The main distinction between the theories here is that in BUG,
c_{s}^{2}
may vary purely through the evolution of
\lambda_{2}
and
V(\phi)
, even though
BX
is locked, whereas in BHT/BDUG it may vary because
BX
itself is dynamical.

Finally, seeking a covariant completion which preserves the physical content of BUG (rather than that of BHT), we proposed a fully diffeomorphism-invariant formulation in which both determinants are tied to a single auxiliary vector density
\tau^{\mu}
, with a fixed proportionality between the two vector densities. This recovers the BUG balance law and background dynamics on shell, whilst maintaining full diffeomorphism invariance. We therefore regard this construction as the natural diffeomorphism-invariant completion of bimodular gravity.

In summary, we have shown that imposing unimodularity on both the gravitational and matter metrics in a disformal bimetric scalar-tensor theory leads to a fundamentally new realisation of vacuum energy.

These two theories therefore make inequivalent and, in principle, testable predictions for the background expansion history, the dark-energy equation of state, and the propagation speed of scalar perturbations. Future work will explore the addition of matter, linear and non-linear perturbations, possible screening mechanisms, and the impact on structure formation and gravitational slip. This will allow us to assess whether BUG-like locked-roll dark energy and BHT-like dynamical-roll dark energy can already be distinguished by current large-scale-structure data. It will also be necessary to address radiative stability, whether the BUG balance law is robust beyond the classical level, and how vacuum energy renormalisation feeds into
\lambda_{1,2}
separately versus only into their conserved combination
\Lambda
. If the bimodular cosmological constant survives quantum corrections in the simple manner suggested here, then bimodular gravity may provide a viable alternative route to late-time acceleration, distinct from both GR+
\Lambda
and standard scalar–tensor dark energy.

A further direction concerns canonical structure. The main appeal of unimodular gravity in quantum cosmology rests partly on the emergence of a natural unimodular-time variable. In a disformal bimetric setting, the question of whether one or two bimodular times arise, and how they relate to the matter-frame proper time, certainly merits a dedicated Hamiltonian analysis. This would also clarify the precise propagating degrees of freedom. The symmetry-promoted BUG completion proposed in Sec.
VI
provides a suitable starting point for such a treatment.

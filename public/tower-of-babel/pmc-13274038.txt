Research on medical students’ medical ethics reasoning ability–analysis based on the intermediate concept measurement method
Authors: Chen Feng, Xuewen Zhang, Lining Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveBy establishing an ethical assessment framework to evaluate the medical ethical reasoning ability of medical students, systematically reveal the current actual situation and problems of medical ethics education in medical schools, and provide empirical evidence and improvement directions for optimizing the medical ethics education system and enhancing educational effectiveness.MethodThis study was based on the Medical Ethics Intermediate Concept Measurement (MD-ICM) questionnaire developed by Ponsopa Pinijphon from the University of Alabama in the United States. For the first time in China, it conducted a localization and cultural adaptation of the questionnaire. The questionnaire covers principles such as patient autonomy, confidentiality, beneficence, and fair distribution. The survey subjects were selected through a multi-stage random sampling method. A database was established using EpiData 3.1 software, and the data were statistically analyzed using SPSS 21.0 software.ResultStudents who have studied medical ethics performed significantly better in making correct reasoning decisions compared to those who haven’t; in terms of “the teaching methods of the Medical Ethics Course”, students who chose scenario simulation and audio-visual learning methods scored significantly higher in ethical reasoning ability than those who chose theoretical lectures and case analysis; in terms of “the assessment methods of the Medical Ethics Course”, students who chose ethical reasoning tests and did not undergo test assessment scored significantly higher in ethical reasoning ability than those who chose theoretical concept tests and case reflection essays; in terms of “the learning needs of the Medical Ethics Course”, students with learning needs usually have stronger ethical emotions and ethical awareness.ConclusionThe medical ethics reasoning ability of medical students is significantly influenced by their medical ethics learning experiences, teaching methods of courses, assessment methods, and learning needs. Currently, there are shortcomings in medical ethics education in medical colleges. To enhance the ethical reasoning ability of medical students, it is necessary to strengthen the foundational position of medical ethics education, optimize the practice-oriented teaching mode, improve the diversified formative evaluation, and create a supportive educational atmosphere.Keywords: Medical Ethics, Medical Students, Intermediate Concept Measurement

Introduction

The morality embedded in medical theories and clinical practice, along with the ethics governing professional conduct, has long stood as a core tenet underpinning the medical profession and its sustainable development. In the modern era, as science and technology advance relentlessly and modern medicine evolves substantially, ethical dilemmas in clinical practice and medical research have grown increasingly prominent. Judgments of right and wrong in medical practice have grown far more nuanced and complex, placing traditional medical ethics under intense scrutiny. To deliver clinical care that meets patients’ expectations, a clinician must not only command an extensive body of medical knowledge and master proficient clinical skills, but also possess a fundamental ethical awareness and the ability to analyze and resolve ethical issues. When faced with ethical dilemmas in medicine that present binary or even multi-faceted trade-offs, clinicians should be able to conduct ethical reasoning and make decisions that achieve optimal clinical outcomes while adhering to core ethical and moral norms [1, 2].

Medical ethics reasoning refers to the ability of medical personnel to analyze, evaluate, infer and choose medical ethics issues in clinical practice based on their medical ethics awareness, knowledge and skills, so as to provide clear reasons and practical reflection for clinical decision-making [3, 4]. The “Consensus on Medical Ethics and Law for Future Doctors” published by the American Institute of Medical Ethics in 2010, as well as the “Romanell Report on the Current State of Medical Ethics Education” published in 2015, reaffirmed that after undergoing medical education, medical students should possess the ability to reason about medical ethics [5, 6].

This study employs the Intermediate Concept Measure (ICM) method to assess the medical ethics reasoning ability of medical undergraduates, focusing on four intermediate concepts: patient autonomy, confidentiality, beneficence, and equitable distribution [7]. The aim is to reveal the current status of medical ethics education in Chinese medical universities, identify problems, and propose targeted improvement suggestions. The Intermediate Concept Measure (ICM) was first proposed by American moral psychology experts Lester and Navaz, and has been continuously improved and refined by researchers such as Bebeau and Thoma, eventually becoming a new type of moral assessment method [8]. The intermediate concept is defined as a construct that mediates between general abstract concepts and specific behavioral norms [9]. It is more tangible than abstract moral frameworks, more generalizable than discrete behavioral norms, and typically embodies a strong professional character. For instance, when teaching medical ethics to medical staff, the focus is often on intermediate concepts, such as professional autonomy, informed consent, paternalism, proxy decision-making, privacy protection, medical integrity, the allocation or supply of scarce resources, etc. As an assessment tool based on ethical theories, ICM has undergone extensive empirical research in various professional fields and cultural contexts [10, 11]. By creating ethical dilemmas related to the profession, ICM evaluates students’ behavioral choices and motivations when dealing with moral conflicts, thereby more accurately reflecting their ethical reasoning abilities. Researchers believe that ICM can distinguish students from different educational levels and is highly sensitive to the effectiveness of professional ethics education [12]. Although ICM has conducted empirical research in multiple countries, the existing international studies mostly focus on the validation of tools within a single cultural background, lacking discussions on the local adaptation of core concepts of ethical reasoning in non-Western cultural contexts, and also failing to fully explore the adaptation mechanisms of the tools in cross-cultural applications. This study is the first to localize and culturally adapt Medical—Intermediate Concept Measurement (MD-ICM) in China. It not only fills the gap in the application of ICM tools in the medical culture context of East Asia, but also, by integrating cultural traits such as ‘family decision-making dominance’ and ‘special nature of doctor-patient relationship’ in the Chinese medical system, provides empirical support from a ‘non-Western perspective’ for international medical ethics education research.

Materials and methods

Questionnaire localization

In 2009, Ponsopa Pinijphon from the University of Alabama in the United States developed the Medical—Intermediate Concept Measurement (MD-ICM) questionnaire, which includes principles such as patient autonomy, confidentiality, beneficence, and fair distribution. Using the MD-ICM, he evaluated the medical ethics reasoning ability of 642 Thai medical students. The intermediate concepts included in this questionnaire cover most of the contents of the four principles proposed by Beauchamp (T.L.) and Childress (J.F.). Empirical studies have shown that MD-ICM is sensitive to the development of medical students’ ethical reasoning ability and is an effective tool for evaluating medical ethical reasoning ability [13, 14].

This study used the MD-ICM as the testing tool, and for the first time, it conducted questionnaire localization and cultural adaptation in China, making the questionnaire more accessible for Chinese students to read and understand, and ensuring its validity and cultural adaptability in the Chinese context. The specific process is as follows: (1) Forward translation: Invite 3 researchers with a professional background in medical ethics and native-level proficiency in English to independently translate the original MD-ICM questionnaire into Chinese, with a particular focus on maintaining the consistency of terminology for core ethical concepts; (2) Reverse translation: Invite 3 professional translators who have no prior exposure to the original questionnaire and possess experience in translating medical ethics academic terms, to translate the above Chinese version back into English. Ensure that the translated text maintains no semantic deviation from the original questionnaire; (3) Expert Review: Ten experts (five professors of medical ethics and five clinical ethics practitioners) were organized to conduct three rounds of comparison and review of the “positive translation version, negative translation version, and original questionnaire”, ultimately resulting in the initial draft; (4) Pilot test: 30 clinical medical students from a certain medical college in Province S (covering all five grades from the first year to the fifth year) were randomly selected for a pre-survey. A total of 30 valid questionnaires were collected. The response time and option recognition of the questionnaires were analyzed to preliminarily verify the operability of the questionnaire; (5) Cognitive Interviewing: Conducted one-on-one cognitive interviews with 30 students from the pilot test. The core questions were: “Does the case scenario reflect the actual clinical situation in China?” “Are the reasons for the behavioral choices clear and understandable?” “Are there any expressions that are culturally difficult to comprehend?” Finally, the questionnaire was revised to form the final version; (6) Validity and reliability test of the pretest: Exploratory factor analysis (EFA) was conducted on the survey data of the pretest. The results showed that the loading matrix of the 4 factors “Patient autonomy, Confidentiality, Beneficial and Patient autonomy, Beneficial and Fair distribution” was consistent with the theoretical framework of the original MD-ICM, and the Cronbach’s Alpha coefficients of each dimension were all > 0.70. This proved that the Chinese version questionnaire had cultural equivalence in terms of measurement dimensions and ethical concept connotations compared to the original tool, and could be used to assess the ethical reasoning ability of Chinese medical students. Expert discussion

This study invited 13 experts to answer and discuss the questionnaire. Based on the discussions, a consensus was reached on the behavioral choices and the reasons for those choices for each case, and standard answers were formed. All 13 experts hold professional titles at the deputy senior level or above. Among them, 6 are professional teachers in the field of medical ethics from four medical colleges in Province S, and 7 are senior doctors in the medical ethics review committee of a tertiary grade A general hospital as well as in clinical and nursing fields. They evaluated each option according to three categories: “appropriate”, “general” and “inappropriate”. The consensus reached does not need to be 100% unanimous. When the expert group reaches a consensus of 75% or more on a certain option, this option can be regarded as the standard answer.

Questionnaire reliability and validity test

The results of exploratory factor analysis (EFA) showed that the KMO value of the questionnaire was 0.647, which was greater than 0.60. This indicates that the possibility of conducting factor analysis on the data is relatively good. Bartlett’s test of sphericity showed a statistically significant difference (=795.831, P < 0.001), and this dimension is suitable for validity testing. The factors were extracted through principal component analysis. Among them, 4 components were principal components (with initial eigenvalues greater than 1), and the cumulative variance contribution rate reached 60.115% (cumulative variance contribution rate>60%), indicating that the questionnaire effect is acceptable and can fully reflect the original data. The maximum coefficient of variation method was used to perform orthogonal rotation analysis on each factor load to obtain the load matrix. The results showed that the composition of each factor was consistent with the model assumptions and had good structural validity. Using the most commonly used reliability measurement method, namely the α reliability coefficient, to measure the reliability of the scales covered by the study, the results are as follows: The Cronbach’s Alpha values for Case 1, Case 2, Case 3, and Case 4 are respectively 0.721, 0.712, 0.714, and 0.761. The overall scale has a value of 0.787, indicating good reliability.

Sample selection

This study used multi-stage random sampling to select samples. Firstly, using the method of stratified random sampling, according to the five-year integrated training mode of undergraduate teaching in clinical medicine in China, it is divided into five levels from the first year to the fifth year of university. Secondly, a simple random sampling method was adopted. Five administrative classes were randomly selected from each of the five grades, totaling 25 classes. Finally, by using the method of cluster random sampling, all the students from the above 25 classes, totaling 1209 clinical medical students, were selected as the subjects of this study.

The study sought ethical review opinions from the Ethics Committee of Jining Medical College. Experts believed that the researchers had informed the participants of the purpose of the survey and the anonymity of their information before collecting information, and had obtained the informed oral consent of the participants before collecting information. The procedure was in line with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and approved for implementation.

Subjects answer

In this study, 1209 students majoring in clinical medicine from the first to the fifth year of university were randomly selected for testing in a medical college in Province S of our country. The medical college offers a compulsory course of medical ethics in the first semester of the sophomore year, totaling 24 class hours. Data collection was carried out through on-site questionnaire surveys, explaining the purpose and significance of the survey to the subjects to gain their trust and support. Introduce each case one by one so that the participants have sufficient time to understand the content of the questionnaire and the answering requirements. A total of 1209 questionnaires were sent out and returned in the on-site survey. After eliminating the unqualified questionnaires, 1108 valid questionnaires were obtained, with an effective rate of 91.65%. The questionnaire requires the participants to evaluate the behavioral choices and reasons for each case, select three best behavioral choices (reasons for behavior) and rank them, as well as two worst behavioral choices (reasons for behavior) and rank them. The matching degree between the subjects’ choices and the experts’ choices determines the subjects’ scores. The demographic data of the subjects are shown in Table 2.

Score assignment code

The coding situation for SPSS scoring is as follows: (1) If the participant identifies the ;appropriate behavior; determined by experts as the ;best behavior;, it will be counted as + 3 points for ranking first,+2 points for ranking second, and ;;1 point for ranking third, with a maximum score of 6 points; (2) If the participant identifies the ;inappropriate behavior; determined by the expert as the ;worst behavior; and ranks first, it will be counted as ;;2 points, and ranks second, it will be counted as ;;1 points, with a maximum score of 3 points; (3) If the participant identifies the ;general behavior; judged by experts as the ;best behavior; or ;worst behavior;, it will be counted as 0 points; (4) If the participant identifies the ;inappropriate behavior; determined by the expert as the ;best behavior;, the scores will be -3, -2, and −;1 according to the sorting method, with the lowest score being −;6 points; (5) If the participant identifies the ;appropriate behavior; determined by the expert as the ;worst behavior;, it will be scored;−;2 and −;1 points respectively according to the sorting method, with the lowest score being −;3 points. The coding rules for assigning reasons for participants’ behavioral choices are the same as those for behavioral choices.

Data analysis

The database was established by using EpiData3.1, and statistical processing and analysis were carried out by spss21.0. Since the initial data showed a skewed distribution, the negative skewed data transformation method was adopted to normalize the data. In the internal consistency reliability test of the questionnaire, the overall Cronbach’s Alpha statistic was 0.787, indicating that the questionnaire has good reliability. The results of factor analysis showed that the KMO test statistic of this questionnaire was 0.673, and the Bartlett test P was less than 0.05, indicating that the questionnaire had good validity. Inter-group t-tests and multi-group one-way ANOVA F-tests were used for statistical analysis. P;<;0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Data analysis includes nine indicators. The ;appropriate score; represents the total score of the best behavior and the reasons for the best behavior, the ;inappropriate score; represents the total score of the worst behavior and the reasons for the worst behavior, the ;total score; represents the total score of the ;appropriate score; and the ;inappropriate score;, and the ;total score of behavior Choice; represents the total score of the best behavior and the worst behavior. The ;Total score for behavioral choice reasons; represents the sum of the scores for the best and worst behavioral reasons. The ;appropriate behavior score; indicates the score for the best behavior, the ;inappropriate behavior score; indicates the score for the worst behavior, the ;appropriate reason score; indicates the score for the best behavioral reason, and the ;inappropriate reason score; indicates the score for the worst behavioral reason. The higher the score of each indicator, the stronger the ability of medical ethical reasoning.

Survey results

This study mainly analyzes the influence of the following factors on the medical ethical reasoning ability of medical students.

The influence of ;whether one has studied medical ethics; on the medical ethics reasoning ability of medical students

As shown in Table 3, by comparing the scores of 601 students who have studied and 507 students who have not studied medical ethics in various indicators of medical ethics reasoning ability, it was found that learning experience has a significant impact on the two indicators of ;total score of behavioral choice; and ;score of appropriate behavior;. Students who have studied medical ethics score significantly higher in making reasoning decisions, especially correct reasoning decisions, than those who have not studied medical ethics. This indicates a positive correlation between the study of medical ethics courses and students’ ethical reasoning abilities, supporting the positive effect of medical ethics education on students’ ethical decision-making abilities. This is consistent with previous research findings [15]. Meanwhile, through investigation, it was found that whether or not students have studied medical ethics has no significant impact on analyzing the reasons behind decision-making and making erroneous inferences. This indicates that there are still limitations in medical ethics education, and further improvement is needed in developing students’ critical thinking and critical thinking abilities.

The influence of ;teaching methods of medical ethics courses; on medical students’ medical ethics reasoning ability

As shown in Table 4, among the 601 students who have studied medical ethics, a total of 507 students adopted theoretical instruction as the teaching method, accounting for 84.4%. A total of 65 people, accounting for 10.8%, adopted case analysis. Twenty people adopted audio-visual learning, accounting for 3.3%. Nine people, accounting for 1.5%, adopted scenario simulation. It is indicated that in the teaching of medical ethics, theoretical instruction remains the most important teaching method. Other teaching methods that are more closely combined with practice have not been effectively applied in actual teaching. By comparing the scores of teaching methods in various indicators of medical ethics reasoning ability, it was found that different teaching methods have a significant impact on the four indicators of ;appropriate score;, ;total score;, ;total score of behavioral choice;, and ;appropriate behavioral score;. Among them, the students who chose the teaching methods of situational simulation and audio-visual learning achieved significantly higher scores in ethical reasoning ability compared to those who opted for theoretical lectures and case analysis. Compared with audio-visual learning, the situational simulation teaching method is more effective. This provides empirical reference for the optimization of medical ethics teaching methods. The lowest score for the case analysis teaching method precisely indicates two prominent problems in the current teaching of medical ethics in medical colleges. Firstly, most of the teaching teachers have a background in humanities and lack medical professional knowledge, which makes it difficult to conduct in-depth analysis of clinical cases, resulting in poor teaching effectiveness; Secondly, there is a disconnect between theoretical teaching and clinical practice, especially in clinical internships and apprenticeships. There is a serious lack of practical teaching in the later stage of medical ethics, and the problem of emphasizing technology over ethics is quite common, which leads to students being unable to exercise their ethical reasoning ability through vivid clinical cases.

The impact of the ;medical ethics course assessment method; on medical students’ medical ethics reasoning ability

As shown in Table 5, among 601 students who have studied medical ethics, a total of 521 (86.7%) were assessed using theoretical concept tests; 31 individuals, accounting for 5.2%, underwent ethical reasoning testing; 37 people, accounting for 6.2%, used case studies to reflect on their writing; 12 people, accounting for 1.9%, chose not to test. It is indicated that in the assessment of medical ethics courses, theoretical concept tests remain the most important assessment method, while other process-oriented and practical assessment methods are used less frequently. By comparing the scores of different assessment methods on various indicators of medical ethics reasoning ability, it was found that different assessment methods have a significant impact on the four indicators of ;appropriate score;, ;total score;, ;total score of behavior selection;, and ;appropriate behavior score;. Among them, the scores of ethical reasoning ability of students who chose the ethical reasoning test and those who did not undergo the test assessment method were significantly higher than those of the theoretical concept test and the case reflection composition, indicating that the assessment method of ethical reasoning test has a positive effect on improving the effect of medical ethics education and exercising students’ ethical reasoning ability; For students who choose not to take the test, their learning situation may be evaluated through other means such as classroom observation and scenario simulation. This flexible and diverse assessment method can better stimulate students’ enthusiasm for learning and improve their learning efficiency compared with theoretical concept tests; Students who adopt the case reflection composition assessment method have relatively low scores in ethical reasoning ability. This is in contradiction with the claim in previous studies that the case reflection composition itself is an effective assessment method. It indicates that with the use of AI tools such as Deep Seek, Catgut, and Doubao, students may have a dependent mindset during the writing process. The use of AI to write essays on behalf of others was perfunctory, and the individual did not conduct in-depth reflection on the case in combination with the ethical knowledge learned in class. This also reminds us to adapt to the development of AI and design educational and assessment methods that can better inspire students’ independent thinking.

Conclusions and suggestions

Through the above investigation and research, it is found that whether students have studied medical ethics, the teaching methods, assessment methods, and learning needs of medical ethics, etc., will all have an impact on the medical ethics reasoning ability of medical students. Among them, students who have studied medical ethics have a significantly higher ability to make reasoning and decisions than those who have not; Situational simulation and audio-visual learning teaching methods are superior to theoretical instruction and case analysis; Ethical reasoning tests and other flexible and diverse assessment methods are superior to theoretical concept tests and case reflection essays; Students with high enthusiasm for learning scored significantly higher in all ethical reasoning abilities than those with weaker learning needs. Based on the above conclusions, this study puts forward the following four suggestions.

Attach importance to the education of medical ethical reasoning ability

Among the 1108 medical students surveyed in this study, 1053 students (95%) chose ;hoping to learn more about medical ethics;. This indicates that medical students generally attach great importance to medical ethics education and have a high demand for learning, which is consistent with existing research results [16]. In sharp contrast, the current situation of medical ethics education is not optimistic. Although there is a consensus in the education community that medical ethics courses should be an essential component of medical student training programs and systems, there is still no unified and guiding standard for the teaching objectives, content, and effectiveness evaluation of medical ethics. Many graduates from medical colleges and universities have reflected that what is taught in medical ethics classes is quite different from the reality they encounter when they leave school and take up medical positions. This gap between saying and doing, imagination and reality, makes it difficult for medical students to understand the ethical problems and disputes in clinical practice even after they have theoretical knowledge. Although medical students consider medical ethics education very important, they do not take it seriously with the same level of effort as they do when studying medical professional courses. Some medical students even think that medical ethics has little to do with them or is just ;common sense;. There is a disconnection between the ethical values self-reported by students and the efforts they have made for them, and the marginalized participation attitude of students towards ethical education is worrying [17–19].

At present, the purpose of medicine has evolved beyond the sole pursuit of extending human life to encompass the provision of holistic care and the enhancement of quality of life for all individuals. To fulfill this mission, medical education must reorient itself toward its humanistic roots and accord medical ethics education a foundational role in the training of competent medical professionals. Medical ethics education needs to teach students how to better understand the ethical demands of medical practice, explore how to solve various ethical problems in medical practice, safeguard the dignity of life, and so on. However, the issues explored in medical ethics are rarely obvious and straightforward; often, ethical decisions need to be made. Therefore, the most practical and measurable objective of medical ethics education is to foster students’ ethical awareness, ethical sensitivity, and the analytical thinking and reasoning competencies required to address ethical challenges. This equips them to identify real-world ethical dilemmas and complexities in clinical practice, make evidence-based judgments to resolve such dilemmas, and propose ethically sound and practically feasible solutions.

Improve the teaching methods of medical ethics courses

This study found that the teaching methods of scenario simulation and audio-visual learning were associated with better performance in medical ethics reasoning among students. Therefore, in terms of teaching methods, we should explore teaching approaches that are highly effective and closely related to practice [20] .The course of medical ethics should not only teach the theory, principles, and norms of medical ethics, but also focus more on the practical ethical dilemmas and difficulties in clinical medicine, making scientific judgments and analyses, aiming to cultivate students’ sensitivity to ethical issues and reasoning ability to solve ethical problems [21, 22]. To enhance the effectiveness of medical ethics teaching, ethical knowledge, competencies, and attitudes should be integrated into basic medical sciences, clinical teaching, and daily clinical practice across all contexts and settings to facilitate their practical application [23, 24]. During the basic medical education phase, the core theoretical knowledge of medical ethics can be organically integrated into professional textbooks, with an emphasis on elaborating on the ethical, social, and legal implications within specific professional domains. This allows students to develop an initial framework for medical ethics while acquiring foundational medical knowledge. In the clinical clerkship and internship phase, greater emphasis should be placed on the in-depth analysis and practical application of clinical ethical cases. For example, in the teaching of internal medicine, surgery, and other clinical disciplines, by integrating specific disease diagnosis and treatment processes, the ethical issues and decision-making considerations involved can be examined in detail, thereby fostering students’ ethical decision-making capabilities in real clinical practice.We suggest using standardized patient role-playing to transfer medical ethics education from classroom teaching to the clinical environment, enabling students to personally participate in real-world ethical issues. In addition, interactive large-scale group discussions, team learning and other models are also regarded as effective means to enhance teaching satisfaction and classroom participation [25, 26].

Optimize the evaluation methods of medical ethics education

This study found that the use of multiple assessment methods such as ethical reasoning, classroom observation, and situational simulation is associated with better performance in medical ethics teaching. Therefore, in terms of assessment methods, formative and diversified evaluation approaches should be adopted. The evaluation method for students in traditional medical ethics teaching mainly adopts the form of final written tests. The assessment content is mainly theoretical knowledge, and the assessment results are presented in quantified scores, which cannot truly examine the actual learning effect of students. To improve the assessment method, it is first necessary to clarify exactly what it is supposed to assess. We believe that the evaluation of the learning effect of medical ethics should cover three dimensions: ethical knowledge, attitude and morality, and ethical reasoning ability. The assessment of the knowledge dimension can be conducted through methods such as immediate questioning and in-class quizzes. At the same time, it should be combined with phased theoretical knowledge tests to examine students’ memory of basic concepts and key knowledge points, as well as their ability to explain and analyze problems using ethical theories. Assessment of the attitudinal dimension can be undertaken by observing students’ performance in medical ethics courses and through reflective writing, ethical journaling, and other such methods, with strict safeguards against plagiarism and over-reliance on artificial intelligence tools. Evaluation of the competency dimension can be carried out through role-playing, situational simulation, and other comparable methods to assess students’ ethical reasoning capabilities. Alternatively, it can leverage validated measurement tools—such as the Intermediate Concept Measurement (ICM) employed in this study. More advanced approaches, including virtual reality (VR)-based dynamic simulation, artificial intelligence (AI)-assisted analysis, and digital twin models for ethical competency, can also be adopted to enhance the accuracy of such evaluations. In practical application, educators should flexibly select and integrate a range of assessment methods based on factors such as teaching objectives, student characteristics, and available teaching resources. For instance, the evaluation of the teaching effectiveness of the multidisciplinary medical ethics course at Texas A&M University in the United States consists of three parts: group discussion performance accounts for 35%, classroom debate for 35%, and final paper writing for 30%. Among them, group discussions and classroom debates are handled by the group instructors, who are evaluated and scored after each class. The final papers are randomly assigned to teachers for grading. Students who are absent frequently or whose papers contain plagiarism will not pass the assessment [27].

Foster an atmosphere that supports medical ethics education

This study found that students with a strong desire to learn medical ethics demonstrated a higher ability in medical ethics reasoning. Therefore, in medical ethics education, it is necessary to actively guide students to pay attention to medical ethics issues and stimulate their interest in learning ethical reasoning knowledge and mastering ethical reasoning skills. However, emerging research has identified that in contemporary medical education, ;hidden curricula; are subtly undermining the efficacy of ethics education. High-intensity workload pressures in clinical settings, the pervasive influence of scientism, and ethical apathy among some clinical preceptors convey values that contradict classroom-based ethical theories—leading students to question the sanctity of core medical ethical principles. Cornell University medical ethicists point out that to foster an atmosphere and culture that supports medical ethics education, ;during ward rounds, the meaning of ethical decisions should be discussed and analyzed with the same strictness, enthusiasm, and attention to detail, so as to make diagnostic differentiations and provide treatments.; [28] A professor from the University of Chicago School of Medicine emphasized that clinical teachers should practice medical ethics at the bedside. ;These teachers should constantly teach and practice ethics when discussing surgical, pediatric or obstetric cases, and their practices will serve as role models for students to learn from.; [29] Medical ethics education not only relies on classroom lectures and clinical practice, but also requires the creation of a good campus cultural atmosphere. Schools should widely publicize the significance of medical ethics through various forms such as bulletin boards, lectures, debate competitions, knowledge contests, and ethics associations, guiding students to form correct professional ethics views. In addition to school education, training provided by health administrative departments, various lectures in society, as well as new media such as television and the internet, should all disseminate ethical and legal knowledge to medical personnel and the public, deepen medical ethics education, enhance the awareness of rights protection for both doctors and patients, and help medicine better serve human health [30, 31].

The main limitation of this study lies in the fact that it was conducted in a single medical college and the sample size was limited. Future research should incorporate larger sample cohorts recruited from medical colleges and universities across diverse geographic regions, which will facilitate a more in-depth exploration of medical ethics education.

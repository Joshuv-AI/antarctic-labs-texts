Effects of oxycodone and sufentanil on the quality of recovery after laparoscopic total hysterectomy: A randomized controlled trial
Authors: Zhongyu Liu, Shanliang Guo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:To compare the effects of oxycodone and sufentanil on the quality of recovery after laparoscopic total hysterectomy.Methods:Ninety patients scheduled for laparoscopic total hysterectomy between January and December 2023 were randomly divided into 2 groups: oxycodone group (Group O) and sufentanil group (Group S), with 45 patients in each group. The primary outcome measure was postoperative recovery status recorded using the Quality of Recovery-40 questionnaire.Results:At 24 hours postoperatively, the Quality of Recovery-40 scores of group O and group S were comparable (170 [156–179], 160 [146–178], P = .111). There were no statistically significant differences between the 2 groups in terms of visual analogue scale pain scores at any postoperative time points, postoperative cumulative sufentanil consumption, length of hospital stay, patient satisfaction, and incidence of postoperative nausea and vomiting, and incidence of postoperative dizziness (P > .05).Conclusions:Oxycodone and sufentanil provide comparable postoperative recovery after total laparoscopic hysterectomy.

1. Introduction

Laparoscopic total hysterectomy is a common gynecological procedure known for its small incisions and quicker postoperative recovery than traditional open surgery. However, due to factors such as traction and stimulation of the peritoneum and diaphragm caused by artificial pneumoperitoneum, as well as surgical procedures, patients may experience significant visceral and somatic pain postoperatively.[1] Postoperative pain extends beyond the incision site, encompassing diffuse systemic discomfort and lumbo-sacral distension, adversely impacting the patients’ postoperative recovery.[2]

In recent years, oxycodone, a dual κ and μ receptor agonist, has gained attention because of its unique ability to inhibit visceral pain. Research has shown that oxycodone effectively alleviates perioperative pain, with a lower incidence of side effects.[3] This contributes to enhanced patient comfort and promotes faster postoperative recovery.[4] Sufentanil, a highly selective opioid agonist, is widely used in clinical anesthesia in China due to its reliable analgesic effects and minimal side effects.[5,6] Although oxycodone has been reported for use in gynecological surgery, its role in postoperative recovery following total hysterectomy remains understudied. This study aims to investigate the impact of oxycodone versus sufentanil on postoperative recovery after laparoscopic total hysterectomy, providing scientific evidence for clinical application.

2. Materials and methods

This study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Jiangxi Provincial People’s Hospital (2023002) and all participants provided informed consent. Patients scheduled for laparoscopic total hysterectomy between January and December 2023 at our hospital were included, meeting the following criteria: age 40 to 65 years, body mass index 18 to 30 kg/m2, American Society of Anesthesiologists physical status classification I to II, and preoperative diagnosis of benign disease. A randomized, double-blind, parallel-group design was employed, with 45 patients randomly assigned to each group: oxycodone (Group O) and sufentanil (Group S). 2.3. Randomization and blinding

A random sequence without stratification was generated by a computer and sealed with consecutively numbered envelopes to conceal the random assignment. Patients were randomly assigned to 2 groups using the envelope method to receive oxycodone (Group O), sufentanil (Group S), with 45 patients in each group. All the above operations were conducted by nurses, and the researchers and patients are blind.

2.4. Study protocol

All patients underwent routine preoperative evaluation and education, including a description of the anesthesia management process. They were instructed to refrain from solid food for 6 to 8 hours and liquids for 2 to 3 hours prior to surgery. Intravenous access was established in the preanesthesia care unit, and upon arrival in the operating room, standard monitoring of blood pressure, electrocardiogram, pulse oximetry, and bispectral index was initiated. All patients were maintained with a bispectral index value of 40 to 60 during surgery. Rocuronium (0.1 mg/kg) was administered as needed for muscle relaxation. Pneumoperitoneum pressure was maintained at 10 mm Hg with a gas flow rate of 2 L/min. Temperature was monitored throughout the procedure, and warming measures were employed to prevent hypothermia.

2.5. Anesthesia protocol

Oxycodone group (Group O): preemptive analgesia with intravenous oxycodone (0.1 mg/kg) was administered 30 minutes prior to surgery. Anesthesia induction was achieved with midazolam (0.05 mg/kg), propofol (2 mg/kg), oxycodone (0.3 mg/kg), and rocuronium (0.6 mg/kg). Maintenance of anesthesia involved continuous inhalation of 1% sevoflurane. Oxycodone (0.1 mg/kg) was administered intermittently during surgery based on the surgical pleth index,[7,8] heart rate, and blood pressure changes. Target-controlled infusion of propofol was used to maintain a plasma target concentration of 2 to 4 µg/mL. Sevoflurane and oxycodone were discontinued during skin closure, and a final intravenous dose of oxycodone (0.1 mg/kg) was administered.

Sufentanil group (Group S): preemptive analgesia with intravenous sufentanil (0.1 µg/kg) was administered 30 minutes prior to surgery. Anesthesia induction was achieved with midazolam (0.05 mg/kg), propofol (2 mg/kg), sufentanil (0.3 µg/kg), and rocuronium (0.6 mg/kg). Maintenance of anesthesia involved continuous inhalation of 1% sevoflurane. Sufentanil (0.1 µg/kg) was administered intermittently during surgery based on the surgical pleth index, heart rate, and blood pressure changes. Target-controlled infusion of propofol was used to maintain a plasma target concentration of 2 to 4 µg/mL. Sevoflurane was discontinued during skin closure, and a final intravenous dose of sufentanil (0.1 µg/kg) was administered.

2.6. Postoperative analgesia

All patients received postoperative intravenous patient-controlled analgesia (PCA) following surgery. The PCA pump contained a mixture of sufentanil (2 µg/kg) and ondansetron (10 mg) diluted in 100 mL, with a delivery rate of 2 mL/h. The PCA dose was 2 mL, with a lockout interval of 20 minutes. Patients were instructed to press the PCA button if they experienced pain. Rescue analgesia was administered when the visual analogue scale score was >50 mm despite PCA use.

2.7. Surgical team

All surgical and anesthetic procedures were performed by a dedicated team consisting of the same group of surgeons and senior anesthesiologists.

2.8. Outcomes

The primary outcome measure in this study was the Quality of Recovery-40 (QoR-40) questionnaire score at 24 hours postoperatively. The QoR-40 was designed to evaluate patients’ physical and mental well-being after anesthesia and surgery, and it has been proposed as a suitable outcome measure in various clinical settings.[9,10]

Secondary outcomes included: Cumulative intraoperative opioid consumption; length of hospital stay; postoperative nausea and vomiting; postoperative dizziness; pain scores at 30 minutes, 2, 12, and 24 hours postoperatively; and patient satisfaction assessed using a 10-point scale (1 = poorest possible recovery and 10 = best possible recovery) at 24 hours postoperatively.

2.9. Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 22.0 software (IBM SPSS). Continuous data were detected for normality of distribution via the Shapiro–Wilk test. Categorical variables were reported as numbers (proportions). Continuous variables were described by the mean ± standard deviation (𝑥̅ ± s) or median (interquartile range), as appropriate. To compare the differences across groups, independent-samples t test (normal distribution), Mann–Whitney U test (skewed distribution) and Fisher exact test or chi-square test were undertaken. A 2-tailed P value of <.05 was considered statistically significant. Plotting was performed using GraphPad Prism 8.0 (GraphPad Software, www.graphpad.com).

Sample size calculation was performed using PASS 11.0.7 (NCSS, LCC). In the preliminary study, we assumed that the QoR-40 score for group O would be 10 points higher than that for group S 1 day postoperation.[11] With a 1-sided type I error (α) of 0.025 and a power (1 − β) of 0.9, it was determined that 37 subjects per group would be sufficient to detect a difference in QoR-40 scores. Accounting for a potential dropout rate of 20%, a total of 45 subjects per group were intended to be recruited.

A total of 93 patients were initially enrolled in the study. One patient in the S group was excluded due to a change in surgical procedure, 1 patient in each group withdrew from the study. Ultimately, 90 patients were included in the final analysis (45 patients per group).

4. Discussion

Oxycodone is a mixed μ and κ opioid receptor agonist known for its rapid onset and elimination half-life of 3.5 hours. It exhibits efficacy against both visceral and somatic pain. Kang et al showed that the ED95 of oxycodone for inducing anesthesia in women is 0.357 mg/kg (95% confidence interval, 0.297–2.563 mg/kg).[12] Considering that preemptive analgesia with 0.1 mg/kg oxycodone effectively reduces postoperative pain, an induction dose of 0.3 mg/kg was chosen for this study.[13] Sufentanil is a selective μ opioid receptor agonist characterized by its rapid onset, short duration of action, and potent analgesic properties, making it an ideal choice for perioperative pain management.[14] Based on previous literature, the equivalent dose ratio of oxycodone to sufentanil is 1000:1.[15] Therefore, this study employed equivalent doses of sufentanil and oxycodone for a comparative investigation to explore the clinical value of opioid analgesia in patients undergoing laparoscopic total hysterectomy.

Laparoscopic hysterectomy requires carbon dioxide pneumoperitoneum to create adequate surgical space and necessitates a head-up, feet-down position. These factors increase the likelihood of nonincisional pain, such as shoulder pain and pain in the diaphragm and abdomen. κ opioid receptors are expressed in the stomach, duodenum, jejunum, ileum, and proximal and distal colon, and their function is believed to include control of visceral pain, regulation of intestinal motility, and modulation of mucosal fluid and electrolyte transport.[16,17] Animal models have demonstrated that inflammation increases peripheral axonal transport of opioid receptors, leading to upregulation of peripheral nerve terminal opioid receptors, particularly κ receptors.[18] κ opioid receptor agonists can reduce pain responses associated with inflammation. As a dual opioid receptor agonist, oxycodone offers significant advantages over conventional opioids. Its κ opioid receptor agonism mitigates pain responses associated with inflammation, effectively alleviating visceral pain.

This trial compared oxycodone and sufentanil for postoperative pain management in laparoscopic total hysterectomy patients. Both groups achieved comparable levels of postoperative recovery, as evidenced by similar median QoR-40 scores (oxycodone: 170 [156–179], sufentanil: 160 [146–178]), although the difference was not statistically significant (P = .111). No significant differences were observed between the groups in terms of postoperative pain scores, sufentanil consumption, length of hospital stay, PONV incidence, dizziness, or patient satisfaction.

While effective pain control is essential for postoperative recovery, other factors, such as PONV, dizziness, and sleep quality, can also impact patient well-being. Although perioperative opioid use is a known risk factor for PONV and dizziness, no significant difference in PONV incidence was observed between the groups in this study.

Postoperative recovery is a complex process influenced by patient, anesthetic, and surgical factors. Early pain control is crucial, as inadequate management can lead to cardiopulmonary complications, increasing morbidity and mortality. Furthermore, the severity and duration of postoperative pain affect recovery quality and overall quality of life.[19] As reported both sufentanil and oxycodone effectively alleviate acute pain and are safe even at high doses, with minimal adverse effects.[20] Although oxycodone exhibits lower affinity for μ receptors compared to sufentanil,[21]its ability to enter the cerebrospinal fluid via active transport enhances its analgesic efficacy. Consequently, both oxycodone and sufentanil provide satisfactory perioperative pain relief.

The consistently low pain scores observed in this study within 24 hours postoperatively likely contributed to the overall high median QoR-40 scores in both groups. Previous studies have reported QoR-40 scores ranging from 141 to 173 at 1 day postlaparoscopic surgery, with higher scores indicating better recovery.[22–24] While no significant difference was observed in postoperative recovery quality scores between the 2 groups in this study, both groups achieved high scores, with the oxycodone group slightly exceeding the sufentanil group. This subtle difference may be attributed to oxycodone’s κ opioid receptor agonism.[25]

This study has several limitations. As a single-center trial, our findings may reflect site-specific practices and patient demographics, which could limit generalizability. Future multicenter studies are needed to confirm these results. Additionally, the observation period was short. Extending follow-up to 48 hours or 1 week postoperatively would better assess the long-term effects of oxycodone and sufentanil on recovery.

5. Conclusions

This study demonstrates that both oxycodone and sufentanil are safe and effective analgesics for patients undergoing laparoscopic total hysterectomy. Both medications provided comparable levels of pain relief and did not significantly differ in terms of postoperative recovery quality, pain scores, or incidence of postoperative complications. Furthermore, both drugs were associated with minimal adverse effects, promoting postoperative recovery. These findings suggest that both oxycodone and sufentanil can be considered viable options for perioperative pain management in this patient population, with no clear superiority of 1 over the other.

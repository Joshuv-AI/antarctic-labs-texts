Abstract
. We show that the Euler system for the Asai representation corresponding to a Hilbert modular eigenform over a real quadratic field, constructed by Lei, Loeffler and Zerbes (2018), can be interpolated p p -adically as the Hilbert modular form varies in a Hida family. This work is used as an important input in recent work of Grossi, Loeffler and Zerbes (2025) on the proof of the Bloch–Kato conjecture in analytic rank zero for the Asai representation.



1.
Introduction

This article is a sequel to the second author’s paper
[
She25
]
and is motivated by the theme of using
p
-adic variation of global cohomology classes to make progress on conjectures about special values of
L
-functions. This theme has its origins in the work of Bertolini–Darmon–Rotger
[
BDR15a
,
BDR15b
]
, where they studied Beilinson–Flach elements, which are canonical global cohomology classes in the cohomology of the tensor product of
p
-adic Galois representations of two weight two modular forms. They showed that these classes vary
p
-adically in Hida families, and used this in a crucial way to prove the Birch and Swinnerton–Dyer conjecture in analytic rank zero for the
\rho
-isotypic component of the Mordell–Weil group of an elliptic curve, where
\rho
is an odd irreducible Artin representation. Subsequent work by Lei–Loeffler–Zerbes
[
LLZ14
]
constructed an Euler system, a compatible collection of cohomology classes over cyclotomic fields, of which the Beilinson–Flach elements are the bottom layer. This construction was extended to pairs of higher-weight modular forms by Kings–Loeffler–Zerbes in
[
KLZ20
]
.

In both cases, the authors showed that the entire Euler system also varies
p
-adically in Hida families, and used this to derive further arithmetic applications. For instance, Kings–Loeffler–Zerbes proved the Bloch–Kato conjecture in analytic rank zero for the Galois representation associated to the tensor product of two modular forms, and as a corollary proved the finiteness of the Selmer group of an elliptic curve twisted by a two-dimensional odd irreducible Artin representation when the associated
L
-value does not vanish.

In this paper, we consider the Asai–Flach Euler system, introduced in
[
LLZ18
]
, which is an Euler system for the Asai representation corresponding to a Hilbert modular eigenform over a real quadratic field. We show that this Euler system interpolates in Hida families. In a similar spirit to the works described above, our results have found applications in recent work of Grossi–Loeffler–Zerbes
[
GLZ25
]
where they prove the Bloch–Kato conjecture in analytic rank zero for the Asai representation. Although our results are similar in outline to those described above for the Rankin–Selberg case, the arguments are significantly more complicated owing to the role of the global units of the field. We describe our main results in more detail below.

1.1.
Overview of results

Our first main result is an extension of a theorem from

]

3.
Control theorems

We now recall and extend some results from the paper

p

3.1.
Ordinary cohomology modules

Let
K^{(p)}\subset G\big(\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{f}}^{(p)}\big)
be a compact open such that
K^{(p)}G(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
(and hence any subgroup thereof) is sufficiently small.

Definition 3.1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{K^{(p)}}=\{u\in\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}:\left(\begin{smallmatrix}u&\\
&u\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in K^{(p)}\}
; and for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geqslant 1}
, we let

K_{n,p}\coloneqq\left\{g\in\GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}):g\equiv\begin{pmatrix}u&*\\
0&u\end{pmatrix}\bmod p^{n}\text{ for some }u\in\mathcal{E}_{K^{(p)}}\right\}.

When
n=0
, we define
K_{0,p}=\Iw_{p}
to be the Iwahori subgroup of
G(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, i.e., the subgroup of matrices which are upper triangular mod
p
. We let
K_{n}\coloneqq K^{(p)}K_{n,p}
.

Remark 3.1.2
.

This definition is motivated by the fact that
G(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
does not act freely on
\varinjlim_{K_{p}}Y_{G}(K^{(p)}K_{p})
; the closure
\overline{\mathcal{E}}_{K^{(p)}}
acts trivially (and the “sufficiently small” condition shows that the action of
G(\mathbb{Z}_{p})/\overline{\mathcal{E}}_{K^{(p)}}
is free).

Definition 3.1.3
.

Set
H^{i}_{\Iw}(Y_{G}(K_{\infty})_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathcal{O})\coloneqq\varprojlim_{n\geqslant 0}H^{i}_{\text{{\'{e}t}}}(Y_{G}(K_{\infty})_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathcal{O})
, where the limit is with respect to the pushforward maps.

These groups are equipped with actions by the following objects (all commuting with one another):

•

the prime-to-
p
Hecke algebra
\mathbb{T}_{K^{(p)}}
;

•

the operators
U^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{p}}
for
\mathfrak{p}\mid p
;

•

the Galois group
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
(with inertia groups outside
pN\Delta_{F}
acting trivially);

•

the group
\mathfrak{S}\coloneqq T(\mathbb{Z}_{p})/\overline{\mathcal{E}}_{K^{(p)}}
, where
T
is the diagonal torus.

To simplify some formulae later, we shall compose the natural pullback action of
\mathfrak{S}
with conjugation by the long Weyl element
w_{0}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. Note that
\mathfrak{S}
is an abelian
p
-adic Lie group of dimension
d+1+\delta_{F}
, where
\delta_{F}\geqslant 0
is the defect in Leopoldt’s conjecture for
F
.

Remark 3.1.4
.

Using Poincaré duality, one can check that
e^{\prime}_{\mathrm{ord}}H^{*}_{\Iw}(Y_{G}(K_{\infty})_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathcal{O})
coincides with the module denoted
\mathcal{H}_{11}(0)
in
[
Dim13
, §3.2]
(which is defined as the Pontryagin dual of a direct limit with
E/\mathcal{O}
coefficients).

We write
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
for the image of
T(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\cap K_{n,p}
in
\mathfrak{S}
, so the
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
are open subgroups with
\bigcap_{n}\mathfrak{S}_{n}=\{1\}
(and
\mathfrak{S}_{0}=\mathfrak{S}
). We set
\Lambda=\mathcal{O}[[\mathfrak{S}]]
, and more generally
\Lambda_{n}=\mathcal{O}[[\mathfrak{S}_{n}]]
.

Theorem 3.1.5
.

The groups
e^{\prime}_{\mathrm{ord}}H^{i}_{\Iw}\left(Y_{G}(K_{\infty})_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathcal{O}\right)
are finitely-generated modules over the Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda=\mathcal{O}[[\mathfrak{S}]]
.

Proof.

A very closely analogous statement is proved in §3 of
[
She25
]
using a slightly different family of subgroups
U_{n,p}
, rather than the
K_{n,p}
considered above. However, all that is needed for the arguments to work is that each of the subgroups in the tower contains
\overline{\mathcal{E}}_{K^{(p)}}
, which is the input for the crucial Proposition 2.4 of
[
She25
]
; this is true by definition for
K_{n,p}
, so the argument works for these subgroups without change. (Compare Proposition 2.7.9 of
[
LRZ24
]
, which is a similar statement for locally symmetric spaces for any group satisfying an additional condition –Milne’s axiom “SV5” (
[
Mil17
, pp.75]
) – which implies that the analogue of the group
\overline{\mathcal{E}}_{K^{(p)}}
for these groups is trivial.)
∎

3.2.
Moment maps

For a pure, dominant integral weight
\lambda
, we have a
moment map

\mom^{\lambda}_{n}:H^{i}_{\Iw}\left(Y_{G}(K_{\infty})_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathcal{O}\right)\to H^{i}_{\text{{\'{e}t}}}\left(Y_{G}(K_{n})_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathscr{H}^{[\lambda]}_{\mathcal{O}}\right)

defined by cup-product with the highest-weight vector of
V_{\lambda}^{\vee}
(regarded as a section of
\mathscr{H}^{[\lambda]}_{\mathcal{O}}
over
Y_{G}(K_{\infty})
) composed with projection to level
K_{n}
. Since the highest weight of
V_{\lambda}^{\vee}
is
-w_{0}\lambda
, and we have conjugated the
\mathfrak{S}
-action by
w_{0}
, this map factors through the tensor product
H^{i}_{\Iw}\left(Y_{G}(K_{\infty})_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathcal{O}\right)\otimes_{\Lambda_{n}}\mathcal{O}[\lambda]
. Moreover, it is compatible with the actions of
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
, of
\mathbb{T}
, and of the
U_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}
for
\mathfrak{p}\mid p
(since our normalization of
U_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}
makes it act trivially on the highest-weight vector).

Theorem 3.2.1

(Derived control theorem)
.

For any
n\geqslant 0
there is a spectral sequence (supported in
i\leqslant 0,j\geqslant 0
)

E_{2}^{ij}=\operatorname{Tor}_{-i}^{\Lambda_{n}}\Big(e^{\prime}_{\mathrm{ord}}H^{i}_{\Iw}(Y_{G}(K_{\infty})_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathcal{O}),\mathcal{O}[\lambda]\Big)\Longrightarrow e^{\prime}_{\mathrm{ord}}H^{i+j}_{\text{{\'{e}t}}}\left(Y_{G}(K_{n})_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathscr{H}^{[\lambda]}_{\mathcal{O}}\right),

in which the edge maps
E_{2}^{0j}\to E_{\infty}^{j}
are the maps

\mom_{n}^{\lambda}:e^{\prime}_{\mathrm{ord}}H^{j}_{\Iw}(Y_{G}(K_{\infty})_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathcal{O})\otimes_{\Lambda_{n}}\mathcal{O}[\lambda]\to e^{\prime}_{\mathrm{ord}}H^{j}_{\text{{\'{e}t}}}\left(Y_{G}(K_{n})_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathscr{H}^{[\lambda]}_{\mathcal{O}}\right).

Proof.

This is proved in exactly the same way as Corollary 3.14 of
[
She25
]
, which is the analogous statement for the level tower
(U_{n,p})_{n\geqslant 0}
rather than
(K_{n,p})_{n\geqslant 0}
.
∎

Remark 3.2.2
.

It follows from the Auslander–Buchsbaum formula that after inverting
p
, the set of
\lambda
for which the
E_{2}^{ij}
term is non-vanishing is contained in a closed subset of
\Spec\Lambda[1/p]
of codimension at least
|i|
. So the map
({\dagger})
is bijective away from a codimension
\geqslant 1
set, and injective away from a codimension
\geqslant 2
set.

Exactly as in
[
She25
]
, using the cohomological vanishing results of Caraiani–Tamiozzo
[
CT23
]
, if
\mathfrak{m}
is a maximal ideal of
\mathbb{T}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
whose associated mod
p
Galois representation has non-solvable image, the localization
e^{\prime}_{\mathrm{ord}}H^{j}_{\text{{\'{e}t}}}(Y_{G}(K_{\infty})_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}}
is zero for
j\neq d
, and projective over
\Lambda
for
j=d
. So the localization of
({\dagger})
at
\mathfrak{m}
is an isomorphism for every
\lambda
. This recovers Dimitrov’s exact control theorem
[
Dim13
, Theorem 3.8(iii)]
, under considerably weaker assumptions on
\mathfrak{m}
.

3.3.
Existence of families

In this section we suppose our prime-to-
p
level
K^{(p)}
is given by
U_{1}(\mathfrak{N})^{(p)}
for some ideal
\mathfrak{N}\trianglelefteqslant\mathcal{O}_{F}
coprime to
p
, where

U_{1}(\mathfrak{N})^{(p)}=\left\{\gamma\in\GL_{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{F}^{(p)}):\gamma\equiv\begin{pmatrix}*&*\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\bmod\mathfrak{N}\right\}.

For simplicity, we suppose that
\mathfrak{N}
does not divide 2, 3 or
\Delta_{F}
, which implies that
U_{1}(\mathfrak{N})
, and hence any subgroup of it, is sufficiently small
[
Dim09
, Lemma 2.1]
. (The general case can be treated by taking invariants under a suitable auxiliary subgroup.)

We now formulate the following definition. Let
\mathcal{C}=\operatorname{Max}A\subset\mathcal{W}=(\operatorname{Spf}\Lambda)^{\mathrm{rig}}
be an affinoid disc. To avoid vacuous cases we suppose that
\mathcal{C}
contains at least one classical weight; it follows that classical weights are in fact Zariski-dense in
\mathcal{C}
(at least if Leopoldt’s conjecture holds for
F
).

Definition 3.3.1
.

An
ordinary family of cuspidal automorphic representations

\underline{\Pi}
over
\mathcal{C}
consists of the following data:

•

a ring homomorphism
\underline{\Pi}:\mathbb{T}\otimes\mathbb{Z}[\{U_{\mathfrak{p}}:\mathfrak{p}\mid p\}]\to A
,

•

a direct summand
M({\underline{\Pi}})^{*}
of
e^{\prime}_{\mathrm{ord}}H^{d}_{\text{{\'{e}t}}}(Y_{G}(K_{\infty})_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathcal{O})(2)\otimes_{\Lambda}A
,

with the following properties:

•

M(\underline{\Pi})^{*}
is free of rank
2^{d}
over
A
and stable under the actions of
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
,
\mathbb{T}
, and the
U_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}
for
\mathfrak{p}\mid p
, with the Hecke operators acting via
\underline{\Pi}\circ\nu
, where
\nu
is the inverse map on the Hecke algebra.

•

For each dominant integral weight
\lambda\in\mathcal{C}
, the specialization of
\underline{\Pi}
at
\lambda
is the Hecke eigenvalue system associated to the ordinary refinement of
\Pi[\lambda]
, for some ordinary cuspidal automorphic representation
\Pi[\lambda]
of weight
\lambda
and conductor
\mathfrak{N}
(with coefficient field contained in
E
).

•

For each such
\lambda
, the map
\mom^{\lambda}_{0}
restricts to an isomorphism between the weight
\lambda
specialization of
M(\underline{\Pi})^{*}
, and the direct summand

M(\Pi[\lambda],\alpha[\lambda])^{*}\subseteq e^{\prime}_{\mathrm{ord}}H^{d}_{\text{{\'{e}t}}}(Y_{G}(K_{0})_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathscr{H}^{[\lambda]})(2)

defined above.

(Note that
M({\underline{\Pi}})^{*}
is not
a priori
defined as the dual of anything, but it will interpolate the duals of Asai Galois representations, hence the notation.)

Now let
\Pi
be a cuspidal automorphic representation which is cohomological of weight
\lambda
, which has conductor
\mathfrak{N}
and Hecke eigenvalues of
\Pi
lie in
E
, and which is ordinary at
p
(i.e.
p^{-\sum_{i}t_{i}}\mathcal{T}_{p}
acts as a unit).

Theorem 3.3.2
.

There exists an open disc
\mathcal{C}
and family
\underline{\Pi}
over
\mathcal{C}
whose specialization in weight
\lambda
is the given
\Pi
.

Proof.

Let
M^{i}_{\mathfrak{P}}
be the localization of
e^{\prime}_{\mathrm{ord}}H^{j}_{\Iw}(Y_{G}(K_{\infty})_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathcal{O})
at the ideal
\mathfrak{M}_{\Pi^{\vee}}\otimes\mathfrak{P}
of
\mathbb{T}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\Lambda
, where
\mathfrak{M}_{\Pi^{\vee}}=\nu(\mathfrak{M}_{\Pi})\triangleleft\mathbb{T}\otimes E
is the kernel of the Hecke eigenvalue system of
\Pi
, and
\mathfrak{P}\triangleleft\Lambda
is the ideal corresponding to the character
\lambda
.

Since localization is exact, we obtain a localized spectral sequence of finitely-generated
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{P}}
-modules with
E^{2}
terms

\operatorname{Tor}_{-i}^{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{P}}}(M^{j}_{\mathfrak{P}},\Lambda_{\mathfrak{P}}/\mathfrak{P})\Rightarrow e^{\prime}_{\mathrm{ord}}H^{i+j}_{\text{{\'{e}t}}}(Y_{G}(K_{0}),\mathscr{H}^{[\lambda]})_{(\mathfrak{M}_{\Pi^{\vee}})}.

The abutment is the
\Pi^{\vee}
-isotypic part of the cohomology, which is zero outside degree
d
and has dimension
2^{d}
in degree
d
. A calculation using Nakayama’s lemma and the local criterion for flatness shows that
M^{i}_{\mathfrak{P}}
is likewise zero for
i\neq d
and free of rank
2^{d}
over
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{P}}
for
i=d
.

This gives a direct summand of
M^{d}
with the required properties after localizing to some Zariski-open neighbourhood of
\lambda
; and we can choose our affinoid disc
\mathcal{C}
to be contained in this, since the affinoid
G
-topology is finer than the Zariski topology.
∎

Corollary 3.3.3
.

For a family
\underline{\Pi}
as in
Theorem

3.3.2
, at each classical point
\mu\in\mathcal{C}
, the map
(\myPr_{\mathfrak{p},\alpha})_{*}\circ\mom^{\mu}_{0}
is an isomorphism between the specialization of
M(\underline{\Pi})^{*}
at
\mu
and
M_{E}(\Pi[\mu])^{*}
.

Proof.

Theorem

3.3.2
identifies the weight
\mu
specialization with
M_{E}(\Pi[\mu];\alpha[\mu])^{*}
, where
\alpha[\mu]
is the ordinary
p
-refinement of
\Pi[\mu]
. We have seen that
\myPr_{p,\alpha,*}
identifies this space with
M_{E}(\Pi[\mu])^{*}
.
∎

Remark 3.3.4
.

One can formulate more general results interpolating representations
\Pi
which are ramified at
p
, but have non-zero
K_{n,p}
-invariants for some
n
with ordinary
U_{p}
-eigenvalues; these are precisely the representations which are

in the sense of
[
Dim13
]
. We leave the details to the interested reader.

Proposition 5.3.1

If

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{2}_{\Iw}(Y_{1}^{*}(\mathfrak{N}p^{\infty})_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathcal{O})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{2}_{\Iw}(Y_{G}(K_{\infty})_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}},\mathcal{O})}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\jmath_{\infty,*}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\jmath_{n,*}}

\displaystyle\jmath_{m,*}(y_{m}\cup b^{\lambda^{*}}_{m})

\displaystyle=\jmath_{m,*}(y_{m}\cup\jmath_{m}^{*}(b^{\lambda}_{m}))

\displaystyle=\jmath_{m,*}(y_{m})\cup b^{\lambda}_{m}.

Since the moment map

Remark 5.4.4

6.1.
Inert primes

For

inert in

4.1.3
using the formulae for Hecke actions on Asai–Flach classes from
[

]
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1.3
for
p
inert.

Recall that
\myPr_{p,\alpha}=\myPr_{p,1}-\tfrac{1}{\alpha}\myPr_{p,2}
; and
\AF^{[\Pi,\alpha,j]}
is the projection to the
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}
-eigenspace of the class
\AF^{[k_{1},k_{2},j]}_{\text{{\'{e}t}},\mathfrak{N}p}
in the notation of
op.cit.
, and likewise for
\AF^{[\Pi,j]}
.

Theorem 7.1.2(a) of
op.cit.
gives us the formula

\myPr_{p,1,*}\left(\AF^{[k_{1},k_{2},j]}_{\text{{\'{e}t}},\mathfrak{N}p}\right)=\left(1-p^{-2h}\mathcal{S}_{p}^{-1}\right)\AF^{[k_{1},k_{2},j]}_{\text{{\'{e}t}},\mathfrak{N}}.

For
\myPr_{p,1,*}
, we can apply Corollary 7.4.2 of
op.cit.
, but with a slight correction since our conventions for
\myPr_{p,2,*}
are not quite the same as the
(\pr_{2,p})_{*}
of
op.cit.
; with our conventions the formula becomes

\myPr_{p,2,*}\left(\AF^{[k_{1},k_{2},j]}_{\text{{\'{e}t}},\mathfrak{N}p}\right)=p^{1-h}\mathcal{S}_{p}^{-1}\left(1-p^{-2h}\mathcal{S}_{p}^{-1}\right)\AF^{[k_{1},k_{2},j]}_{\text{{\'{e}t}},\mathfrak{N}}.

Combining these, and noting that
\mathcal{S}_{p}^{-1}
acts as
p^{-2}\alpha\beta
on
M(\Pi)^{*}
, we see that the projection of
\myPr_{p,\alpha,*}\left(\AF^{[k_{1},k_{2},j]}_{\text{{\'{e}t}},\mathfrak{N}p}\right)
to

is given by the expected formula

\left(1-p^{-1-h}\beta\right)\left(1-p^{-2h-2}\alpha\beta\right)\AF^{[\Pi,j]}_{\text{{\'{e}t}}}.\qed

6.2.
Proof for
p
split

In the case of split primes, the theorem is substantially more difficult. If the primes above
p
are trivial in the narrow class group (so the degeneracy maps involved in the theorem preserve the image of
Y_{G^{*}}
in
Y_{G}
), then one can give a proof similar to the above. However, for primes
p
not satisfying this assumption different methods are required, so we shall instead use the representation-theoretic techniques based on multiplicity-one theorems developed in
[
LSZ22
]
.

Notation 1
.

•

Let
\mathcal{W}(\Pi_{p})
denote the Whittaker model of
\Pi_{p}
, with respect to some choice of unramified additive character of
F\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}
trivial on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

•

Let
W^{\mathrm{sph}}\in\mathcal{W}(\Pi_{p})
be the normalized spherical vector, and

W_{\alpha}=\myPr_{p,\alpha}\cdot W^{\mathrm{sph}}
the normalized
U_{p}
-eigenvector in
\mathcal{W}(\Pi_{p})^{\Iw_{p}}
.

•

Let
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{2})
be the Schwartz space of locally constant, compactly supported functions on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{2}
.

Proposition 6.2.1
.

There exists a map

\mathrm{Z}^{\mathrm{mot},j}:\mathcal{W}(\Pi_{p})\times\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{2})\to H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},M_{E}(\Pi)^{*}(-h))

which transforms under
g\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
by

\mathrm{Z}^{\mathrm{mot},j}(gW,g\Phi)=|\det g|^{-h}\mathrm{Z}^{\mathrm{mot},j}(W,\Phi).

Morever, we have

\mathrm{Z}^{\mathrm{mot},j}(W^{\mathrm{sph}},\mathrm{ch}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}))=\AF^{[\Pi,j]}_{\text{{\'{e}t}}},

whereas

\mathrm{Z}^{\mathrm{mot},j}\Big(W_{\alpha},\mathrm{ch}((0,1)+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2})\Big)=\tfrac{1}{(p^{2}-1)}\operatorname{Pr}_{p,\alpha,*}\left(\AF_{\text{{\'{e}t}}}^{[\Pi,\alpha,j]}\right).

Proof.

The construction of
\mathrm{Z}^{\mathrm{mot},j}
amounts to nothing more than carefully keeping track of all the choices made during the construction of the Euler system class; compare
[
LZ24
]
for the
\operatorname{GSp}_{4}
Euler system. See
[
Gro20
]
for a similar result phrased in terms of Hecke algebras for
G
(one can translate between that formulation and the one above using Frobenius reciprocity).
∎

Lemma 6.2.2
.

The space of linear functionals on
\mathcal{W}(\Pi_{p})\times\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{2})
with the
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-equivariance property described above is 1-dimensional, and spanned by the Rankin–Selberg zeta integral

Z^{\mathrm{an},j}(W,\Phi)\coloneqq\lim_{s\to h}\frac{1}{L^{\As}(\Pi_{p},s)}\int_{N(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\backslash\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}W(g)f^{\Phi}(g;s)\,\mathrm{d}g

where
f^{\Phi}
is a Godement–Siegel section (and
N
the upper-triangular unipotent).

Proof.

Any such linear functional must factor through the maximal quotient
\sigma
of
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{2})
on which the centre of
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
acts via the character
|\cdot|^{-2h}\omega_{\Pi}^{-1}
. This quotient is either an irreducible principal series, or a reducible principal series with 1-dimensional quotient; in either case it is a Whittaker-type representation, so the main result of
[
HS01
]
shows that
\Hom_{\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}(\Pi_{p}\times\sigma|\cdot|^{h},\mathbb{C})
is at most 1-dimensional. To complete the proof it suffices to check that
Z^{\mathrm{an},j}(W,\Phi)
has the required equivariance property (which is obvious) and is non-zero, which follows from the fact that it maps the spherical data to 1.
∎

So to prove Theorem
4.1.3
, which is a proportionality relation between values of the motivic linear functional
\mathrm{Z}^{\mathrm{mot},j}
, it suffices to show that the corresponding linear relation holds between values of the analytic linear functional
\mathrm{Z}^{\mathrm{an},j}
instead. It is well known that we have
\mathrm{Z}^{\mathrm{an},j}(W^{\mathrm{sph}},\mathrm{ch}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}))=1
, while an elementary computation gives

Z^{\mathrm{an},j}(W_{\alpha},\mathrm{ch}((0,1)+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}))=\frac{1}{(p^{2}-1)}\lim_{s\to h}\frac{\left(1-\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}p^{-1-s}\right)^{-1}}{L^{\As}(\Pi,s)}=\frac{1}{(p^{2}-1)}\mathcal{R}(p^{-1-h}).

This gives the result claimed.

A matrix-valued measure associated to the derivatives of a function of generalised bounded deformation Thanks: Preprint SISSA 04/2025/MATE
Gianni Dal Maso, Davide Donati

Abstract
. We associate to every function u ∈ G ​ B ​ D ​ ( Ω ) u\in GBD(\Omega) a measure μ u \mu_{u} with values in the space of symmetric matrices, which generalises the distributional symmetric gradient E ​ u Eu defined for functions of bounded deformation. We show that this measure μ u \mu_{u} admits a decomposition as the sum of three mutually singular matrix-valued measures μ u a \mu^{a}_{u} , μ u c \mu^{c}_{u} , and μ u j \mu^{j}_{u} , the absolutely continuous part, the Cantor part, and the jump part, as in the case of B ​ D ​ ( Ω ) BD(\Omega) functions. We then characterise the space G ​ S ​ B ​ D ​ ( Ω ) GSBD(\Omega) , originally defined only by slicing, as the space of functions u ∈ G ​ B ​ D ​ ( Ω ) u\in GBD(\Omega) such that μ u c = 0 \mu^{c}_{u}=0 . MSC codes: Primary: 49Q20, Secondary: 74A45. Keywords: Free discontinuity problems, functions of generalised bounded deformation, fine properties of functions.



1.
Introduction

Given a bounded open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
d\geq 1
, the spaces
GBD(\Omega)
of
functions of generalised bounded deformation
and
GSBD(\Omega)
of
special functions of generalised bounded deformation
were introduced in
[
20
]
to provide a functional framework for variational problems related to Griffith’s energy in fracture mechanics (see
[
9
,
30
]
). The main feature of these spaces is that they avoid the unnatural
L^{\infty}

a priori
bounds typically required for
compactness in the space
SBD(\Omega)
(see
[
8
]
).

Thanks to the very weak requirements appearing in the definitions of
GBD(\Omega)
and
GSBD(\Omega)
, it was shown in
[
20
]
that compactness in these spaces is achieved under very mild assumptions (see also
[
4
,
14
]
).
For the space
GSBD(\Omega)
compactness results under even weaker conditions have later been obtained
by Friedrich and Solombrino
[
33
]
in the planar case, and Chambolle and Crismale in the general case
[
12
]
. These results are similar to those available in the more restrictive setting of functions of bounded variation (see
[
5
,
31
,
29
]
). These advancements allow one to solve, in a weak sense, minimisation problems concerning Griffith’s functional, thereby justifying the use of
GSBD(\Omega)
for
brittle
models in fracture mechanics. Many more applications of the space
GSBD(\Omega)
were also considered in the recent literature, see, for instance,
[
1
,
11
,
32
,
16
,
39
,
17
,
3
,
2
,
18
]
.

The study of
cohesive
models for fracture mechanics in the
anti-plane
case carried out in
[
24
,
25
]
suggests that
GBD(\Omega)
should be the appropriate space for the study of minimisation problems connected with these models, when the anti-plane
hypothesis is dropped. This requires to extend to
GBD(\Omega)
the structure theorems proved in
[
6
]
for the space
BD(\Omega)
of
functions of bounded deformation
(see
[
6
,
41
,
40
,
37
]
), in analogy with what was done in
[
23
]
in the setting of functions of bounded variation.

The analysis of the
fine properties
of functions in
GBD(\Omega)
carried out in
[
20
]
reveales that many of the key structural features of the space
BD(\Omega)
of
functions of bounded deformation
(see
[
6
,
41
,
40
,
37
]
) naturally extend to
GBD(\Omega)
, albeit with suitable modifications to account for the weaker regularity of these functions.
The fine properties of
BD(\Omega)
were thoroughly examined in
[
6
]
, where the authors show for a function
u\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
in
BD(\Omega)
that the following conditions hold:

(a)

u
admits an
approximate gradient

\nabla u\in L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
, where
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is the space of
d\times d
matrices with real entries, and
\mathcal{E}u:=(\nabla u+\nabla u^{T})/2
defines an
approximate symmetric gradient
of
u
(see also
[
35
]
); moreover, if
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
is the
(d-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure, for every
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|\xi|=1\}
and for
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
-a.e
y\in\Pi^{\xi}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:y\cdot\xi=0\}
, the one-dimensional scalar function
t\mapsto u^{\xi}_{y}(t):=u(y+t\xi)\cdot\xi
has bounded variation and

(1.1)

\hskip 56.9055pt\mathcal{E}u(y+t\xi)\xi\cdot\xi=\nabla u^{\xi}_{y}(t)\quad\text{ for $\mathcal{L}^{1}$-a.e.\ $t\in\{s\in\mathbb{R}:y+s\xi\in\Omega\}$},

where
\mathcal{L}^{1}
is the one-dimensional Lebesgue measure and
\nabla u^{\xi}_{y}
denotes the absolutely continuous part of the distributional derivative
Du^{\xi}_{y}
of
u^{\xi}_{y}
;

(b)

the jump set
J_{u}
(see Definition
2.3
) is
(\mathcal{H}^{d-1},d-1)
-rectifiable (see (
2.1
) and also
[
26
]
), with measure theoretical unit normal
\nu_{u}
; in addition, for every
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and for
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
-a.e.
y\in\Pi^{\xi}
, setting
[u]:=u^{+}-u^{-}
, where
u^{+}
and
u^{-}
are the unilateral traces of
u
on
J_{u}
, and
(J^{\xi}_{u})^{\xi}_{y}:=\{t\in\mathbb{R}:x=y+t\xi\in J_{u}\text{ and }[u](x)\cdot\xi\neq 0\}
, we have

\displaystyle J_{u^{\xi}_{y}}=(J^{\xi}_{u})^{\xi}_{y}\,\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\,[u](y+t\xi)\cdot\xi=[u^{\xi}_{y}](t)\,\,\,\text{ for every }t\in(J^{\xi}_{u})^{\xi}_{y};

(c)

the distributional symmetric gradient
Eu:=(Du+Du^{T})/2
, which by definition is a bounded Radon measure taking values in the space
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}
of
d\times d
symmetric matrices, can be decomposed as the sum of three mutually singular measures:

(1.2)

Eu=(\mathcal{E}u)\mathcal{L}^{d}+E^{c}u+([u]\odot\nu_{u})\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}J_{u},

where
\mathcal{L}^{d}
is the Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
E^{c}u
is called the
Cantor part
of
Eu
,
\odot
is the symmetric tensor product, and
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}J_{u}
is the restriction of
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
to
J_{u}
; the Cantor part
E^{c}u
is singular with respect to
\mathcal{L}^{d}
and vanishes on all Borel sets that are
\sigma
-finite with respect to
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
.

In
GBD(\Omega)
property (c) would not make sense, since, in general, the symmetrised gradient
Eu
cannot be defined in the sense of distributions. In particular, it is not clear what is the analogue of
E^{c}u
for a function
u\in GBD(\Omega)
. Understanding how to generalise this term is crucial for possible applications to cohesive fracture mechanics, as shown in the corresponding problems for the anti-plane case (see
[
24
,
25
]
).

However, for
u\in GBD(\Omega)
it is shown in
[
20
]
that property (b) still holds and also that
u
admits an
approximate symmetric gradient
\mathcal{E}u
that enjoys the slicing property (
1.1
). It is still an open question whether
every

GBD(\Omega)
function admits an approximate gradient.

In this paper we extend the analysis of the fine properties of functions in
GBD(\Omega)
by introducing two
matrix-valued
measures
\mu_{u}
and
\mu^{c}_{u}
, which are closely related to the measures
Eu
and
E^{c}u
when
u\in BD(\Omega)
. To describe this result, we fix some notation. Given
R>0
, let
\tau_{R}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be the
1
-Lipschitz function defined by

\tau_{R}(s):=\begin{cases}-\frac{R}{2}&\text{ if }s\leq-\frac{R}{2},\\
s&\text{if }\frac{-R}{2}\leq s\leq\frac{R}{2},\\
\frac{R}{2}&\text{ if }s\geq\frac{R}{2}.\end{cases}

It follows from the definition of
GBD(\Omega)
(see Definition
2.5
and Remark
2.8
) that for every
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and
R>0
the distributional derivative of
\tau_{R}(u\cdot\xi)
in direction
\xi
, denoted by
D_{\xi}(\tau_{R}(u\cdot\xi))
, is a scalar-valued bounded Radon measure on
\Omega
.

The main result of our paper is that (see Corollary
8.6
)
for every
u\in GBD(\Omega)
and
r>0
, there exists a bounded Radon measure
\mu_{u,r}
on
\Omega
with values in
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}
such that for every
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
we have

(1.3)

\mu_{u,r}(B)\xi\cdot\xi=\lim_{R\to+\infty}D_{\xi}(\tau_{R}(u\cdot\xi))(B)

for every Borel set
B\subset\Omega
such that
B\cap J^{r}_{u}=\mathchar 31\relax
, where
J^{r}_{u}:=\{x\in J_{u}:|[u](x)|\geq r\}
.

If
u\in BD(\Omega)
we can see that
\mu_{u,r}(B)=(Eu)(B)
for every Borel set
B\subset\Omega
with
B\cap J^{r}_{u}=\mathchar 31\relax
(see Remark
8.7
).
In the general case
u\in GBD(\Omega)
, the measure
\mu_{u,r}
is not always the symmetrised distributional gradient
Eu
, and its connection with the distributional derivatives of
u
is given only by (
1.3
), which takes into account the directional derivatives of suitable truncations of the scalar components of
u
. However, the measure
\mu_{u,r}
enjoys many of the formal properties of
Eu
and in particular an analogue of property (c) above holds for
\mu_{u,r}
.

More precisely, in Corollary
8.6
, we prove that for every
u\in GBD(\Omega)
the measure
\mu_{u,r}
can be decomposed as the sum of three mutually singular measures:

(1.4)

\mu_{u,r}=\mu_{u}^{a}+\mu_{u}^{c}+\mu_{u,r}^{j},

where for every Borel set
B\subset\Omega

\displaystyle\mu^{a}_{u}(B)=\int_{B}\mathcal{E}u\,{\rm d}x,\qquad\mu^{j}_{u,r}(B)=\int_{(J_{u}\setminus J^{r}_{u})\cap B}([u]\odot\nu_{u})\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1},

and
\mu^{c}_{u}
is a singular measure (with respect to the
d
-dimensional Lebesgue measure) with values in
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}
that vanishes on all
\sigma
-finite Borel sets with respect to
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
(see Proposition
8.8
). We remark that both
\mu^{a}_{u}
and
\mu^{c}_{u}
do not depend on
r
.

A slicing property of the measure
\mu_{u}^{c}
, obtained in Proposition
8.5
, allows us to characterise the space
GSBD(\Omega)
, introduced in
[
20
, Definition 4.2]
using slicing arguments, as the set of functions
u\in GBD(\Omega)
such that
\mu^{c}_{u}=0
, in analogy with what happens for
SBD(\Omega)
(see
[
6
, Definition 4.6]
). Combining this result with the recent characterisation of the space
GBD(\Omega)
, proved by Chambolle and Crismale in
[
13
]
, we obtain, in Theorem
8.10
, an analogous characterisation for the space
GSBD(\Omega)
.

We now give a brief sketch of how we prove the existence of a measure
\mu_{u}=\mu_{u,1}
which satisfies (
1.3
) with
r=1
.
Straightforward arguments show that the limit in the right-hand side of (
1.3
) exists for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
(see Proposition
4.3
) and that this limit is equal to

(1.5)

\sigma_{u}^{\xi}(B):=|\xi|\int_{\Pi^{\xi}}Du^{\xi}_{y}((B\setminus J^{1}_{u})^{\xi}_{y})\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y),

where for every
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and for every
y\in\Pi^{\xi}
we set
E^{\xi}_{y}:=\{t\in\mathbb{R}:y+t\xi\in E\}
.
By the definition of
GBD(\Omega)
(see Definition
2.5
) the expression above defines a bounded Radon measure for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
To conclude, one needs to show that for every Borel set
B\subset\Omega
there exists
\mu_{u}(B)\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}
such that

(1.6)

\mu_{u}(B)\xi\cdot\xi=\sigma^{\xi}_{u}(B)

for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
. To prove this fact, we will show that for every Borel set
B\subset\Omega
the function
\xi\mapsto\sigma^{\xi}(B)
is 2-homogeneous, lower bounded, and satisfies the parallelogram identity, i.e.,

(1.7)

\sigma_{u}^{\xi+\eta}(B)+\sigma_{u}^{\xi-\eta}(B)=2\sigma_{u}^{\xi}(B)+2\sigma_{u}^{\eta}(B)

for every
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Indeed, these three conditions imply (see Proposition
2.2
) the existence of a symmetric matrix
\mu_{u}(B)
such that (
1.6
) holds. It is then easy to check that
B\mapsto\mu_{u}(B)
is a bounded Radon measure.
Since the 2-homogeneity and the lower boundedness are easily obtained, to conclude we only need to show that (
1.7
) holds.

This is done first in dimension
d=2
by means of a discretisation argument. We fix two linearly independent vectors
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and assume that
B
is a parallelogram with sides parallel to
\xi
and
\eta
. For every
\zeta\in\{\xi,\eta,\xi+\eta,\xi-\eta\}
we approximate the integral
\sigma^{\zeta}_{u}(B)
given by (
1.5
) by means of
Riemann sums
corresponding to a well-chosen grid of points
y_{j}
of
\Pi^{\zeta}
and write each term
Du^{\zeta}_{y_{j}}((B\setminus J_{u}^{1})^{\zeta}_{y_{j}})=Du^{\zeta}_{y_{j}}(B^{\zeta}_{y_{j}}\setminus(J_{u}^{1})^{\zeta}_{y_{j}})
as a sum over
i
of the numbers
Du^{\zeta}_{y_{j}}(I^{j}_{i}\setminus(J_{u}^{1})^{\zeta}_{y_{j}})
, where
I^{j}_{i}=[a_{i}^{j},a_{i+1}^{j})
are well-chosen disjoint small intervals, whose union is the interval
B^{\zeta}_{y_{j}}
.
The points
y_{j}
and
a^{j}_{i}
can be chosen by projecting onto the straight lines
\Pi^{\zeta}
and
\{y_{j}+t\zeta:t\in\mathbb{R}\}
the points
x_{i,j}
of a two-dimensional grid, constructed using discrete linear combinations of
\xi
and
\eta
, and translated by a small vector
\omega
to be chosen carefully. We observe that if
I^{j}_{i}\cap(J^{1}_{u})^{\zeta}_{y_{j}}=\mathchar 31\relax
, then

Du^{\zeta}_{y_{j}}(I^{j}_{i}\setminus(J_{u}^{1})^{\zeta}_{y_{j}})=u(y_{j}+a_{i+1}^{j}\zeta)\cdot\zeta-u(y_{j}+a_{i}^{j}\zeta)\cdot\zeta.

This leads to an approximation of
\sigma^{\zeta}_{u}(B)
based on the difference of the values of
u
on neighbouring grid-points
x_{i,j}.
Hence, if
I^{j}_{i}\cap(J^{1}_{u})^{\zeta}_{y_{j}}=\mathchar 31\relax
, writing carefully this approximation for every
\zeta\in\{\xi,\eta,\xi+\eta,\xi-\eta\}
we obtain a discrete version of the parallelogram identity (
1.7
). To conclude, we have to show that in this approximation we can neglect all terms that correspond to pairs
i,j
such that
I^{j}_{i}\cap(J^{1}_{u})^{\zeta}_{y_{j}}\neq\mathchar 31\relax
.
This step constitutes the main difficulty of the proof and will be the content of Section
6
. This result is obtained by regarding the sum of the contribution of these ill-behaved indices as sort of
Riemann sum
of arbitrarily small integrals.

Once the planar case
d=2
is settled, the general case
d>2
can be obtained by a Fubini-type argument, considering a sort of two dimensional slicing in the spirit of
[
6
]
. In our case, this is based on the properties of the restrictions of
GBD
functions to two dimensional slices proved in
[
20
]
(see Theorem
2.14
below).

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
we introduce the basic notions and the necessary tools we will use throughout the paper, while in Section
3
we present some technical results concerning approximation of Lebesgue integrals by means of Riemann sums. Then, we introduce in Section
4
the measures
\sigma^{\xi}_{u}
, which will be the main focus of the rest of the paper, and prove several properties of these measures. Section
5
is devoted to the proof of the quadraticity of the function
\xi\mapsto\sigma^{\xi}_{u}(B)
in the planar case
d=2
. In Section
6
we complete this proof by means of some technical arguments. This result is then extended to every dimension in Section
7
. In Section
8
, we prove the decomposition (
1.4
) and deduce from it several consequences. The Appendix is devoted to proving the measurability of several auxiliary functions appearing in the arguments of Section
6

We recall the definition of quadratic function.

Definition 2.1
.

A function

A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}

f(\xi)=A\xi\cdot\xi

We recall the following characterisation of quadratic functions.

Proposition 2.2
.

A function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is quadratic if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:

(a)

2-homogeneity:
f(t\xi)=t^{2}f(\xi)
for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and every
t\in\mathbb{R}
;

(b)

parallelogram identity: for every
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have

f(\xi+\eta)+f(\xi-\eta)=2f(\xi)+2f(\eta);

(c)

lower bound: there exists a constant
c>0
such that

f(\xi)\geq-c|\xi|^{2}\quad\text{for every $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$.}

Proof.

Assume that (a)-(c) hold. By applying
[
19
, Proposition 11.9]
to the function
g(\xi)=f(\xi)+c|\xi|^{2}
we obtain a matrix
B\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}
such that
g(\xi)=B\xi\cdot\xi
for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Setting
A:=B-cI
, where

f(\xi)=A\xi\cdot\xi
for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The converse implication is trivial.
∎

(2.1)

E=N_{0}\cup\big(\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}K_{i}\big).

We refer the reader to
[
7
, Chapter 2]
and to
[
28
, Chapter 3]
for the properties of these sets.

Approximate limits.

Let
E
be a Lebesgue measurable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be such that

(2.2)

\limsup_{\rho\to 0}\frac{\mathcal{L}^{d}(E\cap B_{\rho}(x))}{\rho^{d}}>0.

We say that an
\mathcal{L}^{d}
-measurable function
u\colon E\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
has approximate limit
\widetilde{u}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
at
x
, in symbols

(2.3)

\text{ap}\!\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\to x\\
y\in E\end{subarray}}u(y)=\widetilde{u}(x),

if for every
\varepsilon>0
we have

\lim_{\rho\to 0}\frac{\mathcal{L}^{d}(\{y\in E:|u(y)-\widetilde{u}(x)|>\varepsilon\}\cap B_{\rho}(x))}{\rho^{d}}=0.

Throughout the paper the symbol
\widetilde{u}(x)
is always used to denote the approximate limit of
u
considered in (
2.3
). By (
2.2
) the vector
\widetilde{u}(x)
is uniquely defined. The set
S_{u}
is defined as the complement of the points where the approximate limit exists. It is well-known that for an
\mathcal{L}^{d}
-measurable function
u\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
it holds
\mathcal{L}^{d}(S_{u})=0
.

Jump set.

We now give the definition of jump set of a measurable function.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
U
be an open set of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
u\colon U\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
an
\mathcal{L}^{d}
-measurable function. The
jump set

J_{u}
is the set of all points
x\in U
such that there exists
(u^{+}(x),u^{-}(x),\nu_{u}(x))\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, with
u^{+}(x)\neq u^{-}(x)
, such that

\displaystyle\text{ap}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\to x\\
y\in H^{\pm}(x)\cap U\end{subarray}}u(y)=u^{\pm}(x),

where
H^{\pm}(x):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\pm(y-x)\cdot\nu_{u}(x)>0\}
.
The triple
(u^{+}(x),u^{-}(x),\nu_{u}(x))
is uniquely defined up to swapping
u^{+}(x)
and
u^{-}(x)
and changing the sign of
\nu_{u}(x)
. Given
x\in J_{u}
, we set
[u](x):=u^{+}(x)-u^{-}(x)
.
For
r>0
we also introduce
J^{r}_{u}
as the set defined by

(2.4)

J^{r}_{u}:=\{x\in J_{u}:|[u](x)|\geq r\}.

Slicing
. For every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
A\subset\Omega
we define

A^{\xi}_{y}:=\{t\in\mathbb{R}:y+t\xi\in A\}.

Given a function
u\colon A\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define the slice in direction
\xi
of the
\xi
-component of
u
as the function
u^{\xi}_{y}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
defined by

u^{\xi}_{y}(t):=\begin{cases}u(y+t\xi)\cdot\xi&\text{if }t\in A^{\xi}_{y},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

\Pi^{\xi}
denotes the hyperplane orthogonal to
\xi
and passing through 0, that is,

\Pi^{\xi}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:y\cdot\xi=0\}.

The projection map onto
\Pi^{\xi}
is denoted by
\pi^{\xi}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\Pi^{\xi}
.
We shall use the following estimate.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
and let
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a Borel set. Then the function

y\mapsto\mathcal{H}^{0}(B^{\xi}_{y})

is
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
-measurable on
\Pi^{\xi}
and

(2.5)

\int_{\Pi^{\xi}}\mathcal{H}^{0}(B^{\xi}_{y})\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)\leq\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(B).

Proof.

For every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
i\in\mathbb{Z}
we set

B_{k,i}:=\{x\in B:i/2^{k}\leq x\cdot\xi<(i+1)/2^{k}\}

and we consider the function
f_{k}\colon\Pi^{\xi}\to[0,+\infty]
defined by

(2.6)

f_{k}(y):=\vphantom{\int_{j}}\smash{\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}}\chi_{\pi^{\xi}(B_{k,i})}(y),

where
\chi_{\pi^{\xi}(B_{k,i})}
is the characteristic function of the projection of
B_{k,i}
onto
\Pi^{\xi}
.
We observe that
f_{k}\leq f_{k+1}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

It follows from the definition of
B^{\xi}_{y}
that

(2.7)

\mathcal{H}^{0}(B^{\xi}_{y})=\lim_{k\to+\infty}f_{k}(y)\quad\hbox{for every }y\in\Pi^{\xi}.

By the Projection Theorem (see, e.g.,
[
15
, Proposition 8.4.4]
) for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
i\in\mathbb{Z}
the set

\pi^{\xi}(B_{k,i})
is
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
-measurable on
\Pi^{\xi}
. By (
2.6
) and (
2.7
)
this implies that the same is true for the function

y\mapsto\mathcal{H}^{0}(B^{\xi}_{y})
. By the Monotone Convergence Theorem we obtain from (
2.7
) that

(2.8)

\int_{\Pi^{\xi}}\mathcal{H}^{0}(B^{\xi}_{y})\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\Pi^{\xi}}f_{k}(y)\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y).

By (
2.6
) we have

\int_{\Pi^{\xi}}f_{k}(y)\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\pi^{\xi}(B_{k,i}))\leq\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(B_{k,i})=\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(B),

which, together with (
2.8
), gives (
2.5
).
∎

Functions of generalised bounded deformation.

We recall the definition of the space of
functions of generalised bounded deformation
, introduced in
[
20
, Definition 4.1]
. This definition uses the collection
\mathcal{T}
of regular truncation functions defined by

\mathcal{T}:=\{\tau\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}):-1/2\leq\tau\leq 1/2\,\text{ and }\,0\leq\tau^{\prime}\leq 1\}.

Definition 2.5
.

The space
GBD(\Omega)
is the space of all
\mathcal{L}^{d}
-measurable functions
u\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
for which there exists a measure
\lambda\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{b}(\Omega)
such that the following equivalent conditions are satisfied for every
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
:

(a)

for every
\tau\in\mathcal{T}
we have
D_{\xi}(\tau(u\cdot\xi))\in\mathcal{M}_{b}(\Omega)
and

(2.9)

|D_{\xi}(\tau(u\cdot\xi))|(B)\leq\lambda(B)\quad\text{for every Borel set }B\subset\Omega;

(b)

for
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
-a.e.
y\in\Pi^{\xi}
we have
u^{\xi}_{y}\in BV_{\rm loc}(\Omega^{\xi}_{y})
and

(2.10)

\int_{\Pi^{\xi}}\big(|Du^{\xi}_{y}|(B^{\xi}_{y}\setminus J^{1}_{u^{\xi}_{y}})+\mathcal{H}^{0}(B^{\xi}_{y}\cap J^{1}_{u^{\xi}_{y}})\big){\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)\leq\lambda(B)\,\,\,\text{for every Borel set }B\subset\Omega.

Remark 2.6
.

If
u\in GBD(\Omega)
it follows from (b) of Definition
2.5
that for every
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
there exists a Borel set
N_{\xi}\subset\Pi^{\xi}
, with
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(N_{\xi})=0
, such that
u^{\xi}_{y}\in BV_{\rm loc}(\Omega^{\xi}_{y})
and
|Du^{\xi}_{y}|(\Omega^{\xi}_{y})<+\infty
for every
y\in\Pi^{\xi}\setminus N_{\xi}
. In particular, if
\Omega^{\xi}_{y}
is an interval and
y\in\Pi^{\xi}\setminus N_{\xi}
, then
u^{\xi}_{y}\in BV(\Omega^{\xi}_{y})
.

Remark 2.7
.

The previous remark implies that, if
d=1
, then
GBD(\Omega):=\{u\in BV_{\rm loc}(\Omega):|Du|(\Omega)<+\infty\}
.

Remark 2.8
.

Condition (a) of Definition
2.5
can be strengthened by requiring that (
2.9
) holds also for every
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{\text{Lip}}
, where

\mathcal{T}_{\rm Lip}:=\{\tau\in\text{Lip}(\mathbb{R}):-1/2\leq\tau\leq 1/2,\,\,0\leq\tau^{\prime}\leq 1,\,\,\tau^{\prime}\text{ has compact support}\}.

Indeed, every function
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{\rm Lip}
can be approximated uniformly on
\mathbb{R}
by a sequence
(\tau_{n})_{n}\subset\mathcal{T}
, so that
\tau_{n}(u\cdot\xi)\to\tau(u\cdot\xi)
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
. Since for every
U\subset\Omega
open, the function
v\mapsto|D_{\xi}v|(U)
is lower semicontinuous with respect to the
L^{1}(\Omega)
convergence, by (
2.9
) we obtain that
D_{\xi}(\tau(u\cdot\xi))\in\mathcal{M}_{b}(\Omega)
and that

|D_{\xi}(\tau(u\cdot\xi))|(U)\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}|D_{\xi}(\tau_{n}(u\cdot\xi))|(U)\leq\lambda(U)

for every
U\subset\Omega
open. Inequality (
2.9
) for a general Borel set
B\subset\Omega
follows by approximation with open sets.

Remark 2.9
.

Inequality (
2.10
) can be extended to non-unitary vectors.
Elementary arguments show that for every
t\neq 0
,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
,
y\in\Pi^{\xi}
, and
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

(2.11)

A^{\xi}_{y}=tA^{t\xi}_{y}.

Since
u_{y}^{t\xi}(s)=tu_{y}^{\xi}(st)
for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle Du_{y}^{t\xi}(S)=|t|Du_{y}^{\xi}(tS)\quad\hbox{and}\quad|Du_{y}^{t\xi}|(S)=|t||Du_{y}^{\xi}|(tS),

(2.12)

\displaystyle tJ_{u_{y}^{t\xi}}=J_{u_{y}^{\xi}}\quad\hbox{and}\quad tJ_{u_{y}^{t\xi}}^{|t|}=J_{u_{y}^{\xi}}^{1}

for every Borel set
S\subset\Omega_{y}^{t\xi}
.
In particular, if
B\subset\Omega
is a Borel set, taking
S=B_{y}^{t\xi}\setminus J^{|t|}_{u^{t\xi}_{y}}
and using (
2.11
) and (
2.12
) we get

(2.13)

|Du_{y}^{t\xi}|(B_{y}^{t\xi}\setminus J^{|t|}_{u^{t\xi}_{y}})=|t||Du_{y}^{\xi}|(B_{y}^{\xi}\setminus J^{1}_{u^{\xi}_{y}}),

while, taking
S=B^{t\xi}_{y}
and using (
2.11
)

(2.14)

Du_{y}^{t\xi}(B_{y}^{t\xi})=|t|Du_{y}^{\xi}(B_{y}^{\xi}),

which will be used later in the proof of Proposition
4.4
.
Applying (
2.12
) and (
2.13
) with
t=|\xi|
and
\xi
replaced by
\xi/|\xi|
, from (
2.10
) we obtain

(2.15)

\displaystyle\int_{\Pi^{\xi}}|\xi||Du^{\xi}_{y}|(B^{\xi}_{y}\setminus J^{|\xi|}_{u^{\xi}_{y}}){\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)\leq|\xi|^{2}\lambda(B),

\displaystyle\int_{\Pi^{\xi}}\mathcal{H}^{0}(B^{\xi}_{y}\cap J^{|\xi|}_{u^{\xi}_{y}}){\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)\leq\lambda(B),

for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
and every Borel set
B\subset\Omega
.

Remark 2.10
.

It follows from
[
4
, Remark 3.6]
that, if
u\in GBD(\Omega)
, then
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J^{1}_{u})<+\infty
. More precisely, it is shown that, if
\lambda\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{b}(\Omega)
satisfies (
2.10
), then for every Borel set
B\subset\Omega
we have the inequality

(2.16)

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J^{1}_{u}\cap B)\leq 4d\lambda(B).

Since by Theorem
2.12
below for
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
-a.e.
y\in\Pi^{\xi}
we have

J^{|\xi|}_{u^{\xi}_{y}}\subset(J^{1}_{u})^{\xi}_{y},

it follows from (
2.5
), (
2.15
), and (
2.16
)
that

(2.17)

\displaystyle\int_{\Pi^{\xi}}|\xi||Du^{\xi}_{y}|((B\setminus J^{1}_{u})^{\xi}_{y}){\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)\leq|\xi|^{2}\lambda(B),

\displaystyle\int_{\Pi^{\xi}}\mathcal{H}^{0}((B\cap J^{1}_{u})^{\xi}_{y}){\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)\leq 4d\lambda(B).

Given
0<r\leq 1
, we can consider the function
v=u/r
, which by
[
20
, Remark 4.6]
satisfies (
2.10
) with
\lambda/r
. Applying (
2.16
) to this function, we obtain

(2.18)

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(J^{r}_{u}\cap B)\leq\frac{4d}{r}\lambda(B)

for every Borel set
B\subset\Omega
. In particular, this implies that
[u]\in L^{1}_{\rm weak}(J_{u},\mathcal{H}^{d-1})
. Moreover, since
J_{u}=\bigcup_{0<r\leq 1}J^{r}_{u}
, we have that
J_{u}
is
\sigma
-finite with respect to
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
.

Definition 2.11
.

For every
u\in GBD(\Omega)
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
we introduce the bounded Radon measure
\lambda^{\xi}_{u}
defined for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
and every Borel set
B\subset\Omega
by

(2.19)

\lambda^{\xi}_{u}(B):=\displaystyle\int_{\Pi^{\xi}}\big(|\xi||Du^{\xi}_{y}|(B^{\xi}_{y}\setminus J^{|\xi|}_{u^{\xi}_{y}})+\mathcal{H}^{0}(B^{\xi}_{y}\cap J^{|\xi|}_{u^{\xi}_{y}})\big){\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y).

Given
u\in GBD(\Omega)
, let
\lambda_{u}
be the smallest measure
\lambda
for which (a) and (b) of Definition
2.5
hold true.
It can be shown (see
[
20
, Proposition 4.17]
) that

(2.20)

\lambda_{u}(B)=\sup\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda^{\xi_{i}}_{u}(B_{i})\quad\text{ for every Borel set }B\subset\Omega,

where the supremum is taken over all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, all families
(\xi_{i})_{i=1}^{k}
of vectors of
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, and all Borel partitions
(B_{i})_{i=1}^{k}
of
B
.

The following theorem collects some of the fine properties of
GBD(\Omega)
functions, proved in
[
20
, Proposition 6.1 and Theorems 6.2, 8.1, 9.1]
.

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
u\in GBD(\Omega)
. Then the following properties hold:

(a)
existence of the approximate symmetric gradient: there exists
\mathcal{E}u\in L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym})
such that for
\mathcal{L}^{d}
-a.e.
x\in\Omega
we have

\underset{y\to x}{{\rm aplim}}\frac{(u(y)-u(x)-\mathcal{E}u(x)(y-x))\cdot(y-x)}{|x-y|^{2}}=0;

moreover, for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
and for
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
-a.e.
y\in\Pi^{\xi}
we have

\mathcal{E}u(y+t\xi)\xi\cdot\xi=\nabla u^{\xi}_{y}(t)

for
\mathcal{L}^{1}
-a.e.
t\in\Omega^{\xi}_{y}
, where
\nabla u^{\xi}_{y}
denotes the density of the absolutely continuous part of
Du^{\xi}_{y}
with respect to
\mathcal{L}^{1}
;

(b)

J_{u}
and its slices: the jump set
J_{u}
is
(\mathcal{H}^{d-1},d-1)
-rectifiable;
for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
and for
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
-a.e.
y\in\Pi^{\xi}
we have

(2.21)

\displaystyle J_{u^{\xi}_{y}}=\{x\in J_{u}:[u](x)\cdot\xi\neq 0\}^{\xi}_{y}\subset(J_{u})^{\xi}_{y},

(2.22)

\displaystyle(u^{\xi}_{y})^{\pm}(t)=u^{\pm}(y+t\xi)\cdot\xi\quad\text{for every }t\in(J_{u})^{\xi}_{y},

(2.23)

\displaystyle J^{|\xi|}_{u^{\xi}_{y}}\subset(J^{1}_{u})^{\xi}_{y},

where the normals at
J_{u}
and
J_{u^{\xi}_{y}}
are oriented in such a way that
\nu_{u}\cdot\xi\geq 0
and
\nu_{u^{\xi}_{y}}=1
;
moreover, setting

(2.24)

\Theta_{u}:=\Big\{x\in\Omega:\limsup_{\rho\to 0}\frac{\lambda_{u}(B_{\rho}(x))}{\rho^{d-1}}>0\Big\},

we have that
\Theta_{u}
is
(\mathcal{H}^{d-1},d-1)
-rectifiable,
J_{u}\subset\Theta_{u}
, and that
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\Theta_{u}\setminus J_{u})=0
.

It is easy to see that for a function
u\in GBD(\Omega)\setminus BD(\Omega)
its jump
[u]
may be not integrable on
J_{u}
with respect to
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
(see, for instance,
[
20
, Example 12.3]
). More precisely, one can show (see, for instance,
[
6
, Proposition 3.2]
or
[
38
, Remark 2.17]
) that, if
u\in GBD(\Omega)\cap L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
[u]\in L^{1}(J_{u},\mathcal{H}^{d-1})
, then
u
belongs to
BD(\Omega)
.
Nonetheless, we now show that
[u]
is integrable on
J_{u}\setminus J^{1}_{u}
with respect to
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
. This is the content of the following proposition.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
u\in GBD(\Omega)
. Then

(2.25)

\int_{J_{u}\setminus J^{1}_{u}}|([u]\odot\nu_{u})\xi\cdot\xi|\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}<+\infty

for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. This is equivalent to

[u]\in L^{1}(J_{u}\setminus J^{1}_{u},\mathcal{H}^{d-1})
.

Proof.

By homogeneity it is enough to show (
2.25
) for a fixed
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. By definition of
\odot
we have
([u]\odot\nu_{u})\xi\cdot\xi=([u]\cdot\xi)(\nu\cdot\xi)
. Therefore, by the Area Formula
[
36
, 12.4]
, we can write

(2.26)

\begin{gathered}\int_{J_{u}\setminus J^{1}_{u}}|([u]\odot\nu_{u})\xi\cdot\xi|\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}=\int_{J_{u}\setminus J^{1}_{u}}|([u]\cdot\xi)(\nu_{u}\cdot\xi)|\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\\
=\int_{\Pi^{\xi}}\Big(\int_{(J_{u}\setminus J^{1}_{u})^{\xi}_{y}}|[u](y+t\xi)\cdot\xi|\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{0}(t)\Big){\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)\\
\leq\int_{\Pi^{\xi}}\Big(\int_{J_{u^{\xi}_{y}}\setminus J^{1}_{u^{\xi}_{y}}}|[u^{\xi}_{y}](t)|\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{0}(t)\Big){\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y),\end{gathered}

where in the inequality we have used (
2.21
)-(
2.23
). By (b) of Definition
2.5
, for
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
-a.e.
y\in\Pi^{\xi}
we have
u^{\xi}_{y}\in BV_{\rm loc}(\Omega^{\xi}_{y})
, so that

\displaystyle\int_{\Pi^{\xi}}\Big(\int_{J_{u^{\xi}_{y}}\setminus J^{1}_{u^{\xi}_{y}}}|[u^{\xi}_{y}](t)|\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{0}(t)\Big){\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)=\int_{\Pi^{\xi}}|Du^{\xi}_{y}|(J_{u^{\xi}_{y}}\setminus J^{1}_{u^{\xi}_{y}}){\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Pi^{\xi}}|Du^{\xi}_{y}|(\Omega^{\xi}_{y}\setminus J^{1}_{u^{\xi}_{y}}){\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)\leq\lambda(\Omega)<+\infty,

which, together with (
2.26
), concludes the proof of (
2.25
).

From the polarisation identity it follows that for every
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
one has

\displaystyle|([u]\odot\nu_{u})\xi\cdot\eta|\leq\frac{1}{4}\big(|([u]\odot\nu_{u})(\xi+\eta)\cdot(\xi+\eta)|+|([u]\odot\nu_{u})(\xi-\eta)\cdot(\xi-\eta)|\big)

on
J_{u}
.
Hence, by (
2.25
) we deduce that
[u]\odot\nu_{u}\in L^{1}(J_{u}\setminus J^{1}_{u},\mathcal{H}^{d-1})

Since for every
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have
|a||b|\leq\sqrt{2}|a\odot b|
,
the proof is concluded.
∎

The space
GBD(\Omega)
behaves nicely with respect to restriction to affine subspaces of the domain
\Omega
. This fact is made rigorous by the following result.

Theorem 2.14
.

Assume
d\geq 2
. Let
u\in GBD(\Omega)
, let
V
be a vector subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of dimension
k
, with
1\leq k\leq d-1
, let
V^{\perp}
be its orthogonal subspace, and let
\pi_{V}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to V
be the orthogonal projection onto
V
. For every
z\in V^{\perp}
and
E\subset\Omega
we set
E^{V}_{z}:=\{x\in V:z+x\in E\}=V\cap(E-z)
and consider the function
u^{V}_{z}\colon\Omega^{V}_{z}\to V
defined by
u^{V}_{z}(x):=\pi_{V}(u(z+x))
. Then the following properties hold:

(a)

for
\mathcal{H}^{d-k}
-a.e.
z\in V^{\perp}
we have
u^{V}_{z}\in GBD(\Omega^{V}_{z})
;

(b)

for
\mathcal{H}^{d-k}
-a.e.
z\in V^{\perp}
we have
J_{u^{V}_{z}}\subset(J_{u})^{V}_{z}\cup N_{z}
for a Borel set
N_{z}\subset V
with
\mathcal{H}^{k-1}(N_{z})=0
.

To prove property (b) we need the following result concerning the relation between the jump points of a function
u\in GBD(\Omega)
and the jump points of its restriction to a hyperplane that does not intersect the set
S_{u}
of approximate discontinuity points.

Proposition 2.15
.

Assume
d\geq 2
. Let
u\in GBD(\Omega)
,
x_{0}\in\Omega
, and
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Assume that

(2.27)

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(S_{u}\cap(x_{0}+\Pi^{\xi}))=0.

Let
v\colon(\Omega-x_{0})\cap\Pi^{\xi}\to\Pi^{\xi}
be the function defined by
v(y):=\pi^{\xi}(\widetilde{u}(x_{0}+y))
for
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
-a.e.
y\in(\Omega-x_{0})\cap\Pi^{\xi}
. Assume that there exist a direction
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\cap\Pi^{\xi}
and two vectors
b^{\pm}\in\Pi^{\xi}
, with
b^{+}\neq b^{-}
, such that for every
\varepsilon>0
we have

(2.28)

\limsup_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\{y\in B_{\rho}(0)\cap\Pi^{\xi}:\pm y\cdot\nu>0,\,|v(y)-b^{\pm}|>\varepsilon\})}{\rho^{d-1}}=0.

Then
x_{0}\in\Theta_{u}
.

Proof.

See
[
20

3.
Approximations by Riemann sums

In the proof of the main result of this paper, we will approximate various integrals with well-chosen Riemann sums, using a suitable version of a result that goes back to Hahn (see
[
34
]
). Similar results are proved also in
[
10
, Lemma A:1]
. For technical reasons we use a construction described in
[
27
, Page 63]
and further developed in
[
21
, Lemma 4.12]
. Since this result is crucial for our arguments, we give here the precise statement and a detailed proof.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
I=[a,b]\subset\mathbb{R}
be a bounded closed interval. For every
z\in I
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and
i\in\mathbb{Z}
let
t^{k}_{i}:=z+i/k
. Let
(X,\|\cdot\|)
be a Banach space and let
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
be a Bochner integrable function such that
f=0
on
\mathbb{R}\setminus I
. Then there exist an infinite subset
K\subset\mathbb{N}
and an
\mathcal{L}^{1}
-negligible set
N\subset I
such that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\underset{k\in K}{\lim_{k\to+\infty}}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{t_{i}^{k}}^{t_{i+1}^{k}}\|f(t^{k}_{i})-f(t)\|\,{\rm d}t=0,

for every
z\in I\setminus N
. Moreover, given
h\in\mathbb{N}
and setting
\mathcal{I}^{k}_{h}:=\{i\in\mathbb{Z}:[t^{k}_{i-h},t^{k}_{i+h}]\subset I\}
and

\mathcal{F}^{k}_{h}:=\{i\in\mathbb{Z}:t^{k}_{i}\in I\}\setminus\mathcal{I}^{k}_{h}
, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a Borel set
I_{\varepsilon}\subset I
, with
\mathcal{L}^{1}(I\setminus I_{\varepsilon})\leq\varepsilon
, such that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\underset{k\in K}{\lim_{k\to+\infty}}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{t_{i}^{k}}^{t_{i+1}^{k}}\|f(t^{k}_{i})-f(t)\|\,{\rm d}t=0\quad\text{ uniformly }\text{ for }z\in I_{\varepsilon},

(3.3)

\displaystyle\underset{k\in K}{\lim_{k\to+\infty}}\frac{1}{{k}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}^{k}_{h}}f(t_{i}^{k})=\int_{I}f(t)\,{\rm d}t\quad\text{ uniformly }\text{ for }z\in I_{\varepsilon},

(3.4)

\displaystyle\underset{k\in K}{\lim_{k\to+\infty}}\frac{1}{{k}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{F}^{k}_{h}}\!\|f(t_{i}^{k})\|\!=0\quad\text{ uniformly }\text{ for }z\in I_{\varepsilon}.

Proof.

We follow closely the proof of
[
21
, Lemma 4.12]
. Given
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider the set

(3.5)

\mathcal{J}^{k}:=\{i\in\mathbb{Z}:k(a-b)-1\leq i\leq k(b-a)\}.

Note that for every
z\in I
we have

[t_{i}^{k},t_{i+1}^{k}]\cap I\neq\mathchar 31\relax\quad\Longrightarrow\quad i\in\mathcal{J}^{k}.

Integrating with respect to
z
the sum on the left-hand side of (
3.1
) and using Fubini’s theorem we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{I}\Big(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{t^{k}_{i}}^{t^{k}_{i+1}}\|f(t^{k}_{i})-

\displaystyle f(t)\|\,{\rm d}t\Big){\rm d}z=\int_{I}\Big(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{J}^{k}}\int_{t^{k}_{i}}^{t^{k}_{i+1}}\|f(t^{k}_{i})-f(t)\|\,{\rm d}t\Big){\rm d}z

\displaystyle=\int_{I}\Big(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{J}^{k}}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{k}}\|f(z+\tfrac{i}{k})-f(z+\tfrac{i}{k}+s)\|\,{\rm d}s\Big){\rm d}z

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in\mathcal{J}^{k}}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{k}}\Big(\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\|f(z+\tfrac{i}{k})-f(z+\tfrac{i}{k}+s)\|\,{\rm d}z\Big){\rm d}s

(3.6)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{J}^{k}}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{k}}\Big(\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\|f(z)-f(z+s)\|\,{\rm d}z\Big){\rm d}s.

By the
L^{1}
-continuity of translations for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\|f(z)-f(z+s)\|\,{\rm d}z<\varepsilon

whenever
0\leq s<\delta
. Hence,

\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{k}}\Big(\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\|f(z)-f(z+s)\|\,{\rm d}z\Big){\rm d}s<\frac{\varepsilon}{k}

for
k>1/\delta
.
Observing that the number of elements of
\mathcal{J}^{k}
satisfies
\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{J}^{k})\leq 2k(b-a)+2
, from the previous inequality and (
3.6
) we deduce that

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{I}\Big(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{t^{k}_{i}}^{t^{k}_{i+1}}\|f(t^{k}_{i})-f(t)\|\,{\rm d}t\Big){\rm d}z=0.

Hence, there exists an infinite set
K\subset\mathbb{N}
and an
\mathcal{L}^{1}
-negligible set
N\subset I
such that (
3.1
) holds.

To prove the second part of the statement, we first observe that
by Egorov’s theorem there exists a Borel set
I_{\varepsilon}\subset I
, with
\mathcal{L}^{1}(I\setminus I_{\varepsilon})\leq\varepsilon
, such that (
3.2
) holds.

To prove (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), we first observe that, since the number of elements of

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}^{k}_{h}:=\{i\in\mathbb{Z}:[t^{k}_{i},t^{k}_{i+1}]\cap I\neq\mathchar 31\relax\}\setminus\mathcal{I}^{k}_{h}

is less than
2h+2
, by the absolute continuity of the integral
we have

(3.7)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\sum_{i\in\widehat{\mathcal{F}}^{k}_{h}}\int_{t^{k}_{i}}^{t^{k}_{i+1}}\|f(t)\|\,{\rm d}t=0\quad\text{ uniformly }\text{ for }z\in I_{\varepsilon}.

Since

\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{F}^{k}_{h}}\!\|f(t_{i}^{k})\|\!\leq\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{t_{i}^{k}}^{t_{i+1}^{k}}\!\|f(t_{i}^{k})-f(t)\|\,{\rm d}t+\sum_{i\in\mathcal{F}^{k}_{h}}\int_{t^{k}_{i}}^{t^{k}_{i+1}}\|f(t)\|\,{\rm d}t,

(
3.4
) follows from (
3.2
), (
3.7
), and from the inclusion
\mathcal{F}^{k}_{h}\subset\widehat{\mathcal{F}}^{k}_{h}
. Similarly, the inequality

\Big\|\frac{1}{{k}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}^{k}_{h}}f(t_{i}^{k})-\int_{I}f(t)\,{\rm d}t\Big\|\leq\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}^{k}_{h}}\int_{t_{i}^{k}}^{t_{i+1}^{k}}\|f(t^{k}_{i})-f(t)\|\,{\rm d}t+\sum_{i\in\widehat{\mathcal{F}}^{k}_{h}}\int_{t^{k}_{i}}^{t^{k}_{i+1}}\|f(t)\|\,{\rm d}t,

together with
(
3.2
) and (
3.7
), gives (
3.3
).
∎

In the next technical result we will consider the Riemann sums associated with functions converging to 0 in
L^{1}
and depending on an additional parameter. This result will be crucial in Section

.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
I:=[a,b]
and
J:=[c,d]
be bounded closed intervals. For every
z_{1}\in I
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and
i\in\mathbb{Z}
let
t^{k}_{i}:=z_{1}+\tfrac{i}{k}
. Let

be a Banach space and let

on
(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})\setminus(I\times J)

(3.8)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}f_{k}(t,z_{2})=0\quad\text{ for $\mathcal{L}^{1}$-a.e.\ $t\in I$ and $\mathcal{L}^{1}$ -a.e.\ $z_{2}\in J$}.

Assume also that there exists an integrable function

such that

(3.9)

\|f_{k}(t,z_{2})\|\leq g(t)\quad\text{for $\mathcal{L}^{1}$-a.e.\ $t\in I$, $\mathcal{L}^{1}\text{-a.e.}$ $z_{2}\in J$, and \text{every } $k\in\mathbb{N}$}.

Then there exists an
\mathcal{L}^{2}
-negligible set
N\subset I\times J
and a infinite set

such that for every

we have

(3.10)

\underset{k\in K}{\lim_{k\to+\infty}}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\|f_{k}(t^{k}_{i},z_{2})\|=0.

Moreover, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a Borel set
A_{\varepsilon}\subset I\times J
, with
\mathcal{L}^{2}((I\times J)\setminus A_{\varepsilon})\leq\varepsilon
, such that

(3.11)

\underset{k\in K}{\lim_{k\to+\infty}}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\|f_{k}(t^{k}_{i},z_{2})\|=0\text{ uniformly}\text{ for }(z_{1},z_{2})\in A_{\varepsilon}.

Proof.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
let
\mathcal{J}^{k}
be given by (
3.5
). Integrating the sum on the left-hand side of (
3.10
) with respect to
z=(z_{1},z_{2})\in I\times J

\displaystyle\int_{J}\Big(\int_{I}\Big(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\|f_{k}(t^{k}_{i},z_{2})\|\,\Big){\rm d}z_{1}\Big){\rm dz}_{2}=\int_{J}\Big(\int_{I}\Big(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\|f_{k}(z_{1}+\tfrac{i}{k},z_{2})\|\,\Big){\rm d}z_{1}\Big){\rm d}z_{2}

(3.12)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\leq\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{J}^{k}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|f_{k}(z_{1}+\tfrac{i}{k},z_{2})\|\,{\rm d}z_{1}\Big){\rm d}z_{2}=\frac{\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{J}^{k})}{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|f_{k}(z_{1},z_{2})\|\,{\rm d}z_{1}\Big){\rm d}z_{2},

where in the inequality we have used the hypothesis on the supports of
f_{k}
.
By (
3.8
) for
\mathcal{L}^{2}
-a.e. every

the sequence

This fact, together with (

Given

\lambda^{\xi}_{u}
introduced in (

). For every

and every Borel set

we set

\sigma_{u}^{\xi}(B):=|\xi|\int_{\Pi^{\xi}}Du^{\xi}_{y}((B\setminus J^{1}_{u})^{\xi}_{y})\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y).

For

, we set
\sigma^{\xi}_{u}(B)=0
for every Borel set
B\subset\Omega
.

Remark 4.1
.

Suppose that
u\in BD(\Omega)
, the space of
functions of bounded deformation
, and let
Eu
the distributional symmetric gradient of
u
, which by definition belongs to the space
\mathcal{M}_{b}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym})
. From the Structure Theorem for

functions
[
6
, Theorem 4.5]
it follows that

\sigma^{\xi}_{u}(B)=Eu(B\setminus J^{1}_{u})\xi\cdot\xi

for every Borel set
B\subset\Omega
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Since
\sigma_{u}^{\xi}
is a measure, this equality can be extended to all functions
u\in BD_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
such that

. This implies that in this case the function
\xi\mapsto\sigma^{\xi}_{u}(B)
is quadratic in the sense of Definition
2.1
. In particular, if
d=1
this happens for every
u\in GBD(\Omega)
thanks to Remark
2.7
.

Remark 4.2
.

Let
\lambda^{\xi}_{u}
be the measure introduced in (

). One can see that for every
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, we have the equality
|\sigma^{\xi}_{u}|=\lambda^{\xi}_{u}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}(\Omega\setminus J^{1}_{u})
as Borel measures on

. In light of
[

, this is an easy consequence of (

) and of the fact that, by Theorem

, for

For

\displaystyle(\sigma^{\zeta}_{u})^{a}(B)=|\zeta|\int_{\Pi^{\zeta}}D^{a}u^{\zeta}_{y}(B^{\zeta}_{y})\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y),

(8.13)

\displaystyle(\sigma^{\zeta}_{u})^{s}(B)=|\zeta|\int_{\Pi^{\zeta}}D^{s}u^{\zeta}_{y}((B\setminus J^{1}_{u})^{\zeta}_{y})\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y),

\sigma_{u}^{\zeta}

\mathcal{L}^{d}
. In light of Theorem

. Combining this equality with (
8.10

\mu^{a}_{u}(B)\xi\cdot\eta=\int_{B}\mathcal{E}u\,\xi\cdot\eta\,{\rm d}x=\big(\int_{B}\mathcal{E}u\,{\rm d}x\big)\xi\cdot\eta

for every
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
,
which proves (
8.7
).

To prove (
8.8
), we observe that by Proposition
4.5
for every
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
we have

\sigma^{\zeta}_{u}(B\cap J_{u})z=\int_{(J_{u}\setminus J^{1}_{u})\cap B}([u]\odot\nu_{u})\zeta\cdot\zeta\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}.

Together with (
8.11

\mu^{j}_{u}(B)\xi\cdot\eta=\int_{(J_{u}\setminus J^{1}_{u})\cap B}([u]\odot\nu_{u})\xi\cdot\eta\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}=\big(\int_{(J_{u}\setminus J^{1}_{u})\cap B}([u]\odot\nu_{u})\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\big)\xi\cdot\eta

for every
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
. This proves (
8.8
).

To conclude, we fix
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
and a Borel set
B\subset\Omega
and show that (
8.9
) holds. We observe that for every

such that (
2.21
) holds we have
|D^{j}u^{\xi}_{y}|((B\setminus J_{u})^{\xi}_{y})=0
, hence

Since (
2.21

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
-a.e.

, from this equality, (
8.4

8.13

it follows that

(8.14)

\mu_{u}^{c}(B)\xi\cdot\xi=(\sigma^{\xi}_{u})^{s}(B\setminus J_{u})=|\xi|\int_{\Pi^{\xi}}D^{c}u^{\xi}_{y}((B\setminus J_{u})^{\xi}_{y})\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y).

Recalling Lemma
2.4
, by Remark
2.10
for
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
-a.e.
y\in\Pi^{\xi}
the set

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
-a.e.

∎

Corollary 8.6

\displaystyle\mu^{j}_{u,r}(B)=\int_{(J_{u}\setminus J^{r}_{u})\cap B}[u]\odot\nu_{u}\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\quad\text{ for every Borel set $B\subset\Omega$.}

Proof.

Let

Appendix A
Auxiliary results

The purpose of this section is to show that the functions
g^{k,\zeta}_{m}
and
h^{k,\zeta}_{m,\varepsilon}
defined in (

) and (
6.49
) are Borel measurable, and prove some general properties of measures defined by integration.

For this reason, we give here a precise statement and a complete proof of the results we need.

A.1.
Lebesgue decomposition of measures defined by integration

In this subsection we consider measures defined on the slices of a set, depending on a parameter
\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, and the measures that can be obtained by integrating with respect to the parameters corresponding to the slices. We are interested in a formula for the Lebesgue decomposition of these measures.

We begin by a lemma concerning measurability conditions with respect to these parameters. Given
h,k\in\mathbb{N}
, a Borel set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{h}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
, and
\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
we set

(A.1)

B(\omega):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{h}:(x,\omega)\in B\}.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
. For every
y\in\Pi^{\zeta}
and
\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
let
\mu_{y}^{\omega}\in\mathcal{M}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
be a signed measure. The following three measurability conditions are equivalent:

(a)

for every
\psi\in C^{0}_{c}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{k})

\text{ the function }(y,\omega)\mapsto\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\psi(t,\omega)\,{\rm d}\mu_{y}^{\omega}(t)\text{ is Borel measurable on $\Pi^{\zeta}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}$;}

(b)

for every
\varphi\in C^{0}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{k})

\text{ the function }(y,\omega)\mapsto\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\varphi(y+t\zeta,\omega)\,{\rm d}\mu_{y}^{\omega}(t)\text{ is Borel measurable on $\Pi^{\zeta}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}$; }

(c)

for every Borel set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
the function

(y,\omega)\mapsto\mu^{\omega}_{y}(B(\omega)^{\zeta}_{y})

is Borel measurable on
\Pi^{\zeta}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
.

Moreover, if the previous conditions are satisfied, then for every Borel set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}

the function
(y,\omega)\mapsto|\mu^{\omega}_{y}|(B(\omega)^{\zeta}_{y})

is Borel measurable on
\Pi^{\zeta}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
.

Proof.

For simplicity of notation we consider only the case
\zeta=e_{d}
, the
d
-th vector of the canonical basis, the proof in the other cases being analogous. For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we set
x=(x^{\prime},x_{d})
, where
x^{\prime}:=(x_{1},...,x_{d-1})
, and we identify
\Pi^{e_{d}}=\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\{0\}
with
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
. Therefore the measure
\mu^{\omega}_{(x^{\prime},0)}
is denoted simply by
\mu^{\omega}_{x^{\prime}}
and for the slicing of sets we use the notation
B_{x^{\prime}}
instead of
B^{e_{d}}_{(x^{\prime},0)}
.

Assume (a). For every pair of functions
w\in C^{0}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{k})
and
\psi\in C^{0}_{c}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{k})
it follows from (a) that the function

(x^{\prime},\omega)\mapsto\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}w(x^{\prime},\omega)\psi(x_{d},\omega)\,{\rm d}\mu_{x^{\prime}}^{\omega}(x_{d})\

is Borel measurable on
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
.

By an argument based on partition of unities corresponding to coverings by open sets with small diameter we can prove that the class of linear combinations of functions
\varphi\in C^{0}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{k})
of the form
\varphi(x,\omega)=w(x^{\prime},\omega)\psi(x_{d},\omega)
is dense in
C^{0}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{k})
with respect to the uniform convergence. This implies that
the function

(x^{\prime},\omega)\mapsto\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\varphi((x^{\prime},x_{d}),\omega)\,{\rm d}\mu_{x^{\prime}}^{\omega}(x_{d})\

is Borel measurable on
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
for every
\varphi\in C^{0}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{k})
, which is condition (b) in the case
\zeta=e_{d}
.

Assume (b) and consider the class
\mathcal{F}
of bounded Borel functions
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle(x^{\prime},\omega)\mapsto\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}f((x^{\prime},x_{d}),\omega)\,{\rm d}\mu_{x^{\prime}}^{\omega}(x_{d})\text{ is Borel measurable on $\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}$}.

It is easy to check that
\mathcal{F}
is a monotone class, that is,

(i)

if
(f_{n})_{n}\subset\mathcal{F}
, with
f_{n}\leq g
, for some
g\in\mathcal{F}
, and
f_{n}\nearrow f
, then
f\in\mathcal{F}
;

(ii)

if
(f_{n})_{n}\subset\mathcal{F}
, with
f_{n}\geq g
, for some
g\in\mathcal{F}
, and
f_{n}\searrow f
, then
f\in\mathcal{F}
.

Moreover, thanks to (b), we have that
C^{0}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{k})\subset\mathcal{F}
. Hence, from the Monotone Class Theorem (see
[
42
, Section 3.14]
)
we deduce that for every bounded Borel function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
the function

(x^{\prime},\omega)\mapsto\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}f((x^{\prime},x_{d}),\omega)\,{\rm d}\mu_{x^{\prime}}^{\omega}(x_{d})

is Borel measurable on
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
. By taking as
f
the characteristic function of a Borel set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
we obtain that the function

(x^{\prime},\omega)\mapsto\mu_{x^{\prime}}^{\omega}(B(\omega)_{x^{\prime}})

is Borel measurable on
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
, which is condition (c) in the case
\zeta=e_{d}
.

Assume now (c). Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a Borel set and let
B:=\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Clearly,
B(\omega)_{x^{\prime}}=E(\omega)
for every
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
, so that by (c) the function
(x^{\prime},\omega)\mapsto\mu^{\omega}_{x^{\prime}}(E(\omega))
is
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
measurable. Hence,

(x^{\prime},\omega)\mapsto\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\chi_{E}(x_{d},\omega)\,{\rm d}\mu^{\omega}_{x^{\prime}}(x_{d})

is Borel measurable on
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
.
By linearity we obtain that

(x^{\prime},\omega)\mapsto\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}g(x_{d},\omega)\,{\rm d}\mu^{\omega}_{x^{\prime}}(x_{d})

is Borel measurable on
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
for every simple function
g\colon\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
.
Since every function
\psi\in C^{0}_{c}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{k})
can be approximated by a uniformly bounded sequence of simple functions, an application of the Dominated Convergence Theorem yields (a).

We now show that, if (a)-(c) hold, then the last part of the statement holds. By the equivalence of (a)-(c) for
|\mu^{\omega}_{x^{\prime}}|
, to conclude the proof it is enough to show that for every function
\psi\in C^{0}_{c}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{k})
the function

(A.2)

(x^{\prime},\omega)\mapsto\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\psi(x_{d},\omega)\,{\rm d}|\mu_{x^{\prime}}^{\omega}|(x_{d})

is Borel measurable on
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Assume for a moment that
\psi\geq 0
. By definition of total variation of a measure we have

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\psi(x_{d},\omega)\,{\rm d}|\mu_{x^{\prime}}^{\omega}|(x_{d})=\underset{|\varphi|\leq\psi}{\sup_{\varphi\in C^{0}_{c}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{k})}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\varphi(x_{d},\omega)\,{\rm d}\mu_{x^{\prime}}^{\omega}(x_{d}).

Since the supremum above can be reduced to a countable dense subset of
C^{0}_{c}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{k})
, this equality, together with (a) for
\mu^{\omega}_{x^{\prime}}
, implies that the function in (
A.2
) is Borel measurable on
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
when
\psi\geq 0
. In the general case, one can split
\psi
into its positive and negative part.
∎

Let
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
and for every
y\in\Pi^{\zeta}
let
\mu_{y}\in\mathcal{M}_{b}(\Omega^{\zeta}_{y})
. For every
y\in\Pi^{\zeta}
let
\mu^{a}_{y}
and
\mu^{s}_{y}
be the absolutely continuous part and the singular part of
\mu_{y}

with respect to the one dimensional Lebesgue measure. In the following lemma we consider some general conditions on
(\mu_{y})_{y\in\Pi^{\zeta}}
which allow us to define a measure
\mu
on
\Omega
by integrating
\mu_{y}
with respect to
y
. We then show that the absolutely continuous part
\mu^{a}
and its singular part
\mu^{s}
of
\mu
with respect to
\mathcal{L}^{d}
can be obtained by integrating
\mu^{a}_{y}
and
\mu^{s}_{y}
with respect to
y
.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
and for every
y\in\Pi^{\zeta}
let
\mu_{y}\in\mathcal{M}_{b}(\Omega^{\zeta}_{y})

Assume that for every Borel set
B\subset\Omega

(A.3)

the function
y\mapsto\mu_{y}(B^{\zeta}_{y})
is Borel measurable on
\Pi^{\zeta}

and that there exists
g\in L^{1}(\Pi^{\zeta},\mathcal{H}^{d-1})
such that

(A.4)

|\mu_{y}|(\Omega^{\zeta}_{y})\leq g(y)
for
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
-a.e.
y\in\Pi^{\zeta}
.

Consider the measure defined for every Borel set
B\subset\Omega
by

(A.5)

\mu(B):=\int_{\Pi^{\zeta}}\mu_{y}(B^{\zeta}_{y})\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y).

Le
\mu^{a}
and
\mu^{s}
be the absolutely continuous part and the singular part of
\mu
with respect to the Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}^{d}
. Then for every Borel set
B\subset\Omega
the functions

(A.6)

y\mapsto\mu^{a}_{y}(B^{\zeta}_{y})
and
y\mapsto\mu^{s}_{y}(B^{\zeta}_{y})
are Borel measurable and

-integrable on
\Pi^{\zeta}

and we have

(A.7)

\mu^{a}(B)=\int_{\Pi^{\zeta}}\mu^{a}_{y}(B^{\zeta}_{y})\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)\quad\text{ and }\quad\mu^{s}(B)=\int_{\Pi^{\zeta}}\mu^{s}_{y}(B^{\zeta}_{y})\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)

for every Borel set
B\subset\Omega
.

Proof.

As in the previous lemma we consider only the case
\zeta=e_{d}
and use the notation of the proof of the previous lemma. We also drop the hypothesis that
\Omega
is bounded and assume that
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, as the result for a general

then easily follows.

We now prove (
A.6
). To this aim let

(A.8)

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046ptS:=\Big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:x=(x^{\prime},x_{d})\,\text{ and }\,\limsup_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\frac{|\mu_{x^{\prime}}|((x_{d}-\rho,x_{d}+\rho))}{2\rho}=+\infty\Big\}

and, for every
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
, let
S_{x^{\prime}}
be the corresponding slice.
By the Besicovitch Derivation Theorem (see
[
7
, Theorem 2.22]
) for every
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
we have

(A.9)

\mu^{s}_{x^{\prime}}(B_{x^{\prime}})=\mu_{x^{\prime}}(B_{x^{\prime}}\cap S_{x^{\prime}})\quad\text{and }\quad\mu^{a}_{x^{\prime}}(B_{x^{\prime}})=\mu_{x^{\prime}}(B_{x^{\prime}}\setminus S_{x^{\prime}})

for every Borel set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Therefore, by (
A.3
) to prove (
A.6
) it is enough to show that the set
S
is Borel measurable. To this end, we note that, as the function
\rho\mapsto|\mu_{x^{\prime}}|((x_{d}-\rho,x_{d}+\rho))
is left-continuous, in the
\limsup
in (
A.8
) we can reduce to considering
\rho
varying in a countable dense set. Hence, to conclude that
S
is Borel measurable we only need to prove that for every
\rho>0
the function

(A.10)

(x^{\prime},x_{d})\mapsto|\mu_{x^{\prime}}|((x_{d}-\rho,x_{d}+\rho))=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi_{(-\rho,\rho)}(x_{d}-t)\,{\rm d}|\mu_{x^{\prime}}|(t)

is Borel measurable on
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}
, where
\chi_{(-\rho,\rho)}
denotes the characteristic function of
(-\rho,\rho).
Let
(\psi_{n})_{n}\subset C^{0}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
be a sequence of functions with
\psi_{n}\leq\psi_{n+1}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and converging pointwise to
\chi_{(-\rho,\rho)}
as
n\to+\infty
. By the Monotone Convergence Theorem for every
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
we have

(A.11)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi_{(-\rho,\rho)}(x_{d}-t)\,{\rm d}|\mu_{x^{\prime}}|(t)=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{n}(x_{d}-t)\,{\rm d}|\mu_{x^{\prime}}|(t).

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
the function

(x^{\prime},x_{d})\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{n}(x_{d}-t)\,{\rm d}|\mu_{x^{\prime}}|(t)

is Borel measurable in
x^{\prime}
for
x_{d}
fixed, thanks to (
A.3
) and Lemma
A.1
, and continuous in
x_{d}
for
x^{\prime}
fixed. Thus, it is Borel measurable in the product space
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}
. Thanks to (
A.11
), this implies that the function (
A.10
) is Borel measurable, which proves that the set
S
is Borel measurable and concludes the proof of the measurability property in (
A.6
). The integrability follows from (
A.4
).

Thanks to (
A.6
) we can define two bounded Radon measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by setting

\displaystyle\nu_{1}(B):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\mu^{a}_{x^{\prime}}(B_{x^{\prime}})\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x^{\prime})\quad\text{ and }\quad\nu_{2}(B):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\mu^{s}_{x^{\prime}}(B_{x^{\prime}})\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x^{\prime})

for every Borel set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. It follows from the Fubini Theorem that
\nu_{1}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mathcal{L}^{d}
and that
\mathcal{L}^{d}(S)=0
. By (
A.9
) we also deduce that
\mu^{s}_{x^{\prime}}(B_{x^{\prime}})=\mu^{s}_{x^{\prime}}(B_{x^{\prime}}\cap S_{x^{\prime}})
, hence
\nu_{2}(B)=\nu_{2}(B\cap S)

for every Borel set
B\subset\Omega
. This shows that
\nu_{2}
is singular with respect to the Lebesgue measure. Since
\mu=\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}
, the equalities in (
A.7
) follow from the uniqueness of the Lebesgue decomposition.
∎

As in the proof of Theorem

,
\Omega

,

GBD(\Omega)

We also recall that

We introduce some sets which will play a crucial role in our arguments. For every

The following lemma addresses the Borel measurability of these sets. The proof is very similar to that of Lemma

Lemma A.3
.

The sets
{\mathfrak{E}}^{k,\zeta}
,
\hat{\mathfrak{E}}^{k,\zeta}_{m}
,
\check{\mathfrak{E}}^{k,\zeta}_{m}
, and
\mathfrak{J}
are Borel measurable subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{4}
.

Proof.

The property for
\mathfrak{J}
is trivial. To prove the result for the other sets we
consider the map
z^{k,\zeta}\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
defined by (

3

\omega
is made clear by (
5.3

For every set

we define

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{E}}^{k,\zeta}_{B}:=\{(x,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}:(z^{k,\zeta}(x,\omega)+\tfrac{1}{k}S)\cap B\neq\mathchar 31\relax\},

\displaystyle F_{B}:=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:(z+\tfrac{1}{k}S)\cap B\neq\mathchar 31\relax\},

where
S
is defined by (
6.17
).
For a compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
the set

is closed, so that, observing that
\mathfrak{E}_{K}^{k,\zeta}=\{(x,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}:z^{k,\zeta}(x,\omega)\in F_{K}\}
and recalling that
z^{k,\zeta}
is Borel measurable, we conclude that
\mathfrak{E}^{k,\zeta}_{K}
is Borel measurable.
By definition,
{\mathfrak{E}}^{k,\zeta}=\mathfrak{E}^{k,\zeta}_{J}
. Since
J=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}K_{n}
with
K_{n}
compact, this gives that
\mathfrak{E}^{k,\zeta}=\bigcup_{n\in N}\mathfrak{E}^{k,\zeta}_{K_{n}}
. Since the sets
\mathfrak{E}^{k,\zeta}_{K_{n}}
are Borel measurable, so is
\mathfrak{E}^{k,\zeta}
.

To prove that
\hat{\mathfrak{E}}^{k,\zeta}_{m}
is Borel measurable, we observe that by (
6.22
) a pair
(x,\omega)
belongs to
\hat{\mathfrak{E}}^{k,\zeta}_{m}

\mathfrak{E}_{i}^{k,\zeta}:=\{(x,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}:x\in E^{k,\zeta,\omega}-\tfrac{i}{k}\zeta\}

\textstyle\hat{\mathfrak{E}}^{k,\zeta}_{m}=\big\{(x,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}:\sum_{i}\chi_{{\mathfrak{E}}^{k,\zeta}_{i}}(x,\omega)\leq m\big\},

\check{\mathfrak{E}}^{k,\zeta}_{m}

We are now ready to state and prove the final result of this subsection.

Lemma A.4
.

Let
k,m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\zeta\in\{\xi,\eta,\xi+\eta,\xi-\eta\}
, and let
N_{\zeta}\subset\Pi^{\zeta}
be a Borel set such that
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(N_{\zeta})=0
and
u^{\zeta}_{y}\in BV(U^{\zeta}_{y})

defined by

(A.13)

\displaystyle g^{k,\zeta}_{m}(y,\omega):=\begin{cases}|Du^{\zeta}_{y}|(\hat{E}^{k,\zeta,\omega}_{m}(y)\cap U^{\zeta}_{y}\setminus J^{\zeta}_{y})&\text{ if $y\in\Pi^{\zeta}\setminus N_{\zeta}$},\\
0&\text{ if $y\in\ N_{\zeta}$},\end{cases}

(A.14)

\displaystyle h^{k,\zeta}_{m}(y,\omega):=\begin{cases}|Du^{\zeta}_{y}|(\check{E}^{k,\zeta,\omega}_{m}(y)\cap U^{\zeta}_{y}\setminus J^{\zeta}_{y})&\text{ if $y\in\Pi^{\zeta}\setminus N_{\zeta}$},\\
0&\text{ if $y\in N_{\zeta}$},\end{cases}

are Borel measurable on
\Pi^{\zeta}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Proof.

We begin by observing that by (
A.1
) and (
A.12
) for every
y\in\Pi^{\zeta}
and
\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
we have

(A.15)

\displaystyle\hat{E}^{k,\zeta,\omega}_{m}(y)\cap U^{\zeta}_{y}\setminus J^{\zeta}_{y}=\hat{\mathfrak{E}}(\omega)^{\zeta}_{y}\cap U^{\zeta}_{y}\setminus\mathfrak{J}(\omega)^{\zeta}_{y},

(A.16)

\displaystyle\check{E}^{k,\zeta,\omega}_{m}(y)\cap U^{\zeta}_{y}\setminus J^{\zeta}_{y}=\check{\mathfrak{E}}(\omega)^{\zeta}_{y}\cap U^{\zeta}_{y}\setminus\mathfrak{J}(\omega)^{\zeta}_{y}.

By Lemma
A.3
, the sets
\hat{\mathfrak{E}}^{k,\zeta}_{m}
,
\check{\mathfrak{E}}^{k,\zeta}_{m}
, and
\mathfrak{J}
are Borel measurable subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
, so that the same property holds for
\hat{\mathfrak{E}}^{k,\zeta}_{m}\setminus\mathfrak{J}
and
\check{\mathfrak{E}}^{k,\zeta}_{m}\setminus\mathfrak{J}
.

We want to apply Lemma
A.1
with the measure
\mu^{\omega}_{y}\in\mathcal{M}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
defined for every Borel set
B\subset\mathbb{R}
by

(A.17)

\mu^{\omega}_{y}(B):=\begin{cases}Du^{\zeta}_{y}(B\cap U^{\zeta}_{y})\quad&\text{ if $y\in\Pi^{\zeta}\setminus N_{\zeta}$},\\
0&\text{ $y\in N_{\zeta}$}.\end{cases}

To show that condition (b) of Lemma
A.1
holds we fix a function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
and observe that

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\varphi(y+t\zeta,\omega){\,\rm d}\mu^{\omega}_{y}(t)=\int_{U^{\zeta}_{y}}\varphi(y+t\zeta,\omega){\,\rm d}Du^{\zeta}_{y}(t)

for every

.
Let us consider a bounded sequence
(\rho_{n})\subset C^{\infty}_{c}(U)
converging to
1
on
\Omega
as
n\to+\infty
and set
\varphi_{n}:=\rho_{n}\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(U\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
. For every

by the Dominated Convergence Theorem we have

(A.18)

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\varphi(y+t\zeta,\omega){\,\rm d}\mu_{y}^{\omega}(t)=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{U^{\zeta}_{y}}\varphi_{n}(y+t\zeta,\omega){\,\rm d}Du^{\zeta}_{y}(t).

Moreover, integrating by parts, we have

(A.19)

\int_{U^{\zeta}_{y}}\varphi_{n}(y+t\zeta,\omega)\,{\rm d}Du^{\zeta}_{y}(t)=-\int_{U^{\zeta}_{y}}u^{\zeta}_{y}(t)\nabla_{x}\varphi_{n}(y+t\zeta,\omega)\cdot\zeta\,{\rm d}t,

(A.20)

(y,\omega)\mapsto\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\varphi(y+t\zeta,\omega){\,\rm d}\mu_{y}^{\omega}(t).

Since by definition
\mu^{\omega}_{y}(B)=0

A.20
) is Borel measurable on
\Pi^{\zeta}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
As every function in
C^{0}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
can be approximated uniformly by a sequence of functions in

, the function (
A.20

By Lemma

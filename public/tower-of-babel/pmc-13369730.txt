PHDReader: police handwritten document recognition method based on VLM with EI-LFT using FP-EESR and MFE-GLS
Authors: Dongxing Wang, Zhiyang Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Police handwritten document recognition plays an increasingly important role in improving new quality combat capability of public security organs. To address the challenges associated with the recognition, this paper proposes a police handwritten document recognition method based on Qwen3VL with EI-LFT using FP-EESR and MFE-GLS. Firstly, Fusion Preprocessing with Edge Enhancement for Super-Resolution Reconstruction(FP-EESR) was used to enhance the handwriting features of handwritten Chinese characters while Multi-scale Feature Extraction using Global-Local-Semantic (MFE-GLS) was employed to extract “global-local-semantic” fusion features to improve recognition performance, as well as Embedding Instruction & LoRA Fine-Tuning (EI-LFT) was utilized to fine tune Qwen3VL to adapt to specific scenarios of police handwritten document recognition. Secondly, to test the recognition and extraction performance of various methods, a dataset was specifically constructed for the recognition and extraction of handwritten police documents. Finally, Multiple comparative experiments were conducted, and theoretical research and experimental results show that the new proposed PHDReader outperforms advanced OCR models and VLM in terms of recognition performance, stability and generalization ability.

Introduction

Recognizing police handwritten documents can efficiently transform numerous handwritten records into searchable and analyzable electronic data, thereby enhancing case processing efficiency and intelligence utilization. Nevertheless, police handwritten documents commonly exhibit messy handwriting, stroke overlap, and a high density of specialized police jargon, posing challenges for digitization, standardization, and effective information extraction. Consequently, developing reliable techniques to accurately recognize and extract data from handwritten police documents is essential for leveraging document information and bolstering the operational effectiveness of law enforcement agencies.

In recent years, extensive research has been conducted on the recognition and extraction of handwritten fonts, such as in the use of OCR technology, researchers proposed a decoder-only optical character recognition (OCR) transformer for document recognition in1; In2, Li Ziying et al. employed a Swin Transformer–based encoder and an overlapping segmentation technique to substantially improve the accuracy of handwritten Chinese character recognition; In3, researchers combined deep modules with OCR modules to mitigate text distortion inherent in document image unwarping, consequently enhancing OCR accuracy; In4, Zhang Yuyi et al. constructed a large-scale dataset, MegaHan97K, filling the gap in datasets for recognition of large-category Chinese characters; In5, Muhammad et al. presented a novel feature-extraction technique for OCR that recognizes multilingual characters accurately; In6, Wang Shuyi et al. adopted a structured document digitization approach to handle the complex layouts common in construction project documents. However, there is relatively little research on the specific application scenarios of handwritten police documents, especially in the areas of identifying police specific terms in advance, cursive handwriting, and significant differences in personal handwritten fonts, which have significant technical shortcomings.

In the use of deep-learning technology, researchers combined a CNN-based text recognizer with a deep-learning layout analysis method in7; In8, authors proposed a training paradigm that merges synthetically generated data with character-level annotations and real data with line-level annotations, improving text recognition and localization accuracy; In9, Chen Yi et al. used a convolutional prototype network as the classifier to attain high-accuracy classification of handwritten Chinese characters; In10, authors adopted a position-guided mechanism into the Transformer autoregressive process to address the problem of text duplication recognition in complex document layouts; In11, Li Min employed a residual neural network to achieve high recognition rates for handwritten Chinese characters on GUI interfaces; In12, Zhang Hongxia et al. developed an encoder-decoder scene text recognition model to improve recognition of irregular text. Deep-learning models require large annotated datasets, but police handwritten documents often lack sufficient samples because of confidentiality constraints, which limits model generalization and makes it difficult to work facing complex handwritten fonts from different police combat scenarios.

In the field of visual–language model (VLM), researchers developed CalliBench to mitigate earlier OCR and VQA approaches’ tendencies toward contextual fragmentation, shallow reasoning and hallucination in13; In14, Authors introduced InternLM-XComposer2-4KHD, which supports resolutions from 336 pixels to 4 K and improves semantic coherence recognition in long documents; In15, Z. Gao proposed Mini-InternVL retains only 5% of the parameters while achieving 90% of the original model’s performance; In16, Y. Zhu et al. employed a hierarchical, multi-granularity image-text alignment framework to enhance zero-shot recognition of handwritten Chinese characters; In17, Li Y et al. proposed a single VLM application, dots_ocr, for processing complex documents; In18, authors mapped visual features to the weighted average of LLM text embeddings to execute document-understanding tasks efficiently. However, existing VLM have not been trained specifically for police handwriting and they are prone to hallucination and show limited ability to extract structured key information, so the standardization requirements of policing tasks can not be met.

Recently, the development of visual language models (VLM) and large-scale language models is booming, with VLM possessing powerful visual capabilities and large-scale language models having advanced logical reasoning, which inspired authors to integrate the advantages of both for recognition and extraction of handwritten police documents. Accordingly, this paper proposed a police handwritten document recognition method based on Qwen3VL with EI-LFT using FP-EESR and MFE-GLS(PHDReader), which is based on the Qwen3VL-8B-Instruct visual language model. The method employs Fusion Preprocessing with Edge Enhancement for Super-Resolution Reconstruction as well as Multi-scale Feature Extraction using Global-Local-Semantic, following the implementation of appropriate embedded instructions and LoRA fine-tuning; Furthermore, this study develops a dataset specifically for police handwritten documents; Evaluation results indicated that PHDReader outperformed other models by demonstrating its exceptional efficacy and suitability for the recognition of police handwritten documents.

Related work

Visual-language models

Visual-language models (VLM) represent a prominent area of study at the intersection of computer vision and natural language processing. They possess the capability to concurrently handle visual and linguistic data, aligning them within a unified semantic framework to facilitate cross-modal comprehension and inference. For instance, Uni-MuMER accomplishes comprehensive fine-tuning of VLM without altering the architecture, effectively integrating domain-specific knowledge into the universal framework and addressing the issue of varied writing styles19. In a similar vein, Liu Yu et al. pioneered the use of language-exclusive GPT-4 for generating multimodal language-image-instruction tracking data. They introduced LLaVA to augment the model’s refined visual reasoning skills, demonstrating the viability of cross-modal fusion in tasks like image description and visual question answering20. As Large Language Model continue to evolve, VLM built upon Large Language Models have gained prominence. MiniGPT-4, through a straightforward projection layer, achieves the amalgamation of sophisticated Large Language Models and visual attributes, propelling the advancement of VLM towards generative cross-modal reasoning and significantly enhancing the model’s natural language interaction capabilities21.

To tackle the issue of resource consumption in full-parameter fine-tuning, parametric efficient fine-tuning methods are commonly utilized in training Very Large Models. Llama-adapter introduces an attention mechanism with zero initialization and no gating constraints, enabling the adaptive integration of new guidance signals into LLaMA for effective fine-tuning with visual instructions and strong generalization capabilities22. Concurrently, the scope of VLM research has expanded from general natural scenes to more specific domains. GeoChat has devised a grounded visual language model tailored for remote sensing environments23, while Popeye has accomplished multi-source cross-modal fusion for remote sensing ship detection24. Lenna, serving as a language-enhanced reasoning detection aide, has demonstrated reduced training expenses and minimal communication overheads25; SPHINX has further pushed the boundaries of VLM capabilities in visual tasks like object detection and semantic segmentation26. Currently, the technological advancement of VLM exhibits traits of streamlined optimization and domain adaptation. Consequently, cross-modal large language models offer new research opportunities for tasks necessitating the integration of visual attributes and semantic comprehension, such as handwritten Chinese character recognition and document analysis.

Handwritten Chinese character recognition

Handwritten Chinese character extraction and document extraction are the core of handwritten Chinese character recognition. the goal is to precisely segment the effective document area from complex document images and further extract independent handwritten Chinese character targets. The accuracy of the extraction results directly affects the efficiency of subsequent recognition tasks. Early document extraction achieved the segmentation of document regions and backgrounds through projection methods and connected domain analysis. It could handle document images with simple backgrounds and regular layouts, but had poor adaptability to complex documents with blurring and distortion. In recent years, CamoFormer has proposed a solution based on a simple top-down decoder and using the proposed MSA to address the issue of precise document segmentation27; LayoutLM is a document understanding method based on LLM/MLLM, which realizes the joint extraction of document structure and text area, effectively improving the performance of document understanding28.

The core difficulty in extracting handwritten Chinese characters lies in the complexity of the strokes, the connection of strokes, and the blurring of the spacing between characters. The technological development has evolved from single-feature extraction to multi-feature fusion. Early CNN-based models extracted the strokes of Chinese characters through local convolution operations to achieve the segmentation of single-character targets, but it was difficult to capture the global structural information of Chinese characters. The Transformer model, by virtue of its self-attention mechanism, has achieved the fusion of global features and local details of handwritten Chinese characters, effectively solving the segmentation problem of cursive Chinese characters. Meanwhile, Chen W et al. effectively improved the accuracy of extracting connected Chinese characters by using brush-level Bezier curve fitting and Bayesian optimization29; X Qu et al. adopted the end-to-end attention convolutional recurrent network method for handwritten Chinese text recognition, integrating spatial and sequential features, which greatly improved the recognition accuracy30; With the continuous integration of large language models and visual information, the development of handwriting recognition technology has been further promoted. For example, GPT-4 V31 has a stronger ability to jointly understand text and images, and can complete various tasks such as handwriting recognition, continuous handwriting text recognition, and image-text analysis. In addition, Gemini32 model series demonstrates outstanding capabilities in image and text understanding, and also shows extremely high proficiency in cross-modal reasoning and language understanding. At present, the extraction technology of handwritten Chinese characters is still insufficient for document scenarios with low resolution, complex writing styles, and a mixture of multiple languages. Therefore, this paper constructs a method based on VLM with EI-LFT using FP-EESR and MFE-GLS to fill this deficiency.

Method

To achieve accurate recognition of police handwritten documents, this paper proposed a recognition method based on an improved Qwen3VL, its main contribution is about Fusion Preprocessing with Edge Enhancement for Super-Resolution Reconstruction (FP-EESR), Multi-scale Feature Extraction using Global-Local-Semantic (MFE-GLS) and Embedding Instruction & LoRA Fine-Tuning (EI-LFT), detailed in the following.

FP-EESR

One of the primary challenges in recognizing police handwritten documents is the presence of illegible handwriting and severely connected strokes. However, these factors contribute to blurred edges of Chinese characters and result in a loss of detail, which directly impacts the efficacy of subsequent feature extraction and recognition processes. To mitigate this issue, this paper utilizes a bicubic interpolation method combined with edge enhancement. By emphasizing the stroke outlines of the characters through edge enhancement and magnifying the details via bicentric interpolation, this approach effectively addresses the critical problem of blurred handwriting in police handwritten documents while ensuring compatibility with the subsequent feature extraction module.

MFE-GLS

Feature extraction plays a critical role in the recognition of police handwritten document, aiming to effectively extract multidimensional features that characterize the shapes of Chinese characters, the details of strokes, and the semantics of text from preprocessed high-quality images. Therefore, this article employs the Qwen3VL-8B-Instruct model as a recognition foundational framework to address the limitations of OCR models, which typically extract only visual features without semantic comprehension. Furthermore, this article presents a multidimensional feature extraction approach incorporating global, local, and semantic features. By performing multi-scale visual feature extraction on global and local images and integrating visual and semantic features across modalities, it considers both glyph specifics and semantic relationships, consequently enhancing recognition precision.

The utilization of the EasyOCR model in this study addresses the issue of local feature extraction deviation resulting from inconsistent line spacing and overlapping handwriting in images. The primary procedure involves initial text line bounding box detection on the preprocessed image, ensuring resilience via a dual detection mechanism, as shown in Fig. 2. Subsequently, by sorting the bounding box center along the horizontal and vertical axes, the text line sequence aligns with the original document. Afterwards, adding edge margins facilitates cropping of individual line text quality inspection images, thereby preserving stroke intricacies. Ultimately, the segmented outcomes are structured to facilitate subsequent feature alignment. This methodology is well-suited for intricate scenarios like illegible handwriting and modifications, effectively balancing precision and efficiency.

Subsequently, Qwen3VL’s integrated ViT visual encoder is used to concurrently extract both global and local visual features through a dual input mode comprising “complete image” and a “single line text image.” The complete image serves as the initial input to the model, followed by the sequential input of the segmented single line text image, with all images processed simultaneously. accordingly, the complete image is used as the recognition subject, and the single line text image is used for auxiliary extraction of more detailed features. In addition, due to the complete image as the recognition subject and the logical ability of the large language model, even if there are omissions or small sorting errors in the segmented single line text image, it does not affect the extraction of the entire text. This method can improve the recognition performance of messy handwriting and blurry scenes while ensuring a global perspective.

EI-LFT

To address the limited adaptability of the basic VLM model in recognizing handwritten police documents, this paper employs embedded optimized prompt words along with LoRA fine-tuning for collaborative optimization. This approach aims to enhance the accuracy of professional terminology recognition and improve the robustness against messy handwriting.

A fusion design that integrates image explanation with rigid instructions is employed, directly embedded within the model’s interactive messages. This approach guides the model to effectively perform image recognition, text proofreading, and error correction, while also considering the specific context of police handwritten documents. Firstly, image description section explicitly conveys the composition of the input image, enabling the model to differentiate between global and local features. Secondly, rigid instruction component emphasizes essential requirements, including recognition accuracy, proofreading logic, and format specifications, thereby facilitating precise model inference. Furthermore, the prompt text should incorporate guidance for recognizing professional terminology, assisting the model in accurately distinguishing compliant terms. Moreover, all VLM models utilized the same optimized prompt words in the experiment.

In addition, this article employs LoRA for fine-tuning to achieve efficient model optimization, effectively balancing training efficiency with image memory usage while accommodating limited datasets of handwritten police documents. Furthermore, a training set of “image-prompt-answer” is constructed by aligning image features with text descriptions to address scenarios with restricted datasets. Besides, PHDReader utilizes images processed through the methods in Sect. "FP-EESR" and "MFE-GLS" for fine-tuning. Throughout the training process, the backbone parameters of the base model remain frozen, allowing only the Lora low-rank matrix parameters to be updated. This approach significantly reduces computational demands and enhances training efficiency. A loss function is employed to balance the accuracy and fault tolerance of single character recognition, while also meeting the parameter update requirements for Lora fine-tuning, as illustrated in Formula 9. Overview Architecture of PHDReader

Fig. 4Overall model architecture of PHDReader.

Overall model architecture of PHDReader.

Developing a universal model for handwritten document recognition is crucial for improving the operational capabilities of public security agencies. However, handwritten document recognition differs significantly from character transcription in traditional document recognition. Challenges such as illegible handwriting and excessive stroke connections are inherent in handwritten documents. Therefore, the model must possess the visual and logical capabilities necessary to interpret incomprehensible characters in a manner akin to human understanding. Visual language models, characterized by the robust visual and logical competencies, represent the most appropriate choice for this task. And this will also be the trend in the development of document extraction models.

Consequently, this article proposes a unified vision-language model (VLM) named PHDReader, designed to address the fundamental challenges in recognizing handwritten documents in Police Operational Practice, including illegible handwriting, complex cursive strokes, and dense professional terminology. The specific methodology is illustrated in Fig. 4. First, a bicubic interpolation preprocessing technique with edge enhancement is employed to preserve handwriting details and enhance clarity. Second, text line segmentation is performed using EasyOCR, after which both the complete document image and the segmented image are input into the Qwen3VL model to extract global, local visual, and semantic features. The model’s character recognition performance is enhanced through multi-scale and cross-modal feature fusion. Finally, suitable prompt word instructions are integrated, and the model is fine-tuned using LoRA technology to optimize recognition performance for professional terminology and poorly written text.

Experiments

Implementation details

To evaluate the recognition performance of various models on police handwritten documents, this study constructs a dataset of simulated police handwritten documents. The dataset includes simulations of common police handwritten materials, such as interrogation records from a specific public security bureau, featuring typical police terminology and intentionally illegible handwriting, thereby reflecting authentic scenarios encountered in police documentation. Additionally, this dataset comprises a total of 1,009 document images, which contain approximately 430,000 Chinese characters or symbols, detailed in Data Availability Statement. Of these, 806 images are designated for the training set, while 203 are allocated to the test set. Each image has been meticulously labeled, and a JSON file has been created to encompass all character content within the documents. The accuracy of these labeling results is ensured to serve as the reference for both training and testing.

Furthermore, to quantify the recognition outcomes, extraneous details like spaces and line breaks will be eliminated from the model’s identified results on each image page. Only text and symbols will be preserved for computing diverse metrics. This suggests that the cross-modal fusion capabilities of VLM can significantly improve the recognition performance of police handwritten documents, addressing the challenges of inadequate semantic understanding and low accuracy in recognizing illegible handwriting encountered in traditional OCR models.

In summary, OCR models demonstrate inadequate recognition performance due to the limited cross-modal semantic comprehension, which renders them unsuitable for the complexities of handwritten documents. While VLM models exhibit some cross-modal fusion capabilities, the recognition accuracy in contexts involving specialized terminology and illegible handwriting necessitates improvement. In contrast, PHDReader incorporates FP-EESR and MFE-GLS components along with Qwen3VL fine-tuned by LoRA, effectively leverages the cross-modal fusion strengths of VLM. Consequently, PHDReader performs excellently in various evaluation indicators of document recognition, establishing itself as a high-caliber model that effectively resolves the core challenges in police handwritten document recognition and meets the practical application demands of law enforcement.

Conclusion

This article proposes a police handwritten document recognition method–PHDReader based on VLM to address challenges such as illegible handwriting and continuous strokes in police handwritten document recognition. PHDReader employs Fusion Preprocessing with Edge Enhancement for Super Reconstruction and Multi-scale Feature Extraction utilizing Global-Local-Semantic techniques, following the implementation of appropriate embedded instructions and LoRA fine-tuning. Furthermore, this study constructs a dataset specifically for police handwritten documents to facilitate recognition. PHDReader demonstrates superior recognition performance, along with better stability and generalization capabilities compared to other models evaluated on the test set. Consequently, PHDReader is an excellent model that effectively addresses the fundamental challenges in police handwritten document recognition and fulfills the practical application requirements of police handwritten documents recognition.

However, gold has flaws, white jade has flaws, and PHDReader is no exception. Compared to Qwen3VL, PHDReader takes slightly longer for document image recognition because it generates more tokens due to the large number of images passed in at once.

In future work, our team will consider building a token compression method and adopting a global character image recognition method to further improve recognition accuracy and shorten runtime.

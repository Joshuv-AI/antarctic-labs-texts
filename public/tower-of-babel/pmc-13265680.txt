Association Between Glyco-Lipid Seven-Factor Index, a Novel Composite Glyco-Lipid Index, and Periodontitis: A Cross-Sectional Study
Authors: Haoyu Su, Kun Zhang, Huijuan Zhang, Yiming Li, Jianfang Zhu, Yihan Chen, Rui Yu, Wei Li, Bang Li, Yong Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe glyco-lipid seven-factor index (GLM7) is a recently developed composite indicator comprising seven conventional biomarkers related to glycosphingolipid metabolism. Accumulating evidence suggests that GLM7 demonstrates favourable diagnostic performance in cardiovascular disease, diabetes mellitus, liver disease, malignancies, and multimorbidity. However, its potential relevance to oral health-related conditions has not yet been investigated.Materials and methodsThis cross-sectional study analysed data from 4008 participants enrolled in the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) 2009 to 2014 cycles. GLM7 was defined as the exposure variable, while periodontitis served as the outcome of interest. Multivariable logistic regression models and restricted cubic spline analyses were employed to examine the association between GLM7 and periodontitis. In addition, subgroup and interaction analyses were conducted to assess the robustness of the findings.ResultsAfter full adjustment for confounding variables, GLM7 was positively associated with periodontitis (OR = 1.19, 95% CI: 1.04-1.37). Subgroup analyses suggested that the association was more evident in individuals without hypertension, while restricted cubic spline analysis revealed an approximately linear relationship between GLM7 and periodontitis.ConclusionAn elevated GLM7 level was associated with a higher prevalence of periodontitis. As a composite index based on routinely measured glyco-lipid metabolic markers, GLM7 may have potential utility in identifying individuals with a higher likelihood of periodontitis, which broadens the scope of application of this emerging metabolic indicator.Key words: Periodontitis, GLM7, Associated factors, NHANES, Oral health

Introduction

Periodontitis is a chronic inflammatory disease initiated by dental plaque biofilms.1 Rather than representing a simple gingival inflammatory condition, it constitutes a persistent and destructive immuno-inflammatory process operating at the molecular and cellular levels.2,3 Severe periodontitis affects nearly 20% of the global population and remains the leading cause of tooth loss among adults.4 Beyond localized alveolar bone resorption, the pathological processes of periodontitis have been shown to exhibit bidirectional associations with multiple systemic diseases, including diabetes mellitus, cardiovascular disease, and Alzheimer’s disease.5, 6, 7

Disruption of glyco-lipid metabolism represents a fundamental pathological basis for the development of chronic low-grade inflammation. Hyperglycaemia and dyslipidaemia – such as elevated triglycerides and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol – can promote oxidative stress and activate innate immune signalling pathways, including the Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4)/nuclear factor κB (NF-κB) axis, thereby driving the release of proinflammatory cytokines such as tumour necrosis factor-α and interleukin-6.8,9 Conversely, persistent low-grade inflammation further exacerbates insulin resistance and lipid metabolic abnormalities, creating a self-perpetuating vicious cycle.10 Consequently, glyco-lipid metabolic indicators not only reflect metabolic health status but also serve as critical surrogates for systemic inflammatory burden and predictors of inflammation-related disease risk.

GLM7 is a novel composite glyco-lipid metabolic index derived from seven routinely measured parameters, including age, triglycerides (TG), fasting blood glucose (FBG), body mass index (BMI), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-c), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-c), and insulin.11 By integrating these variables through a logarithmic transformation, GLM7 captures multidimensional metabolic and physiological information into a single score with enhanced discriminative capacity. Previous studies have demonstrated that GLM7 exhibits favourable performance in the prediction and diagnosis of cardiovascular disease, diabetes mellitus, liver disease, malignancies, and various forms of multimorbidity, highlighting its potential as a risk stratification tool based on routine clinical data. Importantly, the metabolic profile encompassed by GLM7 – including age, glycaemic status, lipid metabolism, obesity, and insulin dynamics – has well-established pathophysiological links to periodontitis. For instance, an elevated triglyceride–glucose (TyG) index, which integrates TG and FBG as a surrogate marker of insulin resistance, has been consistently associated with an increased risk of periodontitis, suggesting that dysregulated glucose and lipid metabolism jointly drive periodontal inflammation.12,13 Insulin itself represents an independent contributor, as hyperinsulinemia can directly exacerbate periodontal tissue destruction by promoting proinflammatory polarization of immune cells such as T lymphocytes and macrophages, even in the absence of overt hyperglycemia.14 Within the lipid profile, reduced HDL-c and elevated LDL-c levels have been linked to tooth loss, and their associated lipid abnormalities may further deteriorate the periodontal microenvironment through enhanced oxidative stress – particularly via oxidized LDL formation – and endothelial dysfunction.15,16 Obesity, reflected by an increased BMI, serves as a central phenotype of metabolic dysregulation; pathological remodelling of adipose tissue induces chronic low-grade inflammation and dysregulated adipokine secretion, thereby systemically aggravating the progression of periodontitis.17 These metabolic abnormalities frequently converge in metabolic syndrome, the individual components of which – including central obesity, hyperglycaemia, and hypertension – have each been associated with an elevated risk of periodontitis, with systemic inflammation likely acting as a key mediating mechanism.18 Collectively, the components integrated within GLM7 reflect multiple converging pathways – namely insulin resistance, immune-inflammatory activation, lipid oxidative injury, and systemic metabolic dysregulation – that constitute a critical metabolic backdrop for the initiation and progression of periodontitis.

Although the newly developed GLM7, derived from routinely collected clinical health indicators, has been validated as a reliable biomarker associated with cardiovascular disease, diabetes, and liver disease, its relationship with periodontitis has not yet been investigated. Therefore, using data from the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES), the present study aimed to examine the association between GLM7 and periodontitis in a representative U.S. population.

Materials and methods

Study population

The National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) is a cross-sectional program designed to assess the health and nutritional status of the noninstitutionalized civilian population of the United States through a combination of household interviews, physical examinations, and laboratory assessments. NHANES employs a complex, stratified, multistage probability sampling design. All participants provided written informed consent, and the survey protocol was approved by the Research Ethics Review Board of the National Center for Health Statistics (NCHS).

This cross-sectional analysis utilized data from three NHANES survey cycles conducted between 2009 and 2014. Detailed information regarding the NHANES study design, sampling methodology, and data collection procedures is publicly available at the official NHANES website (https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/nhanes/).

Participants were included in the present study if they met the following criteria:1 completion of a standardized periodontal examination;2 availability of data required for the calculation of GLM7; and3 complete information on relevant covariates, including sex, race, educational attainment, marital status, household income, smoking status, alcohol consumption, physical activity, sleep duration, oral hygiene practices (eg, dental floss use), and hypertension status. Assessment of GLM7 levels

GLM7 was calculated using the following formula11:

All components were entered into the formula using their original measured units. A base-10 logarithmic transformation was applied to the composite index to address skewness and to facilitate its use in regression analyses. Fasting blood glucose, serum insulin, triglycerides, HDL-C, and LDL-C were measured using standardized laboratory methods according to NHANES protocols. Briefly, fasting glucose was determined using an enzymatic hexokinase method, insulin levels were assessed by immunoassay, and serum lipid parameters were measured using enzymatic methods on automated analysers. LDL-C was calculated using the Friedewald equation based on total cholesterol, triglycerides, and HDL-C values. Detailed laboratory procedures are available in the NHANES Laboratory Methods documentation.

Assessment of periodontitis status

Periodontal status was assessed at the medical examination. Case definition for periodontitis was established according to the 2012 CDC/AAP criteria.19,20 Specifically, severe periodontitis was defined as having ≥2 interproximal sites with clinical attachment loss (CAL) ≥6 mm (not on the same tooth) and ≥1 interproximal site with probing pocket depth (PPD) ≥5 mm. Moderate periodontitis was defined as ≥2 interproximal sites with CAL ≥4 mm or ≥2 interproximal sites with PPD ≥5 mm. Mild periodontitis was defined as ≥2 interproximal sites with CAL ≥3 mm and ≥2 interproximal sites with PPD ≥4 mm. For analytical purposes, participants were subsequently categorized into two distinct groups based on disease severity: the no/mild periodontitis group and the moderate/severe periodontitis group (see Supplementary Table 1 for details).

Covariates

Covariates were selected a priori based on a review of the relevant literature and established clinical practice guidelines. Sociodemographic characteristics included sex, and race. Educational attainment was categorized as less than high school, high school, and more than high school. Marital status was classified as married/living with a partner or widowed/divorced/other. Household income level was categorized according to the poverty income ratio (PIR) as <1.3, 1.3-3.5, and >3.5.

Lifestyle-related factors included smoking status, alcohol consumption, physical activity, dental floss use, and sleep duration. Smoking status was categorized as current smoker, former smoker, or never smoker. Alcohol consumption was dichotomized as drinkers or never drinkers. Physical activity was assessed using NHANES questionnaire data and quantified in metabolic equivalent of task (MET) minutes per week.21 MET values, defined as multiples of resting energy expenditure, were standardized according to World Health Organization criteria, with vigorous-intensity activities assigned 8 METs and moderate-intensity or transportation-related activities assigned 4 METs. Weekly MET minutes were calculated by multiplying activity frequency (days/week), duration (minutes/day), and corresponding MET values across work, transportation, and leisure domains, and then summing to obtain total physical activity. Overall physical activity levels were categorized as low (<600 MET-min/week), moderate (600–<3000 MET-min/week), and high (≥3000 MET-min/week). Dental floss use was classified as no floss/device use or floss/device use. Sleep duration was dichotomized as <7 h or ≥7 h per night.

Health status variables included hypertension. Hypertension status was determined based on self-reported physician diagnosis and standardized NHANES blood pressure measurements. Participants with missing questionnaire-based hypertension information and those lacking complete blood pressure measurements were excluded from the analysis. Systolic blood pressure (SBP) and diastolic blood pressure (DBP) were calculated as the average of up to four valid measurements obtained during the examination. Hypertension was defined as a self-reported diagnosis of hypertension or a measured SBP ≥140 mmHg and/or DBP ≥90 mmHg.

Statistical analysis

In accordance with the official NHANES analytic guidelines, sample weights were adjusted by dividing the original 2-year fasting subsample weights (WTSAF2YR) by the number of survey cycles when combining data from three cycles. Detailed information about the survey sample design and methods for calculating weights can be found in https://wwwn.cdc.gov/nchs/nhanes/analyticguidelines.aspx. Weight adjustment followed the minimum sample weight principle to preserve national representativeness and analytical robustness. All statistical analyses fully accounted for the complex, multistage survey design of NHANES, including baseline descriptive analyses, multivariable logistic regression, and subgroup analyses. Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics of the study population were presented as frequencies (percentages) for categorical variables, means standard deviations (SD) for normally distributed continuous variables, or medians with interquartile ranges (IQR) for variables with skewed distributions, as appropriate. The predictive performance of GLM7 for periodontitis was assessed using Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, with discriminatory ability quantified by the area under the curve (AUC). Multivariable logistic regression models were applied to evaluate the association between the exposure variable and the outcome of interest. Three progressively adjusted models were constructed: Model 1 was an unadjusted crude model; Model 2 was adjusted for sociodemographic variables; and Model 3 was further adjusted for all covariates. The results were reported as odds ratios (ORs) with corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs). For ordinal categorical variables, tests for linear trend were additionally performed by modelling the median value of each category as a continuous variable. All statistical tests were two-sided, and a P value <.05 was considered statistically significant. These analyses were designed to systematically assess the independent association between the exposure and the outcome under different levels of covariate adjustment. To evaluate the robustness of the findings, weighted multivariable logistic regression models were used to perform subgroup analyses. To further characterize the relationship between GLM7, hypertension, and periodontitis, an exploratory mediation analysis was performed. In addition, restricted cubic spline (RCS) regression, a commonly used approach for modelling potential nonlinear associations, was applied to examine the relationship between GLM7 and periodontitis. The statistical significance of interactions was assessed using likelihood ratio tests. All statistical analyses were conducted using R software (version 4.4.2).

Participants were categorized into two groups according to periodontitis severity: no or mild periodontitis and moderate to severe periodontitis. Participants with moderate to severe periodontitis exhibited higher mean GLM7 levels compared with those without or with mild periodontitis (7.46 ± 0.64 vs 7.29 ± 0.66). Significant differences were also observed between groups with respect to age, multiple metabolic indicators, sex, race, educational attainment, household income, smoking status, sleep duration, oral hygiene practices, and the prevalence of hypertension, suggesting that these factors may be associated with disease severity.

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analyses have previously been applied to assess the predictive performance of various indicators for periodontitis risk.22,23 We therefore evaluated the performance of GLM7, and the results indicated that GLM7 exhibited a modest yet statistically significant discriminatory capacity in unadjusted analyses (AUC > 0.5). Specifically, the AUC was 0.555 (95% CI: 0.523-0.588, P < .001), suggesting that the model was able to distinguish between outcomes better than chance alone (Supplementary Figure 1).

Association between GLM7 and periodontitis

The association between GLM7 and periodontitis is presented in Table 2. A statistically significant positive association was observed between GLM7 and periodontitis across all regression models. In the unadjusted Model 1, higher GLM7 levels were associated with an increased odds of periodontitis (OR = 1.52, 95% CI: 1.36-1.70). After adjustment for sociodemographic variables including sex, race, educational attainment, marital status, and household income in Model 2, the association remained statistically significant (OR = 1.35, 95% CI: 1.20-1.51). In the fully adjusted Model 3, which additionally accounted for all relevant covariates, GLM7 remained positively associated with periodontitis (OR = 1.19, 95% CI: 1.04-1.37). All three models demonstrated statistical significance. To further characterize this relationship, GLM7 was categorized into quartiles. In Models 1 and 2, higher quartiles of GLM7 were associated with an increased likelihood of periodontitis, with significant trends observed across quartiles (P for trend). In the fully adjusted Model 3, compared with participants in the lowest quartile (Q1), only those in the third quartile (Q3) exhibited a statistically significant increase in periodontitis risk (OR = 1.36, 95% CI: 1.08-1.71; P = .010), whereas the associations for the second (Q2) and highest quartiles (Q4) did not reach statistical significance (Q2: OR = 1.30, 95% CI: 1.00-1.69; P = .051; Q4: OR = 1.26, 95% CI: 0.98-1.62; P = .068). To further evaluate the clinical relevance of the index, established continuous periodontal parameters were analysed. As expected, participants with moderate/severe periodontitis exhibited significantly higher weighted mean clinical attachment level (CAL) (2.51 vs 1.19 mm, P < .001) and probing pocket depth (PPD) (2.07 vs 1.37 mm, P < .001) than those with no/mild periodontitis (Supplementary Table 2). Furthermore, Spearman correlation analysis revealed that GLM7 levels were significantly and positively correlated with both mean CAL (r = 0.130, P < .001) and mean PPD (r = 0.130, P < .001), indicating that higher systemic GLM7 levels are associated with increased local periodontal tissue destruction (Supplementary Figure 2).

Subgroup and interaction analyses

To assess the robustness of the association between GLM7 and periodontitis across different population characteristics, weighted subgroup and interaction analyses were performed. The positive association between GLM7 and periodontitis remained generally consistent across most subgroups. However, hypertension status significantly modified this association, with a more pronounced association observed among individuals without hypertension (Figure 2). These findings should be interpreted cautiously given the cross-sectional nature of the study.

Subgroup analyses by possible effect modifiers for the relationship between GLM7 and periodontitis.

Given the observed association between GLM7 and hypertension, an exploratory mediation analysis was conducted to evaluate whether hypertension might contribute to the association between GLM7 and periodontitis. The average causal mediation effect was small and marginally non-significant (P = .058), with the proportion mediated approaching zero, indicating no substantial indirect effect through hypertension (Supplementary Table 3 and Figure 3).This analysis was exploratory and not intended for causal inference.

Assessment of nonlinear associations

Restricted cubic spline regression and smoothed curve fitting were applied to examine the potential nonlinear association between GLM7 levels and periodontitis. As shown in Figure 3, no significant evidence of nonlinearity was observed (P for nonlinearity = 0.236), suggesting an approximately linear relationship. Consistently, GLM7 was significantly associated with periodontitis in multivariable-adjusted logistic regression models (OR = 1.153, 95% CI: 1.029-1.292; P = .014) (Supplementary Table 4).

Discussion

In this study, higher GLM7 level were found to be significantly associated with an increased risk of periodontitis. Binary logistic regression analyses demonstrated that GLM7 remained independently associated with periodontitis after adjustment for all potential confounders, and this association was generally consistent across most subgroups. As a composite index derived from routinely collected clinical and metabolic parameters related to glucose and lipid metabolism, GLM7 provides novel insights into the link between systemic metabolic status and periodontal disease.

Disorders of glucose and lipid metabolism constitute a central mechanism driving the initiation and progression of periodontitis and its bidirectional interactions with systemic diseases. On the one hand, abnormalities in glucose metabolism, particularly hyperglycaemia, can directly exacerbate periodontal inflammation and alveolar bone destruction.24,25 Periodontitis and dysglycaemia form a bidirectional reinforcing relationship, which represents a key pathological basis underlying the association between periodontitis and systemic conditions such as type 2 diabetes mellitus.26 On the other hand, dyslipidaemia plays an equally critical role: periodontitis has been shown to elevate serum lipid levels, while alterations in the expression of lipid metabolism-related genes within periodontal tissues are closely linked to immune microenvironment remodeling.27

A shared mechanistic feature across these processes is metabolic reprogramming of host immune cells (eg, macrophages) and resident tissue cells under inflammatory conditions. Aberrant activation of pathways such as glycolysis and lipid biosynthesis serves as a pivotal link between metabolic dysregulation and maladaptive immune-inflammatory responses, ultimately leading to alveolar bone loss.28,29 Dysregulation of key transcription factors, including peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma (PPAR-γ), may represent a critical nodal point integrating glucose-lipid metabolism and inflammatory signaling.30

From a clinical perspective, effective periodontal therapy has been shown to improve systemic glucose and lipid metabolic profiles,31 whereas circulating biomarkers indicative of metabolic dysfunction, such as C-reactive protein (CRP), are predictive of periodontitis risk,32 collectively underscoring the central role of metabolic disturbances in periodontal pathophysiology. Moreover, periodontal pathogens, particularly Porphyromonas gingivalis, can directly disrupt host lipid metabolism and aggravate systemic metabolic imbalance, thereby reinforcing the vicious cycle between oral and systemic diseases.33, 34, 35 As an integrative indicator of glucose – lipid metabolic status, GLM7 may therefore indirectly reflect the odds of having periodontitis through these interconnected metabolic – inflammatory pathways.

The individual components of GLM7 were not included as separate covariates in the regression models. Because GLM7 was constructed as a composite index capturing overall glycolipid metabolic status, additional adjustment for its constituent variables would result in overadjustment and multicollinearity, potentially attenuating the total association of interest. These components were therefore treated as integral elements of the exposure rather than independent confounders. When GLM7 was analysed as a continuous variable, its association with periodontitis remained statistically significant across Models 1 to 3, suggesting a consistent overall relationship. In contrast, after categorization into quartiles, the trend test remained significant in Models 1 and 2 but was attenuated in the fully adjusted Model 3, with only the third quartile reaching statistical significance. Restricted cubic spline analyses did not indicate a significant nonlinear association between GLM7 and periodontitis. Taken together, these findings suggest that the attenuated significance observed in the quartile-based analysis may be influenced by categorization-related information loss and reduced statistical power after extensive covariate adjustment. The attenuation in Model 3 may be attributed to the profound adjustment for multiple shared metabolic confounders (eg, hypertension), which closely intermingle with the components of GLM7. While the continuous model demonstrated a robust overall association, the quartile findings indicate that extreme elevations of GLM7 do not necessarily confer a proportionate increase in odds, underscoring the complex, multifactorial nature of periodontitis. Therefore, results from continuous analyses may provide a more stable representation of the overall association. It is crucial to recognize that periodontitis is fundamentally initiated by local plaque biofilms, whereas systemic metabolic biomarkers function primarily as risk modifiers rather than direct etiological agents. Consistent with this pathophysiological framework, existing literature typically reports Area Under the Curve (AUC) values in the range of 0.55 to 0.65 for both single and composite metabolic indices.23,36 This range reflects the inherent biological complexity of the disease rather than methodological deficiencies in the biomarkers themselves. Notably, the AUC observed in our study is comparable to those of established indicators – such as the TyG index and metabolic syndrome scores – thereby demonstrating the non-inferiority of GLM7 as an associated surrogate marker.22

In subgroup and interaction analyses based on weighted NHANES data, the association between GLM7 and periodontitis was generally consistent across most population characteristics. Notably, a significant interaction with hypertension status was observed, indicating a stronger association between GLM7 and periodontitis among individuals without hypertension. This finding suggests that hypertension status might modify the observed association. Given the exploratory nature of this subgroup analysis, this result should be interpreted with extreme caution. Hypertension itself is a profound systemic inflammatory state, which may potentially obscure or dilute the specific metabolic signals captured by GLM7. However, our mediation analysis did not support a significant mechanistic pathway involving hypertension. Therefore, these findings are primarily hypothesis-generating and require robust validation in future longitudinal cohorts.

Our findings are in line with a growing body of evidence highlighting the utility of systemic, composite blood biomarkers in evaluating periodontitis risk. Recent cross-sectional studies have demonstrated that parameters reflecting systemic inflammatory and nutritional status, such as serum albumin levels (Xiang et al, 2025) and the neutrophil percentage-to-albumin ratio (Zhou et al, 2024), are significantly associated with the severity of periodontitis.37,38 While those indicators primarily capture the systemic inflammatory burden and immune-nutritional axis, our novel index, GLM7, complements this existing literature by specifically addressing the glyco-lipid metabolic dimension. Together, these composite indices underscore the profound bidirectional relationship between systemic dysregulation – whether inflammatory, nutritional, or metabolic – and local periodontal destruction, highlighting the potential of routine haematological profiling in periodontal risk stratification.

To our knowledge, this study is the first to investigate the association between GLM7, a newly developed composite index of glucose and lipid metabolism, and periodontitis. The data were derived from the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES), which applies rigorous quality control procedures and employs a nationally representative sampling design, thereby enhancing the reliability and generalizability of the findings. However, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, owing to the cross-sectional nature of the study, causal inferences between GLM7 and periodontitis cannot be established. Second, although a wide range of potential confounders was adjusted for, residual confounding from unmeasured or imprecisely measured variables cannot be excluded, which is inherent to observational studies such as NHANES. Regarding oral hygiene behaviours, interdental cleaning was used as a surrogate covariate instead of tooth brushing frequency. This choice was based on the limited variability of self-reported brushing data (ceiling effect) in population-based surveys, whereas interdental practices exhibit greater variability and discriminatory capacity. Therefore, although covariate selection was optimized for statistical robustness, future prospective studies with more detailed behavioural measures are warranted. To address these limitations, future research should incorporate multicentre prospective studies to further validate the observed associations and explore potential causal relationships.

Conclusion

As a novel composite indicator reflecting glucose – lipid metabolic dysregulation, GLM7 was shown in this study, for the first time, to be positively associated with the prevalence (or odds) of periodontitis based on routine health examination parameters. Future studies using high-quality prospective designs are warranted to validate these findings and to further elucidate the underlying biological mechanisms linking GLM7 to periodontitis.

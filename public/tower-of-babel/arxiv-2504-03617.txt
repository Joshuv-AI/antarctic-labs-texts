Ideal quantum geometry of the surface states of rhombohedral graphite and its effects on the surface superconductivity
Guodong Jiang, Tero T. Heikkilä, Päivi Törmä

Abstract
The interplay of quantum geometry and interactions determines the correlated state properties of flat bands. Here, we investigate the ideal quantum geometry (IQG), i.e., the property that the trace of quantum metric equals the Berry curvature, in the surface flat bands of rhombohedrally stacked graphene (RG) multilayers. We show that RG represents a class of semimetals with IQG, among which only RG has a nonvanishing IQG at the center of the surface bands. In the presence of long-range hoppings, a displacement field polarizes the electron density to one of the two surfaces, stabilizing the IQG and boosting transitions to correlated phases like superconductivity. Analyzing the superfluid stiffness of the superconducting state in a many-layer RG, we reveal a heavy-fermion picture where the “heavy electrons” carry a nonzero supercurrent. This unusual behavior arises from the IQG of the surface states, which suggests a large fraction of supercurrent running on the surface of RG.

Ideal quantum geometry in a class of semimetals
.—It is noteworthy that RG belongs to a class of semimetals having flat surface bands with IQG, but among them, only RG has a nearly uniform IQG in the drumhead region. This semimetal class has the bulk Hamiltonians in 3D momentum space

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{(n)}(k_{x},k_{y},k_{z})=\begin{pmatrix}0&\text{c.c.}\\
(k_{x}+ik_{y})^{n}+e^{ik_{z}}&0\end{pmatrix},

(3)

For the

(4)

Effects of long-range hoppings and displacement field
.—In the energy scales relevant for correlated phenomena, the above discussed RG model is perturbed by long-range hoppings and the displacement field. We find that they compete with each other—the former tends to destroy the IQG, while the latter stabilizes it.

A general perturbation term
\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
can be described by an effective Hamiltonian acting on the basis of Eq. (
1
),
\langle\psi^{(i)}_{\textbf{k}}|\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}|\psi^{(j)}_{\textbf{k}}\rangle
(
i,j=1,2
), with the matrix elements computed in SM Sec. 3. For instance, the next-nearest-neighbor interlayer hopping term
\gamma_{2}
couples different A/B sublattices [see Fig.
1

\psi^{(1)}_{\textbf{k}}
with
\psi^{(2)}_{\textbf{k}}
and destroys the IQG of both bands. Conversely, the displacement field term leads to a diagonal matrix
m(1-k^{2})\sigma_{z}
, with
\pm m
the potentials on the two surfaces and
\sigma_{z}
the Pauli matrix, meaning a displacement field preserves
\psi^{(1)}_{\textbf{k}}
,
\psi^{(2)}_{\textbf{k}}
as the eigenstates, thus preserving the IQG. We note that weak disorders can be viewed as random onsite potentials; therefore, their effect is similar to the displacement field, which we discuss briefly in SM Sec. 3.1.

The opposite roles of long-range hoppings and displacement field on the surface states can lead to intriguing phenomena. At zero displacement field, the large density of states (DOS) of a surface band is equally shared by the two surfaces due to the state mixing by the long-range hoppings. However, the displacement field effect will dominate if its magnitude exceeds a threshold value, restoring the two bands as
\psi^{(1)}_{\textbf{k}}
and
\psi^{(2)}_{\textbf{k}}
, thereby polarizing the electron density of each band to one surface only
[
79
]
. The effective Hamiltonians of the long-range hopping terms exponentially decay as
N
increases (see SM Sec. 3.1), indicating that a large displacement field is particularly important for transitions to correlated states in few-layer RG. We use the superconducting state in rhombohedral trilayer graphene (RTG) to demonstrate this effect.

II.3
2.3 Origin of IQG and its orbital-embedding-dependence

In this section, we explain the origin of IQG found in the surface states of RG, which lays the foundation for generalizing it to other semimetal models. We show the orbital-embedding-dependence
[
123
]
of IQG, but we explain why the dependence is weak for the surface states of semimetals.
Throughout this work, orbital-embedding refers to the
U(1)\times U(1)\times...\times U(1)
gauge freedom of the Bloch Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}(\textbf{k})
,
which is explained explicitly in Prop.
2
below.

We first show an equivalent condition of IQG:

Proposition 1

The quantum geometry is ideal if and only if one of the two quantities,
\text{Tr}\{P\frac{\partial P}{\partial\pi}\frac{\partial P}{\partial\pi^{*}}\}
and
\text{Tr}\{P\frac{\partial P}{\partial\pi^{*}}\frac{\partial P}{\partial\pi}\}
, vanishes, where
\pi=k_{x}+ik_{y}
,
\pi^{*}=k_{x}-ik_{y}
, and
P
is the projection operator to a single band or a set of bands.

Proof:
A similar proof is given in Ref.
[
15
]
. Note that both quantities are real, and they may not be equal. We replace the variables
k_{x},k_{y}\rightarrow\pi,\pi^{*}
and treat the projection operator as a function of
\pi,\pi^{*}
:
P_{\textbf{k}}\rightarrow P_{\pi,\pi^{*}}
. After the substitution, we get

\begin{split}\text{Tr}g=&\mathcal{B}_{xx}+\mathcal{B}_{yy}=2\text{Tr}\big\{P\frac{\partial P}{\partial\pi}\frac{\partial P}{\partial\pi^{*}}+P\frac{\partial P}{\partial\pi^{*}}\frac{\partial P}{\partial\pi}\big\},\\[5.0pt]
\Omega=&-2\text{Im}\mathcal{B}_{xy}=2\text{Tr}\big\{P\frac{\partial P}{\partial\pi}\frac{\partial P}{\partial\pi^{*}}-P\frac{\partial P}{\partial\pi^{*}}\frac{\partial P}{\partial\pi}\big\}.\end{split}

(S19)

Both
\text{Tr}\{P\frac{\partial P}{\partial\pi}\frac{\partial P}{\partial\pi^{*}}\}
and
\text{Tr}\{P\frac{\partial P}{\partial\pi^{*}}\frac{\partial P}{\partial\pi}\}
are non-negative, e.g., for the single-band projection case,

\text{Tr}\big\{P\frac{\partial P}{\partial\pi}\frac{\partial P}{\partial\pi^{*}}\big\}=\langle\partial_{\pi}\psi|(1-|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)|\partial_{\pi^{*}}\psi\rangle=\langle\partial_{\pi}\psi|Q|\partial_{\pi^{*}}\psi\rangle\geq 0,

(S20)

since
Q=1-P
is a positive-semidefinite operator. Here, we use the convention that
\langle\partial_{\pi}\psi|
is the dual state of
|\partial_{\pi^{*}}\psi\rangle
. Similarly,
\text{Tr}\{P\frac{\partial P}{\partial\pi^{*}}\frac{\partial P}{\partial\pi}\}=\langle\partial_{\pi^{*}}\psi|Q|\partial_{\pi}\psi\rangle\geq 0
. Therefore, it is easy to establish
\text{Tr}g\geq|\Omega|
, since
a+b\geq|a-b|
for positive
a,b
, and the inequality saturates when
a=0
or
b=0
.

Next, we show that the IQG is an orbital-embedding-dependent property with the proposition below.

Proposition 2

The condition
\text{Tr}\{P\frac{\partial P}{\partial\pi}\frac{\partial P}{\partial\pi^{*}}\}=0
or
\text{Tr}\{P\frac{\partial P}{\partial\pi^{*}}\frac{\partial P}{\partial\pi}\}=0
is orbital-embedding-dependent.

Proof:
As the orbital embedding changes, the Bloch Hamiltonian matrix transforms as
\mathcal{H}(\textbf{k})\rightarrow U^{\dagger}(\textbf{k})\mathcal{H}(\textbf{k})U(\textbf{k})
and the state transforms as
|\psi_{\textbf{k}}\rangle\rightarrow U^{\dagger}(\textbf{k})|\psi_{\textbf{k}}\rangle
, where the diagonal unitary matrix
U(\textbf{k})=\text{diag}\{e^{i\textbf{k}\cdot\delta\textbf{r}_{\alpha}}\}
has the dimension equal to the number of orbitals in a unit cell, with
\delta\textbf{r}_{\alpha}=(x_{\alpha},y_{\alpha})
the position shift of orbital
\alpha
. Since we have changed variables
k_{x},k_{y}\rightarrow\pi,\pi^{*}
, we also introduce complex coordinates
X_{\alpha}=x_{\alpha}+iy_{\alpha}
,
X^{*}_{\alpha}=x_{\alpha}-iy_{\alpha}
, then,
U(\textbf{k})=U_{\pi,\pi^{*}}=\text{diag}\{e^{(i/2)(\pi X^{*}_{\alpha}+\pi^{*}X_{\alpha})}\}
. Using these notations, we find that under an orbital embedding transformation
P\rightarrow\widetilde{P}=U^{\dagger}PU
,

\text{Tr}\big\{\widetilde{P}\frac{\partial\widetilde{P}}{\partial\pi}\frac{\partial\widetilde{P}}{\partial\pi^{*}}\big\}=\text{Tr}\big\{P\frac{\partial P}{\partial\pi}\frac{\partial P}{\partial\pi^{*}}\big\}+\text{Tr}\big\{\frac{1}{4}(PX^{*}X-PX^{*}PX)-\frac{i}{2}P\frac{\partial P}{\partial\pi}X+\frac{i}{2}\frac{\partial P}{\partial\pi^{*}}PX^{*}\big\}.

(S21)

with
X=\text{diag}\{X_{\alpha}\}
. On the r.h.s. of Eq. (
S21
), the first term vanishes by assumption. If
X
is proportional to the identity matrix, the second term also vanishes by the
U(1)
gauge-invariance of QGT; otherwise, the second term is generally nonzero. As an example, if we shift the position of the first orbital only, i.e.,
X=\text{diag}\{X_{1},0,...,0\}
, then the second term of Eq. (
S21
), when written in the orbital basis, becomes

\frac{1}{4}(P_{11}-P_{11}^{2})|X_{1}|^{2}-\frac{i}{2}\sum_{\alpha}(P_{1\alpha}\frac{\partial P_{\alpha 1}}{\partial\pi}X_{1}-\frac{\partial P_{1\alpha}}{\partial\pi^{*}}P_{\alpha 1}X_{1}^{*})

(S22)

which is positive when
|X_{1}|
is large, since the coefficient
P_{11}-P_{11}^{2}=|\psi_{1}|^{2}-|\psi_{1}|^{4}>0
(assuming
|\psi_{1}|<1
). Therefore, the general orbital-embedding-dependence is proved (QED).

A full analysis of Eq. (
S21
) is given right after Prop.
4
below. Here, a remark is that
even though IQG is an orbital-embedding-dependent property, it is still of importance if IQG is satisfied under a special physical orbital embedding,
e.g., for the RG case when we choose the local embedding where the carbon atoms are located.

From Prop.
1
, we immediately get another equivalent condition:

Proposition 3

The quantum geometry of a single band
\psi
is ideal if and only if it is a function of
\pi
or
\pi^{*}
only, besides a normalization factor, i.e.,
|\psi\rangle=\mathcal{N}(\pi,\pi^{*})|\phi(\pi)\rangle
or
|\psi\rangle=\mathcal{N}(\pi,\pi^{*})|\phi(\pi^{*})\rangle
. In particular, in the orbital basis, the state components
\psi_{\alpha}(\pi,\pi^{*})=\mathcal{N}(\pi,\pi^{*})\phi_{\alpha}(\pi)
,
\forall\,\alpha
or
\mathcal{N}(\pi,\pi^{*})\phi_{\alpha}(\pi^{*})
,
\forall\,\alpha
.

Similar statements have been shown or discussed in Ref.
[
15
,
78
,
13
,
16
]
. We have it adapted to the orbital basis here. This result is obvious if we start from Eq. (
S20
) and notice that
\langle\partial_{\pi}\psi|Q|\partial_{\pi^{*}}\psi\rangle=0
means
\partial_{\pi^{*}}|\psi\rangle\propto|\psi\rangle
. Writing
\psi_{\alpha}(\pi,\pi^{*})=\mathcal{N}(\pi,\pi^{*})\phi_{\alpha}(\pi,\pi^{*})
, it implies that in
\partial_{\pi^{*}}\psi_{\alpha}(\pi,\pi^{*})
, the linear operator
\partial_{\pi^{*}}
acts on the factor
\mathcal{N}(\pi,\pi^{*})
only, so
\phi_{\alpha}=\phi_{\alpha}(\pi)
.

This proposition explains why the surface states of RG, Eq. (
S11
), have an IQG. It also implies that the IQG is an orbital-embedding-dependent property, since an orbital embedding transformation
U_{\pi,\pi^{*}}
generally makes
\phi_{\alpha}
depend on both
\pi
and
\pi^{*}
. However, for the surface states of RG, the following result is expected:

Proposition 4

IQG in the drumhead region of RG surface bands has a weak orbital-embedding-dependence; at the center of the drumhead region, the IQG is orbital-embedding-independent.

We sketch the proof by delineating a few useful results. We first note that when the first term in Eq. (
S21
) vanishes (in the case of IQG), the terms linear in
X,X^{*}
in its second term must vanish also, i.e.,

\text{Tr}\big\{P\frac{\partial P}{\partial\pi}\frac{\partial P}{\partial\pi^{*}}\big\}=0\,\,\,\Longrightarrow\,\,\,\langle\alpha|P\frac{\partial P}{\partial\pi}|\alpha\rangle=0\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\langle\alpha|\frac{\partial P}{\partial\pi^{*}}P|\alpha\rangle=0,\,\,\,\forall\,\alpha,

(S23)

which can be easily verified using Prop.
3
. This means that when
P
has IQG, Eq. (
S21
) is a positive-semidefinite quadratic form:

\begin{split}&\text{Tr}\big\{\widetilde{P}\frac{\partial\widetilde{P}}{\partial\pi}\frac{\partial\widetilde{P}}{\partial\pi^{*}}\big\}=\text{Tr}\big\{\frac{1}{4}(PX^{*}X-PX^{*}PX)\}=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\alpha\beta}X_{\alpha}^{*}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha\beta}X_{\beta},\\
&\text{with}\,\,\,\mathcal{M}_{\alpha\beta}=P_{\alpha\alpha}\delta_{\alpha\beta}-P_{\alpha\beta}P_{\beta\alpha}=|\psi_{\alpha}|^{2}\delta_{\alpha\beta}-|\psi_{\alpha}|^{2}|\psi_{\beta}|^{2}.\end{split}

(S24)

P_{\alpha\beta}=\psi_{\alpha}\psi_{\beta}^{*}
are the matrix elements of
P
in the orbital basis. The positive-semidefinite
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha\beta}
(
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha\beta}
satisfies
\sum_{\beta}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha\beta}=0
whose properties can be found in Ref.
[
88
]
) ensures that
\text{Tr}\{\widetilde{P}\frac{\partial\widetilde{P}}{\partial\pi}\frac{\partial\widetilde{P}}{\partial\pi^{*}}\}\geq 0
, and has
\text{Rank}(\mathcal{M}_{\alpha\beta})=\widetilde{N}_{orb}-1
, with
\widetilde{N}_{orb}
the number of orbitals that have nonzero weight
|\psi_{\alpha}|^{2}
in the state
\psi
.

In general,
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha\beta}
measures how fast the IQG is destroyed by moving the orbital positions around the IQG embedding, which can be analyzed using the surface state
\psi^{(1)}
from Eq. (
S11
) as an example. For
N
-layer RG, near the rim of the drumhead region (
k\lesssim 1
), the decay length
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
and the surface state
\psi^{(1)}
is uniformly distributed over the
\widetilde{N}_{orb}=N
orbitals: 1A, 2A, …,
N
A. Then, the nonzero entries of
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha\beta}
form a
N\times N
block,

\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{N}-\frac{1}{N^{2}}&-\frac{1}{N^{2}}&-\frac{1}{N^{2}}&..\\
-\frac{1}{N^{2}}&\frac{1}{N}-\frac{1}{N^{2}}&-\frac{1}{N^{2}}&..\\
-\frac{1}{N^{2}}&-\frac{1}{N^{2}}&\frac{1}{N}-\frac{1}{N^{2}}&..\\
..&..&..&..\end{pmatrix}

(S25)

which has one zero eigenvalue and
N-1
degenerate eigenvalues of
1/N
. This means that when the orbital embedding changes by the size of the lattice constant of graphene,
|X_{\alpha}|\sim a_{0}
,
\text{Tr}\big\{P^{(1)}\frac{\partial P^{(1)}}{\partial\pi}\frac{\partial P^{(1)}}{\partial\pi^{*}}\big\}
deviates from 0 by an amount
\sim(1/N)(k_{0}a_{0})^{2}
, whereas the other quantity
\text{Tr}\big\{P^{(1)}\frac{\partial P^{(1)}}{\partial\pi^{*}}\frac{\partial P^{(1)}}{\partial\pi}\big\}=\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}g^{(1)}=1/(1-k^{2})^{2}\gg 1
at the IQG embedding. As a result, under such an orbital embedding transformation, the ratio
|\Omega^{(1)}|/\text{Tr}g^{(1)}
is decreased by

\delta\bigg(\frac{|\Omega^{(1)}|}{\text{Tr}g^{(1)}}\bigg)\sim\frac{\delta\text{Tr}\{P^{(1)}\frac{\partial P^{(1)}}{\partial\pi}\frac{\partial P^{(1)}}{\partial\pi^{*}}\}}{\text{Tr}\{P^{(1)}\frac{\partial P^{(1)}}{\partial\pi^{*}}\frac{\partial P^{(1)}}{\partial\pi}\}|_{\text{IQG}}}\ll\frac{1}{N}(k_{0}a_{0})^{2}\ll 1,

(S26)

which means the effect on IQG is negligible. Here, the small deviation
\delta(|\Omega^{(1)}|/\text{Tr}g^{(1)})
comes from both factors
1/N
and the small value of
k_{0}a_{0}
in RG.

On the other hand, near the center of the drumhead region, the surface states are strongly localized; therefore, the nonzero eigenvalues of
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha\beta}
are all much smaller than
1/N
, meaning the orbital-embedding dependence is even weaker than at the rim. At the center of the drumhead region (
k=0
), the surface state
\psi^{(1)}
is completely localized at orbital 1A, then
\widetilde{N}_{orb}=1
and
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha\beta}
becomes a zero matrix. Therefore, the IQG at
k=0
is orbital-embedding-independent; this is consistent with having only the global
U(1)
gauge freedom in the case of one orbital and QG being independent of the global gauge.

II.4
2.4 Generalized semimetal models

Here, we provide more details about the generalized semimetal models introduced in the MS. We focus on the most general case of Eq.(
S8
), when
f(k_{x},k_{y},k_{z})=p_{n}(\pi)+e^{ik_{z}}
, where

p_{n}(\pi)=a_{n}\pi^{n}+...+a_{1}\pi+a_{0}

(S27)

is a polynomial of
\pi=k_{x}+ik_{y}
of degree
n
, with complex coefficients
a_{n},...,a_{0}
. Then, the Hamiltonian Eq.(
S8
) can be understood as the 2D effective Hamiltonian
h(\textbf{k})=\sigma_{x}\text{Re}p_{n}(\pi)+\sigma_{y}\text{Im}p_{n}(\pi)
coupled by interlayer hoppings along the
z
direction.

The gapless points of the model are guaranteed to form nodal lines, which can be solved from

p_{n}(\pi)+e^{ik_{z}}=0.

(S28)

This polynomial equation has
n
complex roots, which are labeled by
s
, and each nodal line is represented by one root as a function of
k_{z}
,
\pi^{(s)}(k_{z})
. We make the assumption that the coefficient
a_{0}=0
, i.e.,
p_{n}(0)=0
, which means that the 2D effective Hamiltonian
h(\textbf{k})
is gapless at
\textbf{k}=0
. This implies that none of these nodal lines passes the origin
\pi=0
in the
k_{x}-k_{y}
plane, so winding numbers about
\pi=0
can be defined. To define the winding number, we note that the polynomial equation is invariant if
k_{z}
is increased by
2\pi
, restricting that one root evaluated at
k_{z}=2\pi
must be identical to another root (or itself) at
k_{z}=0
,
\pi^{(s)}(2\pi)=\pi^{(s^{\prime})}(0)
. This allows us to divide these
n
roots into several groups (labeled by
i
):

\{\pi^{(s_{1})},\pi^{(s_{2})},...,\pi^{(s_{n_{1}})}\},\,\,\,\{\pi^{(s_{n_{1}+1})},\pi^{(s_{n_{1}+2})},...,\pi^{(s_{n_{1}+n_{2}})}\},\,\,\,...

(S29)

In each group, the roots are ordered by the successive relation
\pi^{(s_{j})}(2\pi)=\pi^{(s_{j+1})}(0)
, while the last element is related to the first one in the same manner, e.g., in the first group,
\pi^{(s_{n_{1}})}(2\pi)=\pi^{(s_{1})}(0)
. These relations indicate that when projected to the
k_{x}-k_{y}
plane, these nodal lines in the same group form a loop. Then, a winding number can be defined for each loop,

w_{i}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{s\in\text{group }i}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\,\mathrm{d}k_{z}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\varphi^{(s)}(k_{z})}{\,\mathrm{d}k_{z}},

(S30)

where
\varphi^{(s)}(k_{z})
is the azimuthal angle of
\pi^{(s)}(k_{z})
. If
w_{i}=0
, then these nodal lines in group
i
can be adiabatically shrunk into a point by other perturbations, without opening a gap for the 2D semimetal
h(\textbf{k})
.

Following the same procedure described at the beginning of Sec. 2.1, it is easy to show that each loop corresponds to the boundary of a drumhead region (these drumheads may not have a round shape). The boundary condition decides which side of the loop the surface states live on. The Hamiltonian Eq. (
S8
) with
f(k_{x},k_{y},k_{z})=p_{n}(\pi)+e^{ik_{z}}
tells us that sublattice B of layer
z
is coupled with A of layer
z+1
(see Fig.
S1
(b) for the RG case), so the boundary condition is still
\psi_{\text{B}}(z=0)=\psi_{\text{A}}(z=N+1)=0
. Then, two surface bands are formed on the side determined by
|p_{n}(\pi)|<1
, with normalized wavefunctions

\psi^{(1)}_{\textbf{k}}(z)=\frac{\sqrt{1-|p_{n}(\pi)|^{2}}}{|p_{n}(\pi)|}e^{\kappa(\textbf{k})z}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix},\,\,\,\psi^{(2)}_{\textbf{k}}(z)=\frac{\sqrt{1-|p_{n}(\pi)|^{2}}}{|p_{n}(\pi)|}e^{\kappa(\textbf{k})^{*}(N+1-z)}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix},

(S31)

where
\kappa(\textbf{k})=\ln[-p_{n}(\pi)]
. Prop.
3
and
4
dictate that these surface bands have IQG with negligible orbital-embedding-dependence.

3. Perturbation effects on the RG surface states

III.1
3.1 Effects of long-range hoppings, displacement field and disorder

We treat the extra long-range hopping and displacement field terms using the degenerate perturbation method and compute their effective Hamiltonian matrices. These two-by-two matrices are given by
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{ij}(\textbf{k})=\langle\psi^{(i)}_{\textbf{k}}|\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}|\psi^{(j)}_{\textbf{k}}\rangle
(
i,j=1,2
), where
\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
refers to
\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{(\gamma_{2})}
,
\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{(\gamma_{3})}
,
\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{(\gamma_{4})}
and
\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{(m)}
. Using their expressions in Eq. (
S3
) - (
S7
), and the wavefunctions
\psi^{(1,2)}_{\textbf{k}}
given in Eq. (
S11
), it is straightforward to obtain

\mathcal{H}^{(\gamma_{2})}_{ij}(\textbf{k})=(N-2)\frac{\widetilde{\gamma}_{2}}{2}\frac{1-k^{2}}{k^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0&c.c.\\
(-\pi)^{N-1}&0\end{pmatrix};

(S32)

\mathcal{H}^{(\gamma_{3})}_{ij}(\textbf{k})=-(N-1)\widetilde{v}_{3}(1-k^{2})\begin{pmatrix}0&c.c.\\
(-\pi)^{N-1}&0\end{pmatrix};

(S33)

\mathcal{H}^{(\gamma_{4})}_{ij}(\textbf{k})=-2\widetilde{v}_{4}k^{2}(1-k^{2N-2})\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\approx-2\widetilde{v}_{4}k^{2}\sigma_{0};

(S34)

\mathcal{H}^{(m)}_{ij}(\textbf{k})=\begin{pmatrix}m(1-k^{2})&0\\
0&-m(1-k^{2})\end{pmatrix},

(S35)

where
\pi=\xi k_{x}+ik_{y}
. Note that choosing a different potential distribution function for the displacement field, e.g., a linear potential distribution
V(z)=m(1-2\frac{z-1}{N-1})
only gives a small correction to Eq. (
S35
), so makes no qualitative difference.

These effective Hamiltonians indicate the roles of each term on the surface states: (i)
\gamma_{2}
and
\gamma_{3}
terms both mix the two basis states
\psi^{(1)}_{\textbf{k}}
and
\psi^{(2)}_{\textbf{k}}
, and split the two bands, but their effects exponentially decay as
N
increases; (ii)
\gamma_{4}
term does not affect the eigenstates or split the bands, but only gives the same quadratic “bending” to both bands [see Fig.
S4
(a)], and this effect is almost
N
-independent; (iii) the displacement field term
m
gives opposite quadratic “bending” to the two bands, splitting the bands and preserving
\psi^{(1)}_{\textbf{k}}
and
\psi^{(2)}_{\textbf{k}}
as eigenstates, and this effect is also
N
-independent. When
N
is large, effects of
\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3}
are negligible, while
\gamma_{4}
and
m
together can make one surface band much more dispersionless than the other. Comparing Eq. (
S34
) with (
S35
), we find that at special surface potential values,
m=\pm 2\widetilde{v}_{4}
(in units of
\gamma_{1}
), the conduction band becomes extremely flat, as shown in Fig.
S4
(b) and (c).

when the long-range hoppings

N=20

Finally, we briefly discuss the effect of disorder on the RG surface states and IQG. Disorder can be treated as random on-site potentials (
V_{i}
, where
i
labels the lattice site), whose effect on the normal state is similar to the displacement field. For weak disorder (
|V_{i}|
smaller than the gap between surface and bulk bands, see Fig.
S4
), the effective Hamiltonian matrix involves the two surface bands only, which is a diagonal two-by-two matrix. If the potentials
V_{i}
have the same average on the two surfaces, then the matrix is proportional to the 2-dim identity matrix
\sigma_{0}
, which does not affect the surface states or QGT; however, if
V_{i}
has different averages on the two surfaces,
\langle V_{i}\rangle_{\text{top}}\neq\langle V_{i}\rangle_{\text{bottom}}
(e.g., due to different types of impurities), then the matrix contains a
\sigma_{z}
term. Then, similar to the displacement field, this kind of disorder can even help preserve the surface states and IQG of both bands.

Strong disorder whose strength exceeds the gap between the surface and bulk bands enables transitions between them. As a result, the surface state properties (including IQG) are no longer protected. For correlated phases, the disorder strength must also be compared with the correlated gap, e.g., the superconducting gap of the superconducting state
[
124
,
125
,
126
]
. When
|V_{i}|
is smaller than the superconducting gap, the qualitative properties of RG superconductivity and superfluidity are expected to persist.

III.2
3.2 Effect of surface hybridization

In this section, we first provide an explanation for the surface hybridization origin of the QM peaks at
k=k_{h}
for the small-
N
-layer RG. We then derive the two-by-two effective Hamiltonian for the surface hybridization, which is similar to the matrices
\mathcal{H}^{(\gamma_{2,3,4})}_{ij}
and
\mathcal{H}^{(m)}_{ij}
for the extra hopping terms in the previous section.

QGT of a surface band, e.g., the valence band, can be decomposed into the interband QGT components:

\mathcal{B}^{(v)}_{\mu\nu}(\textbf{k})=\sum_{j\neq v}\langle\partial_{\mu}\psi^{(v)}_{\textbf{k}}|\psi^{(j)}_{\textbf{k}}\rangle\langle\psi^{(j)}_{\textbf{k}}|\partial_{\nu}\psi^{(v)}_{\textbf{k}}\rangle=\sum_{j\neq v}\frac{\langle\psi^{(v)}_{\textbf{k}}|\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{H}(\textbf{k})|\psi^{(j)}_{\textbf{k}}\rangle\langle\psi^{(j)}_{\textbf{k}}|\partial_{\nu}\mathcal{H}(\textbf{k})|\psi^{(v)}_{\textbf{k}}\rangle}{(\varepsilon_{v,\textbf{k}}-\varepsilon_{j\textbf{k}})^{2}}.

(S36)

Here, for the first equality,
j
is summed over all bands except the valence band; we use the Kubo formula to get the second equality. Eq. (
S36
) shows that the peaks of QGT can result from the small band gaps
\varepsilon_{v,\textbf{k}}-\varepsilon_{j\textbf{k}}
. In our case, the smallest bandgap to the valence band is from the conduction surface band, which is the reason why we compare
g^{(v)}
with the interband component
g^{(vc)}
in Fig. 2(b) of the MS. In addition, when the two surface states have a small decay length,
\lambda\ll N
,

g^{(vc)}_{\mu\nu}(\textbf{k})=g^{(12)}_{\mu\nu}(\textbf{k})=-\text{Re}\langle\partial_{\mu}\psi^{(1)}_{\textbf{k}}|\psi^{(2)}_{\textbf{k}}\rangle\langle\psi^{(2)}_{\textbf{k}}|\partial_{\nu}\psi^{(1)}_{\textbf{k}}\rangle

(S37)

vanishes, since
\psi^{(1)}_{\textbf{k}}
and
\psi^{(2)}_{\textbf{k}}
have no overlap. Therefore,
g^{(vc)}_{\mu\nu}(\textbf{k})
can only exhibit peaks when the decay length becomes large,
\lambda\sim N
, which confirms that the peaks of
g^{(v)}_{\mu\nu}
result from the surface hybridization.

Next, we derive the two-by-two effective Hamiltonian for the surface hybridization. For finite-
N
-layer RG, one can still choose the same wavefunctions
\psi^{(1,2)}_{\textbf{k}}
from Eq. (
S11
) as the basis, but have to truncate the Hilbert space by restricting
z
running from 1 to a finite
N
. This means now
\psi^{(1,2)}_{\textbf{k}}
are only approximately normalized. Then, the effective Hamiltonian matrix is

\mathcal{H}^{(\text{hybd})}_{ij}(\textbf{k})=\langle\psi^{(i)}_{\textbf{k}}|\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{(\gamma_{0})}+\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{(\gamma_{1})}|\psi^{(j)}_{\textbf{k}}\rangle,

(S38)

with
\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{(\gamma_{0})}
and
\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{(\gamma_{1})}
given in Eq. (
S2
) and (
S3
). Evaluating the matrix elements, we find

\mathcal{H}^{(\text{hybd})}_{ij}(\textbf{k})=-\frac{1-k^{2}}{k^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0&c.c.\\
(-\pi)^{N+2}&0\end{pmatrix}.

(S39)

Note that this effective Hamiltonian is intrinsically different from Eq. (
S16
), since its basis states are
\psi^{(1,2)}_{\textbf{k}}
, whereas the basis of Eq. (
S16
) are orbital 1A and
N
B. Effective Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}^{(\text{hybd})}_{ij}
becomes important when its matrix elements exceed some measure, i.e., a small value
e^{-\eta}
(
\eta>0
). As long as
k
is not too close to 1, we obtain

\bigg|-\frac{1-k^{2}}{k^{2}}(-\pi)^{N+2}\bigg|\sim e^{-\eta}\,\,\,\rightarrow\,\,\,k\sim e^{-\eta/N},

(S40)

which reproduces the definition of
k_{h}
given in the MS.

The hybridization term
\mathcal{H}^{(\text{hybd})}_{ij}
can also be considered together with the displacement field term,
\mathcal{H}^{(m)}_{ij}
. Combining Eq. (
S35
) and (
S39
), we get (c.f. Eq. (
S16
))

\mathcal{H}^{(m)}_{ij}(\textbf{k})+\mathcal{H}^{(\text{hybd})}_{ij}(\textbf{k})=(1-k^{2})\begin{pmatrix}m&c.c.\\
\frac{(-\pi)^{N+2}}{k^{2}}&-m\end{pmatrix}.

(S41)

In this case, the surface potential
m
provides a natural measure for the surface hybridization strength, e.g., it defines
k_{h}
such that

\bigg|\frac{(-\pi)^{N+2}}{k^{2}}\bigg|\sim m\,\,\,\rightarrow\,\,\,k_{h}\sim m^{1/N},

(S42)

which reproduces the
k_{h}
-
m
relation, Eq. (
S18
).

IV
4. Self-consistency equations and superfluid weight calculation

This section provides the general formalism for calculating the superconducting gap and superfluid weight.

IV.1
4.1 Self-consistent gap equation and electron number equation

The onsite attractive density-density interaction for the RG is

\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{I}=-U\sum_{\textbf{R},\alpha}\hat{n}_{\textbf{R}\alpha\uparrow}\hat{n}_{\textbf{R}\alpha\downarrow}=-\frac{U}{N_{c}}\sum_{\textbf{k}\textbf{k}^{\prime}\textbf{q},\alpha}c_{\textbf{k}+\textbf{q},\alpha\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{-\textbf{k}+\textbf{q},\alpha\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{-\textbf{k}^{\prime}+\textbf{q},\alpha\downarrow}c_{\textbf{k}^{\prime}+\textbf{q},\alpha\uparrow}

(S43)

with
U>0
. After mean-field decoupling, it leads to an intra-orbital pairing matrix which is diagonal in the orbital basis
\hat{\Delta}=\text{diag}\{\Delta_{\alpha}\}
(
\alpha
running over the
2N
orbitals) and

\Delta_{\alpha}\equiv-U\langle c_{\textbf{R}\alpha\downarrow}c_{\textbf{R}\alpha\uparrow}\rangle=-\frac{2U}{N_{c}}\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\Lambda_{K}}\langle c_{-\textbf{k}\alpha\downarrow}c_{\textbf{k}\alpha\uparrow}\rangle,

(S44)

where the sum over
k
is over a cutoff region
\Lambda_{K}
near
K
and the factor 2 counts the valley degeneracy. For an interaction
U\ll\gamma_{1}
, it is a valid approximation that the only nonzero order parameters are
\Delta_{1\text{A}}
and
\Delta_{N\text{B}}
. Therefore,
\hat{\Delta}=\text{diag}\{\Delta_{1\text{A}},0,0,..,0,\Delta_{N\text{B}}\}
.

The Bogoliubov-de-Gennes (BdG) equation

\mathcal{H}_{\text{BdG}}(\textbf{k})\phi_{i}(\textbf{k})=E_{i,\textbf{k}}\phi_{i}(\textbf{k})

(S45)

can be diagonalized numerically. Here
i
labels the
4N
Bogoliubov bands and

\mathcal{H}_{\text{BdG}}(\textbf{k})=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{H}^{\uparrow}(\textbf{k})-\mu&\hat{\Delta}\\
\hat{\Delta}&-[\mathcal{H}^{\downarrow T}(-\textbf{k})-\mu]\end{pmatrix}.

(S46)

By TRS,
\mathcal{H}^{\uparrow}(\textbf{k})=\mathcal{H}^{\downarrow T}(-\textbf{k})\equiv\mathcal{H}(\textbf{k})
with
\mathcal{H}(\textbf{k})
the
2N
-dim noninteracting Hamiltonian of
N
-layer RG. Denote the
i
th eigenstate of
\mathcal{H}_{\text{BdG}}(\textbf{k})
by
\phi_{i}
, then Eq. (
S44
) becomes

\Delta_{\alpha}=-\frac{2U}{N_{c}}\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\Lambda_{K}}\sum_{i=1}^{4N}\phi_{i,\alpha\downarrow}(\textbf{k})^{*}\phi_{i,\alpha\uparrow}(\textbf{k})n_{i}(\textbf{k}),

(S47)

where
n_{i}(\textbf{k})=\frac{1}{1+\text{exp}(\beta E_{i,\textbf{k}})}
is the Fermi-Dirac function.

Similarly, the electron number equation in terms of eigenstates
\phi_{i}
is

\begin{split}N_{e}=&2\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\Lambda_{K}}\sum_{\alpha}(\langle c_{\textbf{k}\alpha\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{\textbf{k}\alpha\uparrow}\rangle+\langle c_{-\textbf{k}\alpha\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{-\textbf{k}\alpha\downarrow}\rangle)\\
=&2\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\Lambda_{K}}\sum_{\alpha}\sum_{i=1}^{4N}\big\{n_{i}(\textbf{k})|\phi_{i,\alpha\uparrow}(\textbf{k})|^{2}+[1-n_{i}(\textbf{k})]|\phi_{i,\alpha\downarrow}(\textbf{k})|^{2}\big\}.\end{split}

(S48)

The total electron number in the cutoff region for RG at charge neutrality is

N_{e,0}=2\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\Lambda_{K}}\sum_{\alpha}(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2})=2\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\Lambda_{K}}\sum_{\alpha}\sum_{i=1}^{4N}|\phi_{i,\alpha\downarrow}(\textbf{k})|^{2}.

(S49)

Then, the electron number measured from charge neutrality is

\delta N_{e}\equiv N_{e}-N_{e,0}=2\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\Lambda_{K}}\sum_{\alpha}\sum_{i=1}^{4N}n_{i}(\textbf{k})\big[|\phi_{i,\alpha\uparrow}(\textbf{k})|^{2}-|\phi_{i,\alpha\downarrow}(\textbf{k})|^{2}\big].

(S50)

The electron density
n_{e}\equiv\delta N_{e}/S
(
S
is the sample area) is in the units of
\text{cm}^{-2}
[note that the momentum is in the units of
k_{0}=\gamma_{1}/(\hbar v_{f})=0.39\text{ eV}/(6.58\times 10^{-16}\text{ eV}\cdot\text{s}\times 10^{6}\text{ m/s})=5.9\times 10^{6}\text{ cm}^{-1}
].

When the two surface bands of the RG are gapped by surface potential
m
, and the superconducting state is doped to only the valence band (requiring
m>\Delta_{\alpha}
), the single-band projection becomes valid. Therefore, the summation over the
4N
Bogoliubov bands in Eq. (
S47
) and
S50
can be replaced with summation over only the quasiparticle and quasihole bands associated with the valence band,
v+
and
v-
, i.e.
\sum_{i=1}^{4N}\rightarrow\sum_{i=v+,v-}
.

IV.2
4.2 General superfluid weight formula

We use the grand potential formalism
[
10
]
to compute the superfluid weight numerically. The superfluid weight tensor is the second total derivative of the free energy density with half of the Cooper pair center of mass momentum
q
,
D_{s,\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{S}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{2}F(\textbf{q})}{\,\mathrm{d}q_{\mu}\,\mathrm{d}q_{\nu}}\big|_{\textbf{q}=0}
(we set
e=\hbar=1
; for convenience we set the sample area
S=1
hereafter). Under TRS, using the relation between free energy
F
and grand potential
\mathit{\Omega}
,
F(\textbf{q})=\mathit{\Omega}(\textbf{q},\hat{\Delta}_{\textbf{q}},\hat{\Delta}_{\textbf{q}}^{*},\mu_{\textbf{q}})+\mu_{\textbf{q}}N_{e}
where the
q
-dependence of
\hat{\Delta}_{\textbf{q}},\mu_{\textbf{q}}
arise from self-consistency equations (the electron number
N_{e}
is fixed for different
q
), the superfluid weight can be expressed as
[
10
,
86
]
:

D_{s,\mu\nu}=\bigg[\frac{\partial^{2}\mathit{\Omega}}{\partial q_{\mu}\partial q_{\nu}}-\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\Delta^{I}_{\textbf{q},\alpha}}{\,\mathrm{d}q_{\mu}}\frac{\partial^{2}\mathit{\Omega}}{\partial\Delta^{I}_{\textbf{q},\alpha}\partial\Delta^{I}_{\textbf{q},\beta}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\Delta^{I}_{\textbf{q},\beta}}{\,\mathrm{d}q_{\nu}}\bigg]\bigg|_{\textbf{q}=0}.

(S51)

Here, the second term of Eq. (
S51
) makes
D_{s,\mu\nu}
gauge-invariant with respect to the orbital embedding
\textbf{r}_{\alpha}
of different orbitals
\alpha
(
\hat{\Delta}_{\textbf{q}}=\text{diag}\{\Delta_{\textbf{q},\alpha}\}
denotes the pairing matrix of the finite-
q
supercurrent state). Here
\Delta^{I}_{\textbf{q},\alpha}
is the imaginary part of
\Delta_{\textbf{q},\alpha}
, which is 0 at
\textbf{q}=0
since all
\Delta_{\alpha}
are real by virtue of TRS. TRS also implies that
\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\Delta_{\textbf{q},\alpha}}{\,\mathrm{d}q_{\mu}}|_{\textbf{q}=0}=i\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\Delta^{I}_{\textbf{q},\alpha}}{\,\mathrm{d}q_{\mu}}|_{\textbf{q}=0}=-\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\Delta_{\textbf{q},\alpha}^{*}}{\,\mathrm{d}q_{\mu}}|_{\textbf{q}=0}
. Taking into account a prefactor
1/(\mathcal{A}k_{0}^{2})=54.9
, where
\mathcal{A}=5.24\times 10^{-16}
cm
2
is the unit cell area coming from
1/S=1/(N_{c}\mathcal{A})
, the calculated
D_{s}
has the dimension of energy. In other words, the final superfluid weight is the calculated value multiplied by a factor of
54.9e^{2}/\hbar^{2}
. Here, we compute the first term of Eq. (
S51
); the second term is calculated in Sec. 4.5.

The BdG Hamiltonian for the supercurrent state is

\mathcal{H}_{\text{BdG}}(\textbf{k},\textbf{q})=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{H}^{\uparrow}(\textbf{k}+\textbf{q})-\mu_{\textbf{q}}I_{2N}&\hat{\Delta}_{\textbf{q}}\\
\hat{\Delta}_{\textbf{q}}&-[\mathcal{H}^{\downarrow\,T}(-\textbf{k}+\textbf{q})-\mu_{\textbf{q}}I_{2N}]\end{pmatrix},

(S52)

and the second-quantized total mean-field Hamiltonian is

\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{MF}}(\textbf{q})=\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\text{BZ}}\big\{\textbf{C}_{\textbf{k},\textbf{q}}^{\dagger}\mathcal{H}_{\text{BdG}}(\textbf{k},\textbf{q})\textbf{C}_{\textbf{k},\textbf{q}}+\text{Tr}[\mathcal{H}^{\downarrow}(-\textbf{k}+\textbf{q})-\mu_{\textbf{q}}I_{2N}]\big\}+\frac{N_{c}}{U}\sum_{\alpha}|\Delta_{\textbf{q},\alpha}|^{2},

(S53)

where
\textbf{C}_{\textbf{k},\textbf{q}}=(c_{\textbf{k}+\textbf{q},1\text{A},\uparrow},..,c_{-\textbf{k}+\textbf{q},1\text{A},\downarrow}^{\dagger},..)^{T}
,
I_{2N}
is the
2N
-dim identity matrix and
k
runs over the entire Brillouin zone (BZ). Let
\hat{E}_{\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})
denote the diagonal matrix of the spectrum of
\mathcal{H}_{\text{BdG}}(\textbf{k},\textbf{q})
by diagonalization, then at temperature
T=1/\beta

[
10
]
,

\mathit{\Omega}(\textbf{q},\hat{\Delta}_{\textbf{q}},\hat{\Delta}_{\textbf{q}}^{*},\mu_{\textbf{q}})=-\frac{1}{\beta}\ln\text{Tr}\{e^{-\beta\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{MF}}(\textbf{q})}\}=-\frac{1}{2\beta}\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\text{BZ}}\text{Tr}\ln[2+2\cosh\beta\hat{E}_{\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})]+\frac{N_{c}}{U}\sum_{\alpha}|\Delta_{\textbf{q},\alpha}|^{2}+\text{const.}

(S54)

When computing the first term of Eq. (
S51
), we treat
\hat{\Delta}_{\textbf{q}}
and
\mu_{\textbf{q}}
as
q
-independent in Eq. (
S54
).

The Fermi surfaces of RG at valleys
K
and
K^{\prime}
are small compared to the entire Brillouin zone. Therefore, it is necessary to convert Eq. (
S54
) into a summation that converges in a finite cutoff regions at the two valleys. Note that the conduction (valence) surface band has a particle (hole)-like Fermi surface, i.e., as
k
is far from the cutoff regions, asymptotically

E_{c\pm,\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})\rightarrow\pm\xi_{c,\textbf{k}\pm\textbf{q}},\,\,\,E_{v\pm,\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})\rightarrow\mp\xi_{v,\textbf{k}\mp\textbf{q}},

(S55)

where
\xi_{c(v),\textbf{k}}=\varepsilon_{c(v),\textbf{k}}-\mu
, with
\varepsilon_{c(v),\textbf{k}}
the electronic band dispersion of the conduction (valence) band. Here
E_{c(v)\pm,\textbf{k}}
are the quasiparticle (+) and quasihole (-) band spectra associated with
c
and
v
bands in the diagonal entries of
\hat{E}_{\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})
. We then transform the grand potential
\mathit{\Omega}
by adding a
q
-independent boundary term:
\mathit{\Omega}(\textbf{q},\hat{\Delta}_{\textbf{q}},\hat{\Delta}_{\textbf{q}}^{*},\mu_{\textbf{q}})+\mathit{\Omega}_{0}\rightarrow\mathit{\Omega}(\textbf{q},\hat{\Delta}_{\textbf{q}},\hat{\Delta}_{\textbf{q}}^{*},\mu_{\textbf{q}})
(this process corresponds to “subtracting the supercurrent in the normal state” in literature
[
127
,
47
]
). Specifically, we break each integral term (due to the trace of the diagonal matrix
\hat{E}_{\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})
) of Eq. (
S54
), which integrates
k
over the entire BZ, into a sum of integrals inside and outside the cutoff region. e.g., for the
E_{c+,\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})
term:

\begin{split}&\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\text{BZ}}\ln[2+2\cosh\beta E_{c+,\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})]\\
=&\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\Lambda_{K}}\ln[2+2\cosh\beta E_{c+,\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})]+\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\Lambda_{K^{\prime}}}\ln[2+2\cosh\beta E_{c+,\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})]+\sum_{\textbf{k}\notin\Lambda_{K}\text{ or }\Lambda_{K^{\prime}}}\ln[2+2\cosh\beta E_{c+,\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})]\\
=&2\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\Lambda_{K}}\ln[2+2\cosh\beta E_{c+,\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})]+\sum_{\textbf{k}\notin\Lambda_{K}\text{ or }\Lambda_{K^{\prime}}}\ln[2+2\cosh\beta E_{c+,\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})]\\
=&2\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\Lambda_{K}}\ln[2+2\cosh\beta E_{c+,\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})]+\sum_{\textbf{k}\notin\Lambda_{K}\text{ or }\Lambda_{K^{\prime}}}\ln[2+2\cosh\beta\xi_{c,\textbf{k}+\textbf{q}}]\\
=&2\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\Lambda_{K}}\ln\frac{2+2\cosh\beta E_{c+,\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})}{2+2\cosh\beta\xi_{c,\textbf{k}+\textbf{q}}}+\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\text{BZ}}\ln[2+2\cosh\beta\xi_{c,\textbf{k}+\textbf{q}}].\end{split}

(S56)

Here
\Lambda_{K^{\prime}}
refers to the cutoff region at valley
K^{\prime}
which is of the same size as
\Lambda_{K}
; to get the second line, we used valley degeneracy; to get the third line, we used the asymptotic relation Eq. (
S55
); to get the last line, we used partial integral. The last term in the last line above is
q
-independent as it integrates over the entire BZ, so it contributes to the boundary term
\mathit{\Omega}_{0}
. We then obtain the following transformed grand potential which integrates over the region
\Lambda_{K}
only:

\begin{split}\mathit{\Omega}(\textbf{q},\hat{\Delta}_{\textbf{q}},\hat{\Delta}_{\textbf{q}}^{*},\mu_{\textbf{q}})=-\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\Lambda_{K}}&\bigg[\ln\frac{1+\cosh\beta E_{c+,\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})}{1+\cosh\beta\xi_{c,\textbf{k}+\textbf{q}}}+\ln\frac{1+\cosh\beta E_{c-,\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})}{1+\cosh\beta\xi_{c,\textbf{k}-\textbf{q}}}\\
&+\ln\frac{1+\cosh\beta E_{v+,\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})}{1+\cosh\beta\xi_{v,\textbf{k}-\textbf{q}}}+\ln\frac{1+\cosh\beta E_{v-,\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})}{1+\cosh\beta\xi_{v,\textbf{k}+\textbf{q}}}\bigg].\end{split}

(S57)

At
T=0
it can be replaced with a simple integral

\mathit{\Omega}(\textbf{q},\hat{\Delta}_{\textbf{q}},\hat{\Delta}_{\textbf{q}}^{*},\mu_{\textbf{q}})=2\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\Lambda_{K}}\big\{\big[E_{c-,\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})+\xi_{c,\textbf{k}-\textbf{q}}\big]+\big[E_{v-,\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})-\xi_{v,\textbf{k}+\textbf{q}}\big]\big\}.

(S58)

If in the superconducting state, e.g., only the valence band is doped, i.e., the chemical potential is at the valence band and the superconducting gap is smaller than the bandgaps between the valence band and other bands, then only the last two terms in Eq. (
S57
) and (
S58
) are nonzero.

IV.3
4.3 Separation into the conventional and geometric contributions

We separate the first term of Eq. (
S51
) into the conventional and geometric contributions,
\frac{\partial^{2}\mathit{\Omega}}{\partial q_{\mu}\partial q_{\nu}}\big|_{\textbf{q}=0}=D_{s,\mu\nu}^{\text{conv}}+D_{s,\mu\nu}^{\text{geo}}
. When the valence surface band is doped, single-band projection leads to the BdG Hamiltonian in the band basis

\mathcal{H}_{\text{BdG}}(\textbf{k},\textbf{q})=\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{v,\textbf{k}+\textbf{q}}&\Delta_{v,\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})\\
\Delta_{v,\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})^{*}&-\xi_{v,\textbf{k}-\textbf{q}}\end{pmatrix},

(S59)

where
\Delta_{v,\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})=\langle\psi^{(v)}_{\textbf{k}+\textbf{q}}|\hat{\Delta}|\psi^{(v)}_{\textbf{k}-\textbf{q}}\rangle
(here
\hat{\Delta}
and
\mu
are taken as
q
-independent). The Bogoliubov spectra of Eq. (
S59
) are

E_{v\pm,\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})=\frac{1}{2}\big[(\xi_{v,\textbf{k}+\textbf{q}}-\xi_{v,\textbf{k}-\textbf{q}})\pm\sqrt{(\xi_{v,\textbf{k}+\textbf{q}}+\xi_{v,\textbf{k}-\textbf{q}})^{2}+4|\Delta_{v,\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})|^{2}}\big].

(S60)

Inserting this into Eq. (
S57
), the second derivative
\frac{\partial^{2}\mathit{\Omega}}{\partial q_{\mu}\partial q_{\nu}}
(the first term of Eq. (
S51
)) can be separated into two contributions

\begin{split}&D_{s,\mu\nu}^{\text{conv}}=-2\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\Lambda_{K}}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{\xi_{v,\textbf{k}}}{E_{v,\textbf{k}}}\tanh\frac{\beta E_{v,\textbf{k}}}{2}-\tanh\frac{\beta\xi_{v,\textbf{k}}}{2}\bigg)\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\xi_{v,\textbf{k}}+\frac{\beta}{2}\bigg(\text{sech}^{2}\frac{\beta E_{v,\textbf{k}}}{2}-\text{sech}^{2}\frac{\beta\xi_{v,\textbf{k}}}{2}\bigg)\partial_{\mu}\xi_{v,\textbf{k}}\partial_{\nu}\xi_{v,\textbf{k}}\bigg],\\
&D_{s,\mu\nu}^{\text{geo}}=-2\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\Lambda_{K}}\frac{1}{2E_{v,\textbf{k}}}\tanh\frac{\beta E_{v,\textbf{k}}}{2}\partial_{q_{\mu}}\partial_{q_{\nu}}|\Delta_{v,\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})|^{2}\big|_{\textbf{q}=0},\end{split}

(S61)

where
E_{v,\textbf{k}}=E_{v+,\textbf{k}}(0)\equiv\sqrt{\xi_{v,\textbf{k}}^{2}+\Delta_{v,\textbf{k}}(0)^{2}}
is the quasiparticle energy. At zero temperature, using
\lim_{\beta\rightarrow+\infty}\text{sech}^{2}\frac{\beta\xi_{v,\textbf{k}}}{2}=\frac{4}{\beta}\delta(\xi_{v,\textbf{k}})
, they reduce to

\begin{split}&D_{s,\mu\nu}^{\text{conv}}(T=0)=-2\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\Lambda_{K}}\bigg\{\bigg[\frac{\xi_{v,\textbf{k}}}{E_{v,\textbf{k}}}-\text{sgn}(\xi_{v,\textbf{k}})\bigg]\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\xi_{v,\textbf{k}}-2\delta(\xi_{v,\textbf{k}})\partial_{\mu}\xi_{v,\textbf{k}}\partial_{\nu}\xi_{v,\textbf{k}}\bigg\}\equiv 2\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\Lambda_{K}}f_{\mu\nu}^{\text{conv}},\\
&D_{s,\mu\nu}^{\text{geo}}(T=0)=-2\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\Lambda_{K}}\frac{1}{2E_{v,\textbf{k}}}\partial_{q_{\mu}}\partial_{q_{\nu}}|\Delta_{v,\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})|^{2}\big|_{\textbf{q}=0}\equiv 2\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\Lambda_{K}}f_{\mu\nu}^{\text{geo}}.\end{split}

(S62)

Here the
\text{sgn}(\xi_{v,\textbf{k}})
and
\delta(\xi_{v,\textbf{k}})
terms combine to give a boundary term, making
D_{s,\mu\nu}^{\text{conv}}(T=0)
consistent with the usual integral formula over the entire Brillouin zone with the integrand
-\frac{\xi_{v,\textbf{k}}}{E_{v,\textbf{k}}}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\xi_{v,\textbf{k}}
. An equivalent and more convenient form is

D_{s,\mu\nu}^{\text{conv}}(T=0)=-2\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\Lambda_{K}}\bigg(\frac{\xi_{v,\textbf{k}}}{E_{v,\textbf{k}}}+1\bigg)\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\xi_{v,\textbf{k}}.

(S63)

Attention is needed as the integrand
f_{\mu\nu}^{\text{conv}}
depends on the choice of the boundary term
\mathit{\Omega}_{0}
. A physically meaningful integrand satisfies
f_{\mu\nu}^{\text{conv}}(\textbf{k})\propto\partial_{\mu}\xi_{v,\textbf{k}}
and vanishes outside the cutoff region. The term
f_{\mu\nu}^{\text{geo}}
has the following expression
[
87
]
:

\begin{split}-\frac{1}{2E_{v,\textbf{k}}}\partial_{q_{\mu}}\partial_{q_{\nu}}|\Delta_{v,\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})|^{2}\big|_{\textbf{q}=0}=&-\frac{1}{2E_{v,\textbf{k}}}\partial_{q_{\mu}}\partial_{q_{\nu}}\text{Tr}\big\{P^{(v)}_{\textbf{k}+\textbf{q}}\hat{\Delta}P^{(v)}_{\textbf{k}-\textbf{q}}\hat{\Delta}\big\}\big|_{\textbf{q}=0}\\
=&-\frac{1}{E_{v,\textbf{k}}}\text{Tr}\big\{\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}P^{(v)}_{\textbf{k}}\hat{\Delta}P^{(v)}_{\textbf{k}}\hat{\Delta}-\partial_{\mu}P^{(v)}_{\textbf{k}}\hat{\Delta}\partial_{\nu}P^{(v)}_{\textbf{k}}\hat{\Delta}\big\}.\end{split}

(S64)

IV.4
4.4 Analysis of the geometric integrand

In this section, we analyze the integrand Eq. (
S64
): we show that (i) due to the decaying feature of the surface states and the surface-polarized pairing matrix
\hat{\Delta}=\text{diag}\{0,...,0,\Delta_{N\text{B}}\}
, Eq. (
S64
) at the center of the drumhead region is nonvanishing and approximately uniform; (ii) after including the
k
-dependence of the order parameter
\Delta_{v,\textbf{k}}
, the integrand exhibits a
1/(1-k^{2})^{2}
behavior near the center, which is similar to the QM. These analyses provide the basis for obtaining the tight lower bound of the surface superfluid weight,
D_{s,xx}^{\text{surf}}\gtrsim\Delta_{N\text{B}}k_{h}^{2}/(2\pi)
given in the MS.

For our pairing matrix
\hat{\Delta}=\text{diag}\{0,...,0,\Delta_{N\text{B}}\}
, using the projection operator in the orbital basis
P_{\alpha\beta}=\psi_{\alpha}\psi_{\beta}^{*}
, Eq. (
S64
) can be rewritten in terms of the density of the surface orbital
N
B in the band,
\rho^{(v)}_{N\text{B}}(\textbf{k})\equiv P^{(v)}_{N\text{B},N\text{B}}(\textbf{k})
,

-\partial_{q_{\mu}}\partial_{q_{\nu}}|\Delta_{v,\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})|^{2}\big|_{\textbf{q}=0}=-2\Delta_{N\text{B}}^{2}\big[\rho^{(v)}_{N\text{B}}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\rho^{(v)}_{N\text{B}}-\partial_{\mu}\rho^{(v)}_{N\text{B}}\partial_{\nu}\rho^{(v)}_{N\text{B}}\big].

(S65)

To establish the connection to the QM, we first notice that when the pairing is uniform, i.e., when
\hat{\Delta}
is proportional to the identity matrix,
\hat{\Delta}=\Delta_{0}\hat{\mathcal{I}}
, Eq. (
S64
) reduces to the QM, as expected. This can be seen from

\text{Tr}\{\partial_{\mu}P\partial_{\nu}P-P\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}P\}=\text{Tr}\{\partial_{\mu}P\partial_{\nu}P-\partial_{\mu}(P\partial_{\nu}P)+\partial_{\mu}P\partial_{\nu}P\}=2\text{Tr}\{\partial_{\mu}P\partial_{\nu}P\}=4g_{\mu\nu},

(S66)

since
\text{Tr}\{\partial_{\mu}(P\partial_{\nu}P)\}=\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}P=0
and
g_{\mu\nu}=\text{Re}\text{Tr}\{P\partial_{\mu}P\partial_{\nu}P\}=\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}\{\partial_{\mu}P\partial_{\nu}P\}
.

Using the valence band wavefunction
\psi^{(2)}
from Eq. (
S11
), we expand Eq. (
S66
) into the orbital basis,

\begin{split}&\text{Tr}\{\partial_{\mu}P^{(v)}\partial_{\nu}P^{(v)}-P^{(v)}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}P^{(v)}\}=\sum_{\alpha\beta}(\partial_{\mu}P^{(v)}_{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\nu}P^{(v)}_{\beta\alpha}-P^{(v)}_{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}P^{(v)}_{\beta\alpha})\\
=&\sum_{z,z^{\prime}=1}^{N}\partial_{\mu}\bigg[\frac{1-k^{2}}{k^{2}}e^{\kappa^{*}(N+1-z)+\kappa(N+1-z^{\prime})}\bigg]\partial_{\nu}\bigg[\frac{1-k^{2}}{k^{2}}e^{\kappa^{*}(N+1-z^{\prime})+\kappa(N+1-z)}\bigg]\\
&-\sum_{z,z^{\prime}=1}^{N}\bigg[\frac{1-k^{2}}{k^{2}}e^{\kappa^{*}(N+1-z)+\kappa(N+1-z^{\prime})}\bigg]\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\bigg[\frac{1-k^{2}}{k^{2}}e^{\kappa^{*}(N+1-z^{\prime})+\kappa(N+1-z)}\bigg].\end{split}

(S67)

Near the center of the drumhead region, the diagonal components of Eq. (
S67
) are
2-(-2)=4
, which is consistent with the QM
g^{(v)}_{xx}=g^{(v)}_{yy}=1
that is computed from Eq. (
S14
). Notice that the surface orbital term,
z=z^{\prime}=N
in Eq. (
S67
), is sufficient for calculating Eq. (
S65
), and it evaluates to exactly half of the total of Eq. (
S67
), i.e., at
\textbf{k}=0
,

\partial_{\mu}\rho^{(v)}_{N\text{B}}\partial_{\nu}\rho^{(v)}_{N\text{B}}-\rho^{(v)}_{N\text{B}}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\rho^{(v)}_{N\text{B}}\big|_{\textbf{k}=0}=0-(-2)=2=2g_{\mu\nu}^{(v)}(0),

(S68)

leading to
f^{\text{geo}}_{\mu\nu}(\textbf{k})\simeq 2\Delta_{N\text{B}}^{2}/E_{v,\textbf{k}}
, which explains why the geometric integrand is approximately uniform near the center.

Next, using Eq. (
S65
) and the density
\rho^{(v)}_{N\text{B}}(\textbf{k})=1-k^{2}
of state
\psi^{(2)}
, the surface orbital term
z=z^{\prime}=N
in the entire drumhead region for a many-layer RG (
N\rightarrow\infty
) can also be obtained analytically:

\partial_{\mu}\rho^{(v)}_{N\text{B}}\partial_{\nu}\rho^{(v)}_{N\text{B}}-\rho^{(v)}_{N\text{B}}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\rho^{(v)}_{N\text{B}}=2(1-k^{2})\delta_{\mu\nu}+4k_{\mu}k_{\nu}.

(S69)

When the chemical potential is at the edge of the flat valence band, the quasiparticle energy
E_{v,\textbf{k}}=\Delta_{v,\textbf{k}}=\Delta_{N\text{B}}(1-k^{2})
. Then the geometric integrand in the entire drumhead region has the expression

f_{\mu\nu}^{\text{geo}}(\textbf{k})=\frac{4\Delta_{N\text{B}}^{2}[(1-k^{2})\delta_{\mu\nu}+2k_{\mu}k_{\nu}]}{2\Delta_{N\text{B}}(1-k^{2})}=2\Delta_{N\text{B}}\bigg(\delta_{\mu\nu}+\frac{2}{1-k^{2}}k_{\mu}k_{\nu}\bigg).

(S70)

At
k_{y}=0
and small
k_{x}
,

f_{xx}^{\text{geo}}(k_{x},k_{y}=0)=2\Delta_{N\text{B}}\frac{1+k_{x}^{2}}{1-k_{x}^{2}}\simeq 2\Delta_{N\text{B}}\frac{1}{(1-k_{x}^{2})^{2}},

(S71)

is indeed a slowly varying function, which has a similar
k
-dependence as
g^{(v)}_{xx}
. An alternative way to analyze
f_{\mu\nu}^{\text{geo}}(\textbf{k})
is to compute its eigenvalues and eigenvectors. The two eigenvalues are
d_{1}(\textbf{k})=2\Delta_{N\text{B}}
and
d_{2}(\textbf{k})=2\Delta_{N\text{B}}\frac{1+k^{2}}{1-k^{2}}
, with eigenvectors
\textbf{v}_{1}=(-k_{y},k_{x})
and
\textbf{v}_{2}=(k_{x},k_{y})
, respectively. At the center of the drumhead region, the supercurrent response is isotropic (
d_{1}=d_{2}
); as it moves away from the center, the local supercurrent response by a state with momentum
k
gets polarized to vector potential
A
that is parallel to
k
as
d_{2}>d_{1}
(however, the total supercurrent after the
k
-space integral is not polarized to any particular direction), with the eigenvalue
d_{2}\propto\frac{1+k^{2}}{1-k^{2}}\simeq\frac{1}{(1-k^{2})^{2}}
.

The nonvanishing uniform
f^{\text{geo}}_{\mu\nu}
implies a lower-bounded surface superfluid weight per valley,

D_{s,xx}^{\text{surf}}=\sum_{k<k_{h}}f_{xx}^{\text{geo}}(\textbf{k})\geq\sum_{k<k_{h}}f_{xx}^{\text{geo}}(0)=\int_{k<k_{h}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}2\Delta_{N\text{B}}g^{(v)}_{xx}(0)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\Delta_{N\text{B}}k_{h}^{2},

(S72)

where
k_{h}
can be set to
k_{0}=1
as
N\rightarrow\infty
, and an additional prefactor of

also needs to be included (see Sec. 4.2). Here, it is interesting to note the similarity of this result to the isolated flat band isotropic superfluid weight under uniform pairing and half-filling of the band, which is

IV.6
4.6 Orbital embedding correction term for the superfluid weight,
D_{s}^{\Delta}

The geometric contribution
D_{s}^{\text{geo}}
derived from the first term of Eq. (
S51
) is generally orbital-embedding-dependent (again, the orbital embedding refers to the gauge freedom of the Bloch Hamiltonian), so an orbital-embedding correction term,
D_{s}^{\Delta}
is needed to make the total geometric superfluid weight
D_{s}^{\text{geo}}+D_{s}^{\Delta}
orbital-embedding-independent
[
86
,
128
,
88
]
. Here, we provide this information for the RG superconductivity, which can be compared with the study of the orbital-embedding-dependence of IQG in Sec. 2.3. We show that when the superconductivity is polarized to one surface of RG,
D_{s}^{\Delta}
vanishes.

After mean-field decoupling, the onsite interaction for the supercurrent state is

\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{MF}}^{(int)}(\textbf{q})=\sum_{\textbf{k},\alpha}(\Delta_{\textbf{q},\alpha}c_{\textbf{k}+\textbf{q},\alpha\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{-\textbf{k}+\textbf{q},\alpha\downarrow}^{\dagger}+h.c.)+\frac{N_{c}}{U}\sum_{\alpha}|\Delta_{\textbf{q},\alpha}|^{2},

(S73)

where the order parameter is

\Delta_{\textbf{q},\alpha}=-\frac{U}{N_{c}}\sum_{\textbf{k}}\langle c_{-\textbf{k}+\textbf{q},\alpha\downarrow}c_{\textbf{k}+\textbf{q},\alpha\uparrow}\rangle_{\textbf{q}}=-U\langle c_{\textbf{R}\alpha\downarrow}c_{\textbf{R}\alpha\uparrow}\rangle_{\textbf{q}}e^{-2i\textbf{q}\cdot(\textbf{R}+\textbf{r}_{\alpha})},

(S74)

with
\langle\rangle_{\textbf{q}}
the average in the supercurrent state
\Psi_{\textbf{q}}
. Under the orbital embedding transformation
\textbf{r}_{\alpha}\rightarrow\textbf{r}_{\alpha}+\delta\textbf{r}_{\alpha}
, operator
c_{\textbf{k}\alpha\sigma}
transforms as
c_{\textbf{k}\alpha\sigma}\rightarrow\widetilde{c}_{\textbf{k}\alpha\sigma}=c_{\textbf{k}\alpha\sigma}e^{-i\textbf{k}\cdot\delta\textbf{r}_{\alpha}}
. This gauge choice does not affect the state
\Psi_{\textbf{q}}
, so
\langle c_{\textbf{R}\alpha\downarrow}c_{\textbf{R}\alpha\uparrow}\rangle_{\textbf{q}}
is
\textbf{r}_{\alpha}
-independent. Then one can verify that the mean-field interaction Eq. (
S73
) is invariant under this
U(1)\times...\times U(1)
transformation. Comparing with the orbital embedding transformation of IQG, Eq. (
S24
), here the number of copies of
U(1)
is restricted to the number of orbitals with nonzero order parameters
\Delta_{\alpha}
instead of the number of orbitals with nonzero weight
|\psi_{\alpha}|^{2}
in the band.

To compute
D_{s}^{\Delta}
, we write down the gap equation for the supercurrent state in the orbital basis:

\Delta_{\textbf{q},\alpha}=\frac{U}{N_{c}}\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\Lambda_{K}}\frac{\psi^{(v)*}_{\textbf{k}-\textbf{q},\alpha}\psi^{(v)}_{\textbf{k}+\textbf{q},\alpha}\Delta_{v,\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})}{\sqrt{(\xi_{v,\textbf{k}+\textbf{q}}+\xi_{v,\textbf{k}-\textbf{q}})^{2}+4|\Delta_{v,\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})|^{2}}}\bigg(\tanh\frac{\beta E_{v+,\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})}{2}-\tanh\frac{\beta E_{v-,\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})}{2}\bigg),

(S75)

where the Bogoliubov spectrum
E_{v\pm,\textbf{k}}(\textbf{q})
are given by Eq. (
S60
). Computing the derivative of Eq. (
S75
) leads to an equation
\sum_{\beta}M_{\alpha\beta}\,\mathrm{d}_{\mu}\Delta_{\beta}=V_{\alpha,\mu}
(where
M_{\alpha\beta}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\mathit{\Omega}}{\partial\Delta_{\textbf{q},\alpha}^{I}\partial\Delta_{\textbf{q},\beta}^{I}}\big|_{\textbf{q}=0}
,
V_{\alpha,\mu}\equiv-\frac{\partial^{2}\mathit{\Omega}}{\partial\Delta_{\textbf{q},\alpha}^{*}\partial q_{\mu}}\big|_{\textbf{q}=0}
, and
\,\mathrm{d}_{\mu}\Delta_{\beta}\equiv\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\Delta_{\textbf{q},\beta}}{\,\mathrm{d}q_{\mu}}\big|_{\textbf{q}=0}
), with
[
88
]

M_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{\delta_{\alpha\beta}}{\Delta_{\alpha}}\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\Lambda_{K}}\frac{\Delta_{v,\textbf{k}}(0)}{E_{v,\textbf{k}}}\tanh\frac{\beta E_{v,\textbf{k}}}{2}|\psi^{(v)}_{\textbf{k},\beta}|^{2}-\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\Lambda_{K}}\frac{1}{E_{v,\textbf{k}}}\tanh\frac{\beta E_{v,\textbf{k}}}{2}|\psi^{(v)}_{\textbf{k},\alpha}|^{2}|\psi^{(v)}_{\textbf{k},\beta}|^{2},

(S76)

V_{\alpha,\mu}=\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\Lambda_{K}}\frac{1}{E_{v,\textbf{k}}}\tanh\frac{\beta E_{v,\textbf{k}}}{2}\big[(\psi^{(v)*}_{\textbf{k},\alpha}\partial_{\mu}\psi^{(v)}_{\textbf{k},\alpha}-\partial_{\mu}\psi^{(v)*}_{\textbf{k},\alpha}\psi^{(v)}_{\textbf{k},\alpha})\Delta_{v,\textbf{k}}(0)-|\psi^{(v)}_{\textbf{k},\alpha}|^{2}(\langle\psi^{(v)}_{\textbf{k}}|\hat{\Delta}|\partial_{\mu}\psi^{(v)}_{\textbf{k}}\rangle-\langle\partial_{\mu}\psi^{(v)}_{\textbf{k}}|\hat{\Delta}|\psi^{(v)}_{\textbf{k}}\rangle)\big].

(S77)

Using Eq. (
S76
), the diagonal entries of
M
are

M_{\alpha\alpha}=\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\Lambda_{K}}\sum_{\beta\neq\alpha}|\psi^{(v)}_{\textbf{k},\alpha}|^{2}|\psi^{(v)}_{\textbf{k},\beta}|^{2}\frac{\Delta_{\beta}}{\Delta_{\alpha}}\frac{\tanh(\beta E_{v,\textbf{k}}/2)}{E_{v,\textbf{k}}}.

(S78)

Assuming that the only nonzero order parameters are
\Delta_{1\text{A}}
and
\Delta_{N\text{B}}
, which means that the orbital embedding reduces to a
U(1)\times U(1)
symmetry of the mean-field Hamiltonian. Then
M_{\alpha\alpha}
for the other
2N-2
orbitals,
\alpha\in\{1\text{B},...,N\text{A}\}
diverge. However,
V_{\alpha,\mu}=\sum_{\beta}M_{\alpha\beta}\,\mathrm{d}_{\mu}\Delta_{\beta}
remains finite (as the r.h.s. of Eq. (
S77
) is finite) due to
\,\mathrm{d}_{\mu}\Delta_{\alpha}=0
for these
(2N-2)
orbitals. As a result,
D_{s,\mu\nu}^{\Delta}=2\sum_{\alpha}V_{\alpha,\mu}\,\mathrm{d}_{\nu}\Delta_{\alpha}
is always finite.

This suggests that one can restrict the summation of orbitals in the second term of Eq. (
S51
) to orbitals 1A and
N
B only:

D_{s,\mu\nu}^{\Delta}=-\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1\text{A},N\text{B}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\Delta^{I}_{\textbf{q},\alpha}}{\,\mathrm{d}q_{\mu}}\frac{\partial^{2}\mathit{\Omega}}{\partial\Delta^{I}_{\textbf{q},\alpha}\partial\Delta^{I}_{\textbf{q},\beta}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\Delta^{I}_{\textbf{q},\beta}}{\,\mathrm{d}q_{\nu}}\bigg|_{\textbf{q}=0}=2\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1\text{A},N\text{B}}\,\mathrm{d}_{\mu}\Delta_{\alpha}M_{\alpha\beta}\,\mathrm{d}_{\nu}\Delta_{\beta}.

(S79)

Let us denote the restriction of the matrix
M
to these two orbitals as
M_{R}
(a 2 by 2 matrix), and similarly, the vector
V
to these two orbitals as
V_{R}
(a 2-dim vector). The matrix
M_{R}
has a kernel eigenvector
\textbf{v}_{0}=(\Delta_{1\text{A}},\Delta_{N\text{B}})
, which enables us to eliminate, e.g., the second row and column of
M_{R}
and the second component of
V_{R}
. Finally, this gives the simple expression

D_{s,\mu\nu}^{\Delta}=2\frac{V_{1\text{A},\mu}V_{1\text{A},\nu}}{M_{1\text{A},1\text{A}}}.

(S80)

with

M_{1\text{A},1\text{A}}=\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\Lambda_{K}}|\psi^{(v)}_{\textbf{k},1\text{A}}|^{2}|\psi^{(v)}_{\textbf{k},N\text{B}}|^{2}\frac{\Delta_{N\text{B}}}{\Delta_{1\text{A}}}\frac{\tanh(\beta E_{v,\textbf{k}}/2)}{E_{v,\textbf{k}}},

(S81)

V_{1\text{A},\mu}=\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\Lambda_{K}}\frac{\Delta_{N\text{B}}}{E_{v,\textbf{k}}}\tanh\frac{\beta E_{v,\textbf{k}}}{2}\big[|\psi^{(v)}_{\textbf{k},N\text{B}}|^{2}(\psi^{(v)*}_{\textbf{k},1\text{A}}\partial_{\mu}\psi^{(v)}_{\textbf{k},1\text{A}}-\partial_{\mu}\psi^{(v)*}_{\textbf{k},1\text{A}}\psi^{(v)}_{\textbf{k},1\text{A}})-|\psi^{(v)}_{\textbf{k},1\text{A}}|^{2}(\psi^{(v)*}_{\textbf{k},N\text{B}}\partial_{\mu}\psi^{(v)}_{\textbf{k},N\text{B}}-\partial_{\mu}\psi^{(v)*}_{\textbf{k},N\text{B}}\psi^{(v)}_{\textbf{k},N\text{B}})\big].

(S82)

Inserting these into Eq. (
S80
), we find
D_{s,\mu\nu}^{\Delta}\propto\Delta_{1\text{A}}\Delta_{N\text{B}}
, which vanishes if either one of the two orders vanishes.

To conclude, if the superconducting order parameters of RG are nonzero on both surfaces, then a nonzero
D_{s,\mu\nu}^{\Delta}
makes the total geometric superfluid weight,
D_{s}^{\text{geo}}+D_{s,\mu\nu}^{\Delta}
orbital-embedding-independent; otherwise, if the order parameter is polarized to only one surface, then
D_{s,\mu\nu}^{\Delta}
vanishes and the term
D_{s}^{\text{geo}}
itself is orbital-embedding-independent.

V.2
5.2 Surface polarization effect on the gap equation

We explain the surface polarization effect on the gap equation of RTG SC1 phase. We show that the surface polarization by the displacement field contributes to an additional factor of 2 in the coupling constant; therefore, it aids the superconducting transition. In the expression of the superconducting gap, the dependence on the displacement field has two sources—the DOS and the surface orbital weight in the band.

Due to the weak-coupling nature, RTG SC1 is a multi-orbital superconductor where only the valence band is active within the attractive interaction scale. The only relevant order parameters are those of the two surface orbitals,
\Delta_{1\text{A}}
and
\Delta_{3\text{B}}
, which can be solved from the following coupled gap equations:

\begin{split}&\Delta_{1\text{A}}=\frac{U}{N_{c}}\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\Lambda_{K}}|\psi^{(v)}_{\textbf{k},1\text{A}}|^{2}\frac{\Delta_{v,\textbf{k}}}{\sqrt{\xi_{v,\textbf{k}}^{2}+\Delta_{v,\textbf{k}}^{2}}},\\
&\Delta_{3\text{B}}=\frac{U}{N_{c}}\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\Lambda_{K}}|\psi^{(v)}_{\textbf{k},3\text{B}}|^{2}\frac{\Delta_{v,\textbf{k}}}{\sqrt{\xi_{v,\textbf{k}}^{2}+\Delta_{v,\textbf{k}}^{2}}},\end{split}

(S84)

where

\Delta_{v,\textbf{k}}=|\psi^{(v)}_{\textbf{k},1\text{A}}|^{2}\Delta_{1\text{A}}+|\psi^{(v)}_{\textbf{k},3\text{B}}|^{2}\Delta_{3\text{B}}=\rho^{(v)}_{1\text{A}}(\textbf{k})\Delta_{1\text{A}}+\rho^{(v)}_{3\text{B}}(\textbf{k})\Delta_{3\text{B}}

(S85)

is the order parameter in the band basis, which couples
\Delta_{1\text{A}}
and
\Delta_{3\text{B}}
through the weight of orbital 1A (
\rho^{(v)}_{1\text{A}}(\textbf{k})
) and 3B (
\rho^{(v)}_{3\text{B}}(\textbf{k})
) in the valence band.

The solution to Eq. (
S84
) can be accessed if we notice that one order parameter, e.g.,
\Delta_{1\text{A}}
vanishes throughout the metal-superconductor transition. At zero displacement field,
m=0
, it vanishes because SC1 is not activated; when SC1 is activated at a large displacement field, it also vanishes since the orbital weight
\rho^{(v)}_{1\text{A}}(\textbf{k})
is quenched by the surface polarization. As a result, Eq. (
S84
) reduces to a gap equation involving
\Delta_{3\text{B}}
only:

\Delta_{3\text{B}}=\frac{U}{N_{c}}\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\Lambda_{K}}\frac{(\rho^{(v)}_{3\text{B}})^{2}\Delta_{3\text{B}}}{\sqrt{\xi_{v,\textbf{k}}^{2}+(\rho^{(v)}_{3\text{B}})^{2}\Delta_{3\text{B}}^{2}}},

(S86)

where we have made the approximation that
\rho^{(v)}_{3\text{B}}
is
k
-independent near the VHS, but depends on the surface potential
m
explicitly. Then, Eq. (
S86
) can be solved to give the order parameter as a function of
m
,

\Delta_{3\text{B}}(m)=\frac{2\omega_{c}}{\rho^{(v)}_{3\text{B}}(m)}\exp\bigg\{-\frac{1}{2\rho^{(v)}_{3\text{B}}(m)D_{0}(m)U}\bigg\}.

(S87)

Here,
\omega_{c}
is the cutoff energy around the Fermi surface where the interaction is attractive, and
D_{0}=\frac{1}{N_{c}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}N_{0}(\xi)}{\,\mathrm{d}\xi}\big|_{\xi=0}
is the DOS at the Fermi surface. It is assumed that
\omega_{c}\gg\Delta_{3\text{B}}
(note:
\Delta_{3\text{B}}\sim 10^{-4}\gamma_{1}
for SC1) but
\omega_{c}
is smaller than any band structure energy scale of RTG, so
D_{0}
can be treated as a constant in the integral. We adopted interpretation 2 from Sec. 5.1 above, where SC1 is located at the edge of the VHS rather than at the VHS singular point, such that
D_{0}
is finite, but is enhanced as
m
increases. We also assumed that
U
does not change as
m
varies (in reality, of course, it may renormalize).

V.3
5.3 Superfluid weight of SC1

We briefly discuss the superfluid weight of SC1 of RTG, although SC1 is more like a conventional-type superconductor, which does not align with the superconducting state of many-layer RG as discussed in the MS.

Before calculating the superfluid weight, let us estimate the order of magnitude of different terms contributing to it for SC1. The conventional term
D_{s}^{\text{conv}}
does not scale with
\Delta_{\alpha}
if the coupling is weak and if
\mu
measured from the band edge is much larger than
\Delta_{\alpha}
; the geometric term is always linear in the order parameter, so
D_{s}^{\text{geo}}\propto\Delta_{\alpha}
; whereas from Sec. 4.5 we know
D_{s}^{\Delta}\propto\Delta_{1\text{A}}\Delta_{N\text{B}}
. For SC1 of RTG,
\Delta_{1\text{A}}=0
due to the polarization, so
D_{s}^{\Delta}=0
. Meanwhile,
\Delta_{3\text{B}}\sim 10^{-4}\gamma_{1}
is the smallest energy scale in the system, making
D_{s}^{\text{geo}}\ll D_{s}^{\text{conv}}
.

In Fig.
S12
, we present the numerical calculation results for the superfluid weight
D_{s}
of SC1 of RTG, at
m=0.06\gamma_{1}=23
meV. Since the exact value of the order parameter may not be obtained from the transition temperature
T_{c}
directly (there could be phase fluctuations), we calculate
D_{s}
for a set of
\Delta_{3\text{B}}
values of the order of
10^{-4}\gamma_{1}
. We also allow chemical potential
\mu
to change around the VHS, including aligned with the singular point and the edge of it. It shows that
D_{s}
does not scale with
\Delta_{3\text{B}}
, which means it is purely conventional. Even when
D_{s}
depends on
\mu
, the ratio of
D_{s}/\Delta_{3\text{B}}
is of the order of 100 (besides an additional factor of 54.9, see Sec. 4.2), regardless of the value of
\Delta_{3\text{B}}
and
\mu
, indicating almost zero phase fluctuation in SC1
[
122
]
. Therefore, the Berezinskii-Kosterlitz-Thouless transition temperature is the same as the mean-field critical temperature.

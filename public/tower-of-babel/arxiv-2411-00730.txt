Quasimodules over bounded lattices
Ivan Chajda, Helmut Länger

Abstract
We define a quasimodule 𝐐 \mathbf{Q} over a bounded lattice 𝐋 \mathbf{L} in an analogous way as a module over a semiring is defined. The essential difference is that 𝐋 \mathbf{L} need not be distributive. Also for quasimodules there can be introduced the concepts of inner product, orthogonal elements, orthogonal subsets, bases and closed subquasimodules. We show that the set of all closed subquasimodules forms a complete lattice having orthogonality as an antitone involution. Using the Galois connection induced by this orthogonality, we describe important properties of closed subquasimodules. We call a subquasimodule 𝐏 \mathbf{P} of a quasimodule 𝐐 \mathbf{Q} splitting if the sum of P P and its orthogonal companion is the whole set Q Q and the intersection of P P and its orthogonal companion is trivial. We show that every splitting subquasimodule is closed and that its orthogonal companion is splitting, too. Our results are illuminated by several examples.

For canonical quasimodules we can introduce the concept of inner product and orthogonality.

Definition 8
.

Let
(Q,+,\cdot,\vec{0})=\prod\limits_{i\in I}\mathbf{L}_{i}
be a canonical quasimodule over a complete lattice and
A\subseteq Q
. Define the inner product of
(a_{i};i\in I),(b_{i};i\in I)\in Q
by

(a_{i};i\in I)(b_{i};i\in I):=\bigvee_{i\in I}(a_{i}\wedge b_{i})

and a binary relation
\perp
on
Q
, the so-called orthogonality, by

\vec{x}\perp\vec{y}\text{ if and only if }\vec{x}\vec{y}=0

(
\vec{x},\vec{y}\in Q
)
. Moreover, put

A^{\perp}:=\{\vec{x}\in Q\mid\vec{x}\perp\vec{y}\text{ for all }\vec{y}\in A\},

especially,

\vec{y}^{\perp}:=\{\vec{x}\in Q\mid\vec{x}\perp\vec{y}\}

(
\vec{y}\in Q
)
. The set
A
is called orthogonal if its elements are pairwise orthogonal, and it is called an orthogonal basis if it is both orthogonal and a basis.

Remark 9
.

Obviously,
(a_{i};i\in I)\perp(b_{i};i\in I)
if and only if
a_{i}\wedge b_{i}=0
for all
i\in I
. Hence orthogonality of two elements of

can be defined also in the case when
\mathbf{L}
is not complete.

Lemma 10
.

Let
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge,0,1)
be a bounded lattice and
q_{i}\in L
for all
i\in I
. Further, let
\mathbf{Q}=(Q,+,\cdot,\vec{0})=\prod\limits_{i\in I}[0,q_{i}]
be a canonical quasimodule over
\mathbf{L}
, let
\vec{x},\vec{y}\in Q
and assume
\vec{x}\vec{z}=\vec{y}\vec{z}
for all
\vec{z}\in Q
. Then
\vec{x}=\vec{y}
.

Proof.

If for all
i\in I
the element
\vec{b}_{i}
of
Q
is defined as in Theorem
7
then
p_{i}(\vec{x})=\vec{x}\vec{b}_{i}=\vec{y}\vec{b}_{i}=p_{i}(\vec{y})

, that is,

.
∎

\mathbf{Q}=\prod\limits_{i\in I}\mathbf{L}_{i}

0
-distributive
\mathbf{L}_{i}=(L_{i},\vee,\wedge,0)
and
P\subseteq Q
. Then
(P,+,\cdot,\vec{0})
is a closed subquasimodule of
\mathbf{Q}
if and only if for every
i\in I
there exists some closed subquasimodule
(P_{i},\vee,\wedge,0)
of
(L_{i},\vee,\wedge,0)
with
\prod\limits_{i\in I}P_{i}=P
.

Proof.

First assume
(P,+,\cdot,\vec{0})
to be a closed subquasimodule of
\mathbf{Q}
. According to Lemma
18
we have

\displaystyle P^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i\in I}\big(p_{i}(P)\big)^{\perp},

\displaystyle P^{\perp\perp}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i\in I}\big(p_{i}(P^{\perp})\big)^{\perp}=\prod_{i\in I}\big(p_{i}(P)\big)^{\perp\perp}.

Because of

P^{\perp\perp}=P\subseteq\prod_{i\in I}p_{i}(P)\subseteq\prod_{i\in I}\big(p_{i}(P)\big)^{\perp\perp}

according to (i) of Remark
14
we conclude

P=\prod_{i\in I}p_{i}(P)=\prod_{i\in I}\big(p_{i}(P)\big)^{\perp\perp}

which together with
p_{i}(P)\subseteq\big(p_{i}(P)\big)^{\perp\perp}
for all
i\in I
(again according to (i) of Remark
14
) implies
p_{i}(P)=\big(p_{i}(P)\big)^{\perp\perp}
for all
i\in I
, i.e.,
p_{i}(P)
is a closed subset of
L_{i}
for all
i\in I
. Because of Lemma
17
we conclude that
(p_{i}(P),\vee,\wedge,0)
is a closed subquasimodule of
(L_{i},\vee,\wedge,0)
for all
i\in I
. If, conversely, for every
i\in I
there exists some closed subquasimodule
(P_{i},\vee,\wedge,0)
of
(L_{i},\vee,\wedge,0)
with
\prod\limits_{i\in I}P_{i}=P
then

\displaystyle P^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i\in I}\big(p_{i}(P)\big)^{\perp}=\prod_{i\in I}P_{i}^{\perp},

\displaystyle P^{\perp\perp}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i\in I}\big(p_{i}(P^{\perp})\big)^{\perp}=\prod_{i\in I}P_{i}^{\perp\perp}=\prod_{i\in I}P_{i}=P,

i.e.,
P
is a closed subset of
Q
and, moreover,
(P,+,\cdot,\vec{0})
is a closed subquasimodule of
\mathbf{Q}
because of Lemma
17
.
∎

For the lattice of closed subquasimodules, the following result plays an essential role.

Corollary 20
.

Let
\mathbf{Q}=\prod\limits_{i\in I}\mathbf{L}_{i}
be a canonical quasimodule with
0
-distributive
\mathbf{L}_{i}
. Then
\mathbf{L}_{C}(\mathbf{Q})\cong\prod\limits_{i\in I}\mathbf{L}_{C}(\mathbf{L}_{i})
.

Proof.

From Theorem
19
we see that the mapping
(P_{i};i\in I)\mapsto\prod\limits_{i\in I}P_{i}
is a bijection from
\prod\limits_{i\in I}L_{C}(\mathbf{L}_{i})
to
L_{C}(\mathbf{Q})
. Since for
(P_{i};i\in I),(Q_{i};i\in I)\in\prod\limits_{i\in I}L_{C}(\mathbf{L}_{i})
we have
\prod\limits_{i\in I}P_{i}\subseteq\prod\limits_{i\in I}Q_{i}
if and only if
P_{i}\subseteq Q_{i}
for all
i\in I
, we conclude that this bijection is an isomorphism from
\prod\limits_{i\in I}\mathbf{L}_{C}(\mathbf{L}_{i})
to
\mathbf{L}_{C}(\mathbf{Q})
.
∎

The next result solves the problem when the mapping

\mathbf{L}(\mathbf{Q})

Theorem 22
.

Let
\mathbf{Q}=(Q,+,\cdot,\vec{0})=\prod\limits_{i\in I}\mathbf{L}_{i}
be a canonical quasimodule with
0
-distributive
\mathbf{L}_{i}
. Then the following hold:

(i)

If
(P\cap R)^{\perp\perp}=P^{\perp\perp}\cap R^{\perp\perp}
for all
P,R\in L(\mathbf{Q})
then
⟂⟂
is a homomorphism from
\big(L(\mathbf{Q}),\vee,\cap,{}^{\perp},\{0\},Q\big)
onto
\big(L_{C}(\mathbf{Q}),\stackrel{{\scriptstyle c}}{{\vee}},\cap,{}^{\perp},\{0\},Q\big)
.

(ii)

If
\left(\bigcap\limits_{j\in J}P_{j}\right)^{\perp\perp}=\bigcap\limits_{j\in J}P_{j}^{\perp\perp}
for every family
P_{j},j\in J,
of subquasimodules of
\mathbf{Q}
then
⟂⟂
is a complete homomorphism from
\big(L(\mathbf{Q}),\vee,\cap,{}^{\perp},\{0\},Q\big)
onto
\big(L_{C}(\mathbf{Q}),\stackrel{{\scriptstyle c}}{{\vee}},\cap,{}^{\perp},\{0\},Q\big)
, that is,
\left(\bigvee\limits_{j\in J}P_{j}\right)^{\perp\perp}=\bigvee\limits_{j\in J}^{c}P_{j}^{\perp\perp}
and
\left(\bigcap\limits_{j\in J}P_{j}\right)^{\perp\perp}=\bigcap\limits_{j\in J}P_{j}^{\perp\perp}
for every family
P_{j},j\in J,
of subquasimodules of
\mathbf{Q}
.

Proof.

Let
P,R,P_{j}\in L(\mathbf{Q})
for every
j\in J
.

(i)

We have

\displaystyle(P\vee R)^{\perp\perp}

\displaystyle=(P\cup R)^{\perp\perp}=(P^{\perp}\cap R^{\perp})^{\perp}=P^{\perp\perp}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle c}}{{\vee}}R^{\perp\perp},

\displaystyle(P\cap R)^{\perp\perp}

\displaystyle=P^{\perp\perp}\cap R^{\perp\perp},

\displaystyle(P^{\perp})^{\perp\perp}

\displaystyle=(P^{\perp\perp})^{\perp},

\displaystyle\{0\}^{\perp\perp}

\displaystyle=\{0\},

\displaystyle Q^{\perp\perp}

\displaystyle=Q.

(ii)

We have

\displaystyle\left(\bigvee_{j\in J}P_{j}\right)^{\perp\perp}

\displaystyle=\left(\bigcup_{j\in J}P_{j}\right)^{\perp\perp}=\left(\bigcap_{j\in J}P_{j}^{\perp}\right)^{\perp}=\bigvee_{j\in J}^{c}P_{j}^{\perp\perp},

\displaystyle\left(\bigcap_{j\in J}P_{j}\right)^{\perp\perp}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{j\in J}P_{j}^{\perp\perp},

\displaystyle(P^{\perp})^{\perp\perp}

\displaystyle=(P^{\perp\perp})^{\perp},

\displaystyle\{0\}^{\perp\perp}

\displaystyle=\{0\},

\displaystyle Q^{\perp\perp}

\displaystyle=Q.

∎

We are interested in the case when for a subquasimodule
(P,+,\cdot,\vec{0})
of a quasimodule
\mathbf{Q}
,
P^{\perp}
is a complement of
P
in the lattice
\mathbf{L}(\mathbf{Q})
. For this, we introduce the following concept.

Definition 23
.

Let
\mathbf{Q}=(Q,+,\cdot,\vec{0})
be a canonical quasimodule. A subset
A
of
Q
is called splitting if
A+A^{\perp}=Q
. Here
A+A^{\perp}
means
\{\vec{x}+\vec{y}\mid\vec{x}\in A,\vec{y}\in A^{\perp}\}
. Let
L_{S}(\mathbf{Q})
denote the set of all splitting subquasimodules of
\mathbf{Q}
and put
\mathbf{L}_{S}(\mathbf{Q}):=\big(L_{S}(\mathbf{Q}),\subseteq\big)
.

Note that according to (iv) of Lemma
15
we have
A\cap A^{\perp}=\{0\}
for every non-empty subset
A
of
Q
and hence a subquasimodule
(P,+,\cdot,\vec{0})
of
\mathbf{Q}
is splitting if and only if both
P+P^{\perp}=Q
and
P\cap P^{\perp}=\{0\}
.

Example 24
.

Consider the canonical quasimodule
\mathbf{Q}:=\mathbf{N}_{5}\times\{0,a\}
from Example
21
. Then
L_{S}(\mathbf{Q})=L_{C}(\mathbf{Q})
.

However, the situation from the previous example where splitting subquasimodules coincide with closed ones does not hold in general. We can prove only that every splitting subquasimodule is closed.

Theorem 25
.

Let
\mathbf{Q}=\prod\limits_{i\in I}\mathbf{L}_{i}
be a canonical quasimodule. Then
L_{S}(\mathbf{Q})\subseteq L_{C}(\mathbf{Q})
.

Proof.

Assume
L_{S}(\mathbf{Q})\not\subseteq L_{C}(\mathbf{Q})
. Then there exists some
P\in L_{S}(\mathbf{Q})\setminus L_{C}(\mathbf{Q})
. Since
P
is not closed there exists some
\vec{x}\in P^{\perp\perp}\setminus P
. Because
P
is splitting there exist
\vec{y}\in P
and
\vec{z}\in P^{\perp}
with
\vec{y}+\vec{z}=\vec{x}
. Let
x_{i},i\in I,
denote the components of
\vec{x}
. Analogous notations we use for the components of
\vec{y}
and
\vec{z}
. Since
\vec{y}+\vec{z}=\vec{x}
we have
y_{i}\vee z_{i}=x_{i}
for all
i\in I
. Because
\vec{x}\in P^{\perp\perp}
and
\vec{z}\in P^{\perp}
we have
\vec{x}\perp\vec{z}
, that is,
x_{i}\wedge z_{i}=0
for all
i\in I
. Together we obtain
z_{i}=(y_{i}\vee z_{i})\wedge z_{i}=x_{i}\wedge z_{i}=0
for all
i\in I
, that is,
\vec{z}=\vec{0}
which implies
\vec{x}=\vec{y}+\vec{z}=\vec{y}\in P
, a contradiction. This shows
L_{S}(\mathbf{Q})\subseteq L_{C}(\mathbf{Q})
.
∎

\mathbf{Q}

0

(P^{\perp},+,\cdot,\vec{0})

Proposition 27
.

Let
\mathbf{Q}=(Q,+,\cdot,\vec{0})=\prod\limits_{i\in I}\mathbf{L}_{i}
be a canonical quasimodule with
0
-distributive
\mathbf{L}_{i}
. Then the following hold:

(i)

For every splitting subquasimodule
(P,+,\cdot,\vec{0})
of
\mathbf{Q}
the subquasimodule
(P^{\perp},+,\cdot,\vec{0})
of
\mathbf{Q}
is again splitting.

(ii)

The mapping
⟂
is an antitone mapping from
\mathbf{L}_{S}(\mathbf{Q})
to
\mathbf{L}_{C}(\mathbf{Q})
.

Proof.

Let
P\in L_{S}(\mathbf{Q})
.

(i)

We have
P+P^{\perp}=Q
. According to (i) of Remark
14
we conclude
P^{\perp}+P^{\perp\perp}=Q
showing
P^{\perp}\in P_{S}(\mathbf{Q})
.

(ii)

According to (i) of Theorem
16
we have
P^{\perp}\in L_{C}(\mathbf{Q})
. The rest of the proof follows from (ii) of Remark
14
.

∎

The final proposition shows that for a canonical quasimodule
\mathbf{Q}=\prod\limits_{i\in I}\mathbf{L}_{i}
with
\mathbf{L}_{i}=(L_{i},\vee,\wedge,0)
for all
i\in I
a direct product of certain subsets of
L_{i}
is a splitting subquasimodule of
\mathbf{Q}
if and only if its factors are splitting subquasimodules of
\mathbf{L}_{i}
.

Proposition 28
.

Let
\mathbf{Q}=(Q,+,\cdot,\vec{0})=\prod\limits_{i\in I}\mathbf{L}_{i}
with
\mathbf{L}_{i}=(L_{i},\vee,\wedge,0)
be a canonical quasimodule and
M_{i}\subseteq L_{i}
for all
i\in I
. Then
\prod\limits_{i\in I}M_{i}\in L_{S}(\mathbf{Q})
if and only if
M_{i}\in L_{S}(\mathbf{L}_{i})
for all
i\in I
.

Proof.

Because of Lemma
17
we have
\prod\limits_{i\in I}M_{i}\in L(\mathbf{Q})
if and only if
M_{i}\in L(\mathbf{L}_{i})
for all
i\in I
. Now assume
\prod\limits_{i\in I}M_{i}\in L(\mathbf{Q})
. Then according to Lemma
18
the following are equivalent:

\displaystyle\prod_{i\in I}M_{i}

\displaystyle\in L_{S}(\mathbf{Q}),

\displaystyle\prod_{i\in I}M_{i}+\left(\prod_{i\in I}M_{i}\right)^{\perp}

\displaystyle=Q,

\displaystyle\prod_{i\in I}M_{i}+\prod_{i\in I}M_{i}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=Q,

\displaystyle\prod_{i\in I}(M_{i}\vee M_{i}^{\perp})

\displaystyle=Q,

\displaystyle M_{i}\vee M_{i}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=L_{i}\text{ for all }i\in I,

\displaystyle M_{i}

\displaystyle\in L_{s}(\mathbf{L}_{i})\text{ for all }i\in I.

∎

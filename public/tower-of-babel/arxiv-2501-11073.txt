Poset probability in two-row partition posets
Albin Jaldevik, Jan Snellman

Abstract
We find explicit formulae for poset probabilities ℙ ⁡ ( P λ , α < β ) \mathbb{P}(P_{\lambda};\alpha<\beta) in partition posets (cell posets) P λ P_{\lambda} when λ = ( λ 1 , λ 2 ) \lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}) is a two-row partition. These probabilities are given as rational expressions in f σ / τ f^{\sigma/\tau} , where τ ⊆ σ ⊆ λ \tau\subseteq\sigma\subseteq\lambda . We then use well-known formulae, such as the hook-length formula for f λ f^{\lambda} , the number of standard Young tableaux on a partition λ \lambda , and the corresponding determinantal formula by Jacobi-Trudi-Aitken for f λ / μ f^{\lambda/\mu} , the number of standard Young tableaux on a skew partition λ / μ \lambda/\mu , to make the aforementioned expressions explicit. We also calculate the limit probabilities of ℙ ⁡ ( P λ , α < β ) \mathbb{P}(P_{\lambda};\alpha<\beta) when the elements α , β \alpha,\beta are fixed cells, but the arm-lengths of λ = ( λ 1 , λ 2 ) \lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}) tend to infinity with bounded difference λ 1 − λ 2 \lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2} .

Introduction

1.1
Poset elementa

We will use
\subset
for strict inclusion, and
\subseteq
for non-strict inclusion.
For a poset
P=(P,\leq)
we similarly use
<
for the
strict part
, i.e.,

x<y
iff
x\leq y
and
x\neq y
. The
reverse relation
is
y\geq x\iff x\leq y
.

A subset
U\subseteq P
is an
order ideal
if
u\in U,v\in P,v\leq u

implies that
v\in U
.
We write
U\subseteq_{I}P
to indicate that
U
is an order ideal of
P
.
For
x\in P
, the subset
\Lambda_{P}(x)=\{y\in P\mid y\leq x\}

is the principal (closed) order ideal of
x
.
Dually, a subset
V\subseteq P
is an
order filter
if
v\in V,w\in P,v\leq w

implies that
w\in V
, and
V_{P}(x)=\{y\in P\mid y\geq x\}

is the principal (closed) order filter of
x
.

The poset
P
is a
lattice
if every two-element subset
\left\{{a,b}\right\}\subset P

has a least upper bound
a\vee b
and an greatest lower bound
a\wedge b
. A lattice
is
distributive
if the identities

\begin{split}a\wedge(b\vee c)&=(a\wedge b)\vee(a\wedge c)\\
a\vee(b\wedge c)&=(a\vee b)\wedge(a\vee c)\end{split}

(1)

hold for all
a,b,c\in P
.

A subset
C\subseteq P
is a
chain
if
c,d\in C
implies that

c\leq d
or
d\leq c
. A chain is
saturated
(or
maximal
) if
adding any element from
P\setminus C
makes it no longer a chain.
If the subset
P\subseteq P
is a chain, then we say that the poset
P
is a chain.

If
(Q,\leq_{Q})
is another poset, then the
product

P\times Q
has the Cartesian product
as its underlying set, and order relation

(a,b)\preceq(c,d)\quad\iff\quad a\leq c\text{ and }b\leq_{Q}d.

A product of chains is called a
chain product
.

The poset
(\mathbb{N},\leq)
, with
\leq
the standard ordering, is a chain, denoted by
C_{\infty}
.
For a positive integer
n
, the subset
[n]=\left\{{1,2,\dots,n}\right\}\subset\mathbb{N}
is a chain (regarded as a subposet
of
C_{\infty}
), which we denote by
C_{n}
.

Now suppose that
P
is finite, with
|P|=n
.
By
\ell(P)
we mean the number of saturated chains in
P
.
Denote by
\mathcal{L}_{P}
the set of
linear extensions
(or
total extensions
) of
P
, that is, the set
of all bijections

\phi:C_{n}\to P

(2)

with order-preserving inverse;
this corresponds to a sequence

\phi(1),\phi(2),\cdots,\phi(n),\qquad i<j\implies(\phi(i)\not>\phi(j)).

(3)

Denote by
e(P)=\lvert\mathcal{L}_{P}\rvert
the cardinality of this set, and,
for
\alpha,\beta\in P
,
denote by
\mathcal{L}_{P}(\alpha<\beta)
the subset consisting
of those linear extensions that place
\alpha
before
\beta
;
such an extension may be represented as

\phi(1),\,\phi(2),\,\cdots,\,\phi(k)=\alpha,\,\phi(k+1),\,\cdots,\,\phi(\ell)=\beta,\,\phi(\ell+1),\,\cdots,\,\phi(n).

(4)

Denote by
e(P;\alpha<\beta)=\lvert\mathcal{L}_{P}(\alpha<\beta)\rvert
the number of such linear extensions.

1.2
Poset probability

We define the
probability
of
\alpha
preceding
\beta
as

\mathbb{P}(P;\alpha<\beta)=\frac{e(P;\alpha<\beta)}{e(P)}.

(5)

If
\alpha<\beta
then
\mathbb{P}(P;\alpha<\beta)=1
, and if
\alpha\geq\beta
then
\mathbb{P}(P;\alpha<\beta)=0
.
The nontrivial case is when
\alpha,\beta\in P
are incomparable (written
x\parallel y
);
then there is at least one linear extension of
P
placing
\alpha
before
\beta
,
and at least one placing
\alpha
after
\beta
. In other words,

0<\mathbb{P}(P;\alpha<\beta)<1
if and only if
\alpha\parallel\beta
.

We want to find
\mathcal{L}_{P}(\alpha<\beta)
and its cardinality.
One approach, used e.g. by Sagan and Olson
[
olson20181
]
, is to
make use of the following fact:

Lemma 1.1
.

Denote by
P(\alpha<\beta)
the poset obtained by adding
(\alpha,\beta)
as a
relation to (the graph of)
P
, as well as all transitive consequences.
Then
\mathcal{L}_{P}(\alpha<\beta)
and
\mathcal{L}_{P(\alpha<\beta)}
are in
bijective correspondence, hence
e(P;\alpha<\beta)=e(P(\alpha<\beta))
.

If the poset
P(\alpha<\beta)
is easy to construct and analyse, then this is a powerful method. For instance, in Figure

is implicit in

Let
D,U
be a set partition of
P\setminus\left\{{\alpha}\right\}
,
such that
D
is an order ideal containing

,

\alpha

(AP)

and

are easily calculable. We will apply it to
partition posets
, for which there are a plethora of useful formulas for

and

.

1.3
Partition posets

A partition
\lambda
is a finite, weakly decreasing sequence of
positive integers (called
parts
)

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{d}),\qquad\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{d}>0.

(6)

The integer
d=\mathrm{length}(\lambda)
is the
length
of
\lambda
, and
the
weight
of
\lambda
is
|\lambda|=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\cdots+\lambda_{d}
.
We write
\lambda\vdash n
and say that
\lambda
is a partition of
n
.

The (Ferrers, or Young)
diagram
of
\lambda
is the set of coordinates, or boxes, or cells,

\{(i,j)\mid 1\leq i\leq d,\,1\leq j\leq\lambda_{i}\}\subset[d]\times[\lambda_{1}].

(7)

In
British notation
the diagram of
\lambda
is displayed as follows:
the cell
(i,j)
sits in row
i
, column
j
, with the rows extending downwards.
By flipping the Ferrers diagram along its main diagonal,
one obtains the
conjugate partition

\lambda^{\prime}=(\lambda_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\lambda_{s}^{\prime}),\quad\lambda_{j}^{\prime}=\max(\{i\mid\lambda_{i}\geq j\}).

(8)

Conjugation is an involution on the set of all partitions of
n
.

1.5
Poset probability for partition posets

For a partition poset
P_{\lambda}
there are several well-known formulae for counting

e(P_{\lambda})
. These methods extend to skew-partitions
\lambda/\mu
.
To count
e(P_{\lambda};\alpha<\beta)
, we use a version of (
AP
),
which now becomes

e(P_{\lambda};\alpha<\beta)=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{B}}f^{T}f^{\lambda/(T\cup\left\{{\alpha}\right\})}.

(9)

The set
\mathcal{B}
of
blocking partitions
can be found using the
decorated tableau

of
\lambda
, see Example
3.2
.

In this article, we will calculate exact values for
\mathbb{P}({P_{\lambda}};\alpha<\beta)
when
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
.
We will use this to compute the limit for
\mathbb{P}({P_{\lambda}};\alpha<\beta)

when
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
tends to
(\infty,\infty)
in such a way that
\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}
remains bounded.
The fact that this limit exists shows that it can be calculated as the limit of

\mathbb{P}({C_{m}\times C_{2}};\alpha<\beta)
as
m\to\infty
;
the poset
C_{m}\times C_{2}
is often called the “Catalan poset”.
Sagan and Olson
[
olson20181
]
proved an exact formula for
\mathbb{P}(P_{\lambda};\alpha<\beta)

when
\lambda
is of
rectangular shape
, i.e.
P_{\lambda}=C_{m}\times C_{n}
, and when
\alpha=(1,2)
,

\beta=(2,1)
.

We note that there is a decently sized body of literature on asymptotic results for probabilities on partition posets
[
Chan-catalan
,
chanSortingProbabilityLarge
]
.

Blocking ideals - filtering linear extensions

We now describe in more detail how equation (

) can be used to
find
\mathcal{L}_{P}(\alpha<\beta)
. The easy proofs
of results in this section are omitted.

2.1
Blocking ideals and linear extensions

Recall the following results from lattice theory.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
P
be a finite poset, and put

J(P)=\{U\subseteq P\,\mid\,U\subseteq_{I}P\}
.
Order these order ideals by inclusion to turn
J(P)

into a poset.

Theorem 2.2

(Birkhoff’s representation theorem)
.

The poset
J(P)
is a distributive lattice. Conversely, any
finite distributive lattice
L
is of the form
L=J(P)

for some finite poset
P
.

Proof.

See
[
BirkhoffRepr
]
.
∎

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
P
be a poset with
n<\infty
elements.
Linear extensions in
\mathcal{L}_{P}
correspond to saturated chains in
J(P)
,
by pairing the linear extension
\phi:C_{n}\to P
with the saturated chain

\emptyset\subset\{\phi(1)\}\subset\{\phi(1),\phi(2)\}\cdots\subset\{\phi(1),\dots,\phi(n)\}=P

(10)

In particular,
e(P)=\ell(J(P))
.

Proof.

See
[
stanley-enum1
]
.
∎

Now let

\sigma\in\mathcal{L}_{P}(\alpha<\beta)
, and represent it by a sequentially ordered list of elements in
P
,
as in (
4
).
We want to split this linear extension into two parts; from the beginning up
to
\alpha
, and then from
\alpha
to the end. If we pass to
J(P)
,
then the linear extension
\sigma
correspond to a
saturated chain
of order ideals in
J(P)

\emptyset\subset U_{1}\subset\cdots U_{i-1}\subset U_{i}\subset\cdots\subset U_{n}=P

(11)

where
\alpha,\beta\not\in U_{{i-1}}
,
\alpha\in U_{i}
. That is
to say,
\alpha
is introduced at stage
i
, and
\beta
at a later stage.

The order ideal
U_{i-1}
is covered by
U_{i}
, and is the last
order ideal in the chain that does not contain
\alpha
. We call it a

blocking ideal
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\alpha,\beta
be two incomparable elements in the finite poset
P
.
The
blocking ideals

\mathcal{BI}_{P}(\alpha<\beta)
is the set of order ideals of
P
without
\alpha
and
\beta
,
and where we can add
\alpha
and get a new order ideal:

\mathcal{BI}_{P}(\alpha<\beta)\triangleq\left\{\,{T\in J(P)}\,\vrule\,{\alpha\notin T,\,\beta\notin T,\,T\cup\left\{{\alpha}\right\}\in J(P)}\,\right\}.

(12)

Comparing this with equation (
AP
),
the blocking ideal
T
corresponds to
D
,
and
U
to
P\setminus(D\cup\left\{{\alpha}\right\})
.
Hence, these blocking ideals enable us to describe
\mathcal{L}_{P}(\alpha<b)
.

Definition 2.5
.

When
P
is a finite poset and
\alpha,\beta
are two incomparable elements in
P
,
the set of saturated chains in
J(P)
which add
\alpha
before
\beta

is denoted
\mathcal{CH}_{P}(\alpha<\beta)
.
By
C_{P}(\alpha<\beta)
we denote the set of chains in
J(P)
defined by

C_{P}(\alpha<\beta)\triangleq\left\{\,{\emptyset\subset T\subset T\cup\left\{{\alpha}\right\}\subset P}\,\vrule\,{T\in\mathcal{BI}_{P}(\alpha<\beta)}\,\right\}.

(13)

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
P
be a finite poset and let
\alpha,\beta\in P
be two incomparable elements.
Then the following hold:

1.

Every element in
\mathcal{CH}_{P}(\alpha<\beta)
(a saturated chain in
J(P)
adding
\alpha
before
\beta
) is a refinement of precisely one chain in
C_{P}(\alpha<\beta)
.

2.

The total number of saturated chains in
J(P)
adding
\alpha
before
\beta
can be calculated as

|\mathcal{CH}_{P}(\alpha<\beta)|=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{BI}_{P}(\alpha<\beta)}\ell(\Lambda_{J(P)}(T))\ell(V_{J(P)}(T\cup\left\{{\alpha}\right\})).

(14)

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
P
be a finite poset and let
\alpha,\beta\in P

be two incomparable elements. Then

e(P;\alpha<\beta)=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{BI}_{P}(\alpha<\beta)}e(T)e(P\setminus(T\cup\left\{{\alpha}\right\})).

(LINab)

2.2
The structure of blocking ideals

The following description of blocking ideals provides a way of finding all
blocking ideals in a finite poset
P
with a fixed antichain
a\parallel b
.
In the next section, we will apply it to partition posets to obtain a

blocking expansion
, allowing us to calculate
e(P;\alpha<\beta)
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
P
be a finite poset and let
\alpha,\beta\in P
be two incomparable elements.

1.

The
complete ideal

D_{P}(\alpha,\beta)
is defined as

D_{P}(\alpha,\beta)\triangleq P\setminus(V_{P}(\alpha)\cup V_{P}(\beta))=\{z\in P\,\mid\,z\not\geq\alpha,\,z\not\geq\beta\}.

(COM)

2.

The
constant ideal

A_{P}(\alpha)
, also called the
fixed part
, is the open
principal order ideal of
\alpha
:

A_{P}(\alpha)\triangleq\Lambda_{P}(\alpha)\setminus\left\{{\alpha}\right\}=\{z\in P\,\mid\,z<\alpha\}.

(FIX)

3.

The
variable part

Definition 3.4

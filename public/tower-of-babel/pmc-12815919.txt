Immersive experience in virtual reality gamification teaching: analysis of the mediating effect on educational learning outcomes
Authors: JianLin Lin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The deep integration of virtual reality (VR) technology and education is driving structural changes in teaching paradigms. This study systematically explored the immersion mechanism and educational effects in VR gamification teaching scenarios through a mixed experimental design, and constructed a sample database comprising 720 participants. The research found that the technical threshold for imparting high-complexity knowledge requires a frame rate of ≥ 90FPS and a latency of ≤ 60ms, while creative tasks can achieve an operational accuracy rate of over 85% with moderate rendering quality. The dynamic task complexity adjustment algorithm and multimodal collaboration toolkit proposed in the study effectively balance the interference of hardware performance differences on the learning curve, enabling the experimental group to achieve an operational accuracy rate of 89.5% and a technical adaptability rate of 94.5% in virtual surgery training scenarios. The study breaks through the traditional linear analysis paradigm, establishes a nonlinear coupling law between technical performance parameters and cognitive behavioral variables, and provides quantitative decision support for the standardized development of educational VR systems.

Introduction

The integration of virtual reality technology and gamification teaching is reshaping modern education models. Virtual reality technology, by simulating real-life scenarios through immersive environments, can significantly enhance learners’ language application abilities1. This conclusion has been further validated in the field of physical education2, where the combination of digital intelligent technology and curriculum ideology has been proven to stimulate student engagement by enhancing teacher-student interaction3. Interdisciplinary research shows that environmental conflict teaching, through case analysis and immersive experiences (Fig. 1), can effectively cultivate students’ interdisciplinary research abilities4. However, the depth and breadth of technology application are still limited by the adaptability of teaching scenarios5. For example, although the three-dimensional visualization of nuclide charts in physics education can enhance learning interest6, the issue of matching hardware equipment with teaching objectives has not been fully resolved7. This indicates that the combination of virtual reality and gamification teaching provides a new path for educational innovation, but its systematic integration still requires further exploration8.

In current educational practice, high-immersion design has not yet been fully translated into improved learning outcomes9. It has been found that teachers’ role stress and psychological distress indirectly affect the quality of teaching engagement in virtual teaching environments10. Although traditional teaching methods can directly improve student performance, there is a lack of systematic analysis on their mediating effect on learning motivation11. Research in cross-cultural contexts further reveals that while bidirectional immersive English teaching can enhance teachers’ sense of identity, the correlation model between technical parameters and learning outcomes has not yet been established12. Furthermore, the effect of bullet screen technology in enhancing classroom participation through perceived interactivity has been found to be limited by individual cognitive differences among students13. These evidences indicate that existing research mostly focuses on single technology or teaching dimensions (as shown in Fig. 2), lacking a systematic investigation of the interaction between technical performance and psychological mechanisms, which leads to the untapped potential of high-immersion design14. In virtual reality collaborative environments, social presence significantly affects learning outcomes through the mediating role of cognitive presence15, but the moderating effect of technical fluency on the path has not been quantified16. Although immersive virtual reality can enhance knowledge transfer ability, the synergistic effect of environmental realism and task complexity has not been distinguished17. Meanwhile, technical performance directly affects user decision-making efficiency18, and the cultural sensitivity teacher training framework suggests that immersive design needs to consider both learners’ cognitive characteristics and equipment performance thresholds19. These gaps highlight the necessity of building a “technology-cognition-emotion” collaborative model.

This study is the first to incorporate technical performance into the mediation effect analysis framework (as shown in Fig. 3), aiming to clarify the mechanism of immersion on educational effectiveness and explore the moderating effect of technical parameters20. Nuclide teaching experiments indicate that differences in the performance of virtual reality equipment can lead to divergent learning curves21, while ecosystem theory models demonstrate that inclusive teaching environments reduce teachers’ job burnout through teaching efficacy22. Molecular biology experiments further confirm that the complementary design of virtual and physical laboratories can simultaneously enhance knowledge mastery and teaching confidence23. By integrating these findings, this study will construct a dynamic regulation model to provide theoretical support and practical guidance for the iterative development of virtual reality educational products.

Current research in the field of virtual reality education exhibits a clear “technology-cognition” dualistic split. In the technology dimension, existing literature primarily focuses on optimizing single performance parameters, such as the linear impact of frame rate improvement on attention (technology determinism paradigm), while neglecting the nonlinear coupling relationship between hardware performance and cognitive load. Such research often treats technical parameters as independent variables, failing to reveal their dynamic adjustment mechanisms in complex teaching scenarios. In the teaching dimension, scholars have overly emphasized static measurements of psychological constructs, such as the cross-cultural applicability of flow scales (psychological reductionism paradigm), lacking a systematic investigation of how technological carriers shape cognitive pathways. This split leads to two key theoretical gaps: firstly, the quantitative relationship between technology performance thresholds and cognitive state transitions has not been established, and existing research cannot explain why the same device produces divergent learning curves in different tasks; secondly, the synergistic effects generated by multimodal interactions are reduced to the arithmetic sum of single-channel influences, failing to capture the multiplier effect of social presence and hardware performance. More fundamentally, current literature has not yet constructed a unified analytical framework to integrate three core elements: the physical constraints of device performance, the psychological laws of cognitive processes, and the situational characteristics of teaching scenarios. This theoretical fragmentation significantly underestimates the potential of high immersion design, akin to observing a rainbow through a beam splitter while refusing to acknowledge the continuity of the spectrum. The technology-cognition synergy model proposed in this study aims to break down the theoretical barriers across these three dimensions, by establishing a nonlinear mapping relationship between performance parameters and psychological variables, revealing the dynamic mechanism of immersion generation, and ultimately achieving cross-level prediction of “hardware performance-cognitive state-teaching effectiveness”.

Theoretical model and hypothesis

Construction of theoretical model

This study integrates the Technology Acceptance Model24 with the Flow Theory25 to propose a dynamic path model of “immersion - mediating variables - learning effectiveness” (Fig. 4). The model encompasses three core dimensions: immersion (including spatial presence, interaction, and social presence), mediating variables (including flow state and intrinsic motivation), and moderating variables (i.e., technological performance).

Fig. 4The dynamic path model of this study.

The dynamic path model of this study.

X1 represents spatial presence (linear combination of environmental realism and visual rendering); X2 denotes interactive perception (quantified interaction behavior based on hyperbolic tangent function); M1 indicates flow state (three-dimensional immersion effect calculated through time integration equation); M2 corresponds to intrinsic motivation (social-task coupling variable combining Michaelis-Menten equation and hyperbolic function).

The standardized coefficients in the dynamic path model are calculated using a three-stage process: First, a maximum likelihood estimation model is constructed using AMOS 28.0 software, and the initial path coefficients are calculated based on multimodal data from 720 participants. Second, Bootstrap sampling is employed to generate coefficient distributions 1000 times, eliminating sampling bias within a 95% confidence interval. Finally, the stability of the coefficients is verified through multi-group analysis of structural equation modeling, where the X1-X2 path coefficient of 0.47 (SE = 0.03) corresponds to the covariance of spatial presence → interaction perception, and the M1-M2 path coefficient of 0.61 (SE = 0.05) reflects the cross-scenario robustness of flow state → intrinsic motivation. All coefficients undergo Holm-Bonferroni correction to control for multiple comparison errors.

Based on Flow theory and self-determination theory, this study proposes Hypothesis H1: Immersion influences learning effectiveness through a dual-path nonlinear effect of flow state and intrinsic motivation. This hypothesis suggests that immersion does not directly and linearly affect learning outcomes, but rather requires attention integration through flow state and continuous drive from intrinsic motivation. The two factors collaboratively form a nonlinear transmission mechanism, jointly promoting the enhancement of learning effectiveness.

Based on collaborative learning theory and social cognitive theory, hypothesis H2 posits that in collaborative task scenarios, the mediating effect of social presence increases superlinearly with the density of collaborative behavior. Specifically, once the density of collaborative behavior surpasses a critical threshold, the mediating role of social presence on learning outcomes intensifies at a faster rate, reflecting the scale effect of collaborative interaction.

Based on the Technology Acceptance Model and Cognitive Load Theory, Hypothesis H3 proposes that technical performance optimizes the interactive experience by dynamically adjusting the frame rate, thereby enhancing the flow generation rate. This hypothesis emphasizes that reasonably adjusting technical parameters such as frame rate can reduce interaction barriers and cognitive load, enhance users’ perception of the smoothness of the interaction process, and thus accelerate the generation of flow states.

The selection of complex nonlinear functions is based on rigorous theoretical grounds: the integral Eq. (4) simulates the time-accumulated effect of flow states (refer to Csikszentmihalyi’s attention integration theory); the scattering characteristics of the Mie Eq. (5) quantify the radiation range of social influence; and the boundary value characteristics (−1 to 1) of the hyperbolic function (2) perfectly match the polarity characteristics of the Likert scale. These mathematical forms have been tested through model fitting in pre-experiments (ΔBIC > 7.2), demonstrating significant advantages over linear models.

In Formula 1, α = 0.83 (environmental realism weight) is extracted through principal component analysis, while β = 0.91 (rendering quality coefficient) originates from GPU shader performance calibration. For Formula 2, ξ (interaction frequency) is calculated by the VRTK plugin with a 15ms time window, and µ (interface usability) is standardized using the SUS scale. The threshold parameter η = 3.2 in Formula 3 is determined through ROC curve analysis. The upper limit of the integral t1 = 120 s in Formula 4 corresponds to the physiological limit of attention maintenance. The scattering coefficient σ = 1.25 in Formula 5 is calibrated based on eye movement data. The attenuation factor λ = 0.07/dB in Formula 6 is fitted through psychophysical experiments. In the text, complex mathematical formulas such as the Laplacian operator and the Milne equation primarily serve as conceptual frameworks for describing the nonlinear relationships in educational phenomena, rather than as absolute physical laws governing human cognition. We always prioritize the essence of education as the core, and these formulas aim to quantify the abstract relationship between psychology and technology interaction to enhance theoretical rigor. We kindly request understanding of the limitations of this methodological choice, and future research will further integrate qualitative analysis to deepen the theoretical foundation.

Empirical analysis

Research methods

This study employs multimodal measurement: (1) at the subjective level, the Flow State Scale-2 is used; (2) at the objective level, gaze entropy and Inter-Blinks Interval (IBI) are tracked using a Tobii eye tracker, and their correlation with flow state has been confirmed by a study published in the journal Psychophysiology (r = 0.68, p < 0.01). After data fusion of the two methods, α = 0.89, indicating a 12% improvement in validity compared to a single scale.

This study adopts a mixed experimental design, combining quantitative analysis and case studies. Data collection covers four dimensions: sample distribution, variable measurement, technical parameters, and equipment configuration. The specific results are shown in Table 2 (Fig. 5).

The sample size of 360 individuals in each of the experimental and control groups in this study ensures sufficient statistical power (as shown in Table 2). The gender ratio in the experimental group is 54.7% male vs. 53.3% male in the control group, and the similarity in age distribution and academic background, with 58.3% in science and engineering vs. 54.2%, effectively controls for potential confounding variables. The broad geographical coverage, including samples from the eastern, central, western regions, as well as overseas, enhances the ecological validity of the research conclusions. The mean difference in VR usage experience, with the experimental group at 45.2 h vs. the control group at 43.8 h, falls within a reasonable fluctuation range, indicating that the variable of technical familiarity has been effectively balanced.

User perception data is collected every 5 min using a 7-point Likert scale (α = 0.92), while physiological signals (heart rate, skin conductance) are synchronously recorded at 10 Hz using the Empatica E4. Table 3 shows that interaction behavior data is captured using the VRTK plugin, recording user interactions such as clicks, drags, and voice commands, along with their frequency and accuracy through timestamps. Flow state data is tracked using the Tobii Pro Glasses 3 eye tracker, measuring gaze hotspots, pupil diameter, and blink frequency, and calculating the focus index based on the time interval between task completion. Technical performance data is collected in real-time through the Unity Profiler, measuring frame rate, latency, and GPU load rate, and is synchronously stored in the cloud log system.

Regarding the sample size issue in the four experimental scenarios, this study adopted a two-stage verification strategy to ensure the reliability of the conclusions. Firstly, a sensitivity analysis of scenario parameters was conducted through a pilot experiment (n = 120) to determine that the key difference dimensions (such as task complexity, collaboration density, etc.) of each scenario were statistically significant (p < 0.05). Although the sample size per group (60–80 participants) in the formal experiment stage was slightly lower than the recommended value for traditional multi-scenario studies, validity was ensured through three measures: (1) employing a repeated measures design, where each participant completed all scenario tasks to reduce the interference of individual differences; (2) using Bayesian Factor Analysis (BF > 10) to verify the substantiality of scenario differences; (3) confirming the practical significance of differences through effect size calculation (η² > 0.25). This design, while ensuring ecological validity, compensated for sample size limitations through methodological optimization.

Discussion

Theoretical contribution

This study, by integrating the Technology Acceptance Model and Flow Theory, constructs a dynamic framework for the impact of immersion on educational effectiveness for the first time. The research reveals the nonlinear adjustment mechanism of technical performance parameters such as frame rate and resolution on the immersion pathway, breaking through the traditional linear analysis paradigm of “technology-effect” in educational technology research. For example, in high frame rate environments such as ≥ 60fps, the promotion effect of interaction on flow state is enhanced, while when the delay exceeds 100ms, the cognitive gain of spatial presence significantly diminishes. This finding echoes the proposed framework for cultivating culturally sensitive teachers, indicating that technical performance thresholds need to dynamically match learners’ cognitive characteristics. In addition, the study verifies the superlinear growth law of social presence in collaborative tasks with β = 0.41, filling the research gap in the field of virtual collaborative learning regarding mediating effects. Compared to the dominant role of interaction in independent tasks with β = 0.52, this model provides differentiated theoretical basis for multi-scenario instructional design. This conclusion complements the research on bidirectional immersive language teaching, jointly supporting the theoretical hypothesis of “environment-behavior-cognition” collaborative optimization.

Practical significance

The research results provide operable technical adaptation strategies for the development of virtual reality educational products. For example, low-end devices with a frame rate of ≤ 45fps can prioritize interactive experience through dynamic loading algorithms, significantly improving operational accuracy (Table 16). This strategy aligns with the proposed concept of “layered technical adaptation,” which can alleviate the problem of learning effectiveness differentiation caused by hardware performance differences. At the instructional design level, it is recommended to adopt the “high frame rate VR + collaborative tasks” mode for highly complex knowledge, with a knowledge retention rate of 76.8%, significantly higher than the traditional method’s 62.4%. At the same time, for learners with high anxiety, it is necessary to simplify the physical engine load by reducing task complexity to avoid cognitive overload. This finding echoes practical interventions in teacher stress research.

However, the cultural background of the sample in this study is relatively homogenous, which limits the generalizability of conclusions regarding the social presence effect. For instance, differences in sensitivity to social cues among Western learners may affect the stability of path coefficients in collaborative tasks, and the cognitive load in immersive environments may accumulate nonlinearly with usage duration. The cross-sectional data in this study struggles to capture such dynamic patterns. Therefore, the study did not track changes in marginal effects over long-term technology use. In the future, it is necessary to verify the external validity of the model through cross-cultural comparative experiments. Subsequently, longitudinal studies can be conducted to analyze the decay thresholds of parameters such as frame rate and resolution, as well as their long-term impact on the learning curve.

Conclusion

Core conclusions

This study reveals the mechanism of immersion in virtual reality gamification teaching on learning effectiveness and clarifies the dynamic coupling relationship between technical performance parameters and cognitive behavior. Firstly, immersion significantly enhances learning effectiveness through the dual-path synergistic effect of flow state and intrinsic motivation, where the enhancement effect of social presence in collaborative tasks far exceeds that in independent task scenarios. Secondly, the dynamic adjustment mechanism of technical performance is the core to ensure a highly adaptable teaching experience. It achieves stable output across device scenarios by reducing the interference of hardware performance differences on the learning curve. Further analysis shows that the design of a multimodal collaboration density function can effectively quantify the promotion effect of social presence on learning behavior, while the dynamic task complexity adjustment strategy can specifically alleviate the cognitive load of high-anxiety learners. Compared to traditional static models, the bidirectional technology-cognition collaborative framework proposed in this paper exhibits significant advantages in cross-scenario generalization ability, collaboration efficiency, and technical adaptability, providing a theoretical basis for the standardized development of virtual reality education systems35.

Application suggestions

In terms of educational technology development, it is recommended to prioritize the use of adaptive technology performance tuning algorithms to dynamically optimize rendering parameters for different hardware configurations, ensuring a basic immersive experience for users on low-end devices. For example, GPU load can be balanced by simplifying model details and adopting dynamic loading strategies36, while retaining core interactive functions. Additionally, developing a multimodal collaboration toolkit that integrates voice, motion, and text feedback can enhance social presence, especially in high-collaboration scenarios such as medical simulation surgery and engineering simulation. At the teaching practice level, it is recommended to design virtual learning scenarios based on differentiated knowledge types. For complex skill training, a mode combining high-frame-rate rendering with collaborative tasks should be adopted to enhance knowledge transfer efficiency; for theoretical concept teaching, emphasis can be placed on interactive design to deepen understanding through virtual object manipulation. Meanwhile, educators need to establish a dynamic task difficulty evaluation system to monitor learners’ cognitive load in real time and automatically adjust task complexity based on technical parameters, thereby creating a more inclusive virtual learning environment.

Aliased Time-Modulated Array OFDM System
Marcin Wachowiak, André Bourdoux, Sofie Pollin

Abstract
The time-modulated array is a simple array architecture in which each antenna is connected to an RF switch that serves as a modulator. The phase shift is achieved by digitally controlling the relative delay between the periodic modulating sequences of the antennas. The practical use of this architecture is limited by two factors. First, the switching frequency is high, as it must be a multiple of the sampling frequency. Second, the discrete modulating sequence introduces undesired harmonic replicas of the signal with non-negligible power. In this paper, aliasing is exploited to simultaneously reduce sideband radiation and switching frequency. To facilitate coherent combining of aliased signal blocks, the transmit signal has a repeated block structure in the frequency domain. As a result, a factor A A reduction in switching frequency is achieved at the cost of a factor A A reduction in communication capacity. Doubling A A reduces sideband radiation by around 2.9 dB.

II-A

Time modulation

Consider a time-modulated antenna, which is connected to the
N
throw/state switch. The RF switch acts as a discrete phase shifter - each state of the switch results in a different phase shift of the input signal.
The phase shift of
n
-th switch state is given by

\varphi(n)=2\pi\frac{n}{N},

(1)

where
n
is the index of the state
n\in\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\}
.
The switch operates at a frequency
f_{\mathrm{s}}
. The pulse duration of each state is

T_{\mathrm{p}}=AT_{\mathrm{s}},\ A\in\mathbb{N}^{+},

(2)

where
A
is the aliasing factor that increases the pulse duration and reduces pulse frequency.
The pulse frequency is

f_{\mathrm{p}}=\frac{1}{T_{\mathrm{p}}}=\frac{f_{\mathrm{s}}}{A}.

(3)

The modulating signal is a periodically repeated sequence of
N
rectangular pulses with a phase given by (
1

[
7
]
.
Due to the discretized switching instants and phase shift values, the basic building block of the TMA modulating signal is a rectangular pulse. Consider a periodical rectangular pulse of duration
T_{\mathrm{p}}
with unit amplitude and phase shift of
\varphi(n)

g_{n}(t)=e^{j\varphi(n)}\sum_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}\rect{\left(\frac{t}{T_{\mathrm{p}}}-\frac{T_{\mathrm{p}}}{2}+iNT_{\mathrm{p}}\right)}.

(4)

The pulse is periodical with a period

The Fourier coefficient of the

\displaystyle G_{n}(k)

\displaystyle=e^{j\varphi(n)}\sinc{\left(\pi\frac{k}{N}\right)}e^{-j\pi\frac{k}{N}}.

(5)

The modulating (control) signal
c(t)
is composed of a sequence of
N
delayed complex pulses from (
4

\displaystyle c(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}g_{n}\left(t-nT_{\mathrm{p}}\right).

(6)

The frequency domain representation of the modulating signal is

\displaystyle C(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}G_{n}(k)e^{-j2\pi\frac{n}{N}T_{\mathrm{p}}f_{\mathrm{p}}}

\displaystyle=\sinc{\left(\pi\frac{k}{N}\right)}e^{-j\pi\frac{k}{N}}I(k)

(7)

I(k)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}e^{j2\pi\frac{n}{N}\left(1-i\right)}
determines the existence of the

-th spectral component at frequency

\displaystyle I(k)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1,&k=1+zN\\
0,&k\neq 1+zN\end{cases},\quad z\in\mathbb{Z}

(8)

To simplify the following analysis, the discrete harmonic representation of the modulating sequence is transformed into a continuous frequency range

\displaystyle C(f)

\displaystyle=\alpha(i)\delta\left(f-\left(\frac{f_{\mathrm{p}}}{N}+if_{\mathrm{p}}\right)\right)

where

i
-th harmonic component and

is the Dirac delta function. The
i
-th harmonic components are located at frequency
\frac{f_{\mathrm{p}}}{N}+if_{\mathrm{p}}
.

To gain more insight into the amplitude and phase of the harmonic components,

can be expressed as

\displaystyle\alpha(i)=

\displaystyle\left|\sinc{\left(\pi\left(i+\frac{1}{N}\right)\right)}\right|e^{j\phi_{\alpha}(i)},

(10)

where
\phi
is the function describing the phase of the
\alpha(i)
harmonic component

\phi_{\alpha}{\left(i\right)}=\begin{cases}-\frac{\pi}{N},&i\geq 0\\
-\frac{\pi}{N}-\pi,&i<0\end{cases}.

(11)

The length of the modulating sequence

affects the power of the harmonics by narrowing the main lobe of the
\sinc

f_{\mathrm{mod}}=f_{\mathrm{p}}/(NA)
- the modulating frequency. The length
N
affects the phase of the harmonic components; however, note that the relative difference between the positive and negative components is constant and equal to
\pi

II-B

Phase shifting

The phase shifting of the signal is achieved by a cyclic shift of the modulating sequence. Consider an oversampling factor
O_{\tau}
that increases the operating frequency of the switch
O_{\tau}
times. The pulse duration or the sampling frequency is not affected. The oversampling allows to obtain a refined timing resolution, which results in a multiplied number of effective phase shifts. Given a sequence of length
N
and pulse duration
T_{\mathrm{p}}
, there are
D
possible discrete delay values given by

D=N\frac{T_{\mathrm{p}}}{T_{\mathrm{sw}}}=N\frac{T_{\mathrm{p}}}{\frac{T_{\mathrm{p}}}{O_{\tau}}}=NO_{\tau}

(12)

The switch operates at frequency
f_{sw}=O_{\tau}f_{\mathrm{p}}=\frac{O_{\tau}}{A}f_{\mathrm{s}}
which is decreased by a factor
A
compared to the max oversampling frequency
O_{\tau}f_{\mathrm{s}}
.
The delay (cyclic shift) of the time domain modulating sequence is equivalent to the phase rotation in the frequency domain as follows

\displaystyle C(f,d)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}\left\{c\left(t-dT_{\mathrm{sw}}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=\alpha(i)e^{-j2\pi dT_{\mathrm{sw}}f}\delta{\left(f-\frac{f_{\mathrm{p}}}{N}-if_{\mathrm{p}}\right)},

(13)

where
d\in\{0,1,\ldots,D-1\}
is the index of the selected digital delay.
The phase shift due to the cyclic delay of the phase modulating sequence is

\displaystyle\varphi_{\tau}(i,d)

\displaystyle=-2\pi dT_{\mathrm{sw}}\left(\frac{f_{\mathrm{p}}}{N}+if_{\mathrm{p}}\right)

\displaystyle=-2\pi\frac{d}{D}-2\pi\frac{d}{O_{\tau}}i.

(14)

It can be seen that the oversampling increases the phase-shifting resolution by an order of
O_{\tau}
. However, for
d
values other than multiples of
O_{\tau}
there is an additional phase shift dependent on the harmonic index
i
.
Extending (
10
) with (
II-B
) to include the effects of the time delay on the harmonic component results in

\alpha(i,d)=\alpha(i)e^{j\varphi_{\tau}(i,d)}.

(15)

II-C

Aliasing

The maximum bandwidth of signal transmitted by the conventional TMA to avoid aliasing is determined by the spacing of the harmonic components equal to
f_{\mathrm{s}}/A
(
3
). In this paper, aliasing combined with the block structure of the signal in the frequency domain and precoding is exploited to introduce destructive interference between the harmonics of the transmitted signal to reduce the sideband radiation (SR).

Consider a baseband signal of bandwidth
B=1/f_{\mathrm{s}}
that consists of
A
(aliasing factor) joint/adjacent rectangular blocks of equal width
B/A
in the frequency domain. The frequency domain representation of the single block is

S_{a}(f)=v(a)\rect{\left(\frac{f}{\frac{f_{\mathrm{s}}}{A}}\right)},

(16)

where
v(a)
is complex frequency-flat precoding coefficient per block and
a\in\{0,1,\ldots,A-1\}
is the block index.
The total baseband signal can be written as a convolution of precoded finite Dirac delta comb of length
A
and rectangular function

\displaystyle S(f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=0}^{A-1}S_{a}\left(f-a\frac{f_{\mathrm{s}}}{A}+\frac{A-1}{2A}f_{\mathrm{s}}\right)

(17)

\displaystyle=\rect{\left(\frac{f}{\frac{f_{\mathrm{s}}}{A}}\right)}*\sum_{a=0}^{A-1}v(a)\delta{\left(f-\left(a-\frac{A-1}{2}\right)\frac{f_{\mathrm{s}}}{A}\right)}.

The time modulation of this signal with a precoded-shifted frequency-domain representation is achieved by multiplying the time domain signal with a modulating sequence
C(f,d)
, which in the frequency domain corresponds to the convolution of (
9
) with (
17
)

\displaystyle Y(f,d)=

\displaystyle S(f)*C(f,d)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a=0}^{A-1}v(a)\alpha(i,d)\delta{\left(f-\left(a-\left\lfloor\frac{A-1}{2}\right\rfloor+\Delta_{f}+i\right)\frac{f_{\mathrm{s}}}{A}\right)}

\displaystyle*\rect{\left(\frac{f}{\frac{f_{\mathrm{s}}}{A}}\right)},

(18)

where
\Delta_{f}=\left\lfloor\frac{A-1}{2}\right\rfloor-\frac{A-1}{2}+\frac{1}{N}
is a fractional shift in frequency due to time modulation and centering of the convolution kernel
v(a)
.
The aliased signal with overlapping frequency blocks can be written as

\displaystyle Y(f,d)=

\displaystyle\sum_{a=0}^{A-1}v\left(a\right)\alpha\left(i-a+\left\lfloor\frac{A-1}{2}\right\rfloor,d\right)

\displaystyle\rect{\left(\frac{f}{\frac{f_{\mathrm{s}}}{A}}\right)}*\delta{\left(f-\left(\Delta_{f}+i\right)\frac{f_{\mathrm{s}}}{A}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{\mathrm{A}}(i,d)\rect{\left(\frac{f}{\frac{f_{\mathrm{s}}}{A}}-\left(\Delta_{f}+i\right)\right)}.

(19)

where
\alpha_{\mathrm{A}}(i,d)
is the complex coefficient resulting from aliasing of
A
precoded harmonic components
\alpha(i,d)
. Expanding
\alpha(i,d)
with (
15
) and (
II-B
) gives

\displaystyle\alpha_{\mathrm{A}}(i,d)=

\displaystyle e^{-j2\pi\frac{d}{D}}e^{-j2\pi\frac{d}{O_{\tau}}i}e^{-j2\pi\frac{d}{O_{\tau}}\left\lfloor\frac{A-1}{2}\right\rfloor}

\displaystyle\sum_{a=0}^{A-1}v(a)e^{j2\pi\frac{d}{O_{\tau}}a}\alpha\left(i-a+\left\lfloor\frac{A-1}{2}\right\rfloor\right)

(20)

II-D

Precoder design

The destructive interference of the sideband radiation is achieved by the design of the precoding vector
v(a)
. However, oversampling introduces an additional phase component
e^{j2\pi\frac{d}{O_{\tau}}}
in the sum of (
II-C
). To compensate for the phase shift, the precoder is extended with a conjugate of it

v(a,d)=v(a)e^{-j2\pi a\frac{d}{O_{\tau}}}.

(21)

The oversampling
O_{\tau}
determines the periodicity of the phase shift and the number of additional precoding vectors required to compensate for it across all
d
values. The precoder is chosen based on the
d
value per antenna. When considering oversampling, multiple precoders are required - increasing the complexity of the array architecture. As digital precoding is required, the precoders are implemented as separate digital basebands. The number of digital baseband units does not scale with antennas. However, each antenna might require a different precoder, which can be provided by a switched distribution network, as shown in Fig.
1
.
Given the extended precoder the sum in (
II-C
) simplifies to

\displaystyle\alpha_{\mathrm{A}}(i,d)=

\displaystyle e^{j\varphi_{\tau_{\mathrm{A}}}(i,d)}\sum_{a=0}^{A-1}v(a)\alpha\left(i-a+\left\lfloor\frac{A-1}{2}\right\rfloor\right),

(22)

where
e^{j\varphi_{\tau_{\mathrm{A}}}}=e^{-j2\pi\frac{d}{D}}e^{-j2\pi\frac{d}{O_{\tau}}\left(i+\left\lfloor\frac{A-1}{2}\right\rfloor\right)}
is the phase shift due to the selected delay value
d
.
The desired baseband signal is within the harmonic indices
i_{\mathrm{bb}}\in\{-(A-1)+\left\lfloor\frac{A-1}{2}\right\rfloor,\ldots,\left\lfloor\frac{A-1}{2}\right\rfloor\}
. All other harmonic components
i\notin i_{\mathrm{bb}}
are considered sideband radiation and should be minimized.
By evaluating the phase of the harmonic components for
i>\left\lfloor\frac{A-1}{2}\right\rfloor
it can be seen that the argument of
\alpha
in (
22
) is always positive, resulting in constant phase given by (
11
) for all positive sideband harmonic indices. Similarly for the negative sideband indices
i<-(A-1)+\left\lfloor\frac{A-1}{2}\right\rfloor
the argument of
\alpha
is always negative. Therefore, the harmonics on the positive and negative of the sideband have a constant phase within each sideband.
The amplitude of the harmonic components decreases with increasing
|i|
. Each sideband exhibits a different monotonicity and rate of change/slope (for
N>2
) of the amplitude of the harmonic components. This renders the amplitude precoding cumbersome and limits its feasibility.

In the following, a phase only precoder is considered to simultaneously minimize both sidebands.
Given the constant phase of the summed sideband harmonic components, a natural choice to minimize the sum is to alter the sign of the summed components by applying an alternating precoder

v(a)=(-1)^{a}=e^{j\pi a}.

(23)

The alternating precoder introduces destructive interference between the harmonic components at the sidebands at the cost of introducing power variations in the power of the passband signal. The fluctuations are the result of the summation of a different subset of precoded harmonic components. The
0
-th harmonic component dominates the amplitude of the result and determines the phase. Note, that the aliased signal in the desired passband range has an alternating phase precoding, which can be easily compensated at the receiver.
After the convolution/aliasing, indices of baseband signal of interest have a flipped range
i_{\mathrm{bb}_{\mathrm{A}}}=\{-\left\lfloor\frac{A-1}{2}\right\rfloor,\ldots,A-1-\left\lfloor\frac{A-1}{2}\right\rfloor\}
.

Beamforming with aliased TMA

The ULA TMA array is considered to be operating in the far field with isotropic antenna elements spaced by
d_{\lambda}
. Taking the first element as a reference the path difference per antenna element in the direction
\theta
is

\displaystyle\Delta d_{m}

\displaystyle=md_{\lambda}\frac{c}{f_{\mathrm{c}}}\sin{(\theta)},

(25)

where
d_{\lambda}
is the spacing between antenna elements expressed in wavelengths and
f_{\mathrm{c}}
is the center frequency that the array is designed at.
To achieve beamforming, the modulating signal per antenna is cyclically shifted by
md
samples. The phase shift at the
m
-th antenna according to (
22
) is then

\displaystyle\phi_{m}(i,d)

\displaystyle=-2\pi m\frac{d}{D}\left(1+\left(i+\left\lfloor\frac{A-1}{2}\right\rfloor\right)N\right).

(26)

The array factor (AF) of the TMA at the
i
-th harmonic frequency for a selected delay
d
is

\displaystyle AF(\theta,i,d)

\displaystyle=\alpha(i)\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}e^{j\phi_{m}(i,d)}e^{-j2\pi\frac{\Delta d_{m}}{c}\left(f_{\mathrm{c}}+\frac{f_{\mathrm{p}}}{N}+if_{\mathrm{p}}\right)}.

(27)

where the factor
1/\sqrt{M}
normalizes the total transmitted power to unity.
Assuming that the frequencies of the harmonic components with significant power are negligible compared to the carrier frequency
\left(\frac{1}{N}+i\right)f_{\mathrm{p}}\ll f_{\mathrm{c}}
the AF can be simplified to

\displaystyle AF(\theta,i,d)

\displaystyle=\alpha(i)\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}e^{-2\pi md\left(\frac{1}{D}+\frac{1}{O_{\tau}}\left(i+\left\lfloor\frac{A-1}{2}\right\rfloor\right)+d_{\lambda}\sin{(\theta)}\right)}.

(28)

Depending on the harmonic index and delay value, the formed beams are pointed in directions

\theta(i,d)=-\arcsin{\left(\frac{d}{d_{\lambda}}\left(\frac{1}{D}+\frac{1}{O_{\tau}}\left(i+\left\lfloor\frac{A-1}{2}\right\rfloor\right)\right)\right)}.

(29)

In the presence of oversampling
O_{\tau}\geq 2
the beamforming directions are also dependent on the harmonic index
i
. When
d
is not a multiple of
O_{\tau}
, the phase shift across the passband blocks
i\in i_{\mathrm{bb}_{\mathrm{A}}}
is not the same leading to different beamforming directions depending on the block index. As a result only
A\frac{d}{O_{\tau}}
blocks are beamformed in the desired direction - reducing the effective bandwidth, while the remaining harmonics are beamformed in other directions.

The oversampling effectively improves the phase shifting resolution, and given the reduced switching frequency, it can be easily achieved. However, its feasibility is limited due to the loss of effective beamformed bandwidth and the requirement of an additional precoding vector and signal distribution for each oversampling factor.

III
Conclusion

The proposed ATMA architecture allows to reduce the sideband radiation and switching frequency by splitting the baseband signal into repeated blocks, which are differently precoded and later aliased. The number of blocks is an additional dimension that can be tuned along with the phase shifter resolution
N
to reduce the sideband radiation and allow the TMA to meet the ACLR criteria of a conventional system while having a simpler architecture. The oversampling with aliased TMA requires an additional precoder per each oversampling factor
O_{\tau}
, limiting the practicality of large oversampling. Since the scheme preserves the total bandwidth but reduces the spectral efficiency, it is well-suited for radar applications as well as radar-centric integrated communication and sensing systems.

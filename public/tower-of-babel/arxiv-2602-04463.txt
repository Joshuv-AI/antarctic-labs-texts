Simple 2-approximations for bad triangle transversals and some hardness results for related problems
Florian Adriaens, Nikolaj Tatti

Abstract
Given a signed graph, the bad triangle transversal ( BTT ) problem asks to find the smallest number of edges that need to be removed such that the remaining graph does not have a triangle with exactly one negative edge (a bad triangle). We propose novel 2-approximations for this problem, which are much simpler and faster than a folklore adaptation of the 2-approximation by 26 for finding a minimum triangle transversal in unsigned graphs. One of our algorithms also works for weighted BTT and for approximately optimal feasible solutions to the bad triangle cover LP. Using a recent result of 10 on approximating the bad triangle cover LP, we obtain a ( 2 + ϵ ) (2+\epsilon) approximation in time almost equal to the time needed to find a maximal set of edge-disjoint bad triangles (which would give a standard 3-approximation). Additionally, several inapproximability results are provided. For complete signed graphs, we show that BTT is NP-hard to approximate with factor better than 2137 2136 \frac{2137}{2136} . Our reduction also implies the same hardness result for related problems such as correlation clustering (cluster editing), cluster deletion and the min. strong triadic closure problem. On complete signed graphs, BTT is closely related to correlation clustering. We show that the correlation clustering optimum is at most 3 / 2 3/2 times the BTT optimum, by describing a pivot procedure that transforms BTT solutions into clusters. This improves a result by 40 , which states that their ratio is at most two.

Introduction

A
signed graph
is a graph where every edge either has a positive or a negative label.
Such graphs model a variety of different scientific phenomena.
In physics, they are used to model the ground-state energy of Ising models
[
23
]
.
In social networks interactions between individuals can often be categorized into two categories: trust versus distrust, friendly or antagonistic, etc.
Several authors have used signed graphs to find polarized communities in these networks
[
6
,
37
]
.
An important aspect in the edge formation of social networks is the sign of triangles.
According to structural balance theory from social psychology
[
11
]
, triangles with either one or three positive edges are more plausible, and this prevalence has been observed in real-life social networks
[
27
,
36
]
.
Many methods for link and sign prediction try to capitalize on this.
The survey of
[
36
]
lists several other important data mining tasks in signed social media networks.

Another important application of signed graphs is clustering based on similarities between objects.
Clustering is a standard approach for summarizing large datasets, having a surfeit of objectives and algorithms to solve these objectives.
In the context of signed graphs, a popular approach is
correlation clustering

[
4
]
.
Here the goal is to cluster a given signed graph such that positive inter-cluster edges plus negative intra-cluster edges are minimized. Correlation clustering has widespread applications in biology, social network analysis, document clustering and computer vision, see for example the survey of
7

Recently, connections between correlation clustering and bad triangle covering problems have been studied
[

,
5
,
41

10
,
29
,
3
,
21
]
. Here,
a

is a triangle with exactly one negative edge, and

is a set of edges that intersect all bad triangles.
The

problem (

BTT
while also drawing strengthened connections between the
BTT

1.1
Results for general graphs

Our first contribution is two novel 2-approximations for
BTT
.
Before providing any additional details, we first discuss two existing baselines for this problem.

First, note that including all edges from any maximal set of edge-disjoint bad triangles immediately gives a 3-approximation, as

3

We will refer to this algorithm as the

After solving the standard cover linear program (LP) relaxation, the algorithm of

Our 2-approximations for

Our algorithms are both simpler and faster than the algorithm of

They are specifically designed for signed graphs, in the sense that they do not work for covering triangles in unsigned graphs.

Theorem
4
states that a ratio of two appears to be the correct one for
BTT

Indeed, a straightforward reduction shows that this problem is as hard to approximate as vertex cover. Therefore, a better than 2-approximation is unlikely
[

1.2.2
Connections to

In its most simple form, Correlation Clustering (

[

]

Every inter-cluster edge and every intra-cluster non-edge counts as a disagreement.
This problem, also known as Cluster Editing, can be equivalently formulated on complete signed graphs, by labeling every edge as positive and every non-edge as negative.

CC

(a cycle with exactly one negative edge)
[

14

.

This formulation also holds for general signed graphs.

The importance of bad triangles for

19

The first constant-factor approximation for

was due to a method based on packing edge-disjoint bad triangles
[
4
]
.
Later, a fractional bad triangle packing argument was used to prove that the famous pivot algorithm yields a 3-approximation

There is a relationship between

of

The inequality
\text{OPT}_{\Delta}\leq\text{OPT}_{\textsf{CC}}

40

\text{OPT}_{\textsf{CC}}\leq 2\text{OPT}_{\Delta}
.
He proved this by transforming a feasible bad triangle cover into a valid clustering using pivot on an auxiliary graph.
The auxiliary graph is constructed by

Mistakes are then attributed to the pivot mistakes made on the auxiliary graph, which can be charged to the number of sign flips.
By using a pivot procedure with more refined inclusion probabilities, we improve this bound to a factor of
3/2
.

Theorem 3
.

For complete signed graphs, it holds that
\text{OPT}_{\textsf{CC}}\leq\frac{3}{2}\text{OPT}_{\Delta}
.

The algorithmic question of transforming a feasible bad triangle cover

We prove Theorem

If one could do this in poly-time for even smaller values, say

3
Hardness results

Here we present several negative results concerning the approximability of
BTT
. We make a distinction between general signed graphs and complete signed graphs.
We start by a simple observation regarding the achievable approximation ratio for algorithms based on
\text{LP}_{\Delta}
.

Lemma 4
.

The integrality gap of
\text{LP}_{\Delta}
is at least two, even in complete signed graphs.

Proof.

We use the same example that is used to show an integrality gap for CC
[
14
]
.
Consider a clique of
n
nodes with only negative edges between them. Add a new vertex that is joined to every node of the clique with positive edges. An optimal fractional solution will assign a value of
1/2
to all the positive edges. An optimal integral solution will pick
n-1
positive edges. The ratio
2(n-1)/n
approaches two as
n
increases.
∎

Therefore, any approximation algorithm based on
\text{LP}_{\Delta}
cannot achieve competitive ratio better than two, even in complete signed graphs. Next we show that in general graphs a ratio of two is most likely the best one. For complete signed graphs it leaves open the possibility of an approximation ratio smaller than two, although Lemma
4
shows that such method cannot be based on rounding
\text{LP}_{\Delta}
.

Theorem 4
.

BTT
is as hard to approximate as vertex cover.

Proof.

Let
G
be an unsigned graph for which we want to find a minimum vertex cover.
Create a signed graph
H
by labeling all edges in
G
as negative. Add a new node
u
to
H
and connect it to each node in
G
with a positive edge.
We can convert a solution
F
to
BTT
on
H
—which might use some negative edges—to a solution
P
to
BTT
on
H
with the following two properties:
(i)

P
only contains positive edges, and
(ii)
has size
|P|\leq|F|
. Indeed, for every negative edge
e\in F
select either of the two positive edges that together with
e
form a bad triangle, and add one of those positive edges to
P
.
This works because every negative edge in
H
is a part of exactly one bad triangle, whereas every positive edge in
H
can be part of multiple bad triangles.

P
naturally defines a vertex cover on
G
, by including in the cover the point
v
for each edge in
(u,v)\in P
.
Because
P
is a feasible solution to
BTT
on
H
, this gives a valid vertex cover of
G
.
Therefore, any
\alpha
-approximation for
BTT
leads to an
\alpha
-approximation for vertex cover.
∎

By using the result on vertex cover by
24
, Theorem
4
implies that
BTT
is UGC-hard to approximate with factor
2-\epsilon
for any
\epsilon>0
in general graphs.

3.1

Theorem

Instead, we reduce from the minimum 2CNF deletion problem (

The following gap problem is shown to be NP-hard by

Let

\text{OPT}_{\textsf{MD-2CNF}}>(3\delta-\epsilon)n

Our reduction uses “wheel-gadgets” (specifically,

Intuition.

The intuition behind the reduction is that to cover a hexagram (if one disregards bad triangles formed by clause edges for now), an optimal
BTT
solution will pick all 9 teeth edges from either the set of even or odd teeth. These are the only optima, all other covers consist of at least 10 edges.

The choice of odd or even teeth edges will capture whether the corresponding variable will be assigned to true or false.
The clause edges will guide the variable assignment such that as many as possible clauses are satisfied.
However, clause edges also form bad triangles (with hexagram edges and with each other), so we cannot simply assume that an optimal
BTT
solution on
G
picks even or odd teeth from each hexagram.

Call a cover
consistent
if for every hexagram, the cover contains either all edges from the even teeth, or all edges from the odd teeth, and no other hexagram edges. See for example, Figure
3
. We will show that there exists a consistent optimal
BTT
solution of
G
.
This allows us to relate its optimal value to the
MD-2CNF
optimum.
By applying Theorem
5
, we obtain a gap from which Theorem
6
will follow.

Gap.

Let
\text{OPT}_{\Delta}(G)
be the optimal value of
BTT
on
G
, and
\text{OPT}_{\textsf{MD-2CNF}}
be the optimal value of
MD-2CNF
on instances from Theorem
5
.
The following two lemmas are the crux of the reduction.

Lemma 5
.

\text{OPT}_{\Delta}(G)\leq 11n+\text{OPT}_{\textsf{MD-2CNF}}.

Proof.

Given an optimal assignment to
MD-2CNF
, we construct a feasible bad triangle cover on
G
as follows. If variable
x
in the assignment is set to true, then include in the cover all the edges from the even teeth of the
x
-hexagram. If
x
is false, include all the edges from the odd teeth.
This requires
9
edges per variable, and covers all the bad triangles that do not contain a clause edge.

Now consider a satisfied clause
C_{\ell}
.
There are two edges incident to the clause node
c_{\ell}
.
It suffices to include only one of these clause edges in the cover, for all the bad triangles formed by both clause edges to be covered.

For an unsatisfied clause, we select both clause edges.
This gives a feasible cover.
Since an instance from Theorem
5
has
n
variables and
2n
clauses, we find

\begin{split}\text{OPT}_{\Delta}(G)&\leq 9n+(2n-\text{OPT}_{\textsf{MD-2CNF}})+2\text{OPT}_{\textsf{MD-2CNF}}\\
&=11n+\text{OPT}_{\textsf{MD-2CNF}}.\end{split}

∎

Lemma 6
.

\text{OPT}_{\Delta}(G)\geq 11n+\text{OPT}_{\textsf{MD-2CNF}}.

Proof.

We show the existence of a
consistent
optimal bad triangle cover. Recall that a consistent cover is one that for every hexagram contains either all even teeth edges or all odd teeth edges (and only those).
Since every negative edge in
G
is part of at most one bad triangle, we may assume that we have an optimal cover
C_{\text{OPT}}
that only uses positive edges.
We describe how to modify
C_{\text{OPT}}
into a consistent cover of equal cost.

Consider the
x
-hexagram for any variable
x
.
The conditions of Theorem
5
state that
x
appears exactly once as negated in the 2SAT formula.
Let
C_{k}
be the clause which contains the literal
\overline{x}
.
Let
x_{\text{odd}}\in x\text{-hexagram}
be the odd crown connected to clause node
c_{k}
.
There are two cases, depending on whether the edge
(x_{\text{odd}},c_{k})
is part of
C_{\text{OPT}}
or not.

Case
(a)
: If
(x_{\text{odd}},c_{k})\in C_{\text{OPT}}
, then by optimality
C_{\text{OPT}}
contains all even-teeth edges from the
x
-hexagram, and only those. Thus,
C_{\text{OPT}}
already consistently covers the
x
-hexagram.

Case
(b)
: If
(x_{\text{odd}},c_{k})\notin C_{\text{OPT}}
, then the two
x
-hexagram edges incident to

x_{\text{odd}}
must be part of
C_{\text{OPT}}
.
Covering a hexagram with 9 edges can only be done by selecting either the even or odd teeth edges.
Hence, if
C_{\text{OPT}}
contains 9
x
-hexagram edges, then necessarily
C_{\text{OPT}}
must contain all odd-teeth edges from the
x
-hexagram, and only those.
If
C_{\text{OPT}}
contains 10 edges from the
x
-hexagram, then we can modify
C_{\text{OPT}}
as follows. Delete all
x
-hexagram edges from
C_{\text{OPT}}
. Add both
(x_{\text{odd}},c_{k})
and all even teeth edges from the
x
-hexagram to
C_{\text{OPT}}
.
This gives a feasible cover.
Since we have removed 10 edges and added 10 new edges to
C_{\text{OPT}}
this remains optimal.
This argument also shows that by optimality,
C_{\text{OPT}}
cannot contain more than 10 edges from the
x
-hexagram.
Figure
3
shows an example.

Repeating this modification for every variable

will produce a consistent optimal cover.
A consistent optimal cover includes 9 edges per hexagram.
Consider any clause node
c_{\ell}
and its two incident clause edges
(x_{i},c_{\ell})
and
(c_{\ell},y_{j})
.
When neither the tooth containing
x_{i}
nor the tooth containing

is included in the cover (in other words, when the clause is not satisfied), then a consistent optimal cover must contain
both

(x_{i},c_{\ell})
and
(c_{\ell},y_{j})
.
Moreover, any cover must always pick one of these two clause edges, even for satisfied clauses, since
(x_{i},c_{\ell})
and
(c_{\ell},y_{j})
form a bad triangle with each other.
There are
n
variables and

\displaystyle\text{OPT}_{\Delta}(G)=9n

\displaystyle+\big(2n-(\#\text{satisfied clauses})\big)

\displaystyle+2(\#\text{unsatisfied clauses}),

and the lemma follows.
∎

Combining Lemma
5

with Theorem

Theorem 6
.

For any

\gamma>0
, it is NP-hard to approximate

on complete graphs with a factor of
\frac{2137}{2136}-\gamma
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5
and Lemma
6
we find that
\text{OPT}_{\Delta}(G)=11n+\text{OPT}_{\textsf{MD-2CNF}}.

Using Theorem
5
for
\delta=\frac{1}{194}

, it is NP-hard to distinguish between

\begin{split}\text{OPT}_{\Delta}(G)&<\bigg(11+\frac{2}{194}+\epsilon\bigg)n=\bigg(\frac{2136}{194}+\epsilon\bigg)n\quad\text{ and }\\

∎

5
Conclusions

This paper makes several algorithmic contributions to the
bad triangle transversal
problem in signed graphs (
BTT
), which asks for the minimum number of edges to be deleted such that the remaining graph does not have a triangle with exactly one negative edge. Our contributions are both positive (better and faster algorithms), and negative (novel hardness results).
A lingering open theoretical direction is to obtain better-than-2 approximations for
BTT
on complete signed graphs, similarly like the recent results on correlation clustering.

It would also be interesting to implement and compare our proposed algorithms with existing baselines for
BTT
. One difficulty is solving
\text{LP}_{\Delta}
as it might have many variables and constraints (both can be of size
\Theta(n^{2})
), causing a solver to quickly run out of memory.

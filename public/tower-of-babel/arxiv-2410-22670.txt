Crepant Transformation correspondence For Toric Stack Bundles
Qian Chao, Jiun-Cheng Chen, Hsian-Hua Tseng

Abstract
. We prove a crepant transformation correspondence in genus zero Gromov-Witten theory for toric stack bundles related by crepant wall-crossings of the toric fibers. Specifically, we construct a symplectic transformation that identifies I I -functions of toric stack bundles suitably analytically continued using Mellin-Barnes type integrals. We compare our symplectic transformation with a Fourier-Mukai isomorphism between the K K -groups.

1.4.
Givental’s Symplectic Formalism

The original reference for Givental’s formalism is

Givental’s symplectic space is defined to be

\mathcal{H}=H^{\bullet}_{\text{CR},T}(X)\otimes_{R_{T}}S_{T}(\!(z^{-1})\!)\llbracket Q\rrbracket,

where

\Omega(f,g)=-\text{Res}_{z=\infty}(f(-z),g(z))_{\text{CR}}dz,

for any

of the orbifold Poincaré pairing of

and

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{+}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{-},

where

\mathcal{H}_{+}=H^{\bullet}_{\text{CR},T}(X)\otimes_{R_{T}}S_{T}[z]\llbracket Q\rrbracket,

The subspaces

induces a non-degenerate pairing between

and
\mathcal{H}_{-}
. This identifies

Let

\mathbf{p}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{k}(-z)^{-k-1},\quad\mathbf{q}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}q_{k}z^{k}.

2.1.
Toric Variety Associated To A Fan

Fix lattices
N,M
dual to each other and denote
N_{\mathbb{R}}=N\otimes\mathbb{R},M_{\mathbb{R}}=M\otimes\mathbb{R}
. A convex polyhedral cone, or simply a cone, is a set in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
of the form

\sigma=\text{Cone}(S)=\left\{\sum_{u\in S}\lambda_{u}u:\lambda_{u}\geq 0\right\},

where
S\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}
is finite and call
\sigma
a cone generated by
S
. The dual cone of
\sigma
is defined to be

\sigma^{\vee}=\left\{m\in M_{\mathbb{R}}:\langle m,u\rangle\geq 0\ \text{for all}\ u\in\sigma\right\},

which is a cone in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
and
(\sigma^{\vee})^{\vee}=\sigma
. We define the hyperplane

H_{m}=\left\{u\in N_{\mathbb{R}}:\langle m,u\rangle=0\right\}.

A face of the cone
\sigma
is
\tau=H_{m}\cap\sigma
for some
m\in\sigma^{\vee}
, denoted by
\tau\preceq\sigma
. Every face of
\sigma
is again a cone. Moreover, an intersection of faces or face of a face is again a face of
\sigma
. A face of codimension
1
is called a facet and a face of dimension
1
is called an edge.

A cone is called rational if the generating set
S
is in
N\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}
. A strongly convex rational cone has minimal generators called the ray generators of the edges. We call such a cone simplicial if its minimal generators are linearly independent over
\mathbb{R}
, and call such a cone smooth if the minimal generators form part of a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
N
.

A rational cone
\sigma\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}
gives a semigroup
S_{\sigma}=\sigma^{\vee}\cap M
, and the affine variety associated to
\sigma
is

U_{\sigma}=\text{Spec}(\mathbb{C}[S_{\sigma}]).

A fan
\Sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
is a finite collection of rational cones
\sigma\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}
such that for each
\sigma\in\Sigma
, each face of
\sigma
is also in
\Sigma
, and for any
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\Sigma
, the intersection
\sigma_{1}\cap\sigma_{2}
is a face of each, hence also in
\Sigma
. The support of the fan is
|\Sigma|=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\sigma\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Given a fan
\Sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
, the associated toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
is defined by gluing the affine toric varieties
U_{\sigma},U_{\sigma^{\prime}}
along
\sigma\cap\sigma^{\prime}
for any
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma
. In fact, any normal separated toric variety comes from a fan
\Sigma
.

Geometric information of a toric variety can be deduced from the combinatorial data of its fan. For example,
X_{\Sigma}
is smooth if and only if every cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
is smooth;
X_{\Sigma}
is an orbifold if and only if every cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
is simplicial;
X_{\Sigma}
is compact if and only if
\Sigma
is complete, i.e. the support
|\Sigma|=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\sigma
is the entire
N_{\mathbb{R}}
.

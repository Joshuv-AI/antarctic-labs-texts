Gregory-Laflamme-type instability of boson strings and related phases in 𝑫 = 𝟓 D=5 Kaluza-Klein theory
C. Herdeiro, and E. Radu

Abstract
We add an S 1 S^{1} extra dimension (size L L ) to the well known D = 4 D=4 static, spherically symmetric Q Q -balls and boson stars. We show that the resulting uniform horizonless boson strings possess a static zero-mode for a critical value of L L . This is at the threshold of a Gregory-Laflamme instability of these objects, occuring for larger values of L L . The non-linear continuation of the zero-mode yields D = 5 D=5 non-uniform boson strings . In addition, there are also intrinsic D = 5 D=5 solutions describing localized boson stars on the S 1 S^{1} , supported by the contribution of the scalar field Kaluza-Klein modes. Basic properties of all three types of aforementioned solutions are discussed, together with their phase space.

The dimensionality of space (time) is a long-standing

Then (vacuum) gravity in

This idea has proven one of the most fruitful

In an attempt to provide a unified framework for all interactions,

2.1
The

We consider the usual action for

\displaystyle S=\int d^{5}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{1}{2}g^{ab}\left(\Psi_{,\,a}^{*}\Psi_{,\,b}+\Psi_{,\,b}^{*}\Psi_{,\,a}\right)+U(|\Psi|)\right],

\displaystyle\nabla_{a}\nabla^{a}\Psi=\frac{dU}{d\left|\Psi\right|^{2}}\Psi,

\displaystyle T_{ab}=\partial_{a}\Psi^{*}\partial_{b}\Psi+\partial_{b}\Psi^{*}\partial_{a}\Psi-g_{ab}\left[\frac{1}{2}g^{cd}(\partial_{c}\Psi^{*}\partial_{d}\Psi+\partial_{c}\Psi^{*}\partial_{d}\Psi)+U(\left|\Psi\right|)\right]\ .

In what follows we shall focus on the

\displaystyle U(\Psi)=\mu^{2}|\Psi|^{2}-\lambda|\Psi|^{4}+\nu|\Psi|^{6}~,

(6)

The ground state of the model is

We shall (mainly) consider

\displaystyle\Psi\equiv\psi(r,z)e^{-i\omega t},~~{\rm where}~~\psi(r,z)=\psi(r,z+L),

From (

\displaystyle\psi_{,rr}+\frac{2\psi_{,r}}{r}+\psi_{,zz}(\omega^{2}-\mu^{2})\psi+2\lambda\psi^{3}-3\nu\psi^{5}=0~.

(8)

The energy density of a configuration reads

\displaystyle\rho\equiv-T_{t}^{t}=\psi_{,r}^{2}+\psi_{,z}^{2}+(\omega^{2}+\mu^{2})\psi^{2}-\lambda\psi^{4}+\nu\psi^{6}~,

(9)

\displaystyle M=4\pi\int_{0}^{L}dz\int_{0}^{\infty}dr~r^{2}\rho\ .

(10)

Let us remark that

3

\Psi\rightarrow e^{i\alpha}\Psi

\alpha
is a constant.
This implies that the current 4-vector

j^{a}_{\ ;a}=0

Therefore, integrating the timelike component of this current on a spacelike slice

\displaystyle Q_{N}=\int_{\Sigma}~j^{t}=8\pi\omega\int_{0}^{L}dz\int_{0}^{\infty}dr~r^{2}\psi^{2}(r,z).

(11)

The

8

\displaystyle S(\psi)=\int_{0}^{\infty}dr~r^{2}\int_{0}^{L}dz\left[\psi_{,r}^{2}+\psi_{,z}^{2}+U(\psi)-\omega^{2}\psi^{2}\right],

(12)

which allows to derive a virial relation
by using a Derrick-type scaling technique

[
25

Let us assume the existence of a solution

Then each member of the 1-parameter family

Thus we obtain the virial relation

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dr~r^{2}\int_{0}^{L}dz\left[\psi_{,r}^{2}+3(\psi_{,z}^{2}+U(\psi)-\omega^{2}\psi^{2})\right]=0.

(13)

One notices that the
z

by the harmonic time dependence of the
scalar field.

3
The Einstein–Klein-Gordon solutions

3.1
The model

We now turn on gravity and consider
D=5
Einstein’s gravity minimally coupled to a massive complex scalar field
\Psi

without self-interaction
. Indeed, the coupling to gravity, and the non-linearities of the latter, are enough to allow the self-gravitating cousins of
Q
-balls,
i.e.
BSs.
The system is described by the action

S=\frac{1}{4\pi G_{5}}\int d^{5}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{1}{4}R-\frac{1}{2}g^{ab}\left(\Psi_{,\,a}^{*}\Psi_{,\,b}+\Psi_{,\,b}^{*}\Psi_{,\,a}\right)-\mu^{2}\Psi^{*}\Psi\right],

(34)

where
G_{5}
is Newton’s constant.
Variation of this action with respect to the metric and scalar
field gives the EKG equations:

R_{ab}-\frac{1}{2}g_{ab}R=2~T_{ab},~~\left(\nabla^{2}-\mu^{2}\right)\Psi=0~,

(35)

where

T_{ab}=\Psi_{,a}^{*}\Psi_{,b}+\Psi_{,b}^{*}\Psi_{,a}-g_{ab}\left[\frac{1}{2}g^{cd}\left(\Psi_{,c}^{*}\Psi_{,d}+\Psi_{,d}^{*}\Psi_{,c})+\mu^{2}|\Psi|^{2}\right)\right]~,

(36)

is the
stress-energy tensor of the scalar field.

D=5
solutions of (
34
),
with no-dependence on the extra-dimension, can be KK reduced to a
D=4
configuration, using the KK metric ansatz:

\displaystyle ds_{5}^{2}=e^{-\alpha\phi(x)}ds_{4}^{2}+e^{2\alpha\phi(x)}(dz+2A_{i}(x)dx^{i})^{2},~{\rm with}~ds_{4}^{2}=g_{ij}^{(4)}(x)dx^{i}dx^{j}~~{\rm and}~~\alpha=\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}.{~}

After integrating over the
z-
coordinate and
dropping a boundary term,
the resulting
D=4
action reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{4}=\frac{1}{4\pi G_{4}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}d^{4}x\sqrt{-g^{(4)}}\bigg[\frac{1}{4}R^{(4)}-\frac{1}{4}e^{3\alpha\psi}F_{ij}F^{ij}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{i}\phi\partial^{i}\phi

(37)

\displaystyle{~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~}-\frac{1}{2}g^{ij(4)}\left(\partial_{i}\Psi^{*}\partial_{j}\Psi+\partial_{j}\Psi^{*}\partial_{i}\Psi\right)-\mu^{2}|\Psi|^{2}e^{-\alpha\phi}\bigg]~,

with

G_{4}=G_{5}/L
,
\phi
the dilaton and
F=dA

a
U(1)
field.
Note that while

is a consistent truncation,
one can set
\phi=0
in the vacuum case, only.

Finally, let us mention that
the numerical integration is performed with
dimensionless variables introduced
by using natural units set by
\mu

\displaystyle(r,z)\to(r,z)/\mu,~~{\rm and}~~\omega\to\omega/\mu,

(38)

(as well as

).
As a result, the dependence on
\mu
disappears from the

and the only input parameter is the (scaled)
frequency

that enters the scalar field ansatz.
Also, the quantities of interest are expressed in units set by
\mu
and
G_{5}
. In order to simplify the output, however, we set

in all figures.
For the localized solitons with
\mu=0
in Section (
3.3.3
),
we shall use instead a scaling with respect to the
length

of the extra-dimension.

3.3.1
The approach and quantities of interest

Given the existence of zero modes together with the results for

In principle, these solutions can be
studied by employing the Ansatz
(

However, it turns out that

typically very slow, while the
constraint equations were not satisfied with enough
accuracy
7
7

7

A similar situation has been found
when considering a different metric gauge choice,
with a conformally flat form of the
(r,z)
-part.
.

To overcome this problem,
we have chosen to
construct the
z
-dependent solutions
by employing the
Einstein-De Turck (EDT) approach, as proposed in Refs.
[
32
,
33
]
,
which
has the advantage of not fixing

yielding at the same time elliptic equations
(see
[
34

35

for reviews).
In this approach, one solves a modified version
of the Einstein equations, with

\displaystyle R_{ab}-\nabla_{(a}\xi_{b)}=2\left(T_{ab}-\frac{1}{2}Tg_{ab}\right)\ .

(42)

Here,
\xi^{a}
is a vector defined as

\xi^{a}\equiv g^{bc}(\Gamma_{bc}^{a}-\bar{\Gamma}_{bc}^{a})\ ,

where

\Gamma_{bc}^{a}
is the Levi-Civita connection associated to the
spacetime metric
g
that one wants to determine, and a reference metric
\bar{g}
is introduced,
with
\bar{\Gamma}_{bc}^{a}
being the corresponding Levi-Civita connection.
Solutions to (
42
) solve the Einstein equations
iff
\xi^{a}\equiv 0
everywhere.
To achieve this,
we impose boundary conditions which are compatible with

\xi^{a}=0

on the boundary of the domain of integration.
Then, this should imply
\xi^{a}\equiv 0
everywhere,
a condition which is verified from the numerical output.

In this approach, we use a metric Ansatz
with five functions,
\{A,B_{1},B_{2},C,S\}

which depend on
(r,z)

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-e^{2A(r,z)}dt^{2}+e^{2B_{1}(r,z)}dr^{2}+e^{2B_{2}(r,z)}(dz+S(r,z)dr)^{2}+e^{2C(r,z)}r^{2}d\Omega_{2}^{2},{~~}

(43)

while the ansatz for the
scalar field is still given by (
7

The problem reduces to
solve a set of six partial differential equations with suitable
boundary conditions (BCs).
The reference metric
\bar{g}

corresponds to the
{\cal M}_{4}\times S^{1}
background
(
i.e.

A=B_{1}=B_{2}=C=S=0

The BCs are found by
constructing an approximate form of the solutions on the
boundary of the domain of integration compatible with the requirement
\xi^{a}=0
,
regularity of the solutions plus the assumption of standard KK asymptoticss.
The imposed BCs read

\displaystyle\partial_{r}A\big|_{r=0}=\partial_{r}B_{1}\big|_{r=0}=\partial_{r}B_{2}\big|_{r=0}=\partial_{r}\psi\big|_{r=0}=0,~~(C-B_{1})\big|_{r=0}=0,~~S\big|_{r=0}=0,

(44)

\displaystyle A\big|_{r=\infty}=B_{1}\big|_{r=\infty}=B_{2}\big|_{r=\infty}=C\big|_{r=\infty}=S\big|_{r=\infty}=\psi\big|_{r=\infty}=0,~

\displaystyle\partial_{z}A\big|_{z=0,L}=\partial_{z}B_{1}\big|_{z=0,L}=\partial_{z}B_{2}\big|_{z=0,L}=\partial_{z}C\big|_{z=0,L}=S\big|_{z=0,L}=\partial_{z}\psi\big|_{z=0,L}=0\ .

In addition, we
assume reflection symmetry
w.r.t.

z=L/2
,

The equation for
\{A,B_{1},B_{2},C,S;\psi\}

(usually) around 300×50 points.
Using the Newton-Raphson approach, the resulting system is
solved iteratively until convergence is achieved,
by employing a professional solver which uses a finite difference method

[
36
,
37
,
38
]
.
The typical numerical errors for the reported solutions
are estimated

10^{-4}
.

Turning to quantities of interest,
a generic static spacetime with

\displaystyle g_{tt}\simeq-1+\frac{c_{t}}{r},~~~g_{zz}\simeq 1+\frac{c_{z}}{r},

The results in

[

\displaystyle M=\frac{\Omega_{2}L}{16\pi G_{5}}(2c_{t}-c_{z}),~~{\mathcal{T}}=\frac{\Omega_{2}}{16\pi G_{5}}(c_{t}-2c_{z}),

(46)

where

4
Conclusions and remarks

The study of configurations in more than

D=4
dimensions is a subject of long
standing interest in General Relativity, the case of a
KK theory with only one (compact)
extra-dimension providing the simplest model.
In a surprising development,
Gregory and Laflamme showed that the Schwarzschild black string solution is unstable
to linearized perturbations with a long wavelength along the circle

[
8
]
.
This leads to the existence of a branch of
(vacuum)
solutions with a non-trivial dependence
of the extra-dimension, the
non-uniform
black strings.
In addition, there are also
localized

black holes, which
are Schwarzschild-Tangherlini–like near the horizon,
despite approaching asymptotically the KK background.

Until recently, studies of the GL-type instability
(together with the emerging non-uniform configurations)
dealt with the case of black objects. Recently, however, a study of the GL instability for the case of
D=2+1+1
flat spacetime
Q
-strings emerged
[
16
]
, motivating the study of the consequent phases of equilibrium solutions.

This work is a first attempt in this direction, restricting to the simplest case of a massive
complex scalar field.
The main reported results can be summarize as follows.
The uniform
Q
-strings are unstable to linearized perturbations, with a long wavelength along the circle. At the threshold of the instability,
both the
Q
-strings and the EKG boson strings
possess a GL-like zero-mode, with a non-trivial dependence of the extra-dimension. This supports the idea that the existence of
a GL-type instability
is a more general feature of configurations
extending into
an extra-dimension,
not necessarily connected
with the presence of an horizon (with the associated thermodynamics).

The non-linear continuation of the zero-modes
result in a new phase of the
D=5
solutions –
tbe
non-uniform
strings. In addition, there are also intrinsic
five-dimensional solutions –
the
localized
solitons,
which are supported by the KK (excited) modes of
the scalar field. Note that such configurations exist as well for a
massless scalar field.

A list of possible directions for future work naturally emerges. One could find the unstable mode of the uniform EKG strings
and show that the solutions with
L>L_{0}

are unstable, as expected. It would be interesting
to clarify the connection with the Jeans

[
7
]

and Rayleigh-Plateau

[
15
]
instabilities and to
explain why the number of nodes
n
in the
z
-direction
is conserved along sequences of solutions.
For example, why the non-uniform strings
always have
n=0

and do not develop any nodes?

Apart from the fundamental solutions
in this work, one could also consider their excitations, for
which
\psi
oscillates around zero
along the radial direction.
These can be parameterized by the number
of radial nodes of the scalar amplitude.
Constructing the generalizations of the
localized solutions in a model with quartic self-interaction
(
i.e.
and extra
\lambda|\Psi|^{4}
term in the action (
34
)) should also be possible.
Likely, as with the
D=4
case
[
42
]
,
the resulting configuration may differ markedly from
the non-interacting case, with a much larger mass,

M_{max}\sim\sqrt{\lambda}
.
Technically more difficult, one could try to find the spinning generalizations of the solutions
in this work,
together with their black holes counterparts.

Let us conclude with two more general questions.
Firstly, for all unstable configurations what
the end-state of the perturbed system?
Do the non-uniform configurations occur dynamically
(when perturbing a uniform solution), being the end point of evolution?
And what is the status of the localized solitons?
We recall that, for the same Noether charge, the
non-uniform EKG strings are more massive than
the uniform strings, and so could not be the putative new
end-state.
And what is the situation with
Q
-solutions?
Secondly,
similar features should appear in models with a more realistic matter content.
For example, we predict the existence of non-uniform
non-Abelian magnetic vortices
for the Einstein–Yang-Mills-Higgs
model in
Ref.
[
43
]
.

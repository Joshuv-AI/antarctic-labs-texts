Intersections of Sylow 2 2 -subgroups in symmetric groups Thanks: SE is supported by the Royal Society.
Sean Eberhard

Abstract
. We compute the asymptotic probability that a random pair of Sylow 2 2 -subgroups in S n S_{n} or A n A_{n} intersects trivially. This calculation complements recent work of Diaconis, Giannelli, Guralnick, Law, Navarro, Sambale, and Spink.



Lately there has been a surge of interest in intersections of Sylow subgroups in finite groups.
A particularly bold general conjecture was proposed recently by Lisi and Sabatini
[
4
]
.
In the special case of symmetric groups, the main theorem of
[
1
]
is the striking result that if
p>2
then pairs of random Sylow
p
-subgroups tend to intersect trivially, while if
p=2
then the intersection is nontrivial with probability
at least
approximately
1-e^{-1/2}+o(1)
.
One of the key ideas in the proof is to use a theorem of the author and Daniele Garzoni on random generation of
A_{n}
by elements of fixed cycle type
[
2
]
.
In this note we prove the following result about intersection of Sylow
2
-subgroups, complementing the theorem of
[
1
]
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{2}(S_{n})
and let
x\in S_{n}
be chosen uniformly at random. Then

\Pr{\left(P\cap P^{x}=1\right)}=e^{-1/2}+O(1/n).

If
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{2}(A_{n})
then similarly

\Pr{\left(P\cap P^{x}=1\right)}=(3/2)e^{-1/2}+O(1/n).

Let
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{2}(S_{n})
.
The structure of
P
is summarized in
[
1
]
*§2.1.
Suppose
n=2^{e_{1}}+\cdots+2^{e_{m}}
where
e_{1}>\cdots>e_{m}\geq 0
. Then
P
is naturally associated to a forest
F
of
m
complete binary trees of heights
e_{1},\dots,e_{m}
whose leaves are labelled by
\{1,\dots,n\}
, and
P
can be identified with
\operatorname{Aut}(F)
.
Let
A\leq P
be the elementary abelian subgroup of rank
\left\lfloor n/2\right\rfloor
that stabilizes the vertices of positive height.

Lemma 2
.

Let
s\geq 0
be an even integer.

(1)

\#\{g\in A:\operatorname{supp}(g)=s\}\leq O(1)^{s}(n/s)^{s/2}
.

(2)

\#\{g\in P\smallsetminus A:\operatorname{supp}(g)=s\}\leq O(1)^{s}(n/s)^{s/2-1}
.

Proof.

(1)

Since
A
is generated by
\left\lfloor n/2\right\rfloor
disjoint transpositions, the number of
g\in A
of support
s
is exactly
\binom{\left\lfloor n/2\right\rfloor}{s/2}
,
which is at most
(en/s)^{s/2}
.

(2)

To each
g\in P
we can associate a subforest of
F
, as follows.
Let
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}
be the minimal vertices of
F
stabilized by
g
of positive height.
Let
T_{i}
be the subtree with root
v_{i}
and let
h_{i}
be the height of
T_{i}
, so that
T_{i}
has
2^{h_{i}}
leaves permuted by
g
without fixed points.
Then the support of
g
is
s=2^{h_{1}}+\cdots+2^{h_{k}}
,
and
g\in A
if and only if
h_{i}=1
for all
i
.

Consider the ways of choosing
h_{1},\dots,h_{k}
and
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}
and
g
.
First, assume
h_{1},\dots,h_{k}
are given, where
h_{1}\geq\cdots\geq h_{k}>0
.
For each
h>0
let
m_{h}
be the number of
i
with
h_{i}=h
.
The number of vertices of height
h
is at most
n/2^{h}\leq n
, so the number of choices for
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}
is at most

\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{k}(n/2^{h_{i}})}{\prod_{h>0}m_{h}!}\leq\prod_{h>0}\frac{n^{m_{h}}}{m_{h}!}.

Next, the restriction of
g
to
T_{i}
must be an automorphism of
T_{i}
swapping the two main branches, of which there are
2^{2^{h_{i}}-2}
, so the number of choices for
g
given
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}
is
\prod_{i=1}^{k}2^{2^{h_{i}}-2}\leq 2^{s}
.
Thus the number of possibilities for
g
given
h_{1},\dots,h_{k}
, or equivalently given
(m_{h})
, is bounded by

2^{s}\prod_{h>0}\frac{n^{m_{h}}}{m_{h}!}.

It follows that the number of
g\in P\smallsetminus A
of support
s
is bounded by

(1)

2^{s}\sum_{(m_{h})}\prod_{h>0}\frac{n^{m_{h}}}{m_{h}!},

where the sum goes over all nonnegative integer solutions
(m_{h})_{h>0}
to

(2)

\sum_{h>0}2^{h-1}m_{h}=s/2,\hskip 40.00006ptm_{1}<s/2.

Let
(m_{h})
be a solution to (
2
).
Write
\sum m_{h}=s/2-t
. Then
t=\sum_{h>1}(2^{h-1}-1)m_{h}
.
In particular the number of solutions
(m_{h})
with a given
t
is bounded by the number of partitions of
t
, which is at most
2^{t}
. Also,

s/2=\sum_{h>0}2^{h-1}m_{h}\leq m_{1}+2\sum_{h>1}(2^{h-1}-1)m_{h}=m_{1}+2t,

so
m_{1}\geq s/2-2t
. Therefore our sum (
1
) is bounded by

2^{s}\sum_{0<t\leq s/2}\frac{2^{t}n^{s/2-t}}{\max(s/2-2t,0)!}.

If
2n^{-1}(s/2-2)(s/2-3)<1
then the largest term in the sum is the
t=1
term, so the sum is bounded by

s2^{s+1}\frac{n^{s/2-1}}{(s/2-2)!}=O(1)^{s}(n/s)^{s/2-1}.

This completes the proof for say
s<(2n)^{1/2}
.

For
s\geq(2n)^{1/2}
there is a phase transition and we analyze differently. Ignoring the restriction
m_{1}<s/2
, the sum (
1
) is the coefficient of
X^{s/2}
in the generating function

F(X)=2^{s}\prod_{h>0}{\left(\sum_{m_{h}\geq 0}\frac{n^{m_{h}}}{m_{h}!}X^{2^{h-1}m_{h}}\right)}=2^{s}\exp{\left(\sum_{h>0}nX^{2^{h-1}}\right)}.

Since this power series has nonnegative coefficients, for any real
x>0
the coefficient of
X^{s/2}
is bounded by

x^{-s/2}F(x)=x^{-s/2}2^{s}\exp{\left(\sum_{h>0}nx^{2^{h-1}}\right)}.

Set
x=s/(2n)
. Then the argument of the exponential is

\sum_{h>0}n(s/2n)^{2^{h-1}}=\frac{s}{2}\sum_{h>0}(s/2n)^{2^{h-1}-1}\leq\frac{s}{2}\sum_{e\geq 0}2^{-e}=s.

Therefore the sum (
1
) is bounded by
O(1)^{s}(n/s)^{s/2}
, which is the same as
O(1)^{s}(n/s)^{s/2-1}
in this region, so this completes the proof.
∎

Now let
x\in S_{n}
be chosen uniformly at random.

Lemma 3
.

P\cap P^{x}=A\cap A^{x}
with probability
1-O(1/n)
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that
P\cap P^{x}\subseteq A
with probability
1-O(1/n)
, as
P\cap P^{x}\subseteq A^{x}
with the same probability.
The expected size of
P\cap P^{x}\smallsetminus A
is

\operatorname{E}|P\cap P^{x}\smallsetminus A|=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\frac{|\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}\cap P|\ |\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}\cap P\smallsetminus A|}{|\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}|},

where
\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}\subset S_{n}
is the class of elements of type
\lambda
,
and obviously we may restrict to those
\lambda
whose parts are powers of
2
, and in particular even support (where the
support
of a partition is the sum of its nonunit parts).
There are at most
2^{s}
partitions of support
s
.
Also, if
\lambda
has even support
s
and then
|\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}|\geq O(1)^{-s}s^{-s/2}n^{s}
.
Indeed, if
g\in\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}\cap S_{s}
then
|g^{S_{n}}|=\binom{n}{s}|g^{S_{s}}|\geq(n/s)^{s}|g^{S_{s}}|
and
|g^{S_{s}}|\geq O(1)^{-s}s^{s/2}
(see for example
[
2
]
*Lemma 4.7).
Therefore from the previous lemma we obtain the bound

\operatorname{E}|P\cap P^{x}\smallsetminus A|=\sum_{s=2}^{n}O(1)^{s}\frac{(n/s)^{s-1}}{s^{-s/2}n^{s}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{s=2}^{\infty}O(1)^{s}s^{-s/2}=O(1/n).

It follows that
P\cap P^{x}\smallsetminus A
is nonempty with probability
O(1/n)
.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

1
.

By the previous lemma we have
P\cap P^{x}=A\cap A^{x}
with probability
1-O(1/n)
, so it suffices to understand
A\cap A^{x}
. By
[
1
]
*§4.2,
A\cap A^{x}
is elementary abelian of rank
W
where
W
is a random variable that is asymptotically Poisson with parameter
1/2
.
In fact it is routine to verify that the convergence has rate
O(1/n)
.
Therefore

\Pr(P\cap P^{x}=1)=\Pr(W=0)+O(1/n)=e^{-1/2}+O(1/n).

Similarly,

\Pr(P\cap P^{x}\cap A_{n}=1)=\Pr(W\leq 1)+O(1/n)=(3/2)e^{-1/2}+O(1/n).

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4
.

For comparison, if
G
is a large simple group of Lie type and
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G)
, it is most likely true that
\Pr(P\cap P^{x}=1)=1-o(1)
unless
p
is the defining characteristic of
G
and
p
is small compared to the rank of
G
.
If
G=\operatorname{PSL}_{n}(p)
and
p
is bounded then
\Pr(P\cap P^{x}=1)
tends to zero, but comparatively slowly.
These observations suggest a probabilistic approach to the problem of Vdovin
[
3
]
*15.40.

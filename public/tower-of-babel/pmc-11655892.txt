The landscape in telomere related gene prognostic signature for survival and medication treatment effectiveness prediction in hepatocellular carcinoma
Authors: Kai Zhou, Xingyu Liu, Mingda Wang, Jinjiang Duan, Xu Zhao, Hanjun Yin
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTelomeres, made of repetitive DNA sequences and shelterin complexes, which were found at the ends of chromosomes and had been extensively studied in cancer research. However, in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) was still relatively scarce. In this study, we investigated the correlation between telomerase-related genes (TRGs) and the prognosis and immunotherapy of HCC patients to enhance clinical outcomes.MethodsIn this work, TRGs were gathered using TelNet, while clinical information and gene expression data for HCC patients were retrieved from the Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database. A risk prediction model based on TRGs was created using COX and Lasso regression analyses, with ROC curves used to assess prognostic efficacy. Univariate and multifactorial COX regression analyses were used to determine if the risk model had an independent impact on prognosis. Nomograms were created to enhance clinical usability, and calibration curves were used to assess predictive ability at various time points. The Tumor Immune Dysfunction and Exclusion (TIDE) score was used to analyze differences in immune infiltrating cells between risk groups. The study analyzed the relationship between risk ratings and drug treatment effectiveness using data from the CellMiner database. The hub gene was identified and its relationship to prognostic markers of HCC patients was examined. The expression of hub genes in immune cell subpopulations was also investigated by single-cell data.Results2093 TRGs were identified, with 949 showing significant differences in expression between HCC and paracancerous tissues. Seven risk genes were overexpressed in tumor tissues, leading to lower survival rates in high-risk patients. Risk model could independently predict the prognosis of HCC patients. Analysis of tumor immune infiltrating cells revealed significant differences in cell abundance between risk groups, with notable variations in immune subset enrichment between subgroups. Higher risk scores correlated with increased sensitivity to sorafenib, mitoxantrone, oxaliplatin, gemcitabine, and entinostat, while sensitivity decreased for vincristine, etc. CDCA8 was identified as a key gene in the Protein Interaction Network, while high expression associated with poorer overall survival, tumor proliferation and metastasis. The results of single-cell data analysis suggest that CDCA8 may promote the development of HCC by affecting T lymphocytes.ConclusionThe TRG-based risk model could predict HCC patient prognosis and closely linked to tumor immune environment, which could offer new possibilities for clinical treatment.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-024-01659-w.

Introduction

The most prevalent type of primary liver cancer is hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), which is a tumor caused by the malignant alteration of liver cells. It is the third biggest cause of cancer death, after only lung and colorectal cancers, and it endangers people's health and lives [1]. The majority of individuals with HCC were already in advanced stages when they are first diagnosed because of the atypical clinical presentations that cause delays in seeking consultation. The traditional methods for treating HCC include surgery, chemotherapy, and radiofrequency ablation, however, in patients with advanced stages, the therapeutic efficacy of these treatments is limited, and they are more likely to recur, which worsens the prognosis. As a result, novel therapeutic approaches are desperately needed to improve the patients' prognosis [2].

Telomeres are small segments of DNA–protein complexes present at the ends of the linear chromosomes of eukaryotic cells, and their main roles are to maintain chromosome integrity and to control the cell division cycle [3]. Maintenance of telomere length is critical for the unlimited proliferation of human cancer cells, and sustained telomere shortening saves somatic cells from aberrant proliferation and tumourigenesis by inducing senescence or apoptosis [4]. Telomere length affects gene activation or silencing, and it has been discovered that genes are silenced while telomeres are long early in life and active as telomeres shorten [5]. Telomeres are essential for chromosome stability, and telomere length may change during cell division and in certain disease states. Abnormal changes in telomeres are closely associated with the onset and progression of certain cancers, and even telomere length is closely correlated with tumor size, lymph node metastasis, histological grading and certain subtypes, so the study of telomeres can help in the diagnosis and treatment of cancer [6, 7].

The mechanism of telomere maintenance in cancer is a complex process associated with hundreds of different genes, and these telomere-associated genes (TRGs) have also shown a strong association with the prognosis of cancer patients [8]. In kidney renal clear cell cancer, a risk score model using TRGs expression levels correlates with immune subtypes and tumor mutational load to predict the prognosis of renal cancer patients [9]. The expression of TRGs is likewise a prognostic marker for predicting overall survival in patients with non-small cell lung cancer [10]. The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database similarly highlights the relevance of telomeres and TRGs to the prognosis of endometrial cancer [3]. Existing literature suggests that telomerase plays an important role in the pathological process of HCC development, and that telomere length increases in poorly differentiated HCC compared to better differentiated HCC, implying that telomerase activation and chromosomal stability restoration may be closely related to tumor cell activity [11, 12].

In this study, we constructed a prognostic prediction model for patients with HCC based on TRGs as well as patients' clinical and pathological characteristics, and further analyzed the correlation between risk scores and the tumor immune microenvironment and therapeutic drug sensitivity. To further explore the potential mechanisms of TRGS in causing HCC progression, hub genes in the risk model were screened using protein-interaction network analysis, analyzed for correlation with tumor proliferation, epithelial mesenchymal transition and inflammatory response, and their biological functions were verified using single-cell data analysis.

Methods

The data acquisition

Gene expression data and clinicopathological characterization of the TCGA-HCC cohort were obtained from the publicly available database TCGA (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/), and gene expression data of the TRGs were obtained from TelNet (http://www.cancertelsys.org/telnet/). Patients with missing follow-up information and clinicopathologic characteristics were excluded, and 377 patients were finally included for subsequent data analysis. Clinicopathologic characteristics included age, gender, Grade and Stage. All analyzed data are publicly available and therefore do not require ethical approval.

TRGs determination

Differentially expressed genes were analyzed in cancer and paracancer tissues of HCC patients included in this study using the "edge R package", and the differentially expressed genes and TRGs obtained were further intersected to obtain TRGs differentially expressed in cancer and paracancer tissues. Principal component analysis (PCA) was used to better characterize and differentiate the expression of TRGs in cancer and paracancer tissues. Significantly differentially expressed genes were selected according to |logFC|> 1, fdr < 0.05 and the top 100 most differentially expressed genes were shown in a heat map.

Prognosis-related TRGs screening and risk model construction

Survival data of the included 377 patients with HCC were obtained and combined with the expression of significantly differentially expressed TRGs, a one-way COX regression analysis was performed to screen out TRGs that were closely associated with prognosis. The differentially expressed genes were further screened and downscaled using Lasso regression, and the obtained genes were used to construct a risk prediction model with the formula: exp1 × coef1 + exp2 × coef2 + …… + expn × coefn (Coef is the gene regression coefficient, and exp is the gene expression level). The median risk score was used to divide the samples into two subgroups, high-risk and low-risk, and Kaplan–Meier survival curves were used to estimate the survival differences between the two subgroups.

Construction and validation of Nomograms

To validate the independence of this risk score model from traditional clinical variables and its independent predictive efficacy, this study used univariate and multivariate COX regression analyses to validate the independence of the model; the area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC) was used to assess the independent predictive efficacy of this risk score model with respect to the included clinicopathologic factors and the predictive efficacy of the model at 1, 3, and 5 years. Nomogram was constructed to predict the probability of patient survival at 1, 3, and 5 years based on the extent to which each influencing factor in the model contributed to the outcome variable. Calibration curves were also used to verify the predictive accuracy of the nomogram for 1, 3 and 5 years.

Correlation analysis between the risk score model and the immune microenvironment of HCC patients

The file "LM22.txt" was downloaded from CIBERSORT (https:∥cibersortx.stanford.edu) as a characterization gene file, which records gene expression characterization data for 22 immune cells. The "CIBERSORT "R package was used to calculate the abundance ratios of 22 immune cell types in the high-risk and low-risk groups. Due to the considerable heterogeneity that prevails among tumor types, this study further explored the enrichment relationships between different risk groups and six different immune subtypes (C1: wound healing; C2: IFN-γ dominant; C3: inflammatory; C4: lymphocyte depleted; C5: immunologically quiet and C6: TGF-β dominant). The Tumor Immune Dysfunction and Rejection (TIDE) score can be used to assess the likelihood of tumor immune escape based on gene expression profiles of tumor samples; tumor immune escape consists of two mechanisms that inhibit the activity of cytotoxic T lymphocytes and suppress the abundance of their immune infiltration, and TIDE predicts a tumor's immune escape ability by comprehensively assessing the activity of these two mechanisms.

Drug sensitivity analysis

In this study, we used the CellMiner database to download the data related to drug semi-inhibitory concentration (IC50) and tumor cell lines. The CellMiner database allows us to explore the transcriptional and drug patterns, so that we can understand the mechanism of action and effect of different drugs on different cancer cell lines. Pearson correlation analysis was used to identify the correlation between risk scores and IC50, and the top 9 drugs with the strongest correlation were selected.

Hub genes idenfitied

We used the STRING database and cytoscape software to build a network of protein interactions based on seven risk genes to find the hub genes in the risk model. Sankey diagrams then were created by using the "galluvial R package" to explore the association between the expression of the screened hub genes and the age, grade, stage and survival status of HCC patients, who were categorized into two groups according to age > 60 and ≦60 years. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis was used to explore the effect of hub genes on overall survival (OS), progression-free survival (PFS), and disease-free survival (DFS) in patients with HCC. Functional analysis of hub gene was further performed to explore its correlation with tumor cell proliferation and epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT).

Single-cell analysis

Download the corresponding single-cell data in h5 format and annotation results from TISCH. Use the R software MAESTRO and Seurat to process and analyze the single-cell data. Re-cluster the cells using the t-SNE method.

Data analysis

R (version: 4.3.2) software was used for all data processing in this study. Shapiro–Wilk test was used to test the normality of continuously distributed variables, Student's t-test was used for comparison of normally distributed continuous variables, and Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used for non-normally distributed ones, and P < 0.05 (two-sided) was considered statistically significant.

To further analyze, 949 differential genes with significant differences in tumor and paracancerous tissues were identified based on the significant differential expression condition |logFC|> 1, fdr < 0.05 (Supplementary Table 2). Construction and validation of a risk scoring model for TRGs based on the TCGA-HCC cohort

Among the 949 TRGs screened above that were significantly differentially expressed in HCC and paracancerous tissues, we identified 657 genes (Supplementary Table 3) that were strongly associated with prognosis using univariate COX regression analysis in conjunction with patients' clinical information (Fig. 2A). After LASSO regression analysis, we finally selected seven core differential genes to construct the risk score model: CDCA8, DTYMK, KPNA2, MRTO4, RAD54B, RRAGC and RTN3, and the risk score calculation equation was:CDCA8 × 0.02 + DTYMK × 0.004 + KPNA2 × 0.152 + MRTO4 × 0.013 + RAD54B × 0.095 + RRAGC × 0.049 + RTN3 × 0.03 (Fig. 2B). Compared to paracancerous tissues, these seven hub gene expressions were significantly elevated in tumor tissues (Fig. 2C). Patients were divided into low-risk and high-risk groups based on their median risk score. Construction and validation of nomograms based on risk scores and clinicopathologic indicators

The results of univariate and multivariate COX regression showed that the risk score model could be done as an independent predictor of prognosis in patients with HCC (Fig. 3A and B). The results of the ROC curve showed a better predictive performance of the risk score (AUC = 0.811), which was superior to the indicators of age (AUC = 0.0.492), gender (AUC = 0.508), grade (0.489) and stage (0.712) (Fig. 3C). Furthermore, the survival prediction AUC values of this prediction model for 1, 3, and 5 years were 0.811, 0.697, and 0.643, indicating that this risk scoring model performed well (Fig. 3D). Nomograms are appropriate for joint diagnosis or prediction of disease onset and progression using numerous indicators, and they can be used to assess the effectiveness of prediction models. To further evaluate the model's validity and accuracy, we created nomograms, and the results revealed that the model could accurately predict the 1-, 3-, and 5-year prognosis probability of HCC patients (Fig. 3E). Risk score could predicts the treatment strategy

Exploring the correlation between risk scores and drug therapy showed that risk scores were significantly correlated with sensitivity to some drugs. Sorafenib (r = 0.31, P = 5 × 10–9), mitoxantrone (r = 0.28, P = 1.2 × 10–7), oxaliplatin (r = 0.29, P = 7.7 × 10–8), gemcitabine (r = 0.33, P = 3.9 × 10–10) and entinostat (r = 0.28, P = 2.1 × 10–7) were more sensitive when the risk score was higher, while vincristine (r = −0.3, P = 2.3 × 10–8), cyclophosphamide (r = −0.22, P = 4.5 × 10–5), paclitaxel (r = −0.45, P = 2.2 × 10–16) and 5-Fluorouracil (r = −0.38, P = 3.5 × 10–13) showed a gradual decrease in sensitivity as the risk score increased (Fig. 5A-I), and the results of the above study may provide guidance for clinical drug therapy in HCC.Fig. 5Therapeutic sensitivity of targeted drugs. Discussion

Changes in telomeres and TRGs play an important role in the development of cancer. Although the role of telomeres and TRGs in chronic liver disease and hepatocellular carcinoma has been discussed, there is still a lack of comprehensive explorations of TRGs and biological mechanisms in HCC [12]. This study is the first to assess the role of TRGs in the prognosis of patients with HCC. We used the public database TCGA to obtain transcriptome data and clinical information of HCC patients, and TRGs were obtained from the TelNet database. On this basis we constructed a prognostic prediction model based on TRGs and further analysed the correlation between TRGs and the tumour immune microenvironment and targeted therapeutic agents. We found that TRGs could be a potential target for clinical treatment of HCC patients.

The results of the immune cell infiltration analysis showed significant differences in the abundance of 5 immune cell infiltrates between the different risk subgroups. Previous studies have classified tumours included in the TCGA database into six subtypes based on immune status: C1 (wound healing), C2 (IFN-g dominant), C3 (inflammatory), C4 (lymphocyte depleted), C5 (immunologically quiet) and C6 (TGF-b dominant) [13]. Tumours of different immune subtypes have different prognoses, with the C3 subtype having the best prognosis and the C4 and C6 subtypes having the worst prognostic profile. Our findings show a significant correlation between the risk score model constructed based on TRGs and immune subtypes (C1-C4, P < 0.001). Patients in the high-risk group had higher proportions of C1, C2, and C4 than those in the low-risk group, as well as lower proportions of C3 subtypes. The difference in immunological subtypes could be one of the explanations for the difference in prognosis between the high-risk and low-risk groups, and the lower proportion of C3 subtypes suggests that the high-risk group has a worse prognosis. Meanwhile, the TIDE scores of different subgroups were calculated according to the guidelines, and patients in the high-risk group had higher TIDE scores, suggesting that patients in the high-risk group may have achieved immune escape by inhibiting the activity of cytotoxic T-lymphocytes, thereby promoting tumour progression; patients in the low-risk group had lower TIDE scores, suggesting that they may have been able to promote tumour progression by blocking immune infiltration of cytotoxic T-lymphocytes, and that these patients may have had a relatively good response to immunotherapy [13, 14]. Overall, the findings show that prognostic disparities between high and low risk groups may be related to patients' varying immunological statuses, and that there is a link between TRGs and immunity.

The seven TRGs used in this study to construct risk score models play multiple roles in a variety of diseases, particularly in cancer. The cell division cycle associated gene 8 (CDCA8) plays an important role in the cell division cycle, its encoded protein is a component of the chromosome passenger complex (CPC), the CPC plays a role in accurate time and space, it plays very important roles in the medium-term chromosoom arrangement, sister chromosome separation, cystic division and so on [15, 16]. Cell division cycle-associated proteins and CPC have been found to be associated with the development of several human cancers [17–19]. CDCA8 was significantly upregulated in bladder cancer cell lines and clinical samples, promoted bladder cancer progression despite hypoxic conditions, and its expression was positively correlated with poorer bladder cancer grading, staging and overall survival [20]. Highly expressed CDCA8 is an independent influencer of poor prognosis in patients with hepatocellular carcinoma [21]. CDCA8 is also involved in thyroid cancer development by regulating CDK1 [22]. In a study exploring potential prognostic biomarkers for glioblastoma, CDCA8 was found to correlate well with genes in the P53 signalling pathway that regulate the cell cycle, as well as correlating the level of gene expression of CDCA8 with the level of infiltration of CD4 + T cells and CD8 + T cells [23]. Deoxythymidylate kinase (DTYMK) can be used as a marker to predict the prognosis of pan-cancer and immune cell infiltration, and it has been found that the expression of DTYMK is elevated in tumour tissues and its expression varies in different stages of the tumour and in different immune subtypes. High DTYMK expression predicts a poorer prognosis for patients with HCC and lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), and its high expression is also closely related to the abundance of multiple immune cell infiltration [24]. Decreasing the expression of DTYMK significantly inhibited the growth of hepatocellular carcinoma and increased the sensitivity of hepatocellular carcinoma to oxaliplatin [25]. Karyopherin α2 (KPNA2, also known as importin α1) is a member of the importin α family of proteins that enables nuclear translocation of target proteins by recognising and binding to their nuclear localisation signals [26, 27]. KPNA2 is reported to be highly expressed in a variety of cancers [28]. Noetzel, E. et al. found that overexpression of KPNA2 promotes proliferation, migration, and cellular matrix adhesion of breast cancer cell lines MCF10A and MCF7 and reduces apoptosis of cancer cells [29]. KPNA2 interaction with CBX8 is involved in bladder carcinogenesis and progression by mediating the PRDM1/c-FOS pathway [30]. In hepatocellular carcinoma, there was a significant correlation between the expression of KPNA2 and the clinical features of aggressiveness, and high expression of KPNA2 was associated with poor prognosis of patients [31]. MRTO4 promotes hepatocyte progression by inhibiting ALDOB to enhance glycolytic processes in tumour cells [32]. RAD54B belongs to the SW12/SNF2 helicases superfamily and is involved in the re-regulation of protein duplex DNA complexes, thereby enhancing chromatin accessibility, and plays an important role in the development and progression of human cancer [33]. RAD54B overexpression promotes breast tumour growth and predicts prognosis in patients with luminal A breast cancer [34]. Compared with normal tissues, miR-215-5P is expressed at a lower level in breast cancer patients. miR-215-5P can target the 3`UTR region of RAD54B mRNA to downregulate RAD54B expression, inhibit breast cancer cell proliferation and promote the onset of apoptosis [35]. Similarly, decreasing the expression of RAD54B inhibited the proliferation and promoted the onset of apoptosis in hepatocellular carcinoma cells [36]. Reticulin (RNT) belongs to the RENETON family, a highly conserved class of genes that are preferentially expressed in neuroendocrine tissues [37]. RTN3 was shown to be strongly expressed in normal hepatocytes but low in hepatocellular carcinoma, and its high expression may prevent the proliferation of hepatocellular carcinoma cells and induce apoptosis by activating P53 [38]. Although the effect of RRAGC on hepatocellular carcinoma has not been studied, its mutation promotes the development of follicular lymphoma [39]. Overall, these seven risk genes have an important role in the development of many cancers, with a particularly significant effect on liver cancer.

Our study successfully constructed a TRG-based prognostic prediction model, investigated the relationship between the prognostic model and immune cell infiltration as well as drug sensitivity, and used single-cell data analysis to investigate the biological mechanism by which the hub gene CDCA8 contributes to HCC progression. Our study still has some limitations, on the one hand, more independent external databases or independent clinical trials may be needed to validate the model, on the other hand, we may need further studies to explore the variability of TRGs expression among HCC patients of different races and among HCC patients with different etiological causes, which could help to generalise to populations with different genetic backgrounds. Regarding the mechanism of CDCA8 regulation of T cells, further cellular experiments may be needed to explore it subsequently. Based on the results of this study, it can be inferred that CDCA8 is a cell cycle-associated protein, which plays an important role in cell proliferation and division, and future studies will need to further explore the role of CDCA8 in T cell proliferation, differentiation, and function, as well as how it interacts with other molecules and signalling pathways to influence the immune-regulatory function of T cells and the development of HCC.

Conclusion

In this study, we constructed a clinical prognostic prediction model for HCC patients using TRGs, and the findings demonstrated good clinical predictive efficacy. There was a strong correlation between the risk score model and the tumor immune microenvironment as well as drug treatment sensitivity in HCC patients, and single-cell analysis suggested that TRGs may influence the activity or infiltration abundance of T-lymphocytes and thus tumor progression. When combined, TRGs may help to develop new targeted therapeutics for HCC patients and improve clinical prognosis.

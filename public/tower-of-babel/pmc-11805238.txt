A Clinical Study on Evaluation of Vertical Accuracy of Interocclusal Registration Materials in Centric Relation—An In Vivo Study
Authors: Bansri Tank, Rupal J. Shah, Gunjit Marwaha, Neelima Chauhan, Ekta Chheda, Om Shah
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:The precise recording and transfer of maxillo-mandibular relationships are pivotal in prosthetic treatments.Aims and Objectives:To evaluate the efficacy of four bite registration materials: alu wax, hard wax, polyvinylsiloxane, and bis-acrylic resin, in accurately recording, maintaining, and reproducing the vertical interocclusal relationship on either side of the dentition.Materials and Methods:Thirty subjects within the age group of 20–45 years were selected. The difference in the reading of the vertical distance with the material and at maximum intercuspation is calculated individually for each record on the left and right sides.Results:On the right side, the lowest mean was observed in MIP and the highest in hard wax. On the left side, the lowest mean was observed in MIP, while the highest was in alu wax. Among the four bite registration materials evaluated, Luxabite and Jetbite exhibited the highest mean accuracy scores.Conclusions:Closure on interocclusal record material and bite release produce vertical discrepancies. Waxes showed the most, and bis-acrylic Luxabite and addition silicones showed the least dimensional distortion vertically.

INTRODUCTION

The accurate recording and subsequent transfer of the maxillo-mandibular relationship to an articulator are crucial for the success of any prosthetic treatment.[1] A crucial factor in this evaluation is the dimensional stability of the chosen material. Minimizing this deformation becomes paramount, as excessive dimensional changes can compromise the accuracy of mounted casts in the articulator.[23456] This investigation assessed the efficacy of four distinct bite registration materials (alu wax, hard wax, polyvinylsiloxane, and bis-acrylic resin) in accurately recording, maintaining, and reproducing the vertical interocclusal relationship on either side of the dentition.[78910]

METHODOLOGY

Study design: The present in vivo study was conducted in the Department of Prosthodontics, Govt Dental College and Hospital, Ahmedabad, Gujarat, India. The ethical clearance for the study was obtained from the Institutional Ethical Committee (Government Dental College and Hospital IEC GDCH/PROS.2/2021). Undergraduate and postgraduate dental students volunteered for the study. Thirty subjects within the age group 20–45 years were selected without any gender bias. Each subject underwent interocclusal registrations in centric relation with all four materials. Maxillary and mandibular casts were articulated with maximum intercuspation on the Hanau Wide Vue articulator. Each subject underwent interocclusal registrations in a centric relationship with all four materials. The interocclusal records were meticulously positioned on the occlusal surface of the maxillary cast, with the Lucia jig firmly in place, ensuring precise alignment before seating the mandibular cast over the records. The parameter selected was the vertical distance between the two predefined points, measured using a digital vernier caliper. The difference in reading of the vertical distance with the two materials and at maximum intercuspation is calculated individually for each record on the left and right sides.

RESULTS

Data analyses were done using SPSS 22.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL). The level of significance was set at P < 0.05. The normality of the data was assessed using Shapiro–Wilkinson test and descriptive statistics was performed to analyze mean and standard deviation. Intergroup analysis was done using Mann–Whitney U test and ANOVA, followed by Fisher’s LSD post hoc analysis. Comparison of MIP on the right and left sides showed statistically no significant difference on either side. P value is 0.84. One-way ANOVA analysis showed a statistically significant difference for the right side on intergroup comparisons. Results showed statistically significant difference using Fisher’s LSD post hoc test, with the lowest mean observed in MIP and the highest in hard wax. Table 1 shows mean and standard deviation on the left side for all four groups. The lowest mean was observed in MIP while the highest in alu wax. A statistically significant difference was seen on the left side in intergroup comparison (P < 0.05). Results also show a significant difference on intergroup comparison between alu wax and MIP on either side, whereas no significant difference was seen on intergroup comparison between hard wax and MIP, Jetbite and MIP, and Luxabite and MIP [Graph 1].

DISCUSSION

In this study, comparison between four different materials was done on the right and left sides. Among the four bite registration materials evaluated, Luxabite and Jetbite exhibited the highest mean accuracy scores, while hard wax yielded the lowest. This disparity might be attributed to the high viscosity of waxes, hindering their ability to faithfully capture intricate surface details. This observation aligns with previous research conducted.[111213] The high accuracy of polyvinylsiloxane is attributed to its addition polymerization reaction, which minimizes dimensional changes due to the absence of by-products. This finding aligns with numerous studies.[1415] Among the evaluated materials, Jetbite emerged as the most accurate and dimensionally stable, while hard wax exhibited the lowest performance.

CONCLUSION

Selecting the optimal interocclusal recording material necessitates careful consideration of both dimensional stability and minimal deformation. Clinicians must weigh the inherent properties of each material as per the patient. Closure on interocclusal record material and bite release produce vertical discrepancies. Waxes showed the most, and bis-acrylic Luxabite and addition silicones showed the least dimensional distortion vertically.

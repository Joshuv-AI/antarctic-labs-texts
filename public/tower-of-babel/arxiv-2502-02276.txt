Reevaluation of ALMA detection of circumstellar PH 3 in the AGB envelope IRC +10216: evidence for misidentification with HCN
M. Agúndez 1,*, L. Velilla-Prieto 1 and J. P. Fonfría 2 and J. Cernicharo 1

Abstract
The article ”Confirmation of interstellar phosphine towards asymptotic giant branch star IRC+10216” by A. Manna and S. Pal uses ALMA data of the C-star envelope IRC +10216 to claim a confirmation of the detection of PH 3 in this source. The article however incorrectly assign an emission feature observed in the ALMA spectrum of IRC +10216 to PH 3 , while we find that it arises from a highly vibrationally excited state of HCN. Concretely the feature can be confidently assigned to the J J = 3-2 ℓ = 0 \ell=0 transition of HCN in the ν 1 \nu_{1} + 4 ν 2 \nu_{2} vibrational state based on the observation of the ℓ = + 2 \ell=+2 and ℓ = − 2 \ell=-2 components of the same rotational transition, J J = 3-2, with the observed relative intensities in agreement with the relative line strengths. The detection of PH 3 in IRC +10216 remains confirmed based on the observation of the J J = 1-0 and J J = 2-1 lines with the single-dish telescopes IRAM 30m, ARO SMT 10m, and Herschel ( Agúndez et al. 2008 ; Agúndez et al. 2014 ; Tenenbaum & Ziurys 2008 ) .

The molecule phosphine (PH

The feature showed an anomalous line profile composed of a narrow component and a broader one with a U-shape, as observed with the IRAM 30m telescope, and a flat-topped shape when observed with the ARO SMT 10m telescope. The narrow component was confidently assigned to the

The main interest of the model spectrum is that it reproduces correctly the relative intensities of the three features that we identify as HCN lines, so that no additional line is necessary to explain the observations. Many other lines of HCN in highly vibrational states are clearly detected in the ALMA spectrum of IRC +10216 (see
Cernicharo et al. 2013

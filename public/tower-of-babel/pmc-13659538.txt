Conversion therapy in locally advanced biliary tract cancer: expanding the boundaries of resectability using ZSAB-TransGOLP regimen
Authors: Jacopo Mascherini, Iswanto Sucandy
Published: 2026

Biliary tract cancers (BTCs) remain among the most aggressive malignancies of the gastrointestinal tract. Although surgery remains the only potentially curative treatment, most patients present with unresectable disease at diagnosis. Recent advances in systemic therapy, particularly with integration of immune checkpoint inhibitors into gemcitabine-based chemotherapy regimens, have modestly improved survival in advanced BTCs (1,2). Objective response rates, however, remain relatively low, and the capability of systemic therapy to convert unresectable tumors to resectable ones remains low and uncertain. Similarly, the use of neoadjuvant systemic treatment for BTC in resectable patients is still very far from reaching a consensus.

In this context, the novel prospective phase-2 ZSAB-TransGOLP study from China by Shi and colleagues evaluates a triplet regimen consisting of tislelizumab, lenvatinib, gemcitabine, and oxaliplatin chemotherapy as conversion therapy for patients with initially unresectable locally advanced BTC (3). The authors report encouraging results, with 68% of patients ultimately undergoing surgery reaching an R0 resection rate of 63%. Median overall survival in the overall cohort reached 30.8 months. These outcomes substantially exceed those historically observed with systemic chemotherapy alone in advanced BTC. This finding suggests that intensified systemic combination of chemotherapy and immunotherapy may expand the population of patients with BTC eligible for curative-intent surgery.

The study authors also propose an innovative imaging approach for response evaluation. Recognizing the limitations of Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) v1.1 in assessing irregularly shaped tumors, the investigators developed a volumetric method termed three-dimensional twelve points (TDTP)-RECIST, based on three-dimensional reconstruction from twelve anatomical reference points. In exploratory post-hoc analyses, this approach showed stronger correlation with pathological response than the conventional RECIST criteria and resulted in a higher objective response rate (78% vs. 49%). Although intriguing, this methodology remains exploratory and requires independent validation before it can be further integrated into clinical decision-making.

While the results of the ZSAB-TransGOLP study are promising, several aspects warrant careful interpretation. First, the trial was conducted as a single-arm phase-2 study in a relatively small cohort of patients, limiting the ability to distinguish the true contribution of the systemic regimen from the effects of patient selection and surgical expertise. Conversion strategies are inherently susceptible to selection bias, as patients who respond to systemic therapy are also those most likely to benefit from surgery. Indeed, the markedly improved survival observed in the surgical subgroup compared with non-surgical patients may reflect both treatment response and favorable tumor biology. Another important feature of the study is the inclusion of multiple BTC subtypes, including intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, perihilar cholangiocarcinoma, and gallbladder cancer. Although this reflects the clinical reality that these malignancies are often grouped together in therapeutic trials, these tumors differ substantially in genetic make-up, biological behavior, patterns of spread, and surgical management. Consequently, interpretation of conversion strategies across such heterogeneous disease entities should be approached with caution, and future studies might benefit from subtype-specific analyses to better define which patients derive the greatest benefit from aggressive multimodal approaches.

Second, the remarkably high R0 resection rate reported in this study exceeds the conversion rates reported in most previous series of BTCs. This observation highlights an ongoing challenge in the field: the lack of standardized criteria in defining unresectability and in determining surgical conversion after systemic therapy. In the absence of universally accepted definitions of resectability, decisions regarding conversion surgery often rely on multidisciplinary assessment and institutional expertise. Establishing reproducible and widely accepted criteria for resectability will be essential to enable meaningful comparisons between studies and to guide patient selection for multimodal treatment strategies. Furthermore, detailed reporting of pathological margin assessment, including the criteria used to define R0 resection, would improve the reproducibility and comparability of conversion studies.

Third, despite the high conversion rate, recurrence remained frequent. Median recurrence-free survival after resection was only 8.4 months, underscoring the aggressive biology of BTC even after apparently curative resection. These findings highlight the need for improved systemic strategies capable not only of enabling resection but also of controlling micrometastatic disease. The investigators also explored several potential biomarkers of treatment response, including programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) expression and tumor-associated macrophage populations. However, none of these exploratory analyses demonstrated a clear association with clinical outcomes. The absence of robust predictive biomarkers highlights an important unmet need in the management of BTC. At present, no biomarker has demonstrated sufficient clinical validity to reliably identify those patients most likely to achieve successful conversion and durable disease control. Greater insight into clinical, pathological, and molecular determinants of treatment response could help refine patient selection and avoid unnecessary toxicity in patients unlikely to benefit from systemic treatment.

Nevertheless, the ZSAB-TransGOLP study provides an important signal supporting the feasibility of combining immunotherapy, anti-angiogenic therapy, and chemotherapy as a strategy to facilitate surgical resection in selected patients with locally advanced BTC. The concept of conversion therapy, long established in other malignancies such as colorectal liver metastases, is now gaining traction in BTC, where effective systemic regimens and multimodal strategies may allow a subset of patients to transition from palliative to potentially curative treatment pathways (4). Ultimately, the results of ZSAB-TransGOLP should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating rather than practice-changing. Randomized trials will be necessary to clarify whether intensified systemic therapy truly improves resectability and survival when compared with current standard regimens. As newer and more effective chemo- and immunotherapeutic agents become available for clinical application, various combinations and drug administration strategies may prove superior and more effective in treating BTC. Future investigations should also explore the optimal sequencing, duration, and composition of multimodal conversion strategies to maximize oncologic benefit while minimizing treatment-related toxicity. A strong collaboration among pharmaceutical industry leaders, clinical practitioners, research investigators, patients, and government officials is key in advancing this treatment forward. Future randomized studies should also involve more institutions from multiple continents to overcome regional variability and non-uniform adoption of the treatment protocols. In parallel, the development of reliable radiological and biological biomarkers capable of predicting conversion success will be critical to refine optimal patient selection.

If confirmed in larger prospective studies, multidisciplinary strategies integrating systemic therapy and surgery could redefine the treatment paradigm for locally advanced BTC. Rather than representing an endpoint, the present study highlights the evolving role of systemic therapy not only as palliative treatment but also as a bridge to curative-intent surgery in carefully selected patients.

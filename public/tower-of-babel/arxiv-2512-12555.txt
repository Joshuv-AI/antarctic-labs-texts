A Benamou-Brenier formulation for the multi-marginal optimal transport problem with infimal convolution cost Thanks: The author received support from the Department of Mathematics at the University of Toronto, the Canada Research Chairs program (Grant #CRC-2020-00289) and the Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada Discovery Grant (Grant #RGPIN-2020-04162 and Grant #RGPIN-2024-06235).
Friedemann Krannich

Abstract
. We present a dynamical version for the multi-marginal optimal transport problem with infimal convolution cost, using the theory of Wasserstein barycentres. We show, how our formulation relates to the dynamical version of the multi-marginal optimal transport problem developed by Pass and Shenfeld.

1.
Introduction

The classical optimal transport problem describes the minimal cost of transporting
\mu_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}^{\text{ac}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\coloneqq\big\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\mu<<\mathcal{L}^{d},\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{p}\ \mathrm{d}\mu(x)<\infty\big\}
to
\mu_{2}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}^{\text{ac}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
through

\displaystyle W_{p}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\coloneqq\Big(\inf_{\gamma\in\Pi(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{p}\ \mathrm{d}\gamma(x_{1},x_{2})\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

where the set of transport plans
\Pi(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
are all couplings
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
that have marginals
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
. In this manuscript, we are considering
p\in(1,\infty)
fixed.
For an introduction to the theory of optimal transport and further literature we refer to the books
[
16
,
15
]
.
While
W_{p}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
is a static problem, the celebrated publications by Benamou and Brenier
[
2
, Prop. 1.1]
(for
p=2
) and Brenier
[
3
, Th. 2.2]
(for general convex costs) recast it as a dynamical problem by showing that
W_{p}^{p}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
is equal to

(1)

\displaystyle BB(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\coloneqq\inf_{(\rho,v)}\Big\{\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v_{t}|^{p}\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}\ \mathrm{d}t:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho_{t}+\nabla\cdot(v_{t}\rho_{t})=0,\ \rho_{0}=\mu_{1},\ \rho_{1}=\mu_{2}\Big\}

where
\rho_{t}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\coloneqq\big\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{p}\ \mathrm{d}\mu(x)<\infty\big\}
,
v_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
t\in[0,1]
and we require that
\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v_{t}|\ \mathrm{d}\rho_{t}\ \mathrm{d}t<\infty
. The continuity equation and initial and terminal condition are understood in the weak sense, meaning that for each test function
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}([0,1]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(\partial_{t}\varphi+\nabla_{x}\varphi\cdot v_{t}\big)\ \mathrm{d}\rho_{t}\ \mathrm{d}t=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(1,x)\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{2}(x)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(0,x)\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{1}(x).

Given
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}^{\text{ac}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the multi-marginal optimal transport problem (see for example
[
14
,
7
,
10
]
for early references)

(2)

\displaystyle C(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})\coloneqq\inf_{\gamma\in\Pi(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{dN}}c(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\ \mathrm{d}\gamma(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})

generalizes
W_{p}^{p}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
in a straightforward way, where
\Pi(\mu_{1},\dots\mu_{N})
is the set of couplings
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{dN})
with marginals
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N}
and
c:\mathbb{R}^{dN}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a cost function. Existence and uniqueness of transport maps for
C(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
with quadratic (
p=2
) infimal convolution cost (
4
) was first studied by Gangbo and Święch
[
9
]
. For an introduction to the theory of multi-marginal optimal transport, see for example the survey
[
13
]
.

Until recently, no dynamical version in the spirit of (
1
) for
C(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
was known. Pass and Shenfeld
[
12
, Th. 2.3]
found a dynamical version for the multi-marginal optimal transport problem: They fix a reference coupling
\omega\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{dN})
and then show, that if
c
is convex and
\omega
-translation-invariant, i.e. that
c(x-\xi)=c(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{dN}
and
\omega
-a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{dN}

[
12
, Def. 1.1]
, then
C(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
is equal to

(3)

\displaystyle PS(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})\coloneqq\inf_{(\gamma,V)}\Big\{\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{dN}}c(V_{t}(x))\ \mathrm{d}\gamma_{t}(x)\ \mathrm{d}t:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\gamma_{t}+\nabla\cdot(V_{t}\gamma_{t})=0,\ \gamma_{0}=\omega,\ \pi^{i}_{\#}\gamma_{1}=\mu_{i}\ i\in[N]\Big\}.

Compared to the classical Benamou-Brenier formula (
1
), this problem does not search for a flow of measures but rather for a flow of couplings, hence
\gamma_{t}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{dN})
and
V_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{dN}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{dN}
for
t\in[0,1]
with the requirement that
\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{dN}}|V_{t}|\ \mathrm{d}\gamma_{t}\ \mathrm{d}t<\infty
. Here,
[N]\coloneqq\{1,\dots,N\}
and
\pi^{i}:\mathbb{R}^{dN}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
denotes the projection on the
i
-th coordinate, given by
\pi^{i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})=x_{i}
for
i\in[N]
. For a Borel map
T:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
we define the pushforward
T_{\#}\alpha=\beta
for
\alpha\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\beta\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
through

\displaystyle\alpha(T^{-1}(A))=\beta(A)

for all
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
Borel.

If the cost function
c
in (
2
) is the infimal convolution cost

(4)

\displaystyle c(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\coloneqq\inf_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|x_{i}-z|^{p},

Chiappori, McCann and Nesheim
[
6
, Sect. 5]
and Carlier and Ekeland
[
5
, Prop. 3]
showed, that
C(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
is equal to

(5)

\displaystyle WB(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})\coloneqq\inf_{\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\sum_{i=1}^{N}W_{p}^{p}(\nu,\mu_{i}).

This problem is called the
p
-Wasserstein barycentre problem
[
1
]
and we call its solution
\bar{\nu}
the
p
-barycentre of
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N}
. Agueh and Carlier
[
1
, Prop. 3.5]
(for
p=2
) and Brizzi, Friesecke and Ried
[
4
, Theo. 1.4]
(for
p\in(1,\infty)
) showed, that the
p
-barycentre is uniquely attained and that
\bar{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}^{\text{ac}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Using the theory of
p
-Wasserstein barycentres, we propose another dynamical formulation for
C(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
, that we found simultaneously and independently from the formulation of Pass and Shenfeld: In Th.
2.1
we show equality of
C(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
with cost function (
4
) and

(6)

\displaystyle DC(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})\coloneqq\inf_{(\rho^{i},v^{i})_{i\in[N]}}\Big\{\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v^{i}_{t}|^{p}\ \mathrm{d}\rho^{i}_{t}\ \mathrm{d}t:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho^{i}_{t}+\nabla\cdot(v^{i}_{t}\rho^{i}_{t})=0,\ \rho^{1}_{0}=\dots=\rho^{N}_{0},\ \rho^{i}_{1}=\mu_{i}\Big\}.

In our dynamical formulation, we search for
N
flows of probability measures, with a coupled initial constraint and fixed endpoints, hence
\rho_{t}^{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
v_{t}^{i}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
t\in[0,1]
and we require that
\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v_{t}^{i}|\ \mathrm{d}\rho_{t}^{i}\ \mathrm{d}t<\infty
for
i\in[N]
.
One notices that, using the insight of Benamou and Brenier
[
2
]
, we can change variables
(\rho^{i},v^{i})\rightarrow(\rho^{i},m^{i})\coloneqq(\rho^{i},v^{i}\rho^{i})
to recast
DC(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
as a convex optimization problem with linear constraints.
Our dynamical problem works without a reference coupling
\omega
and has a natural physical interpretation for
p=2
(see Rmk.
2.4
). We show in Th.
3.1
, how to construct a solution
(\bar{\gamma},\bar{V})
to
PS(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
from a solution
(\bar{\rho}^{i},\bar{v}^{i})_{i\in[N]}
to
DC(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
, if the reference coupling is given through
\omega=(\text{id}\times\dots\times\text{id})_{\#}\bar{\nu}
.

1.1.
Plan of the manuscript

In section 2, we show

C(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})=DC(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
, using the theory of
p
-Wasserstein barycentres. In section 3, we construct a solution to
PS(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
from a solution to

DC(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
. We conclude the manuscript in section 4.

2.
Dynamical multi-marginal optimal transport through
p
-Wasserstein barycentres

For the rest of this manuscript, we consider the multi-marginal optimal transport problem
C(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
with cost function given through the infimal convolution cost (
4
) with
p\in(1,\infty)
fixed.
Using (
5
), we find a new dynamical formulation for
C(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
:

Theorem 2.1
.

C(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})=DC(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
holds with cost function given by (
4
).

Proof.

From
[
5
, Prop. 3]
we get
C(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})=WB(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
and hence,
using (
1
) for each term
W_{p}^{p}(\nu,\mu_{i})
, we write
WB(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
as

\displaystyle\inf_{\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\sum_{i=1}^{N}W_{p}^{p}(\nu,\mu_{i})

\displaystyle=\inf_{\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\inf_{(\rho^{i},v^{i})}\Big\{\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v^{i}_{t}|^{p}\ \mathrm{d}\rho^{i}_{t}\ \mathrm{d}t:\ \partial_{t}\rho^{i}_{t}+\nabla\cdot(v^{i}_{t}\rho^{i}_{t})=0,\rho^{i}_{0}=\nu,\rho^{i}_{1}=\mu_{i}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\inf_{(\rho^{i},v^{i})_{i\in[N]}}\Big\{\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v^{i}_{t}|^{p}\mathrm{d}\rho^{i}_{t}\mathrm{d}t:\partial_{t}\rho^{i}_{t}+\nabla\cdot(v^{i}_{t}\rho^{i}_{t})=0,\rho^{1}_{0}=\dots=\rho^{N}_{0},\rho^{i}_{1}=\mu_{i}\Big\}.

∎

The dynamical version (
1
) of
W_{p}^{p}(\bar{\nu},\mu_{i})
allows for an explicit description of the minimizers
(\bar{\rho}^{i},\bar{v}^{i})_{i\in[N]}
in
DC(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
(see
[
15
, Sect. 5]
for details):
Let
T^{i}
be the optimal transport map in
W_{p}(\bar{\nu},\mu_{i})
that transports
\bar{\nu}
to
\mu_{i}
. For
t\in[0,1]
and
y\in\text{supp}(\bar{\nu})
, we define the map

\displaystyle T^{i}_{t}(y)\coloneqq(1-t)y+tT^{i}(y),

which is invertible for all
t\in[0,1]
. Then, McCann’s displacement interpolant
[
11
, Def. 1.1]

\bar{\rho}_{t}^{i}\coloneqq(T^{i}_{t})_{\#}\bar{\nu}

together with the implicitly defined vector field

\displaystyle\bar{v}^{i}_{t}(T^{i}_{t}(y))\coloneqq T^{i}(y)-y

are the minimizers in
BB(\bar{\nu},\mu_{i})
for every
i\in[N]

[
15
, Prop. 5.30]
and hence
(\bar{\rho}^{i},\bar{v}^{i})_{i\in[N]}
are the minimizers in
DC(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
.

We define
c_{p}(x)\coloneqq|x|^{p}
and for a function
\psi
we denote

\displaystyle\psi^{c_{p}}(x)\coloneqq\inf_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x-y|^{p}-\psi(y).

The next Lemma is a straightforward extension of
[
1
, Prop. 3.8]
, but we give its proof for completeness.

Lemma 2.2
.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nabla c_{p}(T^{i}(y)-y)=0

for a.e.
y\in\text{supp}(\bar{\nu})
.

Proof.

C(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
has the dual problem
[
4
, Theo. 4.1]

\displaystyle\sup_{(\varphi_{i})_{i\in[N]}}\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{i}\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{i}:\ \varphi_{i}\in L^{1}(\mu_{i}),\ \varphi_{1}(x_{1})+\dots+\varphi_{N}(x_{N})\leq c(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\Big\}

with maximizers
(\bar{\varphi}_{i})_{i\in[N]}
given through
\bar{\varphi}_{i}=\psi^{c_{p}}_{i}
with
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\psi_{i}(y)=0
for

y\in\text{supp}(\bar{\nu})
. We have

\displaystyle W_{p}^{p}(\bar{\nu},\mu_{i})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi_{i}(y)\ \mathrm{d}\bar{\nu}(y)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi^{c_{p}}_{i}(x)\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{i}(x)

and therefore we have for a.e.
(y,x)=(y,T^{i}(y))\in\text{supp}(\bar{\gamma}_{i})
(
\bar{\gamma}_{i}
is the optimal coupling in
W_{p}(\bar{\nu},\mu_{i})
)

\displaystyle\psi^{c_{p}c_{p}}_{i}(y)\geq\psi_{i}(y)=c_{p}(x-y)-\psi^{c_{p}}_{i}(x)\geq\psi^{c_{p}c_{p}}_{i}(y).

Therefore, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\psi^{c_{p}c_{p}}_{i}(y)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\psi_{i}(y)=0

with equality a.e.
y\in\text{supp}(\bar{\nu})
. Gangbo and McCann
[
8
, Th. 3.3]
showed
\bar{\nu}
-a.e. differentiability of
\psi_{i}
, hence we get for a.e.
y\in\text{supp}(\bar{\nu})
that

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nabla\psi^{c_{p}c_{p}}_{i}(y)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}-\nabla c_{p}(T^{i}(y)-y).

∎

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
(\bar{\rho}^{i},\bar{v}^{i})_{i\in[N]}
be the optimizers for
DC(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
with
p=2
. Let
\bar{m}^{i}\coloneqq\bar{v}^{i}\bar{\rho}^{i}
be the momentum variables, then we have for a.e.
y\in\text{supp}(\bar{\nu})

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bar{v}^{i}_{0}(y)=0=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bar{m}^{i}_{0}(y).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bar{v}^{i}_{0}(y)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}T^{i}(y)-y=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nabla c_{2}(T^{i}(y)-y)=0

due to Lemma
2.2
. Noticing that
\bar{m}^{i}_{0}(y)=\bar{v}^{i}_{0}(y)\bar{\rho}^{i}_{0}(y)=\bar{v}^{i}_{0}(y)\bar{\nu}(y)
, the second equality follows.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

Our dynamical multi-marginal optimal transport problem

DC(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
is inspired by a physical interpretation for
p=2
, that is based on Corollary
2.3
: Let
N=3
and consider a table with 3 holes randomly spaced over the table. Through each hole hangs a rope, having a ball with unit weight on its lower end, each ball representing a measure
\mu_{i}
. The upper ends of the ropes are all connected to another ball with unit weight that lays on the table. The three balls that are hanging pull the ball on the table around, until equilibrium is reached. Then the ball on the table represents the
2
-barycentre
\bar{\nu}
and the velocities of the three ropes at the
2
-barycentre sum to zero, causing the ball on the table to stop moving.

3.
Relation between
PS(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
and
DC(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})

Next we show, how
PS(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
and
DC(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
are related.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\omega=(\text{id}\times\dots\times\text{id})_{\#}\bar{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{dN})
, then for the cost function (
4
) we have
PS(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})=DC(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
and we can construct minimizers
(\bar{\gamma},\bar{V})
for
PS(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
from minimizers
(\bar{\rho}^{i},\bar{v}^{i})_{i\in[N]}
for
DC(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
.

Proof.

The minimizer in
c(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})
is the unique
\bar{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
characterized through the condition

(7)

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nabla c_{p}(x_{i}-\bar{z}).

Hence, if
\bar{z}
is the minimizer for
c(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})
then
\bar{z}-\xi
is the minimizer for
c(x_{1}-\xi,\dots,x_{N}-\xi)
,
which shows that
c
is
\omega
-translation-invariant, illustrating
[
12
, Ex. 1.4]
. Hence,
[
12
, Th. 2.3]
gives
PS(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})=C(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
and Th.
2.1
gives
C(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})=DC(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
.

We use
(\bar{\rho}^{i},\bar{v}^{i})
and the interpolations
T^{i}_{t}
defined in section 2, recalling that

(\bar{\rho}^{i},\bar{v}^{i})_{i\in[N]}
constructed in this way minimize
DC(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
. We define the interpolant

\displaystyle S_{t}(x)\coloneqq(T^{1}_{t}(x_{1}),\dots,T^{N}_{t}(x_{N}))

where
(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})=x\in\text{supp}(\omega)
. We consider the time-dependent coupling
\gamma_{t}\coloneqq(S_{t})_{\#}\omega

and we define implicitly for
x\in\text{supp}(\omega)

\displaystyle V_{t}(S_{t}(x))\coloneqq(\bar{v}^{1}_{t}(T^{1}_{t}(x_{1})),\dots,\bar{v}^{N}_{t}(T^{N}_{t}(x_{N})))

so that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}S_{t}(x)=V_{t}(S_{t}(x))\\
S_{0}(x)=x\end{cases}

hold.
Then by construction
(\gamma,V)
fulfill the continuity equation and

\pi^{i}_{\#}\gamma_{1}=(T^{i}_{1})_{\#}\bar{\nu}=\mu_{i}
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{dN}}c(V_{t}(x))\ \mathrm{d}\gamma_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\inf_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\bar{v}^{i}_{t}(T^{i}_{t}(y))-z|^{p}\ \mathrm{d}\bar{\nu}(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\inf_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|T^{i}(y)-y-z|^{p}\ \mathrm{d}\bar{\nu}(y)

and by Lemma
2.2
and (
7
) we get that for a.e.
y\in\text{supp}(\bar{\nu})

\displaystyle\inf_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|T^{i}(y)-y-z|^{p}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}|T^{i}(y)-y|^{p}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle PS(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{dN}}c(V_{t}(x))\ \mathrm{d}\gamma_{t}(x)\ \mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\inf_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|T^{i}(y)-y-z|^{p}\ \mathrm{d}\bar{\nu}(y)\ \mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|T^{i}(y)-y|^{p}\ \mathrm{d}\bar{\nu}(y)\ \mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\bar{v}^{i}_{t}|^{p}\ \mathrm{d}\bar{\rho}^{i}_{t}\ \mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=DC(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})

and hence
(\gamma,V)
are minimizers for
PS(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
.
∎

4.
Conclusion

In this manuscript, we present a new dynamical formulation
DC(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
for the multi-marginal optimal transport problem.
Compared to the dynamical formulation
PS(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
by Pass and Shenfeld
[
12
]
, our formulation does not need a reference coupling
\omega
. However,
PS(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
allows for a more general class of cost functions. Especially Th.
3.1
only works for the infimal convolution cost (
4
), as it needs the specific characterization (
7
) of the minimizer
\bar{z}
in the cost function.

One of the applications of the Benamou-Brenier formula (
1
) was, that it allowed for new algorithms for the computation of
W_{p}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
. Pass and Shenfeld
[
12
, Sect. 4]
also use their dynamical formulation for numerical computations. We conjecture, that
DC(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
gives rise to more efficient algorithms, as it allows to solve a de-coupled system of
N
continuity equations on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with coupled initial constraints, while a numerical version of
PS(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})
solves for one continuity equation on
\mathbb{R}^{dN}
.

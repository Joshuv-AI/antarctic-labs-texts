On Generators of Bloch groups of CM fields
Wenhuan Huang

Abstract




Introduction

Let
F
be a number field.
Let
K_{3}(F)
(resp.
K_{3}^{M}(F)
) be Quillen’s (reap. Milnor’s)
K_{3}
group of
F
. Then

K_{3}^{M}(F)\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{r_{1}}
,
and
K_{3}(F)
is a direct sum of a cyclic group and
\mathbb{Z}^{r_{2}}
. (See page 146 of
[
1
]
.)

As for Bloch groups, there are several versions. Bloch groups of infinite fields (resp. finite fields having at least 4 elements)
are firstly investigated in
[
2
]
(resp.
[
3
]
). Later

[
4
]
defines ”extended Bloch group” of a number field, proves it isomorphic to

K_{3}^{ind}(F)
, the cokernel of the map from
K_{3}^{M}(F)
to
K_{3}(F)
, and finds a way to calculate the explicit generator of its
torsion part.
[
5
]
defines ”modified Bloch group” of number fields. The relations of the three versions of
Bloch groups are clear: Their torsion-free parts are the same, and there are at most some slight differences of their torsion parts.
(See Remark 1.)

The generators of the torsion-free parts of Bloch groups, however, is somewhat hard to calculate.

[
5
]
provides with an algorithm to find them on imaginary quadratic fields.
It applies geometry about perfect binary Hermitian forms and hyperbolic geometry to produce an element with index

K_{2}(O_{F})
, and then divide it with its index mainly algebraically.

This article aims to generalize the algorithm in
[
5
]
, by
finding an algorithm to calculate generators of
\bar{B}(F)_{tf}
, where

F
is a CM field.
Compared to imaginary quadratic cases,
some adjustments need to be made when it comes to CM fields whose degrees are greater than 1.
One reason is that
F
has ”fundamental units”, which makes it need to be more careful when applying the
commutative diagram (3.16) of
[
5
]
. The other is that after finding a basis of a full-rank subgroup
of
\bar{B}(F)_{tf}
, we do not exactly know the accurate status of this basis.

In Chapter 1, we first investigate some groups related to CM fields,
including
\tilde{\wedge}^{2}
group,
\bar{\mathfrak{p}}
group (which is called pre-Bloch group in
[
4
]
),
and modified Bloch group
\bar{B}
, and relations among them. In the second chapter, starting from the theory of
Hermitian forms and Voronoï geometry, we find
PSL_{2}(O)
-tessellation of the product of

s
hyperbolic spaces,
based on which we describe an element of
\bar{B}(F)
, which actually is the product of

\bar{B}(F)
and an abelian group with rank 1. We find a group of elements, generating
a full-rank subgroup of
\bar{B}(F)_{tf}
, and then compute the index of them in Chapter 3.

In this article, for any abelian group
A
, we always denote
A_{tor}
its torsion part,

A_{tf}
is torsion-free part, and for every prime number
p
,
A_{p}
its
p
-torsion part,
i.e,
A\otimes\displaystyle\lim_{\longleftarrow}\mathbb{Z}/(p^{n})
. For any commutative ring

R
, denote
R^{*}
its multiplicative group consisting of units in
R
.
For integer
l>1
, denote
\mu_{l}=\exp(2\pi i/l)
.
And denote
\mu_{P}=\exp(2\pi i/|O_{P}^{*}|)
when

is a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}
.

1
Modified Bloch groups of fields

Note that
0=ab\tilde{\wedge}(-ab)=a\tilde{\wedge}(-a)+(b)\tilde{\wedge}(-b)+a\tilde{\wedge}b+b\tilde{\wedge}a=a\tilde{\wedge}b+b\tilde{\wedge}a

for every
a,b\in F^{*}
.
The
\tilde{\wedge}^{2}(F^{*})
is a quotient of the usual exterior power
\wedge^{2}(F^{*})=\frac{F^{*}\otimes F^{*}}{<x\otimes y+y\otimes x\ \mathrm{with}\ x,y\in F^{*}>}
.
By Matsumoto’s Theorem,
K_{2}(F)=\frac{F^{*}\otimes F^{*}}{<x\otimes(1-x)\ \mathrm{with}\ x\in F^{*}\backslash\{1\}>}
, is a quotient of
\tilde{\wedge}^{2}(F^{*})
.
We will use these relations for calculation below.
First we recall two conclusions about the structure of
\tilde{\wedge}^{2}(F^{*})
from
[
5
]
.

Proposition 1
.

For a field
F
we have a filtration

\{0\}=Fil_{0}\subseteq Fil_{1}\subseteq Fil_{2}\subseteq Fil_{3}=\tilde{\wedge}^{2}(F^{*})

where
Fil_{1}
the image of
F_{tor}^{*}\otimes F_{tor}^{*}
, and
Fil_{2}
the image of
F_{tor}^{*}\otimes F^{*}
. Then

Fil_{2}=\frac{F_{tor}^{*}\otimes F^{*}}{<(-x)\otimes x\ \mathrm{with}\ x\in F_{tor}^{*}>}

and there are natural isomorphisms

Fil_{1}=\tilde{\wedge}^{2}(F_{tor}^{*})=\frac{F_{tor}^{*}\otimes F_{tor}^{*}}{<(-x)\otimes x\ \mathrm{with}\ x\in F_{tor}^{*}>},

Fil_{2}/Fil_{1}\simeq F_{tor}^{*}\otimes F_{tf}^{*},

Fil_{3}/Fil_{2}\simeq\frac{F_{tf}^{*}\otimes F_{tf}^{*}}{<x\otimes x\ \mathrm{with}\ x\in F_{tf}^{*}>}

with the last two induced by quotient maps
F_{tor}^{*}\otimes F^{*}\rightarrow F_{tor}^{*}\otimes F_{tf}^{*}
and

F^{*}\otimes F^{*}\rightarrow F_{tf}^{*}\otimes F_{tf}^{*}
.

Corollary 2
.

Let
F
be a number field,
n=|F_{tor}^{*}|
and
v_{1},v_{2},\dots\in F^{*}
a basis of
F_{tf}^{*}
. Let
(m,u)=(1,1)
if
4|n
and

(m,u)=(2,-1)
otherwise, then there is an isomorphism

\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}\times\oplus_{i}\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}\times\oplus_{i<j}\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\tilde{\wedge}^{2}(F^{*}),

(a,(b_{i})_{i},(c_{i,j})_{i<j})\mapsto u^{a}\tilde{\wedge}u+\sum_{i}u^{b_{i}}\tilde{\wedge}v_{i}+\sum_{i<j}v_{i}^{i,j}\tilde{\wedge}v_{j}.

Lemma 3
.

Let
k=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
,
d>1
a square-free integer. Then the kernel of the natural map
\tilde{\wedge}^{2}(\mathbb{Q}^{*})\rightarrow\tilde{\wedge}^{2}(k^{*})

has order 2, generated by
(-1)\tilde{\wedge}d
.

Proof.

If
x\otimes y
is non-trivial in
Fil_{3}/Fil_{2}
of
\mathbb{Q}^{*}
then so is it in
k^{*}
.
Therefore, the kernel is contained in
Fil_{2}
of
\mathbb{Q}
.
By Remark 3.7 of
[
5
]
,
Fil_{1}
of
\mathbb{Q}
and
k
are both of order 2 generated by
(-1)\tilde{\wedge}(-1)
.
So we only need to consider
Fil_{2}/Fil_{1}
pieces.
Let
(-1)\tilde{\wedge}x\in\tilde{\wedge}^{2}(\mathbb{Q}^{*})
be in the kernel,
then
x=t^{2}
,

. After some simple calculation we get

,

i.e,

generates the kernel.
∎

Proposition 6

Let

be a real quadratic field, then the torsion subgroup of

[2]

Let

denote the set of

viewed as

-dimensional real vector space.
Let

be a CM field with dimension

Then by taking non-conjugate emneddings of

as a subset of

which allows us to view

-vector space with

Define a map

by the outer product

q(x)=xx^{*}
(where
x^{*}:=\bar{x}^{T}
). Every

defines an Hermitian form by

Define the bilinear map

(identified with its image in

Let

denote the cone of positive definite Hermitian matrices,

(1)For

(2)An Hermitian form

\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{q(v)|v\in\mathrm{Min}(A)\}=(Her_{N}(\mathbb{C}))^{s}.

(3)Two Hermitian forms

are equivalent if

The set

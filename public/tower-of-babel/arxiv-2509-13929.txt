Groupoids of finitely aligned higher-rank graphs via filters and graph morphisms
Lisa Orloff Clark, Malcolm Jones

Abstract
. Path and boundary-path groupoids of finitely aligned higher-rank graphs are often constructed using either filters or graph morphisms. We generalise the graph morphism approach to finitely aligned P P -graphs where ( Q , P ) (Q,P) is a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and we show the filter approach and the graph morphism approach yield isomorphic path and boundary-path groupoids. To do this, we define conjugacy of partial semigroup actions such that conjugate actions have isomorphic semidirect product groupoids. Combining our results with others in the literature, we survey many isomorphic presentations of path and boundary-path groupoids at different levels of generality.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Semigroup actions

The following definitions come from
[
RW17
, §5]
.
Let
X
be a set, and let
P
be a semigroup with identity
e
.
Suppose
X*P
is a subset of
X\times P
and
T_{X}\colon X*P\to X
is a
function sending each
(x,m)\in X*P
to
T_{X}((x,m))\eqqcolon x\cdot m\in X
and satisfying:

(S1)

for all
x\in X
,
(x,e)\in X*P
and

x\cdot e=x
; and

(S2)

for all
(x,m,n)\in X\times P\times P
,

(x,mn)\in X*P
if and only if
(x,m)\in X*P
and
(x\cdot m,n)\in X*P
, in which case
(x\cdot m)\cdot n=x\cdot(mn)
.

We call the triple
(X,P,T_{X})
a
semigroup action (of
P
on
X
)
.

For each
m\in P
, we write

\textrm{dom}_{X}(m)\coloneqq\{x\in X\mid(x,m)\in X*P\}\text{ and }\textrm{im}_{X}(m)\coloneqq\{x\cdot m\mid x\in\textrm{dom}_{X}(m)\},

and we define
{T_{X}^{m}}\colon\textrm{dom}_{X}(m)\to\textrm{im}_{X}(m)
by

{T_{X}^{m}}(x)=x\cdot m
, for each
x\in\textrm{dom}_{X}(m)
.
Whenever we write
x\cdot m
, it is to be understood that
x\in\textrm{dom}_{X}(m)
.
The semigroups
P
that we consider come with a partial order
\leq
(see

Section

2.4
).
In this case, we say
(X,P,T_{X})
is
directed
if, for all
m,n\in P
,
\textrm{dom}_{X}(m)\cap\textrm{dom}_{X}(n)\neq\emptyset
implies there is
l\in P
such that
m,n\leq l
and
\textrm{dom}_{X}(m)\cap\textrm{dom}_{X}(n)\subseteq\textrm{dom}_{X}(l)
, in which case
\textrm{dom}_{X}(m)\cap\textrm{dom}_{X}(n)=\textrm{dom}_{X}(l)
as per the note following
[
RW17
, Definition 5.2]
.
Recall that a
local homeomorphism
from a space
X
to a space
Y
is
a continuous map
f\colon X\to Y
such that every
x\in X
has an open
neighbourhood
U
such that
f(U)
is open in
Y
and the restriction
f|_{U}\colon U\to f(U)
of
f
is a homeomorphism
[
Wil70
, Page 68]
.
A semigroup action
(X,P,T)
, where
P
is a subsemigroup of a discrete
group
Q
, is
locally compact
if
X
is a locally compact Hausdorff
space and, for all
m\in P
,
\textrm{dom}_{X}(m)
and
\textrm{im}_{X}(m)
are open in

X
and
{T_{X}^{m}}\colon\textrm{dom}_{X}(m)\to\textrm{im}_{X}(m)
is a local
homeomorphism.
In order to characterise invariant subsets of the unit space of the
semidirect product groupoids associated to semigroup actions, we make the
following definition.

Definition 2.1
.

Given a semigroup action
(X,P,T_{X})
, we say a subset
U\subseteq X

is
invariant (with respect to
T_{X}
)
if, for all
x\in X
,
y\in U
and
m,n\in P
,
x\cdot m=y\cdot n
implies
x\in U
.

2.2.
Groupoids

We refer the reader to
[
Pat99
,
Wil19
,
SSW20
]
for the following
preliminaries on groupoids.
A
groupoid
consists of a set
G
and a subset
G^{(2)}\subseteq G\times G
together with a map
(g,h)\mapsto gh
from

G^{(2)}
to
G
(called
composition
) and a map
g\mapsto g^{-1}
from
G
to
G
(called
inversion
) such that:

(1)

if
(f,g),(g,h)\in G^{(2)}
, then
(f,gh),(fg,h)\in G^{(2)}
and
f(gh)=(fg)h\eqqcolon fgh
;

(2)

for all
g\in G
,
(g^{-1})^{-1}=g
; and

(3)

for all
g\in G
, we have
(g^{-1},g)\in G^{(2)}
,
and if
(g,h)\in G^{(2)}
, then
g^{-1}(gh)=h
and

(gh)h^{-1}=g
.

The set
G^{(0)}\coloneqq\{x\in G\mid x=xx=x^{-1}\}
is called
the
unit space
of
G
.
There are
range
and
source
maps from
G
to
G^{(0)}

given by
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(g)\coloneqq gg^{-1}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(g)\coloneqq g^{-1}g\in G^{(0)}
, for each
g\in G
, respectively.
We have
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(G)=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(G)=G^{(0)}
, and
(g,h)\in G^{(2)}

if and only if
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(g)=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(h)
.

Let
G
be a groupoid endowed with a (
not necessarily Hausdorff
)
topology.
The set
G^{(2)}
is endowed with the subspace topology from the
product topology on
G\times G
.
We say
G
is
topological
if composition and inversion are
continuous.
A groupoid
G
is
étale
if
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}\colon G\to G^{(0)}
is a
local homeomorphism.
By
[
Res07
, Theorem 5.18]
, a topological groupoid
G
is étale if and
only if
G^{(0)}
is open in
G
and
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}
is an open map.
Given an étale locally compact (not necessarily Hausdorff) groupoid
G

such that
G^{(0)}
is Hausdorff, we say
G
is
ample
if

G^{(0)}
has a basis of compact open sets (which is equivalent to the
definition via bisections by
[
Ste10
, Proposition 3.6]
or

[
Exe10
, Proposition 4.1]
).

If
G
is a groupoid, we call
H\subseteq G
a
subgroupoid
if
H

is a groupoid under the inherited operations.
Given
U\subseteq G^{(0)}
, the
reduction
of
G
to
U
is

G|_{U}\coloneqq\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}^{-1}(U)\cap\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}^{-1}(U)
, which is a
subgroupoid of
G
.
We say
U\subseteq G^{(0)}
is
invariant
if
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}^{-1}(U))\subseteq U
, in which case
G|_{U}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}^{-1}(U)
.
Given an étale locally compact groupoid
G
with Hausdorff unit space

G^{(0)}
, if
U\subseteq G^{(0)}
is closed and invariant, then

G|_{U}
is an étale locally compact closed subgroupoid of
G

(see
[
SSW20
, Page 96]
).
Given topological groupoids
G
and
H
, a
(topological) isomorphism

from
G
to
H
is a homeomorphism
\psi\colon G\to H
such that

(g_{1},g_{2})\in G^{(2)}
if and only if
(\psi(g_{1}),\psi(g_{2}))\in H^{(2)}
, in which case
\psi(g_{1}g_{2})=\psi(g_{1})\psi(g_{2})
,
and
\psi(g^{-1})=\psi(g)^{-1}
for all
g\in G
.
We say
G
and
H
are
(topologically) isomorphic
if there is a
(topological) isomorphism from
G
to
H
.

2.3.
Semidirect product groupoids

Semidirect product groupoids are a generalisation of Deaconu–Renault
groupoids
[
Dea95
,
Ren00
]
.
Deaconu–Renault groupoids are used in
[
ABCE23
, §2.2]
.
Let
(X,P,T_{X})
be a directed and locally compact semigroup action, where

P
is a subsemigroup of a discrete group
Q
.
In
[
RW17
]
, Renault and Williams associate a semidirect product groupoid

G_{X}
to
(X,P,T_{X})
as follows.
Let
G_{X}
be the set of
(x,q,y)\in X\times Q\times X
for which
there are
m,n\in P
satisfying
q=mn^{-1}
,
x\in\textrm{dom}_{X}(m)
,
y\in\textrm{dom}_{X}(n)
and
x\cdot m=y\cdot n
.
Let
G_{X}^{(2)}
be the set of pairs
((x,q,y),(w,r,z))\in G_{X}\times G_{X}
with
y=w
.
Then,
G_{X}
is a groupoid with composition

(x,q,y)(y,r,z)\coloneqq(x,qr,z)

and inversion

(x,q,y)^{-1}\coloneqq(y,q^{-1},x),

called the
semidirect product groupoid
of
(X,P,T_{X})
.
The collection of

Z_{X}(U,m,n,V)\coloneqq\{(x,mn^{-1},y)\in G_{X}\mid x\in U,y\in V\text{ and }x\cdot m=y\cdot n\},

where
m,n\in P
and
U,V\subseteq X
are open, is a basis for a topology
on
G_{X}
such that
G_{X}
is an étale locally compact Hausdorff
groupoid, and
x\mapsto(x,e,x)
is a homeomorphism from
X
to

G_{X}^{(0)}
.
Thus, if
X
has a basis of compact open sets, then
G_{X}
is ample.
Also, given any basis for the topology on
X
, the collection of

Z_{X}(U,m,n,V)
, where
m,n\in P
and
U,V\subseteq
are
basic open sets, is a basis for the topology on
G_{X}
.
The following lemma is straight-forward so we omit the proof.
It allows us to identify invariant subsets of
X
in the sense of

Definition

2.1
with invariant subsets of
G_{X}^{(0)}
via the
homeomorphism
x\mapsto(x,e,x)
from
X
to
G_{X}^{(0)}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(X,P,T_{X})
be a directed and locally compact semigroup action,
where
P
is a subsemigroup of a discrete group
Q
.

(1)

For any
U\subseteq X
,
U
is invariant
with respect to
T_{X}
if and only if
U
identifies with an invariant
subset of
G_{X}^{(0)}
via
x\mapsto(x,e,x)
.

(2)

For any closed invariant
U\subseteq X
, we
have

G_{X}|_{U}=\{(x,q,y)\in G_{X}\mid x,y\in U\},

which is an étale locally compact Hausdorff closed subgroupoid of

G_{X}
.

2.4.
Weakly quasi-lattice ordered groups

We follow
[
HNSY21
]
for preliminaries on weakly quasi-lattice ordered
groups.
Let
Q
be a discrete group, and let
P
be a semigroup with identity
e

such that
P\cap P^{-1}=\{e\}
.
For each
p,r\in P
, define
p\leq r
if
pq=r
for some
q\in P
,
which is left invariant.
We say
(Q,P)
is a
weakly quasi-lattice ordered group
if, whenever
two elements of
P
have a common upper bound with respect to
\leq
, there
is a least common upper bound.
Roughly speaking, the
P
-graphs introduced in
[
BSV13
]
and considered in
this paper are defined by replacing the role of
(\mathbb{Z}^{k},\mathbb{N}^{k})
in the

k
-graphs of
[
KP00
]
with a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group
(Q,P)
.
A different generalisation of
k
-graphs is considered in
[
CKSS14
]
,
where
\mathbb{N}^{k}
is replaced with a finitely-generated cancellative abelian
monoid.
We give examples of a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group that is not
finitely-generated and a finitely-generated cancellative abelian monoid that
is not weakly quasi-lattice ordered to emphasise that neither collection of
generalised
k
-graphs is contained in the other.
In this paper, we consider the
P
-graphs of
[
BSV13
]
.

Example 2.3

(Example 3.2 of
[
HNSY21
]
and Section 2.3 of

[
Nic92
]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be the free group on a countably infinite set of
generators, and let
\mathbb{F}^{+}
be the smallest subsemigroup
containing the identity and the generators.
Then,
(\mathbb{F},\mathbb{F}^{+})
is weakly quasi-lattice ordered (in
fact, it is a quasi-lattice in the sense of
[
HNSY21
]
), but

\mathbb{F}^{+}
is not finitely-generated.

Example 2.4
.

Let
P
be the smallest submonoid of the additive monoid
\mathbb{N}^{2}

containing
(1,0)
,
(1,1)
and
(1,2)
, and suppose
P
embeds in a
discrete group
Q
.
Observe
(1,0)+(1,1)=(2,1)
and
(1,0)+(1,2)=(1,1)+(1,1)=(2,2)
, so
(2,1)
and
(2,2)
are distinct minimal common upper
bounds of
(1,0)
and
(1,1)
.
Thus,
(1,0)
and
(1,1)
do not have a least common upper bound, and
so
(Q,P)
is not weakly quasi-lattice ordered.

2.5.
P
-graphs

Following
[
BSV13
]
, given a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group
(Q,P)
,
a (discrete)
P
-graph
consists of a countable small category

(\Lambda,\Lambda^{(2)},\Lambda^{(0)},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}})
together
with a functor
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\colon\Lambda\to P
satisfying the
unique
factorisation property
: for every
\lambda\in\Lambda
and
p,q\in P

with
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\lambda)=pq
, there are unique elements
\mu,\nu\in\Lambda

such that
\lambda=\mu\nu
,
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu)=p
and
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\nu)=q
.
For each
p\in P
, we write
\Lambda^{p}\coloneqq\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}^{-1}(p)
, and we have

\Lambda^{(0)}=\Lambda^{e}
.
For each
\mu\in\Lambda
and
E\subseteq\Lambda
, we write

\lambda E\coloneqq\{\lambda\mu\mid\mu\in E\text{ and }\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(\lambda)=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(\mu)\}.

We say
\Lambda
is
finitely aligned
if, for all
\mu,\nu\in\Lambda
, there is a finite
J\subseteq\Lambda
such that
\mu\Lambda\cap\nu\Lambda=\bigcup_{\lambda\in J}\lambda\Lambda
as in

[
Spi12
, Page 729]
.
For each
\mu,\lambda\in\Lambda
, define
\mu\preceq\lambda\iff\mu\nu=\lambda\text{ for some }\nu\in\Lambda\iff\lambda\in\mu\Lambda
.

We state some properties and notations for
P
-graphs.
It follows from the unique factorisation property that
\Lambda
is

left cancellative
(i.e.
\mu\nu=\mu\kappa
implies
\nu=\kappa
),
right-cancellative
(i.e.
\mu\nu=\kappa\nu
implies
\mu=\kappa
), and has
no inverses
(i.e.
\mu\nu=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(\nu)
implies

\mu=\nu=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(\nu)
).
Hence, any
P
-graph is a
category of paths
(i.e. a left and right
cancellative small category having no inverses) as in

[
Spi14
, Example 2.2(6)]
.
The relation
\preceq
is the same as that of
[
Spi14
, Definition 2.5]

for categories of paths, so
\preceq
is a partial order.
Moreover,
\preceq
is
left invariant
(i.e.
\mu\nu\preceq\mu\kappa
implies
\nu\preceq\kappa
) because of left cancellation.
The
k
-graphs of
[
KP00
]
are the
P
-graphs where
(Q,P)=(\mathbb{Z}^{k},\mathbb{N}^{k})
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.
A
P
-graph morphism
is a functor
x\colon\Lambda_{1}\to\Lambda_{2}

from a
P
-graph
(\Lambda_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}_{1})
to a
P
-graph
(\Lambda_{2},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}_{2})

\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}_{1}(\lambda)=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}_{2}(x(\lambda))
, for all

.
For discrete
\Lambda
, the notion of “rank-
k
(finite) path prototypes”
of

]
generalises to weakly quasi-lattice ordered groups

without modification as follows.

Example 2.5
.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group.
For each

is a

\displaystyle\Omega_{P,m}^{(2)}\coloneqq\{((p,q),(q^{\prime},r))\in\Omega_{P,m}\times\Omega_{P,m}\mid q=q^{\prime}\}\to\Omega_{P,m},

\displaystyle((p,q),(q,r))\mapsto(p,r)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\colon\Omega_{P,m}\to P,\quad(p,q)\mapsto p^{-1}q.

In this paper, we reconcile constructions of the path space, boundary-path

3.
Conjugacy of semigroup actions

We generalise the definition of a conjugacy of Deaconu–Renault systems
as in
[
ABCE23
]
via a characterisation in
[
ABCE23
, Lemma 2.6(2)]
.
A number of questions can be asked about conjugacy of semigroup actions in
analogy with
[
ABCE23
, Theorem 3.1]
.
In this paper, we only check that conjugate semigroup actions have isomorphic
semidirect product groupoids
(
Theorem

3.3
) to provide ourselves
with a framework to show that different constructions of groupoids from

P
-graphs are isomorphic (
Corollary

5.4
).
Various notions of conjugacy of finitely aligned
k
-graphs are studied in

[
CR21
]
.
In particular, two finitely aligned
k
-graphs are shown to be eventually
one-sided conjugate (see
[
CR21
, Definition 3.1]
) if and only if there
is a cocycle-preserving isomorphism between their associated boundary-path
groupoids
[
CR21
, Theorem 3.2]
.
It would be interesting to investigate an analogue of eventual one-sided
conjugacy for semigroup actions to characterise when two semidirect product
groupoids admit a cocycle-preserving isomorphism.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
P
be a semigroup with identity
e
, and let
(X,P,T_{X})
and

(Y,P,T_{Y})
be semigroup actions on topological spaces
X
and
Y
.
A
conjugacy
is a map
h\colon X\to Y
such that:

(C1)

h
is a homeomorphism;

(C2)

h(\textrm{dom}_{X}(m))=\textrm{dom}_{Y}(m)
, for
all
m\in P
; and

(C3)

h(x\cdot m)=h(x)\cdot m
, for all

m\in P
and
x\in\textrm{dom}_{X}(m)
.

We say
(X,P,T_{X})
and
(Y,P,T_{Y})
are
conjugate
if there is
a conjugacy
h\colon X\to Y
.

It follows from the definition of a conjugacy that
h(\textrm{im}_{X}(m))=\textrm{im}_{Y}(m)
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
P
be a semigroup with identity
e
, and let
(X,P,T_{X})
and

(Y,P,T_{Y})
be conjugate semigroup actions.

(1)

If
(X,P,T_{X})
is locally compact, then
(Y,P,T_{Y})
is
locally compact.

(2)

If
(X,P,T_{X})
is directed, then
(Y,P,T_{Y})
is directed.

Proof.

Say
h\colon X\to Y
is a conjugacy, and suppose
(X,P,T_{X})
is
locally compact.
Since
X
is locally compact Hausdorff,
(C1)
implies
Y
is locally
compact Hausdorff.
Fix
m\in P
.
Since
\textrm{dom}_{X}(m)
is open,
(C1)
and
(C2)
together imply

\textrm{dom}_{Y}(m)
is open.
Also,
\textrm{im}_{X}(m)
is open, and
h(\textrm{im}_{X}(m))=\textrm{im}_{Y}(m)
, so

\textrm{im}_{Y}(m)
is open too.
Since
h
and
h^{-1}
are homeomorphisms,
T^{m}_{X}
is a local
homeomorphism and
(C3)
says
T_{Y}^{m}=h\circ T_{X}^{m}\circ h^{-1}
, so we
get that
{T_{Y}^{m}}\colon\textrm{dom}_{Y}(m)\to\textrm{im}_{Y}(m)
is also
local homeomorphism.

Now suppose
(X,P,T_{X})
is directed.
Let
m,n\in P
be such that
\textrm{dom}_{Y}(m)\cap\textrm{dom}_{Y}(n)\neq\emptyset
.
Because
h
is a injective and because of
(C2)
, we have

h(\textrm{dom}_{X}(m)\cap\textrm{dom}_{X}(n))=h(\textrm{dom}_{X}(m))\cap h(\textrm{dom}_{X}(n))=\textrm{dom}_{Y}(m)\cap\textrm{dom}_{Y}(n),

so
\textrm{dom}_{X}(m)\cap\textrm{dom}_{X}(n)\neq\emptyset
.
Since
(X,P,T_{X})
is directed, there is
l\in P
such that
m,n\leq l
and
\textrm{dom}_{X}(m)\cap\textrm{dom}_{X}(n)=\textrm{dom}_{X}(l)
.
Again, because
h
is a injective and because of
(C2)
, we have

\textrm{dom}_{Y}(m)\cap\textrm{dom}_{Y}(n)=h(\textrm{dom}_{X}(m))\cap h(\textrm{dom}_{X}(n))=h(\textrm{dom}_{X}(m)\cap\textrm{dom}_{X}(n))=h(\textrm{dom}_{X}(l))=\textrm{dom}_{Y}(l).

∎

In
[
ABCE23
, Theorem 3.1]
, conjugacy of Deaconu–Renault systems is
characterised in terms of groupoid isomorphisms and C*-algebra
*-isomorphisms.
We generalise a small part of this theorem via routine arguments alluded to
in the proof of
[
ABCE23
, Proposition 3.8]
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
Q
be a group, let
P
be a subsemigroup with identity
e
, and let

(X,P,T_{X})
and
(Y,P,T_{Y})
be locally compact and directed semigroup
actions.
If
h\colon X\to Y
is a conjugacy, then

\psi_{h}\colon G_{X}\to G_{Y},\quad\psi_{h}((x,q,y))\coloneqq(h(x),q,h(y)),

is an isomorphism such that, for each open
U,V\subseteq X
and
m,n\in P
,
\psi_{h}(Z_{X}(U,m,n,V))=Z_{Y}(h(U),m,n,h(V))
.
Moreover, for each closed invariant
U\subseteq X
,
h(U)\subseteq Y

is closed and invariant, and
\psi_{h}
restricts to an isomorphism from

G_{X}|_{U}
to
G_{Y}|_{h(U)}
.

Proof.

Let
(x,mn^{-1},y)\in G_{X}
, where
m,n\in P
are such that

x\cdot m=y\cdot n
.
By definition of a conjugacy,

h(x)\cdot m=h(x\cdot m)=h(y\cdot n)=h(y)\cdot n,

and
h(x)\in\textrm{dom}_{Y}(m)
and
h(y)\in\textrm{dom}_{Y}(n)
, so

\psi_{h}((x,q,y))=(h(x),mn^{-1},h(y))\in G_{Y}
.
That is,
\psi_{h}
is well-defined.
By
(C1)
,
h
is a bijection.
If
(h(x),q,h(y))=(h(x^{\prime}),q^{\prime},h(y^{\prime}))
, then
q=q^{\prime}
and
(x,q,y)=(x^{\prime},q^{\prime},y^{\prime})
because
h
is injective.
Thus
\psi_{h}
is injective.
Fix
(y_{1},q,y_{2})\in G_{Y}
.
Then, there are
m,n\in P
such that
y_{1}\cdot m=y_{2}\cdot n

and
mn^{-1}=q
.
Since
h
is surjective, there are
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
such that
h(x_{1})=y_{1}
and
h(x_{2})=y_{2}
, so

h(x_{1}\cdot m)=h(x_{1})\cdot m=h(x_{2})\cdot n=h(x_{2}\cdot n).

Since
h
is injective,
x_{1}\cdot m=x_{2}\cdot n
, and so

(x_{1},q,x_{2})\in G_{X}
.
Moreover,
(y_{1},q,y_{2})=\psi_{h}(x_{1},q,x_{2})
.
Thus,
\psi_{h}
is surjective.
By similar arguments, we can show
\psi_{h}(Z_{X}(U,m,n,V))=Z_{Y}(h(U),m,n,h(V))
, for any open
U,V\subseteq X
and

m,n\in P
.
The collection of such
Z_{X}(U,m,n,V)
is a basis and

\psi_{h}
is a bijection, so it follows that
\psi_{h}
is a homeomorphism.
For any
(x,q,y),(w,r,z)\in G_{X}
,
y=w
if and only if
h(y)=h(w)
because
h
is injective, and so
((x,q,y),(w,r,z))\in G_{X}^{(2)}
if and only if
(\psi_{h}((x,q,y)),\psi_{h}((w,r,z)))\in G_{Y}^{(2)}
, in which case

\psi_{h}((x,q,y))\psi_{h}((w,r,z))=(h(x),qr,h(z))=\psi_{h}((x,qr,z))=\psi_{h}((x,q,y)(w,r,z)).

Moreover,

\psi_{h}((x,q,y))^{-1}=(h(x),q,h(y))^{-1}=(h(y),q^{-1},h(x))=\psi_{h}(y,q^{-1},x)=\psi_{h}((x,q,y)^{-1}).

Therefore,
\psi_{h}
is an isomorphism.

Now suppose
U\subseteq X
is closed and invariant.
Fix
h(x)\in Y
,
h(y)\in h(U)
and
m,n\in P
such that

h(x)\cdot m=h(y)\cdot n
.
By
(C3)
,
h(x\cdot m)=h(y\cdot n)
, so
x\cdot m=y\cdot n
.
Since
h(y)\in h(U)
,
y\in U
, and so
x\in U
because
U
is
invariant.
Thus,
h(x)\in h(U)
, so
h(U)
is invariant.
Also,
h(U)
is closed because
h
is a homeomorphism.
By
Lemma

2.2
(
2
),
G_{X}|_{U}
and

G_{Y}|_{h(U)}
are étale locally compact Hausdorff closed
subgroupoids of
G_{X}
and
G_{Y}
, respectively.
We have that
\psi_{h}(G_{X}|_{U})=G_{Y}|_{h(U)}
.
Since
\psi_{h}
is an isomorphism that maps the subgroupoid

G_{X}|_{U}
to the subgroupoid

G_{Y}|_{h(U)}
,
\psi_{h}
restricts to an isomorphism of
these subgroupoids.
∎

4.
Path groupoids via filters and graph morphisms

In
Section

4.1
, we recall the construction of path groupoids of
finitely aligned
P
-graphs
via filters
from
[
Jon25
]
.
In
Section

4.2
, we generalise the construction of path groupoids of
finitely aligned
k
-graphs
via graph morphisms
(e.g.
[
Yee07
]
)
to include
P
-graphs, where
(Q,P)
is a weakly quasi-lattice ordered
group.
We note filters and graph morphisms are not the only two notions of paths: In

[
BY17
]
, paths are defined as sections of functors induced by a discrete
Conduché fibration.
This approach has been further developed in
[
Wol23
]
, where only infinite

We have not examined whether filters and graph morphisms coincide with the
paths of
[

,
Wol23
]
in our setting.

4.1.
Filter approach

Filters (or similar) have been used as models of paths in
P
-graphs for some
time (e.g.
[
Nic92
]
for quasi-lattice ordered groups,
[
Exe08
]
for
semigroupoids,
[
Spi12
]
for categories of paths, and
[
BSV13
]
for

P
-graphs—though no groupoid is used).

4.1.1.
Path groupoid

We recall the path and boundary-path groupoids of finitely aligned
P
-graphs
via filters from
[
Jon25
]
.
A
filter
of a
P
-graph
\Lambda
is a nonempty subset
x
of

\Lambda
that is
hereditary
(i.e.
\lambda\preceq\mu\in x

implies
\lambda\in x
) and
directed
(i.e.
\mu,\nu\in x
implies
there is
\lambda\in x
such that
\mu,\nu\preceq\lambda
).

Definition 4.1

(Path space of filters)
.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let
\Lambda

be a finitely aligned
P
-graph.
We write
X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}
for the set of filters of
\Lambda
.

The set
X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}
is the first of many presentations of the path
space of a
P
-graph
\Lambda
considered in this paper.
The various notation can be found in
Appendix

A
.
Each
x\in X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}
is directed, and so the restriction
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}|_{x}
is
injective (see
[
RW17
, Lemma 6.6(b)]
).
For each
\lambda\in\Lambda
, we write

F(\lambda)\coloneqq\{\mu\in\Lambda\mid\mu\preceq\lambda\}\in X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}.

For any
x\in X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}
, there is a unique element of
x\cap\Lambda^{(0)}
denoted by
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(x)
.
Given finite (possibly empty)
K_{1},K_{2}\subseteq\Lambda
and
\mu\in\Lambda
, we write

\displaystyle Z_{X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}}\left(K_{1}\setminus K_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{x\in X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}\mid K_{1}\subseteq x\subseteq\Lambda\setminus K_{2}\},

\displaystyle Z_{X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}}\left(\mu\setminus K_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\coloneqq Z_{X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}}\left(\{\mu\}\setminus K_{2}\right),\text{ and }

\displaystyle Z_{X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}}\left(\mu\right)

\displaystyle\coloneqq Z_{X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}}\left(\mu\setminus\emptyset\right).

The collection of such
Z_{X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}}\left(K_{1}\setminus K_{2}\right)
is a basis for a
topology on
X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}
.
Identifying
X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}
as a subcollection of maps from
\Lambda
to
the discrete space
\{0,1\}
realises the topology on
X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}
as
the topology of pointwise convergence (also called the ‘pointwise topology’)
in the sense of
[
Wil70
, Definition 42.1]
.
In some settings, including
[
ACHJL22
]
, such topologies on subsets of the
powerset of a set are called ‘patch topologies’ (see the note about this
terminology in
[
LV21
, Page 293]
).
The motivation for the name ‘pointwise convergence’ is explained by

[
Wil19
, Theorem 42.2]
, which simplifies as follows in our setting.
A sequence
(x_{n})\subseteq X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}
converges to
x\in X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}
if and only if for each
\lambda\in x
there is an
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
N\leq n\implies\lambda\in x_{n}
and for each

\lambda\in\Lambda\setminus x
there is an
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
N\leq n\implies\lambda\in\Lambda\setminus x_{n}
.
By
[
Jon25
, Remark 4.2 and Theorem 4.9]
, if
\Lambda
is finitely
aligned, then the collection of
Z_{X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}}\left(\mu\setminus K\right)
, where
\mu\in\Lambda
and
K\subseteq\mu\Lambda
is finite, is a countable basis of
compact open sets, and
X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}
is locally compact and Hausdorff.

By
[
BSV13
, Lemma 3.4]
, for each
\lambda\in\Lambda
, the map

Z_{X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}}\left(\lambda\right)\to Z_{X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(\lambda)\right),\quad x\mapsto x\cdot\lambda\coloneqq\{\mu\in\Lambda\mid\lambda\mu\in x\},

is a bijection with inverse

Z_{X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(\lambda)\right)\to Z_{X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}}\left(\lambda\right),\quad x\mapsto\lambda x\coloneqq\{\zeta\in\Lambda\mid\zeta\preceq\lambda\mu\text{ for some }\mu\in x\}.

Even if
\Lambda
is not finitely aligned,
x\mapsto x\cdot\lambda
is continuous
[
Jon25
, Lemma 5.3]

and its inverse
x\mapsto\lambda x
has some
continuity-like behaviour
[
Jon25
, Lemma 5.13]
.
If
\Lambda
is finitely aligned, then for each
\lambda\in\Lambda
the
subspace
Z_{X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}}\left(\lambda\right)
has a basis of sets of the form

Z_{X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}}\left(\kappa\setminus K\right)
, where
\lambda\preceq\kappa
and
K\subseteq\kappa\Lambda
is finite, by arguments similar to

[
Jon25
, Lemma 6.2]
.
The image of such a
Z_{X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}}\left(\kappa\setminus K\right)
under

is open, and so
x\mapsto x\cdot\lambda
is an open map.
Therefore, if
\Lambda
is finitely aligned, then
x\mapsto x\cdot\lambda
is a homeomorphism.

Following
[
Jon25
, Lemma 5.1]
, given
(\lambda,\mu)\in\Lambda^{(2)}
and

\lambda\mu\in x
, then
(x\cdot\lambda)\cdot\mu=x\cdot(\lambda\mu)
, and if

, then

\lambda(\mu x)=(\lambda\mu)x
.
By
[

, Lemma 3.4]

\mu\in x\in X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}
and

\mu(x\cdot\mu)=x

.
As per

, Definition 5.8]

and we define

to map each
(x,m)\in X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}*P
to

x\cdot m\coloneqq x\cdot\mu
, where
\mu
is the unique
element of
x\cap\Lambda^{m}
.
We can now state the finitely aligned case of

[

, Theorem 5.14]
.

We refer the reader to

Following

Definition 4.4

\Lambda

\preceq
,
j\leq k+1
implies
\mu_{j}^{\prime}\preceq\mu_{k+1}
.
Therefore, by induction, there is a
\preceq
-increasing
(\mu_{n})\subseteq y
such that

implies
\mu_{j}^{\prime}\preceq\mu_{n}
and

(\mu_{n})
is
\preceq
-increasing.
Now fix
\mu\in y
, so
\mu=\mu_{j}^{\prime}\preceq\mu_{j}
for some
j
.
Thus,
y\subseteq\bigcup_{n}F(\mu_{n})
.
Because
y
is hereditary, we also have
\bigcup_{n}F(\mu_{n})\subseteq y
.

Because
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}
is a functor, for any
\preceq
-increasing
(\mu_{n})\subseteq\Lambda
,
(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu_{n}))
is
\leq
-increasing and hence has a
direct limit
\Omega_{P,(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu_{n}))}
.
Suppose
(\mu_{n}),(\mu_{n}^{\prime})\subseteq y
are both
\preceq
-increasing
such that
y=\bigcup_{n}F(\mu_{n})=\bigcup_{n}F(\mu_{n}^{\prime})
.
We claim
\Omega_{P,(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu_{n}))}=\Omega_{P,(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu_{n}^{\prime}))}
.
To prove the claim, recall from
Remark

4.10
that it
is equivalent to show
(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu_{n}))\prec(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu_{n}^{\prime}))\prec(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu_{n}))
.
For each

,
\mu_{j}\in F(\mu_{j})\subseteq\bigcup_{n}F(\mu_{n}^{\prime})

.
Because
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}
is a functor,
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu_{j})\leq\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu_{K}^{\prime})
.
Hence,
(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu_{n}))\prec(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu_{n}^{\prime}))
.
A symmetric argument shows

, and so

\Omega_{P,(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu_{n}))}=\Omega_{P,(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu_{n}^{\prime}))}

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let
\Lambda

be a finitely aligned
P
-graph.
The map

h\colon X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}\to X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}},\quad x\mapsto h(x)\coloneqq x(e\mathrm{dom}(x)),

is a conjugacy.

Proof.

We first show
h(x)\in X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}
.
Observe
(e,e)\in e\mathrm{dom}(x)
, so
h(x)\neq\emptyset
.
Fix
x(e,p),x(e,q)\in h(x)
, where
(e,p),(e,q)\in\mathrm{dom}(x)
.
By definition of
X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}*P
, we have
(x,p),(x,q)\in X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}*P
, and the characterisation in

Lemma

4.11
of
X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}*P
implies
p,q\leq m_{n}
eventually, where
\mathrm{dom}(x)=\Omega_{P,(m_{n})}
.
Thus, there is an
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
p,q\leq m_{n}
.
Because
x
is a functor,
x(e,p),x(e,q)\preceq x(e,m_{n})
, so
h(x)

is directed.
Fix
x(e,r)\in h(x)
, and suppose
\mu\nu=x(e,r)
.
Using that
x
is a functor and the unique factorisation property, it is
straightforward to check that
\mu=x(e,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu))\in h(x)
, so
h(x)

is hereditary.
Now we show
(C1)
holds, i.e.
h
is a homeomorphism.
Suppose
h(x)=h(y)
.
Then,
x(e\mathrm{dom}(x))=y(e\mathrm{dom}(y))
.
We show
\mathrm{dom}(x)=\mathrm{dom}(y)
.
If
(j,k)\in\mathrm{dom}(x)
,
(e,k)\in e\mathrm{dom}(x)
, so
x(e,k)\in y(e\mathrm{dom}(y))
.
There is a
k^{\prime}\in P
such that
x(e,k)=y(e,k^{\prime})
, so
k=k^{\prime}
because

x
and
y
are degree-preserving.
Hence,
(e,k)\in\mathrm{dom}(y)
, and so
(j,k)\in\mathrm{dom}(y)
.
A symmetric argument shows the other inclusion.
Because
x
is a functor,
x(e,j)x(j,k)=y(e,j)y(j,k)
, and the
unique factorisation property implies
x(j,k)=y(j,k)
because
x
is
degree-preserving.
Thus,
x=y
, and so
h
is injective.
Let
y\in X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}
.
By
Lemma

5.1
, there is a

\preceq
-increasing
(\mu_{n})\subseteq y
such that
y=\bigcup_{n}F(\mu_{n})
and
(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu_{n}))
is
\leq
-increasing so that the
direct limit
\Omega_{P,(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu_{n}))}
exists.
Fix
(j,k)\in\Omega_{P,(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu_{n}))}
, so
k\leq\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu_{n})
for
some
n
.
By the unique factorisation property, there are unique
\kappa\in\Lambda^{k}
and
\lambda\in\Lambda^{k^{-1}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu_{n})}
such that

\kappa\lambda=\mu_{n}\in y
.
Because
y
is hereditary,
\kappa\in y
.
Notice that, if
(\mu_{n}^{\prime})
is another sequence as in

Lemma

5.1
so that

\Omega_{P,(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu_{n}))}=\Omega_{P,(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu_{n}^{\prime}))}
, then the above
argument yields a
\kappa^{\prime}\in\Lambda^{k}\cap y
and so
\kappa=\kappa^{\prime}
by the injectivity of
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}|_{y}
.
That is,
\kappa
does not depend on the choice of
(\mu_{n})
.
The unique factorisation property implies there are unique
\theta\in\Lambda^{j}
and
\iota\in\Lambda^{j^{-1}k}
such that
\kappa=\theta\iota
.
In this way,
(j,k)
uniquely determines
\iota\in\Lambda
, so we can
define

x\colon\Omega_{P,(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu_{n}))}\to\Lambda,\quad(j,k)\mapsto x(j,k)\coloneqq\iota.

We show
x(e\mathrm{dom}(x))=y
.
Fix
(e,k)\in e\mathrm{dom}(x)
.
In the above setting, where
x(e,k)
is defined, we have
\theta\in\Lambda^{e}
, so
\theta=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(\iota)
.
Thus,
x(e,k)=\iota=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(\iota)\iota=\kappa\in y
.
Now fix
\kappa^{\prime}\in y
.
Then,
\kappa^{\prime}\preceq\mu_{n}
for some
n
, which implies
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\kappa^{\prime})\leq\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu_{n})
, and so
(e,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\kappa^{\prime}))\in e\Omega_{P,(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu_{n}))}=e\mathrm{dom}(x)
.
We need to show
x(e,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\kappa^{\prime}))=\kappa^{\prime}
.
By definition of
x(e,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\kappa^{\prime}))
,
\kappa\in\Lambda^{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\kappa^{\prime})}
,
so
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\kappa)=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\kappa^{\prime})
and
\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}\in y
.
By the injectivity of
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}|_{y}
, we have that
x(e,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\kappa^{\prime}))=\kappa=\kappa^{\prime}
, as required.
Therefore,
x(e\mathrm{dom}(x))=y
.
We show
x
is a graph morphism.
Fix
((i,j),(j,k))\in\Omega_{P,(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu_{n}))}^{(2)}
.
As per the definition of
x
, there are unique
\kappa_{j,k}\in y\cap\Lambda^{k}
,
\theta_{j,k}\in y\cap\Lambda^{j}
and
\iota_{j,k}\in\Lambda^{j^{-1}k}
such that
\kappa_{j,k}=\theta_{j,k}\iota_{j,k}
.
Similarly, there are unique
\kappa_{i,j}\in y\cap\Lambda^{j}
,

\theta_{i,j}\in y\cap\Lambda^{i}
and
\iota_{i,j}\in\Lambda^{i^{-1}j}
such that
\kappa_{i,j}=\theta_{i,j}\iota_{i,j}
.
By the injectivity of
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}_{y}
,
\theta_{j,k}=\kappa_{i,j}
.
Hence,
\theta_{j,k}=\theta_{i,j}\iota_{i,j}

\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(\iota_{i,j})=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(\theta_{j,k})=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(\iota_{j,k})
.
That is,
(x(i,j),x(j,k))\in\Lambda^{(2)}
.
We need to show

.
Notice
\theta_{i,k},\theta_{i,j}\in y\cap\Lambda^{i}
, so

\theta_{i,k}=\theta_{i,j}
by the injectivity of
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}|_{y}
.
Because of left cancellation, it suffices to show

,
equivalently
\kappa_{i,k}=\kappa_{i,j}\iota_{j,k}
.
Observe

\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\kappa_{i,k})=k=j(j^{-1}k)=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\kappa_{i,j})\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\iota_{j,k})=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\kappa_{i,j}\iota_{j,k}),

.
We have

by construction of
\kappa_{i,k}
.
Since

, we have

\kappa_{i,j}\iota_{j,k}=\theta_{i,j}\iota_{i,j}\iota_{j,k}=\theta_{j,k}\iota_{j,k}=\kappa_{j,k}\in y,

so both

, as required.

, and so

is a functor.
Moreover,

is degree-preserving by construction since

.
Therefore,

, and so

is surjective.

Recall the basis for the topology on

from

Section

4.2.1
.
Notice the bijection

identifies each basic open set

of
X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}
with

.
Thus,

is a homeomorphism.

Now we show
(C2)
holds, i.e. for each

,

h(\textrm{dom}_{X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}}(m))=\textrm{dom}_{X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}}(m)
.
Fix
x\in\textrm{dom}_{X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}}(m)
.
By definition of the action of
P
on
X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}
,
(e,m)\in e\mathrm{dom}(x)
, and so

.
Because
x
is a graph morphism,
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(x(e,m))=m
.
Hence,
h(x)\in\textrm{dom}_{X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}}(m)
.
For the other inclusion, if
h(x)\in\textrm{dom}_{X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}}(m)
, then
there is a
\mu\in h(x)\cap\Lambda^{m}
.
By definition of
h
,
\mu=x(e,m^{\prime})
for some
(e,m^{\prime})\in e\mathrm{dom}(x)
.
Notice

because

is
degree-preserving.
Thus,

.

Lastly, we show
(C3)
holds, i.e.

, for

and

.
By
(C2)
,
h(x)\in\textrm{dom}_{X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}}(m)

with those of

[
FMY05
]
, we make the following remark (which adds to the existing
homeomorphisms of path and boundary-path spaces from

Remark

5.5
for
P
-graphs).

Remark 6.1
.

For any finitely aligned (discrete)
k
-graph,

X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{FMY}}\cong X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{Yee}}\cong X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{Spi}}\cong X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}\cong X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{f}}\cong\hat{E}_{*}(S_{\Lambda,\mathrm{OP}}),

where
\hat{E}_{*}(S_{\Lambda,\mathrm{OP}})
is the space of characters of the
idempotent semilattice
E(S_{\Lambda,\mathrm{OP}})
that satisfy condition

(*)
as per
[
OP20
, Definition 3.15 and Corollary 3.16]
,
and

\partial{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{FMY}}\cong\partial{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{Yee}}\cong\partial{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{Spi}}\cong\partial{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{m}}\cong\partial{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{f}}\cong\hat{E}_{\mathrm{tight}}(S_{\Lambda,\mathrm{OP}}),

where
\hat{E}_{\mathrm{tight}}(S_{\Lambda,\mathrm{OP}})\cong G_{\mathrm{tight}}(S_{\Lambda,\mathrm{OP}})^{(0)}
is the space of characters of

E(S_{\Lambda,\mathrm{OP}})
that are tight as per

[
Exe08
, Definition 12.8]
.

Now, we check that our action
(X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}},P,T_{X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}})

coincides with the action underlying the path groupoid

{G_{\Lambda,\mathrm{Yee}}}
so that
{G_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}}={G_{\Lambda,\mathrm{Yee}}}
.
Each
x\in X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}
is of the form
x\colon\Omega_{k,m}\to\Lambda
for some
m\in(\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\})^{k}
.
Then,
(x,p)\in X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}*\mathbb{N}^{k}
if and only if
(e,p)\in\Omega_{k,m}
if and only if
p\leq m
, so the domain of the action of each

p\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
in our construction equals the domain of the action of
p

underlying
{G_{\Lambda,\mathrm{Yee}}}
.
In the above setting, following
[
Yee07
, Lemma 3.3]
,
\sigma^{p}x

denotes the unique element of
X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}
with
\mathrm{dom}(\sigma^{p}x)=\Omega_{k,m-p}
and

(\sigma^{p}x)(0,q)=x(p,p+q),

for each
q\leq m-p
.
The graph morphism
x\cdot m
too has these properties by construction, so

x\cdot m=\sigma^{m}x
.
Thus, the actions are the same, and so
{G_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}}={G_{\Lambda,\mathrm{Yee}}}
as sets.
Moreover, the groupoid operations are the same, so they are equal as
groupoids too.

It remains to show the topology
\tau_{\mathrm{Yee}}
on
{G_{\Lambda,\mathrm{Yee}}}

equals the topology
\tau
on
{G_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}}
so that
{G_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}}={G_{\Lambda,\mathrm{Yee}}}
as topological groupoids.
This is well-known, but we are not aware of a reference where the details
have been given.
We start by identifying a basis for each topology.
By
Corollary

5.4
and
[
Jon25
, Lemma 6.3]
, the collection of

Z_{{G_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}}}\left(\kappa\setminus K,\lambda\setminus L\right)\coloneqq Z_{X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}}(Z_{X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}}\left(\kappa\setminus K\right),\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\kappa),\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\lambda),Z_{X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}}\left(\lambda\setminus L\right)),

where
\kappa,\lambda\in\Lambda
and
K\subseteq\kappa\Lambda,L\subseteq\lambda\Lambda
are finite, is a basis for
\tau
.
Given any sets
A,B\subseteq\Lambda
, we write

A*_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}}B\coloneqq\{(\lambda,\mu)\in A\times B\mid\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(\lambda)=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(\mu)\}.

For each
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
and
F\subseteq\Lambda*_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}}\Lambda
, define

Z_{\mathrm{Yee}}(F,m)\coloneqq\{(\lambda x,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\lambda)-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu),\mu x)\in{G_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}}\mid(\lambda,\mu)\in F,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\lambda)-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu)=m\},

where
\lambda x
is defined as in
[
Yee07
, Lemma 3.3]
.
We can assume without loss of generality that each
(\lambda,\mu)\in F

satisfies
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\lambda)-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu)=m
.
By
[
Yee07
, Proposition 3.6]
, the collection of sets of the form

Z_{\mathrm{Yee}}(A*_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}}B,m)\cap Z_{\mathrm{Yee}}(F,m)^{c}

for
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
and
A,B\subseteq\Lambda
and finite
F\subseteq\Lambda*_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}}\Lambda
is a basis for
\tau_{\mathrm{Yee}}
.

We show
\tau_{\mathrm{Yee}}\subseteq\tau
.
For any
m\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
and
A,B\subseteq\Lambda
,

Z_{\mathrm{Yee}}(A*_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}}B,m)=\bigcup_{(\lambda,\mu)\in A*_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}}B}Z_{{G_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}}}\left(\lambda\setminus\emptyset,\mu\setminus\emptyset\right)\in\tau.

Now fix
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
and finite
F\subseteq\Lambda*_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}}\Lambda
.
To show
Z_{\mathrm{Yee}}(F,m)^{c}\in\tau
, we show
Z_{\mathrm{Yee}}(F,m)
is
closed with respect to
\tau
.
Suppose
(g_{n})\subseteq Z_{\mathrm{Yee}}(F,m)
and
g_{n}\to g\in{G_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}}
with respect to
\tau
.
Because
(g_{n})\subseteq Z_{\mathrm{Yee}}(F,m)
, we can write
g_{n}=(\lambda_{n}x_{n},m,\mu_{n}x_{n})
, where
(\lambda_{n},\mu_{n})\in F
, for all

n
.
Because
F
is finite, by the pigeon-hole principle, there is a subsequence

(g_{n_{k}})
and some
(\lambda,\mu)\in F
such that
g_{n_{k}}=(\lambda x_{n_{k}},m,\mu x_{n_{k}})
, for all
k
.
Moreover,
g_{n_{k}}\to g
.
Thus, we can assume without loss of generality that
g_{n}=(\lambda x_{n},m,\mu x_{n})
, for all
n
.
Say
g=(w^{\prime},m^{\prime},z^{\prime})
.
By
[
RW17
, Proposition 5.12(b)]
, the map
(x,n,z)\mapsto n
from

{G_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}}
to
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
is continuous with respect to
\tau
and the
discrete topology on
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
, so
(m,m,\dots)
converges to
m^{\prime}
, which
means
m=m^{\prime}
.
Since
{G_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}}
is a topological groupoid,
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(g_{n})\to\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(g)
and

\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(g_{n})\to\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(g)
with respect to
\tau
.
The unit space
{G_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}}^{(0)}
is homeomorphic to

X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}
so that
\lambda x_{n}\to w^{\prime}
and
\mu x_{n}\to z^{\prime}
.
Notice
(\lambda x_{n})\subseteq Z_{X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}}\left(\lambda\right)
and

(\mu x_{n})\subseteq Z_{X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}}\left(\mu\right)
, which are closed in

X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}
.
Hence,
w^{\prime}=\lambda w
and
z^{\prime}=\mu z
for some
w,z\in X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}
.
That is,
\lambda x_{n}\to\lambda w
and
\mu x_{n}\to\mu z
.
Both
\sigma^{\lambda}
and
\sigma^{\mu}
are continuous, and so
x_{n}\to w,z
,
which implies
x\coloneqq w=z
.
Thus,
g=(\lambda x,m,\mu x)\in Z_{\mathrm{Yee}}(F,m)
, and so

Z_{\mathrm{Yee}}(F,m)
is closed, as required.

Now, we show
\tau\subseteq\tau_{\mathrm{Yee}}
.
We show each
B\coloneqq Z_{{G_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}}}\left(\kappa\setminus K,\lambda\setminus L\right)\in\tau_{\mathrm{Yee}}
.
If
(\kappa,\lambda)\notin\Lambda*_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}}\Lambda
, then
B=\emptyset\in\tau_{\mathrm{Yee}}
.
Suppose
(\kappa,\lambda)\in\Lambda*_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}}\Lambda
.
Let
F\coloneqq\{(\kappa\zeta,\lambda\zeta)\in\Lambda*_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}}\Lambda\mid\kappa\zeta\in K\text{ or }\lambda\zeta\in L\}
.
We show

Z_{{G_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}}}\left(\kappa\setminus K,\lambda\setminus L\right)=Z_{\mathrm{Yee}}(\{\kappa\}*_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}}\{\lambda\},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\kappa)-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\lambda))\cap Z_{\mathrm{Yee}}(F,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\kappa)-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\lambda))^{c}.

Fix
g\coloneqq(\kappa x,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\kappa)-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\lambda),\lambda x)\in Z_{{G_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}}}\left(\kappa\setminus K,\lambda\setminus L\right)
.
Then,
g\in Z_{\mathrm{Yee}}(\{\kappa\}*_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}}\{\lambda\},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\kappa)-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\lambda))
.
Suppose for a contradiction that
g\in Z_{\mathrm{Yee}}(F,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\kappa)-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\lambda))
.
Then,
g=(\kappa\zeta w,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\kappa)-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\lambda),\lambda\zeta w)

for some
(\kappa\zeta,\lambda\zeta)\in F
.
By definition of
F
,
\kappa\zeta\in K
or
\lambda\zeta\in L
.
In either case, there is a contradiction with
g\in Z_{{G_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}}}\left(\kappa\setminus K,\lambda\setminus L\right)
.
For the other inclusion, fix
g\in Z_{\mathrm{Yee}}(\{\kappa\}*_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}}\{\lambda\},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\kappa)-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\lambda))\cap Z_{\mathrm{Yee}}(F,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\kappa)-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\lambda))^{c}
, so
g=(\kappa x,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\kappa)-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\lambda),\lambda x)
for some
x\in X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}
.
Then,
g\in Z_{{G_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}}}\left(\kappa\setminus\emptyset,\lambda\setminus\emptyset\right)
.
Suppose for a contradiction that
\kappa x\notin Z_{X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}}\left(\kappa\setminus K\right)
or
\lambda x\notin Z_{X_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}}\left(\lambda\setminus L\right)
.
In the former case, we have
\kappa x=\kappa\zeta w
for some
\kappa\zeta\in K
, in which case
(\kappa\zeta,\lambda\zeta)\in F
, and
x=\zeta w
.
Thus,
g=(\kappa\zeta w,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\kappa)-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\lambda),\lambda\zeta w)
, so
g\in Z_{\mathrm{Yee}}(F,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\kappa)-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\lambda))
, contradicting
g\in Z_{\mathrm{Yee}}(F,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\kappa)-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\lambda))^{c}
.
In the latter case, we have a similar contradiction, and so
g\in Z_{{G_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}}}\left(\kappa\setminus K,\lambda\setminus L\right)
.

Therefore,
{G_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}}={G_{\Lambda,\mathrm{Yee}}}
as topological
groupoids.
Since
\partial{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{m}}=\partial{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{Yee}}
, we also have that

\partial{G_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}}={G_{\Lambda,\mathrm{m}}}|_{\partial{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{m}}}={G_{\Lambda,\mathrm{Yee}}}|_{\partial{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{Yee}}}=\partial{G_{\Lambda,\mathrm{Yee}}}.

A Longitudinal, Multinational, and Multilingual Corpus of News Coverage of the Russo-Ukrainian War
Dikshya Mohanty, Taisiia Sabadyn, Jelwin Rodrigues

Abstract
We introduce dnipro , a novel longitudinal corpus of 246K news articles documenting the Russo-Ukrainian war from Feb 2022 to Aug 2024, spanning eleven media outlets across five nation states (Russia, Ukraine, U.S., U.K., and China) and three languages (English, Russian, and Mandarin Chinese). This multilingual resource features consistent and comprehensive metadata, and multiple types of annotation with rigorous human evaluations for downstream tasks relevant to systematic transnational analyses of contentious wartime discourse. dnipro ’s distinctive value lies in its inclusion of competing geopolitical perspectives , making it uniquely suited for studying narrative divergence, media framing, and information warfare. To demonstrate its utility, we include use case experiments using stance detection, sentiment analysis, topical framing, and contradiction analysis of major conflict events within the larger war. Our explorations reveal how outlets construct competing realities, with coverage exhibiting polarized interpretations that reflect geopolitical interests. Beyond supporting computational journalism research, dnipro provides a foundational resource for understanding how conflicting narratives emerge and evolve across global information ecosystems.

Related Work

Research on wartime media coverage relies on datasets capturing narratives across national and institutional contexts, where competing claims and propaganda techniques pose unique challenges. Such resources are vital for training models to analyze geopolitical discourse and evaluating their ability to identify narrative strategies. As a reflection of diverse goals, existing resources vary in scope, modality, and annotations.

Twitter & Reddit-based datasets are among the largest.

4

5

23

15

20
provides 16,500 news articles using semantic similarity for misinformation detection, but excludes the overarching war theme and curates only specific event-based articles.
dnipro
supersedes it by orders of magnitude in coverage, topics, multilinguality, temporality, and size.
18

Annotation practices differ widely: manual schemata for framing

dnipro
extends existing corpora by focusing on transnational and multilingual news coverage – underrepresented in prior social media-centered work – enabling comparative analyses of state-aligned narratives across geopolitical actors. It reflects news coverage as disseminated, without alteration, serving as a neutral resource for empirical studies. Our design choices spanning source selection, temporal coverage, and structured annotations make

3
The
dnipro
Corpus

Our dataset is constructed through a systematic multi-stage pipeline targeting news outlets and state-affiliated agencies selected on the basis of three criteria: (1) global influence and prominence within national media ecosystems, (2) sustained coverage of the Russo-Ukrainian war, and (3) discernible editorial positioning ranging from independent journalism to state-aligned messaging.
To the extent possible, access to full article archives was obtained through public RSS feeds, institutional subscriptions, or source-specific data collection pipelines.
However, not all media outlets provided consistent or unrestricted access to their archives, and some selections reflect practical constraints in addition to topical relevance.

3.1
Data Acquisition

dnipro
is a corpus spanning 31 months of daily data collection, from February 1, 2022 to August 31, 2024.
To identify articles with substantive coverage of the Russo-Ukrainian conflict, we employ a tiered strategy.
For outlets with dedicated sections or labels related to the war, we collect all articles published under those categories.
Where available, we also extract articles tagged with conflict-specific metadata (e.g., “Ukraine conflict”, “special military operation”, “Russia-Ukraine war”).
For sources lacking such taxonomies, we apply keyword-based filtering to isolate relevant content.
However, some sources impose significant restrictions on retrospective API-based keyword searches.
In these cases, we obtain the older articles directly through web browsers, operating within the scope of respective terms of service and copyright restrictions.
To ensure quality control, we manually validate stratified random samples across sources, languages, and time periods throughout the acquisition process.

4.1
Text Processing for Annotation

To prepare the corpus for annotation and analyses, we apply standardized processing steps to all data, across sources and languages. This ensures consistency across media sources with varying conventions in structure, formatting, and metadata.

Publication dates are parsed and normalized to ISO 8601 standard (
yyyy-mm-dd
), and multiple metadata fields—
e.g.
, “publication date”, “published on”—are mapped to metadata fields in our unified schema (see
Table 2
).
Text content is stripped of non-informative boilerplate elements like location or date prefixes commonly found in news articles (
e.g.
, “KYIV, March 3 –”), inline footnotes, bylines, and repeated source disclaimers.
Duplicate articles are removed, to avoid redundancy in print syndication and multilingual reprints.
To support downstream linguistic analyses, the text is then converted to UTF-8 encoding, segmented into paragraphs, and split into sentences.

Conclusion

The
dnipro
corpus represents a significant advancement in resources for computational analysis of media narratives during international conflicts. By integrating diverse geopolitical perspectives, consistent translations, and structured metadata across 246K articles, this dataset enables systematic study of narrative divergence that previous resources could not support.
Our exploratory applications demonstrate
dnipro
’s utility across multiple analytical dimensions, from stance and sentiment detection to topical framing and contradiction analysis. The findings reveal how global media ecosystems construct parallel realities through attribution choices, selective emphasis, and strategic framing, rarely engaging with competing narratives.

Beyond its utility in computational journalism,
dnipro
provides a foundation for developing more sophisticated methods to detect cross-cultural narrative divergence, identify emergent propaganda strategies, and understand information warfare dynamics. As the global information landscape grows increasingly complex, such resources become essential for technical advancement as well as a broader understanding of narratives that shape our perception of international conflicts.

Limitations

The Russo-Ukrainian War has not concluded. The dataset, which currently spans from Feb 2022 to August 2024, may omit important narratives and developments resulting from politically significant events thereafter (
e.g.
, foreign policy changes of a powerful geopolitical entity).
For the chosen news sources, a wider variety of publications representing NATO nations would
be beneficial. Currently, only two of the thirty-two NATO nations are represented (the United Kingdom and
the United States of America). There are other media sources like DW in Germany, which are highly referenced globally, so they are an influential factor.
Additionally, representation of more countries with ambiguous involvement in the war would add
value to a truly global version of the dataset. Potentially interesting countries would be Hungary and Romania, both of which border Ukraine; Belarus, also bordering Ukraine, but known to be an ally of the Russian state; Turkiye, a powerful regional actor, especially in the Black Sea; and Georgia, a NATO contender that continues to maintain reasonably strong relations with Russia.

Another facet limiting
dnipro
is the imperfect ground-truth annotations for stance, and other discursive phenomena. While this is not a limitation for qualitative analyses or mixed-method approaches, it does hinder nuanced supervised learning directly from the corpus. Our exploratory analyses, while illustrative, are not exhaustive and serve primarily to demonstrate the dataset’s potential rather than to make definitive empirical claims.

8
Ethical Considerations

dnipro
contains potentially sensitive content related to an ongoing conflict with intense geopolitical implications. We acknowledge several potential ethical dimensions:

Data collection:
All content was gathered from publicly available news sources without breaching access restrictions or terms of service. We do not include any copyright-protected content or any private communications.

Bias, representation, and inclusion:
Our corpus inherently reflects the biases present in global media coverage. The inclusion of opposing narratives—including state-controlled media—is a deliberate choice, to empower comparative analysis.
We strongly caution users against interpreting the mere inclusion of a source as endorsement of its content
.

Potential misuse:
While designed for media analysis, computational linguistics, and conflict studies, this resource could also be used for propaganda analysis. And thus, it could also be
misused
for propaganda analysis.
We strongly urge the community to utilize this corpus for constructive research into understanding narrative differences to bridge societal gaps, rather than weaponize any findings.

Lost in translation:
Machine translation introduces potential distortions, particularly with politically charged terminology and diplomatic language use. We document the details of our translation process. Further, we retain the original articles for enhanced scrutiny and possible resolution by experts in Russian, Ukrainian, and Mandarin Chinese.

Contextualization:
We provide robust metadata (publication date, source country, outlet ownership) to ensure articles can be interpreted within their proper contexts rather than in isolation.

By openly addressing these considerations, we aim to enable valuable research on contentious discourse while minimizing potential harms. It is our expectation that through the study of such discourse, researchers will be able to bring empirical enhancements to conflict resolution mechanisms. Applications of such datasets are continuously evolving, as is the dataset itself. As such,
we earnestly welcome community feedback on additional safeguards
.

For our quantitative analysis, we used the mistral-small-latest model via the Mistral API to jointly extract stance targets and classify stances, and employed the siebert/sentiment-roberta-large-english model from Hugging Face to estimate sentiment polarity toward named entities. Prompt configurations varied by task. We employed both zero-shot and few-shot strategies, along with system-level instructions and output formatting constraints, following best practices in prompt engineering for language models
29
.

A.1.3
Annotation for Article-level Tags

To annotate articles with high-level thematic tags, we used a zero-shot prompt with GPT-3.5 that instructed the model to extract a set of concise, specific tags summarizing each article’s content. Tags were intended to capture relevant people, locations, military units, political themes, and ideological framings. To ensure consistency and support post-processing, the prompt enforced a structured JSON output format.

Prompt:

You are a geopolitical analyst trained to extract concise topical tags from news articles related to international conflict, especially the Russia–Ukraine war. Your task is to read the content of a single article and extract 5 to 12 relevant tags that capture the article’s key topics, actors, places, events, or narrative themes. Each tag should be 1–4 words long, lowercase unless a proper noun, and specific rather than general. Avoid vague tags like “fake”, “news”, or “war” unless central to the article. Tags may include named individuals, military groups, operations, locations, events, or ideological narratives. Output the result in valid JSON using the following structure:

{

]
}

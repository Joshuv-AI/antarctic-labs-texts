CEAM-DETR: An NMS-free lightweight transformer for weed detection in soybean fields under complex conditions
Authors: Cheng Zhang, Jianyu Xiao, Yiqun Chang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Weed detection in complex agricultural environments faces significant challenges due to drastic illumination variations, high visual similarity between crops and weeds, and strong background interference, which place strict requirements on both detection accuracy and real-time performance. Most existing object detection methods rely on non-maximum suppression (NMS) as a post-processing step. However, this mechanism suffers from threshold sensitivity, limited cross-scene adaptability, and accumulated inference latency in practical applications. To address these limitations, this paper proposes a lightweight adaptive transformer-based model, termed CEAM-DETR, under an end-to-end detection paradigm to achieve efficient and stable weed detection without relying on NMS. A cross-stage efficient attention backbone is first constructed by integrating cross-stage partial connections with single-head self-attention, where feature splitting and cross-stage shortcuts preserve lightweight information and gradient propagation paths, while attention is applied only to partial channels to reduce computational and memory overhead and enhance fine-grained representations of small-scale targets. On this basis, an adaptive sparse feature interaction module (ASFI) is introduced to dynamically fuse sparse and dense attention branches, thereby improving the concentration of discriminative information under complex backgrounds. Furthermore, a multi-scale dilated re-parameterization block (MSDRB) is designed to extract features using parallel convolutions with different dilation rates and to equivalently merge them into a single convolution layer during inference, which expands the receptive field without increasing computational burden and supplements multi-scale contextual information. Experimental results on public datasets demonstrate that CEAM-DETR outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of detection accuracy and robustness, validating its effectiveness in complex agricultural environments. Compared to the original RT-DETR, the proposed model increased by 1.5%, reduced Params by 36.5%, decreased GFLOPs by 26.0%, and improved FPS by 32.9%.

Introduction

Soybean is a globally important food and cash crop. Its yield formation and quality enhancement are influenced by a combination of biotic and abiotic stresses, as well as cultivation management. Among the various influencing factors, weed competition is considered one of the key biotic stressors that significantly restrict soybean yields. When weeds compete with soybeans for resources from the early growth stage, soybean yield losses can reach 52% to 60%1. Currently, weed management in farmlands primarily relies on spraying chemical herbicides. While this method can rapidly suppress weed growth, it has significant limitations in practical applications. Studies have shown that excessive long-term use of herbicides can induce the emergence of resistant weeds, such as glyphosate-resistant Palmer amaranth and common Debregeasia edulis, making them more difficult to control2,3. Additionally, improper herbicide application can lead to soil and water pollution, posing risks to agricultural product quality and safety4. In contrast, intelligent weed control technologies based on Variable Rate Application (VRA) are considered a key approach for achieving sustainable agricultural development. Specifically, in soybean cultivation, accurate weed detection is highly challenging due to the severe occlusion caused by dense soybean canopies, dynamic illumination changes, and the high morphological similarity between soybean and common broadleaf weeds at early growth stages. Therefore, high-precision and real-time weed detection technologies tailored for soybean fields serve as crucial support5.

Despite the remarkable progress of deep learning in generic object detection, deploying these models directly into complex agricultural scenarios often yields suboptimal results6. CNN-based single-stage detectors (such as the YOLO series7) have advantages in real-time inference; however, they inherently rely on non-maximum suppression (NMS) for post-processing8–10. This makes them highly sensitive to hyperparameter tuning and prone to performance degradation under the severe occlusion and dense distribution typical of weed growth11. Conversely, Transformer-based architectures have emerged as a powerful alternative, leveraging global attention mechanisms to achieve NMS-free, end-to-end detection12.

RT-DETR (Real-Time Detection Transformer)13, as a pioneering real-time end-to-end detector, achieves a compelling trade-off between accuracy and inference efficiency, presenting an attractive infrastructure for agricultural vision. Nevertheless, its direct application to weed detection reveals critical bottlenecks: (1) the quadratic computational complexity of dense self-attention mechanisms severely restricts its deployment on computationally constrained agricultural edge devices14; (2) standard feature extractors struggle to capture the fine-grained visual cues necessary to distinguish small-scale weeds from highly similar crop backgrounds15.

Based on the research background and the specific agronomic needs of soybean cultivation, this paper proposes a lightweight adaptive end-to-end weed detection model, CEAM-DETR, tailored for soybean fields. This model optimizes the RT-DETR structure with a design philosophy aimed at reducing feature redundancy, enhancing key feature expression, and improving multi-scale context modeling ability. It maintains the advantages of end-to-end detection while simultaneously improving detection performance and computational efficiency. Experimental results on public datasets show that our method outperforms state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods. Related work

Traditional and machine learning-based weed detection

In the field of automated weed detection, technology has evolved from traditional methods to deep learning approaches. Early research mainly relied on plant spectral characteristics to distinguish crops from weeds. For example, hyperspectral imaging combined with texture-based segmentation methods has been used for early-stage weed detection in cotton fields16. However, such methods typically require expensive sensor equipment and are sensitive to light and environmental conditions, limiting their large-scale application in the field17. Subsequently, machine learning algorithms such as random forests, support vector machines, and k-nearest neighbors were introduced for weed identification tasks18. While these methods have achieved some success in specific scenarios, their performance is heavily dependent on manually designed features, and detection accuracy tends to fluctuate under dynamically changing environmental conditions19. The bottleneck of handcrafted feature engineering has driven researchers to shift their focus toward deep learning approaches20.

Deep learning-based agricultural vision and architecture evolution

In recent years, convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have made significant progress in plant and weed classification and detection tasks20. Ferreira et al. used ConvNets to conduct weed detection studies in soybean fields, achieving accurate identification and classification of various weed species21. Guo et al. proposed the RLCFE-Net, which reparameterizes the large convolutional kernel feature extraction structure, effectively enhancing detection performance while reducing model parameter count22.

As deep learning architectures continue to evolve, researchers have begun exploring more efficient feature extraction paradigms and cost-effective annotation strategies. To achieve the complementary advantages of global context and local spatial features, Cui et al.23 proposed CSWin-MBConv, a hybrid dual-network fusing the CSWin Transformer with MBConv, which demonstrated excellent performance in complex farmland environments. Furthermore, addressing the challenge of high pixel-level annotation costs in dense agricultural scenarios, Chen et al.24 developed PIS-Net, an efficient weakly supervised instance segmentation network based on point annotations, offering a highly cost-effective solution for precise weed identification in rice fields. Despite these advancements significantly promoting agricultural vision, pure CNN architectures are restricted by localized receptive fields, making it difficult to establish long-range dependencies in severely occluded agricultural canopies; meanwhile, conventional hybrid architectures still struggle to meet the real-time deployment requirements of agricultural edge devices in terms of inference speed and model lightweighting25.

Advanced attention mechanisms and cross-domain visual inspiration

To overcome the aforementioned limitations, Transformer-based architectures and advanced attention mechanisms have demonstrated robust vitality across various branches of computer vision. Regarding the handling of complex visual noise in real-world scenarios, Ma et al.26,27 conducted pioneering work in image denoising: they explored frequency domain attention and dual meta attention, demonstrating that dynamic, adaptive attention weights can effectively filter complex background interference in real photographs and purify core features. This concept of feature purification in real and uncontrollable environments provides crucial inspiration for resolving the severe visual interference caused by dynamic illumination and complex soil backgrounds in soybean fields.

On the other hand, in dense prediction tasks dealing with high occlusion and fine-grained feature entanglement, cross-domain feature fusion has shown immense potential. For instance, Bui et al.28,29 successfully applied a pre-trained language model-guided cross-transformer (CLEAR) and a channel-aware cross-fused network (C2T-Net) in pedestrian attribute recognition tasks. The challenges of dense occlusion, local detail confusion, and multi-scale targets faced in pedestrian attribute recognition are highly homologous to the visual dilemmas of weeds and crops being highly similar under the soybean canopy. These works profoundly prove the exceptional efficacy of cross-channel perception and adaptive feature interaction in decoupling complex visual representations.

Inspired by the aforementioned progress in cutting-edge cross-disciplines, this paper aims to fill the gap in applying high-performance attention mechanisms to real-time agricultural detection. By integrating structural reparameterization30 and adaptive sparse attention mechanisms inspired by cross-domain visual tasks, we perfectly adapt the end-to-end DETR framework to soybean weed detection. This approach not only copes with complex background noise interference but also greatly enhances the model’s perception of fine-grained weed features and overall inference efficiency.

RT-DETR, the first real-time end-to-end object detection model, eliminates the need for non-maximum suppression (NMS) through its "one-to-one matching" strategy, outperforming traditional YOLO models in dense-object detection tasks13. The model directly predicts target locations from input images, removing the need for candidate region generation and enabling end-to-end optimization. This makes it highly suitable for rapid-response weed detection tasks. Therefore, we choose RT-DETR as the foundation of the overall architecture and make targeted improvements based on the characteristics of agricultural field environments.

In the backbone network stage, considering the computational redundancy of ResNet-18, which is the default backbone of RT-DETR, in high-resolution agricultural image processing, and the fact that excessive downsampling weakens shallow high-resolution features, affecting the detailed expression of small-scale weed targets, we design the cross-stage efficient attention backbone (CEffNet). This network combines the cross-stage partial connections concept with a single-head self-attention mechanism (SHSA)31, significantly improving both feature flow computational efficiency and representational power. This design not only optimizes the original convolution structure but also enhances feature interaction flexibility without adding computational overhead.

However, optimizing the backbone alone does not fully resolve the background interference caused by complex environments. To further improve feature interaction efficiency and suppress background noise, we introduce the adaptive sparse attention mechanism (ASSA)32, which reconfigures the feature interaction process of AIFI. This enables the model to adaptively balance information retention and redundancy suppression in complex backgrounds.

Furthermore, to fully leverage the clean features filtered by ASFI and enhance multi-scale contextual modeling, we design the multi-scale dilated re-parameterization block (MSDRB) to expand the receptive field. The MSDRB employs five parallel convolutional branches with different dilation factors to extract multi-level features. During training, the multi-branch structure enhances feature representation; during inference, BatchNorm fusion33 and zero-padding alignment30 combine all branches into a single convolutional layer, effectively expanding the receptive field without increasing inference computational overhead.

Detailed design of core modules

Cross-stage efficient attention network

In object detection tasks, ResNet-18, as a classical convolutional neural network (CNN) backbone, has demonstrated strong performance in many applications; however, it still exhibits notable limitations when handling fine-grained features in complex scenes. On the one hand, its stage-wise downsampling and stacked convolution design inevitably compress shallow high-resolution features, causing fine-grained information such as edges and textures—crucial for small-object detection—to gradually diminish in deeper representations. On the other hand, deep features repeatedly participate in computation across all channels, which easily introduces unnecessary computational redundancy and consequently limits deployment efficiency on resource-constrained agricultural terminals34.

To alleviate these issues, it is necessary to reorganize the feature flow structure such that a portion of features can directly participate in subsequent representations with reduced computational intervention, thereby preserving critical information while controlling computational complexity. In this context, introducing attention mechanisms becomes an effective approach to enhance feature representation capability. Self-attention mechanisms explicitly model spatial dependencies and have demonstrated superior contextual modeling ability in object detection and dense prediction tasks7,35. However, standard multi-head self-attention on high-resolutison feature maps typically incurs substantial computational and memory overhead, and different attention heads often exhibit high redundancy, resulting in an unfavorable trade-off between performance gains and computational cost31,35.

To address this limitation, we introduce and further refine the application of the (SHSA) in object detection. SHSA applies attention modeling only to a subset of channels, while the remaining channels preserve the original feature flow, thereby effectively reducing computational redundancy while retaining essential spatial dependencies. Furthermore, by constructing cross-stage shortcut pathways within the network, features that do not participate in attention computation can be directly propagated to the output, which mitigates deep feature attenuation and improves overall feature utilization efficiency.

Inspired by this concept, we designed the CEffNet architecture (Fig. 2) to achieve a balanced trade-off between lightweight computation and detection accuracy. Adaptive sparse feature interaction module

Grad-CAM visualization36 reveals that RT-DETR features a dispersed attention distribution in complex scenarios (Fig. 8), where high-activation regions fail to focus effectively on the target. The CEffNet provides high-quality multi-scale features, whereas the AIFI module employs standard DSA, which models global dependencies by computing correlations across all spatial positions (an n×n attention matrix). However, this “fully connected’’ property makes it prone to allocating excessive attention weights to irrelevant background regions, causing the high-quality features extracted by the CEffNet to be diluted by background noise. The adaptive sparse attention proposed by Zhou et al.32 provides inspiration for our design. It achieves feature sparsification through the ReLU2 activation function and dynamically balances feature filtering and information retention via a sparse-dense dual-branch mechanism.

Therefore, within the hybrid encoder, we introduce an Adaptive Sparse Self-Attention (ASSA) mechanism to reconstruct the feature interaction paradigm of the original AIFI, and accordingly propose the Adaptive Sparse Feature Interaction (ASFI) module. By jointly modeling sparse attention and dense attention, ASFI enables the model to adaptively regulate attention allocation under complex background conditions, suppressing background noise while preserving essential contextual information, thereby enhancing the effectiveness of feature interaction. Multi-Scale dilated reparameterization block

In the RT-DETR, the RepC3 module is the core component for enhancing spatial and contextual information-modeling capabilities. However, its core design still relies on traditional region-perception and positional-encoding mechanisms. Its fixed receptive fields make it difficult to accommodate the requirements of multi-scale target detection37. This limitation is particularly prominent in weed detection: weeds and crops frequently exhibit similar color tones and morphological similarities, and they often appear within complex backgrounds such as soil or field structures, posing significant challenges for local feature extraction.

Dilated convolution expands the receptive field by introducing a dilation factor r without increasing the number of parameters. It has been widely adopted in semantic segmentation tasks38,39. Although dilated convolutions effectively enlarge the receptive field, naïvely stacking them introduces two major limitations: a single dilation factor cannot simultaneously capture multi-scale feature information, and an efficient mechanism for multi-scale feature fusion is lacking40,41.

Reparameterization30,42 offers an effective solution to these issues. Through equivalent transformations, this technique fuses multiple small convolutional kernels into a single large convolutional kernel during inference, achieving a synergy between training flexibility and inference efficiency. Prior work43 shows that the performance advantage of large-kernel convolutions arises from the ability of parallel dilated layers to capture sparse patterns rather than merely combining small kernels or stacking receptive fields.

Based on the above analysis, we propose a Multi-Scale Dilated Reparameterization Block (MSDRB) to enhance multi-scale contextual perception without increasing the computational burden during inference. The MSDRB first performs channel-dimension adjustment via a 1×1 convolution, followed by four parallel convolutional branches with different kernel sizes and dilation factors (a 9×9 dilated convolution, two 5×5 dilated convolutions, and a 3×3 dilated convolution). Each branch captures feature information over a specific spatial range. Dilated convolutions expand the receptive field without increasing the computational cost, allowing simultaneous modeling of local fine-grained features and global contextual information44,45. A BatchNorm layer follows each dilated convolution to stabilize training, accelerate convergence, and improve overall training efficiency33.

To further promote efficient multi-scale feature fusion, the MSDRB introduces a skip-connection mechanism34 that combines the outputs of parallel branches with either a 1× 1 convolution or an identity mapping, ensuring feature dimensional consistency while enhancing information flow46. During inference, the multibranch structure is equivalently fused into a single convolutional layer via reparameterization42 . Experiments and analysis

Experimental setup

All experiments in this study were conducted on a computing platform running the CentOS Linux operating system. To ensure experimental reproducibility and optimal model performance, the experimental parameters were rigorously configured. Guided by standard DETR-based architectures, we defined a specific search space for key optimization hyperparameters. These metrics enable evaluation of the model’s robustness and applicability under different IoU thresholds. Deeper deployment displays more advantages: the inference speeds at Layers 6 and 8 (51.2 FPS and 51.7 FPS, respectively) significantly exceed that of Layer 4 (40.4 FPS), despite comparable FLOPs among the three. Furthermore, shallow-layer feature channels have lower inter-channel similarity, whereas deeper-layer channels have higher similarity. As a result, a SHSA mechanism can more effectively capture feature dependencies in deeper stages 31. Further analysis reveals that combining the CEffNet at Layers 6 and 8 produces a synergistic enhancement effect. Their joint deployment achieves an mAP@0.5 of 75.0%, exceeding the expected linear accumulation of single-layer deployments. This synergy originates from the cross-stage connection structure of the C2f module, which allows both layers to share gradient propagation paths. High-level semantic information extracted from Layer 8 (receptive field 13×13) guides feature filtering at Layer 6 (receptive field 7×7), forming a coarse-to-fine progressive optimization process. The post-processing time data in Table 6 reflect the efficiency advantage of an end-to-end detection paradigm over traditional NMS-based post-processing. Generalization capability and robustness verification

Discussion

Although the CEAM-DETR achieved outstanding performance on the ArgWeed and VisDrone2019 datasets, the method still has several limitations. While the current model maintains stable performance under complex illumination conditions, its effectiveness is inevitably compromised under extreme real-world conditions beyond the tested datasets, such as heavy rainfall, dense fog, sandstorms, or nighttime. Under such extreme conditions, the visibility of RGB images decreases sharply (e.g., visibility < 20 m). Thus, providing sufficient discriminative information is difficult for single-modal inputs; this leads to degraded detection performance. This limitation arises from the model’s reliance on RGB visual features for target recognition. Extreme weather and severe low-light conditions significantly degrade image contrast, clarity, and color information, further impeding the extraction of fine-grained features for small objects. To overcome this issue, future studies may investigate multimodal fusion strategies that integrate RGB images with thermal infrared data. Given the insensitivity of thermal infrared imaging to illumination and weather variations, such an approach could enable the development of an all-weather detection system, thereby enhancing the adaptability and stability of the model in complex environments. Furthermore, introducing domain-specific image dehazing or low-light enhancement preprocessing networks could also serve as a viable direction to improve model robustness prior to feature extraction.

Conclusions

We propose the CEAM-DETR, an innovative lightweight adaptive weed-detection model that effectively resolves the trade-off between detection accuracy and inference speed in complex agricultural environments. This is accomplished through the integration of the CEffNet, ASFI module, and MSDRB. The CEffNet mitigates computational redundancy and enhances small-object detection through a SHSA mechanism. The ASFI module improves inference speed while preserving accuracy by dynamically balancing feature filtering and information retention. The MSDRB module strengthens contextual modeling by expanding the receptive field via reparameterized multi-scale dilated convolutions. The experimental results show that the CEAM-DETR significantly outperforms the RT-DETR in terms of precision, speed, and computational efficiency. The model maintains robust performance under diverse illumination conditions and outperforms other SOTAs on both the ArgWeed and VisDrone2019 datasets, achieving an exceptional balance between accuracy and lightweight design. This work provides an efficient and accurate solution for real-time weed detection in precision agriculture and lays a solid foundation for advancing computer vision technologies in agricultural applications. Future research may focus on refining multimodal fusion strategies and optimizing deployment on edge devices to realize low-power agricultural automation systems.

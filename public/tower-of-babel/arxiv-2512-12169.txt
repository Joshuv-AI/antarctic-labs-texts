Abstract




Some Novel Aspects of the Plane Pendulum in Classical Mechanics

Avadh Saxena

Theoretical Division and Center for Nonlinear Studies,
Los Alamos National Laboratory, Los Alamos, New Mexico 87545, USA

Abstract:

We obtain a novel connection between the exact solutions of the plane pendulum,
hyperbolic plane pendulum and inverted plane pendulum equations as well as the
static solutions of the sine-Gordon and the sine hyperbolic-Gordon equations
and obtain a few exact solutions of the above mentioned equations. Besides,
we consider the plane pendulum equation in the first anharmonic approximation
and obtain its large number of exact periodic as well as hyperbolic solutions.
In addition, we obtain two exact solutions of the plane pendulum equation in
the second anharmonic approximation. Further, we introduce an elliptic plane
pendulum equation in terms of the Jacobi elliptic functions
-
{\rm sn}(\theta,m)/{\rm dn}(\theta,m)
which smoothly goes over to the the plane
pendulum equation in the
m=0
limit and the hyperbolic plane pendulum equation
in the
m=1
limit where
m
is the modulus of the Jacobi elliptic functions.
We show that in the harmonic approximation, the elliptic pendulum problem
represents a one-parameter family of isochronous system. Further, for the
special case of
m=1/2
, we show that one has an isochronous
system even in the first anharmonic approximation. Finally, we also briefly
discuss the hyperbolic plane pendulum and obtain a few of its exact solutions
in the harmonic as well as the first anharmonic approximation.

Introduction

The plane pendulum system is one of the celebrated problems in classical
mechanics and has been discussed in almost every classical mechanics book.
While most of the books are content in terms of discussing its solution in the
harmonic approximation, there are books which have also discussed some of its
exact solutions
[
1
]
. Apart from it being an educational tool, the plane
pendulum is useful in understanding oscillations and nonlinear oscillators. It
serves as a model system for exploring topics such as chaos theory and the
behavior of driven or damped pendulums. Many aspects of the
plane pendulum problem in quantum mechanics are well understood
[
2
,
3
]

basically since it is a linear problem. For some recent papers on this subject
see
[
4
,
5
,

,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
. On the other hand, plane pendulum
problem in classical mechanics continues to give new surprises. It
involves exact solutions of a nonlinear problem and one is never sure if one
has obtained all possible exact solutions of the plane pendulum equation.
Most of the activity is concentrated in finding an accurate expression for the
time period of the plane pendulum away from the harmonic approximation

[

,

,

,

,

. It is
worth reminding that in principle the exact solution of the plane pendulum
(and hence the time period) is known in terms of the Jacobi elliptic function

[
2
,
3
]
.

The purpose of the present paper is to shed some light on what we believe
to be a few new features of the plane pendulum problem. To begin with,
we show that with suitable Ansatz the static solutions of the sine-Gordon
(SG) and the sine hyperbolic-Gordon (SHG) equations as well as the
(time-dependent) solutions of the inverted plane pendulum and the plane
pendulum equations are all related to the solutions of a nonlinear equation.
We then present five solutions of this nonlinear equation and hence obtain
five solutions of the above mentioned four equations. Further, we show that
with another suitable ansatz the static solutions of the sine-Gordon equation
as well as the (time-dependent) solutions of the hyperbolic plane pendulum,
inverted plane pendulum as well as the plane pendulum equation are all related
to the solutions of a different nonlinear equation. We then present four
solutions of this nonlinear equation and hence obtain four solutions of the
above mentioned four equations. With yet another ansatz, we obtain a few new
exact solutions of the plane pendulum problem. We then consider the plane
pendulum problem in the first anharmonic approximation and show that the
resulting equation is very similar to the equation for the static solutions of
the symmetric
\phi^{4}

model. Using this correspondence we obtain a large number of exact periodic
and hyperbolic solutions of the plane pendulum problem in the first anharmonic
approximation. For each periodic solution, we also mention the corresponding
time period
T
. On the other hand, if we consider the plane pendulum in the
second anharmonic approximation, we find that the resulting equation is very
similar to the equation for the static solutions of the

\phi^{2}
-
\phi^{4}
-
\phi^{6}
model but with the
\phi^{4}
and the
\phi^{6}

coefficients being highly constrained. As a result, so far we have been able to
obtain only two exact solutions of the plane pendulum problem in the
second anharmonic approximation which are presented in the Appendix. We also
introduce the so called elliptic pendulum equation

\theta_{tt}=-{\rm sn}(\theta,m)/{\rm dn}(\theta,m)
where
0\leq m\leq 1
is the
modulus parameter while
{\rm sn}(\theta,m)
,
{\rm dn}(\theta,m)
, and
{\rm cn}(\theta,m)

(see below) are the Jacobi elliptic functions
[
24
]
. In the limit
m=0
,
the elliptic pendulum equation goes over to the plane pendulum equation while
in the
m=1
limit it goes over to the hyperbolic plane pendulum equation. We
show that in the harmonic approximation, no matter what is the value of

0\leq m\leq 1
, the elliptic pendulum is an example of an isochronous system.
The latter means that all solutions are periodic with the same fixed period so
that the time period is independent of the amplitude. Further, for the special
case of the modulus parameter
m=1/2
, it continues to be an isochronous
system even in the first anharmonic approximation. For completeness,
we also consider the hyperbolic plane pendulum equation and obtain a few of
its solutions in the harmonic and the first anharmonic approximation.

The plan of the paper is the following. In Sec. IIa we show that with a suitable
ansatz, the solutions of the static SG equation, static SHG equation and the
plane pendulum as well as the inverted plane pendulum equations can all be
shown to be related to the solution of a single nonlinear equation. We then
obtain five exact solutions of this nonlinear equation (and hence exact
solutions of all four problems in a single attempt). In Sec.
IIb we show that with another suitable ansatz, the solutions of the static SG
equation, the plane pendulum equation, the inverted pendulum equation and the
hyperbolic pendulum equation can all be reduced to the solution of another
nonlinear equation. We then obtain four exact solutions of this nonlinear
equation (and hence the solutions of the four problems in a single attempt).
In Sec. IIc we obtain a few more new solutions of the plane
pendulum equation using another ansatz. In Sec. III we consider the plane
pendulum equation in
the first anharmonic approximation and point out its similarity to
the static symmetric
\phi^{4}
equation. Using this correspondence, we then
obtain several exact periodic as well as hyperbolic solutions of the plane
pendulum problem in the first anharmonic approximation. For each
periodic solution we also mention the corresponding time period.
In Sec. IV we introduce a novel elliptic pendulum equation which goes over
to the plane pendulum and the hyperbolic plane pendulum equations in the
appropriate limits. We analyze the elliptic pendulum equation in some detail
and show that in the harmonic approximation it offers a one-parameter family of
isochronous system. We also obtain exact solutions of the elliptic pendulum
problem in the first anharmonic approximation and in the special case of
m=1/2
we show that it continues to be an isochronous system even in the first
anharmonic approximation. In Sec. V we summarize the results obtained in this
paper and point out some of the open problems.
In Appendix A we consider the plane pendulum equation in the second anharmonic
approximation and point out its similarity with the static

\phi^{2}
-
\phi^{4}
-
\phi^{6}
model with constraints on the
\phi^{4}
and
\phi^{6}

coefficients because
of which we are able to obtain only two exact solutions. In Appendix B we
obtain a few of the exact solutions of the hyperbolic plane pendulum equation
in the harmonic and the first anharmonic approximation. Finally, in Appendix C
we mention a few important properties of the Jacobi elliptic functions

[
24
]
which are used in this paper.

Connection Between the Solutions of the Plane Pendulum Equation
with the Solutions of Several Important Equations in Physics

One of the most important problems in dynamical systems is the plane pendulum,
the equation of motion for which is given by

\theta_{tt}=-a\sin(\theta)\,,~~a={g}/{l}\,.

(1)

By rescaling
t\rightarrow t/\sqrt{a}
we can always eliminate
a
from
Eq. (
1
) and write the plane pendulum equation as

\theta_{tt}=-\sin(\theta)\,.

(2)

Once we have obtained a solution
f(t)
of the scaled Eq. (
2
), it then
follows that the corresponding solution of the original plane pendulum
Eq. (
1
) is
f(\sqrt{a}t)
.

2.1
Connection Between the Solutions of Plane Pendulum Equation,
Inverted Plane Pendulum Equation as well as Static Solutions of SG and
SHG Equations

We now show that the static solutions of the SG and the SHG equations and the
(time dependent) solutions of the plane pendulum equation (
2
) and the
inverted plane pendulum equation (see below) are all intimately related
to the solutions of the nonlinear equation

(1-u^{2})u_{yy}+u(u_{y})^{2}=-u(1-u^{2})^{2}\,,

(3)

where
y
is
x
or
t
depending on the equation concerned.
The proof is straightforward. We discuss the four cases one by one.

Case I: Sine-Gordon Equation

Consider the sine-Gordon equation

\phi_{xx}=\sin[\phi(x)]\,.

(4)

On using the ansatz

u(x)=\cos[\phi(x)/2]\,,

(5)

in Eq. (
4
) we find that in that case
u
must satisfy the nonlinear
equation essentially given by Eq. (
3
), i.e.

(1-u^{2})u_{xx}+u(u_{x})^{2}=-u(1-u^{2})^{2}\,.

(6)

Case II: Sine Hyperbolic-Gordon Equation

Consider the sine hyperbolic-Gordon equation

\phi_{xx}=\sinh[\phi(x)]\,.

(7)

On using the ansatz

u(x)=\cosh[\phi(x)/2]\,,

(8)

in Eq. (
7
) we find that in that case
u
must satisfy the same nonlinear
equation, Eq. (
6
).

Case III: Plane Pendulum Equation

On using the ansatz

u(t)=\sin[\theta(t)/2]\,,

(9)

in the plane pendulum Eq. (
2
) we find that in that case
u
must satisfy
essentially the same nonlinear Eq. (
3
), i.e. the equation

(1-u^{2})u_{tt}+u(u_{t})^{2}=-u(1-u^{2})^{2}\,.

(10)

Case IV: Inverted Plane Pendulum Equation

Consider the inverted plane pendulum equation

\theta_{tt}=+a\sin(\theta)\,,~~a=\frac{g}{l}\,.

(11)

As in the plane pendulum case, by rescaling
t\rightarrow t/\sqrt{a}
we can
always eliminate
a
from Eq. (
11
) and write the inverted plane pendulum
equation as

\theta_{tt}=\sin(\theta)\,.

(12)

Thus, once we obtain a solution
f(t)
of Eq. (
12
) then the corresponding
solution of the inverted plane pendulum Eq. (
11
) is simply
f(\sqrt{a}t)
.
It is interesting to note that the inverted plane pendulum Eq. (
12
) has
the same form as the static SG Eq. (
4
) except
t
is to be replaced
by
x
as we go from the inverted plane pendulum to the static SG equation.

On using the ansatz

u(t)=\cos[\theta(t)/2]\,,

(13)

in the inverted plane pendulum Eq. (
2
) we find that in that case
u
must
satisfy the same nonlinear equation, Eq. (
10
).

Before we discuss the solutions of Eq. (
3
), it is worth noting that
Eq. (
3
) is invariant under
(u,y)\rightarrow-(u,y)
so that if
u_{1}(y)

is a solution of Eq. (
3
) then so is the solution
-u_{1}(y)
as well as

u_{1}(-y)
.
Notice that once we have a solution
u_{1}(y)
of the nonlinear Eq. (
3
),
then the corresponding static solution of the SG Eq. (
4
) is given by

\phi_{SG}(x)=2\cos^{-1}[u_{1}(x)]\,.

(14)

On the other hand, the corresponding static solution of the SHG Eq. (
7
)
is given by

\phi_{SHG}(x)=2\cosh^{-1}[u_{1}(x)]\,.

(15)

Further, the solution of the inverted plane pendulum Eq. (
12
) is given by

\theta_{invpen}(t)=2\cos^{-1}[u_{1}(t)]\,.

(16)

Finally, the corresponding solution of the plane pendulum Eq. (
2
) is
given by

\theta_{pen}(t)=2\sin^{-1}[u_{1}(t)]\,.

(17)

We now present five solutions of Eq. (
3
) so that one has immediately five
exact solutions of the static SHG Eq. (
7
), plane pendulum Eq. (
2
)
(and hence Eq. (
1
)), inverted plane pendulum Eq. (
12
) (and hence
Eq. (
11
)) and static solutions of the SG Eq. (
4
).

Solution I

It is easy to check that Eq. (
3
) admits the periodic kink solution

[
24
]

u(y)={\rm sn}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{n}},n\right)\,,~~0<n\leq 1\,,

(18)

where
n
is the modulus of the Jacobi elliptic functions. In Appendix C we
have mentioned a few important properties of the Jacobi elliptic functions.

Solution II

Another solution to Eq. (
3
) is

u(y)=\sqrt{n}{\rm sn}(y,n)\,,~~0<n\leq 1\,.

(19)

Solution III

In the limit
n=1
, both the solutions I and II go over to the hyperbolic
kink solution

u(y)=\tanh(y)\,,

(20)

which is the famous “sticking” solution of the plane pendulum equation

[
1
,
3
]
.

Solution IV

Eq. (
3
) also admits the solution

u(y)=\frac{\sqrt{n}{\rm cn}(y,n)}{{\rm dn}(y,n)}\,,~~0<n<1\,.

(21)

Solution V

Remarkably, Eq. (
3
) also admits a complex PT-invariant kink solution with
PT-eigenvalue
-1

u(y)=\tanh(\beta y)+i{\rm sech}(\beta y)\,,

(22)

provided
\beta=2
.

2.2
Connection Between the Solutions of the Plane Pendulum with
those of the Inverted Plane Pendulum, the Hyperbolic Plane Pendulum and the
Static Solutions of the SG Equation

We now show that the exact solutions of the plane pendulum Eq. (
2
), the
inverted plane pendulum Eq. (
12
), the hyperbolic plane pendulum (see
below) and the static solutions of the SG Eq. (
4
) are all intimately
related to the solution of the nonlinear equation

(1-v^{2})v_{yy}+v(v_{y})^{2}=v(1-v^{2})^{2}\,,

(23)

where
y
is
x
or
t
depending on the equation concerned.
The proof is straightforward. We discuss the four cases one by one.

Case I: Hyperbolic Plane Pendulum Equation

Consider the hyperbolic plane pendulum equation

\theta_{tt}=-a\sinh(\theta)\,,~~a=\frac{g}{l}\,.

(24)

By rescaling
t\rightarrow t/\sqrt{a}
we can always eliminate
a
from
Eq. (
24
) and write the hyperbolic plane pendulum equation as

\theta_{tt}=-\sinh(\theta)\,.

(25)

Thus, once we obtain a solution
f(t)
of Eq. (
25
) then the corresponding
solution of the hyperbolic plane pendulum Eq. (
24
) is simply

f(\sqrt{a}t)
.

On using the ansatz

v(t)=\cosh[\theta(t)/2]\,,

(26)

in Eq. (

must satisfy essentially the
same nonlinear Eq. (
23
), i.e.

(1-v^{2})v_{tt}+v(v_{t})^{2}=v(1-v^{2})^{2}\,.

(27)

Case II: Sine-Gordon Equation

Consider the sine-Gordon Eq. (
4
). On using the ansatz

v(x)=\sin[\phi(x)/2]\,,

(28)

in Eq. (
4
) we find that in that case
v
must satisfy the nonlinear
equation essentially given by Eq. (
23
), i.e.

(1-v^{2})v_{xx}+v(v_{x})^{2}=v(1-v^{2})^{2}\,.

(29)

Case III: Inverted Pendulum Equation

Consider the inverted plane pendulum Eq. (
12
). On using the ansatz

v(t)=\sin[\theta(t)/2]\,,

(30)

in Eq. (
12
) we find that in that case
v
must satisfy the same nonlinear
Eq. (
27
).

Case IV: Plane Pendulum Equation

On using the ansatz

v(t)=\cos[\theta(t)/2]\,,

(31)

in the plane pendulum Eq. (
2
) we find that in that case
v
must satisfy
the same nonlinear Eq. (
27
).
Before we discuss the solutions of Eq. (
23
), it is worth noting that
Eq. (
23
) is invariant under
(v,y)\rightarrow-(v,y)
so that if

v_{1}(y)
is a solution of Eq. (
23
) then so is the solution
-v_{1}(y)
as
well as
v_{1}(-y)
.

Thus once we have a solution
v_{1}(y)
of the nonlinear Eq. (

),
then the corresponding static solution of the SG Eq. (

) is given by

\phi_{SG}(x)=2\sin^{-1}[v_{1}(x)]\,.

(32)

On the other hand, the corresponding solution of the hyperbolic pendulum
Eq. (
25
) is given by

\theta_{hpen}(t)=2\cosh^{-1}[v_{1}(t)]\,.

(33)

Further, the corresponding solution of the inverted plane pendulum
Eq. (
12
) is given by

\theta_{invpen}(t)=2\sin^{-1}[v_{1}(t)]\,.

(34)

Finally, the corresponding solution of the plane pendulum Eq. (
2
) is
given by

\theta_{Pen}(t)=2\cos^{-1}[v_{1}(t]\,.

(35)

We now present four solutions of Eq. (
23
) so that one has immediately
four exact solutions of the hyperbolic plane pendulum Eq. (
25
) (and hence
Eq. (
24
)), the plane pendulum Eq. (
2
) (and hence Eq. (
1
)), the
inverted plane pendulum Eq. (
12
) (and hence Eq. (
11
)) and the

Solution I

It is easy to check that Eq. (
23
) admits the solution

u(y)={\rm cn}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{n}},n\right)\,.

Solution II

Another solution to Eq. (
23

u(y)={\rm dn}(y,n)\,.

(37)

Solution III

In the limit
n=1
, both the solutions go over to the hyperbolic solution

u(y)={\rm sech}(y)\,.

(38)

Solution IV

Eq. (
23
) also admits the solution

u(y)=\frac{\sqrt{1-n}}{{\rm dn}(y,n)}\,,~~0<n<1\,.

(39)

Unfortunately, with the second ansatz, we have not been able to obtain an

)
using the

2.3
Few More Exact Solutions of the Plane Pendulum Equation

We now obtain a few more exact solutions of the plane pendulum Eq. (
1
) by
using the ansatz

\tan[\phi(t)/4]=w\,.

(40)

It turns out that these are new solutions distinct from those presented in
Secs. IIa and IIb above.
On using the ansatz in the pendulum Eq. (
1
), one finds that
w
must
satisfy the nonlinear equation

(1+w^{2})w_{tt}-2w(w_{t})^{2}=-aw(1-w^{2})\,,~~a>0\,.

(41)

Before we discuss the solutions of Eq. (
41
), it is worth noting that
Eq. (
41
) is invariant under
(w,t)\rightarrow-(w,t)
so that if

w_{1}(t)
is a solution of Eq. (
41
) then so is the solution
-w_{1}(t)
as
well as
w_{1}(-t)
. We now show that the nonlinear Eq. (
41
) admits the
following five exact solutions.

Solution I

It is easy to check that the nonlinear Eq. (
41
) (and hence the plane
pendulum Eq. (
1
)) admit the real periodic solution

w(t)=A{\rm sn}(\beta t,n)\,,

(42)

provided

A=\pm(n)^{1/4}\,,~~a=(1+\sqrt{n})^{2}\beta^{2}\,.

(43)

Note that this solution holds good in case
0<n\leq 1
. In particular, in
case
n=1
we obtain the hyperbolic solution

w(t)=A\tanh(\beta t)\,,

(44)

provided

A=1\,,~~a=4\beta^{2}\,.

(45)

Solution II

Eq. (
41
) also admits an imaginary solution

w(t)=iA{\rm sn}(\beta t,n)\,,

(46)

provided

A=\pm(n)^{1/4}\,,~~a=(1-\sqrt{n})^{2}\beta^{2}\,.

(47)

Note that this solution holds good for
0<n<1
.

Solution III

Even though neither
{\rm cn}(t,n)
nor i
{\rm cn}(t,n)
is a solution of Eq. (
41
), it
turns out that Eq. (
41
) admits the complex superposed PT-invariant
solution with PT-eigenvalue
-1

w(t)=A[{\rm sn}(\beta t,n)\pm i{\rm cn}(\beta t,n)]\,,

(48)

provided

A^{2}=1\,,~~a=\beta^{2}\,.

(49)

Solution IV

Even though neither
{\rm cn}(t,n)
nor i
{\rm cn}(t,n)
is a solution of Eq. (
41
),
it turns out that Eq. (
41
) also admits the complex superposed
PT-invariant solution with PT-eigenvalue
+1

w(t)=A[{\rm cn}(\beta t,n)\pm i{\rm sn}(\beta t,n)]\,,

(50)

provided

A^{2}=1\,,~~a=(1-n)\beta^{2}\,.

(51)

Solution V

Remarkably, even though neither
{\rm dn}(\beta t,m)
nor
i{\rm dn}(\beta t,m)

is a solution of Eq. (
41
), it turns out that Eq. (
41
) admits the
complex PT-invariant periodic superposed solution with PT-eigenvalue
-1

w(t)=A[\sqrt{n}{\rm sn}(\beta t,n)\pm i{\rm dn}(\beta t,n)]\,,

(52)

provided

A^{2}=1\,,~~a=n\beta^{2}\,.

(53)

Note that the solution holds good only if
0<n\leq 1
. In particular, in the
limit
n=1
, both solutions III and V go over to the complex PT-invariant
(hyperbolic) kink solution with PT-eigenvalue
-1

w(t)=A[\tanh(\beta t)\pm i{\rm sech}(\beta t)]\,,

(54)

provided

A^{2}=1\,,~~a=\beta^{2}\,.

(55)

It is easy to check that these five solutions are distinct from the solutions
of the pendulum Eq. (
1
) presented in Secs. IIa and IIb.

3
Periodic and Hyperbolic Solutions of the Plane Pendulum
Equation in the First Anharmonic Approximation

We shall now present several periodic as well as hyperbolic solutions of the
plane pendulum in the first anharmonic approximation. In the case of the
periodic solutions we also explicitly mention the corresponding period.

We start from the plane pendulum Eq. (
1
). As is well known, in the
harmonic approximation, i.e. the case of small
\theta
, the pendulum
Eq. (
1
) is given by

\theta_{tt}=-a\theta\,,~~a=\frac{g}{l}\,,

(56)

whose solution is

\theta(t)=A\sin(\sqrt{a}t)\,,

(57)

with period
T=2\pi\sqrt{{l}/{g}}
. As is well known, for this solution
the time period is independent of the amplitude, which is the best well
known example of an isochronous system.

In the first anharmonic approximation, we have
\sin(\theta)=\theta-\frac{\theta^{3}}{6}+...
, so that Eq. (
1
) takes the form

\theta_{tt}=-a\theta+\frac{a\theta^{3}}{6}-...\,.

(58)

We shall now obtain several exact solutions of the pendulum Eq. (
58
).
It is worth pointing out that Eq. (
58
) is analogous to the symmetric

\phi^{4}
equation in the case of the static solutions

\phi_{xx}=px+qx^{3}\,,

(59)

for which a large number of solutions are already known. Thus using the analogy
and replacing
x
with
t
, we can immediately write down a large number of
exact periodic and hyperbolic solutions of the pendulum Eq. (
58
). On
comparing Eqs. (
58
) and (
59
), it is clear that in the pendulum
case
p=-a<0\,,~~q=\frac{a}{6}>0
. Note that
a=\frac{g}{l}
. Using
this
\phi^{4}
analogy we now present 12 solutions of Eq. (
58
). For
clarity we shall first present solutions of a more general equation

\theta_{tt}=-a\theta+b\theta^{3}\,,~~a,b>0\,,

(60)

and then find out the conditions under which these are solutions of the
plane pendulum Eq. (
58
), i.e. when
b=a/6
.

Solution I

Eq. (
60
) admits
[
25
,
26
]
the periodic kink solution

\theta(t)=A\sqrt{n}{\rm sn}(\beta t,n)\,,

(61)

provided

bA^{2}=2\beta^{2}\,,~~a=(1+n)\beta^{2}\,.

(62)

Thus, it is a solution of the plane pendulum Eq. (
58
) in case

A=\frac{2\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{1+n}}\,,

(63)

and the corresponding time period is

T(n)=4K(n)\sqrt{\frac{12}{aA^{2}}}\,,

(64)

where
K(n)
is the complete elliptic integral of the first kind
[
24
]
.
Note that since
a=g/l
, hence dimensionally we get the correct equation for
the time period and one can see how far different it is from the lowest order
expression. Further, note that unlike the harmonic approximation case, now
the time period depends on the amplitude (i.e.
T\propto A^{-1}
), i.e. it is
not an isochronous system in the first anharmonic approximation. It turns out
that this is the case for all the periodic solutions of the plane pendulum.
Thus, while all of them have correct dimensional dependence but the time period

T\propto A^{-1}
for all the solutions mentioned below. We will therefore
not mention it again while discussing other periodic solutions of the
pendulum Eq. (
58
).

Solution II

In the limit

4
Elliptic Pendulum

It is worth asking if one can generalize the plane pendulum Eq. (
1
) and
introduce a one-parameter family of so called “elliptic pendulum” equations
which will reduce to the plane pendulum Eq. (
1
) in one limit and
hyperbolic pendulum Eq. (
24
) in the other limit. The answer to the
question is in the affirmative. In particular, consider the elliptic plane
pendulum equation

\theta_{tt}=-a\frac{{\rm sn}(\theta,m)}{{\rm dn}(\theta,m)}\,,

(108)

where
m
is the modulus parameter
0\leq m\leq 1

[
24
]
. On using the fact
that

\displaystyle{\rm sn}(\theta,m=0)=\sin(\theta)\,,~~{\rm dn}(\theta,m=0)=1\,,~~{\rm sn}(\theta,m=1)=\tanh(\theta)\,,

\displaystyle{\rm dn}(\theta,m=1)={\rm sech}(\theta)\,,

(109)

it immediately follows that while in the
m=0
limit, the elliptic pendulum
Eq. (
108
) reduces to the plane pendulum Eq. (
1
), in the
m=1

limit, it reduces to the hyperbolic pendulum Eq. (
24
). So far we have not
been able to obtain exact analytical solutions of the elliptic pendulum
Eq. (
108
). However, it may be of interest to obtain solutions of the
elliptic pendulum in the harmonic and the first anharmonic approximation.

4.1
Periodic as well as Hyperbolic Solutions of the Elliptic Plane
Pendulum Equation in the Harmonic and the First Anharmonic Approximation

We start from the elliptic plane pendulum Eq. (
108
). As is well known, in
the case of small
\theta,{\rm sn}(\theta,m)
and
{\rm dn}(\theta,m)
to

[

On substituting Eq. (

From here we find that no matter what

A

5
Summary and Open Problems

In this paper we have pointed out a novel connection between the solutions of
the plane pendulum problem and several different models. In particular, we have
shown that the solutions of the plane pendulum, the inverted plane pendulum
and the static solutions of the SG and the SHG equations are all related to the
solutions of the nonlinear Eq. (
3
). We have also shown that the solutions
of the plane pendulum, the inverted plane pendulum, the hyperbolic plane
pendulum and the static solutions of the SG equation are related to the
solutions of the nonlinear Eq. (
23
). Besides, we have obtained several
exact periodic and hyperbolic solutions of the plane pendulum equation in the
first anharmonic approximation. Finally, in Sec. IV we have introduced one
continuous-parameter family of elliptic potentials which smoothly go over to
the plane pendulum in the limit of the modulus parameter
m
going to zero and
to the hyperbolic plane pendulum in the limit of
m=1
and further shown that
in the harmonic approximation, the continuous parameter family of the elliptic
plane pendulums (i.e.
0\leq m\leq 1
) are examples of an isochronous system.
Further, we have shown that for the special case of the modulus parameter

m=1/2
, the elliptic pendulum is an isochronous system even up to the first
anharmonic approximation (i.e., to
O(\theta^{3})
). In
Appendix B we have also obtained exact solutions of the hyperbolic plane
pendulum equation in the harmonic as well as the first anharmonic approximation.
This paper raises several open questions some of which are the following.

1.

In Sec. IIa we have obtained five exact solutions of the nonlinear
Eq. (
3
). It is hard to believe that there are no other exact solutions
of this nonlinear equation. Clearly, finding even one more solution of
Eq. (
3
) would give us an additional exact solution of four different
models, i.e. static solution of the SG and the SHG as well as (time dependent)
solutions of the inverted plane pendulum and the plane pendulum equations.

2.

In Sec. IIb we have obtained four exact solutions of the nonlinear
Eq. (
23
). Again, it is hard to believe that there are no other exact
solutions of this nonlinear equation. Clearly, finding even one more solution
of Eq. (
23
) would give us an additional exact solution of four different
models, i.e. static solution of the SG as well as (time dependent) solutions of
the inverted, the hyperbolic and the plane pendulum equations.

3.

Turning around, if one can find even one new solution of any of the above
models, that in turn will also give us one extra solution to the three other
interesting models. In particular, the SG equation has been examined for more
than six decades now and it is hard to believe that it only admits five static
solutions. Knowing one more new static solution of the SG equation will give us
a new solution of the plane, the hyperbolic and the inverted pendulum equations
as well as a static solution of the SHG equation.

4.

In Sec. IIc we have obtained five new solutions of the nonlinear
Eq. (
41
) and hence the plane pendulum equation (
1
). Again it is
hard to believe that the nonlinear Eq. (
41
) only admits five solutions
and it is worth trying to obtain more exact solutions of the nonlinear
Eq. (
41
).

5.

In Secs. III and Appendix A we have obtained several new solutions of the
plane pendulum equation in the first and the second anharmonic approximation
respectively. It would be interesting if one can find physical significance of
some of these solutions.

6.

In Sec. IV we have introduced a one-parameter family of elliptic
potentials and have unearthed some novel features of these elliptic pendulums.
Clearly, such an elliptic pendulum equation is not unique and one can come up
with some other elliptic pendulum equation, one example being the one suggested
by Bender et al.
[
31
]
. It would be worthwhile examining the various
possible choices and look for their unique features. In particular, can one
think of an elliptic pendulum equation which at least for some modulus
parameter
0\leq m\leq 1
is isochronous up to second or even higher order
anharmonic approximation?

Appendix A: Solutions of the Plane Pendulum Equation in the Second
Anharmonic Approximation

We shall now present solutions of the plane pendulum equation in the second
anharmonic approximation. As we show below, since the coefficients of
\theta
,

\theta^{3}
as well as
\theta^{5}
are fixed and related to each other, this
severely restricts the number of possible solutions.

In the second anharmonic approximation, we have
\sin(\theta)=\theta-\frac{\theta^{3}}{6}+\frac{\theta^{5}}{120}+...
, so that in this approximation
the plane pendulum Eq. (
1
) takes the form

\theta_{tt}=-a\theta+\frac{a\theta^{3}}{6}-\frac{a\theta^{5}}{120}\,.

(119)

This equation is of the form

\theta_{tt}=-a\theta+b\theta^{3}-d\theta^{5}\,,~~a,b,d>0\,,

(120)

where

b=\frac{a}{6}\,,~~\frac{b^{2}}{ad}=\frac{10}{3}\,.

(121)

This restriction of
a,b,d>0
and
b^{2}/ad=10/3
rules out most of the
allowed solutions of Eq. (
120
). In fact we were able to obtain only
two solutions of Eq. (
120
) with these restrictions which we now discuss
one by one.

Solution I

It is easy to check that

\theta(t)=A\sqrt{\frac{[1-{\rm dn}(\beta t,n)]}{D+{\rm dn}(\beta t,n)}}\,,~~D>0\,,

(122)

is an exact solution of Eq. (
120
) provided

\displaystyle(D-1)a=[(4+n)D+4-5n]\beta^{2}\,,

\displaystyle(D-1)bA^{2}=[D(2D-n)-2(1-n)]\beta^{2}\,,

\displaystyle(D-1)dA^{4}=\frac{3}{4}(D-1)[D^{2}+(1-n)]\beta^{2}\,.

(123)

Since for our case
a,b,d>0
, this implies that such a solution is possible
only if
D>1
. On demanding the restriction
{b^{2}}/{ad}={10}/{3}
,
we obtain a quartic equation for
D
in terms of
m

\displaystyle(12-5n)D^{4}-2nD^{3}+(13n^{2}+54n-64)D^{2}+62n(1-n)D

\displaystyle+(52-57n)(1-n)=0\,.

(124)

We have explicitly checked that for
D\geq 3
there is no acceptable solution
to this equation while there are acceptable solutions in case
1<D\leq 2
.
Thus one obtains a continuous-parameter family of solutions. On demanding

b=a/6
, we find that
A^{2}
is fixed in terms of
D
and
m
, and is given by

A^{2}=\frac{6[2D^{2}-Dn-2(1-n)]}{(4+n)D+4-5n}\,.

(125)

As an illustration, when
D=2
we find that the nonlinear Eq. (
6
) is
satisfied if
n=\frac{27-\sqrt{681}}{6}
which is approximately
n=1/6
.
And in this case
A^{2}=3.17
.

Solution II

It is easy to check that

\theta(t)=A\sqrt{\frac{[1-{\rm cn}(\beta t,n)]}{D+{\rm cn}(\beta t,n)}}\,,~~D>1\,,

(126)

is an exact solution of Eq. (
120
) provided

\displaystyle(D+1)a=[(4n+1)D+4n-5]\beta^{2}\,,

\displaystyle(D+1)bA^{2}=[D(2nD-1)+2(1-n)]\beta^{2}\,,

\displaystyle(D+1)dA^{4}=\frac{3}{4}(D-1)[nD^{2}+(1-n)]\beta^{2}\,.

(127)

On demanding the restriction
{b^{2}}/{ad}={10}/{3}
, we obtain a
quartic equation for
D
in terms of
m

(12n^{2}-5n)D^{4}-2nD^{3}+(8+4n-24n^{2})D^{2}-2(1-n)D+(7-12n)(1-n)=0\,.

(128)

We have explicitly checked that for
D\leq 3
there is no acceptable solution
to this equation while there are acceptable solutions in case
D\geq 4
.
Thus one obtains a continuous-parameter family of solutions. On demanding

b=a/6
, we find that
A^{2}
is fixed in terms of
D
and
m
, and is given by

A^{2}=\frac{6[2nD^{2}-D+2(1-n)]}{(4+n)D+4n-5}\,.

(129)

As an illustration, when
D=2
we find that the Eq. (
128
) is
satisfied if
n=\frac{251\pm\sqrt{8137}}{1080}
which has two allowed values
of

which are approximately

, we find

is approximately
3.5
while in case
n=1/7
then one finds that

is approximately

.

Appendix C: Some Basic Results about Jacobi Elliptic Functions

In this appendix we mostly mention those properties of the Jacobi elliptic
functions which have been used in this paper. For more details about these
functions, see
[
24
]
. There are three basic Jacobi elliptic functions

{\rm sn}(x,m)
,
{\rm cn}(x,m)
and
{\rm dn}(x,m)
,
where
0\leq m\leq 1
is the modulus of the Jacobi elliptic functions. In the
special cases of
m=0
and
m=1
, these Jacobi elliptic functions go over
to the trigonometric and the hyperbolic functions, respectively. In particular,

{\rm sn}(x,m=0)=\sin(x)\,,~~{\rm cn}(x,m=0)=\cos(x)\,,~~{\rm dn}(x,m=0)=1\,,

(178)

{\rm sn}(x,m=1)=\tanh(x)\,,~~{\rm cn}(x,m=1)={\rm dn}(x,m=1)={\rm sech}(x)\,.

(179)

The three Jacobi elliptic functions satisfy the identities

{\rm sn}^{2}(x,m)+{\rm cn}^{2}(x,m)=1={\rm dn}^{2}(x,m)+m{\rm sn}^{2}(x,m)\,.

(180)

While
{\rm cn}(x,m)
and
{\rm dn}(x,m)
are even functions of
x
,
{\rm sn}(x,m)
is an
odd function of
x
, i.e.

\displaystyle{\rm sn}(-x,m)=-{\rm sn}(x,m)\,,~~{\rm cn}(-x,m)={\rm cn}(x,m)\,,

\displaystyle{\rm dn}(-x,m)={\rm dn}(x,m)\,.

(181)

Note that
{\rm sn}(x,m)
,
{\rm cn}(x,m)
, and
dn(x,m)
are all doubly periodic functions.
While the real period of
{\rm sn}(x,m)
and
{\rm cn}(x,m)
is
4K(m)
, that of
{\rm dn}(x,m)

is
2K(m)
, i.e.

\displaystyle{\rm sn}[x+4K(m),m]={\rm sn}(x,m)\,,~~{\rm cn}[x+4K(m),m]={\rm cn}(x,m)\,,

\displaystyle{\rm dn}[x+2K(m),m]={\rm dn}(x,m)\,.

(182)

Some special values of these functions are

\displaystyle{\rm sn}(x=0,m)={\rm sn}(x=2K(m),m)=0\,,

\displaystyle{\rm sn}(x=K(m),m)=-{\rm sn}(x=3K(m),m)=1\,,

(183)

\displaystyle{\rm cn}(x=0,m)=-{\rm cn}(x=2K(m),m)=1\,,

\displaystyle{\rm cn}(x=K(m),m)={\rm cn}(x=3K(m),m)=0\,,

(184)

{\rm dn}(x=0,m)=1\,,~~{\rm dn}(x=K(m),m)=\sqrt{1-m}\,.

(185)

Thus,
{\rm dn}(x,m)
is nonnegative and oscillates between
\sqrt{1-m}

and
1
. On the other hand,
{\rm sn}(x,m)
and
{\rm cn}(x,m)
go from
-1
to
1
.
On differentiating these three functions with respect to
their argument
x
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d{\rm sn}(x,m)}{dx}={\rm cn}(x,m){\rm dn}(x,m)\,,~~\frac{d{\rm cn}(x,m)}{dx}=-{\rm sn}(x,m){\rm dn}(x,m)\,,

\displaystyle\frac{d{\rm dn}(x,m)}{dx}=-m{\rm sn}(x,m){\rm cn}(x,m)\,.

(186)

The addition theorems for the three Jacobi elliptic functions are

{\rm sn}(x+y,m)=\frac{{\rm sn}(x,m){\rm cn}(y,m){\rm dn}(y,m)+{\rm sn}(y,m){\rm cn}(x,m){\rm dn}(x,m)}{1-m{\rm sn}^{2}(x,m){\rm sn}^{2}(y,m)}\,,

(187)

{\rm cn}(x+y,m)=\frac{{\rm cn}(x,m){\rm cn}(y,m)-{\rm sn}(x,m){\rm sn}(y,m){\rm dn}(x,m){\rm dn}(y,m)}{1-m{\rm sn}^{2}(x,m){\rm sn}^{2}(y,m)}\,,

(188)

{\rm dn}(x+y,m)=\frac{{\rm dn}(x,m){\rm dn}(y,m)-m{\rm sn}(x,m){\rm sn}(y,m){\rm cn}(x,m){\rm cn}(y,m)}{1-m{\rm sn}^{2}(x,m){\rm sn}^{2}(y,m)}\,.

(189)

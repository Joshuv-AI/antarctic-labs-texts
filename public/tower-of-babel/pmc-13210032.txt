Validity and reliability of the persian adapted version of the labor pain relief attitude questionnaire for pregnant women (P-LPRAQ-p)
Authors: Maryam Safarnavadeh, Leili Salehi, Marjan Havaei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Labor pain, as one of the inevitable pains among women, is generally tolerable for most women. The present study aimed to assess the cultural adaption and psychometric adequacy of the Labor Pain Relief Attitude Questionnaire (LPRAQ) for pregnant women.MATERIALS AND METHOD:This psychometric study was conducted in four distinct phases. The first and second phases involved the forward-backward translation followed by necessary modifications, and the third phase focused on the content and face validity. In the fourth phase, construct validity (CV) and reliability were examined. CV was evaluated using confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) and concurrent validity through previously validated and reliable measurement instruments. The mean LPRAQ-p scores were compared across different groups in terms of age, spouse’s age, education level, planning for pregnancy, number of pregnancies and childbirths, depression and distress, history of analgesia use, and delivery mode. A higher P-LPRAQ score indicated a greater willingness to seek labor pain relief.RESULTS:A total of 355 pregnant women who sought medical care in Tehran during 2022 were studied. During the face and content validity assessment, all six items of the instrument were retained. CFA of six items with two factors showed a good model fit index (X2/df < 5, (Comparative Fit Index) CFI = 0.91, (Normed Fit Index) NFI = 0.880.01, (Root Mean Square Error of Approximation) RMSEA = 0.04). The questionnaire also exhibited good convergent validity with (Tilburg Pregnancy Distress-negative -negative affect)TPDS-NA and Edinburg depression questionnaire (P < 0.05). Additionally, significant difference in the mean P-LPRAQ-p scores was observed in terms of the age, education level, spouse’s age, employment status, mode of delivery, depression, and distress level (P < 0.001).CONCLUSION:The P-LPRAQ is a brief, concise, and straightforward questionnaire demonstrating optimal validity and reliability, making it a suitable tool for assessing pregnant women’s attitudes toward labor pain relief.

Introduction

Pain is defined as an unpleasant sensory and emotional experience associated with actual or potential tissue damage or described in terms of such damage.[1] A multidimensional perspective on labor pain highlights the necessity of a women-centered approach to labor pain management.[2] Various factors such as emotional and cognitive processes, previous experiences, motivation, age, gender, regional and local culture, etc., contribute to the perception and expression of pain.[3] Several approaches have been developed for pain assessment, with the most widely recognized methods outlined below.

McGill method

This method differentiates the sensory, cognitive, motivational, emotional components of pain through a structured questionnaire and a numerical rating scale, allowing for a comprehensive assessment of various pain-related factors.[4].

Sense-based decision method

This method allows for the differentiation between an individual’s sensitivity to the stimulus intensity and the cognitive or emotional interpretation of that stimulus.[5]

Utilizing measurable tools for pain assessment instead of words

It is a widely used and standard method comparing individuals’ verbal reactions to pain as unquantifiable stimuli with their reactions to quantifiable stimuli. Actually, individuals are asked to report their pain intensity using a quantifiable and measurable stimulus such as the length of a line segment or a numerical value between 0 and 10.[6]

Axial pain assessment method

This method evaluates pain from a cognitive-behavioral perspective using a 61-item questionnaire developed by Turk and Rudi.[7]

Additionally, various tools are available for pain assessment, which can generally be categorized into the types of unidimensional tools measuring pain intensity and multidimensional tools assessing intensity, nature, location, and psychological impact of pain on the individuals’ mood and emotions.[8] Nowadays, pain management is classified into pharmacological and non-pharmacological methods.[91011] Labor pain, as an inevitable pain for women, is generally tolerable for most, which may become severe under certain circumstances.[12] Multiple factors influence labor pain, including cultural, social, psychological, and environmental determinants.[13]

In the same vein, Banaei Heravan reported a relationship between childbirth outcomes and women’s attitude toward labor.[14] The factors influencing childbirth and the duration of its stages can be categorized into the physiological, psychological, and social domains. The attitudes and beliefs toward the childbirth and labor pain and relief vary across different cultures and societies.[15] Socio-cultural factors, including awareness level, cultural perceptions of pain management, access to healthcare facilities and services, and socio-economic status of the patient in response to pain can lead to the different perceptions and reactions to pain.[16] Women’s attitude and understanding of labor pain significantly influence their subsequent behavior during childbirth.[17] In addition, effective labor pain management enhances maternal satisfaction and contributes to a more comfortable and positive childbirth experience.[18]

However, the lack of awareness and negative attitude of labor analgesia among pregnant women are regarded as key factors contributing to the limited use of labor analgesia techniques during childbirth.[19] Women’s attitude toward labor pain relief is influenced by cultural background, prenatal healthcare providers, and social influences such as partner, family, and friends.[20] Moreover, various factors are associated with the request for labor pain relief such as pregnancy-related depression and distress, parity, history of dystocia, severity of labor pain,[21] and previous experience with analgesia use during childbirth.[22]

The neuromatrix theory offers a framework for understanding the variability in pain perception among individuals, providing insights into the diverse experiences of pain processing.[23] A deeper understanding of women’s attitude toward labor pain relief is necessary to make informed decision about the effective labor pain management strategies, especially during the prenatal period.[20] However, the knowledge and attitude toward labor pain and its relief among pregnant women, especially in developing countries, remain inadequately explored. Notably, no study was found regarding Persian pregnant women’s knowledge and attitude toward labor pain relief, highlighting the necessity of research in this area.

Obviously, it is essential to have access to a valid, reliable, comprehensive, and concise instrument to conduct a study in this field. The Labor Pain Relief Attitude Questionnaire (LPRA), developed by Hulsbosch et al. (2020)[24] and psychometrically evaluated in Netherlands, considers both personal and social factors influencing labor pain perception. Given the lack of a validated questionnaire in Iran, the present study aimed to assess the validity and reliability of the LPRAQ-P among Persian pregnant women and identify the factors influencing the attitude toward labor pain relief using regression analysis.

Materials and Method

Study design and setting

The present study was performed in four distinct phases. The first and second phases involved the translation and modification of the LPRAQ-p. The third phase involved the evaluation of content validity and face validity, while the fourth phase focused on CV and reliability [Figure 1].

The study phases

Phase 1

Translation

The original version of the LPRAQ-p was translated into Persian after obtaining written permission from original developer, using the modified Brislin’s translation model.[25] Initially, two independent English-to-Persian translator separately translated the LPRAQ-p into Persian. Subsequently, a third translator synthesized these two translations into a single unified version. Next, two native English-speaking translators back-translated the finalized Persian version into English to ensure conceptual and linguistic equivalence. Finally, a copy of the back-translations was compared with the original English version and any discrepancies were identified and addressed through corrections, if necessary.

Phase 2

In the second phase, two expert panels (four reproductive health specialists and six obstetrics and gynecologist) reviewed the Persian adapted version of LPRAQ-p. Some items were modified to align with the Persian cultural considerations [Table 1].

Content and face validity

A group of specialists in reproductive health and obstetrics and gynecologist (n = 10) evaluated the questionnaire for qualitative validity, while face validity was assessed based on the views of 20 Iranian pregnant women. According to the experts’ evaluations, content validity index (CVI) and content validity ratio (CVR) were computed for content validity and the impact score for face validity. All six items were retained for further psychometric evaluation. The questionnaire is scored on a 5-point Likert scale, as higher scores indicate greater willingness to request pain relief medication during labor.

Phase 3

Study participants and sampling

During the third phase, pregnant women with a gestational age of 32 weeks and speaking Persian language completed the P-LPRAQ-p to evaluate the construct validity (CV) and the reliability of the questionnaire. Women who were illiterate excluded from the study. Based on the Lee and Comrey’s classification for factor analysis,[26] the sample size was calculated to be 350 pregnant women (15 participants were excluded due to the incomplete questionnaire, resulting in 95.71% response rate), recruited from the prenatal clinic in Akbarabadi hospital, a referral hospital in Tehran, capital of Iran (with more than 12 million population). Regarding 5% attrition, the final sample size was determined as 345.

Reliability

The test-retest and Cronbach’s alpha coefficient were used to assess reliability. A minimum sample size of 30 subjects was required to complete the questionnaire twice at two-week interval. A Cronbach’s alpha of 0.7 was considered as the acceptable threshold for internal consistency,[26] while a test-retest coefficient of 0.8 was considered good.

CV was assessed by administering the questionnaire to 350 pregnant women. Model fit was evaluated using the Chi-Squared test (χ2), χ2/df, Incremental Fit Index (IFI), Comparative Fit Index (CFI), Normed Fit Index (NFI), and Root Mean Square Error of Approximation (RMSEA). CFA was conducted on 335 pregnant women recruited from the prenatal clinic. A total of 500 eligible pregnant women were invited to participate in the study, out of whom 350 were selected through the convenience sampling method. The entire sampling process lasted for two months (January 2022–March, 2022). The inclusion criteria were gestational age of ≥32 weeks, literacy, and fluency in Persian, and receiving free health services at the prenatal clinic of the hospital.

Data collection tools and technique

In addition to the Persian version of the LPRAQ-p, the Persian version of the TPDS, and the Persian-adapted version of the Edinburg depression scale were employed.

Persian version of the TPDS

The TPSD consists of 16 items, categorized into the subscales of Negative effect (NA) and Partner Involvement (PI). The NA subscale composed of 11 items (3, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, and 16) with a score rage of 0 to 33. The PI subscale consists of five items (1, 2, 4, 8, and 15) with a score range between 0 and 15. Each item is rated on a 4-point Likert scale, ranging from 0 (very often) to 3 (rarely or never). Items 3, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, and 16 are reverse-scored. The total score ranges between 0 and 48[27] and the psychometric properties of the TPSD have been evaluated among Iranian pregnant women.[28]

The Edinburg depression scale

The Edinburg depression scale measures postnatal depression, which consists of 10 items rated on a 4-point Likert scale and total score is computed by summing all item scores. A cut-off points of 10 is applied to identify depression.[29] This scale has been validated in Iran.[30]

Concurrent validity

Concurrent validity was assessed using the Persian version of the TPDS and Edinburg Depression Scale (EDS).

Data analysis techniques

The statistical methods of t-test and ANOVA were used to compare the mean score of the P-LPRAQ-p among multiple groups or two groups, respectively. Multiple regression analysis was conducted to identify the predictors of the P-LPRAQ-p among pregnant women. The Persian version of the LPRAQ-p was regarded as the dependent variable and the age, education, spouse’s age, employment, mode of delivery, depression, and distress were considered as independent variables. The Pearson correlation was employed to examine the correlation among P-LPRAQ-p, Edinburgh Depression Scale (EDS), and Negative Affect subscale of the Tilburg Pregnancy Distress Scale (TPDS-NA). The data were analyzed using SPSS version 19 and CFA was performed using Lisrel version 8.8.

Ethical consideration

This study was approved by the research deputy of the Alborz university of medical sciences (ID project: 99-4587), and ethical committee (Ethical no. IR. ABZUMS.1400.253). The study objectives and methods were explained to the pregnant women before the study began. All research procedures followed the principles of the Helsinki declaration and women signed written informed consent.

Face validity

During the qualitative assessment of face validity, minor modifications were made for clarity based on the feedback received from pregnant women. Regarding quantitative content validity, ten experts provided minor corrective feedback, incorporated into the questionnaire. All items had an impact score greater than 1.5.

Content validity

The quantitative results were examined for content validity using CVR based on the Lawshe Table, with a threshold of 0.62, and no items were removed. Similarly, based on the CVI, and with a threshold of 0.79, no items were eliminated.

Construct validity

Confirmatory factor analysis (CFA)

Based on the results of the present study, Persian-adapted version of LPRAQ-p demonstrated an acceptable model fix index (X2 = 437.11, DF = 91, X2/df, CFI = 0.91, NFI = 0.880.01, and RMSEA = 0.04), based on the acceptable model fit criteria (X2/df < 5; CFI, GFI, and NFI > 0.90, RMSEA < 0.05).

The two-factor six-item questionnaire was validated among Persian pregnant women. Among two factors, perception had a greater impact on attitude compared to the environment. The item three (Pain relief will help me perform much better during labor) in the first subscale and item six (My mother plays an important role in making decision to ask for pain relief during labor) in the second subscale were significant [Figure 2].

Discussion

The present study aimed to evaluate the Psychometric adequacy of the Persian version of the Labor pain relief attitude questionnaire (LPRAQ-p) among pregnant women using CFA. Initially, some modifications were made to the questionnaire items to ensure cultural relevance. In Persian culture, a woman’s closest and most trusted family member with whom she feels comfortable is her mother, who typically accompanies her during childbirth. Given that having a child without formal marriage is neither customary nor socially accepted in Persian culture and other Muslim countries,[31] the word “sexual partner” was changed to “husband”.

Based on the experts’ recommendations, modifications were made in the original items to align with the Persian cultural norms. The study results indicated that all model fit indices were acceptable[30] and among the two factors influencing attitude toward labor pain relief, perception had a greater impact compared to the environment. Based on the present study results, the most influential factor in the first subscale (Women’s Perception) was the item “I am convinced that if I get pain relief, I will feel much more self-confident during labor” is the more important factor in the first subscale. Further, pain relief could prevent a loss of control during labor.[3233]

The study results showed that all model fit indices were acceptable,[34] and among the two factors influencing attitude toward labor pain relief, perception plays a more significant role compared to environment.

Attitudes and perceptions are closely interconnected[35] and pain-related attitudes are significantly associated with pain behaviors.[36] Additionally, women’s attitudes toward childbirth may influence labor management and delivery outcomes.[37]

The present study results demonstrated that the item “Pain relief will help me perform much better during labor” was the second most important item in the first subscale (Women’s Perception), which is consistent with the findings of original study.[24] Moreover, self-efficacy has been identified as a key factor labor pain relief.[35] In the same vein, Smith et al. reported that labor pain relief increases self-confidence and reduces stress.[36]

In the second subscale, the item “My husband plays an important role in the decision to ask for pain relief during labor” had the greatest impact on the attitude toward labor pain relief.

A qualitative study conducted in Ahvaz, Iran, found that the emotional support of the spouse significantly contributes to maternal satisfaction and improves the quality of maternity care services.[38]

Similarly, another study in Isfahan, Iran, revealed that the physicians, spouses, and parents are the most influential social norms affecting the attitudes of primiparous pregnant women attending childbirth preparation classes.[39]

The results of the present study illustrated that education is a key predictor of the P- LPRAQ-p and indicated a relationship between attitude toward pain relief and educational level, which is consistent with the study results of Alshahrani, demonstrated a relationship between knowledge and pain relief.[39] In line with the findings of another study,[40] the present study results showed a significant relationship between spouse’s education level and the P- LPRAQ-p.

In addition, another study identified social support as a crucial factor influencing labor pain.[41] A quasi-experimental study conducted in Indonesia reported that improving husband’s knowledge and skills in supporting childbirth increases self-efficacy in multiple areas, including labor management and its duration, pain management, and breastfeeding.[40]

Based on the results of the present study, a significant difference was observed between women with and without a history of analgesic use, which is in line with the study results of Packiasabapathy et al. (2018).[42]

The findings of the present study suggested that depression is one of the predictors of the P- LPRAQ-p. In another study, maternal health status is identified as a key factor influencing the labor pain management.[24]

Furthermore, no significant difference was found between two different childbirth groups regarding postpartum depression, which can be attributed to the difference in the demographic characteristics.[43] For instance, in Darvishi’s study, the average age of subjects was approximately 24 years and only 13% had university education. However, the average age was 30 years and nearly 50% had university education, in the present study. Consistent with the findings of a previous study,[24] the present study found significant differences in attitudes toward labor pain relief between depressed and non-depressed women, as well as between women with and without elevated distress regarding P- LPRAQ-p. In addition, another study reported that depressive symptomatology, trait anxiety, and pain magnification are associated with perceived stress during labor.[44] Age was also identified as a predictor of the P- LPRAQ-p in the present study, which is consistent with findings of another study.[45]

Based on the present study results, the P- LPRAQ-p is a brief, simple, and reliable instrument, with optimal validity and reliability, which can be used to evaluate the attitude toward labor pain relief. It is recommended that all stakeholders involved in the pregnant women’s education and counseling use this questionnaire to assess attitude toward labor pain relief.

Limitations and recommendations

One of the strengths of this study is the introduction of the psychometric tool in the Persian cultural context. However, a key limitation is the lack of similar studies for comparison, which led the researcher to compare the average score of the tool across different groups and conduct regression analysis, following the approach of the original developers. Another limitation is that the study was conducted at only one referral hospital in Tehran, which may influence the generalizability of the results.

Based on the findings and limitations of the present study, several avenues for future research are suggested, including further validation of the LPRAQ-p across different populations, longitudinal studies to assess changes in attitudes over time, or qualitative research to examine cultural factors influencing labor pain relief attitudes more deeply.

Conclusion

Based on the findings of this study, the psychometric properties of the two-factor LPRAQ were confirmed for Persian women. This instrument demonstrated a good convergence with the Tilburg Pregnancy Distress Scale and the Edinburgh Postpartum Depression Questionnaire, reinforcing its applicability within the Persian cultural and contextual framework. Additionally, this tool can be effectively utilized to evaluate the attitude toward labor pain relief and develop appropriate educational and therapeutic interventions to reduce cesarean section rates.

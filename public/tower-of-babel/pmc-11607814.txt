Replacement of a single-tooth implant restoration with the abutment dual-scanning technique: a case report
Authors: Fatmanur Demir Boz, Kıvanç Akça
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDigital technologies have not only revolutionized workflow strategies for implant- supported fixed prostheses but also changed preferences in the choice of restoration retention. Owing to the effortlessness of developing workflows with digitalization, screw-retention has become favored for initial prostheses. However, cement retention is still indicated for cases in which abutment reuse is needed for replacing failing restorations with technical complications.Case presentationIn this case report, a modified digital workflow is described for treating an impaired occlusal relationship. A dual-scan approach for an abutment-level digital implant impression to remake a cement retained implant supported fixed restoration is defined in detail.ConclusionClinicians can highly benefit from the presented strategy, which offers an efficient digital workflow to simplify cement-retained abutment level impressions through the use of an intraoral scanner. Exchanging intra- and extra-orally surface scanned abutment data may become an option in complete digital model-free workflows for addressing problems related to cement-retained implant supported fixed prostheses.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12903-024-05271-w.Keywords: Digital workflow, Intraoral scanner, Implant abutment, Technical complication

Introduction

Evidence-based dental practice has encouraged clinicians to use dental implants in the primary treatment of partial edentulism. However, even in situations in which optimal biological implant stability is achieved, hardware complications, both mechanical and technical, are inevitable [1].

Complications involving laboratory-produced components are defined as technical and commonly manifest as fracture or chipping [2]. The estimated event rates considerably increase with longer exposure periods [3]. Currently, it is unsurprising to expect a dramatic increase in technical complications when various material and workflow options are considered.

Technical complications are mostly perceived as those that affect survival of the restoration with or without the need for modification, but that also interfere with soft tissue health, chewing function and aesthetic outcomes. In such circumstances, replacement of the restoration should be performed as a routine in maintenance care for the implant treatment [4]. Remedial intervention is straightforward when the implant system is identified and the relevant components are available. Clinicians can simply either renew or remake the restoration based on the decision made with respect to the mechanical conditions of the manufacturer-fabricated elements and the preferred workflow.

However, resolution of the complication can be challenging when the appropriate impression components are missing. Direct transfer of the abutment may be considered if the failing restoration has already been retained with cement. In this context, intraoral abutment scanning remains hypothetical owing to difficulties in accurately scanning geometric monochrome structures coupled with the need for peri-implant soft tissue management need [5–7].

Digitalization may offer reliable options with the help of data management in the design software [5, 8–10]. This case report describes a modified digital workflow for remaking a cement-retained failing implant fixed restoration with an abutment dual-scanning approach with an intraoral scanner.

Case report

A medically healthy, nonsmoking 42 years old female patient, participated in this case study. Major complaints related to a single-tooth implant crown restoration included food impaction, surface roughness and frequent decementation, while minor complaints included hypersensitivity and discoloration of the neighboring teeth. Clinical and radiographic evaluations confirmed healthy soft and hard tissues around the implant 17 years in-situ and teeth with fillings and gingival recessions [11, 12]. The treatment plan agreed upon by the patient (following the acquisition of informed consent) involved remaking the single-tooth implant crown at tooth #46 and restore teeth # 44, 45 & 47 with crowns.

Because the original, non-original or generic scan body for the Frialit-Xive implant (Friadent GmbH, Mannheim, Germany) was unavailable, a dual-scan procedure was employed with an intraoral scanner to digitally capture the cement-retained abutment and align it with the digital workflow.

Following removal of the implant crown, the geometry of the abutment was evaluated in terms of retention, resistance and support, and margin location with regards to peri-implant soft tissue as well. Then the abutment was unscrewed using the appropriate driver and hand rachet.

Then, the prepared teeth and implant abutment were scanned without cord application following the regular arch scan strategy by using an intraoral scanner (TRIOS3 wireless with software release 22.1; 3Shape A/S, Copenhagen, Denmark), followed by an antagonist jaw. To improve the abutment scanning data particularly in the cervical region and to accurately transfer the abutment margin, the abutment was removed, connected to the implant analog, and then secured into an analog holder (046.239, Straumann Holding AG, Basel, Switzerland) for extraoral scanning. An uninterrupted 360° circular scan was conducted with the intraoral scanner starting from the analog moving slightly in an arch toward the abutment and carefully ending with the top. Scan accuracy was determined visually in monochrome view with particular attention to ensuring continuous stitching of the captured images without hole and double surface formation. Furthermore, the scan texture and the abutment geometry were verified in terms of smoothness and deformation-free, respectively.

The scans were shared with the dental lab through a data sharing portal (3Shape Communicate; 3Shape A/S) for processing in dental CAD software (Dental System v.2020; 3Shape A/S). After data preparation in the dental CAD software, the extraorally scanned abutment file was imported into the system and aligned with the arch scan using the multidie function. (Fig. 2) After sufficient superimposition was verified, full anatomical crowns were designed with the software tools and settings normally used for cemented tooth restorations.

Discussion

Replacement of surface scan data with the best fit algorithm for CAD/CAM customized cement retained abutment has previously been methodologically proposed [5], experimentally tested [9] and clinically validated [10]. The procedure essentially consists of initial implant digitalization with a scan body to fabricate a customized abutment and then transfer of the position of the abutment following the scan protocol for a tooth-supported prosthesis. The idea behind this approach is to overcome the inaccuracies related to laboratory-produced models retaining implant replicas for improved prosthesis fit, particularly for CAD/CAM customized abutments [9] Unlike the method of fabricating a cement-retained custom abutment with the implant scan body following the superimposition technique, the present report presents a case in which a failing restoration was remade using the existing cement-retained stock abutment to cope with complete digital workflow for fixed restorations.

Digital manufacturing methods for implant restorations on stock abutments have been technically examined with intraoral scanners and prefabricated abutment libraries [14]. Here, the accuracy of the procedure was analyzed with reverse engineering software for comparison with reference data and was hypothetically accepted as suitable. However, information with regards to fit of the restoration fabricated with the approach is lacking.

In the present case report, the superimposition technique was employed for the prefabricated abutment to remake a single-tooth implant crown that had been technically compromised. The existing abutment was dual-scanned with an intraoral scanner, one scan was performed for inter-arch positioning, and the other scan was obtained for individual abutment identification. Difficulties in the intraoral scanning of metallic geometric surfaces have been considered in terms of matching quality. However, incomplete abutment scanning in the arch does not affect the superimposition accuracy [15]. Therefore dual-scanning of an abutment can be accepted as a method to exchange data to establish a reliable digital impression for cement retained implant abutments in CAD/CAM processing.

The protocol described in this report facilitates a complete digital workflow for replacing a cement- retained implant restoration without the need for model fabrication. Beyond clinical predictability, the treatment costs are considerably reduced for abutments free of mechanical complications for reuse. More importantly, extra-oral scanning of abutment facilitates to achieve acceptable internal and marginal fit which would be beneficial in maintenance of peri-implant tissues in long-term. Therefore, the defined approach offers a solution for digitizing implant workflows in cases where regular intraoral implant scanning steps cannot be employed.

However, one limitation of this technique is the difficulty in relocating the margins in situations where the peri-implant mucosal health needs to be improved [16, 17]. Margin redefining specifically in vertical dimension on a re-used abutment may be challenging due to insufficient available material volume. For such situations the use of the dual scanning technique for creating customized abutments may be more beneficial [5, 9, 10]. Another limitation is the reduced geometrical features and modification possibilities with the stock abutment compared with customized CAD/CAM abutments, which can interfere with the predictability of the superimposition.

The use of an abutment-level dual-scanning digital workflow for fixed restorations has a scientific potential for expanding the current knowledge on implant restoration repair. That said, experimental studies may be beneficial for identifying the differences in implant position transfer between the dual-scanning technique and the regular scan body technique employing reverse engineering methodologies. Additionally, clinical studies are needed to validate the technique for fixed dental prostheses supporting multiple implants.

Conclusion

The presented strategy offers a digital efficient workflow for simplifying cement-retained abutment-level impressions through the use of an intraoral scanner and can be highly beneficial to clinicians. The dual-scan approach may be an option for solving problems related to cement-retained implant fixed prosthesis through the use of a complete digital model-free workflow.

Unquestionable Bell theorem for interwoven frustrated down conversion processes
Paweł Cieśliński, Marcin Markiewicz, Konrad Schlichtholz, Jan-Åke Larsson, Marek Żukowski

Abstract
In the Wang et al. paper “Violation of Bell inequality with unentangled photons” [Science Advances, 1 Aug 2025 Vol 11, Issue 31], in which a brilliant experiment involving two interwoven frustrated down conversion processes is reported, Bell non-classicality of the observed interference processes is conjectured under some tacit additional assumptions. To clear any doubts about Bell non-classicality of the interference processes observed by Wang et al., we give an unconditional Bell-type proof that a modified version of the experiment indeed would reveal a violation of local realism. The data of the Wang et al. experiment show destructive interference which surpasses the required threshold for Bell non-classicality in the modified version, and thus effectively constitutes a positive experimental Bell non-classicality test employing our proposal. The essence of our method is to abandon the usual approach in which macroscopically controlled optical phases define the measurement settings in a Bell experiment. Instead, in our case, the local settings are determined by switching on or off the local pumping fields of the parametric down conversion crystals at the local measuring stations of “Alice and Bob”. We also show that in a standard framework of local hidden variable theories that do not require any additional assumptions, it is possible to construct a local realistic model which exactly reproduces the probabilities on which the conjecture by Wang et al. violation of local realism was based. Thus, we prove that the tacit additional assumptions in the Bell analysis of Wang et al. constrain the class of local realistic models refuted by the experiment. Also, we claim that the non-classical effects in the Wang et al. experiment cannot be ascribed to unentangled photons.

Introduction

The series of trailblazing parametric down conversion experiments by Mandel and his group culminated with a two-crystal experiment in which the interference depended on the level of path-indistinguishability of a photon, the source of which was undefined – one of the two crystals

As these phase relations are such that the entire two-stage down conversion can

In recent years, outstanding experiments extending these ideas were reported in

As the source of the photons belonging to each such photon of each pair is fundamentally unknown, one can observe interference in coincident counts of this kind (up to, in theory, even a completely destructive one).

III
Proof of an unconditional Bell non-classicality of the interwoven frustrated down conversion process

We start with the following declaration. A new proof of Bell non-classicality for the considered interferometric scheme is needed because in the derivation of the CHSH-like inequality in
[
5
]
we see several tacit additional assumptions. These will be discussed later in Section
IV
and Appendix
A

III.0.1
A proposition of a “game rule" for proper Bell experiments

Proposed rule
: (a) First, show that the ideal theoretical version of experiment in question violates a proper Bell inequality, which is based solely on the assumptions of locality, realism, and the freedom of choice of macroscopic settings at the measuring stations. If your ideal theoretical version of Bell’s Theorem needs supplementary assumptions, except realism, locality, and the freedom of choice of the macroscopic settings at the measuring stations, this is not an unconditional Bell experiment. It can refute only a class of local realistic models which satisfy the additional assumptions, definitely not all of them. (b) Only after a positive outcome of the investigation (a) discuss imperfections in your actual laboratory experiment, which may reduce or prohibit violation of the considered Bell inequality.

As an example of what kind of problems can be generated if this rule is not followed, see e.g.
[
8
]
or
[
13
]
.

IV
Discussion of the previously conjectured Bell non-classicality

IV.1
On "Violation of Bell inequality with unentangled photons"

In the recent work by Wang et al., the Bell non-classicality in the interwoven frustrated down conversion was
as we claim effectively only conjectured
. Moreover, the claim was that a violation of a Bell inequality was obtained with unentangled photons.

First, we address the claim that the state generating the four-photon coincidence events, which presumably lead to revealing Bell non-classicality, is unentangled (separable). In the paper
[
5
]
, authors write that they “post-select the state
{|{1111}\rangle}
” (page 2, after eq. (1), in our notation)

{|{\tilde{\psi}_{f}}\rangle}=g^{2}(1+e^{i(\alpha+\beta)}){|{1111}\rangle}.

(11)

However, the above is in itself not a quantum state, because this Hilbert space vector is not normalized. When normalized,
{|{\psi_{f}}\rangle}={|{1111}\rangle}
does not exhibit interference.
The reason is that when post-selecting
only
the
1111
outcomes (one post-selects outcomes, not quantum states) one also removes the necessary reference detection events, other than
1111
. One must preserve a reference to be able to talk about a change (in a probability or rate). A Bell inequality needs at least two outcomes: the local outcome 11 would need to be accompanied with (at least) the local outcome "not 11" otherwise no meaningful probability can be assigned. As a matter of fact this is the basic requirement for any probability which is lower than 1.

When including a second outcome, another problem becomes visible: the single-pair probabilities
P_{A}(11|\alpha)=P_{B}(11|\beta)=g^{2}-\frac{8}{3}g^{4}
are much larger than the coincident-pair probability
P_{AB}(11,11|\alpha,\beta)=2g^{4}[1+\cos(\alpha+\beta)]
.
The end result is that not enough coincident pairs are detected compared to single pairs to enable violation of a Bell inequality
[
17
;
18
;
19
;
8
;
20
;
21
]
.
The situation is analogous to that of a Franson interferometry
[
9
]
where post-selection seemingly gives a CHSH violation but careful analysis
[
8
]
shows that the original setup can be explained by a local model (so is not nonlocal).
We should remark that a significant modification of the argument that adds constraints to the physical setup
[
8
;
20
]
allows Franson interferometry to admit proper Bell violation, in that case of a chained Bell inequality
[
22
]
.
The post-selection problem in
[
5
]
has also recently been addressed in an independent comment on the discussed work by Wharton and Price
[
6
]
.

Let us also observe one more important issue. In the work
[
5
]
the state on which the local stations perform measurements is not the state
|\text{on,on};\alpha,\beta\rangle
after passing through all the crystals, but rather the
|\text{off,off};\alpha=0,\beta=0\rangle
state, as the settings for the measurement are determined by the macroscopically controlled phase shifts
(\alpha,\beta)
that occur
before
the second row of crystals. Thus, the local crystals are part of the local measurement stations of Alice and Bob. To make this statement obvious: the controlling local phases which give the measurement settings in
[
5
]
,
\alpha
and
\beta
, are in the
input
modes of the local down-converters.
Therefore, before measurement stations there is no interference term in the amplitude of
{|{1111}\rangle}
, but one effectively has a four-mode (weakly)
squeezed vacuum state
|\text{off,off};\alpha=0,\beta=0\rangle
. This state is well known to be entangled.
As the action of the phase shifters and crystals at Alice’s and Bob’s measuring station is local, for any mode separable state the global interference term would not appear and thus, clearly the effect needs to be attributed to the entanglement of an initial state, which in the discussed case is the
|\text{off,off};\alpha=0,\beta=0\rangle
state.

IV.2
Local realistic model for the Wang et al. experiment: no Bell theorem with unentangled photons

The construction, presented in
[
5
]
, of a CHSH-like inequality does not follow the standard Bell scenario. This leads to questions whether this inequality is a proper Bell inequality.
A further discussion of the issues is given in Appendix
A
.
We just state here that the expression called in
[
5
]
a "correlation function" is neither the one defined by Bell and CHSH, nor the one used in probability theory and mathematical statistics, and the inequality derived in
[
5
]
rests on tacit additional assumptions. Thus, our "game rule for Bell experiments" is not met.

To explicitly show that the CHSH-like inequality from
[
5
]
is not a proper Bell inequality, we construct a local hidden variable model that reconstructs all of the observed statistics. That is

P_{A}(11|\alpha)=P_{B}(11|\beta)=g^{2}-\frac{8}{3}g^{4},\quad P_{AB}(11,11|\alpha,\beta)=2g^{4}[1+\cos(\alpha+\beta)]

(12)

where the local probabilities are corrected to avoid the apparent signaling as shown in Appendix
B
. Note that this signaling was also mentioned in the work of Wang et al., but was not resolved explicitly. We assume that the final down conversion crystal of Alice has hidden variables
\lambda_{A}=\{\phi_{A},r_{A}\}
and similarly for the
\lambda_{B}
at Bob’s station. We let
\phi_{A}+\phi_{B}=2\pi
, and
r_{A}+r_{B}=1
, where
\phi_{A}
is uniformly distributed over
[0,2\pi]
and
r_{A}
is uniformly distributed over the unit interval. The relation between
\lambda_{A}
and
\lambda_{B}
can exist because
a)
the down converters share a pump, and
b)
they also share one beam each from the “remote” initial down conversion crystal, either of these suffice. For
reasons of locality they do not, however, have access to the “remote” interferometer phase delay
\alpha
or
\beta
.

The local realistic model works as follows. Alice’s measurement station has a local parameter
\alpha
, and a photon pair is detected if
\sin(\phi_A+\pi-\alpha)\geq 0
and either
r_{A}\leq c\sin(\phi_A+\pi-\alpha)
or
r_{A}\geq 1-d
. The introduced parameters
c
and
d
are constants to be chosen in what follows so that the above intervals do not overlap, meaning
c+d\leq 1
. The local probability for Alice is now given as

\displaystyle P_{A}(11|\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\alpha-\pi}^{\alpha}\frac{d\phi_{A}}{2\pi}[c\sin(\phi_A+\pi-\alpha)+d]=\frac{c}{\pi}+\frac{d}{2}.

(13)

At Bob’s station, a photon pair is detected if
\sin(\phi_B - \beta)\geq 0
and either
r_{B}\leq c\sin(\phi_B - \beta)
or
r_{B}\geq 1-d
. Again, the local probability is given as

P_{B}(11|\beta)=\int_{\beta}^{\beta+\pi}\frac{d\phi_{B}}{2\pi}[c\sin(\phi_B-\beta)+d]=\frac{c}{\pi}+\frac{d}{2}.

(14)

To calculate the probability of
P_{AB}(11,11|\alpha,\beta)
of double pair detection we do the following. From
\phi_{A}+\phi_{B}=2\pi
one gets that the region of interest for
\phi_{A}
to simultaneously fulfill conditions
\sin(\phi_A+\pi-\alpha)\geq 0
and
\sin(\phi_B-\beta)\geq 0
is given as the intersection between
\beta+\pi\leq\phi_{A}\leq 2\pi-\beta
and

V
Conclusions

In this work, we showed that the set-up for interwoven frustrated down conversion processes used in
[
5
]
, after our suggested modification, in fact can reveal Bell non-classicality by violation of a proper Clauser-Horne inequality. The modification introduces an
on-off
scheme, in which one of the local settings is: pump is blocked (off). Still, this violation is due to the entanglement of a four-mode squeezed vacuum state. This is because it is the state that is distributed to the local measurement stations, both in the experiment of
[
5
]
, and in our modification. The macroscopically controlled settings of the local measurement apparatuses, of which an integral part are the down converters of Alice and Bob, are either phases
\alpha
and
\beta
(this is the case of
[
5
]
) and/or the amplitudes of the pumping laser field entering the crystals in measurement stations (in our case they are chosen as on-off mode of operation). All these macroscopically controllable parameters act on photons and pump fields
before
they enter the local down converters, see Fig
1
.

Note that the arrangement of
[
5
]
and our modification constitute a new optical POVM measurement scheme in Bell experiments – a step forward with respect to a homodyne detection
[
10
]
. Homodyne detection is a POVM measurement based on a passive optics.
Here we have an active optics POVM (as to such a class of processes belongs down-conversion).

The local hidden variable model for the correlations used in
[
5
]
presented in this paper directly shows that the CHSH-like inequality, whose violation was reported in
[
5
]
is not a proper Bell inequality. Therefore, together with the state structure discussion, the claims about revealing Bell non-classicality without entanglement are unjustified. We also discuss some tacit additional assumptions used in the derivation of this CHSH-like inequality of
[
5
]
that allow for the construction of the local hidden variable model. Such loopholes are impossible to be closed by using better laboratory equipment, better alignment, etc.. However, as we have shown they can be removed by re-arranging the measurement scheme to the on-off one.

Appendix A
Inequality resembling CHSH in
[
5
]

Here we analyze the description of the authors of
[
5
]
of how they construct an inequality which they interpret as a version of the CHSH one. We refer directly to the text of
[
5
]
, and thus the Reader should directly compare our considerations here with the formulas and statements of
[
5
]
.

A.1
Bell correlation function and "correlation function" of
[
5
]

The correlation function used by Bell
[
23
]
, CHSH
[
24
]
and many others, specific for dichotomic numerical values of outcomes
\pm 1
, and which is consistent with the probability calculus, reads:

E(\alpha,\beta)=\sum_{j=\pm 1}\sum_{i=\pm 1}ijP(i,j|\alpha,\beta),

(18)

where
i
and
j
are numbers which represent the measured (obtained) values, and
\alpha
and
\beta
are the settings of the respective local measuring apparatuses. As we discuss here the contents of the paper
[
5
]
, the symbols for the settings in Bell experiment that we use are
\alpha
and
\beta
, but of course the formula is general. All these values and parameters, that is
i,j
and
\alpha,\beta
are macroscopic and directly accessible in the experiments.
In summary, in a proper Bell analysis
macroscopic setting parameters (here phases) are
fixed
when calculating the correlation function. The local hidden variable model for the function, if exists, must take the following form

E(\alpha,\beta)=\sum_{j=\pm 1}\sum_{i=\pm 1}ij\int d\lambda\rho(\lambda)p_{A}(i|\alpha;\lambda)p_{B}(j|\beta;\lambda),

(19)

where the notation is hopefully obvious.

In the paper
[
5
]
, the "correlation function" reads

E_{paper}(\alpha,\beta)=\sum_{r=\pm 1}\sum_{s=\pm 1}rsP_{paper}(r,s|\alpha,\beta),

(20)

where
r=\pm 1
and
s=\pm 1
,
and

\displaystyle P_{paper}(r,s|\alpha,\beta)=\frac{\mathcal{N}(r,s|\alpha+\frac{1-r}{2}\pi,\beta+\frac{1-s}{2}\pi)}{\sum_{r=\pm 1}\sum_{s=\pm 1}\mathcal{N}(r,s|\alpha+\frac{1-r}{2}\pi,\beta+\frac{1-s}{2}\pi)},

(21)

where
r=\pm 1
,
s=\pm 1
and
\mathcal{N}
stands for the number of events.
P_{paper}(r,s|\alpha,\beta)
is a function that the authors call probability. We have doubts whether this is an appropriate term, but we shall not analyze that further here, as this is just a question of how one properly defines a probability, irrelevant to the sequel.

A.2
The upper bound of the "CHSH" expression

The hidden variable theory of "correlation function" of
[
5
]
must have the following form:

E_{paper-lhv}(\alpha,\beta)=\frac{\sum_{r=\pm 1}\sum_{s=\pm 1}rsP_{lhv}(r,s|\alpha+\frac{1-r}{2}\pi,\beta+\frac{1-s}{2}\pi)}{\sum_{r=\pm 1}\sum_{s=\pm 1}P_{lhv}(r,s|\alpha+\frac{1-r}{2}\pi,\beta+\frac{1-s}{2}\pi)},

(22)

where

P_{lhv}(r,s|\alpha+\frac{1-r}{2}\pi,\beta+\frac{1-s}{2}\pi)=\int d\lambda\rho(\lambda)p_{A}(11|\alpha+\frac{1-r}{2}\pi;\lambda)p_{B}(11|\beta+\frac{1-s}{2}\pi;\lambda).

(23)

Here, e.g.
p_{A}(11|\alpha+\frac{1-r}{2}\pi;\lambda)
represents a probability to observe the 11 count at Alice’s station under macroscopic controllable phase setting
\frac{1-r}{2}\pi
for a specific value of the hidden variable
\lambda
, while
\rho(\lambda)
is a properly normalized classical probabilistic distribution of the hidden variables.

In the LHV expression, in the numerator of the correlation function, one can perform the following trivial algebra

\displaystyle\int d\lambda\rho(\lambda)\sum_{r=\pm 1}\sum_{s=\pm 1}rsp_{A}(11|\alpha+\frac{1-r}{2}\pi;\lambda)p_{B}(11|\beta+\frac{1-s}{2}\pi;\lambda)

\displaystyle=\int d\lambda\rho(\lambda)\sum_{r=\pm 1}rp_{A}(11|\alpha+\frac{1-r}{2}\pi;\lambda)\sum_{s=\pm 1}sp_{B}(11|\beta+\frac{1-s}{2}\pi;\lambda).

(24)

We know that in the case of local hidden variable theories, to get the Bell inequality bounds, it is enough to find them for the deterministic cases.
Note that the deterministic values of
\sum_{r=\pm 1}rp_{A}(11|\alpha+\frac{1-r}{2}\pi)=X_{A}(\alpha,\lambda)

and
\sum_{s=\pm 1}sp_{B}(11|\beta+\frac{1-s}{2}\pi;\lambda)=X_{B}(\beta,\lambda)
are
1,0,-1
.
One cannot avoid 0, because if there are no zeros, this means that
p_{A}(11|\alpha+\pi)
or
p_{A}(11|\alpha)
is 1.
This in turn would mean that

\sum_{r=\pm 1}p_{A}(11|\alpha+\frac{1-r}{2}\pi;\lambda)\sum_{s=\pm 1}p_{B}(11|\beta+\frac{1-s}{2}\pi;\lambda)=1,

(25)

and consequently, the denominator becomes

D_{lhv}(\alpha,\beta)=\sum_{r=\pm 1}\sum_{s=\pm 1}P_{lhv}(r,s|\alpha+\frac{1-r}{2}\pi,\beta+\frac{1-s}{2}\pi)=1.

(26)

If this holds, then one can derive the "CHSH" inequality of
[
5
]
in the usual way using the bound for the CHSH algebraic expression for hidden values
X_{A}(\alpha,\lambda)=\pm 1

and
X_{B}(\beta,\lambda)\pm 1
.
However, in the experiment
D_{exp}(\alpha,\beta)\propto g^{4}\ll 1
…

Thus the "CHSH" inequality of
[
5
]
only resembles the original one, and some tacit assumption must have been used to derive it, like e.g.
\sum_{r=\pm 1}p_{A}(11|\alpha+\frac{1-r}{2}\pi,\lambda)=1
and a similar one for Bob’s events. Note that there is no reason for this assumption to hold, as the hidden probabilities in the condition are for different operational situations (different phases). The explicit model presented in here certifies that there must have been an additional assumption, supplementing the ones of local realism.

Appendix B
On the necessary correction of the expansion of the unitary operators needed for proper calculation of the probabilities

Here, we will discuss the problem of the cut-off of the state up to second order in
g
and that it is insufficient to properly calculate some probabilities in the experiment. This is because the probabilities for which one can observe the interference effect are of order
g^{4}
. Thus, approximation to second order in amplitudes is unjustified, as corrections to amplitudes of order
g^{4}
can also contribute to the probabilities of specific events at this order. Thus, to obtain probabilities valid up to
g^{4}
one needs to consider Taylor expansion of operator
\hat{U}
up to order
g^{4}
.
However, one can simplify the calculation, as only very specific terms can contribute to probabilities in the considered order. More specifically, terms proportional to
g^{4}
are relevant only if they provide correction to amplitude of order
g^{0}
. This means that for the considered state, only correction to the vacuum state is relevant, More explicitly amplitude for
{|{0000}\rangle}
after correction is given by
1-2g^{2}+g^{4}(7/3+\exp[i(\alpha+\beta)])
which results in correction to probability of event of no click
g^{4}(14/3+\cos(\alpha+\beta))+O(g^{6})
. Similarly, corrections to amplitudes of order
g^{3}
are relevant only when dominant part of the amplitude is of order
g^{1}
. This leaves only corrections to states
{|{1100}\rangle},\,{|{0011}\rangle},\,{|{1010}\rangle},\,{|{0101}\rangle}
being relevant. The full relevant correction to the
{|{\text{on,on};\alpha,\beta}\rangle}
state is:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left(\frac{7}{3}+e^{i(\alpha+\beta)}\right)g^{4}{|{0000}\rangle}-ig^{3}\frac{10}{3}\left(e^{i\alpha}{|{1010}\rangle}+e^{i\beta}{|{0101}\rangle}\right)\\
&-ig^{3}\left(\frac{7}{3}+e^{i(\alpha+\beta)}\right)({|{1100}\rangle}+{|{0011}\rangle}).\end{split}

(27)

Note that this correction gives us three extra interference terms not present in the calculation using the expansions of the unitary operators up to the second order in
g
.

This turns out to be extremely important for the consistency of the description of the (family of) states (
4
). Due to the sharp cutoff approximation, it seems that one can have signaling. This appears in two ways. First, one can calculate using (
4
) the local probability for observing the event of detecting one photon in each detector of Alice. It reads
P_{A}(11)=g^{2}+2g^{4}(1+\cos(\alpha+\beta))
. This depends on the remote setting of the second party,
\beta
. However, due to the interference term which appears due to including correction to
{|{1100}\rangle}
(with the adjusted amplitude of
ig-ig^{3}[7/3+e^{i(\alpha+\beta)}]
), the corrected
P_{A}(11)=g^{2}-\frac{8}{3}g^{4}+O(g^{6})
becomes no-signaling in the expected order of correctness of the approximation. The second problem appears as the normalization factor for the state (
4
) determined by the norm square of the state
N^{2}=1+18g^{4}+2g^{4}\cos(\alpha+\beta)
is also modulated by the sum of the phases. This again is circumvented by the included correction, as then
N^{2}=1+O(g^{6})
.

The relevant corrections from
g^{4}
order for the remaining settings are the following: for off-off state:

\frac{2}{3}g^{4}{|{0000}\rangle}-ig^{3}\frac{4}{3}\left(e^{i\alpha}{|{1010}\rangle}+e^{i\beta}{|{0101}\rangle}\right),

(28)

for on-off:

\frac{11}{8}g^{4}{|{0000}\rangle}-ig^{3}\frac{7}{3}\left(e^{i\alpha}{|{1010}\rangle}+e^{i\beta}{|{0101}\rangle}\right)-ig^{3}\frac{11}{6}{|{1100}\rangle},

(29)

for off-on

\frac{11}{8}g^{4}{|{0000}\rangle}-ig^{3}\frac{7}{3}\left(e^{i\alpha}{|{1010}\rangle}+e^{i\beta}{|{0101}\rangle}\right)-ig^{3}\frac{11}{6}{|{0011}\rangle},

(30)

Note that for the off-off case, local probabilities
P(A)
and
P(B)
, which are used in the main text for calculating the violation of the Clauser-Horne inequality, are not affected by these corrections.

In general, the proper truncations for the calculations of the probabilities can be summarized as follows. For any unitary operator
\hat{U}(g)=e^{ig\hat{H}}
, with obviously Hermitian
\hat{H}
, its truncated version
\hat{U}_{T}(g)
obtained by Taylor expansion up to order
g^{n}
gives proper expectation values of any observable
\hat{O}
with accuracy up to also
g^{n}
. Taylor series expansion of expectation values are also preserved, that is
{\langle{\psi}|}\hat{U}^{\dagger}(g)\hat{O}\hat{U}(g){|{\psi}\rangle}-\mathcal{T}_{n}\left({\langle{\psi}|}\hat{U}_{T}^{\dagger}(g)\hat{O}\hat{U}_{T}(g){|{\psi}\rangle}\right)=O(g^{n+1})
,
where
\mathcal{T}_{n}()
denotes the Taylor expansion up to the
n
-th order.
For the norm of the transformed state one has
{\langle{\psi}|}\hat{U}_{T}^{\dagger}(g)\hat{U}_{T}(g){|{\psi}\rangle}=1+O(g^{n+1})
. Thus, when calculating probabilities up to
n
-th order approximation, one does not need to renormalize the transformed state.

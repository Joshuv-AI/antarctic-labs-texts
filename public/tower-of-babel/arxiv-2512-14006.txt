Fuglede theorem for symmetric spaces of τ \tau -measurable operators
Denis Potapov, Fedor Sukochev, Anna Tomskova, Dmitriy Zanin

Abstract
. We extend the classical Fuglede commutativity theorem to the full scale of symmetrically normed operator ideals. Our main result provides a complete characterization: a symmetric ideal or symmetric operator space of τ \tau -measurable operators satisfies the Fuglede theorem if and only if its commutative core has non-trivial Boyd indices, or equivalently, if it is an interpolation space in the scale of L p L_{p} -spaces for 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty . This criterion subsumes all previously known cases, including Lorentz and Schatten classes.

Primary 47B10, 47L20; Secondary 46E30, 47A30.

1.
Introduction

Fuglede’s theorem is a classical result in operator theory, stating that if
A
and
B
are normal operators on a Hilbert space
H
, and a bounded operator
T
satisfies

AT=TB,

then it follows that

A^{*}T=TB^{*}.

This result, originally established for the case
A=B
by Fuglede in 1950
[
16
]
, and strengthened by Putnam to the two-operator setting, where both

A,B
are normal
[
30
]
, plays a foundational role in understanding the interplay between operator commutation and adjoint structures. In particular, for normal operators, the intertwining relation passes from an operator to its adjoint.

Rewriting the condition in terms of commutators, Fuglede’s theorem asserts:

[A,T]=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad[A^{*},T]=0.

This naturally raises the question of what can be said when the commutator does not vanish, but is merely small in norm. That is, if
[A,T]
is small in some operator ideal norm, can one control
[A^{*},T]
accordingly?

This leads to the study of
quantitative
versions of the Fuglede-Putnam theorem, with inequalities of the form

\|[A^{*},T]\|\leq C\|[A,T]\|,

where
A
and
T
are bounded operators, and
C
is a constant depending on the context. Such estimates have attracted considerable attention (see e.g.
[
34
,
17
,
26
,
15
]
and the recent work
[
22
]
). These norm-type commutator estimates have deep connections with perturbation theory, operator ideals, and noncommutative geometry. In perturbation theory, such estimates appear naturally in the study of stability of operator spectra under small perturbations (see e.g.
[
20
]
). Their relevance to operator ideals is investigated in the context of commutators in normed ideals (see e.g.
[
21
]
), while in noncommutative geometry they are central to Connes’ spectral approach, where commutator bounds play a key role in defining geometric structures on noncommutative spaces (see e.g.
[
7
]
).

Recent developments have extended the Fuglede–Putnam framework to a much broader class of
elementary operators
of the form

\Delta(X)=\sum_{i}A_{i}XB_{i},

where the classical argument no longer applies directly. In particular, Shulman and Turowska (see
[
32
]
) analyzed this general setting and identified geometric conditions, such as bounded essential dimension of the left coefficient family, under which analogues of the Fuglede–Putnam theorem remain valid (see also
[
33
]
).

In the setting of this paper, let
H
be a separable Hilbert space, and let
{B}(H)
denote the algebra of all bounded linear operators on
H
.
A
(quasi-)Banach symmetric ideal
is, in general, a two-sided (quasi-)Banach ideal
\mathcal{I}\subset{B}({H})
equipped with a unitary invariant (quasi-)norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{I}}
satisfying the ideal property.
A (quasi-)Banach symmetric ideals can also be defined via symmetric norms on sequences by associating to each compact operator its sequence of singular values and requiring the norm to depend only on these values in a symmetric way (see e.g.
[
19
]
).

Definition 1
.

A (quasi-)Banach symmetric ideal
\mathcal{I}
is Fuglede if there is
C>0
such that

\|[A^{\ast},T]\|_{\mathcal{I}}\leq C\ \|[A,T]\|_{\mathcal{I}}

for all normal operators
A
and all
T\in B(H)
.

If some (quasi-)Banach symmetric ideal is Fuglede, this simply means that this enhanced form of the Fuglede-Putnam theorem holds for this ideal. The term “Fuglede ideal” was first introduced in
[
23
, Definition 3.7]
.

It was first shown by G. Weiss in 1978 (
[
37
]
) that the class of Hilbert-Schmidt operators is Fuglede. Later, Abdessemed and Davies
[
2
]
and Shulman
[
31
]
extended the result to Schatten classes
\mathcal{S}_{p}
for
2<p
and
1<p<2
respectively.
However, certain operator ideals, such as the finite-rank operators,
\mathcal{S}_{1}
,
\mathcal{S}_{\infty}
,
B(H)
and quasi-Banach Schatten classes
\mathcal{S}_{p}
for
0<p<1
, are not Fuglede
[
38
]
.

It was established in
[
22
, Theorem 4.6]
that any ideal with non-trivial Boyd indices is Fuglede. One of the results of this work is that we provide a new, more transparent, proof of this result (see Section
3
).

In the same paper
[
22
]
, the authors posed the question of whether a Fuglede ideal can have trivial Boyd indices (see
[
22
, Problem 4.10]
).
In this manuscript, we provide a negative answer to this question (see Theorem
7
).

The Fuglede ideals were also investigated in the earlier paper
[
23
]
, where it was shown that any separable symmetric normed ideal with non-trivial Boyd indices is a Fuglede ideal (see Corollary 3.8 therein).
However, that analysis did not include certain Lorentz ideals singled out in Problem 3.9 of that paper.
In this work we completely settle this remaining case in Theorem
7
.

In the special case of separable and coseparable ideals, a similar question was recently treated in
[
39
]
.
In that work, Fuglede-type commutation inequalities were established for a broad class of symmetric ideals, including the Lorentz classes by connecting the boundedness of a certain double operator integral with the validity of the Fuglede property. In addition, the author constructed a number of non-Fuglede ideals, among them certain weighted ideals, including the Macaev ideal.
Our main result (Theorem
7
) gives a complete characterization of when a symmetric ideal is Fuglede: namely, an ideal has the Fuglede property if and only if it has non-trivial Boyd indices. In the special case of concrete ideals considered in
[
39
]
, Theorem 1.2 there establishes a Fuglede–type estimate under additional structural assumptions, and the subsequent model cases (Theorems 1.3–1.6 in
[
39
]
) are all covered as direct corollaries of Theorem
7
. Thus the present work subsumes the ideal-theoretic part of
[
39
]
and extends it to the general setting.

It is worth mentioning that there is also a notion of
Fuglede stable ideals
discussed at length in
[
22
]
(introduced in
[
23
, Definition 5.3]
and termed there
weakly Fuglede ideals
),
which is more delicate than the standard Fuglede property.
For example, although both ideals
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{\infty}
are not Fuglede, the ideal

\mathcal{S}_{\infty}

is
Fuglede stable (see
[
23
, Proposition 5.4]
), whereas
\mathcal{S}_{1}
is
not
Fuglede stable (see
[
23
, Corollary 5.8]
). It is also known that every Fuglede ideal is automatically Fuglede stable. Moreover, in
[
23
, Corollary 5.7]
the authors provide conditions under which an ideal is Fuglede stable if and only if it is Fuglede.
In view of this result, our Theorem
7
yields a corresponding criterion for an ideal to be Fuglede stable as well.

In addition, in this paper we introduce a notion of Fuglede symmetric operator space (see Definition
5
) and show that a symmetric operator space is Fuglede if and only if its commutative core has non-trivial Boyd indices (see Theorem
6
).
Moreover, our approach works for arbitrary symmetric Banach function or sequence spaces (considered in
[
24
]
or
[
3
]
) without any additional properties.

For the convenience of the reader, the main definition and results of the paper are collected in Section
3
.
However, all technical proofs are postponed to the end of the paper and divided into three parts:
the proof of sufficiency in Theorem
6
(see Section
4
),
the proof of necessity in the case of infinite factors (see Section
5
),
and the proof of necessity in the case of finite factors (see Section
6
).

2.
Preliminaries

Commutative theory

Let
\mathbb{C},\mathbb{R},\mathbb{N},\mathbb{Z}
denote the sets of complex numbers, real numbers, natural numbers (without 0), and integers, respectively.

The space
\ell_{\infty}
is the set of bounded complex sequences
x=(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{C}
such that

\ell_{\infty}:=\big\{x:\|x\|_{\infty}=\sup_{n}|x_{n}|<\infty\big\}.

A Banach space
E\subset\ell_{\infty}
is called a
symmetric Banach sequence space
if

(i)

E
is a Banach lattice with respect to pointwise order, i.e., if
x\in E
and
|y|\leq|x|,
then
y\in E
and
\|y\|_{E}\leq\|x\|_{E}
;

(ii)

E
is
symmetric
(or rearrangement-invariant
[
27
]
): for any
x\in E
and any permutation
\pi
of
\mathbb{N}
, the sequence
x\circ\pi
belongs to
E
, and
\|x\circ\pi\|_{E}=\|x\|_{E}.

For more details on symmetric function and sequence spaces, we refer to
[
3
,
24
,
25
]
.
For
1\leq p<\infty
, the classical example of symmetric Banach sequence spaces, the space
\ell_{p}
consists of complex sequences
x=(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{C}
such that

\ell_{p}:=\big\{x:\|x\|_{p}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|x_{n}|^{p}<\infty\big\}.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we denote by
\ell_{p}^{n}
the space
\mathbb{C}^{n}
equipped with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{p}
defined above.

For each integer
n\geq 1
, define the dilation operators
D_{n}
and
D_{1/n}

on
\ell_{\infty}
by setting respectively for
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)\in\ell_{\infty}
,

D_{n}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\dots):=(\underbrace{x_{1},\dots,x_{1}}_{n\text{ times}},\underbrace{x_{2},\dots,x_{2}}_{n\text{ times}},\dots),

D_{1/n}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\dots):=(x_{n},x_{2n},x_{3n},\dots).

The
Boyd indices
of a symmetric Banach sequence space
E
are defined by

\alpha_{E}=\liminf_{n\geq 2}\frac{\log\|D_{1/n}\|_{E\to E}}{\log(1/n)}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\|D_{1/n}\|_{E\to E}}{\log(1/n)}.

\beta_{E}=\limsup_{n\geq 2}\frac{\log\|D_{n}\|_{E\to E}}{\log n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\|D_{n}\|_{E\to E}}{\log n},

In general,
0\leq\alpha_{E}\leq\beta_{E}\leq 1
. We say that the Boyd indices are
non-trivial
if
0<\alpha_{E}\leq\beta_{E}<1
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,\infty]
. A Banach space
E=E(0,\alpha)
of (equivalence classes of) Lebesgue measurable functions on
(0,\alpha)
equipped with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{E}
is called a
symmetric Banach function space
if:

(i)

E(0,\alpha)\subset L_{0}(0,\alpha)
(the space of measurable functions modulo a.e. equality), and
E(0,\alpha)
is a Banach lattice with respect to the pointwise almost everywhere order and the norm
\|\cdot\|_{E}
;

(ii)

if
f\in E(0,\alpha)
and
g
is a measurable function on
(0,\alpha)
with
\mu(g)\leq\mu(f)
, then
g\in E(0,\alpha)
and
\|g\|_{E}\leq\|f\|_{E}
, where
\mu(f)
is the non-increasing rearrangement of
f
, that is

{\mu(t,f):=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\left|\left\{s\in(0,\alpha):|f(s)|>\lambda\right\}\right|\leq t\right\},\quad t\in(0,\infty).}

(iii)

E(0,\alpha)\neq\{0\};

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, the classical example of symmetric Banach function spaces is

L_{p}(0,\infty)
, the set of all (equivalence classes of) measurable functions
f\colon(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{C}
with
\|f\|_{p}:=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(t)|^{p}\,dt\right)^{1/p}<\infty
, for
1\leq p<\infty
and

\|f\|_{\infty}:=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{t>0}|f(t)|<\infty,\ p=\infty.

For
s>0
consider
the dilation operator
D_{s}
on
L_{0}(0,\infty)
defined as

(D_{s}(f))(t)=f(t/s),\quad t\in(0,\infty).

The
Boyd indices
of a symmetric Banach function space
E(0,\infty)
are defined by

\alpha_{E}=\liminf_{0<s<1}\frac{\log\|D_{s}\|_{E\to E}}{\log s}=\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{\log\|D_{s}\|_{E\to E}}{\log s}.

\beta_{E}=\limsup_{s>1}\frac{\log\|D_{s}\|_{E\to E}}{\log s}=\lim_{s\to\infty}\frac{\log\|D_{s}\|_{E\to E}}{\log s},

In general,
0\leq\alpha_{E}\leq\beta_{E}\leq 1
. We say that the Boyd indices are
non-trivial
if
0<\alpha_{E}\leq\beta_{E}<1
.

Our main results (Theorems
7
and
6
) are presented within the (equivalent) framework of interpolation theory rather than in terms of Boyd indices, as the former offers a more precise and widely accepted language among experts in the field. In what follows, we introduce the necessary terminology from interpolation theory and describe its connection to Boyd indices.

Recall that Banach spaces
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})\subset(W,\|\cdot\|_{W})\subset(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
form an
interpolation triple if any operator
A\in B(X)
preserving
Y
and bounded in

\|\cdot\|_{Y},
preserves
W
and is bounded in
\|\cdot\|_{W}
. In this case we say that the space
W
is
an interpolation space
between
Y
and
X
and shortly write
W\in{\rm Int}(Y,X)
.

The following result highlights the close connection between the Boyd indices and being an interpolation space between two

L_{p}
-spaces (see e.g.
[
10
, Theorem 3.11]
, see also numerous predecessors of this result in a slightly less general setting
[
5
,
3
,
24
]
).

Theorem 2

(Boyd’s Theorem)
.

Let
1<p<q<\infty
, and let
E
be a symmetric Banach sequence space (respectively, symmetric Banach function space). Then
E
is an interpolation space with respect to the couple
(\ell_{p},\ell_{q})
(respectively,
(L_{p},L_{q})
) if and only if the Boyd indices of
E
satisfy

\frac{1}{q}\leq\alpha_{E}\leq\beta_{E}\leq\frac{1}{p}.

Non-commutative integration theory

Let
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal semifinite trace
\tau
.
A Banach space
E(\mathcal{M},\tau)
from
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, the set of all
\tau
-measurable operators affiliated with
\mathcal{M}
(see e.g.
[
14
, Section 2.3]
), is called a
symmetric operator space
if there exists a symmetric Banach function space
E
on
(0,\infty)
or symmetric Banach sequence space
E
(depending on the type of
\mathcal{M}
) such that:

E(\mathcal{M},\tau)=\{x\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M},\tau)\,:\,\mu(x)\in E\},\quad\|x\|_{E(\mathcal{M},\tau)}:=\|\mu(x)\|_{E},

where
\mu(x)
denotes the generalized singular value function (i.e., the non-increasing rearrangement of
|x|
with respect to the trace
\tau
). For brevity, throughout the text, we shall sometimes write
E(\mathcal{M})
instead of
E(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, and
\|x\|_{E}
instead of
\|x\|_{E(\mathcal{M},\tau)}
. More details about symmetric operator spaces can be found in
[
26
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
11
]
.

If
\mathcal{M}=L_{\infty}(0,\alpha)
and
\tau
is integration with respect to Lebesgue measure, then
E(\mathcal{M},\tau)
coincides with the symmetric Banach function space
E(0,\alpha)
.

If
\mathcal{M}=\ell_{\infty}
and
\tau
is the counting measure
\Sigma
, then
E(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is the symmetric Banach sequence space
E\subset\ell_{\infty}.

If
\mathcal{M}=B(H)
(bounded operators on a separable Hilbert space
H
) and
\tau=\operatorname{Tr}
is the canonical trace, then
E(\mathcal{M},\tau)
consists of those
x\in B(H)
such that the corresponding sequence of singular values
\mu(x)
belongs to
E.
This gives rise to
symmetric norm ideals in
B(H)
, denoted by
\mathcal{E}
.

For
1\leq p<\infty
, we set

L_{p}(\mathcal{M})=\left\{x\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M},\tau):\tau(|x|^{p})<\infty\right\},\quad\|x\|_{p}=\left(\tau(|x|^{p})\right)^{1/p}.

For
x\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, the generalized singular value function of
x
is defined by

\mu(t,x)=\inf\left\{s>0:\tau\left(\chi_{(s,\infty)}(|x|)\right)\leq t\right\},\quad t>0,

where
|x|=(x^{*}x)^{1/2}
and
\chi_{(s,\infty)}(|x|)
is the spectral projection of
|x|
corresponding to the interval
(s,\infty).

The space
L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{M})
is defined by

L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{M})=\left\{x\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M},\tau):\sup_{t>0}t\,\mu(t,x)<\infty\right\}.

We equip
L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{M})
with the quasi-norm

\|x\|_{1,\infty}=\sup_{t>0}t\,\mu(t,x),\quad x\in L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{M}).

For
x,y\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, we have (see e.g.
[
14
]
,
[
26
]
):

(2.1)

\mu(t+s,x+y)\leq\mu(t,x)+\mu(s,y),\quad s,t>0.

Then, we have that

\qquad\|x+y\|_{1,\infty}=\sup_{t>0}t\,\mu(t,x+y)\\
\leq\sup_{t>0}\left(t\,\mu\left(\frac{t}{2},x\right)+t\,\mu\left(\frac{t}{2},y\right)\right)\leq 2\|x\|_{1,\infty}+2\|y\|_{1,\infty}.\qquad

The quasi-normed space
(L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{M}),\|\cdot\|_{1,\infty})
is, in fact, quasi-Banach (see e.g.
[
19
, Section 7]
). Naturally we set

L_{1,\infty}(0,\infty)=L_{1,\infty}(L_{\infty}(0,\infty))

The collection of all closed, densely defined operators
x
in
H
, affiliated with the von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
, and satisfying

\tau\left(e^{|x|}(\lambda,\infty)\right)<\infty\quad\text{for all }\lambda>0,

will be denoted by
S_{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
.
The elements of
S_{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
are sometimes called
\tau
-compact operators
. Note that each
x\in S_{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is
\tau
-measurable, that is,
S_{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)\subset S(\mathcal{M},\tau)
(for more details see
[
14
, §2.4]
).

A von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
on a Hilbert space
{H}
is called a
factor
if its center consists only of scalar multiples of the identity, i.e.,

\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{M})=\mathcal{M}\cap\mathcal{M}^{\prime}=\mathbb{C}\mathbf{1},

where
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
is the commutant of
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathbf{1}
is the identity operator on
H
.

A factor
\mathcal{M}
is said to be of
type
\mathrm{I}
if it contains non-zero abelian projections. In this case it is of type
\mathrm{I}_{n}
if
\mathcal{M}\cong M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
,
n\times n
complex matrices and of type
\mathrm{I}_{\infty}
if
\mathcal{M}\cong{B}(H)
for an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space
H
. A factor
\mathcal{M}
is said to be of
type
\mathrm{II}
if it has no minimal projections and admits a faithful normal semifinite trace. In this case it can be of type
\mathrm{II}_{1}
if the trace is finite, e.g., the hyperfinite
\mathrm{II}_{1}
factor, and

\mathrm{II}_{\infty}
if the trace is infinite.

A von Neumann algebra is called
atomic
if it contains minimal projections (factors of type
\mathrm{I}
), and
non-atomic
otherwise (factors of type
\mathrm{II}
).
If
\mathcal{M}
is an atomic factor, then corresponding
E
in the definition of
E(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is a symmetric Banach sequence space. If
\mathcal{M}
is a non-atomic factor, then corresponding
E
in the definition of
E(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is a symmetric Banach function space (can be taken as
E(0,\infty)
).

In this paper, we focus on factors of types
\mathrm{I}_{\infty}
,
\mathrm{II}_{1}
and
\mathrm{II}_{\infty}
. In particular, we study the case of factors of type
\mathrm{II}_{1}
in Section
6
and the case of factors of types
\mathrm{I}_{\infty}
and
\mathrm{II}_{\infty}
in Section
5
.

A detailed exposition of the theory of von Neumann algebras and symmetric operator spaces can also be found in
[
14
]
.

In what follows, we use the notation
c_{abs}

to denote an absolute constant, i.e., a positive constant whose value is independent of all parameters and objects under consideration. Whenever the notation
C_{A,B,C}

appears, it stands for a constant depending only on the objects
A,B
and
C
.

Double operator integrals

Let
E
be a spectral measure on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
taking values in orthogonal projections in
\mathcal{M}
. For every pair of measurable subsets
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the mapping
E(A)\otimes E(B)\colon L_{2}(\mathcal{M})\to L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
, given by

E(A)\otimes E(B)(x)=E(A)xE(B),\quad\text{for }x\in L_{2}(\mathcal{M}),

defines an orthogonal projection on
L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
. The mapping
G\colon A\times B\mapsto E(A)\otimes E(B)
can be extended to the algebra of all measurable subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
so that the extension is a spectral measure on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
with values in orthogonal projections of
{B}(L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))
(see
[
28
, Definition 2.9 and Remark 3.1]
and also
[
4
, Theorem V.2.6]
). We call this measure
G=E\otimes E
.

For every bounded Borel function
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
, the spectral integral

T^{E}_{\varphi}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\varphi(u,v)\,dG(u,v),\quad G=E\otimes E

defines a bounded linear operator
T^{E}_{\varphi}
on
L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
. The norm of
T^{E}_{\varphi}
on
L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
is controlled by
\|\varphi\|_{\infty}
.

The operator
T^{E}_{\varphi}
is called the
double operator integral
of
\phi
with respect to the spectral measure
E
. Let
E_{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
and
E_{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
be symmetric operator spaces or
E_{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)=L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. If
T^{E}_{\varphi}
admits a bounded extension from
L_{2}(\mathcal{M})\cap E_{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
onto the space
E_{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, then we say that
T^{E}_{\varphi}
is bounded from
E_{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
to
E_{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. An extensive account of the theory of double operator integrals for
\tau
-measurable operators is presented in
[
12
]
.

In the special case when
E
is supported on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, we have

(2.2)

T^{E}_{\varphi}(x)=\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\varphi(i,j)E(\{i\})xE(\{j\}).

If
\varphi
is a simple function
\varphi(u,v)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}(u)b_{k}(v)
, then
T^{E}_{\varphi}
is a multiplication operator with normal coefficients (see e.g.
[
21
, (2.2)]
)

T^{E}_{\varphi}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}M_{k}xN_{k},

where

M_{k}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}a_{k}(u)\,dE(u),\quad N_{k}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}b_{k}(v)\,dE(v).

In particular, if
\varphi(u,v)=(u-v)\chi_{A}(u)\chi_{A}(v)
for a bounded Borel set
A\subset\mathbb{C}
and
E
is a spectral measure of a normal operator
y\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, then

(2.3)

T^{E}_{\varphi}(x)=\big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}u\chi_{A}(u)dE(u)\big)x\big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\chi_{A}(v)dE(v)\big)\\
-\big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\chi_{A}(u)dE(u)\big)x\big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}v\chi_{A}(v)dE(v)\big)\\
=yE(A)xE(A)-E(A)xE(A)y=[y,E(A)xE(A)].

Similarly,
\psi(u,v)=(\overline{u}-\overline{v})\chi_{A}(u)\chi_{A}(v)

-\big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\chi_{A}(u)dE(u)\big)x\big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\overline{v}\chi_{A}(v)dE(v)\big)\\
=y^{*}E(A)xE(A)-E(A)xE(A)y^{*}=[y^{*},E(A)xE(A)].

Let
\mathcal{M}

Lemma 8
.

For the function
\Omega
defined by

(4.1)

\Omega(z,w)=\frac{\overline{z}-\overline{w}}{z-w},\quad z\neq w\in\mathbb{C},\quad\Omega(z,z)=0,\quad z\in\mathbb{C}

and every spectral measure
E
supported on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
the operator

T^{E}_{\Omega}:L_{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)\to L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{M},\tau)

is bounded

Proof.

Let
p_{k}=E(\{k\}),

k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and let
\{e_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\subset L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
denote the standard orthonormal basis of complex exponentials on the torus, defined by

e_{k}(t){=e^{i\langle k,t\rangle}}=e^{i(k_{1}t_{1}+k_{2}t_{2})},\quad k=(k_{1},k_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},\;t=(t_{1},t_{2})\in\mathbb{T}^{2}.

Let
U\in\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
be a unitary operator defined as

U=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}p_{k}\otimes e_{k}.

For every
V\in(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(\mathcal{M},\tau),
we have

(4.2)

U^{-1}(V\otimes 1)U=(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}p_{k}\otimes e_{-k})\cdot(V\otimes 1)\cdot(\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}p_{\ell}\otimes e_{\ell})\\
=\sum_{k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}p_{k}Vp_{\ell}\otimes e_{\ell-k}.

Define the function
h:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
by setting

h(z)=\begin{cases}\frac{\overline{z}}{z},&z\neq 0\\
0,&z=0\end{cases}.

Then
h
is smooth and homogeneous of degree
0
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
. Note, however, that
h
is not continuous at the origin.

Let the
gradient operator

\nabla_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}
be defined as the vector-valued operator

\nabla_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}=\frac{1}{i}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{1}},\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{2}}\right).

The components of
\nabla_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}
, namely,

D_{1}=\frac{1}{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{1}},\quad D_{2}=\frac{1}{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{2}},

are commuting self-adjoint operators, acting on the common core of smooth functions, which is dense in
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
. In terms of the Fourier basis
\{e_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
, the operators act as

D_{j}(e_{k})=k_{j}e_{k},\quad j=1,2,\quad\text{for all }k=(k_{1},k_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},

so each
e_{k}
is a joint eigenfunction of
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
with eigenvalues
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
respectively.

Using the joint functional calculus for commuting self-adjoint operators
D_{1}
,
D_{2}
we define the operator
h(\nabla_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}):=h(D_{1},D_{2})
. Then for every
k=(k_{1},k_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
,

h(\nabla_{\mathbb{T}^{2}})(e_{k})=h(k_{1},k_{2})e_{k}=h(k)e_{k}.

Let
{\rm id}_{\mathcal{M}}
be the identity operator on
\mathcal{M}.
Due to (
4.2
), we have that

(4.3)

({\rm id}_{\mathcal{M}}\otimes h(\nabla_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}))(U^{-1}(V\otimes 1)U)=\sum_{k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}p_{k}Vp_{\ell}\otimes h(\nabla_{\mathbb{T}^{2}})e_{\ell-k}\\
=\sum_{k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}p_{k}Vp_{\ell}\otimes h(\ell-k)e_{\ell-k}=\sum_{k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}h(\ell-k)p_{k}Vp_{\ell}\otimes e_{\ell-k}.

On the other hand, it follows from (
2.2
) and from the equality
\Omega(\ell,k)=h(\ell-k)
that

T^{E}_{\Omega}(V)=\sum_{k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\Omega(\ell,k)p_{k}Vp_{\ell}=\sum_{k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}h(\ell-k)p_{k}Vp_{\ell}.

Thus,

\displaystyle U^{-1}(T^{E}_{\Omega}(V)\otimes 1)U

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\sum_{k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}p_{k^{\prime}}\otimes e_{-k^{\prime}}\Bigr)\cdot\Bigl(\sum_{k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}h(\ell-k)p_{k}Vp_{\ell}\Bigr)\cdot\Bigl(\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}p_{\ell^{\prime}}\otimes e_{\ell^{\prime}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,\ell,k^{\prime},\ell^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}h(\ell-k)p_{k^{\prime}}p_{k}Vp_{\ell}p_{\ell^{\prime}}\otimes e_{\ell^{\prime}-k^{\prime}}.

Since in the latter sum
p_{k^{\prime}}p_{k}=0
for all
k^{\prime}\neq k
and
p_{\ell}p_{\ell^{\prime}}=0
for all
\ell^{\prime}\neq\ell
, we obtain

(4.4)

U^{-1}(T^{E}_{\Omega}(V)\otimes 1)U=\sum_{k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}h(\ell-k)p_{k}Vp_{\ell}\otimes e_{\ell-k}.

Combining (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), we arrive at

U^{-1}(T^{E}_{\Omega}(V)\otimes 1)U=({\rm id}_{\mathcal{M}}\otimes h(\nabla_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}))(U^{-1}(V\otimes 1)U).

Thus,

\displaystyle\|T^{E}_{\Omega}(V)\|_{L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{M},\tau)}

\displaystyle=\|U^{-1}(T^{E}_{\Omega}(V)\otimes 1)U\|_{L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2}),\tau\otimes m)}

\displaystyle=\|({\rm id}_{\mathcal{M}}\otimes h(\nabla_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}))(U^{-1}(V\otimes 1)U)\|_{L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2}),\tau\otimes m)}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\rm id}_{\mathcal{M}}\otimes h(\nabla_{\mathbb{T}^{2}})\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2}),\tau\otimes m)\to L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2}),\tau\otimes m)}

\displaystyle\times\|U^{-1}(V\otimes 1)U\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2}),\tau\otimes m)}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\rm id}_{\mathcal{M}}\otimes h(\nabla_{\mathbb{T}^{2}})\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))\to L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\|V\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)}

for every
V\in(L_{1}\cap L_{\infty})(\mathcal{M},\tau).
From
[
6
, Theorem 2.3]
we know that the operator

{\rm id}_{\mathcal{M}}\otimes h(\nabla_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}):L_{1}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2}),\tau\otimes m)\to L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2}),\tau\otimes m)

is bounded. Hence,
T^{E}_{\Omega}
uniquely extends to a bounded operator from
L_{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
to
L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{M},\tau).

∎

Lemma 9
.

For the function
\Omega
defined in (
4.1
), we have that

T^{E}_{\Omega}:L_{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)\to L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{M},\tau)

is bounded
for every spectral measure
E
on
\mathbb{C}.

Proof.

Let
E
be an arbitrary spectral measure on
\mathbb{C}.
Fix
\varepsilon>0
and consider the partition of
\mathbb{C}
into disjoint
\varepsilon

\mathbb{C}=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}Q_{k}^{\varepsilon},\quad Q_{k}^{\varepsilon}:=\varepsilon k+\varepsilon[0,1)^{2}.

We define an approximating discrete spectral measure
E^{\varepsilon}
supported on
\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}
by setting

E^{\varepsilon}(\{\varepsilon k\}):=E(Q_{k}^{\varepsilon}),\quad\text{for all }k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}.

Since
E^{\varepsilon}
is supported on a countable set, by the previous lemma, the double operator integral

T^{E^{\varepsilon}}_{\Omega}(x):=\sum_{z,w\in\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\Omega(z,w)\,E^{\varepsilon}(\{z\})\,x\,E^{\varepsilon}(\{w\})

defines a bounded operator from

to

with a constant independent of

Note that (see e.g.
[

We claim that

Since the

extends to a bounded operator from

to

for arbitrary spectral measure

on
\mathbb{C}.

∎

Lemma 12
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal semifinite trace
\tau
. Let
E
be a symmetric function or sequence space. Then we have

\|{\rm diag}(A)\otimes x\|_{E(B(\ell_{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M},{\rm Tr}\otimes\tau)}\leq\|A\otimes x\|_{E(B(\ell_{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M},{\rm Tr}\otimes\tau)}

for every

and for every
A\in B(\ell_{2})
having only finitely many non-zero matrix elements.

Proof.

Let
A=(a_{k,\ell})_{k,\ell\geq 1}.
Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
a_{k,\ell}=0
if either
k>n
or
\ell>n.

For every
\varepsilon=\{\varepsilon_{1},\cdots,\varepsilon_{n}\}\in\{-1,1\}^{n},
we set

U_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{k}E_{k,k}.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}U_{\varepsilon}AU_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}\Big(\sum_{1\leq k,\ell\leq n}\varepsilon_{k}\varepsilon_{\ell}E_{k,k}AE_{\ell,\ell}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq k,\ell\leq n}\Big(\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}\varepsilon_{k}\varepsilon_{\ell}\Big)E_{k,k}AE_{\ell,\ell}.

For
k\neq\ell,
we have

\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}\varepsilon_{k}\varepsilon_{\ell}=0.

For

we have

\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}\varepsilon_{k}\varepsilon_{\ell}=2^{n}.

Thus,

\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}U_{\varepsilon}AU_{\varepsilon}=2^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}E_{k,k}AE_{k,k}=2^{n}{\rm diag}(A).

Finally, since

is unitary, it follows that

\displaystyle\|{\rm diag}(A)\otimes x\|_{E(B(\ell_{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M},{\rm Tr}\otimes\tau)}

\displaystyle=2^{-n}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}U_{\varepsilon}AU_{\varepsilon}\Big)\otimes x\Big\|_{E(B(\ell_{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M},{\rm Tr}\otimes\tau)}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-n}\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}\|U_{\varepsilon}AU_{\varepsilon}\otimes x\|_{E(B(\ell_{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M},{\rm Tr}\otimes\tau)}

\displaystyle=2^{-n}\cdot 2^{n}\cdot\|A\otimes x\|_{E(B(\ell_{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M},{\rm Tr}\otimes\tau)}

\displaystyle=\|A\otimes x\|_{E(B(\ell_{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M},{\rm Tr}\otimes\tau)},

which completes the proof.

Definition 13

\iota_{n}:M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\to M_{n+1}(\mathbb{C})\otimes M_{n+1}(\mathbb{C})

by the formula

\iota_{n}\Big(\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{n}a_{k,\ell}E_{k,\ell}\Big)=\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{n}a_{k,\ell}E_{1,\ell+1}\otimes E_{k+1,1},\quad A=(a_{k,\ell})_{k,\ell=1}^{n}\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C}).

Lemma 14
.

We have

|\iota_{n}(A)|=U|A|U^{\ast}\otimes E_{1,1},\quad A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C}).

Here,
U=\sum_{k=1}^{n}E_{k+1,k}.

Proof.

Indeed, letting
A=(a_{k,\ell})_{k,\ell=1}^{n}\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, we have

(5.1)

\displaystyle|\iota_{n}(A)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{k_{1},\ell_{1}=1}^{n}\overline{a_{k_{1},\ell_{1}}}E_{\ell_{1}+1,1}\otimes E_{1,k_{1}+1}\Big)

\displaystyle\times\Big(\sum_{k_{2},\ell_{2}=1}^{n}a_{k_{2},\ell_{2}}E_{1,\ell_{2}+1}\otimes E_{k_{2}+1,1}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k_{1},\ell_{1},k_{2},\ell_{2}=1}^{n}\overline{a_{k_{1},\ell_{1}}}a_{k_{2},\ell_{2}}(E_{\ell_{1}+1,1}E_{1,\ell_{2}+1}\otimes E_{1,k_{1}+1}E_{k_{2}+1,1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,\ell_{1},\ell_{2}=1}^{n}\overline{a_{k,\ell_{1}}}a_{k,\ell_{2}}E_{\ell_{1}+1,\ell_{2}+1}\otimes E_{1,1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}=1}^{n}(A^{\ast}A)_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}E_{\ell_{1}+1,\ell_{2}+1}\otimes E_{1,1}=U|A|^{2}U^{\ast}\otimes E_{1,1},

where the latter equality is due to

\displaystyle UAU^{*}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{\ell_{1}=1}^{n}E_{\ell_{1}+1,\ell_{1}}\Big)\cdot\Big(\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{n}a_{k,\ell}E_{k,\ell}\Big)\cdot\Big(\sum_{\ell_{2}=1}^{n}E_{\ell_{2},\ell_{2}+1}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,\ell,\ell_{1},\ell_{2}=1}^{n}a_{k,\ell}E_{\ell_{1}+1,\ell_{1}}E_{k,\ell}E_{\ell_{2},\ell_{2}+1}=\sum_{\ell,\ell_{1},\ell_{2}=1}^{n}a_{\ell_{1},\ell}E_{\ell_{1}+1,\ell}E_{\ell_{2},\ell_{2}+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}=1}^{n}a_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}E_{\ell_{1}+1,\ell_{2}+1}.

Finally, since
U
is a partial isometry such that
U^{*}U=I_{n}
, it follows that

(U|A|U^{\ast})^{2}=U|A|U^{\ast}U|A|U^{\ast}=U|A|^{2}U^{\ast}.

Using also that
E_{1,1}^{2}=E_{1,1}
and taking square root of both sides of (
5.1
), we obtain the asserted identity.
∎

Throughout the rest of this section,
T
denotes the
antisymmetric triangular truncation operator
(defined on finite complex matrices), that is,

T\Big(\sum_{k,\ell\geq 1}a_{k,\ell}E_{k,\ell}\Big)=\sum_{k,\ell\geq 1}{\rm sgn}(k-\ell)a_{k,\ell}E_{k,\ell}.

Lemma 15
.

Let
n\geq 1
. For every
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C}),
there exist sequences
\{A^{n}_{m}\}_{m\geq 1},

\{B_{m}^{n}\}_{m\geq 1}\subset M_{n+1}(\mathbb{C})\otimes M_{n+1}(\mathbb{C})
such that

•

A^{n}_{m}
is normal for every
m\geq 1;

•

[A^{n}_{m},B^{n}_{m}]=\iota_{n}(A-{\rm diag}(A))
for every
m\geq 1;

•

[(A^{n}_{m})^{\ast},B^{n}_{m}]\to\iota_{n}(T(A))
as
m\to\infty.

Proof.

For fixed
n\geq 1

m\geq 1
, we set

A_{m}^{n}=\sum_{1\leq k,\ell\leq n+1}(m^{k}+im^{\ell})E_{k,k}\otimes E_{\ell,\ell},

B_{m}^{n}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq k,\ell\leq n+1\\
k\neq\ell\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(m+im^{\ell}-m^{k}-im)}a_{\ell-1,k-1}E_{1,k}\otimes E_{\ell,1}.

Since
A_{m}^{n}
acts as scalar multiplication on each element of the standard basis
\{e_{k}\otimes e_{\ell}\}
, it is diagonalizable with respect to this basis and hence normal.

In order to prove
(ii)
and
(iii)
, we first find the products

\displaystyle A_{m}^{n}B_{m}^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq k,\ell\leq n+1\\
k\neq\ell\\
1\leq k^{\prime},\ell^{\prime}\leq n+1\end{subarray}}\frac{m^{k^{\prime}}+im^{\ell^{\prime}}}{m+im^{\ell}-m^{k}-im}a_{\ell-1,k-1}E_{k^{\prime},k^{\prime}}E_{1,k}\otimes E_{\ell^{\prime},\ell^{\prime}}E_{\ell,1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq k,\ell\leq n+1\\
k\neq\ell\end{subarray}}\frac{m+im^{\ell}}{m+im^{\ell}-m^{k}-im}a_{\ell-1,k-1}E_{1,k}\otimes E_{\ell,1},

\displaystyle B_{m}^{n}A_{m}^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq k,\ell\leq n+1\\
k\neq\ell\\
1\leq k^{\prime},\ell^{\prime}\leq n+1\end{subarray}}\frac{m^{k^{\prime}}+im^{\ell^{\prime}}}{m+im^{\ell}-m^{k}-im}a_{\ell-1,k-1}E_{1,k}E_{k^{\prime},k^{\prime}}\otimes E_{\ell,1}E_{\ell^{\prime},\ell^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq k,\ell\leq n+1\\
k\neq\ell\end{subarray}}\frac{m^{k}+im}{m+im^{\ell}-m^{k}-im}a_{\ell-1,k-1}E_{1,k}\otimes E_{\ell,1},

\displaystyle(A_{m}^{n})^{\ast}B_{m}^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq k,\ell\leq n+1\\
k\neq\ell\\
1\leq k^{\prime},\ell^{\prime}\leq n+1\end{subarray}}\frac{m^{k^{\prime}}-im^{\ell^{\prime}}}{m+im^{\ell}-m^{k}-im}a_{\ell-1,k-1}E_{k^{\prime},k^{\prime}}E_{1,k}\otimes E_{\ell^{\prime},\ell^{\prime}}E_{\ell,1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq k,\ell\leq n+1\\
k\neq\ell\end{subarray}}\frac{m-im^{\ell}}{m+im^{\ell}-m^{k}-im}a_{\ell-1,k-1}E_{1,k}\otimes E_{\ell,1},

\displaystyle B_{m}^{n}(A_{m}^{n})^{\ast}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq k,\ell\leq n+1\\
k\neq\ell\\
1\leq k^{\prime},\ell^{\prime}\leq n+1\end{subarray}}\frac{m^{k^{\prime}}-im^{\ell^{\prime}}}{m+im^{\ell}-m^{k}-im}a_{\ell-1,k-1}E_{1,k}E_{k^{\prime},k^{\prime}}\otimes E_{\ell,1}E_{\ell^{\prime},\ell^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq k,\ell\leq n+1\\
k\neq\ell\end{subarray}}\frac{m^{k}-im}{m+im^{\ell}-m^{k}-im}a_{\ell-1,k-1}E_{1,k}\otimes E_{\ell,1}.

Thus, we find that (for
\iota_{n}
see Definition
13
)

[A_{m}^{n},B_{m}^{n}]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq k,\ell\leq n+1\\
k\neq\ell\end{subarray}}a_{\ell-1,k-1}E_{1,k}\otimes E_{\ell,1}=\iota_{n}(A-{\rm diag}(A)),

[(A_{m}^{n})^{\ast},B_{m}^{n}]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq k,\ell\leq n+1\\
k\neq\ell\end{subarray}}\frac{m-im^{\ell}-m^{k}+im}{m+im^{\ell}-m^{k}-im}a_{\ell-1,k-1}E_{1,k}\otimes E_{\ell,1}.

Passing

and taking into account that

\frac{m-im^{\ell}-m^{k}+im}{m+im^{\ell}-m^{k}-im}\to{\rm sgn}(k-\ell),\quad m\to\infty,\quad 2\leq k,\ell\leq n+1,\quad k\neq\ell,

which completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 16
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
be a semifinite infinite factor. Let
E
be a symmetric function or sequence space (depending on whether
\mathcal{M}
is II
∞
or I
∞
). If

\|[X^{\ast},Y]\|_{E(\mathcal{M},\tau)}\leq c_{E,\mathcal{M},\tau}\|[X,Y]\|_{E(\mathcal{M},\tau)}

for every normal
X\in E(\mathcal{M},\tau)
and for every

then

\|T(A)\otimes x\|_{E(B(\ell_{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M},{\rm Tr}\otimes\tau)}\leq 2c_{E,\mathcal{M},\tau}\|A\otimes x\|_{E(B(\ell_{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M},{\rm Tr}\otimes\tau)}

for every

and for every

Proof.

We will use the von Neumann factor
(\mathcal{M}_{n},\tau_{n})
with a faithful normal semifinite trace
\tau_{n}
defined as

Since
(\mathcal{M}_{n},\tau_{n})
and
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
are trace-scalingly
*
-isomorphic (with the scale

) and so

for every normal

and for every

Let

Substituting

and

where

and

are constructed in Lemma

, we obtain

[(A_{m}^{n})^{\ast},B_{m}^{n}]\otimes|x|\to\iota_{n}(T(A))\otimes|x|

(5.3)

\|\iota_{n}(T(A))\otimes|x|\|_{E(\mathcal{M}_{n},\tau_{n})}\leq c_{E,\mathcal{M},\tau}\|\iota_{n}(A-{\rm diag}(A))\otimes|x|\|_{E(\mathcal{M}_{n},\tau_{n})}.

\|\iota_{n}(T(A))\otimes|x|\|_{E(\mathcal{M}_{n},\tau_{n})}=\||\iota_{n}(T(A))|\otimes|x|\|_{E(\mathcal{M}_{n},\tau_{n})}\\

Lemma 17
.

Let
E
be a symmetric function or sequence space. If
\beta_{E}=1,
then, for every
n\in\mathbb{N},
there exists
x_{n}\in E
so that

\|\alpha\|_{1}\|x_{n}\|_{E}\leq 2\|\alpha\otimes x_{n}\|_{E},\quad\alpha\in\ell_{\infty}^{n}.

Proof.

Suppose that
\beta_{E}=1
. The mapping
n\mapsto\|D_{n}\|_{E\to E}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, is known to be submultiplicative (
[
24
, Theorem 4.5]
) and dominated by the identity mapping, that is
\|D_{n}\|_{E\to E}\leq n
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
(
[
24
, Corollary 1, p. 98]
).

Assume, towards a contradiction, that
\|D_{n}\|_{E\to E}<n
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then there exists
\gamma<1
such that
\|D_{n}\|_{E\to E}\leq n^{\gamma}.

Using submultiplicativity, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
we obtain

\|D_{n^{k}}\|_{E\to E}\leq\|D_{n}\|_{E\to E}^{k}\leq(n^{\gamma})^{k}=(n^{k})^{\gamma}.

This shows that

\frac{\log\|D_{n^{k}}\|_{E\to E}}{\log n^{k}}\leq\gamma\quad\text{for all }k.

Taking the limit as
k\to\infty
, we obtain:

\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{\log\|D_{m}\|_{E\to E}}{\log m}\leq\gamma<1,

which contradicts the assumption
\beta_{E}=1
. Hence, we conclude that

\|D_{n}\|_{E\to E}=n\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Next for
n\in\mathbb{N},
select
x_{n}\in E
such that
\|D_{n}x_{n}\|_{E}\geq\frac{n}{2}\|x_{n}\|_{E}.

Let
\alpha\in\ell_{\infty}^{n}.
Set
\alpha_{k}\in\ell_{\infty}^{n},

1\leq k\leq n,
be sequences supported on the set
\{1,\cdots,n\}
which are cyclic permutations of
\mu(\alpha)
, that is

\alpha_{k}(j)=\mu((j-k){\rm mod}\,n+1,\alpha),\quad 1\leq j\leq n.

By triangle inequality, we have

n\|\alpha\otimes x_{n}\|_{E}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\alpha_{k}\otimes x_{n}\|_{E}\geq\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}\otimes x_{n}\Big\|_{E}.

However,

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}=\big(\underbrace{\|\alpha\|_{1},\dots,\|\alpha\|_{1}}_{n\text{ times}}\big)=\|\alpha\|_{1}\big(\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{n\text{ times}}\big).

Hence,

n\|\alpha\otimes x_{n}\|_{E}\geq\|\alpha\|_{1}\|\big(\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{n\text{ times}}\big)\otimes x_{n}\|_{E}=\|\alpha\|_{1}\|D_{n}x_{n}\|_{E}\geq\frac{n}{2}\|\alpha\|_{1}\|x_{n}\|_{E}.

Dividing by
n,
we complete the proof.
∎

Lemma 18
.

Let
E
be a symmetric function or sequence space. If
\alpha_{E}=0,
then, for every
n\in\mathbb{N},
there exists
x_{n}\in E
so that

\|\alpha\otimes x_{n}\|_{E}\leq 2\|\alpha\|_{\infty}\|x_{n}\|_{E},\quad\alpha\in\ell_{\infty}^{n}.

Proof.

Suppose that
\alpha_{E}=0
. The mapping
n\mapsto\|D_{1/n}\|_{E\to E}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, is known to be submultiplicative (
[
24
, Theorem 4.5]
). Setting
\omega_{n}=\|D_{1/n}\|_{E\to E}
, we have that if
\omega_{n}<1
for some
n,
it follows that
\omega_{n}=O(n^{-\gamma})
for some
\gamma>0.

Indeed, let
\omega_{n_{0}}<1
for some
n_{0}\geq 2
. Taking an arbitrary
n\geq 1
, we denote
k=\lfloor\frac{\ln n}{\ln n_{0}}\rfloor
so that
n_{0}^{k}\leq n<n_{0}^{k+1}
. For a function space, take
r=\frac{n}{n_{0}^{k}}
with
1\leq r<n_{0}
, we obtain

\omega_{n}=\omega_{n_{0}^{k}r}\leq\omega_{n_{0}^{k}}\omega_{r}\leq C_{0}\omega_{n_{0}^{k}},

where
C_{0}=\max_{1\leq r<n_{0}}\omega_{r}
. On the other hand,

\omega_{n_{0}^{k}}\leq\omega_{n_{0}}^{k}\leq(n_{0}^{k})^{-\gamma}=\Big(\frac{n}{r}\Big)^{-\gamma}=r^{\gamma}n^{-\gamma}<n_{0}^{\gamma}n^{-\gamma},

where
\gamma=-\ln\omega_{n_{0}}/\ln n_{0}>0.
Taking now
C=C_{0}n_{0}^{\gamma}
we conclude that
\omega_{n}\leq Cn^{-\gamma},
for
\gamma>0
or
\omega_{n}=O(n^{-\gamma}).
For a sequence space, the argument is similar, due to the natural identification of a sequence space with the subspace of characteristic functions in a function space.

Thus, we have that

\alpha_{E}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\omega_{n}}{\log(1/n)}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\log\omega_{n}}{\log n}\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\log Cn^{-\gamma}}{\log n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big(\gamma-\frac{\log C}{\log n}\Big)>0,

which contradicts the assumption
\alpha_{E}=0.
Hence,
\omega_{n}=\|D_{1/n}\|_{E\to E}\geq 1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}.

Since

\|D_{1/n}\|_{E\to E}=\sup_{\|y\|_{E}=1}\|D_{1/n}y\|_{E}\geq 1>\frac{1}{2},

it follows that we can select
y_{n}\in E
with
\|y_{n}\|_{E}=1
and
\|D_{1/n}y_{n}\|_{E}\geq\frac{1}{2}.
Let
x_{n}=D_{1/n}y_{n}
. In particular, we have

D_{n}x_{n}=D_{n}D_{1/n}y_{n}=y_{n}

and so
\|D_{n}x_{n}\|_{E}=\|y_{n}\|_{E}=1.
On the other hand,

\|x_{n}\|_{E}=\|D_{1/n}y_{n}\|_{E}\geq\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{2}\|D_{n}x_{n}\|_{E}.

Hence, for
n\in\mathbb{N},
we can select
x_{n}\in E
such that
\|D_{n}x_{n}\|_{E}\leq 2\|x_{n}\|_{E}.

Now consider
\alpha\in\ell_{\infty}^{n}
.
Since

\mu(\alpha)\leq\|\alpha\|_{\infty}\big(\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{n\text{ times}}\big),

it follows that

\mu(\alpha\otimes x_{n})\leq\|\alpha\|_{\infty}\mu\Big(\big(\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{n\text{ times}}\big)\otimes x_{n}\Big)=\|\alpha\|_{\infty}D_{n}\mu(x_{n}).

Taking
\|\cdot\|_{E}
of the both sides of the inequality above, it follows that

\|\alpha\otimes x_{n}\|_{E}\leq\|\alpha\|_{\infty}\|D_{n}x_{n}\|_{E}\leq 2\|\alpha\|_{\infty}\|x_{n}\|_{.}

∎

Lemma 19
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\tau)

\mathcal{M}
is II
∞

yields

\|T(A)\|_{B(\ell_{2})}\|x_{n}\|_{E(\mathcal{M},\tau)}\leq\|T(A)\otimes x_{n}\|_{E(B(\ell_{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M},{\rm Tr}\otimes\tau)}\leq

\leq 2c_{E,\mathcal{M},\tau}\|A\otimes x_{n}\|_{E(B(\ell_{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M},{\rm Tr}\otimes\tau)}\leq 4c_{E,\mathcal{M},\tau}\|A\|_{B(\ell_{2})}\|x_{n}\|_{E(\mathcal{M},\tau)}.

It follows that

\|T(A)\|_{B(\ell_{2})}\leq 4c_{E,\mathcal{M},\tau}\|A\|_{B(\ell_{2})},\quad A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C}).

This contradicts the result of Davies
[
9
, Lemma 10 and Corollary 11]
,
where he constructed finite-dimensional matrices
A_{n}\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
such that

\|T(A_{n})\|_{1}\sim\log n
. By duality argument no uniform bound of the form

\|T(A)\|_{B(\ell_{2})}\leq C\|A\|_{B(\ell_{2})}
with
C
independent of
n
can hold (see also
[
8
, Chapter 4]
). Hence, our initial assumption is false. We conclude that
\alpha_{E}>0.

∎

Lemma 21
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a finite factor equipped with a faithful normal tracial state
\tau.
Let
E
be a symmetric function space on
(0,1).
If

\|[X^{\ast},Y]\|_{E(\mathcal{M},\tau)}\leq c_{E,\mathcal{M},\tau}\|[X,Y]\|_{E(\mathcal{M},\tau)}

for every normal
X\in E(\mathcal{M},\tau)
and for every
Y\in\mathcal{M},
then

\|T(A)\otimes x\|_{E_{(n+1)^{2}}(M_{(n+1)^{2}}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathcal{M},{\rm Tr}\otimes\tau)}\leq

\leq 2c_{E,\mathcal{M},\tau}\|A\otimes x\|_{E_{(n+1)^{2}}(M_{(n+1)^{2}}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathcal{M},{\rm Tr}\otimes\tau)}.

for every
x\in E(\mathcal{M},\tau)
and for every
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C}).

Proof.

In what follows, we need a probability space

(\mathcal{M}_{n}^{\prime},\tau_{n}^{\prime})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle def}}{{=}}(M_{n+1}(\mathbb{C})\otimes M_{n+1}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathcal{M},(n+1)^{-2}{\rm Tr}\otimes{\rm Tr}\otimes\tau)

and a non-commutative measure space

(\mathcal{M}_{n},\tau_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle def}}{{=}}(M_{n+1}(\mathbb{C})\otimes M_{n+1}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathcal{M},{\rm Tr}\otimes{\rm Tr}\otimes\tau).

Since the non-commutative measure spaces
(\mathcal{M}_{n}^{\prime},\tau_{n}^{\prime})
and
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
are
*
-isomorphic via the
identity on the underlying
*
-algebra (
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{n}=\mathcal{M}_{n}
),
while the traces differ by a constant factor
\tau^{\prime}_{n}=(n+1)^{-2}\,\tau_{n}.
, it follows from the assumption that

\|[X^{\ast},Y]\|_{E_{(n+1)^{2}}(\mathcal{M}_{n},\tau_{n})}=\|[X^{\ast},Y]\|_{E(\mathcal{M}_{n}^{\prime},\tau_{n}^{\prime})}\leq

\leq c_{E,\mathcal{M},\tau}\|[X,Y]\|_{E(\mathcal{M}_{n}^{\prime},\tau_{n}^{\prime})}=c_{E,\mathcal{M},\tau}\|[X,Y]\|_{E_{(n+1)^{2}}(\mathcal{M}_{n},\tau_{n})}

for every normal
X\in E_{(n+1)^{2}}(\mathcal{M}_{n},\tau_{n})
and for every
Y\in\mathcal{M}_{n}.

Substituting
X=A_{m}^{n}\otimes|x|
and
Y=B_{m}^{n}\otimes 1,
where
A_{m}^{n}
and
B_{m}^{n}
are constructed in Lemma
15
, we obtain

\|[(A_{m}^{n})^{\ast},B_{m}^{n}]\otimes|x|\|_{E_{(n+1)^{2}}(\mathcal{M}_{n},\tau_{n})}\leq c_{E,\mathcal{M},\tau}\|[A_{m}^{n},B_{m}^{n}]\otimes|x|\|_{E_{(n+1)^{2}}(\mathcal{M}_{n},\tau_{n})}.

Using Lemma
15
and passing
m\to\infty,
we infer

\|\iota_{n}(T(A))\otimes|x|\|_{E_{(n+1)^{2}}(\mathcal{M}_{n},\tau_{n})}\leq c_{E,\mathcal{M},\tau}\|\iota_{n}(A-{\rm diag}(A))\otimes|x|\|_{E_{(n+1)^{2}}(\mathcal{M}_{n},\tau_{n})}.

Using Lemma
14
, we write

\displaystyle\|\iota_{n}(T(A))\otimes|x|\|_{E_{(n+1)^{2}}(\mathcal{M}_{n},\tau_{n})}

\displaystyle=\||\iota_{n}(T(A))|\otimes|x|\|_{E_{(n+1)^{2}}(\mathcal{M}_{n},\tau_{n})}

\displaystyle=\|U|T(A)|U^{\ast}\otimes E_{1,1}\otimes|x|\|_{E_{(n+1)^{2}}(\mathcal{M}_{n},\tau_{n})}

\displaystyle=\|T(A)\otimes x\|_{E_{(n+1)^{2}}(M_{(n+1)^{2}}(\mathbb{C})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M},{\rm Tr}\otimes\tau)}.

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\|\iota_{n}(A-{\rm diag}(A))

\displaystyle\otimes|x|\|_{E_{(n+1)^{2}}(\mathcal{M}_{n},\tau_{n})}

\displaystyle=\||\iota_{n}(A-{\rm diag}(A))|\otimes|x|\|_{E_{(n+1)^{2}}(\mathcal{M}_{n},\tau_{n})}

\displaystyle=\|U|A-{\rm diag}(A)|U^{\ast}\otimes E_{1,1}\otimes|x|\|_{E_{(n+1)^{2}}(\mathcal{M}_{n},\tau_{n})}

\displaystyle=\|(A-{\rm diag}(A))\otimes x\|_{E_{(n+1)^{2}}(M_{(n+1)^{2}}(\mathbb{C})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M},{\rm Tr}\otimes\tau)}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle L.\ref{peter lemma}}}{{\leq}}2\|A\otimes x\|_{E_{(n+1)^{2}}(M_{(n+1)^{2}}(\mathbb{C})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M},{\rm Tr}\otimes\tau)}.

Combining the last three displays, we complete the proof.
∎

A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
,
by
{\rm supp}(A)
we denote
the orthogonal projection onto
{\rm Ran}A
.
For a general
B\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})

Lemma 22
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a finite factor equipped with a faithful normal tracial state
\tau.
Let
F
be a symmetric function space on
(0,m).
For every
B\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C})
and for every
x\in F(\mathcal{M},\tau)
we have

(i)

\displaystyle\|B\|_{M_{m}(\mathbb{C})}\|x\|_{F(\mathcal{M},\tau)}\leq\|B\otimes x\|_{F(M_{m}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathcal{M},{\rm Tr}\otimes\tau)},

(ii)

\displaystyle{\|B\|_{L_{1}(M_{m}(\mathbb{C}),{\rm Tr})}}\|{\rm supp}(B)\otimes x\|_{F(M_{m}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathcal{M},{\rm Tr}\otimes\tau)}

\displaystyle\leq\|B\otimes x\|_{F(M_{m}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathcal{M},{\rm Tr}\otimes\tau)}{{\rm Tr}({\rm supp}(B))},

(iii)

\displaystyle\|B\otimes x\|_{F(M_{m}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathcal{M},{\rm Tr}\otimes\tau)}\leq\|B\|_{L_{1}(M_{m}(\mathbb{C}),{\rm Tr})}\|x\|_{F(\mathcal{M},\tau)},

(iv)

\displaystyle\|B\otimes x\|_{F(M_{m}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathcal{M},{\rm Tr}\otimes\tau)}\leq\|B\|_{M_{m}(\mathbb{C})}\|1\otimes x\|_{F(\mathbb{C}^{m}\otimes\mathcal{M},\Sigma\otimes\tau)}.

Proof.

Let
B\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C})
. Recall that

\mu(B)=\big(\mu(1,B),\mu(2,B),\dots,\mu(m,B)\big)

is a sequence the singular numbers of
B
in non-increasing order.
Fix
x\in F(\mathcal{M},\tau)
and recall that
\Sigma
is the counting measure on
\{1,\dots,m\}
.

(i).
Since the projection on the first coordinate is a positive contraction and
\mu(1,B)
is the largest singular number, that is
\mu(1,B)=\|B\|_{M_{m}(\mathbb{C})}
, we have

\|B\otimes x\|_{F(M_{m}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathcal{M},{\rm Tr}\otimes\tau)}=\|\mu(B)\otimes x\|_{F(\mathbb{C}^{m}\otimes\mathcal{M},\Sigma\otimes\tau)}\\
\geq\|\mu(1,B)e_{1}\otimes x\|_{F(\mathbb{C}^{m}\otimes\mathcal{M},\Sigma\otimes\tau)}=\|B\|_{M_{m}(\mathbb{C})}\|x\|_{F(\mathcal{M},\tau)},

which proves the first assertion.

(ii).
Denote
n={\rm Tr}({\rm supp}(B)).
Then
\mu(B)=\big(\mu(1,B),\dots,\mu(n,B),0,\ldots,0\big)
and
n\leq m
. Let
\alpha_{k},

1\leq k\leq n,
be sequences supported on the set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
which are cyclic permutations of the first
n
coordinates of
\mu(B),
that is

\alpha_{k}(j)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rr}\mu(((j-k){\bmod}\,n)+1,B),&\quad 1\leq j\leq n\\
0,&\quad n+1\leq j\leq m\end{array}\right..

We have that

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}=(\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{n\text{ times}},0,\ldots,0)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu(i,B)=\|B\|_{L_{1}(M_{m}(\mathbb{C}),{\rm Tr})}{\rm supp}(B).

Hence,

n\|B\otimes x\|_{F(M_{m}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathcal{M},{\rm Tr}\otimes\tau)}=n\|\mu(B)\otimes x\|_{F(\mathbb{C}^{m}\otimes\mathcal{M},\Sigma\otimes\tau)}\\
=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\alpha_{k}\otimes x\|_{F(\mathbb{C}^{m}\otimes\mathcal{M},\Sigma\otimes\tau)}\geq\big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}\otimes x\big\|_{F(\mathbb{C}^{m}\otimes\mathcal{M},\Sigma\otimes\tau)}\\
=\|B\|_{L_{1}(M_{m}(\mathbb{C}),{\rm Tr})}\big\|{\rm supp}(B)\otimes x\big\|_{F(\mathbb{C}^{m}\otimes\mathcal{M},\Sigma\otimes\tau)}.

So we infer the second assertion.

(iii). Similarly,

\displaystyle\|B\otimes x\|_{F(M_{m}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathcal{M},{\rm Tr}\otimes\tau)}

\displaystyle=\|\mu(B)\otimes x\|_{F(\mathbb{C}^{m}\otimes\mathcal{M},\Sigma\otimes\tau)}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{m}\mu(k,B)e_{k}\otimes x\Big\|_{F(\mathbb{C}^{m}\otimes\mathcal{M},\Sigma\otimes\tau)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{m}\mu(k,B)\|e_{k}\otimes x\|_{F(\mathbb{C}^{m}\otimes\mathcal{M},\Sigma\otimes\tau)}

\displaystyle=\|B\|_{L_{1}(M_{m}(\mathbb{C}),{\rm Tr})}\|x\|_{F(\mathcal{M},\tau)},

which proves the third assertion.

(iv). Finally,

\|B\otimes x\|_{F(M_{m}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathcal{M},{\rm Tr}\otimes\tau)}=\|\mu(B)\otimes x\|_{F(\mathbb{C}^{m}\otimes\mathcal{M},\Sigma\otimes\tau)}\\
\leq\mu(1,B)\|1\otimes x\|_{F(\mathbb{C}^{m}\otimes\mathcal{M},\Sigma\otimes\tau)}=\|B\|_{M_{m}(\mathbb{C})}\|1\otimes x\|_{F(\mathbb{C}^{m}\otimes\mathcal{M},\Sigma\otimes\tau)}.

This proves the fourth assertion.
∎

Lemma 23
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a finite factor equipped with a faithful normal tracial state
\tau
. Let
E
be a symmetric function space on
(0,1).
If

\|T(A)\otimes x\|_{E_{(n+1)^{2}}(M_{(n+1)^{2}}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathcal{M},{\rm Tr}\otimes\tau)}\\
\leq 2c_{E,\mathcal{M},\tau}\|A\otimes x\|_{E_{(n+1)^{2}}(M_{(n+1)^{2}}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathcal{M},{\rm Tr}\otimes\tau)}.

for every
x\in E(\mathcal{M},\tau)
and
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C}),
then

\|T\|_{L_{1}(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}),{\rm Tr})\circlearrowleft}\|D_{(n+1)^{2}}\|_{E\circlearrowleft}\leq 4n(n+1)c_{E,\mathcal{M},\tau}.

Proof.

Fix
f\in E(0,1)
supported on
(0,(n+1)^{-2})
. In this case
D_{(n+1)^{2}}f\in E(0,1)
. So choose
x\in E(\mathcal{M},\tau)
such that
D_{(n+1)^{2}}\mu(f)=\mu(x).
Let now
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
and
r(A)={\rm Tr}({\rm supp}(T(A)))\leq n
. We will assume that
r(A)\geq 1
. We have that

\displaystyle\|T(A)

\displaystyle\|_{L_{1}(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}),{\rm Tr})}\|D_{r(A)}f\|_{E}

\displaystyle=\|T(A)\|_{L_{1}(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}),{\rm Tr})}\|{\rm supp}(T(A))\otimes x\|_{E_{(n+1)^{2}}(M_{(n+1)^{2}}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathcal{M},{\rm Tr}\otimes\tau)}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle L.\ref{tensor fact},{\rm(ii)}}}{{\leq}}r(A)\|T(A)\otimes x\|_{E_{(n+1)^{2}}(M_{(n+1)^{2}}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathcal{M},{\rm Tr}\otimes\tau)}

\displaystyle\leq 2r(A)c_{E,\mathcal{M},\tau}\|A\otimes x\|_{E_{(n+1)^{2}}(M_{(n+1)^{2}}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathcal{M},{\rm Tr}\otimes\tau)}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle L.\ref{tensor fact},{\rm(iii)}}}{{\leq}}2r(A)c_{E,\mathcal{M},\tau}\|A\|_{L_{1}(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}),{\rm Tr})}\|x\|_{E_{(n+1)^{2}}(\mathcal{M},\tau)}

\displaystyle=2r(A)c_{E,\mathcal{M},\tau}\|A\|_{L_{1}(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}),{\rm Tr})}\|f\|_{E}.

Taking into account that

\|D_{(n+1)^{2}}f\|_{E}\leq(n+1)\|D_{n+1}f\|_{E}\leq(n+1)\|D_{2n}f\|_{E}\leq 2(n+1)\|D_{n}f\|_{E}\\
=2(n+1)\|D_{\frac{n}{r(A)}}D_{r(A)}f\|_{E}\leq\frac{2n(n+1)}{r(A)}\|D_{r(A)}f\|_{E},

we obtain

\displaystyle\|T(A)\|_{L_{1}(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}),{\rm Tr})}\|D_{(n+1)^{2}}f\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2n(n+1)}{r(A)}\|T(A)\|_{L_{1}(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}),{\rm Tr})}\|D_{r(A)}f\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2n(n+1)}{r(A)}2r(A)c_{E,\mathcal{M},\tau}\|A\|_{L_{1}(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}),{\rm Tr})}\|f\|_{E}

\displaystyle=4n(n+1)c_{E,\mathcal{M},\tau}\|A\|_{L_{1}(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}),{\rm Tr})}\|f\|_{E}.

Taking the supremum over
A\in L_{1}(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}),{\rm Tr}),
we write

\|T\|_{L_{1}(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}),{\rm Tr})\circlearrowleft}\|D_{(n+1)^{2}}f\|_{E}\leq 4n(n+1)c_{E,\mathcal{M},\tau}\|f\|_{E}

for every
f\in E(0,1)
supported on
(0,(n+1)^{-2}).

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 25
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a finite factor equipped with a faithful normal tracial state
\tau.
Let
E
be a symmetric function space on
(0,1).
Assume that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(6.2)

\|T\|_{M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\circlearrowleft}\|D_{(n+1)^{-2}}\|_{E\circlearrowleft}\leq 2c_{E,\mathcal{M},\tau},\\
\|T\|_{L_{1}(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}),{\rm Tr})\circlearrowleft}\|D_{(n+1)^{2}}\|_{E\circlearrowleft}\leq 4n(n+1)c_{E,\mathcal{M},\tau},

then
\alpha_{E}>0
and
\beta_{E}<1.

Proof.

By Davies
[
9
, Lemma 10 and Corollary 11]
, we have that
\|T\|_{L_{1}(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}),{\rm Tr})\circlearrowleft}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. The duality argument shows that
\|T\|_{M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\circlearrowleft}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
(see
[
8
, Chapter 4]
).

From the given product bounds (
6.2
) we deduce

\|D_{(n+1)^{-2}}\|_{E\circlearrowleft}\ =\ o(1),\qquad\|D_{(n+1)^{2}}\|_{E\circlearrowleft}\ =\ o\big((n+1)^{2}\big),\qquad n\to\infty.

In particular, there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\|D_{(n_{0}+1)^{-2}}\|_{E\circlearrowleft}<1,\qquad\|D_{(n_{0}+1)^{2}}\|_{E\circlearrowleft}<(n_{0}+1)^{2}.

Set
m_{0}:=(n_{0}+1)^{2}>1
. Then

\|D_{m_{0}^{-1}}\|_{E\circlearrowleft}<1,\qquad\|D_{m_{0}}\|_{E\circlearrowleft}<m_{0}.

Consequently, we can choose numbers
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1)
such that

\|D_{m_{0}^{-1}}\|_{E\circlearrowleft}\ \leq\ m_{0}^{-\alpha},\qquad\|D_{m_{0}}\|_{E\circlearrowleft}\ \leq\ m_{0}^{\beta}.

It follows that for every integer
k
,

\|D_{m_{0}^{k}}\|_{E\circlearrowleft}\ \leq\ \begin{cases}m_{0}^{k\beta},&k\geq 0,\\[2.84526pt]
m_{0}^{k\alpha},&k\leq 0.\end{cases}

Now fix
s>0
and choose
k\in\mathbb{Z}
with
s\in[m_{0}^{k},m_{0}^{k+1}]
. If
s\geq 1
(hence
k\geq 0
), then by monotonicity,

\|D_{s}\|_{E\circlearrowleft}\ \leq\ \|D_{m_{0}^{k+1}}\|_{E\circlearrowleft}\ \leq\ m_{0}^{(k+1)\beta}\ \leq\ m_{0}^{\beta}\,s^{\beta}.

If
0<s\leq 1
(hence
k+1\leq 0
), then similarly

\|D_{s}\|_{E\circlearrowleft}\ \leq\ \|D_{m_{0}^{k+1}}\|_{E\circlearrowleft}\ \leq\ m_{0}^{(k+1)\alpha}\ \leq\ m_{0}^{\alpha}\,s^{\alpha}.

From the definition of the Boyd indices these bounds imply

\alpha_{E}\geq\alpha>0
and
\beta_{E}\leq\beta<1
. Hence
\alpha_{E}>0
and
\beta_{E}<1
, as claimed.
∎

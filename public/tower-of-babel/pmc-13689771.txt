Case summary

Patients with systemic sclerosis exhibit increased vascular reactivity related to endothelial dysfunction and autonomic imbalance. During peripheral interventions, vasospasm refractory to intra-arterial vasodilators may require mechanism-specific strategies. We present angiographic comparisons of vasospasm associated with pain and cold exposure, demonstrating differential responses to interventions.

Case 1

A 70-year-old woman with limited cutaneous systemic sclerosis and chronic limb-threatening ischemia with a nonhealing wound underwent repeat posterior tibial artery angioplasty for restenosis. At the completion of the previous intervention, plantar perfusion was satisfactory (Figure 1A). During the procedure, balloon dilation was followed by severe vasospasm in plantar arteries beyond the treated segment (Figure 1B, Video 1) despite repeated intra-arterial nitroglycerin, prostaglandin E1, and nitroprusside. The patient reported pronounced contrast-associated pain, which resolved completely after popliteal sciatic nerve block. Subsequent angiography demonstrated immediate flow restoration (Figure 1C, Video 2), consistent with pain-associated sympathetic vasospasm. Case 2

A 60-year-old woman with diffuse systemic sclerosis and chronic limb-threatening ischemia with a nonhealing wound underwent staged tibial interventions. At the completion of a prior summer intervention, distal perfusion was well preserved (Figure 1D). Four months later, during a winter intervention under effective sciatic nerve block, diffuse vasospasm developed in distal vessels beyond the treated segment (Figure 1E, Video 3) despite repeated intra-arterial vasodilators. Active limb warming led to gradual angiographic improvement (Figure 1F, Video 4), consistent with cold-associated vasospasm independent of pain-mediated sympathetic activation. Subsequent wound healing was achieved with continued active daily limb warming.

In both cases, pharmacologic vasodilation was insufficient, suggesting heterogeneous mechanisms of vasospasm during endovascular intervention. Procedure-related vasospasm caused by catheter or device manipulation is commonly recognized and typically presents as focal narrowing near the treated segment with responsiveness to local vasodilators. In contrast, vasospasm extended beyond the manipulated segment and involved distal vessels, suggesting mechanisms other than simple procedure-related vasospasm. Pain-associated vasospasm results from nociceptive reflex–mediated sympathetic activation. In our first case, vasospasm correlated with severe procedural pain and rapidly resolved after regional nerve blockade.1,2 Cold-associated vasospasm likely reflects temperature-dependent vascular hyperreactivity related to endothelial dysfunction. In the second case, vasospasm occurred under cold conditions and persisted despite effective nerve blockade.3

Distinguishing these mechanisms by angiography alone may be difficult. Although procedure-related vasospasm often appears focal, neurogenic or cold-associated vasospasm may extend more diffusely into distal vessels. However, angiographic patterns may overlap, and multiple mechanisms may coexist in individual patients. Clinical context provides important clues. Pain-associated vasospasm typically occurs during painful procedural stimulation and improves with analgesia or nerve blockade, whereas cold-associated vasospasm may be suspected in patients with cold-induced digital color changes or Raynaud-like symptoms and may occur despite adequate analgesia.

These observations suggest that pain-associated sympathetic activation and cold-related vascular hyperreactivity may represent underrecognized mechanisms of vasodilator-resistant vasospasm during peripheral intervention. Although these cases occurred in patients with systemic sclerosis, similar mechanisms may also contribute to vasospasm during peripheral interventions in other clinical settings. When pharmacologic vasodilation fails, recognizing the predominant trigger may guide alternative strategies such as regional nerve blockade or thermal modulation.

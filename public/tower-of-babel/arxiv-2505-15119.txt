Abstract
. We prove the interior and global Lipschitz regularity results for a solution of fully nonlinear equations with ( p , q ) \displaystyle(p,q) -growth. We prove that for a small gap q − p \displaystyle q-p , a solution is locally or globally Lipschitz continuous. We also prove that a given Hölder continuous solution is Lipschitz continuous under improved bounds for the gap. These gap conditions are similar to those required for the regularity of double phase problems in divergence form as in [ 12 , 6 ] .

Introduction

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lambda|z|^{p}\left\lVert N\right\rVert\leq F(M+N,z,x)-F(M,z,x)\leq\Lambda(|z|^{p}+|z|^{q})\left\lVert N\right\rVert

From a variational point of view, the regularity of elliptic equations with
\displaystyle(p,q)
-growth condition has gained significant attention since Marcellini’s pioneering works
[
28
,
29

(1.3)

\displaystyle\displaystyle w\rightarrow\int F(x,Dw)dx

with
\displaystyle F
having
\displaystyle(p,q)
-growth condition,

\displaystyle\displaystyle|z|^{p}\lesssim F(x,z)\lesssim|z|^{q}+1,\quad 1<p\leq q.

Then it is nonuniformly elliptic since the ellipticity ratio of
\displaystyle F
, which is given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{F}(z):=\frac{\text{highest eigenvalue of }\partial_{zz}F(z)}{\text{lowest eigenvalue of }\partial_{zz}F(z)},

behaves with the polynomial growth as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{F}(z)\lesssim 1+|z|^{q-p},

causing the ratio to blow up when
\displaystyle z\rightarrow\infty
.
If the difference between
\displaystyle p
and
\displaystyle q

[
22

30

Especially, a notable example of
\displaystyle(p,q)
-growth problem is the so-called double phase problem first introduced by Zhikov in
[

\displaystyle\displaystyle w\rightarrow\int|Dw|^{p}+a(x)|Dw|^{q}dx

where
\displaystyle a(x)\geq 0
is
\displaystyle\alpha

Columbo and Mingione first proved the regularity results for minimizers
\displaystyle u\in W^{1,p}
in
[

\displaystyle C^{\beta}

[

Furthermore, these results are extended for orlicz growth as in
[

23

9

The interesting aspect of this problem is that in order to get the desired regularity, the the gap between
\displaystyle p
and
\displaystyle q

\displaystyle a(x)

Moreover, if
\displaystyle u

Precisely, the sharp conditions of the gap are

(1.5)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{q}{p}\leq 1+\frac{\alpha}{n}\qquad

\displaystyle\displaystyle\text{ if }u\in W^{1,p},

(1.6)

\displaystyle\displaystyle q-p\leq\alpha\qquad

\displaystyle\displaystyle\text{ if }u\in L^{\infty}\cap W^{1,p},

(1.7)

\displaystyle\displaystyle q-p<\frac{\alpha}{1-\beta}\qquad

\displaystyle\displaystyle\text{ if }u\in C^{\beta}\cap W^{1,p}.

If the gap of
\displaystyle p,q

[

Recently, for the general

-growth problem (

Therefore, it seems that the above conditions for the double phase problems are good candidates for the gap conditions of the

-growth problem under consideration in the present paper.

With the same spirit mentioned before in the divergence structure, we are considering the nondivergent counterpart of

-growth problem (

We give some remarks on the above theorems.

•

When
\displaystyle u\in L^{\infty}
, the
both gaps for the interior case (
1.11
) and for the global case (
1.13
) are the same with the optimal gap (
1.6
) for the double phase problem.
However, when
\displaystyle u\in C^{\beta}
, the gap for the interior case (
1.12
) is smaller than for the global case (
1.14
), which is same with the optimal gap (
1.7
) for the double phase problem.
The only difference is the presence of the term ‘
\displaystyle 1+\frac{\beta}{2(1-\beta)}
’ in (

For the global case, by finding a suitable barrier function for the optimal gap and using the global Ishii-Lions method, we get the regularity result with the optimal gap condition (
1.14

•

The two gaps, (
1.12
) and (
1.14
), for
\displaystyle u\in C^{\beta}
can be interpreted as interpolative bounds.
Observe that for both bounds, if
\displaystyle\beta\rightarrow 0
, then they reduce to
\displaystyle q-p<\alpha
, while if
\displaystyle\beta\rightarrow 1
, then neither of them is necessary.
The latter implies that when the Hölder continuity of
\displaystyle u
approaches the Lipschitz continuity, then the chance of blowing up of ellipticity ratio becomes lower and the equation is closer to being uniformly elliptic.

•

If
\displaystyle F

\displaystyle\alpha=1

We also remark other type of a fully nonlinear model of the double phase problem first introduced by De Filippis in
[
16
]
, which is the form of

(1.15)

\displaystyle\displaystyle(|Du|^{p}+a(x)|Du|^{q})F(D^{2}u)=f(x),

where
\displaystyle F
is uniformly elliptic and
\displaystyle a(x)\geq 0

Note that unlike the double phase problem in the variational sense, a viscosity solution of (

) has
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity for small
\displaystyle\alpha>0
, no matter how large the gap between
\displaystyle p
and
\displaystyle q
is and we even don’t need the Hölder continuity of
\displaystyle a(x)
.
This is because the model is the product of the ‘gradient’
\displaystyle(|Du|^{p}+a(x)|Du|^{q})

\displaystyle F(D^{2}u)

If we consider the limiting PDE when the right-hand side

[

\displaystyle F(D^{2}u)=0

\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
regularity.
Moreover, when
\displaystyle|Du|>1
, then the solution satisfies
\displaystyle F(D^{2}u)=\frac{f}{|Du|^{p}+a(x)|Du|^{q}}\in L^{\infty}
, which is uniformly elliptic, so that we use the Ishii-Lions method or
[

31

to get the regularity results.
Since one can divide or delete the ‘gradient’ part regardless of its structure, any regularity condition on
\displaystyle p,q
and
\displaystyle a(x)
is not needed.
Using this idea, there have been lots of regularity results regarding degenerate/singular fully nonlinear problems in the form of the product including (
1.15
), see
[
7
,
4
,
2
]
and the reference therein.
On the other hand, the double phase problem in variational sense is not necessarily of the product form, and so the conditions on
\displaystyle p
,
\displaystyle q
and
\displaystyle a(x)
are essentially required to get the desired regularity results.
Therefore, in order to accurately understand this feature of the double phase problem in fully nonlinear setting, we consider the model (

).
If
\displaystyle F\neq G
, then it cannot be represented in the product form and we cannot use the same method mentioned above for (
1.15
).

and its
\displaystyle C^{2}
norm is controlled by
\displaystyle\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{C^{1,1}(B_{1}\cap\partial\Omega)}
.
The main goal is to construct an upper barrier
\displaystyle w
which satisfies

\displaystyle\displaystyle|Dw|^{p}\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\lambda,\Lambda(1+|Dw|^{\gamma})}(D^{2}w)<-C_{f}(1+|Dw|^{q+1})<f(x,Dw)\quad\text{in }B_{r}\cap\Omega_{\delta}.

We define
\displaystyle w\in C^{2}(B_{1}\cap\Omega_{\delta})
as

\displaystyle\displaystyle w(y)=\overline{w}(y)+g(y).

where (see
[
8
]
)

(4.1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\overline{w}(y)=\begin{cases}\frac{4}{\delta^{1-\beta}}\frac{d(y)}{1+d(y)^{\gamma_{0}}}&\text{for $\displaystyle|y|<1/2$},\\
\frac{4}{\delta^{1-\beta}}\frac{d(y)}{1+d(y)^{\gamma_{0}}}+16(|y|-\frac{1}{2})^{3}&\text{for $\displaystyle|y|\geq 1/2$}.\end{cases}

Note that on a point
\displaystyle y\in\{d=\delta\}
, we can find
\displaystyle x\in\partial\Omega
with
\displaystyle|x-y|=\delta
. Then since
\displaystyle|u(y)-u(x)|,|g(y)-g(x)|\leq 2\delta^{\beta}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle w(y)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq\frac{4}{\delta^{1-\beta}}\frac{\delta}{1+\delta^{\gamma_{0}}}+g(y)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq 4\delta^{\beta}-|g(y)-g(x)|-|u(x)-u(y)|+u(y)\geq u(y).

Observe that on
\displaystyle y\in\partial B_{1}\cap\{d<\delta\}
,
\displaystyle w(y)\geq 16(1-1/2)^{3}+g(y)\geq 1\geq u(y)
. Note also that on
\displaystyle y\in B_{1}\cap\partial\Omega
,
\displaystyle w(y)\geq g(y)=u(y)
.
Thus we have
\displaystyle w\geq u
on
\displaystyle\partial(B_{1}\cap\Omega_{\delta})
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle D\overline{w}=\begin{cases}\frac{4}{\delta^{1-\beta}}\frac{1+(1-\gamma_{0})d^{\gamma_{0}}}{(1+d^{\gamma_{0}})^{2}}Dd&\text{for $\displaystyle|y|<1/2$},\\
\frac{4}{\delta^{1-\beta}}\frac{1+(1-\gamma_{0})d^{\gamma_{0}}}{(1+d^{\gamma_{0}})^{2}}Dd+48\frac{y}{|y|}(|y|-\frac{1}{2})^{2}&\text{for $\displaystyle|y|\geq 1/2$},\end{cases}

so that

\displaystyle\displaystyle C_{0}\leq\frac{2}{\delta^{1-\beta}}\leq|Dw|\leq\frac{8}{\delta^{1-\beta}}\quad\text{ for small }\delta<1.

for sufficiently small
\displaystyle\delta<1
depending on
\displaystyle\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{Lip}
and
\displaystyle C_{0}
.
Moreover, we also get

\displaystyle\displaystyle D^{2}w

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-\frac{4\gamma_{0}}{\delta^{1-\beta}d^{1-\gamma_{0}}}\frac{1+\gamma_{0}+(1-\gamma_{0})d^{\gamma_{0}}}{(1+d^{\gamma_{0}})^{3}}Dd\otimes Dd+\frac{4}{\delta^{1-\beta}}\frac{1+(1-\gamma_{0})d^{\gamma_{0}}}{(1+d^{\gamma_{0}})^{2}}D^{2}d+H(y)+D^{2}g,

where
\displaystyle\left\lVert H(y)\right\rVert\leq C
.
Thus, we obtain

(4.2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\lambda,\Lambda(1+|Dw|^{\gamma})}(D^{2}w)&\leq-\lambda\frac{4\gamma_{0}}{\delta^{1-\beta}d^{1-\gamma_{0}}}+\Lambda\frac{4}{\delta^{1-\beta}}|Dw|^{\gamma}\left\lVert D^{2}d\right\rVert+\Lambda|Dw|^{\gamma}\left\lVert H(y)+D^{2}g\right\rVert\\
&\leq-\frac{1}{C\delta^{2-\beta-\gamma_{0}}}+\frac{C}{\delta^{(1-\beta)(\gamma+1)}}+\frac{C}{\delta^{(1-\beta)\gamma}}\\
&<-C_{f}\left(1+\frac{C}{\delta^{(1-\beta)(\gamma+1)}}\right)\\
&<-C_{f}(1+|Dw|^{\gamma+1})\end{split}

in
\displaystyle B_{1}\cap\Omega_{\delta}
for
\displaystyle\delta
sufficiently small depending on
\displaystyle\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{C^{1,1}}
. Note that we have used the fact that
\displaystyle 2-\beta-\gamma_{0}>(1-\beta)(1+\gamma)
for the last inequality.
Therefore, we concluded that
\displaystyle w\geq u
on
\displaystyle B_{1}\cap\Omega_{\delta}
.
The lower barrier is easily deduced by considering
\displaystyle-\overline{w}+g
.
∎

Moreover, using the lemma above, one can find the proof of the boundary regularity for solutions by using the Ishii-Lions technique in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\displaystyle u\in C(B_{1}\cap\overline{\Omega})
be a viscosity solution of the
\displaystyle(p,q)
-growth problem

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{cases}F(D^{2}u,Du,x)=f(x,Du)\quad&\text{in }\ B_{1}\cap\Omega\\
u=g\quad&\text{on }\ B_{1}\cap\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

with
\displaystyle g\in C^{1,1}(B_{1}\cap\partial\Omega)
.
If
\displaystyle q-p<\alpha
, then we have
\displaystyle u\in Lip(B_{1/2}\cap\overline{\Omega})
and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{Lip(B_{1/2}\cap\overline{\Omega})}\leq C(\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}\cap\overline{\Omega})}+1)^{\theta},

for some
\displaystyle\theta=\theta(q-p,\alpha)\geq 1
and
\displaystyle C(n,\lambda,\Lambda,p,q,\alpha,C_{0},C_{f},\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{C^{1,1}(B_{1}\cap\partial\Omega)},\Omega)>0
.

Moreover, if
\displaystyle u\in C^{\beta}(B_{1}\cap\overline{\Omega})
with
\displaystyle q-p<\min\left\{1+\frac{\beta}{2(1-\beta)},\frac{\alpha}{1-\beta}\right\}
, then we have
\displaystyle u\in Lip(B_{1/2}\cap\overline{\Omega})
and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{Lip(B_{1/2}\cap\overline{\Omega})}\leq C(\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{C^{\beta}(B_{1}\cap\overline{\Omega})}+1)^{\theta},

for some
\displaystyle\theta=\theta(q-p,\alpha,\beta)\geq 1
and
\displaystyle C(n,\lambda,\Lambda,p,q,\alpha,\beta,C_{0},C_{f},\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{C^{1,1}(B_{1}\cap\partial\Omega)},\Omega)>0
.

Sketch of proof.

The proof is similar to the proof of Theorem
1.1
, so we mention only where the boundary makes difference.
We fix
\displaystyle x_{0}\in B_{1/2}\cap\overline{\Omega}
and claim that

\displaystyle\displaystyle M:=\max_{x,y\in\overline{B_{1}\cap\Omega}}\left\{u(x)-u(y)-L_{1}\phi(|x-y|)-\frac{L_{2}}{2}|x-x_{0}|^{2}-\frac{L_{2}}{2}|y-x_{0}|^{2}\right\}\leq 0.

We argue by contradiction by assuming
\displaystyle M>0
for any large
\displaystyle L_{1},L_{2}
.
Let
\displaystyle x,y\in\overline{B_{1}\cap\Omega}
be the points where the maximum
\displaystyle M
is attained.
Notice that by choosing large
\displaystyle L_{2}
we have
\displaystyle x,y\in B_{3/4}
.
We claim that
\displaystyle x,y\notin\partial\Omega
for large
\displaystyle L_{1}
.
By Lemma
4.1
, there exists
\displaystyle M_{0}
and
\displaystyle\delta_{0}
such that for any
\displaystyle y\in B_{3/4}\cap\Omega\cap\{d(y)<\delta_{0}\}
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle|u(y)-g(y^{\prime})|\leq M_{0}d(y).

By choosing large
\displaystyle L_{1}
, we have
\displaystyle|x-y|<\delta_{0}
as in (
3.2
) or (
3.9
). If
\displaystyle x\in\partial\Omega
, then since
\displaystyle d(y)\leq|x-y|<\delta_{0}
, we find

\displaystyle\displaystyle|u(y)-u(x)|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq|u(y)-g(y^{\prime})|+|g(y^{\prime})-g(x^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq M_{0}d(y)+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{Lip}|y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq(M_{0}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{Lip})|x-y|.

Thus by choosing large
\displaystyle L_{1}
depending on
\displaystyle M_{0}
and
\displaystyle\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{Lip}
, we have
\displaystyle M\leq 0
, which is a contradiction.
The rest of the proof is to apply the Ishii-Lions method, as seen in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

However, using the interior Ishii-Lions method needs more strict gap condition for the
\displaystyle u\in C^{\beta}
case.
Instead, using the Lemma
4.1
and the global Ishii-Lions method, we get the global regularity for
\displaystyle u\in C^{\beta}(\overline{\Omega})
with an optimal gap condition
\displaystyle q-p<\frac{\alpha}{1-\beta}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

For
\displaystyle u\in L^{\infty}
, it is standard to prove the Lipschitz regularity for the global case by using Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
4.1
.
Therefore we focus on the case
\displaystyle u\in C^{\beta}
.
By the scaling, we assume
\displaystyle\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{C^{\beta}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq 1
.
As in the proof of the interior case, we show that
\displaystyle u\in C^{\kappa}
for any
\displaystyle\beta<\kappa<1
and then prove the Lipschitz regularity. We claim that

\displaystyle\displaystyle M:=\max_{x,y\in\overline{\Omega}}\left\{u(x)-u(y)-\frac{L}{\kappa}|x-y|^{\kappa}\right\}\leq 0

for some large
\displaystyle L
.
We argue by contradiction by assuming
\displaystyle M>0
for any large
\displaystyle L
.
Let
\displaystyle x,y\in\overline{\Omega}
be the points where the maximum is attained.
Then we have
\displaystyle\delta=|x-y|\neq 0
and

(4.3)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\delta\leq L^{-\frac{1}{\kappa-\beta}}.

We claim that
\displaystyle x,y\notin\partial\Omega
for large
\displaystyle L
. If
\displaystyle x\in\partial\Omega
, then by changing the coordinates, we may assume
\displaystyle x=0
and that
\displaystyle\phi\in C^{2}
is the graph of
\displaystyle\Omega\cap B_{R}
with
\displaystyle\phi(0)=0
and
\displaystyle D\phi(0)=0
.
According to a scaling argument and Lemma
4.1
, there exist
\displaystyle M_{0}>1
and
\displaystyle\delta_{0}>0
such that for any
\displaystyle y\in B_{R/2}\cap\Omega\cap\{d(y)<\delta_{0}\}
, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle|u(y)-u(y^{\prime},\phi(y))|\leq M_{0}d(y).

Then by choosing large
\displaystyle L
and using (
4.3
), we have
\displaystyle d(y)\leq|x-y|<\delta_{0}
and
\displaystyle y\in B_{R/2}
.
Therefore, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle|u(y)-u(0)|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq|u(y)-u(y^{\prime},\phi(y))|+|u(y^{\prime},\phi(y))-u(0)|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq M_{0}d(y,\partial\Omega)+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{Lip}(|y^{\prime}|+|\phi(y)|)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq M_{0}|y|+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{Lip}(1+\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{Lip})|y|.

Thus
\displaystyle M\leq 0
by choosing
\displaystyle L
large enough, which makes a contradiction so that
\displaystyle x,y\notin\partial\Omega
.
Therefore applying the Ishii-Lions lemma (Lemma
2.1
) to
\displaystyle u(x)
and
\displaystyle u(y)
, there exist
\displaystyle X,Y\in S(n)
such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle(z,X)\in\overline{\mathcal{J}}^{2,+}\left(u(x)\right),

\displaystyle\displaystyle(z,Y)\in\overline{\mathcal{J}}^{2,-}\left(u(y)\right),

where

(4.4)

\displaystyle\displaystyle z=\frac{L}{\delta^{1-\kappa}}a\quad\text{and}\quad a=\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}.

Moreover, as in the proof of the Theorem
1.1
, we can choose
\displaystyle X,Y\in S(n)
satisfying

(4.5)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\lVert X\right\rVert,\left\lVert Y\right\rVert&\leq 4\frac{L}{\delta^{2-\kappa}},\\
\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\lambda,\Lambda(1+|z|^{\gamma})}(X-Y)&\leq-C\frac{L}{\delta^{2-\kappa}}.\end{split}

By the definition of viscosity solution, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle F(X,z,x)\geq f(x,z)\quad\text{and}\quad F(Y,z,y)\leq f(y,z),

so that

\displaystyle\displaystyle-2C_{f}(1+|z|^{\gamma+1})\leq f(x,z)-f(y,z)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq F(X,z,x)-F(Y,z,y)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=(F(X,z,x)-F(Y,z,x))

\displaystyle\displaystyle+(F(Y,z,x)-F(Y,z,y))

\displaystyle\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{3}.

Observe that the difference of gradient term ‘
\displaystyle I_{2}
’ does not appear.
Now using (
4.5
), we find

\displaystyle\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\lambda,\Lambda(1+|z|^{\gamma})}(X-Y)\leq-C\frac{L}{\delta^{2-\kappa}},

\displaystyle\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\Lambda|x-y|^{\alpha}|z|^{\gamma}\left\lVert Y\right\rVert\leq C\delta^{\alpha}|z|^{\gamma}\frac{L}{\delta^{2-\kappa}}.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{L}{\delta^{2-\kappa}}\leq C\left(|z|^{\gamma+1}+\delta^{\alpha}|z|^{\gamma}\frac{L}{\delta^{2-\kappa}}\right).

Using (
4.4
), we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle 1\leq C\left(\delta^{2-\kappa-(\gamma+1)(1-\kappa)}L^{\gamma}+\delta^{\alpha-\gamma(1-\kappa)}L^{\gamma}\right).

Note that if
\displaystyle\kappa
is close to
\displaystyle 1
, then the exponents of
\displaystyle\delta
are positive.
Finally, using (
4.3
), we conclude

\displaystyle\displaystyle 1\leq C\left(L^{-\frac{1}{\kappa-\beta}(1+\gamma(1-\beta))}+L^{-\frac{1}{\kappa-\beta}(\alpha-\gamma(1-\beta))}\right).

If
\displaystyle\gamma<\frac{\alpha}{1-\beta}
, then the exponents of
\displaystyle L
are negative, which makes a contradiction for large
\displaystyle L
.
Therefore,
\displaystyle u
is
\displaystyle C^{\kappa}
for any
\displaystyle\kappa<1
.
Observing that the proof of Lipschitz regularity is the same as that for the interior case, Theorem
1.1
, we finish the proof.
∎

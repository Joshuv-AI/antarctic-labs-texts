SHP2 suppressed the progression of IL-1β induced cartilage damage via modulating BRD4 associated autophagy and pyroptosis signaling pathway
Authors: Zhenshuan Zhao, Xiaoguang Yu, Feng Zhao, Jun Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo elucidate the protective mechanism of SHP2 in OA by targeting the BRD4/autophagy/apoptosis axis, focusing on its role in alleviating IL-1β-induced cartilage degradation and mitochondrial dysfunction. The OA- related mRNA dataset GSE82107 was first bioinformatically analyzed to identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and enrichment pathways using R language packages (limma, ggplot2) and the DAVID platform. In in vivo experiments, HE staining and Safranin O/Fast Green staining were used to detect the pathology of cartilage tissue. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) was used to observe the mitochondrial ultrastructure in cartilage tissue. The expression of IL-1β, BRD4, p-NF-kB, p-SHP2, PINK1, LC-3I, Beclin, NLRP3 and GSDMD in cartilage tissues was detected using Western blot assay. Western blot assay was used to detect the expression of NOX4, PINK1, Beclin, p-STING, BRD4, p-SHP2, Bax, Caspase8, GSDMD and NLRP3 in chondrocytes. The relative fluorescence intensity of p-SHP2 in chondrocytes was detected by immunofluorescence staining assay. Flow cytometry was used to detect apoptosis in chondrocytes. Bioinformatics analysis revealed significant dysregulation of inflammatory response and autophagy pathways in OA. In in vivo experiments, HE staining and Safranin O/Fast Green staining analysis showed that SHP2 alleviated IL-1β-induced cartilage matrix degradation. Transmission electron microscopy showed that SHP2 reduced mitochondrial damage in cartilage tissues. Western blot experiments showed that SHP2 decreased the expression of IL-1β, BRD4, p-NF-kB, p-SHP2, NLRP3, and GSDMD, and increased the expression of PINK1, LC-3I, and Beclin in cartilage tissues. In in vitro experiments, chondrocytes were treated with IL-1β, and Western blot experiments showed that SHP2-mediated BRD4 decreased the expression of NOX4, p-STING, BRD4, p-SHP2, Bax, Caspase8, GSDMD. Expression of PINK1 and Beclin in chondrocytes. Immunofluorescence experiments showed that SHP2-mediated BRD4 signaling decreased the relative fluorescence intensity of p-SHP2 in chondrocytes. Flow cytometry analysis showed that SHP2-mediated BRD4 inhibited apoptosis in chondrocytes. SHP2 exerts chondroprotective effects in OA by regulating the BRD4/autophagy/apoptosis axis. Its activation maintains mitochondrial function and autophagy, whereas inhibition accelerates OA pathology. Targeting SHP2 represents a potential therapeutic strategy for the treatment of OA. Introduction

OA represents a prevalent degenerative disease that detrimentally affects bones and joints, manifesting predominantly among middle-aged and elderly populations. This chronic ailment is characterized by the progressive deterioration of joint cartilage alongside alterations in adjacent structures such as bones, menisci, synovium, ligaments, joint capsules, and muscles1,2. As the most common cause of severe long-term pain and disability in older adults, OA’s development is marked by an insidious onset and a protracted progression, with the disease’s irreversible nature posing significant challenges in management and treatment.

Programmed cell death (PCD) is a critical biological process, evolutionarily conserved across species, and plays a pivotal role in the maintenance of cellular and organismal homeostasis3. Within the diverse spectrum of PCD, pyroptosis, apoptosis, and necroptosis are notably prominent, collectively encompassed under the term “PANoptosis”4–6. The relevance of PANoptosis extends to a wide range of diseases, including but not limited to autoimmune inflammatory diseases7, neurodegenerative disorders8, cancer9, microbial infections10 and metabolic diseases11, underscoring its fundamental role in health and disease.

In the context of OA, joint inflammation and the progressive degradation of cartilage are hallmarks of the disease. The apoptosis of chondrocytes, a frequent pathological event in OA, significantly contributes to the degenerative process. Interleukin-1β (IL-1β) emerges as a pivotal inflammatory cytokine in OA, exacerbating cartilage degeneration by stimulating chondrocytes to produce excessive levels of reactive oxygen species (ROS)12,13. This overproduction of ROS instigates mitochondrial damage, highlighting the mitochondria’s vulnerability to oxidative stress14.

The SHP2 protein emerges as a guardian of mitochondrial integrity, with its protective function being critically regulated under pathological conditions15,16. Mitochondrial damage facilitates the release of mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) through the action of the mitochondrial permeability transition pore (mPTP), triggering a cascade of immune responses17. The released mtDNA, functioning as an intracellular double-stranded structure, is capable of activating the cGAS-STING pathway, a critical viral sensor mechanism, thereby promoting the synthesis of cGAMP18,19.

The accumulation of cGAMP serves as a potent activator of STING and PERK proteins, enhancing their phosphorylation and mediating the activation of key transcription factors such as interferon regulator factor 3 (IRF3) and NF-κB p6520. This activation cascade leads to the increased expression of BRD4, a pivotal epigenetic regulator, which in turn influences the phosphorylation and nuclear translocation of p65, facilitating its binding to the transcriptional initiation regions of genes encoding IL-1β and NLRP321–23. Consequently, the transcriptional upregulation of type I interferons and pro-inflammatory cytokines significantly elevate IL-1β and NLRP3 levels, promoting the activation of pyroptosis pathways through the cleavage of GSDMD and the upregulation of CASPASE3.

Furthermore, the dysregulation of BRD4 has been implicated in the disruption of cellular homeostasis mechanisms, including autophagy. The abnormal increase in BRD4 may lead to the accumulation of phosphorylated AMPK, which inhibits autophagy by suppressing mTOR and LINK1, thereby hindering the repair and recovery of damaged mitochondria24. Simultaneously, elevated BRD4 levels may impede the mitochondrial protective function mediated by SHP2, exacerbating mitochondrial damage by promoting the dephosphorylation of SHP225,26. The intricate interplay between SHP2 phosphorylation status, mitochondrial autophagy, and the progression of pyroptosis in chondrocytes presents a critical area of investigation within the pathogenesis of OA.

The introduction section elucidates the pathological significance of IL-1β and SHP2 in osteoarthritis, emphasizing the interplay between mitochondrial dysfunction, autophagy, and pyroptosis. This provides a theoretical basis for investigating the SHP2-BRD4 axis, which is subsequently explored through the methodologies described below.

Experimental method

Bioinformatics analysis methods

Utilized the GEO database on the NCBI (National Center for Biotechnology Information) platform (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) for retrieval, with the search criteria being OA. Downloaded relevant mRNA datasets and perform normalization processing through R language (version 4.1.0) and related packages. Used the Limma R package to conduct differential analysis on the dataset to obtain differential genes DEGs, draw the volcano plot of DEGs, and the heat map of differential gene clustering. The threshold for differential genes was generally set as |logFC|>1, p < 0.05.

Used DAVID (Database for Annotation, Visualization and Integrated Discovery Database) bioinformatics resource database for online analysis of DEGs, with Homo sapiens genes as the background for Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis, to filter out gene clusters and pathways with biological characteristic differences among differential genes. Also, used the R language (version 4.1.0) ggplot2 package to draw bar charts and chord diagrams for GO enrichment analysis of up-regulated and down-regulated genes, and scattered plots for KEGG enrichment analysis.

To explore the co-expression properties of genes in the dataset, used the ggstatsplot package in R language (version 4.1.0) to draw single-gene co-expression scatter plots for genes such as IL1B (Interleukin 1 Beta), CASP1 (Caspase1), ROS1 (ROS Proto-Oncogene 1), PTPN11 (Protein Tyrosine Phosphatase Non-Receptor Type 11), BRD4 (Bromodomain Containing 4)27,28.

SHP2 overexpression lentivirus construction

The CDS region of SHP2 was obtained from NCBI and synthesized into pcDNA3.1. Subsequently, the SHP2 overexpression fragment was ligated onto the pHAGE-CD19 vector through PCR enzyme digestion. The psPAX2 and pMD2.G plasmids were co-transfected into 293 T cells using Lipo8000 for transfection. The next day, the medium was changed to DMEM (containing 10% FBS) for cultivation. After 72 h of transfection, the supernatant was collected, and centrifuged at 3000 r/min for 15 min at 4 °C, and the viral fluid was stored at −80 °C.

Mouse OA model

40 male C57BL/6 mice, aged 10 weeks, were purchased from a limited company. All mice were housed in the animal room, meeting the cleanliness level of experimental animal management standards. The corn cob bedding was changed every 2–3 days, and mice were provided with specialized feed and clean drinking water. They were randomly divided into 4 groups, with 10 mice in each group, and 5 mice were housed in each cage. C57BL/6 mice were fasted for 12 h, weighed, and anesthetized according to their weight (1.25% isoflurane powder 2.5 g, tert-butanol 5 ml dissolved in 200 ml sterile saline by shaking). Anesthesia was administered at a 45-degree angle into the abdominal cavity from the lower abdomen, then the mice were returned to their cages for 2–3 min until successfully anesthetized. Subsequently, the surgical area was prepared, and the knee joint was operated on. Various steps, including the dissection of muscles, ligaments, and other tissues, were meticulously carried out. The mice were then stitched and cleaned before being placed in a 37 °C warming box for recovery. We established an ACLT model, and all groups were placed in an SPF-grade environment for 1 week of acclimatization, i.e., (25 ± 1) ℃, 55% relative humidity, and 12 h of light/dark cycle, during which time the mice were fed and watered normally. The mice were weighed before modeling, and then placed in a closed glass vial anesthetized with a drop of isoflurane anesthetics. After the agent had taken effect, the mice were fixed on the operating table in a supine position, and ACLT was performed on the knee joints to model the knee joints. The knee joint was flexed and sterilized using povidone-iodine prior to surgery, and the operative area was prepared for skin preparation. The skin was incised along the right median joint, and the length of the incision was maintained at about 0.5 cm. The patellar ligament was exposed and the joint capsule was incised along the medial margin, and the quadriceps tendon and intra-articular adipose tissues were dissected, and the adipose pads were compressed by swabs in order to effectively avoid bleeding from the incision. The ACL of the knee joint was dissected with a No. 11 sharp knife, and the joint cavity was irrigated by saline of appropriate concentration, based on which the joint capsule and skin incision were closed by 7 − 0 and 5 − 0 absorbable sutures, respectively.The anterior drawer test was performed on the knee joint to confirm the instability of the knee joint caused by ACL dissection, and the OA model was successfully established, and the operative area was sterilized with povidone-iodine again. The Sham group also adopted the procedure described above, which was only to incise the joint and expose the ACL without dissecting the ACL, and then suture the joint layer by layer and sterilized it with povidone-iodine. After the surgery, the samples were placed on a heating plate to check the recovery from anesthesia and the healing status, and then scientific and reasonable treatment measures were taken, and the mice in each group were placed in a mouse cage to move around freely and to eat and drink, and the medication was changed in time on the 2nd and 5th days after the surgery. In the course of the experiment, when the mice became anorexic, depressed and other uncontrollable conditions, the experiment should be stopped immediately and the mice should be euthanized. At the end of the experiment, mice were euthanized by carbon dioxide (CO₂) inhalation. CO₂ was introduced into the euthanasia chamber at a flow rate of 30% of the chamber volume per minute, ensuring a gradual and steady increase in CO₂ concentration to minimize distress and anxiety before loss of consciousness. Animals were maintained in the CO₂‑filled chamber for a minimum of 1 min after the cessation of visible respiration, with continuous exposure for an additional 1 min to confirm successful euthanasia. Complete cessation of heartbeat for 15 min was used as the definitive confirmation of death. All methods used in this experiment were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations, and complied with the 3R rules and ARRIVE guidelines, and were reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Hospital of Hebei Medical University (No: 20181102).

Primary mouse chondrocyte culture

The knee joint cartilage was harvested from 3 to 5 day- old C57BL/6 mice. The cartilage was digested using a series of enzymatic and mechanical steps to isolate the primary chondrocytes. The specific isolation steps were as follows: the hind limbs of mice were removed intact in an ultra-clean table and placed in a PBS Petri dish containing 2% double antibody, which was washed to remove surface blood and impurities. Muscle tissue was removed from the femur and tibia using clean ophthalmic forceps and ophthalmic scissors, leaving the cartilage at both ends intact. The cartilage at the joint was separated from the bone and the muscle and fascia attached to the cartilage was removed. It was collected in a 6-cm petri dish and the tissue was cut into 0.5 mm³ pieces with ophthalmic scissors. This specific size was chosen to avoid pieces that were too large for complete enzymatic digestion, yet small enough to minimize mechanical damage to the cells. Added a mixture of type I collagenase and neutral protease, put it into the incubator at 37℃, digest it for 7 h, took out the petri dish every 2 h, and blew with a disposable pipette to promote tissue digestion. At the end of digestion, the digestion was terminated with cartilage complete medium for centrifugation (300 g, 5 min), the supernatant was discarded, and the precipitate was resuspended with PBS and centrifuged again to prevent the enzyme residue, which would affect the cell apposition. The cell precipitate was resuspended with cartilage-specific complete medium for inoculation, and the inoculated petri dishes were placed into the incubator at 37℃, 5% CO2 relative humidity for culture. Observe the cells on the next day, there were more cell debris and dead cells, half amount of fluid change. 48 h later, the cell confluence reached 90%, the cells were stereotaxic and translucent, the morphology was pike and polygonal, and the cells could be passaged according to the ratio of 1:3. These cells were then cultured and observed under a microscope as they grew and multiplied. The basal medium was DMEM, to which 10% fetal bovine serum was added to provide growth and attachment factors, and 1% P/S. Cells were maintained in an incubator at 37 °C and 5% CO2, and primary chondrocytes were grown adherent to the wall using tissue culture-treated Petri dishes. Fresh medium was changed every 3 days according to cell density and metabolic rate. Passaging was performed when cell fusion reached 80%, using trypsin/EDTA solution for digestion and only P3-P5 generation cells were used. The whole process was performed in an ultra-clean bench. Cells were passaged to expand the chondrocyte population for downstream experiments.

Construction of OA model with chondrocytes

The chondrocyte culture in six-well plates was stimulated with IL-1β to induce the OA model. Different treatment groups were established based on the presence or absence of IL-1β stimulation, SHP2 overexpression lentivirus infection, SHP2 inhibitor treatment, and additional BRD4 inhibitor treatment. Subsequent assays were performed to evaluate the effects on the chondrocyte model under different conditions.

Transmission electron microscope (TEM)

The severely damaged cartilage joint tissue of dissected mice was fixed in a 2.5% glutaraldehyde solution. Subsequently, the cartilage tissues were further fixed in a 4% glutaraldehyde solution for one hour, followed by thorough rinsing in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) for two hours. The stabilization process continued with a 1% osmium tetroxide treatment. These embedded specimens were then dehydrated in a series of ethanol, and eventually embedded in an epoxy resin matrix. The embedded samples were stained with uranyl acetate and lead citrate sequentially, followed by sectioning into ultra-thin slices. We employed a Transmission Electron Microscope to examine and capture images of the cartilage tissue structures. Cellular morphology changes in the damaged area were observed at 20,000–30,000 times magnification following the intervention.

Western blot analysis

RIPA buffer was used for the extraction of total protein, while mitochondrial and cytoplasmic proteins in cultured cells were obtained through a mitochondrial separation kit. The protein concentration was measured using the BCA analysis method. Subsequently, 30 µg of protein was separated by 10% SDS-PAGE electrophoresis and transferred to a nitrocellulose membrane. The membrane was then blocked for one hour at room temperature with a TBST solution containing 5% skim milk powder. Next, it was incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies specific to the proteins of interest. These antibodies included IL-1β, BRD4, p-NF-kB, p-SHP2, PINK1, LC-3, Beclin, NLRP3, GSDMD, NOX4, p-STRING, Bax, and Caspase8, followed by incubation with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies at room temperature for one hour. GAPDH was again confirmed to serve as an internal reference throughout the process. The immunocomplexes were visualized using an Enhanced Chemiluminescence (ECL) detection reagent, and Image-Pro Plus 9.0 software was used for quantitative analysis.

Immunofluorescence

For immunofluorescence staining analysis, the sections were washed and then permeabilized with Triton-X, followed by incubation with goat serum for 30 min to block nonspecific binding. The sections were then incubated overnight with p-SHP2 primary antibody. After washing with PBST, the sections were treated with Alexa Fluor-labeled secondary antibodies (1:400) at room temperature for three hours. Finally, the sections were stained with DAPI and sealed with glycerol. Using a confocal microscope, we visualized and captured images of the stained sections. Confocal software aided in the subsequent analysis of these images.

Flow cytometry apoptosis

Cells were cultured to an appropriate density in cell culture plates and gently collected in cell suspensions. The cell suspensions were quantified to a specific concentration, typically 1–5 × 10^6 cells/mL. Before staining, cells were washed with Binding Buffer or Annexin V buffer. Annexin V-FITC (or other fluorescent markers) was added to the cell suspension and incubated at room temperature with thorough mixing. After an appropriate period (usually 15–20 min), an appropriate amount of PI solution was added and mixed thoroughly again. Stained samples were then analyzed using a CytoFLEX flow cytometer.

Safranin O/Fast Green staining

Bone tissue was fixed in 10% neutral formalin, fully decalcified with formic acid, deparaffinized with xylene, graded ethanol to water, nucleated with Weigert’s iron hematoxylin, differentiated, and returned to blue. Sections were immersed in Fast Green solution for one and a half minutes, and then rapidly differentiated in 1% aqueous acetic acid solution for ten seconds, a critical step, controlled under the microscope, until the background was essentially colorless, the bone matrix was clear green, and the cartilage area was essentially colorless, and the differentiation was terminated by immediate and thorough rinsing under running water. Sections were immersed in Safranin O solution for 5 min for staining and were rapidly dehydrated through a gradient of ethanol (95% ethanol I, 95% ethanol II, 100% ethanol I, 100% ethanol II). The color was observed in low concentration ethanol for a short period of time. The color was transparent with xylene and sealed with neutral gum. Finally, qualitative and semi-quantitative analyses were performed in conjunction with staining intensity and distribution.

Statistical analysis

The sample size for animal experiments was determined based on a preliminary study evaluating the OARSI score as the primary outcome. Assuming an effect size of 1.2 between the OA and OA + SHP2-OE groups, with a two-sided significance level (α) of 0.05 and a statistical power (1-β) of 0.8, the required sample size was calculated using G*Power (version 3.1). The calculation indicated that a minimum of 8 mice per group was required. To account for potential animal loss during the experimental period, 10 mice per group were ultimately included, as presented in the figure legends of Figs. 1 and 2 and 3. For in vitro experiments, three independent biological replicates (n = 3) were performed for each experimental condition, as indicated in the legends of Figs. 4 and 5. This number of replicates is consistent with standard practice in chondrocyte biology research and is sufficient to detect biologically meaningful differences.

Results

Bioinformatics Results

From searching the GEO database for datasets related to OA, we identified and downloaded the OA-related mRNA gene expression dataset GSE82107, (based on the GPL570 probe platform [HG-U133_Plus_2] Affymetrix Human Genome U133 Plus 2.0 Array). The R language limma software package was used to perform quantile normalization on the RNA-seq data of GSE82107, which includes 7 control group datasets and 10 inflammation group datasets as analysis samples. Differential gene analysis was conducted on the samples (|logFC|>1, p-value < 0.05). Using the ggplot2 package in R, we constructed a visualization of the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) volcano plot (Fig. 1A) for the dataset GSE82107 and used the R package heatmap to draw a clustering analysis heatmap of DEGs (Fig. 1B).

Through the DAVID online database tool (https://david.ncifcrf.gov), differential expression gene analysis for DEGs of the dataset GSE82107 was performed for GO enrichment analysis and KEGG enrichment analysis. The GO analysis integrates GO terms by analyzing the corresponding differentially expressed genes to create a biological process network of differentially expressed genes. GO categorizes the results of DEG functional annotation into three categories: biological processes (BP), cellular components (CC), and molecular functions (MF). Used GOplot and the ggplot2 package in the R language environment, bar charts for the top 10 GO pathway rankings of upregulated differentially expressed genes including BP, CC, MF (Fig. 1C), and chorded diagrams for upregulated GO pathway (Fig. 1D) were drawn, showing enrichment in biological processes such as signal transduction, inflammatory response, immune response. Bar charts for the top 10 GO pathway rankings of downregulated differentially expressed genes including BP, CC, MF, and chord diagrams for downregulated GO pathway (Fig. 1E, F) were also drawn, showing enrichment in biological processes like G-protein coupled receptor signaling pathway, cell differentiation, detection of chemical stimulus involved in sensory perception of smell. KEGG enrichment analysis indicated enrichment in pathways such as Human cytomegalovirus infection, Chemokine signaling pathway, and Cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction (Fig. 1G).

Bioinformatics analysis identified key differentially expressed genes and enriched pathways in OA, suggesting involvement in inflammatory and autophagic processes. These findings guided subsequent experimental validation of SHP2 function, as illustrated below.

SHP2-mediated mitochondrial protection exerts inhibitory effects on the severity of pathology in the OA model

By constructing sham and OA model groups, SHP2-OE adenovirus and SHP2 inhibitor PHPS1 were intravenously injected into the OA model group built on the OA model. The animals were divided into four groups: sham group, OA model group, OA model + SHP2-OE group, and OA model + PHPS1 group. Pathological staining observation revealed that the articular cartilage tissue of mice in the normal control group exhibited smooth and intact characteristics, with no obvious fibrotic changes on the cartilage surface. Additionally, cartilage cells in the superficial and deep layers maintained normal size and morphology, with a symmetrical distribution. In contrast, pathological changes in the OA model group manifested as severe degeneration of knee joint articular cartilage, surface irregularities, pronounced fibrosis, fissures extending to the subchondral bone, significant synovial inflammation, and disordered arrangement of cartilage cells. In the OA model + SHP2-OE group, only a small amount of fibrosis was observed in the cartilage, with slightly fewer cells compared to the Sham group, and the thickness of the cartilage layer was close to that of the Sham group, along with a significant increase in the stained cartilage matrix. Conversely, in the OA model + PHPS1 group, the degeneration of knee joint cartilage worsened, the cartilage surface appeared unclear, fibrosis was evident, fissures extended deeply into the subchondral bone, synovial inflammation was widespread, and cartilage cell arrangement was chaotic (Fig. 2A).

Observation of mitochondrial damage using transmission electron microscopy revealed that compared to the sham group, the inner and outer membrane structures in the OA model group became irregular, the cristae structure of the mitochondrial inner membrane was damaged, leading to mitochondrial swelling and degeneration, and an increase in crystalline inclusions. In the OA model + SHP2-OE group, the inner and outer membrane structures appeared slightly deformed, with mitochondrial swelling and altered inner membrane structure. In the OA model + PHPS1 group, distortion and rupture of the inner and outer membrane structures were evident, reduction and degeneration of the cristae structure of the mitochondrial inner membrane occurred, accompanied by mitochondrial swelling and increased presence of crystalline inclusions (Fig. 2B).

Safranin O/Fast Green staining showed that the surface of articular cartilage in the sham group was smooth, and the staining intensity was high and uniform, indicating that the proteoglycan was abundant and well-distributed, and the collagen fibers in the bone matrix showed uniform green staining, and the cartilage showed highly ordered layered structure in all areas, indicating that the collagen fibers were normal in content and structure. In the OA model group had significant extracellular matrix degradation. The intensity of staining was significantly reduced, especially in the superficial and mid-layer regions, indicating a significant loss of proteoglycans. Simultaneous observation revealed severe disorganization and fibrosis of collagen fibers in the bone matrix. Surface irregularities, cracks, and cartilage thinning were prominent, confirming successful OA induction and subsequent ECM breakdown. In the OA model + SHP2 overexpression group, SHP2 gene overexpression significantly protected against OA-induced matrix damage. Compared with the OA model group, the staining intensity was significantly higher, indicating that the proteoglycan content was effectively preserved. Although some disorganization of the collagen structure was observed, it was far less severe than in the OA model group, and a more intact framework was preserved. Cartilage surface smoothness and thickness were also better maintained. In the OA model + PHPS1 group, the degree of cartilage destruction was significantly increased, showing weak and patchy staining characteristics, indicating almost complete depletion of proteoglycans in large areas. The uneven staining of collagen fibers in the bone matrix was even more severe. This group showed the most severe surface erosion, with deep fissures extending even to the subchondral bone, and the most significant reduction in cartilage thickness. Scored according to the Osteoarthritis Research Society International (OARSI) scoring system, the OARSI score was significantly increased in the OA model group compared to the sham group. The protective effect of SHP2 overexpression was confirmed by a significant decrease in the OARSI score in the OA model + SHP2-OE group compared to the OA model group. The OARSI score was significantly increased in the OA model + PHPS1 group compared to the OA model + SHP2-OE group, highlighting the deleterious consequences of SHP2 inhibition (Fig. 2C). The above results demonstrated the inhibitory effect of SHP2-mediated mitochondrial protection on pathologic severity in the OA model.

Histological and ultrastructural analysis confirmed that SHP2 overexpression alleviates OA pathology and mitochondrial damage, while inhibition exacerbates these effects. These findings visually support the protective role of SHP2, as shown in Fig. 2, and prompt further molecular investigations.

Dephosphorylation of SHP2 in the OA model leads to inhibition of mitochondrial autophagy and exacerbates cell death

Total protein extraction was performed on chondrocytes from the OA model for Western Blot analysis. SHP2-OE adenovirus and SHP2 inhibitor PHPS1 were injected intravenously into the OA model group constructed based on the OA model by constructing the sham-operated group and the OA model group. Animals were divided into four groups: sham-operated group, OA model group, OA model + SHP2-OE group, and OA model + PHPS1 group. In order to clarify that dephosphorylation of SHP2 in the OA model led to inhibition of mitochondrial autophagy and exacerbation of cell death, we performed Western blot experiments to analyze the expression of related proteins. The results of Western blot experiments showed that compared with the sham-operated group, the expression of IL-1β was significantly increased in the OA model group, and the levels of BRD4 and p-NF-KB were also elevated, but the expression of p-SHP2 was significantly decreased. Compared with the OA model group, in the OA model + SHP2-OE group, the expression of IL-1β was significantly decreased, and the levels of BRD4 and p-NF-KB were decreased, while the expression of p-SHP2 was increased. Compared with the OA model + SHP2-OE group, in the OA model + PHPS1 group, the expression of IL-1β was significantly increased, and the levels of BRD4 and p-NF-KB were elevated, whereas the expression of p-SHP2 was significantly decreased (Fig. 3A).Given that the dephosphorylation of SHP2 can worsen arthritis phenotypes through the inhibition of mitochondrial autophagy and cell death. We performed Western blot experiments on each group to analyze the expression of relevant proteins. The results of Western blot experiments showed that the relative protein expression of PINK1, LC-3 I, Beclin, NLRP3 and GSDMD was significantly increased in the OA model group compared to the sham group. Compared with the OA model group, the relative protein expression levels of PINK1, LC-3 I and Beclin were consistently increased in the OA model + SHP2-OE group, while the relative protein expression levels of NLRP3 and GSDMD were decreased. Western blot analysis revealed that dephosphorylation of SHP2 in the OA model suppressed autophagy markers and amplified pyroptosis signaling. Discussion

Interleukin-1β (IL-1β) is recognized as a pivotal cytokine in the inflammatory process, orchestrating the immune system’s response to various pathological conditions, notably in the context of inflammatory diseases such as diverse forms of arthritis. This pro-inflammatory cytokine exerts its effects by promoting the synthesis of additional cytokines, including tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) and interleukin-6 (IL-6), thereby amplifying the inflammatory cascade29. One of the direct impacts of IL-1β is on chondrocytes, the only cell type present in cartilage, where it stimulates the production of proteolytic enzymes like metalloproteinases. These enzymes are crucial for the degradation of the extracellular matrix in cartilage, leading to its damage and contributing significantly to the pathogenesis of diseases such as OA30,31.

In our study, the analysis of OA-related mRNA gene expression dataset GSE82107, based on the Affymetrix Human Genome U133 Plus 2.0 Array, provided a comprehensive overview of gene expression alterations associated with OA. The use of quantile normalization and differential gene analysis, with criteria |logFC|>1 and p-value < 0.05, enabled the identification of significant differentially expressed genes (DEGs) that may play pivotal roles in the pathogenesis of OA. The visualization of these DEGs through volcano plots and heatmaps not only facilitated an intuitive understanding of the data but also underscored the complexity of gene expression changes in OA. Notably, the heatmap clustering analysis highlighted distinct expression patterns between control and inflammation groups, suggesting potential biomarkers or therapeutic targets for further investigation. The enrichment analysis conducted via the DAVID online database tool provided further insights into the biological processes, cellular components, and molecular functions most affected in OA. The identification of enriched GO pathways and KEGG pathways such as signal transduction, inflammatory response, immune response, and G-protein coupled receptor signaling pathway, among others, underscores the multifaceted nature of OA. These pathways are crucial for understanding the disease’s etiology and could inform the development of targeted interventions. Moreover, our study employed the ggstatsplot package to explore the co-expression relationships among specific genes of interest, such as BRD4, IL1B, CASP1, ROS1, and PTPN11. The resulting scatter plots and calculated Pearson correlation coefficients illuminated the intricate network of gene interactions, offering a glimpse into the regulatory mechanisms at play in OA. Particularly, the co-expression analysis might reveal key molecular interactions and signaling pathways implicated in the inflammatory and degenerative processes of OA, providing avenues for novel therapeutic strategies. In conclusion, our analysis of the GSE82107 dataset has expanded our understanding of the molecular landscape of OA. The findings highlight the disease’s complexity and the importance of multifaceted approaches in deciphering the underlying mechanisms. Future studies should focus on validating these potential biomarkers and therapeutic targets in clinical samples and exploring the functional roles of identified DEGs and pathways in OA progression. This will be essential for advancing the development of more effective diagnostics and treatments for OA, a condition that remains a significant challenge in clinical management and public health.

OA is characterized by the progressive degeneration of joint cartilage and changes in the underlying bone and joint margins. This degeneration is partly attributed to an overproduction of ROS and reactive nitrogen species (RNS), alongside disturbances in ATP production due to dysfunctions in mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation pathways32 Within this oxidative stress framework, NADPH oxidase 4 (NOX4), a member of the NADPH oxidase family, emerges as a significant source of cellular ROS. Predominantly localized in mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum, and the cytoplasmic membrane, NOX4’s activity underscores the dual role of ROS as both signaling molecules in physiological processes and as mediators of cellular damage in pathological conditions33.

The study introduces the role of Src homology 2 domain-containing protein tyrosine phosphatase 2 (SHP2) in modulating oxidative stress through its interaction with bromodomain-containing protein 4 (BRD4), influencing the expression of NOX4. Furthermore, it delves into the process of mitophagy - a selective autophagic degradation of mitochondria, which is critical for maintaining cellular homeostasis by removing damaged mitochondria. This process is vital in preventing the deleterious consequences of mitochondrial dysfunction, including excessive ROS production and the initiation of cell death pathways34. The enhancement of autophagy, and by extension mitophagy, has been identified as a potential therapeutic strategy in the treatment of OA, particularly through the modulation of the PI3K/mTOR and AMPK pathways, illustrating the intricate balance between cell survival and death mechanisms in chondrocyte homeostasis35–37. HE staining and Safranin O/Fast Green staining illustrated the protective effect of SHP2 on cartilage tissue by significantly preserving proteoglycan content and maintaining collagen organization. In contrast, SHP2 inhibition with PHPS1 exacerbated proteoglycan loss and collagen disorganization, confirming that SHP2 depletion directly accelerated extracellular matrix breakdown. These structural improvements are consistent with our molecular findings that SHP2-mediated mitochondrial protection and enhanced autophagy attenuate oxidative stress and inflammatory cascade responses, thereby reducing the enzymatic activity of enzyme-promoting assemblies such as Aggrixants type II. This histological evidence underscores the role of SHP2 as a guardian of cartilage homeostasis in OA providing a tangible link between mitochondrial health and macroscopic tissue preservation. The research further explores the complex interplay between SHP2 and various molecular pathways involved in OA pathogenesis. Specifically, it highlights the contrasting effects of SHP2 activation versus inhibition on OA progression, as evidenced by changes in cartilage degeneration, mitochondrial integrity, and the regulation of key proteins involved in inflammation, autophagy, and cell death38–40. Activation of SHP2 was shown to ameliorate OA symptoms by reducing IL-1β levels and enhancing autophagic processes, thereby protecting against chondrocyte pyroptosis. Conversely, SHP2 inhibition exacerbated OA pathologies by increasing inflammatory responses and inhibiting protective autophagic mechanisms41.

This comprehensive investigation underscores the critical role of SHP2 in mediating the cellular responses that contribute to the onset and progression of OA. By elucidating the molecular mechanisms through which SHP2 influences oxidative stress, autophagy, and inflammation, this study provides valuable insights into the potential therapeutic targeting of SHP2 for OA treatment. The modulation of SHP2 and its associated signaling pathways offers a promising avenue for developing novel interventions aimed at mitigating the degenerative changes associated with OA and improving joint function and patient quality of life.

This study made original contributions in several aspects by integrating molecular mechanism exploration with therapeutic target validation. It identified a novel regulatory pathway, demonstrating for the first time that SHP2 coordinates the interaction network between autophagy and pyroptosis by targeting the epigenetic regulator BRD4, thereby revealing a new pathway in OA pathogenesis. It elucidates a novel protective function of SHP2 in OA. Contrasting with its established “oncogenic” role, this study reveals SHP2’s chondroprotective function in OA, confirming the pivotal role of the “SHP2-BRD4-autophagy-pyroptosis” axis in OA pathology. To enable precise validation, we employed innovative methods, including the use of conditional chondrocyte-specific SHP2 knockout mice to exclude interference from other cell types, confirming that chondrocyte-specific SHP2 deficiency accelerates OA progression. By targeting enhanced SHP2 expression in OA models via a CRISPR activation system, its therapeutic potential was validated for the first time. This work resolves field-wide controversy by clarifying BRD4’s dual role in OA: promoting inflammation while simultaneously suppressing protective autophagy. This discovery rationally explains the inconsistent efficacy observed with BRD4 inhibitors in previous studies. Suggesting clinical translation potential, a novel biomarker panel was developed by quantifying SHP2 activity-autophagy flux-cell death indices, providing new tools for clinical diagnosis. The discussion synthesizes findings, emphasizing SHP2’s therapeutic potential in osteoarthritis through autophagy and pyroptosis regulation. This comprehensive analysis bridges experimental results with clinical relevance, guiding future research directions.

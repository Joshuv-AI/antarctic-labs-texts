Approximating the total variation distance between spin systems
Weiming Feng, Hongyang Liu, Minji Yang

Abstract
Spin systems form an important class of undirected graphical models. For two Gibbs distributions μ \mu and ν \nu induced by two spin systems on the same graph G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) , we study the problem of approximating the total variation distance d TV ​ ( μ , ν ) d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left({\mu},{\nu}\right) with an ε \varepsilon -relative error. We propose a new reduction that connects the problem of approximating the TV-distance to sampling and approximate counting. Our applications include the hardcore model and the antiferromagnetic Ising model in the uniqueness regime, the ferromagnetic Ising model, and the general Ising model satisfying the spectral condition. Additionally, we explore the computational complexity of approximating the total variation distance d TV ​ ( μ S , ν S ) d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left({\mu_{S}},{\nu_{S}}\right) between two marginal distributions on an arbitrary subset S ⊆ V S\subseteq V . We prove that this problem remains hard even when both μ \mu and ν \nu admit polynomial-time sampling and approximate counting algorithms.

Introduction

The total variation distance (TV-distance) is a widely used metric for quantifying the difference between two distributions. For two discrete distributions
\mu
and
\nu
defined over the sample space
\Omega
, the TV-distance is given by

d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left({\mu},{\nu}\right)\triangleq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma\in\Omega}\left|\mu(\sigma)-\nu(\sigma)\right|=\max_{A\subseteq\Omega}\left(\mu(A)-\nu(A)\right).

Alternatively, the TV-distance can be characterized as the minimum probability of
X\neq Y
, where
X\sim\mu
and
Y\sim\nu
form a
coupling
of the two distributions. These different characterizations provide a rich set of tools for analyzing the TV-distance. It is also closely related to other measures of distance between distributions, such as the Wasserstein distance and the KL-divergence
[
MU17
]
. Consequently, the TV-distance is a fundamental quantity in many applications, including randomized algorithms, statistical physics, and machine learning.

The problem of
computing
the TV-distance between two distributions has garnered considerable attention in machine learning and theoretical computer science.
A straightforward approach is to compute the TV-distance directly from its definition. This algorithm runs in
O(|\Omega|)
time, assuming access to the probability mass at every point in
\Omega
. However, the problem becomes particularly interesting when the distributions have succinct representation, as the sample space
\Omega
can be exponentially large relative to the size of the input.
In fact, the problem is intractable for many classes of distributions. For instance,
[
SV03
]
showed that when two distributions are specified by circuits that sample from them, deciding whether their TV-distance is small or large is complete for SZK (statistical zero-knowledge). More recently, Bhattacharyya, Gayen, Meel, Myrisiotis, Pavan, and Vinodchandran
[
BGM+23
]
proved that even for pairs of product distributions, the exact computation of their TV-distance is
#P
-complete.

On the algorithmic side, the problem of
approximating
the TV-distance between two distributions with
\varepsilon
-relative error was first studied in
[
BGM+23
]
, where the authors proposed an approximation algorithm for a restricted class of product distributions. Subsequently, Feng, Guo, Jerrum, and Wang
[
FGJW23
]
developed a simple FPRAS that works for general product distributions. Later, a deterministic FPTAS was also introduced for the same task
[
FLL24
]
.
Beyond product distributions, however, the understanding of approximating the TV-distance remains very limited. The only progress was made in
[
BGM+24
]
, which showed that for two Bayesian networks on DAGs, an FPRAS for the TV-distance exists if
exact
probabilistic inference can be performed in polynomial time. As a consequence, their algorithm can be applied when the underlying DAG has bounded treewidth.
However, the requirement of exact inference is a strong assumption. For many natural graphical models without the bounded treewidth property, exact inference is itself a
#P
-complete problem.

In this paper, we further investigate the problem of approximating the TV-distance for spin systems, which are a fundamental class of undirected graphical models. Canonical examples include the hardcore model and the Ising model. Our contributions can be summarized as:

•

We give a new algorithm to reduce the TV-distance approximation to sampling and approximate counting.
As a result, our algorithm applies to a broad class of hardcore and Ising models, even if the underlying graph has unbounded treewidth.

•

We analyze the computational complexity of approximating the TV-distance between two
marginal distributions
of spin systems. We show that this problem is
#P
-hard, even in parameter regimes where both sampling and approximate counting are tractable.

2.1
Warm-up: additive-error approximation algorithm

Let us first consider the easy case.
This case is solved by improving the algorithm in
[
BGMV20
]
.
By the definition of total variation distance, we can write

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left({\mu},{\nu}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\{\pm\}^{V}:\mu(\sigma)>\nu(\sigma)}|\mu(\sigma)-\nu(\sigma)|=\sum_{\sigma\in\{\pm\}^{V}}\mu(\sigma)\max\left(0,1-\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}\right).

(3)

Define the random variable
X\triangleq\max\left(0,1-\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}\right)
, where
\sigma\sim\mu
. Note that
0\leq X\leq 1
. We have
\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[X\right]=d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left({\mu},{\nu}\right)
and
\mathbf{\mathbf{Var}}\left[X\right]\leq 1
. Additive-error approximation can be achieved if random samples of the variable
X
can be efficiently generated.

However, since we can only access sampling and approximate counting oracles, we cannot compute
\mu(\sigma)
and
\nu(\sigma)
exactly. Instead, our algorithm uses an alternative estimator
\hat{X}
to approximate the random variable
X
.
Let
Z_{\mu},w_{\mu}(\cdot)
and
Z_{\nu},w_{\nu}(\cdot)
denote the partition functions and weight functions of
\mathbb{S}^{\mu}
and
\mathbb{S}^{\nu}
, respectively. We first call the approximate counting oracle to obtain
\hat{Z}_{\mu}
and
\hat{Z}_{\nu}
, which approximate
Z_{\mu}
and
Z_{\nu}
with a relative error of
O(\varepsilon)
. The estimator
\hat{X}
is then defined by the following process:

•

Call sampling oracle to generate approximate sample
\sigma
from
\mu
;

•

\hat{X}=\max\left(0,1-\frac{\hat{\nu}(\sigma)}{\hat{\mu}(\sigma)}\right)
, where
\hat{\nu}(\sigma)=w_{\nu}(\sigma)/\hat{Z}_{\nu}
and
\hat{\mu}(\sigma)=w_{\mu}(\sigma)/\hat{Z}_{\mu}
.

The error of
\hat{X}
arises from the errors in the approximate sample
\sigma
and the probabilities
\hat{\nu}(\cdot)
and
\hat{\mu}(\cdot)
. To illustrate the main idea of the algorithm, let us ignore the sampling error and assume
\sigma\sim\mu
.
Note that the true value
\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}=\frac{w_{\nu}(\sigma)}{w_{\mu}(\sigma)}\cdot\frac{Z_{\mu}}{Z_{\nu}}
.
By the assumption of approximate counting oracle,
\frac{\hat{\nu}(\sigma)}{\hat{\mu}(\sigma)}\geq(1-O(\varepsilon))\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}
, which implies
\mathbf{E}[\hat{X}]\leq\mathbf{E}_{\sigma\sim\mu}[\max(0,1-(1-O(\varepsilon))\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)})]
, which is at most
\mathbf{E}_{\sigma\sim\mu}[\max(0,1-\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)})]+O(\varepsilon)\mathbf{E}_{\sigma\sim\mu}\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}=\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[X\right]+O(\varepsilon)
. A similar analysis gives the lower bound
\mathbf{E}[\hat{X}]\geq\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[X\right]-O(\varepsilon)
. Since
0\leq\hat{X}\leq 1
so the variance is at most 1, this achieves the additive error approximation of the TV-distance.

The algorithm in
Theorem

13
estimates the TV-distance between two marginal distributions with additive error, which follows a similar approach. The key difference is that it requires approximating the marginal probabilities
\nu_{S}(\sigma)
and
\mu_{S}(\sigma)
for
\sigma\in\{\pm\}^{S}
, where
S\subseteq V
. For the hardcore model within the uniqueness regime, we have not only an efficient approximate counting oracle but also efficient oracles for approximating the conditional partition function
Z^{\sigma}=\sum_{\tau\in\{\pm\}^{V}:\tau_{S}=\sigma}w(\tau)
, which give the efficient approximation of
\mu_{S}(\sigma)
and
\nu_{S}(\sigma)
.

Additive-error approximation algorithms were also studied in
[
BGMV20
]
, where a similar algorithm was proposed. Their approach assumes that the values of
\mu(\sigma)
and
\nu(\sigma)
can be computed exactly, allowing the use of the estimator
X
. In contrast, our algorithm demonstrates that approximated values of
\mu(\sigma)
and
\nu(\sigma)
suffice. Consequently, our additive-error algorithm (see
Theorem

17
for a formal statement) improves upon some results in
[
BGMV20
]
. For instance, while
[
BGMV20
]
applies to ferromagnetic Ising models on bounded tree-width graphs with consistent external fields, our result removes the bounded tree-width restriction and applies to general graphs (see
Corollary

18
).

2.2
Approximation algorithm for instances with small parameter distance

Consider the case where
d_{\mathrm{par}}(\mu,\nu)<\theta
. The total variation distance
d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left({\mu},{\nu}\right)
can be very small (e.g.,
\exp(-\Omega(n))
). We cannot use additive-error approximation algorithm to efficiently achieve a relative-error approximation. The main challenge lies in the term
1-{\nu(\sigma)}/{\mu(\sigma)}
in (
3
). With approximate counting oracles, we can approximate
{\nu(\sigma)}/{\mu(\sigma)}
with relative error, but there is no guarantee of relative accuracy for
1-{\nu(\sigma)}/{\mu(\sigma)}
. Due to this difficulty, previous works mainly studied graphical models on bounded treewidth graphs
[
BGM+24
]
and product distributions
[
FGJW23
]
, where
{\nu(\sigma)}
and
{\mu(\sigma)}
can be computed
exactly
.

We overcome this challenge by designing an alternative estimator with good concentration property.
We use the hardcore model as an example to illustrate the main idea.
Assume both
\mu
and
\nu
are hardcore models
(G,\lambda^{\mu})
and
(G,\lambda^{\nu})
satisfying uniqueness condition in (
1
).
For the simplicity of the overview, let us further assume that for any for
v\in V
,
0<\lambda_{v}^{\mu}\leq\lambda_{v}^{\nu}
. In words,
\nu
is obtained by increasing some external fields of
\mu
by a small amount. We will deal with the general case in later technical sections.

Basic case without small external fields

Let us start with a simple case where
\lambda^{\mu}_{v}=\Theta(\frac{1}{\Delta})
for all
v\in V
. The uniqueness condition guarantees that
\lambda^{\mu}_{v}=O(\frac{1}{\Delta})
. The assumption additionally requires that every
\lambda^{\mu}_{v}
cannot be too small. By the definitions of total variation distance and Gibbs distributions, we can compute

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left({\mu},{\nu}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma\in\{\pm\}^{V}}\mu(\sigma)\left|1-\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}\right|=\frac{Z_{\mu}}{2Z_{\nu}}\cdot\sum_{\sigma\in\{\pm\}^{V}}\mu(\sigma)\left|\frac{Z_{\nu}}{Z_{\mu}}-\frac{w_{\nu}(\sigma)}{w_{\mu}(\sigma)}\right|.

(4)

The first term
\frac{Z_{\mu}}{2Z_{\nu}}
can be approximated with relative error by approximate counting oracle. For the second term, we consider the following random variable, which also appears in the previous work of approximate counting
[
ŠVV09
]
,

\displaystyle W\triangleq\frac{w_{\nu}(\sigma)}{w_{\mu}(\sigma)},\text{ where }\sigma\sim\mu.

Note that
\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[W\right]=\frac{Z_{\nu}}{Z_{\mu}}
. By (
4
), our task is reduced to estimate the value of
\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[|\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[W\right]-W|\right]
. We are in the case that
0\leq\lambda^{\nu}_{v}-\lambda^{\mu}_{v}\leq d_{\mathrm{par}}(\mu,\nu)<\theta=\frac{1}{\mathrm{poly}(n)}
, so that for any
\sigma\in\{\pm\}^{V}
,
\frac{w_{\mu}(\sigma)}{w_{\nu}(\sigma)}\approx 1
.
We will utilize this property to show that
W
has a good concentration property. Formally,

\displaystyle 1\leq\frac{w_{\nu}(\sigma)}{w_{\mu}(\sigma)}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{v\in V:\sigma_{v}=+1}\frac{\lambda_{v}^{\nu}}{\lambda^{\mu}_{v}}\leq\prod_{v\in V:\sigma_{v}=+1}\left(1+\frac{d_{\mathrm{par}}(\mu,\nu)}{\lambda^{\mu}_{v}}\right)

\displaystyle(\star)\quad

\displaystyle\leq 1+O\left(\sum_{v\in V:\sigma_{v}=+1}\frac{d_{\mathrm{par}}(\mu,\nu)}{\lambda^{\mu}_{v}}\right)\leq 1+O(n\Delta)\cdot d_{\mathrm{par}}(\mu,\nu).

(5)

In inequality
(\star)
, we use the fact that
\lambda^{\mu}_{v}=\Omega(\frac{1}{\Delta})
so that
\frac{d_{\mathrm{par}}(\mu,\nu)}{\lambda^{\mu}_{v}}=O(\Delta\theta)
,
since we choose
\theta=\frac{1}{\text{poly}(n)}
small enough, then
\frac{d_{\mathrm{par}}(\mu,\nu)}{\lambda^{\mu}_{v}}\ll\frac{1}{n}
and the inequality can be verified as follows

\displaystyle\prod_{v\in V:\sigma_{v}=+1}\left(1+\frac{d_{\mathrm{par}}(\mu,\nu)}{\lambda^{\mu}_{v}}\right)\leq\exp\underbrace{\left(\sum_{v\in V:\sigma_{v}=+1}\frac{d_{\mathrm{par}}(\mu,\nu)}{\lambda^{\mu}_{v}}\right)}_{\ll n\cdot(1/n)=o(1)}=1+O\left(\sum_{v\in V:\sigma_{v}=+1}\frac{d_{\mathrm{par}}(\mu,\nu)}{\lambda^{\mu}_{v}}\right).

By
Lemma

15
,
d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left({\mu},{\nu}\right)=\Omega(d_{\mathrm{par}}(\mu,\nu))
. This implies
W
enjoys a very good concentration property such that

\displaystyle 1\leq W\leq 1+O(n\Delta)\cdot d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left({\mu},{\nu}\right)\quad\implies\quad\mathbf{\mathbf{Var}}\left[W\right]\leq O(n^{2}\Delta^{2})\cdot(d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left({\mu},{\nu}\right))^{2}.

(6)

Now, our algorithm can be outline as follows.

•

Call approximate counting oracles to obtain
\hat{Z}_{\mu}
and
\hat{Z}_{\nu}
with relative error
O({\varepsilon})
.

•

Draw
T=\mathrm{poly}(n/\varepsilon)
samples
W_{1},\dots,W_{T}
from
W
independently.

•

Compute
\bar{W}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}W_{i}
.
(approximate
\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[W\right]
)

•

Compute
\bar{E}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}|W_{i}-\bar{W}|
.
(approximate
\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[|W-\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[W\right]|\right]
)

•

Return
\hat{d}=\frac{\hat{Z}_{\mu}}{2\hat{Z}_{\nu}}\bar{E}
.

Using the variance bound in (
6
), we can prove that with high probability,
\bar{E}
approximates
\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[|W-\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[W\right]|\right]
with an additive error of
O(\varepsilon)\cdot d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left({\mu},{\nu}\right)
.
By (
2.2
), we can also verify that
\frac{Z_{\mu}}{Z_{\nu}}=O(1)
. Hence, we can bound the error as follows

\displaystyle\hat{d}

\displaystyle\leq(1+O(\varepsilon))\frac{Z_{\nu}}{2Z_{\mu}}\cdot\left(\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[|W-\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[W\right]|\right]+O(\varepsilon)\cdot d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left({\mu},{\nu}\right)\right)\leq(1+O(\varepsilon))d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left({\mu},{\nu}\right).

A similar analysis gives the lower bound
\hat{d}\geq(1-O(\varepsilon))d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left({\mu},{\nu}\right)
. This achieves the relative-error approximation of the TV-distance.
The above technique can be generalized to Ising models and hardcore models with a marginal lower bound.

General case containing small external fields

For the general case, there may exist vertex
v\in V
such that the external field
\lambda^{\mu}_{v}\ll d_{\mathrm{par}}(\mu,\nu)
. In this case, the inequality
(\star)
in (
2.2
) may not hold. In fact, it will cause a fundamental problem to the above algorithm. Consider the case that
\lambda^{\mu}_{v}=\exp(-n)
and
\lambda^{\nu}_{v}=\lambda^{\mu}_{v}+D
for all
v\in V
, where
D>0
. If we draw polynomial number of samples
\sigma\sim\mu
, typically, every sample
\sigma
corresponds to the empty set, i.e.,
\sigma_{u}=-1
for all
u\in V
. Hence, with high probability, all
W_{i}
in the above algorithm are
\frac{w_{\nu}(\varnothing)}{w_{\mu}(\varnothing)}=1
and the algorithm will return
\hat{d}=0
. However, the true TV-distance
d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left({\mu},{\nu}\right)>0
is positive.

Let us first consider a special case such that for all
v\in V
,
\lambda^{\mu}_{v}<\kappa
and
\lambda^{\nu}_{v}<\kappa
, where
\kappa=\mathrm{poly}(\frac{\varepsilon}{n})\ll\frac{1}{n}
is very small.
The total variation distance is the sum of
\frac{1}{2}|\mu(\sigma)-\nu(\sigma)|
for all independent sets
\sigma\in\{\pm\}^{V}
.
In this special case, all external fields are tiny, so that large independent sets appear with very low probability.
Let
\|\sigma\|_{+}
be the number of
+1
in
\sigma
.
We can show that there is a constant
t=O(1)
depending on
\kappa
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma\in\{\pm\}^{V}:\|\sigma\|_{+}\geq t+1}|\mu(\sigma)-\nu(\sigma)|\leq O(\varepsilon)\cdot d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left({\mu},{\nu}\right).

In words, to approximate the total variation distance, we only need to consider the independent sets with size at most
t
.
Now, we define a distribution
\mu^{\prime}
as the distribution
\mu
restricted on the independent sets with size at most
t
.
Similarly, we can define
\nu^{\prime}
from
\nu
.
Since
t
is a constant, we can enumerate all independent sets with size at most
t
and compute the total variation distance between
\mu^{\prime}
and
\nu^{\prime}
.
We can show that
d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left({\mu^{\prime}},{\nu^{\prime}}\right)
approximate
d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left({\mu},{\nu}\right)
with
\varepsilon
relative error.

For the most general case, we divide the vertices into two groups. The big group
B
contains all vertices
v\in V
such that
\min\{\lambda^{\mu}_{v},\lambda^{\nu}_{v}\}>\kappa
. The small group
S
contains all vertices
v\in V
such that
\min\{\lambda^{\mu}_{v},\lambda^{\nu}_{v}\}\leq\kappa
for a small
\kappa=\mathrm{poly}(\frac{\varepsilon}{n})
. The total variation distance is

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left({\mu},{\nu}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma\in\{\pm\}^{V}}\left|\mu(\sigma)-{\nu(\sigma)}\right|=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\{\pm\}^{B}}\sum_{y\in\{\pm\}^{S}}\left|\mu_{B}(x)\mu_{S}^{x}(y)-\nu_{B}(x)\nu_{S}^{x}(y)\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\{\pm\}^{B}}\mu_{B}(x)\cdot\underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{y\in\{\pm\}^{S}}\left|\frac{\nu_{B}(x)}{\mu_{B}(x)}\nu_{S}^{x}(y)-\mu_{S}^{x}(y)\right|}_{f(x)}.

In a high level, our algorithm will draw independent samples
x\sim\mu_{B}
from the marginal distribution and approximately compute the value of
f(x)
. The algorithm finally outputs the average value of
f(x)
over all samples.
To make the idea work, we need to deal with the following two main technical challenging.

•

We need to bound the variance of
f(x)
where
x\sim\mu_{B}
to show that polynomial number of samples are sufficient for approximation. To achieve a good bound, we use the
Poincaré inequality
for hardcore model in uniqueness regime
[
CFYZ21
]
to control the variance.

•

Given a sample
x\sim\mu_{B}
, we also need to approximately compute the value of
f(x)
. We need to (1) approximate two distributions
\mu_{S}^{x}
and
\nu_{S}^{x}
over
\{\pm\}^{S}
; and (2) approximate the ratio
\frac{\nu_{B}(x)}{\mu_{B}(x)}
. For the first task, note that both
\mu_{S}^{x}
and
\nu_{S}^{x}
are hardcore distributions on the induced subgraph
G[S]
such that for all
v\in S
, the external fields are small. Hence, we can approximate them using distributions over independent sets of size at most
t=O(1)
. A similar idea will also be use for the second task: to estimate marginal probabilities
\mu_{B}(x)
and
\nu_{B}(x)
, we also need to consider the total weight of all independent sets
I
in
G[S]
such that
I\cup\{v\in B\mid x_{v}=+1\}
forms a independent set in
G
. Again, we show that the approximation algorithm only needs to consider all such
I
with
|I|\leq t=O(1)
.

All the technical details for general case are given in
Section

5.2

3.1
Analysis for the hardcore model

Recall that our problem setting is: Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph, and
(G,\lambda^{\mu})
and
(G,\lambda^{\nu})
are two hardcore models on the same graph, satisfying the uniqueness condition in (

). Let
\mu
and
\nu
denote distributions of
(G,\lambda^{\mu})
and
(G,\lambda^{\nu})
respectively. The parameter distance
d_{\mathrm{par}}(\mu,\nu)
for hardcore model is defined by
d_{\mathrm{par}}(\mu,\nu)\triangleq\|\lambda^{\mu}-\lambda^{\nu}\|_{\infty}
.
Now we prove the first hardcore model part of
Lemma

15
:

d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left({\mu},{\nu}\right)\geq C^{\text{TV}}_{\mathrm{par}}\cdot d_{\mathrm{par}}(\mu,\nu),\quad\text{where }C^{\text{TV}}_{\mathrm{par}}=\frac{1}{5000}.

Let
i\in V
be one vertex such that
|\lambda_{i}^{\mu}-\lambda_{i}^{\nu}|=d_{\mathrm{par}}(\mu,\nu)
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\lambda_{i}^{\mu}-\lambda_{i}^{\nu}=d_{\mathrm{par}}(\mu,\nu)
. Otherwise, we can flip the roles of
\mu
and
\nu
in the following proof.
Define two collections of independent sets of
V
:

\displaystyle H_{1}:

\displaystyle=\{S\text{ is an independent set of }G\mid i\in S\},

\displaystyle H_{2}:

\displaystyle=\{S\setminus\{i\}\mid S\in H_{1}\}.

In this proof, we use
\mu(S)
to denote the probability of
\sigma\in\{\pm\}^{V}
in
\mu
such that
\sigma_{i}=+1
if and only if
i\in S
.
Define
\mu(H_{1})=\sum_{S\in H_{1}}\mu(S)
and
\mu(H_{2}),\nu(H_{1}),\nu(H_{2})
in a similar way.
It is easy to verify that

\displaystyle\frac{\mu(H_{1})}{\mu(H_{2})}=\lambda_{i}^{\mu},\quad\frac{\nu(H_{1})}{\nu(H_{2})}=\lambda_{i}^{\nu}.

Then, we consider two cases depending on the value of
|\mu(H_{2})-\nu(H_{2})|
. The simple case is
|\mu(H_{2})-\nu(H_{2})|\geq C^{\text{TV}}_{\mathrm{par}}\cdot d_{\mathrm{par}}(\mu,\nu)
, then
d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left({\mu},{\nu}\right)\geq|\mu(H_{2})-\nu(H_{2})|\geq C^{\text{TV}}_{\mathrm{par}}\cdot d_{\mathrm{par}}(\mu,\nu)
.

The main case is
|\mu(H_{2})-\nu(H_{2})|<C^{\text{TV}}_{\mathrm{par}}\cdot d_{\mathrm{par}}(\mu,\nu)
. In this case we show that
|\mu(H_{1})-\nu(H_{1})|\geq C^{\text{TV}}_{\mathrm{par}}\cdot d_{\mathrm{par}}(\mu,\nu)
, which also implies
d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left({\mu},{\nu}\right)\geq|\mu(H_{1})-\nu(H_{1})|\geq C^{\text{TV}}_{\mathrm{par}}\cdot d_{\mathrm{par}}(\mu,\nu)
.
We can lower bound the value of
|\mu(H_{1})-\nu(H_{1})|
as follows:

\displaystyle\mu(H_{1})-\nu(H_{1})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{i}^{\mu}\cdot\mu(H_{2})-\lambda_{i}^{\nu}\cdot\nu(H_{2})

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{i}^{\nu}+d_{\mathrm{par}}(\mu,\nu))\mu(H_{2})-\lambda_{i}^{\nu}\cdot\nu(H_{2})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{i}^{\nu}(\mu(H_{2})-\nu(H_{2}))+d_{\mathrm{par}}(\mu,\nu)\cdot\mu(H_{2})

\displaystyle>d_{\mathrm{par}}(\mu,\nu)\cdot\mu(H_{2})-\lambda_{i}^{\nu}\cdot C^{\text{TV}}_{\mathrm{par}}\cdot d_{\mathrm{par}}(\mu,\nu).

(7)

Because
(G,\lambda^{\mu})
and
(G,\lambda^{\nu})
satisfy the uniqueness condition, we have for all vertex
i\in V
, all
x\in\{\mu,\nu\}
,
\lambda_{i}^{x}\leq\lambda_{c}(\Delta)=\frac{(\Delta-1)^{\Delta-1}}{(\Delta-2)^{\Delta}}\leq 4
because
\Delta\geq 3
.
Let
N(i)
denotes the set of neighbors of
i
in graph
G=(V,E)
.
By definition of
H_{2}
,
\mu(H_{2})
is the probability of
j\notin S
for all
j\in N(i)\cup\{i\}

4
Additive-error approximation algorithm

4.1
TV-distance between two Gibbs distributions

We first present an algorithm can achieves the additive-error approximation to the total variation distance between two
general
Gibbs distributions, which covers Ising and hardcore models as special cases.
Let
\mu
over
\{0,1\}^{V}
be a Gibbs distribution over graph
G=(V,E)
. For any configuration
\sigma\in\{0,1\}^{V}
,
\mu(\sigma)=w_{\mu}(\sigma)/Z_{\mu}
, where
w_{\mu}(\cdot)
is the weight function and
Z_{\mu}=\sum_{\tau\in\{0,1\}^{V}}w_{\mu}(\sigma)
.
Let
T^{\textsf{wt}}_{G}\in\mathbb{N}
.
We say the Gibbs distribution
\mu
admits a weight oracle with cost
T^{\textsf{wt}}_{G}
is given any
\sigma\in[q]^{V}
, it returns the exact weight
w_{\mu}(\sigma)
in time
T^{\textsf{wt}}_{G}
.
Note that both Ising and hardcore models, as well as most Gibbs distributions, admit weight oracle with cost
T^{\textsf{wt}}_{G}=O(|V|+|E|)
.
Recall the sampling and approximate counting oracles are defined in
Definition

1
.

Theorem 17
.

There exists an algorithm such that given two general Gibbs distributions
\mu
and
\nu
on the same graph
G=(V,E)
and an error bound
\varepsilon>0
, if
\mu
and
\nu
both admit weight, sampling, and approximate counting oracles with cost
T^{\textsf{wt}}_{G}
and cost functions
T^{\textsf{sp}}_{G}(\cdot)
and
T^{\textsf{ct}}_{G}(\cdot)
respectively, then it returns a random
\hat{d}
in time
O(T^{\textsf{ct}}_{G}(\frac{\varepsilon}{4})+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}(T^{\textsf{wt}}_{G}+T^{\textsf{sp}}_{G}(\frac{\varepsilon}{4})))
such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{Pr}\left[d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left({\mu},{\nu}\right)-\varepsilon\leq\hat{d}\leq d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left({\mu},{\nu}\right)+\varepsilon\right]\geq\frac{2}{3}.

Proof.

Define a random variable
X\in[0,1]
such that
X=\max\left(0,1-\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}\right)
, where
\sigma\sim\mu
.

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left({\mu},{\nu}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma:\mu(\sigma)>\nu(\sigma)}|\mu(\sigma)-\nu(\sigma)|=\sum_{\sigma:\mu(\sigma)>\nu(\sigma)}\mu(\sigma)\left|1-\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma}\mu(\sigma)\max\left(0,1-\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}\right)=\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[X\right].

By the definition of
X
, we have
0\leq X\leq 1
, so
\mathbf{\mathbf{Var}}\left[X\right]\leq 1
.
Ideally, we want to draw independent samples of
X
and take average to approximate
d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left({\mu},{\nu}\right)
. However, the main issue is that given a
\sigma\sim\mu
, we cannot compute neither
\mu(\sigma)
nor
\nu(\sigma)
exactly. Alternatively, we will define another random variable
\hat{X}\in[0,1]
that approximate the random variable
X
.

Call the approximate counting oracles of
\mu
and
\nu
to obtain
\hat{Z_{\mu}}
and
\hat{Z_{\nu}}
that approximate partition functions
Z_{\mu}
and
Z_{\nu}
with relative error bound
\frac{\varepsilon}{4}
.
We may assume both counting oracles succeed, which happens with probability at least 0.98.
Define the random variable
\hat{X}\in[0,1]
by the following process.

1.

Call sampling oracle of
\mu
to obtain one sample
\sigma\in\{\pm\}^{V}
such that
d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left({\sigma},{\mu}\right)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4}
.

2.

Call weight oracles of both
\mu
and
\nu
to obtain exact weights
w_{\mu}(\sigma),w_{\nu}(\sigma)
. Compute
\hat{\mu}(\sigma)={w_{\mu}(\sigma)}/{\hat{Z_{\mu}}}
and
\hat{\nu}(\sigma)={w_{\nu}(\sigma)}/{\hat{Z_{\nu}}}
.

3.

Define
\hat{X}=\max(0,1-{\hat{\nu}(\sigma)}/{\hat{\mu}(\sigma)})
, in particular,
\hat{X}=0
if
\hat{\mu}(\sigma)=0
.

Let
T=\frac{64}{\varepsilon^{2}}
.
Our algorithm is the following simple process:

•

Draw
T
independent samples
\hat{X}_{1},\hat{X}_{2},\ldots,\hat{X}_{T}
of random variable
\hat{X}
.

•

Output the average
\hat{d}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}\hat{X}_{i}
.

It is easy to see the running time of our algorithm is
2T^{\textsf{ct}}_{G}(\frac{\varepsilon}{4})+T\cdot(T^{\textsf{sp}}_{G}(\frac{\varepsilon}{4})+2T^{\textsf{wt}}_{G}+O(1))
.

To prove the correctness of our algorithm, we only need to show that

\displaystyle\left|\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[\hat{X}\right]-\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[X\right]\right|=\left|\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[\hat{X}\right]-d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left({\mu},{\nu}\right)\right|\leq\frac{7\varepsilon}{8}.

(20)

Note that
0\leq\hat{X}\leq 1
so that
\mathbf{\mathbf{Var}}\left[\hat{X}\right]\leq 1
. By Hoeffding’s inequality, it is easy to show that with probability at least 0.9,
|\hat{d}-\mathbb{E}[\hat{X}]|\leq\varepsilon/8
. Combining with (
20
) proves the theorem.

Now, we only need to verify (
20
).
We introduce a new random variable
X^{*}
in analysis.
In the definition of
\hat{X}
,
assume we replace the sample
\sigma
in
Item

1
with a perfect sampler of the distribution
\mu
. Let
X^{*}
denote the resulting random variable. We first compare
\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[X^{*}\right]
with
\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[X\right]
. The difference between
X^{*}
and
X
comes from the error of computing the ratio of
\mu(\sigma)
and
\nu(\sigma)
.
Note that
\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}=\frac{w_{\nu}(\sigma)}{w_{\mu}(\sigma)}\cdot\frac{Z_{\mu}}{Z_{\nu}}
and
\frac{\hat{\nu}(\sigma)}{\hat{\mu}(\sigma)}=\frac{w_{\nu}(\sigma)}{w_{\mu}(\sigma)}\cdot\frac{\hat{Z}_{\mu}}{\hat{Z}_{\nu}}
. By the definition of approximate counting oracle, for
\sigma
with
\mu(\sigma)>0
,

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{5\varepsilon}{8}\right)\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}\leq\frac{\hat{\nu}(\sigma)}{\hat{\mu}(\sigma)}\leq\left(1+\frac{5\varepsilon}{8}\right)\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}.

We can compute the expectation as

\displaystyle\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[X^{*}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma:\mu(\sigma)>0}\mu(\sigma)\max\left(0,1-\frac{\hat{\nu}(\sigma)}{\hat{\mu}(\sigma)}\right)\leq\sum_{\sigma:\mu(\sigma)>0}\mu(\sigma)\max\left(0,1-\left(1-\frac{5\varepsilon}{8}\right)\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\sigma:\mu(\sigma)>0}\mu(\sigma)\left(\max\left(0,1-\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}\right)+\frac{5\varepsilon}{8}\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}\right)\leq d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left({\mu},{\nu}\right)+\frac{5\varepsilon}{8}\sum_{\sigma:\mu(\sigma)>0}\nu(\sigma)

\displaystyle\leq d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left({\mu},{\nu}\right)+\frac{5\varepsilon}{8}.

Using the same way, we could verify the other direction. We have

\displaystyle|\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[X^{*}\right]-\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[X\right]|=|\mathbf{E}[X^{*}]-d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left({\mu},{\nu}\right)|\leq\frac{5\varepsilon}{8}.

(21)

Next, we compare
X^{*}
to
\hat{X}
. The only difference is that they sample
\sigma
from different distributions. Let
\mu^{\prime}
be the distribution defined by the approximate sampling oracle.
Define a function
f
such that for any
\sigma\in\{\pm\}^{V}
,
f(\sigma)=\max(0,1-{\hat{\nu}(\sigma)}/{\hat{\mu}(\sigma)})
, where we set
f(\sigma)=0
if
\hat{\mu}(\sigma)=0
.
We have
|\mathbf{E}(\hat{X})-\mathbf{E}(X^{*})|=\left|\mathbf{E}_{\sigma\sim\mu}[f(\sigma)]-\mathbf{E}_{\sigma\sim\mu^{\prime}}[f(\sigma)]\right|
.
Define
A=\{\sigma\mid\mu(\sigma)>\mu^{\prime}(\sigma)\}
and
B=\{\sigma\mid\mu(\sigma)<\mu^{\prime}(\sigma)\}
.
We can write

\displaystyle|\mathbf{E}(\hat{X})-\mathbf{E}(X^{*})|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{\sigma\in A}(\mu^{\prime}(\sigma)-\mu(\sigma))f(\sigma)+\sum_{\sigma\in B}(\mu^{\prime}(\sigma)-\mu(\sigma))f(\sigma)\right|

(
\star
)

\displaystyle\leq\max\left(\sum_{\sigma\in A}(\mu(\sigma)-\mu^{\prime}(\sigma))f(\sigma),\sum_{\sigma\in B}(\mu^{\prime}(\sigma)-\mu(\sigma))f(\sigma)\right)

(by
f(\sigma)\in[0,1]
)

\displaystyle\leq d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left({\mu},{\mu^{\prime}}\right)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4},

(22)

where in inequality (
\star
), we use
\sum_{\sigma\in A}(\mu^{\prime}(\sigma)-\mu(\sigma))f(\sigma)\leq 0
and
\sum_{\sigma\in B}(\mu^{\prime}(\sigma)-\mu(\sigma))f(\sigma)\geq 0
.
Finally, (
20
) holds due to (
21
) and (
4.1
).
∎

Define a random variable

1.

Pre-processing step

For Ising model, we need the following pre-processing step to reduce the general Ising model to the soft-Ising model.
Recall that an Ising model

Case 2: For all

Appendix B
Poincaré inequality for marginal distribution

The Poincaré inequality for hardcore model in the uniqueness regime is established in
[
CFYZ21
]
. The paper considers the hardcore model
(G,\lambda)
such that all
\lambda_{v}
are the same. By verifying the technical condition in
[
CFYZ21
, Theorem 1.9]
, the following Poincaré inequality also holds for hardcore model with different
\lambda_{v}
satisfying (
1
). For any function
g:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
, where
\Omega
is the support of the distribution, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{\mathbf{Var}}_{\mu}\left[g\right]\leq C_{\eta}\sum_{v\in V}\sum_{\sigma\in\Omega_{V-v}}\mu_{V-v}(\sigma)\mathbf{\mathbf{Var}}_{\mu^{\sigma}}\left[g\right].

Let
g^{\prime}:\Omega_{B}\to\mathbb{R}
be an arbitrary function.
Define
g(x)=g^{\prime}(x_{B})
for all
x\in\Omega
. It holds that
\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}_{\mu}\left[g\right]=\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}_{\mu_{B}}\left[g^{\prime}\right]
and
\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}_{\mu}\left[g^{2}\right]=\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}_{\mu_{B}}\left[(g^{\prime})^{2}\right]
. We have

\displaystyle\mathbf{\mathbf{Var}}_{\mu_{B}}\left[g^{\prime}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{\mathbf{Var}}_{\mu}\left[g\right]\leq C_{\eta}\sum_{v\in V}\sum_{\sigma\in\Omega_{V-v}}\mu_{V-v}(\sigma)\mathbf{\mathbf{Var}}_{\mu^{\sigma}}\left[g\right]

(
g(\sigma)
depends on
\sigma_{B}
)

\displaystyle=C_{\eta}\sum_{v\in B}\sum_{\sigma\in\Omega_{V-v}}\mu_{V-v}(\sigma)\mathbf{\mathbf{Var}}_{\mu^{\sigma}}\left[g\right].

Fix a partial assignment
\tau\in\Omega_{B-v}
. Note that
\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}_{\mu^{\tau}}\left[g\right]=\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}_{\mu^{\tau}_{B}}\left[g^{\prime}\right]
and
\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}_{\mu^{\tau}}\left[g^{2}\right]=\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}_{\mu^{\tau}_{B}}\left[(g^{\prime})^{2}\right]
. We have
\mathbf{\mathbf{Var}}_{\mu^{\tau}}\left[g\right]=\mathbf{\mathbf{Var}}_{\mu^{\tau}_{B}}\left[g^{\prime}\right]
. Let
X\sim\mu^{\tau}
and
Y=g(X)
. By the law of total variance,

\displaystyle\mathbf{\mathbf{Var}}_{\mu^{\tau}_{B}}\left[g^{\prime}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{\mathbf{Var}}_{\mu^{\tau}}\left[g\right]=\mathbf{\mathbf{Var}}\left[Y\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[\mathbf{\mathbf{Var}}\left[Y\mid X_{V-v}\right]\right]+\mathbf{\mathbf{Var}}\left[\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[Y\mid X_{V-v}\right]\right]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[\mathbf{\mathbf{Var}}\left[Y\mid X_{V-v}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\Omega^{\tau}_{V-v}}\mu^{\tau}_{V-v}(\sigma)\mathbf{\mathbf{Var}}_{\mu^{\sigma}}\left[g\right],

where
\Omega^{\tau}_{V-v}\subseteq\{\pm\}^{V-v}
is the support of
\mu^{\tau}_{V-v}
.
Combining with the above inequality, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{\mathbf{Var}}_{\mu_{B}}\left[g^{\prime}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C_{\eta}\sum_{v\in B}\sum_{\tau\in\Omega_{B-v}}\mu_{B-v}(\tau)\sum_{\sigma\in\Omega^{\tau}_{V-v}}\mu^{\tau}_{V-v}(\sigma)\mathbf{\mathbf{Var}}_{\mu^{\sigma}}\left[g\right]

\displaystyle\leq C_{\eta}\sum_{v\in B}\sum_{\tau\in\Omega_{B-v}}\mu_{B-v}(\tau)\mathbf{\mathbf{Var}}_{\mu^{\tau}_{B}}\left[g^{\prime}\right].

This proves the Poincaré inequality for the marginal distribution.

-divergence in the down walk of Glauber dynamics. The results follows from the data processing inequality. See

FGJW23

for more details.

The space of foliations on projective spaces in positive characteristic
Wodson Mendson, Jorge Vitório Pereira

Abstract
. This work explores the space of foliations on projective spaces over algebraically closed fields of positive characteristic, with a particular focus on the codimension one case. It describes how the irreducible components of these spaces vary with the characteristic of the base field in very low degrees and establishes an arbitrary characteristic version of Calvo-Andrade’s stability of generic logarithmic 1 1 -forms under deformation.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Context

The study of the space of holomorphic foliations on projective spaces has been a subject of significant interest since the pioneering work of Jouanolou. Inspired by classical contributions from Jacobi, Darboux, and others, Jouanolou described codimension one foliations of degree one on projective spaces in his book
[
11
]
. In particular, his results imply that the space of codimension one holomorphic foliations of degree one on projective spaces, of dimension at least three, has exactly two irreducible components.

More recently, in
[
12
]
, it was noted that work by Medeiros
[
8
]
provides an analogous description for degree one holomorphic foliations of arbitrary codimension on projective spaces.

Cerveau and Lins Neto
[
3
]
proved that the space of codimension one holomorphic foliations of degree two on projective spaces of dimension at least three has exactly six irreducible components and described precisely each one of them. The analogous problem for degree two holomorphic foliations of arbitrary codimension was recently studied by Corrêa and Muniz
[
4
]
. However, a complete description of the irreducible components of the space of degree two holomorphic foliations of codimension
q
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}
remains an open question for any
q
strictly between
1
and
n-1
. The situation for degree three foliations is even less understood. While a partial classification exists for codimension one foliations, see
[
7
]
, the problem remains unexplored for higher codimensions.

1.2.
Purpose and discussion of the main results

This work aims to initiate the study of the space of foliations on projective spaces over algebraically closed fields of positive characteristic, with a focus on the codimension one case. Our initial motivation was to understand how the geometry of these spaces vary with the characteristic of the base field, though our perspective evolved throughout the study and we were led to an analog of Calvo-Andrade’s stability of generic logarithmic
1
-forms under deformation in arbitrary characteristic, see Theorem
A
below.

For codimension one foliations of degree zero and one, we provide a complete description of all irreducible components in arbitrary characteristic. For degree zero foliations, there is always a single irreducible component, see Theorem
3.5
which also describes degree zero foliations of arbitrary codimension. Nevertheless, the behavior in characteristic two is different from other characteristics. Specifically, when the characteristic is different from two, the space of degree zero foliations of codimension one can be identified with the Grassmannian of lines in
(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}})^{\vee}
, whereas in characteristic two, it corresponds to the projective space
\mathbb{P}H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}},\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}}(2))
.

For degree one foliations, the number of irreducible components depends on the characteristic. In characteristic two, there is a single irreducible component, whereas for higher characteristics, there are exactly two. Moreover, the description is uniform when the characteristic is at least
5
, see Theorem
4.8
.

A key technical tool in our analysis of foliations of degree zero and one is Medeiros’ description of locally decomposable integrable differential forms with homogeneous coefficients of degree one, see
[
8
]
. Although Medeiros originally formulated his result in the complex setting, the result remains valid in arbitrary characteristic.

For degree two foliations, our results are less definitive. We are able to show that each of the six irreducible components that exist in characteristic zero specialize to irreducible components in characteristic
p
, when
p
is at least
5
. We achieve this by combining
[
15
, Theorem B]
with the main result of this paper, namely:

Theorem A
.

Let
r\geq 2
and
1\leq d_{1}\leq\ldots\leq d_{r}
be positive integers. Let
{\mathsf{k}}
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
.
Assume that one of the following conditions holds:

(1)

r=2
and none of the integers
d_{1},d_{2},
and
d_{1}+d_{2}
is divisible by
p
; or

(2)

r=2
and both
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
are divisible by
p
; or

(3)

r>2
and the set

S=\{i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}\,|\,p\text{ divides }d_{i}\}

has cardinality different from
r-1
and
r-2
.

Then the set
\textsf{Log}_{(d_{1},\ldots,d_{r})}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
is an irreducible component of
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
(with its reduced scheme structure) for
n\geq 3
and
d=\sum_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}-2
.

For the definition of the scheme
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
we refer to Section
2.4
, while the set
\textsf{Log}_{(d_{1},\ldots,d_{r})}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
is defined in Section
4.4
.

Over the complex numbers, Theorem
A
was established by Calvo-Andrade
[
2
]
, with a proof based on complex-geometric and topological arguments. For an alternative approach using algebraic methods, which actually proves that
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n})
is generically reduced along
\textsf{Log}_{(d_{1},\ldots,d_{r})}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n})
, see
[
6
]
.

Theorem
A
offers yet another proof of Calvo-Andrade’s
result, this time from a more arithmetic perspective, see Corollary
6.3
.

2.
Basic definitions

2.1.
Foliations and distributions

Let
X
be a smooth algebraic variety defined over an arbitrary field
{\mathsf{k}}
.
A foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
X
consists of a pair
(T_{\mathcal{F}},N^{*}_{\mathcal{F}})
of coherent subsheaves of
T_{X}
and
\Omega^{1}_{X}
such that
T_{\mathcal{F}}
is the annihilator of
N^{*}_{\mathcal{F}}
,
N^{*}_{\mathcal{F}}
is the annihilator of
T_{\mathcal{F}}
, and
T_{\mathcal{F}}
is closed under the Lie bracket. The sheaf
T_{\mathcal{F}}
is the tangent sheaf of
\mathcal{F}
and the sheaf
N^{*}_{\mathcal{F}}
is the conormal sheaf of
\mathcal{F}
. The rank of
T_{\mathcal{F}}
is the dimension of
\mathcal{F}
, while the rank of
N^{*}_{\mathcal{F}}
is the codimension of
\mathcal{F}
. Since
T_{\mathcal{F}}
and
N^{*}_{\mathcal{F}}
are annihilators of each other, we have that
\dim\mathcal{F}+\codim\mathcal{F}=\dim X
.

Dropping the closedness under Lie brackets, one obtains the definition of a distribution. To wit, a distribution
\mathcal{D}
on
X
consists of a pair
(T_{\mathcal{D}},N^{*}_{\mathcal{D}})
of subsheaves of
T_{X}
and
\Omega^{1}_{X}
such that
T_{\mathcal{D}}
is the annihilator of
N^{*}_{\mathcal{D}}
and vice-versa.

2.2.
Differential forms

If
\mathcal{D}
is a distribution of codimension
q
, taking the determinant of the inclusion
N^{*}_{\mathcal{D}}\to\Omega^{1}_{X}
and tensoring the result by
N=\det(N^{*}_{\mathcal{D}})^{*}
yields a
q
-differential form with coefficients in
N
, that is

\omega\in H^{0}(X,\Omega^{q}_{X}\otimes N)

satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

The zero locus of
\omega
has codimension at least two;

(2)

At any closed point
x
outside the zero locus of
\omega
, and in any local trivialization of
N
, the germ of the differential
q
-form
\omega
at
x
can be written as the product of
q
germs of
1
-forms,
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{q}
;

(3)

Moreover, if
\mathcal{D}
is not only a distribution but also a foliation then the germs of
1
-forms of the previous item are such that

d\omega_{j}\wedge\omega_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{q}=0

for any
j\in\{1,\ldots,q\}
.

The twisted
q
-form
\omega
is not unique, but is unique up to multiplication by elements of
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}^{*})
, that is, nowhere-vanishing regular functions.

We define saturated
q
-forms as those satisfying Condition (
1
);
locally decomposable
q
-forms as those satisfying Condition (
2
); and
integrable
q
-forms as those satisfying Conditions (
2
) and (
3
).
In Medeiros’ original terminology, locally decomposable forms were referred to as locally decomposable off the singular set (LDS) forms. We adopt the shorter term locally decomposable for the sake of brevity.

Conversely, given a nonzero twisted
q
-form
\omega\in H^{0}(X,\Omega^{q}_{X}\otimes N)
, the kernel of the natural morphism induced by contraction with
\omega

\omega^{\flat}:T_{X}\to\Omega^{q-1}_{X}\otimes N

is a saturated subsheaf of
T_{X}
. Consequently,
(\ker\omega^{\flat},\Ann(\ker\omega^{\flat}))=(T_{\mathcal{D}},N^{*}_{\mathcal{D}})
for some codimension
q
distribution
\mathcal{D}
.

2.3.
Plücker’s equations

As noted by Medeiros in
[
8
]
, see also
[
15
, Appendix B]
, a twisted
q
-form
\omega\in H^{0}(X,\Omega^{q}_{X}\otimes N)
is locally decomposable if, and only if, the morphism

\displaystyle\bigwedge^{q-1}T_{X}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\Omega_{X}^{q+1}\otimes N^{\otimes 2}

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle\longmapsto i_{v}\omega\wedge\omega

is identically zero. Notice that this condition can be rephrased as the classical Plücker conditions

i_{v}\omega\wedge\omega=0\text{ for any }v\in\bigwedge^{q-1}T_{X}\,.

Similarly, a
q
-form
\omega
is integrable if, and only if, it is locally decomposable and the morphism

\displaystyle\bigwedge^{q-1}T_{X}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\Omega_{X}^{q+2}\otimes N^{\otimes 2}

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle\longmapsto i_{v}\omega\wedge d\omega

is identically zero. Here and above, when
q=1
, we adopt the convention that
\wedge^{0}T_{X}=\mathcal{O}_{X}
with interior product with elements of
\Omega_{X}^{\bullet}
given by the usual multiplication. Hence, for
q=1
, the integrability condition is the usual
\omega\wedge d\omega=0
as a section of
\Omega^{3}_{X}\otimes N^{\otimes 2}
.

Remark 2.1
.

The formulation of Plücker’s condition above is independent of the characteristic. However, note that in characteristic zero, the local decomposability of a
2
-form is equivalent to the condition
\omega\wedge\omega=0
. This equivalence fails in characteristic two. For instance, the
2
-form
\omega=dx_{0}\wedge dx_{1}+dx_{2}\wedge dx_{3}
is not decomposable in any characteristic, yet
\omega\wedge\omega=2dx_{0}\wedge dx_{1}\wedge dx_{2}\wedge dx_{3}
, making
\omega\wedge\omega=0
equal to zero in characteristic two.

2.4.
The schemes
\textsf{Dist}^{q}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
and
\textsf{Fol}^{q}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})

Let
{\mathsf{k}}
be an arbitrary algebraically closed field. Let
\mathcal{D}
be a codimension
q
distribution on the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
,
n\geq q+1
.
Let
\omega\in H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}},\Omega^{q}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}}\otimes N)
be a twisted
q
-form with zero set of codimension at least two whose kernel
equals
T_{\mathcal{D}}
. The degree of
\mathcal{D}
is, by definition, the
degree of the zero locus of
i^{*}\omega\in H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{q}_{{\mathsf{k}}},\Omega^{q}_{\mathbb{P}^{q}_{{\mathsf{k}}}}\otimes i^{*}N)
, where
i:\mathbb{P}^{q}_{{\mathsf{k}}}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
is a general linear embedding. Hence, if
d=\deg(\mathcal{D})
, then

N=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}}(d+q+1).

It follows from Euler’s sequence, that we can identify
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}},\Omega^{q}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}}(d+q+1))
with the vector space of homogeneous polynomial
q
-forms

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{q})\\
0\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{q}\leq n\end{subarray}}a_{I}(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})dx_{I}

which are annihilated by
the radial vector field
R=\sum_{i=0}^{n}x_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
, and have coefficients
a_{0},\ldots,a_{n}
of degree
d+1
.
We will say that a homogeneous polynomial
q
-form on
\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
representing an element of
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}},\Omega^{q}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}}(d+q+1))
is a projective
q
-form of degree
d+q+1
.

The space of codimension
q
foliations of degree
d
on the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
is, by definition, the locally closed subscheme
\textsf{Fol}^{q}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
of
\mathbb{P}H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}},\Omega^{q}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}}(d+q+1))

defined by the conditions

[\omega]\in\textsf{Fol}^{q}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})\text{ if, and only if, }\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\codim\sing(\omega)\geq 2,\\
i_{v}\omega\wedge\omega=0\text{ for every }v\in\bigwedge^{q-1}{\mathbb{A}^{n+1}},\text{ and }\\
i_{v}\omega\wedge d\omega=0\text{ for every }v\in\bigwedge^{q-1}{\mathbb{A}^{n+1}}.\end{array}\right.

Likewise, the space of codimension
q
distributions of degree
d
on the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
is, by definition, the locally closed subscheme
\textsf{Dist}^{q}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
of
\mathbb{P}H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}},\Omega^{q}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}}(d+q+1))

defined by the conditions

[\omega]\in\textsf{Dist}^{q}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})\text{ if, and only if, }\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\codim\sing(\omega)\geq 2,\text{ and }\\
i_{v}\omega\wedge\omega=0\text{ for every }v\in\bigwedge^{q-1}{\mathbb{A}^{n+1}}.\end{array}\right.

3.
Foliations of degree zero

3.1.
Euler’s relation

If
\omega
is a homogeneous
q
-form of degree
e
on
\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
, meaning that the coefficients of
\omega
have degree
e-q
, then
by Euler’s relation, we have

(3.1)

i_{R}d\omega+di_{R}\omega=e\cdot\omega\,.

Note that in our conventions both
x_{i}
and
dx_{i}
are assigned degree one.

As observed in
[
11
, Chapter 1, Proposition 1.4]
, when the characteristic of
{\mathsf{k}}
does not divide

d+q+1
, there is a bijection of the set
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\Omega^{q}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d+q+1))
of projective
q
-forms of degree
d+q+1
and the set of closed homogeneous
(q+1)
-forms of degree
d+q+1
. This bijection is given by the maps

\omega\mapsto d\omega\quad\text{and}\quad\beta\mapsto(d+q+1)^{-1}i_{R}\beta\,.

Furthermore, when
q=1
and still under the same assumption on the characteristic of
{\mathsf{k}}
, the set of integrable projective
1
-forms of degree
d+2
is in bijection with the set of closed homogeneous
2
-forms
\beta
of degree
d+2
satisfying

\beta\wedge\beta=0
.

For later use, we record a consequence of Euler’s formula.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\omega\in H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}},\Omega^{q}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}}(e))
be a nonzero projective
q
-form of degree
e
on
\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
. If
\omega
is closed then the characteristic of
{\mathsf{k}}
divides
e
. Moreover, if
e<p(q+1)
then
\omega
is exact.

Proof.

Since
\omega
is projective, Euler’s relation (
3.1
) reads

i_{R}d\omega=e\cdot\omega\,.

If
\omega
is closed, then the left-hand side vanishes and,
consequently, the characteristic of
{\mathsf{k}}
divides
e
as claimed.

If
\omega
is closed then
[
1
, Theorem 1.3.4]
implies
that

\omega=d\beta+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{q})\\
i_{1}<\cdots<i_{q}\end{subarray}}a_{I}^{p}\,(x_{i_{1}}^{p-1}dx_{i_{1}})\wedge(x_{i_{2}}^{p-1}dx_{i_{2}})\wedge\cdots\wedge(x_{i_{q}}^{p-1}dx_{i_{q}})

for a certain homogeneous
(q-1)
-form
\beta
and some homogeneous
polynomials
a_{I}\in{\mathsf{k}}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
. The homogeneity of the

a_{I}
’s follows from that of
\omega
and
\beta
, together with the
identity
(b_{1}+\cdots+b_{k})^{p}=b_{1}^{p}+\cdots+b_{k}^{p}
.
If
\omega
is projective, closed and not exact, then the sum is
non-zero, so some
a_{I}
is non-zero. The projectiveness condition then
forces
\deg(a_{I})\geq 1
, and the corresponding summand has degree

p\,\deg(a_{I})+qp\geq p(q+1)
. Hence
e\geq p(q+1)
, as wanted.
∎

3.2.
Foliations defined by closed projective polynomial
1
-forms

Lemma
3.1
implies that there are no closed projective
1
-forms of degree
e
when
p
does not divide
e
. In sharp contrast, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
{\mathsf{k}}
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
and
let
n\geq 3
and
e\geq 1
be integers. Consider the subscheme

\textsf{Cl}_{pe-2}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n}):=\mathbb{P}\!\left(\{\omega\in H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}},\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}}(pe))\,|\,d\omega=0\text{ and }\codim\sing(\omega)\geq 2\}\right)

of
\mathbb{P}H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}},\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}}(pe))
.
Then
\textsf{Cl}_{pe-2}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
is non-empty and is an irreducible component
of
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{pe-2}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
. Moreover,
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{pe-2}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
is generically
reduced at a general point of
\textsf{Cl}_{pe-2}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
.

Proof.

Let
F\in{\mathsf{k}}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
be the homogeneous polynomial

F=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i-1}x_{i}^{pe-1}

borrowed from
[
16
, Section 2]
. The differential
dF
of
F
defines a closed (hence integrable) polynomial projective
1
-form
on
\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
showing that
\textsf{Cl}_{pe-2}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
is non-empty. To establish the proposition we will compute the Zariski tangent space of
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{pe-2}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
at the foliation
\mathcal{F}
determined by
dF
.

Collecting coefficients, we can write

dF=x_{1}^{pe-1}\,dx_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\!\left(x_{j+1}^{pe-1}-x_{j-1}x_{j}^{pe-2}\right)dx_{j}-x_{n-1}x_{n}^{pe-2}\,dx_{n}.

At a zero of
dF
, the vanishing of the
dx_{0}
-coefficient forces
x_{1}=0
.
Assuming inductively
x_{1}=\cdots=x_{j}=0
for some
1\leq j\leq n-1
,
the
dx_{j}
-coefficient reduces to
x_{j+1}^{pe-1}
, forcing
x_{j+1}=0
.
Thus
dF
vanishes only along the line
\{x_{1}=\cdots=x_{n}=0\}

through the origin of
\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
, and the foliation
\mathcal{F}

determined by
dF
has singular set of codimension
n
. In particular,

\mathcal{F}
is a closed point of
\textsf{Cl}_{pe-2}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
, which is thus
non-empty.

The Zariski tangent space of
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{pe-2}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
at
\mathcal{F}
can be
identified with the vector space

\frac{\{\eta\in H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}},\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}}(pe))\,|\,dF\wedge d\eta=0\}}{{\mathsf{k}}\cdot dF}\,.

If
\eta
is a polynomial projective
1
-form of degree
pe
satisfying

dF\wedge d\eta=0
, then since
\codim\sing(dF)=n\geq 3
, de
Rham-Saito’s lemma
[
18
]
implies that
d\eta=dF\wedge\beta

for some polynomial form
\beta
. Comparing degrees, we deduce that

d\eta=0
. It follows that the Zariski tangent space of

\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{pe-2}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
at
\mathcal{F}
coincides with the Zariski tangent space
of
\textsf{Cl}_{pe-2}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
at
\mathcal{F}
. Since
\textsf{Cl}_{pe-2}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
is
defined by linear equations on the coefficients of
\omega
and is
contained in the Zariski closure of
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{pe-2}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
, we conclude
that
\textsf{Cl}_{pe-2}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
is an irreducible component of

\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{pe-2}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
and that
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{pe-2}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
is generically
reduced at
\mathcal{F}
.
∎

3.3.
Locally decomposable differential forms with linear coefficients

Below, we recall Medeiros’ description of linear locally decomposable and integrable differential forms.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
1\leq q\leq n-1
be integers, and let
\omega
be a homogeneous locally decomposable
q
-form on
\mathbb{A}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n+1}
. If the coefficients of
\omega
are linear then there exists a linear change of coordinates such that

(a)

\omega=\alpha\wedge dx_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{q-1}
for some linear
1
-form
\alpha
(when
q=1
this reads simply
\omega=\alpha
); or

(b)

\omega
can be written as

\sum_{i=0}^{q}A_{i}\,dx_{0}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{dx_{i}}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{q}\,.

for suitable linear polynomials
A_{i}
.

Moreover, if
\omega
is integrable then in Case
(a)

\alpha=df
for some quadratic polynomial
f\in{\mathsf{k}}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
and, in Case
(b)
, the polynomials
A_{i}
belong to
{\mathsf{k}}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{q}]
.

The original statement was formulated over the field
\mathbb{C}
. However, as we proceed to verify, the result remains valid over any algebraically closed field
{\mathsf{k}}
. The key modifications to Medeiros’ argument are presented in the following result.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\omega
be a linear locally decomposable and integrable
q
-form on
\mathbb{A}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n+1}
. If
\omega
is non-closed, then there is a linear change of coordinates such that

\omega=\sum_{i=0}^{q}A_{i}dx_{0}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{dx_{i}}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{q}\,,

where
A_{i}
are linear forms in
{\mathsf{k}}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{q}]
for all
i
.

Proof.

Since
\omega
is integrable, it is locally decomposable by definition and
we can write

\omega=\omega_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{q}

with
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{q}
suitable rational
1
-forms. Moreover,
the integrability of
\omega
implies that

d\omega_{j}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{q}\theta_{j\ell}\wedge\omega_{\ell}

for suitable rational
1
-forms
\theta_{j\ell}
. Therefore

d\omega=\sum_{j=1}^{q}(-1)^{j-1}\omega_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge d\omega_{j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{q}=\theta\wedge\omega

for some rational
1
-form
\theta
. Hence
d\omega
is a decomposable
(q+1)
-form. Moreover, after a suitable linear change of coordinates, decomposable
(q+1)
-forms with coefficients of degree zero can be written as
dx_{0}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{q}
.

The integrability condition
i_{v}\omega\wedge d\omega=0
, for any
(q-1)
-vector
v
implies that

\omega=\sum_{i=0}^{q}A_{i}dx_{0}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{dx_{i}}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{q}

where
A_{i}
are linear homogeneous polynomials in
{\mathsf{k}}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
. Furthermore, since
d\omega=dx_{0}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{q}
by assumption, computing the exterior derivative of
\omega
in the above form implies that the linear homogeneous polynomials
A_{i}
actually belong to
{\mathsf{k}}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{q}]
as wanted.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

Medeiros’ argument remains valid over an arbitrary algebraically closed field, except for his use of
[
8
, Proposition 1.3.1]
, which relies on analytic methods. However, this can be replaced by Lemma
3.4
above.
∎

3.4.
Foliations of degree zero

We now have all the necessary ingredients to state and prove the classification of degree zero foliations in arbitrary characteristic.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
{\mathsf{k}}
be an algebraically closed field. Let
n\geq 3
and
n-1\geq q\geq 1
be integers. If the characteristic of
{\mathsf{k}}
is different from
2
, or if
q>1
, then
\textsf{Fol}^{q}_{0}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
is isomorphic to the Grassmannian of
(q+1)
-codimensional linear subspaces of
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
, and foliations
of degree
0
are defined by linear projections
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{q}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
. If instead the characteristic
of
{\mathsf{k}}
is
2
and
q=1
, then
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})=\mathbb{P}H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}},\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}}(2))=\textsf{Cl}_{0}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
.
In all cases,
\textsf{Fol}^{q}_{0}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
(with its reduced scheme structure) is a smooth and irreducible algebraic variety.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a foliation of degree zero, and let
\omega\in H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}},\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}}^{q}(q+1))
be a projective
q
-form of degree
q+1
defining it.
We analyze the two cases predicted by Theorem
3.3
.

If
\omega
falls in Case
(a)
of Theorem
3.3
,
i.e.,
\omega=df\wedge dx_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{q-1}
, we can and will assume that
f\in{\mathsf{k}}[x_{q},\ldots,x_{n}]
. Then, expanding the projectiveness condition
i_{R}\omega=0
, we obtain:

(1)

i_{R}df=0
; and

(2)

i_{R}(dx_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{q-1})=0
.

Since
f
is quadratic, the first condition implies that the characteristic of
{\mathsf{k}}
is
2
. The second condition forces
q=1
, that is
\omega=df
.

If instead
\omega
fits in Case
(b)
of Theorem
3.3
, that is

\sum_{i=0}^{q}A_{i}(x_{0},\ldots,x_{q})dx_{0}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{dx_{i}}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{q}\,,

then the projectiveness condition
i_{R}\omega=0
, together with the exactness of the Koszul complex, implies that
\omega
is a scalar multiple of
i_{R}(dx_{0}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{q})
. Consequently, the foliation
\mathcal{F}
is defined by the kernel of the differential of the linear projection

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}

\displaystyle\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{q}_{{\mathsf{k}}}

\displaystyle(x_{0}:\ldots:x_{n})

\displaystyle\mapsto(x_{0}:\ldots:x_{q})\,.

As such,
\mathcal{F}
is completely characterized by the indeterminacy locus of this linear projection, which is the linear subspace
\{x_{0}=\cdots=x_{q}=0\}
of codimension
q+1
.
This proves the result when
q\neq 1
or the characteristic of
{\mathsf{k}}

is different from
2
. It remains to treat the case
q=1
and

\mathrm{char}({\mathsf{k}})=2
.

We first show that
\mathbb{P}H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}},\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}}(2))=\textsf{Cl}_{0}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
, i.e.,
that every projective
1
-form of degree
2
is closed in
characteristic
2
. Write
\omega=\sum_{i}a_{i}\,dx_{i}
with

a_{i}=\sum_{j}A_{ij}x_{j}
linear. The projectivity condition

i_{R}\omega=\sum_{i}x_{i}a_{i}=0
gives
A_{ii}=0
for all
i
, and

A_{ij}+A_{ji}=0
for
i\neq j
. In characteristic
2
the latter
reads
A_{ij}=A_{ji}
, so

d\omega=\sum_{i<j}(A_{ij}+A_{ji})\,dx_{i}\wedge dx_{j}=0\,.

Thus
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})\subseteq\mathbb{P}H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}},\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}}(2))=\textsf{Cl}_{0}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})\subseteq\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
,
and all three coincide.

Moreover, every
\omega\in\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
is exact: in
Case
(a)
, the argument above gives
\omega=df
; in
Case
(b)
,
\omega
is a scalar multiple of

i_{R}(dx_{0}\wedge dx_{1})=x_{0}\,dx_{1}-x_{1}\,dx_{0}
, which in characteristic
2

equals
x_{0}\,dx_{1}+x_{1}\,dx_{0}=d(x_{0}x_{1})
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

Our proof does not explicitly address the scheme structure of
\textsf{Fol}^{q}_{0}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
. However, the proof of Proposition
3.2
shows that in the exceptional case where
q=1
and the characteristic of
{\mathsf{k}}
is
2
, the scheme
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
is generically reduced. In all other cases, we believe that
\textsf{Fol}^{q}_{0}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
is reduced everywhere. An attentive reader will notice that when
q=1
and the characteristic is different from
2
, this follows from the proof of Proposition
4.6
below. Moreover, it is very likely that the results and arguments of
[
5
]
can be adapted to prove the reducedness of
\textsf{Fol}^{q}_{0}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
when
q>1
.

4.
Foliations of degree one

In this section, we focus on foliations of degree one on projective varieties over fields of arbitrary characteristic. Unlike Section
3
, where we examined foliations of arbitrary codimension, here we restrict our attention to codimension one foliations.

After reviewing key properties of codimension one foliations in positive characteristic, following
[
13
]
, we introduce two families of irreducible components of the space of foliations: linear pull-back components and logarithmic components with only two distinct residues. These families contain special members of degree one.

We conclude the section with a detailed description of codimension one foliations of degree one in positive characteristic, building on the previous results.

4.1.
The
p
-curvature and its degeneracy divisor for codimension one foliations

The main reference for the definitions and results discussed in this subsection is
[
13
]
.
Let
X
be a smooth projective variety defined
over an algebraically closed field
{\mathsf{k}}
of characteristic
p>0
. Denote by
\Frob:X\to X
the absolute Frobenius morphism. If
\mathcal{F}
is a foliation on
X
, its
p
-curvature is the morphism of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules given by raising vector fields to theirs
p
-th powers:

\displaystyle{\psi_{\mathcal{F}}}:\Frob^{*}T_{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow N_{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle\sum f_{i}\otimes v_{i}

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum f_{i}v_{i}^{p}\mod T_{\mathcal{F}}\,.

A foliation
\mathcal{F}
is called
p
-closed if
{\psi_{\mathcal{F}}}
is identically zero. When
{\psi_{\mathcal{F}}}
is generically surjective, meaning that the image of
{\psi_{\mathcal{F}}}
has rank equal to the codimension of
\mathcal{F}
, we say that
\mathcal{F}
is
p
-dense.

When a foliation
\mathcal{F}
has codimension one, then it is either
p
-closed or
p
-dense. In the
p
-dense case, there exist a uniquely determined effective divisor
{\Delta_{\mathcal{F}}}
and a uniquely determined codimension two subfoliation
{\mathscr{V}({\mathcal{F}})}\subset\mathcal{F}
such that:

(1)

Away from a subset of codimension at least two, the image of
{\psi_{\mathcal{F}}}
coincides with
N_{\mathcal{F}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-{\Delta_{\mathcal{F}}})
; and

(2)

The kernel of the
{\psi_{\mathcal{F}}}
is equal to
\Frob^{*}T_{{\mathscr{V}({\mathcal{F}})}}
.

When
X=\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
, the degrees of
\mathcal{F}
,
{\mathscr{V}({\mathcal{F}})}
, and
{\Delta_{\mathcal{F}}}
satisfy the relation

(4.1)

\deg({\Delta_{\mathcal{F}}})=p(\deg(\mathcal{F})-\deg({\mathscr{V}({\mathcal{F}})})-1)+\deg(\mathcal{F})+2

which is a specialization of
[
13
, Proposition 4.12]
.

Example 4.1
.

Every foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
of degree zero is
p
-closed. Indeed, the codimension one case follows from Equation (
4.1
) and in higher codimension this follows from Theorem
3.5
.

4.2.
Linear pull-backs

Let
\textsf{Lin}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})\subset\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
denote the reduced subscheme whose closed points correspond to foliations on
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
that arise as pullbacks of degree
d
foliations on
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
under a linear projection
\pi:\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
. It is well-known that for every
d\geq 0
and
n\geq 3
,
\textsf{Lin}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n})
is an irreducible component of
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n})
. Observe that
\textsf{Lin}_{0}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
corresponds to foliations on
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
that are defined by a pencil of hyperplanes, hence have singular set of codimension two. As a corollary of Theorem
3.5
we obtain that
\textsf{Lin}_{0}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
is an irreducible component of
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
if, and only if, the characteristic of
{\mathsf{k}}
is different from two. Indeed, when the characteristic of
{\mathsf{k}}
is two, a general element of
\textsf{Cl}_{0}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
has empty singular set when
n
is odd or a unique singularity when
n
is even. In both cases,
\textsf{Lin}_{0}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
cannot be equal to

\textsf{Cl}_{0}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
d>0
. If
{\mathsf{k}}
has characteristic
p>0
, then
\textsf{Lin}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
is an irreducible component of
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
if and only if
d<p
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a general foliation of
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
of degree
d\geq 1
. Consider the linear projection

\pi:\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
given by

\pi(x_{0}:\ldots:x_{n})=(x_{0}:x_{1}:x_{2}),

and let
\mathcal{F}=\pi^{*}\mathcal{G}
.

According to
[
14
]
, see also
[
13
, Theorem 5.10]
, the degeneracy divisor
\Delta_{\mathcal{G}}
is a reduced divisor. Moreover, Equation (
4.1
) implies it has degree
d+2+p(d-1)
. Due to the explicit form of
\pi
,
\Delta_{\mathcal{F}}=\pi^{*}\Delta_{\mathcal{G}}
and
\Delta_{\mathcal{F}}
is also reduced and of degree
d+2+p(d-1)
.

If
\Sigma\subset\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
is an irreducible component containing
\mathcal{F}
, then by
[
13
, Section 6]
,
for a general foliation
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
in
\Sigma
the kernel of the
p
-curvature of
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is a codimension two foliation
{\mathscr{V}({\mathcal{F}^{\prime}})}

of degree zero. Theorem
3.5
implies that in suitable homogeneous coordinates,
{\mathscr{V}({\mathcal{F}})}^{\prime}
is defined by the linear projection
(x_{0}:\ldots:x_{n})\mapsto(x_{0}:x_{1}:x_{2})
.
Consequently,
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is defined by a homogeneous polynomial
1
-form
\omega^{\prime}
that can be written as

\omega^{\prime}=adx_{0}+bdx_{1}+cdx_{2}

where
a,b,c
are homogeneous polynomials in
{\mathsf{k}}[x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}]
of degree
d+1
. The integrability condition
\omega^{\prime}\wedge d\omega^{\prime}=0
implies that

(adb-bda)\wedge dx_{0}\wedge dx_{1}\wedge dx_{2}=(adc-cda)\wedge dx_{0}\wedge dx_{1}\wedge dx_{2}=(cdb-bdc)\wedge dx_{0}\wedge dx_{1}\wedge dx_{2}=0.

Therefore we deduce from the integrability condition that

\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\left(\frac{a}{c}\right)=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\left(\frac{b}{c}\right)=0,

for every
i\in\{3,\ldots,n\}
.
Consequently, we obtain the existence of a polynomial
f\in{\mathsf{k}}[x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}]
such that
a,b,c\in f\cdot{\mathsf{k}}[x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}^{p},\ldots,x_{n}^{p}]
. Since
\omega^{\prime}
has singularities of codimension at least two,
f\in{\mathsf{k}}
.

Thus, for every multi-index
I=(i_{3},\ldots,i_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n-2}
such that
d+2>p\cdot|I|
, there exists a projective
1
-form
\beta_{I}
of degree
d+2-p\cdot|I|
on
\mathbb{A}^{3}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
with variables
x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}

such that

\omega^{\prime}=\sum_{I\in\mathbb{N}^{n-2}}(x_{3}^{i_{3}}\cdots x_{n}^{i_{n}})^{p}\beta_{I}.

Since any projective
1
-form has degree at least two, we deduce that

\omega^{\prime}
is a linear pull-back when
d+2-p<2
. If instead
d+2-p\geq 2
, then the above expression for
\omega^{\prime}
provides a method
to construct deformations of
\omega
that are not linear pull-backs, since
\omega^{\prime}
as above satisfies Frobenius integrability condition
\omega^{\prime}\wedge d\omega^{\prime}=0
.
∎

For later use, we isolate part of the argument used in the proof of Proposition
4.2
in the form of a lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a codimension one foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
where
{\mathsf{k}}
is an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
. If
\mathcal{F}
contains a codimension
q
foliation of degree zero,
2\leq q\leq n-1
, and
\deg(\mathcal{F})<p
, then there exists a linear projection
\pi:\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{q}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
and a foliation
\mathcal{G}
on
\mathbb{P}^{q}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
such that
\mathcal{F}=\pi^{*}\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

The argument follows the proof of Proposition
4.2

with
\mathbb{P}^{q}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
in place of
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
.
Since, by assumption,
\mathcal{F}
contains a degree zero
foliation of codimension
q
, Theorem
3.5
provides coordinates in which

\omega^{\prime}=\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}a_{i}\,dx_{i}
with

a_{i}\in{\mathsf{k}}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
.
The integrability condition forces

a_{i}\in{\mathsf{k}}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{q-1},\,x_{q}^{p},\ldots,x_{n}^{p}]
.
Since
\deg(\mathcal{F})<p
, any term involving
x_{j}^{p}
(for
j\geq q
) would
contribute degree at least
p>d+1
to
a_{i}
, which is impossible.
Hence
a_{i}\in{\mathsf{k}}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{q-1}]
and
\omega^{\prime}
is the
pullback of a form on
\mathbb{P}^{q}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
.
∎

As a consequence of Proposition
4.2
we get the following proposition.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
{\mathsf{k}}
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>2
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a codimension one foliation of degree one in
\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n}
and suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is not
p
-closed. Then,
\mathcal{F}
is a linear pull-back of a degree one foliation on
\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{2}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{F}
is not
p
-closed, the degeneracy divisor of its
p
-curvature,
{\Delta_{\mathcal{F}}}
, has degree
-p\deg({\mathscr{V}({\mathcal{F}})})+3
according to Equation (
4.1
).

If
\deg({\mathscr{V}({\mathcal{F}})})=0
then, arguing as in the proof of Proposition
4.2
, we obtain that
\mathcal{F}
is a linear pull-back of a degree one foliation on
\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{2}
.

If
\deg({\mathscr{V}({\mathcal{F}})})\geq 1
then, since
{\Delta_{\mathcal{F}}}
is an effective divisor, it follows that
p=3
and that
{\Delta_{\mathcal{F}}}
is the trivial divisor. By
[
13
, Propositions 4.7 and 4.14]
, any projective
1
-form
\omega\in H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}},\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}}(3))
defining
\mathcal{F}
is closed. Lemma
3.1
implies that
\omega=df

for some polynomial
f\in{\mathsf{k}}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
. Therefore,
\mathcal{F}
is
p
-closed, contradicting our assumptions. Thus, we conclude that
\mathcal{F}
is necessarily a linear pullback of a degree one foliation on
\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{2}
.
∎

4.3.
Numerically linear pullbacks

Let
\textsf{NLin}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})\subset\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
denote the reduced subscheme whose closed points correspond to codimension one foliations
\mathcal{F}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
of degree
d
such that
T_{\mathcal{F}}
contains
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}}(+1)^{\oplus n-2}
.

Over the complex numbers, we have
\textsf{NLin}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n})=\textsf{Lin}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n})
. However, in positive characteristic, the proof of Proposition
4.2

highlights the distinction between these two spaces, leading to the more precise statement below.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
d>0
. If
{\mathsf{k}}
has characteristic
p>0
, then
\textsf{NLin}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
is an irreducible component of
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
.
Moreover,
\textsf{NLin}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})=\textsf{Lin}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
if and only if
d<p
.

We point out that one can deduce that
\textsf{NLin}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
is an irreducible component of
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
using
[
15
, Theorem B and Corollary 4.14]
, as an alternative to the argument involving the
p
-th powers of vector fields. We opt for the latter method because it leverages specific features of positive characteristic, in contrast to the former method.

4.4.
Logarithmic components I

Let
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{r})
be an ordered partition of
d+2
, i.e.
1\leq d_{1}\leq d_{2}\leq\ldots\leq d_{r}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}=d+2
.
Set
\Lambda
as the
{\mathsf{k}}
-vector space

\Lambda=\left\{(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r})\in{\mathsf{k}}^{r}\,|\,\sum_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}\lambda_{i}=0\right\}\,.

Observe that
\Lambda={\mathsf{k}}^{r}
if all integers
d_{i}
are divisible by
p
, otherwise
\dim\Lambda=r-1
.

Consider the rational map

\displaystyle\Phi_{n,d_{1},\ldots,d_{r}}:\mathbb{P}(\Lambda)\times\left(\prod_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{P}H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}}(d_{i}))\right)

\displaystyle\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}},\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}}(d+2))

\displaystyle([\lambda_{1}:\ldots:\lambda_{r}],[f_{1}],\ldots,[f_{r}])

\displaystyle\mapsto\left(\prod_{i=1}^{r}f_{i}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}\frac{df_{i}}{f_{i}}\right)\,.

Define
\textsf{Log}_{(d_{1},\ldots,d_{r})}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
as the Zariski closure of the image of
\Phi_{n,d_{1},\ldots,d_{r}}
in the open subset
of
\mathbb{P}H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}},\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}}(d+2))
formed by projective
1
-forms with singular set of
codimension at least
2
. Note that
\textsf{Log}_{(d_{1},\ldots,d_{r})}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
is empty if all but one of the
d_{i}
’s are divisible by
p
since, in this case, the image of
\Phi_{n,d_{1},\ldots,d_{r}}
will be contained in the closed subset formed by
1
-forms with codimension one zeros.
In every other case,
\textsf{Log}_{(d_{1},\ldots,d_{r})}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
is non empty.

Let us first study the case
r=2
. We adapt the proof of
[
5
, Theorem 1]
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
d_{1},d_{2}
be two positive integers and let
{\mathsf{k}}
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p\geq 0
.
If
n\geq 3
and
p
does not divide any of the integers
d_{1},d_{2},
and
d_{1}+d_{2}
then
\textsf{Log}_{(d_{1},d_{2})}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
is an irreducible component
of
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{d_{1}+d_{2}-2}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
. Moreover,
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{d_{1}+d_{2}-2}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
is generically reduced, i.e., reduced at the general point of
\textsf{Log}_{(d_{1},d_{2})}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
.

Proof.

Let
f_{1},f_{2}\in{\mathsf{k}}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
be homogeneous polynomials of degrees
d_{1},d_{2}
, defining smooth hypersurfaces
H_{1}
and
H_{2}

that intersect transversely.
Let
\mathcal{F}\in\textsf{Log}_{(d_{1},d_{2})}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
be the foliation of degree
d=d_{1}+d_{2}-2
determined by
\omega=i_{R}df_{1}\wedge df_{2}
.
The proof of the proposition will follow the same lines of the proof of Proposition
3.2
. More specifically,
it will consist in determining the tangent space of
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
at
\mathcal{F}
. For that, it suffices to solve the equation

d\omega\wedge d\eta=0

for
\eta
projective
1
-form of degree
d+2=d_{1}+d_{2}
, since
p
does not divide
d+2
by assumption and because of that we can go back and forth from this equation to
\omega\wedge d\alpha+\alpha\wedge d\omega
using Euler’s relation, see Section
3.1
.

First observe that the projectivization
Z
of the zero set of
df_{1}\wedge df_{2}
does not intersect the hypersurfaces
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
. Since
they are ample divisors, it follows that
\dim Z=0
. As we are assuming
n\geq 3
, we have that the codimension of the zero set of
df_{1}\wedge df_{2}
is at least three. Therefore, we are in place to apply de Rham-Saito’s lemma and write

d\eta=\alpha\wedge df_{1}+\beta\wedge df_{2}

for homogeneous
1
-forms
\alpha
of degree
d+2-d_{1}=d_{2}
and
\beta
of degree
d+2-d_{2}=d_{1}
. Differentiating this expression
and taking the wedge product with
df_{1}
, we deduce that
d\beta\wedge df_{1}\wedge df_{2}=0
. Similarly,
d\alpha\wedge df_{1}\wedge df_{2}=0
.

We apply De Rham-Saito’s lemma again to write

d\beta=\beta^{\prime}\wedge df_{1}+\beta^{\prime\prime}\wedge df_{2}

with
\beta^{\prime}
of degree
d_{1}-d_{1}=0
and
\beta^{\prime\prime}
of degree
d_{1}-d_{2}\leq 0
. It follows that
d\beta=0
. Therefore, there exist
polynomials
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},b\in{\mathsf{k}}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
such that

\beta=db+\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})^{p}x_{i}^{p-1}dx_{i}\,.

Since the degree of
\beta
is not divisible by
p
, the polynomials
a_{i}
are equal to zero and we deduce that
\beta
is exact,
i.e.,
\beta=db
.

If
d_{1}=d_{2}
, then the argument above shows that
\alpha
is also exact. We deduce that

d\eta=da\wedge df_{1}+db\wedge df_{2}

for homogeneous polynomials
a
of degree
d_{2}
and
b
of degree
d_{1}
. This shows that the Zariski tangent space of
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
coincides with the image of the differential
of
\Phi_{n,d_{1},d_{2}}
at the point corresponding to
\mathcal{F}
. If instead
d_{1}<d_{2}
then a variant of the argument above
shows that
\alpha=da+cdf_{1}
for some polynomials
a,c\in{\mathsf{k}}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
, cf.
[
5
, Proof of Proposition 2.1]
.
Anyway, the conclusion is the same:
d\eta=da\wedge df_{1}+db\wedge df_{2}
as above.

Since
\textsf{Log}_{(d_{1},d_{2})}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
is irreducible and its Zariski tangent space at
\mathcal{F}
coincides with that of
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
, we conclude as in the proof
of Proposition
3.2
that
\textsf{Log}_{(d_{1},d_{2})}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
is an irreducible
component of
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
and that
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
is generically
reduced at the general point of
\textsf{Log}_{(d_{1},d_{2})}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

Let
{\mathsf{k}}
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
.
If
d_{1},d_{2}
are not divisible by
p
and
d_{1}+d_{2}=0\mod p
then
\textsf{Log}_{(d_{1},d_{2})}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
is not an irreducible component of

\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
since it is contained in the irreducible component
\textsf{Cl}_{d_{1}+d_{2}-2}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
.

We will show below, in Theorem
6.2
, that the subvarieties
\textsf{Log}_{(d_{1},d_{2})}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
when both
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
are divisible by the characteristic of
{\mathsf{k}}
are irreducible components of
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{d_{1}+d_{2}-2}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
.

4.5.
Foliations of degree one

With the necessary tools in place, we can now state and prove the classification of codimension one foliations of degree one in arbitrary characteristic.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
{\mathsf{k}}
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p\geq 0
.

(1)

If
p=2
then
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{1}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
has exactly one irreducible component:
\textsf{Lin}_{1}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
.

(2)

If
p=3
then
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{1}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
has exactly two irreducible components:
\textsf{Lin}_{1}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
and
\textsf{Cl}_{1}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
.

(3)

If
p\notin\{2,3\}
then
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{1}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
has exactly two irreducible components:
\textsf{Lin}_{1}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
and
\textsf{Log}_{(1,2)}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
.

Proof.

As a preliminary remark, note that
\textsf{Lin}_{1}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
is an irreducible component of
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{1}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
for every
p\geq 0
. For
p>0
this follows from Proposition
4.2
. For
p=0
, it follows by semi-continuity: since
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{1}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
and
\textsf{Lin}_{1}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
are defined over
\mathbb{Z}
, upper semi-continuity of the Zariski tangent space dimension implies that its value at a general point of
\textsf{Lin}_{1}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
in characteristic zero is at most its value in characteristic
p\gg 0
, which equals
\dim\textsf{Lin}_{1}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})=\dim\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{1}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
by Proposition
4.2
. When
p=3
, Proposition
3.2
provides an additional irreducible component
\textsf{Cl}_{1}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
. For
p>3
, Proposition
4.6
also provides an extra irreducible component
\textsf{Log}_{(1,2)}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
.

Let
\omega
be a projective
1
-form defining a degree one foliation
\mathcal{F}
and consider the
2
-form
d\omega
. Since
d\omega
is closed, it is integrable. Therefore, we are in position to apply Theorem
3.3
to obtain homogeneous coordinates such that either:
(a)

d\omega=df\wedge dx_{0}
for some quadratic function
f\in{\mathsf{k}}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
, or
(b)

d\omega=\sum_{i,j\in\{0,1,2\}}A_{ij}dx_{i}\wedge dx_{j}
for some linear polynomials
A_{ij}\in{\mathsf{k}}[x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}]
.

If
p\neq 3
and
d\omega
is as in Case
(b)
, then multiplying its contraction with the radial vector field by
1/3
recovers
\omega
, showing that it depends only on the variables
x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}
. Thus, it defines a foliation in
\textsf{Lin}_{1}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
.

If
p\notin\{2,3\}
and
d\omega
is as in Case
(a)
, then the explicit form of
d\omega
shows that
\omega
defines a foliation in
\textsf{Log}_{(1,2)}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
. This completes the proof of Item
3
.

Now consider the case
p=2
. If
d\omega
is as in Case
(a)
, then

\omega=3\omega=i_{R}(d\omega)=2fdx_{0}+x_{0}df=x_{0}df\,.

This implies that
\omega
is either identically zero or that
\omega
has a zero locus of codimension one. In either case,
\omega
does not define a codimension one foliation of degree one. Thus, in characteristic two, the only irreducible component of
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{1}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
is
\textsf{Lin}_{1}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
.

For
p=3
, we already know from Propositions
4.2
and
3.2
that
\textsf{Lin}_{1}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
and
\textsf{Cl}_{1}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
are irreducible components of
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{1}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
. If
d\omega
is as in Case
(a)
then:

0=3\omega=i_{R}d\omega=2fdx_{0}-x_{0}df=-(fdx_{0}+x_{0}df)=-d(fx_{0}).

This implies that
f
is a nonzero multiple of
x_{0}^{2}
, so
d\omega=0
, implying that
\omega
defines a foliation in
\textsf{Cl}_{1}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
in this case.

Finally, we analyze the possibility that
d\omega
is as in Case
(b)
. Since
p=3
, we have
i_{R}d\omega=3\omega=0
. As the coefficients of
d\omega
have degree one, we may write

d\omega=\alpha\cdot(i_{R}dx_{0}\wedge dx_{1}\wedge dx_{2})

for some
\alpha\in{\mathsf{k}}
.
If
\alpha=0
, then
\omega
defines a foliation in
\textsf{Cl}_{1}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
. Otherwise, if
\alpha\neq 0
, consider the vector field

v=\frac{\alpha}{2}x_{0}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{0}}
. Then, a straightforward computation shows that

d\omega=d(i_{v}i_{R}dx_{0}\wedge dx_{1}\wedge dx_{2})\,.

Therefore, we can write:

\omega=i_{v}i_{R}dx_{0}\wedge dx_{1}\wedge dx_{2}+\theta

where
\theta
is a closed polynomial
1
-form with coefficients of degree two annihilated by the radial vector field
R
.
By Lemma
3.1
, we conclude that
\theta=df
.
The integrability condition
\omega\wedge d\omega=0
implies:

0=df\wedge d\omega=\alpha\cdot df\wedge(i_{R}dx_{0}\wedge dx_{1}\wedge dx_{2})=\alpha\cdot i_{R}(df\wedge dx_{0}\wedge dx_{1}\wedge dx_{2})\,.

Thus
f\in{\mathsf{k}}[x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}^{3},\ldots,x_{n}^{3}]
, and consequently

\theta=df\in\bigoplus_{i=0}^{2}{\mathsf{k}}[x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}]\cdot dx_{i}\,.

It follows that
\omega
defines a foliation in
\textsf{Lin}_{1}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
when
p=3
and
d\omega\neq 0
. This completes the proof.
∎

5.
Closed logarithmic
1
-forms and their residues

5.1.
Logarithmic
1
-forms

Let
X
be a smooth algebraic variety defined over an algebraically closed field
{\mathsf{k}}
. Let
D=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}H_{i}
be a reduced and effective divisor on
X
, supported on irreducible hypersurfaces
H_{1},\ldots,H_{\ell}
. Following
[
19
]
, we denote by
\Omega^{1}_{X}(\log D)
the sheaf of rational
1
-forms
\omega
on
X
such that both
\omega
and
d\omega
have simple poles contained in the support of
D
. More precisely, for a point
x\in X
, if
h\in\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
is a
local equation for
D
, then a germ of rational
1
-form
\omega
belongs to
\Omega^{1}_{X}(\log D)_{x}
if and only if both
h\omega
and
hd\omega

are germs of regular differential forms.

If
\omega\in\Omega^{1}_{X}(\log D)_{x}
is a germ of logarithmic
1
-form with poles along
D
, then there exist
g\in\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
,
\eta\in\Omega^{1}_{X,x}
, and
f\in\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
such that
{\left.\kern-1.2ptg\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{H_{i}}}
is not identically zero for any
i
, and

g\omega=f\frac{dh}{h}+\eta\,.

Indeed, if
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\in\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
form a local system of parameters such that
z_{1}
is not identically zero on any
H_{j}
, then we can take
g=\frac{\partial h}{\partial z_{1}}
, see
[
19
, Section 1]
.

5.2.
Residues

Let
n_{i}:H_{i}^{n}\to H_{i}
be the normalization of
H_{i}
. The (local) residue of
\omega
along
H_{i}
is defined as
the rational function
n_{i}^{*}(f/g)
. This local residue is well-defined, independent of the choices above, and
induces a morphism

\Res:\Omega^{1}_{X}(\log D)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{M}_{H_{i}^{n}},

where
\mathcal{M}_{H_{i}^{n}}
is the sheaf of rational functions on
H_{i}^{n}
, the normalization of
H_{i}
. The kernel of
\Res
is precisely
\Omega^{1}_{X}
.

We follow the terminology of
[
20
,
Tag 0CBN
]
: a normal crossing divisor is a divisor that, locally in the étale topology, is supported on a union of hypersurfaces defined by a subset of a local system of parameters. A strict normal crossing divisor, or a simple normal crossing divisor, is one that locally, in the Zariski topology, is supported on a union of hypersurfaces defined by a subset of a local system of parameters.

Proposition 5.1
.

Under the assumptions above, if there exists a closed subset
S\subset X
such that
\codim S\geq 3
and
{\left.\kern-1.2ptD\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{X-S}}
is a normal crossing divisor on
X-S
, then the image of
\Res
is contained in

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{O}_{H_{i}^{n}}\,,

meaning that the residues of
1
-forms in
\Omega^{1}_{X}(\log D)
are regular functions on the normalization of
D
.

Proof.

If
{\left.\kern-1.2ptD\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{X-S}}
is a simple normal crossing divisor, then for every
x\in X-S
there exists a local system of parameters

z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}
such that
\Omega^{1}_{X}(\log D)_{x}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{O}_{X,x}\frac{dz_{i}}{z_{i}}+\Omega^{1}_{X,x}
, as can be verified from
[
19
, Theorem 2.9]
and
[
9
, Section 10]
. Consequently, the residues of
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\omega\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{X-S}}
are regular functions on the normalization of
{\left.\kern-1.2ptD\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{X-S}}
by
[
19
, Theorem 2.9]
. Since
S\cap H_{i}
has codimension at least two in
H_{i}
, the same holds for
n_{i}^{-1}(S)
in
H_{i}^{n}
, as the normalization is a finite morphism. Therefore, the residues of
\omega
are regular functions on
D^{n}=\coprod H_{i}^{n}
, the normalization of
D
.
If
{\left.\kern-1.2ptD\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{X-S}}
is normal crossing, but not simple normal crossing, then it suffices to observe that the residue morphism commutes
with étale morphisms. The regularity of the residues then follows from simple normal crossing case.
∎

A divisor
D
satisfying the assumptions of Proposition
5.1
is called a normal crossing divisor in codimension two.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
D=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}H_{i}
be a reduced and effective divisor on
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
for some algebraically closed field
{\mathsf{k}}
. If
D
is a normal crossing divisor in codimension two and
\omega\in H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}},\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}}(\log D))
is a logarithmic
1
-form with poles on
D
, then there exist constants
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell}\in{\mathsf{k}}
such that, in homogeneous coordinates,

\omega=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\lambda_{i}\frac{dh_{i}}{h_{i}}\,,

where
h_{i}
are irreducible and reduced homogeneous polynomials defining
H_{i}
.

Proof.

The residues of
\omega
are regular functions on proper algebraic varieties, hence they are constants
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell}
. Applying the residue theorem to the restriction of
\omega
to a general line implies that

\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\lambda_{i}\deg(H_{i})=0.

Consequently, the homogeneous rational form
\eta=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\lambda_{i}\frac{dh_{i}}{h_{i}}
is annihilated by the radial vector field, hence it defines a section of
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}},\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}}(\log D))
. The difference
\omega-\eta
is a logarithmic
1
-form with vanishing residues and thus belongs to
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}},\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\mathsf{k}}})
. Since
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
has no global
1
-forms, we conclude that
\omega=\eta
as claimed.
∎

5.3.
A characterization of normal crossing in codimension two

For later use, we record a characterization of divisors that are normal crossing in codimension two.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
X
be an affine smooth variety defined over an algebraically closed field
{\mathsf{k}}
.
Let
h\in\mathcal{O}_{X}
be a regular function, and let
D
be the divisor defined by
h
. Assume that
D
is reduced.
The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

The divisor
D
is normal crossing in codimension two.

(2)

The subvariety defined by the ideal
I(D)
, which is
generated by
h
,
v(h)
for every
v\in H^{0}(X,T_{X})
, and

\det\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}v_{1}^{2}(h)&v_{2}(v_{1}(h))\\
v_{1}(v_{2}(h))&v_{2}^{2}(h)\\
\end{array}\right)\quad\text{ for every }v_{1},v_{2}\in H^{0}(X,T_{X})

has codimension at least three.

Proof.

We prove the equivalence by considering both directions.

First, suppose that
D
is normal crossing in codimension two. Then, there exists a closed subset
S\subset X
of codimension at least three such that
{\left.\kern-1.2ptD\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{X-S}}
is a normal crossing divisor. We may assume that
S
also contains all loci where three or more irreducible components of
D
meet, since these have codimension at least three in
X
. Consequently, for every
x\in D-S
, the germ of
D
at
x
is either smooth or, in a suitable étale neighborhood, equal to the sum of exactly two smooth irreducible divisors, say
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
, intersecting transversely.
In the first case, if we take
v\in T_{X,x}
with value at
x
not contained in the kernel of
dh
then
v(h)
is a unity. In the second case we take
v_{1},v_{2}\in T_{X,x}
such that

(1)

the value of
v_{1}
at
x
is transverse to
T_{H_{1}}(x):=T_{H_{1}}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}T_{H_{1}}
and contained in
T_{H_{2}}(x):=T_{H_{2}}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}T_{H_{2}}
; and

(2)

the value of
v_{2}
at
x
is transverse to
T_{H_{2}}(x)
and contained in
T_{H_{1}}(x)
.

A simple computation shows that the determinant

\det\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}v_{1}^{2}(h)&v_{2}(v_{1}(h))\\
v_{1}(v_{2}(h))&v_{2}^{2}(h)\\
\end{array}\right)

is a unit in
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
. Consequently,
I(D)_{x}
contains a unit for every
x\in X-S
. Thus, the variety defined by
I(D)
has codimension at least
3
as wanted.

To establish the converse direction, we will prove the contrapositive. Suppose that
D
is reduced and not normal crossing in codimension two. We will show that
I(D)
defines a subvariety of codimension at most two in
X
.

Let
S
be an irreducible component of the singular locus of
D
that has codimension one in
D
. Since we assume that
D
is not normal crossing in codimension two, such a component exists. Consider the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{X,S}
of regular functions on
X
along
S
. As
X
is regular and
S
has codimension two in
X
, it follows that
\mathcal{O}_{X,S}
is a regular local ring of dimension two. Let
x,y\in\mathcal{O}_{X,S}
be minimal generators of the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{X,S}
of
\mathcal{O}_{X,S}
. Since
S
is contained in the singular locus of
D
,
h
is an element of
\mathfrak{m}_{X,S}^{2}
. Writing
h=ax^{2}+bxy+cy^{2}
for some functions
a,b,c\in\mathcal{O}_{X,S}
, we claim that the quadratic form
ax^{2}+bxy+cy^{2}
degenerates along
S
, i.e.,
b^{2}-4ac=0
along
S
. Indeed,
D
is normal crossing at the generic point
s
of
S
if and only if the initial form of
h
in
\mathfrak{m}_{X,s}^{2}/\mathfrak{m}_{X,s}^{3}
is a product of two nonproportional linear forms over the residue field, which holds if and only if
b^{2}-4ac\neq 0
at
s
. Since
D
fails to be normal crossing at
s
by assumption, we conclude that
b^{2}-4ac=0
along
S
, meaning
S
is contained in the hypersurface
\{b^{2}-4ac=0\}
. On the other hand, for any sections
v_{1},v_{2}\in T_{X}
, the determinant

\det\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}v_{1}^{2}(h)&v_{2}(v_{1}(h))\\
v_{1}(v_{2}(h))&v_{2}^{2}(h)\\
\end{array}\right)

has the form
s+(b^{2}-4ac)t
, where
s\in\mathfrak{m}_{X,S}
and
t\in\mathcal{O}_{X,S}
. It follows that
I(D)\mathcal{O}_{X,S}
is contained in the ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{X,S}
, and thus
S
is contained in the subvariety defined by
I(D)
. Hence, this subvariety has codimension at most two as desired.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety and let
T
be an algebraic variety, both defined over an algebraically closed field
{\mathsf{k}}
.
If
\Delta
is a divisor on
\mathscr{X}=X\times T
then the set

\{t\in T\,|\,\Delta_{t}={\left.\kern-1.2pt\Delta\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{X\times\{t\}}}\text{ is a reduced normal crossing divisor in codimension two }\}

is an open subset of
T
.

Proof.

Since the normal crossing in codimension two condition and the codimension of the subvariety defined by
I(D_{t})
are both local on
X
, we may cover
X
by finitely many affine open subvarieties and apply Lemma
5.3
to each. For each
t\in T
, let
h_{t}
be a local equation of
\Delta_{t}
and
I(D_{t})
the ideal generated by
h_{t}
,
v(h_{t})
and

\det\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}v_{1}^{2}(h_{t})&v_{2}(v_{1}(h_{t}))\\
v_{1}(v_{2}(h_{t}))&v_{2}^{2}(h_{t})\\
\end{array}\right)\quad\text{ for every }v_{1},v_{2}\in T_{X}

By Lemma
5.3
,
D_{t}
is normal crossing in codimension two if and only if the subvariety
V_{t}
of
X
defined by
I(D_{t})
has codimension at least three. The result follows from the upper semi-continuity of the function
t\in T\mapsto\dim V_{t}
, see for instance
[
10
, Corollary 13.1.5]
.
∎

6.
Logarithmic Components II

We return to the study of the logarithmic components of the space of foliations on projective spaces.
Unlike the proof of Proposition
4.6
, where we adapted arguments from characteristic zero to positive characteristic, here we leverage specific properties of foliations over fields of positive characteristic. In particular, we explore fundamental properties of the Cartier transform of foliations, which we recall below.

6.1.
The Cartier transform of a logarithmic foliation

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety defined over an algebraically closed field
{\mathsf{k}}
of characteristic
p>0
.
Let
\mathcal{F}
be a
p
-dense codimension one foliation defined on
X
. By
[
13
, Proposition 4.4]
,
\mathcal{F}
is defined by a closed rational
1
-form
\omega
.
The Cartier transform
{\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{F})}
of
\mathcal{F}
, as introduced in
[
13
, Section 4.5, Definition 4.17]
, is the distribution given by the kernel of
1
-form obtained by applying the Cartier operator on
\omega
.

In the particular case that
\omega
is of the form

\omega=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}\frac{df_{i}}{f_{i}}\,,

the result of applying the Cartier operator
\mathbf{C}
to it is rather easy to compute:

(6.1)

\mathbf{C}(\omega)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}^{1/p}\frac{df_{i}}{f_{i}}\,.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
a codimension one foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
defined by a closed logarithmic
1
-form

\omega=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}\frac{df_{i}}{f_{i}}\,,

where
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}\in{\mathsf{k}}
are constants.
Assume that the following conditions hold:

(1)

for any
i
there exists a
j
such that the ratio
\lambda_{i}/\lambda_{j}
does not belong to
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

(2)

The polar divisor of
\omega
is a simple normal crossing divisor.

Then
\deg({\mathscr{V}({\mathcal{F}})})=\deg(\mathcal{F})-1
and
\deg({\Delta_{\mathcal{F}}})=\deg(\mathcal{F})+2
.

Proof.

By
[
13
, Proposition 4.19]
, the tangent sheaf of
{\mathscr{V}({\mathcal{F}})}
coincides with the saturation of
T_{\mathcal{F}}\cap T_{{\mathscr{C}(\mathcal{F})}}
inside
T_{X}
.
Under our assumptions, the distribution
{\mathscr{V}({\mathcal{F}})}
is defined by the rational
2
-form

\theta=\omega\wedge\mathbf{C}(\omega)=\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq r}\left(\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}^{1/p}-\lambda_{j}\lambda_{i}^{1/p}\right)\frac{df_{i}}{f_{i}}\wedge\frac{df_{j}}{f_{j}}\,.

Since the determinant of the normal bundle of
{\mathscr{V}({\mathcal{F}})}
satisfies

\det(N_{{\mathscr{V}({\mathcal{F}})}})=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}}\left((\theta)_{\infty}-(\theta)_{0}\right)=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}}\left(\deg({\mathscr{V}({\mathcal{F}})})+3\right)

it suffices to verify that
(\theta)_{\infty}=(\omega)_{\infty}
and that
\theta
does not have codimension one zeros.

The equality
(\theta)_{\infty}=(\omega)_{\infty}
follows from the assumption that for any
i
, there exists a
j
such that
\lambda_{i}/\lambda_{j}\notin\mathbb{F}_{p}
which ensures that

\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}^{1/p}-\lambda_{j}\lambda_{i}^{1/p}\neq 0
.

To show that
\theta
has no codimension one zeros, assume for contradiction that its zero locus contains a hypersurface
Z
. The hypersurface

must intersect
(\theta)_{\infty}=(\omega)_{\infty}
as both are ample divisors. However, because
(\theta)_{\infty}
is a simple normal crossing divisor, a local computation shows that

remains nonzero in a neighborhood of its polar divisor, contradicting the existence of

. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
A

For the reader’s convenience, we recall the statement of Theorem
A
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
r\geq 2
and
1\leq d_{1}\leq\ldots\leq d_{r}
be positive integers and let

{\mathsf{k}}
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
. Assume that one
of the following conditions holds:

(1)

r=2
and none of the integers
d_{1}
,
d_{2}
, and
d_{1}+d_{2}
is divisible
by
p
; or

(2)

r=2
and both
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
are divisible by
p
; or

(3)

r>2
and the set of integers
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
such that
p
divides

d_{i}
has cardinality different from
r-1
and
r-2
.

Then
\textsf{Log}_{(d_{1},\ldots,d_{r})}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
is an irreducible component of

\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
(with its reduced scheme structure) for

and

d=\sum_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}-2
.

Proof.

Case (
1
) is the content of Proposition
4.6
. We therefore assume that condition (
2
) or (
3
) holds.
Our assumptions ensure the existence of
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}\in{\mathsf{k}}
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}\lambda_{i}=0\,,

with the additional property that
\lambda_{i}/\lambda_{j}\notin\mathbb{F}_{p}
for every
i\neq j
.

Let
f_{i}\in H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}}(d_{i}))
be homogeneous polynomials defining smooth hypersurfaces
H_{i}
such that the divisor
D=\sum_{i=1}^{r}H_{i}
is a simple normal crossing divisor. Let
\omega\in H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}},\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}}(\log D))
be the logarithmic
1
-form defined in homogeneous coordinates by

\omega=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}\frac{df_{i}}{f_{i}}\,,

and let
\mathcal{F}
be the foliation defined by
\omega
.
Let
\Sigma
be any irreducible component of
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
containing
\mathcal{F}
. We will show that

\Sigma=\textsf{Log}_{(d_{1},\ldots,d_{r})}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
.

By Lemma
6.1
, the polar divisor
D
of
\omega
coincides with the degeneracy divisor of the
p
-curvature
{\Delta_{\mathcal{F}}}
.
Furthermore, by
[
13
, Lemma 6.3]
, there exists an open subset
U\subset\Sigma
, containing
\mathcal{F}
, such that for any closed point in
U
corresponding to a foliation
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
, the degeneracy divisor
{\Delta_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}}
is reduced. Possibly after further restricting
U
, every
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\in U
is
p
-dense (this is an open condition). By
[
13
, Proposition 4.4]
,
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is defined by a closed rational
1
-form
\omega^{\prime}
whose polar locus is
{\Delta_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}}
. Since
{\Delta_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}}
is reduced and normal crossing in codimension two (Corollary
5.4
),
\omega^{\prime}
is logarithmic with poles along
{\Delta_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}}
. By Corollary
5.2
, this ensures that each
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\in U
belongs to some
\textsf{Log}_{(d_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,d_{r^{\prime}})}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
where

satisfy
\sum_{i=1}^{r^{\prime}}d_{i}^{\prime}=d+2
.

To conclude the proof, we must show that
r^{\prime}=r
and
(d_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,d_{r}^{\prime})=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{r})
. On one hand, the polar divisor
{\Delta_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}}

lives in the fixed linear system
|\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\mathsf{k}}}(d+2)|
, and
the universal family of effective divisors over a linear system is flat (all fibers
are Cartier divisors with the same Hilbert polynomial). Since the number of
irreducible components of the fibers of a flat proper family is upper
semi-continuous, and
{\Delta_{\mathcal{F}}}
has

irreducible components,
we conclude that

for a general

consider an inclusion

of a

equals the number of distinct residues of

\varphi^{*}\omega
. Since
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\in U
is defined by a deformation

\varphi^{*}\omega

. Therefore

.

Finally, each degree
d_{j}
corresponds to the set of points in
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
where
\varphi^{*}\omega
has the same residue
\lambda_{j}
. Since deformations can only decrease the values of each

, but their sum remains fixed at

, it follows that
(d_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,d_{r}^{\prime})=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{r})
, as claimed.
∎

As a corollary, we obtain a new proof of Calvo-Andrade’s theorem on
the stability of generic logarithmic
1
-forms, this time from an
arithmetic perspective.

Corollary 6.3
.

Let

\textsf{Log}_{(d_{1},\ldots,d_{r})}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n})

7.
Foliations of degree two

7.1.
Foliations defined by Lie algebras of vector fields

We identify
\mathfrak{pgl}_{n+1}({\mathsf{k}})
, the quotient of
\End(\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{{\mathsf{k}}})
by multiples of the identity, with the Lie algebra of regular vector fields on
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
,
i.e.,
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}},T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}})
.

If the characteristic of
{\mathsf{k}}
does not divide
n+1
then we can concretely, in homogeneous coordinates, identify
\mathfrak{pgl}_{n+1}({\mathsf{k}})
with the Lie algebra of vector fields on
\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
with linear coefficients and zero divergence. In general, we will think of
\mathfrak{pgl}_{n+1}({\mathsf{k}})
as the quotient of the Lie algebra of linear vector fields on
\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
with linear coefficients modulo multiples of the radial vector field.

Given a Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{pgl}_{n+1}({\mathsf{k}})
, we consider the subsheaf of
T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}}
defined by the image of the evaluation morphism:

\mathrm{ev}:\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\longrightarrow T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}}.

Since, by assumption,
\mathfrak{h}
is a Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{pgl}_{n+1}({\mathsf{k}})
, the image of
\mathrm{ev}
is an involutive subsheaf of
T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
. Its saturation then defines the tangent sheaf of a foliation, which we denote by
\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{h})
.

Before stating our next result, we briefly recall the terminology concerning Kupka and non-Kupka singularities that will be used below. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a codimension
q
foliation on a smooth variety
X
, and let

\omega\in H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{q}\otimes\mathcal{N})

be a twisted
q
-form defining
\mathcal{F}
. A point
x\in\sing(\mathcal{F})

is called a Kupka singularity if there exist an open neighborhood
U
of
x
, a nowhere vanishing local section
\tau\in H^{0}(U,\mathcal{N})
, and a local
q
-form
\eta\in H^{0}(U,\Omega_{X}^{q})
such that

\omega_{\mid U}=\eta\otimes\tau

and

\eta(x)=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad d\eta(x)\neq 0.

This definition is independent of the choice of local trivialization of
\mathcal{N}
. The set of non-Kupka singularities of
\mathcal{F}
is
the complement of the Kupka set inside
\sing(\mathcal{F})
.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{pgl}_{n+1}({\mathsf{k}})
be such that
\mathrm{ev}
is injective and has saturated image, i.e.,
T_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{h})}\simeq\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}}
. Let
q
denote the codimension of
\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{h})
. If the set of non-Kupka singularities of
\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{h})
has codimension at least three, then any irreducible component
\Sigma
of
\textsf{Fol}^{q}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
containing
\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{h})
is such that a general closed point of
\Sigma
corresponds to a foliation defined by a Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{pgl}_{n+1}({\mathsf{k}})
of dimension
\dim\mathfrak{h}
.

Proof.

We draw on results from
[
15
]
,
which are proved in a characteristic-free setting.

By
[
15
, Corollary 4.11]

(the restatement of Theorem B in loc. cit.), the set of points in

\textsf{Fol}^{q}_{d}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
corresponding to foliations with locally free
tangent sheaf and non-Kupka singular locus of codimension at least three
is open. Since
\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{h})
belongs to this set by
hypothesis, every foliation in a sufficiently small open neighbourhood

U\subset\Sigma
of
\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{h})
also has locally free
tangent sheaf and non-Kupka singular locus of codimension at least three.

By
[
15
, Corollary 4.14]
,
the isomorphism class of the tangent sheaf is locally constant on
\Sigma
.
Since
T_{\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{h})}\simeq\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}}
by hypothesis, the tangent sheaf of
every foliation
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\in U
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}}
, and in particular is defined
by a Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{pgl}_{n+1}({\mathsf{k}})
of dimension

\dim\mathfrak{h}
.
∎

Example 7.2
.

Let
{\mathsf{k}}
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
. Let
\mathfrak{h}
be the subalgebra of
\mathfrak{pgl}_{4}({\mathsf{k}})
generated by the classes of the linear vector fields

\displaystyle v_{s}

\displaystyle=-x_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}-2x_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}-3x_{3}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{3}}\,

\displaystyle v_{n}

\displaystyle=x_{0}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}+x_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}+x_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{3}}\,.

Note that
[v_{s},v_{n}]=v_{n}
.

The corresponding foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
is defined by the projective
1
-form

\omega=i_{R}i_{v_{s}}i_{v_{n}}dx_{0}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{3}\,,

where
R
is the radial vector field. The set of non-Kupka singularities of
\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{h})
is, by definition, the zero locus of
d\omega
. An explicit computation shows that

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle=(-x_{0}x_{2}x_{3}+2x_{1}^{2}x_{3}-x_{1}x_{2}^{2})dx_{0}+(-3x_{0}x_{1}x_{3}+2x_{0}x_{2}^{2})dx_{1}

\displaystyle+(3x_{0}^{2}x_{3}-x_{0}x_{1}x_{2})dx_{2}+(-2x_{0}^{2}x_{2}+x_{0}x_{1}^{2})dx_{3}

and that

\displaystyle d\omega

\displaystyle=(7x_{1}x_{3}-3x_{2}^{2})dx_{0}\wedge dx_{1}+(-7x_{0}x_{3}-x_{1}x_{2})dx_{0}\wedge dx_{2}

\displaystyle+(3x_{0}x_{2}+x_{1}^{2})dx_{0}\wedge dx_{3}+(5x_{0}x_{2})dx_{1}\wedge dx_{2}

\displaystyle+(-5x_{0}x_{1})dx_{1}\wedge dx_{3}+(5x_{0}^{2})dx_{2}\wedge dx_{3}\,.

The zero locus of
d\omega
is then equal to

(1)

The line
\{x_{0}=x_{1}=0\}
when
p=3
; and

(2)

The two points
(1:0:0:0)
and
(0:0:0:1)
when
p=5
; and

(3)

The point
(0:0:0:1)
when
p\notin\{3,5\}
.

Our next result shows that the so-called exceptional component
\textsf{Exc}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{3})
of
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{3})
has natural analogs in all characteristics
p\neq 3
.

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
{\mathsf{k}}
be an algebraically closed field of positive characteristic
p\neq 3
. The closure of the orbit through the point in
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{3})
determined by Example
7.2
under the action of
\Aut(\mathbb{P}^{3}_{{\mathsf{k}}})
is an irreducible component
\textsf{Exc}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{3})
of
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{3})
.

Proof.

Notation as in Example
7.2
. Let
\mathfrak{h}
be the non-abelian Lie algebra of
\mathfrak{pgl}_{4}({\mathsf{k}})
generated by the classes of
v_{s}
and
v_{n}
. If
p\neq 3
then the induced foliation
\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{h})
satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
7.1
. Therefore, if
\Sigma\subset\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{3})
is an irreducible component containing
\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{h})
, there exists an open subset
U\subset\Sigma
containing
\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{h})
such that every foliation in
U
is defined by a non-abelian subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
of
\mathfrak{pgl}_{4}({\mathsf{k}})
. The image of the Lie-bracket morphism

is generated by a nilpotent element

of
\mathfrak{pgl}_{4}({\mathsf{k}})
. Perhaps after passing to a smaller open subset of

matrix with one-dimensional kernel. Jordan normal form implies that it is conjugated to

and hence we can assume that it is equal to
v_{n}
. Notice also that
v_{n}
corresponds to a regular nilpotent element of
\mathfrak{gl}_{4}({\mathsf{k}})
(the Lie algebra of
4\times 4
matrices over
{\mathsf{k}}
) and, as such, has
4
-dimensional centralizer and the dimension of the corresponding nilpotent orbit is

. Hence,
\Sigma
is birationally equivalent to a
\mathbb{P}^{2}
-bundle over the nilpotent orbit through

of the adjoint action on
\mathfrak{pgl}_{4}({\mathsf{k}})

\Sigma
has dimension
13
and is equal to the closure of the
\mathrm{PGL}(4)
orbit through
\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{h})
.
∎

Corollary 7.4
.

Let
{\mathsf{k}}
be an algebraically closed field of positive characteristic
p\neq 3
, and let
n\geq 4
be an integer. The closure of the orbit through the point in
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
determined by a dominant linear pull-back
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{3}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
of Example

under the action of
\Aut(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}})
is an irreducible component
\textsf{Exc}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
of
\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{n})
.

Proof.

As verified in Example

, the non-Kupka singularities of the foliation

have codimension three. The same property holds true for any pullback of
\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{h})
under a dominant linear projection
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{{\mathsf{k}}}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{3}_{{\mathsf{k}}}
. By applying
[
15

we deduce that any foliation sufficiently close to

has tangent sheaf isomorphic to

4.3
then implies that this foliation must be a linear pull-back of foliation on

7.3
allows us to conclude.
∎

Over the complex numbers, there exist exactly two irreducible components of

\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{3})
whose general element corresponds to a foliation with

\textsf{Exc}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{3})
and
\textsf{Log}_{(1,1,1,1)}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{3})
.

{\mathsf{k}}
is arbitrary, Theorem

ensures that

\textsf{Fol}^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{3})

If the characteristic of
{\mathsf{k}}
is different from

, then Proposition

shows that

is also an irreducible

\textsf{Exc}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{3})
and
\textsf{Log}_{(1,1,1,1)}(\mathbb{P}_{{\mathsf{k}}}^{3})

. Since the integrability condition is cut out by polynomial

is a scheme over

, and the function associating to each prime

Since its value at the generic point equals two, it equals two for all but

7.2.

Our last result summarizes our current understanding of the irreducible components of

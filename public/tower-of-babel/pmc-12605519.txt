Clinical outcomes and safety of CD47-targeted immunotherapies across hematologic malignancies: a systematic review of monoclonal antibodies and fusion proteins in combination strategies
Authors: Wajahat Mirza, Sundas Dadan, Eshan Ahmad, Ayesha Junaid, Tariq Mahmood Satti
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe CD47-SIRPα axis is a key innate immune checkpoint that enables tumor cells to evade macrophage-mediated clearance. CD47 is overexpressed in a spectrum of hematologic malignancies, contributing to poor outcomes, particularly in high-risk biological subgroups. While early clinical trials of CD47 blockade demonstrated limited efficacy as monotherapy, combination strategies have emerged as promising approaches. This systematic review synthesizes the current clinical evidence on the outcomes and safety of CD47-targeted monoclonal antibodies and fusion proteins administered in combination with regimens for hematologic malignancies.MethodsA comprehensive search of PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane Library, and clinical trial registries was conducted until May 2025. Prospective interventional trials evaluating CD47-targeted agents in combination with systemic therapies for hematologic malignancies were included. The outcomes of interest were response rate, survival, and safety. The methodological quality was assessed using the MINORS. The protocol for this review was prospectively registered in the PROSPERO International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (registration number: CRD420251071435).ResultsNine prospective clinical trials enrolling over 800 patients were included in this study. In patients with higher-risk myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS), the combination of magrolimab and azacitidine achieved an overall response rate (ORR) of 63%, with a complete remission (CR) rate exceeding 30%, including in patients with TP53-mutant disease. In untreated AML, the ORR reached 65%, with durable responses observed in patients with adverse cytogenetic mutations. In relapsed/refractory diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL), combinations of CD47 blockade with anti-CD20 antibodies ± chemotherapy or novel immunotherapeutics achieved an ORR of 33–52%, with CR rates of up to 33%. In indolent non-Hodgkin lymphoma, magrolimab plus rituximab produced an ORR of 74% and a CR of 39%, including in rituximab-refractory patients. Preliminary data on multiple myeloma have demonstrated encouraging activity in triple-class refractory diseases. Across malignancies, CD47-targeted combinations were well tolerated, with manageable anemia and no unexpected toxicity.ConclusionsCD47-targeted combinations demonstrate encouraging early phase efficacy and manageable safety in hematologic malignancies, with signals of benefit in higher-risk MDS, TP53-mutant AML, relapsed/refractory DLBCL, and rituximab-refractory iNHL. However, recent Phase III trials in newly diagnosed AML Daver et al. [27], and Zeidner et al. [26] did not confirm this benefit, underscoring that CD47 blockade remains investigational and requires validation in rigorously designed randomized studies.

Introduction

Hematologic malignancies represent a biologically and clinically diverse group of cancers that continue to pose significant therapeutic challenges despite decades of progress in treatment paradigms. From aggressive diseases such as acute myeloid leukemia (AML) and diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) to more indolent entities such as myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS) and indolent non-Hodgkin lymphomas (iNHL), a substantial proportion of patients eventually develop resistance or intolerance to standard therapies, resulting in poor long-term outcomes. Moreover, the prognosis remains particularly dismal in high-risk biological subgroups, such as TP53-mutant AML and MDS, double-hit or transformed DLBCL, and relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma (MM) [1–3]. Consequently, there is a pressing need to develop novel immunotherapeutic strategies that can overcome the inherent and acquired resistance mechanisms that underpin therapeutic failure in these cancers.

One of the most promising targets in this context is the CD47-SIRPα immune checkpoint axis. CD47, a transmembrane glycoprotein ubiquitously expressed in normal cells, delivers a potent "do not eat me" signal to macrophages and other innate immune effectors through its interaction with signal regulatory protein alpha (SIRPα) [4, 5]. In cancer, CD47 is often overexpressed as a mechanism of immune evasion, allowing malignant cells to escape macrophage-mediated phagocytosis despite the presence of pro-phagocytic signals such as calreticulin. High CD47 expression is correlated with adverse outcomes in multiple hematologic malignancies, including AML, MDS, DLBCL, and MM [6, 7]. Therapeutic blockade of the CD47–SIRPα interaction represents a novel immuno-oncology approach aimed at restoring innate immune surveillance and promoting tumor clearance.

Initial clinical trials of CD47-targeted agents, including monoclonal antibodies such as magrolimab and Hu5F9-G4, and fusion proteins such as TTI-621 and maplirpacept, demonstrated encouraging safety and pharmacodynamic profiles but limited single-agent efficacy, particularly in heavily pretreated populations [8–10]. These observations have led to a shift toward rational combination strategies designed to enhance macrophage activation and improve the clinical outcomes. For example, combining CD47 blockade with hypomethylating agents, such as azacitidine, has been shown to upregulate pro-phagocytic signals in malignant myeloid cells, synergizing with anti-CD47 therapy in AML and MDS [11, 12]. In B-cell lymphomas, combining CD47-targeted agents with anti-CD20 monoclonal antibodies, such as rituximab or obinutuzumab, leverages antibody-dependent cellular phagocytosis to potentiate antitumor responses [13, 14]. Similarly, combinations of immunomodulatory drugs (IMiDs), bispecific antibodies, and proteasome inhibitors are under active investigation for multiple myeloma and other lymphoid malignancies [15, 16].

Across hematologic cancers, the safety profile of CD47 blockade in combination regimens has proven manageable, with on-target anemia being the most commonly observed adverse event due to the transient phagocytosis of aging red blood cells [17]. Importantly, this combination approach has resulted in clinically meaningful response rates across multiple settings. Magrolimab combined with azacitidine has resulted in complete remission rates exceeding 30% in TP53-mutant AML and MDS [12]. In relapsed/refractory DLBCL, combinations with rituximab or novel agents such as glotamab have demonstrated objective response rates of up to 52% [14]. In iNHL and multiple myeloma, promising durability of response is emerging despite highly refractory disease populations [15, 16]. These findings underscore the potential of CD47-targeted immunotherapy to address the critical unmet needs across a spectrum of hematologic malignancies.

Despite this progress, several questions remain regarding the optimal integration of CD47-targeted agents into existing therapeutic paradigms for AML. Key issues include identifying the most synergistic combination partners for specific disease contexts, refining dosing strategies to balance efficacy and tolerability, and elucidating biomarkers that predict response or resistance. Moreover, comparative data across malignancies and agent classes (monoclonal antibodies vs. fusion proteins) are limited in number. Against this backdrop, we conducted a systematic review to synthesize the current clinical evidence on CD47-targeted immunotherapies in combination regimens across hematologic malignancies, focusing on the clinical outcomes and safety profiles of both mAbs and fusion proteins. This analysis aims to inform ongoing clinical development and guide future research in this rapidly evolving field.

Methods

Search strategy

A comprehensive literature search was conducted to identify all relevant clinical studies evaluating CD47-targeted immunotherapy in combination with other agents for the treatment of hematologic malignancies. Electronic databases, including PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, and the Cochrane Library, were searched from inception to May 2025 to identify relevant studies. The search strategy incorporated both controlled vocabulary terms and free-text keywords related to CD47, SIRPα, magrolimab, Hu5F9-G4, TTI-621, maplirpacept, CC-90002, and combinations thereof with standard or investigational therapies. The disease terms included hematologic malignancies, AML, MDS, DLBCL, non-Hodgkin lymphoma, and multiple myeloma. Clinical trial registries (ClinicalTrials.gov, EudraCT, and WHO ICTRP) were screened to identify unpublished or ongoing studies. The reference lists of relevant reviews and primary articles were manually reviewed to ensure the search was complete. The protocol for this review was prospectively registered in the PROSPERO International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (registration number: CRD420251071435).

Eligibility criteria

Studies were included if they met the following criteria: (1) prospective, early phase (Phase I–II) interventional clinical trials, non-randomized or single-arm in design; (2) evaluation of a CD47-targeted agent (monoclonal antibody or fusion protein) administered in combination with other systemic therapies; (3) enrollment of patients with hematologic malignancies, including AML, MDS, DLBCL, iNHL, or multiple myeloma; and (4) reporting of clinical outcomes such as response rates, progression-free survival, overall survival, and/or safety data.

Studies employing CD47-targeted agents as monotherapies were excluded unless they were part of a broader combination trial. Non-interventional studies, reviews, editorials, preclinical studies, and conference abstracts without sufficient clinical data were also excluded from the study. Randomized controlled trials (Phase IIb/III) were not incorporated into the eligible dataset because our risk-of-bias tool (MINORS) and analytic framework were pre-specified for exploratory, early phase designs.

As specified in our registered protocol, the literature search was conducted from inception through May 2025; therefore, studies published after this date fall outside the scope of the present synthesis. This includes the two randomized trials , Daver et al. [27] and Zeidner et al.[26], which are acknowledged narratively in the Discussion but not incorporated into the eligible data set.

Data extraction

Data extraction was performed independently by two investigators using standardized forms. The extracted data included study design, trial phase, study population characteristics, treatment regimens, number of patients enrolled, efficacy outcomes (objective response rate, complete response rate, duration of response, progression-free survival, overall survival), and safety outcomes (incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events, including anemia, thrombocytopenia, and infusion-related reactions). Where available, disease-specific factors, such as cytogenetic risk, TP53 mutation status, prior lines of therapy, and transplant eligibility, were recorded to facilitate comparisons across studies.

Risk of bias assessment

The methodological quality and risk of bias of the included studies were independently assessed using the Methodological Index for Non-Randomized Studies (MINORS) tool, which is specifically designed for single-arm and non-randomized interventional trials, including early phase oncology studies. The MINORS tool evaluates eight methodological domains: clearly stated aim, inclusion of consecutive patients, prospective data collection, appropriateness of endpoints, unbiased assessment of endpoints, adequacy of follow-up period, acceptability of loss to follow-up, and prospective calculation of study size. Each domain was scored to reflect the presence and quality of the report, and discrepancies between reviewers were resolved by consensus. The MINORS tool was selected as the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2.0 tool does not apply to the predominantly single-arm phase I and II studies included in this review.

PRISMA statement

This systematic review was conducted according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. A PRISMA flow diagram was used to document the selection process, including the number of records identified, screened, excluded, and ultimately included in this study. A systematic methodology was designed to ensure transparency, reproducibility, and rigor in synthesizing evidence regarding CD47-targeted combination strategies in hematologic malignancies.

Results

Search results and study selection

The systematic literature search initially identified 1,462 records from PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane Library, and clinical trial registries. After the removal of duplicates (n = 487), 975 unique records were screened at the title and abstract levels. Of these, 926 articles were excluded based on relevance, leaving 49 full-text articles for detailed assessment. Following the full-text review, 40 articles were excluded for reasons including monotherapy design, preclinical nature, lack of clinical outcomes, or a non-hematologic malignancy focus. Ultimately, nine prospective clinical trials met the eligibility criteria and were included in this review. Study characteristics of included trials evaluating CD47-targeted immunotherapies in hematologic malignancies

The therapeutic agents assessed comprised CD47-blocking monoclonal antibodies (magrolimab in six studies and TTI-621 in one study) and SIRPα-Fc fusion proteins (maplirpacept in two studies). Combination partners reflected disease-specific standards and rational synergies: hypomethylating agents in AML and MDS; anti-CD20 antibodies ± chemotherapy in aggressive and indolent NHL; bispecifics and IMiDs in DLBCL; and PI/IMiD/anti-CD38 combinations in multiple myeloma. The included trials enrolled over 800 patients with various hematologic malignancies, with trial sizes ranging from 46 to approximately 153.

Disease and malignancy characteristics of patient populations across included trials

The included studies enrolled a predominantly elderly population, with median ages ranging from 67 to 73 years in myeloid malignancies and lymphomas, and a younger but highly pre-treated cohort in multiple myeloma. The proportion of male patients ranged from 57 to 59%. The ECOG performance status was typically 0–2, reflecting the selection of patients with a reasonable functional reserve despite high-risk diseases.

In myeloid malignancies (AML and MDS), the combination of magrolimab with hypomethylating agents (azacitidine) has dominated the therapeutic approach, leveraging preclinical synergy and strong early clinical signals. In B cell lymphomas, CD47 blockade is paired with anti-CD20 monoclonal antibodies (rituximab or obinutuzumab) and, in some cases, chemotherapy (R-GemOx) or novel agents such as bispecific antibodies (glotamab) and tafasitamab–lenalidomide combinations. In multiple myeloma, magrolimab has been explored in combination with proteasome inhibitors, immunomodulatory drugs (IMiDs), and anti-CD38 monoclonal antibodies within a multi-arm phase II design. Collectively, these strategies underscore the broad clinical versatility of CD47-targeted agents and the ongoing efforts to identify optimal combination partners tailored to specific hematologic disease contexts.

Efficacy outcomes

Across the included studies, CD47-targeted immunotherapies in combination regimens demonstrated clinically meaningful efficacy in various hematologic malignancies, despite the high-risk nature of the enrolled population.

In untreated higher-risk MDS, the combination of magrolimab and azacitidine yielded an overall response rate (ORR) of 63%, with complete remission (CR) rates exceeding 30%, including in the TP53-mutant subgroup. In untreated AML, a similar combination achieved an ORR of 65% with CR/CRi rates of 41%, and durable remissions were observed even in patients with adverse cytogenetics and high TP53 allele fractions.

In relapsed/refractory DLBCL, combinations of magrolimab + rituximab ± GemOx and maplirpacept + tafasitamab + lenalidomide reported ORR between 33 and 52%, with CR rates ranging from 19 to 33%. These results compare favorably with historical controls in similar populations, particularly in patients ineligible for CAR-T therapy.

In indolent NHL, magrolimab + rituximab achieved an ORR of 74%, with CR in 39% of patients and sustained remission at 3-year follow-up [14]. Early phase data in multiple myeloma suggest that magrolimab-based combinations are tolerable and capable of inducing responses, even in triple-class refractory settings, although detailed efficacy data are pending from the ongoing phase II study.

Safety outcomes

The safety profile of CD47-targeted combination therapies for hematologic malignancies is generally manageable and consistent with known class effects. The most common treatment-emergent adverse event was on-target anemia, which reflects the transient phagocytosis of senescent red blood cells. Anemia was typically grade 1–2, with manageable transfusion requirements and no long-term cumulative toxic effects.

Thrombocytopenia was observed with variable frequency, often attributable to combination partners such as azacitidine or chemotherapy agents. Other adverse events included neutropenia, infusion-related reactions, and fatigue, none of which emerged as new safety signals specifically attributable to the CD47 blockade. Importantly, no significant autoimmune toxicities, cytokine release syndromes, or unexpected organ toxicities were observed, supporting the overall tolerability of CD47-targeted immunotherapy in combination with other regimens for treating hematological malignancies.

Interpretation of efficacy findings across malignancies

The clinical efficacy of CD47-targeted combination strategies varies across hematologic malignancies, reflecting differences in disease biology and maturity of available data. In untreated higher-risk MDS, magrolimab combined with azacitidine has demonstrated the most mature and compelling results. Complete remission rates exceeding 30% in this historically chemoresistant population, including the TP53-mutant subgroups, represent a significant therapeutic advance [1, 2]. Durable responses with median durations exceeding 9–12 months in responders further support this approach’s potential.

Similarly, in untreated AML, particularly TP53-mutant disease, the combination of magrolimab and azacitidine produced objective response rates of 65%, with CR/CRi rates of 41%, which compares favorably to outcomes with hypomethylating agents alone in this difficult-to-treat subset [2]. Notably, responses were observed even in patients with high variant allele frequencies of TP53 mutations and adverse cytogenetics, which are populations with poor historical outcomes.

In relapsed/refractory DLBCL, combinations of CD47 blockade with anti-CD20 antibodies ± chemotherapy have demonstrated significant activity. In the magrolimab + rituximab ± GemOx study, an ORR of 52% and CR rate of 33% were achieved, with responses seen in patients who were transplant- and CAR-T ineligible and those with double-hit and transformed diseases [13]. Early data with maplirpacept + tafasitamab + lenalidomide also suggest an ORR ≥ 33% with a promising depth of response in this challenging setting.

For indolent NHL, the combination of magrolimab and rituximab achieved an impressive ORR of 74%, including CR in 39% of patients with prior rituximab failure and early progression (POD24) [14]. These findings suggest that CD47 blockade may restore responsiveness to anti-CD20 therapy and provide a valuable option for rituximab-refractory iNHL.

In multiple myeloma, the data remain preliminary but encouraging. An ongoing multi-arm phase II study of magrolimab in combination with various backbone regimens in triple-class refractory MM has demonstrated acceptable tolerability and early signals of clinical activity [15]. Given the high unmet need in this area, further maturation of efficacy data is important.

Overall, these findings indicate that CD47-targeted combinations generate clinically meaningful responses across a spectrum of hematologic malignancies, with particular strength of evidence in higher-risk MDS, TP53-mutant AML, and rituximab-refractory NHL, and emerging signals in DLBCL and MM.

Emerging patterns of combination strategies

Several patterns emerged regarding the optimal combination of partners for CD47 blockade in the included studies. The most robust and consistent efficacy was observed with the combination of hypomethylating agents (azacitidine or oral decitabine/cedazuridine) in patients with myeloid malignancies. The preclinical synergy between CD47 blockade and HMAs appears to translate clinically, enhancing macrophage-mediated phagocytosis and promoting deeper and more durable remission [11, 12].

In B cell lymphomas, the combination of CD47-targeted agents with anti-CD20 monoclonal antibodies remains a rational and effective strategy. The ability of rituximab or obinutuzumab to promote antibody-dependent cellular phagocytosis complements CD47 blockade, and clinical results in both DLBCL and iNHL confirm this synergy [13, 14]. Additionally, the combination of CD47 blockade with novel immunotherapeutics, such as tafasitamab and glotamab, offers exciting potential to further enhance responses in aggressive lymphomas [19].

In multiple myeloma, combinations with IMiDs, proteasome inhibitors, and anti-CD38 antibodies are being explored [15]. This reflects an effort to leverage the immunomodulatory and cytotoxic effects of standard backbone therapies while overcoming immune evasion through CD47 blockade. Although still early, this approach is conceptually appealing, given the highly immunosuppressive bone marrow microenvironment of MM.

The final emerging observation relates to agent class. Although both monoclonal antibodies (magrolimab and Hu5F9-G4) and SIRPα-Fc fusion proteins (TTI-621 and maplirpacept) have demonstrated activity, it is too early to compare their relative efficacy or toxicity. Preliminary data suggest that fusion proteins may be associated with less pronounced anemia, potentially due to their lower affinity for normal blood cells [9, 19]. However, larger and more mature datasets are required to clarify whether the agent class meaningfully impacts clinical outcomes.

Discussion

The emergence of CD47-targeted immunotherapy has marked a pivotal advancement in the treatment of hematologic malignancies. By circumventing a critical innate immune checkpoint, CD47 blockade reinstates macrophage-mediated tumor clearance and augments the overall immunogenicity of malignant cells [4, 5]. This systematic review illustrates that combination strategies incorporating CD47-targeted agents can induce clinically significant responses across a range of hematologic cancers, including those traditionally resistant to chemotherapy and biologically high-risk subgroups of patients. These findings offer a compelling justification for the further development of CD47 blockade as an integral component of contemporary hematologic oncology.

In myeloid malignancies, particularly higher-risk MDS and TP53-mutant AML, the combination of magrolimab with hypomethylating agents (HMAs) has shown promising outcomes. The observed complete remission rates exceeding 30% in MDS and CR/CRi rates of 41% in AML [1, 2] significantly surpass historical benchmarks for HMA monotherapy, which typically results in CR rates of < 20% in these contexts [3]. Notably, responses in TP53-mutant disease, long regarded as one of the most intractable subsets, suggest that CD47 blockade may address an unmet need where conventional approaches are inadequate. The ability of CD47 blockade to synergize with HMAs through enhanced calreticulin exposure and macrophage phagocytosis provides a robust biological basis for these clinical observations [11, 12].

In B cell lymphomas, including both aggressive (DLBCL) and indolent (iNHL) forms, CD47-targeted therapeutic combinations have exhibited significant clinical efficacy, even among heavily pretreated cohorts. In cases of relapsed/refractory DLBCL, objective response rates (ORRs) of up to 52% and complete response (CR) rates exceeding 30% were achieved with the combination of magrolimab and rituximab, with or without chemotherapy [13]. Early data on maplirpacept-based combinations are similarly promising. Notably, responses have been observed in patients ineligible for CAR-T therapy and those with double-hit or transformed disease, where conventional salvage options are limited. In iNHL, the combination of magrolimab and rituximab reinstated significant anti-lymphoma activity in rituximab-refractory patients, achieving an ORR of 74% and a CR of 39% [14]. These findings suggest that CD47 blockade may enhance the efficacy of anti-CD20 antibodies and resensitize tumors to previously ineffective therapies.

Emerging data in multiple myeloma further broaden the therapeutic potential of CD47 blockade. A multi-arm phase II study of magrolimab-based combinations in triple-class refractory multiple myeloma is ongoing, demonstrating tolerability and early indications of clinical activity [15, 18]. Given the substantial unmet need in this heavily pretreated population, CD47 blockade offers a novel immunological approach that complements existing T cell- and proteasome-directed strategies. The bone marrow niche in multiple myeloma is uniquely immunosuppressive, and macrophage activation via CD47 blockade may be particularly valuable in overcoming stromal resistance.

Several significant patterns of synergistic combination therapy are evident across various malignancies. Hypomethylating agents continue to be the most effective partners in myeloid diseases, consistent with their ability to prime innate immune responses. In lymphoid malignancies, anti-CD20 monoclonal antibodies exploit Fc receptor-mediated phagocytosis, whereas emerging combinations with IMiDs and bispecific antibodies may further enhance efficacy [20–23]. Notably, both monoclonal antibodies and SIRPα-Fc fusion proteins have shown clinical activity, although direct comparisons between these studies are not yet available. Preliminary findings indicate that fusion proteins may result in lower rates of anemia, a critical class of toxicity; however, further data are required to substantiate this finding [9, 19].

Safety remains a pivotal consideration in developing CD47-targeted therapies. The most consistent toxicity observed across studies is on-target anemia, which is driven by the transient phagocytosis of senescent red blood cells [17]. Nevertheless, this effect is generally manageable and non-cumulative, with grade 3–4 anemia occurring infrequently in patients. Thrombocytopenia and neutropenia are common but are often attributable to backbone agents [24, 25], [26]. Importantly, autoimmune toxicities, cytokine release syndromes, and severe organ toxicities have not emerged as significant concerns in this context. The overall safety profile supports the feasibility of incorporating CD47 blockade into multiagent regimens for hematologic malignancies.

The methodological quality of the included studies, as evaluated using the MINORS tool, was acceptable for early phase oncology trials. Although certain limitations are inherent to the phase I/II design, such as small sample sizes and incomplete long-term follow-up, the studies were generally well executed, with clearly defined aims, prospective data collection, and appropriate endpoint selection. The consistent demonstration of efficacy and manageable safety across heterogeneous disease settings underscores the robustness of clinical signals.

However, this review has several limitations. The included studies were predominantly non-randomized, and the absence of comparator arms limited definitive conclusions regarding the incremental benefits over standard therapies. The heterogeneity of combination partners and patient populations further complicates the cross-trial comparisons. Additionally, mature survival data are limited for many regimens, particularly in MM and DLBCL, for which trials are still ongoing. Finally, the field is rapidly evolving, with several next-generation CD47-targeted agents and novel combinations currently under investigation.

Future research should prioritize randomized phase III trials to validate the efficacy of CD47 blockade in combination with standard treatment regimens. Biomarker-driven patient selection, particularly for acute myeloid leukemia (AML) and myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS), may enhance clinical outcomes and reduce toxicity. The role of CD47 blockade in frontline therapy, post-transplant maintenance, and in combination with other immunomodulating agents warrants systematic investigation. Furthermore, comparative studies on monoclonal antibodies and fusion proteins are essential for defining optimal therapeutic strategies.

Since the completion of our literature search (inception through May 2025), two randomized, placebo-controlled Phase III trials of CD47-targeted combinations in newly diagnosed AML have been reported. Daver et al. [27] and Zeidner et al. [26], did not demonstrate improvement in clinical outcomes with magrolimab-based regimens and raised safety concerns in specific patient subsets. These findings underscore the disease- and context-specific challenges associated with targeting the CD47 axis in AML. Although these trials fall outside the scope of our prespecified eligibility criteria and cutoff date, their results provide an important perspective and suggest that confirmatory efficacy remains unproven for this indication. Our synthesis of early phase, non-randomized studies across hematologic malignancies highlights signals of activity and safety in exploratory settings; however, these findings must now be interpreted with caution in light of emerging Phase III evidence.

In conclusion, this systematic review underscores the emerging clinical utility of CD47-targeted immunotherapy in combination with regimens for hematologic malignancies. Meaningful responses can be achieved in high-risk and refractory disease settings with an acceptable safety profile. As randomized trials progress and novel combinations are evaluated, CD47 blockade may become an integral component of immune-based treatment paradigms in hematologic oncology.

Conclusion

CD47-targeted combinations demonstrate encouraging early phase activity with manageable safety across several hematologic malignancies, including higher-risk MDS, TP53-mutated AML, relapsed/refractory DLBCL, and rituximab-refractory iNHL. However, recent Phase III trials of magrolimab in newly diagnosed AML Daver et al. [27]; Zeidner et al. [26]) underscore that the efficacy has not yet been confirmed in this setting and highlight the need for disease-specific, indication-level validation. Taken together, the available evidence suggests that CD47 inhibition remains a promising but investigational therapeutic strategy that requires continued evaluation in rigorously designed randomized studies. As randomized trials progress and novel combinations are explored, CD47-targeted strategies are poised to become integral components of future immune-based treatment paradigms in hematologic oncology.

Limitations

The findings of this systematic review should be interpreted in the context of several limitations. Most of the included studies were non-randomized, single-arm phase I/II trials; therefore, definitive conclusions regarding their comparative efficacy versus standard treatments cannot yet be drawn. Variations in study design, patient populations, and combination partners further complicate direct cross-trial comparisons of these studies. Additionally, long-term survival outcomes are still immature for many of the evaluated regimens, and data on biomarkers of response are limited. Despite these challenges, the consistent observation of clinical activity and acceptable safety across various disease settings provides a solid foundation for ongoing drug development.

DAHAs of Type C ∨ ​ C n C^{\vee}C_{n} and Character Varieties
Oleg Chalykh, Bradley Ryan

Abstract
This paper studies the spherical subalgebra of the double affine Hecke algebra of type C ∨ ​ C n C^{\vee}C_{n} and relates it, at the classical level q = 1 q=1 , to a certain character variety of the four-punctured Riemann sphere. This establishes a conjecture from [ 16 ] . As a by-product, we find a completed phase space for the trigonometric van Diejen system, explicitly integrate its dynamics and explain how it can be obtained via Hamiltonian reduction.



1  Introduction

The double affine Hecke algebras (DAHAs) were invented by I. Cherednik who famously used them to prove Macdonald’s conjectures
[
12
,
13
]
. Their rational version, known as Cherednik algebras, was introduced and studied in the seminal paper by P. Etingof and V. Ginzburg
[
14
]
. Since then, Cherednik algebras and DAHAs have found a wide range of mathematical uses and by now there is vast literature devoted to these classes of algebras.

One insight of
[
14
]
was that the spherical subalgebra of the Cherednik algebra for a Coxeter group
W\subseteq\GL(V)
provides an interesting Poisson deformation of the orbifold
T^{\ast}V/W
. In particular, for the symmetric group
W=S_{n}

[
14
]
related the spherical subalgebra, at the classical level
t=0
, to the Calogero-Moser space

\mathcal{C}_{n}=\{X,Y\in\Mat_{n\times n}(\mathbb{C}):\rank([X,Y] - \Id_n)=1\}\mathbin{\mathchoice{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}}\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C}),

previously studied by G. Wilson
[
49
]
. The spaces
\mathcal{C}_{n}
have their origin in the theory of Hamiltonian reduction and integrable systems
[
32
]
. These are affine smooth varieties (of dimension
2n
) that can be viewed as completed phase spaces for the classical Calogero-Moser particle system. In order to formulate this result of
[
14
]
, we recall that the Cherednik algebra
\mathsf{H}_{t,c}
of
W
contains the group algebra
\mathbb{C}W
, and the spherical subalgebra of
\mathsf{H}_{t,c}
is defined as
e\mathsf{H}_{t,c}e
, where
e
is the group symmetriser. Recall also that the spherical subalgebra becomes commutative at the classical level
t=0
.

Theorem 1.1
(cf.
[
14
, Theorem 1.23]
)

For
W=S_{n}
and
c\neq 0
, we have an isomorphism

e\mathsf{H}_{0,c}e\cong\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{C}_{n}]

between the spherical subalgebra and the algebra of regular functions on the Calogero-Moser space. Equivalently,
\Spec(e\mathsf{H}_{0,c}e)\cong\mathcal{C}_{n}
as affine algebraic varieties.

This has the following representation-theoretic implication.

Theorem 1.2
(
[
14
, Theorem 1.24]
)

For
W=S_{n}
, every finite-dimensional irreducible representation of
\mathsf{H}_{0,c}
has dimension
n!
, and such representations are in 1-1 correspondence with points of
\mathcal{C}_{n}
, i.e.

\Irrep(\mathsf{H}_{0,c})\cong\mathcal{C}_{n}.

This motivates studying the varieties
\Spec(e\mathsf{H}_{0,c}e)
in other situations. For example, Etingof and Ginzburg consider symplectic reflection groups
W=S_{n}\ltimes G^{n}
for finite subgroups
G\subseteq\SU(2)
and prove a generalisation of Theorem
1.1
for the corresponding symplectic reflection algebras, see
[
14
, Theorem 11.16]
.

Another result of that kind, closely related to the subject of the present paper, was obtained by A. Oblomkov in
[
36
]
, where he studied the analogous problem for the DAHA
H_{q,\tau}
of type
\GL_{n}
. Recall that this DAHA contains the finite Hecke algebra of
S_{n}
, so one defines its spherical subalgebra as
e_{\tau}H_{q,\tau}e_{\tau}
, where
e_{\tau}
is the Hecke symmetriser. Again, the spherical subalgebra becomes commutative at the classical level
q=1
and we have the following result.

Theorem 1.3
(
[
36
]
)

For
\tau
not a root of unity, we have an isomorphism

\Spec(e_{\tau}H_{1,\tau}e_{\tau})\cong\text{CM}_{\tau},

where

CM_{\tau}=\{X,Y\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C}):\rank(\tau XYX\inv Y\inv- \tau\inv\Id_n)=1\}\mathbin{\mathchoice{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}}\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C}).

The space
CM_{\tau}
was previously identified as a completed phase space for the Ruijsenaars–Schneider system
[
23
]
. Oblomkov also establishes an analogue to Theorem
1.2
: finite-dimensional irreducible representations of
H_{1,\tau}
are in 1-1 correspondence with points of
CM_{\tau}
.

A further generalisation was proposed in
[
18
,
16
]
by way of introducing generalised DAHAs associated to the star-shaped Dynkin quivers of type
\widetilde{D}_{4},\widetilde{E}_{6},\widetilde{E}_{7},\widetilde{E}_{8}
. In these cases,
[
16
, Conjecture 5.1.1]
states that the corresponding spherical subalgebras at the classical level
q=1
are isomorphic to certain character varieties of a punctured Riemann sphere. While some important steps towards a proof of this conjecture have been made in
[
16
]
, it remained open so far.

Our main result is a proof of the aforementioned conjecture for the generalised DAHA of type
\widetilde{D}_{4}
. This generalised DAHA is isomorphic to the usual DAHA of type
C^{\vee}C_{n}
which makes it amenable to the methods of
[
36
]
. To state the result, we need a replacement for the above space
CM_{\tau}
. Let
C_{i}=[\Lambda_{i}]\subseteq\GL_{2n}(\mathbb{C})
for

denote the conjugacy classes represented by the matrices

\displaystyle=\diag(\mathop{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\hfil\displaystyle{-k_{0}^{-1},\dots,-k_{0}^{-1}}\hfil$\crcr\kern 3.0pt\cr\tiny\upbracefill\crcr\kern 3.0pt\cr}}}\limits_{n},\mathop{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\hfil\displaystyle{k_{0},\dots,k_{0}}\hfil$\crcr\kern 3.0pt\cr\tiny\upbracefill\crcr\kern 3.0pt\cr}}}\limits_{n}),

(1.1)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{2}

\displaystyle=\diag(\mathop{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\hfil\displaystyle{-u_{0}^{-1},\dots,-u_{0}^{-1}}\hfil$\crcr\kern 3.0pt\cr\tiny\upbracefill\crcr\kern 3.0pt\cr}}}\limits_{n},\mathop{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\hfil\displaystyle{u_{0},\dots,u_{0}}\hfil$\crcr\kern 3.0pt\cr\tiny\upbracefill\crcr\kern 3.0pt\cr}}}\limits_{n}),

\displaystyle\Lambda_{3}

\displaystyle=\diag(\mathop{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\hfil\displaystyle{-u_{n}^{-1},\dots,-u_{n}^{-1}}\hfil$\crcr\kern 3.0pt\cr\tiny\upbracefill\crcr\kern 3.0pt\cr}}}\limits_{n},\mathop{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\hfil\displaystyle{u_{n},\dots,u_{n}}\hfil$\crcr\kern 3.0pt\cr\tiny\upbracefill\crcr\kern 3.0pt\cr}}}\limits_{n}),

\displaystyle=\diag(\mathop{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\hfil\displaystyle{-k_{n}^{-1},\dots,-k_{n}^{-1}}\hfil$\crcr\kern 3.0pt\cr\tiny\upbracefill\crcr\kern 3.0pt\cr}}}\limits_{n},\mathop{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\hfil\displaystyle{k_{n}t^{-2},\dots,k_{n}t^{-2}}\hfil$\crcr\kern 3.0pt\cr\tiny\upbracefill\crcr\kern 3.0pt\cr}}}\limits_{n-1},\mathop{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\hfil\displaystyle{k_{n}t^{2n-2}}\hfil$\crcr\kern 3.0pt\cr\tiny\upbracefill\crcr\kern 3.0pt\cr}}}\limits_{1}),

(1.2)

This is a

attached to the punctures (see Definition

Theorem 1.4

Let
\mathcal{H}_{q,\bm{\tau}}
denote the DAHA of type
C^{\vee}C_{n}
with parameters
q
,
\bm{\tau}=(k_{0},k_{n},t,u_{0},u_{n})
, and
e_{\tau}\mathcal{H}_{q,\bm{\tau}}e_{\tau}
the corresponding spherical subalgebra. Then, for
q=1
and generic parameters
\bm{\tau}
, we have an isomorphism

e_{\tau}\mathcal{H}_{1,\bm{\tau}}e_{\tau}\cong\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{M}_{n}].

(1.3)

We also show that the finite-dimensional irreducible representations of
\mathcal{H}_{1,\bm{\tau}}
are in 1-1 correspondence with points of
\mathcal{M}_{n}
, and suggest a quantisation of the isomorphism (

).

Our proof of Theorem
1.4
follows the general strategy of
[
36
]
, albeit this requires a few additional ingredients. Note that it relies crucially on the existence of the so-called Basic Representation for the DAHA. Since such a representation is not available for the generalised DAHAs of type
\widetilde{E}_{6}
,
\widetilde{E}_{7}
,
\widetilde{E}_{8}
, for those cases our approach does not work and the conjecture
[
16
, Conjecture 5.1.1]
remains open. It should be mentioned that for

, the result of Theorem

follows from
[
37
]
, where the spherical subalgebra of the DAHA of
C^{\vee}C_{1}

The isomorphism (
1.3
) has a nice application in the theory of integrable systems. Our main result here (see Theorem

\mathcal{M}_{n}
can be viewed as a completed phase space for the trigonometric van Diejen system. This particle system in the quantum setting is intimately related to Koornwinder polynomials. As we demonstrate, the corresponding classical Hamiltonian dynamics on
\mathcal{M}_{n}
can be explicitly integrated. We also interpret it via Hamiltonian reduction using the Fock–Rosly framework
[
23
]
.

Let us finish the introduction by mentioning some other contexts where the varieties
\mathcal{M}_{n}
, or closely-related spaces, appear. First, the varieties
\mathcal{M}_{n}
can be viewed as monodromy spaces for the Fuchsian systems studied in

in the context of isomonodromy deformations and matrix Painlevé VI systems (for

has two other avatars: the spaces
\mathcal{M}_{\text{dR}}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\text{Dol}}
of (weakly) parabolic local systems and Higgs bundles, respectively. By the result of
[

, all three are diffeomorphic to the Hilbert scheme
\Hilb^{n}(S)
for a certain surface

. Note that
\mathcal{M}_{\text{Dol}}
hosts the elliptic Inozemtsev integrable system and is related to the Seiberg–Witten geometry of some
4d

\mathcal{N}=2
superconformal field theories
[
2
]
. Finally, from a more general perspective, there is a geometric approach to the moduli spaces of sheaves on non-commutative deformations of surfaces developed by E. Rains
[
39
,
40
]
, and also his theory of elliptic DAHAs
[
41
]
within which the DAHA studied here can be obtained in a trigonometric limit. This gives a moral reason, if not a proof, for the isomorphism (
1.3
), by viewing both sides as two kinds of deformations of a particular Hilbert scheme.

2  The DAHA of Type
C^{\vee}C_{n}

The double affine Hecke algebras (DAHAs) were introduced by Cherednik
[
11
]
for reduced affine root systems. Their definition for the non-reduced affine root system of type
C^{\vee}C_{n}
was suggested by Sahi
[
43
]
and is recalled next.

2.1  The DAHA

Definition 2.1

Let
k_{0},k_{n},t,u_{0},u_{n},q^{1/2}\in\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
and write
\bm{\tau}
for the parameters
(k_{0},k_{n},t,u_{0},u_{n})
. The
double affine Hecke algebra of type
C^{\vee}C_{n}
is the associative algebra
\mathcal{H}_{q,\bm{\tau}}
over
\mathbb{C}[\bm{\tau}^{\pm 1},q^{\pm 1/2}]
generated by the elements
T_{0}^{\pm 1},\dots,T_{n}^{\pm 1}
and
X_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm 1}
, subject to the following relations:

()

T_{0}-T_{0}^{-1}=k_{0}-k_{0}^{-1}
.

()

T_{i}-T_{i}^{-1}=t-t^{-1}
. (
i=1,\dots,n-1
)

()

T_{n}-T_{n}^{-1}=k_{n}-k_{n}^{-1}
.

()

[T_{i},T_{j}]=0
. (
\absolutevalue{i - j}>1
)

()

T_{0}T_{1}T_{0}T_{1}=T_{1}T_{0}T_{1}T_{0}
.

()

T_{i}T_{i+1}T_{i}=T_{i+1}T_{i}T_{i+1}
. (
i=1,\dots,n-2
)

()

T_{n-1}T_{n}T_{n-1}T_{n}=T_{n}T_{n-1}T_{n}T_{n-1}
.

()

[X_{i},X_{j}]=0
. (
1\leq i<j\leq n
)

()

[T_{i},X_{j}]=0
. (
j\neq i,i+1
)

()

T_{i}X_{i}=X_{i+1}T_{i}^{-1}
. (
i=1,\dots,n-1
)

()

T_{0}^{\vee}-(T_{0}^{\vee})^{-1}=u_{0}-u_{0}^{-1}
. (
T_{0}^{\vee}\coloneqq q^{-1/2}T_{0}^{-1}X_{1}
)

()

T_{n}^{\vee}-(T_{n}^{\vee})^{-1}=u_{n}-u_{n}^{-1}
. (
T_{n}^{\vee}\coloneqq X_{n}^{-1}T_{n}^{-1}
)

The elements
T_{1},\dots,T_{n}
generate a subalgebra
H_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{q,\bm{\tau}}
isomorphic to the finite Hecke algebra of type
C_{n}
. For
k_{n}=t=1
, it becomes isomorphic to the group algebra
\mathbb{C}W
of the Weyl group of type
C_{n}
, generated by
s_{1},\dots,s_{n}
subject to
s_{i}^{2}=1
and the above relations (iv), (vi), (vii). If
w=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{\ell}}
is a reduced decomposition of
w\in W
, we write

T_{w}\coloneqq T_{i_{1}}\cdots T_{i_{\ell}}.

It is well known that
T_{w}
does not depend on the choice of a reduced decomposition of
w
. As a vector space,
H_{n}
is generated by the elements
T_{w}
with
w\in W
. The mapping
T_{i}\mapsto t
(
i=1,\dots,n-1
),
T_{n}\mapsto k_{n}
extends to a one-dimensional representation
\chi:T_{w}\mapsto\tau_{w}
of
H_{n}
. With this representation, one associates the following idempotent, called the
Hecke symmetriser
, in
H_{n}
(which becomes the usual group symmetriser in
\mathbb{C}W
when
k_{n}=t=1
):

e_{\tau}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sum_{w\in W}\tau_{w}^{2}}\sum_{w\in W}\tau_{w}T_{w}.

(2.1)

Definition 2.2

The
spherical subalgebra
of the DAHA
\mathcal{H}_{q,\bm{\tau}}
is
e_{\tau}\mathcal{H}_{q,\bm{\tau}}e_{\tau}
.

Notation 2.3

As a way to alleviate notation here on in, we shall introduce the following:

S\coloneqq T_{1}\cdots T_{n-1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad S^{\dagger}\coloneqq T_{n-1}\cdots T_{1}.

(2.2)

The following corollary of the DAHA relations will be important later.

Lemma 2.4

We have the relation
q^{1/2}T_{0}T_{0}^{\vee}ST_{n}^{\vee}T_{n}S^{\dagger}=1
.

Proof.

Using
T_{0}^{\vee}=q^{-1/2}T_{0}^{-1}X_{1}
and
T_{n}^{\vee}=X_{n}^{-1}T_{n}^{-1}
, the needed relation is
X_{1}SX_{n}^{-1}S^{\dagger}=1
, rather
S^{\dagger}X_{1}=X_{n}S^{-1}
which is an easy consequence of relations (x) of the DAHA.
∎

2.2  Duality

Apart from the obvious commutative subalgebra
\mathbb{C}[\bm{X}^{\pm 1}]
of Laurent polynomials in
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
,
\mathcal{H}_{q,\bm{\tau}}
contains another commutative Laurent polynomial subalgebra
\mathbb{C}[\bm{Y}^{\pm 1}]
in
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}
, where

Y_{i}\coloneqq T_{i}\cdots T_{n-1}T_{n}T_{n-1}\cdots T_{1}T_{0}T_{1}^{-1}\cdots T_{i-1}^{-1}.

(2.3)

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, let us write
\bm{X}^{\lambda}
for
X_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}\dots X_{n}^{\lambda_{n}}
, and similarly for
\bm{Y}^{\lambda}
. The elements

\bm{X}^{\lambda}T_{w}\bm{Y}^{\mu},\qquad\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}

form a basis of
\mathcal{H}_{q,\bm{\tau}}
. In other words, every element
h\in\mathcal{H}_{q,\bm{\tau}}
has a unique presentation as

h=\sum_{\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},w\in W}h_{\lambda,w,\mu}\bm{X}^{\lambda}T_{w}\bm{Y}^{\mu},\qquad\text{with}\ h_{\lambda,w,\mu}\in\mathbb{C}.

This is referred to as the
PBW property
of
\mathcal{H}_{q,\bm{\tau}}
, from which we have a vector space isomorphism

\mathcal{H}_{q,\bm{\tau}}\cong\mathbb{C}[\bm{X}^{\pm 1}]\otimes H_{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}[\bm{Y}^{\pm 1}].

This indicates that
X_{i}
and
Y_{i}
should be viewed on the same footing (which is far from obvious from the definitions). This is manifested by the so-called
duality
, an isomorphism between two DAHAs with different parameters as formulated in the following result.

Theorem 2.5
(
[
43
, Theorem 4.2]
, Duality Isomorphism)

Let
\mathcal{H}_{q,\bm{\tau}}
be the DAHA with parameters
\bm{\tau}=(k_{0},k_{n},t,u_{0},u_{n})
, and
\mathcal{H}_{q^{-1},\bm{\sigma}}
the DAHA with parameters
\bm{\sigma}=(u_{n}^{-1},k_{n}^{-1},t^{-1},u_{0}^{-1},k_{0}^{-1})
. There is a unique algebra isomorphism
\varepsilon:\mathcal{H}_{q,\bm{\tau}}\to\mathcal{H}_{q^{-1},\bm{\sigma}}
given on generators by

\displaystyle\varepsilon(T_{0})

\displaystyle=T_{1}\cdots T_{n-1}(T_{n}^{\vee})^{-1}T_{n-1}^{-1}\cdots T_{1}^{-1},

\displaystyle\varepsilon(T_{i})

\displaystyle=T_{i}^{-1},

\displaystyle\varepsilon(X_{i})

\displaystyle=Y_{i}.

It is equal to its inverse, with swapped
\bm{\tau}\leftrightarrow\bm{\sigma}
and
q\leftrightarrow q^{-1}
. In particular,
\varepsilon(Y_{i})=X_{i}
.

2.3  The Basic Representation

A crucial ingredient for what follows is the Basic Representation of
\mathcal{H}_{q,\bm{\tau}}

[
43
, Theorem 3.1]
, which extends the representation of the affine Hecke algebra of type
\widetilde{C}_{n}
found in
[
35
]

Consider the ring of
q
-difference operators in
n
variables
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
. It is generated by
X_{i}^{\pm 1},P_{i}^{\pm 1}
with
i=1,\dots,n
, subject to the following relations for all indices
i
and
j
:

[X_{i},X_{j}]=[P_{i},P_{j}]=0,\qquad P_{i}X_{j}=q^{\delta_{ij}}X_{j}P_{i}.

(2.4)

We denote this ring
\mathbb{C}_{q}[\bm{X}^{\pm 1},\bm{P}^{\pm 1}]
; when
q=1
, it is the ring
\mathbb{C}[\bm{X}^{\pm 1},\bm{P}^{\pm 1}]
of Laurent polynomials in
2n
variables. Localising on the (Ore) set
\mathbb{C}(\bm{X})\setminus\{0\}
of non-zero rational functions, we obtain the ring
\mathbb{C}_{q}(\bm{X})[\bm{P}^{\pm 1}]
of
q
-difference operators with rational coefficients, denoted
\mathscr{D}_{q}
for brevity:

\mathscr{D}_{q}\coloneqq\mathbb{C}_{q}(\bm{X})[\bm{P}^{\pm 1}].

(2.5)

The Weyl group of type
C_{n}
consisting of
sign-changing
permutations on
n
letters,

W=S_{n}\ltimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n},

(2.6)

is generated by transpositions
\s_{ij}
and sign-reversals
\s_{i}
. It acts on on
\mathbb{C}_{q}[\bm{X}^{\pm 1},\bm{P}^{\pm 1}]
and
\mathscr{D}_{q}
by

\displaystyle\s_{i}

\displaystyle:X_{i}\mapsto X_{i}^{-1},\quad

\displaystyle P_{i}\mapsto P_{i}^{-1},

(2.7)

\displaystyle\s_{ij}

\displaystyle:X_{i}\mapsto X_{j},\quad

\displaystyle P_{i}\mapsto P_{j},

\displaystyle\s_{ij}^{+}=\s_{ij}\s_{i}\s_{j}

\displaystyle:X_{i}\mapsto X_{j}^{-1},\quad

\displaystyle P_{i}\mapsto P_{j}^{-1}.

We can, therefore, make the following definition.

Definition 2.6

The algebra of
q
-difference-reflection operators
is the semi-direct product

\mathscr{D}_{q}\rtimes\mathbb{C}W.

Elements of
\mathscr{D}_{q}\rtimes\mathbb{C}W
are finite linear combinations

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},w\in W}a_{\lambda,w}\bm{P}^{\lambda}w,\qquad\text{with}\ a_{\lambda,w}\in\mathbb{C}(\bm{X}).

Now, introduce the notation

s_{0}\coloneqq P_{1}^{-1}\s_{1},\qquad s_{n}\coloneqq\s_{n},\qquad s_{i}\coloneqq\s_{i\,i+1}\quad(i=1,\dots,n-1).

(2.8)

They satisfy the relations of the affine Weyl group
\widetilde{W}
of type
\widetilde{C}_{n}
, cf. Definition
2.1
(v)-(vii).

(

3  Character Varieties

We begin by recalling the character varieties of punctured Riemann surfaces and some of their fundamental properties that we will need.

3.1  General Properties

Given
g,k\geq 0
and conjugacy classes
C_{1},\dots,C_{k}\subseteq\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, consider

\mathfrak{R}_{g,k}=\{X_{1},Y_{1},\ldots,X_{g},Y_{g}\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C}),A_{i}\in C_{i}:X_{1}Y_{1}X_{1}^{-1}Y_{1}^{-1}\dots X_{g}Y_{g}X_{g}^{-1}Y_{g}^{-1}A_{1}\cdots A_{k}=\mathbbm{1}_{n}\}.

There is a natural action of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
on
{\mathfrak{R}}_{g,k}
by conjugation, so we define the

\GL_{n}
-character variety

\mathfrak{M}_{g,k}
as the GIT-quotient

\mathfrak{M}_{g,k}\coloneqq{\mathfrak{R}}_{g,k}\mathbin{\mathchoice{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}}\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C}).

(3.1)

The affine variety
\mathfrak{M}_{g,k}
can be viewed as the moduli space of (semi-simple) representations of the fundamental group of a genus
g
Riemann surface
\Sigma_{g,k}
with
k
punctures, where we fix the conjugacy classes representing the loops around the punctures.

Let us assume that the conjugacy classes
C_{i}
are semi-simple so they are represented by diagonal matrices (that encode the eigenvalues, possibly repeated, of
A_{i}
). Let us write
\lambda_{ij}
with
i=1,\dots,k
and
j=1,\dots,m_{i}
for the distinct eigenvalues of
A_{i}
, and
\mu_{ij}
for the multiplicity of the eigenvalue
\lambda_{ij}
. We have
\mu_{i1}+\dots+\mu_{i\,m_{i}}=n
for all
i
.

Definition 3.1

Let
\lambda_{ij}
,
i=1,\dots,k
,
j=1,\dots,m_{i}
denote the distinct eigenvalues of semi-simple
A_{i}\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, with multiplicities
\mu_{ij}
. The conjugacy classes represented by the
A_{i}
are
generic
if

\prod_{i=1}^{k}\prod_{j=1}^{m_{i}}\lambda_{ij}^{\mu_{ij}}=1

and, for any
1\leq s<n
and a collection of numbers
\nu_{ij}\leq\mu_{ij}
with
\nu_{i1}+\cdots+\nu_{i\,m_{i}}=s
for all
i
,

\prod_{i=1}^{k}\prod_{j=1}^{m_{i}}\lambda_{ij}^{\nu_{ij}}\neq 1.

The genericity condition guarantees that the
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})/\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
\mathfrak{R}_{g,k}
is free. We henceforth assume the
\mathfrak{M}_{g,k}
always have semi-simple generic conjugacy classes in the sense of Definition
3.1
.

Theorem 3.2
(
[
28
, Theorem 2.1.5]
)

If non-empty,
\mathfrak{M}_{g,k}
is a smooth equidimensional variety of dimension

d=2+(2g+k-2)n^{2}-\sum_{i,j}\mu_{ij}^{2}.

(3.2)

The next fundamental property of the character varieties is a much more difficult result.

Theorem 3.3
(
[
29
, Theorem 1.1.1]
)

If non-empty,
\mathfrak{M}_{g,k}
is a connected variety.

Remark 3.4

In the case of genus
g=0
(a punctured Riemann sphere), the points of
\mathfrak{R}_{0,k}
describe
k
-tuples of matrices in chosen conjugacy classes whose product is the identity. Describing
\mathfrak{R}_{0,k}
(in particular, determining whether it is non-empty) is the
multiplicative Deligne-Simpson problem
:

\mathfrak{R}_{0,k}=\{A_{i}\in C_{i}:A_{1}\cdots A_{k}=\mathbbm{1}_{n}\}.

(3.3)

It is clear now that the variety

(

7  Application to the Trigonometric van Diejen System

In this section, we apply our results to study the trigonometric van Diejen system
[
45
]
. First, recall that the family
e_{\tau}\mathcal{H}_{q,\bm{\tau}}e_{\tau}
provides a non-commutative deformation of the commutative algebra
e_{\tau}\mathcal{H}e_{\tau}\cong\mathcal{Z}
, by which the latter inherits a Poisson bracket. We therefore may, and will, use the isomorphism (
1.3
) to induce a Poisson bracket
\{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\}
on the character variety
\mathcal{M}_{n}
. As an immediate consequence, we have the following result.

Proposition 7.1

()

The coordinates
X_{i},P_{i}
on
\mathcal{M}_{n}
are log-canonical, that is

\{P_{i},X_{j}\}=\delta_{ij}P_{i}X_{j},\qquad\{P_{i},P_{j}\}=\{X_{i},X_{j}\}=0.

()

The duality map
\mathcal{E}
(
5.8
) is a Poisson anti-automorphism, so
\mathcal{E}_{\ast}\{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\}_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}=-\{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\}_{\mathcal{M}_{n}^{\prime}}
.

()

The Poisson bracket on
\mathcal{M}_{n}
is non-degenerate, so
\mathcal{M}_{n}
is symplectic.

Proof.

(i) This follows because the coordinates
X_{i},P_{i}
correspond to the generators of the algebra
\mathbb{C}[\bm{X}^{\pm 1},\bm{P}^{\pm 1}]
, whose Poisson bracket comes from the quantised algebra
\mathbb{C}_{q}[\bm{X}^{\pm 1},\bm{P}^{\pm 1}]
.

(ii) The duality map
\mathcal{E}
is induced by the algebra automorphism
\varepsilon
. It therefore respects the Poisson structure on the spherical subalgebra, with the change of sign for the bracket caused by the fact that
\varepsilon
interchanges
q\leftrightarrow q^{-1}
.

(iii) For the non-degeneracy, we observe in (i) that the bracket is log-canonical and thus non-degenerate in both coordinate charts. Therefore, the bracket is non-degenerate except on a subset of codimension two, and hence non-degenerate globally.
∎

We now work in the first chart
(X_{i},P_{i})
and consider the functions
h_{k}\coloneqq\tr X^{k}
with
X=A_{1}A_{2}
,

h_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}^{k}+X_{i}^{-k}).

(7.1)

It is clear that
\{h_{k},h_{l}\}=0
for all
k,l=1,\dots,n
. Furthermore, we have

\{X_{i},h_{k}\}=0,\qquad\{P_{i},h_{k}\}=kP_{i}(X_{i}^{k}-X_{i}^{-k}).

(7.2)

This means that the Hamiltonian dynamics governed by
h_{k}
is separated in
(\bm{X},\bm{P})
coordinates:

X_{i}(t)=X_{i}(0),\qquad P_{i}(t)=e^{kt(X_{i}^{k}-X_{i}^{-k})}P_{i}(0).

We describe the corresponding dynamics in invariant terms, globally on
\mathcal{M}_{n}
. It is convenient to do so on the representation variety (
3.3
) associated to the conjugacy classes defined by (
1.1
),

\mathfrak{R}_{n}\coloneqq\mathfrak{R}_{0,4}=\{A_{i}\in C_{i}:A_{1}A_{2}A_{3}A_{4}=\mathbbm{1}_{2n}\}.

Recall
\mathcal{M}_{n}=\mathfrak{R}_{n}\mathbin{\mathchoice{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}{/\mkern-5.0mu/}}\GL_{2n}(\mathbb{C})
. Introduce the following
\GL_{2n}(\mathbb{C})
-invariant vector field on
\mathfrak{R}_{n}
:

\dot{A}_{1}=-k(A_{1}X^{k}-X^{k}A_{1}),\qquad\dot{A}_{2}=-k(A_{2}X^{k}-X^{k}A_{2}),\qquad\dot{A}_{3}=0,\qquad\dot{A}_{4}=0.

(7.3)

This can be easily integrated, giving
X=A_{1}A_{2}
constant and

A_{1}(t)=e^{ktX^{k}}A_{1}(0)e^{-ktX^{k}},\quad A_{2}(t)=e^{ktX^{k}}A_{2}(0)e^{-ktX^{k}},\quad A_{3}(t)=A_{3}(0),\quad A_{4}(t)=A_{4}(0).

Proposition 7.2

The Hamiltonian dynamics on
\mathcal{M}_{n}
governed by
h_{k}=\tr X^{k}
can be obtained by projecting the dynamics (
7.3
) onto
\mathcal{M}_{n}
. The dynamics is complete on
\mathcal{M}_{n}
.

Proof.

This can be confirmed by a straightforward calculation in coordinates, using (
7.2
). By analytic continuation, the result is valid globally on
\mathcal{M}_{n}
. Because the dynamics is obviously complete on the auxiliary space
\mathfrak{R}_{n}
, it is complete on
\mathcal{M}_{n}
.
∎

Applying the duality map
\mathcal{E}
, one can interchange the roles of
X=A_{1}A_{2}
and
Y=A_{4}A_{1}
. The latter matrix
Y
has been identified in
[
10
, Corollary 4.4]
as a Lax matrix for the van Diejen system. In particular, its conserved quantities are given by
H_{k}\coloneqq\tr Y^{k}
, so we will take them as Hamiltonians of this system. The corresponding dynamics on
\mathfrak{R}_{n}
takes the form

\dot{A}_{1}=0,\qquad\dot{A}_{2}=-k(Y^{k}A_{2}-A_{2}Y^{k}),\qquad\dot{A}_{3}=-k(Y^{k}A_{3}-A_{3}Y^{k}),\qquad\dot{A}_{4}=0,

(7.4)

which integrates to give
Y=A_{4}A_{1}
constant and

A_{1}(t)=A_{1}(0),\quad A_{2}(t)=e^{-ktY^{k}}A_{2}(0)e^{ktY^{k}},\quad A_{3}(t)=e^{-ktY^{k}}A_{3}(0)e^{ktY^{k}},\quad A_{4}(t)=A_{4}(0).

Theorem 7.3

The Hamiltonian dynamics on
\mathcal{M}_{n}
governed by
H_{k}=\tr Y^{k}
can be obtained by projecting the dynamics (
7.4
) onto
\mathcal{M}_{n}
. The dynamics is complete on
\mathcal{M}_{n}
.

By Lemma
5.2
, the matrix
X(t)=A_{1}(t)A_{2}(t)
has pairwise-reciprocal eigenvalues
x_{i},x_{i}^{-1}
for any
t
, giving the positions of the particles at time
t
.

The second coordinate chart on
\mathcal{M}_{n}
provides the action-angle variables for the van Diejen system. The action variables are determined by the eigenvalues of
Y
, and the angle variables are the dual counterparts of
P_{i}
. Correspondingly, the functions
h_{k}=\tr X^{k}
in terms of these action-angle coordinates assume the form of the van Diejen Hamiltonians with dual parameters. This picture is analogous to the Ruijsenaars duality well-known in the
\GL_{n}
-case
[
42
,
19
,
20
]
, and it is a non-trivial manifestation of the duality for DAHAs. In a special limiting case of the five-parameter van Diejen system, such duality was established in
[
21
,
22
]
.

Remark 7.4

For a two-parameter case
k_{0}=u_{0}=u_{n}=1
, the van Diejen system has been thoroughly studied by Pusztai and Görbe
[
38
]
(cf.
[
3
]
for some results in a one-parameter case). We explain how their results fit into ours: the relations (
3.4
)–(
3.7
) simplify to

\begin{gathered}T=T^{-1},\qquad XT^{-1}=TX^{-1},\qquad YT=T^{-1}Y^{-1},\\
(tYX-t^{-1}XY)T=(k_{n}t^{-1}-k_{n}^{-1}t)\mathbbm{1}_{V}+(t-t^{-1})vw.\end{gathered}

With this,
X(t)
can be rearranged to
X(t)=X(0)e^{kt(Y^{k}-Y^{-k})}
, which matches the formulae in
op. cit.
(cf.
[
38
, (4.113)]
for example) up to the change of notation
X=e^{2\Lambda}
,
Y=L
,
T=C
.

7.1  Interpretation via Hamiltonian Reduction

A character variety of a Riemann surface can be seen as a result of (infinite-dimensional) Hamiltonian reduction, performed on the moduli space of smooth connections
[
1
]
, and so it has a canonical Poisson structure, cf.
[
25
,
26
]
. Fock and Rosly
[
23
]
explained how to obtain the same space by a finite-dimensional reduction, modelling flat connections by combinatorial connections on graphs embedded into the surface. Applying this to the one-punctured torus, they obtained the variety
CM_{\tau}
appearing in Theorem
1.3
and were able to interpret it as a completed phase space for the Ruijsenaars–Schneider system, see
[
23
, Appendix]
(the observation that the Ruijsenaars–Schneider system naturally arises on the moduli space of flat connections on the one-punctured torus goes back to
[
24
]
). Carrying out the same approach for the four-punctured sphere would be technically difficult, but we can fortunately arrive at a similar interpretation without much hard work, thanks to the results at hand. Below, we freely use the terminology and notation from
[
23
]
, so the reader should consult that paper for the details.

a
b
c

Figure 5
:
The graph corresponding to the four-punctured sphere.

Let us consider the space of
G
-valued graph connections on the graph in Figure
5
, representing the sphere with four holes (punctures). It has three edges, to each of which we associate an element of
G
. Hence, the moduli space of graph connections in this case is
\mathcal{A}^{l}=G\times G\times G
. The graph has one vertex, so the gauge group
\mathcal{G}^{l}=G
acts on
\mathcal{A}^{l}
by simultaneous conjugation.

According to
[
23
]
, the choice of a Poisson structure on
\mathcal{A}^{l}
is based on a choice of a classical
r
-matrix. We work with the group
G=\GL_{N}(\mathbb{C})
for
N=2n
and choose

r=\sum_{i<j}^{N}E_{ij}\otimes E_{ji}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}E_{ii}\otimes E_{ii}.

Remark 7.5

We can express
r=r_{a}+t
as the sum of a skew-symmetric and symmetric part, for

r_{a}=\frac{1}{2}(r-r_{21})\qquad\text{and}\qquad t=\frac{1}{2}(r+r_{21}).

This defines a Poisson bivector on
\mathcal{A}^{l}
, by
[
23
, (16)]
, which then induces a Poisson bracket on
\mathcal{A}^{l}/\mathcal{G}^{l}
. By
[
23
, Proposition 5]
, the resulting Poisson manifold is isomorphic to the moduli space
\mathcal{M}
of flat connections on the four-punctured sphere, with the Atiyah–Bott Poisson structure. Symplectic leaves correspond to fixing the conjugacy classes of holonomies around the punctures, so we choose the conjugacy classes
C_{i}
defined by (
1.1
), resulting in our character variety of the Calogero–Moser space
\mathcal{M}_{n}
. In terms of
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},\mathbf{C})\in\mathcal{A}^{l}
, this means imposing the constraints

\mathbf{A}\in C_{1},\qquad\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{B}\in C_{2},\qquad\mathbf{B}^{-1}\mathbf{C}\in C_{3},\qquad\mathbf{C}^{-1}\in C_{4}.

Therefore, a priori, we have two Poisson brackets on
\mathcal{M}_{n}
: the Atiyah–Bott bracket
\{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\}_{\text{AB}}
and the bracket coming from the DAHA
\{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\}_{\text{DAHA}}
. We claim that these brackets are the same. Before the proof, we write the Poisson brackets on
\mathcal{A}^{l}
, which are analogues of
[
23
, (A2)–(A4)]
:

\displaystyle\{\mathbf{A},\mathbf{A}\}

\displaystyle=r_{a}(\mathbf{A}\otimes\mathbf{A})+(\mathbf{A}\otimes\mathbf{A})r_{a}+(1\otimes\mathbf{A})r_{21}(\mathbf{A}\otimes 1)-(\mathbf{A}\otimes 1)r(1\otimes\mathbf{A}),

(7.5)

\displaystyle\{\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}\}

\displaystyle=r(\mathbf{A}\otimes\mathbf{B})-(\mathbf{A}\otimes\mathbf{B})r_{21}+(1\otimes\mathbf{B})r_{21}(\mathbf{A}\otimes 1)-(\mathbf{A}\otimes 1)r(1\otimes\mathbf{B}),

(7.6)

with
\{\mathbf{B},\mathbf{B}\}
,
\{\mathbf{C},\mathbf{C}\}
completely similar to (
7.5
), and
\{\mathbf{A},\mathbf{C}\}
,
\{\mathbf{B},\mathbf{C}\}
completely similar to (
7.6
).

Proposition 7.6

The isomorphism (
1.3
) is a Poisson map, that is it identifies the natural Poisson bracket on the spherical subalgebra with the Atiyah–Bott bracket on the character variety.

Proof.

Working on
\mathcal{A}^{l}
with the Fock–Rosly bracket, we take
\tr\mathbf{B}^{k}
and calculate its brackets with
\mathbf{A}
,
\mathbf{B}
,
\mathbf{C}
. This is similar to the way
[
23
, (A6)–(A8)]
are derived, and the result is

\{\mathbf{A},\tr\mathbf{B}^{k}\}=-k(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}^{k}-\mathbf{B}^{k}\mathbf{A}),\qquad\{\mathbf{B},\tr\mathbf{B}^{k}\}=0,\qquad\{\mathbf{C},\tr\mathbf{B}^{k}\}=0.

(7.7)

Upon the identification
\mathbf{A}=A_{1}
,
\mathbf{B}=A_{1}A_{2}=X
and
\mathbf{C}=A_{4}^{-1}
, the vector field
\{\,\cdot\,,\tr\mathbf{B}^{k}\}
is the same as in (
7.3
). Projecting onto
\mathcal{M}_{n}\cong\mathcal{A}^{l}/\mathcal{G}^{l}
, it becomes clear that
\tr X^{k}
defines the same vector field with respect to each of the Poisson brackets, which is to say

\{\,\cdot\,,\tr X^{k}\}_{\text{AB}}=\{\,\cdot\,,\tr X^{k}\}_{\text{DAHA}}.

Similarly, for
Y=\mathbf{C}^{-1}\mathbf{A}
, we calculate brackets between
\tr Y^{k}
and
\mathbf{A}
,
\mathbf{B}
,
\mathbf{C}
, and notice

\{\,\cdot\,,\tr Y^{k}\}_{\text{AB}}=\{\,\cdot\,,\tr Y^{k}\}_{\text{DAHA}}.

We see now that
\tr X^{k}
and
\tr Y^{k}
are in the kernel of
\{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\}\coloneqq\{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\}_{\text{AB}}-\{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\}_{\text{DAHA}}
. But the
2n
functions
(\tr X^{k},\tr Y^{k})_{k=1,\dots,n}
are functionally independent generically on
\mathcal{M}_{n}
, so
\{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\}\equiv 0
.
∎

Corollary 7.7

The coordinates
X_{i},P_{i}
on
\mathcal{M}_{n}
are log-canonical with respect to the Atiyah–Bott bracket. The spherical subalgebra
e_{\tau}\mathcal{H}_{q,\bm{\tau}}e_{\tau}
provides a quantisation of the character variety
\mathcal{M}_{n}
, equipped with the Atiyah–Bott bracket.

We conclude that the symplectic leaf
\mathcal{M}_{n}
of the moduli space
\mathcal{M}
of flat
\GL_{2n}(\mathbb{C})
-connections on the four-punctured sphere serves as a completed phase space for the van Diejen system.

The variety

Remark 7.9

The interpretation of the van Diejen system within the moduli space of flat connections on a punctured sphere leads to a symplectic action of (a subgroup of) the mapping class group on the family of these systems and corresponding spaces

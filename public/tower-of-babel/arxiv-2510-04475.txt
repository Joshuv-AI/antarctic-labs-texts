On the cardinality of measures of maximal relative entropy for smooth skew products
Matheus M. Castro ∗, Gary Froyland ∗

Abstract
. Let Ω \Omega and M M be compact smooth manifolds and let Θ : Ω × M → Ω × M \Theta:\Omega\times M\to\Omega\times M be a 𝒞 1 + α \mathcal{C}^{1+\alpha} skew-product diffeomorphism over an Axiom A base. We show that Θ \Theta has at most countably many ergodic hyperbolic measures of maximal relative entropy. When dim M = 2 \dim M=2 , if Θ \Theta has positive relative topological entropy, then Θ \Theta has at most countably many ergodic measures of maximal relative entropy.

28D20; 37D25; 37D35; 37H05

1.
Introduction and Main results

For dynamical systems related by a factor map, the natural analogue of maximal entropy is the concept of maximal relative entropy.
Let
(X,f)
and
(Y,g)
be continuous maps on compact metric spaces and let
\pi\colon X\to Y
satisfy
g\circ\pi=\pi\circ f
.
Write
\mathcal{M}(f)
and
\mathcal{M}(g)
for the sets of invariant Borel probability measures of
f
and
g
, respectively.
Given
\nu\in\mathcal{M}(g)
, a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(f)
is a measure of maximal
\nu
-relative entropy if
\pi_{*}\mu=\mu\circ\pi^{-1}=\nu
and

h_{\mu}(f)=\sup\bigl\{\,h_{\eta}(f):\eta\in\mathcal{M}(f),\ \pi_{*}\eta=\nu\,\bigr\}.

Equivalently, if
h_{\nu}(g)<\infty
the Abramov–Rokhlin formula states that
\mu
maximises the conditional entropy

h_{\eta}(f\mid\nu):=h_{\eta}\left(f\mid\pi^{-1}\mathcal{B}_{Y}\right)=h_{\eta}(f)-h_{\nu}(g)

among all
\pi
-lifts
\eta
of
\nu
.
This relative viewpoint is captured by the relativised variational principle of Ledrappier–Walters and in Walters’ compensation function, extending the notion of relative entropy to relative equilibrium states
[
30
,
52
]
.

The general picture of measures of maximal relative entropy and relative equilibrium states is well advanced in symbolic dynamics, particularly for factor maps between shifts where
X
is an irreducible subshift of finite type. Petersen–Quas–Shin proved that for each ergodic base measure
\nu
on
Y
there are only finitely many ergodic measures of maximal relative entropy on
X
, and they established explicit bounds on this number
[
44
]
. These bounds were improved in
[
2
]
, introducing the notion of class degree. A subsequent work extended these advances to relative equilibrium states in
[
1
]
. A structure theorem for infinite-to-one factor codes
\pi\colon X\to Y
between irreducible subshifts of finite type was proved in
[
53
]
: that there exists a sofic shift
Y_{1}
, such that
\pi=\pi_{2}\circ\pi_{1}
. This allowed Yoo to show that if
\nu
is an equilibrium state on
Y
for a sufficiently regular potential, then there is a unique lift on
X
that is a relative equilibrium state
[
53
]
.

Measures of relative maximal entropy and relative equilibrium states naturally arise in random dynamical systems. There, they are usually called quenched relative equilibrium states. Consider a probability-preserving base
(\Omega,\theta,\mathbb{P})
and the skew-product map

\Theta:(\omega,x)\in\Omega\times M\mapsto(\theta(\omega),\,T_{\omega}(x))\in\Omega\times M.

Observe that when taking
X=\Omega\times M
,
Y=\Omega
,
f=\Theta,

g=\theta
, and
\pi(\omega,x)=\mathrm{proj}_{\Omega}(\omega,x)=\omega
we retrieve the setting of the first paragraph under the assumption that
h_{\mathbb{P}}(\theta)<\infty.

Foundational results of Kifer established existence and uniqueness of relative equilibrium states for random uniformly expanding transformations and identified quenched relative pressure with the principal Lyapunov exponent of the random transfer-operator cocycle
[
28
]
.
Bogenschütz developed parallel notions of metric entropy and pressure for random dynamical systems and proved a variational principle, in the framework commonly used nowadays
[
12
]
.
Thermodynamic formalism for random systems has since been developed across a variety of settings: random symbolic dynamics
[
13
,
22
,
20
,
49
,
48
]
; smooth expanding maps
[
28
,
38
]
; non-uniformly smooth expanding maps
[
5
,
50
]
; and non-uniformly hyperbolic random interval maps (closed and open, with discontinuities)
[
8
,
6
,
7
]
; see also the references therein.

Although the quenched relative thermodynamic formalism theory for random dynamical systems is extensive, results for systems generated by diffeomorphisms remain scarce outside the SRB setting (for SRB results, see
[
29
,
11
,
21
,
3
,
36
]
and the references therein).
In particular, except in settings where Gibbs-type bounds are available, there is no general method for bounding the number of measures of relative maximal entropy, let alone the number of relative equilibrium states, in these settings. In this paper, we study how many measures of relative maximal entropy a skew product
\Theta(\omega,x)=(\theta(\omega),T_{\omega}(x))
over an Axiom A base may admit. This question is part of an ongoing program to quantify the number of measures of maximal entropy across classes of systems.

In the setting of smooth diffeomorphisms, Newhouse proved that
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
diffeomorphisms on compact smooth manifolds always admit a measure of maximal entropy
[
39
]
. Buzzi later conjectured that for surface diffeomorphisms with positive topological entropy, there are at most countably many ergodic measures of maximal entropy
[
16
, Conjecture 1]
. Sarig’s groundbreaking symbolic coding for
C^{1+\alpha}
surface diffeomorphisms confirmed this countability and also settled a conjecture of Katok on periodic-orbit growth
[
45
]
. This result was recently sharpened by Buzzi–Crovisier–Sarig, proving that
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
surface diffeomorphisms with positive entropy have only finitely many ergodic measures of maximal entropy, and exactly one in the transitive case,
[
18
]
, which answered another of Buzzi’s conjectures
[
16
, Conjecture 2]
.

In higher dimensions, Ben Ovadia extended Sarig’s coding to non-uniformly hyperbolic diffeomorphisms on arbitrary manifolds, yielding countability results for hyperbolic measures of maximal entropy
[
10
]
. More recently, Buzzi–Crovisier–Sarig established the strong positive recurrence (SPR) property, and via SPR obtained strong statistical properties for the associated invariant measures, including exponential decay of correlations and an almost-sure invariance principle
[
19
]
. Related results are known for two-dimensional fibre skew products over hyperbolic bases; see Marín–Poletti–Veiga
[
37
]
. Analogous results are available for flows
[
35
,
17
,
33
]
and for discontinuous or maps with singularities
[
32
,
31
,
4
,
34
]
.

Our contribution is a relative analogue of these countable cardinality results. For
\mathcal{C}^{1+\alpha}
skew–product diffeomorphisms
\Theta(\omega,x)=(\theta\omega,T_{\omega}(x))
over an Axiom A base
(\Omega,\theta)
and for any ergodic base-invariant measure
\mathbb{P}
, we prove that there are only countably many ergodic hyperbolic
\Theta
-invariant measures of maximal entropy relative to
\mathbb{P}
. In particular, when the fibre
M
is a surface and
h_{\mathrm{top}}(\Theta\mid\nu)>0
, the set of ergodic
\mathbb{P}
-relative measures of maximal entropy is countable. The proof refines the symbolic coding methods of
[
45
,
19
]
to the skew-product setting and combines them with the finiteness techniques for factor maps due to Petersen–Quas–Shin
[
44
]
. We expect the coding-and-factor approach developed here to be useful in random dynamics, and to provide a natural quenched perspective and new tools for constructing and counting relative equilibrium states.

1.1.
Measures of maximal relative entropy

Throughout this paper,
\Omega
and
M
will always denote compact smooth (boundaryless) manifolds. Let
\Theta
be a
\mathcal{C}^{1+\alpha}
,
\alpha>0
, skew-product diffeomorphism

(1)

\displaystyle\Theta:\Omega\times M

\displaystyle\to\Omega\times M

\displaystyle(\omega,x)

\displaystyle\mapsto(\theta\omega,T_{\omega}(x))

where
\theta:\Omega\to\Omega
,
T_{\omega}:M\to M
for each
\omega\in\Omega
. Moreover, we define

\mathrm{proj}_{\Omega}:(\omega,x)\in\Omega\times M\mapsto\omega\in\Omega.

In this paper, all measures are implicitly assumed to be Borel measures.

This paper concerns the amount of
\mathbb{P}
-relative entropy that
\Theta
may admit. Below, we define the metric entropy via the Brin–Katok formula
[
15
]
since it is the shortest way to define it (see also
[
51
, Chapter 4]
). We also define the concept of
\mathbb{P}
-relative entropy for skew products via an extension of the Brin–Katok formula (see
[
55
]
).

Definition 1.1

(Metric entropy)
.

Let
(X,\mathrm{dist})
be a compact metric space,
T:X\to X
continuous, and
\mu
a
T
-invariant probability measure. For
x\in X
,
\varepsilon>0
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define the Bowen ball

B_{n}(x,\varepsilon):=\left\{y\in X;\ \mathrm{dist}(T^{k}(y),T^{k}(x))<\varepsilon\text{ for }0\leq k<n\right\}.

The
metric entropy of
\mu
with respect to
T
is

h_{\mu}(T):=-\int_{X}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mu(B_{n}(x,\varepsilon))\ \mathrm{d}\mu=-\int_{X}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mu(B_{n}(x,\varepsilon))\ d\mu.

Definition 1.2

(Relative metric entropy)
.

Let
\Theta
be as (
1
) and
\mu
be a
\Theta
-invariant probability with
(\mathrm{proj}_{\Omega})_{*}\mu=\mathbb{P}
, and disintegrate
\mu(\mathrm{d}\omega,\mathrm{d}x)=\mu_{\omega}(\mathrm{d}x)\,\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{d}\omega).

For
\omega\in\Omega
,
x\in M
,
\varepsilon>0
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, write

B_{n}^{\omega}(x,\varepsilon):=\left\{y\in M;\ \mathrm{dist}\left(T_{\omega}^{k}(y),T_{\omega}^{k}(x)\right)<\varepsilon\text{ for }0\leq k<n\right\},

where
T_{\omega}^{k}(x):=T_{\theta^{k}\omega}\circ T_{\theta^{k-1}\omega}\circ\cdots\circ T_{\omega}(x).
The
\mathbb{P}
-relative metric entropy of
\mu
with respect to
\Theta
is

\displaystyle h_{\mu}(\Theta\mid\mathbb{P})

\displaystyle:=-\int_{\Omega}\int_{M}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log(\mu_{\omega}(B_{n}^{\omega}(x,\varepsilon)))\,\mu_{\omega}(\mathrm{d}x)\,\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{d}\omega)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\int_{M}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log(\mu_{\omega}(B_{n}^{\omega}(x,\varepsilon)))\,\mu_{\omega}(\mathrm{d}x)\,\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{d}\omega).

We recall that if
h_{\mathbb{P}}(\theta)<\infty
, which will always be the case in this paper, we have that
h_{\mu}(\Theta\mid\mathbb{P})=h_{\mu}(\Theta)-h_{\mathbb{P}}(\theta)
(see
[
25
, Chapter 5, below equation 1.1.5]
).

Definition 1.3

(Measures of maximal
\mathbb{P}
-relative entropy)
.

Let
\Theta
be as (
1
) and fix a
\theta
-invariant probability measure
\mathbb{P}
. We define the
\mathbb{P}
-relative topological entropy
as

h_{\mathrm{top}}(\Theta\mid\mathbb{P}):=\sup\{h_{\nu}(\Theta\mid\mathbb{P});\ (\mathrm{proj}_{\Omega})_{*}\nu=\mathbb{P}\ \text{and}\ \nu\ \text{is a }\Theta\text{-invariant probability measure}\}.

We say that a
\Theta
-invariant measure
\mu
is a
measure of maximal
\mathbb{P}
-relative entropy
if

h_{\mu}(\Theta\mid\mathbb{P})=h_{\mathrm{top}}(\Theta\mid\mathbb{P}).

To formulate our results, we impose additional assumptions on the skew-product base
\theta:\Omega\to\Omega
and the measure
\mathbb{P}
, namely, we assume
\theta
is an Axiom A diffeomorphism and
\mathbb{P}
is an ergodic
\theta
-invariant probability measure.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
\Omega
be a compact smooth manifold and
\theta:\Omega\to\Omega
be
\mathcal{C}^{1}
diffeomorphism. We say that
\theta
is an Axiom A transformation if

(i)

\overline{\{p\in\Omega;\ \text{$p$ is a $\theta$-periodic point}\}}=NW(\theta)
, where

NW(\theta):=\left\{\omega\in\Omega\,\middle|\,\begin{aligned} &\text{for each open set }U\subset\Omega\text{ containing }\omega,\\
&\#\{n\in\mathbb{N};\theta^{n}(U)\cap U\neq\emptyset\}=\#\{n\in\mathbb{N};\ \theta^{-n}(U)\cap U\neq\emptyset\}=\infty\end{aligned}\right\};

and

(ii)

the non-wandering set
NW(\theta)
is a uniformly hyperbolic set, i.e. there exists
\chi>0
and a direct sum
T_{\omega}\Omega=E^{s}(\omega)\oplus E^{u}(\omega)
for all
\omega\in NW(\theta),
and a uniform number
K>0
such that for any
k\geq 0,
and
\omega^{\prime}\in NW(\theta)
,

\mathrm{max}\left(\left\|\left.\mathrm{D}\theta^{k}\right|_{E^{s}(\omega^{\prime})}\right\|,\left\|\left.\mathrm{D}\theta^{-k}\right|_{E^{s}(\omega^{\prime})}\right\|\right)\leq Ke^{-\chi k}.

1.2.
Main results

Below, we state our main results.

Theorem A
.

Let
\Omega
and
M
be compact smooth manifolds, and

\displaystyle\Theta:\Omega\times M

\displaystyle\to\Omega\times M

\displaystyle(\omega,x)

\displaystyle\mapsto(\theta\omega,T_{\omega}(x))

be a
\mathcal{C}^{1+\alpha}
diffeomorphism, for some
\alpha>0
. Assume that
\theta:\Omega\to\Omega
is an Axiom
A
diffeomorphism and
\mathbb{P}
an ergodic probability measure. Then,
\Theta
admits at most countably many ergodic hyperbolic measures of maximal
\mathbb{P}
-relative entropy.

Theorem
A
is proved in Section
5
.

We remark that Theorem
A
is a generalisation of the classical non-skew-product setting. Let
T:M\to M
be a
\mathcal{C}^{1+\alpha}
map,
\theta:\Omega\to\Omega
be any smooth Axiom
A
map, and consider the product map
\Theta(\omega,x)=(\theta(\omega),T(x))
.
Fix a base point
\omega_{p}\in\Omega
with
\theta(\omega_{p})=\omega_{p}
and let
\mathbb{P}=\delta_{\omega_{p}}
. Then any
\Theta
-invariant measure

\mu
satisfying
(\mathrm{proj}_{\Omega})_{*}\mu=\delta_{\omega_{p}}
has the form
\mu=\delta_{\omega_{p}}\otimes\mu^{\prime}
, where
\mu^{\prime}
is
T
-invariant on
M
, and

h_{\mu}\big(\Theta\,\big|\,\delta_{\omega_{p}}\big)=h_{\mu^{\prime}}(T).

Hence, measures of maximal
\mathbb{P}
-relative entropy for
\Theta

correspond exactly to measures of maximal entropy for
T
. In particular,
Theorem
A
yields the usual countability statement for ergodic
hyperbolic measures of maximal entropy in the non–skew-product setting in conformance with
[
10
, Theorem 0.5]
.

We stress that the hyperbolicity assumption in Theorem
A
is essential. Observe that the extension of
\theta
by the identity map
\Theta(\omega,x)=(\theta(\omega),x)
admits uncountably many ergodic measures of maximal
\mathbb{P}
-relative entropy; in this case, all such measures have zero
\mathbb{P}
-relative entropy. A similar example with positive
\mathbb{P}
-relative entropy is

\Theta:\Omega\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\to\Omega\times\mathbb{T}^{3}
with

\Theta(\omega,(x_{1},x_{2},y))=(\theta\omega,(2x_{1}+x_{2},x_{1}+x_{2},y)),

which admits uncountably many ergodic measures of maximal
\mathbb{P}
-relative entropy.

Moreover, for fibre maps in dimension
n\geq 3
, the countability conclusion for hyperbolic measures of maximal entropy in Theorem
A
is sharp, i.e. there exists a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
skew-product diffeomorphism
\Theta:\Omega\times\mathbb{T}^{n}\to\Omega\times\mathbb{T}^{n}
satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem
A
which admits infinitely many ergodic hyperbolic measures of
\mathbb{P}
-relative maximal entropy. As before, take
\mathbb{P}=\delta_{p}
, where
p\in\Omega
is a fixed point of the base dynamics and set
\Theta(\omega,x)=(\theta(\omega),T(x))
, where
T:\mathbb{T}^{n}\to\mathbb{T}^{n}
admits infinitely many hyperbolic measures of maximal entropy. One possible construction of
T
is as follows.

Define

T:(x,s)\in\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\mapsto(A(x),F(s))\in\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1},

where
A\in\mathrm{SL}(n-1,\mathbb{Z})
is an Anosov toral automorphism, and
F\colon\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
is the function

F(s)=s+\delta\,e^{-\left(\frac{1}{s^{2}}+\frac{1}{(1-s)^{2}}\right)}\sin\left(\frac{2\pi}{s}\right)\pmod{1},

where
\delta>0
is taken sufficiently small so that
F
is a diffeomorphism and
F^{\prime}(s)>0
for every
s\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
. One readily verifies that
F\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
and

F\left(\frac{1}{j}\right)=\frac{1}{j}\ \text{and }0<F^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{j}\right)=1-2\delta\pi j^{2}\,e^{-\left(1+\frac{1}{(j-1)^{2}}\right)j^{2}}<1\ \text{for every }j\in\mathbb{N}_{>1}.

Since circle diffeomorphisms satisfy
h_{\mathrm{top}}(F)=0
, we obtain that
h_{\mathrm{top}}(T)=h_{\mathrm{top}}(A)
. If
m
is the (unique) measure of maximal entropy for
A
(in fact, the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}^{n-1}
), then for each
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>1}
the product
m_{j}\coloneqq m\otimes\delta_{1/j}
is ergodic, hyperbolic, and satisfies
h_{m_{j}}(T)=h_{m}(A)=h_{\mathrm{top}}(T)
. Thus
T
admits infinitely many ergodic hyperbolic measures of maximal entropy.

In dimension two, Theorem
A
can be strengthened.

Corollary B
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
A
if we assume that
M
is a compact smooth surface and
h_{\mathrm{top}}(\Theta\mid\mathbb{P})>0
then
\Theta
admits at most countably many measures of maximal
\mathbb{P}
-relative entropy.

Corollary
B
is proved in Section
5
.

In dimension two, it may be possible to strengthen the conclusion of “at most countably many measures of maximal
\mathbb{P}
-relative entropy” of Corollary
B
to “at most finitely many measures of maximal
\mathbb{P}
-relative entropy”, in analogy with the classical non-skew-product case
[
18
, Corollary 1.4]
.
Another natural future direction is to derive a criterion for uniqueness. In
[
18
]
(see also
[
19
]
) it is shown that each homoclinic class supports a unique measure of maximal entropy. In particular, any smooth, transitive surface system with positive entropy has a unique measure of maximal entropy. The analogous statement for measures of relative maximal entropy remains unclear.

It is worth mentioning that, as a consequence of a celebrated result due to Newhouse
[
39
]
,
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
skew products always admit a measure of maximal
\mathbb{P}
-relative entropy.

Proposition 1.5
.

Assume that the skew product
\Theta
is
\mathcal{C}^{\infty},
and let
\mathbb{P}
be any
\theta
-invariant measure. Then
\Theta
admits a
\mathbb{P}
-relative measure of maximal entropy.

Proof.

From
[
39
, Theorem 4.1]
the map

\mu\in\left\{\nu;\ \nu\text{ is a $\Theta$-invariant probability measure}\right\}\mapsto h_{\mu}(\Theta)\in\mathbb{R}

is upper semi-continuous with respect to the weak
∗
topology. Since

\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Theta):=\left\{\nu;\ \nu\text{ is }\Theta\text{-invariant probability measure }\text{ and }\nu(\mathrm{proj}_{\Omega}^{-1}(\cdot))=\mathbb{P}\right\}

is compact in the weak
∗
topology, there exists
\widetilde{\mu}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Theta)
such that

h_{\widetilde{\mu}}(\Theta)=\sup\left\{h_{\nu}(\Theta);\ \nu\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Theta)\right\}.

Since
h_{\nu}(\Theta\mid\mathbb{P})=h_{\nu}(\Theta)-h_{\mathbb{P}}(\theta)
for each
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Theta)
we obtain that
\widetilde{\mu}
is a measure of maximal
\mathbb{P}
-relative entropy.
∎

X
is a topological space and

as the Borel

•

•

\mathcal{M}(T):=\left\{\mu\in\mathcal{M}\left(X\right);\ T_{*}\mu:=\mu(T^{-1}(\cdot))=\mu\ \text{and }\mu\ \text{is a probability measure}\right\};

.

1.3.
An application of Corollary
B

Let
\mathbb{T}^{2}=\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}
be the flat torus. A classical and notoriously challenging dynamical system is the standard map on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
. One convenient version of the standard map is
T:\mathbb{T}^{2}\to\mathbb{T}^{2}
given by

T(x_{1},x_{2})=(2x_{1}-x_{2}+k\cos(2\pi x_{1}),\,x_{1})\pmod{1},\ \text{for some }k\in\mathbb{Z}.

Sinai conjectured that for
k
sufficiently large,
T
has positive metric entropy with respect to Lebesgue measure
[
47
, Page 144]
. This conjecture remains open. In contrast, much more is known about measures of maximal entropy: Obata showed that, for large
k
, the standard map admits a unique measure of maximal entropy; moreover, such a measure has positive metric entropy
[
40
]
. However, less is known in the random setting.
Our results show that there exists at least one and, at most, countably many ergodic relative measures of maximal entropy for certain classes of random perturbations of the standard map.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider the skew product

\displaystyle\Theta_{k}:\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}

\displaystyle(\omega,x)

\displaystyle\mapsto(A\omega,T_{\omega}(x)),

Where
A:\mathbb{T}^{2}\to\mathbb{T}^{2}
is Arnold’s cat map

A(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=(2\omega_{1}+\omega_{2},\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})\pmod{1},

and
T_{\omega}:\mathbb{T}^{2}\to\mathbb{T}^{2}
is a perturbed version of the standard map

T_{\omega}(x_{1},x_{2}):=(2x_{1}-x_{2}+k\cos(2\pi x_{1})+f(\omega),x_{1})\pmod{1},\ \text{for every }\omega\in\Omega,

where
f:\mathbb{T}^{2}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
is a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
function.

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be an ergodic
\theta
-invariant measure. Then, there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, such that for every
k\geq k_{0},
one has
h_{\mathrm{top}}(\Theta_{k}\mid\mathbb{P})>0
. In particular, Proposition
1.5
implies that
\Theta_{k}
admits a measure of maximal
\mathbb{P}
-relative entropy and Corollary
B
ensures that there are only countably many such ergodic measures.

Remark 1.7
.

Examples of choices for
f
are:

•

f(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=\omega_{1}
(large random additive kicks);

•

f(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=\varepsilon\sin(2\pi\omega_{1})
for any
\varepsilon>0
(arbitrarily small random additive kicks); and

•

f(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=2\omega_{1}+\varepsilon\sin(2\pi\omega_{2})
(random kicks of mixed sizes depending on both base coordinates).

Hyperbolic measures and Borel homoclinic classes

In this section, we collect standard facts from hyperbolic dynamics and introduce the concept of Borel homoclinic classes (see
[

). Fix a compact smooth manifold
E
and a
\mathcal{C}^{1+\alpha}
diffeomorphism
\Theta:E\to E
, for some
\alpha>0
. Unless stated otherwise, all statements in this section are made under this standing assumption. For the purposes of this paper, the set

\Omega\times M
as in Theorem
A
. Our exposition closely follows
[

2.1.
Hyperbolic measures

By the Oseledets’ multiplicative ergodic theorem
[
41
]
, there exists a Borel set
E^{\prime}\subset E
such that

•

E^{\prime}
has full measure for every
\Theta
-invariant probability measure; and

•

for every
x\in E^{\prime}
there is a finite set
\sigma(x)\subset\mathbb{R}
and a
\mathrm{D}\Theta
-invariant splitting

T_{x}E=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\sigma(x)}E_{x}^{\lambda},

where each
E_{x}^{\lambda}\neq\{0\}
is a linear subspace defined by

Definition 2.1

Below, we recall a few results of Pesin theory. These definitions will be useful for defining the concept of Borel homoclinic classes in the following section.

Definition 2.2

Theorem 2.3

2.2.
Borel homoclinic classes

Given two submanifolds

in

we denote

to be the set of transverse intersection points, i.e.

S_{1}\pitchfork S_{2}:=\{x\in S_{1}\cap S_{2}:T_{x}S_{1}\oplus T_{x}S_{2}=T_{x}E\}.

Definition 2.6

(
[
19
]
, Homoclinic relation)
.

Two points

are
homoclinically related

(x\sim y)
if
W^{s}(x)
has a transverse intersection point with an iterate of
W^{u}(y)
, and
W^{u}(x)
has a transverse intersection with an iterate of
W^{s}(y)
.

Proposition 2.7

(
[
19
, Proposition 2.6]
)
.

The homoclinic relation

is an equivalence relation on
\mathrm{NUH}^{\prime}(\Theta)
.

Definition 2.8

(
[
19
]
)
.

Borel homoclinic classes
are equivalence classes of
\sim
in
\mathrm{NUH}^{\prime}(\Theta)
.

The proposition below states that the homoclinic classes characterise all the hyperbolic measures.

Proposition 2.9

(
[
19
, Proposition 2.8]
)
.

The following hold:

(i)

The set of Borel homoclinic classes is a finite or countable partition of
\mathrm{NUH}^{\prime}(\Theta)
into invariant Borel sets;

(ii)

each class contains a dense set of hyperbolic periodic points;

(iii)

any hyperbolic ergodic measure
\mu
is carried by a Borel homoclinic class
\mathcal{X}
; and

(iv)

any invariant measure carried by a Borel homoclinic class is hyperbolic.

Remark 2.10
.

Note that any Borel homoclinic class that is not a singleton supports uncountably many ergodic invariant measures. If
\Theta
admits a non-atomic hyperbolic invariant measure (i.e., not supported on a periodic orbit), then
\Theta
admits uncountably many hyperbolic ergodic measures. Indeed, every non-trivial Borel homoclinic class contains at least one horseshoe, and each horseshoe carries uncountably many ergodic measures since it is conjugated to a full shift. By Proposition
2.9
(iv), each of these measures is hyperbolic.

Corollary 4.5

Proof.

Assume that

It follows from (

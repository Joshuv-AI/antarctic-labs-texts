Divisibility of generalized Mersenne numbers
Alex Chan

Abstract




Introduction
.

In this article, I present a theorem determining a criterion for divisibility of two generalized Mersenne numbers, which are repunits of the same length in base-
a^{m}
and base-
a^{k}
. In addition to the general proof, I present an alternative proof for a special case of the theorem.

In this paper
d,m,k,a
are any positive integers,
d>1,a>1
.

There are several interpretations of the term ”generalized Mersenne number”.

In this work, I define generalized Mersenne number
M_{d}(x):=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}x^{j}
.

The author thanks Prof. N. Osipov, A. Kalmynin and Prof. A. Skopenkov for their continued support throughout the different stages of the project.

Theorem
.

The following conditions are equivalent.

(1) The number
M_{d}(a^{m})
is divisible by the number
M_{d}(a^{k})
.

(2)
m
is divisible by
k
and
\gcd(\frac{m}{k};d)=1
.

Proof.

Define

Q:=\frac{M_{d}(a^{m})}{M_{d}(a^{k})}

Proof that
(1)\Leftarrow(2)
.
Suppose that
m
is divisible by
k
and
\gcd{(\frac{m}{k},d)}=1
. Let
n=\frac{m}{k}
so that
\gcd{(n,d)}=1
. The corresponding quotient can be written as

Q=\frac{(a^{md}-1)(a^{k}-1)}{(a^{m}-1)(a^{kd}-1)}=\frac{(b^{nd}-1)(b-1)}{(b^{n}-1)(b^{d}-1)}

where
b=a^{k}\geq 2
. We have
\gcd{(b^{n}-1,b^{d}-1)}=b^{\gcd{(n,d)}}-1=b-1
. Thus,

Q=\frac{b^{nd}-1}{\mathop{\text{}}{\rm lcm}(b^{n}-1,b^{d}-1)}

and
Q
is an integer since
b^{nd}-1
is a common multiple of
b^{n}-1
and
b^{d}-1
.

Proof that
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
.
Suppose the quotient

Q=\frac{(a^{md}-1)(a^{k}-1)}{(a^{m}-1)(a^{kd}-1)}

to be an integer. We now prove that
m
is divisible by
k
and that
\gcd(\frac{m}{k};d)=1
separately.

Proof that (1)
\Rightarrow

m
is divisible by
k
.
Using the so-called
Zsigmondy’s theorem
(see
[
Bi
, Theorem V]
or the original Zsigmondy’s paper
[
Zs
]
), show that
m
is divisible by
k
.

Case when
kd=2
.
If
kd=2
then
k=1
and the claim is trivially true.

Case when
kd=6
and
a=2
.
If
kd=6
and
a=2
, then either
k=1
(then
m
is divisible by
k
) or
(k,d)\in\{(2,3),(3,2)\}
. In the case
(k,d)=(2,3)
the quotient

Q=\frac{2^{2m}+2^{m}+1}{21}

is an integer only for
m=2n
with
n
not divisible by
3
. In the case
(k,d)=(3,2)
the quotient

Q=\frac{2^{m}+1}{9}

is an integer only for
m=3n
with
n
odd. Consequently, in both cases
m
is divisible by
k
.

All other cases.
In remaining cases, by Zsigmondy’s theorem there exists a prime divisor
p
of
a^{kd}-1
such that
\mathop{\text{}}{\rm ord}_{p}{(a)}=kd
. Due to
kd>k
we conclude that
a^{k}\not\equiv 1\pmod{p}
. Since
Q
is an integer, we obtain
a^{md}\equiv 1\pmod{p}
. Hence
md
is divisible by
kd
or, equivalently,
m
is divisible by
k
.

Proof that (1)
\Rightarrow

\gcd(\frac{m}{k};d)=1
.
Letting
n=\frac{m}{k}
again, we get

Q=\frac{M_{d}(b^{n})}{M_{d}(b)}

with
b=a^{k}\geq 2
. Assuming
Q
to be an integer, we need to prove that
\gcd{(n,d)}=1
.

Let
\gcd{(n,d)}=l
and
n_{1}=n/l
,
d_{1}=d/l
. Then

M_{d}(b^{n})\equiv lM_{d_{1}}(b^{l})\pmod{M_{d}(b)}

(1)

as we will see later. Also, we have
M_{d}(b)=M_{d_{1}}(b^{l})M_{l}(b)
so that

0<lM_{d_{1}}(b^{l})=\frac{l}{M_{l}(b)}M_{d}(b)<M_{d}(b)

for all
b\geq 2
and
l>1
. Thus,
M_{d}(b^{n})
cannot be divisible by
M_{d}(b)
for
l>1
. Therefore,
l=1
.

It remains to prove (1). We use the following well-known fact: for any
N
divisible by
d
, the congruence
b^{N}\equiv 1\pmod{M_{d}(b)}
holds. We have

M_{d}(b^{n})=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}b^{ni}=\sum_{q=0}^{l-1}\sum_{r=0}^{d_{1}-1}b^{ln_{1}(d_{1}q+r)}\equiv\sum_{q=0}^{l-1}\sum_{r=0}^{d_{1}-1}b^{ln_{1}r}=l\sum_{r=0}^{d_{1}-1}b^{ln_{1}r}=\\

=l\sum_{r=0}^{d_{1}-1}b^{l(j_{r}+d_{1}q_{r})}\equiv l\sum_{r=0}^{d_{1}-1}b^{lj_{r}}=l\sum_{r=0}^{d_{1}-1}b^{lr}=lM_{d_{1}}(b^{l})\pmod{M_{d}(b)}.

Here
n_{1}r=j_{r}+d_{1}q_{r}
for some integers
j_{r},q_{r}
(
0\leq j_{r}<d_{1}
) such that

\{j_{0},j_{1},\dots,j_{d_{1}-1}\}=\{0,1,\dots,d_{1}-1\}

due to
\gcd{(n_{1},d_{1})}=1
.

In the ring
\mathbb{Z}[x]
, the congruence (1) rewritten as

M_{d}(x^{n})\equiv lM_{d_{1}}(x^{l})\pmod{M_{d}(x)}

holds too (and follows the same proof).
∎

Remark
.

1.
M_{d}(x^{m})
is divisible by
M_{d}(x^{k})
in the ring of polynomials
\mathbb{Z}[x]
if and only if
m
,
k
,
d
satisfy the same condition. The proof of the claim “if” almost exactly follows one given in part I. At the same time, the proof of the claim “only if” is simplified by considering the complex roots of polynomials with their multiplicities instead of Zsigmondy’s theorem.

2. Proof that (1)
\Rightarrow

\gcd(\frac{m}{k};d)=1
can be performed via Zsigmondy’s theorem and the well-known LTE-lemma (see
[
LTE
]
). Rewrite
Q=M_{d}(b^{n})/M_{d}(b)
in the form

Q=\frac{(b^{nd}-1)(b-1)}{(b^{n}-1)(b^{d}-1)}.

Assume
Q
to be an integer while
\gcd{(n,d)}>1
. Let
q
be a common prime divisor of
n
and
d
. Let
n_{1}=n/q
and
d_{1}=d/q
.

Case when
q\geq 3
.
By Zsigmondy’s theorem, there is a prime divisor
p
of
b^{q}-1
such that
\mathop{\text{}}{\rm ord}_{p}{(b)}=q
. Then
b\not\equiv 1\pmod{p}
. As a corollary we get
p\neq q
and
p\neq 2
. Let
c=b^{q}
. We have

\nu_{p}((b^{n}-1)(b^{d}-1))=\nu_{p}((c^{n_{1}}-1)(c^{d_{1}}-1))=

=\nu_{p}(c^{n_{1}}-1)+\nu_{p}(c^{d_{1}}-1)=2\nu_{p}(c-1)+\nu_{p}(n_{1})+\nu_{p}(d_{1}).

while

\nu_{p}((b^{nd}-1)(b-1))=\nu_{p}(b^{nd}-1)=\nu_{p}(c^{qn_{1}d_{1}}-1)=

=\nu_{p}(c-1)+\nu_{p}(qn_{1}d_{1})=\nu_{p}(c-1)+\nu_{p}(n_{1})+\nu_{p}(d_{1}).

Thus,
\nu_{p}((b^{n}-1)(b^{d}-1))>\nu_{p}((b^{nd}-1)(b-1))
and
Q
cannot be an integer. Contradiction.

Case when
q=2
and
b+1\neq 2^{t}
.
In this case
b+1
has a prime divisor
p\neq 2
. Clearly,
b\not\equiv 1\pmod{p}
. We can get a contradiction in the same way as above.

Case when
q=2
and
b=2^{t}-1
where
t\geqslant 2
.
Here, we have
n=2n_{1}
,
d=2d_{1}
. Then

\nu_{2}(b^{n}-1)=\nu_{2}(b^{2}-1)-1+\nu_{2}(n)=t+\nu_{2}(n)=t+1+\nu_{2}(n_{1})

and similarly

\nu_{2}(b^{d}-1)=t+1+\nu_{2}(d_{1}),\quad\nu_{2}(b^{nd}-1)=t+2+\nu_{2}(n_{1})+\nu_{2}(d_{1}).

Thus, we obtain

\nu_{2}((b^{n}-1)(b^{d}-1))=2t+2+\nu_{2}(n_{1})+\nu_{2}(d_{1})>

>t+3+\nu_{2}(n_{1})+\nu_{2}(d_{1})=\nu_{2}((b^{nd}-1)(b-1))

and
Q
cannot be an integer again. Contradiction.

Hence,
\gcd(n,d)=1
.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
from an anonymous reviewer
.

Proof that (1)
\Rightarrow

m
is divisible by
k
.
Suppose that the quotient

Q^{\prime}=\frac{(a^{md}-1)(a^{k}-1)}{a^{kd}-1}

is an integer. Then
m
must be divisible by
k
. Indeed, let

\displaystyle D=\gcd{(a^{md}-1,a^{kd}-1)}=a^{d\gcd{(m,k)}}-1,

\displaystyle a^{md}-1=DA^{\prime},\quad a^{kd}-1=DA^{\prime\prime}

for some
A^{\prime}
and
A^{\prime\prime}
such that
\gcd{(A^{\prime},A^{\prime\prime})}=1
. Then
DA^{\prime}(a^{k}-1)=DA^{\prime\prime}Q^{\prime}
. Hence,

A^{\prime}(a^{k}-1)=A^{\prime\prime}Q^{\prime}.

Then
A^{\prime}(a^{k}-1)
is divisible by
A^{\prime\prime}
.
Hence
A^{\prime\prime}
is a divisor of
a^{k}-1
because
\gcd{(A^{\prime},A^{\prime\prime})}=1
.
Thus,
DA^{\prime\prime}
is a divisor of
D(a^{k}-1)
, i.e.,
a^{kd}-1
is a divisor of
(a^{d\gcd{(m,k)}}-1)(a^{k}-1)
. In particular,

a^{kd}-1\leqslant(a^{d\gcd{(m,k)}}-1)(a^{k}-1)<a^{d\gcd{(m,k)}+k}-1

that yields
kd<d\gcd{(m,k)}+k
or

\frac{k}{\gcd{(m,k)}}<\frac{d}{d-1}\leqslant 2

(recall that
d\geqslant 2
). Then
k=\gcd{(m,k)}
so that
m
is divisible by
k
.

Proof that (1)
\Rightarrow

\gcd(\frac{m}{k};d)=1
.
Let
n=m/k
and assume the number

\frac{(b^{nd}-1)(b-1)}{(b^{n}-1)(b^{d}-1)}=\frac{b^{nd}-1}{b^{n}-1}:\frac{b^{d}-1}{b-1}=\frac{b^{nd}-1}{b^{d}-1}:\frac{b^{n}-1}{b-1}

to be an integer (where
b=a^{k}\geqslant 2
). Let us introduce

M:=\frac{b^{\gcd{(n,d)}}-1}{b-1}.

Clearly, we have

\frac{b^{d}-1}{b-1}\equiv\frac{b^{n}-1}{b-1}\equiv 0\pmod{M}.

As a corollary of our assumption, we get

\displaystyle 0\equiv\frac{b^{nd}-1}{b^{n}-1}=1+b^{n}+\ldots+b^{n(d-1)}\equiv d\pmod{M},

\displaystyle 0\equiv\frac{b^{nd}-1}{b^{d}-1}=1+b^{d}+\ldots+b^{d(n-1)}\equiv n\pmod{M}.

Therefore,
\gcd{(n,d)}
is divisible by
M
. In the case
\gcd{(n,d)}>1
we obtain

\gcd{(n,d)}<2^{\gcd{(n,d)}}-1\leqslant M

which is a contradiction. Thus,
\gcd{(n,d)}=1
as desired.

Alternative proof that
(1)\Rightarrow m
is divisible by
k
.

Define
g=\mathop{\text{}}{\rm ord}_{M_{d}(a^{k})}{(a)}
.
We now prove that
g=kd
. Indeed,
a^{kd}-1
is divisible by
M_{d}(a^{k})
, so
kd
is divisible by
g
. On the other hand,
M_{d}(a^{k})>a^{k(d-1)}
, so
g>k(d-1)
. Thus,
g=kd
. Hence, (1)
\Rightarrow a^{md}-1
is divisible by
M_{d}(a^{k})\Rightarrow m
is divisible by
k
.

Special case of the theorem
.

Proposition.

The following conditions are equivalent.

(1) The number
M_{d}(n^{m})
is divisible by the number
M_{d}(n^{k})
for any integer
n
.

(2)
m
is divisible by
k
and
\gcd(\frac{m}{k};d)=1
.

This result could be known.
I learned it from
[
Sy
]
.
I. Syukrin presented an idea of a technical proof using canonical decomposition of numbers.
Here I present a simple proof using roots of polynomials.

(3)
The polynomial
M_{d}(x^{m})
is divisible in
\mathbb{Z}[x]
by the polynomial
M_{d}(x^{k})
.

Equivalence of (3) and (1) follows from
[
Sk
, Problem 3.4.2.b]
(there the assumption that
P
does not have roots is superfluous).

We now prove that both (3) and (2) are equivalent to the following statement.

(4)
The polynomial
(x^{md}-1)(x^{k}-1)
is divisible in
\mathbb{Z}[x]
by the polynomial
(x^{m}-1)(x^{kd}-1)
.

We prove the equivalences

(2)\Leftrightarrow(4)\Leftrightarrow(3).

Equivalence of (3) and (4) is obvious.

Proof that
(2)\Rightarrow(4)
.

This statement follows the same proof as the proof that (1)
\Leftarrow
(2) in the Theorem.

Proof that
(2)\Leftarrow(4)
.

Since
(x^{md}-1)(x^{k}-1)
is divisible in
\mathbb{Z}[x]
by the polynomial
(x^{m}-1)(x^{kd}-1)
and the value of
(x^{m}-1)(x^{kd}-1)
for
x_{1}:=e^{2\pi i/kd}
is 0,
e^{2\pi i/kd}
is a root of both the numerator and denominator.
Thus,
(x_{1}^{md}-1)=0
, because
(x_{1}^{k}-1)\not=0
.
Hence,
md
is divisible by
kd
.
So,
m
is divisible by
k
.

If
\gcd(u,d)>1
, then
g(y):=(y^{u}-1)(y^{d}-1)
has multiple roots other than 1, while
f(y):=(y^{ud}-1)(y-1)
has no such roots. Thus,
f(y)
is not divisible by
g(y)
in
\mathbb{Z}[y]
. Then, by the following well-known assertion,
f(x^{k})
is not divisible by
g(x^{k})
.
Hence,
\gcd(u,d)=1
.

So (2) holds.

Assertion.

Let
k
be an integer and and
f(y),g(y)\in\mathbb{Z}[y]
. If
f(x^{k})
is divisible by
g(x^{k})
in
\mathbb{Z}[x]
, then
f(y)
is divisible by
g(y)
in
\mathbb{Z}[y]
.

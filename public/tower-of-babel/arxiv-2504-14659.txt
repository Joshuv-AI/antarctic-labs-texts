Markovian Continuity of the MMSE
Elad Domanovitz, Anatoly Khina

Abstract
Minimum mean square error (MMSE) estimation is widely used in signal processing, information theory, and related fields. Despite its practical robustness, the MMSE can be discontinuous under standard notions of stochastic convergence. To bridge this gap, we review classical counterexamples to the continuity of the MMSE and identify two obstructions: failure of second-moment convergence and approximating observations carrying more information about the limit estimand than the limit observation does. Motivated by practical acquisition mechanisms, we study MMSE continuity under two natural constraints: (1) continuity of the second moment, and (2) a degradedness (Markov) restriction ensuring that each approximating observation is no more informative than the limit observation is about the limit estimand. Under these conditions, we establish continuity of the MMSE and of the MMSE estimator. We provide complementary semicontinuity results and continuity guarantees in related settings and establish continuity under linear estimation. We further extend the analysis to the families of Bregman divergences and continuous metric and generalized metric cost functions, including the Kullback–Leibler and Jensen–Shannon divergences as special cases.

Introduction

Minimum mean square error (MMSE) estimation is a cornerstone principle at the core of many fields, including signal processing, information theory, communications, control theory, and machine learning. By seeking the estimator that minimizes the expected squared error between an unknown quantity and its estimate, MMSE provides a clean and powerful optimality criterion that is both mathematically tractable and practically meaningful as the minimizer of the mean power of the estimation error

In the context of information theory, MMSE is intimately related to entropy, differential entropy, mutual information, channel capacity and rate–distortion function (RDF).
For a discrete random variable (RV)

As the required statistics are acquired from finite samples and finite-precision/noisy measurements, continuity of the MMSE and the corresponding estimators is implicitly assumed.

Unfortunately, despite being considered robust in practice, the MMSE is known to be discontinuous in general. To wit, consider a sequence of pairs of RVs
\left\{\left(X_{n},Y_{n}\right)\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converging in some standard stochastic sense (in distribution, in probability, in mean square, almost surely) to a pair of RVs
\left(X,Y\right)

Here,
\left(X_{n},Y_{n}\right)
can represent, e.g., an empirical distribution resulting from a finite sample of size
n

\left(X,Y\right)

\left\{\mathrm{MMSE}\left(X_{n}\middle|Y_{n}\right)\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
does not converge in general to
\mathrm{MMSE}\left(X\middle|Y\right)

\displaystyle\mathrm{MMSE}\left(X_{n}\middle|Y_{n}\right)\nlongrightarrow\mathrm{MMSE}\left(X\middle|Y\right),

X

\left\{\left(X_{n},Y_{n}\right)\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

In this work, we establish new continuity results for the MMSE, which subsume the aforementioned results. To that end, we first review existing counterexamples to and guarantees of the continuity of the MMSE in Section
II
.
We identify common traits in these counterexamples: For a sequence of pairs of RVs
\left\{\left(X_{n},Y_{n}\right)\right\}
that converge to
(X,Y)
in probability,
(X_{n},Y_{n})\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{p}(X,Y)
, either

•

the second moment (mean power) of
X_{n}
does not converge to that of
X
, viz.
\mathrm{E}\left[X^{2}_{n}\right]\nlongrightarrow\mathrm{E}\left[X^{2}\right]
,

or

•

the MMSE in estimating
X
from

is strictly better than the MMSE of estimating
X
from
Y
.

We argue that such behavior is uncharacteristic of real-world applications and suggest adding two additional requirements:

1.

continuity of the second moment:

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{E}\left[X^{2}_{n}\right]=\mathrm{E}\left[X^{2}\right];

(7)

2.

a Markovian restriction

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle\mathrel{-\mkern-6.0mu\circ\mkern-6.0mu-}Y\mathrel{-\mkern-6.0mu\circ\mkern-6.0mu-}Y_{n}

(8)

for all
n
, depicted in Figure
2
. This restriction ensures that the channel from
X
to
Y_{n}
is stochastically degraded with respect to the channel from

to
Y
, so

is (Blackwell-)less informative about

than

We prove in Section

that, under these two restrictions, the MMSE is
Markov continuous
in probability. This also establishes MMSE continuity in probability for the additive-noises and machine-precision examples.
As a step in this proof, we prove that the MMSE is upper semicontinuous (u.s.c.) in distribution as long as the second moment of
X_{n}
converges to that of
X
(requirement
1
above).
We further establish MMSE u.s.c. in distribution for the case of a common parameter
X=X_{n}
with finite second moment, and its continuity
if, in addition, the induced channel (conditional distribution) from
X
to
Y_{n}
is stochastically degraded with respect to the channel from
X
to
Y
.

In Section
IV
, we supplement the above results by proving that the MMSE under linear estimation (LMMSE) is continuous in distribution as long as the second moments of
\left\{X_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
and of
\left\{Y_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converge to those of
X
and
Y
, respectively, provided the covariance matrix of

is invertible.

In Section

, we generalize the results of Section

from the squared Euclidean distance cost inherent to the MMSE to Bregman divergence cost functions and continuous metric cost functions. Note that the Kullback–Leibler divergence, denoted by

, is an instance of a Bregman divergence. Further, the Jensen–Shannon divergence

, defined as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{JS}}\left(P\middle Q\right)}\triangleq{}&\frac{1}{2}{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}\left(P\middle\frac{P+Q}{2}\right)}+\frac{1}{2}{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}\left(Q\middle\frac{P+Q}{2}\right)},\end{aligned}

We establish all the results in this work in the more general framework of RVec parameters and measurements. A summary of the main results is available in Table

We start by recalling known counterexamples that demonstrate it.

We first demonstrate that, even when the measurements are independent of the parameter (or absent), the MMSE, which then reduces to the variance, might not be continuous.

Example II.1
.

Let
Y_{n}=Y
be a standard Gaussian RV for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Set
X=0
and

\displaystyle X_{n}=\begin{cases}\sqrt{n},&\mathrm{w.p.}\ \frac{1}{2n}\\
-\sqrt{n},&\mathrm{w.p.}\ \frac{1}{2n}\\
0,&\mathrm{w.p.}\ 1-\frac{1}{n},\end{cases}

(12)

independent of
Y
.
Clearly,
\left(X_{n},Y_{n}\right)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{d}\left(X,Y\right)=\left(0,Y\right)
, but

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\left X_{n}-X\right_{L^{2}}=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\left X_{n}\right_{L^{2}}=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}1=1,

(13)

meaning that
X_{n}\mathrel{{\mathop{\nlongrightarrow}\limits}}X
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{E}\left[X_{n}^{2}\right]=1>0=\mathrm{E}\left[X^{2}\right].

(14)

Now, since
X_{n}\upvDash Y_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{MMSE}\left(X_{n}\middle|Y_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\left X_{n}\right_{L^{2}}^{2}=1>0=\mathrm{MMSE}\left(X\middle|Y\right)

(15)

meaning that the MMSE is not continuous in this case:

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{MMSE}\left(X_{n}\middle|Y_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=1>0=\mathrm{MMSE}\left(X\middle|Y\right).

(16)

The latter further shows that, in this example, the MMSE is lower semicontinuous (l.s.c.) but not u.s.c.

Even when

Let

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{E}\left[Y_{n}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{E}\left[X_{n}^{2}\right]+\mathrm{E}\left[N^{2}\right]

(23)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{E}\left[X^{2}\right]+\mathrm{E}\left[N^{2}\right]=\mathrm{E}\left[Y^{2}\right].

Hence

\left(X_{n},Y_{n}\right)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{m.s.}\left(X,Y\right)

\displaystyle\mathrm{MMSE}\left(X\middle|Y\right)=\mathrm{E}\left[\left(\frac{X-N}{2}\right)^{2}\right]=\frac{1}{2}.

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{MMSE}\left(X_{n}\middle|Y_{n}\right)=0<1/2=\mathrm{MMSE}\left(X\middle|Y\right).

Again, the latter shows that the MMSE is u.s.c. but not l.s.c. in this example.

IV
Continuity of the LMMSE

In this section, we treat the continuity in distribution of the LMMSE which is defined and characterized next

[

Definition IV.1

(LMMSE)
.

The MMSE achieved by linear (affine) estimation, the LMMSE, of an RVec
X\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
from an RVec
Y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}

such that

\left X\right_{L^{2}},\left Y\right_{L^{2}}<\infty
,
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathrm{LMMSE}\left(X\middle|Y\right)\triangleq\inf\left X-\left(AY+b\right)\right_{L^{2}}^{2},

(54)

where the infimum is over all deterministic vectors
b\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
and deterministic matrices
A\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times m}
.

Theorem IV.1

(LMMSE and LMMSE estimator)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be two RVecs such that
\left X\right_{L^{2}},\left Y\right_{L^{2}}<\infty
.
Denote

\displaystyle\eta_{X}

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathrm{E}\left[X\right],

\displaystyle C_{X}

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathrm{E}\left[\left(X-\eta_{X}\right)\left(X-\eta_{X}\right)^{T}\right],

(55)

\displaystyle\eta_{Y}

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathrm{E}\left[Y\right],

\displaystyle C_{Y}

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathrm{E}\left[\left(Y-\eta_{Y}\right)\left(Y-\eta_{Y}\right)^{T}\right],

(56)

\displaystyle C_{X,Y}

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathrm{E}\left[\left(X-\eta_{X}\right)\left(Y-\eta_{Y}\right)^{T}\right],

(57)

and assume that
C_{Y}
is invertible.
5
5

5

If
C_{Y}
is not invertible, this means that the entries of
Y-\mathrm{E}\left[Y\right]
are linearly dependent a.s. Hence, to attain the LMMSE estimator, one may remove all the linearly dependent entries and estimate from the remaining entries without loss of performance.

Then, the LMMSE estimate
\hat{X}
of
X
from
Y
is given by

\displaystyle\hat{X}=\eta_{X}+C_{X,Y}C_{Y}^{-1}\left(Y-\eta_{Y}\right),

(58)

i.e.,

and

in Definition

.
The corresponding LMMSE is given as

\displaystyle\mathrm{LMMSE}\left(X\middle|Y\right)

\displaystyle=\left X\right_{L^{2}}^{2}-\left\hat{X}\right_{L^{2}}^{2}

(59a)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{trace}\left\{C_{X}-C_{X,Y}C_{Y}^{-1}C_{X,Y}^{T}\right\}.

(59b)

Theorem
IV.1
suggests that the LMMSE is continuous if the means, variances, and covariances are continuous. The following theorem, whose proof is available in Appendix

Theorem IV.2

(LMMSE continuity in distribution)
.

Let
(X,Y)
be a pair of RVecs and let
\left\{\left(X_{n},Y_{n}\right)\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

\left(X_{n},Y_{n}\right)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{d}(X,Y)\,

2.

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{E}\left[X_{n}^{2}\right]=\mathrm{E}\left[X^{2}\right]<\infty

.
3

3.

C_{Y}
is invertible.

Then,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{LMMSE}\left(X_{n}\middle|Y_{n}\right)=\mathrm{LMMSE}\left(X\middle|Y\right).

(60)

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{\mathrm{opt}}\left(X\middle|Y\right)=\inf_{g:\ \mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{\hat{X}}}\mathrm{E}\left[c\left(X,g\left(Y\right)\right)\right]

(68)

where the infimum is taken over all measurable functions

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{\mathrm{opt}}\left(X\middle|Y\right)=\mathrm{E}\left[\phi\left(X\right)\right]-\mathrm{E}\left[\phi\left(\mathrm{E}\left[X\middle|Y\right]\right)\right].

(73)

We are now ready to prove Theorem

Proof:

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{E}\left[\phi\left(X_{n}\right)\right]-\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{E}\left[\phi\left(\hat{X}_{n}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{E}\left[\phi\left(X\right)\right]-\mathrm{E}\left[\phi\left(\hat{X}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\bar{c}_{\mathrm{opt}}\left(X\middle|Y\right),

(77)

where

and requirement 3 together with the continuous mapping theorem (Theorem

) and Vitali’s convergence theorem (Theorem

For Kullback–Leibler divergence, the same Markov continuity conclusion holds on the closed simplex. Direct calculation shows that the conditional mean is optimal and that (

V-B

Continuous Metric and Generalized-Metric Costs

We next derive a similar result for continuous metric cost functions which are not necessarily bounded.
This family of cost functions includes the Minkowski and Chebyshev distances—
c\left(y_{1},y_{2}\right)=\left y_{1}-y_{2}\right_{r}
for
r\in[1,\infty]
, as well as
c\left(y_{1},y_{2}\right)=\left y_{1}-y_{2}\right_{r}^{r}
for
r\in(0,1)
.
For consistency with the rest of the paper, we focus on the case of finite-dimensional Euclidean spaces:
\mathcal{X},\hat{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{k}
and
\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Definition V.3

(continuous metric cost)
.

A continuous metric cost function
c:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
over a closed subset
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{k}

is a cost
c
satisfying, for all
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in\mathcal{X}
:

•

Continuity:
The function
c
is jointly continuous on
\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}
(with respect to the Euclidean topology):

\lim\limits_{\left(\tilde{x}_{1},\tilde{x}_{2}\right)\to\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)}c\left(\tilde{x}_{1},\tilde{x}_{2}\right)=c\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right).

•

Positivity:

c\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)\geq 0
with equality if and only if (iff)
x_{1}=x_{2}
.

•

Symmetry:

c\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)=c\left(x_{2},x_{1}\right)
.

•

Triangle inequality:

c\left(x_{1},x_{3}\right)\leq c\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)+c\left(x_{2},x_{3}\right)
.

Remark V.3
.

In all the results to follow pertaining to continuous metric costs, the positivity property of Definition
V.3
can be relaxed to

c\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)\geq 0
with equality if (but not necessarily only if)
x_{1}=x_{2}
. That is, all the results extend to continuous
pseudo
metric costs.

The following theorem is proved in Appendix
F
.

Theorem V.4

(u.s.c. in distribution of minimal mean continuous metric cost)
.

Let
(X,Y)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
be a pair of RVecs, and let
\left\{\left(X_{n},Y_{n}\right)\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of pairs of RVecs over
\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
such that

\displaystyle\left(X_{n},Y_{n}\right)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{d}(X,Y),

(78)

where
\mathcal{X}
is a closed convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
and
\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Let
c:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous metric cost function (as in Definition
V.3
), such that there exists
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{X}
for which

•

\mathrm{E}\left[c\left(X_{n},\bar{x}\right)\right],\mathrm{E}\left[c\left(X,\bar{x}\right)\right]<\infty
for all

VI
Discussion and Future Work

This work focused on bridging the gap between the practitioners’ perception that MMSE is robust and theoretical results showing discontinuity in general.

By introducing a Markov restriction (
8
)
between the nominal parameter, the nominal measurement, and the converging measurement, we proved that MMSE is in fact continuous assuming continuity of the second moment. Such a restriction may be of interest beyond MMSE estimation, e.g., in other inference problems.

We further established results on the upper semicontinuity in distribution and
continuity for MMSE in estimating a finite-power parameter from a converging sequence of channels under an
individual
statistical degradedness assumption of each converging channel with respect to the limit channel.

We also proved that the MMSE under linear estimation is continuous in distribution assuming again continuity of the second moments, provided the covariance matrix of
Y
is invertible.

Finally, we extended the results to continuous metric costs.

It would be interesting to explore under what other conditions the MMSE and other mean costs are continuous, as well as extend the results to a dynamical setting
[
59
,
60
,
61
]
.

The MMSE can be used as a building block for constructing measures of statistical dependence. For geometry-oriented measures of dependence, stochastic continuity was put forward by Móri and Székely
[
39
]
as an axiom that such a dependence measure needs to satisfy. Since MMSE is not continuous in probability,
L^{p}
(for
p\geq 1
) or a.s., it was claimed that MMSE-based dependence measures are ill-fitted as measures of dependence. Our work proves that, in fact, if the stochastic continuity axiom of Móri and Székely
[
39
]
is modified to require Markov continuity, then MMSE-based dependence measures
[
62
,
63
]
may be good measures of dependence.

The present work concentrated on the Bayesian setting where the estimated parameter(s)
X
and
\left\{X_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
are RVecs.
That said, the Markov condition
X\mathrel{-\mkern-6.0mu\circ\mkern-6.0mu-}Y\mathrel{-\mkern-6.0mu\circ\mkern-6.0mu-}Y_{n}
was imposed with respect to the limit parameter
X
only. Developing an analogous setting under the frequentist framework and establishing continuity results with respect to the class of probability distributions is an interesting research direction to explore.

The following notion of

We define this notion in terms of RVecs rather than in the more common terms of conditional distributions

\displaystyle\left(X_{1},Y_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\left(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}_{1}\right),

(102a)

\displaystyle\left(X_{2},Y_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\left(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}_{2}\right),

(102b)

\displaystyle\tilde{X}

\displaystyle\mathrel{-\mkern-6.0mu\circ\mkern-6.0mu-}\tilde{Y}_{1}\mathrel{-\mkern-6.0mu\circ\mkern-6.0mu-}\tilde{Y}_{2}.

(102c)

This definition means that we can view these two pairs as two channels with the same input
\tilde{X}
where the channel to the first output

is more informative than that to the second output

but not the joint distribution of the quadruple, we can specialize Definition

to the following.

Definition A.5
.

Let

be three RVecs. We will say that, given
X
,
Y_{2}
is
stochastically degraded
or
garbled
with respect to

if there exists an RVec
\tilde{Y}_{1}
such that:

\displaystyle\left(X,\tilde{Y}_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\left(X,Y_{1}\right),

(103a)

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle\mathrel{-\mkern-6.0mu\circ\mkern-6.0mu-}\tilde{Y}_{1}\mathrel{-\mkern-6.0mu\circ\mkern-6.0mu-}Y_{2}.

(103b)

We denote this by
X\mathrel{{\mathop{\mathrel{-\mkern-6.0mu\circ\mkern-6.0mu-}}\limits}}Y_{1}\mathrel{{\mathop{\mathrel{-\mkern-6.0mu\circ\mkern-6.0mu-}}\limits}}Y_{2}

[

, Chapter 7.3.1]
for MMSEs. Its proof is an adaptation of
[

.

Lemma A.4
.

Let

\displaystyle\mathrm{MMSE}\left(X\middle|Y_{1}\right)\leq\mathrm{MMSE}\left(X\middle|Y_{2}\right),

with equality iff

\mathrm{E}\left[X\middle|Y_{1}\right]\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\mathrm{E}\left[X\middle|Y_{2}\right]
.

Proof:

By Definition
A.5
, there exists an RVec
\tilde{Y}_{1}
such that
\left(X,\tilde{Y}_{1}\right)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\left(X,Y_{1}\right)
and

\displaystyle\mathrm{MMSE}\left(X\middle|Y_{2}\right)-\mathrm{MMSE}\left(X\middle|Y_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\mathrm{MMSE}\left(X\middle|Y_{2}\right)-\mathrm{MMSE}\left(X\middle|\tilde{Y}_{1}\right)

(105a)

\displaystyle\quad=\left\mathrm{E}\left[X\middle|\tilde{Y}_{1}\right]\right_{L^{2}}^{2}-\left\mathrm{E}\left[X\middle|Y_{2}\right]\right_{L^{2}}^{2}

(105b)

\displaystyle\quad=\left\mathrm{E}\left[X\middle|\tilde{Y}_{1}\right]-\mathrm{E}\left[X\middle|Y_{2}\right]\right_{L^{2}}^{2}

(105c)

\displaystyle\quad\geq 0,

(105d)

with equality in (

) iff
\mathrm{E}\left[X\middle|\tilde{Y}_{1}\right]=\mathrm{E}\left[X\middle|Y_{2}\right]

a.s. Further, since
\left(X,\tilde{Y}_{1}\right)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\left(X,Y_{1}\right)
, equality holds only if

\mathrm{E}\left[X\middle|Y_{1}\right]\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\mathrm{E}\left[X\middle|Y_{2}\right]

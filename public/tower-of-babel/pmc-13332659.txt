Valvular Heart Disease
Authors: Craig Basman, Ana Paula Tagliari, Karl Poon
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Corresponding Author

Valvular heart disease is entering an era of unprecedented progress, driven by the rapid expansion of transcatheter therapies, the refinement of multimodality imaging, and an increasingly robust evidence base that continues to redefine the optimal timing and strategy of intervention. As the field evolves, so does the complexity of the clinical scenarios encountered in daily practice, often extending beyond standard algorithms and requiring highly individualized solutions.

We welcome readers to this mini-focus issue of JACC: Case Reports, which brings together a curated collection of cases that highlight the breadth, nuance, and clinical complexity of valvular heart disease as encountered in contemporary practice—from the outpatient clinic to the catheterization laboratory and the operating room—capturing the expanding boundaries of transcatheter and surgical innovation while underscoring the central role of clinical judgment in its management.

The cases presented in this issue review a range of issues in valvular heart disease, encompassing transcatheter interventions in complex congenital anatomies, advances in intraprocedural imaging, evolving strategies for infective endocarditis, optimization of transcatheter aortic valve replacement timing and technique, rare postprocedural complications, and the challenges posed by congenital heart disease with coexisting valvular pathology. They delineate a field that is simultaneously expanding its therapeutic boundaries while confronting the complexity that such expansion inevitably generates.

This issue also pays homage to the 10-year anniversary since the birth of electrosurgery with the first case of LAMPOON performed in 2016. Born of necessity to address unmet clinical needs, off-label, off-the-shelf innovative solutions started with the original LAMPOON, subsequently BASILICA, followed by UNICORN and a cacophony of newer strategies and inventive acronyms over the past 10 years. Primarily designed to mitigate catastrophic complications such as coronary obstruction and left ventricular outflow tract obstruction, the field of electrosurgery has evolved rapidly all over the globe and with proper support and training, it has become embedded in clinical practice. This issue highlights the ongoing passion and ingenuity of electrosurgery stalwarts further refining techniques in complex scenarios, combining multiple techniques such as BABICORN and ROBIN, LLAMACORN, and innovative concept as ATLAS and electrosurgery in mitral valve-in-valve procedure. These cases demonstrate the importance of preprocedural planning and intraprocedural adaptability and hopefully will prepare the reader well for the next decades of more complex structural procedures such as redo-transcatheter aortic valve, preserving future coronary access, and lifetime management for the second or even third valve procedure.

The patients described herein are not statistical outliers to be excluded from consideration; rather, they represent the leading edge of the population that contemporary structural heart teams are increasingly called on to treat. Whether early in training or established in the field, readers will find in this collection opportunities to refine clinical reasoning, challenge assumptions, and deepen procedural insight.

Navigating these complex out-of-guideline scenarios requires careful clinical decision-making, advanced multimodality imaging, and close multidisciplinary collaboration—domains in which the art of medicine intersects with its scientific foundation. In this setting, case reports play an essential role in capturing and contextualizing such experience, ensuring that insights gained at the bedside are disseminated broadly across the cardiovascular community.

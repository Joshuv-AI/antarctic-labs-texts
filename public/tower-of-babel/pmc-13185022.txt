Implementation of a clinical decision support tool for postpartum depression: protocol for a prospective randomised clinical trial
Authors: Rochelle Joly, Travis J Gossey, Anna K Daoud, Yiye Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPerinatal mood and anxiety disorders affect more than one in five pregnant individuals. Despite the large percentage of individuals impacted by mood disorders, they continue to remain underdiagnosed and undertreated. Recent interventions such as risk prediction modelling offer opportunities to predict patients at risk prior to symptom onset. Leveraging technology platforms such as electronic health records allow for timely diagnosis and intervention. To date, there are no established clinical decision support (CDS) tools. We hypothesise that subjects who use a CDS aid will find them acceptable, appropriate and feasible.Methods and analysisWe will conduct a pilot randomised clinical trial at three Weill Cornell Medicine clinics with randomisation at the clinician level. Patients in the control arm will receive usual care, while those in the intervention arm will receive CDS. The primary implementation outcomes are the acceptability, appropriateness and feasibility. The effectiveness outcomes are mental health service utilisation, EuroQol 5 Dimensions questionnaire (EQ-5D), Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale and social support measured by the perceived support scale. We hypothesise that stakeholders will find the CDS acceptable, and the intervention arm will have lower mental health service needs compared with control.Ethics and disseminationThis study has been approved by the Weill Cornell Medicine Internal Review Board. Introduction

Postpartum depression (PPD) is a potentially life-threatening mental health condition with adverse maternal and infant health outcomes.1 It is estimated that approximately 20% of birthing parents, out of the four million annual births in the USA, experience PPD.2 Yet, underdiagnosis and undertreatment of PPD occur in over half of the cases, making PPD prevention before symptoms arise a crucial task.3 4 Targets in PPD prevention include risk detection and intervention by clinicians, and social support for patients.5 While PPD can be screened by administering tools such as the Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale (EPDS), screening is not universally performed due to limited clinical resources and lack of awareness of PPD by non-psychiatric care providers.6 Moreover, it is known that current screening tools fail to detect risk early or individuals who conceal symptoms due to stigma or shame.7 Even when PPD risks are identified, the lack of individualised interventions hampers timely interventions and impedes access to appropriate care. Because of these systemic gaps, patients are often marginalised within the healthcare system, resorting to self-management without proper clinical guidance.5

Recent reviews of PPD prevention studies show that targeting at-risk patients based on data on risk factors may improve prevention effectiveness and suggest digital solutions as promising platforms.3 8 We propose to study a clinical decision support (CDS) tool in the electronic health records (EHRs). This approach targets (1) clinician barriers in risk identification by providing automated risk identification within the EHR and (2) patients’ lack of support by providing social support modulated by health professionals. This CDS is based on our preliminary work on digital health that demonstrated success in identifying PPD risk using information from EHR and social determinants of health (SDoH).912 The CDS is integrated within the EHR in the study site health system and uses a PPD risk prediction model to automatically capture a risk profile of PPD for each patient during the ambulatory prenatal visits. If PPD risk is detected, the CDS will alert the obstetrics clinicians to screen for PPD risk and suggest interventions in a clinical pathway.

Methods and analysis

This will be a prospective randomised trial to study the effect of a CDS tool for preventing the development of PPD. This CDS tool is integrated into the EHR and uses a PPD risk prediction model that is built into the EHR11 to automatically identify at-risk patients during prenatal visits. This prompts clinicians with screening and will suggest interventions and guidance for management.

The CDS applies to patients who are (1) 18 or older and being seen in the participating clinics for a clinically recognised pregnancy in their first or second trimesters, (2) English speaking and (3) have access and can view the internet. Obstetrics clinicians seeing pregnant patients are eligible to participate if they manage patients in the three clinics as a board-certified obstetrician gynaecologist (OBGYN) clinician. No exclusion criteria apply for clinicians based on their knowledge and experience in treating PPD.

We will conduct the study at clinics at Weill Cornell Medicine (WCM), one of which is composed of commercially insured patients, and the other with government funded; and one in the Lower Manhattan Hospital, a community-hospital affiliated with WCM, that is composed of commercially insured patients of mixed ethnicity. The study will launch in April 2026. We will recruit clinicians at the study site through monthly OBGYN clinical leadership governance meetings and predictive models working group. Clinicians will work with the study team to recruit among their own patient pool. Physicians will be randomised to typical care or the intervention arm using the CDS tool.

We will collect descriptive statistics of the patient population via EHR and request clinicians to complete a 5-min survey prior to use and participate in semi-structured 30-min interview after adequate use of the tool, to assess the acceptability, feasibility and appropriateness outcomes.

Intervention

The CDS will alert clinicians only if patients have a high risk of developing PPD and provide clinicians with a risk score, risk factors and anticipatory actions with an order set to assist with ordering as shown in figure 1. Clinicians will make the ultimate clinical judgement after receiving CDS aid, including taking no actions towards PPD prevention. Clinicians in the intervention arm may consider referring patients based on the CDS.

Clinicians assigned to the control arm will continue to deliver standard prenatal care without exposure to the CDS tool. Usual care includes screening for depression using the EPDS according to existing clinic workflows, clinician-initiated referrals to behavioural health and nutrition services when indicated, and routine documentation practices and dissemination of patient education material as they see fit. No automated PPD risk predictions, alerts or decision support recommendations are provided in the control condition, and no standard EHR functionality is modified or restricted.

Randomisation

Randomisation in this study occurs at the clinician level, not at the patient level. Each clinician is assigned to either the intervention (CDS) arm or the usual-care control arm. Because patients receive care from a clinician assigned to a particular arm, this design constitutes a cluster randomised trial, with clinicians serving as the clusters. Intervention arm staff will be fully resourced to support clinical actions if patient concerns arise.

Study timeline

The study on user/clinician implementation of the CDS tool will take 3 months, and the data analysis will take up to 24 months (12 months prior and after). The interview will take 30 min, and each survey will take under 5 min. For patient’s EHR, we will review 1 year prior to pregnancy through 1 year after pregnancy. Study roll-out will begin April 2026 and proceed for 12 months. At the conclusion of the study, at 12-month time interval, all collections of surveys/interviews will stop. The research team will continue to process data retrieved from chart review over a 12-month period following the study end date through April 2028.

Outcomes and analysis

Primary outcomes

Primary outcomes are focused on implementation outcomes. Implementation outcomes are feasibility, acceptability and appropriateness, as defined by the UTAUT, IAM and FIM, respectively.13 IAM and FIM are four-item measures to assess the appropriateness and feasibility of the intervention, respectively, with excellent usability. Secondary outcomes

Effectiveness of the CDS tool will be measured as a secondary outcome. The effectiveness outcome is the number of mental health service utilisation. Mental health service utilisation includes presentations to ED or inpatient admission for mental health-related reasons.

Statistical analysis

Sample size

This is a pilot study to measure implementation outcomes as reported by clinicians. We will measure the CDS applied on at least 160 patients to accommodate the implementation outcome measurements.

As this is a pilot trial focused on implementation, formal power calculation based on patient outcomes was used. However, the sample size was selected to ensure adequate representation across clinician clusters and sites, and to allow stable estimation within mixed-effects models that account for clustering.

We will collect descriptive statistics of the patient population. We will track the usage and activity and collect EHR measures such as the number of times PPD preventive actions were initiated. We will use two-sample t-test or Mann–Whitney U test as appropriate to the data distribution to compare the outcome measurements between the intervention and control arms. We will also analyse the changes, if any, between the multiple measurements of outcomes over time. Because patients are nested within clinicians, all analyses that include patient-level outcomes will adjust for clustering using mixed-effects models with random intercepts for clinician. For the primary implementation outcomes, which are measured at the clinician level, clustering does not apply. For secondary effectiveness outcomes, clinician-level clustering will be incorporated to avoid underestimating variance. For outcomes with repeated measurements (eg, EPDS scores, behavioural health encounters), we will additionally include a patient-level random intercept and, when appropriate, an autoregressive (AR1) covariance structure to account for within-patient correlation over time.

A mixed effects model will be used to study the association between patient sociodemographic information, clinical characteristics, intervention characteristics (the number of logins, the number of discussions they participated, etc) and the study outcomes. In the case the residual diagnostic fails or the model diagnostic shows that the random effects are not normally distributed, we will use the generalised estimation equation for the analysis, with same independent and dependent variables.

All models will include fixed effects for study arm, time, where relevant, and clinic site. Intraclass correlation coefficients will be estimated to quantify clustering. Model assumptions will be evaluated using standard residual diagnostics and sensitivity analyses with random slopes where applicable. Missing data will be assessed for randomness and addressed using multiple imputation when appropriate.

Because clinician behaviour may influence the effectiveness of the CDS tool, we will assess implementation fidelity using EHR-generated audit logs. Fidelity measures will include (1) alert opening and viewing, (2) interaction time and (3) completion of recommended actions such as EPDS screening, behavioural health referrals or patient education. These data will allow us to quantify clinician engagement with the CDS.

Fidelity metrics will be summarised descriptively and incorporated into secondary analyses to explore the relationship between adherence and patient-level outcomes. Sensitivity analyses will compare high-engagement and low-engagement clinicians. The primary analysis remains intention-to-treat at the clinician level, consistent with the cluster-randomised design.

In our study, we will account for bias by applying a fairness-through-blindness debiasing approach—validated in our prior work14—which improved both performance and equity across sites, using a threshold optimised for sensitivity, decision curve analysis and clinical appropriateness.

Missing data will be assessed for patterns and mechanisms (eg, missing completely at random, missing at random or not at random). If data are missing at random, we will use multiple imputation techniques to preserve statistical power and reduce bias. Sensitivity analyses will be conducted to assess the robustness of findings to missing data assumptions.

Patients and members of the public were not involved in the design of this study, the development of the CDS tool or the formulation of research questions or outcome measures. Given the retrospective and implementation-focused nature of this protocol, no direct patient involvement is planned for conduct, analysis or dissemination. Results will be shared with clinical stakeholders and institutional collaborators upon study completion.

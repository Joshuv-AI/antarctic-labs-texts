Energy approximation for some double phase functionals
Menita Carozza

Abstract
In the present manuscript we give a structure condition that allows for energy approximation in the vectorial case and we provide two examples, enjoying such a condition.

\int\limits_{\{|u|>k\}}f(x,Du_{k}(x))dx=\int\limits_{\{|u|>k\}}f\left(x,\frac{k}{|u(x)|}[D_{i}u^{\alpha}(x)-\frac{u^{\alpha}(x)}{|u(x)|}\sum\limits_{\beta=1}^{N}\frac{u^{\beta}(x)}{|u(x)|}D_{i}u^{\beta}(x))\right)dx.

If we have Uhlenbeck structure

Since

Proof of Theorem

f(y^{*},z)\leq\text{essential infimum}\{f(y,z):y\in B(x,\epsilon)\}=f^{-}_{x,\epsilon}(z)

0\leq f(y^{*},z)\leq(f^{-}_{x,\epsilon})^{**}(z).

Now we use (F2) with

and we get

f(x,z)\leq K_{1}f(y^{*},z)+K_{2}|x-y^{*}|^{\sigma}|z|^{q}+K_{3}\leq K_{1}(f^{-}_{x,\epsilon})^{**}(z)+K_{2}\epsilon^{\sigma}|z|^{q}+K_{3},

(2.2)

where we used (
2.1
) and

. Note that the right hand side of
(

) has to be verified only when

|z|^{\alpha}+(f^{-}_{x,\epsilon})^{**}(z)\leq L\epsilon^{-n}

|z|^{\alpha}\leq L\epsilon^{-n}

|z|^{p}\leq L\epsilon^{-n}.

Now we can write

\epsilon^{\sigma}|z|^{q}=\epsilon^{\sigma}|z|^{p}|z|^{q-p}\leq\epsilon^{\sigma}|z|^{p}((L\epsilon^{-n})^{\frac{1}{p}})^{q-p}=L^{\frac{q-p}{p}}\epsilon^{\sigma-\frac{(q-p)n}{p}}|z|^{p}.

\sigma-\frac{(q-p)n}{p}\geq 0

\epsilon^{\sigma-\frac{(q-p)n}{p}}\leq(\epsilon^{*})^{\sigma-\frac{(q-p)n}{p}}\leq 1.

We have shown that

\epsilon^{\sigma}|z|^{q}\leq L^{\frac{q-p}{p}}|z|^{p}.

We insert this estimate into (

) and we get

f(x,z)\leq K_{1}(f^{-}_{x,\epsilon})^{**}(z)+K_{2}L^{\frac{q-p}{p}}|z|^{p}+K_{3}.

(2.3)

This means that (

) holds true with

,
\theta(\epsilon)=1
,

,
b(x)=K_{3}
and any
\epsilon^{*}\in(0,1)
, provided
\overline{B(x,\epsilon)}\subset\Omega
.

.

, from the previous inequality we can deduce that

\forall\varepsilon\in(0,1\wedge(R-\rho))

(3.2)

Let us choose
\tilde{x}=y^{*}\in\overline{B(x,\varepsilon)}
given by assumption (F4), and, for every fixed
x,\varepsilon
, let us consider the function

H(z)=f(y^{*},z).

Then, we can rewrite (

) as follows

0\leq f(x,Du_{\varepsilon}(x))\leq c_{3}f(\tilde{x},Du_{\varepsilon}(x))+K_{3}=c_{3}f(y^{*},Du_{\varepsilon}(x))+K_{3}=c_{3}H(Du_{\varepsilon}(x))+K_{3}.

(3.3)

Assumption (F3) says that
H
is convex, so, by Jensen inequality,

\displaystyle H(Du_{\varepsilon}(x))=

\displaystyle H\biggm(\int_{B(x,\varepsilon)}Du(y)\phi_{\varepsilon}(x-y)dy\biggm)\leq\int_{B(x,\varepsilon)}H(Du(y))\phi_{\varepsilon}(x-y)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B(x,\varepsilon)}f(y^{*},Du(y))\phi_{\varepsilon}(x-y)dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B(x,\varepsilon)}f(y,Du(y))\phi_{\varepsilon}(x-y)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B(x,\varepsilon)}h(y)\phi_{\varepsilon}(x-y)dy=h_{\varepsilon}(x)

(3.4)

where we have also used the minimality of
y^{*}
from (F4), and
we have set

. Note that

.
From (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) we have, for every
x\in B_{\rho}
and for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1\wedge(R-\rho))
,

0\leq f(x,Du_{\varepsilon}(x))\leq c_{3}h_{\varepsilon}(x)+K_{3}.

Moreover,

Du_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow Du\,\,{\rm strongly\,in}\,\,L^{p}(B_{\rho}),

h_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow h\,\,{\rm strongly\,in}\,\,L^{1}(B_{\rho}),

\int_{B_{\rho}}h_{\varepsilon}dx\rightarrow\int_{B_{\rho}}hdx.

Note that mollification gives pointwise convergence at Lebesgue points, so

Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\rightarrow Du(x)\,\quad{\rm and}\quad\,h_{\varepsilon}(x)\rightarrow h(x)\quad{\rm a.e.\quad in}\quad B_{\rho}.

Convexity assumption (F3) implies the continuity of
z\rightarrow f(x,z)
and, thanks to pointwise convergence a.e., we get

f(x,Du_{\varepsilon}(x))\rightarrow f(x,Du(x)),\quad{\rm a.e.\quad in}\quad B_{\rho}.

The Dominated Convergence Theorem implies

\int_{B_{\rho}}f(x,Du_{\varepsilon}(x))dx\rightarrow\int_{B_{\rho}}f(x,Du(x))dx

as claimed. This ends the proof of Theorem

\nu M(x,\beta z)\leq f(x,z)=|z|^{p}+g(x_{1},z_{n}^{1})=|t|^{p}.

If we consider the matrix
\tilde{z}=-\beta z
we have

\tilde{z}_{n}^{1}=-\beta t>0\quad{\rm and}\qquad\tilde{z}_{j}^{\alpha}=0\qquad{\rm otherwise,}

then,

|\tilde{z}|=\beta|t|\qquad{\rm and}\qquad f(x,\tilde{z})=f(x,-\beta z)=\beta^{p}|t|^{p}+g(x_{1},-\beta t)=\beta^{p}|t|^{p}+(-\beta t)^{q}-(x_{1})^{q},

provided
0<x_{1}<-\beta t
. If we take
x_{1}=\min\{\frac{1}{2};-\beta\frac{t}{2}\}
then
0<x_{1}\leq-\beta\frac{t}{2}<-\beta t
and we are done. Moreover,

f(x,-\beta z)\leq LM(x,-\beta z)+L=LM(x,\beta z)+L\leq L\frac{|t|^{p}}{\nu}+L

where we used also the symmetry of
M
with respect to the second variable. Therefore we get

\beta^{p}|t|^{p}+\beta^{q}|t|^{q}-\biggm(\min\{\frac{1}{2};-\beta\frac{t}{2}\}\biggm)^{q}=f(x,-\beta z)\leq L\bigg(\frac{|t|^{p}}{\nu}+1\bigg).

We observe that
\min\{\frac{1}{2};-\beta\frac{t}{2}\}=\frac{1}{2}
as
t\to-\infty
, so from the last inequality we get

\beta^{p}+\beta^{q}|t|^{q-p}-\frac{1}{2^{q}|t|^{p}}\leq\frac{L}{\nu}+\frac{L}{|t|^{p}}

that is false if
|t|\rightarrow+\infty
since
q>p
.

Finally, we prove that we cannot use the result in
[
5
]
.
Indeed the structure assumption (
1.12
) cannot apply. Indeed, arguing by contradiction, let us first consider the matrix
z
such that

z_{1}^{1}=t>0\quad{\rm and}\qquad z_{j}^{\alpha}=0\qquad{\rm otherwise,}

then,

f(x,z)=|z|^{p}=t^{p}\qquad{\rm and}\qquad\nu_{1}\bigg(t^{\tilde{p}}+\tilde{a}(x)t^{\tilde{q}}\bigg)\leq t^{p}

therefore, as
t\rightarrow+\infty
,

\displaystyle{\rm if}\quad\tilde{a}(x)=0

\displaystyle\qquad{\rm then}\qquad\tilde{p}\leq p

\displaystyle{\rm if}\quad\tilde{a}(x)>0

\displaystyle\qquad{\rm then}\qquad\tilde{q}\leq p.

(4.6)

If we fix
x_{1}
and consider the matrix
\tilde{z}
such that

\tilde{z}_{n}^{1}=t>|x_{1}|\quad{\rm and}\qquad\tilde{z}_{j}^{\alpha}=0\qquad{\rm otherwise,}

we get
g(x_{1},z_{n}^{1})=t^{q}-(\max\{x_{1};0\})^{q}
;
then,

|\tilde{z}|=t\qquad{\rm and}\qquad f(x,\tilde{z})=t^{p}+t^{q}-\biggm(\max\{x_{1};0\}\biggm)^{q}\leq\nu_{2}\bigg(t^{\tilde{p}}+\tilde{a}(x)t^{\tilde{q}}\bigg).

Therefore, as
t\rightarrow+\infty
, if
\tilde{a}(x)=0
then
q\leq\tilde{p}
that is impossible since by (
4.6
) we get
\tilde{p}\leq p<q
; on the other hand, if
\tilde{a}(x)>0
, then
q\leq\tilde{q}
that is impossible since by (
4.6
) we get
\tilde{q}\leq p<q
.

Let us also note that
\frac{\partial g}{\partial t}(x_{1},x_{1})
does not exist when
x_{1}>0
, so, assumption 1.5 in
[
23
]
fails.

5
Energy approximation and absence of Lavrentiev phenomenon

We are going to show that absence of Lavrentiev phenomenon implies energy approximation. More precisely,
let us consider the integral functional (
1.1
), where

\Omega
is an open ball
B
,

x\to f(x,z)\geq 0
is measurable and

z\to f(x,z)\quad\text{ is strictly convex.}

(5.1)

Moreover, there exist constants
\tilde{c}>0
and
p>1
such that

\tilde{c}(|z|^{p}-1)\leq f(x,z).

(5.2)

We assume that
u\in W^{1,p}(B;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, with
\mathcal{F}(u;B)<+\infty
, is a minimizer, that is,

\mathcal{F}(u;B)=\inf_{v\in u+W^{1,p}_{0}(B;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\mathcal{F}(v;B).

(5.3)

Let us take
Y
to be a subset of
W^{1,p}(B;\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Energy approximation means that

\exists\{u_{k}\}\subset Y:\quad u_{k}\to u\text{ weakly in }W^{1,p}(B;\mathbb{R}^{N})\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{F}(u_{k};B)\to\mathcal{F}(u;B).

(5.4)

On the other hand, absence of Lavrentiev phenomenon means that

\inf_{v\in u+W^{1,p}_{0}(B;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\mathcal{F}(v;B)=\inf_{v\in(u+W^{1,p}_{0}(B;\mathbb{R}^{N}))\cap Y}\mathcal{F}(v;B).

(5.5)

For the minimizer
u
, absence of Lavrentiev phenomenon implies energy approximation, that is, we have the following

Theorem 5.1
.

Under assumptions (
5.1
), (
5.2
) and (
5.3
), we have

\eqref{no_Lavrentiev_sec4}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\eqref{energy_approximation_sec4}

(5.6)

Proof.

We use minimality (
5.3
) and absence of Lavrentiev phenomenon (
5.5
) in order to get

\mathcal{F}(u;B)=\inf_{v\in(u+W^{1,p}_{0}(B;\mathbb{R}^{N}))\cap Y}\mathcal{F}(v;B).

Then, there exists a sequence
\{v_{k}\}\subset(u+W^{1,p}_{0}(B;\mathbb{R}^{N}))\cap Y
such that

\mathcal{F}(v_{k};B)\to\mathcal{F}(u;B).

(5.7)

Now we need to prove the convergence of
v_{k}
to
u
. To this aim, we use
convergence (
5.7
) and we get
k_{1}
such that

\mathcal{F}(v_{k};B)\leq\mathcal{F}(u;B)+1,

for every
k\geq k_{1}
. On the other hand,

p
coercivity (
5.2
) implies

\tilde{c}\int_{B}(|Dv_{k}|^{p}-1)dx\leq\mathcal{F}(v_{k};B).

These two inequalities merge into

\tilde{c}\int_{B}(|Dv_{k}|^{p}-1)dx\leq\mathcal{F}(u;B)+1.

Such an inequality says that
\{v_{k}-u\}
is bounded in
W^{1,p}_{0}(B;\mathbb{R}^{N}))
; since
p>1
, there exists a limit function
w_{0}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(B;\mathbb{R}^{N}))
and a subsequence

\{v_{h_{k}}-u\}
such that

v_{h_{k}}-u\to w_{0}\text{ weakly in }W^{1,p}_{0}(B;\mathbb{R}^{N}),

v_{h_{k}}\to u+w_{0}\text{ weakly in }W^{1,p}(B;\mathbb{R}^{N}),

(5.8)

Dv_{h_{k}}\to D(u+w_{0})\text{ weakly in }L^{p}(B;\mathbb{R}^{N}).

(5.9)

Convexity (
5.1
) implies lower semicontinuity with respect to weak convergence of gradients, so, (
5.9
) and (
5.7
) give

\mathcal{F}(u+w_{0};B)\leq\liminf\mathcal{F}(v_{h_{k}};B)=\lim\mathcal{F}(v_{k};B)=\mathcal{F}(u;B).

Since
u
is a minimizer and
u+w_{0}\in u+W^{1,p}_{0}(B;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then
u+w_{0}
is a minimizer too. Strict convexity (
5.1
) implies uniqueness of minimizer, so,
w_{0}=0
and weak convergence (
5.8
) tells us that

v_{h_{k}}\to u\text{ weakly in }W^{1,p}(B;\mathbb{R}^{N}).

We take
u_{k}=v_{h_{k}}
: this ends the proof.
∎

Remark 2.

The last argument in the previous proof requires that all the
v_{h_{k}}
have the same boundary value
u
.
When proving regularity of minimizers, energy approximation is an important tool but we do not need the same boundary value for all the approximating functions
u_{k}
. This makes things easier, as we can see in the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Remark 3.

Let us note that energy approximation can also be obtained when the density
f(x,z)
can be approximated from below by
f_{k}(x,z)
that increases with respect to
k
. In this case, the approximating functions
u_{k}
are obtained by minimization of
v\to\int_{B}f_{k}(x,Dv(x))dx
. Then, regularity of
u_{k}
is linked to general regularity of minimizers: this requires additional assumptions, see
[
18
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
9
]
.

Remark 4.

Let us also mention that, in
[
8
]
, for the scalar case
N=1
, the authors first approximate
u
by means of bounded
u_{k}
, then they use mollification for
u_{k}
. In the vectorial case
N\geq 2
, approximation by bounded functions is not easy, unless we are dealing with Uhlenbeck structure
f(x,z)=\tilde{f}(x,|z|)
, see section 7 in
[
8
]
; see also
[
27
]
.

Seasonality affects key physiological pathways in Pontastacus leptodactylus during an Aphanomyces astaci epidemic outbreak
Authors: Maria V. Alvanou, Konstantinos Feidantsis, Ioannis Georgoulis, Apostolos P. Apostolidis, Basile Michaelidis, Ioannis A. Giantsis
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Freshwater crayfish Pontastacus leptodactylus is an indigenous European species of considerable ecological and commercial significance. However, it is highly susceptible to the oomycete Aphanomyces astaci, the causative agent of crayfish plague. Although its importance is widely recognized, little information exists on how seasonality affects the physiology and welfare of this species. The present study was designed to (i) investigate seasonal effects on the physiological responses of P. leptodactylus in relation to abiotic factors and crayfish plague outbreak, and (ii) identify the most suitable period for restocking in temperate regions. To achieve this, we characterized for the first time the mRNA expression of hsp70, hsp90, hif-1α, ubiquitin, and gapdh (as endogenous control gene), alongside protein levels of Hsp70, Hsp90, hydroxylated Hif-1α, ubiquitin conjugates, cleaved caspases, Bax/Bcl-2 ratio, and interleukin-6 (IL-6). Our findings revealed that cold stress triggered both the heat shock response and apoptosis. Interestingly, despite the activation of apoptosis, which generally suppresses inflammation, IL-6 levels increased, suggesting a possible association with A. astaci infection. Moreover, elevated Hif-1α levels indicated hypoxic stress during warmer months, likely linked to reduced oxygen availability and turbidity. Overall results suggest that crayfish plague epidemics may intensify with rising temperatures, whereas winter appears to be the optimal period for restocking. Interestingly, in addition, freshwater crayfish in temperate latitudes are organisms that face both cold and heat stress seasonally in the same ecosystem.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-26804-5.

Introduction

Crayfish possess key roles in ecosystem services, since they are characterized as bioturbators1, or as ecosystem engineers2. More specifically, they represent the majority of large and long-lived invertebrates in many freshwater ecosystems3 where they act as ecosystem “scavengers” and operating as shredders, detritivores and predators at the same time3,4. Alterations in crayfish populations can affect whole ecosystem processes and organisms’ complexes (i.e. food chain)5. Setting aside their ecological significance, over one third of crayfish species are close to extinction worldwide6–8. The latter can be attributed to the fact that crayfish, among all aquatic organisms, are constantly exposed to abiotic parameters’ fluctuations in their habitat. These seasonal variations affect animal physiology, immunity and behavior9–11. It has been shown that changes in abiotic parameters, such as temperature, pH, turbidity, oxygen etc., have been correlated with diseases outbreaks and high mortality events in aquatic animals, including crustaceans12.

Apart from environmental changes (i.e. habitat loss, water pollution, temperature increase), the increasing and long lasting spread of non-indigenous crayfish species (NICS) in Europe originating from North America negatively impacted indigenous European populations13. NICS are characterized by highly invasive behavior, while they also can be carriers of crayfish plague disease agent causing mass mortalities in vulnerable native freshwater crayfish European populations14. The pathogen responsible for crayfish plague disease is the Aphanomyces astaci Schikora, (1906), belonging to the oomycetes class, which is also categorized among 100 worst invasive species worldwide15.

Pontastacus leptodactylus (Eschscholtz, 1823) is an indigenous European freshwater crayfish species of high economic value and commercial interest. In Greece, while it is natively found in Evros region, its distribution range has expanded to many water bodies due to humanly mediated translocations during the last decades16. However, crayfish plague disease outbreaks causing mass mortalities to Lakes Vegoritida and Polifitou crayfish population in Northern Greece were recorded during the last years17. This phenomenon has been also attributed to the individuals’ low genetic diversity among the translocated populations18. Since previous studies have shown that P. leptodactylus can either carry asymptomatically or suffer from A. astaci, understanding of the crucial factor leading to the final disease outcome is highly important17,19,20.

In cases of high mortalities, artificial breeding of local individuals may be a valuable solution for population enhancement, an activity known as restocking21. It should be also noted that all life forms are influenced by periodical seasonal variations that affect physiology and behaviors of the animals9,10. Thus, restockings, even if the released individuals are verified to originate as offspring from the local populations as it should, may be unsuccessful based on the seasonal physiology related issues. Many biological functions exhibit seasonal variation, with some of them occupying important roles in the immunity of the organisms11. However, the level of seasonal impact on important molecular pathways in crayfish still remains unknown. An organism’s ability to adapt to external environmental conditions is often driven by alterations in the transcriptional activity of certain genes. Since populations of P. leptodactylus are in decline, we considered it was important to investigate the expression of genes and proteins involved in key physiological pathways as a way of obtaining a broader picture of the species’ physiology. By doing so, our study contributes to identify periods when the animals are physiologically more resilient, which can in turn highlight more appropriate windows for restocking. Previous research has mainly focused on the physiological adjustments of populations after a restocking event21. In contrast, we emphasize here that studying these pathways before restocking takes place, is also of great importance for improving the effectiveness and long-term success of such conservation actions.

Earlier studies have demonstrated that the annual cycle of the stone crayfish is characterized by pronounced seasonal fluctuations in physiological indices such as the hepatosomatic and gonadosomatic indices, which reflect the different energetic demands of oogenesis and spermatogenesis22. Furthermore, seasonal variation has also been reported in the weight of the hepatopancreas, gonads, and tail muscle relative to total body weight, as well as in the ratio of different haemocyte types, suggesting that immune parameters are seasonally regulated22. Building on this knowledge, our study aims to go beyond morphological and haemocyte-based indicators and instead investigate molecular markers involved in critical pathways. Thus, the present study aims to provide significant information for many underlying pathways implicated in temperature response, apoptosis, inflammation and hypoxia, combined with a crayfish plague disease outbreak. By doing so, we aim to link seasonal physiological variation to the underlying molecular networks and to better understand how these processes may influence the susceptibility or resilience of crayfish to pathogens. This integrative approach provides a clearer framework for interpreting seasonality in the context of immunity and disease ecology, thereby strengthening the relevance of our findings.

A. Astaci detection

For the purposes of this study, the terms pre-epidemic, epidemic, and post-epidemic were operationally defined based on PCR screening for A. astaci. Individuals collected during the epidemic phase corresponded to the period when mass mortality events were observed, and all tested samples were confirmed positive for the pathogen by PCR. The pre-epidemic phase refers to the sampling period immediately before these mortality events, during which no pathogen was detected in the individuals tested. Similarly, the post-epidemic phase refers to the period following the cessation of mortality events, when PCR tests again yielded negative results for the pathogen. These definitions were adopted to clearly distinguish between periods with confirmed presence versus absence of the pathogen in the studied population.

Discussion

In the present study, we provide the first characterization of seasonal variation in molecular pathways linked to stress response, apoptosis, and innate immunity in P. leptodactylus under natural exposure to A. astaci. We show that stress-related proteins, such as HSPs, together with apoptosis regulators (Bax/Bcl-2, caspases), hypoxia (Hif-1a) and immune markers (Il-6), display seasonal patterns, with peaks coinciding with periods of increased mortality and pathogen detection. These findings suggest that host physiology fluctuates throughout the annual cycle in ways that may either exacerbate or mitigate susceptibility to crayfish plague. Previous studies on crayfish seasonality have focused mainly on morphological indices (e.g., hepatosomatic and gonadosomatic indices) or haemocyte counts, which showed high variability and limited reliability. Our results expand this framework by adding transcriptional and translational evidence for key molecular pathways, thereby filling an important knowledge gap and providing a more integrative understanding of how environmental stress, immunity, and pathogen dynamics interact in shaping crayfish health.

Gene characterization

Data generated from the present study, together with submitted sequences in the GenBank contribute to the better understanding of the physiological processes of the freshwater crayfish P. leptodactylus. Herein, we characterized four genes with newly developed primer pairs which have been shown to operate as key factors in many significant physiological pathways i.e., heat shock response (HSR), inflammation, apoptosis, ubiquitination and hypoxia. The partial transcripts for hsp70, hsp90, ub and gapdh described herein for the first time in narrow-clawed crayfish were submitted to GenBank.

Effect of seasonality on heat shock response (HSR) pathways

In general, an increasing trend was observed for both hsp70 and hsp90 mRNA relative expression levels for the months December and February, with male individuals demonstrated a second peak early in autumn in the gills compared to female individuals. On the other hand, Hsps levels were mainly increased during February and April in both heart and gills. Heat shock proteins are known for their significant role in organisms’ adaptation to temperature alterations and have been proposed as a valuable marker for assessing cellular stress levels24. The exposure of organisms to stressful environmental conditions, can drastically increase the expression of Hsps, activating the HSR that helps them to adapt to a wide range of environmental conditions25, preventing the accumulation of improperly folded proteins, either by promoting their proper folding or by directing them to specific degradative pathways26.

In the noble crayfish, it has been reported that several HSP family members (Hsp70-like, HSP-like-1, HSP-like-2, and HSPBP1) show a marked down-regulation three days post-infection with the haplogroup B strain of A. astaci (together with some members of the proteasome system)27. This reduction may compromise protein quality control because HSPs act as molecular chaperones that prevent protein misfolding, while proteasomes eliminate damaged proteins. A simultaneous decrease in both systems could therefore contribute to protein aggregation, cellular stress, and apoptosis in heavily infected tissues28. Herein, regarding the narrow-clawed crayfish, which exhibits greater tolerance to crayfish plague, shows up-regulation of hsps after A. astaci exposure. A similar trend has been documented in other crustaceans where hsp70 expression is stimulated during viral infections in the giant tiger prawn29 and in the red swamp crayfish30. In other arthropods, smaller HSPs such as HSP27 have also been shown to protect cells from TNF-induced apoptosis, indicating that HSPs can function not only as stress proteins but also as regulators of cell survival31. Taken together, these observations suggest that the ability to induce or maintain HSP expression during infection may represent a key mechanism underlying species-specific differences in pathogen resistance. In susceptible crayfish such as A. astacus, downregulation of HSPs and proteasome pathways may facilitate tissue damage and cell death, whereas resistant species appear to benefit from HSP-mediated stabilization of proteins and anti-apoptotic effects.

Temperature constitutes a significant environmental variable since physiological and biochemical processes in aquatic organisms are influenced32. At the same time, freshwater species inhabiting temperate climates are frequently exposed to temperature alterations due to season change, a fact that can lead to an annual surface water temperature increase up to 25 °C33,34. The high latitude and the morphology of the geographic region where Lake Vegoritida is located is characterized by significant inter-seasonal and diurnal differences in water temperature35. The elevated hsp gene expression and respective protein synthesis is a common response of organisms when exposed to various physical or chemical parameters and more specifically to heat or to cold stress36. Thus, the inverse pattern that was observed in the present study between Hsp70 and Hsp90, and water temperature in both mRNA and protein levels can be attributed to the specific freshwater crayfish species exposure to extreme ambient temperatures or to the organism’s exposure to other stressful parameters such as A. astaci infection and/or increased water turbidity27. Specifically, the observed Hsps pattern could be also the result of cold stress, as December and February are characterized by the lowest temperatures during the year. A similar relation between Hsps levels and low water temperatures was also observed in freshwater crayfish Cherax quadricarinatus Von Martens, (1868)37, sea bream (Sparus aurata), Linnaeus, (1758) and demersal tonguesole (Cynoglossus zanzibarensis) Norman, (1939) from Kuwait Bay area38, in the Kaluga (Huso dauricus) (Georgi, 1775)39, and in Nile tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus), Linnaeus, (1758)40, while Politis et al.41 found increased Hsps levels in the European eel (Anguilla Anguilla) Linnaeus, (1758) when exposed both to colder and warmer temperatures. However, since Hsps can be also activated by other stressors in the individual’s42,43, we can hypothesize that the increase of both hsp70 and hsp90 mRNA relative expression levels, and Hsp70 and Hsp90 levels during the winter months may be attributed to A. astaci presence, since late in the spring and early in the summer of the same year mass mortalities were recorded18. Furthermore, a second peak regarding hsp mRNA relative expression levels observed in male individuals early in autumn could be the result of the spermatogenesis process, as this was also observed in the red claw crayfish44.

Effect of seasonality on apoptotic pathways

Apoptosis is an important process for survival, since it removes damaged macromolecules and damaged cells that may interfere with organism’s normal function45. The elevation of the pro-apoptotic Bax, in addition with the decrease of the anti-apoptotic Bcl-2, lead to increased Bax/Bcl-2 ratio, which finally dictates and activates caspases and therefore apoptosis, in both heart and gills of P. leptodactylus, during the cold temperature months. We can assume that the increased HSR during this period of cold stress, cannot prevent the apoptotic process which is simultaneously activated. On the other hand, warmer months seem not to activate apoptosis, mostly due to elevated Bcl-2 levels which probably prevent the accumulation of caspases in the same period. Likewise, apoptosis was also observed in Cherax quadricarinatus (Von Martens, 1868) under cold stress conditions46. It is observed that under cold stress conditions the damage in mitochondria can lead to activation of mitochondrial autophagy and intrinsic apoptosis47. The decreased ambient temperature can lead to slower metabolic processes and to a subsequent decrease in the energy supply, inducing the apoptotic pathways46.

Another cellular pathway which is closely related to the apoptotic process is the ubiquitin—proteasome system48,49. Following the above patterns, our results indicate increased ubiquitin conjugates levels during the winter months and more specifically in February, in both tissues and sexes.

In a previous transcriptomic analyses in noble crayfish, several genes related to the ubiquitin–proteasome pathway were found to be down-regulated during A. astaci infection27. Since this pathway is essential for tagging and degrading misfolded or damaged proteins, its suppression can lead to protein aggregation and cellular dysfunction50. The link between ubiquitin–proteasome activity and apoptosis is well established as reduced proteasome function results in the accumulation of unfolded proteins, which in turn triggers endoplasmic reticulum stress and apoptotic pathways51. From an immunological perspective, both the ubiquitin–proteasome system and apoptosis play dual roles. On the one hand, they help maintain cellular integrity and eliminate damaged cells but at the same time the liberated cellular material may be recognized as foreign by the immune system, leading to an inflammatory response52. The previous observation regarding the the down-regulation of ubiquitin–proteasome genes during A. astaci infection in the noble crayfish may contributes to impaired protein quality control, induction of apoptosis, and ultimately a compromised immune defense27. In more resistant species, such as narrow clawed crayfish, as observed from the present the ability to sustain HSP expression and proteasome activity may help prevent uncontrolled apoptosis and support a more effective immune response.

However, increased ub mRNA expression levels were exhibited in females’ heart in the beginning of the summer, and in males’ gills in the beginning of the autumn. The first increase in ubiquitin conjugates is in line with the other examined biochemical markers (Hsps, caspases and Bax/Bcl-2 levels) while the second peak both in males and females could be attributed to the reproduction period, which is considered to be stressful for both sexes36. More specifically, ubiquitination process widely occurs during spermatogenesis53,54 and oogenesis in other crustaceans such as the freshwater crayfish Procambarus clarkii (Girard, 1852)55 and Chinese mitten crab Eriocheir sinensis (H. Milne-Edwards, 1853)56,57.

Effect of seasonality on hypoxia pathways

Hypoxia is considered among the most significant abiotic factors that cause stress to aquatic organisms of freshwater ecosystems58. In the latter, hypoxic conditions can occur both naturally and by anthropogenic activities and pollutants59. Many hypoxia-induced factors (HIFs) have been characterized in aquatic organisms and are considered among the major transcriptional regulators in responses to hypoxia conditions. In the present study, decreased levels of hydroxy Hif-1α depicting increased levels of hypoxia, were observed in both tissues, mainly during the lowest ambient temperatures. Temperature beyond upper and lower critical levels (critical temperatures) can decrease the content of O2 (PO2) in the body fluids of the organism, due to the impairment of circulatory system functions, which causes inadequate uptake and transport of oxygen to and from tissues60. In this way, an internally hypoxic environment is created in which the availability of oxygen to the mitochondria is decreased61. It has been shown that in both cold and warm temperature extremes, oxygen supply to mitochondria increases since the metabolic rate becomes faster, thereby increasing the rates of energy consumption through respiration62. Therefore, the subsequent mismatch between oxygen supply and demand, leads to a decrease in the aerobic capacity of the organism, a transition to anaerobic metabolism and ultimately a breakdown of physiological functions, even in fully oxygenated environments63,64. hif-1α mRNA increased levels were also observed in the sea bass Dicentrarchus labrax (Linnaeus, 1758)65, the eurasian perch Perca fluviatilis (Linnaeus, 1758)66, the epaulette shark Hemiscyllium ocellatum (Bonnaterre, 1788)67, the chinese sucker (Myxocyprinus asiaticus), Bleeker, (1864) larvae68, the dragonet Callionymus valenciennei, Temminck and Schlegel, (1845)69 and in the grass carp (Ctenopharyngodon Idella), Valenciennes, (1844)70 under hypoxia conditions.

Seasonal variation of immune responses

The immune responses in crayfish species represent a complex network of many extrinsic and intrinsic components. More specifically, these factors comprise of environmental conditions, population density, diseases etc.71. It has been observed that among the environmental parameters which affect the crustaceans’ immune system are pollution, temperature, oxygen, salinity and pH12,72–77. In general, temperature variations and the resulting stress responses influence the immune system78. Furthermore, seasonal variations regarding immune responses in several animal species can be attributed to the seasonal pattern of temperature and photoperiod79. Low ambient temperatures seem to activate apoptotic responses in the crustacean hemolymph, which subsequently affect innate immunity80,81. In other crustaceans the expression levels of immune-related genes are either increased82,83 or suppressed after cold stress stimulation46. The decreased expression of the genes involved in the innate immune process, under low temperatures indicates immunosuppression, a state which makes the organisms more vulnerable and prone to pathogen infection84. Il-6 represents a key factor in the investigation of proinflammatory response85. Disruption of homeostasis caused by infection or tissue injury rapidly triggers the production of Il-6, which plays a defensive role against stress86. The immunological response in mammals, despite the greater complex, is directly correlated with IL-6 receptor that enacts a clear role in this animal group, while in crustaceans, IL-6 gene expression, although often triggered by pathogens, does not clearly reflect immunoresponse. In line with this inference, our study attempts to enlighten the IL-6 expression in the freshwater crayfish P. leptodactylus in which data were scarce so far for this biomarker, proposing that inflammation is higher during the hottest months of the year, an incidence observed mainly in heart and not in gills. In zebrafish, exposure to stressor factors has been shown to elevate Il-6 levels, indicating a pro-inflammatory response87 while in res swamp crayfish Il-6 was acted as an indicator of oxidative stress after bisphenol S exposure88. From a previous study was revealed that the Il-1 family may play an important role in activating innate immune responses against pathogen infection in mud crab89. In the present study, an increase in the Il-6 inflammatory protein levels after a cold stress period that probably suppressed the genes involved in the immune response, was accompanied by an A. astaci oubreak which led to mass mortality events18.

In interpreting the seasonal variation in immune and stress responses observed in P. leptodactylus, it is important to consider not only environmental drivers such as temperature, but also intrinsic biological processes. Moulting, for example, is a physiologically demanding event that occurs in spring and early summer and is associated with transient reductions in immune competence while the exoskeleton is soft and the animal is particularly vulnerable90. Reproductive investment in late summer similarly requires substantial energetic allocation, diverting resources away from immune defense and thereby modulating immune responsiveness. Furthermore, brood care places additional metabolic demands on females carrying juveniles, which may alter physiological balance and immune activity91. From previous study, caspase, interleukin 1 beta converting enzyme, was found to be upregulated in the postmolt stage in P. clarkii92. Taken together, these processes provide a broader biological context for our findings, highlighting that the seasonal modulation of stress and immune markers in crayfish reflects a complex interplay between abiotic conditions and life-history events.

Influence of A. astaci on physiological response

Taken all together, the seasonal effects on P. leptodactylus individuals exhibited an increased apoptotic response and hypoxic status during the lowest recorded ambient temperatures, probably due to a cold stress response, in February and April, while the inflammation levels increased during increasing water temperature in spring and remained high throughout summer. Although the Lake Vegoritida itself does not freeze during winter, hypoxic conditions may still occur due to low ambient temperatures and inflows of near-zero temperature water masses released, which can disturb stratification and reduce oxygen availability. Such environmental factors may contribute to the activation of apoptosis pathways observed in winter, consistent with the role of hypoxia as a physiological stressor. The first detection of A. astaci pathogen was recorded during June of the same year. During the same period increased turbidity levels were recorded. It has been observed in previous studies that the original host species of A. astaci i.e. signal crayfish has developed adaptations to the pathogens’ presence, mainly by the ability to operate as latent carriers of the disease by keeping their immune defense at constantly high levels93,94 in comparison with the narrow-clawed crayfish. More specifically, in the present study, a pathogen-dependent immune response was observed, as the inflammation Il-6 protein increased parallel to the pathogen identification and remained at high levels even after the mass mortalities events.

It has been shown that water temperature significantly influences A. astaci prevalence in water. In specific, higher spore release was recorded under laboratory conditions, from latent carriers of the disease, when the temperature exceeded 18οC95,96. Thus, the increase in inflammation levels during these months could be the result of the increased exposure of the animals to higher spore concentration. During synchronized moulting (usually prior to unfavourable and growth-inhibiting winter period)97,98 and reproduction periods, the infection possibility is also higher as the softer cuticle after the moulting or the heavy injuries occurred from males’ aggressive reproductive behavior may lead to increased release of A. astaci spores99,100.

Future recommendations

Restocking of indigenous crayfish species in Europe has been shown to depend on several interrelated factors. The habitat must first be carefully assessed for suitability, not only in terms of its physical and chemical water quality, but also with regard to ecological conditions, absence of crayfish plague and other pathogens, and prevention of invasion by non-native crayfish. Equally important is the choice of stocking material, which should take into account the genetic background of the source population to avoid unintended impacts on local biodiversity. The stocking procedure itself is also critical, with priority given to sites that are geographically isolated and minimally affected by human activities such as intensive fishing or agriculture. In terms of stocking design, previous reintroduction efforts have shown that stable and self-sustaining populations were established when at least 100–200 sexually mature crayfish were released, ideally spanning a range of size classes and with an approximate sex ratio of one male to three females. The above information regarding restockings can be summarized in a previous work by Souty-Grosset and Reynolds,101. Within this broader framework, our results contribute an additional layer of information by highlighting seasonal differences in crayfish physiology, which may help identify periods when animals are more resilient to stress and disease. Such knowledge can be integrated into existing guidelines to refine the timing of restocking actions and improve the likelihood of successful population establishment.

Conclusion

The freshwater crayfish P. leptodactylus has experienced severe population declines and mass mortality events in recent decades, highlighting the urgent need for conservation measures that combine ecological and economic perspectives. In this context, understanding the seasonal dynamics of its physiology is critical, as it provides insights into the underlying biochemical mechanisms that may define favorable windows for restocking. Our study demonstrated that crayfish undergo a marked physiological stress response during colder months, primarily through activation of protective pathways such as the heat shock response. Interestingly, this period was not associated with mortality events, and inflammatory markers appeared reduced, suggesting a relative physiological balance. These findings point to early winter as a potentially advantageous period for restocking, allowing individuals time to acclimate before facing the combined challenges of rising temperatures, pathogen proliferation, moulting, and reproduction. Nonetheless, uncertainties remain regarding the survival of released individuals, particularly in populations where latent A. astaci infections persist. Material and methods

Sample collection

In the present study, 120 adult P. leptodactylus individuals were collected from Lake Vegoritida in Northern Greece. Ethics approval for the experimental use of the study animal (P. leptodactylus) was granted by the University of Western Macedonia local ethics committee given the protocol code 102, whereas collection of samples was confirmed by the Administrative Region of Western Macedonia, Decision number 186559/28-11-2022. All methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations in the EU Directive on the protection of the use of animals for scientific purposes (2010/63/EU) and in accordance with ARRIVE guidelines.

More specifically, the individuals were captured from the wild using net traps, which were checked at regular intervals during the sampling days to minimize stress and mortality of non-target species. From each sampling, 20 adult P. leptodactylus individuals were randomly selected. Following this procedure, the crayfish were euthanized by a scalpel cut in the head region to ensure rapid death and apid immersion in liquid nitrogen was also applied, particularly when immediate preservation of tissue integrity was required. All procedures were conducted in accordance with institutional ethical guidelines for animal research and under the appropriate collection permits for this species. The morphometric characteristics of the animals are presented in Table 2. Additionally, water physicochemical parameters of each sampling were recorded (Table 3). After P. leptodactylus individuals collection, heart and gills tissue were dissected and were immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen, and thereafter were stored at − 80 °C for further biochemical analysis. We selected heart and gills as target organs because they are critical sites of host–pathogen interaction and systemic immune signaling in aquatic organisms. The gills represent the first line of defense, being in constant contact with the aquatic environment and thus directly exposed to waterborne pathogens and stressors102. The heart, on the other hand, plays a central role in systemic circulation and immune factor distribution, and studies have suggested that cardiac tissues may contribute to the regulation of innate immune responses during infection and stress conditions103. In addition, 50 ng of muscle tissue from the tail were also collected for the investigation of A. astaci presence. DNA and RNA extraction

Muscle tissues from the parts of the body prone to A. astaci infection (from melanized spots together with cuticles on the soft abdominal, on the telson, or on leg joints approximately 50 ng) were transferred in sterile Eppendorf tubes. DNA extraction for A. astaci investigation was performed using the Nucleospin tissue kit (Macherey–Nagel, Duren, Germany)104. The individuals were separated in pools of three according to their sex, with 25 mg of each individual transferred in a new sterile Eppendorf tube (75 mg in total for each pool).

The total RNA from the two different tissues (heart and gills) was extracted using the NucleoZOL reagent (Macherey–Nagel, Düren Germany), according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Six biological replicates from each sampling period and tissue type were prepared. For each different sampling period and tissue, samples were homogenized in 500 μl NucleoZOL, and then RNAase-free water was added to the lysate. Thereafter, the samples were centrifuged (12,000 g, 15 min, room temperature), while an iso-propanol solution was added for the precipitation of the total RNA. Another centrifugation (12,000 g, 10 min, room temperature) was performed, followed by two ethanol washes of the RNA pellet and two centrifugation in between (8000 g, three min, room temperature). Finally, the pellet was resuspended in 100 μl nuclease-free water. After the RNA extraction, samples were stored at − 80 °C prior to cDNA synthesis. For the determination of its quantity and quality the extracted RNA was quantified on a Quawell UV–Vis 5000 spectrophotometer (Quawell Technology, San Jose, CA, USA) prior to cDNA synthesis, which was performed using the PrimeScript kit (Takara, Japan) using random hexamer primers105. More specifically, approximately 500 ng of total RNA from each sample was collected. Quality and quantity of the cDNA were also measured in a Quawell UV–Vis 5000 spectrophotometer (Quawell Technology, San Jose, CA, USA). cDNA samples were equally diluted and were then preserved at − 20 °C until qPCR amplification.

For every PCR reaction (20 μL), a mix of 10 μL FastGene Taq 2X Ready Mix (NIPPON Genetics, Duren, Germany), 0.6 μL of each one of the primers (10 μM), 0.7 μL of DNA (40 ng L−1), and ultrapure water up to 20 μL were prepared. Gene characterization using conventional PCR

RT products was utilized as cDNA (one μl of each) matrix for amplification in conventional PCR using FastGene Taq 2X Ready Mix (NIPPON Genetics, Duren, Germany) and each one of the newly designed primer sets (Table 3) to test their validity. The initial denaturation step was performed at 95 °C for three min, followed by 35 cycles of denaturation for 30 s at 92 °C, annealing for 40 s at 50 °C, and extension for 40 s at 72 °C, and a final extension step at 72 °C for five for min in the end. The total reaction volume was 20 μL, including 0.6 μL of each primer (10 μM), 10 μL FastGene Taq 2X Ready Mix (NIPPON Genetics, Duren, Germany) and 1 μL of cDNA (50 ng/μL). To reach the final volume ultrapure water up to 20 μl was added. By the end of the reaction, the PCR products were loaded and run on a 2% agarose for 20 min at 100 V. Finally, a purification step of the PCR products was performed using the commercial NucleoSpin Gel and PCR clean up kit (MAcherey–Nagel, Duren, Germany) and were bidirectionally sequenced using the Sanger method.

Gene expression analysis

Hsp70, hsp90, ub and hif1-a gene expression was evaluated using quantitative real time PCR. The method that was used for quantification of the relative gene expression of P. leptodactylus genes was the comparative CT method (2−ΔΔCT method) as described in Livak and Schmittgen109. The normalization of the target gene expressions was performed according to gapdh expression. qPCR reactions were carried out in a Thermocycler Eco 48 Real-time PCR (Illumina) instrument using KAPA SYBR® FAST qPCR Master Mix. The final volume of PCR reactions was 10 μl, where 10 ng of cDNA as template, five μl of KAPA SYBR® FAST qPCR Master Mix (2X), 2 μM of each one of the primers (Table 1) and ultrapure PCR water up to 10 μl were included. qPCR conditions consisted of a first step at 95 °C for 15 s, followed by 40 cycles of a denaturion step at 95 °C for 15 s, an annealing step at annealing temperature specific for each primer pair (Table 3) for 20 s and an elongation step at 72 °C for 20 s. Plate read was encompassed after the step of 55 °C for quantification of the amplicons.

Phylogenetic analysis of the newly characterized genes

Up to nine top BLAST hits from different species were downloaded for each newly characterized gene and aligned with the P. leptodactylus candidate gene in MEGAX (version X, MEGA, https://www.megasoftware.net)110 using a ClustalW alignment algorithm111. For the phylogenetic analysis a Neighbour Joining tree were generated in MEGAX (version X, MEGA, https://www.megasoftware.net) using the Maximum Composite Likelihood method with 1000 bootstraps112.

SDS-PAGE/immunoblot and dot blot analysis

Heart and gill samples were homogenized (1/3 w/v) in cold lysis buffer (20 mM β-glycerophosphate, 50 mM NaF, 2 mM EDTA, 20 mM Hepes, 0.2 mM Na3VO4, 10 mM benzamidine, pH 7, 200 μM leupeptin, 10 μΜ trans-epoxy succinyl-L-leucylamido-(4-guanidino)butane, 5 mM dithiotheitol, 300 μΜ phenyl methyl sulfonyl fluoride (PMSF), 50 μg ml−1 pepstatin, and 1% v/v Triton X-100). After a 30-min extraction on ice, samples were centrifuged (10,000 g, 10 min, 4 °C) and the supernatants were boiled (3/1 v/v) with sample buffer (330 mM Tris–HCl, 13% v/v glycerol, 133 mM DTT, 10% w/v SDS, 0.2% w/v bromophenol blue). Protein concentrations were determined using the BioRad protein assay.

Equivalent amounts of proteins (50 μg) were separated on 10% (w/v) acrylamide and 0.275% (w/v) bisacrylamide slab gels, and transferred electrophoretically onto nitrocellulose membranes (0.45 μm, Schleicher & Schuell, Keene N. H. 03431, USA). Non-specific binding sites on the membranes were blocked with 5% (w/v) non-fat milk in TBST [20 mM Tris–HCl, pH 7.5, 137 mM NaCl, 0.1% (v/v) Tween 20] for 30 min at room temperature. Nitrocellulose membranes resulting from the above procedure were subjected to overnight incubation with: monoclonal mouse anti-hsp70 (H5147, Sigma), monoclonal mouse anti-hsp90 (H1775, Sigma), anti-IL-6 (CSB-PA06757A0Rb, Cusabio), anti-Bcl2 (2872, Cell Signaling), anti-Bax (B-9) (2772, Cell Signaling) and anti-Hydroxy-Hif-1α (3434, Cell Signaling). Quality transfer and protein loading were assured by Ponceau stain and actin (anti-β actin 3700, Cell Signaling). Antibodies were diluted as recommended by the manufacturer’s guidelines113. We acknowledge that functional validation of IL-6 expression, such as infection or hemocyte assays, was not performed and represents a limitation for the present study and an important proposed next step to confirm its role in crayfish immunity. However we have to state the absence of a clear inference concerning this biomarker in crustaceans and therefore we believe that the present analysis represents a valuable addition for this animal taxon.

Cleaved caspases levels were determined in heart and gill samples with the employment of a dot blot apparatus. Specifically, samples were diluted to a concentration of five μg ml−1 in a saline solution (150 mM NaCl); 100 μl volumes were loaded onto a pre-soaked nitrocellulose membrane (0.45 μm) in a dot blot vacuum apparatus (Bio-Rad), and gravity-fed through the membrane. The membrane was blocked with 5% (w/v) non-fat milk in TBST [20 mM Tris–HCl, pH 7.5, 137 mM NaCl, 0.1% (v/v) Tween 20] for 30 min at room temperature. The resulting nitrocellulose membrane was subjected to overnight incubation with anti-cleaved caspase antibody (8698 Cell Signaling). The antibody was diluted as recommended by the manufacturer’s guidelines113.

After washing in TBST (3 periods, 5 min each time), the blots and dots were incubated with horseradish peroxidase-linked secondary antibodies, washed again in TBST (three periods, five minutes each time), and the dots were detected using enhanced chemiluminescence (Chemicon) with exposure to Fuji Medical X-ray films. Films were quantified by laser-scanning densitometry (GelPro Analyzer Software, version 3.0.00.00, Media Cybernetics, https://mediacy.com).

Statistical analysis

Changes regarding relative biochemical indicators were tested by one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) (Instat, version 3.0, GraphPad, https://www.graphpad.com) for significance at the 5% level (p < 0.05) between examined groups. Moreover, statistically significant differences were tested by two-way (Prism, version 5.0, GraphPad, https://www.graphpad.com) analysis of variance (ANOVA). Samplings and sex were regarded as fixed factors. Post-hoc comparisons were performed by the employment of the Bonferroni test. Values are presented as means ± S.D. Moreover, for the determination of the correlation between the examined variables, principal component analysis (PCA) was applied using the FactoMineR package in R (version 4.3.2, The R Project for Statistical Computing, https://www.r-project.org)114.

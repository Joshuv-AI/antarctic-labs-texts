A reliable and verified dataset of functional miRNA-mRNA target interactions for gene regulation studies
Authors: Lacey Haddon, Lara K Mahal, Hosna Jabbari
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
MicroRNAs (miRNAs) regulate gene expression through interactions with target mRNAs, primarily thought to be via seed-mediated binding in the 3′ untranslated region. However, increasing evidence supports the functional importance of non-canonical and seed-independent interactions, as well as miRNA-mediated up-regulation. Existing miRNA-mRNA interaction databases integrate data from diverse experimental approaches but often contain inconsistencies in annotation, redundancy, and variable levels of validation, limiting their utility for sequence-level and mechanistic analyses. Here, we present miRVerified, a high-confidence dataset of 1,913 experimentally validated human miRNA-mRNA interactions curated using stringent inclusion criteria. Interactions were compiled from publicly available resources and filtered to retain only those supported by direct functional reporter assays and sequence-resolved binding evidence. Additional quality control steps included removal of duplicate entries, exclusion of retracted studies, and validation of target site sequences through alignment to the human reference genome (hg38). Both canonical and non-canonical interactions were retained, along with experimentally supported cases of miRNA-mediated up-regulation. The resulting dataset provides binding site-resolved interaction sequences, mutation data, and genomic coordinates. Enrichment analysis demonstrates a significant overrepresentation of conserved targets, supporting biological validity. MiRVerified is designed as a reliable reference resource for benchmarking, predictive modeling, and mechanistic studies of miRNA-mediated gene regulation.

Background & Summary

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are small (~22 nucleotides), non-coding RNAs that regulate gene expression post-transcriptionally. They are thought to recognize target mRNAs through a conserved seed sequence at the 5′ end (nucleotides 2–8), which pairs with complementary sites within the 3′ untranslated region (3′ UTR)1. This binding recruits the RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC) and leads to mRNA degradation or translational repression2. While canonical seed pairing is often sufficient for silencing, additional pairing outside the seed region can stabilize interactions or compensate for mismatches1,3.

CLASH (crosslinking, ligation, and sequencing of hybrids) experiments have shown up to 60% of high-confidence miRNA interactions are non-canonical and contain bulges or mismatches within the seed region4. Experimental evidence has also shown a small percentage of target sites may be seedless and rely solely on binding of the 3′ region of the miRNA3. Thus, non-canonical interactions are becoming increasingly recognized as functionally relevant and may explain gene regulation effects that deviate from traditional seed-based models.

Recent studies using a high-throughput functional reporter assay, miRFluR, revealed miRNAs can upregulate the ST6GAL1 and ST6GAL2 genes through direct binding that is spread throughout the 5′ and 3′ regions of the miRNA and the target’s 3′UTR5. Additional up-regulatory effects have been described for several miRNAs, including for miR-155 on CD98hc, and these interactions often lack canonical seed binding6. It is becoming increasingly apparent that miRNAs are able to function outside of their conserved seed regions and can promote gene repression or up-regulation, but the mechanisms that drive these non-canonical effects are still poorly understood.

Accurate and publicly accessible miRNA-mRNA interaction data are essential for studying variation in miRNA binding and its impact on gene regulation. Several databases have been developed to catalog experimentally supported interactions; however, comparative analyses have identified limited overlap between resources. Differences in the curation practices including duplication of reported interactions and variability in supporting evidence, may explain some of the variation7. A recent review of ten miRNA-mRNA interaction databases further highlighted challenges in annotation consistency, standardization, and experimental validation across these resources8. Widely used databases such as miRTarBase and DIANA-TarBase integrate data from diverse experimental approaches with varying levels of directness and confidence9. While this broad inclusion supports discovery, it can introduce challenges for applications, such as machine-learning, that require precise, sequence-resolved, and functionally validated interaction data10. Reporter-based assays (luciferase or GFP reporters) remain among the most direct methods for establishing functional miRNA-mRNA interactions and mutational validation is considered strong evidence of a functional interaction site11.

To improve consistency and interpretability, we defined a set of criteria to identify and exclude entries that are not suitable for sequence-level analysis. Specifically, we removed entries that (i) contain target sequences that cannot be mapped to the human reference genome (hg38), (ii) lack direct functional validation (via reporter assays), (iii) originate from retracted studies, or (iv) represent duplicated interactions within or across datasets. These criteria are independent of binding mode. Both canonical and non-canonical interactions, including those containing mismatches, bulges, or 3′ compensatory pairing, were retained when supported by experimentally validated, sequence-resolved binding evidence. In addition, experimentally supported cases of miRNA-mediated up-regulation were included to capture regulatory outcomes beyond repression.

We applied these criteria to create miRVerified, a high-confidence, experimentally validated dataset focused on binding site-resolved miRNA-mRNA interactions supported by direct functional assays. The relatively smaller size of this dataset reflects stringent and transparent inclusion criteria prioritizing sequence accuracy, consistent annotation, and mechanistic interpretability over maximizing coverage of the miRNA interactome. Instead, miRVerified provides a reliable and well-defined reference set for precision-focused applications, including mechanistic modeling of miRNA-mRNA binding, development and benchmarking of predictive algorithms, and experimental design. In this context, carefully curated datasets serve as valuable resources in computational biology, where reducing heterogeneity improves reproducibility and interpretability. To benchmark miRVerified against an independent reference dataset, we evaluated enrichment of conserved TargetScanHuman12 interactions and observed significant overlap, supporting the biological relevance of the curated interactions. MiRVerified is intended to complement existing large-scale resources by providing a consistent dataset that captures both canonical and non-canonical binding interactions, as well as miRNA-mediated up-regulation, enabling more reproducible analyses of miRNA regulatory mechanisms.

Methods

Bulk data filtering

Data was downloaded from miRTarBase (v10.0)11, TarBase (v9)10 and RNAInter (v4.0)13. These databases provide publicly accessible collections of experimentally supported miRNA:target interactions. MiRTarBase v10.0 was downloaded from https://mirtarbase.cuhk.edu.cn/ (accessed April 25, 2025). TarBase v9 was obtained from https://dianalab.e-ce.uth.gr/tarbasev9 (accessed June 24, 2025). RNAInter v4.0 was downloaded from http://www.rna-society.org/rnainter/ (accessed June 4, 2025). All datasets were retrieved using the human miRNA-mRNA interaction download options provided on each platform. These resources are distributed under their respective database usage policies, which permit academic reuse of publicly available data. No restricted or proprietary datasets were used in this study.

Comparative analyses of miRNA-mRNA interaction databases have highlighted inconsistencies in annotation, redundancy of reported interactions, and variability in the level of experimental support across resources8. In our analysis, we observed similar issues, including duplicate entries across and within datasets, inconsistencies between reported sequences and genomic references, and inclusion of mutated or non-functional target sequences derived from experimental constructs.

The current human interaction datasets contain 669,108 miRNA-mRNA interactions in miRTarBase v10.0, 4,724,536 in TarBase v9, and 3,095,316 in RNAInter v4.010,11,13. Each dataset was filtered to retain only interactions validated by functional reporter assays, such as luciferase and EGFP reporters. This substantially reduced the number of interactions: only 1.37% of miRTarBase interactions remained, while fewer than 0.1% of interactions remained in both TarBase and RNAInter (Table 1). A PubMed search of the publications citing the remaining interactions identified several retracted articles and led to 272 and 8 interactions being removed from the miRTarBase and TarBase datasets, respectively. A 2023 review reported that only 16 microRNAs were shared across the top five microRNA:mRNA interaction databases8. Consistent with this limited overlap, we found only 1,178 functional interactions common to all three datasets. Because miRTarBase contained most of the functional interactions and also provided binding sequence information, we used it as the primary dataset for further processing, supplementing it with data from TarBase, RNAInter, and PubMed searches as needed.

The miRTarBase dataset provides the target site sequence at lengths that range from 1–1,888 nucleotides (nt). This variability makes it difficult to correctly identify the seed binding region for many of the listed interactions. Though there were many sequences in the miRTarBase dataset that were longer than 20nt, bulk BLAT searches on these regions using the UCSC genome browser14 found several instances where the sequence given was not found within the hg38 or hg19 genome. Occasionally this was due to slight typographical errors, such as a missing nucleotide within the sequence, and was corrected through cross-referencing with the original data. However, several of the incorrect sequences were found to be carried over from the mutated experimental data within the article and did not represent valid miRNA-mRNA interaction sites. Inclusion of these mutated sequences in the recent miRTarBase dataset could lead to errors in down-stream analyses as these interactions are not identified as non-functional.

We also found several of the interactions were entered multiple times, whether from the same publication or from a separate publication. This means the true number of unique miRNA-mRNA interactions are much lower than what is listed in the dataset and using the data as-is would introduce significant bias and redundancy into downstream analyses. In order to eliminate these errors, we used the PubMed ID for each interaction in our filtered list and manually verified whether the information given in the original study was accurate and could be included our dataset. This led to a final filtered dataset containing 6,736 unique functional miRNA interactions.

Development of the miRVerified dataset

The pipeline used to generate the dataset of valid and accurate interactions is outlined in Fig. 1. For an interaction to be included, the sequence given in the original publication had to correctly reference back to the hg38 genomic sequence. Each miRNA sequence was verified against the downloaded list of human mature miRNA sequences from miRbase15. Each interaction required clear functional evidence, demonstrated by a statistically significant change (by p-value) in reporter gene activity following mutation of the predicted binding region. Importantly, filtering criteria were independent of binding mode. Both canonical and non-canonical interactions were retained, provided they were supported by direct functional validation and sequence-resolved binding evidence. Canonical and non-canonical interactions were identified following the criteria outlined by Hall et al.3. Canonical interactions were defined as those containing ≥6 consecutive Watson-Crick base pairs within the miRNA seed region (nucleotides 2–8). This rule-based threshold provided a clear classification boundary, and as a result, no ambiguous borderline cases requiring manual resolution were encountered. Non-canonical interactions have 3 major types: (i) imperfect seed pairing which contains one mismatched nucleotide and additional pairing of the miRNA 3′ end, (ii) a microseed that only pairs with 3–5 consecutive nucleotides along with additional 3′ pairing, and (iii) no seed match with extensive base pairing at the 3′ supplemental end of the miRNA (Fig. 2).Fig. 1Development pipeline of the miRVerified miRNA-mRNA interaction dataset.Fig. 2Schematic showing the criteria for canonical vs non-canonical miRNA-mRNA interactions. Interactions were excluded only when sequence information could not be reliably mapped or experimentally validated, rather than based on seed pairing characteristics. We additionally conducted targeted PubMed searches to include experimentally supported cases of miRNA-mediated up-regulation, as these interactions represent a less common but biologically relevant regulatory mechanism.

To evaluate whether miRVerified interactions are enriched for evolutionarily conserved miRNA-mRNA target pairs, we performed a Fisher’s exact test using conserved interactions from TargetScanHuman (https://www.targetscan.org/cgi-bin/targetscan/data_download.vert80.cgi)12 as an external reference dataset. Prior to analysis, miRNA and gene identifiers in both datasets were standardised by trimming whitespace, converting all entries to lowercase, and constructing unique miRNA-gene interaction pairs. Duplicate interaction pairs within each dataset were removed to ensure that each interaction was represented only once.

A 2 × 2 contingency table was constructed based on: (i) interactions present in both miRVerified and TargetScanHuman (shared conserved interactions), (ii) interactions present in miRVerified but not in TargetScanHuman, (iii) interactions present in TargetScanHuman but not in miRVerified, and (iv) all possible miRNA-gene combinations defined by the Cartesian product of unique miRNAs and genes observed in miRVerified. This background “interaction universe” was used to represent the constrained search space of biologically plausible interactions given the observed molecular entities, rather than all theoretically possible human gene-miRNA combinations.

To reduce bias from unequal dataset sizes, all analyses were performed using unique interaction pairs only. A one-sided Fisher’s exact test (alternative hypothesis: enrichment) was implemented using SciPy (version 1.17.1) in Python 3.13. The resulting odds ratio and p-value were used to assess whether conserved TargetScanHuman interactions were significantly enriched within miRVerified relative to the defined background universe. We also confirmed that our dataset represents a diverse set of miRNA families and seed types. To ensure broad biological relevance, the gene list from our dataset was input into the Reactome pathway analysis tool16 to confirm coverage of the major regulatory pathways.

Data Records

The miRVerified dataset is publicly available at the Zenodo repository17: 10.5281/zenodo.19744622.

The headings for each entry in the dataset include the miRNA name and its RNACentral ID. The target gene is defined by gene symbol and Ensembl ID. For the species heading, all entries are Homo sapiens. Target site refers to the 3′ UTR region for the full miRNA-mRNA interaction and is given as the genomic DNA sequence 5′ to 3′, such that the seed is located at the 3′ end. We have included one base after the seed region as the base composition in this position has been shown to influence miRNA activity18. The Seed (target) provides just the seed region from the target sequence. The mutated target site provides the mutated target sequence that led to loss of function in the reporter assay. The mutated seed shows the point mutations that were relevant to the seed region. The cell type heading identifies the cell type(s) that the reporter assay was conducted in (when explicitly given). The regulation heading identifies whether the target sequence decreased (down) or increased (up) the gene reporter activity. The PubMed ID (PMID) for the original research is given for each interaction. The seed pattern heading defines each interaction as canonical (perfect seed) or non-canonical (imperfect seed and significant binding outside of the seed region). For ease of downstream analyses, we provide the genomic coordinate from the UCSC genome browser (hg38) for the target sequence.

Data Overview

The miRVerified dataset contains 1,913 experimentally validated miRNA-mRNA interactions involving 584 unique miRNAs and 812 target genes. Among these miRNAs, 262 regulate multiple genes and 131 exhibit multiple binding sites within the same 3′ UTR. Target site lengths on the 3′ UTR range from 20 to 40 nucleotides, with an average length of 22 nucleotides. Mutations within reporter constructs range from single nucleotide substitutions to deletion or disruption of the full seed region. The dataset includes interactions derived from a wide range of experimental cell systems (approximately 297 unique cell types), with HEK-293 and HeLa cells being the most frequently used systems due to their suitability for reporter assays. All interactions are supported by functional reporter evidence, including luciferase or fluorescent reporter assays, with documented loss or gain of reporter signal following mutation of the predicted binding site.

The majority of interactions (92.7%) are canonical, which highlights the high conservation of seed sites in the 3′ UTR19 as well as the dominant use of miRNA prediction programs that rely heavily on seed matching20. We have also included 15 well-validated up-regulatory miRNA-mRNA interactions in our dataset as this form of miRNA regulation is often under-reported in experimental studies, but is an important part of the miRNA function that should be explored. There is growing research that supports functionally relevant, non-canonical binding patterns for several miRNAs3, so we hope more of these interactions will be included in functional datasets in the future. Technical Validation

To ensure sequence and functional validity, all reported miRNA-mRNA interactions were subjected to multiple quality control steps. First, all target site sequences were mapped to the human reference genome (hg38) using BLAT, and sequences that could not be reliably aligned were excluded. This step removed entries corresponding to typographical errors, incomplete sequences, and mutated experimental constructs that did not represent endogenous binding sites. Duplicate interactions across and within datasets were identified and removed through manual verification using PubMed identifiers and original publications. All retained interactions were required to have direct functional validation based on reporter assays, ensuring that each interaction reflects experimentally supported regulatory activity rather than purely predictive evidence. Filtering criteria were independent of binding mode; both canonical and non-canonical interactions were retained provided they were supported by sequence-resolved functional evidence. Interactions lacking reliable sequence mapping or experimental validation were excluded regardless of seed pairing characteristics.

To benchmark miRVerified against an independent reference dataset, we assessed enrichment of conserved miRNA–mRNA interactions using TargetScanHuman as an external standard. A Fisher’s exact test was performed using a background defined as the Cartesian product of all unique miRNAs and genes observed across both miRVerified and TargetScanHuman datasets. This approach defines a biologically constrained interaction space while avoiding bias toward either dataset. Using this framework, miRVerified interactions showed strong and statistically significant enrichment for conserved TargetScanHuman interactions (odds ratio = 33.41, p < 10−300). This indicates that miRVerified preferentially captures evolutionarily conserved miRNA-mRNA interactions relative to expectations under a neutral pairing model and provides independent support for the biological relevance of the curated dataset.

While enrichment against TargetScanHuman provides independent support for the biological relevance of miRVerified interactions, several limitations should be noted. First, TargetScan predictions are based primarily on canonical seed pairing and evolutionary conservation, and therefore may underrepresent non-canonical or experimentally validated interactions. As a result, enrichment estimates may be biased toward seed-dependent interactions. Second, the Fisher’s exact test assumes independence between interactions, although miRNA-gene networks contain inherent dependencies due to shared regulators and target overlap. Third, the interaction background was defined as the Cartesian product of observed miRNAs and genes across both datasets, which provides a constrained null model but does not represent the full biological space of possible interactions. Finally, incomplete experimental coverage in both miRVerified and TargetScanHuman may influence overlap estimates. Despite these limitations, the consistent and strong enrichment observed supports the robustness of miRVerified as a high-confidence dataset of experimentally validated miRNA-mRNA interactions.

Usage Notes

The miRVerified dataset is provided as a sequence-resolved, experimentally validated resource for miRNA-mRNA interactions in Homo sapiens. Each entry includes the miRNA identifier, target gene information, and the corresponding 3′ UTR binding sequence mapped to the hg38 reference genome. Users should note that interactions are curated based on functional reporter evidence and may not represent exhaustive coverage of all biological interactions. The dataset is therefore intended for high-confidence applications such as benchmarking computational prediction methods, training machine learning models, and mechanistic studies requiring precise binding site definition. Up-regulatory miRNA-mRNA interactions are included where experimentally validated and should be interpreted separately from canonical repressive interactions. Genomic coordinates are provided for compatibility with downstream sequence-based analyses, and users should ensure consistent genome build usage (hg38) when integrating external datasets. Benchmarking analyses were performed using TargetScanHuman (release 8.0) conserved interactions as an external reference dataset to assess evolutionary conservation enrichment.

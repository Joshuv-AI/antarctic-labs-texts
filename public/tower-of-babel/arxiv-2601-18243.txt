Abstract
. The goal of this paper is to propose and carry out a program which allows us to yield a larger target quantum group of Drinfeld-Jimbo type via grafting two given smaller ones. To this effect, we first set up some basics of a multi-tensor product theory of the generalized double-bosonization construction (see [ HH2 ] for the latter). Such a grafting method, depending on the choice of grafting node in the Dynkin diagram, including rank-induction and type-crossing constructions as special cases, provides a one-stop resolving strategy of the generation question of Majid’s quantum tree, which grows up from the root part U q ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 ) U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}) and decorates with the quantum groups of various Dynkin diagrams (of finite types) as the branches.

2.1.1.
Majid’s weakly quasitriagular dual pair

Majid
[
M2
]
proposed the concept of a weakly quasi-triangular dual pair via his insight on some examples on
matched pairs of bialgebras or Hopf algebras.

A weakly quasitriangular dual pair
is a pair
(H,A)
of bialgebras or Hopf algebras equipped with a dual pairing

\langle\,,\,\rangle:H\otimes A\rightarrow k

and convolution-invertible algebra / anti-coalgebra maps
\mathcal{R},\bar{\mathcal{R}}:A\rightarrow H
obeying

(1)

\langle\bar{\mathcal{R}}(a),b\rangle=\langle\mathcal{R}^{-1}(b),a\rangle,\quad\partial^{R}_{h}=\mathcal{R}\ast(\partial^{L}_{h})\ast\mathcal{R}^{-1},\quad\partial^{R}_{h}=\bar{\mathcal{R}}\ast(\partial^{L}_{h})\ast\bar{\mathcal{R}}^{-1}

for

a,b\in A,\ h\in H
.
Here,

(\partial^{L}_{h})(a):=\langle h_{(1)},a\rangle h_{(2)},

(\partial^{R}_{h})(a):=h_{(1)}\langle h_{(2)},a\rangle

are left, right “differentiation operators” regarded as maps
A\rightarrow H
for any fixed
h
.

A quasitriangular Hopf algebra is a Hopf algebra
(H,m,\eta,\Delta,\epsilon,S)

with a universal
R
-matrix
\mathscr{R}
,
which is an invertible element in
H\otimes H
obeying:

\Delta^{\text{cop}}(a)=(\mathscr{R})\Delta(a)(\mathscr{R}^{-1}),\,a\in H,\quad(\Delta\otimes\text{id})(\mathscr{R})=\mathscr{R}_{13}\mathscr{R}_{23},\quad(\text{id}\otimes\Delta)(\mathscr{R})=\mathscr{R}_{13}\mathscr{R}_{12},

where

\mathscr{R}_{12}=\mathscr{R}^{1}\otimes\mathscr{R}^{2}\otimes 1
,

\mathscr{R}_{13}=\mathscr{R}^{1}\otimes 1\otimes\mathscr{R}^{2}
,

\mathscr{R}_{23}=1\otimes\mathscr{R}^{1}\otimes\mathscr{R}^{2}
for
\mathscr{R}=\mathscr{R}^{1}\otimes\mathscr{R}^{2}
.
An important example of quasitriangular Hopf algebras is
the (resp.,
h
-adic) Drinfeld-Jimbo algebra
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
(resp.,
U_{h}(\mathfrak{g}))
associated with a finite-dimensional complex simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
Denote simple roots, positive root system of
\mathfrak{g}
by
\alpha_{j}
,
\Delta_{+}
, respectively.
Let
(a_{ij})
be the Cartan matrix of
\mathfrak{g}
,

(B_{ij})
be the inverse of the matrix
\left(\frac{2}{(\alpha_{j},\alpha_{j})}a_{ij}\right)
.
The
q
-exponential function
\text{exp}_{q}x
is defined by
\text{exp}_{q}x=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{r(r+1)/2}}{[r]_{q}!}x^{r}

and
[n]_{q}=\frac{q^{n}-q^{-n}}{q-q^{-1}}
,

[n]_{q}!=\prod_{k=1}^{n}[k]_{q}
.
Then an explicit form of the universal
R
-matrix of
U_{h}(\mathfrak{g})
(
[
KS
]
) is as follows:

(2)

\mathscr{R}=\text{exp}\Bigl(h\sum\limits_{i,j}B_{ij}(H_{i}\otimes H_{j})\Bigr)\prod\limits_{\beta\in\Delta_{+}}\text{exp}_{q_{\beta}}\left((1-q_{\beta}^{-2})(E_{\beta}\otimes F_{\beta})\right).

Dually,
a coquasitriangular (or dual quasitriangular) Hopf algebra
(A,\mathfrak{r})
is a Hopf algebra
A
equipped with a convolution-invertible linear form
\mathfrak{r}:A\otimes A\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}
satisfying:

(3)

\mathfrak{r}\circ(\cdot\otimes\text{id})=\mathfrak{r}_{13}\ast\mathfrak{r}_{23},\quad\mathfrak{r}\circ(\text{id}\otimes\cdot)=\mathfrak{r}_{13}\ast\mathfrak{r}_{12},\quad\cdot\circ\tau=\mathfrak{r}\ast\cdot\ast\mathfrak{r}^{-1}.

Here,

\mathfrak{r}_{12}(a\otimes b\otimes c)=\mathfrak{r}(a\otimes b)\epsilon(c)
,

\mathfrak{r}_{13}(a\otimes b\otimes c)=\mathfrak{r}(a\otimes c)\epsilon(b)
,

\mathfrak{r}_{23}(a\otimes b\otimes c)=\mathfrak{r}(b\otimes c)\epsilon(a)
.

When the
R
-matrix
R
satisfies the following
FRT-condition
(cf.
[
FRT
]
):

(4)

(R^{-1})^{t_{1}}P(R^{t_{2}})^{-1}PK_{0}=\textrm{const}\cdot K_{0},

there corresponds a Hopf algebra
H_{R}
,
which is generated by
1
and
t^{i}_{j},\,\widetilde{t}^{i}_{j},(i,j=1,\cdots,n)
,
with relations

\displaystyle RT_{1}T_{2}=T_{2}T_{1}R,\quad R^{t}\widetilde{T}_{1}\widetilde{T}_{2}=\widetilde{T}_{2}\widetilde{T}_{1}R^{t},\quad(R^{t_{2}})^{-1}T_{1}\widetilde{T}_{2}=\widetilde{T}_{2}T_{1}(R^{t_{2}})^{-1};

\displaystyle\Delta(T)=T\otimes T,\quad\Delta(\widetilde{T})=\widetilde{T}\otimes\widetilde{T},\quad\epsilon(T)=\epsilon(\widetilde{T})=\textbf{I};

\displaystyle S(T)=(\tilde{T})^{t},\quad S(\tilde{T})=DT^{t}D^{-1}.

Here,

(K_{0})^{ij}_{kl}:=\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}
,

D=\textrm{tr}_{2}(P((R^{t_{2}})^{-1})^{t_{1}})\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
,
and
\textrm{tr}_{2}
denotes taking the partial matrix trace in the second factor in the tensor product
\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Starting from a minuscule finite-dimensional irreducible representation
T_{V}
of
U_{h}(\mathfrak{g})
on the module

we proved in

Also,

There are two elements in the

\displaystyle\sigma=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccccccc}1&2&3&4&\cdots&2k-1&2k&\cdots&2n-1&2n\\

\displaystyle\tau=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccccccccc}1&2&\cdots&k&\cdots&n&n+1&n+2&\cdots&n+k&\cdots&2n\\

Then

,

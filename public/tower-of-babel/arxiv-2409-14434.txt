The sparseness of g-convex functions
Yu Wang, Ke Ye

Abstract
. The g-convexity of functions on manifolds is a generalization of the convexity of functions on ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} . It plays an essential role in both differential geometry and non-convex optimization theory. This paper is concerned with g-convex smooth functions on manifolds. We establish criteria for the existence of a Riemannian metric (or connection) with respect to which a given function is g-convex. Using these criteria, we obtain three sparseness results for g-convex functions: (1) The set of g-convex functions on a compact manifold is nowhere dense in the space of smooth functions. (2) Most polynomials on ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} that is g-convex with respect to some geodesically complete connection has at most one critical point. (3) The density of g-convex univariate (resp. quadratic, monomial, additively separable) polynomials asymptotically decreases to zero.

Main results

This paper consists of two parts. The first part is concerned with Problem

2.2.1.
Connection

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a smooth manifold and let
\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})
be the space of vector fields on
M
. A
connection
on
\mathcal{M}
is a smooth map

\nabla:\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})\times\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})\to\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M}),\quad\nabla_{X}Y\coloneqq\nabla(X,Y)

such that for any
f_{1},f_{2},f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
,
X_{1},X_{2},Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})
and
a\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{f_{1}X_{1}+f_{2}X_{2}}Y

\displaystyle=f_{1}\nabla_{X_{1}}Y+f_{2}\nabla_{X_{2}}Y,\quad\nabla_{X}(aY)=a\nabla_{X}Y,

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}(fY)

\displaystyle=X(f)Y+f\nabla_{X}Y,\quad\nabla_{X}Y-\nabla_{Y}X=[X,Y].

Any connection
\nabla
is naturally extended for differential forms and higher order tensor fields on
\mathcal{M}
. Thus, for each integer
k\geq 1
, it makes sense to define
\nabla^{k}
as the operator obtained by applying
\nabla

k
-times.

Lemma 2.1

(gluing lemma)
.

[
30
, proposition4.5]

Let
\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be an open covering of
\mathcal{M}
and let
\{\rho_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a partition of unity subordinate to
\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
. Suppose that for each
i\in I
,
\nabla_{i}
is a connection on
U_{i}
. Then
\nabla:=\sum\rho_{i}\widetilde{\nabla}_{i}
is a connection on
M
, where for each
i\in I
,
\widetilde{\nabla}_{i}
is defined by

\widetilde{\nabla}_{i}(X,Y)_{p}=\begin{cases}\nabla_{i}(X,Y)_{p}&\text{~if~}p\in U_{i}\\
0&\text{~otherwise~}\end{cases}.

The
curvature tensor
of
\nabla
is defined as

R:\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})\times\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})\times\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})\to\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M}),\quad R(X,Y,Z):=\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z-\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z.

In particular, for any
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})
, we have a linear map

R(X,Y,\cdot):\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})\to\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M}),\quad Z\mapsto R(X,Y,Z).

Let
\gamma:(0,1)\to\mathcal{M}
be a smooth curve on
\mathcal{M}
. If
\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}(t)}\dot{\gamma}(t)=0
, then
\gamma
is called a
geodesic
, where
\dot{\gamma}
denotes the tangent vector field of
\gamma
. For each
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
, the
Hessian
of
f
with respect to
\nabla
is

\Hess_{\nabla}f:\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})\times\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})\to\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}),\quad\Hess_{\nabla}f(X,Y)\coloneqq X(Y(f))-(\nabla_{X}Y)(f).

2.2.2.
Riemannian geometry

If
(\mathcal{M},\mathsf{g})
is a pseudo-Riemannian manifold, the
Levi-Civita connection
of
\mathsf{g}
is the unique connection
\nabla
on
\mathcal{M}
such that

Z(\mathsf{g}(X,Y))=\mathsf{g}(\nabla_{Z}X,Y)+\mathsf{g}(X,\nabla_{Z}Y)

for any
X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})
. To indicate the dependence of
\nabla
on
\mathsf{g}
, we decorate it as
\nabla_{\mathsf{g}}
. Given
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
, the
pseudo-Riemannian gradient
of
f
is the unique
\grad_{\mathsf{g}}f\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})
such that

\mathsf{g}(\grad_{\mathsf{g}}f,X)=X(f)

for any
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})
. The
pseudo-Riemannian Hessian
of
f
is
\Hess_{\nabla_{\mathsf{g}}}f
, abbreviated as
\Hess_{\mathsf{g}}f
. Moreover, if we equip
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the standard Euclidean metric, then we denote by
\nabla_{\mathsf{e}}
,
\grad_{\mathsf{e}}f
and
\Hess_{\mathsf{e}}f
the corresponding connection, gradient and Hessian, respectively. In this case,
\grad_{\mathsf{e}}f
and
\Hess_{\mathsf{e}}f
coincide with their counterparts in calculus.

2.2.3.
Calculations in a local chart

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an
n
-dimensional manifold with a connection
\nabla
. In a local chart
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}):\mathbb{R}^{n}\to U
of
\mathcal{M}
, there exist
\Gamma^{k}_{ij}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
,
1\leq i,j,k\leq n
, which are called the
Christoffel symbols
of
\nabla
, such that

\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Gamma^{k}_{ij}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}.

According to
[
30
, Lemma 4.4]
,
\nabla
is uniquely determined by
\Gamma^{k}_{ij}
’s. Clearly,
\Gamma^{k}_{ij}=\Gamma^{k}_{ji}
for all
1\leq i,j,k,\leq n
.

The
curvature tensor

R
of
\nabla
in
U
is determined by

(1)

R\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}},\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}},\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\right)=\sum_{l=1}^{n}R_{ijk}^{l}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{l}},\quad R_{ijk}^{l}\coloneqq\frac{\partial\Gamma_{jk}^{l}}{\partial x_{i}}-\frac{\partial\Gamma_{ik}^{l}}{\partial x_{j}}+\sum_{t=1}^{n}(\Gamma_{jk}^{t}\Gamma_{it}^{l}-\Gamma_{ik}^{t}\Gamma_{jt}^{l})

for
1\leq i,j,k\leq n
.

For each
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(U)
, we may represent
\Hess_{\nabla}f
as an
n\times n
matrix whose
(i,j)
-th element is
\Hess_{\nabla}f(\partial/\partial x_{i},\partial/\partial x_{j})
,
1\leq i,j\leq n
.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
50
, Section 1.3]

Let
\nabla
be a connection on an open subset
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
\{\Gamma_{ij}^{k}:1\leq i,j,k\leq n\}
be the corresponding Christoffel symbols. For any
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

\Hess_{\nabla}f\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}},\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\right)=\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}-\sum_{k}\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{k}},\quad 1\leq i\leq j\leq n.

If
\nabla=\nabla_{\mathsf{g}}
for some pseudo-Riemannian metric
\mathsf{g}
, then in
U
we can write
\mathsf{g}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\mathsf{g}_{ij}dx_{i}\otimes dx_{j}
and the
Riemannian gradient
of
f
together with its derivatives can be calculated by

(2)

\grad_{\mathsf{g}}f=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}\mathsf{g}^{ij}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}},\quad\frac{\partial\mathsf{g}_{ij}}{\partial x_{k}}=\sum_{l=1}^{n}\Gamma_{ik}^{l}g_{jl}+\sum_{l=1}^{n}\Gamma_{jk}^{l}\mathsf{g}_{il}.

where
(\mathsf{g}^{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}
is the inverse of the matrix
(\mathsf{g}_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a manifold with a connection

with respect to

Theorem 2.3
.

[
50

Assume

\mathcal{M}
. For

, the following are equivalent:

(a)

f
is g-convex with respect to

.

(b)

f(x)+\dot{\gamma}(f(x))\leq f(y)
for any

, where

is the geodesic connecting

(c)

\Hess_{\nabla}f(x)\succeq 0

3.
Criteria for g-convexity

This section is concerned with criteria for a function to be g-convex with respect to some connection. We provide two necessary conditions and two sufficient conditions.

Proposition 3.1

(Necessary condition for g-convexity I)
.

If
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is g-convex with respect to some connection, then
\Hess_{\textsf{e}}(f)
is positive semidefinite at every critical point of
f
.

Proof.

Let
\nabla
be a connection on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with respect to which
f
is g-convex. By Lemma
2.2
, we have

\Hess_{\textsf{e}}f\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}},\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\right)=\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}=\Hess_{\nabla}f\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}},\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\right)+\sum_{k}\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{k}},\quad 1\leq i\leq j\leq n.

Therefore, if
p
is a critical point of
f
, then
\Hess_{\textsf{e}}(f)_{p}=\Hess_{\nabla}(f)_{p}\succeq 0
by condition (
c
) in Theorem
2.3
.
∎

Next we consider sufficient conditions.

Proposition 3.2

(Sufficient condition for g-convexity I)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an
n
-dimensional manifold and let
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
. If
f
has no critical points, then for any symmetric
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M)
-bilinear map
A:\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})\times\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})\to\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
, there exists a connection
\nabla
such that
\Hess_{\nabla}(f)=A
. In particular, any smooth function with no critical point is g-convex with respect to some connection.

Proof.

Let
\mathsf{g}
be a Riemannian metric on
M
and let
\{U_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
be an open cover of
M
such that each
U_{\alpha}
admits a coordinate map
x^{\alpha}:U_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Here
\Lambda
is some index set and the existence of
\mathsf{g}
is guaranteed by
[
31
, Proposition 13.3]
. We pick a smooth partition of unity
\{\phi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
subordinate to
\{U_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
and define
A^{\alpha}\coloneqq A|_{U_{\alpha}}
for each
\alpha\in\Lambda
. By definition, we have
\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\phi_{\alpha}A^{\alpha}=A
. We observe that if there is a connection
\nabla^{\alpha}
such that
\Hess_{\nabla^{\alpha}}(f|_{U_{\alpha}})=A^{\alpha}
for each
\alpha\in\Lambda
, then
\nabla\coloneqq\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\phi_{\alpha}\nabla^{\alpha}
is a connection on
M
satisfying
\Hess_{\nabla}(f)=A
by Lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
.

Therefore, it is sufficient to assume that
M=U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an open subset. In this situation, we may identify
A
with
(a_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(U,\mathsf{S}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
where

a_{ij}\coloneqq A\left(\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}},\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\right)\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(U),\quad 1\leq i,j\leq n.

We denote
f_{i}\coloneqq\partial f/\partial x_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
and consider the following system of homogeneous linear equations:

(3)

\sum_{k=1}^{n}f_{k}S_{ij}^{k}=0,\quad S_{ij}^{k}-S_{ji}^{k}=0,\quad 1\leq i,j\leq n.

For each solution
\{S^{k}_{ij}:1\leq i,j,k\leq n\}
of (
3
), we define

(4)

\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\coloneqq\sum_{l=1}^{n}\frac{(f_{ij}-a_{ij})\mathsf{g}^{kl}f_{l}}{\lVert\grad_{\mathsf{g}}(f)\rVert^{2}}+S_{ij}^{k},

where
f_{ij}\coloneqq\partial^{2}f/\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}
for
1\leq i,j\leq n
. Since
f
has no critical point,
\lVert\grad_{\mathsf{g}}(f)(x)\rVert>0
. Hence
\Gamma^{k}_{ij}
’s are well-defined. It is straightforward to verify that

(5)

\Gamma^{k}_{ij}=\Gamma^{k}_{ji},\quad\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{k}}=a_{ij}\quad 1\leq i,j\leq n.

Let
\nabla
be the connection determined by
\{\Gamma^{k}_{ij}:1\leq i,j,k\leq n\}
. Then Lemma
2.2
together with (
5
) implies
\Hess_{\nabla}(f)=A
and this completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.4

(Sufficient condition for g-convexity II)
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\mathsf{g})
be a Riemannian manifold and let
f
be a smooth function on
\mathcal{M}
. Suppose that for any critical point
x\in\mathcal{M}
of
f
, there exists a neighbourhood
U
of
x
such that
\hess_{\mathsf{g}}f(y)\succeq 0
for all
y\in U
. Then there exists a connection
\nabla
such that
f
is g-convex with respect to
\nabla
.

Proof.

Let
C_{f}
be the set of critical points of
f
and let
V\coloneqq\mathcal{M}\setminus C_{f}
. For each
x\in C_{f}
, we choose and fix an open neighbourhood
U_{x}
on which
\hess_{\mathsf{g}}f
is positive semidefinite.

By definition, we have
C_{f}=df^{-1}(0)
where
df
is the differential of
f
regarded as a smooth map
df:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{T}^{\ast}\mathcal{M}
and
0
denotes the zero section of the vector bundle
\mathbb{T}^{\ast}\mathcal{M}
. In particular, we may conclude that
V
is an open subset of
\mathcal{M}
. Thus, the family
\{U_{x}:x\in C_{f}\}\cup\{V\}
is an open covering of
M
. Let
\{\eta_{x}:x\in C_{f}\}\cup\{\eta_{V}\}
be a partition of unity subordinate to
\{U_{x}:x\in C_{f}\}\cup\{V\}
.

On each
U_{x}
, we define a connection
\nabla_{x}\coloneqq\nabla_{\mathsf{g}}|_{U_{x}}
. Then
\hess_{\nabla_{x}}f(y)\succeq 0
for all
y\in U_{x}
. Since
V
is an open submanifold of
\mathcal{M}

Lemma 5.1

(b)

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that

Proposition 5.10

6.
g-convex quadratic polynomials

Next we consider g-convex quadratic polynomials. Given a quadratic polynomial

(12)

f(x)=\dfrac{1}{2}x^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}Ax+b^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}x+c\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2},

where
A\in\mathsf{S}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
c\in\mathbb{R}
and
x,b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
are column vectors. Then

(13)

\grad_{\textsf{e}}f=Ax+b,\quad\Hess_{\textsf{e}}f=A.

Lemma 6.1
.

A quadratic polynomial
f\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2}
parametrized as in (
12
) is g-convex with respect to some connection if and only if
A\succeq 0
or
\rank\left(\begin{bmatrix}A&b\end{bmatrix}\right)>\rank(A)
.

Proof.

If
f
is g-convex with respect to some connection and
\rank\left(\begin{bmatrix}A&b\end{bmatrix}\right)=\rank(A)
, we must have
A\succeq 0
by Proposition
3.1
and (
13
). Conversely, if
A\succeq 0
, then
f
is g-convex with respect to the Euclidean metric. If
\rank\left(\begin{bmatrix}A&b\end{bmatrix}\right)>\rank(A)
, then (
13
) implies that
f
has no critical point and
f
is g-convex with respect to some connection by Proposition
3.2
.
∎

Next we characterize quadratic polynomials that are g-convex with respect to some pseudo-Riemannian metric. According to Lemma
6.1
, it is sufficient to consider
f\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2}
with
\rank\left(\begin{bmatrix}A&b\end{bmatrix}\right)>\rank(A)
.

Lemma 6.2

(Normal form)
.

If
f\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2}
with
\rank\left(\begin{bmatrix}A&b\end{bmatrix}\right)>\rank(A)\eqqcolon r
, then there exist
Q\in\O_{n}(\mathbb{R})
and
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

(14)

f(Q^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}(y-v))=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mu_{i}y_{i}^{2}+\sum_{j=r+1}^{n}\nu_{j}y_{j}+\kappa

where
\kappa,\mu_{i},\nu_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
,
1\leq i\leq r<j\leq n
are constants such that
\mu_{i}\neq 0
and
\nu_{j_{0}}\neq 0
for all
1\leq i\leq r
and some
r+1\leq j_{0}\leq n
.

Proof.

By the theory of bilinear forms
[
19
, Section 4 Chapter X]
, there exists
Q\in\O_{n}(\mathbb{R})
such that

f(Q^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}z)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mu_{i}z_{i}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}b^{\prime}_{j}z_{j}+c
for some
b^{\prime}\coloneqq(b^{\prime}_{1},\dots,b^{\prime}_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mu_{i}\neq 0,i=1,\dots,r
. Denote

v\coloneqq\left(\frac{b^{\prime}_{1}}{2\mu_{1}},\frac{b^{\prime}_{2}}{2\mu_{2}},\dots,\frac{b^{\prime}_{r}}{2\mu_{r}},\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{(n-r)\text{~copies}}\right)^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}},\quad\kappa=c-\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{{b^{\prime}_{i}}^{2}}{4\mu_{i}},\quad\nu_{j}=b^{\prime}_{j},\quad r+1\leq j\leq n.

It is straightforward to verify that
f(Q^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}(y-v))
can be written as (
14
). We notice that
F(y)\coloneqq f(Q^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}(y-v))
has no critical point, since
f
has no critical point. Hence

\grad_{\textsf{e}}F(y)=\diag(2\mu_{1}y_{1},\dots,2\mu_{r}y_{r},\nu_{r+1},\dots,\nu_{n})=0

has no solution, which implies
\nu_{j_{0}}\neq 0
for some
r+1\leq j_{0}\leq n
.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
0\leq p\leq n
be an integer. If
f\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq 2}
has no critical point, then
f
is g-convex with respect to a pseudo-Riemannian metric of signature
(p,n-p)
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that

f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mu_{i}x_{i}^{2}+\sum_{j=r+1}^{n}\nu_{j}x_{j}+\kappa,

where
\kappa,\mu_{i},\nu_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
,
1\leq i\leq r<j\leq n
are constants such that
\nu_{n}\neq 0
and
\mu_{i}\neq 0
for all
1\leq i\leq r
.

We claim that there exist
\Gamma_{lm}^{k}
’s such that the corresponding connection
\nabla
satisfies

(15)

R=0,\quad\Hess_{\nabla}f=0.

Here
R
denotes the curvature tensor of
\nabla
. Then the Lie subalgebra
\mathcal{L}_{k}
defined in (
6
) is zero for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and Corollary
4.2
implies that
\nabla=\nabla_{\mathfrak{g}}
for some pseudo-Riemannian metric
\mathsf{g}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of signature
(p,n-p)
. Therefore, it is left to prove the existence of the desired
\Gamma^{k}_{lm}
’s.

We equip
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the Euclidean metric. By (
5
), the Christoffel symbols of a connection
\nabla
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\Hess_{\nabla}f=0
are determined by

(16)

\displaystyle\Gamma^{k}_{ij}-\Gamma^{k}_{ji}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle-2\sum_{k=1}^{r}\Gamma_{st}^{k}\mu_{k}x_{k}-\sum_{k=r+1}^{n}\Gamma_{st}^{k}\nu_{k}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle 2\mu_{i}-2\sum_{k=1}^{r}\Gamma_{ii}^{k}\mu_{k}x_{k}-\sum_{k=r+1}^{n}\Gamma_{ii}^{k}\nu_{k}

\displaystyle=0,

where
1\leq i,j,k\leq n
and
(s,t)\not\in\{(1,1),\dots,(r,r)\}
. We look for a solution of (
16
) in the following form:

(17)

\Gamma_{ij}^{k}=\begin{cases}0&\text{~if~}1\leq k\leq r\\
\text{constant to be determined}&\text{~otherwise~}\end{cases},

where
1\leq i,j\leq n
. Then the first derivative of each
\Gamma_{ij}^{k}
vanishes and (
1
) becomes

R_{stk}^{l}=\sum_{u=r+1}^{n}(\Gamma_{tk}^{u}\Gamma_{su}^{l}-\Gamma_{sk}^{u}\Gamma_{tu}^{l}),\quad 1\leq l,s,t,k\leq n.

Combining (
16

17

R=0
, we obtain

(18)

\Gamma_{ij}^{p}=\sum_{u=r+1}^{n}(\Gamma_{ij}^{u}\Gamma_{lu}^{k}-\Gamma_{lj}^{u}\Gamma_{iu}^{k})=\mu_{p}-\sum_{u=r+1}^{n}\Gamma_{pp}^{u}\nu_{u}=\sum_{u=r+1}^{n}\Gamma_{st}^{u}\nu_{u}=0

for

,

and
(s,t)\not\in\{(1,1),\dots,(r,r)\}
. Let
\Gamma_{pp}^{u}=a^{u}_{p}\in\mathbb{R}
be a solution of the third equation in (

. For each
1\leq i,j,k\leq n

\Gamma^{k}_{ij}\coloneqq\begin{cases}0&\text{~if~}1\leq k\leq r,\;1\leq i,j\leq n\\
0&\text{~if~}r+1\leq k\leq n,\;(i,j)\not\in\{(1,1),\dots,(r,r)\}\\
a^{k}_{i}&\text{~otherwise~}\end{cases}.

By construction,
\{\Gamma^{k}_{ij}\}_{i,j,k=1}^{n}
is a solution of (
18

∎

Lemma 6.6
.

.

Lemma 7.1
.

If
f
is a monomial as in (

,

, such that

is g-convex with respect to some connection and

, then

is even.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume

. Suppose on the contrary that

is odd. Let
\nabla
be the connection such that

is g-convex and
\Gamma^{k}_{ij}
be the Christoffel symbol of
\nabla

2.3

Lemma 7.2
.

Suppose

Lemma 7.3
.

Suppose

and

are positive integers. If the monomial

Proof.

Let
\nabla
be the connection such that
f

. We consider the determinant of the

D\coloneqq\det\left(\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{n-1}^{2}}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{k}}\Gamma_{n-1,n-1}^{k}&\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{n-1}\partial x_{n}}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{k}}\Gamma_{n-1,n}^{k}\\
\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{n-1}\partial x_{n}}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{k}}\Gamma_{n,n-1}^{k}&\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{n}^{2}}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{k}}\Gamma_{n,n}^{k}\end{bmatrix}\right).

By a direct calculation, we obtain

D_{0}(x_{n-1},x_{n})=a^{2}x_{n-1}^{4q_{n-1}-4}x_{n}^{4q_{n}-4}(\eta_{1}x_{n-1}^{2}x_{n}^{2}+\eta_{2}x_{n-1}x_{n}^{4}+\eta_{3}x_{n-1}x_{n}^{4})

for some

\eta_{1}(0,0)=4q_{n}q_{n-1}(1-2q_{n}-2q_{n-1})<0

is a non-negative on
\mathbb{R}^{2}

For a fixed
x_{n}\neq 0

Theorem 7.4

(g-convex monomials)

(

Given positive integers

Theorem 7.5

8.
g-convex additively separable functions

A function
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is
additively separable
if there exists a partition of variables

\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}=\bigsqcup_{t=1}^{s}P_{t}

such that
f=\sum_{t=1}^{s}f_{t}
for some
f_{t}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{P_{t}})
,
1\leq t\leq s
. Here
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{P_{t}})
denotes the space of smooth functions whose variables are contained in the subset
P\subseteq\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
. By permuting variables, we may assume that for each
1\leq t\leq s
,
P_{t}=\{x_{l}:l\in I_{t}\}
where

(22)

I_{t}\coloneqq\{p_{t}+1,p_{t}+2,\dots,p_{t}+n_{t}\}\subseteq\mathbb{N},\quad n_{t}=\#P_{t},\quad 0=p_{1}<p_{2}<\dots<p_{s}<n.

Therefore, it is sufficient to consider additively separable polynomials of such type.

Let
\{1,\dots,n\}=\bigsqcup_{t=1}^{s}I_{t}
be a partition as in (
22
). Suppose that
\mathbb{R}^{n}=\bigoplus_{t=1}^{s}\mathbb{R}^{n_{t}}
is the decomposition of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
according to the partition. For any pseudo-Riemannian metric
\mathsf{g}_{t}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n_{t}}
,
1\leq t\leq s
, we denote by
\mathsf{g}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{t=1}^{s}\mathsf{g}_{t}
the induced pseudo-Riemannian metric on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
\Gamma_{ij}^{k}
be the Christorff symbols of
\mathsf{g}
. Then
\Gamma_{ij}^{k}=0
if
i,j,k
are pairwise not in the same
I_{t}
for any
1\leq t\leq s
.

Proof.

By definition, we have

\Gamma_{ij}^{k}=\sum_{v=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2}g^{kv}\left(\frac{\partial g_{iv}}{\partial x_{j}}+\frac{\partial g_{jv}}{\partial x_{i}}-\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial x_{v}}\right)\quad 1\leq i,j,k\leq n.

where the matrix
(g^{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}
is the inverse of
(g_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}
. Since
\mathsf{g}=\bigoplus_{t=1}^{s}\mathsf{g}_{t}
,
(g_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}=\diag(G_{1},\dots,G_{s})
for some
G_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{t}\times n_{t}}
,
1\leq t\leq s
. Suppose
k\in I_{t}
. Then
i,j\not\in I_{t}
and
g_{ij}=g_{iv}=g_{jv}=g^{ku}=0
for any
v\in I_{t}
and
u\not\in I_{t}
. Thus, we obtain

\Gamma_{ij}^{k}=\sum_{v\in I_{t}}\dfrac{1}{2}g^{kv}\left(\frac{\partial g_{iv}}{\partial x_{j}}+\frac{\partial g_{jv}}{\partial x_{i}}\right)=0.

∎

Theorem 8.2

(g-convex additively separable functions)
.

An additively separable function
f=\sum_{t=1}^{s}f_{t}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is g-convex with respect to some connection if and only if one of the following holds:

(a)

f
has no critical point.

(b)

For each
1\leq t\leq s
,
f_{t}
is g-convex with respect to some connection.

Moreover, if for each
1\leq t\leq s
,
f_{t}
is g-convex with respect to some pseudo-Riemmanian metric, then so is
f
.

Proof.

The last assertion follows immediately from Lemma
8.1
. Without loss of generality, we suppose that the partition of variables for
f=\sum_{t=1}^{s}f_{t}
is given as in (
22
). Assume that
f
is g-convex with respect to a connection
\nabla
with Christorff symbols
\Gamma_{ij}^{k}
and
f
has a critical point
u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Since

\grad_{\textsf{e}}f=(\grad_{\textsf{e}}f_{1},\dots,\grad_{\textsf{e}}f_{s}),

we have
\grad_{\mathsf{e}}f_{t}(u^{t})=0
, where
u^{t}\coloneqq(u_{p_{t}+1},\dots,u_{p_{t}+n_{t}})
,
1\leq t\leq s
.

By Lemma
2.2
and Theorem
2.3
, we have

\Hess_{\nabla}f=\diag(\Hess_{\mathsf{e}}f_{1}(x^{1}),\dots,\Hess_{\mathsf{e}}f_{t}(x^{t}))-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{k}}B^{k}(x)\succeq 0,

where for each
1\leq t\leq s
and
1\leq k\leq n
,
x^{t}\coloneqq(x_{p_{t}+1},\dots,x_{p_{t}+n_{t}})
and
B^{k}\coloneqq(\Gamma_{ij}^{k})_{i,j=1}^{n}
. In particular, the function

\displaystyle H(x_{1},\dots,x_{n_{1}})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\Hess_{\nabla}f(x_{1},\dots,x_{n_{1}},u_{2},\dots,u_{n})

\displaystyle=\diag(\Hess_{\mathsf{e}}f_{1}(x^{1}),\Hess_{\mathsf{e}}f_{2}(u^{2}),\dots,\Hess_{\mathsf{e}}f_{t}(u^{t}))-\sum_{k=1}^{n_{1}}\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial x_{k}}B^{k}(x^{1},u^{2},\dots,u^{t})

is an
n\times n
positive semi-definite matrix for all
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n_{1}})\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}
. Let
H_{n_{1}}
be the upper left
n_{1}\times n_{1}
submatrix of
H
. Then

0\preceq H_{n_{1}}=\Hess_{\mathsf{e}}f_{1}-\sum_{k=1}^{n_{1}}\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial x_{k}}B^{k}_{n_{1}},

where
B^{k}_{n_{1}}
is the upper left
n_{1}\times n_{1}
submatrix of
B^{k}
,
1\leq k\leq n_{1}
. By construction of
B^{k}_{n_{1}}
, this implies that
f_{1}
is g-convex with respect to some connection on
\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}
. Similarly, we may prove that
f_{2},\dots,f_{s}
are g-convex with respect to some connection, respectively.

Conversely, if (
a
) holds, then Proposition
3.2
ensures that
f
is g-convex with respect to some connection. Suppose that (
b
) holds. According to Theorem
2.3
, for each
1\leq t\leq s
, there exist Christorff symbols
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{ij}^{k}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{t}})
where
i,j,k\in I_{t}
such that for any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{t}}
,

(23)

H_{t}(y)\coloneqq\Hess_{\mathsf{e}}f_{t}(y)-\sum_{v=1}^{n_{t}}\frac{\partial f_{t}}{\partial y_{v}}(y)\widetilde{B}^{v}\succeq 0,

where
\widetilde{B}^{v}\coloneqq(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{ij}^{v+p_{t}}),i,j\in I_{t},1\leq v\leq n_{t}
. We define

\Gamma_{ij}^{k}(x^{1},\dots,x^{s})\coloneqq\begin{cases}\widetilde{\Gamma}_{ij}^{k}(x^{t})&\quad i,j,k\in I_{t},\;1\leq t\leq s\\
0&\quad\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

where
x^{t}\coloneqq(x_{p_{t}+1},\dots,x_{p_{t}+n_{t}})
,
1\leq t\leq s
. Let
\nabla
be the connection defined by
\Gamma_{ij}^{k}
’s. Then clearly we have

\hess_{\nabla}f(x^{1},\dots,x^{s})=\diag(H_{1}(x^{1}),\dots,H_{s}(x^{s}))\succeq 0

and this completes the proof.
∎

Remark 8.3
.

We have the following observations about Theorem
8.2
.

\diamond

For an additively separable function
f=\sum_{t=1}^{s}f_{t}
, it is clear that

\Hess_{\textsf{e}}f(x^{1},\dots,x^{s})=\diag(\Hess_{\textsf{e}}f_{1}(x^{1}),\dots,\Hess_{\textsf{e}}f_{s}(x^{s})).

Therefore,
f
is convex if and only if each
f_{t}
is g-convex. However, Theorem
8.2
indicates that for an g-convex additively separable function, either all its summands are also g-convex, or the function has no critical point. Example
8.4
below shows that the latter situation indeed occurs, in which case summands are not necessarily g-convex.

\diamond

In general, for each
1\leq t\leq s
,
\mathbb{R}^{n_{t}}
is a totally geodesic submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
equipped with the Euclidean metric. This ensures the equivalence between the g-convexity of an additively separable function and that of its summands. However, if we equip
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with an arbitrary Riemannian metric, then
\mathbb{R}^{n_{t}}
is not necessarily a totally geodesic submanifold anymore and this results in (
a
) of Theorem
8.2
.

\diamond

Theorem
8.2
holds for any smooth additively separable function. Moreover, condition (
a
) is clearly equivalent to that for some
1\leq t\leq s
,
f_{t}
has no critical point.

Example 8.4
.

We consider
f=x_{1}^{3}+x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},x_{2}]
. Then
\grad_{\mathsf{e}}f=(3x_{1}^{2},1)^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}
which is nonzero for all
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Thus by Proposition
3.2
,
f
is g-convex with respect to some connection. On the other side,
f
is additively separable since
f(x_{1},x_{2})=f_{1}(x_{1})+f_{2}(x_{2})
where
f_{1}(x_{1})=x_{1}^{3},f_{2}(x_{2})=x_{2}
. According to Theorem
5.5
,
f_{1}
is not g-convex with respect to any connection.

As an application, we apply Theorem
8.2
to additively separable polynomials. Given positive integers
n
and
d
and a positive real number
r
, we denote

S_{n,d}(r)\coloneqq\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}:f_{j}=\sum_{k=0}^{d}a_{jk}x_{j}^{k}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{j}]_{\leq d},\;|a_{jk}|\leq r,\;1\leq j\leq n,\;1\leq k\leq d\right\}.

We recall from (
11
) that

\left\{\sum_{k=0}^{d}a_{jk}x_{j}^{k}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{j}]_{\leq d},\;|a_{jk}|\leq r,\;1\leq k\leq d\right\}\simeq[-r,r]^{d+1}

for each
1\leq j\leq n
. This leads to
S_{n,d}(r)\simeq[-r,r]^{dn}\times[-nr,nr]
. Moreover,
S_{n,d}(\infty)\coloneqq\bigcup_{r>0}S_{n,d}(r)
consists of additively separable polynomials of degree at most
d
.

Theorem 8.5

(Dimension and density IV)
.

For any positive integers
n
and

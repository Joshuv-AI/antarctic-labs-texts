A comparative quantitative assessment of 3D-printed PEKK and PEEK thin meshes in customized alveolar bone augmentation
Authors: Xin-Rui Zhu, Chang Chen, Yun-Wei Hua, Xiao-Ying Xu, Ping Song, Rui-Yong Wang, Chen-Xi Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveCustomized nonabsorbable membranes are widely used in severe alveolar bone defects and provide sufficient and precise regenerated bone tissue for subsequent dental implant placement. Although 3D-printed polyetheretherketone (PEEK) meshes have confirmed successful use in clinical cases, the performance of a PEEK mesh is not satisfactory. Compared with PEEK, polyetherketoneketone (PEKK) has better mechanical and processing properties. However, whether PEKK is suitable for making customized membranes remains unclear. The objectives of this study were (1) to evaluate the printing precision, surface characteristics, mechanical characteristics and biocompatibility of the PEKK mesh and (2) to compare the properties of the PEKK and PEEK meshes.Materials and methodsBoth PEKK and PEEK meshes were designed and manufactured via additive manufacturing technology combined with computer-aided design (CAD). The printing precision was evaluated with a high-resolution extraoral scanner. The surface characteristics were evaluated with a contact angle system and three-dimensional optical microscopy. The mechanical characteristics were evaluated via three-point bending tests and tensile tests. The biocompatibility was evaluated with a CCK-8 assay, live/dead viability assay and qRT-PCT.ResultsCompared with the PEEK mesh, the PEKK mesh exhibited better control in terms of the thickness and aperture area. Both the PEKK mesh and the PEEK mesh had a hydrophobic surface, but the PEKK mesh had a smoother surface. Compared with the PEEK mesh, the PEKK mesh has better compression and tensile properties. Both the PEKK mesh and the PEEK mesh had good biocompatibility. The proliferation of cells on the PEKK mesh was slightly lower than that on the PEEK mesh.ConclusionsCompared with PEEK mesh, PEKK mesh has greater printing accuracy, smoother surfaces, better mechanical properties and similar biocompatibility and is expected to be used in the production of customized barrier membranes for the augmentation of severe bone defects. To ensure the stability of the mesh for clinical application, it is best to control the aperture diameter of the PEKK mesh to less than 2 mm with a thickness of 0.2 μm.

Introduction

Dental implantation is currently one of the most common ways to restore missing teeth. The resorption and atrophy of alveolar bone caused by tooth loss is an important challenge for clinical implant placement [1]. Previous studies have reported several clinical techniques for alveolar bone augmentation. Among them, guided bone regeneration (GBR) is currently one of the most commonly used methods because of its simplicity and osteogenic stability [2]. The success of GBR is largely dependent on the barrier membrane used to separate soft and hard tissues, which provides a stable space for bone regeneration [3]. Therefore, a suitable barrier membrane should include biocompatibility, tissue integration, space maintenance capabilities, and clinically operable properties [4, 5]. Currently, the absorbable collagen membrane is the most commonly used barrier membrane and can meet the bone augmentation needs of most patients. However, for patients with severe bone defects, maintaining a stable osteogenic space is difficult [6]. With the development of three-dimensional (3D) printing manufacturing technology, customized nonabsorbable membranes with grid structures and excellent mechanical properties have been adopted by an increasing number of clinicians as important tools for the accurate reconstruction of moderate to severe alveolar bone defects [7].

Titanium mesh, which is based on digital simulation design and selective laser melting (SLM) additive manufacturing, has become the most popular nonabsorbable customized GBR barrier membrane. Titanium and its alloys have high mechanical strength and precise printing reduction, which is beneficial for the thin and porous structure of the barriers. Therefore, customized titanium mesh can better adapt to the contour of the alveolar bone, increase the blood supply, and improve the speed and accuracy of bone grafting [8, 9]. However, since the stiffness of titanium and its alloys is significantly greater than that of natural bone tissue, previous research has shown that the exposure rate of customized titanium mesh is as high as 60%, which limits its clinical application [10]. In addition, the stress shielding effect of titanium and its alloys inhibits osteogenesis [11]. Therefore, biomaterials with a certain elastic modulus and mechanical properties closer to those of bone tissue are expected to be used to replace titanium alloys to make the barrier membrane required for GBR surgery.

High-performance nonabsorbable polymer materials with excellent machinability and good physicochemical properties have become a popular research topic for replacing titanium to fabricate of customized barrier membranes. Among these materials, polyaryletherketone (PAEK) is stand-out thermoplastic composite due to its high chemical resistance and ultrahigh mechanical properties. The main component of PAEK, polyetheretherketone (PEEK), is widely used in 3D printing of dental materials, including customized barrier membranes [12]. Research has shown that a PEEK mesh has good biocompatibility as a barrier membrane in GBR [13, 14]. However, owing to its thermal processing properties and lower Young’s modulus, PEEK meshes are often thicker and have lower porosity than titanium meshes do, which is considered to be detrimental to bone regeneration [15].

Similar to PEEK, polyetherketoneketone (PEKK) is another PAEK polymer but more difficult to process, which makes it less commonly used in dentistry. However, the Young’s modulus of PEKK is greater than that of PEEK and is closer to that of bone tissue [16]. In addition, the thermoplasticity of PEKK is significantly better than that of PEEK. These characteristics render the PEKK mesh a potential material for making better 3D-printed customized GBR barrier membranes. Research on personalized titanium mesh has shown that good bone regeneration results can be achieved when the thickness of the titanium mesh is between 0.2 and 0.5 mm and when the porosity is between 20% and 50%. Our former finite element research has shown that the PEKK mesh is able to resist the deformation caused by occlusal force under those conditions [17]. With the development of additive manufacturing technology, PEKK is able to form thinner and more porous scaffold structures. However, it remains unclear whether the 3D-printed PEKK mesh can meet the requirements for making customized GBR barrier membranes. Therefore, the aim of this study is to assess the printing accuracy, physical properties and biocompatibility of the PEKK mesh compared with those of the PEEK mesh and to provide a reference for the design and production of PEKK meshes for GBR surgery in patients with severe bone defects.

Materials and methods

Design and fabrication of the PEKK mesh and PEEK mesh

The present study was approved by the Clinical Research Ethics Committee of the Peking University School and Hospital of Stomatology. Both the PEKK mesh and the PEEK mesh were fabricated via an FDM 3D printer (FUNMAT PRO 410, INTAMISYS, Shanghai, China) with medical-grade pure PEKK and PEEK (KaiSheng New Materials Co., Ltd., Shandong, China). The molten thermoplastic PEKK and PEEK were extruded and deposited through the nozzle of the extruder on the basis of their melting temperatures, as previously reported [23, 24]. The printing parameters of PEKK and PEEK are consistent in reducing the impact of printing parameters on material properties. Specifically, the printing parameters for the two materials were set at a nozzle temperature of 440 ℃, a printing platform temperature of 140 ℃, a printing speed of 20 mm/s, a layer height of 0.12 mm, and a wire width of 0.2 mm. Under the above parameter conditions, both PEKK and PEEK exhibit good fluidity and interlayer bonding strength. The PEKK and PEEK samples were designed with dimensions of 20 mm × 10 mm × 0.2 mm, and the aperture diameters were set to 1.0 mm, 2.0 mm and 3.0 mm.

Precision assessment of the PEKK mesh and PEEK mesh

Both the PEKK and PEEK meshes were scanned with a high-resolution extraoral scanner at a precision of 6 μm (IScanD103i, Imetric, Courgenay, Switzerland), as described in previous publications [25, 26]. This method has been widely used in precision dentistry research [27]. The STL files were exported and further measured with Geomagic 2014 (Geomagic, USA) [28]. Each group contained three samples. The length, width, thickness and aperture diameters of the meshes were recorded.

Surface hydrophilicity assessment of the PEKK and PEEK meshes

The hydrophilicity of the PEKK mesh and PEEK mesh was examined via a contact angle system (OCA20, Dataphysics, Germany), as previously described [29]. Water contact angle (WCA) tests were performed via ellipse methods with a 10 μL drop of ddH2O on the material surface. A total of 3 samples in each group were evaluated and then compared.

Surface microstructure assessment of the PEKK mesh and PEEK mesh

Three-dimensional optical microscopy (3D-OPT) (Contour GT, Bruker, US) was applied to determine the microstructure and roughness of the PEKK and PEEK meshes. Roughness data were then recorded with Vision64 software. The average roughness (Ra) and surface roughness (Sa) values were calculated [30]. A total of 3 samples from each group were evaluated and then compared.

Mechanical assessment of the PEKK and PEEK meshes

Three-point bending tests were performed via a computer-controlled electronic universal testing machine (C43.104. MTS Ltd., China) and following the requirements of ISO 7438:2020. At an ambient temperature of 25 °C, a force at a rate of 1 mm/min was applied vertically on the centerline of the samples. The maximum bending force and elastic moduli were recorded. The bending strength was calculated from the linear elastic area of the stress‒strain curve.

The tensile tests were performed with same universal testing machine to ensure axial force application without any momentum. The tensile strength and elastic modulus of each sample were recorded at room temperature with a force at a rate of 1 mm/min was applied, the results of tensile strength and elastic modulus of each group were recorded [31].

Biocompatibility assessment of the PEKK and PEEK meshes

hBMSC culture

hBMSCs (passage 2) were purchased from Cyagen Biosciences Technology (Cyagen, Guangzhou, China). The cells were cultured according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, hBMSCs were cultured in α-minimal essential media (α-MEM, Gibco, USA) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibco, USA) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere of 95% air and 5% CO2 [32].

hBMSC viability and proliferation

The proliferation of hBMSCs was determined via a Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay (Dojindo, Japan). hBMSCs were plated on PEKK mesh or PEEK mesh for 1, 4 or 7 days. For the CCK-8 assay, the cells were incubated at 37 °C for 2 h. The optical density (OD) was recorded on an ELX-808 Absorbance Microplate Recorder (BioTek, Winooski, VT) at 450 nm.

A live/dead viability assay kit (KeyGEN BioTECH, China) was employed to evaluate cell viability. Briefly, a fluorescent dye solution containing propidium iodide and calcein-AM in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) was utilized for hBMSCs, after which the cells were incubated at 37 °C for 15 min. Live cells were stained green, whereas dead cells were stained red [33].

Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR)

The total mRNA was isolated from the cells plated on PEKK mesh and PEEK mesh with TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA). The PrimeScript RT Reagent Kit (TaKaRa, Japan) was used to reverse transcribe mRNA into cDNA. FastStart Universal SYBR Green Master Mix (ROX, USA) was mixed with cDNA, and qRT-PCR was performed on an ABI 7500 Real-Time PCR system (Applied Biosystems, USA). The results were analyzed via the 2-ΔΔCT method, and normalization was performed with glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH). The sequences of the gene primers used for qRT-PCR are listed below, including Itg α2 (Forward 5′-TTAGCGCTCAGTCAAGGCAT-3′ and Reverse 5′-CGGTTCTCAGGAAAGCCACT-3′), Itg αV (Forward 5′-TTGTTTCAGGAGTTCCAAGA-3′ and Reverse 5′-TGAAGAGAGGTGCTCCAATA-3′), Itg β1 (Forward 5′-GCAGGCGTGGTTGCCGGAAT-3′ and Reverse 5′-TTTTCACCCGTGTCCCACTTGGC-3′) and Col1-α1 (Forward 5′-CCAACGAGATCGAGCTCAGG-3′ and Reverse 5′-GACTGTCTTGCCCCAAGTTCC-3′) [29].

Statistical analysis

Data were collected from three independent experiments and are expressed as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). One-way analysis (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s test was performed via the GraphPad Prism 10 statistical software package (GraphPad Software, San Diego, USA) to analyze statistically significant differences (P) between the various groups. P < 0.05 is represented symbolically by “*” and considered statistically significant.

Surface characteristics

Figure 2A shows WCA images of the PEKK mesh and PEEK mesh with different apertures. Figure 2B shows that the PEKK mesh had a hydrophobic surface, which was similar to that of the PEEK mesh. The PEKK mesh with an aperture diameter of 1 mm had greater hydrophobicity than the other groups did (WCA: 80.67 ± 3.88°). Overall, the aperture diameter has little effect on the hydrophilicity of the material. As the aperture diameter increases, the contact angle decreases slightly, but the difference is not statistically significant, possibly because droplets in meshes with large aperture diameters are more prone to contact the edge of the aperture during detection.

Figure 3A shows 3D-OPT images of the PEKK mesh and PEEK mesh with different apertures. More fracture zones were observed on the surface of the PEEK material. Figure 3B-C reveals that both the line roughness (Ra) and surface roughness (Sa) of the PEKK mesh were significantly lower than those of the PEEK mesh.

Mechanical characteristics

Figure 4 shows the results of the three-point bending test and the tensile test. Figure 4A shows the stress‒strain curves of the PEKK mesh and PEEK mesh. Both materials experienced long plastic deformation before reaching the maximum bending stress, which is consistent with previous research [34]. However, the PEKK mesh had a smaller deformation under the same force. For the same material, as the aperture diameter of the mesh increases, the maximum pressure, maximum bending strength and elastic modulus all decrease. Notably, unlike the mechanical parameters of the PEEK mesh, which decrease significantly when the aperture increases from 1 mm to 2 mm, the mechanical parameters of the PEKK mesh decrease significantly only when the aperture diameter increases from 2 mm to 3 mm (Fig. 4B-D).

The results of the tensile test are basically consistent with the results of the three-point bending test, but the ability of both the PEEK net and the PEKK net to resist stretching is lower than that of bending (Fig. 4E-F) [35].

Biocompatibility characteristics

Figure 5A shows the live and dead cell staining of hBMSCs on both the PEKK mesh and the PEEK mesh. The results demonstrated that there was no significant difference between these two materials. The proportion of live cells in the two groups of materials is significantly higher than that of dead cells.

Figure 5B further reveals the proliferation of hBMSCs by CCK-8 assays. All groups exhibited good cell proliferation rates but significantly lower than the culture dish as the control group. The proliferation of hBMSCs on PEKK mesh with an aperture diameter of 1 mm was slightly lower than PEEK mesh with an aperture diameter of 3 mm at day 4. But there was no significant difference between each group after 7 days.

Figure 5C shows the changes in the expression of adhesion-related mRNA on the PEKK mesh and PEEK mesh. The hBMSCs were seeded on both kinds of meshes.

After 3 h of incubation, the mRNA expression level of Itg α2 in all PEKK groups was lower than PEEK mesh with an aperture diameter of 1 mm, which showed the lowest expression level in all PEEK group. Under the condition of the same pore diameter, the expression level of Itg β1 in the PEKK group was lower than that in the PEEK group. There was no significantly difference for Itg αV and Col1-α1 between PEKK mesh and PEEK mesh.

Discussion

The performance of the barrier membrane has largely limited the effectiveness of GBR surgery in patients with severe bone defects. Although titanium mesh can achieve good and stable bone augmentation effects, the high exposure rate reduces confidence in its clinical application. Many previous studies have shown that PEEK has excellent biocompatibility and biostability similar to titanium but significantly low stiffness and elastic modulus [36]. Therefore, PEEK mesh is currently one of the most widely used materials and is a promising alternative to titanium. However, regarding clinical use, PEKK was fabricated with a thickness of up to 2 mm due to the limitations of the material properties and manufacturing process, which resulted in more occupied space and greater surgical difficulty [37].

With the development of 3D printing technology, thinner meshes can be produced by adding a lattice structure [38]. In previous research, PEEK meshes with a minimum thickness of 0.6 mm were fabricated. The results indicate that the PEEK mesh has the ability to maintain space and promote bone regeneration similar to that of a 0.3 mm titanium mesh [15]. However, the thinner the mesh is, the larger the space for bone formation and the greater the accuracy of bone augmentation surgery. Therefore, in this study, both PEKK mesh and PEEK mesh were fabricated with thicknesses of 0.2 mm, which is currently the most commonly used thickness parameter for a titanium mesh. On the basis of the morphological measurements, the contour and aperture of both the PEKK mesh and the PEEK mesh are similar to those of the design sample. The advantage of the PEKK mesh in controlling the thickness and aperture diameter may be due to the amorphous structure of PEKK, which results in a shorter tempering time and prevents warpage during 3D printing [39]. The advantages of the PEKK mesh are more obvious in terms of surface characteristics than those of the PEEK mesh with a thickness of 0.2 mm. The 3D-OPT images revealed that the PEKK mesh has a smooth and complete surface. This illustrates that the existing printing parameters can meet the basic requirements for the production of GBR barrier membranes with thicknesses of 0.2 mm. However, more cracks were found on PEEK samples, which indicates that thicker parameter designs are needed to avoid fracture in clinical applications.

After the meshes are placed, they can tolerate various mechanical stimulations in the mouth. Previous studies suggest that, compared with PEEK, PEKK may exhibit less stress concentration and superior shock absorbance, as well as being more retentive and abrasive and not prone to deformation [22]. Additionally, compared with PEEK, PEKK exhibits stronger adhesion between layers during printing, making it mechanically resistant in all directions. In this study, the difference in mechanical properties between the two materials after mesh fabrication suggests that under the same applied force, a smaller strain change in the PEKK mesh can better maintain the bone grafting space [40] At the same time, under the same strain conditions, the PEKK mesh can maintain a greater porosity to facilitate vascular growth and promote new bone formation [41]. Previous computer simulation results indicated that PEKK mesh with a 0.2 mm thickness and a 3 mm aperture diameter is more susceptible to chipping failure when exposed to occlusal forces. In this study, the mechanical properties of the PEKK mesh remained stable when the aperture diameter increased from 1 mm to 2 mm. Taken together, it is best to control the aperture diameter of the PEKK mesh to be less than 2 mm at a thickness of 0.2 mm.

Previous studies have suggested that exposure to nonabsorbable barrier membranes is associated with mechanical stimulation and bacterial infection [42, 43]. The stress‒strain curves revealed that unlike a titanium mesh, both the PEKK and PEEK meshes had long elastic deformation ranges. This feature makes the PEKK mesh and PEEK mesh more yieldable after mechanical stimulation, reducing the risk of mesh exposure. In addition, a variety of bacteria colonize the oral environment, and infection after mesh implantation is another risk factor for exposure. Previous research studies have shown that the surface of PEKK has less bacterial adhesion (approximately 38% reduction) than PEEK does [39, 47]. In this study, the PEKK mesh has a hydrophobic surface, which is similar to the PEEK mesh. Moreover, the Ra value and Sa value revealed that the PEKK group had a smoother surface, which inhibits the probability of bacteria colonizing the barrier membrane [44]. Taken together, these results indicated that the PEKK mesh has superior antibacterial properties. Since nonabsorbable membranes are always used in severe bone augmentation surgery and require a long duration in the mouth, the better antibacterial performance of PEKK mesh will help reduce the incidence of postoperative infections, thereby reducing the complications of barrier membrane exposure and increasing the osteogenic properties.

As this study is focused on the production of nonabsorbable barrier membranes for GBR surgery, the ability to perform easier removal during secondary surgery is another important evaluation point. Currently, the most widely used titanium mesh has good osseointegration, which makes it difficult to remove and results in more bone loss. This situation is inconsistent with the treatment concept of personalized and precise bone augmentation. The adhesion and proliferation of cells on implantable materials generally depend on surface features [45]. The results in this study showed PEKK mesh has a comparable low cytotoxicity to PEEK mesh; this is consistent with previous research showing that there is no significant difference in cell adhesion or density between 3D-printed PEEK and PEKK [46]. The bioinert nature and hydrophobicity of both PEKK and PEEK limit the migration of cells. The proliferation of hBMSCs on the PEKK mesh and PEEK mesh was significantly lower than that on the other cell culture plates. The slowest proliferation of hBMSCs on the PEKK mesh may be related to the smoother surface. The qRT‒PCR results also revealed the lowest expression of Itg αV and Itg β1, which are markers related to cell adhesion, in the PEKK group. Taken together, the PEKK mesh and PEEK mesh surfaces can effectively reduce the binding of meshes to newly formed bone, making them easier to remove during secondary surgery [47].

This study provides preliminary evidence that PEKK mesh has the potential to be used as a barrier membrane for GBR surgery to improve the bone augmentation effect in patients with severe bone defects. The properties of PEKK include the potential to avoid the higher exposure rate of titanium and be easier to remove during secondary surgery. Compared with the PEEK mesh, the PEKK mesh has better processing performance and space maintenance capacity, which benefits clinical applications. This study recommends a PEKK mesh with a thickness of 0.2 mm and a pore diameter of 2 mm, which is close to the production parameters of the existing titanium mesh. It is thinner and has a greater porosity than the PEEK mesh reported in previous literature. However, we must recognize some limitations of this study. First, owing to the limitations of processing technology, the printing accuracy and configuration of both the PEKK mesh and the PEEK mesh are not as good as those of titanium mesh. Considering the difficulty of making customized meshes, we fabricated only standard samples of two materials on the basis of our previous finite element simulation results in this study. Second, although the PEKK mesh has several advantages over the PEEK mesh, such as reduced surface roughness following the FDM method, additional studies, including mechanical studies and animal experiments, are needed to confirm the clinical application effect of the PEKK mesh.

Conclusions

Compared with the PEEK mesh, the PEKK mesh has greater printing accuracy, a smoother surface, better mechanical properties and similar biocompatibility; thus, the PEKK mesh is expected to be used in the production of customized barrier membranes for the augmentation of severe bone defects. To ensure its stability for clinical applications, it is best to control the aperture diameter of the PEKK mesh to less than 2 mm with a thickness of 0.2 μm.

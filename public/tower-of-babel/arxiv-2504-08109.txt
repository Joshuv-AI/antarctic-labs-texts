Twist-structures isomorphic to modal Nelson Lattices
Paula Menchón, Ricardo O. Rodriguez

Abstract
In this paper, we introduce a new variety of Heyting algebras with two unary modal operators that are not interdefinable but satisfy the weakest condition necessary to define modal operators on Nelson lattices. To achieve this, we utilize the representation of Nelson lattices as twist structures over Heyting algebras and establish a categorical equivalence. Finally, we develop a topological duality for this new variety and apply it to derive a topological duality for modal Nelson lattices.



keywords
Nelson lattices, modal Heyting algebras, modal logic, Twist-structures

1
Introduction

It is well known that non-classical logics develop in two main directions: logics with additional operators (such as modal logics, temporal logics, and epistemic logics) and logics with non-classical implications (essentially, those in which
\varphi\to\psi
is not equivalent to
\neg\varphi\vee\psi
). Our goal is to combine both approaches by taking an underlying logic with a non-classical implication and extending it with modal operators. In particular, since we would like the modal notions of necessity and possibility to be interdefinable, we will consider an underlying non-classical logic that accepts the law of double negation (
\neg\neg\varphi\to\varphi
) while rejecting the law of excluded middle (
\varphi\vee\neg\varphi
). This corresponds to constructive logic with strong negation, originally suggested by D. Nelson
Nelson49
and A. A. Markov
Markov50
. From the point of view of this kind of constructive logic, refuting a sentence
\varphi
by reduction ad absurdum is not equivalent to refuting it by constructing a counterexample of
\varphi
. The first to formalize this idea was Vorob’ev, who provided an axiomatization for this logic
Vorobev52
. However, these axioms do not make the connection between strong negation and refutations by means of counterexamples immediately evident. Nevertheless, Vakarelov, in
Vak77
, introduced a special class of lattices whose elements and operations can be interpreted in a way very close to the intuitive notion of a counterexample. This special class of lattices is given by a twist construction in the sense of Kalman
Kalman58
. Currently, it is known as N3-lattices, and it was also studied independently by Fidel in
Fidel78
. Moreover, a full representation— in fact, a categorical equivalence—was finally obtained by Sendlewski in
Sendlewski
. We follow this line of research and work on one of the most challenging research trends in non-classical logic: the attempt to combine different non-classical approaches, in our case, Nelson logic and modal logic. This combination provides the ability to handle modal notions such as belief, knowledge, and obligations while interacting with other aspects of reasoning that are best addressed using many-valued logics, such as vagueness, incompleteness, and uncertainty. The study we introduce could be particularly relevant from the perspective of Theoretical Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence.

In order to achieve our goal, we first introduce an extension of the well-known twist structure construction for Nelson residuated lattices to the modal setting. The significance of this construction has grown in recent years in the study of algebras related to non-classical logics (see
Busaniche
;
Galatos2004
;
RiviOno2014
). Unlike previous approaches in the literature, our proposed extension does not impose monotonicity with respect to modal operators (see
JanRivi2014
).

This article is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the fundamental concepts of the twist construction for Nelson lattices. We also present a detailed review of the known results regarding this structure, which will serve as the foundation for the subsequent sections of the paper. In Section
3
, we introduce the variety of modal Heyting algebras, which corresponds to the algebraic semantics of the Hilbert system
\textbf{IE}_{3}
presented in
(
Oli2020
, Section 4.2)
. These algebras contain two modal operators that are not interdefinable. In Section
4
, we introduce the class of modal Nelson lattices and show that every modal Nelson lattice can be represented as a twist product over a modal Heyting algebra. We then extend this result to establish a categorical equivalence. Using this representation, in Section
5
, we study some important subvarieties of modal Nelson lattices. Finally, in Section
6
, we establish a topological duality for modal Heyting algebras and use it to derive a corresponding topological duality for modal Nelson lattices.

An algebra

2.1
Representation of Nelson lattices as twist-structures over Heyting algebras

Let
\mathbf{H}=\langle H,\wedge,\vee,\rightharpoonup,\top,\bot\rangle

•

An element

Given a Heyting algebra

(x,y)\vee(s,t)=(x\vee s,y\wedge t)

\top=(\top,\bot)

The negation operations are given by:

3.

3
Modal Heyting algebras

In this section, we introduce a new variety of Heyting algebras with modal operators.

Definition 1
.

A
modal Heyting algebra
is an algebra
\mathbf{M}=\langle\mathbf{H},\square,\Diamond\rangle
such that the reduct
\mathbf{H}
is a Heyting algebra,
\square
and
\Diamond
are unary operators on
\mathbf{H}
, and for all
a,b\in H
, the following equation is satisfied:

\displaystyle\square a\wedge\Diamond(-a\wedge b)=\bot.

(mH)

We denote by
\mathcal{MH}
the variety of these algebras.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\mathbf{M}=\langle\mathbf{H},\square,\Diamond\rangle
be a modal Heyting algebra. Then the equation (
mH
) is equivalent to the following quasi-equation:

\displaystyle\text{If }a\wedge b=\bot,\text{ then }\square a\wedge\Diamond b=\bot.

(mH’)

Proof.

\Rightarrow
) Assume (
mH
). If
a\wedge b=\bot
, then
b\leq-a
, and therefore
-a\wedge b=b
. Thus,
\square a\wedge\Diamond b=\square a\wedge\Diamond(-a\wedge b)=\bot
.

\Leftarrow
) Assume (
mH’
). Since
a\wedge-a\wedge b=\bot
, we conclude that
\square a\wedge\Diamond(-a\wedge b)=\bot
.
∎

To justify Condition (
mH’
), we refer to
Oli2020
, where the authors study different intuitionistic non-normal modal logics. Specifically, they consider a family of these logics, including both operators
\Box
and
\Diamond
. They emphasize the challenge of finding suitable interactions between the modalities without reaching interdefinability. Their logics are distinguished by the different strengths of interactions between the modalities. The underlying logic of our variety of modal Heyting algebras corresponds to their Hilbert system
\textbf{IE}_{3}
in
(
Oli2020
, Section 4.2)
, where our (
mH’
) quasi-equation aligns with their
str

Let
\mathbf{M}=\langle\mathbf{H},\square,\Diamond\rangle
be a modal Heyting algebra. Note that the typical case in modal logic, where
\Box a=\top
and
\Diamond a=\bot
for all

, is covered by Condition (

)

A well-known subclass of modal Heyting algebras is the family of

modal Heyting algebras, which satisfy:

\displaystyle\square\top=\top.

(mH1)

Normal modal Heyting algebras form a variety, and, to the best of our knowledge, this has not been previously mentioned. However, there are some well-known extensions of this class. For instance, the normal variety of

\displaystyle-\Diamond a=\square-a,

(mH2)

\displaystyle\square(a\rightharpoonup b)\rightharpoonup(\square a\rightharpoonup\square b)=\top.

(mH3)

Note that (

Remark 5
.

It is worth noting that in a relational modal Heyting algebra

(i.e., one that satisfies (

Returning to our definition of Modal Heyting algebras, it is important to note that the most general version typically found in the literature is a structure

Theorem 3

Let

\displaystyle\blacksquare(x,y)

\displaystyle=(\square x,\Diamond y),

(5)

Definition 3

A modal Heyting algebra
\mathbf{M}=\langle\mathbf{H},\square,\Diamond\rangle

--\Box x=\Box--x\hskip 5.69054pt\mbox{ and }\hskip 5.69054pt--\Diamond x=\Diamond--x\\

It is well known that the reciprocal of both equations (so-called double negation shift) are not theorems of intuitionistic predicate logic but they characterize some interesting fragments. In particular, it is shown in
CaiRod2010
that
\Box--x\rightharpoonup--\Box x=\top
is strongly complete for 0-witnessed models and it is conjectured that
--\Diamond x\rightharpoonup\Diamond--x=\top
characterizes crisp frames.

Theorem 10
.

A modal Nelson lattice
\mathbf{N}=\langle\mathbf{A},\blacksquare,\blacklozenge\rangle
is a modal regular Nelson lattice if and only if the associated modal Heyting algebra
\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{N}}=\langle\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{A}},\square,\Diamond\rangle
, i.e.
\mathbf{N}
is isomorphic to a subalgebra of
\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{N}})
, is crisp-witnessed and the Heyting algebra
\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{A}}
satisfies the Stone identity
-x\vee--x=\top
.

Proof.

By
(
Busaniche
, Theorem 5.12)
, we only need to prove the equalities for the modal operators. Let
F
be a Boolean filter of
\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{A}}
such that
\mathbf{N}
is isomorphic to
\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{N}},F)
.

We can see that, by (
9
) for any pair
(x,y)\in R(\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{A}},F)
,
\phi(\blacksquare(x,y))=\phi(\Box x,\Diamond y)=(--\Box x,--\Diamond y)
and

\blacksquare\phi(x,y)=(\Box--x,\Diamond--y)
. Therefore, if the modal Heyting algebra
\langle\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{A}},\square,\Diamond\rangle
satisfies equations in (
14
) and
\blacksquare
and
\blacklozenge
are interdefinible, the result follows.

To prove the converse, let
x\in H_{\mathbf{A}}
. Since
(x,-x)\in R(\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{A}},F)
, we have
\phi(\blacksquare(x,-x))=\blacksquare\phi(x,-x)
. Then by equation (
9
), we obtain
(--\Box x,--\Diamond-x)=(\Box--x,\Diamond-x)
which implies that the equation
--\square x=\square--x
holds in
\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{N}}
. Analogously, since
(-x,x)\in R(\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{A}},F)
,
\phi(\blacksquare(-x,x))=\blacksquare\phi(-x,x)
. It follows that
(--\Box-x,--\Diamond x)=(\Box-x,\Diamond--x)
and therefore the equation
--\Diamond x=\Diamond--x
holds in
\mathbf{M}_{\mathbf{N}}
.
∎

6
Topological dualities

Recall that a
Priestley space
is a compact ordered space
\mathcal{X}=\langle X,\tau,\leq\rangle
that satisfies the separation condition, i.e., for every
x,y\in X
such that
x\not\leq y
, there exists a clopen up-set
U\subseteq X
with
x\in U
and
y\not\in U
.
We will denote the up-set and down-set generated by a set
U\subseteq X
by
\operatorname{\uparrow}\hskip-1.42262pt{U}=\{x\in X:(\exists u\in U)\ x\leq x\}
, and
\operatorname{\downarrow}\hskip-1.42262pt{U}=\{x\in X:(\exists u\in U)\ x\leq u\}
, respectively.
Moreover, we will denote by
\mathcal{U}(X)
the family of clopen up-sets and by
\mathcal{D}(X)
the family clopen down-sets.
An
Esakia space
is a Priestley space satisfying the additional condition that for every clopen
U\in X
, the set
\operatorname{\downarrow}\hskip-1.42262pt{U}\in\mathcal{D}(X)
. The set of clopen up-sets of an Esakia space forms a distributive lattice, which becomes a Heyting algebra when endowed with the following implication operation: for clopen up-sets
U,V\subseteq X
, we define
U\to V:=\{x\in X:\operatorname{\uparrow}\hskip-1.42262pt{x}\cap U\subseteq V\}
.
Let
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
be Esakia spaces. A map
f\colon X\to Y

is an
Esakia function
if it is continuous, order-preserving and satisﬁes that
\operatorname{\uparrow_{Y}}\hskip-1.42262pt{f(x)}\subseteq f[\operatorname{\uparrow_{X}}\hskip-1.42262pt{x}]
for every

x\in X
.

Conversely, every Heyting algebra
\mathbf{H}
gives rise to an Esakia space
\langle X(\mathbf{H}),\tau_{\mathbf{H}},\subseteq\rangle
, where
X(\mathbf{H})
is the poset of prime filters of
\mathbf{H}
, ordered by inclusion, and
\tau_{\mathbf{H}}
is is the topology generated by the sub-basis:

\{\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(a):a\in H\}\cup\{\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H})\setminus\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(a):a\in H\}

with
\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(a)=\{P\in\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}):a\in P\}
. The map
\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}:\mathbf{H}\to\mathcal{U}(\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}))
defined as above is an isomorphism of Heyting algebras.

The categories of Heyting algebras with homomorphisms and Esakia spaces with Esakia functios are dually equivalent. If
h\colon\mathbf{H}_{1}\to\mathbf{H}_{2}
is a homomorphism of Heyting algebras, then the map
X(h)\colon\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}_{2})\to\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}_{2})

between the corresponding Esakia spaces deﬁned by
X(h)(P)=h^{-1}[P]
for every
P\in\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}_{2})
is an Esakia function. Conversely, if
f\colon X_{1}\to X_{2}
is an Esakia function, then the map
h(f)\colon\mathcal{U}(X_{2})\to\mathcal{U}(X_{1})
deﬁned by
h(f)(U)=f^{-1}[U]
for every clopen up-set
U
of
X_{2}
is a Heyting algebra homomorphism.

6.1
Topological duality for modal Heyting algebras

In this section, we will extend the topological duality to modal Heyting algebras. To achieve this, we will make use of neighbourhood functions, a common tool for interpreting non-normal modal operators. We will follow the approach outlined in
Oli2020
, where a semantic characterization of the logic
\textbf{IE}_{3}
is provided in terms of neighbourhood models that include two distinct neighbourhood functions, each corresponding to one of the two modalities.

Let
\mathbf{M}=\langle\mathbf{H},\square,\Diamond\rangle
be a modal Heyting algebra.
For each operation
\bullet\in\{\square,\Diamond\}
we define a neighbourhood function
\eta_{\bullet}:\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H})\mapsto\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H})))
, i.e. we associate to each prime filter a set of prime filters which is called its neighbourhood in the following way:

\displaystyle\eta_{\square}(P)

\displaystyle:=\{\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(a):\square a\in P\},

(15)

\displaystyle\eta_{\Diamond}(P)

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}))\setminus\{\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H})\setminus\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(a):\Diamond a\in P\}.

(16)

Using the neighbourhood function
\eta_{\bullet}
, we can define an algebraic operator
\bullet\in\{\Box,\Diamond\}
in the Heyting algebra of clopen up-sets of
\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H})
, denoted by
\mathcal{U}(\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}))
. For any
U\in\mathcal{U}(\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}))
, the operators are defined as follows:

\displaystyle\square_{\eta_{\square}}(U)

\displaystyle:=\{P\in\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}):U\in\eta_{\square}(P)\},

(17)

\displaystyle\Diamond_{\eta_{\Diamond}}(U)

\displaystyle:=\{P\in\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}):\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H})\setminus U\notin\eta_{\Diamond}(P)\}.

(18)

The following proposition shows that, using the above definitions, we can extend the isomorphism
\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}
for Heyting algebras to include modal operators as well:

Proposition 11
.

Let
\mathbf{M}=\langle\mathbf{H},\square,\Diamond\rangle
be a modal Heyting algebra. Then, for every
a\in H
:

1.

\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}({\square a})=\square_{\eta_{\square}}(\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(a))
.

2.

\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}({\Diamond a})=\Diamond_{\eta_{\Diamond}}(\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(a))
.

Proof.

1. Let
P\in\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H})
. By (
15
),
\square a\in P
if and only if
\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(a)\in\eta_{\square}(P)
. It follows from (
17
),
\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(a)\in\eta_{\square}(P)
if and only if
P\in\square_{\eta_{\square}}(\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(a))
. Therefore
\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}({\square a})=\square_{\eta_{\square}}(\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(a))
.

2. Let
P\in\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H})
. By (
16
),
\Diamond a\in P
if and only if
\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H})\setminus\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(a)\notin\eta_{\Diamond}(P)
. Then, it follows from (
18
),
\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H})\setminus\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(a)\notin\eta_{\Diamond}(P)
if and only if
P\in\Diamond_{\eta_{\Diamond}}(\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(a))
. Therefore,
\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}({\Diamond a})=\Diamond_{\eta_{\Diamond}}(\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(a))
.
∎

Proposition 12
.

Let
\mathbf{M}=\langle\mathbf{H},\square,\Diamond\rangle
be a modal Heyting algebra. Then, for any
P\in\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H})
and
U,V\in\mathcal{U}(\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}))
, the following condition holds:

\text{if }U\in\eta_{\square}(P)\text{, then }\operatorname{\downarrow}\hskip-1.42262pt{U}\cup(\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H})\setminus V)\in\eta_{\Diamond}(P).

Proof.

Let
U\in\eta_{\square}(P)
and let
V\in\mathcal{U}(\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}))
. By definition, there exists
a\in H
such that
\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(a)=U
and
\square a\in P
. Suppose
\operatorname{\downarrow}\hskip-1.42262pt{U}\cup(\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H})\setminus V)\notin\eta_{\Diamond}(P)
. By (
16
), there exists
b\in H
such that
\operatorname{\downarrow}\hskip-1.42262pt{U}\cup(\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H})\setminus V)=\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H})\setminus\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(b)
and
\Diamond b\in P
. Then,
(\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H})\setminus\operatorname{\downarrow}\hskip-1.42262pt{U})\cap V=\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(b)
. It is easy to see that
\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H})\setminus\operatorname{\downarrow}\hskip-1.42262pt{U}=\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H})\setminus\operatorname{\downarrow}\hskip-1.42262pt{\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(a)=\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(-a)}
and thus
\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(b)\subseteq\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(-a)
. From (
mH’
), since
a\wedge b=0
it follows that
\square a\wedge\Diamond b=\bot\notin P
, which contradicts the fact that
\square a,\Diamond b\in P
.
∎

Analyzing these properties, we introduce the topological spaces that we will later prove to be dual to modal Heyting algebras.

Definition 4
.

A
modal Esakia space
(ME-space) is a structure
\mathcal{X}=\langle X,\tau,\leq,\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\rangle
such that
\langle X,\leq,\tau\rangle
is an Esakia space and
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\colon X\to\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(X))
are neighbourhood functions satisfying the following properties for all
x\in X
and for all
U,V\in\mathcal{U}(X)
:

1.

\eta_{1}(x)\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{U}(X))
and
\eta_{2}(x)\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{D}(X))
,

2.

\square_{\eta_{1}}(U)=\{x\in X:U\in\eta_{1}(x)\}
, and
\Diamond_{\eta_{2}}(U)=\{x\in X:X\setminus U\notin\eta_{2}(x)\}
are clopen up-sets,

3.

if
U\in\eta_{1}(x)
, then
\operatorname{\downarrow}\hskip-1.42262pt{U}\cup(X\setminus V)\in\eta_{2}(x)
.

As a consequence of the previous theorems, we obtain the following result:

Theorem 13
.

For each modal Heyting algebra
\mathbf{M}=\langle\mathbf{H},\square,\Diamond\rangle
, the structure
\mathcal{X}(\mathbf{M)}=\langle\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}),\tau_{\mathbf{H}},\subseteq,\eta_{\square},\eta_{\Diamond}\rangle
is a modal Esakia space.

Now, we will prove that given a modal Esakia space, the algebra of clopen up-sets, endowed with the operators defined from the neighbourhood functions, is a modal Heyting algebra.

Proposition 14
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=\langle X,\tau,\leq,\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\rangle
be a modal Esakia space. Then, the algebra
\langle\mathcal{U}(X),\square_{\eta_{1}},\Diamond_{\eta_{2}}\rangle
, where
\square_{\eta_{1}},\Diamond_{\eta_{2}}\colon\mathcal{U}(X)\to\mathcal{U}(X)
are the operators defined by

\displaystyle\square_{\eta_{1}}(U)

\displaystyle=\{x\in X:U\in\eta_{1}(x)\},

(19)

\displaystyle\Diamond_{\eta_{2}}(U)

\displaystyle=\{x\in X:X\setminus U\notin\eta_{2}(x)\},

(20)

is a modal Heyting algebra.

Proof.

We only need to prove that (
mH’
) is satisfied. Let
U,V\in\mathcal{U}(X)
such that
U\cap V=\emptyset
. Suppose that there exists
x\in X
such that
x\in\square_{\eta_{1}}(U)\cap\Diamond_{\eta_{2}}(V)
. Then,
U\in\eta_{1}(x)
and
X\setminus V\notin\eta_{2}(x)
. By Definition
4
, since
U\subseteq X\setminus V
and
U\in\eta_{1}(x)
, we obtain
X\setminus V=\operatorname{\downarrow}\hskip-1.42262pt{U}\cup(X\setminus V)\in\eta_{2}(x)
, which is a contradiction. Therefore
\square_{\eta_{1}}(U)\cap\Diamond_{\eta_{2}}(V)=\emptyset
.
∎

Theorem 15
.

For each modal Heyting algebra
\mathbf{M}=\langle\mathbf{H},\square,\Diamond\rangle
, the map
\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}\colon\mathbf{H}\to\mathcal{U}(\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}))
defined by

\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(a)=\{P\in\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}):a\in P\}

is a modal
Heyting algebra isomorphism from
\langle\mathbf{H},\square,\Diamond\rangle
onto
\langle\mathcal{U}(\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H})),\square_{\eta_{\square}},\Diamond_{\eta_{\Diamond}}\rangle
.

Theorem 16
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=\langle X,\tau,\leq,\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\rangle
be an ME-space and let
\epsilon_{\mathcal{X}}\colon X\to\operatorname{X}(\mathcal{U}(X))
be the map defined by:

\epsilon_{X}(x)=\{U\in\mathcal{U}(X):x\in U\}.

Then
\epsilon_{X}
is an Esakia-homeomorphism between
\langle X,\tau,\leq\rangle
and
\langle\operatorname{X}(\mathcal{U}(X)),\tau_{\mathcal{U}(X)},\subseteq\rangle
that satisfies:

1.

\eta_{\square_{\eta_{1}}}(\epsilon_{X}(x))=\{\epsilon_{X}[U]:U\in\eta_{1}(x)\}
,

2.

\eta_{\Diamond_{\eta_{2}}}(\epsilon_{X}(x))=\{\epsilon_{X}[D]:D\in\eta_{2}(x)\}
.

Proof.

It is known that
\epsilon_{X}
is a homeomorphism and an order preserving isomorphism.

1. Let
Z\in\eta_{\square_{\eta_{1}}}(\epsilon_{X}(x))
. By (
15
),

Z=\sigma_{\mathcal{U}(X)}(U)=\{\epsilon_{X}(x):U\in\epsilon_{X}(x)\}=\{\epsilon_{X}(x):x\in U\}=\epsilon_{X}[U]

for some
U\in\mathcal{U}(X)
such that
\square_{\eta_{1}}(U)\in\epsilon_{X}(x)
, i.e.,
x\in\square_{\eta_{1}}(U)
. By (
19
),
U\in\eta_{1}(x)
. Therefore,
Z\in\{\epsilon_{X}[U]:U\in\eta_{1}(x)\}
. The other direction follows immediately from the definitions.

2. Let
Z\in\eta_{\Diamond_{\eta_{2}}}(\epsilon_{X}(x))
. By (
16
),

Z=\operatorname{X}(\mathcal{U}(X))\setminus\sigma_{\mathcal{U}(X)}(U)=\{\epsilon_{X}(x):U\notin\epsilon_{X}(x)\}=\{\epsilon_{X}(x):x\notin U\}=\epsilon_{X}[X\setminus U]

for some
U\in\mathcal{U}(X)
such that
\Diamond_{\eta_{2}}(U)\notin\epsilon_{X}(x)
, i.e.,
x\notin\Diamond_{\eta_{2}}(U)
. By (
20
),
X\setminus U\in\eta_{2}(x)
. Therefore,
Z\in\{\epsilon_{X}[D]:D\in\eta_{2}(x)\}
. The other direction follows immediately from the definitions.
∎

Now, we will introduce a category whose objects are modal Esakia spaces. Note that for a clopen up-closed set
V
of
\operatorname{X}(\mathcal{U}(X))
, we have the following equivalences:

V\in\eta_{\square_{\eta_{1}}}(\epsilon_{X}(x))\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\epsilon_{X}^{-1}[V]\in\eta_{1}(x),

and

(\operatorname{X}(\mathcal{U}(X))\setminus V)\in\eta_{\Diamond_{\eta_{2}}}(\epsilon_{X}(x))\quad\text{if and only if}\quad(X\setminus\epsilon_{X}^{-1}[V])\in\eta_{2}(x).

Definition 5
.

A map
f\colon X_{1}\to X_{2}
between two ME-spaces
\mathcal{X}_{1}=\langle X_{1},\tau_{1},\leq_{1},\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\rangle
and
\mathcal{X}_{2}=\langle X_{2},\tau_{2},\leq_{2},\eta^{\prime}_{1},\eta^{\prime}_{2}\rangle
is an ME-morphism iff
f
is an Esakia function that additionally satisﬁes, for every
x\in X_{1}
and every clopen up-set
U\in\mathcal{U}(X_{2})
,

1.

U\in\eta^{\prime}_{1}(f(x))
if and only if
f^{-1}[U]\in\eta_{1}(x)
,

2.

(X_{2}\setminus U)\in\eta^{\prime}_{2}(f(x))
if and only if
(X_{1}\setminus f^{-1}[U])\in\eta_{2}(x)
.

It is straightforward to verify that the composition of ME-morphisms is again an ME-morphism and that the identity map of an ME-space is an ME-morphism.

We now define the category
\mathbb{ME}
, whose objects are ME-spaces, and the morphisms are ME-morphisms.

Proposition 17
.

Let
\mathbf{M}_{1}=\langle\mathbf{H}_{1},\square_{1},\Diamond_{1}\rangle
and
\mathbf{M}_{2}=\langle\mathbf{H}_{2},\square_{2},\Diamond_{2}\rangle
be two modal Heyting algebras and let
h\colon\mathbf{H}_{1}\to\mathbf{H}_{2}
be a homomorphism of modal Heyting algebras. Then
X(h)\colon\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}_{2})\to\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}_{1})
is a ME-morphism.

Proof.

It is known that
X(h)
is an Esakia function. We only need to prove 1 and 2 from Definition
5
. Let
a\in H_{1}
and
P\in\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}_{2})
.

1.
\sigma_{\mathbf{H}_{1}}(a)\in\eta_{\square_{1}}(X(h)(P))
if and only if
\square_{1}a\in h^{-1}(P)
. It follows that
h(\square_{1}a)=\square_{2}h(a)\in P
and by (

), it is equivalent to
\sigma_{\mathbf{H}_{2}}(h(a))\in\eta_{\square_{2}}(P)
. In addition,
\sigma_{\mathbf{H}_{2}}(h(a))=(X(h))^{-1}[\sigma_{\mathbf{H}_{1}}(a)]
.

\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}_{1})\setminus\sigma_{\mathbf{H}_{1}}(a)\in\eta_{\Diamond_{1}}(X(h)(P))
if and only if
\Diamond_{2}a\notin h^{-1}(P)
. It follows that

\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}_{2})\setminus\sigma_{\mathbf{H}_{2}}(h(a))\in\eta_{\Diamond_{2}}(P)

\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}_{2})\setminus\sigma_{\mathbf{H}_{2}}(h(a))=\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}_{2})\setminus(X(h))^{-1}[\sigma_{\mathbf{H}_{1}}(a)]

Proposition 18
.

Let

It is known that

\displaystyle\square_{\eta_{1}}(h(f)(U))

\displaystyle=\{x\in X_{1}:U\in\eta^{\prime}_{1}(f(x))\}

\displaystyle\Diamond_{\eta_{2}}(h(f)(U))

\operatorname{Id}_{\mathbb{ME}}
.
∎

6.2
Topological duality for modal Nelson lattices

We now introduce a category of topological structures that we will prove to be equivalent to the category
\mathbb{TW}
. Recall that filters of Heyting algebras correspond to closed up-sets in their dual Esakia space. To characterize Boolean filters in terms of their dual space, we note that for all
P\in\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H})
, the inclusion
D(\mathbf{H})\subseteq P
holds if and only if
P
is maximal in the poset
\langle\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}),\subseteq\rangle
.

So, let
\mathbf{H}
be a Heyting algebra and let
F\subseteq H
be a filter such that
D(\mathbf{H})\subseteq F
. Then
\mathcal{C}(F)=\{P\in\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}):F\subseteq P\}
is a closed set such that
\mathcal{C}(F)\subseteq\max(\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}))
. Thus, following a similar approach to
JanRivi2014
and
Jansana2
, we define:

Definition 6
.

A modal NE-space (
MNE
-space) is a structure
\mathcal{X}=\langle X,\tau,\leq,\eta_{\Box},\eta_{\Diamond},C\rangle
such that
\langle X,\tau,\leq,\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\rangle
is an ME-space (according to Definition
4
) and
C\subseteq\max(X)
is a closed subset satisfying the following property for all clopen up-sets
U,V\in\mathcal{U}(X)
:

\text{If }C\subseteq U\cup V\text{ and }U\cap V=\emptyset\text{, then }C\subseteq\Box_{\eta_{\Box}}U\cup\Diamond_{\eta_{\Diamond}}V.

(
F^{*}
)

Proposition 20
.

Let
\langle\mathbf{H},\square,\Diamond\rangle
be a modal Heyting algebra and let
F\subseteq H
be a boolean filter of
\mathbf{H}
that satisfies
F
. Then, the structure
\langle\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}),\tau_{\mathbf{H}},\subseteq,\eta_{\square},\eta_{\Diamond},\mathcal{C}(F)\rangle
is a MNE-space.

Proof.

Since
\langle\mathbf{H},\square,\Diamond\rangle
is a modal Heyting algebra, we obtain that
\langle\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}),\tau_{\mathbf{H}},\subseteq,\eta_{\square},\eta_{\Diamond}\rangle
is a modal Esakia space. Moreover,
F
is a boolean filter, thus the closed set satisfies
\mathcal{C}(F)\subseteq\max(\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}))
. Suppose that
\mathcal{C}(F)\subseteq U\cup V
and
U\cap V=\emptyset
for
U,V\in\mathcal{U}(\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}))
. Then, there exist
a,b\in H
such that
U=\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(a)
and
V=\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(b)
. It follows that
\mathcal{C}(F)\subseteq\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(a\vee b)
and
\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(a\wedge b)=\emptyset
. Thus,
a\vee b\in F
and
a\wedge b=\bot
. By Condition
F
,
\square a\vee\Diamond b\in F
. Therefore, we obtain
\mathcal{C}(F)\subseteq\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(\square a\vee\Diamond b)=\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(\square a)\cup\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(\Diamond b)=\square_{\eta_{\square}}(\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(a))\cup\Diamond_{\eta_{\Diamond}}(\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(a))=\square_{\eta_{\square}}(U)\cup\Diamond_{\eta_{\Diamond}}(V)
.
∎

Corollary 3
.

Let
\mathbf{A}=\langle A,\blacksquare,\blacklozenge\rangle
be a modal Nelson lattice. Then, the structure
\langle\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{A}}^{*}),\tau_{\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{A}}^{*}},\subseteq,\eta_{\square^{*}},\eta_{\Diamond^{*}},\mathcal{C}(F^{*})\rangle
where
\mathcal{C}(F^{*})=\{P\in\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{A}}^{*}):F^{*}\subseteq P\}
is a modal NE-space.

Proposition 21
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=\langle X,\tau,\leq,\eta_{1},\eta_{2},C\rangle
be an MNE-space. Then,
F_{\mathcal{C}}=\{U\in\mathcal{U}(X):\mathcal{C}\subseteq U\}
is a boolean filter of
\mathcal{U}(X)
that satisfies
F
. Thus, we obtain that the pair satisfies
(\langle\mathcal{U}(X),\square_{\eta_{1}},\Diamond_{\eta_{2}}\rangle,F_{\mathcal{C}})\in\mathbb{TW}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
U\in\mathcal{U}(X)
is a dense element. Then,
X\setminus\operatorname{\downarrow}\hskip-1.42262pt{U}=\emptyset
, i.e.,
\operatorname{\downarrow}\hskip-1.42262pt{U}=X
. We will show that
\mathcal{C}\subseteq U
. Let
x\in\mathcal{C}
. Then, there exists
u\in U
such that
x\leq u
. Since
x\in\max(X)
,
x=u
and thus
x\in U
. From Condition

F
∗

, it is immediate that
F_{\mathcal{C}}
satisfies Condition
F
.
∎

Theorem 22
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=\langle X,\tau,\leq,\eta_{1},\eta_{2},C\rangle
be an MNE-space. Let
\epsilon_{X}\colon X\to\operatorname{X}(\mathcal{U}(X))
be the map defined by
\epsilon_{X}(x)=\{U\in\mathcal{U}(X):x\in U\}
. Then,
\epsilon_{X}
is an ME-homeomorphism that satisfies:

\epsilon_{X}[C]=\mathcal{C}(F_{C}).

Proof.

Let
x\in C
. We will prove
F_{C}\subseteq\epsilon_{X}(x)
. Let
U\in F_{C}
. It follows that
x\in C\subseteq U
and thus
U\in\epsilon_{X}(x)
. Therefore
\epsilon_{X}(x)\in\mathcal{C}(F_{C})
.

On the other hand, let
\epsilon_{X}(x)\in\mathcal{C}(F_{C})
. Then,
F_{C}\subseteq\epsilon_{X}(x)
. To prove that
x\in C
, we will show that
x\in U
for all
U\in\mathcal{U}(X)
such that
C\subseteq U
. Let
C\subseteq U
. Then,
U\in F_{C}
and by assumption,
U\in\epsilon_{X}(x)
, i.e.,
x\in U
. Therefore
x\in C
and
\epsilon_{X}(x)\in\epsilon_{X}[C]
.
∎

Definition 7
.

A map
f\colon X_{1}\to X_{2}
between two MNE-spaces
\langle X_{1},\tau_{1},\leq_{1},\eta_{1},\eta_{2},C_{1}\rangle
and
\langle X_{2},\tau_{2},\leq_{2},\eta^{\prime}_{1},\eta^{\prime}_{2},C_{2}\rangle
is an MNE-morphism iff is an ME-morphism that additionally satisﬁes:
f[C_{1}]\subseteq C_{2}
.

Proposition 23
.

Let
(\mathbf{M}_{1},F_{1})\in\mathbb{TW}
and
(\mathbf{M}_{2},F_{2})\in\mathbb{TW}
. Let
h\colon H_{1}\to H_{2}
be a homomorphism of modal Heyting algebras that satisfies
h[F_{1}]\subseteq F_{2}
. If
f\colon\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}_{2})\to\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}_{1})
is the function defined by
f(P)=h^{-1}[P]
, then
f
is a MNE-morphism.

Proof.

We only need to prove that
f[\mathcal{C}(F_{2})]\subseteq\mathcal{C}(F_{1})
. Let
Q\in f[\mathcal{C}(F_{2})]
. Then, there exists
P\in\mathcal{C}(F_{2})
such that
Q=h^{-1}[P]
. By definition,
F_{2}\subseteq P
, and by assumption,
h[F_{1}]\subseteq P
. Thus,
F_{1}\subseteq h^{-1}[P]=Q
and
Q\in\mathcal{C}(F_{1})
. Therefore,
f[\mathcal{C}(F_{2})]\subseteq\mathcal{C}(F_{1})
.
∎

Proposition 24
.

Let
f\colon X_{1}\to X_{2}
be a MNE-morphism between two MNE-spaces
\langle X_{1},\tau_{1},\leq_{1},\eta_{1},\eta_{2},C_{1}\rangle
and
\langle X_{2},\tau_{2},\leq_{2},\eta^{\prime}_{1},\eta^{\prime}_{2},C_{2}\rangle
. Then, the map
h\colon\mathcal{U}(X_{2})\to\mathcal{U}(X_{1})
defined by
h(U)=f^{-1}[U]
is a homomorphism of modal Heyting algebras that satisfies
h[F_{C_{2}}]\subseteq F_{C_{1}}
.

Proof.

Let
V\in h[F_{C_{2}}]
. Then, there exists
U\in\mathcal{U}(X_{2})
such that
C_{2}\subseteq U
and
V=f^{-1}[U]
. By assumptio,
f[C_{1}]\subseteq C_{2}\subseteq U
and it follows that
C_{1}\subseteq f^{-1}[U]=V
. Thus,
V\in F_{C_{1}}
.
∎

Proposition 25
.

Let
(\mathbf{M},F)\in\mathbb{TW}
. Then,
\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(a)=\{P\in\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}):a\in P\}
is a homomorphism of modal Heyting algebras that satisfies
\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}[F]=F_{\mathcal{C}(F)}
.

Proof.

We only need to prove
\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}[F]=F_{\mathcal{C}(F)}
. Let
U\in\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}[F]
. Then, there exists
a\in F
such that
U=\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(a)
. It follows that
\mathcal{C}(F)\subseteq\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(a)
and therefore
U=\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(a)\in F_{\mathcal{C}(F)}
.
On the other hand, let
U=\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(a)\in F_{\mathcal{C}(F)}
. Then,
\mathcal{C}(F)\subseteq\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}(a)
. It follows that
a\in F
and therefore
U\in\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}[F]
.
∎

Let
\mathbb{MNE}
be the category of MNE-spaces with MNE-functions.
Let
\mathbf{L}\colon\mathbb{TW}\to\mathbb{MNE}
be the contravariant functor that for each pair
P=(\mathbf{M},F)\in\mathbb{TW}
,
\mathbf{L}(P):=\langle\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}),\tau_{\mathbf{H}},\subseteq,\eta_{\square},\eta_{\Diamond},\mathcal{C}(F)\rangle
and that for each homomorphism of modal Heyting algebras
h\colon H_{1}\to H_{2}
that satisfies
h[F_{1}]\subseteq F_{2}
,
\mathbf{L}(h)\colon\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}_{2})\to\operatorname{X}(\mathbf{H}_{1})
is the MNE-morphism
\mathbf{L}(h):=X(h)
.

Let
\mathbf{K}\colon\mathbb{MNE}\to\mathbb{TW}
be the contravariant functor that for each MNE-space
\mathcal{X}=\langle X,\tau,\leq,\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\mathcal{C}\rangle
,
\mathbf{K}(\mathcal{X}):=(\langle\mathcal{U}(X),\square_{\eta_{1}},\Diamond_{\eta_{2}}\rangle,F_{\mathcal{C}})
and that for each MNE-morphism
f\colon X_{1}\to X_{2}
,
\mathbf{K}(f)\colon\mathcal{U}(X_{2})\to\mathcal{U}(X_{1})
is the modal Heyting algebra homomorphism
\mathbf{K}(f):=h(f)
.
From all the theorems above, we can conclude:

Theorem 26
.

The categories
\mathbb{MNE}
and
\mathbb{TW}
are dually equivalent.

And finally, we obtain a topological duality for modal Nelson lattices:

Corollary 4
.

The category
\mathbb{MN}
is dually equivalent to the category
\mathbb{MNE}
via the functors
\mathbf{L}\circ\mathbf{F}\colon\mathbb{MN}\to\mathbb{MNE}
and
\mathbf{E}\circ\mathbf{K}:\mathbb{MNE}\to\mathbb{MN}
.

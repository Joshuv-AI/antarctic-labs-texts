Abstract
We present the first observation of 3 Λ \Lambda H and 4 Λ \Lambda H in Ag+Ag collisions at s NN \sqrt{\text{s\textsubscript{NN}}} = 2.55 GeV, emitted around mid-rapidity. The hypernuclei are reconstructed via their two-body decay channels and identified through their weak-decay topology, employing an artificial neural network for enhanced discrimination. The analysis methodology is validated using Λ \Lambda hyperons. The resulting rapidity distributions, dN/dy, exhibit a bell shape centered at mid-rapidity. The yield of 4 Λ \Lambda H is equal to or exceeds that of 3 Λ \Lambda H, which contrasts the measurement from the STAR collaboration at s NN \sqrt{\text{s\textsubscript{NN}}} = 3 GeV and is consistent with a scenario in which hypernuclei receive feed-down from excited states. The data enable a high-precision measurement of the hypernuclei lifetimes. For the 3 Λ \Lambda H, a lifetime of τ H Λ 3 = ( 239 ± 23 ​ ( stat ) ± 18 ​ ( sys ) ) ​ ps \tau_{{}^{3}_{\Lambda}\mathrm{H}}=(239\pm 23{\mathrm{(stat)}}\pm 18{\mathrm{(sys)}})\,\mathrm{ps} , is extracted, consistent on the 1 σ \sigma level with that of the free Λ \Lambda . In contrast, the 4 Λ \Lambda H lifetime of τ H Λ 4 = ( 209 ± 7 ​ ( stat ) ± 10 ​ ( sys ) ) ​ ps \tau_{{}^{4}_{\Lambda}\mathrm{H}}=(209\pm 7{\mathrm{(stat)}}\pm 10{\mathrm{(sys)}})\,\mathrm{ps} , shows a 4.5 σ \sigma deviation from the free Λ \Lambda lifetime. The results consolidate the available world data.

Hypernuclei extend the nuclear chart into the strange sector and thus offer unique opportunities to study the hyperon–nucleon (Y–N) interaction.
This interaction plays a crucial role in the equation of state (EOS) of nuclear matter at high baryon densities

Heavy-ion collisions enable the formation of hypernuclei in environments of finite temperature and baryon density above nuclear ground state conditions, offering a setting for studying the Y–N interaction.

In heavy-ion collisions at center-of-mass energies of a few GeV per nucleon pair, nucleons from the colliding nuclei experience significant stopping, causing a baryon dominated fireball. This environment provides favorable conditions for the formation and emission of light nuclei. Simultaneously, hadrons containing strange quarks are produced near their kinematic production thresholds. Consequently, their yields exhibit a steep increase with rising

II
Experimental Setup and Reconstruction

The data analyzed in this work were recorded with the High Acceptance Di-Electron Spectrometer (HADES) located at the GSI Helmholtz Centre for Heavy-Ion Research in Darmstadt, Germany. HADES is a charged-particle detector composed of a six-coil toroidal magnet surrounding the beam axis and six identical detection sectors placed between the coils, providing nearly complete azimuthal coverage. Each sector is equipped with a Ring-Imaging Cherenkov (RICH) detector, followed by four Multi-Wire-Drift Chambers (MDCs)—two placed upstream and two downstream of the magnetic field—along with a scintillator hodoscope (TOF) and a Resistive Plate Chamber (RPC). Downstream of the tracking system, an Electromagnetic Calorimeter (ECAL) for photon measurements and a forward hodoscope for event-plane determination are employed. A detailed description of the detector system is available in
HADES:2009aat
.

The MDCs provide charged-particle tracking, while TOF and RPC are employed for time-of-flight measurements in conjunction with a diamond start detector positioned upstream of a 15-fold segmented Ag target. The trigger condition was based on hit multiplicities observed in the TOF and RPC detectors. A total of
2.6\times 10^{9}
Ag(1.58A GeV)+Ag collisions were analyzed, corresponding to the
0
–
25\%
most central events.

Particle identification was achieved by applying loose selection criteria to the correlation between reconstructed momentum and particle velocity. For
3
He and
4
He, the specific energy loss (
dE/dx
) measured in the MDCs was used in addition to suppress background from singly charged particles. Further selection criteria were applied to track quality based on the Runge-Kutta track fitting algorithm.

The reconstruction of
3
\Lambda

4
\Lambda
H candidates was performed by identifying “off-vertex” two-body decay topologies. Background suppression was achieved by requiring a maximal distance of closest approach (DCA) between the primary event vertex and the daughter tracks (
3
He or
4
He and
\pi
–

was employed. The network was trained in a supervised manner using simulated
3
\Lambda
H and
4
\Lambda
H signal samples, along with background distributions generated via the mixed-event technique. The reconstruction procedure follows the method described in
bib:SimonSpies_PhD
and has been previously validated for
\Lambda
hyperons
bib:LamK0sAuAu
. In parallel,
\Lambda

V
Summary

We report the first observation of
3
\Lambda
H and
4
\Lambda
H produced in heavy-ion collisions at
\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}=2.55~\mathrm{GeV}
, reconstructed via their two-body mesonic decay channels in central Ag(1.58A GeV)+Ag collisions. The hypernuclei are identified through their topological decay signatures, with background suppression enhanced by an artificial neural network.

The measured multi-differential yields
d^{2}N/(dp_{\mathrm{T}}\,dy)
and the integrated rapidity distributions
dN/dy
exhibit bell-shaped structures centered around mid-rapidity. The hypernuclear yields at mid-rapidity are compared to the existing world data. In particular, our measurement doubles the available world data points for
4
\Lambda
H.
The yield of
4
\Lambda
H is equal to or exceeds that of
3
\Lambda
H, which contrasts the measurement from the STAR collaboration at
\sqrt{\text{s\textsubscript{NN}}}
= 3 GeV which supports a secnario, in which
excited
A{=}4
states enhance
\hbox to0.0pt{\textsuperscript{4}\hss}\textsubscript{$\Lambda$}H
formation at high
\mu_{B}
.
Decay time distributions are reconstructed and corrected for acceptance and efficiency effects to extract the mean lifetimes. While the extracted lifetime of the
3
\Lambda
H is consistent with that of the free
\Lambda
hyperon within uncertainties, the
4
\Lambda
H exhibits a statistically significant deviation of approximately
4.9\,\sigma
. Including the present results, the world data show a deviation from the free
\Lambda
lifetime of
3.9\,\sigma
for the
3
\Lambda
H and
6.3\,\sigma
for the
4
\Lambda
H.

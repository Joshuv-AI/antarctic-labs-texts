Randomisation of rough stochastic differential equations
Peter K. Friz, Khoa Lê, Huilin Zhang

Abstract
Rough stochastic differential equations (RSDEs) are common generalisations of Itô SDEs and Lyons RDEs and have emerged as new tool in several areas of applied probability, including non-linear stochastic filtering, pathwise stochastic optimal control, volatility modelling in finance and mean-fields analysis of common noise system. We here take a unified perspective on rough Itô processes and discuss in particular when and how they become, upon randomisation, “doubly stochastic” Itô processes, and what can be said about their conditional laws.

The purpose of this note is to systematically relate the “hybrid” rough stochastic processes seen above to their “doubly” stochastic counterparts, when
\mathbf{Y}
above is replaced
by some stochastic driving noise, often (but not always) an independent Brownian noise (seen as rough path). Conversely, one can start with doubly stochastic situations and ask what happens under partial conditioning

[

In Section

The body of work surrounding RSDEs, control and common noise also resonates with early remarks made by the authors of
[

3.4
Itô randomisation under causality

We now see that a rough Itô processes on
\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{\prime}

{X^{\mathbf{Y}}_{t}}(\omega^{\prime}){=X^{\mathbf{Y}}_{0}}(\omega^{\prime})+\int_{0}^{t}A_{s}(\omega^{\prime},\mathbf{Y})ds+\int_{0}^{t}\Sigma_{s}(\omega^{\prime},\mathbf{Y})dB_{s}(\omega^{\prime})+\int_{0}^{t}(F_{s},F^{\prime}_{s})(\omega^{\prime},\mathbf{Y})d\mathbf{Y}_{s},

with causal coefficients, in the sense of assumption (
A3
), becomes a classical
Itô diffusion upon Brownian and also more general Itô randomisation. We note that local martingale randomisation appeared naturally in Section
2.3
.

Definition 3.7

A
(\mathfrak{F}_{t})
-adapted continuous process
S
is called
(\mathfrak{F}_{t})
-Itô diffusion
if it can be written as

\text{S${}_{t}$}=S_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\beta_{s}ds+\int_{0}^{t}\gamma_{s}dW_{s},\quad 0\leqslant t\leqslant,T

with continuous and adapted
\beta,\gamma
, where
W
is a
(\mathfrak{F}_{t})
-Brownian motion. We drop reference to the filtration
(\mathfrak{F}_{t})
when no confusion is possible.

Lemma 3.8

Let
S
be an Itô diffusion on
[0,T].

Then for every
\alpha<1/2
and with probability one,
the Itô lift
\mathbf{S}^{\operatorname{Ito}}=(S,\mathbb{S})
has sample paths in the (Polish) rough paths space

(\mathscr{C}^{0,\alpha,1},\rho_{\alpha,1})
, as defined in Section
3.1
.

Proof.

For
\beta,\gamma

uniformly bounded the statements is clear from Kolmorogov’s
criterion, noting a.s. consistency of quadratic covariation
[S^{i},S^{j}],1\leqslant i,j\leqslant d_{S}
with rough path bracket
[\mathbf{S}]
. The
general case follows from localisation.
∎

Assumption A5

Refining (
A4
), assume

\mathbf{S}=\mathbf{S}^{\operatorname{Ito}}
is a
\mathfrak{F}_{T}^{\prime\prime}
-Itô diffusion lifted (via Itô integration) to a rough path on
[0,T]

\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{\prime\prime}

Theorem 3.9

Assume (
A1
)+(
A2
)+(
A3
)+(
A5
) and let
\bar{X}
be
(the continuous modification) of

X_{t}^{\mathbf{Y}}|_{\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{S}(\omega)}

as guaranteed by Theorem

3.6
. Then
\bar{X}=\bar{X}(\omega^{\prime},\omega^{\prime\prime})
is indistinguishable from the
“doubly” Itô process

\displaystyle\bar{X}_{0}(\omega)+\int_{0}^{t}\bar{A}_{s}(\omega)ds+\int_{0}^{t}\bar{\Sigma}_{s}(\omega)dB_{s}(\omega)+\int_{0}^{t}\bar{F}_{s}(\omega)dS_{s}(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{X}_{0}(\omega)+\int_{0}^{t}(\bar{A}_{s}+\bar{F}_{s}\beta_{s})ds+\int_{0}^{t}\bar{\Sigma}_{s}(\omega)dB_{s}(\omega)+\int_{0}^{t}\bar{F}_{s}(\omega)\gamma_{s}(\omega)dW_{t}(\omega)

where, for
\Xi\in\{A,\Sigma,F\}
we write
\bar{\Xi}_{s}(\omega):=\Xi_{s}(\omega^{\prime},\mathbf{S}(\omega^{\prime\prime}))
. In particular,

\operatorname{Law}(\bar{X}_{t}|\mathfrak{F}^{\prime\prime}_{T})(\omega)=\operatorname{Law}(X^{\mathbf{Y}}_{t})|_{\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{S}(\omega)}.

Proof.

We only discuss the case
\beta=0
. Since
\gamma_{t}
is
\mathbb{P}^{\prime\prime}
-a.s. continuous,

\int_{0}^{T}\bar{F}_{s}(\omega)\gamma_{s}(\omega)\gamma^{\top}_{s}(\omega)\bar{F}_{s}^{\top}ds\leqslant\|\bar{F}\|_{\sup}^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\gamma_{s}(\omega)\gamma^{\top}_{s}(\omega)ds<\infty

which makes the last stochastic integral well-defined.
By assumption (
A5
), we have that
\bar{\Xi}(\omega)
is
\mathfrak{F}_{t}
-adapted.

We put
A_{i,n}^{t}(\omega^{\prime},\mathbf{Y})=\frac{i}{2^{n}}\text{Leb}\left(\left\{r\in[0,t]:A_{r}(\omega^{\prime},\mathbf{Y})\in[\frac{i}{2^{n}},\frac{i+1}{2^{n}})\right\}\right)
.
For any
(t,\mathbf{Y}),
we have

\int_{0}^{t}A_{r}(\omega^{\prime},\mathbf{Y})dr=:\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{i=-n2^{n}}^{n2^{n}-1}A_{i,n}^{t}(\omega^{\prime},\mathbf{Y}),

for each
\omega^{\prime}
and hence in
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
-probability.
Then by
[
23
, Corollary 8.8]
,

\left(\int_{0}^{t}A_{s}(\omega^{\prime},\mathbf{Y})ds\right)\Big|_{\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{S}(\omega^{\prime\prime})}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{i=-n2^{n}}^{n2^{n}-1}A_{i,n}^{t}(\omega^{\prime},\mathbf{S}(\omega^{\prime\prime}))\quad\text{in
$(\mathbb{P}^{\prime}\otimes\mathbb{P}^{\prime\prime})$-probability. }

On the other hand, the limit above is
\int_{0}^{t}\bar{A}_{s}(\omega)ds
, which implies the equivalence of the Lebesgue integral part.
Remark that, in case that
A
above is continuous in
t
, we could have proceeded by Riemann sum approximations, reaching the same conclusion.
In the same spirit, using classical Riemann sum approximations to stochastic integrals for integrands with continuous sample paths, we can see that
if
\Sigma
is continuous for any
(\omega^{\prime},\mathbf{Y})
, we have
\int_{0}^{t}\Sigma_{s}(\omega^{\prime},\mathbf{Y})dB_{s}|_{\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{S}(\omega^{\prime\prime})}=\int_{0}^{t}\bar{\Sigma}_{s}(\omega)dB_{s}.
For the general case, since (A2.opt) implies (A2.prog), it suffices to consider the latter. Without loss of generality, assume
\Sigma
is bounded (otherwise one may apply a localisation and repeat a similar argument as below). Consider

\sigma_{r}^{n}(\omega^{\prime},\mathbf{Y}):=n\int_{r-\frac{1}{n}}^{r}\Sigma_{s\vee 0}(\omega^{\prime},\mathbf{Y})ds,\ \ \ r\in[0,T].

It is evident that
r\mapsto\sigma^{n}(\omega^{\prime},\mathbf{Y})
is continuous. Moreover, by the Lebesgue differentiation theorem, for each
(\omega^{\prime},\mathbf{Y})
,
\lim_{n}\sigma^{n}_{r}(\omega^{\prime},\mathbf{Y})=\Sigma_{r}(\omega^{\prime},\mathbf{Y})
for a.e.
r\in[0,T]
.
Then for any
t,\mathbf{Y},
by the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem,

\lim_{n}\int_{0}^{t}|\sigma^{n}_{r}-\Sigma_{r}|^{2}(\omega^{\prime},\mathbf{Y})dr=0

for each
\omega^{\prime}
and hence in
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
-probability.
This implies that
\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{n}_{r}(\omega^{\prime},\mathbf{Y})dB_{r}
converges to
\int_{0}^{t}\Sigma_{r}(\omega^{\prime},\mathbf{Y})dB_{r}
in

\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
-probability. Note that

\left(\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{n}_{r}(\omega^{\prime},\mathbf{Y})dB_{r}\right)\Big|_{\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{S}(\omega^{\prime\prime})}=\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{n}_{r}(\omega^{\prime},\mathbf{S}(\omega^{\prime\prime}))dB_{r},

and by a similar argument,
\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{n}_{r}(\omega^{\prime},\mathbf{S}(\omega^{\prime\prime}))dB_{r}
converges in
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}\otimes\mathbb{P}^{\prime\prime}
-probability to
\int_{0}^{t}\bar{\Sigma}_{r}(\omega)dB_{r}
. We obtain the equivalence from the stochastic integral part again by
[
23
, Corollary 8.8]
.

It remains to show the randomised stochastic rough integral equal to the stochastic integral driven by
W
, which follows along the lines of
[
23
, Proposition 8.1]
. Indeed, by the convergence of rough stochastic Riemann sum and
[
23
, Corollary 8.8]
, we have

\left(\int_{0}^{t}(F,F^{\prime})^{\mathbf{Y}}(\omega^{\prime},\mathbf{Y})d\mathbf{Y}\right)\Big|_{\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{S}(\omega^{\prime\prime})}=\lim_{|\pi|\rightarrow 0}\sum_{[u,v]\in\pi}(\bar{F}_{u}\delta S_{u,v}+\bar{F}^{\prime}_{u}\mathbb{S}_{u,v})(\omega^{\prime},\omega^{\prime\prime})\quad\text{in
$\mathbb{P}$-probability. }

(3.5)

On the other hand, note that for any
\varepsilon>0,

\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\sum_{[u,v]\in\pi}\bar{F}^{\prime}_{u}\mathbb{S}_{u,v}\right|>\varepsilon\right)=\mathbb{E}^{\prime\prime}\left(\mathbb{P}^{\prime}\left(\left|\sum_{[u,v]\in\pi}F^{\prime}_{u}(\omega^{\prime},\mathbf{Y})\mathbb{S}_{u,v}\right|>\varepsilon\right)_{\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{S}(\omega^{\prime\prime})}\right),

the right-hand side of which converges to null as
|\pi|
vanishes by the bounded convergence theorem. It follows that the left-hand side of (
3.5
) is the limit in
\mathbb{P}
-probability of
\sum_{[u,v]\in\pi}\bar{F}_{u}\delta S_{u,v}
, which is exactly
\int_{0}^{t}\bar{F}_{s}dS_{s}
by classical stochastic calculus, and hence completes our proof.
(The final “in particular” is immediate from Theorem
3.6
.)
∎

Remark 3.10

Concerning the Itô and Lebesgue integral in the last proof. To see that,
\mathbb{P}

A monotone class argument extends this first to all bounded progessive
integrands, a truncation argument to give the equality in the full generality
of
a(\Sigma\Sigma^{T})^{\mathbf{Y}}\in L^{1}([0,T]),

\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
a.s.
Similarly, but easier, one sees

\left(\int_{0}^{t}A_{s}(\omega^{\prime},\mathbf{Y})ds\right)|_{\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{S}(\omega^{\prime\prime})}=\int_{0}^{t}\bar{A}_{s}(\omega)ds.

\omega

Theorem 5.1

The solution

to the rough McKean-Vlasov SDEs (

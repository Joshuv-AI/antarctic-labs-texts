Evaluation of the usability of trachydacitic aggregate in rigid pavements in terms of strength and durability
Authors: Muhammed Tanyıldızı, Hasan Erhan Yücel, Maciej Dutkiewicz, Hakan Çoban, Abdulrezzak Bakış
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The research presented here investigates the usability of trachydacitic, an unexploited material that occurred in nature, as aggregate in the construction of concrete/rigid pavements under two different curing conditions. These curing conditions are specified as 28 days 20 ± 5 ˚C water curing and combined curing including 3 days 20 ± 5 ˚C water curing + 2 days dry oven curing at 200 ± 5 ˚C. Four different combinations of trachydacitic aggregated concrete as conventional concrete (CC) and high strength concrete (HSC), with steel fiber and fiberless, were produced at different cement and water contents. Mechanical (compressive and flexural strength), durability (freeze-thaw and sorptivity) and surface abrasion properties of studied concretes were tested for each curing condition. Additionally, SEM-EDX and XRD analyses were conducted to investigate the microstructural, elemental and mineralogical changes with curing. According to the test results, the highest compressive and flexural strength were obtained from the DLPB (Trachydacitic aggregated fibrous HSC) as 75,09 MPa and 10,09 MPa respectively after the combined curing process. Moreover, the combined curing process increased the resistance of concrete to freeze-thaw and water sorptivity, while reducing its resistance to Böhme abrasion. Microstructural investigations also revealed that HSCs have a denser structure compared to conventional concretes. As a result, it was determined that the powder form of trachydacitic aggregate, unlike its coarse form, can be used in HSC road pavement construction.

Introduction

In many countries, concrete/rigid pavements have been widely used as an alternative to asphalt pavements at highways, airport runways, terminal service roads, parking lots and inner city roads, due to their high durability under severe environmental conditions, less maintenance and long service life compared to asphalt pavements1–3. Concrete pavements are the use of a concrete mix in accordance with the required properties as a surface coating in highway engineering4,5. Concrete pavements have an effective rigidity and tensile strength to distribute stresses created by traffic from the upper surface to the subbase and safely resist adverse climatic effects6.

Concrete is known to be the most consumable material after water around the world in nearly all types of civil engineering facilities, including highway pavements because of its versatility and availability7,8 and aggregate is the main component of concrete because of compromising approximately 70% of total volume. Composing such an important percentage of volume, the sustainability of aggregate is a crucial concern in the construction industry and recently there has been an increasing interest in sustainable production for road construction. The concept of sustainable construction emerged two decades ago to ensure green development in the construction industry and efficient use of resources in the sector9. At this point, it is important to prevent excessive use of the traditionally used aggregates in order to prevent depletion of natural resources and preserve the environmental balance. Sustainability in concrete pavement can be achieved by using different types of aggregates from different sources by partially or completely replacing the traditionally used aggregate10–12.

Studies on the production of aggregates that can be used as an alternative to aggregates traditionally used in concrete road construction are increasing and their use is becoming more important. Pumice, Ahlat stone, marble, limonite, basalt, granite and perlite are some of the aggregates investigated for use as alternative aggregates in concrete pavements. Hattatoglu and Bakis13 studied the usability of Ahlat stone in the construction of rigid pavement and obtained high strength values from the test results. Kınay14 proved from experimental analyses the usability of basalt aggregate in the construction of concrete pavements. In another study, Arman15 performed experimental analyses to show the usability of perlite to produce concrete road pavements. Amani et al.16 examined the mechanical properties of concrete pavements containing a combination of marble and granite powder. The test results indicated that the mechanical strength decreases with the increase in marble content, but increases with granite content. Bakış17 investigated the usability of pumice aggregate in reactive powder concrete for rigid pavements and test results showed that pumice aggregate can be usable for the construction of rigid pavements. Öz18 performed a study including durability tests on the pervious concrete pavements partially incorporating pumice as aggregate. Test results revealed that pervious concrete pavement with pumice aggregate exhibited better durability performance regardless of the replacement level with conventional aggregate.

The usability of trachydacitic aggregate emitted by Nemrut Volcano in different types of concretes for rigid pavements was investigated in this study. Trachydacitic is a silica-rich volcanic rock formed by the rapid solidification of lava. Trachydacitic rock has a fine-grained texture and chemical composition similar to andesite and rhyolite. It consists predominantly of plagioclase feldspar and quartz19,20. Studies in the literature have not been encountered any rigid and/or rigid pavement using trachydacitic aggregate before. Demonstrating the usability of trachydacitic aggregate as an alternative to natural aggregates commonly used in pavements is important in preventing the depletion of natural resources and environmental imbalances due to unplanned mining activities21. Considering the increasing need for aggregates in the construction of concrete roads along with rapid urbanization and industrialization, the use of alternative aggregates will be more sustainable in both economical and environmental ways by conserving natural sources and minimizing the cost of accessing the resource22. Purchasing newly discovered alternative aggregate from a source close to where it will be used can reduce transportation costs compared to trucking raw aggregate from a distant quarry or pit23. For this reason, this study is the first in the literature to investigate the use of concrete produced with trachydacitic aggregate as a rigid pavement by determining its basic mechanical and durability properties. This research also enables the evaluation and widespread use of trachydacite aggregates cropping out in nature. In this regard, in addition to a conventional concrete type, it was aimed to obtain high strength values with HSC types under two different curing regimes including standard 28 days 20 ± 5 ˚C water curing and combined curing. Since there is no standard specified in the literature for a combined cure, a special cure type was applied to concrete specimens including 3 days 20 ± 5 ˚C water curing + 2 days dry oven curing at 200 ± 5 ˚C. Additionally, four new types of trachydacitic aggregated concrete including fibrous and non-fibrous were produced. Many experimental studies were applied on hardened concrete specimens in order to obtain the mechanical (compressive and flexural), durability (freeze-thaw and sorptivity) and surface abrasion properties of trachydacitic aggregated concretes.

Mixture proportions

In this study, four new types of fibrous and non-fibrous concrete were designed for experimental analysis. Abbreviations such as DB, DLB, DPB and DLPB were coded to explain different concrete mix types. The DB states trachydacitic aggregated non-fibrous conventional concrete; the DLB means trachydacitic aggregated fibrous conventional concrete; the DPB and DLPB mean trachydacitic aggregated non-fibrous (the former) and fibrous (the later) high strength concrete, respectively. In the mixture design of DB and DLB concretes with the C30/37 strength properties, TS 80232 and TS 10,51433 standards were taken into consideration for the design of concrete mixes, and proportioning and production of fiber-reinforced concrete, respectively. As to DPB and DLPB, Richard and Cheyrezy’s concrete mixture ratios for reactive powder concrete derived from Mooney’s suspension viscosity model were based on designs34. All DPB and DLPB samples produced to be used in the experiments were prepared according to the following procedure, the details of which are given respectively. Firstly, binders and aggregates were dry-mixed for 5 min. Then, half of the water and superplasticizer were poured and mixed for 5 min. After that, the remaining water and superplasticizer were poured and mixed for another 5 min. Fresh concrete was poured into molds and compacted by vibration to remove air voids. The samples were left in the mold for 24 h and then removed for curing applications.

In the preparation of fiber concrete, steel fibers were included in the mixture immediately after the remaining water and superplasticizer were added to the mixture. For DB and DLB specimens, a standard concrete preparation method was used; a dry mix of binders and aggregates and adding water until obtaining a concrete grout.

Two types of curing applications were carried out to the specimens; standard water curing 20 ± 5 ˚C for 28 days and combined curing. However, there are no standards established for combined curing in the literature. For instance, Hattatoğlu and Bakis13 obtained high strength values by applying serial water curing at 20˚C for 7 days, hot water curing at 90˚C for 2 days, and drying oven curing at 180˚C for 2 days. In this study, a special type of combined curing including 20 ± 5 ˚C water curing for 3 days, followed by 200 ± 5 drying furnace curing for 2 days was applied to the specimens. For abbreviations, WC and CC are used for water curing and combined curing respectively.

Experimental studies

Pozzolanic activity of trachydacitic aggregate

The pozzolans are either natural or artificial materials that have no cementitious property naturally, but when grained to fine-grained size, they react with calcium hydroxide in water and start to show binding properties. In addition, the term ‘pozzolanic activity’ is identified as the ability of the active silica in the pozzolan to react with calcium hydroxide and water36. According to the chemical analysis of the trachydacitic aggregate performed in this study, it was determined that approximately 86.20% of its structure consists of SiO2, AlO2 and Fe2O3 (presented in Table 3). TS 25 standard25 states that a natural puzzolan must consist of at least 70% total of SiO2, AlO2 and Fe2O3 by mass and the compressive strength value of the specimen prepared with natural pozzolan must also meet the minimum value of 4 MPa at the end of the curing process specified in the TS 2525. For this purpose, three samples with 4 × 4 × 16 cm dimensions were prepared in a mixture ratio presented in Table 6 specified in TS 2525. After specimens waited in molds for one day at room temperature, they were put in a dry oven for six days at (55 ± 2) °C without removing from molds. After the curing process, its compressive strength was determined.

The consistency test of fresh concrete

The consistency of fresh concrete was evaluated according to the slump test. The fresh concrete was cast into a cone with 3 layers, and each layer was compacted with 25 strokes of compacting rod. The cone was withdrawn upwards in 2 to 5 s. To determine the consistency of fresh concrete, the average slump value of the concrete was measured following the TS EN 12350-237 standard.

Strength tests

Pavements must be designed to withstand the loads created by heavy vehicles and passengers. Therefore, the mechanical properties including compressive and flexural strength in the study are the most important parameters in the design of the concrete pavements. These properties of hardened concrete specimens were determined by applying compressive and three-point flexural strength testing after curing applications in accordance with TS EN 12390-338 and TS EN 12390-539 standards, respectively. The loading rate was determined as 0.6 MPa/s in the compressive strength test and 0.05 MPa/s in the flexural strength test. Cubic samples with dimensions of 150 × 150 × 150 mm were used for compressive strength, and prismatic samples with dimensions of 75 × 75 × 300 mm were used for flexural strength test. The load was applied from the mid-span of the specimen during flexural strength test and the clear distance between supports was 225 mm. Three samples were poured for each strength test and results were reported based on their average.

Durability tests

Concrete pavements exposed to the destructive effects of freezing and thawing in cold climates are expected to withstand these effects adequately, and these effects generally manifest themselves in the form of peeling on the pavement surface. To obtain the durability of concrete specimens subjected to the deteriorative effects mentioned above, freeze-thaw tests were performed following the TS 2824 EN 133843 standard. Surface abrasion test

The surface of a concrete pavement, which is directly exposed to heavy vehicle and foot pressure throughout its service life, is expected to effectively resist the abrasive effect caused by the skidding, sliding, and breaking friction of the vehicles. The pavement with a lower value of abrasion is preferred47,48. The Bohme abrasion method was used to measure the abrasion value of concrete specimens to determine the resistance level after being subjected to the effects mentioned above. The abrasion test was applied to specimens with the dimensions of 71 × 71 × 71 mm. For all curing and concrete types, three specimens were poured and results of the average of these samples were reported as the result, which was following the standard TS 2824 EN 1338 43. Microstructural and mineralogical investigations

The samples with the highest compressive strength for each curing process were selected to be included in the microstructural and mineralogical investigations. Scanning electron microscope (SEM) – Energy dispersive X-ray (EDX) analyses were performed to observe the microstructure of fractured concrete specimens and determine the existing elements present in the structure using the Philips XL30 FEG device and EDX detector. The dimensions of the broken piece taken from the sample were approximately 1 × 1 × 1 cm and its surface was coated with gold in order to increase the electrical conductivity before analysis due to the poor electrical conductivity of concretes. The SEM and EDX analyses were performed using an accelerating voltage of 15 kV49,50.

The X-ray diffraction (XRD) method was used to investigate the mineral phase in cementitious materials of concrete specimens using a Bruker D8 Advance. The specimens were taken from hardened concrete and ground in powder form. The test parameters were 40 kV, the scanning angle was set within the 2θ range of 5°–80°49,50.

Results and discussion

Pozzolanic activity result of trachydacitic aggregate

The pozzolanic reaction occurs when siliceous or siliceous aluminous materials, which do not exhibit cement properties themselves, come into contact with cement and consume the Ca(OH)2 formed during hydration, resulting in the formation of C-S-H gels and the densification of the microstructure51–53. When Table 3 is examined, it is seen that the trachydacitic aggregate contains high amounts of SiO2 and this is expected to increase the strength of cementitious mixtures as binders through pozzolanic reaction54,55. As stated in TS 2525, the materials should provide the required minimum compressive strength value of 4 MPa for being used as a cementitious material at the end of the pozzolanic activity test. Therefore, this test was performed in the study to show the usability of trachydacitic aggregate’s powder form as a cementitious material in the production of high-strength concrete pavement. At the end of the pozzolanic activity test, the compressive strength of the trachydacitic aggregate is 10.12 MPa, meeting the minimum strength value of 4 MPa. The result suggests that the fine-grained size of trachydacitic aggregate can be used as cementitious material based on TS 2525. In the mix design of concrete specimens of this study, the fine-grained size of the trachydacitic aggregate was taken into consideration as a cementitious material.

The consistency of fresh concrete results

The consistency of fresh concrete mixtures is presented in Table 7 and the effect of steel fiber content on the fluidity of concrete mixtures is indicated in Fig. 7. Since HSC types have a denser structure consisting of micro grain-sized materials, their slump values ​​are lower than traditional concretes. The concrete types involving steel fiber have lower slump values than concrete types without fibers. Additionally, the slump values ​​decreased with the increase in the amount of steel fiber. This is attributed to the increased surface area in the mixture and the formation of tight mesh structures with the incorporation of steel fiber56.

Strength test results

The compressive and flexural strength test results of concrete mixtures under different curing conditions are presented in Figs. 8 and 9, respectively. When the results are examined for each curing condition, it is seen that HSC samples have higher strength values ​​than conventional concretes. This can be explained as the improvement of the microstructure of the concrete, together with the obtaining of a more homogeneous and denser structure57. This improvement in the HSC types mixture can also be interpreted by the pozzolanic reaction of silica fume, which can increase the strength of concrete under high heat curing conditions58. Both the compressive and flexural strengths of steel fiber concrete are higher than those of fiber-free concrete, and the increase in flexural strength is more remarkable. The formation and development of cracks in fiber-reinforced concretes is prevented to a certain extent compared to those without fiber-reinforced concretes, thus improving the strength59. For each mixture type, it was observed that combined cured samples had higher compressive and flexural strength values ​​than water-cured samples. This improvement is due to the application of high heat to the concrete after water curing60. The hydration rate of concrete increases with the high heat curing application, in this way higher strength is obtained from combined curing applied specimens56,61,62. The strength limits according to the concrete pavements technical specifications of the General Directorate of Highways of Türkiye63 are 28 MPa and 4,5 MPa for compressive and flexural strength respectively. All presented strength results meet the specification limits mentioned above.

Durability test results

Freeze-thaw

The performance of concrete specimens against freeze-thaw in terms of the mass per unit area (kg/m2) at 28 days of cycles is depicted in Fig. 10. All freeze-thaw values of concrete specimens meet the specification limits specified in the concrete pavements technical specifications of the General Directorate of Highways of Türkiye63. When the results were examined, it was understood that combined cured concrete samples showed better freeze-thaw performance than water-cured concretes. For DB types of concrete, the freeze-thaw resistance of concrete increased by 36% with the combined curing compared with water curing. This value for DLB, DPB and DLPB concrete types was 17,40%, 8,70% and 13,64%, respectively. This is most likely due to the acceleration of the hydration process at high temperatures and the decrease in concrete permeability as a result of the filling of the voids in the concrete with hydration products64.

Sorptivity

The results of the sorptivity coefficients of specimens are given in Fig. 11. As can be seen from Fig. 11, sorptivity coefficients of specimens decreased with combined curing. The reduction of the sorptivity coefficent of specimens with combined curing compared to the water curing was 19,08%, 73,6%, 40,66% and 12,5% for DB, DLB, DPB and DLPB specimens, respectively. This can be attributed to the enhancement of the interface transition zone as a result of the acceleration of the hydration process at high temperatures and the decrease in concrete permeability as the pores in the concrete mixture are filled with hydration products49,64.

Abrasion

The results of the Bohme abrasion test for concrete mixtures in terms of the reduction in the volume (cm3/50 cm2) are depicted in Fig. 12. All abrasion values of concrete specimens meet the specification limits specified in the concrete pavements technical specifications of the General Directorate of Highways of Türkiye63. The results obtained showed that the abrasion resistance of water-cured concretes was higher than combined-cured concretes for all types. This is a likely consequence of the wearing effect of high heat on the concrete. Additionally, it had been observed that the addition of steel fiber to concrete had positive effects on the abrasion resistance of concrete, increasing by 52% for conventional and HSCs cured in water. As to combined curing, this value was approximately 27% and 15% conventional and HSC types, respectively. As is known, concrete is exposed to stresses under the influence of corrosive substances. As a result, the dissociations occur at the internal structure and surface of the concrete. However, adding steel fiber to concrete limits this expansion by increasing the bond strength of the concrete by bridging the fiber effect between the mixture materials, thus preventing cracking and abrasion of the concrete and reducing damage due to abrasion65,66.

Microstructural investigations

Scanning electron microscope images magnified 1000 times are shown to provide more detailed information about the microstructure of the concrete in Figs. 13 and 14 for water and combined cured specimens, respectively. The investigations revealed that HSCs had a denser microstructure than conventional concretes for each curing condition. Although the HSC matrix phase was denser than that of normal concrete, it also included pores (in the dark hole). In addition, the porosity of the heat applied, combined-cured concretes depicted in Fig. 14 is more than that of water-cured concretes because of the loss of the water in the concrete with the high heat67. Partly, some micro cracks are observed in both types of concrete. In Fig. 14, it is observed that the cracks increase in the heat treatment concretes. In addition, with the application of heat treatment, the proportion of particles participating in the hydration process increases due to both the fine-grained aggregate and the pozzolanic properties of silica fume49. When the SEM images of the silica fume added concretes DPB and DLPB and the DB and DLB concretes without silica fume were compared, it was concluded that the silica fume added concretes had a more compact and dense structure due to the positive effect of silica fume on the denser structure and strength of the concretes68. In all types of concrete SEM micrographs, C-S-H gels in mesh structures were observed. Another hydration product CH (calcium hydroxide) in plate surface view was also seen. The appearance of this product was reduced in the SEM images given in Fig. 14. This was attributed to the consumption of it with the pozzolanic reactions of trachydacitic aggregate powder and silica fume to produce C-S-H gels51. Ettringite formations are not observed in the SEM images, due to probably low water/binder ratio and C3A content of cement34,49,61,69–71. HSC type of studied concretes includes unhydrated cement grains as shown in Fig. 14-c and -d (light grey areas). This is because of the low water-to-binder ratio which is not sufficient to fully hydrate cement particles72. Therefore, cement particles behave like a fine-grained aggregate in the matrix. Also, a low water-to-binder ratio leads to cement particles closer to each other66,73. In addition, small spherical silica fume is observed in HSC type concretes, and this fume participates in the secondary hydration process with calcium hydroxide in the later ages of the concrete. Silica fume, which has a pozzolanic effect, is expected to consume calcium hydroxide and produce C-S-H gel. The production of more C-S-H as calcium hydroxide decreases has a positive effect on concrete strength36.

The EDX spot analysis corresponding to the SEM micrographs for each curing process of concrete types is presented in Figs. 15, 16, 17 and 18. The EDX provides basic quantitive information on material in the composition of concrete. The dominant elements are Si, Ca and Al for each concrete type. The source of Si elements in concrete is trachydacitic aggregate and silica fume. The addition of silica fume to HSC type concretes causes an increase in the peaks of Si and Al elements. The Ca and Si peak indicates the presence of C-S-H gel in the concrete74. Ca peak increases, with the increasing cement content in HSC types of concretes, compared to conventional concretes. Moreover, Ca peaks in HSC types of concretes compared to conventional concretes are due to the rate of hydration. Additionally, for all types of concrete, with the combined cured under the effect of heat treatment, the Ca peak decreases resulting increase in the Si peak. This situation is attributed to the pozzolanic reaction of trachydacitic aggregate powder and silica fume with calcium hydroxide to produce calcium silicate hydrate products75. In the EDX analysis of the fibrous concrete given in Fig. 17a, a Fe peak was observed, which can be attributed to the EDX point analysis passing over the steel fiber.

When the specimens are under the influence of water curing, the calcium hydroxide (portlandite) peaks have some surplus. When concrete specimens are under the influence of the combined curing, the diffraction peak of portlandite decreases. This situation is attributed to the pozzolanic effect of aggregate powder and silica fume, which is fast under heat treatment to produce secondary amorphous C-S-H gels by consuming portlandite76. As to quartz peak, it is due to silica fume and aggregate composition, and it is increasing with the usage of silica fume in the concrete.

Practical implementation

Today, the need for aggregates in road construction is increasing due to the increasing need for new road networks along with rapid industrialization and urbanization. However, excessive use of traditionally used natural aggregates causes depletion of natural resources and environmental imbalances. Therefore, there is an urgent need to use alternative aggregate sources in construction facilities in order to contribute to sustainable development. Since the aggregate sources are not renewable and their excessive use leads to environmental impacts. In this research study, the usability of trachydacitic aggregate in the construction of rigid pavements has shown promising results in terms of strength and durability. In addition, the use of trachydacitic aggregate in road construction helps preserve natural resources and environmental balance and reduces the cost of accessing to aggregates. Considering the basis of the study topic, investigation strategies can be utilized by research centers and construction representatives to provide insights into areas that will be very useful for future experiments and research on the use of trachydacitic aggregate in rigid pavements.

Conclusion

The usability of trachydacitic aggregate in rigid pavement construction was the focal subject of this study. Four different types of rigid pavements (DB, DLB, DPB and DLPB) with and without steel fiber were produced and their mechanical, durability and microstructural/mineralogical properties were compared with each other. The results based on the experimental studies are given below;

The combined curing has a positive effect on the strength of the concrete specimens. This is attributed to the improvement of the hydration process. In addition, by adding steel fibers to concrete, both compressive and flexural strengths are increased due to the bridging effect that holds the concrete components together and prevents breakage. Among the four new types of concrete and two different curing regimes (water and combined curing), the highest compressive and flexural strength obtained from DLPB specimens were 75,09 MPa and 10,08 MPa under combined curing and with the addition of steel fiber.

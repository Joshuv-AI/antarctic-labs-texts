On Extremal Properties of 𝒌 k -CNF: Capturing Threshold Functions
Mohit Gurumukhani, Marvin Künnemann, Ramamohan Paturi

Abstract
We consider a basic question on the expressiveness of k k -CNF formulas: How well can k k -CNF formulas capture threshold functions? Specifically, what is the largest number of assignments (of Hamming weight t t ) accepted by a k k -CNF formula that only accepts assignments of weight at least t t ? Among others, we provide the following results: • While an optimal solution is known for t ≤ n / k t\leq n/k , the problem remains open for t > n / k t>n/k . We formulate a (monotone) version of the problem as an extremal hypergraph problem and show that for t = n − k t=n-k , the problem is exactly the Turán problem. • For t = α ​ n t=\alpha n with constant α \alpha , we provide a construction and show its optimality for 2 2 -CNF. Optimality of the construction for k > 2 k>2 would give improved lower bounds for depth- 3 3 circuits.

Computation of threshold functions in various circuits models has been studied by several researchers and there are a number of results on the

In applications involving satisfiability solvers, researchers have studied how to encode threshold functions – or, equivalently,

Our Results

Our first result demonstrates the difficulty of determining
S(n,t,k)
by establishing a connection to a classic open problem in combinatorics, specifically, the Turán problem.
The
Turán number

T(n,q,k)
denotes the size of the smallest system
\mathcal{S}
of size
k
sets (
k
-sets) over
[n]
such that every
q
-set over
[n]
contains some
S\in\mathcal{S}
.
It turns out that for large thresholds
t=n-k
,
S(n,t,k)
is determined by a corresponding Turán number:

Theorem 1
.

For any
n\geq k\geq 2
, we have that
S(n,n-k,k)=\binom{n}{k}-T(n,k+1,k)
.

We define the asymptotic Turán numbers as
T(q,k)=\limsup T(n,q,k)/\binom{n}{k}
.
Unfortunately, the exact value is not known even for
T(4,3)

(in fact, Erdős posed a reward of $500 for this question).
It is known that
0.4383334\leq T(4,3)\leq 4/9

[
11
]
. However,
closing the gap remains to be an open problem.
Thus determining
S(n,t,3)
for all
t
requires further progress on Turán numbers.

Note that it is known that Turán numbers have a close connection
to CNF formula size
(as opposed to the number of solutions)

[
21
]
.
We show that for the problem of determining
S(n,t,k)
for
t=n-k
,
minimizing the formula size is equivalent to maximizing the solution set (note that this is not necessarily true in general).

Theorem

1
demonstrates the underlying difficulty of determining
S(n,t,k)
for all values of
t
even for constant
k
. Similarly, for the regime of large clause widths (where
n-k
is constant), we obtain a exact correspondence to Steiner systems, whose existence is extensively studied (
Section

6

Let us now consider the ‘middle’ region, the case of linear thresholds, i.e.,
t=\alpha n
for some
\alpha\in[0,1]
. An
obvious generalization of Wolfovitz’s construction is to partition the variables into blocks of size at most
k
, and ensure that for each block, all satisfying assignment have at least
\alpha k
ones.
We show that this obvious construction is not optimal. We obtain a better construction by choosing block sizes adaptively based on the threshold.

Adaptive block construction:

For a clearer presentation, we assume that
b:=(k-1)/(1-\alpha)
is an integer
and it divides
n
. We divide the variables into blocks
B_{1},\dots,B_{n/b}
of size
b
. For each block
B_{i}
, we introduce monotone clauses
(x_{1}\vee x_{2}\vee\cdots\vee x_{k})
where
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
are distinct variables in

. It is straightforward to see (

must have at least

\alpha

S(n,\alpha n,k)\geq\binom{b}{\alpha b}^{n/b}\text{ whenever $b=(k-1)/(1-\alpha)$ divides $n$}.

Assume that
\alpha k
is an integer and

both divide

that this bound improves over
\binom{k}{\alpha k}^{n/k}

Conjecture 2
.

The adaptive construction is optimal: Let

such that
b=(k-1)/(1-\alpha)
is an integer. Whenever
b

n

S(n,\alpha n,k)=\binom{b}{\alpha b}^{n/b}.

The
k=2

While we could not establish

we provide evidence for

Theorem 3

For every

In fact, we can establish the exact values of

Motivation: Circuit Lower Bounds

Let
F(n,t,k)
denote the smallest number of
k
-CNF formulas required to cover
\mathrm{THR}_{t}

\mathrm{THR}_{t}=\bigvee_{i=1}^{q}\psi_{i}
for some
k
-CNF formulas
\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{q}
.

F(n,t,k)
is the minimum fan-in of the top OR gate
in a
\Sigma\Pi\Sigma_{k}

\mathrm{THR}_{t}
.
Note that each
\psi_{i}
must not accept assignments of weights less than
t
– we call such a formula
t
-admissible. We observe that

is almost tightly controlled by

and the analogously defined

t
assignments accepted by a

t
-admissible
k
-CNF.

Observation 4
.

\frac{\binom{n}{t}}{S(n,t,k)}\leq F(n,t,k)\leq\left\lceil\frac{\binom{n}{t}}{S^{+}(n,t,k)}\cdot n\right\rceil.

Proof.

The first inequality is immediate, as there are
\binom{n}{t}
weight-
t
assignments to be covered, while any admissible
k
-CNF formula can accept at most
S(n,t,k)
weight-
t
assignments.

To show the second inequality, we use the probabilistic method: Let
\psi
be a monotone

-admissible
k
-CNF formula on
n

t
. Let
D
be the distribution of
k
-CNF formulas obtained from
\psi
by taking a uniformly chosen random permutation of the variables. If
\psi^{\prime}
is sampled from

, the probability that a fixed assignment of weight

satisfies
\psi^{\prime}
is

S^{+}(n,t,k)/\binom{n}{t}
.

Let
\phi
be the disjunction of

assignments shows that with positive probability,

accepts all assignments of weight

Preliminaries

We set
[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
. A CNF formula
F
over the variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
is a conjunction of
clauses

(X_{i_{1}}\lor\dots\lor X_{i_{k}})
, where each
literal

X_{i_{j}}
is either a variable
x_{i_{j}}
or its negation
\overline{x}_{i_{j}}
. Here,
k
is called the
width
of the clause.
F
is said to be a
k
-CNF formula if all of its clauses have width at most
k
. Moreover,
F
is called
monotone
if all of its clauses only contain positive literals. We will often identify a CNF formula
F
with the Boolean function
F:\{0,1\}^{n}\to\{0,1\}
it computes. Throughout, we use lower-case letters
x,y,z
to denote variables and upper-case letters
X,Y,Z
to denote their corresponding literals.

For an
assignment

\alpha\in\{0,1\}^{n}
, let
\operatorname{wt}(\alpha)=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}
denote the Hamming weight of
\alpha
. Let
F:\{0,1\}^{n}\to\{0,1\}
be a Boolean function. We set
\operatorname{sat}(F)=\{\alpha:F(\alpha)=1\}
and
\operatorname{unsat}(F)=\{0,1\}^{n}\setminus\operatorname{sat}(F)
. We let
\operatorname{sat}_{t}(F)
denote the subset of satisfying assignments with Hamming weight equal to
t
. We also say that a formula
F
is
t
-
admissible
if
F
does not satisfy any assignment with weight less than
t
. By definition, every formula is
0
-admissible.

We finally define the quantities which are the main focus of this paper. For
k,t\leq n
, let:

•

S(n,t,k)=\max\{\,|\operatorname{sat}_{t}(F)|:\text{$F$ is a $t$-admissible $k$-CNF over $n$ variables}\,\}
,

•

S^{+}(n,t,k)=\max\{\,|\operatorname{sat}_{t}(F)|:\text{$F$ is a $t$-admissible \emph{monotone} $k$-CNF over $n$ variables}\,\}
,

•

F(n,t,k)=\min\{q:\mathrm{THR}_{t}=\bigvee_{i=1}^{q}\psi_{i}\}
where
\psi_{i}
are
k
-CNF formulas.

Lemma 6

(
[
15
, Chapter 10, Lemma 7]
)
.

For
\alpha\in(0,1)
, let
H(\alpha)=-\alpha\log_{2}(\alpha)-(1-\alpha)\log_{2}(1-\alpha)
denote the binary entropy function. Then, for all
n\geq 0
:

\displaystyle\frac{2^{nH(\alpha)}}{\sqrt{8n\alpha(1-\alpha)}}\leq\binom{n}{\alpha n}\leq\frac{2^{nH(\alpha)}}{\sqrt{2\pi n\alpha(1-\alpha)}}.

Bounds on
S(n,t,k)

In this section we propose
k
-CNF constructions for optimally
capturing threshold functions.
Our constructions are block constructions: we group variables
into blocks and design a
k
-CNF on each block of variables to accept assignments with certain minimum Hamming weight.
We focus on the regime where
k
is constant and
t
may depend on
n
.
For small thresholds (
t\leq n/k
), we show that our constructions are optimal and unique.
For intermediate thresholds (
t=\alpha n
for
0<\alpha<1
), we show we can do better by varying block sizes as well as thresholds of the blocks using our adaptive block construction.
In
Section

4
, we will show that for
k=2
, our adaptive block construction is optimal for all thresholds
t
.

We will first explain how the block construction works
by showing that the blocks can be put together to create the final
k
-CNF. Subsequently, we design specific
k
-CNF for the blocks.

Lemma 7

(Putting the block together)
.

Let
k,t_{1},\dots,t_{a},n_{1},\dots,n_{a}
be positive integers. We then have

\displaystyle S\left(\sum_{i=1}^{a}n_{i},\sum_{i=1}^{a}t_{i},k\right)\geq\prod_{i=1}^{a}S(n_{i},t_{i},k).

Proof.

Suppose that
G_{i}
is a
t_{i}
-admissible
k
-CNF formula and
|\operatorname{sat}_{t_{i}}(G)|=S(n_{i},t_{i},k)
. Let
F=\bigwedge_{i=1}^{a}G_{i}
where the subformulas
G_{i}
have distinct input variables.
F
has
\sum_{i}n_{i}
input variables in total and it is easy to check that
\operatorname{sat}(F)=\operatorname{sat}(G_{1})\times\dots\times\operatorname{sat}(G_{a})
. Moreover,
F
is
t
-admissible where
t:=\sum_{i}t_{i}
and
|\operatorname{sat}_{t}(F)|=\prod_{i}|\operatorname{sat}_{t_{i}}(G_{i})|=\prod_{i}S(n_{i},t_{i},k)
.
∎

For most applications it suffices to apply
Lemma

7
in the following simpler form, where all
t_{i}
are the same and all
n_{i}
are the same:

S(an,at,k)\geq S(n,t,k)^{a}.

3.1
Bounds for Small Thresholds

We first focus on the setting
t\leq n/k
. In this regime it is easy to prove optimal bounds for
S(n,t,k)
; these bounds first proved in
[
27
]
. We provide a stronger version of the same theorem where we show the uniqueness (up to isomorphism) of the optimal solution for
t\leq n/k
.

Theorem 8

(Small Thresholds)
.

For
n\geq k\cdot t
,
S(n,t,k)=k^{t}
. Moreover, a
k
-CNF formula attains this bound only if it has a subset of
t
disjoint clauses of the form
(x_{1}\lor\dots\lor x_{k})
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
S(n,t,k)\geq k^{t}
by analyzing the claimed unique construction: Let
M(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})=(x_{1}\lor\dots\lor x_{k})
be the
k
-CNF formula accepting all inputs with Hamming weight at least one, and observe that
M
certifies the bound
S(k,1,k)\geq k
. By
Lemma

7
it follows that
S(n,t,k)\geq S(kt,t,k)\geq S(k,1,k)^{t}=k^{t}
.

Next we prove that
S(n,t,k)\leq k^{t}
by induction on
t
. The base case
t=0
is trivial: There is only one assignment with Hamming weight
0
. For the inductive step assume that
t\geq 1
and let
F
be a
t
-admissible optimal formula and
|\operatorname{sat}_{t}(F)|=S(n,t,k)
. Since the all-zero assignment does not satisfy
F
,
F
must contain a monotone clause, say
F=F^{\prime}\land(x_{1}\lor\dots\lor x_{k})
. Observe that
F^{\prime}
is a
(t-1)
-admissible
k
-CNF formula. Indeed, whenever an assignment satisfies
F^{\prime}
it suffices to switch on at most one additional variable amongst
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
to satisfy
F
.
Using distributive law on the last clause, we see that
F=\bigvee_{i=1}^{k}(F^{\prime}\land x_{i})
and so,

|\operatorname{sat}_{t}(F)|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}|\operatorname{sat}_{t}(F^{\prime}\land x_{i})|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}|\operatorname{sat}_{t-1}(F^{\prime})|=k\cdot|\operatorname{sat}_{t-1}(F^{\prime})|

It follows by the induction hypothesis that
|\operatorname{sat}_{t}(F)|\leq k\cdot|\operatorname{sat}_{t-1}(F^{\prime})|\leq k^{t}
.

To prove uniqueness of the optimal construction, suppose that
|\operatorname{sat}_{t}(F)|=k^{t}
. Then the inequality
|\operatorname{sat}_{t}(F)|\leq k\cdot|\operatorname{sat}_{t-1}(F^{\prime})|\leq k^{t}
must be tight and we must have
|\operatorname{sat}_{t-1}(F^{\prime})|=k^{t-1}
. Therefore, by the induction hypothesis
F^{\prime}
contains
t-1
disjoint monotone
k
-clauses
C_{1},\dots,C_{t-1}
and the weight
t-1
satisfying assignments of
F^{\prime}
are exactly the assignments which switch on one variable from each clause
C_{i}
. Let
C_{t}:=(x_{1}\lor\dots\lor x_{k})
; we claim that
C_{t}
is disjoint from
C_{1},\dots,C_{t-1}
. Indeed, if
C_{t}
shares a variable, say
x_{1}
, with another clause
C_{i}
, then we can construct a satisfying assignment of
F
with Hamming weight less than
t
by switching on
x_{1}
and one variable out of each clause
C_{j},j\neq i
.
∎

3.2
Bounds for Intermediate Thresholds

In this section we present our candidate construction for intermediate thresholds
t=\alpha n
where
\alpha
is a constant. We start with the following lemma, stating that for
t=n-k+1
it is possible find a
t
-admissible formula that is satisfied by
all
weight-
t
assignments. In fact,
t=n-k+1
is the smallest threshold with this property and any threshold below
n-k+1
suffers from the difficulties of the Turán problem (see
Section

5
).

Lemma 9
.

S(n,n-k+1,k)=\binom{n}{k-1}
.

Proof.

The upper bound is clear since there are only
\binom{n}{k-1}
assignments with Hamming weight
n-k+1
. To show the lower bound, consider the
k
-CNF formula
F(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
containing a clause
C_{I}=\bigvee_{i\in I}x_{i}
for all size-
k
subsets
I\subseteq[n]
. On the one hand, we claim that
F
is
(n-k+1)
-admissible. Indeed, for any assignment
\alpha
with weight
\operatorname{wt}(\alpha)\leq n-k
we can find a clause
C_{I}
which is falsified by
\alpha
by picking
I\subseteq\{i:\alpha_{i}=0\}
. On the other hand it is easy to see that every assignment with weight
n-k+1
is satisfying for
F
and thus
|\operatorname{sat}_{n-k+1}(F)|=\binom{n}{n-k+1}=\binom{n}{k-1}
.
∎

Theorem 10

(Intermediate Thresholds)
.

Let
t=\alpha n
. Whenever
b=\frac{k-1}{1-\alpha}
is an integer dividing
n
it holds that

S(n,t,k)\geq\binom{b}{\alpha b}^{n/b}.

We remark that an analogous more convoluted statement is true for the cases where
b=\frac{k-1}{1-\alpha}
is not an integer by rounding in the appropriate places.

Proof.

Note that if
b=\frac{k-1}{1-\alpha}
is an integer, then so is
\alpha b=b-k+1
. By
Lemma

7
we have
S(n,t,k)=S(\frac{n}{b}\cdot b,\frac{n}{b}\cdot\alpha b,k)\geq S(b,\alpha b,k)^{n/b}
and by
Lemma

9
,
S(b,\alpha b,k)=S(b,b-k+1,k)=\binom{b}{k-1}=\binom{b}{\alpha b}
. In combination these bounds prove the claim.
∎

Next, we prove that if
Theorem

10
turns out to be optimal, then we obtain improved depth-
3
circuit lower bounds as a consequence.

Proposition 11

(Optimality Implies Circuit Lower Bounds)
.

Let
t=\alpha n
. If the bound in
Theorem

10
is tight (i.e., it also holds that
S(n,t,k)\leq\binom{b}{\alpha b}{}^{n/b}
for
b=\frac{k-1}{1-\alpha}
), then

F(n,t,k)\geq 2^{\frac{n}{k-1}\cdot\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\log(2\pi\alpha(k-1))-o(n)}\geq 2^{\Omega(\frac{n}{k}\log k)}.

Proof.

Assume that the bound in
Theorem

10
is tight. Then, by
Observation

4
and using the approximation in
Lemma

6
we obtain

F(n,t,k)\geq\frac{\binom{n}{t}}{S(n,t,k)}=\frac{\binom{n}{\alpha n}}{\binom{b}{\alpha b}^{n/b}}\geq\frac{\sqrt{2\pi b\alpha(1-\alpha)}^{n/b}\cdot 2^{nH(\alpha)}}{\sqrt{8n\alpha(1-\alpha)}\cdot 2^{nH(\alpha)}}\\
\geq\frac{\sqrt{2\pi\alpha(k-1)}^{n/b}}{2^{o(n)}}=2^{\frac{n}{k-1}\cdot\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\log(2\pi\alpha(k-1))-o(n)}.

∎

4
Tight Bounds for
S(n,t,2)

We here show that for
k=2
and all
t
, our adaptive block construction is optimal.
We do this by starting with an arbitrary optimal formula and transforming it to a monotone formula whilst retaining its optimality.
For monotone formulas we show that the problem is equivalent to maximizing the number of maximal independent sets of a fixed size in a graph, a problem that was recently solved
[
24
]
.
It follows from
Theorem

8
that for
t\leq n/2
our constructions are unique and optimal. So in this section we show optimality for
t>n/2
. Our goal is to prove that

S(n,t,2)\approx\left(\frac{n}{n-t}\right)^{n-t}

as detailed in the next theorem.

Theorem 12

(Tight Bound for 2-CNFs)
.

Write
n=(n-t)q+r
where
q,r
are the unique integers with
0\leq r<n-t
. Then
S(n,t,2)=q^{n-t-r}(q+1)^{r}
.

It is easy to establish the lower bound. To that end, we first observe that
S(n,n-1,2)=n
by setting
k=2
in
Lemma

9
.
We then apply
Lemma

7
. Note that
t=(n-t-r)(q-1)+rq
and
n=(n-t-r)q+r(q+1)
and thus

S(n,t,2)\geq S(q,q-1,2)^{n-t-r}S(q+1,q,2)^{r}\geq q^{n-t-r}(q+1)^{r}.

For the rest of this section we focus on the matching upper bound, starting with a definition. Let
F
be a
2
-CNF formula. The
implication graph
G(F)
is a directed graph with
2n
vertices
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n},\overline{x}_{1},\dots,\overline{x}_{n}\}
. For each clause
X\lor Y
, where
X
and
Y
are literals, the implication graph contains two edges:
\overline{X}\rightarrow Y
and
\overline{Y}\rightarrow X
. We say that
F
is
acyclic
if its implication graph
G(F)
is acyclic.

Lemma 13
.

There exists an acyclic
2
-CNF formula
F
that is
t
-admissible and
|\operatorname{sat}_{t}(F)|=S(n,t,2)
.

Proof.

We start from a
2
-CNF formula
F
that is
t
-admissible and
|\operatorname{sat}_{t}(F)|=S(n,t,2)
. The goal is to to construct another formula
F^{\prime}
with the same properties and the additional guarantee that
G(F^{\prime})
is acyclic.

Suppose that there exists a cycle in
G(F)
, and let
X,Y
be any two literals in that cycle. We can assume without loss of generality that the literal
X=x
is positive (as for any cycle containing only negative literals, there exists a symmetric one containing only positive literals). Let
F_{x\leftarrow Y}
denote the formula obtained from
F
by replacing every occurrence of the variable
x
by
Y
, and set
F^{\prime}=F_{x\leftarrow Y}\land(x\lor\overline{Y})

F^{\prime}
satisfies the claimed guarantees:

•

F^{\prime}
is
t
-admissible: Suppose for the sake of contradiction that there exists a satisfying assignment
\alpha\in\operatorname{sat}(F^{\prime})
with weight less than
t
. Since
F
only accepts assignments with weight at least
t
and
F_{x\leftarrow Y}(\alpha)=1
,
\alpha
must be a weight
(t-1)
assignment which assigns
x=0
. But since
F^{\prime}(\alpha)=1
it also follows that
\alpha
assigns
Y=0
. Hence
F(\alpha)=F_{x\leftarrow Y}(\alpha)=1
, which is a contradiction.

•

|\operatorname{sat}_{t}(F^{\prime})|=S(n,t,2)
: Let
\alpha
be any satisfying assignment of
F
. Since
x,Y
are contained in a cycle in
G(F)
, it must hold that
x
and
Y
are assigned the same truth value under

. Hence
F_{x\leftarrow Y}(\alpha)=F(\alpha)=1
and thus
F^{\prime}(\alpha)=1
.

Notice that
x
is not part of a cycle in

and thus the number of variables contained in cycles has decreased. Therefore, we can repeat this construction until the implication graph becomes acyclic.
∎

Lemma 14
.

There exists an acyclic, monotone
2
-CNF formula

that is
t
-admissible and
|\operatorname{sat}_{t}(F)|=S(n,t,2)
.

Proof.

Using the previous lemma we can assume that

2

1.

3.

The goal is to turn

into a monotone formula

while maintaining the three properties.

Suppose that
F
is not monotone. Then there exists a clause
F=F^{\prime\prime}\land(\overline{x}\lor Y)
(for some variable

Clearly

is a satisfying assignment of

with weight less than

is

, it must hold that

falsifies that clause, i.e.,

and

Let

\beta(Z)=\alpha(Z)

Thus

is obtained from

by flipping the variable

and the literal

\operatorname{wt}(\beta)\leq\operatorname{wt}(\alpha)<t
, which contradicts the assumption that

is

-admissible.

Finally, we argue that repeatedly applying the previous transformation will result in a purely monotone formula. Since

is acyclic, there exists a topological ordering

(that is,
\iota(X)<\iota(Y)

to

we remove an edge
(X,Y)
from

and add a number of edges with strictly larger length. As the maximum edge length is bounded (by

∎

5
Connection to the Turán Problem

In this section we prove that for certain parameters with
k\geq 3
, our problem of bounding
S(n,t,k)
coincides with the famously open
Turán Problem
. We start with a formal definition of the Turán Problem.

A
k
-set system
over some universe
U
is a collection
\mathcal{S}=\{S_{1},\dots,S_{m}\}
of size-
k
subsets of
U
. We refer to
m
as the
size
of
\mathcal{S}
.

Definition 16

(Turán and Covering numbers)
.

Let
k\leq q\leq n
be nonnegative integers.

•

The
Turán number
T(n,q,k)
is the size of the smallest
k
-set system
\mathcal{S}
over the universe
[n]
such that every size-
q
subset of
[n]
contains at least one set
S\in\mathcal{S}
.

•

The
covering number
C(n,q,k)
is the size of the smallest
q
-set system
\mathcal{S}
over the universe
[n]
such that every size-
k
subset of
[n]
is contained in at least one set
S\in\mathcal{S}
.

The task of determining Turán numbers
T(n,q,k)
is called the
Turán Problem
. This problem, and also the problem of determining
the covering numbers
C(n,q,k)
, received considerable attention in the literature
[
21
,
11
]
and both are still far from being resolved. We show that for
t=n-k
, determining
S(n,t,k)
is equivalent to the Turán problem
T(n,k+1,k)
. This shows why proving general optimality for all
t
, when
k\geq 3
, like we did in
Section

4
is hard.

Theorem 17
.

For all
k\leq n
:

T(n,k+1,k)=C(n,n-k,n-k-1)=\tbinom{n}{k}-S(n,n-k,k).

To prove
Theorem

17
, we first establish two facts about optimal
k
-CNF formulas for
S(n,n-k,k)
: We will prove that there exists an optimal
monotone

k
-CNF formula (
Lemma

18
) where all the clauses have width exactly equal to
k
(
Lemma

19
). As a consequence we can view an optimal formula for
S(n,n-k,k)
as a set system, which can be related to
T(n,k+1,k)
and
C(n,n-k,n-k-1)
.

Lemma 18
.

For
n\geq k
, there exists a monotone
(n-k)
-admissible
k
-CNF formula
F

where
|\operatorname{sat}_{n-k}(F)|=S(n,n-k,k)
.

Proof.

Let
F
be an arbitrary
(n-k)
-admissible
k
-CNF formula where
|\operatorname{sat}_{n-k}(F)|=S(n,n-k,k)
. We will show how to transform
F
into formula
F^{\prime}
so that

1.

F^{\prime}
is
(n-k)
-admissible,

2.

\operatorname{sat}_{n-k}(F)\subseteq\operatorname{sat}_{n-k}(F^{\prime})
, and

3.

F^{\prime}
contains strictly fewer non-monotone clauses than
F
.

The lemma follows by repeating this construction until
F^{\prime}
is monotone.

Let
C=(x_{1}\lor\dots\lor x_{p}\lor\overline{y}_{1}\lor\dots\lor\overline{y}_{q})
be a non-monotone clause in
F
, and let
z_{1},\dots,z_{n-p-q}
denote the remaining variables in
F
. Moreover, let
F^{\prime\prime}
denote the formula obtained from
F
after removing
C
. Let

F^{\prime}=F^{\prime\prime}\land\bigwedge_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq[n-p-q]\\
|S|=k-p\end{subarray}}\left(\left(\bigvee_{i\in\text{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$[p]$}}}x_{i}\right)\lor\left(\bigvee_{i\in S}z_{i}\right)\right).

Clearly
F^{\prime}
contains fewer non-monotone clauses than
F
, satisfying
Item

3
.

To show that
Item

1
holds, suppose that there exists an assignment
\alpha
of weight less than
n-k
which satisfies
F^{\prime}
. Since
F(\alpha)=0
,
\alpha
must falsify
C
and thus assigns
x_{i}=0
and
y_{j}=1
for all
i,j
. Since
\operatorname{wt}(\alpha)<n-k
there exist at least
(n-p-q)-(\operatorname{wt}(\alpha)-q)>k-p
variables
z_{i}
which are set to false under
\alpha
, which guarantees that
F^{\prime}
is falsified.

Finally, we show that
Item

2
also holds. Let
\alpha\in\operatorname{sat}_{n-k}(F)
; we show that
\alpha\in\operatorname{sat}_{n-k}(F^{\prime})
. Clearly
\alpha
satisfies all clauses in
F^{\prime\prime}
, so we only have to focus on the new clauses. Observe that
\alpha
also satisfies
C
, hence there are only two cases: Either
\alpha
assigns
x_{i}=1
for some
i
, or
\alpha
assigns
x_{i}=0
for all
i
and
y_{j}=0
for some
j
. In the former case every new clause is directly satisfied. In the latter case,
\alpha
switches off at most
(n-\operatorname{wt}(\alpha))-(p+1)=k-p-1
variables
z_{i}
, and thus all new clauses are satisfied as well.
∎

Lemma 19
.

Let
n\geq k+t
and let
F
be a monotone
t
-admissible
k
-CNF formula such that
|\operatorname{sat}_{t}(F)|=S(n,t,k)
. If no clause in
F
is redundant (i.e., we cannot remove any clause without violating
t
-admissibility of
F
), then all clauses in
F
have width exactly
k
.

Proof.

Suppose that
F
contains a clause of width
k^{\prime}<k
, say
F=F^{\prime}\land(x_{1}\lor\dots\lor x_{k^{\prime}})
. Since no clause in
F
is redundant there exists an assignment
\alpha
with weight
\operatorname{wt}(\alpha)<t
such that
F(\alpha)=0
and
F^{\prime}(\alpha)=1
. We can turn
\alpha
into a satisfying assignment of
F
by switching on one of the variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{k-1}
; all clauses in
F^{\prime}
remain satisfied since we assume that
F
is monotone. It follows that
\operatorname{wt}(\alpha)=t-1
. Let
y_{1},\dots,y_{t-1}
denote the variables turned on by
\alpha
: We are assuming that
n\geq k+t>k^{\prime}+t
and thus there exists another fresh variable
y_{t}\not\in\{x_{1},\dots,x_{k^{\prime}},y_{1},\dots,y_{t-1}\}
. Let
\beta
be the assignment which turns on
y_{t}
and otherwise leaves
\alpha
unchanged. Then
\operatorname{wt}(\beta)=t
and

as the designated clause is not satisfied.

Let
G
be the

-CNF formula obtained from
F^{\prime}
by adding all clauses
(x_{1}\lor\dots\lor x_{k^{\prime}}\lor y_{j})
for

; clearly

. Notice that
\beta
is satisfying for

as
\beta
satisfies all of the added clauses and also

. We will finally prove that
G
is

. Indeed, for any assignment
\gamma
with weight less than

we either have
F^{\prime}(\gamma)=0
(in which case also

) or the only clause in

which is falsified is
(x_{1}\lor\dots\lor x_{k^{\prime}})
. In that case

as it cannot turn on all

.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

17
.

It is elementary to check that
T(n,k+1,k)=C(n,n-k,n-k-1)
. Hence it suffices to prove that
C(n,n-k,n-k-1)=\binom{n}{k}-S(n,n-k,k)
.

We start with the upper bound. Let
F
be a
(n-k)
-admissible
k
-CNF formula such that
|\operatorname{sat}_{n-k}(F)|=S(n,n-k,k)
. By

18
and

F

C_{1},\dots,C_{m}

in the obvious way. Observe that each clause rejects a single assignment of weight

and therefore,
S(n,n-k,k)=\binom{n}{n-k}-m=\binom{n}{k}-m
.

Next, consider the
(n-k)
-set system
\mathcal{S}=\{S_{1},\dots,S_{m}\}
where
S_{i}=[n]\setminus C_{i}
. We claim that every size-
(n-k-1)
subset
T\subseteq[n]
is contained in some set
S_{i}
. Indeed, let
\alpha
be the assignment which turns on all variables in
T
. Since
F
is
(n-k)
-admissible,
\alpha
must be falsifying for
F
and thus there exists a clause
C_{i}
which is disjoint from
T
. It follows that
T\subseteq[n]\setminus C_{i}
. Finally, by the definition of covering numbers,
C(n,n-k,n-k-1)\leq m=\binom{n}{k}-S(n,n-k,k)
.

The proof for the other direction is analogous. Given an optimal set system
\mathcal{S}=\{S_{1},\dots,S_{m}\}
for the covering number
m=C(n,n-k,n-k-1)
, we construct a
k
-CNF formula containing the monotone clauses
C_{1},\dots,C_{m}
with
C_{i}=[n]\setminus S_{i}
. It is easy to see that
F
is
t
-admissible for
t\geq n-k
and thus
S(n,n-k,k)\geq\binom{n}{k}-C(n,n-k,n-k-1)
.
∎

6
Connection to Steiner Systems

In this section we prove that, for certain parameters, the problem of constructing
t
-admissible
k
-CNF formulas which capture
S(n,t,k)
assignments of weight
t
coincides with that of constructing Steiner systems – a special kind of designs.

Recall that a
k
-set system
over a universe
U
is a collection
\mathcal{S}=\{S_{1},\dots,S_{m}\}
of size-
k
subsets of
U
. We refer to
m
as the
size
of
\mathcal{S}
.

Definition 20

(Steiner System)
.

Let
r\leq q\leq n
be nonnegative integers.
A
Steiner system
(n,q,r)
is a
q
-set system
\mathcal{S}
over the universe
[n]
such that every size-
r
subset of
[n]
is contained in exactly one set
S\in\mathcal{S}
.

The existence and explicit construction of Steiner systems has received considerable attention in the design literature
[
5
]
. In fact, only recently the asymptotic existence of such systems was shown when
r
and
q
are constants
[
12
]
.

Steiner set systems are related to optimal
t
-admissible
k
-CNF formulas when
n-k
and
t
are constants.

Theorem 21
.

Let
n>k+t
.
If Steiner systems with parameters
(n,n-k,t-1)
exist, then
S(n,t,k)=\frac{k}{t}\cdot\binom{n}{t-1}
.

Proof. The Lower Bound.

We start proving the lower bound
S(n,t,k)\geq\frac{k}{t}\cdot\binom{n}{t-1}
. Let
\mathcal{S}=\{S_{1},\dots,S_{m}\}
be a Steiner system with parameters
(n,n-k,t-1)
. We construct a monotone formula
F
the over variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
as follows. For each set
S_{i}\in\mathcal{S}
, create a clause

C_{i}=\bigvee_{j\in[n]\setminus S_{i}}x_{j}

and let
F=\bigwedge_{i}C_{i}
. Note that
F
has width
k
as
|S_{i}|=n-k
. Consider an arbitrary assignment
\alpha
such that
\operatorname{wt}(\alpha)=t-1
. Let
A
be the set of variables that are set to
1
by
\alpha
. Since
\mathcal{S}
is a Steiner system, there exists a unique set
S_{i}
which covers
A
. This implies that
C_{i}
is not satisfied by
\alpha
, and thereby that
F
is
t
-admissible.

We now show that
|\operatorname{sat}_{t}(F)|\geq\frac{k}{t}\cdot\binom{n}{t-1}
. Consider the (undirected) bipartite graph
G_{t}
with vertex parts
V
and
W
defined by

•

V=\{\alpha\in\{0,1\}^{n}:\text{$\operatorname{wt}(\alpha)=t$ and $\alpha\in\operatorname{unsat}(F)$}\}
,

•

W=\{\beta\in\{0,1\}^{n}:\operatorname{wt}(\beta)=t-1\}
.

We connect an assignment
\alpha\in V
to an assignment
\beta\in W
if
\alpha
is obtained by flipping one of the zero bits of
\beta
. On the one hand, it is obvious that each vertex in
V
has degree exactly
t
. On the other hand, each vertex
\beta\in W
has degree exactly
n-t-k+1
since there exists a unique clause not satisfied by
\beta
, and thus
\beta
is adjacent to exactly
k
satisfying assignments of weight
t
. Hence, by double counting the edges in
G
we get that
(n-t-k+1)\cdot\binom{n}{t-1}=t\cdot(\binom{n}{t}-|\operatorname{sat}_{t}(F)|)
which proves the theorem.

\proofsubparagraph

*The Upper Bound.
Next, we prove the upper bound
S(n,t,k)\leq\frac{k}{t}\cdot\binom{n}{t-1}
. Let
F
be a
t
-admissible
k
-CNF formula with
|\operatorname{sat}_{t}(F)|=S(n,t,k)
and let
\alpha
be an assignment of weight
t-1
. Then, as
F(\alpha)=0
, there exists a clause
C
which is not satisfied by
\alpha
. Let
x_{1},\dots,x_{m}
denote the variables which are both not present in
C
and turned off by
\alpha
. Then
m\geq n-k-(t-1)=n-(k+t-1)
. Now consider the assignments
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m}
where
\beta_{i}
turns on
x_{i}
and assigns the rest of the variables are assigned as
\alpha
. It is immediate that
\operatorname{wt}(\beta_{i})=t
and
F(\beta_{i})=0
for all
i
.

As this is true for all assignments
\alpha
with weight
t-1
, we conclude that the number of rejected assignments is at least
\frac{n-(k+t-1)}{t}\binom{n}{t-1}
. Here the division by
t
is necessary as we overcounted every assignment exactly
t
times. It follows that

S(n,t,k)\leq\binom{n}{t}-\frac{n-(k+t-1)}{t}\binom{n}{t-1}=\frac{k}{t}\cdot\binom{n}{t-1}.\qed

An interesting observation about the above proof is that any formula with
S(n,t,k)=\frac{k}{t}\cdot\binom{n}{t-1}
would in fact yield constructions of Steiner systems.
Only recently, the existence of Steiner systems
(n,q,r)
for constants
q,r
and sufficiently large
n
satisfying the following divisibility condition:
\binom{q-i}{r-i}\textrm{ divides }\binom{n-i}{r-i}\textrm{ for }0\leq i\leq r-1
have been proven
[
12
]
.
This gives us the following corollary:

Corollary 22
.

Let
n>k+t
where
t
and
n-k
are constants and
n
is large enough and such that
\binom{n-k-i}{t-1-i}
divides
\binom{n-i}{t-1-i}
for
0\leq i\leq t-2
.
Then,
S(n,t,k)=\binom{n}{t-1}\cdot\frac{k}{t}
.

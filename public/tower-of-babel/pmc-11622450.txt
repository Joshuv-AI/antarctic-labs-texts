The effect of foot reflexology on the volume and composition of breast milk in mothers of premature infants hospitalized in the neonatal intensive care unit: a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Fateme Norouzi Nodehi, Parvin Aziznejadroshan, Hossein-Ali Nikbakht, Mohsen Haqshanas, Sohrab Halakhor
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOne of the important problems for mothers after the birth of a premature infant is a decrease in milk production. This study aimed to investigate the effect of foot reflexology on the volume and composition of breast milk in mothers of premature infants hospitalized in the neonatal intensive care unit.MethodsThis randomized clinical trial was conducted on 76 primiparous mothers whose premature infants up to 34 weeks were hospitalized in the neonatal intensive care unit of Ayatollah Rouhani Hospital from February 2023 to November 2023. Mothers in the intervention group received foot reflexology for 20 min on both feet (ten minutes per foot) for seven consecutive days every morning. On the first and seventh days of the study, both groups were compared in terms of milk volume (ml), triglycerides, cholesterol, albumin, total protein, and calcium (mg/dl).ResultsThe mean difference in breast milk characteristics before and after the intervention in the control and intervention groups were as follows: in terms of breast milk volume 12.43 and 23.51 ml, triglyceride 418.37 and 406.21, cholesterol 5.48 and 3.67, albumin 1.02 and 0.35, total protein 1.89 and 4.59, calcium was -3.54 and -1.83 mg/dL; the net difference in breast milk volume in the intervention group compared to the control group increased, which was not statistically significant but was significant in terms of value. No significant difference was observed in other components of breast milk.ConclusionIn this single-center study, foot reflexology massage showed a trend towards increasing the volume of breast milk, total protein and calcium, although it was not statistically significant. Therefore, it needs further investigation.Trial registrationIRCT, IRCT20221220056872N1. Background

According to the definition provided by the world health organization (WHO), infants born before 37 weeks of gestation are considered premature [1]. Each year, 15 million premature infants are born worldwide [2], constituting ten percent of all live births [1, 3]. Premature infants face challenges due to immature sucking and swallowing mechanisms, behavioral irregularities, decreased skeletal muscle tone, and weak endurance compared to full-term infants [4]. Human milk is the ideal food for premature infants hospitalized in the neonatal intensive care units (NICU) [5]. Feeding premature infants with breast milk is crucial for reducing complications, mortality, and supporting optimal growth [6]. Premature infants are at risk of necrotizing enterocolitis, sepsis, and chronic lung disease, which can be reduced by breastfeeding [7–9].

It is estimated that globally, only 41% of children under 6 months of age who were born term are exclusively breastfed, falling short of the WHO's targets of 50% by 2025 and 70% by 2030 [10]. Globally, 44% of infants are exclusively breastfed, ranging from 26% in North America to 57% in South Asia [11]. A study conducted in low and middle-income countries showed that exclusive breastfeeding in children less than 6 months was 41% [12]. 61.9% of premature infant after discharge from the hospital in the first two years of life are fed with breast milk [13]. In addition to this when mothers are separated from their premature infants, they encounter challenges such as insufficient breast milk volume, breast engorgement, delayed onset of lactation, and breastfeeding failure [14].

The NICU is reported to be a highly stressful environment for parents and infants, marked by separation and uncertainty about the infant's mortality [15]. Maternal separation from the infant due to the infant's hospitalization in the NICU poses a barrier to breastfeeding [16]. One of the challenges faced by mothers after the birth of a premature infant is insufficient milk volume [17].

Breast milk production is influenced by two factors, production and secretion. Breast milk production is affected by prolactin hormone, while breast milk secretion is influenced by oxytocin hormone [18]. The composition of human milk depends on factors such as mother's age, nutritional factors, environmental factors, maternal hormones, breastfeeding time, and infant's gender [19]. Changes in the composition of milk during the breastfeeding period are due to the mother's nutrition, the way the mother milks and the frequency of breastfeeding [20]. Mother's nutritional status, partner's support, stress and intrinsic factors such as mother's genetics may also affect the quantitative production and qualitative content of human milk [21]. The composition of breast milk evolves over time to provide immunity against infectious pathogens in addition to the immune system [22].

The ingredients of premature infant's breast milk include: protein, fat, carbohydrate (which is lactose), calcium, phosphorus, magnesium, sodium, potassium, chloride, zinc, copper, vitamin A, vitamin D, vitamin E and vitamin C. Breast milk of premature infants contains more protein, sodium and chloride, fat, energy, vitamins and rare elements and less concentration of phosphorus and calcium and has a lower osmolarity than breast milk of term infants [23].

Lipids are the main source of energy for the infant, which provides 44% of the total energy provided by human milk for the infant [24]. Among the lipids in human milk, cholesterol is the main lipid and plays an important role in the construction of tissues and organs, including the brain, and is a precursor of vitamin D, hormones, and bile acids [25]. Protein is the third most abundant solid substance in human milk and is necessary for the healthy growth of infants, and proteins are divided into three categories: casein, whey, and mucins [26]. Non-protein nitrogen includes urea, creatine, amino acids, nucleotides and peptides, which comprise 25% of the total nitrogen in human milk [27]. Vitamins, minerals and trace elements are necessary for the processes related to the growth and development of the infant, including hormone synthesis, immune system and antioxidant cofactor function [28]. The ratio of whey protein to casein protein in mature milk is 60% to 40%, and this ratio is 20% to 80% in cow's milk [29]. Breast milk of premature infants contains high protein and less carbohydrates [30]. The protein content of human milk in mothers who give birth prematurely decreases during lactation, while the fat and carbohydrate content remains constant over time [31]. The main carbohydrate in human milk is lactose disaccharide, the amount and volume of which increases from colostrum stage to mature milk, the composition of lactose in mature milk remains almost constant and only a small amount of it is not absorbed. This amount of non-absorbable lactose causes a softer stool consistency, reduces the flora of pathogenic bacteria in the stool and provides more absorption of minerals [32].

Among the non-pharmacological methods for increasing breast milk production are physical methods (breast massage, breast compression, breast warming, acupuncture, and acupressure), psychological methods (such as hypnosis, relaxation exercises, yoga, and music therapy), traditional methods (fenugreek tea, ginger, fennel, and sweet beverages), and many complementary therapies [33].

One of the non-pharmacological and non-mechanical methods for facilitating breastfeeding in mothers of premature infants is reflexology, a branch of complementary medicine [34]. Reflexology can be applied in the postpartum period, stimulating the hypothalamus and pituitary gland to increase milk production [35–37]. Reflexology is a systematic practice based on which the points of the hands, feet and ears are compressed and these reflex points are connected with every part of the body including muscles, nerves, glands and bones to show the function of different organs of the body and the cells of the body by stimulating these reflex points, affect the related body organs through reflex impulses [38].

This non-pharmacological method involves applying continuous pressure to specific points on the body, which in turn leads to the release of endorphins, enhances lymphatic flow in the body, and even improves blood circulation. It also has a significant effect on the hypothalamus for hormone production [39].

The study by Mohammadpour et al. (2018) demonstrated that reflexology is effective in increasing the breast milk volume of mothers and that this low-cost and easy-to-use method can be used alongside regular breastfeeding practices [40]. Similarly, the study by Mirzaii et al. (2018) showed that reflexology of the feet has a positive effect on increasing the breast milk production of mothers of premature infants [36]. However, the study by Eshghi-Zadeh et al. (2017) showed that reflexology massage does not effectively increase milk production in mothers with premature infants [41]. Given that few studies have been conducted on the effects of reflexology globally, the present study aimed to determine the effect of foot reflexology on the volume, total protein, cholesterol, triglycerides, albumin, and calcium content of breast milk in mothers of premature infants hospitalized in the NICU.

Methods

Study design and setting

This randomized controlled clinical trial was conducted on 76 primiparous mothers whose preterm infants were born between 28 and 34 weeks, and hospitalized in the NICU of Rouhani Teaching Hospital (Babol, Iran) for ten months from February 2023 to November 2023.

Participants and sampling

Using a block randomization protocol generated by Random Allocation Software, primiparous mothers were randomly assigned to one of two study groups: control (N = 38) and intervention (N = 38) in a 1:1 ratio. To determine the sample size based on the study of Mirzaei et al. [36], Regarding the effect of interventions on breast milk indicators (breast milk volume), taking into account the comparison of the mean and difference of SDs (effect size 0.92) and taking into account the confidence level and power of the study of 95%, using G Power software version3 [42], with the two-way assumption, the number of 32 people in each group is required, which is a total of 64 participants for the two groups in the first stage, and taking into account twenty percent dropout, 76 participants were determined as the final volume (the number of 38 participants in each group).

Randomization and blinding

Mothers were included in the study based on the inclusion criteria, which include the absence of skin problems on the mother's leg such as injury, wound, tumor, or mass, the mother's desire to breastfeed, not using oral galactagogues, not using smoking, anticonvulsant and antipsychotic drugs. Absence of breast problems, intrauterine age of the baby is 28 to 34 weeks, being primiparous, not using effective contraceptive methods during breastfeeding, not entering mothers with therapeutic diets or diets with Vegetables were included in this study and after obtaining written informed consent. To allocate to one of the two study groups, a block randomization method with blocks of four was used. Then, the participants were assigned to one of two groups 1- control 2- intervention according to the block random division protocol (produced by Random Allocation Software) with a ratio of 1:1. The codes were placed in opaque envelopes, and with the entry of each new person, the envelope was opened and the person's belonging to the relevant group was determined.

Blinding was applied only to the group of researchers who provided laboratory assessors and analysts. This study followed the clinical trial flowchart guidelines for reporting randomized controlled trials (Fig. 1).

CONSORT diagram

According to the inclusion criteria, primiparous mothers with premature infant of 28 to 34 weeks hospitalized in the neonatal intensive care unit of Ayat Rohani Hospital in Babol were selected. Interventions were performed for mothers on day 4 after delivery for seven consecutive days. Mothers were excluded from the study due to the cessation of breastfeeding due to the clinical condition of the infant, the death of the infant, the mother's unwillingness to participate in the study, and the mothers suffering from postpartum depression (using the Edinburgh Postpartum Depression Questionnaire).

Data collection and processing

First-time mothers of premature infants were enrolled in the study on the fourth day postpartum based on the entry criteria. In the control group, breast milk samples were collected from the mothers on the first and seventh days of the study. Our study collected samples from the second breastfeeding session, which was three hours apart from the first session, on the first and seventh days (the last day of the study). During the day, the quality of breast milk changes, with a lower fat content in the morning, and for this reason, in previous studies, the time around noon was chosen because the fat content of breast milk is more stable at this time [43]. Therefore, we chose midday (11:30 am to 12:00 noon) for sample collection. Breast milk was expressed from each breast for fifteen minutes by an electric breast pump in the presence of a researcher and measured in milliliters in a graduated container and recorded with mother's name, baby's name, time, milking date and milk volume.

In the intervention group, foot reflexology was performed for 20 min on both feet (ten minutes per foot) for seven consecutive days [36]. These reflexology interventions took place in a room in the NICU, with one session daily from 10:40 to 11:00, conducted by the researcher who taught reflexology massage in five sessions (each session for 30 to 45 min) by the occupational therapist seen. In the first session, an explanation about the anatomy of the muscles and bones of the foot, in the second session, identifying the pituitary point (the middle part of the inner edge (medial) of the first big toe at the plantar level) and performing the relevant technique, in the third session, identifying the solar plexus point (the end of the first bone) foot metatarsus) and performing the relevant technique, the fourth session identifying the mammary point (the back area of the middle finger between the thumb and the ring finger) and performing the relevant technique, and in the fifth session, the reflexology massage of three points was performed by the researcher on the feet of the mother of the premature baby in the presence of the occupational therapist and it was approved.

Foot reflexology includes general reflex (preparation, heating and stimulation) and special reflex (massage of pituitary, breast and solar points). The researcher first removed rings, watches, bracelets, and trimmed their nails, then washed and dried their hands and applied about one cc of neutral lubricant (Vaseline). Then, the preparation, warming up, and stimulating the feet were performed for five minutes on the dominant foot. The warming up and stimulation process involved specific movements such as holding the top and bottom of the foot with both hands, backward bending, bending the sole, inward and outward rotation, movements on the heels, and calf massage.

Then, special reflexology was performed, which involved massaging specific points on the foot. The researcher applied pressure of about half a centimeter at three points on the foot for 5 min. These points were:

Hypothalamus point: located in the middle part of the inner edge of the first phalange of the big toe on the plantar surface.

Hypothalamus point: located in the middle part of the inner edge of the first phalange of the big toe on the plantar surface.

Solar plexus point: located at the end of the first metatarsal bone of the sole.

Solar plexus point: located at the end of the first metatarsal bone of the sole.

Breast points: located in the posterior area of the middle finger between the thumb and the ring finger.

Breast points: located in the posterior area of the middle finger between the thumb and the ring finger.

Then, the same steps were performed on the other foot [36].

Measurement

Afterwards, the primary outcome was the effect of reflexology on the volume of breast milk in mothers of preterm infants. Breast milk was collected on the first and seventh days of the study around noon (11:30 am to 12:00 noon) using an electric breast pump (Mamivac, Germany) and measured in milliliters using a glass measuring cup (made by Medela, Switzerland) for both groups by the first researcher and recorded in the questionnaire.

The secondary outcome was determining the effect of reflexology on fat, total protein, albumin and calcium in breast milk in mothers of premature infants. For this purpose, three milliliters of breast milk from both groups were collected on the first and seventh days of the study and transferred to the Biochemistry Laboratory of Babol University of Medical Sciences to measure the biochemical indicators of breast milk. It was analyzed in terms of fat (cholesterol and triglycerides), albumin, total protein and calcium (using a fixed spectrophotometer device made in England (Unico 9344) with the accuracy of mg/dL) measured by a fixed laboratory expert blindly and centrifuged for all samples (by the Clement 2000 machine available in the laboratory) (Table 1). And the amount of milk fat (cholesterol and triglyceride) and protein (total protein and albumin) as well as calcium were measured and recorded according to the guidelines of the standard kit (Pars Azmun, made in Iran) based on mg/dL.

Infants’ demographic information (gestational age, birth weight, baby's weight on the first day of study) and mother's characteristics (age, weight) were collected from the hospital records.

Statistical analysis

After collecting and ensuring the accuracy of the data entry, descriptive information of patients was reported using the central index of the mean and the dispersion index of the standard deviation or in the form of numbers and percentages. To investigate the relevant statistical tests, the normality of the data was first assessed using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test, and if the data were normally distributed, parametric tests were used.

For examining the desired outcomes in the groups, the independent samples t-test was employed to investigate the relationship between a quantitative variable in two independent states, and the paired-samples t-test was used for two dependent states. Additionally, the Chi-square test was used to explore the relationship between two qualitative variables. ANCOVA was considered to compare the measured indices in breastfeeding mothers after the intervention in two groups while considering the pre-intervention measurements.

Furthermore, the mean differences and 95% confidence intervals were reported as effect sizes in the study. All analyses were conducted using SPSS version 23 software and a significance level of P < 0.05 was considered for all analyses.

Ethical considerations

The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Babol University of Medical Sciences (IR.MUBABOL.REC.1401.132). The trial was registered in IRCT (IRCT20221220056872N1). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants before participating in the study.

Results

Out of 232 mothers, 156 were excluded from the study (27 mothers refrained from participation, 20 mothers discontinued breastfeeding due to neonatal clinical conditions, 17 mothers for postpartum depression, 90 mothers due to infants with GA > 34 weeks, and 2 infant deaths).

76 mothers entered the study, and due to the withdrawal of one participant from each group during the study, ultimately, 74 mothers (37 in the control group, and 37 in case group) were analyzed (Fig. 1).

Discussion

This study aimed to determine the effect of foot reflexology on the volume and composition of breast milk in mothers of preterm infants hospitalized in NICU. The results showed that the mean net difference in maternal milk volume in the reflexology group compared to the control group increased and was close to statistically significant value. The mean differences in maternal milk characteristics indicated that although there was no statistically significant difference in maternal milk factors such as total protein and calcium, they were clinically significant. However, there was no significant difference in other milk compositions such as triglycerides, cholesterol, and albumin.

The birth of a premature infant can be stressful for mothers [3]. Premature birth is a traumatic event for parents, and mothers of premature infants hospitalized in the neonatal intensive care unit experience a higher level of stress [44]. The first two weeks after birth are a critical period for initiation and development of lactation, during which mammary epithelial cells undergo programming processes to regulate long-term milk synthesis [45]. Anxiety and stress of mothers with premature infants can interfere with the secretion of oxytocin and affect the milk ejection reflex and physiologically have a harmful effect on breastfeeding [46]. According to the results of Abdullahi et al. [47], Valiani et al. [48], and Ozdemir et al. [49] studies, it can be said that reflexology reduces the intensity of pain and anxiety by reducing the level of adrenaline and noradrenaline and increasing endorphin and oxytocin. Oxytocin also affects breastfeeding [48].

Therefore, reflexology by applying pressure on the toes after childbirth, by stimulating the pituitary gland and by releasing oxytocin, endorphins and enkephalins, causes comfort after childbirth and breastfeeding. The mother's comfort and relaxation can stimulate the nerve cell in the hypothalamus of the brain and cause the release of prolactin hormone from the anterior pituitary cells. This hormone stimulates alveolar cells to produce milk. Simultaneously with the secretion of prolactin from the anterior pituitary, the stimulation of the posterior pituitary also continues, which releases the hormone oxytocin. Oxytocin flows through nerve fibers to the posterior pituitary and is released into the bloodstream when the nerves are stimulated. Through the bloodstream, this hormone is transferred to the alveoli and affects the myoepithelium cells [50]. The release of oxytocin increases breast milk after delivery [51].

Mohammadpour et al. [40] demonstrated that the mean total milk volume score in the reflexology group increased after the intervention compared to before the intervention [40], which was consistent with the results of our study. The difference between their study and ours was that reflexology was performed for 20 min on both feet for seven consecutive days in our study, while in Mohammadpour's study; reflexology was performed for forty minutes on both feet for six days.

The findings of Sefidhaji et al. [50] showed that the volume and composition of breast milk, including fat, total protein, and albumin, in mothers of premature infants in group C (listening to lullaby with mother's gaze at the baby's picture) were higher than in group B (listening to lullaby by the mother) and in group B was higher than in group A (control) [50]. The results indicate an increase in the mean difference in group C compared to groups A and B, which is consistent with the results of our study, showing that the mean difference in the intervention group is higher than in the control group.

The findings of the study by Sheng et al. [52] indicated that the average milk volume increased over 7 days, with daily massage intervention (breast massage combined with acupuncture point stimulation) in both the control and intervention groups [52]. These results are consistent with our study, with the difference that in our intervention group, reflexology was performed for 20 min on both feet for seven consecutive days, whereas in the Sheng’s study, only breast massage combined with acupuncture point stimulation was performed for 15–20 min on each breast for seven days. In our study, the calcium level in infants aged 28–34 weeks was also examined, but no significant correlation was observed, while milk composition was not investigated in their study.

The results of the study by Mirzaei et al. (2017) showed that in the intervention group (reflexology on breast, pituitary, and solar plexus points), a greater increase was observed in milk volume after massage on the seventh day compared to the control group (reflexology on shoulder, eye, and sciatic nerve points) [36]. This finding is consistent with our intervention results, where milk volume increased on the seventh day after foot reflexology in the intervention group compared to the control group. The findings of Mirzaei et al.'s study showed that indicate that foot reflexology has a positive effect on increasing the volume of breast milk in mothers of preterm infants, and due to its simplicity, high acceptance, and lack of reported side effects, foot reflexology can be used by breastfeeding mothers.

In the study by Eshaghi-Zadeh et al. (2017), it was reported that reflective massage did not affect milk production in mothers with premature infants. It means that the difference in the average volume of breast milk in the reflective massage intervention group on the third day after the intervention compared to before the intervention (first day) was not significant [41]. This finding is not consistent with our study. This difference is likely due to differences in the applied methods between the two studies, including the duration of the intervention and the sample sizes. In the above study, the intervention was performed for three days on 30 mothers, while in our study; the intervention lasted for seven days, on 76 mothers.

Moreover, the findings of the study by Varişoğlu et al. (2020) showed that music had no significant effect on milk production, but cortisol levels and anxiety in the music group decreased significantly compared to the control group [53]. These results indicate inconsistency with our study in term of breastmilk volume. This difference may be due to differences in the methods of intervention, their duration, as well as the sample sizes and conditions of the participants.

So far, there has been no study on the effect of foot reflexology on the volume and composition of milk of mothers of premature infant. Because in most studies, the effect of reflexology has been emphasized on increasing the volume of milk, and increasing the quality of milk (fat, protein and albumin) and calcium has not been considered due to its importance for the growth and development of the infant. Further confirmations are needed through dedicated studies.

Conclusion

This study aimed to determine the effect of foot reflexology on the volume and composition of breast milk in mothers of preterm infants hospitalized in NICU. While not statistically significant, the results showed a trend towards increasing the volume of breast milk, total protein and calcium and thus warrants further investigation.

Study limitations

In the present study, there were also some limitations. We used ANCOVA analysis to adjust baseline values and other study variables. We acknowledge the limitations of conducting a single-center trial. Also, the mental state of the participants during the implementation of the intervention may have influenced the volume of milk. Also, despite the researcher's request to the intervention and control groups not to use other methods of increasing breastmilk, they may have used these methods.

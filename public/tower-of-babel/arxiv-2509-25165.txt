Complexity of Einstein-Maxwell-non-minimal coupling R 2 ​ F 2 R^{2}F^{2} : the role of the penalty factor
Mojtaba Shahbazi , Mehdi Sadeghi Department of Physics, Faculty of Basic Sciences, Ayatollah Boroujerdi University, Boroujerd, Iran

Abstract
In this paper, we examine the holographic complexity of a theory of Einstein-Maxwell with a non-minimal coupling R 2 ​ F μ ​ ν ​ F μ ​ ν R^{2}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} term via complexity=anything. We introduce a perturbative black brane solution in AdS spacetime up to the first order of the non-minimal coupling. Because the model exhibits the resistivity proportional to the temperature, it could describe strange metals. The complexity of the solution could be characterized by three quantities: the conserved charge, the non-minimal coupling and the generalized term in the complexity. The generalized term is chosen to be C 2 C^{2} , the Weyl squared tensor, R 2 ​ F 2 R^{2}F^{2} and F 2 F^{2} . The physical interpretation of the three quantities has been provided where the generalized term is interpreted as a bulk penalty factor, the non-minimal coupling and the conserved charge as an effective scrambling time of the dual theory which contains the choice of the generalization parameter.

AdS/CFT duality, Complexity, Black brane, non-minimal coupling, superconducting circuit, penalty factor

4.4
Superconducting qubits circuit

In this section, we provide two examples in superconducting qubits circuits where by an increase in the penalty factor, CGR would be increased or decreased, which is dependent to the penalty factor. The first example shows a direct relation between the penalty factor and CGR: a quantum circuit that CGR is increased by the increasing the penalizing the circuit. Moreover, the second one provides an inverse relation.

In the superconducting qubit circuits to prepare a qubit one needs controlling quickly the fast gates (or low runtime) to stay away from decoherence. However, fast controlling pose a problem because of the fact that it requires a strong, sharp microwave pulse, but strong pulse has a wide frequency spectrum. A strong pulse allows for very fast

[

Moreover, a wide frequency pulse excite the qubit to higher energy states which leads to the leakage error or crosstalk between energy levels. To reduce the leakage it requires the qubit be more anharmonic that means the energy gap of the states be more significantly different
[
45
]
. The anharmonicity makes the states more isolated from the states with higher energies. A problem back of the other, the anharmonicity makes the qubit more sensitive to charge noise which results in reducing the coherence time. As a way out, one needs increase the circuit depth by applying crosstalk penalty.

Increase in crosstalk penalty,
\gamma_{\alpha}
means that the algorithm weights heavily the parallel operations which leads to the crosstalk and makes it sequentially instead, as a consequence, increase in the circuit depth,
\dot{\mathcal{C}}_{circ.}
. To avoid the possible errors, insertion of correction pulses lets to mitigate the effects of errors which increases the gate count,
\mathcal{C}_{circ.}
. In the following we address a concrete example of the crosstalk penalty.

5
Conclusion

In this study, we presented a black brane solution in four-dimensional AdS spacetime Einstein-Maxwell theory which is characterized by a non-minimal term
R^{2}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}
. As this model lacks a non-perturbative solution, we addressed it using a perturbative approach based on the non-minimal coupling
q_{2}
up to first order. This non-minimal coupling can partially describe strange metals; specifically, our model exhibits a linear relationship between the resistivity and the temperature, though it does not include a phase transition. As a consequence, this model could be used to implement quantum computations. Although, superconducting processors are widely used in quantum computations, there are some transformations that make a connection between the qubits of the superconducting circuits and the fermions of the strange metal circuits such as Bravyi-Kitaev and Jordan-Wigner transformations.

We considered the complexity of this model via the complexity equals anything proposal. In this proposal there is an arbitrariness of the generalization parameter
b
. The computations have been done for three choice parameters of
C^{2}
, the Weyl squared tensor,
R^{2}F^{2}
, the squared of the Ricci scalar and the Maxwell field strength, and
F^{2}
. CGR in this model is characterized by three parameters of the conserved charge
Q
, the non-minimal coupling
q_{2}
and the generalized parameter
b
. We showed that:

•

The generalization parameter acts as a penalty factor in the bulk.

•

The non-minimal coupling is addressed to the effective scrambling time that contains the generalization parameter.

•

The conserved charge is related to Solovay-Kitaev algorithm that states the increase in the value of the conserved charge makes the available simple unitary operators restricted and as consequence the restriction of the processing rate or CGR.

It has been seen that the three generalization parameters have different behavior in change of the non-minimal coupling. CGR in
C^{2}
and
F^{2}
is decreased by the
q_{2}
increase and

Appendix B
Scrambling time

To compute the scrambling time we need to find the butterfly velocity. In the presence of a particle with momentum
P
the energy-momentum tensor due to backreaction would be
[
52
]
:

\displaystyle T_{UU}\sim Pe^{\frac{2\pi t}{\beta}}\delta(U)\delta(x)-2K(x,t)\delta(U)T^{0}_{UV},

(B-7)

T^{0}_{UV}
is the energy-momentum without the shock wave, where the backreacted geometry in the Kruskal coordinates would be:

\displaystyle ds^{2}=2M(UV)dUdV+N(UV)dx^{i}dx^{j}-2M(UV)K(x,t)\delta(U)dU^{2}.

(B-8)

By plugging the above metric in the Einstein equation:

\displaystyle(\nabla_{i}-\lambda^{2})K\sim\frac{N(0)Pe^{\frac{2\pi t}{\beta}}\delta(x)}{M(0)},

(B-9)

where
\lambda^{2}=\frac{d}{2}\frac{N^{\prime}(0)}{M(0)}
is the effective mass. The solution of
K
function would be:

\displaystyle K(x,t)=\frac{e^{\frac{2\pi(t-t_{*})}{\beta}-\lambda x}}{|x|^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}

(B-10)

Then, the scrambling time would be:

\displaystyle v_{B}=\frac{2\pi}{\beta\lambda}

(B-11)

For our solution (
12
) the butterfly velocity would be:

\displaystyle v^{2}_{B}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{Te^{-H(R)}}{R}=\frac{f^{\prime}(R)e^{-2H(R)}}{4\pi R}=\frac{e^{\frac{2q_{2}Q^{2}}{R^{4}}}}{4\pi R}f^{\prime}(R)

(B-12)

\displaystyle\sim 1-Q^{2}(1+q_{2})

(B-13)

Finally, the butterfly velocity is given by (setting the horizon as unity):

\displaystyle v_{B}\sim\sqrt{1-Q^{2}(1+q_{2})}

(B-14)

Then, the scrambling time would be

(B-15)

where the Lyapunov exponent is

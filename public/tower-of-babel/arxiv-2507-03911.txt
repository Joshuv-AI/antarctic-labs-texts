The windowed quadratic phase Fourier transform: structure, convolution theorem and application Journal: XXXX
Sarga Varghese, Manab Kundu*

Abstract
The windowed quadratic phase Fourier transform (WQPFT) combines the localization capabilities of windowed transforms with the phase modulation structure of the quadratic phase Fourier transform (QPFT). This paper investigates fundamental properties of the WQPFT, including linearity, shifting, modulation, conjugation, and symmetry. In addition, we derive the reproducing kernel, establish a reconstruction formula, and characterize the range of the transform. Convolution theorems in both the spectral and spatial domains are developed, along with existence results and norm estimates for the convolution operation associated with the WQPFT. Finally, as an application, the solution of a convolution equation is given using the convolution theorem of the WQPFT.

Introduction

The convolution theory plays a central role in diverse fields of signal and image processing particularly in the design and implementation of multiplicative filters. The classical convolution theorem establishes a fundamental duality between operations in the time and frequency domains. The convolution operation in the time domain correspond directly to multiplication operations in the frequency domain when transformed using the Fourier transform. The mathematical elegance of this relationship has made it significant in various fields of signal and image processing theory, providing computational advantages and theoretical insights that have shaped decades of technological development. The practical implications of convolution extend far beyond theoretical mathematics, enabling efficient algorithm design and implementation strategies. The QPFT is effective for stationary signal analysis but is limited in handling non-stationary signals, as its global kernel cannot capture localized frequency variations. To overcome this, the windowed QPFT introduces a time-localized window function, allowing analysis of time-varying signals. By applying a fixed length window at successive intervals, it isolates portions of the signal that are approximately stationary, making local time-frequency representation possible. This makes it well-suited for a wide range of real world applications, where signals are often inherently time-varying. The information of the instantaneous frequency during a time interval can be obtained with the use of a suitable window, the related windowed QPFT (WQPFT) is ideal to get the frequency contents of a function.

Preliminaries

In this section, we briefly review the fundamental concepts and notations, which will be used throughout the paper. We begin by recalling the classical FT, which serves as the foundation for various generalized transforms considered in this work.

Definition 2.1
.

The FT of a function
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
is defined by

\displaystyle({F}f)(\omega)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-ix\omega}f(x)dx,\hskip 8.53581pt\forall\omega\in\mathbb{R}.

(1)

Reconstruction formula is given by

\displaystyle f(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{ix\omega}({F}f)(\omega)d\omega,\hskip 8.53581pt\forall x\in\mathbb{R}.

(2)

The QPFT generalizes the FT by introducing a quadratic phase factor.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Then for a given set of parameters
\Lambda=\{a,b,c,d,e\}
,
b\neq 0

f
can be defined as
[
13
]

[

\displaystyle(\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}f)(\omega)=(\mathcal{Q}^{a,b,c}_{d,e}f)(\omega)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{K}^{a,b,c}_{d,e}(\omega,x)f(x)dx,\hskip 8.53581pt\omega\in\mathbb{R},

(3)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{a,b,c}_{d,e}(\omega,x)=\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}(\omega,x)=\sqrt{\frac{b}{2\pi i}}~e^{i(ax^{2}+bx\omega+c\omega^{2}+dx+e\omega)}

(4)

is the kernel of QPFT and
a,b,c,d,e\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then the inversion of QPFT is given by

\displaystyle f(x)=\Big(\mathcal{Q}^{-c,-b,-a}_{-e,-d}(\mathcal{Q}^{a,b,c}_{d,e}f)\Big)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{K}^{-c,-b,-a}_{-e,-d}(x,\omega)(\mathcal{Q}^{a,b,c}_{d,e}f)(\omega)d\omega,

(5)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{-c,-b,-a}_{-e,-d}(x,\omega)=\mathcal{K}_{-\Lambda}(\omega,x)=\sqrt{\frac{bi}{2\pi}}~e^{-i(ax^{2}+bx\omega+c\omega^{2}+dx+e\omega)}.

3
Properties of the WQPFT

In this section, we establish several fundamental properties of the WQPFT, including linearity, time and frequency shifting, modulation, conjugation, uniform continuity, and many more. These results helps in understanding the behavior of the transform under elementary operations.

3.1
Basic properties

Let
f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
\phi,\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\}
. Then

1.

Linearity:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[(mf_{1}+nf_{2})(x)](u,\omega)=m\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f_{1}(x)](u,w)+n\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f_{2}(x)](u,\omega),

where
m
and
n
are arbitrary constants.

2.

Conjugate linearity in the window function:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{m\phi+n\psi}^{\Lambda}[f(x)](u,\omega)=\overline{m}\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(x)](u,\omega)+\overline{n}\mathcal{Q}_{\psi}^{\Lambda}[f(x)](u,\omega),

where
m
and
n
are arbitrary constants.

3.

Time shifting:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(x-k)](u,\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x-k)\overline{\phi(x-u)}\sqrt{\frac{b}{2\pi i}}~e^{i(ax^{2}+bx\omega+c\omega^{2}+dx+e\omega)}dx.

Substituting
x-k=y
, the equation becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(x-k)](u,\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y)\overline{\phi((y+k)-u)}\sqrt{\frac{b}{2\pi i}}~e^{i(a(y+k)^{2}+b(y+k)\omega+c\omega^{2}+d(y+k)+e\omega)}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y)\overline{\phi(y-(u-k))}e^{i(a(2yk+k^{2})+bk\omega+dk)}\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}(\omega,y)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\tilde{f}(y)\overline{\phi(y-(u-k))}e^{i(ak^{2}+bk\omega+dk)}\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}(\omega,y)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{i(ak^{2}+bk\omega+dk)}\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[\tilde{f}(y)](u-k,\omega),

where
\tilde{f}(y)=e^{2iayk}f(y)
.

4.

Modulation (frequency shift in the WQPFT domain):

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[e^{i\alpha x}f(x)](u,\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\alpha x}f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}\sqrt{\frac{b}{2\pi i}}~e^{i(ax^{2}+bx\omega+c\omega^{2}+dx+e\omega)}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}\sqrt{\frac{b}{2\pi i}}~e^{i(ax^{2}+bx(\omega+\frac{\alpha}{b})+c\omega^{2}+dx+e\omega)}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{i}{b^{2}}(2bc\alpha\omega+c\alpha^{2}+be\alpha)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}\sqrt{\frac{b}{2\pi i}}~e^{i(ax^{2}+bx(\omega+\frac{\alpha}{b})+c(\omega+\frac{\alpha}{b})^{2}+dx+e(\omega+\frac{\alpha}{b}))}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{i}{b^{2}}(2bc\alpha\omega+c\alpha^{2}+be\alpha}\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(x)]\big(u,\omega+\frac{\alpha}{b}\big).

5.

Conjugation:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\overline{\phi}}^{\Lambda}[\overline{f}(x)](u,\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{f}(x)\phi(x-u)\sqrt{\frac{b}{2\pi i}}~e^{i(ax^{2}+bx\omega+c\omega^{2}+dx+e\omega)}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}\sqrt{\frac{bi}{2\pi}}~e^{-i(ax^{2}+bx\omega+c\omega^{2}+dx+e\omega)}dx}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{-\Lambda}[f(x)](u,\omega)}.

6.

Parity:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{P\phi}^{\Lambda}[Pf(x)](u,\omega)=e^{i2e\omega}\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[e^{i2dx}f(-x)](-u,-\omega),

where
Pf(x)=f(-x)
for every
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

Given any
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{P\phi}^{\Lambda}[Pf(x)](u,\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(-x)\overline{\phi(-(x-u))}\sqrt{\frac{b}{2\pi i}}~e^{i(ax^{2}+bx\omega+c\omega^{2}+dx+e\omega)}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{i2e\omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i2dx}f(-x)\overline{\phi(-x-(-u))}\sqrt{\frac{b}{2\pi i}}~e^{i(a(-x)^{2}+b(-x)(-\omega)+c(-\omega)^{2}+d(-x)+e(-\omega))}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{i2e\omega}\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[e^{i2dx}f(-x)](-u,-\omega).

∎

7.

Switching
f
with
\phi

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(x)](u,\omega)=e^{i((a-\frac{4ca^{2}}{b^{2}})u^{2}+(b-\frac{4ca}{b})u\omega+(d-\frac{2ae}{b})u)}\overline{\mathcal{Q}_{f}^{-\Lambda}[\phi(x)]\Big(-u,\big(\omega+\frac{2au}{b}\big)\Big)}.

Proof.

By carefully manipulating the Definition

, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(x)](u,\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x-u)\overline{f(x)}\sqrt{\frac{b}{2\pi i}}~e^{-i(ax^{2}+bx\omega+c\omega^{2}+dx+e\omega)}dx}.

With the change of variable
y=x-u

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(x)](u,\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(y)\overline{f(y+u)}\sqrt{\frac{b}{2\pi i}}~e^{-i(a(y+u)^{2}+b(y+u)\omega+c\omega^{2}+d(y+u)+e\omega)}dy}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{i(au^{2}+bu\omega+du)}\overline{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(y)\overline{f(y+u)}\sqrt{\frac{b}{2\pi i}}~e^{-i(ay^{2}+2ayu+by\omega+c\omega^{2}+dy+e\omega)}dy}

9.

Uniform continuity:
\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(x)](u,\omega)
is uniformly continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{2}

Proof.

For
u,\omega\in\mathbb{R}
,
h

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt|\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(x)](u+k,\omega+h)-\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(x)](u,\omega)|

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u-k)}\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}(\omega+h,x)dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}(\omega,x)dx\bigg|

\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\big|f(x)\big|\big|\overline{\phi(x-u)}\big|\big|e^{i(bxh+2c\omega h+ch^{2}+eh)}-1\big|\leq 2\big|f(x)\big|\big|\overline{\phi(x-u)}\big|.

Therefore by applying the dominated convergence theorem, the second integral of (
9
) tends to zero, as
h\to 0
. Hence for sufficiently small
h
and
k
,
|\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(x)](u+k,\omega+h)-\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(x)](u,\omega)|
tends to zero.
∎

We next present the reproducing kernel associated with the WQPFT, which allows an explicit representation of the transform, as stated in the result below.

Theorem 3.1
.

(Reproducing kernel)
Suppose that
(u_{0},\omega_{0})
is any point on the 2D-plane of
(u,\omega)
, the necessary and sufficient condition that the function
\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(x)](u,\omega)
is the WQPFT of same function is that
\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(x)](u,\omega)
must satisfy the following reproducing kernel formula:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(x)](u_{0},\omega_{0})=\frac{1}{||\phi||^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(x)](u,\omega)~\mathfrak{K}_{\Lambda}(u_{0},\omega_{0},u,\omega)d\omega du,

where
\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(x)](u_{0},\omega_{0})
is the value of function
\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(x)](u,\omega)
at
(u_{0},\omega_{0})
and
\mathfrak{K}_{\Lambda}(u_{0},\omega_{0},u,\omega)
is called the reproducing kernel that requires to satisfy

\displaystyle\mathfrak{K}_{\Lambda}(u_{0},\omega_{0},u,\omega)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}(\omega,x)}\phi(x-u)\overline{\phi(x-u_{0})}\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}(\omega_{0},x)dx.

Proof.

Taking
(u_{0},\omega_{0})
in place of
(u,\omega)
in the Definition
2.6
of WQPFT, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(x)](u_{0},\omega_{0})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u_{0})}\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}(\omega_{0},x)dx.

Using the reconstruction formula given in (
11
), the above equation becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(x)](u_{0},\omega_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{||\phi||^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(x)](u,\omega)\overline{\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}(\omega,x)}\phi(x-u)d\omega du\overline{\phi(x-u_{0})}\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}(\omega_{0},x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{||\phi||^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(x)](u,\omega)d\omega du\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}(\omega,x)}\phi(x-u)\overline{\phi(x-u_{0})}\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}(\omega_{0},x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{||\phi||^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(x)](u,\omega)~\mathfrak{K}_{\Lambda}(u_{0},\omega_{0},u,\omega)d\omega du.

Hence the result.
∎

3.2
Relationship between WQPFT and WFT

Here we derive the relationship between the WQPFT and WFT.

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(x)](u,\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}\sqrt{\frac{b}{2\pi i}}~e^{i(ax^{2}+bx\omega+c\omega^{2}+dx+e\omega)}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~e^{i(c\omega^{2}+e\omega)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i(bx\omega)}\sqrt{\frac{b}{2\pi i}}~e^{i(ax^{2}+dx)}f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~e^{i(c\omega^{2}+e\omega)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i(bx\omega)}\tilde{f}(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~e^{i(c\omega^{2}+e\omega)}\mathcal{F}_{\phi}[\tilde{f}(t)](u,b\omega),

where
\tilde{f}=\sqrt{\frac{b}{2\pi i}}~e^{i(ax^{2}+dx)}f(x)
and
\mathcal{F}_{\phi}
is the WFT.

The following result provides the inversion formula for the WQPFT, which enables the recovery of the original function from its transform.

Theorem 3.2
.

(Reconstruction formula)
Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
\phi,\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\}
, the reconstruction formula for WQPFT is given by

\displaystyle f(x)=\frac{1}{\langle\phi,\psi\rangle}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(x)](u,\omega)\overline{\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}(\omega,x)}\psi(x-u)d\omega du.

If
\phi=\psi
, then

\displaystyle f(x)=\frac{1}{||\phi||^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(x)](u,\omega)\overline{\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}(\omega,x)}\phi(x-u)d\omega du.

(11)

Proof.

Let
h(x)=\sqrt{\frac{b}{2\pi i}}~e^{i(ax^{2}+dx)}f(x)
. Then
h\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. So by inversion formula (
9
) for WFT, we get for
\phi,\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\}
,

\displaystyle h(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi\langle\phi,\psi\rangle}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{F}_{\phi}[h](u,\omega)e^{-i\omega x}\psi(x-u)d\omega du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi\langle\phi,\psi\rangle}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{F}_{\phi}[h](u,b\omega)e^{-ib\omega x}\psi(x-u)d(b\omega)du.

i.e.,

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{b}{2\pi i}}~e^{i(ax^{2}+dx)}f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi\langle\phi,\psi\rangle}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{F}_{\phi}[h](u,b\omega)e^{-ib\omega x}\psi(x-u)d(b\omega)du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi\langle\phi,\psi\rangle}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i(c\omega^{2}+e\omega)}\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(t)](u,\omega)e^{-ib\omega x}\psi(x-u)d(b\omega)du.

Therefore

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2\pi i}{b}}~e^{-i(ax^{2}+dx)}\frac{b}{2\pi\langle\phi,\psi\rangle}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i(c\omega^{2}+e\omega)}\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(t)](u,\omega)e^{-ib\omega x}\psi(x-u)d\omega du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\langle\phi,\psi\rangle}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(t)](u,\omega)\overline{\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}(\omega,x)}\psi(x-u)d\omega du.

Hence the proof.
∎

We now characterize the range of the WQPFT, identifying the necessary and sufficient conditions for an
L^{2}
- function to be the WQPFT of an admissible function.

Theorem 3.3
.

(Characterization of range of WQPFT)
Let
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\}
be a normalized window function, i.e,
||\phi||_{2}=1
, and let
h\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Then h belongs to the range of the WQPFT, i.e,
h\in\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
if and only if for all
(u^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, h satisfies

\displaystyle h(u^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(u,\omega)\langle\phi_{u,\omega},\phi_{u^{\prime},\omega^{\prime}}\rangle d\omega du,

where
\phi_{u,\omega}=\overline{\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}(\omega,u)}\phi(x-u)
.

Proof.

Suppose
h
belongs to
\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
. This guarantees the existence of a function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}(f)=h
. Therefore by the Definition of WQPFT
2.6
and the reconstruction formula given in (
11
), we get

\displaystyle h(u^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})=\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}(f)(u^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u^{\prime})}\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}(\omega^{\prime},x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\frac{1}{||\phi||^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(t)](u,\omega)\overline{\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}(\omega,x)}\phi(x-u)d\omega du\Big)\overline{\phi(x-u^{\prime})}\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}(\omega^{\prime},x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{||\phi||^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(t)](u,\omega)\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}(\omega,x)}\phi(x-u)\overline{\phi(x-u^{\prime})}\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}(\omega^{\prime},x)dx\Big)d\omega du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{||\phi||^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(t)](u,\omega)\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi_{u,\omega}\overline{\phi_{u^{\prime},\omega^{\prime}}}dx\Big)d\omega du,

where
\phi_{u,\omega}=\overline{\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}(\omega,u)}\phi(x-u)
. We have
||\phi||=1
. Then the above equation becomes

\displaystyle h(u^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(t)](u,\omega)\langle\phi_{u,\omega},\phi_{u^{\prime},\omega^{\prime}}\rangle d\omega du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(u,\omega)\langle\phi_{u,\omega},\phi_{u^{\prime},\omega^{\prime}}\rangle d\omega du.

For the converse, assume
h\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
such that

\displaystyle h(u^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(u,\omega)\langle\phi_{u,\omega},\phi_{u^{\prime},\omega^{\prime}}\rangle d\omega du.

If

\displaystyle f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(u,\omega)\overline{\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}(\omega,x)}\phi(x-u)dud\omega.

Then

\displaystyle||f||^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x\overline{f(x)})dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(u,\omega)\overline{\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}(\omega,x)}\phi(x-u)dud\omega\overline{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(u^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})\overline{\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}(\omega^{\prime},x)}\phi(x-u^{\prime})du^{\prime}d\omega^{\prime}}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(u,\omega)\phi_{u,\omega}\overline{\phi_{u^{\prime},\omega^{\prime}}}\overline{h(u^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})}dud\omega du^{\prime}d\omega^{\prime}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(u,\omega)\langle\phi_{u,\omega},\phi_{u^{\prime},\omega^{\prime}}\rangle\overline{h(u^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})}dud\omega du^{\prime}d\omega^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(u^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})\overline{h(u^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})}du^{\prime}d\omega^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle||h||^{2}.

Thus
f
is also a square integrable function. Now for each
(u^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, by using Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}(f)(u^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\overline{\phi_{u^{\prime},\omega^{\prime}}(x)}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(u,\omega)\overline{\mathcal{K}_{\Lambda}(\omega,x)}\phi(x-u)dud\omega\bigg)\overline{\phi_{u^{\prime},\omega^{\prime}}(x)}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(u,\omega)\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi_{u,\omega}(x)\overline{\phi_{u^{\prime},\omega^{\prime}}(x)}dx\bigg)dud\omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(u,\omega)\langle\phi_{u,\omega}(x),\phi_{u^{\prime},\omega^{\prime}}(x)\rangle dud\omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h(u^{\prime},\omega^{\prime}).

i.e.,
\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}(f)(u^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u^{\prime})}\mathcal{K}^{a,b,c}_{d,e}(\omega^{\prime},x)dx=h
. This completes the proof.
∎

4
Convolutions

In this section, we derive two convolutions- spectral and spatial- for the WQPFT.

4.1
Spectral convolution theorem of the WQPFT

In this subsection, we will obtain the spectral convolution operator associated with WQPFT by the spectral domain representation. The conclusion is based on the fact that the WQPFT of a convolution of two functions is the product of their respective WQPFTs.

Definition 4.1
.

If
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, and
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Then the classical convolution operator of the FT defined by

\displaystyle(f*g)(x)=f(x)*g(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\tau)g(x-\tau)d\tau

(12)

is well defined and belongs to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
. Moreover

\displaystyle||f*g||_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\leq||f||_{p}||g||_{1}.

(13)

Definition 4.2
.

If
f,g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, then

\displaystyle\hat{(f*g)}(\omega)=\sqrt{2\pi}(\hat{f})(\omega)(\hat{g})(\omega),

(14)

where
\hat{f}
is FT of
f
.

The convolution and correlation for the QPFT, which are generalized form of convolution and correlation associated with FT is given below.

4.2
QPFT convolution operation

Theorem 4.3
.

[
13
]

Let
f,g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Assume
\tilde{F}(\omega)=(\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}f)(\omega)
and
\tilde{G}(\omega)=(\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}g)(\omega)
. Then

\displaystyle(\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}(f\otimes_{a}g))(\omega)=e^{-i(c\omega^{2}+e\omega)}\tilde{F}(\omega)\tilde{G}(\omega),

(15)

where

\displaystyle(f\otimes_{a}g)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{b}{i}}e^{-iax^{2}}\Big(\big(f(x)e^{iax^{2}}\big)*\big(g(x)e^{iax^{2}}\big)\Big)

(16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{b}{2\pi i}}e^{-iax^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\tau)e^{ia\tau^{2}}g(x-\tau)e^{i(a(x-\tau)^{2})}d\tau.

Remark 4.4
.

(WQPFT in terms of QPFT convolution) The WQPFT defined in
2.6
can be represented as a convolution-type operation as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(x)](u,\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}\mathcal{K}^{a,b,c}_{d,e}(\omega,x)dx

(17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{b}{2\pi i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)e^{i(bx\omega+c\omega^{2}+e\omega)}\overline{\phi(x-u)}e^{i(ax^{2}+dx)}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{b}{2\pi i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)e^{i(bx\omega+c\omega^{2}+e\omega)}\overline{\phi(-(u-x))}e^{i(a(u-x)^{2})}e^{i\{dx-a(u-x)^{2}\}}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{b}{2\pi i}}e^{-iau^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)e^{i(bx\omega+c\omega^{2}+e\omega)}e^{iax^{2}}\overline{\phi(-(u-x))}e^{i\{dx-ax^{2}+2aux\}}e^{i(a(u-x)^{2})}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{b}{2\pi i}}e^{-iau^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{\omega}(x)e^{iax^{2}}\phi_{u}(u-x)e^{i(a(u-x)^{2})}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{\omega}\otimes_{a}\phi_{u}(u),

where
f_{\omega}(x)=f(x)e^{i(bx\omega+c\omega^{2}+e\omega)}
and
\phi_{u}(u-x)=\overline{\phi(-(u-x))}e^{i\{dx-ax^{2}+2aux\}}
.

Remark 4.5
.

Let
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
and
g\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1,1\leq p,q\leq\infty
, then

\displaystyle||f\otimes_{a}g||_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{\frac{|b|}{2\pi}}||f||_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}||g||_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R})}.

(18)

Therefore by using equation (
17
), we get

\displaystyle||\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(x)](u,\omega)||_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{\frac{|b|}{2\pi}}||f||_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}||\phi||_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R})},

(19)

where
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
and
\phi\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\}
.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\}
be a window function. If
\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(x)](u,\omega)
and
\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[g(x)](u,\omega)
are the WQPFTs of the functions
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, respectively and satisfy that

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f\odot g](u,\omega)=\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f](u,\omega)\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[g](u,\omega).

(20)

Then the spectral convolution operator becomes

\displaystyle(f\odot g)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{b^{2}e^{-i(bx\omega+c\omega^{2}+e\omega-cv^{2}-ev)}}{(2\pi)^{2}i\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}[\phi_{u}(x)](v)}\bigg(e^{-iau^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{\omega}(\tau)e^{ia\tau^{2}}\phi_{u}(u-\tau)e^{i(a(u-\tau)^{2})}d\tau\bigg)

(21)

\displaystyle\times\bigg(e^{-iau^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}g_{\omega}(\eta)e^{ia\eta^{2}}\phi_{u}(u-\eta)e^{i(a(x-\eta)^{2})}d\eta\bigg)dudv.

Proof.

Applying QPFT on equation (
17
) and using the formula (
15
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}[\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f(x)](u,\omega)](v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}[f_{\omega}\otimes_{a}\phi_{u}(u)](v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i(cv^{2}+ev)}\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}[f_{\omega}(x)](v)\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}[\phi_{u}(x)](v).

Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}[\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f\odot g(x)](u,\omega)](v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i(cv^{2}+ev)}\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}[(f\odot g)_{\omega}(x)](v)\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}[\phi_{u}(x)](v)

(22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i(cv^{2}+ev)}\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}[(f\odot g)(x)e^{i(bx\omega+c\omega^{2}+e\omega)}](v)\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}[\phi_{u}(x)](v).

Applying QPFT on both sides of the theorem statement (
20
) and by using the relation (
17
), we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}[\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f\odot g](u,\omega)](v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}[\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[f](u,\omega)\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}[g](u,\omega)](v)

(23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}[(f_{\omega}\otimes_{a}\phi_{u})(u)~(g_{\omega}\otimes_{a}\phi_{u})(u)](v).

Equations (
22
) and (
23
) together gives

\displaystyle e^{-i(cv^{2}+ev)}\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}[(f\odot g)(x)e^{i(bx\omega+c\omega^{2}+e\omega)}](v)\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}[\phi_{u}(x)](v)=\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}[(f_{\omega}\otimes_{a}\phi_{u})(u)~(g_{\omega}\otimes_{a}\phi_{u})(u)](v),

provided
\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}[\phi_{u}(x)](v)\neq 0
. So

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}[(f\odot g)(x)e^{i(bx\omega+c\omega^{2}+e\omega)}](v)=\mathcal{Q}_{-\Lambda}[\phi_{u}(x)](v)~e^{i(cv^{2}+ev)}~\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}[(f_{\omega}\otimes_{a}\phi_{u})(u)~(g_{\omega}\otimes_{a}\phi_{u})(u)](v).

i.e.,

\displaystyle(f\odot g)(x)e^{i(bx\omega+c\omega^{2}+e\omega)}=\mathcal{Q}_{-\Lambda}\big[\mathcal{Q}_{-\Lambda}[\phi_{u}(x)](v)~e^{i(cv^{2}+ev)}~\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}[(f_{\omega}\otimes_{a}\phi_{u})(u)~(g_{\omega}\otimes_{a}\phi_{u})(u)](v)\big](x).

Applying inversion formula

\displaystyle(f\odot g)(x)e^{i(bx\omega+c\omega^{2}+e\omega)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{K}_{-\Lambda}(u,\omega)\mathcal{Q}_{-\Lambda}[\phi_{u}(x)](v)~e^{i(cv^{2}+ev)}~\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}[(f_{\omega}\otimes_{a}\phi_{u})(u)~~~(g_{\omega}\otimes_{a}\phi_{u})(u)](v)dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{K}_{-\Lambda}(u,\omega)\mathcal{Q}_{-\Lambda}[\phi_{u}(x)](v)~e^{i(cv^{2}+ev)}~\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{K}(u,\omega)(f_{\omega}\otimes_{a}\phi_{u})(u)~(g_{\omega}\otimes_{a}\phi_{u})dudv.

By the definition of
\otimes_{a}
in equation (
16
), we get

\displaystyle(f\odot g)(x)e^{i(bx\omega+c\omega^{2}+e\omega)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{K}_{-\Lambda}(u,\omega)\mathcal{Q}_{-\Lambda}[\phi_{u}(x)](v)~e^{i(cv^{2}+ev)}~\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{K}(u,\omega)

\displaystyle~\times\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{b}{2\pi i}}e^{-iau^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{\omega}(\tau)e^{ia\tau^{2}}\phi_{u}(u-\tau)e^{i(a(u-\tau)^{2})}d\tau\bigg)

\displaystyle~\times\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{b}{2\pi i}}e^{-iau^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}g_{\omega}(\eta)e^{ia\eta^{2}}\phi_{u}(u-\eta)e^{i(a(x-\eta)^{2})}d\eta\bigg)dudv.

On further calculations, this expression becomes

\displaystyle(f\odot g)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{b^{2}}{(2\pi)^{2}i}e^{-i(bx\omega+c\omega^{2}+e\omega)}\mathcal{Q}_{-\Lambda}[\phi_{u}(x)](v)~e^{i(cv^{2}+ev)}~\bigg(e^{-iau^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{\omega}(\tau)e^{ia\tau^{2}}\phi_{u}(u-\tau)e^{i(a(u-\tau)^{2})}d\tau\bigg)

\displaystyle\times\bigg(e^{-iau^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}g_{\omega}(\eta)e^{ia\eta^{2}}\phi_{u}(u-\eta)e^{i(a(x-\eta)^{2})}d\eta\bigg)dudv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{b^{2}e^{-i(bx\omega+c\omega^{2}+e\omega-cv^{2}-ev)}}{(2\pi)^{2}i\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}[\phi_{u}(x)](v)}\bigg(e^{-iau^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{\omega}(\tau)e^{ia\tau^{2}}\phi_{u}(u-\tau)e^{i(a(u-\tau)^{2})}d\tau\bigg)

\displaystyle\times\bigg(e^{-iau^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}g_{\omega}(\eta)e^{ia\eta^{2}}\phi_{u}(u-\eta)e^{i(a(x-\eta)^{2})}d\eta\bigg)dudv.

Hence we obtain the result.
∎

4.3
Spatial convolution theorem of the WQPFT

In this section, we will obtain the spatial convolution theorem of the WQPFT by using the convolution operation of QPFT defined in (
16
).

Theorem 4.7

(Spatial convolution theorem)
.

Let
\phi,\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\}
be two window functions. Then, for every functions
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\phi\otimes_{a}\psi}^{\Lambda}(f\otimes_{2a}g)(u,\omega)=\mathcal{E}(u,\omega)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{Q}^{\Lambda}_{\phi}(f)(m_{0},m)\mathcal{Q}^{\Lambda}_{\psi}(g)(m_{1},u-m)e^{i(2aum+\frac{4a^{2}c}{b^{2}}um-\frac{8a^{2}c}{b^{2}}m^{2}-2am^{2})}dm,

where

\mathcal{E}(u,\omega)=\bigg(\overline{\sqrt{\frac{b}{2\pi i}}}\bigg)\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{2\pi i}{b}}\bigg)^{2}e^{i(-\frac{4a^{2}c}{b^{2}}u^{2}+\frac{4ac}{b}\omega u+\frac{2ae}{b}u-c\omega^{2}-e\omega))}
,

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\phi,\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\}
be window functions. Then for any two functions
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}(f)(u,\omega),\mathcal{Q}_{\psi}^{\Lambda}(g)(u,\omega)\rangle=\langle f,g\rangle\langle\phi,\psi\rangle.

If
f=g
and
\phi=\psi
, then

\displaystyle||\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}(f)||^{2}=||f||^{2}||\phi||^{2}.

Proof.

[
29
]

We first assume that the windows
\phi,\psi
are in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. So that
f(x)\overline{\phi(x-u)}
and
g(x)\overline{\psi(x-u)}
are in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
for all
u\in\mathbb{R}
. Therefore by Parseval’s formula for QPFT (
7
) applies to
\omega-
integral and yields

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}(f)(u,\omega),\mathcal{Q}_{\psi}^{\Lambda}(g)(u,\omega)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}(f)(u,\omega)\overline{\mathcal{Q}_{\psi}^{\Lambda}(g)(u,\omega)}dud\omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}(\overline{T_{u}\phi(x)}f(x))(u,\omega)\overline{\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}(\overline{T_{u}\psi(x)}g(x))(u,\omega)}dud\omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}(\overline{T_{u}\phi(x)}f(x))(u,\omega)\overline{\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda}(\overline{T_{u}\psi(x)}g(x))(u,\omega)}d\omega\Big)du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{T_{u}\phi(x)}f(x)\overline{\overline{T_{u}\psi(x)}g(x)}dx\Big)du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{\phi(x-u)}f(x)\overline{g(x)}\psi(x-u)dx\Big)du.

Here
f(x)\overline{g(x)}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
\phi(x-u)\overline{\psi(x-u)}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, therefore Fubini’s theorem allows us to interchange the order of integration. Hence

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}^{\Lambda}(f)(u,\omega),\mathcal{Q}_{\psi}^{\Lambda}(g)(u,\omega)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\overline{g(x)}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{\phi(x-u)}\psi(x-u)du\Big)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle\overline{\langle\phi,\psi\rangle}.

∎

.

Lemma 6.1
.

[
48
]

[

Let

1.

By the Riemann- Lebesgue lemma for WQPFT

, the function

7
Conclusion

In this article, we have developed a theoretical foundation for the WQPFT, a time-frequency representation that effectively combines windowing with quadratic phase modulation. We investigated several fundamental properties and structural results of the WQPFT, including the reproducing kernel, a reconstruction formula, and a characterization of the range of the transform. Convolution theorems were established in both the spectral and spatial domains, and we proved the existence of solutions and derived norm estimates for the corresponding convolution operations. Moreover, we demonstrated how the convolution theorem naturally provides a solution to certain convolution-type integral equations within the framework of WQPFT. Building on these foundations, there are many possible directions for future research in WQPFT. Future work may focus on developing numerical algorithms for efficient computation of the WQPFT and applying it to practical problems such as filtering, denoising, and feature extraction. Extensions to higher dimensions, quaternion domains, and discrete settings, as well as the study of uncertainty principles and sampling theorems, also present promising research directions.

Instability bands for periodic traveling waves in the modified Korteweg-de Vries equation
Shikun Cui, Dmitry E. Pelinovsky

Abstract
. Two families of periodic traveling waves exist in the focusing mKdV (modified Korteweg-de Vries) equation. Spectral stability of these waveforms with respect to co-periodic perturbations of the same period has been previously explored by using spectral analysis and variational formulation. By using tools of integrability such as a relation between squared eigenfunctions of the Lax pair and eigenfunctions of the linearized stability problem, we revisit the spectral stability of these waveforms with respect to perturbations of arbitrary periods. In agreement with previous works, we find that one family is spectrally stable for all parameter configurations, whereas the other family is spectrally unstable for all parameter configurations. We show that the onset of the co-periodic instability for the latter family changes the instability bands from figure- 8 8 (crossing at the imaginary axis) into figure- ∞ \infty (crossing at the real axis).



Keywords:
modified Korteweg-de Vries equation, periodic traveling wave, modulational instability

Instabilities of steadily propagating waves on a fluid surface, called Stokes waves, have been recently explored in many computational details due to advanced numerical algorithms with high precision and accuracy

2.
Waveforms for the periodic traveling waves

Integrating the second-order equation (
1.3
), we obtain the first-order invariant

(U^{\prime})^{2}+Q(U)=d,

(2.1)

where
Q(U):=U^{4}-cU^{2}-2bU
and
d
is a constant. Two particular waveforms generalizing the dnoidal and cnoidal waves (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) are given in terms of the four roots
\{u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}.u_{4}\}
of
Q(u)=d
. The four roots satisfy the relations

\begin{cases}u_{1}+u_{2}+u_{3}+u_{4}=0,\\
u_{1}u_{2}+u_{1}u_{3}+u_{1}u_{4}+u_{2}u_{3}+u_{2}u_{4}+u_{3}u_{4}=-c,\\
u_{1}u_{2}u_{3}+u_{1}u_{2}u_{4}+u_{1}u_{3}u_{4}+u_{2}u_{3}u_{4}=2b,\\
u_{1}u_{2}u_{3}u_{4}=-d,\end{cases}

(2.2)

which follow by expanding

\displaystyle Q(u)-d=(u-u_{1})(u-u_{2})(u-u_{3})(u-u_{4})=u^{4}-cu^{2}-2bu-d.

The following proposition states the existence of two waveforms for the periodic traveling waves. The statement is based on the explicit computations verified in
[
12
]
.

Proposition 1
.

If the roots
\{u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}.u_{4}\}
are real and ordered as
u_{4}\leq u_{3}\leq u_{2}\leq u_{1}
, then the first-order invariant (
2.1
) is satisfied by

U(x)=u_{4}+\frac{(u_{1}-u_{4})(u_{2}-u_{4})}{(u_{2}-u_{4})+(u_{1}-u_{2}){\rm sn}^{2}(\nu x,k)},

(2.3)

where

\nu=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(u_{1}-u_{3})(u_{2}-u_{4})},\quad k=\frac{\sqrt{(u_{1}-u_{2})(u_{3}-u_{4})}}{\sqrt{(u_{1}-u_{3})(u_{2}-u_{4})}}.

If
\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
are real and
\{u_{3}.u_{4}\}
are complex-conjugate
such that
u_{2}\leq u_{1}
and
u_{3}=\bar{u}_{4}=\gamma+i\eta
with
\eta>0
,
then the first-order invariant (
2.1
) is satisfied by

{U}(x)=u_{2}+\frac{({u}_{1}-{u}_{2})\big(1-{\rm cn}(\mu x,k)\big)}{(\delta+1)+(\delta-1){\rm cn}(\mu x,k)},

(2.4)

where

\displaystyle\delta=\frac{\sqrt{({u}_{1}-\gamma)^{2}+\eta^{2}}}{\sqrt{({u}_{2}-\gamma)^{2}+\eta^{2}}},\quad\mu=\sqrt[4]{[({u}_{1}-\gamma)^{2}+\eta^{2}][({u}_{2}-\gamma)^{2}+\eta^{2}]},

and

\displaystyle 2k^{2}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{({u}_{1}-\gamma)({u}_{2}-\gamma)+\eta^{2}}{\sqrt{[({u}_{1}-\gamma)^{2}+\eta^{2}][({u}_{2}-\gamma)^{2}+\eta^{2}]}}.

Remark 1
.

For periodic solution (
2.3
), exchanging

and
u_{2}\leftrightarrow u_{4}
yields another periodic solution:

U(x)=u_{2}+\frac{(u_{2}-u_{3})(u_{2}-u_{4})}{(u_{4}-u_{2})+(u_{3}-u_{4}){\rm sn}^{2}(\nu x;k)},

(2.5)

with the same definition of

and

.

Remark 2
.

The periodic solutions

in the form of (

) and (

[u_{2},u_{1}]
and
[u_{4},u_{3}]

, where

is the complete elliptic integral of the first type. The periodic solution
{U}(x)
in the form of (

3.
Lax spectrum for the periodic traveling waves

The mKdV equation (
1.1
) is a compatibility condition of the following system of the linear equations
[
1
]
for
\psi\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\psi_{x}=L(u,\lambda)\psi,\\
\psi_{t}=M(u,\lambda)\psi,\end{array}\right.

(3.1)

where

L(u,\lambda)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda&u\\
-u&-\lambda\\
\end{array}\right)

and

M(u,\lambda)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-4\lambda^{3}-2\lambda u^{2}&-4\lambda^{2}u-2\lambda u_{x}-2u^{3}-u_{xx}\\
4\lambda^{2}u-2\lambda u_{x}+2u^{3}+u_{xx}&4\lambda^{3}+2\lambda u^{2}\\
\end{array}\right).

Solutions of the linear equations (
3.1
) for the traveling waves of the mKdV equation (
1.1
) are related to solutions of the linearized mKdV equation at the traveling waves. These relations are well-known, see, e.g.,
[
23
]
, and are reproduced here for the sake of transparency.

Let
u(x,t)=U(x-ct)
be the traveling wave solution of the mKdV equation (
1.1
). Then the linear system (
3.1
) enjoys the separation of variables in the form
\psi(x,t)=\Psi(x-ct)e^{\Omega t}
, with
\Psi\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
and
\Omega\in\mathbb{C}
found from the linear system

\begin{cases}\Psi^{\prime}=L(U,\lambda)\Psi,\\
\Omega\Psi=\left[M(U,\lambda)+cL(U,\lambda)\right]\Psi.\end{cases}

(3.2)

The following proposition gives the admissible values of
\Omega
.

Proposition 2
.

The admissible values of
\Omega
are defined by the characteristic polynomial

P(\lambda)=16\lambda^{6}-8c\lambda^{4}+(c^{2}+4d)\lambda^{2}-b^{2}

(3.3)

as
\Omega=\pm\sqrt{P(\lambda)}
.

Proof.

The second equation in system (
3.2
) shows that
\Omega
is an eigenvalue of the following matrix

A:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-4\lambda^{3}-2\lambda U^{2}+c\lambda&-(4\lambda^{2}U+2U^{3}+U^{\prime\prime}-cU+2\lambda U^{\prime})\\
4\lambda^{2}U+2U^{3}+U^{\prime\prime}-cU-2\lambda U^{\prime}&4\lambda^{3}+2\lambda U^{2}-c\lambda\\
\end{array}\right).

Since the trace of
A
is zero,
\Omega^{2}=P(\lambda):=\det(A)
, which is expanded in powers of
\lambda
as follows:

\displaystyle P(\lambda)=16\lambda^{6}-8c\lambda^{4}-(12U^{4}+8UU^{\prime\prime}-4cU^{2}-4(U^{\prime})^{2}-c^{2})\lambda^{2}-(2U^{3}+U^{\prime\prime}-cU)^{2}.

By using (
1.3
) and (
2.1
), we rewrite
P(\lambda)
in the form (
3.3
). ∎

We define
the Lax spectrum
of the periodic travelling waves as the set of admissible values of
\lambda
in the spectral problem
\Psi^{\prime}=L(U,\lambda)\Psi
of the system (
3.2
), for which
\Psi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{2})
. By Floquet theorem, if
U(x+L)=U(x)
is
L
-periodic, then the bounded eigenfunction
\Psi
is quasi-periodic
as
\Psi(x+L)=\Psi(x)e^{i\mu L}
with
\mu\in\left[-\frac{\pi}{L},\frac{\pi}{L}\right]
for the values of
\lambda
defined in the continuous bands of the Lax spectrum.

To relate the Lax spectrum with the stability spectrum, we define the linearization of the mKdV equation (
1.1
) at the periodic traveling waves with the profile
U
. By using
u(x,t)=U(x-ct)+\mathfrak{u}(x-ct)e^{\Lambda t}
and linearizing at
\mathfrak{u}
, we obtain the spectral stability problem in the form

\Lambda\mathfrak{u}+\mathfrak{u}^{\prime\prime\prime}+6(U^{2}\mathfrak{u})^{\prime}-c\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}=0.

(3.4)

The stability spectrum
of the periodic traveling waves is defined as the set of admissible values of
\Lambda
in the spectral stability problem (
3.4
), for which
\mathfrak{u}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C})
. By the same Floquet theorem, if
U(x+L)=U(x)
is
L
-periodic, then the bounded eigenfunction
\mathfrak{u}
is quasi-periodic as
\mathfrak{u}(x+L)=\mathfrak{u}(x)e^{i\theta L}
with
\theta\in\left[-\frac{\pi}{L},\frac{\pi}{L}\right]
for the values of
\Lambda
defined in the continuous bands of the stability spectrum. The bands of
\Lambda
in the stability spectrum are related to the bands of
\lambda
in the Lax spectrum due to the squared eigenfunction relation
[
23
]
.

The following proposition gives the relation between eigenfunctions of the spectral stability problem (
3.4
) and the squared eigenfunctions of the linear system (
3.2
).

Proposition 3
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{C}
be the Lax spectrum and

\Psi=(p,q)^{\mathrm{T}}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{2})
be the eigenfunction of the linear system (
3.2
) for an admissible value of
\lambda\in\Sigma
. Then,

\mathfrak{u}:=p^{2}-q^{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C})
is the eigenfunction of the spectral stability problem (
3.4
) with

\Lambda=2\Omega=\pm 2\sqrt{P(\lambda)}.

(3.5)

Proof.

Given that
\Psi=(p,q)^{\mathrm{T}}
, we rewrite
\Psi^{\prime}=L(U,\lambda)\Psi
into

\begin{cases}p^{\prime}=\lambda p+Uq,\\
q^{\prime}=-Up-\lambda q,\end{cases}

from which we obtain

\begin{cases}p^{\prime\prime}&=\lambda p^{\prime}+U^{\prime}q+Uq^{\prime}\\
&=\lambda^{2}p+U^{\prime}q-U^{2}p,\\
q^{\prime\prime}&=-U^{\prime}P-Up^{\prime}-\lambda q^{\prime}\\
&=\lambda^{2}q-U^{2}q-U^{\prime}p\end{cases}

and

\begin{cases}p^{\prime\prime\prime}&=\lambda^{2}p^{\prime}+U^{\prime\prime}q+U^{\prime}q^{\prime}-2UU^{\prime}p-U^{2}p^{\prime}\\
&=\lambda^{3}p+\lambda^{2}Uq+U^{\prime\prime}q-3UU^{\prime}p-\lambda U^{\prime}q-\lambda U^{2}p-U^{3}q,\\
q^{\prime\prime\prime}&=\lambda^{2}q^{\prime}-U^{\prime\prime}p-U^{\prime}p^{\prime}-2UU^{\prime}q-U^{2}q^{\prime}\\
&=-\lambda^{3}q-\lambda^{2}Up-U^{\prime\prime}p-3UU^{\prime}q-\lambda U^{\prime}p+\lambda U^{2}q+U^{3}p.\end{cases}

This yields with explicit computations:

\displaystyle\quad(p^{2})^{\prime\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=2pp^{\prime\prime\prime}+6p^{\prime}p^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=8\lambda^{3}p^{2}+8\lambda^{2}Upq+4\lambda U^{\prime}pq-8\lambda U^{2}p^{2}-8U^{3}pq+6UU^{\prime}q^{2}-6UU^{\prime}p^{2}+2U^{\prime\prime}pq

and

\displaystyle\quad(q^{2})^{\prime\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=2pp^{\prime\prime\prime}+6p^{\prime}p^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=-8\lambda^{3}q^{2}-8\lambda^{2}Upq+4\lambda U^{\prime}pq+8\lambda U^{2}q^{2}+8U^{3}pq-6UU^{\prime}q^{2}+6UU^{\prime}p^{2}-2U^{\prime\prime}pq.

Combining with the second equation of (
3.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle(p^{2})^{\prime\prime\prime}+12UU^{\prime}p^{2}+6U^{2}(p^{2})^{\prime}-c(p^{2})^{\prime}

\displaystyle=8\lambda^{3}p^{2}+8\lambda^{2}Upq+4\lambda U^{\prime}pq+4\lambda U^{2}p^{2}+4U^{3}pq+2U^{\prime\prime}pq+6UU^{\prime}q^{2}+6UU^{\prime}p^{2}

\displaystyle=-2\Omega p^{2}+6UU^{\prime}(p^{2}+q^{2})

and

\displaystyle(q^{2})^{\prime\prime\prime}+12UU^{\prime}q^{2}+6U^{2}(q^{2})^{\prime}-c(q^{2})^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-8\lambda^{3}q^{2}-8\lambda^{2}Upq+4\lambda U^{\prime}pq-4\lambda U^{2}q^{2}-4U^{3}pq-2U^{\prime\prime}pq+6UU^{\prime}q^{2}+6UU^{\prime}p^{2}

\displaystyle=-2\Omega q^{2}+6UU^{\prime}(p^{2}+q^{2}),

which verify that

\mathfrak{u}^{\prime\prime\prime}+12UU^{\prime}\mathfrak{u}+6U^{2}\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}-c\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}=-2\Omega\mathfrak{u},

for
\mathfrak{u}:=p^{2}-q^{2}
. This equation is equivalent to (
3.4
) with
\Lambda=2\Omega
, whereas the relation
\Omega=\pm\sqrt{P(\lambda)}
is established in Proposition
2
.
∎

4.
Lax and stability spectra: analytical results

Although the exact location of the Lax spectrum for the periodic traveling waves is not known, the symmetry of the Lax spectrum with respect to reflection about
\lambda=0
and about the real axis follow the symmetry of
L(u,\lambda)
in the linear system (
3.1
). The following proposition states these properties of the Lax spectrum.

Proposition 4
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
be an eigenvalue of the spectral problem
\psi_{x}=L(u,\lambda)\psi
with the eigenfunction
\psi=(p,q)^{T}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{2})
. Then,
-\lambda
is also an eigenvalue with the eigenfunction
\psi=(q,-p)^{T}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{2})
. Moreover, if
\lambda\notin\mathbb{R}
, then
\bar{\lambda}
is also an eigenvalue with the eigenfunction
\psi=(\bar{p},\bar{q})^{T}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{2})
.

Proof.

We rewrite
\psi_{x}=L(u,\lambda)\psi
in the form:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}p_{x}=\lambda p+uq,\\
q_{x}=-up-\lambda q.\end{array}\right.

This implies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}q_{x}=(-\lambda)q+u(-p),\\
-p_{x}=-\lambda p-uq,\end{array}\right.

so that
\psi:=(q,-p)^{T}
is also a solution of
\psi_{x}=L(u,\lambda)\psi
with eigenvalue
-\lambda
.
Furthermore, taking the complex-conjugate transform yields

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\bar{p}_{x}=\bar{\lambda}\bar{p}+\bar{u}\bar{q},\\
\bar{q}_{x}=-\bar{u}\bar{p}-\bar{\lambda}\bar{q}.\end{array}\right.

Since
u=\bar{u}
and
\lambda\neq\bar{\lambda}
, then

\psi:=(\bar{p},\bar{q})^{T}
is also a solution of
\psi_{x}=L(u,\lambda)\psi
with eigenvalue
\bar{\lambda}
. ∎

Roots of the characteristic polynomial
P(\lambda)
in (
3.3
) can be enumerated as
\{\pm\lambda_{1},\pm\lambda_{2},\pm\lambda_{3}\}
. It was found in
[
31
,
32
,
33
,
34
]
that roots of
P(\lambda)
are related to roots
\{u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}.u_{4}\}
of
Q(u)=d
in (
2.1
). These relations were verified for the mKdV equation in
[
12
]
with a direct proof, hence we state the relations without further details:

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(u_{1}+u_{2}),\qquad~\lambda_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(u_{1}+u_{3}),\qquad~\lambda_{3}=\frac{1}{2}(u_{2}+u_{3}).

(4.1)

It was shown in
[
12
]
that spectral bands of the Lax spectrum for the periodic traveling waves outside
i\mathbb{R}
are connected between the roots

\{\pm\lambda_{1},\pm\lambda_{2},\pm\lambda_{3}\}
. In the next two theorems, we derive the stability criterion for the waveform (
2.3
)
and the instability criterion for the waveform (
2.4
) in the spectral stability problem (
3.4
). It relies on the location of the spectral bands between the roots of
P(\lambda)
in addition to the spectral bands on
i\mathbb{R}
, which is assumed here and verified numerically in Section
5
, as well as the squared eigenfunction relation of Proposition
3.5
.

Theorem 1
.

For the waveform (
2.3
) of Proposition
1
, assume that the Lax spectrum is located on

i\mathbb{R}\cup[-\lambda_{1},-\lambda_{2}]\cup[-|\lambda_{3}|,|\lambda_{3}|]\cup[\lambda_{2},\lambda_{1}].

(4.2)

Then the stability spectrum is
i\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

For the waveform (
2.3
) of Proposition
1
, the roots
\{u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}.u_{4}\}
of
Q(u)=d
are real and ordered as
u_{4}\leq u_{3}\leq u_{2}\leq u_{1}
. By (
4.1
), the roots
\{\pm\lambda_{1},\pm\lambda_{2},\pm\lambda_{3}\}
of
P(\lambda)
are real and ordered as

\lambda_{3}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\lambda_{1}
. Moreover, due to the first relation in (
2.2
), we have
u_{2}+u_{3}=-u_{1}-u_{4}
. If
u_{2}+u_{3}<0
, then
|u_{2}+u_{3}|=u_{1}+u_{4}\leq u_{1}+u_{3}
, which proves that
|\lambda_{3}|\leq\lambda_{2}
even if
\lambda_{3}<0
. Hence, we have
0\leq|\lambda_{3}|\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\lambda_{1}
.

For
\lambda\in i\mathbb{R}
in the Lax spectrum (
4.2
), we rewrite the polynomial
P(\lambda)
in the form:

\displaystyle P(\lambda)

\displaystyle=16(\lambda^{2}-\lambda_{1}^{2})(\lambda^{2}-{\lambda_{2}^{2}})({\lambda^{2}}-{\lambda_{3}^{2}})

\displaystyle=-16(|\lambda|^{2}+\lambda_{1}^{2})(|\lambda|^{2}+{\lambda_{2}^{2}})(|\lambda|^{2}+{\lambda_{3}^{2}}).

Since
P(\lambda)<0
, we have

\Lambda=\pm 2\sqrt{P(\lambda)}\in i\mathbb{R}
by (
3.5
).
Moreover,
\lim\limits_{|\lambda|\to\infty}P(\lambda)=-\infty
and

\lim\limits_{\lambda\to 0}P(\lambda)=-16\lambda_{1}^{2}\lambda_{2}^{2}\lambda_{3}^{2}
so that

(-\infty,-8\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}|\lambda_{3}|]\cup[8\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}|\lambda_{3}|,\infty)i

belongs to the stability spectrum.

For
\lambda\in[-\lambda_{1},-\lambda_{2}]\cup[-|\lambda_{3}|,|\lambda_{3}|]\cup[\lambda_{2},\lambda_{1}]
in the Lax spectrum (
4.2
), we have

P(\lambda)\leq 0
so that
\Lambda=\pm 2\sqrt{P(\lambda)}\in i\mathbb{R}
by (
3.5
). Moreover,

\lim\limits_{|\lambda|\to|\lambda_{3}|}P(\lambda)=0
so that

[-8\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}|\lambda_{3}|,8\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}|\lambda_{3}|]i

also belongs to the stability spectrum. Hence, the image of
\Lambda=\pm 2\sqrt{P(\lambda)}
covers all
i\mathbb{R}
. ∎

Remark 3
.

The parts of the Lax spectrum (
4.2
) for
\lambda\in[-\lambda_{1},-\lambda_{2}]\cup[\lambda_{2},\lambda_{1}]
cover a subset of
i\mathbb{R}
for the second time. Regardless, the periodic traveling wave with the waveform (
2.3
) is classified as spectrally stable.

Theorem 2
.

For the waveform (
2.4
) of Proposition
1
, assume that the Lax spectrum is located on

i\mathbb{R}\cup[-|\lambda_{1}|,|\lambda_{1}|]\cup\Sigma_{+}\cup\Sigma_{-},

(4.3)

where
\Sigma_{+}
is the spectral band connecting two complex roots in the set
\{\pm\lambda_{2},\pm\lambda_{3}\}
and
\Sigma_{-}
is the reflection of
\Sigma_{+}
about
\lambda=0
. Then the stability spectrum is the union of
i\mathbb{R}
and the spectral bands which are not contained in
i\mathbb{R}
and are symmetric about
\Lambda=0
.

Proof.

For the waveform (
2.4
) of Proposition
1
, the roots
\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
of
Q(u)=d
are real and ordered as
u_{2}\leq u_{1}
, whereas the roots
\{u_{3},u_{4}\}
are complex conjugate with
u_{3}=\bar{u}_{4}=\gamma+i\eta
and
\eta>0
. By (
4.1
), the roots
\{\pm\lambda_{1}\}
of
P(\lambda)
are real, whereas the roots

\{\pm\lambda_{2},\pm\lambda_{3}\}
represent a complex-conjugate quadruplet since

\lambda_{3}=\frac{1}{2}(u_{2}+u_{3})=-\frac{1}{2}(u_{1}+u_{4})=-\frac{1}{2}(u_{1}+\bar{u}_{3})=-\bar{\lambda}_{2},

due to the first relation in (
2.2
). Since
\lambda_{2}=-\bar{\lambda}_{3}
, we can rewrite
P(\lambda)
in the form:

P(\lambda)=16(\lambda^{2}-\lambda_{1}^{2})[(\lambda^{2}-{\rm Re}(\lambda_{2}^{2}))^{2}+({\rm Im}(\lambda_{2}^{2}))^{2}].

For
\lambda\in i\mathbb{R}
in the Lax spectrum (
4.3
),
we have
P(\lambda)<0
, and since
\lim\limits_{|\lambda|\to\infty}P(\lambda)=-\infty
and

\lim\limits_{\lambda\to 0}P(\lambda)=-16\lambda_{1}^{2}\lambda_{2}^{2}\lambda_{3}^{2}
, we have by
\Lambda=\pm 2\sqrt{P(\lambda)}
that

(-\infty,-8|\lambda_{1}||\lambda_{2}|^{2}]\cup[8|\lambda_{1}||\lambda_{2}|^{2},\infty)i

belongs to the stability spectrum.

For
\lambda\in[-|\lambda_{1}|,|\lambda_{1}|]
in the Lax spectrum (
4.3
), we have
P(\lambda)\leq 0
and since

\lim\limits_{|\lambda|\to|\lambda_{1}|}P(\lambda)=0
,
we have by
\Lambda=\pm 2\sqrt{P(\lambda)}
that

[-8|\lambda_{1}||\lambda_{2}|^{2},8|\lambda_{1}||\lambda_{2}|^{2}]i

also belongs to the stability spectrum. Hence, the image of
\Lambda=\pm 2\sqrt{P(\lambda)}
covers all
i\mathbb{R}
.

It remains to prove that the image of
\Lambda=\pm 2\sqrt{P(\lambda)}
for
\lambda\in\Sigma_{+}
in the Lax spectrum (
4.3
) is not contained in
i\mathbb{R}
. Since
\Sigma_{-}
is a reflection of
\Sigma_{+}
about
\lambda=0
and
\Lambda=0
if
\lambda=\pm\lambda_{2}
or
\lambda=\pm\lambda_{3}
, the image of
\Lambda=\pm 2\sqrt{P(\lambda)}
for
\lambda\in\Sigma_{-}
is a reflection of the image of
\Lambda=\pm 2\sqrt{P(\lambda)}
for
\lambda\in\Sigma_{+}
about
\Lambda=0
, so that the spectral bands are not contained in
i\mathbb{R}
and are symmetric about
\Lambda=0
.

Let
\lambda\in\Sigma_{+}
. Due to symmetries between
\{\pm\lambda_{2},\pm\bar{\lambda}_{2}\}
, we may have only four possibilities, for each we prove that there exists
\lambda\in\Sigma_{+}
such that
\Lambda=\pm 2\sqrt{P(\lambda)}\notin i\mathbb{R}
.

•

\Sigma_{+}
connects
\lambda_{2}
and
\bar{\lambda}_{2}
and crosses
\mathbb{R}
outside the segment
[0,|\lambda_{1}|]
. For
\lambda_{0}\in\Sigma_{+}\cap\mathbb{R}
,
P(\lambda_{0})>0
so that
\Lambda=\pm 2\sqrt{P(\lambda_{0})}\in\mathbb{R}
.

•

\Sigma_{+}
connects
\lambda_{2}
and
\bar{\lambda}_{2}
and crosses
\mathbb{R}
inside the segment
(0,|\lambda_{1}|]
.
\Sigma_{+}
and
\mathbb{R}
intersect perpendicularly at
\lambda_{0}\in\Sigma_{+}\cap\mathbb{R}
due to the symmetry of
Lax spectrum in Proposition
4
and
P(\lambda_{0})\leq 0
. Hence, there is
\lambda\in\Sigma_{+}
such that
|\lambda-\lambda_{0}|
is small and
|{\rm Re}(\lambda-\lambda_{0})|\ll|{\rm Im}(\lambda)|
. Since
P^{\prime}(\lambda_{0})\in\mathbb{R}
, we have
{\rm Re}(P(\lambda))\leq 0
and
{\rm Im}(P(\lambda))\neq 0
for this
\lambda
so that
\Lambda=\pm 2\sqrt{P(\lambda)}\notin i\mathbb{R}
.

•

\Sigma_{+}
connects
\lambda_{2}
and
0
but does not intersect
(\mathbb{R}\cup i\mathbb{R})\backslash\{0\}
. Since
P(0)<0
,
P^{\prime}(0)=0
, and
P^{\prime\prime}(0)\in\mathbb{R}
, we have
{\rm Re}(P(\lambda))<0
and
{\rm Im}(P(\lambda))\neq 0
for every
\lambda\in\Sigma_{+}
with small
|\lambda|
and
{\rm Re}(\lambda){\rm Im}(\lambda)\neq 0
so that
\Lambda=\pm 2\sqrt{P(\lambda)}\notin i\mathbb{R}
.

•

\Sigma_{+}
connects
\lambda_{2}
and
-\bar{\lambda}_{2}
and crosses
i\mathbb{R}
.
\Sigma_{+}
and
i\mathbb{R}
intersect perpendicularly at
\lambda_{0}\in\Sigma_{+}\cap i\mathbb{R}
due to the symmetry of
Lax spectrum in Proposition
4
and
P(\lambda_{0})<0
. Hence, there is
\lambda\in\Sigma_{+}
such that
|\lambda-\lambda_{0}|
is small and
|{\rm Im}(\lambda-\lambda_{0})|\ll|{\rm Re}(\lambda)|
. Since
P^{\prime}(\lambda_{0})\in i\mathbb{R}
, we have
{\rm Re}(P(\lambda))<0
and
{\rm Im}(P(\lambda))\neq 0
for this
\lambda
so that
\Lambda=\pm 2\sqrt{P(\lambda)}\notin i\mathbb{R}
.

The list of four possibilities above is complete.

6.
Conclusion

We have studied the spectral stability of the periodic traveling waves in the focusing mKdV equation and showed that the instability bands for the cnoidal periodic waves transform from figure-
8
into figure-
\infty
due to the co-periodic instability bifurcation. This transformation is rather generic for other models with periodic traveling waves (Stokes waves)
[
10
,
24
,
20
]
.

The conclusion was obtained by using a relation between squared eigenfunctions of the Lax pair and eigenfunctions of the linearized mKdV equation at the periodic traveling waves. The location of the Lax spectrum remains an open problem, especially for the cnoidal periodic waves. It is expected that the elliptic function theory can be useful to compute it explicitly.

Given universality of the focusing mKdV equation for many applications in fluids, optics, and plasmas, the conclusions obtained in this work can be used for the comprehensive study of the modulational instability of the cnoidal periodic waves in the relevant non-integrable models.

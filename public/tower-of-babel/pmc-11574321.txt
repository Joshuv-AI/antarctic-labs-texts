The estimation of the horizontal parameters of gravity waves by the monostatic Rayleigh Lidar
Authors: Xuanyu Zheng, Xueming Li, Qihai Chang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractMonostatic Rayleigh Lidar is mainly used for the observation of the middle atmosphere temperature and vertical activity of gravity waves. It was thought that it could not be used for the study of the gravity waves horizontal activity. Through the area integration of gravity waves that vary with time and space, it is derived that the echo signal received by the Lidar contains the horizontal wavenumber information, which proves that the monostatic Rayleigh Lidar can realize the estimation of horizontal activity of gravity waves. The feasibility is verified by using the Rayleigh Lidar observation data from the Yanqing Observatory of the Chinese Academy of Sciences. This method is suitable for the short scale horizontal activity observation of gravity waves. To improve the observation reliability of this method, it is necessary to make the Rayleigh Lidar have a reasonable divergence angle, narrow linewidth laser, and higher vertical spatial resolution.Keywords: Monostatic, Rayleigh Lidar, Middle atmosphere, Horizontal fluctuationSubject terms: Atmospheric optics, Imaging and sensing

Introduction

The gas composition in the range of 30–80 km in the middle atmosphere is relatively pure and thin, and the Rayleigh Lidar based on the principle of elastic scattering is the only effective observation method for the middle atmosphere temperature activities before spaceborne radiometers become routine atmospheric observation techniques. The long-term observation and research of temperature activity by the middle atmosphere Lidar since the late 70s of the last centuries strongly supports the theory that gravitational wave activity affects temperature structure, atmospheric component transport, and energy momentum redistribution, and provides important data support for the construction of global numerical weather models1–3.

Early Lidar technology could only observe the relative density disturbance of the atmosphere by the number of echo photons scattered elastically. Hauchecorne and Chanin used the hydrostatic equilibrium principle and the ideal gas equation of state to give a method for inverting the temperature through echo photons, so that Rayleigh Lidar technology can be used independently for the middle atmosphere temperature observation4. Gardner, Shelton, and Voelz et al. gave the theory of gravity wave parameter spectrum of atmospheric density disturbance through the observation and study of sodium layer fluorescence Lidar, which laid the foundation for the application of the middle atmosphere Lidar in gravitational wave research5,6. Sica et al. achieved temperature observation over 100 km by compressing the laser linewidth, reducing the divergence angle, and using a super-large aperture energy product system of Rayleigh Lidar, and achieved an effective temperature profile observation with high spatial resolution of 48 m at an altitude of 90 km7. Chanin used the double-edge technique for the first time to observe the wind field in the middle atmosphere, which became a common method for Lidar wind measurement in the middle atmosphere and an important means for the study of gravity waves in the middle atmosphere8. With the rapid iteration of laser system miniaturization, space-borne, airborne and vehicle-based Lidar technology has been used for wind and temperature measurement in the middle atmosphere9,10, and the horizontal propagation observation of medium and large-scale gravitational waves has been realized. With the continuous development of innovative laser detection methods, various fluorescence and optical frequency discrimination technologies are used for atmospheric temperature, wind field observation and metal layer observation11–13.

Most of the middle atmosphere Rayleigh Lidars based on elastic scattering are in a monostatic state, which can be used for temperature observation and vertical wavenumber spectrum study of gravity waves but cannot be used to the horizontal activity of gravity waves. The vertical propagation of gravity waves enables the bottom-up transport of material and energy, and its horizontal propagation provides a transmission carrier for the global atmospheric circulation. Lidar with elastic scattering properties has a wide distribution around the world. If it can be made to observe the horizontal activity of gravity waves, it will undoubtedly provide better support for the optimization of atmospheric numerical models. In this paper, the feasibility of the study of gravity wave horizontal activity in the middle atmosphere by the monostatic Rayleigh Lidar is analyzed, verified and discussed.

Conclusion

Through the analysis of the working principle of Rayleigh Lidar and its interaction with atmospheric activity, it is concluded that the spatiotemporal propagating transverse wave number is hidden in the amplitude of time-dependent fluctuations under the condition of spatial integration. Therefore, it is possible to study the horizontal fluctuation through a monostatic Rayleigh Lidar observation of the middle atmosphere. Rayleigh Lidar data verify that the method can be used to estimate the horizontal fluctuation parameters of gravity waves in the middle atmosphere. Due to the upper limit of saturation wavelength, this method is suitable for short scale horizontal fluctuation observation, and it is necessary to reasonably select the laser divergence angle to achieve full height. To improve the signal intensity and the observed horizontal wavelength range, the spatial resolution and divergence angle should be increased as much as possible. The current high-power laser megahertz linewidth compression, narrowband filtering and fast light pulse triggering technologies can support the implementation of this method. Rayleigh Lidar equipment for the middle atmosphere temperature observation is spread all over the world, and the horizontal activity observation function of gravity waves can effectively advance the understanding of dynamic couples in the middle atmosphere.

Data processing and discussion

The data used in this paper are taken from the Lidar observations at the Beijing Yanqing Observatory of the Meridian Project of Chinese Academy of Sciences14. The Lidar system is designed with dual wavelengths and three channels. It can simultaneously emit laser beams with a divergence angle of 0.5 mrad at 532 nm and 589 nm wavelengths, and uses Mie scattering, Rayleigh scattering and sodium resonance fluorescence scattering three-channel receiving technology to effectively detect the atmospheric temperature in the altitude range of 35–80 km and the density of sodium layer in the altitude range of 80–110 km. The original echo profile is the accumulated result of 5000 laser pulses with a repetition rate of 30 Hz laser in 162s, with a spatial resolution of 96 m.

The influence of divergence angles of laser to the wavelength range and observational reliability.

The divergence angle is a characteristic parameter of the laser, which makes the laser spot increase with distance. The echo photons received by the Lidar are the accumulation of elastic backscattered photons of the molecules in the illuminated cross-section, which also realizes the spatial sampling of the gravity wave in the middle atmosphere and stores the wave information in the count of echo photons. The influence of spot diameter or divergence angle on wavelength was analyzed by using Eq. 10. For the sake of consistency, the perturbation amplitude Nd in Eq. 10 is removed from the calculation. Figure 5 shows the relationship between wavelength and amplitude at an altitude of 35 km under the condition of different Lidar divergence angles. The results show that the relationship between wavelength and amplitude is nonlinear, and there is a saturation wavelength range. The saturation wavelength is caused by the rapid decrease of the wavelength information in the photon count due to the increase of the wavelength scale relative to the laser spot diameter. In fact, the wavelength saturation region shows a small monotonic slope change, and the specific saturation wavelength also depends on the choice of wave amplitude Nd. The spot diameter is calculated using the atmospheric refractive index of the model16.

Assuming that the vertical perturbation and the horizontal perturbation are constant, the ratio of the two depends on the ratio of the side to the top area. When the side area of the backscatter volume is larger than the top area, the perturbation of vertical propagation accounts for the main component. When the side area of the unit backscatter volume element is less than the top area, the horizontal propagation perturbation accounts for the main component.

Therefore, to improve the reliability of horizontal wavenumber observation, the spatial resolution of Lidar should be improved as much as possible to reduce the influence of the horizontal flux of the vertical fluctuation. The observation volume of the Lidar in this paper is about 192 m high and 17.5 m in diameter at the height of 35 km, which is quite different from the ideal conditions for the horizontal fluctuation of the observation. This is the reason why the data processing requires careful signal processing; otherwise, it is difficult to find clear periodic fluctuations as shown in Fig. 2.

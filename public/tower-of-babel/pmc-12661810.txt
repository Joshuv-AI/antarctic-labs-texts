Effects of cardiac decellularized extracellular matrix hydrogel as a functional myocardial patch on the growth of cardiomyocytes
Authors: Jun Hu, Wei Xiao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesTo address the immune rejection and inflammatory reactions that currently exist in functional myocardial patches when promoting myocardial cell growth.MethodsThe cardiac acellular Decellularized Extracellular Matrix (dECM) hydrogel was prepared and used as a functional myocardial patch for immune rejection and inflammatory reaction.ResultsCompared with hyaluronic acid hydrogel and cardiomyocytes in tissue culture plate, the cardiomyocytes in the cardiac dECM hydrogel had the strongest cell viability. After 5 days of culture, the cell viability increased to 292.9% relative to control. Moreover, after the hydrogel culture, the relative expression levels of CACNA1C and ATP2A2 genes in cardiomyocytes reached 3.43 and 1.83, respectively, which were also higher than those in the other two groups.ConclusionsFrom the above results, cardiac dECM hydrogel can promote the growth of cardiomyocytes.

Background

With the improvement of economic level, the emphasis on physical health is constantly increasing [1]. The heart is one of the most important organs in the human body and other organisms, and its growth and development process is complex [2]. As the basic structural and functional unit of the heart, cardiomyocytes have a significant impact on the development of the human heart and the treatment of various heart diseases through their growth and development [3, 4]. A low number of cardiomyocytes can have adverse effects on various functions of the heart, thereby affecting human health [5]. Therefore, many scholars have explored cardiomyocytes to promote their growth using different methods. For example, H. Li et al. developed an engineered cardiac tissue device based on closed-loop control to regulate the contractile force of cardiomyocytes, which was used for testing in myocardial growth. The results showed that the device could increase the vitality of cardiomyocytes by 23.3% [6]. M. N. Duc et al. proposed a bioimpedance-based cell necrosis ablation method to ablate necrotic cells during cardiac growth and ensure the growth of cardiomyocytes. The test results showed that this method could accelerate the ablation of necrotic cells [7]. In addition, to slow down the death rate of cardiomyocytes in cardiovascular diseases, S. Mandala et al. designed a myocardial infarction detection system based on convolutional neural networks. The test results showed that the detection accuracy of the system could reach 96.7%, and timely intervention was carried out to slow down the death rate of cardiomyocytes [8]. F. L. Bueno Palomeque et al. proposed a myocardial cell growth measurement method based on morphological remodeling and T-wave dominant carriers to intervene cardiac spherical markers in patients with intrauterine growth restriction. The test results showed that this method could accurately test the morphology of cardiomyocytes during the growth process, thereby helping doctors effectively intervene in the growth of cardiomyocytes in patients [9]. Although the above methods can promote the growth of cardiomyocytes, the various materials used in these methods may trigger chronic inflammatory reactions or cause serious immune rejection reactions in cardiomyocytes. Therefore, improvements are necessary for these methods [10].

Decellularized Extracellular Matrix (dECM) is a natural material that retains the three-dimensional structure, adhesive proteins, proteoglycans, and other active ingredients after removing the extracellular matrix [11]. The dECM hydrogel is a three-dimensional hydrophilic network structure generated by various reactions based on dECM. This material preserves the natural three-dimensional growth environment of cardiomyocytes, which is used to treat various diseases [12]. For example, L. Lian et al. conducted research on dECM. The results showed that the material could generate mature tissue like constructs, providing a bioink library for volumetric bio coatings of biomaterials in the medical field [13]. S. P. John et al. analyzed the research on the preparation of cardiac tissue engineering biomaterials using dECM. The results showed that the material could be used to produce cardiac engineering tissue materials [14]. K. Wu et al. developed an injectable hydrogel based on dECM for the base cell derived factor to improve the retention rate and survival rate of exogenous myocardial cell transplantation in myocardial infarction. The results showed that the hydrogel could improve the retention rate and survival rate of exogenous myocardial cell transplantation [15]. F. Blaudez et al. designed an extracellular matrix melting electrocautery scaffold based on dECM to improve bone formation ability. The test results showed that the scaffold could promote bone formation [16].

In summary, although there are currently many methods to promote the growth of cardiomyocytes, these methods may also cause various inflammatory reactions and reduce myocardial cell viability. dECM is a natural biomaterial, and the cardiac dECM can simulate the growth environment of cardiomyocytes, thereby reduce inflammatory reactions and other rejection situations, and ensure the growth of cardiomyocytes. Therefore, a cardiac dECM hydrogel is prepared and used as a myocardial patch to promote the growth of biological cardiomyocytes and treat various heart diseases. The innovation of the research lies in preparing injectable hydrogel from the action of cardiac acellular mechanism, constructing functional minimally invasive patch in the form of “patch” in vitro, and using it as a myocardial patch to quantify the dual effects of matrix promoting growth and maturation. In addition, growth factors are introduced into dECM hydrogel, which can regulate the growth, differentiation and function of cells and improve the performance of myocardial patch. Although prior studies in various pre-clinical infarction models have already validated the therapeutic potential of cardiac dECM hydrogels, this study provides additional evidence for in vitro cell growth promotion and gene upregulation. The contribution of the research is to analyze the effect of cardiac dECM hydrogel on the growth of cardiomyocytes, help promote scientific research in the field of cardiomyocytes, and provide reference for other tissue engineering and regenerative medicine applications.

The results of dECM hydrogel in anti-infection ability and healing promoting effects

After analyzing the growth condition of dECM hydrogel on cardiomyocytes, the hydrogel was used in animals for verification. The experimental animals were C57BL/6 J mice, a recognized model for cardiovascular research, aged 8–12 weeks and weighing 20–25 g. Seventy mice were selected and divided into 7 groups with an average of 10 mice in each group. All experiments were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) and strictly followed the “3R” principle to ensure ethical compliance throughout the entire process. Under anesthesia (using isoflurane inhalation anesthesia, maintaining anesthesia depth to ensure smooth breathing and muscle relaxation in mice), a standard myocardial injury model was created at the heart site of each mouse. The specific operation is as follows: expose the heart through open chest surgery, use fine surgical instruments to create a full-thickness myocardial injury wound with a size of about 2 mm × 2 mm on the myocardium, and simulate myocardial damage. The study aims to simulate the repair process after myocardial injury, with a focus on examining the effects of different treatment methods on anti-infection and promoting healing. The model is designed to simulate normal wound healing. The treatment methods are as follows: control group, without any treatment, the dECM hydrogel load does not require additional drugs (dECM), and the prepared dECM hydrogel is evenly applied on the surface of the myocardial injury wound, with a thickness of about 0.5 mm, covering the entire wound area. Prepare a suitable concentration of ciprofloxacin solution using ciprofloxacin alone (CIP), and inject it locally into the surrounding tissue of myocardial injury wounds at a volume of 50 μL per mouse. Prepare an appropriate concentration of nitric oxide donor (GSNO) solution using a single GSNO and inject it locally around the myocardial injury wound in a volume of 50 μ l per mouse. dECM hydrogel is loaded with CIP (dECM/CIP). During the preparation of dECM hydrogel, CIP is evenly mixed in the hydrogel to make the final concentration of CIP reach the preset value. Then, the dECM hydrogel loaded with CIP is applied to the myocardial injury wound to cover the wound. dECM hydrogel is loaded with GSNO (dECM/GSNO). During the preparation of dECM hydrogel, GSNO is added and mixed evenly to reach a specific concentration. The dECM hydrogel loaded with GSNO is applied to myocardial injury wounds. dECM hydrogel is loaded with CIP and GSNO (dECMCIP/GSNO). In the preparation stage of dECM hydrogel, CIP and GSNO are added simultaneously. After full mixing, they are applied to the wound of myocardial injury. The myocardial wounds of mice were treated with the above seven methods, respectively. Finally, HE staining, COL I staining, TNF-α staining, and CD31 staining were performed on the cells extracted from the wound center to evaluate the recovery of cell tissue structure, degree of inflammation, and healing process based on the staining results. At the initial stage of dECM hydrogel application, positive staining may occur, because the hydrogel itself contains collagen. However, over time, the collagen synthesized by the wound itself gradually increases. By comparing the staining results at different timepoints, the deposition of collagen in the hydrogel and the new collagen in the wound can be clearly distinguished, so as to accurately assess the dynamic changes of collagen protein in the process of wound healing. Discussion

The cardiac dECM hydrogel is derived from cardiac cell tissue and retains most of the structural components and biological activity of cells. It can simulate natural cardiac tissue and has biological activity. In addition, cardiac dECM hydrogel can provide suitable microenvironment for cardiomyocytes [17]. Due to the above characteristics, this material can serve as a functional myocardial patch, providing support for the regeneration and repair of cardiomyocytes. Myocardial patch refers to the composite of bioactive substances with certain functions and suitable scaffold materials to construct biomaterials that can be used for transplantation, repair, or replacement of autologous myocardium in living organisms [18]. Functional myocardial patches are myocardial patches with certain mechanical properties, which can simulate natural myocardial tissue and provide the necessary conditions and environment for the growth and repair [19]. Cardiac dECM functional patches are generally used to repair cardiac tissue damage, usually implanted into the heart area through surgery, to help the organism restore heart structure and function [20]. It is mainly used for myocardial repair after myocardial infarction, congenital heart disease defects, and cardiac trauma [21].

To analyze the effect of cardiac dECM hydrogel as a functional myocardial patch on cardiomyocytes, this study first prepared dECM from pig cardiomyocytes, then prepared dECM hydrogel, and made it into a functional myocardial patch to analyze the effect of dECM hydrogel on the growth of cardiomyocytes. The results showed that after HE staining and Masson staining, the content of cell nuclei in dECM was significantly reduced compared to cardiac tissue cells. The DNA content in dECM was much lower than that in normal myocardial tissue. The DNA content in normal cardiac tissue cells reached 853.5 ng/mg, while the DNA content in the prepared dECM was only 37.2 ng/mg. This result is roughly consistent with the results drawn by W. Guo et al., but the DNA content in the dECM prepared by W. Guo et al. reached 100 ng/mg, which was a relatively high content. From the research method proposed by W. Guo et al., when preparing dECM, the concentration of decellularizing reagents used for dECM is relatively low, and the decellularization time is also relatively short. Therefore, the dECM still contains many cellular residues, resulting in a higher DNA content in the dECM [22]. In this study, the reagent concentration and decellularization time were sufficient, resulting in lower nuclear and DNA content in the prepared dECM. Furthermore, further analysis was conducted on the proliferation of cardiomyocytes. Y. Efraim et al. and the cardiomyocytes used in this study were all immature mammals [23]. The research results drawn by O. Bergmann et al. showed that the annual turnover rate of cardiomyocytes in humans was about 1% at the age of 25, and decreased to 0.45% at the age of 75. After adulthood, the cardiac cell cycle of mammals stagnated, and the expression level of downregulation cycle inhibitors increased, resulting in extremely weak proliferative capacity [24].

Then, the structure of dECM hydrogel prepared based on dECM was analyzed. Compared with ordinary HA hydrogel, the surface of dECM hydrogel was relatively rough. The relatively rough dECM hydrogel can provide more attachment sites for cells, which is conducive to their adhesion, extension and formation of local microenvironment. Pore size can affect the diffusion and metabolic efficiency of nutrient positions in cells. The compressive modulus can disperse the mechanical stimuli that cells receive. When the pore diameter and stiffness are almost the same, dECM hydrogel shows higher cell adhesion rate, spreading area and proliferation rate. This result shows that the basic structure of the prepared dECM hydrogel is similar to that of ordinary hydrogel. Therefore, it can be speculated that the prepared dECM hydrogel can provide a suitable living environment for cells just like ordinary hydrogel. The above results are similar to the experimental results drawn by H. J. Ju et al., but the difference is that the dECM hydrogel prepared by H. J. Ju et al. has a large pore size. The reason for this phenomenon may be that the H. J. Ju et al. still retains most of the cell fragments when preparing the dECM, which leads to the filling effect in the subsequent hydrogel formation process, making the pore size increase [25]. The modulus can be increased to 10 kPa by adding nanocellulose, combined with dynamic culture under 10% cyclic stretching. The experiment confirmed that the arrangement of cardiomyocytes, calcium transients, and contraction force were significantly improved, thus solving the problem of stiffness mismatch. The results showed that TNF-α and CD31 were increasing, which may be due to the strong initial inflammatory reaction caused by dECM hydrogel. In the case of infected wounds, this may help to fight infection, making hydrogels potentially “anti-infective”.

After analyzing the prepared dECM hydrogel, to analyze the effect of dECM hydrogel functional myocardial patch on the growth of cardiomyocytes. After different incubation periods, compared with TCP control group and HA hydrogel, the activity of cells in dECM hydrogel incubator was the strongest. After being cultured in dECM hydrogel for 1 day, 3 days and 5 days, the cell viability of cardiomyocytes in dECM hydrogel incubator could reach 147.4%, 217.3% and 292.9%, respectively. In addition, among the expression levels of CACNA1C and ATP2A2 genes in the Ca2+ channel of the cardiomyocytes in the three incubators, the dECMS hydrogel relative expression level was the highest. After being cultured in dECM hydrogel for 5 days, the expression levels of NKX2.5 and GATA4 transcription factors in the cardiomyocytes of the incubator were also the highest, reaching 1.91 and 2.18. The above results are similar to the experimental results obtained by L. Gao et al., who found that the expression level of NKX2.5 and GATA4 transcription factors in dECM hydrogel can reach a higher level when treating high pancreatitis cells [26]. Therefore, in the neonatal rat ventricular myocyte model, dECM hydrogel scaffold significantly up-regulated the expression of key genes, such as CACNA1C, ATP2A2, NKX2.5, and GATA4. This transcriptional profile change directly transformed into three major advantages: sarcomere maturation, enhanced electromechanical coupling, and paracrine vascularization, which ultimately increased the contractility and conduction speed of the engineered myocardial patch. From this result, dECM hydrogel may provide sufficient nutrition and living environment for cardiomyocytes due to its unique microstructure and chemical composition, thus promoting the growth and proliferation of cardiomyocytes. In addition, the CACNA1C and ATP2A2 of dECM hydrogel can regulate the Ca2+ channel based on the high expression level, thus affecting the activity and contraction of electrical signals of cardiomyocytes. It can be observed that when dECM hydrogel upregulates CACNA1C and ATP2A2, calcium transient increases. The increase in single-cell contraction amplitude, while the HA group showed neither overall contraction nor significant single-cell contraction enhancement, proves that dECM directly improves the electromechanical coupling and tissue level contraction ability of cardiomyocytes by regulating Ca2+channel function.

Conclusion

From the above results, dECM hydrogel can promote the growth of cardiomyocytes as a functional myocardial patch. dECM hydrogel shows promise in supporting cardiomyocyte growth, which is beneficial to myocardial repair strategies. However, this study only analyzes the effect of dECM hydrogel in a specific cell culture environment. Future research will further expand the influence of this hydrogel on the growth of cardiomyocytes under other conditions. In addition, in the future, attention should be paid to the process and effect of adding growth factors to the dECM hydrogel to prepare functional patches, without considering the setting of this specific control to further analyze the impact of related factors.

Material preparation and performance testing

Cardiac dECM hydrogel

dECM refers to a biomaterial that removes cellular components from tissues through physical, chemical, or enzymatic methods, retaining only the natural structure of the extracellular matrix and active ingredients, such as collagen and elastin [27]. This material is a biodegradable material that can degrade in living organisms, reducing various waste and energy consumption caused by traditional treatment methods, thereby achieving environmental protection. Moreover, dECM hydrogel has played a role in the recruitment of progenitor cells and functional recovery of myocardial infarction in rats, indicating that this hydrogel has certain therapeutic effect on myocardial infarction [28]. dECM hydrogel refers to the three-dimensional hydrophilic network structure formed by cross-linking dECM powder or solution through physical, chemical or enzymatic reactions [29]. The cardiac dECM hydrogel refers to the hydrogel formed by the processing of dECM extracted from cardiac tissue, which is a bionic biomaterial [20]. In Fig. 1a, dECM retains the structure and composition of the extracellular matrix, removes cell membrane antigens and nuclear substances, and can significantly reduce immune rejection reactions. Moreover, this material can fully preserve the three-dimensional fiber network structure of the extracellular matrix, providing biomimetic physical support and suitable for various biomimetic materials. Preparation of cardiac dECM

After learning the characteristics of cardiac dECM hydrogel in the previous section, to further analyze the efficacy of cardiac dECM hydrogel, the effect of this material as a myocardial patch on the growth of cardiomyocytes is discussed. This study first prepares the cardiac dECM, and its preparation method is shown in Fig. 13.Fig. 13Preparation method of cardiac dECM(Source from: Author’s self-drawn)

Preparation method of cardiac dECM

(Source from: Author’s self-drawn)

As shown in Fig. 13, when preparing the dECM for the heart, the source of the heart tissue is as follows: pig heart tissue was selected as the raw material for the study, which was obtained from healthy commercial pigs in the local slaughterhouse. The breed was Yorkshire × Changbai hybrid pig, August Ling, with a weight of 120 kg, and the discarded heart tissue organs were used. The heart used in the experiment was provided free of charge by the slaughterhouse, which is a byproduct of meat processing and complies with the “3R principle” of “reduction, substitution, and optimization”. When selecting, only healthy pig hearts without lesions or drug residues are selected, which are verified by the slaughterhouse veterinarian through quarantine reports. Obtain fresh and unfrozen pig hearts, and rinse the surface of the pig hearts with tap water to remove various impurities. After washing the pig heart clean, remove all the right ventricle cells and tissues from the pig heart, as well as all the fat and fascia from the left ventricle. Retain the myocardial tissue of the left ventricle with pig cardiac tissue cells. Cut the preserved myocardial tissue into approximately 2 mm × 2 mm × 2 mm cubes at a temperature of around 4 °C. Then, divide the cubes into different conical flasks and add decellularization solution. Add 1% Sodium Dodecyl Sulfate (SDS) solution to decellularize the cardiomyocytes. Simultaneously, add 4 ml of penicillin–streptomycin (PS) solution, stir and clean under a magnetic stirrer for 4 days at a stirring speed of 150 rpm. SDS is an anionic surfactant with a molecular structure consisting of hydrophilic and hydrophobic groups. The cell membrane is mainly composed of phospholipid bilayers, with a hydrophobic interior and a hydrophilic exterior. The hydrophobic end of SDS can be inserted into the lipid layer of the cell membrane, disrupting the integrity of the cell membrane and causing leakage of cellular contents, thereby achieving cell dissolution and removal. When the concentration of SDS is below 0.5%, the residual amount of cell nuclei in cardiac tissue is significantly higher, and the decellularization effect is not ideal. However, excessive concentration of SDS can more thoroughly remove cells, but it can also cause excessive damage to the Extracellular Matrix (ECM). When the concentration of SDS exceeds 2%, the collagen content and elastic modulus of the dECM in the heart significantly decrease. Therefore, a 1% SDS concentration is a balanced choice that minimizes ECM damage while ensuring effective decellularization. Decellularization is a dynamic process, in which SDS solution gradually penetrates into the heart tissue over time, interacts with cells, and removes cellular components. By observing the tissue changes at different sampling points, 4 days are selected as the decellularization time. The observation results show that in the first 2 days, removing cells from the surface of the tissue is the main process, while there are still many cells inside the tissue. On the third day, the number of cells inside the organization decreases significantly, but there are still some residual cells. Until the fourth day, the cells in the organization are basically removed. Therefore, it is chosen to decellularize for 4 days. During stirring, change the SDS solution and PS solution every other day to obtain the cardiac dECM. However, there are still residual SDS and PS solutions added to the dECM, and the cardiac dECM needs to be washed in a large amount of phosphate buffer solution for 8 consecutive washes, with each wash lasting 30 min. Phosphate buffer solution can decompose, dilute and carry away SDS and PS in the gaps and surfaces of myocardial tissue. After rinsing, wash multiple times with physiological saline and disinfect with ethanol to remove residual cell debris, reagents, bacteria, and other impurities in the myocardial tissue. Transfer the processed cardiac dECM to a 50 ml centrifuge tube and freeze it using a freeze dryer for 24 h at a temperature ranging from − 40 °C to − 80 °C. After freezing, take a grinder to grind it into powder state, set the grinding speed to 200–500 rpm, and grind for 10 min. Finally, store it in a drying oven for preservation. During the drying process, freeze–drying is performed first. In the freeze dryer, the temperature is controlled within the range of − 50 °C to − 60 °C, the vacuum degree is less than or equal to 0.05 mbar, and the drying time is between 24 and 36 h. After secondary drying, raise the temperature to the range of 20 °C–25 °C for 2 h, and finally obtain the high school post cardiac dECM. When storing the dECM, it should be refrigerated at 4 °C, dry, and away from light. The reagents used in the preparation process are shown in Table 1. Preparation result detection method

After preparing dECM and dECM hydrogel, the reliability of the preparation results is tested. First, dECM and dECM hydrogel will be subject to “fixation embedding slicing” treatment, and the treatment methods of the two materials are the same. The process is as follows: fix the material with 4% paraformaldehyde at pH 7 and 4 °C. Dehydrate the material using four different gradients of ethanol: 70%, 80%, 90%, and 100%, with each gradient dehydrated for 1 h. Then, use xylene I and II to perform transparency treatment on the material for 30 min. Afterwards, conduct wax immersion treatment at 60 °C for 2 h. Then, a standard paraffin embedding machine is used for embedding treatment, and finally a paraffin slicer is used for wax cutting treatment, cutting it into a thickness of 5 µm. When detecting dECM, Hematoxylin–eosin Staining (HE) is used to stain dECM and cardiac tissue (Harris hematoxylin + 1% eosin). By comparing the staining results of dECM with those of cardiac tissue, the residual cell status of dECM is analyzed. After HE staining, the nucleus are stained blue by hematoxylin, while the cytoplasm and extracellular matrix are stained red by eosin dye [32]. Further analysis is conducted using the Masson staining method. The staining procedure is as follows: first, dewax the heart dECM and hydrate it with xylene I and II and ethanol for 5 min. Then, the material was nuclear stained with Weigert iron hematoxylin A + B mixture (1:1) for 10 min to make the cell nucleus appear black blue. Further treat it with 1% hydrochloric acid ethanol for 3–5 s to remove its distinctive background, and then rinse with running water. Afterwards, stain the muscle fibers with 0.5% Li Chun Hong acid fuchsin solution for 5 min to make them red, and remove excess red dye with 1% phosphomolybdic acid. Stain collagen fibers with 2% aniline blue for 2 min to make them appear blue. Finally, treat with ethanol and xylene for 1 min each for dehydration and sealing.

The DNA extraction and quantification methods in the material are as follows: when extracting DNA, the phenol chloroform extraction method is used to extract DNA. A lysis buffer containing SDS and EDTA and protease K are added to the myocardial tissue and dECM, and digested in a water bath at around 55 °C for several hours to completely lyse the cells. Add a mixture of phenol chloroform isoamyl alcohol in a volume ratio of 25:24:1, with an amount equal to the volume of the lysis buffer. Transfer the aqueous phase to a new centrifuge tube and repeat the extraction steps 1–2 times to further remove the protein. Then add 0.1 times the volume of 3 M sodium acetate with a pH of 5.2 and 2 times the volume of anhydrous ethanol to the supernatant, gently mix, and at this point, DNA will form a white flocculent precipitate. Collect DNA precipitate by centrifugation, and wash the precipitate 1–2 times with 70% ethanol to remove residual salt. Finally, dry the DNA precipitate and dissolve it in an appropriate amount of TE buffer or sterile double distilled water to obtain a DNA solution. Then, use the fluorescent dye binding method to quantify DNA. First, prepare a series of DNA standard solutions with different concentrations using known concentrations of DNA standards. Add appropriate amounts of PicoGreen dye to each solution and measure the fluorescence intensity on a fluorescence spectrophotometer or enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay. Plot the standard curve with DNA concentration as the horizontal axis and fluorescence intensity as the vertical axis. Then take the DNA solution to be tested, add PicoGreen dye, and measure the fluorescence intensity under the same instrument conditions. Convert the measured fluorescence intensity of the sample to DNA content based on the obtained standard curve. The DNA content is ultimately standardized by wet weight to avoid the influence of dry weight on DNA distribution and content.

After detecting the prepared dECM, the prepared dECM hydrogel is analyzed by microscope, and the microstructure of dECM hydrogel is compared with that of ordinary Hyaluronic Acid (HA) hydrogel to analyze whether the prepared dECM hydrogel meets the standard. Take Nikon Eclipse Ti–E upright fluorescence microscope and NIS Elements AR 5.20.01 software to collect images. The microscopic structure of cells was analyzed by the results of HE staining and Masson staining, and the pore size of hydrogel is identified by importing the image obtained by scanning electron microscope into the image analysis software NIS-Elements AR 5.20.01.

From Fig. 15, there are many methods to make cardiac dECM hydrogel into functional myocardial patch. Functional myocardial patches can be prepared by combining dECM hydrogel with polymer materials, adding growth factors or nanomaterials to dECM hydrogel, adding active factors, or controlling the flow direction of hydrogel. The functional myocardial patch is prepared by adding growth factors into the hydrogel. Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor (VEGF) is selected as the additive in the hydrogel. First, 10 ng/mL of VEGF is dissolved in phosphate buffered physiological saline of physiological concentration (0.01 M, pH 7.2–7.4), and the precooled dECM solution is added drop by drop. At 4 °C, the solution is slowly stirred at 300–800 rpm for 2 h to ensure that VEGF and dECM solution are fully mixed. Then, under 100W and 5 s/5 s pulse conditions, ultrasound treatment is performed for 2 min to eliminate bubbles and promote uniform distribution of VEGF. Then, 0.1% glutamialdehydee (GA) is added to fix VEGF in the hydrogel network to prepare functional myocardial patches. In myocardial repair, 10 ng/mL VEGF has been shown to effectively induce endothelial cell migration and angiogenesis while promoting myocardial cell survival and functional recovery. The 0.1% GA concentration is optimized, which is enough to fix VEGF in the hydrogel network, prevent VEGF from releasing rapidly in the body, and ensure its long-term release and biological activity. When using 0.1% glutaraldehyde to fix VEGF in the hydrogel, first carry out the neutralization reaction, and immediately add an appropriate amount of sodium borohydride (NaBH4) solution to the cross-linked gel. The aldehyde group of GA is reduced to the alcohol group, so as to terminate the cross-linking reaction and reduce the toxicity of GA. Slowly stir the mixture at a speed of 300–800 rpm at 4 °C for 30 min to 1 h to ensure sufficient contact between NaBH4 and unreacted GA and initiate a reduction reaction. To ensure that the concentration of residual GA and NaBH4 in the hydrogel is within the safe range, the residual crosslinking agent test is required. The hydrogel extract is co-cultured with cells to observe the cell survival rate and morphological changes. If the cell survival rate is higher than 90% and the cell morphology is normal, it can be considered that the residual crosslinking agent concentration is within a safe range. To further control cell toxicity, NaBH4 is used for secondary reduction treatment to reduce GA toxicity. Although 0.1% GA cross-linking has potential toxicity, after the second reduction of NaBH4, the cell survival rate, hemolysis rate, and in vivo inflammation index of the hydrogel all meet the safety standards of biomaterials. An EDC/NHS low toxicity cross-linking system has been established as an alternative to ensure that the VEGF fixation effect and function are not reduced. In this functional patch, the cardiac dECM hydrogel is still the main component of this functional patch, and the main substance for this material to play the role is also dECM hydrogel.

Detection method for myocardial cell growth

To verify the effect of cardiac dECM hydrogel as a functional myocardial patch on the growth of cardiomyocytes, the development of cardiomyocytes during the growth process is analyzed. In this study, cardiomyocytes are cultured in three different ways: cardiac dECM hydrogel, HA, and Tissue Culture Plate (TCP). To match the pig derived dECM hydrogel, primary ventricular myocytes of newborn Sprague Dawley rats (1–3 days) are used for digestion and separation according to the modified Neonatal Cardiomyocyte Isolation System (Worthington Biochemical). After the cells are purified by Percoll gradient centrifugation, they were inoculated on the surface of dECM hydrogel, HA hydrogel and TCP at a density of 5× 105 cells/cm2. The culture medium was DMEM/F-12 containing 10% FBS, and is cultured at 37 °C and 5% CO2. When fusing cardiac myocytes with dECM hydrogel for scaffold preparation, place 100 µL dECM prepolymer on ice, take 25 µL cell suspension, gently blow it for three times, and finally obtain a mixed volume of 125 µL. Then, inject the mixture into the mold, and incubate it at 37 °C for 5 min. dECM self-crosslinks into glue, and then demould to prepare the scaffold. TCP is a control. The reason for choosing HA as the experimental control group is that HA has been widely used as the basic material of cardiovascular tissue engineering. It is set as the control group, which can directly observe the performance difference between dECM hydrogel and HA hydrogel, and verify the performance advantages of dECM hydrogel.

Both DNA testing and cell survival rate were analyzed using one-way ANOVA for statistical analysis.

Some inequalities for the weighted log canonical thresholds
Nguyen Xuan Hong

Abstract
. In this paper, we will study the relations on the weighted log canonical thresholds of plurisubharmonic functions. We prove some inequalities for the weighted log canonical thresholds.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
4B05; 32S05; 32U05; 32U25

1.
Introduction

Research on the singularities of the plurisubharmonic functions plays an important role of several complex variables and complex geometry. Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
containing the origin
0
and let
\varphi
be a plurisubharmonic function in
\Omega
.
A basic characteristic of singularity of
\varphi
at
0
is its Lelong number which is defined by

\nu_{\varphi}(0):=\liminf_{z\to 0}\frac{\varphi(z)}{\log\|z\|}.

One more characteristic was introduced by Demailly and Kollár in
[
7
]
as a quantitative way of measuring singularities of a plurisubharmonic function.
Let
f
be a holomorphic function in
\Omega
. Hiep
[
12
]
called the number

c_{0}^{f}(\varphi):=\sup\{c\geq 0:|f|^{2}e^{-2c\varphi}\text{ is integrable near }0\}

is the weighted log canonical threshold of
\varphi
with weight
f
at
0
. The strong openness conjecture of Demailly
[
5
]
states that

a:=c_{0}^{f}(\varphi)<+\infty\Rightarrow|f|^{2}e^{-2a\varphi}\text{ is not integrable near }0.

The conjecture was proved by
[
9
,
12
]
(also see
[
3
]
).

Now, let
I\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{0}
be a coherent ideal and let
\{f_{j}\}_{0\leq j\leq m}
be generators of
I
.
The jumping number of
\varphi
is defined by

c_{0}^{I}(\varphi):=\sup\{c\geq 0:\sum_{0\leq j\leq m}|f_{j}|^{2}e^{-2c\varphi}\text{ is integrable near }0\}.

Especially, the number
c_{0}^{\mathcal{O}_{0}}(\varphi)
is just the complex singularity exponent
c_{0}(\varphi)
. Moreover, when
\{z_{1}^{2m},\ldots,z_{n}^{2m}\}
are generators of
I
, the number
c_{0}^{I}(\varphi)
is called the weighted log canonical threshold
c_{m}(\varphi)
of
\varphi
at
0
.
More generally, we define

c_{t}(\varphi):=\sup\{c\geq 0:\|z\|^{2t}e^{-2c\varphi}\text{ is integrable near }0\},\ t>-n.

The strong openness conjecture tells us that

b:=c_{t}(\varphi)<+\infty\Rightarrow\|z\|^{2t}e^{-2b\varphi}\text{ is not integrable near }0.

This article aims to study the relations on the weighted log canonical thresholds of plurisubharmonic functions.
A well-known inequality due to Skoda
[
22
]
tells us that

\frac{1}{\nu_{\varphi}(0)}\leq c_{0}(\varphi)\leq\frac{n}{\nu_{\varphi}(0)}

provided
\nu_{\varphi}(0)>0
. Thus, there exists is an inequality in the relationship between the Lelong number and the complex singularity exponent of plurisubharmonic functions. Our first main theorem is the following, which is a generalization of
[
22
]
to the weighted log canonical thresholds.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
containing the origin
0
. Assume that
t>-n
is a real number and
\varphi
is a plurisubharmonic function in
\Omega
.
Then,
\nu_{\varphi}(0)\in(0,+\infty)
if and only if
c_{t}(\varphi)\in(0,+\infty)
. Moreover,

\frac{1}{\nu_{\varphi+\frac{|t|-t}{c_{t}(\varphi)}\log\|z\|}(0)}\leq c_{t}(\varphi)\leq\frac{t+n}{\nu_{\varphi}(0)}

provided that
\nu_{\varphi}(0)>0
.

The sharpness of Theorem
1.1
is explained as follows. Let
\varphi
and
\psi
be plurisubharmonic functions in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
defined by

(1.1)

\varphi(z):=\log|z_{1}|\ \text{ and }\ \psi(z):=\log\|z\|,\text{ where }z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}).

It is easy to see that

\nu_{\varphi}(0)=\nu_{\psi}(0)=1,\ c_{t}(\psi)=n+t

and

c_{t}(\varphi)=1\ \text{ when }\ t(n-1)>0.

Therefore,

c_{t}(\varphi)=\frac{1}{\nu_{\varphi}(0)},\ \text{ when }\ t\geq 0,\ n\geq 2

and

c_{t}(\psi)=\frac{t+n}{\nu_{\psi}(0)},\ \forall t>-n.

Now, assume that
1\leq k\leq n
is an integer number.
A result of Hiep
[
13
]
tells us that

c_{k-n}(\varphi)=\sup\{c_{0}(\varphi|_{H}):H\text{ are }k\text{-dimensional linear subspaces through }0\}.

Later on,
Guan and Zhou
[
10
]
showed that

c_{k-n+2}(\varphi)-c_{k-n+1}(\varphi)\leq c_{k-n+1}(\varphi)-c_{k-n}(\varphi)

provided that
k\leq n-2
and
\nu_{\varphi}(0)>0
.
Our second main theorem is the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
containing the origin
0
and let
\varphi
be a plurisubharmonic function in
\Omega
such that
\nu_{\varphi}(0)>0
.
Then,

0\leq\frac{c_{p}(\varphi)-c_{q}(\varphi)}{p-q}\leq\frac{c_{s}(\varphi)-c_{t}(\varphi)}{s-t}\leq\frac{c_{t}(\varphi)}{t+n}\leq\frac{1}{\nu_{\varphi}(0)}

for any
p>q\geq s>t>-n
.

Let
\varphi
and
\psi
be as in (
1.1
). Then,

\frac{c_{p}(\varphi)-c_{q}(\varphi)}{p-q}=\frac{c_{s}(\varphi)-c_{t}(\varphi)}{s-t}=0,\ \forall p>q\geq s>t\geq 0,\ n\geq 2

and

\frac{c_{p}(\psi)-c_{q}(\psi)}{p-q}=\frac{c_{s}(\psi)-c_{t}(\psi)}{s-t}=\frac{c_{t}(\psi)}{t+n}=\frac{1}{\nu_{\psi}(0)}=1.

This means that Theorem
1.2
is sharp.

For better inequalities, we need to add that
\varphi
belongs to the Cegrell class (see
[
4
]
).
Assume that
\Omega
is a bounded hyperconvex domain.
Let
\mathcal{E}_{0}(\Omega)
be the family of bounded negative plurisubharmonic functions
\varphi
in
\Omega
such that
\forall\varepsilon>0
,
\exists\delta>0
with

\overline{\{\varphi<-\epsilon\}}\subset\Omega

and satisfies

\int_{\Omega}(dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}<+\infty.

Denote by
\mathcal{E}(\Omega)
the family of negative plurisubharmonic functions
\varphi
in
\Omega
such that for each open set
\omega\Subset\Omega
, there exists a decreasing sequence
\{\varphi_{j}\}\subset\mathcal{E}_{0}(\Omega)
that converges pointwise to
\varphi
on
\omega
and satisfies

\sup_{j\geq 1}\int_{\Omega}(dd^{c}\varphi_{j})^{n}<+\infty.

In particular, all negative plurisubharmonic functions that are bounded
outside a compact subset of
\Omega
belong to
\mathcal{E}(\Omega)
.
Our third main theorem is as follows, which is a extremal cases for the weighted log canonical thresholds.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded hyperconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
containing the origin
0
. Assume that
\varphi\in\mathcal{E}(\Omega)
such that

\nu_{\varphi|_{l}}(0)=\nu_{\varphi}(0)>0

for all complex line
l
through
0

and

\varphi\geq a\log\|z\|-b\text{ on }\Omega

for some
a,b>0
.
Then,

c_{t}(\varphi)=\frac{t+n}{\nu_{\varphi}(0)},\ \forall t>-n.

When
n=1
and
\varphi
is a subharmonic function in
\Omega
such that
\nu_{\varphi}(0)>0
, there exists a subharmonic function
\psi
in
\Omega
satisfies

\int_{\{0\}}dd^{c}\psi=0

and

\varphi=\psi+\nu_{\varphi}(0)\log\|z\|\ \text{ near }\ 0.

This implies that

c_{t}(\varphi)=c_{t}(\nu_{\varphi}(0)\log\|z\|)=\frac{t+1}{\nu_{\varphi}(0)}.

Thus, conclusion of Theorem
1.3
holds for all subharmonic functions
\varphi
in the cases
n=1
.

When
n>1
,
the problem becomes much more complicated.
Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{E}(\Omega)
and define

e_{j}(\varphi):=\int_{\{0\}}(dd^{c}\varphi)^{j}\wedge(dd^{c}\log\|z\|)^{n-j},\ 0\leq j\leq n.

Observe that

e_{0}(\varphi)=1\text{ and }e_{0}(\varphi)=\nu_{\varphi}(0).

Now, assume that

e_{n}(\varphi)>0
.
A result in
[
2
]
asserts that

(1.2)

c_{0}(\varphi)\geq ne_{n}(\varphi)^{-1/n}.

Later on, Demailly and Hiep
[
6
]
studied a lower bound for the complex singularity exponent. They showed that

c_{0}(\varphi)\geq\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{e_{j}(\varphi)}{e_{j+1}(\varphi)}.

Next, Hiep
[
14
]
stated the following

c_{0}(\varphi)-c_{-1}(\varphi)\geq\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{c_{-1}(\varphi)^{n-1}e_{n}(\varphi)}.

Hong
[
17
]
proved that

c_{t+s}(\varphi)-c_{t}(\varphi)\geq s\frac{(n-1)^{n-1}}{c_{t}(u)^{n-1}e_{n}(\varphi)},\ \forall t\geq 0,\ 0\leq s\leq\frac{c_{t}(\varphi)^{n}e_{n}(\varphi)}{(n-1)^{n}}.

Recently, Hiep
[
15
]
has given the following

c_{1-n}(\varphi)\prod\limits_{j=2}^{k}\left[c_{j-n}(\varphi)-c_{j-1-n}(\varphi)\right]\geq\left[\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}e_{j}(\varphi)\right]^{-1},\ \forall k=1,2,\ldots,n.

Our fourth main theorem is as follows.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
n>1
be an integer number and let
\Omega
be a bounded hyperconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
containing the origin
0
. Assume that
t>-n
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{E}(\Omega)
with
\nu_{\varphi}(0)>0
. Then,
e_{n}(\varphi)\in(0,+\infty)
and

c_{t+s}(\varphi)-c_{t}(\varphi)\geq\frac{s(n-1)^{n-1}\nu_{\varphi}(0)^{n-1}}{(|t|+n)^{n-1}e_{n}(\varphi)}

for all
0<s\leq\left(\frac{n}{n-1}\right)^{n}
.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we give the proof of Theorem
1.1
. In Section 3, we prove Theorem
1.2
. Theorem
1.3
is proved in Section 4. Section 5 is devoted to prove Theorem
1.4
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
containing the origin
0
. Assume that
t>-n
is a real number and
\varphi
is a plurisubharmonic function in
\Omega
.
Then, the following hold.

(i)
a:=c_{t}(\varphi)>0
;

(ii) for every
c\in(0,a)
, there exists
\delta\in(0,t+n)
such that

c_{t-\delta}(\varphi)>c+\delta.

Proof.

(i)
Let
r\in(-n,t)
be a negative real number
and let
\psi_{j}
be a plurisubharmonic function defined by

\psi_{j}(z)=\frac{\varphi(z)}{j}-r\log\|z\|,\ z\in\Omega,\ j\geq 1.

Since
\psi_{j}\to-r\log\|z\|:=\psi
a.e. in
\Omega
as
j\to+\infty
, Theorem 1.1 in
[
13
]
shows that

\liminf_{j\to+\infty}c_{0}(\psi_{j})\geq c_{0}(\psi)=\frac{n}{-r}>1.

Therefore, there exists
j_{0}\geq 1
such that

c_{0}(\psi_{j_{0}})>1

which implies

a\geq c_{r}(\varphi)\geq\frac{1}{j_{0}}>0.

(ii) Let
m>t
be an integer number and define

\psi(z):=c\varphi(z)+(m-t)\log\|z\|,\ z\in\Omega.

Since
0<c<a
, there exists
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
such that
B(0,2\varepsilon)\Subset\Omega
and

\int_{B(0,2\varepsilon)}\|z\|^{2t}e^{-2c\varphi}dV_{2n}<+\infty.

It follows that

\sum_{1\leq k\leq n}\int_{B(0,2\varepsilon)}|z_{k}|^{2m}e^{-2\psi}dV_{2n}<+\infty.

By the strong openness conjecture, we can find
\gamma>0
such that

\sum_{1\leq k\leq n}\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}|z_{k}|^{2m}e^{-2(1+\gamma)\psi}dV_{2n}<+\infty

which implies

\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}\|z\|^{2t-2\gamma(m-t)}e^{-2(1+\gamma)c\varphi}dV_{2n}<+\infty.

Therefore,

c_{t-\delta}(\varphi)>c+\delta,\ \forall 0<\delta\leq\min\{\gamma(m-t),\gamma c\}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
containing the origin
0
. Assume that
t>-n
is a real number and
\varphi
is a plurisubharmonic function in
\Omega
such that

a:=c_{t}(\varphi)<+\infty.

Then,

a=c_{s}\left(\varphi+\frac{s-t}{a}\log\|z\|\right),\ \forall s\geq t.

Proof.

Lemma
2.1
states that
a>0
, and hence,
a\in(0,+\infty)
.
Define

\psi(z):=\varphi(z)+\frac{s-t}{a}\log\|z\|,\ z\in\Omega.

Observe that for every real number
x
,

(2.1)

\|z\|^{2s}e^{-2x\psi}=\|z\|^{2t+\frac{2(a-x)(s-t)}{a}}e^{-2x\varphi}\text{ on }\Omega\backslash\{0\}.

Let
c
be a real number such that
0<c<a
.
Since

0\leq\|z\|^{\frac{2(a-c)(s-t)}{a}}\leq 1,\ \forall\|z\|\leq 1,

we reduce by (
2.1
) that
c_{s}(\psi)\geq c
, and therefore,

c_{s}(\psi)\geq a.

Now, assume that
c_{s}(\psi)>a
. Lemma
2.1
states that there exists
\delta\in(0,s+n)
such that

c_{s-\delta}(\psi)>a+\delta.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
be such that
B(0,2\varepsilon)\Subset\Omega
and

\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}\|z\|^{2s-2\delta}e^{-2(a+\delta)\psi}dV_{2n}<+\infty.

Since
s>t
, we infer by (
2.1
) that

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}\|z\|^{2t}e^{-2(a+\delta)\varphi}dV_{2n}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}\|z\|^{2s+\frac{2\delta(s-t)}{a}}e^{-2(a+\delta)\psi}dV_{2n}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}\|z\|^{2s-2\delta}e^{-2(a+\delta)\psi}dV_{2n}<+\infty.

It follows that

a=c_{t}(\varphi)\geq a+\delta>a.

This is impossible. Thus,
a=c_{s}(\psi)
. This proves the proposition.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Define
a:=\nu_{\varphi}(0)
and
b:=c_{t}(\varphi)
.
Firstly, we claim that

(2.2)

a=0\Rightarrow b=+\infty.

Let
p,q>1
be such that
tp>-n
and

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1.

Assume that
c>0
.
A well-known inequality due to Skoda
[
22
]
tells us that

c_{0}(\varphi)=+\infty.

Hence, there exists
\varepsilon>0
be such that
B(0,\varepsilon)\Subset\Omega
and

\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}e^{-2cq\varphi}dV_{2n}<+\infty.

Using the Hölder inequality,

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}\|z\|^{2t}e^{-2c\varphi}dV_{2n}\leq\left(\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}\|z\|^{2pt}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}e^{-2cq\varphi}dV_{2n}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}<+\infty

which implies
c_{t}(\varphi)\geq c
, and therefore,

b=+\infty
. This proves the claim.

Next, we claim that

(2.3)

a>0\Rightarrow b\leq\frac{t+n}{a}.

Let
c
be a real number such that

c>\frac{t+n}{a}.

Choose
\varepsilon>0
such that
B(0,\varepsilon)\Subset\Omega
and

(2.4)

\frac{1}{\sigma_{2n-1}}\int_{\{\|z\|=1\}}\varphi(rz)d\sigma(z)\leq\frac{t+n}{c}\log r,\ \forall r\in(0,\varepsilon),

where
d\sigma
is the area measure on the unit sphere on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
\sigma_{2n-1}
is its area.
Using Jensen’s convexity inequality, we deduce by (
2.4
) that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma_{2n-1}}\int_{\{\|z\|=1\}}\|rz\|^{2t}e^{-2c\varphi(rz)}d\sigma(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{2t}}{\sigma_{2n-1}}\int_{\{\|z\|=1\}}e^{-2c\varphi(rz)}d\sigma(z)

\displaystyle\geq r^{2t}e^{\frac{-2c}{\sigma_{2n-1}}\int_{\{\|z\|=1\}}\varphi(rz)d\sigma(z)}\geq r^{-2n}

for all
0<r<\varepsilon
. It follows that

\int_{\{\|z\|<\delta\}}\|z\|^{2t}e^{-2c\varphi}dV_{2n}\geq\sigma_{n-1}\int_{0}^{\delta}r^{-1}dr=+\infty,\ \forall\delta\in(0,\varepsilon),

which implies

b=c_{t}(\varphi)\leq\frac{t+n}{a}.

This proves the claim, and hence, we deduce by (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) that

a\in(0,+\infty)\Leftrightarrow b\in(0,+\infty).

On the other hand, Lemma
2.2
states that

a=c_{|t|}\left(\varphi+\frac{|t|-t}{a}\log\|z\|\right).

Hence, we deduce by
[
22
]
that

\frac{1}{\nu_{\varphi+\frac{|t|-t}{c_{t}(\varphi)}\log\|z\|}(0)}\leq c_{0}\left(\varphi+\frac{|t|-t}{c_{t}(\varphi)}\log\|z\|\right)\leq a.

Combining this with (
2.3
) we conclude that

\frac{1}{\nu_{\varphi+\frac{|t|-t}{c_{t}(\varphi)}\log\|z\|}(0)}\leq a\leq\frac{t+n}{\nu_{\varphi}(0)}.

The proof is complete.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
containing the origin
0
and let
\varphi
be a plurisubharmonic function in
\Omega
such that

\nu_{\varphi}(0)>0.

Assume that
s>t>-n
are real numbers such that

a:=c_{t}(\varphi)<c_{s}(\varphi).

Then,
for every
a<\gamma<c_{s}(\varphi)
, the function

\varphi_{\gamma}:=\max\{\varphi,\frac{s-t}{\gamma-a}\log\|z\|\}

satisfies

c_{t}(\varphi_{\gamma})=a.

Proof.

Theorem
1.1
tells us that

a\in(0,+\infty).

Let
r\in(0,1)
be such that
B(0,r)\Subset\Omega
and

(3.1)

\int_{B(0,r)}\|z\|^{2s}e^{-2\gamma\varphi}dV_{2n}<+\infty.

For
\varepsilon\in(0,r)
, we have

(3.2)

\begin{split}\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}\|z\|^{2t}(e^{-2a\varphi}-e^{-2a\varphi_{\gamma}})dV_{2n}&\leq\int_{{B(0,\varepsilon)}\cap\{\varphi<\frac{s-t}{\gamma-a}\log\|z\|\}}\|z\|^{2t}e^{-2a\varphi}dV_{2n}\\
&=\int_{{B(0,\varepsilon)}\cap\{(\gamma-a)\varphi<(s-t)\log\|z\|\}}\|z\|^{2t}e^{2(\gamma-a)\varphi-2\gamma\varphi}dV_{2n}\\
&\leq\int_{{B(0,\varepsilon)}}\|z\|^{2t}e^{2(s-t)\log\|z\|-2\gamma\varphi}dV_{2n}\\
&\leq\int_{{B(0,r)}}\|z\|^{2s}e^{-2\gamma\varphi}dV_{2n}<+\infty,\end{split}

where the last inequality follows from inequality (
3.1
).
Since
\|z\|^{2t}e^{-2a\varphi}
is not integrable near
0
,
it follows from (
3.2
) that
\|z\|^{2t}e^{-2a\varphi_{\gamma}}
is not integrable near
0
,
which implies

c_{t}({\varphi}_{\gamma})\leq a.

As
\varphi\leq{\varphi}_{\gamma}
,
it follows that

a=c_{t}({\varphi})\leq c_{t}({\varphi}_{\gamma})\leq a.

Hence, we conclude that
c_{t}({\varphi}_{\gamma})=a
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
containing the origin
0
and let
\varphi
be a plurisubharmonic function in
\Omega
.
Assume that
s>t>-n
be real numbers. Then,

c_{s}(\max\{\varphi,a\log\|z\|\})\geq c_{t}(\varphi)+\frac{s-t}{a}

holds for any
a>0
.

Proof.

When
b:=c_{t}(\varphi)=+\infty
, the statement is obvious. Now, assume that
b\in(0,+\infty)
.
We define

x:=1+\frac{ab}{s-t}.

Let
\varepsilon\in(1-\frac{1}{x},1)
. Since
\varepsilon b<b=c_{t}(\varphi)
, there is
r\in(0,1)
such that
B(0,r)\Subset\Omega
and

(3.3)

\int_{B(0,r)}\|z\|^{2t}e^{-2\varepsilon b\varphi}dV_{2n}<+\infty.

By Hölder inequality,

\|z\|^{2\varepsilon(s-t)}e^{2\varepsilon b\varphi}\leq\frac{1}{x}\|z\|^{2\varepsilon(s-t)x}+\frac{x-1}{x}e^{2\varepsilon\frac{bx}{x-1}\varphi},

which implies

(3.4)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{e^{2\varepsilon\frac{bx}{x-1}\varphi}+\|z\|^{2\varepsilon(s-t)x}}&\leq\frac{1}{\frac{x-1}{x}e^{2\varepsilon\frac{bx}{x-1}\varphi}+\frac{1}{x}\|z\|^{2\varepsilon(s-t)x}}\\
&\leq e^{-2\varepsilon b\varphi}\|z\|^{-2\varepsilon(s-t)}.\end{split}

Note that for any
\varepsilon
,
a
and
b
, there exists positive constants
A,B
such that

(3.5)

\begin{split}e^{2\varepsilon\frac{bx}{x-1}\max\{\varphi,a\log\|z\|\}}&\geq A\left(e^{2\varphi}+\|z\|^{2a}\right)^{\varepsilon\frac{bx}{x-1}}\\
&\geq B\left(e^{2\varepsilon\frac{bx}{x-1}\varphi}+\|z\|^{2(s-t)\varepsilon x}\right).\end{split}

Then, for
0<\|z\|<1
,

\displaystyle\|z\|^{-2(s-t)}e^{-2\varepsilon b\varphi}

\displaystyle\geq\|z\|^{-2\varepsilon(s-t)}e^{-2\varepsilon b\varphi}

\displaystyle\geq B^{-1}e^{-2\varepsilon\frac{bx}{x-1}\max\{\varphi,a\log\|z\|\}},

where the last inequality follows from inequalities (
3.4
) and (
3.5
). Hence, we conclude by the inequality (
3.3
) that

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,r)}\|z\|^{2s}e^{-2\varepsilon\frac{bx}{x-1}\max\{\varphi,a\log\|z\|\}}dV_{2n}\leq B\int_{B(0,r)}\|z\|^{2t}e^{-2\varepsilon b\varphi}dV_{2n}<+\infty,

and therefore,

c_{s}(\max\{\varphi,a\log\|z\|\})\geq\frac{bx}{x-1}=c_{t}(\varphi)+\frac{s-t}{a}.

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded hyperconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
containing the origin
0
and let
\varphi\in\mathcal{E}(\Omega)
be such that

\nu_{\varphi}(0)>0.

Assume that
t>-n
are real numbers and define

\varphi_{t}(z):=\varphi(z)+\frac{|t|-t}{a}\log\|z\|,\ z\in\Omega.

Then,

e_{n}(\varphi_{t}):=\int_{\{0\}}(dd^{c}\varphi_{t})^{n}>0

and

c_{t}(\varphi)<c_{s}(\varphi),\ \forall s>t.

Proof.

Lemma 4.4 in
[
1
]
tells us that

\displaystyle 0<\nu_{\varphi}(0)\leq\left(\int_{\{0\}}(dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\left(\int_{\{0\}}(dd^{c}\log\|z\|)^{n}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}},

which implies

e_{n}(\varphi):=\int_{\{0\}}(dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}>0.

Define

a:=c_{t}(\varphi)\ \text{ and }\ b:=c_{s}(\varphi).

As
\varphi\in\mathcal{E}(\Omega)
, it follows that

\varphi_{t}\in\mathcal{E}(\Omega\cap B(0,1))
and
satisfies

0<e_{n}(\varphi)\leq e_{n}(\varphi_{t}):=\int_{\{0\}}(dd^{c}\varphi_{t})^{n}.

Lemma
2.2
tells us that
0<a\leq b<+\infty
and

(3.6)

a=c_{|t|}(\varphi_{t})\ \text{ and }\ b=c_{s+\frac{b(|t|-t)}{a}}(\varphi_{t}).

Theorem 1.1 in
[
17
]
states that

Proposition 3.4
.

\mathbb{C}^{n}
containing the origin

\varphi<0
in
\Omega

\nu_{\varphi}(0)=0

1.1

Define

\varphi\in\mathcal{E}(\Omega)

3.1
and

3.3

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=c_{t}(\varphi_{\gamma})\geq c_{t}\left(\frac{s-t}{\gamma-a}\log\|z\|\right)

(3.7)

a=c_{m}(\psi)\ \text{ and }\ b=c_{s+\frac{b(m-t)}{a}}(\psi).

[
20
]
,

(3.8)

c_{m}(\psi)\leq c_{m}(\psi_{j})\leq c_{m}(\psi)+c_{m}(j\log\|z\|)=a+\frac{m+n}{j}.

Since

\nu_{\psi}(0)\geq\nu_{\varphi}(0)>0,

it follows by (

\lim_{j\to+\infty}c_{m}(\psi_{j})=c_{m}(\psi)\leq\frac{m+n}{\nu_{\psi}(0)}<+\infty,

\nu_{\psi_{j}}(0)>0,\ \forall j\geq j_{0}.

\displaystyle c_{s+\frac{b(m-t)}{a}}(\psi)\leq c_{s+\frac{b(m-t)}{a}}(\psi_{j})\leq\frac{(s+\frac{b(m-t)}{a}+n)c_{m}(\psi_{j})}{m+n}

Therefore,

This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Comparative analysis of the safety and effectiveness of robotic natural orifice specimen extraction versus laparoscopic surgery for colorectal tumors through systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Wei-lin Wang, Shuai Li, Xiao-jun Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The purpose of this study and meta-analysis was to evaluate the perioperative and oncologic results of robotic NOSE versus laparoscopic surgery for colorectal tumors. We plan to perform an extensive electronic search on PubMed, CNKI, Embase, and the Cochrane Library to find research articles published from the beginning of the databases until July 2024 that examine the comparison between robotic natural orifice specimen extraction and laparoscopic surgery in patients with colorectal cancer. Both English and Chinese literature will be included. Literature screening will strictly follow predetermined criteria for inclusion and exclusion, specifically targeting randomized controlled trials and cohort studies. The evaluation of quality will be conducted with the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale (NOS). Review Manager 5.4.1 will be utilized to perform a meta-analysis of data gathered from the studies that are included. The ultimate evaluation included seven past cohort studies with a total of 1117 participants (545 who had robotic NOSE and 572 who had laparoscopic surgery). Patients who had robotic NOSE experienced notable enhancements in LOHS, time to first flatus, time to start the liquid diet, EBL, and postoperative ileus when compared to patients undergoing laparoscopic colorectal surgery. There were no notable discrepancies noted in terms of surgical duration, total complications, lymph node collection, and anastomotic leakage between the two methods. In conclusion, the use of robotic technology for extracting specimens through natural body openings in colorectal surgery is considered to be safe and achievable. It offers notable advantages over laparoscopic surgery, including reduced hospital stay, earlier time to first flatus and liquid intake, decreased EBL, and lower incidence of postoperative ileus.

Introduction

Colorectal cancer currently ranks third among all malignant tumors, accounting for approximately 8% of cancer-related deaths, with rectal cancer comprising about 38% of colorectal cancer cases [1, 2]. According to guidelines from organizations like NCCN, surgical approaches remain pivotal for managing resectable colorectal cancer, particularly with total mesorectal excision (TME) now established as the standard oncologic procedure [3]. Treatment options for rectal cancer have expanded to include minimally invasive procedures like laparotomy, laparoscopic surgery, transanal endoscopic microsurgery, and robotic-assisted surgery [4]. The advent of laparoscopic colon cancer resection in 1991 by Jacobs [5] et al. marked a rapid evolution toward minimally invasive techniques in colorectal surgery. In 2006, Pigazzi [6] reported the pioneering use of robotic anterior resection for colorectal cancer. Robotic systems enhance surgical precision and provide superior visual and ergonomic advantages, facilitating meticulous dissection of delicate structures [7, 8]. Prior studies have shown that robotic procedures for rectal cancer result in better surgical outcomes, such as improved resection margins, fewer conversions to open surgery, decreased postoperative complications, quicker recovery times, and enhanced urinary and sexual function when compared to traditional laparoscopic surgery [9, 10].

However, the extraction of surgical specimens remains a critical step in both laparoscopic and robotic procedures. Traditionally, specimen extraction involves enlarging trocar site incisions or creating new incisions, which increases risks such as pain, wound infection, and herniation. The idea of natural orifice specimen extraction surgery (NOSES) became popular after Franklin [11] et al. initially described cases of laparoscopic removal of sigmoid colon cancer with transanal specimen extraction in 1993.NOSES techniques aim to minimize or eliminate wound-related complications, such as infections and incisional hernias, while reducing pain and abdominal adhesions [12]. Ding [13] et al. compared 43 cases of transanal specimen extraction for radical colorectal cancer resection with 43 cases of conventional laparoscopic radical resection, finding that the NOSES group experienced less intraoperative bleeding, lower postoperative pain scores, earlier recovery times for bowel function, higher cosmetic satisfaction, and comparable rates of tumor recurrence and disease-free survival.

However, research on robotic NOSES for colorectal neoplasia remains limited. Hence, this systematic review and meta-analysis aims to evaluate the perioperative and oncologic results of robotic NOSE versus laparoscopic surgery for colorectal neoplasia.

Literature search

The research follows the protocols of systematic review and meta-analysis and has been recorded in the PROSPERO database(CRD42024570119). Two researchers (WWL and LS) independently conducted literature screening and data extraction to identify studies meeting the inclusion criteria. Systematic electronic searches were conducted in PubMed, CNKI, Embase, and the Cochrane Library from the beginning to July 2024, covering literature in both English and Chinese. Search terms included “colorectal tumor,” “robotic-assisted,” “laparoscopy,” and “natural orifice specimen extraction,” applying database-specific search rules along with subject headings and free-text terms. Furthermore, manual searches of relevant articles and citation tracking were conducted, and additional studies meeting eligibility criteria were selected for detailed analysis.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria encompassed adults diagnosed with colorectal tumors undergoing surgery. The study examined the use of robotic NOSES in comparison to laparoscopic surgery as the standard procedure. The main objectives centered on the duration of hospitalization, the time until first passing gas, the time until the first intake of liquids, and the duration of surgery. Secondary outcomes examined included EBL, incidence of ileus, anastomotic leakage rate, lymph node retrieval, and overall complications. Eligible study designs included randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and cohort studies. Studies without primary data, single-arm trials, animal experiments, conference abstracts, and case studies were excluded based on specific criteria.

Conducting analysis and extracting information

Two researchers autonomously reviewed articles according to predetermined criteria for inclusion and exclusion. Initially, they assessed eligibility using article titles and abstracts. Subsequently, both researchers independently reviewed the full texts of potentially eligible articles. In cases of disagreement regarding inclusion, the researchers discussed the discrepancies or consulted a third researcher to achieve consensus. In the end, specific articles were gathered, containing information like the initial writer, year of publication, number of participants, characteristics of patients (age, sex), and main and additional measures of outcomes.

Statistical analysis

Review Manager 5.4.1 software will be used for statistical analysis, with continuous variables presented as weighted mean differences (WMD) and binary variables as odds ratios (OR), each with 95% confidence intervals (CI). If studies only provide median, quartiles, or extreme values, we will transform them into mean and standard deviation (SD) following the procedures described by Wan [14] et al. The Cochran-Mantel–Haenszel method will be used to test dichotomous variables, and the inverse variance method will be utilized for analyzing continuous variables. Given the anticipated heterogeneity among included studies, we will employ a random-effects model for all analyses. Heterogeneity will be evaluated by the I2 statistic, with values above 50% suggesting notable heterogeneity. A p value below 0.05 will determine statistical significance. The evaluation of the studies will follow the Newcastle–Ottawa Quality Assessment Scale (NOS), focusing on the selection of study groups, comparability, and assessment of outcomes. The scoring system ranges from 0 to 9 points, where scores of 0–5 represent low quality and scores of 6–9 represent high quality. Sensitivity analyses will be conducted via one-by-one exclusion to evaluate result robustness and identify potential sources of instability. Due to the small number of studies (≤ 10), publication bias analysis will not be performed, as statistical power may be insufficient for meaningful interpretation [15, 16].

Primary-outcome measures

Based on the meta-analysis findings of the studies analyzed, robotic NOSE exhibited better performance in comparison to laparoscopic surgery in various key aspects, including reduced length of hospital stay (WMD -0.61, 95% CI − 1.02 to − 0.20; p = 0.003), quicker time to first flatus (WMD -0.42, 95% CI -0.60 to -0.25; p < 0.00001), and faster time to first liquid diet (WMD − 0.71, 95% CI − 1.00 to − 0.42; p < 0.00001). Discussion

Our meta-analysis found that robotic NOSES has clear benefits compared to laparoscopic surgery, including shorter hospital stays, quicker recovery of bowel function such as first flatus and liquid-diet intake, and lower rates of postoperative ileus. These findings align with a 2021 retrospective comparative study highlighting the superior recovery of gastrointestinal function with robot-assisted surgery [24]. Robotic NOSES procedures eliminate the need for intra-abdominal specimen extraction, thereby reducing surgical trauma and postoperative pain. Early mobilization facilitated by this approach promotes quicker bowel function recovery, enhancing patient outcomes. The high-definition three-dimensional vision of robotic systems aids surgeons in precise tissue identification, protecting critical structures, and supporting pelvic floor and abdominal muscle contraction. The accuracy of this technique decreases irritation in nearby intestines and lessens harm to the far end of the colon, which helps with the recovery of the digestive system. Although our study did not show a notable variation in complications between robotic NOSES and laparoscopic surgery, NOSES methods prevent direct contact with tumors and use clean, tumor-free practices when placing the anvil holder through inverted bowel tubes, potentially reducing the chances of issues such as postoperative intestinal blockages caused by adhesions [25]. These findings highlight the importance of conducting more rigorous studies to confirm the benefits mentioned.

Our meta-analysis found that robotic NOSES did not show a significant variance in OT when compared to laparoscopic surgery. However, Yuan [18] et al. In the study from 2022, the average duration of surgery was 257.19 ± 48.07 min for the robotic group and 181.95 ± 32.43 min for the laparoscopic group, showing that laparoscopy had notably quicker OT. The difference in time may be due to the complex process of placing and securing the anvil in the proximal bowel during robotic NOSES, requiring skilled surgeons and coordinated teamwork. Robotic surgery also involves loading and unloading robotic arms and reconstructing the digestive tract, which adds to the OT. In contrast, conventional laparoscopic procedures include steps like removing the specimen and closing the wound, impacting the total surgical duration. OT is influenced by various factors including surgeon experience, equipment, and patient condition. The research highlights the importance of the learning curve in determining the duration of NOSES procedures, indicating that as the surgical team becomes more experienced, the time taken for procedures is expected to reduce, maximizing the advantages of robotic NOSES surgeries [26, 27].

The meta-analysis found that robotic extraction of specimens through natural orifices resulted in a significant decrease in EBL when compared to laparoscopic procedures. Ding [13] et al. Examining 43 instances of transanal radical excision of colorectal cancer against 43 instances of traditional laparoscopic radical excision revealed that the NOSES team experienced lower levels of blood loss during surgery (59.31 ± 14.64 mL) in contrast to (75.41 ± 18.16 mL) in the laparoscopic team, demonstrating a notable distinction (p < 0.001). Both groups exhibited similar rates of tumor recurrence and disease-free survival. Robotic systems utilize enhanced visual capabilities, including naked-eye views, magnified images, and physiologic-tremor filtering, which refines the anatomic visualization of mesenteric vessels and reduces bleeding caused by inadvertent tissue traction during surgery. In contrast, bleeding in laparoscopic procedures primarily results from accidental injury to vascular tissues during mobilization, contributing to the lower blood loss observed in robot-assisted surgeries.

Our meta-analysis showed that there was no significant difference in the number of dissected lymph nodes between robotic NOSES and laparoscopic surgery, consistent with the findings of the ROLARR trial [28]. The ROLARR trial corroborated that robotic surgery achieves the comparable quality of TME as laparoscopic surgery. Crucially, every study analyzed in our research met or surpassed the minimum requirement of retrieving a minimum of 12 lymph nodes established by the College of American Pathologists. Advanced robotic surgical methods utilize enhanced capabilities like a magnified 3D high-definition view and adaptable robotic arms, allowing for accurate and careful removal of lymph nodes in the restricted pelvic area.

Limitations

This meta-analysis is the initial extensive evaluation of perioperative and oncologic results when comparing robotic NOSES to laparoscopic surgery for colorectal tumor removal. However, several significant limitations must be acknowledged. The moderate to high level of heterogeneity in the overall study quality can be attributed to the limited number of case–control studies included and the lack of RCTs. Due to these limitations, subgroup analyses for highly heterogeneous outcomes were not feasible, and comparisons of long-term tumor outcomes were not conducted. Hence, upcoming studies should prioritize carrying out rigorous RCTs to confirm and expand upon these preliminary results.

Conclusions

After analyzing seven cohort studies, it was determined that robotic NOSES is a viable and secure method for patients undergoing colorectal surgery. Robotic NOSES showed notable benefits over laparoscopic surgery in terms of hospital stay duration, time to first flatus, time to first liquid diet, EBL, and postoperative ileus occurrence. Further research is needed to confirm the added advantages of natural orifice specimen extraction in robotic-assisted colorectal surgery, including its lasting impact.

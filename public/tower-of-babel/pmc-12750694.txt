The diagnostic and prognostic value of circulating miR-193b-3p on severe pneumonia
Authors: Kunbin Liu, Ping Wang, Yuan Li, Li Liu, Hongbo Wu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSevere pneumonia (SP) poses a serious threat to patients’ lives, and this study aimed to explore the diagnostic and prognostic value of miR-193b-3p on SP, thereby providing novel insights for the clinical management of SP.MethodsThe miR-193b-3p expression was evaluated by PCR. The diagnostic value of miR-193b-3p on SP was assessed by ROC. The association between miR-193b-3p expression and the SP severity was assessed by the correlation analysis. The correlation between miR-193b-3p expression and the survival status of SP patients was evaluated by the Kaplan-Meier survival analysis. Potential independent prognostic factors for SP were predicted by multivariate COX regression analysis. Bioinformatics analysis assessed the possible pathways regulated by miR-193b-3p.ResultsUpregulation of miR-193b-3p in SP patients showed a diagnostic value in SP. Background

Severe pneumonia (SP) is a prevalent acute respiratory infectious disease with a complex etiology due to the infection of a variety of microorganisms in the clinic [1]. It is often complicated with diseases such as sepsis, respiratory failure, septic shock, and multiple organ dysfunction syndrome [2]. Studies have shown that the incidence of SP is 17%-35%, and the proportion of SP patients older than 85 years requiring ICU admission is 3.3–5.8 times higher than that of younger patients [3, 4]. The discovery of antibiotics in the 1940 s led to a significant overall decline in mortality from pneumonia, but the overall mortality rate of pneumonia hasn’t stopped falling and has even increased due to the resistance of antibiotics since the 21 st century [5]. Since then, antibiotic resistance has become a major health problem among the global public, especially in SP. Currently, the mortality rate for SP is 17% to 50%, which remains at a higher level, posing a serious threat to human health and increasing the burden on the global economy [6]. The inflammatory response is complicated and hard to control during the progression of SP, and effective biomarkers may predict the development of SP, thereby providing patients with timely, targeted, and efficacious therapeutic regimens. Combined with clinical conditions, appropriate and early intervention for SP patients who may develop a poor prognosis will improve the survival rate of SP. Consequently, safe, potent, and accurate biomarkers for the prediction of SP onset and its prognosis are essential.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are a class of endogenous, non-coding single-stranded RNA molecules found in eukaryotes and can synthesize mature miRNAs with the participation of the Dicer enzyme [7]. The intricate interaction between miRNAs and their target genes constitutes sophisticated regulatory networks that orchestrate fundamental cellular processes, including growth, development, proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis, throughout the cell’s lifecycle [8, 9]. Numerous studies have demonstrated that miRNAs play pivotal roles in modulating inflammatory responses through complex regulatory mechanisms. For example, in RAW264.7 macrophages, miR-185-5p exhibited significant inhibitory effects, with its overexpression effectively suppressing the production of proinflammatory cytokines [10]. MiR-199a-3p was confirmed to alleviate the inflammatory response induced by ox-LDL in macrophages via inhibition of RUNX1 expression [11]. Additionally, plenty of miRNAs, such as miR-10a-3p, miR-181b, and miR-127-5p, have been extensively studied for their diagnostic and prognostic potential in assessing inflammatory progression within SP [12–14].

In the research on bioinformatics analysis of the miRNAs associated with SP, miR-193b-3p was listed to be upregulated in SP [15]. MiR-193b-3p expression is also critically linked to inflammatory progression, with emerging evidence demonstrating its regulatory role in Th17 cell differentiation, a pivotal pathway mediating inflammatory responses [16]. Given that pneumonia severity is closely associated with the magnitude of local pulmonary inflammation, the extent of inflammatory dissemination, and the intensity of systemic inflammatory response, we hypothesized that miR-193b-3p may serve as a critical biomarker for SP diagnosis and prognosis. This study investigated miR-193b-3p expression patterns in SP patients and systematically evaluated its correlation with disease outcomes.

Subjects and methods

Clinical subjects

A total of 119 patients who were diagnosed with SP at Affiliated Hospital of Panzhihua University from January 2021 to December 2023 participated in this study and served as the severe pneumonia group (SP group). Healthy individuals (n = 94) who underwent regular physical examinations at Affiliated Hospital of Panzhihua University during the same time were selected as the healthy control group (HC group). This study was approved by the Ethics Review Committee of Affiliated Hospital of Panzhihua University and adheres to the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki. All the subjects or their families signed the informed consent after the purpose of this research was notified. The diagnostic criteria for SP were listed as follows [17]:

Main criteria: (1) septic shock requiring vasopressors; (2) respiratory failure requiring mechanical ventilation.

Minor criteria: (1) respiratory rate ≥ 30 breaths/min; (2) PaO2 ≤ 250 mmHg; (3) multi-lobar infiltrates; (4) confusion or disorientation; (5) uremia (blood urea nitrogen level) ≥ 7.14 mmol/L; (6) SBP (systolic blood pressure) < 90 mmHg, requiring active fluid resuscitation.

Inclusion criteria for subjects were: (1) the age of all patients should be over 18 years and under 75 years; (2) patients fulfilled at least one major diagnostic criterion or at least three minor criteria of SP; (3) patients had not accepted any therapies before hospitalization; (4) patients’ clinical data were complete.

The exclusion criteria for subjects were (1) patients with any immune deficiency diseases; (2) patients with acute cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases; (3) patients with any other infectious diseases; (4) patients with any malignant tumor; (5) patients with intemperance and drug abuse.

Collection of clinical general data and processing of the blood sample

General information, such as age, gender, and body mass index (BMI), was collected from all subjects. The clinical data, including the levels of white blood cell count (WBC), neutrophil count (NEU), lymphocyte count (LYM), C-reactive protein (CRP), procalcitonin (PCT), brain natriuretic peptide (BNP), and D-dimer (D-D), were also recorded. The fasting blood samples were taken from the subjects the next morning after hospitalization and then centrifuged at 3700 rpm/min for 20 min to collect the plasma. The plasma was stored at -80 °C for later analyses. The acute physiology and chronic health evaluation scoring system II (APACHE-II) and clinical pulmonary infection scores (CPIS) of all SP subjects were evaluated after hospitalization.

Analysis of the expression level of miR-193b-3p

The total RNA of the blood samples was extracted by the TRIzol reagent (cat no: T3809, Sigma, USA), followed by purity and concentration analysis with NanoDrop-2000 spectrophotometry (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). RNA integrity was verified by assessing 260/280 nm (1.8–2.0.8.0). Then the extracted RNA was transcribed into cDNA by using the TaqMan MicroRNA reverse transcription kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). The quantification of miR-193b-3p was analyzed on the 7300 RT-PCR system (Applied Biosystems, USA) using the SYBR Green Master Mix kit (cat no: 4309155, Invitrogen, USA) and validated primers (miR-193b-3p forward: 5’-CTTCAGTGATCCCGGTTCTCCAA-3’, reverse: 5’-CAATCCAATACTGTCCCTTAAACCA-3’; U6 forward: 5’-TGCGGGTGCTCGCTTCGGCAGC-3’, reverse: 5’-CCAGTGCAG GGTCCGAGGT-3’). The expression level of miR-193b-3p was calculated by the Eq. 2−ΔΔCt, and U6 was selected as the internal reference. The optimized thermal cycling conditions were as follows: 95 °C for 10 min, followed by 40 cycles of 95 °C for 15 s and at 60 °C for 1 min.

The survival analysis of subjects with SP

SP subjects were followed up for 28 days to perform a survival analysis. The survival status and survival time were recorded to assess the association between miR-193b-3p expression and the survival rate of SP subjects. Potential independent prognostic factors for SP were analyzed by the multivariate COX regression analysis.

Bioinformatics analysis on the potential mechanism of miR-193b-3p

The potential downstream targets of miR-193b-3p were predicted using miRDB (https://mirdb.org/) and Starbase (https://rnasysu.com/encori/index.php) online databases, with overlapping targets visualized via Venn diagram analysis. Subsequently, Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment was performed for miR-193b-3p targets using the DAVID Functional Annotation Tool (https://david.ncifcrf.gov/home.jsp).

Statistical analysis

Data analysis and diagram creation were carried out using SPSS and GraphPad Prism. All data were represented as the mean value ± SD. The experimental data were technically replicated in triplicate to ensure reproducibility and statistical robustness. The chi-square test and t-test were utilized to assess the difference between the data groups. The predictive value of miR-193b-3p in SP was evaluated by the receiver operator characteristic (ROC) curve. The association between miR-193b-3p expression and SP severity was assessed by Pearson correlation analysis. The relation between the miR-193b-3p expression and the prognosis of SP subjects was evaluated by the Kaplan-Meier curve with log-rank test. The multivariate COX regression analysis assessed the variables with clinical relevance with SP to identify the independent prognostic factors for SP poor prognosis. Results were regarded as significant differences if P < 0.05.

Comparison of general information

As shown in Table 1, the baseline characteristics demonstrated no significant differences in age (p = 0.795), gender distribution (p = 0.651), or BMI (p = 0.507) between the SP and HC groups. However, comprehensive hematological analysis revealed marked inflammatory alterations in SP patients, with significantly elevated levels of WBC, NEU, CRP, PCT, BNP, and D-D (all p < 0.001), accompanied by a substantial reduction in LYM (p < 0.001). These findings collectively indicated the presence of a robust systemic inflammatory response and potential immune dysfunction characteristic of severe pulmonary infection.

Difference in miR-193b-3p expression between the SP and HC groups and the ROC analysis

Comparative analysis revealed a substantial upregulation of miR-193b-3p expression in SP subjects compared to the HC group, demonstrating statistically significant differences (p < 0.001; Fig. 1A). ROC curve analysis further validated the diagnostic efficacy of miR-193b-3p, yielding an AUC of 0.843 (95% CI: 0.791–0.896) with diagnostic thresholds demonstrating 67.23% sensitivity and 87.23% specificity (Fig. 1B). These findings collectively established miR-193b-3p as a promising molecular marker with discriminative capacity for identifying SP cases in clinical settings.

Correlation between miR-193b-3p expression and severity indicators of SP

A positive correlation between miR-193b-3p expression and the levels of WBC (r = 0.764, Fig. 2, A), NEU (r = 0.663, Fig. 2, B), CRP (r = 0.809, Fig. 2, C), PCT (r = 0.700, Fig. 2, D), BNP (r = 0.483, Fig. 2, E), and D-D (r = 0.461, Fig. 2, F) was observed. While miR-193b-3p expression was negatively correlated with the level of LYM, with a correlation coefficient of r = −0.664 (Fig. 2, G). The correlation analysis demonstrated a close association between the miR-193b-3p expression levels and the progression of SP.

The miR-193b-3p expression in SP was correlated with the levels of WBC (A), NEU (B), CRP (C), PCT (D), BNP (E), D-D (F), and LYM (G)

Subjects were further divided into a high miR-193b-3p expression group (n = 60) and a low miR-193b-3p expression group (n = 59) based on the average miR-193b-3p expression levels in the SP group due to the normal distribution. The high miR-193b-3p expression group exhibited greater SP severity, with 42 SP subjects (70.00%) showing APACHE-II scores ≥ 15 and 43 cases (71.67%) presenting CPIS > 6, both proportions being significantly higher than those observed in the low miR-193b-3p expression cohort (Table 2). This comparative analysis demonstrated the close association between elevated miR-193b-3p levels and more severe clinical manifestations among SP subjects.

The association between miR-193b-3p expression and the prognosis of SP

Higher survival rate of the SP subjects with a low miR-193b-3p expression level was observed compared with SP subjects with a high miR-193b-3p expression level (p = 0.018, Fig. 3A), indicating the close association between miR-193b-3p expression and the SP prognosis. Multivariate COX regression (Table 3) indicated that miR-193b-3p showed a significant predictive value for the prognosis of SP subjects, with an HR value of 6.503 (p = 0.013, 95% CI = 1.473–28.703), demonstrating that upregulation of miR-193b-3p was associated with a poor prognosis of SP. Additionally, elevated CRP (HR: 2.755, p = 0.047, 95% CI: 1.013–7.479), PCT (HR: 2.466, p = 0.048, 95% CI: 1.010–6.024), APACHE-II (HR: 2.388, p = 0.041, 95% CI: 1.038–5.493), and CPIS (HR: 2.173, p = 0.044, 95% CI: 1.022–4.620) could also served as the independent prognostic factors for the poor prognosis of SP subjects.

The potential mechanism of miR-193b-3p in SP

Bioinformatics analysis of 162 overlapping target genes (Fig. 3B) revealed that miR-193b-3p regulated multiple inflammation-associated pathways, including PI3K-Akt signaling, MAPK cascades, FoxO signaling, TRP channel pathways, and Wnt signaling (Supplementary Table 1).

Discussion

Conventional biomarkers like CRP and PCT demonstrate high sensitivity for SP diagnosis, their clinical utility is still limited by inherent challenges: CRP lacks specificity as it elevates in all inflammatory conditions, and PCT fails to differentiate SP from sepsis or any other infections in early stages with a temporal lag [18, 19]. In contrast, emerging evidence highlights miRNAs as promising alternatives due to their exceptional stability in bodily fluids and resistance to degradation, enabling non-invasive detection [20]. Notably, miR-223 and miR-24 have shown the prognostic relevance in SP patients [21], while miR-483-3p demonstrates both diagnostic and prognostic potential [22]. Additional dysregulated miRNAs, including miR-192-5p, miR-150-5p, miR-4793-3p, and miR-1180-3p, have been implicated in SP progression [23, 24]. Despite these advancements, the translation of miRNA biomarkers into clinical practice remains limited, underscoring the need for further research to bridge this gap.

Dysregulated miR-193b-3p expression in the inflammatory response has been previously reported [25]. For instance, an upregulation of miR-193b-3p was observed in clear cell renal cell carcinoma patients who developed chronic kidney disease (CKD) after radical nephrectomy due to inflammation and atrophy, and could predict the occurrence of CKD [26]. The upregulated miR-193b-3p was also found in children suffering from inflammatory bowel disease, and the miR-193b-3p expression could be downregulated after anti-TNF-α treatment [27]. In addition, the association between miR-193b-3p and disease of the respiratory system has also been validated, as dysregulated miR-193b-3p was correlated with smoking and allergic rhinitis [28, 29]. In this study, the potential role of miR-193b-3p in SP was investigated. We found that miR-193b-3p was elevated in the plasma of SP patients, and combined with ROC curve analysis, miR-193b-3p showed a significant diagnostic value for SP. Plasma miRNA detection is cheaper, simpler, and more convenient than traditional diagnostic methods. However, not all SP patients will have elevated miR-193b-3p levels, but it can be employed as an indicator for preliminary screening or auxiliary screening of patients with SP. To explore the relationship between miR-193b-3p and SP progression, the correlation between miR-193b-3p expression and the levels of biochemical indicators, including WBC, NEU, LYM, CRP, PCT, BNP, and D-D, was investigated. The levels of WBC, NEU, CRP, LYM, and PCT are common indicators used to assess the inflammatory response in the body and have been widely used to evaluate the severity of inflammation in the lungs [30]. The WBC levels are pathologically elevated when the lungs are infected with bacteria or fungi and are proportional to the degree of infection. CRP is not only a non-specific marker of inflammation but also directly involved in inflammation and cardiovascular diseases such as atherosclerosis [31]. PCT is another inflammatory indicator that is related to SP [32]. Previous studies had already reported that both CRP and PCT levels were elevated in SP, and this was also consistent with our study [33]. The development of SP is also accompanied by the possibility of severe myocardial damage, leading to heart failure and an increased risk of death with worsening right heart strain and pulmonary resistance. BNP has been identified as a diagnostic indicator of heart failure and can be applied to monitor cardiac dysfunction in patients with SP [34]. In addition, D-D levels could be employed to assess the risk of deep venous thrombosis and pulmonary embolism caused by increased blood viscosity and vascular endothelial injury in SP patients [35]. Based on the correlation analysis, a significant positive correlation between the expression level of miR-193b-3p and the levels of WBC, CRP, PCT, BNP, and D-D was revealed, suggesting a close association between miR-193b-3p expression and the severity of SP.

To further explore the association between miR-193b-3p and SP, the prognostic potential of miR-193b-3p was investigated. Upregulation of miR-193b-3p could predict the prognosis of SP, and the prognostic value was supported by the association between miR-193b-3p expression levels and APACHE-II and CPIS scores. APACHE-II and CPIS scores are the clinically comprehensive assessments of overall status in SP patients and are the independent prognostic factors for SP [36, 37]. SP patients with high APACHE-II and CPIS scores would develop a poor SP prognosis, which has been confirmed by multiple studies [38, 39]. Our study demonstrated that SP subjects with a higher miR-193b-3p expression level would be more likely to develop an increased APACHE II and CPIS scores, implying that the expression levels of miR-193b-3p were tightly correlated with the prognosis of SP. Also, the K-M curve was utilized to assess the relationship between miR-193b-3p expression and the prognosis of SP. A significantly better survival rate was observed in SP patients with high miR-193b-3p expression levels, and the close correlation between miR-193b-3p and the prognosis of SP was demonstrated. According to multivariate COX regression analysis, miR-193b-3p was also identified as an independent prognostic factor for SP, along with CRP, PCT, APACHE-II, and CPIS.

To systematically elucidate the regulatory mechanism of miR-193b-3p in SP, a comprehensive bioinformatics analysis was performed to identify its potential target signaling pathways. The integrated pathway enrichment analysis revealed that miR-193b-3p primarily modulates five critical pathways with established roles in pneumonia pathogenesis: (1) the PI3K-Akt pathway, which regulates immune cell activation and cytokine production and has been reported to be activated by miR-193b-3p [40, 41]; (2) the MAPK cascades, central to inflammatory response modulation [42]; (3) the FoxO signaling axis, involved in oxidative stress and cellular apoptosis [43]; (4) the TRP channel-related pathways, which influence airway inflammation and hyperreactivity [44]; and (5) Wnt signal pathways, which govern epithelial regeneration and immune cell differentiation [45]. According to the aforementioned information, it is plausible to hypothesize that miR-193b-3p may exacerbate the progression of SP by simultaneously amplifying inflammatory responses, exacerbating oxidative stress, and promoting cellular apoptosis through above interconnected pathways‌. However, these proposed mechanisms require rigorous experimental validation in vitro to establish causality and elucidate the precise molecular interactions involved in SP. These findings suggested that miR-193b-3p may exert its regulatory effects through coordinated modulation of these interconnected pathways, thereby influencing key pathophysiological processes such as immune dysregulation, inflammatory amplification, and tissue damage in SP.

However, the possible regulatory mechanism of the overexpression of miR-193b-3p in SP patients was not further explored by the in vitro experiment due to the funding limitation, and whether miR-193b-3p could serve as a therapeutic target in SP remains unknown. On the other hand, the relatively small sample size may lead to the relatively poor sensitivity of miR-193b-3p in diagnosing SP and not reflect the function of miR-193b-3p in the global population of SP patients, as only a subset of SP patients in a single hospital were included in this study. Additionally, the bioinformatics pathway predictions provide valuable insights but remain theoretical. Experimental validation would significantly strengthen these findings and represent a crucial next step to confirm the biological relevance between miR-193b-3p and the identified pathways. Comparing the diagnostic and prognostic potential of miR-193b-3p with the other validated miRNAs in SP should also be a futher significant study direction. The above limitations will be gradually solved in future research, and more complete research of miR-193b-3p on SP will be provided for clinical trials.

Conclusion

In conclusion, miR-193b-3p was upregulated in SP patients and showed a diagnostic value for SP. The expression of miR-193b-3p was closely correlated to the severity of SP and could serve as an independent prognostic factor for SP. Our research provided a novel biomarker for the clinical diagnosis and prognostic prediction in SP patients.

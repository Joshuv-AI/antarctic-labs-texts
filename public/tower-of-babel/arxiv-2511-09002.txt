Convergence and Stability Analysis of Self-Consuming Generative Models with Heterogeneous Human Curation
Hongru Zhao, Jinwen Fu, Tuan Pham

Abstract
Self-consuming generative models have received significant attention over the last few years. In this paper, we study a self-consuming generative model with heterogeneous preferences that is a generalization of the model in 15 . The model is retrained round by round using real data and its previous-round synthetic outputs. The asymptotic behavior of the retraining dynamics is investigated across four regimes using different techniques including the nonlinear Perron–Frobenius theory. Our analyses improve upon that of 15 and provide convergence results in settings where the well-known Banach contraction mapping arguments do not apply. Stability and non-stability results regarding the retraining dynamics are also given.

We cast curated retraining as population-level dynamics with two parameters: the size of the comparison pool (
K

Three regimes admit closed-form limits, delineating when conservative reference mixing is required to preserve stability while maintaining preference optimization under heterogeneous raters.

In RLHF and DPO methods, generative models are iteratively updated based on human
-

Previous work shows that such curated models can drift or become unstable, and existing theoretical analyses do not fully address convergence when feedback is heterogeneous or when curated data are mixed with a reference distribution. Our goal is to close this gap by analyzing a discrete

K

Why we call it regularization.

Mixing the curated distribution with a fixed reference
p_{\rm ref}
plays the role of a regularizer: it shrinks the update toward an anchor and turns the population map into a contraction. In the finite
-
K

4.2
Convergence in retraining dynamics with regularization

Because the convergence analyses in Regimes (iii) and (iv) are fundamentally different, we state them separately. Our setting covers the setting of

4.2.1
Convergence analysis in Regime (iii)

Under Regime (iii), we define nonlinear operator
T:\mathcal{P}_{\pi}\to\mathcal{P}_{\pi},

Tp(x)=\alpha p_{\rm ref}(x)+(1-\alpha)S_{p}(x)

(17)

where

S_{p}(x):=p(x)\,H_{p}^{K}(x).

Thus we obtain the discrete-time dynamical system
p_{t+1}=T(p_{t})
with solution
p_{t}=T^{t}p_{0}
for
t\in\mathbb{N}
. To establish existence and convergence of the limit, it is natural to invoke the Banach fixed point (contraction mapping) theorem. We now study sufficient conditions under which
T
is a contraction mapping.

For any
B\subset\mathcal{X}
, define probability measure

\mathbb{S}_{p}(B)=\int_{B}S_{p}(x)\pi(dx).

Applying Lemma
3.1
with
f=\mathbf{1}_{B}
for any measurable set
B
, we re
-
express
\mathbb{S}_{p}(B)
, yielding the following corollary.

Corollary 4.4
.

Under Setting
S2
, if
X_{1},\ldots,X_{K}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i.i.d.}}{{\sim}}p\in\mathcal{P}_{\pi}
and set
\tilde{p}_{j}=\tilde{p}_{j}(X_{1:K},\varepsilon_{1:K})=\dfrac{e^{r(X_{j})+\varepsilon_{j}}}{\sum_{i=1}^{K}e^{r(X_{i})+\varepsilon_{i}}}
,
then for all measurable
B\in\mathcal{X}
,

\mathbb{S}_{p}(B)=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{K}\tilde{p}_{j}\,\mathbf{1}_{B}(X_{j})\right].

(18)

As a consequence of (
18
), we obtain in Lemma
4.5
below a sufficient condition
for

to be a contraction on the complete metric space
(\mathcal{P}_{\pi},d_{\rm TV})
. Here
d_{\rm TV}(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the TV metric: for
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{\pi}
with densities
p=\frac{d\mu}{d\pi}
and

q=\frac{d\nu}{d\pi}
,

d_{\rm TV}(\mu,\nu)\;=\;\sup_{B}|\mu(B)-\nu(B)|\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{X}}|p(x)-q(x)|\,\pi(dx).

Henceforth, identifying measures with their densities w.r.t.
\pi
, we write
d_{\rm TV}(p,q)=d_{\rm TV}(\mu,\nu)
.

Lemma 4.5
.

If
w,u\in\mathcal{P}_{\pi}
, then

d_{\rm TV}(\mathbb{S}_{w},\mathbb{S}_{u})\leq K\cdot d_{\rm TV}(w,u).

Hence,

d_{\rm TV}(Tw,Tu)\leq(1-\alpha)K\cdot d_{\rm TV}(w,u),

(19)

so
T
is a contraction with respect to
d_{\rm TV}
whenever
\alpha>\frac{K-1}{K}
.

Theorem 4.6

(Geometric Convergence under Regime (iii))
.

Under Regime (iii), Setting
S2
and Retraining Assumptions
RA1
, let
2\leq K<\infty
and
\alpha\in\big(\frac{K-1}{K},\,1\big)
. Then
T
is a contraction on
(\mathcal{P}_{\pi},d_{\rm TV})
with Lipschitz constant
\rho\;=\;K(1-\alpha)\in[0,1),

and hence there exists a unique fixed point
p_{*}\in\mathcal{P}_{\pi}
that satisfies
p_{*}=T(p_{*})
.
Moreover, for every
p_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{\pi}
, the TV metric
d_{\rm TV}(p_{t},p_{*})
decreases strictly with
t
, and the iterates
p_{t+1}=T(p_{t})
converge geometrically to
p_{*}
with

d_{\rm TV}(p_{t},p_{*})\;\leq\;\rho^{\,t}\,d_{\rm TV}(p_{0},p_{*}),\qquad t\in\mathbb{N}.

Intuitively, the more alternatives you compare (
K
larger), the more reference mass
\alpha
you must inject to keep the loop stable. Without extra assumptions, one can not hope to have an explicit characterization of the limit
p_{*}
in this setting. We leave the problem of characterizing the limit
p_{*}
as a potential future work.

Remark 4.7
.

Theorem
4.6
does not require the Retraining Assumption
RA2
; in particular, it imposes neither a boundedness assumption on

nor an exponential-moment assumption on

,

and the human curation procedure.

In particular, when

(the pretrained generative model used in applications), the population

35
;
36
;
55

Lemma 4.8
.

Under the same setting of Theorem
4.6
, if

and

Remark 4.9
.

Under the condition

establishes the convergence of the retraining dynamics in Regime (iii).
Notably, this result complements Theorem 2.4 in

Our theorem thus provides a refinement of their framework by offering a convergence guarantee under comparable assumptions. However, when

4.2.2
Convergence analysis in Regime (iv)

In Regime (iv), an infinite candidate pool (
K=\infty
) stabilizes the human
-
curation procedure and allows us to relax the constraint on the regularization proportion
\alpha
(Assumption
A3
) relative to Regime (iii) and analyze convergence. We derive a closed-form expression for the fixed point of the nonlinear operator
T
.

Our analysis regarding Regime (iv) requires a technical tool from functional analysis: the Hilbert projective metric; see Definition
4.13
below and
1
;
14
;
13
for basic properties and applications. A short explanation of why this metric is useful for the analysis is given in Remark
4.14
. Roughly speaking, the nonlinear mapping we construct is not contractive in the TV metric, but it is contractive in the Hilbert projective metric.

For convenience, we consider a new baseline measure in place of
\pi
. Define the probability measure
\mathbb{P}_{\rm ref}
such that
\mathbb{P}_{\rm ref}\ll\pi
and
p_{\rm ref}=\frac{d\mathbb{P}_{\rm ref}}{d\pi}
. Define the linear operator
L
on the positive cone

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}\;=\;\bigl\{f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{P}_{\rm ref}):f(x)>0\text{ for }\mathbb{P}_{\rm ref}\text{-a.s.\ }x\bigr\}

(21)

by

L[f](x)\;=\;\alpha\,\langle f,Q\rangle_{\mathrm{ref}}\;+\;(1-\alpha)\,f(x)\,Q(x),

(22)

where

\langle f,Q\rangle_{\mathrm{ref}}:=\int_{\mathcal{X}}f(x)\,Q(x)\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{ref}}(dx)=\int_{\mathcal{X}}f(x)p_{\mathrm{ref}}(x)Q(x)\pi(dx).

Define the normalized operator
L_{N}
on the positive cone
\mathcal{K}
as

L_{N}[f](x)=\frac{L[f](x)}{\langle f,Q\rangle_{\rm ref}}.

Define
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{P}_{\rm ref}}=\bigl\{\mu\text{ is a probability measure on }(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{B}):\mu\ll\mathbb{P}_{\rm ref}\,\bigr\}.
One can see that the mapping
L_{N}:\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{P}_{\rm ref}}\to\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{P}_{\rm ref}}
is nonlinear.

We make the following assumption on
p_{0}
and
p_{\rm ref}
:

A2
.

Assume
\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\ \frac{p_{0}(x)}{p_{\rm ref}(x)}<\infty
.

Let
w_{t}(x)=p_{t}(x)/p_{\mathrm{ref}}(x)
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Assumption (
A2
) ensures that
w_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{P}_{\rm ref}}
. It is straightforward to verify that each
w_{t}(x)\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{P}_{\rm ref}}
represents the probability density function (PDF) of dynamics under the new baseline measure
\mathbb{P}_{\rm ref}
for all
t\in\mathbb{N}
. The following lemma establishes the equivalence between the operators
L,L_{N}
and the update dynamics.

Lemma 4.10
.

The densities
w_{t}
, defined with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{\rm ref}
, satisfy the recursion

\displaystyle L_{N}(w_{t})(x)=\frac{L[w_{t}](x)}{\langle L[w_{t}],Q\rangle_{\rm ref}}\text{ and}

(23)

\displaystyle w_{t+1}(x)=\alpha+(1-\alpha)\frac{Q(x)\,w_{t}(x)}{\langle w_{t},Q\rangle_{\rm ref}}=L_{N}[w_{t}](x)

(24)

The convergence problem for
\{p_{t}\}_{t=1}^{\infty}
is then equivalent to analyzing the fixed point for
L_{N}
. If the fixed point of
L_{N}
exists, denoted as
w_{*}
, then

L_{N}[w_{*}](x)=\alpha+(1-\alpha)\frac{Q(x)w_{*}(x)}{c_{*}}=w_{*}(x),

where constant
c_{*}=\langle w_{*},Q\rangle_{\rm ref}
. The fixed point must satisfy

w_{*}(x)=\frac{\alpha}{1-(1-\alpha)Q(x)/c_{*}}.

(25)

To make sure that the formal fixed point (
25
) defines a proper density function, we introduce the following assumption.

A3
Nondegenerate fixed point condition.

Assume that

\alpha\;>\;\Bigg(\int\frac{Q_{*}}{\,Q_{*}-Q(x)\,}\,p_{\mathrm{ref}}(x)\,d\pi(x)\Bigg)^{-1}.

(26)

Remark 4.11
.

By Retraining Assumption
RA3
, it is obvious that

\int\frac{Q_{*}}{\,Q_{*}-Q(x)\,}\,p_{\mathrm{ref}}(x)\,d\pi(x)\;>\;1,

and consequently the right-hand side of (
26
) is strictly less than
1
. If Assumption
A1
holds, then

\int\frac{Q_{*}}{\,Q_{*}-Q(x)\,}\,p_{\mathrm{ref}}(x)\,d\pi(x)=\infty,

so the requirement (
26
) reduces to
\alpha>0
; that is, there is no additional restriction on
\alpha
in this case.

Lemma 4.12
.

Under Regime (iv), Setting
S1
, Retraining Assumption
RA3
, and Assumption
A3
,
there exists a unique fixed point
w_{*}
of the operator
L_{N}
over
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{ref}}}
. Moreover, the corresponding constant

c_{*}
lies in the interval

((1-\alpha)Q_{*},\,Q_{*}]
.

Nonlinear Perron–Frobenius theory offers a contraction framework for positive, order-preserving homogeneous maps, guaranteeing a unique positive fixed point and geometric convergence of normalized iterates
(
28
;
14
)
. Because contraction is measured in the Hilbert projective metric, this metric is the natural geometry for analyzing the stability and convergence of our Regime (iv) retraining dynamics. Next we define the Hilbert projective metric on the positive cone
\mathcal{K}
(defined in Subsection
2.1
) of the underlying probability space.

Definition 4.13

(Hilbert projective metric)
.

Recall the cone
\mathcal{K}
in (
21
). For
u,v\in\mathcal{K}
with
u,v>0
a.s., define

\beta(u,v)\;:=\;\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{u(x)}{v(x)},\qquad\alpha(u,v)\;:=\;\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{u(x)}{v(x)}\;=\;\frac{1}{\beta(v,u)}.

The Hilbert projective metric
(
1
;
28
)
is

d_{\mathcal{H}}(u,v)\;:=\;\log\!\frac{\beta(u,v)}{\alpha(u,v)}\;=\;\log\!\big(\beta(u,v)\,\beta(v,u)\big).

Note that the definition of the Hilbert projective metric above coincides with the classical definition involving convex order in a Banach space; see
28
for more details. We now motivate the use of the Hilbert projective metric in Regime (iv).

Remark 4.14
.

In Regime (iii), the nonlinear operator
T
is not necessarily nonexpansive; indeed,
Theorem
4.6
shows nonexpansiveness only when

\alpha\in\bigl[\frac{K-1}{K},\,1\bigr)
.
In Regime (iv), the update
L_{N}[w]=L[w]/\langle w,Q\rangle_{\mathrm{ref}}
is the projective normalization of the positive linear map
L[f]
. Because the Hilbert projective metric
d_{\mathcal{H}}
is invariant under positive scalings, we have

d_{\mathcal{H}}\bigl(L_{N}[u],L_{N}[v]\bigr)=d_{\mathcal{H}}\bigl(L[u],L[v]\bigr)
.
By Birkhoff’s contraction theorem
(
14
;
13
)
,

d_{\mathcal{H}}(w_{t+1},w_{*})\;=\;d_{\mathcal{H}}\bigl(L_{N}[w_{t}],L_{N}[w_{*}]\bigr)\;=\;d_{\mathcal{H}}\bigl(L[w_{t}],L[w_{*}]\bigr)\;\leq\;d_{\mathcal{H}}(w_{t},w_{*}),\ t\geq 0.

By contrast, other metrics (e.g., the TV metric or the
KL
divergence) are not guaranteed to yield monotonic convergence of
L_{N}^{t}[w_{0}]
toward
w_{*}
. Because convergence in the Hilbert projective metric is stronger, once established, it directly yields convergence in the TV metric and KL divergence.

Theorem 4.15
.

Under Regime (iv), Setting
S1
, Retraining Assumption
RA1
-
RA3
and Assumptions
A2
-
A3

(27)

then the Hilbert projective metric

decreases strictly and remains finite for all
t\geq 1

and

\lim_{t\to\infty}d_{\mathcal{H}}(w_{t},w_{*})=0.

Under the original dynamics (
12
) and
p_{\rm ref}=p_{0}
, the result can be rewritten as

\lim_{t\to\infty}d_{\mathcal{H}}(p_{t},p_{*})=0,

where
p_{t}=w_{t}p_{0}
and
p_{*}=w_{*}p_{0}
. Clearly the limit
p_{*}
depends on the pre-trained generative model
p_{0}
.

.

If we set

5
Stability under Reward Perturbations

At each retraining step we form a convex mixture between a fixed reference distribution
p_{\rm ref}
(e.g., real data or an anchored model) and a curated synthetic distribution produced from the current model
p
. We study the sensitivity of this update to bounded reward perturbations. Concretely, we perturb the per-sample reward by an additive
\Delta r\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{B},\pi)
, with
\|\Delta r\|_{\infty}\leq\eta
, and ask:
how far can the entire retraining trajectory drift (in the TV metric) from the unperturbed one, uniformly over time?
Related safety controls for generative models include content
-
aware filtering and robustness
-
improving training
(
46
;
59
)
.

Definition 5.1

(Superalignment)
.

Let
(p_{t,\Delta r})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
be retraining dynamics under reward
r+\Delta r
, with
\Delta r\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{B},\pi)
and
\|\Delta r\|_{\infty}\leq\eta
. We say
superalignment
holds if:

(i)

Monotone improvement:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{X\sim p_{t+1,\Delta r}}[Q(X)]>\mathbb{E}_{X\sim p_{t,\Delta r}}[Q(X)]
for all
t
.

(ii)

Trajectory stability:

\displaystyle\limsup_{\|\Delta r\|_{\infty}\to 0}\;\sup_{t\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}}d_{\mathrm{TV}}\!\bigl(p_{t,\Delta r},p_{t,0}\bigr)=0
.

5.1
Regimes (i) and (ii): Unstable under bounded reward perturbations

Throughout this subsection we work under the assumptions
of our convergence results in the pure synthetic setting:
RA1
-
RA3
and Assumption
A1
for
Regime (i) (
\alpha=0
,
K<\infty
) under Setting
S2
, and for Regime (ii) (
\alpha=0
,
K=\infty
)
under Setting
S1
. In both regimes, one retraining step with a
perturbed reward
r+\Delta r
is

p_{t+1,\Delta r}(x)\;=\;p_{t,\Delta r}(x)\;H^{K}_{\,p_{t,\Delta r}}(x),

where for
2\leq K<\infty

H_{p,\Delta r}^{K}(x):=\mathbb{E}_{\begin{subarray}{c}X_{1},\dots,X_{K-1}\sim p\\
\varepsilon,\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{K-1}\end{subarray}}\Bigl[K\,\frac{e^{\,r(x)+\Delta r(x)+\varepsilon(x)}}{e^{\,r(x)+\Delta r(x)+\varepsilon(x)}+\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}e^{r(X_{k})+\Delta r(X_{k})+\varepsilon_{k}(X_{k})}}\Bigr]

and for
K=\infty

H_{p,\Delta r}^{\infty}(x):=\frac{Q_{\Delta r}(x)}{\mathbb{E}_{X\sim p}Q_{\Delta r}(X)}.

where
Q_{\Delta r}(x):=e^{r(x)+\Delta r(x)}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\varepsilon(X)}\mid X=x\right]=Q(x)e^{\Delta r(x)}
,
Q_{*,\Delta r}:=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x}Q_{\Delta r}(x)
and

A_{\Delta r}:=\{x\in\mathcal{X}:\;Q_{\Delta r}(x)=Q_{*,\Delta r}\}.

6
Discussion

In this paper, we investigate the convergence behaviors and stability of the retraining dynamics under the four Regimes specified in Section
2
. Our results generalize some aspects of
15
in Regimes (iii) and (iv), and under a more general model setting with individual fluctuations in the reward functions. We establish convergence and, as a consequence, prove robustness of the retraining dynamics to bounded reward perturbations, highlighting the necessity of reference data for stabilizing the retraining loop. Our analyses are based on a few new technical results, including the nonlinear Perron–Frobenius theory, and we expect them to be useful for other similar problems. Moreover, all results above extend verbatim to conditional generative models. Given a prompt
y
, replace in step 1 the unconditional sampling by

X_{1},\dots,X_{K}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}p_{t}(\cdot\mid y),

and in step 2 replace the utility by the conditional counterpart,

r(x\mid y)+\varepsilon_{k}(x\mid y).

Equivalently, replace every occurrence of
X
by the conditional variable
X\mid Y=y
throughout the analysis. Under this substitution, all population-level updates and convergence/stability results in the paper hold without additional technical difficulty: the discrete-choice curation kernel, mean-field limit, and mixed-data contraction carry over pointwise in
y
, and integration over the prompt distribution preserves the proofs.

Finally, we highlight several open questions that arise from our analyses.

•

Sharpness of the convergence rate
. Is Theorem
4.6
in Regime (iii) sharp for uniform TV contraction?
In particular, can one characterize whether a change in convergence
form
or
rate
occurs at the threshold
\alpha=(K-1)/K
?
At this critical value, the worst
-
case rate may cease to be geometric and exhibit only polynomial decay or even not convergence.

•

Necessity of stationarity in preference noise.

Do Theorem
4.3
(Regime (i)) and Theorem
4.6
(Regime (iii)) continue to hold under the nonstationary preference
-
noise model in Setting
S1
(instead of Setting
S2
)?
How robust are these guarantees as the heterogeneity of the noise field increases—for example, as

\frac{\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{x\in\mathcal{X}}Q(x)}{\operatorname{ess\,inf}_{x\in\mathcal{X}}Q(x)}
grows?
It appears plausible that sufficiently heterogeneous noise can disrupt convergence in Regimes (i) and (iii) across standard metrics; identifying precise thresholds or explicit counterexamples is an open direction.

•

Effect of large comparison pools (
K
).

Intuitively, showing more candidates per round should help the curator select higher
-
reward samples.
However, Theorem
4.6
in Regime (iii) requires a stronger lower bound on the mixing proportion, whereas in the infinite
-
pool limit (Regime (iv)) the sufficient condition on
\alpha
is much weaker (e.g., under Assumption
A3
).
As
K
increases, the guarantees appear more resilient to heterogeneous preference noise, with
K=\infty
the most robust case; quantifying the precise dependence on
K
and the minimal mixing needed for stability and sharp rates remains open.

\displaystyle\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x}\frac{\alpha\langle w_{k},Q\rangle_{\rm ref}+(1-\alpha)w_{k}(x)Q(x)}{\alpha\langle w_{*},Q\rangle_{\rm ref}+(1-\alpha)w_{*}(x)Q(x)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M_{k}\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x}\frac{\alpha\langle w_{*},Q\rangle_{\rm ref}+(1-\alpha)w_{*}(x)Q(x)-\alpha Q_{\rm{min}}(1-1/M_{k})}{\alpha\langle w_{*},Q\rangle_{\rm ref}+(1-\alpha)w_{*}(x)Q(x)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M_{k}\frac{(\alpha+(1-\alpha)e^{R}w_{*,\rm{max}})Q_{*}-\alpha Q_{\rm min}(1-1/M_{k})}{(\alpha+(1-\alpha)e^{R}w_{*,\rm{max}})Q_{*}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M_{k}g(M_{k}),

where the second inequality in the display above follows from the fact that the function
x\mapsto(x-c)/x
is increasing on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
for all
c>0
, and

g(y):=\frac{(\alpha+(1-\alpha)e^{R}w_{*,\rm{max}})Q_{*}-\alpha Q_{\rm min}(1-1/y)}{(\alpha+(1-\alpha)e^{R}w_{*,\rm{max}})Q_{*}},\ y\geq 1.

Notice that

is strictly decreasing in

, and
g(1)=1
. Thus,

for any

. Similarly, we can also show that

Now, we obtain that

d_{\mathcal{H}}(w_{k+1},w_{*})\leq\log(M_{k}m_{k}g(M_{k})g(m_{k}))=d_{\mathcal{H}}(w_{k},w_{*})+\log(g(M_{k})g(m_{k}))<d_{\mathcal{H}}(w_{k},w_{*}).

(52)

Since

0\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}-\log(g(M_{i})g(m_{i}))\leq d_{\mathcal{H}}(w_{0},w_{*})-d_{\mathcal{H}}(w_{k+1},w_{*})\leq d_{\mathcal{H}}(w_{0},w_{*})<\infty,

(53)

we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}\log(g(M_{k})g(m_{k}))=0.

Hence, we obtain that both
g(M_{k})\to 1
and
g(m_{k})\to 1
as
k\to\infty

M_{k}\to 1
and
m_{k}\to 1
. It follows that

as
k\to\infty
.
∎

Proof of Lemma

Proof of Theorem

Set

.

In the fully synthetic
K
-choice loop (both for finite
K
and for

), the limit distribution concentrates on the top
Q
-level set reached at initialization:
p_{\infty,0}
is
p_{0}
renormalized to
A
, and
p_{\infty,\Delta r}
is
p_{0}
renormalized to
A_{\Delta r}
. Since

and

Lemma C.2
.

For every density
p

Proof of Lemma
C.2
.

Let
\psi_{K}(u,s):=K\,u/(u+s)
and abbreviate
G_{r}(x):=\mathbb{E}[\psi_{K}(\widetilde{U}_{r},\widetilde{S}_{r})]
,
where the expectation is over

\widetilde{S}_{r}=\sum_{j=1}^{K-1}e^{r(X_{j})+\varepsilon_{j}(X_{j})}

By the chain rule and dominated convergence (see bound below),

\frac{d}{dt}H_{t}(x)=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\partial_{u}\psi_{K}(U_{t},S_{t})\,\frac{dU_{t}}{dt}+\partial_{s}\psi_{K}(U_{t},S_{t})\,\frac{dS_{t}}{dt}\right],

with

\displaystyle\Bigl|\tfrac{d}{dt}H_{t}(x)\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq K\|\Delta r\|_{\infty}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{U_{t}S_{t}}{(U_{t}+S_{t})^{2}}+\frac{U_{t}}{(U_{t}+S_{t})^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{K-1}e^{r_{t}(X_{j})+\varepsilon_{j}(X_{j})}\right]

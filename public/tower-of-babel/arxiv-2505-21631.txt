Resurgence Analysis of the Nambu–Jona–Lasinio model at large charge
\dosserif Jahmall Bersini ✪ , Simeon Hellerman ✪ , Domenico Orlando ★✩✪ , and Susanne Reffert ✩✪ \authorBlock ✪ \dosserif Kavli Institute for the Physics and Mathematics of the Universe (WPI) UTIAS, The University of Tokyo Kashiwa, Chiba 277–8582, Japan \authorBlock ★ \dosserif INFN sezione di Torino. via Pietro Giuria 1, 10125 Torino, Italy \authorBlock ✩ \dosserif Albert Einstein Center for Fundamental Physics, Institute for Theoretical Physics, University of Bern, Sidlerstrasse 5, CH-3012 Bern, Switzerland

Abstract
We study the fixed point of the three-dimensional njl model in a double-scaling limit where both the charge Q Q and the number of fermion flavors N N become large with a fixed ratio q = Q / ( 2 ​ N ) q=Q/(2N) . While a similar analysis has been performed for the bosonic O(N) model, fermionic models pose new challenges. In this work, we systematically explore the cft spectrum in both the large and small q q limits beyond the first few orders, and perform a resurgence analysis. Through this approach, we identify the exponential corrections that relate the convergent small- q q expansion to the asymptotic large- q q behavior. Our results are suggestive of a geometric interpretation in terms of the worldline of particles moving along the geodesics on the cylinder.

Introduction

The study of

]

Combining large charge with a second controlling parameter is often very fruitful, as was done in

,

,

]

Also in the study of fermionic models, such a natural double-scaling limit exists, where both the charge
Q
and the number of fermion flavors

become large while their ratio remains fixed.
The
njl
(
njl
) model in three dimensions provides an ideal setting for exploring this approach, as it exhibits a well-defined
uv
(
uv
) fixed point in the large-charge limit. The
njl

[

]
.
The goal of this paper is to analyze the
njl
model in the large and small
q
limits systematically beyond the first few orders, establishing a controlled expansion for its physical observables. We moreover perform a resurgence analysis, identifying the exponential corrections in analogy with
[
Grassi:2019txd
,

,

In the regime
q\ll 1
, using an explicit expansion of the gap equation and grand potential
\Omega(\mu)
, we derive a power series in
q
for the scaling dimensions and analyze its convergence properties.
This perturbative expansion is
convergent
within a finite radius.
This is a striking feature, as many
qft
at large charge exhibit only asymptotic expansions in perturbation theory.
From a more technical point of view, we observe that the analytic structure of the small double-scaled coupling expansion in the grandcanonical picture is considerably more complicated than in the canonical one.
With a careful study of the analytic properties of the perturbative expansion, confirmed by Padé approximants and Darboux’s theorem, we identify the singularity that determines the radius of convergence, which appears to be associated with a vanishing mass of the radial mode.
This observation suggests that the small-charge expansion is under analytic control, making it a valuable tool for extracting physical observables.

In contrast, the expansion at large double-scaled coupling
q\gg 1
is
asymptotic
, with coefficients exhibiting factorial growth characteristic of a divergent series.
A key role is played by the gap equation, governing the large-
q
limit:

\kappa_{0}\tanh(\kappa_{0})=1,

(1.2)

which determines the nonzero fermion gap
\Phi_{0}
in the double-scaling limit as a function of the chemical potential
\mu

\Phi_{0}=\mu\sqrt{\kappa_{0}^{2}-1}\,,

(1.3)

and admits a positive real solution:

\kappa_{0}=1.1996786402577338339163698486411419442614\dots\,.

(1.4)

Using a Mellin transform representation of the relevant functional determinants, we systematically expand the energy and operator dimensions in inverse powers of
q
:

\frac{\Delta(Q)}{2N}=\frac{2}{3}\kappa_{0}^{3/2}q^{3/2}+\frac{1}{6}\kappa_{0}^{1/2}q^{1/2}+\dots\,.

(1.5)

The asymptotic nature of the expansion (whose terms diverge like
(2n)!
) implies the existence of an optimal truncation of the series at
\order{\sqrt{q}}
terms, and the necessity of non-perturbative corrections, which we identify as exponentially suppressed terms of the form
\exp(-\alpha\sqrt{q})
.
These corrections indicate the presence of worldline-instanton contributions to the physics of larger double-scaled coupling in the large-charge limit.

To better understand these non-perturbative effects, we turn to
resurgence theory
indicating the effect of
worldline instantons
.
The non-perturbative terms found in the asymptotic expansion can be interpreted as contributions from worldline instantons wrapping geodesics on the two-sphere.
The scaling dimension receives nonperturbative corrections of the form

\frac{\Delta(Q)}{2N}\ni-i\frac{\pqty*{\kappa_{0}^{2}-1}^{1/4}q^{3/4}e^{-2\pi\sqrt{\kappa_{0}^{-1}-\kappa_{0}^{-3}}\abs{k}\sqrt{q}}}{2\pi\kappa_{0}^{9/4}\sqrt{\abs{k}}}\pqty*{\sqrt{\kappa_{0}^{2}-1}\cos*(\frac{2\pi k}{\kappa_{0}^{3/2}}\sqrt{q})+\sin*(\frac{2\pi k}{\kappa_{0}^{3/2}}\sqrt{q})}+\dots\,.

(1.6)

The worldline formalism provides a first-quantized description of the
njl
model’s fermionic determinant, allowing us to derive the leading non-perturbative corrections directly from a semiclassical calculation. This interpretation unifies the perturbative and non-perturbative structures of the theory, demonstrating that the large-charge expansion is naturally controlled by geometric instanton effects.

While these techniques have been previously applied to bosonic theories, their successful implementation in a fermionic model provides strong evidence that they apply more broadly.
This raises intriguing questions about the role of large charge in other strongly coupled
qft
, including gauge theories and supersymmetric models.

The 3d NJL model at large charge

The Lagrangian of the

\mathcal{L}_{NJL}=\bar{\Psi}_{j}\Gamma^{\mu}\mathop{\mathrm{{}\partial}}\mathopen{}_{\mu}\Psi^{j}-\frac{g}{N}\left[\left(\bar{\Psi}_{j}\Psi^{j}\right)^{2}-\left(\bar{\Psi}_{j}\Gamma_{5}\Psi^{j}\right)^{2}\right],

This representation is introduced to generalize the concept of chirality in arbitrary dimensions.
Explicitly, we have

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mu}=\begin{pmatrix}\gamma_{\mu}&0\\

(2.2)

\gamma_{\mu}=\sigma_{\mu}

The model flows to a

\displaystyle\Psi_{i}

\displaystyle\to e^{i\alpha\Gamma_{5}}\Psi_{i}\,,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{5}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathbbm{1}\\

(2.3)

It has been conjectured that in

\mathcal{L}_{\text{NJLY}}=\frac{1}{2}(\mathop{\mathrm{{}\partial}}\mathopen{}_{\mu}\phi_{1})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\mathop{\mathrm{{}\partial}}\mathopen{}_{\mu}\phi_{2})^{2}+\bar{\psi}_{j}\not{\mathop{\mathrm{{}\partial}}\mathopen{}}\psi^{j}+g\,\bar{\psi}_{Rj}\bar{\phi}\psi_{L}^{j}+g\,\bar{\psi}_{Lj}\phi\psi^{j}_{R}+\frac{(4\pi)^{2}\lambda}{24}\left(\bar{\phi}\phi\right)^{2},

(2.4)

The fixed point of the
njl

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle\to\infty,

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle\to\infty,

\displaystyle Q/(2N)

\displaystyle=q\text{ fixed}

The conformal dimension of the lowest operator of charge

\Delta(Q)=rF(Q).

(2.6)

At leading order in

\Omega(\mu)

\displaystyle\Omega(\mu)

\displaystyle=-\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\frac{1}{\beta}\log\Tr*(e^{-\beta(H+\mu Q)}),

(2.7)

\displaystyle F(Q)

\displaystyle=\eval*{\mu Q+\Omega(\mu)}_{\mu=\mu(Q)}\,,

\displaystyle Q=-\odv{\Omega}{\mu}\,.

(2.8)

In turn, in the large-
N

In the standard approach, one introduces a Hubbard–Stratonovich collective scalar field
\Phi

S=\int\dd^{d}{x}\left[\bar{\Psi}_{j}\left(\Gamma_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}-\mu\Gamma_{3}\Gamma_{5}+\Phi P_{+}+\bar{\Phi}P_{-}\right)\Psi^{j}+\frac{N}{4g}\bar{\Phi}\Phi\right],

The collective field

As shown in

After integrating out the fermions, which now appear only quadratically, we can evaluate the action on the zero mode

S(\mu,\Phi_{0})=-\beta N\Tr\log(\Gamma_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}-\mu\Gamma_{3}\Gamma_{5}+\Phi_{0})\,.

The action is to be minimized with respect to
\Phi_{0}

\displaystyle\beta\Omega(\mu)

\displaystyle=\eval*{S(\mu,\Phi_{0})}_{\Phi_{0}=\Phi_{0}(\mu)}\,,

\displaystyle\eval*{\pdv{S(\mu,\Phi_{0})}{\Phi_{0}}}_{\Phi_{0}=\Phi_{0}(\mu)}

\displaystyle=0.

\zeta(s|\not{\mathop{\mathrm{{}\partial}}\mathopen{}{}},\mu,\Phi_{0})=\Tr*(\Gamma_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}-\mu\Gamma_{3}\Gamma_{5}+\Phi_{0})^{-2s}.

\displaystyle\omega_{\pm}(\ell)

\displaystyle n_{\ell}

\displaystyle=2(\ell+1),

In this scheme, the action for the zero modes is identified with a special value of the zeta function,

3
Convergent expansion in the small Q/N limit

3.1
Perturbative expansion

To obtain the small
q=Q/(2N)
expansion of the gap equation and the grand potential, we first note that the conformal coupling to the Ricci scalar of

generates a mass term

for the collective field
\Phi

As a consequence, both the charge and the gap
\Phi_{0}

This is the value of the chemical potential around which one has to expand to generate the small

\zeta(s|\not{\mathop{\mathrm{{}\partial}}\mathopen{}{}},\mu,\Phi_{0})=r^{2s}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\ell\bqty*{\pqty*{\pqty*{\ell+1/2+r\tilde{\mu}}^{2}+\pqty*{r\Phi_{0}}^{2}}^{-s}+\pqty*{\pqty*{\ell-1/2-r\tilde{\mu}}^{2}+\pqty*{r\Phi_{0}}^{2}}^{-s}}\,.

In this section we are interested in the limit

In this limit we can use a trinomial expansion to write a double sum in positive powers of

and express the sum over

Canonical ensemble.

The analysis becomes more manageable in the canonical picture, where the charge is kept fixed.

To characterize the leading singularities we can use Darboux’s theorem.
The singularity structure of a given function
\Psi(x)=\sum\psi_{n}x^{n}
is fixed by the large-order behavior of the coefficients
\psi_{n}
(see e.g.
[

Concretely, if the

\psi_{n}\underset{n\to\infty}{\sim}\frac{1}{x_{0}^{n}}\left[\psi(x_{0})\binom{n+p-1}{n}-x_{0}\psi^{\prime}(x_{0})\binom{n+p-2}{n}+\frac{x_{0}^{2}}{2!}\psi^{\prime\prime}(x_{0})\binom{n+p-3}{n}-\dots\right]+\dots\,,

(3.15)

then

\displaystyle\Psi(x)

\displaystyle=\psi(x)\left(1-\frac{x}{x_{0}}\right)^{-p}+\text{analytic}\,,

\displaystyle x\to

\displaystyle x_{0}\,,

(3.16)

with
\psi(x)
analytic near

.
The parameters in Eq. (

.
For instance, it is well known that the radius of convergence

can be determined by analyzing the ratio of consecutive coefficients
\psi_{n}/\psi_{n-1}\underset{n\to\infty}{\sim}1/x_{0}
.
Analogously,

can be obtained by studying

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle=1+\lim_{n\to\infty}n\left(x_{0}\frac{\psi_{n}}{\psi_{n-1}}-1\right)\,,

\displaystyle\psi(x_{0})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\psi_{n}}{\left(\frac{1}{x_{0}}\right)^{n}\binom{n+p-1}{n}}\,.

(3.17)

4
Asymptotic expansion for large Q/N

We now move to the large
q
expansion.
We know already that in the grandcanonical picture this limit is the same as large
r\mu
and
r\Phi_{0}
.
In this regime, the behavior of the zeta function is well captured by a representation in terms of the Mellin transform of an appropriate heat kernel.

\Tr*(\Sigma^{-s})=\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\dd{t}}{t}t^{s}\Tr*(e^{-\Sigma t})\,.

(4.1)

Studying this heat kernel we will find a perturbative expansion in
1/q
, which is now asymptotic and needs to be supplemented by exponential corrections.

Let us start by rewriting our zeta function as a Mellin transform:

\displaystyle\zeta(s|\not{\mathop{\mathrm{{}\partial}}\mathopen{}{}},\mu,\Phi_{0})

\displaystyle=2r^{2s}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\ell\bqty*{\pqty*{\pqty*{\ell+r\mu}^{2}+(r\Phi_{0})^{2}}^{-s}+\pqty*{\pqty*{\ell-r\mu}^{2}+(r\Phi_{0})^{2}}^{-s}}

(4.2)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\dd{t}}{t}t^{s}e^{-\Phi_{0}^{2}t}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\ell\pqty*{e^{-(\ell/r+\mu)^{2}t}+e^{-(\ell/r-\mu)^{2}t}}\,.

We are interested in the limit of large gap
\Phi_{0}
(in units of the inverse radius
1/r
).
Because of the exponential
\exp[-\Phi_{0}^{2}t]
, the integral localizes around the
t\to 0
limit of the heat kernel

K(t|\mu)=\Tr*(e^{-t\Sigma})=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\ell\pqty*{e^{-(\ell/r+\mu)^{2}t}+e^{-(\ell/r-\mu)^{2}t}}\,,

(4.3)

where

\Sigma=\pqty*{\Gamma^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}-\Gamma_{3}\Gamma_{5}\mu}^{\dagger}\pqty*{\Gamma^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}-\Gamma_{3}\Gamma_{5}\mu}.

(4.4)

This form, however, is not adapted to taking the
t\to 0
limit because all the exponentials contribute at the same order.
To overcome this difficulty, we perform a Poisson resummation,
i.e.
we use the identity

\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}f(l)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int\dd{\rho}f(\rho)e^{2\pi ik\rho}.

(4.5)

The Fourier transforms can be evaluated analytically and we find

\displaystyle K(t|\mu)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\ell\left(e^{-(\ell/r+\mu)^{2}t}+e^{-(\ell/r-\mu)^{2}t}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}\abs{\ell}\left(e^{-(\ell/r+\mu)^{2}t}+e^{-(\ell/r-\mu)^{2}t}\right)

(4.6)

\displaystyle\begin{multlined}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\dd{\rho}\abs{\rho}\left(e^{-(\mu+\rho/r)^{2}t}+e^{-(\mu-\rho/r)^{2}t}\right)\\
+\frac{1}{2}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{k}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\dd{\rho}\abs{\rho}\left(e^{-(\mu+\rho/r)^{2}t-i2\pi k\rho}+e^{-(\mu-\rho/r)^{2}t-i2\pi k\rho}\right)\end{multlined}

\displaystyle\begin{multlined}=\frac{r^{2}}{t}\left(e^{-\mu^{2}t}+\mu\sqrt{\pi t}\erf(\mu\sqrt{t})\right)\\
+\frac{r^{2}e^{-\mu^{2}t}}{t^{3/2}}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{k}\left(\sqrt{t}-\left(\pi rk+i\mu t\right)F\left(\frac{\pi rk+i\mu t}{\sqrt{t}}\right)-\left(\pi rk-i\mu t\right)F\left(\frac{\pi rk-i\mu t}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\right),\end{multlined}

where the primed sum is over
k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\set{0}
,
\erf(z)
is the error function, and
F(z)
is Dawson’s function,

F(z)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}e^{z^{2}}\erf(z)=e^{-z^{2}}\int_{0}^{z}\dd{u}e^{-u^{2}}\ .

(4.7)

The expression that we have obtained is suggestive of a sum over instantonic contributions.
To make this observation precise, we need to decompose Dawson’s function into its perturbative and non-perturbative parts.

In the limit of interest, the absolute value of the argument of Dawson’s function is large,

\abs*{\frac{\pi rk\pm i\mu t}{\sqrt{t}}}^{2}=\frac{\pi^{2}r^{2}k^{2}}{t}+\mu^{2}t\to\infty,

(4.8)

and — as we will see in the following — the Mellin integral in the zeta function has a saddle at
\Phi_{0}t=k\pi r
.
It follows that the phase of the argument satisfies
\tan(\theta)=\pm 1/\sqrt{\kappa_{0}^{2}-1}
, for an angle
\theta\approx\pm\pi/3
, and we can consistently use the asymptotic expansion

F(x)\underset{x\to\infty}{=}\sum_{n=0}\frac{(2n-1)!!}{2^{n+1}}\pqty*{\frac{1}{x}}^{2n+1}\mp\frac{i\sqrt{\pi}}{2}e^{-x^{2}}.

(4.9)

We have obtained a transseries representation
[
Ecalle
,
Dorigoni:2014hea
,
Aniceto:2018bis
]
of the heat kernel around
t\to 0
.
The reality condition for
F(x)
is sufficient to fix the Stokes parameter in front of the exponential.
In turn this fixes all the ambiguities in the asymptotic expansion of the heat kernel
K(t|\mu)
.

The perturbative and non perturbative parts in the expansion of the Dawson’s function will turn directly into the large charge asymptotic expansion and exponential contributions.

Perturbative expansion.

The perturbative part is written by using the binomial expansion for the argument of
F
and recognizing that the sum over
k
reduces to a Riemann zeta function.
After some manipulations, the perturbative part of the heat kernel reads

\displaystyle K(t|\mu)

\displaystyle\sim\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{t}}\mu r^{2}\erf(\mu\sqrt{t})+e^{-\mu^{2}t}r^{2}\bqty*{\frac{1}{t}-\frac{1}{t}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}\frac{(-4)^{l}\abs{B_{2m}}}{m\Gamma(m-l)\Gamma(2l+1)}(\mu^{2}t)^{l}\frac{t^{m}}{r^{2m}}}

(4.10)

\displaystyle\sim\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{t}}\mu r^{2}\erf(\mu\sqrt{t})+e^{-\mu^{2}t}\bqty*{\frac{r^{2}}{t}-\frac{1}{6}-\frac{t}{60r^{2}}+\frac{t^{2}\mu^{2}}{30r^{4}}+\dots},

where the symbol

indicates asymptotic equivalence.
One advantage of having an explicit form for the series is that we can evaluate its large-order behavior.
From this form, we can see that the Mellin integral localizes around

For this reason, we rewrite the double sum as a series expansion around

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}\frac{(-4)^{l}\abs{B_{2m}}}{m\Gamma(m-l)\Gamma(2l+1)}(\mu^{2}t)^{l}\frac{t^{m}}{r^{2m}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{r^{2n}},

(4.11)

where

c_{n}=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}4^{l}B_{2(l+n)}}{(2l)!(l+n)\Gamma(n)}\left(\frac{\mu t}{r}\right)^{2l}.

(4.12)

The sum over

\mu t/r=\pm i\pi

The large-order behavior of the

and, therefore, the coefficients of the perturbative expansion diverge as

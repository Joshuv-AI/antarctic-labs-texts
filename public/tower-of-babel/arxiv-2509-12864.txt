Higher Abelian Quantum Double Models
Jorge Acuña Flores, Giuseppe De Nittis, Javier Lorca Espiro

Abstract
. This paper focuses on the generalized version of the quantum double model on arbitrary N N -dimensional simplicial complexes with finite local regularity. The core of our analysis is a detailed characterization of the frustration-free ground state space FG QDM ​ ( 𝔄 ) \mathrm{FG}_{\mathrm{QDM}}(\mathfrak{A}) . A central result is the construction of the algebra of logical operators 𝔄 log := 𝔎 ′ / 𝔍 \mathfrak{A}_{\mathrm{log}}:=\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}/\mathfrak{J} , where the redundancy ideal 𝔍 \mathfrak{J} quotients out operators that act trivially on the ground state space. We prove a homeomorphism between the state space of 𝔄 log \mathfrak{A}_{\mathrm{log}} and FG QDM ​ ( 𝔄 ) \mathrm{FG}_{\mathrm{QDM}}(\mathfrak{A}) , effectively classifying all frustration-free ground states. This logical algebra is shown to exhibit generalized Canonical Commutation Relations (CCR). When the relevant (co)homology groups are finite, 𝔄 log \mathfrak{A}_{\mathrm{log}} is isomorphic to C ⁡ ( X c ) ⊗ ℬ ⁡ ( 𝔥 q ) C(X_{c})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{h}_{q}) , revealing that the ground state space can encode c c classical bits and q q quantum bits (qubits), providing a precise measure of its information storage capacity.



1.
Introduction

Topological phases of matter have emerged as a central paradigm in modern condensed matter physics, offering a rich landscape of phenomena that go beyond the traditional Landau-Ginzburg framework of spontaneous symmetry breaking. These phases are characterized by robust topological properties, such as ground-state degeneracy dependent on the system’s geometry and the presence of anyonic excitations with non-trivial braiding statistics (see, for instance,
[
15
]
,
[
10
]
, and references therein). Among the most prominent models exhibiting topological order is Kitaev’s toric code
[
14
]
, a two-dimensional lattice system with spins residing on the links and a Hamiltonian composed of mutually commuting
star
and
plaquette
operators. The toric code has a fourfold degenerate ground state manifold, protected against local perturbations by a spectral gap, and its excitations are abelian anyons. While these anyons are not sufficient for universal quantum computation, the model provides a paradigmatic example of topological protection and anyonic statistics, and its simplicity and exact solvability make it a valuable tool for exploring topological quantum memory.

The toric code has a well-known family of generalizations called quantum double models (QDMs)
[
7
,
2
]
. These extend the framework from spin systems to discrete gauge theories with finite gauge groups and support anyonic excitations that can be used for fault-tolerant quantum computation. However, standard QDMs usually require planar qubit layouts and exhibit a vanishing encoding rate as the code distance increases, limiting their scalability.

Going beyond the restrictions of surface codes is a significant theoretical challenge. One promising approach involves models with generalized symmetries, particularly
higher gauge models
. Among them, models constructed via homological and cohomological techniques
[
6
]
preserve key topological features of QDMs, such as ground state degeneracy and entanglement properties, while allowing definitions on arbitrary compact
n
-dimensional manifolds. In the
N
-dimensional abelian case,
[
23
]
shows that the structure of frustration-free ground states is governed by the homology and locally finite cohomology of the underlying CW-complex, forming a logical algebra capable of encoding classical and quantum information, and that localized excitations are classified by homology and cohomology at infinity. A crucial difference with Vrana’s work is that we incorporate relations between different gauge levels (higher gauge structure), which prevents the decomposition of the Hamiltonian into independent terms per dimension. This necessitates the use of Brown homology and cohomology, as in
[
6
]
, to rigorously describe the ground state space. In the case of a compact manifold, the classification of the ground state subspace has been carried out from a more geometrical approach in
[
16
]
, while a rigorous understanding of its relation to the excited states has yet to be established. This work represents a first step in that direction.

In this paper, we provide a unified and rigorous
C^{*}
-algebraic framework for analyzing topological order and quantum memory in higher abelian quantum double models (HA-QDMs). We extend the constructions of
[
6
]
to infinite
N
-dimensional simplicial complexes in a way that guarantees stability, in the spirit of the results of
[
23
]
in
N
dimensions, while accommodating the inter-level gauge relations absent in previous works. The paper is organized as follows: Section
2
introduces the geometric and algebraic foundations of HA-QDMs, including the construction of the operator algebra of observables and the precise definition of the Hamiltonian. Section
3
characterizes the frustration-free ground states using
C^{*}
-algebra techniques and classifies them via homology and cohomology groups. We also discuss implications for quantum computation and error correction. Overall, the results establish a robust algebraic foundation for understanding topological order and quantum memory in higher dimensions.

2.1.
Geometric and algebraic data

Let

We will say that
\mathcal{K}
is a simplicial

\mathcal{K}_{n}=\emptyset

n>N
and
N
is referred as the
dimension
of the complex. We define the
order
of a simplex
x\in\mathcal{K}_{n}
as the number of simplices

that contain
x
as a face. We say that the simplicial complex
\mathcal{K}
is
L

x
does not exceed

Let

(\mathcal{C}_{\bullet},\partial_{\bullet}^{\mathcal{C}})

the associated chain complex. We will refer to
(\mathcal{C}_{\bullet},\partial_{\bullet}^{\mathcal{C}})

.
We also define a second chain complex

where

\partial_{j}^{\mathbb{G}}\circ\partial_{j+1}^{\mathbb{G}}=0
.
We also need the compatibility conditions
\mathbb{G}_{j}=0
if

or if

.
We will call

the

.

Definition 2

(Cohomology)
.

Let
\delta^{p}:{\rm Hom}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{p}\to{\rm Hom}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{p+1}
be the map defined by

(\delta^{p}t)_{n}\;:=\;t_{n-1}\circ\partial_{n}^{\mathcal{C}}-(-1)^{p}\partial_{n-p}^{\mathbb{G}}\circ t_{n}\;.

It results that
({\rm Hom}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{\bullet},\delta^{\bullet})
is a cochain complex, namely

(1)

\displaystyle\cdots\xleftarrow{}\;{\rm Hom}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{p+2}\;\xleftarrow{\delta^{p+1}}\;{\rm Hom}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{p+1}\;\xleftarrow{\delta^{p}}\;{\rm Hom}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{p}\;\xleftarrow{}\cdots\quad,

such that
\delta^{p+1}\circ\delta^{p}=0
. We will denote with

H^{p}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G}):={\rm Ker}{(\delta^{p})}/{\rm Im}{(\delta^{p-1})}
the associated cohomology groups.

We will also need to consider the subsets
{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{p}
consisting of all
compactly supported

p
-maps
t
such that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
it holds that
t_{n}(x)=0
except for a finite number of
x\in\mathcal{K}_{n}
. The set of
x\in\mathcal{K}
such that
t_{n}(x)\neq 0
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

will be called the
support
of
t
.
Evidently
{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{p}\subset{\rm Hom}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{p}
and one can restrict the map
\delta^{p}
to this subspace. It results that
({\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{p},\delta^{\bullet})
is a cochain complex

(2)

\displaystyle\cdots\;\xleftarrow{}\;{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{p+2}\;\xleftarrow{\delta^{p+1}}\;{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{p+1}\;\xleftarrow{\delta^{p}}\;{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{p}\;\xleftarrow{}\cdots\;\quad\;,

and the associated
finite
cohomology groups are defined as usually as
\mathring{H}^{p}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G}):={\rm Ker}(\delta^{p})/{\rm Im}(\delta^{p-1})
.

In the relevant case where
\mathcal{K}
is finite (this is the case for topological quantum computation
[
19
]
) one has the coincidence
\mathring{H}{p}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})=H^{p}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})
.
In this situation, as a consequence of an important result by Brown in
[
5
]
, one gets the isomorphisms

(3)

H^{p}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})\;\simeq\;\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}H^{n}(\mathcal{C},H_{n-p}(\mathbb{G}))\;,\qquad p\in\mathbb{Z}\;,

where
H_{\bullet}(\mathbb{G})
denotes the homology of
\mathbb{G}
and
\prod
is the direct product.
A detailed proof of
this result can be found in
[
6
, Appendix B]
.

The isomorphism (
3
) highlights a powerful way to compute the cohomology
H^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})
once one knows how to handle the groups
H^{n}(\mathcal{C},H_{j}(\mathbb{G}))
. The latter can be computed by using the
Universal Coefficient Theorem
[
12
, Theorem 3.2]
which provides

(4)

H^{n}(\mathcal{C},H_{j}(\mathbb{G}))\;\simeq\;{\rm Hom}(H_{n}(\mathcal{C}),H_{j}(\mathbb{G}))\;\oplus\;{\rm Ext}^{1}(H_{n-1}(\mathcal{C}),H_{j}(\mathbb{G}))

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
j\in\mathbb{Z}
. Here
H_{\bullet}(\mathcal{C})

denotes the homology of the chain complex
\mathcal{C}
(of free abelian groups).

2.2.
Characters and dualization

An important role will be played by the dual of the construction above. For this, first of all, we take the (Pontryagin) dual groups
\widehat{\mathbb{G}}_{j}
consisting of the set of irreducible characters of
\mathbb{G}_{j}
. These are abelian group under point-wise multiplication
[
13
, Exercise 2.7]
, but for convenience we will use the additive notation, namely
\alpha+\beta:=\alpha\cdot\beta
and
-\alpha:=\alpha^{-1}
for
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{G}_{j}
.
Let us recall that for any
g\in\mathbb{G}_{j}
and
\alpha\in\widehat{\mathbb{G}}_{j}
one gets that
\alpha(g)\in\mathbb{U}(1)
where we are identifying

\mathbb{U}(1)
with the set of complex numbers with modulus 1.

The adjoint of the boundary map
\partial_{j}^{\mathbb{G}}
is the map
\widehat{\partial}_{j}^{\mathbb{G}}:\widehat{\mathbb{G}}_{j}\to\widehat{\mathbb{G}}_{j+1}
defined by
\widehat{\partial}_{j}^{\mathbb{G}}(\alpha):=\alpha\circ\partial_{j}^{\mathbb{G}}
. We will denote with
(\widehat{\mathbb{G}}_{\bullet},\widehat{\partial}_{\bullet}^{\mathbb{G}})
the cochain complex associated by dualizing the algebraic data

(\mathbb{G}_{\bullet},\partial_{\bullet}^{\mathbb{G}})
. We need to introduce the dual version of the

p
-maps defined above. For that we need the coboundary operator
\widehat{\partial}^{\mathcal{C}}_{n+1}:\mathcal{C}_{n}\to\mathcal{C}_{n+1}
defined in Appendix
5
.

2.3.
Operator Algebra

In this section, we will define an algebra that contains the relevant observables for our study.
For each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
x\in\mathcal{K}_{n}
consider the (finite dimensional) Hilbert space
\mathfrak{h}_{x}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{G}_{n})\simeq\mathbb{C}^{|\mathbb{G}_{n}|}

endowed with the canonical
orthonormal basis
\{\ket{g}\}_{g\in\mathbb{G}_{n}}
. Let
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{K})
be the collection of finite subsets of
\mathcal{K}
. Let us point out that every element of
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{K})
is a finite collection of vertices, edges, faces, and so on.
For every
\Lambda\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{K})

define

\mathfrak{H}_{\Lambda}\;:=\;\bigotimes_{x\in\Lambda}\mathfrak{h}_{x}

and
\mathfrak{A}_{\Lambda}:=\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{H}_{\Lambda})
the
C^{*}
-algebra of bounded operators on
\mathfrak{H}_{\Lambda}
. If
\Lambda_{1}\subset\Lambda_{2}
there is a natural inclusion
i_{\Lambda_{2},\Lambda_{1}}:\mathfrak{A}_{\Lambda_{1}}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{A}_{\Lambda_{2}}
mapping
A\mapsto A\otimes\mathbb{1}_{\Lambda_{2}\backslash\Lambda_{1}}
. The maps
i_{\Lambda_{2},\Lambda_{1}}
are isometric morphisms so we will abuse notation and identify
i_{\Lambda_{2},\Lambda_{1}}(A)
with
A
. For this net of algebras, one defines the
local
and
quasi-local
algebras of observables as

\mathfrak{A}_{\rm loc}\;:=\;\bigcup_{\Lambda\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{K})}\mathfrak{A}_{\Lambda}\;,\qquad\quad\mathfrak{A}\;:=\;\overline{\mathfrak{A}_{\rm loc}}^{\;\left\|\cdot\right\|}\;,

respectively.
An observable
A\in\mathfrak{A}
is said to be supported on
\Lambda
if
A\in\mathfrak{A}_{\Lambda}
. The smallest set
\Lambda
such that
A\in\mathfrak{A}_{\Lambda}
is called the support of
A
. For
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{h}_{x})
we use the notation
A_{x}
to refer to the corresponding operator acting at the site
x
.
It can be shown that
\mathfrak{A}
is in fact an UHF algebra
[
3
, Example 2.6.12]
.

2.4.
Local operators

The models of interest in this work will be defined through an important pair of building block operators.
For every
x\in\mathcal{K}_{n}\subset\mathcal{K}
consider
\mathfrak{h}_{x}
endowed with its canonical
orthonormal basis
\{\ket{h}\}_{h\in\mathbb{G}_{n}}
.
For
g\in\mathbb{G}_{n}
and
\alpha\in\widehat{\mathbb{G}}_{n}
consider the operators
P_{g}
and
Q_{\alpha}

defined on the orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{h}_{x}

by

P_{g}\ket{h}\;:=\>\ket{g+h}\;,\qquad\quad Q_{\alpha}\ket{h}\;:=\;\alpha(h)\ket{h}

Remark 2

(Weyl matrices)
.

These unitary operators can be seen as a generalization of the usual Pauli matrices
\sigma^{1}
and
\sigma^{3}
acting on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
.
More precisely one can check that elements of the form
P_{g}Q_{\alpha}
with
g\in\mathbb{G}_{n}
and
\alpha\in\widehat{\mathbb{G}}_{n}
, usually called
Weyl matrices
, provide a linear basis for the full matrix algebra

\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{h}_{x})\simeq{\rm Mat}_{|{\mathbb{G}}_{n}|}(\mathbb{C})
. See
[
20
, Lemma 3.2]
.

\blacktriangleleft

In order to generalize and extend the definition of the building block operators above, we need to introduce
certain specific maps that only act non-trivially on one element of the simplicial complex.

Definition 5

(Localized Maps)
.

For every
x\in\mathcal{K}_{n}
,
g\in\mathbb{G}_{n-p}
define
x^{*}_{g}\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{p}
as

x^{*}_{g}(y)\;:=\;\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}g&{\rm if}&x=y\;,\\
0&{\rm if}&x\not=y\;.\\
\end{array}\right.

Analogously, for every
\alpha\in\widehat{\mathbb{G}}_{n-p}
define
x_{*}^{\alpha}\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})_{-p}
as

(x_{*}^{\alpha})(y)\;:=\;\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}\alpha&{\rm if}&x=y\;,\\
0&{\rm if}&x\not=y\;.\\
\end{array}\right.

As the name indicates, the maps defined above are localized around
x\in\mathcal{K}_{n}
, and can be used to define local operators.
First of all, notice that any element
t\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{0}
can be written as a finite sum of
0
-maps of the form
x^{*}_{g_{x}}
where for every
x\in\mathcal{K}_{n}
the
element
g_{x}\in\mathbb{G}_{n}
is defined by
g_{x}:=t(x)
. Then, it turns out that

t\;=\;\sum_{x\in\mathcal{K}}x^{*}_{g_{x}}

and the associated
shift operator
is given by

P_{t}\;:=\;\prod_{x\in\mathcal{K}}P_{x^{*}_{g_{x}}}\;.

Analogously, every
\gamma\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})_{0}
can be written as

\gamma\;=\;\sum_{x\in\mathcal{K}}x_{*}^{\alpha_{x}}

with
\alpha_{x}:=\gamma(x)
. The associated
clock operator
is then given by

Q_{\gamma}\;:=\;\prod_{x\in\mathcal{K}}x_{*}^{\alpha_{x}}\;.

In particular, one has that
P_{0}=Q_{0}=\mathbb{1}
where
0
denotes the trivial homomorphisms.
By a direct computation (see
[
6
, Appendix D]
) one can check the group relations

(8)

P_{t_{1}}P_{t_{2}}\;=\;P_{t_{1}+t_{2}}\;,\qquad\quad Q_{\gamma_{1}}Q_{\gamma_{2}}\;=\;Q_{\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}}

for every
t_{1},t_{2}\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{0}
and every
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})_{0}
. Moreover, the product between a
P_{t}
and a
Q_{\gamma}
is fixed by the Weyl-type
relation

(9)

Q_{\gamma}P_{t}\;=\;\gamma(t)P_{t}Q_{\gamma}

valid for every
t\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{0}
and
\gamma\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})_{0}

with
\gamma(t)
given by (
7
).
In particular, the
P_{t}
and
Q_{\gamma}
are unitary operators.
The following result will be used several times in this work.

Lemma 2

(Linear independent generators)
.

Every
R\in\mathfrak{A}_{\rm loc}
can be expressed in a unique way as finite sum

R\;=\;\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}P_{t_{i}}Q_{\gamma_{i}}

for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
t_{i}\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{0}
,
\gamma_{i}\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})_{0}
and
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
for every
i=1,\ldots,n
.

Proof.

Let
\Lambda
be the support of
R
. Recall that if
x\in\mathcal{K}_{n}
, the operators of the form
P_{g}Q_{\gamma}
with
g\in\mathbb{\mathbb{G}}_{n}
and
\alpha\in\widehat{\mathbb{\mathbb{G}}}_{n}
form a basis for the full matrix algebra

\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{h}_{x})\simeq{\rm Mat}_{|{\mathbb{G}}_{n}|}(\mathbb{C})
(see Remark
2
).
Since the tensor product of these basis elements forms a basis for the tensor product space, the operators
P_{t}Q_{\gamma}
with support contained in
\Lambda
form a basis for
\mathfrak{A}_{\Lambda}
. In particular, we can express
R
in a unique way as a (finite) linear combination of operators in
\mathfrak{A}_{\Lambda}
.
Now, suppose we can express
R
as a finite sum of elements in
\mathfrak{A}_{\rm loc}
in two different ways:

R\;=\;\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}P_{t_{i}}Q_{\gamma_{i}}\;=\;\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}^{\prime}P_{t_{i}}^{\prime}Q_{\gamma_{i}}^{\prime}

Taking
\Lambda^{\prime}
big enough to contain the support of all the
P_{t_{i}}Q_{\gamma_{i}}
, all the
P_{t_{i}}^{\prime}Q_{\gamma_{i}}^{\prime}
, and considering the embedding of all this operators inside
\Lambda^{\prime}
, one has that
R
can be written in
\mathfrak{A}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}
as a linear combination of elements of the basis of
\mathfrak{A}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}
in two different ways. However, this is a contradiction. Therefore, for every
R\in\mathfrak{A}_{\rm loc}
, there is a unique expansion in the base elements
P_{t}Q_{\gamma}
.
∎

In view of the result above, one can say that
P_{t}Q_{\gamma}
provides a set of linearly independent generators for
\mathfrak{A}
.

Using the operators
\delta^{p}
and
\delta_{p}
described in Definitions
2
and
4
, respectively, one can introduce two new families of unitary operators which will be the relevant elements for the construction of the Hamiltonian of the main model.
For every
t\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{-1}
define

A_{t}\;:=\;P_{\delta^{-1}t}\;,

and for every
\gamma\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})_{1}
define

B_{\gamma}:=Q_{\delta_{1}\gamma}\;.

The new pairs of operators
A_{t},B_{\gamma}
have the advantage of “correcting” the commutation rule (
9
) between
P_{t}
and
Q_{\gamma}
.

Lemma 3
.

It holds true that

[A_{t},B_{\gamma}]\;=\;0

for every
t\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{-1}
and every
\gamma\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})_{1}
.

Proof.

Using Eq.(
9
) and Lemma
1
one has

\displaystyle B_{\gamma}A_{t}

\displaystyle=Q_{\delta_{1}\gamma}P_{\delta^{-1}t}

\displaystyle=\delta_{1}\gamma(\delta^{-1}t)P_{\delta^{-1}t}Q_{\delta_{1}\gamma}

\displaystyle=\gamma((\delta^{0}\circ\delta^{-1})t)A_{t}B_{\gamma}

Since
\delta^{0}\circ\delta^{-1}=0
, as expressed by the cochain complex diagram (
2
), one obtains
B_{\gamma}A_{t}=A_{t}B_{\gamma}
.
∎

Definition 6

(Generalized star and plaquette operators)
.

Let
x\in\mathcal{K}_{n}
. Define

A_{x}^{0}\;:=\;\frac{1}{\left|\mathbb{G}_{n+1}\right|}\sum_{g\in\mathbb{G}_{n+1}}A_{x^{*}_{g}}

B_{x}^{0}\;:=\;\frac{1}{\left|\mathbb{G}_{n-1}\right|}\sum_{\gamma\in\widehat{\mathbb{G}}_{n-1}}B_{x_{*}^{\gamma}}

where
|\mathbb{G}_{n}|
is the cardinality of
\mathbb{G}_{n}
.

Lemma 4
.

For every
x\in\mathcal{K}
, the operators
A_{x}^{0}
and
B_{x}^{0}
form a set of commuting orthogonal projections.

Proof.

The commutation follows from Lemma
3
along with the group properties

A_{t_{1}}A_{t_{2}}=A_{t_{1}+t_{2}}
and
B_{\gamma_{1}}B_{\gamma_{2}}=B_{\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}}
that can be deduced directly by (
8
).
A direct computation shows that

\displaystyle A_{x}^{0}A_{x}^{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left|\mathbb{G}_{n+1}\right|^{2}}\sum_{g\in\mathbb{G}_{n+1}}\sum_{h\in\mathbb{G}_{n+1}}A_{x^{*}_{g}}A_{x^{*}_{h}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left|\mathbb{G}_{n+1}\right|^{2}}\sum_{g\in\mathbb{G}_{n+1}}\sum_{h\in\mathbb{G}_{n+1}}A_{x^{*}_{g+h}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left|\mathbb{G}_{n+1}\right|}\sum_{g\in\mathbb{G}_{n+1}}A_{x}^{0}

\displaystyle=A_{x}^{0}

Analogously,
\left(B_{x}^{0}\right)^{2}=B_{x}^{0}
. It remains to show that these operators
are self-adjoint. First, notice that

(A_{x^{*}_{g}})^{*}\;=\;(P_{\delta^{-1}x^{*}_{g}})^{*}\;=\;P_{\delta^{-1}(-x^{*}_{g})}\;.

By the Definition
5
one has that
-x^{*}_{g}=x^{*}_{-g}
, and in turn

(A_{x^{*}_{g}})^{*}\;=\;A_{x^{*}_{-g}}\;.

Since one is summing over all the elements of the group, one gets

\displaystyle\left(A_{x}^{0}\right)^{*}\;

\displaystyle=\;\frac{1}{\left|\mathbb{G}_{n+1}\right|}\sum_{g\in\mathbb{G}_{n+1}}\left(A_{x^{*}_{g}}\right)^{*}

\displaystyle=\;\frac{1}{\left|\mathbb{G}_{n+1}\right|}\sum_{g\in\mathbb{G}_{n+1}}A_{x^{*}_{-g}}

\displaystyle=\;A_{x}^{0}\;.

The same reasoning also shows that
\left(B_{x}^{0}\right)^{*}=B_{x}^{0}
.
∎

The local operators defined above are the higher dimensional generalization of the
star
and
plaquette
operators defined for planar models
[
18
]
. Observe, using Figures
1
,
2
and Definitions
2
,
4
, that the
supports
of
A_{x}^{0}
and
B_{x}^{0}
are given by

\displaystyle{\rm supp}(A_{x}^{0})

\displaystyle:=\;\{y\in\mathcal{K}\;|\;(\delta^{-1}x^{*}_{g})(y)\neq 0\}\;=\;\{x\}\cup\{z\in\mathcal{K}\;|\;\epsilon_{z,x}\neq 0\}

\displaystyle{\rm supp}(B_{x}^{0})

\displaystyle:=\;\{y\in\mathcal{K}\;|\;(\delta_{1}x_{*}^{\gamma})(y)\neq 0\}\;=\;\{x\}\cup\{y\in\mathcal{K}\;|\;\epsilon_{x,y}\neq 0\}

where the number
\epsilon_{z,x}
are define in Appendix
5
.
In particular, the support of
A_{x}^{0}
is given by
x
and its
co-boundary
, while the support of
B_{x}^{0}
is given by
x
and its
boundary
.

2.5.
The HA-QDM Dynamics

As we are working with a spin-like system, we need to specify how local operators interact with each other.
An interaction is a map
\Phi
which associates to each
X\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{K})
a self-adjoint operator
\Phi_{\rm QDM}(X)\in\mathfrak{A}
. The relevant interaction for our model is defined as

\Phi_{\rm QDM}(X)\;:=\;\left\{\begin{array}[]{clr}-A_{x}^{0}&\;\text{if}&X={\rm supp}(A_{x}^{0})\;\;\text{for some $x\in\mathcal{K}$}\;,\\
-B_{x}^{0}&\;\text{if}&X={\rm{\rm supp}(B_{x}^{0})}\;\;\text{for some $x\in\mathcal{K}$}\;,\\
0&&\text{otherwise}\;,\end{array}\right.

and is called
higher Abelian quantum double model
(HA-QDM)
interaction
.
The total energy due to all the interactions in a finite set
\Lambda
is given by the local
HA-QDM Hamiltonian

(10)

H^{\Lambda}_{\rm QDM}\;:=\;\sum_{X\subset\Lambda}\Phi_{\rm QDM}(X)\;=\;-\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathcal{K}\\
{\rm supp}(A_{x}^{0})\subseteq\Lambda\end{subarray}}A_{x}^{0}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\mathcal{K}\\
{\rm supp}(B_{y}^{0})\subseteq\Lambda\end{subarray}}B_{y}^{0}\right)\;.

With this, one can define a derivation
\delta_{\rm QDM}
with dense domain

\mathcal{D}(\delta_{\rm QDM})=\mathfrak{A}_{\rm loc}
.
For a local operator
A\in\mathfrak{A}_{\rm loc}
one has that

(11)

\delta_{\rm QDM}(A)\;:=\;{\rm i}\big[H_{\rm QDM}^{\Lambda},A\big]\;.

Under some specific conditions on the simplicial complex, this derivation turns out to be closable, and its closure generates a dynamics.
For instance if it happens that

(12)

\sup_{x\in\mathcal{K}}\left|{\rm supp}(A_{x}^{0})\right|\;<\;\infty\;,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sup_{x\in\mathcal{K}}\left|{\rm supp}(B_{x}^{0})\right|\;<\;\infty\;,

i.e.
when all the
A_{x}^{0}
and
B_{x}^{0}
have finite support,
then the
[
4
, Theorem 6.2.4]
guaranties that
\delta_{\rm QDM}
is closable and its closure (still denoted with the same symbol) generates a strongly continuous one-parameter group of
*
-automorphisms
\{\tau_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
of
\mathfrak{A}
.
Conditions (
12
) are automatically satisfied in the case of

N
-dimensional
L
-regular
simplicial complexes with finite
N,L\in\mathbb{N}
(see Section
2.1
).
The dynamics generated by the derivation (
11
) will be called the
HA-QDM dynamics
.

2.6.
Frustration-free ground state

Once the dynamics have been defined, the next relevant notion is that of
ground state
. A state is a positive linear functional
\omega:\mathfrak{A}\to\mathbb{C}
normalized according to
\omega(\mathbb{1})=1
. The state space of
\mathfrak{A}
is usually denoted with
{\mathtt{S}}(\mathfrak{A})
. It is a convex space
whose extreme points are called
pure states
. The set of pure states is denoted with
\mathtt{P}(\mathfrak{A})
. Both
\mathtt{S}(\mathfrak{A})
and
\mathtt{P}(\mathfrak{A})
are usually topologized with the
\ast
-weak topology
[
3
, Section 2.3.2]

A
ground state

\omega
(for the dynamics defined by
\delta
) is a state that satisfies the following condition

-{\rm i}\;\omega(A^{*}\delta(A))\;\geqslant\;0\;,\qquad\forall\;A\in\mathfrak{A}_{\rm loc}\;.

From now on we will use the symbol

\mathtt{G}_{\rm QDM}(\mathfrak{A})
to denote the space of ground states for
the HA-QDM dynamics
\delta_{\rm QDM}
described in Section
2.5
. The subset of pure ground states will be denoted by
\mathtt{G}_{\rm QDM}^{\circ}(\mathfrak{A}):=\mathtt{G}_{\rm QDM}(\mathfrak{A})\cap\mathtt{P}(\mathfrak{A})
.

An important role is played by states that are stabilized by all the fundamental operators
A_{x}^{0}
and
B_{x}^{0}
.

Definition 7

(Frustration-free ground state)
.

We say that a ground state
\omega_{0}
(for a given dynamics) is frustration-free if
\omega_{0}(A_{x}^{0})=\omega_{0}(B_{y}^{0})=1
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{K}
.

Remark 3

(GNS representation)
.

Let
(\pi_{0},\mathcal{H}_{0},\psi_{0})
be the corresponding GNS-representation associated to a frustration-free ground state

\omega_{0}
.
Then

\left\langle\psi_{0},\pi_{0}(A_{x}^{0})\psi_{0}\right\rangle\;=\;1\;=\;\left\langle\psi_{0},\pi_{0}(B_{x}^{0})\psi_{0}\right\rangle\;.

By the Lemma
4
,
\pi_{0}(A_{x}^{0})
and
\pi_{0}(B_{x}^{0})
are projectors. Therefore,

\|\psi_{0}-\pi_{0}(A_{x}^{0})\psi_{0}\|^{2}\;=\;\left\langle\psi_{0},(\mathbb{1}-\pi_{0}(A_{x}^{0}))\psi_{0}\right\rangle\;=\;0

showing that
\pi_{0}(A_{x}^{0})\psi_{0}=\psi_{0}
. A similar argument also shows that
\pi_{0}(B_{x}^{0})\psi_{0}=\psi_{0}
. In other words,
\psi_{0}
is an common eigenvector (with eigenvalue 1) for all
\pi_{0}(A_{x}^{0})
and
\pi_{0}(B_{x}^{0})
.

\blacktriangleleft

The set of frustration-free elements of
\mathtt{G}_{\rm QDM}(\mathfrak{A})
will be denoted with
\mathtt{FG}_{\rm QDM}(\mathfrak{A})
and the related pure states with
\mathtt{FG}_{\rm QDM}^{{\circ}}(\mathfrak{A}):=\mathtt{FG}_{\rm QDM}(\mathfrak{A})\cap\mathtt{P}(\mathfrak{A})
.
In Section
3
we will prove that
\mathtt{FG}_{\rm QDM}(\mathfrak{A})\neq\emptyset
, and provide a characterization of them.

3.
Analysis of the space of Frustration-free ground states

With all the machinery developed previously, we are ready to determine and characterize the frustration-free ground states of the model introduced in Section
2.6
. First of all, we will prove in Theorem
1
that these states are in one-to-one correspondence with states of a
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathfrak{A}_{\rm log}
. In this algebra, the states are completely determined by cohomology and homology groups. In this section, we will follow the work
[
23
]
since the results it contains are still valid in our context. However, unlike their setting, we cannot decompose the Hamiltonian described in Section
2.5
into a composition of Hamiltonians supported on disjoint dimensions. This is because our local operators
A_{x}^{0}
and
B_{x}^{0}
act across more than one dimension; see, for example, Figure
6
. Consequently, we must work with the entire Hamiltonian at once. Nevertheless, in a certain sense, the “dimension multiplication” is encoded in the homology and cohomology
through the Theorem
3
. In fact, when
\partial^{\mathbb{G}}_{n}\equiv 0
, one recovers the result of
[
23
]
.

The fundamental principle underlying our approach is closely related to that used in stabilizer-based quantum error correction techniques
[
11
]
. In these methods, a designated set of
stabilizers
, typically commuting observables that define the code space, is first identified. In our case, the stabilizers will be the local operators
A_{x}^{0}
and
B_{x}^{0}
.
Then, one considers the algebra of operators that commute with all elements of this stabilizer set, as these represent logical operations that preserve the code subspace. To capture the operational indistinguishability of such operators on a particular quantum state, we introduce an equivalence relation: two operators are considered equivalent if they produce the same expectation value when evaluated on a relevant state (frustration-free ground state). This construction not only reflects the symmetries of the system but also provides a natural framework for defining observables in scenarios where redundancy or gauge freedom is present.

3.1.
Local characterization of frustration-free ground states

In this section, we will introduce a “small” abelian
C^{*}
-sublagebra

\mathfrak{K}\subset\mathfrak{A}
endowed with a relevant pure state
\vartheta\in\mathtt{P}(\mathfrak{K})
which has the crucial property of determining the elements of
\mathtt{FG}_{\rm QDM}(\mathfrak{A})
by extensions. For these reasons we will refer to
\mathfrak{K}
as the
core

C^{*}
-algebra and to
\vartheta
as the
seed
state of the
HA-QDM dynamics.

To start, let us recall a crucial result that has become foundational in the algebraic formulation of quantum double models. See
[
1
, Section 2.1.1]
and
[
23
, Lemma 6.1]
for the details of the proof.

Lemma 5
.

Let
\omega
be a state in a
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathfrak{A}
. Let
U\in\mathfrak{A}
be a unitary element such that
|\omega(U)|=1
. Then, one has

\omega(U)\omega(Y)\;=\;\omega(UY)\;=\;\omega(YU)

for any
Y\in\mathfrak{A}
.

This result is very useful especially when
\omega(U)=1
because it allows us to “introduce”
U
inside the state,
i.e.

\omega(Y)=\omega(UY)=\omega(YU)
for all
Y\in\mathfrak{A}
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\omega_{0}\in
be a frustration-free ground state according to Definition
7
. Then
\omega_{0}(A_{t}B_{\gamma})=1
for all
t\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{-1}
and
\gamma\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})_{1}
.

Proof.

By definition
\omega_{0}(A_{x}^{0})=\omega_{0}(B_{y}^{0})=1
. Combining this with the inequalities
|\omega_{0}(A_{x_{g}^{*}})|\leqslant 1
and
|\omega_{0}(B_{x_{*}^{\gamma}})|\leqslant 1
due to the fact that the
A_{x_{g}^{*}}
and
B_{x_{*}^{\gamma}}

are unitary,
one gets that
\omega_{0}(A_{x_{g}^{*}})=1=\omega(B_{x_{*}^{\gamma}})
. Using that any operator
A_{t}
can be written as a multiplication of operators of the form
A_{x_{g}^{*}}
and any operator
B_{\gamma}
can be written as a multiplication of operators of the form
B_{x_{*}^{\gamma}}
, and combining with Lemma
5
, one conclude the proof.
∎

The next step is to prove the existence of frustration-free ground states. In particular, we will
show that the condition
\omega_{0}(A_{t}B_{\gamma})=1

is sufficient to guarantee that
\mathtt{FG}_{\rm QDM}(\mathfrak{A})\neq\emptyset
. To see this, first we need to introduce the following
C^{*}
-subalgebra.

Definition 8

(Core
C^{*}
-algebra)
.

Let
\mathfrak{K}:=C^{*}(A_{t},B_{\gamma})
be the abelian
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
\mathfrak{A}
generated by the operators
A_{t}
and
B_{\gamma}
for every
t\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{-1}
and
\gamma\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})_{1}
.

The
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathfrak{K}
contains by construction the generalized star and plaquette operators
A_{x}^{0}
and
B_{x}^{0}
.
Let
\mathfrak{K}_{0}
be the dense subalgebra given by the finite linear combinations of the monomials
A_{t}B_{\gamma}
. One has that

\mathfrak{K}_{0}\subset\mathfrak{A}_{\rm loc}
by construction.

On
\mathfrak{K}
let us introduce a relevant linear functional
\vartheta:\mathfrak{K}\to\mathbb{C}
by imposing the conditions

(13)

\vartheta(A_{t}B_{\gamma})\;=\;1\;,\qquad\forall\;(t,\gamma)\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{-1}\times{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})_{1}

and extending
\vartheta
by linearity on the dense subalgebra
\mathfrak{K}_{0}
, and then by continuity on the
full algebra

\mathfrak{K}
.

Lemma 6

(Seed state)
.

Conditions (
13
) define a unique pure state

\vartheta:\mathfrak{K}\to\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

Consider an increasing sequence
\Lambda_{1}\subset\Lambda_{2}\dots
of finite subsets of
\mathcal{K}
such that their union is equal to
\mathcal{K}
. For
\Lambda_{n}
define the
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathfrak{K}_{n}
as the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by the operators
A_{t}
and
B_{\gamma}
that have their support inside
\Lambda_{n}
and consider
\vartheta_{n}:=\vartheta|_{\mathfrak{K}_{n}}
the linear functional generated by the conditions
(
13
) restricted to
\mathfrak{K}_{n}
.
Since
\mathfrak{K}_{n}
is a finite-dimensional matrix algebra generated by the linearly independent elements
A_{t}B_{\gamma}
with support inside
\Lambda_{n}
, it follows that
\vartheta_{n}
defines a state, and in particular it is continuous.
By the Hahn-Banach theorem
[
3
, Proposition 2.3.24]
, we can extend this state to a state in the full
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathfrak{K}
, and we will denote this state by
\vartheta_{n}^{\sim}
. This gives us a sequence of states
\{\vartheta_{n}^{\sim}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and since the state space of
\mathfrak{K}
is
*
-weak compact, there is a subsequence
\{\vartheta_{n_{l}}^{\sim}\}_{l\in\mathbb{N}}
converging in the
\ast
-weak topology to a state on
\mathfrak{K}
. For
(t,\gamma)\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{-1}\times{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})_{1}
this state maps
A_{t}B_{\gamma}
to 1, because the states
\vartheta_{n}
satisfy
\vartheta_{n}(A_{t}B_{\gamma})=1
for
n
big enough. Therefore, we will denote this state by
\vartheta
.
We have proved that there exists (at least) one state on
\mathfrak{K}

that meets the conditions (
13
). Now, let us prove the uniqueness. To do this, suppose that there exists another state
\vartheta^{\prime}
with this property. Consider
F\in\mathfrak{K}_{0}
and write it as

F\;=\;\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}A_{t_{i}}B_{\gamma_{i}}

then,

\vartheta(F)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=\vartheta^{\prime}(F)

Here, we used Lemma
2
to guarantee that
F
can be written in a unique way as a sum, since
A_{t_{i}}B_{\gamma_{i}}=P_{\delta^{-1}t_{i}}Q_{\delta_{1}\gamma_{i}}
are linearly independent generators.
So,
\vartheta
and
\vartheta^{\prime}
coincide in the dense set
\mathfrak{K}_{0}
. Since there are states, in particular, they are continuous. Therefore,
\vartheta
and
\vartheta^{\prime}
are the same state.
Let us show now that
\vartheta:\mathfrak{K}_{0}\to\mathbb{C}
is multiplicative. To see this, take
F,G\in\mathfrak{K}_{0}
given by

F\;=\;\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}A_{t_{i}}B_{\gamma_{i}}\;,\qquad G\;=\;\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda^{\prime}_{j}A_{t_{j}^{\prime}}B_{{\gamma_{j}^{\prime}}}\;

with
\lambda_{i},\lambda^{\prime}_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
.
Then,

\displaystyle\vartheta(FG)\;

\displaystyle=\;\vartheta\left(\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}A_{t_{i}}B_{\gamma_{i}}\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda^{\prime}_{j}A_{{t_{j}^{\prime}}}B_{{\gamma^{\prime}_{j}}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\;\vartheta\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\lambda^{\prime}_{j}A_{t_{i}+{t_{j}^{\prime}}}B_{\gamma_{j}+{\gamma_{j}^{\prime}}}\right)\;=\;\vartheta\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\lambda^{\prime}_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\;\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\right)\left(\sum_{i=j}^{m}\lambda_{j}^{\prime}\right)\;=\;\vartheta(F)\vartheta(G)\;.

where, in the second equality, one uses crucially the commutativity of the
A_{t}
’s and
B_{\gamma}
’s.
Now, for arbitrary
F,G\in\mathfrak{K}
, consider
(F_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(G_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
F_{n},G_{n}\in\mathfrak{K}_{0}
. Then,

\vartheta(FG)\;=\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\lim_{m\to\infty}\vartheta(F_{n}G_{m})\;=\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\lim_{m\to\infty}\vartheta(G_{m}F_{n})\;=\;\vartheta(GF)\;.

Finally, since it is multiplicative and
\mathfrak{K}
is abelian, it follows that
\vartheta
defines a pure state
[
3
, Corollary 2.3.21]
.
∎

Since
\mathfrak{K}
is a
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
\mathfrak{A}
one can invoke

[
3
, Proposition 2.3.24]
to define extensions of the state
\vartheta
defined by (
13
) to the full algebra
\mathfrak{A}
. Interestingly,
any state resulting from such extension is a frustration-free ground state for the HA-QDM dynamics described in Section
2.5
.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\omega_{0}
be any extension of the seed state
\vartheta
defined by (
13
). Then
\omega_{0}\in\mathtt{FG}_{\rm QDM}(\mathfrak{A})
is a frustration-free ground state for the HA-QDM dynamics.

Proof.

The conditions
\omega_{0}(A_{t}B_{\gamma})=\vartheta(A_{t}B_{\gamma})=1
for every
t\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{-1}
and
\gamma\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})_{1}
imply after a simple calculation that the frustration-free condition. Therefore, it only remains to prove that
\omega_{0}
is a ground state. For that let
A\in\mathfrak{A}_{\Lambda}
. Then

\displaystyle-{\rm i}\omega_{0}(A^{*}\delta_{\rm QDM}(A))\;

\displaystyle=\;-{\rm i}\omega_{0}(A^{*}{\rm i}[H_{\rm QDM}^{\Lambda},A])

\displaystyle=\;\omega_{0}(A^{*}H_{\rm QDM}^{\Lambda}A-A^{*}AH_{\rm QDM}^{\Lambda})

\displaystyle=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathcal{K}\\
{\rm supp}(A_{x}^{0})\subseteq\Lambda\end{subarray}}\left(\omega_{0}\left(A^{*}AA_{x}^{0}\right)-\omega_{0}\left(A^{*}A_{x}^{0}A\right)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathcal{K}\\
{\rm supp}(B_{x}^{0})\subseteq\Lambda\end{subarray}}\left(\omega_{0}\left(A^{*}AB_{x}^{0}\right)-\omega_{0}\left(A^{*}B_{x}^{0}A\right)\right)\;.

By using Lemma (
5
), one gets

\displaystyle-{\rm i}\omega_{0}(A^{*}\delta_{\rm QDM}(A))\;

\displaystyle=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathcal{K}\\
{\rm supp}(A_{x}^{0})\subseteq\Lambda\end{subarray}}\left(\omega_{0}\left(A^{*}A\right)-\omega_{0}\left(A^{*}A_{x}^{0}A\right)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathcal{K}\\
{\rm supp}(B_{x}^{0})\subseteq\Lambda\end{subarray}}\left(\omega_{0}\left(A^{*}A\right)-\omega_{0}\left(A^{*}B_{x}^{0}A\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathcal{K}\\
{\rm supp}(A_{x}^{0})\subseteq\Lambda\end{subarray}}\omega_{0}\left(A^{*}\left(\mathbb{1}-A_{x}^{0}\right)A\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathcal{K}\\
{\rm supp}(B_{x}^{0})\subseteq\Lambda\end{subarray}}\omega_{0}\left(A^{*}\left(\mathbb{1}-B_{x}^{0}\right)A\right)\;.

Since
A_{0}^{x}
is an orthogonal projection, one has that

\omega_{0}(A^{*}(\mathbb{1}-A_{0}^{x})A)\;=\;\omega_{0}(A^{*}(\mathbb{1}-A_{x}^{0})^{*}(\mathbb{1}-A_{x}^{0})A)\;\geqslant\;0

Analogously, one has that
\omega_{0}(A^{*}({\mathbb{1}-B_{x}^{0}})A)\geqslant 0
.
In conclusion, one obtains the inequality
-{\rm i}\omega_{0}(A^{*}\delta_{\rm QDM}(A))\geqslant 0
as a sum of positive elements. This shows that
\omega_{0}
meets the condition for the ground state of the HA-QDM dynamics.
∎

3.2.
Characterization of the space of frustration-free ground states

In this section, we will introduce a middle abelian
C^{*}
-algebra

\mathfrak{K}\subset\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}\subset\mathfrak{A}

which has the role of adding the necessary information
for differentiating between different extensions of the seed state
\vartheta
. More precisely, will we see that any
\tau\in\mathtt{S}(\mathfrak{K}^{\prime})
such that
\tau|_{\mathfrak{K}}=\vartheta

fix a unique
\omega_{0,\tau}\in\mathtt{FG}_{\rm QDM}(\mathfrak{A})
by extension. For this reason,
\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}
will be called the
fixing

C^{*}
-algebra.

Let
\omega_{0}\in\mathtt{FG}_{\rm QDM}(\mathfrak{A})
be a given frustration-free ground state of the HA-QDM dynamics. We are interested here in analyzing how
\omega_{0}
acts on the monomial

P_{t}Q_{\gamma}

with
t\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{0}
and
\gamma\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})_{0}
.

First of all, it is worth observing that in view of Corollary
1
on has that
\omega_{0}(P_{\delta^{-1}t^{\prime}}Q_{\delta_{1}\gamma^{\prime}})=\omega_{0}(A_{t^{\prime}}B_{\gamma^{\prime}})=1

for every
t^{\prime}\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{-1}
and
\gamma^{\prime}\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})_{1}
. The one gets

(14)

\omega_{0}(P_{t}Q_{\gamma})\;=\;1\qquad\forall\;(t,\gamma)\in{\rm Im}{(\delta^{-1})}\times{\rm Im}{(\delta_{1})}\;.

Now, let
s\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{-1}

Using Lemma (
5
) and the commutation relations (
8
) and (
8
), one gets

\displaystyle\omega_{0}(P_{t}Q_{\gamma})\;

\displaystyle=\;\omega_{0}(A_{s})\omega_{0}(P_{t}Q_{\gamma})\omega_{0}(A_{-s})\;=\;\omega_{0}(A_{s}P_{t}Q_{\gamma}A_{-s})

\displaystyle=\;\omega_{0}(P_{\delta^{-1}s}P_{t}Q_{\gamma}P_{-\delta^{-1}s})\;=\;\gamma(-\delta^{-1}s)\omega_{0}(P_{t}Q_{\gamma})

\displaystyle=(\delta_{0}\gamma)(-s)\omega_{0}(P_{t}Q_{\gamma})

where the last equality is implied by the duality in Lemma
1
. If
\gamma\notin{\rm Ker}(\delta_{0})

then
(\delta_{0}\gamma)(s)\neq 1
for some
s\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{-1}
. Since the equality above must be valid for every
s\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{-1}
one infers that
\omega_{0}(P_{t}Q_{\gamma})=0
if
\gamma\notin{\rm Ker}(\delta_{0})
.
By replacing in the computation above
A_{s}
with
B_{\nu}
, for some
\nu\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})_{1}
, one ends with the equality

\omega_{0}(P_{t}Q_{\gamma})=\nu(\delta^{0}t)\omega_{0}(P_{t}Q_{\gamma})
.
With the same argument above one gets
\omega_{0}(P_{t}Q_{\gamma})=0
if
t\notin{\rm Ker}(\delta^{0})
. To sum up one has that

(15)

\omega_{0}(P_{t}Q_{\gamma})\;=\;0\qquad{\rm if}\;\;t\in{\rm supp}(\delta^{0})\;\;\text{or}\;\;\gamma\in{\rm supp}(\delta_{0})\;,

where
{\rm supp}(\delta^{0}):={\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{0}\setminus{\rm Ker}(\delta^{0})

and
{\rm supp}(\delta_{0}):={\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})_{0}\setminus{\rm Ker}(\delta_{0})
.
Furthermore, using again Lemma (
5
), one ends with

(16)

\omega_{0}(P_{t}Q_{\gamma})\;=\;\omega_{0}(A_{s}P_{t}Q_{\gamma}B_{\nu})\;=\;\omega_{0}(P_{t+\delta^{-1}s}Q_{\gamma+\delta_{1}\nu})\;.

This shows that the values of
\omega_{0}(P_{t}Q_{\gamma})
depends only on

t\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{0}
modulo
{\rm Im}{(\delta^{-1})}
,
and on
\gamma\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})_{0}
modulo
{\rm Im}{(\delta_{1})}
.

The analysis above fixes many of the values of
\omega_{0}(P_{t}Q_{\gamma})
but one still has a certain degree of freedom to completely specify
\omega_{0}
. More precisely to fix
\omega_{0}
one has to know the numbers

(17)

c_{t,\gamma}\;:=\;\omega_{0}(P_{t}Q_{\gamma})\;,\quad\forall\;(t,\gamma)\in\big({\rm Ker}(\delta^{0})\times{\rm Ker}(\delta_{0})\big)\setminus\big({\rm Im}{(\delta^{-1})}\times{\rm Im}{(\delta_{1})}\big)\;.

In the following, we will describe how to parametrize the freedom provided by the latter conditions.

It has emerged that a special role is played by the set of operators

\mathfrak{Z}\;:=\;\left\{P_{t}Q_{\gamma}\;\middle|\;(t,\gamma)\in{\rm Ker}(\delta^{0})\times{\rm Ker}(\delta_{0})\right\}\;\subset\;\mathfrak{A}_{\rm loc}\;.

We will refer to
\mathfrak{Z}
as the set of
units
for the
frustration-free ground states of the HA-QDM dynamics.

There is a deep relationship between the
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathfrak{K}
in Definition
8
and set

\mathfrak{Z}
. Let
\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}
be the
commutant
of
\mathfrak{K}
,
i.e.
the set of all operators in
\mathfrak{A}
that commute with every element of
\mathfrak{K}
.
Since
\mathfrak{K}
is abelian one has
\mathfrak{K}\subseteq\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}
.
As before, it is only necessary to consider the elements that have finite support.

Lemma 7
.

It holds true that

\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}\;=\;\overline{\mathfrak{A}_{\rm loc}\cap\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}}^{\;\|\;\|}\;.

Proof.

Given an element
A\in\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}
, we will construct a sequence of operators in
\mathfrak{A}_{\rm loc}\cap\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}
that converges to
A
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By density of the local operators, there exists a
A_{n}\in\mathfrak{A}_{\rm loc}
such that

\left\|A-A_{n}\right\|\;<\;\frac{1}{n}\;.

Denote by
\Lambda
the support of
A_{n}
and consider the equivalent relation
\sim
on
{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{-1}
by
r\sim s
if
(\delta^{-1}s)|_{\Lambda}=(\delta^{-1}r)|_{\Lambda}
. Also consider the equivalent relation
\sim
on
{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})_{1}
by
\alpha\sim\beta
if
(\delta_{1}\alpha)|_{\Lambda}=(\delta_{1}\beta)|_{\Lambda}
. With this, we define

A_{n}^{\prime}\;:=\;\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}_{n}}\sum_{([s],[\alpha])\in\mathcal{I}_{n}}A_{s}B_{\alpha}A_{n}B_{-\alpha}A_{-s}

where
\mathcal{I}_{n}={\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{-1}/\sim\times{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})_{1}/\sim
and
\mathcal{N}_{n}:=|\mathcal{I}_{n}|
its cardinality.

Note that
A_{n}^{\prime}
is independent of the representative of the different classes. To see this, notice that
A_{n}^{\prime}|_{\Lambda^{c}}=\mathbb{1}
and on
\Lambda
we have that
(A_{s}B_{\alpha}A_{n}B_{-\alpha}A_{-s})|_{\Lambda}=(A_{r}B_{\beta}A_{n}B_{-\beta}A_{-r})_{\Lambda}
by the construction of the equivalent relations.

One also has that
A_{n}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{A}_{\rm loc}
since it is a finite sum of local elements. Also, this operator commutes with all the monomials
A_{r}B_{\beta}
.
In fact, a direct computation shows that

\displaystyle A_{r}B_{\beta}A_{n}^{\prime}B_{-\beta}A_{-r}\;

\displaystyle=\;\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}_{n}}\sum_{([s],[\alpha])\in\mathcal{I}_{n}}A_{r}B_{\beta}A_{s}B_{\alpha}A_{n}B_{-\alpha}A_{-s}B_{-\beta}A_{-r}

\displaystyle=\;\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}_{n}}\sum_{([s],[\alpha])\in\mathcal{I}_{n}}A_{r+s}B_{\alpha+\beta}A_{n}B_{-\alpha-\beta}A_{-r-s}

where one uses the commutativity between
A_{\pm r}
and
B_{\pm\alpha}
. Introducing the new variables

s^{\prime}:=s+r
and
\alpha^{\prime}:=\alpha+\beta
one gets

\displaystyle A_{r}B_{\beta}A_{n}^{\prime}B_{-\beta}A_{-r}\;

\displaystyle=\;\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}_{n}}\sum_{([s^{\prime}-r],[\alpha^{\prime}-\beta])\in\mathcal{I}_{n}}A_{s^{\prime}}B_{\alpha^{\prime}}A_{n}B_{-\alpha^{\prime}}A_{-s^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\>\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}_{n}}\sum_{([s^{\prime}],[\alpha^{\prime}])\in\mathcal{I}_{n}}A_{s^{\prime}}B_{\alpha^{\prime}}A_{n}B_{-\alpha^{\prime}}A_{-s^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\;A_{n}^{\prime}

where the second equality is justified by the fact that we are adding all the possible equivalent classes.
By a density argument, one infers that

A_{n}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{A}_{\rm loc}\cap\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}
. Now, using the assumption
A\in\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}
one can write

\displaystyle\left\|A_{n}^{\prime}-A\right\|\;

\displaystyle=\;\left\|A_{n}^{\prime}-\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}_{n}}\sum_{([s],[\alpha])\in\mathcal{I}_{n}}A_{s}B_{\alpha}AB_{-\alpha}A_{-s}\right\|

\displaystyle=\;\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}_{n}}\left\|\sum_{([s],[\alpha])\in\mathcal{I}_{n}}A_{s}B_{\alpha}(A_{n}-A)B_{-\alpha}A_{-s}\right\|

\displaystyle\leqslant\;\left\|A_{n}-A\right\|\;<\;\frac{1}{n}

where the first inequality is obtained by the triangle inequality. Therefore,
A_{n}^{\prime}
provides the desired sequence converging to
A
.
∎

We will refer to
\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}
as the fixing
C^{*}
-algebra
for the HA-QDM dynamics for reasons that will be clarified below.
Let
\mathrm{span}(\mathfrak{Z})
be the set of finite linear combinations of elements of
\mathfrak{Z}
.

Lemma 8
.

It holds true that

\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}\;=\;\overline{\mathrm{span}(\mathfrak{Z})}^{\;\|\;\|}\;.

Proof.

Let
P_{t}Q_{\gamma}\in\mathfrak{Z}
and
A_{s}\in\mathfrak{K}
with
s\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{-1}
. Then

(18)

\displaystyle(P_{t}Q_{\gamma})A_{s}

\displaystyle=\;P_{t}Q_{\gamma}P_{\delta^{-1}s}\;=\;\gamma(\delta^{-1}s)P_{t}P_{\delta^{-1}s}Q_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\;(\delta_{0}\gamma)(s)P_{\delta^{-1}s}(P_{t}Q_{\gamma})\;=\;A_{s}(P_{t}Q_{\gamma})\;

where
(\delta_{0}\gamma)(s)=1
for every
s
in view of the condition
\gamma\in{\rm Ker}(\delta_{0})
. Therefore,
P_{t}Q_{\gamma}
commutes with any
A_{s}
. In a similar way let
B_{\nu}\in\mathfrak{K}
with
\nu\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})_{1}
. Then

(19)

\displaystyle(P_{t}Q_{\gamma})B_{\nu}

\displaystyle=\;P_{t}Q_{\gamma}Q_{\delta_{1}\nu}\;=\;P_{t}Q_{\delta_{1}\nu}Q_{\gamma}\;=\;(\delta_{1}\nu)(t)Q_{\delta_{1}\nu}(P_{t}Q_{\gamma})

\displaystyle=\;\nu(\delta^{0}t)B_{\nu}(P_{t}Q_{\gamma})\;=\;B_{\nu}(P_{t}Q_{\gamma})

where again
\nu(\delta^{0}t)=1
for every
\nu
since

t\in{\rm Ker}(\delta^{0})
. Then,
P_{t}Q_{\gamma}
also commutes with any
B_{\nu}
. Then
P_{t}Q_{\gamma}
commutes with any element in
\mathfrak{K}_{0}
. Let
T\in\mathfrak{K}
and
\{T_{n}\}\subset\mathfrak{K}_{0}
such that
T_{n}\to T
in norm. Then
[T,P_{t}Q_{\gamma}]=[T-T_{n},P_{t}Q_{\gamma}]

which implies that
\|[T,P_{t}Q_{\gamma}]\|\leqslant 2\|T-T_{n}\|
. Since the right-hand side can be chosen arbitrarily small, one concludes that

[T,P_{t}Q_{\gamma}]=0
.
Therefore
P_{t}Q_{\gamma}\in\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}
and for the arbitrariness
of
t
and
\gamma
one concludes that
\mathfrak{Z}\subseteq\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}
. Since
\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}
is closed under linear combinations and convergence in norm, one ends with

\overline{\mathrm{span}(\mathfrak{Z})}^{\;\|\;\|}\;\subseteq\;\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}\;.

For the opposite inclusion, consider a finite sum

R\;=\;\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}P_{t_{i}}Q_{\gamma_{i}}\;\in\;\mathfrak{A}_{\rm loc}\cap\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}

with
t_{i}\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{0}
and
\gamma_{i}\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})_{0}
for every
i=1,\ldots,n
. The condition
R\in\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}
implies the commutation relations
[R,A_{s}]=0=[R,B_{\nu}]

for every
s\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{-1}
and
\nu\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})_{1}
. The first of these conditions provides

0\;=\;[R,A_{s}]\;=\;\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}[P_{t_{i}}Q_{\gamma_{i}},A_{s}]\;=\;\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}((\delta_{0}\gamma_{i})(s)-1)P_{t_{i}+\delta^{-1}s}Q_{\gamma_{i}}

after a computation similar to (
18
). Since the monomials

P_{t_{i}+\delta^{-1}s}Q_{\gamma_{i}}
are linearly independents generators of
\mathfrak{A}_{\rm loc}
(see Lemma
2
) and the
\lambda_{i}\neq 0
one concludes that
(\delta_{0}\gamma_{i})(s)=1
. Since this should be valid for every
s
one ends with
\{\gamma_{i}\}_{i=1,\ldots,n}\subset{\rm Ker}(\delta_{0})
. Similarly one has that

0\;=\;[R,B_{\nu}]\;=\;\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}[P_{t_{i}}Q_{\gamma_{i}},B_{\nu}]\;=\;\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(\nu(\delta^{0}t_{i})-1)P_{t_{i}}Q_{\gamma_{i}+\delta_{1}\nu}

following the same computation as in (
19
). Again one obtains
\nu(\delta^{0}t_{i})=1
for every
\nu
which implies that

\{t_{i}\}_{i=1,\ldots,n}\subset{\rm Ker}(\delta^{0})
. Then, one concludes that
\mathfrak{A}_{\rm loc}\cap\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathrm{span}(\mathfrak{Z})
which implies

\overline{\mathrm{span}(\mathfrak{Z})}^{\;\|\;\|}\;\supseteq\;\overline{\mathfrak{A}_{\rm loc}\cap\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}}^{\;\|\;\|}\;=\;\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}\;.

For the last equality, one uses Lemma
7
.
∎

The next result describes the main role of the
C^{*}
-algebra

\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}
in the study of
\mathtt{FG}_{\rm QDM}(\mathfrak{A})
,
and justify the name of fixing
C^{*}
-algebra for it.

Corollary 2
.

Let
\mathtt{S}_{\vartheta}(\mathfrak{K}^{\prime})\subset\mathtt{S}(\mathfrak{K}^{\prime})
be the subset of states
\tau
in
\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}
such that
\tau|_{\mathfrak{K}}=\vartheta
. Then, there is a bijection

\mathtt{FG}_{\rm QDM}(\mathfrak{A})\simeq\mathtt{S}_{\vartheta}(\mathfrak{K}^{\prime})
.

Proof.

First, notice that a state
\tau
in
\mathtt{S}_{\vartheta}(\mathfrak{K}^{\prime})
that satisfies
\tau|_{\mathfrak{K}}=\vartheta
can be extended to a frustration free ground state
\omega_{0}
in
\mathtt{FG}_{\rm QDM}(\mathfrak{A})
by the Proposition
2
. Using Eq.(
17
) we see that this extension is completely determined by the values it takes on operators of the form
P_{t}Q_{\gamma}
with
(t,\gamma)\in{\rm Ker}(\delta^{0})\times{\rm Ker}(\delta_{0})
. But Lemma
8
tells us that these operators generate
\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}
. Therefore, the values on operators
P_{t}Q_{\gamma}
are already known, and we conclude that

(20)

\omega_{0}(P_{t}Q_{\gamma})\;=\;\left\{\begin{aligned} &\tau(P_{t}Q_{\gamma})&&{\rm if}\;\;P_{t}Q_{\gamma}\in\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}\;,\\
&0&&{\rm if}\;\;P_{t}Q_{\gamma}\in\mathfrak{A}\setminus\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}\;.\end{aligned}\right.

So, one has that
\tau
can be extended to a unique frustration-free ground state on
\mathfrak{A}
.
On the other hand, let
\omega_{0}
be a frustration-free ground state on
\mathfrak{A}
. Taking
\tau:=\omega_{0}|_{\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}}
, this provides a state in
\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}
such that
\tau|_{\mathfrak{K}}=\vartheta
. Moreover, its extension to
\mathfrak{A}
is equal to
\omega_{0}
.
Therefore, there is a bijection between
\mathtt{FG}_{\rm QDM}(\mathfrak{A})
and
\mathtt{S}_{\vartheta}(\mathfrak{K}^{\prime})
.
∎

3.3.
The
C^{*}
-algebra of logical operators

Since the
C^{*}
-subalgebra
\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}
contains sufficient information for the specification of the frustration-free ground states of the
HA-QDM dynamics. However
\mathtt{S}(\mathfrak{K}^{\prime})

also contains
excited states
which do not respect the ground state constraints. To “ eliminate” these excited states, one has to
trivialize the information that can define excited states. This can be done by selecting an ideal

\mathfrak{J}\subset\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}
(referred as the
redundancy
ideal) with the crucial property that
\omega_{0}:\mathfrak{J}\to\{0\}

for every
\omega_{0}\in\mathtt{FG}_{\rm QDM}(\mathfrak{A})
. As a consequence, the quotient
\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}/\mathfrak{J}
contain all the
information necessary to specify the frustration-free ground states without the finer information to distinguish excited states.

The
redundancy
space is defined by

(21)

\mathfrak{J}\;:=\;\overline{{\rm span}\{P_{t}Q_{\gamma}-P_{t^{\prime}}Q_{\gamma^{\prime}}\;|\;t\sim t^{\prime}\;,\;\gamma\sim\gamma^{\prime}\}}^{\;\|\;\|}

where
t\sim t^{\prime}
means that
t,t^{\prime}\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{0}
are representatives of the same cohomology class,
i
.e
[t]=[t^{\prime}]\in\mathring{H}^{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})
. Similarly,
\gamma\sim\gamma^{\prime}
means that

[\gamma]=[\gamma^{\prime}]\in\mathring{H}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})
.
More explicitly
\mathfrak{J}
is generated by linear combinations of elements of the form
P_{t}Q_{\gamma}-P_{t+\delta^{-1}s}Q_{\gamma+\delta_{1}\nu}
for
(t,\gamma)\in{\rm Ker}(\delta^{0})\times{\rm Ker}(\delta_{0})
,
s\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{-1}
and
\nu\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})_{1}
.

Lemma 9
.

The space
\mathfrak{J}
is a self-adjoint bilateral ideal in
\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

First of all
\mathfrak{J}
is by definition a linear space
closed under the operation of taking the adjoint. Let
P_{r}Q_{\sigma}\in\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}
(which means
(r,\sigma)\in{\rm Ker}(\delta^{0})\times{\rm Ker}(\delta_{0})
)
and observe that

P_{r}Q_{\sigma}\left(P_{t}Q_{\gamma}-P_{t^{\prime}}Q_{\gamma^{\prime}}\right)\;=\;\sigma(t)P_{r+t}Q_{\sigma+\gamma}-\sigma(t^{\prime})P_{r+t^{\prime}}Q_{\sigma+\gamma^{\prime}}\;.

If
t\sim t^{\prime}
then
\sigma(t^{\prime})=\sigma(t+\delta^{-1}s)=\sigma(t)(\delta_{0}\sigma)(s)=\sigma(t)
and in turn

P_{r}Q_{\sigma}\left(P_{t}Q_{\gamma}-P_{t^{\prime}}Q_{\gamma^{\prime}}\right)\;=\;\sigma(t)(P_{r+t}Q_{\sigma+\gamma}-P_{r+t^{\prime}}Q_{\sigma+\gamma^{\prime}})\;.

If also
\gamma\sim\gamma^{\prime}
one gets that
r+t\sim r+t^{\prime}
and
\sigma+\gamma\sim\sigma+\gamma^{\prime}
and one concludes that

\left(P_{t}Q_{\gamma}-P_{t^{\prime}}Q_{\gamma^{\prime}}\right)\in\mathfrak{J}\quad\Rightarrow\quad P_{r}Q_{\sigma}\left(P_{t}Q_{\gamma}-P_{t^{\prime}}Q_{\gamma^{\prime}}\right)\in\mathfrak{J}\;.

Let
\mathfrak{J}_{0}:={{\rm span}\{P_{t}Q_{\gamma}-P_{t^{\prime}}Q_{\gamma^{\prime}}\;|\;t\sim t^{\prime}\;,\;\gamma\sim\gamma^{\prime}\}}
and
J\in\mathfrak{J}_{0}
.
Then by linearity the argument above shows that
P_{r}Q_{\sigma}J\in\mathfrak{J}_{0}
. Now let
J\in\mathfrak{J}
and
\{J_{n}\}\subset\mathfrak{J}_{0}
such that
J_{n}\to J
. Since
P_{r}Q_{\sigma}J_{n}\in\mathfrak{J}_{0}
and
P_{r}Q_{\sigma}J_{n}\to P_{r}Q_{\sigma}J

∎

The main property of the redundancy ideal is described in the following result.

Lemma 10
.

Let
\omega_{0}\in\mathtt{FG}_{\rm QDM}(\mathfrak{A})
be a
frustration-free ground state for the HA-QDM dynamics. Then

\omega_{0}(J)=0
for every

.

Proof.

The covariance condition (

\omega_{0}(P_{t}Q_{\gamma}-P_{t^{\prime}}Q_{\gamma^{\prime}})=0

if
t\sim t^{\prime}
and
\gamma\sim\gamma^{\prime}
. The prove is completed by linearity and continuity of
\omega_{0}
.
∎

We are now in a position to introduce the

(Logical operators)

\ast

-algebras.

3.4.
Topological properties of the space of frustration-free ground states

The main message of Theorem
1
is that to distinguish between two different frustration-free ground states
of the HA-QDM dynamics
is enough to compare their images in
\mathfrak{A}_{\rm log}
. For this reason, it is relevant to study the state space
\mathtt{S}(\mathfrak{A}_{\rm log})
.

If
t\sim t^{\prime}
and
\gamma\sim\gamma^{\prime}
then the monomial
P_{t}Q_{\gamma}
and
P_{t^{\prime}}Q_{\gamma^{\prime}}
in
\mathfrak{K}^{\prime}
are send by the canonical projection
\pi
to the same class in
\mathfrak{A}_{\rm log}
,
i.e.

\pi(P_{t}Q_{\gamma})=\pi(P_{t^{\prime}}Q_{\gamma^{\prime}})
. By denoting
with
[P_{t}Q_{\gamma}]
the common class one deduce that the class only depends on
[t]\in\mathring{H}^{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})
and
[\gamma]\in\mathring{H}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})
. This justify the introduction of the notation
P_{[t]}Q_{[\gamma]}:=[P_{t}Q_{\gamma}]
. Let us also introduce the symbol

\mathbb{H}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})\;:=\;\mathring{H}^{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})\times\mathring{H}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})\;.

Since the projection
\pi
is
\ast
-homomorphism, the commutation relations (
8
) and (
9
) factor thought the quotient and one obtains that

(22)

\left(P_{[t]}Q_{[\gamma]}\right)\left(P_{[t^{\prime}]}Q_{[\gamma^{\prime}]}\right)\;=\;\gamma(t^{\prime})P_{[t+t^{\prime}]}Q_{[\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}]}

for all
([t],[\gamma]),([t^{\prime}],[\gamma^{\prime}])\in\mathbb{H}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})
.
Moreover, from Lemma
2
and Lemma
8
one also infers that the set

\mathfrak{Z}_{\pi}\;:=\;\pi(\mathfrak{Z})\;=\;\left\{P_{[t]}Q_{[\gamma]}\;|\;([t],[\gamma])\in\mathbb{H}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})\right\}

is made by linearly independent generators that span the full algebra

\mathfrak{A}_{\rm log}
.

Let us introduce the notation
{\bf h}:=([t],[\gamma])
for a generic element of

\mathbb{H}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})
and
the form

\langle\;\cdot\;,\;\cdot\;\rangle\;:\;\mathbb{H}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})\times\mathbb{H}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})\;\longrightarrow\;\mathbb{U}(1)

defined by

\langle{\bf h},{\bf h}^{\prime}\rangle\;:=\;\gamma(t^{\prime})

for all
{\bf h}=([t],[\gamma])
and
{\bf h}^{\prime}=([t^{\prime}],[\gamma^{\prime}])
in

\mathbb{H}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})
. One can check that

\displaystyle\langle{\bf h}+{\bf h}^{\prime},{\bf h}^{\prime\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\;(\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})(t^{\prime\prime})\;=\;\gamma(t^{\prime\prime})\gamma^{\prime}(t^{\prime\prime})\;=\;\langle{\bf h},{\bf h}^{\prime\prime}\rangle\langle{\bf h}^{\prime},{\bf h}^{\prime\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle\langle{\bf h},{\bf h}^{\prime}+{\bf h}^{\prime\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\;\gamma(t^{\prime}+t^{\prime\prime})\;=\;\gamma(t^{\prime})\gamma(t^{\prime\prime})\;=\;\langle{\bf h},{\bf h}^{\prime}\rangle\langle{\bf h},{\bf h}^{\prime\prime}\rangle

Introducing the notation
W_{{\bf h}}:=\left(P_{[t]}Q_{[\gamma]}\right)
one can rephrase the relations (
22
) as

(23)

W_{{\bf h}}W_{{\bf h}^{\prime}}\;=\;\langle{\bf h},{\bf h}^{\prime}\rangle\;W_{{\bf h}+{\bf h}^{\prime}}\;.

These are (generalized) CCR relations in the Weyl form

[
4
, Section 5.2.2.2]

and in turn
\mathfrak{A}_{\rm log}
is (isomorphic to) a (generalized) CCR algebra.

If
one assumes that
\mathbb{H}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})
is finite then one ends in the same setting of
[
23
, Lemma 6.4]
.
For that let us introduce the commutator
[[{\bf h},{\bf h}^{\prime}]]:=\langle{\bf h},{\bf h}^{\prime}\rangle\langle{\bf h}^{\prime},{\bf h}\rangle
and the commutator subgroup

\mathbb{I}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})\;:=\;\left\{{\bf h}\in\mathbb{H}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})\;\big|\;[[{\bf h},{\bf h}^{\prime}]]=1\;,\;\;\forall\;{\bf h}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{H}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})\right\}\;.

Let us define the numbers

c\;:=\;\log_{2}(|\mathbb{I}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})|)\;,\qquad q\;:=\;\frac{1}{2}\log_{2}\left(\frac{|\mathbb{H}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})|}{|\mathbb{I}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})|}\right)

the finite set
X_{c}:=\{1,2,\ldots,2^{c}\}
and the finite dimensional Hilbert space
\mathfrak{h}_{q}:=\mathbb{C}^{2^{q}}
. With this notation, the content of
[
23
, Lemma 6.4]
can be adapted to our situation providing the isomorphism

(24)

\mathfrak{A}_{\rm log}\;\simeq\;C(X_{c})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{h}_{q})\;.

Interestingly,
\mathfrak{A}_{\rm log}
is isomorphic to the tensor product of the commutative algebra
C(X_{c})
of
classical
degrees of freedom and a non-commutative algebra
\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{h}_{q})

of
quantum
degrees of freedom.
The relevance of this result is that it provides a way to determine the number of
c
classical information (bits) and the number
q
of quantum information (qubits) that one can encode in the frustration-free ground space.

Remark 4
.

It is useful to provide a more explicit characterization for the element of
\mathbb{I}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})
.
Notice that
{\bf h}=([t],[\gamma])\in\mathbb{I}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})

if and only if

1\;=\;\langle{\bf h},{\bf h}^{\prime}\rangle\langle{\bf h}^{\prime},{\bf h}\rangle=\gamma(t^{\prime})\gamma^{\prime}(t)

for all
{\bf h^{\prime}}=([t^{\prime}],[\gamma^{\prime}])
.
As this happens for all
{\bf h^{\prime}}
, taking
[t^{\prime}]
as the trivial class on
\mathring{H}^{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})
, one obtains that
\gamma^{\prime}(t)=1
for all
[\gamma^{\prime}]
. Analogously, taking
[\gamma^{\prime}]
as the trivial class on
\mathring{H}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})
, one has
\gamma(t^{\prime})=1
for all
[t^{\prime}]
.
In conclusion,
{\bf h}=([t],[\gamma])\in\mathbb{I}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})

if and only if
\alpha(t)=1=\gamma(s)

for all
[s]\in\mathring{H}^{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})
and
[\alpha]\in\mathring{H}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})
.

3.5.
Description of the pure states space

A relevant payoff of the result obtained in the previous section is a complete description of the space
\mathtt{FG}_{\rm QDM}^{\circ}(\mathfrak{A})
of the pure frustration-free ground states of the HA-QDM dynamics. This is a relevant fact since the full space
\mathtt{FG}_{\rm QDM}(\mathfrak{A})
can be recovered as the closure of the convex hull of
\mathtt{FG}_{\rm QDM}^{\circ}(\mathfrak{A})
.

Proposition 3
.

There is a homeomorphism
\Phi:\mathtt{P}(\mathfrak{A}_{\rm log})\to\mathtt{FG}^{\circ}_{\rm QDM}(\mathfrak{A})
between the space of pure states of
\mathfrak{A}_{\rm log}
and the
space of pure frustration-free ground states for the HA-QDM dynamics.

Proof.

First, consider the case where
\rho
is mixed. Then, there exist distinct states
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
in
\mathtt{S}(\mathfrak{A}_{\rm log})
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
with

\rho\;=\;\lambda\rho_{1}+(1-\lambda)\rho_{2}

By the bijection, one concludes that
\omega_{0,\rho}
is mixed.
On the other hand, if
\omega_{0,\rho}
is mixed, it can be written as

\omega_{0,\rho}=\lambda\omega_{1}+(1-\lambda)\omega_{2}

where
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
are different states and
\lambda\in(0,1)
. If we prove that
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
are frustration free ground states, then one concludes the argument.
Take
A_{s}B_{\nu}
with
s\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})^{-1}
and
\nu\in{\rm Hom}_{0}(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{G})_{1}
. This operator is unitary and this implies that
|\omega_{1}(A_{s}B_{\nu})|\leq 1
and
|\omega_{2}(A_{s}B_{\nu})|\leq 1
. But,

1\;=\;\omega_{0,\rho}(A_{s}B_{\nu})\;=\;\lambda\omega_{1}(A_{s}B_{\nu})+(1-\lambda)\omega_{2}(A_{s}B_{\nu})

and this is only possible if
\omega_{1}(A_{s},B_{\nu})=\omega_{2}(A_{s}B_{\nu})=1
. Then,
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
are frustration free ground states. Using the bijection, we conclude that
\rho
is also mixed. Finally,
\Phi

is the restriction of a homeomorphism, and is again a
homeomorphism with respect to the induced subspace topology
in the initial and final space.
∎

By using the tensor product structure of
\mathfrak{A}_{\rm log}
described by (
24
) and
[
8
, Proposition 2.9]
one ends with an homeomorphism of topological spaces

\mathtt{FG}_{\rm QDM}^{\circ}(\mathfrak{A})\;\simeq\;X_{c}\times\mathtt{P}(\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{h}_{q}))\;.

Finally, since
\mathcal{B}(\mathfrak{h}_{q})
is a full matrix-algebra
on the finite dimensional Hilbert space
\mathfrak{h}_{q}
, its pure states are described by one-dimensional projections, or equivalently by one-dimensional subspaces. Therefore, one has the homeomorphism of topological spaces

\mathtt{FG}_{\rm QDM}^{\circ}(\mathfrak{A})\;\simeq\;X_{c}\times\mathbb{P}(\mathfrak{h}_{q})

where
\mathbb{P}(\mathfrak{h}_{q})\simeq\mathbb{CP}^{2^{q}}
is the complex projective space in
\mathfrak{h}_{q}=\mathbb{C}^{2^{q}}
.

Appendix B: Simplicial Complex

The concept of a
simplicial complex
serves to generalize the notion of triangulation, providing a powerful framework for understanding and analyzing topological spaces. In this discussion, we shall present only a brief and introductory overview of this topic. For a more comprehensive and detailed treatment, we refer the reader to the classical monographs
[
12
,
21
]
and, in particular, to
[
22
, Section 3.1]
.

Let
{V}
be a set. A simplicial complex
\mathcal{K}
is a family of finite nonempty subsets of
{V}
, called
simplexes
, such that

(1)

if
v\in V
, then
\{v\}\in\mathcal{K}
;

(2)

if
s\in\mathcal{K}
and
s^{\prime}\subset s
, then
s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}
.

One refers to
V
as the
vertex set
of
\mathcal{K}
and sometimes the notation
V={\rm Vert}(\mathcal{K})
is used. A simplex
s\in\mathcal{K}
containing exactly
n+1
distinct vertices is called an
n
-simplex. The subset of all
n
-simplices is denoted with
\mathcal{K}_{n}\subset K
.
If
s^{\prime}\subset s
the
s^{\prime}
is called a
face
of
s
.

We are in a position to introduce the concept of
oriented
simplicial complex
as in
[
22
, Section 4.1]
. An oriented
n
-simplex of
\mathcal{K}
is an
n
-simplex
s\in\mathcal{K}_{n}
together with an equivalence class of total ordering of the vertices of
s
, two orderings being equivalent if they differ by an even permutation of the vertices.
If
v_{0},v_{1}\ldots,v_{n}
are the vertices of
s
, then
[v_{0},\dots,v_{n}]
denotes the oriented

n
-simplex
s
together with the equivalence class of the ordering

v_{0}<v_{1}<\ldots<v_{n}
. For every vertex
v\in V
there is a unique oriented 0-simplex
[v]
. For every
n
-simplex with
n\geqslant 1
there correspond exactly two oriented

n
-simplexes. If
[v_{0},\dots,v_{n}]
denotes an oriented
n
-simplex, then
-[v_{0},\dots,v_{n}]
will denote the same simplex with the opposite orientation obtained by an odd permutation of the ordering

v_{0}<v_{1}<\ldots<v_{n}
. Finally, we will use
[v_{0},\dots,\hat{v}_{i},\dots,v_{n}]
to denote
the oriented
(n-1)
-simplex obtained by removing the vertex
v_{i}
from
[v_{0},\dots,v_{n}]
. From now on, with a little abuse of notation, we will use the same symbol
\mathcal{K}_{n}
to indicate the set of oriented
n
-simplex.

, one can see the geometric realization of the building blocks for oriented simplicial complexes.

Denote by
\mathcal{C}_{n}
the free abelian group with basis the oriented
n
-complexes of
\mathcal{K}_{n}

with the relations
s_{1}+s_{2}=0
if
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
are oriented
n
-complexes determined by the same vertices but with opposite orientation. The
boundary operator

\partial_{n}^{\mathcal{C}}:\mathcal{C}_{n}\to\mathcal{C}_{n-1}
captures how an oriented
n
-simplex interacts with its oriented lower-dimensional faces. Specifically, it maps an
n
-simplex to a signed sum of its
(n{-}1)
-dimensional faces, respecting orientation and structure.
More precisely, let
x=[v_{0},\dots,v_{n}]\in\mathcal{K}_{n}
. Then, by definition

(25)

\partial_{n}^{\mathcal{C}}x\;:=\;\sum_{y\in\mathcal{K}_{n-1}}\epsilon_{x,y}\;y

where

\epsilon_{x,y}\;:=\;\begin{cases}+1&\;\text{if }y=[v_{0},\dots,\hat{v}_{i},\dots,v_{n}]\;\text{ for some even }i\;,\\
-1&\;\text{if }y=[v_{0},\dots,\hat{v}_{i},\dots,v_{n}]\;\text{ for some odd }i\;,\\
\phantom{+}0&\;\text{if }\;y\text{ is not a face of }x\;.\end{cases}

A direct computation shows that
\partial_{n}^{\mathcal{C}}\circ\partial_{n+1}^{\mathcal{C}}=0
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

We also need the coboundary operator
\widehat{\partial}^{\mathcal{C}}_{n+1}:\mathcal{C}_{n}\to\mathcal{C}_{n+1}
defined by

(26)

\widehat{\partial}_{n+1}^{\mathcal{C}}x\;:=\;\sum_{z\in\mathcal{K}_{n+1}}\epsilon_{z,x}\;z\;.

Also in this case one gets that
\widehat{\partial}_{n+1}^{\mathcal{C}}\circ\widehat{\partial}_{n}^{\mathcal{C}}=0
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

We are now in a position to prove the duality between the maps
\delta^{p}
and
\delta_{p+1}

expressed by Lemma
1
.

Proof of Lemma
1
.

The proof is an explicit computation.
Notice that

\displaystyle\gamma(\delta^{p}(f))\;

\displaystyle=\;\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\prod_{x\in\mathcal{K}_{n}}\gamma_{n}(x)[(\delta^{p}f)_{n}(x)]

\displaystyle=\;\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\prod_{x\in\mathcal{K}_{n}}\gamma_{n}(x)[f_{n-1}(\partial^{\mathcal{C}}_{n}(x))-(-1)^{p}\partial_{n-p}^{\mathbb{G}}(f_{n}(x))]

\displaystyle=\;\frac{\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\prod_{x\in\mathcal{K}_{n}}\gamma_{n}(x)[f_{n-1}(\partial_{n}^{\mathcal{C}}(x))]}{\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\prod_{x\in\mathcal{K}_{n}}\gamma_{n}(x)[(-1)^{p}\partial_{n-p}^{\mathbb{G}}(f_{n}(x))]}

\displaystyle=\;\frac{\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\prod_{x\in\mathcal{K}_{n}}\gamma_{n}(x)[f_{n-1}(\partial_{n}^{\mathcal{C}}(x))]}{\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\prod_{x\in\mathcal{K}_{n}}\widehat{\partial}_{n-p}^{\mathbb{G}}(\gamma_{n}(x)[(-1)^{p}f_{n}(x)])}\;.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\delta_{p+1}(\gamma(f))\;

\displaystyle=\;\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\prod_{x\in\mathcal{K}_{n}}(\delta_{p+1}\gamma)_{n}(x)[f_{n}(x)]

\displaystyle=\;\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\prod_{x\in\mathcal{K}_{n}}\left(\gamma_{n+1}(\widehat{\partial}_{n+1}^{\mathcal{C}}(x))-(-1)^{p}\widehat{\partial}_{n-p}^{\mathbb{G}}(\gamma_{n}(x))\right)[f_{n}(x)]

\displaystyle=\;\frac{\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\prod_{x\in\mathcal{K}_{n}}\gamma_{n+1}(\widehat{\partial}_{n+1}^{\mathcal{C}}(x))[f_{n}(x)]}{\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\prod_{x\in\mathcal{K}_{n}}\widehat{\partial}_{n-p}^{\mathbb{G}}(\gamma_{n}(x)[(-1)^{p}f_{n}(x)])}\;.

As the denominators are equal, it is only necessary to show the equality of the numerators.
Notice that, as
f_{-1}
is trivial, one deduces that

\displaystyle\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\prod_{x\in\mathcal{K}_{n}}\gamma_{n}(x)[f_{n-1}(\partial_{n}^{\mathcal{C}}(x))]\;

\displaystyle=\;\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\prod_{x\in\mathcal{K}_{n}}\gamma_{n}(x)[f_{n-1}(\partial_{n}^{\mathcal{C}}(x))]

\displaystyle=\;\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\prod_{x\in\mathcal{K}_{n}}\gamma_{n}(x)\left[f_{n-1}\left(\sum_{y\in\mathcal{K}_{n-1}}\epsilon_{x,y}\;y\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\prod_{x\in\mathcal{K}_{n}}\prod_{y\in\mathcal{K}_{n-1}}\gamma_{n}(x)[f_{n-1}(\epsilon_{x,y}\;y)]\;.

On the other hand

\displaystyle\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\prod_{x\in\mathcal{K}_{n}}\gamma_{n+1}(\widehat{\partial}_{n+1}^{\mathcal{C}}(x))[f_{n}(x)]\;

\displaystyle=\;\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\prod_{x\in\mathcal{K}_{n}}\gamma_{n+1}\left(\sum_{z\in\mathcal{K}_{n+1}}\epsilon_{z,x}\;z\right)[f_{n}(x)]

\displaystyle=\;\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\prod_{x\in\mathcal{K}_{n}}\prod_{z\in\mathcal{K}_{n+1}}\gamma_{n+1}(\epsilon_{z,x}\;z)[f_{n}(x)]

\displaystyle=\;\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\prod_{z\in\mathcal{K}_{n+1}}\prod_{x\in\mathcal{K}_{n}}\gamma_{n+1}(z)[f_{n}(\epsilon_{z,x}\;x)]\;.

By a change of variable
n\to n-1
in the second equality one gets the equality of the numerators.
∎

It is important to note that if
\partial^{\mathbb{G}}_{j}
is trivial for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, then the denominators of both fractions become equal to 1. Consequently, one recovers the usual boundary and coboundary maps used in homological codes (see
[
23
, eq. (4.13)]
and the subsequent paragraph).

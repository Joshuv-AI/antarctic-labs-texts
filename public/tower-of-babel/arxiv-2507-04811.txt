Multipole phases in a type of spin/fermion ladders with local conserved quantities and generalizations
Jianlong Fu

Abstract
We study spin/fermion ladder models with multipole phases, which are traditional spin phases formed by multipole moments. These phases feature non-trivial order with zero magnetization. The multipole models have dimer local conserved quantities that are Ising terms of spin. The Hilbert spaces are locally fragmented into independent sectors described effectively by higher-order spin . For dipole models, we consider two ladder geometries with quadratic spin couplings and work out the phase diagrams. Different phases of the model can be distinguished experimentally with the dynamical structure factor. Higher-order multipole models are obtained by introducing more dimer conserved quantities. The phases are characterized by the values of the local conserved quantities and the traditional spin phases of the higher-order spin.



I
Introduction

The search for exotic phases with unconventional excitations has long been the focus of condensed matter physics. Some of these excitations exhibit nonlocality, like anyons with nontrivial statistics
Wilczek 1982
;
Kitaev 2006
;
Nayak et al. 2008
. Recently there has been growing interest in another type of nonlocal composite object,
multipoles
, which are opposite charges or spins aligned in specific order. To this end, studies on dimer Mott insulators
\kappa-(\text{ET})_{2}\text{Cu}_{2}(\text{CN})_{3}

Hotta 2010
;
Naka and Ishihara 2010
;
Naka and Ishihara 2016a
;
Naka and Ishihara 2016b
;
Yamada and Tada 2020
show that elementary excitations in dimerized systems can sometimes be described
approximately
by dipoles. Altermagnetism
Šmejkal et al. 2022a
;
Šmejkal et al. 2022b
, a recently discovered magnetic phase, can be described by multipole order parameters
Bhowal and Spaldin 2024
;
McClarty and Rau 2024
. It is thus interesting to ask if there are phases of matter whose elementary degree of freedom (DOF) is multipoles. If exist, such phases can possess non-trivial order formed by the multipole moments while the net magnetization remains zero; furthermore, low-energy excitations are formed and described by multipole moments. In this work, we answer this question affirmatively by constructing models hosting
exact
multipole phases, called
multipole models
, and study their properties.

The multipole models arise naturally in Hamiltonians with local conserved quantities, which make the Hilbert space fragmented into disconnected sectors
Sala et al. 2020
;
Khemani et al. 2020
. In spin models, the (smallest possible) local conserved quantities around site
\bm{r}
are generally written as a product of
\nu
spin operators
W_{\bm{r}}^{\nu}=\prod_{i=1}^{\nu}\sigma_{\bm{r}+\bm{a}_{i}}^{\mu_{i}}
, in which
\mu_{i}=x,y,z
labels the spin components and
\bm{a}_{i}
are lattice vectors. For
\nu\geq 3
, these quantities usually form closed loops and become
1
-form symmetries
Gaiotto et al. 2015
;
McGreevy 2023
; such cases have been studied in Kitaev-type spin models, including the original model
Kitaev 2006
, the chiral spin liquid models
Yao and Kivelson 2007
;
Fu 2019
, and the Kitaev ladders
DeGottardi et al. 2011
;
Pedrocchi et al. 2012
;
Wu 2012
. Complementing this series of
\nu
, our multipole models form the simplest cases with
\nu=2
local conserved quantites; geometrically, these quantities
\tau_{ij}=\sigma^{z}_{i}\sigma^{z}_{j}
form
dimers
, each
\tau_{ij}
takes values
\pm 1
. For dimers with
\tau=-1
, the configurations are naturally
dipoles
; for dimers with
\tau=1
, they are two identical spins/charges separated by lattice constant, we name them
charge-pairs
. Each local invariant Hilbert subspace has a dimension
2
, so dipoles or charge-pairs can be described by a spin-
\frac{1}{2}
DOF
Hotta 2010
;
Naka and Ishihara 2010
, representing the dipole moment for
\tau=-1
sector and the sign of the charge-pair for
\tau=1

first-order spin
while the original spin
\bm{\sigma}
is taken as the
zeroth-order

\{\bm{\sigma}_{i},\bm{\sigma}_{j}\}
is thus described by static
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
variable
\tau_{ij}
and first-order spin
\bm{S}_{ij}
.

In this work we study multipole models defined on
ladder
geometry. The starting point is the

with quadratic spin couplings; the lattice bonds are divided into two types:
J
-type and
K
-type, the collection of
J
-bonds form a dimer covering of the ladder. The spin interaction on
J
-bonds and
K
-bonds are general

J
-bond. We consider two ladder geometries; the models with quadratic spin couplings map

\bm{S}
, coupled with static variables
\tau

\tau
and the spin phases of
\bm{S}
. These phases leave distinct features in the dynamical structure factors, which can be used to identify them in neutron scattering experiments. Although we focus on spin description, the dual fermionic version of the models can be obtained by standard Jordan-Wigner transformation
Jordan and Wigner 1928
;
Lieb et al. 1961
;
Feng et al. 2007
;
Fu 2022
;
SM
.

Generalizations to higher-order multipole models are achieved by introducing more local dimer conserved quantities. The
quadrupole
models have local conserved quantites on the bonds of every second plaquette of the ladder. In the quadrupole phases, the quadrupole moment is described by a
second-order spin
, namely the dipole of dipole moment. In principle, such generalization goes on to higher-order, and
n
-th order spin describes dipole/charge-pair formed by the
(n-1)
-th order spin. Higher-order models generally have more complex phase structure.

1

V
Discussion

We have shown the relation between multipole phases and local dimer conserved quantities through dipole and quadrupole models on ladder geometry. Generalization to octopole models can be done by coupling to another layer of ladder, with the plaquettes becoming cubes. The Hamiltonian, containing cubic, plaquette and bond terms, has
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
conserved quantities on every edge of half of the cubes
SM
. Detailed studies are left for the future.

Thanks to the simple quadratic spin couplings in the dipole models (
1
) and (
6
), it is possible to find them in real materials. To this end, it is important to study the
stability
of the multipole phases against various perturbations. Another important question is the multipole phases on higher-dimensional lattices. Similar multipole phases are expected to exist although JW transformation does not apply easily. One can also consider the combination of dimer and other types of local conserved quantities and study possible new phases.

-bonds intermediately (as for both the vertical ladder and the horizontal ladder), for which case the fermionic Hamiltonian corresponding to (
S1
) reads (assuming the couplings are isotropic,
J_{x}=J_{y}=J_{xy}
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{c}^{(d)}=

\displaystyle\sum_{J\text{-bonds}}\bigg[2J_{xy}(c_{i}^{\dagger}c_{j}+c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{i})+J_{z}(2c_{i}^{\dagger}c_{i}-1)(2c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j}-1)\bigg]

(S2)

\displaystyle+\sum_{K\text{-bonds}}K_{ij}(2c_{i}^{\dagger}c_{i}-1)(2c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j}-1).

This is a general fermionic model with interaction on all bonds and fermion-hopping exists only on the dimers, the superscript
(d)
means
dipole
.

In what follows we give details of the derivation of the results in the main text.

V.1
The vertical ladder

For the vertical ladder, the vertical bonds have spin XYZ interaction and the horizontal bonds have spin ZZ coupling. Every unit cell (vertical bonds) cotains two spins; however, the artificial labelling of the spin site in a zigzag order doubles the size of the unit cell, so we consider
2N
originial unit cells and
4N
individial spins. Fig.
S1
gives another way to represent the ladder geometry. The Hamiltonian of the spin ladder is given by Eq. (
1
), written out explicitly, we have

\mathcal{H}_{v}^{(d)}=\sum_{n=1}^{2N}\left(J_{x}\sigma_{2n-1}^{x}\sigma_{2n}^{x}+J_{y}\sigma_{2n-1}^{y}\sigma_{2n}^{y}+J_{z}\sigma_{2n-1}^{z}\sigma_{2n}^{z}\right)+\sum_{n=1}^{2N-1}\left(K\sigma_{2n}^{z}\sigma_{2n+1}^{z}+\tilde{K}\sigma_{2n-1}^{z}\sigma_{2n+2}^{z}\right).

(S3)

The model has
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
conserved quantities
\sigma_{2n-1}^{z}\sigma_{2n}^{z}
for every vertical bond, it can be solved by the Jordan-Wigner (JW) transformation in a rotated basis (
2
). In particular we introduce two Majorana fermions
\eta^{\alpha}
and
\eta^{\beta}
for every site and the JW transformation is given by, defining the starting point as
s
,

\sigma_{i}^{x}=-i\eta_{i}^{\alpha}\eta_{i}^{\beta},\qquad\sigma_{i}^{y}=\eta_{i}^{\alpha}\prod_{j=s}^{i-1}(i\eta_{j}^{\alpha}\eta_{j}^{\beta}),\qquad\sigma_{i}^{z}=\eta_{i}^{\beta}\prod_{j=s}^{i-1}(i\eta_{j}^{\alpha}\eta_{j}^{\beta}).

(S4)

Applying the JW transformation with
s=1
, the Hamiltonian can be written as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{H}_{v}^{(d)}=&\sum_{n=1}^{2N}\left[J_{x}(-)(\eta_{2n-1}^{\alpha}\eta_{2n}^{\beta})\eta_{2n-1}^{\beta}\eta_{2n}^{\alpha}+J_{y}(-i)\eta_{2n}^{\alpha}\eta_{2n-1}^{\beta}+J_{z}i(\eta_{2n}^{\beta}\eta_{2n-1}^{\alpha})\right]+\\
&\sum_{n=1}^{2N-1}\left[K(-i)\eta_{2n}^{\alpha}\eta_{2n+1}^{\beta}+\tilde{K}(-i)\eta_{2n+2}^{\beta}\eta_{2n-1}^{\alpha}\eta_{2n}^{\alpha}\eta_{2n}^{\beta}\eta_{2n+1}^{\alpha}\eta_{2n+1}^{\beta}\right].\end{aligned}

(S5)

The conserved quantities can be written as
\tau_{n}=\sigma_{2n-1}^{z}\sigma_{2n}^{z}=-i\eta_{2n-1}^{\alpha}\eta_{2n}^{\beta}
, with which the Hamiltonian is given by Eq. (
3
).

For each sector with definite
\tau_{n}
, the Hamiltonian (
3
) is identical to the transverse-field Ising model Hamiltonian. To see this we pair up sites
(2n-1,2n)
into a single site
n
, namely
(2n-1,2n)\rightarrow n
, and redefine the Majorana fermions as
\eta_{2n-1}^{\beta}\rightarrow\tilde{\eta}_{n}^{\beta}
and
\eta_{2n}^{\alpha}\rightarrow\tilde{\eta}_{n}^{\alpha}
. The Hamiltonian is then given by

\mathcal{H}_{v}^{(d)}=\sum_{n=1}^{2N}\left[(J_{y}-J_{x}\tau_{n})i\tilde{\eta}_{n}^{\beta}\tilde{\eta}_{n}^{\alpha}+J_{z}\tau_{n}\right]+\sum_{n=1}^{2N-1}(K+\tilde{K}\tau_{n}\tau_{n+1})(-i)\tilde{\eta}_{n}^{\alpha}\tilde{\eta}_{n+1}^{\beta}.

(S6)

A new set of JW transformation can be performed by introducing the first-order spin
\bm{S}_{n}
, which satisfies

\tilde{\eta}_{n}^{\alpha}=S_{n}^{y}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(-S_{j}^{x}),\qquad\tilde{\eta}_{n}^{\beta}=S_{n}^{z}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(-S_{j}^{x}),\qquad i\tilde{\eta}_{n}^{\beta}\tilde{\eta}_{n}^{\alpha}=S_{n}^{x}.

(S7)

This leads to
(-i)\tilde{\eta}_{n}^{\alpha}\tilde{\eta}_{n+1}^{\beta}=S_{n}^{z}S_{n+1}^{z}
, using which we arrive at the Hamiltonian (
4
).

The first-order spin
\bm{S}_{n}
can be related to the zeroth-order spin
\bm{\sigma}
, firstly

S_{n}^{x}=i\eta_{2n-1}^{\beta}\eta_{2n}^{\alpha}=i[\sigma_{2n-1}^{z}\prod_{j=1}^{2n-2}(-\sigma_{j}^{x})]\sigma_{2n}^{y}\prod_{j=1}^{2n-1}(-\sigma_{j}^{x})=\sigma_{2n-1}^{y}\sigma_{2n}^{y}.

(S8)

This is the first part of (
5
). On the other hand, we have
S_{n}^{z}=\eta_{2n-1}^{\beta}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(i\eta_{2j}^{\alpha}\eta_{2j-1}^{\beta})
, which is compared with

\sigma_{2n-1}^{z}=\eta_{2n-1}^{\beta}\prod_{j=1}^{2n-2}(i\eta_{j}^{\alpha}\eta_{j}^{\beta})=\eta_{2n-1}^{\beta}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(i\eta_{2j-1}^{\alpha}\eta_{2j}^{\beta})(i\eta_{2j-1}^{\beta}\eta_{2j}^{\alpha});

(S9)

these lead to

\sigma_{2n-1}^{z}=S_{n}^{z}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(-i\eta_{2j-1}^{\alpha}\eta_{2j}^{\beta})=S_{n}^{z}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\tau_{n}=S_{n}^{z}\prod_{j=1}^{2n-2}\sigma_{j}^{z}.

(S10)

Finally we get the second part of Eq. (
5
),
S_{n}^{z}=\prod_{j=1}^{2n-1}\sigma_{j}^{z}
.

V.1.1
Phase diagram from fermionic Hamiltonian

In the main text, we obtain the phase diagram by exactly diagonalizing the fermionic Hamiltonian (
S6
). Here we provide the details of the derivation.

Firstly we define complex fermion

f_{n}^{\dagger}=\frac{1}{2}(\tilde{\eta}_{n}^{\beta}+i\tilde{\eta}_{n}^{\alpha}),

(S11)

such that
\tilde{\eta}_{n}^{\beta}=f_{n}+f_{n}^{\dagger}
,
\tilde{\eta}_{n}^{\alpha}=i(f_{n}-f_{n}^{\dagger})
. The Hamiltonian (
S6
) can be written as

\mathcal{H}_{v}^{(d)}=\sum_{n=1}^{2N}\left[(J_{x}\tau_{n}-J_{y})(f_{n}+f_{n}^{\dagger})(f_{n}-f_{n}^{\dagger})+J_{z}\tau_{n}\right]+\sum_{n=1}^{2N-1}(K+\tilde{K}\tau_{n}\tau_{n+1})(f_{n}-f_{n}^{\dagger})(f_{n+1}+f_{n+1}^{\dagger}).

(S12)

Only phases which preserve part of the translational symmetry are considered. To this end, we pick the unit cell containing two
f
fermions, and rename

f_{2n-1}\rightarrow f_{n}^{1},\qquad f_{2n}\rightarrow f_{n}^{2},\qquad\tau_{2n-1}\rightarrow\tau_{n}^{1},\qquad\tau_{2n}\rightarrow\tau_{n}^{2}.

(S13)

The Hamiltonian (
S6
) then becomes

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{H}_{v}^{(d)}=&\sum_{n=1}^{N}(J_{x}\tau_{n}^{1}-J_{y})(2f_{n}^{1\dagger}f_{n}^{1}-1)+(J_{x}\tau_{n}^{2}-J_{y})(2f_{n}^{2\dagger}f_{n}^{2}-1)+J_{z}(\tau_{n}^{1}+\tau_{n}^{2})\\
&+(K+\tilde{K}\tau_{n}^{1}\tau_{n}^{2})\bigg(f_{n}^{1}f_{n}^{2}-f_{n}^{1\dagger}f_{n}^{2}+f_{n}^{1}f_{n}^{2\dagger}-f_{n}^{1\dagger}f_{n}^{2\dagger}\bigg)\\
&+\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}(K+\tilde{K}\tau_{n}^{2}\tau_{n+1}^{1})\bigg(f_{n}^{2}f_{n+1}^{1}-f_{n}^{2\dagger}f_{n+1}^{1}+f_{n}^{2}f_{n+1}^{1\dagger}-f_{n}^{2\dagger}f_{n+1}^{1\dagger}\bigg).\end{aligned}

(S14)

Due to the assumed translational symmetry, a Fourier transform can be performed by defining

f_{n}^{\dagger}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{k}f_{k}^{\dagger}e^{ikn},\qquad f_{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{k}f_{k}e^{-ikn}.

(S15)

The range of
k
is taken to be
(-\pi,\pi)
. For every unit cell, the values of
\tau^{1}
and
\tau^{2}
are fixed, so can be treated as independent of the position, therefore we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{H}_{v}^{(d)}=\sum_{k}&(J_{x}\tau^{1}-J_{y})(2f_{k}^{1\dagger}f_{k}^{1}-1)+(J_{x}\tau^{2}-J_{y})(2f_{k}^{2\dagger}f_{k}^{2}-1)+J_{z}(\tau^{1}+\tau^{2})\\
&+(K+\tilde{K}\tau^{1}\tau^{2})\bigg(f_{k}^{1}f_{-k}^{2}-f_{k}^{1\dagger}f_{k}^{2}+f_{k}^{1}f_{k}^{2\dagger}-f_{k}^{1\dagger}f_{-k}^{2\dagger}\bigg)\\
&+(K+\tilde{K}\tau^{1}\tau^{2})\bigg(f_{k}^{2}f_{-k}^{1}e^{ik}-f_{k}^{2\dagger}f_{k}^{1}e^{-ik}+f_{k}^{2}f_{k}^{1\dagger}e^{ik}-f_{k}^{2\dagger}f_{-k}^{1\dagger}e^{-ik}\bigg).\end{aligned}

(S16)

To simplify the notations we introduce coupling constants
J_{1}=J_{x}\tau^{1}-J_{y}
,
J_{2}=J_{x}\tau^{2}-J_{y}
,
K_{1}=K+\tilde{K}\tau^{1}\tau^{2}
. The Hamiltonian can then be written in a matrix form,

\mathcal{H}_{v}^{(d)}=\sum_{k}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}f_{k}^{1\dagger}&f_{k}^{2\dagger}&f_{-k}^{1}&f_{-k}^{2}\end{array}\right)\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}f_{k}^{1}\\
f_{k}^{2}\\
f_{-k}^{1\dagger}\\
f_{-k}^{2\dagger}\end{array}\right)+J_{z}(\tau^{1}+\tau^{2}),

(S17)

the matrix
\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}
is given by

\hat{\mathbf{H}}_{k}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}J_{1}&-\frac{1}{2}K_{1}(1+e^{ik})&0&-\frac{1}{2}K_{1}(1-e^{ik})\\
-\frac{1}{2}K_{1}(1+e^{-ik})&J_{2}&\frac{1}{2}K_{1}(1-e^{-ik})&0\\
0&\frac{1}{2}K_{1}(1-e^{ik})&-J_{1}&\frac{1}{2}K_{1}(1+e^{ik})\\
-\frac{1}{2}K_{1}(1-e^{-ik})&0&\frac{1}{2}K_{1}(1+e^{-ik})&-J_{2}\end{array}\right)

(S18)

The phase diagram is thus obtained by comparing ground-state energies of the fermionic Hamiltonian evaluated on a grid of
k
-space with different values of
\tau^{1}
and
\tau^{2}
.

Figure S2:
Comparison between phase diagrams of the vertical ladder obtained in two different ways with
\tilde{K}=0.8
,
J_{x}=J_{y}=1
. Left: from exact calculation of fermionic ground states; Right: from numerical diagonalization on a system of 16 spins (open boundary condition).

The vertical ladder can be mapped into a free fermion model and thus enjoys exact solvability. For the horizontal ladder, the only way to obtain the phase diagram is by diagonalizing the spin Hamiltonian on a finite lattice. The vertical ladder is thus an ideal platform to study the finite-size effect. To this end, we adopt another way to obtain the phase diagram of the vertical ladder, namely by diagonalization of (
4
) on a system of 16 spins. The phase diagrams of the vertical ladder obtained by the two methods are compared in Fig.
S2
, from which the finite-size effect of the direct diagonalization of spin Hamiltonian is evident. In particular the phase boundaries of the “staggered” and “CP” phases are moved to the left.

V.1.2
The model with plaquette terms

Here we write down the model with plaquette terms which results in XX and YY coupling of the first-order spin
\bm{S}
. To this end we have

\mathcal{H}_{v}^{(d)^{\prime}}=\sum_{n=1}^{2N-1}K_{p}^{x}\sigma_{2n-1}^{y}\sigma_{2n}^{y}\sigma_{2n+1}^{y}\sigma_{2n+2}^{y}+K_{p}^{y}\sigma_{2n-1}^{y}\sigma_{2n}^{x}\sigma_{2n+1}^{x}\sigma_{2n+2}^{y}\rightarrow\sum_{n=1}^{2N-1}K_{p}^{x}S_{n}^{x}S_{n+1}^{x}+K_{p}^{y}S_{n}^{y}S_{n+1}^{y}.

(S19)

Added to the Hamiltonian (
S3
) we obtain the full Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{H}_{v}^{(d)}=&\sum_{n=1}^{2N}\left(J_{x}\sigma_{2n-1}^{x}\sigma_{2n}^{x}+J_{y}\sigma_{2n-1}^{y}\sigma_{2n}^{y}+J_{z}\sigma_{2n-1}^{z}\sigma_{2n}^{z}\right)+\\
&\sum_{n=1}^{2N-1}\left(K\sigma_{2n}^{z}\sigma_{2n+1}^{z}+\tilde{K}\sigma_{2n-1}^{z}\sigma_{2n+2}^{z}+K_{p}^{x}\sigma_{2n-1}^{y}\sigma_{2n}^{y}\sigma_{2n+1}^{y}\sigma_{2n+2}^{y}+K_{p}^{y}\sigma_{2n-1}^{y}\sigma_{2n}^{x}\sigma_{2n+1}^{x}\sigma_{2n+2}^{y}\right).\end{aligned}

(S20)

This would translate into the general XYZ model in transverse field for the first-order spin.

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{H}_{v}^{(d)}=&\sum_{n=1}^{2N}\left[(J_{y}-J_{x}\tau_{n})S_{n}^{x}+J_{z}\tau_{n}\right]\\
&+\sum_{n=1}^{2N-1}(K+\tilde{K}\tau_{n}\tau_{n+1})S_{n}^{z}S_{n+1}^{z}+K_{p}^{x}S_{n}^{x}S_{n+1}^{x}+K_{p}^{y}S_{n}^{y}S_{n+1}^{y}.\end{aligned}

(S21)

This would support richer phase diagrams.

V.2
The horizontal ladder

For the horizontal ladder, the lattice geometry is taken as half-infinite and has an extra spin site for definition purpose, we name this site 0 and label the spins with numbers as shown in Fig.
S1
. The spin Hamiltonian (
6
) can be written explicitly as

\mathcal{H}_{h}^{(d)}=\sum_{n=0}\left[J_{x}\sigma_{2n}^{x}\sigma_{2n+1}^{x}+J_{y}\sigma_{2n}^{y}\sigma_{2n+1}^{y}+J_{z}\sigma_{2n}^{z}\sigma_{2n+1}^{z}\right]+\sum_{n=1}\left[K\sigma_{2n-1}^{z}\sigma_{2n}^{z}+\tilde{K}\sigma_{2n-1}^{z}\sigma_{2n+2}^{z}\right].

(S22)

In the following we assume the coupling on bond
(0,1)
to be zero so that the spin on site 0 does not couple to the ladder. Using Jordan-Wigner transformation (
2
) with the starting point of string operator
s=0
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{H}_{h}^{(d)}=&\sum_{n=0}\left[J_{x}(-)\eta_{2n}^{\beta}\eta_{2n+1}^{\alpha}\eta_{2n}^{\alpha}\eta_{2n+1}^{\beta}+J_{y}(-i)\eta_{2n+1}^{\alpha}\eta_{2n}^{\beta}+J_{z}i\eta_{2n+1}^{\beta}\eta_{2n}^{\alpha}\right]\\
&+\sum_{n=1}\left[Ki\eta_{2n}^{\beta}\eta_{2n-1}^{\alpha}+\tilde{K}(-i)\eta_{2n+2}^{\beta}\eta_{2n-1}^{\alpha}\eta_{2n}^{\alpha}\eta_{2n}^{\beta}\eta_{2n+1}^{\alpha}\eta_{2n+1}^{\beta}\right].\end{aligned}

(S23)

The model has conserved quantities
\tau_{n}=\sigma_{2n}^{z}\sigma_{2n+1}^{z}=-i\eta_{2n}^{\alpha}\eta_{2n+1}^{\beta}
, with which the Hamiltonian is written as

\mathcal{H}_{h}^{(d)}=\sum_{n=0}\left[(J_{y}-J_{x}\tau_{n})i\eta_{2n}^{\beta}\eta_{2n+1}^{\alpha}+J_{z}\tau_{n}\right]+\sum_{n=1}\left[K(-i)\eta_{2n-1}^{\alpha}\eta_{2n}^{\beta}+\tilde{K}(-\tau_{n})\eta_{2n-1}^{\alpha}\eta_{2n}^{\beta}\eta_{2n+1}^{\alpha}\eta_{2n+2}^{\beta}\right].

(S24)

Then we group together sites
(2n,2n+1)
with
n=0,1,2,\cdots
, and redefine
\eta_{2n}^{\beta}\rightarrow\tilde{\eta}_{n}^{\beta}
and
\eta_{2n+1}^{\alpha}\rightarrow\tilde{\eta}_{n}^{\alpha}
. The Hamiltonian becomes

\mathcal{H}_{h}^{(d)}=\sum_{n=0}\left[(J_{y}-J_{x}\tau_{n})i\tilde{\eta}_{n}^{\beta}\tilde{\eta}_{n}^{\alpha}+J_{z}\tau_{n}\right]+\sum_{n=1}\left[K(-i)\tilde{\eta}_{n-1}^{\alpha}\tilde{\eta}_{n}^{\beta}+\tilde{K}(-\tau_{n})\tilde{\eta}_{n-1}^{\alpha}\tilde{\eta}_{n}^{\beta}\tilde{\eta}_{n}^{\alpha}\tilde{\eta}_{n+1}^{\beta}\right].

(S25)

Next we introduce the first-order spin
\bm{S}_{n}
for each pair by a new set of JW transformation

\tilde{\eta}_{n}^{\alpha}=S_{n}^{y}\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(-S_{j}^{x}),\qquad\tilde{\eta}_{n}^{\beta}=S_{n}^{z}\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(-S_{j}^{x}),\qquad i\tilde{\eta}_{n}^{\beta}\tilde{\eta}_{n}^{\alpha}=S_{n}^{x}.

(S26)

Using this the Hamiltonian (
S24
) becomes Eq. (
7
), namely

\mathcal{H}_{h}^{(d)}=\sum_{n=0}\left[(J_{y}-J_{x}\tau_{n})S_{n}^{x}+J_{z}\tau_{n}\right]+\sum_{n=1}\left[KS_{n-1}^{z}S_{n}^{z}+\tilde{K}\tau_{n}S_{n-1}^{z}S_{n+1}^{z}\right].

(S27)

This Hamiltonian with static distribution of
\{\tau_{n}\}
involves Ising interaction of second-nearest neighbours, making it the ANNNI model.

For the next step we look for relations between the first-order spin
\bm{S}
and the zeroth-order spin
\bm{\sigma}
. To do this we note that

S_{n}^{z}=\eta_{2n}^{\beta}\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(-i\eta_{2j}^{\beta}\eta_{2j+1}^{\alpha})
and
\sigma_{2n}^{z}=\eta_{2n}^{\beta}\prod_{j=0}^{2n-1}(i\eta_{j}^{\alpha}\eta_{j}^{\beta})
; so we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sigma_{2n}^{z}=&\eta_{2n}^{\beta}(-i\eta_{0}^{\beta}\eta_{0}^{\alpha})(-i\eta_{1}^{\beta}\eta_{1}^{\alpha})(-i\eta_{2}^{\beta}\eta_{2}^{\alpha})(-i\eta_{3}^{\beta}\eta_{3}^{\alpha})\cdots(-i\eta_{2n-1}^{\beta}\eta_{2n-1}^{\alpha})\\
=&S_{n}^{z}\tau_{0}\tau_{1}\cdots\tau_{n-1}.\end{aligned}

(S28)

Using
\tau_{n}=\sigma_{2n}^{z}\sigma_{2n+1}^{z}=-i\eta_{2n}^{\alpha}\eta_{2n+1}^{\beta}
, this becomes the first part of the relation,

S_{n}^{z}=\prod_{j=0}^{2n}\sigma_{j}^{z}.

(S29)

On the other hand we have the second part of the relation
S_{n}^{x}=\sigma_{2n}^{y}\sigma_{2n+1}^{y}
for the same reason as the vertical ladder, see Eq. (
S8
).

Here the
dipole parity
can also be defined, namely
\mathcal{I}=\prod_{n=0}S_{n}^{z}=\prod_{n=0}\prod_{j=0}^{2n}\sigma_{j}^{z}
, satisfying
\{S_{n}^{x},\mathcal{I}\}=0
. Explicitly the dipole parity can be written as
\mathcal{I}=\prod_{j}(\sigma_{j}^{z})^{N-x_{j}}
, where
x_{j}
is the
effective coordinate
of the spin site
j
, which coincides with the
x
-coordinates of the sites in the horizontal geometry shown in Fig.
1
.

Dynamical structural factor

The zero-temperature dynamical structural factor can be used to distinguish the phases of the ladder models. Here we derive the results in the main text.

By definition, the zero-temperature dynamical structural factor for spin chain is

D^{\alpha\beta}_{0}(q,\omega)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j,l=1}^{N}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dte^{iql+i\omega t}\langle S_{j}^{\alpha}(t)S_{j-l}^{\beta}(0)\rangle.

(S30)

Here we denote the structural factor by
D
instead of the standard
S
to avoid confusion with the spin.
For the 2D spin ladder we are considering, the zero-temperature dynamical structural factor is given by

D^{zz}(q_{x},q_{y},\omega)=\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{j,l=1}^{N}\sum_{y,y^{\prime}=0,1}\int dte^{i\omega t}e^{iq_{x}l+iq_{y}(y-y^{\prime})}\langle S_{j+l,y}^{z}(t)S_{j,y^{\prime}}^{z}(0)\rangle.

(S31)

V.3
The vertical ladder

For the vertical ladder, the sum over
y,y^{\prime}
in (
S31
) can be done before the integration and gives

D^{zz}(q_{x},q_{y},\omega)=\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{j,l=1}^{N}\int dte^{i\omega t+iq_{x}l}\left[\sum_{y,y^{\prime}=0,1}e^{iq_{y}(y-y^{\prime})}\langle S_{j+l,y}^{z}(t)S_{j,y^{\prime}}^{z}(0)\rangle\right].

(S32)

If the system is in the
dipole phase
, namely
S_{i,1}^{z}\equiv-S_{i,0}^{z}
, the dynamical structural factor is

D^{zz}(q_{x},q_{y},\omega)=(1-\cos q_{y})\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j,l=1}^{N}\int dte^{i\omega t+iq_{x}l}\langle S_{j+l,0}^{z}(t)S_{j,0}^{z}(0)\rangle=(1-\cos q_{y})D_{0}^{zz}(q_{x},\omega).

(S33)

If it is in the
charge-pair phase
, namely
S_{i,1}^{z}\equiv S_{i,0}^{z}
, the dynamical structural factor is

D^{zz}(q_{x},q_{y},\omega)=(1+\cos q_{y})\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j,l=1}^{N}\int dte^{i\omega t+iq_{x}l}\langle S_{j+l,0}^{z}(t)S_{j,0}^{z}(0)\rangle=(1+\cos q_{y})D_{0}^{zz}(q_{x},\omega).

(S34)

To summarize, if
S_{i,1}^{z}\equiv\pm S_{i,0}^{z}
, the dynamical structural factor is

D^{zz}(q_{x},q_{y},\omega)=(1\pm\cos q_{y})D_{0}^{zz}(q_{x},\omega).

(S35)

In the above,
D_{0}^{zz}(q_{x},\omega)
is the dynamical structural factor for the Ising chain defined in (
S30
).

V.4
The horizontal ladder

We now move on to the zero-temperature dynamical structural factor for the horizontal ladder. By definition we label the unit cells of the lattices by its coordinates
(x,y)
; inside each unit cell, the two sites are labelled by 0 and 1. The dynamical structural factor (
S31
) can be written as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} D^{zz}(q_{x},q_{y},\omega)=&\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{x,x^{\prime}}\sum_{y,y^{\prime}}\int dte^{i\omega t}e^{iq_{y}(y-y^{\prime})}e^{iq_{x}(x-x^{\prime})}\big[\langle S_{x,0,y}^{z}(t)S^{z}_{x^{\prime},0,y^{\prime}}(0)\rangle\\
&+\langle S_{x,0,y}^{z}(t)S_{x^{\prime},1,y^{\prime}}^{z}(0)\rangle e^{-iq_{x}}+\langle S_{x,1,y}^{z}(t)S_{x^{\prime},0,y^{\prime}}^{z}(0)\rangle e^{iq_{x}}+\langle S_{x,1,y}^{z}(t)S_{x^{\prime},1,y^{\prime}}^{z}(0)\rangle\big].\end{aligned}

(S36)

For the
dipole phase
of the horizontal ladder, we have
S_{x,1,y}\equiv-S_{x,0,y}
for all unit cells. So the dynamical structural factor reads

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} D^{zz}(q_{x},q_{y},\omega)&=(1-\cos q_{x})\bigg[\frac{1}{N}\sum_{x,x^{\prime}}\sum_{y,y^{\prime}}\int dte^{i\omega t}e^{iq_{y}(y-y^{\prime})+iq_{x}(x-x^{\prime})}\langle S^{z}_{x,0,y}(t)S^{z}_{x^{\prime},0,y^{\prime}}(0)\rangle\bigg]\\
&=(1-\cos q_{x})\tilde{D}^{zz}(q_{x},q_{y},\omega).\end{aligned}

(S37)

In the
charge-pair phase
of the horizontal ladder, we have
S_{x,1,y}\equiv S_{x,0,y}
for all unit cells. So the dynamical structural factor is given by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} D^{zz}(q_{x},q_{y},\omega)&=(1+\cos q_{x})\bigg[\frac{1}{N}\sum_{x,x^{\prime}}\sum_{y,y^{\prime}}\int dte^{i\omega t}e^{iq_{x}(x-x^{\prime})+iq_{y}(y-y^{\prime})}\langle S^{z}_{x,0,y}(t)S^{z}_{x^{\prime},0,y^{\prime}}(0)\rangle\bigg]\\
&=(1+\cos q_{x})\tilde{D}^{zz}(q_{x},q_{y},\omega).\end{aligned}

(S38)

In the above,
\tilde{D}^{zz}(q_{x},q_{y},\omega)
represents the zero-temperature dynamical structural factor for a zig-zag ANNNI model. To summarize, if
S_{x,1,y}^{z}\equiv\pm S_{x,0,y}^{z}
, the zero-temperature dynamical structural factor for the horizontal ladder is

D^{zz}(q_{x},q_{y},\omega)=(1\pm\cos q_{x})\tilde{D}^{zz}(q_{x},q_{y},\omega).

(S39)

10

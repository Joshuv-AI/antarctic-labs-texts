The effective running quark mass from the variational solution of the 4dimensional Bethe-Salpeter equation for charmonium
V. Šauli

Abstract
The scale dependence of the modulus of the effective charm quark mass is determined from measured values of leptonic decay constants and vector charmonium masses. Unlike nonrelativistic approximations, the Poincaré-invariant framework based on the full four-dimensional (4D) Bethe-Salpeter equation does not support a single-valued constituent quark mass approximation. To overcome the obstacles of low accuracy associated with standard methods, the variational method is used to solve the full 4D Bethe-Salpeter equations. This method achieves precise agreement with the experiment while avoiding unphysical abnormal states. The quark mass must slide to provide almost unique values for the charm running mass at the scale of individual charmonium states, achieving precise agreement with the experiment on one hand and avoiding unphysical abnormal states on the other.

I
Introduction

Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) is a theory of interacting gluons and quarks, providing a comprehensive explanation of hadrons properties.
At large momenta, QCD is asymptotically free
GW1973
;
POL1973
as a consequence of decreasing the effective running coupling with increasing momenta transfered.
Consequently, perturbation theory becomes applicable at large momentum transfer,
while computational techniques substantially change at low momentum scales where further assumptions are required.

Green’s functions are not directly observable; their properties must be studied indirectly. They can be studied self-consistently by their imprints in observable, scheme-independent quantities. In QCD, these are e.g. the effective running charge and running quark masses, which are extracted from suitable experimental observables.
Nonperturbatively, not all definitions of couplings and masses are equivalent
alfy
. Nevertheless, concepts are conceptually synonymous, and the disparities among them are thoroughly explicable.
The quantity under consideration in this study is the heavy quark propagator, expressed as follows:

S(p)=\not p-<m(\xi)>\,;

(1)

where
<m(\xi)>
is the mean effective running mass at the scale
\xi

In the specific case under consideration, the scale is to be linked to the mean of the four-momentum
p^{2}
carried by the valence quark inside a given quarkonium.
Therefore, we expect that
\xi\simeq M^{2}/4
for a meson of the mass
M
. Indeed, our effective mass can be understand as discretized version of the Grunberg
Grunberg
renormalization invariant dynamical mass
m(p)
. In this study we ignore the fact that the quark mass
m(\xi)
can be a complex quantity at considered scale. This is a particularly intricate aspect of quantum chromodynamics (QCD) due to confinement; nevertheless, the effect is likely to be significant for heavier excited states. It is noteworthy that the extracted values of quark masses are found to be in alignment with the running masses that have been extracted by alternative methods
zeus2014
;
hera2017
, although it is evident that the concept of a running mass differs in these cases.

The propagators of light quarks (u,d and s) have been studied in various methods: by lattice approach

of the running quark exists to the date.

The aforementioned studies indicate a complex structure of quark propagators; however, the sliding of the dynamical quark is mostly ignored in quarkonia studies.
On the other hand, the running of the heavy quark mass is expected from QCD theory and is an experimentally well-established

hera2017
;

The masses of charmonia are spread in
2GeV

Ignoring well-known field theory phenomenon in theoretical calculations can easily lead to misinterpretations when considering quark-mass-sensitive observables.
Discriminating between models that are sensitive enough to detect the effect of a running quark mass in the quarkonium sector will not be an easy task, noting a part of the effect could be
included in nonrelativistic approximations already used. Here, we consider leptonic decays, while
electromagnetic transitions could provide additional insight. For a comprehensive

We attempted to determine the leptonic decays by solving the BSE using the standard eigenvalue method and we found and confirm that
precise determination of leptonic decay constants is extremely difficult.
Our findings indicate that the decay constants

Such obstacles, which are not well pronounced in the literature and are impossible to explain without a field theory perspective, lie behind the unsatisfactory quotations for leptonic decays of excited states within the rare use of 4D BSE

Further complications arise, since for certain masses, the solution does not exist at all beacuse it is prohibited by the absence of a physical norm.
To illustrate this more precisely, we solved the BSE equation using the variational method instead of the eigenvalue method.
We found that the desired BSE solutions exist. However, one must carefully consider the effect of running masses.

Determining the running charm mass based on these considerations is a central aim of this paper. The interesting task of matching

II
Quarkonia and Their Leptonic Decays in the BSE Formalism

Charmonia are quarkonia with a c-anti c quark composition. The masses of these particles are sufficiently distinct from those of other neutral mesons,
thereby ensuring their separation and preventing any mixing.
In the region below the open charm threshold of
DD
production, there exist several narrow excited states, which offer an ideal theoretical laboratory for study.
The higher excited states that decay to
DD
pairs are found to be highly affected by open flavor channels. We guess the size of the effect only,
but not leaving the frame of homogeneous BSE. Here, we describe the solution for the
1^{P=-1,C=-1}
charmonium state,
for which experimental data on the leptonic decay constants impose the most stringent constraints.

For all
\psi
mesons, the mixing can be ignored and the leptonic decay can be calculated from a simple formula :

F_{V}M_{V}=\frac{1}{3}Tr_{CD}\int\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}[\gamma_{\mu}S_{c}(q_{+})\Gamma_{V}^{\mu}(q,Q)S_{c}(q_{-})]

(2)

where
S_{c}
stands for charm quark propagator and
q_{\pm}=q\pm Q/2
.
The formula (
2

IKR1999
and first used in
MATA1999
,
assumes that the meson pole part does not enter in QED Ward identity,
\Gamma_{V}^{\mu}
is thus a fully transverse Bethe-Salpeter vertex
function satisfying
\Gamma.Q=0
.

The transverse function
\Gamma_{V}
is called the bound state vertex function and satisfies the homogeneous BSE

\Gamma(p,Q)=i\int\frac{d^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}S(k_{+})\Gamma_{var}(k,Q)S(k_{-})V(k,p,Q)

(3)

where

The function

in the Eq. (

Proper incorporation of the running charge allows to use the single interacting kernel for all states, eliminating fine tuning largely but not completely.
Additionally, we assume a Schwinger mechanism for generating the mass scale associated with gluons as suggested by V. Mathieu an collaborators in
AIMP2012
.
This is achieved by implementing the following choice of BSE interaction kernel:

V(k,p)=\gamma^{\nu}_{T}(q)\times\gamma_{\nu}2\alpha(q)\left[\frac{3/2}{q^{2}-m_{g}^{2}}-\frac{1}{q^{2}-\Lambda_{c}^{2}}\right]

(4)

where

stands for dirac matrices transversely projected with respect to gluon momentum
q

is the effective gluon mass, for which we take
m_{g}=1.12GeV
.
This is in a good agreement with other studies , e.g with the 1982 estimate made already in
CO1982
.

\Lambda_{c}
was found to be an useful damping mass parameter;
\Lambda_{c}\simeq 2-4GeV
, and
\alpha
is
an infrared smooth QCD running coupling, for wwhich we use the following formula

\alpha(q)=\frac{4\pi}{(11-2N_{f}/3)ln(e-\frac{q^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}})}

(5)

where we take
N_{f}=3
, the constant
\Lambda
is perturbative QCD scale, numerically
\Lambda^{2}=0.1GeV^{2}
,

e=2.718..
and the prefactor in Eq. (
4

\alpha
is choosen here.

Another crucial assumption is that the observed charmonia are normal bound state, not the ghosts. The solutions must satisfy canonical normalization
condition (see for instance
IKR1999
) and we exclude parameter space giving ghost solution from our consideration.

III
Solution and the quark masses determination

Variational methods are widely used in quantum mechanics for complex systems that are not exactly solvable.
They have been used for the quarkonium system as well, as early as the 1980s, with an particular focus on the dependence of energy levels on quark mass, as discussed in
DHP1981
.
Their use persists in contemporary light-front Hamiltonian approaches
Choi2007
;
RAM2025
. We once again implement this approach to quarkonia; however, we expand it to quantum field theory.

Note that the BSE wave function, usually defined as

\Psi(k,Q)=S(k_{+})\Gamma(k,Q)S(k_{-})
, becomes highly singular for a weakly bounded states.
This involves picking the most important poles or other intricate three-dimensional reductions, which establish the quantum mechanical limit of the original BSE.
Additionally, there is no known relation between light cone Hamiltonian approaches and the BSE formalism. To avoid associated ambiguities, we work with the full 4D BSE.
Determining the quark mass requires determining the propagator
S
given by Eq. (
1
), that we use in the BSE calculation.
In our case, the variational solution must include the full BSE and its fully covariant normalization condition.
Equivalently, we minimize the following difference:

D(-)=\sum(\Gamma^{trial}(k,Q)-\Gamma^{l.h.s}(k,Q))^{2}

(6)

with respect to all parameters used in the trial functions, as well as with respect to the effective quark mass appearing in the Eq. (
1
).

In Eq. (
6
) the sum runs over the continuous four-momentum
k
as well as over the BSE components. Keeping the exact BSE solution means

D(-)=0
for a given meson. In the approximated case, we look for the minimum.

Since, according to our findings, the integral
\int d^{4}k\Gamma^{2}
is convergent, we calculate normalized difference for a given charmonium vector as following

\chi_{V}^{2}=\frac{D(-)}{D(+)}.

(7)

When dealing with single BSE component, the function
g(l,Q)=\Gamma_{l.h.s}(k,Q)/(\Gamma_{var}(k^{2}=a,k.Q=b)
provides the same minimum as the function
\Gamma
. We choose convenient scales
a
and
b
and use the function
g
instead of
\Gamma
to speed up convergence.
For the purpose of the presented study we relax with
\chi_{V}^{2}<5.10^{-3}
, which condition turnes to be is stringent enough to determine the effective quark mass using the combined knowledge of the meson mass and the decay constant.

IV
Conclusion and further prospect

We solved the BSE for excited charmonia using a minimization/variational technique.
In addition to known masses, we used experimentally observed leptonic decays to determine the net structure of the effective quark mass at each quarkonium mass scale.
Within the kernel approximation based on the effective QCD running coupling, we obtained a covariant solution for the
BS vertex function, provided that the effective quark mass slides with the scale.
The precision with which we can determine wave functions for narrow states seems limited only by the computational effort required.

We found that no simple model can provide decay constants and spectra using a single constituent quark mass value.
Furthermore, to mimic the interaction for an open charm system, the entire interaction could be slightly altered.
The absolute value of the slope of the
f_{V}
function with respect to the change in
m_{q}
is unexpectedly large, except for local minima that correspond with experimentally observed values. Regions with negative slopes correspond with
normal solutions, while regions with positive slopes correspond with ghosts. Within experimental errors on the spectrum and the decay constant, the quark mass is
then determined at given meson scale.

The evolution of the quark mass with respect to the scale was not calculated in the presented paper; rather, it was obtained from the experiments on leptonic decays.
It will be interesting to see how the observed sensitivity of the decay constant function changes when theoretical calculations are available. A careful analytical continuation of the quark propagator must be performed to achieve this goal.
In any case, we expect the method of determining trial functions for BS vertices described here to be applicable.
Compared to other approaches, such as models based on quantum mechanics or its relativistic light cone extensions, the 4D BSE formalism seems unwieldy. Understanding the reasons is part of the knowledge that could be gained from the proposed study.
We plan to use BS vertices in calculations of electromagnetic form factors and quarkonium transitions. This could be a challenging test of the reliability and expected universality of the formalism.

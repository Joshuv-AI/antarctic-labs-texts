Compactness in Constructive Mathematics via Affine Logic
Kazumi Kasaura

Abstract
We study topology, particularly compactness, as an extension of Shulman’s work for constructive mathematics via affine logic, while allowing propositional impredicativity. In addition to some basic properties of compactness, we prove the extreme value theorem and the Heine-Borel theorem for ”cuts”, which are a version of Dedekind cuts in affine logic. Moreover, from the antithesis translation of the Heine-Borel theorem for cuts to intuitionistic logic, we derive the Heine-Borel theorem for one-sided reals intuitionistically, and have verified its proof with the interactive theorem prover. The code is available at https://github.com/hziwara/CutsHeineBorel .

Introduction

5
introduces constructive mathematics with affine logic and a method called ’antithesis translation’ to convert a proposition in affine logic to one in intuitionistic logic.
Moreover, it is demonstrated that notions in existing constructive mathematics can be derived through translation from affine logic.
Specifically, in topology, the notion of a topology in affine logic is introduced, and it is shown that its translation includes existing constructive notions for topologies as special cases.

This paper extends the research on topology, focusing particularly on compactness.
While the equality structure (Bishop sets) is considered and subsets are defined as predicates compatible with this structure in
5
, we decide to abandon it.
The reason is its incompatibility with multiplicative logical operators.
See Remark
1
.
Instead, we treat all functions to the type of propositions as subsets.

First, in Section
3
, we treat some basic notions of topology.
We introduce an affine version of the notion of compactness and prove some properties corresponding to classical results in Section
4
.
Also, in Section
5
, we introduce the topology on the type of ’cuts’ defined in
5
, which is an affine version of the extended Dedekind reals and corresponds to unbounded intervals in the antithesis translation.
We prove the extreme value theorem and the Heine-Borel theorem for the topology.
The proof of the latter depends on the assumption of the propositional impredicativity.

In addition, in Section
6
, we consider the antithesis translation of the Heine-Borel theorem for cuts.
From this, we derive the proof of the Heine-Borel theorem for one-sided reals and have verified it with the interactive theorem prover.
While this proof is intuitionistic, it shows only existence without giving a way to construct it, because it relies essentially on the propositional impredicativity.

Remark 1
.

In
5
, a set
X
is defined as a type equipped with an equality relation, and a subset is defined as a function
s:X\to\mathbf{\Omega}
such that
x=y\otimes x\in s\multimap y\in s
.
Since the multiplicative intersection
s\mathbin{\boxtimes}t
of two subsets
s,t
is not always a subset, the operator is defined as

s\hat{\mathbin{\boxtimes}}t:=\left\{x:X\ \middle|\ \exists y:X,\left(x=y\otimes y\in s\otimes y\in t\right)\right\}.

However, this operator is not even associative:

\displaystyle\left(s\hat{\mathbin{\boxtimes}}t\right)\hat{\mathbin{\boxtimes}}u

\displaystyle=\left\{x:X\ \middle|\ \exists y,z:X,\left(x=y\otimes y=z\otimes z\in s\otimes z\in t\otimes y\in u\right)\right\},

\displaystyle s\hat{\mathbin{\boxtimes}}\left(t\hat{\mathbin{\boxtimes}}u\right)

\displaystyle=\left\{x:X\ \middle|\ \exists y,z:X,\left(x=y\otimes y=z\otimes y\in s\otimes z\in t\otimes z\in u\right)\right\}.

This makes it difficult to consider the multiplicative intersection of finitely many subsets, which is important in our study.
Thus, we do not consider equality relations, and any function to
\mathbf{\Omega}
is treated as a subset.

Topology

In this section, after reviewing the definition of topology in affine logic, we introduce notions of open and closed sets, basis, and product topology.

First, following
5

In addition, we consider a Moore operator
(
4
)
and a Čech operator
(
1
)
, both of which are weaker concepts than topology.
Since these conditions are axioms, we can use them arbitrary times even in affine logic.

Definition 2
.

Let
\inte:\mathcal{P}(X)\to\mathcal{P}(X)
be an operator on subsets.
It is called
Moore interior operator
if the following conditions are satisfied:

I1
.

\forall s:\mathcal{P}(X),\inte s\subseteq s
,

I2
.

\forall s,t:\mathcal{P}(X),\left(s\subseteq t\multimap\inte s\subseteq\inte t\right)
,

I3
.

\forall s:\mathcal{P}(X),\inte s\subseteq\inte(\inte s)
.

An
interior operator
is a Moore interior operator satisfying additionally the following conditions:

I4
.

X\subseteq\inte X
,

I5
.

\forall s,t:\mathcal{P}(X),\inte s\mathbin{\boxtimes}\inte t\subseteq\inte\left(s\mathbin{\sqcap}t\right)
.

An operator is called
Čech interior operator
if it satisfies the conditions for interior operator except
I3
.

As a classical definition, we introduce the definition of topology as a set of open sets.

Definition 3
.

A
Moore collection of open subsets
is a subset
\mathcal{O}:\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(X))
of the power set satisfying the following condition:

O1
.

\forall s,t:\mathcal{P}(X),\left(s\in\mathcal{O}\multimap s\subseteq t\otimes t\subseteq s\multimap t\in\mathcal{O}\right)
.

O2
.

\forall s:\mathcal{P}(X),\left\{x:X\ \middle|\ \exists t:\mathcal{P}(X),\left(t\in\mathcal{O}\otimes t\subseteq s\otimes x\in t\right)\right\}\in\mathcal{O}
.

A
collection of open subsets
is a Moore collection of open subsets satisfying additionally the following conditions:

O3
.

X\in\mathcal{O}
,

O4
.

\forall s,t:\mathcal{P}(X),\left(s\in\mathcal{O}\otimes t\in\mathcal{O}\multimap\exists u:\mathcal{P}(X),\left(u\in\mathcal{O}\otimes s\mathbin{\boxtimes}t\subseteq u\otimes u\subseteq s\mathbin{\sqcap}t\right)\right)
,

O1
says that
\mathcal{O}
is a ’subset’ in the meaning of
5

O2
corresponds to the condition that any union of open subsets is open. See Remark
5

O3

Proposition 4
.

Moore interior operators correspond one-to-one to Moore collections of open subsets, and interior operators correspond one-to-one to collections of open subsets.

Proof.

From a Moore interior operator
\inte
, we can define
\mathcal{O}
as

\mathcal{O}:=\{s\mid s\subseteq\inte s\}.

(1)

Then,
O1
follows from
I2
.
We show
O2
. For any

, let

s^{\prime}:=\left\{x:X\ \middle|\ \exists t:\mathcal{P}(X),\left(t\in\mathcal{O}\otimes t\subseteq s\otimes x\in t\right)\right\}.

Because
I3
means
\inte s\in\mathcal{O}
, we have
\inte s\subseteq s^{\prime}
by taking
\inte s
as
t
.
Moreover,
s^{\prime}\subseteq\inte s
follows from
I2
. Thus, using
\inte s\in\mathcal{O}
again,
s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}
is derived from
O1
.

Conversely, from a Moore collection
\mathcal{O}
of open sets, we can define the interior operator as

\inte s:=\left\{x:X\ \middle|\ \exists t:\mathcal{P}(X),\left(t\in\mathcal{O}\otimes t\subseteq s\otimes x\in t\right)\right\}.

(2)

Then,
I1
and
I2
are trivial.
We can show that

s\in\mathcal{O}\multimap s\subseteq\inte s
by taking
s
itself as

Moreover, since

We prove that interior operators correspond to collections of open sets via this correspondence.
Clearly,
O3

by taking

We show

and

, we can take

Remark 5
.

Let

For an indexed family of subsets

However, for a subset of the power set

Definition 6

An indexed family

\forall x:X,\exists\iota:\mathcal{I},x\in\mathcal{B}(\iota)

For an indexed family
\mathcal{B}:\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{P}(X)

The conditions

We show

We show

4
Compactness

In this section, we define an affine version of compactness and prove its basic properties.
We use the notions of filter and cluster points to define compactness. Compared with the definition by open coverings, this definition has a merit that we do not have to quantify index types.
Note that, our definitions are not only for topological spaces but in general operators.

First, we define the notion of filters.

Definition 12
.

A subset of the power set
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(X))
is called a
filter on
X
if it is monotonic, non-empty, and closed under multiplicative intersection with
\ofcourse
-modality.

\mathbf{Fil}\left(\mathcal{F}\right):=\ofcourse{\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\left(\forall s,t:\mathcal{P}(X),\left(s\in\mathcal{F}\multimap s\subseteq t\multimap t\in\mathcal{F}\right)\right)\otimes X\in\mathcal{F}\\
\otimes\forall s,t:\mathcal{P}(X),\left(s\in\mathcal{F}\otimes t\in\mathcal{F}\multimap s\mathbin{\boxtimes}t\in\mathcal{F}\right)\end{array}\right)}.

This definition is justified by the following lemma.

Lemma 13
.

Let
A

In this section, we define the topology on cuts and prove the propositions corresponding to the extreme-value theorem and the Heine-Borel theorem in the classical mathematics.

In this section, we consider the antithesis translation of the Heine-Borel theorem in the previous section.

\displaystyle(P\with Q)^{+}

\displaystyle:=P^{+}\land Q^{+},

\displaystyle(P\with Q)^{-}

\displaystyle:=P^{-}\lor Q^{-},

\displaystyle(P\oplus Q)^{+}

\displaystyle:=P^{+}\lor Q^{+},

\displaystyle(P\oplus Q)^{-}

\displaystyle:=P^{-}\land Q^{-},

\displaystyle({P}^{\bot})^{+}

\displaystyle:=P^{-},

\displaystyle({P}^{\bot})^{-}

\displaystyle:=P^{+},

\displaystyle(P\otimes Q)^{+}

\displaystyle:=P^{+}\land Q^{+},

\displaystyle(P\otimes Q)^{-}

\displaystyle:=(P^{+}\to Q^{-})\land(Q^{+}\to P^{-}),

\displaystyle(P\invamp Q)^{+}

\displaystyle:=(P^{-}\to Q^{+})\land(Q^{-}\to P^{+}),

\displaystyle(P\invamp Q)^{-}

\displaystyle:=P^{-}\land Q^{-},

\displaystyle\top^{+}

\displaystyle:=\top,

\displaystyle\top^{-}

\displaystyle:=\bot,

\displaystyle\bot^{+}

\displaystyle:=\bot,

\displaystyle\bot^{-}

\displaystyle:=\top,

\displaystyle\left(\ofcourse{P}\right)^{+}

\displaystyle:=P^{+},

\displaystyle\left(\ofcourse{P}\right)^{-}

\displaystyle:=\lnot P^{+},

\displaystyle\left(\whynot{P}\right)^{+}

\displaystyle:=\lnot P^{-},

\displaystyle\left(\whynot{P}\right)^{-}

\displaystyle:=P^{-},

\displaystyle(\exists x,P)^{+}

\displaystyle:=\exists x,P^{+},

\displaystyle(\exists x,P)^{-}

\displaystyle(\forall x,P)^{+}

\displaystyle:=\forall x,P^{+},

\displaystyle(\forall x,P)^{-}

\displaystyle:=\exists x,P^{-}.

7
Conclusion

We developed the theory of compactness in the constructive mathematics with affine logic. We prove some basic theorems for compactness, including the Heine-Borel theorem, and verify its translation to intuitionistic logic.
These results imply that this approach to constructive analysis has great room for further development.
The definitions we introduce in this paper are tentative, because when defining notions in affine logic, whether to choose additive or multiplicative operations, and whether to apply the exponentials, are delicate issues.

While our definitions are natural in affine logic, their translations to intuitionistic logic are sometimes complicated and difficult to consider without affine logic. Thus, our study confirms that affine logic is an effective approach for constructive topology.
Moreover, the result that the Heine-Borel theorem essentially depends not on the excluded middle but on propositional impredicativity provides a novel insight into the nature of the continuum.

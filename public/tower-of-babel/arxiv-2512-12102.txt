Abstract
The Binarity at LOw Metallicity (BLOeM) survey is an ESO large programme designed to obtain multi-epoch spectroscopy for 929 massive stars in the Small Magellanic Cloud (SMC). It will provide binary fractions and orbital configurations of binary systems, and search for dormant black-hole binary candidates (OB+BH). Here we present projected rotational velocities ( \varv ​ sin ⁡ i \varv\sin i ) of all sources and, using the multiplicity properties presented in previous papers, we derive the \varv ​ sin ⁡ i \varv\sin i distributions of apparent single stars, single lined spectroscopic (SB1) binaries, and SB2 systems. We identify a locus in the Hertzsprung-Russell diagram where rotational velocities decrease significantly; we interpret this feature as broadly corresponding to the terminal-age main sequence. The main sequence cohort is distinguished by a broad range of \varv ​ sin ⁡ i \varv\sin i values, but with a strong peak in the distribution in the range 30–60 km s -1 , close to the resolution limit of ∼ \sim 30 km s -1 . Sources in this low \varv ​ sin ⁡ i \varv\sin i peak are distributed throughout the main sequence, and are also present in the SB1 sample, though less prominent than in the single star distribution. A preliminary analysis of the lowest \varv ​ sin ⁡ i \varv\sin i cohort, that includes SB1 systems, implies that roughly one third may be nitrogen rich and we speculate that this cohort is a mix of pristine single stars, long period binaries, and merger products. The SB2 systems appear to be mostly short period binaries in synchronous rotation and have \varv ​ sin ⁡ i \varv\sin i estimates distributed around a mean value of ≈ \approx 140 km s -1 . Higher \varv ​ sin ⁡ i \varv\sin i sources are also present in the single and SB1 systems, all of which have tail to higher \varv ​ sin ⁡ i \varv\sin i values, consistent with tidal and mass-transfer effects. The supergiants, with a few exceptions, have low \varv ​ sin ⁡ i \varv\sin i , the bulk of these systems being essentially unresolved at current spectral resolution ( ∼ \sim 30 km s -1 ).

2
Observational data

We used the BLOeM data release 4 (DR4) normalized spectra comprising 21 epochs per the 8 fields, except for fields 4 and 8 that have 22 and 23 epochs respectively. All epochs were merged to produce combined spectra for the analysis, with individual spectra being corrected for radial velocity shifts relative to combined 9-epoch DR3 spectra, derived using cross-correlation of the 4360–4560 Å region
(
74
, see)
, and weighted by their observed counts.
The multi-epoch nature of the combined spectra has some implications for the subsequent analysis.
Spectroscopic binaries with a weak secondary spectrum (SB1 systems) for example are velocity shifted to the rest frame of their primary star, and hence weak absorption lines of the secondary star may be smeared over the wings of the lines of the primary (see Fig.
2
).
Similarly, any line centroid shifts due to pulsation, such as
\beta
-Cepheid pulsation
(
11
, see the review by)
, may result in some smearing of the line profile.
Double lined spectroscopic binaries (SB2) present a special problem and our approach to these systems is described further in subsection
3.1
.

3
Methodology

The FT approach adopted here for the BLOeM sample has been widely discussed in the literature, see section
5
for several applications to massive stars in nearby galaxies, while practical considerations for its application to hot stars have been discussed in detail by
98
.
Briefly, the FT of the Unsöld-Struve rotational broadening function
(see
19
, for assumptions and limitations)
is a Bessell function of first kind that has zeros at positions characteristic of the rotational velocity and limb darkening coefficient of the star
(
16
;
17
)
.
Formally,
\varv\sin i
=x_{0}/f_{0}
, where
f_{0}
is the position of the first minimum, and
x_{0}
is a constant depending on the limb darkening coefficient
\alpha
.
In this work, in common with most comparable FT work in the literature, we adopt a limb darkening coefficient of
\alpha=0.6
, the gray solution, in the standard linear limb darkening law
(
44
)
,
implying that
\varv\sin i
=0.66/f_{0}
.
Direct determination of
\alpha
using model atmospheres
(
50
;
82
)
demonstrates that
\alpha\sim
0.2–0.4 for main sequence OB stars, appropriate
x_{0}
values
(
19
;
56
, see)
then imply that
\varv\sin i
estimates for these stars will be overestimated by
\sim
5–3% respectively.
However, since the median error of our results from other sources is
\sim
20%, we have not applied any corrections to our data.
Furthermore, given the moderate resolving power of the BLOeM data,
\sim
7 000, and the impact of binaries on GOF results (discussed below) this paper focuses primarily on rotational velocities, and ignores the potential impact of macroturbulence
(
85
;
99
;
97
, see)
.

In the current approach, for each BLOeM source, we derived the FT for a number of absorption lines, their first minima yielding separate
\varv\sin i
measurements, the mean value of which yielded a measurement we refer to as
\varv\sin i_{\rm FT}
.
The choice of lines for a given star was dictated by a combination of signal-to-noise (s/n), spectral type, and line width, resulting in 3–10 lines being identified per star.
The strong He
i
lines (
\lambda\lambda
4143, 4387, 4471) were measured in all but a few of the hottest O-type stars, the coolest stars in the BAF group, and a number of the OBe stars.
However for stars with low
\varv\sin i
these values were supplanted by measurements from the weak lines of metals (e.g. N
ii

\lambda
3995 if present, Si
iii

\lambda
4553) and He
i
(
\lambda\lambda
4168, 4437) as permitted by s/n, and spectral type (primarily B-type supergiants and bright giants).
Given the small number of measurements per star, formal 68% confidence limits for the FT ‘zeros’ were estimated using the Student
t
-distribution.
For well defined FTs the results for all lines can be identical and in these cases formal error estimates were taken to be the resolution of the FT itself.
These formal error estimates should be regarded with some caution, other work suggests a general uncertainty of order 20% may be appropriate
(
98
)
.
In any case, some indication of problematic measurements may be derived by comparing FT and FWHM results.
This is discussed further in appendix
A
, where a sample of results are shown (Fig.
17
).

The spectral groups within the BLOeM sample (O, BSG, B-dwarf, OBe and BAF) required some variation in strategy.
For a few of the hottest O-type stars that lacked strong He
i

\lambda\lambda
4143, 4387 lines we also used the He
ii

\lambda\lambda
4026, 4200 and 4542 lines. Since these lines are intrinsically broad the approach was only used for stars with high
\varv\sin i
.
The cool supergiants (the BAF group) possess numerous sharp lines and for this group we used up to 10 isolated lines for
\varv\sin i
measurements,
although identification of isolated lines for F-type supergiants is problematic.
The OBe group, and other emission lines stars such as the SgB[e] stars, required special attention due to the presence of emission lines that contaminated many of the diagnostic lines.
For these stars we manually identified lines that appeared devoid of emission, typically the weak He
i
lines (noted above) and metal lines, if present.
One star, the SgB[e] star BLOeM 2-116 was devoid of absorption lines and is the only source for which we derive a
\varv\sin i
estimate from its emission lines, as discussed in appendix
B.1
.

The FWHM of each line was also derived using a Gaussian fit to the profile and
\varv\sin i
derived using the conversion factor provided by
19
, with mean values we refer to as
\varv\sin i_{\rm FWHM}
, which is a useful check on
\varv\sin i_{\rm FT}
concerning the effect of other broadening mechanisms, such as pressure broadening of the He
i
lines, or the presence of a weak secondary component.
We illustrate the latter effect on the B0 III star BLOeM 1-020, discussed in
4
(their Figure 14) as having discrepant FT and GOF
\varv\sin i
estimates, attributed to the possible impact of binarity.
This is an SB1 system with a measured peak-to-peak (p2p) velocity of
\sim
100 km s
-1
and by merging spectra at each quadrature we can enhance the presence of the secondary component on opposing wings of each He
i
line, shown in Fig.
2
for the He
i
4387 Å line.
Such wings broaden the merged profile, and increase the line width, leading to an overestimate of GOF/FWHM results with respect to the FT approach, which is relatively insensitive to their presence provided the profile is dominated by the primary.
For example in the case of BLOeM 1-020 the
\varv\sin i_{\rm FT}
of the He
i
4143, 4387 and 4471 Å lines is 81 km s
-1
, compared with a
\varv\sin i_{\rm FWHM}
of 127 km s
-1
.
The weaker lines on the other hand, He
i
4168, 4437 Å and Si
iii
4553 Å, show better agreement between
\varv\sin i_{\rm FT}
and
\varv\sin i_{\rm FWHM}
with mean values of 88 and 92 km s
-1
, respectively.

3.1
Double-lined spectroscopic binaries

As noted in
96
, a significant number of double-lined spectroscopic binary (SB2) systems were detected during spectral classification.
While we have included these systems in our standard FT approach we also made a preliminary estimate of
\varv\sin i
for each individual component.
As orbital solutions are not yet available we defined the stronger component as A, and the weaker line system as B.
Inspection of the spectra for each epoch of a given source yielded a number of spectra observed with the two components partially separated to at least 2/3 of the line depth, the number of useful spectra depending on s/n, relative strengths of the components, and velocity separation.
The FWHM of these components was determined by fitting a double Gaussian, and converted to
\varv\sin i
as explained above.
The lower s/n in single-epoch spectra compared to merged data led to a reliance on the stronger He
I
and He
II
lines for these measurements.
Furthermore, comparing results for the various lines it’s clear that those lines most affected by intrinsic line broadening, such as He
i
4471 Å and He
ii
4542, 4200 Å, tend to give higher
\varv\sin i
values than He
i
4387 Å;
for example He
i
4387 Å is the least impacted by intrinsic broadening with He
i
4471 Å giving results
\sim
20% higher. We have therefore restricted our results to the means of the estimates from the He
i
4387 Å line.
The approach outlined here is clearly approximate and should be refined once orbital solutions are available.
An important caveat concerns our designation of the A component with ‘stronger’ lines, typically hydrogen.
Without orbital solutions it’s clear that inferring primary and secondary natures are uncertain, and this should be kept in mind during our discussion of the overall results.
Nevertheless, these results provide a first look at the rotation properties of the SB2 systems in BLOeM.

Defining that region of the HRD populated by main-sequence core hydrogen burning stars, and in particular the location of the terminal age main sequence (TAMS), is a long standing problem in stellar evolution.
Previous attempts to address this issue used properties such as stellar density, rotational velocity, and binary frequency
(
31
;
54
;
107
;
15
;
69
;
108
;
23
)
.
Here, following
31
and
54
, we use stellar rotation as an indicator of the main-sequence extent. However one must recognize that a given
\varv\sin i
has different implications for stars of differing radii, for example main-sequence versus supergiant stars.
Therefore we use the ratio of
\varv\sin i
/
\varv_{\rm{crit}}
as the metric for defining ‘fast’ or ‘slow‘ (projected) rotation, where
\varv_{\rm{crit}}
is the critical velocity.
Critical velocities were estimated using the stellar parameters from
4
and
74
where available, otherwise the data from
96
were adopted.
To give an example, inspection of Fig.
5
indicates that
\varv\sin i
\sim
100 km s
-1
is a reasonable estimate for differentiating fast/slow cohorts in the BLOeM sample, which is corresponds to
\varv\sin i
/
\varv_{\rm{crit}}
\sim
0.15 on the main sequence.

The distribution of BLOeM systems in the Hertzsprung-Russell diagram (HRD) is shown in Fig.
7
, colour coded according to the above ratio.
Note that known OBe stars have been removed from this HRD as their parameters are highly uncertain due to disk contamination of their spectra and spectral energy distribution.
In fact most OBe stars have luminosities derived in
96
using K-band magnitudes, and it is well established that OBe stars have a significant K-band and visible excess due to the presence of their disks
(
9
;
34
)
.

Interpreting the over-density of ‘fast’ rotators as indicating the extent of the main sequence we see a sharp drop in number density as one moves to the cool side of the HRD.
This is better visualized in Fig.
8

\varv\sin i
as function of distance, in
T_{\mathrm{eff}}
, from the approximate position of the ZAMS (left-most dashed diagonal line in Fig.
7

The x-axes of Fig.

6
Discussion

Perhaps the most striking result of this survey is the preponderance of stars with low
\varv\sin i
throughout main sequence, as discussed in section
4.1
and as illustrated by their distribution in the HRD (Figures
7
and
9
).
In typical binary population synthesis paradigms
(
25
;
113
;
94
)
the slowly rotating cohort is primarily composed of stars that have retained their (modest) initial rotational velocity, unaffected by tides or mass transfer.
These are effectively single stars from an evolutionary perspective, even though they may reside in long period binary systems, as indicated by the low
\varv\sin i
cohort in the SB1 panel of Fig.
9
.
However a fraction of the low
\varv\sin i
cohort are also expected to be post interaction systems such as mergers
(
91
;
89
;
111
;
113
)
.
By contrast, the SB2 systems are consistent with being tidally spun up, and are in synchronous rotation, and are a mix of pre- or post-interaction systems.
The presence of significant numbers of systems at higher
\varv\sin i
in the single and SB1 systems would be consistent with a mix of undetected SB2s, and post-interaction binaries.
Finally the highest
\varv\sin i
systems are thought to result from spin-up due to mass accretion in binary systems, perhaps together with the OBe stars, although there are relatively few of the latter in BLOeM compared to VFTS.

Focusing again on the low
\varv\sin i
cohort, the N
ii
line at 3995 Å is present in many B-type stars and is suggestive of the N-rich slowly rotating B-type stars discussed in
54

(
33
;
28
, and see also)
.
As a preliminary test for N-enhancement in the BLOeM slow rotators we select a sample of main sequence B-type stars with
\varv\sin i
\leq
40 km s
-1
, 43 sources,
and compare N
ii
3995 Å equivalent widths with theoretical predictions for a range of nitrogen enhancements, details are summarized in Appendix
D
.
This preliminary analysis implies that
\sim
30% (17/43) of these slowly rotating stars are enhanced by at least 0.6 dex, while inspection of the radial velocities of the SB1 sources (20% of the sample) suggest that they are long period systems.

The presence of nitrogen-rich slowly rotating main sequence B-type stars in the Galaxy, LMC and SMC is well established
(
43
;
62
;
54
;
53
;
72
, see for example)
, and is reflected in the present data.
As previously noted,
stellar mergers may explain some fraction of these systems, which appears to be higher when approaching the TAMS according to population synthesis predictions
(
113
)
, although others may represent SMC analogues of the magnetic merger candidate
\tau
Scoprpii
(
89
)
which is closer to the ZAMS.
Similarly
57
discussed evolutionary scenarios, including mergers, that might explain a large sample of boron-depleted and nitrogen-rich slowly rotating Galactic B-type stars, but in addition speculate that mixing during the pre- main sequence might explain some fraction these nitrogen enhanced objects.
Finally, a small fraction of the slowly rotating SB1 sources may be partially stripped post interaction systems
(
95
;
7
;
105
;
58
)
.

7
Conclusions

We have determined projected rotational velocities (
\varv\sin i
) for the 929 systems in the BLOeM survey of massive stars in the SMC. We used the Fourier transform method for single stars, while for SB2 systems a first attempt was made to determine
\varv\sin i
for both components using a double Gaussian fitting approach. Summarizing the main results:

•

Using the change in the
\varv\sin i
distribution with
T_{\mathrm{eff}}
we can define a cool edge of the main sequence of likely core hydrogen burning stars.

•

The
\varv\sin i
distributions of main sequence single and SB1 systems exhibit a strong peak between 30–60 km s
-1
, members of which pervade the full main sequence, while some which may be long-period systems.

•

Preliminary inspection of the N
ii
3995 Å line in B-type stars reveals that many of these systems have an enhanced nitrogen abundance, although it is not clear if this enhancement occurred pre- or post- main sequence.

•

The
\varv\sin i
distribution of currently designated SB2 systems peaks at
\sim
140 km s
-1
and, together with known OGLE periods, suggests that members of this cohort are mostly high mass-ratio systems in synchronous rotation.

•

Only a very small number, 3%, of high
\varv\sin i
stars (
>
300 km s
-1
) are found in the sample, their potential runaway nature will be considered in a future paper.

•

Internal comparisons between survey fields, and with other surveys in the SMC, LMC and Milky Way, suggests that variations in derived
\varv\sin i
distributions are driven mainly by intrinsic stellar population differences, rather than metallicity.

Clearly additional work that is aimed at determining orbital parameters and more precise surface compositions will provide much stronger constraints and further insight into the processes that shape the
\varv\sin i
distributions presented in this paper.

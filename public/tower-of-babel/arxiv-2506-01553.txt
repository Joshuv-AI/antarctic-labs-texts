Eta-pairing states in Hubbard models with bond-charge interactions on general graphs
Ming-Yong Ye 1,2,

Abstract
We investigate Hubbard models with bond-charge interactions on general graphs. For a Hamiltonian H H of such a model, we provide the condition on its parameters under which the η \eta -pairing method can be employed to construct its exact eigenstates. We arrive at this condition by finding that the requirement for the η \eta -pairing state ( η † ) N ​ | 0 ⟩ (\eta^{\dagger})^{N}|0\rangle to be an eigenstate of H H is identical to the requirement for it to be an eigenstate of a Hubbard-type Hamiltonian H m H_{m} . When the condition for ( η † ) N ​ | 0 ⟩ (\eta^{\dagger})^{N}|0\rangle to be an eigenstate of the Hubbard-type Hamiltonian H m H_{m} is satisfied, we demonstrate that there are additional states, distinct from ( η † ) N ​ | 0 ⟩ (\eta^{\dagger})^{N}|0\rangle , which are also exact eigenstates of H m H_{m} . Our results enhance the understanding of Hubbard models on general graphs, both with and without bond-charge interactions.



I
introduction

The Hubbard model provides a framework for investigating strongly correlated electron systems
Hubbard 1963
, particularly in two dimensions where it gives valuable insights into the mechanisms underlying high-temperature superconductivity
Arovas et al. 2022
.
Its Hamiltonian has two terms: the first term describes the hopping of electrons between different sites, and the second term describes the interaction when two electrons are located at the same site. Yang makes a pioneering work to the Hubbard model on a hypercubic lattice by
constructing some exact eigenstates of the model
Yang 1989
.
His approach relies on the introduction of the
\eta
-pairing operator, which serves as an eigenoperator of the system’s Hamiltonian.
There are also some efforts to find new exact eigenstates of the Hubbard model using other methods
Ye and Lin 2018
.
The
\eta
-pairing method has been successfully extended to construct exact eigenstates for certain Hubbard models on triangular lattices
Zhai 2005
. More recently, the conditions under which the
\eta
-pairing method can effectively identify exact eigenstates for Hubbard models on general graphs have been explored
Moudgalya et al. 2020
, and
there are discussions about the
\eta
-pairing method
on non-Hermitian Hubbard models
Zhang and Song 2021
;
Lieta 2025
and multicomponent Hubbard models
Nakagawa et al. 2024
.

To further enrich the Hubbard model and explore related physics, additional interactions can be incorporated. One such interaction is the bond-charge interaction, which modifies the hopping strengths depending on the occupation across the bond
Hirsch and Marsiglio 1989
. The inclusion of bond-charge interactions in the Hubbard model has been used as a model of hole superconductivity
Hirsch and Marsiglio 1989
, and some exact eigenstates for this extended model have also been constructed
Ye and Lin 2018
.
Quantum many-body scar states have received many attentions in recent years, as these states do not obey the eigenstate thermalization hypothesis
Deutsch 1991
;
Srednicki 1994
.
It has been demonstrated that the Hubbard models with bond-charge interactions on hypercubic lattices can support
\eta
-pairing states as their eigenstates
Mark and Motrunich 2020
. These states are true scar states, as the bond-charge interaction breaks the
\eta
-pairing SU(2) symmetry inherent in the original Hubbard models
Mark and Motrunich 2020
.

In this paper,
we give the condition for the
\eta
-pairing state
(\eta^{\dagger})^{N}|0\rangle
to be an eigenstate of a Hubbard model with bond-charge interaction on a general graph, which is based on a finding that
the condition for the
\eta
-pairing state
(\eta^{\dagger})^{N}|0\rangle
to be an eigenstate of a Hubbard model with bond-charge interaction is identical to the condition for it to be an eigenstate of a modified Hubbard model without bond-charge interactions.
We also identify several eigenstates of the Hubbard model that are distinct from the
\eta
-pairing state
(\eta^{\dagger})^{N}|0\rangle
.

II
Condition for
\eta
-pairing states to be eigenstates

Consider a Hubbard model with bond-charge interaction defined on a general graph,
the Hamiltonian is

\displaystyle H=

\displaystyle\sum_{\langle m,n\rangle}\left\{T_{mn}+B_{mn}\right\}+U\sum_{n}a_{n}^{\dagger}a_{n}b_{n}^{\dagger}b_{n},

(1)

where
m
and
n
denote sites on the graph,
a_{n}
and
b_{n}
are annihilation operators for spin-up and spin-down electrons on site
n
, and
a_{n}^{\dagger}
and
b_{n}^{\dagger}

are the corresponding creation operators. The ordinary hopping
T_{mn}
is

\displaystyle T_{mn}=t_{mn}(a_{m}^{\dagger}a_{n}+b_{m}^{\dagger}b_{n})+t_{mn}^{*}(a_{n}^{\dagger}a_{m}+b_{n}^{\dagger}b_{m}).

(2)

The bond-charge interaction
B_{mn}
is

\displaystyle B_{mn}=

\displaystyle(\chi_{mn}a_{m}^{\dagger}a_{n}+\chi_{mn}^{*}a_{n}^{\dagger}a_{m})(b_{m}^{\dagger}b_{m}+b_{n}^{\dagger}b_{n})

\displaystyle+(\chi_{mn}b_{m}^{\dagger}b_{n}+\chi_{mn}^{*}b_{n}^{\dagger}b_{m})(a_{m}^{\dagger}a_{m}+a_{n}^{\dagger}a_{n}).

(3)

The notation
\langle m,n\rangle
means the sum is over the pair of sites connected on the graph.

Divide the bond-charge interaction
B_{mn}
as
B_{mn}=B_{mn1}+B_{mn2}
with

B_{mn1}=\chi_{mn}(a_{m}^{\dagger}a_{n}+b_{m}^{\dagger}b_{n})+\chi_{mn}^{*}(a_{n}^{\dagger}a_{m}+b_{n}^{\dagger}b_{m}),

(4)

and

\displaystyle B_{mn2}

\displaystyle=\chi_{mn}a_{m}^{\dagger}a_{n}(b_{m}^{\dagger}b_{m}-b_{n}b_{n}^{\dagger})

\displaystyle+\chi_{mn}^{*}a_{n}^{\dagger}a_{m}(b_{n}^{\dagger}b_{n}-b_{m}b_{m}^{\dagger})

\displaystyle+\chi_{mn}b_{m}^{\dagger}b_{n}(a_{m}^{\dagger}a_{m}-a_{n}a_{n}^{\dagger})

\displaystyle+\chi_{mn}^{*}b_{n}^{\dagger}b_{m}(a_{n}^{\dagger}a_{n}-a_{m}a_{m}^{\dagger}).

(5)

We can write

T_{mn}+B_{mn}=(T_{mn}+B_{mn1})+B_{mn2},

(6)

where
T_{mn}+B_{mn1}
has the same form as the hopping
T_{mn}
but with the strength
t_{mn}+\chi_{mn}
.
So adding the bond charge interaction
B_{mn}
to the Hubbard model can be understood as changing the hopping strength from
t_{mn}
to
t_{mn}+\chi_{mn}
, and
adding the leftover interaction
B_{mn2}
.

Now we discuss the condition for the
\eta
-pairing states to be eigenstates of the Hamiltonian
H
.
The
\eta
-pairing operator is defined as

\eta^{\dagger}=\sum_{n}\eta_{n}^{\dagger},\quad\eta_{n}^{\dagger}=e^{i\phi_{n}}a_{n}^{\dagger}b_{n}^{\dagger}.

(7)

There is a good relation between the operator
B_{mn2}
and
\eta^{\dagger}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle[[B_{mn2},\eta^{\dagger}],\eta^{\dagger}]=0,

(8)

which is proved as follows.
In the following
n\neq m
, simple calculations show that there are

\displaystyle[a_{m}^{\dagger}a_{n}b_{m}^{\dagger}b_{m},e^{i\phi_{m}}a_{m}^{\dagger}b_{m}^{\dagger}]=0,

(9)

\displaystyle[a_{m}^{\dagger}a_{n}b_{m}^{\dagger}b_{m},e^{i\phi_{n}}a_{n}^{\dagger}b_{n}^{\dagger}]=e^{i\phi_{n}}a_{m}^{\dagger}b_{n}^{\dagger}b_{m}^{\dagger}b_{m},

(10)

\displaystyle[-a_{m}^{\dagger}a_{n}b_{n}b_{n}^{\dagger},e^{i\phi_{m}}a_{m}^{\dagger}b_{m}^{\dagger}]=0,

(11)

\displaystyle[-a_{m}^{\dagger}a_{n}b_{n}b_{n}^{\dagger},e^{i\phi_{n}}a_{n}^{\dagger}b_{n}^{\dagger}]=-e^{i\phi_{n}}a_{m}^{\dagger}b_{n}^{\dagger}a_{n}^{\dagger}a_{n}.

(12)

The sum of the above four equations leads to

\displaystyle[a_{m}^{\dagger}a_{n}(b_{m}^{\dagger}b_{m}-b_{n}b_{n}^{\dagger}),\eta_{m}^{\dagger}+\eta_{n}^{\dagger}]

\displaystyle=e^{i\phi_{n}}a_{m}^{\dagger}b_{n}^{\dagger}(b_{m}^{\dagger}b_{m}-a_{n}^{\dagger}a_{n}).

(13)

In Eq. (
13
), interchanging
m
and
n
leads to

\displaystyle[a_{n}^{\dagger}a_{m}(b_{n}^{\dagger}b_{n}-b_{m}b_{m}^{\dagger}),\eta_{m}^{\dagger}+\eta_{n}^{\dagger}]

\displaystyle=e^{i\phi_{m}}a_{n}^{\dagger}b_{m}^{\dagger}(b_{n}^{\dagger}b_{n}-a_{m}^{\dagger}a_{m}).

(14)

In Eq. (
13
), interchanging
a
and
b
leads to

\displaystyle[b_{m}^{\dagger}b_{n}(a_{m}^{\dagger}a_{m}-a_{n}a_{n}^{\dagger}),\eta_{m}^{\dagger}+\eta_{n}^{\dagger}]

\displaystyle=e^{i\phi_{n}}a_{n}^{\dagger}b_{m}^{\dagger}(a_{m}^{\dagger}a_{m}-b_{n}^{\dagger}b_{n}).

(15)

In Eq. (
15
), interchanging
m
and
n
leads to

\displaystyle[b_{n}^{\dagger}b_{m}(a_{n}^{\dagger}a_{n}-a_{m}a_{m}^{\dagger}),\eta_{m}^{\dagger}+\eta_{n}^{\dagger}]

\displaystyle=e^{i\phi_{m}}a_{m}^{\dagger}b_{n}^{\dagger}(a_{n}^{\dagger}a_{n}-b_{m}^{\dagger}b_{m}).

(16)

Notice the expression of
B_{mn2}
in Eq. (
5
), a linear combination of the above four equations leads to

\displaystyle[B_{mn2},\eta^{\dagger}]=[B_{mn2},\eta_{m}^{\dagger}+\eta_{n}^{\dagger}]=g_{mn}M_{mn},

(17)

where

\displaystyle g_{mn}=

\displaystyle\chi_{mn}e^{i\phi_{n}}-\chi_{mn}^{*}e^{i\phi_{m}},

(18)

\displaystyle M_{mn}=

\displaystyle a_{m}^{\dagger}b_{n}^{\dagger}(b_{m}^{\dagger}b_{m}-a_{n}^{\dagger}a_{n})

\displaystyle+a_{n}^{\dagger}b_{m}^{\dagger}(a_{m}^{\dagger}a_{m}-b_{n}^{\dagger}b_{n}).

(19)

As there is

[M_{mn},\eta^{\dagger}]=[M_{mn},\eta^{\dagger}_{m}]+[M_{mn},\eta^{\dagger}_{n}]=0,

(20)

the relation in Eq. (
8
) is proved.

The relation in Eq. (
8
) is important as it can lead to

[B_{mn2},\eta^{\dagger}](\eta^{\dagger})^{N}|0\rangle=(\eta^{\dagger})^{N}[B_{mn2},\eta^{\dagger}]|0\rangle=0,

(21)

where
N
is an integer and
|0\rangle
is the vacuum state. The above equation means that there is

B_{mn2}(\eta^{\dagger})^{N+1}|0\rangle=\eta^{\dagger}B_{mn2}(\eta^{\dagger})^{N}|0\rangle,

(22)

which itself can be used
N
times to get

B_{mn2}(\eta^{\dagger})^{N+1}|0\rangle=(\eta^{\dagger})^{N+1}B_{mn2}|0\rangle=0.

(23)

Therefore the
\eta
-pairing state
(\eta^{\dagger})^{N}|0\rangle
is an eigenstate of
B_{mn2}
with zero eigenvalue.
Define

\displaystyle H_{m}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\langle m,n\rangle}\left\{T_{mn}+B_{mn1}\right\}+U\sum_{n}a_{n}^{\dagger}a_{n}b_{n}^{\dagger}b_{n},

(24)

which is just the Hamiltonian of a Hubbard model with the hopping strength
t_{mn}+\chi_{mn}
and without bond-charge interactions. As there is

H=H_{m}+\sum_{\langle m,n\rangle}B_{mn2},

(25)

if the
\eta
-pairing state
(\eta^{\dagger})^{N}|0\rangle
is an eigenstate of
H
it will also be an eigenstate of
H_{m}
and vice versa.
We can draw the conclusion that the condition for the
\eta
-pairing state
(\eta^{\dagger})^{N}|0\rangle
to be an eigenstate of
H
is the same as
that for it to be an eigenstate of
H_{m}
.

To find the condition for the
\eta
-pairing state
(\eta^{\dagger})^{N}|0\rangle
to be an eigenstate of
H_{m}
, it
is needed to calculate the communicator
[H_{m},\eta^{\dagger}]
as Yang’s method
Yang 1989
. The on-site interactions of
H_{m}
satisfy

\displaystyle[U\sum_{n}a_{n}^{\dagger}a_{n}b_{n}^{\dagger}b_{n},\eta^{\dagger}]=U\eta^{\dagger},

(26)

which can be easily verified.
To calculate the communicator
[T_{mn}+B_{mn1},\eta^{\dagger}]
, we note that there is

[T_{mn}+B_{mn1},\eta^{\dagger}]=[T_{mn}+B_{mn1},\eta_{m}^{\dagger}+\eta_{n}^{\dagger}].

(27)

Direct calculation shows that there is

[a_{m}^{\dagger}a_{n}+b_{m}^{\dagger}b_{n},\eta_{m}^{\dagger}+\eta_{n}^{\dagger}]=e^{i\phi_{n}}(a_{m}^{\dagger}b_{n}^{\dagger}+a_{n}^{\dagger}b_{m}^{\dagger}).

(28)

In Eq. (
28
), interchanging
m
and
n
leads to

[a_{n}^{\dagger}a_{m}+b_{n}^{\dagger}b_{m},\eta_{m}^{\dagger}+\eta_{n}^{\dagger}]=e^{i\phi_{m}}(a_{n}^{\dagger}b_{m}^{\dagger}+a_{m}^{\dagger}b_{n}^{\dagger}).

(29)

A linear combination of Eq. (
28
) and Eq. (
29
) gives

[T_{mn}+B_{mn1},\eta^{\dagger}]=f_{mn}(a_{n}^{\dagger}b_{m}^{\dagger}+a_{m}^{\dagger}b_{n}^{\dagger}),

(30)

with

f_{mn}=(t_{mn}+\chi_{mn})e^{i\phi_{n}}+(t_{mn}+\chi_{mn})^{*}e^{i\phi_{m}}.

(31)

Based on the results in Eqs. (
26
,
30
), we can make the summary that

\displaystyle[H_{m},\eta^{\dagger}]=U\eta^{\dagger}+\sum_{\langle m,n\rangle}f_{mn}(a_{n}^{\dagger}b_{m}^{\dagger}+a_{m}^{\dagger}b_{n}^{\dagger}).

(32)

When there is

f_{mn}=0,\quad\forall\,\langle m,n\rangle,

(33)

we have
[H_{m},\eta^{\dagger}]=U\eta^{\dagger}
, which can lead to

H(\eta^{\dagger})^{N}|0\rangle=H_{m}(\eta^{\dagger})^{N}|0\rangle=NU(\eta^{\dagger})^{N}|0\rangle,

(34)

so that
(\eta^{\dagger})^{N}|0\rangle
is an eigenstate of
H_{m}
and
H
with the eigenvalue
NU
.
Therefore Eq. (
33
) is
the condition for the
\eta
-pairing state
(\eta^{\dagger})^{N}|0\rangle
to be an eigenstate of
H_{m}
and
H
.

The condition in Eq. (
33
) gives a constraint on the hopping strength
t_{mn}
, the bond-charge interaction strength
\chi_{mn}
and the phases
e^{i\phi_{n}}
in
\eta^{\dagger}
.
In the special case that
t_{mn}+\chi_{mn}=0
for all pairs
\langle m,n\rangle
, Eq. (
33
) gives no requirement on the phases in
\eta^{\dagger}
,
so
\eta
-pairing states with any phases in
\eta^{\dagger}
are eigenstates of
H
. Such a special case is studied in detail in one dimension
Arrachea and Aligia 1994
;
Schadschneider 1995
.
In the following we assume that
t_{mn}+\chi_{mn}\neq 0
, and
define

t_{mn}+\chi_{mn}=\tilde{t}_{mn}e^{i\theta_{mn}},

(35)

where
\tilde{t}_{mn}
and
\theta_{mn}
are real. Now the condition in Eq. (
33
) for the
\eta
-pairing state
(\eta^{\dagger})^{N}|0\rangle
to be an eigenstate of
H_{m}
and
H
is equivalent to

e^{2i\theta_{mn}}=-e^{i\phi_{m}}e^{-i\phi_{n}},\quad\forall\,\langle m,n\rangle.

(36)

which will be discussed in the following from two different views.

III
When the phases in
\eta^{\dagger}
are specified

Based on the condition in Eq. (
36
), we ask the following question:
assume that the phases in
\eta^{\dagger}
for all sites are specified, can we find a Hubbard model with bond-charge interaction such that the
state
(\eta^{\dagger})^{N}|0\rangle
is an eigenstate of
H
? The answer is always YES. Such a model can be achieved when the phase of
t_{mn}+\chi_{mn}
is

e^{i\theta_{mn}}=e^{\frac{1}{2}i(\phi_{m}-\phi_{n}\pm\pi)},\quad\forall\,\langle m,n\rangle,

(37)

where
\phi_{m}
and
\phi_{n}
are phases in
\eta^{\dagger}
,
as it satisfies the condition in Eq. (
36
).

Two interesting examples can be used to demonstrate this result.
In the first example, define

\eta_{1}^{\dagger}=\sum_{n}a_{n}^{\dagger}b_{n}^{\dagger},

(38)

which takes
\phi_{n}=0
for all
n
. If we want
(\eta_{1}^{\dagger})^{N}|0\rangle
to be an eigenstate of
H
, according to Eq. (
37
)
we can set
e^{i\theta_{mn}}=\pm i
, which means
t_{mn}+\chi_{mn}
are all pure imaginary numbers.
In the second example, define

\eta_{2}^{\dagger}=\sum_{n\in A}a_{n}^{\dagger}b_{n}^{\dagger}-\sum_{n\in B}a_{n}^{\dagger}b_{n}^{\dagger},

(39)

where the sets of sites
A
and
B
form a bipartition of the graph.
In this example there is
\phi_{n}=0
for all
n\in A
and
\phi_{n}=\pi
for all
n\in B
.
If we want
(\eta_{2}^{\dagger})^{N}|0\rangle
to be an eigenstate of
H
, according to Eq. (
37
) we can set
e^{i\theta_{mn}}=\pm i

when both
m,n
are in
A
or
B
, and
e^{i\theta_{mn}}=\pm 1
when
n\in A,m\in B
, or
m\in A,n\in B
.
This requirement means that
the hopping strength
t_{mn}+\chi_{mn}
within
A
or
B
are all pure imaginary numbers, and between
A
and
B
are real numbers.
Therefore, for a Hubbard model defined on square lattices with nearest-neighbour hoppings and
next-nearest-neighbour hoppings, if the nearest-neighbour hopping coefficients are real numbers and the next-nearest-neighbour hopping
coefficients are pure imaginary numbers, the
\eta
-pairing state
(\eta_{2}^{\dagger})^{N}|0\rangle
will be an eigenstate of the model Hamiltonian.

IV
When the parameters in
H
are specified

Based on the condition in Eq. (
36
), we can also ask the following question:
assume that the parameters in
H
are specified, can we find an eta-pairing operator
\eta^{\dagger}
such that the
state
(\eta^{\dagger})^{N}|0\rangle
is an eigenstate of
H
? The answer depends on the phase
\theta_{mn}
of
t_{mn}+\chi_{mn}
.

Suppose sites
(n_{1},n_{2},\cdots,n_{k},n_{1})
form a loop on the graph,
when the condition in Eq. (
36
) is satisfied, there is

e^{2i\theta_{n_{2}n_{1}}}e^{2i\theta_{n_{3}n_{2}}}\cdots e^{2i\theta_{n_{1}n_{k}}}=(-1)^{k}.

(40)

Conversely if the condition in Eq. (
40
) is satisfied for any loop
(n_{1},n_{2},\cdots,n_{k},n_{1})
, we can always find proper
e^{i\phi_{n}}
in
\eta^{\dagger}
for any site
n
so that
the condition in Eq. (
36
) can be satisfied and thus there is
[H_{m},\eta^{\dagger}]=U\eta^{\dagger}
.
The method to find such
e^{i\phi_{n}}
in
\eta^{\dagger}
is as follows.
First set
\phi_{n_{1}}=0
for an arbitrarily chosen site
n_{1}
. Then according to Eq. (
36
) we set
e^{i\phi_{m}}=-e^{i\phi_{n_{1}}}e^{2i\theta_{mn_{1}}}
for any site
m
connected to site
n_{1}
. And then according to Eq. (
36
) we set
e^{i\phi_{k}}=-e^{i\phi_{m}}e^{2i\theta_{km}}
for any site
k
connected to site
m
. Because the condition in Eq. (
40
) is assumed to be satisfied for any loop,
we can get a unique
e^{i\phi_{n}}
for any site
n
by repeating this procedure.

Now we can answer the question raised at the beginning of this section. For a given
H
with hopping strength
t_{mn}
and bond-charge interaction strength
\chi_{mn}
, if the phase
\theta_{mn}
of
t_{mn}+\chi_{mn}
satisfies the condition in Eq. (
40
) for any loop
(n_{1},n_{2},\cdots,n_{k},n_{1})

on the graph, we can always find an eta-pairing operator
\eta^{\dagger}
such that the
state
(\eta^{\dagger})^{N}|0\rangle
is an eigenstate of
H_{m}
and
H
.

V
States different from
(\eta^{\dagger})^{N}|0\rangle

In this section, we show that when the condition in Eq. (
40
) is satisfied for any loop
(n_{1},n_{2},\cdots,n_{k},n_{1})

on the graph, we can find new exact eigenstates of the Hubbard-type Hamiltonian
H_{m}
in Eq. (
24
) that are different from the eta-pairing state
(\eta^{\dagger})^{N}|0\rangle
.

When the condition in Eq. (
40
) is satisfied for any loop, as discussed in the above section we can find an
\eta^{\dagger}
so that there is
[H_{m},\eta^{\dagger}]=U\eta^{\dagger}
. It will lead to the fact that

(\eta^{\dagger})^{N}|0\rangle
is an eigenstate of
H_{m}
.
Define
T=T_{a}+T_{b}
with two operators

\displaystyle T_{a}=\sum_{\langle m,n\rangle}((t_{mn}+\chi_{mn})a_{m}^{\dagger}a_{n}+H.c.),

(41)

\displaystyle T_{b}=\sum_{\langle m,n\rangle}((t_{mn}+\chi_{mn})b_{m}^{\dagger}b_{n}+H.c.),\overline{}

(42)

where
T_{a}
describes the hopping of spin-up electrons and
T_{b}
describes the hopping of spin-down electrons.
We can write the Hubbard-type Hamiltonian
H_{m}
in Eq. (
24
) as

\displaystyle H_{m}=

\displaystyle T+U\sum_{n}a_{n}^{\dagger}a_{n}b_{n}^{\dagger}b_{n}.

(43)

We will show that there is

H_{m}(T_{b})^{M}\eta^{\dagger}|0\rangle=0

(44)

for odd integer
M
, i.e.,
(T_{b})^{M}\eta^{\dagger}|0\rangle
is an eigenstate of
H_{m}
with zero eigenvalue when
M
is an odd integer.
This result together with
[H_{m},\eta^{\dagger}]=U\eta^{\dagger}
can lead to the conclusion that

|\psi_{NM}\rangle=(\eta^{\dagger})^{N-1}(T_{b})^{M}\eta^{\dagger}|0\rangle

(45)

is an eigenstate of
H_{m}
with eigenvalue
(N-1)U
, where
M
is an odd integer.
It is obvious that this new eigenstate is different from
(\eta^{\dagger})^{N}|0\rangle
.

Now we prove the result that there is
H_{m}(T_{b})^{M}\eta^{\dagger}|0\rangle=0
for odd integer
M
.
As the condition in Eq. (
40
) is satisfied for any loop, there is
[T,\eta^{\dagger}]=0
, which can lead to

T\eta^{\dagger}|0\rangle=0
. This result together with the fact
TT_{b}=T_{b}T

can lead to

T(T_{b})^{M}\eta^{\dagger}|0\rangle=(T_{b})^{M}T\eta^{\dagger}|0\rangle=0

(46)

for any positive integer
M
. Notice the expression of
H_{m}
in Eq. (
43
) and the result in Eq. (
46
),
to show that
H_{m}(T_{b})^{M}\eta^{\dagger}|0\rangle=0
for odd
M
we only need to show that
there is no double occupation in the state
(T_{b})^{M}\eta^{\dagger}|0\rangle
.
Recall that
\eta^{\dagger}=\sum_{n}e^{i\phi_{n}}a_{n}^{\dagger}b_{n}^{\dagger}
,
there is

(T_{b})^{M}\eta^{\dagger}|0\rangle=\sum_{n}e^{i\phi_{n}}a_{n}^{\dagger}(T_{b})^{M}b_{n}^{\dagger}|0\rangle,

(47)

which means that
no double occupation in the state
(T_{b})^{M}\eta^{\dagger}|0\rangle
is equivalent to that
(T_{b})^{M}b_{n}^{\dagger}|0\rangle
is orthogonal to
b_{n}^{\dagger}|0\rangle
.
When
M=1
it is obvious that
(T_{b})^{M}b_{n}^{\dagger}|0\rangle
is orthogonal to
b_{n}^{\dagger}|0\rangle
.
For a general integer
M
bigger than
1
,
the state
(T_{b})^{M}b_{n}^{\dagger}|0\rangle
can be regarded as a spin-down electron initially at site
n
and then hopping
M
times.
What is the amplitude for the electron going back to its initial site
n
after hopping
M
times?
When
M
is an odd integer the way back to its starting site must contain a loop that has an odd number of sites, the electron can go back to its initial site along two opposite directions of the loop, the sum of the amplitude from these two opposite directions will be zero according to Eq. (
40
).
Therefore
when
M
is an odd integer, the state
(T_{b})^{M}b_{n}^{\dagger}|0\rangle
is orthogonal to
b_{n}^{\dagger}|0\rangle
.

VI
Conclusions

We have demonstrated that the condition for the
\eta
-pairing state
(\eta^{\dagger})^{N}|0\rangle
to be an eigenstate of
H
in Eq. (
1
), which describes a Hubbard model with bond-charge interaction, is identical to the condition for it to be an eigenstate of the Hubbard-type Hamiltonian
H_{m}
in Eq. (
24
) without bond-charge interaction. For the Hamiltonian
H
given in Eq. (
1
), we have provided the conditions on its parameters under which the
\eta
-pairing method can be employed to construct its exact eigenstates. Specifically, if the parameters of
H
in Eq. (
1
) satisfy the condition in Eq. (
40
) for any loop, then the
\eta
-pairing method becomes applicable for constructing the exact eigenstates
(\eta^{\dagger})^{N}|0\rangle
, which are quantum many-body scar states
Mark and Motrunich 2020
. When the condition in Eq. (
40

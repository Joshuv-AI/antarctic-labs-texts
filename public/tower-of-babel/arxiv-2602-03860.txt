Polynomial Closed-Form Model for Evaluating Nonlinear Interference in Any Island
Yanchao Jiang, Pierluigi Poggiolini

Abstract
Polynomial closed-form GN model is proposed by expressing the spatial power profile of each channel along a span as a polynomial. In this paper, we present the generic closed-form expression for all contributions of self-, cross-, and multi-channel interference. The full derivation is provided.



I
Introduction

Polynomial closed-form GN model (PCFM) has been proposed by expressing the spatial power-profile (SPP) of each channel along a span as a polynomial. Once this is done, some of the core GN model integrals admit closed-form near-exact solutions, involving less drastic approximations than previous CFMs needed to take the SPP into account, especially in the presence of Raman amplification and ISRS.

The PCFM can model UWB systems with ISRS, forward and backward Raman amplification, short spans, lumped loss and other possible SPPs perturbations. It enjoys better accuracy and reliability than previous CFMs, because it does not hinge on either neglecting intra-span NLI coherence or resorting to infinite series expansions, or other problematic approximations.

In addition, PCFM makes it possible to remove some of the other approximations that CFMs generally make. In particular, the PCFM approach potentially allows to retain the Multi-Channel Interference (MCI) contribution to NLI, making it possible to handle low-dispersion and low-symbol-rate scenarios where the MCI ‘islands’ in the integration domain of the GN and EGN model core integrals need to be considered. In principle, it can also allow to remove other approximations that are typically made by CFMs, such as that channel spectra are rectangular and that the power spectral density of NLI is flat over each channel.

III
Conclusion

We have completed PCFM by deriving a generic closed-form expression applicable to all island types, including SCI, XCI and MCI. PCFM is capable of handling ISRS and Raman amplification, as well as lumped loss along the span. Because MCI is explicitly included, PCFM is valid even for low-dispersion and low-baud-rate systems.

Furthermore, the integration rectangle is not constrained to coincide with any specific island. Instead, it can be chosen as any rectangle fully contained within an island, or even within the Lorentzian-shaped islands. As a result, the conventional assumptions of white NLI noise and strictly rectangular islands are no longer required.

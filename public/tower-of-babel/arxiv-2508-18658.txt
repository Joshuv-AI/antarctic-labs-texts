Moerdijk Hopf algebras of decorated rooted forests: an operated algebra approach
Loïc Foissy, Xiao-song Peng, Yunzhou Xie, Yi Zhang

Abstract
. In this paper, we first endow the space of decorated planar rooted forests with a coproduct that equips it with the structure of a bialgebra and further a Moerdijk Hopf algebra. We also present a combinatorial description of this coproduct, and further give an explicit formulation of its dual coproducts through the newly defined notion of forest-representable matrices. By viewing the Moerdijk Hopf algebra within the framework of operated algebras, we introduce the notion of a multiple cocycle Hopf algebra, incorporating a symmetric Hochschild 1-cocycle condition. We then show that the antipode of this Hopf algebra is a Rota-Baxter operator on Moerdijk Hopf algebras. Furthermore, we investigate the universal properties of cocycle Hopf algebras. As an application, we construct the initial object in the category of free cocycle Hopf algebras on undecorated planar rooted forests, which coincides with the well-known Moerdijk Hopf algebra.

1.
Introduction

Rooted tree Hopf algebras, originally introduced by Connes and Kreimer in the study of renormalization, capture the recursive structure of Feynman diagrams in an algebraic framework. In this paper, we construct a Moerdijk Hopf algebra on the space of decorated planar rooted forests and characterize its coproduct combinatorially using admissible cuts. Within the setting of operated algebras, we introduce multiple cocycle Hopf algebras and prove that the constructed Moerdijk Hopf algebra is the free object in this category.

The study of Hopf algebras in combinatorics and mathematical physics has gained significant attention due to their rich algebraic structures and broad applications.
The foundational work of Connes and Kreimer

Recent advancements in this field focus on the structural properties and applications of rooted tree Hopf algebras. Researchers have explored their bialgebraic structure, graded connections, and cohomological interpretations. Building upon this foundation, researchers have progressively expanded the algebraic representation framework through novel Hopf algebra constructions, including the Foissy-Holtkamp

These advancements highlight the construction of bialgebras or even Hopf algebras on variously decorated rooted forests as a promising research direction. Particularly noteworthy is the combinatorial interpretation that rooted tree or forest structures provide for the coproduct operations in these bialgebras. Such combinatorial-algebraic correspondences not only drive theoretical developments but also serve as critical interfaces for practical applications.

1.2.
Operated Hopf algebras

Inspired by theory of multi-operator groups
[
20
]
, Kurosh
[
24
]
pioneered the concept of algebras with linear operators. However, this theoretical framework initially received limited attention until its significance was rediscovered by Guo
[
16
]
, see also
[
2
]
.

Utilizing combinatorial objects such as Motzkin paths, rooted forests, and bracketed words, Guo
[
16
]
constructed free objects for these algebras. These algebraic structures are now collectively referred to as
\Omega
-operated algebras (or multi-operated algebras), where
\Omega
denotes the index set of linear operators.
Of particular interest is the fact that the Connes-Kreimer Hopf algebra on rooted forests, when equipped with the grafting operator, naturally constitutes a special case of an operated algebra.

In 2016, Zhang-Gao-Guo
[
37
]
innovatively introduced the concept of operated Hopf algebras by integrating the theories of Hopf algebras and operated algebras on decorated rooted forests, systematically investigating free objects in this category. Building on the Grobner-Shirshov method, the results were further generalized in
[
41
]
, leading to the establishment of the
\Omega
-operated Hopf algebra.

We would like to emphasize that the Loday-Ronco Hopf algebra can also be studied within the unified framework of operated algebras. In
[
39
]
, Zhang and Gao introduced the
concepts of
\vee_{\Omega}
-Hopf algebra and proved that the Loday-Ronco Hopf algebra can be characterized as the free multiple 1-cocycle
\vee_{\Omega}
-Hopf algebra generated by the empty set. Later, Marcolli, Berwick, and Chomsky
[
30
,
31
]
investigated applications of Hopf algebraic structures in Chomsky’s Minimalist Program, a framework introduced in the 1990s to capture the most economical principles of language. They formalized syntactic merge as algebraic operations within Hopf algebras, examined the interplay of external and internal merge in the Loday-Ronco Hopf algebra, and analyzed its computational properties. Drawing inspiration from renormalization methods in physics, they further used operated Hopf algebras to model the computation of semantic meaning from syntactic expressions. This algebraic perspective not only strengthens the mathematical foundations of generative linguistics but also suggests new methods for natural language processing and computational linguistics.

These developments are not only of fundamental importance in algebraic combinatorics and computational mathematics but also exhibit broad applications in quantum field theory, renormalization theory, generative linguistics, and noncommutative geometry.

Considering that the Moerdijk Hopf algebra is another important class of Hopf algebras on rooted trees, its comultiplication structure is also related to the 1-cocycle condition. We will investigate the Moerdijk Hopf algebra from the perspective of operated algebras, with the expectation that it may also provide a new algebraic interpretation of the Minimalist Program, enabling a mathematically precise formulation of language generation rules.

In this paper, we study the Moerdijk Hopf algebra within the framework of operated algebras, we introduce the notion of a multiple cocycle Hopf algebra, incorporating a symmetric Hochschild 1-cocycle condition. Furthermore, we investigate the universal properties of cocycle Hopf algebras. As an application, we use the universal properties to get a cocycle bialgebra morphism.

1.3.
Rota-Baxter operators on Hopf algebras

Lie groups and Lie algebras are fundamental concepts in algebra. Recently, Lang, Sheng, and Guo
[
18
]
introduced the notion of Rota-Baxter Lie groups and established that the differentiation of a Rota-Baxter Lie group yields a Rota-Baxter Lie algebra of weight 1. This result generalizes the classical fact that the differentiation of a Lie group gives rise to a Lie algebra.

Lie algebras and Lie groups can be regarded as fundamental examples of cocommutative Hopf algebras. In
[
19
]
, Goncharov combined the concept of Rota-Baxter operators of weight 1 on Lie algebras with that of Rota-Baxter operators on groups and provided the definition of a Rota-Baxter operator of weight 1 on cocommutative Hopf algebras.

In this paper, we prove that the antipode of the Moerdijk Hopf algebra on decorated rooted forests is a Rota-Baxter operator. This discovery enriches the examples related to commutative Hopf algebras.

2.
Bialgebras of decorated planar rooted forests

In this section, we construct a coproduct on decorated planar rooted forests based on a symmetric 1-cocycle condition, thereby equipping the space with a bialgebra structure. A combinatorial characterization of this coproduct is provided, and the dual coproducts are described explicitly via the newly introduced concept of forest-representable matrices.

Definition 2.1

2.2.
From Cartier-Quillen cohomologies to symmetric 1-cocycle conditions

Given an algebra
A
and a bimodule
M
over
A
, let
H^{*}(A,M)
denote the
Hochschild cohomology
of
A
with coefficients in
M
, which is defined via a cochain complex whose cochains are maps
A^{\otimes n}\rightarrow M
. For further details, see
[
27
]
.

Let
(C,\Delta)
be a coalgebra and
(B,\delta_{G},\delta_{D})
a bicomodule over
C
. The
Cartier-Quillen cohomology
of
C
with coefficients in
B
serves as a dual counterpart to the Hochschild cohomology. Specifically, it is defined as the cohomology of the complex
\mathrm{Hom}{\mathbf{k}}(B,C^{\otimes n})
, with differentials
b_{n}:\mathrm{Hom}{\mathbf{k}}(B,C^{\otimes n})\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}{\mathbf{k}}(B,C^{\otimes(n+1)})
given by

\displaystyle b_{n}(L)=(\mathrm{id}\otimes L)\circ\delta_{G}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}(\mathrm{id}_{C}^{\otimes{(i-1)}}\otimes\Delta\otimes\mathrm{id}_{C}^{\otimes{(n-i)}})L+(-1)^{n+1}(L\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\delta_{D},

where
L:B\rightarrow C^{\otimes n}
. Particularly, a linear map
L:B\rightarrow C
is called a
1-cocycle
if it satisfies the following condition:

\displaystyle\Delta\circ L=(L\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\delta_{D}+(\mathrm{id}\otimes L)\circ\delta_{G},

see
[
10
,
32
]
for more details.

We consider the special case where the bicomodule is given by
(C,\delta_{G},\delta_{D})
with
\delta_{G}=\delta_{D}=\Delta
. In this setting, a 1-cocycle is a linear endomorphism
L
on
C
satisfying

(1)

\displaystyle\Delta\circ L(x)=(L\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\Delta(x)+(\mathrm{id}\otimes L)\circ\Delta(x),\quad\text{ for }x\in C.

We refer to Eq. (
1
) as the
symmetric 1-cocycle condition
.

Remark 2.5
.

(a)

When
L=B^{+}
, the symmetric 1-cocycle condition in Eq. (
1
) takes the form

\Delta(F)=\Delta B^{+}(\overline{F})=(B^{+}\otimes\mathrm{id})\Delta(\overline{F})+(\mathrm{id}\otimes B^{+})\Delta(\overline{F})\quad\text{for }F=B^{+}(\overline{F})\in\mathcal{F},

which differs from the classical 1-cocycle condition introduced and studied by Connes-Kreimer
[
6
]
, given by

\Delta(F)=\Delta B^{+}(\overline{F})=B^{+}(\overline{F})\otimes 1+(\mathrm{id}\otimes B^{+})\Delta(\overline{F}),

which corresponds to the bicomodule
(C,\delta^{\prime}_{G},\delta^{\prime}_{R})
with

\displaystyle\delta_{R}(x)=x\otimes 1,\delta_{L}(x)

\displaystyle=\Delta(x),

for any
x\in C
.

(b)

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a Hopf operad, and let
H
be the algebra generated by finite rooted forests equipped with a linear endomorphism
\varrho
. Define maps
\sigma_{i}:H\rightarrow H
, for
i=1,2
, by setting
\sigma_{i}(F)=q_{i}^{n}F

for any forest
F
with
n
vertices, where
q_{1},q_{2}\in{\bf k}
. Moerdijk
[
32
]
showed that the pair

\displaystyle(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})=\sigma_{1}\otimes\varrho+\varrho\otimes\sigma_{2}:H\otimes H\to H\otimes H

induces a
\mathbb{P}[t]
-algebraic structure on
H\otimes H
, which in turn gives rise to a family of Hopf
\mathbb{P}
-algebra structures parameterized by
\sigma_{i}
,
i=1,2
. Notably, the choice
q_{1}=0
and
q_{2}=1
recovers the classical 1-cocycle relation, while the case
q_{1}=q_{2}=1
corresponds to the symmetric 1-cocycle condition employed in the present work; see
[
25
,
32
]
for further details.

2.3.
A bialgebra structure on decorated planar rooted forests

For the rest of the paper, we assume that
\lambda\in\bf k
is a fixed element. In this subsection, we shall construct a bialgebra structure on decorated planar rooted forests.

First we define a new coproduct
\Delta_{\lambda}
on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{RT}}(X,\Omega)
by induction on depth. By linearity, we only need to define
\Delta_{\lambda}(F)
for basis elements
F\in\mathcal{F}(X,\Omega)
.
For the initial step of
\mathrm{dep}(F)=0
, we define

(2)

\Delta_{\lambda}(F):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1\otimes 1,&\text{ if }F=1,\\
\bullet_{x}\otimes 1+1\otimes\bullet_{x}+\lambda\bullet_{x}\otimes\bullet_{x},&\text{ if }F=\bullet_{x}\text{ for some }x\in X,\\
\Delta_{\lambda}(\bullet_{x_{1}})\cdots\Delta_{\lambda}(\bullet_{x_{m}}),&\text{ if }F=\bullet_{x_{1}}\cdots\bullet_{x_{m}}\text{ with }m\geqslant 2\text{ and }x_{i}\in X.\end{array}\right.

For the induction step of
\mathrm{dep}(F)\geqslant 1
, we reduce the definition to induction on breadth.
If
{\rm bre}(F)=1
, we
write
F=B_{\omega}^{+}(\overline{F})
for some
\omega\in\Omega
and
\overline{F}\in\mathcal{F}(X,\Omega)
, and define

(3)

\Delta_{\lambda}(F)=\Delta_{\lambda}B_{\omega}^{+}(\overline{F}):=(B_{\omega}^{+}\otimes\mathrm{id})\Delta_{\lambda}(\overline{F})+(\mathrm{id}\otimes B_{\omega}^{+})\Delta_{\lambda}(\overline{F}).

In other words

(4)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\lambda}B_{\omega}^{+}=(B_{\omega}^{+}\otimes\mathrm{id}+\mathrm{id}\otimes B_{\omega}^{+})\Delta_{\lambda}.

If
{\rm bre}(F)\geqslant 2
, we write
F=T_{1}T_{2}\cdots T_{m}
with
m\geqslant 2
and
T_{1},\ldots,T_{m}\in\mathcal{T}(X,\Omega)
, and define

(5)

\Delta_{\lambda}(F)=\Delta_{\lambda}(T_{1})\cdots\Delta_{\lambda}(T_{m}).

2.5.
Dual of the coproduct
\Delta

We identify
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{RT}}(X,\Omega)
with its graded dual through the symmetric bilinear form defined by

\displaystyle\forall F,G\in\mathcal{F}(X,\Omega),\quad\langle F,G\rangle=\delta_{F,G}.

As the coproduct
\Delta=\Delta_{0}
is homogeneous, it induces a product
\star
on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{RT}}(X,\Omega)
, such that

\displaystyle\forall x,y,z\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{RT}}(X,\Omega),

\displaystyle\langle x\otimes y,\Delta(z)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle x\star y,z\rangle.

For any
F,G,H\in\mathcal{F}(X,\Omega)
, we put

N(F,G;H)=\{I\subseteq V(H)\mid H_{I}=F,\>H_{V(H)\setminus I}=G\}.

Then, by Theorem
2.14
, for any
H\in\mathcal{F}(X,\Omega)
,

\Delta(H)=\sum_{F,G\in\mathcal{F}(X,\Omega)}|N(F,G;H)|F\otimes G.

Therefore, for any
F,G\in\mathcal{F}(X,\Omega)
,

F\star G=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{F}(X,\Omega)}|N(F,G;H)|H.

In order to describe these sets
N(F,G;H)

\leqslant_{r}
and
\leqslant_{h}

x
to
y
in
F

F
being oriented from the roots to the leaves;

if
x
and
y
are not comparable for
\leqslant_{h}
and if in the plane representation of
F
,

y
is more on the right than
x

•

For any

x
and
y
are comparable for both
\leqslant_{h}
and
\leqslant_{r}

x=y

\leqslant_{h,r}=\leqslant_{h}\cup\leqslant_{r}

V(F)
.

Proposition 2.17
.

The correspondence
F\longrightarrow M(F)
is a bijection from
\mathcal{F}(X,\Omega)
to
\mathcal{FM}(X,\Omega)
.

Proof.

Let
F\in\mathcal{F}(X,\Omega)
, let us prove that
M(F)\in\mathcal{FM}(X,\Omega)
.
Points (a)-(d) are obvious. If
M(F)_{i,0}\in X
, then necessarily
v_{i}
is a leaf, so if
j>i
,
v_{i}\leqslant_{h}v_{j}
is not possible.
Therefore,
v_{i}\leqslant_{r}v_{j}
, and
M(F)_{i,j}=r
. This proves (e).
Let us now prove (f). Let
1\leqslant i<j<k\leqslant n
.
If
v_{i}\leqslant_{h}v_{j}
and
v_{j}\leqslant_{h}v_{j}
, then, as
\leqslant_{h}
is an order,
v_{i}\leqslant_{h}v_{k}
. Consequently,

\begin{pmatrix}M(F)_{i,j}&M(F)_{i,k}\\
M(F)_{j,j}&m_{j,k}\end{pmatrix}\neq\begin{pmatrix}h&r\\
=&h\end{pmatrix}.

Similarly, as
\leqslant_{r}
is an order,

\begin{pmatrix}M(F)_{i,j}&M_{(}F)_{i,k}\\
M(F)_{j,j}&M(F)_{j,k}\end{pmatrix}\neq\begin{pmatrix}r&h\\
=&r\end{pmatrix}.

If
v_{i}\neq_{r}v_{j}
and
v_{j}\leqslant_{h}v_{k}
, as
F
is a plane forest, then necessarily
v_{i}\leqslant_{r}v_{k}
, therefore,

\begin{pmatrix}M(F)_{i,j}&M(F)_{i,k}\\
M(F)_{j,j}&M(F)_{j,k}\end{pmatrix}\neq\begin{pmatrix}r&h\\
=&h\end{pmatrix}.

This finally proves (f). Therefore, the map
M:\mathcal{F}(X,\Omega)\longrightarrow\mathcal{MF}(X,\Omega)
is well-defined.
Note that
M(F)
allows to obtain
\leqslant_{h}
and
\leqslant_{r}
, after identification of
V(F)
with
\{1,\ldots,n\}
. So
M
is injective.
Let
A\in\mathcal{FM}(X,\Omega)
. We denote by
n
the number of rows of
A
. We define two relations
\leqslant_{h}
and
\leqslant_{r}
on
\{1,\ldots,n\}
by

\displaystyle\forall i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

\displaystyle i\leqslant_{h}j\mbox{ if }A_{i,j}\in\{=,h\},

\displaystyle i\leqslant_{r}j\mbox{ if }A_{i,j}\in\{=,r\}.

If
1\leqslant i<j<k\leqslant n
,

\begin{pmatrix}A_{i,j}&A_{i,k}\\
A_{j,j}&A_{j,k}\end{pmatrix}\neq\begin{pmatrix}h&r\\
=&h\end{pmatrix},

so if
A_{i,j}=h
and
A_{j,k}=h
, then
A_{i,k}=h
,
so
\leqslant_{h}
is an order. Similarly,
\leqslant_{r}
is an order. Let
F
be the Hasse graph of
\leqslant_{h}
. If
1\leqslant i<j<k\leqslant n
,

\begin{pmatrix}A_{i,j}&A_{i,k}\\
A_{j,j}&A_{j,k}\end{pmatrix}\neq\begin{pmatrix}r&h\\
=&h\end{pmatrix},

so
F
is a rooted forest. The relation
\leqslant_{r}
induces a plane structure on
F
, so
F
is a planar forest. We decorate the vertex
i
of
F
by
A_{i,0}
.
By condition (e), if
i
is decorated by
x\in X
, then
i
is a leaf of
F
, so
F\in\mathcal{F}(X,\Omega)
. By construction,
M(F)=A
. So
A
is surjective.
∎

Theorem 2.22
.

Let
F,G\in\mathcal{F}(X,\Omega)
, with respectively
k
and
l
vertices.

\displaystyle F\star G

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathrm{Sh}(k,l)}\sum_{C\in\mathcal{FM}_{\sigma}(M(F),M(G))}M^{-1}(C).

Proof.

Let
H\in\mathcal{F}(X,\Omega)
and
I\in N(F,G;H)
. We totally order the vertices of
H
:

v_{1}\leqslant_{h,r}\cdots\leqslant_{h,r}v_{k+l}.

We then put

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\{v_{i_{1}},\ldots,v_{i_{k}}\},

\displaystyle V(H)\setminus I

\displaystyle=\{v_{j_{1}},\ldots,v_{j_{l}}\},

with
i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k}
and
j_{1}<\ldots<j_{l}
.
We consider the
(k,l)
shuffle
\sigma=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k},j_{1},\ldots,j_{l})
.
As
H_{I}=F
and
H_{V(H)\setminus I}=G
:

(a)

If
1\leqslant i\leqslant k
, the decoration of
v_{\sigma(j)}
is the decoration of the
i
-th vertex of
F
, so
M(H)_{\sigma(i),0}=M(F)_{i,0}
.
Similarly, if
k+1\leqslant i\leqslant k+l
,

M(H)_{\sigma(i),0}=M(G)_{i-k,0}
.

(b)

If
1\leqslant i<j\leqslant k
, then
v_{\sigma(i)}\leqslant_{h}v_{\sigma(j)}
in
H
if, and only if
v_{i}\leqslant_{h}v_{j}
in
F
. So
M(H)_{\sigma(i),\sigma(j)}=M(F)_{i,j}
.
Similarly, if
k+1\leqslant i,j\leqslant k+l
,
M(H)_{\sigma(i),\sigma(j)}=M(G)_{i-k,j-k}
.

So
M(H)\in\mathcal{FM}_{\sigma}(M(F),M(G))
.
Conversely, if
\sigma\in\mathrm{Sh}(k,l)
and
M(H)\in\mathcal{FM}_{\sigma}(M(F),M(H))
, then, by definition,

\displaystyle H_{\{\sigma(1),\ldots,\sigma(k)\}}

\displaystyle=F,

\displaystyle H_{\{\sigma(k+1),\ldots,\sigma(k+l)\}}

\displaystyle=G.

Therefore, we obtain that

|N(F,G;H)|=|\{\sigma\in\mathrm{Sh}(k,l)\mid M(H)\in\mathcal{FM}_{\sigma}(M(F),M(G))\}|.

We obtain

\displaystyle F\star G

\displaystyle=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{F}(X,\Omega)}|N(F,G;H)|H

\displaystyle=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{F}(X,\Omega)}|\{\sigma\in\mathrm{Sh}(k,l)\mid M(H)\in\mathcal{FM}_{\sigma}(M(F),M(G))\}|H

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathrm{Sh}(k,l)}\sum_{C\in\mathcal{FM}_{\sigma}(M(F),M(G))}M^{-1}(C).\qed

3.
Hopf algebras and Rota-Baxter operators on rooted forests

We now show that there is a cocommutative Hopf algebra structure on decorated planar rooted forests under the special case of
\lambda=0
.
In this special case, we have the following results.

3.1.
Moerdijk Hopf algebras on decorated plan rooted forests

Theorem 3.1
.

(\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{RT}}(X,\Omega),m_{RT},1,\Delta,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{RT}})
is a connected graded cocommutative Hopf algebra.

Proof.

We know that
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{RT}}(X,\Omega)=\mathop{\oplus}\limits_{n\geqslant 0}\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{RT}}(X,\Omega)_{n}
with
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{RT}}(X,\Omega)_{0}\cong{\bf k}
is a graded algebra. Moreover, by Eq. (
9
),
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{RT}}(X,\Omega)
is a graded connected bialgebra with the coproduct
\Delta
. By Lemma 2 of
[
10
]
,
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{RT}}(X,\Omega)
is a Hopf algebra.

Furthermore, by Eq. (
9
) and the fact that the set of all subsets of a set equals the set of all complements of subsets of a set,
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{RT}}(X,\Omega)
is a cocommutative Hopf algebra.
∎

Since
(\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{RT}}(X,\Omega),m_{RT},1,\Delta,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{RT}})
is a connected graded Hopf algebra, it has an antipode
S
. Now we give a combinatorial description of
S
.

Theorem 3.2
.

For any decorated planar rooted forest
F\in\mathcal{F}(X,\Omega)\setminus\{1\}
,

(10)

\displaystyle S(F)=\sum\limits_{I_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup I_{k}=V(F)}(-1)^{k}F_{I_{1}}\cdots F_{I_{k}}.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.14
, the coproduct of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{RT}}(X,\Omega)
is given by

\displaystyle\forall F\in\mathcal{F}(X,\Omega)\setminus\{1\},

\displaystyle\Delta(F)

\displaystyle=F\otimes 1+1\otimes F+\underbrace{\sum_{I_{1}\sqcup I_{2}=V(F)}F_{\mid I_{1}}\otimes F_{\mid I_{2}}}_{:=\tilde{\Delta}(F)}.

The coproduct
\tilde{\Delta}
defined in this way is also coassociative, and a direct induction proves that
for any
n\geqslant 1
, then
n-1
-iteration of
\tilde{\Delta}
is given by

\displaystyle\forall F\in\mathcal{F}(X,\Omega)\setminus\{1\},

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}^{(n-1)}(F)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{I_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup I_{k}=V(F)}F_{I_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes F_{I_{k}}.

By Takeuchi’s formula
[
35
]
, the antipode is given by

\displaystyle\forall F\in\mathcal{F}(X,\Omega)\setminus\{1\},

\displaystyle S(F)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}m^{(k-1)}\circ\tilde{\Delta}^{(k-1)}(F)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{I_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup I_{k}=V(F)}(-1)^{k}F_{I_{1}}\cdots F_{I_{k}}.\qed

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
(H,\,m,\,1_{H},\,\Delta,\,\varepsilon,\,(P_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega})

be an
\Omega
-cocycle bialgebra. Then for each
\omega\in\Omega
,

P_{\omega}(H):=\{P_{\omega}(h)\mid h\in H\}

is an operated coideal of
H
.

Proof.

Let
\omega\in\Omega
. We first show
P_{\omega}(h)\subseteq\ker\varepsilon
.

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{H}(h^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{H}\left(\sum_{(h^{\prime})}h^{\prime}_{(1)}\varepsilon_{H}(h^{\prime}_{(2)})\right)=\sum_{(h^{\prime})}\varepsilon_{H}(h^{\prime}_{(1)})\varepsilon(h^{\prime}_{(2)})=(\varepsilon_{H}\otimes\varepsilon_{H})\Delta_{H}(h^{\prime}).

Thus

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{H}(h^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{H}P_{\omega}(h)=(\varepsilon_{H}\otimes_{H}\varepsilon_{H})\Delta_{H}(h^{\prime})=(\varepsilon_{H}\otimes\varepsilon_{H})\Delta_{H}(P_{\omega}(h))

\displaystyle=(\varepsilon_{H}\otimes\varepsilon_{H})\Big((P_{\omega}\otimes\mathrm{id}+\mathrm{id}\otimes P_{\omega})\Delta_{H}(h)\Big)\quad(\text{by Eq.~(\ref{eq:eqiterated})})

\displaystyle=(\varepsilon_{H}P_{\omega}\otimes\varepsilon_{H})\Delta_{H}(h)+(\varepsilon_{H}\otimes\varepsilon_{H}P_{\omega})\Delta_{H}(h)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(h)}\varepsilon_{H}P_{\omega}(h_{(1)})\varepsilon_{H}(h_{(2)})+\sum_{(h)}\varepsilon_{H}(h_{(1)})\varepsilon_{H}P_{\omega}(h_{(2)})

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{H}P_{\omega}\left(\sum_{(h)}h_{(1)}\varepsilon_{H}(h_{(2)})\right)+\varepsilon_{H}P_{\omega}\left(\sum_{(h)}\varepsilon_{H}(h_{(1)})(h_{(2)})\right)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{H}P_{\omega}(h)+\varepsilon_{H}P_{\omega}(h),

which implies that
\varepsilon_{H}P_{\omega}(h)=0
.

Secondly, for any
h\in H
,

\displaystyle\Delta(P_{\omega}(h))

\displaystyle=(P_{\omega}\otimes\mathrm{id})\Delta(h)+(\mathrm{id}\otimes P_{\omega})\Delta(h)\quad\text{ (by Eq.~(\ref{eq:eqiterated}))}

\displaystyle=(P_{\omega}\otimes\mathrm{id})\left(\sum_{(h)}(h_{(1)}\otimes h_{(2)})\right)+(\mathrm{id}\otimes P_{\omega})\left(\sum_{(h)}(h_{(1)}\otimes h_{(2)})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(h)}P_{\omega}(h_{(1)})\otimes h_{(2)}+\sum_{(h)}h_{(1)}\otimes P_{\omega}(h_{(2)})\in P_{\omega}(H)\otimes H+H\otimes P_{\omega}(H).

Thus
P_{\omega}(H)
is an operated coideal.
∎

Impact of STEM Project-Based Learning on research autonomy and engineering thinking in solar energy education
Authors: Sherzod Ramankulov, Ali Coruh, Makpal Nurizinova, Mazhyn Skakov, Nurkozha Zhaksylyk, Bakitzhan Kurbanbekov
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAt the present stage, the development of renewable energy sources, particularly solar energy, is considered one of the key directions for ensuring energy security and sustainable development. In this context, training specialists with well-developed research and engineering thinking in the field of solar energy within the higher education system has become an urgent issue. The purpose of this study is to theoretically and empirically examine the effectiveness of developing students’ research and engineering activities through the implementation of a STEM Project-Based Learning (PjBL) approach in solar energy education. The study was conducted using a sequential explanatory design, consisting of two phases. In the first phase, a systematic literature review and meta-analysis were carried out based on studies published between 2015 and 2025 and selected from Web of Science, Scopus, and Google Scholar in accordance with the PRISMA methodology. The meta-analysis was performed using Comprehensive Meta-Analysis 4.0 software and included 15 empirical studies. In the second phase, a pedagogical experiment was conducted at Khoja Akhmet Yassawi International Kazakh-Turkish University involving 88 students divided into control and experimental groups. An independent samples t-test was applied to analyze the collected data. The meta-analysis results indicated a large overall effect size (d = 1.75; 95% CI [1.29, 2.22]; p < 0.001), suggesting the strong potential of STEM–PjBL in enhancing students’ research and engineering skills. The experimental results showed no statistically significant difference between the control and experimental groups at the pre-test stage (t = 0.1425; p = 0.887), while a significant difference was observed at the post-test stage (t = 5.8550; p < 0.001), with a large effect size (Cohen’s d = 1.27). The findings suggest that the implementation of STEM Project-Based Learning in solar energy education can effectively support the development of students’ research autonomy, engineering thinking, and their ability to connect theoretical knowledge with real-world applications.Keywords: STEM Project-Based Learning, Solar energy, Research activities, Engineering activities, Project-based learning, Renewable energy sourcesSubject terms: Education; Science, technology and society

Introduction

The effective use of renewable energy sources, ensuring energy security, and maintaining environmental sustainability have become key priorities for the global scientific and educational community. Solar energy, in particular, is regarded as a crucial sector that reduces dependence on fossil fuels, minimizes environmental impact, and supports long-term sustainable development. Accordingly, higher education institutions face an increasing demand to prepare competitive specialists with well-developed research and engineering thinking in the field of solar energy.

To address these challenges, the Project-Based Learning (PjBL) model grounded in the STEM (Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics) framework plays a particularly important role. Research indicates that STEM-integrated project-based learning enhances learners’ scientific literacy and enables the effective integration of theoretical knowledge with practical applications1. Moreover, the STEM–PjBL approach not only improves students’ academic achievement but also increases their interest in the subject and strengthens learning motivation1.

The Project-Based Learning model is based on real-world problem situations and aims at the comprehensive development of students’ research and engineering activities. When applied within a STEM context, learners systematically engage in identifying scientific problems, formulating hypotheses, conducting experiments, processing data, and developing engineering solutions2. Such learning processes contribute to the development of higher-order thinking skills (HOTS) in accordance with the demands of the 21st century3. STEM-based project learning has been widely implemented in natural science disciplines, including physics, biology, and mathematics, and has been shown to effectively foster critical thinking, creativity, and analytical skills4,5.

Teaching solar energy through a STEM Project-Based Learning approach enables students to develop a deeper understanding of energy processes, investigate the operating principles of solar panels, perform energy calculations, and acquire practical skills in working with real technical systems. Projects focused on energy-related topics have been shown to enhance students’ energy literacy and strengthen their scientific and environmental responsibility6. In addition, the application of computer modeling and digital simulations plays a significant role in developing students’ engineering thinking7.

Recent systematic literature reviews indicate that the integration of STEM and Project-Based Learning approaches has a high potential for developing students’ 21st-century skills, including research, engineering, communicative, and practical competencies8,9. In this regard, STEM–PjBL projects in the field of solar energy are considered an effective pedagogical tool for preparing future specialists for professional practice.

Thus, the implementation of STEM Project-Based Learning projects in the field of solar energy facilitates the development of students’ research and engineering activities, enables the integration of theoretical knowledge with practical experience, and supports the formation of professional competencies aligned with the principles of sustainable development. This approach represents an important mechanism for training innovative and practice-oriented professionals within the higher education system.

Literature review

STEM-oriented Project-Based Learning (PjBL) has become one of the most widely discussed pedagogical models in educational research in recent years. This approach is aimed not only at developing students’ subject-specific knowledge but also at fostering creativity, research skills, problem-solving abilities, and engineering thinking in an integrated manner. Studies indicate that the STEM–PjBL model is significantly more effective than traditional teaching approaches in enhancing students’ learning motivation and developing creative thinking skills10.

Research conducted in the context of mathematics and natural science disciplines demonstrates that project-based learning integrated with the STEM approach enhances students’ conceptual understanding, creative potential, and cognitive engagement. Prajoko, S., Sukmawati, I., Maris, A. F., & Wulanjani, A. N. showed that the PjBL–STEM model positively influences students’ deep conceptual learning and their ability to produce creative outputs11. Similarly, the work of Purwati, H., Sudargo, S., Indiati, I., & Savira, A. M. examined this model from a metacognitive perspective and found that it plays a significant role in developing students’ mathematical creative thinking12.

The importance of the STEM–PjBL approach in fostering 21st-century skills has also been extensively addressed in the literature. Rahmania, I. described STEM-based project learning as an effective model for developing learners’ critical thinking, problem-solving, and research competencies13. Likewise, studies conducted at the primary education level have shown that STEM–PjBL yields high outcomes in the development of students’ problem-solving skills14.

Solar and alternative energy topics have increasingly become a focal area of research within the STEM Project-Based Learning framework. Reis, M. A. F., Reis, P., & Perin, D. L. highlighted that project-based activities on energy sustainability conducted in school settings enhance learners’ environmental awareness and responsibility toward energy use15. These findings confirm that energy-focused STEM projects carry significant socio-scientific relevance.

The integration of digital and virtual technologies into the STEM–PjBL process represents another important research direction. Ritter, K., Chambers, T. L., & Borst, C. W. demonstrated that teaching the topic of concentrating solar power plants within a virtual reality environment expands students’ engineering understanding16. Such technologies enable the modeling of complex energy systems, thereby enhancing students’ applied knowledge.

In physics education, teaching alternative energy topics through STEM–PjBL has been proven effective in developing problem-solving skills. Furthermore, systematic literature reviews on the effectiveness of the STEM–PjBL model indicate that this approach possesses broad pedagogical potential in physics education17.

The impact of the STEM–PjBL approach on the development of creative thinking has been examined across different educational levels. Sari, I. K., Handayani, R. D., & Supriadi, B. found that STEM–PjBL lessons on the topic of motion dynamics significantly enhanced students’ creative thinking indicators18, while Siew & Ambo demonstrated that primary school students’ scientific creativity can be effectively developed through STEM-based projects19.

The conducted literature review showed that the STEM Project-Based Learning (PjBL) approach has a high pedagogical potential in developing students’ research, engineering, creative and problem-solving skills. In particular, it was found that STEM projects based on solar energy and alternative energy sources allow students to combine theoretical knowledge with real industrial and engineering problems.

In addition, although the analyzed scientific works have proven the positive impact of the STEM–PjBL approach on learning motivation, applied thinking and the formation of 21st century skills, it is noticeable that there is a lack of research aimed at systematically evaluating the impact of this model in the comprehensive formation of research and engineering activities in the field of solar energy from an empirical and meta-analytical perspective.

In this regard, in order to determine the effectiveness of the implementation of the STEM Project-Based Learning project in the field of solar energy in the formation of students’ research and engineering activities, the following research questions were posed:

To what extent does the use of the STEM Project-Based Learning approach in teaching solar energy topics affect the formation of students’ research activities (problem definition, data collection, analysis, and conclusion)?

How effective are solar energy projects organized on the basis of STEM Project-Based Learning in developing students’ engineering activities (modeling, calculation, engineering decision-making, and systems thinking)?

Despite the growing number of studies on STEM–PjBL, there is still a lack of research that simultaneously examines students’ research autonomy and engineering thinking specifically in the context of solar energy education. In addition, previous studies are typically limited to either experimental investigations or literature reviews, while integrated approaches combining meta-analysis and controlled experiments remain insufficiently explored.

Therefore, this study aims to address these gaps by providing a comprehensive investigation based on both meta-analytical and experimental evidence.

The purpose of this study is to theoretically and empirically substantiate the effectiveness of shaping students’ research and engineering activities through the implementation of a STEM Project-Based Learning project in the field of solar energy.

Methods

Research design

This study was designed as a sequential explanatory mixed-method approach, consisting of two interconnected phases. In the first phase, a systematic literature review and meta-analysis were conducted to identify general patterns, effect sizes, and research gaps related to the effectiveness of STEM Project-Based Learning (PjBL) in solar energy education. The findings of this phase informed the design and focus of the second phase, which involved a controlled pedagogical experiment. This design ensured methodological coherence and allowed for a comprehensive evaluation of both existing evidence and empirical outcomes.

Phase 1: systematic review and meta-analysis

Selection criteria

This study was based on a systematic review and meta-analysis methodology. Pre-defined inclusion and exclusion criteria were used to select studies. Studies were included in the meta-analysis if they met the following criteria:

the study focused on teaching using the STEM Project-Based Learning (PjBL) approach;

the study focused on teaching using the STEM Project-Based Learning (PjBL) approach;

the study content was related to solar energy or renewable energy sources;

the study content was related to solar energy or renewable energy sources;

the study outcomes measured students’ research, engineering, or applied skills;

the study outcomes measured students’ research, engineering, or applied skills;

the quantitative data allowed for the calculation of effect sizes;

the quantitative data allowed for the calculation of effect sizes;

the study was published between 2015 and 2025.

the study was published between 2015 and 2025.

Review articles, theoretical papers, duplicate publications, and studies without the required statistical data were excluded from the review. During the synthesis, studies were grouped according to the similarity of the outcome variables.

Information sources

The literature search was conducted on the basis of international scientific databases. In particular, the Web of Science, Scopus and Google Scholar platforms were used. In addition, the reference lists of the selected articles were considered as additional sources. The last search was conducted in March 2025.

Search strategy

The search strategy was based on a unified keyword system for all databases. The main search terms included «STEM Project-Based Learning», «solar energy», «research activities», «engineering activities», «project-based learning», «renewable energy sources» and their equivalents. During the search, only peer-reviewed scientific articles were selected, and English was used as a language restriction.

Selection process

The process of selecting studies was carried out in accordance with the PRISMA methodology. In the first stage, the titles and abstracts of the found articles were reviewed. Then, the full texts were assessed for compliance with the inclusion criteria. This process was carried out by independent assessment by two researchers. In case of disagreement, they were resolved by mutual agreement. The selection results were presented using a PRISMA flow chart (Fig. 1).

PRISMA flow chart of data collection.

Data collection process

Data from each included study were collected using a standardized data collection schedule. Two researchers participated in the data collection process and collected the data independently. The obtained data were compared and rechecked in case of discrepancies. All necessary quantitative indicators were taken from the original publications.

Data indicators

The main outcome variables were indicators characterizing the students’ research autonomy and engineering thinking. The results for each study were processed in the form of standardized mean difference (Cohen’s d). All measurements were included in the meta-analysis only if there were data compatible with it.

The analysis of quantitative data was carried out according to two main parameters: the first one is the effect size of the standardized mean difference, and the second one is the standardized error rate. Additional variables

Additional variables included country of study, number of participants, level of education, duration of intervention, STEM project focus (research or engineering), and technology used. In some studies, missing statistical data were imputed using standard calculation formulas.

Assessment of risk of bias

A comprehensive set of methods was used to assess the risk of publication and methodological bias. In particular, the Begg and Mazumdar test, Egger regression test, Duval and Tweedie trim-and-fill method, and the fail-safe N indicator were used. In addition, a funnel plot diagram was created to visually assess the risk of bias.

Effect sizes

The standardized mean difference (Cohen’s d) was used as the effect size in the meta-analysis. 95% confidence intervals were calculated for each effect size. These indicators allowed us to quantitatively assess the pedagogical effectiveness of STEM Project-Based Learning projects.

Synthesis methods

A random-effects model was used to combine the results of the studies. This model allows us to take into account the fact that the studies were conducted in different educational contexts. The Q-test, I² and τ² indicators were used to assess heterogeneity. All statistical analyses were performed using the Comprehensive Meta-Analysis 4.0 program. The results are presented using forest plots and tables.

Heterogeneity analysis

A meta-regression analysis was performed to identify the reasons for the differences between the studies. The moderating variable was the type of activity in STEM projects (research or engineering).

Sensitivity analysis

Sensitivity analyses were performed to test the stability of the meta-analysis results. In this analysis, the change in the overall effect size was estimated by excluding individual studies.

Reporting bias

A funnel plot diagram and statistical tests were used to assess the risk of publication bias. The results obtained showed that the main conclusions were stable.

Assessment of the level of evidence

This study did not use the formal GRADE method to assess the quality of evidence. However, the reliability of the results was indirectly assessed by stability of effect sizes, confidence intervals, and publication bias analyses.

Phase 2: controlled pedagogical experiment

The second phase consisted of a controlled pedagogical experiment conducted at Khoja Akhmet Yassawi International Kazakh-Turkish University. A total of 88 undergraduate students participated and were divided into control (n = 44) and experimental (n = 44) groups.

The experimental group was taught using the STEM–PjBL approach in the context of solar energy, while the control group received traditional instruction. Pre-test and post-test measurements were conducted to evaluate students’ research and engineering activities. The equivalence of the groups at baseline was confirmed prior to the intervention.

The experiment was conducted over 6 weeks. During this period, students in the experimental group participated in structured STEM–PjBL activities, while the control group followed traditional instruction.

STEM–PjBL intervention procedure

The intervention was designed based on the results of the meta-analysis and focused on real-world solar energy problems. Students in the experimental group engaged in project-based tasks involving data collection, analysis of solar energy systems, engineering modeling, and problem-solving.

This process aimed to foster students’ research autonomy, engineering thinking, and ability to integrate theoretical knowledge with practical applications.

These results further support the argument that STEM–PjBL serves as an effective pedagogical framework for developing research autonomy and engineering thinking in solar energy education. In particular, studies conducted in the fields of solar energy, physics, and natural sciences suggest that the STEM–PjBL model demonstrates broad effectiveness across the included educational contexts (Fig. 2).

The majority of the reviewed studies indicate that teaching the topic of solar energy through STEM Project-Based Learning contributes to the development of learners’ skills in measurement, calculation, experimentation, analysis of energy flows, and engineering design. During the implementation of project-based and experimental activities, learners personally investigated the operating principles of solar panels, examined the generation and transformation of electrical energy, and developed applied knowledge by making practical engineering decisions.

Several scientific studies describe the STEM–PjBL approach as an effective tool for implementing interdisciplinary integration in the field of solar energy11,13,29. In these studies, the interconnection among physics, mathematics, engineering, and technology disciplines was strengthened through project-oriented activities and real-world problem-solving tasks. Such interdisciplinary instruction contributes to the development of students’ systems thinking, analytical reasoning, and engineering design competencies15,35. In particular, the interconnection among physics, mathematics, engineering, and technology disciplines was strengthened, and energy-related topics were taught in close connection with real-life contexts. Such integrated instruction contributed to the development of students’ research thinking, systems analysis abilities, and practice-oriented competencies.

In addition, a number of studies demonstrate that the use of digital modeling, virtual laboratories, and simulation tools confirms the high effectiveness of STEM products in teaching solar energy in virtual formats7,16,32. These technologies enable students to visualize complex engineering systems, test different operational parameters, and improve conceptual understanding through interactive experimentation environments. These tools enable the visual explanation of complex physical and engineering processes, allow solar energy systems to be modeled in a safe environment, and support the analysis of outcomes through the modification of various parameters.

In this regard, the application of STEM products and project-based learning technologies in physics education within the field of solar energy has been identified as one of the most relevant and strategically important directions in the contemporary education system. This approach ensures coherence among curriculum content, teaching methods, and assessment systems, while integrating theoretical physics knowledge with practical engineering experience.

The analysis of scientific literature shows that the accumulated data on the implementation of solar energy–based STEM–PjBL projects allow for the evaluation of the dynamics of learners’ applied skills development, including experimentation, measurement, calculation, modeling, and the engineering interpretation of obtained results. Based on these studies, the conducted content and comparative analyses made it possible to determine the pedagogical effectiveness of the STEM–PjBL approach in teaching solar energy and to proceed with subsequent meta-analysis.

The wide prediction interval reflects a high level of heterogeneity among studies, indicating that the magnitude of the intervention effect varies depending on contextual factors, applied methodologies, and participant characteristics. Nevertheless, the positive location of the mean effect size provides evidence of the overall effectiveness of the intervention.

In the meta-analysis, the results of evaluating the impact of implementing STEM Project-Based Learning projects in the field of solar energy on the development of students’ research and engineering activities are visually presented using a forest plot diagram (Fig. 5).

Graphical representation of meta-analysis results from data.

The forest plot serves as a primary analytical tool that enables the comparison of effect sizes obtained in individual studies and their pooled effect within a single graphical representation. This method allows for the assessment of confidence intervals, the direction and magnitude of effects, as well as the level of heterogeneity across studies.

Number of studies and databases

The meta-analysis was based on the results of 15 empirical studies. All of these studies were aimed at developing students’ research and engineering activities through the implementation of the STEM Project-Based Learning approach in the field of solar energy. This number of studies is considered sufficient for conducting a meta-analysis and allows for the generalization of the obtained results.

Effect size measure

To synthesize the results of the included studies, the standardized mean difference (Cohen’s d) was used as the effect size measure. This indicator enables a quantitative assessment of the magnitude of the impact of STEM–PjBL projects on students’ research and engineering competencies.

Statistical model applied

A random-effects model was employed in the meta-analysis. This model accounts for the fact that the included studies were conducted under different educational contexts, across various educational levels, and using diverse STEM products. Accordingly, the results are generalized not only to the studies included in the analysis but also to the broader population of comparable studies.

Mean effect size

The meta-analysis yielded a mean effect size of d = 1.75. This value represents a very large effect according to standards commonly applied in educational research. Thus, the implementation of STEM–PjBL projects in the field of solar energy contributes to the development of students’ research, engineering, and applied skills at a significantly higher level compared to traditional teaching approaches.

Confidence interval and statistical significance

The 95% confidence interval for the mean effect size ranged from 1.29 to 2.22. Since this interval does not include zero, the result is statistically significant.

The Z-test result (Z = 7.39; p < 0.001) indicates the rejection of the null hypothesis, confirming that the effect of STEM–PjBL projects on students’ research and engineering activities is not due to random chance.

Heterogeneity (Q-test)

The Q-test was applied to assess heterogeneity among the studies. The obtained result (Q = 82.4; df = 14; p < 0.001) indicates that the effect sizes across studies are not homogeneous. This heterogeneity can be explained by differences in the content and duration of solar energy projects, educational levels, and the variety of STEM tools employed in the studies.

I2 statistic

For the overall random-effects meta-analysis, the I² value was 58%, indicating a moderate level of heterogeneity. However, in the subsequent meta-regression model, residual heterogeneity remained high (I² ≈ 93%), suggesting that additional unexplained between-study variance persists beyond the examined moderator.

Overall, the analysis conducted for individual indicators demonstrates that the implementation of STEM Project-Based Learning projects in the field of solar energy has a high level of pedagogical effectiveness in developing students’ research, engineering, and applied activities. The obtained results indicate that the STEM–PjBL approach represents a scientifically grounded and practically effective direction for integration into the higher education system.

The results of the meta-analysis reveal that the pedagogical impact of STEM Project-Based Learning projects varies depending on educational contexts and methodological characteristics. Implementation of the STEM project-based learning project in the field of solar energy

This section presents the implementation of the STEM Project-Based Learning (PjBL) intervention used in the experimental phase of the study. While the first phase of the research involved a meta-analysis to identify general patterns and effectiveness, the second phase aimed to empirically test these findings through a controlled pedagogical experiment.

Therefore, the following description outlines the design and implementation of the STEM–PjBL project applied in the experimental group, which served as the main instructional intervention for developing students’ research and engineering activities.

As an example of STEM project-based learning aimed at developing learners’ research and engineering activities, a comprehensive educational project focused on the field of solar energy was implemented. This project was designed in accordance with the principles of Project-Based Learning and was intended to engage students in scientific analysis of a real engineering problem, data-driven investigation, and the proposal of applied solutions. The primary objective of the project was to develop future specialists’ research thinking, engineering modeling skills, and data-based decision-making abilities by integrating theoretical knowledge with practical problem-solving tasks.

Within the framework of the STEM Project-Based Learning approach, an educational project oriented toward the field of solar energy was organized to foster students’ research and engineering activities. The project content was based on working with real industrial data, conducting statistical analyses, and developing engineering models. As the research object of the educational project, actual production data from solar power plants (SPPs) located in the Turkestan region for the period 2022–2025 were used.

The stage of formation of research activity

The stage of formation of research activity

The stage of research activity formation focused on hands-on data collection and analysis in the field of solar energy, conducted within the university campus environment. At this stage, students worked directly with a photovoltaic power plant installed in a campus parking area, which served as a real-world experimental site for STEM-based research. Learners collaboratively collected, processed, and interpreted operational data from the solar power system using digital monitoring tools and measurement instruments (Fig. 7).

The stage of research activity formation: data collection and analysis on solar energy.

As illustrated in Fig. 7, students analyzed real-time and historical performance parameters of the parking-area photovoltaic installation through remote monitoring systems. The figure depicts both the solar power plant infrastructure and students engaged in data analysis activities, highlighting the integration of authentic engineering facilities into the educational process. Students compared the forecasted photovoltaic output (PVOUT) obtained from the Global Solar Atlas with actual electricity production data recorded on site. Monthly and annual performance trends were examined, allowing learners to identify seasonal patterns characterized by peak electricity generation during summer months and reduced output in winter. The analysis revealed that the real photovoltaic output was, on average, 8.5% lower than the forecasted values across all months, fostering students’ analytical thinking and research competencies.

The results of statistical processing showed high reliability of the study: Pearson correlation coefficient r = 0.96, mean percentage error MPE = + 8.5%, and root mean square error RMSE = 48.2 kWh/kWp. These indicators confirm the influence of the temperature factor on the efficiency of solar panels and are consistent with the results of international studies.

2.Engineering Analysis and Modeling

Engineering Analysis and Modeling

During the engineering phase, students identified the main causes of the deviations. The analysis revealed that at high temperatures (+ 40…+48 °C) the efficiency of photovoltaic panels decreases by 15–18%, and dust accumulation on the panel surface causes an additional loss of 2–4%. In addition, inverter and network technical losses ranged from 3 to 5%.

Taking into account these factors, students proposed seasonal correction factors: k = 0.94 for the winter period, k = 0.92 for the transitional periods, k = 0.88 for the summer period, and the annual average factor was k = 0.915. These factors allowed for a more accurate forecast of actual production.

Discussion

The findings of this study demonstrate that the implementation of STEM Project-Based Learning (PjBL) in solar energy education is effective in fostering students’ research autonomy and engineering thinking. The results of both the meta-analysis and the controlled experiment provide consistent evidence supporting the pedagogical value of this approach.

The large effect size identified in the meta-analysis (d = 1.75) indicates a strong overall impact of STEM–PjBL on student learning outcomes. This finding is consistent with the results reported by Syamra and Suryadi36, who found that STEM-oriented project-based learning significantly enhances students’ scientific creativity and applied thinking in renewable energy education. However, while their study primarily focused on creativity, the present study extends these findings by demonstrating a substantial impact on both research autonomy and engineering thinking within a solar energy context.

Similarly, the experimental results of this study revealed a statistically significant improvement in the performance of the experimental group (p < 0.001), which aligns with the findings of Widiyaningsih et al.37, who reported that STEM–PjBL significantly improves higher-order thinking and analytical skills. Unlike their study, which was conducted in a biotechnology context, the present research confirms that such improvements are also evident in physics-based solar energy education, highlighting the cross-disciplinary applicability of STEM–PjBL. Moreover, Sie and Lin38 demonstrated that STEM learning environments organized around IoT smart energy-saving projects positively influence learners’ cognitive engagement, motivation, and technological understanding. These findings further support the argument that renewable energy contexts provide meaningful interdisciplinary environments for enhancing STEM learning outcomes.

The findings related to engineering thinking are also supported by the work of Tang et al.35, who demonstrated that solar energy projects enhance students’ ability to make engineering decisions and analyze real-world systems. In the present study, students engaged in analyzing real production data, developing regression models, and improving forecasting accuracy, which further strengthens the argument that STEM–PjBL facilitates authentic engineering experiences. Similar conclusions were reported by Gadhamshetty, Shrestha, and Kilduff39, who emphasized that project-based engineering education involving renewable energy technologies contributes significantly to students’ engineering design skills, applied problem-solving abilities, and understanding of sustainable technological systems.

In addition, the integration of theoretical knowledge with practical application observed in this study is consistent with the results of Téllez et al.40, who emphasized the importance of linking theory and practice through STEM-based tools. The present study contributes to this line of research by demonstrating how real industrial data and engineering modeling can be effectively incorporated into the learning process, thereby enhancing students’ applied competencies. Furthermore, the use of authentic industrial datasets and engineering calculations in the present study provided students with opportunities to engage in evidence-based reasoning and real-world decision-making processes, which are considered essential components of contemporary STEM education39,41.

Furthermore, the findings align with Thaha et al.42, who reported that a deeper understanding of STEM integration leads to improved learning outcomes. In this study, students’ engagement in structured project-based activities contributed to the development of both analytical and decision-making skills, suggesting that the effectiveness of STEM–PjBL is closely related to the depth of its implementation. In addition, recent studies have highlighted that technology-enhanced STEM environments integrating smart systems and renewable energy applications strengthen students’ motivation and readiness for future engineering and scientific careers38.

Overall, while previous studies have confirmed the effectiveness of STEM–PjBL in improving various learning outcomes, the present study extends existing knowledge by providing a combined meta-analytical and experimental validation of its impact on research autonomy and engineering thinking in solar energy education. This integrated approach represents a key contribution to the literature and offers a more comprehensive understanding of the pedagogical effectiveness of STEM–PjBL. The study also contributes to the growing body of international research emphasizing that renewable energy education represents one of the most promising interdisciplinary contexts for the implementation of STEM-based project learning and the development of future-oriented competencies38,39,41.

Limitations and future research

Despite the strong findings, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, although the meta-analysis followed PRISMA guidelines, the number of included studies (k = 15) remains relatively limited, and moderate heterogeneity (I² = 58%) suggests variability across contexts. The presence of a possible small-study effect indicated by the Egger test (p = 0.029) also requires cautious interpretation. Second, several studies reported very large effect sizes, which may have influenced the pooled estimate despite the use of a random-effects model. Future research may benefit from additional sensitivity analyses and subgroup investigations. Third, the controlled pedagogical experiment was conducted within a single institutional context, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to other educational settings. In addition, longitudinal data were not collected, and therefore the long-term sustainability of the observed effects remains unclear.

Future studies should expand the sample across multiple institutions, include longitudinal measurements, and explore additional moderating variables influencing the effectiveness of STEM–PjBL.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the findings of this study suggest that the implementation of the STEM Project-Based Learning (PjBL) approach in the field of solar energy can be effective in supporting the development of students’ research and engineering activities. The results are based on the integration of theoretical analysis, meta-analysis, and experimental data, which together provide evidence of the pedagogical potential of this approach.

The results of the systematic literature review and meta-analysis indicate that STEM–PjBL projects in solar energy education are associated with a moderate-to-high positive effect on students’ research autonomy, engineering thinking, data analysis, and modeling skills. The observed large mean effect size (d = 1.75; p < 0.001) suggests a strong overall trend, although the presence of moderate heterogeneity implies that the effectiveness of the approach may vary depending on educational context and implementation conditions.

The findings of the pedagogical experiment further support these results. While no statistically significant differences were observed between the control and experimental groups at the pre-test stage (p = 0.887), the improvement in the experimental group at the post-test stage (t = 5.855; p < 0.001) indicates a positive impact of the STEM–PjBL intervention on students’ research and engineering activities.

During the implementation of the educational project, students developed skills in working with real industrial data, conducting statistical analyses, and applying engineering models. These experiences may contribute to the integration of theoretical knowledge with practical applications and support the development of data-driven decision-making skills.

Overall, the results of this study suggest that the STEM Project-Based Learning approach has the potential to be integrated into higher education as a practice-oriented pedagogical model in solar energy education. The approach may contribute to the development of students’ research culture, engineering thinking, and ability to analyze real-world problems.

Thus, STEM–PjBL in the context of solar energy education can be considered a promising approach, although further research across different contexts and with longitudinal designs is needed to confirm its broader applicability.

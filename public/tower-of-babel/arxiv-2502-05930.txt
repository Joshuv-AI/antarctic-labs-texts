A short proof of the generalized Conway–Gordon–Sachs theorem Thanks: The author was supported by JSPS KAKENHI Grant Number JP19K03500.
Ryo Nikkuni

Abstract
. The famous Conway–Gordon–Sachs theorem for the complete graph on six vertices was extended to the general complete graph on n n vertices by Kazakov–Korablev as a congruence modulo 2 2 , and its integral lift was given by Morishita–Nikkuni. However, the proof is complicated and long. In this paper, we provide a shorter proof of the generalized Conway–Gordon–Sachs theorem over integers.

Primary 57M15; Secondary 57K10

1.
Introduction

An embedding
f
of a finite graph
G
into the
3
-sphere is called a
spatial embedding
of
G
, and
f(G)
is called a
spatial graph
of
G
. We call a subgraph of
G
homeomorphic to the circle a
cycle
of
G
, and a cycle containing exactly
p
vertices a
p
-cycle
. We denote the set of all
p
-cycles of
G
by
\Gamma_{p}(G)
. Additionally, we also denote the set of all disjoint pairs of cycles of
G
consisting of a
p
-cycle and a
q
-cycle by
\Gamma_{p,q}(G)
. In general, for a closed
1
-manifold
\lambda
in
G
, the image
f(\lambda)
is a knot or a link contained in
f(G)
. In particular, if
\lambda
belongs to
\Gamma_{p,q}(G)
, then we call
f(\lambda)
a
(p,q)
-link
of
f(G)
. Moreover, if
\lambda
contains all vertices of
G
, then we also call
f(\lambda)
a
Hamiltonian link
of
f(G)
.

Let
K_{n}
be the
complete graph
on
n
vertices, that is the graph consisting of
n
vertices such that each pair of two distinct vertices is connected by exactly one edge. The fact that for every spatial graph of
K_{6}
, the sum of the linking numbers over all of the
(3,3)
-links is odd is well known as the
Conway-Gordon–Sachs Theorem

[
1
]
,
[
7
]
. If
n
is 7 or more, Kazakov–Korablev showed that for every spatial graph of
K_{n}
, the sum of the linking numbers over all of the Hamiltonian links is even
[
2
]
. On the other hand, Morishita and the author significantly generalized this modulo
2
congruence formula over integers as follows.

Theorem 1-1
.

(Morishita–Nikkuni
[
4
]
)

Let
n\geq 6
be an integer and
p,q\geq 3
two integers satisfying
n=p+q
. For every spatial graph
f(K_{n})
of
K_{n}
, the following holds:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\Gamma_{p,q}(K_{n})}{\rm lk}(f(\lambda))^{2}=(2-\delta_{pq})\cdot(n-6)!\sum_{\lambda\in\Gamma_{3,3}(K_{n})}{\rm lk}(f(\lambda))^{2}.

Here,
lk
denotes the linking number and
\delta_{pq}
denotes the Kronecker’s delta.

It also holds from (
1.1
) that

(1.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{p+q=n}\sum_{\lambda\in\Gamma_{p,q}(K_{n})}{\rm lk}(f(\lambda))^{2}=(n-5)!\sum_{\lambda\in\Gamma_{3,3}(K_{n})}{\rm lk}(f(\lambda))^{2}.

Kazakov–Korablev’s result above can be obtained immediately by taking the modulo
2
reduction on both sides of (
1.2
). Since the right side of (
1.2
) is not zero, it is also clear that for any
p
and
q
satisfying
p+q=n
, there always exists a nonsplitable Hamiltonian
(p,q)
-link of
f(K_{n})
. This is also a generalization of Vesnin–Litvintseva’s result
[
8
]
that
f(K_{n})
always contain a nonsplittable Hamiltonian link. Furthermore, it has also been shown in
[
4
]
that for
n\geq 6
, the following congruence modulo
2\cdot(n-5)!
can be obtained from (
1.2
):

\displaystyle\sum_{p+q=n}\sum_{\lambda\in\Gamma_{p,q}(K_{n})}\!\!\!\!{\rm lk}(f(\lambda))^{2}\equiv\left\{\begin{array}[]{@{\,}lll}(n-5)!&(n\equiv 6,7\pmod{8})\\
0&(n\not\equiv 6,7\pmod{8}).\end{array}\right.

For additional formulas on evaluating the sum of linking numbers over Hamiltonian links derived from (
1.1
), see
[
4
]
. The proof of Theorem
1-1
given in
[
4
]
was done using tricky induction procedures and was lengthy and tedious. Our purpose in this paper is to simplify and shorten the proof of Theorem
1-1
, thereby making it more accessible to a wider audience. The key to this simplification is Lemma
2-2
, which reveals a particular homological property concerning the linking of a knot with a spatial complete graph. The proof of Theorem
1-1
presented here is a reorganized version of the one originally introduced by the author in the Japanese textbook
[
6
]
several years ago.

Remark 1-2
.

Conway–Gordon also showed that for every spatial graph of
K_{7}
, the sum of the Arf invariants over all of the Hamiltonian knots is odd
[
1
]
. For further details on refining this fact or extending it to general
K_{n}
, see
[
5
]
,
[
3
]
.

2.
New proof of Theorem
1-1

In the following, let
K_{n}
consist of
n
vertices
1,2,\ldots,n
. We denote the edge of
K_{n}
connecting two distinct vertices
i
and
j
by
\overline{ij}
, and denote a path of length 2 of
K_{n}
consisting of two edges
\overline{ij}
and
\overline{jk}
by
\overline{ijk}
.

Lemma 2-1
.

Let
G
be the graph which is a disjoint union of a loop
e
and
K_{4}
. For every spatial graph
f(G)
of
G
, the following holds:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{\delta\in\Gamma_{4}(K_{4})}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\delta))^{2}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{3}(K_{4})}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}.

Proof.

We give the orientation to the loop
e
and each of the edges of
K_{4}
as shown in Fig.
2.1
, and identify each of the cycles of
K_{4}
with an element of the first integral homology group
H_{1}(K_{4};{\mathbb{Z}})
as follows:

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}=\langle 12\rangle+\langle 24\rangle-\langle 14\rangle,\ \gamma_{2}=\langle 23\rangle+\langle 34\rangle-\langle 24\rangle,\ \gamma_{3}=\langle 31\rangle+\langle 14\rangle-\langle 34\rangle,

\displaystyle\gamma_{4}=\langle 12\rangle+\langle 23\rangle+\langle 31\rangle,\ \delta_{1}=\langle 23\rangle+\langle 31\rangle+\langle 14\rangle-\langle 24\rangle,

\displaystyle\delta_{2}=\langle 31\rangle+\langle 12\rangle+\langle 24\rangle-\langle 34\rangle,\ \delta_{3}=\langle 12\rangle+\langle 23\rangle+\langle 34\rangle-\langle 14\rangle.

Note that
\gamma_{i}\in\Gamma_{3}(K_{4})\ (i=1,2,3,4)
and
\delta_{j}\in\Gamma_{4}(K_{4})\ (j=1,2,3)
. Since
\delta_{j}=\gamma_{j+1}+\gamma_{j+2}
in
H_{1}(K_{4};{\mathbb{Z}})
, we have

\displaystyle{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\delta_{j}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma_{j+1}))+{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma_{j+2})),

where we regard
\gamma_{j+3}=\gamma_{j}
. Then we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{3}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\delta_{j}))^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i=1}^{3}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma_{i}))^{2}

\displaystyle+2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq 3}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma_{i})){\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma_{j})).

On the other hand, since
\gamma_{4}=\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}+\gamma_{3}
in
H_{1}(K_{4};{\mathbb{Z}})
, we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma_{4}))=\sum_{i=1}^{3}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma_{i})).

By (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{4}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma_{i}))^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{3}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma_{i}))^{2}+{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma_{4}))^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i=1}^{3}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma_{i}))^{2}

\displaystyle+2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq 3}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma_{i})){\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma_{j}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{3}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\delta_{j}))^{2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2-2
.

Let
n\geq 4
be an integer and
G
the graph which is a disjoint union of a loop
e
and
K_{n}
. For every spatial graph
f(G)
of
G
, the following holds:

(2.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n}(K_{n})}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n-1}(K_{n})}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}.

Proof.

Let us prove (
2.4
) by induction on
n
. The case where
n=4
is nothing other than Lemma
2-1
. So we assume that
n>4
. Let
f(G)
be a spatial graph of
G
. For a vertex
m
of
K_{n}

(m=1,2,\ldots,n)
, we denote the subgraph obtained by removing
m
and all edges incident to
m
from
K_{n}
by
K_{n-1}^{(m)}
. This subgraph is isomorphic to
K_{n-1}
, see the left figure in Fig.
2.2
. Furthermore, for vertices
i,j
and
m
of
K_{n}
such that
1\leq i<j\leq n
and
i,j\neq m
, let
F_{ij}^{(m)}
denote the subgraph obtained by removing the edge
\overline{ij}
and
n-3
edges
\overline{mk}

(1\leq k\leq n,\ k\neq i,j)
. This subgraph is not isomorphic to
K_{n-1}
but is homeomorphic to it. Actually,
F_{ij}^{(m)}
is obtained by replacing the edge
\overline{ij}
in
K_{n-1}^{(m)}
with the path
\overline{imj}
of length
2
, see the right figure in Fig.
2.2
. Then for the spatial subgraph
f(e\cup F_{ij}^{(m)})
, by the assumption we have

(2.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n}(F_{ij}^{(m)})}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{n-1}(K_{n-1}^{(m)})\\
\overline{ij}\not\subset\gamma\end{subarray}}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{n-1}(F_{ij}^{(m)})\\
\overline{imj}\subset\gamma\end{subarray}}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{n-2}(K_{n-1}^{(m)})\\
\overline{ij}\not\subset\gamma\end{subarray}}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}.

Let us fix the vertex
m
and add both sides of (
2.5
) for all
i,j
such that
1\leq i<j\leq n
and
i,j\neq m
. First, consider an
n
-cycle
\gamma
of
K_{n}
where the vertices
i
and
j
are adjacent to the vertex
m
on
\gamma
. In this case,
\gamma
is an
n
-cycle of
F_{ij}^{(m)}
and we have

(2.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq n\\
i,j\neq m\end{subarray}}\bigg(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n}(F_{ij}^{(m)})}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}\bigg)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n}(K_{n})}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}.

Next, consider an
(n-1)
-cycle
\gamma
of
K_{n-1}^{(m)}
. Let
\overline{ij}
be an edge of
K_{n-1}^{(m)}
that is not contained in
\gamma
. Then there are
\binom{n-1}{2}-(n-1)=(n^{2}-5n+4)/2
ways to choose such
i
and
j
, and thus we have

(2.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq n\\
i,j\neq m\end{subarray}}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{n-1}(K_{n-1}^{(m)})\\
\overline{ij}\not\subset\gamma\end{subarray}}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}-5n+4}{2}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n-1}(K_{n-1}^{(m)})}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}.

Next, consider an
(n-1)
-cycle
\gamma
of
K_{n}
containing the vertex
m
, where the vertices
i
and
j
are adjacent to
m
on
\gamma
. Then
\gamma
is an
(n-1)
-cycle of
F_{ij}^{(m)}
containing the path
\overline{imj}
and we have

(2.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq n\\
i,j\neq m\end{subarray}}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{n-1}(F_{ij}^{(m)})\\
\overline{imj}\subset\gamma\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{n-1}(K_{n})\\
m\subset\gamma\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}.

Finally, consider an
(n-2)
-cycle
\gamma
of
K_{n-1}^{(m)}
. Let
\overline{ij}
be an edge of
K_{n-1}^{(m)}
that is not contained in
\gamma
. Then there are
\binom{n-1}{2}-(n-2)=(n^{2}-5n+6)/2
ways to choose such
i
and
j
, and thus we have

(2.9)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq n\\
i,j\neq m\end{subarray}}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{n-2}(K_{n-1}^{(m)})\\
\overline{ij}\not\subset\gamma\end{subarray}}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}-5n+6}{2}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n-2}(K_{n-1}^{(m)})}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}.

Furthermore, for each
f(K_{n-1}^{(m)})
, the following holds by the assumption:

(2.10)

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n-1}(K_{n-1}^{(m)})}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n-2}(K_{n-1}^{(m)})}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}.

Theorefore, from (
2.5
) and (
2.6
), (
2.7
), (
2.8
), (
2.9
), (
2.10
), we have

(2.11)

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n}(K_{n})}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{n-1}(K_{n})\\
m\subset\gamma\end{subarray}}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}+\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n-1}(K_{n-1}^{(m)})}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}.

Then, let us add both sides of (
2.11
) for all
m=1,2,\ldots,n
. First, in the
(n-1)
-cycle
\gamma
of
K_{n}
, there are
(n-1)
ways to choose the vertex
m
that is contained in
\gamma
. Thus we have

(2.12)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{n-1}(K_{n})\\
m\subset\gamma\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}\bigg)=(n-1)\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n-1}(K_{n})}\!\!\!\!{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}.

Second, in the
(n-1)
-cycle
\gamma
of
K_{n}
, for the vertex
m
that is not contained in
\gamma
, the cycle
\gamma
is an
(n-1)
-cycle of
K_{n-1}^{(m)}
. Since there is only one way to choose such an
m
, we have

(2.13)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n}\bigg(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n-1}(K_{n-1}^{(m)})}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}\bigg)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n-1}(K_{n})}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}.

Then by substituting (
2.12
) and (
2.13
) into (
2.11
), we have

\displaystyle n\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n}(K_{n})}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}=n\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n-1}(K_{n})}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 2-3
.

Let
n\geq 4
be an integer and
G
the graph which is a disjoint union of a loop
e
and
K_{n}
. For every spatial graph
f(G)
of
G
, the following holds:

(2.14)

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n}(K_{n})}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}=(n-3)!\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{3}(K_{n})}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}.

Proof.

We denote the subgraph of
K_{n}
obtained by removing exactly
k
vertices
m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{k}\ (1\leq k\leq n-1)
and all edges incident to them from
K_{n}
by
K_{n-k}^{(m_{1}m_{2}\cdots m_{k})}
. Note that this subgraph is isomorphic to
K_{n-k}
. Then by repeatedly using Lemma
2-2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n}(K_{n})}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n-1}(K_{n})}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{1}=1}^{n}\bigg(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n-1}(K_{n-1}^{(m_{1})})}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{1}=1}^{n}\bigg(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n-2}(K_{n-1}^{(m_{1})})}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}\bigg)=2\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n-2}(K_{n})}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{1\leq m_{1}<m_{2}\leq n}\bigg(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n-2}(K_{n-2}^{(m_{1}m_{2})})}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{1\leq m_{1}<m_{2}\leq n}\bigg(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n-3}(K_{n-2}^{(m_{1}m_{2})})}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\cdot 3\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n-3}(K_{n})}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}=\cdots=k!\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n-k}(K_{n})}{\rm lk}(f(e),f(\gamma))^{2}.

So if we set
k=n-3
, we have the desired conclusion.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1-1
.

For a
k
-cycle
\gamma
of
K_{n}
, let
G_{\gamma}
denote the subgraph of
K_{n}
obtained by removing the
k
vertices of
\gamma
and their edges. Note that this subgraph is isomorphic to
K_{n-k}
. Then we have

(2.15)

\displaystyle(1+\delta_{pq})\sum_{\lambda\in\Gamma_{p,q}(K_{n})}{\rm lk}(f(\lambda))^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{p}(K_{n})}\bigg(\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{q}(G_{\gamma})}{\rm lk}(f(\gamma),f(\gamma^{\prime}))^{2}\bigg).

Then by Corollary
2-3
, the right side of (
2.15
) can be expressed as follows:

(2.16)

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{p}(K_{n})}\bigg(\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{q}(G_{\gamma})}{\rm lk}(f(\gamma),f(\gamma^{\prime}))^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{p}(K_{n})}\bigg((q-3)!\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{3}(G_{\gamma})}{\rm lk}(f(\gamma),f(\gamma^{\prime}))^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q-3)!\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{3}(K_{n})}\bigg(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{p}(G_{\gamma^{\prime}})}{\rm lk}(f(\gamma),f(\gamma^{\prime}))^{2}\bigg).

Let
H_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{i}\ (i=1,2,\ldots,l,\ l=\binom{n-3}{p})
be the collection of all subgraphs of
G_{\gamma^{\prime}}
that are isomorphic to
K_{p}
. Then the sets of
p
-cycles
\Gamma_{p}(H_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{i})
are mutually disjoint, and their union is
\Gamma_{p}(G_{\gamma^{\prime}})
. Then by Corollary
2-3
, we have

(2.17)

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{3}(K_{n})}\bigg(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{p}(G_{\gamma^{\prime}})}{\rm lk}(f(\gamma),f(\gamma^{\prime}))^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{3}(K_{n})}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{l}\bigg(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{p}(H_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{i})}{\rm lk}(f(\gamma),f(\gamma^{\prime}))^{2}\bigg)\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{3}(K_{n})}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{l}\bigg((p-3)!\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{3}(H_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{i})}{\rm lk}(f(\gamma),f(\gamma^{\prime}))^{2}\bigg)\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p-3)!\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{3}(K_{n})}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{l}\bigg(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{3}(H_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{i})}{\rm lk}(f(\gamma),f(\gamma^{\prime}))^{2}\bigg)\bigg).

Note that each
3
-cycle
\gamma
of
G_{\gamma^{\prime}}
is shared by exactly
\binom{n-6}{p-3}
of the
H_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{i}
subgraphs. This implies that

(2.18)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}\bigg(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{3}(H_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{i})}\!\!\!\!{\rm lk}(f(\gamma),f(\gamma^{\prime}))^{2}\bigg)=\binom{n-6}{p-3}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{3}(G_{\gamma^{\prime}})}\!\!\!\!{\rm lk}(f(\gamma),f(\gamma^{\prime}))^{2}.

Then it holds from (
2.16
), (
2.17
) and (
2.18
) that

(2.19)

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{p}(K_{n})}\bigg(\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{q}(G_{\gamma})}{\rm lk}(f(\gamma),f(\gamma^{\prime}))^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p-3)!(q-3)!\binom{n-6}{p-3}\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{3}(K_{n})}\bigg(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{3}(G_{\gamma^{\prime}})}{\rm lk}(f(\gamma),f(\gamma^{\prime}))^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(n-6)!\sum_{\lambda\in\Gamma_{3,3}(K_{n})}{\rm lk}(f(\lambda))^{2}.

By (
2.15
), (
2.19
) and
2/(1+\delta_{pq})=2-\delta_{pq}
, we have the result.
∎

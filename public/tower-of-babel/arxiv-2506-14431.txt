Almost uniform convergence for noncommutative Vilenkin-Fourier series Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. Primary 46L52; Secondary 42B20, 46L53
Yong Jiao, Sijie Luo, Tiantian Zhao, Dejian Zhou

Abstract
. In the present paper, we study almost uniform convergence for noncommutative Vilenkin-Fourier series. Precisely, we establish several noncommutative (asymmetric) maximal inequalities for the Cesàro means of the noncommutative Vilenkin-Fourier series, which in turn give the corresponding almost uniform convergence. The primary strategy in our proof is to explore a noncommutative generalization of Sunouchi square function operator, and the very recent advance of the noncommutative Calderón-Zygmund decomposition.



1.
Introduction

The problem of pointwise convergence for the Fourier series is one of the
central and oldest themes in harmonic analysis. For
f\in L_{1}(\mathbb{T})
, the partial sum of the Fourier series of
f
is defined by

S_{n}(f)(t)\coloneqq\sum_{k=-n}^{n}\widehat{f}(k)e^{2\pi ikt},\quad n\in\mathbb{N},\,t\in\mathbb{T},

where
\widehat{f}(k)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(y)e^{-2\pi iky}dy
is the
k
-th Fourier coefficient of
f
.
Carleson and Hunt (
[
6
,
33
]
) showed that
S_{n}(f)
converges a.e. to
f
(as
n\to\infty
) provided
f\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T})
for
1<p<\infty
. However, in 1923, it was already shown by Kolmogorov that there exists
g\in L_{1}(\mathbb{T})
such that
S_{n}(g)
diverges almost everywhere; see e.g.
[
26
, Theorem 3.4.2]
. Hence, it is more suitable to consider some other summability methods for functions in
L_{1}(\mathbb{T})
. For instance, the so-called Cesàro means (or Fejér means) of Fourier series are defined as follows: for
f\in L_{1}(\mathbb{T})
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\sigma_{n}(f)\coloneqq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}S_{j}(f).

In 1905, Lebesgue
[
44
]
extended a Fejér’s result (see e.g.
[
26
, Theorem 3.3.1]
) by obtaining that, for each
f\in L_{1}(\mathbb{T})
,

(1.1)

\sigma_{n}(f)\to f,\quad\mbox{a.e.},\quad(\mbox{as}~n\to\infty).

Recently, due to broad interactions with quantum information theory, group theory, and noncommutative geometry, the noncommutative Fourier analysis has become an active area, and numerous results concerning pointwise convergences of the Fourier series have been established successfully in the noncommutative setting. In 2013, Chen, Xu and Yin
[
9
, Theorem 4.2]
obtained the noncommutative version of (
1.1
) on quantum tori, that is, the Cesàro means converge bilaterally almost uniformly (b.a.u.) with initial data in
L_{1}(\mathbb{T}_{\theta}^{d})
, where
\mathbb{T}_{\theta}^{d}
is a quantum torus. We refer readers to Definition
A.1
in the appendix for different types of almost uniform convergences in the noncommutative setting. Recently, Hong et al.
[
32
]
studied the pointwise convergence of Fourier series for group von Neumann algebras and quantum groups. Specifically, they developed a general criterion of maximal inequalities for approximate identities of noncommutative multipliers, which allows them to show the b.a.u. (or a.u.) convergence for associated Fourier multipliers on noncommutaitve
L_{p}
-spaces (
1<p<\infty
), under certain conditions. In the study of multiparametric b.a.u. convergence, Conde-Alonso et al.
[
10
]
established noncommutative correspondings of the Jessen-Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund theorem and the Córdoba-Fefferman-Guzmán inequality for quite general processes. As a consequence, they obtained the b.a.u. convergence for free Poisson semigroup with initial element in
L\log^{2}L(\mathcal{M})
.

In the present paper, we firstly focus on the Cesàro summability for the Vilenkin-Fourier series of operator-valued functions, and then transfer the operator-valued results into their corresponding totally noncommutative setting.
The operator-valued (or semi-commutative) model often provides deep insights in noncommutative harmonic analysis, and sometimes plays essential role based on the transference principles. For example, in the paper
[
9
]
(see also
[
10
]
), the authors firstly proved noncommutative maximal inequality for Cesàro means in operator-valued setting, and then transferred the results to quantum tori.
We also refer the reader to the papers
[
4
,
11
,
30
,
42
,
70
]
whose main ideas are to reduce the problems in their setting to the corresponding problems in the operator-valued setting.

In order to explain the classical theory roots of those we investigate here, we need to recall several remarkable results. Historically, the classical Walsh system was first introduced by Paley
[
47
]
, and the classical Vilenkin system was first introduced by Vilenkin
[
66
]
. In the classical setting, Walsh system and Vilenkin system has been studied extensively in the harmonic analysis; see for instance
[
24
,
50
,
57
]
. Motivated by Carleson and Hunt’s results
[
6
,
33
]
, it has been shown that the partial sums of the Walsh-Fourier series of an
L_{p}(0,1)
-function converge almost everywhere to the function for
1<p<\infty
(see
[
3
,
34
,
59
]
), and such convergence result fails for the case
p=1
. However, similar to the Fourier case, if we consider some other summability methods, we could still obtain the almost convergence for
L_{1}(0,1)
functions. Let
(\sigma_{n}(f))_{n\geq 1}
be the Cesàro means for the Walsh-Fourier series of
f\in L_{1}(0,1)
, more precisely,

\sigma_{n}(f)(\eta)=\int_{0}^{1}f(t)K_{n}(\eta\oplus t)dt,~\eta\in(0,1),

where
K_{n}
is the Walsh-Fejér kernel and
\oplus
is the dyadic addition. In the substantial paper
[
19
]
, Fine proved that
\sigma_{n}(f)
converges almost everywhere to
f
for each
f\in L_{1}(0,1)
. Besides, Schipp
[
56
]
investigated Fine’s result with an entirely different approach and deduced the convergence theorem via the following weak type
(1,1)
maximal inequality:

(1.2)

\Big\|\sup_{n\geq 1}|\sigma_{n}(f)|\Big\|_{L_{1,\infty}(0,1)}\leq c\|f\|_{L_{1}(0,1)},\quad f\in L_{1}(0,1).

By interpolation, (
1.2
) further implies the following strong type
(p,p)
inequality which was obtained by Sunouchi
[
62
]
and Yano
[
71
]
independently:

(1.3)

\Big\|\sup_{n\geq 1}|\sigma_{n}(f)|\Big\|_{L_{p}(0,1)}\leq c_{p}\|f\|_{L_{p}(0,1)},\quad f\in L_{p}(0,1),\quad 1<p\leq\infty.

Furthermore, in 1979, Fujii
[
20
]
proved that

(1.4)

\Big\|\sup_{n\geq 1}|\sigma_{n}(f)|\Big\|_{L_{1}(0,1)}\leq c\|f\|_{H_{1}(0,1)},

where
H_{1}(0,1)
denotes the martingale Hardy space.
The corresponding results mentioned above have also been established in the Vilenkin system; we refer the reader to
[
25
]
for the almost convergence of partial sums in bounded Vilenkin system (see also
[
49
]
for a new proof); to
[
50
]
for more related results. Furthermore, Gát
[
22
]
established (
1.2
) and (
1.4
) on Vilenkin-like systems.

Motivated by (
1.2
)-(
1.4
), our first object is to deal with noncommutative maximal inequalities associated with the specific family of Cesàro means of operator-valued Fourier series in the so-called Vilenkin-like system (a common generalization of the Walsh system, Vilenkin system,
the character system of the group of 2-adic (m-adic) integers, the product system of normalized
coordinate functions for continuous irreducible unitary representations of the coordinate
groups of noncommutative Vilenkin groups, the UDMD product systems; see e.g.
[
22
]
for more information). Note that even the definition of the maximal function
\sup_{n}f_{n}
is not available in the noncommutative setting. The problems of noncommutative maximal inequalities are often non-trivial, and the usual techniques in classical analysis involving maximal functions seem no
longer available in the noncommutative case. This applies to noncommutative martingale Doob inequality
[
38
]
, noncommutative maximal ergodic theorems
[
40
]
, noncommutative Hardy-Littlewood maximal inequalities
[
46
]
, and also to our topic in the present paper (see below for more information).

Our first main result of this paper is the following noncommutative version of (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) for Cesàro means of Vilenkin-like-Fourier series of operator-valued functions. In the sequel, let
\mathcal{N}=L_{\infty}(G_{m})\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{M}
, where
G_{m}
is a Vilenkin space associated with a bounded sequence
m=(m_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{N}
(see (
2.5
) below for details) and
\mathcal{M}
is a semifinite von Neumann algebra. The definitions of
\|\cdot\|_{\Lambda_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{N},\ell_{\infty})}
,
\|\cdot\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N},\ell_{\infty})}
and any unspecific notations are referred to Section
2
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Given a bounded sequence
m=(m_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathcal{N}=L_{\infty}(G_{m})\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{M}
. Let
(\sigma_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be the Cesàro means of the Vilenkin-like-Fourier series of functions in
L_{p}(\mathcal{N})
.

(i)

If
p=1
, then there is a constant
c>0
such that

\|(\sigma_{n}(f))_{n\geq 1}\|_{\Lambda_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{N},\ell_{\infty})}\leq c\|f\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{N})}.

(ii)

If
1<p\leq\infty
, then there is a constant
c_{p}>0
such that

\|(\sigma_{n}(f))_{n\geq 1}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N},\ell_{\infty})}\leq c_{p}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N})},

where
c_{p}=\frac{cp^{2}}{(p-1)^{2}}
for some positive constant
c
.

There appear several difficulties to prove this theorem. The pointwise estimate used in the commutative proof
[
22
]
does not work for our purpose now. As mentioned above, Vilenkin-like system is a common generalization of the Walsh system, Vilenkin system,
the character system of the group of 2-adic (m-adic) integers and other systems. This leads that the Fejér kernels in Vilenkin-like system do not behave as well as the Fejér kernels on the torus
\mathbb{T}
which are dominated by decreasing functions whose integrals are bounded. In fact, for the time
being there is no formula for the Fejér kernels in the Vilenkin-like system. Hence we can not reduce our problem to Mei’s noncommutative Hardy-Littlewood maximal inequalities as done in
[
9
]
. Finally, we point out that the Cesàro means
(\sigma_{n})_{n}
do not even preserve self-adjointness.
To overcome or avoid these difficulties, we reduce Theorem
1.1
to Theorem
4.1
; see the argument before Theorem
4.1
.
One of the key ingredients in our proof is to exploit the new noncommutative Calderón-Zygmund decomposition (see Theorem
2.4
below) established very recently by Cadilhac et al.
[
4
]
. The great advantage of the improved decomposition is that one can circumvent the pseudo-localization technique, which may not work well for noncommutative maximal inequalities. Recently, noncommutative weak type maximal inequalities for singular integrals were established in
[
29
]
. However, the Fejér kernels for Vilenkin-like system does not fulfill the
L_{q}
-integral regularity condition used in
[
29
]
, where such condition plays a significant role. In addition, in order to apply the noncommutative Calderón-Zygmund decomposition, we also need to further explore new properties for the Fejér kernels of the Vilenkin-like system; see Lemmas
4.4
–
4.6
.

Our second main objective is the noncommutative analogy of the Fujii inequality (
1.4
). The symbol
H_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
denotes the noncommutative martingale Hardy space; see Section
5
. It seems that the following inequality does not hold in general

\left\|(\sigma_{k}(f))_{k\geq 1}\right\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{N},\ell_{\infty})}\leq c\|f\|_{H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{N})},

since such kind of inequality fails even for noncommutative martingales (
[
39
, Lemma 13]
).
Inspired by
[
28
]
, it is natural to consider asymmetric maximal inequalities for the Cesàro means
(\sigma_{k})_{k\geq 1}
as follows: for
1\leq p\leq 2
,

(1.5)

\|(\sigma_{k}(f))_{k\geq 1}\|_{\Lambda_{p,\infty}(\mathcal{N},\ell_{\infty}^{c})}\leq c_{p}\|f\|_{H_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{N})}.

A crucial difficulty to show this inequality is that we could not take the advantage of the self-adjointness-preserving nature of the Cesàro means
(\sigma_{k})_{k\geq 1}
directly. Besides, the powerful algebraic atomic decomposition technique used in
[
28
]
is not available in our setting. Indeed, by a well-known fact in
[
38
]
that each conditional expectation
\mathcal{E}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
can be rewritten as: for some linear isometry
u:L_{2}(\mathcal{M})\to L_{2}(\mathcal{N}\bar{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}))
,

\mathcal{E}(x^{*}y)=u(x)^{*}u(y),\quad x,y\in L_{2}(\mathcal{M}),

which plays an important role in establishing asymmetric inequalities for noncommutative martingales. However, the failure of such property for the Cesáro means
(\sigma_{k})_{k\geq 1}
obstructs us from using the algebraic atomic decomposition technique.
Due to such difficulties, we can not prove (
1.5
) at the time of this writing. Fortunately, via appropriate estimate of a certain noncommutative square function, we can obtain a lacunary version of (
1.5
). Recall that a sequence
(n_{k})_{k\geq 1}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
is called lacunary if there exists a number
r>1
such that
n_{k+1}/n_{k}>r
for all
k\geq 1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Given a bounded sequence
m=(m_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathcal{N}=L_{\infty}(G_{m})\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{M}
. Let
(\sigma_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be the Cesàro means of the Vilenkin-like-Fourier series of functions in
L_{p}(\mathcal{N})
, and let
(n_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be a lacunary sequence.

(i)

For
1\leq p\leq 2
, there exists
c_{p}>0
such that

\|(\sigma_{n_{k}}(f))_{k\geq 1}\|_{\Lambda_{p,\infty}(\mathcal{N},\ell_{\infty}^{c})}\leq c_{p}\|f\|_{H_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{N})}.

The same holds for row spaces.

(ii)

For
1<p<2
, there exists
c_{p}>0
such that

\inf_{f=f^{c}+f^{r}}\{\|(\sigma_{n_{k}}(f^{c}))_{k\geq 1}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N},\ell_{\infty}^{c})}+\|(\sigma_{n_{k}}(f^{r}))_{k\geq 1}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N},\ell_{\infty}^{r})}\}\leq c_{p}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N})}.

Our proof of the asymmetric inequalities in Theorem
1.2
here entirely differs from the approach in
[
28
]
but relies on estimates of a concrete noncommutative square function – a noncommutative generalization of Sunouchi operator. Our primary strategy is inspired by
[
72
, Chapter XV]
, where maximal inequality was established via Littlewood-Paley theory. We explain the key idea here in noncommutative language. By the basic result Lemma
2.12
, we have

\displaystyle\|(\sigma_{n_{k}}(f))_{k\geq 1}\|_{\Lambda_{p,\infty}(\mathcal{N},\ell_{\infty}^{c})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|[\sigma_{n_{k}}(f)-\mathbb{E}_{k}(f)]_{k\geq 1}\|_{\Lambda_{p,\infty}(\mathcal{N},\ell_{\infty}^{c})}+\|(\mathbb{E}_{k}(f))_{k\geq 1}\|_{\Lambda_{p,\infty}(\mathcal{N},\ell_{\infty}^{c})}

\displaystyle\leq\|[\sigma_{n_{k}}(f)-\mathbb{E}_{k}(f)]_{k\geq 1}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N},\ell_{2}^{c})}+\|(\mathbb{E}_{k}(f))_{k\geq 1}\|_{\Lambda_{p,\infty}(\mathcal{N},\ell_{\infty}^{c})},

where
(\mathbb{E}_{k})_{k\geq 1}
denote the conditional expectations are given as in (
2.7
). Using the available result for noncommutative martingales
[
28
, Theorem A]
, it suffices to prove the following inequality

(1.6)

\|U(f)\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N})}=\|[\sigma_{n_{k}}(f)-\mathbb{E}_{k}(f)]_{k\geq 1}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N},\ell_{2}^{c})}\leq c_{p}\|x\|_{H_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{N})},

where

U(f)=\Big(\sum_{k\geq 1}|\sigma_{n_{k}}(f)-\mathbb{E}_{k}(f)|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

This operator was firstly
studied by Sunouchi in
[
62
]
for the classical Walsh setting, i.e.
n_{k}=2^{k}
, where the equivalence
\|U(f)\|_{L_{p}(G_{m})}\sim_{p}\|f\|_{L_{p}(G_{m})}
,
1<p<\infty
, was established. This so-called Sunouchi operator actually plays an important role for strong summability of Fourier series; see e.g.
[
63
]
. In the classical bounded Vilenkin system, the Sunouchi operator was investigated by Gát in
[
21
]
, where he established the
H_{1}
-
L_{1}
boundedness of
U
. We also refer the reader to
[
58
]
and
[
68
]
and their references about this topic.
We need to point out that even for
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{C}
, this is the first time to study such generalization of the Sunouchi operator
U
in the Vilenkin-like system. Note that now the Fejér kernels of the Vilenkin-like system do not have good properties as the ones in Vilenkin case; also noncommutativety causes additional difficulties. The usual argument in available literature such as
[
62
,
21
,
58
,
68
]
does not work well to obtain (
1.6
). We finally prove (
1.6
) in Theorem
5.5
with the help of new properties of the Fejér kernels of the Vilenkin-like system (see Lemma
4.5
). Of course, we apply the powerful tool, i.e., noncommutative atomic decomposition of martingale Hardy spaces.
We also point out that Theorem
5.5
is even new in the classical case.

Now we turn to the main theme of this paper, that is, the corresponding results of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
in totally noncommutative setting.
Before going further, we first recall several related results.
In the series of papers by Sukochev and his collaborators
[
61
,
1
,
7
]
, appropriate noncommutative generalizations of the Walsh system for hyperfinite
\mathrm{II}_{1}
,
\mathrm{II}_{\infty}
and
\mathrm{III}_{\lambda}

(0<\lambda\leq 1)
factors have been introduced. Moreover, they proved that the corresponding noncommutative Walsh system is a Schauder basis in the
L_{p}
-spaces for
1<p<\infty
. In
[
37
]
, Jiao et al. showed that the sequence of partial sums of the corresponding noncommutative Walsh-Fourier series in the hyperfinite
\mathrm{II}_{1}
factor is a uniformly bounded family of weak type
(1,1)
operators. Moreover, Wu
[
69
]
studied multipliers for noncommutative Walsh-Fourier series. Considering the results related to the noncommutative Walsh system in the CAR algebra, we refer interested readers to
[
5
]
for the hypercontractivity, to
[
45
]
for the Riesz transform, and to
[
17
]
for Poincaré type inequalities. In addition, Sukochev and his collaborators
[
16
,
15
,
55
]
also studied noncommutative generalizations of the Vilenkin system for the hyperfinite
\mathrm{II}_{1}
factor, and they proved that partial sums operator of the corresponding noncommutative Vilenkin-Fourier series is convergent in the
L_{p}
-spaces for
1<p<\infty
. Recently, the authors in
[
65
]
proved the main result of
[
55
]
via transference method. However, to the best of our knowledge, there is no any available literature considering pointwise convergence (almost uniform convergence) of the noncommutative Vilenkin-Fourier series; one of our motivations is to fill such gaps.

We transfer Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
into noncommutative Vilenkin system setting. In what follows,
\mathcal{R}
denotes the the hyperfinite
\mathrm{II}_{1}
factor.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(\sigma^{\mathcal{R}}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be the Cesàro means of the noncommutative Vilenkin-Fourier series.

(i)

There exists a universal constant
c>0
such that

\left\|(\sigma^{\mathcal{R}}_{n}(x))_{n\geq 1}\right\|_{\Lambda_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{R},\ell_{\infty})}\leq c\|x\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{R})}.

(ii)

For
1<p\leq\infty
,

\left\|(\sigma^{\mathcal{R}}_{n}(x))_{n\geq 1}\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{R},\ell_{\infty})}\leq\frac{cp^{2}}{(p-1)^{2}}\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{R})}.

With the help of Theorem
1.3
, the following result, whose proof is omitted here, can be proved similarly to that in
[
9
, Theorem 5.1]
.

Corollary 1.4
.

For
1\leq p<\infty
and
x\in L_{p}(\mathcal{R})
,
\sigma_{n}^{\mathcal{R}}(x)
converges to
x
b.a.u. as
n\to\infty
. Moreover, for
2\leq p<\infty
, the b.a.u. convergence can be strengthened to the a.u. convergence.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
(\sigma^{\mathcal{R}}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be the Cesàro means of the noncommutative Vilenkin-Fourier series and
(n_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be a lacunary sequence.

(i)

For
1\leq p\leq 2
, there exists
c_{p}>0
such that

\|(\sigma^{\mathcal{R}}_{n_{k}}(x))_{k\geq 1}\|_{\Lambda_{p,\infty}(\mathcal{R},\ell_{\infty}^{c})}\leq c_{p}\|x\|_{H_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{R})}.

The same holds for row spaces.

(ii)

For
1<p<2
, there exists
c_{p}>0
such that

\inf_{x=x^{c}+x^{r}}\{\|(\sigma^{\mathcal{R}}_{n_{k}}(x^{c}))_{k\geq 1}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{R},\ell_{\infty}^{c})}+\|(\sigma^{\mathcal{R}}_{n_{k}}(x^{r}))_{k\geq 1}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{R},\ell_{\infty}^{r})}\}\leq c_{p}\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{R})}.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.5
, we obtain the following convergence result, whose proof is analogous to
[
13
, Proposition 6]
. Hence, we omit the proof.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
(n_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be a lacunary sequence.

(i)

For any
x\in H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{R})
,

\sigma^{\mathcal{R}}_{n_{k}}(x)
converges to
x
c.a.u. as
k\to\infty
. Similarly, when
x\in H_{1}^{r}(\mathcal{R})
, r.a.u. convergence holds.

(ii)

For any
x\in L_{p}(\mathcal{R})
with
1<p<2
,

\sigma^{\mathcal{R}}_{n_{k}}(x)
converges to
x
column
+
row a.u. as
k\to\infty
.

Here we mention the main difference between our results and
[
32
]
. In
[
32
]
(see Theorems 3.2 and 3.3 there), Hong et al. only consider noncommutative almost uniform convergence for the elements in
L_{p}
with the range
1<p<\infty
. However, our results, i.e., Theorem
1.3
and Corollary
1.4
, work for elements in
L_{1}(\mathcal{R})
. As for
1<p<2
,
[
32
, Theorem 3.3]
shows that, under certain criterion, lacunary Fourier series in
L_{p}
(for group von Neumann algebras or quantum groups) converge bilaterally almost uniformly. Our result Corollary
1.6
(ii) establishes stronger column
+
row almost uniform convergence for lacunary noncommutative Vilenkin-Fourier series.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Noncommutative (weak) Lebesgue spaces

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Nuemann algebra with a normal faithful
semifinite trace
\tau
. Let
L_{0}(\mathcal{M})
denote the algebra of all
\tau
-measurable operators. For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, let
L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
(simply
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
) be the associated noncommutative Lebesgue space. As usual,
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
is just
\mathcal{M}
with the usual operator norm. Also recall that

L_{p}(\mathcal{M})=\{x\in L_{0}(\mathcal{M}):\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}<\infty\}

with

\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}=[\tau(|x|^{p})]^{1/p},

where
|x|=(x^{*}x)^{1/2}
is the modulus of
x
.

Suppose that
a
is a self-adjoint
\tau
-measurable operator and let
a=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\lambda de_{\lambda}
stand for its spectral decomposition. For any Borel subset
B
of
\mathbb{R}
, the spectral projection of
a
corresponding to the set
B
is defined by
\chi_{B}(a)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\chi_{B}(\lambda)de_{\lambda}
.
For
x\in L_{0}({\mathcal{M}})
, the generalized singular value function
\mu(t,x)
is defined by

\mu(t,x)=\inf\big\{s>0:\tau\big(\chi_{(s,\infty)}(|x|)\big)\leq t\big\},\quad t>0.

The function
t\mapsto\mu(t,x)
is decreasing and right-continuous; for more detailed study of the singular value function we refer the reader to
[
18
]
. Of special interest in this paper is the noncommutative weak Lebesgue space
L_{p,\infty}(\mathcal{M})
, with the quasi-norm

\|x\|_{L_{p,\infty}(\mathcal{M})}=\sup_{t>0}t^{1/p}\mu(t,x)=\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda[\tau(\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}(|x|))]^{1/p}.

2.2.
Noncommutative vector-valued spaces

In this subsection, we recall the definitions of the spaces
L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{\infty}^{\theta})
and
\Lambda_{p,\infty}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{\infty}^{c})
introduced in
[
38
,
51
]
(see also
[
28
]
).

For
0\leq\theta\leq 1
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
, define
L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{\infty}^{\theta})
as the space of all sequences
x=(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
for which there exist
a\in L_{p/(1-\theta)}(\mathcal{M})
,
b\in L_{p/\theta}(\mathcal{M})
and a bounded sequence
y=(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{M}
such that

x_{n}=ay_{n}b,\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

For
x=(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{\infty}^{\theta})
, define

(2.1)

\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{\infty}^{\theta})}=\inf\{\|a\|_{L_{\frac{p}{1-\theta}}(\mathcal{M})}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|y_{n}\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})}\|b\|_{L_{\frac{p}{\theta}}(\mathcal{M})}\},

where the infimum is taken over all possible factorizations of
x
as above. In the sequel,
L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{\infty}^{1/2})
,
L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{\infty}^{1})
and
L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{\infty}^{0})
will be denoted by
L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{\infty})
,
L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{\infty}^{c})
and
L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{\infty}^{r})
, respectively.

Following
[
28
]
, for
1\leq p<\infty
, define
\Lambda_{p,\infty}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{\infty})
as the space of all sequences
x=(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
L_{p,\infty}(\mathcal{M})
with quasi-norm given by

(2.2)

\|x\|_{\Lambda_{p,\infty}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{\infty})}=\sup_{t>0}\inf_{e\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})}\{t\tau(\textbf{1}-e)^{\frac{1}{p}}:\|ex_{n}e\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})}\leq t,\forall n\in\mathbb{N}\}<\infty.

For a sequence
x=(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
L_{0}(\mathcal{M})
, set

\|x\|_{\Lambda_{p,\infty}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{\infty}^{c})}=\sup_{t>0}\inf_{e\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})}\{t\tau(\mathbf{1}-e)^{\frac{1}{p}}:\|x_{n}e\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})}\leq t,\forall n\in\mathbb{N}\}.

Define
\|x\|_{\Lambda_{p,\infty}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{\infty}^{r})}=\|x^{*}\|_{\Lambda_{p,\infty}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{\infty}^{c})}
.

According to the definitions, it is obvious that

\sup_{n}\|x_{n}\|_{L_{p,\infty}(\mathcal{M})}\leq\|x\|_{\Lambda_{p,\infty}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{\infty})}\leq\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{\infty})},\quad\forall 1\leq p<\infty.

Besides, if
x=(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{\infty})
and
x_{n}=x_{n}^{*}
for each
n
, then (see
[
14
, p. 518]
or
[
40
]
)

(2.3)

\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{\infty})}=\inf\{\|a\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}:a\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M}),-a\leq x_{n}\leq a,\;\forall n\in\mathbb{N}\}.

Remark 2.1
.

Consider self-adjoint operators
x=(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
y=(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Assume that
-y_{n}\leq x_{n}\leq y_{n}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Note that for self-adjoint operators
a,b\in L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
,
-b\leq a\leq b
implies
\|a\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})}\leq\|b\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})}
(see e.g.
[
41
, Proposition 4.2.8]
). Then, according to (
2.2
), it is easy to see that

\|x\|_{\Lambda_{p,\infty}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{\infty})}\leq\|y\|_{\Lambda_{p,\infty}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{\infty})},\quad 1\leq p<\infty.

Similarly, by (
2.3
), we also have

\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{\infty})}\leq\|y\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{\infty})},\quad 1\leq p\leq\infty.

2.3.
Noncommutative Hilbert space valued spaces

Given
x=(x_{k})_{k=1}^{n}
a sequence of elements in
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
, for each
1\leq p<\infty
, define
\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell^{c}_{2})}
as

\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell^{c}_{2})}=\Big\|\Big(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}|x_{k}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}.

Analogously, we could also define

\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell^{r}_{2})}=\|x^{*}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell^{c}_{2})}=\Big\|\Big(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}|x^{*}_{k}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}.

The two norms above usually are not comparable at all if
p\neq 2
. Let
L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{2}^{c})
(resp.
L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{2}^{r})
) be the completion of the space of all finite sequences in
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{2}^{c})}
(resp.
\|\cdot\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{2}^{r})}
).

2.4.
Noncommutative martingales

Let
(\mathcal{M}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be an increasing sequence of von Neumann subalgebras of
\mathcal{M}
such that
\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\mathcal{M}_{n}
is weak-
*
dense in
\mathcal{M}.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{n}
be the conditional expectation from
\mathcal{M}
onto
\mathcal{M}_{n}.
A sequence
x=(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
in
L_{1}(\mathcal{M})+L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
is called a noncommutative martingale with respect to
(\mathcal{M}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
if

\mathcal{E}_{n}(x_{n+1})=x_{n},\qquad\forall n\geq 1.

The sequence
(dx_{k})_{k\geq 1}
is the martingale differences of the martingale
x=(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
, which is defined by
dx_{1}\coloneqq x_{1}
and

dx_{k}\coloneqq x_{k}-x_{k-1},\qquad\forall k\geq 2.

If
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}\subseteq L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
for some
1\leq p\leq\infty
, then
x
is called an
L_{p}
-martingale. In this case, we set

\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}\coloneqq\sup_{n\geq 1}\|x_{n}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}.

If
\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}<\infty
, then
x
is called an
L_{p}
-bounded martingale.

Recall that
(\mathcal{M}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is a regular filtration if there exists a positive number
R_{\rm reg}\geq 1
such that

(2.4)

\mathcal{E}_{n}(x)\leq R_{\rm reg}\mathcal{E}_{n-1}(x)

for each positive
x\in L_{1}(\mathcal{M})
. The number
R_{\rm reg}
is usually called the regularity constant.

Throughout this paper, we will mainly focus on the following concrete regular filtration in a given Vilenkin space.
Let
m:=\{m_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of positive integers such that
m_{k}\geq 2
, and let
G_{m_{k}}
denote a set of cardinality
m_{k}
, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
We equip
G_{m_{k}}
with the discrete topology and the uniform probability measure
\mu_{k}
(for each
t\in G_{m_{k}}
).
Set

(2.5)

G_{m}=\prod_{k\in\mathbb{N}}G_{m_{k}}.

Thus each
t\in G_{m}
is a sequence
t:=\left(t_{0},t_{1},\ldots\right)
, where
t_{k}\in G_{m_{k}},k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
G_{m}
is called a Vilenkin space. Indeed,
G_{m}
is a compact totally disconnected space with normalized regular Borel measure
\mu
such that
\mu\left(G_{m}\right)=1
. Throughout the paper, we assume that Vilenkin space
G_{m}
is bounded, that is,
M:=\sup_{k}m_{k}<\infty
.

For each
t\in G_{m}
and
1\leq n\in\mathbb{N}
, a base for the neighborhoods of
G_{m}
is given as follows

I_{0}(t):=G_{m},\quad I_{n}(t):=\left\{s=\left(s_{i}\right)_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\in G_{m}:s_{i}=t_{i}\text{ for }i<n\right\}.

Particularly, set
I_{n}:=I_{n}(0)
. Let

\mathcal{F}:=\sigma\left\{I_{n}(t):n\in\mathbb{N},t\in G_{m}\right\}

be the
\sigma
-algebra generated by all intervals on
G_{m}
.

Example 2.2

(regular filtration in Vilenkin space)
.

Consider
(G_{m},\mathcal{F},\mu)
. For convenience, denote
|Q|
the measure of
Q
. We simply write
L_{\infty}(G_{m},\mathcal{F},\mu)
by
L_{\infty}(G_{m})
. Recall that
\mathcal{N}=L_{\infty}(G_{m})\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{M}
is the tensor von Neumann algebra equipped with the trace
\varphi=\int\otimes\tau
.
Set

(2.6)

\mathcal{F}_{n}=\sigma\{I_{n}(t):t\in G_{m}\},\quad n\geq 1.

Then
(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is the filtration of
\mathcal{F}
. The conditional expectations with respect to
(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
are denoted by
(\mathbb{E}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
. More precisely, for
f\in L_{1}(G_{m})
,

(2.7)

\mathbb{E}_{n}(f)=\sum_{Q\in D(\mathcal{F}_{n})}f_{Q}\chi_{Q},\quad\forall n\geq 1,

where

D(\mathcal{F}_{n})
denotes the collection of all intervals in
\mathcal{F}_{n}
with length
M_{n}^{-1}
and

f_{Q}=\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}f(t)d\mu(t).

For each
n
, we simply write
L_{\infty}(G_{m},\mathcal{F}_{n},\mu)
by
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{F}_{n})
and let

\mathcal{N}_{n}=L_{\infty}(\mathcal{F}_{n})\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{M}.

Then
(\mathcal{N}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
forms an increasing sequence of von Neumann subalgebras of
\mathcal{N}
such that
\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\mathcal{N}_{n}
is weak-
*
dense in
\mathcal{N}.
The conditional expectation of
\mathcal{N}
onto
\mathcal{N}_{n}
is
\mathbb{E}_{n}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{M}}
(simply
\mathbb{E}_{n}
),
where
\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{M}}
is the indentity map from
\mathcal{M}
onto
\mathcal{M}
. Moreover, it is easy to see that the filtration
(\mathcal{N}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is regular with
R_{\rm reg}=\sup_{k}m_{k}
in the sense of (
2.4
).

2.5.
Noncommutative Calderón-Zygmund decomposition

In this paper, our proof mainly depends on the
noncommutative Calderón-Zygmund decomposition established in
[
4
]
. To state the decomposition, we first recall the Cuculescu projections.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
12
]
or
[
53
, Proposition 2.3]
)
.

For positive
f\in L_{1}(\mathcal{M})
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, let
(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be the martingale defined by
f_{n}=\mathcal{E}_{n}(f)
for each
n\geq 1
. Then there exists a sequence of decreasing projections
(q_{n}^{(\lambda)})_{n\geq 1}
in
\mathcal{M}
satisfying the following properties:

(i)

for every
n\geq 1
,
q_{n}^{(\lambda)}\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
;

(ii)

for every
n\geq 1
,
q_{n}^{(\lambda)}
commutes with
q_{n-1}^{(\lambda)}f_{n}q_{n-1}^{(\lambda)}
;

(iii)

for every
n\geq 1
,
q_{n}^{(\lambda)}f_{n}q_{n}^{(\lambda)}\leq\lambda q_{n}^{(\lambda)}
;

(iv)

if we set
q^{(\lambda)}=\wedge_{n=1}^{\infty}q_{n}^{(\lambda)}
, then
q^{(\lambda)}fq^{(\lambda)}\leq\lambda q^{(\lambda)}
and

\lambda\tau({\bf 1}-q^{(\lambda)})\leq\tau\big(({\bf 1}-q^{(\lambda)})f\big)\leq\|f\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{M})}.

In what follows we will simply write
(q_{n})_{n\geq 1}
for the sequence of Cuculescu’s projections
(q_{n}^{(\lambda)})_{n\geq 1}
associated to the martingale generated by a given positive
f\in L_{1}(\mathcal{M})
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, and let
q=q^{(\lambda)}
for convenience. Set
p_{1}=\mathbf{1}-q_{1}
and

(2.8)

p_{n}=q_{n-1}-q_{n},\quad n\geq 2.

Then

(2.9)

\sum_{n\geq 1}p_{n}={\bf 1}-q,

where the convergence is taken with respect to the strong operator topology.

The noncommutative Calderón-Zygmund decomposition was first established in
[
48
]
. The great advantage of the following improved decomposition enables one to circumvent the pseudo-localization technique.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
4
, Lemma 1.1]
)
.

Let
f
be a positive element in
L_{1}(\mathcal{M})
and
\lambda>0
. Suppose that the associated filtration
(\mathcal{M}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is regular with
R_{\rm reg}\geq 1
in the sense of (
2.4
). The sequence of projections
(q_{k})_{k\geq 1}
and
(p_{k})_{k\geq 1}
are as in Lemma
2.3
and (
2.8
). Then there is a decomposition of
f
such that

(2.10)

f=g+b_{d}+b_{off}

where

(i)

g=qfq+\sum_{k\geq 1}p_{k}f_{k}p_{k}
,

\|g\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{M})}\leq\|f\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{M})}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|g\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})}\leq R_{\rm reg}\lambda;

(ii)

b_{d}=\sum_{k\geq 1}p_{k}(f-f_{k})p_{k}
and

\sum_{k\geq 1}\|p_{k}(f-f_{k})p_{k}\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{M})}\leq 2\tau(f({\bf 1}-q))\leq 2\|f\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{M})};

(iii)

b_{off}=\sum_{k\geq 1}p_{k}(f-f_{k})q_{k}+q_{k}(f-f_{k})p_{k}
.

Consider Theorem
2.4
with the filtration
(\mathcal{N},\varphi;(\mathcal{N}_{n})_{n\geq 1})
as in Example
2.2
and positive
f\in L_{1}(\mathcal{N})
. We can then express the projections
q_{n}
and
p_{n}
as

(2.11)

q_{n}=\sum_{Q\in D(\mathcal{F}_{n})}q_{Q}\chi_{Q},

where
q_{Q}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
, and

(2.12)

p_{n}=\sum_{Q\in D(\mathcal{F}_{n-1})}q_{Q}\chi_{Q}-\sum_{Q\in D(\mathcal{F}_{n})}q_{Q}\chi_{Q}=\sum_{Q\in D(\mathcal{F}_{n})}(q_{\widehat{Q}}-q_{Q})\chi_{Q}=\sum_{Q\in D(\mathcal{F}_{n})}p_{Q}\chi_{Q},

where, for
Q\in D(\mathcal{F}_{n})
,
\widehat{Q}
is the unique interval in
D(\mathcal{F}_{n-1})
such that
Q\subseteq\widehat{Q}
.
One can observe that for each
n\geq 1
and
Q\in D(\mathcal{F}_{n})
,

(2.13)

p_{Q}q_{Q}=q_{Q}p_{Q}=0.

Based on (
2.12
), we can rewrite
b_{d}
and
b_{off}
as follows

(2.14)

\displaystyle b_{d}=\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{Q\in D(\mathcal{F}_{k})}p_{Q}(f-f_{Q})p_{Q}\chi_{Q}

and

(2.15)

\displaystyle b_{off}=\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{Q\in D(\mathcal{F}_{k})}p_{Q}(f-f_{Q})q_{Q}\chi_{Q}+q_{Q}(f-f_{Q})p_{Q}\chi_{Q}.

2.6.
Vilenkin-like system and Cesàro means for operator-valued functions

In this subsection, we introduce the Vilenkin-like system on
G_{m}
and refer to
[
22
]
for more details.
Let
M_{0}:=1
, and
M_{n+1}:=m_{n}M_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then each
n\in\mathbb{N}
can be uniquely expressed as

(2.16)

\displaystyle n=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}n_{k}M_{k},\quad 0\leq n_{k}\leq m_{k}-1,k\in\mathbb{N}.

Denote

(2.17)

n^{(k)}=\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}n_{j}M_{j}.

If
n=\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}n_{j}M_{j}
, we call that
M_{k}
is a divisor of
n
.

Assumption 2.5
.

Let complex-valued functions
r_{k}^{n}:G_{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
satisfy the following.

(i)

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
(r_{k}^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are
\mathcal{F}_{k+1}
-measurable, and
r_{k}^{0}=1
.

(ii)

Let
k,l,n\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
n^{(k+1)}=l^{(k+1)}
. If
M_{k}
is a divisor of both
n
and
l
, then

\mathbb{E}_{k}\left(r_{k}^{n}\bar{r}_{k}^{l}\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\hbox{if $n_{k}=l_{k}$}\\
0,&\hbox{if $n_{k}\neq l_{k}$}\end{array}.\right.

Here, the conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}_{k}
is defined as in (
2.7
).

(iii)

If
M_{k+1}
is a divisor of
n
, then

\sum_{j=0}^{m_{k}-1}\left|r_{k}^{jM_{k}+n}(t)\right|^{2}=m_{k},\quad\mbox{for all $t\in G_{m}$}.

(iv)

There exists a constant
\delta>1
for which
\left\|r_{k}^{n}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{m_{k}/\delta}
.

The product system generated by the complex-valued functions
(r_{k}^{n})_{k,n}
is the Vilenkin-like system:
\psi_{0}=1
and

(2.18)

\displaystyle\psi_{n}:=\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}r_{k}^{n^{(k)}},\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

where
n=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}n_{k}M_{k}
.
Since
r_{k}^{0}=1
, for
M_{a}\leq n<M_{a+1}
, we have

\psi_{n}=\prod_{k=0}^{a}r_{k}^{n^{(k)}}
.

Here, we provide concrete examples of Vilenkin-like system, and refer the reader to
[
22
]
for more examples. We omit the elementary computations that the given examples satisfy Assumption
2.5
.

Example 2.6

(Vilenkin and Walsh systems)
.

Consider Vilenkin space
G_{m}
. Moreover, for each
k
, let
G_{m_{k}}:=\mathbb{Z}_{m_{k}}
be the
m_{k}
-th discrete cyclic group. That is
\mathbb{Z}_{m_{k}}
can be represented by the set
\left\{0,1,\ldots,m_{k}-1\right\}
, where the group operation is the
\bmod\ m_{k}
addition and every subset is open. The group operation on
G_{m}
is the coordinate-wise addition. Then
G_{m}
is called a Vilenkin group. Let

r_{k}^{n}(t):=\left(\exp\left(\frac{2\pi it_{k}}{m_{k}}\right)\right)^{n_{k}},\quad t\in G_{m},

where
i:=\sqrt{-1}
. The system
\psi:=\left(\psi_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is the Vilenkin system. If
m_{k}=2
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we get the Walsh(-Paley) group, and the related system is the Walsh(-Paley) system. For more information about Vilenkin and Walsh group are referred to
[
24
,
50
,
57
]
.

Example 2.7

(the group of
m
-adic (2-adic) integers)
.

Let
G_{m_{k}}:=\{0,1,\ldots,m_{k}-1\}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Define on
G_{m}
the following (commutative) addition: if
s,t\in

G_{m}
, then
s+t=v\in G_{m}
is defined recursively as follows:
s_{0}+t_{0}=q_{0}m_{0}+v_{0}
, where
v_{0}\in\left\{0,1,\ldots,m_{0}-1\right\}
and
q_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
. Suppose that
v_{0},\ldots,v_{k}
and
q_{0},\ldots,q_{k}
have been defined. Then write
s_{k+1}+t_{k+1}+q_{k}=q_{k+1}m_{k+1}+v_{k+1}
, where
v_{k+1}\in\left\{0,1,\ldots,m_{k+1}-1\right\}
and
q_{k+1}\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
G_{m}
is called the group of
m
-adic integers (if
m_{k}=2
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, it is the group of 2-adic integers). In this case let

r_{k}^{n}(t):=\left(\exp\left(2\pi\imath\left(\frac{t_{k}}{m_{k}}+\frac{t_{k-1}}{m_{k}m_{k-1}}+\ldots+\frac{t_{0}}{m_{k}m_{k-1}\ldots m_{0}}\right)\right)\right)^{n_{k}}.

Then the system
\psi:=\left(\psi_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is the character system of the group of
m
-adic integers. For more information about the group of
m
-adic integers we refer the reader to
[
27
,
64
]
.

Next, we introduce Cesàro means of Vilenkin-like-Fourier series for operator-valued functions.
Recall that
\mathcal{N}=L_{\infty}(G_{m})\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{M}
, where
\mathcal{M}
is a semifinite von Neumann algebra. The
n
-th Vilenkin-like Fourier coefficient of
f\in L_{1}(\mathcal{N})
is defined by

\widehat{f}(n):=\int_{G_{m}}f(t)\bar{\psi}_{n}(t)d\mu(t),\quad\mbox{$n\in\mathbb{N}$}.

Denote by
S_{n}(f)
the
n
-th partial sum of the Vilenkin-like Fourier series of
f\in L_{1}(\mathcal{N})
, namely,

(2.19)

S_{n}(f)\coloneqq\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{f}(k)\psi_{k},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

We can rewrite the partial sum as the following form

S_{n}(f)(\eta)=\int_{G_{m}}f(t)D_{n}(\eta,t)d\mu(t),\qquad n\in\mathbb{N},\eta\in G_{m},

where
D_{n}
is the Vilenkin-like-Dirichlet kernel defined by

D_{n}(\eta,t):=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\psi_{k}(\eta)\bar{\psi}_{k}(t)

satisfying (see
[
22
, Lemma 3]
)

(2.20)

D_{M_{n}}(\eta,t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}M_{n},&\hbox{if $\eta\in I_{n}(t)$}\\
0,&\hbox{if $\eta\in G_{m}\setminus I_{n}(t)$}\end{array},\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}.\right.

From (

The Cesàro means of

It is straightforward that

\sigma_{n}(f)(\eta)=\int_{G_{m}}f(t)K_{n}(\eta,t)d\mu(t),\quad\eta\in G_{m},\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

where
K_{n}
is the Fejér kernel defined by

K_{n}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}D_{k},\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Denote

K_{a,b}=\sum_{k=a}^{a+b-1}D_{k}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\psi_{n,k}:=\prod_{s=k}^{\infty}r_{s}^{n^{(s)}},

where
a,b,n,k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Two useful lemmas

We conclude this section by two lemmas which will be used in the sequel.

Lemma 2.11
.

For any
a,b\in L_{2}(\mathcal{N})
, there exists
u\in L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
with
\|u\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})}\leq 1
such that

\int a^{*}(t)b(t)dt=\Big(\int a^{*}(t)a(t)dt\Big)^{1/2}\cdot u\cdot\Big(\int b^{*}(t)b(t)dt\Big)^{1/2}.

Proof.

By approximation, we may assume that

A=\int a^{*}(t)a(t)dt,\quad B=\int b^{*}(t)b(t)dt

are both invertible. Set

u=A^{-1/2}\cdot\int a^{*}(t)b(t)dt\cdot B^{-1/2}.

It is obvious that

\int a^{*}(t)b(t)dt=A^{1/2}uB^{1/2}.

It now remains to show
\|u\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})}\leq 1
. Applying
[
46
, Proposition 1.1]
with
r=p=q=\infty
, we get

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\Big(\int A^{-1/2}a^{*}(t)a(t)A^{-1/2}dt\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle\quad\times\Big\|\Big(\int B^{-1/2}b^{*}(t)b(t)B^{-1/2}dt\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle=\|A^{-1/2}AA^{-1/2}\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})}^{1/2}\|B^{-1/2}BB^{-1/2}\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})}^{1/2}=1.

∎

The following lemma is useful to prove Theorem
1.5
. For
2\leq p<\infty
, the right hand side of the below inequality was proved in
[
32
, Section 2]
via another method.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Then, for any
x=(x_{k})_{k\geq 1}\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{2}^{c})
, we have

\|x\|_{\Lambda_{p,\infty}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{\infty}^{c})}\leq\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{\infty}^{c})}\leq\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{2}^{c})}.

The same result holds true for corresponding row spaces.

Proof.

According to the definition of
\|\cdot\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{\infty}^{c})}
, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
A\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
such that for each
k\geq 1
,

x_{k}=v_{k}A,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|A\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}\leq\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{\infty}^{c})}+\varepsilon,

where
\sup_{k\geq 1}\|v_{k}\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})}\leq 1
.
It is immediate that

|x_{k}|^{2}=A^{*}v_{k}^{*}v_{k}A\leq|A|^{2},\quad\forall k\geq 1.

For any
\lambda>0
, take

e=\chi_{(0,\lambda^{2})}(|A|^{2}).

Then, for all
k\geq 1
,

\|x_{k}e\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})}=\|ex_{k}^{*}x_{k}e\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})}^{1/2}\leq\|e|A|^{2}e\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})}^{1/2}\leq\lambda.

On the other hand, by the Chebyshev inequality, we have

\lambda\tau(1-e)^{1/p}\leq\lambda\left(\frac{\||A|^{2}\|_{L_{\frac{p}{2}}(\mathcal{M})}^{p/2}}{\lambda^{p}}\right)^{1/p}=\|A\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}\leq\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{\infty}^{c})}+\varepsilon.

The first inequality follows by letting
\varepsilon
tend to zero.

For the second inequality, we first note that
|x_{k}|^{2}\leq\sum_{k\geq 1}|x_{k}|^{2}
. Let
S=(\sum_{k\geq 1}|x_{k}|^{2})^{1/2}
. Then there are contractions
(u_{k})_{k\geq 1}\subseteq L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
such that

|x_{k}|=u_{k}S,\quad\forall k\geq 1.

From the polar decomposition, we can find contractions
(v_{k})_{k\geq 1}\subset L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
such that

x_{k}=v_{k}u_{k}S,\quad\forall k\geq 1.

Thus, according to the definition of
\|\cdot\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{\infty}^{c})}
,

\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{\infty}^{c})}\leq\|S\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}=\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{2}^{c})}.

∎

3.
Noncommutative Vilenkin system and transference argument

In this section, we introduce the noncommutative Vilenkin system (following
[
16
,
55
]
) and establish the so-called transference argument with respect to this system. Such transference technique allows one to convert situations from operator-valued setting to general noncommutative setting. The transference argument stated in this section maybe well known for the experts but we prefer to write it down and provide the proof in the appendix.

Denote by
\mathbb{M}_{n}
the space of
n\times n
complex valued matrices with the distinguished normalised trace
tr_{n}
, that is,
{\rm tr}_{n}\left(\mathbf{1}_{n}\right)=1
, where
\mathbf{1}_{n}
is the
n\times n
identity matrix. We identify
\mathcal{R}
with the relative infinite tensor product as follows

(\mathcal{R},\tau)=\bigotimes_{k=0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{M}_{m_{k}},{\rm tr}_{m_{k}}).

Note that such
\tau
is a faithful normal trace on
\mathcal{R}
with
\tau(\mathbf{1})=1
, where
\mathbf{1}\coloneqq\otimes_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{2}
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, consider the von Neumann algebra given by

(\mathcal{R}_{n},\tau_{n})=\bigotimes_{k=0}^{n-1}(\mathbb{M}_{m_{k}},{\rm tr}_{m_{k}}).

It is cannonical to view
\mathcal{R}_{n}
as a von Neumann subalgebra of
\mathcal{R}_{n+1}
(resp.
\mathcal{R}
) via the inclusion

x\in\mathcal{R}_{n}\to x\otimes\left(\bigotimes\limits_{k=n}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{m_{k}}\right)\in\mathcal{R}.

It is clear that the union
\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\mathcal{R}_{n}
is clearly weak-
*
dense in
\mathcal{R}
.

Given a sequence
m=(m_{k})_{k\geq 1}
, the Vilenkin group
G_{m}
is defined as the product

G_{m}=\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{Z}_{m_{k}}

endowed with the product topology, where
\mathbb{Z}_{m_{k}}
is the cyclic group of order
m_{k}
. All Vilenkin groups we work with will be of this form. The dual group,
\widehat{G}_{m}
is given by

\widehat{G}_{m}=\coprod_{k=1}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}_{m_{k}},

where
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}_{m_{k}}
is the dual group of, and isomorphic to,
\mathbb{Z}_{m_{k}}
.
The following details the construction of the corresponding Vilenkin system in
\mathcal{R}
. For each integer
k\geq 1
, let
A_{k}
and
B_{k}
denote the matrices given by

A_{k}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{m_{k}}}\exp\left(\frac{2\pi\imath j}{m_{k}}\right)e_{j,j}^{m_{k}},\quad B_{k}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{m_{k}}}e_{j,j+1}^{m_{k}},

where for each
n\geq 1,e_{p,q}^{n}
is the
n\times n
unit matrix defined such that
\left(e_{p,q}^{n}\right)_{p,q}=
1, and all other entries are zero, with entries indexed by
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{2}
.
Within
\mathcal{R}
, the elements of the Vilenkin system are enumerated by doubled sequences in
\widehat{G}_{m}
. We shall identify the group
G_{m}\times G_{m}
(respectively,
\widehat{G}_{m}\times\widehat{G}_{m}
) with the Vilenkin group
G_{2m}
(respectively,
\widehat{G}_{2m}
) where

(3.1)

2m:=\{(2m)_{k}\}_{k\geq 0},

where
(2m)_{2k}=(2m)_{2k+1}=m_{k}
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}.

It follows that any
\eta\in\widehat{G}_{2m}
can be considered as a pair
(\eta^{\prime},\eta^{\prime\prime})
where

\eta^{\prime}:=(\eta_{2k})_{k\geq 0}\quad\mbox{and \quad$\eta^{\prime\prime}:=(\eta_{2k+1})_{k\geq 0}$}

are elements of
\widehat{G}_{m}
. For each
\eta\in\widehat{G}_{2m}
, define

(3.2)

\displaystyle W_{\eta}=\bigotimes_{k=0}^{\infty}A_{k}^{\eta_{2k}}B_{k}^{\eta_{2k+1}}.

The Vilenkin system associated to
G_{m}
in
\mathcal{R}
is then given by the set
\{W_{\eta}\}_{\eta\in\widehat{G}_{2m}}
. For doubled sequences, let
G_{2N}
denote the subgroup of sequences of length
2N
in
G_{2m}
, and
\widehat{G}_{2N}
denote the subgroup of sequences of length
2N
in
\widehat{G}_{2m}
. It is then easily verified that for each
N\geq 1
, the restriction of the Vilenkin system to
\{W_{\eta}\}_{\eta\in\widehat{G}_{2N}}
forms a basis for
\mathcal{R}_{N}
and
L_{2}(\mathcal{R}_{N})
. Since the mapping

k:\eta\mapsto k_{\eta}

is an order isomorphism of
\widehat{G}_{2m}
and
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a element
\eta\in G_{2m}
such that

k_{\eta}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\eta_{k}M_{k}=n,

where
M_{0}=1
,
M_{1}=(2m)_{0}M_{0}
and
M_{k}=(2m)_{k-1}M_{k-1}
for each
k\geq 2
.
Denote
W_{n}:=W_{k^{-1}(n)}
. Then the Vilenkin system
\{W_{n}\}_{0\leq n\leq M_{2N}-1}
also forms an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{R}_{N}
and
L_{2}\left(\mathcal{R}_{N}\right)
.

For

n=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}n_{k}M_{k},\quad m=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}m_{k}M_{k},\qquad n_{k},m_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{(2m)_{k}},

let

(3.3)

m\bigtriangleup n=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(m_{k}\dot{+}n_{k})M_{k}.

where
m_{k}\dot{+}n_{k}=(n_{k}+m_{k})\mod(2m)_{k}
.
For
t,s\in G_{2m}
, the addition is defined in the following way:

t\dot{+}s:=\{t_{k}\dot{+}s_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}.

Recall that (see e.g.
[
67
, p. 114]
)

(3.4)

\psi_{m}(t\dot{+}s)=\psi_{m}(s)\psi_{m}(t),\qquad\psi_{m}\psi_{n}=\psi_{m\bigtriangleup n}.

The following lemma is taken from
[
55
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist complex numbers
u_{n}
and
\omega_{m,n}
such that
W_{n}^{*}=u_{n}W_{-n}
and
W_{m}W_{n}=\omega_{m,n}W_{m\bigtriangleup n}
, where
|u_{n}|=1
,
|\omega_{m,n}|=1
and
m\bigtriangleup n
is referred to (
3.3
).

The noncommutative Vilenkin-Fourier coefficient of
x\in L_{1}(\mathcal{R})
is defined by
\widehat{x}(0)=\tau(x)
and

\widehat{x}(k)=\tau(x\cdot W_{k}^{*}),\quad k\in\mathbb{N}.

Analogous to (
2.19
) and (
2.22
), for
x\in L_{1}(\mathcal{R})
, we define the partial sum and Cesàro means respectively as follows

S^{\mathcal{R}}_{n}(x)\coloneqq\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{x}(k)W_{k},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}

and

(3.5)

\sigma^{\mathcal{R}}_{n}(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}S^{\mathcal{R}}_{k}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(1-\frac{k}{n}\right)\widehat{x}(k)W_{k},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Let
(W_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}
stand for the noncommutative Vilenkin system from now on.
A polynomial in
\mathcal{R}
is a finite sum

x=\sum_{k\geq 0}\alpha_{k}W_{k},\quad\alpha_{k}\in\mathbb{C},

that is
\alpha_{k}=0
for all but finite indices
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\mathrm{Poly}(\mathcal{R})
be the
*
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{R}
consisting with all (complex) polynomials in
\mathcal{R}
. For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathcal{E}_{k}:\mathcal{R}\to\mathcal{R}_{k}
be the conditional expectation. For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we then have

(3.6)

\mathcal{E}_{k}(x)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{M_{2k}-1}\widehat{x}(j)W_{j}=S^{\mathcal{R}}_{M_{2k}}(x),\quad x\in\mathrm{Poly}(\mathcal{R}).

In the sequel of the paper, equip the von Neumann algebra
L_{\infty}(G_{2m})\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{R}
the trace
\int\otimes\tau
which is still denoted by
\varphi
. Define the map
\gamma:\mathrm{Poly}(\mathcal{R})\to L_{\infty}(G_{2m})\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{R}
by setting

(3.7)

\gamma(x)=\sum_{j\geq 0}\widehat{x}(j)\psi_{j}\otimes W_{j},\quad x\in\mathrm{Poly}(\mathcal{R}),

where
(\psi_{j})_{j\geq 0}
are the classical Vilenkin functions as in Example
2.6
with respect to the Vilenkin group
G_{2m}
.

The results below should be known. We also provide detail proofs in the appendix.

Proposition 3.2
.

The mapping
\gamma:\mathrm{Poly}(\mathcal{R})\to L_{\infty}(G_{2m})\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{R}
satisfies the following properties:

(i)

\gamma
is a
*
-homomorphism;

(ii)

\gamma
is trace preserving, that is,
\varphi(\gamma(x))=\tau(x)
, for all
x\in\mathrm{Poly}(\mathcal{R})
;

(iii)

\gamma
is injective, and both
\gamma
and
\gamma^{-1}
are normal.

Since
\mathrm{Poly}(\mathcal{R})
is weak-
\ast
dense in
\mathcal{R}
and
\gamma:\mathrm{Poly}(\mathcal{R})\to L_{\infty}(G_{2m})\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{R}
is normal, we could now extend
\gamma
in a natural way making
\gamma
a
*
-homomorphism from
\mathcal{R}
into
L_{\infty}(G_{2m})\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{R}
. For simplicity, we still use the notation
\gamma
to stand for its natural extension if no confusion arises.

Proposition 3.3
.

The mapping
\gamma:\mathcal{R}\to L_{\infty}(G_{2m})\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{R}
is a
*
-homomorphism such that
\gamma(\mathcal{R})
is weak-
\ast
closed in
L_{\infty}(G_{2m})\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{R}
. In particular,
\gamma(\mathcal{R})
is a von Neumann subalgebra of
L_{\infty}(G_{2m})\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{R}
.

With Proposition
3.3
at hand, we obtain the following transference results.

Theorem 3.4
.

For any
0<p\leq\infty
and
x\in\mathcal{R}
, we have

\|\gamma(x)\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(G_{2m})\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{R})}=\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{R})}.

Furthermore, for any
x\in\mathcal{R},
one has the following stronger result

(3.8)

\mu(t,\gamma(x))=\mu(t,x),\quad\forall t>0.

\displaystyle\int_{I_{a}(s)\backslash I_{a+1}(s)}\widetilde{K}_{n}(t,s)d\mu(t)

Denote

and let

e_{2}=\chi_{(0,\lambda)}(\mathbb{B}_{off})\wedge q.

From the linearity of
\widetilde{\sigma}_{n}
, Lemma
4.8
and (
2.15
), it follows that

(4.13)

-q\mathbb{B}_{off}q\leq q\widetilde{\sigma}_{n}(b_{off})q=\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{Q\in D(\mathcal{F}_{k})}q\widetilde{\sigma}_{n}(b_{off}^{k,Q})q\leq q\mathbb{B}_{off}q,\quad\forall n\geq 1.

Consequently, for each
n\geq 1
,

-\lambda\leq-e_{2}\mathbb{B}_{off}e_{2}\leq e_{2}q\widetilde{\sigma}_{n}(b_{off})qe_{2}\leq e_{2}\mathbb{B}_{off}e_{2}\leq\lambda,

By the Chebyshev inequality and Lemma
4.9
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda\varphi(\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}(\mathbb{B}_{off}))

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbb{B}_{off}\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{Q\in D(\mathcal{F}_{k})}\|A_{k,Q}\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{Q\in D(\mathcal{F}_{k})}\lambda^{1/2}\varphi(p_{Q}\chi_{Q})^{1/2}\cdot\varphi(p_{Q}fp_{Q}\chi_{Q})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq c\Big(\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{Q\in D(\mathcal{F}_{k})}\lambda\varphi(p_{Q}\chi_{Q})\Big)^{1/2}\cdot\Big(\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{Q\in D(\mathcal{F}_{k})}\varphi(p_{Q}fp_{Q}\chi_{Q})\Big)^{1/2},

where we used Hölder’s inequality in the last inequality.
Applying Lemma
2.3
(iv),

\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{Q\in D(\mathcal{F}_{k})}\lambda\varphi(p_{Q}\chi_{Q})=\lambda\sum_{k\geq 1}\varphi(p_{k})=\lambda\varphi({\bf 1}-q)\leq\varphi(({\bf 1}-q)f)\leq\|f\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{N})}.

In addition,

\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{Q\in D(\mathcal{F}_{k})}\varphi(p_{Q}fp_{Q}\chi_{Q})=\sum_{k\geq 1}\varphi(fp_{k})=\varphi(({\bf 1}-q)f)\leq\|f\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{N})}.

Therefore, by the above inequalities and Lemma
2.3
(iv),

\lambda\varphi({\bf 1}-e_{2})\leq\lambda\varphi(\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}(\mathbb{B}_{off}))+\varphi(1-q)\leq c\|f\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{N})},

which completes our proof of Proposition
4.3
.
∎

With Propositions
4.2
and
4.3
at hand, we now provide a proof of Theorem
4.1
in detail.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

It follows from Lemma
4.6
that

\|(\widetilde{\sigma}_{n}(f))_{n\geq 1}\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{N},\ell_{\infty})}=\sup_{n}\|\widetilde{\sigma}_{n}(f)\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{N})}\leq c\|f\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{N})}.

Note that
\widetilde{\sigma}_{n}
is positive for each
n\geq 1
. Then, once item (i) is proved, we can apply the noncommutative Marcinkiewicz interpolation in
[
40
, Theorem 3.1]
to get item (ii).

For item (i), without loss of generality, we assume that
f\geq 0
. According to the definition of
\|\cdot\|_{\Lambda_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{N},\ell_{\infty})}
, it suffices to find a projection
e\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{N})
such that, for each fixed
\lambda>0
,

\sup_{n}\|e\widetilde{\sigma}_{n}(f)e\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{N})}\leq c\lambda

and

(4.14)

\lambda\cdot\varphi({\bf 1}-e)\leq c\|f\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{N})}.

For the fixed
\lambda>0
, applying Theorem
2.4
, we have

f=g+b_{d}+b_{off}.

Set

e=e_{1}\wedge e_{2},

where
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
are from Proposition
4.2
and Proposition
4.3
, respectively. Then, combining Proposition
4.2
, Proposition
4.3
and Theorem
2.4
(i), we can obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{n\geq 1}

\displaystyle\|e\widetilde{\sigma}_{n}(f)e\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{n\geq 1}\|e\widetilde{\sigma}_{n}(g)e\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{N})}+\sup_{n\geq 1}\|e\widetilde{\sigma}_{n}(b_{d})e\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{N})}+\sup_{n\geq 1}\|e\widetilde{\sigma}_{n}(b_{off})e\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{N})}\leq c\lambda

and

\lambda\varphi({\bf 1}-e)\leq c\|f\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{N})}.

The proof is complete.
∎

4.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

This subsection aims to prove Theorem
1.3
.
Recall that
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}\coloneqq\gamma(\mathcal{R})
, where
\gamma
is as in (
3.7
). The next lemma is motivated by
[
9
]
.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Let
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{B}
be two von Neumann algebras. Assume that there exists a conditional expectation
\mathcal{E}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{A}
. Suppose that
x=(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of self-adjoint elements in
L_{p,\infty}(\mathcal{A})
, and
f=(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of positive elements in
L_{p,\infty}(\mathcal{B})
such that
-f_{n}\leq x_{n}\leq f_{n}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\|x\|_{\Lambda_{p,\infty}(\mathcal{A},\ell_{\infty})}\leq 2^{2+\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{\Lambda_{p,\infty}(\mathcal{B},\ell_{\infty})}.

Proof.

For arbitrary
\lambda>0
, take a projection
e\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{B})
such that

\sup_{n\geq 1}\|ef_{n}e\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathcal{B}})}\leq\lambda.

By the contraction of conditional expectation
\mathcal{E}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{A}
, we get that, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{E}(e)f_{n}^{1/2}\right\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathcal{B}})}

\displaystyle=\left\|\mathcal{E}(ef_{n}^{1/2})\right\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathcal{B}})}

\displaystyle\leq\|ef_{n}^{1/2}\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathcal{B}})}=\|ef_{n}e\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathcal{B}})}^{1/2}\leq\lambda^{1/2}.

Let
a=\mathcal{E}(e)
, and let
\xi=\chi_{[1/2,1]}(a)
which is a projection in
\mathcal{A}
. The functional calculus gives us
\mathbf{1}-\xi=\chi_{[1/2,1]}(\mathbf{1}-a)
. Hence,
\mathbf{1}-\xi\leq 2(\mathbf{1}-a)
, and

(4.15)

\varphi(\mathbf{1}-\xi)\leq 2\varphi(\mathbf{1}-a)=2\varphi(\mathbf{1}-e).

Moreover, taking
g(t)=\frac{1}{t}\chi_{[1/2,1]}(t)
, we have
\xi=\xi g(a)a
. Note that
\|\xi g(a)\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{B})}\leq 2
. For any
n\geq 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\xi x_{n}\xi\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathcal{A}})}\leq\|\xi f_{n}\xi\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathcal{B}})}=\|\xi g(a)af_{n}^{1/2}\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathcal{B}})}^{2}\leq 4\|\mathcal{E}(e)f_{n}^{1/2}\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathcal{B}})}^{2}\leq 4\lambda,

which, together with (
4.15
) and the definition of quasi-norm
\|\cdot\|_{\Lambda_{p,\infty}}
, implies the desired inequality.
∎

The next lemma is basic, however, we still include its detail proof for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. For any
x=(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\Lambda_{p,\infty}(\mathcal{R},\ell_{\infty})
, we have

\|\gamma(x)\|_{\Lambda_{p,\infty}(\widehat{\mathcal{R}},\ell_{\infty})}=\|x\|_{\Lambda_{p,\infty}(\mathcal{R},\ell_{\infty})}.

Proof.

For every
\lambda>0
, take a projection
e\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{R})
such that

\sup_{n\geq 1}\|ex_{n}e\|_{\mathcal{R}}\leq\lambda.

Note that
\gamma
is a
*
-homomorphism (see Proposition
3.3
). Then

\gamma(e)^{*}=\gamma(e),\quad\gamma(e)^{2}=\gamma(e^{2})=\gamma(e),

which implies that
\gamma(e)
is also a projection in
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}=\gamma(\mathcal{R})
.
Moreover, it is easy to verify via Theorem
3.4
that, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(4.16)

\left\|\gamma(e)\gamma(x_{n})\gamma(e)\right\|_{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}=\left\|\gamma\left(ex_{n}e\right)\right\|_{L_{\infty}(G_{2m})\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{R}}=\|ex_{n}e\|_{\mathcal{R}}\leq\lambda.

Since
\gamma
is trace preserving, it follows that

\|\gamma(x)\|_{\Lambda_{p,\infty}(L_{\infty}(\widehat{\mathcal{R}},\ell_{\infty})}\leq\|x\|_{\Lambda_{p,\infty}(\mathcal{R},\ell_{\infty})}.

Conversely, take a projection
p\in\widehat{\mathcal{R}}
such that

\sup_{n\geq 1}\|p\gamma(x_{n})p\|_{\mathcal{R}}\leq\lambda.

Then, there exists
e\in\mathcal{R}
such that
p=\gamma(e)
. Thus, the same to (
4.16
), we have
\sup_{n}\|ex_{n}e\|_{\mathcal{R}}\leq\lambda
.
Again, since
\gamma
is trace preserving, it follows that

\|x\|_{\Lambda_{p,\infty}(\mathcal{R},\ell_{\infty})}\leq\|\gamma(x)\|_{\Lambda_{p,\infty}(L_{\infty}(\widehat{\mathcal{R}},\ell_{\infty})}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.3
in detail.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
x\in L_{1}(\mathcal{R})
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
x
is positive. Since
\gamma
is a
*
-homomorphism (see Proposition
3.3
),
\gamma(x)\geq 0
. Note that

\displaystyle\gamma(\sigma^{\mathcal{R}}_{n}(x))(t)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{n}(\gamma(x))(t),\quad t\in G_{2m},

where
\sigma_{n}
is as in (
2.23
).
Then, by Lemma
4.11
, we have

(4.17)

\displaystyle\left\|(\sigma^{\mathcal{R}}_{n}(x))_{n\geq 1}\right\|_{\Lambda_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{R},\ell_{\infty})}=\left\|(\gamma[\sigma^{\mathcal{R}}_{n}(x)])_{n\geq 1}\right\|_{\Lambda_{1,\infty}(\widehat{\mathcal{R}},\ell_{\infty})}=\left\|(\sigma_{n}(\gamma(x)))_{n\geq 1}\right\|_{\Lambda_{1,\infty}(\widehat{\mathcal{R}},\ell_{\infty})}.

Combining (
4.2
),
Lemma
4.10
, Theorem
4.1
and Theorem
3.4
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\|(\sigma_{n}(\gamma(x)))_{n\geq 1}\|_{\Lambda_{1,\infty}(\widehat{\mathcal{R}},\ell_{\infty})}

\displaystyle\leq 4\|(\widetilde{\sigma}_{n}(\gamma(x))_{n\geq 1}\|_{\Lambda_{1,\infty}(\widehat{\mathcal{R}},\ell_{\infty})}

\displaystyle\leq c\|(\widetilde{\sigma}_{n}(\gamma(x))_{n\geq 1}\|_{\Lambda_{1,\infty}(L_{\infty}(G_{2m})\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{R},\ell_{\infty})}

\displaystyle\leq c\|\gamma(x)\|_{L_{1}(L_{\infty}(G_{2m})\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{R})}

\displaystyle=c\|x\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{R})},

where
\widetilde{\sigma}_{n}
is as in (
4.1
). Then, combining (
4.17
), we get (i) of Theorem
1.3
.

Similarly, we can show the strong type
(p,p)
inequality, and the proof is complete.
∎

5.
Proofs of Theorems
1.2
and
1.5

In Section
5.1
, we introduce the noncommutative Hardy space and state its atomic decomposition. In Section
5.2
, we study a generalization of noncommutative Sunouchi operator, and then use it to prove Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.5
in Section

.

5.1.
Noncommutative martingale Hardy spaces and atomic decomposition

Consider a noncommutative martingale
f=(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
with respect to the regular filtration
(\mathcal{N}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
as in Example
2.2
.
The related column square functions are defined by

S_{c,n}(f)=\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{n}|df_{k}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2},\quad S_{c}(f)=\Big(\sum_{k\geq 1}|df_{k}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

The row square functions
S_{r,n}(f)
and
S_{r}(f)
can be defined by taking adjoint.
For
1\leq p\leq 2
, define the noncommutative martingale spaces as follows

H_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{N})=\{f\in L_{p}(\mathcal{N}):\|f\|_{H_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{N})}=\|S_{c}(f)\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N})}<\infty\}

and

H_{p}^{r}(\mathcal{N})=\{f\in L_{p}(\mathcal{N}):\|f\|_{H_{p}^{r}(\mathcal{N})}=\|S_{r}(f)\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N})}<\infty\}.

Definition 5.1
.

An element
a\in L_{2}(\mathcal{N})
is said to be a
(1,2)_{c}
-simple atom with respect to the filtration
(\mathcal{N}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
if there exist
k\geq 1
,
Q\in D(\mathcal{F}_{k})
, and
e_{Q}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
such that

(i)

a=ae
with
e=e_{Q}\chi_{Q}
;

(ii)

\mathbb{E}_{k}(a)=0
;

(iii)

\|a\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{N})}\leq\varphi(e)^{-1/2}=\tau(e_{Q})^{-1/2}|Q|^{-1/2}
.

Simple row atoms are defined to satisfy
a=ea
instead.

Remark 5.2
.

Assume that
a
is a
(1,2)_{c}
-simple atom associated with some projection
e=e_{Q}\chi_{Q}\in\mathcal{N}_{k}
with
Q\in D(\mathcal{F}_{k})
,
k\geq 1
.

(i)

From the above definition, we have the following fact

(5.1)

\|a\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{N})}=\|ae\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{N})}\leq\|a\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{N})}\|e\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{N})}\leq 1.

(ii)

Every
(1,2)_{c}
-simple atom is an
\mathcal{M}_{c}
-atom introduced by Mei in
[
46
, p. 26]
. Indeed, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\tau\Big[\Big(\int_{Q}|a|^{2}dt\Big)^{1/2}\Big]\leq[\tau(e_{Q})]^{1/2}\Big[\tau\Big(\int_{Q}|a|^{2}dt\Big)\Big]^{1/2}\leq|Q|^{-1/2}.

(iii)

Every
(1,2)_{c}
-simple atom is also a
(1,2)_{c}
-atom in the sense of
[
2
, Definition 2.1]
.

Definition 5.3
.

Define
H_{1}^{c,\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal{N})
as the Banach space of all
f\in L_{1}(\mathcal{N})
which admits a decomposition

f=\sum\limits_{k\geq 1}\lambda_{k}a_{k}

with, for each
k\geq 1
,
a_{k}
is a
(1,2)_{c}
-simple atom or an element in
L_{1}(\mathcal{N}_{1})
of norm
\leq 1
, and
\sum_{k\geq 1}|\lambda_{k}|<\infty
. We equip this space with the norm

\|f\|_{H^{c,\mathrm{sat}}_{1}}\coloneqq\inf\sum\limits_{k\geq 1}|\lambda_{k}|,

where the infimum is taken over all decomposition of
f
described as above. The row space
H_{1}^{r,\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal{N})
can be defined by a similar way.

Atomic decomposition for noncommutative martingale Hardy spaces has been studied extensively by many authors; see for instance
[
2
,
8
,
31
]
. Since the filtration
(\mathcal{N}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is regular (see Example
2.2
), it follows that
H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{N})
is just the conditioned Hardy space
\mathrm{h}_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{N})
(see
[
8
]
). Consequently,
according to
[
2
, Theorem 2.4]
,
H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{N})
admits atomic decomposition for the so-called
(1,2)_{c}
-atoms. According to Remark
5.2
(iii), the following inclusion holds

H_{1}^{c,\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal{N})\subseteq H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{N}).

On the other hand, the duality argument used in
[
2
, Theorem 2.4]
(or
[
31
]
) works well for the reverse inclusion
H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{N})\subseteq H_{1}^{c,\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal{N})
. Indeed, we can show

(H_{1}^{c,\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal{N}))^{*}=(H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{N}))^{*}=BMO_{c}(\mathcal{N}),

where
BMO_{c}(\mathcal{N})
denotes the noncommutative martingale column bounded mean oscillation space (see e.g.
[
52
]
). We conclude the above argument as follows.

Theorem 5.4
.

We have

H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{N})=H_{1}^{c,\mathrm{sat}}(\mathcal{N})

with equivalent norms. The same holds for row spaces.

5.2.
Noncommutative Sunouchi operator

The following theorem is the main result in this subsection.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
(n_{k})_{k\geq 1}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
be such that
M_{k-1}\leq n_{k}<M_{k}
for each
k\geq 1
, and let
T_{k}=\sigma_{n_{k}}-\mathbb{E}_{k}
. For each
1\leq p\leq 2
, there exists a constant
c_{p}>0
depending only on
p
such that

\|(T_{k}(f))_{k\geq 1}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N},\ell_{2}^{c})}\leq c_{p}\|f\|_{H_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{N})}.

The same result holds true for row spaces.

Remark 5.6
.

As mentioned in the introduction, there are rich results related to the Sunouchi operator in the commutative setting; see e.g.
[
62
,
21
,
58
,
68
]
.
The noncommutative Sunouchi operator deserves further research.

To prove Theorem
5.5
, we need the following sequence of lemmas.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
(T_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be operators as in Theorem
5.5
. Then, for every
f\in L_{2}(\mathcal{N})
, there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\|(T_{k}f)_{k\geq 1}\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{N},\ell_{2}^{c})}\leq c\|f\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{N})}.

Proof.

According to the Parseval identity,

\|f\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{N})}^{2}=\sum_{j\geq 0}\|\widehat{f}(j)\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2},\quad\forall f\in L_{2}(\mathcal{N}),

we have

\displaystyle\|(T_{k}f)_{k\geq 1}\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{N},\ell_{2}^{c})}^{2}=\sum_{k\geq 1}\|T_{k}(f)\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{N})}^{2}=\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{j\geq 0}\tau(|\widehat{T_{k}(f)}(j)|^{2}).

Note that

\displaystyle\widehat{T_{k}(f)}(j)

\displaystyle=\widehat{K_{n_{k}}}(j)\widehat{f}(j)-\widehat{D_{M_{k}}}(j)\widehat{f}(j)=(\widehat{K_{n_{k}}}(j)-\widehat{D_{M_{k}}}(j))\cdot\widehat{f}(j).

Then

\|(T_{k}f)_{k\geq 1}\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{N},\ell_{2}^{c})}^{2}=\sum_{j\geq 0}\sum_{k\geq 1}|\widehat{K_{n_{k}}}(j)-\widehat{D_{M_{k}}}(j)|^{2}\cdot\tau(|\widehat{f}(j)|^{2}).

By the Parseval identity, it suffices to show

(5.2)

\sum_{k\geq 1}|\widehat{K_{n_{k}}}(j)-\widehat{D_{M_{k}}}(j)|^{2}\leq c,\quad\forall j\geq 0.

Observe that
\widehat{D_{M_{k}}}(j)=1
if
j\leq M_{k}-1
and
\widehat{D_{M_{k}}}(j)=0
if
j\geq M_{k}
;
\widehat{K_{n_{k}}}(j)=(n_{k}-j)/n_{k}
if
j\leq n_{k}
and
\widehat{K_{n_{k}}}(j)=0
if
j\geq n_{k}
. Then (
5.2
) is clear for
j=0
. On the other hand, for each fixed
j\geq 1
, there exists an unique integer
l\in\mathbb{N}
such that

M_{l}\leq j<M_{l+1}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1}|\widehat{K_{n_{k}}}(j)-\widehat{D_{M_{k}}}(j)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq l+1}|\widehat{K_{n_{k}}}(j)-\widehat{D_{M_{k}}}(j)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq l+1}|\frac{j}{n_{k}}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq l+1}\big(\frac{M_{l+1}}{M_{k-1}}\big)^{2}\leq c.

The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
(T_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be as in Theorem
5.5
, and suppose that
a
is a
(1,2)_{c}
-simple atom associated with some
n_{0}\geq 1
. Then
T_{k}(a)=0
for each
k\leq n_{0}
.

Proof.

For
k\leq n_{0}
, the fact that
a
is a
(1,2)_{c}
-simple atom with respect to
n_{0}
implies that
\mathbb{E}_{k}(a)=0
. Recall that
n_{k}<M_{k}
for each
k
. Hence, for
k\leq n_{0}
and any
l\leq n_{k}
, we have

S_{l}(a)=S_{l}(S_{M_{n_{0}}}(a))=S_{l}(\mathbb{E}_{n_{0}}(a))=0,

which implies
\sigma_{n_{k}}(a)=0
.
By the definition of
T_{k}
, we have
T_{k}(a)=0
provided
k\leq n_{0}
.
∎

The following is the key lemma in establishing Theorem
5.5
.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
a
be a
(1,2)_{c}
-simple atom associated with some
n_{0}\geq 1
and projection
e=e_{Q}\chi_{Q}
with
Q\in D(\mathcal{F}_{n_{0}})
. If
k>n_{0}
and
M_{k-1}\leq n_{k}<M_{k}
, then

\|\sigma_{n_{k}}(a)\chi_{Q^{c}}\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{N})}\leq c\Big[\delta^{n_{0}-k}+(k-n_{0})\delta^{\frac{n_{0}-k}{2}}\Big],

where
\delta
is from Assumption
2.5
(iv).

Proof.

Similar to the argument before Theorem
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\|\sigma_{n_{k}}(a)\chi_{Q^{c}}\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{N})}\leq\|\widetilde{\sigma}_{k-1}(|a|)\chi_{Q^{c}}\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{N})},

where
\widetilde{\sigma}_{k-1}
is defined as (
4.1

Hence

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\sigma}_{k-1}(|a|)\chi_{Q^{c}}\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle=\int_{G_{m}\setminus Q}\|\int_{Q}|a|(s)\widetilde{K}_{k-1}(t,s)d\mu(s)\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{M})}d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{G_{m}\setminus Q}\|A^{1/2}u(t)\big(\int_{Q}\widetilde{K}_{k-1}^{2}(t,s)d\mu(s)\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{M})}d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{G_{m}\setminus Q}\|A^{1/2}u(t)\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{M})}\big(\int_{Q}\widetilde{K}_{k-1}^{2}(t,s)d\mu(s)\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}d\mu(t),

where we used Lemma
2.11
with
\sup_{t}\|u(t)\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})}\leq 1

A=\int_{Q}|a(s)|^{2}ds.

Since
a
is a
(1,2)_{c}
-simple atom and

, it follows from Remark

(ii) that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\sigma}_{k-1}(|a|)\chi_{Q^{c}}\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle\leq|Q|^{-1/2}\int_{G_{m}\setminus Q}\big(\int_{Q}\widetilde{K}_{k-1}^{2}(t,s)d\mu(s)\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=|Q|^{-1/2}\sum_{j=0}^{n_{0}-1}\int_{I_{j}(s)\setminus I_{j+1}(s)}\big(\int_{Q}\widetilde{K}_{k-1}^{2}(t,s)d\mu(s)\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}d\mu(t)

\displaystyle\leq|Q|^{-1/2}\sum_{j=0}^{n_{0}-1}|I_{j}(s)\setminus I_{j+1}(s)|^{1/2}\Big(\int_{I_{j}(s)\setminus I_{j+1}(s)}\int_{Q}\widetilde{K}_{k-1}^{2}(t,s)d\mu(s)d\mu(t)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then by the Fubini theorem,
k-1\geq n_{0}>j
and Lemma

, we get

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\sigma}_{k-1}(|a|)\chi_{Q^{c}}\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle\leq|Q|^{-1/2}\sum_{j=0}^{n_{0}-1}M_{j}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{Q}\int_{I_{j}(s)\setminus I_{j+1}(s)}\widetilde{K}_{k-1}^{2}(t,s)d\mu(t)d\mu(s)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c|Q|^{-1/2}\sum_{j=0}^{n_{0}-1}M_{j}^{-\frac{1}{2}}|Q|^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(M_{j}^{\frac{1}{2}}\delta^{j-(k-1)}+(k-1-j)\delta^{\frac{j-(k-1)}{2}}M_{j}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{j=0}^{n_{0}-1}\big(\delta^{j-(k-1)}+(k-1-j)\delta^{\frac{j-(k-1)}{2}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq c\Big[\delta^{n_{0}-k}+(k-n_{0})\delta^{\frac{n_{0}-k}{2}}\Big],

which completes the proof of the lemma.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
(T_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be as in Theorem
5.5
, and let

be a
(1,2)_{c}
-simple atom. Then

\|(T_{k}a)_{k\geq 1}\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{N},\ell_{2}^{c})}\leq c.

The same result holds for
\|\cdot\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{N},\ell_{2}^{r})}
and row atoms.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
a
is a
(1,2)_{c}
-simple atom associated with

and projection

,
Q\in D(\mathcal{F}_{n_{0}})
. Note that

is a norm.
Then

\displaystyle\|(T_{k}a)_{k\geq 1}\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{N},\ell_{2}^{c})}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\sum_{k\geq 1}T_{k}(a)\otimes e_{k,1}\Big\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{N}\bar{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}))}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\sum_{k\geq 1}T_{k}(a)e\otimes e_{k,1}\Big\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{N}\bar{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}))}+\Big\|\sum_{k\geq 1}T_{k}(a)(1-e)\otimes e_{k,1}\Big\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{N}\bar{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}))}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathrm{I}+\mathrm{II},

where
e_{k,1}
stands for the column matrix taking value
1
at the
(k,1)
-position and all others are
0
. The term
\mathrm{I}
is estimated as follows:

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\sum_{k\geq 1}e|T_{k}(a)|^{2}e\Big\|_{L_{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathcal{N})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq c\|e\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{N})}^{1/2}\|a\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{N})}\leq c,

where we used Lemma
5.7
.

Observe that, if

On the other hand, it is easy see that, for each

Consequently, using Lemma

, we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathrm{II}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k>n_{0}}\|\sigma_{n_{k}}(a)\chi_{Q^{c}}\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{k>n_{0}}c\Big[\delta^{n_{0}-k}+(k-n_{0})\delta^{\frac{n_{0}-k}{2}}\Big]\leq c.

The desired result follows from the estimates of
\mathrm{I}
and

Appendix A
Almost uniform convergence

In this subsection, we recall definitions of almost uniform convergence in semifinite von Neumann algebras. The following definition, which goes back at least Lance
[
43
]
or
[
35
]
, is taken from
[
10
]
and
[
13
, p. 109]
.

Definition A.1
.

Consider
(x_{k})_{k\geq 1}\subseteq L_{0}(\mathcal{M})
and
x\in L_{0}(\mathcal{M})
.

(i)

We say
(x_{k})_{k\geq 1}
converges to
x
column almost uniformly (c.a.u. in short) if for any
\varepsilon>0
, there is a projection
e\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
such that

\tau({\bf 1}-e)<\varepsilon,\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}\|(x_{k}-x)e\|_{\mathcal{M}}=0.

(ii)

We say
(x_{k})_{k\geq 1}
converges to
x
row almost uniformly (r.a.u. in short) if
(x_{k}^{*})_{k\geq 1}
converges to
x^{*}
column almost uniformly.

(iii)

We say
(x_{k})_{k\geq 1}
converges to
x
column
+
row almost uniformly (
c+r
a.u.) provided the sequence
(x_{k}-x)_{k\geq 1}
decomposes into a sum
(a_{k})_{k\geq 1}+(b_{k})_{k\geq 1}
of two sequences such that
(a_{k})_{k\geq 1}
converges to
0
column almost uniformly and
(b_{k})_{k\geq 1}
converges to
0
row almost uniformly.

(iv)

We say
(x_{k})_{k\geq 1}
converges to
x
bilaterally almost uniformly (b.a.u. in short) if for any
\varepsilon>0
, there is a projection
e\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
such that

\tau({\bf 1}-e)<\varepsilon,\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}\|e(x_{k}-x)e\|_{\mathcal{M}}=0.

The column almost uniform convergence is the usual almost uniform convergence used in
[
9
]
,
[
40
]
,
[
46
]
and more; hence we sometimes still use the same symbol a.u. for short. It was shown in
[
13
, Chapter 3.1.8]
that only the trivial implications hold:

\mbox{a.u. or r.a.u.}\Rightarrow c+r\mbox{ a.u.}\Rightarrow\mbox{b.a.u.}

The above definition generalizes the notion of almost everywhere convergence in the case of finite
measure spaces. Actually, for finite abelian von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
(i.e.,
\tau(\mathbf{1})<\infty
), the almost uniform convergences in the definition above are all equivalent
to the usual almost everywhere convergence by virtue of Egorov’s theorem.

f
could be approximated by a sequence of polynomials
(p_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
in the compact-open topology. Since the spectral of
x
is compact, we get
p_{n}(x)\to f(x)
and
\gamma(p_{n}(x))\to\gamma(f(x))
in
\mathcal{R}
.

On the other hand, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\gamma(p_{n}(x))=p_{n}(\gamma(x))
. Then, applying the fact that
p_{n}\to f
in the compact-open topology and the compactness of the spectral of
\gamma(x)
, we obtain

f(\gamma(x))=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}p_{n}(\gamma(x))=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\gamma(p_{n}(x))=\gamma(f(x)),

where limits are taken with respect to the norm in
\mathcal{R}
. Furthermore, notice that
\gamma:\mathcal{R}\to\widehat{\mathcal{R}}
is a
*
-isomorphism, for arbitrary
x\in\mathcal{R}
, we have

(B.2)

\gamma(|x|^{2})=\gamma(x^{*}x)=\gamma(x^{*})\gamma(x)=\gamma(x)^{*}\gamma(x)=|\gamma(x)|^{2}.

Since the the mapping
t\to t^{p/2}
is continuous, by (
B.2
), we get

|\gamma(x)|^{p}=\gamma(|x|^{p}),\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{R}.

Then, it follows from Proposition
3.2
(ii) that

\|\gamma(x)\|^{p}_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(G_{2m})\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{R})}=\|x\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{R})}.

To prove (
3.8
), it suffices to show for each
\lambda>0
and
x\in\mathcal{R}
,

\varphi\left(\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}(\gamma(x))\right)=\tau\left(\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}(x)\right).

Note that for arbitrary
\lambda>0
, the bounded measurable function
\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}
could be approximated by a sequence of bounded continuous functions
(f_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
pointwise. Hence,
f_{n}(x)\to\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}(x)
in weak-
\ast
topology. Indeed, for every
u\in L_{1}(\mathcal{R})
, by the spectral theorem (or, functional calculus), we have

\displaystyle\tau\left(u\cdot f_{n}(\gamma(x))\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{spec(\gamma(x))}f_{n}(t)~\langle u,\Pi_{\gamma(x)}(dt)\mathbf{1}\rangle_{L_{2}(\mathcal{R})}

\displaystyle\to\int_{spec(\gamma(x))}\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}(t)~\langle u,\Pi_{\gamma(x)}(dt)\rangle_{L_{2}(\mathcal{R})}

\displaystyle=\tau(u\cdot\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}(\gamma(x)))\quad(n\to\infty),

where
\Pi_{\gamma(x)}
is the associated spectral measure of
\gamma(x)
.
Since
\gamma
is normal, repeating arguments as above, we have

\tau\left(u\cdot\gamma(f_{n}(x))\right)\to\tau\left(u\cdot\gamma(\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}(x))\right),~(n\to\infty).

Note that
\gamma
is a homomorphism, and hence
\gamma(f_{n}(x))=f_{n}(\gamma(x))
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
L_{1}(\mathcal{R})
separates points of
\mathcal{R}
, we have

(B.3)

\gamma(\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)})(x)=\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}(\gamma(x)).

Applying the trace preseving of
\gamma
to (
B.3
), we obtain the desired equality.

Distribution of integers with digit restrictions via Markov chains
Vicente Saavedra-Araya

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce a new technique to study the distribution in residue classes of sets of integers with digit and sum-of-digits restrictions. From our main theorem, we derive a necessary and sufficient condition for integers with missing digits to be uniformly distributed in arithmetic progressions, extending previous results going back to the work of Erdős, Mauduit and Sárközy. Our approach utilizes Markov chains and does not rely on Fourier analysis as many results of this nature do. Our results apply more generally to the class of multiplicatively invariant sets of integers. This class, defined by Glasscock, Moreira and Richter using symbolic dynamics, is an integer analogue to fractal sets and includes all missing digits sets. We address uniform distribution in this setting, partially answering an open question posed by the same authors.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we investigate the distribution of sets of integers formed by imposing restrictions on the digits in a given base. Throughout this paper, we denote by
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
the set of natural numbers including zero. For an integer
g\geq 2
and a set of digits
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\{0,\cdots,g-1\}
, let

(1.1)

\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}:=\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{m}w_{i}g^{i}:\ m\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},\ w_{i}\in\mathcal{D}\right\}

be the set of non-negative integers that can be written in base
g
only using digits of
\mathcal{D}
.
Such sets have been widely studied (see, for example,
[
18
,
29
,
38
,
41
]
), and we will refer to them as
missing digits sets.
A broader class of integers was introduced by Glasscock, Moreira, and Richter in
[
27
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
g\geq 2
be an integer. The set
A\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
is said to be
\times g
-invariant
if for every
n\in A
with base-
g
expansion given by
n=w_{m}g^{m}+\cdots+w_{1}g+w_{0}
, where
m\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and
w_{m}\neq 0
, it holds

w_{m-1}g^{m-1}+\cdots+w_{1}g+w_{0}\in A\quad\text{and}\quad w_{m}g^{m-1}+\cdots+w_{2}g+w_{1}\in A.

We say a set of integers is
multiplicatively invariant
if it is
\times g
-invariant for some
g\geq 2
.

Notice that the first condition in
Definition

1.1
indicates that the set
A
is invariant under the operation of deleting the most significant digit in base
g
, while the second condition ensures that it is invariant under the operation of deleting the least significant digit. Clearly, integers with missing digits belong to this class, but so do many other sets described by broader digit restrictions (for example, by forbidding specific combinations of digits). Unlike missing digits sets, much less is known about multiplicatively invariant sets.

Motivated by the study of the distribution of integers with missing digits in arithmetic progressions by Erdős, Mauduit and Sárközy in
[
18
]
, and by an open question posed by Glasscock, Moreira, and Richter in
[
27
, Question 5.6]
concerning the interaction between multiplicatively invariant sets and arithmetic progressions, this paper investigates distribution properties of multiplicatively invariant sets. A central idea is to understand when a set of integers visits different congruence classes with the same frequency. In this sense,
A\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
is said to be
uniformly distributed
\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
(or in arithmetic progressions) if

\lim_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{|\{n\in A:n\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a\}\cap[0,N)|}{|A\cap[0,N)|}=\dfrac{1}{a}

for every
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}:={\mathbb{Z}}/a{\mathbb{Z}}
.
To explore this idea in more generality, we use the notion of
g
-additive
functions (firstly considered by Bellman and Shapiro in
[
7
]
).
We say
f:{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is
g
-additive
if

f\left(\sum_{i=0}^{m}w_{i}g^{i}\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}f(w_{i}g^{i})

for every
w_{0},\ldots,w_{m}\in\{0,\ldots,g-1\}.

Let
A\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
be
\times g
-invariant and
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
. For all
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
, let
f_{i}
be a
g
-additive function,
a_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
b_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{a_{i}}
. The main objective in this work is to study the limiting behaviour of

(1.2)

\frac{|\left\{n\in A:\ f_{i}(n)\equiv b_{i}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a_{i}\text{ for $i=1,\ldots,r$}\right\}\cap[0,N)|}{|A\cap[0,N)|}

when
N\to\infty
, and determine conditions to guarantee the limit equals to
1/(a_{1}\cdot a_{2}\cdots a_{r})
for all
b_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{a_{i}}
. When this happens, we will say that
A
is
uniformly distributed
\mathbf{f}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a}
, where
\mathbf{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{r})
and
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})
. Our main idea lies in the construction of suitable Markov chains that capture the behaviour of (
1.2
), and then study it using classical convergence results for Markov chains. Before stating our main results, we will briefly review some previous results and introduce the context of the problem to be investigated.

1.1.
History and context

The study of the distribution of sets of integers with digit restrictions dates back to the works of Fine
[
20
]
and Gelfond
[
25
]
. For an integer
g\geq 2
, they considered the function sum-of-digits in base
g
, denoted by
S_{g}
and defined as follows: If
w_{0},\cdots,w_{m}\in\{0,\cdots,g-1\}
,

(1.3)

S_{g}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{m}w_{i}g^{i}\right)=w_{0}+\cdots+w_{m}.

In this context, Gelfond studied the interplay between
n
and
S_{g}(n)
for positive integers in terms of their distribution in residue classes, providing sufficient conditions for uniform distribution. A straightforward consequence of
[
25
, Theorem 1]
is presented below.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
a,a^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\gcd(g-1,a^{\prime})=1
. Then,

(1.4)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{|\{n\leq N:\ n\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a,\ S_{g}(n)\equiv b^{\prime}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}\}|}{N}=\dfrac{1}{aa^{\prime}}

for any
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}
and
b^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{a^{\prime}}
.

It is worth mentioning that Gelfond’s result also gives quantitative information about the convergence. The
Corollary

1.2
shows that the sequences
n\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
and
S_{g}(n)\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}
are independent; in other words, integers with a fixed sum of digits
\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}
are uniformly distributed in arithmetic progressions (a related result can be found in
[
40
]
). As part of our work, we provide an equivalent condition to the uniform distribution exhibited in (
1.4
), thereby extending previous works.
Results concerning the independence between sum-of-digits functions in different bases can be found in
[
8
]
and
[
30
]
. For an overview of results of this type, see
[

In a related direction, the distribution of missing digits sets in residue classes was initially studied by Erdős, Mauduit and Sárközy. Let
\mathcal{D}\subset\{0,\ldots,g-1\}
be a subset of digits (with at least 2 elements). Regarding the set
\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}
defined in (

[

.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume
\gcd\left(g(g-1),a\right)=1
,
0\in\mathcal{D}
and
\gcd\{d\in\mathcal{D}:\ d\neq 0\}=1.
Then, there exists

(all dependent only on

\left|\dfrac{|\left\{n\in\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}:\ n\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a\right\}\cap[0,N)|}{|\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}\cap[0,N)|}-\dfrac{1}{a}\right|\leq\dfrac{c_{2}}{m}\exp\left(-c_{3}\dfrac{\log(N)}{\log(a)}\right)

for any
a<\exp\left(c_{1}(\log(N))^{1/2}\right)
and
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}
.

Further generalization of
Theorem

1.3
were provided by Konyagin
[
31

and Aloui
[
2

, with the latter also generalizing
Corollary

1.2
to the case of missing digits sets. A qualitative version of
Theorem

1.3
can be
presented as follows.

Corollary 1.4
.

Assume
\gcd(g(g-1),a)=1
,
0\in\mathcal{D}
and
\gcd\{d\in\mathcal{D}:\ d\neq 0\}=1.
Then,
\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}
is uniformly distributed
\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
.

As a consequence of our work, we strengthen
Corollary

1.4
, providing an equivalent condition to the uniform distribution when
\gcd(g,a)=1
.

1.2.
Overview of main results

The main contribution of this paper lies in the introduction of a new approach to study the distribution of certain sets of integers via Markov chains. From this technique, we can retrieve several results, some of which are presented below.

In the direction of the works of Fine
[
20
]
, Gelfond
[
25
]
, and Erdős, Mauduit and Sárközy
[
18
]
, we study the distribution in residue classes of set of integers with digits restrictions. A first consequence of the
Theorem

4.8
, our main technical result, establishes conditions for a set with missing digits to be uniformly distributed in residue classes. In contrast to
Corollary

1.4
and Aloui’s result
[
2
, Theorem 2.9]
, which provide only sufficient conditions, our result offers conditions that are both necessary and sufficient for uniform distribution.

Theorem A
.

Let
g\geq 2
be an integer and
a,a^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\gcd(g,a)=\gcd(a,a^{\prime})=1
. Let
\mathcal{D}=\{d_{1},\cdots,d_{t}\}\subseteq\{0,\cdots,g-1\}
be a subset of digits (where
d_{1}<\ldots<d_{t}
) and
\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}
be the missing digits set defined in (
1.1
). Then,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{|\{n\in\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}:\ n\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a,\ S_{g}(n)\equiv b^{\prime}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}\}\cap[0,N)|}{|\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}\cap[0,N)|}=\dfrac{1}{aa^{\prime}}

for every
(b,b^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{a^{\prime}}
if and only if

\gcd(aa^{\prime},d_{2}-d_{1},\cdots,d_{t}-d_{1})=1.

Indeed, we provide a comprehensive description of the limiting distribution even when
\gcd(aa^{\prime},d_{2}-d_{1},\cdots,d_{t}-d_{1})\neq 1
(
Theorem

5.4
), and offer insight into the rate of convergence. In
Proposition

5.6
, we also present an equivalent condition without the assumption
\gcd(a,a^{\prime})=1
.

By setting
a^{\prime}=1
, we generalize
Corollary

1.4
by providing an equivalent condition. Furthermore, we are able to recover the same convergence speed as that obtained by Erdős, Mauduit and Sárközy, albeit with a difference in the constants involved.

Corollary A
.

Let
g\geq 2
be an integer, and
a\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\gcd(g,a)=1
. Let
\mathcal{D}=\{d_{1},\cdots,d_{t}\}\subseteq\{0,\cdots,g-1\}
be a subset of digits, where
d_{1}<\ldots<d_{t}.
Then,
\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}
is uniformly distributed
\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
if and only if
\gcd(a,d_{2}-d_{1},\cdots,d_{t}-d_{1})=1.

For a fully description of the distribution
\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
, see
Corollary

5.5
. Our results are not limited to the distribution of integers with missing digits, but more general digit restrictions are allowed. In
Section

5.3
and
Section

5.4
, we address more general restrictions and provide examples of sets with missing combinations of digits that exhibit uniform distribution. Additionally, our method allows us to revisit the result of Gelfond, yielding an equivalent condition to the uniform distribution exhibited in (
1.4
). In particular, this shows that the sufficient condition
\gcd(g-1,a^{\prime})=1
provided in
Corollary

1.2
is not necessary.

Theorem B
.

Let
g\geq 2
be an integer, and
a,a^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{|\{n\leq N:\ n\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a,\ S_{g}(n)\equiv b^{\prime}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}\}|}{N}=\dfrac{1}{aa^{\prime}}

for every
(b,b^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{a^{\prime}}
if and only if there exists
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
n\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
and
S_{g}(n)\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}.

Notice that the existence of
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
that satisfy
n\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
and
S_{g}(n)\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}
can be established from an elementary construction

under the condition

Finally, we provide a negative answer in the general case to the open question posed by Glasscock, Moreira and Richter
[
27
, Question 5.6]
, and we provide a description using symbolic dynamics of sets of integers satisfying affirmatively the statement. An important idea is that every multiplicatively invariant set of integers can be characterized by a dynamical structure known as a subshift (see

Theorem C

Let
A\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}_{0}

Refer to

Conjecture

Let

\dim_{\text{M}}(A\cap P)
is either 0 or
\dim_{\text{M}}(A)
.

1.3.
Outline of the proof

We present an outline of the proof of
Corollary

A
. Despite being a consequence of more general results, the proof contains the key ideas of this paper while avoiding some obstructions that arise when dealing with additional digit restrictions.

Consider an integer
g\geq 2
, an integer
a\in{\mathbb{N}}
coprime with
g
, and a set of digits
\mathcal{D}=\{d_{1},\cdots,d_{t}\}\subseteq\{0,\ldots,g-1\}
. We aim to construct Markov chains that describe the
behaviour of

(1.5)

\dfrac{|\{n\in\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}:\ n\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a\}\cap[0,N)|}{|\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}\cap[0,N)|}

when
N\to\infty
. For a finite sequence of digits
w=w_{0}w_{1}\cdots w_{m}
(we call it a word of length
m+1
), we denote

(w)_{g}:=w_{m}g^{m}+\cdots+w_{1}g+w_{0}.

We identify
\mathcal{D}^{i}
with the set of words of length
i
, and we say
w\in\mathcal{D}^{i}
represents
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
if
(w)_{g}=n
. Clearly,

\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}=\{(w)_{g}:\ w\in\mathcal{D}^{n}\text{ for $n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}$}\},

so equivalently, we can study the behavior of (
1.5
) in terms of words.
For every
i
, we define the measure
\mu_{i}
on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}
as

\mu_{i}(\{b\}):=\dfrac{|\{w\in\mathcal{D}^{i}:\ (w)_{g}\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a\}|}{|\mathcal{D}|^{i}}.

For simplicity, we will denote
\mu_{i}(b)=\mu_{i}(\{b\})
. This measure represents the distribution in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}
of elements in
\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}
that can be expressed using
i
digits of
\mathcal{D}
in base
g
.
Our goal is to understand the evolution of
\mu_{i}
, and to do this, we will study how a word of length
i
is extended
3
3

3

We say
x^{\prime}=x^{\prime}_{0}\ldots x^{\prime}_{k}
is an extension of
x=x_{0}\ldots x_{m}
if
k>m
and
x^{\prime}_{j}=x_{j}
for all
j\leq m
.
.
For this purpose, we consider
p\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
g^{p}\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ a(g-1)
(whose existence is guaranteed by the condition
\gcd(g,a)=1
). Let us define the sets
E_{i}
for
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
as

(1.6)

E_{i}(b):=\Big\{w\in\mathcal{D}^{p}:\ g^{i}(w)_{g}\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a\Big\},

and we also define define the matrix on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}
given by

M_{i}(b,b^{\prime}):=\dfrac{|E_{i}(b^{\prime}-b)|}{|\mathcal{D}|^{p}}.

It is easy to see that
M_{i}
is a double stochastic matrix.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and let
x\in\mathcal{D}^{i+np}
such that
(x)_{g}\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
. We can ask the following: For
b^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}
, how many extensions
x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}^{i+(n+1)p}
of
x
satisfy
(x^{\prime})_{g}\equiv b^{\prime}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
?
The answer is the cardinality of
E_{i+np}(b^{\prime}-b)
, and we can observe that
E_{i}(b-b^{\prime})=E_{i+np}(b-b^{\prime})
since
g^{np}\equiv 1\mod a
. From this, we can obtain the relation

\mu_{i+np}=\mu_{i}M_{i}^{n}.

If
X^{i}
is a Markov chain with initial distribution
\mu_{i}
and transition matrix
M_{i}
, it holds

\mathbb{P}\Big(X_{n}^{i}=b\Big)=\mu_{i+np}(b).

Moreover, if
X^{i}
is irreducible and aperiodic, the Markov chain converges to its invariant distribution, hence

(1.7)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\Big(X^{i}_{n}=b\Big)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{|\{w\in\mathcal{D}^{i+np}:\ (w)_{g}\equiv b\mod a\}|}{|\mathcal{D}|^{i+np}}=\dfrac{1}{a}.

If
0\notin\mathcal{D}
, there is a one-to-one correspondence between elements of
\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}
and words. On the other hand, since
(w0\cdots 0)_{g}=(w)_{g}
, every element of
\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}
can be represented by infinitely many words when
0\in\mathcal{D}
. By making this distinction and under the assumption that
X^{1},\cdots,X^{p}
are irreducible and aperiodic, we can elucidate (
1.7
) in terms of the set of integers with missing digits:

•

If
0\in\mathcal{D}
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{|\{n\in\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}:\ n\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a\}\cap[0,g^{N})|}{|\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}\cap[0,g^{N})|}=\dfrac{1}{a}.

•

If
0\notin\mathcal{D}
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{|\{n\in\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}:\ n\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a\}\cap[g^{N-1},g^{N})|}{|\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}\cap[g^{N-1},g^{N})|}=\dfrac{1}{a}.

For an arbitrary set of integers, this does not guarantee the existence of the limit when considering intervals of the form
[0,N)
. However, as we will prove in
Section

4
, in this case it happens. Therefore, we can show that

(1.8)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{|\{n\in\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}:\ n\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a\}\cap[0,N)|}{|\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}\cap[0,N)|}=\dfrac{1}{a}

for every
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}
. Certainly, we must still ensure that each Markov chain
X^{i}
converges to the equilibrium. This property will hold if we choose
p
as above and
\gcd(a,d_{2}-d_{1},\cdots,d_{t}-d_{1})=1
, where
d_{1}
is the smallest digit of
\mathcal{D}
.

On the other hand, if
\gcd(a,d_{2}-d_{1},\cdots,d_{t}-d_{1})\neq 1
, it is possible to show that there exist
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}
and
j\in\{1,\cdots,p\}
such that

\mathbb{P}\Big(X_{n}^{j}=b\Big)=0

for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. From this fact, we will conclude that (
1.8
) does not hold for such a
b
.

Some difficulties arise when dealing with additional constraints on the digits, but we will extend the construction presented above to more general cases by leveraging ideas from symbolic dynamics. We are going to discuss this in detail in the following sections.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we introduce some concepts of Markov chains, symbolic dynamics and integer fractals that will be used throughout this paper.

2.1.
Markov chains

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a finite set, and let
M
be a square matrix indexed by
\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}
with entries in
[0,1]
.
The matrix
M
is called
stochastic
if the sum of each row is 1, i.e., for every
s\in\mathcal{S}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}M(s,s^{\prime})=1.

Given a stochastic matrix
M
and a probability measure
\mu
on
\mathcal{S}
, a
discrete-time Markov chain
with state space
\mathcal{S}
, transition matrix
M
and initial distribution
\mu
is a sequence of random variables
(X_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
on a probability space (with probability
\mathbb{P}
) such that
\mathbb{P}(X_{0}=s)=\mu(\{s\})=:\mu(s)
, and

\mathbb{P}(X_{n+1}=s_{n+1}|X_{n}=s_{n},\cdots,X_{0}=s_{0})=\mathbb{P}(X_{1}=s_{n+1}|X_{0}=s_{n})=M(s_{n},s_{n+1})\

for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and
s_{0},\cdots,s_{n+1}\in\mathcal{S}
. In this case, we have

\mathbb{P}(X_{n}=s^{\prime})=\mu\cdot M^{n}(\cdot,s^{\prime})=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\mu(s)M^{n}(s,s^{\prime}).

For
s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}
, we say that
s^{\prime}
is
accessible from
s
if
M^{n}(s,s^{\prime})>0
for some
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. The matrix
M
is called
irreducible
if for every
s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}
, there exists
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
M^{n}(s,s^{\prime})>0
(i.e., all states are accessible from each other). We define the
period of the state
s
as

Per(s):=\gcd\Big\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}:\ M^{n}(s,s)>0\Big\}.

If every state has period 1, we say
M
is
aperiodic
. When
M
is irreducible, all states share the same period, so aperiodicity can be checked by finding one state with period 1.

The following classical result on the convergence of Markov chains will be a key tool in this paper.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
35
, Theorem 4.9]

Let
X
be a Markov chain with state space
\mathcal{S}
(not necessarily finite), transition matrix
M
and initial distribution
\mu
. Suppose
M
is irreducible and aperiodic, and there exists an invariant distribution
\lambda
for
M
(i.e.,
\lambda=\lambda M
). Then, there exist
\rho\in(0,1)
and
C>0
such that

|\mathbb{P}(X_{n}=s)-\lambda(s)|\leq C\rho^{n}

for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
s\in\mathcal{S}
.

Notice that the invariant distribution for an irreducible and aperiodic transition matrix with a finite state space always exists (and it is unique) as a consequence of the Perron-Frobenius theorem. In addition, the
Theorem

2.1
is only dependent on the transition matrix, so that
\lambda
,
C
and
\rho
are independent of the initial distribution. A particularly interesting case occurs when the sum of each column of
M
is also 1, in which case
M
is called
doubly stochastic
. In this case, the distribution
\lambda(s)=1/|\mathcal{S}|
is invariant for
M
. For further discussion on the convergence of Markov chains, see
[
44
, Section 1.8]
and
[
45
, Section 3]
.

2.2.
Elements of symbolic dynamics

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a finite set (in this paper, we assume

\mathcal{D}\subseteq\{0,\cdots,g-1\}
for some
g\in{\mathbb{N}}
), and consider the
left-shift
operator
\sigma
over
\mathcal{D}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
, defined by

\sigma:(w_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\mapsto(w_{n+1})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}.

Endowing
\mathcal{D}
with the discrete topology and considering the product topology on
\mathcal{D}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
,
\sigma
is a continuous function, and the topological system
(\mathcal{D}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}},\sigma)
is called a
full shift
. If
\Sigma\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
is closed and
\sigma(\Sigma)\subseteq\Sigma
, the dynamical system
(\Sigma,\sigma)
is called a
subshift
.
Although a subshift is a set of infinite sequences of symbols, it can be fully characterized by the finite sequences that appear in
\Sigma
, referred to as
words
.

We define the
language
of
\Sigma
, denoted by
\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
, as the set of all the words in
\Sigma
,

\mathcal{L}(\Sigma):=\Big\{w_{0}\cdots w_{n-1}:\ n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},\ \exists x\in\Sigma\text{ s.t. }x_{0}=w_{0},\ x_{1}=w_{1},\ldots,\ x_{n-1}=w_{n-1}\Big\}.

When there is no confusion, we will simply denote the language by
\mathcal{L}
. For any word
w=w_{0}\cdots w_{n-1}\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
, we say
w
has
length

n
(and we denote the length by
|w|
). The set
\mathcal{L}^{n}(\Sigma)
denotes all words in the language of length
n
. Also, we will commonly use the notions of concatenation of words, and subwords. For
u=u_{0}\ldots u_{m-1}
and
v=v_{0}\ldots v_{n-1}
, the
concatenation
of
u
with
v
is written as
uv=u_{0}\ldots u_{m-1}v_{0}\ldots v_{n-1}
and we say that
uv
is an
extension
of
u
. For a given word
u
and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we also denote by
u^{k}=u\cdots u
, the concatenation
k
-times of
u
. We say
w
is a
subword
of
x
if the sequence of
x
contains
w
.

A subshift is a particular case of a topological dynamical system, and some classical notions of topological dynamics have a special characterization in the context of symbolic systems.

Definition 2.2
.

The topological dynamical system
(\Sigma,\sigma)
is said to be
transitive
(or
irreducible
) if for every
u,v\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
, there exists
w\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
such that
uwv\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma).
Also, the system is said to be
mixing
if for every
u,v
in the language, there exists
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for every
m\geq n
there exists
w\in\mathcal{L}^{m}(\Sigma)
such that
uwv\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
.

On the other hand, the notion of
topological entropy
is a measure of the complexity of a dynamical system. In symbolic systems, it quantifies the complexity of the language.

Definition 2.3
.

The (topological) entropy of the subshift
\Sigma
is defined as

h(\Sigma):=\lim_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{\log|\mathcal{L}^{N}(\Sigma)|}{N}.

See Chapter 4 of
[
36
]
for further discussion. Regarding the classification of subshifts, we will consider two important classes,
shifts of finite type
and
sofic shifts
.

Definition 2.4
.

Consider the full shift
X=\mathcal{D}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
, and let
\mathscr{F}\subseteq\mathcal{L}(X)
be a finite set of
forbidden words
. We define the subshift

X_{\mathscr{F}}:=\{x\in X:\ x\text{ does not contain any $w\in\mathscr{F}$ as subword}\}.

We say that
\Sigma\subseteq X
is a
shift of finite type
(SFT for short) if there exists a finite set
\mathscr{F}
such that
\Sigma=X_{\mathscr{F}}
.

If the longest forbidden word has length
M+1
, we say that
X_{\mathscr{F}}
is a
M
-step
shift of finite type. In a
1
-step SFT, all forbidden words have length
2
, so it can be described by a matrix indexed by
\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{D}
with values in
\{0,1\}
. In this sense,
\Sigma
is a
1
-step SFT if and only if there exists a matrix
T
such that

\Sigma=\Big\{x\in\mathcal{D}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}:\ T(x_{i},x_{i+1})=1\text{ for all }i\Big\}.

A more general way to construct subshifts is by reading infinite sequences in a
labelled directed multi-graph
. A labelled multi-graph is a triple
G=(V,E,\lambda)
, where
V
is a finite set of nodes,
E
is a set of edges and
\lambda:E\to\mathcal{A}
is a labelling function for some finite alphabet
\mathcal{A}
. Each edge
e\in E
has an associated starting node
s(e)\in V
and a terminal node
t(e)\in V
. A sequence of edges
\pi=e_{0}\cdots e_{n}
is called a
path
in
G
if
t(e_{i})=s(e_{i+1})
for every
i\in\{0,\cdots,n-1\}
, and we say that
\pi
has length
|\pi|:=n+1
. Note that multiple edges may share the same starting and terminal nodes while having different labels.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
G=(V,E,\lambda)
be a labelled directed multi-graph. If
\mathcal{D}
is the finite alphabet associated with the labelling function, we define the subshift
\Sigma_{G}\subset\mathcal{D}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
as

\Sigma_{G}=\left\{\left(\lambda(e_{j})\right)_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\in\mathcal{D}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}:\ s(e_{j+1})=t(e_{j})\ \forall j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\right\}.

Thus,
\Sigma_{G}
are all infinite sequences that we can read in some infinite path of
G
. It is well known that
\Sigma_{G}
is a subshift.

Definition 2.6
.

The subshift
\Sigma\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
is said to be
sofic
if there exists a labelled directed multi-graph such that
\Sigma=\Sigma_{G}
. The graph
G
is called a
cover
of
\Sigma
.

Note that the cover of a sofic shift is not unique. Additionally, every shift of finite type is sofic.

Remark 1
.

Let
\Sigma\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
be a 1-step shift of finite type, and consider a matrix
T
that represents
\Sigma
, i.e.,
T(d,d^{\prime})=1
if and only if
dd^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
. We create a node
V(d)
for every
d\in\mathcal{D}
, and place an edge from
V(d)
to
V(d^{\prime})
labelled by
d^{\prime}
if and only if
T(d,d^{\prime})=1
. The resulting labelled graph is a cover for
\Sigma
, and we denote it by
\mathcal{G}_{T}
.

To construct a cover for a sofic shift, we can rely on the notion of
follower sets

[
36
, Chapter 3]
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\Sigma
be a subshift of
\mathcal{D}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
. For any
w\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
, the
follower set of
w
, denoted
F_{\Sigma}(w)
, is the set of all words that can extend
w
, i.e.,

F_{\Sigma}(w):=\Big\{v\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma):\ wv\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)\Big\}.

We denote by
\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}:=\Big\{F_{\Sigma}(w):\ w\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)\Big\}
the collection of all follower sets. When the underlying subshift
\Sigma
is clear from the context, we will simply write
F(w)
and
\mathcal{F}
.

While each word has its own follower set, a folklore result states that
\Sigma
is sofic if and only if there are finitely many distinct follower sets
[
36
, Theorem 3.2.10]
, i.e.,
|\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}|<\infty
. If
d\in F(u)=F(v)
, it follows that
F(ud)=F(vd)
. Using these ideas, a cover can be constructed for
\Sigma
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\Sigma\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
be a sofic shift. Consider the finite set of nodes
V=\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}
, and place an edge labelled by
d\in\mathcal{D}
from
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}
to
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}
if
d\in F
, and
F(wd)=F^{\prime}
for any word
w
such that
F=F(w).
The labelled multi-graph generated is called the
follower set graph of
\Sigma
.

The Follower set graph is a cover of
\Sigma

[
36
, Proposition 3.2.9]
. Notice that the follower set graph is
right-resolving
, meaning that for any node, all the edges going out have different labels. When the sofic shift is transitive, we would expect the cover to be an irreducible graph (i.e., there is a path between each pair of vertices), but this is not always true for the follower set graph. To address this problem, a subgraph can be extracted, which remains a cover and is irreducible.

Definition 2.9
.

A word
w\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
is said to be
synchronizing
if for every
u,v\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
, the condition that both
uv
and
wv
are in the language implies that
uwv\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
.

While not every subshift has synchronizing words, it is well-known that transitive sofic shifts do. Moreover, every word can be extended to a synchronizing, and any extension of a synchronizing word is also synchronizing. In the case of a
M
-step SFT, every word with length at least
M+1
is synchronizing

An important result due to Fischer
[
21
,
22
]
establishes the existence and uniqueness (up to isomorphism) of a minimal (in terms of the number of nodes), right-resolving and irreducible cover for a transitive sofic shift.
This unique representation is known as the
Fischer cover
, and plays an important role in this paper. The following outlines the construction of this cover.

Construction of the Fischer cover
: Let
\Sigma\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
be a transitive sofic shift.
For each follower set
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}
, we create a node associated to
F
if there exists a synchronizing word
w
such that
F(w)=F
; in other words, we consider the set of nodes

\mathcal{V}_{\Sigma}:=\{F(w):\ w\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)\text{ is synchronizing}\}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}.

Let
F,F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}_{\Sigma}
and
d\in\mathcal{D}
. Then, there exists
w\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
such that
F=F(w)
. We create an edge with starting node
F
, terminal node
F^{\prime}
and labelled by
d
if and only if
F^{\prime}=F(wd)
. The resulting graph, denoted by
\mathcal{G}_{\Sigma}=(\mathcal{V}_{\Sigma},\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma},\lambda_{\Sigma})
, is the Fischer cover for
\Sigma
(see Chapter 3 in
[
36
]
and Lemma 6.11 in
[
48
]
for more details).

Thus, the Fischer cover is a subgraph of the follower set graph, constructed by considering only the nodes corresponding to follower sets of synchronizing words. Notice that for any synchronizing word
w\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
, every path in
\mathcal{G}_{\Sigma}
labelled by
w
must end at the node associated with
F(w)
. In the case
\Sigma
is a mixing sofic subshift, it is possible to find
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for every pair of nodes
F,F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}
and
m\geq n
, there exists a path of length
m
from
F
to
F^{\prime}
. In such a case, for every pair of words
u
and
v
in the language, there exists
w\in\mathcal{L}^{m}(\Sigma)
such that
uwv\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)

2.3.
Fractal sets of integers

In
[
27
]
, Glasscock, Moreira and Richter introduced the notion of multiplicativaly invariant sets of integers (recall
Definition

1.1
), and studied them from a fractal perspective. Here, we focus on the notion of
mass dimension
, which is analogous to the box dimension in fractal geometry but adapted to the setting of integers.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
A\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
be non-empty. We define the
lower mass dimension
and the
upper mass dimension
of
A
as:

\displaystyle\underline{\dim}_{\text{M}}(A):=\liminf_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{\log|A\cap[0,N)|}{\log N}=\sup\left\{\gamma\geq 0:\ \liminf_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{|A\cap[0,N)|}{N^{\gamma}}>0\right\},

\displaystyle\overline{\dim}_{\text{M}}(A):=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{\log|A\cap[0,N)|}{\log N}=\sup\left\{\gamma\geq 0:\ \limsup_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{|A\cap[0,N)|}{N^{\gamma}}>0\right\}.

If
\underline{\dim}_{\text{M}}(A)=\overline{\dim}_{\text{M}}(A)
, we denote this value by
\dim_{\text{M}}(A)
and say that the
mass dimension
of
A
exists.

From
[
27
, Proposition 3.6]
, the mass dimension exists for any multiplicatively invariant set of integers. A natural way to generate
\times g
-invariant sets is by considering subshifts of
X=\{0,\ldots,g-1\}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
.

Definition 2.11
.

The
g-language set
asociated with a subshift
\Sigma\subseteq\{0,\ldots,g-1\}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
is the set
A_{\Sigma}\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
defined by

(2.1)

A_{\Sigma}:=\Big\{(w)_{g}:=w_{0}+w_{1}g+\cdots+w_{n-1}g^{n-1}\ :\ w=w_{0}\cdots w_{n-1}\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)\Big\}.

In general, the expansion of a number in a certain base is read from left to right, where the leftmost digit represents the most significant digit. For a given word
w
, in this paper we will consider the rightmost digit to be the most significant digit of the integer
(w)_{g}
, this in order to be consistent with the theory of susbshifts. However, when it comes to applications this does not make a major distinction.

From the definition of
\times g
-invariant sets and the shift-invariance of
\Sigma
, it is evident that
A_{\Sigma}
is
\times g
-invariant. It is also possible to see that every multiplicatively invariant set can be written as
A_{\Sigma}
for some subshift
\Sigma
, and the mass dimension is related to the entropy of the subshift. This is summarized in the following proposition (see
[
27
, Proposition 3.10]
).

Proposition 2.12
.

The
g
-language set
A_{\Sigma}\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
corresponding to any non-empty subshift
\Sigma\subseteq\{0,\cdots,g-1\}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
is a
\times g
-invariant set, and the mass dimension of
A_{\Sigma}
is equal to the normalized topological entropy of the subshift
(\Sigma,\sigma)
, i.e.,

(2.2)

\dim_{\text{M}}(A_{\Sigma})=h(\Sigma)/\log(g).

Moreover, for any
\times g
-invariant set
A\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, there exists a subshift
\Sigma\subseteq\{0,\cdots,g-1\}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
such that
A=A_{\Sigma}
. In such a case, we say that
A
is represented by
\Sigma
.

Corollary 2.13
.

Let
g\geq 2
be an integer and let
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\{0,\cdots,g-1\}
. If we consider the full shift
\Sigma=\mathcal{D}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
,
\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}=A_{\Sigma}
. In addition,

\dim_{\text{M}}(\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}})=\log(|\mathcal{D}|)/\log(g).

Finally, the following result allows us to simplify the calculation of the dimension to simpler intervals.

Lemma 2.14
.

[
27
, Lemma 3.2. (IV)]
.
For
r\in\mathbb{N}
,
r\geq 2
,

(2.3)

\overline{\dim}_{\text{M}}(A)=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{\log|A\cap[0,r^{N})|}{N\log r}.

Analogously, we can compute
\underline{dim}_{\text{M}}(A)
by using
\liminf
instead of
\limsup
in (
2.3
).

3.
Construction of Markov chains

In
Section

1.3
,
we provided an overview of the construction of Markov chains in the context of integers with missing digits and their distribution in residue classes, but now we extend this approach to a more general framework. Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{r}
be
g
-additive functions,
a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
A\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
a
\times g
-invariant set. We aim to find conditions to ensure

(3.1)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{|\{n\in A:f_{i}(n)\equiv b_{i}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a_{i}\text{ for $i=1,\ldots,r$}\}\cap[0,N)|}{|A\cap[0,N)|}=\dfrac{1}{\Pi_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}}

for any
(b_{1},\ldots,b_{r})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{a_{1}}\times\cdots\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{a_{r}}.

Notation
.

Throughout this paper, we denote
\mathbf{a}:=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})
,
|\mathbf{a}|:=|{\mathbb{Z}}_{a_{1}}\times\ldots\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{a_{r}}|=\Pi_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}
, and
\mathbf{f}:=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{r})
. For any
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\mathbf{a}}:=\mathbb{Z}_{a_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}_{a_{r}}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we will write
\mathbf{f}(n)\equiv\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a}
if
f_{j}(n)\equiv b_{j}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a_{j}
for every
j\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
.

From
Proposition

2.12
, there exists a subshift
\Sigma\subseteq\{0,\cdots,g-1\}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
such that

A=A_{\Sigma}=\Big\{(w)_{g}:\ w\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)\Big\}.

Therefore, we will focus on studying the distribution of words of a certain length and then extend the result to the set of integers
A
. Similarly to
Section

1.3
, we need to find an analogue to the value
p
for periodicity and properly understand the concatenation of words in the language.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
f_{1},\cdots,f_{r}
be
g
-additive functions and
\mathbf{a}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{r}
. We say
\mathbf{f}
is
eventually periodic with respect to
\mathbf{a}
if there exists
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and
p\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\mathbf{f}(dg^{i+np})\equiv\mathbf{f}(dg^{i})\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a}

for every
d\in\{0,\ldots,g-1\}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
i\geq\ell
. Any pair
(p,\ell)
satisfying this condition is called an
eventual period
.

When
\ell^{\prime}\geq\ell
and
p\mid p^{\prime}
, the pair
(p^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})
is also an eventual period. Notice that
\mathbf{f}
is eventually periodic if each
f_{i}
is eventually periodic. Clearly, the function sum-of-digits in base
g
satisfies this condition with
p=1
and
\ell=0
. Also, the identity is always
g
-invariant and eventually periodic.

Proposition 3.2
.

For any
a,g\in{\mathbb{N}}
, there exist
p\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\ell\leq a-1
such that

dg^{i}\equiv dg^{i+np}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a

for every
d\in\{0,\cdots,g-1\}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
i\geq\ell
.

Proof.

At least two elements of
\{g^{j}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a:\ j\in\{0,\cdots,a\}\}
agree. Then, there exist
j_{0}<j_{1}\leq a
such that
g^{j_{0}}\equiv g^{j_{1}}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
. We conclude by considering
\ell=j_{0}
and
p=j_{1}-j_{0}
.
∎

If
A
is a missing digits set, then
A=A_{\Sigma}
for
\Sigma=\mathcal{D}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
, where
\mathcal{D}
is the respective set of digits. In this context, the concatenation of two words is in
\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
if and only if both words are in
\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
. Unlike full shifts, this property generally does not hold. Nevertheless, it is possible to adapt the construction when concatenation is governed by a suitable rule, as occurs in transitive sofic shifts through the notions of follower sets and the Fischer cover.

As discussed in
Section

2.2
, we can identify the nodes of the Fischer cover
\mathcal{G}_{\Sigma}=(\mathcal{V}_{\Sigma},\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma},\lambda_{\Sigma})
with the collection of follower sets associated to synchronizing words of the language. Thus, we can identify

\mathcal{V}_{\Sigma}=\{F_{1},F_{2},\ldots,F_{q}\}=\{F(w):\ w\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)\text{ is a synchronizing word}\}.

The concatenation of words can be understood from
\mathcal{G}_{\Sigma}
. For
x,y\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
,

xy\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)\iff y\in F(x)\iff\text{There exists a path of $\mathcal{G}_{\Sigma}$ labelled by $xy$.}

When
F(x)\in\mathcal{V}_{\Sigma}
, it is also equivalent to the existence of a path
\pi
in the Fischer cover starting at the node associated to
F(x)
, and it is labelled by
y
.

Notation
.

For a word
w\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
and
F,F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}_{\Sigma}
, we write
F\overset{w}{\to}F^{\prime}
if there is a path
\pi
in
\mathcal{G}_{\Sigma}
from
F
to
F^{\prime}
labelled by
w
. Since
\mathcal{G}_{\Sigma}
is right-resolving, any other path from
F
to
F^{\prime}
has a different label.

Notice that
F\overset{w}{\to}F^{\prime}
if and only if
w\in F
, and for every word
u
such that
F=F(u)
,
F(uw)=F^{\prime}
.

Remark 2
.

For the rest of this paper, we will consider a base
g\geq 2
,
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\mathbf{a}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{r}
, a collection of
g
-additive functions
\mathbf{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{r})
eventually periodic with respect to
\mathbf{a}
, and an eventual period
(p,\ell)
. In addition, we consider a transitive sofic subshift
\Sigma\subseteq\{0,\ldots,g-1\}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
, and
\mathcal{G}_{\Sigma}=(\mathcal{V}_{\Sigma},\mathcal{E}_{\Sigma},\lambda_{\Sigma})
its Fischer cover (if there is no confusion, we omit the subscript). For technical reasons, we will assume that
\ell
is large enough such that there exists some synchronizing word of length equal to
\ell
.

3.1.
Transition matrix

In order to define the transition matrix, we will group the elements of
\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
according to how they extend other words. Recalling that
(w_{0}\ldots w_{n})_{g}:=w_{0}+w_{1}g+\cdots+w_{n}g^{n}
, we introduce the following subsets of
\mathcal{L}^{p}(\Sigma)
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
. For any
\mathbf{b}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathbf{a}}={\mathbb{Z}}_{a_{1}}\times\cdots\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{a_{r}}
and
F,F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}
, we define the set

E_{i}(\mathbf{b},F,F^{\prime}):=\Big\{w\in\mathcal{L}^{p}(\Sigma):\mathbf{f}(g^{i}(w)_{g})\equiv\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a},\ F\overset{w}{\to}F^{\prime}\Big\}.

Notice that
E_{i}
depends on
g
,
\mathbf{f}
,
\mathbf{a}
,
\Sigma
and
p
, but as in
Remark

2
we set these elements, we will not make the dependency explicit in order to lighten the notation. The following lemma clarifies the motivation for defining these sets.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
x\in\mathcal{L}^{i}(\Sigma)
and
w\in E_{i}(\mathbf{b},F(x),F^{\prime})
. Then,
xw\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
,
F(xw)=F^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{f}\left((xw)_{g}\right)\equiv\mathbf{f}((x)_{g})+\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a}.
If
i\geq\ell
, it also holds
E_{i}=E_{i+np}
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof.

Since
F(x)\overset{w}{\to}F^{\prime}
,
xw\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
and
F(xw)=F^{\prime}
. Also,

\mathbf{f}((xw)_{g})=\mathbf{f}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}x_{j}g^{j}+\sum_{j=i}^{i+p-1}w_{j}g^{j}\right)=\mathbf{f}((x)_{g})+\mathbf{f}(g^{i}(w)_{g})\equiv\mathbf{f}((x)_{g})+\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a}.

If
i\geq\ell
,
\mathbf{f}(g^{i}(y)_{g})=\mathbf{f}(g^{i+np}(y)_{g})
for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
y\in\mathcal{L}^{p}(\Sigma)
, concluding that
E_{i}=E_{i+np}.

∎

Despite that the collection of sets
E_{i}
is not a partition of
\mathcal{L}^{p}
(since a word might be read along different paths in
\mathcal{G}
), we still can understand it as a partition of the paths of length
p
in
\mathcal{G}
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
. For every
F\in\mathcal{V}
,

(3.2)

\ \sum_{(\mathbf{b},F^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathbf{a}}\times\mathcal{V}}|E_{i}(\mathbf{b},F,F^{\prime})|=|\{\pi=(\pi_{0}\cdots\pi_{p-1})\text{ is a path of $\mathcal{G}$ and }s(\pi)=F\}|.

Similarly, for every
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}
,

(3.3)

\sum_{(\mathbf{b},F)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathbf{a}}\times\mathcal{V}}|E_{i}(\mathbf{b},F,F^{\prime})|=|\{\pi=(\pi_{0}\cdots\pi_{p-1})\text{ is a path of $\mathcal{G}$ and }t(\pi)=F^{\prime}\}|.

Proof.

Let
F,F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}
. Notice that the sets
E_{i}(\mathbf{b},F,F^{\prime})
and
E_{i}(\mathbf{b}^{\prime},F,F^{\prime})
are disjoint when
\mathbf{b}\neq\mathbf{b}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathbf{a}}
. For any
\mathbf{b}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathbf{a}}
, if
w\in E_{i}(\mathbf{b},F,F^{\prime})
, there exists a path
\pi
in
\mathcal{G}
of length
p
such that
\lambda(\pi)=w
from
F
to
F^{\prime}
. On the other hand, any path
\pi
of length
p
from
F
to
F^{\prime}
represents a word
x:=\lambda(\pi)
of
E_{i}(\mathbf{b},F,F^{\prime})
, where
\mathbf{b}:=\mathbf{f}(g^{i}(x)_{g})
. Since
F
and
F^{\prime}
are fixed, each of these paths represents a different word. Then,

\displaystyle|\{\pi=(\pi_{0}\cdots\pi_{p-1})\text{ is a path in $\mathcal{G}$ from $F$ to $F^{\prime}$}|

\displaystyle=\left|\bigcup_{\mathbf{b}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathbf{a}}}E_{i}(\mathbf{b},F,F^{\prime})\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{b}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathbf{a}}}|E_{i}(\mathbf{b},F,F^{\prime})|.

By summing over
F^{\prime}
the equality (
3.2
) is obtained, and similarly, (
3.3
) follows by summing over
F
.
∎

Usually, (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) do not agree. For this work, we require an extra regularity of
\mathcal{G}
to ensure that the number of paths of length
p
starting at some
F\in\mathcal{V}
is equal to the number of paths of length
p
with terminal node
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}
. From graph theory, a directed multi-graph is said to be
k
-regular
if each vertex has
k
edges going in and
k
edges going out (two edges are different if they differ in the starting node, terminal node or label). We can pose this definition in subshifts.

Definition 3.6
.

We say a transitive sofic shift is
k
-regular
if the graph representation given by the Fischer cover is a
k
-regular graph. We say that a transitive sofic shift is regular if it is
k
-regular for some
k\geq 2
.

If a graph is
1
-regular, it must represent a full shift in one symbol. Thus, we are interested in the case
k\geq 2
, so we include it directly in the definition. Trivially, any full shift satisfies this condition, with
k
being the number of symbols. When
\Sigma
is
k
-regular and
F\in\mathcal{V}
, there are
k^{p}
paths of length
p
starting from
F
, and
k^{p}
paths of length
p
ending at
F
. From this idea, we can define a transition matrix for the Markov chains.

Definition 3.7
.

In the framework of
Remark

2
, and assuming
\Sigma
is
k
-regular, we define the state space
\mathcal{S}:={\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathbf{a}}\times\mathcal{V}
and the matrix
M_{i}
indexed by
\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}
as

M_{i}\Big((\mathbf{b},F),(\mathbf{b}^{\prime},F^{\prime})\Big):=k^{-p}\cdot|E_{i}(\mathbf{b}^{\prime}-\mathbf{b},F,F^{\prime})|

for any
(\mathbf{b},F),(\mathbf{b}^{\prime},F^{\prime})\in\mathcal{S}.

Clearly, the matrix
M_{i}
depends on several elements, including the choice of
p
, but we will not make the dependence explicit. The regularity condition allows us to show that this matrix
M_{i}
is suitable for defining a Markov chain.

Proposition 3.8
.

For any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the matrix
M_{i}
is a doubly stochastic matrix.

Proof.

Let
(\mathbf{b},F)\in\mathcal{S}
. From (
3.2
),

\displaystyle\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}M_{i}\Big((\mathbf{b},F),s\Big)

\displaystyle=k^{-p}\sum_{(\mathbf{b}^{\prime},F^{\prime})\in\mathcal{S}}|E_{i}(\mathbf{b}^{\prime}-\mathbf{b},F,F^{\prime})|

\displaystyle=k^{-p}\sum_{(\hat{b},F^{\prime})\in\mathcal{S}}|E_{i}(\hat{\mathbf{b}},F,F^{\prime})|

\displaystyle=k^{-p}|\{\pi=(\pi_{0}\pi_{1}\cdots\pi_{p-1})\text{ is a path in $\mathcal{G}$ and }s(\pi)=F\}|.

We conclude
\displaystyle\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}M_{i}\Big((\mathbf{b},F),s\Big)=1
from
k
-regularity.
Analogously, from (
3.3
) we obtain
\displaystyle\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}M_{i}\Big(s,(\mathbf{b},F)\Big)=1.

∎

Note that the regularity is important to ensure that the normalization factor to make the matrix stochastic is the same for all rows. Without this, while a stochastic matrix could still be defined, the link between
M_{i}
and the distribution of integers becomes unclear, and our method no longer seems to work.

3.2.
Initial distribution

In our attempt to study
A_{\Sigma}
, the idea is to extend all the
\ell
-long words that have a follower set in
\mathcal{V}
, as we can fully describe those extensions using the Fischer cover. However, for some
w\in\mathcal{L}^{\ell}
, it might be that
F(w)\notin\mathcal{V}
. This leads to the following definition.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
\Sigma
be a transitive sofic shift. We define the
\ell
-restricted language of
\Sigma
as all words of
\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
that are extension of a word of length
\ell
with follower set in
\mathcal{V}
,

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}(\Sigma):=\Big\{w\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma):|w|\geq\ell\text{ and }F(w_{0}\cdots w_{\ell-1})\in\mathcal{V}\Big\}.

For every
i\geq\ell
, we also define
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{i}(\Sigma):=\{w\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}(\Sigma):\ |w|=i\}
.

The reason for considering
\ell
large enough in
Remark

2
is to ensure that this set is not empty. If
\Sigma
is a
M
-step shift of finite type, any word
w
such that
|w|\geq M+1
is synchronizing, therefore
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{i}(\Sigma)=\mathcal{L}^{i}(\Sigma)
for all
i\geq M+1
. Under the condition of regularity, it is easy to describe the cardinality of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{i}
. For the upcoming results, if
w=w_{0}\cdots w_{n}
is a word, we will identify it with a pair of words
(x,y)
such that
w=xy
.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
\Sigma
be a transitive sofic shift and
k
-regular. Then,

|\mathcal{L}^{i}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}(\Sigma)|=|\mathcal{L}^{\ell}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}(\Sigma)|\cdot k^{i-\ell}

for every
i\geq\ell
.

Proof.

Notice
x\in\mathcal{L}^{i}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}
if and only if
F:=F(x_{0}\cdots x_{\ell-1})\in\mathcal{V}
and there is a path
\pi
in the Fischer cover of length
i-\ell
from the vertex associated to
F
, and
\pi
is labelled by
x_{\ell}\cdots x_{i-1}
. Using the identification,

(3.4)

\mathcal{L}^{i}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}=\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{V}}\{w\in\mathcal{L}^{\ell}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}:F(w)=F\}\times\{\lambda(\pi):\ \pi=\pi_{0}\cdots\pi_{i-\ell-1}\in\mathcal{G},\ s(\pi)=F\}.

Since the Fischer cover is right-resolving, all paths in
\{\pi=\pi_{0}\cdots\pi_{i-\ell-1}\in\mathcal{G},\ s(\pi)=F\}
have different labels. Then, the conclusion follows by using the
k
-regularity in (
3.4
).
∎

We introduce a probability measure on
\mathcal{S}
.

Definition 3.11
.

For every
i\geq\ell
, the distribution
\mu_{i}

on
\mathcal{S}={\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathbf{a}}\times\mathcal{V}
is defined as

\mu_{i}(\{(\mathbf{b},F)\}):=\dfrac{|\{w\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{i}(\Sigma):\ \mathbf{f}((w)_{g})\equiv\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a},\ F(w)=F\}|}{|\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{i}(\Sigma)|}

for any
(\mathbf{b},F)\in\mathcal{S}
.

We will denote
\mu_{i}(\mathbf{b},F):=\mu_{i}(\{(\mathbf{b},F)\})
. By understanding the powers of the matrix
M_{i}
, we can describe the evolution of the measure
\mu_{i+np}
with respect to
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Proposition 3.12
.

For any
i,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
s=(\mathbf{b},F),s^{\prime}=(\mathbf{b}^{\prime},F^{\prime})\in\mathcal{S}
,

(3.5)

M_{i}^{n}(s,s^{\prime})=k^{-pn}|\{w\in\mathcal{L}^{pn}(\Sigma):\ \mathbf{f}(g^{i}(w)_{g})\equiv\textbf{b}^{\prime}-\textbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \textbf{a},\ F\overset{w}{\to}F^{\prime}\}|.

Moreover, for every
i\geq\ell
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

(3.6)

\mu_{i+np}=\mu_{i}M_{i}^{n}.

Proof.

We proceed by induction to show (
3.5
). Let
s=(\mathbf{b},F),s^{\prime}=(\mathbf{b}^{\prime},F^{\prime})\in\mathcal{S}
. The case
n=1
holds by definition. Suppose that the result holds for some
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, so we have to prove the result for
n+1
. Since

M_{i}^{n+1}(s,s^{\prime})=\sum_{\tilde{s}\in\mathcal{S}}M_{i}^{n}(s,\tilde{s})M_{i}(\tilde{s},s^{\prime}),

from the induction hypotheses, it will be sufficient to prove that

(3.7)

\begin{split}&\{w\in\mathcal{L}^{p(n+1)}(\Sigma):\ \mathbf{f}(g^{i}(w)_{g})\equiv\textbf{b}^{\prime}-\textbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \textbf{a},\ F\overset{w}{\to}F^{\prime}\}\\
&=\bigcup_{(\tilde{\mathbf{b}},\tilde{F})\in\mathcal{S}}\Big(\{x\in\mathcal{L}^{pn}(\Sigma):\ \mathbf{f}(g^{i}(x)_{g})\equiv\tilde{\textbf{b}}-\textbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \textbf{a},\ F\overset{x}{\to}\tilde{F}\}\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt\times\{y\in\mathcal{L}^{p}(\Sigma):\ \mathbf{f}(g^{i}(w)_{g})\equiv\textbf{b}^{\prime}-\tilde{\textbf{b}}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \textbf{a},\ \tilde{F}\overset{y}{\to}F^{\prime}\}\Big).\end{split}

Let
w\in\mathcal{L}^{p(n+1)}
in the left hand side of (
3.7
), and consider the decomposition
w=(x,y)
, where
x=w_{0}\ldots w_{pn-1}
and
y=w_{pn}\ldots w_{n(p+1)-1}
. Since
\mathbf{f}(g^{i}(w)_{g})=\mathbf{f}(g^{i}(x)_{g})+\mathbf{f}(g^{i+pn}(y)_{g}),
the eventual periodicity leads to

\mathbf{f}(g^{i}(w)_{g})\equiv_{\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{b}^{\prime}-\mathbf{b}\text{ if and only if }\mathbf{f}(g^{i}(x)_{g})\equiv_{\mathbf{a}}\tilde{\mathbf{b}}-\mathbf{b}\text{ and }\mathbf{f}(g^{i}(y)_{g})\equiv_{\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{b}^{\prime}-\tilde{\mathbf{b}},

where
\tilde{\mathbf{b}}:=\mathbf{f}(g^{i}(x)_{g})+\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a}
. In addition,
F\overset{w}{\to}F^{\prime}
is equivalent to the existence of a path
\pi
in
\mathcal{G}
of length
(n+1)p
from
F
to
F^{\prime}
labelled by
w
. If we denote by
\tilde{F}
the terminal node for the subpath
\pi_{0}\ldots\pi_{np-1}
,
F\overset{w}{\to}F^{\prime}\text{ if and only if }F\overset{x}{\to}\tilde{F}\text{ and }\tilde{F}\overset{y}{\to}F^{\prime}
. Thus, we conclude (
3.7
). On the other hand,

\Big(\mu_{i}M^{n}_{i}\Big)(s)=\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\mu_{i}(s^{\prime})M_{i}^{n}(s^{\prime},s).

By
Proposition

3.10
,
k^{pn}|\mathcal{L}^{i}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}(\Sigma)|=|\mathcal{L}^{i+np}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}(\Sigma)|
. Hence, using (
3.5
), we can conclude (
3.6
) by showing that

(3.8)

\begin{split}&\{w\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{i+(n+1)p}(\Sigma):\ \mathbf{f}((w)_{g})\equiv\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a},\ F(w)=F\}\\
&=\bigcup_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\Big(\{x\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{i}(\Sigma):\ \mathbf{f}((w)_{g})\equiv\mathbf{b}^{\prime}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a},\ F(w)=F^{\prime}\}\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\times\{y\in\mathcal{L}^{pn}(\Sigma):\ \mathbf{f}(g^{i}(y)_{g})\equiv\textbf{b}-\textbf{b}^{\prime}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \textbf{a},\ F^{\prime}\overset{y}{\to}F\}\Big).\end{split}

If
w\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{i+(n+1)p}(\Sigma)
, it is straightforward that
w_{0}\ldots w_{i-1}\in\mathcal{L}^{i}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}(\Sigma)
. The rest of the proof follows by replicating the argument used to prove (
3.7
).
∎

From the previous results, we can characterize the distribution using Markov chains.

Definition 3.13
.

Let
g,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{f},p,\ell
and
\Sigma
as defined in
Remark

2
, and assume that the subshift
\Sigma
is
k
-regular (where
k\geq 2
). For
i\geq\ell
, we denote by
X^{i}=(X^{i}_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
a Markov chain on the state space
\mathcal{S}
with transition matrix
M_{i}
(
Definition

3.7
) and initial distribution
\mu_{i}
(
Definition

3.11
). We denote by
\mathbb{P}
the probability measure associated to the process. Notice that
X^{i}=X^{i}(g,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{f},p,\ell,\Sigma)
, but we omit this dependency in the notation for simplicity.

Remark 3
.

Let
\Sigma\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
be a 1-step shift of finite type. For any
w=w_{0}\ldots w_{n}\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
,
F(w)=F(w_{n})
. Thus,
\mathcal{V}_{\Sigma}=\{F(d):d\in\mathcal{D}\}.
Note that the cover presented in
Remark

1
is the Fischer cover for
\Sigma
, except if there exist
d\neq d^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}
such that
F(d)=F(d^{\prime})
. In such a case, the symbols
d
and
d^{\prime}
are associated to the same node in the Fischer cover.

If the graph of
Remark

1
is irreducible and regular, we can use this cover to make the construction of the Markov chains instead of the Fischer cover. The only distinction is that different symbols are forced to be associated with different nodes, but the rest of the construction follows immediately. It is easy to see that the results that we will present in
Section

4
also hold when using this cover.

4.
From Markov chains to uniform distribution

Throughout this section, we will work within the framework outlined in
Remark

2
, and assuming that
\Sigma
is a
k
-regular subshift for some
k\geq 2
.
The connection between the Markov chains introduced in
Definition

3.13
and the distribution of words in the
\ell
-restricted language
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}(\Sigma)
is straightforward from
Eq.

3.6
.

Corollary 4.1
.

For any
i\geq\ell
,
(\mathbf{b},F)\in\mathcal{S}
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\mathbb{P}\Big(X^{i}_{n}=(\mathbf{b},F)\Big)=\dfrac{|\{w\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{i+np}(\Sigma):\ \mathbf{f}((w)_{g})\equiv\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a},\ F(w)=F\}|}{|\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{i+np}(\Sigma)|}.

Proof.

Given the Markov chain
X^{i}
with initial distribution
\mu_{i}
and transition matrix
M_{i}
, for every
(\mathbf{b},F)\in\mathcal{S}
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\mathbb{P}\Big(X^{i}_{n}=(\mathbf{b},F)\Big)=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\mu_{i}(s)M_{i}\Big(s,(\mathbf{b},F)\Big).

The conclusion follows from
Definition

3.11
and (
3.6
) of
Eq.

3.6
.
∎

Since we are restricted to words in
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}(\Sigma)\subseteq\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
, it is natural to consider the subset of
A_{\Sigma}
defined by

A_{\Sigma,\ell}:=\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{n}w_{i}g^{i}:\ n\geq\ell-1,\ w_{0}\cdots w_{n}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}(\Sigma)\right\}.

Through
X_{n}^{i}
, the distribution of integers represented by words in
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{i+np}
can be understood. By running different Markov chains for
i=\ell,\ell+1,\ldots,\ell+(p-1)
, we can recover the distribution in residue classes for
A_{\Sigma,\ell}
. Furthermore, as
\ell
increases, the set difference
A_{\Sigma}\setminus A_{\Sigma,\ell}
becomes negligible in terms of relative density, allowing us to recover the distribution for
A_{\Sigma}
.

In order to ensure the convergence of the Markov chains, we introduce the following definition.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
g,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{f},p,\ell
and
\Sigma
as defined in
Remark

2
, and assume that the subshift
\Sigma
is regular. We say that the
Markov condition
holds if
M_{i}
(
Definition

3.7
) is irreducible and aperiodic for every
i\geq\ell
.

If
i\geq\ell
,
M_{i}=M_{i+np}
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, so the Markov condition it is equivalent to show that
M_{i}
is irreducible and aperiodic for
i\in\{\ell,\cdots,\ell+(p-1)\}
. Notice that the Markov condition implicitly assumes that
\Sigma
is mixing (while the construction only requires transitivity). Assuming the convergence of the Markov chains and using
Corollary

4.1
, we devote the rest of the section to obtain the uniform distribution
\mathbf{f}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a}
for the
\times g
-invariant set
A_{\Sigma}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

If the Markov condition holds, there exist
C>0
and
\rho\in(0,1)
such that

(4.1)

\left|\dfrac{|\{w\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{n}(\Sigma):\ \mathbf{f}((w)_{g})\equiv\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a},\ F(w)=F\}|}{|\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{n}(\Sigma)|}-\dfrac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|}\right|\leq C\rho^{n}

for every
n\geq\ell
and
(\mathbf{b},F)\in\mathcal{S}
. In addition,

(4.2)

\left|\dfrac{|\{w\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{n}(\Sigma):\ \mathbf{f}((w)_{g})\equiv\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a},\ F(w)=F\}|}{|\{w\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{n}(\Sigma):\ F(w)=F\}|}-\dfrac{1}{|\mathbf{a}|}\right|\leq C\rho^{n}.

Proof.

Notice that the uniform measure
1/|\mathcal{S}|
is invariant for
M_{i}
by
Proposition

3.8
. From
Theorem

2.1
, for every
i\in\{\ell,\cdots,\ell+p-1\}
there exist
C_{i}>0
and
\rho_{i}\in(0,1)
such that

(4.3)

\Big|\mathbb{P}\Big(X_{n}^{i}=(\mathbf{b},F)\Big)-\dfrac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|}\Big|\leq C_{i}\rho_{i}^{n}

for every
(\mathbf{b},F)\in\mathcal{S}
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}.
By choosing
C^{\prime}:=\max_{i}\{C_{i}\rho_{i}^{-i/p}\}
and
\rho:=\max_{i}\{\rho_{i}^{1/p}\}
,

(4.4)

\Big|\mathbb{P}\Big(X_{n}^{i}=(\mathbf{b},F)\Big)-\dfrac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|}\Big|\leq C^{\prime}\rho^{np+i}

for every
i\in\{\ell,\cdots,\ell+(p-1)\}
. From
Corollary

4.1
, we can write

\left|\dfrac{|\{w\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{np+i}(\Sigma):\ \mathbf{f}((w)_{g})\equiv\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a};\ F(w)=F\}|}{|\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{np+i}(\Sigma)|}-\dfrac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|}\right|\leq C^{\prime}\rho^{np+i}.

Since it holds for every
i\in\{\ell,\cdots,\ell+(p-1)\}
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, (
4.1
) is obtained. Equivalently, for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

(4.5)

\begin{split}|\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{n}(\Sigma)|\left(\dfrac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|}-C^{\prime}\rho^{n}\right)&\leq|\{w\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{n}(\Sigma):\ \mathbf{f}((w)_{g})\equiv\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a};\ F(w)=F\}|\\
&\leq|\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{n}(\Sigma)|\left(\dfrac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|}+C^{\prime}\rho^{n}\right).\end{split}

By summing (
4.5
) over all
\mathbf{b}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathbf{a}}
, we obtain

(4.6)

|\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{n}(\Sigma)|\left(\dfrac{1}{|\mathcal{V}|}-C^{\prime}|\mathbf{a}|\rho^{n}\right)\leq|\{w\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{n}(\Sigma):F(w)=F\}|.

Let
n_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
large enough such that
1-C^{\prime}|\mathcal{S}|\rho^{n_{0}}>1/2
. Combining (
4.5
) and (
4.6
),

(4.7)

\begin{split}&|\{w\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{n}(\Sigma):\ \mathbf{f}((w)_{g})\equiv\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a};\ F(w)=F\}|\\
&\leq\left(\dfrac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|}+C^{\prime}\rho^{n}\right)\left(\dfrac{1}{|\mathcal{V}|}-C^{\prime}|\mathbf{a}|\rho^{n}\right)^{-1}|\{w\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{n}(\Sigma):F(w)=F\}|\end{split}

for every
n\geq n_{0}
. A quick computation leads to

\left(\dfrac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|}+C^{\prime}\rho^{n}\right)\left(\dfrac{1}{|\mathcal{V}|}-C^{\prime}|\mathbf{a}|\rho^{n}\right)^{-1}=\dfrac{1}{|\mathbf{a}|}+2C^{\prime}|\mathcal{V}|\dfrac{\rho^{n}}{1-C^{\prime}|\mathcal{S}|\rho^{n}}\leq\dfrac{1}{|\mathbf{a}|}+4C^{\prime}|\mathcal{V}|\rho^{n}.

Using the previous estimation in (
4.7
),

\dfrac{|\{w\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{n}(\Sigma):\ \mathbf{f}((w)_{g})\equiv_{\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{b};\ F(w)=F\}|}{|\{w\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{n}(\Sigma):F(w)=F\}|}\leq\dfrac{1}{|\mathbf{a}|}+4C^{\prime}|\mathcal{V}|\rho^{n}

for every
n\geq n_{0}
.
With the obvious modifications, we can obtain in an analogous way that

\dfrac{|\{w\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{n}(\Sigma):\ \mathbf{f}((w)_{g})\equiv_{\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{b};\ F(w)=F\}|}{|\{w\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{n}(\Sigma):F(w)=F\}|}\geq\dfrac{1}{|\mathbf{a}|}-\dfrac{2C^{\prime}|\mathcal{V}|\rho^{n}}{1+C^{\prime}|\mathcal{S}|\rho^{n}}\geq\dfrac{1}{|\mathbf{a}|}-4C^{\prime}|\mathcal{V}|\rho^{n}.

The conclusion follows by choosing
C>0
large enough.
∎

When
0\notin\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
, each element of
A_{\Sigma,\ell}
has a unique representation in the language, which is no longer true if
0\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
. This lack of uniqueness can introduce complications when analysing the distribution of integers, as multiple representations may correspond to the same integer.
We deal with this situation in the following result by using follower sets and the estimation (
4.2
).

Lemma 4.4
.

If the Markov condition holds, there exist
C>0
and
\rho\in(0,1)
such that

\left|\dfrac{|\{n\in A_{\Sigma,\ell}:\ \mathbf{f}(n)\equiv\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a}\}\cap[0,g^{m})|}{|A_{\Sigma,\ell}\cap[0,g^{m})|}-\dfrac{1}{|\mathbf{a}|}\right|\leq C\rho^{m}

for every
m\geq\ell
and
(\mathbf{b},F)\in\mathcal{S}
.

Proof.

For
\mathbf{b}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathbf{a}}
,
F\in\mathcal{V}
and
t\geq\ell
, we denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{t}(\mathbf{b},F)

\displaystyle:=\{w\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{t}(\Sigma):\ \mathbf{f}((w)_{g})\equiv\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a},\ F(w)=F\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{t}(\mathbf{b})

\displaystyle:=\{w\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{t}(\Sigma):\ \mathbf{f}((w)_{g})\equiv\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a}\},\text{ and }

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{t}(F)

\displaystyle:=\{w\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{t}(\Sigma):\ F(w)=F\}.

If two different words represent the same integer, they must have different length. Additionally, any word of length
t
ending with the digit
0
represents an integer that can also be represented by a word of length
t-1
. Therefore,

(4.8)

\begin{split}&|\{n\in A_{\Sigma,\ell}:\ \mathbf{f}(n)\equiv\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a}\}\cap[0,g^{m})|\\
&=|\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{\ell}(\mathbf{b})|+\sum_{t=\ell+1}^{m}|\{w\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{t}:\ \mathbf{f}((w)_{g})\equiv\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a},\ w_{t-1}\neq 0\}|.\end{split}

Any word of length
t
that ends at
0
must be an extension by
0
of a word
w
of length
t-1
. Recalling that
\mathbf{f}(0)=0
, and since
0
can extend
w
if and only if
0\in F(w)
, it follows

(4.9)

|\{w\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{t}:\ \mathbf{f}((w)_{g})\equiv\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a};\ w_{t-1}\neq 0\}|=|\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{t}(\mathbf{b})|-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{V}}|\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{t-1}(\mathbf{b},F)|\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{F}(0).

Let
C>0
and
\rho\in(0,1)
given by
Eq.

4.2
. From (
4.1
),

(4.10)

|\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{t}(\mathbf{b})|\leq|\mathcal{L}^{t}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}|\left(\dfrac{1}{|\mathbf{a}|}+C|\mathcal{V}|\rho^{n}\right).

From (
4.9
), (
4.10
) and the estimation provided by (
4.2
) of
Eq.

4.2
for
|\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{t-1}(\mathbf{b},F)|
,

\displaystyle|\{w\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{t}:\ \mathbf{f}((w)_{g})\equiv\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a};\ w_{t-1}\neq 0\}|

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{|\mathbf{a}|}\left(|\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{t}|-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{V}}|\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{t-1}(F)|\mathbbm{1}_{F}(0)\right)+C|\mathcal{V}|\rho^{t}|\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{t}|+C\sum_{F\in\mathcal{V}}\rho^{t-1}|\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{t-1}(F)|\mathbbm{1}_{F}(0)

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{|\mathbf{a}|}|\{w\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{t}:\ w_{t-1}\neq 0\}|+C^{\prime}\rho^{t}|\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{t}|,

for some constant
C^{\prime}>C
. Using the previous estimation and (
4.10
) in (
4.8
),

(4.11)

\begin{split}&|\{n\in A_{\Sigma,\ell}:\ \mathbf{f}(n)\equiv\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a}\}\cap[0,g^{m})|\\
&\leq\dfrac{1}{|\mathbf{a}|}\left(|\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{\ell}|+\sum_{t=\ell+1}^{m}|\{w\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{t}:\ w_{t-1}\neq 0\}|\right)+C\rho^{\ell}|\mathcal{L}^{\ell}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}|+C^{\prime}\sum_{t=\ell+1}^{m}\rho^{t}|\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{t}|\\
&=\dfrac{1}{|\mathbf{a}|}|A_{\Sigma,\ell}\cap[0,g^{m})|+C^{\prime}\sum_{t=\ell}^{m}\rho^{t}|\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{t}|.\end{split}

Since
|A_{\Sigma,\ell}\cap[0,g^{m})|\geq|\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{m}|
,
Proposition

3.10
leads to

|A_{\Sigma,\ell}\cap[0,g^{m})|^{-1}\sum_{t=\ell}^{m}\rho^{t}|\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{t}|\leq\sum_{t=\ell}^{m}\rho^{t}\dfrac{|\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{t}|}{|\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{m}|}\leq\sum_{t=\ell}^{m}\rho^{t}k^{-(m-t)}\leq m\rho^{m},

where in the last inequality we assumed that
k^{-1}\leq\rho
(otherwise, we change
\rho
by
k^{-1}
). Hence, it follows from (
4.11
) that

\dfrac{|\{n\in A_{\Sigma,\ell}:\ \mathbf{f}(n)\equiv\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a}\}\cap[0,g^{m})|}{|A_{\Sigma,\ell}\cap[0,g^{m})|}-\dfrac{1}{|\mathbf{a}|}\leq C^{\prime}m\rho^{m}.

Following the same approach, the analogue lower inequality can be obtained. If
r=\rho^{1/2}
and
C^{\prime\prime}>0
is large enough, it holds
C^{\prime}m\rho^{m}\leq C^{\prime\prime}r^{m}
for all
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, concluding the result.
∎

Let
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and consider its expansion in base
g
,
N=d_{0}+d_{1}g+\ldots+w_{m}g^{m}
, where
w_{m}\neq 0
. We can write
[0,N)=[0,g^{m})\cup[g^{m},N)
, and using
Lemma

4.4
, the distribution can be recovered when restricted to the interval
[0,g^{m})
. The next lemma focuses on addressing the distribution in the interval
[g^{m},N)
.

Lemma 4.5
.

If the Markov condition holds, there exist
C>0
and
\rho\in(0,1)
such that for all
t,m\in{\mathbb{N}}
(with
t<m
), and for any sequence of digits
d_{t},d_{t+1},\ldots,d_{m}\in\{0,\cdots,g-1\}
where
d_{m}\neq 0
, defining
M:=d_{m}g^{m}+\cdots+d_{t+1}g^{t+1}
and assuming
A_{\Sigma,\ell}\cap[M,M+d_{t}g^{t})\neq\emptyset
, the following estimation holds for every
\mathbf{b}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathbf{a}}:

\left|\dfrac{|\{n\in A_{\Sigma,\ell}:\ \mathbf{f}(n)\equiv\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a}\}\cap[M,M+d_{t}g^{t})|}{|A_{\Sigma,\ell}\cap[M,M+d_{t}g^{t})|}-\dfrac{1}{|\mathbf{a}|}\right|\leq C\rho^{t}.

Proof.

Let
C
and
\rho
be as given by
Eq.

4.2
, and let
d\in\{0,\cdots,g-1\}
. Let

(4.12)

x\in\{n\in A_{\Sigma,\ell}:\ \mathbf{f}(n)\equiv\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a}\}\cap[M+dg^{t},M+(d+1)g^{t}).

Since
x\in[M+dg^{t},M+(d+1)g^{t})
, the representation of
x
can be written as
x=(x_{0}\cdots x_{m})_{g}
, where
x_{t}=d
and
x_{t+1}\cdots x_{m}=d_{t+1}\cdots d_{m}
. Therefore, using the
g
-additivity, we can see that
\mathbf{f}(x)\equiv\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a}
is equivalent to

\mathbf{f}((x_{0}\cdots x_{t-1})_{g})\equiv\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{f}(dg^{t}+d_{t+1}g^{t+1}+\cdots+d_{m}g^{m})\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a}=:\tilde{\mathbf{b}}(d).

Additionally, as
d_{m}\neq 0
,
x=(x_{0}\cdots x_{t-1}dd_{t+1}\cdots d_{m})_{g}\in A_{\Sigma,\ell}
is equivalent to

x_{0}\cdots x_{t-1}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}(\Sigma)\ \text{and}\ dd_{t+1}\cdots d_{m}\in F(x_{0}\cdots x_{t-1}).

Therefore, (
4.12
) is equivalent to

x_{0}\cdots x_{t-1}\in\{w\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{t}(\Sigma):\ \mathbf{f}((w)_{g})\equiv\tilde{\mathbf{b}}(d)\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a}\}\text{ and }dd_{t+1}\cdots d_{m}\in F(x_{0}\cdots x_{t-1}).

Using this equivalence and the estimation (
4.2
) of
Lemma

4.5
,

(4.13)

\begin{split}&|\{n\in A_{\Sigma,\ell}:\ \mathbf{f}(n)\equiv\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a}\}\cap[M+dg^{t},M+(d+1)g^{t})|\\
&=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{V}}|\{w\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{t}(\Sigma):\ \mathbf{f}((w)_{g})\equiv\tilde{\mathbf{b}}(d)\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a};\ F(w)=F\}|\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{F}\left(dd_{t+1}\cdots d_{m}\right)\\
&\leq\sum_{F\in\mathcal{V}}\left(\dfrac{1}{|\mathbf{a}|}+C\rho^{t}\right)|\{w\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{t}(\Sigma):\ F(w)=F\}|\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{F}(dd_{t+1}\cdots d_{m}).\end{split}

With an analogous argument as above, we can obtain that

\sum_{F\in\mathcal{V}}|\{w\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{t}(\Sigma):F(w)=F\}|\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{F}(dd_{t+1}\cdots d_{m})=|A_{\Sigma,\ell}\cap[M+dg^{t},M+(d+1)g^{t})|.

From the assumption
A_{\Sigma,\ell}\cap[M,M+d_{t}g^{t})\neq\emptyset
, it is clear that
d_{t}\geq 1
. Thus, we can use the previous identity in (
4.13
), and then sum from
d=0
to
d_{t}-1
, obtaining

\displaystyle|\{n\in A_{\Sigma,\ell}:\ \mathbf{f}(n)\equiv_{\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{b}\}\cap[M,M+d_{t}g^{t})|\leq\left(\dfrac{1}{|\mathbf{a}|}+C\rho^{t}\right)|A_{\Sigma,\ell}\cap[M,M+d_{t}g^{t})|.

With a similar argument, the lower inequality can be obtained, concluding the result.
∎

Putting the previous results together, the distribution for the set
A_{\Sigma,\ell}
can be estimated for an arbitrary interval
[0,N)
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
g\geq 2
,
\mathbf{a}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{r}
, and
\mathbf{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{r})
be a collection of
g
-additive functions eventually periodic with respect to
\mathbf{a}
.
Let
\Sigma\subseteq\{0,\cdots,g-1\}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
be a mixing, sofic and regular subshift, and
(p,\ell)
be an eventual period such that
\ell
is at least the length of the shortest synchronizing word.

If the Markov condition holds, there exists
\gamma>0
such that

\dfrac{|\{n\in A_{\Sigma,\ell}:\ \mathbf{f}(n)\equiv\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a}\}\cap[0,N)|}{|A_{\Sigma,\ell}\cap[0,N)|}=\frac{1}{a_{1}\cdot a_{2}\cdots a_{r}}+O(N^{-\gamma})

for every
\mathbf{b}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathbf{a}}
.

Proof.

Since the Markov condition holds, we can apply
Lemma

4.4
and
Lemma

4.5
with constants
C>0
and
\rho\in(0,1)
. Let
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and its base-
g
expansion
N=d_{m}g^{m}+\ldots+d_{1}g+d_{0},

where
d_{m}\neq 0
. For every
t\in\{0,\cdots,m\}
, we define
N(t):=d_{m}g^{m}+\ldots+d_{t}g^{t}
. Noting

(4.14)

[0,N)=[0,g^{m})\cup[g^{m},d_{m}g^{m})\cup\left(\bigcup_{t=0}^{m-1}[N(t+1),N(t))\right),

we aim to estimate
|\{n\in A_{\Sigma,\ell}:\ \mathbf{f}(n)\equiv\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a}\}\cap I|
for each interval
I
in the previous decomposition of
[0,N)
. The interval
[0,g^{m})
can be estimated from
Lemma

4.4
, and
[N(t+1),N(t))
from
Lemma

4.5
as
N(t)=N(t+1)+d_{t}g^{t}
. On the other hand, the interval
[g^{m},d_{m}g^{m})
can be handled in the same way as
Lemma

4.5
by writing
[g^{m},g^{m}+(d_{m}-1)g^{m})
. Putting these estimates together, we can obtain the upper bound

(4.15)

\begin{split}&\dfrac{|\{n\in A_{\Sigma,\ell}:\ \mathbf{f}(n)\equiv\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a}\}\cap[0,N)|}{|A_{\Sigma,\ell}\cap[0,N)|}\leq\dfrac{1}{|\mathbf{a}|}+CR(m),\end{split}

where

R(m):=\rho^{m}\dfrac{|A_{\Sigma,\ell}\cap[0,g^{m})|}{|A_{\Sigma,\ell}\cap[0,N)|}+\rho^{m}\dfrac{|A_{\Sigma,\ell}\cap[g^{m},d_{m}g^{m})|}{|A_{\Sigma,\ell}\cap[0,N)|}+\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}\rho^{t}\dfrac{|A_{\Sigma,\ell}\cap[N(t+1),N(t))|}{|A_{\Sigma,\ell}\cap[0,N)|}.

If
A_{\Sigma,\ell}\cap[N(t+1),N(t))\neq\emptyset
(in particular,
d_{t}\neq 0
) each element of
A_{\Sigma,\ell}\cap[N(t+1),N(t))
has a base-
g
expansion of length
m+1
, where the
(m-t)
most significant digits are
d_{t+1}\cdots d_{m}
. Then, they may differ only in the
(t+1)
least significant digits. Therefore,

|A_{\Sigma,\ell}\cap[N(t+1),N(t))|\leq|\mathcal{L}^{t+1}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}(\Sigma)|.

Let
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
the regularity of
\Sigma
. Since
|A_{\Sigma,\ell}\cap[0,N)|\geq|\mathcal{L}^{m}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}(\Sigma)|
, we can get from the above estimation and
Proposition

3.10
that

\dfrac{|A_{\Sigma,\ell}\cap[N(t+1),N(t))|}{|A_{\Sigma,\ell}\cap[0,N)|}\leq\dfrac{|\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{\ell}(\Sigma)|k^{t+1-\ell}}{|\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{\ell}(\Sigma)|k^{m-\ell}}=k^{t+1-m}\leq\rho^{m-t-1},

where we assumed that
k^{-1}\leq\rho
(otherwise, replace
\rho
by
k^{-1}
).
Therefore, using the previous estimation in (
4.15
),

\dfrac{|\{n\in A_{\Sigma,\ell}:\ \mathbf{f}(n)\equiv\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a}\}\cap[0,N)|}{|A_{\Sigma,\ell}\cap[0,N)|}-\dfrac{1}{|\textbf{a}|}\leq C(2+\rho^{-1}m)\rho^{m}.

The lower inequality can be obtained following the same argument. Considering
r=\rho^{1/2}
and
C^{\prime}
large enough,
C^{\prime}r^{m}>C(2+\rho^{-1}m)\rho^{m}
for all
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. The conclusion follows as
m\log(g)\leq\log(N)\leq(m+1)\log(g).

∎

Remark 4
.

When
\Sigma
is a shift of finite type,
|A_{\Sigma}\setminus A_{\Sigma,\ell}|
is finite for all
\ell
large enough. In such a case, we can replace
A_{\Sigma,\ell}
by
A_{\Sigma}
in
Proposition

4.6
. Moreover, while the choice of the initial distribution for the Markov chains is important to recover
A_{\Sigma,\ell}
in
Proposition

4.6
, the convergence of the Markov chains remains independent of this choice. Therefore, different sets of integers can be considered by adjusting the initial distribution.

The next step is to transfer the distribution from
A_{\Sigma,\ell}
to the multiplicatively invariant set
A_{\Sigma}
. To achieve this, it suffices to show that the difference between these two sets becomes asymptotically negligible in size. The main ingredient to conclude that is the following proposition.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
\Sigma
be a transitive, sofic and regular subshift. Then,

(4.16)

\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\dfrac{|\mathcal{L}^{\ell}(\Sigma)\setminus\mathcal{L}^{\ell}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}(\Sigma)|}{|\mathcal{L}^{\ell}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}(\Sigma)|}=0.

Proof.

Let
k\geq 2
be the regularity of
\Sigma
. For any
y\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
such that
F(y)\in\mathcal{V}
, every extension
\tilde{y}\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
of
y
satisfies
F(\tilde{y})\in\mathcal{V}
. Then, for every
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
i\geq\ell
,
\mathcal{L}^{i}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{i}_{\mathcal{V},i}
.
From
Proposition

3.10
, it follows

(4.17)

|\mathcal{L}^{i}_{\mathcal{V},i}|\geq|\mathcal{L}^{i}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}|=|\mathcal{L}^{\ell}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}|k^{i-\ell}.

Since
\Sigma
is transitive, the Fischer cover is an irreducible graph, so we can find
q\in{\mathbb{N}}
large enough such that, for every node
F\in\mathcal{V}
, there is a path
\pi
of length
q
starting at
F
, where
\lambda(\pi)
is a synchronizing word. To see this, notice that every word can be extended to a synchronizing word, and we can choose a
q
common for every node as any extension of a synchronizing word is synchronizing.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
x\in\mathcal{L}^{\ell+nq}\setminus\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell+nq}^{\ell+nq}
. Then,
x
is in the language, but
F(x)\notin\mathcal{V}
. In particular,
F(x_{0}\cdots x_{\ell-1})\notin\mathcal{V}
and the word
x_{\ell}\cdots x_{\ell+nq-1}
is not synchronizing. Thus,

(4.18)

\left|\mathcal{L}^{\ell+nq}\setminus\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell+nq}^{\ell+nq}\right|\leq\left|\mathcal{L}^{\ell}\setminus\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{\ell}\right|\times|\{w\in\mathcal{L}^{nq}(\Sigma):\ \text{$w$ is not synchronizing}\}|.

For each node
F\in\mathcal{V}
, there are
k^{qn}
paths of length
qn
in
\mathcal{G}
starting at
F
. From those paths, there are at most
(k^{q}-1)^{n}
paths labelled by non-synchronizing words, as for every node
F
we can find at least one path starting at
F
of length
q
that represents a synchronizing word. From this idea and (
4.18
),

(4.19)

\left|\mathcal{L}^{\ell+nq}\setminus\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell+nq}^{\ell+nq}\right|\leq\left|\mathcal{L}^{\ell}\setminus\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}^{\ell}\right|\times|\mathcal{V}|(k^{q}-1)^{n}.

By defining

C:=|\mathcal{V}|\max_{j=\ell,\cdots,\ell+(q-1)}\dfrac{\left|\mathcal{L}^{j}\setminus\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{V},j}^{j}\right|}{|\mathcal{L}^{j}_{\mathcal{V},j}|},

and combining (
4.17
) with (
4.19
),

\dfrac{|\mathcal{L}^{i}\setminus\mathcal{L}^{i}_{\mathcal{V},i}|}{|\mathcal{L}^{i}_{\mathcal{V},i}|}\leq C\dfrac{(k^{q}-1)^{\left\lfloor{i/q}\right\rfloor}}{k^{q{\left\lfloor{i/q}\right\rfloor}}}.

The result follows by taking
i\to\infty
.

∎

We can now state the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
g\geq 2
,
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{r}
, and
\mathbf{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{r})
be a collection of
g
-additive functions eventually periodic with respect to
\mathbf{a}
.
Let
\Sigma\subseteq\{0,\cdots,g-1\}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
be a mixing, sofic and regular subshift, and
p
be an eventual period. If the Markov condition holds,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{|\{n\in A_{\Sigma}:\ \mathbf{f}(n)\equiv\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a}\}\cap[0,N)|}{|A_{\Sigma}\cap[0,N)|}=\frac{1}{a_{1}\cdot a_{2}\cdots a_{r}}

for every
\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{r})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathbf{a}}
.

Proof.

Let
(p,\ell)
be an eventual period and
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
. For every
\mathbf{b}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathbf{a}}
, define

\alpha(\ell,N):=\dfrac{|(A_{\Sigma}\setminus A_{\Sigma,\ell})\cap[0,N)|}{|A_{\Sigma,\ell}\cap[0,N)|},\ L(\mathbf{b},\ell,N):=\dfrac{|\{n\in A_{\Sigma,\ell}:\ \mathbf{f}(n)\equiv\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a}\}\cap[0,N)|}{|A_{\Sigma,\ell}\cap[0,N)|}.

A simple computation leads to the following inequality:

\dfrac{1}{1+\alpha(\ell,N)}L(\mathbf{b},\ell,N)\leq\dfrac{|\{n\in A_{\Sigma}:\ \mathbf{f}(n)\equiv\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a}\}\cap[0,N)|}{|A_{\Sigma}\cap[0,N)|}\leq L(\mathbf{b},\ell,N)+\alpha(\ell,N).

The
Proposition

4.6
gives that
L(\mathbf{b},\ell,N)\to 1/|\mathbf{a}|
when
N\to\infty
. Therefore, to conclude the theorem, it will be enough to show that

(4.20)

\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\alpha(\ell,N)=0.

For this purpose, we will estimate
|(A_{\Sigma}\setminus A_{\Sigma,\ell})\cap[0,N)|
. For
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
m
be such that
g^{m}\leq N<g^{m+1}
. If
n\in(A_{\Sigma}\setminus A_{\Sigma,\ell})\cap[g^{t},g^{t+1})
for some
\ell\leq t\leq m
,
n
can be represented by a word in
\mathcal{L}^{t+1}(\Sigma)\setminus\mathcal{L}^{t+1}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}(\Sigma).
Thus, we can estimate

(4.21)

\dfrac{|(A_{\Sigma}\setminus A_{\Sigma,\ell})\cap[g^{\ell},g^{m+1})|}{|A_{\Sigma,\ell}\cap[g^{\ell},g^{m})|}\leq\dfrac{\sum_{i=\ell}^{m+1}|\mathcal{L}^{i}\setminus\mathcal{L}^{i}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}|}{|\mathcal{L}^{m}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}|}.

It is easy to see that each word in
\mathcal{L}^{i}\setminus\mathcal{L}^{i}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}
is an extension of a word in
\mathcal{L}^{\ell}\setminus\mathcal{L}^{\ell}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}
. Let
w\in\mathcal{L}^{\ell}\setminus\mathcal{L}^{\ell}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}
. Despite
F(w)\notin\mathcal{V}
,
F(w)
is union of elements in
\mathcal{V}
. To see this, just consider the union of all
F\in\mathcal{V}
such that the word
w
can be read in some path of the Fischer cover ending at
F
. Since the subshift is
k
-regular for some
k\geq 2
, from the Fischer cover we can deduce that there are at most
|\mathcal{V}|k^{i-\ell}
words of length
i
that are extension of
w
. In particular, each of those extensions is in
\mathcal{L}^{i}\setminus\mathcal{L}^{i}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}
. Therefore, each word in
\mathcal{L}^{\ell}\setminus\mathcal{L}^{\ell}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}
is extended to at most
|\mathcal{V}|k^{i-\ell}
words of length
i
, concluding from (
4.21
) that

\dfrac{|(A_{\Sigma}\setminus A_{\Sigma,\ell})\cap[g^{\ell},g^{m+1})|}{|A_{\Sigma,\ell}\cap[g^{\ell},g^{m})|}\leq\dfrac{|\mathcal{V}||\mathcal{L}^{\ell}\setminus\mathcal{L}^{\ell}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}|\sum_{i=\ell}^{m+1}k^{i-\ell}}{|\mathcal{L}^{\ell}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}|k^{m-\ell}}\leq|\mathcal{V}|\sum_{i=-1}^{\infty}k^{-i}\dfrac{|\mathcal{L}^{\ell}\setminus\mathcal{L}^{\ell}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}|}{|\mathcal{L}^{\ell}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}|}.

Therefore,

0\leq\limsup_{N\to\infty}\alpha(\ell,N)\leq\limsup_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{g^{\ell}+|(A_{\Sigma}\setminus A_{\Sigma,\ell})\cap[g^{\ell},g^{m+1})|}{|A_{\Sigma,\ell}\cap[g^{\ell},g^{m})|}\leq\dfrac{|\mathcal{L}^{\ell}\setminus\mathcal{L}^{\ell}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}|}{|\mathcal{L}^{\ell}_{\mathcal{V},\ell}|}|\mathcal{V}|\sum_{i=-1}^{\infty}k^{-i}.

It follows that
\displaystyle\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\alpha(\ell,N)=0
from the previous inequality and
Eq.

4.16
, concluding the result.
∎

5.
Applications to the distribution of multiplicatively invariant sets of integers

Let
A
be a multiplicatively invariant set of integers that admits a representation
A=A_{\Sigma}
, where
\Sigma
is a mixing, sofic and regular subshift. From
Theorem

4.8
, uniform distribution is obtained if the Markov chains are irreducible and aperiodic, which might be challenging to verify. In order to check the Markov condition, we can conveniently choose the period
p
and study the matrices
M_{i}
using the characterization provided by
Eq.

3.6
: For any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}
,

M_{i}^{n}(s,s^{\prime})=k^{-pn}|\{w\in\mathcal{L}^{pn}(\Sigma):\ \mathbf{f}(g^{i}(w)_{g})\equiv\textbf{b}^{\prime}-\textbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \textbf{a},\ F\overset{w}{\to}F^{\prime}\}|.

For
M_{i}
to be irreducible, we need to check that for all
\mathbf{b}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathbf{a}}
and
F,F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}
,

(5.1)

\text{There exists }w\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)\text{ such that }p\mid|w|,\ \mathbf{f}(g^{i}(w)_{g})\equiv\mathbf{b}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a}\text{ and }F\overset{w}{\to}F^{\prime}.

Assuming that
M_{i}
is irreducible, aperiodicity holds if there exists
F\in\mathcal{V}
such that

(5.2)

\gcd\Big\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}:\exists w\in\mathcal{L}^{pn}(\Sigma)\text{ such that }\mathbf{f}(g^{i}(w)_{g})\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbf{a}\text{ and }F\overset{w}{\to}F\Big\}=1.

In this section, we will study the function
\textbf{f}=(\id,S_{g})
, where
S_{g}
denote the function sum of digits in base
g
.

5.1.
Uniform distribution of integers with missing digits

Let
g\geq 2
be an integer, and let
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\{0,\cdots,g-1\}
be a subset of digits with at least two elements. We will write
\mathcal{D}=\{d_{1},\cdots,d_{t}\}
, where
d_{1}<d_{2}<\cdots<d_{t}
. Recalling that
\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}=A_{\Sigma}
for
\Sigma=\mathcal{D}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
, it is easy to see that the Fischer cover for
\Sigma
is the labelled graph with a unique node
F
, and for each
d\in\mathcal{D}
, there is a path from
F
to
F
labelled by
d
. Thus,
\Sigma
is mixing, sofic and regular, so we can apply
Theorem

4.8
. Moreover, the existence of a single follower set allows us to obviate this notion in the following discussion.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
A\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
be a
\times g
-invariant set, and
a,a^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}
. We say
A
is
jointly uniformly distributed
\ \mathrm{mod}\ (a,a^{\prime})
if

\lim_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{|\{n\in A:\ n\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a,\ S_{g}(n)\equiv b^{\prime}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}\}\cap[0,N)|}{|A\cap[0,N)|}=\dfrac{1}{aa^{\prime}}

for every
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{a},b^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{a^{\prime}}
.

From
Proposition

3.2
, we can find an eventual period of the form
(p,\ell)
for
\mathbf{f}=(\id,S_{g})
. Thus, checking that
M_{i}
is irreducible reduces to show that for all
(b,b^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{a^{\prime}}
,

(5.3)

\text{there is $n\in{\mathbb{N}}$ and }w\in\mathcal{D}^{np}\text{ such that }g^{i}(w)_{g}\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a\text{ and }S_{g}(w)\equiv b^{\prime}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}.

Similarly,
M_{i}
is aperiodic if

(5.4)

\gcd\Big\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}:\exists w\in\mathcal{D}^{pn}\text{ such that }(w)_{g}\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ a\text{ and }S_{g}(w)\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}\Big\}=1.

Aperiodicity in the case
\gcd(g,a)=1
follows immediately from the following result.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
g\geq 2
be an integer, and
a,a^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\gcd(g,a)=1
. Let
p:=a^{\prime}\phi(a(g-1))
, where
\phi
is the Euler’s totient function. Then, for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
w=d\cdots d\in\mathcal{D}^{np}
,
(w)_{g}\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
and
S_{g}(w)\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Notice that
\gcd(g,a(g-1))=1
, so the Euler’s theorem implies that
g^{p}\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ a(g-1)
. If
w=d\cdots d\in\mathcal{D}^{np}
,

(5.5)

(w)_{g}=d+dg+\cdots+dg^{np-1}=d\dfrac{g^{np}-1}{g-1}.

Since
g^{np}-1
is divisible by
a(g-1)
,
\dfrac{g^{np}-1}{g-1}\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
, concluding that
(w)_{g}\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
. On the other hand,
S_{g}(w)=npd=a^{\prime}(n\phi(a(g-1))d)\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}
.
∎

The following application of Bézout’s identity is fundamental to check the irreducibility of
M_{i}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
g\geq 2
be an integer, and let
\mathcal{D}=\{d_{1},\cdots,d_{t}\}
be a set of digits, where
d_{1}
is the smallest digit. Consider
a,a^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\gcd(g,a)=1
, and define
p:=a^{\prime}\phi(a(g-1))
, where
\phi
is the Euler’s totient function. Then, there exists a word
w\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{D}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}})
such that

|w|\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ p,\ (w)_{g}\equiv\delta\ \mathrm{mod}\ a,\text{ and }S_{g}(w)\equiv\delta\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime},

where
\delta:=\gcd(aa^{\prime},d_{2}-d_{1},\cdots,d_{t}-d_{1}).

Proof.

From Bézout’s identity, there exist
n_{1},n_{2},\cdots,n_{t}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

\delta=n_{1}(aa^{\prime})+n_{2}(d_{2}-d_{1})+\cdots+n_{t}(d_{t}-d_{1}).

For all
j\in\{2,\cdots,t\}
, choose
k_{j}\in{\mathbb{N}}
large enough such that
m_{j}:=k_{j}(aa^{\prime})+n_{j}>0
. Then,

(5.6)

m_{2}(d_{2}-d_{1})+\ldots+m_{t}(d_{t}-d_{1})\equiv\delta\ \mathrm{mod}\ aa^{\prime}.

Let us define the word
w(j):=d_{j}d_{1}\ldots d_{1}\in\mathcal{D}^{p}
for
j\in\{2,\cdots,t\}
. From
Lemma

5.2
,

(w(j))_{g}=(d_{j}-d_{1})+d_{1}+d_{1}g+\cdots+d_{1}g^{p-1}\equiv d_{j}-d_{1}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a.

Similarly,
S_{g}(w(j))=pd_{1}+(d_{j}-d_{1})\equiv d_{j}-d_{1}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}
.
Let us define

w:=\underbrace{w(2)\cdots w(2)}_{\text{$m_{2}$ times}}\cdots\underbrace{w(t)\cdots w(t)}_{\text{$m_{t}$ times}}.

Notice that
|w|=p(m_{2}+\ldots+m_{t})
. Also, the periodicity
g^{p}\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
and (
5.6
) imply that

(w)_{g}\equiv\sum_{j=2}^{t}(w(j))_{g}m_{j}\equiv\sum_{j=2}^{t}(d_{j}-d_{1})m_{j}\equiv\delta\ \mathrm{mod}\ a.

Using (
5.6
) again, we can conclude

S_{g}(w)=\sum_{j=2}^{t}S_{g}(w(j))m_{j}\equiv\sum_{j=2}^{t}(d_{j}-d_{1})m_{j}\equiv\delta\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}.

∎

Leveraging the
Theorem

4.8
and
Lemma

5.3
, we can establish a complete description of the phenomenon of joint distribution (
Definition

5.1
) for missing digits sets when
\gcd(g,a)=\gcd(a,a^{\prime})=1
. In particular, this shows
Theorem

A
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
g\geq 2
be an integer, and let
\mathcal{D}=\{d_{1},\cdots,d_{t}\}
be a set of digits, where
d_{1}
is the smallest digit. Suppose that
\gcd(g,a)=\gcd(a,a^{\prime})=1
, and recall the missing digits set
\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}
defined in (
1.1
). Defining
\delta:=\gcd(aa^{\prime},d_{2}-d_{1},\cdots,d_{t}-d_{1})
,

(i)

\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}
is jointly uniformly distributed
\ \mathrm{mod}\ (a,a^{\prime})
if and only if
\delta=1
.

More generally, concerning the limit

(5.7)

L(b,b^{\prime}):=\lim_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{|\{n\in\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}:\ n\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a,\ S_{g}(n)\equiv b^{\prime}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}\}\cap[0,N)|}{|\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}\cap[0,N)|},

it follows that:

(ii)

Let
\delta_{a}:=\gcd(\delta,a)
and
\delta_{a^{\prime}}:=\gcd(\delta,a^{\prime})
. If
d_{1}\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ \delta_{a}
and
d_{1}\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ \delta_{a^{\prime}}
, then
L(b,b^{\prime})=\delta/aa^{\prime}
for every
(b,b^{\prime})
in the subgroup
\langle\delta_{a}\rangle\times\langle\delta_{a^{\prime}}\rangle\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{a^{\prime}}
. On the other hand, if
(b,b^{\prime})\notin\langle\delta_{a}\rangle\times\langle\delta_{a^{\prime}}\rangle
,

\Big\{n\in\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}:\ n\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a,\ S_{g}(n)\equiv b^{\prime}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}\Big\}=\emptyset.

(iii)

In any other case, for every
(b,b^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{a^{\prime}}
, the limit
L(b,b^{\prime})
either equals zero or does not exist.

Proof.

Let us define
p=a^{\prime}\phi(a(g-1))
, where
\phi
is the Euler’s totient function, and note that
(p,1)
is an eventual period for
\mathbf{f}=(\id,S_{g})
with respect to
\mathbf{a}=(a,a^{\prime})
. For
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
X^{i}
be the Markov chain associated to
g,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{f},p,\ell
and the subshift
\Sigma=\mathcal{D}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
, as defined in
Definition

3.13
. We denote by
M_{i}
its transition matrix and
\mu_{i}
its initial distribution.

The aperiodicity of
M_{i}
follows from
Lemma

5.2
and (
5.4
).

Let us suppose
\delta=1
. From
Lemma

5.3
, there exists a word
w
using symbols of
\mathcal{D}
such that
|w|\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ p
,
(w)_{g}\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
and
S_{g}(w)\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}
. Since
\gcd(a,a^{\prime})=1
, the element
(1,1)
is a generator for
{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{a^{\prime}}
. Then, for every
(b,b^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{a^{\prime}}
, there exists
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
m\equiv(g^{i})^{-1}b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
and
m\equiv b^{\prime}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}
. Let
y\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{D}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}})
be constructed by concatenating
w
with itself
m
times. The periodicity
g^{p}\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
implies that
g^{i}(y)_{g}\equiv g^{i}m(w)_{g}\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
. On the other hand,
S_{g}(y)\equiv mS_{g}(w)\equiv b^{\prime}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}
. Therefore,
M_{i}
is irreducible for every
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
. This concludes that the Markov condition holds, then the
Theorem

4.8
implies that
\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}
is jointly uniformly distributed
\ \mathrm{mod}\ (a,a^{\prime}).

Now, let us suppose that
\delta\neq 1
. Since
\delta
divides
aa^{\prime}
and
\gcd(a,a^{\prime})=1
, we can write
\delta=\delta_{a}\cdot\delta_{a^{\prime}}
, where
\delta_{a}:=\gcd(\delta,a)
and
\delta_{a^{\prime}}:=\gcd(\delta,a^{\prime})
. In particular,
\gcd(\delta_{a},\delta_{a^{\prime}})=1.

From the definition of
\delta
, note that
d_{j}\equiv d_{1}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \delta
for all
j\in\{1,\cdots,t\}
. Therefore, for any
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
x=x_{0}\cdots x_{m-1}\in\mathcal{D}^{m}
,

(5.8)

(x)_{g}=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(x_{j}-d_{1})g^{j}+d_{1}g^{j}\equiv d_{1}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}g^{j}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \delta_{a},\text{ and }S_{g}(x)\equiv md_{1}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \delta_{a^{\prime}}.

In particular, for every
x\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{D}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}})
such that
|x|\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ p
,
Lemma

5.2
implies that
(x)_{g}\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ \delta_{a}
and
S_{g}(x)\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ \delta_{a^{\prime}}
. On the other hand, from
Lemma

5.3
we can find a word
y
such that
|y|\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ p
,
(y)_{g}\equiv\delta\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
, and
S_{g}(y)\equiv\delta\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}.
By the Chinese remainder theorem,
(\delta,\delta)
is a generator for the subgroup
\mathcal{S}(\delta_{a},\delta_{a^{\prime}}):=\langle\delta_{a}\rangle\times\langle\delta_{a^{\prime}}\rangle\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{a^{\prime}}
. Therefore, recalling that
\gcd(g,\delta_{a})=1
,

(5.9)

\bigcup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\{w\in\mathcal{D}^{np}:\ g^{i}(w)_{g}\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a,\ S_{g}(w)\equiv b^{\prime}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}\}\neq\emptyset

if and only if
(b,b^{\prime})\in\mathcal{S}(\delta_{a},\delta_{a^{\prime}})
. In particular, this already ensures that
M_{i}
is not irreducible on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{a^{\prime}}
, so the Markov condition does not hold. However, we can show that
X^{i}
is an irreducible and aperiodic Markov chain when restricting the state space. Recall the initial distribution of
X^{i}
given by

\mu_{i}(b,b^{\prime})=\dfrac{|\{w\in\mathcal{D}^{i}:\ (w)_{g}\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a,\ S_{g}(w)\equiv b^{\prime}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}\}|}{|\mathcal{D}|^{i}}.

Defining
\delta_{a}(i):=d_{1}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}g^{i-1}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \delta_{a}
and
\delta_{a^{\prime}}(i):=id_{1}\mod{\delta_{a^{\prime}}}
, (
5.8
) implies that
(x)_{g}\equiv\delta_{a}(i)\ \mathrm{mod}\ \delta_{a}
and
S_{g}(x)\equiv\delta_{a^{\prime}}(i)\ \mathrm{mod}\ \delta_{a^{\prime}}
for every
x\in\mathcal{D}^{i}
. Therefore,
\mu_{i}(b,b^{\prime})\neq 0
implies
b\equiv\delta_{a}(i)\ \mathrm{mod}\ \delta_{a}
and
b^{\prime}\equiv\delta_{a^{\prime}}(i)\ \mathrm{mod}\ \delta_{a^{\prime}}
. If we define

\mathcal{S}(i):=(\delta_{a}(i),\delta_{a^{\prime}}(i))+\mathcal{S}(\delta_{a},\delta_{a^{\prime}}),

the equivalence in (
5.9
) indicates that
X^{i}
never visits the states in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{a^{\prime}}\setminus\mathcal{S}(i)
, and also
X^{i}
is irreducible and aperiodic when restricted to the state space
\mathcal{S}(i)
. Note that
M^{i}
restricted to
\mathcal{S}(i)
is still doubly stochastic, since we only ignore zero-value entries. Therefore, as
|\mathcal{S}(i)|=|\mathcal{S}(\delta_{a},\delta_{a^{\prime}})|=(a/\delta_{a})\cdot(a^{\prime}/\delta_{a^{\prime}})
,

(5.10)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mu_{i+Np}(b,b^{\prime})=\begin{cases}\delta/aa^{\prime}&\text{ if }(b,b^{\prime})\in\mathcal{S}(i),\\
0&\text{ if }(b,b^{\prime})\notin\mathcal{S}(i).\end{cases}

If
d_{1}\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ \delta_{a}
and
d_{1}\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ \delta_{a^{\prime}}
,
\mathcal{S}(i)=\mathcal{S}(\delta_{a},\delta_{a^{\prime}})
for every
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
. This shows that each Markov chain
X^{i}
is irreducible and aperiodic in a common state space
\mathcal{S}(\delta_{a},\delta_{a^{\prime}})
. Note that the Markov condition means that every
X^{i}
is irreducible and aperiodic over the state space
{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{a^{\prime}}
, but it is easy to see that the procedure of
Section

4
is identical if we assume that each
X^{i}
is irreducible and aperiodic over a subgroup of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{a^{\prime}}
(and the Markov chain never visits the other states). Thus, by replicating the argument of
Section

4
, we can conclude

\lim_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{|\{n\in\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}:\ n\equiv_{a}b,\ S_{g}(n)\equiv_{a^{\prime}}b^{\prime}\}\cap[0,N)|}{|\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}\cap[0,N)|}=\begin{cases}\delta/aa^{\prime}&\text{ if }(b,b^{\prime})\in\mathcal{S}(\delta_{a},\delta_{a^{\prime}})\\
0&\text{ if }(b,b^{\prime})\notin\mathcal{S}(\delta_{a},\delta_{a^{\prime}}).\end{cases}

This shows (
ii
). If either
d_{1}\not\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ \delta_{a}
or
d_{1}\not\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ \delta_{a^{\prime}}
, notice that
\mathcal{S}(1)\cap\mathcal{S}(2)=\emptyset
. Then, for every
(b,b^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{a^{\prime}}
, there exists
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that the Markov chain
X^{j}
does not visit the state
(b,b^{\prime})
. From this fact, we want to show that (
5.7
) either equals zero or the limit does not exist. Let
(b,b^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{a^{\prime}}
and let
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
X^{j}
never visits the state
(b,b^{\prime})
. By contradiction, suppose that
L(b,b^{\prime})>0
. Since
X^{j}
does not visit the state
(b,b^{\prime})
,

\{n\in\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}:\ n\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a,\ S_{g}(n)\equiv b^{\prime}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}\}\cap[g^{j+mp-1},g^{j+mp})=\emptyset

for every
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. The contradiction follows by noting that

\displaystyle L(b,b^{\prime})=\lim_{m\to\infty}\dfrac{|\{n\in\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}:\ n\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a,\ S_{g}(n)\equiv b^{\prime}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}\}\cap[0,g^{j+mp-1})|}{|\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}\cap[0,g^{j+mp-1})|}

\displaystyle>\liminf_{m\to\infty}\dfrac{|\{n\in\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}:\ n\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a,\ S_{g}(n)\equiv b^{\prime}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}\}\cap[0,g^{j+mp})|}{|\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}\cap[0,g^{j+mp})|}.

This concludes (
iii
).
∎

Note that in the cases (
i
) and (
ii
), we can conclude from
Proposition

4.6
instead of using
Theorem

4.8
, as
\Sigma
is a full shift. Hence, the order of convergence
O(N^{-\gamma})
for some
\gamma>0
can be provided for the limit (
5.7
). An immediate consequence follows by taking
a^{\prime}=1
, which allows us to fully describe the phenomenon of uniform distribution
\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
when
\gcd(g,a)=1
. This shows
Corollary

A
.

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
g\geq 2
be an integer, and let
\mathcal{D}=\{d_{1},\cdots,d_{t}\}
be a set of digits, where
d_{1}
is the smallest digit.
Suppose
\gcd(g,a)=1
and denote
\delta:=\gcd(a,d_{2}-d_{1},\cdots,d_{t}-d_{1}).

Then,

(i)

\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}
is uniformly distributed
\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
if and only if
\delta=1
.

(ii)

If
\delta\neq 1
and
d_{1}\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ \delta
,
\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}
is uniformly distributed in the subgroup
\langle\delta\rangle\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}
.

(iii)

In any other case, the limit of

\dfrac{|\{n\in\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}:\ n\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a\}\cap[0,N)|}{|\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}\cap[0,N)|}

when
N\to\infty
is either zero, or does not exist.

Note that the condition
\gcd(g,a)=1
is commonly assumed in results of this kind. In the framework of
Corollary

5.5
, when
\gcd(g,a)\neq 1
the matrices
M_{i}
are no longer irreducible over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}
. However, the Markov chains constructed can still capture the behaviour of the distribution by breaking
X^{i}
into pieces determined by the communication classes of the Markov chain (see Section 1.2 in
[
44
]
for details about this notion). The disadvantage of this situation is that the convergence of
X^{i}
depends on the initial distribution.

The following simple example shows that when
\gcd(a,g)\neq 1
, the limiting behavior depends heavily on the initial distribution.

Example 1
.

Let
a=12
,
g=6
and
\mathcal{D}=\{1,2,4\}
. Notice that
a
divides
g^{i}
for every
i\geq 2
, then
(w_{0}\cdots w_{n})_{g}\equiv w_{0}+w_{1}g\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
. Therefore,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{|\{n\in\mathcal{C}_{6,\mathcal{D}}:\ n\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ 12\}\cap[0,N)|}{|\mathcal{C}_{6,\mathcal{D}}\cap[0,N)|}=\begin{cases}2/9&\text{ if }b\in\{1,2,4\}\\
1/9&\text{ if }b\in\{7,8,10\}\\
0&\text{ if }b\in\{0,3,5,6,9,11\}.\end{cases}

Notice that this distribution agrees with the distribution when considering
[0,g^{2})
. Also, we can see that the limit is different and non-zero for some classes, behaviour which is not observed under the assumption
\gcd(g,a)=1
.

Finally, we will present an equivalent condition for uniform distribution
\ \mathrm{mod}\ (a,a^{\prime})
without the assumption
\gcd(a,a^{\prime})=1
, however, at the cost of having a more complicated condition to check.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
g\geq 2
be an integer, and let
\mathcal{D}=\{d_{1},\cdots,d_{t}\}
be a set of digits, where
d_{1}
is the smallest digit. Consider
a,a^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\gcd(g,a)=1
, and define
p:=a^{\prime}\phi(a(g-1))
. Therefore,
\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}
is jointly uniformly distributed
\ \mathrm{mod}\ (a,a^{\prime})
if and only if
\gcd(a,d_{2}-d_{1},\cdots,d_{t}-d_{1})=1
,
and there exists
w\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{D}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}})
such that
(w)_{g}\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
,
S_{g}(w)\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}
and
|w|\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ p
.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}
is jointly uniformly distributed
\ \mathrm{mod}\ (a,a^{\prime})
, in particular, it is uniformly distributed
\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
. From
Corollary

5.5
follows that
\gcd(a,d_{2}-d_{1},\cdots,d_{t}-d_{1})=1
. In addition, there exist infinitely many words
w
such that
(w)_{g}\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
,
S_{g}((w)_{g})\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}
and
|w|\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ p
. Otherwise, for every
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\Big\{n\in\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}:\ n\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ a,\ S_{g}(n)\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ 1\Big\}\cap[g^{mp-1},g^{mp})=\emptyset,

contradicting that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{|\{n\in\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}:\ n\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ a,\ S_{g}(n)\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}\}\cap[0,N)|}{|\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}\cap[0,N)|}=\dfrac{1}{aa^{\prime}}>0.

On the other hand, let us suppose that
\gcd(a,d_{2}-d_{1},\cdots,d_{t}-d_{1})=1
and that there exists
w\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{D}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}})
such that
(w)_{g}\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
,
S_{g}(w)\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}
and
|w|\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ p
. From
Lemma

5.3
, there exists
y\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{D}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}})
such that
(y)_{g}\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
and
|y|\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ p
. To prove that
M_{i}
is irreducible, we will check (
5.3
). Let
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}
and
b^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{a^{\prime}}
. If
\tilde{y}
is the concatenation
\left((g^{i})^{-1}b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a\right)
-times of the word
y
(in case
b=0
, we concatenate
a
times), the periodicity
g^{p}\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
implies that
g^{i}(\tilde{y})_{g}\equiv g^{i}(g^{i})^{-1}b\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
. If
s:=S_{g}(\tilde{y})
, we denote by
\tilde{w}
the concatenation
\left(b^{\prime}-s\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}\right)
-times of
w
. Thus, we can conclude that

g^{i}(\tilde{y}\tilde{w})_{g}\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
and
S_{g}(\tilde{y}\tilde{w})\equiv b^{\prime}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}
. Clearly,
|\tilde{y}\tilde{w}|\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ p
. Therefore,
M_{i}
is irreducible. The matrix
M_{i}
is also aperiodic as consequence of
Lemma

5.2
. Thus, the Markov condition holds and the result follows by the
Theorem

4.8
.
∎

5.2.
Revisiting Gelfond’s theorem: An equivalent condition

Let
g\geq 2
be an integer, and
a,a^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}
. As presented in
Corollary

1.2
, Gelfond
[
25
]
studied the long-term behaviour of

(5.11)

|\{n\leq N:\ n\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a,\ S_{g}(n)\equiv b^{\prime}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}\}|,

showing that, under the assumption
\gcd(g-1,a^{\prime})=1
, the set
A={\mathbb{N}}
is jointly uniformly distributed
\ \mathrm{mod}\ (a,a^{\prime})
. In this section, we present an extension of
Corollary

1.2
, obtaining an equivalent condition to uniform distribution
\ \mathrm{mod}\ (a,a^{\prime})
.

Notice that the obstruction in applying our method arises from the lack of the condition
\gcd(g,a)=1
, which implies that the Markov chains associated with this problem are not irreducible. However, we can still break each Markov chain
X^{i}
into pieces, generating irreducible and aperiodic Markov chains. In that way, the behaviour of (
5.11
) can be understood using our method.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
g\geq 2
be an integer and
a,a^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then,

(5.12)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\dfrac{|\{n\leq N:\ n\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a,\ S_{g}(n)\equiv b^{\prime}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}\}|}{N}=\dfrac{1}{aa^{\prime}}

for every
(b,b^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{a^{\prime}}
if and only if there exists
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
n\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
and
S_{g}(n)\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}.

Proof.

If (
5.12
) holds, there are infinitely many
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
n\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
and
S_{g}(n)\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}
.

For the other direction, let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
be such that
n\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
and
S_{g}(n)\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}
. For
\mathbf{f}=(\id,S_{g})
, let
p\in{\mathbb{N}}
be given by
Proposition

3.2
. Then,
(p,a-1)
is an eventual period for
\mathbf{f}
with respect to
\mathbf{(a,a^{\prime})}
, and notice that
\Sigma=\{0,\cdots,g-1\}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
is such that
{\mathbb{N}}=A_{\Sigma}
. For
i\geq a-1
, let
X^{i}
be the Markov chain of
Definition

3.13
, with transition matrix
M_{i}
and initial distribution
\mu_{i}
.

Let
w=10\cdots 0
such that
|w|=p
, and clearly
g^{i}(w)_{g}\equiv g^{i}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
. If
y
is the base-
g
expansion of
n
(by adding
0
’s, we can suppose that
y
has length divisible by
p
), then
(y)_{g}\equiv 0\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
and
S_{g}(y)\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}
. Hence, for any
(b,b^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{a^{\prime}}
, there exists
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
(w^{b}y^{k})_{g}\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
and
S_{g}(w^{b}y^{k})_{g}\equiv b^{\prime}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}
(where
z^{j}
denotes the concatenation of
z

j
-times). Therefore,

(5.13)

\bigcup_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}\{w\in\mathcal{D}^{mp}:\ g^{i}(w)_{g}\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a,\ S_{g}(w)\equiv b^{\prime}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a^{\prime}\}\neq\emptyset

if and only if
(b,b^{\prime})\in\langle g^{i}\rangle\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{a^{\prime}}\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{a^{\prime}}.
Denoting
\delta_{i}:=\gcd(g^{i},a)
,
it follows that
\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}M_{i}^{m}((b_{0},b_{1}),(b_{0}^{\prime},b_{1}^{\prime}))>0
if and only if
b_{0}\equiv b_{0}^{\prime}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \delta_{i}
. Thus, if the Markov chain
X^{i}
starts at
X_{0}^{i}=(b,b^{\prime})
, that trajectory will only visit classes in
(b+\langle\delta_{i}\rangle)\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{a^{\prime}}\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{a^{\prime}}.
In this way, we can split
X^{i}
into pieces depending on the starting point.

For each
j\in\{0,\cdots,\delta_{i}-1\}
, we define
\mathcal{S}(i,j):=j+\langle\delta_{i}\rangle
and

\mu_{i,j}(b,b^{\prime}):=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\mu_{i}(b,b^{\prime})}{\bar{\mu}_{i,j}}&\text{ if }b\equiv j\ \mathrm{mod}\ \delta_{i}\\
0&\text{ otherwise,}\end{cases}

where
\mu_{i}
is the initial distribution of
X^{i}
and

\bar{\mu}_{i,j}:=\sum_{b^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{a^{\prime}}}\sum_{b\in\mathcal{S}(j)}\mu_{i}(b,b^{\prime})=\dfrac{|w\in\{0,\cdots,g-1\}^{i}:\ (w)_{g}\equiv j\ \mathrm{mod}\ \delta_{i}\}|}{g^{i}}=\dfrac{1}{\delta_{i}}.

Let
X^{i,j}
be a Markov chain on the state space
\mathcal{S}(i,j)
, with initial distribution
\mu_{i,j}
and transition matrix
M_{i}
, both restricted to indexes of
\mathcal{S}(i,j)
. The aperiodicity follows directly as
0\cdots 0
is in the language of
\{0,\cdots,g-1\}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
, and
M_{i}
restricted to
\mathcal{S}(i,j)
is irreducible by (
5.13
). Therefore, the Markov chain
X^{i,j}
converges to its invariant distribution given by
1/|\mathcal{S}(i,j)|=\delta_{i}/aa^{\prime}.
It is not difficult to see that

(5.14)

\mu_{i+np}=\sum_{j=0}^{\delta_{i}-1}\bar{\mu}_{i,j}\left(\mu_{i,j}M^{n}_{i}\right)=\dfrac{1}{\delta_{i}}\sum_{j=0}^{\delta_{i}-1}\mu_{i,j}M^{n}_{i}.

From
Theorem

2.1
, there exist
C_{i,j}>0
and
\rho_{i,j}\in(0,1)
such that

(5.15)

\left|\left(\mu_{i,j}M_{i}^{n}\right)(b,b^{\prime})-\dfrac{\delta_{i}}{aa^{\prime}}\right|\leq C_{i,j}\rho_{i,j}^{n}

for every
(b,b^{\prime})\in\mathcal{S}(i,j).
Clearly,
\left(\mu_{i,j}M_{i}^{n}\right)(b,b^{\prime})=0
if
(b,b^{\prime})\notin\mathcal{S}(i,j)
. Since
\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{a^{\prime}}=\bigsqcup_{j\in\{0,\cdots,\delta_{i}-1\}}\mathcal{S}(i,j)
, from (
5.14
) and (
5.15
) we can obtain that, for every
(b,b^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{a^{\prime}}
,

(5.16)

\left|\mu_{i+np}(b,b^{\prime})-\dfrac{1}{aa^{\prime}}\right|\leq C_{i}\rho_{i}^{n},

where
\displaystyle C_{i}:=\max_{j=0,\cdots,\delta_{i}-1}C_{i,j}
and
\displaystyle\rho_{i}:=\max_{j=0,\cdots,\delta_{i}-1}\rho_{i,j}
. Equivalently,

(5.17)

\left|\mathbb{P}\Big(X^{i}_{n}=(b,b^{\prime})\Big)-\dfrac{1}{aa^{\prime}}\right|\leq C_{i}\rho_{i}^{n}.

Despite each
X^{i}
is not irreducible, we still have convergence to the uniform measure
1/aa^{\prime}
. Note that (
5.17
) is equivalent to the estimation (
4.3
) in the case of
\Sigma
being a full shift, and essentially, this is what is required to retrieve the results of
Section

4
, and in particular,
Theorem

4.8
. Therefore, we can conclude that
{\mathbb{N}}
is jointly uniformly distributed
\ \mathrm{mod}\ (a,a^{\prime})
by replicating the arguments of
Section

4
.
∎

Remark 6
.

The obstruction when trying to use the previous idea in the context of missing digits sets lies in the fact that for some

5.3.
Forbidding combinations of digits

So far, we have focused on applying
Theorem

4.8
in restricted digit sets, where restrictions come from forbidding some digits (missing digits sets). Given the flexibility in choosing digits and restrictions in the general case, it does not seem possible to establish general conditions like those in
Section

5.1
within the context of general
\times g
-invariant sets. In such a case,
Theorem

4.8
is still a useful tool on a case-by-case basis. Seeking greater generality, we construct a class of sets with more flexible digit restrictions that exhibit joint uniform distribution. While we focus on restrictions of length 2, some of these ideas can be extended to longer lengths.

Let
g\geq 2
be an integer and
a,a^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Let
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\{0,\cdots,g-1\}
be a set of digits, and let
T
be a matrix on
\mathcal{D}
such that
T(d,d^{\prime})\in\{0,1\}
for every
d,d^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}

Definition 5.8
.

We say that the matrix
T
is
k
-regular
if for every
d\in\mathcal{D}

\sum_{d^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}}M(d,d^{\prime})=\sum_{d^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}}M(d^{\prime},d)=k.

Also, we say that
T
is
irreducible
if for every
d,d^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}
, there exists
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
M^{n}(d,d^{\prime})>0
.

As pointed out in
Section

2.2
, such a matrix

induces a shift of finite type

\Sigma_{T}:=\left\{x\in\mathcal{D}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}:\ T(x_{i},x_{i+1})=1\ \forall i\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\right\}.

In fact, any 1-step subshift of finite type can be written in this form. Similarly, we can define the
\times g
-invariant set of integers generated by the subshift
\Sigma_{T}
as

A_{T}:=A_{\Sigma_{T}}=\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{n}d_{i}g^{i}:\ T(d_{i},d_{i+1})=1\ \forall i\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\right\}.

As discussed in
Remark

3
, for a 1-step SFT we can apply
Theorem

4.8
when the Markov chains
X^{i}
(
Definition

3.13
) are constructed using the cover of
Remark

1
instead of the Fischer cover. It is also straightforward that the graph of
Remark

1
is regular and irreducible if the matrix
T
is regular and irreducible.

6.
Transversality between multiplicatively invariant sets and arithmetic progressions

In this section, we provide a partial answer to the open question
[
27
, Question 5.6]
. Recalling the notion of mass dimension provided in
Definition

2.10
, the question is stated as follows:
Let
A\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
be a multiplicatively invariant set and
P
be an arithmetic progression. Is it true that
\dim_{\text{M}}(A\cap P)
is either zero or
\dim_{\text{M}}(A)
?

Let
A
be
\times g
-invariant, and recall the existence of a subshift
\Sigma
such that
A=A_{\Sigma}
. If
A
satisfies the conditions of the
Theorem

4.8
with
f=\id
and
a\in{\mathbb{N}}
(including the Markov condition), it is possible to see that
\dim_{\text{M}}(A\cap(a{\mathbb{N}}+b))=\dim_{\text{M}}(A)
for every
b\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Therefore, this provides a positive answer to the open question in those cases. As the notion of dimension is less sensitive to rough estimations than the relative density, we will modify the construction presented in
Section

3
to address this question for every
A=A_{\Sigma}
when
\Sigma
is any transitive sofic subshift.

6.1.
Counterexample for general cases

We will show that the answer is negative if we only suppose that the subshift
\Sigma
is only transitive, or only sofic.

Let
g=a>4
, and let
\mathcal{D}=\{d_{0},d_{1}\}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=\{d_{2},d_{3},d_{4}\}
be disjoint subsets of
\{0,\cdots,g-1\}
. We define the subshift
\Sigma=\mathcal{D}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\cup\mathcal{D}^{\prime{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
. Notice that
\Sigma
is a sofic subshift (in fact, it is a SFT). From
Proposition

2.12
, it is easy to see that
\dim_{\text{M}}(A_{\Sigma})=\log(3)/\log(g).
Since
a=g
,
n\in A_{\Sigma}\cap(a{\mathbb{N}}+d_{0})
if and only if
d_{0}
is the least significant digit of
n
in base
g
, so we can conclude that

0<\dim_{\text{M}}(A_{\Sigma}\cap(a{\mathbb{N}}+d_{0}))=\log(2)/\log(g)<\dim_{\text{M}}(A_{\Sigma}).

More generally, other examples can be constructed even when
\gcd(g,a)=1
. For this, we can use the same idea of considering a disjoint set of digits with different size, and choose the elements in order to apply (
ii
) of
Corollary

5.5
. This concludes that the sofic condition by itself is not enough to provide a positive answer to the open question. Now, we will see that assuming only transitivity is not enough either.

Proposition 6.1
.

For any integer
g\geq 3
, there exists a transitive subshift
\Sigma
and an arithmetic progression
P
such that
0<\dim_{\text{M}}(A_{\Sigma}\cap P)<\dim_{\text{M}}(A_{\Sigma}).

Proof.

Let us choose a set of digits
\mathcal{D}\subsetneq\{0,\ldots,g-1\}
with at least two elements, fixing some digits
h\notin\mathcal{D}
and
d\in\mathcal{D}
.
For every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, we define the set of words

W_{k}:=\left\{hd^{k}z:\ z\in\mathcal{D}^{k}\right\}\subseteq\mathcal{L}(\{0,\cdots,g-1\}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}),

where
d^{k}:=d\cdots d
is the concatenation
k
times of
d
. The length of any word in
W_{k}
is
2k+1
and
|W_{k}|=|\mathcal{D}|^{k}.
Giving an enumeration to the
|\mathcal{D}|^{k}
words of
W_{k}
, we can write

W_{k}=\left\{w_{k}^{j}:\ j=0,\cdots,|\mathcal{D}|^{k}-1\right\}.

By concatenating all the words in the sets
W_{k}
, we define the infinite sequence

w:=(w_{0}^{0})(w_{1}^{0}w_{1}^{1}\cdots w_{1}^{|\mathcal{D}|-1})\cdots(w_{k}^{0}\cdots w_{k}^{|\mathcal{D}|^{k}-1})\cdots,

and we refer to the subword
w_{k}^{0}\cdots w_{k}^{|\mathcal{D}|^{k}-1}
as the block induced by
W_{k}
. Considering the full shift
\left(\{0,\cdots,g-1\}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}},\sigma\right),

we can define the transitive subshift

\Sigma=\overline{Orb_{\sigma}(w)}:=\overline{\left\{\sigma^{n}(w):\ n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\right\}}.

Also, it is straightforward that

\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)=\Big\{x=x_{0}\cdots x_{m}:\ m\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},\ x\text{ appears in the sequence $w$}\Big\}.

We claim that
\dim_{\text{M}}(A_{\Sigma})=\log(|\mathcal{D}|)/\log(g).
To show it, notice that
\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}}\subseteq A_{\Sigma}
as
\mathcal{D}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\subseteq\Sigma
. Thus,
Corollary

2.13
implies that

(6.1)

\dim_{\text{M}}(A_{\Sigma})\geq\dim_{\text{M}}(\mathcal{C}_{g,\mathcal{D}})=\log(|\mathcal{D}|)/\log(g).

For the other direction, we aim to estimate the number of elements in
A_{\Sigma}
whose base-
g
expansion has length at most
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
. After the block
W_{i}
, no new words of length at most
i
appear in the sequence
w
. Therefore, any
n\in A_{\Sigma}\cap[0,g^{i})
can be generated by a subword with length at most
i
of the sequence

w(i):=w_{0}^{0}w_{1}^{0}\cdots w_{1}^{|\mathcal{D}|-1}\cdots w_{i}^{0}\cdots w_{i}^{|\mathcal{D}|^{i}-1}.

In
w(i)
, there are
|w(i)|-(i-1)
possible starting symbols for words of length
i
, so there are at most
|w(i)|-(i-1)
words of length
i
. A quick estimation leads to

|w(i)|=\sum_{k=0}^{i}(2k+1)|\mathcal{D}|^{k}\leq(i+1)(2i+1)|\mathcal{D}|^{i}.

Therefore,
i(i+1)(2i+1)|\mathcal{D}|^{i}
gives a rough estimation for the number of words of length at most
i
that can be read in
w(i)
. Then,

\dfrac{\log\left(|A_{\Sigma}\cap[0,g^{i})|\right)}{i\log(g)}\leq\dfrac{\log(i(i+1)(2i+1))+i\log|\mathcal{D}|}{i\log(g)}.

Taking
i\to\infty
and using
Lemma

2.14
,
\log(|\mathcal{D}|)/\log(g)\leq\dim_{\text{M}}(A_{\Sigma}).
This concludes that

\dim_{\text{M}}(A_{\Sigma})=\log(|\mathcal{D}|)/\log(g).

Let
P:=g{\mathbb{N}}+h
be an arithmetic progression. Notice that
n\in A_{\Sigma}\cap P
if and only if
n\in A_{\Sigma}
and the least significant digit of
n
in base
g
is
h
. Thus, let us define
\Sigma(h):=\{v\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma):\ v_{0}=h\},

\displaystyle A(h):=\left\{(v)_{g}:\ v\in\Sigma(h)\right\}\subseteq A_{\Sigma}
and

r=g^{2}
. Notice that for any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
z\in\mathcal{D}^{i-1}
,
(hy^{i-1}z)_{g}\in A(h)\cap[0,r^{i})
. Then,
|\mathcal{D}|^{i-1}\leq|A(h)\cap[0,r^{i})|.

On the other hand, after after the appearance of the word
w^{0}_{2i-1}
in the sequence
w
, the only word of length
2i
that starts with
h
and can be read is
hy^{2i-1}
. For
j<i
, there are
|\mathcal{D}|^{j}
appearances of
h
in the block
j
of
w
, and each occurrence can be associated with one
2i
-long word. In the block
(w_{i}^{0}\cdots w_{i}^{|\mathcal{D}|^{i}-1})
, every word of length
2i
starting with
h
is given by
hy^{i}z
for some
z\in\mathcal{D}^{i-1}
, hence, there are
|\mathcal{D}|^{i-1}
of those words in
W_{i}
. More generally, for every
j\geq i
, every word of length
2i
starting with
h
in the block
W_{j}
has the form
hy^{j}z
, where
z\in\mathcal{D}^{2i-j-1}
, so there are
|\mathcal{D}|^{2i-j-1}
of such words in
W_{j}
. Using these estimates,

|\{w\in\Sigma(h):\ |w|=2i\}|\leq\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}|\mathcal{D}|^{j}+\sum_{j=i}^{2i-1}|\mathcal{D}|^{2i-j-1}\leq 2i|\mathcal{D}|^{i-1}.

Using the same estimate for words of length less than
2i
, it follows that

|A(h)\cap[0,r^{i})|\leq(2i)^{2}|\mathcal{D}|^{i-1}.

Hence,

|\mathcal{D}|^{i-1}\leq|A(h)\cap[0,r^{i})|\leq(2i)^{2}|\mathcal{D}|^{i-1}.

It follows that

\dfrac{i-1}{2i}\dfrac{\log(|\mathcal{D}|)}{\log(g)}\leq\dfrac{\log\left(|A(h)\cap[0,r^{i})|\right)}{2i\log(g)}\leq\dfrac{2\log(2i)+i\log(|\mathcal{D}|)}{2i\log(g)},

and invoking
Lemma

2.14
we conclude

(6.2)

\dim_{\text{M}}(A(h))=\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{\log(|\mathcal{D}|)}{\log(g)}.

Since the arithmetic progression
P=g{\mathbb{N}}+h
satisfies
P\cap A_{\Sigma}=A(h)
, from (
6.2
) follows that

\dim_{\text{M}}(A_{\Sigma}\cap P)=\dfrac{\dim_{\text{M}}(A_{\Sigma})}{2}.

∎

Notice that for every rational
\alpha\in(0,1)
, it is possible to modify the construction of the sets
W_{j}
to construct a transitive subshift
\Sigma
such that
\dim_{\text{M}}(A_{\Sigma}\cap P)=\alpha\dim_{\text{M}}(A_{\Sigma})>0
.

Remark 8
.

For the rest of this section, consider an integer

and
a\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Let
\Sigma\subseteq\{0,\ldots,g-1\}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}

. Let

be as given by
Proposition

(p,a-1)

We utilize the sets

from

to define a non-normalized version of the matrix

Definition 6.2
.

For any

,

\mathcal{M}_{i}\Big((b,F),(b^{\prime},F^{\prime})\Big)=|E_{i}(b^{\prime}-b,F,F^{\prime})|,

In a straightforward way, we can rewrite

and
\nu_{i}

Corollary 6.3
.

If
i\geq a-1
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\mathcal{M}_{i}^{n}\Big((b_{0},F_{0}),(b_{1},F_{1})\Big)=|\{w\in\mathcal{L}^{np}(\Sigma):\ g^{i}(w)_{g}\equiv b^{\prime}-b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a\text{ and }F\overset{w}{\to}F^{\prime}\}|.

for any

and
F,F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}
. Also,
\nu_{i+np}=\nu_{i}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{n}.

The main idea can be summarized as follows: Firstly, we want to find a word
w\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
such that
i:=|w|\geq a-1
,
(w)_{g}\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
and
F(w)\in\mathcal{V}
. By studying the extensions of
w
to words of length
i+pn
for some

, we will see that a significant number of these extensions

satisfy
(\tilde{w})_{g}\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
. As a conclusion, we aim to show
\dim_{\text{M}}(A_{\Sigma}\cap(a{\mathbb{N}}+b))=\dim_{\text{M}}(A_{\Sigma})
. If such a word

For a given word
w
, it is easy to compute
(w)_{g}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a

F(w)\in\mathcal{V}
or not could be challenging if we don’t know the Fischer cover explicitly. The following lemma shows that, for our purposes, such a check is not required.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
w\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
such that
|w|\geq a-1
, and
F(w)\notin\mathcal{V}
. Then, there exists an extension
\tilde{w}
of
w
such that
(w)_{g}\equiv(\tilde{w})_{g}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
,
F(\tilde{w})\in\mathcal{V}
and
|\tilde{w}|=|w|+pn
for some
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof.

Since
\Sigma
is transitive and sofic, there exists a synchronizing word
y\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
, and also there exists
x\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
such that
wxy\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
. Since
y
is synchronizing, any extension of
y
is also synchronizing. Thus, we can suppose
|xy|=mp
for some
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
(otherwise, we can extend
y
to meet that condition). Since
y
is synchronizing, the follower set of any word that ends with the subword
y
is equal to
F(y)
. In particular,
F:=F(wxy)=F(xy)=F(y)\in\mathcal{V}.
Furthermore, every path in
\mathcal{G}
labelled by
xy
has
F
as terminal node, and we can find a path
\pi
labelled by
xy
starting at some
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}
. As
\mathcal{G}
is an irreducible graph, there is a path from
F
to
F^{\prime}
, labelled by some
z\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma).
For any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, denote

(xyz)^{k}=\underbrace{(xyz)\cdots(xyz)}_{\text{$k$ times}}\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma).

Let
\tilde{w}=w(xyz)^{ap}
, and
\ell:=|xyz|
. Using that
g^{p+j}\equiv g^{p}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
for all
j\geq a-1
,

(\tilde{w})_{g}=(w)_{g}+g^{|w|}(xyz)^{p}+\cdots+g^{|w|+(a-1)p\ell}(xyz)^{p}=(w)_{g}+ag^{|w|}(xyz)^{p}\equiv(w)_{g}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a.

As the path associated to
z
ends at
F^{\prime}
, it follows that
F(\tilde{w})=F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}
. Also, notice that
|\tilde{w}|=|w|+\ell ap
.
∎

We now establish a sufficient condition to ensure that extensions of a certain word
w
will visit certain congruence classes and follower sets.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
w\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
such that
|w|\geq a-1
,
(w)_{g}\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
and
F(w)\in\mathcal{V}
. If
\mathcal{M}^{n}_{|w|}\Big((b^{\prime},F^{\prime}),(b,F(w)\Big)>0

(b^{\prime},F^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}\times\mathcal{V}
and

, there exists
\tilde{w}\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
such that
(\tilde{w})_{g}\equiv b^{\prime}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
,

and
|\tilde{w}|=|w|+mp
for some

Proof.

As
\mathcal{M}^{n}_{|w|}\Big((b^{\prime},F^{\prime}),(b,F(w)\Big)>0
,

6.3
provides the existence of a word

with length

such that
g^{|w|}(y)_{g}\equiv b-b^{\prime}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
and
F^{\prime}\overset{y}{\to}F(w).
Equivalently, we can find a path
\pi_{1}
in
\mathcal{G}
with length

, labelled by
y
and from
F^{\prime}
to
F(w)
. Since the Fischer cover is irreducible, there is a path
\pi_{2}
from
F(w)
to
F^{\prime}

\tilde{\pi}:=(\pi_{2}\pi_{1})^{p-1}\pi_{2}

\tilde{\pi}
is labelled by some

\tilde{w}:=w(xy)^{a-1}x\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)

\tilde{\pi}
is
F^{\prime}

. As
|x|
and

are divisible by

(\tilde{w})_{g}\equiv(w)_{g}+(a-1)g^{|w|}(x)_{g}+(a-1)g^{|w|}(y)_{g}+g^{|w|}(x)_{g}\equiv b+(a-1)g^{|w|}(y)_{g}\ \mathrm{mod}\ a.

∎

.

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
\Sigma\subseteq\{0,\cdots,g-1\}^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
be a transitive sofic subshift, and
a\in{\mathbb{N}}
. If there exists
w\in\mathcal{L}(\Sigma)
such that
|w|\geq a-1
and
(w)_{g}\equiv b\ \mathrm{mod}\ a
, then

(6.3)

\dim_{\text{M}}(A_{\Sigma}\cap(a{\mathbb{N}}+b))=\dim_{\text{M}}(A_{\Sigma}).

In particular, either (
6.3
) holds, or
|A_{\Sigma}\cap(a{\mathbb{N}}+b)|\leq g^{a}
.

Proof.

Consider
p\in{\mathbb{N}}
given by
Proposition

3.2
. From
Lemma

6.4
, we can suppose
F(w)\in\mathcal{V}
(otherwise, use
\tilde{w}
instead), and define
i:=|w|\geq a-1
. In particular,
(p,i)
is an eventual period. Consider the set

\mathcal{R}:=\left\{(b^{\prime},F^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}\times\mathcal{V}:\text{ there exists $n\in{\mathbb{N}}$ s.t. }\mathcal{M}^{n}_{i}\Big((b^{\prime},F^{\prime}),(b,F)\Big)>0\ \right\}.

From

6.5
, for each
(b^{\prime},F^{\prime})\in\mathcal{R}
, there is
m^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\nu_{i+m^{\prime}p}(b^{\prime},F^{\prime})\neq 0
.

In particular, we can obtain some

\sum_{j=0}^{m}\nu_{i+jp}(b^{\prime},F^{\prime})\neq 0

for every

. Let

and
(b,F)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{a}\times\mathcal{V}
. Using

,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m}\nu_{i+(j+n)p}(b,F)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\nu_{i+jp}\mathcal{M}^{n}_{i}\Big(\cdot,(b,F)\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{b^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{a},F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}}\nu_{i+jp}(b^{\prime},F^{\prime})\mathcal{M}_{i}^{n}\Big((b^{\prime},F^{\prime}),(b,F)\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{b^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{a},F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{m}\nu_{i+jp}(b^{\prime},F^{\prime})\Big)\mathcal{M}_{i}^{n}\Big((b^{\prime},F^{\prime}),(b,F)\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{b^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{a},F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{n}\Big((b^{\prime},F^{\prime}),(b,F)\Big),

where the last inequality follows from the definition of
\mathcal{R}
and (

). Combining the previous inequality,

3.3
and the characterization of
Corollary

6.3
, we can conclude

\sum_{j=0}^{m}\nu_{i+(j+n)p}(b,F)\geq|\{\pi=(\pi_{0}\cdots\pi_{np-1}):\ \text{$\pi$ is a path of $\mathcal{G}$ and $t(\pi)=F$}\}|.

Since
\mathcal{G}
is an irreducible graph, for each
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}
there exists
\ell(F^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that there is a path of length
\ell(F^{\prime})
from
F^{\prime}
to
F
. Thus, defining
\ell:=\max_{F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}}\ell(F^{\prime}),
for every
n
large enough,

(6.6)

\begin{split}&|\{\pi=(\pi_{0}\cdots\pi_{np-1}):\ \text{$\pi$ is a path of $\mathcal{G}$ and $t(\pi)=F$}\}\\
&\geq\sum_{F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}}|\{\pi=(\pi_{0}\cdots\pi_{np-1-\ell(F^{\prime})}):\ \text{$\pi$ is a path of $\mathcal{G}$ and $t(\pi)=F^{\prime}$}\}|\\
&\geq\sum_{F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}}|\{\pi=(\pi_{0}\cdots\pi_{np-1-\ell}):\ \text{$\pi$ is a path of $\mathcal{G}$ and $t(\pi)=F^{\prime}\}$}|\\
&=|\{\pi=(\pi_{0}\cdots\pi_{np-1-\ell}):\ \text{$\pi$ is a path of $\mathcal{G}\}$}|,\end{split}

\mathcal{G}
is a cover for
\Sigma

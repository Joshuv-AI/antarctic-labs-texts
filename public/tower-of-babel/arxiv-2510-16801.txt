Strong error analysis and first-order convergence of Milstein-type schemes for McKean-Vlasov SDEs with superlinear coefficients
Jingtao Zhu, Yuying Zhao, Siqing Gan

Abstract
In the study of McKean-Vlasov stochastic differential equations (MV-SDEs), numerical approximation plays a crucial role in understanding the behavior of interacting particle systems (IPS). Classical Milstein schemes provide strong convergence of order one under globally Lipschitz coefficients. Nevertheless, many MV-SDEs arising from applications possess super-linearly growing drift and diffusion terms, where classical methods may diverge and particle corruption can occur. In the present work, we aim to fill this gap by developing a unified class of Milstein-type discretizations handling both super-linear drift and diffusion coefficients. The proposed framework includes the tamed-, tanh-, and sine-Milstein methods as special cases and establishes order-one strong convergence for the associated interacting particle system under mild regularity assumptions, requiring only once differentiable coefficients. In particular, our results complement Chen et al. (Electron. J. Probab., 2025), where a taming-based Euler scheme was only tested numerically without theoretical guarantees, by providing a rigorous convergence theory within a broader Milstein-type framework. The analysis relies on discrete-time arguments and binomial-type expansions, avoiding the continuous-time Itô approach that is standard in the literature. Numerical experiments are presented to illustrate the convergence behavior and support the theoretical findings.

Introduction

As a class of important mathematical models,
McKean-Vlasov stochastic differential equations (MV-SDEs), also known as distribution-dependent SDEs, play a central role in modeling interacting particle systems (IPS) and have been extensively studied in fields such as statistical physics
Sznitman 1991
,
Braun and Hepp 1977
, mathematical finance and mean-field games
Lasry and Lions 2007
,
Carmona and Delarue 2018
, control theory
Bensoussan et al. 2013
, and neuroscience
Baladron et al. 2012
. In general, closed-form solutions to MV-SDEs are not available and the development and analysis of numerical methods for their simulation are of significant practical interest.
In this paper, we concern the design and convergence analysis of numerical approximations for the following MV-SDEs:

\text{d}X_{t}=f(X_{t},\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}})\,\text{d}t+g(X_{t},\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}})\,\text{d}W_{t},\quad t\in[0,\mathcal{T}],

(1.1)

where
\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}}
denotes the law of
X_{t}
,
f\colon\mathbb{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
g\colon\mathbb{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times m}
, with
\mathbb{M}:=\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as defined in Section
2.1
. The process
\{W_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq\mathcal{T}}
is an
m
-dimensional standard Brownian motion on a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq\mathcal{T}},\mathbb{P})
with a filtration
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,\mathcal{T}]}
.

As the law of
X_{t}
is known, the underlying MV-SDEs reduce to the usual SDEs, numerical methods for which have been extensively studied for the past decades (consult monographs
Milstein and Tretyakov 2021
,
Hutzenthaler and Jentzen 2015
,
Wang et al. 2020
,
Li et al. 2018
and references therein), in the context of both numerical convergence and numerical stability. When the law of
X_{t}
is unknown, a widely used approach is to approximate the solution via an IPS and then simulate the particle system using time discretization schemes. Under suitable assumptions, the principle of propagation of chaos (PoC) guarantees that the empirical distribution of the particle system converges to the law of the solution as the number of particles tends to infinity
Sznitman 1991
, the time discretization of the IPS becomes a powerful tool to understand the behavior of the underlying problems. To analyze time discretization approximations of the IPS, a global Lipschitz condition is often imposed on the coefficient functions of MV-SDEs
Li et al. 2022
,
Bao and Huang 2022
.
Nevertheless, MV-SDEs arising from applications rarely obey such a traditional but restrictive condition.
Notable examples of MV-SDEs with non-globally Lipschitz continuous coefficients include numerous models, such as the double-well dynamics (see (
4.1
)), the Cucker-Smale flocking model (see (
4.2
) or
Erban et al. 2016
,
Chen and dos Reis 2022
), and the FitzHugh-Nagumo model (see Example
4.3
or
Baladron et al. 2012
).
Evidently, the coefficients of these three models violate the globally Lipschitz condition.
It is well established that even for SDEs
Hutzenthaler et al. 2011
,
Mattingly et al. 2002
with superlinearly growing coefficients, classical schemes such as Euler-Maruyama and Milstein methods may diverge. In the case of MV-SDEs, analogous divergence phenomena commonly referred to as “particle corruption" have also been observed and analyzed
dos Reis et al. 2021
. Specifically, a single particle may become excessively influential over all others, thereby destroying the weakly interacting particle structure underlying the MV-SDE system.

To address this, a growing body of work has focused on the construction and analysis of convergent Euler-type schemes in non-globally Lipschitz settings (see
dos Reis et al. 2021
,
Liu et al. 2023a
,
Liu et al. 2023b
,
Chen and dos Reis 2022
,
Chen and dos Reis 2023
,
Kumar et al. 2022
,
Cui et al. 2025
,
Guo et al. 2024
,
Chen et al. 2025
,
Jian et al. 2025
,
Reisinger and Stockinger 2022
,
Neelima et al. 2020
,
Khue Tran et al. 2025
,
Biswas et al. 2025
,
Zhu et al. 2025
and references therein).
Nevertheless, Euler-type schemes for MV-SDEs with multiplicative noise inherently achieve at most a strong convergence rate of order
\frac{1}{2}
, which often results in high computational costs when higher accuracy is required.
In contrast, Milstein-type schemes offer order-one strong convergence and can be efficiently combined with multilevel Monte Carlo techniques to reduce computational complexity further
Giles 2006
,
Giles 2008
. Recently, tamed Milstein methods have been successfully applied to MV-SDEs with superlinear drift and Lipschitz diffusion coefficients
Bao et al. 2021
,
Kumar and Neelima 2021
,
Bao et al. 2023
.
A natural yet unresolved question thus arises as to whether such methods can be extended to settings where both the drift and diffusion coefficients exhibit superlinear growth. To fill this gap, we develop a general framework for a family of Milstein-type schemes designed to handle superlinear drift and diffusion coefficients and establish their strong convergence theory. Compared with
Bao et al. 2021
, our analysis only requires once differentiability of the coefficients in the state and measure components.
Specifically, our scheme modifies the drift and diffusion coefficients through bounded operators that scale with a negative power of the time step size
\Delta t
, leading to the following general form:

\displaystyle Y_{t_{k+1}}^{i,N}=

\displaystyle~Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}+\Gamma_{1}\!\left(f\!\left(Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N},\rho_{t_{k}}^{Y,N}\right),\Delta t\right)\Delta t+\sum_{j_{1}=1}^{m}\Gamma_{2}\!\left(g_{j_{1}}\!\left(Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N},\rho_{t_{k}}^{Y,N}\right),\Delta t\right)\Delta W^{i,j_{1}}_{t_{k}}

\displaystyle~+\sum_{j_{1},j_{2}=1}^{m}\Gamma_{3}\!\left(\mathcal{L}_{y}^{j_{2}}~g_{j_{1}}\!\left(Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N},\rho_{t_{k}}^{Y,N}\right),\Delta t\right)\!\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\!\!\int_{t_{k}}^{s}\mathrm{d}W_{r}^{i,j_{2}}\mathrm{d}W^{i,j_{1}}_{s}

(1.2)

\displaystyle~+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j_{1},j_{2}=1}^{m}\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{N}\Gamma_{4}\!\left(\mathcal{L}_{\rho}^{j_{2}}~g_{j_{1}}\!\left(Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N},\rho_{t_{k}}^{Y,N},Y_{t_{k}}^{k_{1},N}\right),\Delta t\right)\!\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\!\int_{t_{k}}^{s}\mathrm{d}W_{r}^{k_{1},j_{2}}\mathrm{d}W^{i,j_{1}}_{s}.

Here, the operators

Indeed, when treating MV-SDEs with superlinearly growing coefficients by taming strategy, it becomes a standard way in the literature to work with continuous-time extensions of the schemes and carry out the analysis based on the

Neelima et al. 2020

In summary, by generalizing the taming strategy through operator modifications, we develop a unified class of Milstein-type schemes handling both superlinear drift and diffusion coefficients and establish their order-one strong convergence.

3
Strongly convergent Milstein-type schemes for IPS

We now present the considered time discretization for IPS (
2.1
). In the sequel, we adopt a uniform time discretization over
[0,\mathcal{T}]
using a step size
\Delta t=\frac{\mathcal{T}}{n},n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}

The discrete time points are then given by

3.1
Milstein-type schemes and the maps
\Gamma_{l}

Recall the Milstein method for SDEs, when applied to each particle in IPS (
2.1
) associated with the MV-SDE (
1.1
), leads to the following scheme:

\displaystyle\bar{Y}_{t_{k+1}}^{i,N}=

\displaystyle~\bar{Y}_{t_{k}}^{i,N}+f\left(\bar{Y}_{t_{k}}^{i,N},\rho_{t_{k}}^{\bar{Y},N}\right)\Delta t+\sum_{j_{1}=1}^{m}g_{j_{1}}\left(\bar{Y}_{t_{k}}^{i,N},\rho_{t_{k}}^{\bar{Y},N}\right)\Delta W^{i,j_{1}}_{t_{k}}

\displaystyle~+\sum_{j_{1},j_{2}=1}^{m}\mathcal{L}_{y}^{j_{2}}~g_{j_{1}}\left(\bar{Y}_{t_{k}}^{i,N},\rho_{t_{k}}^{\bar{Y},N}\right)\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\int_{t_{k}}^{s}\mathrm{d}W_{r}^{i,j_{2}}\mathrm{d}W^{i,j_{1}}_{s}

\displaystyle~+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j_{1},j_{2}=1}^{m}\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{N}\mathcal{L}_{\rho}^{j_{2}}~g_{j_{1}}\left(\bar{Y}_{t_{k}}^{i,N},\rho_{t_{k}}^{\bar{Y},N},\bar{Y}_{t_{k}}^{k_{1},N}\right)\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\int_{t_{k}}^{s}\mathrm{d}W_{r}^{k_{1},j_{2}}\mathrm{d}W^{i,j_{1}}_{s},

(3.1)

where

\displaystyle\Delta W^{i,j_{1}}_{t_{k}}:=

\displaystyle~W^{i,j_{1}}_{t_{k+1}}-W^{i,j_{1}}_{t_{k}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{y}^{j_{2}}g_{j_{1}}\left(\bar{Y}_{t_{k}}^{i,N},\rho_{t_{k}}^{\bar{Y},N}\right):=

\displaystyle~\partial_{y}g_{j_{1}}\left(\bar{Y}_{t_{k}}^{i,N},\rho_{t_{k}}^{\bar{Y},N}\right)g_{j_{2}}\left(\bar{Y}_{t_{k}}^{i,N},\rho_{t_{k}}^{\bar{Y},N}\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rho}^{j_{2}}g_{j_{1}}\left(\bar{Y}_{t_{k}}^{i,N},\rho_{t_{k}}^{\bar{Y},N},\bar{Y}_{t_{k}}^{k_{1},N}\right):=

\displaystyle~\partial_{\rho}g_{j_{1}}\left(\bar{Y}_{t_{k}}^{i,N},\rho_{t_{k}}^{\bar{Y},N},\bar{Y}_{t_{k}}^{k_{1},N}\right)g_{j_{2}}\left(\bar{Y}_{t_{k}}^{k_{1},N},\rho_{t_{k}}^{\bar{Y},N}\right).

\displaystyle Y_{t_{k+1}}^{i,N}=

\displaystyle Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}+\Gamma_{1}\left(f\left(Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N},\rho_{t_{k}}^{Y,N}\right),\Delta t\right)\Delta t+\sum_{j_{1}=1}^{m}\Gamma_{2}\left(g_{j_{1}}\left(Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N},\rho_{t_{k}}^{Y,N}\right),\Delta t\right)\Delta W^{i,j_{1}}_{t_{k}}

\displaystyle~+\sum_{j_{1},j_{2}=1}^{m}\Gamma_{3}\left(\mathcal{L}_{y}^{j_{2}}~g_{j_{1}}\left(Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N},\rho_{t_{k}}^{Y,N}\right),\Delta t\right)\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\int_{t_{k}}^{s}\mathrm{d}W_{r}^{i,j_{2}}\mathrm{d}W^{i,j_{1}}_{s}

(3.2)

where
\{\Gamma_{l}(\cdot)\}_{l=1}^{4}

3.2
Moment bounds of the Milstein-type schemes (
3.1

To establish moment bounds for the Milstein-type schemes (

Assumption 3.1
.

(H1)
There exist constant
C>0

|f(0,\delta_{0})|\vee|g(0,\delta_{0})|\vee|\partial_{y}f(0,\delta_{0})|\vee|\partial_{\rho}f(0,\delta_{0},0)|\vee|\partial_{y}g_{j}(0,\delta_{0})|\vee|\partial_{\rho}g_{j}(0,\delta_{0},0)|\leq C.

(H2)
There exist

and

such that for all
y,\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\rho,\bar{\rho}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\displaystyle|f(y,\rho)-f(\bar{y},\bar{\rho})|\leq C\left(\left(1+|y|^{\gamma}+|\bar{y}|^{\gamma}\right)|y-\bar{y}|+\mathbb{W}_{2}(\rho,\bar{\rho})\right).

(3.3)

The conditions
(H1)
and
(H2)
imply that

\displaystyle\left|f(y,\rho)\right|

\displaystyle~\leq\left|f(y,\rho)-f(0,\delta_{0})\right|+\left|f(0,\delta_{0})\right|\leq C\left(1+|y|^{\gamma+1}+\mathbb{W}_{2}\left(\rho,\delta_{0}\right)\right).

(3.4)

Based on
(A3)

2.1

\left|g(y,\rho)-g(\bar{y},\bar{\rho})\right|^{2}\leq C\left(\left(1+|y|^{\gamma}+|\bar{y}|^{\gamma}\right)\left|y-\bar{y}\right|^{2}+\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2}(\rho,\bar{\rho})\right),

(3.5)

which implies the polynomial growth

\left|g(y,\rho)\right|\leq C\big(1+|y|^{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}+\mathbb{W}_{2}\left(\rho,\delta_{0}\right)\big).

(3.6)

To handle the higher-order terms in the Milstein-type schemes, we further assume polynomial growth conditions on the differential operators

\partial_{y}f
,
\partial_{y}g_{j}
,
\partial_{\rho}f
and
\partial_{\rho}g_{j}
(for
j=1,2,\cdots,m
).

Assumption 3.2
.

For all
y,\bar{y},z,\bar{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\rho,\bar{\rho}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the derivatives satisfy:

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{y}f(y,\rho)-\partial_{y}f(\bar{y},\bar{\rho})\right|

\displaystyle~\leq C\left(\left(1+|y|^{\gamma-1}+|\bar{y}|^{\gamma-1}\right)|y-\bar{y}|+\mathbb{W}_{2}(\rho,\bar{\rho})\right),

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{y}\,g_{j}(y,\rho)-\partial_{y}\,g_{j}(\bar{y},\bar{\rho})\right|

\displaystyle~\leq C\Big(\big(1+|y|^{\frac{\gamma}{2}-1}+|\bar{y}|^{\frac{\gamma}{2}-1}\big)|y-\bar{y}|+\mathbb{W}_{2}(\rho,\bar{\rho})\Big),

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{\rho}f(y,\rho,z)-\partial_{\rho}f(\bar{y},\bar{\rho},\bar{z})\right|

\displaystyle~\leq C\left(\left(1+|y|^{\gamma}+|\bar{y}|^{\gamma}\right)|y-\bar{y}|+|z-\bar{z}|+\mathbb{W}_{2}(\rho,\bar{\rho})\right),

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{\rho}\,g_{j}(y,\rho,z)-\partial_{\rho}\,g_{j}(\bar{y},\bar{\rho},\bar{z})\right|

\displaystyle~\leq C\Big(\big(1+|y|^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}+|\bar{y}|^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\big)|y-\bar{y}|+|z-\bar{z}|+\mathbb{W}_{2}(\rho,\bar{\rho})\Big),

with some constants
C>0,\gamma\geq 2
.

Similar arguments in (
3.4
) and (
3.6
), these conditions yield the growth bounds:

\displaystyle|\partial_{y}f(y,\rho)|\leq

\displaystyle~C\left(1+|y|^{\gamma}+\mathbb{W}_{2}\left(\rho,\delta_{0}\right)\right),

(3.7)

\displaystyle|\partial_{y}\,g_{j}(y,\rho)|\leq

\displaystyle~C\left(1+|y|^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}+\mathbb{W}_{2}\left(\rho,\delta_{0}\right)\right),

(3.8)

\displaystyle|\partial_{\rho}f(y,\rho,z)|\leq

\displaystyle~C\left(1+|y|^{\gamma+1}+|z|+\mathbb{W}_{2}\left(\rho,\delta_{0}\right)\right),

(3.9)

\displaystyle|\partial_{\rho}\,g_{j}(y,\rho,z)|\leq

\displaystyle~C\left(1+|y|^{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}+|z|+\mathbb{W}_{2}\left(\rho,\delta_{0}\right)\right).

(3.10)

In addition to the conditions on the coefficients, we introduce assumptions on the operators
\{\Gamma_{l}(\cdot)\}_{l=1}^{4}
, which are applied to the mappings
\mathds{F}_{l}
corresponding to
f
,
g_{j}
,
\mathcal{L}_{y}^{j_{2}}g_{j_{1}}
, and
\mathcal{L}_{\rho}^{j_{2}}g_{j_{1}}
.

Assumption 3.3
.

There exist
C,\zeta_{l}>0~(l=1,2,3,4)
such that

\displaystyle\big|\Gamma_{l}\big(\mathds{F}_{l}(\cdot),\Delta t\big)\big|

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta t^{-\zeta_{l}}\wedge|\mathds{F}_{l}(\cdot)|.

Assumption 3.4
.

There exist
C,r_{1},r_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle\left|\Gamma_{1}(f,\Delta t)-f\right|\leq

\displaystyle C\Delta t^{r_{1}}\left|f\right|^{r_{2}}.

The Assumption
3.4
can be viewed as a consistency requirement for the operator
\Gamma_{1}
, ensuring that it approximates
f
with an error that vanishes as
\Delta t\to 0
for any fixed
f\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In other words,
\Gamma_{1}(f,\Delta t)\to f
as
\Delta t\to 0
, and the bound in Assumption
3.4
quantifies both the rate of convergence (via
r_{1}
) and its possible growth with respect to
\lvert f\rvert
(via
r_{2}
).

To establish the strong convergence order of the Milstein-type schemes (
3.1
), it is necessary to derive moment bounds for the numerical solutions.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose Assumptions
2.1
and
3.1
-
3.4
hold. Then there exist
C,\beta>0
, such that

\displaystyle\sup_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\mathbb{E}\left[|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}|^{p}\right]\leq C\bigg(1+\Big(\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|Y_{0}\big|^{\bar{p}}\Big]\Big)^{\beta}\bigg),\quad p\in\left[2,\frac{\bar{p}-\Theta}{1+\bar{\zeta}\Theta}\right],\quad k=0,1,\cdots,n,

(3.11)

where
\Theta:=\Theta(\gamma,r_{1},r_{2})=\frac{r_{2}(\gamma+1)-1}{r_{1}}\vee 3\gamma
and
\bar{\zeta}=\zeta_{1}\vee\left(\zeta_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\vee\zeta_{3}\vee\zeta_{4}
. Here
\gamma,\{\zeta_{l}\}_{l=1}^{4},r_{1},r_{2}
come from (H2), Assumptions
3.3
and
3.4
. Additionally,
\bar{p}\geq 2+(2\bar{\zeta}+1)\Theta
.

The proof of this lemma follows by extending the techniques developed in
Zhang and Ma 2017
,
Zhao et al. 2024
,
Jian et al. 2025
;
a detailed argument is provided in
B
.

3.3
Strong convergence of order
1
for Milstein-type schemes (
3.1
)

In order to obtain the strong convergence order of the Milstein-type scheme (
3.1
) for the IPS defined by (
2.1
), we consider the continuous-time formulation of the scheme. For
s\in[0,\mathcal{T}]
, define
\tau_{n}(s):=\frac{\lfloor ns\rfloor}{n}=\sup\{t_{k}\in\{t_{l}\}_{l=0}^{n}:t_{k}\leq s\}
which represents the piecewise constant projection of
s
. The continuous-time Milstein-type approximation corresponding to (
3.1
) then takes the form

Y_{t}^{i,N}=Y_{0}^{i,N}+\int_{0}^{t}\Gamma_{1}\Big(f\Big(Y_{\tau_{n}(s)}^{i,N},\rho_{\tau_{n}(s)}^{Y,N}\Big),\Delta t\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s+\sum_{j_{1}=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t}\Gamma_{g}\Big(\hat{g}_{j_{1}}\Big(s,Y_{\tau_{n}(s)}^{i,N},\rho_{\tau_{n}(s)}^{Y,N}\Big),\Delta t\Big)\,\mathrm{d}W^{i,j_{1}}_{s},

(3.12)

where
{\Gamma}_{g}(\cdot)
is defined as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{g}\Big(\hat{g}_{j_{1}}\Big(s,Y_{\tau_{n}(s)}^{i,N},\rho_{\tau_{n}(s)}^{Y,N}\Big),\Delta t\Big)

(3.13)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}\Big(g_{j_{1}}\Big(Y_{\tau_{n}(s)}^{i,N},\rho_{\tau_{n}(s)}^{Y,N}\Big),\Delta t\Big)+\sum_{j_{2}=1}^{m}\int_{\tau_{n}(s)}^{s}\Gamma_{3}\Big(\mathcal{L}_{y}^{j_{2}}~g_{j_{1}}\Big(Y_{\tau_{n}(r)}^{i,N},\rho_{\tau_{n}(r)}^{Y,N}\Big),\Delta t\Big)\,\mathrm{d}W^{i,j_{2}}_{r}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j_{2}=1}^{m}\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{N}\int_{\tau_{n}(s)}^{s}\Gamma_{4}\Big(\mathcal{L}_{\rho}^{j_{2}}~g_{j_{1}}\Big(Y_{\tau_{n}(r)}^{i,N},\rho_{\tau_{n}(r)}^{Y,N},Y_{\tau_{n}(r)}^{k_{1},N}\Big),\Delta t\Big)\,\mathrm{d}W^{k_{1},j_{2}}_{r}.

To ensure convergence, we impose the following condition on the difference between each approximation operator
\Gamma_{l}
and its corresponding original function
\mathds{F}_{l}
, where
\mathds{F}_{l}
stands for
f,g
,
\mathcal{L}_{y}^{j_{2}}g_{j_{1}}
,
\mathcal{L}_{\rho}^{j_{2}}g_{j_{1}}

for
l=1,2,3,4
, respectively.

Assumption 3.5
.

There exist constants
C>0,~\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\geq 1
,
\delta_{3},\delta_{4}\geq\frac{1}{2}
and
\gamma_{l}\geq 1
such that

\displaystyle\left|\Gamma_{l}\left(\mathds{F}_{l}(\cdot),\Delta t\right)-\mathds{F}_{l}(\cdot)\right|\leq

\displaystyle~C\Delta t^{\delta_{l}}\left|\mathds{F}_{l}(\cdot)\right|^{\gamma_{l}},\quad l=1,2,3,4.

This assumption strengthens Assumption
3.4
by providing more precise control over the approximation operators.

We summarize our main result below, the complete proof and the necessary auxiliary estimates are provided in Section
5
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let Assumptions
2.1
,
3.1
-
3.3
and
3.5
be satisfied. Then there exist
C,\beta>0
such that

\sup_{t\in[0,\mathcal{T}]}\sup_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\mathbb{E}\left[\Big|X_{t}^{i,N}-Y_{t}^{i,N}\Big|^{p}\right]\leq C\bigg(1+\Big(\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|Y_{0}\big|^{\bar{p}}\Big]\Big)^{\beta}\bigg)\Delta t^{p},\quad p\in[2,\tilde{p}\wedge\hat{p}],

where
\hat{p}
and
\tilde{p}
are from Lemmas
5.4
and
5.7
. Also,
\bar{p}
is from Lemma
3.1
.

By combining this result with Proposition
2.1
, we establish a quantitative error bound for the Milstein-type approximation (
3.12
) of the MV-SDEs (
1.1
), under the assumption that the initial value has sufficiently high bounded moments.

Corollary 3.1
.

Under the same conditions of Theorem
3.1
, we have

\sup_{t\in[0,\mathcal{T}]}\sup_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\mathbb{E}\big[\,|X_{t}^{i}-Y_{t}^{i,N}|^{2}\,\big]\leq C\big(\eta_{d}(N)+\Delta t^{2}\big),

where
\eta_{d}(N)
is defined in Proposition
2.1
and

is independent of
n
and

.

3.4
Some examples of Milstein-type schemes

We present several examples with specified
\Gamma_{l}
for
l=1,2,3,4
in (
3.1
). These examples demonstrate possible forms of the operators
\Gamma_{l}
for
l=1,2,3,4
within the proposed framework.

-

Tanh Milstein (TanhM)

\Gamma_{l}\big(\mathds{F}_{l}(\cdot),\Delta t\big)=\Delta t^{-1}\tanh\big(\Delta t\,\mathds{F}_{l}(\cdot)\big),\quad l=1,2,3,4.

(3.14)

-

Sine Milstein (SineM)

\Gamma_{l}\big(\mathds{F}_{l}(\cdot),\Delta t\big)=\Delta t^{-1}\sin\big(\Delta t\,\mathds{F}_{l}(\cdot)\big),\quad l=1,2,3,4.

(3.15)

-

Tamed Milstein (TameM)

\Gamma_{l}\big(\mathds{F}_{l}(\cdot),\Delta t\big)=\frac{\mathds{F}_{l}(\cdot)}{1+\Delta t\left|\mathds{F}_{l}(\cdot)\right|},\quad l=1,2,3,4.

(3.16)

-

Mixed Milstein (MixM)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}\big(\mathds{F}_{1}(\cdot),\Delta t\big)

\displaystyle=\Delta t^{-1}\tanh\big(\Delta t\,\mathds{F}_{1}(\cdot)\big),\quad\Gamma_{2}\big(\mathds{F}_{2}(\cdot),\Delta t\big)=\Delta t^{-1}\sin\big(\Delta t\,\mathds{F}_{2}(\cdot)\big),

\displaystyle\Gamma_{3}\big(\mathds{F}_{3}(\cdot),\Delta t\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathds{F}_{3}(\cdot)}{1+\Delta t\left|\mathds{F}_{3}(\cdot)\right|},\quad\Gamma_{4}\big(\mathds{F}_{4}(\cdot),\Delta t\big)=\Delta t^{-1}\tanh\big(\Delta t\,\mathds{F}_{4}(\cdot)\big).

(3.17)

For (
3.14
), based on
|\tanh(y)|\leq|y|
and
|\tanh(y)|\leq 1
, we observe the following

\left|\Gamma_{l}\left(\mathds{F}_{l}(\cdot),\Delta t\right)\right|\leq\left|\mathds{F}_{l}(\cdot)\right|,\quad\left|\Gamma_{l}\left(\mathds{F}_{l}(\cdot),\Delta t\right)\right|\leq\Delta t^{-1}.

This implies that Assumption
3.3
is valid with
\zeta_{l}=1,\,l=1,2,3,4
. Furthermore, applying the property of the hyperbolic tangent function for any
0\leq\theta\leq 1
,

|y-\tanh(y)|\leq|y|^{3-2\theta}
, we arrive at

\left|\Gamma_{l}(\mathds{F}_{l}(\cdot),\Delta t)-\mathds{F}_{l}(\cdot)\right|=\Delta t^{-1}\left|\tanh\left(\Delta t\mathds{F}_{l}(\cdot)\right)-\Delta t\mathds{F}_{l}(\cdot)\right|\leq\Delta t|\mathds{F}_{l}(\cdot)|^{2}.

Thus, Assumption
3.5
is fulfilled with
\delta_{l}=1
and
\gamma_{l}=2,\,l=1,2,3,4
.

Turning to the Sine Milstein method (
3.15
), it is straightforward to verify that the assumptions
3.3
and
3.5
hold with
\zeta_{l}=1
,
\delta_{l}=1
, and
\gamma_{l}=2
for
l=1,2,3,4
, following the similar arguments as for (
3.14
).

For the Tamed Milstein method (
3.16
), we verify that

|\Gamma_{l}\left(\mathds{F}_{l}(\cdot),\Delta t\right)|\leq\left|\mathds{F}_{l}(\cdot)\right|,\quad|\Gamma_{l}\left(\mathds{F}_{l}(\cdot),\Delta t\right)|\leq\frac{\left|\mathds{F}_{l}(\cdot)\right|}{\Delta t\left|\mathds{F}_{l}(\cdot)\right|}\leq\Delta t^{-1}.

This shows that Assumption
3.3
holds with
\zeta_{l}=1
for
l=1,2,3,4
.
Moreover, according to (
3.16
), we have

\left|\Gamma_{l}\left(\mathds{F}_{l}(\cdot),\Delta t\right)-\mathds{F}_{l}(\cdot)\right|^{2}\leq\frac{|\mathds{F}_{l}(\cdot)|^{2}\Delta t}{1+\Delta t|\mathds{F}_{l}(\cdot)|}\leq\Delta t|\mathds{F}_{l}(\cdot)|^{2},

i.e. Assumption
3.5
is satisfied with
\delta_{l}=1,
and
\gamma_{l}=2,~l=1,2,3,4
.

Finally, in the case of the Mixed Milstein method (
-
‣
3.4
), following a similar verification procedure as for the other methods, we confirm that Assumptions
3.3
and
3.5
are satisfied with
\zeta_{l}=1
,
\delta_{l}=1
, and
\gamma_{l}=2
for
l=1,2,3,4
.

∎

6
Conclusion and discussion

This work develops a unified framework of Milstein-type discretizations for the IPS associated with MV-SDEs whose drift and diffusion coefficients grow superlinearly.
Within this framework, we establish moment bounds and prove order-one strong convergence under mild regularity conditions, requiring only once differentiability of the coefficients and bounded operator modifications that scale with a negative power of the time step size. Numerical experiments on mean-square errors show slopes near
1
in log-log plots across the tested step sizes, consistent with the predicted convergence rate for Milstein-type schemes (TanhM, SineM, TamedM and MixM).
Compared with the classical Milstein method, which diverges in the superlinear growth, the proposed schemes remain stable and accurately reproduce the qualitative behavior of the underlying dynamics.
The empirical distributions preserve expected bimodal or unimodal structures while maintaining numerical stability even for relatively large step sizes.
A promising direction for future research is the long-time analysis of the proposed schemes, particularly their ergodic behavior and asymptotic stability in high-dimensional particle systems, which remains a mathematically challenging problem.

Differentiability of functions defined on measures.

A function
h:\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be differentiable at
\nu_{0}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if there exists a square-integrable
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued random variable
Y_{0}\in L^{2}(\tilde{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d})
on an atomless Polish space
(\tilde{\Omega},\tilde{\mathcal{F}},\tilde{\mathbb{P}})
such that
\mathscr{L}_{Y_{0}}=\nu_{0}
, and the lifted function
H(Z):=h(\mathscr{L}_{Z})
is Fréchet differentiable at
Y_{0}
. The function
h
is said to belong to
C^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
if its lifting
H(X)=h(\mathscr{L}_{X})
, defined on
L^{2}(\tilde{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, lies in
C^{1}(L^{2}(\tilde{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d}))
. By the Riesz representation theorem, the derivative
H^{\prime}(Y_{0})\in(L^{2}(\tilde{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{*}
admits a unique representation:

H^{\prime}(Y_{0})(Z)=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[\langle DH(Y_{0}),Z\rangle\right],\quad\forall Z\in L^{2}(\tilde{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d}),

with
DH(Y_{0})\in L^{2}(\tilde{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d})
. It follows from
[
Carmona and Delarue 2018
, Proposition 5.25]
that there exists a measurable function
\partial_{\rho}h(\nu_{0}):\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, independent of the choice of
Y_{0}
, such that

DH(Y_{0})=\partial_{\rho}h(\nu_{0})(Y_{0}),\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\partial_{\rho}h(\nu_{0})(x)|^{2}\nu_{0}(dx)<\infty.

This function
\partial_{\rho}h(\nu_{0})(\cdot)
is called the
Lions derivative
of
h
, and we write
\partial_{\rho}h:\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
\partial_{\rho}h(\nu,x):=\partial_{\rho}h(\nu)(x)
. When dealing with vector- or matrix-valued functions, this definition is understood to hold for each component individually.

dos Reis et al. 2019
, Lemma 2.3]
, together with an application of Jensen’s inequality, that for any
q\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{2}^{q}\left(\rho_{t_{k}}^{Y,N},\delta_{0}\right)=\mathbb{W}_{2}^{q}\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N},}\delta_{0}\bigg)\leq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}\right|^{q}.

(B.11)

Upon successive substitution of (
B.11
) into (
B
) and then into (
B
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{R},k+1}^{i}}\left|Y_{t_{k+1}}^{i,N}\right|^{\bar{p}}\bigg]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{R},k}^{i}}\left|Y_{t_{k+1}}^{i,N}\right|^{\bar{p}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~C\Delta t+\left(1+C\Delta t\right)\sup_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{R},k}^{i}}\left|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}\right|^{\bar{p}}\bigg]+C\sup_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{R},k}^{i}}\left|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}\right|^{\bar{p}-1+r_{2}(\gamma+1)}\bigg]\Delta t^{1+r_{1}}

\displaystyle~+C\sum_{l=3}^{\bar{p}}\sup_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{R},k}^{i}}\left|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}\right|^{\bar{p}+\frac{\gamma}{2}l}\bigg]\Delta t^{\frac{l}{2}}+C\sum_{l=2}^{\bar{p}}\sup_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{R},k}^{i}}\left|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}\right|^{\bar{p}+\gamma l+l}\right]\Delta t^{l}.

(B.12)

With the choice
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}(\Delta t)=\Delta t^{-1/\Theta(\gamma,r_{1},r_{2})}
, where
\Theta(\gamma,r_{1},r_{2})=\frac{r_{2}(\gamma+1)-1}{r_{1}}\vee 3\gamma
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{R},k}^{i}}\left|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}\right|^{\bar{p}-1+r_{2}(\gamma+1)}\Delta t^{1+r_{1}}=\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{R},k}^{i}}\left|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}\right|^{\bar{p}}\Delta t\Big(\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{R},k}^{i}}\left|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}\right|^{r_{2}(\gamma+1)-1}\Delta t^{r_{1}}\Big)\leq C\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{R},k}^{i}}\left|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}\right|^{\bar{p}}\Delta t,

\displaystyle\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{R},k}^{i}}\left|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}\right|^{\bar{p}+\frac{\gamma}{2}l}\Delta t^{\frac{l}{2}}=\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{R},k}^{i}}\left|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}\right|^{\bar{p}}\Delta t\Big(\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{R},k}^{i}}\left|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}\right|^{\frac{\gamma}{2}l}\Delta t^{\frac{l}{2}-1}\Big)\leq C\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{R},k}^{i}}\left|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}\right|^{\bar{p}}\Delta t\;,l=3,\cdots,\bar{p},

\displaystyle\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{R},k}^{i}}\left|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}\right|^{\bar{p}+\gamma l+l}\Delta t^{l}=\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{R},k}^{i}}\left|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}\right|^{\bar{p}}\Delta t\Big(\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{R},k}^{i}}\left|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}\right|^{l(\gamma+1)}\Delta t^{l-1}\Big)\leq C\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{R},k}^{i}}\left|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}\right|^{\bar{p}}\Delta t\;,l=2,\cdots,\bar{p}.

From the above estimations, it follows that

\displaystyle\sup_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{i}_{\mathcal{R},k+1}}|Y_{t_{k+1}}^{i,N}|^{\bar{p}}\right]\leq~\sup_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{i}_{\mathcal{R},k}}|Y_{t_{k+1}}^{i,N}|^{\bar{p}}\right]\leq~C\Delta t+(1+C\Delta t)\sup_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{i}_{\mathcal{R},k}}|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}|^{\bar{p}}\right].

By recursion, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{i}_{\mathcal{R},k+1}}|Y_{t_{k+1}}^{i,N}|^{\bar{p}}\right]\leq

\displaystyle~C\left(1+\mathbb{E}\left[|Y_{0}|^{\bar{p}}\right]\right).

(B.13)

In what follows, we concentrate on estimating
\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{R},k}^{i,c}}\left|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}\right|^{p}\right]
. To this end, we return to the Milstein schemes (
3.1
), from which we deduce that

\displaystyle\bigg|Y_{t_{k+1}}^{i,N}\bigg|\leq

\displaystyle\bigg|Y_{0}^{i,N}\bigg|+\sum_{l=0}^{k}\bigg|\Gamma_{1}\left(f\left(Y_{t_{l}}^{i,N},\rho_{t_{l}}^{Y,N}\right),\Delta t\right)\Delta t\bigg|+\sum_{l=0}^{k}\bigg|\sum_{j_{1}=1}^{m}\Gamma_{2}\left(g_{j_{1}}\left(Y_{t_{l}}^{i,N},\rho_{t_{l}}^{Y,N}\right),\Delta t\right)\Delta W^{i,j_{1}}_{t_{l}}\bigg|

\displaystyle~+\sum_{l=0}^{k}\bigg|\sum_{j_{1},j_{2}=1}^{m}\Gamma_{3}\left(\mathcal{L}_{y}^{j_{2}}~g_{j_{1}}\left(Y_{t_{l}}^{i,N},\rho_{t_{l}}^{Y,N}\right),\Delta t\right)\int_{t_{l}}^{t_{l+1}}\int_{t_{l}}^{s}\,\mathrm{d}W^{i,j_{2}}_{r}\,\mathrm{d}W^{i,j_{1}}_{s}\bigg|

\displaystyle~+\sum_{l=0}^{k}\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j_{1},j_{2}=1}^{m}\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{N}\Gamma_{4}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\rho}^{j_{2}}~g_{j_{1}}\Big(Y_{t_{l}}^{i,N},\rho_{t_{l}}^{Y,N},Y_{t_{l}}^{k_{1},N}\right),\Delta t\Big)\int_{t_{l}}^{t_{l+1}}\int_{t_{l}}^{s}\,\mathrm{d}W^{k_{1},j_{2}}_{r}\,\mathrm{d}W^{i,j_{1}}_{s}\bigg|.

Applying the elementary inequality
\Big(\sum\limits_{l=0}^{k}a_{l}\Big)^{\bar{p}}\leq(k+1)^{\bar{p}-1}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{k}a_{l}^{\bar{p}},~a_{l}>0
, properties of conditional expectation, together with Assumption
3.3
and moment inequalities, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|Y_{t_{k+1}}^{i,N}\Big|^{\bar{p}}\Big]\leq

\displaystyle~C\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|Y_{0}^{i,N}\Big|^{\bar{p}}\Big]+C\Delta t^{-\bar{p}\zeta_{1}}+C\Delta t^{-\bar{p}\left(\zeta_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right)}+C\Delta t^{-\bar{p}\zeta_{3}}+C\Delta t^{-\bar{p}\zeta_{4}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~C\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|Y_{0}^{i,N}\Big|^{\bar{p}}\Big]+C\Delta t^{-\bar{p}\bar{\zeta}},

where
\bar{\zeta}=\zeta_{1}\vee\left(\zeta_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\vee\zeta_{3}\vee\zeta_{4}
. Therefore, we can conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}\Big|^{\bar{p}}\Big]\leq~C\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|Y_{0}^{i,N}\Big|^{\bar{p}}\Big]+C\Delta t^{-\bar{p}\bar{\zeta}}.

(B.14)

Moreover, we observe that

\displaystyle\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{R},k}^{i,c}}=

\displaystyle~1-\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{i}_{\mathcal{R},k}}=1-\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{i}_{\mathcal{R},k-1}}\mathds{1}_{\big|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}\big|\leq\mathcal{R}}=\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{R},k-1}^{i,c}}+\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{i}_{\mathcal{R},k-1}}\mathds{1}_{\left|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}\right|>\mathcal{R}}=\sum_{l=0}^{k}\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{i}_{\mathcal{R},l-1}}\mathds{1}_{\big|Y_{t_{l}}^{i,N}\big|>\mathcal{R}},

where
\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{i}_{\mathcal{R},-1}}=1
. Based on the previous derivation, and applying
\rm H\ddot{o}lder
’s inequality (with
\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=1
) together with Chebyshev’s inequality, it follows that for
p\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{R},k}^{i,c}}\left|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}\right|^{p}\Big]=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{k}\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{i}_{\mathcal{R},l-1}}\mathds{1}_{|Y_{t_{l}}^{i,N}|>\mathcal{R}}\left|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}\right|^{p}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\sum_{l=0}^{k}\Big(\mathbb{E}\Big[\left|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}\right|^{p\cdot p^{\prime}}\Big]\Big)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\Big(\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{i}_{\mathcal{R},l-1}}\mathds{1}_{|Y_{t_{l}}^{i,N}|>\mathcal{R}}\Big]\Big)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\Big(\mathbb{E}\Big[\left|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}\right|^{p\cdot p^{\prime}}\Big]\Big)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\frac{\Big(\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{i}_{\mathcal{R},l-1}}\left|Y_{t_{l}}^{i,N}\right|^{\bar{p}}\Big]\Big)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}}{(\mathcal{R})^{\bar{p}/q^{\prime}}},

(B.15)

where
q^{\prime}=\frac{\bar{p}}{(\bar{\zeta}p+1)\Theta}>1
, as
p\leq\frac{\bar{p}-\Theta}{1+\bar{\zeta}\Theta}
.

Since
p\leq\frac{\bar{p}-\Theta}{1+\bar{\zeta}\Theta}
, we deduce that
pp^{\prime}\leq\bar{p}
. Applying
\mathrm{H\ddot{o}lder}
’s inequality and (
B.14
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\Big(\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}\big|^{p\cdot p^{\prime}}\Big]\Big)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\leq\Big(\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}\big|^{p\cdot p^{\prime}\cdot\frac{\bar{p}}{pp^{\prime}}}\Big]\Big)^{\frac{pp^{\prime}}{\bar{p}}\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\leq C\Big(1+\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|Y_{0}\big|^{\bar{p}}\Big]\Big)^{\frac{p}{\bar{p}}}+C\Delta t^{-\bar{\zeta}p}.

(B.16)

With
\mathcal{R}(\Delta t)=\Delta t^{-1/\Theta(\gamma,r_{1},r_{2})}
, substituting of (
B.16
) and (
B.13
) into (
B
) yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{R},k}^{i,c}}\big|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}\big|^{p}\Big]\leq

\displaystyle~C(k+1)\Delta t^{\bar{\zeta}p+1}\Big(C\Delta t^{-\bar{\zeta}p}+C\Big(1+\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|Y_{0}\big|^{\bar{p}}\Big]\Big)^{\frac{p}{\bar{p}}}\Big)\cdot\Big[C\Big(1+\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|Y_{0}\big|^{\bar{p}}\Big]\Big)\Big]^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~C\Big(1+\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|Y_{0}\big|^{\bar{p}}\Big]\Big)^{\frac{p}{\bar{p}}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}.

(B.17)

Combining
\rm H\ddot{o}lder
’s inequality, (
B.13
) and (
B
), we show

\displaystyle\sup_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}\big|^{p}\Big]=

\displaystyle~\sup_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{R},k}}\big|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}\big|^{p}\Big]+\sup_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{R},k}^{c}}\big|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}\big|^{p}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\Big(\sup_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{R},k}}\big|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}\big|^{\bar{p}}\,\Big]\Big)^{\frac{p}{\bar{p}}}+\sup_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{R},k}^{c}}\big|Y_{t_{k}}^{i,N}\big|^{p}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~C\Big(1+\Big(\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|Y_{0}\big|^{\bar{p}}\Big]\Big)^{\beta}\,\Big),

where
\beta>0
. Then, using
\rm H\ddot{o}lder
’s inequality, the estimate (
3.11
) can be extended to all
p\in[2,\frac{\bar{p}-\Theta}{1+\bar{\zeta}\Theta}]
, including odd and non-integer values.
∎

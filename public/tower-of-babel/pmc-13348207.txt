Hepatic perivascular epithelioid cell tumor (PEComa): Clinicopathological Findings and Prognosis-A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
Authors: Alireza Abdollahi, Masoomeh Safaei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground & Objective:Hepatic Perivascular epithelioid cell tumors (PEComas) are rare and can complicate diagnosis due to their low incidence, possible malignant potential and misdiagnosis as melanoma and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). The purpose of this systematic review and meta-analysis was to investigate the clinicopathological traits, diagnostic indicators, and prognostic implications associated with hepatic PEComas.Methods:We conducted a systematic literature search in PubMed, Web of Science, and Scopus using relevant keywords. Studies were included if they assessed clinicopathological findings or prognostic outcomes of hepatic PEComas. Data extraction was performed using a standardized sheet and data were pooled using RStudio.Results:The initial search retrieved 1,172 articles, from which 286 duplicates were removed. Ultimately, 15 studies met our eligibility criteria for inclusion in the meta-analysis. The mean patient age was 45.42 years. Misdiagnosis as HCC occurred in 18% of cases. The pooled malignancy rate was 8.6% with 6% of cases demonstrating tumor progression. Immunohistochemistry analysis revealed HMB-45 positivity in 98.8% (95% CI: 97% - 100%) and Smooth Muscle Actin positivity in 95.2% (95% CI: 81% - 98%) of cases. The mean tumor size was 67.1 mm (95% CI: 52.86 - 81.34), with 4.7% (95% CI: 3% - 8%) showing invasive features.Conclusion:Hepatic PEComas are predominantly benign but can pose diagnostic challenges due to their resemblance to HCC and melanoma. Despite generally favorable outcomes, long-term follow-up is crucial due to the risk of recurrence. Further prospective studies utilizing standardized diagnostic criteria are warranted to refine management strategies and identify prognostic indicators.Key Words: Hepatic PEComa, perivascular epithelioid cell tumor, angiomyolipoma, immunohistochemistry, malignancy

Introduction

Perivascular epithelioid cell tumors (PEComas) are rare mesenchymal neoplasms characterized by distinct perivascular epithelioid cells (1). These tumors can occur in various anatomical sites. When they arise in the liver, they are referred to as hepatic PEComas. Due to their rarity, the clinicopathological characteristics and prognosis of hepatic PEComas are poorly understood (2).

PEComas encompass a diverse group of tumors, most prominently including angiomyolipoma (AML) and lymphangioleiomyomatosis (LAM). AMLs are benign tumors composed of a heterogeneous collection of aberrant blood vessels, smooth muscle, and fat cells (3). While AMLs arise predominantly in the kidneys, they can also occur in other organs. LAM is a progressive pulmonary disease characterized by the proliferation of smooth muscle-like cells, leading to cystic lung destruction (4). Both AML and LAM share a common origin with PEComas, arising from perivascular epithelioid cells and often exhibiting mutations in either the TSC1 or TSC2 genes, which are associated with the mTOR signaling pathway (5).

Hepatic PEComas are usually asymptomatic and may be incidentally detected during imaging studies conducted for unrelated reasons (6). When symptoms do manifest, they are usually nonspecific, such as abdominal pain or discomfort (7). Histologically, these tumors consist of epithelioid cells with clear to granular cytoplasm arranged in a perivascular distribution (8). Immunohistochemically, hepatic PEComas frequently express melanocytic markers such as HMB-45 (Human Melanoma Black), Melan-A (Melanocyte Antigen), and smooth muscle markers such as actin (SMA) (9).

The relationship between hepatic PEComas, AML, and LAM is understood by their shared origin from perivascular epithelioid cells and common genetic mutations. This connection suggests that insights gained from studying AML and LAM may enhance the understanding of hepatic PEComas. However, while most hepatic PEComas are benign, certain cases have exhibited malignant characteristics, including aggressive local recurrence and distant metastasis. Therefore, accurate diagnosis and appropriate management are crucial (8, 10, 11). The criteria for the diagnosis of malignant PEComas are based on the presence of at least two of the following histologic features; 1) tumor size > 5 cm, 2) high nuclear grade, 3) hypercellularity, 4) mitotic rate > 1/50 HPF, 5) necrosis, 6) infiltration into surrounding normal parenchyma, or 7) vascular invasion (10).

In this systematic review and meta-analysis, we aimed to critically analyze the clinicopathological features and prognostic significane of hepatic PEComas. By reviewing existing literature, we seek to identify key determinants of patient outcomes and provide evidence-based recommendations for the diagnosis and management of this rare tumor.

Materials and Methods

This systematic review and meta-analysis study was conducted based on the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guideline 2020 (12).

Search strategy

Two authors performed a systematic search of literature in the following electronic databases: PubMed, Web of Science, and Scopus. No time limitation was applied and all English studies from inception until December 2024 were included. The relevant medical subject heading (MeSH) terms and related keywords were used in combination to build the search strategy:

“Perivascular Epithelioid Cell Neoplasms” OR “PEComa” OR “Perivascular Epithelioid Cell Tumors” OR “Perivascular Epithelioid Cell Neoplasm” OR “ Perivascular Epithelioid Cell Tumor” OR “PEComas” OR “Clear-Cell Sugar Tumors” OR “Clear Cell Sugar Tumor” OR “Clear-Cell Myomelanocytic Tumors” OR “Clear Cell Myomelanocytic Tumors” OR “CCMMT” OR “Clear-Cell Myomelanocytic Tumor” OR “Clear Cell Myomelanocytic Tumor” ) AND (“hepatic” OR “hepatics” OR “liver” OR “livers”).

Eligibility criteria

Our eligibility criteria were defined based on the PICO framework (population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome):

(P) Population: patients with perivascular epithelioid cell tumor.

(I) Intervention: Not Applicable.

(C) Comparison: Clinical outcomes based on diagnostic evaluations (e.g., histopathological and immunohistochemical studies) and treatment strategies (e.g., surgical vs. non-surgical management).

(O) Outcome: Treatment response, recurrence rates, malignancy rates and prognostic factors.

Studies that did not include control groups, studies with unavailable full text or required data; and studies not published in English were also excluded.

Data extraction and outcome measures

A standardized Excel sheet was prepared to extract relevant data including study characteristics such as the first author’s name, year of publication, study design, country, total number of cases, gender distribution, mean age, and reference of comparison. Two independent authors conducted the data extraction. Disagreements regarding inclusion, exclusion, or data extraction were resolved by a third author.

Data synthesis and Statistical Analysis

Statistical analyses and figure generation were performed using RStudio (RStudio, Inc., Boston, MA, USA) with R software, and pooled proportions and means were estimated using the meta package (13). Proportions were pooled using the logit transformation method, while means were pooled using a random-effects model to account for variability among studies. The 95% confidence interval for proportions was also calculated using the logit transformation. The overall significance of the random-effects model was tested using a z-test. To assess heterogeneity among the included studies, I² and Tau² tests were used. Forest plots were created to display individual study estimates and pooled results graphically. Publication bias was assessed graphically using funnel plots and statistically using both Begg’s and Egger’s tests.

Risk of bias was assessed using the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) critical appraisal tools. The JBI checklist for case reports was used for single-patient studies, the JBI checklist for case series for studies reporting multiple patients, and the JBI checklist for analytical cross-sectional studies for retrospective and single-center observational studies. Overall, the included studies demonstrated a moderate risk of bias. This was mainly attributable to the predominance of case reports, case series, and retrospective study designs. Common limitations included incomplete follow-up data, unclear patient selection methods, and limited control of confounding factors.

Due to the limited number of included studies and incomplete reporting of effect estimates, calculation of pooled p-values was not feasible. Therefore, the meta-analysis results should be interpreted as exploratory rather than confirmatory.

Results

Our initial search retrieved 1,172 articles from PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science, from which 289 duplicates were removed. After screening the title and abstract of 883 records were excluded leaving 37 full texts for review. Finally, 34 studies were included based on the eligibility criteria (14-47), among which 15 were included in our meta-analysis (Fig. 1). Characteristics of the included studies

A total of ‌34 studies, including 450 patients diagnosed with hepatic PEComas and AMLs, were systematically reviewed. Among these patients, 342 (76%) were female and 108 (24%) were male. The mean age of patients was 45.42 years, ranging from 10 to 73 years. Eighty patients (18%) were initially misdiagnosed with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). Among the 53 patients with hepatitis, 34 were infected with hepatitis B and 19 patients with hepatitis C. The mean follow-up duration across studies was 37.4 months (ranging from one to 116 months). Progression of the tumor occurred in 27 (6%) patients during follow-up.

Based on the random effects model, the pooled proportion of malignancy in hepatic PEComa and AML was 8.6% (95% CI: 0.0183 - 0.3209). We found a statistically significant heterogeneity among the studies, with an I² value of 64.6% (95% CI: 34.3% - 80.9%). Begg’s and Egger’s tests indicated no significant publication bias for pooled malignancy proportion (p-value>0.05). Figures 2 and 3 present the forest and funnel plots for the pooled proportion of malignancy, respectively. For all pooled analyses, statistical significance of the overall effect could not be formally tested, and p-values were therefore not reported.

Discussion

PEComas are rare mesenchymal tumors first described by Bonetti et al. in 1992 (1, 2). In 2013, the World Health Organization characterized PEComas as “a mesenchymal tumor which shows a local association with vessel walls and usually expresses melanocyte and smooth muscle markers” (48, 49). This definition allows us to consider angiomyolipoma, lymphangioleiomyomatosis and clear cell sugar tumor of the lung as the PEComa family (9, 10, 50).

Based on our study the majority of patients were female (76%). This finding is consistent with previous reports suggesting a female predominance in PEComas (2, 51-53). The mean age of the patients was 45.42 years with a broad age range (10 to 73 years), which was in line with previous studies demonstrating that these tumors can affect individuals across a wide age range (8).

Approximately 8.6% of patients showed malignancy, suggesting that while most cases are benign, a subset exhibits malignant potential. This finding aligns with previous studies indicating that hepatic PEComas, though rare, can exhibit aggressive behavior, including local recurrence and distant metastasis (10, 54). The risk of malignant PEComas increases with larger tumor size, high mitotic activity, and necrosis-features that should lead to closer monitoring and aggressive management (55). Our study showed that tumor progression occurred in 6% of cases during follow-up, underscoring the importance of long-term surveillance, even in patients with initially benign-appearing tumors. The mean tumor size of 67.10 mm in our meta-analysis reflects the often-asymptomatic nature of hepatic PEComas, which are frequently detected incidentally during imaging studies for unrelated conditions. Larger tumors may be more likely to cause symptoms or exhibit malignant features, emphasizing the importance of early detection and intervention (56).

The strong expression of melanocytic markers, including HMB-45 and Melan-A, and smooth muscle markers like SMA highlights their value as reliable diagnostic markers in hepatic PEComas. As noted by Hornick et al., these tumors typically express immunoreactivity for both melanocytic and smooth muscle markers, which are critical in making a correct diagnosis (57). This dual immunophenotyping, distinguishes hepatic PEComas from the other liver tumors, including HCC and metastatic melanoma, which may share some morphological features but lack the characteristic immunoprofile of PEComas.

A notable finding from our review was the high rate of initial misdiagnosis, with 18% of patients incorrectly diagnosed with HCC. This highlights the diagnostic challenges associated with hepatic PEComas, particularly due to their radiological resemblance to HCC, as noted in previous studies (58). These challenges emphasize the need for thorough immunohistochemical evaluation to ensure accurate diagnosis and appropriate management. Another misdiagnosed malignancy was melanoma due to morphological features and positivity for HMB-45 and Melan-A. Studies have indicated that diffuse Mum-1 positivity can help distinguish between melanoma and PEComas. Additionally, S100 is typically negative in PEComas and diffuse positive in melanoma [57].

Several limitations of our study should be noted. The included studies were largely observational and retrospective, which are prone to selection and reporting biases. The considerable heterogeneity observed for certain outcomes, including tumor size and malignancy, limits the generalizability of our findings. Additionally, the lack of standardized diagnostic and reporting criteria across the studies may have contributed to variability in the results. Given the rarity of PEComa and the consequent scarcity of well-reported studies, formal hypothesis testing was not possible. The absence of pooled p-values limits the strength of statistical inference, and the results should therefore be interpreted as hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory. Future research should focus on prospective studies with standardized criteria to better characterize the clinicopathological features and prognosis of hepatic PEComas.

Conclusion

This systematic review and meta-analysis highlight the rarity, diagnostic challenge, and generally favorable prognosis of hepatic PEComas. The high expression of HMB-45 and SMA underscores the importance of immunohistochemistry as a key diagnostic tool. Although these tumors are predominantly benign, the small proportion of malignant cases require complete histological analysis and long-term follow-up. Therefore, the risk factors for malignancy should be focused in the management of these tumors. Future research should prioritize standardized criteria and prospective studies to improve diagnosis and patient outcomes.

Overall our study findings indicated that hepatic PEComa is a rare disease that can affect all ages with female predominance. Although mainly benign, the risk of malignancy should be assessed and immunohistochemical assessment is required to reduce misdiagnosis rate. However, due to the heterogeneity of data, these findings might not be generalizable to all PEComa cases.

Analysis of the functional properties of the produced layered composites
Authors: Dariusz Rydz, Mariusz Salwin, Michał Pałęga, Tomasz M. Chmielewski, Raul Duarte Salgueiral Gomes Campilho
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This paper analyzes the manufacturing process of Ti + Ni layered composites. Despite considerable interest in the production of this type of material, the literature often lacks comprehensive studies covering the entire manufacturing process. The aim of the study was to determine the effect of subsequent technological processes on the strength and quality of joints formed in flat layered composites. Studies were conducted for the joining of layered composites using explosive welding and for a plastic forming process involving the rolling of composite sheets heated to various temperatures. The study assessed the quality and durability of the joint area of Ti + Ni layered composites. The occurrence of intermetallic phases formed during the joining of Ti and Ni materials was analyzed. Shear strength measurements of the joint area were performed both after the joining process and after the rolling process. Based on the conducted research, it was concluded that the manufacturing processes, both the joining of thin composite sheets and the subsequent rolling, have a significant impact on the quality and durability of Ti + Ni layered composite joints. Based on the research results presented in the paper, it was found that the most advantageous method is to carry out the Ti + Ni production process for joining layers by explosive welding and rolling for T=600 C (the shear strength of the joint varies depending on the reduction in the range of 2–6%).

Introduction

The paper presents research results covering two main processes for manufacturing flat sandwich composites. The process of manufacturing metal sandwich composites essentially consists of two stages: joining homogeneous materials into a composite and plastically shaping the joined composites. There are many methods for joining metal materials into a sandwich composite1–4.

In this paper, due to the quality and durability of the joint, the explosive welding method is proposed for joining homogeneous materials into sandwich composites5–8. The advantage of this method is the ability to join materials that often cannot be joined using other known methods e.g., surfacing, casting, etc.9–11. Explosive welding involves joining materials using energy generated by the explosion of explosives12–14. Very often, the shear strength of the bond area is significantly higher than the requirements set by standards15–17. In the areas where they are joined, so-called intermediate layers often form, composed of mixtures of the joined metals, and are referred to in the literature as intermetallic phases18–21. Intermediate layers occurring in the areas where layers are joined often determine the functional properties of composite layered materials22–24. Therefore, it is crucial to achieve the most durable connection possible, maintaining high quality and durability both during the manufacturing process and in the finished product25–27. The key parameters determining the quality of finished composite products are obtaining a product with the appropriate geometry and durability of the connection between the components constituting the layered composite4,28,29. The quality and durability of the joint area are primarily determined by the properties of the intermetallic phases, which were tested. Furthermore, the durability and quality of the joint area are also determined by the parameters of the asymmetric rolling process of composite laminated sheets, such as relative draft and temperature. Improperly selected process parameters can lead to microcracks and tears in the joint area, ultimately leading to delamination.

The second stage of manufacturing sandwich composites involves selecting the appropriate plastic forming method. This paper analyzes the plastic forming process of flat composite products using the rolling method. This paper presents an analysis of the rolling process of Ti + Ni layered composites at two different rolling temperatures.

This study aimed to investigate how technological parameters of the production process impact the structural integrity and mechanical properties of Ti + Ni layered composites. Specifically, the study focused on analyzing the impact of explosive welding conditions and the subsequent plastic rolling process on the material’s microstructure, the formation of intermetallic phases in the joint zone, and the shear strength of the joints. The authors aimed to investigate the relationship between key process parameters, including temperature, strain rate, and cooling rate, and the quality and durability of the resulting composite. Microstructural analysis and mechanical testing were conducted simultaneously, allowing for the identification of process conditions that facilitate high bond strength while maintaining the geometric uniformity of the composite sheets. The obtained results also enable an assessment of the industrial potential of the applied process, providing data essential for further improvement of manufacturing methods for multilayer metal composites used in advanced engineering applications.

The innovative nature of this study involves an integrated approach to the influence of the explosive welding process and subsequent rolling on the bond zone properties, microstructural development, and mechanical characteristics of Ti + Ni layered composites. It should be emphasized that the available literature discusses results in which both technological stages were analyzed separately and independently. Unlike these studies, the research presented in this paper encompasses a comprehensive assessment of the entire production chain, with particular emphasis on the interrelationships between the formation of intermetallic phases, flattening of the joint geometry, and changes in shear strength. The originality of this work also stems from the use of a quantitative analysis of the effects of rolling temperature (500–600 °C) and relative strain (ε), which allowed for the determination of optimal parameter ranges in which a balance between bond stability and structural homogeneity occurs. The research results constitute a significant contribution to deepening our understanding of the industrial significance of interdiffusion and recrystallization processes, indicating a new direction in controlling the production of multilayer metal composites. From a practical perspective, the proposed technological model can be used in industrial process automation and digitalization systems. This approach facilitates the development of modern, intelligent, and sustainable methods for producing engineering materials.

Analyzing the manufacturing process of layered Ti + Ni composites is crucial for modern production management. It should be noted that it primarily influences the functional properties of the material, product reliability, and the efficiency of the entire technological process24,30,31. In modern industrial systems, where efficiency, durability, and cost-effectiveness are key, the design and processing of composite materials constitutes a key competitive advantage32–35. The research conducted by the authors indicates that advanced technologies, including explosive welding and rolling, not only enable the permanent joining of metal layers with dissimilar properties but also enable precise control of the quality of the joining zone and the formation of intermetallic phases at the microstructural level30–33. Maintaining and controlling appropriate process parameters38–40, particularly temperature, strain rate, and cooling conditions, is essential to ensure optimal strength and uniformity of the joint, which directly translates into increased durability and reliability of the finished product34–37. The research results confirm that Ti + Ni composites manufactured by explosive welding exhibit high shear strength and excellent structural integrity. It should be emphasized that this is crucial for maintaining the quality and stability of industrial processes. The research conducted and the results obtained can undoubtedly contribute to the development of intelligent manufacturing systems that integrate knowledge from materials science with adaptive process control. This approach promotes sustainable and highly efficient production. In summary, the research results presented in this paper provide valuable guidance for the development of modern manufacturing technologies and production management, particularly in the industrial application of high-performance multilayer materials.

Materials and methods

Titanium, as a material, belongs to the group of reactive metals. Due to its favorable strength-to-density ratio, it is among the most sought-after materials. Furthermore, it exhibits good corrosion resistance, good creep resistance, and excellent corrosion resistance. Titanium becomes covered with an oxide layer in oxidizing environments, which results in its ability to catalyze chemical and electrochemical reactions. Furthermore, titanium, as a cathode material, is characterized by a high affinity for hydrogen and the ability to form hydrides2. Nickel and titanium alloys, including NiTi, are also cathode materials due to their ability to accumulate hydrogen13 and exhibit catalytic properties in the electro emission process14. These properties are significant due to the potential use of nickel-titanium-based composite materials, for example, in hydride batteries and electrolyzers12.

These studies were conducted on samples after direct joining of the composite materials and after subsequent stages of the rolling process.

Based on the literature13,28,32 and shear strength tests conducted at two stages of the production process, it can be concluded that the explosive welding process is the most effective. According to Polish standards, the shear strength of the joined layered composites should be at least 150 MPa. The shear strength of Ti + Ni layered composites after explosion welding is more than twice that required by standards, which is an advantage of this method. The second stage of the manufacturing process involves rolling the layered composites, which reduces the thickness of the individual layers. This process was performed on samples heated to temperatures of T=600 °C and T=500 °C. The rolling process was carried out for two relative reductions. A comparative analysis of the obtained test results shows that the rolling process, under the assumed process conditions, reduces the shear strength of the tested joint areas. Analyzing the effect of the deformation value on the shear strength of the joint areas, it can be concluded that the shear strength decreases with increasing strain. This phenomenon may be due to uneven deformation of the composite layers during the rolling process. Another factor influencing the shear strength of the joint area is temperature. Samples heated to T = 600 °C showed a significantly smaller effect on weakening the joint area of the Ti + Ni layered composites.

Quantitative analysis of SEM and EDS data was performed to evaluate the evolution of the bond zone geometry and the thickness of the intermetallic layers as a function of the applied deformation and rolling temperature. Measurements were performed on representative micrographs taken along the interface using a Phenom XL microscope. The average thickness of the intermetallic layer after explosion welding was approximately 4.2 ± 0.3 μm. After rolling at T = 500 °C, the layer thickness increased to 6.7 ± 0.5 μm, and at T = 600 °C, it increased to 8.4 ± 0.6 μm. These results indicate that diffusion processes intensify with increasing temperature, leading to a gradual thickening of the intermetallic phase. Moreover, the wave amplitude at the interface decreased from 12.5 μm (after welding) to 5.3 μm (after rolling at 500 °C) and 3.1 μm (after rolling at 600 °C), confirming a significant flattening effect associated with plastic deformation. The measurement uncertainty was estimated to be ± 0.2 μm for the geometric parameters and ± 1.5 at% for the EDS element concentrations, based on repeated measurements (n = 10). This quantitative assessment provides clear evidence that increasing the rolling temperature and deformation not only enhances diffusion but also promotes structural homogenization, which directly affects the mechanical properties of the Ti + Ni layered composites.

To ensure the reliability of the obtained mechanical data, a statistical analysis was performed on the shear strength measurements (Rs and Rsm). For each process variant, five tests were performed, and the mean value and standard deviation were calculated. After explosion welding, the average shear strength was 391.9 ± 4.1 MPa, indicating very high repeatability of the results and uniform joint quality. After rolling at T = 600 °C at ε = 7.18%, the average shear strength was 382.1 ± 9.3 MPa, while for ε = 11.8% it decreased slightly to 367.0 ± 11.5 MPa. Composites rolled at T = 500 °C showed lower mean values of 353.6 ± 8.7 MPa for ε = 3.6% and 334.7 ± 12.4 MPa for ε = 8.21%. The increasing standard deviation observed at higher strain levels suggests an increase in heterogeneity of the bond zone due to local strain concentration and diffusion-induced microstructural changes. Despite this variability, all tested samples exceeded the minimum shear strength required by industry standards (150 MPa) by more than twice. These results confirm that, although higher strains and temperatures lower the average shear strength, the bond quality remains stable within acceptable tolerances, demonstrating the robustness of the proposed manufacturing process.

Discussion

This paper presents and discusses the technological process for optimal conditions. Naturally, the development process was preceded by a number of implementation challenges. The main research issues included the presence of intermetallic phases, Ni3Ti, NiTi, and NiTi2, within the joint. These, combined with deformation, weakened the joint area and even led to delamination. This problem was caused by brittle fracture of the hard and low-plasticity intermetallic phases. This resulted in microcracks and tears in the joint area, resulting from shear stresses caused by the rolling process. Therefore, during the analysis of the production of flat layered composites, the most favorable results for this process were discussed and presented.

Previous studies on the fabrication of Ti + Ni sandwich composites, such as those by Shen et al.29 or Acarer and Gülenç14, have similarly investigated the effect of explosive welding and post-rolling on the structure and mechanical properties of the bond zone. Shen et al. demonstrated that the formation of amorphous structures in Ni/Ti layers produced by cold rolling was attributed to the limitation of diffusion processes. In contrast, the present study found that higher hot rolling temperatures favored the appearance of intermetallic phases, such as NiTi and Ni₃Ti. According to the results of Acarer and Gülenç14, the explosive welding parameters, especially the explosion energy and interlayer spacing, essentially determine the waviness and shear strength of the weld. The present study further deepens these analysis by demonstrating how the rolling temperature and strain rate modify the geometry and homogeneity of the bond zone. While Lesuer et al.1 emphasized the impact resistance of composites, this study focused on optimizing bond strength, an industrially important consideration. The results show that rolling at 600 °C maintains high shear strength and improves bond uniformity, consistent with the observations of Mróz et al.28 for other metal composite systems. Overall, this study innovatively combines bonding mechanisms and forming parameters in an integrated technological model, providing a more detailed picture of the complexity and controllability of the sandwich composite manufacturing process than previous studies.

The decrease in shear strength of Ti + Ni sandwich composites with increasing rolling temperature is primarily attributed to recrystallization and interdiffusion processes. At higher temperatures, the mobility of Ti and Ni atoms increases, promoting the formation of intermetallic phases such as NiTi₂ and Ni₃Ti in the bonding zone. These phases are more complex and more brittle than the base metals and therefore act as local stress collectors, which can lead to microcrack formation and a decrease in shear strength. At the same time, recrystallization, which reduces the strain energy during rolling, causes an increase in grain size, thereby reducing the dislocation density and strain hardening of the metals. This combined effect explains why, although the bonding zone becomes more macroscopically homogeneous, the microstructural stability and mechanical integrity are somewhat weakened. The degree of strain (ε) also plays a key role: at lower strains, the bonding zone is less flat but more resistant to stress accumulation, while at higher strains, the zone becomes flat, but the thickness of the intermetallic layers and the proportion of brittle boundary layers increase. However, rolling at 600 °C provides a suitable balance, where interdiffusion favorably contributes to stable bonding of the layers while avoiding excessive brittleness. This relationship highlights the importance of precise temperature and strain control in optimizing the production of layered composites.

The novelty of this study lies in the integrated analysis of the effects of explosive welding and subsequent rolling on the bond zone of Ti + Ni composites, examining both microstructural and mechanical aspects. Compared to previous studies, in which these processes were analyzed independently, the research presented in this paper encompasses the entire technological chain. In this way, the authors demonstrate how the bond geometry, intermetallic phase composition, and shear strength vary in response to the production process parameters. It is worth noting that the obtained results also have significant applications and industrial implications. The study demonstrated that optimized rolling after explosive welding in the temperature range of 500–600 °C enables the production of a stable, high-strength joint, the shear strength of which exceeds that required by industrial standards by more than twice.

Furthermore, the results demonstrate that the wavy structure of the bond zone can be effectively controlled and flattened during rolling, which is a significant advantage for subsequent industrial processing. The research conducted contributes to the advancement of knowledge in the design of composite materials, detailing the relationships between temperature, strain, and microstructure. The research results indicate new directions in the development of manufacturing technologies based on the precise control of material microstructure. The findings may find applications in the aerospace industry, energy storage systems, and the production of medical devices, where high joint strength and corrosion resistance are required.

The research results obtained indicate significant potential for implementing modern production management and developing Industry 4.0 concepts. Process dependencies, including temperature (T), strain rate (ε), and cooling rate, analysis during the production of Ti + Ni layered composites, can be directly used in predictive models and adaptive process control systems. Such technological solutions enable real-time monitoring and automatic optimization of production parameters, minimizing the risk of defects and production losses. In practice, this research supports the development of the “digital twin” concept for manufacturing processes, in which joint quality and material behavior can be predicted based on modeling of microstructural changes. The knowledge thus acquired can provide significant support for strategic production management by implementing more economical, energy-efficient, and environmentally sustainable production processes. Furthermore, the research results confirm that integrating advanced material technologies with quality management and decision support systems is a key step in the development of modern, intelligent industrial plants. The approach presented in this paper is crucial not only for the development of materials science but also for production engineering and industrial management. It demonstrates how experimental results can be transformed into applied knowledge and digital process management strategies, which constitute the basis for building a competitive, data-driven modern industry.

Conclusion

The process of rolling thin sheets from layered composites composed of difficult-to-deform Ti + Ni materials is complex and difficult to develop. Based on experimental studies, it can be concluded that the process of rolling thin sheets from layered composites proposed in this work can be successfully applied in industrial practice. The experimental results allow for the following conclusions: After joining the composite elements using explosive welding, a durable joint with a shear strength of nearly 390 MPa was obtained. The study analyzed two production processes: the process of joining the composite layers and the process of rolling the Ti + Ni composite plates. Tests showed that the explosive welding process resulted in a wavy joint. Strength tests of the joint areas after rolling at various heating temperatures of the composite plates and relative strain values indicate a slight weakening of the joint area. The shear strength of the joint area after rolling for the considered process parameters exceeds the strength of the joint area twice as much as specified in applicable standards. It should be emphasized that rolling Ti + Ni layered composites at 600 °C significantly improves the quality and durability of the joint area. The goal of this study was achieved, and the obtained research results have practical applications.

The research results presented in this paper are significant for the development of modern production management and advanced manufacturing systems. Analysis of the forming process of Ti + Ni layered composites indicates that precise control of technological parameters, particularly explosive welding and subsequent rolling, is a key requirement to obtain high-quality and durable materials. From a production management perspective, this finding necessitates continuous monitoring and optimization of process parameters, such as temperature, strain rate, and cooling rate, to maintain product quality stability. The research clearly demonstrated that in industrial applications, explosive welding not only enables the effective joining of difficult-to-weld metals but also shapes the geometry of the bonding zone, which can be further modified during the rolling process. This approach improves the structural uniformity and functional properties of the resulting composite. Consequently, digital process control and data-driven decision-making are increasingly important in modern production systems. Furthermore, the conducted research contributes to the development of innovative manufacturing concepts that integrate materials science modeling with predictive maintenance and feedback from quality control systems. According to the research results, the developed model for manufacturing Ti + Ni composites using explosive welding and controlled rolling can serve as a model for developing adaptive, energy-efficient, and sustainable production strategies. Consequently, modern production management should focus on the integrated optimization of materials, processes, and technologies, along with digitalization and quality management.

Schizochytrium sp. as a sustainable docosahexaenoic acid source improves growth performance, fatty acid profile, and hepato-intestinal health in juvenile darkbarbel catfish (Pelteobagrus vachelli)
Authors: Ke Cheng, Xinyao Zhang, Wenyi Wu, Di Peng, Lixue Dong, Hua Wen, Rui Ruan, Ming Jiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSchizochytrium sp. has become a potential feed additive due to its rich contents of functional components such as docosahexaenoic acid (DHA), but its dose effects and physiological effects on darkbarbel catfish have not yet been clarified. This study evaluated the effects of six graded levels of dietary Schizochytrium sp. powder (0, 5, 10, 15, 20, and 25 g/kg; designated D0, D1, D2, D3, D4, and D5, respectively) on 30-d post-hatching juvenile darkbarbel catfish (Pelteobagrus vachelli) with an initial body weight of 0.38 ± 0.011 g, using a total of 1800 fish (three replicates per group, 100 fish per replicate) over a 10-week feeding trial. The optimal inclusion level of Schizochytrium sp. powder was also determined. Results showed that fish in the D4 group exhibited the highest weight gain rate (WGR) and specific growth rate (SGR) (P < 0.05), accompanied by significantly decreased feed conversion ratio (FCR) and feed intake (FI) compared with the D0 group (P < 0.05). Nutrient composition analysis revealed no significant changes in crude fat or moisture (P > 0.05), but compared with the D0 group, crude protein increased significantly in D2, D3, and D5 groups (P < 0.001). For fatty acids, D4 group exhibited the highest polyunsaturated fatty acid (PUFA) and ∑n-6 PUFA contents, the lowest saturated fatty acids (SAFA) content and health indices (atherogenic index and thrombosis index) (P < 0.05), while monounsaturated fatty acid (MUFA) content significantly decreased with increasing addition (P < 0.001). Principal component analysis confirmed PUFA, C18:1n-9c and MUFA as key factors driving intergroup differences. Histologically, the D4 group had significantly increased intestinal villus length and muscularis thickness compared with the D0 group (P < 0.05), whereas the D5 group showed obvious liver damage. Physiologically, D4 group had the highest activities of superoxide dismutase (SOD), catalase (CAT) and total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC), the lowest content of malonic dialdehyde (MDA), and the activities of digestive enzymes (lipase and protease) were also significantly enhanced in liver and intestine (P < 0.05). In conclusion, 20 g/kg Schizochytrium sp. powder promotes growth, optimizes fatty acid profiles, and enhances intestinal health and physiological function in juvenile darkbarbel catfish, while excess supplementation (25 g/kg) causes liver damage. This study revealed the application value and dose-effect relationship of Schizochytrium sp. powder as an aquatic feed additive, and provides a comprehensive theoretical basis for its precise application in freshwater fish farming.Keywords: Docosahexaenoic acid, Fatty acid profile, Hepato-intestinal health, Microalgae, Feed additive, Juvenile darkbarbel catfish

Introduction

Schizochytrium sp. is a type of unicellular, spherical marine microalgae, characterized by abundant nutrients such as proteins and lipids, as well as high-value bioactive substances such as docosahexaenoic acid (DHA), squalene, and astaxanthin (Yaguchi et al., 1997). As a sustainable microbial source of DHA, it boasts distinct environmental and economic advantages over traditional fish oil: it requires minimal land and water resources for cultivation, and avoids the overexploitation of marine fisheries. Its stable industrial production also mitigates supply chain risks associated with fish oil, while its high DHA yield (25%–45% of total fatty acids) enhances cost-effectiveness in feed applications (Sarker et al., 2016; Zhang et al., 2023). Among its bioactive components, DHA, as the core lipid component, makes Schizochytrium sp. an important microbial source for industrial DHA production (Raghukumar, 2008). Docosahexaenoic acid (C22:6n-3) is an omega-3 long-chain polyunsaturated fatty acid (n-3 LC-PUFA) with important physiological functions. At the physiological level of animals, DHA is a key structural lipid that constitutes the cell membrane, especially that of nerve cells and retinal cells, and is crucial for promoting the development of juvenile brain tissue and maintaining the normal structure and function of the retina (Hashimoto et al., 2017). In addition, DHA has shown multiple benefits for human health, including reducing the risk of cardiovascular disease, regulating gut microbiota balance, and exerting anti-inflammatory effects (Zárate et al., 2017). Given its extensive physiological functions and economic value, Schizochytrium sp. and its derivative products (such as algae powder and algae oil) have been successfully applied in fields such as infant formula, functional foods, and dietary supplements (Valencia et al., 2007; Zou et al., 2023).

Schizochytrium sp. powder has received widespread attention and research as a new functional feed ingredient in livestock, poultry, and aquaculture industries in recent years. Supplementation of livestock and poultry diets with appropriate levels of Schizochytrium sp. powder has been demonstrated to enhance the animals' digestive and absorptive efficiency of nutrients, strengthen their immune defense capabilities against pathogenic microorganisms, optimize the intestinal microecological environment, improve overall antioxidant status, and ultimately promote the healthy growth and production performance of livestock and poultry (Abbas et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2020). Significant advancements have also been achieved in aquaculture research, Schizochytrium sp. meal supplementation significantly altered the dominant intestinal microbiota structure of silver pomfret (Pampus argenteus), accelerating growth rate and elevating unsaturated fatty acid contents in muscle (Li et al., 2023). Furthermore, dietary Schizochytrium sp. powder effectively enhanced antioxidant capacity (Yao et al., 2022) and improved anti-inflammatory responses (Dineshbabu et al., 2019) in the hepatopancreas of Litopenaeus vannamei. Additionally, Schizochytrium sp. powder supplementation modulated inflammatory reactions in tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus) by modulating the inflammatory and anti-inflammatory signaling pathway (Sprague et al., 2017). These findings collectively demonstrate the considerable potential of Schizochytrium sp. powder in regulating nutrition and health of aquatic species.

Darkbarbel catfish (Pelteobaggrus vachelli), a freshwater fish native to China, holds significant economic value. However, the fry breeding process of darkbarbel catfish is plagued by issues such as high feed costs, relatively slow growth rates and unstable survival rates (Zheng et al., 2010), which seriously restrict the efficient and sustainable development of this industry. The early developmental stages of fish are highly sensitive to nutritional requirements. Studies indicated that larval fish exhibit a substantial dietary demand for n-3 LC-PUFAs such as DHA. Adequate DHA provision plays a critical and irreplaceable role in ensuring normal growth and development, enhancing survival rates, as well as improving stress resistance in larval fish (Villalta et al., 2005). As mentioned above, Schizochytrium sp. powder demonstrates promising application prospects in aquatic feeds owing to its high DHA content and other bioactive compounds. However, there is still a relative lack of research on the effects of Schizochytrium sp. powder on the growth performance of juvenile darkbarbel catfish. Therefore, the purpose of this study is to explore the effects of different levels of Schizochytrium sp. powder on the growth and key physiological health indicators of juvenile darkbarbel catfish, so as to clarify its application effect and appropriate dosage, and provide scientific support for the healthy cultivation of darkbarbel catfish.

Materials and methods

Animal ethics statement

This study was reviewed and approved by the Animal Experimental Ethics Committee of the Laboratory Animal Centre, Yangtze River Fisheries Research Institute, Chinese Academy of Fishery Sciences (ethical approval No.: YFI2023JM05). All procedures involving fish were performed in compliance with the committee's guidelines.

Feed preparation

In this study, six isonitrogenous and isolipidic experimental diets were formulated with Schizochytrium sp. powder (fatty acid composition was shown in Table S1. DHA content: 26.85%; Huison Biology Co., Ltd., Xiamen, Fujian, China) as the additive at inclusion levels of 0 (control), 5, 10, 15, 20, and 25 g/kg, corresponding to dietary groups D0, D1, D2, D3, D4, and D5, respectively. To ensure uniformity in particle size, all feed ingredients were pulverized with an ultra-fine grinder (40B, Suzheng Drying Equipment Co., Ltd., Changzhou, Jiangsu, China) and subsequently sieved through a 60-mesh standard sieve (pore size: 0.25 mm). Following precise weighing as per Table 1, all feed ingredients were homogenized by mixing for 30 min. After mixing, 30% (w/w) deionized water was added to the mixture, which was stirred to form a feed dough. The dough was then processed into cylindrical pellets with a diameter of 1 mm using a twin-screw extrusion granulator (F26, Guangzhou Huagong Optoelectronic Technology Co., Ltd., Guangzhou, Guangdong, China). The pellets were immediately dried in an oven at 60 °C for 2 h until the moisture content dropped below 10%. After cooling to room temperature, the pellets were transferred into bags, appropriately labeled by group, and stored at −20 °C for subsequent use. It is worth noting that during the first two weeks of the growth trial experiment, due to the small size of the darkbarbel catfish, the feed was further ground into small particles of 0.3 to 0.5 mm. Growth trial

The experimental darkbarbel catfish were purchased from Xinchuang Fishery Co., Ltd., Jingzhou, Hubei, China. After transportation, the fish were acclimated in the indoor recirculating aquaculture system at the Yangtze River Fisheries Research Institute, Chinese Academy of Fishery Sciences (Wuhan, Hubei, China) for 14 d. During the acclimation period, the fish were fed a basal diet daily to maintain normal metabolism. After acclimation, 1800 healthy darkbarbel catfish (30 d post hatching [dph]) of uniform size were selected, with an average body weight of 0.38 ± 0.01 g. All darkbarbel catfish were randomly divided into six groups, with three replicate culture tanks per group (each tank had an effective volume of 500 L, a diameter of 100 cm, and a water depth of 73.5 cm), and 100 darkbarbel catfish were stocked in each tank. During the growth trial, fish were fed the experimental diets prepared in Section 2.2 at 08:30, 12:00, and 17:30 daily. The daily ration was set at 10% of the total fish biomass and adjusted appropriately according to fish growth. The growth trial lasted for 10 weeks from August to October, 2023. During the culture period, the key water quality indicators of the recirculating aquaculture system were strictly controlled within the following ranges: dissolved oxygen content was maintained at approximately 6.0 mg/L; pH value was stabilized at 7.4 ± 0.2; ammonia nitrogen was kept below 0.05 mg/L; water temperature was kept at 26 to 28 °C; and the lighting condition was natural photoperiod.

Sample collection

Upon completion of the growth trial, the fish were subjected to a 24-h fasting period, after which all individuals in each group were enumerated and weighed. A random sample of thirty fish was collected from each tank for the determination of moisture, ash, crude protein, and crude fat content, and 15 fish were used for whole body fatty acid analysis. A separate cohort of 30 fish was randomly sampled from each tank to collect liver and intestine samples: part of the liver and intestinal tissues were immediately fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde solution for subsequent histopathological observation, another part of the tissue samples was quickly frozen in liquid nitrogen and then transferred to a −80 °C refrigerator for storage, ready for enzyme activity determination and analysis. Anesthetization of all experimental fish was achieved using MS-222 (60 mg/L, E107465, Shanghai Aladdin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) before sacrifice to alleviate pain.

Growth performance measurement

The feeding status of darkbarbel catfish was observed every day, and the actual feed intake (FI) of each group was recorded. After the 10-week growth trial, the biological indicators of body weight, body length, visceral mass weight and liver weight of each fish were measured to calculated weight gain rate (WGR), specific growth rate (SGR), feed conversion ratio (FCR), protein efficiency ratio (PER), FI, condition factor (CF), hepatosomatic index (HSI), and viscerosomatic index (VSI). Determination of nutrition composition in diet and whole fish

Moisture, ash, crude protein and crude fat

Moisture was determined using the oven drying method (AOAC, 2006; method 925.10). The sample was dried at 105 °C to a constant weight and the weight loss ratio was calculated. The crude protein was analyzed using the Kjeldahl method (AOAC, 2006; method 984.13), and the sample was digested with sulfuric acid and analyzed on an automatic nitrogen analyzer (Kjelflex K-360, BUCHI Labortechnik AG, Flawil, Switzerland). The crude fat was analyzed using the Soxhlet extraction method (AOAC, 2006; method 920.39). The fat in the sample was extracted with ether, and the solvent was evaporated before weighing. The ash content was analyzed by high-temperature burning method (AOAC, 2006; method 942.05), and the sample was burned to constant weight at 550 °C. The residual inorganic substances were the ash content. The organic matter content was calculated using 1000 g/kg minus the ash content.

Fatty acid composition analysis

Approximately 3 g of freeze-dried feed or whole fish body samples were placed in 10 mL centrifuge tubes, and 5 mL of chloroform-methanol solution (volume ratio 2:1) was added. After high-speed homogenization, the mixture was shaken on a shaker for 2 h to ensure thorough mixing, then filtered with quantitative filter paper. After adding 4 mL of deionized water to the filtrate and shaking to mix, the mixture was centrifuged at 1200 × g for 5 min. The supernatant and intermediate protein layer were discarded, and the lower layer solution was evaporated to dryness in a 40 °C water bath. After evaporation, 1 mL of chromatographically pure n-hexane was added to dissolve the extracted lipids, followed by 1 mL of 0.4 mol/L potassium hydroxide-methanol solution for methylation for 30 min. Subsequently, 2 mL of deionized water was added, after shaking and mixing, centrifugation was conducted at 1200 × g for 5 min, and the upper layer solution was stored at −20 °C for subsequent analysis. Fatty acid composition was determined with gas chromatograph (GC9720 Plus, Fuli Analytical Instrument Co., Ltd., Taizhou, Zhejiang, China). The mixture was injected for detection at a volume of 1 μL. Fatty acid methyl esters were identified by comparison with fatty acid methyl ester mixed standards (47015-U, Sigma–Aldrich Co. LLC, St. Louis, MO, USA). The relative content of fatty acid composition was calculated by the area normalization method, expressed as a percentage of total fatty acids. The critical GC operating conditions were set as follows: the chromatographic column used was a RBX-FAME highly polar capillary column (100 m × 0.25 mm × 0.25 μm; serial No: 345307C06, Fuli Analytical Instrument Co., Ltd., Taizhou, Zhejiang, China), the temperature program was initiated at 100 °C and held for 13 min, then increased to 180 °C at a rate of 5 °C/min and held for 8 min, further increased to 200 °C at a rate of 2 °C/min and held for 10 min, and finally increased to 230 °C at a rate of 10 °C/min and held for 10 min, with the inlet temperature set at 270 °C and the split ratio at 100:1. Histopathological observations of the liver and intestine

The preparation of paraffin sections was commissioned by Wuhan Servicebio Technology Co., Ltd. (Wuhan, Hubei, China). In short, the liver and intestinal tissues fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde collected in Section 2.4 were subjected to gradient ethanol dehydration, xylene transparency, paraffin embedding, sectioning, and hematoxylin-eosin (H&E) staining, and then the pathological changes of the liver and intestinal tissues were observed using an optical microscope (IRX50, Sunny Group Co., Ltd., Yuyao, Zhejiang, China). Three fields of view were randomly selected from each section for photographing and recording. Based on histomorphological characteristics, key indicators such as the integrity of hepatocyte arrangement and the morphology of intestinal villi (e.g., villus length and width, and musclaris thickness) were evaluated. Quantitative analysis of all captured images was performed using Image J software (version:1.53, National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA).

Enzyme activity analysis

The liver and intestine samples obtained in Section 2.4 were added with appropriate amount of pre-cooled phosphate buffered saline (PBS) to make 10% tissue suspension, and then centrifuged at 4 °C and 1200 × g for 10 min to obtain the supernatant. The obtained supernatant was used to detect protein concentration (BCA method, Cat No. A045-4-2), superoxide dismutase (SOD; Cat No. A001-1-2), malondialdehyde (MDA; Cat No. A003-1-2), total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC; Cat No. A015-2-1), catalase (CAT; Cat No. A007-1-1), alkaline phosphatase (AKP; Cat No. A059-2-2), acid phosphatase (ACP; Cat No. A060-2-2), amylase (AMS; Cat No.C016-1-2), and lipase (LPS; Cat No.A054-2-2). The aforementioned indicators were performed in accordance with the instructions of the test kit of Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute (Nanjing, Jiangsu, China) and was determined using a microplate reader (MinDRay BS-400, Mindray Bio-Medical Electronics Co., Ltd., Shenzhen, Guangdong, China). The protease activity in the intestine was detected using the Folin-phenol reagent (F743373, Shanghai Aladdin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) method, and the specific experimental steps were referred to by Wang et al. (2019), the absorbance was measured at 660 nm using a microplate reader (MinDRay BS-400, Mindray Bio-Medical Electronics Co., Ltd., Shenzhen, Guangdong, China), and the protease activity was calculated using the tyrosine standard curve.

Statistic analysis

All raw data were presented as mean and standard error of the mean (SEM). One-way ANOVA and Tukey mean multiple comparisons method were used for data analysis to analyze the difference significance of the results. Fatty acid analysis was performed using Origin Pro 2017C software (OriginLab Corporation, Northampton, MA, USA). Principal component analysis (PCA) and Pearson correlation coefficient analysis were conducted on the whole-fish fatty acid composition via the covariance matrix method to obtain the correlations between fatty acid indicators. The whole-fish fatty acid data were first subjected to sine/cosine or logarithmic transformation to achieve homogeneous variance, meeting the linear relationship requirement of PCA, followed by one-way ANOVA and Duncan's multiple comparison test. For factor extraction in PCA, the criterion of eigenvalue >0.5 was adopted. Mantel test was performed using the Mantel function in R studio (Version 4.0.3) to calculate the correlation between two matrices and output the corresponding r-value and P-value.

Results

Growth performance

The effects of dietary Schizochytrium sp. powder on the growth performance of darkbarbel catfish juveniles are shown in Table 2. Compared with the D0 group, the WGR and SGR in D1-D5 groups first increased and then decreased with the elevation of dietary Schizochytrium sp. powder level (P < 0.05), reaching the peak values in D4 group. In comparison with D0 group, the FCR and FI in D3 and D4 groups were significantly decreased (P < 0.05), while no significant difference was observed between these two groups (P > 0.05). Additionally, the PER in D1, D3, and D4 groups was significantly increased compared with D0 group (P = 0.002). Regarding the morphological indices, compared with D0 group, the CF in D1-D5 groups was significantly increased (P < 0.001), and the highest CF value was found in D4 group. However, no significant differences in HSI and VSI were detected among all groups (P > 0.05).

Analysis of changes in fatty acid composition of whole fish

Table 4 showed the effects of dietary Schizochytrium sp. powder on the fatty acid composition of juvenile darkbarbel catfish. The contents of most individual fatty acids (e.g., C14:0, C16:1n-7, C20:3n-3) and ∑n-3 PUFA showed no significant differences among groups (P > 0.05). However, significant differences were observed in SAFA (P < 0.001), C18:1n-9c (P < 0.001), MUFA (P < 0.001), C18:2n-6c (P < 0.001), ∑n-6 PUFA (P < 0.001) and PUFA (P < 0.001), as well as hypercholesterolemia index (HI), atherogenic index (AI), and thrombosis index (TI) (P < 0.001) among groups. The SAFA content, HI, AI, and TI were the highest in D5 group and the lowest in D4 group; MUFA content decreased overall with the increase in Schizochytrium sp. powder addition (P < 0.001), being the highest in D0 group and the lowest in D4 group; PUFA and ∑n-6 PUFA contents were the highest in D4 group and the lowest in D5 group, with PUFA content in D4 group being significantly higher than that in all other groups (P < 0.001). Notably, C18:2n-6c content in D4 group sharply increased to 15.50%, which was significantly higher than that in other groups (P < 0.001). Discussion

Against the backdrop of global intensive development of aquaculture, the development of high-quality feed additives is one of the core directions to improve breeding efficiency and product quality. Schizochytrium sp., rich in DHA, high-quality protein, and functional polysaccharides, is regarded as an ideal resource to replace traditional fish oil. Its application in marine fish farming has been widely verified (Tibbetts et al., 2020; Neylan, 2022), but systematic research on its dose-effect mechanism, tissue response differences, and nutritional regulation rules in freshwater economic fish (such as darkbarbel catfish) is still lacking. Therefore, this study systematically analyzed the effects of different dietary Schizochytrium sp. powder levels on growth performance, nutritional composition, histomorphology, and antioxidant-related indicators and digestive enzymes activities of juvenile darkbarbel catfish, revealing the application value and dose-effect mechanism of Schizochytrium sp. powder as a feed additive for freshwater fish.

In the present study, there was a significant dose-dependent relationship between the dietary Schizochytrium sp. powder level and the growth performance of juvenile darkbarbel catfish. Specifically, the WGR and SGR in the groups with 5 to 20 g/kg Schizochytrium sp. powder addition gradually increased with the elevation of the addition level, accompanied by a significant decrease in the FCR; in contrast, the growth performance in the group with 25 g/kg Schizochytrium sp. powder addition was significantly lower than that in the group with 20 g/kg addition. This result was consistent with the research conclusion of Santos et al. (2019) on Nile tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus), confirming the important function of Schizochytrium sp. powder in promoting fish growth. The growth-promoting mechanism can be presumably explained from two aspects: first, the rich nutrients in Schizochytrium sp. can provide high-quality nitrogen sources and energy substrates for juvenile fish, thereby reducing the loss in feed conversion (Zhang et al., 2021). Second, an appropriate dose of Schizochytrium sp. powder improves the intestinal digestive and absorptive functions (Ma et al., 2022). For instance, the optimized intestinal villus morphology in D4 group (20 g/kg Schizochytrium sp. powder addition) in this experiment further enhances the nutrient utilization efficiency. However, the decline in growth performance in D5 group (25 g/kg Schizochytrium sp. powder addition) was presumably associated with the hepatic metabolic burden induced by the high-dose Schizochytrium sp. powder. A previous study conducted in author's laboratory also confirmed that dietary Schizochytrium sp. powder addition exceeding 2 g/kg would cause liver damage in Nile tilapia (Peng et al., 2024).

Whole-body nutritional composition analysis revealed that dietary Schizochytrium sp. powder addition increased the protein level in juvenile darkbarbel catfish, whereas no significant effects were observed on the whole-body moisture, crude fat, and ash contents. This indicated that Schizochytrium sp. powder may primarily regulate the protein metabolism of darkbarbel catfish, and the increase in body protein content is likely associated with the protein source inherent in Schizochytrium genus, which chemical composition accounts for approximately 45.3% of total protein (Hadley et al., 2017). In addition, the optimization of intestinal morphology in each group and the increase in the activity of digestive enzymes (especially proteases and AMS) enhanced the efficiency of protein digestion and absorption, providing sufficient substrates for protein synthesis. Two previous studies on tilapia also confirmed that dietary supplementation of Schizochytrium genus could significantly enhance the crude protein content in fish (Ungsethaphand et al., 2010; Santos et al., 2019).

In terms of whole-body fatty acid composition, dietary Schizochytrium sp. powder addition significantly and systematically reshaped the whole-body fatty acid profile of juvenile darkbarbel catfish. Among these changes, the most prominent effects were observed in the levels of SAFA, MUFA, and PUFA, particularly those of the n-6 series. This finding was consistent with the known lipid composition of Schizochytrium sp., which is rich in DHA and may also contain substantial amounts of other PUFAs and SAFAs depending on the strain and culture conditions (Jiang et al., 2017; Zhou et al., 2007). The significant increase in ∑n-6 PUFA and PUFA in D4 group (20 g/kg Schizochytrium sp. powder addition), followed by a subsequent decrease in these components in D5 group (25 g/kg Schizochytrium sp. powder addition), indicated that there exists an optimal addition level to maximize the deposition of these beneficial fatty acids. Excessively high dietary addition may disrupt lipid metabolism or lead to the preferential utilization of PUFAs for energy supply via β-oxidation (Mata-Sotres et al., 2022). This phenomenon has also been reported in other fish species fed high-lipid diets, for instance, grass carp enhance adipocyte differentiation and lipolysis by increasing the expression of peroxisome proliferator activated receptor gamma (pparγ) and carnitine palmitoyltransferase 1 (cpt1) in adipose tissue, in order to adapt to high lipid intake (Yuan et al., 2016). Principal component analysis strongly confirmed these changes, identifying PUFA, C18:1n-9c, and MUFA as the key components responsible for the clear separation among treatment groups. This thus validated the efficacy of dietary Schizochytrium sp. powder intervention in altering systemic fatty acid metabolism. Notably, a sharp increase in C18:2n-6c (the precursor of ARA) was observed in D4 group. In contrast, the content of other n-6 PUFA components (e.g., C20:2n-6, C20:3n-6, and C20:4n-6) showed no significant or only slight changes among groups, confirming that C18:2n-6c was the dominant driver of the n-6 PUFA elevation in D4 group. According to reports by Tocher et al. (2000) and Bell et al. (2001, 2002), fish have a limited capacity to synthesize ARA from C18:2n-6c due to the related enzyme's preference for n-3 substrates and end-product inhibition. Supplementation with Schizochytrium sp. likely provided abundant C18:2n-6c, which could not be fully converted to ARA and thus accumulated, leading the elevation of n-6 PUFA.

It is worth noting that the DHA (C22:6n-3) content in D3 and D4 groups was significantly higher than that in D0 group, further confirming the value of Schizochytrium sp. powder as a functional feed additive. The DHA it contains can be efficiently absorbed and deposited by juvenile fish, significantly improving the efficiency of DHA enrichment, which is consistent with the studies in juvenile pirarucu (Arapaima gigas) (Nobre et al., 2025) and Nile tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus) (dos Santos et al., 2019). However, there was no significant change in EPA (C20:5n-3) and ARA (C20:4n-6), which may be due to the steady-state regulation of these two key LC-PUFAs by juvenile fish and the preferential deposition of DHA due to exogenous supplementation. Furthermore, the significant changes in healthy lipid indices, including AI, TI, and HI, hold important practical implications. These indices dropped to the lowest level in the D4 group with the highest PUFA content, indicating that from the perspective of human consumers, the nutritional value of fish slices has been potentially improved. Lower AI and TI values are conducive to reducing the risk of cardiovascular diseases (Kromhout et al., 2018; Niroumand et al., 2015). Therefore, dietary Schizochytrium sp. powder addition can effectively reshape the fatty acid composition of juvenile darkbarbel catfish in a dose-dependent manner. The addition level corresponding to the D4 diet in this study appears to be the optimal choice for increasing the overall PUFA content and improving the healthy lipid indices of fish, without causing a decline in key functional PUFAs.

Histological changes in the liver and intestine are key indicators for evaluating metabolic health and nutritional tolerance in fish. In this study, the liver in D5 group (25 g/kg Schizochytrium sp. powder addition) exhibited typical symptoms of hepatic steatosis, indicating that excessive lipid intake exceeded the normal metabolic capacity of the liver, leading to abnormal lipid accumulation in hepatocytes (Cao et al., 2019). The main cause of liver damage in D5 group was presumably the large amount of exogenous lipids exceeding the metabolic capacity of juvenile darkbarbel catfish, resulting in lipid accumulation in hepatocytes (Cao et al., 2019; Mata-Sotres et al., 2022). Additionally, D5 showed decreased ACP and SOD activities, confirming impaired lipid metabolism and oxidative stress-induced liver damage. This pathological result was mutually corroborated by the metabolic abnormalities (decreased PUFA deposition) observed in D5 group in the fatty acid composition analysis. In contrast, the intestine in D4 group (20 g/kg Schizochytrium sp. powder addition) showed a significant increase in villus length and musclaris thickness, while villus width was elevated in D1–D4 groups (5–20 g/kg Schizochytrium sp. powder addition). This morphological optimization implied an expansion of the intestinal absorptive surface area and an enhancement of peristaltic capacity, thereby indicating an improvement in nutrient absorption efficiency (Wang et al., 2020).

Changes in enzyme activities in the liver and intestine explain the causes of the aforementioned histological results from a physiological mechanism perspective. In terms of antioxidant defense, D2-D4 groups (10–20 g/kg Schizochytrium sp. powder addition), particularly D4 group (20 g/kg Schizochytrium sp. powder addition), exhibited a significant enhancement in antioxidant capacity in both the liver and intestine. This indicated that appropriate Schizochytrium sp. supplementation can effectively activate the endogenous antioxidant system of fish, strengthen its scavenging capacity against oxidative stress, and protect the structure and function of cell membranes from damage (Peng et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2022). However, the SOD activity in D5 group (25 g/kg Schizochytrium sp. powder addition) significantly decreased in both tissues, implying that its antioxidant defense system had been impaired under excessive lipid stress. This result directly corresponds to the liver damage observed in this group.

In terms of digestive metabolism, the significant increase in the activities of AKP and ACP in the liver, coupled with the AMS, LPS, and protease activities in the intestine, collectively indicated that the D4 diet comprehensively improved the nutrient metabolic vitality of the fish. Previous studies have also shown that completely replacing fish oil with Schizochytrium sp. oil can improve the survival rate, digestive enzyme activity, antioxidant response, and lipid metabolism of large yellow croaker (Larimichthys crocea) larvae (Yao et al., 2022). It is speculated that the abundant lipids contained in Schizochytrium sp. may have induced the adaptive upregulation of lipase activity (Liu et al., 2025), while other unknown growth-promoting factors might have synergistically promoted the maturation of overall digestive function, but further research is still needed to confirm this hypothesis.

Conclusion

This study evaluated the effects of dietary Schizochytrium sp. powder on juvenile darkbarbel catfish and identified its optimal application value. Results showed a significant dose-dependent effect that 5 to 20 g/kg addition promoted growth, increased whole-body crude protein, optimized fatty acid profile, improved intestinal morphology, and activated antioxidant/digestive enzymes (peaking in 20 g/kg Schizochytrium sp. powder addition), while 25 g/kg Schizochytrium sp. powder addition induced hepatic damage and reduced performance due to metabolic overload (Fig. S1). In summary, 20 g/kg Schizochytrium sp. powder is optimal for balancing growth, nutritional quality, and tissue health in juvenile darkbarbel catfish. This study revealed the application value and dose-effect of Schizochytrium sp. powder as an aquatic feed additive, and providing comprehensive theoretical basis for its precise application in freshwater fish farming.

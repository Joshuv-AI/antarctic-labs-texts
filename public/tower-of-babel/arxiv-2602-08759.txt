Which F 3 F_{3} -by- ℤ \mathbb{Z} s are CAT(0)?
Leo Delage

Abstract
In this note we point out a mistake in theorem 4.4 of [ 12 ] , which states that a semidirect product F 3 ⋊ ϕ ℤ F_{3}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z} whose defining automorphism ϕ \phi is unipotent-polynomially-growing and fixes a free factor of rank 2 2 is a CAT(0) group. We give and prove the corrected statement: such a group is CAT(0), if and only if ϕ \phi is the identity or if the element of F 2 F_{2} twisting the non-fixed generator is not in the commutator subgroup of F 2 F_{2} . This gives new examples of free-by-cyclic groups that cannot act properly by semisimple isometries on a CAT(0) space, that are similar to Gersten’s examples [ 7 ] . We also construct CAT(0) structures for new examples of F 3 F_{3} -by- ℤ \mathbb{Z} s by thickening the strips in Bridson’s tree of spaces construction [ 4 ] .



1
Introduction

A
free-by-cyclic group
is a semidirect product
F_{n}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
of a free group
F_{n}
of rank
n\geq 1
with
\mathbb{Z}
. It is determined by a free group automorphism
\phi\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(F_{n})
, and sometimes called the
suspension
of
\phi
. It is presumably a hard open problem to determine which free group automorphisms have a CAT(0) suspension, despite the great amount of knowledge available for classifying free group automorphisms.

Significant subclasses of free group automorphisms are known to have CAT(0) suspensions.
All exponentially-growing automorphisms that do not act periodically on a nontrivial conjugacy class define word-hyperbolic free-by-cyclic groups
[
2
,
5
]
, and these are cocompactly cubulated thanks to the boundary separation criterion in
[
1
,
8
]
. Geometric iwips, i.e. automorphisms obtained from a pseudo-Anosov homeomorphism of a surface with boundary, give rise to fundamental groups of compact hyperbolic
3
-manifolds with boundary
[
11
]
which are known to be CAT(0). All
F_{2}
-by-
\mathbb{Z}
s act geometrically on a CAT(0) square complex
[
6
]
.

Most known examples of free-by-cyclic groups that are not CAT(0) are similar to Gersten’s example
[
7
]
: this is a linearly-growing automorphism
\phi\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(F_{3})
given as
\phi(a)=a
,
\phi(b)=ba
,
\phi(c)=ca^{2}
. The first equation implies that the automorphism letter
t
and the generator
a
span a
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
that should act on an embedded plane as described in the flat torus theorem. The other two equations imply that
t
,
ta=at
and
ta^{2}=a^{2}t
are all conjugate and should therefore translate by the same length in that plane. So for point
p
in the plane,
t\cdot p,(at)\cdot p,(a^{2}t)\cdot p
all lie on a circle of the same radius. This is impossible since those same three points also lie on a common translation axis for
a
giving a circle and a line intersecting in three points in that plane. In fact, this obstruction prevents Gersten’s group
F_{3}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
from even acting properly by semisimple isometries on a CAT(0) space.

We slightly extend the list of non-CAT(0) examples by generalizing Gersten’s obstruction as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\phi\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(F_{3})
be of one of the following types:

1.

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\phi:&a&\mapsto&a\\
&b&\mapsto&b\\
&c&\mapsto&cw(a,b)\end{array}

with
w\in[F_{2},F_{2}]
,
w\neq 1

2.

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\phi:&a&\mapsto&a\\
&b&\mapsto&ba^{k}\\
&c&\mapsto&cw(a,b)\end{array}

with
k>0
and
w(a,b)\in F_{2}
cyclically reduced,
b
-balanced, of
b
-height
h(w)=1
, and totally balanced (see
2.1
).

Then
F_{3}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
does not act properly by semisimple isometries on a CAT(0) space; in particular, it is not a CAT(0) group.

So far, all obstructions to being CAT(0) for free-by-cyclic groups come from polynomially-growing behaviors. Of course, one can also write exponentially-growing automorphisms whose restriction to some polynomially-growing subgroup has the obstruction. It is therefore relevant to ask when a polynomially-growing automorphism defines a CAT(0) free-by-cyclic group. By twisting Bridson’s tree of space construction (
[
4
]
, II.11), Samuelson constructed CAT(0)
2
-complexes for many polynomially-growing automorphisms
[
12
]
, and Lyman used a similar construction for symmetric and palindromic automorphisms
[
9
]
.

Using a tree of different spaces, we construct CAT(0)
3
-complexes for new examples with polynomially-growing defining automorphism
\phi\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(F_{3})
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\phi\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(F_{3})
be of one of the following types:

1.

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\phi:&a&\mapsto&a\\
&b&\mapsto&b\\
&c&\mapsto&cw(a,b)\end{array}

with
w\notin[F_{2},F_{2}]
or
w=1

2.

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\phi:&a&\mapsto&a\\
&b&\mapsto&ba^{k}\\
&c&\mapsto&cw(a,b)\end{array}

with
k>0
and
w(a,b)\in F_{2}
cyclically reduced,
b
-balanced, of
b
-height
h(w)>0
, and satisfying either
\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=0}^{h(w)}N_{a,j}(w)\right)^{2}<k\sum_{j=0}^{h(w)}(1+2(j_{0}-j))N_{a,j}(w)
where
j_{0}\in\{0,\ldots,h(w)\}
is the starting level of
w
, or
\displaystyle 0<\sum_{j=0}^{h(w)}(-1)^{j_{0}-j}N_{a,j}(w)<k
.
(see
2.1
for notations).

Then
F_{3}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
is a CAT(0) group.

The first case consists of those automorphism fixing a free factor of rank
2
that were left by theorem
1.1
. It seems necessary to reprove that they are CAT(0) since Samuelson’s theorem 4.4
[
12
]
had a mistake in its statement and proof. Indeed, it asserts that even those automorphisms as in the first case in theorem
1.1
have CAT(0) suspension, which we prove here is not true. The mistake in the proof can be found at the very beginning of page 8: the statement "decreasing
r
does not increase this distance" is not true when
\theta>\frac{\pi}{2}
, which needs to be the case if one wants the "distance between corners" to be less than
1
.

The second case consists of automorphisms that do twist both letters
b
and
c
nontrivially, with additional conditions on the word
w(a,b)\in F_{2}
twisting
c
. Since Samuelson’s theorem already deals with the case of a word
w
having a nonzero sum of powers of
b
, we restrict our attention to those words where this sum is zero (we call them "
b
-balanced"). The first inequality will guarantee that the letters
a
move some point in the right way and shorten the displacement of the automorphism letter
t
, as some angular parameter
\theta
tends to
0
. The second inequality gives additional cases that work when
\theta\longrightarrow\pi
.

Even with these new examples, a number of polynomially-growing automorphisms
\phi\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(F_{3})
are not known to be CAT(0) or not. It is quite possible that more CAT(0) examples can be constructed through combination theorems with different splittings or semi-splittings, or maybe with yet other variations of Bridson’s construction. Completely solving the problem seems hard because it requires dealing with finite index phenomena, but there may be some hope if one restricts to UPG automorphisms (see
2.1
).

Most of the examples provided by theorem
1.2
do not satisfy the "rich linearity" condition from
[
10
]
that prevents a free-by-cyclic group from acting geometrically on a CAT(0) cube complex, therefore it may be relevant to ask if they are cocompactly cubulated.

In section
2
, we begin by introducing notation and terminology to describe our automorphisms of interest (
2.1
). We then review the basic results of CAT(0) geometry that we will use (
2.2
). Finally, we describe the tree of spaces construction that we will use for the CAT(0) examples and we state preliminary lemmas about them (
2.3
). In the next section (
3
), we prove the first parts of theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, thereby correcting the mistake in theorem 4.4 of Samuelson’s paper
[
12
]
. Finally, we prove the second parts of both theorems in section
4
(theorem
4.1
and example
4.3
).

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Unipotent polynomially-growing automorphisms

Let
n\geq 2
. An automorphism
\phi
of
F_{n}=F_{\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}}
is
unipotent polynomially-growing (UPG)
if it is of the form

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\phi(x_{1})&=&x_{1}\\
\phi(x_{2})&=&x_{2}w_{2}(x_{1})\\
&\vdots&\\
\phi(x_{n})&=&x_{n}w_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})\end{array}

where
w_{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1})\in F_{i-1}=F_{\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1}\}}
(here we call them the
twisting words
of
\phi
). Using (improved relative) train-track theory, it was proven that any polynomially-growing outer automorphism
[\phi]\in{\mathrm{Out}}(F_{n})
has a nontrivial power
[\phi]^{n}
that admits a representative
\widetilde{\phi^{n}}\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(F_{n})
that is conjugate to an unipotent-polynomially-growing automorphism
\psi

[
3
]
. Consequently, the suspensions
F_{n}\rtimes_{\widetilde{\phi^{n}}}\mathbb{Z}
and
F_{n}\rtimes_{\psi}\mathbb{Z}
are isomorphic and are finite index subgroups in
F_{n}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
.

Let
\phi\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(F_{n})
be UPG with twisting words
w_{2},\ldots,w_{n}
. The free-by-cyclic group
F_{n}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
is presented as

F_{n}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}=\left\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},t\left|\begin{array}[]{rcl}tx_{1}t^{-1}&=&x_{1}\\
tx_{2}t^{-1}&=&x_{2}w_{2}(x_{1})\\
&\vdots&\\
tx_{n}t^{-1}&=&x_{n}w_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\end{array}\right.\right\rangle

which can be rewritten as an iterated HNN extension

F_{n}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}=\left\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},t\left|\begin{array}[]{rcl}[t,x_{1}]&=&1\\
x_{2}^{-1}tx_{2}&=&w_{2}(x_{1})t\\
&\vdots&\\
x_{n}^{-1}tx_{n}&=&w_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})t\end{array}\right.\right\rangle.

We focus on the case
n=3
and use for notation
(a,b,c)=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
. In that case, a UPG automorphism is determined up to conjugacy by
w_{1}=a^{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
w_{2}\in F_{2}=F_{\{a,b\}}
. Replacing
\phi
with a conjugate by the automorphism
a\mapsto a^{-1}
,
b\mapsto b
,
c\mapsto c
if necessary, we may assume
k\geq 0
without changing
F_{3}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
up to isomorphism. In first approach, we assume that
w(a,b)
is cyclically reduced.

For
w=w(a,b)\in F_{\{a,b\}}
, we say that
w
is
b
-balanced
if the sum of the powers of
b
in
w(a,b)
equals zero.

Assume
w
is cyclically reduced and
b
-balanced. The
b
-height
of
w
, denoted
h(w)
, is the maximal sum of powers of
b
in a cyclic subword of
w
. The
bottom
of
w
is the set of occurrences of
a
or
a^{-1}
where a cyclic subword attaining this maximum begins. If
w\neq 1
, this set is nonempty: indeed, the last occurrence of a power of
b
(if any) before a cyclic subword attaining the maximum has to be a negative power of
b
, while the first occurrence of a power of
b
(if any) in that cyclic subword must be a positive power of
b
, so by reduction a nontrivial power of
a
must separate them. Similarly, the
top
of
w
is the set of occurrences of
a
or
a^{-1}
where a cyclic subword attaining the maximum ends. Any cyclic subword starting in the bottom and ending in the top of
w
maximizes the sum of powers of
b
.

More generally, for
0\leq j\leq h(w)
, the
j
-th level
of
w
is the set of occurrences of
a
or
a^{-1}
where a cyclic subword starting in the bottom and whose sum of powers of
b
equals
j
ends (or, equivalently, where a cyclic subword ending in the top and whose sum of powers of
b
equals
h(w)-j
begins). We also allow for empty levels, in case no
a
or
a^{-1}
occurs between consecutive
b
’s or
b^{-1}
’s. Thus the
0
-th level is the bottom and the
h(w)
-th level is the top. The
starting level
of
w
is the level entered at the last occurrence of
b
or
b^{-1}
and exited at the first occurrence of
b
or
b^{-1}
. We shall often denote it by
j_{0}
.

The
a
-weight of the
j
-th level of
w
, denoted
N_{a,j}(w)
, is the sum of the powers of
a
that occur in the
j
-th level. These quantities will appear in theorem
4.1
.

For an example, let
w=ba^{2}b^{-1}a^{-1}b^{-1}aba^{-2}b^{2}a^{-1}b^{-1}ab^{-1}a^{3}
. The levels of
b
may be represented as follows:

\begin{array}[]{rccccccccccccccc}\text{Level }3\text{ (Top)}&&&&&&&&&&&a^{-1}\\
&&&&&&&&&&b&&b^{-1}&\\
\text{Level }2&&a^{2}&&&&&&&&&&&a&\\
&b&&b^{-1}&&&&&&b&&&&&b^{-1}\\
\text{Level }1&&&&a^{-1}&&&&a^{-2}&&&&&&&a^{3}\\
&&&&&b^{-1}&&b&\\
\text{Level }0\text{ (Bottom)}&&&&&&a&\end{array}

We have
h(w)=3
,
j_{0}=1
,
N_{a,0}(w)=1
,
N_{a,1}(w)=-1-2+3=0
,
N_{a,2}=2+1=3
, and
N_{a,3}=-1
.

In the tree of spaces construction
2.3
, the diagram above corresponds to a sequence of adjacent spaces met by a combinatorial path that we shall use to estimate translation lengths. The stacks of
a
’s will correspond to vertex spaces that are euclidean planes and in which these
a
’s translate, and the letters
b
in-between will translate across edge spaces binding the planes. The gluing will roughly have the effect of locally turning
b
into a rotation and
b^{-1}
into the inverse rotation, thus the translation axes of the
a
’s at a local scale will essentially depend on their level in
w
only.

2.2
CAT(0) spaces

We recall here general definitions and facts about CAT(0) spaces and groups. They can be found in
[
4
]
.

A metric space
(X,d)
is
geodesic
if any two points in
X
are joined by a
geodesic
, i.e. an isometrically embedded interval. A
geodesic triangle
in
X
is a triple
\Delta=([x,y],[y,z],[z,x])
with
x,y,z\in X
and where
[x,y]
,
[y,z]
,
[z,x]
(the
sides
of
\Delta
) are choices of geodesics between
x
and
y
,
y
and
z
, and
z
and
x
respectively. The
Euclidean comparison triangle

\bar{\Delta}=([\bar{x},\bar{y}],[\bar{y},\bar{z}],[\bar{z},\bar{x}])\subset\mathbb{E}^{2}
is the unique (up to isometry) triangle of the euclidean plane such that
d(\bar{x},\bar{y})=d(x,y)
,
d(\bar{y},\bar{z})=d(y,z)
and
d(\bar{z},\bar{x})=d(z,x)
. Given a point
p
in one of the three sides of
\Delta
, say
[x,y]
, its
comparison point

\bar{p}
is the point of
[\bar{x},\bar{y}]
such that
d(\bar{p},\bar{x})=d(p,x)
(and so
d(\bar{p},\bar{y})=d(p,y)
).

A metric space
(X,d)
is
CAT(0)
if it is geodesic and for all geodesic triangle
\Delta
, for any
p,q
in the union of the sides of
\Delta
, we have
d(p,q)\leq d(\bar{p},\bar{q})
. CAT(0) metric spaces are
uniquely geodesic
(i.e. there is exactly one geodesic between any two points).

A metric space
(X,d)
is
proper
if its closed balls are compact.

Let
G
be a discrete group acting on a metric space
X
by isometries. The action is
(metrically) proper
if for all
x\in X
, there exist
\varepsilon>0
such that
\{g\in G|gB(x,\varepsilon)\cap B(x,\varepsilon)\}
is finite. In particular, the action is properly discontinuous; the two notions coincide if
X
is proper. It is
cocompact
if there exists a compact
K\subset X
such that
G\cdot K=X
. A proper cocompact action is called
geometric
. A group
G

is a CAT(0) group
if it acts geometrically on some CAT(0) space
(X,d)
. In particular, such a group is finitely generated by the
Švarc-Milnor
lemma and the CAT(0) metric space
X
is proper by the metric
Hopf-Rinow
theorem.

Let
X
be a CAT(0) space and
\gamma\in\mathrm{Isom}(X)
. The
translation length
of
\gamma
is the number

\|\gamma\|=\inf_{x\in X}d(\gamma(x),x)

and the
minimal set
of
\gamma
is
{\mathrm{Min}}(\gamma)=\{x\in X|d(\gamma(x),x)=\|\gamma\|\}
. The isometry
\gamma
is
semisimple
is its minimal set is nonempty (otherwise it is
parabolic
). If
\gamma
is semisimple, it is called
elliptic
if
\|\gamma\|=0
(i.e.
\gamma
has fixed points) and
hyperbolic
if
\|\gamma\|>0
. A group
G
is sometimes called
weakly CAT(0)
if it acts properly by semisimple isometries on a CAT(0) space. A geometric action is always by semisimple isometries.

A subset of the CAT(0) space
X
is
convex
if any geodesic with endpoints in
X
lies in
X
. A convex subset of a CAT(0) space is itself CAT(0). If a nonempty closed convex subset
C
is stabilized by an isometry
\gamma
, then the translation lengths of
\gamma
and its restriction to
C
are the same, and
\gamma
is semisimple if and only if its restriction to
C
is semisimple.

The minimal set
{\mathrm{Min}}(\gamma)
of an isometry
\gamma
of
X
is a closed convex subset. If
\gamma
is hyperbolic, then
{\mathrm{Min}}(\gamma)
is isometric to a cartesian product
C\times\mathbb{R}
(with the
l^{2}
-metric) where
C
is a closed convex subset of
X
. Moreover, any isometry
g
of
X
that commutes with
\gamma
stabilizes
{\mathrm{Min}}(\gamma)
and its restriction to
{\mathrm{Min}}(\gamma)
preserves the product structure, i.e. it is of the form
(g_{C},g_{\mathbb{R}})
where
g_{C}
is an isometry and
g_{\mathbb{R}}
is a translation. If
g
is semisimple, then so is
g_{C}
. We have
\gamma_{C}=id_{C}
, and
\gamma_{\mathbb{R}}
is a translation by
\|\gamma\|
. The fibres
\{c\}\times\mathbb{R}
of
{\mathrm{Min}}(\gamma)
are the
axes
of
\gamma
, i.e. the isometrically embedded real lines in
X
that are left invariant by
\gamma
; hence
{\mathrm{Min}}(\gamma)
is the union of the axes of
\gamma
.

For
\gamma,g\in\mathrm{Isom}(X)
, if
{\mathrm{Min}}(\gamma)
is the minimal set of
\gamma
then we have
{\mathrm{Min}}(g\gamma g^{-1})=g{\mathrm{Min}}(\gamma)
and
\|g\gamma g^{-1}\|=\|\gamma\|
.

2.3
The thickened construction

We start by recalling the combination theorem from
[
4
]
, chapter II.11.

Suppose a group
G
splits over a graph of groups
(\mathscr{G}(V,E,\alpha,\bar{\penalty\ }),(G_{v})_{v\in V},(G_{e})_{e\in E},(i_{e,\alpha})_{e\in E})
. Suppose
G_{v}
acts on a space
X_{v}
and
G_{e}
acts on a space
X_{e}
for all

and
e\in E
(with
X_{e}=X_{\bar{e}}
), and that there are
G_{e}
-invariant subspaces

and
G_{e}
-equivariant maps
f_{e,\alpha}:X_{e,\alpha}\longrightarrow X_{\alpha(e)}
for all

. Then one may glue the actions together according to the Bass-Serre tree

as follows: take copies
\tilde{X}_{\tilde{v}}
of
X_{v}
for all lift
\tilde{v}\in T
of
v\in V
and copies
\tilde{X}_{\tilde{e}}
of
X_{e}
for all lifts
\tilde{e}\subset T
of
e\in E
(with only one copy for each pair of opposite edges), and glue every copy
\tilde{x}\in\tilde{X}_{\tilde{e}}
of a point

to the copy
\tilde{y}
of

in
\tilde{X}_{\widetilde{\alpha(e)}}
, such that
\widetilde{\alpha(e)}
is the initial vertex of
\tilde{e}
in
T
, and if
g\in\mathrm{Stab}(\widetilde{\alpha(e)})
then

is glued to

(see figure
1
).

Figure 1:

\mathbb{Z}^{2}
acting on planes (blue and green respectively), amalgamated over
\mathbb{Z}

Let
X
be the resulting space. Then for all lift
\tilde{v}\in T

and

are cocompact for all

, then the action of

Assume now that the
(X_{v},d_{v})
and the
(X_{e},d_{e})
are metric spaces and the maps

are isometric embeddings. If the actions of

on

and

and

, and if there is

such that

for all

on

and

,
X_{v}
and
X_{e}
are CAT(0), and

is a convex subspace of

. Then

These facts altogether allow to prove, for instance, that a free product of CAT(0) groups is CAT(0) (taking
X_{e}
to be segments and

to be the endpoints of

, and any map for

In the case of an HNN extension with CAT(0) vertex group

and

, this construction produces a geometric action of

and

defined by the restriction of
\phi
to
F_{\{a,b\}}
is CAT(0), but one cannot repeat the procedure to obtain a geometric CAT(0) action of
F_{3}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
, as this group is not CAT(0)
[
7
]
.

However, Samuelson proved that if
\phi\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(F_{3})
satisfies
\phi(a)=a
,
\phi(b)=ba^{k}
with
k\neq 0
, and
\phi(c)=cw(a,b)
where the sum of powers of
b
in
w
is nonzero, then one can adjust some geometric parameters in the first step of the construction in such a way to have
\|t\|=\|w(a,b)t\|
, so that the second step works
[
12
]
. His idea was to bring the axes of
t
and
w
"sufficiently aligned" by making
\|a\|
(or, equivalently, the angle
\theta>0
between the axes of
t
and
ta^{k}
) very small, so that the displacement of a point of the axis of
t
under
w
is essentially due to the contributions of
b
; then to introduce a "twist" parameter by which
b
translates as it moves a point across the flat strip between the planes, and to take advantage of the nonzero exponent sum to make the total twist "cancel" with
t
so as to arrange
\|wt\|<\|t\|=1
; and to conclude by continuously increasing
\|wt\|
back to
1
by enlarging the width of the strips.

We shall thus be interested in the remaining cases where the sum of powers of
b
in
w
is zero. In this situation, Samuelson’s twist parameter will not be of any help, because the introduced translations will tend to cancel altogether. Instead, we will regard
b
as having a "rotating" effect on the axes in the flat, and show that in some cases these rotations move a point in the right way. We achieve this by replacing the strips in the construction with a product of a thin rectangle with a line.

For
k\geq 0
, let
\phi_{k}
be the automorphism of
F_{2}
such that
\phi_{k}(a)=a
and
\phi_{k}(b)=ba^{k}
. We have the HNN splitting
F_{2}\rtimes_{\phi_{k}}\mathbb{Z}\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}\bigast_{b^{-1}tb=ta^{k}}
, where
\mathbb{Z}^{2}=\langle a,t\rangle
. We let
X_{v}=\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a plane on which
G_{v}=\mathbb{Z}^{2}
acts by translations. We want a metric such that
\|t\|=\|ta^{k}\|=1
. If
k=0
, under this condition the metric is determined by the choice of
\|a\|>0
and the angle
\varphi\in(0,\pi)
between the axes of
t
and
a
. If
k>0
, it is determined by the angle
\theta\in(0,\pi)
between the axes of
t
and
ta^{k}
; we then have
\|a\|=\frac{2}{k}\sin(\frac{\theta}{2})
(see figure
2
). We choose orthonormal coordinates of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
t
translates by the vector
\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}
and
ta^{k}
by
\begin{pmatrix}\cos(\theta)\\
\sin(\theta)\end{pmatrix}
. We then let
\varepsilon>0
and
D>0
and set
X_{e}=\mathbb{R}\times[-D,D]\times[0,\varepsilon]
,
X_{e,\alpha}=\mathbb{R}\times[-D,D]\times\{0\}
and
X_{\bar{e}}=\mathbb{R}\times[-D,D]\times\{\varepsilon\}
. Finally, for
(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}\times[-D,D]
, we set
f_{e,\alpha}(u,v,0)=(u,v)
and
f_{\bar{e},\alpha}(u,v,\varepsilon)=(\cos(\theta)u-\sin(\theta)v,\sin(\theta)u+\cos(\theta)v)
(see figure

If

, we denote by

the space obtained from the gluing construction, and
X_{0}(\varphi,\|a\|,D,\varepsilon)
if

. This is a CAT(0) space on which
F_{2}\rtimes_{\phi_{k}}\mathbb{Z}
acts geometrically. As in Samuelson’s theorem, we need a lemma for a future intermediate value argument:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
k\geq 0
,
D>0
, and let
\theta\in(0,\pi)
if
k>0
and
\varphi\in(0,\pi)
,
\|a\|>0
if
k=0
. Let
g\in F_{2}\rtimes_{\phi_{k}}\mathbb{Z}
, and let
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
be such that
f(\varepsilon)
is the translation length of
g
in
X_{k}(\theta,D,\varepsilon)
if
k>0
and in
X_{0}(\varphi,\|a\|,D,\varepsilon)
if
k=0
. Then
f
is a continuous function.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. For
\varepsilon^{\prime}\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
, there is a natural,
F_{2}\rtimes_{\phi_{k}}\mathbb{Z}
-equivariant, piecewise-linear homeomorphism
I:X_{k}(\theta,D,\varepsilon)\longrightarrow X_{k}(\theta,D,\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{\prime})
(resp.
X_{0}(\varphi,\|a\|,D,\varepsilon)\longrightarrow X_{0}(\varphi,\|a\|,D,\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{\prime})
) obtained by piecing together the linear maps
\mathbb{R}\times[-D,D]\times[0,\varepsilon]\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\times[-D,D]\times[0,\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{\prime}]
and the identity maps on the planes. The map
I
restricted to the edge spaces
X_{e}
is
\Lambda=\max(1+\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime}}{\varepsilon},\frac{1}{1+\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime}}{\varepsilon}})
-bilipschitz, hence so is
I
by definition of a length pseudo-metric obtained by gluing length spaces. If
g
is the identity, then
f
is constant equal to
0
and therefore continuous at
\varepsilon
. Otherwise (since
F_{2}\rtimes_{\phi_{k}}\mathbb{Z}
is torsion-free and the actions are by semisimple isometries because of cocompactness) we have
\|g\|>0
on either space and

0<\frac{1}{\Lambda}f(\varepsilon)\leq f(\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{\prime})\leq\Lambda f(\varepsilon),

which proves continuity at
\varepsilon
since
\Lambda\longrightarrow 1
as
\varepsilon^{\prime}\longrightarrow 0
.

We now formulate the key lemmas for proving that in certain situations, we can arrange
\|wt\|<\|t\|=1
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
k>0
and
w\in F_{2}
be cyclically reduced and
b
-balanced of length
N
, and let
j_{0}\in\{0,\ldots,h(w)\}
be the starting level of
w
. Let
\theta\in(0,\pi)
,
D\geq 2N
and
\varepsilon>0
. Let
\rho
be the rotation of the plane
X_{v}
by the angle
\theta
in the coordinates above, and let
\vec{a}
be the vector in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
by which
a
translates in these coordinates. Let
x_{0}
be the point of
X_{v}
with coordinates
(-1,0)
, and let
\tilde{x}_{0}\in X_{k}(\theta,D,\varepsilon)
be its lift in the lift
\tilde{X}_{\tilde{v}}
of
X_{v}
stabilized by
G_{v}=\langle a,t\rangle
. Let
x_{1}\in X_{v}
be the point whose coordinates are
\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{h(w)}N_{a,j}(w)\rho^{j_{0}-j}\vec{a}
, and let
\tilde{x}_{1}
be its lift in
\tilde{X}_{\tilde{v}}
. Then
d(wt\tilde{x}_{0},\tilde{x}_{1})\leq N\varepsilon
.

Proof.

For
l\in\{0,\ldots,N\}
, let
w_{l}
be the suffix of
w
of length
N
. Let
y_{0}=t\tilde{x}_{0}
be the origin of
\tilde{X}_{\tilde{v}}
. Let
\tau:F_{2}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Isom}(\tilde{X}_{\tilde{v}})=\mathrm{Isom}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
be the group homomorphism sending
a
to the translation by
\vec{a}
and
b
to the rotation
\rho^{-1}
.

We now see by induction that for all
l\in\{0,\ldots,N\}
, we have
d(w_{l}y_{0},\tau(w_{l})y_{0})\leq l\varepsilon
and
d(\tau(w_{l})y_{0},y_{0})\leq 2l
. This is clear for
l=0
. To go from
l
to
l+1
, first consider the case where the first letter of
w_{l+1}
is
a^{\nu}
,
\nu\in\{-1,1\}
. Since
\tau(w_{l})y_{0}\in\tilde{X}_{\tilde{v}}
, we have
a^{\nu}\tau(w_{l})y_{0}=\tau(w_{l})y_{0}+\nu\vec{a}=\tau(a^{\nu})\tau(w_{l}y_{0})=\tau(w_{l+1}y_{0})
, hence
d(w_{l+1}y_{0},\tau(w_{l+1})y_{0})=d(a^{\nu}w_{l}y_{0},a^{\nu}\tau(w_{l})y_{0})=d(w_{l}y_{0},\tau(w_{l})y_{0})\leq(l+1)\varepsilon
and
d(\tau(w_{l+1})y_{0},y_{0})\leq d(\tau(w_{l})y_{0},y_{0})+\|a\|\leq 2l+2
, since
\|a\|=\frac{2}{k}\sin(\frac{\theta}{2})\leq 2
.

Now assume that the first letter of
w_{l+1}
is
b^{\nu}
,
\nu\in\{-1,1\}
. Then
b^{\nu}\tau(w_{l})y_{0}\in\tilde{X}_{b^{\nu}\tilde{v}}
is a distance at most
2l\leq D
from
b^{\nu}y_{0}
, so it is in the lift
\tilde{X}_{\tilde{e}}
of
X_{e}
connecting
\tilde{X}_{\tilde{v}}
to
\tilde{X}_{b^{\nu}\tilde{v}}
. According to the gluing, the matching point in
\tilde{X}_{\tilde{v}}
on the opposite side of
\tilde{X}_{\tilde{e}}
is
\rho^{-\nu}(b)\tau(w_{l})y_{0}=\tau(w_{l+1})y_{0}
(see figure
3
), thus
d(w_{l+1}y_{0},\tau(w_{l+1})y_{0})\leq d(w_{l}y_{0},\tau(w_{l})y_{0})+d(b^{\nu}\tau(w_{l})y_{0},\tau(w_{l+1})y_{0})\leq l\varepsilon+\varepsilon
. Also, since
\rho
fixes
y_{0}
, we have
d(\tau(w_{l+1})y_{0},y_{0})=d(\tau(w_{l})y_{0},y_{0})\leq 2l
. This concludes the induction.

It only remains to check that
\tilde{x}_{1}=\tau(w)y_{0}
. Indeed,
\tau(w)
is the sum over the occurrences of
a^{\nu}
(
\nu\in\{-1,1\}
) of
\nu\rho^{-n}\vec{a}
, where
n
is the sum of powers of the
b
’s that occur before said occurrence of
a^{\nu}
in
w
. We have
n=j-j_{0}
if the occurrence of
a^{\nu}
is in the
j
-th level, and regrouping the terms gives the desired formula.

∎

:

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
w\in F_{2}
be a word of length

, and let
N_{a}\in\mathbb{Z}
be the sum of powers of

in

. Let
\varphi\in(0,\pi)
,
\|a\|>0
,
D\geq N\|a\|
and
\varepsilon>0
, and let
\vec{a}
be the vector by which
a
translates in the above coordinates for
X_{v}
. Let
x_{0}\in X_{v}
have coordinate
(-1,0)
,
x_{1}\in X_{v}
have coordinates
N_{a}\vec{a}
, and let
\tilde{x}_{0}\in X_{k}(\varphi,\|a\|,D,\varepsilon)
and
\tilde{x}_{1}\in X_{k}(\varphi,\|a\|,D,\varepsilon)
be their lifts in the lift

of

and the second inequality in the induction by

3
Automorphisms fixing two generators

We are now ready to prove the corrected statement of Samuelson’s theorem 4.4.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\phi
be the automorphism of
F_{3}=F_{\{a,b,c\}}
such that
\phi(a)=a
,
\phi(b)=b
, and
\phi(c)=cw(a,b)
where
w(a,b)\in F_{\{a,b\}}
. If
w(a,b)=1
or
w(a,b)\notin[F_{\{a,b\}},F_{\{a,b\}}]
, then
F_{3}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
is CAT(0). Otherwise,
F_{3}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
cannot act properly by semisimple isometries on a CAT(0) space.

Proof.

If
w(a,b)=1
, then
F_{3}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}=F_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}
is clearly CAT(0). Assume
w(a,b)\notin[F_{\{a,b\}},F_{\{a,b\}}]
. This means that either the sum of powers of
a
or the sum of powers of
b
in
w
is nonzero. Up to a change of basis, we may assume
N_{a}\neq 0
. In that situation, clearly we may choose
\varphi\in(0,\pi)
,
\|a\|>0
such that
N_{a}\vec{a}
is in the open euclidean disk of center
(-1,0)
and radius
1
; for example, one may choose
\vec{a}=\frac{1}{2N_{a}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
1\end{pmatrix}
. Therefore, for
\varepsilon<\frac{1-d(\vec{a},(-1,0))}{N}
and
D\geq 2\|a\|
, lemma
2.3
proves that we have
\|wt\|<1
in
X_{0}(\varphi,\|a\|,D,\varepsilon)
. If
b
appears in
w
, we find values of the parameters such that
\|wt\|=1=\|t\|
by increasing
\varepsilon
continuously and using lemma
2.1
. Otherwise, since the axis of
wt
is in the plane
\tilde{X}_{\tilde{v}}
, we can choose
\vec{a}
such that
\|wt\|=1
in a way similar to the case where
k>0
. In either case, the tree of spaces construction (with strips as edge spaces) works a second time, proving that
F_{3}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
is CAT(0).

Now assume
1\neq w(a,b)\in[F_{\{a,b\}},F_{\{a,b\}}]
and suppose for a contradiction that
F_{3}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
acts properly by semisimple isometries on a CAT(0) space
X
. We may assume by rescaling that the translation length of the automorphism letter
t
is
\|t\|=1
. The minimal set
{\mathrm{Min}}(t)
of
t
splits isometrically as a product
C\times\mathbb{R}
, where
C\subset X
is a closed convex subset of
X
, such that
t
acts on
{\mathrm{Min}}(t)
as
\mathrm{id}_{C}\times 1
through this splitting. Now, any element
x\in F_{\{a,b\}}
commutes with
t
, and therefore leaves
{\mathrm{Min}}(t)
invariant and acts as a product isometry
x_{C}\times x_{\mathbb{R}}
in the coordinates of the splitting, where
x_{\mathbb{R}}
is a translation of
\mathbb{R}
by some length
x_{\mathbb{R}}
. The map
x\mapsto x_{\mathbb{R}}
is a group homomorphism into the abelian group
\mathbb{R}
, so since
w(a,b)
is in the commutator subgroup of
F_{\{a,b\}}
, we have
w(a,b)_{\mathbb{R}}=0
and
w(a,b)
leaves
C\times\{0\}
invariant. Since
w\neq 1
is semisimple, it has an axis
\Delta
in
C
on which it translates by some number
\|w(a,b)\|>0
. Therefore,
w(a,b)t
has an axis in the flat plane
\Delta\times\mathbb{R}
, on which its translation length is given by the Pythagorean theorem as

\|w(a,b)t\|=\sqrt{\|w(a,b)\|^{2}+1}>1.

This contradicts the equality
c^{-1}tc=w(a,b)t
, for conjugate elements must have same translation length.

∎

4
Examples with
k>0

We now turn our attention to cases 2. in theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
k>0
and
w\in F_{\{a,b\}}
be a cyclically reduced
b
-balanced word not in
\langle a\rangle
. Let
\phi
be the automorphism of
F_{3}=F_{\{a,b,c\}}
given by
\phi(a)=a
,
\phi(b)=ba^{k}
, and
\phi(c)=cw(a,b)
. If one of the following conditions holds:

1.

We have

\left(\sum_{j=0}^{h(w)}N_{a,j}(w)\right)^{2}<k\sum_{j=0}^{h(w)}(1+2(j_{0}-j))N_{a,j}(w),

where
j_{0}
is the starting level of
w
;

2.

We have

0<\sum_{j=0}^{h(w)}(-1)^{j_{0}-j}N_{a,j}(w)<k

Then
F_{3}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
is CAT(0).

Proof.

Lemma
2.2
proves that if there exists
\theta\in(0,\pi)
such that
\sum_{j=0}^{h(w)}N_{a,j}(w)\rho^{j_{0}-j}\vec{a}
is in the open euclidean disk of radius
1
, then for
D\geq 2N
(where
N
is the length of
w
) and
\varepsilon>0
small enough, we have
\|wt\|<1
, and lemma
2.1
proves that continuously increasing
\varepsilon
allows to find values of the parameters for which
\|wt\|=1=\|t\|
(here we are using that
b
appears in the cyclically reduced word
w
to ensure that
\|wt\|\longrightarrow+\infty
as
\varepsilon\longrightarrow+\infty
), so that the tree of spaces construction works a second time (with strips as edge spaces) to produce a geometric action of
F_{3}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
on a CAT(0) space. Thus, it suffices to show that either condition 1 or 2 imply the existence of such a parameter
\theta\in(0,\pi)
.

We write coordinates in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with complex numbers for ease of computation. We thus have
\vec{a}=\frac{2}{k}\sin(\frac{\theta}{2})e^{i\frac{\pi+\theta}{2}}
, and
\rho
acts as multiplication by
e^{i\theta}
, and we want to find
\theta\in(0,\pi)
such that, for some
0\leq j_{0}\leq h(w)
,

\delta(\theta)=\left|1+\sum_{j=0}^{h(w)}N_{a,j}(w)ie^{i(j_{0}-j+\frac{1}{2})\theta}\frac{2}{k}\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\right|^{2}<1.

We will find conditions 1 and 2 using series expansions near
\theta=0
and
\theta=\pi
, respectively. For
\theta
near
0
, we find

1+\sum_{j=0}^{h(w)}N_{a,j}(w)ie^{(j_{0}-j+\frac{1}{2})\theta}\frac{2}{k}\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)=1+\frac{i}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{h(w)}N_{a,j}(w)\theta-\sum_{j=0}^{h(w)}\frac{j_{0}-j+\frac{1}{2}}{k}N_{a,j}(w)\theta^{2}+O(\theta^{3}),

then

\delta(\theta)=1+\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{h(w)}N_{a,j}(w)\right)^{2}-\sum_{j=0}^{h(w)}\frac{2(j_{0}-j)+1}{k}N_{a,j}(w)\right)\theta^{2}+O(\theta^{3}).

For
\theta
close to
0
, this is less than
1
provided that the quadratic term is negative, which gives condition 1.

For
\theta
near
\pi
, we get

1+\sum_{j=0}^{h(w)}N_{a,j}(w)ie^{i(j_{0}-j+\frac{1}{2})\theta}\frac{2}{k}\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\underset{\theta\longrightarrow\pi}{\longrightarrow}1-\frac{2}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{h(w)}(-1)^{j_{0}-j}N_{a,j}(w),

and the limit of
\delta(\theta)
as
\theta\longrightarrow\pi
is less than
1
if
0<\frac{2}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{h(w)}(-1)^{j_{0}-j}N_{a,j}(w)<2
.
This gives condition 2.

∎

Remark 4.2
.

In the special case where
w\in[F_{2},F_{2}]
, i.e.
N_{a}(w)=\sum_{j=0}^{h(w)}N_{a,j}(w)=0
, the first condition becomes
\sum_{j=0}^{h_{(}w)}jN_{a,j}(w)<0
. This can clearly be achieved, for example with
w=[a,b]
, so we do get new examples indeed. Cases where both conditions fail include the cases where all
N_{a,j}
’s are zero.

Let
w(a,b)\in F_{2}
be cyclically reduced and
b
-balanced, and let
0\leq j\leq h(w)
. We say that the
j
-th level of
w
is
balanced
if
N_{j}=0
. We say that
w
is
totally balanced
if all its levels are balanced.

Example 4.3
.

Let
k>0
and
w\in F_{\{a,b\}}
be cyclically reduced,
b
-balanced, totally balanced and such that
h(w)=1
. Let
\phi\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(F_{\{a,b,c\}})
be given by
\phi(a)=a
,
\phi(b)=ba^{k}
,
\phi(c)=cw(a,b)
. Then
F_{3}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
cannot act properly by semisimple isometries on a CAT(0) space.

Proof.

We will find a translation length conflict in the minimal set of the automorphism letter
t
, as in the proof of proposition
3.1
. The elements
\alpha=a
and
\beta=bab^{-1}
are fixed by
\phi
and generate a free subgroup of rank
2
in
F_{2}
, and the assumptions imply that
w
(or a cyclic permutation of it) is in the commutator subgroup of that subgroup. Therefore,
F_{3}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
contains a subgroup of the type described in proposition
3.1
and hence cannot act properly by semisimple isometries on a CAT(0) space.

∎

Remark 4.4
.

All the non-CAT(0) examples provided so far have linearly-growing
\phi
. This leaves question 4.2 in Samuelson’s paper open.

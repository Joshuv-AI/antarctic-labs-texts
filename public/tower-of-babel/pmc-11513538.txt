The quality of nursing care in dying children from the nurses’ point of view in Iran: A cross-sectional study
Authors: Fatemeh Ahmadi-Forg, Mohammad Hossein Sattari, Hadi Hasani
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundNowadays, the quality of nursing care is one of the most essential factors in health services and patient care. The quality of end-of-life care is of high importance, especially in vulnerable groups such as children. In the final stages of life, the needs of patients and their companions, as well as stressful conditions cause avoidance of participation in treatment, and dissatisfaction of caregivers which can reduce the quality of patient care. The present study aimed to examine the quality of nursing care in children from the point of view of nurses in hospitals related to Birjand, Iran.Materials and methodsThe present descriptive study was conducted on 350 nurses. The data collection tools used in this study were demographic questionnaire and Quality Patient Care Scale (QUALPAC).ResultsA total of 270 completed questionnaires were entered into the statistical analysis. The average quality of nursing care score in dying children was 266.17 ± 27.79. Also, the results of the present study showed that the gender, age, marital status, and work experience of nurses have no significant effect on the quality of nursing care in dying children from the nurses’ viewpoint.ConclusionThe quality of nursing care of dying children from the point of view of nurses was satisfactory. Nurses reported that the physical dimension of nursing care had the highest quality and the psychosocial dimension had the lowest quality. Therefore, psychological support and follow-ups for improving social support for these clients are suggested to the nursing managers and healthcare administrators.

Introduction

Nurses, as the largest part of the health care system, play a critical role in the ultimate quality of hospital services [1]. Nowadays, the quality of nursing care is one of the most important factors in health services quality standards and patient care [2]. The high costs of health care services that the patient and insurance companies pays; demand a high quality of nursing care services too [3]. One of the important and critical types of nursing care is end-of-life care, especially in pediatrics [4].

In the final stages of life, the different needs of patients and their families, as well as the stressful conditions can cause avoidance of participation in the treatment process, dissatisfaction of caregivers, and neglecting the care details by healthcare providers, which can reduce the quality of patient care [5].

All over the world, there are many different models and routines of end-of-life care for dying patients according to ethical principles and the cultural context of that region [6]. Therefore, providing a single definition of high-quality end-of-life care is hard [7]. The quality of end-of-life care is a complex concept that includes values, beliefs, and attitudes of people, which is related to many different issues in health care systems and can be looked at from different psychosocial, physical, and communication dimensions [8].

Nurses play an essential role in improving the quality of end-of-life care due to their close relationship with patients and deep involvement with patients and caregivers [9]; Maybe this is the reason why in some studies, the quality of end-of-life care in hospitals has been estimated to be lower from the viewpoint of nurses compared to that reported by physicians [10].

Relieving pain and other symptoms at the end of life is a very important element in providing high-quality and appropriate care to the patient and her/his family. The psychospiritual dimensions of care are very important in providing end-of-life care to help the patients and their families accept death and have less death anxiety [11]. Nurses should always consider that family participation is important in improving end-of-life care. Death is a very complicated experience for families and requires great care due to the patient's physical and emotional condition [12].

Widger et al. (2008) in a study regarding the quality of care for dying children from the perspective of parents, indicated that while most parents were initially reluctant to mention their negative experiences during face-to-face interviews, they revealed their bad experiences and feeling with a little more questioning afterward. Most of their problems include communication failures with healthcare professionals, lack of teamwork, poor end-of-life care, and follow-ups [13]. Another study that examined the same issue from the viewpoint of nurses in the US, mentioned that the biggest challenge is poor relationships between healthcare professionals and children's families [14]. To the best of our knowledge, there is little information on the quality of nursing care of dying children in Iran. Given the importance of this topic and the gap in the available Iranian literature, we decided to investigate the quality of nursing care in dying children from the nurses' point of view in Birjand, Iran, 2022.

Materials and Methods

Study design, setting, and participants

This was a cross-sectional study to determine the quality of nursing care in dying children from the point of view of nurses in Iran, in 2022. The participants were randomly selected using the coin toss method.

The study population was nurses working in the pediatric ward, infant wards, PICU, and NICU in the hospitals related to Birjand University of Medical Sciences, Birjand, Iran. Inclusion criteria were having at least a bachelor's degree in nursing and 6 months of work experience; and exclusion criteria were unwillingness to participate in the study.

In this study; two questionnaires were used to gather data including a demographic questionnaire, and Quality Patient Care Scale (QUALPAC). These questionnaires were distributed to nurses who met the inclusion criteria randomly. In this regard, the researcher entered the wards and then selected the nurses who were in the ward at that present moment randomly using a coin toss method. After that, the researcher handed out the questionnaires to the selected nurses individually and after explaining the research aim to them, asked them to fill out the questionnaires. The researcher then waited a couple of hours and got the questionnaires back from the nurses. He came back to the wards several times again and selected new participants with the same method.

Variables and data measurement tools

The data collection tool in this study included a demographic questionnaire and quality patient care scale.

The demographic questionnaire gathered information about the gender, age, marital status, and working experience of nurses. The Quality Patient Care Scale (QUALPAC) was first designed in 1972 and revised several times afterward; and has been used in different countries such as the USA, England, Nigeria, and so on. We used the Persian version of this scale that its validity and reliability were confirmed by Hosseinzadeh with a Cronbach's alpha of 0.93 [15].

This questionnaire has 64 four-Likert-based questions and three different dimensions including psychosocial (questions 1 to 27), physical (questions 28 to 50), and communicational (questions 51 to 64). Therefore, the range of scores can vary from 27 to 108 in which scores lower than 85.3 show low quality of care; scores between 85.4 and 170 show moderate quality; and above 170 show sufficient quality of nursing care [15].

The score of each question of QUALPAC was calculated based on a Likert scale to determine the quality of nursing care in dying children (never = 1, rarely = 2, sometimes = 3, often = 4, always = 5).

Statistical methods

For analyzing the data, we used the “expected average score” in the Likert-based questionnaires. By the expected average score, we mean the middle option in the Likert options. For example, if we have five options (never = 1, rarely = 2, sometimes = 3, often = 4, always = 5), the middle option (sometimes = 3) is the neutral answer that a person can answer. This method is a well-known way for analyzing the Likert-based questionnaires and that is the reason why the options in every question in Likert questionnaires must be in odd numbers.

The expected average score in each question is equal to 3 which is in the middle of the Likert range; therefore, for analyzing the expected score of each dimension, we multiplied the number of questions by three. Then we used the one-sample t-test in SPSS, 26 to see whether the difference between the obtained scores and the expected scores are statically meaningful in each dimension or not.

Discussion

The present study showed that the quality of nursing care in dying children was significantly higher than the expected average (of the Likert scale) from the point of view of nurses. Also, the physical dimension of the quality of nursing care obtained the highest score and the psychosocial dimension obtained the lowest score which can show that this dimension needs more attention.

In this regard, Jamsahar et al. evaluated that the quality of nursing care is in good status from the point of view of nurses in Tehran, Iran which is consistent with the results of the present study. The findings of their study revealed that while 84 % of nurses evaluated the psychosocial dimension of nursing care as optimal; only 37.4 % of patients reported that from their point of view, the psychosocial dimension of nursing care is in an optimal status. This gap was also seen in other dimensions of nursing care as well. It is suggested to investigate the reasons behind this difference in future research [16]. The results of a study by Azimilolaty et al. revealed that the quality of nursing care was satisfactory from the nurses’ point of view. They also find a direct relationship between quality of patient care and burnout of nurses; especially emotional burnout of nurses. This may justify some of the underlying causes of the gap between the point of view of nurses versus the point of view of patients in terms of the quality of care, especially the psychological dimensions of caring. For a better understanding of these factors, further studies with deep analysis of these variables are needed [17]. Also, the study of Khaki et al. suggested that the overall quality of nursing care is good from the viewpoint of nurses in Iran [18].

Hosseinzadeh et al. assessed the quality of end-of-life care provided by intensive care unit nurses in Iran. They reported that the quality of patient care from the nurses' point of view was satisfactory which is in line with the findings of the present study. They also reported that 81.7 % of nurses evaluated the physical dimensions of the quality of nursing care as satisfactory, and 62 % of nurses evaluated the psychosocial dimensions as satisfactory [15].

Gaalan et al. evaluated the quality of nursing care in Mongolia from the nurse's point of view. In their study, it was concluded that the quality of nursing care was at a high level. Also, they found that the quality of nursing care had a direct relationship with nursing competency level and nursing practice environment; which is suggested to make these variables count in future research [19].

The study by Motamedzadeh et al. indicated that the quality of nursing care was at an average level during the COVID-19 pandemic in Iran which can be justified by the urgent situation of the pandemic and the high level of working burnout of that time [20]. Raesi et al. also reported the quality of nursing care as moderate during the COVID-19 pandemic in Iran from the nurses’ point of view. They also added that the level of work-family conflicts had a direct and positive relationship with the quality of nursing care during the COVID-19 pandemic [21].

The results of our study also showed that the gender, age, marital status and work experience of nurses have no significant effect on the quality of nursing care in dying children. This is consistent with the results of Jamsahar et al. [16]. Also, in the study of Khaki et al. no significant statistical relationship was observed between the variables of age and work experience with the quality of nursing care; which is in line with the results of the present study [18]. On the other hand, Asgari et al. reported that the score of quality of nursing care improves with increasing the age of nurses [2]; and Reyasi et al. also reported that the quality of nursing care improves with increasing the work experience and age [22]. The two abovementioned results might be due to the differences in the target study population, or other variables that may have a direct or indirect relationship with the quality of nursing care; and need to be assessed in a larger sample size. Also, the pediatric wards demand different skills and attitudes from other wards in the hospital which can be the possible reasons behind different results of the abovementioned studies.

The findings of this study can help healthcare managers to increase the quality of nursing care, especially in delicate situations such as caring of a dying child. Variables such as nurse burnout, work-family conflicts, environment, etc. can have an impact on the quality of nursing care. The importance of patient satisfaction in the healthcare sector and the remaining underlying causes show that there is a need for more research in this field.

Conclusion

The quality of nursing care of dying children from the point of view of nurses was satisfactory. Nurses reported that the physical dimension of nursing care had the highest quality and the psychosocial dimension had the lowest quality. Therefore, psychological support and follow-ups for improving social support for these clients are suggested to the nursing managers and healthcare administrators.

Also, there is a need for deeper research on the quality of nursing care from the point of view of nurses and patients in different settings. Finding the underlying variables that can have an impact on the quality of nursing care is of high importance.

Limitations

This study assessed the nurses within a region of the Iranian nation therefore the results are not fully applicable to the whole country or all the nurses in different countries. This limitation exists in most new topic investigations and for reducing this limitation we need more diverse studies in different regions and contexts.

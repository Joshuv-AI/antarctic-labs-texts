Affordable fitness tracker step count, heart rate and calorie expenditure validation in controlled environment: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Dhananjaya Sutanto, Billy C.L. So
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundObesity prevalence continues to increase globally, with increased physical activity considered as the most effective intervention. Fitness activity tracker have been shown to assist people increase their physical activity level, with increasing number of features and affordability over the past decade.MethodAn affordable fitness tracker, Archon Alive 001 was compared to Actigraph wGT3x-BT, with participants walking on treadmill at three, four, five and eight kilometers per hour (kph) and hand tally used as criterion measurement. Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) of Archon and Actigraph relative to hand tally at varying speeds, and related bivariate correlation were calculated, along with multivariate analysis of variance to assess the effect of speed and participant demographics on tracker MAPE. Polar OH1 heart rate monitor synchronized with PNOĒ metabolic analyzer was used to monitor heart rate and calorie expenditure. MAPE and correlation between PNOĒ and Archon calorie expenditure was calculated. Bland-Altman analysis and Intraclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC) was used to compare heart rate measurement between Polar and Archon.ResultsCompared to hand tally calculations, Archon step count was accurate, with average MAPE of 3.46%, and average correlation of r = 0.986, p < 0.001, while Actigraph average MAPE was 31.46%, with average correlation of r = 0.513, p = 0.001 at various speed. Age and speed had small effect on Archon’s accuracy. Sex had small effect, while speed had a large effect on Actigraph accuracy. Archon heart rate had Intraclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC) of 75.8%, with mean difference of -3.33 beats per minute (bpm) and 95% Limit of Agreement at -31.55 bpm to 24.90 bpm relative to Polar OH1. Archon total calorie expenditure output had 29.3% MAPE, and correlation r = 0.629, p < 0.001 relative to PNOĒ.DiscussionArchon Alive demonstrated high accuracy in step counting, along with acceptable reliability and accuracy for heart rate and calorie expenditure measurements, making it sufficient for monitoring exercise intensity. Heart rate and calorie expenditure outputs were insufficiently accurate for clinical assessment.

Introduction

The rising prevalence of obesity in Hong Kong [1] and worldwide [2] raises significant concerns on both public health and individual quality of life. Obesity is closely linked to various comorbidity, including cardiac disease and type 2 diabetes (T2D) [3]. Physical activity prescription is crucial for weight management [4], and has been demonstrated to be effective in managing chronic metabolic conditions such as T2D [5] and cardiac disease [6]. Current physical activity guidelines recommend varying levels of Moderate to Vigorous Physical Activity (MVPA) tailored to different life stages [7, 8]. Walking and running are cost-effective exercise options, with the use of measurement tools shown to increase users’ physical activity over time [9].

Traditionally, pedometers have been employed to objectively quantify step counts [10]. The advent of accelerometer-based activity trackers has provided more granular outcome measures, beneficial for both cross-sectional and intervention studies [11]. Advancements in miniature sensor technology [12], resulted in a greater selection of activity trackers available across different price ranges.

Recently marketed fitness trackers offer features such as step counts, heart rate monitoring, and calorie expenditure estimates, utilizing proprietary algorithms and optical sensors [13]. Heart rate monitoring is valuable during aerobic exercises, helping users maintain aerobic training intensity within specified ranges [14], and assists in determining recovery times between sets during strength training [15]. Seniors and cardiac rehabilitation patients can also benefit from continuous heart rate monitoring during exercise to ensure training safety [16, 17]. Information on calorie expenditure during exercise can be instrumental for individuals managing their weight [18]. Overall, these additional functionalities support users in achieving their health and fitness objectives.

Despite the diversity of fitness trackers available at various price points, most physical activity studies have relied on the Actigraph wGT3x-BT (ActiGraph LLC, Pensacola, FL, USA) [19], which is regarded as the main assessment tool for physical activity assessment [19]. Studies assessing heart rate relied on separate photoplethysmography heart rate sensor such as Polar OH1 (Polar Electro Oy, Helsinki, FIN) [20]. Calorie expenditure would be assessed with cardio-metabolic gas analysis of the participant’s breath, such as using PNOĒ metabolic analyzer (PNOĒ, Malden, MA, USA) [21]. However, significant costs associated with using separate measurement sensors for data collection and analysis can be prohibitive for organizations with limited budgets or for large-scale population studies that does not require clinically significant accuracy.

There is increasing number of affordable activity trackers that uses three axis accelerometer, similar to those used in Actigraph wGT3x-BT for step count. These trackers also incorporate photoplethysmographic heart rate sensors similar to those used in Polar OH1 for heart rate measurement. Most of these trackers, including the Archon Alive 001 (Archon Wellness Ltd, Hong Kong S.A.R.) would incorporate propriety algorithm to calculate the wearer’s calorie expenditure for the day, and during specific activities. Archon Alive 001 was selected in this study as it is an affordable product (under 45 United States Dollar as of June 30, 2025) widely available in the local market where the study was conducted. The availability of an affordable activity tracker that demonstrates comparable effectiveness in step counting, heart rate monitoring, and calorie expenditure estimation would facilitate broader implementation of activity tracking in public health initiatives.

The aim of this study was to assess the validity of Archon Alive 001 step count, heart rate and energy expenditure measurement during walking at different speeds and running. The study results serve as valuable consideration by various users when selecting measurement tools that align with their specific constraints and purposes. The hypothesis was that both Archon Alive and Actigraph had measurement deviation below 30% from step count based on Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) analysis, consistent with prior study on other sensors [22], and Archon Alive heart rate measurement have strong correlation to Polar OH1 results. However, Archon Alive energy expenditure may undercount calorie expenditure approximation when compared to PNOĒ.

Methods

Study design

This cross-sectional study compared the step count measurement of Archon Alive 001 and Actigraph wGT3x-BT with manual hand count. Archon Alive 001 heart rate measurement was compared to Polar OH1, while Archon Alive 001 calorie expenditure was compared to PNOĒ. Participants were tasked to walk and run at different speeds on a treadmill during the outcome measurement.

Subject recruitment

A recent systematic review on the accuracy of wrist-worn activity tracking devices reported an average of 30.6 participants per study [9]. Consequently, the target recruitment number for the current study was set at 35 participants. Recruitment efforts were conducted through social media platforms and word of mouth.

Inclusion & exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria were: (1) Men and women aged 18–65 years, and (2) Body mass index (BMI) of 18–25 kg/m2.

Exclusion criteria were: (1) Exercise contraindication based on physical activity readiness questionnaire (PAR-Q), (2) Indication or diagnosis of chronic diseases (such as cancer, cardiovascular disease, diabetes, bone fracture or infection), (3) artificial lower limb joints, walking aid use, pregnancy within one year, or personal mobility restrictions (due to connective tissue diseases, rheumatoid arthritis, or central nervous system disorders).

Applied instrumentation

Actigraph wGT3X-BT: validated wrist worn 3-dimensional accelerometer that provides step count [10] and commonly used to quantify physical activity in research settings [11]. Actigraph sensor was set to collect data at 10 s interval.

PNOĒ: validated portable cardio-metabolic analyzer that assesses energy expenditure during treadmill walking with high reliability [21]. PNOĒ was paired with Polar OH1 which is a validated photoplethysmographic heart rate monitor [20] with the output set at 3 s interval. Participant worn a standard Hans Rudolph mask with mouthpiece and breathes through a flow sensor (Hans Rudolph Inc., Kansas City, MO, USA).

Archon Alive AA001: wrist worn fitness activity monitor that combines data from a 3-dimensional accelerometer with optical heart rate sensor and SPO2 sensor. During data collection, the sensor was set at sports mode that provided heart rate output interval at 3 s and step count interval at 1 min.

Hand tally: the observer would manually count the number of steps the participants walked at each stage [22].

Sensor placement

Both the Actigraph and Archon activity trackers were worn on the non-dominant wrist with the relevant device settings adjusted accordingly, and their positions randomized [23]. The tracker positioned more distally was placed closer to the ulnar head. Polar OH1 was placed on the non-dominant arm brachium near the antecubitis. PNOĒ was fastened on the back using a propriety harness, and the flow sensor attached on the mask.

Treadmill protocol

Participants walked on Freemotion t10.8 treadmill (iFIT Inc., Logan, UT, USA) for three stages, first: 3 km per hour (kph), second: 4 kph, and third stage: 5 kph, based on previous wrist worn activity tracker validation study at various walking speeds under controlled condition [22]. Additional running protocol of 8 kph was added to provide a sufficiently varied training stimulus that elicit changes in participant’s heart rate, while ensuring that individuals with diverse fitness backgrounds could complete the protocol without experiencing any adverse effect. Each stage is 3-minute in duration, with 1-minute break in between, where participants straddled the treadmill and remained stationary while an observer adjusted the speed.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics on participant’s age, sex, weight, height and BMI were calculated to provide participant background demographic information. Archon alive step count, calorie expenditure, and heart rate was extracted from the Archon cloud platform. Actigraph step count was extracted using Actilife 6.13.6 (ActiGraph LLC, Pensacola, FL, USA) and the combined Polar heart rate and PNOĒ calorie expenditure measurements was extracted from the PNOĒ cloud platform. Hand tally result was used as the criterion measurement for the step count.

MAPE scores for step count was calculated for each running speed from both activity trackers by dividing the absolute bias (counted steps – measured steps) with criterion measures [22, 24]. MAPE ≤ 3% is considered as clinically irrelevant [25]. Multivariate Analysis of Variance (MANCOVA) were used to assess the effect of treadmill speed on Archon and Actigraph step count MAPE, with sex, age, height, weight and BMI as the co-variates. Demographic factors were analyzed as potential confounder as age, sex, BMI, and type of activity had been previously shown to affect wrist worn heart rate sensor accuracy [26, 27]. MAPE analysis would also be conducted comparing calorie expenditure as measured by Archon Alive and PNOĒ. Bivariate correlation analysis would assess the correlation between the step counts measured by the criterion measurement with Archon Alive and Actigraph over the measurement period, and calorie expenditure between Archon Alive and PNOĒ.

Heart rate measurements from Polar and Archon Alive were converted to 1 min intervals, prior to having Intraclass Correlation Coefficients (ICC) two-way random effects model with absolute agreement type of relationship and single rater unit [28]. Bland-Altman analysis conducted on the matching dataset, with 95% Limit of Agreement (LoA) calculated [29]. Statistical analysis was done using IBM (International Business Machines Corp., Armonk, NY, U.S.A.) SPSS Statistics version 29.0.2.0 [20] and Microsoft (Redmond, Washington, U.S.A) Excel 2016.

Discussion

Both Archon and Actigraph sensors were attached to the participant’s non-dominant forearm, with the three dimension accelerometer output from both sensors used to calculate the number of steps the user took [30]. Accelerometer algorithms need to balance sensitivity to the number of actual steps taken, with specificity that the accelerometer output was the result of steps taken, excluding other arm movements, that could undercount compared to waist placed accelerometers [31]. The sports mode setting on Archon Alive in this study may enhance sensitivity for step counting, resulting in a small MAPE mean and standard deviation, along with a high correlation to hand tally counts. Actigraph’s step count algorithm, which operates under free-living conditions, may contribute to a significant undercount bias, with prior study on wrist-worn Actigraph had MAPE relative to hand tally, and past study recommended waist placement for Actigraph [32].

Age had a weak effect on Archon accuracy, which has been observed in other devices [33] and could be due to subtle walking gait changes experienced among older individuals, indicating that compensatory Archon algorithm adjustment based on age can further improve its accuracy. Sex had been shown to affect gait [34], resulting in a weak effect on Actigraph accuracy. Speed had a weak effect on Archon and strong effect on Actigraph accuracy. The average walking speed of working age healthy adults ranges from four [35] to five kph [36]. At this average walking speeds, both Archon and Actigraph exhibited higher accuracy relative to their respective performance at faster and slower speeds, with the Archon demonstrated greater overall accuracy than the Actigraph across the measured velocity range. This could be attributed to the step count algorithms of both fitness trackers optimized for walking at this range of speed. The overall low MAPE and high correlation between Archon step count relative to the hand tally indicated that over the range of speed used in this study, the sports mode on Archon provides high step counting fidelity. Short bouts of MVPA can provide greater health benefits compared to total daily step count [37], and using fitness trackers with dedicated sports mode could provide more accurate assessment of the user’s activity during the exercise period. Users should be aware that age and sex could affect the step count accuracy.

Heart rate outcome analysis revealed that Archon had good overall reliability relative to Polar, although a small overall overestimation in Archon’s measurements was accompanied by large positive and negative errors, warranting cautious interpretation against reference standards. The average population aiming for approximate exercise intensity level may find the Archon heart rate reading to be sufficient, despite the reading not being medically accurate. Researchers utilizing the archon for exercise monitoring will also benefit from the heart rate monitoring during sports mode to ensure that their participants engaged in physical activity at sufficient intensity. The significant heart rate over and undercount was observed, with large range of limit of agreement, along with the ICC not meeting the clinical threshold indicated that that the archon heart rate measurement was not clinically accurate, possibly due to sensor location affecting photoplethysmography sensor accuracy [38]. People with cardiovascular concerns, especially cardiac rehabilitation patients, and athletes training on specific heart rate zone should use more accurate measurement devices. Users noticing medically concerning results from the heart rate output should consider more accurate heart rate measurement devices [39] or consult with a medical practitioner.

Calorie expenditure during exercise can be influenced by fat-free mass, fat mass, sex, age, waist-to-thigh ratio, ethnicity [40] and movement efficiency [41] of the user, with mass, sex and age required to set up the user profile on the archon software. Lower accuracy on the calorie expenditure output was expected based on the limited information used for approximation. Moderate correlation between the calorie expenditure measured by Archon compared to PNOĒ, with MAPE > 5% indicated that the Archon calorie expenditure results were not sufficiently accurate for research and medical purposes. Researchers and Clinicians should also be aware that despite the Archon device and app displaying all the measurements, missing data could occur during extraction for further analysis. Step count and heart rate missing data from the Archon server is a critical flaw for medical application. As it affects only 2 out of 37 data points for the heart rate calculation and data extraction was done prior to any data analysis and blinded of data result from other calculations, it should not significantly affect the results. The users from the general population can use this measurement as a benchmark to compare their daily calorie expenditure relative to their approximate calorie consumption for specific body composition goals.

Limitation

This study focused on outcome measure validation in controlled settings. Past step count validations in free living condition used correlation between the sensors as their validation criterion [42, 43], which had been shown to significantly undercount the actual number of steps [44]. Archon free living step count may over or undercount the actual number of steps. Advancements of video motion recognition utilizing artificial intelligence may allow future study to use a more accurate criterion measurement for fitness tracker free living validation [45]. Our recruited participant demographics had normal BMI, and BMI had been shown to affect step count accuracy [26]. Only one affordable fitness tracker was assessed in this study considering the variety of available options with related annual model updates in different markets. Future studies utilizing affordable fitness trackers may consider using previously validated types, or conduct their own validation to ensure measurement accuracy and reliability. Future studies may also consider validating the measurements on participants engaging in different forms of activities such as ball games, as the accelerometer output from these activities may require specific algorithms to provide accurate results.

Conclusion

Affordable fitness trackers with sports mode function can provide accurate step count measurements and reasonable approximation of the user’s training intensity and calorie expenditure in controlled environments. These trackers could assist the average population achieve their fitness goals and for organizations monitoring the exercise intensity, duration and frequency of their participants. Despite their convenience and functionality, these trackers are not substitutes for clinical monitoring devices that may be needed in some research or clinical settings. Vulnerable population group such as cardiac rehabilitation patients should use more accurate sensor to ensure their safety. As different affordable fitness tracker may use sensors and algorithms with varying accuracy and reliability, users should consider devices that had been previously validated.

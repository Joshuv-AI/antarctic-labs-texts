Simply slicing knots
Anthony Conway, Patrick Orson, Mark Pencovitch

Abstract
. Given a simply-connected 4 4 -manifold with boundary the 3 3 -sphere, this paper establishes sufficient conditions for a knot in the boundary to be sliced by a locally flat disc in the 4 4 -manifold, whose complement has finite cyclic fundamental group. In addition, necessary and sufficient conditions are described to ensure that such discs exist stably, that is after taking the connected sum of the 4 4 -manifold with copies of S 2 × S 2 S^{2}\times S^{2} .



1.
Introduction

Given a
4
-manifold
N
with boundary
S^{3}
, one can ask which knots
K\subset S^{3}
bound locally flat slice discs
D\subset N
with a given disc group
\pi_{1}(N\setminus D)
.
When the disc group is abelian, we call
D
a
simple
slice disc.
Freedman proved that a knot
K\subset S^{3}
bounds a locally flat slice disc in
D^{4}
with disc group
\mathbb{Z}
if and only if it has Alexander polynomial one
[
Fre82
,
FQ90
]
.
More generally, a knot
K\subset S^{3}
admits such a slice disc in a simply-connected, compact
4
-manifold
N
if and only if its Blanchfield form is presented by a size
b_{2}(N)
nondegenerate hermitian matrix
A(t)
such that
A(1)
represents the intersection form of
N

[
CPP25
]
.
We call a knot
G
-slice
in
N
if it bounds a locally flat slice disc with disc group isomorphic to
G
. In this article, we investigate which knots are
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
-slice in simply-connected
4
-manifolds, for
d\neq 0
.

The criteria we obtain to guarantee a simple slice disc exists will depend on the relative homology class of the hypothesised disc, so this article could also be thought as asking when a given nonzero class
x\in H_{2}(N,\partial N)
of divisibility
d
is represented by a simple slice disc (which will necessarily be a
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
-disc) with boundary a given
K
.
Our main results provide sufficient conditions for this to occur (Theorem
1.1
) as well as a necessary and sufficient conditions for this to occur stably, i.e. for there to exist an integer
k\geq 0
so that
K
bounds a disc
D\subset N\#^{k}(S^{2}\times S^{2})
representing the class
x\oplus 0\in H_{2}(N\#^{k}(S^{2}\times S^{2}))
(Theorem
1.10
).
In the case that
d
is a prime power, and provided a disc exists for some
k
, we give a formula computing the smallest such
k
in terms of the algebraic topology of
N
and the Levine-Tristram signature of
K
(Corollary
1.13
).

Results of the type we obtain in this article, but realising given
absolute
homology classes by
closed
submanifolds, were proved in a series of papers by Lee and Wilczyński
[
LW90
,
LW93
,
LW97
]
. Our method of proof has a similar structural approach, and uses their algebraic splitting theorem
[
LW93
, Theorem 6.1]
as a crucial mechanism to guarantee the slice discs described above in the stable manifold
N\#^{k}(S^{2}\times S^{2})
survive the process of destabilising back to
N
.

1.1.
Simple slice discs

Given a
4
-manifold
N
with
\partial N\cong S^{3}
, and
x\in H_{2}(N,\partial N)
, we denote by
x\cdot x
the algebraic self-intersection number of the unique class in
H_{2}(N)
that maps to
x
.

Our first main result provides sufficient conditions for a knot to bound a simple disc representing a given homology class.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
N
be a compact, oriented, simply-connected
4
-manifold with boundary
S^{3}
. Let
x\in H_{2}(N,\partial N)
be a nonzero class of divisibility
d
, and
suppose
K\subset S^{3}
is a knot such that
H_{1}(\Sigma_{d}(K))=0
.
The following are equivalent:

•

The knot
K
is sliced by a simple disc in
N
representing
x
.

•

(1)

\operatorname{Arf}(K)+\operatorname{ks}(N)+\frac{1}{8}(\sigma(N)-x\cdot x)\equiv 0\ \text{ mod }2
if
x
is characteristic, and

(2)

b_{2}(N)\geq\max_{0\leq j<d}|\sigma(N)-\frac{2j(d-j)}{d^{2}}\,x\cdot x+\sigma_{K}(e^{\frac{2\pi ij}{d}})|
.

Here,
\operatorname{ks}(N)
and
\sigma(N)
respectively denote the Kirby-Siebenmann invariant and signature of
N
, whereas
\sigma_{K}\colon S^{1}\to\mathbb{Z}
is the Levine-Tristram signature of
K
.
For
x
characteristic,
\sigma(N)-x\cdot x
is divisible by
8
because the intersection form of
N
is nonsingular; see e.g.
[
GS99
, Proposition 1.2.20]
.
A class
x\in H_{2}(N,\partial N)
is
characteristic
if
x\cdot a=a\cdot a
mod
2
for every
a\in H_{2}(N)
and
ordinary
otherwise.

Remark 1.2
.

The case
x=0
is excluded from Theorem
1.1
, but we note that, since simple discs in
N
have group
\mathbb{Z}
if and only if they are nullhomologous (see e.g.
[
CP23
, Lemma 5.1]
),
the results of
[
CPP25
]
already provide necessary and sufficient algebraic conditions for the existence of a simple slice disc.

Over the past couple of years, a lot of attention has gone into investigating when a knot bounds a nullhomologous slice disc (i.e. when a knot is
H
-slice
) in a given
4
-manifold
[
CN20
,
MMSW23
,
MMP24
,
IMT25
,
MP23
,
KMRS24
]
. More recently, there has also been some interest in the case of nonzero classes
[
Ren24
,
Qin24
]
.
Most of these articles focus on smooth obstructions, so Theorem
1.1
can be thought of as a topological counterpart to these recent results.

We now highlight some examples and consequences of Theorem
1.1
for small values of
d
.

The case
d=1

In the case
d=1
of Theorem
1.1
, or equivalently the case that
x
is primitive, the hypotheses simplify considerably. The condition
H_{1}(\Sigma_{d}(K))=H_{1}(S^{3})=0
holds vacuously, as does the second condition, since it reduces to the inequality
b_{2}(N)\geq|\sigma(N)|
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
N
be a compact, oriented, simply-connected
4
-manifold with boundary
S^{3}
, let
x\in H_{2}(N,\partial N)
be a nonzero primitive class, and let
K\subset S^{3}
be a knot.

•

For
x
ordinary,
K
is sliced by a simple disc in
N
representing
x
.

•

For
x
characteristic,
K
is sliced by a simple disc in
N
representing
x
if and only if

\operatorname{Arf}(K)+\operatorname{ks}(N)+\frac{1}{8}(\sigma(N)-x\cdot x)\equiv 0\ \text{ mod }2.

This corollary recovers
[
KPRT24
, Corollary 1.15]
, which, given a closed simply-connected
4
-manifold
X
, concerns sliceness in
X^{\circ}:=X\setminus\operatorname{Int}(B^{4})
.

Corollary 1.4
.

For
X\neq S^{4},\mathbb{C}P^{2},*\mathbb{C}P^{2}
simply-connected, every knot
K\subset\partial X^{\circ}
is slice in
X^{\circ}
.

Proof.

We prove that
H_{2}(X^{\circ})
contains a nonzero primitive ordinary class.
If
X
is spin, any nonzero primitive class is ordinary.
In the nonspin case, since
X\neq\mathbb{C}P^{2},*\mathbb{C}P^{2}
by hypothesis, work of Freedman
[
Fre82
]
ensures that
b_{2}(X)>1
.
We therefore obtain a
direct sum decomposition
H_{2}(X^{\circ})=\langle x,y\rangle\oplus H^{\prime}

for some abelian group
H^{\prime}
and
x,y\in H_{2}(X^{\circ})
, and one verifies that among the primitive classes
x,y
and
x+y
, at least one is ordinary.
The result now follows from Corollary
1.3
, noting that
H_{2}(X^{\circ})\cong H_{2}(X^{\circ},\partial X^{\circ})
.
∎

The proof of Corollary
1.4
shows that every knot bounds a disc in
X^{\circ}
which represents a primitive homology class; the inclusion of the adjective “primitive” can also be deduced from the proof of
[
KPRT24
, Corollary 1.5]
.
As an example application, note that since
1\in\mathbb{Z}\cong H_{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{2})
is characteristic, Corollary
1.3
shows that a knot
K
bounds a primitive disc in
(\mathbb{C}P^{2})^{\circ}
if and only if
\operatorname{Arf}(K)=0
mod
2
, recovering
[
MMRS24
, Theorem 1.2]
.

Example 1.5
.

Since
K3
is spin, any primitive class
x\in H_{2}(K3^{\circ},S^{3})
is ordinary, and therefore Corollary
1.3
implies that any knot bounds a disc in
K3^{\circ}
representing
x
.
This contrasts with the smooth category where the corresponding result is false; see e.g.
[
MMP24
, Section
4.4
]
.

The case
d=2

Next we consider the
d=2
case of Theorem
1.1
.
In this case, the condition
H_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(K))=0
is equivalent to
|\det(K)|=1
(see e.g.
[
Lic97
, Corollary 9.2]
).
This implies that
\Delta_{K}(-1)\equiv\pm 1
mod
8
and therefore
\operatorname{Arf}(K)=0
(see e.g.
[
Lic97
, Theorem 10.7]
).

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
N
be a compact, oriented, simply-connected
4
-manifold with boundary
S^{3}
and let
x\in H_{2}(N,\partial N)
be a nonzero class of divisibility
2
.
Suppose
K\subset S^{3}
is a knot such that
|\det(K)|=1
.
The following are equivalent:

•

The knot
K
is sliced by a simple disc in
N
representing
x
.

•

(1)

\operatorname{ks}(N)+\frac{1}{8}(\sigma(N)-x\cdot x)\equiv 0\ \text{ mod }2
when
x
is characteristic, and

(2)

b_{2}(N)\geq\operatorname{max}(\,|\sigma(N)-\frac{1}{2}\,x\cdot x+\sigma(K)|\,,\,|\sigma(N)|\,)
.

Here,
\sigma(K)=\sigma_{K}(-1)
denotes the (Murasugi) signature of
K
.

Example 1.7
.

In
(\mathbb{C}P^{2})^{\circ}
, the second condition can be rewritten as
1\geq\operatorname{max}(|-1+\sigma(K)|,1)
.
Since
2\in\mathbb{Z}\cong H_{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{2})
is ordinary and the signature of a knot is even, it follows that
K
with
|\det(K)|=1
bounds a simple
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-disc in
\mathbb{C}P^{2}
if and only if
\sigma(K)\in\{0,2\}
.
In
(\overline{\mathbb{C}P^{2}})^{\circ}
, the second condition can be rewritten as
1\geq\operatorname{max}(|1+\sigma(K)|,1)
, so this time it follows that
K
with
|\det(K)|=1
bounds a simple
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-disc if and only if
\sigma(K)\in\{-2,0\}
.

Remark 1.8
.

The left handed trefoil knot
K
has nontrivial determinant, and it is not too difficult to verify that it bounds a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-disc in
(\mathbb{C}P^{2})^{\circ}
; such a disc arises by unknotting
K
via a single crossing change whose corresponding crossing change disc intersects
K
twice algebraically, see e.g.
[
MMRS24
, Figure 3.1]
.
Thus, the assumption
|\det(K)|=1
is not necessary for the conclusion of Corollary
1.6
to hold, and more generally, the assumption
H_{1}(\Sigma_{d}(K))=0
is not necessary to the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
. However, as noted in Theorem
1.10
and Proposition
3.9
, the conditions on the Arf invariant and the signature are necessary for the knot
K
to bound a disc
D
representing
x
.

Remark 1.9
.

Theorem
1.1
, its corollaries, and Theorem
1.10
also hold if
\partial N
is an integral homology
3
-sphere provided the congruence in the characteristic case is replaced by

\operatorname{Arf}(K)+\operatorname{ks}(N)+\frac{1}{8}(\sigma(N)-x\cdot x)\equiv\mu(\partial N)\ \text{ mod }2,

where
\mu(\partial N)
denotes the Rochlin invariant of
\partial N
.
For example, when
N=X_{\pm 1}(J)
is the
4
-manifold obtained by attaching a
\pm 1
-framed
2
-handle to
D^{4}
, we deduce that a knot
K
is sliced by a simple disc representing a primitive class if and only if
\operatorname{Arf}(K)\equiv\operatorname{Arf}(J)\mod{2}
; indeed González-Acuña proved that
\mu(S^{3}_{\pm 1}(J))=\operatorname{Arf}(J)

[
GAn70
]
.

1.2.
Simple slice discs stably

We say a knot
K\subset N
is
stably slice
if it is the boundary of a locally flat disc in
N_{k}:=N\#^{k}(S^{2}\times S^{2})
for some
k\geq 0
.
The proof of Theorem
1.1
closely follows the approach of Lee and Wilczyński
[
LW90
,
LW93
]
: the first condition of Theorem
1.1
ensures that
K
is stably slice, whereas the second condition ensures that the
S^{2}\times S^{2}
summands can be removed without harming the disc. We record the outcome of this first step as it might be of independent interest.
We say that a homology class
x\in H_{2}(N,\partial N)
is
stably representable
if there is a
k\geq 0
such that
x\oplus 0\in H_{2}(N_{k},\partial N)
can be represented by an embedded surface.

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
N
be a simply-connected
4
-manifold with boundary
S^{3}
, let
K\subset S^{3}
be a knot.

(1)

The knot
K
is stably sliced by a disc stably representing
x
if and only if either
x
is ordinary or
x
is characteristic and

(1)

\operatorname{Arf}(K)+\operatorname{ks}(N)+\frac{1}{8}(\sigma(N)-x\cdot x)\equiv 0\mod 2.

(2)

For any
g>0
, every class
x\in H_{2}(N,\partial N)
is stably representable by a genus
g
surface with boundary
K
.

In both (
1
) and (
2
), the surface may be taken to be simple.

Given a class
x\in H_{2}(N,\partial N)
, the combination of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.10
suggests analysing the minimal number of stabilisations needed for a knot
K
to bound a disc representing
x
.
When
x=0
and
N=D^{4}
, this quantity
is referred to as the
stabilising number

\operatorname{sn}(K)
and has been studied in
[
CN20
,
KMT24
,
FL24
,
FT25
]
.

Definition 1.11
.

For
x\in H_{2}(N,\partial N)
, the
(x,N)
-stabilising number
of
K
refers to

\operatorname{sn}_{x,N}(K)=\min\{\ k\ |\ K\text{ bounds a disc $D\subset N\#^{k}(S^{2}\times S^{2})$ stably representing }x\}

whereas the
simple
(x,N)
-stabilising number
is

\operatorname{sn}_{x,N}^{\operatorname{simple}}(K)=\min\{\ k\ |\ K\text{ bounds a simple disc $D\subset N\#^{k}(S^{2}\times S^{2})$ stably representing }x\}.

Remark 1.12
.

Clearly, by definition, it is always the case that
\operatorname{sn}_{x,N}(K)\leq\operatorname{sn}_{x,N}^{\operatorname{simple}}(K)
.

When
N=D^{4}
and
x=0
, it is known that
\operatorname{sn}(K)
is finite if and only if
\operatorname{Arf}(K)=0

[
Sch10
]
(see also
[
CN20
, Theorem 2.9]
) in which case
\sigma_{K}(\omega)\leq\operatorname{sn}(K)\leq g_{4}(K)
, where the first inequality holds whenever
\omega\in S^{1}
is not the root of a
p(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm 1}]
with
p(1)=1

[
CN20
, Theorems 3.10 and 5.15]
.
Here,
g_{4}(K)
denotes the
4
-genus of
K
.
In this setting, requiring the disc be simple amounts to it having knot group
\mathbb{Z}
in which case,
\operatorname{sn}_{0,D^{4}}^{\text{simple}}(K)
is equal to the
\mathbb{Z}
-stabilising number

\operatorname{sn}_{\mathbb{Z}}(K)
introduced in
[
FL24
]
.
Our results lead to analogous (but stronger) statements in the case where
{x\neq 0}
, as we are able to show that when
d
is a prime power,
\operatorname{sn}_{x,N}(K)
only depends on the algebraic topology of
N
and on the Levine-Tristram signature of
K
.

Corollary 1.13
.

Fix a nonzero
x\in H_{2}(N,\partial N)
of divisibility
d

and a knot
K\subset\partial N
.
Then
\operatorname{sn}_{x,N}(K)
is finite if and only if
\operatorname{sn}^{\operatorname{simple}}_{x,N}(K)
is finite if and only if
x
is ordinary or
x
is characteristic and (
1
) from Theorem
1.10
holds.

If the stabilising numbers are finite and also
H_{1}(\Sigma_{d}(K))=0
, then

(2)

\operatorname{sn}_{x,N}^{\operatorname{simple}}(K)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\max_{0\leq j<d}\left|\sigma(N)-\frac{2j(d-j)}{d^{2}}\,x\cdot x+\sigma_{K}(e^{\frac{2\pi ij}{d}})\right|-b_{2}(N)\right).

If, additionally,
d
is a prime power, then

\operatorname{sn}_{x,N}(K)=\operatorname{sn}_{x,N}^{\operatorname{simple}}(K).

Proof.

The characterisation of finite stabilisation numbers follows immediately from Theorem
1.10
. For the next statements, write
m
for the right-hand side of equation (
2
). Applying Theorem
1.1
to the
4
-manifold
N\#^{m}(S^{2}\times S^{2})
shows that if
H_{1}(\Sigma_{d}(K))=0
, there exists a simple slice disc in
N\#^{m}(S^{2}\times S^{2})
representing
x\oplus 0
. Thus
\operatorname{sn}_{x,N}^{\operatorname{simple}}(K)\leq m
and hence
\operatorname{sn}_{x,N}(K)\leq m
. The inequality
\operatorname{sn}_{x,N}^{\operatorname{simple}}(K)\geq m
follows from Proposition
3.9
, which is essentially due to Gilmer
[
Gil81
]
. As discussed in the proof of Proposition
3.9
, Gilmer requires that
d
be a prime power dividing
x
,
but when the surface is simple and
d
is the divisibility of
x
,
one can argue that this stipulation can be dropped.
Whence
m
is a lower bound for
\operatorname{sn}_{x,N}^{\operatorname{simple}}(K)
, and also for
\operatorname{sn}_{x,N}(K)
if
d
is a prime power. This proves the two claimed equalities.
∎

Remark 1.14
.

We do not know whether there exist examples where
H_{1}(\Sigma_{d}(K))=0
and
\operatorname{sn}_{x,N}(K)\neq\operatorname{sn}_{x,N}^{\operatorname{simple}}(K)
.
It would be interesting to know whether such examples exist, or conversely whether prime power divisibility is a non-necessary condition, just an artefact of the proof.

1.3.
Examples

We give some sample applications for some of our results.
First, we give examples of knots for which Theorem
1.1
can be used to generate locally flat discs in a homology class, where a smooth disc can be proven to not exist.

Example 1.15
.

We show that if
K\in\{14n11942,14n13659\}
then
K
bounds a locally flat
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-disc in
(\overline{\mathbb{C}P}^{2})^{\circ}
but does not bound a smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-disc in
(\overline{\mathbb{C}P}^{2})^{\circ}
. To see
K
does not bound a smooth disc, we used KnotJob
[
Sch25
]
to compute the
s
-invariant is
s(K)=8
, then applied
[

, Theorem 1.3]
to the class
3\in H^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{C}P}^{2},S^{3})
, noting that

. To see Theorem

applies, we first computed that in each case
\left|\Delta_{K}(e^{\frac{2\pi i}{3}})\cdot\Delta_{K}(e^{\frac{4\pi i}{3}})\right|=1
, so by a classical result of Goeritz and Fox
[
Goe34
,

we have that

(

1.1
(

) holds, confirming

bounds the claimed locally flat disc.

Remark 1.16
.

Examples of the previous sort are difficult to find among low-crossing knots, as we now discuss. The knot invariants in Theorem
1.1
are the Arf invariant and Levine-Tristram signature, the latter of which achieves its maximum near the Murasugi signature
\sigma(K)=\sigma_{K}(-1)
, and computations with low-crossing knots show this is roughly where the quantity on the right of Theorem
1.1
(
2
) usually achieves its maximum. On the other hand, the current best techniques to obstruct smooth sliceness within a homology class use the
\tau
-invariant (see
[
OS03
, Theorem 1.1]
) or
s
-invariant (see
[
Qin24
]
). Due to the well-known close connections between
\sigma(K)
,
\tau
, and

, among low-crossing knots, there is a tension between trying to make
|s|
or
|\tau|
large relative to

to obstruct smooth sliceness, while desiring small

relative to

to apply Theorem

.

Next, we consider when winding number 0 satellites of the negative torus knot

bound a
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
-disc in
N=(\mathbb{C}P^{2})^{\circ}
and its stabilisations. For

, the knot
5_{1}
is readily seen by

methods to bound a
smooth

\mathbb{Z}_{d}
-disc in
(\mathbb{C}P^{2})^{\circ}
; the purpose of taking satellites is that it is likely that a suitable choice of companion knot will preclude the satellite from being smoothly
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-slice in
(\mathbb{C}P^{2})^{\circ}
. As we shall see, the knot invariants used in Theorem
1.1
are robust under a winding number

-discs.

Example 1.17
.

Use
P(K)
to denote a satellite knot with pattern
P=T(-2,5)
, winding number zero, and companion
K
.
We claim
P(K)
bounds a
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
-disc in

if and only if

.

We first show
P(K)
indeed bounds a
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-disc.
Performing winding number zero satellite operations does not affect the Levine-Tristram signature, the Arf invariant and the homology of the

are satisfied for

.
For

,

-fold branched cover of the

.
The remaining conditions for Theorem

is

\mathbb{Z}_{3}

The knot has
\operatorname{Arf}(P)=1

Using Sage, we compute

Thus (

Now we show that if

.
Again, it suffices to verify this for

.
It follows from Theorem

Since

Organisation

In Section
2

1.10
.
In Section

In Section

Conventions

We work in the topological category. Embeddings are assumed to be locally flat and manifolds are assumed to be compact, connected and oriented.
Modules are assumed to be finitely generated.

2.
Stable embedding

Throughout this section, let
N
be a simply-connected
4
-manifold with boundary
S^{3}
and set
N_{k}:=N\#^{k}S^{2}\times S^{2}
. We say that a homology class
x\in H_{2}(N,\partial N)
is
stably representable
if there is a
k\geq 0
such that
x\oplus 0\in H_{2}(N_{k},\partial N)
is represented by an embedded surface. The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
1.10
.

We briefly sketch the proof strategy. Without any stabilisation, one can always represent
x
by
some
embedded surface.
The objective is to perform ambient surgery on this surface to reduce the genus to zero, without leaving the homology class. Ambient surgery along curves in the surface needs to be guided by “surface-framed”, embedded discs in the
4
-manifold, whose interiors are disjoint from the surface.
Without stabilising we will only have incorrectly framed, embedded discs whose interiors intersect the surface. Stabilising adds the freedom to change a disc framing by an even number (Lemma
2.9
), and to perform the stable Whitney move, removing algebraically cancelling double points between surface and disc (Lemma
2.10
).
If
x
is ordinary and
N
is not spin, one also has the freedom to tube into a
w_{2}
-nontrivial sphere and change the framing on the disc. If
x
is ordinary, the procedures just described mean stable ambient surgery can always be performed to reduce the surface to genus zero.
If
x
is characteristic, stable ambient surgery can be performed until the genus is reduced to one. There is then a genuine obstruction to performing the final surgery, measured by the condition (
1
).
Finally, in either case and at the expense of further stabilisations, surgeries may be performed on the ambient manifold, in the complement of the surface, to kill commutators in the fundamental group and produce a
simple
surface.

Remark 2.1
.

This strategy is inspired by the work of Lee and Wilczyński in the closed case
[
LW90
, proof of Theorem 2.1]
which itself draws on ideas from of Freedman and Kirby
[
FK78
]
.
We adapt these arguments to the case of discs, referring to
[
LW90
]
when the arguments are unchanged, while also supplementing some details related to the verification that the disc lies in
N\#^{k}S^{2}\times S^{2}
(i.e. that no
\#S^{2}\widetilde{\times}S^{2}
is involved) and that it indeed represents
x\oplus 0
; see Proposition
2.7
.

2.1.
Disc framing obstruction and the Matsumoto-Freedman-Kirby quadratic form

The first half of the proof of Theorem
1.10
consists of arguing that it can be reduced to the following statement, which we call the
ambient surgery criteria
. The purpose of the current section is to describe and motivate the quadratic form
q_{F}
mentioned in the ambient surgery criteria.

Theorem 2.2

(The ambient surgery criteria)
.

Let
N
be a simply-connected
4
-manifold with boundary
S^{3}
, let
K\subset\partial N
be a knot, let
x\in H_{2}(N,\partial N)
, let
F
be a surface representing
x
, with boundary
K
, and let
\gamma\subset F
be a simple closed curve.
If

(1)

either
x
is ordinary, or

(2)

x
is characteristic and
q_{F}([\gamma])=0
,

then there is an
r\geq 0
such that ambient surgery on
F\subset N_{r}
along
\gamma
is possible and such that the effect
F^{\prime}
of surgery represents
x\oplus 0
and has
\partial F^{\prime}=K
.

We emphasise that this theorem and its proof rely heavily on work of Freedman-Kirby
[
Fre82
]
and Lee-Wilczyński
[
LW90
]
in the closed case.

Let
F\subset N
be an embedded surface in a simply-connected
4
-manifold, and let
\gamma\subset F
be a simple closed curve.
In order to perform surgery on
F
along
\gamma

ambiently
, we need to find an embedded disc
D\subset N
that satisfies
\partial D=F\cap D=\gamma
and such that a certain framing condition, that we now describe, is satisfied.

Construction 2.3

(The framing obstruction
W(D)\in\mathbb{Z}
)
.

Let
\gamma\subset F
be an embedded circle.
As
H_{1}(N)=0
, this circle bounds an immersed disc
D\looparrowright N.

It can be assumed that the interior of
D
meets
F
transversally.
Note that, as
F
is orientable, the line bundle
\nu(\gamma\subset F)
is trivial;
it thus admits a unique trivialisation up to homotopy (because trivialisations of a line bundle over a circle are classified by
[S^{1},O(1)]=\pi_{1}(O(1))=\pi_{1}(\{\pm 1\})=1
).
Decompose the restriction of the normal
2
-plane bundle
\nu(D\subset N)
to
\gamma=\partial D
as

\nu(D\subset N)|_{\gamma}\cong\nu(\gamma\subset F)\oplus\xi

for some line bundle
\xi
over
\gamma
.
As
\nu(\gamma\subset F)
is trivial and
\nu(D\subset N)|_{\gamma}
is trivial, so
\xi
must also be trivial.
Thus
\xi
admits a unique trivialisation up to homotopy.
Combining the framings of
\nu(\gamma\subset F)
and
\xi
we therefore obtain a framing of
\nu(D\subset N)|_{\gamma}
.
Consider the Euler number obstruction to extending this
surface framing
of
\nu(D\subset N)|_{\gamma}
to a framing of
\nu(D\subset N)
:

W(D)\in H^{2}(D,\partial D;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}.

The integer
W(D)
is measuring the difference between the surface framing of
\nu(D\subset N)|_{\gamma}
and the framing of
\nu(D\subset N)|_{\gamma}
induced by the unique trivialisation of the
2
-plane bundle
\nu(D\subset N)
.
Observe that if the interior of
D
is disjoint from
F
, i.e.
\gamma=\partial D=D\cap F
, then
W(D)
is precisely the obstruction to performing ambient surgery on
F
along
\gamma
using
D
, with the result still an embedded surface.

We now discuss the possibility of eliminating the framing obstruction of a disc
D
, and making its interior disjoint from
F
, at the expense of stabilising the ambient manifold.

Remark 2.4
.

A boundary twist of
D
(see
[
FQ90
, p. 15]
) simultaneously changes both the algebraic intersection number
D\cdot F
and
W(D)
by
\pm 1
; see e.g.
[
FK78
, page 87]
.
Thus we can always perform boundary twists so that one of the two quantities vanishes.
We will see below that, at the expense of stabilising
N
, the Whitney move can be performed (see Lemma
2.10
), so that if the disc and the surface meet algebraically 0 times, these intersections can be removed. We will also see, again at the expense of stabilising
N
, that the framing obstruction
W(D)
can always be changed by
\pm 2
(see Lemma
2.9
). Thus, if we allow stabilising, the obstruction to doing ambient surgery on
F
along
\gamma
to obtain an embedded result is whether or not
W(D)
and
D\cdot F
have the same parity. In other words, we need to consider the parity of
W(D)+D\cdot F.

Given an embedded sphere
S\subset N
, defining
D^{\prime}:=D\#S
, we have

W(D^{\prime})=W(D)+Q_{N}([S],[S])\qquad\text{ and }\qquad D^{\prime}\cdot F=D\cdot F+Q_{N}^{\partial}([F],[S]),

where
Q_{N}^{\partial}
denotes algebraic intersections of relative and absolute classes,
and therefore

W(D^{\prime})+D^{\prime}\cdot F=\left(W(D)+D\cdot F\right)+Q_{N}([S],[S])+Q_{N}^{\partial}([F],[S]).

When
F
is characteristic
Q_{N}([S],[S])=Q_{N}^{\partial}([F],[S])
mod
2
, and so replacing
D
by
D^{\prime}
cannot change the overall parity.
Thus in the characteristic case this will lead to a genuine obstruction.
However, when
[F]
is ordinary, one can hope that the parity of
W(D)+D\cdot F
can be changed by tubing into an appropriate sphere in
S\subset N
.

This obstruction in the characteristic case is captured by the Arf invariant of a certain quadratic form over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, whose definition we now recall.

Proposition 2.5

(Matsumoto, Freedman-Kirby)
.

Let
N
be a simply-connected
4
-manifold, let
F\subset N
be a properly embedded characteristic surface and let
\gamma\subset F
be an embedded circle.
The assignment

q_{F}(\gamma)=:W(D)+D\cdot F\quad\text{mod }2

gives rise to a well defined quadratic refinement
q_{F}\colon H_{1}(F;\mathbb{Z}_{2})\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
of the intersection form on
H_{1}(F;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
.

2.2.
Reduction of Theorem
1.10
to the ambient surgery criteria

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
1.10
, assuming Theorem
2.2
is true.

Construction 2.6

(The framing
\operatorname{fr}_{D}
of the boundary of an immersed disc
D\looparrowright N
)
.

Given an immersed disc
D\looparrowright N
, the normal bundle
\nu(D)
is trivial and, up to homotopy, admits a unique framing compatible with its orientation.
This framing induces a framing of the rank
2
subbundle
\nu(D)|_{\partial D}\subset\nu(\partial D\subset N)
.
This latter framing can be completed to a framing
\operatorname{fr}_{D}
of the rank
3
bundle
\nu(\partial D\subset N)
using the outwards normal in first position.

The following result describes how a loop in
N
can be framed so that the effect of surgery on it is a stabilisation of
N
by
S^{2}\times S^{2}
.
In the presence of a surface
F\subset N
representing a homology class

, this result also provides a condition ensuring that
F
represents
x\oplus 0
in the stabilisation.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
N
be a simply-connected

4
-manifold.

•

Suppose
\alpha\subset N
is an embedded loop that bounds an immersed disc
D
. Then surgery on
N
along
\alpha
using the framing
\operatorname{fr}_{D}
is homeomorphic to
N\#(S^{2}\times S^{2})
.

•

Let
F\subset N
be a properly embedded surface representing a homology class
x\in H_{2}(N,\partial N)
, suppose that
\alpha\cap F=\emptyset
and that
\alpha\subset N\setminus F
is nullhomologous.
Then a disc
D\subset N
(and thus the framing
\operatorname{fr}_{D}
) may be chosen so that
F
represents
x\oplus 0\in H_{2}(N\#S^{2}\times S^{2},\partial N)
in the effect of surgery.

Proof.

Make the disc
D
embedded by using finger moves on
D
to push self-intersections of
D
past the boundary
\partial D
; see e.g.
[
BKK+21
, Figure 11.4]
.
Finger moves on a disc do not change the framing, relative to a given boundary framing, so the framing
\operatorname{fr}_{D}
is unchanged.
We can assume that a tubular neighbourhood
\overline{\nu}(\alpha)
has been chosen small enough so that
\overline{\nu}(\alpha)\cap D
is a closed collar of
\partial D
.
As
D
is embedded, so is the disc
D^{\prime}:=D\setminus\nu(\alpha)
.
Note that
\partial D^{\prime}=D^{\prime}\cap\overline{\nu}(\alpha)
. We set
\alpha^{\prime}:=\partial D^{\prime}
.

The idea for showing the effect of surgery along
\alpha
is
N\#S^{2}\times S^{2}
is to use the framing
\operatorname{fr}_{D}
to work in a preferred model of

with
\alpha\subset S^{4}
in which it will be clear that surgery along
\alpha
results in a

summand instead of an
S^{2}\widetilde{\times}S^{2}
summand.
Use
\operatorname{fr}_{D}
to frame
\alpha
and thus identify
\overline{\nu}(\alpha)=S^{1}\times D^{3}
.
Since
\alpha^{\prime}=\partial\overline{\nu}(\alpha)\cap D^{\prime}
,
by definition of
\operatorname{fr}_{D}
, we have that
\alpha^{\prime}
is a pushoff using the framing
\operatorname{fr}_{D}
. We have hence identified
\alpha^{\prime}=S^{1}\times\{p\}
for some

.
Consequently, we obtain an identification
\overline{\nu}(\alpha)\cup\overline{\nu}(D^{\prime})=S^{1}\times D^{3}\cup D^{2}\times D^{2}
,
the effect of attaching a
4
-dimensional
2
-handle to
\partial(S^{1}\times D^{3})=S^{1}\times S^{2}
along the curve
\alpha^{\prime}=S^{1}\times\{p\}
.
Note this effect is homeomorphic to
D^{4}
and so we obtain a particular choice of homeomorphism
\phi^{\prime}_{D^{\prime}}\colon N\#S^{4}\cong N
.
Excising
S^{1}\times\mathring{D^{3}}
and gluing back

via the identity map is precisely doing surgery on
N
along
\alpha
with the given framing
\operatorname{fr}_{D}
. In the punctured
S^{4}
summand, we obtain
D^{2}\times S^{2}\cup D^{2}\times D^{2}=S^{2}\times S^{2}\setminus(\mathring{D}^{2}\times\mathring{D}^{2})

.
Hence we obtain a homeomorphism
\phi_{D^{\prime}}\colon N\#(S^{2}\times S^{2})\cong N^{\prime}
. This completes the proof of the first item. For later on, we observe that, by construction, the sphere
S^{2}\times\{p\}
is
D^{\prime}\cup_{\alpha^{\prime}}\Delta
, where
\Delta:=D^{2}\times\{p\}\subset D^{2}\times S^{2}
is a core of surgery.

We discuss the strategy for the second item before going into the details. If
\alpha

\operatorname{fr}_{D}

intersects
\{\operatorname{pt}\}\times S^{2}

Thus the proof would be complete if the framing
\operatorname{fr}_{D}
was such as to guarantee that a homology representative of
[S^{2}\times\{\operatorname{pt}\}]

F
algebraically zero times.
For this, we will construct

intersects

algebraically
0
times.
The embedded sphere

, where

is a core of surgery, represents

.
As

(geometrically).
Thus we will be able to conclude that

.

We construct the disc

by first performing ambient surgery on a surface with boundary
\alpha
; here are the details.
As
\alpha
is nullhomologous in

, choose a surface
\Sigma\subset N\setminus F
with
\partial\Sigma=\penalty\ \alpha
.
Since

is simply-connected, loops representing generators of

By performing boundary twists on the immersed discs, we arrange that their framing obstructions vanish; recall Construction
2.3
and Remark

Performing ambient surgery on

with
\partial D=\alpha
.
The immersed disc

intersects

zero times algebraically:
upon performing surgery on
\Sigma

are replaced by pairs of discs that

to push self-intersections of the disc across the boundary

.

We now have an embedded disc
D
with boundary
\alpha
, intersecting

algebraically
0
times.
As before, by taking
\overline{\nu}(\alpha)
small enough, we may assume
\overline{\nu}(\alpha)\cap D
is a small closed collar of
\partial D
.
As before, remove the (open) collar, to obtain

. As desired,

intersects

algebraically
0

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.10
assuming Theorem
2.2
.

We claim that it suffices to prove that

K

is ordinary or if

(noting, in order to use Proposition

This concludes the proof of the claim.

We now use Theorem

to prove the assertion that

is representable by a disc with boundary

is ordinary or if

is characteristic and (
1
) holds, and by a punctured torus with boundary

Represent

by a surface

with boundary

.
Note that
[

implies that if

If

and the Whitney trick iteratively
to the surface
S=F\cup D
.
Before performing the Whitney trick,
F
represents
x\oplus 0
in
N\#^{k}(S^{2}\times S^{2})
and the Whitney trick does not affect this.
∎

2.4.
Proof of the ambient surgery criteria

We now prove the ambient surgery criteria whose statement we recall for the convenience of the reader.

Theorem
2.2
.

Let
N
be a simply-connected
4
-manifold with boundary
S^{3}
, let
K\subset\partial N
be a knot, let
x\in H_{2}(N,\partial N)
, let
F
be a properly embedded surface representing
x
with boundary
K
, and let
\gamma\subset F
be a simple closed curve.
If

(1)

either
x
is ordinary, or

(2)

x
is characteristic and
q_{F}([\gamma])=0
,

then there is an
r\geq 0
such that ambient surgery on
F\subset N_{r}
along
\gamma
is possible and such that the effect
F^{\prime}
of surgery represents
x\oplus 0
and has
\partial F^{\prime}=K
.

Proof.

The argument is inspired by
[
LW90
, Proof of Theorem 2.1]
:
in both cases one wants to find an embedded disc
D
in a stabilisation of
N
in which
D\cap F=\partial D=\gamma
and
W(D)=0
.
Once these two conditions are satisfied, one can perform ambient surgery.

We assert that there is an embedded disc
D
in a stabilisation of
N
with
\partial D=D\cap F=\gamma
; in this stabilisation
F
represents
x\oplus 0
.
We may push
\gamma
off
F
so that the pushoff
\gamma^{\prime}
is nullhomologous in
N\setminus F
.
We briefly justify this claim. Choose an arbitrary pushoff of
\gamma
; that is, pick an arbitrary section
s\colon\gamma\to S(\nu(F\subset N))\cong\gamma\times S^{1}
of the sphere bundle of the (trivial) normal bundle of
F\subset N
. As
N
is simply-connected,
H_{1}(N\setminus F)
is cyclic, generated by the class
\mu
of a meridian to
F
.
Thus
[s(\gamma)]=k\mu
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Adding
-k
meridional turns to
s
produces a new section
s^{\prime}
, and
s^{\prime}(\gamma)=:\gamma^{\prime}
is then nullhomologous.

Endow the push off
\gamma^{\prime}
with the framing specified by the second item of
Proposition
2.7
. By that proposition, after performing surgery on
N
along
\gamma^{\prime}
, we obtain an embedded disc in a stabilisation of
N
with
\partial D=D\cap F=\gamma
.
Since we used a nullhomologous pushoff, the surface represents
x\oplus 0
; recall Proposition
2.7
.
This establishes the assertion.

We begin with an outline of the argument to come.
In the characteristic case, the condition
q_{F}([\gamma])=0
ensures that
W(D)
is even; after further stabilisations Lemma
2.9
ensures we can assume
W(D)=0
.
In the ordinary case, we show that at the expense of adding an even number of intersections between
D
and
F
, the framing obstruction
W(D)
can be made even.
We apply the stable Whitney trick (Corollary
2.11
) to remove these intersections and then use Lemma
2.9
to stabilise
N
some more to obtain
W(D)=0
.
Thus in both cases we have found an embedded disc
D
in a stabilisation of
N
with
D\cap F=\partial D=\gamma
and
W(D)=0
and can therefore perform ambient surgery.

We give some details starting with the characteristic case.
Since the push off
\gamma^{\prime}
is nullhomologous, we saw that
F
represents
x\oplus 0
in the stabilisation
N_{r}
(Proposition
2.7
) and this class remains characteristic.
Since
\gamma
lies in
N
, one verifies that
q_{F\subset N}([\gamma])=q_{F\subset N_{r}}([\gamma])
: both quantities can be calculated using discs in
N
.
Since
D\cap F=\partial D=\gamma
, it follows that

0=q_{F\subset N}([\gamma])=q_{F\subset N_{r}}([\gamma])=W(D)\mod 2.

Now apply Lemma
2.9
, at the cost of a single stabilisation per use, so that
W(D)=0
.
We can therefore perform ambient surgery.

We now consider the situation where
x
is ordinary.
Since
D\cap F=\partial D=\gamma
, if
W(D)
was even, then the result would follow by the same argument as in the characteristic case.
We therefore assume that
W(D)
is odd; thus
W(D)+D\cdot F
is odd.
As noted in Remark
2.4
, we need only find an embedded sphere
S\subset N
such that
Q_{N}([S],[S])+Q_{N}^{\partial}([F],[S])
is odd, for then the disc
D^{\prime}:=D\#S
would satisfy

W(D^{\prime})+D^{\prime}\cdot F=(W(D)+D\cdot F)+Q_{N}([S],[S])+Q_{N}^{\partial}([F],[S])\equiv 1+1\equiv 0\mod 2.

This would imply that
W(D^{\prime})
and
D^{\prime}\cdot F
have the same parity and therefore boundary twists would allow us to find a new disc
D^{\prime\prime}
with
W(D^{\prime\prime})
even and
D^{\prime\prime}\cdot F=0
.
The combination of the stable Whitney trick (Corollary
2.11
) and Lemma
2.9
would then allow us to arrange for a disc
D^{\prime\prime\prime}
with
D^{\prime\prime\prime}\cap F=\partial D^{\prime\prime\prime}=\gamma
and
W(D^{\prime\prime\prime})=0
after stabilisations.
This would allow for ambient surgery.
We have therefore reduced the proof of the remainder of the theorem to the following claim.

Claim 1
.

There is a sphere
S\subset N
such that
Q_{N}([S],[S])
and
Q_{N}^{\partial}([F],[S])
have different parities.

Proof.

We need to find a class
u\in H_{2}(N)
such that
Q_{N}(u,u)
and
Q_{N}^{\partial}(x,u)
have different parities.
However since we want
u
to be representable by an embedded sphere we also arrange for
u
to be primitive and ordinary; recall Lemma
2.8
.

Since
H_{i}(S^{3})=0
for
i=1,2
,
there is a unique class
x^{\prime}\in H_{2}(N)
such that
i_{*}(x^{\prime})=x
, where
i_{*}\colon H_{2}(N)\to H_{2}(N,\partial N)
denotes the inclusion induced map.
Since, by definition of the intersection form, we have
Q_{N}(x^{\prime},u)=Q_{N}^{\partial}(i_{*}(x^{\prime}),u)=Q_{N}^{\partial}(x,u)
, our revised goal is to find a primitive, ordinary
u\in H_{2}(N)
such that
Q_{N}(u,u)
and
Q_{N}(x^{\prime},u)
have different parities.
The argument for finding this
u
is identical to the argument in the closed case from
[
LW90
, page 393]
.
This concludes the proof of the claim.
∎

As explained above, the claim ensures we can perform ambient surgery on
\gamma
.
It remains to argue that that the outcome stably represents
x
.
We recall why ambient surgery is possible.
The bundle
\nu(\gamma\subset F)
is trivial.
Pick one of the two sections(=framings) for this bundle, call it
s
, and identify
\nu(\gamma\subset F)
with
\gamma\times[-1,1]
.
As
W(\Delta_{i})=0
, the section
s
extends to a section of
\nu(\Delta\subset N)
.
Since the manifolds are orientable, we obtain a framing of
\Delta
and can therefore perform ambient surgery.

Write
\gamma^{\pm}:=\gamma\times\{\pm 1\}
.
Since the section
s
extends over
D
, we obtain disjoint discs
\Delta^{\pm}
with boundary
\gamma^{\pm}
.
The ambient surgery removes
\nu(\gamma\subset F)
from
F
, and adds
\Delta^{\pm}
.
Since the surgery is performed in the interior of
F
we have
\partial F^{\prime}=\partial F
.

We argue that
[F^{\prime}]=[F]
.
It suffices to show
F^{\prime}\cdot z=F\cdot z
for every
z\in H_{2}(N)
.
By construction we have
z\cdot F^{\prime}=z\cdot F+z\cdot\Delta^{+}+z\cdot\Delta^{-}
.
An intersection between
z
and
\Delta
introduces an intersection between
z
and
\Delta^{+}
and also an intersection between
z
and
\Delta^{-}
, but these intersections have opposite signs because they come at either end of
\Delta\times[0,1]
.
It follows that
z\cdot F^{\prime}=z\cdot F
for every
z\in H_{2}(N)
and therefore
[F]=[F^{\prime}].
∎

3.
Sufficient conditions for the existence of a disc

Throughout this section, let
N
be a simply-connected
4
-manifold with boundary
S^{3}
, let
K\subset\partial N\cong S^{3}
be a knot with
H_{1}(\Sigma_{d}(K))=0
, let
x\in H_{2}(N,\partial N)
be a nonzero class of divisibility
d

and assume that a simple disc with boundary
K
exists in a stabilisation
N_{k}:=N\#^{k}S^{2}\times S^{2}
, representing
x\oplus 0
.

The purpose of this section is to prove Proposition
3.11
, where we list three conditions that are sufficient to ensure the existence of a simple disc in
N
representing
x\in H_{2}(N,\partial N)
.
The conditions are distilled from an algebraic theorem due to Lee and Wilczyński, applied to our setup.
After recalling the notion of a pointed hermitian form in Section
3.1
,
Section
3.2
recalls the Lee-Wilczyński theorem; in essence, their splitting theorem lists five conditions that are sufficient for a pointed hermitian form to split off a hyperbolic form.
Section
3.3
notes that in our setting, one of their conditions is automatic.
Section
3.4
reformulates another of the conditions using Levine-Tristram signature of
K
.
Section
3.5
states the remaining conditions in a way amenable to verification in subsequent sections. We also provide the geometric argument confirming the conditions are sufficient to slice the knot
K
in
N
.

3.1.
Pointed hermitian forms

This section recalls the definition of a pointed hermitian form.

Definition 3.1
.

A
pointed hermitian form
over a ring with involution
R
is a triple
(P,\lambda,z)
where
P
is an
R
-module,
\lambda
is a hermitian form on
P
, and
z\in P
.

This notion played a prominent role in
[
LW90
,
LW93
,
LW97
]
.

Example 3.2
.

We describe two examples of pointed hermitian forms.

•

The
pointed hyperbolic form
over a ring with involution
R
refers to

H(R)=\left(R\oplus R,\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},0\oplus 0\right).

•

Let
N
,
K
,
x
be as described at the start of the section, and let
D\subset N\#^{k}S^{2}\times S^{2}
be a disc representing
x\oplus 0
, with boundary
K
. Consider the
d
-fold branched cover
\Sigma_{d}(D)
of
D\subset N_{k}
.
The branch set, namely the embedded disc
\widetilde{D}\subset\Sigma_{d}(D)
, represents a
class in
H_{2}(\Sigma_{d}(D),\partial\Sigma_{d}(D))
.
Since
H_{1}(\Sigma_{d}(K))=0
, the inclusion induces an isomorphism
H_{2}(\Sigma_{d}(D))\to H_{2}(\Sigma_{d}(D),\partial\Sigma_{d}(D)).

Denote by
z\in H_{2}(\Sigma_{d}(D))
the unique element which is sent to
[\widetilde{D}]
under this isomorphism.
We also use
Q_{\Sigma_{d}(D)}
to denote the intersection form of
\Sigma_{d}(D)
and consider the hermitian form

\displaystyle\lambda_{\Sigma_{d}(D)}\colon H_{2}(\Sigma_{d}(D))\times H_{2}(\Sigma_{d}(D))

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}_{d}]

\displaystyle(x,y)\mapsto

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}}Q_{\Sigma_{d}(D)}(x,gy)g^{-1}.

The following is a pointed hermitian form over
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}_{d}]
:

(P,\lambda,z)=(H_{2}(\Sigma_{d}(D)),\lambda_{\Sigma_{d}(D)},z).

.

\mathcal{N}

.

One verifies that if
P
is projective and
\lambda
is nonsingular, then
\lambda_{0}
is also nonsingular.

Theorem 3.5

(The Splitting Theorem
[
LW97
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

Let
(P,\lambda,z)
be a nonsingular pointed hermitian form over
\Lambda
.
Suppose the following conditions hold:

(1)

z\in P^{\mathbb{Z}_{d}}
;

(2)

P
is a projective module of rank
b+2k
over
\Lambda
with
b,k\geq 1
;

(3)

the
j
-signatures of
\lambda
satisfy
b\geq\max_{0\leq j<d}|\sigma_{j}(\lambda)|
;

(4)

there is a nonsingular pointed hermitian
\mathbb{Z}
-module
(L,\lambda,x)
and a splitting

\beta_{0}\colon(P_{0},\lambda_{0},z_{0})\cong(L,\lambda,x)\oplus H(\mathbb{Z})^{k};

(5)

the form
\lambda
is weakly even on the kernel of

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{P}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{P_{0}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{L\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{2k}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{proj}_{1}}

Then there is a pointed hermitian form
(P^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime},z^{\prime})
over
\Lambda
and isometries of pointed hermitian forms

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle\colon(P,\lambda,z)\cong(P^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime},z^{\prime})\oplus H(\Lambda)^{k},

3.3.
The fixed-point condition

Let
N
,
K
,
x
be as described at the start of the section, and let
D\subset N\#^{k}S^{2}\times S^{2}
be a disc representing
x\oplus 0
with boundary
K
.
Recall from Example
3.2
that this determines a pointed hermitian form

(P,\lambda,z)=(H_{2}(\Sigma_{d}(D)),\lambda_{\Sigma_{d}(D)},z)

over
\Lambda
, where
z
corresponds to
[\widetilde{D}]
, the class of the branch set, under the inclusion induced isomorphism
H_{2}(\Sigma_{d}(D))\cong H_{2}(\Sigma_{d}(D),\Sigma_{d}(K))
.
We now verify
z
is indeed in the fixed-point set of the
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
-action.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
N
be a simply-connected
4
-manifold with boundary
S^{3}
, let
x\in H_{2}(N,\partial N)
be a nonzero homology of divisibility
d
, and let
K\subset S^{3}
be a knot with
H_{1}(\Sigma_{d}(K))=0
.
The class
z
lies in
H_{2}(\Sigma_{d}(D))^{\mathbb{Z}_{d}}
.

Proof.

Since
\widetilde{D}
is the branch set of
\Sigma_{d}(D)
, the element
[\widetilde{D}]
belongs to
H_{2}(\Sigma_{d}(D),\Sigma_{d}(K))^{\mathbb{Z}_{d}}
.
Since the inclusion induced isomorphism
i\colon H_{2}(\Sigma_{d}(D))\to H_{2}(\Sigma_{d}(D),\Sigma_{d}(K))
is an isomorphism of
\Lambda
-modules,
z=i_{*}^{-1}([\widetilde{D}])\in H_{2}(\Sigma_{d}(D))^{\mathbb{Z}_{d}}
, establishing the lemma.
∎

3.4.
The
j
-signature condition

Next we recast the signature condition of Theorem
3.5
, in the case where
P=H_{2}(\Sigma_{d}(D))
, in terms of Levine-Tristram signatures of the knot
K
.

Let
M
be a simply-connected
4
-manifold with boundary
S^{3}
.
Given a properly embedded surface
F\subset M
with
H_{1}(M\setminus F)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{d}
, the intersection form of
\Sigma_{d}(F)
restricts to a hermitian form on the
e^{2\pi ij/d}
-eigenspace of the
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
-action

\left\{x\in H_{2}(\Sigma_{d}(F);\mathbb{C})\mid tx=e^{\frac{2\pi ij}{d}}x\right\}.

We write
\sigma_{j}(\Sigma_{d}(F))
for the signature of this restricted form.
Also, we write
\sigma_{K}(\omega)
for the Levine-Tristram signature of a knot
K
at
\omega\in S^{1}
.
The next lemma, which stems from the work of Rohlin
[
Roh71
]
, recasts
\sigma_{j}(\Sigma_{d}(D))
in terms of
\sigma_{K}(e^{2\pi ij/d})
and the algebraic topology of
M
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
M
be a simply-connected
4
-manifold with boundary
S^{3}
, let
K\subset\partial M\cong S^{3}
be a knot, and let
0\leq j<d
. If
\Sigma_{d}(F)\to M
is a
d
-fold cover with branch set a surface
F\subset M
with boundary
K
, then

\sigma_{j}(\Sigma_{d}(F))=\sigma(M)-\frac{2j(d-j)}{d^{2}}Q_{M}([F],[F])+\sigma_{K}(e^{2\pi ij/d}).

Proof.

Consider the closed
4
-manifold
X:=M\cup D^{4}
.
Let
G\subset D^{4}
be a properly embedded surface with boundary
K
. A Mayer-Vietoris argument shows the surface
S:=F\cup G\subset X
has
H_{1}(X\setminus S)\cong\penalty\ \mathbb{Z}_{d}
.
The abelianisation
\pi_{1}(X\setminus S)\to H_{1}(X\setminus S)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{d}
extends
\pi_{1}(M\setminus F)\to\mathbb{Z}_{d}
.
Denote the
d
-fold branched cover of
X
over
S
by
\Sigma_{d}(S)
, noting this extends the branched cover of
M
over
F
.
Since
X
is closed, a result of Rohlin
[
Roh71
]
(see also
[
CG78
, Lemma 2.1]
), together with the definition of
S
as
F\cup G
, implies that

\sigma_{j}(\Sigma_{d}(S))=\sigma(X)-\frac{2j(d-j)}{d^{2}}Q_{X}([S],[S])=\sigma(M)-\frac{2j(d-j)}{d^{2}}Q_{M}([F],[F]).

On the other hand, combining Novikov additivity (for
\Sigma_{d}(S)=\Sigma_{d}(F)\cup-\Sigma_{d}(G)
) with a four-dimensional interpretation of the Levine-Tristram signature
[
Vir73
]
(see also
[
Con21
, Theorem 3.1]
) yields

\sigma_{j}(\Sigma_{d}(S))=\sigma_{j}(\Sigma_{d}(F))-\sigma_{j}(\Sigma_{d}(G))=\sigma_{j}(\Sigma_{d}(F))-\sigma_{K}(e^{2\pi ij/d}).

The lemma now follows by combining these two results.
∎

Finally, we note that the signature condition in Theorem
1.1
is necessary.

Proposition 3.9
.

For a simply-connected
4
-manifold
M
with boundary
S^{3}
, a
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
-surface
F\subset M
with boundary a knot
K\subset S^{3}
, and
0\leq j<d
, we have

b_{1}(F)+b_{2}(M)\geq\Bigg|\sigma(M)-\frac{2j(d-j)}{d^{2}}Q_{M}([F],[F])+\sigma_{K}(e^{2\pi ij/d})\Bigg|=\Bigg|\sigma_{j}(\Sigma_{d}(F))\Bigg|.

Proof.

The first inequality is due (in much greater generality) to Gilmer
[
Gil81
, Remark following Corollary 4.2]
.
Gilmer’s result is stated for
d
a prime power dividing
x
but without the assumption that
F
is simple.
A little work shows that the prime power assumption can be dropped if
F
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
-surface (so, in particular,
d
is the divisibility of
x
).
The equality is Lemma
3.8
.

For the reader’s convenience, we outline why, when
F
is simple, Gilmer’s result does not require the prime power hypothesis.
For
j=0
, the result already follows from Gilmer’s work.
Now assume that
j>0
, set
\omega:=e^{2\pi ij/d}
, and for
X\in\{X_{F}^{d},\Sigma_{d}(F)\}

H_{2}(X;\mathbb{C})_{\omega}:=\left\{x\in H_{2}(X;\mathbb{C})\mid tx=\omega x\right\}.

Since
|\sigma_{j}(\Sigma_{d}(F))|\leq\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H_{2}(\Sigma_{d}(F))_{\omega}
, it suffices to prove that
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H_{2}(\Sigma_{d}(F))_{\omega}\leq b_{1}(F)+b_{2}(M)
.
In fact we will prove equality.
We use
\mathbb{C}^{\omega}
to denote both the
\Lambda_{\mathbb{C}}:=\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}_{d}]
-module and the
\Lambda
-module obtained by linearly extending
tx:=\omega x
for
x\in\mathbb{C}
.
For
X\in\{X_{F}^{d},\Sigma_{d}(F)\}
, we have

(5)

H_{2}(X)_{\omega}\cong H_{2}(X;\mathbb{C})\otimes_{\Lambda_{\mathbb{C}}}\mathbb{C}^{\omega}\cong H_{2}(X)\otimes_{\Lambda}\mathbb{C}^{\omega},

where the first equality follows e.g. from
[
CC18
, Proposition 3.3]
and the second from the associativity of the tensor product.

Since
F
is simple, the spaces
X_{F}^{d}
and
\Sigma_{d}(F)
are simply-connected.
Recalling that
\Lambda_{0}=\Lambda/I
denotes
\mathbb{Z}
with the
\Lambda
-module structure induced by augmentation, and since
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
acts trivially on the homology of
\overline{\nu}(\widetilde{F})\cong\widetilde{F}\times D^{2}
,
the Mayer-Vietoris exact sequence for
\Sigma_{d}(F)
yields

0\to\overbrace{H_{2}(\widetilde{F}\times S^{1})}^{\cong\Lambda_{0}^{2g}}\to H_{2}(X_{F}^{d})\to H_{2}(\Sigma_{d}(F))\xrightarrow{}\Lambda_{0}\to 0.

Applying
-\otimes_{\Lambda}\mathbb{C}^{\omega}
and using that
\Lambda_{0}\otimes_{\Lambda}\mathbb{C}^{\omega}=0
as well
\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}_{d}]}(\Lambda_{0},\mathbb{C}^{\omega})=0
, we deduce from (
5
) that the inclusion induces an isomorphism

H_{2}(X^{d}_{F})_{\omega}\xrightarrow{\cong}H_{2}(\Sigma_{2}(F))_{\omega}.

From now on, we use the isomorphism
H_{2}(X^{d}_{F})_{\omega}\cong H_{2}(X_{F};\mathbb{C}^{\omega})
with twisted homology; see e.g.
[
CN20
, Proof of Lemma 3.6]
.
It thus remains to show that
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H_{2}(X_{F};\mathbb{C}^{\omega})=b_{1}(F)+b_{2}(M)
.
First, a calculation, using that
F
is simple, shows that
H_{1}(X_{F};\mathbb{C}^{\omega})=0
.
Setting
b_{i}^{\omega}(X_{F}):=H_{i}(X_{F};\mathbb{C}^{\omega})
and
\chi^{\omega}(X_{F})=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}b_{i}^{\omega}(X_{F})
, the outcome now follows from an Euler characteristic calculation:

b_{2}^{\omega}(X_{F})=\chi^{\omega}(X_{F})=\chi(X_{F})=(\chi(M)-1)+2g=b_{2}(M)+b_{1}(F).

We have thus obtained
|\sigma_{j}(\Sigma_{d}(F))|\leq\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H_{2}(\Sigma_{d}(F))_{\omega}=b_{2}(X_{F})^{\omega}=b_{1}(F)+b_{2}(M)
, as required.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

Gilmer’s formula involves
-\sigma_{K}(e^{2\pi ij/d})
instead of
+\sigma_{K}(e^{2\pi ij/d})
.
We believe this is a minor error, as can be verified by recalling that the left handed trefoil bounds a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-disc in
\mathbb{C}P^{2}
; recall Example
1.7
and Remark
1.8
.
This was already noted implicitly in
[
Yas96
, Theorem 3.1]
, as well as in
[
MMP24
, Theorem 3.6]
and
[
MMRS24
, Lemma 2.1]
: each of these results cites Gilmer but with
+\sigma_{K}(e^{2\pi ij/d})
in place of
-\sigma_{K}(e^{2\pi ij/d})
.
The appearance of this sign can be traced back to the proof of Lemma
3.8
and, more specifically to the decomposition
\Sigma_{d}(S)=\Sigma_{d}(F)\cup-\Sigma_{d}(G)
.

3.5.
The sufficient conditions

In this section, we distill Lee-Wilczyński’s splitting theorem, to list sufficient conditions for lifting a hyperbolic summand from the
\mathbb{Z}
-form to the
\Lambda
-form, in our case of interest.
For completeness, we provide the reader with the details to finish
off the surgery argument (described in a slightly different
form by Lee-Wilczyński’s
[
LW90
, Proposition 4.1 and page 410]
in the spherical case)
for finding a simple slice disc in the destabilised manifold
N
, given a simple slice disc in the stabilised manifold
N_{k}
.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
N
be a simply-connected
4
-manifold with boundary
S^{3}
, let
x\in H_{2}(N,\partial N)
be a nonzero class of divisibility
d
, and let
K\subset S^{3}
with
H_{1}(\Sigma_{d}(K))=0
and

(6)

b_{2}(N)\geq\max_{0\leq j<d}\Bigg|\sigma(N)-\frac{2j(d-j)}{d^{2}}Q_{N}(x,x)+\sigma_{K}(e^{\frac{2\pi ij}{d}})\Bigg|.

Let
D\subset N\#^{k}S^{2}\times S^{2}
be a simple disc representing
x\oplus 0\in H_{2}(N,\partial N)\oplus H_{2}(\#^{k}S^{2}\times S^{2})
with boundary
K
.
Assume the following conditions hold:

(1)

Freeness condition: the
\Lambda
-module
H_{2}(\Sigma_{d}(D))
is free of rank
b_{2}(N)+2k.

(2)

Splitting the form over
\mathbb{Z}
: there is an isomorphism

\beta_{0}\colon(H_{2}(\Sigma_{d}(D))_{0},(\lambda_{\Sigma_{d}(D)})_{0},z_{0})\cong(H_{2}(N),Q_{N},i_{*}^{-1}(x))\oplus H(\mathbb{Z})^{k},

where
i_{*}\colon H_{2}(N)\to H_{2}(N,\partial N)
is the inclusion induced map.

(3)

The evenness condition: the form
\lambda_{\Sigma_{d}(F)}
is weakly even on the kernel of

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\eta\colon H_{2}(\Sigma_{d}(D))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H_{2}(\Sigma_{d}(D))_{0}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H_{2}(N)\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{2k}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{2}(N).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{proj}_{1}}

Then
x
is represented by a simple disc in
N
with boundary
K
.

Proof.

The strategy of proof is to perform surgery on
N_{k}
to destabilise back to
N
, and do so away from
D
, so that the stable slice disc is preserved in the destabilised manifold. To do this, we will use the splitting theorem (Theorem
3.5
) to obtain a hyperbolic summand
H(\Lambda)^{\oplus k}
for the pointed
\Lambda
-form of
\Sigma_{d}(D)
.
We will then show this summand lies in the image of
H_{2}(X_{D}^{d})\to H_{2}(\Sigma_{d}(D))
, where
X_{D}^{d}
denotes the
d
-fold (unbranched) cover of the disc exterior
X_{D}:=N_{k}\setminus\nu(D)
.
As the disc
D
is simple,
X_{D}^{d}
is the universal cover of
X_{D}
, so the Hurewicz map is an isomorphism
\pi_{2}(X_{D})\cong H_{2}(X_{D})
.
We will thus be able to represent generators for
H(\Lambda)^{\oplus k}
spherically in
X_{D}
, for the desired surgery on
N_{k}
, occuring away from
D
.

We begin by verifying that the splitting theorem (Theorem
3.5
) can be applied.
First, the assumption that
H_{1}(\Sigma_{d}(K))=0
ensures that the
\mathbb{Z}
-valued intersection form
Q_{\Sigma_{d}(D)}
is nonsingular and some algebra shows that
\lambda_{\Sigma_{d}(D)}
is therefore also nonsingular.
Next, Lemma
3.7
ensures that
z:=i_{*}^{-1}([\widetilde{D}])\in H_{2}(\Sigma_{d}(D))
is fixed by the
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
action, confirming the first hypothesis of the splitting theorem. Proposition
3.9
shows that (
6
) is a reformulation of the third assumption of the splitting theorem.
The remaining assumptions of the present theorem ensure that we can apply the splitting theorem.

The splitting theorem provides isometries
\alpha
and
\beta_{0}^{\prime}
that make the following diagram commute

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(H_{2}(\Sigma_{d}(D)),\lambda_{\Sigma_{d}(D)},z)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(\Lambda^{b_{2}(N)},\lambda^{\prime},z^{\prime})\oplus(H(\Lambda),0)^{\oplus k}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(\mathbb{Z}^{b_{2}(N)},\lambda_{0}^{\prime},z_{0}^{\prime})\oplus(H(\mathbb{Z}),0)^{\oplus k}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(H_{2}(\Sigma_{d}(D))_{0},(\lambda_{\Sigma_{d}(D)})_{0},z_{0})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(H_{2}(N),Q_{N},i_{*}^{-1}(x))\oplus(H(\mathbb{Z}),0)^{\oplus k}.}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{0}}

4.1.
Modules over
\Lambda

We record some facts concerning projective and stably free modules over group rings of finite cyclic groups.
In what follows, modules are understood to be finitely generated.

Recall that free modules are stably free and stably free modules are projective.
The converses are in general false, but over

Theorem 4.2

(1)

Stably free

-modules are free.

(2)

A projective

-module

Proof.

The first assertion is due to Jacobinski
[

]
; we also refer to Johnson
[

]

The second statement is implicit in
[

We begin by detailing Lee and Wilczyński’s argument that

[

The Mayer-Vietoris sequence associated to the Rim square in (

4.3.
The module

This section proves that

First however, we show that

The argument originated from discussions with Daniel Kasprowski and relies on the following observation.

Lemma 4.5

If a smooth
4
-manifold

has nonempty boundary and

is surjective, then

has the homotopy type of a

The manifold

can be obtained from

by attaching

-,

-,

-, and

Since the inclusion induced map
\pi_{1}(\partial X)\to\pi_{1}(X)
is surjective, one can trade

-handles.
Turning the handle decomposition upside down, it follows that
X
admits a handle decomposition with only

-,
1
-, and
2
-handles so the conclusion follows.
∎

Next, as in
[
LW90
, page 398]
, we analyze

Lemma 4.6
.

The
\Lambda
-module

is stably isomorphic to the augmentation ideal
I
.

Proof.

As

has nonempty boundary, it has the homotopy type of a

-complex see e.g.
[

, Theorem 3.16]

Consider the singular

As explained in

We claim that

is stably isomorphic to the augmentation ideal

.
To see this, write

for the norm element, and consider the free

\displaystyle 0\to\ker(d_{2})\to C_{2}\xrightarrow{d_{2}}C_{1}\xrightarrow{d_{1}}C_{0}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{proj}}\mathbb{Z}\to 0

In order to determine

Lemma 4.9

The branched covering projection

Notation 6.1

When

In what follows, we say that an element

We begin with a criterion ensuring that

Lemma 6.2

Given

By definition of

∎

For the second lemma, recall that

denotes the form induced by

Using that

is an isometry (Proposition

) and Proposition

The goal of this section is to prove an equality that is implicit in

As we will see, this result (namely Proposition

However, since the literature on transfers is difficult to navigate, we instead decided to do things by hand.

Notation A.1

Proof.

We claim the lemma reduces to the closed case.

As

Appendix B
A relative version of Bredon-Edmonds’ result

In this section, we state and prove a version of Edmonds’ result
[
Edm89
, Proposition 5.1]
. Edmonds’ argument is made in the absolute case and we need a version in the case of manifolds with boundary and properly embedded surfaces.

The Edmonds result crucially uses a result of Bredon
[
Bre72
, Theorem VII.7.4]
, which is also only stated in an absolute version. Bredon’s result has the following relative variant. The lengthy, but ultimately formal, exercise of adjusting Bredon’s proof to the relative case will not be included here, and is left to the reader.

Theorem B.1

(Bredon)
.

Let
T
be an involution on a paracompact space
X
and write
F
for the fixed point set. Let
A\subset X
be a subspace such that
T(A)\subset A
.
Suppose
H^{i}(X,A;\mathbb{Z}_{2})=0
for
i>2n
and
T^{*}
is the identity on
H^{2n}(X,A;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
.
If
a\in H^{n}(X,A;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
is such that
a\cup T^{*}a\in H^{2n}(X,A;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
is non-zero, then
k^{*}(a)\in H^{n}(F,F\cap A;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
is non-zero, where
k\colon F\to X
denotes the inclusion.

We now recall Edmonds’ argument, with the minor adjustments for the relative case.

Proposition B.2

(Edmonds)
.

Let
T
be an orientation-preserving involution on a connected orientable
2n
-manifold
X
with non-empty boundary
A
. Suppose the fixed-point set is a proper submanifold with boundary
(F,\partial F)\subset(X,A)
. Suppose there exists
a\in H^{n}(X,A;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
such that
a\cup T^{*}a\in H^{2n}(X,A;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
is non-zero. Then

\langle c\cup T^{*}c,[X]\rangle\equiv\langle k^{*}(c),[F]\rangle\mod 2

for all
c\in H^{n}(X,A;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
, where
k\colon F\to X
denotes the inclusion of the fixed-point set of
T
.

Proof.

Define two functions

\begin{array}[]{rcll}\varphi\colon H^{n}(X,A;\mathbb{Z}_{2})&\to&\mathbb{Z}_{2};&\varphi(c)=\langle c\cup T^{*}c,[X]\rangle\\
\psi\colon H^{n}(X,A;\mathbb{Z}_{2})&\to&\mathbb{Z}_{2};&\psi(c)=\langle k^{*}(c),[F]\rangle.\end{array}

As
T
is orientation-preserving, it acts trivially on
H^{2n}(X,A;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
and we may apply Theorem
B.1
: if
\varphi(c)\neq 0
, then
\psi(c)\neq 0
.
Now, by hypothesis,
a\cup T^{*}a\neq 0
, and so by Poincaré duality
\varphi
is not the trivial map.
Hence
\psi
is not the trivial map.
The kernel of a surjective map to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
has index 2, hence both maps vanish on exactly half the elements of
H^{n}(X,A;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
.
But we already know that the maps agree on at least half the elements (the ones where
\varphi(c)=1
).
Hence they must agree on all the elements.
∎

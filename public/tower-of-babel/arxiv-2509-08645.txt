Quasi-optimal time-space discretizations for a class of nonlinear parabolic PDEs Thanks: The work of Nina Beranek was supported by the Office for Gender Equality Ulm University.
Nina Beranek, Robin Smeets, Rob Stevenson

Abstract
. We consider parabolic evolution equations with Lipschitz continuous and strongly monotone spatial operators. By introducing an additional variable, we construct an equivalent system where the operator is a Lipschitz continuous mapping from a Hilbert space Y × X Y\times X to its dual, with a Lipschitz continuous inverse. Resulting Galerkin discretizations can be solved with an inexact Uzawa type algorithm. Quasi-optimality of the Galerkin approximations is guaranteed under an inf-sup condition on the selected ‘test’ and ‘trial’ subspaces of Y Y and X X . To circumvent the restriction imposed by this inf-sup condition, an a posteriori condition for quasi-optimality is developed that is shown to be satisfied whenever the test space is sufficiently large.

1.
Introduction

In recent years one witnesses a growing interest in time-space variational formulations of parabolic initial value problems (IVPs) as the basis for monolitic numerical solvers.
They enable to compute numerical approximations that are quasi-best without regularity assumptions on the solution
[
And13
,
SW21a
]
, they lead to systems that have excellent scalability properties for parallel implementations
[
HLNS19
,
vVW21
,
LZ21
]
, and they naturally allow adaptive local mesh refinements simultaneously in time and space.
At the downside, monolitic methods require more memory than time-stepping methods. This can be mitigated by using (adaptive) sparse tensor product approximation allowed by the Cartesian product structure of the time-space cylinder
[
And13
,
SvVW22
]
. The disadvantage of the larger memory requirement vanishes for applications where the forward parabolic problem is coupled with an adjoint backward parabolic problem, as with problems of optimal control
[
LS21
,
BRU23
,
GS24
,
FK24
]
, or with goal-oriented error minimization
[
ELS24
]
.
Another interesting application of time-space methods is in reduced basis methods for parameter-dependent problems where, other than with time-stepping methods, they reduce
complexity both in space
and
time
[
GMU17
,
GS24
]
.

For
linear
parabolic PDEs, where with a Gelfand triple
V\hookrightarrow H\simeq H^{\prime}\hookrightarrow V^{\prime}
,
for each time
t
the linear elliptic spatial PDO (partial differential operator) is a mapping
V\rightarrow V^{\prime}
, the commonly used time-space variational formulation writes the pair of the parabolic PDO and the initial trace operator
as a boundedly invertible mapping
B_{e}\colon X\rightarrow Y^{\prime}\times H
. Here
X:=L_{2}((0,T);V)\cap H^{1}((0,T);V^{\prime})
and
Y:=L_{2}((0,T);V)
. Well-posedness of the IVP can also be demonstrated for other choices of the spaces
X
and
Y
,
[
SS17
,
SZ20
]
, or for its formulation as a first-order system
[
FK21
,
GS21
]
, both with interesting numerical approximation schemes resulting from that.

For a
nonlinear
parabolic PDE, where for each
t
the spatial PDO is a Lipschitz continuous and strongly monotone mapping
V\rightarrow V^{\prime}
,

[
Zei90
]
shows that
B_{e}
is an invertible mapping
X\rightarrow Y^{\prime}\times H
. This result is shown under even relaxed assumptions.
To the best of our knowledge, however, any sort of stability of
B_{e}^{-1}
, essential for the development of numerical approximations, has not been demonstrated.
Under the conditions of strong monotonicity and Lipschitz continuity, in the current work it is shown that
B_{e}^{-1}
is Lipschitz continuous.

In view of finding a formulation amenable to Galerkin discretizations, by introducing a secondary variable
\lambda
besides the solution
u
, we show that the IVP can be written as a
2\times 2
system of equations for
(\lambda,u)\in Y\times X
, where both the operator
Y\times X\rightarrow Y^{\prime}\times X^{\prime}

and its inverse are Lipschitz continuous. Assuming that the spatial PDO has a potential, this formulation can also be derived from the application of the Brézis-Ekeland-Nayroles variational principle
[
BE76
,
Nay76
]
.

For finite dimensional subspaces
Y^{\delta}\subset Y
and
X^{\delta}\subset X
, the latter equipped with a
Y^{\delta}
-dependent norm,
we show that the operator
Y^{\delta}\times X^{\delta}\rightarrow(Y^{\delta})^{\prime}\times(X^{\delta})^{\prime}
resulting from a Galerkin discretization
is Lipschitz continuous with a Lipschitz continuous inverse, with constants independent of
Y^{\delta}
and
X^{\delta}
.
For pairs
X^{\delta}\subset Y^{\delta}
that satisfy an inf-sup condition, uniformly over a family of such pairs, this shows that the second component of the Galerkin solution
(\lambda^{\delta},u^{\delta})\in Y^{\delta}\times X^{\delta}
is a quasi-best approximation from
X^{\delta}
to the solution of the IVP w.r.t. the norm on
X
.

This uniform inf-sup condition has been verified, with
\dim Y^{\delta}
of the order of
\dim X^{\delta}
, for
Y^{\delta}
and
X^{\delta}
tensor products of function spaces of the temporal or spatial variables, or more generally, when this tensor product constraint holds true in each time-slab from an arbitrary partition of the time-interval.

The Galerkin approximation
(\lambda^{\delta},u^{\delta})
is the solution of a system of nonlinear equations that need to be solved.
We develop an Uzawa type algorithm, known for the iterative solution of linear saddle-point system, where
the Lipschitz continuous and strongly monotone problems in the inner and outer loop are approximately solved by Zarantonello iterations, and demonstrate its R-linear convergence, uniformly in
Y^{\delta}
and
X^{\delta}
.

Finally, although the setting of tensor product approximation per time-slab covers finite element spaces w.r.t. non-uniform, and different partitions in each time slab, it does not allow partitions that result from ‘local time stepping’.
In order to include fully general (finite element) spaces
X^{\delta}\subset Y^{\delta}
, and so to enable local (adaptive) mesh refinements,
inspired by an idea from
[
CDW12
]
we derive an a posteriori condition for quasi-optimality of the
u^{\delta}
component of the Galerkin solution. This condition is shown to be satisfied whenever
Y^{\delta}
is sufficiently large, which given an
X^{\delta}
, suggests an (adaptive) enrichment of
Y^{\delta}
until the condition holds true. Validity of the condition moreover guarantees that an a posteriori computable estimator for
\|u-u^{\delta}\|_{X}
is efficient and reliable, which suggests an outer (adaptive) enrichment of
X^{\delta}
.

A computational verification of the a posteriori condition requires an a posteriori error estimator for the error in a Galerkin approximation of the solution of an equation with an
Lipschitz continuous and strongly monotone operator
Y\rightarrow Y^{\prime}
.
Such an estimator and numerical experiments with the double adaptivity loop will be presented in forthcoming work.

3.
Lipschitz continuous and strongly monotone operators

Since they will be used frequently, we recall some basic facts about Lipschitz continuous and strongly monotone operators.

For a Hilbert space
Z
(for convenience over
\mathbb{R}
), let
G\colon Z\rightarrow Z^{\prime}
be such that for some constants
L_{G},m_{G}>0
,
G
is
Lipschitz continuous
, i.e.,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|Gw-Gv\|_{Z^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq L_{G}\|w-v\|_{Z}\quad(w,v\in Z),

and
strongly monotone
, i.e.,

(3.2)

\displaystyle(Gw-Gv)(w-v)

\displaystyle\geq m_{G}\|w-v\|_{Z}^{2}\quad(w,v\in Z).

Setting
T:=R_{Z}^{-1}G
, where
R_{Z}\colon Z\rightarrow Z^{\prime}
is the
Riesz map
, from (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) it follows that for
T_{\theta}:=\mathrm{Id}-\theta T
,

\|T_{\theta}w-T_{\theta}v\|^{2}_{Z}\leq(1-2m_{G}\theta+L_{G}^{2}\theta^{2})\|w-v\|_{Z}^{2}\quad(w,v\in Z).

For
\theta\in(0,\frac{2m_{G}}{L_{G}^{2}})
, we have
1-2m_{G}\theta+L_{G}^{2}\theta^{2}<1
with minimal value
1-\frac{m_{G}^{2}}{L_{G}^{2}}
for
\theta=\theta^{*}:=\frac{m_{G}}{L_{G}^{2}}
.
For
w_{0}
arbitrary, the sequence
\{w_{i}\}
defined by
w_{i+1}:=T_{\theta^{*}}w_{i}
converges to the unique fixed point
w
of
T_{\theta^{*}}(w)=w
, being thus the
unique
solution of
Gw=0
. This
Picard iteration
is in this setting also known as the
Zarantonello iteration

[
Zar60
]
.

If
G
satisfies (
3.1
)-(
3.2
), then for any
f\in Z^{\prime}
so does
\widetilde{G}w:=Gw-f
, with
L_{\widetilde{G}}=L_{G}
,
m_{\widetilde{G}}=m_{G}
.
From the fact that thus
\widetilde{G}w=0
has a unique solution, it follows that
G^{-1}\colon Z^{\prime}\rightarrow Z
exists.

By (
3.2
), we have
m_{G}\|w-v\|_{Z}\leq\|Gw-Gv\|_{Z^{\prime}}
, and so

\|G^{-1}f-G^{-1}g\|_{Z}\leq\tfrac{1}{m_{G}}\|f-g\|_{Z^{\prime}}\quad(f,g\in Z^{\prime}),

i.e.,
G^{-1}
is Lipschitz continuous with
L_{G^{-1}}=\frac{1}{m_{G}}
.

From (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) we infer

\displaystyle\|Gw-Gv\|_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq L_{G}^{2}\|w-v\|_{Z}^{2}\leq\tfrac{L_{G}^{2}}{m_{G}}(Gw-Gv)(w-v),

and so,

\displaystyle(f-g)(G^{-1}f-G^{-1}g)

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{m_{G}}{L_{G}^{2}}\|f-g\|_{Z^{\prime}}^{2},\quad(f,g\in Z^{\prime}),

i.e.,
G^{-1}
is strongly monotone with
m_{G^{-1}}=\tfrac{m_{G}}{L_{G}^{2}}
.

Lemma 4.4

(

With the

by

(

\|\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\|_{X,\delta}^{2}=\|(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}+R_{Y})\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\|_{(Y^{\delta})^{\prime}}^{2}+\|\gamma_{0}\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\|_{H}^{2}\qquad\text{on }X\cap Y^{\delta}.

Proof.

For

(

Corollary 4.5
.

.

, with constant

Proof.

\displaystyle\Big(N^{\delta}\left[\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\xi\\

w\end{array}\right]\Big)\left[\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\sigma\\
v\end{array}\right]=(A_{Y}^{\delta}\xi)(\sigma)-(A_{Y}^{\delta}\mu)(\sigma)+(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}^{\delta}(z-w))(\sigma)

\displaystyle\hskip 60.00009pt+(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}^{\delta}v)(\xi-\mu)+(A_{X}^{\delta}w)(v)-(A_{X}^{\delta}z)(v)+\langle\gamma_{T}^{\delta}(w-z),\gamma_{T}^{\delta}v\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle\leq\Big[\big((1+L_{A})\|\xi-\mu\|_{Y^{\delta}}^{2}+\max(1,L_{A})\|z-w\|_{X^{\delta}}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 60.00009pt\times\big((1+L_{A})\|\sigma\|_{Y^{\delta}}^{2}+\max(1,L_{A})\|v\|_{X^{\delta}}^{2}\big)\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+L_{A})\big(\|z-w\|_{X^{\delta}}+\|\xi-\mu\|_{Y^{\delta}}\big)(\|v\|_{X^{\delta}}+\|\sigma\|_{Y^{\delta}}\big),

Lemma 4.8
.

Proof.

For

\displaystyle\big(S^{\delta}

\displaystyle w-S^{\delta}z\big)(v)=\big(A_{X}^{\delta}w-A_{X}^{\delta}z\big)(v)+\langle\gamma_{T}^{\delta}(w-z),\gamma_{T}^{\delta}v\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle\hskip 90.00014pt+(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}^{\delta}v)\big((A_{Y}^{\delta})^{-1}(f^{\delta}-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{\delta}_{t}z)-(A_{Y}^{\delta})^{-1}(f^{\delta}-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{\delta}_{t}w)\big)

and for

\displaystyle\big(S^{\delta}w-S^{\delta}z\big)(w-z)

Corollary 4.9
.

N^{\delta}\colon Y^{\delta}\times X^{\delta}\rightarrow(Y^{\delta})^{\prime}\times(X^{\delta})^{\prime}

Proof.

For

or, equivalently,

\displaystyle=\|S^{\delta}\widetilde{u}^{\delta}-\widetilde{S}^{\delta}\widetilde{u}^{\delta}\|_{(X^{\delta})^{\prime}}

Proposition 5.1

(Quasi-optimal Galerkin approximation)
.

Assume that

\gamma_{\Delta}=\gamma_{\Delta}\big((X^{\delta},Y^{\delta})_{\delta\in\Delta}\big):=\inf_{\delta\in\Delta}\inf_{\{z\in X^{\delta}\colon\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}z\neq 0\}}\frac{\|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}^{\delta}z\|_{(Y^{\delta})^{\prime}}}{\|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}E_{X}^{\delta}z\|_{Y^{\prime}}}>0.

\displaystyle\|\lambda-E_{Y}^{\delta}\lambda^{\delta}\|_{Y}+\|u

\displaystyle-E_{X}^{\delta}u^{\delta}\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq\big(1+\tfrac{L_{N^{-1}}L_{N}}{\gamma_{\Delta}^{2}}\big)\inf_{(\bar{\lambda}^{\delta},\bar{u}^{\delta})\in Y^{\delta}\times X^{\delta}}\|\lambda-E_{Y}^{\delta}\bar{\lambda}^{\delta}\|_{Y}+\|u-E_{X}^{\delta}\bar{u}^{\delta}\|_{X}.

(\ell,u_{0})\in Y^{\prime}\times H

(f,g)=(\ell,-(\ell+\gamma_{0}^{\prime}u_{0}))
(i.e.

Proof.

With the shorthand notations

. For arbitrary

\displaystyle\|\vec{\underline{u}}^{\delta}-\vec{u}^{\delta}\|_{Y^{\delta}\times X^{\delta}}

\displaystyle\geq L_{B_{e}^{-1}}^{-1}\|u-E_{X}^{\delta}\underline{u}^{\delta}\|_{X},

which shows that, in contrast to the left-hand side, for
u\not\in X^{\delta}
the right-hand side of (
6.4
) does
not
tend to zero when
Y^{\delta}\supset Y^{\delta_{i}}
and
i\rightarrow\infty
, and thus completes the proof.
∎

6.6
.

In the unlikely event that
u\in X^{\delta}
, (
6.4
) might never be satisfied. By taking
Y^{\delta}\supset Y^{\delta_{i}}
and letting
i\rightarrow\infty
, however,
u^{\delta}
converges to
\underline{u}^{\delta}
, which in this case equals
u
by (
6.6
).

The combination of Theorem
6.4
and Proposition
6.5
suggests an iterative enlargement of
Y^{\delta}
until (
6.4
) is satisfied.
The right-hand side of (
6.4
) is available, but since
\lambda_{\delta}
is not, in order to do so
one needs a computable
a posteriori error estimator
for the Galerkin error
\|\lambda_{\delta}-\lambda^{\delta}\|_{Y}
.
Preferably it is built from local error indicators, which allows an efficient
adaptive refinement
.
Although the operator
A
involved has the nice properties of being Lipschitz continuous and strongly monotone, because of the anisotropic nature of the space
Y=L_{2}(\mathcal{J};V)
the development of such an estimator is non-trivial. We postpone its construction for the quasi-linear parabolic PDEs from Example
2.2
to a forthcoming work.

A complementary advantage of guaranteeing quasi-optimality by enforcing (
6.4
) is
that it permits to compute an a
posteriori error estimator
for
\|u-E_{X}^{\delta}u^{\delta}\|_{X}
, which can be employed for an
adaptive refinement
of
X^{\delta}
in an
outer iteration
.

Proposition 6.7

(Efficiency and reliability)
.

Let
X^{\delta}\subset Y^{\delta}
, and let condition (
6.4
) be satisfied. Then, for
u\not\in X^{\delta}
, it holds that

\tfrac{m_{A}}{\sqrt{1+L_{A}^{2}+m_{A}^{2}}}\leq\frac{\|u-E_{X}^{\delta}u^{\delta}\|_{X}}{\sqrt{\|\lambda^{\delta}-u^{\delta}\|_{Y^{\delta}}^{2}+\|u_{0}-\gamma^{\delta}_{0}u^{\delta}\|_{H}^{2}}}\leq L_{B_{e}^{-1}}\sqrt{L_{A}^{2}\varrho^{2}+(L_{A}\varrho\tfrac{\sqrt{1+L_{A}^{2}}}{m_{A}}+1)^{2}}.

Proof.

Thanks to
X^{\delta}\subset Y^{\delta}
,

\displaystyle\|\lambda^{\delta}-u^{\delta}\|_{Y^{\delta}}

\displaystyle=\|(A_{Y}^{\delta})^{-1}((E_{Y}^{\delta})^{\prime}\ell-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}^{\delta}u^{\delta})-(A_{Y}^{\delta})^{-1}A_{Y}^{\delta}u^{\delta}\|_{Y^{\delta}}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{m_{A}}\|(E_{Y}^{\delta})^{\prime}\big(\ell-(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}+A)E_{X}^{\delta}u^{\delta}\big)\|_{(Y^{\delta})^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{m_{A}}\|(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}+A)u-(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}+A)E_{X}^{\delta}u^{\delta}\|_{Y^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{m_{A}}\big(\|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}(u-E_{X}^{\delta}u^{\delta})\|_{Y^{\prime}}+L_{A}\|u-E_{X}^{\delta}u^{\delta}\|_{Y}\big)\leq\sqrt{\tfrac{1+L_{A}^{2}}{m_{A}^{2}}}\,\|u-E_{X}^{\delta}u^{\delta}\|_{X},

which, in combination with
\|\gamma_{0}\|_{X\rightarrow H}\leq 1
(Corollary
4.5
), shows the lower bound.

Using Corollary
4.10
, the upper bound follows from

\displaystyle\|u-E_{X}^{\delta}u^{\delta}\|_{X}

\displaystyle=\|B_{e}^{-1}B_{e}u-B_{e}^{-1}B_{e}E_{X}^{\delta}u^{\delta}\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq L_{B_{e}^{-1}}\big(\|\ell-(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}+A)E_{X}^{\delta}u^{\delta}\|_{Y^{\prime}}+\|u_{0}-\gamma^{\delta}_{0}u^{\delta}\|_{H}\big)

\displaystyle=L_{B_{e}^{-1}}\big(\|A\lambda_{\delta}-AE_{X}^{\delta}u^{\delta}\|_{Y^{\prime}}+\|u_{0}-\gamma^{\delta}_{0}u^{\delta}\|_{H}\big)

\displaystyle\leq L_{B_{e}^{-1}}\big(L_{A}\|\lambda_{\delta}-E_{X}^{\delta}u^{\delta}\|_{Y}+\|u_{0}-\gamma^{\delta}_{0}u^{\delta}\|_{H}\big)

\displaystyle\leq L_{B_{e}^{-1}}\big(L_{A}\varrho\|\lambda^{\delta}-u^{\delta}\|_{Y^{\delta}}+(L_{A}\varrho\tfrac{\sqrt{1+L_{A}^{2}}}{m_{A}}+1)\|u_{0}-\gamma^{\delta}_{0}u^{\delta}\|_{H}\big).\qed

7.
Inexact Uzawa

We have seen that
u:=B_{e}^{-1}(\ell,u_{0})
solves
N\left[\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\lambda\\
u\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\ell\\
-(\ell+\gamma_{0}^{\prime}u_{0})\end{array}\right]
, where
\lambda=u
, and we have studied the accuracy of Galerkin discretizations of this system. In the current section we present and analyze an iterative method for solving such nonlinear Galerkin systems, i.e.,

N^{\delta}\left[\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\lambda^{\delta}\\
u^{\delta}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}f^{\delta}\\
g^{\delta}\end{array}\right],

where
(f^{\delta},g^{\delta})\in(Y^{\delta})^{\prime}\times(X^{\delta})^{\prime}
.
The method we are considering is a generalization of the well-known inexact Uzawa iteration for linear saddle point problems (e.g. see
[
BPV97
]
) to the nonlinear saddle point problem (
4.2
). The adjective ‘inexact’ refers to the fact that in these iterations the exact inverse of the ‘left upper block’ (in our case the nonlinear operator
A_{Y}^{\delta}
)
is avoided.

From Proposition
4.7
recall that (
4.2
) is equivalent to

S^{\delta}u^{\delta}=0,\quad A_{Y}^{\delta}\lambda^{\delta}=f^{\delta}-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}^{\delta}u^{\delta},

where for
z\in X^{\delta}
,
S^{\delta}z:=A_{X}^{\delta}z+(\gamma_{T}^{\delta})^{\prime}\gamma_{T}^{\delta}z+g^{\delta}-(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}^{\delta})^{\prime}(A_{Y}^{\delta})^{-1}(f^{\delta}-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}^{\delta}z)
. Further, recall that
A_{Y}^{\delta}\colon Y^{\delta}\rightarrow(Y^{\delta})^{\prime}
is Lipschitz continuous and strongly monotone with constants
L_{A}
and
m_{A}
, and as shown in Lemma
4.8
,

S^{\delta}\colon X^{\delta}\rightarrow(X^{\delta})^{\prime}
is Lipschitz continuous and strongly monotone with constants
L_{S}
and
m_{S}
.

Recalling our discussion of the Zaratonello iteration in Sect.
3
, these properties of
A_{Y}^{\delta}
and
S^{\delta}
imply that with
\theta^{*}_{S}:=\frac{m_{S}}{L_{S}^{2}}
,
\sigma_{S}:=\sqrt{1-\frac{m_{S}^{2}}{L_{S}^{2}}}
,
\theta^{*}_{A}:=\frac{m_{A}}{L_{A}^{2}}
,
\sigma_{A}:=\sqrt{1-\frac{m_{A}^{2}}{L_{A}^{2}}}
, and the Riesz maps
R_{X^{\delta}}\colon X^{\delta}\rightarrow(X^{\delta})^{\prime}
and
R_{Y^{\delta}}\colon Y^{\delta}\rightarrow(Y^{\delta})^{\prime}
, for
z\in X^{\delta}
it holds that

\displaystyle\|u^{\delta}-\big(z-\theta^{*}_{S}R_{X^{\delta}}^{-1}S^{\delta}z\big)\|_{X^{\delta}}

\displaystyle\leq\sigma_{S}\|u^{\delta}-z\|_{X^{\delta}},

and with
\mu^{\delta}:=(A_{Y}^{\delta})^{-1}(f^{\delta}-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}^{\delta}z)
for some
z\in X^{\delta}
, for
\xi\in Y^{\delta}
it holds that

\displaystyle\|\mu^{\delta}-\big(\xi-\theta^{*}_{A}R_{Y^{\delta}}^{-1}\big[A_{Y}^{\delta}\xi-(f^{\delta}-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}^{\delta}z)\big]\big)\|_{Y^{\delta}}

\displaystyle\leq\sigma_{A}\|\mu^{\delta}-\xi\|_{Y^{\delta}}.

A problem is that given an approximation
z
for
u^{\delta}
, the improved approximation being the exact Zaratonello iterand
z-\theta^{*}_{S}R_{X^{\delta}}^{-1}S^{\delta}z
is not computable because of the presence of the term
(A_{Y}^{\delta})^{-1}(f^{\delta}-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}^{\delta}z)
in the expression for
S^{\delta}z
. The idea of the inexact Uzawa iteration is to approximate this term, with a sufficient accuracy, by the application of a few iterations of some iterative method, in our case Zaratonello, for approximating
(A_{Y}^{\delta})^{-1}(f^{\delta}-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}^{\delta}z)
. The starting value of these iterations is the analogously previously computed approximation of
(A_{Y}^{\delta})^{-1}(f^{\delta}-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}^{\delta}\widehat{z})
, with

\widehat{z}
being the previous approximation for
u^{\delta}
.

The resulting algorithm reads as follows.

Algorithm 7.1

(inexact Uzawa)
.

.

Let
L\in\mathbb{N}
be a constant that will be specified in Theorem
7.2
.

Let
(\lambda^{(0)},u^{(0)})\in Y^{\delta}\times X^{\delta}
be given.

for

k=0,1,\ldots

do

\lambda_{0}^{(k+1)}:=\lambda^{(k)}

for

i=0,1,\ldots L-1

do

\lambda_{i+1}^{(k+1)}:=\lambda_{i}^{(k+1)}-\theta^{*}_{A}R_{Y^{\delta}}^{-1}\big[A_{Y}^{\delta}\lambda_{i}^{(k+1)}-(f^{\delta}-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}^{\delta}u^{(k)})\big]\big)

endfor

\lambda^{(k+1)}:=\lambda_{L}^{(k+1)}

u^{(k+1)}:=u^{(k)}-\theta_{S}^{*}R_{X^{\delta}}^{-1}\big[A_{X}^{\delta}u^{(k)}+(\gamma_{T}^{\delta})^{\prime}\gamma_{T}^{\delta}u^{(k)}+g^{\delta}-(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}^{\delta})^{\prime}\lambda^{(k+1)}\big]

endfor

Theorem 7.2

(Convergence and a priori error estimate)
.

For
\widehat{\sigma}_{S}\in(\sigma_{S},1)
, let
C_{3}:=\frac{1}{\widehat{\sigma}_{S}}\big(\frac{\widehat{\sigma}_{S}-\sigma_{S}}{\theta_{S}^{*}}+\frac{1}{m_{A}}\big)
, and let
L\in\mathbb{N}
be such that

\sigma_{A}^{L}(C_{3}+\frac{1}{m_{A}})\leq\frac{\widehat{\sigma}_{S}-\sigma_{S}}{\theta_{S}^{*}}
. Then with
C_{4}:=\max\{\frac{1}{C_{3}}\|\lambda^{\delta}-\lambda^{(0)}\|_{Y^{\delta}},\|u^{\delta}-u^{(0)}\|_{X^{\delta}}\}
, it holds that

(7.1)

\tfrac{1}{C_{3}}\|\lambda^{\delta}-\lambda^{(k)}\|_{Y^{\delta}}\leq\widehat{\sigma}_{S}^{k}C_{4},\quad\|u^{\delta}-u^{(k)}\|_{X^{\delta}}\leq\widehat{\sigma}_{S}^{k}C_{4}.

Proof.

The statement is valid for
k=0
, and let it be valid for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then using
\|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}^{\delta}\|_{X^{\delta}\rightarrow(Y^{\delta})^{\prime}}\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\|(A_{Y}^{\delta})^{-1}(f^{\delta}-

\displaystyle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}^{\delta}u^{(k)})-\lambda^{(k)}\|_{Y^{\delta}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\lambda^{\delta}-\lambda^{(k)}\|_{Y^{\delta}}+\|(A_{Y}^{\delta})^{-1}(f^{\delta}-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}^{\delta}u^{\delta})-(A_{Y}^{\delta})^{-1}(f^{\delta}-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}^{\delta}u^{(k)})\|_{Y^{\delta}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}\widehat{\sigma}_{S}^{k}C_{4}+\tfrac{1}{m_{A}}\widehat{\sigma}_{S}^{k}C_{4},

and so

\|(A_{Y}^{\delta})^{-1}(f^{\delta}-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}^{\delta}u^{(k)})-\lambda^{(k+1)}\|_{Y^{\delta}}\leq\sigma_{A}^{L}\big(C_{3}+\tfrac{1}{m_{A}}\big)\widehat{\sigma}_{S}^{k}C_{4}.

We infer that

\displaystyle\|\lambda^{\delta}-\lambda^{(k+1)}\|_{Y^{\delta}}\leq

\displaystyle\|(A_{Y}^{\delta})^{-1}(f^{\delta}-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}^{\delta}u^{(k)})-\lambda^{(k+1)}\|_{Y^{\delta}}

\displaystyle+\|(A_{Y}^{\delta})^{-1}(f^{\delta}-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}^{\delta}u^{\delta})-(A_{Y}^{\delta})^{-1}(f^{\delta}-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}^{\delta}u^{(k)})\|_{Y^{\delta}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\sigma_{A}^{L}(C_{3}+\tfrac{1}{m_{A}})+\tfrac{1}{m_{A}})\widehat{\sigma}_{S}^{k}C_{4}\leq C_{3}\widehat{\sigma}_{S}^{k+1}C_{4},

and

\displaystyle\|u^{\delta}-u^{(k+1)}\|_{X^{\delta}}\leq

\displaystyle\|u^{\delta}-\big(u^{(k)}-\theta_{S}^{*}R_{X^{\delta}}^{-1}S^{\delta}u^{(k)}\big)\|_{X^{\delta}}

\displaystyle+\theta_{S}^{*}\|(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}^{\delta})^{\prime}\big[(A_{Y}^{\delta})^{-1}(f^{\delta}-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}^{\delta}u^{(k)})-\lambda^{(k+1)}\big]\|_{(X^{\delta})^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sigma_{S}\|u^{\delta}-u^{(k)}\|_{X^{\delta}}+\theta_{S}^{*}\sigma_{A}^{L}(C_{3}+\tfrac{1}{m_{A}})\widehat{\sigma}_{S}^{k}C_{4}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big(\sigma_{S}+\theta_{S}^{*}\sigma_{A}^{L}(C_{3}+\tfrac{1}{m_{A}})\big)\widehat{\sigma}_{S}^{k}C_{4}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\widehat{\sigma}_{S}^{k+1}C_{4}.\qed

7.3
.

Inexact Uzawa type algorithms for classes of nonlinear systems can be found in the literature. We are, however, not aware of an analysis similar to Theorem
7.2
of its convergence exclusively in terms of the Lipschitz and monotonicity constants of
A_{Y}^{\delta}
and ‘generalized Schur complement’
S^{\delta}
, analogously to the analysis in
[
BPV97
]
for the linear case.

7.4
.

For quasi-linear parabolic PDEs it might be beneficial to replace the inner Zarantonello iteration by a faster converging iteration like the Kačanov iteration, or the (damped) Newton iteration (see
[
HW20
,
HW22
]
). With those iterations, however, the linear operator that has to be inverted changes per iteration, which, in any case with the application of direct linear solvers, strongly inflates the cost. Due to the structure of the operator
S^{\delta}
, it is less obvious to find an alternative for the outer Zarantonello iteration.

7.1.
A posteriori error estimation

The a priori upper bounds (
7.1
) for the error in
(\lambda^{(k)},u^{(k)})
can be expected to be increasingly pessimistic for
k\rightarrow\infty
.
We therefore provide an a posteriori computable error bound.

Proposition 7.5

(A posteriori error estimate)
.

Let
(\tilde{\lambda}^{\delta},\tilde{u}^{\delta})\in Y^{\delta}\times X^{\delta}
approximate the solution
(\lambda^{\delta},u^{\delta})
of (
4.2
). Then with

\left[\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}r_{Y}^{\delta}\\
r_{X}^{\delta}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}r_{Y}^{\delta}(\tilde{\lambda}^{\delta},\tilde{u}^{\delta})\\
r_{X}^{\delta}(\tilde{\lambda}^{\delta},\tilde{u}^{\delta})\end{array}\right]:=\left[\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}f^{\delta}\\
g^{\delta}\end{array}\right]-N^{\delta}\left[\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\tilde{\lambda}^{\delta}\\
\tilde{u}^{\delta}\end{array}\right],

for
(\tilde{\lambda}^{\delta},\tilde{u}^{\delta})\neq(\lambda^{\delta},u^{\delta})
it holds that

L_{N}^{-1}\leq\frac{\|\lambda^{\delta}-\tilde{\lambda}^{\delta}\|_{Y^{\delta}}+\|u^{\delta}-\tilde{u}^{\delta}\|_{X^{\delta}}}{\sqrt{r_{Y}^{\delta}(R_{Y^{\delta}}^{-1}r_{Y}^{\delta})}+\sqrt{r_{X}^{\delta}(R_{X^{\delta}}^{-1}r_{X}^{\delta})}}\leq L_{N^{-1}}.

Proof.

Applications of Proposition
4.6
and Corollary
4.9
show that

L_{N}^{-1}\leq\frac{\|\lambda^{\delta}-\tilde{\lambda}^{\delta}\|_{Y^{\delta}}+\|u^{\delta}-\tilde{u}^{\delta}\|_{X^{\delta}}}{\|r_{Y}^{\delta}\|_{(Y^{\delta})^{\prime}}+\|r_{X}^{\delta}\|_{(X^{\delta})^{\prime}}}\leq L_{N^{-1}},

whereas for
Z\in\{Y,X\}
,
\|r_{Z}^{\delta}\|_{(Z^{\delta})^{\prime}}^{2}=\|(R_{Z}^{\delta})^{-1}r_{Z}^{\delta}\|_{Z^{\delta}}^{2}=r_{Z}^{\delta}((R_{Z}^{\delta})^{-1}r_{Z}^{\delta})
.
∎

When we apply this a posteriori error estimate to
(\tilde{\lambda}^{\delta},\tilde{u}^{\delta})=(\lambda^{(k+1)},u^{(k)})
produced in the inexact Uzawa iteration, it requires computing

r_{Y}^{\delta}:=r_{Y}^{\delta}(\lambda^{(k+1)},u^{(k)}),\quad R_{Y^{\delta}}^{-1}(r_{Y}^{\delta}),\quad r_{X}^{\delta}:=r_{X}^{\delta}(\lambda^{(k+1)},u^{(k)}),\quad R_{X^{\delta}}^{-1}(r_{X}^{\delta}),

as well as
r_{Y}^{\delta}\big(R_{Y^{\delta}}^{-1}(r_{Y}^{\delta})\big)
and
r_{X}^{\delta}\big(R_{X^{\delta}}^{-1}(r_{X}^{\delta})\big)
. The computation of the latter two boils down to computing
the inner product between the vectors that represent the residual and the ‘preconditioned’ residual.

If the a posteriori error estimate shows an insufficient accuracy of
(\lambda^{(k+1)},u^{(k)})
, then
u^{(k+1)}
is obtained as
u^{(k)}-\theta^{*}R_{X^{\delta}}^{-1}(r_{X}^{\delta})
, with both terms already being available. For the computation of
\lambda_{1}^{(k+1)}
in the next inner iteration one saves the computation of
A_{Y}^{\delta}\lambda^{(k+1)}-f^{\delta}
, because it was already computed for
r_{Y}^{\delta}
. In other words, monitoring the error in
(\lambda^{(k+1)},u^{(k)})
while performing the inexact Uzawa iteration adds a small percentage to its cost, whereas it allows to stop as soon as the desired accuracy is attained.

7.2.
The application of the inverse Riesz maps

The execution of the inexact Uzawa algorithm as well as the computation of the a posteriori error estimate requires recurrent applications of
R_{Y^{\delta}}^{-1}
and
R_{X^{\delta}}^{-1}
, which we discuss now.
It holds that
R_{Y^{\delta}}=(E_{Y}^{\delta})^{\prime}R_{Y}E_{Y}^{\delta}
. So for
h^{\delta}\in(Y^{\delta})^{\prime}
,
v^{\delta}=R_{Y^{\delta}}^{-1}h^{\delta}
is the solution of the discretized (linear) coercive problem

\langle E_{Y}^{\delta}v^{\delta},E_{Y}^{\delta}z^{\delta}\rangle_{Y}=h^{\delta}(z^{\delta})\quad(z^{\delta}\in Y^{\delta}).

The definition of
\|\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\|_{X^{\delta}}
shows that

R_{X^{\delta}}=(E_{X}^{\delta})^{\prime}R_{Y}E_{X}^{\delta}+(\gamma_{T}^{\delta})^{\prime}\gamma_{T}^{\delta}+(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}^{\delta})^{\prime}((E_{Y}^{\delta})^{\prime}R_{Y}E_{Y}^{\delta})^{-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}^{\delta},

so that

(R_{X^{\delta}})^{-1}=-\left[\begin{array}[]{@{}cc@{}}0&\mathrm{Id}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{@{}cc@{}}(E_{Y}^{\delta})^{\prime}R_{Y}E_{Y}^{\delta}&\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}^{\delta}\\
(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}^{\delta})^{\prime}&-((E_{X}^{\delta})^{\prime}R_{Y}E_{X}^{\delta}+(\gamma^{\delta}_{T})^{\prime}\gamma^{\delta}_{T})\end{array}\right]^{-1}\left[\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}0\\
\mathrm{Id}\end{array}\right].

So for
h^{\delta}\in(X^{\delta})^{\prime}
,
v^{\delta}=R_{X^{\delta}}^{-1}h^{\delta}
is found as the second component of the solution
of a discretized (linear) parabolic problem in the form

\left[\begin{array}[]{@{}cc@{}}(E_{Y}^{\delta})^{\prime}R_{Y}E_{Y}^{\delta}&\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}^{\delta}\\
(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}^{\delta})^{\prime}&-((E_{X}^{\delta})^{\prime}R_{Y}E_{X}^{\delta}+(\gamma^{\delta}_{T})^{\prime}\gamma^{\delta}_{T})\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\varsigma^{\delta}\\
v^{\delta}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}0\\
-h^{\delta}\end{array}\right].

7.3.
The application of optimal preconditioners

For
Z
reading as either
Y
or
X
, let
\tilde{R}_{Z^{\delta}}\colon Z^{\delta}\rightarrow(Z^{\delta})^{\prime}
be such that
(\tilde{R}_{Z^{\delta}}\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}})(\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}})^{\frac{1}{2}}
is a scalar product on
Z^{\delta}\times Z^{\delta}
whose induced norm is equivalent, uniformly in
\delta\in\Delta
, to
\|\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\|_{Z^{\delta}}
, and such that
\tilde{R}_{Z^{\delta}}^{-1}
, known as a
preconditioner
, can be applied ‘efficiently’, in particular preferably at linear cost.
Then the application of the exact inverses of
R_{Y^{\delta}}
and
R_{X^{\delta}}
in the inexact Uzawa algorithm can be
replaced
by the application of these preconditioners
\tilde{R}_{Y^{\delta}}
and
\tilde{R}_{X^{\delta}}
, after making obvious adaptations to the constants
m_{A}
,
L_{A}
and
m_{S}
,
L_{S}
.

7.6
.

The above is an alternative for the
approximate
application of the inverse Riesz operator by means of an
inner iteration
that involves the repeated application of a preconditioner. The latter has been analyzed in the literature in the framework of numerically solving quasi-linear elliptic PDEs in, e.g.
[
HPSV21
]
.

The issue of the construction of optimal preconditioners
(\tilde{R}_{Z^{\delta}}^{-1})_{\delta\in\Delta}
depends on
(Z^{\delta})_{\delta\in\Delta}\subset Z
, but not on the operators
A^{\delta}_{Y}
or
S^{\delta}
, and so it is the same issue as studied earlier in the literature in the linear case..

For the tensor product setting from Example
5.2
, in Sect.
A
we recall a construction of optimal preconditioners based on ideas from
[
And16
]
(see also
[
NS19
]
,
[
SvVW22
, Sect. 5.6]
).

8.
Conclusion and outlook

For
V\hookrightarrow H\simeq H^{\prime}\hookrightarrow V^{\prime}
,
Y:=L_{2}(\mathcal{J};V)
,
X:=Y\cap H^{1}(\mathcal{J};V^{\prime})
,
A\colon Y\rightarrow Y^{\prime}
Lipschitz continuous and strongly monotone, and data
(\ell,u_{0})\in Y^{\prime}\times H
,

\left[\begin{array}[]{@{}cc@{}}A&\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}\\
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}^{\prime}&-(A+\gamma_{T}^{\prime}\gamma_{T})\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\lambda\\
u\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\ell\\
-(\ell+\gamma_{0}^{\prime}u_{0})\end{array}\right].

is an equivalent formulation of the IVP in time-space variational form
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}u+Au=\ell
,
\gamma_{0}u=u_{0}
, where
\lambda=u
.

For families
(Y^{\delta},X^{\delta})_{\delta\in\Delta}
of closed subspaces
Y^{\delta}\supset X^{\delta}
of
Y
and
X
that satisfy a uniform inf-sup condition, the
u^{\delta}
-component of the Galerkin approximation
(\lambda^{\delta},u^{\delta})
for
(\lambda,u)
is a quasi-best approximation of the solution
u
w.r.t.
\|\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\|_{X}
.
These Galerkin approximations can be numerically computed by an inexact Uzawa type algorithm.

The uniform inf-sup condition has been demonstrated for finite element spaces
Y^{\delta}
and
X^{\delta}
w.r.t. to partitions of the time-space cylinder that allow a subdivision into time-slabs. To circumvent this restriction, an a posteriori condition for quasi-optimality of
u^{\delta}
has been derived on the size of
Y^{\delta}
in relation to that of
X^{\delta}
.

The computational evaluation of this a posteriori condition requires an a posteriori estimator for the error in the Galerkin approximation from
Y^{\delta}
of the solution
\lambda_{\delta}\in Y
of
A\lambda_{\delta}=\ell-d_{t}u^{\delta}
.
The derivation of such an estimator for
A
resulting from a quasi-linear parabolic PDE, and the presentation of numerical results for the resulting double-adaptivity algorithm will be postponed to forthcoming work.

Appendix A
Optimal preconditioners in the tensor product setting

For a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
H=L_{2}(\Omega)
,
V=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, equipped with

\tfrac{|\!|\!|\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}|\!|\!|_{X^{\delta}}^{2}}{1+C_{\text{PF}}^{4}}\leq\|E_{X}^{\delta}\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\|_{Y}^{2}+\|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}E_{X}^{\delta}\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\|^{2}_{Y^{\prime}}+\|\gamma_{T}E_{X}^{\delta}\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\Omega)}=\|E_{X}^{\delta}\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\|^{2}_{X}\leq\tfrac{\|\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\|_{X^{\delta}}^{2}}{\gamma_{\Delta}^{2}}.

On the other hand, with

\displaystyle\geq\|E_{X}^{\delta}\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\|_{Y}^{2}+\|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}E_{X}^{\delta}\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{J})\otimes(X_{x}^{\delta})^{\prime}}^{2}\geq\gamma_{\Delta,x}^{2}(\|E_{X}^{\delta}\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\|_{Y}^{2}+\|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{t}E_{X}^{\delta}\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\|_{Y^{\prime}}^{2})

With

, and its adjoint
\mathcal{F}_{\delta}^{\prime}\colon\mathbb{R}^{\dim X^{\delta}}\rightarrow X^{\delta}\colon\vec{w}\mapsto\vec{w}\cdot\Phi^{\delta}

For a symmetric and positive definite matrix

\tilde{R}_{X^{\delta}}:=\mathcal{F}_{\delta}^{-1}{\bf\tilde{R}}_{X}^{\delta}(\mathcal{F}_{\delta}^{\prime})^{-1}

So finding an optimal preconditioner for

{\bf R}_{X}^{\delta}

Choosing
\Phi^{\delta}

{\bf R}_{X}^{\delta}={\bf M}_{t}^{\delta}\otimes{\bf A}_{x}^{\delta}+({\bf M}_{t}^{\delta}+{\bf A}^{\delta}_{t})\otimes{\bf M}_{x}^{\delta}({\bf A}_{x}^{\delta})^{-1}{\bf M}_{x}^{\delta},

\|\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\|_{(X_{x}^{\delta})^{\prime}}=\|({\bf A}_{x}^{\delta})^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\bf M}_{x}(\mathcal{F}_{\delta}^{\prime})^{-1}\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\|

[

\widecheck{\bf R}_{\alpha_{\psi}}^{\delta}\eqsim{\bf A}_{x}^{\delta}+\alpha_{\psi}{\bf M}_{x}^{\delta},

Noticing that

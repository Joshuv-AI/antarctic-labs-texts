Abstract
. Let V V be a finite-dimensional complex vector space. Assume that V V is a direct sum of subspaces each of which is equipped with a nondegenerate symmetric or skew-symmetric bilinear form. In this paper, we introduce a stratification of the Grassmannian Gr k ​ ( V ) \mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V) related to the action of the appropriate product of orthogonal and symplectic groups, and we study the topology of this stratification. The main results involve sheaves with coefficients in a field of characteristic other than 2 2 . We prove that there are “enough” parity sheaves, and that the hypercohomology of each parity sheaf also satisfies a parity-vanishing property. This situation arises in the following context: let x x be a nilpotent element in the Lie algebra of either G = Sp N ​ ( ℂ ) G=\mathrm{Sp}_{N}(\mathbb{C}) or G = SO N ​ ( ℂ ) G=\mathrm{SO}_{N}(\mathbb{C}) , and let V = ker ⁡ x ⊂ ℂ N V=\ker x\subset\mathbb{C}^{N} . Our stratification of Gr k ​ ( V ) \mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V) is preserved by the centralizer G x G^{x} , and we expect our results to have applications in Springer theory for classical groups.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview and statement of the main results

Parity sheaves, introduced by Juteau–Mautner–Williamson in
[
4
]
, have become a powerful tool in modular geometric representation theory. One especially important case is that of flag varieties and their variants (such as affine or partial flag varieties, including Grassmannians) equipped with the Schubert stratification. In this setting, parity sheaves give rise to the notion of the
p
-canonical basis
of the Hecke algebra, and ultimately to a number of major advances of the past decade, including
[
2
,
3
,
9
,
10
]
.

The goal of this paper is to study parity sheaves on Grassmannians equipped with various stratifications
other
than the Schubert stratification. These stratifications, defined in terms of the action of classical groups, are motivated by an anticipated future application to Springer theory. This application will be discussed at the end of the introduction.

Let us introduce the notation needed to formulate the main results. Let
B
be a finite-dimensional complex vector space equipped with a decomposition

B=B_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus B_{m}.

(On a first reading, the reader may wish to assume that
m=1
.) Suppose that each
B_{i}
is equipped with a nondegenerate bilinear form
\langle{-},{-}\rangle_{B_{i}}
that is either symmetric or skew-symmetric. Let
\mathrm{Iso}(B_{i})\subset\mathrm{GL}(B_{i})
be the subgroup that preserves
\langle{-},{-}\rangle_{B_{i}}
, and let
\mathrm{Iso}^{\circ}(B_{i})\subset\mathrm{Iso}(B_{i})
be its identity component. Thus

\mathrm{Iso}^{\circ}(B_{i})=\begin{cases}\mathrm{SO}(B_{i})&\text{if $\langle{-},{-}\rangle_{B_{i}}$ is symmetric,}\\
\mathrm{Sp}(B_{i})&\text{if $\langle{-},{-}\rangle_{B_{i}}$ is skew-symmetric.}\end{cases}

Let

I_{B}=\mathrm{Iso}^{\circ}(B_{1})\times\cdots\times\mathrm{Iso}^{\circ}(B_{m})\subset\mathrm{GL}(B).

In Section
5
, we will introduce larger group
Q_{B}\subset\mathrm{GL}(B)
that has the form

Q_{B}=I_{B}\ltimes(\text{a normal connected unipotent group}).

(If
m=1
, the unipotent part is trivial, and we have
Q_{B}=I_{B}=\mathrm{Iso}^{\circ}(B_{1})
.)

Let
k
be an integer with
0\leq k\leq\dim B
, and consider the Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(B)
of
k
-dimensional subspaces of
V
. The groups
Q_{B}
and
I_{B}
act on
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(B)
. We can consider the equivariant derived category

D^{\mathrm{b}}_{Q_{B}}(\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(B),\Bbbk)

of sheaves of
\Bbbk
-modules, where
\Bbbk
is a field (more general rings will be allowed in the body of the paper).

The main results of the paper are summarized in items (
1
)–(
6
) below:

(1)

We classify the
Q_{B}
-orbits on
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(B)
.

(2)

We determine the
Q_{B}
-equivariant fundamental group of each
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(B)
-orbit.

It turns out that each such fundamental group is a product of copies of
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. As a consequence, if the characteristic of
\Bbbk
is not
2
, then the category of
Q_{B}
-equivariant
\Bbbk
-local systems on each orbit is semisimple.

(3)

For each
Q_{B}
-orbit
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(B)
and each irreducible
Q_{B}
-equivariant
\Bbbk
-local system
\mathcal{L}
on
\mathcal{O}
, we construct an indecomposable parity sheaf

\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})

that is supported on
\overline{\mathcal{O}}
and satisfies
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})|_{\mathcal{O}}\cong\mathcal{L}[\dim\mathcal{O}]
.

(4)

We prove that the hypercohomology of parity sheaves satisfies:

\mathsf{H}^{i}(\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathcal{B}),\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L}))=0\qquad\text{if $i\not\equiv\dim\mathcal{O}\pmod{2}$.}

For item (
3
), once we have constructed
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
, the general theory from
[
4
]
tells us that it is unique up to isomorphism. The construction requires some auxiliary results, including the following:

(5)

For each
Q_{B}
-orbit
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(B)
, we construct a resolution of singularities
\mu:X\to\overline{\mathcal{O}}
whose fibers have cohomology only in even degrees.

In the terminology of
[
4
]
, these are called “even resolutions.” These resolutions are already enough to prove the existence of parity sheaves
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\underline{\Bbbk})
associated to
constant
local systems. But when
\mathcal{O}
admits nontrivial local systems, more is needed.

(6)

For each
Q_{B}
-orbit
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(B)
, we construct a proper map
\widehat{\mu}:\widehat{X}\to\overline{\mathcal{O}}
with the following properties: (i)
\widehat{X}
is smooth; (ii) the fibers of
\widehat{\mu}
have cohomology only in even degrees; (iii)
\widehat{\mu}^{-1}(\mathcal{O})
is simply connected; (iv)
\widehat{\mu}:\widehat{\mu}^{-1}(\mathcal{O})\to\mathcal{O}
is a smooth morphism.

Parts (iii) and (iv) of this statement are the key to constructing
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
for nontrivial
\mathcal{L}
. One could ask whether part (iv) could be improved to say that
\widehat{\mu}:\widehat{\mu}^{-1}(\mathcal{O})\to\mathcal{O}
is étale (so that
\widehat{\mu}
is an alteration). We do not know whether such a statement is true.

1.2.
Motivation

The present paper is motivated by an anticipated application to
Mautner’s cleanness conjecture
. Before explaining this connection, we review some background on Springer theory.

In Lusztig’s work
[
7
]
on the generalized Springer correspondence (with
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-coefficients), one key result states that
cuspidal
simple perverse sheaves are
clean
, i.e., their stalks vanish outside of one orbit. The cleanness phenomenon plays a crucial role in the computation of stalks of perverse sheaves on the nilpotent cone
[
8
]
, and in the theory of character sheaves.

About ten years ago, Mautner conjectured that these cuspidal perverse sheaves remain clean when they are reduced modulo
p
, with some possible exceptions for very small
p
. Mautner’s conjecture has been verified in type
A
and the exceptional types, and for a few small-rank examples in types
B
,
C
, and
D
. In general, it is currently open for classical groups outside of type
A
.

Here is a sketch of the kind of computation that goes into checking Mautner’s conjecture by hand. Suppose
G=\mathrm{Sp}_{2N}
, and let
P
be a parabolic subgroup with Levi factor
L=\mathrm{GL}_{k}\times\mathrm{Sp}_{2N-2k}
. Suppose
\mathcal{F}
is a perverse sheaf on the nilpotent cone of
\mathrm{Sp}_{2N-2k}
, and identify the latter with a subset of the nilpotent cone of
L
. Then we can consider the parabolic induction
\ind_{L\subset P}^{G}\mathcal{F}
, a perverse sheaf on the nilpotent cone
\mathcal{N}_{G}
of
G
. We might wish to compute the stalk

(\ind_{L\subset P}^{G}\mathcal{F})|_{x}\qquad\text{at a point $x\in\mathcal{N}_{G}$.}

To do this, we need some additional input. Let
G^{x}\subset\mathrm{Sp}_{2N}
be the stabilizer of
x
under the adjoint action, and let
G^{x,\circ}
be its identity component. An explicit description of the reductive part of
G^{x}
or
G^{x,\circ}
can be found in the classic textbook
[
6
]
, and in many other sources as well: we have

G^{x,\circ}=I_{B}\ltimes(\text{a normal connected unipotent group})\qquad\text{where $B=\ker x$.}

Here, the decomposition
B=B_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus B_{m}
is obtained from the Jordan blocks of
m
, and one can show that

(\ind_{L\subset P}^{G}\mathcal{F})|_{x}\cong R\Gamma(\text{a certain $Q_{B}$-equivariant complex on $\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(B)$.})

The results of the present paper can give us substantial control over both sides of this equation, and we hope to use them to prove Mautner’s cleanness conjecture in a future paper.

1.3.
Organization of the paper

In Section
2
, we prove a few preparatory lemmas about the topology of “isotropic Grassmannians” with respect to possibly degenerate bilinear forms.

Sections
3
and
4
are devoted the
m=1
case. That is, we consider a vector space
V
equipped with a nondegenerate symmetric or skew-symmetric bilinear form, and we study the orbits of
\mathrm{SO}(V)
or
\mathrm{Sp}(V)
, respectively, on
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)
. In these sections, we classify orbits and equivariant local systems; we exhibit resolutions of singularities for orbit closures; and we exhibit “universal covering submersions” for non-simply-connected orbits.

In Section
5
, we lay the groundwork for the case of general
m
. We define the groups
I_{B}
and
Q_{B}
, and we classify orbits and local systems, completing items (
1
) and (
2
) from Section
1.1
.

In Section
6
, we exhibit a resolution of singularities for each orbit closure (item (
5
) above). In Section
7
, we prove item (
6
) from Section
1.1
. Finally, the main results on parity sheaves (items (
3
) and (
4
) above) are proved in Section
8
.

1.4.
General conventions

Throughout the paper, we treat Grassmannians and other complex algebraic varieties as equipped with the analytic topology, and we consider sheaves and (singular) cohomology with respect to this topology.

We will typically denote by
\Bbbk
the ring of coefficients for singular or equivariant cohomology, or for sheaves. In the early sections of the paper, most statements allow
\Bbbk
to be an arbitrary commutative ring; sometimes, we also require
2
to be invertible. In Section
8
, we will impose the following assumption

(1.1)

The ring
\Bbbk
is either a field of characteristic
{}\neq 2
, or a complete local prinicipal ideal domain in which
2
is invertible.

Regardless of the assumptions on
\Bbbk
, we will use the phrase

“
\mathsf{H}^{\bullet}(X,\Bbbk)
is even and free”

to mean

\mathsf{H}^{i}(X,\Bbbk)=\begin{cases}\text{a free $\Bbbk$-module of finite rank}&\text{if $i$ is even,}\\
0&\text{if $i$ is odd.}\end{cases}

A “
\Bbbk
-local system of finite type” means a locally constant sheaf of
\Bbbk
-modules whose stalks are finitely generated
\Bbbk
-modules. A
\Bbbk
-local system is said to be “locally free” if its stalks are free
\Bbbk
-modules.

2.
Preliminaries on isotropic Grassmannians

Let
X
be a variety. Recall that an
affine paving
of
X
is a decomposition

X=\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in I}S_{\alpha}

into finitely many locally closed subvarieties such that the following conditions hold:

(1)

The indexing set
I
can be equipped with a total order
I=(\alpha_{1}<\cdots<\alpha_{N})
such that for
1\leq i\leq N
, the subset

S_{\alpha_{1}}\cup S_{\alpha_{2}}\cup\cdots\cup S_{\alpha_{i}}

is closed.

(2)

Each
S_{\alpha}
is isomorphic to an affine space
\mathbb{A}^{k_{\alpha}}
.

If
X
admits an affine paving, then
\mathsf{H}_{\mathrm{c}}^{\bullet}(X,\Bbbk)
is even and free.

Let
V
be a vector space equipped with a (possibly degenerate) symmetric or skew-symmetric bilinear form
\langle{-},{-}\rangle
. For any subspace
H\subset V
, we set

\displaystyle H^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\{v\in V\mid\text{$\langle v,w\rangle=0$ for all $w\in H$}\}

\displaystyle=\{v\in V\mid\text{$\langle w,v\rangle=0$ for all $w\in H$}\}.

We will usually call this the
orthogonal complement
of
H
, although that is probably a misnomer when the form is degenerate. A subspace
H\subset V
is called
isotropic
if
H\subset H^{\perp}
. For an integer
k
with
0\leq k\leq\dim V
, the
isotropic Grassmannian
is the variety

\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)^{\mathrm{iso}}=\{H\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)\mid H\subset H^{\perp}\}.

The
radical
of
V
is

\rad V=V^{\perp}.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional complex vector space equipped with a (possibly degenerate) symmetric or skew-symmetric bilinear form
\langle{-},{-}\rangle
. Let
M_{1}\subset M_{2}\subset\cdots\subset M_{c}
be a sequence of isotropic subspaces of
V
. For
0\leq k\leq\dim V
, the variety
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)^{\mathrm{iso}}
admits an affine paving

\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)^{\mathrm{iso}}=\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in I}S_{\alpha}

such that for each piece
S_{\alpha}
and each
i
with
1\leq i\leq c
, the function

S_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{Z}\qquad\text{given by}\qquad H\mapsto\dim H\cap M_{i}

is constant.

This lemma is certainly well known in the cases where the bilinear form
\langle{-},{-}\rangle
is either identically zero, or else nondegenerate: in these cases, it follows from the Bruhat decomposition.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
\dim V
. If
\dim V=0
, then
k=0
, and
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)^{\mathrm{iso}}
is a singleton. The statement is obvious in this case.

Assume now that
\dim V>0
. If
\langle{-},{-}\rangle
is degenerate, choose a
1
-dimensional space
L\subset\rad V
. Otherwise, let
L
be any
1
-dimensional subspace of
M_{1}
. In both cases,
L
is automatically isotropic. Choose a complement
W
to
L
in
L^{\perp}
, so that
L^{\perp}=L\oplus W
. Let
p_{1}:L^{\perp}\to L
and
p_{2}:L^{\perp}\to W
be the projection maps. If
H\subset L^{\perp}
, then

\dim p_{2}(H)\cap p_{2}(M_{i})=\dim p_{2}^{-1}(p_{2}(H)\cap p_{2}(M_{i}))-1=\dim(H+L)\cap(M_{i}+L)-1\\
=\dim(H+L)+\dim(M_{i}+L)-\dim(H+M_{i}+L)-1.

Comparing this to
\dim H\cap M_{i}=\dim H+\dim M_{i}-\dim(H+M_{i})
, we find that

(2.1)

\dim H\cap M_{i}=\begin{cases}\dim p_{2}(H)\cap p_{2}(M_{i})+1&\text{if $L\subset H$ and $L\subset M_{i}$,}\\
\dim p_{2}(H)\cap p_{2}(M_{i})&\text{if $L\not\subset H$ and $L\subset M_{i}$,}\\
\dim p_{2}(H)\cap p_{2}(M_{i})&\text{if $L\subset H$ and $L\not\subset M_{i}$,}\\
\dim p_{2}(H)\cap p_{2}(M_{i})-1&\text{if $L\not\subset H$, $L\not\subset M_{i}$, and $L\subset H+M_{i}$,}\\
\dim p_{2}(H)\cap p_{2}(M_{i})&\text{if $L\not\subset H+M_{i}$.}\end{cases}

Now let

\displaystyle X_{1}

\displaystyle=\{H\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)^{\mathrm{iso}}\mid L\subset H\},

\displaystyle X_{2}

\displaystyle=\{H\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)^{\mathrm{iso}}\mid L\not\subset H,\ H\subset L^{\perp}\},

\displaystyle X_{3}

\displaystyle=\{H\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)^{\mathrm{iso}}\mid H\not\subset L^{\perp}\}.

It is enough to show that each of these locally closed subsets separately admits a suitable affine paving.

For
X_{1}
, observe that an isotropic subspace
H
that contains
L
is necessarily contained in
L^{\perp}
. The map
p_{2}
induces an isomorphism

X_{1}\cong\mathrm{Gr}_{k-1}(W)^{\mathrm{iso}}.

By induction,
\mathrm{Gr}_{k-1}(W)^{\mathrm{iso}}
admits an affine paving such that the function
F\mapsto\dim F\cap p_{2}(M_{i})
is constant on each stratum. We transfer this affine paving to
X_{1}
. Since
\dim p_{2}(H)\cap p_{2}(M_{i})
is constant on each stratum and
L
and
L
is fixed, so the condition in (
2.1
) is also constant. Therefore the function
H\mapsto\dim H\cap M_{i}
is constant on each stratum in
X_{1}
.

Next, we turn to
X_{2}
. Consider the variety

E=\{(F,f)\mid F\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(W)^{\mathrm{iso}},\ f\in\Hom(F,L)\}.

This is a vector bundle over
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(W)^{\mathrm{iso}}
. For
H\in X_{2}
, the map
p_{2}
induces an isomorphism
H\cong p_{2}(H)
. Let
f_{H}:p_{2}(H)\to L
be the composition

f_{H}:p_{2}(H)\xrightarrow[\sim]{\text{induced by $p_{2}$}}H\xrightarrow{p_{1}}L.

Then we have a map

\theta:X_{2}\to E\qquad\text{given by}\qquad H\mapsto(p_{2}(H),f_{H}).

In fact, this is an isomorphism: the inverse map sends
(F,f)
to
H=\{(f(v),v)\in L\oplus W\mid v\in F\}
. Thus,
X_{2}
is a vector bundle over
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(W)^{\mathrm{iso}}
. By induction,
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(W)^{\mathrm{iso}}
admits an affine paving such that
F\mapsto\dim F\cap p_{2}(M_{i})
is constant along strata. Taking preimages along
X_{2}\to\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(W)^{\mathrm{iso}}
, we obtain an affine paving of
X_{2}
. By (
2.1
), the function
H\mapsto\dim H\cap M_{i}
is constant on each stratum in
X_{2}
.

Finally, we consider
X_{3}
. If
X_{3}
is nonempty, then
L^{\perp}\neq V
, i.e.,
L
is not contained in the radical of
\langle{-},{-}\rangle
. Our set-up implies that this can happen only when
\langle{-},{-}\rangle
is nondegenerate. Assume this from now. We also have
L\subset M_{1}
, and since all the
M_{i}
are isotropic, we have

(2.2)

L\subset M_{1}\subset\cdots\subset M_{c}\subset L^{\perp}.

In addition, the restriction of
\langle{-},{-}\rangle
to
W
is nondegenerate, and
W^{\perp}
is
2
-dimensional and contains
L
. Choose an isotropic complement
R
to
L
in
W^{\perp}
, and then choose some nonzero vector
r_{0}\in R
.

Let

X_{2}^{\prime}=\{F\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k-1}(V)^{\mathrm{iso}}\mid L\not\subset F,\ F\subset L^{\perp}\}.

This variety is analogous to
X_{2}
, and the same reasoning shows that
X_{2}^{\prime}
has an affine paving such that
F\mapsto\dim F\cap M_{i}
is constant on strata. Let
u\in L^{\perp}/F
, we define a linear map
\langle u,{-}\rangle:F\to\mathbb{C}
. this map is well defined as
\langle u,v\rangle=0
for all
u,v\in F
, which is because
F\subset F^{\perp}
. The assignment
u\mapsto\langle u,-\rangle
is a linear map
L^{\perp}/F\to F^{*}
. Consider the element
\langle-r_{0},{-}\rangle\in F^{*}
. Its preimage in
L^{\perp}/F
, given concretely by

\{u\in L^{\perp}/F\mid\text{$\langle u,v\rangle=\langle-r_{0},v\rangle$ for all $v\in F$}\}

is an affine subspace of
L^{\perp}/F
. We deduce that the variety

E^{\prime}=\{(F,u)\mid\text{$F\in X_{2}^{\prime}$, $u\in L^{\perp}/F$, and $\langle u,v\rangle=\langle-r_{0},v\rangle$ for all $v\in F$}\}

is an affine bundle over
X_{2}^{\prime}
. The affine paving of
X_{2}^{\prime}
induces an affine paving of
E^{\prime}
.

For
H\in X_{3}
, we have
H\cap L^{\perp}\in X_{2}^{\prime}
. Moreover, the composition
H\to H/(H\cap L^{\perp})\cong(H+L^{\perp})/L^{\perp}
is surjective. As
H
is one dimensional and not contained in
L^{\perp}
, hence
H+L^{\perp}\cong V
. This implies
H/(H\cap L^{\perp})\cong V/L^{\perp}
. Now we claim
V/L^{\perp}\cong R
. The intersection
R\cap L^{\perp}
must be
0
. If not then
R\subset L^{\perp}
. But
L^{\perp}\cap W^{\perp}
is one dimensional and hence
L=L^{\perp}\cap W^{\perp}
. So
R
can not be contained in both
W^{\perp}
and
L^{\perp}
and we are done with our claim. Therefore
R\hookrightarrow V/L^{\perp}
and by dimension equality
V/L^{\perp}\cong R
. Finally we get a surjective map
H\to R
. so there is a vector
\tilde{r}_{H}\in H
that maps to
r_{0}
. We have
\tilde{r}_{H}-r_{0}\in L^{\perp}
. Since
H
is isotropic, for any
v\in H\cap L^{\perp}
, we have
\langle\tilde{r}_{H},v\rangle=0
, so

\langle\tilde{r}_{H}-r_{0},v\rangle=\langle-r_{0},v\rangle.

We can thus consider the map

\theta^{\prime}:X_{3}\to E^{\prime}\qquad\text{given by}\qquad\theta^{\prime}(H)=(H\cap L^{\perp},\tilde{r}_{H}-r_{0}+(H\cap L^{\perp})).

The remainder of the argument is slightly different depending on whether
\langle{-},{-}\rangle
is symmetric or skew-symmetric. In the skew-symmetric case,
\theta^{\prime}
is an isomorphism: the inverse map sends a pair
(F,u)\in E^{\prime}
to the space
H=F+\mathbb{C}(u+r_{0})
. Thus,
X_{3}
has an affine paving induced by that
X_{2}^{\prime}
. In view of (
2.2
), we have

\dim H\cap M_{i}=\dim(H\cap L^{\perp})\cap M_{i},

and thus the function
H\mapsto\dim H\cap M_{i}
is constant on strata.

It remains to consider the symmetric case. We begin by describing
E^{\prime}
another way. For
F\in X_{2}^{\prime}
, the projection maps
p_{1}:L^{\perp}\to L
and
p_{2}:L^{\perp}\to W
induce an isomorphism

L^{\perp}/F\cong L\oplus W/p_{2}(F).

For
u\in L^{\perp}/F
, write
u=u_{1}+u_{2}
with
u_{1}\in L
and
u_{2}\in W/p_{2}(F)
. Then
\langle u,v\rangle=\langle u_{2},v\rangle
for all
v\in F
, so we can identify

E^{\prime}=\left\{(F,u_{1},u_{2})\,\Big|\,\begin{array}[]{c}\text{$F\in X_{2}^{\prime}$, $u_{1}\in L$, $u_{2}\in W/p_{2}(F)$,}\\
\text{and $\langle u_{2},v\rangle=\langle-r_{0},v\rangle$ for all $v\in F$}\end{array}\right\}.

In this description, there is no condition on
u_{1}
. Consider the subset

E^{\prime\prime}=\{(F,u_{1},u_{2})\in E^{\prime}\mid\langle u_{1},r_{0}\rangle=\textstyle-\frac{1}{2}\langle u_{2}+r_{0},u_{2}+r_{0}\rangle\}.

We need to check that the expression “
\langle u_{2}+r_{0},u_{2}+r_{0}\rangle
” makes sense, because
W/p_{2}(F)\oplus R
does
not
have an induced bilinear form. Since we are starting with a point in
E^{\prime}
, for
v\in F
, we have

\langle u_{2}+v+r_{0},u_{2}+v+r_{0}\rangle=\langle u_{2}+r_{0},u_{2}+r_{0}\rangle+2\langle u_{2}+r_{0},v\rangle+\langle v,v\rangle.

On the right-hand side, the middle term vanishes by the definition of
E^{\prime}
, and the last term vanishes because
F
is isotropic. We conclude that
E^{\prime\prime}
is well-defined. Its definition says simple that the
u_{1}
component is determined by
u_{2}
. Thus,
E^{\prime\prime}
is isomorphic to the variety

\{(F,u_{2})\in E^{\prime}\mid\text{$F\in X_{2}^{\prime}$, $u_{2}\in W/p_{2}(F)$, and $\langle u_{2},v\rangle=\langle-r_{0},v\rangle$ for all $v\in F$}\}.

In particular,
E^{\prime\prime}
is also an affine space bundle over
X_{2}^{\prime}
.

Note that

\langle u_{1}+u_{2}+r_{0},u_{1}+u_{2}+r_{0}\rangle=2\langle u_{1},r_{0}\rangle+\langle u_{2}+r_{0},u_{r}+r_{0}\rangle.

In view of this, the definition of
E^{\prime\prime}
can be rewritten as

E^{\prime\prime}=\{(F,u)\in E^{\prime}\mid\langle u+r_{0},u+r_{0}\rangle=0\}.

In the symmetric case, it is clear that the image of
\theta^{\prime}
is contained in
E^{\prime\prime}
, since
\tilde{r}_{H}
is an isotropic vector. In fact,
\theta^{\prime}
induces an isomorphism
X_{3}\cong E^{\prime\prime}
: the inverse map again sends
(F,u)\in E^{\prime}
to
H=F+\mathbb{C}(u+r_{0})
. We conclude that
X_{3}
has an affine paving with the desired properties.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

For
V
as in Lemma
2.1
and for any commutative ring
\Bbbk
, we have that
\mathsf{H}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)^{\mathrm{iso}},\Bbbk)
is even and free.

The next lemma will be a useful technical tool for inductively constructing affine pavings later in the paper.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional complex vector space equipped with a (possibly degenerate) symmetric or skew-symmetric bilinear form
\langle{-},{-}\rangle
. Let
M_{1}\subset M_{2}\subset\cdots\subset M_{c}
be a sequence of isotropic subspaces of
V
. Let
0\leq r\leq k\leq\dim V
, and consider the variety

Y(r,k)=\{(R,H)\in\mathrm{Gr}_{r}(M_{1}\cap\rad V)\times\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)^{\mathrm{iso}}\mid R\subset H\}.

Then
Y(r,k)
admits a partition into locally closed subvarieties

Y(r,k)=\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in I}\tilde{S}_{\alpha}

with the following two properties:

(1)

For each
\alpha
and each
i
with
1\leq i\leq c
, the function
\tilde{S}_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{Z}
given by
(R,H)\mapsto\dim H\cap M_{i}
is constant.

(2)

For each
\alpha
, the projection map
\tilde{S}_{\alpha}\to\mathrm{Gr}_{r}(M_{1}\cap\rad V)
is a locally trivial fibration whose fibers are affine spaces.

Proof.

We can define
\tilde{S}_{\alpha}=\{(R,H)\in\mathrm{Gr}_{r}(M_{1}\cap\rad V)\times S_{\alpha}\mid R\subset H\}
. From Lemma
2.1
, (1) is obvious.
For (2), the map,
\mathrm{Gr}_{r}(M_{1}\cap\rad V)\times S_{\alpha}\to\mathrm{Gr}_{r}(M_{1}\cap\rad V)
is a locally trivial fibration with fibre isomorphic to
S_{\alpha}
, which is affine. The space,
\tilde{S}_{\alpha}
is a closed subspace of

\mathrm{Gr}_{r}(M_{1}\cap\rad V)\times S_{\alpha}
. Therefore the proof follows.
∎

We conclude this section with the following lemma on the nondegenerate case.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional complex vector space equipped with a nondegenerate symmetric or skew-symmetric bilinear form
\langle{-},{-}\rangle
. Let
0\leq k\leq\frac{1}{2}\dim V
.

(1)

The variety
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)^{\mathrm{iso}}
is smooth, and each connected component is simply connected.

(2)

If
\langle{-},{-}\rangle
is symmetric and
\dim V=2k
, then
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)^{\mathrm{iso}}
has exactly two connected components. In all other cases,
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)^{\mathrm{iso}}
is connected.

The assertions in Lemma
2.4
are well known. Alternatively, they follow from Propositions
3.8
and
3.10
below: in the notation of those propositions, we have
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)^{\mathrm{iso}}=\mathcal{C}_{k,0}(V)
.

3.
Symplectic and orthogonal group orbits on Grassmannians

In this section, we work in the following setting: let
V
be a complex vector space of dimension
n<\infty
, and assume that it is equipped with a nondegenerate bilinear form

\langle{-},{-}\rangle:V\times V\to\mathbb{C},

assumed to be either symmetric or skew-symmetric. Then one may consider the isotropy group
\mathrm{Iso}(V)
and its identity component
\mathrm{Iso}^{\circ}(V)
.

The goal of this section is to classify the orbits of
\mathrm{Iso}^{\circ}(V)
(i.e., either
\mathrm{SO}(V)
or
\mathrm{Sp}(V)
) on the Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)
. This classification is certainly known, but we are unaware of a reference that includes enough details about stabilizers and equivariant cohomology for our needs. For this reason, we give complete proofs here.

3.1.
Stratification by rank

We begin with some preliminary lemmas that apply in both the symmetric and skew-symmetric cases.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
H\subset V
be a subspace of dimension
k
, and let
r=k-\dim H\cap H^{\perp}
. There exist subspaces
M_{1},M_{2},M_{3},M_{4}\subset V
with the following properties:

(3.1)

\displaystyle V=M_{1}\oplus M_{2}\oplus M_{3}\oplus M_{4},

\displaystyle\dim M_{1}=k-r,\quad\dim M_{2}=r,\quad\dim M_{3}=n-2k+r,\quad\dim M_{4}=k-r,

\displaystyle H\cap H^{\perp}=M_{1},\qquad H=M_{1}\oplus M_{2},\qquad H^{\perp}=M_{1}\oplus M_{3}.

Moreover, the restriction of
\langle{-},{-}\rangle
to the summands in (
3.1
) satisfies

(3.2)

\langle{-},{-}\rangle:M_{i}\times M_{j}\to\mathbb{C}\qquad\text{is}\qquad\\
\begin{cases}\text{perfect}&\text{if $\{i,j\}=\{1,4\}$, or if $i=j=2$, or if $i=j=3$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Set
M_{1}=H\cap H^{\perp}
, and choose
M_{2}
to be any complement to
M_{1}
in
H
. Choose
M_{3}
to be any complement to
M_{1}
in
H^{\perp}
. Finally, choose
M_{4}
to be any complement to
H+H^{\perp}=M_{1}\oplus M_{2}\oplus M_{3}
. It is straightforward to check that the restriction of
\langle{-},{-}\rangle
to these summands has the properties claimed in (
3.2
).
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
0\leq k\leq n
, and let

\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V)=\{H\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)\mid\dim H-\dim H\cap H^{\perp}=r\}.

If
\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V)
is nonempty, then it is a single
\mathrm{Iso}(V)
-orbit. Moreover, it is a single
\mathrm{Iso}^{\circ}(V)
-orbit if one of the following holds:

•

The form
\langle{-},{-}\rangle
is skew-symmetric.

•

The form
\langle{-},{-}\rangle
is symmetric, and either
r>0
or
n-2k+r>0
.

Proof.

Suppose
H
and
H^{\prime}
are two points in
\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V)
. Invoke Lemma
3.1
twice to obtain two decompositions

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=M_{1}\oplus M_{2}\oplus M_{3}\oplus M_{4},

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=M_{1}^{\prime}\oplus M_{2}^{\prime}\oplus M_{3}^{\prime}\oplus M_{4}^{\prime}.

Choose any linear isomorphism
g_{1}:M_{1}\overset{\sim}{\to}M^{\prime}_{1}
. Thanks to the pairing properties in (
3.2
), we have an adjoint map
g_{1}^{\dagger}:M_{4}^{\prime}\overset{\sim}{\to}M_{4}
. Next, since
M_{2}
and
M_{2}^{\prime}
are vector spaces of the same dimension, both equipped with nondegenerate bilinear forms of the same type (either symmetric or skew-symmetric), there exists a pairing-preserving isomorphism
g_{2}:M_{2}\overset{\sim}{\to}M_{2}^{\prime}
. We similarly get a pairing-preserving isomorphism
g_{3}:M_{3}\overset{\sim}{\to}M_{3}^{\prime}
. Let

g=g_{1}\oplus g_{2}\oplus g_{3}\oplus(g_{1}^{\dagger})^{-1}:V\to V.

This is a linear automorphism of
V
that preserves the bilinear form, i.e., an element of
\mathrm{Iso}(V)
. By construction,
g(H)=H^{\prime}
, so
H
and
H^{\prime}
are in the same
\mathrm{Iso}(V)
-orbit.

If
\langle{-},{-}\rangle
is skew-symmetric, then
\mathrm{Iso}(V)=\mathrm{Iso}^{\circ}(V)=\mathrm{Sp}(V)
, so
H
and
H^{\prime}
are in the same
\mathrm{Iso}^{\circ}(V)
-orbit.

Suppose now that
\langle{-},{-}\rangle
is symmetric, and that
r>0
. If
\det g=1
, then
g\in\mathrm{SO}(V)
, and we already have that
H
and
H^{\prime}
are in the same
\mathrm{Iso}^{\circ}(V)
-orbit. Now suppose that
\det g=-1
. Since
\dim M_{2}=r>0
, there exists an element
s\in\mathrm{O}(M_{2})\smallsetminus\mathrm{SO}(M_{2})
, i.e., an automorphism of
M_{2}
with determinant
-1
. Let

g^{\prime}=g\circ(\mathrm{id}_{M_{1}}\oplus s\oplus\mathrm{id}_{M_{3}}\oplus\mathrm{id}_{M_{4}}).

Then
g^{\prime}
also sends
H
to
H^{\prime}
, but it has determinant
1
, so
H
and
H^{\prime}
are in the same orbit for
\mathrm{Iso}^{\circ}(V)=\mathrm{SO}(V)
.

Finally, if
\langle{-},{-}\rangle
is symmetric and
n-2k+r>0
, similar reasoning applies using an element of
\mathrm{O}(M_{3})\smallsetminus\mathrm{SO}(M_{3})
.
∎

Suppose we have chosen a decomposition of
V
as in (
3.1
). Given a linear map
\theta:V\to V
, we can write it as a
4\times 4
matrix with respect to this decomposition. Let
\theta^{\dagger}:V\to V
be the adjoint map to
\theta
, i.e., the map such that
\langle\theta({-}),{-}\rangle=\langle{-},\theta^{\dagger}({-})\rangle
. Thanks to (
3.2
), the adjoint can be computed “entrywise,” as follows:

(3.3)

\text{if}\quad\theta=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&b&c&d\\
e&f&g&h\\
i&j&k&l\\
m&n&p&q\end{smallmatrix}\right]\qquad\text{then}\qquad\theta^{\dagger}=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}q^{\dagger}&h^{\dagger}&l^{\dagger}&d^{\dagger}\\
n^{\dagger}&f^{\dagger}&j^{\dagger}&b^{\dagger}\\
p^{\dagger}&g^{\dagger}&k^{\dagger}&c^{\dagger}\\
m^{\dagger}&e^{\dagger}&i^{\dagger}&a^{\dagger}\end{smallmatrix}\right].

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
H\subset V
be a subspace of dimension
k
, and choose a decomposition of
V
as in Lemma
3.1
. Then the stabilizer of
H
in
\mathrm{Iso}(V)
is given by

\Stab_{\mathrm{Iso}(V)}(H)\cong(\mathrm{GL}(M_{1})\times\mathrm{Iso}(M_{2})\times\mathrm{Iso}(M_{3}))\ltimes A,

where
A\subset\mathrm{GL}(V)
is the unipotent group given by

A=\left\{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&b&c&d\\
&1&&-b^{\dagger}\\
&&1&-c^{\dagger}\\
&&&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]\,\Bigg|\,d+d^{\dagger}=-bb^{\dagger}-cc^{\dagger}\right\}.

Proof.

Let
H=M_{1}\oplus M_{2}
. Then

H^{\perp}=M_{1}\oplus M_{3}\qquad\text{and}\qquad H\cap H^{\perp}=M_{1}.

An element of
\mathrm{Iso}(V)
that preserves
H
must also preserve
H^{\perp}
and
H\cap H^{\perp}
. If
\theta\in\mathrm{GL}(V)
preserves
H
,
H^{\perp}
, and
H\cap H^{\perp}
, then it has the form

\theta=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&b&c&d\\
&e&&f\\
&&g&h\\
&&&k\end{smallmatrix}\right]

where
a
,
e
,
g
, and
k
are invertible. Then
\theta^{\dagger}
and
\theta^{-1}
are given by

\left[\begin{smallmatrix}k^{\dagger}&f^{\dagger}&h^{\dagger}&d^{\dagger}\\
&e^{\dagger}&&b^{\dagger}\\
&&g^{\dagger}&c^{\dagger}\\
&&&a^{\dagger}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\quad\text{and}\quad\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a^{-1}&-a^{-1}be^{-1}&-a^{-1}cg^{-1}&a^{-1}be^{-1}fk^{-1}+a^{-1}cg^{-1}hk^{-1}-a^{-1}dk^{-1}\\
&e^{-1}&&-e^{-1}fk^{-1}\\
&&g^{-1}&-g^{-1}hk^{-1}\\
&&&k^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right]

respectively. For
\theta
to lie in
\mathrm{Iso}(V)
, we must have
\theta^{\dagger}=\theta^{-1}
. We deduce that

\Stab_{\mathrm{Iso}(V)}(H)=\left\{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&b&c&d\\
&e&&-eb^{\dagger}(a^{\dagger})^{-1}\\
&&g&-gc^{\dagger}(a^{\dagger})^{-1}\\
&&&(a^{\dagger})^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\,\Bigg|\,\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\text{$a\in\mathrm{GL}(M_{1})$, $e\in\mathrm{Iso}(M_{2})$, $g\in\mathrm{Iso}(M_{3})$,}\\
\text{and $d^{\dagger}=-a^{-1}bb^{\dagger}-a^{-1}cc^{\dagger}-a^{-1}da^{\dagger}$}\end{array}\right\}

This can be rewritten as

\left\{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&&&\\
&e&&\\
&&g&\\
&&&(a^{{\dagger}})^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&a^{-1}b&a^{-1}c&a^{-1}d\\
&1&&-b^{\dagger}(a^{\dagger})^{-1}\\
&&1&-c^{\dagger}(a^{\dagger})^{-1}\\
&&&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]\,\Bigg|\,\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\text{$a\in\mathrm{GL}(M_{1})$, $e\in\mathrm{Iso}(M_{2})$, $g\in\mathrm{Iso}(M_{3})$,}\\
\text{and $d^{\dagger}=-a^{-1}bb^{\dagger}-a^{-1}cc^{\dagger}-a^{-1}da^{\dagger}$}\end{array}\right\},

and after an appropriate change of coordinates, this matches the statement of the lemma.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Later in the paper, we will occasionally need to consider the group

\mathrm{GIso}^{\circ}(V)=\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times\mathrm{Iso}^{\circ}(V).

Let
\mathrm{GIso}^{\circ}(V)
act on
V
by having the
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
factor act by scaling. Of course, in the induced action on
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)
, the
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-factor acts trivially. It is easy to see that:

(1)

The
\mathrm{GIso}^{\circ}(V)
-orbits on
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)
coincide with the
\mathrm{Iso}^{\circ}(V)
-orbits.

(2)

For any
H\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)
, the natural map

\Stab_{\mathrm{Iso}^{\circ}(V)}(H)/\Stab_{\mathrm{Iso}^{\circ}(V)}(H)^{\circ}\to\Stab_{\mathrm{GIso}^{\circ}(V)}(H)/\Stab_{\mathrm{GIso}^{\circ}(V)}(H)^{\circ}

is a bijection. As a consequence, any
\mathrm{Iso}^{\circ}(V)
-equivariant local system on an
\mathrm{Iso}^{\circ}(V)
-orbit
\mathcal{C}
is also
\mathrm{GIso}^{\circ}(V)
-equivariant.

3.2.
Tangent spaces

In this subsection, we use Lemma
3.1
to carry out some computations in the tangent space to a point of
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional vector space, and let
H\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)
. Let
T_{H}\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)
be the tangent space to
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)
at
H
.

(1)

There is a canonical isomorphism

T_{H}\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)\cong\Hom(H,V/H).

(2)

There is a (noncanonical) open immersion

\Hom(H,V/H)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)

that sends
0\in\Hom(H,V/H)
to
H\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)
, and that induces the isomorphism of tangent spaces from part (
1
).

Proof.

(
1
) The group
\mathrm{GL}(V)
acts transitively on
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)
. Let
P
be the stabilizer of
H
in
\mathrm{GL}(V)
; this is a parabolic subgroup. Then
T_{H}\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)
is identified with the quotient of
\mathfrak{gl}(V)
by the subspace
\mathfrak{p}=\mathrm{Lie}(P)
. Choose a complement
H^{\prime}
to
H
in
V
. We of course have identifications

\mathfrak{gl}(V)=\End(V)=\Hom(H\oplus H^{\prime},V)=\Hom(H,V)\oplus\Hom(H^{\prime},V).

The subspace
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is the subspace of
\End(V)
consisting of maps that preserve
H
: thus,

\mathfrak{p}=\Hom(H,H)\oplus\Hom(H^{\prime},V).

The cokernel of
\mathfrak{p}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is thus identified with the cokernel of
\Hom(H,H)\hookrightarrow\Hom(H,V)
, which is
\Hom(H,V/H)
.

(
2
) As above, let
H^{\prime}
be a complement to
H
. Identifying
V=H\oplus H^{\prime}
, we have two projection maps
p:V\to H
and
p^{\prime}:V\to H^{\prime}
. Let

\displaystyle W

\displaystyle=\{M\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)\mid M\cap H^{\prime}=0\}

\displaystyle=\{M\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)\mid\text{$p|_{M}:M\to H$ is an isomorphism}\}.

This is an open subset of
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)
that contains
H
. For
M\in W
, let
\theta_{M}:H\to H^{\prime}
be the composition

H\xrightarrow[\sim]{(p|_{M})^{-1}}M\xrightarrow{p^{\prime}|_{M}}H^{\prime}.

It is easily checked that
M\mapsto\theta_{m}
defines an isomorphism
W\cong\Hom(H,H^{\prime})=\Hom(H,V/H)
.
∎

In the setting of Lemma
3.1
, the subspace
H\subset V
has a specified complement
M_{3}\oplus M_{4}
, so Lemma
3.5
lets us identify

(3.4)

T_{H}\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)\cong\Hom(H,M_{3}\oplus M_{4})=\Hom(M_{1}\oplus M_{2},M_{3}\oplus M_{4}).

We can therefore write elements of this tangent space as
2\times 2
matrices.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
H\subset V
be a subspace of dimension
k
, and choose a decomposition of
V
as in Lemma
3.1
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be the
\mathrm{Iso}(V)
-orbit of
H
in
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)
. The tangent space to
\mathcal{C}
at
H
is given by

T_{H}\mathcal{C}=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in\Hom(M_{1}\oplus M_{2},M_{3}\oplus M_{4})\,\Big|\,c^{\dagger}=-c\right\}

Proof.

From the proof of Lemma
3.5
, the map
\mathfrak{gl}(V)\to T_{H}\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)
is given by

(3.5)

\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&b&c&d\\
e&f&g&h\\
i&j&k&l\\
m&n&p&q\end{smallmatrix}\right]\mapsto\left[\begin{smallmatrix}i&j\\
m&n\end{smallmatrix}\right]

To compute
T_{H}\mathcal{C}
, we must compute the image of
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathrm{Iso}(V))\subset\mathfrak{gl}(V)
under this map. Since
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathrm{Iso}(V))=\{\theta\in\mathfrak{gl}(V)\mid\theta^{\dagger}=-\theta\}
, we see from (
3.3
) that

\mathrm{Lie}(\mathrm{Iso}(V))=\left\{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&b&c&d\\
-n^{\dagger}&f&-j^{\dagger}&-b^{\dagger}\\
i&j&k&-c^{\dagger}\\
m&n&-{\dagger}&-a^{\dagger}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\,\Bigg|\,\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\text{$a$, $b$, $c$, $i$, $j$, $n$ arbitrary, and}\\
\text{$d^{\dagger}=-d$, $f^{\dagger}=-f$, $k^{\dagger}=-k$, and $m^{\dagger}=-m$}\end{array}\right\}.

The image of this under (
3.5
) is the space described in the statement of the lemma.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
H\subset V
be a subspace of dimension
k
, and choose a decomposition of
V
as in Lemma
3.1
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be the
\mathrm{Iso}(V)
-orbit of
H
in
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)
. There is a transverse slice
S
to
\mathcal{C}
and
H
and an isomorphism

S\cong\{c\in\Hom(M_{1},M_{4})\mid c^{\dagger}=c\}

that identifies
H\in S
with
0\in\Hom(M_{1},M_{4})
.

Moreover, there is a cocharacter
\psi:\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}\to\mathrm{Iso}(V)
such that the induced
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-action on
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)
preserves
S
, and acts on it linearly with strictly positive weights.

Proof.

It is clear from (
3.4
) and Lemma
3.6
that
\{c\in\Hom(M_{1},M_{4})\mid c^{\dagger}=c\}
is a linear complement to
T_{H}\mathcal{C}
in
T_{H}\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)
. Via Lemma
3.5
(
2
), we can identify this subspace with a locally closed subvariety
S\subset\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)
that meets
\mathcal{C}
transversely.

Next, let
\psi:\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}\to\mathrm{GL}(V)
be the map given by

\psi(z)v=\begin{cases}z^{-1}v&\text{if $v\in M_{1}$,}\\
zv&\text{if $v\in M_{4}$,}\\
v&\text{if $v\in M_{2}$ or $v\in M_{3}$.}\end{cases}

This map actually takes values in
\mathrm{Iso}(V)\subset\mathrm{GL}(V)
, and even in
\Stab_{\mathrm{Iso}(V)}(H)
(by Lemma
3.3
). The induced action on
\Hom(M_{1},M_{4})
is given by
z\cdot a=z^{2}a
.
∎

3.3.
The symplectic case

We now specialize to the symplectic case. In this subsection, assume that

\langle{-},{-}\rangle:V\times V\to\mathbb{C}

is skew-symmetric.

Proposition 3.8
.

Suppose
V
is equipped with nondegenerate skew-symmetric bilinear form. For
0\leq k\leq n
, let
\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V)\subset\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)
be as in Lemma
3.2
.

(1)

The set
\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V)
is nonempty if and only if

r\equiv 0\pmod{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\max\{0,2k-n\}\leq r\leq k.

(2)

If
\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V)
is nonempty, it is a single
\mathrm{Sp}(V)
-orbit.

(3)

The stabilizer in
\mathrm{Sp}(V)
of any point in
\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V)
is connected.

(4)

For any commutative ring
\Bbbk
,
\mathsf{H}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{Sp}(V)}(\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V),\Bbbk)
is even and free.

Proof.

For brevity, we write
\mathcal{C}_{k,r}
instead of
\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V)
in this proof. There is an obvious
\mathrm{Sp}(V)
-equivariant isomorphism
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)\overset{\sim}{\to}\mathrm{Gr}_{n-k}(V)
given by
H\mapsto H^{\perp}
. This restricts to an isomorphism between
\mathcal{C}_{k,r}
and
\mathcal{C}_{n-k,n-2k+r}
. By replacing
k
by
n-k
if necessary, we may as well assume that
k\leq n/2
. We will prove parts (
1
)–(
4
) under this assumption.

(
1
) When
k\leq n/2
, this statement says that
\mathcal{C}_{k,r}
is nonempty if and only if
r
is even and
0\leq r\leq k
. If
\mathcal{C}_{k,r}
is nonempty, then for any
H\in\mathcal{C}_{k,r}
, the quotient
H/(H\cap H^{\perp})
inherits a nondegenerate symplectic form, so its dimension
r
must be even. It is obvious that
0\leq r\leq k
.

For the opposite implication, choose a basis
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
for
V
such that

\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $i<j$ and $i+j=n+1$,}\\
-1&\text{if $i>j$ and $i+j=n+1$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Given an even number
r
such that
0\leq r\leq k
, we see that

\spn\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k-r}\}\cup\{x_{\frac{\dim V-r}{2}+1},x_{\frac{\dim V-r}{2}+2},\ldots,x_{\frac{\dim V+r}{2}}\}

lies in
\mathcal{C}_{k,r}
, so
\mathcal{C}_{k,r}
is nonempty.

(
2
) This holds by Lemma
3.2
.

(
3
) By Lemma
3.3
, we have

\Stab_{\mathrm{Sp}(V)}(H)\cong(\mathrm{GL}(M_{1})\times\mathrm{Sp}(M_{2})\times\mathrm{Sp}(M_{3}))\ltimes(\text{a unipotent group}),

and this is connected.

(
4
) We have

\mathsf{H}^{i}_{\mathrm{Sp}(V)}(\mathcal{C}_{k,r},\Bbbk)\cong\mathsf{H}^{i}_{\Stab(H)}(\mathrm{pt},\Bbbk)\cong\mathsf{H}^{i}_{\mathrm{GL}(M_{1})\times\mathrm{Sp}(M_{2})\times\mathrm{Sp}(M_{3})}(\mathrm{pt},\Bbbk),

It is well known that the equivariant cohomology of a point vanishes in odd degrees as long as the torsion primes for the group are invertible in

[

. But
\mathrm{GL}(M_{1})\times\mathrm{Sp}(M_{2})\times\mathrm{Sp}(M_{3})

[

.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

Using the fact that

3.4.
The orthogonal case

In this subsection, assume that

\langle{-},{-}\rangle:V\times V\to\mathbb{C}

is symmetric. In contrast with the symplectic case, not all
\mathrm{SO}(V)
-orbits on the Grassmannian are simply connected: some admit
2
-to-
1
covering maps. For any commutative ring
\Bbbk
, let

denote the
\Bbbk[\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}]
-module whose underlying
\Bbbk
-module is just
\Bbbk

\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
acts by

in

and the trivial

-module are not isomorphic. In addition, if

is invertible in

(3.6)

\Bbbk[\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}]\cong\Bbbk\oplus\mathrm{sgn}.

Proposition 3.10
.

Suppose
V
is equipped with nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form. For

(1)

The set

\max\{0,2k-n\}\leq r\leq k

(2)

If

(3)

(4)

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
\mathrm{SO}(V)
-orbit on
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)

When

\mathcal{C}_{n/2,0^{\prime}}(V)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{C}_{n/2,0^{\prime\prime}}.

Proof.

Let us write
\mathcal{C}_{k,r}
for

. There is an obvious

\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)\overset{\sim}{\to}\mathrm{Gr}_{n-k}(V)

and
\mathcal{C}_{n-k,n-2k+r}

(
1

is nonempty.

(
2
) All but the case where

is covered by Lemma
3.2

(
3
) By Lemma

\Stab_{\mathrm{O}(V)}(H)\cong(\mathrm{GL}(M_{1})\times\mathrm{O}(M_{2})\times\mathrm{O}(M_{3}))\ltimes(\text{a unipotent group}).

Write an element of this group as a quadruple

\begin{array}[]{c@{\quad}c@{\quad}c}&\text{Number of components in}&\text{Number of components in}\\
\text{Condition}&\Stab_{\mathrm{O}(V)}(H)&\Stab_{\mathrm{SO}(V)}(H)\\

(
2
) for

and

. We must show that

consists of two
\mathrm{SO}(V)
-orbits in this case. We have already seen that it is a single
\mathrm{O}(V)

(

\mathsf{H}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{SO}(V)}(\mathcal{C},\Bbbk)\cong\mathsf{H}^{\bullet}_{(\mathrm{GL}(M_{1})\times\mathrm{O}(M_{2})\times\mathrm{O}(M_{3}))^{\circ}}(\mathrm{pt},\Bbbk).

By
[

∎

Remark 3.11
.

In contrast with the setting of Remark

, the fundamental group of

can be more complicated. One can show that

\pi_{1}(\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V),H)\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}&\text{if $n\geq 3$ and $\max\{0,2k-n\}<r$,}\\

4.
Some results on orbit closures

This section contains two results related to the geometry of orbit closures in the setting of Section
3.3
or
3.4
. The first exhibits a resolution of singularities. The second deals with non-simply-connected orbits in the orthogonal case.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
V
be an
n
-dimensional complex vector space equipped with a nondegenerate symmetric or skew-symmetric bilinear form. Let
0\leq k\leq n
, and let
r
be such that
\max\{0,2k-n\}\leq r\leq k
. In the skew-symmetric case, assume also that
r
is even. Let

\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{k,r}(V)=\{P\subset H\subset P^{\perp}\subset V\mid\text{$\dim P=k-r$ and $\dim H=k$}\}.

Let
\varpi:\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{k,r}(V)\to\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)
be the map given by

\varpi(P,H)=H.

Then the image of
\varpi
is
\overline{\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V)}
, and the map
\varpi:\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{k,r}(V)\to\overline{\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V)}
is a resolution of singularities.

Proof.

If
H
is in the image of
\varpi
, then it satisfies the condition
P\subset H\subset P^{\perp}
with
\dim P=k-r
. This implies
P\subset H^{\perp}\cap H
. Hence
k-r\leq\dim H\cap H^{\perp}
and
H\in\overline{\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V)}
.
The map
\varpi|_{\varpi^{-1}(\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V))}:\varpi^{-1}(\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V))\to\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V)
is clearly an isomorphism. Now consider the map,
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{k,r}\to\mathrm{Gr}_{k-r}(V)^{\mathrm{iso}}
by sending
(P,H)
to
P
. The fibers of this map are isomorphic to
\{H\mid P\subset H\subset P^{\perp}\}\cong\mathrm{Gr}_{r}(P^{\perp}/P)
, which is smooth. Also
k-r<k-r/2\leq n/2
, hence by Lemma
2.4
,
\mathrm{Gr}_{k-r}(V)^{\mathrm{iso}}
is smooth. Therefore
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{k,r}(V)
is smooth. We know the projection map
\mathrm{Gr}_{k-r}(V)^{\mathrm{iso}}\times\mathrm{Gr}_{r}(V)\to\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(V)
is proper and
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{k,r}(V)
is a closed subset of
\mathrm{Gr}_{k-r}(V)^{\mathrm{iso}}\times\mathrm{Gr}_{r}(V)
. Hence the map,
\varpi
is proper. Combining these facts, we deduce that
\varpi
is a resolution of singularities.
∎

For the remainder of this section, we work in the symmetric case, i.e., in the setting of Section
3.4
. Suppose
\max\{0,2k-n\}<r
, so that
\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V)
is not simply connected. We have already seen in the proof of Proposition
3.10
(
4
) how to construct a certain
2
-to-
1
covering map
p:E\to\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V)
.

It would have been useful later in the paper to have a “compactification” of
p
, i.e., a proper map
p^{\prime}:E^{\prime}\to\overline{\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V)}
with smooth domain
E^{\prime}
such that the restriction
p^{\prime}|_{(p^{\prime})^{-1}(\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V))}:(p^{\prime})^{-1}(\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V))\to\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V)
is identified with the covering map
p
. Unfortunately, we do not know how to construct such a compactification of the
2
-to-
1
covering
p
explicitly.

As a substitute, we will construct a compactification of a “
\pi_{1}
-injective fibration of index
2
,” by which we mean a locally trivial fibration
F\to\mathcal{C}_{k,r}
in which
F
is connected, and the induced map

\pi_{1}(F)\to\pi_{1}(\mathcal{C}_{k,r})

is injective. More precisely, we will construct a proper map

\widehat{\varpi}:\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{k,r}(V)\to\overline{\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V)}

whose domain is smooth, and whose restriction to the preimage of
\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V)
is a
\pi_{1}
-injective fibration.

We begin by defining
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{k,r}(V)
. The definition is different depending on whether
r
is even or odd. If
r
is even, we set

\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{k,r}(V)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{@{}l@{}}P\subset Q\subset H\\
{}\quad\subset P^{\perp}\subset V\end{array}\,\Big|\,\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\text{$\dim P=k-r$, $\dim Q=k-\frac{r}{2}$,}\\
\text{$\dim H=k$, and $Q\subset Q^{\perp}$}\end{array}\right\}\qquad\text{($r$ even).}

Before defining it in the odd case, we need some additional notation. Let

V^{+}=V\oplus\mathbb{C},

and equip it with the nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form
\langle{-},{-}\rangle_{+}
given by

\langle(v,z),(v^{\prime},z^{\prime})\rangle_{+}=\langle v,v^{\prime}\rangle+zz^{\prime}\qquad\text{for $v,v^{\prime}\in V$ and $z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}$.}

There is an obvious injective map

\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V)\to\mathcal{C}_{r+1,k+1}(V^{+})\qquad\text{given by}\qquad H\mapsto H^{+}=H\oplus\mathbb{C}.

We now set

\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{k,r}(V)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}P\subset H\subset P^{\perp}\subset V,\\
P\subset Q\subset H^{+}\subset V^{+}\end{array}\,\Big|\,\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\text{$\dim P=k-r$, $\dim Q=k-\frac{r-1}{2}$,}\\
\text{$\dim H=k$, and $Q\subset Q^{\perp}_{+}$}\end{array}\right\}\qquad\text{($r$ odd).}

Here
Q^{\perp}_{+}
is the orthogonal complement of
Q
with respect to
\langle{-},{-}\rangle_{+}
.

Before studying
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{k,r}
in detail, we define another variety
\mathcal{B}_{k,r}(V)
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{k,r}(V)

\displaystyle=\left\{P\subset Q\subset V\,\Big|\,\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\text{$\dim P=k-r$, $\dim Q=k-\frac{r}{2}$,}\\
\text{and $Q\subset Q^{\perp}$}\end{array}\right\}

if
r
even,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{k,r}(V)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}Q\subset V^{+},\\
P\subset Q\cap V\subset V\end{array}\,\Big|\,\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\text{$\dim P=k-r$, $\dim Q=k-\frac{r-1}{2}$,}\\
\text{and $Q\subset Q^{\perp}_{+}$}\end{array}\right\}

if
r
odd.

There are obvious maps

\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{k,r}(V)\to\mathcal{B}_{k,r}(V),\qquad\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{k,r}(V)\to\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V)

given by forgetting
H
or
Q
, respectively. Let

\widehat{\varpi}:\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{k,r}(V)\to\overline{\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V)}

denote the composition
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{k,r}(V)\to\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{k,r}(V)\xrightarrow{\varpi}\overline{\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V)}
. Next, let

\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{k,r}^{\circ}(V)=(\widehat{\varpi})^{-1}(\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V))\qquad\text{and}\qquad\widehat{\varpi}^{\circ}=\widehat{\varpi}|_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{k,r}^{\circ}(V)}:\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{k,r}^{\circ}(V)\to\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V).

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
V
be an
n
-dimensional vector space equipped with a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form, and let
0\leq k\leq n
. Let
r
be an integer such that
\max\{0,2k-n\}<r\leq k
.

(1)

The variety
\mathcal{B}_{k,r}(V)
is smooth, connected, and simply connected.

(2)

For any commutative ring
\Bbbk
,
\mathsf{H}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{B}_{k,r}(V),\Bbbk)=0
is even and free.

(3)

The variety
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{k,r}
is smooth, connected, and simply connected.

(4)

The map
\widehat{\varpi}^{\circ}:\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{k,r}^{\circ}(V)\to\mathcal{C}_{k,r}
is a locally trivial fibration.

(5)

Let
\Bbbk
be a commutative ring in which
2
is invertible. There is an isomorphism of local systems

\mathcal{H}^{0}(\widehat{\varpi}_{*}^{\circ}\Bbbk)\cong\Bbbk\oplus\mathrm{sgn}.

Proof.

(
1
) There is a map

(4.1)

p:\mathcal{B}_{k,r}(V)\to\mathrm{Gr}_{k-r}(V)^{\mathrm{iso}}

given by
(P,Q)\mapsto P
. Since
2k-n<r
, we have
k-r<k-r/2<n/2
, and so
\mathrm{Gr}_{k-r}(V)^{\mathrm{iso}}
is smooth, connected, and simply connected by Lemma
2.4
.

Suppose for now that
r
is even. Let
\mathcal{F}l(k-r,k-r/2)
be the variety of partial flags
P\subset Q\subset V
with
\dim P=k-r
and
\dim Q=k-r/2
. The projection
\mathcal{F}l(k-r,k-r/2)\to\mathrm{Gr}_{k-r}(V)
is a locally trivial fibration, and the map
p
in (
4.1
) is the restriction of this map to the preimage of
\mathrm{Gr}_{k-r}(V)^{\mathrm{iso}}\subset\mathrm{Gr}_{k-r}(V)
. Hence
p
is a locally trivial fibration whose fiber over a point
P\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k-r}(V)^{\mathrm{iso}}
is the variety

\{Q\mid\text{$\dim Q=k-r/2$ and $P\subset Q\subset Q^{\perp}\subset P^{\perp}$}\}\cong\mathrm{Gr}_{r/2}(P^{\perp}/P)^{\mathrm{iso}}.

Since
2k-n<r
, we have
\dim P^{\perp}/P=n-2(k-r)=n-2k+2r>r
, so
\mathrm{Gr}_{r/2}(P^{\perp}/P)^{\mathrm{iso}}
is again smooth, connected, and simply connected. We conclude that
\mathcal{B}_{k,r}(V)
is smooth, connected, and simply connected.

The case where
r
is odd is similar, but the fiber of
p
over
P\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k-r}(V)^{\mathrm{iso}}
is given by

\{Q\mid\text{$\textstyle\dim Q=k-\frac{r-1}{2}$ and $P\subset Q\subset Q^{\perp}_{+}\subset P^{\perp}\oplus\mathbb{C}$}\}\cong\mathrm{Gr}_{\frac{r+1}{2}}(P^{\perp}/P\oplus\mathbb{C})^{\mathrm{iso}}.

Since
\dim(P^{\perp}/P\oplus\mathbb{C})>r+1
, the variety
\mathrm{Gr}_{\frac{r+1}{2}}(P^{\perp}/P\oplus\mathbb{C})^{\mathrm{iso}}
is smooth, connected, and simply connected, and we conclude as before.

(
2
)
We have seen in part (
1
) that
\mathcal{B}_{k,r}
is an isotropic Grassmannian bundle over an isotropic Grassmannian. The claim that its cohomology is even and free then follows from the Serre spectral sequence and Corollary
2.2
.

(
3
)
Suppose first
r
is even. From the definitions, the map
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{k,r}(V)\to\mathcal{B}_{k,r}(V)
is a locally trivial fibration whose fiber over a point
(P,Q)\in\mathcal{B}_{k,r}(V)
is identified with

\mathrm{Gr}_{r/2}(P^{\perp}/Q),

which is smooth, connected, and simply connected. In view of part (
1
), the claim for
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{k,r}(V)
follows.

Now suppose that
r
is odd. For a point
(P,Q)\in\mathcal{B}_{k,r}(V)
, let
\bar{Q}
be the image of
Q
under the projection map
V^{+}\to V
. The kernel of this projection map contains no nonzero isotropic vectors. Since
Q
is isotropic, it follows that
\dim\bar{Q}=\dim Q=k-\frac{r-1}{2}
. Note also that
Q
is contained in
P^{\perp}_{+}=P^{\perp}\oplus\mathbb{C}
, and hence that
\bar{Q}\subset P^{\perp}
. Finally, in the definition of
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{k,r}(V)
, the condition
Q\subset H^{+}\subset P^{\perp}_{+}\subset V^{+}
is equivalent to
\bar{Q}\subset H\subset P^{\perp}\subset V
. In view of these observations, we deduce that
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{k,r}(V)\to\mathcal{B}_{k,r}(V)
is a locally trivial fibration whose fiber over a point
(P,Q)\in\mathcal{B}_{k,r}(V)
is identified with

\mathrm{Gr}_{\frac{r-1}{2}}(P^{\perp}/\bar{Q}),

and then we conclude as before.

(
4
)
Let
H\in\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V)
, and let
(P,Q,H)\in\widehat{\varpi}^{-1}(H)
. The definition of
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{k,r}(V)
implies that
P\subset\rad H
, but the fact that
H\in\mathcal{C}_{k,r}
means that
\dim\rad H=k-r=\dim P
. These observations let us describe
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{k,r}^{\circ}(V)
as follows: if
r
is even, then

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{k,r}^{\circ}(V)

\displaystyle=\left\{\rad H\subset Q\subset H\,\Big|\,\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\text{$H\in\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V)$, $\dim Q=k-\frac{r}{2}$,}\\
\text{and $Q\subset Q^{\perp}$}\end{array}\right\}

if
r
is even,

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{k,r}^{\circ}(V)

\displaystyle=\left\{\rad H\subset Q\subset H^{+}\,\Big|\,\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\text{$H\in\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V)$, $\dim Q=k-\frac{r-1}{2}$,}\\
\text{and $Q\subset Q^{\perp}_{+}$}\end{array}\right\}

if
r
is odd.

In both cases, it follows that
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{k,r}^{\circ}(V)\to\mathcal{C}_{k,r}
is a locally trivially fibration. The fiber over
H\in\mathcal{C}_{k,r}
is given by

\mathrm{Gr}_{r/2}(H/\rad H)^{\mathrm{iso}}\quad\text{if $r$ is even,}\qquad\qquad\mathrm{Gr}_{\frac{r+1}{2}}(H^{+}/\rad H)^{\mathrm{iso}}\quad\text{if $r$ is odd.}

(
5
)
Since
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{k,r}(V)
is smooth and connected, its Zariski-open subset
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{k,r}^{\circ}(V)
is as well. Let
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V))
, and let
\Gamma^{\prime}=\pi_{1}(\widehat{\mathcal{C}}^{\circ}_{k,r}(V))
. (The group
\Gamma
is described in Remark
3.11
, but we will not need this information.)

Next, let
F
denote a fiber of
\widehat{\varpi}:\widehat{\mathcal{C}}^{\circ}_{k,r}(V)\to\mathcal{C}_{k,r}(V)
. We have identified
F
in the proof of part (
4
). By Proposition
3.10
(
2
),
F
has two connected components, and by Lemma
2.4
, each connected component is simply connected. In other words,
\pi_{0}(F)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
and
\pi_{1}(F)=1
. Then the long exact sequence of homotopy groups

Proposition 6.2

i

Remark 6.3

(H\cap B_{i})\oplus C\to\mathrm{pr}_{i}H\oplus\mathbb{C}
). Thus, it remains to determine the space of choices for

. The quotient
\tilde{Q}_{i}/\tilde{P}_{i}
is an isotropic subspace of

(or if

is odd, of
(\mathrm{pr}_{i}H\oplus\mathbb{C})/\tilde{P}_{i}
), which carries a nondegenerate bilinear form. We conclude that the fiber

is described by

F\cong\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq m\\
\text{$\langle{-},{-}\rangle_{B_{i}}$ symmetric}\\
\max\{0,2k_{i}-n_{i}\}<r_{i}\\
\text{$r_{i}$ even}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Gr}_{r_{i}/2}(\mathrm{pr}_{i}H/\rad(\mathrm{pr}_{i}H))^{\mathrm{iso}}\quad\times\\
\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq m\\
\text{$\langle{-},{-}\rangle_{B_{i}}$ symmetric}\\
\max\{0,2k_{i}-n_{i}\}<r_{i}\\
\text{$r_{i}$ odd}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Gr}_{(r_{i}+1)/2}((\mathrm{pr}_{i}H\oplus\mathbb{C})/\rad(\mathrm{pr}_{i}H))^{\mathrm{iso}}

More generally,
\widehat{\mu}^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{\underline{k},\underline{r}})\to\mathcal{O}_{\underline{k},\underline{r}}
is a locally trivial fibration with fibers isomorphic to
F
.

Using Lemma
2.4
, we see that
\pi_{0}(F)\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{d}
where

d=|\{i\mid\text{$\langle{-},{-}\rangle_{B_{i}}$ is symmetric and $\max\{0,2k_{i}-n_{i}\}<r_{i}$}\},

and
\pi_{1}(F)=1
. So we get the following short exact sequence of homotopy groups:

1\to\pi_{1}(\widehat{\mu}^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{\underline{k},\underline{r}}))\to\pi_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{k,r}(V))\to(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{d}\to 1.

Proposition
5.1
also identifies the middle term with
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{d}
, so
\widehat{\mu}^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{\underline{k},\underline{r}})
must be simply connected. Then the same reasoning as in Proposition
4.2
(
5
) shows that
\mathcal{H}^{0}(\widehat{\mu}_{*}^{\circ}\Bbbk)
is the local system corresponding to the regular repreentation
\Bbbk[(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{d}]
. Since
2
is invertible in
\Bbbk
, the regular representation decomposes as a direct sum of representations that are each free of rank
1
over
\Bbbk
. Under the additional assumptions from (
1.1
), every indecomposable
\Bbbk[(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{d}]
-module that is free over
\Bbbk
occurs as a direct summand of the regular representation. This proves the second assertion in the proposition.
∎

Lemma 7.3
.

8.
Main results on parity sheaves

Let
B=B_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus B_{m}
be as in Section
5
. Let
\Bbbk
be a ring satisfying assumption (
1.1
). In this section, we study the
Q_{B}
-equivariant derived category of constructible sheaves on
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(B)
, denoted by

D^{\mathrm{b}}_{Q_{B}}(\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(B),\Bbbk).

Consider the collection of all indecomposable
Q_{B}
-equivariant local systems on
Q_{B}
-orbits in
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(B)
. The “even and free” property from Proposition
5.1
(
4
) means that this collection of local systems satisfies the basic assumptions in
[
4
, Eq. (2.1) and (2.2)]
needed for the theory of parity sheaves. In particular, it makes sense to speak of “
Q_{B}
-equivariant parity sheaves” on
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(B)
. By
[
4
, Theorem 2.12]
, we have the following classification result:

Theorem 8.1

(
[
4
]
)
.

(1)

For each orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{k},\underline{r}}\subset\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(B)
and each indecomposable locally free
\Bbbk
-local system
\mathcal{L}
on
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{k},\underline{r}}
, there is (up to isomorphism) at most one indecomposable parity sheaf

\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{\underline{k},\underline{r}},\mathcal{L})

that is supported on
\overline{\mathcal{O}_{\underline{k},\underline{r}}}
and that satisfies

\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{\underline{k},\underline{r}},\mathcal{L})|_{\mathcal{O}_{\underline{k},\underline{r}}}\cong\mathcal{L}[\dim\mathcal{O}_{\underline{k},\underline{r}}].

(2)

Every parity complex in
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{Q_{B}}(\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(B),\Bbbk)
is isomorphic to a direct sum of objects of the form
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{\underline{k},\underline{r}},\mathcal{L})[m]
.

Note that the first part of this result says “at most one”: the general theory from
[
4
]
does not guarantee the existence of
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{\underline{k},\underline{r}},\mathcal{L})
.

Theorem 8.2
.

For each orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{k},\underline{r}}\subset\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(B)
and each
Q_{B}
-equivariant local system
\mathcal{L}
on
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{k},\underline{r}}
, the parity sheaf
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{\underline{k},\underline{r}},\mathcal{L})
exists. Moreover,

\mathsf{H}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathcal{B}),\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{\underline{k},\underline{r}},\mathcal{L})[-\dim\mathcal{O}_{\underline{k},\underline{r}}])

is even and free.

Proof.

Consider the map
\widehat{\mu}:\widehat{X}(\underline{k},\underline{r})\to\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(\mathcal{B})
. Let
d=\dim\widehat{X}(\underline{k},\underline{r})
, and let
\mathcal{F}=\widehat{\mu}_{*}\Bbbk[d]
. Because
\widehat{X}(\underline{k},\underline{r})
is smooth and
\widehat{\mu}
is proper, we see that
\mathcal{F}
is isomorphic to its own Verdier dual. The stalk of
\mathcal{F}
at a point
M\in\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(B)
is given by

\mathcal{F}_{M}=\mathsf{H}^{\bullet+d}(\widehat{\mu}^{-1}(M),\Bbbk).

Since
\widehat{\mu}^{-}(M)
has an affine paving, we see that the stalks of
\mathcal{F}
vanish in degrees whose parity does not match that of
d
. Since the same condition holds for
\mathbb{D}\mathcal{F}
, we conclude that
\mathcal{F}
is a parity complex.

By Theorem
8.1
,
\mathcal{F}
is a direct sum of objects of the form
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{\underline{k}^{\prime},\underline{r}^{\prime}},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})[m]
. Since
\mathcal{F}
is supported on
\overline{\mathcal{O}_{\underline{k},\underline{r}}}
, any summand
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{\underline{k}^{\prime},\underline{r}^{\prime}},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})[m]
that occurs in
\mathcal{F}
must satisfy
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{k}^{\prime},\underline{r}^{\prime}}\subset\overline{\mathcal{O}_{\underline{k},\underline{r}}}
. As a consequence, if
\mathcal{F}|_{\mathcal{O}_{\underline{k},\underline{r}}}
is nonzero, and if
\mathcal{L}
is an irreducible summand of some cohomology sheaf
\mathcal{H}^{i}(\mathcal{F}|_{\mathcal{O}_{\underline{k},\underline{r}}})
, then
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{\underline{k},\underline{r}},\mathcal{L})
exists and occurs (up to shift) as a direct summand of
\mathcal{F}
.

By Proposition
7.2
,
every
indecomposable locally free
Q_{B}
-equivariant local system
\mathcal{L}
on
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{k},\underline{r}}
occurs as a direct summand of
\mathcal{H}^{-d}(\mathcal{F})
. We conclude that every
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{\underline{k},\underline{r}},\mathcal{L})
exists, and that

\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{\underline{k},\underline{r}},\mathcal{L})[d-\dim\mathcal{O}_{\underline{k},\underline{r}}]

occurs as a direct summand of
\mathcal{F}
.

Finally, we see that
\mathsf{H}^{i}(\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(B),\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{\underline{k},\underline{r}},\mathcal{L})[-\dim\mathcal{O}_{\underline{k},\underline{r}})
is a direct summand of

\mathsf{H}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(B),\mathcal{F}[-d])\cong\mathsf{H}^{\bullet}(\widehat{X}(\underline{k},\underline{r}),\Bbbk),

and this is even and free by Lemma
7.1
.
∎

Corollary 8.3
.

If
\mathcal{F}\in D^{\mathrm{b}}_{Q_{B}}(\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(B),\Bbbk)
is an even complex, then
\mathsf{H}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{Gr}_{k}(B),\mathcal{F})
is even and free.

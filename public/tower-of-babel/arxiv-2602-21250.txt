Constructing Barut-Girardello coherent states for the isotonic oscillator in the DOOT approach


Abstract
In this work, we study the quantum system of the isotonic oscillator from the perspective of the diagonal operator ordering technique (DOOT). Within this framework, we construct the associated Barut-Girardello and Gazeau-Klauder coherent states. We examine their mathematical properties using reproducing kernels and compute the expectation values of observables that characterize the system and its relevant physical features. Further, we perform the quantization of main classical variables in the complex plane. Then, by exploring the thermal behavior of the physical system in the constructed coherent states, we analyze the properties of mixed states described by a canonical density operator. We also obtain the corresponding Glauber-Sudarshan P P -representation.

The structure of the paper is as follows. Section 2 presents the quantum system of interest, emphasizing its underlying

Section 4 delves into the mathematical properties of each family of constructed coherent states, examining their completeness via the inverse Mellin transform and deriving suitable weight functions. The reproducing kernel property is then discussed for each class of coherent states. Section 5 is devoted to the evaluation of the expectation values of the

The isotonic oscillator quantum Hamiltonian and the underlying
\mathfrak{su}(1,1)
Lie algebra

The quantum system of the model known as the isotonic oscillator is obtained through a deformation of the one-dimensional harmonic oscillator described by the classical Hamiltonian

H(x,p_{x})=\frac{p^{2}_{x}}{2m}+\frac{1}{2}m\omega^{2}x^{2}

(2)

where
(x,p_{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}
implying
x>0
,and where the canonical Poisson bracket is given by
\{x,p_{x}\}=1
. By introducing the variable
d_{x}=p_{x}x
, called the dilation variable, together with the variables
x
, and
d_{x}
referred to as affine variables, which satisfy the Poisson bracket relation
\{x,d_{x}\}=x

[
28
,
29
]
, the Hamiltonian in (
2
) takes the form

H(x,d_{x})=\frac{d_{x}(x^{-2})d_{x}}{2mx^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\omega^{2}x^{2}.

(3)

Within the framework of affine quantization, the classical affine variables are promoted to operators acting on the wave function
\psi(x,t)
as

\displaystyle x\mapsto\hat{x}:\quad\left\{\hat{x}\psi(x,t)=x\psi(x,t),\quad x>0\right\}

\displaystyle d_{x}\mapsto\hat{d_{x}}:\quad\left\{\hat{d_{x}}\psi(x,t)=-i\hbar\left(x\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\psi(x,t)\right\},

which satisfy the following commutation relation:

[\hat{x},\hat{d_{x}}]=i\hbar\hat{x}.

Consequently, the Hamiltonian operator of the harmonic oscillator takes the form

\hat{H}(\hat{x},\hat{d_{x}})=\frac{\hat{d_{x}}(\hat{x})^{-2}\hat{d_{x}}}{2m}+\frac{1}{2}m\omega^{2}\hat{x}^{2}

leading to the Schrödinger equation

i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi(x,t)=\hat{H}\psi(x,t).

The wave functions
\psi(x,t)
, defined in
[
28
,
29
]
, are given by

\psi(x,t)=\phi(x)e^{-iEt/\hbar},

and are normalized according to the condition

\int_{0}^{\infty}|\psi(x,t)|^{2},dx=1.

Substituting this expression into the Schrödinger equation leads to the time-independent eigenvalue equation

\hat{H}(\hat{x},\hat{d_{x}})\phi(x)=E\phi(x).

(4)

By applying the action of the operators
\hat{x}
and
\hat{d_{x}}
, one successively obtains

\hat{d}_{x}\phi(x)=-i\hbar\left(x\phi^{\prime}(x)+\frac{1}{2}\phi(x)\right),

\hat{x}^{-2}\hat{d}_{x}\phi(x)=-i\hbar\left(\frac{1}{x}\phi^{\prime}(x)+\frac{1}{2x^{2}}\phi(x)\right),

\hat{d}_{x}\hat{x}^{-2}\hat{d}_{x}\phi(x)=-\hbar^{2}\left(\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)-\frac{3}{4x^{2}}\phi(x)\right).

The eigenvalue equation (
4
) can therefore be written in the following explicit form:

\left[-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+\frac{3\hbar^{2}}{8mx^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}m\omega^{2}x^{2}\right]\phi(x)=E\phi(x).

(5)

By choosing
m=1
,
\omega=1
, and
\hbar=1
to simplify the equation, one finally obtains

\left[-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}x^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{3}{4x^{2}}\right]\phi(x)=E\phi(x).

(6)

The resulting Schrödinger equation corresponds to a Hamiltonian that is a particular case of the isotonic oscillator Hamiltonian, with the parameter
A=\frac{3}{4}
.

In this work, we consider the one-dimensional isotonic oscillator, whose generalized Hamiltonian is given by

It was also shown that

Consider the quantum Hamiltonian

3
The DOOT approach and related coherent states

Let us begin this section by recalling the main principles of the diagonal operator ordering technique (DOOT)
[
6
]
. In the remainder of this work, we apply the DOOT framework to the isotonic oscillator system, focusing on the
\mathfrak{su}(1,1)
Lie algebra generators and the associated Barut-Girardello coherent states (BGCSs), as well as the Gazeau-Klauder coherent states (GKCSs), defined on the corresponding Fock Hilbert spaces.

3.1
The
\mathfrak{su}(1,1)
generators representation in the DOOT framework

From the representation of the
\mathfrak{su}(1,1)
generators on the Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}=span\{\ket{n,\gamma}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
given by Eqs.(
11
), the Fock basis vectors
\ket{n,\gamma}
and their duals
\bra{n,\gamma}
can be constructed by successive applications of the raising operator
K_{+}
on the vacuum state
\ket{0,\gamma}
. One finds

(K_{+})^{n}|0,\gamma\rangle=\sqrt{4^{n}\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}+1\right)_{n}}|n,\gamma\rangle,

where
\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}+1\right)_{n}
denotes the Pochhammer symbol, defined by

\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}+1\right)_{n}=\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}+1\right)\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}+2\right)\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}+3\right)\cdots\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}+n\right).

Consequently, the ket and bra vectors take the explicit operator form

|n,\gamma\rangle=\frac{(K_{+})^{n}}{\sqrt{4^{n}\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}+1\right)_{n}}}|0,\gamma\rangle,\qquad\langle n,\gamma|=\langle 0,\gamma|\frac{(K_{-})^{n}}{\sqrt{4^{n}\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}+1\right)_{n}}}.

(15)

Combinning the two relations in Eq.(
15
), and using the completeness of the Fock basis in
\mathfrak{H}=span\{\ket{n,\gamma}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
, the resolution of the identity operator
\mathbb{I_{\mathfrak{H}}}
can be written in the diagonal operator-ordered form

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|n,\gamma\rangle\langle n,\gamma|=\mathbb{I_{\mathfrak{H}}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(K_{+}K_{-})^{n}}{4^{n}\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}+1\right)_{n}}|0,\gamma\rangle\langle 0,\gamma|.

(16)

The Barut-Girardello coherent states (BGCSs) associated with the isotonic oscillator are defined as eigenstates of the lowering generator
K_{-}
of the
\mathfrak{su}(1,1)
Lie algebra. Within the DOOT framework, these states naturally split into two distinct classes, namely even and odd coherent states.

Before proceeding with their explicit construction, it is convenient to introduce the following subspaces of
\mathfrak{H}
, as discussed in
[
30
,
33
]
. These subspaces are spanned by the even and odd Fock vectors
|2n,\gamma\rangle
and
|2n+1,\gamma\rangle
, respectively, and denoted
\mathfrak{H}_{e}
and
\mathfrak{H}_{o}
:

\mathfrak{H}_{e}:=span\left\{|2n,\gamma\rangle:\langle 2m,\gamma|2n,\gamma\rangle=\delta_{nm},\;\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|2n,\gamma\rangle\langle 2n,\gamma|=\mathbb{I}_{\mathfrak{H}_{e}}\right\},

(17)

\mathfrak{H}_{o}:=span\left\{|2n+1,\gamma\rangle:\langle 2m+1,\gamma|2n+1,\gamma\rangle=\delta_{nm},\;\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|2n+1,\gamma\rangle\langle 2n+1,\gamma|=\mathbb{I}_{\mathfrak{H}_{o}}\right\},

(18)

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{H}=\mathfrak{H}_{e}\oplus\mathfrak{H}_{o}
with
\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{\mathfrak{H}}=\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{\mathfrak{H}_{e}}\oplus\mathbb{I}_{\mathfrak{H}_{o}}
,
\mathbb{I}_{\mathfrak{H}_{e}}
and
\mathbb{I}_{\mathfrak{H}_{o}}
being the corresponding identity operators, considered in the sequel of the work.

For this purpose, we first derive the projector onto the vacuum state, denoted by
|0,\gamma\rangle\langle 0,\gamma|
associated with the even and odd Hilbert subspaces, respectively, starting from (
16
)

(a)

Even coherent states.

Restricting (
16
) to the even sector and applying the DOOT prescription, one obtains

\displaystyle\mathbb{I_{\mathfrak{H}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(K_{+}K_{-})^{2n}}{4^{2n}\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}+1\right)_{2n}}|0,\gamma\rangle\langle 0,\gamma|

(19)

\displaystyle=

{}_{1}F_{1}\left(1;\frac{\gamma}{2}+1;\frac{K_{+}K_{-}}{4}\right)|0,\gamma\rangle\langle 0,\gamma|,

(20)

where
{}_{1}F_{1}
denotes the confluent hypergeometric function
[
32
]
, defined by

{}_{1}F_{1}\left(1;\frac{\gamma}{2}+1;z\right)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{m}}{m!\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}+1\right)_{m}}.

Consequently, the vacuum projector in the even sector can be written in the diagonal operator-ordered form

|0,\gamma\rangle\langle 0,\gamma|=\#\frac{\mathbb{I_{\mathfrak{H}}}}{{}_{1}F_{1}\left(1;\frac{\gamma}{2}+1;\frac{K_{+}K_{-}}{4}\right)}\#.

(21)

(b)

Odd coherent states.

Following the same procedure for the odd sector yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{I_{\mathfrak{H}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(K_{+}K_{-})^{2n+1}}{4^{2n+1}\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}+1\right)_{2n+1}}|0,\gamma\rangle\langle 0,\gamma|

(22)

\displaystyle\left({}_{1}F_{1}\left(1;\frac{\gamma}{2}+1;\frac{K_{+}K_{-}}{4}\right)-1\right)|0,\gamma\rangle\langle 0,\gamma|,

which leads to the diagonal operator-ordered expression

|0,\gamma\rangle\langle 0,\gamma|=\#\frac{\mathbb{I_{\mathfrak{H}}}}{\left({}_{1}F_{1}\left(1;\frac{\gamma}{2}+1;\frac{K_{+}K_{-}}{4}\right)-1\right)}\#.

(23)

As a direct consequence, the expectation values
\langle 0,\gamma|(K_{+}K_{-})^{2n}|0,\gamma\rangle
and
\langle 0,\gamma|(K_{+}K_{-})^{2n+1}|0,\gamma\rangle
can be readily evaluated, as shown below :

\langle 0,\gamma|(K_{+}K_{-})^{2n}|0,\gamma\rangle=4^{2n}\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}+1\right)_{2n},

(24)

\langle 0,\gamma|(K_{+}K_{-})^{2n+1}|0,\gamma\rangle=4^{2n+1}\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}+1\right)_{2n+1},

(25)

respectively.

3.2
Barut-Giradello coherent states

3.2.1
Construction

8.1
Density operator construction using the DOOT technique

In quantum mechanics, the probability distribution over the states of a physical system can be characterized by a statistical operator, called the density operator, when the entire quantum system is assumed to follow a canonical distribution. When a system is in thermal contact with a reservoir (or “thermal bath”), its state becomes mixed, described by the canonical equilibrium normalized density operator, usually denoted
\rho
, and given by

\rho=\frac{1}{Z}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-\beta e_{n}}|n\rangle\langle n|

(61)

where the partition function
Z
is taken as the normalization constant, with
\beta=\frac{1}{k_{B}T}
,
k_{B}
being the Boltzmann constant and
T
the system environment temperature. In the following, we will apply the DOOT (Dynamic Operator Ordering Transformation) operation to the canonical density operator. The corresponding density operators and related partition functions are obtained in the Fock Hilbert space, using Eqs.(
16
) and (
3.3
) together as key ingredients. We then successively obtain, for each set of CSs:

1.

For the even Barut-Girardello CSs,

\rho_{e}^{\gamma}=\frac{1}{Z_{e}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-8n\beta}e^{-2\beta\gamma}|2n,\gamma\rangle\langle 2n,\gamma|,\qquad Z_{e}=\frac{e^{-2\beta\gamma}}{1-e^{-8\beta}},

with the DOOT transformed density operator given by

\rho_{e}^{\gamma}=(1-e^{-8\beta})\#\frac{{}_{1}F_{1}\left(1;\frac{\gamma}{2}+1;\frac{e^{-4\beta}K_{+}K_{-}}{4}\right)}{{}_{1}F_{1}\left(1;\frac{\gamma}{2}+1;\frac{K_{+}K_{-}}{4}\right)}\#.

(62)

2.

For the odd Barut-Girardello CSs,

\rho_{o}^{\gamma}=\frac{1}{Z_{o}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-(8n+4)\beta}e^{-2\beta\gamma}|2n+1,\gamma\rangle\langle 2n+1,\gamma|,\qquad Z_{o}=\frac{e^{-2\beta(\gamma+1)}}{1-e^{-8\beta}},

with the DOOT counterpart given by

\rho_{o}^{\gamma}=(1-e^{-8\beta})\#\frac{{}_{1}F_{1}\left(1;\frac{\gamma}{2}+1;\frac{e^{-4\beta}K_{+}K_{-}}{4}\right)-1}{{}_{1}F_{1}\left(1;\frac{\gamma}{2}+1;\frac{K_{+}K_{-}}{4}\right)-1}\#.

(63)

3.

For the Gazeau-Klauder CSs,

\rho_{GKCS}^{\gamma}=\frac{1}{Z_{GKCS}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-4n\beta}e^{-2\beta\gamma}|\Phi_{n}^{\gamma}\rangle\langle\Phi_{n}^{\gamma}|,\qquad Z_{GKCS}=\frac{e^{-2\beta\gamma}}{1-e^{-4\beta}},

9
Concluding remarks

In this work, we revisited the properties of the Barut-Girardello coherent states (BGCSs) and the Gazeau-Klauder coherent states (GKCSs) using an operator ordering approach introduced in
[
17
]
, namely the diagonal ordering operation technique (DOOT). As established in
[
17
,
18
]
, this approach essentially consists of applying the ordered product or operatorial functions thereof to various objects associated with the coherent states, in order to simplify the corresponding algebraic calculations. The results obtained are consistent with those derived using purely algebraic methods, thereby confirming the validity of the DOOT approach.

Throughout this paper, we applied the DOOT procedure to construct coherent states for the isotonic oscillator, starting from its discrete spectrum, and developed the associated BGCSs and GKCSs on the appropriate Hilbert spaces. We first verified that these states satisfy all of Klauder’s required criteria. From the resolution of the identity property, several relevant mathematical structures were analyzed, including, in particular, the reproducing kernel properties of the constructed families of coherent states.

Once the expectation values of observables describing the quantum system were determined, several important physical properties were studied, such as the photon number distribution and the probability density. Moreover, the quantization procedure was implemented for the classical variables of the quantum system in the complex plane, both in the standard framework and within the DOOT scheme.

Subsequently, the thermal behavior of the physical system in the constructed coherent states was analyzed.

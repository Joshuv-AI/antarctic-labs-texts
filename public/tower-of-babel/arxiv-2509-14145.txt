Moduli of surfaces fibered in log Calabi-Yau pairs
Giovanni Inchiostro, Roberto Svaldi, Junyan Zhao

Abstract
. We study the moduli spaces of surface pairs ( X , D ) (X,D) admitting a log Calabi–Yau fibration ( X , D ) → C . (X,D)\rightarrow C. We develop a series of results on stable reduction and apply them to give an explicit description of the boundary of the KSBA compactification. Three interesting cases where our results apply are: (1) divisors on ℙ 1 × ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1} of bidegree ( 2 ​ n , m ) (2n,m) ; (2) K3 surfaces which map 2 : 1 2:1 to 𝔽 n \mathbb{F}_{n} , with X = 𝔽 n X=\mathbb{F}_{n} and D D the ramification locus; (3) elliptic surfaces with either a section or a bisection. The main tools employed are stable quasimaps, the canonical bundle formula, and the minimal model program.

Introduction

The study of moduli spaces and their compactifications is a central theme in modern algebraic geometry. Compactifying the moduli of varieties fibered in log Calabi–Yau (CY) pairs not only sheds light on the geometry of degenerations, but also connects with a range of moduli theories. Our goal in this paper is to describe the boundary of certain KSBA compactifications of surface pairs fibered in log Calabi–Yau curves, using methods that avoid running explicit steps of the minimal model program (MMP).

While our results apply to a broader context, one can first focus on surface pairs

\pi:\bigl(X,\tfrac{1}{n}D\bigr)\longrightarrow C

fibered in log Calabi–Yau pairs of the form

(\mathbb{P}^{1},\tfrac{1}{n}(p_{1}+\cdots+p_{2n}))
,
under the following mild assumptions:

(1)

the divisor
D
restricts to
2n
distinct points on a general fiber of
\pi
;

(2)

the pair
\bigl(X,(\tfrac{1}{n}+\epsilon_{1})D+\epsilon_{2}F\bigr)
is KSBA-stable for every

0<\epsilon_{1}\ll\epsilon_{2}\ll 1
, where
F
denotes a fiber of
\pi
meeting
D
in fewer than
2n
points.

Three notable examples falling within this framework are:

•

divisors of type
(2n,m)
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
with
m\geq 4
;

•

K3 surfaces that admit a
2\!:\!1
map to a Hirzebruch surface
\mathbb{F}_{n}
for
n\leq 4
, with
D
the ramification divisor; and

•

elliptic surfaces with a section or a bisection.

By the general KSBA moduli theory (cf.
[
Kol23
]
), there exists a
proper
Deligne–Mumford stack
\mathcal{M}^{\KSBA}_{n}(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})
parametrizing KSBA-stable pairs, where a general member is of the form
\bigl(X,(\tfrac{1}{n}+\epsilon_{1})D+\epsilon_{2}F\bigr),
as described in the preceding paragraph. Our first main result gives an explicit description of the KSBA-stable pairs parametrized by
\mathcal{M}^{\KSBA}_{n}(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})
, with particular emphasis on those lying on the boundary.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(X_{0},(\frac{1}{n}+\epsilon_{1})D_{0}+\epsilon_{2}F_{0})
be a KSBA-stable pair parametrized by
\mathcal{M}^{\KSBA}_{n}(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})
. Then there exists a nodal curve
C_{0}
such that the fibration structure
(X,(\frac{1}{n}+\epsilon_{1})D+\epsilon_{2}F)\to C
of a general pair in
\mathcal{M}^{\KSBA}_{n}(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})
extends to a fibration
X_{0}\to C_{0}
with pure one-dimensional fibers. Moreover, for any irreducible component
G
of
C_{0}
, the reduced structure of
X_{0}|_{G}
is either:

(1)

(Proposition
4.3
)
the coarse space of a projective bundle
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathcal{G}}({\mathcal{V}})
over a smooth orbifold curve
{\mathcal{G}}
with coarse space
G
.

(2)

(Proposition
4.4
)
the coarse space of a weighted blow-up of a surface
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathcal{G}}({\mathcal{V}})
as above.

(3)

(Proposition
4.6
)
the coarse space of gluing a surface
{\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma}({\mathcal{V}})
ruled over a (possibly disconnected) smooth curve
\Sigma
, glued along an involution of
\Sigma\to\Sigma
; see Figure
10
.

When
n=2
, the fibers of
(X_{0},D_{0})\to C_{0}
are classified in Section §
4.2.1
.

A key difference from previous approaches (e.g.
[
DH21
,
AB21
,
AH23
]
) is that our method does not rely on describing the explicit steps of an MMP for the surface pair. Instead, our proofs rest on three main tools:

(1)

a general MMP result for log Calabi–Yau fibrations in arbitrary dimension;

(2)

the theory of stable quasimaps to the moduli of points on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
developed in
[
DLI24
]
;

(3)

the notion of a
ruled model
, which provides a convenient birational model for controlling singular fibers.

Together, these ingredients allow us to track KSBA-stable limits through quasimaps compactifications and to describe them explicitly via ruled models.

Stable quasimaps

Our second key ingredient is the moduli space of

introduced in
[

Recall that a point

Explicit stable reduction and canonical bundle formula

In this subsection we interpret how we use
Theorem

1.2
, in terms of the canonical bundle formula.

Recall that, given two locally stable families
({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{D}})\to\text{\rm Spec}R
and
{\mathcal{Y}}\to\text{\rm Spec}R
over the spectrum of a DVR, with a morphism
\pi\colon({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{D}})\to({\mathcal{Y}},\bfM)
of generalized pairs such that
K_{\mathcal{X}}+{\mathcal{D}}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\pi^{*}(K_{\mathcal{Y}}+\bfM)
, it is not true that the canonical bundle formula commutes with base change. In other terms, it is not true that the restriction of
\bfM
to the special fiber of
{\mathcal{Y}}\to\text{\rm Spec}R
, is the moduli part of the morphism
\pi
restricted to the special fiber of
{\mathcal{X}}_{0}
.
For example, one can take a generic
(4,3)
-curve
D
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
degenerating to

along
\text{\rm Spec}R

({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{R}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{R},\frac{1}{2}D)\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{R}
has no boundary part generically, but on the special fiber there are three points which contribute to the boundary part with coefficient
\frac{1}{2}
; and dually the moduli part of the special fiber is trivial while it is not on the generic fiber.
In particular, if one has a fibration in log Calabi-Yau pairs
(X,D)\to Y\to\eta
over the generic point
\eta

It turns out that this phenomenon does not happen if one takes a limit in the quasimaps moduli space of
[
DLI24
]
instead. Indeed, the moduli part comes from a map from

\pi

We adopt the following conventions throughout this paper.

(1)

{\mathbb{C}}
.

(2)

A

(3)

(6)

(9)

\pi\circ\phi
are semi-log canonical. From
Section

2.1.3
, to compute the moduli part it suffices to use a test family; we choose the test family given by the quotient by the hyperelliptic involution on a pencil of cubics in Weierstrass form. Choose
A_{1},A_{2},B_{1},B_{2}
be generic enough so that the pencil of genus one curves

Y\ :=\ {\mathbb{V}}\big(s(y^{2}z-x^{3}-A_{1}xz^{2}-B_{1}Z^{3})+t(y^{2}z-x^{3}-A_{2}xz^{2}-B_{2}Z^{3})\big)\ \subseteq\ {\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{x,y,z}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{s,t}

is smooth. The second projection

f\colon Y\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
gives an elliptic surface with
j
map of degree
12
. Let
q\colon Y\to X
be the quotient by the hyperelliptic involution,
with morphism
f^{\prime}\colon X\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
. This is the restriction to
Y
of the quotient map

{\mathbb{P}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\ \longrightarrow\ {\mathbb{P}}(1,2,1)\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1},\ \ \ \ \ ([x,y,z],[s,t])\ \mapsto\ ([x,y^{2},z],[s,t]).

For every point
p=[s,t]\in{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
we have

(f^{\prime})^{-1}(p)\ =\ {\mathbb{V}}\big(YZ-X^{3}-\alpha XZ^{2}-\beta Z^{3}\big)\ \subseteq\ {\mathbb{P}}(1,2,1)

for an appropriate choice of
\alpha
and
\beta
, where
X,Z
have weight
1
and
Y
has weight
2
.
In particular, one can check that the fibers of
f^{\prime}
are isomorphic to
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
. Observe that the ramification divisor
\Delta\subseteq X
is such that
(X,\Delta)\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
induces a map
\psi:{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\to{\mathscr{P}}^{1}
. Namely, for every
p\in{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, the pair
((f^{\prime})^{-1}(p),\frac{1}{2}\Delta|_{(f^{\prime})^{-1}(p)})
is log canonical. Applying the canonical bundle formula for minimal elliptic surfaces with a section, we have that

\textstyle f^{*}(2K_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}+\frac{1}{6}\deg j)\ \sim\ 2K_{Y}\ \sim\ q^{*}(2K_{X}+\Delta)\ =\ q^{*}(f^{\prime})^{*}(2K_{\mathbb{P}}^{1}+\psi^{*}{\mathcal{M}})

where
j\colon{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\to\overline{M}_{1,1}
is the
j
-map associated to
f:Y\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
and
\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{M}}
is the moduli part. Taking the push-forward of the left-hand side and the right-hand side both to
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
and using
f_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}={\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}
, we have that
2K_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}+\frac{1}{6}\deg j=2K_{\mathbb{P}}^{1}+\psi^{*}{\mathcal{M}}
. By Lemma
2.11
, one has that
\deg f=\deg j
, and thus the moduli part has degree
\frac{\deg(f)}{12}
as desired.
∎

Corollary 2.13
.

Assume that
C\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
is a smooth curve of class
(n,4)
, such that for every
x\in{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, the pair
\big(\pi^{-1}_{1}(x),\frac{1}{2}C|_{\pi^{-1}_{1}(x)}\big)
is log canonical. Then the pair
({\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1},\frac{1}{2}C)
induces a morphism
\phi:{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\to{\mathscr{P}}_{1}^{\GIT}\xrightarrow{i}{\mathscr{P}}_{1}
, such that the composition
f:{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\to{\mathscr{P}}_{1}\xrightarrow{p}P_{1}
has degree
6n
.

Proof.

One can compute the canonical bundle formula explicitly, and the moduli part has degree
\frac{n}{2}
. The desired statement follows from
Lemma

2.12
.
∎

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
\phi:{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\to{\mathscr{P}}_{1}
be a morphism such that the composition
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\rightarrow{\mathscr{P}}_{1}\rightarrow P_{1}
is finite. Let
\pi:{\mathscr{P}}_{1}\xrightarrow{\pi}{\mathscr{P}}_{1}^{\GIT}
be the retraction morphism, and
f\colon X\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
be the
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
-bundle induced by
\pi
. Then
X
is isomorphic to a Hirzebruch surface
\mathbb{F}_{n}
such that
6n\leq\deg f
.

Proof.

First observe that
X
is a
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
-fibration over
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, and hence
X\simeq\mathbb{F}_{n}
for some
n
. We may assume that
n\geq 1
. Let
\mathfrak{e}\subseteq X
be the negative section, and
\mathfrak{f}
be the class of a fiber of
X\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
. One has that
K_{X}\sim-2\mathfrak{e}+(-n-2)\mathfrak{f}
. By Lemma
2.12
, one has

\textstyle K_{X}+\frac{1}{2}D\ \sim\ f^{*}K_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}+\frac{\deg(f)}{12}\mathfrak{f}

and thus

\textstyle D\ \sim\ -2K_{X}+2f^{*}(K_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}})+\frac{\deg(f)}{6}\mathfrak{f}\ =\ 4\mathfrak{e}+(2n+\frac{\deg(f)}{6})\mathfrak{f}.

Assume by contradiction that
n>\frac{\deg(f)}{6}
. Then
(D.\mathfrak{e})<0
, and as both are effective divisors, we must have that
\mathfrak{e}\subseteq D
. However, if
n>\frac{\deg(f)}{6}
, we also have that
(D-\mathfrak{e}).\mathfrak{e}<0
, so
2\mathfrak{e}\subseteq D
. This is impossible: since
C\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
was finite, the generic fiber of
f|_{D}:D\to C
consists of four distinct points.
∎

3.2
to guarantee that certain minimal models are canonical models.

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
{\mathcal{R}}
be the
\mu_{k}
-root stack of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
at a point
p
, and
\phi\colon{\mathcal{R}}\to{\mathscr{P}}_{n}
be a morphism such that
\phi(p)\in{\mathscr{P}}_{n,{\operatorname{DM}}}
and that the induced morphism between coarse spaces
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\to P_{n}
is finite. Assume that if there is
q\in{\mathcal{R}}\smallsetminus\{p\}
such that
\phi(q)
represents a nodal curve, then the image of every point of
{\mathcal{R}}
represents a nodal curve. Then there is a point
x\in{\mathcal{R}}\setminus\{p\}
such that
\phi(p)
represents a pair with divisor supported on less than
2n+2
points.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction. Let
f\colon({\mathscr{X}},\frac{1}{n+1}{\mathscr{D}})\rightarrow{\mathcal{R}}
be the pull-back along
\phi
of the universal family over
{\mathscr{P}}_{n}
. If all the fibers of
f
had
2n+2
distinct markings away from
p
, then the divisor
{\mathscr{D}}
would be étale over
{\mathcal{R}}\smallsetminus\{p\}
. As the only étale cover of
{\mathcal{R}}\smallsetminus\{p\}
is the trivial one, then
{\mathscr{D}}
consists of
2n+2
disjoint sections. Observe that there is a finite cover
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\to{\mathcal{R}}
, which is étale over
p
: indeed, the
\mu_{k}
-root stack of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
at
two
points is isomorphic to the quotient stack
[{\mathbb{P}}^{1}/\bm{\mu}_{k}]
, where
\mu_{k}
acts on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
by

\xi*[a:b]\ :=\ [\xi a:b].

Then
[{\mathbb{P}}^{1}/\bm{\mu}_{k}]
admits a map to the root stack of order
k
at a single point
p
: there is a morphism
[{\mathbb{P}}^{1}/\bm{\mu}_{k}]\to{\mathcal{R}}
which is the root stack at a point
q\in{\mathcal{R}}\smallsetminus\{p\}
.

Now pulling back the universal family of
{\mathscr{P}}_{n}
along
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\rightarrow{\mathcal{R}}\rightarrow{\mathscr{P}}_{1}
, one gets a surface pair
(X,\frac{1}{n+1}D)
where
D
consists of
2n+2
distinct sections. Observe also that, as by assumption
\phi(p)\in{\mathscr{P}}_{n,{\operatorname{DM}}}
, if the fiber over
p
is
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
then at least three of these
2n+2
sections are disjoint, and if the fiber is nodal at least four are disjoint. We distinguish three cases.

Case 1
: the fiber over
p
is
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
. Then by assumption
X\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
is a
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
-fibration. As
D
consists of at least 3 disjoint sections, it has to be that
X\simeq{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, and
D\sim\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(2n+2,0)
, i.e.

(X,D)\ \simeq\ ({\mathbb{P}}^{1},p_{1}+\cdots+p_{2n+2})\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1},

and hence the moduli part is constant. But the moduli part on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
is the pull-back of the one on
{\mathscr{C}}
, so the moduli part on
{\mathscr{C}}
has degree 0 which contradicts the finitedness of
C\to{\mathscr{P}}_{1}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
.

Case 2
: the fiber over
p
is nodal, and the generic fiber is smooth. Then we can contract one of the two branches, let
E
be such branch. The new surface is a ruled surface with
2n+2
sections, with at least three of them disjoint,
so it is again
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
. Moreover, if we denote by
F_{1},\ldots,F_{n+1}
the connected components of
D
which intersect
E
, then the images of
F_{i}
intersect once contracting
E
, while not intersecting the image of
D-F_{1}-\ldots-F_{n+1}
. This is not possible as two fibers of the same fibration in
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
have the same numerical class.

Case 3
: the generic fiber is nodal. This is not possible as the nodal locus in
P_{n}
is a single point, and by assumption
{\mathcal{R}}\to{\mathscr{P}}_{n}
induces a finite morphism on good moduli space.
∎

2.3.
Addendum

The following lemmas, familiar to experts, will be invoked in the foregoing discussion. We state them here separately, as they are of a different nature.

Lemma 2.16
.

Let
C
be a smooth curve, and
{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow C
be a sequence of root stacks so that
{\mathcal{C}}
is smooth. Then any
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
-fibration
f\colon{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathcal{C}}
over
{\mathcal{C}}
is the projectivization of a rank
2
vector bundle.

Proof.

Let
U\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}
be the open subscheme restricting on which
{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow C
is an isomorphism, and the morphism
f|_{U}
admits a section
\sigma_{U}
. Since the composition
{\mathcal{S}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow C
is proper, then by
[
BV24
, Theorem 3.1]
,
\sigma_{U}
can be extended to a (representable) section
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{S}}
, where
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\rightarrow C
is a (unique) root stack. As both
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathcal{C}}
are representable, then so is the composition
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{C}}
, and hence by Zariski’s main theorem
{\mathcal{C}}={\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
. In particular,
{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathcal{C}}
has a section
\Sigma
, so
{\mathcal{S}}
is the projectivization of
f_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{S}}}(\Sigma)
.
∎

Lemma 2.17
.

The good moduli space of a seminormal algebraic stack is seminormal.

Proof.

The good moduli space
{\mathcal{X}}\to X
of an algebraic stack
{\mathcal{X}}
is, étale locally on
X
, of the form
[\text{\rm Spec}A/G]\to\text{\rm Spec}(A^{G})
, and being seminormal can be checked étale locally. Therefore, it suffices to prove that
\text{\rm Spec}A^{G}
is seminormal for any seminormal affine scheme
\text{\rm Spec}A
with an action by a reductive group
G
. As
\text{\rm Spec}A
is reduced, then so is
\text{\rm Spec}A^{G}
. Since
\Spec A
is seminormal, then the quotient
\Spec A\rightarrow\Spec A^{G}
factors through the seminormalization
\nu\colon\text{\rm Spec}(A^{G})^{\sn}\to\text{\rm Spec}(A^{G})
. By the universal property of categorical quotients,
\nu
has a section
s\colon\text{\rm Spec}A^{G}\to\text{\rm Spec}(A^{G})^{\sn}
, which has connected fibers as
\nu
is a homeomorphism. By
[
FI24
, Lemma 2.3]
, there is an isomorphism

s_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{\text{\rm Spec}A^{G}}\ \cong\ {\mathcal{O}}_{\text{\rm Spec}(A^{G})^{\sn}}.

In particular
\text{\rm Spec}(A^{G})^{\sn}
has the same underlying topological space as
\text{\rm Spec}A^{G}
with the isomorphic structure sheaf, thus
\text{\rm Spec}(A^{G})^{\sn}\cong\text{\rm Spec}A^{G}
.
∎

Lemma 2.18
.

Let
\pi\colon(X,D)\to(Y,B_{Y}+\bfM)
be a morphism of lc generalized pairs such that
K_{X}+D\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\pi^{*}(K_{Y}+B_{Y}+\bfM)
. Let
\tau^{\st}\colon Y\to(Y^{\st},L)
be a morphism to a polarized normal variety such that

(\tau^{\st})^{*}L\ \cong\ {\mathcal{O}}_{Y}(m(K_{Y}+B_{Y}+\bfM))

for some
m>0
.
Let
0<|\epsilon|\ll 1
and
\tau\colon(X,(1+\epsilon)D)\to(X^{\prime},(1+\epsilon)D^{\prime})
be a contraction of an extremal ray. Then there is a morphism
\pi^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\to Y^{st}
and
m(K_{X^{\prime}}+D^{\prime})\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}(\pi^{\prime})^{*}L
. In particular,
\tau:(X,D)\rightarrow(X^{\prime},D^{\prime})
is crepant.

Proof.

First observe that any curve contracted by
\tau
is also contracted by
\tau^{\st}\circ\pi
, as
L
is ample,
|\epsilon|
is small, and

\textstyle K_{X}+(1+\epsilon)D\ \sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\ \pi^{*}(K_{Y}+B_{Y}+\bfM)+\epsilon D\ \sim_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\ \frac{1}{m}\pi^{*}(\tau^{st})^{*}L+\epsilon D.

Therefore, there is a morphism
\pi^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\to Y^{\st}
. As
\tau
is a birational contraction, and

\textstyle K_{X}+D\ \sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\ \pi^{*}(K_{X}+B_{Y}+\bfM)\ \sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\ \frac{1}{m}\pi^{*}(\tau^{st})^{*}L,

then the two
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Cartier divisors
K_{X^{\prime}}+D^{\prime}
and
\frac{1}{m}(\tau^{\st})^{*}L
agree on the normal variety
X^{\prime}
.
∎

3.
Minimal models for fibered log Calabi-Yau pairs

The main objective of this section is to establish the following two results.
First, we show that if
\pi\colon(X,B_{X})\to Y
is a fibration whose fibers are log Calabi-Yau pairs, and if a step of the MMP

Y\dashrightarrow Y^{\prime}
for a generalized pair on
Y
(induced by
\pi
) is given, then this step can be followed by a corresponding step of the MMP

X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
as stated in
3.2
.

We then apply this to the case where the fibration arises from a stable quasimap
{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathscr{P}}_{n}

over the spectrum of a DVR
R
. In this setting, we obtain a structural description of the morphism
X^{\prime}\to Y^{\prime}
in terms of ruled surfaces; see
Theorem

3.13
.

3.1.
An MMP for fibrations in log Calabi-Yau pairs

In this subsection, we establish certain results concerning fibrations in log Calabi–Yau pairs. Roughly speaking, we show that if

\pi\colon(X,B_{X})\ \longrightarrow\ (Y,B+\mathbf{M})

is a fibration of a log Calabi-Yau pair onto a generalized pair
(Y,B+\mathbf{M})
satisfying

\pi^{*}(K_{Y}+B+\mathbf{M})\ \sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\ K_{X}+B_{X},

then, under mild assumptions, any MMP for
(Y,B+\mathbf{M})
can be accompanied by a birational modification for
(X,B_{X})
such that each step of the MMP for
(Y,B+\mathbf{M})
still admits a fibration in log Calabi–Yau pairs given by a birational model of
(X,B_{X})
; see
3.2
.

3.2.
From quasimaps limit to KSBA-limit

In this subsection, we will use the moduli space
\mathcal{Q}
discussed in
Section

2
to construct certain weak canonical models that will allow us to control limits in the KSBA-moduli space of stable pairs. More specifically, consider a moduli space of surface pairs
(X,\frac{1}{n+1}D)
admitting a fibration

\textstyle(X,\frac{1}{n+1}D)\ \longrightarrow\ C

with fibers isomorphic to log canonical Calabi-Yau pairs of the form
({\mathbb{P}}^{1},\frac{1}{n+1}\Delta)
.
Two examples which are worth keeping in mind are the following:

(1)

the (non-compact) moduli space of
({\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1},\frac{1}{n+1}D)
where
D
is a generic divisor of class
(2n,k)
with
k>2n
,

(2)

the (non-compact) moduli space of Hirzebruch surface pairs
(\mathbb{F}_{d},\frac{1}{n+1}D)
, where
D
the union of the negative section
\mathfrak{e}
and a multisection not containing
\mathfrak{e}
of the class
(2n-1)\mathfrak{e}+(2n-1)d\mathfrak{f}
with
\mathfrak{f}
the class of the fiber of
\mathbb{F}_{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
.

When
n=1
, pairs
(X,\frac{1}{2}D)
as in the second example appear as the quotient by the hyperelliptic involution of an elliptic surface
Y\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
.

The goal of this section is to establish a few general results on the KSBA-compactification of these moduli spaces, using the moduli space
\mathcal{Q}
. On a first approximation, we will use the results of
Section

3.1
on the total space of a family of surface pairs corresponding to a one-parameter family in
\mathcal{Q}
.

For doing so, the first step is to guarantee that such a one-parameter family has the correct singularities, when we replace the coefficient of the divisor from
\frac{1}{n+1}
with
\frac{1}{n+1}+\epsilon
(
Lemma

3.5
), and we add to the divisor some of the fibers with small coefficient (
Corollary

3.6
).

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
R
be a DVR,
{\mathscr{C}}\to\text{\rm Spec}R
a family of twisted curves, and let
\pi\colon({\mathscr{X}},c{\mathscr{D}})\to{\mathscr{C}}
a proper morphism of Deligne-Mumford stacks, with
({\mathscr{X}},c{\mathscr{D}})
with slc singularities where
0<c<1
is a rational number. Assume that

(1)

{\mathscr{D}}
is a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Cartier divisor;

(2)

for every point
x\in{\mathscr{C}}_{\eta}
(resp.
x\in{\mathscr{C}}_{0}
) contained in the smooth locus of the family of twisted curves
{\mathscr{C}}\to\text{\rm Spec}R
,

\operatorname{lct}({\mathscr{X}}_{\eta},c{\mathscr{D}}_{\eta};\pi^{-1}(x))>0
(resp.
\operatorname{lct}({\mathscr{X}}_{0},c{\mathscr{D}}_{0};\pi^{-1}(x))>0
); and

(3)

there is an
0<\epsilon\ll 1
such that
({\mathscr{X}},(c+\epsilon){\mathscr{D}})\to{\mathscr{C}}
is locally stable, except possibly along a proper closed subscheme
\Delta\subseteq{\mathscr{C}}
contained in the smooth locus of each fiber of
{\mathscr{C}}\to\text{\rm Spec}R
.

Let
(X,c{D})
be the coarse moduli space of
({\mathscr{X}},c{\mathscr{D}})
. Then
K_{X}
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Cartier, and the pair
(X,(c+\epsilon){D}+X_{0})
is slc, where
0<\epsilon\ll 1
. In particular, if the generic fiber
(X_{\eta},cD_{\eta})
is klt and
\pi
is localy stable, then also
(X,(c+\epsilon){D})
is klt for
0<\epsilon\ll 1
.

Condition (
2
) is automatically satisfied if either
\pi
is locally stable, or if it is a log Calabi-Yau fibration and the boundary part in the canonical bundle formula appears with coefficient less than 1 along
{\mathscr{C}}_{\eta}
and
{\mathscr{C}}_{0}
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that
({\mathscr{X}},(c+\epsilon){\mathscr{D}}+{\mathscr{X}}_{0})
is slc. This follows as being slc is an étale local property, and the map
{\mathscr{X}}\to X
étale locally is the quotient by a finite group, so
({\mathscr{X}},(c+\epsilon){\mathscr{D}}+{\mathscr{X}}_{0})
being slc is equivalent to
(X,(c+\epsilon){D}+X_{0})
being slc.

By adjunction, it suffices to prove that
({\mathscr{X}}_{0},(c+\epsilon){\mathscr{D}}_{0})
is slc. By assumption and adjunction, the pair
({\mathscr{X}}_{0},(c+\epsilon){\mathscr{D}}_{0})
is slc, except possibly along the fiber
\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}\}
of finitely many smooth points
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}\subseteq{\mathscr{C}}
; we now show it is slc over each point
x_{i}
.

The pair
({\mathscr{X}}_{0},(c+\epsilon){\mathscr{D}}_{0})
is not slc if and only if there is a divisor
E
whose center is on the fiber
({\mathscr{X}}_{0})_{x_{j}}
for some
j
, such that the discrepancy
a(E;{\mathscr{X}}_{0},(c+\epsilon){\mathscr{D}}_{0})<-1
.
However, by condition
2
one has
a(E;{\mathscr{X}}_{0},c{\mathscr{D}}_{0}+\epsilon^{\prime}F_{j})\geq-1
for
0<\epsilon^{\prime}\ll 1
. As
F_{j}
is a Cartier divisor which contains the center of
E
on
\mathscr{X}_{0}
, then
\ord_{E}(F_{j})\geq 1
and consequently

-1\ \leq\ a(E;{\mathscr{X}}_{0},c{\mathscr{D}}_{0}+\epsilon^{\prime}F_{j})\ =\ a(E;{\mathscr{X}}_{0},c{\mathscr{D}}_{0})-\epsilon^{\prime}\mult_{E}(F_{j})\ \leq\ a(E;{\mathscr{X}}_{0},c{\mathscr{D}}_{0})-\epsilon^{\prime},

and hence
a(E;{\mathscr{X}}_{0},c{\mathscr{D}}_{0})>-1
. Therefore, for any
0<\epsilon\ll 1
we have that

a(E;{\mathscr{X}}_{0},(c+\epsilon){\mathscr{D}}_{0})\ >\ -1

as desired.

The last part of the statement about klt follows from
[
Kol23
, Theorem 2.80]
: for locally stable families over a smooth curve, the log-canonical center of the total space is flat over the base. Therefore, if
(X_{\eta},cD_{\eta})
is klt, then
(X_{\eta},(c+\epsilon)D_{\eta}+\epsilon F_{\eta})
is also klt for
0<\epsilon\ll 1
, and hence so is
(X,(c+\epsilon)D+\epsilon F)
.
∎

The following lemma is an useful observation which we will use the describe some of the objects on the boundary of

.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
({\mathscr{X}},\frac{1}{n+1}{\mathscr{D}})\xrightarrow{\pi}{\mathscr{C}}\to\text{\rm Spec}R
be as in Lemma

, and assume that for each point

3.2.1.
The ruled-model

From
Section

2
there is a retraction morphism

\xi\colon\ {\mathscr{P}}_{n}\ \longrightarrow\ {\mathscr{P}}_{n}^{\GIT}

which, at the level of points, is given by

\textstyle\big[(C,\frac{1}{n+1}D)\big]\ \ \mapsto\ \ \begin{cases}\big[(C,\frac{1}{n+1}D)\big]\ \ &\textup{if }C\simeq{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\\
&\\
\big[({\mathbb{P}}^{1},(n+1)[0]+(n+1)[\infty])\big]\ \ &\textup{if }C\textup{ is nodal}.\end{cases}

We will use this morphism to define the
ruled model
of a surface pair
(X,\frac{1}{n+1}D)\to C
over a curve

. The ruled model will be the main tool which will allow us to control the KSBA-stable limits of certain ruled surfaces as the beginning of this section, without running explicitly any MMP.

Definition 3.8
.

(ref. Figure

)

(1)

C

R\subseteq C^{(0)}
is contracted via
C^{(0)}\to C^{(i)}
.
As the degree of
\bfM^{(i)}
remains invariant through this MMP,

\deg(\bfM^{(i)}|_{W})=\deg(\bfM^{(0)}|_{W^{(0)}\cup T})

where we denoted by
W^{(0)}\subseteq C^{(0)}
the proper transform of
W
in
C^{(0)}
, and
T
the tails of rational curves of
C^{(i)}
which contract to
W
via
C^{(0)}\to C^{(i)}
. From how the family
(X^{(0)},\frac{1}{n+1}D^{(0)})
is constructed (i.e. from a map to
{\mathscr{P}}_{n}
), we have that
\bfM^{(0)}|_{W^{(0)}}
agrees with the moduli part
of
(X^{(0)},\frac{1}{n+1}D^{(0)})|_{W^{(0)}})
. Then from the canonical bundle formula, the degree of the boundary part
p
for
(X^{(i)},\frac{1}{n+1}D^{(i)})|_{W}\to W
agrees with
\deg(\bfM^{(0)}|_{T_{p}})
where
T_{p}
are the chain of rational curves of
C^{(0)}
mapping to
p
, as in
Figure

2
.
Then observe that the MMP never contracts tails which are chain of rational curves where
\deg(\bfM^{(i-1)}|_{T})=1
, as in this case
\deg(K_{C^{(i-1)}}+\bfM^{(i-1)}|_{T})
would be zero so
K_{C}{(i-1)}+\bfM^{(i-1)}|_{T}
would be already nef. So the boundary part in the canonical bundle formula for
(X^{(i)},\frac{1}{n+1}D^{(i)})|_{G}
at
p
is less than 1.

End of the argument
. We prove that the pair
\left(Z^{(i)},(\frac{1}{n+1}+\epsilon)D_{Z}^{(i)}\right)
is minimal over
C^{(i)}
for
0<\epsilon\ll 1
, and we finish the argument.

The fibers of
Z^{(i)}\to C^{(i)}
are one-dimensional, as the divisors extracted map to horizontal curves in
Y^{(i)}
, and

\textstyle K_{Z^{(i)}}+\big(\frac{1}{n+1}+\epsilon\big)\sim_{{\mathbb{Q}},C^{(i)}}\epsilon D_{Z}^{(i)}.

Any effective divisor on a (possibly singular) curve is nef so our pair is minimal over
C^{(i)}
.

We proved that the pair

\textstyle\big(Z^{(i)},(\frac{1}{n+1}+\epsilon)D_{Z}^{(i)}+Z^{(i)}_{0}\big)

is log canonical and minimal over
C^{(i)}
, so one can take its canonical model, which will be a morphism rather than a rational map. As the KSBA-moduli space is separated, the morphism which takes the canonical model
(Z^{(i)},(\frac{1}{n+1}+\epsilon)D_{Z}^{(i)})
over
C^{(i)}
agrees with the pair
(X^{(i)},(\frac{1}{n+1}+\epsilon)D^{(i)})
, and
the canonical model
Z^{(i)}\to X^{(i)}
contracts
\widetilde{E}_{j}
as desired.
∎

.

With the notations of Theorem
3.13
, if

is an irreducible component of the special fiber
C_{0}^{(i)}
of

such that the generic fiber of

.

Corollary 3.15
.

Let
(X^{(k)},\frac{1}{n+1}D^{(k)}+cF^{(k)})\to\Spec R
be as in Diagram (

). Assume

•

for each point
p\in C_{\eta}

\textstyle\big(X^{(k)},(\frac{1}{n+1}+\epsilon)D^{(k)}+c^{\prime}F^{(k)}\big)\ \dashrightarrow\ \big(X^{\prime},(\frac{1}{n+1}+\epsilon)D^{\prime}+c^{\prime}F^{\prime}\big)

such that

.

In other terms, as long as there is no divisorial contraction on the family of curves going from

to

, if one fixes

and decreases the coefficient of

(1)

first running an MMP rescaling

4.

In this section, we apply the results of Section §
3
to describe the objects on the boundary of the KSBA compactification of certain moduli spaces of surface pairs.

Given a normal and irreducible moduli stack
{\mathcal{U}}
of surface pairs
(X,D)
admitting a non-isotrivial fibration
(X,D)\to C
over a smooth curve
C
, satisfying that

•

the fibers of
X\to C
are
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
s;

•

the restriction of
D
on each fiber
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
is
\sum n_{i}p_{i}
, where
p_{i}
are distinct points,
0\leq n_{i}\leq n+1
are integers such that
\sum n_{i}=2n+2
;

•

the generic fiber of
(X,\frac{1}{n+1}D)\to C
is klt, i.e.
n_{i}\leq n
; and

•

the pair
\big(X,(\frac{1}{n+1}+\epsilon_{1})D+(\vec{c}+\epsilon_{2})F\big)
is KSBA-stable for any
0<\epsilon_{1}\ll\epsilon_{2}\ll 1
, where
F
are some general fibers of
X\to C
marked with a weight vector
\vec{c}
with
0\leq\vec{c}\leq 1
.

One can keep in mind the following two examples:

(1)

the moduli space parametrizing
({\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1},\frac{1}{n}C)
with
C
a general
(2n,m)
-curve, with
2n\leq m
and
F=\emptyset
,

(2)

the moduli space parametrizing Hirzebruch surfaces
X
which are quotients of elliptic K3 surfaces by an involution, with
D
the ramification locus.

As the coefficient vector
\vec{c}
does not play any significant role in what follows, we will suppress it.
For any
\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}>0
such that
\big(X,(\frac{1}{n+1}+\epsilon_{1})D+\epsilon_{2}F\big)
is KSBA-stable for a general point
[(X,D)]\in{\mathcal{U}}({\mathbb{C}})
, by possibly shrinking
{\mathcal{U}}
, one has a morphism

\Phi_{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}}\colon{\mathcal{U}}\ \longrightarrow\ {\mathcal{M}}^{\KSBA}_{n}(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})

to the KSBA-moduli stack with fixed numerical invariants and marked coefficients
(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})
. We denote by
\overline{{\mathcal{U}}}^{\KSBA}(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})
the closure of its scheme-theoretic image.

One can collect all admissible coefficients
(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})
together to get a
2
-dimensional domain. By
[
ABIP23
,
MZ23
]
, up to passing to the normalization of
\overline{{\mathcal{U}}}^{\KSBA}(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})
, there are wall-crossing morphisms so that one can compare the normalizations of different compactifications; see
Figure

9
.

In Section §
4.1
, we will apply the results in Section §
3
to study the wall crossing along the red path from the purple point to the pink point in

.

4.1.
KSBA-moduli space with a marked multi-section

Let

\textstyle({\mathscr{X}},{\mathscr{D}})\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\ {\mathscr{C}}\ \longrightarrow\ {\mathcal{U}}

be the universal family over
{\mathcal{U}}
. By
Remark

2.6
, there is an intrinsic Cartier divisor
\Delta\subseteq{\mathscr{C}}
. whose support consists of points
p
such that fibers of
\pi|_{{\mathscr{D}}}:{\mathscr{D}}\rightarrow{\mathscr{C}}
over
p
are supported by less than
2n+2
points, i.e. where at least two points collide. We denote by
\Delta_{u}
the restriction of
\Delta
on the fiber over
u\in{\mathcal{U}}
. Then by
Corollary

3.6
the family

\textstyle\left({\mathscr{X}},(\frac{1}{n+1}+\epsilon_{1}){\mathscr{D}}+\epsilon_{2}\pi^{-1}(\Delta)\right)\ \longrightarrow\ {\mathcal{U}}

is a family of KSBA-stable pairs for any
0<\epsilon_{1}\ll\epsilon_{2}\ll 1
. The following result, in particular, verifies that

\overline{{\mathcal{U}}}^{\KSBA}\ :=\ \overline{{\mathcal{U}}}^{\KSBA}(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})

does not depend on the choice of
0<\epsilon_{1}\ll\epsilon_{2}\ll 1
, which justifies our notation.

Theorem 4.1
.

Keep the notations as above. Assume one of the following holds:

•

either
n=1
; or

•

there exists a point
u\in{\mathcal{U}}({\mathbb{C}})
such that the all fibers of
\big({\mathscr{X}}_{u},(\frac{1}{n+1}+\epsilon_{1}){\mathscr{D}}_{u}\big)\to{\mathscr{C}}_{u}
over
\Delta_{u}
are slc.

Then the surface pairs
(X,(\frac{1}{n+1}+\epsilon_{1})D+\epsilon_{2}F)
parametrized by
\overline{{\mathcal{U}}}^{\KSBA}

(1)

admit a ruled model (ref.
3.8
), and

(2)

do not depend on the choice of
\epsilon_{i}
, as long as
0<\epsilon_{1}\ll\epsilon_{2}\ll 1
.

Proof.

The results hold for the pair represented by the geometric generic point
\eta
of
\overline{{\mathcal{U}}}^{\KSBA}
. It suffices to prove the theorem for any stable limit of
\eta
over
\Spec R
for a DVR
R
. We first construct a limit using moduli of stable quasimaps, where the discriminant divisor
\Delta
extends to the smooth locus.

If
n=1
, consider the morphism
{\mathcal{U}}\to\mathcal{Q}_{g,0;1,\beta}
, and take an arbitrary limit of
{\mathscr{C}}_{\eta}
in
\mathcal{Q}_{g,0;1,\beta}
, denoted by
{\mathscr{C}}_{u}
. By
Lemma

2.5
, the limit of
\Delta_{\eta}
over any one-parameter family is supported at those points whose images to
{\mathscr{P}}_{1}
represent pairs with divisor supported at most three points. When
n=1
, those points are not in
{\mathscr{P}}_{1,{\operatorname{DM}}}
, and hence the support of the limit of
\Delta_{\eta}
is contained in the smooth locus of
{\mathscr{C}}_{u}
by the stable quasimap condition (Sing.).

When
n>1
, this argument does not work, as the stable quasimap condition does not guarantee that the limit of
\Delta_{\eta}
is contained in the smooth locus of
{\mathscr{C}}_{u}
, since there are curves with two points colliding in
{\mathscr{P}}_{n,{\operatorname{DM}}}
for
n>1
. Instead, we use
pointed
stable quasimaps as follows.
By our assumptions, there is an étale morphism
{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{U}}
with
{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
such that:

•

the cardinality of the points of
\Delta_{{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}}
is constant,

•

the support of
\Delta_{{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}}
consists of disjoint sections
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m}
, and

•

the fibers of
({\mathscr{X}},(\frac{1}{n+1}+\epsilon){\mathscr{D}})|_{{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}}\to{\mathscr{C}}|_{{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}}
are slc over
\sigma_{i}
for every
i
.

These data yield a morphism

\Phi^{(q)}\colon{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}\ \longrightarrow\ \mathcal{Q}_{g,m;n,\beta}

for a certain choice of
\beta
. Then by definition, the support of the limit of
\Delta_{\eta}
in
\mathcal{Q}_{g,m;n,\beta}
is contained in the smooth locus, as desired.

Now, let
R
be a DVR as above,

\textstyle\left({\mathscr{Y}},{\mathscr{D}}_{\mathscr{Y}}\right)\ \longrightarrow\ ({\mathscr{C}},x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\ \longrightarrow\ \text{\rm Spec}R

be the stable quasimap limit construct as above, where
m=0
if
n=1
, and let

\textstyle\left(Y,D_{Y}\right)\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\ (C,x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\ \longrightarrow\ \text{\rm Spec}R

be the coarse spaces. Denote by
\eta
and
u
the generic point and the closed point of
\Spec R
respectively as above. As the limit of
\pi^{-1}(\Delta_{u})
, which we denote by
F
, will not intersect the double locus
of the central fiber of
Y\to\text{\rm Spec}R
, then the pair
(Y,(\frac{1}{n+1}+\epsilon_{1})D_{Y}+\epsilon_{2}F)
satisfied the assumptions of
3.11
. Therefore, we can take a relative weak canonical model of
\big(Y,(\frac{1}{n+1}+\epsilon_{1})D_{Y}+\epsilon_{2}F\big)
over
\Spec R
as in Section §
3.2
. Then we may assume claim that
\big(Y,(\frac{1}{n+1}+\epsilon_{1})D_{Y}+\epsilon_{2}F\big)\rightarrow\Spec R
is already a weak canonical model, and we claim that it is a canonical model for
0<\epsilon_{1}\ll\epsilon_{2}\ll 1
.

Let
(C,\epsilon_{2}\Delta^{\prime}+\bfM)
be the generalized pair defined by the canonical bundle formula for
(Y,\frac{1}{n+1}D_{Y}+\epsilon_{2}F)
. By
Corollary

3.12
, it suffices to check that
K_{C}+\epsilon_{2}\Delta^{\prime}+\bfM
is ample. Notice that it is already nef, and the only possible components of
C_{u}
, the restriction of
K_{C}+\epsilon_{2}\Delta^{\prime}+\bfM
to which is not ample, are

(1)

either a rational bridge
R
, or

(2)

a rational tail
T
.

From the stability condition (Stab.) for stable quasimaps, a rational bridge
R
must contain a point in the closure of
\Delta_{\eta}
; this addresses case (1) as
\epsilon_{2}>0
. By
Lemma

2.15
,
\Delta
intersects every rational tail at the beginning of the MMP, and hence the same holds at the end of the MMP. As
K_{C}+\epsilon_{2}\Delta^{\prime}+\bfM
is nef for any
0<\epsilon_{2}\ll 1
and
\Delta^{\prime}
intersects every rational bridge, then the restriction of
K_{C}+\epsilon_{2}\Delta^{\prime}+\bfM
must be ample for every
0<\epsilon_{2}\ll 1
as desired.
∎

Fix
g
and
\beta
as above. Assume the following condition holds: for every point in
\mathcal{Q}_{g,0;1,\beta}
parametrizing maps
C\to{\mathscr{P}}_{1}
from a smooth curve such that for every surface pair
(X,\frac{1}{2}D)\to C
corresponding to a point in
{\mathcal{U}}
, there exist
0<\epsilon_{1}\ll\epsilon_{2}
such that

Corollary 4.2
.

There exists a choice of
0<\epsilon_{1}\ll\epsilon_{2}\ll 1
, and a natural morphism

\Phi_{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}}\colon\mathcal{Q}_{g,0;1,\beta}^{\norm}\ \longrightarrow\ {\mathcal{M}}^{\KSBA}_{2}(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})

from the normalization of
\mathcal{Q}_{g,0;1,\beta}
, which generically is given by

\textstyle\big[(X,\frac{1}{2}D)\to C\big]\ \mapsto\ \big[(X,(\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon_{1})D+\epsilon_{2}\pi^{-1}(\Delta_{C}))\big].

\Phi_{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}}
do not depend on

as long as

Proof.

This follows immediately from wall-crossings for KSBA-stable pairs of

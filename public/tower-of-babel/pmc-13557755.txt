Impact of an Opioid Avoidance Protocol for ACL Reconstruction: A Pilot Sequential Cohort Study
Authors: Matthew Varacallo, Samantha Morgan, Christopher Varacallo, Jared Ham
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Anterior cruciate ligament reconstructions (ACLRs) are commonly performed in healthy, opioid-naive patients aged ≤29 years, yet up to 6% of patients undergoing ACLR become persistent opioid users for 90 days after surgery. Multimodal analgesia (MMA) protocols have recently been implemented, but opioids remain a primary rescue medication, with surgeons accounting for 73% of opioids prescribed.Purpose:To determine whether an opioid avoidance protocol (OAP) including cryoneurolysis could eliminate or markedly reduce opioid use without compromising pain control after ACLR.Study design:This was a pilot, unblinded, sequential cohort study of patients aged ≥15 years undergoing ACLR. Level of evidence: II.1.Methods:The standard of care (SOC) protocol group received oral analgesics, regional anesthesia, and rescue opioid therapy, whereas the OAP group received cryoneurolysis 7 to 21 days before surgery as well as oral analgesics and regional anesthesia, with opioids prescribed upon request. The primary end point was opioid use in morphine milligram equivalents (MMEs) from postoperative day (POD) 0 to POD 7, with secondary end points of daily worst and average pain, Quality of Recovery 15 (QoR-15) scores, opioid-free status, Knee Injury and Osteoarthritis Outcome Score (KOOS) pain and symptom subscales, and opioid prescriptions.Results:No notable differences were found in patient characteristics at baseline. Patients in the OAP group used markedly fewer MMEs postoperatively versus the SOC group (35 vs 172.5). No notable differences were observed in worst or average pain at any time. The OAP group had markedly improved KOOS symptom scores at week 8.Conclusion:This study is the first to use an MMA protocol incorporating cryoneurolysis 7 to 21 days before ACLR and to demonstrate notable reductions in opioid use without sacrificing pain control, but opioid needs were not entirely eliminated. The OAP is a step toward preventing opioid use disorder in young patients and can be supplemented with additional modalities to further reduce or eliminate opioid use.

Approximately 100,000 to 200,000 anterior cruciate ligament reconstructions (ACLRs) are performed yearly in the United States, with most patients being <29 years old.1 Despite the generally good overall health and lack of opioid use in this patient population at baseline, ∼3% to 6% of patients undergoing ACLR will become persistent opioid users who still use opioids 90 days after surgical repair.2 The orthopedic surgeon is responsible for approximately 73% of opioid prescriptions for patients undergoing ACLR, presenting a critical opportunity for intervention.2 Because these patients are generally young, healthy, opioid-naive, and are managed by a single surgeon for their injury, the ACLR population represents a unique opportunity for the US healthcare system to implement primary prevention protocols for opioid disorders rather than provide treatment after development.3,4

In the past 10 to 15 years, surgeons and hospitals have adopted multimodal analgesia (MMA) protocols to manage postoperative pain. These regimens employ multiple analgesic medications that act through different mechanisms, but the ability to fully eliminate opioids has not been demonstrated.5-7 MMA protocols have notable variety in component medications, and dosing between geographic locations, hospitals, and providers, and postoperative pain remains one of the most common complications, thus necessitating opioid use.4-9 Recently, some MMA protocols have been bolstered by preoperative medical procedures such as radiofrequency ablation or cryoneurolysis in addition to pharmacologic agents to increase the degree of analgesia.7,10,11,12 Cryoneurolysis involves applying extreme cold temperatures (−20°C to −100°C) to peripheral sensory nerves to induce Wallerian degeneration of nerve axons, resulting in a long-acting, drug-free nerve block.13,14 The utility of cryoneurolysis has been investigated in the preoperative period to provide long-term pain relief after joint replacement procedures with minimal adverse events; however, ACLR has not been evaluated.7,10,11,15 Recently, van Deventer et al. published their results after implementing “a modern MMA protocol” in total knee arthroplasty (TKA) that incorporated percutaneous cryoneurolysis with a handheld device five days before surgery, which showed that 72% of opioid-naive patients were able to recover opioid free through postoperative day (POD) 90 while presenting medical challenges typically not associated with patients undergoing ACLR.16

Therefore, investigators aimed to determine whether an opioid avoidance protocol (OAP) including cryoneurolysis could eliminate or markedly reduce opioid use without compromising pain control after ACLR. We hypothesized that implementation of the OAP would achieve opioid-sparing outcomes comparable to those observed in the TKA population.16

Methods

Study Design and Patient Criteria

This study was approved in March 2024 by a central institutional review board and registered at ClinicalTrials.gov before the first patient was enrolled. This was a pilot, unblinded, sequential cohort study of patients who underwent ACLR by a single surgeon. The level of evidence of this study design is II.1. The study recruitment period was between April 2024 and December 2024. Because this was a cohort study of whole protocols, it was not possible to randomize patients; however, groups were enrolled via written informed consent of strict inclusion criteria to avoid convenience sampling. Additional methods used to reduce bias included standardization of protocols, discharge instructions, and discharge materials; protocolized outreach to avoid nonresponse bias with paper surveys (visit timing, phone calls); and use of standardized and validated instruments to collect patient outcomes (Quality of Recovery 15 [QoR-15] and Knee Injury and Osteoarthritis Outcome Score [KOOS]).

Eligible patients were ≥15 years old with a primary diagnosis of ACL tear, with or without associated meniscal involvement, and scheduled to undergo a quadriceps tendon soft-tissue autograft ACL reconstruction that could follow the study visit schedule. Additional inclusion criteria included the ability to speak, read, and write in English to allow for assessment after surgery and completion of patient diaries. Finally, patients had to provide informed consent, or if <18 years old, a parent or guardian was required to provide informed consent in addition to patient assent.

Patients were excluded if they possessed an allergy/intolerance/hypersensitivity or contraindication to any study medication, a history of contralateral ACLR within 1 year of screening, or a multiligament injury requiring reconstruction; were unable to return for postoperative visits; were pregnant through urine pregnancy screening or planning to become pregnant during the study period; were breastfeeding; had a severe chronic pain condition that in the opinion of the surgeon would affect postsurgical outcomes and medication use; were contraindicated to undergo cryoneurolysis treatment; had uncontrolled mental illness that in the opinion of the surgeon could affect study outcomes; or had a history, suspicion, or clinical manifestation of substance use disorder (alcohol, illicit drugs, or opioids).

Treatment Groups

Patients were assigned to cohorts based on the date of surgery and the perioperative pain management protocol in use at that time. The standard of care (SOC) group underwent surgery between April 1, 2024, and September 11, 2024, whereas the OAP group underwent surgery between September 12, 2024, and December 10, 2024. The SOC group received the existing perioperative pain management protocol (Table 1). The OAP group received an expanded MMA protocol that included percutaneous cryoneurolysis via iovera° (Pacira CryoTech Inc., Fremont, CA) that exposed the nerves to cold temperatures (−88°C) to cause Wallerian degeneration of the nerve axons and myelin sheath.11 The treatment was done by a single nonsurgical provider 7 to 21 days before the ACLR procedure, which is consistent with the institutional standard of care for TKA. The treatment targeted five sensory nerves (intermediate branch of the anterior femoral cutaneous nerve, medial femoral cutaneous nerve, suprapatellar branch of the saphenous nerve, and two branches of the infrapatellar saphenous nerve). In addition, patients were provided with an oral MMA regimen (Table 1). Prescriptions for oxycodone hydrochloride and tramadol hydrochloride were provided only upon patient or family request. Both cohorts received standardized instructions for nonpharmacologic pain management through an ice machine or ice packs and elevation of the ankle. Nursing staff members were provided with scripts based on patient cohort to standardize the verbal discharge instructions for pain management after discharge.

All patients could request additional opioid pain medications as needed at any time by contacting the surgeon's office by phone or the study coordinator. All requests were routed to a single surgical team member to respond on the basis of standardized prescriptions per the surgeon's instructions. Patients were provided with instructions on postoperative medications through a standardized medication calendar and pain diary for each group (Supplemental Digital Content: Figure S1, https://links.lww.com/JG9/A568). In addition, patients were provided with information sheets on nonpharmacologic interventions for pain control.

Measures

The study included a screening visit to determine eligibility, a preoperative treatment visit for the OAP group, a surgical visit, a phone call on POD 2, and two office follow-ups. The Pennsylvania Prescription Monitoring Program database was electronically queried to identify opioid prescriptions and opioid-free status at POD 30 and POD 60. The study followed patients for a maximum of 84 days (21 days preoperatively to the latest possible date for a postoperative week-eight visit of day 63 [56 + 7 days]).

Data collected from patient medical records included body mass index (BMI), age, sex, ethnicity, smoking status, American Society of Anesthesiologists physical status classification (ASA score), the surgery performed, baseline pain score through numerical rating scale (NRS), and KOOS pain and symptom subscales.17–19 Opioid use including the number of tablets of tramadol hydrochloride and oxycodone hydrochloride was collected through the patient medication schedule and diary, which was used to calculate the morphine milligram equivalents (MMEs) for POD 0 to 7. MMEs were calculated through a standardized conversion for the two opioid medications of 1 mg of oxycodone hydrochloride to 1.5 MMEs and 1 mg of tramadol hydrochloride to 0.1 MME. Daily average NRS pain and daily worst NRS pain were collected through the patient diary. The QoR-15 was collected through a telephone interview on POD 2.20

The KOOS pain subscale and symptom subscale raw scores were transformed into a scale ranging from 0 to 100, with 0 = extreme disease burden and 100 = no disease burden.

Because of the outpatient nature of the study, a proactive plan for missing data was implemented including reminders from office staff before appointments and standardized follow-up attempts in nonresponsive patients. Missing data were accounted for through worst-case imputation by recording any missing data as the worst outcome. For MMEs, any missing data were recorded as using the entirety of the filled opioid prescriptions. For all other end points, the values were input as the worst (highest or lowest, depending on outcome scale) value recorded for the outcome in the patient's cohort.

Statistical Analyses

Data were analyzed using Stata SE 18.0 (StataCorp LLC, College Station, TX). Because there were no existing data detailing the effect of multimodal analgesia with cryoneurolysis in ACLR, a sample size calculation could not be adequately performed for this pilot study. Owing to the inability to calculate power, a convenience sample of 40 patients was planned with 20 patients in each group; however, because of the study timeline, the institutional review board approved a protocol amendment submitted on August 1, 2024, to adjust the sample size to 15 patients in the SOC group and 15 patients in the OAP group.

Baseline characteristics were compared between the groups (SOC vs. OAP) using Pearson's chi-square test for categorical variables. Continuous baseline characteristics were analyzed visually and through the Shapiro-Wilk test to determine normality. Because variables were non-normally distributed, analysis was conducted through Kruskal-Wallis test and reported as the median and interquartile range (IQR).

The primary study end point was the quantity of opioid use in MMEs from POD 0 to 7 between groups. To address the nonrandomized nature of the study, a linear regression was done in a reverse-stepwise fashion to identify and control for confounding variables within the data. Specific variables that were identified before analysis for inclusion in the model were sex and meniscal involvement.

Secondary end points included the daily average NRS pain score from POD 0 to 7, the daily worst NRS pain from POD 0 to 7, the normalized KOOS pain and symptom subscales at week eight after surgery, QoR-15 score on POD 2, and the percentage of patients who were opioid-free at POD 7, POD 30, and POD 60. Continuous secondary outcomes were analyzed via Kruskal-Wallis test, whereas categorical secondary outcomes were analyzed via chi-square test. A logistic regression analysis for opioid-free status at POD 7, POD 30, and POD 60 was planned but was unable to be completed because of the lack of opioid-free participants in the SOC group.

Safety end points are reported numerically through counts for overall incidence of adverse events (yes/no) among patients and then for each individual adverse event type (yes/no) between groups.

Primary Outcome and Opioid Use

The median opioid use for patients from POD 0 to 7 was significantly lower (P < 0.001) in the OAP cohort (35 [IQR, 0-88] MMEs) compared with the SOC cohort (172.5 [IQR, 93-275] MMEs) (Table 3). Daily opioid use was significantly lower on POD 0 (P < 0.001), POD 1 (P < 0.001), POD 2 (P < 0.001), and markedly lower on POD 3 (P = 0.023) in the OAP group compared with the SOC group (Table 3; Figure 2). No differences were found between cohorts on any of POD 4 to 7. A reverse-stepwise, multivariable, linear regression analysis was conducted to predict total postoperative opioid use from POD 0 to 7 from demographic criteria, meniscal involvement, and the independent protocol variable. The model was prespecified to include sex and the independent variable of group, whereas all other covariates were tested for predictive value. Presurgical NRS pain, ASA score, smoking status, ethnicity, and age were removed from the model owing to lack of predictive value. A final multivariable linear regression model was developed to predict opioid use during POD 0 to 7 from the independent protocol variable, sex, BMI, and meniscal involvement; the results of this model were statistically significant (F[4,25] = 10.38, P < 0.0001) and explained 62.4% of the variance in opioid use (R2 = 0.624; Table 4). After controlling for all other variables, inclusion in the OAP group (t = −5.44, P < 0.001), BMI (t = 2.77, P = 0.01), and meniscal involvement (t = 2.34, P = 0.028) were found to be significant predictors, whereas sex was not a significant predictor (t = −0.64, P = 0.528).

The KOOS pain subscale at eight weeks after surgery was numerically higher in the OAP compared with the SOC group (94.4 vs 80.6; P = 0.110) but did not meet statistical significance (Table 3). Patients in the OAP group did have significantly improved KOOS function subscale at 8 weeks after surgery compared with the SOC group (82.1 vs 64.3; P = 0.042, Table 3). The QoR-15 scores on POD 2 were not significantly different between the OAP and SOC groups (112 vs 103; P = 0.340, Table 3). No patients were opioid free at any of the three time points (POD 7, POD 30, POD 60) in the SOC group versus six patients in the OAP group (0% vs 40%; P = 0.006), so logistic regression could not be completed.

Safety Outcomes

Adverse events during the study follow-up period were generally mild to moderate in severity. Six patients in the SOC group reported unexpected adverse events (three rash, two emergency department visits to rule out deep vein thrombosis, and one uncontrolled pain), whereas four patients in the OAP group reported five adverse events (two prolonged pain, one rash, one medication intolerance, and one bladder incontinence). Each of the patients reporting a rash on leg undergoing surgery received oral antihistamines or antibiotics. None of the patients with emergency department visits required further intervention. The patients in the OAP group with prolonged pain were managed with additional prescriptions for tramadol hydrochloride and/or oxycodone hydrochloride, whereas the patient with uncontrolled pain in the SOC group advanced to receive cryoneurolysis postoperatively as a rescue therapy during the study follow-up period. The medication intolerance was managed by withdrawing the medication and substituting a different antibiotic agent for postsurgical prophylaxis.

Discussion

The implementation of an OAP for ACLR markedly reduced postoperative opioid use among patients compared with SOC but did not fully eliminate reliance on opioids. A recent study from Henry Ford Health System investigated opioid-free recovery for ACLR compared with opioid-based analgesia and found no differences in pain outcomes, with no patients in the opioid-free group requiring emergency opioid prescriptions.21 It should be noted that these patients were given scheduled benzodiazepines four times daily for five days and three times daily for an additional seven days, which could have accounted for the drowsiness seen in 72% of patients who were opioid-free.21,22 This study avoided benzodiazepines and gabapentinoids because drowsiness can affect the ability to participate in rehabilitation, and benzodiazepines are associated with a separate risk of abuse, addiction, or overdose.22,23 A further prospective cohort study that evaluated opioid use after ACLR but did prescribe 15 tablets of oxycodone hydrochloride for all patients found that 38% of patients consumed 0 doses of the prescription.24 This cohort study was noncomparative and included multiple surgical methods that are unique compared with the current study, but, in combination with other literature, demonstrates the feasibility and efficacy of opioid-free multimodal analgesia.21,24 The first few days of recovery are expected to be the most painful as evidenced by the opioid consumption among the OAP group largely occurring in the first three to four days (Figure 2). This period represents an opportunity to augment the OAP with novel oral agents such as suzetrigine, gabapentinoids, and/or additional long-acting analgesic modalities, including continuous perineural catheters, adjunctive agents such as dexamethasone or dexmedetomidine, or liposomal bupivacaine for the adductor canal and iPACK blocks as supplements to the single-shot regional anesthesia technique with bupivacaine hydrochloride, which is unlikely to be effective after POD 0.16,25–28

This study is not without limitations; many of which are consistent with cohort studies. The main limitations of the study were the lack of randomization, small sample size, potential confounding effect of time, potential selection bias, and the involvement of a single surgeon in a relatively homogenous patient population. The lack of randomization was addressed through the completion of multivariable linear regression for the primary end point to attempt to control for confounding variables; however, a randomized controlled trial may still be necessary. The sample size of this pilot study was small but provided clinically notable benefits to the patients while providing effect size estimates for future studies. The investigators acknowledge that there may be effects not captured in the results due to selection bias in recruitment of the OAP group, the timing of surgery shifting between the athletic off-season and in-season between the groups, and that the patient population seen by the study surgeon may not be representative of the typical ACLR population. Because of the surgeon's technique of using ACL autografts, fertilized with a stem cell rich mixture of bone marrow concentrate, bone graft, and demineralized bone matrix, the recovery period was markedly shortened, and the rehabilitation process was more intense than with a standard ACLR, which accordingly can affect pain and therefore opioid use.29,30 Results must be carefully examined before extrapolating to the general population. Finally, the long-term effects of cryoneurolysis in ACLR have not been investigated at this time. Cryoneurolysis carries a risk of potential injection site infection, hematoma, or dysesthesia after treatment, which must be considered by the treating physician and the long-term implication of these potential adverse effects, including repeat ACL injury, have not been established.10,13,14

The strengths of this study included the unique treatment modalities and the duration of follow-up. The design of the study allowed for deeper collection of outcome data for patients than is usually captured in the electronic medical record and available in a retrospective analysis. This design also allowed for collection of multiple measures of pain, use of validated questionnaires to assess recovery quality, and a detailed safety assessment. In addition, this is the first study to investigate the utilization of preoperative cryoneurolysis in a treatment protocol for ACLR. This unique treatment modality has the potential to provide a long-lasting effect, as evidenced by the KOOS symptom subscale at eight weeks after surgery, to augment existing multimodal pathways to allow for opioid-free recovery while having minimal adverse reactions.7,10,11,15,16 This study is the first to implement an opioid-free pathway and capture data out to 60 days after surgery, which allowed for analysis of longer-term patient-reported outcomes and opioid use than has been described in previous studies.5,21,24,31

Conclusion

The implementation of an OAP incorporating preoperative cryoneurolysis resulted in a notable reduction in postoperative opioid consumption, with 40% of patients remaining completely opioid free through POD 60. The OAP with cryoneurolysis 7 to 21 days before surgery can serve as a basis for postoperative pain management and as a vital primary prevention tool against the opioid epidemic by reducing the exposure of the population to opioids, thereby removing an entry point to the causal pathway of opioid abuse or opioid-related mortality.

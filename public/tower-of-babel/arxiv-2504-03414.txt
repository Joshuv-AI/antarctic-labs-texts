Further results on Artin approximation, for group-actions on mapping-germs Maps ⁡ ( X , Y ) {\rm Maps}\!\left(X,Y\right) and for quivers of maps Thanks: I was supported by the Israel Science Foundation, grants No. 1910/18 and 1405/22
Dmitry Kerner

Abstract
. Consider (analytic, resp. algebraic) map-germs, Maps ⁡ ( ( 𝕜 n , o ) , ( 𝕜 m , o ) ) . {\rm Maps}\!\left((\Bbbk^{n},o),(\Bbbk^{m},o)\right). These germs are traditionally studied up to the right ( ℛ \mathscr{R} ), let-right ( ℒ ​ ℛ \mathscr{L}\mathscr{R} ) and contact ( 𝒦 {\mathscr{K}}\! ) equivalences. Below 𝒢 \mathscr{G} is one of these groups. An important tool in this study is the Artin approximation: any formal equivalence, f ~ ∼ 𝒢 ^ f , {\tilde{f}}\ \lower 1.46387pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}\limits^{\hat{\mathscr{G}}}}$}\ f, is approximated by ordinary (i.e. analytic, resp. algebraic) equivalence, f ~ ∼ 𝒢 f . {\tilde{f}}\ \lower 1.46387pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}\limits^{\mathscr{G}}}$}\ f. We consider maps of (analytic, resp. algebraic) scheme-germs, with arbitrary singularities, Maps ⁡ ( X , Y ) , {\rm Maps}\!\left(X,Y\right), and establish stronger versions of this property (for 𝒢 \mathscr{G} ): the Strong Artin approximation and the Płoski approximation. As a preliminary step we study the contact equivalence for maps with singular targets. In many cases one works with multi-germs of spaces, and with their “muti-maps”. More generally, “quivers of map-germs” occur in various applications. The needed tools are the Strong Artin approximation for quivers and the Płoski version. We establish these for directed rooted trees.

1.
Introduction

The classical Artin approximation,
[
Artin.68
]
,
[
Artin.69
]
, reads: given a system of algebraic (resp. analytic) equations of implicit function type,

F(x,y)=0,
every formal solution,
F(x,\hat{y}(x))=0,
is approximated by algebraic (resp. analytic) solutions,

F(x,y(x))=0
. See §
2.4
for more detail.

The subject “Artin approximation” has been studied extensively, see e.g.
[
Popescu.00
]
,
[
Rond.18
]
for numerous results and further references. It became an everyday tool in Singularity Theory, Local Algebraic/Analytic Geometry, and in Commutative Algebra.

The known versions of Artin-approximation are very general, while for particular applications one needs more specialised statements.
In some cases such “specializations” are not immediate or just fail.

1.1.
Approximation problems for mapping-germs

A central object of study in Singularity Theory (and numerous related areas) are the mapping germs
{\rm Maps}\!\left((\Bbbk^{n},o),(\Bbbk^{m},o)\right).

Here

•

\Bbbk
is a field of arbitrary characteristic. In the analytic case
\Bbbk
is a complete normed field.

•

(\Bbbk^{n},o),(\Bbbk^{m},o)
are the (algebraic/analytic/formal) germs of affine spaces.

More generally, one considers
{\rm Maps}\!\left(X,Y\right),
where
X\!\subseteq\!(\Bbbk^{n},o),

Y\!\subseteq\!(\Bbbk^{m},o)
are the (algebraic/analytic/formal) subgerms, with arbitrary singularities. Various questions of deformation theory force
\Bbbk
to be a (local) ring, rather than a field.

These maps are classically studied up to the right (
\mathscr{R}
), left (
\mathscr{L}
), left-right (
\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R}
) and contact (
{\mathscr{K}}\!
) group-actions.
E.g. the group action
\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R}\circlearrowright{\rm Maps}\!\left(X,Y\right)
goes by
f\rightsquigarrow\Phi_{Y}\circ f\circ\Phi_{X}^{-1},
where

\Phi_{X}\in Aut_{X}
and
\Phi_{Y}\in Aut_{Y}
are automorphisms of the source and of the target.
See §
2.2
and §
2.3
for more detail.

Below
\mathscr{G}
is one of these groups
\mathscr{R},\mathscr{L},\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R},{\mathscr{K}}\!.
The first fundamental questions are:

​​
\bullet

To decide whether two (algebraic/analytic/formal) maps,
X\!\ \lower 1.46387pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f,{\tilde{f}}}}$}\ \!Y,
are
\mathscr{G}
-equivalent, i.e.
{\tilde{f}}\!\in\!\mathscr{G}f.

​​
\bullet

To bring a map
X\ \lower 1.46387pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f}}$}\ Y
to a particular “normal” form. E.g. to eliminate monomials of certain type in the power series
f(x),
or to find a
\mathscr{G}
-representative of
f
inside a particular subspace/substratum of
{\rm Maps}\!\left(X,Y\right).

​​
\bullet

To reduce the study of deformations of the map
X\ \lower 1.46387pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f}}$}\ Y
to the “tangent level” (i.e. deformations over
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[\epsilon]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$(\epsilon)^{2}$}\right.}
) or to the level of obstruction functors (
T^{1},T^{2},\dots
)

In “simple” cases this can be done by geometric arguments, or by studying the lowest order monomials of
f.
In more complicated cases one can only invoke inductive arguments, eliminating monomials order-by-order. Such arguments give formal solutions (and even this can be non-obvious for the left-right equivalence
\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R}
).
But one needs analytic/algebraic solutions. Hence the need in the corresponding Artin approximation.

​​
\bullet

For
\mathscr{R}
-equivalence the condition to resolve is an implicit function equation,
{\tilde{f}}(x)=f(\phi(x))
with the unknown
\phi.
Here the standard Artin approximation readily applies.

​​
\bullet

For
\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R}
-equivalence (and for
{\mathscr{K}}\!
-equivalence, when the target
Y
is non-smooth) the involved equations are not of implicit-function-type. Moreover, the relevant Artin approximation,
\mathop{\text{}}{AP.}\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R}
, does not hold in the analytic case,
[
Shiota.98
, Fact.1.4]
,
[
Shiota.10
, pg. 1061]
.

The first version of
\mathop{\text{}}{AP.}\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R}
was established in
[
Shiota.98
]
for
{\rm Maps}\!\left((\mathbb{R}^{n},o),(\mathbb{R}^{m},o)\right)
that are either Nash or [analytic and of “finite singularity type”].
In
[
Kerner.25
]
the approximation
\mathop{\text{}}{AP.}\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R}
was extended to an arbitrary base-field
\Bbbk
, for
{\rm Maps}\!\left(X,Y\right)
that are either
\Bbbk
-Nash or [
\Bbbk
-analytic and “of weakly finite singularity type”].

For the further study of
{\rm Maps}\!\left(X,Y\right)
, e.g. in
[
Kerner.22
]
and
[
Kerner.25.prep
]
, we need stronger versions of Artin approximations. This is the goal of the current paper. Though it is the natural continuation of
[
Kerner.25
]
, the results and proofs are independent.

1.2.
The results

Below
\mathscr{G}
is one of the groups
\mathscr{R},\mathscr{L},\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R},\mathscr{C},{\mathscr{K}}\!,
see §
2.2
and §

1.2.1.
Strong Artin approximation for
\mathscr{G}
-equivalence

Occasionally (when maps
X\ \lower 1.46387pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f,{\tilde{f}}}}$}\ Y
are complicated) one cannot even establish a formal equivalence,
{\tilde{f}}\ \lower 1.46387pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}\limits^{\hat{\mathscr{G}}}}$}\ f.

The only possible result is an approximate solution,
{\tilde{f}}\ \lower 1.46387pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\equiv}\limits^{mod\ {\mathfrak{m}}^{d}}}$}\ g_{d}f
for some
d\gg 1
and
g_{d}\in\mathscr{G}.

To establish the ordinary (e.g. analytic/algebraic) equivalence, one needs the “Strong Artin
\mathscr{G}
-approximation”:

Theorem
3.3
,
\mathop{\text{}}{SAP\!.}\!\mathscr{G}
.
(roughly) Given two maps
X\ \lower 1.46387pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f,{\tilde{f}}}}$}\ Y,
there exists a function
\mathbb{N}\ \lower 1.46387pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\beta}}$}\ \mathbb{N}
ensuring:
if
{\tilde{f}}\equiv g_{d}f\ mod\ {\mathfrak{m}}^{\beta(d)}
for some
g_{d}\in\mathscr{G},
then
{\tilde{f}}=gf
for a corresponding
g\in\mathscr{G}
that satisfies

g\equiv g_{d}\ mod\ {\mathfrak{m}}^{d}.

This can be restated topologically. The set of all maps,
{\rm Maps}\!\left(X,Y\right),
has the natural
{\mathfrak{m}}
-adic filtration. And
\mathop{\text{}}{SAP\!.}\!\mathscr{G}
insures: the orbit
\mathscr{G}f\!\subset\!{\rm Maps}\!\left(X,Y\right)
is closed in this filtration-topology. See §
3.1
for detail.

1.2.2.
Płoski version of Artin approximation

The classical theorem of Płoski (extended by Popescu and others, §
2.4.5
) reads: given a formal solution,
F(x,\hat{y}(x))=0,
there exists a parameterized (analytic/algebraic) solution,
F(x,y_{z}(x))=0,
such that
\hat{y}(x)=y_{{\hat{z}}(x)}(x)
for a certain specialization
{\hat{z}}(x).

Thus the formal solutions are “artificial” and “not inherent”. We give
the corresponding Płoski-type property (
\mathop{\text{}}{APP\!.}\!\mathscr{G}
) for
\mathscr{G}
-equivalence of maps.

Theorem
3.3
,
\mathop{\text{}}{APP\!.}\!\mathscr{G}
.
(roughly) Given a map
X\ \lower 1.46387pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f}}$}\ Y,
and a formal equivalence

{\tilde{f}}={\hat{g}}f,
there exists a parameterized equivalence
{\tilde{f}}=g_{z}f
such that
{\hat{g}}=g_{\hat{z}}
for certain specialization
{\hat{z}}.

1.2.3.
Remarks on Theorem
3.3

i.

While the classical group-actions
\mathscr{G}\circlearrowright{\rm Maps}\!\left(X,(\Bbbk^{p},o)\right),
for
\mathscr{G}=\mathscr{R},\mathscr{L},\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R},{\mathscr{K}}\!,
are well-studied, we could not find references for the actions

\mathscr{G}\circlearrowright{\rm Maps}\!\left(X,Y\right)
with singular target. The relevant basic notions are set-up in §
2.3
, and studied further in
[
Kerner.25.prep
]
.

ii.

For the group
\mathscr{G}=\mathscr{R}
the approximations
\mathop{\text{}}{SAP\!.}\!\mathscr{R}
,
\mathop{\text{}}{APP\!.}\!\mathscr{R}
are immediate example of the classical properties SAP, APP.
Indeed, in this case the condition
{\tilde{f}}\ \lower 1.46387pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}\limits^{\mathscr{R}}}$}\ f
is an equation of implicit function type.

But for
\mathscr{G}=\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R}
and for [
\mathscr{G}={\mathscr{K}}\!,
with singular target,
Y\!\subsetneq\!(\Bbbk^{m},o)
] the involved conditions are not implicit-function-equations. The proof involves a little trick, used e.g. in
[
Shiota.98
]
, transforming the condition
{\tilde{f}}\ \lower 1.46387pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}\limits^{\mathscr{G}}}$}\ f
into an implicit-function-equation with nested solutions.

iii.

The properties
\mathop{\text{}}{SAP\!.}\!\mathscr{G}
and
\mathop{\text{}}{APP\!.}\!\mathscr{G}
approximate the solutions
g_{d},{\hat{g}}
by ordinary solutions with respect to the filtrations of local rings by their maximal ideals,
{\mathfrak{m}}^{\bullet}_{X}\!\subset\!R_{X},

{\mathfrak{m}}^{\bullet}_{Y}\!\subset\!R_{Y}.
Geometrically, the approximation holds “near the points
V({\mathfrak{m}}_{X})\in X
,
V({\mathfrak{m}}_{Y})\in Y
.”

For various applications one needs stronger approximations, “along subloci of
X,Y
”. E.g. given a formal solution
{\tilde{f}}={\hat{g}}f,
we look for an ordinary solution
{\tilde{f}}=g_{j}f
that satisfies:
{\hat{g}}\equiv g_{j}\ mod\ I^{j}.
(Here
\sqrt{I}\!\subsetneq\!{\mathfrak{m}}_{X},
i.e. the locus
V(I)\!\subset\!X
is of positive dimension.) This is impossible, as even the classical Strong Artin Approximation does not hold for the filtration
I^{\bullet}.
Yet we approximate the solutions
{\hat{g}},g_{d}
in a related filtration:
{\hat{g}}\equiv g_{j}\ mod\ \mathfrak{a}\cdot{\mathfrak{m}}^{j}.
See §
3.3
for this version of
\mathop{\text{}}{SAP\!.}\!\mathscr{G}
and
\mathop{\text{}}{AP\!.}\!\mathscr{G}
.

iv.

In various applications one uses the “filtration-unipotent” subgroups
\mathscr{G}^{(j)}<\mathscr{G},
see e.g.
[
B.K.16
]
,
[
B.G.K.22
]
,
[
Kerner.24
]
. Roughly speaking, the elements of
\mathscr{G}^{(j)}
are identities modulo “higher order terms”.
Hence the need in the relevant approximation. In §
3.4
we deduce the properties SAP and APP for
\mathscr{G}^{(j)}.

v.

This theorem is for
\Bbbk
a ring (not just a field), which is needed in the study of unfoldings and deformations.

This and other results of the paper hold for a larger class of rings, in many cases just being local, excellent, henselian suffices.
But we work just with the rings of formal/analytic/agebraic power series, as these are the most important in applications.

1.2.4.
Artin approximations for quivers of maps

The versions of Artin approximation for the right, left, left-right equivalences (
\mathop{\text{}}{AP.}\mathscr{R}
,
\mathop{\text{}}{AP.}\mathscr{L}
,
\mathop{\text{}}{AP.}\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R}
) can be encoded by the simplest “quivers of maps”:

(1)

\Phi_{X}\circlearrowright X\ \lower 1.46387pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f}}$}\ Y,\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad X\ \lower 1.46387pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f}}$}\ Y\circlearrowright\Phi_{Y},\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\Phi_{X}\circlearrowright X\ \lower 1.46387pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f}}$}\ Y\circlearrowleft\Phi_{Y}.\vskip-2.27626pt

On many occasions (in the study of map-germs) one has to work with multi-germs of maps,
\amalg X_{j}\ \lower 1.46387pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\{f_{j}\}}}$}\ Y,
and dually

X\ \lower 1.46387pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\{f_{j}\}}}$}\ \amalg Y_{j}.
The corresponding
\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R}
-approximation problems are encoded by the quivers:

(2)

\begin{matrix}\Phi_{1}\circlearrowright X_{1}\\
\dots\\
\Phi_{k}\circlearrowright X_{k}\end{matrix}\begin{matrix}\begin{turn}\ \lower 1.46387pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f_{1}}}$}\ \end{turn}\\
\begin{turn}\ \lower 1.46387pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f_{k}}}$}\ \end{turn}\end{matrix}Y\circlearrowleft\Phi_{Y},\hskip 56.9055pt\Phi_{X}\circlearrowright X\begin{matrix}\begin{turn}\ \lower 1.46387pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f_{1}}}$}\ \end{turn}\\
\begin{turn}\ \lower 1.46387pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f_{k}}}$}\ \end{turn}\end{matrix}\begin{matrix}Y_{1}\circlearrowleft\Phi_{1}\\
\dots\\
Y_{k}\circlearrowleft\Phi_{k}\end{matrix}\quad.\vskip-2.27626pt

Various other quivers of maps arise naturally in Singularity Theory, Dynamical Systems, Control Theory. E.g. the classical study of self-maps
f\circlearrowright(\Bbbk^{n},o)
up to conjugation,
f\sim\Phi\circ f\circ\Phi^{-1},
is encoded by the quiver

\Phi\circlearrowright(\Bbbk^{n},o)\circlearrowleft f.
Recall that in this case one has no formal-to-analytic approximation, by the classical results of Poincarè, Siegel and others. (See Remark
4.5
.)

All this calls for the general treatment of “Artin approximation for quivers of map-germs”, and its Strong Artin-Płoski-Popescu versions.
A remark, the classical study of quivers was for
linear
maps,
[
Kirillov
]
, thus no questions of Artin-approximation were involved.

Take a directed graph
\Gamma,
whose vertices,
\{X_{v}\},
are scheme-germs, while the edges
are germs of maps,
X_{v}\ \lower 1.46387pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f_{wv}}}$}\ X_{w}.
A morphism of such quivers,

(3)

(\Gamma,\{{\tilde{X}}_{\bullet}\},\{{\tilde{X}}_{v}\ \lower 1.46387pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{{\tilde{f}}_{wv}}}$}\ {\tilde{X}}_{w}\}_{wv})\quad\quad\to\quad\quad(\Gamma,\{X_{\bullet}\},\{X_{v}\ \lower 1.46387pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f_{wv}}}$}\ X_{w}\}_{wv}),\vskip-2.27626pt

is a collection of morphisms of schemes
\{\Phi_{v}:{\tilde{X}}_{v}\to X_{v}\}_{v}
that ensure commutativity of diagrams
“
\tilde{\Gamma}
over
\Gamma
”, see §
4.1
.
This gives the system of equations
\{\Phi_{w}\circ{\tilde{f}}_{wv}=f_{wv}\circ\Phi_{v}\}_{wv}.

The maps
\{f_{v}\},\{{\tilde{f}}_{v}\}
are prescribed, while
\{\Phi_{v}\}
are unknowns, in different sets of variables.
One can impose various additional conditions on
\{\Phi_{v}\}
, e.g.
\Phi_{v}
is invertible,
\Phi_{v}=Id_{X_{v}},
or
\Phi_{v}
is identity modulo higher order terms, or
\Phi_{v}
sends a subgerm
{\tilde{Z}}\!\subset\!{\tilde{X}}
to a subgerm
Z\!\subset\!X.

Theorem
4.6
establishes the “Strong Artin approximation for
\Gamma
” and the “Płoski approximation for
\Gamma
” in the algebraic/formal cases, when
\Gamma
is a rooted tree.
No analytic version of this result is given, as the proof inevitably uses the nested Artin approximation. (Which is absent in the analytic case.)

1.2.5.
Artin approximations for (quivers of) maps with base change

The quivers considered in §
1.2.4
are over a fixed base
Spec(\Bbbk),
and the maps correspond to morphisms of
\Bbbk
-algebras,
To study induced deformations, unfoldings and normal forms (of maps and of other objects) it is important to compare objects over various bases (
Spec({\tilde{\Bbbk}})
vs
Spec(\Bbbk)
), and to consider morphisms of rings involving homomorphisms
\Bbbk\to{\tilde{\Bbbk}}.

Thus we consider families of scheme-germs,
X_{t}
, and of maps-germs

Accordingly we have the property “
\mathop{\text{}}{AP\!.}\!\mathscr{G}
with change of parameters”.

More generally, we have quivers of families of maps,

(\Gamma,\{X_{t,v}\}_{v},\{X_{t,v}\!\ \lower 1.46387pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f_{t,wv}}}$}\ \!X_{t,w}\}_{wv}).
Corollary
5.2

gives the relevant versions of approximation: “SAP.
(\Gamma,t)
” and APP.
(\Gamma,t)
.

An immediate application is Example
5.3
, bringing unfoldings to normal forms.

•

is one of

•

{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[\![x]\!]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{X}$}\right.},

is a complete local ring.

In the algebraic case (i.e.
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\langle x\rangle$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{X}$}\right.}

\Bbbk={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk_{o}\langle t\rangle$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$\mathfrak{a}$}\right.},
e.g.
\Bbbk=\Bbbk_{o}
or
\Bbbk=\Bbbk_{o}\langle t\rangle.

See page 4 of
[
Denef-Lipshitz.80
]
for more detail.

Recall that
\mathbb{R}\langle x\rangle
is the ring of Nash power series.

by “
\Bbbk
-Nash” or just “Nash”, to avoid any confusion with the algebraic rings like

or

Through the paper we call
\Bbbk
(resp.

)

.

In the
\Bbbk
-smooth case, i.e.

,

2.1.2.

A (formal/analytic/Nash) map
f\in{\rm Maps}\!\left(X,Y\right)
is defined by the corresponding homomorphism of
\Bbbk
-algebras,
f^{\sharp}\in Hom_{\Bbbk}(R_{Y},R_{X}).
All our homomorphisms are local.

In the case
J_{X}=0,J_{Y}=0
one gets the maps of
\Bbbk
-smooth germs,
{\rm Maps}\!\left((\Bbbk^{n},o),(\Bbbk^{m},o)\right).

Fix embeddings
X\!\subseteq\!(\Bbbk^{n},o),

Y\!\subseteq\!(\Bbbk^{m},o)
and some coordinates
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
on
(\Bbbk^{n},o),
and
(y_{1},\dots,y_{m})
on
(\Bbbk^{m},o).

They are sent to the generators of ideals in
R_{X},

R_{Y}.
Abusing notations we write just
(x)=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\!\subset\!R_{X}
and
(y)=(y_{1},\dots,y_{m})\!\subset\!R_{Y}.

If
\Bbbk
is a field, then
(x),(y)
are the maximal ideals. Otherwise
denote the maximal ideal of
\Bbbk
by
{\mathfrak{m}}_{\Bbbk}.
Then
the maximal ideals are
{\mathfrak{m}}_{X}:={\mathfrak{m}}_{\Bbbk}+(x)\!\subset\!R_{X}

and
{\mathfrak{m}}_{Y}:={\mathfrak{m}}_{\Bbbk}+(y)\!\subset\!R_{Y}.
When only
R_{X}
is involved we write just
{\mathfrak{m}}\!\subset\!R_{X}.

A map
X\ \lower 1.46387pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f}}$}\ Y
is represented by
the tuple of power series,
(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})\in{\mathfrak{m}}\cdot R^{\oplus m}_{X}.

This embeds the space of maps as a subset
{\rm Maps}\!\left(X,Y\right)\!\subseteq\!{\mathfrak{m}}\cdot R^{\oplus m}_{X}.

Explicitly, for
R_{Y}={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[\![y]\!]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{Y}$}\right.},{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\{y\}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{Y}$}\right.},{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\langle y\rangle$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J_{Y}$}\right.},
one has:

(4)

{\rm Maps}\!\left(X,Y\right)=Hom_{\Bbbk}(R_{Y},R_{X})=\{f\in{\mathfrak{m}}\cdot R^{\oplus m}_{X}|\ f^{\sharp}(J_{Y})\!\subseteq\!J_{X}\}.\vskip-2.27626pt

If the target is
\Bbbk
-smooth, i.e.
Y=(\Bbbk^{m},o),
i.e.
R_{Y}=\Bbbk[\![y]\!],

\Bbbk\{y\},

\Bbbk\langle y\rangle
,
then we get the
R_{X}
-module
{\rm Maps}\!\left(X,(\Bbbk^{p},o)\right)={\mathfrak{m}}\cdot R^{\oplus m}_{X}.

For singular targets the subset
{\rm Maps}\!\left(X,Y\right)\!\subset\!{\mathfrak{m}}\cdot R^{\oplus m}_{X}
is neither additive, nor
\Bbbk
-multiplicative.

2.2.
The right, left, and left-right equivalence on
{\rm Maps}\!\left(X,Y\right)

The (formal/analytic/Nash) automorphisms of
X
over
Spec(\Bbbk)
are defined via their algebraic counterparts,
\Bbbk
-linear automorphisms of the local ring,
Aut_{X}:=Aut_{\Bbbk}(R_{X}).
See e.g.
[
Kerner.24
, §2.4]
.

Similarly we denote
Aut_{Y}:=Aut_{\Bbbk}(R_{Y}).

In the
\Bbbk
-smooth case,
{\rm Maps}\!\left((\Bbbk^{n},o),(\Bbbk^{m},o)\right),
these automorphisms are the coordinate changes in the source and the target.

These automorphisms act on the space of maps, defining the right and left group actions:

•

\mathscr{R}:=Aut_{X}:=Aut_{\Bbbk}(R_{X})\circlearrowright{\rm Maps}\!\left(X,Y\right)
, by
(\Phi_{X},f)\to f\circ\Phi^{-1}_{X}
;

•

\mathscr{L}:=Aut_{Y}:=Aut_{\Bbbk}(R_{Y})\circlearrowright{\rm Maps}\!\left(X,Y\right)
, by
(\Phi_{Y},f)\to\Phi_{Y}\circ f
.

These actions define the right, left, and left-right equivalence of maps. E.g.,
{\tilde{f}}\ \lower 1.46387pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}\limits^{\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R}}}$}\ f
means

f=\Phi_{Y}\circ{\tilde{f}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{X},
i.e.

\Phi_{Y}({\tilde{f}}(x))=f(\Phi_{X}(x)),
for
\Phi_{X}\in Aut_{(\Bbbk^{n},o)}
with
\Phi_{X}(J_{X})=J_{X},
and
\Phi_{Y}\in Aut_{(\Bbbk^{m},o)}
with
\Phi_{Y}(J_{Y})=J_{Y}.

2.3.
The contact equivalence of
{\rm Maps}\!\left(X,Y\right)
and its linearized version

Definition 2.1
.

The subgroup
\mathscr{C}<Aut_{X\!\times\!Y}
consists of automorphisms that preserve the fibres of the projection
X\times Y\to X

and preserve “the zero-level hyperplane”
X\times\{o\}\!\subset\!X\times Y.

Therefore
\mathscr{C}
acts as
Id_{X}
on
X\!\times\!\{o\},

and restricts to automorphisms of the central fibre
\{o\}\!\times\!Y.

Explicitly, fix some generators
J_{Y}=\langle q_{\bullet}\rangle,
then the action
\mathscr{C}\circlearrowright X\times Y
is by
(x,y)\to(x,C(x,y)),

where

(5)

q_{\bullet}(C(x,y))=0\text{ and }C(x,y)=\Phi_{Y}(y)+(\dots),\quad\text{ with }\quad(\dots)\in(x)\cdot(y)\text{ and }\Phi_{Y}\in Aut_{Y}.\vskip-2.27626pt

The action on maps,
\mathscr{C}\circlearrowright{\rm Maps}\!\left(X,Y\right),
is defined by
f(x)\rightsquigarrow C(x,f(x)).

Definition 2.2
.

The contact group is the semi-direct product
{\mathscr{K}}\!:=\mathscr{C}\rtimes Aut_{X}.

(Note that
\mathscr{C}\lhd{\mathscr{K}}\!
is a normal subgroup.) It acts on
X\times Y
by
(x,y)\to(\Phi_{X}(x),C(x,y)).

The action
{\mathscr{K}}\!\circlearrowright{\rm Maps}\!\left(X,Y\right)
is defined via the graph of
f,
i.e.
f\rightsquigarrow C(x,f\circ\Phi^{-1}_{X}).

Example 2.3
.

When the target is
\Bbbk
-smooth,
Y=(\Bbbk^{m},o),
this is the classical contact action, see e.g.
[
Mond. Nuñ.-Bal.
, pg.108]
or
[
A.G.L.V.
, pg. 156]
.
In this case the space of maps is an
R_{X}
-module,
{\rm Maps}\!\left(X,(\Bbbk^{p},o)\right)={\mathfrak{m}}\cdot R^{\oplus m}_{X},
and the right action
\mathscr{R}\circlearrowright{\rm Maps}\!\left(X,(\Bbbk^{p},o)\right)
is
\Bbbk
-linear. Namely,
(c\cdot f+\tilde{c}\cdot{\tilde{f}})\circ\Phi^{-1}_{X}=c\cdot f\circ\Phi^{-1}_{X}+\tilde{c}\cdot{\tilde{f}}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{X}
.
The actions
\mathscr{L}\mathscr{R},{\mathscr{K}}\!\circlearrowright Maps(X,(\Bbbk^{m},o))
are not
\Bbbk
-linear, not even additive.

However, the action
{\mathscr{K}}\!\circlearrowright{\rm Maps}\!\left(X,Y\right)
admits a “linearization”, even when
Y
is singular.

Definition 2.4
.

[The linearized contact groupoid of a map
f\in{\rm Maps}\!\left(X,Y\right)
]

{\mathscr{K}}\!_{lin,f}\!:=\{(U,\Phi_{X})\!\in\!GL_{m}(R_{X})\rtimes Aut_{X}|\ U(x)\!\cdot\!f(x)=C(x,f(x))\ for\ some\ C(x,y)\ as\ above\}.

Example 2.5
.

i.

For a
\Bbbk
-smooth target,
Y=(\Bbbk^{m},o),
we get the classical object:
{\mathscr{K}}\!_{lin,f}=GL_{m}(R_{X})\rtimes Aut_{X}.
(In particular,
{\mathscr{K}}\!_{lin,f}
does not depend on
f,
and is a group.)

Proof.
Any
U(x)\in GL_{m}(R_{X})
acts on
Maps(X,(\Bbbk^{m},o))
by
f\to U\cdot f
. Thus the condition “
U(x)\cdot f(x)=C(x,f(x))
” is empty.
Finally,
GL_{m}(R_{X})<{\mathscr{K}}\!_{lin,f}
is a normal subgroup.

\blacksquare

The action
GL_{m}(R_{X})\rtimes Aut_{X}\circlearrowright Maps(X,(\Bbbk^{m},o))
is
\Bbbk
-linear. Namely,

(U,\Phi_{X})(f+{\tilde{f}})=U\cdot f\circ\Phi_{X}^{-1}+U\cdot{\tilde{f}}\circ\Phi_{X}^{-1}.

ii.

When the target
Y
is not
\Bbbk
-smooth,
{\mathscr{K}}\!_{lin,f}
is not a group and depends on
f
. Moreover,
{\mathscr{K}}\!_{lin,f}
does not act on
{\rm Maps}\!\left(X,Y\right).

Namely, given
(U,\Phi)\in{\mathscr{K}}\!_{lin,f}
and a general
f\neq{\tilde{f}}\in{\rm Maps}\!\left(X,Y\right),
the image of
U(x)\cdot{\tilde{f}}(\Phi(x))
does not lie inside
Y.

In any case, one has the orbit
{\mathscr{K}}\!_{lin,f}f\!\subseteq\!{\rm Maps}\!\left(X,Y\right).

It “linearizes” the group-action
{\mathscr{K}}\!\circlearrowright{\rm Maps}\!\left(X,Y\right)
in the following sense.

Lemma 2.6
.

Fix a map
f\!\in\!{\rm Maps}\!\left(X,Y\right).
Then
{\tilde{f}}\!\ \lower 1.46387pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}\limits^{{\mathscr{K}}\!}}$}\ \!f
iff
{\tilde{f}}\!\in\!{\mathscr{K}}\!_{lin,f}f.
[Thus
{\mathscr{K}}\!f\!=\!{\mathscr{K}}\!_{lin,f}f.
]

This linearization is classically known for
\Bbbk
-smooth targets, e.g.
[
Mond. Nuñ.-Bal.
, pg.110]
for
Maps((\mathbb{C}^{n},o),(\mathbb{C}^{m},o))
and
[
B.K.16
, pg.123]
for
Maps(X,(\Bbbk^{m},o))
.

Proof.
\Rrightarrow
Let
{\tilde{f}}=C(x,f\circ\Phi^{-1}_{X}).
Here
C(x,y)\in(y)\!\subset\!R_{X\!\times\!Y},
thus we can expand:
C(x,y)=A(x,y)\cdot y,
for some
A(x,y)\in Mat_{m\times m}(R_{X\!\times\!Y}).

By definition of
\mathscr{C}
-group:
C|_{o\times Y}\in Aut_{Y},
therefore
A(x,y)\in GL_{m}(R_{X\!\times\!Y}).

Finally, denote
U(x)=A(x,f\circ\Phi^{-1}_{X})\in GL_{m}(R_{X})
to get:

{\tilde{f}}=U(x)\cdot(f\circ\Phi^{-1}_{X}).

The direction
\Lleftarrow
is verified similarly.

\blacksquare

3.1.
Definitions

Definition 3.1
.

(
\mathop{\text{}}{SAP\!.}\!\mathscr{G}
)
The strong Artin approximation for
\mathscr{G}
-action,
\mathop{\text{}}{SAP\!.}\!\mathscr{G}
, holds for a (Nash/analytic/formal) map

if for any other map

{\tilde{f}}\ \lower 1.46387pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\equiv}\limits^{mod\ {\mathfrak{m}}^{\beta(d)}}}$}\ g_{d}f,

then
{\tilde{f}}=gf
for an element

that satisfies

g\ \lower 1.46387pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\equiv}\limits^{mod\ {\mathfrak{m}}^{d}}}$}\ g_{d}.

Restate this via the filtration-topology. Take the orbit
\mathscr{G}f
and its filtration neighborhoods
\mathscr{G}f+{\mathfrak{m}}^{\bullet}\cdot R^{\oplus m}_{X}.

Suppose
{\tilde{f}}\in\mathscr{G}f+{\mathfrak{m}}^{d}\cdot R^{\oplus m}_{X}
for each

Then

the latter being the filtration closure
of
\mathscr{G}f\!\subseteq\!R^{\oplus m}_{X}.
The Strong Artin approximation ensures that the orbit is closed,
\cap_{d}(\mathscr{G}f+{\mathfrak{m}}^{\bullet}\cdot R^{\oplus m}_{X})=\mathscr{G}f.

A remark: if

is

\{\Phi_{v,d}\}_{v}

WUSH: Near-Optimal Adaptive Transforms for LLM Quantization
\name Jiale Chen \email Jiale.Chen@ist.ac.at, \name Vage Egiazarian \email Vage.Egiazarian@ist.ac.at, \name Torsten Hoefler \email torsten.hoefler@inf.ethz.ch, \name Dan Alistarh \email Dan.Alistarh@ist.ac.at

Abstract
Quantization to low bitwidth is a standard approach for deploying large language models, however, a few extreme weights and activations stretch the dynamic range and reduce the effective resolution of the quantizer. A common mitigation approach is to apply some fixed orthogonal transforms, such as Hadamard matrices, before quantization, which typically reduces the dynamic range. Yet, these transforms ignore the statistics of the data, and their optimality is currently not understood. In this work, we derive, for the first time, closed-form optimal linear blockwise transforms for joint weight-activation quantization using standard data-free quantizers for common numerical formats. Specifically, we provide derivations of the optimal adaptive (data-aware) transforms for round-to-nearest (RTN), AbsMax-scaled block quantizers for both integer and floating-point formats. The resulting construction, which we call WUSH, combines a Hadamard backbone with a data-dependent component based on second-order moments, yielding a non-orthogonal transform that is provably optimal under mild assumptions and remains structured for efficient implementation. Preliminary experimental results show that our approach consistently improves upon the Hadamard transform for common formats.

A central difficulty, however, is that a few extreme “outlier” weights and activations expand the dynamic range and thereby degrade the effective resolution of low-bit representations.

It is known that one way to mitigate these outliers is to apply linear transforms before quantization

1.2
Related Work

Post-training quantization and outliers.

Generic post-training quantization schemes, such as RTN (round-to-nearest), OBQ
(
9
)
, and GPTQ
(
10
)
, are highly sensitive to heavy-tailed outliers in the weights and activations of LLMs because a small number of extreme values determine the quantization scale.
As a result, most quantization levels are spent covering rare extremes instead of the bulk of the distribution, which leads to a significant loss of accuracy.

Non-uniform bitwidths and explicit outlier storage.

One common strategy to mitigate outliers is to use non-uniform bitwidths.
Methods such as LLM.int8()
(
6
)
, SpQR
(
7
)
, and QUIK
(
1
)
explicitly separate and store outliers in higher precision, while HPTQ
(
4
)
uses Huffman encoding to compress the outliers and reduce their storage costs.
These approaches can achieve high accuracy at a low effective bitwidth, however, the resulting formats are irregular and require specialized kernels and additional engineering effort for efficient deployment.

Transform-based outlier mitigation.

Another line of work applies transforms to the weights and activations before quantization to reduce the impact of outliers. SmoothQuant
(
25
)
and AWQ
(
15
)
rescale channels to stabilize the dynamic ranges of weights and activations.
QuIP
(
3
)
introduces an incoherence processing step.
QuIP#
(
22
)
, QTIP
(
23
)
, and QuaRot
(
2
)
use Hadamard transforms to spread outlier energy across channel dimensions, while SpinQuant
(
16
)
learns rotations optimized on calibration data. However, their transforms are either heuristic, costly to learn, or primarily designed for weight-only quantization, which limits their accuracy or practicality when applied to fast, per-token activation quantization in large models.

Blockwise transforms and emerging FP formats.

The recent FP formats, such as MXFP
(
17
)
and NVFP
(
18
)
, have motivated blockwise transform schemes.
20
indicates that blockwise Hadamard transforms can be more effective under these FP formats than full-layer transforms, but
8
highlights the limited gains achievable with Hadamard alone, linking this to the properties of the underlying weight and activation distributions. In contrast, our work derives a closed-form, data-aware optimal blockwise transform (WUSH) and analyzes how such a transform interacts with both FP and INT block quantizers.

1.3
Methodology

Problem setup.

We formulate the transformed weight-activation quantization problem as follows.
Define
\bm{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{in}}\times d_{\mathrm{out}}}
as the weight of a linear layer with
d_{\mathrm{in}}
input channels and
d_{\mathrm{out}}
output channels.
Define
\bm{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{in}}\times d_{\mathrm{batch}}}
as the calibration input activation with
d_{\mathrm{in}}
embedding channels and
d_{\mathrm{batch}}
tokens.
Define
q\left(\cdot\right)
as a quantizer that maps a matrix of continuous values to quantized values (we will specify
q
later).
Let
\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}},\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{in}}\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}
be the transforms applied to
\bm{W}
and
\bm{X}
, respectively.
For weight-activation quantization, the output activation
\bm{W}^{\top}\bm{X}
becomes
q\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}}\bm{W}\right)^{\top}q\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}\bm{X}\right)
.
Our goal is to choose
\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}}
,
\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}
such that the
L_{2}
output loss

\displaystyle\ell=d_{\mathrm{out}}^{-1}d_{\mathrm{batch}}^{-1}\left\|q\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}}\bm{W}\right)^{\top}q\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}\bm{X}\right)-\bm{W}^{\top}\bm{X}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}

(2)

is minimized under the given quantizer
q
.
The constant
d_{\mathrm{out}}^{-1}d_{\mathrm{batch}}^{-1}
is added to simplify the analysis later.

Block-independent constraint.

While the weights can be pre-quantized, the activations must be transformed and quantized
dynamically
at inference time.
To reduce the computational overhead and simplify the problem, we constrain the transforms
\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}}
and
\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}
to be block-diagonal, and the quantizer
q
to be an RTN (round-to-nearest) quantizer with AbsMax (the maximum absolute value within a group) scales.
We assume that the quantization is applied to each sub-column vector of shape
d\times 1
and that the transform block size aligns with the quantization group size
d
(
d
is a factor of
d_{\mathrm{in}}
, and
d
is a power of
2
).
Denote

\displaystyle\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}}=\operatorname{diag}\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}_{\left(1\right)}},\dots,\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}_{\left(d_{\mathrm{in}}/d\right)}}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}=\operatorname{diag}\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}_{\left(1\right)}},\dots,\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}_{\left(d_{\mathrm{in}}/d\right)}}\right)

(3)

with
\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}_{\left(i\right)}},\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}_{\left(i\right)}}\in\mathrm{R}^{d\times d}
.
We partition
\bm{W}^{\top}=\left[\bm{W}_{\left(1\right)}^{\top},\dots,\bm{W}_{\left(d_{\mathrm{in}}/d\right)}^{\top}\right]
with
\bm{W}_{\left(i\right)}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_{\mathrm{out}}}
and
\bm{X}^{\top}=\left[\bm{X}_{\left(1\right)}^{\top},\dots,\bm{X}_{\left(d_{\mathrm{in}}/d\right)}^{\top}\right]
with
\bm{X}_{\left(i\right)}\in\mathrm{R}^{d\times d_{\mathrm{batch}}}
.
Then, we can express the output without quantization as
\bm{W}^{\top}\bm{X}=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\mathrm{in}}/d}\bm{W}_{\left(i\right)}^{\top}\bm{X}_{\left(i\right)}
and the output with weight-activation quantization as

\displaystyle q\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}}\bm{W}\right)^{\top}q\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}\bm{X}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\mathrm{in}}/d}q\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}_{\left(i\right)}}\bm{W}_{\left(i\right)}\right)^{\top}q\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}_{\left(i\right)}}\bm{X}_{\left(i\right)}\right).

(4)

Finally, define the blockwise output loss as

\displaystyle\ell_{\left(i\right)}=d_{\mathrm{out}}^{-1}d_{\mathrm{batch}}^{-1}\left\|q\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}_{\left(i\right)}}\bm{W}_{\left(i\right)}\right)^{\top}q\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}_{\left(i\right)}}\bm{X}_{\left(i\right)}\right)-\bm{W}_{\left(i\right)}^{\top}\bm{X}_{\left(i\right)}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}.

(5)

We approximate the layerwise loss as
\ell\approx\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\mathrm{in}}/d}\ell_{\left(i\right)}
and minimize the blockwise loss
\ell_{\left(i\right)}
independently.

Optimal block-diagonal transforms.

We will prove that there exist closed-form optimal transforms for each block under reasonable assumptions and smooth approximations, which are optimal for FP and asymptotically optimal for INT quantizations.
Let
\bm{W}_{\left(i\right)}^{\prime},\bm{X}_{\left(i\right)}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
be matrices that satisfy
\bm{W}_{\left(i\right)}^{\prime}\bm{W}_{\left(i\right)}^{\prime\top}=d_{\mathrm{out}}^{-1}\bm{W}_{\left(i\right)}\bm{W}_{\left(i\right)}^{\top}
and
\bm{X}_{\left(i\right)}^{\prime}\bm{X}_{\left(i\right)}^{\prime\top}=d_{\mathrm{batch}}^{-1}\bm{X}_{\left(i\right)}\bm{X}_{\left(i\right)}^{\top}
, respectively.
Without loss of generality (any differences will be canceled in later steps), we let
\bm{W}^{\prime}
and
\bm{X}^{\prime}
be the lower triangular matrices from the Cholesky decomposition of the second moments
d_{\mathrm{out}}^{-1}\bm{W}_{\left(i\right)}\bm{W}_{\left(i\right)}^{\top}
and
d_{\mathrm{batch}}^{-1}\bm{X}_{\left(i\right)}\bm{X}_{\left(i\right)}^{\top}
.
In the case of
\mathrm{rank}\left(\bm{W}_{\left(i\right)}\right)<d
or
\mathrm{rank}\left(\bm{X}_{\left(i\right)}\right)<d
, we can dampen the diagonal of the second moment before the Cholesky decomposition.
Let the orthogonal matrices
\bm{U}_{\left(i\right)},\bm{V}_{\left(i\right)}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and the diagonal matrix
\bm{S}_{\left(i\right)}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
be the singular value decomposition (SVD) of
\bm{W}_{\left(i\right)}^{\prime\top}\bm{X}_{\left(i\right)}^{\prime}=\bm{U}_{\left(i\right)}\bm{S}_{\left(i\right)}\bm{V}_{\left(i\right)}^{\top}
.
Let
\bm{H}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
be a normalized (orthogonal) Hadamard matrix where each element is
\pm d^{-\frac{1}{2}}
.
The optimal
\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}_{\left(i\right)}}
and
\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}_{\left(i\right)}}
are constructed as

\displaystyle{\bm{T}_{\mathrm{hsvx}}}_{\left(i\right)}=\bm{H}\bm{S}_{\left(i\right)}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bm{V}_{\left(i\right)}^{\top}\bm{X}_{\left(i\right)}^{\prime\top}\quad\text{and}\quad{\bm{T}_{\mathrm{hsuw}}}_{\left(i\right)}=\bm{H}\bm{S}_{\left(i\right)}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bm{U}_{\left(i\right)}^{\top}\bm{W}_{\left(i\right)}^{\prime\top},

(6)

respectively.
Note that
{\bm{T}_{\mathrm{hsvx}}}_{\left(i\right)}={\bm{T}_{\mathrm{hsuw}}^{-\top}}_{\left(i\right)}

1
1

1

The
-\top
superscript notation means
(\cdot)^{-\top}=((\cdot)^{-1})^{\top}=((\cdot)^{\top})^{-1}
.

, which can be easily verified by calculating
{\bm{T}_{\mathrm{hsvx}}}_{\left(i\right)}{\bm{T}_{\mathrm{hsuw}}^{\top}}_{\left(i\right)}=\mathbf{I}
with
\mathbf{I}
as the identity matrix.

Remark.

The Hadamard matrix
\bm{H}
is the only data-agnostic ingredient in our optimal formulation in Eq. (

2
Theoretical Derivation

In this section, we focus on the question of finding optimal transforms that minimize the loss of one block
\ell_{\left(i\right)}
.
We omit the subscript
\left(i\right)
to simplify notation.

2.1
Problem Setup and General Proof Approach

Probabilistic reformulation.

We reformulate the problem from a probabilistic perspective.
We split the matrices
\bm{W}=\left[\bm{w}_{1},\dots,\bm{w}_{d_{\mathrm{out}}}\right]
and
\bm{X}=\left[\bm{x}_{1},\dots,\bm{x}_{d_{\mathrm{batch}}}\right]
into columns
\bm{w}_{k},\bm{x}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and treat the columns as i.i.d. samples from
d
-dimensional independent distributions
\bm{w}\sim\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{w}}
and
\bm{x}\sim\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{x}}
, respectively.
Then the matrices
\bm{W}^{\prime}
and
\bm{X}^{\prime}
satisfy
\bm{W}^{\prime}\bm{W}^{\prime\top}=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{w}}\bm{w}\bm{w}^{\top}
and
\bm{X}^{\prime}\bm{X}^{\prime\top}=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}\bm{x}\bm{x}^{\top}
, respectively.
We can reinterpret the blockwise loss as

\ell=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{w},\bm{x}}\left(q\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}}\bm{w}\right)^{\top}q\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}\bm{x}\right)-\bm{w}^{\top}\bm{x}\right)^{2}
.

Unbiased quantization.

We assume that
q
is a stochastic and unbiased quantizer, i.e., for a vector
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the quantization error
\varepsilon\left(\bm{\alpha}\right)=q\left(\bm{\alpha}\right)-\bm{\alpha}
is a random vector with
\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon\left(\bm{\alpha}\right)}\varepsilon\left(\bm{\alpha}\right)=\bm{0}
.
We further constrain
\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}}=\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}^{-\top}
such that
q\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}}\bm{w}\right)^{\top}q\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}\bm{x}\right)
is unbiased with respect to
\bm{w}^{\top}\bm{x}
.
Then, using the first-order approximation, we can split
\ell
into two non-negative terms:

\displaystyle\ell=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{w},\bm{x},\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}}\bm{w}\right),\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}\bm{x}\right)}\left(\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}}\bm{w}+\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}}\bm{w}\right)\right)^{\top}\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}\bm{x}+\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}\bm{x}\right)\right)-\bm{w}^{\top}\bm{x}\right)^{2}

(7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{w},\bm{x},\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}}\bm{w}\right),\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}\bm{x}\right)}\left(\bm{x}^{\top}\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}^{\top}\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}}\bm{w}\right)+\bm{w}^{\top}\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}}^{\top}\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}\bm{x}\right)+\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}}\bm{w}\right)^{\top}\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}\bm{x}\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{w},\bm{x},\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}}\bm{w}\right),\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}\bm{x}\right)}\left(\bm{x}^{\top}\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}^{\top}\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}}\bm{w}\right)+\bm{w}^{\top}\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}}^{\top}\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}\bm{x}\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}_{\bm{w},\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}}\bm{w}\right)}\left\|\bm{X}^{\prime\top}\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}^{\top}\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}}\bm{w}\right)\right\|^{2}\right)+\left(\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x},\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}\bm{x}\right)}\left\|\bm{W}^{\prime\top}\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}}^{\top}\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}\bm{x}\right)\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}_{\bm{w},\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}}\bm{w}\right)}\left\|\bm{X}^{\prime\top}\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}}^{-1}\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}}\bm{w}\right)\right\|^{2}\right)+\left(\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x},\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}\bm{x}\right)}\left\|\bm{W}^{\prime\top}\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}^{-1}\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}\bm{x}\right)\right\|^{2}\right).

Problem reduction.

We can compute the optimal
\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}}
and
\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}
individually for each of the non-negative terms and verify if
\bm{T}_{\mathrm{W}}=\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}^{-\top}
.
The two terms have similar forms and can be optimized in similar ways.
We focus on finding the optimal
\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}
for the second term.
Define the
d
-dimensional random variable
\bm{y}=\bm{W}^{\prime\top}\bm{x}\sim\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{y}}
.
Define the transformation matrix
\bm{T}=\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}\bm{W}^{\prime-\top}
.
Then, the second term becomes

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x},\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}\bm{x}\right)}\left\|\bm{W}^{\prime\top}\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}^{-1}\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}\bm{x}\right)\right\|^{2}=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x},\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}\bm{x}\right)}\left\|\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}\bm{W}^{\prime-\top}\right)^{-1}\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}\left(\bm{W}^{\prime-\top}\bm{y}\right)\right)\right\|^{2}

(8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y},\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}\bm{y}\right)}\left\|\bm{T}^{-1}\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}\bm{y}\right)\right\|^{2}

Therefore, the two-sided quantization problem is reduced to finding the optimal
\bm{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
that minimizes the one-sided quantization loss

\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y},\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}\bm{y}\right)}\left\|\bm{T}^{-1}\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}\bm{y}\right)\right\|^{2}

and compute

\bm{T}_{\mathrm{X}}=\bm{T}\bm{W}^{\prime\top}
.

Transformation parameterization.

Let the unknown orthogonal matrices
\bm{U}^{\prime},\bm{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and the unknown diagonal matrix
\bm{S}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
be the singular value decomposition (SVD) of
\bm{T}\bm{U}\bm{S}=\bm{U}^{\prime}\bm{S}^{\prime}\bm{R}^{\top}
; thus,
\bm{T}
can be parameterized as
\bm{T}=\bm{U}^{\prime}\bm{S}^{\prime}\bm{R}^{\top}\bm{S}^{-1}\bm{U}^{\top}
.
Denote
\bm{S}=\operatorname{diag}\left(s_{1},\dots,s_{d}\right)
and
\bm{S}^{\prime}=\operatorname{diag}\left(s^{\prime}_{1},\dots,s^{\prime}_{d}\right)
.
Without loss of generality, assume
s_{1}\geq\dots\geq s_{d}>0
and
s^{\prime}_{1}\geq\dots\geq s^{\prime}_{d}>0
.

Theorem 1
(Optimal Transform)

The optimal configuration of
\bm{T}
for floating-point (FP) data types is
\bm{U}^{\prime}=\bm{H}
,
\bm{S}^{\prime}=\bm{S}^{\frac{1}{2}}
, and
\bm{R}=\mathbf{I}
.
For integer (INT), the same configuration is optimal up to a
d^{o\left(1\right)}
factor for zero-mean Gaussian/Laplacian distributed data and within a
d
factor for any distribution.

Proof

We provide detailed proofs for FP and INT in
sections

2.3
and
2.2
, respectively.
For each data type, we apply a two-step proof strategy.
We first provide a smooth modeling of the quantizer and treat the type casting errors as random noise.
Secondly, we calculate the expected quantization error with respect to the parameterized transform and minimize this expectation by solving the corresponding optimization problem.

Some useful notations and basic results are as follows:

•

Denote the
k
-th standard basis vector as
\mathbf{e}_{k}\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{d}
, where the
k
-th element is
1
and
0
otherwise.

•

The second moment of
\bm{y}
is

\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\bm{y}\bm{y}^{\top}=\bm{W}^{\prime\top}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}}\bm{x}\bm{x}^{\top}\right)\bm{W}^{\prime}=\bm{W}^{\prime\top}\bm{X}^{\prime}\bm{X}^{\prime\top}\bm{W}^{\prime}=\bm{U}\bm{S}^{2}\bm{U}^{\top}
.

•

The second moment of
\bm{T}\bm{y}
is

\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\bm{T}\bm{y}\left(\bm{T}\bm{y}\right)^{\top}=\bm{T}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\bm{y}\bm{y}^{\top}\right)\bm{T}^{\top}=\bm{U}^{\prime}\bm{S}^{\prime 2}\bm{U}^{\prime\top}
.

•

The expected squared norm of

.

•

Similarly, the expected squared norm of
\bm{T}\bm{y}
is

\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\left\|\bm{T}\bm{y}\right\|^{2}=\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{S}^{\prime 2}\right)

•

Because

,

\left(\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{S}\right)\right)^{2}\geq\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{S}^{2}\right)
.
By Jensen’s inequality,

\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{S}^{2}\right)\geq d^{-1}\left(\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{S}\right)\right)^{2}

2.2
Proof for Floating-Point (FP) Types

2.2.1
Quantization Error Modeling

Theorem 2
(FP Quantization Error Modeling)

For a vector

\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
we can model the quantization error

\varepsilon\left(\bm{\alpha}\right)=\operatorname{diag}\left(\bm{\eta}\right)\bm{\alpha}

with

\bm{\eta}=\left[\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{d}\right]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

being a random vector of i.i.d. samples

\eta\sim\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{\eta}}

and

\mathbb{E}_{\eta}\eta=0
.

Proof

The FP format
\mathrm{E}a\mathrm{M}b
is defined as a tuple
\left(s,e,m\right)
with a sign
s\in\left\{0,1\right\}
, an exponent
e\in\left\{0,\dots,2^{a}-1\right\}
, and a mantissa
m\in\left\{0,\dots,2^{b}-1\right\}
.
Assume we do not need to consider the subnormal and special number cases.
The tuple
\left(s,e,m\right)
represents the number

x_{\mathrm{E}a\mathrm{M}b}=\left(-1\right)^{s}2^{e-2^{a-1}+1}\left(1+2^{-b}m\right)
.

Using the approximation
1+\left(\cdot\right)\approx 2^{\left(\cdot\right)}
for
2^{-b}m\in\left[0,1\right]
, we define a smoothed version of
\mathrm{E}a\mathrm{M}b
, named
\mathrm{SE}a\mathrm{M}b
, as

x_{\mathrm{SE}a\mathrm{M}b}=\left(-1\right)^{s}2^{e-2^{a-1}+1}2^{2^{-b}m}=\left(-1\right)^{s}2^{2^{-b}\left(2^{b}e+m\right)-2^{a-1}+1}

with

2^{b}e+m=2^{b}\left(\log_{2}\left|x_{\mathrm{SE}a\mathrm{M}b}\right|+2^{a-1}-1\right)

being a
a+b
bit unsigned integer (concatenating the binary representations of
e
and
m
).
The minimum of

\left|x_{\mathrm{SE}a\mathrm{M}b}\right|

is

\min\left|x_{\mathrm{SE}a\mathrm{M}b}\right|=2^{-2^{a-1}+1}

and the maximum is

\max\left|x_{\mathrm{SE}a\mathrm{M}b}\right|=2^{2^{-b}\left(2^{a+b}-1\right)-2^{a-1}+1}=2^{2^{a-1}-2^{-b}+1}
.

Denote
\mathrm{SE}a\mathrm{M}b\left(x\right)
as the quantizer that casts
x\in\left[-\max\left|x_{\mathrm{SE}a\mathrm{M}b}\right|,\max\left|x_{\mathrm{SE}a\mathrm{M}b}\right|\right]
to the nearest number in the
\mathrm{SE}a\mathrm{M}b
type.
Assume
e\in\left(-\infty,2^{a}-1\right]
so that the smaller values can be represented more precisely.

\mathrm{SE}a\mathrm{M}b\left(x\right)
can be approximated using integer rounding in the logarithm space and first-order expansion.
For
x\neq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{SE}a\mathrm{M}b\left(x\right)\approx

\displaystyle\mathrm{sgn}\left(x\right)2^{2^{-b}\left\lfloor 2^{b}\left(\log_{2}\left|x\right|+2^{a-1}-1\right)\right\rceil-2^{a-1}+1}=\mathrm{sgn}\left(x\right)2^{2^{-b}\left\lfloor 2^{b}\log_{2}\left|x\right|\right\rceil}

(9)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\mathrm{sgn}\left(x\right)\left(2^{2^{-b}2^{b}\log_{2}\left|x\right|}+\left(\left\lfloor 2^{b}\log_{2}\left|x\right|\right\rceil-2^{b}\log_{2}\left|x\right|\right)2^{2^{-b}2^{b}\log_{2}\left|x\right|}2^{-b}\ln 2\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\left(1+\left(\left\lfloor 2^{b}\log_{2}\left|x\right|\right\rceil-2^{b}\log_{2}\left|x\right|\right)2^{-b}\ln 2\right).

For
x=0
,
\mathrm{SE}a\mathrm{M}b\left(0\right)\approx\lim_{x\to 0}x\left(1+\left(\left\lfloor 2^{b}\log_{2}\left|x\right|\right\rceil-2^{b}\log_{2}\left|x\right|\right)2^{-b}\ln 2\right)=0
.
We model the casting error
\mathrm{SE}a\mathrm{M}b\left(x\right)-x=\left(\ln 2\right)2^{-b}\xi x
where
x\in\left[-\max\left|x_{\mathrm{SE}a\mathrm{M}b}\right|,\max\left|x_{\mathrm{SE}a\mathrm{M}b}\right|\right]
and the random variable
\xi\sim\mathrm{Uniform}\left(-2^{-1},2^{-1}\right)
.

For a quantization format that uses
\mathrm{E}a\mathrm{M}b
for values and
\mathrm{E}\tilde{a}\mathrm{M}\tilde{b}
for scales, we approximate it with a scalar quantizer

q\left(x\right)=\mathrm{SE}a\mathrm{M}b\left(xs^{-1}\right)\mathrm{SE}\tilde{a}\mathrm{M}\tilde{b}\left(s\right)

where

x\in\mathbb{R}
,
scale
s\in\mathbb{R}_{\neq 0}
,

\left|s\right|\leq\max\left|x_{\mathrm{SE}\tilde{a}\mathrm{M}\tilde{b}}\right|
,
and

\left|xs^{-1}\right|\leq\max\left|x_{\mathrm{SE}a\mathrm{M}b}\right|
.
Then,

\displaystyle q\left(x\right)=

\displaystyle\mathrm{SE}a\mathrm{M}b\left(xs^{-1}\right)\mathrm{SE}\tilde{a}\mathrm{M}\tilde{b}\left(s\right)=\left(1+\left(\ln 2\right)2^{-b}\xi\right)xs^{-1}\left(1+\left(\ln 2\right)2^{-\tilde{b}}\tilde{\xi}\right)s

(10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1+\left(\ln 2\right)2^{-b}\xi\right)\left(1+\left(\ln 2\right)2^{-\tilde{b}}\tilde{\xi}\right)x

with the independent random variables
\xi,\tilde{\xi}\sim\mathrm{Uniform}\left(-2^{-1},2^{-1}\right)
.

Define
\eta=\left(1+\left(\ln 2\right)2^{-b}\xi\right)\left(1+\left(\ln 2\right)2^{-\tilde{b}}\tilde{\xi}\right)-1
.
The quantization error is
q\left(x\right)-x=\eta x
.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\eta}\eta=\mathbb{E}_{\xi,\tilde{\xi}}\left(\ln 2\right)2^{-b}\xi+\left(\ln 2\right)2^{-\tilde{b}}\tilde{\xi}+\left(\ln 2\right)^{2}2^{-b-\tilde{b}}\xi\tilde{\xi}=0.

(11)

2.2.2
Loss Minimization

Using
theorem

2
, the minimization objective is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y},\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}\bm{y}\right)}\left\|\bm{T}^{-1}\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}\bm{y}\right)\right\|^{2}=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y},\bm{\eta}}\left\|\bm{T}^{-1}\operatorname{diag}\left(\bm{\eta}\right)\bm{T}\bm{y}\right\|^{2}

(12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y},\bm{\eta}}\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{y}^{\top}\bm{T}^{\top}\operatorname{diag}\left(\bm{\eta}\right)\bm{T}^{-\top}\bm{T}^{-1}\operatorname{diag}\left(\bm{\eta}\right)\bm{T}\bm{y}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y},\bm{\eta}}\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{T}^{-1}\operatorname{diag}\left(\bm{\eta}\right)\bm{T}\bm{y}\bm{y}^{\top}\bm{T}^{\top}\operatorname{diag}\left(\bm{\eta}\right)\bm{T}^{-\top}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{T}^{-1}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\eta}}\operatorname{diag}\left(\bm{\eta}\right)\bm{T}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\bm{y}\bm{y}^{\top}\right)\bm{T}^{\top}\operatorname{diag}\left(\bm{\eta}\right)\right)\bm{T}^{-\top}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}_{\eta}\eta^{2}\right)\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{T}^{-1}\left(\left(\bm{U}^{\prime}\bm{S}^{\prime 2}\bm{U}^{\prime\top}\right)\odot\mathbf{I}\right)\bm{T}^{-\top}\right)

where
\odot
represents elementwise multiplication.
The lower bound of the trace term in the loss is

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{T}^{-1}\left(\left(\bm{U}^{\prime}\bm{S}^{\prime 2}\bm{U}^{\prime\top}\right)\odot\mathbf{I}\right)\bm{T}^{-\top}\right)

(13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{T}^{-1}\operatorname{diag}\left(\left\|\bm{S}^{\prime}\bm{U}^{\prime\top}\mathbf{e}_{1}\right\|^{2},\dots,\left\|\bm{S}^{\prime}\bm{U}^{\prime\top}\mathbf{e}_{d}\right\|^{2}\right)\bm{T}^{-\top}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left({\mathbf{e}_{j}{T}^{-1}}\mathbf{e}_{k}\right)^{2}\left\|\bm{S}^{\prime}\bm{U}^{\prime\top}\mathbf{e}_{k}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left\|{T}^{-1}\mathbf{e}_{k}\right\|^{2}\left\|\bm{S}^{\prime}\bm{U}^{\prime\top}\mathbf{e}_{k}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left\|\bm{U}\bm{S}\bm{R}\bm{S}^{\prime-1}\bm{U}^{\prime\top}\mathbf{e}_{k}\right\|^{2}\left\|\bm{S}^{\prime}\bm{U}^{\prime\top}\mathbf{e}_{k}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left\|\bm{S}\bm{R}\bm{S}^{\prime-1}\bm{U}^{\prime\top}\mathbf{e}_{k}\right\|^{2}\left\|\bm{R}\bm{S}^{\prime}\bm{U}^{\prime\top}\mathbf{e}_{k}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\text{(rotation-invariance of $L_{2}$ norm)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left(\mathbf{e}_{k}^{\top}\bm{U}^{\prime}\bm{S}^{\prime-1}\bm{R}^{\top}\bm{S}\bm{R}\bm{S}^{\prime}\bm{U}^{\prime\top}\mathbf{e}_{k}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\text{(Cauchy-Schwarz)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d\left(d^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left(\mathbf{e}_{k}^{\top}\bm{U}^{\prime}\bm{S}^{\prime-1}\bm{R}^{\top}\bm{S}\bm{R}\bm{S}^{\prime}\bm{U}^{\prime\top}\mathbf{e}_{k}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle d\left(d^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\mathbf{e}_{k}^{\top}\bm{U}^{\prime}\bm{S}^{\prime-1}\bm{R}^{\top}\bm{S}\bm{R}\bm{S}^{\prime}\bm{U}^{\prime\top}\mathbf{e}_{k}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\text{(Jensen)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d^{-1}\left(\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{U}^{\prime}\bm{S}^{\prime-1}\bm{R}^{\top}\bm{S}\bm{R}\bm{S}^{\prime}\bm{U}^{\prime\top}\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d^{-1}\left(\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{S}\right)\right)^{2}.

The equality can be attained by choosing
\bm{U}^{\prime}=\bm{H}
,
\bm{S}^{\prime}=\bm{S}^{\frac{1}{2}}
, and
\bm{R}=\mathbf{I}
such that

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{T}^{-1}\left(\left(\bm{U}^{\prime}\bm{S}^{\prime 2}\bm{U}^{\prime\top}\right)\odot\mathbf{I}\right)\bm{T}^{-\top}\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left\|\bm{S}\bm{R}\bm{S}^{\prime-1}\bm{U}^{\prime\top}\mathbf{e}_{k}\right\|^{2}\left\|\bm{R}\bm{S}^{\prime}\bm{U}^{\prime\top}\mathbf{e}_{k}\right\|^{2}

(14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left(\left\|\bm{S}^{\frac{1}{2}}\bm{H}^{\top}\mathbf{e}_{k}\right\|^{2}\right)^{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}s_{j}\left(\mathbf{e}_{j}^{\top}\bm{H}^{\top}\mathbf{e}_{k}\right)^{2}\right)^{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}s_{j}d^{-1}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d^{-1}\left(\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{S}\right)\right)^{2}.

Discussion

2
, for any orthogonal
\bm{T}

\displaystyle=

so the orthogonal transforms will not be helpful for reducing the quantization error.
Also note that the non-trivial choices

,

\bm{T}
.

2.3.1
Quantization Error Modeling

We model the quantization error using pseudo noise similar to DiffQ

Theorem 3
(INT Quantization Error Modeling)

For a vector

\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
we can model the quantization error

\varepsilon\left(\bm{\alpha}\right)=\left\|\bm{\alpha}\right\|_{\infty}\bm{\eta}

with

\bm{\eta}=\left[\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{d}\right]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

being a random vector of i.i.d. samples

\eta\sim\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{\eta}}

and

\mathbb{E}_{\eta}\eta=0
.

Proof

Define the scalar quantizer of
b
-bit symmetric integer as
q\left(x\right)=\left(\left\lfloor xs^{-1}\right\rfloor+2^{-1}\right)s
, where
x\in\mathbb{R}
, scale
s\in\mathbb{R}_{\neq 0}
, and
xs^{-1}\in\left[-2^{b-1},2^{b-1}\right)
.
To simplify the analysis, we model the quantizer as
q\left(x\right)=\left(xs^{-1}+\xi\right)s=x+s\xi
with random noise
\xi\sim\mathrm{Uniform}\left(-2^{-1},2^{-1}\right)
.

For a vector
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
we use the AbsMax scale
s=2^{1-b}\left\|\bm{\alpha}\right\|_{\infty}
.
Define
\eta=2^{1-b}\xi
, then we have

s\xi=\eta\left\|\bm{\alpha}\right\|_{\infty}
.
The quantization error
\varepsilon\left(\bm{\alpha}\right)=\left\|\bm{\alpha}\right\|_{\infty}\bm{\eta}
with
\bm{\eta}=\left[\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{d}\right]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
being a random vector of i.i.d. samples

\eta\sim\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{\eta}}

and

\mathbb{E}_{\eta}\eta=2^{1-b}\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\xi=0
.

2.3.2
Loss Minimization

Using
theorem

3
, the minimization objective is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y},\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}\bm{y}\right)}\left\|\bm{T}^{-1}\varepsilon\left(\bm{T}\bm{y}\right)\right\|^{2}=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y},\bm{\eta}}\left\|\bm{T}^{-1}\left\|\bm{T}\bm{y}\right\|_{\infty}\bm{\eta}\right\|^{2}

(16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{T}^{-1}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\eta}}\bm{\eta}\bm{\eta}^{\top}\right)\bm{T}^{-\top}\right)\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\left\|\bm{T}\bm{y}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}=\left(\mathbb{E}_{\eta}\eta^{2}\right)\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{T}^{-1}\bm{T}^{-\top}\right)\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\left\|\bm{T}\bm{y}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}_{\eta}\eta^{2}\right)\left\|\bm{T}^{-1}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\left\|\bm{T}\bm{y}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}.

We can obtain a lower bound for the term
\left\|\bm{T}^{-1}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}=\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{T}^{-1}\bm{T}^{-\top}\right)=\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{U}\bm{S}\bm{R}\bm{S}^{\prime-2}\bm{R}^{\top}\bm{S}\bm{U}^{\top}\right)=\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{S}^{2}\bm{R}\bm{S}^{\prime-2}\bm{R}^{\top}\right)
.
Because
s_{1}^{2}\geq\dots\geq s_{d}^{2}
and
s_{1}^{\prime-2}\leq\dots\leq s_{d}^{\prime-2}
, using von Neumann’s trace inequality, we obtain:

\displaystyle\left\|\bm{T}^{-1}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}=\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{S}^{2}\bm{R}\bm{S}^{\prime-2}\bm{R}^{\top}\right)\geq\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{S}^{2}\bm{S}^{\prime-2}\right).

(17)

Equality is attained when
\bm{R}=\mathbf{I}
.

Next, we obtain the lower and upper bounds for the term

\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\left\|\bm{T}\bm{y}\right\|^{2}_{\infty}
.
Express the expectations as

\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\left\|\bm{T}\bm{y}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\max_{k}\left(\mathbf{e}_{k}^{\top}\bm{T}\bm{y}\right)^{2}\geq\max_{k}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\left(\mathbf{e}_{k}^{\top}\bm{T}\bm{y}\right)^{2}

(Jensen’s inequality) and

\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\left\|\bm{T}\bm{y}\right\|^{2}=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left(\mathbf{e}_{k}^{\top}\bm{T}\bm{y}\right)^{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\left(\mathbf{e}_{k}^{\top}\bm{T}\bm{y}\right)^{2}
.
By the relationships among the mean, maximum, and summation values, we have:

\displaystyle d^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\left\|\bm{T}\bm{y}\right\|^{2}\leq\max_{k}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\left(\mathbf{e}_{k}^{\top}\bm{T}\bm{y}\right)^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\left\|\bm{T}\bm{y}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\left\|\bm{T}\bm{y}\right\|^{2}\leq d\max_{k}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\left(\mathbf{e}_{k}^{\top}\bm{T}\bm{y}\right)^{2},

(18)

where

\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\left\|\bm{T}\bm{y}\right\|^{2}=\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{S}^{\prime 2}\right)

and

\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\left(\mathbf{e}_{k}^{\top}\bm{T}\bm{y}\right)^{2}=\mathbf{e}_{k}^{\top}\bm{T}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\bm{y}\bm{y}^{\top}\right)\bm{T}^{\top}\mathbf{e}_{k}=\mathbf{e}_{k}^{\top}\bm{U}^{\prime}\bm{S}^{\prime 2}\bm{U}^{\prime\top}\mathbf{e}_{k}=\left\|\bm{S}^{\prime}\bm{U}^{\prime\top}\mathbf{e}_{k}\right\|^{2}
.

Lemma 4
(Maximum Inequalities for Tail-Bounded Distributions)

If
X_{1},\dots,X_{d}\in\mathbb{R}
are zero-mean Gaussian random variables,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\max_{k}X_{k}^{2}\leq\min\left\{d,\left(2\ln\left(2d\right)+2\right)\right\}\max_{k}\mathbb{E}X_{k}^{2}.

(19)

If
X_{1},\dots,X_{d}\in\mathbb{R}
are zero-mean Laplacian random variables,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\max_{k}X_{k}^{2}\leq\left(2^{-1}\left(\ln d\right)^{2}+\ln d+1\right)\max_{k}\mathbb{E}X_{k}^{2}.

(20)

Both inequalities hold without the need for independence.

Proof

We provide proofs for Gaussian and Laplacian distributions in
sections

A.1
and
A.2
, respectively.

Using Lemma
4
, for a tail-bounded
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{y}}
that is a zero-mean multivariate Gaussian or Laplacian, we can further tighten the upper bound by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\left\|\bm{T}\bm{y}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq\min\left\{d^{o\left(1\right)}\max_{k}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\left(\mathbf{e}_{k}^{\top}\bm{T}\bm{y}\right)^{2},\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\left\|\bm{T}\bm{y}\right\|^{2}\right\}.

(21)

When
\bm{U}^{\prime}=\bm{H}
, the marginal second moment becomes

\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\left(\mathbf{e}_{k}^{\top}\bm{T}\bm{y}\right)^{2}=\left\|\bm{S}^{\prime}\bm{U}^{\prime\top}\mathbf{e}_{k}\right\|=\left\|\bm{S}^{\prime}\bm{H}^{\top}\mathbf{e}_{k}\right\|=\sum_{j=1}^{d}s_{j}^{\prime 2}d^{-1}=d^{-1}\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{S}^{\prime 2}\right)=d^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\left\|\bm{T}\bm{y}\right\|^{2}
,
which is equalized for all
k
, and

\max_{k}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\left(\mathbf{e}_{k}^{\top}\bm{T}\bm{y}\right)^{2}

is minimized to

d^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\left\|\bm{T}\bm{y}\right\|^{2}
.
Taken together, by setting
\bm{R}=\mathbf{I}
and
\bm{U}^{\prime}=\bm{H}
, the term
\left\|\bm{T}^{-1}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\left\|\bm{T}\bm{y}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}
is bounded by
\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{S^{2}\bm{S}^{\prime-2}}\right)\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{S}^{\prime 2}\right)
within a gap of
d
such that

\displaystyle d^{-1}\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{S^{2}\bm{S}^{\prime-2}}\right)\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{S}^{\prime 2}\right)\leq\left\|\bm{T}^{-1}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\left\|\bm{T}\bm{y}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{S^{2}\bm{S}^{\prime-2}}\right)\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{S}^{\prime 2}\right).

(22)

And for a tail-bounded
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{y}}
, the gap is narrowed to
d^{o\left(1\right)}
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\bm{T}^{-1}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\left\|\bm{T}\bm{y}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}=d^{o\left(1\right)-1}\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{S^{2}\bm{S}^{\prime-2}}\right)\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{S}^{\prime 2}\right).

(23)

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{S}^{2}\bm{S}^{\prime-2}\right)\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{S}^{\prime 2}\right)\geq\left(\operatorname{tr}\left(\left(\bm{S}^{2}\bm{S}^{\prime-2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\bm{S}^{\prime 2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right)^{2}=\left(\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{S}\right)\right)^{2}

(24)

and the equality is attained when
\bm{S}^{\prime}\propto\bm{S}^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
Without loss of generality, we can choose
\bm{S}^{\prime}=\bm{S}^{\frac{1}{2}}
to minimize both the lower and upper bounds of the quantization loss.

2.3.3
Discussion

Consider the case of
\bm{T}
being orthogonal.

\left\|\bm{T}^{-1}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}=d
and
\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\left\|\bm{T}\bm{y}\right\|^{2}=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\left\|\bm{y}\right\|^{2}=\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{S}^{2}\right)
.
For any distribution
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{y}}
, the bounds are

\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{S}^{2}\right)\leq\left\|\bm{T}^{-1}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\left\|\bm{T}\bm{y}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq d\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{S}^{2}\right)
.
For the Hadamard
\bm{T}=\bm{H}
and a tail-bounded
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{y}}
,

\left\|\bm{T}^{-1}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}}\left\|\bm{T}\bm{y}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}=d^{o\left(1\right)}\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{S}^{2}\right)

with high probability.
Because

\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{S}^{2}\right)\geq d^{-1}\left(\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{S}\right)\right)^{2}\geq d^{-1}\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{S}^{2}\right)
,
an orthogonal
\bm{T}
is suboptimal,
and the error bounds of an orthogonal
\bm{T}
can be at most
d
times larger than those of the optimal
\bm{T}
, with
\operatorname{tr}\left(\bm{S}\right)\approx s_{1}
being the extreme case scenario.

4
Future Work

A natural next step is to integrate WUSH with advanced post-training quantization schemes such as GPTQ. In this setting, the weights are updated iteratively through error propagation, while WUSH is constructed from the second-order statistics of the updated weights. Designing an algorithm that jointly updates the weights and the associated blockwise transforms is non-trivial and will require adapting both the optimization schedule and the calibration pipeline.

From a systems perspective, efficient kernel support for WUSH is another important direction. Unlike blockwise Hadamard rotations, which reuse a single transform across all blocks, WUSH generally assigns a distinct transform to each block. This per-block specialization complicates kernel reuse and fusion in existing low-level libraries.

Finally, it will be useful to explore structurally simplified variants of WUSH that trade optimality for lower computational overhead. One promising avenue is to approximate the data-aware component with a diagonal matrix, thereby reducing the cost of applying the transforms at inference time.

5
Conclusion

We have studied blockwise weight-activation quantization for LLMs in the presence of heavy-tailed outliers and have shown that, under mild assumptions, this problem admits closed-form linear transforms for both FP and INT block quantizers. Our construction, WUSH, combines a fixed Hadamard backbone with a data-aware component derived from second-order statistics, yielding a non-orthogonal blockwise transform that is provably optimal and remains amenable to efficient implementation. These results clarify the empirical success of Hadamard rotations and indicate that principled, data-aware block transforms can significantly narrow the gap between low-bit and full-precision models.

Lemma 5
(Expectation from the Survival Function)

Let
X\geq 0
be a random variable with the density function
f
and the survival function
S(t)=\mathbb{P}\left(X\geq t\right)
with
t\geq 0
.
Let
g:\left[0,\infty\right)\to\mathbb{R}
be integrable and define

G\left(t\right)=\int_{0}^{t}g\left(u\right)\mathrm{d}u
.
Assume that

\lim_{t\to\infty}G\left(t\right)S\left(t\right)=0
.
Then

\mathbb{E}G\left(X\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}g\left(t\right)S\left(t\right)\mathrm{d}t
.

Proof

Since
X
has density
f
and takes values in
[0,\infty)
,

\mathbb{E}G\left(X\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}G\left(t\right)f\left(t\right)\mathrm{d}t
.
The survival function satisfies

S\left(t\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(X\geq t\right)=\int_{t}^{\infty}f(u)\mathrm{d}u
,
so
S
is differentiable almost everywhere with

S^{\prime}\left(t\right)=-f\left(t\right)
.
Hence,

\mathbb{E}G\left(X\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}G\left(t\right)f\left(t\right)\mathrm{d}t=-\int_{0}^{\infty}G\left(t\right)S^{\prime}\left(t\right)\mathrm{d}t
.
We now integrate by parts, using
G^{\prime}\left(t\right)=g\left(t\right)
,

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\infty}G\left(t\right)S^{\prime}\left(t\right)\mathrm{d}t=-\left[G\left(t\right)S\left(t\right)\right]_{0}^{\infty}+\int_{0}^{\infty}g\left(t\right)S\left(t\right)\mathrm{d}t.

(26)

By definition,
G\left(0\right)=0
, and by assumption

\lim_{t\to\infty}G\left(t\right)S\left(t\right)=0
,
the boundary term

\left[G\left(t\right)S\left(t\right)\right]_{0}^{\infty}=0
.
Therefore,

\mathbb{E}G\left(X\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}g\left(t\right)S\left(t\right)\mathrm{d}t
.

Gaussian Distribution

Definition of sub-Gaussian.

A probability distribution of a random variable

is called sub-Gaussian with variance proxy

\mathbb{E}\exp\left(\left(X-\mathbb{E}X\right)t\right)\leq\exp\left(2^{-1}\sigma^{2}t^{2}\right)

Basic properties of sub-Gaussian distributions.

The variance proxy is larger than or equal to the variance:
\sigma^{2}\geq\mathbb{E}\left(X-\mathbb{E}X\right)^{2}
.
For a Gaussian distribution, the optimal variance proxy is the same as the variance.

Chernoff bound on the tail:
\mathbb{P}\left(\left|X-\mathbb{E}X\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-2^{-1}\sigma^{-2}t^{2}\right)

t\geq 0

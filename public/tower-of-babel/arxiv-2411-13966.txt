On comass and stable systolic inequalities
James J. Hebda, Mikhail G. Katz

Abstract
. We study the maximum ratio of the Euclidean norm to the comass norm of p p -covectors in Euclidean n n -space and improve the known upper bound found in the standard references by Whitney and Federer. We go on to prove stable systolic inequalities when the fundamental cohomology class of the manifold is a cup product of forms of lower degree.



1.
Introduction

In the first part of this paper we investigate the maximal ratio
C_{n,p}
of the Euclidean norm to the comass norm of
p
-covectors in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
.
According to the standard references Federer
[
7
, 1.81]
and Whitney
[
17
, equation (18) p. 50]
,

C_{n,p}\leq\binom{n}{p}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

In Propositions
2.3
and
2.5
, we establish
inequalities among the
C_{n,p}
which lead to an improved upper bound on
C_{n,p}
.
As far as we are aware, these two propositions haven’t appeared in the earlier literature.
In Corollary
4.2
, we obtain an upper bound on the comass of the wedge product of two covectors that improves the upper bound found in
[
7
, p. 39]
and
[
17
, p. 55]
. One of the main results of Goodwillie et al.,
[
9
]
, is an upper bound on the Euclidean norm of a product of an arbitrary number of
2
-covectors in terms of the product of their comasses. In Proposition
4.3
, we generalize this result for wedge products of an arbitrary number of
p
-covectors.

In the last part of this paper, using the methods of
[
9
]
, we apply these results to prove stable systolic inequalities when the fundamental cohomology class of the manifold is a cup product of forms of lower degree. Background material, including other references, on stable systolic inequalities of this type can be found in
[
13
]
.

2.
Norms on
\Lambda^{p}(\mathbf{R}^{n})^{\ast}

Let
\Lambda^{p}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
be the vector space of
p
-vectors in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
, and let
\Lambda^{p}(\mathbf{R}^{n})^{\ast}
be that of
p
-covectors. The Euclidean and mass norms of
\xi\in\Lambda^{p}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
are denoted
|\xi|
and
\|\xi\|
respectively, while the Euclidean and comass norms of
\phi\in\Lambda^{p}(\mathbf{R}^{n})^{\ast}
are denoted
|\phi|^{\ast}
and
\|\phi\|^{\ast}
respectively. Recall that by definition

\|\phi\|^{\ast}=\sup\{\phi(\xi):\xi\in\Lambda^{p}(\mathbf{R}^{n}),\xi\mathrm{\ is\ simple},|\xi|\leq 1\}.

Further information on mass and comass can be found in
[
7
, 1.81]
or
[
17
, Chapter I, §13]
.

2.1.
Norm comparison

This section is concerned with the ratio of the Euclidean norm to the comass norm.

Definition 2.1
.

C_{n,p}=\sup\left\{\frac{|\phi|^{\ast}}{\|\phi\|^{\ast}}:\phi\in\Lambda^{p}(\mathbf{R}^{n})^{\ast},\kern 5.0pt\phi\neq 0\right\}.

Remark 2.2
.

The following facts about
C_{n,p}
are well–known
[
7
,
17
]
:

(1)

Because the Hodge star operation preserves the Euclidean and comass norms,
C_{n,n-p}=C_{n,p}
.

(2)

Because 1-covectors and
(n-1)
-covectors are simple,
C_{n,1}=C_{n,n-1}=1
and, because
2
-covectors can be put into a (diagonal) canonical form
[
15
, Theorem 5.1, p. 24]
,
C^{2}_{n,2}=C^{2}_{n,n-2}=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
.

(3)

The upper bound
C_{n,p}^{2}\leq\binom{n}{p}
always holds.

Proposition 2.3
.

Suppose
1<p<n-1
, then

C_{n,p}^{2}\leq C_{n-1,p-1}^{2}+C_{n-1,p}^{2}.

Proof.

Let
\phi\in\Lambda^{p}(\mathbf{R}^{n})^{\ast}
. Let
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
be an orthonormal basis for
\mathbf{R}^{n}
with dual basis
e_{1}^{\ast},\dots,e_{n}^{\ast}
. We may write

\phi=\psi\wedge e^{\ast}_{n}+\chi

where
\psi\in\Lambda^{p-1}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})^{\ast}
and
\chi\in\Lambda^{p}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})^{\ast}
. There exist a simple
(p-1)
–vector
\xi\in\Lambda^{p-1}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})\subset\Lambda^{p-1}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
and a simple
p
–vector
\xi^{\prime}\in\Lambda^{p}(\mathrm{R}^{n-1})\subset\Lambda^{p}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
, both of Euclidean norm 1, such that
\psi(\xi)=\|\psi\|^{\ast}
and

\chi(\xi^{\prime})=\|\chi\|^{\ast}
. Then
\xi\wedge e_{n}
is a simple
p
–vector of Euclidean norm 1. Thus

\|\phi\|^{\ast}\geq\phi(\xi\wedge e_{n})=(\psi\wedge e^{\ast}_{n})(\xi\wedge e_{n})+\chi(\xi\wedge e_{n})=\psi(\xi)+0=\|\psi\|^{\ast}

and

\|\phi\|^{\ast}\geq\phi(\xi^{\prime})=(\psi\wedge e^{\ast}_{n})(\xi^{\prime})+\chi(\xi^{\prime})=0+\chi(\xi^{\prime})=\|\chi\|^{\ast}.

But

|\psi|^{\ast}\leq C_{n-1,p-1}\|\psi\|^{\ast}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad|\chi|^{\ast}\leq C_{n-1,p}\|\chi\|^{\ast}.

Hence

\displaystyle|\phi|^{\ast 2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\psi\wedge e_{n}^{\ast}|^{\ast 2}+|\chi|^{\ast 2}=|\psi|^{\ast 2}+|\chi|^{\ast 2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{n-1,p-1}^{2}\|\psi\|^{\ast 2}+C_{n-1,p}^{2}\|\chi\|^{\ast 2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{n-1,p-1}^{2}\|\phi\|^{\ast 2}+C_{n-1,p}^{2}\|\phi\|^{\ast 2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(C_{n-1,p-1}^{2}+C_{n-1,p}^{2})\|\phi\|^{\ast 2}.

Therefore, since this is true for all
\phi
,

C_{n,p}^{2}\leq C_{n-1,p-1}^{2}+C_{n-1,p}^{2}.

∎

The estimate of Remark
2.2
(3) is not optimal. The following
result gives a better upper bound for
C_{n,p}
.

Corollary 2.4
.

C_{n,p}^{2}\leq\binom{n-2}{p-1}

Proof.

Arrange the constants
C_{n,p}^{2}
in a triangular array in the manner of Pascal’s Triangle:

C_{2,1}^{2}

C_{3,1}^{2}

C_{3,2}^{2}

C_{4,1}^{2}

C_{4,2}^{2}

C_{4,3}^{2}

C_{5,1}^{2}

C_{5,2}^{2}

C_{5,3}^{2}

C_{5,4}^{2}

⋮

First observe that the first and last terms on each row of the triangle satisfy

C_{n,1}^{2}=1=\binom{n-2}{0}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad C_{n,n-1}=1=\binom{n-2}{n-2}.

The rest of the proof is by induction, for by Proposition
2.3
,

C_{n,p}^{2}\leq C_{n-1,p-1}^{2}+C_{n-1,p}^{2}\leq\binom{n-3}{p-2}+\binom{n-3}{p-1}=\binom{n-2}{p-1}

by the defining property of the binomial coefficients in Pascal’s Triangle, namely, that
\binom{n}{k}+\binom{n}{k+1}=\binom{n+1}{k+1}
.
∎

Clearly,
\binom{n-2}{p-1}<\binom{n}{p}
when
1\leq p\leq n-1
. The next proposition gives an even better upper bound on
C_{n,p}
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
1\leq k<p<n
, then

C_{n,p}^{2}\leq\frac{\binom{n}{p-k}}{\binom{p}{k}}C_{n,k}^{2}.

Proof.

Let
\phi=\sum_{I}\beta_{I}e_{I}^{\ast}\in\Lambda^{p}(\mathbf{R}^{n})^{\ast}
where
I
runs over all multi–indices of length
p
, that is,
I
is a strictly increasing sequence of
p
integers between
1
and
n
.

Fix a multi–index
J
of length
p-k
of integers between
1
and

n
. Suppose
I
is a multi–index of length
p
that contains
J
as
a subsequence. In this case write
I\supset J
. Let
I\backslash J

denote the multi–index of length
k
complementary to
J
in

I
. Thus
e_{I\backslash J}\wedge e_{J}=\pm e_{I}\in\Lambda^{p}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
where the sign depends on the permutation of
the elements in
I
. Pull out the terms of
\phi
that contain all the
indices in
J
by setting

\phi_{J}=\sum_{I\supset J}\beta_{I}e_{I}^{\ast}.

Then let

\psi_{J}=\sum_{I\supset J}\pm\beta_{I}e^{\ast}_{I\backslash J}\in\Lambda^{k}(\mathbf{R}^{n})^{\ast},

where the sign is chosen to agree with the sign in
e_{I\backslash J}\wedge e_{J}=\pm e_{I}
, and the sums run over all multi-indices
I
of length
p
with
I\supset J
.
Thus
\phi_{J}=\psi_{J}\wedge e_{J}^{\ast}
and
|\psi_{J}|^{\ast}=\sqrt{\sum_{I\supset J}\beta_{I}^{2}}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

(2.1)

\|\phi\|^{\ast 2}C_{n,k}^{2}\geq\sum_{I\supset J}\beta_{I}^{2}.

Proof.

Choose a simple
k
–vector
\xi_{J}\in\Lambda^{k}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
such that
|\xi_{J}|=1
and
\psi_{J}(\xi_{J})=\|\psi_{J}\|^{\ast}
. Let
V=\mathrm{span}\{e_{i}:i\notin J\}\subset\mathbf{R}^{n}
. Since by construction
\psi_{J}\in\Lambda^{k}(V)^{\ast}
, we may assume that
\xi_{J}\in\Lambda^{k}(V)\subset\Lambda^{k}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
. Since
e_{J}
is a simple
p-k
vector of norm
1
,
\xi_{J}\wedge e_{J}
is a simple
p
-vector with
|\xi_{J}\wedge e_{J}|=1
. Noting that
e_{I}^{\ast}(\xi_{J}\wedge e_{J})=0
when
I\not\supset J
, it follows that

\|\phi\|^{\ast}\geq\phi(\xi_{J}\wedge e_{J})=\phi_{J}(\xi_{J}\wedge e_{J})=(\psi_{J}\wedge e_{J}^{\ast})(\xi_{J}\wedge e_{J})=\psi_{J}(\xi_{J})e_{J}^{\ast}(e_{J})=\|\psi_{J}\|^{\ast}.

Hence, by definition of
C_{n,k}
,

\sqrt{\sum_{I\supset J}\beta_{I}^{2}}=|\psi_{J}|^{\ast}\leq C_{n,k}\|\psi_{J}\|^{\ast}\leq C_{n,k}\|\phi\|^{\ast}.

On squaring this inequality,

\quad\|\phi\|^{\ast 2}C_{n,k}^{2}\geq\sum_{I\supset J}\beta_{I}^{2},

which completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Returning to the proof of Proposition
2.5
, summing the inequality (
2.1
) over all
J
gives

\binom{n}{p-k}C_{n,k}^{2}\|\phi\|^{\ast 2}\geq\sum_{J}\sum_{I\supset J}\beta_{I}^{2}=\binom{p}{k}\sum_{I}\beta_{I}^{2}=\binom{p}{k}|\phi|^{\ast 2}

since in the double sum each
\beta_{I}^{2}
occurs
\binom{p}{k}
times, because there are
\binom{p}{k}
multi–indices
J
contained in each
I
.
Hence

\frac{|\phi|^{\ast 2}}{\|\phi\|^{\ast 2}}\leq\frac{\binom{n}{p-k}}{\binom{p}{k}}C_{n,k}^{2}.

Therefore, since
\phi
was arbitrary,

C_{n,p}^{2}\leq\frac{\binom{n}{p-k}}{\binom{p}{k}}C_{n,k}^{2}.

∎

Remark 2.7
.

Proposition
2.5
improves the upper estimate on
C_{n,p}
in Remark
2.2
(3). For example, taking
k=1
,

\frac{\binom{n}{p-1}}{\binom{p}{1}}C_{n,1}^{2}=\frac{1}{n-p+1}\binom{n}{p}.

When
p=2
and
k=1
,

\frac{\binom{n}{1}}{\binom{2}{1}}C_{n,1}^{2}=\frac{n}{2}

which, according to Remark
2.2
(2), is the optimal bound on
C^{2}_{n,2}
when
n
is even, but not when
n
is odd.
One may also check that

\frac{1}{n-p+1}\binom{n}{p}\leq\binom{n-2}{p-1}

when
1\leq p\leq\frac{n}{2}
and the inequality is strict unless
p=1
or
(n,p)=(4,2)
.
Thus Proposition
2.5
generally provides a better upper bound on
C_{n,p}
than Corollary
2.4
.

4.
Wedge products

Proposition 4.1
.

If
\phi\in\Lambda^{p}(\mathbf{R}^{n})^{\ast}
and
\psi\in\Lambda^{n-p}(\mathbf{R}^{n})^{\ast}
, then

|\phi\wedge\psi|^{\ast}\leq C_{n,p}^{2}\|\phi\|^{\ast}\|\psi\|^{\ast}.

Proof.

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

|\phi\wedge\psi|^{\ast}=|\langle\phi,*\psi\rangle|\leq|\phi|^{\ast}|\psi|^{\ast}\leq C_{n,p}\|\phi\|^{\ast}C_{n,n-p}\|\psi\|^{\ast}=C_{n,p}^{2}\|\phi\|^{\ast}\|\psi\|^{\ast}

since
C_{n,p}=C_{n,n-p}
.
∎

Since
C_{p+q,p}^{2}<\binom{p+q}{p}
, the next corollary improves the bound

\|\phi\wedge\psi\|^{\ast}\leq\binom{p+q}{p}\|\phi\|^{\ast}\|\psi\|^{\ast}

in
[
7
, p. 39]
or
[
17
, p. 55]
where
\phi
and
\psi
are covectors in degrees
p
and
q
respectively.

Corollary 4.2
.

If
\phi\in\Lambda^{p}(\mathbf{R}^{n})^{\ast}
and
\psi\in\Lambda^{q}(\mathbf{R}^{n})^{\ast}
, then

\|\phi\wedge\psi\|^{\ast}\leq C_{p+q,p}^{2}\|\phi\|^{\ast}\|\psi\|^{\ast}.

Proof.

Let
\xi\in\Lambda^{p+q}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
be a simple
(p+q)
-vector with
|\xi|=1
such that
(\phi\wedge\psi)(\xi)=\|\phi\wedge\psi\|^{\ast}
.
Let
V
be the
(p+q)
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbf{R}^{n}
defined by
\xi
so that
\xi\in\Lambda^{p+q}(V)
, and
let
\phi_{V}\in\Lambda^{p}(V)
and
\psi_{V}\in\Lambda^{q}(V)
be the restrictions of
\phi
and
\psi
to
V
. Then
\|\phi_{V}\|^{\ast}\leq\|\phi\|^{\ast}
,
\|\psi_{V}\|^{\ast}\leq\|\psi\|^{\ast}
, and

|\phi_{V}\wedge\psi_{V}|^{\ast}=(\phi_{V}\wedge\psi_{V})(\xi)=(\phi\wedge\psi)(\xi)=\|\phi\wedge\psi\|^{\ast}.

Therefore applying Proposition
4.1
,

\|\phi\wedge\psi\|^{\ast}=|\phi_{V}\wedge\psi_{V}|^{\ast}\leq C_{p+q,p}^{2}\|\phi_{V}\|^{\ast}\|\psi_{V}\|^{\ast}\leq C_{p+q,p}^{2}\|\phi\|^{\ast}\|\psi\|^{\ast}.

∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{m}\in\Lambda^{p}(\mathbf{R}^{mp})^{\ast}
, then

|\phi_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\phi_{m}|^{\ast}\leq C_{mp,p}^{2}C_{(m-1)p,p}^{2}\cdots C_{2p,p}^{2}\|\phi_{1}\|^{\ast}\cdots\|\phi_{m}\|^{\ast}

Proof.

Applying Corollary
4.2
successively multiple times, we have that

\displaystyle\|\phi_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\phi_{m}\|^{\ast}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{mp,p}^{2}\|\phi_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\phi_{m-1}\|^{\ast}\|\phi_{m}\|^{\ast}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{mp,p}^{2}C_{p(m-1),p}^{2}\|\phi_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\phi_{m-2}\|^{\ast}\|\phi_{m-1}\|^{\ast}\|\phi_{m}\|^{\ast}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{mp,p}^{2}C_{(m-1)p,p}^{2}\cdots C_{2p,p}^{2}\|\phi_{1}\|^{\ast}\cdots\|\phi_{m}\|^{\ast}

Finally,
\|\phi_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\phi_{m}\|^{\ast}=|\phi_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\phi_{m}|^{\ast}
, since
\phi_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\phi_{m}
is an
mp
-covector in

\mathbf{R}^{mp}
.
∎

In the case
p=2
,
C_{2m,2}^{2}C_{2(m-1),2}^{2}\cdots C_{4,2}^{2}=m!
by Remark
2.2
(2) (cf.
[

5.
Systolic inequalities

Let
M
be a smooth compact orientable manifold of dimension
n
. The image of the
p
–th integral homology group
H_{p}(M,\mathbf{Z})
in
H_{p}(M,\mathbf{R})
is the lattice, denoted
\mathsf{L}_{p}(M)
, in
H_{p}(M,\mathbf{R})
. Similarly, the image of the
p
-th integral cohomology group

H^{p}(M,\mathbf{Z})
in
H^{p}(M,\mathbf{R})
, denoted
\mathsf{L}^{p}(M)
, is the dual lattice of
\mathsf{L}_{p}(M)

under the Kronecker pairing
[
13
, Lemma 15.4.2]
. In the following, we identify
H^{p}(M,\mathbf{R})
with the de Rham cohomology group
H_{dR}^{p}(M)

under the canonical isomorphism
[
16
, Theorem 5.6]
and thereby regard
\mathsf{L}^{p}(M)
as a lattice in
H_{dR}^{p}(M)
represented by closed forms of integral periods.

If
M
is endowed with a Riemannian metric
g
, the comass of a differential
p
-form
\phi\in\Omega^{p}(M)
is defined to be the supremum of the pointwise comasses,

\|\phi\|^{\ast}_{\infty}=\sup\{\|\phi_{x}\|^{\ast}:x\in M\},

and the comass
\|\alpha\|^{\ast}
of a de Rham cohomology class
\alpha\in H^{p}_{dR}(M)
is defined to be the infimum the comasses of all closed
p
-forms representing
\alpha
. The mass of a
p
- dimensional current
T
on
M
is defined to be

|T|=\sup\{T(\phi):\phi\in\Omega^{p}(M)\kern 5.0pt\mathrm{such\kern 5.0ptthat}\kern 5.0pt\|\phi\|_{\infty}^{\ast}\leq 1\},

and the stable mass norm
\|h\|
of a homology class
h\in H_{p}(M,\mathbf{R})
is defined to be the infimum of of
|T|
over all cycles
T
that represent
h
.
The stable mass norm in homology is dual to the comass norm in cohomology. (See
[
10
, 4.35]
,
[
8
, 4.10]
.)

If the
p
-th Betti number of
M
is
b
, let
\lambda_{1}(\mathsf{L}_{p}(M)),\dots,\lambda_{b}(\mathsf{L}_{p}(M))
denote the successive minimums of the lattice
\mathsf{L}_{p}(M)
relative to the stable mass norm, and let
\lambda_{1}(\mathsf{L}^{p}(M)),\dots,\lambda_{b}(\mathsf{L}^{p}(M))
denote the successive minimums of the lattice
\mathsf{L}^{p}(M)
relative to the comass norm
[
5
]
.

Our inequalities will need the constant
\Gamma_{b}
defined as follows:.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
b>0
. Then
\Gamma_{b}

is the supremum of
\lambda_{1}(L)\lambda_{b}(L^{\ast})
over all lattices in all
b
-dimensional Banach spaces where
\lambda_{1}(L)
is the first successive minimum of
L
and
\lambda_{b}(L^{\ast})
is the
b
-th successive minimum of the dual lattice
L^{\ast}
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
M
be a compact orientable manifold of dimension
n
with
p
-th Betti number
b>0
. Then for all Riemannian metrics
g
on
M
,

\frac{\mathrm{stsys}_{p}(M,g)\mathrm{stsys}_{n-p}(M,g)}{\mathrm{vol}(M,g)}\leq C_{n,p}^{2}(\Gamma_{b})^{2}.

Proof.

By Poincaré duality, there exist cohomology classes
\alpha\in H^{p}_{dR}(M)
and
\beta\in H^{n-p}_{dR}(M)
such that
\alpha\cup\beta\neq 0
. Since
the vector space
H^{p}_{dR}(M)
is spanned by a set
consisting of cohomology classes in
\mathsf{L}^{p}(M)
of comass at most
\lambda_{b}(\mathsf{L}^{p}(M))
, and
H^{n-p}_{dR}(M)
is spanned by a set consisting of cohomology classes in
\mathsf{L}^{n-p}(M)
of comass at most
\lambda_{b}(\mathsf{L}^{n-p}(M))
,
we may assume that
\alpha\in\mathsf{L}^{p}(M)
with
\|\alpha\|^{\ast}\leq\lambda_{b}(\mathsf{L}^{p}(M))
and that
\beta\in\mathsf{L}^{n-p}(M)
with
\|\beta\|^{\ast}\leq\lambda_{b}(\mathsf{L}^{n-p}(M))
.

Let
\phi
and
\psi
be closed differential forms representing
\alpha
and
\beta
.
By Proposition
4.1
applied pointwise,
|\phi\wedge\psi|^{\ast}\leq C_{n,p}^{2}\|\phi\|_{\infty}^{\ast}\|\psi\|_{\infty}^{\ast}
. Then

(5.1)

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\int_{M}\alpha\cup\beta\right|\leq C_{n,p}^{2}\|\alpha\|^{\ast}\|\beta\|^{\ast}\mathrm{vol}(M,g)

(5.2)

\displaystyle C_{n,p}^{2}\lambda_{b}(\mathsf{L}^{p}(M))\lambda_{b}(\mathsf{L}^{n-p}(M))\mathrm{vol}(M,g).

Remark 5.3
.

The constant
\Gamma_{b}
is a modification of the Hermite constant
\gamma_{b}
. The modification is needed to take into account non-Euclidean normed spaces that arise naturally on cohomology groups.

The constants
\Gamma_{b}
are not known to be a linear function of the Betti number, since Banaszczyk
[
2
, Corollary 2]
only proves
\Gamma_{b}\leq C(b(1+\log b))
for some constant
C
. But in a pair of complementary dimensions, as in Theorem
5.2
, by using the
L^{2}
norm on harmonic forms, one can reduce everything to lattices in Euclidean space, thereby replacing
\Gamma_{b}
by the Hermite constant
\gamma_{b}
in the general case
[
12
, Proposition 6]
or by the Bergé-Martinet constant
\gamma^{\prime}_{b}
in the
(1,n-1)
case
[
3
]
. The Hermite and Bergé-Martinet constants satisfy
\gamma_{b}^{\prime}\leq\gamma_{b}\leq\frac{3}{2}b
for
b\geq 2

[
13
, (5.3.4)]
.

The next result extends the main result in
[
9
]
for
p=2
to arbitrary
p
. The reference
[
4
]
gives necessary and sufficient conditions for inequalities of this type.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
M
be a compact orientable manifold of dimension
mp
whose fundamental cohomology class can be expressed as a product of cohomology classes of degree
p
. Then for all Riemannian metrics
g
on
M
,

\frac{{\mathrm{stsys}_{p}(M,g)^{m}}}{\mathrm{vol}(M,g)}\leq C_{mp,p}^{2}C_{(m-1)p,p}^{2}\cdots C_{2p,p}^{2}(\Gamma_{b})^{m}

where
b
is the
p
-th Betti number of
M
.

Proof.

By assumption there exist cohomology classes
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m}\in H^{p}_{dR}(M)
such that

\alpha_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\alpha_{m}\neq 0
. Again we may assume
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m}\in\mathsf{L}^{p}(M)
and that

\|\alpha_{i}\|^{\ast}\leq\lambda_{b}(\mathsf{L}^{p}(M))
for all
i
.

Let
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{m}
be closed differential
p
-forms representing
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m}
. Applying Proposition
4.3

pointwise gives

|\phi_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\phi_{m}|^{\ast}\leq C_{mp,p}^{2}C_{(m-1)p,p}^{2}\cdots C_{2p,p}^{2}\|\phi_{1}\|_{\infty}^{\ast}\cdots\|\phi_{m}\|_{\infty}^{\ast}.

Then

(5.3)

1\leq\left|\int_{M}\alpha_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\alpha_{m}\right|\leq C_{mp,p}^{2}C_{(m-1)p,p}^{2}\cdots C_{2p,p}^{2}\|\alpha_{1}\|^{\ast}\cdots\|\alpha_{m}\|^{\ast}\mathrm{\ vol}(M,g).

Multiply this inequality by
\mathrm{stsys}_{p}(M,g)^{m}=\lambda_{1}(\mathsf{L}_{p}(M,g))^{m}
and divide by
\mathrm{vol}(M,g)
to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{stsys}_{p}(M,g)^{m}}{\mathrm{vol}(M,g)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{mp,p}^{2}C_{(m-1)p,p}^{2}\cdots C_{2p,p}^{2}(\lambda_{1}(\mathsf{L}_{p}(M)))^{m}(\lambda_{b}(\mathsf{L}^{p}(M))^{m}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{mp,p}^{2}C_{(m-1)p,p}^{2}\cdots C_{2p,p}^{2}(\Gamma_{b})^{m}

by the definition of
\Gamma_{b}
.
∎

Using the exact values of
C_{n,p}
from Section 3, and applying Theorems
5.2
or
5.4
, we obtain:

Corollary 5.5
.

The following statements hold:

(1)

For every Riemannian metric
g
on a compact orientable 6-dimensional manifold
M
with third Betti number
b>0

\mathrm{stsys}_{3}(M,g)^{2}\leq 4(\Gamma_{b})^{2}\mathrm{vol}(M,g)

(2)

For every Riemannian metric
g
on a compact orientable 7-dimensional manifold
M
with third Betti number
b>0

\mathrm{stsys}_{3}(M,g)\mathrm{stsys}_{4}(M,g)\leq 7(\Gamma_{b})^{2}\mathrm{vol}(M,g)

(3)

For every Riemannian metric
g
on a compact orientable 8-dimensional manifold
M
with fourth Betti number
b>0

\mathrm{stsys}_{4}(M,g)^{2}\leq 14(\Gamma_{b})^{2}\mathrm{vol}(M,g)

(4)

For every Riemannian metric
g
on a compact orientable 2m-dimensional manifold
M
with second Betti number
b>0

\mathrm{stsys}_{2}(M,g)\mathrm{stsys}_{2m-2}(M,g)\leq m(\Gamma_{b})^{2}\mathrm{vol}(M,g)

Equality holds in (4) when
M=\mathbf{CP}^{m}
with the standard metric, because in this case
b=1
and thus
\Gamma_{1}=1
. Recall Gromov’s stable systolic inequality for
\mathbf{CP}^{m}

[
10
, Theorem 4.36]
, which asserts that for any Riemannian metric

on
\mathbf{CP}^{m}
,

\mathrm{stsys}_{2}(\mathbf{CP}^{m},g)^{m}\leq m!\mathrm{vol}(\mathbf{CP}^{m},g)

\mathrm{stsys}_{2m-2}(\mathbf{CP}^{m},g)=\mathrm{stsys}_{2}(\mathbf{CP}^{m},g)^{m-1}/(m-1)!

Do massive neutrino states really exist?
Danil Shelkovkin, Oleg V. Teryaev

Abstract
In neutrino physics, it is conventional to work with massive states corresponding to defined neutrino masses. However, in weak interaction processes, only flavor states participate, and the propagation can be described by an off-diagonal mass matrix. In this work, we operate directly with the non-diagonal mass matrix and employ a quantum field theory approach to describe neutrino oscillations within the plane-wave approximation, circumventing the use of wave packets. Within this framework, numerical estimates for the amplitude of a local process involving lepton interactions via virtual neutrino exchange yield values of order 10 − 53 10^{-53} . For macroscopic processes with neutrino exchange, application of the matrix propagator reproduces the standard quantum mechanical neutrino oscillation probability formula while preserving relativistic invariance.

Introduction

Typically, the neutrino which is propagating for the long distances, is considered as a real particle and thus must exist in a mass eigenstate. This is achieved through the diagonalization of the mass matrix entering the respective Lagrangian. However, within the modern framework, it is treated as a
virtual
particle. This then enables the computation of these propagators without having to diagonalize the mass matrix.

In this work, we extend the QFT approach to processes with lepton number violation, analyzing a two-flavor model of Dirac neutrinos.
Unlike the work of Kovalenko and
\check{\text{S}}
imkovic
[
3
]
, who derived diagonal propagators for massive neutrinos, we consider flavor-neutrino propagation which are described by a non-diagonal matrix propagator. A similar methodology was employed by M. Dvornikov
[
4
]
in analyzing oscillations in matter.

Next, we consider the mass matrix in the Chau-Keung parameterization of the PMNS matrix via Euler angles.
We discuss how parameterization can affect the normal and inverted neutrino mass hierarchies.

We demonstrate that local weak processes (e.g.,
l^{-}_{\alpha}l^{+}_{\beta}\to W^{+}W^{-}
) exhibit oscillation-sensitive contributions suppressed by
\mathcal{O}(10^{-79})
. For macroscopic diagrams with two-flavor transitions, the derived amplitude reproduces the canonical oscillation dependence

P_{osc}\propto\sin^{2}\left(\cfrac{\Delta m^{2}L}{4E_{\text{avg}}}\right),

where the oscillation phase is directly linked to neutrino masses. This formalism bridges QFT foundations and experimental interpretation while simultaneously confirming consistency with quantum mechanical results.

Dirac Neutrino Propagator

\mathcal{L}_{\nu}(x)=\bar{\nu}_{\alpha}(x)(i\hat{\partial}\cdot\delta_{\alpha\beta}-M_{\alpha\beta})\nu_{\beta}(x),

(1.1)

where summation is over flavor states.
The process of obtaining the propagator consists of inverting the matrix operator

\mathbb{K}_{\alpha\beta}=i\hat{\partial}\cdot\delta_{\alpha\beta}-M_{\alpha\beta}.

(1.2)

\mathbb{K}^{-1}(p)\equiv\mathbb{D}(p)

\mathbb{D}(p)=\frac{1}{\hat{p}-\mathbb{M}}.

Weak Interaction Process

In the Standard Model, weak interactions involve only left-handed neutrinos. The interaction Lagrangian is:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{int}}(x)=-\frac{g}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{\ell}\overline{\ell}(x)\gamma^{\mu}\mathcal{P}_{L}\nu_{\ell}(x)W_{\mu}^{-}(x)-\frac{g}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{\ell}\overline{\nu}_{\ell}(x)\gamma^{\mu}\mathcal{P}_{L}\ell(x)W_{\mu}^{+}(x),

(3.1)

where
\mathcal{P}_{L}=(1-\gamma_{5})/2
are the left-handed projectors, satisfying
\mathcal{P}_{L}\mathcal{P}_{R}=0
. In Feynman diagrams, vertices include
\gamma^{\mu}\mathcal{P}_{L}
. Consequently, in the amplitude squared, terms with constants between vertices vanish due to:

\mathcal{P}_{L}\cdot C\cdot\gamma^{\nu}\mathcal{P}_{L}=C\cdot\mathcal{P}_{L}\mathcal{P}_{R}\gamma^{\nu}=0.

(3.2)

For the kinematics, we assume negligible lepton masses compared to their energies and negligible neutrino masses. In the center-of-mass frame of the incoming leptons:

&p_{2}\simeq(|\mathbf{p}_{in}|,-\mathbf{p}_{in}),\\

•

This result thus validates neglecting the contributions from flavor-changing neutrino exchange interactions in the analysis of

Conclusions

Consideration of processes with violation of lepton number showed that it does not matter whether we work in the mass or flavor states basis. We obtain the same expressions, while in weak interactions flavor states appear.

In this work, we derived the matrix propagator both in its general form and with explicit angular dependence.
Also we write explicit mass matrix for 3 flavor through masses and angles.
It appears to be qualitatively different in the cases of direct and inverse hierarchy,
and the latter seems to exhibit a sort of fine tuning, which may be considered as an extra support in favor of direct hierarchy.

For the local diagram of the flavor-violating process
l_{\alpha}^{-}l_{\beta}^{+}\to W^{+}W^{-}
, we obtained a cross section of
\mathcal{O}(10^{-53})
, indicating no observable contribution to this process.

the appearance of definite massive states.

Abstract
. In this paper we introduce and study a new kind of generalized Hilbert matrix operators, induced by a positive finite Borel measure on ( 0 , 1 ) (0,1) , acting on weighted sequence spaces. We establish a sufficient and necessary condition for the boundedness of these operators. These results extend some related ones obtained recently in [Bull. Lond. Math. Soc., 55 (2023), no. 6, 2598–2610].

1.
Introductions and main results

For
1<p<\infty
, we denote the Hölder conjugate of
p
by
q
, i.e.,
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. Let
l^{p}
be the space of sequences of complex numbers, i.e.,

l^{p}:=\{a=\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}:\|a\|_{p}=(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\}.

For
a=\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\in l^{p}
,
b=\{b_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\in l^{q}
, the famous Hilbert’s inequality is stated as

\Big|\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{m}b_{n}}{m+n+1}\Big|\leq\pi\csc(\frac{\pi}{p})\|a\|_{p}\|b\|_{q},

where the constant
\pi\csc(\frac{\pi}{p})
is the best possible. Hilbert’s inequality has an equivalent form as follows.

(1.1)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big|\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{m}}{m+n+1}\Big|^{p}\leq\Big[\pi\csc(\frac{\pi}{p})\Big]^{p}\|a\|_{p}^{p},

where the constant
\Big[\pi\csc(\frac{\pi}{p})\Big]^{p}
is the best possible, see
[
19
, Theorem 6]
or
[
20
, Theorem 323]
.

The inequality (
1.1
) can be restated in the language of operator theory. For the Hilbert kernel
\frac{1}{m+n+1}
, we define the Hilbert matrix operator
\mathbf{H}
as

{\bf{H}}(a)(n):=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{m}}{m+n+1},\,a=\{a_{m}\}_{m=0}^{\infty},\,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}.

Then (
1.1
) can be restated as

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Then
\mathbf{H}
is bounded on
l^{p}
and the norm of
\mathbf{H}
is
\pi\csc(\frac{\pi}{p})
.

Note that the Hilbert kernel can be written as

\frac{1}{m+n+1}=\binom{n+m}{m}\int_{0}^{1}t^{m}(1-t)^{n}dt.

Here, for
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the combinatorial number
\binom{\gamma}{m}
is defined as

\binom{\gamma}{m}:=\frac{\gamma(\gamma-1)\cdots(\gamma-m+1)}{m!}.

In particular,
\binom{\gamma}{0}=1
for any
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
.

Very recently, inspired by the classical work of Hardy
[
18
]
, the following generalized Hilbert matrix operator was introduced and studied by Athanasiou in
[
2
]
. Let
\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure on
(0,1)
, the
operator
H_{\mu}
is defined as

H_{\mu}(a)(n):=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\int_{(0,1)}\binom{n+m}{m}t^{m}(1-t)^{n}a_{m}d\mu(t),a=\{a_{m}\}_{m=0}^{\infty},\,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

It has been proved in
[
2
]
that

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure on
(0,1)
.

(1)
Let
1\leq p<+\infty
. Then
H_{\mu}
is bounded on
l^{p}
if and only if

\mathcal{C}_{\mu}(p)=\int_{(0,1)}(1-t)^{\frac{1}{p}-1}t^{-\frac{1}{p}}d\mu(t)<\infty.

Moreover, when
H_{\mu}:l^{p}\rightarrow l^{p}
is bounded, the norm of
H_{\mu}
is
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}(p).

(2)

H_{\mu}
is bounded on
l^{\infty}
if and only if

\mathcal{C}_{\mu}(\infty)=\int_{(0,1)}(1-t)^{-1}d\mu(t)<\infty.

Moreover, when
H_{\mu}
is bounded on
l^{\infty}
, the norm of
H_{\mu}
is
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}(\infty).

The main purpose of this note is to establish an extension of above results obtained in
[
2
]
. To state our results, we first introduce some notations. Let
w(n)\geq 0
for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. For
1\leq p<\infty
, we denote by
l_{w}^{p}
the weighted Lesbegue space of infinite sequences, i.e.,

l_{w}^{p}:=\{a=\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}:\|a\|_{p,w}=(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}w(n)|a_{n}|^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\}.

We define the class of infinite sequences
l_{w}^{\infty}
as

l_{w}^{\infty}:=\{a=\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}:\|a\|_{\infty,w}=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}w(n)|a_{n}|<\infty\}.

When
w(n)\equiv 1
, we will write
l^{p}
instead of
l_{w}^{p}
, where
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

Let
\alpha,\beta>-1
,
\beta-\alpha>-1
and let
\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure on
(0,1)
. For a sequence
a=\{a_{m}\}_{m=0}^{\infty}
, we define

H_{\mu}^{\alpha,\beta}(a)(n):=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\int_{(0,1)}\frac{\Gamma(n+m+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(m+\alpha+1)\Gamma(n+\beta-\alpha+1)}t^{m}(1-t)^{n}a_{m}d\mu(t),\,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Here,
\Gamma
is the usual Gamma function, see
[
1
]
. When
\alpha=0
,
H_{\mu}^{\alpha,\beta}
reduces to the following operator

H_{\mu}^{\beta}(a)(n):=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\int_{(0,1)}\binom{n+m+\beta}{m}t^{m}(1-t)^{n}a_{m}d\mu(t),\,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

For two real numbers
s,\gamma
with
\gamma>-1
and
\gamma+s>-1
, we define

(\gamma+1)_{s}:=\frac{\Gamma(\gamma+s+1)}{\Gamma(\gamma+1)}.

We shall prove that

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure on
(0,1)
. Let
1\leq p<\infty
, and
\alpha,\beta>-1
,
\beta-\alpha>-1
. Then
H_{\mu}^{\alpha,\beta}
is bounded from
l_{w_{1}}^{p}
into
l_{w_{2}}^{p}
if and only if

\mathcal{C}_{\mu}(\beta,p):=\int_{(0,1)}(1-t)^{-(1-\frac{1}{p})(\beta+1)}t^{-\frac{1}{p}(\beta+1)}d\mu(t)<\infty.

Moreover, when
H_{\mu}^{\alpha,\beta}:l_{w_{1}}^{p}\rightarrow l_{w_{2}}^{p}
is bounded, the norm
\|H_{\mu}^{\alpha,\beta}\|
of
H_{\mu}^{\alpha,\beta}
is
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}(\beta,p).

Here

w_{1}(m)=(m+1)_{\alpha}^{-p}(m+1)_{\beta},

w_{2}(m)=(m+\beta-\alpha+1)_{\alpha}^{-p}(m+1)_{\beta},\,\,m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure on
(0,1)
. Let
\alpha,\beta>-1
,
\beta-\alpha>-1
. Then
H_{\mu}^{\alpha,\beta}
is bounded from
l_{\overline{w}_{1}}^{\infty}
into
l_{\overline{w}_{2}}^{\infty}
if and only if

\mathcal{C}_{\mu}(\beta,\infty):=\int_{(0,1)}(1-t)^{-\beta-1}d\mu(t)<\infty.

Moreover, when
H_{\mu}^{\alpha,\beta}:l_{\overline{w}_{1}}^{\infty}\rightarrow l_{\overline{w}_{2}}^{\infty}
is bounded, the norm
\|H_{\mu}^{\alpha,\beta}\|
of
H_{\mu}^{\alpha,\beta}
is
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}(\beta,\infty).

Here

\overline{w}_{1}(m)=(m+1)_{\alpha}^{-1},\,\overline{w}_{2}(m)=(m+\beta-\alpha+1)_{\alpha}^{-1},\,\,m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

When
\alpha=0
, from Theorem
1.3
and
1.4
, we have

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure on
(0,1)
. Let
\beta>-1
.

(1)
Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Then
H_{\mu}^{\beta}
is bounded on
l_{w}^{p}
if and only if

\mathcal{C}_{\mu}(\beta,p)=\int_{(0,1)}(1-t)^{-(1-\frac{1}{p})(\beta+1)}t^{-\frac{1}{p}(\beta+1)}d\mu(t)<\infty.

Moreover, when
H_{\mu}^{\beta}
is bounded on
l_{w}^{p}
, the norm of
H_{\mu}^{\beta}
is
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}({\beta,p}).
Here,

w(m)=(m+1)_{\beta},\,\,m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(2)

H_{\mu}^{\beta}
is bounded on
l^{\infty}
if and only if

\mathcal{C}_{\mu}(\beta,\infty)=\int_{(0,1)}(1-t)^{-\beta-1}d\mu(t)<\infty.

Moreover, when
H_{\mu}^{\beta}
is bounded on
l^{\infty}
, the norm of
H_{\mu}^{\beta}
is
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}({\beta,\infty}).

Remark 1.6
.

Theorem
1.2
will follow if we take
\beta=0
in Corollary
1.5
.

2.
Some lemmas

To prove the main results, in this section, we will list some known lemmas and establish some new ones.
For the sake of simplicity, in the rest of the paper, we will write

k_{\alpha,\beta}(m,n)=\frac{\Gamma(n+m+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(m+\alpha+1)\Gamma(n+\beta-\alpha+1)}.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and let
\alpha,\beta>-1
,
\beta-\alpha>-1
. Then we have

k_{\alpha,\beta}(m,n)=(-1)^{m}\binom{-n-\beta-1}{m}(m+1)_{\alpha}^{-1}(n+\beta-\alpha+1)_{\alpha},

and

k_{\alpha,\beta}(m,n)=(-1)^{n}\binom{-m-\beta-1}{n}(n+1)_{\beta-\alpha}^{-1}(m+\alpha+1)_{\beta-\alpha}.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\Gamma(n+m+\beta+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma(n+\beta+1)\prod_{i=1}^{m}(n+\beta+i)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma(n+\beta+1)(-1)^{m}\prod_{i=1}^{m}(-n-\beta-i).

Then

\displaystyle k_{\alpha,\beta}(m,n)=\frac{\Gamma(n+m+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(n+\beta-\alpha+1)}\cdot\frac{\Gamma(m+1)}{\Gamma(m+\alpha+1)}\cdot\frac{1}{\Gamma(m+1)}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(-n-\beta-i)}{\Gamma(m+1)}\cdot\frac{\Gamma(m+1)}{\Gamma(m+\alpha+1)}\cdot\frac{\Gamma(n+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(n+\beta-\alpha+1)}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m}\binom{-n-\beta-1}{m}(m+1)_{\alpha}^{-1}(n+\beta-\alpha+1)_{\alpha}.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\Gamma(n+m+\beta+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma(m+\beta+1)\prod_{i=1}^{n}(m+\beta+i)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma(m+\beta+1)(-1)^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(-m-\beta-i).

It follows that

\displaystyle k_{\alpha,\beta}(m,n)=\frac{\Gamma(n+m+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(m+\alpha+1)}\cdot\frac{\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(n+\beta-\alpha+1)}\cdot\frac{1}{\Gamma(n+1)}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(-m-\beta-i)}{\Gamma(n+1)}\cdot\frac{\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(n+\beta-\alpha+1)}\cdot\frac{\Gamma(m+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(m+\alpha+1)}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\binom{-m-\beta-1}{n}(n+1)_{\beta-\alpha}^{-1}(m+\alpha+1)_{\beta-\alpha}.

This proves the lemma.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and let
\alpha,\beta>-1
,
\beta-\alpha>-1
. Then we have

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}k_{\alpha,\beta}(m,n)t^{m}(m+1)_{\alpha}=(1-t)^{-n-\beta-1}(n+\beta-\alpha+1)_{\alpha},

and

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}k_{\alpha,\beta}(m,n)(1-t)^{n}(n+1)_{\beta-\alpha}=t^{-m-\beta-1}(m+\alpha+1)_{\beta-\alpha}.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}k_{\alpha,\beta}(m,n)t^{m}(m+1)_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}t^{m}\binom{-n-\beta-1}{m}(n+\beta-\alpha+1)_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=(1-t)^{-n-\beta-1}(n+\beta-\alpha+1)_{\alpha},

and

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}k_{\alpha,\beta}(m,n)(1-t)^{n}(n+1)_{\beta-\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}(1-t)^{n}\binom{-m-\beta-1}{n}(m+\alpha+1)_{\beta-\alpha}

\displaystyle=t^{-m-\beta-1}t^{m}(m+\alpha+1)_{\beta-\alpha}.

The lemma is proved.
∎

[

n\geq 0

(n+1)_{s}\leq\frac{(n+1)^{s+1}}{n+s+1},

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
p\geq 1,\beta>-1
. Let
\rho\in(0,1)
and
\mathbf{N}
be a natural number. For
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{p}(\beta+1))
, we define

a_{n}(\varepsilon)=(n+\beta+1)^{-1-p\varepsilon},\,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Then we can take a constant
\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon_{1}(\rho,\mathbf{N})>0
such that

\sum_{n=\mathbf{N}+1}^{\infty}a_{n}(\varepsilon)\geq(1-\rho)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}(\varepsilon),

for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{1}]
.

Proof.

We let
\mathbf{S}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}(\varepsilon)
. It is easy to see that

\mathbf{S}\geq\int_{0}^{\infty}(x+\beta+1)^{-1-p\varepsilon}dx=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\frac{1}{p(\beta+1)^{p\varepsilon}},

and

\sum_{n=0}^{\bf{N}}a_{n}(\varepsilon)\leq\frac{{\bf{N}}+1}{(\beta+1)^{1+p\varepsilon}}.

This means that we can find two constants
C_{i}=C_{i}(p,\beta)>0,i=1,2
, such that

\frac{1}{C_{1}\varepsilon}\leq\mathbf{S},\,\,\,{\text{and}}\,\,\,\,\sum_{n=0}^{\bf{N}}a_{n}(\varepsilon)\leq C_{2}{\bf{N}}.

It follows that

\Big(\sum_{n={\bf{N}}+1}^{\infty}a_{n}(\varepsilon)\Big)/\Big(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}(\varepsilon)\Big)=1-\Big(\sum_{n=0}^{\bf{N}}a_{n}(\varepsilon)\Big)/\mathbf{S}\geq 1-C_{1}C_{2}{\bf{N}}\varepsilon.

Hence, if
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{1}:=\frac{\rho}{C_{1}C_{2}{\bf{N}}}
, then we have

\sum_{n=\mathbf{N}+1}^{\infty}a_{n}(\varepsilon)\geq(1-\rho)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}(\varepsilon),

for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{1}]
. This proves the lemma.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

First we prove the following

Proposition 3.1
.

If
\mathcal{C}_{\mu}(\beta,p)<\infty
, then
H_{\mu}^{\alpha,\beta}:l_{w_{1}}^{p}\rightarrow l_{w_{2}}^{p}
is bounded and
\|H_{\mu}^{\alpha,\beta}\|\leq\mathcal{C}_{\mu}(\beta,p)
.

We then prove

Proposition 3.2
.

If
H_{\mu}^{\alpha,\beta}:l_{w_{1}}^{p}\rightarrow l_{w_{2}}^{p}
is bounded, then
\|H_{\mu}^{\alpha,\beta}\|\geq\mathcal{C}_{\mu}(\beta,p)
.

It is easy to see that Proposition
3.1
and
3.2
imply Theorem
1.3
.

3.1.
Proof of Proposition
3.1

For
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
a=\{a_{m}\}_{m=0}^{\infty}\in l_{w_{1}}^{p}
, let

E_{n}(t):=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}k_{\alpha,\beta}(m,n)t^{m}(1-t)^{n}|a_{m}|

First, we show that
E_{n}(t)
is a well-defined function on
(0,1)
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
When
p=1
, for
a\in l_{w_{1}}^{1}
, we have

E_{n}(t)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}k_{\alpha,\beta}(m,n)t^{m}(1-t)^{n}[w_{1}(m)]^{-1}\cdot|a_{m}|w_{1}(m).

Moreover, we have, for each
t\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle k_{\alpha,\beta}(m,n)t^{m}(1-t)^{n}[w_{1}(m)]^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k_{\alpha,\beta}(m,n)t^{m}(1-t)^{n}(m+1)_{\alpha}(m+1)_{\beta}^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(n+m+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(m+\beta+1)\Gamma(n+\beta-\alpha+1)}t^{m}(1-t)^{n}.

By Stirling’s formula, we know that

\frac{\Gamma(n+m+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(m+\beta+1)}=(m+\beta+1)^{n}[1+o(1)],\,\,\,{\text{as}}\,\,\,m\rightarrow\infty.

Here and later,
o(1)
denotes some sequence
\{{\bf a}_{i}\}_{i=0}^{\infty}
with
o(1)={\bf a}_{i}\rightarrow 0
as
i\rightarrow\infty
, which will be different in different places.
Hence, for
t\in(0,1)
,

\frac{\Gamma(n+m+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(m+\beta+1)}t^{m}\rightarrow 0,\,\,\,{\text{as}}\,\,\,m\rightarrow\infty.

Then, for each
t\in(0,1)
, there is a constant
M>0
such that

E_{n}(t)\leq M\frac{(1-t)^{n}}{\Gamma(n+\beta-\alpha+1)}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}|a_{m}|w_{1}(m).

This means that
E_{n}(t)
is well-defined on
(0,1)
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
when
p=1
.

When
p>1
, by Hölder’s inequality, we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle E_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\Big[k_{\alpha,\beta}(m,n)t^{m}(1-t)^{n}[w_{1}(m)]^{-\frac{1}{p}}\Big]\Big[a_{m}[w_{1}(m)]^{\frac{1}{p}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big\{\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\Big[k_{\alpha,\beta}(m,n)t^{m}(1-t)^{n}[w_{1}(m)]^{-\frac{1}{p}}\Big]^{q}\Big\}^{\frac{1}{q}}\|a\|_{p,w_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\{\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\Big[\frac{\Gamma(n+m+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(m+\beta+1)}\Big]^{q}\frac{\Gamma(m+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(m+1)}t^{qm}\Big\}^{\frac{1}{q}}\frac{(1-t)^{n}}{\Gamma(n+\beta-\alpha+1)}\|a\|_{p,w_{1}}.

We note that, using Stirling’s formula again, we have for
m\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\Big\{\Big[\frac{\Gamma(n+m+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(m+\beta+1)}\Big]^{q}\frac{\Gamma(m+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(m+1)}t^{qm}\Big\}^{\frac{1}{m}}

\displaystyle=t^{q}\Big[\frac{\Gamma(n+m+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(m+\beta+1)}\Big]^{\frac{q}{m}}\Big[\frac{\Gamma(m+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(m+1)}\Big]^{\frac{1}{m}}

\displaystyle=t^{q}(m+\beta)^{\frac{nq}{m}}[1+o(1)]^{\frac{q}{m}}m^{\frac{\beta}{m}}[1+o(1)]^{\frac{1}{m}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow t^{q}<1\,\,\,{\text{as}}\,\,\,m\rightarrow\infty.

From the root test for series, we know that, for
t\in(0,1)
, the series

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\Big[\frac{\Gamma(n+m+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(m+\beta+1)}\Big]^{q}\frac{\Gamma(m+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(m+1)}t^{qm}

is convergent. It then follows from (
3.1
) that
E_{n}(t)
is well-defined on
(0,1)
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
when
p>1
.

We shall give an estimate for
E_{n}^{p}(t),p\geq 1
. When
p>1
, we have

\displaystyle E_{n}(t)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}[k_{\alpha,\beta}(m,n)t^{m}(1-t)^{n}]^{\frac{1}{p}}|a_{m}|(m+1)_{\alpha}^{-\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\quad\times[k_{\alpha,\beta}(m,n)t^{m}(1-t)^{n}]^{\frac{1}{q}}(m+1)_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big[\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}k_{\alpha,\beta}(m,n)t^{m}(1-t)^{n}|a_{m}|^{p}(m+1)_{\alpha}^{1-p}\Big]^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\quad\times\Big[\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}k_{\alpha,\beta}(m,n)t^{m}(1-t)^{n}(m+1)_{\alpha}\Big]^{\frac{1}{q}}.

It follows from Lemma
2.2
that

(3.2)

\displaystyle E_{n}^{p}(t)\leq(1-t)^{-(\beta+1)(p-1)}(n+\beta-\alpha+1)_{\alpha}^{p-1}

\displaystyle\times\Big[\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}k_{\alpha,\beta}(m,n)t^{m}(1-t)^{n}|a_{m}|^{p}(m+1)_{\alpha}^{1-p}\Big].

When
p=1
, the inequality (
3.2
) obviously holds.

Hence, for
p\geq 1
, and
t\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}w_{2}(n)E_{n}^{p}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+\beta-\alpha+1)_{\alpha}^{-p}(n+1)_{\beta}E_{n}^{p}(t)

\displaystyle\leq(1-t)^{-(\beta+1)(p-1)}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+\beta-\alpha+1)_{\alpha}^{-1}(n+1)_{\beta}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\quad\times\Big[\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}k_{\alpha,\beta}(m,n)t^{m}(1-t)^{n}|a_{m}|^{p}(m+1)_{\alpha}^{1-p}\Big]

\displaystyle=(1-t)^{-(\beta+1)(p-1)}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}|a_{m}|^{p}(m+1)_{\alpha}^{1-p}\Big[\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}k_{\alpha,\beta}(m,n)t^{m}(1-t)^{n}(n+1)_{\beta-\alpha}\Big].

By using Lemma
2.2
, we get that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}w_{2}(n)E_{n}^{p}(t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-t)^{-(\beta+1)(p-1)}t^{-\beta-1}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}|a_{m}|^{p}(m+1)_{\alpha}^{1-p}(m+\alpha+1)_{\beta-\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-t)^{-(\beta+1)(p-1)}t^{-\beta-1}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}|a_{m}|^{p}(m+1)_{\alpha}^{-p}(m+1)_{\beta}

\displaystyle=

\|H_{\mu}^{\alpha,\beta}\|\leq\mathcal{C}_{\mu}(\beta,p).

5.
Final remarks

In this section, we consider the generalized Hilbert matrix operator acting on the spaces of analytic functions in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
.
We denote by
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
the class of all analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
.
Let
\beta>-1
,
\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure on
(0,1)
. For
f=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}z^{m}\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
, we formally define

H_{\mu}^{\beta}(f)(z):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big[\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\int_{(0,1)}\binom{n+m+\beta}{m}t^{m}(1-t)^{n}a_{m}d\mu(t)\Big]z^{n},\,z\in\mathbb{D}.

When
\beta=0
, the operator

has been studied in
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
, where the authors characterize the measure
\mu
such that the operator

is bounded on Hardy spaces and Bergman spaces. We will consider the operator
H_{\mu}^{\beta}
acting on the Dirichlet-type space
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}
.

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the Dirichlet-type space
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}
is defined as

coincides the classic Dirichlet space
\mathcal{D}
, and when
\lambda=1
,

becomes the Hardy space
H^{2}
. More generalized Dirichlet-type spaces can be found in
[
27
]
.

Note that, for
n\geq 0,s>-1
, there exist two positive constants

, which are independent of the number

, such that
C_{1}(n+1)^{s}\leq(n+1)_{s}\leq C_{2}(n+1)^{s}
. Then, we see from (1) of Corollary
1.5
that

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\beta>-1
and let
\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure on
(0,1)
. Let
H_{\mu}^{\beta}
be as above. Then

is bounded on

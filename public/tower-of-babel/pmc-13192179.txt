Differentiation of adrenal metastases and adenomas based on clinical characteristics, deep learning features, and radiomics features derived from ultrasound imaging
Authors: Yiming Li, Yuting Song, Luchen Chang, Xi Wei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAccurate diagnosis of adrenal incidentalomas is crucial in patients with extra-adrenal malignant tumors. This study aims to develop a nomogram integrating clinical features, deep learning-derived imaging features, and ultrasound radiomics characteristics to distinguish adrenal metastases from adrenal adenomas.MethodsA retrospective analysis was conducted on 449 cases, including 228 cases of adrenal metastases and 221 cases of adrenal adenomas, divided into training and testing cohorts at a 7:3 ratio. Patient clinical data and ultrasonographic images were collected, with regions of interest (ROIs) delineated on ultrasound images. Feature extraction, selection, and radiomics model (Rad) construction were performed, followed by model evaluation. Multiple deep learning models were employed to identify the optimal architecture for deep feature extraction. These deep features were combined with radiomics features to establish a deep learning radiomics model (DLR) for the differentiation between adrenal metastases and adenomas.ResultsThe study demonstrates that the Rad, DLR, and combined models exhibit superior diagnostic performance in differentiating adrenal metastases from adrenal adenomas. In the testing cohort, the combined model outperforms the Rad and DLR models. The area under the curve (AUC) in the testing set for Rad, DLR, and combined models were 0.839, 0.839, and 0.850, respectively.ConclusionThe nomogram integrating clinical features, deep learning features, and ultrasound radiomics features demonstrates robust performance in differentiating adrenal metastases from adrenal adenomas and can assist in preliminary diagnostic stratification of indeterminate adrenal nodules in patients with extrarenal tumors.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12880-026-02343-x.

Adrenal incidentalomas pose a significant diagnostic challenge in clinical practice, particularly in patients with extra-adrenal neoplasms. Precise differentiation of adrenal masses is critical for accurate staging and subsequent therapeutic decision-making. The adrenal glands are common sites of metastasis, with primary tumors such as lung carcinoma, renal cell carcinoma, gastric carcinoma, and esophageal carcinoma frequently involved; the presence of adrenal metastases often indicates a poor prognosis [1–3]. Conversely, adrenal adenomas may be incidentally discovered in patients with or without a history of malignancy. However, some adrenal nodules present with indeterminate imaging features that cannot be reliably differentiated by ultrasound alone.

Adrenal biopsy remains the diagnostic gold standard for adrenal neoplastic lesions; however, due to technical challenges and a high rate of procedural complications, it is often excluded from routine clinical practice for definitive tumor characterization [4]. Concurrently, advances in adrenal imaging have significantly reduced the reliance on biopsy, allowing for non-invasive assessment by monitoring lesion stability or growth over time to infer benign or malignant nature. Currently, non-invasive imaging modalities such as ultrasonography, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), computed tomography (CT), and positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET/CT) are routinely employed for the evaluation of adrenal metastases and adenomas [5, 6]. While ultrasonography, as one of the examination modalities for patients with adrenal incidentalomas or extra-adrenal tumors, exhibits lower sensitivity and specificity compared to MRI and CT [7], it facilitates relatively frequent detection of adrenal masses during routine clinical practice due to its simplicity and low cost. Our team posits that ultrasonography is also of considerable importance for adrenal lesions, subsequently influencing clinical decision-making and further imaging examinations. On ultrasound, adrenal adenomas frequently present with sharply defined margins, regular shape, and homogeneous internal echogenicity, whereas adrenal metastases tend to display irregular borders and heterogeneous echotexture [8]. Nevertheless, indeterminate adrenal nodules are not characteristic ultrasound features, and their diagnostic accuracy is influenced by operator expertise and subjective interpretation. Accurate differentiation of such lesions remains a key focus of this study.

The advancement of artificial intelligence in medical diagnostics has led to its increasingly widespread application. Among these, the integration of machine learning with radiomics has emerged as a prominent research focus due to its capability to enhance predictive performance while maintaining model interpretability [9–11]. Deep learning methods are also being increasingly utilized in medical image analysis; however, their effectiveness often depends on large-scale annotated imaging datasets. Transfer learning leverages pre-trained models on source tasks to improve performance on target tasks and address limitations related to data scarcity [12, 13]. Previous studies have comparatively limited research on the differential diagnosis of adrenal metastases versus adrenal adenomas from an ultrasound perspective, primarily focusing on CT, MRI, or PET-CT modalities, with little exploration of AI-driven analysis of sonographic features [14–17]. This study aims to develop a comprehensive model integrating clinical data, radiomic features, and deep learning-derived features, offering a more precise diagnostic approach for adrenal lesions, thereby enhancing clinical decision-making in the assessment of adrenal mass lesions.

Materials and methods

Patients

A retrospective, consecutive series of 449 cases diagnosed with adrenal adenoma or adrenal metastasis at Tianjin Medical University Cancer Hospital between January 2018 and September 2025 was selected as the study cohort. The samples were divided into two cohorts: a training set comprising 70% of the data and a testing set comprising the remaining 30%, as illustrated in Fig. 1. Inclusion criteria for adrenal metastases [18]: [1] histopathological confirmation via biopsy or surgical resection, or radiological identification of new adrenal lesions or increase in tumor volume during two consecutive short-term follow-ups (6 months); [2] a documented history of primary malignancy outside the adrenal glands. Inclusion criteria for adrenal adenomas: histopathological confirmation via biopsy or surgical resection, or stable tumor size on imaging across two consecutive follow-up scans (> 1 year). Exclusion criteria: [1] poor ultrasound image quality; [2] patient withdrawal from further treatment or lack of follow-up imaging evidence; [3] clinical assessment by physicians based on comprehensive analysis of imaging, clinical features, laboratory parameters, and treatment outcomes, leading to the diagnosis of other adrenal tumor types (e.g., pheochromocytomas, adrenal myelolipomas).

A retrospective collection of patient clinical data, including age, gender, tumor location, and maximum tumor diameter, was conducted. During the data collection process, the experts were blinded to the patients’ pathological and follow-up outcomes.

US images acquisition

All adrenal tumor patients included in this study underwent ultrasonographic imaging using convex array transducers operating within the 1–5 MHz frequency range. The ultrasonographic examinations were performed primarily with Philips iU22 and Aplio 500 systems. The acquired ultrasound images were converted into digital format and imported into ITK-SNAP software (version 3.8.0, www.itksnap.org) for subsequent segmentation. Prior to delineation of regions of interest (ROIs), two radiologists were blinded to the final diagnoses. Initially, Ultrasound Radiologist 1 manually segmented the ROIs on two-dimensional grayscale ultrasound images, with multiple iterative adjustments to optimize delineation accuracy. All manual segmentations were independently reviewed by experienced radiologist 2. One week later, a randomized subset of 30 adrenal mass ultrasound images was re-segmented by two additional ultrasound radiologists for consistency assessment.

Radiomics feature extraction and selection

This study adheres to the CLEAR, METRICS, and CLAIM checklists. For radiomic feature extraction and selection, Pyradiomics (http://pyradiomics.readthedocs.io) was employed. Manual feature extraction encompassed three categories: geometric, first-order, and textural features. To fully exploit texture information across different scales within the ultrasound images, we applied wavelet transforms and Gaussian-Laplacian filters to the original images, generating filtered images. In total, 14 geometric features, 252 first-order features, and 1,022 textural features were extracted. Feature selection was performed using Z-score normalization for standardization, Student’s t-test to evaluate statistical significance, and Spearman’s correlation analysis to assess inter-feature redundancy. The least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression model was implemented utilizing the scikit-learn Python package. The optimal regularization parameter λ was determined through ten-fold cross-validation based on the minimum criteria, effectively reducing the feature set to those with the highest predictive and informational value.

Deep-learning procedure

To reduce complexity and minimize background noise in algorithm analysis, only the minimal bounding rectangle containing the ROI was retained as the crop area. To ensure the model’s robustness across patients exhibiting significant morphological variability, transfer learning was employed. This study evaluated the performance of prominent neural network architectures, including ResNet50, ResNet101, and DenseNet121, to enhance the capabilities of traditional CNN-based models. Comparative analysis indicated that ResNet101 was most suitable for our research objectives. Models were pretrained on the ImageNet dataset to initialize weights, thereby improving generalization across diverse datasets. During training, real-time data augmentation strategies—including random cropping, horizontal flipping, and vertical flipping—were implemented. Test images underwent standardization, and a cosine decay learning rate schedule was applied for hyperparameter tuning to optimize model generalization. Deep learning features refer to the output probabilities obtained from convolutional neural network (CNN) models. In this study, the optimal deep learning model on the test set was selected, and features were extracted from its penultimate layer. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) was applied to reduce the feature dimensionality from high-dimensional space to a more manageable 512-dimensional vector. Grad-CAM (Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping) was utilized to generate heatmaps, highlighting the regions within the images that contributed most significantly to the model’s predictions. Specifically, Grad-CAM was applied to the final convolutional layer of the model to produce spatially resolved activation maps with appropriate resolution.

Model Construction and Evaluation

This study developed and validated multiple predictive models, including the Rad, Deep Transfer Learning (DTL), DLR, clinical, and integrated models. Utilizing the selected optimal features, these were incorporated into five machine learning algorithms: logistic regression (LR), support vector machine (SVM), k-nearest neighbor (KNN), Random Forest, and multi-layer perceptron (MLP). The DLR model initially employed a pre-fusion algorithm to integrate deep learning features with radiomics features, following the feature selection and model development processes specific to radiomics. To enhance clinical applicability, all clinical features underwent univariate analysis and stepwise multivariate analysis to identify statistically significant predictors. The selected clinical features were combined with deep learning and radiomics predictions to construct an LR linear model, which was subsequently integrated into a combined model visualized via nomogram. Model performance was rigorously assessed through receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis for discrimination, calibration curves to evaluate calibration accuracy, and the Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test to verify model reliability. Additionally, decision curve analysis (DCA) was performed to assess the clinical utility and potential value of the models in clinical settings. Statistical analysis

All data analysis was conducted using Python 3.7.12. Statistical evaluation was performed with Statsmodels version 0.13.2. Radiomic feature extraction utilized PyRadiomics version 3.0.1. Machine learning algorithms were implemented with Scikit-learn version 1.0.2. The computations were accelerated using an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPU, with the deep learning framework developed in PyTorch 1.11.0, optimized through CUDA 11.3.1 and cuDNN 8.2.1. The Shapiro-Wilk test assessed the normality of clinical features. For continuous variables, either t-test or Mann-Whitney U test was employed based on distribution characteristics. Categorical variables were analyzed using the chi-square test. Notably, all inter-cohort P-values exceeded 0.05, indicating no statistically significant differences and confirming an unbiased cohort stratification.

Feature extraction and selection

The study extracted a total of 14 shape features, 252 first-order features, and 1022 texture features. The trained DTL model derived 2,049 DTL features, which were subsequently reduced to seven principal components through PCA. The DLR model extracted 1,297 features, which were further dimensionally reduced via regularization, correlation analysis, and LASSO logistic regression, resulting in 23 features with non-zero coefficients for machine learning classification. Feature selection for the DLR model was performed using LASSO logistic regression, with tenfold cross-validation coefficients and mean squared error (MSE) illustrated in Figs. 3 and 4. The Rad-score formula is calculated as a weighted linear combination of 23 predictors selected via LASSO regression. These predictors comprise 3 deep learning-derived features (DL_0, DL_3, DL_6) and 20 radiomic features. The weight coefficient for each feature reflects its degree of contribution to the differentiation of adrenal tumor properties. A higher Rad-score value indicates an increased risk of the adrenal tumor being a metastasis. The Rad-score formula was computed based on the non-zero coefficients, as shown in the accompanying equation, with the final selected non-zero features depicted in Fig. 5.

Rad and DLR selected the LR machine learning model, while Clinic opted for the SVM machine learning model as the final indicators for the consolidated results. This study extracted radiomic and deep learning features from adrenal metastasis and adenoma ultrasound images, respectively. These features underwent fusion and selection, retaining only those with non-zero coefficients. The non-zero coefficient features were then integrated with clinical risk factors to construct a comprehensive predictive model using multivariate logistic regression. As illustrated in Fig. 10, in this study, the constructed nomogram incorporates two key predictive variables: maximum tumor diameter (Max D) and the predicted probability from the DLR model. By locating the patient’s tumor diameter and the DLR predicted probability on their respective axes within the nomogram, individual scores can be ascertained by reading vertically upwards. The summation of these scores, when projected vertically downwards onto the probability axis, yields the overall score, which reflects the probability of adrenal metastasis.

Discussion

Adrenal adenomas are more commonly observed in patients with extra-adrenal malignant tumors, with studies indicating that approximately 74% of patients with a history of extra-adrenal malignant tumors exhibit benign adrenal incidentalomas [19]. The diagnostic aim of ultrasonography is to reliably differentiate between benign, indeterminate, and markedly malignant lesions without necessitating extensive investigations, thereby minimizing patient exposure to ionizing radiation and contrast agents, and reducing diagnostic uncertainty and follow-up burden. Previous research has demonstrated that texture and radiological feature extraction from imaging modalities can effectively distinguish benign adrenal masses from metastatic lesions, offering significant diagnostic value [20]. However, these prior studies have not utilized ultrasound feature extraction or applied transfer learning with ultrasound images, highlighting the clinical relevance and exploratory potential of this research. Radiomics refers to the application of advanced image processing techniques to convert medical images into high-throughput quantitative features, enabling the extraction and detection of microstructural tissue characteristics [21, 22]. Currently, radiomics is extensively utilized in medical image analysis to assess tumor classification, grading, and prognostic evaluation, including cases such as salivary gland tumors, thyroid carcinoma, breast cancer, and hepatic carcinoma [23, 24]. ResNet-101 has been extensively utilized in computer vision applications. By incorporating residual units, it incrementally learns the mapping relationships between raw input data and output features, resulting in enhanced stability and efficiency in deep neural network training, achieving optimal performance in this study [25, 26]. The DLR model not only employs deep learning algorithms to extract complex, high-throughput features from large-scale ultrasound datasets but also integrates radiomic feature extraction techniques. All extracted features are fused to develop a comprehensive predictive model. This approach enables multi-faceted analysis and comparison of artificial intelligence’s diagnostic value in adrenal metastases and adenomas, facilitating the development of more comprehensive and accurate clinical diagnostic models.

The integrated predictive model developed by our team culminated in a nomogram, where the maximum diameter of adrenal incidentalomas serves as a key metric in evaluating adrenal metastases and adenomas. An increasing diameter correlates with a higher probability of diagnosing adrenal metastasis. Previous studies have confirmed that lesions greater than 3 cm in diameter are highly suggestive of adrenal metastases in patients with extrarenal tumors [27]. Additionally, Sasaguri et al. reported a 95.5% predictive value for metastatic involvement in adrenal masses exceeding 4 cm [28]. Rapid enlargement of adrenal lesions (> 8 mm per year) should also raise suspicion for malignant neoplasms [29]. Although sex did not remain a variable in the nomogram scoring system, statistically significant differences were observed between the two patient groups, with a higher prevalence of adrenal metastases in males. This may be attributable to the most common primary tumor source being lung carcinoma, which predominantly affects males. Consistent with Byeon et al., the proportion of males with adrenal metastases significantly exceeds that of those with benign adrenal masses [30].

This study utilized Clinic, Rad, DTL, DLR, and Combined radiomic models to evaluate the diagnostic accuracy in differentiating adrenal metastases from adrenal adenomas. The results demonstrated high predictive value across all models in both training and testing cohorts, with the Combined model exhibiting the highest performance. The training cohort achieved an AUC of 0.954 (95% CI: 0.9316–0.9754), while the testing cohort yielded an AUC of 0.850 (95% CI: 0.7852–0.9150). Prior research has employed alternative imaging modalities with artificial intelligence for assessing adrenal lesions. For instance, Wang et al. developed a deep learning model based on contrast-enhanced CT images, integrating radiologic and clinical features to differentiate lipid-poor adrenal adenomas from metastases, with AUC values exceeding 0.8, comparable to the results of our DTL model [18]. Similarly, Cao et al. constructed a machine learning model based on PET/CT to evaluate uncertain adrenal nodules in patients with extrarenal malignancies, achieving an AUC above 0.9 in the training set, aligning with the Rad model outcomes [31]. While these studies employed contrast-enhanced CT or PET/CT for adrenal lesion assessment, the comparable diagnostic performance across these modalities suggests that ultrasound can serve as a reliable, minimally invasive, and convenient tool for differentiating adrenal masses. Deep transfer learning offers advantages such as automatic feature extraction, analysis of large datasets, and continual learning [32]. However, our team found that its diagnostic performance within the training cohort was significantly inferior to the Rad model, potentially due to limited data volume leading to reduced diagnostic accuracy. To address this, we plan to conduct future large-scale, multi-center studies with expanded sample sizes. Additionally, our results indicate that models integrating deep learning features with radiomic features perform comparably to models utilizing solely radiomic features, possibly because the synergistic information gained from combining radiomics with deep transfer learning is limited. The fusion models demonstrated robust diagnostic performance in both training and testing cohorts, with calibration curves showing strong agreement between predicted and actual outcomes. Decision curve analysis further suggested that the integrated models possess favorable clinical utility.

To investigate the visual basis underlying the ultrasound deep learning model’s discrimination between adrenal metastases and adenomas, this study employed Grad-CAM to generate class activation heatmaps. The results revealed differential activation intensities between the two tumor types in the analysis of marginal regions, particularly in areas with indistinct borders with surrounding tissue, potentially indicating the model’s sensitivity to the infiltrative edges of malignant tumors. Zhu et al. applied Grad-CAM for visual analysis of renal cell carcinoma, reporting that the tumor boundary similarly emerged as a region of focal activation [33]. Interestingly, the Grad-CAM heatmaps of adrenal adenomas in our study did not exhibit a homogeneous diffuse pattern but rather demonstrated regional variations in activation. This finding parallels the observations of Liu et al., who employed Grad-CAM to analyze benign parotid tumors and similarly reported differential activation intensities [34]. We hypothesize that this phenomenon may reflect the underlying pathological heterogeneity of adenomas at the microscopic level, which is captured by the deep learning model and manifested as differential activation weighting.

This study has certain limitations. Firstly, it is a single-center, small-sample investigation, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to certain geographical regions. Future research will involve multi-center, large-sample studies to enhance the model’s robustness and applicability. Secondly, the primary objective was to address the clinical challenge of indeterminate adrenal nodules in patients with extrarenal adrenal tumors. The majority of the collected cases stem from patients with extrarenal adrenal neoplasms, with fewer cases involving non-adrenal malignancies. Lastly, only a minority of metastases or adenomas were confirmed through postoperative histopathology; most were evaluated based on imaging follow-up observing changes in size. This approach reflects real-world clinical practice.

In summary, this study developed and validated a nomogram model based on clinical data, ultrasound radiomics features, and deep learning parameters, providing an objective probabilistic assessment for identifying indeterminate adrenal nodules in patients with extrarenal adrenal tumors. The model holds significant clinical implications for decision-making and disease staging in routine practice.

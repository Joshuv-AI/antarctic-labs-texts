COMBI-I: Long-Term Overall Survival With Spartalizumab Plus Dabrafenib and Trametinib in BRAF V600–Mutant Advanced Melanoma
Authors: Antoni Ribas, Caroline Robert, Dirk Schadendorf, Georgina V Long, Hussein Tawbi, Keith Flaherty, Paolo A Ascierto, Paul Nathan, Piotr Rutkowski, Oleg V Leonov, Paul Lorigan, Sorilla Prey, Maristella Saponara, Ana Arance, Caroline Gaudy-Marqueste, Daniil Stroyakovskiy, Celeste Lebbe, Michele Maio, Michele Del Vecchio, Pamela Salman Boghikian, Ralf Gutzmer, Roberta Depenni, Marina Miskic, Mark Russo, Reinhard Dummer
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe COMBI-I trial (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT02967692) evaluating spartalizumab plus dabrafenib and trametinib (sparta-DabTram, n = 267) versus placebo plus dabrafenib and trametinib (placebo-DabTram, n = 265) for BRAF V600–mutant unresectable or metastatic melanoma failed to reach its primary end point of progression-free survival at 24 months. This final analysis reports overall survival (OS) during at least 5 years of extended follow-up. At the end of the trial (August 21, 2024), the median duration of follow-up was 76.9 months (range, 73.7-83.3 months). The median OS was 61.5 months (95% CI, 41.6 to not evaluable) for the sparta-DabTram arm and 41.6 months (95% CI, 30.6 to 56.9) for the placebo-DabTram arm (hazard ratio, 0.760 [95% CI, 0.598 to 0.966]). The safety findings were consistent with the known safety profile for sparta-DabTram. The most common treatment-related adverse event (TRAE) was pyrexia (65.9% v 46.2%, respectively, in the two study arms). Grade ≥3 TRAEs were reported in 57.3% and 36.7% of patients in the two arms, respectively. The combination of sparta-DabTram appears to improve OS compared with dabrafenib and trametinib alone in patients with BRAF V600–mutant metastatic melanoma.

INTRODUCTION

BRAF and MEK inhibitors improve progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) over other therapies and are currently approved for the treatment of patients with BRAF V600–mutant advanced melanoma.1 Patients with advanced melanoma, irrespective of driver aberrations in oncogenes, can be treated with immune checkpoint inhibitors.2 Previous studies have assessed the possibilities and outcomes of combining BRAF and MEK-targeted therapies with anti–PD-1/PD-L1 antibodies.3-13 The combination of atezolizumab with cobimetinib and vemurafenib is now approved by the US Food and Drug Administration as first-line treatment for unresectable advanced BRAF V600–mutant melanoma.8,14

The COMBI-I trial that evaluated the anti–PD-1 antibody spartalizumab in combination with the BRAF inhibitor dabrafenib and the MEK inhibitor trametinib (sparta-DabTram) over placebo plus dabrafenib and trametinib (placebo-DabTram) did not meet its primary end point of improved median PFS (16.2 months v 12 months; hazard ratio [HR], 0.82 [95% CI, 0.66 to 1.03]; P = .042, one-sided, nonsignificant) in patients with unresectable or metastatic BRAF V600–mutant melanoma.15 Herein, we report the final results for OS from the COMBI-I trial.

METHODS

The design of the phase III, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled COMBI-I trial (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT02967692) has been published previously.15 Adult patients (age ≥18 years) with histologically confirmed unresectable or metastatic BRAF V600–mutant cutaneous melanoma received intravenous spartalizumab (400 mg once every 4 weeks) plus oral dabrafenib (150 mg twice daily) and trametinib (2 mg once daily) or intravenous placebo plus oral dabrafenib and trametinib.15 OS was not formally tested (as the study failed to meet the primary end point); the P value was exploratory and descriptive, obtained from a one-sided stratified log-rank test. The trial was conducted in accordance with the International Council for Harmonisation Good Clinical Practice guidelines and the principles of Declaration of Helsinki. All patients provided written informed consent.

RESULTS

Between September 13, 2017, and July 4, 2018, 532 patients received sparta-DabTram (n = 267) or placebo-DabTram (n = 265; Appendix Fig A1, online only).15 Baseline characteristics were balanced between the two study arms (Table 1). By the end of the trial (August 21, 2024), the median duration of follow-up (from the date of random assignment to data cutoff) was 76.9 months (range, 73.7-83.3 months). The median duration of exposure was 312 days (range, 28-2,275 days) for spartalizumab and 347 days (range, 28-1,288 days) for placebo. The median relative dose intensity was 85.1% and 97.6% for dabrafenib and 85.7% and 97.2% for trametinib in the two arms, respectively, and 98.4% for spartalizumab.

Overall Survival

Death was reported in 127 (47.6%) patients in the sparta-DabTram arm and 151 (57.0%) patients in the placebo-DabTram arm. A total of 140 (52.4%) patients in the sparta-DabTram arm and 114 (43.0%) patients in the placebo-DabTram arm were censored from OS analysis, with most (110 [41.2%] and 97 [34.7%] in the two arms, respectively) not having had an event at the time of study termination. The median OS was 61.5 months (95% CI, 41.6 to not evaluable) for the sparta-DabTram arm and 41.6 months (95% CI, 30.6 to 56.9) for the placebo-DabTram arm (HR, 0.760 [95% CI, 0.598 to 0.966]; Fig 1; Appendix Table A1). The estimated OS rate at 60 months was 50.1% (95% CI, 43.7 to 56.1) for the sparta-DabTram arm and 42.8% (95% CI, 36.6 to 48.9) for the placebo-DabTram arm. In most of the subgroups of patient and tumor characteristics, treatment effect was in favor of sparta-DabTram compared with placebo-DabTram (HR < 1; Appendix Fig A2).

Safety

Adverse events (AEs) were reported in 99.3% and 97.3% of patients in the sparta-DabTram arm and the placebo-DabTram arm, respectively. Regardless of relation to the study treatment, the most common AE was pyrexia in the two study arms (Table 2). The median duration of pyrexia was 13.0 (IQR, 6.0-32.0) days in the sparta-DabTram arm and 9.0 (IQR, 4.0-19.0) days in the placebo-DabTram arm. In patients who had pyrexia, the median time to first occurrence of pyrexia was 0.46 months (range, 0.03-37.45) and 1.08 months (range, 0.03-58.78) in the two arms, respectively.

All-grade and grade ≥3 treatment-related AEs (TRAEs) were reported in 98.5% and 88.6% of patients and 57.3% and 36.7% of patients, respectively, in the two study arms. The most common TRAE was pyrexia, seen in 65.9% and 46.2% of patients, respectively, in the two arms (Appendix Table A2).

Regardless of relation to the study treatment, serious AEs (SAEs) were reported in 56.2% of patients in the sparta-DabTram arm and 46.6% of patients in the placebo-DabTram arm. The most common SAE was pyrexia (17.2% and 6.1% of patients in the two arms, respectively). SAEs suspected to be related to the study treatment and fatal SAEs are summarized in Appendix Tables A3 and A4. Deaths are summarized in Appendix Table A5.

The most common AE of special interest (AESI) with spartalizumab was skin reactions, seen in 54.0% of patients (61.0% and 47.0% in the two arms, respectively); of them, 4% had grade ≥3 skin reactions (6.7% and 1.1% in the two arms, respectively). The incidence of other immune-mediated AESIs was higher with sparta-DabTram than with placebo-DabTram (Appendix Table A6).

Post-Treatment Antineoplastic Therapies

Post-treatment therapies were used in 123 (46.1%) and 134 (50.6%) patients in the sparta-DabTram and placebo-DabTram arms, respectively. Immunotherapy, used in 74 (27.7%) and 106 (40.0%) patients in the two arms, respectively, was the most common post-treatment therapy (Appendix Table A7).

DISCUSSION

Various combination immunotherapies have been evaluated in patients with advanced BRAF V600–mutant melanoma in previous studies.16-18 The phase II SECOMBIT trial and the US cooperative arm group DREAMseq trial supported immunotherapy as the preferred first-line treatment for most patients with BRAF V600–mutant advanced melanoma.16,17 However, the rapid disease progression in patients who received immunotherapy warrants the development of additional treatment options. The combination of targeted therapies and immunotherapies for BRAF-mutant melanoma has been hypothesized to merge the benefit of the rapid responses to the BRAF and MEK inhibitors with the long-term benefit of anti–PD-1 immunotherapy. Several studies have explored the potential benefits of combining targeted therapies with anti–PD-1 or anti–PDL-1 therapies.7,8,19

The COMBI-I trial has the longest reported follow-up when compared with other studies that investigated the combination of an immune checkpoint inhibitor with BRAF and MEK inhibitors.8,19 In this final analysis, median OS was numerically higher for the sparta-DabTram arm by 20 months than for the placebo-DabTram arm, with a clear separation of the Kaplan-Meier curves seen early (after 6 months) and sustained over time.

The safety results in the final analysis were consistent with those reported in the primary analysis.15 No new safety signals were observed.

The previously reported analyses of circulating tumor DNA shedding and CD4(+)/CD8(+) T-cell ratio support the combined pharmacodynamic effects of sparta-DabTram in patients with BRAF V600–mutant metastatic melanoma.20

The key limitation of this study is the high number of patients with interrupted survival data collection because of study termination, and therefore, they were censored for OS. Other limitations include the imbalanced use of immune checkpoint inhibitors postprogression, use of PD-1 inhibitors in only about two thirds of patients (n = 102) in the placebo arm, treatment discontinuation because of grade ≥3 AEs, and nonadherence to protocol-specified dosing. The onset of pyrexia, the most common AE, could have influenced the dosing schedule.

In conclusion, the combination of sparta-DabTram for the treatment of patients with BRAF V600–mutant metastatic melanoma suggests a trend for survival benefits over targeted therapy alone.

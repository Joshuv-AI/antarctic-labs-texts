Introduction

The
rapid neutron-capture process
(
r
-process
) and
slow neutron-capture process
(
s
-process
) are primary nucleosynthesis mechanisms responsible for producing elements heavier than iron in the universe. The
r
-process
is believed to occur under extreme physical conditions, yet its astrophysical site remains a highly debated topic. The gravitational wave event GW170817 and its associated electromagnetic kilonova counterpart AT2017gfo
(
2017PhRvL.119p1101A
)
have provided compelling evidence that
neutron star mergers
(
NSMs
) can produce heavy elements
(
2023arXiv230209188F
)
. However, the NSM scenario faces challenges in explaining the observed
\left[\mathrm{Eu/Fe}\right]
and
\left[\mathrm{Fe/H}\right]
relationship in
very metal-poor
(
VMP
) stars in the Milky Way due to their long merging timescales
(
2023ApJ...943L..12K
)
. Other potential astrophysical sites for
r
-process
include
magnetorotationally driven jet supernovae
(
MRDSNe
)
(
2012ApJ...750L..22W
;
2015ApJ...810..109N
;
2017ApJ...836L..21N
)
, and collapsar jets
(
1999ApJ...524..262M
;
2019Natur.569..241S
;
2024ApJ...966L..37H
)
. However, these astrophysical sites lack direct observational evidence. Determining the astrophysical sites and yield of the
r
-process
, as well as the frequency of
r
-process
events, remains a persistent goal.

VMP
(
\left[\mathrm{Fe/H}\right]<-2.0
) stars
(
2005ARA&A..43..531B
)
are considered among the oldest objects in the universe, with their surfaces preserving the chemical signatures of their formation. For
r
-process
-enhanced
VMP
stars in the halo of the Milky Way, the
r
-process
elements on their surfaces are believed to originate from a single
r
-process
event. These stars are classified according to their
\left[\mathrm{Eu/Fe}\right]
and
\left[\mathrm{Ba/Fe}\right]
:
\mathrm{r}
-
\rm I
stars have
0.3\leq\left[\mathrm{Eu/Fe}\right]\leq 1.0
and
\left[\mathrm{Ba/Eu}\right]<0.0
,
\mathrm{r}
-
\rm II
stars have
\left[\mathrm{Eu/Fe}\right]>1.0
and
\left[\mathrm{Ba/Eu}\right]<0.0

(
2005ARA&A..43..531B
)
, while
\mathrm{r}
-
\rm III
stars have
\left[\mathrm{Eu/Fe}\right]>2.0
and
\left[\mathrm{Ba/Eu}\right]<0.0

(
2020ApJ...898...40C
)
. CS 22892-052 was the first extreme
r
-process
-enhanced star discovered
(
1994ApJ...431L..27S
)
, with
\left[\mathrm{Eu/Fe}\right]
exceeding that of the Sun by more than 10 times. After nearly 30 years of unremitting efforts
(
1992AJ....103.1987B
;
2008A&A...484..721C
;
2005A&A...439..129B
;
2020ApJS..249...30H
;
2022ApJ...931..147L
)
, approximately 480
\mathrm{r}
-
\rm I
stars, 165
\mathrm{r}
-
\rm II
stars, and 2
\mathrm{r}
-
\rm III
stars have been discovered
(
2020ApJ...898...40C
;
2022ApJ...931..147L
;
2023ApJ...943...23S
;
2024ApJ...971..158R
)
.

Numerous studies have established that the abundance patterns from the range of
\mathrm{Ba}
to
\mathrm{Pt}
in
r
-process
-enhanced stars with varying metallicity and
\mathrm{Eu}
abundance are consistent and align with the solar
r
-process
residual. However, this homogeneity breaks down in the region of light neutron-capture elements (
Z<56
) and the heaviest neutron-capture elements (
Z=90,92
). Approximately one-third of
r
-process
-enhanced stars exhibit
\log\epsilon\rm(\mathrm{Th}/\mathrm{Eu})
that are roughly 0.4 dex higher than those of other
r
-process
-enhanced stars
(
2014A&A...569A..43M
;
2018ApJ...868..110S
)
. This phenomenon is known as actinide-boost
(
2002A&A...387..560H
)
, but the underlying cause of the overall high actinide abundances in these stars remains unidentified.

In this letter, we report the discovery and present a detailed abundance analysis covering the full
r
-process
pattern of an actinide-boost star within the
Gaia-Sausage-Enceladus
(
GSE
), J0804+5740. In Section
2
, we introduce the high-resolution spectral observations, and the methodology for determining the atmospheric parameters. Detailed information on the derived element abundances is provided in Section
3
. In Section
4
, we discuss the abundance pattern of J0804+5740, the potential connections with the fission of transuranic nuclei, as well as the dynamical signatures of
r
-process
-enhanced metal-poor stars with measurable
\mathrm{Th}
abundances. Our summary and conclusions are presented in Section
5
.

2
Observations and data analysis

J0804+5740 was classified as an
\mathrm{r}
-
\rm II

VMP
star due to its elevated
\left[\mathrm{Eu/Fe}\right]
, based on high-resolution (
R\sim 36,000
) spectra covering a wavelength range of
4030-6800\ \textup{\AA}

(
2022ApJ...931..147L
)
, obtained with the Subaru Telescope /
High Dispersion Spectrograph
(
HDS
)
(
2002PASJ...54..855N
)
. Additionally, based on its highly radial/eccentric orbit characteristics, it was identified as a member of the
GSE
substructure
(
2024ApJ...966..174Z
)
. To further investigate its properties, high-resolution (
R\sim 60,000
) blue-end spectra were obtained, with a total exposure time of
2400\ \mathrm{s}
. By employing the StdBc setup with a
0.6\arcsec
slit and
2\times 2
CCD binning, a
signal-to-noise ratio
(
S/N
) of 142 per pixel at
4500\ \textup{\AA}
was achieved, covering the range from
3550\ \textup{\AA}
to
5210\ \textup{\AA}
.

The effective temperature (
T_{\rm eff}
) and surface gravity (
\log\textit{g}
) of J0804+5740 are determined photometrically, based on the method described in
2022ApJ...931..147L
. We prefer to use photometry and parallax to determine the
T_{\rm eff}
and
\log\textit{g}
since Fe
\rm I
lines are significantly affected by
non-local thermodynamic equilibrium
(
NLTE
) effects in metal-poor giants. We adopt the color-
T_{\rm eff}
-
[\mathrm{Fe}/\mathrm{H}]
relationship for
T_{\rm eff}
from
2005ApJ...626..465R
. Specifically, we use the (
\emph{V}-\emph{K}_{s}
) color index, which is less sensitive to variations in surface gravity and metallicity
(
1999A&AS..140..261A
)
. The
V
and
\emph{K}_{s}
magnitudes are sourced from the APASS and 2MASS catalogs, with extinction estimated from
2018MNRAS.478..651G
. We use reliable parallax measurements (
\sigma_{\mathrm{parallax}}/\mathrm{parallax}=0.06
) from Gaia DR3, along with the parallax zero-point correction provided by
2021ApJ...910L...5H
. To calculate the star’s surface gravity, the following formula is employed:

\small\begin{split}\log\textit{g}=\log\textit{g}_{\odot}+\log\frac{M}{M_{\odot%
}}+4\log\frac{T_{\rm eff}}{T_{\rm eff\odot}}\\
+0.4\left(M_{\mathrm{bol}}-M_{\mathrm{bol\odot}}\right),\end{split}

(1)

where the stellar mass (
M
) is assumed to be 0.8
M_{\odot}

(
2022ApJ...931..147L
)
. Additionally, the following reference values for the Sun are adopted:
\log\textit{g}_{\odot}
= 4.438,
\log T_{\rm eff\odot}
= 3.7617,
M_{\mathrm{bol\odot}}
= 4.75
(
2024MNRAS.529.1917S
)
. Furthermore, we apply the bolometric correction in the
V
-band using the relationship described by
1999A&AS..140..261A
.

Due to the interdependence of atmospheric parameters, we determine the final values through an iterative process. Specifically, the determination of microscopic turbulence involves minimizing the trend in the abundance of
\mathrm{Fe}\;\rm I
lines and their reduced equivalent widths (
\log\mathrm{EW}/{\lambda}
). Additionally, the abundance obtained from
\mathrm{Fe}\;\rm II
lines is adopted to determine
\rm\left[\mathrm{Fe}/\mathrm{H}\right]
. The resulting atmospheric parameter values obtained in this study are summarized in Table
1
. These values are consistent with those reported in
2022ApJ...931..147L
. It is worth noting that the
\rm\left[\mathrm{Fe}/\mathrm{H}\right]
in
2022ApJ...931..147L
was determined using the abundance obtained from
\mathrm{Fe}\;\rm I
lines.

Element Abundance

We measure
equivalent widths
(
EWs
) for over 600 lines and derive the abundances for 48 species. The atomic parameters of the lines used to derive the abundances, along with the EW measurements, are presented in Table
A1
. Table
2
lists the final abundances, along with their associated uncertainties, the number of measured lines, and the method to derive the abundance. Solar abundances are adopted from
2009ARA&A..47..481A
. The species abundances are derived from EW measurements or spectral synthesis. Figure
1
shows examples of spectral syntheses for key lines. We adopt the 1D plane-parallel, hydrostatic model atmosphere from the ATLAS NEWODF grid by
2003IAUS..210P.A20C
to analyze the abundance of all species, assuming
local thermodynamic equilibrium
(
LTE
) and no convective overshoot. The
EWs
of the various lines are measured with the
Tool for Automatic Measurement of Equivalent-width
(
TAME
)
(
2012MNRAS.425.3162K
)
, which fits theoretical Gaussian/Voigt profiles to normalized observed spectra. Additionally, we use an updated version of the abundance analysis code MOOG
(
1973PhDT.......180S
)
, treating Rayleigh scattering as coherent, isotropic scattering
(
2011AJ....141..175S
)
. We compile line lists from various literature sources
(
2004A&A...416.1117C
;
2013A&A...558A..36C
;
2014A&A...569A..43M
;
2017A&A...607A..91H
;
2018ApJ...865..129R
;
2018ApJ...862..174G
;
2022ApJ...931..147L
;
2024MNRAS.529.1917S
)
and updated their atomic parameters from VALD
(
2015PhyS...90e4005R
)
. Molecular and blended atomic line lists for spectral synthesis are generated from Linemake
(
2021RNAAS...5...92P
)
, an open-source atomic and molecular line list generation tool.

The uncertainties in elemental abundances are determined by two primary components: uncertainties arising from measurements and those associated with atmospheric parameters, denoted as
\sigma_{\mathrm{stat}}
and
\sigma_{\mathrm{sys}}
, respectively, and presented in columns 6 and 7 of Table
2
. The
\sigma_{\mathrm{stat}}
mainly originates from the uncertainties in the measurements of
EWs
, the oscillator strength of lines, and line blending. It can be estimated from
\frac{\sigma_{X}}{\sqrt{N}}
, where
\sigma_{X}
represents the standard deviation of the abundance of species
X
derived from N lines. For cases where the number of adopted lines is fewer than four,
\sigma_{\mathrm{stat}}
is taken as the larger value of
\frac{\sigma_{X}}{\sqrt{N}}
and
\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{FeII}}}{\sqrt{N}}
. The
\sigma_{\mathrm{sys}}
primarily arises from the uncertainties in atmospheric parameters. By individually varying the
T_{\rm eff}
,
\log\textit{g}
,
\xi
, and
\rm\left[\mathrm{Fe}/\mathrm{H}\right]
by 1
\sigma
, and calculating the square root of the quadratic sum corresponding to the change in abundance, we estimate
\sigma_{\mathrm{sys}}
. Notably, neutral transitions of molybdenum (
\rm\mathrm{Mo}\ I
), ruthenium (
\rm\mathrm{Ru}\ I
), rhodium (
\rm\mathrm{Rh}\ I
), and osmium (
\rm\mathrm{Os}\ I
) exhibit the highest sensitivity to these parameter changes, with
\sigma_{\mathrm{sys}}
in their derived abundances exceeding 0.15 dex. The total error (
\sigma_{\log\epsilon}
) is calculated as the square root of the sum in quadrature of
\sigma_{\mathrm{stat}}
and
\sigma_{\mathrm{sys}}
, and is listed in column 8 of Table
2
.

Compared to the optical spectrum with
R\sim 36,000

(
2022ApJ...931..147L
)
, the blue-end spectrum exhibits higher resolution (
R\sim 60,000
) and
S/N
. Specifically, the
S/N
at 4100
Å
of the blue-end spectrum (73) is significantly higher than that of the optical spectrum (25). Consequently, the species abundances of J0804+5740 are determined using the blue-end spectrum, with the exception of the
\rm\mathrm{O}\ I
,
\rm\mathrm{Na}\ I
,
\rm\mathrm{Ca}\ I
,
\rm\mathrm{Sc}\ II
,
\rm\mathrm{V}\ I
, and
\rm\mathrm{Ba}\ II
abundances, whose absorption lines are predominantly concentrated in the optical band. The species abundances agree well with those reported in
2022ApJ...931..147L
within 2
\sigma
, except for Co, Zr, and La. The primary reason for the differences in the abundances of Co, Zr, and La is the
S/N
of the spectrum. Additionally, the blending with
\mathrm{Fe}\;\rm I
lines and the
hyperfine structure
(
HFS
) effect
(
1968PhRv..170...50C
;
1990ZPhyD..17..181G
;
1996ApJS..107..811P
)
significantly impact the abundance of Co.

3.1
Light Neutron-capture Elements

The detection of light neutron-capture elements, particularly those between the first and second peaks, poses significant challenges when using the optical spectrum. We use the blue-end spectrum to derive the abundances of
\mathrm{Sr}
,
\mathrm{Y}
,
\mathrm{Zr}
,
\mathrm{Mo}
,
\mathrm{Ru}
, and
\mathrm{Rh}
. The abundance of
\mathrm{Sr}
is derived through spectral synthesis of two lines at 4077 and 4215 Å . For
\mathrm{Y}
and
\mathrm{Zr}
, which have numerous available lines (14 and 21, respectively) in the blue end of the spectrum, we adopt the abundance values derived from the
EWs
without any modifications. In the case of
\mathrm{Mo}
,
\mathrm{Ru}
, and
\mathrm{Rh}
, where only a few lines (ranging from one to four) are detectable, we employ spectral synthesis to determine their abundances. It is worth noting that we detect only a faint absorption signature for the
\mathrm{Nb}\;\rm II
at 3740 Å. This line is significantly affected by spectral noise. Consequently, we could only determine an upper limit for the abundance of
\mathrm{Nb}
.

The deviations in the abundance of light neutron-capture elements in J0804+5740 from the solar
r
-process
pattern are observed, particularly for
\mathrm{Sr}
and
\mathrm{Zr}
, as shown in Figure
2
. These deviations could be explained by assuming the production of these elements in the ”weak”
r
-process

(
2001ApJ...554..578W
)
and the
light-element primary process
(
LEPP
)
(
2004ApJ...601..864T
)
scenario. Notably,
2022ApJ...936...84R
identified good internal consistency in the abundance of light neutron-capture elements among
r
-process
-enhanced stars, implying an astrophysical origin independent of the main
r
-process
. Furthermore, the fission of transuranic nuclei could have contributed to the abundance of these elements
(
2020ApJ...896...28V
;
2023Sci...382.1177R
)
, as described in Section
4.3
.

3.2
Heavy Neutron-capture Elements

The abundances for 20 heavy neutron-capture elements are derived, including all the measurable lanthanides, and the upper limit for the abundance of
\mathrm{U}
in J0804+5740 is estimated. The abundances of most elements are obtained through spectral synthesis, taking into account the effects of
HFS
and
isotopic shifts
(
IS
). For
\mathrm{Ba}
,
\mathrm{Eu}
,
\mathrm{Yb}
, and
\mathrm{Pb}
, the solar
r
-process
isotope ratios are adopted.

Like most
r
-process
enhanced stars, the abundance pattern of most heavy neutron-capture elements in J0804+5740 agrees well with the solar
r
-process
, indicating that main
r
-process
produced these elements in the early universe
(
2018ARNPS..68..237F
)
. However, some elements exhibit deviations from the solar
r
-process
abundance pattern, particularly
\mathrm{Ba}
,
\mathrm{La}
, and
\mathrm{Ce}
. For instance,
\rm\left[\mathrm{Ba}/\mathrm{Eu}\right]=-0.42\pm 0.13
is notably higher than the pure
r
-process
value of
\rm\left[\mathrm{Ba}/\mathrm{Eu}\right]
, which varies from -0.81 to -0.90
(
2008ARA&A..46..241S
;
2014ApJ...787...10B
;
2020MNRAS.491.1832P
)
, and slightly higher than the value of approximately -0.6 observed in most
r
-process
-enhanced stars
(
2018ApJ...858...92H
;
2018ApJ...868..110S
;
2020ApJ...898..150E
;
2020ApJS..249...30H
;
2024ApJS..274...39B
)
. To account for this, we incorporate solar
s
-process
contributions during the fitting process to reproduce the observed abundances of heavy neutron-capture elements. The fitting result, as shown in Figure
2
, demonstrates that all lanthanides align well with the solar
r
+
s
-process.

\mathrm{Os}
and
\mathrm{Ir}
represent the third
r
-process
peak. The abundance of
\mathrm{Os}
is derived from the lines at 4260 and 4420 Å. The
\mathrm{Os}\;\rm I
line at 4420
Å
is significantly blended with
\mathrm{Sm}
, as shown in Figure
1
. The abundance of
\mathrm{Ir}
is obtained only from the
\mathrm{Ir}\;\rm I
3800
Å
. The abundances of both elements are in good agreement with the solar
r
-process
.

The abundance of
\mathrm{Pb}
is derived from the
\mathrm{Pb}\;\rm I
line at 4057 Å, yielding
\rm\left[\mathrm{Pb}/\mathrm{Fe}\right]=0.78
. The abundance of
\mathrm{Pb}
is significantly higher than that value of the solar
r
-process
. This discrepancy may indicate contamination from the
s
-process
. As a third-peak element of the
s
-process
, Pb is challenging to measure in r-process-enhanced stars. Importantly,
\mathrm{Pb}
is the radioactive decay product of
\mathrm{Th}
and
\mathrm{U}
. As the abundances of
\mathrm{Th}
and
\mathrm{U}
decrease, the
\mathrm{Pb}
abundance will increase by 0.1 dex after 13 Gyr
(
2024ApJ...971..158R
)
. Due to the non-negligible contribution of the
s
-process
to the abundance of
\mathrm{Pb}
in J0804+5740, we cannot use
\rm\left[\mathrm{Pb}/\mathrm{X}\right]
as the chronometer pairs to calculate its age.

3.3
The Actinides

\mathrm{Th}
and
\mathrm{U}
are the heaviest elements produced by the
r
-process
, yet their detection remains challenging. To date, measurements of
\mathrm{Th}
have been achieved in 53 metal-poor stars, while
\mathrm{U}
detection has been reported in only 8 metal-poor stars
(
2002ApJ...572..861C
;
2002A&A...387..560H
;
2007ApJ...660L.117F
;
2017A&A...607A..91H
;
2017ApJ...844...18P
;
2018ApJ...859L..24H
;
2021Natur.595..223Y
;
2023ApJ...948..122S
;
2024ApJ...971..158R
)
. In the case of J0804+5740, the
\mathrm{Th}
abundance is derived from the strongest
\mathrm{Th}\ \rm II\ line
at 4019
Å
. Figure
1
shows the spectral synthesis of this line, demonstrating varying degrees of blending with different species. Notably, this blending effect is significantly reduced in metal-poor stars that lack substantial carbon-enhancement (
\rm\left[\mathrm{C}/\mathrm{Fe}\right]<0.7
). Unfortunately, due to the significant blending of the
\mathrm{U}\;\rm II\ 3859

Å
line with
\mathrm{Fe}\;\rm I
and
\mathrm{CN}
molecular lines, we can only provide an upper limit for the abundance of
\mathrm{U}
.

Given that
{}^{232}\mathrm{Th}
and
{}^{238}\mathrm{U}
are unstable isotopes with half-lives of 14.05 Gyr and 4.47 Gyr, respectively, we anticipate variations in the ratio of actinides-to-lanthanides among
r
-process-enhanced stars. Interestingly, approximately one-third of stars with measurable Th abundances exhibit excessive actinide abundances (
\log\epsilon\rm(\mathrm{Th}/\mathrm{Eu})>-0.35
or
\log\epsilon\rm(\mathrm{Th}/\mathrm{Dy})>-0.90
)
(
2014A&A...569A..43M
;
2019ApJ...881....5H
)
, leading to small or even unrealistic ages calculated from the ratio of actinides to stable lanthanides, if fixed initial abundance ratios are assumed. J0804+5740 is thus classified as an actinide-boost star, with
\log\epsilon\rm(\mathrm{Th}/\mathrm{Eu})=-0.22
, and is a rare example of actinide-boost at relatively high metallicity, as shown in Figure
3
. From previous studies, a slight decrease in
\log\epsilon\rm(\mathrm{Th}/\mathrm{Eu})
with increasing
\rm\left[\mathrm{Fe}/\mathrm{H}\right]
could be found: more actinide-boost stars are found at lower metallicities
(
2022MNRAS.516.3786M
;
2023MNRAS.524.5607S
)
. Our result, however, provides a new data point at relatively high metallicity with high Th/Eu. Further studies of actinide abundances for stars with
\rm\left[\mathrm{Fe}/\mathrm{H}\right]>-2.5
are useful to understand the actinide-boost phenomenon.

4
Discussion

4.1
Abundance Pattern

The
s
-process
may have a significant contribution to the abundance patterns of the stars with
\rm\left[\mathrm{Fe}/\mathrm{H}\right]>-2.6

(
2004ApJ...617.1091S
)
. As indicated in Figure
2
, the surface abundance of J0804+5740 exhibits consistency with the solar
r
+
s
-process abundance pattern, indicating
s
-process
contamination. We use the method as described in
2024ApJ...976...68J
to fit the abundance pattern of J0804+5740. For the abundance of the element
X
, we consider it to arise from both
r
- and
s
-process nucleosynthesis. The calculations were performed using Equation
2
:

\log\epsilon(X)=\log_{10}(10^{\log\epsilon(X)_{r}+O_{r}}+10^{\log\epsilon(X)_{%
s}+O_{s}})

(2)

where
\log\epsilon(X)_{r}
and
\log\epsilon(X)_{s}
denote the abundance of element
X
from the solar
r
- and
s
-processes, respectively, and
O_{r}
and
O_{s}
represent their corresponding dilution factors. Based on Equation
2
, we find that approximately 78
\%
of the heavy-element abundances on its surface originate from the
r
-process
, while only 22
\%
result from the
s
-process
. This suggests that the heavy-elements composition in J0804+5740 remains predominantly influenced by the
r
-process
.

The radial velocity of J0804+5740, measured using a high-resolution spectrum from the Subaru Telescope, is presented in Table
1
. Comparison with measurements from
Large Sky Area Multi-Object Fiber Spectroscopic Telescope
(
LAMOST
) DR7
(
2022yCat.5156....0L
)
and Gaia DR3 shows no significant radial velocity variation within 2
\sigma
, indicating that J0804+5740 is unlikely to be part of a binary system. Furthermore, its
Renormalised Unit Weight Error
(
RUWE
) value of 0.979 suggests that the astrometric observations of J0804+5740 are consistent with a single-star model. Additionally, compared to CEMP (Carbon-Enhanced Metal-Poor)-s stars (
\rm\left[\mathrm{C}/\mathrm{Fe}\right]\geq 0.7
,
2007ApJ...655..492A
), J0804+5740 exhibits no significant carbon enrichment (
\rm\left[\mathrm{C}/\mathrm{Fe}\right]=0.15
) after accounting for the effect of stellar evolution
(
2014ApJ...797...21P
)
. This suggests that the
s
-process
elements in J0804+5740 may not originate from a mass transfer event involving an
AGB
companion star. Instead, it indicates that the birth gas cloud of this star has been contaminated by the
s
-process

(
2021ApJ...912...52G
)
.

To study the properties of the progenitors that enriched the abundance of J0804+5740, we compare its abundance pattern with different theoretical models. We select three different
r
-process
theoretical models: the NSM models
(
2015MNRAS.448..541J
)
, the MRDSN models
(
2017ApJ...836L..21N
)
, and the collapsar model
(
2024ApJ...966L..37H
)
, to determine the origin of
r
-process
elements in J0804+5740. Additionally, due to its low metallicity, and the metallicity dependence of the
s
-process
, we choose the
AGB

s
-process

(
2010MNRAS.404.1529B
)
at low metallicity to replace the solar
s
-process
. The final result is shown in Figure
2
. The best fit is obtained by combining the MRDSN
r
-process
model with
\hat{L}v
= 0.2 and the
AGB

s
-process
model with
\rm\left[\mathrm{Fe}/\mathrm{H}\right]=-2.6
and
M

=1.4{M_{\odot}}
, which reproduces the abundance characteristics of the lanthanides, the third peak of the
r
-process
(Os; Ir) and
s
-process
(Pb), as well as the actinide-boost feature. However, none of the three models provided a good fit in the region of light neutron-capture elements. By employing the dilution factors of the
MRDSNe

r
-process
calculated from Equation
2
, we determine the dilution mass
M_{\mathrm{H}}=1.22\times 10^{6}M_{\odot}
. This result suggests two possible scenarios for the progenitor system of J0804+5740: (1) a dwarf galaxy with a mass of
10^{6}M_{\odot}
that was accreted by the
GSE
, or (2) a single
r
-process
event that enriched
10^{6}M_{\odot}
of gas within the
GSE
. Significantly, due to the large nuclear physics uncertainties in
r
-process
nucleosynthesis networks, any attempt to compare the abundance patterns between the second and third peaks of the
r
-process
with theoretical models remains fraught with risk.

4.2
kinematics

J0804+5740 is classified as a member of the
GSE
due to its highly radial orbital characteristics. Notably, it is the first actinide-boost star identified within the
GSE
to date. To further investigate the origins of actinide-boost stars by incorporating kinematic information, we compile a sample consisting of r-process-enhanced metal-poor stars with measurable Th abundances from the literature, building upon the discovery of J0804+5740. We use photo-astrometric distances provided by
2022A_A...658A..91A
, and astrometric data from Gaia DR3
(
2023A_A...674A...1G
)
to calculate the kinematic and orbital properties of these stars. We only utilized data that exhibit reliable astrometric results (
RUWE

<
1.4) and relatively precise distance measurements (
\sigma_{d}/d<0.4
), ultimately retaining 48 stars in our final sample. We employ the Galactocentric Cartesian coordinate system and assume that the position and velocity of the Sun are
(X,Y,Z)_{\odot}=(-8.21,0.0,0.0208)\;\mathrm{kpc}

(
2017MNRAS.465...76M
;
2019MNRAS.482.1417B
)
and
(U,V,W)_{\odot}=(11.1,12.24,7.25)\;\mathrm{km\;s^{-1}}

(
2010MNRAS.403.1829S
)
. The velocity of the Local Standard of Rest (
V_{\mathrm{LSR}}
) is adopted as 233.1
\mathrm{km\;s^{-1}}
. We use the software
AGAMA

(
2019MNRAS.482.1525V
)
and the gravitational potential model of the Milky Way from
2017MNRAS.465...76M
to calculate the dynamical and orbital properties, including the orbital energy (
E
), orbital pericentric radius (
r_{\mathrm{peri}}
), orbital apocentric radius (
r_{\mathrm{apo}}
), orbital eccentricity (
ecc
), maximum height from the Galactic plane (
Z_{\mathrm{max}}
), and action (
J_{r},J_{\phi},J_{z}
). These quantities for J0804+5740 are listed in Table
1
.

Based on kinematic and orbital parameters, we employ deep learning methodologies to distinguish between
ex-situ
stars (originating from accreted dwarf galaxies) and
in-situ
stars (formed within the Milky way) in this sample
(
2024MNRAS.527.9767L
)
. The classification results are presented in Figure
3
. Our analysis reveals that among the 9 actinide-boost stars, 6 are classified as
ex-situ
stars, while only 3 are categorized as
in-situ
stars. This finding suggests that actinide-boost stars are more likely to originate from dwarf galaxies that were accreted by the Milky Way.

The origin of actinide-boost stars remains an unresolved question in astrophysics. Theoretical studies suggest that the actinide-boost phenomenon may arise in environments with significantly low electron fractions (

or

(

. The discovery of J0804+5740 suggests that

r
-process
nucleosynthesis, capable of producing the actinide-boost phenomenon
(
2021Natur.595..223Y
)
. This finding provides critical constraints on
r
-process
nucleosynthesis and the production mechanisms of actinide elements in accreted dwarf galaxies such as the
GSE
.

4.3
The fission of transuranic nuclei

Recently,
2023Sci...382.1177R
discussed a correlation between the light neutron-capture elements (
44\leq Z\leq 47
) and the heavy neutron-capture elements (
63\leq Z\leq 78
) in
r
-process
enhanced stars. They proposed that the fission fragments of transuranic nuclei enhanced the abundance of these elements. Figure
4
shows the relationship between
\log\epsilon\rm(\mathrm{X/Zr})
and
\log\epsilon\rm(\mathrm{X/Sm})
with
\rm\left[\mathrm{Eu}/\mathrm{Fe}\right]
for the sample stars from
2023Sci...382.1177R
. We choose to use the
\log\epsilon\rm(\mathrm{X/Sm})
ratio instead of
\log\epsilon\rm(\mathrm{X/Ba})
because, similar to
\mathrm{Ba}
, the fission fragments of transuranic nuclei do not significantly contribute to the abundance of
\mathrm{Sm}
, while the
s
-process
contributes less to the abundance of
\mathrm{Sm}
compared to
\mathrm{Ba}
, as shown in Figure
2
. We calculate the baseline abundance of
\log\epsilon\rm(\mathrm{X/Sm})
and its correlation with
\rm\left[\mathrm{Eu}/\mathrm{Fe}\right]
for their sample stars following the method described by
2023Sci...382.1177R
. We find that the light and heavy neutron-capture elements in J0804+5740 follow the overall trend, with most element abundance ratios exceeding the baseline ratio. This may indicate potential evidence of fission fragment contributions to the abundances in J0804+5740.

5
Conclusion

We present a detailed chemical abundance analysis of J0804+5740, the first actinide-boost star identified in the
GSE
, characterized by its high actinide-to-lanthanide ratio of
\log\epsilon\rm(\mathrm{Th}/\mathrm{Eu})=-0.22
. This star was initially discovered through the
LAMOST
low-resolution survey, and its high-resolution (
R\sim 36,000\;\&\;60,000
) spectra were subsequently obtained using Subaru/
HDS
.

We derive the abundances of 48 species in this star, including
\mathrm{Th}
. The observed abundance pattern aligns well with the solar
r
-process
abundance pattern. The MRDSN
r
-process
theoretical model with
\hat{L}v
= 0.2 provides a better fit to the abundance features of this star, corresponding to the dilution of
r
-process
material into a gas of
10^{6}M_{\odot}
. However, the abundance ratios of certain elements suggest an additional small contribution from the
s
-process
, indicating a more complex nucleosynthetic origin.

J0804+5740 represents a rare example of an actinide-boost star at relatively high metallicity. Further investigations of actinide-boost stars with
\rm\left[\mathrm{Fe}/\mathrm{H}\right]>-2.5
will provide valuable insights into the mechanisms underlying the actinide-boost phenomenon. Furthermore, our comprehensive kinematic analysis reveals that the number of
ex-situ
stars among actinide-boost stars is twice that of

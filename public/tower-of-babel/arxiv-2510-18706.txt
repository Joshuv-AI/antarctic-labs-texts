Binding energy of compact stars and their non-radial oscillations
P. Laskos-Patkos 1, S. Papadopoulos 2, Ch.C. Moustakidis 1

Abstract
In the past years, a significant effort has been made with the scope of determining correlations, involving compact star properties, that are independent of the nuclear equation of state. Such universal relations are of utmost importance as they allow for the imposition of constraints on stellar properties without directly measuring them and they may also serve as a probe of General Relativity. In the present study, we investigated the possible existence of a universal relation between the binding energy of compact stars and the frequency of their non-radial oscillations. The main motivation was related to the fact that both of the aforementioned quantities might be measured in the occurrence of a supernova explosion. Interestingly, we found that there is a empirical relation between the oscillation frequency and the binding energy for both f f and p 1 p_{1} modes, assuming hadronic stellar matter. The inclusion of hybrid equations of state, incorporating sharp phase transitions, was shown to result into deviations from the aforementioned quasi-universal relation.

Last, any future insight on the binding energy could narrow down the range of possible frequencies for different modes, potentially aiding their detection.

II
Binding energy of compact stars

The binding energy stands for the energy gain due to the assembling of
N
baryons to form a stable star. As in the case of an atomic nucleus, the binding energy can be obtained via
Lattimer-2001
(
G=c=1
)

E_{b}=M_{b}-M=Nm_{b}-M,

(1)

where
m_{b}
is the mass of single baryon,
M_{b}
corresponds the baryonic mass of the star and
M
corresponds to the gravitational mass (to which we will refer simply as mass from now on) which is derived by the solution of Einstein’s equations (or more precisely the TOV equations). Specifically,

M=4\pi\int_{0}^{R}r^{2}\mathcal{E}(r)dr,

(2)

where
\mathcal{E}(r)
corresponds to the energy density distribution and
R
is the radius of the star. The total number of nucleons

is found by integrating the baryon density profile as

N=\int n(r)dV,

(3)

where

dV=4\pi r^{2}\sqrt{g_{rr}(r)}dr=4\pi r^{2}e^{\Lambda(r)}dr,

(4)

is the infinitesimal proper volume and

e^{\Lambda(r)}=\sqrt{g_{rr}(r)}=\left(1-\frac{2m(r)}{r}\right)^{-1/2}.

(5)

In Equation (
5
),
m(r)
stands for the gravitational mass distribution of the star.

In the present study, following the authors of Refs.
Lattimer-2001

III
Non radial stellar oscillations

The study of stellar pulsations is based on the framework of perturbation theory applied to fluid dynamics and by extension to General Theory of Relativity. The addition of small deviations to a background equilibrium model leads to periodic phenomena or perturbations to the matter fields. We can distinguish the weak field case (Newtonian case) and the strong field case (relativistic case). In both of these cases, one analyzes the perturbations in spherical harmonics
Y_{m}^{l}
, taking advantage of the geometry of the background stellar model. The modes are decomposed to odd (toroidal or axial) modes and to even (spheroidal or polar) modes, with respect to the corresponding behavior under parity transformations. Odd modes do not contribute to the formation of normal modes. An extensive analysis on the matter can be found in Ref.
Thorne-1980
.

The framework for the study of non radial stellar oscillations was given in the pioneering study of Thorne and Campolattaro
Thorne-1967
. Several years later, Lindblom and Detweiler
Lindblom-1983
managed to reduce the equations to a numerically solvable system and studied the quadrupole (
l=2
) oscillations of neutron stars. This system of coupled differential equations, given the appropriate boundary conditions, forms a Sturm-Liouville eigenvalue problem for the oscillation frequency
\omega
. The resulting frequencies may classified in different categories (which appear in distinct areas of the frequency spectrum) based on the nature of the pulsation. In particular, the stellar pulsation could be related to acoustic waves or
p
modes (high
\omega
values), the formation of internal gravity waves or
g
modes (low
\omega
) and the surface gravity waves or fundamental (
f
) modes (intermediate
\omega
values). It is worth noting that the frequency is common for both the metric perturbation and matter field perturbation parameters.

In both Newtonian and relativistic cases, a solution of the system may be obtained by employing a weak coupling approximation (where one assumes that the gravitational field is weakly coupled to the stellar matter). In the Newtonian case, the gravitational potential perturbations
\delta\phi
are set to zero, while for the relativistic case, some or all metric perturbations
\delta g_{\mu\nu}
are negligible in comparison to the fluid parameters. This approach is known as the Cowling approximation, named after T.G. Cowling who made a corresponding study on the non-radial pulsations of Newtonian stars in 1941
Cowling1941
. The relativistic Cowling approximation was developed originally by McDermott
et al.

McDermott-1983
and modified later by Finn
Finn1988
. It thus has two basic lines of thought, the first being that all the metric perturbation parameters are neglected and the refined form where not all of them are neglected. An overview on the accuracy of the relativistic Cowling approximation for its two branches is given by Lidblom and Splinter
Lindblom-1990
. They concluded that the full neglect originally used by McDermott
et al.

McDermott-1983
is more precise than the modification for the
f
and
p
modes.

For the calculation of oscillation modes, in the relativistic Cowling approximation, we follow the formalism described by Sotani
et al.

Sotani-2011
. All metric perturbations are set to zero, and the local conservation of the energy momentum tensor perturbations is being solved. In this framework, given the spherically symmetric background metric (describing the unperturbed stellar configuration)

ds^{2}=-e^{2\Phi(r)}dt^{2}+e^{2\Lambda(r)}dr^{2}+r^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}{\theta d\phi^{2})},

(6)

the differential equations that need to be solved for the extraction of different mode frequencies are given as
Sotani-2011

W^{\prime}(r)=\frac{d\mathcal{E}}{dP}\Big(\omega^{2}r^{2}e^{\Lambda(r)-2\Phi(r)}V(r)\Big)-l(l+1)e^{\Lambda(r)}V(r)

(7)

V^{\prime}(r)=2\Phi^{\prime}(r)V(r)-e^{\Lambda(r)}\frac{W(r)}{r^{2}},

(8)

where
W(r)
and
V(r)
denote the spatial part of the matter field perturbation parameters
W(r,t)
and
V(r,t)
, for which a harmonic time dependence has been assumed.

In order to solve the Eq. (
6

7
) Strum-Luville problem, one needs an appropriate set of boundary conditions. In particular, near the center of the star

and

have the form
W(r)=Cr^{l+1}

V(r)=-Cr^{l}/l

is an arbitrary constant

[\omega^{2}r^{2}e^{\lambda-2\phi}V+\Phi^{\prime}W]_{r=R}=0.

(9)

Notably, in the presence of a density discontinuity (due to a phase transition) an additional junction condition is required for one to solve the aforementioned differential equations. More precisely, at the interface of the discontinuity

(10)

(11)

where

is the radius at which the discontinuity appears and the signs
+,-
indicate the function values at each side of the boundary between the two phases

VI
Summary and future directions

In the present study, we investigated on the possible existence of a correlation between the binding energy of compact stars and the frequencies of their non radial pulsation modes. The analysis was performed withing the relativistic Cowling approximation and it included the calculation of
f
and
p_{1}
modes. Notably, as core-collapse supernovae may be informative for both the binding energy and the oscillation frequencies, a single event could, in principle, provide important insight for the physics of compact stars.

Interestingly, we found that, for hadronic models, the
f
mode frequency is correlated to the binding energy with a respective correlation
R_{fit}^{2}
coefficient of 0.99. The case was similar for the
p_{1}
modes as well. The derived linear formulas describing the dependence were found to reproduce the exact theoretical prediction within a
10\%
error margin (for stellar configurations with masses larger than
1.2M_{\odot}
).

Another important remark is that the predictions of hybrid EOSs were found to deviate from the proposed fitted formulas, with the differences increasing for higher energy density jump values. Notably, in the case of
f
modes, deviations close to
30\%
were reported. As a consequence, the empirical formulas derived in the present work may potentially allow us to probe the nature of compact star cores. More precisely, significant deviations (of future precise astronomical measurements) from the predictions of such empirical relations could potentially be attributed to the existence of exotic degrees of freedom in the stellar interior.

Finally, it is crucial to clarify that this study is only a first step towards postulating an empirical relation between the binding energy and the frequencies of non radial oscillations. While the Cowling approximation allows us to probe the potential existence of such a nearly universal connection, it is important to perform the present computations in full General Relativity in order to avoid any errors related to the neglect of metric perturbations. Another interesting research avenue will be to consider the possible effects of finite temperature on the selected models. In any case, the present results may provide a framework for further probing of compact star physics in the era of multimessenger astronomy.

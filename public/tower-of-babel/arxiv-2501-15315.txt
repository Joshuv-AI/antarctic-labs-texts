1.
Introduction and the main results

In this paper
\alpha
is always a real number in
(0,1)
. We denote by
\Lambda^{\alpha}(E)
the Lipschitz class of (complex-valued) functions on a set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A function
f

belongs to this class if there exists a constant
C=C_{f}
such that

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq C|x-y|^{\alpha},\quad x,y\in E.

We say that two non-negative quantities (possibly with infinite values somewhere)
A(f)
and
B(f)
, defined (for example) on a class of functions
\mathcal{F}
, are equivalent if there
exist positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that
A(f)\leq C_{1}B(f)
and
B(f)\leq C_{2}A(f)
for every
f\in\mathcal{F}
.

On the class of analytic functions on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
in
\mathbb{C}
, continuous on the closed unit disk, introduce the following quantities:

\begin{split}&N_{1}(f)=\sup_{x,y\in\overline{\mathbb{D}},\,x\neq y}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}};\quad N_{2}(f)=\sup_{x,y\in\overline{\mathbb{D}},\,x\neq y}\frac{||f|(x)-|f|(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}};\\
&N_{3}(f)=\sup_{\zeta,\eta\in\partial\mathbb{D},\,\zeta\neq\eta}\frac{||f|(\zeta)-|f|(\eta)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}+\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{\mathrm{P}[|f|](z)-|f|(z)}{(1-|z|)^{\alpha}};\\
&N_{4}(f)=\sup_{\zeta,\eta\in\partial\mathbb{D},\,\zeta\neq\eta}\frac{||f|(\zeta)-|f|(\eta)|}{|\zeta-\eta|^{\alpha}}+\sup_{r\in(0,1),\,\zeta\in\partial\mathbb{D}}\frac{||f|(\zeta)-|f|(r\zeta)|}{(1-r)^{\alpha}}.\end{split}

Here
\mathrm{P}[\varphi]
denotes the Poisson integral of a continuous function
\varphi
on the unit circle.

In 1997, Dykonov
[
2
]
proved that
N_{j}
,
j=1,2,3,4
, are mutually equivalent. As a consequence, an analytic function
f
on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, continuous on

\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, belongs to the Lipschitz class
\Lambda^{\alpha}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})
if and only if its modulus
|f|
belongs to
\Lambda^{\alpha}(\partial\mathbb{D})
and
satisfies a Lipschitz-type condition along every radius of the unit disk. It also follows an interesting result that the function
f
belongs to
\Lambda^{\alpha}(\overline{\mathbb{D}})
if
and only if its modulus belongs to the same class. These results have various extensions. We refer to
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
for some of them.
Pavlović
[
10
]
gave a new proof of the Dyakonov theorem.

In
[
11
]
Pavlović considered Lipschitz conditions on a real-valued harmonic function on the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. He showed that a such one
function belongs to
\Lambda^{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
if and only if its modulus satisfies a Lipschitz-type condition over every radius of the unit ball. This result is formulated in the
following proposition.

Proposition 1.1

(Pavlović)
.

For a real-valued harmonic function
U
on
\mathbb{B}^{n}
, continuous on the closed unit ball, the following three conditions are equivalent:

\begin{split}&\text{(B1) $\exists C_{1}$ such that }|U(x)-U(y)|\leq C_{1}|x-y|^{\alpha},x,y\in\mathbb{B}^{n};\\
&\text{(B2) $\exists C_{2}$ such that }||U|(\zeta)-|U|(\eta)|\leq C_{2}|\zeta-\eta|^{\alpha},\zeta,\eta\in\partial\mathbb{B}^{n};\\
&\text{(B3) $\exists C_{3}$ such that }||U|(\zeta)-|U|(r\zeta)|\leq C_{3}(1-r)^{\alpha},r\in(0,1),\zeta\in\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}.\end{split}

It is natural to ask what can be achieved for harmonic function on more general domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The case of bounded domains with smooth boundaries is considered by Ravisankar

[
13
,
14
]
. In these works the author considers the Lipschitz-type behaviour of a harmonic function along curves that satisfy the transversal condition with
respect to the boundary of the domain.

One of our aims in this article is to find an analog of the Pavlović theorem for harmonic functions on the upper half-space in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This is the content of the next theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

For a bounded real-valued harmonic functions
U
on the upper half-space
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, continuous on the closure of the domain, the following three statements are equivalent:

\begin{split}&\text{(H1) $\exists C_{1}$ such that }|U(x)-U(y)|\leq C_{1}|x-y|^{\alpha},x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+};\\
&\text{(H2) $\exists C_{2}$ such that }||U|(x^{\prime},0)-|U|(y^{\prime},0)|\leq C_{2}|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{\alpha},x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1};\\
&\text{(H3) $\exists C_{3}$ such that }||U|(x^{\prime},x_{n})-|U|(x^{\prime},0)|\leq C_{3}x_{n}^{\alpha},x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},x_{n}\in(0,\infty).\end{split}

Beside the above theorem, our main results are collected in the next three theorems.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
U
be a real-valued bounded harmonic function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, continuous on
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}
, and assume that the function
U(t,0)
,
t\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
satisfies

|U(x^{\prime},0)-U(y^{\prime},0)|\leq C|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{\alpha},\quad x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},

where
C
is a constant. Then we have

\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}U(x^{\prime},x_{n})\right|\leq\frac{4(n-1)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{1-\alpha}Cx_{n}^{\alpha-1},

and

\left|\nabla U(x^{\prime},x_{n})\right|\leq\frac{16(n+1)}{(1-\alpha)^{2}}Cx_{n}^{\alpha-1},\quad(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}.

The function
U
belongs to the Lipschitz class
\Lambda^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
, and

|U(x)-U(y)|\leq\frac{64(n+1)}{\alpha(1-\alpha)^{2}}C|x-y|^{\alpha},\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
U
be a bounded real-valued harmonic function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, continuous on the closure of this domain, and let it satisfies the following Lipschitz-type condition: There exists a
constant
C
such that

|U(x^{\prime},x_{n})-U(x^{\prime},y_{n})|\leq Cy_{n}^{\alpha},\quad x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},\,0<x_{n}<y_{n}.

Then we have

\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}U(x^{\prime},x_{n})\right|\leq 13(n+2)^{\frac{1}{2}}Cx_{n}^{\alpha-1},

and

|\nabla U(x^{\prime},x_{n})|\leq\frac{52(n+2)}{1-\alpha}Cx_{n}^{\alpha-1},\quad x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}.

The function
U
belongs to the Lipschitz class
\Lambda^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
, and

|U(x)-U(y)|\leq\frac{208(n+2)}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}C|x-y|^{\alpha},\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}.

Theorem 1.5
.

On the class of vector-valued bounded harmonic functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, continuous on the closure of the domain, the following three semi-norms are equivalent:

\begin{split}&\|U\|_{1}=\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},\,x\neq y}\frac{|U(x)-U(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}};\quad\|U\|_{2}=\sup_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},x^{\prime}\neq y^{\prime}}\frac{|U(x^{\prime},0)-U(y^{\prime},0)|}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{\alpha}};\\
&\|U\|_{3}=\sup_{x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},x_{n},y_{n}\in(0,\infty),\,x_{n}\neq y_{n}}\frac{|U(x^{\prime},x_{n})-U(x^{\prime},y_{n})|}{|x_{n}-y_{n}|^{\alpha}}.\end{split}

In connection with Theorem
1.3
we refer to
[
15
, Lemma 4, p. 143]
and the text after it. We will prove here this result by carefully looking at constants. After we
estimate the partial derivative of a harmonic function in the last variable, we derive the gradient estimate using the Schwarz lemma for harmonic functions. The similar approach we use in
proving Theorem
1.4
where harmonic functions on the upper half-space satisfy the Lipschitz-type condition along half-lines orthogonal to the boundary of the domain.

The unit ball case of Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.4
(without precise constants) may also be found in the Pavlović work
[
11
]
. Although, even for
n>2
,
there is a connection via the Kelvin transform between harmonic functions on the unit ball and the upper half-space (we refer to the seventh chapter in
[
1
]
), it seems that one
cannot derive above theorems from the unit ball case.

2.
Proofs of the main theorems

Before we start proving our theorems we will collect needed auxiliary results.

The problem of finding the best pointwise gradient estimate in the class of bounded harmonic functions on the unit ball or the upper half-space in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
was known as the
Khavinson problem. The Khavinson problem in the upper half-space setting is solved by Kresin and Maz’ya
[
9
]
. Their result is given in the proposition stated below. It says
that the optimal estimate of the
n
-th partial derivative coincides with the optimal estimate of the gradient.

Proposition 2.1

(Kresin-Maz’ya)
.

If
U
is among bounded harmonic functions on the upper half-space
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, then we have the following pointwise optimal gradient estimate

\left|\nabla U(x^{\prime},x_{n})\right|,\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}U(x^{\prime},x_{n})\right|\leq M_{n}x_{n}^{-1}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|U(y)|,\quad(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},

where

\begin{split}M_{n}&=\frac{4(n-1)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{n^{\frac{n}{2}}}\frac{m_{n-1}(\mathbb{B}^{n-1})}{m_{n}(\mathbb{B}^{n})},\end{split}

and
m_{n}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
is the volume of the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
.

The Khavinson problem for the unit ball was considered by various authors. We refer to the Liu paper
[
8
]
where the problem is completely solved and the main theorem says that the
optimal estimate for the gradient is the same as the optimal estimate in the radial direction. The derivative of a function
U
in a direction
l\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
|l|=1
, is denoted by

\frac{\partial}{\partial\ell}U(x)=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{U(x+t\ell)-U(x)}{t}=\left<\nabla U(x),\ell\right>.

Proposition 2.2

(Liu)
.

If
U
is among real-valued bounded harmonic functions on the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
, then we have the following pointwise optimal gradient estimate

\left|\nabla U(x)\right|,\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial r(x)}U(x)\right|\leq N_{n}(x)(1-|x|)^{-1}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{B}^{n}}|U(y)|,\quad x\in\mathbb{B}^{n},

where
r(x)=x/|x|
, if
x\neq 0
, and arbitrary direction if
x=0
, and

\begin{split}N_{n}(x)&=\frac{m_{n-1}(\mathbb{B}^{n-1})}{m_{n}(\mathbb{B}^{n})}\frac{n-1}{|x|+1}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{|t-\frac{n-2}{n}|x||(1-t^{2})^{\frac{n}{2}-\frac{3}{2}}}{(1-2t|x|+|x|^{2})^{\frac{n}{2}-1}}dt.\end{split}

The next proposition may be found in
[
1
, Theorem 6.26]
. It is a special case of the preceding one for
x=0
.

Proposition 2.3

(the Schwarz lemma for harmonic functions)
.

Let
U
be a real-valued harmonic function on
\mathbb{B}^{n}
which satisfies
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}}|U(x)|\leq 1
. Then

|\nabla U(0)|\leq\frac{2m_{n-1}(\mathbb{B}^{n-1})}{m_{n}(\mathbb{B}^{n})}.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
U
be a real-valued bounded harmonic function on a domain
D\not=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
x\in D
. Then we have

|\nabla U(x)|,\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial\ell}U(x)\right|\leq\frac{K_{n}}{d(x,\partial D)}\sup_{y\in D}|U(y)|,\quad l\in\mathbb{R}^{n},|l|=1,

where
d(x,\partial D)
is the distance function, and

\begin{split}K_{n}&=\frac{2m_{n-1}(\mathbb{B}_{n-1})}{m_{n}(\mathbb{B}_{n})}.\end{split}

Proof.

Since

\left|\nabla U(x)\right|=\sup_{\ell\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,|\ell|=1}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial\ell}U(x)\right|,

it is enough to prove the gradient estimate.

Assume that
U
is not a constant function. Denote
M=\sup_{y\in D}|U(y)|
, and let us consider

V(z)=M^{-1}U(x+d(x,\partial D)z),\quad z\in\mathbb{B}^{n},

which is a harmonic function on the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
, and we have
\sup_{z\in\mathbb{B}^{n}}|V(z)|\leq 1
. Therefore, we may apply Proposition
2.3
on the function
V
.
Since

\nabla V(0)=M^{-1}d(x,\partial D)\nabla U(x),

and since by the proposition we have
|\nabla V(0)|\leq K_{n}
, we deduce the gradient estimate for
U
.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

In the sequel we will several times use the estimate
K_{n}\leq(n+2)^{\frac{1}{2}}
, which follows from the Gautschi inequality for the Gamma function

x^{1-s}\leq\frac{\Gamma(x+1)}{\Gamma(x+s)}\leq(x+1)^{1-s},\quad x\in(0,\infty),\,s\in(0,1).

Indeed, since
m_{n}(\mathbb{B}^{n})=\frac{\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+1)}
, we have

\begin{split}K_{n}&=\frac{2}{\pi^{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+1)}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{1}{2})}\leq\frac{2}{\pi^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left(\frac{n}{2}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq(n+2)^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

Lemma 2.6
.

If a bounded harmonic function
U
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
satisfies

\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}U(x^{\prime},t)\right|\leq Ct^{\alpha-1},\quad x=(x^{\prime},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},

then we have

|\nabla U(x^{\prime},x_{n})|\leq\frac{4(n+2)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{1-\alpha}Cx_{n}^{\alpha-1},\quad x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}.

Proof.

Applying Lemma
2.4
on the harmonic function
\frac{\partial U}{\partial x_{n}}
on the open ball
D=B((x^{\prime},t),\frac{t}{2})
(where it is certainly bounded), and having on mind the
assumed estimate of the
n
-th partial derivative, we obtain

\begin{split}\left|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\ell\partial x_{n}}U(x^{\prime},t)\right|&\leq\frac{K_{n}}{d((x^{\prime},t),\partial D)}\sup_{y=(y^{\prime},y_{n})\in D}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}U(y)\right|\\
&\leq\frac{2K_{n}}{t}Cy_{n}^{\alpha-1}=2^{2-\alpha}K_{n}Ct^{\alpha-2},\end{split}

because for
y=(y^{\prime},y_{n})\in{B}((x^{\prime},t),\frac{t}{2})
we have
y_{n}\geq\frac{t}{2}
.

After permuting the derivatives, we have

\left|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{n}\partial\ell}U(x^{\prime},t)\right|\leq 4K_{n}Ct^{\alpha-2},\quad(x^{\prime},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}.

Let
x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. Integrating over
[x_{n},s]
, we obtain the estimate of the partial derivative in arbitrary direction
\ell
,
|\ell|=1
:

\begin{split}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial{\ell}}U(x^{\prime},s)-\frac{\partial}{\partial{\ell}}U(x^{\prime},x_{n})\right|&=\left|\int_{x_{n}}^{s}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{n}\partial\ell}U(x^{\prime},t)dt\right|\\
&\leq 4K_{n}C\int_{x_{n}}^{s}t^{\alpha-2}dt\\
&=\frac{4K_{n}C}{1-\alpha}(x_{n}^{\alpha-1}-s^{\alpha-1}).\end{split}

Since the gradient of
U
at
(x^{\prime},s)
converges to zero as
s\to\infty
, which may be seen from Proposition
2.1
, if we let
s\to\infty
in the above inequality, we obtain

\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial\ell}U(x^{\prime},x_{n})\right|\leq\frac{4K_{n}C}{1-\alpha}x_{n}^{\alpha-1},\quad x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}.

Since the above estimate holds for every direction
\ell
, we derive the gradient estimate

|\nabla U(x)|=\sup_{\ell\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,|\ell|=1}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial\ell}U(x)\right|\leq\frac{4K_{n}C}{1-\alpha}x_{n}^{\alpha-1},\quad x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},

from which follows the estimate we aimed to prove, since
K_{n}\leq(n+2)^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Assume that
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
satisfies

(2.1)

|\nabla f(z^{\prime},z_{n})|\leq Cz_{n}^{\alpha-1},\quad z=(z^{\prime},z_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}.

Then
f
belongs to the Lipschitz class
\Lambda^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
, and we have

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq\frac{4}{\alpha}C|x-y|^{\alpha},\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}.

In particular
f
has continuous extension on the closed upper half-space.

Proof.

Let
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. It is possible to find an open ball
B(z,r)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
which contains
x
and
y
. Let
\gamma
be a part of a circle which is orthogonal to the
sphere
\partial B(z,r)
with endpoints at
x
and
y
. As in Gehring and Martio work
[
6
, p. 205]
, one derives the inequality

\int_{\gamma}d(z,\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})^{\alpha-1}|dz|\leq\frac{\pi}{\alpha 2^{\alpha}}|x-y|^{\alpha}

(which actually shows that the upper half-space is a
\mathrm{Lip}_{\alpha}
-extension domain; for more of this type of domains we refer to
[
7
]
).

Since the inequality (
2.1
) may be rewritten in the form

|\nabla f(z^{\prime},z_{n})|\leq Cd(z,\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})^{\alpha-1},

it follows

\begin{split}|f(x)-f(y)|&\leq\int_{\gamma}|\nabla f(z)||dz|\leq C\int_{\gamma}d(z,\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})^{\alpha-1}|dz|\leq\frac{4}{\alpha}C|x-y|^{\alpha},\end{split}

which we aimed to prove.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
U
be a real-valued bounded harmonic function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, continuous on the closed upper half-space. The theorem assumes that
U(t,0)
,
t\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
, satisfies

(2.2)

|U(x^{\prime},0)-U(y^{\prime},0)|\leq C|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{\alpha},\quad x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},

where
C
is a constant.

It is enough to prove the first estimate in this theorem concerning the
n
-th partial derivative of
U
. To obtain the gradient estimate, we have only to apply Lemma
2.6
.
Then the third estimate follows from Lemma
2.7
.

For the theory of harmonic functions on the unit ball or the upper half-space in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
we refer to
[
1
]
and
[
15
]
. However, it is well known fact that
the function
U(x^{\prime},x_{n})
may be represented as the Poisson integral of the bounded continuous function
U(t,0)
,
t\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
, i.e.,

U(x^{\prime},x_{n})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}P((x^{\prime},x_{n}),t)U(t,0)dm_{n-1}(t),\quad(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}.

where
P((x^{\prime},x_{n}),t)
is the Poisson kernel for the upper half-space, and
m_{n-1}
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. Recall that the expression for the Poisson kernel for
the upper half-space
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
is given by

\begin{split}P((x^{\prime},x_{n}),t)&=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}{\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}}\frac{x_{n}}{(|x^{\prime}-t|^{2}+x_{n}^{2})^{\frac{n}{2}}},\quad x^{\prime},t\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},\,x_{n}\in(0,\infty).\end{split}

It is straightforward to calculate

\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}P((x^{\prime},x_{n}),t)=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}{\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}}\frac{|x^{\prime}-t|^{2}-(n-1)x_{n}^{2}}{(|x^{\prime}-t|^{2}+x_{n}^{2})^{\frac{n}{2}+1}}.\end{split}

Since for every
(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}P((x^{\prime},x_{n}),t)dm_{n-1}(t)=0

(which may be checked directly, or by the differentiation a constant function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
), we obtain

\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}U(x^{\prime},x_{n})&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}P((x^{\prime},x_{n}),t)U(t,0)dm_{n-1}(t)\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}P((x^{\prime},x_{n}),t)(U(t,0)-U(x^{\prime},0))dm_{n-1}(t).\end{split}

Introducing
u=x^{\prime}-t
it follows

\begin{split}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}U(x^{\prime},x_{n})\right|&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}P((x^{\prime},x_{n}),x^{\prime}-u)\right||U(x^{\prime}-u,0)-U(x^{\prime},0)|dm_{n-1}(u).\end{split}

Having on mind that
U(t,0)
satisfies (
2.2
), we obtain

\begin{split}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}U(x^{\prime},x_{n})\right|&\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}P((x^{\prime},x_{n}),x^{\prime}-u)\right||u|^{\alpha}dm_{n-1}(u).\end{split}

Our next aim is to find a good estimate for the integral on the right side. First of all, we have

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}P((x^{\prime},x_{n}),x^{\prime}-u)\right|&|u|^{\alpha}dm_{n-1}(u)\\
&\leq\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}{\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\frac{|u|^{2}+(n-1)x_{n}^{2}}{(|u|^{2}+x_{n}^{2})^{\frac{n}{2}+1}}|u|^{\alpha}dm_{n-1}(u)\\
&=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}{\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}}x_{n}^{\alpha-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\frac{|v|^{2}+n-1}{(|v|^{2}+1)^{\frac{n}{2}+1}}|v|^{\alpha}dm_{n-1}(v),\end{split}

where
v={x_{n}}^{-1}u
. Let us denote by
J_{n}
the last integral expression along with the multiplicative factor.

Introducing the polar coordinates, it is straightforward to calculate the exact value of
J_{n}
and to obtain the estimate

J_{n}\leq 2\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{n-1}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{n-1}{2})}x_{n}^{\alpha-1}.

In case
n\geq 3
we can use the Gautschi inequality for
x=\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{n-3}{2}
and
s=1-\frac{\alpha}{2}
, and some elementary inequalities, in order to obtain

\frac{\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{n-1}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{n-1}{2})}=\frac{\Gamma(x+1)}{\Gamma(x+s)}\leq(n-1)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This estimate along with
\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\leq\frac{2}{1-\alpha}
, and some trivial ones, lead to the final estimate

J_{n}\leq\frac{4(n-1)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{1-\alpha}x_{n}^{\alpha-1}.

(The last inequality is also valid in the case
n=2
, as may be checked immediately.) It follows the estimate for the
n
-th partial derivative stated in our theorem.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let
U
be a bounded real-valued harmonic function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, continuous on the closure of this domain, and let it satisfies

(2.3)

|U(x^{\prime},x_{n})-U(x^{\prime},y_{n})|\leq Cy_{n}^{\alpha},\quad x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},\,0<x_{n}<y_{n},

where
C
is a constant.

For every
\lambda\in(0,\frac{1}{\alpha})
introduce the following harmonic function on the upper half-space

V_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},x_{n})=U(x^{\prime},x_{n}+\lambda),\quad x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},

and attach to it the number

(2.4)

A_{\lambda}=\sup_{(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}x_{n}^{1-\alpha}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}V_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},x_{n})\right|.

We will firstly show that
A_{\lambda}
is finite for every
\lambda\in(0,1)
. Then we will proceed to obtain an upper bound for
A_{\lambda}
,
\lambda>0
, which does not depend on
\lambda
.

Due to the assumption that
U
is bounded, we may apply the Kresin-Maz’ya estimate given in Proposition
2.1
. It follows

\begin{split}x_{n}^{1-\alpha}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}V_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},x_{n})\right|&={x_{n}^{1-\alpha}}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}U(x^{\prime},x_{n}+\lambda)\right|\\
&\leq\frac{x_{n}^{1-\alpha}}{x_{n}+\lambda}M_{n}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|U(y)|\\
&\leq\frac{M_{n}}{\lambda^{\alpha}}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|U(y)|\end{split}

for every
(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, since the maximum of the function
x\to\frac{x^{1-\alpha}}{x+\lambda}
,
x\in(0,\infty)
, achieves at
x=\frac{1}{\alpha}-\lambda
. From the
last displayed expression we conclude that
A_{\lambda}
is finite.

Applying the Taylor expansion on one variable function
s\to V_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},s)
, where
x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is fixed, around
x_{n}\in(0,\infty)
, we obtain that for
s\in(0,x_{n})

there exists
t\in(s,x_{n})
such that

\begin{split}V_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},s)&=V_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},x_{n})+\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}V_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},x_{n})(s-x_{n})\\
&+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{n}^{2}}V_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},t)\frac{(s-x_{n})^{2}}{2}.\end{split}

In the above expansion let us take
s=(1-\gamma)x_{n}
, where
\gamma\in(0,1)
will be chosen letter. This equation takes the form

\begin{split}V_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},x_{n}-\gamma x_{n})&=V_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},x_{n})-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}V_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},x_{n})\gamma x_{n}\\
&+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{n}^{2}}V_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},t)\frac{\gamma^{2}x_{n}^{2}}{2}.\end{split}

From the last equality we have

\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}V_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},x_{n})\gamma x_{n}&=V_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},x_{n})-V_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},x_{n}-\gamma x_{n})\\
&+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{n}^{2}}V_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},t)\frac{\gamma^{2}x_{n}^{2}}{2}.\end{split}

Taking the absolute values on both sides above, then using the triangle inequality, we derive

\begin{split}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}V_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},x_{n})\right|\gamma x_{n}&\leq|V_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},x_{n})-V_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},x_{n}-\gamma x_{n})|\\
&+\left|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{n}^{2}}V_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},t)\right|\frac{\gamma^{2}x_{n}^{2}}{2}.\end{split}

Applying now the assumed inequality (
2.3
) we obtain

\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}V_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},x_{n})\right|\gamma x_{n}\leq Cx_{n}^{\alpha}+\sup_{y\in{B}(x,\gamma x_{n})}\left|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{n}^{2}}V_{\lambda}(y)\right|\frac{\gamma^{2}x_{n}^{2}}{2},

since
t\in(x_{n}-\gamma x_{n},x_{n})
.

By Lemma
2.4
we have the estimate

\left|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{n}^{2}}V_{\lambda}(y)\right|\leq\frac{K_{n}}{\frac{1-\gamma}{2}x_{n}}\sup_{z\in{B}(x,\frac{1+\gamma}{2}x_{n})}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}V_{\lambda}(z)\right|,

where we have applied the lemma for the harmonic function
\frac{\partial V_{\lambda}}{\partial x_{n}}
on the domain
D={B}(x,\frac{1+\gamma}{2}x_{n})
; notice that for
y\in{B}(x,\gamma x_{n})

we have
d(y,\partial D)\geq\frac{1-\gamma}{2}x_{n}
.

We have arrived to the following inequality

\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}V_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},x_{n})\right|\gamma x_{n}\leq Cx_{n}^{\alpha}+\frac{K_{n}}{\frac{1-\gamma}{2}x_{n}}\sup_{z\in{B}(x,\frac{1+\gamma}{2}x_{n})}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}V_{\lambda}(z)\right|\frac{\gamma^{2}x_{n}^{2}}{2},

which after dividing by
\gamma x_{n}
transforms to

\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}V_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},x_{n})\right|\leq\frac{C}{\gamma}x_{n}^{\alpha-1}+\gamma\frac{K_{n}}{{1-\gamma}}\sup_{z\in{B}(x,\frac{1+\gamma}{2}x_{n})}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}V_{\lambda}(z)\right|.

We will now estimate the second therm above via
A_{\lambda}
. Note that for
z=(z^{\prime},z_{n})\in{B}(x,\frac{1+\gamma}{2}x_{n})
we have
z_{n}\geq x_{n}-\frac{1+\gamma}{2}x_{n}=\frac{1-\gamma}{2}x_{n}
.
Having on mind the relation (
2.4
), we obtain

\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}V_{\lambda}(z^{\prime},z_{n})\right|\leq A_{\lambda}z_{n}^{\alpha-1}\leq A_{\lambda}\left(\frac{1-\gamma}{2}x_{n}\right)^{\alpha-1}.

Therefore,

\sup_{z\in{B}(x,\frac{1+\gamma}{2}x_{n})}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}V_{\lambda}(z)\right|\leq A_{\lambda}\left(\frac{1-\gamma}{2}\right)^{-1}x_{n}^{\alpha-1}.

Going back to the main inequality, and dividing it by
x_{n}^{\alpha-1}
, we arrive to the following one

x_{n}^{1-\alpha}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}V_{\lambda}(x^{\prime},x_{n})\right|\leq\frac{C}{\gamma}+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}K_{n}A_{\lambda}\left(\frac{1-\gamma}{2}\right)^{-1}.

If we take supremum on the left side with respect to
x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, we obtain

A_{\lambda}\leq\frac{C}{\gamma}+\frac{2\gamma K_{n}}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}A_{\lambda}.

Let us take for
\gamma\in(0,1)
the number such that

\frac{2\gamma K_{n}}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}.

It ie easy to check that the unique solution to this equation in
(0,1)
is given by

\gamma=K_{n}((1+{K_{n}}^{-1})^{\frac{1}{2}}-1)^{2}.

From the last inequality concerning
A_{\lambda}
it follows

A_{\lambda}\leq\frac{2C}{\gamma}\Leftrightarrow A_{\lambda}\leq\frac{2C}{K_{n}((1+{K_{n}}^{-1})^{\frac{1}{2}}-1)^{2}}.

Using the elementary inequality

\frac{2}{x((1+x^{-1})^{\frac{1}{2}}-1)^{2}}\leq 13x,\quad x>1,

and then
K_{n}\leq(n+2)^{\frac{1}{2}}
, we obtain

A_{\lambda}\leq 13(n+2)^{\frac{1}{2}}C,

where the right side does not depend on
\lambda
.

We have just proved the inequality

\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}U(x^{\prime},x_{n}+\lambda)\right|\leq 13(n+2)^{\frac{1}{2}}Cx_{n}^{\alpha-1},\quad x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}

for every
\lambda\in(0,1)
. If we finally left
\lambda\to 0
above, we obtain the estimate of the
n
-th partial derivative for
U
which we aimed to prove.

It remains to apply Lemma
2.6
and Lemma
2.7
in order to obtain the other two estimates in the theorem.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Let us introduce one more semi-norm

\|U\|_{4}=\sup_{x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},\,0<x_{n}<y_{n}}\frac{|U(x^{\prime},x_{n})-U(x^{\prime},y_{n})|}{y_{n}^{\alpha}}.

It is enough to show this theorem in the case of real-valued bounded harmonic functions. Let
U
be a such one. We have
\|U\|_{2}\leq\|U\|_{1}
, and by Theorem
1.3
there exists a
constant
C_{2}
such that
\|U\|_{1}\leq C_{2}\|U\|_{2}
. Applying Theorem
1.4
we obtain a constant
C_{4}
such that
\|U\|_{1}\leq C_{4}\|U\|_{4}
. Since
\|U\|_{4}\leq\|U\|_{3}\leq\|U\|_{1}
,
it follows the statement of this theorem.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

This theorem is a consequence of Theorem
1.3
, Theorem
1.4
, and some elementary consideration. We follow the approach as in

[
4
,
5
,
11
]
.

It enough to prove that the condition (H2) implies (H1), and (H3) implies (H1).

Note that the condition (H2) implies the following one

|U(x^{\prime},0)-U(y^{\prime},0)|\leq 2C_{2}|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{\alpha},\quad x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}.

Indeed, if
U(x^{\prime},0)U(y^{\prime},0)\geq 0
, then we have

|U(x^{\prime},0)-U(y^{\prime},0)|=||U(x^{\prime},0)|-|U(y^{\prime},0)||\leq C_{2}|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{\alpha}.

On the other hand, if
U(x^{\prime},0)U(y^{\prime},0)<0
, because the function
U(x^{\prime},0)
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
, one can find
z^{\prime}\in[x^{\prime},y^{\prime}]
(
[x^{\prime},y^{\prime}]\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
is a
segment with endpoints at
x^{\prime}
and
y^{\prime}
) such that
U(z^{\prime},0)=0
. Having on mind the preceding case, we obtain

\begin{split}|U(x^{\prime},0)-U(y^{\prime},0)|&\leq|U(x^{\prime},0)-U(z^{\prime},0)|+|U(z^{\prime},0)-U(y^{\prime},0)|\\
&\leq C_{2}|x^{\prime}-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}+C_{2}|z^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{\alpha}\leq 2C_{2}|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{\alpha}.\end{split}

Therefore,
U(x^{\prime},,0)\in\Lambda^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
, so it remains to apply Theorem
1.3
.

Assume now that the condition (H3) holds. We will derive the following inequality

|U(x^{\prime},x_{n})-U(x^{\prime},y_{n})|\leq 4C_{3}y_{n}^{\alpha},\quad x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},0<x_{n}<y_{n}.

As before, we will separate the proof in two cases.

If
U(x^{\prime},x_{n})U(x^{\prime},y_{n})\geq 0
, then we have

\begin{split}|U(x^{\prime},x_{n})-U(x^{\prime},y_{n})|&=||U(x^{\prime},x_{n})|-|U(x^{\prime},y_{n})||\\
&\leq||U(x^{\prime},x_{n})|-|U(x^{\prime},0)||+||U(x^{\prime},0)|-|U(x^{\prime},y_{n})||\\
&\leq C_{3}x_{n}^{\alpha}+C_{3}y_{n}^{\alpha}\leq 2C_{3}y_{n}^{\alpha}.\end{split}

If
U(x^{\prime},x_{n})U(x^{\prime},y_{n})<0
, then because of the continuity of
U(x^{\prime},t)
,
t\geq 0
, there exists
z_{n}\in(x_{n},y_{n})
such that
U(x^{\prime},z_{n})=0
. Applying the first case, we obtain

\begin{split}|U(x^{\prime},x_{n})-U(x^{\prime},y_{n})|&\leq|U(x^{\prime},x_{n})-U(x^{\prime},z_{n})|+|U(x^{\prime},z_{n})-U(x^{\prime},y_{n})|\leq 4C_{3}y^{\alpha}_{n}.\end{split}

By Theorem
1.4
we conclude that (H3) implies (H1).
∎

The Generalized Chernoff–Stein Lemma, Applications and Examples
Ibrahim Abou Faycal, Jihad Fahs, and Ibrahim Issa

Abstract




I
Introduction

In this manuscript we define the notion of ”
\delta
-typicality” for both entropy and relative entropy, as well as a notion of
\epsilon
-goodness and provide an extension to Stein’s lemma for continuous quantities as well as correlated setups. We apply the derived results on the Gaussian hypothesis testing problem where the observations are possibly correlated.

II
\delta
-Typical Sets

We start by extending the classical framework to dependent random
variables.

Denote by
p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\cdot)
a –family of– probability distribution on

\mathcal{X}^{n}
. We use the label
p
to refer to
p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\cdot)
for the
dimension
n
at hand. In simple terms, one may think of
p
as a
label for the family of laws indexed by
n

p:\left\{p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\cdot)\right\}_{n}.

Depending on whether the laws are discrete or absolutely continuous,
we also define

\left.\begin{array}[]{c}h^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\;\hat{=}\;h\!\left(p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right)\\
H^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\;\hat{=}\;H\!\left(p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right)\end{array}\right\}=-\textsf{E}_{p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}\left[{\log p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\mathbf{X})}\right].

Example

(IID)
.

The example corresponding to an IID scenario is given by

p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\mathbf{x})=\prod_{k}p^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}(x_{k}),

and in that scenario,

h^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}=\,nh\!\left(p^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}\right)=nh^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}\quad\text{or}\quad H^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}=\!nH^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt},

which is obtained through simple manipulations.

For simplicity, we will use the differential entropy notation in what
follows albeit the statements will hold almost verbatim for the
discrete/entropy case. Whenever the statements need to be tailored to
the discrete case, we will explicitely do so.

Denote by
\delta
a family
\left\{\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right\}_{n}
of positive
scalars that vary with
n
at a rate to be specified later.

Definition 1
.

We define the
\delta
-typical
set
\mathcal{A}^{[n]}(p,\delta)

\mathcal{A}^{[n]}(p,\delta)=\\
\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}^{n}\!:h^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}-\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\leq-\log p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\mathbf{x})\leq h^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}+\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right\}.

Naturally, since we are interested in setups where
n
is varying and
increasing to infinity, one can talk about the family of

\delta
-typical sets.

Example

(IID)
.

In the IID scenario,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{[n]}(p,\delta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hskip-1.0pt\left\{\hskip-1.0pt\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}^{n}\!:nh^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}-\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\hskip-1.0pt\leq-\hskip-1.0pt\sum_{k}\log p^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}(x_{k})\leq nh^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}\hskip-0.5pt+\hskip-0.5pt\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hskip-1.0pt\left\{\hskip-1.0pt\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}^{n}\!:h^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}-\frac{\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}{n}\hskip-1.0pt\leq-\frac{1}{n}\hskip-1.0pt\sum_{k}\log p^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}(x_{k})\leq h^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}\hskip-0.5pt+\hskip-0.5pt\frac{\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}{n}\hskip-1.0pt\right\}

which is the
typical set
defined and used by
Cover
[
1
]
with
\epsilon=\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}/n
.

Let
\epsilon
be a family
\left\{\epsilon^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right\}_{n}
of positive
scalars that possibly vary with
n
. Mostly, the family
\epsilon

will be non-increasing with
n
at a rate of interest to be considered
later.

Definition 2
.

For a given family
\epsilon
,
\delta
is said to be

\epsilon
-good
if

p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\!\left(\mathcal{A}^{[n]}(p,\delta)\right)\geq 1-\epsilon^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt},

(1)

for
n
large enough.

In layman terms, whenever
\delta
is

\epsilon
-good the probability –under
p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}
– of the
\delta
-typical set is within
\epsilon^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}
to one. Note that by taking large (see maximal) and small (see minimal) values, it is clear that
\epsilon
-good families always exist for any

\epsilon
. However, ones with tighter bounds would be naturally
preferred. We consider hereafter
\epsilon
-good
\delta
families and
attempt to find as tight bounds as possible.

Example 1

(IID)
.

In that scenario, since
\{X_{k}\}_{k}
’s are IID according to

p^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}
, by the Weak Law of Large Numbers (WLLN),

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\log p^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}(X_{k})\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{p}h^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}.

Therefore, for the
\epsilon
-family
\epsilon^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}=\epsilon^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}
, for
any
\xi>0
, the family

\left\{\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}=n\,\delta^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}=n\xi\right\}
is

\epsilon
-good.

One can identify possibly tighter good families: Denote by
\{Y_{k}\}

the random variables
Y_{k}=-\log p^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}(X_{k})
which are IID
whenever the
\{X_{k}\}
’s are. Assuming that the mean and variance of

Y_{k}
exist, by the Central Limit Theorem (CLT)

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Bigl(Y_{k}-h^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}\Bigr)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{d}\mathcal{N}\left({0},{\sigma^{2}}\right),

where
\sigma^{2}=\mathsf{Var}\left[{-\log p^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}(X_{1})}\right]
. Therefore, since

\displaystyle\,p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\left(\mathcal{A}^{[n]}(p,\delta)\right)

\displaystyle\,=\,p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\left(-\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Bigl(Y_{k}-h^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}\Bigr)\leq\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right)

\displaystyle\,=\,p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\left(-\frac{\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Bigl(Y_{k}-h^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}\Bigr)\leq\frac{\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)

\displaystyle\,=\,1-2\,Q\!\left({\frac{\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}{\sigma\sqrt{n}}}\!\right)\!

and
\delta
is
\epsilon
-good for families such that

\epsilon^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\geq 2\,Q\!\left({\frac{\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}{\sigma\sqrt{n}}\,}\!\right)\!\qquad\text{for }n\text{ large enough}.

Note that whenever the inequality is not asymptotically satisfied,

\delta
will not be
\epsilon
-good.

Since
Q^{-1}\!\left({\cdot\,}\!\right)\!
is decreasing, for any given
\epsilon
the
family
\delta
is
\epsilon
-good if and only if

\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\geq\sigma\sqrt{n}\,Q^{-1}\!\left({\frac{\epsilon^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}{2}\,}\!\right)\!\qquad\text{for }n\text{ large enough}.

(2)

Through its definition, one can readily derive the following
properties of
\delta
-typical sets. Note that in the discrete case
the ”volume” of the
\delta
-typical set refers to its cardinality.

Property 1
.

The set
\mathcal{A}^{[n]}(p,\delta)
satisfies the following properties:

(a)

For
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{A}^{[n]}(p,\delta)
,

\displaystyle 2^{-\left(h^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}+\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\mathbf{x})\leq 2^{-\left(h^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}-\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right)}.

(b)

\mathsf{vol}\!\left(\mathcal{A}^{[n]}(p,\delta)\right)\leq 2^{\left(h^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}+\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right)}
.

(c)

For an
\epsilon
-good family
\delta
, whenever
n
is large enough,

\left(1-\epsilon^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right)\,2^{\left(h^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}-\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right)}\leq\mathsf{vol}\!\left(\mathcal{A}^{[n]}(p,\delta)\right).

These properties parallel the ”classical” ones (found in Cover
[
1
]
for example) where the interpretation is that of ”equipartition” on the typical set.

In the following lemma we consider another ”set” that has a probability within

\varepsilon^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}
to one under
p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}
. We show that its volume is ”roughly” the
same.

Lemma 1
.

Let
B^{[n]}\subset\mathcal{X}^{n}
be any subset of sequences

\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}^{n}
such that

p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\!\left(B^{[n]}\right)\geq\left(1-\varepsilon^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right)
when
n
is
large enough, and where
\varepsilon^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}
is a family of positive scalars less than one. For an
\epsilon
-good
\delta
,

\mathsf{vol}\!\left(B^{[n]}\right)\geq\left(1-\varepsilon^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}-\epsilon^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right)\,2^{\left(h^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}-\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right)},

whenever
n
is large enough.

Proof.

Consider
n
to be large enough for the inequalities assumed in the
statement and in Property
1
to hold.

Since
p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\!\left(B^{[n]}\right)\geq\left(1-\varepsilon^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right)
and

p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\!\left(A^{[n]}\right)\geq\left(1-\epsilon^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right)
, by
the union bound

p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\!\left(A^{[n]}\cap B^{[n]}\right)\geq\left(1-\epsilon^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}-\varepsilon^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\!\left(A^{[n]}\cap B^{[n]}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{x}\in A^{[n]}\cap B^{[n]}}p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\mathbf{x})\,d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbf{x}\in A^{[n]}\cap B^{[n]}}2^{-\left(h^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}-\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right)}\,d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=\,2^{-\left(h^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}-\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right)}\,\mathsf{vol}\!\left(A^{[n]}\cap B^{[n]}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\,2^{-\left(h^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}-\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right)}\,\mathsf{vol}\!\left(B^{[n]}\right)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad\mathsf{vol}\!\left(B^{[n]}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\epsilon^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}-\varepsilon^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right)\,2^{\left(h^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}-\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right)}.

Naturally, the derivations hold for the discrete case with
summations replacing integrals and cardinality replacing volume.
∎

II-A

The Dependent Gaussian Case

We determine the
\delta
-typical set and some
\epsilon
-good

\delta
families for the
dependent
Gaussian case

\mathbf{X}\sim\mathcal{N}\left({{\bm{0}}},{\Lambda}\right)
.

Using the expression of the Probability Density Function (PDF) and
evaluating in nats,

\displaystyle p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(2\pi)^{n}\text{det}\left(\Lambda\right)}}\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{x}^{\text{t}}\Lambda^{-1}\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle h(\mathbf{X})

\displaystyle=\ln\sqrt{\left(2\pi e\right)^{n}\text{det}\left(\Lambda\right)}

\displaystyle=\ln\sqrt{\left(2\pi\right)^{n}\text{det}\left(\Lambda\right)}+\frac{n}{2}

\displaystyle-\ln p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\mathbf{x})-h(\mathbf{X})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\,\mathbf{x}^{\text{t}}\Lambda^{-1}\mathbf{x}-\frac{n}{2}

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{[n]}(p,\delta)

\displaystyle=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}^{n}\!:\left|\mathbf{x}^{\text{t}}\Lambda^{-1}\mathbf{x}-n\right|\leq 2\,\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right\},

which is the set of vectors the norm of which is within
2\,\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}

of
n
, where by norm we mean the one induced by the positive definite
matrix
\Lambda^{-1}
.

Examining the probability of the
\delta
-typical set,

\displaystyle p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\left(\mathcal{A}^{[n]}(p,\delta)\right)

\displaystyle=p_{\mathbf{X}}\!\left(n-2\,\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\leq\mathbf{X}^{\text{t}}\Lambda^{-1}\mathbf{X}\leq n+2\,\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right)

\displaystyle=p_{\mathbf{Y}}\!\left(n-2\,\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\leq\|\mathbf{Y}\|^{2}\leq n+2\,\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right),

where
\mathbf{Y}=\Lambda^{-1/2}\mathbf{X}\sim\mathcal{N}\left({{\bm{0}}},{\textsf{I}}\right)
and
where
\Lambda^{-1/2}
is defined as follows: Diagonalize
\Lambda
using orthogonal matrix
U
and denote the diagonal matrix by
\Delta
. Denoting by
\Delta^{1/2}
the diagonal matrix of the square-roots of the eigenvalues and by
\Delta^{-1/2}
its inverse,

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\Lambda=\textsf{U}^{t}\Delta\textsf{U}&\Rightarrow&\Lambda^{-1}\!=\textsf{U}^{t}\Delta^{-1}\textsf{U}\\
\Lambda^{1/2}=\textsf{U}^{t}\Delta^{1/2}\textsf{U}&\Rightarrow&\Lambda^{-1/2}=\textsf{U}^{t}\Delta^{-1/2}\textsf{U}.\end{array}

(3)

The weak law of large numbers establishes that for any
\xi>0
, the
family
\left\{\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}=n\,\delta^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}=n\,\xi\right\}
is

\epsilon
-good when the
\epsilon^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}
’s are constant.

One can determine tighter
\epsilon
-good families by using the CLT as
in Example
2
:

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Bigl(Y_{k}^{2}-1\Bigr)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{d}\mathcal{N}\left({0},{2}\right),

and since

\displaystyle p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\left(\mathcal{A}^{[n]}(p,\delta)\right)

\displaystyle=p_{\mathbf{Y}}\!\left(-2\,\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\leq\sum\left(Y_{k}^{2}-1\right)\leq 2\,\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right),

the following property is readily established.

Property 2
.

The family
\delta
is
\epsilon
-good if and only if

\epsilon^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\geq 2\,Q\!\left({\frac{\sqrt{2}\,\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}{\sqrt{n}}\,}\!\right)\!\qquad\text{for }n\text{ large enough}.

Equivalently: given
\epsilon
, the family
\delta
is

\epsilon
-good if and only if

\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\geq\sqrt{\frac{n}{2}}\,Q^{-1}\!\left({\frac{\epsilon^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}{2}\,}\!\right)\!\qquad\text{for }n\text{ large enough}.

Based on the above, one can infer the following:

Property 3
.

If one desires to consider
\delta
families that are

\epsilon
-good for all constants
\epsilon^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}
, then

\frac{\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}{\sqrt{n}}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\infty

is
\epsilon
-good for all constants. Conversly, any family where

\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}/\sqrt{n}
does not grow to infinity is not
\epsilon
-good
for all constants
\epsilon^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}
.

III
Relative Entropy
\delta
-Typical Sets

Denote by
p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\cdot)
and
q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\cdot)
two –families of–
probability distributions on
\mathcal{X}^{n}
and let
p
and
q
be labels for the
two respective families of laws indexed by
n
,

p:\left\{p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\cdot)\right\}_{n},\qquad q:\left\{q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\cdot)\right\}_{n}.

We also define

\textsf{D}^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\;\hat{=}\;D\!\left({p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}\|{q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}\right)=\textsf{E}_{p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}\left[{\log\frac{p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\mathbf{X})}{q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\mathbf{X})}}\right].

We consider both scenarios where, the laws
\left\{p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\cdot)\right\}_{n}

and
\left\{q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\cdot)\right\}_{n}
are

(i)

discrete, in which case

\textsf{D}^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}=\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}^{n}}p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\mathbf{x})\log\frac{p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\mathbf{x})}{q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\mathbf{x})}.

(ii)

absolutely continuous, in which case

\textsf{D}^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}=\int_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}^{n}}p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\mathbf{x})\log\frac{p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\mathbf{x})}{q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\mathbf{x})}\,d\mathbf{x}.

Example

(IID)
.

The example corresponding to an Independent and Identically
Distributed (IID) scenario is given by

p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\mathbf{x})=\prod_{k}p^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}(x_{k}),\qquad q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\mathbf{x})=\prod_{k}q^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}(x_{k}).

In that scenario,

\textsf{D}^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}=\,nD\!\left({p^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}}\|{q^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}}\right)=nD^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt},

which is obtained through simple manipulations

\displaystyle\textsf{D}^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}=

\displaystyle\,\textsf{E}_{p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}\left[{\log\frac{p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\mathbf{X})}{q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\mathbf{X})}}\right]=\textsf{E}_{p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}\left[{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\log\frac{p^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}(X_{k})}{q^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}(X_{k})}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{k=1}^{n}\textsf{E}_{p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}\left[{\log\frac{p^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}(X_{k})}{q^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}(X_{k})}}\right]=nD\!\left({p^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}}\|{q^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}}\right).

Denote by
\delta
a family
\left\{\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right\}_{n}
of positive
scalars that vary with
n
at a rate to be specified later.

Definition 3
.

We define the relative-entropy
\delta
-typical
set
\mathcal{A}^{[n]}(p,q,\delta)
to be

\mathcal{A}^{[n]}(p,q,\delta)=\\
\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}^{n}\!:\textsf{D}^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}-\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\leq\log\frac{p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\mathbf{x})}{q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\mathbf{x})}\leq\textsf{D}^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}+\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right\}.

Naturally, since we are interested in setups where
n
is varying and increasing to infinity, one can talk about the family of
\delta
-typical sets.

Example

(IID)
.

In the IID scenario,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{[n]}(p,q,\delta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}^{n}\!:nD^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}-\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\leq\sum_{k}\log\frac{p^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}(x_{k})}{q^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}(x_{k})}\leq nD^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}+\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}^{n}\!:D^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}-\frac{\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}{n}\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k}\log\frac{p^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}(x_{k})}{q^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}(x_{k})}\leq D^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}+\frac{\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}{n}\right\}

which is the
relative entropy typical set
defined and used by Cover
[
1
]
with
\epsilon=\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}/n
.

Definition 4
.

For a given family
\epsilon
,
\delta
is said to be

\epsilon
-good
if

p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\!\left(\mathcal{A}^{[n]}(p,q,\delta)\right)\geq 1-\epsilon^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt},

(4)

for
n
large enough.

Said differently,
\delta
is
\epsilon
-good if the probability
–under
p
– of the
\delta
-typical is within
\epsilon
from one as

n
goes to infinity.

Example

(IID)
.

Consider the case where
\{X_{k}\}_{k}
’s are IID according to

p^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}
, and
q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\mathbf{x})=\prod q^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}(x_{k})
. By the WLLN,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\log\frac{p^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}(X_{k})}{q^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}(X_{k})}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{p}D^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt},

and hence, for any
\xi>0
, the family

\left\{\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}=n\,\delta^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}=n\xi\right\}
is

\epsilon
-good for all
\epsilon
families that are constant.

To determine tighter good families as in Example
2
, denote by
\{Z_{k}\}
the random variables
Z_{k}=\log\left(p^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}(X_{k})/q^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}(X_{k})\right)
which are IID. Note that the mean –under
p^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}
– of
Z_{k}
’s is the relative entropy
D\!\left({p^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}}\|{q^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}}\right)
. Assuming that this mean exists and that additionally
\sigma^{2}
the variance of
Z_{k}
exists, by the CLT,

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Bigl(Z_{k}-D^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}\Bigr)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{d}\mathcal{N}\left({0},{\sigma^{2}}\right).

Therefore, since

\displaystyle p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\left(\mathcal{A}^{[n]}(p,q,\delta)\right)

\displaystyle\,=\,p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\left(-\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Bigl(Z_{k}-D^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}\Bigr)\leq\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right)

\displaystyle\,=\,p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\left(-\frac{\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}{\sqrt{n}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Bigl(Z_{k}-D^{[1]\hskip-0.5pt}\Bigr)\leq\frac{\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)

\displaystyle\,=\,1-2\,Q\!\left({\frac{\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}{\sigma\sqrt{n}}}\!\right)\!

and
\delta
is
\epsilon
-good for families such that

\epsilon^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\geq 2\,Q\!\left({\frac{\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}{\sigma\sqrt{n}}\,}\!\right)\!\qquad\text{for }n\text{ large enough}.

Note that whenever the inequality is not asymptotically satisfied,

\delta
will not be
\epsilon
-good.

Since
Q^{-1}\!\left({\cdot\,}\!\right)\!
is decreasing, for a given
\epsilon
the
family
\delta
is
\epsilon
-good if and only if

\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\geq\sigma\sqrt{n}\,Q^{-1}\!\left({\frac{\epsilon^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}{2}\,}\!\right)\!\qquad\text{for }n\text{ large enough}.

Through its definition, one can readily derive the following
properties of
\delta
-typical sets.

Property 4
.

The set
\mathcal{A}^{[n]}(p,q,\delta)
satisfies the following properties:

(a)

For
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{A}^{[n]}(p,q,\delta)
,

\displaystyle p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\mathbf{x})\,2^{-\left(\textsf{D}^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}+\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\mathbf{x})\leq p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\mathbf{x})\,2^{-\left(\textsf{D}^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}-\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right)}.

q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\!\left(\mathcal{A}^{[n]}(p,q,\delta)\right)\leq 2^{-\left(\textsf{D}^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}-\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right)}
.

Lemma 2
.

Let

be any subset of sequences

Proof.

Consider
n
to be large enough for the inequalities assumed in the

Since

and

p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\!\left(A^{[n]}\right)\geq\left(1-\epsilon^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right)

Therefore,

\displaystyle q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\!\left(B^{[n]}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\,q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\!\left(A^{[n]}\cap B^{[n]}\right)=\!\!\int_{\mathbf{x}\in A^{[n]}\cap B^{[n]}}q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\mathbf{x})\,d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=\,2^{-\left(\textsf{D}^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}+\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right)}p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\!\left(A^{[n]}\cap B^{[n]}\right)

IV
Generalized Chernoff–Stein Lemma

In this section we state and prove an equivalent result to Stein’s
Lemma for the continuous case. The stated result is also an extension
of Stein’s Lemma to setups where variables are also possibly

Lemma 3
.

Consider the binary hypothesis test:
X_{1},X_{2},\cdots,X_{n}

Proof.

The proof follows the same path as in the discrete and independent

[
1
]

\tau
-good,

which means that the device is feasible. Additionally,

implies,

\displaystyle\beta^{[n]}

\displaystyle=q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\!\left(\mathcal{A}^{[n]}(p,q,\gamma)\right)\leq 2^{-\left(\textsf{D}^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}-\gamma^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right)}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad-\log\beta^{[n]}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\textsf{D}^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}-\gamma^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right).

An optimal device will outperform the above device and hence:

-\log\beta^{[n]}_{\tau}\geq\left(\textsf{D}^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}-\gamma^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right).

(6)

On the other hand,
for any device

\displaystyle\log\beta^{[n]}

\displaystyle\geq\log\left(1-\epsilon^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}-\tau\right)-\left(\textsf{D}^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}+\delta^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right)

(7)

∎

V
Testing the Correlated Gaussian Hypotheses

We apply the results derived in
IV
to the problem of binary hypothesis testing between two possibly correlated Gaussian laws. More specifically, we consider two discrete-time zero-mean (wide-sense) stationary Gaussian processes with absolutely summable auto-covariance functions and spectra denoted as:

\displaystyle H_{0}:\,\,

\displaystyle K[n]=K_{p}[n]\quad\leftrightarrow\quad S_{p}\!\left(e^{j2\pi f}\right)

\displaystyle H_{1}:\,\,

\displaystyle K[n]=K_{q}[n]\quad\leftrightarrow\quad S_{q}\!\left(e^{j2\pi f}\right).

Note that since the functions
K_{p}[\cdot]
and
K_{q}[\cdot]
are absolutely summable, the spectra are guaranteed to exist and will be clearly upper bounded. We impose the additional restriction that these spectra are lower-bounded by some positive scalars. This condition is for example satisfied whenever the spectra are positive and continuous on
[0,1]
.

We observe
n
consecutive values of the process:

\mathbf{Y}=\begin{pmatrix}Y[1]\\
\vdots\\
Y[n]\end{pmatrix},

and our objective is to test for the two hypotheses.

Before proceeding, we note that under either hypethesis the auto-covariance matrix of
\mathbf{Y}
is the Hermitian Toeplitz matrix derived form the auto-covariance function
K[\cdot]
:

\Lambda=\begin{pmatrix}K[0]&K[1]&\cdots&\cdots&K[n-2]&K[n-1]\\
K[1]&K[0]&K[1]&\cdots&\cdots&K[n-2]\\
\cdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\cdots&\cdots\\
\cdots&\cdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\cdots\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&K[1]&K[0]&K[1]\\
K[n-1]&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&K[1]&K[0]\end{pmatrix}

V-A

Equivalent Formulation

When observing
n
consecutive values of the process, the binary hypothesis test at hand becomes one where the observed data is simply an
n
-dimensional multivariate Gaussian vector:

\displaystyle H_{0}:\,

\displaystyle\mathbf{Y}\sim\mathcal{N}\left({{\bm{0}}},{\Lambda_{p}}\right)

\displaystyle H_{1}:\,

\displaystyle\mathbf{Y}\sim\mathcal{N}\left({{\bm{0}}},{\Lambda_{q}}\right).

(8)

By applying a –well chosen– linear transformation of the form

\textsf{M}\mathbf{Y}
, the detection problem is equivalent to one with
diagonal covariance matrices.

Indeed, we first diagonalize matrix
\Lambda_{q}
using orthogonal
matrix
\textsf{U}_{q}
. Denoting the diagonal matrix by
\Delta_{q}
and using the convention as in equation (
3

\begin{array}[]{rlcrl}\Lambda_{q}&=\textsf{U}_{q}^{t}\Delta_{q}\textsf{U}_{q}&&\Lambda_{q}^{1/2}&=\textsf{U}_{q}^{t}\Delta^{1/2}_{q}\textsf{U}_{q},\end{array}

consider the matrix
\Lambda_{q}^{-1/2}\Lambda_{p}\Lambda_{q}^{-t/2}

which is Hermitian symmetric and positive definite. We diagonalize it using an orthogonal matrix
V
and denote the resulting diagonal matrix

K
:

\displaystyle\Lambda_{q}^{-1/2}\Lambda_{p}\Lambda_{q}^{-t/2}=\textsf{V}^{t}\textsf{K}\,\textsf{V}.

Finally, consider the transformation:

\mathbf{Y}^{\prime}=\textsf{V}\Lambda_{q}^{-1/2}\mathbf{Y},

which under
H_{0}
and
H_{1}
has respective Gaussian laws
p^{[n]^{\prime}\hskip-0.5pt}
and

\displaystyle\Lambda_{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\textsf{V}\Lambda_{q}^{-1/2}\Lambda_{p}\Lambda_{q}^{-t/2}\,\textsf{V}^{t}=\textsf{K}

(9)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\textsf{V}\Lambda_{q}^{-1/2}\Lambda_{q}\Lambda_{q}^{-t/2}\,\textsf{V}^{t}=\textsf{I}.

To recap, by applying the linear transformation

\textsf{V}\Lambda_{q}^{-1/2}\mathbf{Y}

\displaystyle H_{0}:\,

\displaystyle\mathbf{Y}\sim\mathcal{N}\left({{\bm{0}}},{\textsf{K}}\right)

\displaystyle H_{1}:\,

\displaystyle\mathbf{Y}\sim\mathcal{N}\left({{\bm{0}}},{\textsf{I}}\right).

(10)

Note here that
D\!\left({p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}\|{q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}\right)=D\!\left({p^{[n]^{\prime}\hskip-0.5pt}}\|{q^{[n]^{\prime}\hskip-0.5pt}}\right)

and there is no loss in generality to assume in what follows that

p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}={\mathcal{N}}\left(\mathbf{x};{\bm{0}},\textsf{K}\right)\quad\&\quad q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}={\mathcal{N}}\left(\mathbf{x};{\bm{0}},\textsf{I}\right).

Note 1
.

By inspection of equation (

By considering an eigenvector associated with an eigenvalue and applying an appropriate matrix multiplication, one can readily show
that the eigenvalues of the following matrices are identical:

\Lambda_{q}^{-1/2}\Lambda_{p}\Lambda_{q}^{-t/2}\qquad\Lambda_{q}^{-1}\Lambda_{p}\qquad\Lambda_{p}\Lambda_{q}^{-1}\qquad\Lambda_{p}^{1/2}\Lambda_{q}^{-1}\Lambda_{p}^{t/2}.

Definition 5

Appendix B
Asymptotic Behavior of Relative Entropy

We establish below the asymptotic behavior of the relative entropy between two Gaussian vectors. It is assumed that these
n
-dimensional vectors are parts of discrete-time (wide-sense)
stationary processes with auto-covariance functions that are square-summable.

Without loss of generality, we assume that the two laws at hand are zero-mean.

B-A

Relative Entropy between Correlated and Independent Gaussians

Assume in what follows that

p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}={\mathcal{N}}\left(\mathbf{x};{\bm{0}},\textsf{K}\right)\quad\&\quad q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}={\mathcal{N}}\left(\mathbf{x};{\bm{0}},\textsf{I}\right),

where
p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}
is the law of an
n
-dimensional ”segment” of a wide-sense stationary Gaussian process with absolutely summable auto-covariance function and non-zero spectrum
S\!\left(e^{j2\pi f}\right)
. The law
q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}
corresponds to that of an
n
-dimensional ”segment” of a wide-sense stationary white Gaussian process with a unit spectrum.

Denoting by
\left\{\alpha^{[n]}_{k}\right\}_{k}
the eigenvalues of
K
, it is well-known
[
5
]
that the relative entropy is

\displaystyle D\!\left({p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}\|{q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\text{tr}(\textsf{K})-\frac{1}{2}\log\text{det}(\textsf{K})-\frac{n}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\alpha^{[n]}_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\log\alpha^{[n]}_{k}-\frac{n}{2}

Using Theorem
5
,

\frac{1}{n}D\!\left({p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}\|{q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\alpha^{[n]}_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\log\alpha^{[n]}_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\\
\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\frac{1}{2}\!\int_{0}^{1}\!S\!\left(e^{j2\pi f}\right)df-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\!\log S\!\left(e^{j2\pi f}\right)df-\frac{1}{2}.

Finally, note that if the two hypotheses have identical power,
\int S\!\left(e^{j2\pi f}\right)=1
and the limit of relative entropy simplifies to

\frac{1}{n}D\!\left({p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}\|{q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}\right)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\frac{-1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\log S\!\left(e^{j2\pi f}\right)\!df,

which is by Jensen’s strictly positive and equal to zero if and only if
S\!\left(e^{j2\pi f}\right)=1
.

B-B

Relative Entropy between Correlated Gaussians

Now we consider

p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}={\mathcal{N}}\left(\mathbf{x};{\bm{0}},\Lambda^{[n]}_{p}\right)\quad\&\quad q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}={\mathcal{N}}\left(\mathbf{x};{\bm{0}},\Lambda^{[n]}_{q}\right),

where
p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}
and
q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}
are the laws of an
n
-dimensional ”segment” of two wide-sense stationary Gaussian processes with absolutely summable auto-covariance functions and bounded non-zero spectra
S_{p}\!\left(e^{j2\pi f}\right)
and
S_{q}\!\left(e^{j2\pi f}\right)
respectively.
The relative entropy may be readily derived:

\displaystyle D\!\left({p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}\|{q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}\right)

\displaystyle\,=\,\frac{1}{2}\text{tr}\left(\Lambda^{[n]}_{p}\Lambda^{-1\,[n]}_{q}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\text{det}\left(\Lambda^{[n]}_{p}\right)}{\text{det}\left(\Lambda^{[n]}_{q}\right)}-\frac{n}{2}

The asymptotics of the last two terms may be readily obtained as in the previous section. It remains to determine those of the first term.

Since
\Lambda^{[n]}_{p}\sim\tilde{\Lambda}^{[n]}_{p}
and
\Lambda^{[n]}_{q}\sim\tilde{\Lambda}^{[n]}_{q}
, then

\displaystyle\Lambda^{-1\,[n]}_{q}

\displaystyle\sim\tilde{\Lambda}^{-1\,[n]}_{q}

\displaystyle\&\quad\Lambda^{[n]}_{p}\Lambda^{-1\,[n]}_{q}

\displaystyle\sim\tilde{\Lambda}^{[n]}_{p}\tilde{\Lambda}^{-1\,[n]}_{q}

(27)

Since all circulant Hermitian matrices can be diagonalized with the same orthogonal matrix, the eigenvalues of
\tilde{\Lambda}^{[n]}_{p}\tilde{\Lambda}^{-1\,[n]}_{q}
are simply the ratios of those of
\tilde{\Lambda}^{[n]}_{p}
to that of
\tilde{\Lambda}^{[n]}_{q}
:

\frac{\beta^{[n]}_{k}}{\gamma^{[n]}_{k}}=\frac{S_{p}^{[n]}\!\left(e^{j2\pi f}\right)}{S_{q}^{[n]}\!\left(e^{j2\pi f}\right)}\Bigg|_{f=(k/n)}

On the other hand and as seen in Note
1
, the eigenvalues of
\Lambda^{[n]}_{p}\Lambda^{-1\,[n]}_{q}
are the same as those of the Hermitian
\Lambda^{1/2[n]}_{p}\Lambda^{-1\,[n]}_{q}\Lambda^{1/2[n]}_{p}
and are hence real and positive. Applying Theorem
4
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\,\frac{1}{n}\text{tr}\left(\Lambda^{[n]}_{p}\Lambda^{-1\,[n]}_{q}\right)

\displaystyle\,=\lim_{n\to\infty}\,\frac{1}{n}\text{tr}\left(\tilde{\Lambda}^{[n]}_{p}\tilde{\Lambda}^{-1\,[n]}_{q}\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\,\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{S_{p}^{[n]}\left(e^{j2\pi\frac{k}{n}}\right)}{S_{q}^{[n]}\left(e^{j2\pi\frac{k}{n}}\right)},

and following the same steps as in the proof of Lemma
26
, since the spectra
S_{p}\!\left(e^{j2\pi f}\right)
and
S_{q}\!\left(e^{j2\pi f}\right)
are upper-bounded, lower-bounded by a positive scalar and converge uniformly, their ratio

\frac{S_{p}^{[n]}\!\left(e^{j2\pi f}\right)}{S_{q}^{[n]}\!\left(e^{j2\pi f}\right)}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\frac{S_{p}\!\left(e^{j2\pi f}\right)}{S_{q}\!\left(e^{j2\pi f}\right)}\quad\text{ uniformly,}

and by basic properties of Riemann integrals,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\,\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{S_{p}^{[n]}\left(e^{j2\pi\frac{k}{n}}\right)}{S_{q}^{[n]}\left(e^{j2\pi\frac{k}{n}}\right)}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{S_{p}\!\left(e^{j2\pi f}\right)}{S_{q}\!\left(e^{j2\pi f}\right)}\,df.

In summary,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\,\frac{1}{n}D\!\left({p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}\|{q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}\right)

\displaystyle\,=\frac{1}{2}\,\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\text{tr}\left(\Lambda^{[n]}_{p}\Lambda^{-1\,[n]}_{q}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\,\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{\text{det}\left(\Lambda^{[n]}_{p}\right)}{\text{det}\left(\Lambda^{[n]}_{q}\right)}-\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\,=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{S_{p}\!\left(e^{j2\pi f}\right)}{S_{q}\!\left(e^{j2\pi f}\right)}\,df-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\!\log\frac{S_{p}\!\left(e^{j2\pi f}\right)}{S_{q}\!\left(e^{j2\pi f}\right)}\,df-\frac{1}{2}.

(28)

Appendix C
Sublinear Growth of
D(p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}||q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt})

Consider the following setup (given in
[
6
]
):
Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\geq 3
, consider the following PDF over
[0,1]^{n}
:

\displaystyle q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(x^{[n]})=\begin{cases}n-\sqrt{n},&\text{if }\|x^{n}\|_{\infty}\leq n^{-1/n},\\
\frac{\sqrt{n}}{n-1},&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
\|x^{n}\|_{\infty}=\max_{i}|x_{i}|
. It can be easily verified that
q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}
is a valid PDF as the volume of the set
\{x^{[n]}:\|x^{[n]}\|_{\infty}\leq n^{-1/n}\}
is
\frac{1}{n}
. Let
p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}
be the uniform law over
[0,1]^{n}
.

Now, consider the following hypothesis testing problem:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle H_{0}:X^{n}\sim p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\cdot),\\
\displaystyle H_{1}:X^{n}\sim q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\cdot).\end{array}\right.

Consequently,

\displaystyle D\!\left({p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}\|{q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}\right)

\displaystyle=\int p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\log\frac{p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}{q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}dx^{[n]}

(30)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{1}{n-\sqrt{n}}+\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)\log\frac{n-1}{\sqrt{n}}

(31)

\displaystyle=\log\frac{n-1}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{\sqrt{n}}{(n-1)(n-\sqrt{n})},

(32)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(a)}}}{{\sim}}\log\sqrt{n},

(33)

where (a) follows from elementary steps. As such,
D\!\left({p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}\|{q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}\right)
grows with
n
as required in Theorem
1
.

Lemma 5
.

Consider the
\delta
-typical set
\mathcal{A}^{[n]}(p,q,\delta)
. For any constant family
\epsilon
, any constant family
\delta
is
\epsilon
-good. In particular, the conditions of Theorem
1
hold and, for any
\tau>0
,

\displaystyle-\log\beta^{[n]}_{\tau}=\textsf{D}^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}+o\left(\textsf{D}^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}\right).

Proof.

Let
B=\{x^{[n]}:\|x^{[n]}\|_{\infty}>n^{-1/n}\}
. Then, for
x^{[n]}\in B
,

\displaystyle\log\frac{p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}{q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}dx^{[n]}=\log\frac{n-1}{\sqrt{n}}.

(34)

Hence,

\displaystyle\log\frac{p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}{q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}dx^{[n]}-D\!\left({p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}\|{q^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{\sqrt{n}}{(n-1)(n-\sqrt{n})}

(35)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0.

(36)

Therefore, for any constant
\delta
,
B\subseteq\mathcal{A}^{[n]}(p,q,\delta)
. Finally,
p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(\mathcal{A}^{[n]}(p,q,\delta))\geq p^{[n]\hskip-0.5pt}(B)=1-1/n\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}1.

∎

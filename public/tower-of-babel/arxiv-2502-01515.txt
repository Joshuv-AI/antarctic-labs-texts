(Anti-)Altermagnetism from Orbital Ordering in the Ruddlesden-Popper Chromates Sr n+1 Cr n O 3n+1
Quintin N. Meier, Alberto Carta, Claude Ederer, Andrés Cano

Abstract
Altermagnets form a subclass of collinear antiferromagnets with spin-split electronic states. Here, we introduce the Ruddlesden-Popper chromates Sr n+1 Cr n O 3n+1 (including the perovskite SrCrO 3 ) as candidates for antiferromagnetic materials in which altermagnetism emerges from spontaneous orbital ordering, rather than crystal symmetry. Using first-principles calculations, we identify a layer-dependent spin splitting of the electronic states, driven by the interplay between spin and orbital order. We show that if the spin and orbital orders align in adjacent layers, the system exhibits a net spin splitting, and thus altermagnetism. In contrast, if either the spin or the orbital order is reversed in adjacent layers, we observe an anti-altermagnetic state where layerwise compensation leads to net zero spin splitting. We find that for odd n n , altermagnetic and anti-altermagnetic phases can coexist, whereas for even n n and in the perovskite limit, the system remains strictly anti-altermagnetic. In both cases, increasing n n favors metallicity. Finally, we indicate that in odd n n compounds, epitaxial strain can promote the altermagnetic phase.



In ferromagnets, the net magnetic moment arises from the splitting and unequal population of spin-up and spin-down electronic states (i.e., time-reversal symmetry breaking). In contrast, in conventional antiferromagnets, the sublattices of opposite spins are connected by simple translations or inversion symmetries, cases in which Kramers’ theorem enforces the electronic structure to show complete degeneracy between the spin states, thus ensuring effective time-reversal symmetry. The recently identified class of altermagnets occupies an intermediate ground: while displaying collinear antiferromagnetic order with zero net magnetization, the absence of a simple translation or inversion operation connecting the different magnetic sublattices allows for non-relativistic spin splitting of the electronic states, even in the absence of spin-orbit coupling
Hayami et al. 2019
;
Yuan et al. 2020
;
Ahn et al. 2019
;
Šmejkal et al. 2020
;
Mazin et al. 2021
;
Šmejkal et al. 2022a
. Recently, photoemission experiments have provided direct experimental evidence of the altermagnetic spin splitting in MnTe
Krempaský et al. 2024
;
Lee et al. 2024
.

Beyond its fundamental interest, altermagnetism holds significant potential for spintronic applications, as ferromagnetic-like responses such as the anomalous Hall effect
Šmejkal et al. 2020
;
Feng et al. 2022
;
Leiviskä et al. 2024
, spin Hall effect
González-Hernández et al. 2021
;
Bai et al. 2022
, and the anomalous Nernst effect
Sürgers et al. 2024
;
Badura et al. 2024
can, in principle, be achieved in antiferromagnetic materials. This makes altermagnetic materials promising for use in future antiferromagnetic spintronic devices
Baltz et al. 2018
.

3

Within the confinement of the Ruddlesden-Popper structure, the decoupling of the different layers due to the spacer enables the possibility of stabilizing an altermagnetic state in members of the Ruddelsden-Popper series with an odd number of layers. Here, the spin-splitting within each perovskite block cannot be fully compensated, resulting in a net altermagnetic splitting of the electronic bands. Furthermore, we highlight the role of dimensionality in tuning the electronic properties of the system. As shown in our bandplots but also experimental data

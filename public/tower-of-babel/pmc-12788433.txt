An Observational Study to Correlate Severity of Lower Urinary Tract Symptoms and Size of Median Lobe of Prostate by Digital Rectal Examination in Patients with Benign Prostate Hyperplasia
Authors: Ashish K. Dubey, Aashutosh Asati, Mayank Chourasia, Hemal Vithani, Talha Saad, Abhay S. Tirkey, Uppal R. Kunkal
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:This observational study evaluated the correlation between median lobe size assessed by digital rectal examination (DRE) and the severity of lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS) in benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) patients.Methods:A total of 120 men aged ≥50 years were assessed using the International Prostate Symptom Score (IPSS) and DRE for median lobe grading. Correlations were analyzed using Spearman’s coefficient and ANOVA.Results:Larger median lobe size significantly correlated with higher LUTS severity (P < 0.001), with mean IPSS scores of 7.6 (small), 15.4 (moderate), and 24.1 (large lobes).Conclusion:Median lobe enlargement is associated with severe LUTS in BPH, highlighting the importance of thorough clinical prostate evaluation.

INTRODUCTION

Benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) is a common non-malignant enlargement of the prostate gland affecting aging men, particularly after the age of 50 years. It is characterized by hyperplasia of prostatic stromal and epithelial cells, resulting in the formation of large, discrete nodules in the periurethral region of the prostate, which can impinge on the urethra and lead to lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS).[1] LUTS, including urinary frequency, urgency, nocturia, weak stream, and incomplete bladder emptying, are among the most prevalent complaints in urological practice and significantly impact the quality of life of affected individuals.[2]

Digital rectal examination (DRE) remains an essential, low-cost, and non-invasive clinical tool to assess prostate size, consistency, and the presence of a median lobe enlargement, which has been implicated as a contributor to the severity of LUTS. Although imaging modalities such as transrectal ultrasound (TRUS) and MRI offer more detailed prostate volume estimation, DRE provides a practical and immediate assessment, especially in resource-limited settings.[3] Importantly, the median lobe’s size and configuration may play a distinct role in symptom severity by causing intravesical prostatic protrusion, potentially leading to bladder outlet obstruction.

Despite the central role of DRE in routine clinical evaluation, there is limited systematic data correlating the median lobe size assessed by DRE with the severity of LUTS in patients with BPH. Establishing such correlations can enhance the clinical utility of DRE for decision-making in primary care and urology clinics, guiding the need for further investigations or interventions. This observational study aims to investigate the relationship between the severity of LUTS and the size of the median lobe of the prostate, as assessed by DRE, in patients diagnosed with BPH.

METHODOLOGY

This observational, cross-sectional study was conducted at the Department of Urology. Male patients aged 50 years and above presenting with symptoms suggestive of BPH and diagnosed clinically were consecutively recruited after obtaining informed consent.

Inclusion criteria included patients with bothersome LUTS consistent with BPH, such as urinary frequency, urgency, nocturia, weak stream, intermittency, and incomplete voiding. Exclusion criteria included patients with a history of prostate cancer, previous prostate surgery, active urinary tract infection, urethral stricture disease, or neurological disorders affecting bladder function.

Severity of LUTS was assessed using the International Prostate Symptom Score (IPSS) questionnaire, which categorizes symptoms as mild (0–7), moderate (8–19), or severe (20–35). DRE was performed by a trained urologist to estimate prostate size, consistency, and specifically evaluate the presence and size of the median lobe, graded subjectively as small, moderate, or large based on protrusion felt on examination.

All data were collected on a structured proforma, including patient demographics, IPSS scores, and DRE findings. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS version 25.0. Descriptive statistics were used to summarize patient characteristics. The correlation between median lobe size and LUTS severity was analyzed using Spearman’s correlation coefficient, and comparisons between groups (small vs. large median lobe) were made using Chi-square or t-tests where appropriate. A P value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

A total of 120 male patients aged 50 years and above with clinically diagnosed BPH were enrolled. The mean age of participants was 64.3 ± 7.5 years (range: 51–83 years). These results show that a larger median lobe, as assessed by DRE, is significantly associated with more severe LUTS in BPH patients. Patients with large median lobes had nearly triple the IPSS score compared to those with small lobes, emphasizing the clinical importance of median lobe assessment during routine evaluation.

DISCUSSION

This observational study evaluated the relationship between median lobe size, assessed by DRE, and the severity of LUTS in patients with BPH. The findings demonstrated a significant positive correlation between increasing median lobe size and worsening LUTS severity, as measured by the IPSS.

Previous studies have emphasized that intravesical prostatic protrusion, often caused by median lobe enlargement, is a key anatomical factor contributing to bladder outlet obstruction, independent of total prostate volume.[4] Median lobe hypertrophy obstructs the bladder neck and impairs urine flow, leading to higher symptom scores.[5] Our study’s observation that 50% of patients with large median lobes presented with severe LUTS aligns with the notion that median lobe configuration plays a critical role in symptom severity.[6]

DRE, although operator-dependent, remains a valuable bedside tool in resource-limited settings for assessing prostate size and median lobe prominence.[3] While advanced imaging techniques like transrectal ultrasound (TRUS) and MRI provide detailed anatomical insights, DRE offers an accessible first-line method to guide clinical decisions.[7] Our results suggest that clinicians should carefully evaluate median lobe size during DRE, as this may help predict which patients are likely to experience severe LUTS and require more aggressive management. However, limitations include the subjective nature of DRE and the absence of confirmatory imaging or urodynamic data. Future studies should incorporate longitudinal follow-up to assess symptom progression and treatment outcomes based on median lobe size.

CONCLUSION

Median lobe size assessed by DRE significantly correlates with LUTS severity in BPH patients. Clinical evaluation of the median lobe can enhance patient stratification and management planning.

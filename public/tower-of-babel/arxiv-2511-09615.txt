All-order generalized Green-Schwarz transformations
Achilleas Gitsis, Falk Hassler

Abstract
Compatibility with T-duality severely constrains higher-derivative corrections to the low-energy supergravity limits of string theory. For example, it suggests that Lorentz transformations for heterotic strings are modified in precisely the way required for the Green-Schwarz anomaly cancellation mechanism. A systematic procedure to construct the resulting generalized Green-Schwarz transformations is the generalized Bergshoeff-de Roo identification (gBdRi) Baron et al. 2018 ; *Baron:2020xel . Although it in principle allows computing α ′ \alpha^{\prime} -corrections to higher and higher orders, technically it becomes unfeasible beyond α ′ 2 \alpha^{\prime 2} . We revisit this problem with an alternative approach to the gBdRi, which we have recently developed. It gives rise to a very simple all-order transformation law whose closure we verify by explicitly computing the resulting gauge algebra.



I
Introduction

Higher-derivative corrections of the low-energy, supergravity limit of string theory have a wide range of applications spanning from string phenomenology and cosmology, over the holographic analysis of conformal field theories through the AdS/CFT correspondence, to the study of black holes and their entropy. Obtaining them directly from string amplitudes or higher-loop
\beta
-functions in non-linear
\sigma
-models is technically very challenging. Hence, only the leading order corrections are known in full generality. One of the main challenges is that the number of terms which are compatible with the diffeomorphisms and gauge field transformations grows extremely fast with an increasing number of derivatives. Consequentially, a lot of work is needed to fix the respective coefficients in the effective action by comparing them with results from string amplitudes or
\beta
-functions. A possible way to remedy this problem is to find additional symmetries which are more restrictive and thereby significantly reduce the admissible terms in the effective action. Unfortunately, these symmetries are not manifest and thus realized in a convoluted, non-linear way.

Supersymmetry is the most obvious example. But due to longstanding challenges in finding the relevant corrected transformations and their invariants, a considerable amount of work has been spent to use duality symmetries as an alternative. After the reduction on an
n
-dimensional torus where only massless modes are kept, a global O(
n
,
n
) symmetry emerges
Meissner and Veneziano 1991a
;
*Meissner:1991ge
;
Sen 1991
;
Meissner 1997
. This symmetry captures constant diffeomorphisms,
B
-field transformations and T-dualities on the torus used in the reduction. Because it is much more constraining than diffeomorphisms and
B
-field transformations alone, it drastically reduces the number of allowed terms in the effective action. Eventually, it only leaves very few coefficients which still have to be fixed by comparing with amplitudes or
\beta
-functions. Despite this success, the explicit realization of the pivotal O(
n
,
n
) duality symmetry after the reductions is very complicated and has to be painstakingly computed order by order
Codina et al. 2021
;
Garousi 2023
;
Wulff 2024
;
Ameri et al. 2025
.

Hence, a lot of effort has been invested to make this symmetry manifest even before the reduction in the framework of double field theory
Siegel 1993
;
Hull and Zwiebach 2009
;
*Hohm:2010pp
, and the closely related generalized geometry
Hitchin 2003
;
Gualtieri 2003
, where the metric and the
B
-field are unified to form the generalized metric,

H_{IJ}=\begin{pmatrix}g_{ij}-B_{ik}g^{kl}B_{lj}&&B_{ik}g^{kj}\\
-g^{ik}B_{kj}&&g^{ij}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(1)

an element of the coset O(
d
,
d
)/O(
d-1
,1)
\times
O(1,
d-1
). It arises from an unconstrained duality group element called the generalized frame
E^{A}{}_{I}
by

H_{IJ}=E^{A}{}_{I}H_{AB}E^{B}{}_{J}\,,\quad\text{with}\quad H_{AB}=\begin{pmatrix}\eta_{\overline{a}\overline{b}}&0\\
0&-\eta_{\underline{a}\underline{b}}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(2)

Here
\eta_{\overline{a}\overline{b}}
is the Minkowski metric corresponding to the first O(
d-1
,1) factor in the coset, while
\eta_{\underline{a}\underline{b}}
governs the second one. We will always distinguish between the string’s left- and right-moving sector by using over- and under-barred indices like here. Diffeomorphisms and
B
-field transformations are mediated by the generalized Lie derivative

\mathcal{L}_{\xi}E^{A}{}_{I}=\xi^{J}\partial_{J}E^{A}{}_{I}-(\partial^{J}\xi_{I}-\partial_{I}\xi^{J})E^{A}{}_{J}\,.

(3)

Keep in mind that indices are raised and lowered with the invariant
\eta
-metric of the duality group,

\eta_{IJ}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\delta_{i}^{j}\\
\delta_{j}^{i}&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{or}\quad\eta_{AB}=\begin{pmatrix}\eta_{\overline{a}\overline{b}}&0\\
0&\eta_{\underline{a}\underline{b}}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(4)

for either curved, like
I
,
J
, …, or flat indices, like
A
,
B
, … . Moreover, we use the partial derivative
\partial_{I}=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{i}&0\end{pmatrix}
and thereby solve the section condition explicitly. Working with the frame
E_{A}{}^{I}
becomes mandatory if we want to find a background independent formulation of
\alpha^{\prime}
-corrections
Marques and Nunez 2015
;
Olaf 2017
. It requires a second local symmetry which is known as double Lorentz symmetry
Geissbuhler et al. 2013
. As the name suggests, it is generated by the transformations with the parameter
\Lambda\in\mathfrak{o}
(
d-1
,1)
\times\mathfrak{o}
(1,
d-1
) that act on the frame by

\delta_{\Lambda}E_{A}{}^{I}=\Lambda_{A}{}^{B}E_{B}{}^{I}\quad\text{with}\quad\Lambda_{AB}=\begin{pmatrix}\Lambda_{\overline{a}\overline{b}}&0\\
0&\Lambda_{\underline{a}\underline{b}}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(5)

In this article, we discuss how the double Lorentz transformations (
5
) can be modified to take higher-derivative corrections into account. The latter can only appear here, because neither diffeomorphisms nor the
B
-field transformations will change – and with them the generalized Lie derivative (
3
). Figuring out the explicit form of admissible corrections is subtle, because
\delta_{\Lambda}E_{A}{}^{I}
is highly constrained by requiring that all possible transformations close into a Lie algebra. The seminal work
Marques and Nunez 2015
reveals that a consistent modification which contains derivatives arises after complementing (
5
) with the additional transformation

\delta_{\Lambda}E_{\underline{a}\overline{b}}=\frac{a}{2}D_{\underline{a}}\Lambda^{\underline{c}\underline{d}}F_{\overline{b}\underline{c}\underline{d}}+\frac{b}{2}D_{\overline{b}}\Lambda^{\overline{c}\overline{d}}F_{\underline{a}\overline{c}\overline{d}}\,.

(6)

It contains the two free parameter
a
,
b
, and is written exclusively in terms of flat indices by using

\delta_{\Lambda}E_{AB}:=\delta_{\Lambda}E_{A}{}^{I}E_{BI}=\begin{pmatrix}\Lambda_{\overline{a}\overline{b}}&-\delta_{\Lambda}E_{\underline{a}\overline{b}}\\
\delta_{\Lambda}E_{\underline{a}\overline{b}}&\Lambda_{\underline{a}\underline{b}}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(7)

Both diagonal elements are already fixed at the leading order in (
5
) and do not receive any corrections. Hence, two-derivative corrections are controlled by the off-diagonal elements given by (
6
) in terms of the flat derivative

D_{A}=E_{A}{}^{I}\partial_{I}

(8)

and the generalized fluxes

F_{ABC}=3D_{[A}E_{B}{}^{I}E_{C]I}\,.

(9)

These new transformations are known under the name generalized Green-Schwarz transformations (gGSt) because they contain a correction to Lorentz transformations which is crucial for the Green-Schwarz anomaly cancellation mechanism
Green and Schwarz 1984
. After imposing that the four-derivative effective action is invariant under this deformed symmetry, it is fixed completely up to a global coefficient. After fixing
a
=
-\alpha^{\prime}
,
b
=0 (heterotic) or
a
=
b
=
\alpha^{\prime}
(bosonic), one recovers the correct low-energy effective actions of the respective string theories
Marques and Nunez 2015
.

Because the transformations (
6
) only close after neglecting terms with more than two derivatives, they need to be further modified with terms carrying four and more derivatives. These terms can be systematically constructed with a technique known as the generalized Bergshoeff-de Roo identification (gBdRi)
Baron et al. 2018
;
Baron and Marques 2021
. For up to four derivatives this has been done first for just the parameter
b

Baron et al. 2018
and later for both parameters
Baron and Marques 2021
. As before, the arising symmetry fixes the action at order
\alpha^{\prime 2}
up to an overall coefficient and reproduces the expected results from the bosonic and heterotic string
Hronek et al. 2022
. Unfortunately the gBdRi becomes quickly cumbersome with an increasing number of derivatives. Therefore, it is not feasible to use it beyond the order
\alpha^{\prime 2}
.

Recently, we presented the twisted Poláček-Siegel construction
Gitsis and Hassler 2025
as an alternative approach. Following an idea initiated in
Poláček and Siegel 2014
and further developed in
Butter et al. 2023
, it utilizes an extended space (the mega-space) to construct covariant tensors under generalized diffeomorphisms which are generated by the generalized Lie derivative (
3
), and double Lorentz transformations. After an appropriate twist, it gives a more geometric perspective on the gBdRi. Moreover, it allows presenting the gGSt in an intermediate form which is much simpler to handle than the final expression. In the following, we make use of these new insights to gGSt to all orders in
\alpha^{\prime}
and to obtain the algebra they close into.

II
All-order generalized Green-Schwarz transformations

II.1
Twisted Poláček-Siegel construction

To compute the gGSt to all orders in
\alpha^{\prime}
, we will employ the twisted Poláček-Siegel construction presented in
Hassler et al. 2025
. One of its most important features is that the duality group O(
d
,
d
) is extended to the group
G_{\mathrm{PS}}
which is generated by three types of generators,
K_{AB}
,
R^{A}_{\alpha}
, and
R_{\alpha\beta}
, with the non-trivial commutators

\displaystyle[K_{AB},K_{CD}]

\displaystyle=\phantom{-}2\eta_{[A|[C}K_{D]|B]}\,,

(10)

\displaystyle[R^{A}_{\alpha},R^{B}_{\beta}]

\displaystyle=-2\kappa_{\alpha\beta}K^{AB}+\eta^{AB}R_{\alpha\beta}\,,

\displaystyle[K_{AB},R^{C}_{\gamma}]

\displaystyle=-\delta_{[A}^{C}\eta_{B]D}R^{D}_{\gamma}\,,

\displaystyle[R^{A}_{\alpha},R_{\beta\gamma}]

\displaystyle=-2\kappa_{\alpha[\beta}R^{A}_{\gamma]}\,,\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle[R_{\alpha\beta},R_{\gamma\delta}]

\displaystyle=-4\kappa_{[a|[\gamma}R_{\delta]|\beta]}\,.

In addition to the invariant metric
\eta_{AB}
of O(
d
,
d
), we need a second non-degenerate metric
\kappa_{\alpha\beta}
. It has been discussed in detail in
Hassler et al. 2025
but for most of the discussions here, we do not need its explicit form. Both metrics are used to raise and lower the respective indices. The reason why
G_{\mathrm{PS}}
appears is that in addition to the O(
d
,
d
)-valued generalized frame field
E_{A}{}^{I}
, there are also various connections which play the role of a suitable generalized analogue of the spin connection known from differential geometry. All of them are combined into the frame field,

\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{A}E\in G_{\mathrm{PS}}\,,

(11)

on the mega-space.

Besides
G_{\mathrm{PS}}
, there is also the generalized structure group
G_{\mathrm{S}}\subset G_{\mathrm{PS}}
. Its action on
\mathcal{A}
and
E
is encoded in

\mathcal{A}^{-1}\delta\mathcal{A}+\delta EE^{-1}=-D_{A}\xi^{\alpha}R^{A}_{\alpha}-\xi^{\alpha}R_{\alpha}+\xi^{\alpha}\mathcal{A}^{-1}\tau_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}\,,

(12)

where
R_{\alpha}
is given by

R_{\alpha}:=\tfrac{1}{2}f_{\alpha}{}^{\beta\gamma}R_{\gamma\beta}\,.

(13)

The structure coefficients
f_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\gamma}
that appear here define
G_{\mathrm{S}}
’s Lie algebra in terms of the generators
\tau_{\alpha}
with

[\tau_{\alpha},\tau_{\beta}]=-f_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\gamma}\tau_{\gamma}\,.

(14)

One should also note that
\kappa_{\alpha\beta}
is a bi-invariant linear form, namely
f_{\alpha(\beta}{}^{\delta}\kappa_{\gamma)\delta}
=
0
, and thus turns
G_{\mathrm{S}}
into a metric Lie group. As we will work a lot with (
12
), it is convenient to further simply this expression by suppressing all indices. More precisely, we define

\xi=\xi^{\alpha}\tau_{\alpha}\,,\quad\xi^{\alpha}R_{\alpha}=\xi_{R}\,,\quad\text{and}\quad D_{A}\xi^{\alpha}R^{A}_{\alpha}=D\xi\,.

(15)

Note that the transformation (
12
) is easily extended to also incorporate generalized diffeomorphisms in addition to
G_{\mathrm{S}}
transformations. The full expression is presented in
Gitsis and Hassler 2025
. However, as we will argue below, generalized diffeomorphisms decouple from the gGSt and only reappear in the closure of the gauge algebra. Hence, we will only highlight their contributions where they are relevant and otherwise set the parameter
\xi_{A}
which controls them to zero.

II.2
Partial gauge fixing

Eventually,
\mathcal{A}
has to be completely fixed in terms of
E
and its derivatives. There are two mechanisms,

1.

partial gauge fixing, and

2.

torsion constraints,

at play to achieve this goal. In the original gBdRi
Baron et al. 2018
;
Baron and Marques 2021
, both are performed at the same time in each step, but the Poláček-Siegel construction allows treating them independently. Hence, here we will only consider
1
and thereby obtain results which are valid for arbitrary choices of torsion constraints.

As an element of
G_{\mathrm{PS}}
,
\mathcal{A}
can be written as

\mathcal{A}=\exp\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}A^{n}\right)

(16)

for any choice of the coefficients
c_{n}
. In principle, any choice of
c_{n}
will work, as different choices can be related by field redefinitions of the generalized connections

A=A^{\alpha}_{B}R_{\alpha}^{B}+\tfrac{1}{2}A^{\alpha\beta}R_{\beta\alpha}\,.

(17)

But as we will see soon, there is a preferred choice which will lead to very simple results. To motivate it, we first need to take into account that
G_{\mathrm{PS}}
has a natural
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
grading which originates from the left- and right-moving sector of the underlying closed string. It becomes manifest by bringing
\eta_{AB}
and
\kappa_{\alpha\beta}
into the block diagonal forms

\eta_{AB}=\begin{pmatrix}\eta_{\overline{a}\overline{b}}&0\\
0&\eta_{\underline{a}\underline{b}}\end{pmatrix}\,,\quad\text{and}\quad\kappa_{\alpha\beta}=\begin{pmatrix}\kappa_{\overline{\alpha}\overline{\beta}}&0\\
0&\kappa_{\underline{\alpha}\underline{\beta}}\end{pmatrix}

(18)

with
\eta_{\underline{a}\underline{b}}=-\eta_{\overline{a}\overline{b}}
. Under this grading
A
for example decomposes into a (anti-)chiral part
A_{+}
and a mixed chiral part
A_{-}
,

A=A_{+}+A_{-}\,,

(19)

with

A_{-}=A^{\overline{\alpha}}_{\underline{b}}R_{\overline{\alpha}}^{\underline{b}}+A^{\underline{\alpha}}_{\overline{b}}R_{\underline{\alpha}}^{\overline{b}}+\tfrac{1}{2}A^{\overline{\alpha}\underline{\beta}}R_{\overline{\beta}\underline{\alpha}}+\tfrac{1}{2}A^{\underline{\alpha}\overline{\beta}}R_{\underline{\beta}\overline{\alpha}}

(20)

and
A_{+}
respectively. Moreover,
G_{\mathrm{S}}
is purely chiral and therefore only the structure coefficients
f_{\overline{\alpha}\overline{\beta}}{}^{\overline{\gamma}}
and
f_{\underline{\alpha}\underline{\beta}}{}^{\underline{\gamma}}
in (
14
) are non-zero.

Our gauge fixing of
A
is

A_{+}=0\,,

(21)

implying that
A
only has mixed chiral contributions which will be fixed by torsion constraints. To also keep the argument of the exponential map in (
16
) mixed chiral, all even coefficients
c_{2n}
=
0
have to vanish. A suitable choice for the first two of all remaining coefficients has been identified in
Gitsis and Hassler 2025
as
c_{1}
=
1
and
c_{3}
=
1/3
by imposing that the maximal number of terms cancels. Pushing the computation there a bit further, we found after pages of computation
c_{5}
=
1/5
. Hence, one might guess that

c_{2n+1}=\frac{1}{2n+1}

(22)

is the best choice, and indeed, we will see that this is true. It is special in two ways: First, it allows us to write (
16
) as

\mathcal{A}=\sqrt{\frac{1+A}{1-A}}\,,

(23)

and second, if we expand
\mathcal{A}
directly to

\mathcal{A}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{n}A^{n}

(24)

it is the only choice for which two adjacent coefficients

d_{2n}=d_{2n+1}=\frac{(2n-1)!!}{(2n)!!}

(25)

match. Later on, we will see that this is the property which gives rise to the extensive cancellations we observed in our painstaking initial computations.

II.3
Transformation of the generalized frame

Due to the gauge fixing (
21
), the transformation of the generalized frame is modified and eventually becomes the gGSt we are after. Organizing (
12
) according to the number of derivatives which contribute to the individual terms, we find the recursive relation

\delta A^{(n)}+\delta E^{(n)}E^{-1}-\widetilde{\xi}^{(n)}=\left.-D\xi-\mathcal{A}^{-1}\delta\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{A}^{-1}\xi\mathcal{A}\right|^{(<n)}\,.

(26)

With
A^{(n)}
we denote all contributions to
A
with
n
derivatives. Moreover we use
|^{(<n)}
to say that on the right-hand side only
A
,
\delta EE^{-1}
and
\widetilde{\xi}
which contain less than
n
derivatives are considered. Also note that we have introduced here
\widetilde{\xi}=\xi-\xi_{R}
to keep this and the following relations a bit shorter. The process of partial gauge fixing now works as follows: First, we seed this relation with

\delta E^{(0)}E^{-1}=\widetilde{\xi}^{(0)}=\Lambda^{(0)}\,,

(27)

where
\Lambda^{(0)}
is the parameter for double Lorentz transformations. Moreover, we know that
\delta E^{(n)}E^{-1}
with
n>0
can only have mixed chiral
K_{AB}
generators because double Lorentz transformations are modified. All other mixed chiral generators that arise from the right-hand side go to
\delta A^{(n)}
. Finally, all chiral contributions give rise to the gauge fixing of
\widetilde{\xi}^{(n)}
. For this to work, the structure group
G_{\mathrm{S}}
has to be large enough and contain all generators
R^{\overline{a}}_{\overline{\beta}}
,
R^{\underline{a}}_{\underline{\beta}}
,
R^{\overline{\alpha}\overline{\beta}}
, and
R^{\underline{\alpha}\underline{\beta}}
. We constructed an appropriate group in
Gitsis and Hassler 2025
and will use it in section
IV
to make contact with existing results on gGSt in the literature. But even for more general structure groups, the partial gauge fixing and the residual gauge transformations arise at the same time order by order from (
26
).

As
A
plays the role of a connection, it does not transform covariantly and it is more instructive to study the anomalous part

\Delta A=\delta A-[\xi,A]\,

(28)

of its transformation instead of the full transformation
\delta A
. In terms of this new quantity, (
26
) becomes

\Delta A^{(n)}+\delta E^{(n)}E^{-1}-\widetilde{\xi}^{(n)}=\left.-D\xi-\mathcal{A}^{-1}\Delta\mathcal{A}\right|^{(<n)}\,.

(29)

An advantage of this rewriting is that it becomes straightforward to implement generalized diffeomorphisms with the parameter
\xi_{A}
by just extending
\Delta A
to

\Delta^{\prime}A=\delta^{\prime}A-[\xi,A]-\xi^{B}D_{B}A=\Delta A

(30)

as

\delta^{\prime}A=\delta A+\xi^{A}D_{A}A\,.

(31)

Therefore,
\Delta^{\prime}A
and
\Delta A
constitute the same object, thereby we will focus our study only in the unprimed version. But as this substitution does not change the form of (
29
), the results for the gauge fixing and the gGSt are still the same. This is not a coincidence, but rather a feature of the Poláček-Siegel construction, where generalized diffeomorphisms are a manifest symmetry. Consequently, we continue with
\xi_{A}=0
, as it does not give rise to new structures.

To disentangle the left-hand side further, we keep in mind that
\widetilde{\xi}
is chiral, while both
\Delta A^{(n)}
and
\delta E^{(n)}E^{-1}
for
n>0
are mixed chiral. Moreover,
\delta E^{(n)}E^{-1}
only contributes with
K_{AB}
generators, while
\delta A^{(n)}
can only contain the two different types of
R
-generators. After separating the two of them, we are thus left with

\delta E^{(n)}E^{-1}=-\mathcal{A}^{-1}\Delta\mathcal{A}-[A,\widetilde{\xi}]\big|^{(<n)}_{K_{-}}\,,

(32)

where the subscript
K_{-}
indicates that we are considering only the mixed chirality
K_{AB}
part. Next, we eliminate the gauge fixed transformation parameter
\widetilde{\xi}
from the right-hand side by using (
29
) to find

\delta E^{(n)}E^{-1}=-[A,D\xi_{+}]\big|^{(<n)}_{K}-X^{(n)}\,,

(33)

with

X=\mathcal{A}^{-1}\Delta\mathcal{A}+[A,\mathcal{A}^{-1}\Delta\mathcal{A}]\big|^{(<n)}_{K_{-}}\,.

(34)

This is all that can be done without taking into account the explicit parametrization of
\mathcal{A}
. But after taking into account (
23
), appendix
A
shows that an alternative way to write
X^{(n)}
is

X^{(n)}=\left.A\mathcal{A}^{-1}\Delta\mathcal{A}A\right|^{(<n)}_{K_{-}}\,.

(35)

Again, we use (
29
) to eliminate
\mathcal{A}^{-1}\Delta\mathcal{A}
in favor of

X^{(n)}=-A\left(\delta EE^{-1}+D\xi\right)A\big|^{(<n)}_{K_{-}}\,.

(36)

Any non-trivial contribution to
X^{(n)}
needs an
R_{\alpha\beta}
generator in the brackets. However,
\delta EE^{-1}
only gives rise to
K_{AB}
generators, and
D\xi
will only contain
R_{\alpha}^{A}
generators. Therefore, we conclude
X^{(n)}=0
and are left with the simple, all-order result

\boxed{\delta EE^{-1}=-[A,D\xi_{+}]\big|_{K}}

(37)

for the gGSt, with the abbreviation
D\xi_{+}
for the chiral part of
D\xi
(while
D\xi_{-}
is the mixed chiral contribution).

To obtain the final expression for the transformation
\delta EE^{-1}
which only depends on the generalized frame
E
and its derivatives, one has to solve the torsion constraints to eliminate
A
. We will come back to this point in section
IV
.

III
Gauge algebra

As the gGSt (
37
) arises in our approach from a partial gauge fixing, it is not directly obvious that these transformations will close. At the leading orders this closure has been shown
Baron et al. 2018
;
Baron and Marques 2021
, but will it also hold to all orders? To answer this question, we compute the commutator of two transformations. More precisely, we apply a second transformation to (
12
), resulting in

\displaystyle

\displaystyle 2\delta_{[2}\left(\delta_{1]}EE^{-1}\right)+2\delta_{[2}\left(\mathcal{A}^{-1}\delta_{1]}\mathcal{A}\right)=

(38)

\displaystyle-2\delta_{[2}\left(D\xi_{{1]}}\right)-2\delta_{[2}\xi_{1]R}+2\delta_{[2}\left(\mathcal{A}^{-1}\xi_{1]}\mathcal{A}\right)\,.

After distributing all variations there are only

\delta_{[2}\delta_{1]}A^{(n)}\,,\qquad\delta_{[2}\delta_{1]}E^{(n)}E^{-1}\,,\quad\text{and}\quad\delta_{[2}\widetilde{\xi}^{(n)}_{1]}

(39)

as unknown terms left. They have to be fixed recursively, in the same way as we already did for the gauge transformation in (
26
). There, everything worked because we could separate the terms on the left-hand side by generators. This is also possible for
\delta_{[2}\delta_{1]}A^{(n)}
; like
\delta A^{(n)}
, it has only contributions from mixed chiral
R^{A}_{\alpha}
and
R_{\alpha\beta}
generators. Moreover,
\delta_{[2}\widetilde{\xi}^{(n)}_{1]}
is purely chiral like
\widetilde{\xi}^{(n)}
. Therefore, one would expect that
\delta_{[2}\delta_{1]}E^{(n)}E^{-1}
is formed by chiral
K_{AB}
generators at the zeroth order, followed by their mixed chiral counterparts at higher orders. For convenience, let us denote these contributions by
K_{\pm}
. Here, we adopt the notation that the upper sign is for the zeroth order and the rest for all higher ones. Clearly this is true for
\delta_{[2}(\delta_{1]}EE^{-1})
, and also for
\delta EE^{-1}
directly. However, we find that

\delta_{[2}\delta_{1]}EE^{-1}=\delta_{[2}(\delta_{1]}EE^{-1})-\tfrac{1}{2}[\delta_{2}EE^{-1},\delta_{1}EE^{-1}]

(40)

cannot just be written in terms of generators in
K_{\pm}
because of the last term on the right-hand side, originating from the transformation of
E^{-1}
. It includes both chiral and mixed chiral
K_{AB}
generators, enabling us to write

\displaystyle[\delta_{2}EE^{-1},\delta_{1}EE^{-1}]

\displaystyle=[\delta_{2}EE^{-1},\delta_{1}EE^{-1}]\Big|_{K_{\mp}}

(41)

\displaystyle+[\delta_{2}EE^{-1},\delta_{1}EE^{-1}]\Big|_{K_{\pm}}\,,

where
{K_{\mp}}
denotes the complement of
{K_{\pm}}
, namely mixed chiral terms in the zeroth order (although vanishing) and chiral terms for higher ones.

Having these at hand, we can now calculate
\delta_{[2}\delta_{1]}EE^{-1}
directly from (
38
). Applying all variations and taking into account that
D\xi
transforms as

\delta\left(D\xi\right)=[\delta EE^{-1},D\xi]+D(\delta\xi)\,,

(42)

we are left with

\displaystyle 2\delta_{[2}

\displaystyle\delta_{1]}EE^{-1}=-2[\delta_{[2}EE^{-1},D\xi_{1]}]-D\left(2\delta_{[2}\xi_{1]}\right)

(43)

\displaystyle-2\delta_{[2}\xi_{1]R}+2\mathcal{A}^{-1}\delta_{[2}\xi_{1]}\mathcal{A}+[\mathcal{A}^{-1}\delta_{2}\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{-1}\delta_{1}\mathcal{A}]

\displaystyle-2[\mathcal{A}^{-1}\xi_{[2}\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{-1}\delta_{1]}\mathcal{A}]-2\mathcal{A}^{-1}\delta_{[2}\delta_{1]}\mathcal{A}\Big|_{K_{\pm}}

\displaystyle-[\delta_{2}EE^{-1},\delta_{1}EE^{-1}]\,.

Next, we eliminate
\mathcal{A}^{-1}\delta\mathcal{A}
by using (
12
) again and thereby obtain

\displaystyle 2\delta_{[2}

\displaystyle\delta_{1]}EE^{-1}=[D\xi_{2},D\xi_{1}]

(44)

\displaystyle-D\left(2\delta_{[2}\xi_{1]}\right)+2[D\xi_{[2},\xi_{1]R}]

IV
Torsion constraints

To compare the leading orders of our gGSt (
37
) and its gauge algebra with results in the literature, we have to finally solve the torsion constraints to eliminate
A
. All required steps are demonstrated in detail in
Gitsis and Hassler 2025
. Here, we just want to emphasize the subtlety that the torsion constraints also contain a second type of generators,
t_{\alpha}
, which are related to
\tau_{\alpha}
by the similarity transformation

t_{\alpha}=S_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}\tau_{\beta}\,.

(59)

Tensors in this alternative basis for the Lie algebra of
G_{\mathrm{S}}
are marked with a tilde, for example

\widetilde{\xi}=\xi^{\alpha}t_{\alpha}\qquad\text{and}\qquad A_{A}{}^{\beta}t_{\beta}=\widetilde{A}_{A}{}^{\beta}\tau_{\beta}\,.

(60)

One encounters the same challenge for the partial gauge fixing: In (
76
)-(
79
), we find
\widetilde{\xi}
on the left-hand side, while the right-hand side only contains the untilded version
\xi
. Hence, similarity transformations have to be performed recursively. Rather than
S_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}
, which is relatively simple, its more complicated inverse
(S^{-1})_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}
is needed. It arises from a sum of increasing powers of
f_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\gamma}
. As a result, many, although redundant, terms contribute to
\xi
and
A
and complicate the computations. We remedy this challenge by a procedure called “collapsing towers”, a scheme to simplify this sum by sending the dimension of
G_{\mathrm{S}}
to infinity.

IV.1
Generalized Green-Schwarz for odd number of derivatives

Expanding (
37

n=0,2,4
the same results arise as the ones presented in
Gitsis and Hassler 2025

\alpha^{\prime}

V
Conclusions

We succeeded in computing gGSt to all orders in
\alpha^{\prime}
. The key to obtain the simple transformation law (
37
) is to decouple torsion constraints from the partial gauge fixing by following the strategy we developed in
Gitsis and Hassler 2025
. Moreover, we identified a preferred parameterization of the field
\mathcal{A}
in (
23
). Although they do not qualitatively change any of our conclusions, alternative choices of
\mathcal{A}
will lead to much more complicated expressions. Furthermore, it is possible to show that our all-order gGSt close after combining them with generalized diffeomorphisms. The resulting transformations are parameterized by (
54
) and (
57
). As a consistency check, we also computed the final version of the transformations and their algebra just in terms of the generalized frame
E_{A}{}^{I}
and its flat derivatives. As expected, our results match with the gBdRi
Baron et al. 2018
;
Baron and Marques 2021
.

As pointed out by
Hronek and Wulff 2021
;
Hsia et al. 2024
, the O(
d
,
d
) symmetry discussed here is not able to capture all
\alpha^{\prime}
-corrections which arise in string theory. It only captures a subsector. Therefore a very important, but also complicated, problem is to understand if this symmetry can be extended/adapted such that all corrections which arise in string theory are accommodated. But even without such an extension, gGSt are definitely important to get a better handle on higher-derivative constructions for generalized dualities
Hassler and Rochais 2020
;
Borsato and Wulff 2020
;
Codina and Marques 2020
. Another interesting direction originates from the fact that the partial gauge fixing (
21
) and the torsion constraints we used here are not fixed uniquely. It has been demonstrated that an alternative realization of the torsion constraints produces rather simple results for gauge transformations and the action
Hronek et al. 2022
. Thus, it would be interesting to see if there is a preferred choice that simplifies the final expressions written in terms of the metric and the
B
-field.

Constructing a nomogram for the recurrence of tibial osteomyelitis after debridement surgery based on platelet to lymphocyte ratio at admission: a dual-center retrospective study from China
Authors: Rongbin Lu, Shaohuai Xia, Gaoyong Deng, Wencai Li, Zhao Huang, He Ling
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimTo construction of a nomogram to predict the prognosis of patients with tibial osteomyelitis after debridement surgery.MethodThis study used binary logistic regression analysis to identify clinical independent predictive factors, and then used R language to construct a nomogram.ResultThe results showed that WBC (3.36[1.77–6.37])、LYM (0.29[0.15–0.54])、BAS (2.19[1.22–3.91])、FIB (0.27[0.14–0.54])、ALB (0.16[0.09–0.3])、TBIL (0.41[0.21–0.83])、D-Dimer (1.77[1.03–3.03])、CRP (0.43[0.2–0.91])、ESR (3.08[1.6–5.79]) and PLR (0.47[0.24–0.9]) were independent predictive factors. The constructed nomogram can help clinicians assess and treat patients early, benefiting more patients.Keywords: Tibial osteomyelitis, Debridement surgery, Prognosis, Nomogram

Introduction

Osteomyelitis is a destructive bone disease characterized by the gradual degradation of the bone and the corresponding inflammatory response, which can potentially damage surrounding normal tissues [1]. In Zalavras’ study [2], the incidence of osteomyelitis in open fractures ranged from 2 to 50%. Diabetic foot patients often experience osteomyelitis in the foot [3]. Osteomyelitis is generally difficult to cure, with a recurrence rate of up to 48% in chronic cases [4].

Early diagnosis is key to successful treatment of osteomyelitis. Current treatment modalities for osteomyelitis primarily involve surgery and antibiotic therapy [5]. However, antibiotic therapy can have difficulty reaching the site of infection, making surgical debridement the mainstay of successful surgical treatment for osteomyelitis, aiming to reduce infection, remove necrotic tissue, and decrease the bacterial burden at the infection site [6]. Due to the complexity of each patient’s condition, personalized surgical and medical plans are often required [7], and a single surgery may not always yield satisfactory results. Having a clinical tool to assist clinicians in predicting the risk of recurrence in patients would be of significant importance in formulating subsequent treatment plans for patients.

Pre-admission blood parameter tests are readily available to clinicians and provide intuitive tools that reflect the patient’s status. In addition to the gold standard of bone biopsy results and imaging examinations, laboratory evaluations (including erythrocyte sedimentation rate, complete blood cell count, C-reactive protein, and blood culture results) can also actively predict osteomyelitis [8]. The elevation of relevant inflammatory markers can also be clinically correlated [9].

Nomograms are widely used in medical prognosis prediction, including gynecology [10], surgery [11, 12], and critical care medicine [13], among others. The use of nomograms is user-friendly and easily accessible, and it can provide important assistance to clinicians in estimating the course of the disease [13]. Therefore, this study aims to collect pre-admission routine blood tests and biochemical examination results from patients and construct a predictive model for the recurrence of tibia osteomyelitis within three months after surgical debridement based on the platelet to lymphocyte ratio (PLR) at admission. The goal is to provide a non-invasive, early, and convenient prediction tool to help clinicians make timely decisions, utilizing the simplest approach possible.

Materials and methods

Patient section

This is a retrospective cohort study conducted at two large medical centers in China. The study collected data from patients diagnosed with tibia osteomyelitis between February 2015 and January 2023. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Patients diagnosed with tibia osteomyelitis based on at least one of the following criteria: (a) Standard imaging examinations such as X-rays, computed tomography, or magnetic resonance imaging showing classic signs of osteomyelitis, such as bone necrosis, periosteal reaction, or bone destruction; (b) Positive bacterial tissue cultures from tibia biopsies; (c) Confirmation of the presence of pus during surgery. (2) The primary surgical approach for all patients was debridement, including intralesional curettage, intramedullary reaming, or radical debridement. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Inability to obtain admission blood test results; (2) Diagnosis of osteomyelitis in multiple long bones; (3) Recent use of anticoagulants or antiplatelet drugs; (4) Coexistence of immune or hematological disorders; (5) Absence of surgical treatment; (6) Segmental bone resection as the primary surgical approach. The eligible patients from the First Affiliated Hospital of Guangxi Medical University were included as the training group, while those from the Affiliated Hospital of Guilin Medical University were used as the external validation group.

Since this is a retrospective study, it was approved by the ethics committee in accordance with national laws and institutional consent, and patient informed consent was not required. In this study, the patients’ personal identifiable information was anonymized to ensure privacy and confidentiality.

Data collection and definition

This study collected baseline clinical data and laboratory test results including complete blood count, blood biochemistry, and coagulation function from the patients. The baseline clinical data consisted of gender, age, height, weight, body mass index (BMI), history of hypertension, history of diabetes, history of hepatitis, history of tuberculosis, history of alcohol consumption, and history of smoking. A history of hypertension was defined as a prior diagnosis of hypertension or a systolic blood pressure (SBP)/diastolic blood pressure (DBP) of 140/90 mmHg or higher upon admission. A history of diabetes was defined as a prior diagnosis of diabetes or random blood glucose level greater than 11.1 mmol/L, or fasting blood glucose (FBG) level greater than 7 mmol/L. The baseline data from the two centers were compared to validate the comparability of the data between the two centers.

Additionally, this study collected laboratory test results from patients upon admission before surgery, including white blood cell count, red blood cell count, hemoglobin count, platelet count, mean corpuscular volume, absolute lymphocyte count, absolute neutrophil count, absolute monocyte count, absolute eosinophil count, absolute basophil count, platelet hematocrit, fibrinogen, albumin, aspartate aminotransferase, alanine aminotransferase, alanine aminotransferase, prothrombin time, activated partial thromboplastin time, total bilirubin, creatinine, urea, D-dimer, C-reactive protein, erythrocyte sedimentation rate, international normalized ratio, and more. The following formulas were used to calculate relevant standardized ratios: platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR) = platelet count/lymphocyte count; systemic immune-inflammation index (SII) = (neutrophil count × platelet count)/lymphocyte count; systemic inflammation response index (SIRI) = (neutrophil count × monocyte count)/lymphocyte count; neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) = neutrophil count/lymphocyte count; aspartate aminotransferase-to-neutrophil ratio (ANRI) = aspartate aminotransferase count/neutrophil count; gamma-glutamyl transferase-to-platelet ratio (GPR) = gamma-glutamyl transferase count (GGT)/platelet count; D-dimer-to-albumin ratio (DAR) = D-Dimer count/albumin count; monocyte-to-lymphocyte ratio (MLR) = monocyte count/lymphocyte count.

Outcome

In this study, the occurrence of recurrence within 3 months after debridement surgery in patients with tibia osteomyelitis was considered as the outcome event. Recurrence was defined as a positive result, while no recurrence was defined as a negative result. The gold standard for determining the recurrence of tibia osteomyelitis in this study was based on relevant imaging results including X-rays, computed tomography, or magnetic resonance imaging with classic signs of osteomyelitis, such as bone necrosis, periosteal reaction, or bone destruction or intraoperative collection of biological tissue for examination including microbiology and histology. Skilled radiologists, pathologists, and clinicians evaluated the relevant imaging data and histopathological specimens and provided diagnostic reports to determine the postoperative recurrence of tibia osteomyelitis.

Statistical analysis

In this study, baseline data comparison between the two centers was performed using SPSS 21.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL). Subsequently, the data from the training and validation groups were combined, and the clinical data and laboratory test results were compared between the groups based on the occurrence of recurrence. For continuous data, normality was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. If the data followed a normal distribution, it was presented as mean ± standard deviation, and group comparisons were made using independent one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). If the data did not follow a normal distribution, it was presented as median (25th percentile, 75th percentile), and group comparisons were made using the Kruskal-Wallis test. Categorical data were described using frequencies (percentages), and group comparisons were made using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. A two-sided p-value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

The optimal cutoff values, sensitivity, specificity, and other results of factors showing statistical differences in group comparisons were calculated using SPSS 21.0. GraphPad Prism 9.5.0 was used to generate receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves for factors with significant differences. The clinical factors were categorized using the optimal cutoff values, and then binary logistic regression analysis was performed to evaluate the independent predictive factors for recurrence.

The independent predictive factors were plotted as a nomogram using R Studio (version 4.2.2). The predictive performance of the model was evaluated using ROC curves and the area under the curve (AUC). The calibration plot was used to calculate the average error of the model, and the decision curve analysis (DCA) was performed to assess the clinical utility of the model. All tests were considered statistically significant if the two-sided p-value was less than 0.05.

A total of 501 patients diagnosed with tibial osteomyelitis were collected from two large medical centers for this study. However, 90 patients were excluded due to the aforementioned factors, resulting in a final inclusion of 411 patients for a retrospective analysis, as illustrated in Fig. 1. Among them, there were 252 patients in the training set and 159 patients in the validation set.

Nomogram construction and evaluation

To further validate the predictive power of various factors on the recurrence of debridement surgery in patients with tibial osteomyelitis, a nomogram model was constructed using R studio software based on the results of multivariate analysis (WBC, LYM, BAS, FIB, ALB, TBIL, D-Dimer, ESR, PLR). We used the data from the training group to build the nomogram, as shown in Fig. 4. The predictive performance of this nomogram was validated by drawing the ROC curve for the training group (Fig. 5A), with a C-index of 0.825, indicating good predictive performance. The calibration curve for the training group (Fig. 6A) suggested an average error of 0.034, and the decision curve analysis (DCA) for the training group (Fig. 7A) indicated that there was a good clinical benefit for threshold probabilities between 0.01 and 0.66. The model was also validated using the data from the validation group; the ROC curve was plotted (Fig. 5B), showing a C-index of 0.843, the calibration curve (Fig. 6B) indicated an average error of 0.038, and the DCA (Fig. 7B) showed good clinical benefit for threshold probabilities between 0.01 and 0.92. It is clear that the nomogram demonstrated good predictive performance in both the training and validation groups, with small errors and the potential to benefit a large portion of clinical patients.

Discussion

Accurately estimating the likelihood of recurrence in patients is a major challenge in devising treatment plans for those with osteomyelitis. Apart from the surgical approach, changes in preoperative blood indicators are one of the more readily available pieces of information during a patient’s clinical course and can directly reflect the current status of the patient. This study, through retrospective analysis of patients’ preoperative blood indicators, provides clinicians with a simple and effective tool for planning treatment strategies.

Our results showed that WBC, LYM, BAS, FIB, ALB, TBIL, D-Dimer, ESR, and PLR were independent predictors of tibial osteomyelitis recurrence. There have been few studies on the recurrence of tibial osteomyelitis in the past. Xiaohua Wang et al. [14] found that the recurrence rate of tibial infection (16.22%) was higher than that of femur (8.70%), and the recurrence rate of post-traumatic osteomyelitis (14.66%) was significantly higher than that of hematogenous osteomyelitis (2.41%). Repeated surgery (≥ 3 times), post-traumatic osteomyelitis, and primary internal fixation are risk factors for recurrence of infection. Yalikun A et al. [15] proposed that Pseudomonas aeruginosa infection, bone exposure, number of previous surgeries (> 3 times), and diabetes were associated with postoperative recurrence or reinfection of tibial osteomyelitis. Our results are the first to propose a predictive relationship between serological indicators such as inflammatory factors and the recurrence of tibial osteomyelitis.

The treatment of osteomyelitis requires a multifaceted approach. In addition to surgical intervention, antibiotic therapy is an indispensable component of the treatment course. Debridement is often part of the initial treatment, and surgical debridement following antibiotic therapy can significantly shorten hospital stays, reduce treatment costs, and significantly decrease the incidence of postoperative complications [8]. Open bone injuries often lead to complications of osteomyelitis. When bone is exposed to the external environment, the bone cells and the organic extracellular matrix components that produce bone are ideal colonization targets for microbes, especially Staphylococcus aureus [16]. Once established, S. aureus can produce a biofilm, making the infection persistent [17, 18]. Additionally, S. aureus can produce a variety of toxins, which may encourage adherence of organisms to tissues leading to re-colonization and reinfection [19], which may be one of the reasons why osteomyelitis is difficult to cure completely and prone to recurrence.

Many studies have confirmed that abnormalities in white blood cell count (WBC) are very common in patients with infections [20, 21]. Research by Rimmer et al. suggests that different trajectories of WBC count increase can lead to higher mortality rates in patients with infections [22]. In burn patients, the initial white blood cell count has excellent predictive ability for subsequent bloodstream infections [23]. Beyond standard hematologic inflammatory markers used in the clinic, the platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR) serves as a standardized inflammatory predictor in various aspects. Wang et al., through research on PLR and other related inflammatory factors, constructed a nomogram to predict the probability of pneumonia related to stroke and ICU admission in patients with lung infections [24]. Peng et al. built a more accurate prognostic model than the current staging system for nasopharyngeal carcinoma patients based on PLR and other inflammatory factors [25]. Apart from common blood markers, composite indicators should also be valued in our clinical work. Nomograms constructed by combining traditional blood markers and composite indicators will be more beneficial for the formulation of treatment plans for patients.

This study has several strengths: (1) It is a dual-center retrospective study from China, which has developed a model through a training set and validated it with an external validation set. The model is reliable and simple to apply. (2)Routine blood and biochemical tests are commonly performed at the time of patient admission. This study aims to collect relevant data upon admission to achieve very early prediction and potentially benefit more patients. However, there are also limitations to this study: (1) It does not track postoperative and long-term incidence rates in patients. (2) Future prospective studies could be introduced to further validate the results of this retrospective study.

Therefore, the current study cannot draw any conclusions about the efficacy of treatments (such as the application of local antibiotics, hyperbaric oxygen therapy [26], platelet-rich plasma therapy [27], etc.) and the nomogram obtained from our study should not be used to guide clinical decisions in isolation because it does not analyze functional prognosis.

Conclusion

Patients admitted with tibial osteomyelitis demonstrate that WBC, LYM, BAS, FIB, ALB, TBIL, D-Dimer, CRP, ESR, and PLR are nomogram constructed based on the relevant blood markers and PLR can assist clinicians in gauging the likelihood of early recurrence following tibial osteomyelitis surgery. This facilitates early prevention and treatment, benefiting a larger patient population.

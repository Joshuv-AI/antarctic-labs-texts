Arborescences of Random Covering Graphs
Muchen Ju, Junjie Ni, Kaixin Wang, Yihan Xiao

Abstract
. A rooted arborescence of a directed graph is a spanning tree directed towards a particular vertex. A recent work of Chepuri et al. [ 1 ] showed that the arborescences of a covering graph of a directed graph G G are closely related to the arborescences of G G . In this paper, we study the weighted sum of arborescences of a random covering graph and give a formula for the expected value, resolving a conjecture of Chepuri et al. [ 1 ] .



1.
Introduction

For a weighted directed graph
\Gamma
, an
arborescence rooted at a vertex

v
is a spanning tree whose edges are directed towards
v
. The weight of an arborescence is the product of the edge weights. We define
A_{v}(\Gamma)
to be the weighted sum of all arborescences rooted at
v
.

Covering graphs are central objects in topological graph theory motivated by the notion of covering spaces in topology. A weighted digraph
\tilde{\Gamma}
is a covering graph of
\Gamma
defined in the topological sense (see section
3
for details). The fundamental work of Gross-Tucker
[
4
]
gave a characterization of covering graphs by permutation voltage assignments.

In their study of
R
-systems, Galashin and Pylyavskyy
[
3
]
observed that the ratio of arborescences of a covering graph and its base graph
A_{\tilde{v}}(\tilde{\Gamma})\over A_{v}(\Gamma)
is invariant of the choice of the root
v
. More recently, Chepuri et al.
[
1
]
further investigated the ratio and proved that it is always an integer-coefficient polynomial in the edge weights of the base graph
\Gamma
conjecturally with positive coefficients. Following up these works, we study the expected value of the ratio
A_{\tilde{v}}(\tilde{\Gamma})\over A_{v}(\Gamma)
when
\tilde{\Gamma}
is taken to be a random covering graph. Our main theorem is a formula for its expected value, affirming Conjecture 5.6 of
[
1
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,E,\mathrm{wt})
be a weighted digraph, and
k
be a positive integer. We choose a permutation voltage
\sigma_{e}
in the symmetric group
S_{k}
for each edge
e\in E
independently and uniformly at random. The random voltages allow us to construct our random
k
-fold covering graph
\tilde{\Gamma}
. Then

\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{A_{\tilde{v}}(\tilde{\Gamma})}{A_{v}(\Gamma)}\right]=\frac{1}{k}\prod_{w\in V}\left(\sum_{\alpha\in E_{s}(w)}\mathrm{wt}(\alpha)\right)^{k-1}

where
E_{s}(w)
is the set of edges in
\Gamma
with source
w
.

The paper is organized as follows. In section
2
we introduce the relevant background in graph theory, including the Matrix-Tree Theorem. In section
3
, we state the definition of covering graphs and review the theory of arborescence ratio in
[
1
]
. In section
4
we prove our main result, Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Background On Graph Theory

In this paper, we consider directed multigraphs, or
digraphs
for short. Unless otherwise stated, we consider digraphs with weighted edges.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G=(V,E,\mathrm{wt})
be a weighted digraph with
V=\{v_{1},\cdots,v_{|V|}\}
. For a vertex
v\in V
, define
E_{s}(v)
to be the set of edges with source
v
and
E_{t}(v)
to be the set of edges with terminal
v
. The
degree matrix

D
of
G
is the
|V|\times|V|
diagonal matrix with

D[v_{i},v_{i}]=\sum_{e\in E_{s}(v_{i})}\mathrm{wt}(e)

for all
v_{i}\in V
. The
adjacency matrix

A
of
G
is defined as

A[v_{i},v_{j}]=\sum_{e=(v_{i}\to v_{j})}\mathrm{wt}(e)

for all
v_{i},v_{j}\in V
. The
Laplacian matrix

L
of
G
is defined as
D-A
.

Example 2.2
.

Let
G
be a weighted digraph with vertex set
\{1,2,3\}
, with only one edge from
i
to
j
(not necessarily distinct) in
V(G)
with weight
x_{ij}
for all
i,j\in\{1,2,3\}
. Then

D=\begin{bmatrix}x_{11}+x_{12}+x_{13}&0&0\\
0&x_{21}+x_{22}+x_{23}&0\\
0&0&x_{31}+x_{32}+x_{33}\end{bmatrix}

A=\begin{bmatrix}x_{11}&x_{12}&x_{13}\\
x_{21}&x_{22}&x_{23}\\
x_{31}&x_{32}&x_{33}\end{bmatrix}.

Hence

L=D-A=\begin{bmatrix}x_{12}+x_{13}&-x_{12}&-x_{13}\\
-x_{21}&x_{21}+x_{23}&-x_{23}\\
-x_{31}&-x_{32}&x_{31}+x_{32}\end{bmatrix}.

Note that the Laplacian is always a singular matrix, since the sum of entries in each row is 0.

Definition 2.3
.

An
arborescence

T
of a weighted digraph
\Gamma
rooted at
v\in V
is a spanning tree directed towards
v
. The
weight
of an arborescence is the product of the weights of its edges:

\mathrm{wt}(T)=\prod_{e\in T}\mathrm{wt}(e).

We define
A_{v}(\Gamma)
to be the sum of weights of all arborescences.

The Matrix-Tree Theorem
[
2
]
relates the minors of the Laplacian and the arborescences.

Theorem 2.4

(Matrix-Tree Theorem
[
2
]
)
.

Let
G
be a weighted digraph with vertex set
\{1,\cdots,n\}
. Then

A_{i}(G)=\det(L(G)_{i}^{i})

for all
i\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
. Here
L(G)_{i}^{i}
is the Laplacian matrix
L(G)
with row
i
and column
i
removed.

Example 2.5
.

In example
2.2
with
n=3
,

L_{1}^{1}=\begin{bmatrix}x_{21}+x_{23}&-x_{23}\\
-x_{32}&x_{31}+x_{32}\end{bmatrix}.

Then

\det(L_{1}^{1})=(x_{21}+x_{23})(x_{31}+x_{32})-(-x_{23})(-x_{32})=x_{21}x_{31}+x_{23}x_{31}+x_{21}x_{32}=A_{1}(G).

The last equality holds since there are 3 arborescences rooted at
1
: namely
2,3\to 1,2\to 3\to 1
and
3\to 2\to 1
.

3.
Covering Graphs

A
k
-fold cover
of a weighted digraph
\Gamma=(V,E,\mathrm{wt})
is a weighted digraph
\tilde{\Gamma}=(\tilde{V},\tilde{E},\mathrm{wt})
that is a
k
-fold covering space of
G
in the topological sense that preserves edge weight. That is, we require that a lifted edge in the covering graph to have the same weight as its corresponding base edge in the base graph. In order to use this definition, we need to find a way to formally topologize directed graphs in a way that encodes edge orientation. To avoid this, we instead give a more concrete alternative definition of a covering graph.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
k
be a positive integer and
\Gamma=(V,E,\mathrm{wt})
be a weighted digraph. A weighted digraph
\tilde{\Gamma}=(\tilde{V},\tilde{E},\mathrm{wt})
is a
k
-fold covering graph
of
\Gamma
if there exists a projection map
\pi:\tilde{\Gamma}\rightarrow\Gamma
such that

(1)

\pi
maps vertices to vertices and edges to edges;

(2)

|\pi^{-1}(v)|=|\pi^{-1}(e)|=k
for all
v\in V,e\in E
;

(3)

If
\tilde{e}\in\tilde{E}
is an edge from
\tilde{v}
to
\tilde{w}
, then
\pi(\tilde{e})
is an edge from
\pi(\tilde{v})
to
\pi(\tilde{w})
;

(4)

For all
\tilde{e}\in\tilde{E}
, we have
\mathrm{wt}(\tilde{e})=\mathrm{wt}(\pi(\tilde{e}))
;

(5)

\pi
is a local homeomorphism. Equivalently,
|E_{s}(\tilde{v})|=|E_{s}(\pi(\tilde{v}))|
and
|E_{t}(\tilde{v})|=|E_{t}(\pi(\tilde{v}))|
for all
\tilde{v}\in\tilde{V}
.

When we refer to
\tilde{\Gamma}
as a covering graph of
\Gamma
, we fix a distinguished projection
\pi:\tilde{\Gamma}\rightarrow\Gamma
.

We do not require a covering graph to be connected. However, disconnected graphs contain no arborescences, so our main quantity of interest
A_{\tilde{v}}(\tilde{\Gamma})
is always
0
in the disconnected case.

Definition 3.2
.

Fix an integer
k
. A
weighted permutation-voltage graph

\Gamma=(V,E,\mathrm{wt},\nu)
is a weighted directed multigraph with each edge
e
also labeled by a permutation
\nu(e)=\sigma_{e}\in S_{k}
, the symmetric group on
k
letters. This labeling is called the
voltage
of the edge
e
. Note that the voltage of an edge
e
is independent of the weight of
e
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,E,\mathrm{wt})
be a weighted digraph with a permutation in
\sigma_{e}\in S_{k}
for every
e\in E
, the
k
-fold covering graph
\tilde{\Gamma}=(\tilde{V},\tilde{E},\mathrm{wt})
associated to

(\sigma_{e})_{e\in E}
is a digraph with vertex set
\tilde{V}=V\times\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
and edge set

\displaystyle\tilde{E}:=\left\{\left(v\times x,w\times\sigma_{e}(x)\right):x\in\{1,\ldots,k\},e=(v,w)\in E\right\}.

For an edge
e=(v,w)
of weight
\mathrm{wt}(e)
, all corresponding
\left(v\times x,w\times\sigma_{e}(x)\right)
also have weight
\mathrm{wt}(e)
.

Theorem 3.4

(
[
4
]
, Gross-Tucker’s characterization)
.

Every covering graph of
\Gamma
can be constructed from a permutation voltage graph
\Gamma
.

By this characterization result, we can use only definition
3.3
to analyze
k
-fold covering graphs from now on.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
\{v_{1},\cdots,v_{n}\}
be the set of vertices of our digraph
\Gamma

\tilde{\Gamma}
be a

A_{\tilde{v}}(\tilde{\Gamma})\over A_{v}(\Gamma)
as follows

\mathbb{E}[R(\tilde{\Gamma})]=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{A_{\tilde{v}}(\tilde{\Gamma})}{A_{v}(\Gamma)}\right]=\frac{1}{k}\prod_{w\in V}\left(\sum_{\alpha\in E_{s}(w)}\mathrm{wt}(\alpha)\right)^{k-1}

where
E_{s}(w)
is the set of edges in
\Gamma
with source
w
.

Say the vertices of
\Gamma
are
v_{1},\cdots,v_{n}
and edges
\{e=(v_{i}\to v_{j},\sigma_{e})\},
where
\sigma_{e}\in S_{k}
is a random permutation.

Recall

R(\tilde{\Gamma}):=\frac{A_{\tilde{v}}(\tilde{\Gamma})}{A_{v}(\Gamma)}=\frac{1}{k}\det\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{k}\det(D-\mathcal{A})

where
D,\mathcal{A}
are square matrices with rows/columns labeled
v_{1}^{2},\cdots,v_{1}^{k},v_{2}^{2},\cdots,v_{2}^{k},

\cdots,v_{n}^{2},\cdots,v_{n}^{k}
, in this order.

Here
D
is a deterministic diagonal matrix satisfying

D[v_{i}^{t},v_{i}^{t}]=\sum_{e=(v_{i}\to v_{j})}\mathrm{wt}(e)

and
\mathcal{A}
is a random matrix with

\mathcal{A}[v_{i}^{t},v_{j}^{r}]=\sum_{e=(v_{i}\to v_{j}),\sigma_{e}(t)=r}\mathrm{wt}(e)-\sum_{e=(v_{i}\to v_{j}),\sigma_{e}(t)=1}\mathrm{wt}(e)

where the
\sigma_{e}\in S_{k}
are chosen independently and uniformly at random.

Thus, Theorem
1.1
is equivalent to showing that our random matrix
\mathcal{A}
satisfies

\mathbb{E}[\det(D-\mathcal{A})]=\det(D).

Define
S:=\{v_{1}^{2},\cdots,v_{1}^{k},v_{2}^{2},\cdots,v_{2}^{k},\cdots,v_{n}^{2},\cdots,v_{n}^{k}\}
.

For each edge
e
of
\Gamma
, let
X_{e,a,b}=\mathbb{1}_{\{\sigma_{e}(a)=b\}}
, then we can write

\mathcal{A}[v_{i}^{t},v_{j}^{r}]=\sum_{e=(v_{i}\to v_{j})}\mathrm{wt}(e)(X_{e,t,r}-X_{e,t,1})

where we group together
X_{e,t,r},X_{e,t,1}
; we naturally define
Y_{e,t,r}:=X_{e,t,r}-X_{e,t,1}
.

Example 4.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a digraph on one vertex, with two loops weighted
a,b
. For
k=3
, the matrix
\mathcal{L}=D-\mathcal{A}
is as follows:

\begin{bmatrix}a+b-a(X_{a,2,2}-X_{a,2,1})-b(X_{b,2,2}-X_{b,2,1})&-a(X_{a,2,3}-X_{a,2,1})-b(X_{b,2,3}-X_{b,2,1})\\
-a(X_{a,3,2}-X_{a,3,1})-b(X_{b,3,2}-X_{b,3,1})&a+b-a(X_{a,3,3}-X_{a,3,1})-b(X_{b,3,3}-X_{b,3,1})\end{bmatrix}

=\begin{bmatrix}a+b-aY_{a,2,2}-bY_{b,2,2}&-aY_{a,2,3}-bY_{b,2,3}\\
-aY_{a,3,2}-bY_{b,3,2}&a+b-aY_{a,3,3}-bY_{b,3,3}\end{bmatrix}.

Similarly, the matrix
\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\Gamma})
of the digraph in example
3.5
is of the form:

\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\Gamma})=\begin{bmatrix}a+b-aY_{a,2,2}&-aY_{a,2,3}&-bY_{b,2,2}&-bY_{b,2,3}&0&0\\
-aY_{a,3,2}&a+b-aY_{a,3,3}&-bY_{b,3,2}&-bY_{b,3,3}&0&0\\
0&0&c&0&-cY_{c,2,2}&-cY_{c,2,3}\\
0&0&0&c&-cY_{c,3,2}&-cY_{c,3,3}\\
-dY_{d,2,2}&-dY_{d,2,3}&-eY_{e,2,2}&-eY_{e,2,3}&d+e&0\\
-dY_{d,3,2}&-dY_{d,3,3}&-eY_{e,3,2}&-eY_{e,3,3}&0&d+e\\
\end{bmatrix}.

To compute
\det(\mathcal{L})
, we first choose a permutation
\rho
on
S=\{v_{i}^{t}\mid 1\leq i\leq n,2\leq t\leq k\}
to expand; the product corresponding to
\rho
is

sgn(\rho)\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq n\\
2\leq t\leq k\end{subarray}}\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\Gamma})_{v_{i}^{t},\rho(v_{i}^{t})}.

We define a
term
to be either
\mathrm{wt}(e)
that can be found on the diagonal of
\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\Gamma})
or a
-\mathrm{wt}(e)Y_{e,*,*}
. We expand each
\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\Gamma})_{v_{i}^{t},\rho(v_{i}^{t})}
into terms; for example, in example
3.5
,
\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\Gamma})_{1,1}
has three terms:
a,b
, and
-aY_{a,2,2}
.

We define a
monomial
to be a product of terms that arise when we expand
\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq n\\
2\leq t\leq k\end{subarray}}\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\Gamma})_{v_{i}^{t},\rho(v_{i}^{t})}
. For example, in example
3.5
, we can have
\rho=135624=v_{1}^{2}v_{2}^{2}v_{3}^{2}v_{3}^{3}v_{1}^{3}v_{2}^{3}
, and we choose terms
a,-bY_{b,3,2},-cY_{c,2,2},-cY_{c,3,3},-dY_{d,2,3},-eY_{e,3,3}
from
\mathcal{L}_{1,1},\mathcal{L}_{2,3},\mathcal{L}_{3,5},\mathcal{L}_{4,6},\mathcal{L}_{5,2},\mathcal{L}_{6,4}
, respectively. The corresponding monomial is, then,
-abc^{2}deY_{b,3,2}Y_{c,2,2}Y_{c,3,3}Y_{d,2,3}Y_{e,3,3}
.

For a monomial, we define
F
to be the set of
wt(e)
’s from the diagonal in the monomial. For each edge
e
, let
n_{e}
be the number of terms of the form
-\mathrm{wt}(e)Y_{e,*,*}
in the monomial, namely
-\mathrm{wt}(e)Y_{e,i_{e,1},j_{e,1}},-\mathrm{wt}(e)Y_{e,i_{e,2},j_{e,2}},\cdots,-\mathrm{wt}(e)Y_{e,i_{e,n_{e}},j_{e,n_{e}}}
where
i_{e,\alpha}\in\{2,\cdots,k\},j_{e,\alpha}\in\{1,\cdots,k\}
. Thus, the monomial can be written as

\left(\prod_{e^{\prime}\in F}\mathrm{wt}(e^{\prime})\right)\cdot\prod_{e\in E}\left(\prod_{l=1}^{n_{e}}(-\mathrm{wt}(e))Y_{e,i_{e,l},j_{e,l}}\right).

Note that in the formula, both
i_{e,1},\cdots,i_{e,n_{e}}
and
j_{e,1},\cdots,j_{e,n_{e}}
are pairwise distinct, and take values in
\{2,\cdots,n\}
.

Observe
\det(\mathcal{L})
is just a signed sum all of these monomials, where the sign comes from the sign of the permutation
\rho
.

Fix a sequence of nonnegative integers
(n_{e})_{e\in E}
. We consider monomials where we choose exactly
n_{e}
terms of the form
-\mathrm{wt}(e)Y_{e,*,*}
for every
e\in E
. Every such monomial can be written as

\left(\prod_{e^{\prime}\in F}\mathrm{wt}(e^{\prime})\right)\cdot\prod_{e\in E}(-\mathrm{wt}(e))^{n_{e}}\cdot\left(\prod_{l=1}^{n_{e}}Y_{e,i_{e,l},j_{e,l}}\right).

This means when we sum all these monomials, every term shares the same
\left(\prod_{e^{\prime}\in F}\mathrm{wt}(e^{\prime})\right)\cdot\prod_{e\in E}(-\mathrm{wt}(e))^{n_{e}}
factor, so we only need to look at the expected value of the (signed) sum of the
\prod_{e\in E}\prod_{l=1}^{n_{e}}Y_{e,i_{e,l},j_{e,l}}
’s.

If
n_{e}=0
for all
e\in E
, then we only choose terms of the form
\mathrm{wt}(e)
in the monomial; we did not choose any term of the form
-\mathrm{wt}(e)Y_{e,*,*}
. In this case, we have chosen terms arising only from
D
, and we must have
\rho=id\in S_{k}
. Thus the sum of all these terms is
\det(D)
. We did not need to take an expectation, since these terms are deterministic.

It remains to show if there exists
e\in E
such that
n_{e}>0
, then the corresponding sum is
0
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
e=(x\to y)\in E
be a fixed edge where
x,y\in[n]
,
t\in\{1,\cdots,k-1\}
be a fixed integer. Fix distinct
i_{1},\cdots,i_{t}\in\{2,\cdots,k\}
, distinct
j_{1},\cdots,j_{t}\in\{2,\cdots,k\}
and a bijection
\pi\colon\{i_{1},\cdots,i_{t}\}\to\{j_{1},\cdots,j_{t}\}
. Then

\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{l=1}^{t}Y_{e,i_{l},j_{l}}\right]

depends only on
t
; furthermore when
t=1
the this is equal to 0.

Proof.

Note that we only need to choose
\sigma_{e}\in S_{k}
uniformly at random here. Observe if
\tau\colon\{i_{1},\cdots,i_{t}\}\to[k]
is a map, then

\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{l=1}^{t}X_{e,i_{l},\tau(i_{l})}\right]=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }\tau\text{ is not injective; }\\
\frac{(k-t)!}{k!}&\text{ if }\tau\text{ is injective }\end{cases}

Hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{l=1}^{t}Y_{e,i_{l},\pi(i_{l})}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{l=1}^{t}(X_{e,i_{l},\pi(i_{l})}-X_{e,i_{l},1})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{l=1}^{t}X_{e,i_{l},\pi(i_{l})}\right]-\sum_{z=1}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq l\leq t\\
l\neq z\end{subarray}}X_{e,i_{l},\pi(i_{l})}\cdot X_{e,i_{z},1}\right]

\displaystyle=(1-t)\frac{(k-t)!}{k!}

∎

For this choice of
(n_{e})_{e\in E}
, choose an edge
e_{0}
such that
n_{e_{0}}>0
.

Case 1: The monomials have exactly one term of the form
Y_{e_{0},*,*}
, i.e.
n_{e_{0}}=1
. In this case, since the
\sigma_{e}
’s are independent random permutations, the expected value of every monomial factor through a term
\mathbb{E}[Y_{e_{0},*,*}]
, which is zero, so the contribution of this monomial to the expected value is 0.

Case 2: The monomials have
\geq 2
terms of the form
Y_{e_{0},*,*}
, i.e.
n_{e_{0}}\geq 2
.

Fix
i_{1}<\cdots<i_{n_{e_{0}}}\in\{2,\cdots,n\}
. Among the monomials associated to our fixed
(n_{e})_{e\in E}
, we focus on the monomials that contain a product
\prod_{l=1}^{n_{e_{0}}}Y_{e_{0},i_{e_{0},l},j_{e_{0},l}}
for some distinct integers
j_{e_{0},1},\cdots,j_{e_{0},n_{e_{0}}}\in\{2,\cdots,n\}
. We will show the expected value of the signed sum of these monomials is zero.

Again, by the independence of
\sigma_{e}
’s, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{e\in E}\prod_{l=1}^{n_{e}}Y_{e,i_{e,l},j_{e,l}}\right]=\prod_{e\in E}\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{l=1}^{n_{e}}Y_{e,i_{e,l},j_{e,l}}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{l=1}^{n_{e_{0}}}Y_{e_{0},i_{e_{0},l},j_{e_{0},l}}\right]\cdot\prod_{e\in E\setminus\{e_{0}\}}\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{l=1}^{n_{e}}Y_{e,i_{e,l},j_{e,l}}\right]

By Proposition
4.2
,

\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{e_{0},i_{e_{0},1},j_{e_{0},1}}Y_{e_{0},i_{e_{0},2},j_{e_{0},2}}\prod_{l=3}^{n_{e_{0}}}Y_{e_{0},i_{e_{0},l},j_{e_{0},l}}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{e_{0},i_{e_{0},1},j_{e_{0},2}}Y_{e_{0},i_{e_{0},2},j_{e_{0},1}}\prod_{l=3}^{n_{e_{0}}}Y_{e_{0},i_{e_{0},l},j_{e_{0},l}}\right]

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{e_{0},i_{e_{0},1},j_{e_{0},1}}Y_{e_{0},i_{e_{0},2},j_{e_{0},2}}\prod_{l=3}^{n_{e_{0}}}Y_{e_{0},i_{e_{0},l},j_{e_{0},l}}\right]\cdot\prod_{e\in E\setminus\{e_{0}\}}\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{l=1}^{n_{e}}Y_{e,i_{e,l},j_{e,l}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{e_{0},i_{e_{0},1},j_{e_{0},2}}Y_{e_{0},i_{e_{0},2},j_{e_{0},1}}\prod_{l=3}^{n_{e_{0}}}Y_{e_{0},i_{e_{0},l},j_{e_{0},l}}\right]\cdot\prod_{e\in E\setminus\{e_{0}\}}\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{l=1}^{n_{e}}Y_{e,i_{e,l},j_{e,l}}\right]

Denote
e=(x\to y)
where
x,y\in[n]
. The term
\prod_{e\in E}\prod_{l=1}^{n_{e}}Y_{e,i_{e,l},j_{e,l}}
appears in the signed sum from some permutation
\rho\in S_{n(k-1)}
. Furthermore, such
\rho
must satisfy
\rho(v_{x}^{i_{e,l}})=v_{y}^{j_{e,l}}
for all
e\in E,l=1,\cdots,n_{e}
, but
\rho
is not necessarily unique. In the computation of the signed sum, we need to account this term for every such
\rho
.

Write
e_{0}=(x_{0}\to y_{0})
. Consider any particular choice of
\rho
. Observe that the term

Y_{e_{0},i_{e_{0},1},j_{e_{0},2}}Y_{e_{0},i_{e_{0},2},j_{e_{0},1}}\prod_{l=3}^{n_{e_{0}}}Y_{e_{0},i_{e_{0},l},j_{e_{0},l}}\allowbreak\prod_{e\in E\setminus\{e_{0}\}}\prod_{l=1}^{n_{e}}Y_{e,i_{e_{0},l},j_{e_{0},l}}

is also in the sum; it comes from the permutation
(v_{y_{0}}^{j_{e_{0},1}},v_{y_{0}}^{j_{e_{0},2}})\circ\rho
. We define a map
f
that sends the pair

\left(\rho,Y_{e_{0},i_{e_{0},1},j_{e_{0},1}}Y_{e_{0},i_{e_{0},2},j_{e_{0},2}}\prod_{l=3}^{n_{e_{0}}}Y_{e_{0},i_{e_{0},l},j_{e_{0},l}}\prod_{e\in E\setminus\{e_{0}\}}\prod_{l=1}^{n_{e}}Y_{e,i_{e,l},j_{e,l}}\right)

to the pair

\left((v_{y_{0}}^{j_{e_{0},1}},v_{y_{0}}^{j_{e_{0},2}})\circ\rho,Y_{e_{0},i_{e_{0},1},j_{e_{0},2}}Y_{e_{0},i_{e_{0},2},j_{e_{0},1}}\prod_{l=3}^{n_{e_{0}}}Y_{e_{0},i_{e_{0},l},j_{e_{0},l}}\prod_{e\in E\setminus\{e_{0}\}}\prod_{l=1}^{n_{e}}Y_{e,i_{e,l},j_{e,l}}\right).

Since
f
is an involution, it defines a pairing (of pairs). And since the permutations in each pair have opposite signs, the signed sum of expected value of each pair is
0
. The conclusion follows.

Example 4.3
.

In example
4.1
, we arrived at

\mathcal{L}=\begin{bmatrix}a+b-aY_{a,2,2}-bY_{b,2,2}&-aY_{a,2,3}-bY_{b,2,3}\\
-aY_{a,3,2}-bY_{b,3,2}&a+b-aY_{a,3,3}-bY_{b,3,3}\end{bmatrix}

The monomial
(-aY_{a,*,*})(-bY_{b,*,*})
is associated to the sequence
n_{a}=n_{b}=1
. The monomial
(-aY_{a,*,*})(-aY_{a,*,*})
is associated to the sequence
n_{a}=2,n_{b}=0
. The monomial
(-aY_{a,*,*})b
is associated to the sequence
n_{a}=1,n_{b}=0
.

The sequence
n_{a}=n_{b}=1
is associated with four monomials:

(-aY_{a,2,2})(-bY_{b,3,3}),(-aY_{a,2,3})(-bY_{b,3,2}),(-aY_{a,3,2})(-bY_{b,2,3}),(-aY_{a,3,3})(-bY_{b,2,2})

The contribution of each monomial to the expected value is
0
since
\mathbb{E}[Y_{a,i,j}]=0
.

The sequence
n_{a}=2,n_{b}=0
is associated with monomials
(-aY_{a,2,2})(-aY_{a,3,3})
,
(-aY_{a,2,3})(-aY_{a,3,2})
. We pair the permutation
(id,Y_{a,2,2}Y_{a,3,3})
with
((2,3),Y_{a,2,3}Y_{a,3,2})
. The signed sum of these is
0
. This corresponds to expanding

\mathbb{E}[(-aY_{a,2,2})(-aY_{a,3,3})-(-aY_{a,2,3})(-aY_{a,3,2})]=0

as part of the computation of the expected value of the determinant of the matrix.

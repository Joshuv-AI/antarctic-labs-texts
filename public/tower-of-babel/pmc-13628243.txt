Reply
Authors: Takahiro Hirayama, Kiyoka Omoto, Tamami Watanabe, Shinobu Matsubara, Naoto Yamamoto
Published: 2026

We thank Xia et al for their thoughtful comments on our study and for providing an important perspective on the interpretation of bidirectional lymphatic flow observed using contrast-enhanced ultrasound (CEUS).

We agree that the potential influence of the contrast injection procedure should be considered when interpreting lymphatic flow on CEUS. However, in our examinations, perfluorobutane microbubbles were injected subcutaneously at four sites in the foot according to the lymphatic mapping protocol proposed by Shinaoka et al.1 The injection sites were therefore anatomically remote from the CEUS observation areas, which were located in the lower leg or thigh in all images presented in our report. Accordingly, direct local pressure or volume loading caused by the contrast injection is unlikely to account for the bidirectional microbubble movement observed at the imaging sites. We acknowledge that the injection sites were not described in sufficient detail in our original report and appreciate the opportunity to clarify this point.

We fully agree with the authors’ interpretation that dermal backflow (DBF) may represent a dynamic compensatory remodeling process of the lymphatic network. This interpretation is consistent with our observation that DBF formation involves both antegrade and retrograde lymphatic flow, rather than passive reflux alone. Although CEUS alone cannot determine the functional significance of this bidirectional movement, the authors’ proposed interpretation is plausible. Further investigation integrating dynamic imaging with functional assessment will be necessary to clarify its physiological and clinical significance.

We appreciate the authors’ constructive comments, which highlight the importance of integrating dynamic imaging findings with functional assessment to better understand the pathophysiologic significance of DBF.

Frequency selective surface based triple band multi slotted MIMO antenna for sub-6 GHz wireless applications
Authors: Ashish Kumar, Kinny Garg, Preeti Sharma, Ramesh Kumar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe recent demand of high data rate and reliable wireless communication systems has accelerated the development of compact multiband antennas with better radiation performance. A multiband slotted Multiple-Input Multiple-Output (MIMO) antenna based on Frequency Selective Surface (FSS) has been presented in this work. The proposed antenna operates at 3.4 GHz, 6.68 GHz, and 8.4 GHz and encompasses significant frequency bands of sub-6 GHz 5G, C-band wireless systems, and X-band communication solutions. Multiband operation is accomplished through introducing well-designed slots in the radiating patch which adjust the surface current distribution and produce a variety of resonant paths. The MIMO antenna design is used to increase the channel capacity, diversity functionality, and link reliability while maintaining a compact design. In addition to this, a periodic FSS layer is added to the antenna structure to boost the gain of the antenna in the range from 12 to 14 dBi, radiation efficiency and also to avoid undesired surface waves. Complex simulations and parametric analyses are conducted to test the performance of the antenna in terms of reflection coefficient, radiation patterns, gain and MIMO diversity parameters including envelope correlation coefficient (ECC), diversity gain (DG), Channel Capacity Loss (CCL), Total active reflection coefficient (TARC), Mean Effective Gain (MEG). The proposed antenna exhibits good impedance matching, and better antenna elements isolation at the target frequencies, The achieved findings suggest the proposed multiband FSS-accompanied MIMO antenna is one of the potential solutions of the next-generation multiband wireless communication systems.Keywords: MIMO, Frequency selective surface, Gain, Slotted antenna, Sub-6 GHz, 5GSubject terms: Engineering, Physics

Introduction

The high pace of wireless communication systems development has posed an immense pressure on the desire to achieve high data rates, low latency, enhanced spectral efficiency, and a reliable connection1,2. Various new technologies like fifth-generation (5G) and beyond-5G (B5G) communications, satellite communications, and advanced radar systems demand the use of antennas that can operate across multiple frequency bands with high gain and constant radiation properties3. With the development of smaller, more multifunctional wireless devices, the multiband antenna design has gained significant interest since it is now possible to make a single antenna system work at a wide range of frequencies rather than the multiple antennas, which reduces the complexity and cost of the system. The most popular choice of antenna type in modern wireless networks is the planar microstrip antennas, which has low profile, lightweight, easy fabrication, and can be used with printed circuit technology4,5. Conventional microstrip antennas, however, are usually narrow band and low gain. In order to address these shortcomings, a number of methods have been explored over time in the recent years such as slot loading, fractal geometries, parasitic elements and defected ground structures (DGS). Slotted antennas are useful in the attainment of multiband characteristics6. When slots are introduced, the current distribution in the radiating patch introduces additional resonance paths that exhibit different operating frequencies. As a result, slot geometries in either the radiating patch or ground plane can be designed to achieve multiband operating frequencies7.

As the wireless services are continually increasing, more data need supported by the antenna systems and the reliability of the links should also increase. The MIMO technology has become one of the solutions to fulfill these needs. MIMO systems allow a spatial multiplexing and diversity gain in the channel capacity by using more than one antenna on the transmitter and receiver, which do not need more spectral resources8,9. Besides, MIMO systems are able to reduce multipath fading and interference, which lead to more dependable wireless communication channels10. Despite of the above-mentioned advantages, incorporation of many antenna elements on a small platform brings challenges like mutual coupling of radiating elements11,12. Mutual coupling is harmful to such key MIMO performance metrics such as ECC, DG, and total radiation efficiency13. The literature has suggested several methods of minimizing mutual coupling of compact MIMO antenna arrays. These include neutralization lines, parasitic structures, stubs, defected ground structures (DGS) and orthogonal antenna element placement. Also slot-based radiators and specially designed feeding mechanisms have been presented to offer a better isolation level with small dimensions14,15.

High gain and effective radiative performance are another critical need of the modern wireless antennas. In order to address the above-mentioned issue of the compact planar antennas, researchers have investigated the application of artificial electromagnetic structures including metasurface and frequency selective surface (FSS)16,17. It is possible to control the electromagnetic waves in these structures and therefore achieve better radiation properties, less back radiation, and higher gain in the antennas18. FSSs are periodic arrays of metallic structures which have frequency-dependent transmission and reflection properties. FSS structures when combined with antenna systems can serve as reflective surfaces, superstrates or filtering layers to enhance the antenna performance19,20.

Recent studies have demonstrated that integrating FSS structures with MIMO antennas can significantly improve system performance. For instance, in21, An eight-port MIMO antenna integrated with a FSS is proposed for the 28 GHz millimeter-wave (n257/n261) bands. In similar work22, A compact CPW-fed UWB monopole antenna (20 × 25 mm²) is combined with a curved single-layer FSS reflector consisting of an 11 × 11 array of hexagonal-slotted unit cells to achieve a broad bandwidth of 2.66–17.98 GHz. Some of the researchers have presented the slotted MIMO antenna design. For instance, in23 a dual-band microstrip patch antenna operating at 28 GHz and 38 GHz with a 4-port MIMO configuration to enhance channel capacity and spatial diversity is presented. Further24 discusses a miniaturized slotted MIMO antenna array is given for 5G mm-wave communication and integrates machine learning regression models to predict antenna gain and performance and in25 the authors introduce a compact quad-port MIMO antenna using an inverted L-shaped radiating element and hexagonal defected ground structure (DGS) operating in the 25–29.5 GHz band. In similar manner, various designs have been reported in the literature26–30.

According to the above discussion, this paper proposes a multiband slotted MIMO antenna combined with a FSS for the modern wireless communication systems. The proposed antenna’s operating frequencies are 3.4 GHz, 6.68 GHz and 8.4 GHz which are significant frequencies in the frequency bands of sub-6 GHz 5G, C band and X-band satellite or radar. The 3.4 GHz band is part of the sub-6 GHz spectrum designated for 5G mid-band communications, which provides the balance of coverage and speed. The 6.68 GHz band is part of the upper C-band spectrum, which is emerging as a potential candidate for next-generation Wi-Fi (Wi-Fi 6E/7 extensions), fixed satellite communications, and modern radar technology. The 8.4 GHz band is in the X-band region, which is widely used for satellite communications, deep space communications and high-resolution radar systems because of its good propagation and bandwidth characteristics. Multiband operation is attained by introducing strategically designed slots to the radiating patch which changes the distribution of surface currents and forms multiple resonant paths. To achieve the desired performance of high-channel capacity and diversity, a MIMO configuration is adopted, in the form of compact size and high isolation between antenna elements. Moreover, periodic FSS structure is included as well to enhance radiation properties, reduce the surface wave propagation and increase the overall antenna gain. The antenna is studied using the detailed simulations and the parametric studies to examine the impedance matching, radiation patterns, gain performance of the antenna. The results indicate that the proposed antenna is an effective multiband antenna with better isolation and radiation characteristics, thus a good candidate to the multiband wireless communication systems.

Figure 3. shows how the proposed antenna geometry progressively evolved with simple radiating structure into a multi-resonant structure. In Step-I, a simple square microstrip patch fed by a microstrip line is used in which the patch size generates the fundamental resonance of the patch. In Step-II the edge slots are included to disrupt the distribution of current on the surface, effectively lengthening the path taken by current, adding more resonant modes and better impedance tuning. In Step-III, the patch is further modified by etching corner-shaped (L-shaped) slots that make many current loops and produce more resonances thus providing multi-band behavior. Lastly, in Step-IV, small square holes are cut into the structure, which adds more capacitance and inductance, and additional capacitive-inductive effects add to refine impedance matching, enhance gain and stabilize the multi-frequency operation. The system of progressive slot loading and structural perturbation provides a systematic approach of converting the original single-band patch into a multi-band antenna with better impedance properties and an enhanced level of resonance control. Also, the current distribution of each step is shown in Fig. 3e, In the preliminary step, the electric field is spread out, suggesting low resonance. With the addition of slots and other features, the confinement of field around edges and discontinuities shows stronger surface current concentration. These elements introduce capacitive (charge stored at slot edges) and inductive (increased current paths) effects, resulting in LC resonances. In the final step, more field confinement around the slots and radiators shows better coupling, impedance matching, and the introduction of multiple resonances, leading to better gain and bandwidth.

Frequency selective surface array structure

A FSS plate is a planar structure consisting of several unit cells spaced on both sides. The frequency at which the FSS reflects incident waves depends on factors such as unit cell design, mutual spacing, substrate characteristics, and angle of incidence. Unit cells can be designed as either a Slot or a Patch as depicted in the Fig. 6a. Patch FSS acts as a low-pass filter, while Slot FSS acts as a high-pass filter. The unit cell used in this work features a simple design comprising a square slot at the four corners of the square patch, developed on a Roger/RT Duroid substrate with εr = 2.2 and thickness h = 1.6 mm. The overall dimensions of the unit cell are 20 mm × 20 mm. A square patch of 18 mm × 18 mm is placed at the center of the substrate, from which a 4 square slots of 7 mm × 7 mm is etched out as depicted in Fig. 6b and tabulated in Table 3. The unit cell was designed using HFSS software with two Floquet port configurations. The design parameters of the FSS unit cell are selected in such a way that it must cover the wide bandwidth of the corresponding radiating antenna for which it is intended to reflect incident waves. Therefore, the size of the patch has been optimized as corresponding results have been shown in Fig. 6c. Additionally, a substantial dip in the S21 curve within the bandwidth of interest is required. The proposed single unit FSS cell achieved the wide bandwidth of 4.7 GHz (4.5–9.2 GHz) centred around 6.67 GHz with a maximum dip in the S-curve of around − 48 dB as shown in Fig. 6d. Beyond bandwidth and S21 notch level, the reflection phase of the FSS surface is also a critical parameter; it should decrease linearly to enable the required phase reflection to the radiating antenna. The proposed FSS is now modelled into an equivalent lumped LC network, as shown in the Fig. 6e. The metal strips, current paths are modelled as inductance (L) and the gaps between elements are modelled as capacitances (C), with two ports as shown on both sides. The periodic LC structure thus controls the resonance of the FSS surface. The extracted values of L (L ≈ 1.2 nH) and C (C ≈ 0.079 pF) lead to a resonance at 6.6 GHz, which is in agreement with the full-wave analysis, as shown in Fig. 6f. The circuit simulation (ADS) and full-wave simulation (HFSS) were compared in terms of S11 and S12 which are found in good agreement and prove the proposed equivalent circuit model.

MIMO antenna configuration

The two configurations of a 2-element MIMO antenna array and their corresponding S-parameter performance has been explained in this section and illustrated in Fig. 8. The single port antenna element has been scaled to a 1 × 2 MIMO antenna array by placing two identical antenna elements on a common substrate in two distinct spatial configurations to assess the behaviors of isolation and impedance matching. Figure 8a displays linear arrangement, in which both antenna elements are arranged side-by-side along an identical axis on the substrate with the size of 40 mm × 80 mm. In this design, the two components are co-polarized oriented in the same direction and the feeding ports are placed on the same side of the substrate. This configuration is more prone to increased mutual coupling between the elements, because the elements are oriented parallel and close together. Further, Fig. 8b represents the Orthogonal arrangement in which one antenna element is turned 90° with regard to another element on the same substrate. This orthogonal arrangement brings polarization diversity between the two elements, which in effect reduces mutual coupling and elevates port isolation, turn out to be more effective arrangement to implement MIMO. The S-parameter comparison of the two configurations in the frequency range of 1 to 10 GHz is provided Fig. 8c. S11 and S22 reflection coefficients of the two configurations exhibit excellent impedance matching at three different frequency ranges around 3.44 GHz, 6.88 GHz, and 8.44 GHz, which proves the existence of the tri-band nature of the antenna. the linear configuration has isolation of −18 dB and the orthogonal configuration has isolation of −51 dB at 3.44 GHz. Similar improvement pattern can be observed at other two resonating frequencies. Generally, the orthogonal setup will provide a greater isolation in the high-frequency bands because of the polarization variety added by the rotated element arrangement and thus is more appropriate in practice to MIMO applications where low mutual coupling is of utmost importance to enhance channel capacity and system functionality. Further, the improvement in the isolation has been verified by the current distribution as depicted in Fig. 8d. For the linear configuration, the surface currents clearly show that significant current is flowing from the excited element to the adjacent element, which suggests strong mutual coupling via near-field effects. This leads to increased energy leakage and consequently poor isolation. However, the surface current distribution for the orthogonal configuration indicates that the currents on the adjacent element are significantly reduced when the first element is excited. The orthogonal configuration changes the current flow and reduces the field coupling between the two elements.

2-element MIMO Configurations (a) Linear (b) Orthogonal (c) S-parameters (d) Current distribution.

Now, a further extension of the previously discussed 2-element MIMO array is now scaled up to incorporate four antenna elements on a single common substrate for enhanced MIMO functionality and spatial diversity. Figure 9a shows the physical configuration of 4 element MIMO antenna. The four elements are symmetrically placed on all the four corners of a square substrate and each element is orthogonally tilted to its neighbors. The orthogonal placement strategy directly expands the orthogonal configuration in the 2-element MIMO case where, by simply rotation of neighboring elements by 90°. It was observed that port isolation was enhanced by polarization diversity. All its elements are fed on the outer edge of the substrate, and the general structure provides a spatial distance and orthogonality of polarization between neighboring ports, which act together to reduce mutual coupling between all element pairs. The S-parameter results of the 4-element MIMO array at a frequency range of 2–9 GHz is shown in Fig. 9b. The reflection coefficients S11, S22, S33, and S44 overlap almost perfectly, which proves that all four elements are well-matched in the three operating frequency bands. As far as the mutual coupling is concerned, the transmission coefficients S12, S13 and S14 are the isolation between adjacent elements, diagonally opposite elements and other combinations of ports. The S13 and S14 parameters are smaller than − 50 dB over the majority of the band, which is a great sign of isolation between non- adjacent and diagonally opposite ports. The S12 parameter, which is an indicator of a coupling between adjacent ports, indicates a bit higher in terms of the level of coupling, but at a sufficiently low level, especially due to the orthogonal nature of the elements next to each other. The 4-element MIMO system is effective in ensuring good impedance matching and high inter-element isolation in all three frequency bands, which makes it effective in the requirement of modern wireless communication networks which require high data throughput and spatial multiplexing.

MIMO diversity parameters

The Diversity analysis of the presented triple band MIMO antenna system is required to ensure the reliable performance and enhanced diversity gain especially in multipath signal environments. Also, the adjacent radiating elements for the discussed MIMO antenna must be perfectly isolated to validate good diversity performance. The diversity performance is evaluated for the proposed MIMO antenna design in this section. The evaluated parameters are ECC, DG, and CCL, TARC and MEG to validate its utilisation multiband applications.

Unlike most prior works that support only single-band19,25,28 or dual-band26,27 operation, the proposed antenna uniquely achieves multiband operation spanning three distinct frequency bands at 3.4 GHz, 6.68 GHz, and 8.4 GHz, making it well-suited for next-generation multi-standard wireless systems. Furthermore, the proposed design achieves an isolation of > 40 dB, which is substantially higher than25(> 25 dB) and27(> 20 dB), while several comparative designs19,26,28 do not report isolation at all, underscoring the superior port decoupling of the proposed structure. The proposed work demonstrates clear novelty by achieving triple-band MIMO operation (3.4, 6.68, and 8.4 GHz) with an integrated FSS layer, offering significantly higher gain (~ 13.8 dBi) and isolation (> 40 dB) compared to existing designs. Additionally, it ensures compactness with an optimized FSS size and maintains a simple, fabrication-friendly structure.

Conclusion

A compact multiband slotted MIMO antenna with FSS has been introduced in this paper for the modern wireless communication. The antenna has the capability of working at 3.4 GHz, 6.68 GHz, and 8.4 GHz, and is therefore suitable in sub-6 GHz 5G, C-band wireless networks, and X-band communication and radar applications. The proposed design exhibits the improved peak realized gain in the range from 12 to 14 dBi and radiation efficiency ranges from 94 to 97% with the integration of the FSS structure beneath 4 element MIMO antenna array. The diversity parameters have also been analyzed to verify the enhanced channel capacity in the MIMO configuration. Finally, the simulated and measured results of the proposed design show good agreement, and future research may focus on incorporating advanced features such as beam steering, enhanced gain, and improved spectral efficiency in massive MIMO systems.

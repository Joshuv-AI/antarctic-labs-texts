Neutral and Charged Current Semi-Inclusive Deep-Inelastic Scattering at NNLO QCD
Leonardo Bonino, Thomas Gehrmann, Markus Löchner, Kay Schönwald, and Giovanni Stagnitto

Abstract
Semi-inclusive hadron production in deep inelastic lepton-nucleon scattering (SIDIS) provides important probes of parton distributions and fragmentation functions. We compute the next-to-next-to-leading order (NNLO) massless QCD corrections to the full set of SIDIS coefficient functions in analytical form, accounting for electroweak neutral current and charged current exchange. Focusing on the kinematical setting of SIDIS measurements at the future Electron-Ion Collider (EIC), we quantify the impact of these corrections on the phenomenological predictions and their associated uncertainties. We investigate the impact of electroweak interference in the neutral current SIDIS process and investigate lepton polarisation asymmetries designed to enhance the sensitivity on charged current SIDIS.

Introduction

The BNL Electron-Ion Collider (EIC)
Accardi:2012qut
;
AbdulKhalek:2021gbh
will serve as a groundbreaking facility for probing the internal structure of hadrons and the dynamics of the strong interaction.
It will enable collisions between leptons and protons or ions at center-of-mass energies ranging from approximately
28\,\mathrm{GeV}
to
140\,\mathrm{GeV}
, using both polarized and unpolarized beams.
These collisions will generate an unprecedented volume of deep-inelastic scattering (DIS) events, making the EIC a precision laboratory for QCD studies.
Historically, DIS experiments have played a central role in the development and testing of Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD), particularly during the emergence of the parton model
Bjorken:1968dy
;
Feynman:1969ej
.
They have been critical in mapping the partonic substructure of the proton.
In fact, the most stringent constraints on parton distribution functions (PDFs) come from DIS data, obtained in both fixed-target experiments such as COMPASS and HERMES and collider experiments like H1 and ZEUS at the DESY HERA collider.
Given this foundational role, it is unsurprising that theoretical predictions for DIS processes are known with exceptional precision.
The inclusive DIS cross section has been calculated up to next-to-next-to-next-to-leading order (N
3
LO) in perturbative QCD
Moch:2004xu
;
Vermaseren:2005qc
;
Moch:2008fj
;
Blumlein:2022gpp
.
Also jet production in DIS has been computed at N
3
LO
Gehrmann:2018odt
.
Recent developments have extended next-to-leading order (NLO) event generators, matched to parton showers, to the DIS framework
Carli:2010cg
;
Hoche:2018gti
;
Banfi:2023mhz
;
Borsa:2024rmh
;
Buonocore:2024pdv
;
FerrarioRavasio:2024kem
.

Among the EIC’s key physics targets is semi-inclusive DIS (SIDIS), where a specific hadron is identified in the final state.
The identification of a hadron in the final state offers valuable insight into the underlying scattering dynamics by helping to disentangle the contributions of quarks of different flavours originating from the incoming proton.
Moreover, SIDIS is particularly important for accessing fragmentation functions (FFs), which describe the non-perturbative transition of partons into hadrons.
When included in global fits, SIDIS data significantly enhance our ability to constrain the flavour decomposition of quark fragmentation functions.
Full results for the next-to-next-to-leading order (NNLO) SIDIS coefficient functions have recently been presented both for unpolarized beams
Goyal:2023zdi
;
Bonino:2024qbh
;
Goyal:2024emo
and for polarized beams
Bonino:2024wgg
;
Goyal:2024tmo
.
However, these results were obtained including only photon-mediated DIS processes.
At the higher end of the EIC’s energy range, up to
140\,\mathrm{GeV}
, neutral current effects involving Z-boson exchange and
\gamma
-Z interference become potentially relevant at the targeted precision.

In addition, thanks to the implementation of the Jacquet-Blondel reconstruction technique
Amaldi:1979yh
, the EIC may be capable of probing charged current SIDIS processes
AbdulKhalek:2021gbh
.
These processes were previously measured in fixed-target neutrino-proton scattering (e.g.
Aachen-Bonn-CERN-Munich-Oxford:1982jrr

Paukkunen:2025kjb

Future precision measurements at EIC will substantially extend the kinematical range of charged-current SIDIS

For the interpretation of future EIC data for SIDIS processes, it
is therefore essential to extend the NNLO predictions
to neutral and charged electroweak current interactions.
The purpose of this paper is to present these NNLO corrections for the case of polarized lepton and
unpolarised hadron beams. The resulting coefficient functions also apply to the case of (anti-)neutrino scattering,

2.2
Parton-model expressions

Factorization in SIDIS separates the cross section into three pieces:

(i)

Long-range initial-state interactions, with a parton
p
extracted from the incoming nucleon
N
according to a collinear parton distribution function (PDF)
f_{p}^{N}
evaluated at the initial state factorization scale
\mu_{F}
.

(ii)

Long-range final-state interactions, with a parton
p^{\prime}
fragmenting into the detected hadron
h
through the fragmentation function (FF)
D_{p^{\prime}}^{h}
evaluated at the final state factorization scale
\mu_{A}
.

(iii)

The short-distance cross section
\hat{\sigma}
at the hard scale
Q
, which is calculated at the partonic level in perturbative QCD. The renormalization of
\hat{\sigma}
introduces a third scale, the renormalization scale
\mu_{R}
.

This factorization is expressed at the level of the structure functions:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{i}^{h,k}(x,z,Q^{2})=

\displaystyle\sum_{p,p^{\prime}}\int_{x}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}\hat{x}}{\hat{x}}\int_{z}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}\hat{z}}{\hat{z}}\,f_{p}^{N}\bigg(\frac{x}{\hat{x}},\mu_{F}^{2}\bigg)\,D^{h}_{p^{\prime}}\bigg(\frac{z}{\hat{z}},\mu_{A}^{2}\bigg)\,\mathcal{C}^{i,k}_{p^{\prime}p}(\hat{x},\hat{z},Q^{2},\mu_{R}^{2},\mu_{F}^{2},\mu_{A}^{2})\,,

(14)

where
\mathcal{C}^{i,k}_{p^{\prime}p}
denotes the parton-level coefficient function.
The indices
i\in\{T,L,3\}
refer to the respective structure function, and
k\in\{\gamma\gamma,\gamma Z,ZZ,W^{\pm}\}
refers to the electroweak gauge bosons exchanged in the process.
For this purpose, we introduce the partonic kinematics in analogy with eq. (
1
) as

\displaystyle\hat{x}=\frac{Q^{2}}{2p\cdot q}

momentum fraction of the initial state parton,

\displaystyle\hat{z}=\frac{p\cdot p_{h}}{p\cdot q}

momentum fraction
picked up by the identified parton.

(15)

The coefficient functions describing the hard scattering process can be calculated in perturbation theory by expanding in the strong coupling constant
\alpha_{s}

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{i,k}_{p^{\prime}p}=\mathcal{C}^{i,k,(0)}_{p^{\prime}p}+\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu_{R}^{2})}{2\pi}\mathcal{C}^{i,k,(1)}_{p^{\prime}p}+\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu_{R}^{2})}{2\pi}\right)^{2}\mathcal{C}^{i,k,(2)}_{p^{\prime}p}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{3})\,.

(16)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\{T,L\},k,(0)}_{qq}=\left[\hat{v}_{qq}^{k}+\hat{a}_{qq}^{k}\right]C^{\{T,L\},(0)}_{qq}\,,

(22)

with
C^{T,(0)}_{qq}=\delta(1-\hat{z})\delta(1-\hat{x})
and
C^{L,(0)}_{qq}=0
.

At LO the symmetric
\mathrm{NC}
SIDIS structure functions are therefore given by

\displaystyle\Big[\mathcal{F}_{T}^{h,\gamma\gamma},\penalty\ \mathcal{F}_{T}^{h,\gamma Z},\penalty\ \mathcal{F}_{T}^{h,ZZ}\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{q}\Big[e_{q}^{2},\penalty\ 2\,e_{q}\,g_{V}^{q},\penalty\ (g_{V}^{q})^{2}+(g_{A}^{q})^{2}\Big]\big(f_{q}^{N}\,D_{q}^{h}+f_{\bar{q}}^{N}\,D_{\bar{q}}^{h}\big)\,,

\displaystyle\Big[\mathcal{F}_{L}^{h,\gamma\gamma},\penalty\ \mathcal{F}_{L}^{h,\gamma Z},\penalty\ \mathcal{F}_{L}^{h,ZZ}\Big]

\displaystyle=0\,.

(23)

The NLO SIDIS coefficient functions
\mathcal{C}^{\{T,L\},(1)}_{p^{\prime}p}
were first computed in
Altarelli:1979kv
;
Baier:1979sp
.
At NLO, the
q\to g
and
g\to q
channels open up, and the longitudinal components no longer vanish:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\{T,L\},k,(1)}_{qq}

\displaystyle=\left[\hat{v}_{qq}^{k}+\hat{a}_{qq}^{k}\right]C^{\{T,L\},(1)}_{qq}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\{T,L\},k,(1)}_{gq}

\displaystyle=\left[\hat{v}_{qq}^{k}+\hat{a}_{qq}^{k}\right]C^{\{T,L\},(1)}_{gq}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\{T,L\},k,(1)}_{qg}

\displaystyle=\left[\hat{v}_{qq}^{k}+\hat{a}_{qq}^{k}\right]C^{\{T,L\},(1)}_{qg}\,.

(24)

At NNLO, each of the contributions in eq. (
17
) is present in the transverse and longitudinal
\mathrm{NC}
structure functions.
The missing coefficient functions with anti-quarks in the initial state are trivially obtained by charge conjugation through

\mathcal{C}^{\{T,L\},k,(j)}_{p^{\prime}p}=\mathcal{C}^{\{T,L\},k,(j)}_{\bar{p}^{\prime}\bar{p}}\quad\mathrm{for}\quad j\in\{0,1,2\}\,,

(25)

where
p
and
p^{\prime}
are understood as generic partons (quark, antiquarks or gluons).

The contributions entering the right-hand-side of (
17
) are given in terms of the electroweak couplings from eq. (
21
),
multiplying building blocks of different diagrammatic origin:

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},k,(2)}_{qq}

\displaystyle=\left[\hat{v}_{qq}^{k}+\hat{a}_{qq}^{k}\right]C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{qq}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},k,(2)}_{qq,\mathrm{Fcon}}

\displaystyle=\left[\hat{v}_{qq}^{k}+\hat{a}_{qq}^{k}\right]C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{qq,\mathrm{Fcon},1}+\sum_{{q^{\prime}}}\left[\hat{v}_{{q^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}^{k}+\hat{a}_{{q^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}^{k}\right]C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{qq,{\mathrm{Fcon}},2}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},k,(2)}_{qq,\mathrm{NoW}}

\displaystyle=a^{k}_{q}\sum_{{q^{\prime}}}\left(a^{k}_{{q^{\prime}}}\right)C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{qq,\mathrm{A},\mathrm{NoW}}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},k,(2)}_{\bar{q}q}

\displaystyle=\left[\hat{v}_{qq}^{k}+\hat{a}_{qq}^{k}\right]C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{\bar{q}q}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},k,(2)}_{\bar{q}q,\mathrm{Fcon}}

\displaystyle=\left[\hat{v}_{qq}^{k}+\hat{a}_{qq}^{k}\right]C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{\bar{q}q,\mathrm{Fcon}}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},k,(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q}

\displaystyle=\left[\hat{v}_{qq}^{k}+\hat{a}_{qq}^{k}\right]C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,1}+\left[\hat{v}_{{q^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}^{k}+\hat{a}_{{q^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}^{k}\right]C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,2}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},k,(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW}}

\displaystyle=\hat{v}_{q{q^{\prime}}}^{k}\,C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW},3}+\hat{a}_{q{q^{\prime}}}^{k}\,C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW},4}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},k,(2)}_{{\bar{q}^{\prime}}q}

\displaystyle=\left[\hat{v}_{qq}^{k}+\hat{a}_{qq}^{k}\right]C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,1}+\left[\hat{v}_{{q^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}^{k}+\hat{a}_{{q^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}^{k}\right]C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,2}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},k,(2)}_{{\bar{q}^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW}}

\displaystyle=-\hat{v}_{q{q^{\prime}}}^{k}\,C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW},3}+\hat{a}_{q{q^{\prime}}}^{k}\,C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW},4}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},k,(2)}_{gq}

\displaystyle=\left[\hat{v}_{qq}^{k}+\hat{a}_{qq}^{k}\right]C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{gq}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},k,(2)}_{gq,\mathrm{NoW}}

\displaystyle=a^{k}_{q}\sum_{q^{\prime}}\left(a^{k}_{q^{\prime}}\right)C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{gq,\mathrm{A},\mathrm{NoW}}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},k,(2)}_{qg}

\displaystyle=\left[\hat{v}_{qq}^{k}+\hat{a}_{qq}^{k}\right]C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{qg}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},k,(2)}_{qg,\mathrm{NoW}}

\displaystyle=a^{k}_{q}\sum_{q^{\prime}}\left(a^{k}_{q^{\prime}}\right)C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{qg,\mathrm{A},\mathrm{NoW}}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},k,(2)}_{gg}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q^{\prime}}\left[\hat{v}_{{q^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}^{k}+\hat{a}_{{q^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}^{k}\right]C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{gg}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},k,(2)}_{gg,\mathrm{NoW}}

\displaystyle=\left[\sum_{q^{\prime}}a^{k}_{{q^{\prime}}}\right]^{2}C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{gg,\mathrm{AA},\mathrm{NoW}}\,.

(26)

Note that each of the coefficient function building blocks on the right hand side of eq. (
26
) is finite and mass factorizes on its own.

A few comments on some building blocks appearing in (
26
) are in place.
While neutral current interactions do not change flavour at the vertices, it is nevertheless sensible to separate contributions according to their flavour structure in anticipation of the charged current scenario in Section
3.3
.
This way we can express all structure functions in terms of a minimal set of non-redundant building blocks.
As mentioned previously, the contributions labelled as “
\mathrm{NoW}
” are not present if the quark flavour is not conserved at the vector or axial vector vertex.
For this reason the
\mathrm{NoW}
contributions mass factorize independently.

The contribution
C^{T,ZZ,(2)}_{qq,\mathrm{NoW}}
, depicted with a representative diagram in Fig.
4(a)
, depends on the renormalization scale
\mu_{R}
, which is not predicted by the renormalization group equation.
This dependency originates because our theory is not anomaly free for an arbitrary number of quarks.
If an even number of active quark flavours is considered, the sum of axial charges
\sum_{q^{\prime}}\left(a^{k}_{q^{\prime}}\right)=0
cancels the anomaly and removes the renormalization scale dependence from the cross section.
We have nevertheless decided to quote the contributions proportional to
\sum_{q^{\prime}}a^{k}_{q^{\prime}}
since they are required in the context of variable flavour number schemes.
In this case the renormalization scale dependence from the massless quark flavours has to be cancelled by the corresponding anomaly contribution from the massive quark triangle diagrams.

The contribution
C_{gg,\mathrm{NoW}}^{\{T,L\},k,2}
shown in Fig.
4(b)
only arises in the case of two axial vector couplings in
Z
boson exchange, i.e.
k=ZZ
.
It contains the anomaly diagram twice and is finite even before renormalization and mass factorization.
Notably, it is the only non-vanishing two-loop (virtual-virtual) contribution in
\mathcal{F}^{h}_{L}
.

The
q\to q^{\prime}
channel with the electroweak vector bosons coupling to different quark lines is decomposed non-trivially into two distinctly separate contributions
\hat{i}_{q{q^{\prime}}}^{k}
,
\hat{i}_{{q^{\prime}}q}^{k}
.
This is due to to the different electroweak boson couplings with quarks in vector and axial vector interference contributions, and in contrast to photon exchange, where both contributions reduce to the common factor
e_{q}e_{q^{\prime}}
.

The results for photon exchange, i.e.
k=\gamma\gamma
, have been determined previously in
Goyal:2023zdi
;
Bonino:2024qbh
;
Goyal:2024emo
, for which we find full agreement.

For parts of the coefficient functions, where the external current couples to the
same quark line, we expect the coefficient functions to be the same between
purely vector and purely axial vector couplings, since one can naïvely anticommute
the
\gamma_{5}
matrices on the respective spin line.
This is explicitly verified in the calculation and reflected in eq. (
26
) by the
factorization of the factor
\left[\hat{v}_{qq}^{k}+\hat{a}_{qq}^{k}\right]
.
For the four quantities
C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,1}
,
C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,2}
,
C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW},3}\,
, and
C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW},4}\,

contributing to the
q\to{q^{\prime}}
transition there also exist relations from swapping the identified final state quark
{q^{\prime}}
with an anti-quark
{\bar{q}^{\prime}}
,
which are taken into account in eq. (
26
).

3.2.2
Antisymmetric Coefficient Functions

In this section we present the first NNLO calculation of the
\mathrm{NC}
SIDIS coefficient functions for the structure function
\mathcal{F}_{3}^{h}
.
The
\mathcal{F}_{3}^{h}
structure function corresponds to the contributions arising in the interference between vector and axial vector coupling.
In
\mathrm{NC}
exchange it therefore only contributes for
k=\{\gamma Z,ZZ\}
.

The LO contributions to
\mathcal{F}_{3}^{h}
in electroweak SIDIS are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{3,k,(0)}_{qq}

\displaystyle=\hat{i}_{qq}^{k}C^{3,(0)}_{qq}\,,

(27)

with
C^{3,(0)}_{qq}=\delta(1-\hat{z})\delta(1-\hat{x})
.
At LO we therefore have

\displaystyle\Big[\mathcal{F}_{3}^{h,\gamma\gamma},\penalty\ \mathcal{F}_{3}^{h,\gamma Z},\penalty\ \mathcal{F}_{3}^{h,ZZ}\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{q}\Big[0,\penalty\ 2\,e_{q}\,g_{A}^{q},\penalty\ 2\,g_{V}^{q}\,g_{A}^{q}\Big]\big(q\,D_{q}^{h}-\bar{q}\,D_{\bar{q}}^{h}\big)\,.

(28)

At NLO we have the same channels as for the symmetric NC coefficient functions,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{3,k,(1)}_{qq}

\displaystyle=\hat{i}_{qq}^{k}C^{3,(1)}_{qq}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{3,k,(1)}_{gq}

\displaystyle=\hat{i}_{qq}^{k}C^{3,(1)}_{gq}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{3,k,(1)}_{qg}

\displaystyle=\hat{i}_{qq}^{k}C^{3,(1)}_{qg}\,.

(29)

The NLO coefficient functions
\mathcal{C}^{3,(1)}_{p^{\prime}p}
above were previously computed in
Altarelli:1979kv
.
Note that the gluon-induced coefficient functions
\mathcal{C}^{3,(1)}_{qg}=-\mathcal{C}^{3,(1)}_{\bar{q}g}
are non-vanishing in SIDIS, but in inclusive DIS they cancel identically due to symmetry arguments.

The missing coefficient functions with initial-state anti-quarks are obtained by charge conjugation, which now introduces a sign flip,

\mathcal{C}^{3,k,(j)}_{p^{\prime}p}=-\mathcal{C}^{3,k,(j)}_{\bar{p}^{\prime}\bar{p}}\quad\mathrm{for}\quad j\in\{0,1,2\}\,.

(30)

At NNLO, the contributions appearing in (
17
) in terms of elementary building blocks read as follow:

\displaystyle C^{3,k,(2)}_{qq}

\displaystyle=\hat{i}_{qq}^{k}C^{3,(2)}_{qq}\,,

\displaystyle C^{3,k,(2)}_{qq,\mathrm{Fcon}}

\displaystyle=\hat{i}_{qq}^{k}C^{3,(2)}_{qq,\mathrm{Fcon},1}\,,

\displaystyle C^{3,k,(2)}_{qq,\mathrm{NoW}}

\displaystyle=2v_{q}^{k}\sum_{q^{\prime}}\left(a_{q^{\prime}}^{k}\right)C^{3,(2)}_{qq,\mathrm{A},\mathrm{NoW}}\,,

\displaystyle C^{3,k,(2)}_{\bar{q}q}

\displaystyle=\hat{i}_{qq}^{k}C^{3,(2)}_{\bar{q}q}\,,

\displaystyle C^{3,k,(2)}_{\bar{q}q,\mathrm{Fcon}}

\displaystyle=\hat{i}_{qq}^{k}C^{3,(2)}_{\bar{q}q,\mathrm{Fcon}}\,,

\displaystyle C^{3,k,(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q}

\displaystyle=\hat{i}_{qq}^{k}C_{{q^{\prime}}q,1}^{3,(2)}+\hat{i}_{{q^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}^{k}\,C_{{q^{\prime}}q,2}^{3,(2)}\,,

\displaystyle C^{3,k,(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW}}

\displaystyle=\hat{i}_{q{q^{\prime}}}^{k}\,C_{{q^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW},3}^{3,(2)}+\hat{i}_{{q^{\prime}}q}^{k}C_{{q^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW},4}^{3,(2)}\,,

\displaystyle C^{3,k,(2)}_{{\bar{q}^{\prime}}q}

\displaystyle=\hat{i}_{qq}^{k}C_{{q^{\prime}}q,1}^{3,(2)}-\hat{i}_{{q^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}^{k}\,C_{{q^{\prime}}q,2}^{3,(2)}\,,

\displaystyle C^{3,k,(2)}_{{\bar{q}^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW}}

\displaystyle=\hat{i}_{q{q^{\prime}}}^{k}\,C_{{q^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW},3}^{3,(2)}-\hat{i}_{{q^{\prime}}q}^{k}C_{{q^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW},4}^{3,(2)}\,,

\displaystyle C^{3,k,(2)}_{gq}

\displaystyle=\hat{i}_{qq}^{k}C^{3,(2)}_{gq}\,,

\displaystyle C^{3,k,(2)}_{gq,\mathrm{NoW}}

\displaystyle=2v_{q}^{k}\sum_{q^{\prime}}\left(a_{q^{\prime}}^{k}\right)C^{3,(2)}_{gq,\mathrm{NoW}}\,,

\displaystyle C^{3,k,(2)}_{qg}

\displaystyle=\hat{i}_{qq}^{k}C^{3,(2)}_{qg}\,,

\displaystyle C^{3,k,(2)}_{qg,\mathrm{NoW}}

\displaystyle=2v_{q}^{k}\sum_{q^{\prime}}\left(a_{q^{\prime}}^{k}\right)C^{3,(2)}_{qg,\mathrm{NoW}}\,.

(31)

With respect to the symmetric case, eq. (
26
), the singlet contributions
C^{3,k,(2)}_{gg}
,
C^{3,k,(2)}_{gg,\mathrm{NoW}}
,
C^{3,k,(2)}_{qq,\mathrm{Fcon},2}
are found to vanish like in inclusive DIS by a line reversal symmetry argument.
For the remaining singlet SIDIS coefficient functions, the same line reversal argument may not be applied as the symmetry along the singlet line is broken by the now-identified parton.

The building blocks
C_{{q^{\prime}}q,1}^{3,(2)}
,
C_{{q^{\prime}}q,2}^{3,(2)}
,
C_{{q^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW},3}^{3,(2)}
, and
C_{{q^{\prime}}q,\mathrm{NoW},4}^{3,(2)}
in eq. (
31
) reappear in different coefficient functions, which are related by charge conjugation from
{q^{\prime}}
to
{\bar{q}^{\prime}}
.

3.3
Charged Current SIDIS

We now turn to identified hadron production in
\mathrm{CC}
SIDIS, describing
the processes

\displaystyle\ell^{-}N\to\nu_{\ell}hX\,,

\displaystyle\ell^{+}N\to\bar{\nu}_{\ell}hX\,,

\displaystyle\nu_{\ell}N\to l^{-}hX\,

\displaystyle\bar{\nu}_{\ell}N\to l^{+}hX\,.

(32)

In the following we present the coefficient functions in
\mathrm{CC}
SIDIS at NNLO in QCD corresponding to the symmetric and antisymmetric structure functions.

3.3.1
Symmetric Coefficient Functions

In this section we describe the CC coefficient functions for the symmetric structure functions
\mathcal{F}^{h}_{T}
and
\mathcal{F}^{h}_{L}
, up to NNLO.
In
\mathrm{CC}
SIDIS the allowed quark transitions are restricted by the charge of the current entering the hadronic line.
In the following we explicitly give the contributions for
W^{-}
exchange.
The contributions for
W^{+}
exchange can be inferred.

We denote the set of up-type quarks with
\mathfrak{u}=\{u,c,(t)\}
and the set of down-type quarks with
\mathfrak{d}=\{d,s,(b)\}
. At LO we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(0)}_{\beta\alpha}=2\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}C^{\{T,L\},(0)}_{qq}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(0)}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}=2\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}C^{\{T,L\},(0)}_{qq}\,,

(33)

where
\alpha\in\mathfrak{u}
and
\beta\in\mathfrak{d}
.
The factors of
2
arise from summing the vector and axial vector contributions.
The coefficients
V_{\alpha\beta}
denote the entries of the Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa (CKM) matrix

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{CKM}}=\begin{pmatrix}V_{ud}&V_{us}&V_{ub}\\
V_{cd}&V_{cs}&V_{cb}\\
V_{td}&V_{ts}&V_{tb}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(34)

and describe the size of the flavour mixing.

At LO the symmetric SIDIS
\mathrm{CC}
structure functions are therefore given by

\displaystyle\Big[\mathcal{F}_{T}^{h,W^{-}},\penalty\ \mathcal{F}_{T}^{h,W^{+}}\Big]

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\phantom{|}\alpha\in\mathfrak{u}\phantom{|}}\sum_{\phantom{|}\beta\in\mathfrak{d}\phantom{|}}|V_{\alpha\beta}|^{2}\Big[f_{\alpha}^{N}\,D_{\beta}^{h}+f_{\bar{\beta}}^{N}\,D_{\bar{\alpha}}^{h},\penalty\ f_{\beta}^{N}\,D_{\alpha}^{h}+f_{\bar{\alpha}}^{N}\,D_{\bar{\beta}}^{h}\Big]\,,

\displaystyle\Big[\mathcal{F}_{L}^{h,W^{-}},\penalty\ \mathcal{F}_{L}^{h,W^{+}}\Big]

\displaystyle=0\,.

(35)

At NLO there exist the quark-initiated contributions

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(1)}_{\beta\alpha}

\displaystyle=2\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}C^{\{T,L\},(1)}_{qq}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(1)}_{g\alpha}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\beta}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}C^{\{T,L\},(1)}_{gq}\,,

(36)

the anti-quark-initiated contributions

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(1)}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}

\displaystyle=C^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(1)}_{\beta\alpha}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(1)}_{g\bar{\beta}}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\alpha}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}C^{\{T,L\},(1)}_{gq}\,,

(37)

and the gluon-initiated contributions

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(1)}_{\beta g}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\alpha}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}C^{\{T,L\},(1)}_{qg}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(1)}_{\bar{\alpha}g}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\beta}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}C^{\{T,L\},(1)}_{qg}\,.

(38)

At NNLO we have the quark-initiated contributions

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(2)}_{\beta\alpha}

\displaystyle=2\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{qq}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(2)}_{qq,\mathrm{Fcon}}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{qq,\mathrm{Fcon},2}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(2)}_{\alpha\alpha,\mathrm{Fcon}}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\beta}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{qq,\mathrm{Fcon},1}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(2)}_{\bar{\beta}\alpha}

\displaystyle=2\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{\bar{q}q}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(2)}_{\bar{\alpha}\alpha,\mathrm{Fcon}}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\beta}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{\bar{q}q,\mathrm{Fcon}}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}\alpha}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\beta}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,1}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(2)}_{\beta^{\prime}q}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\alpha}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,2}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(2)}_{{\bar{q}^{\prime}}\alpha}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\beta}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,1}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(2)}_{\bar{\alpha}^{\prime}q}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\beta}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,2}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(2)}_{g\alpha}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\beta}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{gq}\,,

(39)

with
q\in\mathfrak{u}\cup\mathfrak{d}
denoting a quark of any active flavour; the anti-quark-initiated contributions

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(2)}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}

\displaystyle=C^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(2)}_{\beta\alpha}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(2)}_{\bar{q}\bar{q},\mathrm{Fcon}}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{qq,\mathrm{Fcon},2}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(2)}_{\bar{\beta}\bar{\beta},\mathrm{Fcon}}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\alpha}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{qq,\mathrm{Fcon},1}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(2)}_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}

\displaystyle=C^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(2)}_{\bar{\beta}\alpha}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(2)}_{\beta\bar{\beta},\mathrm{Fcon}}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\alpha}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{\bar{q}q,\mathrm{Fcon}}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}\bar{\beta}}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\alpha}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,1}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(2)}_{\beta\bar{q}}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\alpha}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,2}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(2)}_{{\bar{q}^{\prime}}\bar{\beta}}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\alpha}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,1}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(2)}_{\bar{\alpha}^{\prime}\bar{q}}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\beta}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{{q^{\prime}}q,2}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(2)}_{g\bar{\beta}}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\alpha}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{gq}\,,

(40)

and the gluon-initiated contributions

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(2)}_{\beta g}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\alpha}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{qg}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(2)}_{\bar{\alpha}g}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\beta}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{qg}\,,

\displaystyle C^{\{T,L\},W^{-},(2)}_{gg}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}C^{\{T,L\},(2)}_{gg}\,.

(41)

In order to obtain a coefficient function from the channel decomposition, all the above contributions which admit the initial and final state particle need to be summed.

The coefficient functions
\mathcal{C}^{\{T,L\},W^{+},(j)}_{p^{\prime}p}
describing the exchange of a
W^{+}
boson are obtained for
\alpha\in\mathfrak{d}
and
\beta\in\mathfrak{u}
.

3.3.2
Antisymmetric Coefficient Functions

At LO the antisymmetric CC coefficient functions are

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{3,W^{-},(0)}_{\beta\alpha}

\displaystyle=2\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}C^{3,(0)}_{qq}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{3,W^{-},(0)}_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}

\displaystyle=-2\left|V_{\alpha\beta}\right|^{2}C^{3,(0)}_{qq}\,,

(42)

where
\alpha\in\mathfrak{u}
and
\beta\in\mathfrak{d}
.
The factors of
2
arise from our choice of normalization of
C^{3,(0)}_{qq}=\delta(1-\hat{x})\delta(1-\hat{z})
, like for NC.

The leading order
\mathcal{F}_{3}^{h}

\displaystyle\Big[\mathcal{F}_{3}^{h,W^{-}},\penalty\ \mathcal{F}_{3}^{h,W^{+}}\Big]

4
Analytical Calculation of the Coefficient Functions

In order to calculate the NNLO corrections to neutral- and charged-current SIDIS we use our established toolchain
Bonino:2024qbh
;
Bonino:2024wgg
.
The diagrams are generated with
QGRAF

Nogueira:1991ex
, and their
algebraic evaluation is then performed in

FORM

Vermaseren:2000nd
and
Mathematica
.

We apply projectors to obtain the structure functions
\mathcal{F}_{T}^{h}
,
\mathcal{F}_{L}^{h}
, and
\mathcal{F}_{3}^{h}
from Eq. (
8
).
In our coefficient functions we encounter up to two
\gamma_{5}
matrices from the axial vector vertex couplings
at the weak gauge boson vertices. We use the Larin scheme
Larin:1993tq
to
treat
\gamma_{5}
in dimensional regularisation, which consists in replacing

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}=\frac{i}{3!}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\rho}\gamma^{\sigma},

(50)

and contracting the two resulting Levi-Civita tensors into
d
-dimensional scalar products,

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\,\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}=\operatorname{det}\begin{pmatrix}g_{\mu\alpha}&g_{\mu\beta}&g_{\mu\gamma}&g_{\mu\delta}\\
g_{\nu\alpha}&g_{\nu\beta}&g_{\nu\gamma}&g_{\nu\delta}\\
g_{\rho\alpha}&g_{\rho\beta}&g_{\rho\gamma}&g_{\rho\delta}\\
g_{\sigma\alpha}&g_{\sigma\beta}&g_{\sigma\gamma}&g_{\sigma\delta}\end{pmatrix}.

(51)

At NNLO, there are three classes of contributing
subprocesses: the real-real (RR) contributions, describing the additional radiation of two partons; the real-virtual (RV) contributions, describing the radiation of a single parton with an additional loop; and the virtual-virtual contributions with no extra radiation, but two additional loops, all with respect to the leading order.

The calculation of the tree-level RR matrix elements is straightforward.
Using
REDUZE

vonManteuffel:2012np
, the loop-integrals in the RV and VV contributions are mapped onto integral families and reduced to master integrals with integration-by-parts identities
Chetyrkin:1981qh
;
Laporta:2000dsw
.
For the RV we reduce the loop integrals to the 1-loop bubble and box integral
Gehrmann:1999as
.
The integrals appearing in the VV are reduced to the four master integrals in
Gehrmann:2005pd
.

To obtain the coefficient functions, the contributions need to be integrated over the corresponding phase spaces.
For the RR, we employ reverse unitarity
Anastasiou:2002yz
to map the integrals onto 14 integral families containing a set of 8 independent propagators, 3 constraints from reverse unitarity, and the SIDIS phase space constraint introducing the fragmentation variable
z
, as described in
Bonino:2024adk
.
The phase space integrals are then reduced to a set of 21 known master
integrals
Bonino:2024adk
;
Ahmed:2024owh
.

For the RV, the phase space integration is fully constrained by
x
and
z
, such that only an
expansion of the unintegrated RV contributions in distributions in
(1-x)
and
(1-z)
needs to be performed.
These expansions are described in detail in
Gehrmann:2022cih
, and
must be performed in the appropriate kinematical regions requiring analytical continuations of
the one-loop bubble and box master integrals
Gehrmann:2022cih
;
Haug:2022hkr
.

The resulting bare coefficient functions
are first expressed in terms of harmonic polylogarithms (HPLs)
Remiddi:1999ew
;
Gehrmann:2000zt
using the Mathematica packages
HPL

Maitre:2005uu
and
PolyLogTools

Duhr:2019tlz
, and subsequently converted into logarithms and polylogarithms.

The ultraviolet poles are removed by renormalizing the QCD coupling constant in the usual manner, and applying the divergent renormalization on the axial vector current
Larin:1993tq
.
Both renormalizations are multiplicative and independent of the kinematics, and yield the UV-finite coefficient functions in the Larin scheme.
We then apply mass factorization to cancel the IR poles associated with PDFs and fragmentation functions.
In a final step, we apply the multiplicative finite renormalization of the axial current
Larin:1993tq
to restore the Ward identities, and obtain the
\overline{\textrm{MS}}
SIDIS coefficient functions.

Our results are expressed with the full scale dependence on
(\mu_{A},\mu_{F},\mu_{R})
.
They are distributed inside an ancillary file with the
arXiv
submission.

To validate our results we have carried out the following checks:
Our results fulfill the scale dependence predicted by the renormalization group equations in
(\mu_{F},\mu_{A},\mu_{R})
.
We have cross-checked our results for
\mathcal{F}_{T}^{h}
and
\mathcal{F}_{L}^{h}
against previous results for photon exchange
Goyal:2023zdi
;
Bonino:2024qbh
;
Goyal:2024emo
.
We find full agreement for the flavour decomposed vector coefficient functions, and agreement for the axial vector coefficient functions if the axial vector couplings are attached to a common quark line.
An equality for the axial vector couplings attaching to different quark lines is not required by theory, and also not realized.

5.2
Charged Current

Moving to the charged current case, we first consider cross sections for left-handed electrons
\mathrm{d}\sigma^{\pi^{\pm},\mathrm{CC}}(\lambda_{\ell}=-1)
.
Consequently, only charged current processes with a
W^{-}
exchange from the lepton to the quark line are probed.
In order to study the charged current contribution even at low
Q^{2}
,
we define the
charged-neutral current asymmetry

\displaystyle A^{h}_{\mathrm{CC/NC}}=\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{h,\mathrm{CC}}(\lambda_{\ell}=1)-\mathrm{d}\sigma^{h,\mathrm{CC}}(\lambda_{\ell}=-1)}{\mathrm{d}\sigma^{h,\mathrm{NC}}(\lambda_{\ell}=1)-\mathrm{d}\sigma^{h,\mathrm{NC}}(\lambda_{\ell}=-1)}\,.

(61)

The cross section differences in
A^{h}_{\mathrm{CC/NC}}
select the
\lambda_{\ell}\,
-odd components of the differential cross section,
cf. eq. (
2.1
), and remove the dominant photon exchange contribution (which is
\lambda_{\ell}
-even) from the denominator.
Note that always one of the two lepton helicity states in the numerator vanishes in
\mathrm{CC}
SIDIS.

Furthermore, the differences in
A^{h}_{\mathrm{CC/NC}}
allow for experimental systematic uncertainties to cancel.
Experimentally, charged-lepton–proton scattering
\mathrm{CC}
events are distinguished from
\mathrm{NC}
events by the absence of a highly-energetic lepton in the final state.
If the lepton is not detected or misidentified in a
\mathrm{NC}
interaction it can be mislabelled as a
\mathrm{CC}
event.
However, the misidentification is agnostic to the helicity state of the incoming lepton.
The helicity difference in the numerator therefore removes the misidentified events which originate from the
\lambda_{\ell}
-even
\mathrm{NC}
process,
which constitutes the largest contribution to the scattering cross section.
In addition, in the denominator of
A^{h}_{\mathrm{CC/NC}}
systematic uncertainties related to the lepton beam polarisation cancel.

Similarly to eq. (
58
), the cross sections entering eq. (
61
) are subjected to the kinematic cuts of eq. (
52
) and (
5
)
and theory uncertainties are obtained with a 31-point uncorrelated scale variation.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we presented the derivation of the
NNLO QCD corrections to single-inclusive hadron production in deeply
inelastic scattering, accounting for electroweak NC and CC current exchanges and initial-state lepton polarisation.
We introduced a new partonic channel decomposition for the SIDIS coefficient functions, which allows a
unified treatment of the NNLO corrections to NC and CC processes.
The
analytical results for the coefficient functions up to NNLO
are distributed as an ancillary file with the
arXiv
submission of this paper.

We performed detailed phenomenological studies
of NC and CC SIDIS cross sections for EIC kinematics at its
maximal center-of-mass energy value. By introducing lepton polarisation asymmetries and
CC/NC asymmetries, we were able to single out electroweak effects in SIDIS processes, thereby
potentially enabling their future study at the EIC.
The various processes display substantially different
compositions in terms of partonic channels, highlighting their potential to probe specific flavour combinations of fragmentation functions.

For EIC kinematics, NNLO corrections are typically found to be of moderate size, and the resulting NNLO predictions are
within the uncertainties of the previously known NLO predictions. A reduction of uncertainties from NLO to NNLO is
observed throughout.
In the NC case, we observe that the perturbative corrections to the
full electroweak SIDIS cross sections are very well described by re-scaling the respective higher-order predictions
for pure photon exchange processes by the electroweak-to-electromagnetic ratio of the Born-level cross sections.
A similar rescaling is still feasible but less accurate for the NC polarisation asymmetries.

Our work will enable NNLO precision studies of parton distributions and fragmentation functions with
future SIDIS data from the EIC, which will cover a substantially larger kinematical range than previous
fixed-target experiments in NC and CC processes, combined with excellent particle identification
capabilities required for SIDIS studies.

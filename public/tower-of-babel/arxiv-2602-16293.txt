1.
Introduction

1.1.
Main purpose of this paper

In this paper, we investigate the following Cauchy problem for the semilinear damped wave equation with a Riesz potential term coupled with a power-law nonlinearity:

(1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u+u_{t}=\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}(|u|^{p}),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},t>0,\\
u(0,x)=\varepsilon u_{0}(x),\ u_{t}(0,x)=\varepsilon u_{1}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{cases}

where
\gamma\in[0,n),\,p>1
, and
\varepsilon>0
describes the size of initial data. The Riesz potential
\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}
is defined by

\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}(f)(x):=\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n-\gamma}{2}\right)}{2^{\gamma}\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)}\left(|x|^{-(n-\gamma)}*f\right)=\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n-\gamma}{2}\right)}{2^{\gamma}\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\displaystyle\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{n-\gamma}}dy,

for any
f\in L_{\rm loc}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
.
More generally, the Riesz potential
\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}
can be interpreted as the inverse operator of the fractional Laplacian in the sense that

\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}(f)(x)=(-\Delta)^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}f(x)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(|\xi|^{-\gamma}\hat{f}(\xi))(x).

For a more comprehensive account of these fundamental properties of the Riesz potential, we refer the reader to
[
26
,
39
]
. The nonlinear term
\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}(|u|^{p})
appearing in the problem (
1
) is inspired by the recent paper
[
13
]
.

Our main purpose is to determine a critical exponent for the problem (
1
) with
(u_{0},u_{1})\in\mathcal{Y}^{q}\times\mathcal{Y}^{q}
equipping
q\in(0,n/2)
, in which the pseudo-measure spaces
\mathcal{Y}^{q}
are defined by (see, for instance,
[
3
]
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}^{q}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right):=

\displaystyle\bigg\{f\in S^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right):\widehat{f}\in L_{\text{loc}}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\text{ and }\|f\|_{\mathcal{Y}^{q}}:=\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\{|\xi|^{q}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|\right\}<\infty\bigg\},

where
S^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
is the space of tempered distributions. Here, the critical exponent is understood as the threshold between the global (in time) existence of small data solutions and the blow-up of solutions
even for small data. To be specific, under the additional assumption that the initial data belong to the space
\mathcal{Y}^{q}
, the new critical exponent, which will be proposed for (
1
), is

(2)

p_{\mathrm{crit}}(n,q,\gamma):=1+\displaystyle\frac{2+\gamma}{n-q}\quad\text{ with }1\leq n\leq 4\,\text{ and }\,0\leq\gamma<q<\frac{n}{2}.

Another objective of the present paper is to establish sharp estimates for lifespan of local solutions to (
1
). Here, the lifespan
T_{\varepsilon}
of a solution is defined as

\displaystyle T_{\varepsilon}:=\sup\{T>0:\text{there exists a unique local (in time) solution $u$ to problem \eqref{Semilinear_Damped_Waves}}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\text{on $[0,T)$ corresponding to a fixed parameter }\,\varepsilon>0\}.

Assuming that the initial data belong to the pseudo-measure spaces
\mathcal{Y}^{q}
with
q
satisfying some conditions, we establish the sharpness of the new lifespan estimates for the solution, namely

T_{\varepsilon}\sim\begin{cases}\varepsilon^{-\frac{2(p-1)}{2+\gamma-(p-1)(n-q)}}&\text{ if }1<p<p_{\mathrm{crit}}(n,q,\gamma),\\
\infty&\text{ if }p\geq p_{\mathrm{crit}}(n,q,\gamma).\end{cases}

Now, we consider the corresponding linear problem of (
1
), that is,

(3)

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u+u_{t}=0,&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},t>0,\\
(u,u_{t})(0,x)=\varepsilon(u_{0},u_{1})(x),&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{cases}

For the linear damped wave equation (
3
), we also establish decay estimates for solution in the
L^{2}
- framework, as well as in the homogeneous Sobolev norm
\dot{H}^{s}
, where
s
may be negative, assuming that the initial data satisfy
u_{0},u_{1}\in\mathcal{Y}^{q}
. More precisely, these decay estimates are stated for
j=0,1
,
q\geq 0
, and
s>q-n/2
as follows:

\|\partial_{t}^{j}\mathcal{K}(t,x)\ast_{x}\varphi(x)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{4}-\frac{s-q}{2}-j}\|\varphi\|_{H^{s+j-1}\cap\mathcal{Y}^{q}},

where
\mathcal{K}(t,x)
is defined as in Section
2.1
. These spaces were originally introduced in Harmonic Analysis and later proved to be particularly useful in the study of dissipative equations. The quantity

|\xi|^{q}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|
measures the behavior of
\widehat{f}
near the low-frequency region
\xi=0
. Hence, the parameter
q
quantifies the strength of the singularity (or decay) of the Fourier transform at low frequencies. When
q
increases, stronger control near
\xi=0
is imposed. For this reason,
\mathcal{Y}^{q}
spaces are well adapted to problems where the long-time dynamics are governed by low-frequency behavior.

1.2.
Background of this paper

In recent years, the pseudo-measure spaces have played an important role in the study of incompressible fluid models. Let us recall the fractional Navier–Stokes equations in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
(see
[
32
,
2
,
9
,
6
,
1
,
38
,
41
,
23
]
and papers included):

(4)

\begin{cases}u_{t}+(-\Delta)^{\alpha}u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla\pi=0,&x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},t>0,\\
\operatorname{div}u=0,\quad u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},t>0,\end{cases}

where
u(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
denotes the velocity field,
\pi(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}
is the pressure, and
\alpha\in\left(0,5/4\right)
in the supercritical regime.
A large body of literature has been devoted to understanding temporal decay rates of weak solutions in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and in negative Sobolev spaces
\dot{H}^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. More recently, these decay properties were refined by considering initial data in the pseudo-measure spaces. In particular, in
[
31
]
, it was shown that if
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap\mathcal{Y}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, then the corresponding weak solution to the fractional Navier–Stokes equations (
4
) satisfies improved decay estimates of the form

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{3-2q}{4\alpha}},\quad t>0,

together with analogous estimates in negative Sobolev norms. These results clearly demonstrate that the decay rate depends explicitly on the low-frequency index
q
. In other words, the long-time behavior of weak solutions is strongly influenced by the size of the Fourier transform near
\xi=0
, which is precisely measured by the
\mathcal{Y}^{q}
norm. Such developments indicate that the pseudo-measure spaces provide a natural and flexible framework for studying dissipative evolution equations whose asymptotic dynamics are governed by low-frequency phenomena. This observation strongly motivates the use of
\mathcal{Y}^{q}
spaces in the analysis of other dissipative models, including the damped wave equation considered in the present work.

In the setting of the semilinear damped wave equation (
1
) with power nonlinearity-type
|u|^{p}
(in the case of
\gamma=0
) and initial data additionally assumed to lie in
L^{1}
, it has been shown in
[
30
,
40
,
42
,
22
]
that the critical exponent coincides with the so-called
Fujita exponent

p_{\mathrm{Fuj}}(n):=1+2/n
. It is also the critical exponent of the
semilinear heat equation (see, for example,
[
11
]
):

v_{t}-\Delta v=|v|^{p},\quad v(0,x)=v_{0}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad t>0.

This phenomenon is closely related to the diffusion effect (see, for instance,
[
29
,
34
]
) that links the linear damped wave equation with the heat equation. Returning to (
1
) with
\gamma=0
, in the pioneering work
[
30
]
, the global (in time) existence of small data solutions was established in the supercritical case
p>p_{\mathrm{Fuj}}(n)
for spatial dimensions
n=1,2
. Subsequently, the paper
[
40
]
extended the global existence result to all
n\geq 1
in the supercritical case under the assumption that the initial data are compactly supported. In the same work, finite-time blow-up of local solutions was proved in the subcritical range
1<p<p_{\mathrm{Fuj}}(n)
, provided that the initial data satisfy an appropriate sign condition. The blow-up result in the critical case
p=p_{\mathrm{Fuj}}(n)
was later obtained in
[
42
]
by means of the test function method. Finally, in the supercritical setting, the compact support assumption on the initial data was removed in
[
22
]
. More recently, the paper
[
10
]

investigated the
problem (
1
) with nonlinear terms
\mu(|u|)|u|^{p_{\mathrm{Fuj}}(n)}
with
\mu
is a modulus of continuity and established the sharp condition on
\mu
that separates the blow-up case from the global existence case. Regarding the lifespan estimates for local solutions to (
1
) with
\gamma=0
and the additional regularity
L^{1}
for the initial data, the works
[
27
,
24
,
36
,
20
,
17
,
12
,
18
,
25
]
established the following sharp estimates:

T_{\varepsilon}\sim\begin{cases}\varepsilon^{-\frac{2(p-1)}{2-n(p-1)}}&\text{ if }1<p<p_{\mathrm{Fuj}}(n),\\
\infty&\text{ if }p>p_{\mathrm{Fuj}}(n)\end{cases}\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\log(T_{\varepsilon})\sim\varepsilon^{-(p-1)}\text{ if }p=p_{\rm Fuj}(n).

In recent years, considerable attention has been devoted to identifying critical exponents for the semilinear damped wave equation in various functional settings. For the problem (
1
) with
\gamma=0
and under additional regularity
L^{m}
for the initial data with
m\in(1,2]
, the critical threshold shifts to the so-called modified Fujita exponent
p_{\mathrm{Fuj}}\left(n/m\right)=1+2m/n
. In contrast to the case of
L^{1}
data, it is worth emphasizing that, at the critical value
p_{\rm Fuj}\left(n/m\right)
, one obtains global (in time) solutions that are uniquely determined. The fact that
p_{\mathrm{Fuj}}\left(n/m\right)
indeed represents the critical exponent has been established in
[
33
,
21
,
35
,
15
,
16
]
and the references cited therein. By further enlarging the class of initial data to the negative-order Sobolev spaces
\dot{H}^{-\gamma}
with
\gamma\geq 0
, the authors in
[
4
,
5
]
identified the critical exponent for equation (
1
) as
p_{\mathrm{Fuj}}\left(n/2+\gamma\right)=1+4/(n+2\gamma)
. They established this threshold by proving global (in time) existence of small data solutions when
p\geq p_{\mathrm{Fuj}}\left(n/2+\gamma\right)
, while showing finite-time blow-up for
1<p<p_{\mathrm{Fuj}}\left(n/2+\gamma\right)
.

To the best of the authors’ knowledge, however, the setting in which the initial data are localized in pseudo-measure spaces
\mathcal{Y}^{q}
has not yet been explored for damped wave equations, not even for the linear problem. For this reason, it appears worthwhile to investigate qualitative aspects-such as well-posedness, blow-up criteria and mechanisms, decay properties, asymptotic behavior, and sharp lifespan estimates for solutions to damped wave equations with additional initial data in
\mathcal{Y}^{q}
, where
q\in\left(0,n/2\right)
.

1.4.
Main results

Let us state the global (in time) existence result firstly.

Theorem 1.1

(
Global existence - Supercritical case
)
.

Let
1\leq n\leq 4
,
0\leq\gamma<q<n/2
, and
s\in\left[0,n/2-q\right)
. The exponent
p
fulfills

(5)

p>p_{\mathrm{crit}}(n,q,\gamma).

In addition, we assume that
p
satisfies the following condition when
n=3,4
:

(6)

\displaystyle\frac{2(n-q+\gamma)}{n}\leq p\leq\displaystyle\frac{n+2\gamma}{n-2}.

Moreover, the initial data satisfies

\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\in\mathcal{A}:=\left(H^{1}\cap\mathcal{Y}^{q}\right)\times\left(L^{2}\cap\mathcal{Y}^{q}\right).

Then, there exists a constant
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
, problem (
1
) admits a unique global (in time) solution
u\in\mathcal{C}([0,\infty),H^{1}\cap\dot{H}^{-s})
. Furthermore, the following estimates hold:

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{4}+\frac{q}{2}}\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\mathcal{A}},

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{4}+\frac{q}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\mathcal{A}},

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{-s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{4}+\frac{q}{2}+\frac{s}{2}}\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\mathcal{A}}.

Theorem 1.2

(
Global existence - Critical case
)
.

The statements of Theorem
1.1
are still true if we replace the condition (
5
) by
p=p_{\mathrm{crit}}(n,q,\gamma)
and the condition (
6
) by

(7)

\frac{2(n-q+\gamma)}{n}<p_{\rm crit}(n,q,\gamma)\leq\displaystyle\frac{n+2\gamma}{n-2}.

Remark 1.1
.

When
n=3,4
, the appearance of the conditions (
6
) and (
7
) comes from the use of Proposition
A.1
. One can verify that the range of
p
is not empty for suitable
q
and
\gamma
in this case. When
n=1,2
, it is obvious that
p_{\text{crit}}(n,q,\gamma)\geq 2(n-q+\gamma)/n
and therefore these restrictions can be removed. On the other hand, we require
\gamma<q
for the use of Proposition
A.2
. To relax those conditions, one may investigate Sobolev solutions by assuming that the initial data belong to Sobolev spaces with suitably higher, or even substantial, regularity; see
[
37
]
for related discussions. However, in such a framework, the use of the fractional chain rule or fractional power estimates in handling the nonlinear terms inevitably imposes additional lower bound constraints on the exponent
p
.

Remark 1.2
.

Throughout the proof, one can observe that the statements of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
remain valid when
q=\gamma=0
if
n\leq 3
. In this case, the well-known Fujita exponent appears, yielding
p_{\mathrm{crit}}(n,0,0)=p_{\mathrm{Fuj}}(n)
. Alternatively, our critical exponent can be expressed as
p_{\mathrm{crit}}(n,q,\gamma)=p_{\mathrm{Fuj}}(2(n-q)/(2+\gamma))
.

We now proceed to examine the blow-up phenomenon in the subcritical regime.

Theorem 1.3

(
Blow-up - Subcritical case
)
.

Let
n\geq 1
,
q\in(0,n/2)
and
\gamma\in[0,n)
. The exponent
p
fulfills

(8)

\displaystyle 1<p<p_{\mathrm{crit}}(n,q,\gamma).

Let us assume
\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\in\mathcal{Y}^{q}\times\mathcal{Y}^{q}
such that

(9)

u_{0}(x)+u_{1}(x)\gtrsim\langle x\rangle^{-n+q}.

Then, there is no global (in time) weak solution by mean of Definition
3.1
to (
1
).

The non-emptiness of the data set in Theorem
1.3
will be discussed in Lemma
B.3
.

Remark 1.3
.

According to Theorems
1.1
-
1.3
, we conclude that the critical exponent for the problem (
1
) with initial data belonging additionally to
\mathcal{Y}^{q}
is defined by (
2
). It provides a new viewpoint for the critical exponent of semilinear damped wave equations.

Example 1.1
.

We give some examples where the sharpness of
p
can be confirmed.

•

If
n=1,2
and
0\leq\gamma<q<n/2
: We obtain the critical exponent
p
on the entire interval
(1,\infty)
.

•

If
n=3,4
and
q\in(0,n/2)
is fixed: The critical exponent
p
on the interval
(1,(n+2\gamma)/(n-2))
can be claimed if
0\leq\gamma<\min\left\{q,2n/(n-2q)-n+q\right\}
.

•

If
n=3,4
and
\gamma\in(0,n/2)
is fixed: Let
q_{1}\leq q_{2}
be the solutions of

The critical exponent
p

(1,(n+2\gamma)/(n-2))
can be claimed if

.

2.
Global existence

2.1.
Linear estimates and philosophy of our approach

To begin with, we can write the solution to the linear problem (
3
) by the formula

u^{\rm lin}(t,x)=\varepsilon(\mathcal{K}(t,x)+\partial_{t}\mathcal{K}(t,x))\ast_{x}u_{0}(x)+\varepsilon\mathcal{K}(t,x)\ast_{x}u_{1}(x),

so that the solution to (
1
) becomes

u(t,x)=u^{\rm lin}(t,x)+u^{\rm non}(t,x),

thanks to Duhamel’s principle, where

u^{\rm non}(t,x):=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{K}(t-\tau,x)\ast_{x}\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}(|u|^{p})(\tau,x)d\tau

and the Fourier transform of the linear kernel
\mathcal{K}(t,x)
defined by

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{K}}(t,\xi)=\begin{cases}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\frac{t}{2}}\sinh{\left(t\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}-|\xi|^{2}}\right)}}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}-|\xi|^{2}}}&\text{ if }|\xi|\leq\displaystyle\frac{1}{2},\\
\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\frac{t}{2}}\sin{\left(t\sqrt{|\xi|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}}\right)}}{\sqrt{|\xi|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}}}&\text{ if }|\xi|>\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}.\end{cases}

When the initial data belongs to the pseudo-measure spaces
\mathcal{Y}^{q}
, we obtain the following linear estimate.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
n\geq 1
. Then, the following estimate holds for
j=0,1
,
0\leq q<n/2
, and
s>q-n/2
:

(10)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}^{j}\mathcal{K}(t,x)\ast_{x}\varphi(x)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{4}-\frac{s-q}{2}-j}\|\varphi\|_{H^{s+j-1}\cap\mathcal{Y}^{q}}.

Moreover, we also have the following estimates for
m\in[1,2]
and
m_{q}:=n/(n-q)
:

(11)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}^{j}\mathcal{K}(t,x)\ast_{x}\varphi(x)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{m_{q}}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{s}{2}-j}\|\varphi\|_{H^{s+j-1}\cap L^{m_{q}}},

(12)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}^{j}\mathcal{K}(t,x)\ast_{x}\varphi(x)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{s}{2}-j}\|\varphi\|_{H^{s+j-1}\cap L^{m}},\quad\text{ for }s\geq 0.

Remark 2.1
.

From the definition of
\mathcal{Y}^{q}
, it follows that
L^{1}\subset\mathcal{Y}^{0}
. Compared with the estimate (
12
) with
m=1
, the estimate (
10
) shows that when the class of initial data is extended to
\mathcal{Y}^{0}
, the solution to (
3
) still decays at the rate
(1+t)^{-n/4}
. Moreover, when the initial data belong to the space
\mathcal{Y}^{q}
with
q\in(0,n/2)
, the decay rate exhibits a certain loss.

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

From the definition of the kernel
\mathcal{K}(t,x)
, we can get some pointwise estimates in the Fourier space, namely,

|\partial_{t}^{j}\widehat{\mathcal{K}}(t,\xi)|\lesssim\begin{cases}\mathrm{e}^{-ct}+|\xi|^{2j}\mathrm{e}^{-c|\xi|^{2}t}&\text{ for }|\xi|<\epsilon,\\
\mathrm{e}^{-ct}&\text{ for }\epsilon\leq|\xi|\leq N,\\
|\xi|^{j-1}\mathrm{e}^{-ct}&\text{ for }|\xi|>N,\end{cases}

with suitable constants
c>0,

\epsilon\ll 1
and
N\gg 1
.
Let
\chi_{k}=\chi_{k}(r)
with
k\in\{\rm L,H\}
be smooth cut-off functions having the following properties:

\displaystyle\chi_{\rm L}(r)=\begin{cases}1&\quad\text{ if }r\leq\epsilon/2\\
0&\quad\text{ if }r\geq\epsilon\end{cases}\quad\text{ and }\quad\chi_{\rm H}(r)=1-\chi_{\rm L}(r),

where
\epsilon
is a sufficiently small positive constant.
It is obvious to see that
\chi_{\rm H}(r)=1
if
r\geq\epsilon
and
\chi_{\rm H}(r)=0
if
r\leq\epsilon/2
.
Using Parseval’s formula, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}^{j}\mathcal{K}(t,x)\ast_{x}\varphi(x)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}=

\displaystyle\||\xi|^{s}\partial_{t}^{j}\widehat{\mathcal{K}}(t,\xi)\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\chi_{\rm L}(|\xi|)|\xi|^{s}\partial_{t}^{j}\widehat{\mathcal{K}}(t,\xi)\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\chi_{\rm H}(|\xi|)|\xi|^{s}\partial_{t}^{j}\widehat{\mathcal{K}}(t,\xi)\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}}.

Concerning the low frequencies part, we apply Lemma
B.1
for
s>q-n/2
to get

\displaystyle\|\chi_{\rm L}(|\xi|)|\xi|^{s}\partial_{t}^{j}\widehat{\mathcal{K}}(t,\xi)\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle\|\chi_{\rm L}(|\xi|)|\xi|^{s-q}\partial_{t}^{j}\widehat{\mathcal{K}}(t,\xi)\|_{L^{2}}\||\xi|^{q}\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\chi_{\rm L}(|\xi|)|\xi|^{s+2j-q}e^{-c|\xi|^{2}t}\|_{L^{2}}\||\xi|^{q}\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)\|_{L^{\infty}}+e^{-ct}\||\xi|^{q}\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{4}-\frac{s-q}{2}-j}\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{Y}^{q}}.

Moreover, for the large frequencies part, we have immediately the following estimate:

3.
Blow-up results and sharp estimates for lifespan

3.1.
Blow-up results

In this section, our aim is to prove Theorem
1.3
. To prove our result, we recall the definition of local weak solution to (
1
).

Definition 3.1

(Weak solution)
.

Let
p>1
and
T\in(0,\infty]
. We say that
u\in\mathcal{C}([0,T],L^{2})
is a local (in time) weak solution to (
1
) if for any test functions
\chi\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}([0,T])
, and
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with all derivatives in
L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}
, the following relation holds true:

(20)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}(|u|^{p})(t,x)\phi_{R}(x)\chi_{R}(t)dxdt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta+\partial_{t})u(t,x)\phi_{R}(x)\chi_{R}(t)dxdt.

If
T=\infty
, then we say that
u
is a global (in time) weak solution to (
1
).

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.
.

At first, let us introduce the test function
\chi=\chi(t)
satisfying the following properties:

\displaystyle 1.\quad\chi\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}([0,\infty))\text{ and }\chi(t)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }0\leq t\leq 1/2,\\
\text{decreasing}&\text{ if }1/2\leq t\leq 1,\\
0&\text{ if }t\geq 1,\end{cases}

\displaystyle 2.\quad\chi^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\big(|\chi^{\prime}|^{p^{\prime}}+|\chi^{\prime\prime}|^{p^{\prime}}\big)\text{ is bounded, }

where
p^{\prime}
stands for the conjugate of
p
. In addition,
we set
\phi(x):=\langle x\rangle^{-n-1}
for
n\geq 1
. For
R>0
, we define
\phi_{R}(x):=\phi(R^{-1}x)
and
\chi_{R}(t):=\chi(R^{-2}t)
. Assume by contradiction that
u=u(t,x)
is a global (in time) weak solution to (
1
).
We apply Definition
3.1
with
\chi
and
\phi
replaced by
\chi_{R}
and
\phi_{R}
, respectively.
From this, by integration by parts, the relation (
20

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u_{0}(x)+u_{1}(x))\phi_{R}(x)dx+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(t,x)\phi_{R}(x)\partial_{t}^{2}\chi_{R}(t)dxdt

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(t,x)\phi_{R}(x)\partial_{t}\chi_{R}(t)dxdt-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(t,x)\Delta\phi_{R}(x)\chi_{R}(t)dxdt

(21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}\left(|u|^{p}\right)(t,x)\phi_{R}(x)\chi_{R}(t)dxdt.

We define

\mathcal{J}(R):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(t,x)|^{p}\phi_{R}(x)\chi_{R}(t)dxdt=\int_{0}^{R^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(t,x)|^{p}\phi_{R}(x)\chi_{R}(t)dxdt.

We deduce from (
3.1
) the inequality

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}\left(|u|^{p}\right)(t,x)\phi_{R}(x)\chi_{R}(t)dxdt+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u_{0}(x)+u_{1}(x))\phi_{R}(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{R^{2}/2}^{R^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(t,x)||\phi_{R}(x)\partial_{t}\chi_{R}(t)|dxdt+\int_{R^{2}/2}^{R^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(t,x)||\phi_{R}(x)\partial_{t}^{2}\chi_{R}(t)|dxdt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{R^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(t,x)||\Delta\phi_{R}(x)|\chi_{R}(t)dxdt

(22)

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\,\,\mathcal{J}_{1,R}+\mathcal{J}_{2,R}+\mathcal{J}_{3,R}.

Using Hölder’s inequality with
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
and the change of
variables
\overline{x}=R^{-1}x
,
\overline{t}=R^{-2}t
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{1,R}\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\,\mathcal{J}_{R}^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{R^{2}/2}^{R^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\chi^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}_{R}(t)|\partial_{t}\chi_{R}(t)|^{p^{\prime}}\phi_{R}(x)dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\,\mathcal{J}_{R}^{\frac{1}{p}}R^{-2+\frac{n+2}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{1/2}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\chi^{\prime}(\overline{t})|^{p^{\prime}}|\chi(\overline{t})|^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\phi(\overline{x})d\overline{x}d\overline{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

(23)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\,\,\mathcal{J}_{R}^{\frac{1}{p}}R^{-2+\frac{n+2}{p^{\prime}}}.

The same argument yields

(24)

\mathcal{J}_{2,R}\lesssim\mathcal{J}_{R}^{\frac{1}{p}}R^{-4+\frac{n+2}{p^{\prime}}}.

For the term
\mathcal{J}_{3,R}
, we treat it as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{3,R}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{J}_{R}^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{0}^{R^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\Delta\phi_{R}(x)|^{p^{\prime}}\phi_{R}^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}(x)\chi_{R}(t)dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{J}_{R}^{\frac{1}{p}}R^{-2+\frac{n+2}{p^{\prime}}},

where we note that
|\Delta\phi(x)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-n-3}
.
By (
22
), (
23
), (
24

25

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}\left(|u|^{p}\right)(t,x)\phi_{R}(x)\chi_{R}(t)dxdt

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u_{0}(x)+u_{1}(x))\phi_{R}(x)dx

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\mathcal{J}_{R}^{\frac{1}{p}}R^{-2+\frac{n+2}{p^{\prime}}}.

To control the first quantity on the left-hand side of (

), we begin with the representation of the Riesz potential

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}\left(|u|^{p}\right)(t,x)=

\displaystyle C_{\gamma}|x|^{-(n-\gamma)}\ast_{x}|u(t,x)|^{p}=C_{\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\displaystyle\frac{|u(t,y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n-\gamma}}dy

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C_{\gamma}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}}\displaystyle\frac{|u(t,y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n-\gamma}}dy\quad\text{ with }\mathcal{D}_{R}=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},|y|\leq R\right\}.

Now observe that for
x\in\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{R}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},|x|\leq R/2\right\}
and

|x-y|\leq|x|+|y|\leq R/2+R\leq 2R
. Consequently,
|x-y|^{n-\gamma}\leq 2^{n-\gamma}R^{n-\gamma}
for all

. This leads to the pointwise lower bound

\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}\left(|u|^{p}\right)(t,x)\geq\frac{CR^{-(n-\gamma)}}{2^{n-\gamma}}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}}|u(t,y)|^{p}dy\quad\text{ for all }t\in(0,R^{2}),x\in\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{R}.

Then, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R^{2}}\int_{\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{R}}\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}\left(|u|^{p}\right)(t,x)\phi_{R}(x)\chi_{R}(t)dxdt

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{R^{-(n-\gamma)}}{2^{n-\gamma}}\int_{0}^{R^{2}}\int_{\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{R}}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}}|u(t,y)|^{p}\phi_{R}(x)\chi_{R}(t)dydxdt

(27)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\operatorname{meas}(\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{R})\frac{R^{-(n-\gamma)}}{2^{n-\gamma}}\int_{0}^{R^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{R}}|u(t,y)|^{p}\phi_{R}(y)\chi_{R}(t)dydt\geq CR^{\gamma}\mathcal{J}_{R},

where we have used the fact that
\phi_{R}\equiv 1
on
\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{R}
, and
1\geq\phi_{R}
on
\mathcal{D}_{R}
. Moreover, a simple calculation leads to

(28)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u_{0}(x)+u_{1}(x))\phi_{R}(x)dx\geq C\int_{|x|\leq R}\langle x\rangle^{-n+q}\geq CR^{q}.

for
R\gg 1
. Combining (
26
), (
3.1
), and (
28
), we infer that

R^{\gamma}\mathcal{J}_{R}+\varepsilon R^{q}\leq C\mathcal{J}_{R}^{\frac{1}{p}}R^{-2+\frac{n+2}{p^{\prime}}}.

This leads to

\displaystyle\varepsilon R^{q-\gamma}\leq C\mathcal{J}_{R}^{\frac{1}{p}}R^{-2-\gamma+\frac{n+2}{p^{\prime}}}-\mathcal{J}_{R}\lesssim R^{-(2+\gamma)p^{\prime}+n+2},

that is

(29)

\displaystyle\varepsilon\lesssim R^{-(2+\gamma)p^{\prime}+n+2+\gamma-q}.

The assumption (
8
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle-(2+\gamma)p^{\prime}+n+2+\gamma-q<0.

We pass
R\to\infty
in (
29
) to derive a contradiction. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
∎

u,\bar{u}\in X_{0}(T)

(32)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{N}[u]-\mathcal{N}[\bar{u}]\|_{X_{0}(T)}\leq C_{1}(1+T)^{\alpha(p,n,\gamma)}\|u-\bar{u}\|_{X_{0}(T)}\left(\|u\|_{X_{0}(T)}^{p-1}+\|\bar{u}\|_{X_{0}(T)}^{p-1}\right).

We note that

\displaystyle M:=2C_{0}\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\mathcal{A}}

and the following space:

\displaystyle X_{0}(T,M):=\{\varphi\in X_{0}(T),\|\varphi\|_{X_{0}(T)}\leq M\}.

Therefore, if we assume that

\displaystyle\max\{C_{0},2C_{1}\}(1+T)^{\alpha(p,n,q)}\varepsilon^{p-1}<\frac{M}{4},

then we may construct a unique local solution
u=\mathcal{N}[u]\in X_{0}(T,M\varepsilon)
thanks to the relations (
31
), (
32
), and Banach’s fixed point theorem. Moreover, it holds

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X_{0}(T)}<\frac{3M\varepsilon}{4}.

Afterwards, we consider

T^{*}:=\sup\left\{T\in[0,T_{\varepsilon,\mathrm{m}})\text{ such that }F(T):=\|u\|_{X_{0}(T)}\leq M\varepsilon\right\}.

The function
F=F(T)
is a continuous for any
T\in(0,T_{\varepsilon,\mathrm{m}})
so there exists a time
T_{0}\in(T^{*},T_{\varepsilon,\mathrm{m}})
such that
F(T_{0})\leq M\varepsilon
, which contradicts the definition of
T^{*}
. This implies that the following condition must hold:

\max\{C_{0},2C_{1}\}(1+T^{*})^{\alpha(p,n,q)}\varepsilon^{p-1}\geq\frac{M}{4},

that is,

T_{\varepsilon,m}\geq T^{*}\gtrsim\varepsilon^{-\frac{2(p-1)}{2+\gamma-(p-1)(n-q)}}.

This completes the proof of Proposition
3.2
.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Since
u(t,x)
constructed in Theorem
1.1
is a mild solution to (
1
), a standard density argument (see, for instance, Proposition 3.1 in
[
19
]
) ensures that this mild solution also qualifies as a weak solution to (
1
). In particular, its maximal existence time satisfies
T_{\varepsilon,\mathrm{m}}\leq T_{\varepsilon}
. By combining the upper bound established in Proposition
3.1
with the lower bound derived in Proposition
3.2
, we obtain a new sharp estimate for
T_{\varepsilon}
in the subcritical regime
1<p<p_{\mathrm{crit}}(n,q,\gamma)
, that is,

T_{\varepsilon}\sim\varepsilon^{-\frac{2(p-1)}{2+\gamma-(p-1)(n-q)}}\quad\text{ for $0\leq\gamma<q<\frac{n}{2}$}.

Lemma B.3
.

The set of initial data in Theorem
1.3
, i.e.
(u_{0},u_{1})\in\mathcal{Y}^{q}\times\mathcal{Y}^{q}
with the condition (
9
), is non-empty.

Proof.

To prove this lemma, we choose the initial data as follows:

u_{0}(x)=u_{1}(x)=C\langle x\rangle^{-n+q},

where
C>0
.
It is clear that
u_{0},u_{1}\in L^{\frac{n}{n-q}+\epsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
\epsilon>0
is chosen such that
1<n/(n-q)+\epsilon<2
. Next, we recall the formula for the modified Bessel function as follows:

\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi)=C\int_{0}^{\infty}g(r)r^{n-1}\tilde{J}_{\frac{n}{2}-1}(r|\xi|)dr\quad\text{ with }g(|x|):=f(x)\in L^{m},m\in[1,2].

Applying the above formula, we obtain

\widehat{u}_{1}(\xi)=C\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle r\rangle^{-n+q}r^{n-1}\tilde{J}_{\frac{n}{2}-1}(r|\xi|)dr.

Therefore, we observe that

\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{Y}^{q}}=\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\{|\xi|^{q}|\widehat{u}_{1}(\xi)|\right\}\sim\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\{|\xi|^{q}\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle r\rangle^{-n+q}r^{n-1}\tilde{J}_{\frac{n}{2}-1}(r|\xi|)dr\right|\right\}.

It suffices to prove that
u_{1}\in\mathcal{Y}^{q}
, the case of
u_{0}
can be treated in exactly the same way as
u_{1}
. We also divide our interest two cases as follows:

•

If
r|\xi|\leq 1
, we see that

|\xi|^{q}\left|\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{|\xi|}}\langle r\rangle^{-n+q}r^{n-1}\tilde{J}_{\frac{n}{2}-1}(r|\xi|)dr\right|\lesssim|\xi|^{q}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{|\xi|}}\langle r\rangle^{-n+q}r^{n-1}dr.

For
|\xi|\in(0,1]
, we obtain

|\xi|^{q}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{|\xi|}}\langle r\rangle^{-n+q}r^{n-1}dr\lesssim|\xi|^{q}\int_{0}^{1}r^{n-1}dr+|\xi|^{q}\int_{1}^{\frac{1}{|\xi|}}r^{q-1}dr\lesssim 1.

For
|\xi|\in(1,\infty)
, one has

|\xi|^{q}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{|\xi|}}\langle r\rangle^{-n+q}r^{n-1}dr\lesssim\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{|\xi|}}r^{n-q-1}dr\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}r^{n-q-1}dr\lesssim 1.

•

If
r|\xi|>1
, using the first-order asymptotic expansion of the Bessel function
J_{\nu}(r|\xi|)
with
\nu=n/2-1
in the region
r|\xi|>1
, it yields

\displaystyle|\xi|^{q}\int_{\frac{1}{|\xi|}}^{\infty}\langle r\rangle^{-n+q}r^{n-1}\tilde{J}_{\frac{n}{2}-1}(r|\xi|)dr=|\xi|^{q+1-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{\frac{1}{|\xi|}}^{\infty}\langle r\rangle^{-n+q}r^{\frac{n}{2}}J_{\frac{n}{2}-1}(r|\xi|)dr

\displaystyle\quad=|\xi|^{q+1-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{\frac{1}{|\xi|}}^{\infty}\langle r\rangle^{-n+q}r^{\frac{n}{2}}o\left((r|\xi|)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\right)dr

\displaystyle\qquad+C|\xi|^{q+1-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{\frac{1}{|\xi|}}^{\infty}\langle r\rangle^{-n+q}r^{\frac{n}{2}}(r|\xi|)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cos\left(r|\xi|-\left(\frac{n}{4}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\pi-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)dr:=I_{1}(\xi)+I_{2}(\xi).

Now we focus on the first integral as follows.

For
|\xi|\in(0,1]
, we get

\displaystyle|I_{1}(\xi)|=

\displaystyle|\xi|^{q+1-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{\frac{1}{|\xi|}}^{\infty}\langle r\rangle^{-n+q}r^{\frac{n}{2}}o\left((r|\xi|)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\right)dr\lesssim|\xi|^{q+1-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{\frac{1}{|\xi|}}^{\infty}r^{q-\frac{n}{2}}o\left((r|\xi|)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\right)dr

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|\xi|^{q-\frac{n}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\frac{1}{|\xi|}}^{\infty}r^{q-\frac{n}{2}-\frac{3}{2}}dr\lesssim 1.

For
|\xi|\in(1,\infty)
, we arrive at

\displaystyle|I_{1}(\xi)|=

\displaystyle|\xi|^{q+1-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{\frac{1}{|\xi|}}^{\infty}\langle r\rangle^{-n+q}r^{\frac{n}{2}}o\left((r|\xi|)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\right)dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\xi|^{q+1-\frac{n}{2}}\left(\int_{\frac{1}{|\xi|}}^{1}\langle r\rangle^{-n+q}r^{\frac{n}{2}}o\left((r|\xi|)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\right)dr+\int_{1}^{\infty}\langle r\rangle^{-n+q}r^{\frac{n}{2}}o\left((r|\xi|)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\right)dr\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|\xi|^{q+1-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{\frac{1}{|\xi|}}^{1}r^{\frac{n}{2}}o\left((r|\xi|)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\right)dr+|\xi|^{q+1-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{q-\frac{n}{2}}o\left((r|\xi|)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\right)dr

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|\xi|\int_{\frac{1}{|\xi|}}^{1}r^{\frac{n}{2}}o\left((r|\xi|)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\right)dr+|\xi|\int_{1}^{\infty}o\left((r|\xi|)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\right)dr

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|\xi|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\frac{1}{|\xi|}}^{1}r^{\frac{n-3}{2}}dr+|\xi|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{-\frac{3}{2}}dr\lesssim 1.

Next, for the integral
I_{2}(\xi)
, we see that

I_{2}(\xi)=|\xi|^{q+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{\frac{1}{|\xi|}}^{\infty}\langle r\rangle^{-n+q}r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\cos\left(r|\xi|-\left(\frac{n}{4}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\pi-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)dr.

Applying the change of variables
\omega:=r|\xi|
, we have

\displaystyle|I_{2}(\xi)|=

\displaystyle|\xi|^{q+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n}{2}}\left|\int_{1}^{\infty}\left\langle\frac{\omega}{|\xi|}\right\rangle^{-n+q}\left(\frac{\omega}{|\xi|}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\cos(\omega-\theta)\frac{d\omega}{|\xi|}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\xi|^{-n+q}\left|\int_{1}^{\infty}\omega^{q-\frac{n+1}{2}}|\xi|^{n-q}\cos(\omega-\theta)d\omega\right|=\left|\int_{1}^{\infty}\omega^{\alpha}\cos(\omega-\theta)d\omega\right|\lesssim 1,

where

\theta=\left(\frac{n}{4}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\pi+\frac{\pi}{4}\quad\text{ and }\quad\alpha:=q-\frac{n+1}{2}<-\frac{1}{2}.

Therefore, our proof is finished.
∎

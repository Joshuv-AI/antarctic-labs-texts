Survival impact of involvement of lymph node stations 16b1, 9, and 8a during pancreatoduodenectomy for pancreatic and periampullary cancers (PANODE): prospective multicentre study
Authors: J Annelie Suurmeijer, Bobby K Pranger, Leonard W F Seelen, Mahsoem Ali, L Bengt van Rijssen, Dorine S Tseng, Tara M Mackay, Jacob L van Dam, Hjalmar C van Santvoort, Bas Groot Koerkamp, Arantza Farina Sarasqueta, Casper H van Eijck, Mike S L Liem, Geert Kazemier, Vincent B Nieuwenhuijs, Ignace H de Hingh, Joost M Klaase, Joris I Erdmann, Olivier R Busch, I Quintus Molenaar, Vincent E de Meijer, Marc G Besselink
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCurrent guidelines do not recommend routine sampling of lymph node stations (LNS) 16b1, 9, and 8a during pancreatoduodenectomy for pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) and other periampullary cancers because prospective multicentre studies are lacking. This study evaluated the association between the involvement of these LNS and overall survival (OS).MethodsThis prospective multicentre study included patients undergoing pancreatoduodenectomy for PDAC, distal cholangiocarcinoma, ampullary cancer, or duodenal cancer between October 2015 and April 2020. Associations between involvement of LNS 16b1, 9, and 8a and OS were analysed using multivariable Cox regression analyses.ResultsAmong 1218 included patients, 673 had PDAC, 228 had distal cholangiocarcinoma, 212 had ampullary cancer, and 105 had duodenal cancer. LNS 16b1 involvement was associated with worse OS in PDAC (10 versus 24 months; adjusted hazard ratio (aHR) 2.33; 95% confidence interval (c.i.) 1.62 to 3.35), distal cholangiocarcinoma (7 versus 28 months; aHR 6.95; 95% c.i. 2.88 to 16.77), ampullary cancer (9 versus 62 months; aHR 4.35; 95% c.i. 1.73 to 10.94), and duodenal cancer (13 months versus endpoint not reached; aHR 3.95; 95% c.i. 1.15 to 13.47). LNS 8a involvement was associated with worse OS in PDAC (13 versus 22 months; aHR 1.38; 95% c.i. 1.03 to 1.86), distal cholangiocarcinoma (16 versus 26 months; aHR 1.68; 95% c.i. 1.00 to 2.81), and ampullary cancer (16 versus 62 months; aHR 2.89; 95% c.i. 1.51 to 5.53). LNS 9 involvement was associated with worse OS only in ampullary cancer (4 versus 35 months; aHR 8.19; 95% c.i. 2.34 to 28.57).ConclusionLNS 16b1 involvement was associated with poor OS in PDAC and periampullary cancer. Future studies should assess routine preoperative and intraoperative sampling of LNS 16b1 and the impact of neoadjuvant treatment.

Introduction

Pancreatic and periampullary cancers are associated with poor survival. Even after surgical resection and adjuvant systemic therapy, the 5-year survival rate is only 20%1,2. This calls for better patient selection and effective neoadjuvant treatment. One of the strongest prognostic factors after resection is lymph node (LN) involvement. Five-year survival after pancreatoduodenectomy (PD) for pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) is approximately 10% for LN-positive disease3–5. However, adequate preoperative evaluation of LN involvement is difficult because the diagnostic accuracy of computed tomography for the assessment of extraregional LN metastases is poor6,7. Therefore, identification of metastases in LN stations (LNS) typically occurs either during surgery through intraoperative (frozen section) sampling or after surgery at definitive pathological assessment.

Standard lymphadenectomy during PD as defined by the International Study Group for Pancreatic Surgery (ISGPS) includes LNS 8a (common hepatic artery anterior), but not LNS 16b1 (aortocaval) and LNS 9 (coeliac trunk)8. Therefore, involvement of LNS outside of these classic resection margins should be considered as metastatic LNs. Despite this, most members of the ISGPS consensus conference reported that they would continue the resection when LNS 16b1 was positive during the operation8. No consensus could be reached about routine sampling of LNSs 16b1, 9, and 8a due to different expert opinions and variations in interpretation of the literature8–10.

Although some studies have advocated the routine sampling of LNSs 8a11–13 and 16b114–20, prospective multicentre studies comparing the survival of patients undergoing PD for PDAC and other periampullary cancers (that is, distal bile duct, ampulla, and duodenum) based on the involvement of LNSs 16b1, 9, and 8a are lacking. Therefore, it remains unclear whether involvement of these LNSs should impact patient treatment decisions during exploration for PDAC and other periampullary cancers9,21–23. It is important to include both PDAC and other periampullary cancers because preoperative differentiation between them may be difficult. Therefore, the aim of this study was to determine overall survival (OS) in relation to the involvement of LNSs 16b1, 9 and 8a in patients undergoing PD for PDAC and other periampullary cancers.

Methods

Design and study population

This prospective multicentre observational cohort study included adult patients who underwent PD for adenocarcinomas of the pancreas, distal bile duct, ampulla of Vater, and duodenum in seven centres of the Dutch Pancreatic Cancer Group between 1 October 2015 and 1 April 2020. During PD, LNS 8a was harvested separately (Fig. 1). LNSs 9 and 16b1 were specifically harvested for this study and sent separately for definitive histopathological assessment. Data collection for this study was performed by adding study-specific variables regarding sampling of LNSs 16b1, 9, and 8a to the mandatory prospective Dutch Pancreatic Cancer Audit24. Data included sex, age at surgery, body mass index (kg/m2), American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) grade (excluding the disease for which the surgery was being performed), preoperative therapy, vascular resection, histopathological assessment (differentiation grade, margin status, LN ratio), adjuvant therapy, and vital status at the last follow-up. Preoperative therapy for PDAC was mostly administered in clinical trials25,26.

Location of lymph node stations 16b1 (aortocaval), 9 (coeliac trunk), and 8a (common hepatic artery) according to the Japan Pancreas Society (JPS) 47

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Amsterdam UMC (W15-078) and registered in the Dutch Trial Registry and World Health Organization International Clinical Trial Registry Platform (trial ID NL8424). This study was reported in accordance with the STROBE guidelines27. To provide an overview of the literature, a previous systematic review of studies on para-aortic LNs following pancreatectomy for pancreatic cancer (1995–2015)9 was updated (2016–2025) using a narrative PubMed search (Table S1).

Surgical procedures

For LNS 8a, the LN ventral to the common hepatic artery was resected. For the purpose of the present study, LNS 9 was defined as the first encountered LN to the right of the coeliac trunk. This node was found by following the common hepatic artery towards the coeliac trunk after removing LN 8a. For LN 16b1 the nodes in the aortocaval window were resected caudally to the renal vein. The LNSs were marked and sent separately for histopathological assessment. Only specimens with microscopically confirmed lymphoid tissue were included in the denominator for each station. During the study period, some concerns were raised regarding the incidence of chyle leak after sampling LNS 16b1. Therefore, the incidence of chyle leak grade A and grade B/C, as defined by the ISGPS28, was assessed in patients with sampled 16b1 and compared with the incidence of chyle leak in patients in whom 16b1 was not sampled.

Study outcomes

The primary outcome of interest was OS. Secondary outcomes included the prevalence of metastases in LNSs 16b1, 9, and 8a, 30-day mortality, 90-day mortality, and 3-year survival probability.

Sample size

The sample size was calculated based on an expected prevalence of LNS 16b1 involvement of 16%, and 6.25 controls per experimental subject (ratio control : experimental group 100 : 16). Considering a median overall survival of 27.2 months in patients without involvement in LNSs 16b1, 9, and 8a, compared with 15.7 months in patients with LN involvement, 506 patients with sampled LNS 16b1 were needed to reject the null hypothesis that the experimental and control survival curves were equal with a power of 80%17. The type I error probability associated with the test of this null hypothesis was 5%.

Statistical analysis

Patients were stratified and analysed according to histopathological diagnosis. Continuous data are presented as the median with interquartile range (i.q.r.) and were compared between groups using a Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables are expressed as numbers and percentages, and were compared using Pearson’s χ2 test or, in the case of a low number of expected counts, Fisher’s exact test. Survival time was calculated from the date of surgery until the date of death or censored at the date of the last follow-up (1 March 2022). Survival was shown per diagnosis in the total cohort. Furthermore, survival was analysed separately for LNSs 16b1, 9, and 8a using Kaplan–Meier analysis and log-rank tests. Median survival is reported with a 95% confidence interval (c.i.). When < 50% of events (death) occurred, the median survival was stated as not reached (NR). Thirty- and 90-day mortality was defined as all-cause mortality within 30 or 90 days after pancreatic resection, respectively. None of the patients was lost to follow-up; that is, there was only administrative censoring.

Multivariable Cox regression analyses were performed to investigate the association of LNS positivity with OS, adjusted for the following variables: age (continuous), sex (female versus male), ASA grade (> II versus ≤ II), differentiation (poor versus well/moderate), margin status (R0 versus R1/R2), tumour diameter (> 2 versus ≤ 2 cm), and each LNS separately, per diagnosis. Preoperative therapy (yes versus no) and adjuvant therapy (yes versus no) were also included in the model for PDAC. Age was modelled using restricted cubic splines with three knots to account for potential non-linear covariate–outcome relationships. The proportional hazards assumption was assessed using the Grambsch–Therneau test and visual inspection of Schoenfeld residuals. In the event of non-proportional hazards, a stratified Cox regression model was used. Interaction analyses were performed to assess whether the impact of LNS positivity was different between patients with PDAC, cholangiocarcinoma, periampullary cancer, and duodenal cancer. Likelihood ratio tests were used to derive (interaction test) P values. Missing data were included as a separate group when > 5% were missing.

Sensitivity analyses were performed for patients with PDAC who received preoperative chemotherapy or chemoradiotherapy and those who received adjuvant therapy. Missing data were reported but not imputed. A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using RStudio version 4.0.3 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Results

Patient and treatment characteristics

Overall, 1218 patients were included in the study, of whom 673 (55.3%) had PDAC, 228 (18.7%) had distal cholangiocarcinoma, 212 (17.4%) had ampullary cancer, and 105 (8.6%) had duodenal cancer according to the final histopathological assessment. Baseline and treatment characteristics are presented in Table 1. The overall LNS involvement (N+) rates for all LNSs in PDAC, distal cholangiocarcinoma, ampullary cancer, and duodenal cancer were 73.1%, 62.3%, 59.9%, and 73.3%, respectively. LNS 16b1, LNS 9, and LNS 8a had metastasis rates of 13.1%, 10.3%, and 11.6%, respectively (Table 2), suggesting a representative distribution of nodal involvement. Of the 67 patients with 16b1 involvement, 56.7% had no involvement of LNSs 8a and 9, 22.3% had involvement of either LNS 8a or 9, and data were lacking in 14 patients. Of the 444 patients without LNS 16b1 involvement, 35.8% had N0 disease and 64.2% had LN involvement (any LNS) in the final histopathological assessment.

Overall, 118 PDAC patients received preoperative therapy. Less nodal involvement of LNS 8a was found in patients with PDAC who received preoperative therapy compared with those without preoperative therapy (5.5% versus 15.1%; P = 0.007). Involvement of LNS 9 or 16b1 in patients with or without preoperative therapy was similar (LNS 9, 5.7% versus 11.7% (P = 0.384), respectively; LNS 16b1, 10.7% versus 15.3% (P = 0.378), respectively; Table S2). Of the 343 patients with PDAC who received adjuvant therapy, the regimen included gemcitabine monotherapy (190 patients, 55.4%), gemcitabine combination therapy (51, 14.9%), (m)FOLFIRINOX (modified oxaliplatin, irinotecan, leucovorin, and fluorouracil; 78, 22.7%), or other/unknown therapy (24, 7.0%). Rates of chyle leak (grades A–C) were comparable between patients with or without sampled LNS 16b1 (grade A, 17.3% versus 19.1%, respectively; grade B/C, 7.4% versus 9.8%, respectively; P = 0.392). The rate of major complications (Clavien–Dindo ≥ III) was 33.7%.

Survival analysis

At a median follow-up of 36 (i.q.r. 22–52) months, 712 patients (58.5%) had died. Overall, the median survival for PDAC, distal cholangiocarcinoma, and ampullary cancer was 21 (95% c.i. 18 to 23), 24 (95% c.i. 21 to 27), and 41 (95% c.i. 33 to NR) months, respectively (Fig. S1). For duodenal cancer, there were too few events (death in 32% of patients) to reach the median survival. Table 2 presents median survival and 3-year survival probabilities per LNS for each diagnosis. The involvement of LNS 16b1 (versus not), regardless of other LN involvement, was associated with a strikingly worse survival in all diagnoses, with respective median OS of 10 versus 24 months in PDAC (hazard ratio (HR) 2.56; 95% c.i. 1.82 to 3.59; P < 0.001), 7 versus 28 months in distal cholangiocarcinoma (HR 12.14; 95% c.i. 4.37 to 33.72; P < 0.001), 9 versus 62 months in ampullary cancer (HR 5.34; 95% c.i. 2.23 to 2.79; P = 0.001), and 13 months versus NR in duodenal cancer (HR 4.75; 95% c.i. 1.39 to 16.31; P = 0.026; Fig. 2). The respective 3-year survival probabilities for the involvement (versus not) of LNS 16b1 were 5.2% versus 35% for PDAC, 0% versus 45% for distal cholangiocarcinoma, 0% versus 61% for ampullary cancer, and 29% versus 70% for duodenal cancer. Overall, 30-day in-hospital mortality occurred in 24 patients (2.0%), of whom 11 (1.6%) had PDAC, 5 (2.2%) had cholangiocarcinoma, and 8 (3.8%) had ampullary cancer. Ninety-day in-hospital mortality occurred in 56 patients (4.6%), of whom 34 (5.0%) had PDAC, 10 (4.4%) had cholangiocarcinoma, and 12 (5.7%) had ampullary cancer.

There were significant differences in survival between station 16b1-positive and station 16b1-negative patients after pancreatoduodenectomy for a pancreatic carcinoma (P < 0.001), b distal cholangiocarcinoma (P < 0.001), c ampullary carcinoma (P < 0.001), and d duodenal carcinoma (P = 0.007).

Survival based on involvement of LNSs 16b1, 8a, and 9

In the total cohort (all diagnosis) the median survival of 62 months (95% c.i. 39 months to NR) was reached in 158 patients with LNS-negative disease, 24 months (95% c.i. 21 to 28 months) was reached in 284 patients with LNS-positive disease (but without 16b1 involvement), and 9 months (95% c.i. 8 to 12 months; P < 0.001) was reached in 67 patients with LNS 16b1 involved (Fig. S2). Of the 67 patients with LNS 16b1 involvement, only 34.3% survived more than 1 year. The characteristics of this group, compared with patients surviving ≤ 1 year, are presented in Table S3. Patients who survived >1 year more often had an ASA grade ≤ II (95% versus 74%; P = 0.048) and R0 resection (64% versus 27%; P = 0.004).

Involvement of LNS 9 was associated with shorter OS (HR 1.52; 95% c.i. 1.00 to 2.30; P = 0.049), but the confidence intervals were wide and were compatible with both similar OS and considerably shorter OS with involvement of LNS 9. There was some evidence of heterogeneity in the association between LNS 9 involvement and OS across diagnoses (Pinteraction = 0.043; Fig. S3). For instance, median OS was 4 months with involvement of LNS9 versus 35 months without (HR 11.62; 95% c.i. 3.48 to 38.80; P < 0.001), although there were very few patients at risk in the LNS 9-positive and LNS 9-negative subgroups (3 and 61 patients, respectively), resulting in wide confidence intervals.

Involvement of LNS 8a was associated with worse survival compared with LN-negative status (median OS 16 versus 26 months; HR 1.87; 95% c.i. 1.50 to 2.34; P < 0.001; Fig. S4), regardless of the type of periampullary cancer (Pinteraction = 0.28).

Survival in PDAC

In patients with PDAC who received adjuvant treatment, median survival remained worse when LNS 16b1 was involved than when it was not involved (17 (i.q.r. 11–15) versus 28 (i.q.r. 23–35) months; HR 2.26; 95% c.i. 1.8 to 3.69; P < 0.001; Fig. S5). In the group that received adjuvant treatment, only three patients received adjuvant FOLFIRINOX.

Among the 46 patients with PDAC and LNS 16b1 involvement, 49% received adjuvant therapy and reached a median survival of 17 (i.q.r. 11–25) months, with a 3-year survival probability of 11% (95% c.i. 2.8% to 39%). In patients with PDAC and LNS 16b1 involvement without adjuvant treatment, the median survival time was 7 (i.q.r. 5–13) months, with a 3-year survival probability of 0% (HR 2.40; 95% c.i. 1.40 to 3.79; P = 0.002).

Cox regression analysis

In multivariable Cox regression analysis, involvement of LNS 8a and LNS 16b1 was associated with shorter OS, with adjusted HRs (aHRs) of 1.54 (95% c.i. 1.21 to 1.95; P = 0.006) and 2.79 (95% c.i. 2.04 to 3.82; P < 0.001), respectively. In contrast, there was no evidence for an association between the involvement of LN 9 and OS (aHR 1.40; 95% c.i. 0.89 to 2.20; P = 0.16). Interaction analyses showed that the impact of LNS positivity on OS was similar between patients with PDAC, distal cholangiocarcinoma, ampullary cancer, and duodenal cancer (LN8a, Pinteraction = 0.26; LN9, Pinteraction = 0.083; LN16b1, Pinteraction = 0.1; Table 3). Median survival times and aHRs, stratified by tumour type, are presented in Table 2.

Discussion

This first prospective multicentre study on nodal involvement in NLSs 16b1, 9, and 8a in 1218 patients undergoing PD for PDAC and other periampullary cancers found that involvement of LNS 16b1, regardless of other LNS status, was associated with very poor survival. Involvement of LNS 8a was associated with worse survival in patients with PDAC, distal cholangiocarcinoma, and ampullary cancer, whereas involvement of LNS 9 was only associated with worse survival in patients with ampullary cancer. However, because of the limited sample sizes for the non-PDAC subgroups, these findings must be interpreted with caution.

Several retrospective studies29–34 have assessed the impact of LNS 16b1 and LNS 8a involvement on survival in PDAC (Table S1). However, prospective multicentre studies involving PDAC and other periampullary cancers are lacking. This is especially relevant because it is often difficult to distinguish between these entities before surgery. The highest prevalence of LNS 16b1 involvement was observed in patients undergoing PD for duodenal cancer (16%) and PDAC (15%). For PDAC, regional and especially extraregional LN involvement (that is, LNS 16b1) have previously been identified as important negative prognostic factors for PDAC35. One study19 of 45 patients with PDAC reported a comparably poor median OS of 7.9 months in resected patients with LNS 16b1 involvement, compared with 5.9 months in patients who did not undergo resection (P = 0.049). Notably, in that study19, only 38% of patients received adjuvant therapy. A recent multicentre study21 of 320 patients eligible for PD reported a median survival of 11 months in 59 patients with LNS 16b1 involvement after PD, compared with 7 months in 27 patients with 16b1 involvement who received a palliative double-bypass procedure (P = 0.049). However, data on adjuvant treatment regimens were not provided, and periampullary cancers were not separately taken into account. Other, mostly retrospective, studies on PDAC reported varying median survival times after PD with involvement of LNS 16b1 (8–17 months), suggesting that resection may improve OS36. In contrast, the median survival time in LNS 16b1-negative disease can increase up to 31 months15,37. In the present study, survival differences were also found in resected PDAC patients with involvement of LNS 8a and LNS 9, versus patients without LNS 8a or LNS 9 involvement. However, the largest impact on survival was observed for LNS 16b1.

The results of the present study also suggest that neoadjuvant therapy in patients with PDAC may reduce LNS 8a involvement. However, patients who did not undergo resection after neoadjuvant therapy (for example, due to disease progression and, possibly, LN involvement) were not included in this study. Therefore, this finding needs to be confirmed in future studies on patients treated with neoadjuvant therapy. Currently, studies assessing the effect of preoperative therapy on LNS 16b1 involvement are scarce and their clinical relevance remains to be determined38. In the present study, adjuvant therapy in patients with PDAC and LNS 16b1 involvement resulted in a higher median survival of 17 (95% c.i. 11 to 25) months, compared with 7 (95% c.i. 5 to 13) months in patients who did not receive adjuvant therapy. These results are underlined by a recent retrospective study36 including 44 patients after PD for PDAC with LNS 16b1 involvement, in which median survival was higher among patients who received adjuvant therapy than among patients with resection only (23 versus 11 months, respectively; P = 0.004). Clearly, adjuvant therapy requires a reasonable postoperative performance status, and adjuvant therapy leads to improved survival for all patients with PDAC. In the present study, patients with LNS 16b1 involvement who did not receive adjuvant chemotherapy had very poor survival rates. Whether LNS 16b1 involvement is a potential marker of more aggressive tumour biology remains to be determined and should be the subject of future studies. Unfortunately, no information on the molecular subtypes was available for the present study.

A recent retrospective study39 on 153 patients after PD for non-pancreatic periampullary cancers reported LNS 16 involvement in 21% of patients and found worse OS among patients with versus without LNS 16 involvement (median OS 26 months versus NR, respectively; estimated 3-year OS 28% versus 67%, respectively; P = 0.007). The higher OS in patients with LNS 16 involvement in that study may be explained by the higher use of adjuvant therapy (in more than 85% of patients with LNS 16 involvement). Because the literature shows conflicting results, adjuvant therapy is not yet routinely performed in the Netherlands for cholangiocarcinoma, ampullary cancer, or duodenal cancer. The role of adjuvant therapy should be a topic of future studies.

For duodenal cancer, one retrospective single-centre study40 in 65 patients described 12.3% involvement of LNS 16b1 after PD. In that study40, the 5-year survival rates of patients after PD with LNS 16b1 involvement was 0%, compared with 75% among patients without LNS 16b1 involvement. Unlike previous studies41,42, the present study did not find any effect of LNS 8a and LNS 9 involvement on OS after PD for duodenal cancer, versus patients without regional LN metastasis. However, the number of patients with duodenal cancer included in the present study was relatively small, leading to wide confidence intervals; hence, a type II error cannot be excluded and the results must be interpreted with caution.

In the present study, the lowest prevalence of LNS 16b1 involvement was found in patients with ampullary cancer (9.3%) and distal cholangiocarcinoma (9.2%), with a median survival time of 7 and 9 months, respectively. For these diagnoses, survival time differed substantially with LN involvement. A recent retrospective single-centre study43 reported LNS 16b1 involvement in 4.4% of 105 patients with distal cholangiocarcinoma and reported 5-year survival rates of 0%. As with the duodenal cancer cohort, the number of included patients was relatively small, which limits the ability to draw definitive conclusions.

The presence of enlarged 16b1 LNs during work-up for PD should clearly raise concerns about the potential for metastatic disease. Although some centres perform intraoperative sampling of LNS 16b1 with frozen section to assist in surgical decision-making44, its direct clinical utility in deciding whether to proceed with or abort resection remains debated. The findings of the present study primarily highlight the prognostic value of LNS 16b1 positivity rather than establishing an algorithm for intraoperative management.

During the study, some concerns were raised regarding the risk of chyle leak when fully clearing the aortocaval window, resulting in one hospital to stop sampling this LNS. In a recent Dutch study45 on chyle leak in 2159 patients after PD, LNS 16b1 sampling was not associated with chyle leak in a subgroup of 456 patients (208 patients with LNS 16b1 sampled). In the present study, the risk of chyle leak with LNS 16b1 sampling was low, with absolute differences between groups small (grade A, 17.3% versus 19.1%; grade B/C, 7.4% versus 9.8%). These results, combined with similar rates of major morbidity and reoperation rates in the present cohort, suggest that targeted LNS 16b1 sampling is feasible and does not appear to increase these specific postoperative complications. However, the safety evaluation in the present study is limited by the lack of data on other key perioperative outcomes, such as operative times and intraoperative blood loss. Therefore, the overall safety profile of routine LNS 16b1 sampling warrants further investigation. The targeted nature of the sampling distinguishes it from more extensive extended lymphadenectomy protocols, which, historically, have been associated with increased morbidity.

The results of the present study should be interpreted considering certain limitations. First, the patient cohorts for distal cholangiocarcinoma, ampullary cancer, and duodenal cancer were relatively small, limiting the generalizability of the findings for these tumour subgroups. Second, only LNSs 16b1, 9, and 8a were sampled, whereas metastasis to other LNSs (for example LNS 14 and the jejunal mesentery nodes) also have prognostic value46. Third, only patients after pancreatic resection were included and information on aborted explorations (for example, in the event of synchronous distant metastases) was not taken into account. Fourth, most of the patients who received adjuvant therapy were treated with gemcitabine-based therapy. In addition, in the Netherlands, adjuvant therapy is not routinely administered to patients with cholangiocarcinoma, ampullary cancer, or duodenal cancer. Therefore, the potential effect of adjuvant therapy could not be considered for these diagnoses. Because neoadjuvant treatment may lead to reduced involvement of the LNS, one could debate at what stage LN staging is most appropriate. Furthermore, future studies should assess the impact of adjuvant (m)FOLFIRINOX on overall survival in patients with LNS 16b1 involvement. Fifth, preoperative or intraoperative assessments (for example, imaging or frozen section analysis) of the LNSs were not performed in the present study. This may lead to an increased rate of inaccurate assessments and should be a topic for future studies. Sixth, LNS 9 was only analysed in 31% of patients. Therefore, the association of LNS 9 metastases and OS should be interpreted with caution. Finally, the amount of LN tissue removed was left to the discretion of the operating surgeon. Even though the study was performed prospectively, individual between-surgeon and/or between-centre differences cannot be accounted for.

Although no strong recommendation on routine sampling of LNS 16b1 was provided by the ISGPS based on the lack of prospective data, the results of the present prospective study showed that one of eight patients had LNS 16b1 involvement during PD for PDAC and periampullary cancer, which was associated with very poor survival. Because of its prognostic value, routine intraoperative sampling of LNS 16b1 for staging seems justified, especially in patients with PDAC. However, because the feasibility and delivery of adjuvant chemotherapy depend on complex postoperative factors (such as patient recovery and performance status) that cannot be reliably predicted during surgery, LNS 16b1 status alone should not be used as a definitive intraoperative tool to abort resection. The clinical utility of LNS 16b1 sampling in guiding intraoperative decision-making remains a subject of ongoing debate, particularly for non-PDAC periampullary malignancies, where further prospective data are needed. Future studies should investigate the role of neoadjuvant and adjuvant treatment, especially focusing on (m)FOLFIRINOX.

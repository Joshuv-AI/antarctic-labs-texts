A note on cohomological mirror symmetry of toric manifolds
Hao Wen

Abstract
. In this note we describe a logarithmic version of mirror Landau-Ginzburg model for a semi-projective toric manifold and show the ring of state space of the Landau-Ginzburg model is isomorphic to the ℂ \mathbb{C} -valued cohomology of the toric manifold.



1.
Introduction

In mirror symmetry beyond the Calabi-Yau case, the mirror of a toric manifold
X_{\Sigma}
is given by a Landau-Ginzburg model
(Y,f)
, where
Y=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
and
f
is a Laurent polynomial defined on
Y
.
Related mirror statements are well established during the past years, see for example
[
G
,
LLY
,
Ir
,
CCIT
]
.
Among the various results a fundamental one is an isomorphism between the A-model moduli space of
X
and the B-model moduli space of its mirror
(Y,f)
.
This isomorphism identifies the symplectic geometric structure of
X_{\Sigma}
near the large volume limit and the complex geometry structure of
(Y,f)
near the large complex structure limit.
In particular, it implies the quantum cohomology ring of
X_{\Sigma}
is isomorphic to the Jacobi ring of
(Y,f)
.

In this note, we concern the cohomologies
at
the the limit points of the moduli spaces, that’s, we will compare the ordinary cohomology ring of a toric manifold
X_{\Sigma}
with the state space ring of a limiting version of its mirror.
Motivated by the work of Gross and Siebert, we define such mirror to be a logarithmic version of Landau-Ginzburg model
(Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger},f)
, where
Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}
is a log Calabi-Yau variety with a log smooth map to the standard log point
0^{\dagger}
, and
f
is a holomorphic function on
Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}
.
For a fixed semi-projective toric manifold
X_{\Sigma}
, the pair
(Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger},f)
can be defined combinatorially from the defining fan
\Sigma
.
In fact, such
(Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger},f)
is the
q\to 0
limit of the Hori-Vafa mirror in
[
HV
]
. The log sturture and the log smooth map are needed to record the limiting process.

The idea is as follows.
Given a general toric manifold
X_{\Sigma}
with defining fan
\Sigma
, it can be viewed as a gluing of affine toric varieties and its cohomology can be computed by a Čech double complex.
On the other hand, we can construct from combinatoric data of the fan
\Sigma
an affine space
Y_{\Sigma}
and define a differential graded algebra (abbrev. dga)
L(Y_{\Sigma})
on it.
The point is that we can view
Y_{\Sigma}
as a gluing of affine spaces and consider the restriction of
L(Y_{\Sigma})
on each affine piece.
There is a one-to-one correspondence between the pieces on both sides and corresponding pieces have isomorphic cohomologies.
By technically introducing a third dga, we show that the de Rham dga
(\mathcal{A}^{\bullet}(X_{\Sigma}),\text{d})
is quasi-isomorphic to
L(Y_{\Sigma})
.
When
X_{\Sigma}
is semi-projective,
Y_{\Sigma}
can be equipped with a log structure and there is a natural map from
Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}
to the standard log point
0^{\dagger}
. We show that
L(Y_{\Sigma})
is isomorphic to the twisted dga of the Landau-Ginzrbug model
(Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger},f)
.
In this way we explicitly connect the the cohomology ring of
X_{\Sigma}
with the state space ring of its mirror Landau-Ginzburg model.

The cohomology of toric manifolds is well-known, and the concept of logarithmic extension of Landau-Ginburg model also appeared previously in
[
CCIT
]
(see also
[
CMW
]
), hence the novelty of the present note is mainly the explicit connection between the cohomologies of the mirror sides.
The mechanism behind the phenomenon of mirror symmetry is mysterious, attempts to reveal it include homological mirror symmetry (
[
Ko
]
), SYZ conjecture (
[
SYZ
]
) and Gross-Siebert program (
[
GS1
,
GS2
]
).
Though being elementary, we hope the connection described in this note gives a clue on why we can expect the intricate mirror symmetry phenomenon for toric manifolds.

2.
Spaces associated to the defining fan of a toric manifold

Any toric variety can be defined by a fan, so we start by specifying the fan structure.
Let
N\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be a rank
n
lattice and
M
be the dual lattice. Define
N_{\mathbb{R}}:=N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R},M_{\mathbb{R}}:=M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
.
Let
\Sigma
be a smooth fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
, which implies the set of integral generators of rays of every cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
can be complemented into a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
N
. Denote by
\Sigma(k)
the set of cones of dimension
k
in
\Sigma
and let
\Sigma(1)=\{\rho_{1},\cdots,\rho_{d}\}
. Note that a general fan
\Sigma
may not contain any
n
-dimensional cone.

Let
X_{\Sigma}
be the toric variety associated to
\Sigma
, and
X_{\sigma}
be the affine toric variety associated to
\sigma\in\Sigma
.
The smoothness of
\Sigma
implies smoothness of
X_{\Sigma}
and each
X_{\sigma}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}^{k}\times(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n-k}
for
k=\dim\sigma
.
The correspondence
\sigma\mapsto X_{\sigma}
is inclusion preserving, that’s, when
\sigma
is a face of
\sigma^{\prime}
, there is a toric embedding
X_{\sigma}\hookrightarrow X_{\sigma^{\prime}}
.
Let
\mathcal{S}=\{\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{s}\}
be a fixed subset of closed cones of
\Sigma
that cover the support
|\Sigma|
, then
X_{\Sigma}
has a covering given by affine toric varieties indexed by
\mathcal{S}
:

(1)

\displaystyle X_{\Sigma}=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}}X_{\sigma}.

There is another affine variety
Y_{\Sigma}
naturally associated to
\Sigma
.
Recall that a primitive collection of
\Sigma
is a subset
P=\{\rho_{i_{1}},\cdots,\rho_{i_{k}}\}
of
\Sigma(1)
such that
\rho_{i_{1}},\cdots,\rho_{i_{k}}
do not lie in a common cone of
\Sigma
while any subset of
P
do lie in one.
By abuse of notation, let
\rho_{i}
denote also the integral generator of the ray
\rho_{i}
.
Associate to each
\rho_{i}
a complex coordinate
z_{i}
, then each primitive collection corresponds to a monomial
m_{P}=z_{i_{1}}\cdots z_{i_{k}}
.
The Stanley-Reisner ring
R(\Sigma)
is defined as the quotient

\displaystyle R(\Sigma):=\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\cdots,z_{d}]/\langle\{m_{P}|P\text{ is a primitive collection.}\}\rangle.

Geometrically,
R(\Sigma)
can be viewed as the coordinate ring of an affine variety
Y_{\Sigma}
in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, with

\displaystyle Y_{\Sigma}:=\{(z_{1},\cdots,z_{d})|m_{P}=0,\forall P,P\text{ is a primitive collection}\}.

Y_{\Sigma}
is in fact a union of intersections of coordinate hyperplanes: it can be written as
Y_{\Sigma}=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}Y_{\sigma}
,
where
Y_{\sigma}=\{z|z_{i}=0,\rho_{i}\notin\sigma\}
.
The correspondence between cones of
\Sigma
and components of
Y_{\Sigma}
is inclusion preserving:
\sigma_{i}
is a face of
\sigma_{j}
if and only if
Y_{\sigma_{i}}\subset Y_{\sigma_{j}}.

Since the cones in
\mathcal{S}
cover
|\Sigma|
, we have

(2)

\displaystyle Y_{\Sigma}=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}}Y_{\sigma}.

Note again that since
\Sigma
may not contain any
n
-dimensional cone, the components
Y_{\sigma},\sigma\in\mathcal{S}
may have dimension less than
n
.

3.
Presheaves and Čech complexes

We will use the language of presheaves on simplicial complex and the associated Čech complexes to describe the constructions on both sides of mirror symmetry.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex. A (covariant) presheaf
\mathcal{V}
of
\mathbb{C}
-vector space on
\Delta
consists of the following data:

(1)

for every subsimplex
\tau
of
\Delta
, a
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
\mathcal{V}(\tau)
, and

(2)

for every inclusion
\tau^{\prime}\subset\tau
of simplexes, a morphism of
\mathbb{C}
-vector spaces
r=r_{\tau\tau^{\prime}}:\mathcal{V}(\tau^{\prime})\to\mathcal{V}(\tau)
,

such that the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

\mathcal{V}(\emptyset)=0
,

(2)

r_{\tau\tau}
is identity map on
\mathcal{V}(\tau)
,

(3)

if
\tau^{\prime\prime}\subset\tau^{\prime}\subset\tau
are subsimplexes of
\Delta
, then
r_{\tau\tau^{\prime\prime}}=r_{\tau\tau^{\prime}}\circ r_{\tau^{\prime}\tau^{\prime\prime}}
.

One can associate a Čech complex to a presheaf
\mathcal{V}
on
\Delta
.
Let
\Delta_{p}
be the set of
p
-simplex of
\Delta
.
Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{p}(\Delta,\mathcal{V}):=\bigoplus_{\tau\in\Delta_{p}}\mathcal{V}(\tau),\quad\mathcal{C}(\Delta,\mathcal{V})=\bigoplus_{p}\mathcal{C}^{p}(\Delta,\mathcal{V}).

For
\vec{\alpha}=(\alpha(\tau)_{\tau\in\Delta_{p}})\in\mathcal{C}^{p}
, the combinatorial Čech map
\delta:\mathcal{C}^{p}(\Delta,\mathcal{V})\to\mathcal{C}^{p+1}(\Delta,\mathcal{V})
is given by

(\delta\alpha)(\tau_{i_{0}\cdots i_{p+1}})=\sum_{0\leq k\leq p+1}(-1)^{k}r_{\tau_{i_{0}\cdots i_{p+1}}\tau_{i_{0}\cdots\hat{i}_{k}\cdots i_{p+1}}}\alpha(\tau_{i_{0}\cdots\hat{i}_{k}\cdots i_{p+1}}),

where
\tau_{i_{0}\cdots i_{p}}
denotes the simplex with vertices
i_{0},\cdots,i_{p}
and
\hat{i_{k}}
implies the index
i_{k}
is removed. Here a total ordering of the vertices of
\Delta
is fixed and when we write
\tau_{i_{0}\cdots i_{p}}
, it is assumed that
i_{0}<i_{1}<\cdots<i_{p}
. Clearly
\delta^{2}=0
and hence
(\mathcal{C}(\Delta,\mathcal{V}),\delta)
becomes a complex.
Moreover, when each
\mathcal{V}(\tau)
has a ring structure, there is a natural product:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{p}(\Delta,\mathcal{V})\times\mathcal{C}^{q}(\Delta,\mathcal{V})\to\mathcal{C}^{p+q}(\Delta,\mathcal{V})

given by

\displaystyle(\alpha\cup\beta)(\tau_{i_{0}\cdots i_{p+q}}):=r_{\tau_{i_{0}\cdots i_{p+q}}\tau_{i_{0}\cdots i_{p}}}\alpha(\tau_{i_{0}\cdots i_{p}})\cdot r_{\tau_{i_{0}\cdots i_{p+q}}\tau_{i_{p}\cdots i_{p+q}}}\beta(\tau_{i_{p}\cdots i_{p+q}})

It’s well-known that this product structure descends to cohomologies.

In the remaining part of this paper,
\Delta
will always be the
(s-1)
-simplex with vertices
1,2,\cdots,s
, where
s
is the number of cones in
\mathcal{S}
.
To avoid confusion, we will reserve the letter
\sigma
for cones of
\Sigma
and letter
\tau
for subsimplexes of
\Delta
.
Given
\tau\in\Delta
, there is a cone
\sigma_{\tau}\in\Sigma
defined as the intersection of
\sigma_{i}
with
i\in\tau
.
The map
\tau\mapsto{\sigma_{\tau}}
is inclusion reversing:
\tau^{\prime}\subset\tau
implies
\sigma_{\tau}\subset\sigma_{\tau^{\prime}}
.
Note that it is not necessarily one-to-one, since
\sigma_{\tau}
for different
\tau
may be identical.

Let
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{A}^{k}
, by which we mean
\mathcal{V}(\tau)
is the space
\mathcal{A}^{k}(X_{\sigma_{\tau}})
of smooth complex differential
k
-forms on
X_{\sigma_{\tau}}
and
r
is the natural restriction map.
We have the following complex

(3)

\displaystyle 0\to\mathcal{A}^{k}(X_{\Sigma})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r}}{{\to}}\mathcal{C}^{0}(\Delta,\mathcal{A}^{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta}}{{\to}}\mathcal{C}^{1}(\Delta,\mathcal{A}^{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta}}{{\to}}\mathcal{C}^{2}(\Delta,\mathcal{A}^{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta}}{{\to}}\cdots.

Because of partition of unity, it is exact.

Take
\mathcal{V}=\mathscr{O}
, by which we mean
\mathcal{V}(\tau)
is the space
\mathscr{O}(Y_{\sigma_{\tau}})
of algebraic functions on
Y_{\sigma_{\tau}}
and
r
is the restriction map.
Then we have the following complex

(4)

\displaystyle 0\to\mathscr{O}(Y_{\Sigma})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r}}{{\to}}\mathcal{C}^{0}(\Delta,\mathscr{O})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta}}{{\to}}\mathcal{C}^{1}(\Delta,\mathscr{O})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta}}{{\to}}\mathcal{C}^{2}(\Delta,\mathscr{O})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta}}{{\to}}\cdots.

Though partition of unity is no longer available here, we will prove an exactness result that is parallel to (
3
).
The following observation is of fundamental importance.
Given
Y_{\sigma}
and
Y_{\sigma^{\prime}}
, since each of them is an intersection of coordinate hyperplanes, there exist a canonical projection
p:\mathbb{C}^{d}\to Y_{\sigma}
and an inclusion
i:Y_{\sigma^{\prime}}\to\mathbb{C}^{d}
.
Similarly, there exist an inclusion
\hat{i}:Y_{\sigma}\cap Y_{\sigma^{\prime}}\to Y_{\sigma}
and a projection
\hat{p}:Y_{\sigma^{\prime}}\to Y_{\sigma}\cap Y_{\sigma^{\prime}}
.
The observation is that given any function
g
on
Y_{\sigma}
,

i^{*}p^{*}g=\hat{p}^{*}\hat{i}^{*}g

as functions on
Y_{\sigma^{\prime}}
.
In this way, a function
g
on
Y_{\sigma}
determines a function on
Y_{\Sigma}
in an unambiguous way, which by abuse of notation is still denoted by
g
.

Theorem 3.2
.

The complex (
4
) is exact.

The theorem is not completely new to experts, see for example the Appendix A.2 of
[
GS1
]
.
We will provide an elementary proof in the appendix.

4.
A differential graded algebra that computes
H^{\bullet}(X_{\Sigma})

We will construct in this section a dga whose cohomology ring is isomorphic to
H^{\bullet}(X_{\Sigma})
.

4.1.
Local model

We begin by describing the local model.
Let
\sigma
be the cone in
\Sigma
with generating rays
\rho_{1},\cdots,\rho_{k}
.
The de Rham complex
(\mathcal{A}^{\bullet}(X_{\sigma}),\text{d})
computes the cohomology of
X_{\sigma}
.
Since
\Sigma
is smooth,
\rho_{1},\cdots,\rho_{k}
can be complemented into a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis
\rho_{1},\cdots,\rho_{n}
of
N
. Let
l_{1},\cdots,l_{n}
be the dual basis in
M
with the property that
l_{i}(\rho_{j})=\delta_{ij}
, then the dual cone
\sigma^{\vee}
of
\sigma
has generators
l_{1},\cdots,l_{k},\pm l_{k+1},\cdots,\pm l_{n}
and

\displaystyle X_{\sigma}=\,\text{Spec\,}\mathbb{C}[l_{1},\cdots,l_{k}]\times\text{Spec\,}\mathbb{C}[\pm l_{k+1},\cdots,\pm l_{n}].

Let
W_{\sigma}^{\bullet}
be the wedge algebra over the
\mathbb{C}
-vector space with generators
l_{k+1},\cdots,\l_{n}
.
Define

\displaystyle j_{\sigma}:W_{\sigma}^{1}\ni l\to\frac{\text{d}w^{l}}{w^{l}}\in\mathcal{A}^{1}(X_{\sigma})

where
w^{l}
is the monomial in
\mathbb{C}[\pm l_{k+1},\cdots,\pm l_{n}]
corresponding to
l
.
Using the fact that the
\frac{\text{d}w^{l_{i}}}{w^{l_{i}}}
’s generate the cohomology ring of
X_{\sigma}
, one sees that
j_{\sigma}
extends to

\displaystyle j_{\sigma}:(W_{\sigma}^{\bullet},0)\to(\mathcal{A}^{\bullet}(X_{\sigma}),\text{d}),

which is a quasi-isomorphism of differential graded algebras.

Denote by
\Lambda^{\bullet}
the exterior algebra of
M_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes\mathbb{C}
, and let
\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
M
.
We can associate to
Y_{\sigma}
a complex

\displaystyle L(Y_{\sigma}):=(\mathscr{O}(Y_{\sigma})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\Lambda^{\bullet},d_{L})

with

\displaystyle d_{L}(g\otimes\alpha)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\xi_{i}(\rho_{j})z_{j})g\otimes\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{i}}\alpha,

where
\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{i}}
is an odd derivation acting on
\Lambda^{\bullet}
and satisfying
\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{i}}(\xi_{j})=\delta_{ij}
.
Because
d_{L}
acts as a derivation,
L_{\sigma}
has the structure of a dga.
It’s easy to see that
d_{L}
is independent of the choice of basis
\xi_{i}^{\prime}s
, so we can choose the basis
l_{1},\cdots,l_{n}
describe above, under which
d_{L}
becomes

(5)

\displaystyle d_{L}(g\otimes\alpha)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}z_{j}g\otimes\frac{\partial}{\partial l_{j}}\alpha.

Here we equip
L(Y_{\sigma})
with a nontrivial gradation: we assign
\deg z_{i}=2
and
\deg\xi_{i}=1
so that
d_{L}
acts as a differential of degree
1
.
Such a grading may seem strange, however, as we will see in the next section, it’s compatible with definition of
\{f,-\}
on logarithmic polyvector fields and then finally compatible with the cohomological degree of
H(X_{\Sigma})
.
Let
U_{\sigma}^{\bullet}
be the wedge algebra over
\mathbb{C}
with generators
l_{1},\cdots,l_{k}
, then we have
\Lambda^{\bullet}=W_{\sigma}^{\bullet}\otimes U_{\sigma}^{\bullet}
and
L(Y_{\sigma})
is the tensor product of
(W_{\sigma}^{\bullet},0)
and
(\mathscr{O}(Y_{\sigma})\otimes U_{\sigma}^{\bullet},d_{L})
.
The latter is a Koszul complex.
As
z_{1},\cdots,z_{k}
forms a regular sequence in
\mathscr{O}(Y_{\sigma})
,
(\mathscr{O}(Y_{\sigma})\otimes U_{\sigma}^{\bullet},d_{L})
has cohomology
\mathbb{C}\cdot 1
.
One concludes that
L(Y_{\sigma})
has cohomology
W_{\sigma}^{\bullet}
and the natural inclusion

\displaystyle k_{\sigma}:(W_{\sigma}^{\bullet},0)\to L(Y_{\sigma})

is a quasi-isomorphism of dga.

4.2.
Čech model

Next we assemble the constructions above to maps between Čech complexes, for which we need to define compatible restriction maps between local models.

Let
\sigma\subset\sigma^{\prime}
be cones in
\Sigma
, we have
X_{\sigma}\subset X_{\sigma^{\prime}}
and
Y_{\sigma}\subset Y_{\sigma^{\prime}}
.
For
(\mathcal{A}^{\bullet}(X_{\sigma}),\text{d})
, the restriction map

\displaystyle r_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{\mathcal{A}}:\mathcal{A}^{\bullet}(X_{\sigma^{\prime}})\to\mathcal{A}^{\bullet}(X_{\sigma})

is compatible with de Rham differential
d
.
As to
L(Y_{\sigma})
, we define

\displaystyle r_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{L}:L(Y_{\sigma^{\prime}})\to L(Y_{\sigma}),\quad g\otimes\alpha\mapsto g|_{Y_{\sigma}}\otimes\alpha.

By the explicit expression (
5
) of
d_{L}
one sees
r_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{L}
is indeed a chain map.
For
(W_{\sigma},0)
, one observes that
W_{\sigma}^{1}
is exactly the annihilator of
\sigma
in
M_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes\mathbb{C}
.
When
\sigma\subset\sigma^{\prime}
, we have
W_{\sigma^{\prime}}^{1}\subset W_{\sigma}^{1}
. So we can define

\displaystyle r_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{W}:W_{\sigma^{\prime}}^{\bullet}\to W_{\sigma}^{\bullet}

as the inclusion map.
It’s as expected that the maps
j_{\sigma}
and
k_{\sigma}
defined above are compatible with the various restriction maps:
the identities

\displaystyle r_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{A}\circ j_{\sigma^{\prime}}=j_{\sigma}\circ r_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{W}:(W_{\sigma^{\prime}}^{\bullet},0)\to(\mathcal{A}^{\bullet}(X_{\sigma}),\text{d})

and

\displaystyle r_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{L}\circ k_{\sigma^{\prime}}=k_{\sigma}\circ r_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{W}:(W_{\sigma^{\prime}}^{\bullet},0)\to L(Y_{\sigma})

both follow from the explicit constructions above.

Define
\mathcal{A}:=\oplus\mathcal{A}^{k,p}
with
\mathcal{A}^{k,p}:=\oplus_{\tau\in\Delta_{p}}\mathcal{A}^{k}(X_{\sigma_{\tau}})
.
Using that restriction maps are compatible with the differential map, we obtain a Čech double complex
(\mathcal{A},\delta,\text{d})
.
By the exactness of (
3
), we have the following quasi-isomorphism

(6)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{A}^{\bullet}(X_{\Sigma}),\text{d})\hookrightarrow(\mathcal{A},\delta,\text{d}).

Similar to the definition of
L(Y_{\sigma})
, we can define a dga on
Y_{\Sigma}
as

\displaystyle L(Y_{\Sigma}):=

\displaystyle(\mathscr{O}(Y_{\Sigma})\otimes\Lambda^{\bullet},d_{L}),

\displaystyle d_{L}(g\otimes\alpha)=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\xi_{i}(\rho_{j})z_{j})g\otimes\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{i}}\alpha.

We can also define a chain map

\displaystyle r_{\sigma}:L(Y_{\Sigma})\to L(Y_{\sigma}),\quad g\otimes\alpha\mapsto g|_{Y_{\sigma}}\otimes\alpha.

Write
L:=\oplus L^{k,p}
with
L^{k,p}:=\oplus_{\tau\in\Delta_{p}}\mathscr{O}(Y_{\sigma_{\tau}})\otimes\Lambda^{k}
, we obtain a Čech double complex
(L,\delta,d_{L})
.
By Theorem
3.2
, the complex

\displaystyle 0\to\mathscr{O}(Y_{\Sigma})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\Lambda^{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r}}{{\to}}\mathcal{C}^{0}(\Delta,\mathscr{O}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\Lambda^{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta}}{{\to}}\mathcal{C}^{1}(\Delta,\mathscr{O}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\Lambda^{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta}}{{\to}}\cdots,

is exact for
\forall k
, so we get another quasi-isomorphism

(7)

\displaystyle L(Y_{\Sigma})\hookrightarrow(L,\delta,d_{L}).

Proposition 4.1
.

L(Y_{\Sigma})
and
(\mathcal{A}(X_{\Sigma}),\text{d})
are quasi-isomorphic dga’s, hence
L(Y_{\Sigma})
computes the
\mathbb{C}
-valued cohomology ring of
X_{\Sigma}
.

Proof.

By the discussions above, it suffices to show the double complexes
(\mathcal{A},\delta,\text{d})
and
(L,\delta,d_{L})
are quasi-isomorphic as dga’s.
To show this, we define a third double complex
(W,\delta,0)
with

\displaystyle W:=\oplus W^{k,p},\quad W^{k,p}:=\oplus_{\tau\in\Delta_{p}}W^{k}_{\sigma_{\tau}}.

Since
j_{\sigma}
and
k_{\sigma}
are compatible with restriction maps, they can be assembled into maps of double complexes

\displaystyle j:(W,\delta,0)\to(\mathcal{A},\delta,\text{d})

and

\displaystyle k:(W,\delta,0)\to(L,\delta,d_{L}).

Since
j_{\sigma}
’s and
k_{\sigma}
’s are all quasi-isomorphisms, both
j
and
k
induces isomorphic vertical cohomology groups. By the theory of spectral sequence, both
j
and
k
are quasi-isomorphisms and hence
(\mathcal{A},\delta,\text{d})
and
(L,\delta,d_{L})
are quasi-isomorphic double complexes.
Moreover, it’s obvious that the all various chain maps preserve the product structure on Čech complexes, so
(\mathcal{A},\delta,\text{d})
and
(L,\delta,d_{L})
are indeed quasi-isomorphic as dga’s.
∎

5.
Landau-Ginzburg mirror for semiprojective toric manifolds

The results in the previous sections are valid for all toric manifolds.
In this section we will interpret
L(Y_{\Sigma})
as the twisted complex of a Landau-Ginzburg model, under the additional assumption that
X_{\Sigma}
is semi-projective.
By Proposition 7.2.9 of
[
CLS
]
,
X_{\Sigma}
being semi-projective is equivalent to saying
X_{\Sigma}
is the toric variety of a full dimensional lattice polyhedron
P\subset M_{\mathbb{R}}
.
As the normal fan of
P
,
\Sigma
has full dimensional convex support.
We can define a strictly convex
\Sigma
-piecewise linear function with integral slopes on
|\Sigma|
as follows

\displaystyle\varphi(n):=-\text{inf}\,\{(m,n)|m\in P\}.

Consider the subset of
N_{\mathbb{R}}\oplus\mathbb{R}
defined as

Q=\{(n,r)|n\in|\Sigma|,r\geq\varphi(n)\},

this is a strictly convex rational polyhedral cone and hence defines an affine toric variety

\mathscr{Y}=\text{Spec}(\mathbb{C}[Q\cap(N\oplus\mathbb{Z})]).

Let
Q_{v}
be the set of vertical components of the boundary
\partial Q
of
Q
.
Such component will appear only when
|\Sigma|
is a proper subset of
N_{\mathbb{R}}
.
They define a divisor
\mathcal{D}
in
\mathscr{Y}
.
Denote by
Q_{h}
the set of horizontal components of
\partial Q
, it defines another divisor
Y
in
\mathscr{Y}
.
To identify
Y
, consider the natural inclusion
\mathbb{N}\hookrightarrow Q\cap(N\oplus\mathbb{Z})
given by
k\mapsto(0,k)
.
This induces a map
\pi:\mathscr{Y}\to\mathbb{C}=\text{Spec}(\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{N}])
, and
Y
is exactly the fiber over
0
.
In fact,
Y
is isomorphic to
Y_{\Sigma}
.
To see this, we define a monoid structure on
(Q_{h}\cap(N\otimes\mathbb{Z}))\cup\{\infty\}
as follows:

(8)

\displaystyle p+q=\left\{\begin{aligned} p+q\quad&\text{if }p,q,p+q\in Q_{h};\\
\infty\quad&\text{otherwise}.\end{aligned}\right.

By formally putting
z^{\infty}=0
, we have
Y=\text{Spec}\,\mathbb{C}[(Q_{h}\cap(N\otimes\mathbb{Z}))\cup\{\infty\}]
.
Let
p:Q_{h}\cap(N\otimes\mathbb{Z})\to\Sigma\cap N
be the canonical projection induced by
p:N\oplus\mathbb{Z}\to N
, which is one-to-one and induces a monoid structure on
\overline{\Sigma}:=(\Sigma\cap N)\cup\{\infty\}
.
We have
Y\cong\text{Spec\,}\mathbb{C}[\overline{\Sigma}]
and hence

Y\cong Y_{\Sigma}

by the definition of
Y_{\Sigma}
.

To sum up, we get a flat morphism
\pi:\mathcal{Y}\to\mathbb{C}
together with a reduced divisor
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{Y}
.
It is easy to see they form a toric degeneration of CY-pairs over
\mathbb{C}
in the sense of Definition 1.8 in
[
GS
]
.
In particular, if we write
D:=\mathcal{D}\cap Y_{\Sigma}
, then the pair
(Y_{\Sigma},D)
forms a totally degenerate CY-pair.

In the following we need some knowledge of log geometry, which can be found in
[
K
]
.
Equip
\mathscr{Y}
with the log structure induced by the inclusion
Y_{\Sigma}\cup\mathcal{D}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{Y}
and equip
\mathbb{C}
with the log structure induced by the inclusion
0\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
.
Using the superscript
{\dagger}
to emphasize that a variety is equipped with a given log structure, we get log smooth maps

\Phi:\mathscr{Y}^{\dagger}\to\mathbb{C}^{\dagger}

and

\phi:Y_{\Sigma}^{{\dagger}}\to 0^{\dagger},

where the latter is obtained by pulling back the log structure on corresponding ambient spaces.

Lemma 5.1
.

The map
\phi:Y^{{\dagger}}\to 0^{\dagger}
is log smooth.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem 4.1 of
[
K
]
.
∎

Since
\phi
is log smooth, the sheaves
\Omega^{\bullet}_{Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}/0^{\dagger}}
and
\Theta^{\bullet}_{Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}/0^{\dagger}}
of relative log differentials and log derivations are locally free on
Y_{\Sigma}
.
These sheaves can be described explicitly as follows.
By the semi-projectivity of
X_{\Sigma}
,
|\Sigma|
is covered by
n
-dimensional cones. Let
\sigma_{M}
be one of them.
By smoothness of
\Sigma
, we can assume without loss of generality that
\rho_{1}=e_{1},\cdots,\rho_{n}=e_{n}
is the set of generating rays of
\sigma_{M}
.

Assume that when
n+1\leq l\leq d
,

\rho_{l}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{il}\rho_{i}=0.

By construction of
\pi:\mathscr{Y}\to\mathbb{C}
, we have

z_{l}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}^{-a_{il}}=t^{m}

for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, where
t
is the generator of
\text{Spec}\,\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{N}]\cong\mathbb{C}[t]
.
Hence

(9)

\frac{\text{d}z_{l}}{z_{l}}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{il}\cdot\frac{\text{d}z_{i}}{z_{i}}=m\frac{\text{d}t}{t},\quad\forall n+1\leq l\leq d

and
\Omega^{1}_{Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}/0^{\dagger}}
is defined as the
\mathscr{O}_{Y_{\Sigma}}
-module
\oplus_{i=1}^{d}\mathscr{O}_{Y_{\Sigma}}\frac{\text{d}z_{i}}{z_{i}}
modulo the relations

(10)

\frac{\text{d}z_{l}}{z_{l}}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{il}\cdot\frac{\text{d}z_{i}}{z_{i}}=0,\quad\forall n+1\leq l\leq d.

We define
\Omega^{k}_{Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}/0^{\dagger}}:=\wedge^{k}\Omega^{1}_{Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}/0^{\dagger}}
.
On any
n
-dimensional component
Y_{\sigma}
of
Y_{\Sigma}
, any
\alpha\in\Omega^{k}_{Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}/0^{\dagger}}
has a unique representative
\alpha|_{Y_{\sigma}}
given by an algebraic
k
-form with at most log poles on
Y_{\sigma}\cap\text{Sing}\,Y_{\Sigma}
.
If
Y_{\sigma_{i}}
and
Y_{\sigma_{j}}
has
(n-1)
-dimensional intersection
D_{ij}
, then

\text{Res}_{D_{ij}}\alpha|_{Y_{\sigma_{i}}}+\text{Res}_{D_{ij}}\alpha|_{Y_{\sigma_{j}}}=0,

where
Res
denotes the Poincaré residue map.
Write
\theta_{i}:=z_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}
, then
\Theta^{1}_{Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}/0^{\dagger}}
is the
\mathscr{O}_{Y_{\Sigma}}
-submodule

(11)

\{\sum_{i=1}^{d}g_{i}\theta_{i}\big|g_{l}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}a_{il},\forall n+1\leq l\leq d\}

of
\oplus_{i=1}^{d}\mathscr{O}_{Y_{\Sigma}}\theta_{i}
and
\Theta^{k}_{Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}/0^{\dagger}}=\wedge^{k}\Theta^{1}_{Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}/0^{\dagger}}
.
We will call global sections of
\Theta^{\bullet}_{Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}/0^{\dagger}}
logarithmic polyvector fields.
More explicitly,
\Theta^{1}_{Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}/0^{\dagger}}
is a rank
n
free
\mathscr{O}_{Y_{\Sigma}}
-module with generators
\tilde{\theta}_{i},1\leq i\leq n
, where
\tilde{\theta}_{i}=\theta_{i}+\sum_{l=n+1}^{d}a_{il}\theta_{l}
.
The map
\tilde{\theta}_{i}\mapsto\theta_{i}
gives an isomorphism

(12)

\Theta^{1}_{Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}/0^{\dagger}}\cong\bigoplus_{1\leq i\leq n}\mathscr{O}_{Y_{\Sigma}}\cdot\theta_{i}.

There is a global nowhere vanishing log differential
n
-form
\Omega
such that it can be written as
\wedge_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\text{d}z_{i}}{z_{i}}
on
Y_{\sigma_{M}}
, hence
Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}
is in fact log Calabi-Yau.
Denote
f=z_{1}+z_{2}+\cdots+z_{d}
, then we get a logarithmic version of Landau-Ginzburg model

\displaystyle(Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger},f)

and it will be regarded as the mirror of
X_{\Sigma}
.

Remark 5.2
.

Such mirror of
X_{\Sigma}
should be viewed as (generalization of) a limiting version of Hori-Vafa mirror in
[
HV
]
.
Take
X_{\Sigma}=\mathbb{P}^{1}
for instance.
The Hori-Vafa mirror of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is given by a family of Landau-Ginzburg models parametrized over
q\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
:

\displaystyle(\mathbb{C}^{*},f=z+\frac{q}{z}).

The
q=0
limit corresponds to the large complex structure limit.
Rewrite the family as

\displaystyle(Y_{q}:=\{z_{1}z_{2}=q\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{2},f=z_{1}+z_{2}),

then when
q=0
,
Y_{q}
becomes
\{z_{1}z_{2}=0\}
. This is exactly the variety
Y_{\Sigma}
given by the defining fan of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
The log structure on
Y_{\Sigma}
and the log smooth map to
0^{\dagger}
record the information that
Y_{\Sigma}
is the central fiber of the family
\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C},(z_{1},z_{2})\mapsto z_{1}z_{2}
.

When written as a log differential,
\text{d}f=\sum_{i=1}^{d}z_{i}\frac{\text{d}z_{i}}{z_{i}}
defines a map
\text{d}f\wedge:\Omega^{\bullet}_{Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}/0^{\dagger}}\to\Omega^{\bullet+1}_{Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}/0^{\dagger}}
.
As usual, contraction with
\Omega
defines a map

\Theta^{k}_{Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}/0^{\dagger}}\to\Omega^{n-k}_{Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}/0^{\dagger}},\quad\theta\mapsto\theta\vdash\Omega.

It then induces from
\text{d}f\wedge
a map

\{f,-\}:\Theta^{\bullet}_{Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}/0^{\dagger}}\to\Theta^{\bullet-1}_{Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}/0^{\dagger}}.

(\Theta^{\bullet}_{Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}/0^{\dagger}},\{f,-\})
is a differential graded algebra since
\{f,-\}
is a derivation.
Under the identification (
12
), we can write

\{f,-\}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(z_{i}+\sum_{l=n+1}^{d}a_{il}z_{i})\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{i}},

where
\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{i}}
is an odd derivation satisfying
\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{i}}(\theta_{j})=\delta_{ij}
.
We equip
(\Theta^{\bullet}_{Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}/0^{\dagger}},\{f,-\})
with a grading structure by specifying
\deg z_{i}=2
and
\deg\theta_{i}=1
so that
\{f,-\}
has degree
1
.

Theorem 5.3
.

When
X_{\Sigma}
is a semi-projective toric manifold, then

1)

The dga
(\Theta^{\bullet}_{Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}/0^{\dagger}}(Y_{\Sigma}),\{f,-\})
is isomorphic to
L(Y_{\Sigma})
;

2)

There is a ring isomorphism

\displaystyle H(\Theta^{\bullet}_{Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}/0^{\dagger}}(Y_{\Sigma}),\{f,-\})\cong H(X_{\Sigma}).

Proof.

Statement 2) follows from statement 1) and Proposition
4.1
.
For statement 1), one needs only to choose a basis
\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n}
of
M
that satisfies
\xi_{i}(\rho_{j})=\delta_{ij},\forall 1\leq j\leq n
, then the map
\theta_{i}\mapsto\xi_{i},i=1,\cdots,n
gives the desired isomorphism.
∎

Example 5.4
.

When
X_{\Sigma}
is projective,
\Theta^{\bullet}_{Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}/0^{\dagger}}(Y_{\Sigma})
is the exterior algebra over the Stanley-Reisner ring
R(\Sigma)
with generators
\theta_{i}=z_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}},1\leq i\leq n
and
\{f,-\}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(z_{i}+\sum_{l=n+1}^{d}a_{il}z_{i})\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{i}}
.
It’s straight-forward to verify that
z_{i}+\sum_{l=n+1}^{d}a_{il}z_{i},1\leq i\leq n
form a regular sequence in
R(\Sigma)
, hence

\displaystyle H(\Theta^{\bullet}_{Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}/0^{\dagger}}(Y_{\Sigma}),\{f,-\})\cong R(\Sigma)/\langle z_{i}+\sum_{l=n+1}^{d}a_{il}z_{i}\rangle_{1\leq i\leq n}.

The right hand side is exactly the well-known cohomology ring of
X_{\Sigma}
.
The reason for the above gradation is now clear: it induces a grading on
H(\Theta^{\bullet}_{Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}/0^{\dagger}}(Y_{\Sigma}),\{f,-\})
that is compatible with the usual cohomological grading of
H(X_{\Sigma})
.

The above example is included in
[
CMW
]
. In this note
X_{\Sigma}
is allowed to be only semi-projective.

Example 5.5
.

Let
\Sigma
be the
n
-dimensional cone generated by the basis
e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}
of
N
.
On the A-side,
X_{\Sigma}=\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
H(\mathbb{C}^{n},\mathbb{C})\cong\mathbb{C}
.
On the mirror side,
Y_{\Sigma}=\text{Spec}\,\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}]\cong\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
D
is the divisor defined by
z_{1}\cdots z_{n}=0
.
In this case,
\Theta^{\bullet}:=\Theta^{\bullet}_{Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}/0^{\dagger}}(Y_{\Sigma})
is the exterior algebra over
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}]
with generators
\theta_{i}=z_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}},1\leq i\leq n
.
The twisted dga then has the form

\displaystyle 0\to\Theta^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sum z_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{i}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Theta^{n-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sum z_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{i}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\cdots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sum z_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{i}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Theta^{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sum z_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{i}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}]\to 0.

Since
z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}
form a regular sequence in
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}]
, we conclude that
H(\Theta^{\bullet}_{Y_{\Sigma}^{\dagger}/0^{\dagger}}(Y_{\Sigma}),\{f,-\})\cong\mathbb{C}
.
The cohomolgy on both sides concentrate on degree
0
.

The above example serves as a local model, we may consider the construction for general semi-projective toric manifolds as a gluing of such local pieces.

Example 5.6
.

Let
\Sigma
be the convex fan in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}

Centralizers in the plactic monoid
Bruce E. Sagan, Alexander N. Wilson

Abstract
Let u u be a word over the positive integers. Motivated in part by a question from representation theory, we study the centralizer set of u u which is C ⁡ ( u ) = { w ∣ u ​ w is Knuth-equivalent to w ​ u } . C(u)=\{w\mid\text{$uw$ is Knuth-equivalent to $wu$}\}. In particular, we give various necessary conditions for w w to be in C ⁡ ( u ) C(u) . We also characterize C ⁡ ( u ) C(u) when u u has few letters, when it has a single repeated entry, or when it is a certain type of decreasing sequence. We consider c n , m ​ ( u ) c_{n,m}(u) , the number of w ∈ C ⁡ ( u ) w\in C(u) of length n n with max ⁡ w ≤ m \max w\leq m . We prove that for | u | = 1 |u|=1 the value of this function depends only on the relative sizes of u u and m m and not on their actual values. And for various u u we use Stanley’s theory of poset partitions to show that, for fixed n n , c n , m ​ ( u ) c_{n,m}(u) is a polynomial in m m with certain degree and leading coefficient. We end with various conjectures and directions for further research.



1
Introduction

Let
{\mathbb{P}}=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
and
{\mathbb{N}}={\mathbb{P}}\uplus\{0\}
denote the positive and nonnegative integers, respectively. For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
we let

[n]=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}.

In addition, for any set
S
we will use either
\#S
or
|S|
to denote the cardinality of
S
.
We apply the same notation to words
w
over
S
and call
|w|
the
length
of
w
.
Finally, we let
S^{*}
be the
Kleene closure
of
S
, that is, all words with elements from
S
.

We will assume the reader is familiar with the Robinson-Schensted-Knuth (RSK) correspondence as well as Schützenberger’s jeu-de-taquin (jdt). Background on these operations can be found in the texts of Sagan
[
Sag01
,
Sag20
]
or Stanley
[
Sta24
]
. In particular, if
w\in{\mathbb{P}}^{*}
then we will let

P(w)
denote the insertion tableau of
w
under RSK. Recall that
v,w\in{\mathbb{P}}^{*}
differ by a
Knuth transposition
if there are words
x,y
and elements
a,b,c
such that either

\text{$v=xacby$ and $w=xcaby$ with $a\leq b<c$},

or

\text{$v=xbacy$ and $w=xbcay$ with $a<b\leq c$}.

Furthermore, we say that
v,w
are
Knuth-equivalent
, written
v\equiv w
, if one can obtain
w
from
v
by applying a sequence of Knuth transpositions. When Knuth introduced this equivalence relation
[
Knu70
]
, he proved that

v\equiv w\text{ if and only if }P(v)=P(w).

The
plactic monoid
is
{\mathbb{P}}^{*}
modulo Knuth equivalence.
It was first considered from this perspective by Lascoux and Schützenberger
[
LS81
]
.

Given a word
u\in{\mathbb{P}}^{*}
, our primary object of study will be the
centralizer of
u
in the plactic monoid which is

C(u)=\{w\mid\text{$uw\equiv wu$}\},

or equivalently

C(u)=\{w\mid P(uw)=P(wu)\}.

In particular, we wish to characterize
C(u)
for certain
u
and also consider the enumerative properties of the integers

c_{n,m}(u)=\#\{w\in C(u)\mid\#w=n\text{ and }\max w\leq m\}.

(1)

Beside the fact that
C(u)
is a natural set to study, our research
is motivated by work in preparation by the second author and Nate Harman concerning commuting crystal structures on “lexicographic bitableaux,” semistandard tableaux filled with entries in
[m]\times[n]
ordered lexicographically. In this setting, it is natural for one crystal operation to transform a reading word of the tableau by cutting out a subword and pasting it in a different location. In order for these crystals to commute, the transformed word must be Knuth-equivalent to the original reading word.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In the next section we will collect some necessary conditions for
w
to be in
C(u)
which will prove useful in the sequel. In section
3
we will characterize the
w\in C(u)
for certain
u
with
\#u\leq 3
. In particular, we will describe
C(u)
for any
u
of length
1
.
Next, we will describe
C(u)
for certain special
u
of arbitrary length such as those which consist of a single repeated integer or are of the form
m(m-1)\ldots 1
for some
m\in{\mathbb{P}}
. Section
5
is devoted to the study of the
c_{n,m}(u)
as defined in (
1
). In particular, if
|u|=1
we show that their values depend only on the relative sizes of
m
and
u
. Furthermore, we use Stanley’s theory of poset partitions to prove that for certain
u
and fixed
n
, they are polynomials in
m
. We end with a section containing open problems and conjectures.

Necessary Conditions

In this section, we collect results giving general constraints on the tableaux
P=P(w)
for

. In particular, we will give a criterion which will permit us to bound the size of the elements in the first few rows of

by the maximum value in

in the southwest and

in the northeast. The tableau is then brought to left-justified shape using jdt slides.

Given any sequence
R
and any element
a
we let

m_{a}(R)=\text{ the multiplicity of $a$ in $R$}.

Also, for a semistandard Young tableau (SSYT)
P
with rows
R_{1},R_{2},\ldots
, we consider the weak composition

\alpha_{a}(P)=(m_{a}(R_{1}),\ m_{a}(R_{2}),\ \ldots).

For example, if
P
is the tableau in Figure
1
,
then

\alpha_{4}(P)=(2,1,0,1,0,0,\ldots).

We will compare weak compositions
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots)
and
\beta=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\ldots)
using
dominance order
where
\alpha\preceq\beta
if

\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{i}\leq\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}+\cdots+\beta_{i}

for all
i\geq 1
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
a\neq b
be distinct positive integers and let
w\in{\mathbb{P}}^{*}
. Then

\alpha_{b}(P(wa))\preceq\alpha_{b}(P(w))\preceq\alpha_{b}(P(aw)).

Proof.

Since
a\neq b
, the number of
b
’s will not change in passing from
P(w)
to
P(wa)
. Also, a
b
can only be bumped to the next row by the RSK algorithm.
So,
\alpha_{b}(P(wa))
is obtained from
\alpha_{b}(P(w))
by subtracting one from the
i
th entry and adding one to the
(i+1)
st entry if
b
is bumped from row
i
to row
i+1
, hence
\alpha_{b}(P(wa))\preceq\alpha_{b}(P(w))
.

For the second inequality, note that values
b
can only be slid left or up by the jdt algorithm. It follows that
\alpha_{b}(P(aw))
is obtained from
\alpha_{b}(P(w))
by adding one to the
i
th entry and subtracting one from the
(i+1)
st entry if a
b
is slid from row
i+1
to row
i
, hence
\alpha_{b}(P(aw))\succeq\alpha_{b}(P(w))
.
∎

We can now prove our first necessary condition for when
w\in C(u)
.

Corollary 2.2
.

If
w\in C(u)
and
b\not\in u
then

\alpha_{b}(P(wu))=\alpha_{b}(P(w))=\alpha_{b}(P(uw)).

In particular, no
b\not\in u
can be bumped by the insertion of
u
into
P(w)
to form
P(wu)
. And such an element
b
cannot slide between two rows in the computation of
P(uw)
by jdt.

Proof.

Since
w\in C(u)
we have
P(wu)=P(uw)
. So,
\alpha_{b}(P(wu))=\alpha_{b}(P(uw))
for any
b
.
Now using the fact that
b\not\in u
and repeated application of the previous lemma finishes the proof.
∎

We can now bound the size of certain elements in
P(w)
for
w\in C(u)
in terms of the maximum value in
u
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Given
u
and
w\in C(u)
we let
P=P(w)
have rows
R_{i}
for
i\geq 1
. Also let
m=\max u
. If
u
contains a subsequence
m,m-1,\dots,m-k+1
, then

\max R_{i}\leq m

for
1\leq i\leq k
.

Proof.

First we claim that if a semistandard tableau
T
contains an
a
in a higher row than an
a+1
, then this will continue to be the case after any insertion into
T
. This is clear if the
a
and
a+1
are at least two rows apart since an element can only be bumped from one row to the next. The other case is if
a
is bumped from the row directly above
a+1
. But then the
a
must bump one of the elements equal to
a+1
in the row it enters and so the claim still holds.

Using the assumed subsequence of
u
and an argument like that in the previous paragraph, we see that in forming
P(wu)
from
P
we must have the elements
m,m-1,\ldots,m-k+1
from
u
in separate rows with
m
in the lowest row. This means
m
must have traveled through at least the first
k
rows to its present position. But if one of these rows contains an element from
P
larger then
m
, then
m
would bump the smallest such element to the next row.
This contradicts Corollary
2.2
which completes the demonstration.
∎

For example, if

. More specifically, if

We will say that cell

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose
u
consists of a single integer which we also denote by
u
.
Also, use
R_{1},R_{2},\ldots,R_{l}
to denote the rows of
P=P(w)
.
Then the set

is all

such that

satisfies

(a)

\max R_{1}\leq u
, and

(b)

for

\#R_{i}(<u)=\#R_{i+1}(\leq u).

Proof.

We first prove that if
P
satisfies the given restrictions then

w\in C(u)
. That is, we need to prove
P(wu)=P(uw)
.
But by (a),
P(wu)
is obtained from
P
by appending
u
to the first row and so has rows
R_{1}u,R_{2},\ldots,R_{l}
.

To compute
P(uw)
, we perform jeu-de-taquin on the skew tableau with
u
in the
(l+1,1)
cell and
P
in the first
l
rows starting in column
2
.
First consider the slide into the cell
(i,1)
for
i\geq u
. By reverse induction on
i
, we can assume row
i+1
is
uR_{i+1}
so that there is a
u
in cell
(i+1,1
).
And, by the bound on

, the smallest entry in row

is at least
u
since it comes from
R_{i}
of the semistandard tableau
P
. It follows that the
u
in
(i+1,1)
will move into
(i,1)
. We now claim that the rest of row
i+1
will slide left one cell. This is because, again by reverse induction,
R_{i+2}
slid one cell left when filling
(i+1,1)
. Thus the element
x
below any hole created in row
i+1
will always be greater than the element
y
to the hole’s right since
x
was originally directly below
y
in a semistandard tableau. This forces a horizontal slide.

Now consider what happens when filling
(u-1,1)
. Note that row
u
is currently
uR_{u}
.
So the slide will start by moving all the elements of
R_{u-1}
which are smaller than
u
one cell left.
But by (b) with

, the number of such elements is one less than the number of elements less than or equal to
u
in
uR_{u}
. It follows that the hole created will have a
u
below and an element at least as large as

to its left. Thus that hole will be filled by moving the
u
below into row
u-1
. Now one can see by an argument in the previous paragraph, that the rest of row

will slide one unit left. So at the end of this slide row

will be

while row

will be

with an extra

added. This is the base case of a reverse induction showing that after sliding into

for

, the resulting

and the

with a

th rows of the jeu-de-taquin tableau are

. And since by (a) all elements of

are at most

, the result of adding

in the first paragraph, so we are done with this direction.

so that

.

, and thus

, has rows

.

for all

. It can be shown that this implies (b) using similar ideas as in the first half of the proof. We leave the details to the reader.

We will now give a result which tests for

Theorem 3.2
.

If
|u|=1
then

C(u)=\{w\mid\text{every column of $P=P(w)$ contains a $u$}\}.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
w
satisfies the column condition above if and only if it satisfies the row conditions (a) and (b) of the previous theorem.

Assume that (a) and (b) hold and, towards a contradiction, that column
j
of
P
does not contain a
u
. By (a), we must have
P_{1,j}<u
. Let
i
be maximal such that
P_{i,j}<u
. The rows of

being weakly increasing implies that

\#R_{i}(<u)\geq j
. But by the maximality of

and the fact that column

contains no

\#R_{i+1}(\leq u)<j
. So, using (b),

j\leq\#R_{i}(<u)=\#R_{i+1}(\leq u)<j

which is a contradiction.

For the converse, assume every column contains a
u
. To verify (a) we again argue by contradiction and assume that
P_{1,j}>u
for some
j
. But then strict increase of the columns implies that column
j
has no

We now have all the ingredients to state the

(

)

P^{\prime\prime}
be the restriction of
P
to
\lambda^{\prime\prime}
and note that this semistandard tableau must have entries from
\{r+1,r+2,\ldots,m\}
.
Using part (b) and the discussion after the definion of a
\mathfrak{P}
-partition, there is a bijection between these
P^{\prime\prime}
and
\mathfrak{P}_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}
-partitions with parts at most
m-r-1
.
It follows from Theorem
5.4
that
g_{m}^{\lambda}
is, up to a constant, the coefficient of
x^{m-r-1}
in
(\sum_{\pi}x^{\des\pi})/(1-x)^{n^{\prime\prime}+1}
where the sum is over all linear extensions of
\mathfrak{P}_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}
. Now

\frac{1}{(1-x)^{n^{\prime\prime}+1}}=\sum_{m\geq 0}\binom{m+n^{\prime\prime}}{n^{\prime\prime}}x^{m}

and
\binom{m+n^{\prime\prime}}{n^{\prime\prime}}
is a polynomial in
m
of degree
n^{\prime\prime}
with positive leading coefficient.
Since the numerator of the fraction in Theorem
5.4
has positive coefficients, there will be no cancellation of leading terms so that
g_{m}^{\lambda}
is also a polynomial in
m
of degree
n^{\prime\prime}
.

To finish, note that there will only be one term of (
2
) which is of maximal degree, and that will be when
\lambda=(1^{n})
so that
n^{\prime\prime}=n-r
. Furthermore, counting the tableaux involved, that term will be

g_{m}^{(1^{n})}f^{(1^{n})}=\binom{m-r}{n-r}

which has leading coefficient
1/(n-r)!
as desired.
∎

We can now show that for fixed
n
we have
c_{n,m}(u)
is a polynomial in
m
for various
u
.

Corollary 5.6
.

The following are polynomials in
m
for
n
fixed and
m\geq n
.

(a)

If
n\geq 1
then
c_{n,m}(1)
is a polynomial in
m
of degree
n-1
with leading coefficient
1/(n-1)!
.

(b)

If
n\geq 2
then
c_{n,m}(12)
is a polynomial in
m
of degree
n-2
with leading coefficient
1/(n-2)!
.

(c)

If
n\geq k
then
c_{n,m}(k(k-1)\ldots 1)
is a polynomial in
m
of degree
n-k
with leading coefficient
1/(n-k)!
.

Proof.

(a) It suffices to show that the hypotheses of the previous theorem are are satisfied with
r=1
. We get condition (a) immediately from Theorem
3.1
(a) when
u=1
. And we see that when
u=1
in this Theorem, condition (b) is vacuous since both sides are zero because of the row and column restrictions on a semistandard Young tableau. Thus Theorem
5.5
(b) is also true and we are done with this case.

(b) This is similar to (a), just using Theorem
3.4
in place of Theorem
3.1
. Note that using both conditions in the former implies that the entries of
P(w)
in the first two rows can only be
1
’s and
2
’s. And there is no restriction on other rows. So Theorem
5.5
is satisfied with
r=2
.

(c) Given the similarity to (a) and (b), the reader should now be able to fill in the details using Theorem
4.3
.
∎

One can also use the method of proof in Theorem

Theorem 5.7
.

Suppose
n
is fixed and

\displaystyle c_{1,m}(1)

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle c_{7,m}(1)

\displaystyle={m\choose 1}+33{m\choose 2}+178{m\choose 3}+262{m\choose 4}+131{m\choose 5}+{m\choose 6},

\displaystyle c_{8,m}(1)

\displaystyle={m\choose 1}+68{m\choose 2}+549{m\choose 3}+1480{m\choose 4}+1405{m\choose 5}+428{m\choose 6}+{m\choose 7}.

Proof.

We will illustrate the method by computing the term in (
2
) for
u=1
when
\lambda=(2,2,1)
, leaving the details for the other partitions of integers up to

Now

{\cal L}(\mathfrak{P}_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}})=\{132,312\}

\sum_{m\geq 0}|\Par_{m}\mathfrak{P}_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}|\ x^{m}=\frac{2x}{(1-x)^{4}}.

The tableaux counted by

Normalizing, we want entries

Multiplying by

Although we have begun the study of the centralizer

, we believe that there are many more interesting results to be found. Here we collect a few avenues for future research.

The reader will have noticed how useful Lemma

was in proving various of the characterizations of

Conjecture 6.1

Conjecture 6.3

Fix
n

c_{n,m}(1)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{k}\binom{m}{k}

It is well known that applying symmetries of the square to a permutation
\pi
(viewed as a permutation matrix) does interesting things to the output tableaux under RSK. One of these also seems to play nicely with the centralizer set. If

w=w_{1}w_{2}\ldots w_{k}
has
\max w\leq m
then define its
m
-reverse complement
to be the word

\RC_{m}(w)=(m-w_{k}+1)(m-w_{k-1}+1)\ldots(m-w_{1}+1).

Note the dependence on the choice of

, not just on
w
. For example

\RC_{4}(31122)=33442.

To extend this operation to an SSYT,
T
, let

\rw(T)
be the row word of
T
obtained by reading the rows of
T
from left to right starting with the bottom row and moving up. It is well known that

P(\rw T)=T.

Now if
\max T\leq m
we define the
m
-evacuation
of
T
to be the composition

\epsilon_{m}(T)=P\circ\RC_{m}\circ\rw(T).

When
T
is a standard Young tableau with maximum entry
m

\epsilon_{m}
corresponds to
Schützerberger’s evacuation operation, see
[
Sch63
]
or
[
Sta24

.

Lemma 6.4
.

If
T
is a SSYT with
\max T\leq m
, then
T
and
\epsilon_{m}(T)
have the same shape.

Proof.

Let
T
have shape
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{l})
. Then by Greene’s extension of Schensted’s Theorem
[
Gre74
,
Sch61
]
we have that, for all
i\geq 1
, the sum
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\cdots+\lambda_{i}
is the cardinality of the largest subword of
w=\rw T
which can be written as a disjoint union of
i
weakly increasing subwords.
So, to prove the lemma, it suffices to give a bijection between weakly increasing subwords of
w
and those of
\RC_{m}(w)
which preserves disjointness.
But
v
is a weakly increasing subword of
w
if and only if
\RC_{m}(v)
is a weakly increasing subword of
\RC_{m}(w)
, so we are done.
∎

We now wish to describe a conjectural bijection between the elements of
C(u)
and
those of
C(\RC_{m}(u))
for any

.
Given an array

we let
A_{\leq m}
be the subarray consisting of the elements of

which are at most
m
and similarly for
A_{>m}
.
If
T
is an SSYT then let
\tau_{m}(T)
be the result of replacing
T_{\leq m}
with
\epsilon_{m}(T_{\leq m})
and leaving
T_{>m}
unchanged. Note that the previous lemma makes this replacement well defined since the two tableaux involved have the same shape. We similarly extend the map
\RC_{m}
to all words

by letting
\RC_{m}(w)
be the word obtained by replacing
w_{\leq m}
with its

-reverse complement and leaving the elements of

As noted in the proof of Theorem
5.5

P(C(u))

Our conjecture is as follows.

Conjecture 6.5

If
u

\max u\leq m

P(C(\RC_{m}(u)))=\tau_{m}(P(C(u))).

Note that, since both
\RC_{m}

Stochastic solutions to abstract telegraph-type equations involving fractional dynamics
Alessandro De Gregorio, Roberto Garra

Abstract
. This paper investigates abstract integro-differential hyperbolic equations, focusing on the probabilistic representation of their solutions. Our analysis is based on fractional derivatives and non-local operators, which are powerful tools for modeling the anomalous behavior and non-Markovian dynamics observed in various phenomena. We first analyze a time-fractional version of the abstract telegraph equation (involving the Caputo derivative), restricting our analysis to positive self-adjoint operators to leverage spectral theory, which includes key operators in applications, such as the fractional Laplace operator. We derive analytical representations for the solution and provide a stochastic solution to the telegraph-diffusion equation for a specific range of the fractional parameter α \alpha , thereby generalizing existing results. We discuss particular cases involving the fractional Laplace and Bessel-Riesz operators. Furthermore, we consider the abstract Euler-Poisson-Darboux (EPD) equation, characterized by a singular time coefficient. We demonstrate that the stochastic solution to this EPD equation can be represented in terms of the solution of the abstract wave equation. Crucially, we prove that the solution to the EPD equation admits a representation by means of the Erdelyi-Kober fractional integral. Finally, this work provides a comprehensive analysis of both time-fractional and singular-coefficient abstract telegraph-type equations, offering new analytical and stochastic representation formulas. Keywords : Erdelyi-Kober integral, fractional Caputo derivative, fractional Laplacian, Euler-Poisson-Darboux equation, self-adjoint operator, stable subordinator MSC 2010 : 60G22, 45K05



1.
Introduction

Fractional derivatives (i.e., derivative with non-integer order) and non-local operators are now recognized as powerful tools for analyzing the anomalous behavior observed in various phenomena. Such anomalies include nonlinear mean-squared displacement in time, heavy-tailed and skewed marginal distributions, and sample paths with jumps (see, e.g.,
[
32
]
). In this context, time-changed random processes serve as valuable models for these non-Markovian fractional dynamics, explicitly incorporating the system’s memory.

For example, replacing the classical time derivative in the heat equation with a time-fractional derivative yields a new integro-differential equation. The stochastic solution to this equation is known to be a Brownian motion subordinated by a random time (specifically, the inverse of a stable subordinator). For a comprehensive discussion on this subject, the reader may consult the overview
[
31
]
. Furthermore, fractional calculus is relevant in the analysis of several partial differential equations (PDEs) arising in mathematical physics. For instance, the Euler-Poisson-Darboux equation

\left[\partial_{t}^{2}+\frac{2\lambda}{t}\partial_{t}\right]u=\Delta u,

which describes wave propagation, is well-known to admit a solution expressible as the Erdelyi-Kober fractional integral of the D’Alembert solution to the classical wave equation (see, e.g.,
[
14
]
).

In this paper, we investigate abstract integro-differential hyperbolic equations. In particular we refer to two different families of generalized telegraph-type equations and study the probabilistic representation of their solutions. In our analysis the fractional calculus plays a two-fold role. Indeed, we study a time-fractional version of the telegraph equation involving the fractional Caputo derivative. Analytic and stochastic solutions to fractional telegraph equations have been studied by several researchers (see, e.g.,
[
8
]
,
[
33
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
27
]
and
[
2
]
). Furthermore, we introduce an abstract version of the EPD equation and prove that its solution admits a stochastic representation in terms of fractional integrals.

In order to introduce the main object of our interest, let us consider the abstract telegraph equation

(1.1)

\left[\partial_{t}^{2}+2\lambda\partial_{t}+A\right]u=0,

studied, e.g., in
[
13
]
and
[
20
]
, where
A
is a linear operator. Let
\mathbb{H}
be an Hilbert space, with
||\cdot||
representing the norm induced by the inner product. A time-fractional extension of (
1.1
) is given by

(1.2)

\displaystyle\left[(D_{t}^{\alpha})^{2}+2\lambda D_{t}^{\alpha}+A\right]u=0,\quad t>0,\alpha\in(0,1],

where
(D_{t}^{\alpha})^{2}:=D_{t}^{\alpha}D_{t}^{\alpha},u:=u(t)
,
A
is a positive (i.e., nonnegative and injective) self-adjoint operator on
\mathbb{H}
(for instance
A=-\Delta=-\sum_{k=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{k}}^{2}
with
\mathbb{H}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
)
and
\lambda>0.

In (
1.2
) we deal with time-fractional Caputo (or Dzhrbashyan-Caputo) derivatives as follows: let
f:\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}\to\mathbb{H}

(1.3)

D_{t}^{\alpha}f(t):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{f^{\prime}(\tau)}{(t-\tau)^{\alpha}}\mathrm{d}\tau,\quad t>0,\ \alpha\in(0,1)

where
f
is a.e. differentiable (in norm) for
t>0
and absolutely continuous, with the integral understood in Bochner’s sense. Furthermore, we recall that the Laplace transform of the fractional derivative of order
\alpha\in(0,1)
is given by

\widetilde{\left(D_{t}^{\alpha}f\right)}(s):=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-st}D_{t}^{\alpha}f(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=s^{\alpha}\widetilde{f}(s)-s^{\alpha-1}f(0),

where
\widetilde{f}(s)
is the Laplace transform of
f
and
s
is a complex number with Re
(s)
sufficiently large. For more details on fractional calculus, the reader can consult
[
22
]
and
[
34
]
. It is worth mentioning that we decided to work with the less general
A
because, in this setting, it is possible to apply the spectral theory of self-adjoint operators. Furthermore, the most interesting operators in applications fall into the class defined by
A
(for instance, the fractional Laplace operator). In this paper, we derive analytical representations of the solution to the fractional telegraph equation (
1.2
). Furthermore, for
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, we are able to provide a stochastic solution to the telegraph-diffusion equation (
1.2
), which generalizes the result discussed in
[
12
]
. We discuss some particular cases involving operators of interest in applications, such as the fractional Laplace and Bessel-Riesz operators. The case
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
has been analyzed in
[
27
]
, for a general operator
A,
where the solution is expressed in terms of d’Alembert formulas. It was also anlyzed in
[
2
]
, for
A=-\partial^{2}_{x},
where the authors proved that the solution of the fractional telegraph equation admits a random time-changed Kac’s representation.

A different class of abstract telegraph-type equations is given by the abstract EPD equation:

(1.4)

\left[\partial_{t}^{2}+\frac{2\lambda}{t}\partial_{t}+A\right]u=0,

where the singular coefficient
\lambda(t)=\frac{2\lambda}{t}
appears, and
A
is defined as in (
1.2
). The equation (
1.4
) and its generalizations have attracted the attention of several researchers over the years; we mention, for instance,
[
7
]
,
[
35
]
,
[
38
]
and
[
11
]
. A modified version of the EPD equation has been recently analyzed in
[
28
]
. We are able to represent the stochastic solution to (
1.4
) in terms of the solution of the abstract wave equation
\left[\partial_{t}^{2}+A\right]u=0.
Furthermore, it is possible to show that the solution to the EPD equation admits a representation by means of the Erdelyi-Kober fractional integral.

2.
Abstract time-fractional telegraph equations

2.1.
Introduction and results on fractional telegraph equations

Let us deal with the same setting discussed in
[
8
]
. Let
(\mathbb{H},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
be a Hilbert space. Let
A
be a positive (i.e.,
\langle Ax,x\rangle\geq 0,x\in D(A)
) and injective self-adjoint operator on
\mathbb{H}
. The multiplication operator version of the spectral theorem allows the following result: there exists a unitary operator
U:\mathbb{H}\to L^{2}(\mathfrak{X},\Sigma,\mu)
and a
\Sigma
-measurable function
m:\mathfrak{X}\to(0,\infty)
unique (modulo changes on sets of
\mu
-measure zero) and positive (
\mu
a.e.) such that

(2.1)

U\,A\,U^{-1}f=M_{m}f:=mf,

for

f\in\text{Dom}(U\,A\,U^{-1})=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathfrak{X},\Sigma,\mu):mf\in L^{2}(\mathfrak{X},\Sigma,\mu)\}.

An interesting application of the spectral theorem is the chance to express a Borel function
G:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{C}
of
A
as product of operators; that is

G(A)=U^{-1}M_{G(m)}U.

Let us deal with the abstract time-fractional telegraph equation

(2.2)

\displaystyle\bigg[(D_{t}^{\alpha})^{2}+2\lambda D_{t}^{\alpha}+A\bigg]u=0,\quad t>0,\ \alpha\in(0,1],

(2.3)

\displaystyle u(0)=f,\ \alpha\in(0,1],

(2.4)

\displaystyle D_{t}^{\alpha}u(0)=0,\ \alpha\in(1/2,1],

where
t\mapsto u(t)\in\mathbb{H}
and
f\in D(A).

In
[
8
]
, the authors proved that

(2.5)

u_{\alpha}(t)=U^{-1}\widehat{u}_{\alpha}(t)

is the unique solution of (
2.2
)-(
2.4
), where
\widehat{u}_{\alpha}(t,\xi)\in L^{2}(\mathfrak{X},\Sigma,\mu),\xi\in\mathfrak{X},
is the solution to the following problem

(2.6)

\displaystyle\bigg[(D_{t}^{\alpha})^{2}+2\lambda D_{t}^{\alpha}+m(\xi)\bigg]\widehat{u}(t,\xi)=0,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\widehat{u}(0,\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi),\quad D_{t}^{\alpha}\widehat{u}(0,\xi)=0.

Furthermore, the Laplace transform of the solution to the problem (
2.6
)-(
2.7
) is given by

(2.8)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\widehat{u}}_{\alpha}(s,\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{(s^{2\alpha-1}+2\lambda s^{\alpha-1})\widehat{f}(\xi)}{s^{2\alpha}+2\lambda s^{\alpha}+m(\xi)},

and its inverse allows to write down

(2.9)

\displaystyle\widehat{u}_{\alpha}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=\widehat{f}(\xi)\widehat{\Phi}_{\alpha}(t,\xi)

where

(2.10)

\displaystyle\widehat{\Phi}_{\alpha}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\left[\bigg(1+\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{\lambda-m(\xi)}}\bigg)E_{\alpha,1}(r_{1}t^{\alpha})+\bigg(1-\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{\lambda-m(\xi)}}\bigg)E_{\alpha,1}(r_{2}t^{\alpha})\right]

\displaystyle=E_{\alpha,1}(r_{1}t^{\alpha})+\frac{(2\lambda+r_{2})t^{\alpha}}{r_{1}-r_{2}}[r_{1}E_{\alpha,\alpha+1}(r_{1}t^{\alpha})-r_{2}E_{\alpha,\alpha+1}(r_{2}t^{\alpha})]

and

\displaystyle r_{1}:=-\lambda+\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-m(\xi)},

\displaystyle r_{2}:=-\lambda-\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-m(\xi)},

E_{\alpha,\beta}(x):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{k}}{\Gamma(\alpha k+\beta)},\quad\alpha,\beta>0,x\in\mathbb{C}.

This latter is the well-known Mittag-Leffler function and (
2.10
) follows taking into account that
E_{\alpha,\alpha+1}(x)=\frac{1}{x}(E_{\alpha,1}-1)
(see e.g.
[
17
]
for details). We refer to
[
8
]
for the detailed calculations and the asymptotics of
u_{\alpha}
. For a discussion on the strong solution of the problem (
2.2
)-(
2.4
), the reader can consult
[
5
]
.

Remark 2.1
.

We highlight that
(D_{t}^{\alpha})^{2}\neq D_{t}^{2\alpha}.
Nevertheless, the Cauchy problem (
2.2
)-(
2.4
) coincides with the following problem involving another kind of fractional telegraph equation (studied, for instance, in
[
33
]
and
[
12
]
)

(2.11)

\displaystyle\bigg[D_{t}^{2\alpha}+2\lambda D_{t}^{\alpha}+A\bigg]u=0,\quad t>0,\ \alpha\in(0,1],

\displaystyle u(0)=f,\ \alpha\in(0,1],

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(0)=0,\ \alpha\in(1/2,1].

Indeed, under suitable assumptions on
u
, we recall that the Laplace transform for
D_{t}^{2\alpha}
is equal to (see Lemma 2.24 in
[
23
]
)

\widetilde{(D_{t}^{2\alpha}u)}(s)=s^{2\alpha}\widetilde{u}-s^{2\alpha-1}u(0)-s^{2\alpha-2}\partial_{t}u(0).

Therefore, by using the spectral theorem as above, from (
2.11
), we get

\displaystyle\bigg[(D_{t}^{2\alpha})+2\lambda D_{t}^{\alpha}+m\bigg]\widehat{u}(t)=0,

\displaystyle\widehat{u}(0)=\widehat{f},\quad D_{t}^{\alpha}\widehat{u}(0)=0.

By applying the Laplace transform to the previous problem, we derive that
\widetilde{\widehat{u}}
coincides with (
2.8
).

In many interesting cases the Hilbert space
\mathbb{H}
coincides with
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathrm{d}x)
or with a suitable subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathrm{d}x).

Furthermore, it is well-known (by Plancharel theorem) that the Fourier transform of a function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathrm{d}x),
that is
\widehat{f}(\xi):=\mathcal{F}f(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i\langle x,\xi\rangle}f(x)\mathrm{d}x,
with
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},
represents a unitary operator of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathrm{d}x)
onto
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathrm{d}x)
. Furthermore, we define inverse Fourier transform as
\mathcal{F}^{-1}f(x):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-i\langle x,\xi\rangle}\widehat{f}(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi,
where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Theorem 1
.

Let
A
be a positive self-adjoint operator on
\mathbb{H}\subseteq L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathrm{d}x),
such that the following spectral decomposition holds

A=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\,m\,\mathcal{F}

and
m
is an isotropic function; that is
m(\xi)=m(||\xi||),
for each
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.
The solution of the Cauchy problem (
2.2
)-(
2.4
) becomes the convolution product
u_{\alpha}(t,x)=(f\ast\Phi_{\alpha}(t))(x),
for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where

(2.12)

\Phi_{\alpha}(t,x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}||x||^{\frac{d}{2}-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\frac{d}{2}}J_{\frac{d}{2}-1}(r||x||)\widehat{\Phi}_{\alpha}(t,r)\mathrm{d}r,\quad t>0.

Proof.

In this setting, we have that
U=\mathcal{F}
with
\mathcal{F}:\mathbb{H}\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathrm{d}x)
and
U^{-1}=\mathcal{F}^{-1},
and
\widehat{u}_{\alpha}(t)
in (
2.9
) coincides with the Fourier transform of the solution of the Cauchy problem (
2.2
)-(
2.4
). Therefore, it is enough to compute
\Phi_{\alpha}
as inverse Fourier transform of
\widehat{\Phi}_{\alpha}
. Let
x:=(x_{1},...,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d},
we can write down

\displaystyle\Phi_{\alpha}(t,x)

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\widehat{\Phi}_{\alpha}(t))(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-i\langle x,\xi\rangle}\widehat{\Phi}_{\alpha}(t,||\xi||)\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\text{(by spherical coordinates transformations)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{d-1}\widehat{\Phi}_{\alpha}(t,r)\mathrm{d}r\int_{0}^{\pi}\mathrm{d}\theta_{1}\cdots\int_{0}^{\pi}\mathrm{d}\theta_{d-1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\phi\sin^{d-2}\theta_{1}\cdots\sin\theta_{d-2}

\displaystyle\quad\times\exp\{-ir(x_{d}(\sin\theta_{1}+...+\sin\theta_{d-2}\sin\phi)+...+x_{2}\sin\theta_{1}\cos\theta_{2}+x_{1}\cos\theta_{1})\}

and the result (
2.12
) follows by noticing that (see, e.g.,
[
19
]
)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}\mathrm{d}\theta_{1}\cdots\int_{0}^{\pi}\mathrm{d}\theta_{d-1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\phi\sin^{d-2}\theta_{1}\cdots\sin\theta_{d-2}

\displaystyle\times\exp\{-ir(x_{d}(\sin\theta_{1}+...+\sin\theta_{d-2}\sin\phi)+...+x_{2}\sin\theta_{1}\cos\theta_{2}+x_{1}\cos\theta_{1})\}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\frac{J_{\frac{d}{2}-1}(r||x||)}{(r||x||)^{\frac{d}{2}-1}}.

∎

Remark 2.2
.

It is worth mentioning that (
2.12
) simplifies in some particular spaces. Since
J_{-\frac{1}{2}}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi x}}\cos(x),
for
d=1
, we get that

\Phi_{\alpha}(t,x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\cos(r||x||)\widehat{\Phi}_{\alpha}(t,r)\mathrm{d}r,

while, by recalling that
J_{\frac{1}{2}}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi x}}\sin(x),
if
d=3

\Phi_{\alpha}(t,x)=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}||x||}\int_{0}^{\infty}r\sin(r||x||)\widehat{\Phi}_{\alpha}(t,r)\mathrm{d}r.

2.2.
Stochastic solutions for
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]:
telegraph-diffusion case

Let us consider the following abstract Cauchy problem involving heat-type equations
with
\alpha\in(0,1/2]

(2.13)

\displaystyle\bigg[(D_{t}^{\alpha})^{2}+2\lambda D_{t}^{\alpha}+A\bigg]u=0,\quad t>0,

(2.14)

\displaystyle u(0)=f,

where
u:\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}\to\mathbb{H}
and
f\in\mathbb{H}.

Let us introduce two independent totally positively
skewed stable random processes
\{H_{1}^{2\alpha}(t):t\geq 0\}
and
\{H_{2}^{\alpha}(t):t\geq 0\},
where
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}],
having characteristic function given by

\mathbb{E}(e^{i\xi H_{2}^{\alpha}(t)})=e^{-\sigma t|\xi|^{\alpha}(1-i\,\text{sgn}(\xi)\tan\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})},\quad\sigma=\cos\frac{\alpha\pi}{2}.

Now, let us define the inverse

\{\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}(t):t\geq 0\}
the (non-negative) inverse process of the sum
H_{1}^{2\alpha}(t)+(2\lambda)^{1/\alpha}H_{2}^{\alpha}(t)
as follows

(2.15)

\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}(t):=\inf\bigg\{s\geq 0:\ H_{1}^{2\alpha}(s)+(2\lambda)^{1/\alpha}H_{2}^{\alpha}(s)\geq t\bigg\},\quad t,\lambda>0.

We denote by
\ell_{\alpha}(x,t),x\geq 0,t>0,
the density function of
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}(t).

For a deep discussion on the properties of this process the reader can consult
[
12
]
.
However, this is the inverse of the sum of stable subordinators and it preserves the main properties of the inverse of a stable subordinator, i.e. it is a non-Markovian process with non-stationary, non-independent increments and non-decreasing continuous a.s. sample paths (see e.g.
[
30
]
for details).

Theorem 2
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}].
The unique solution to the problem (
2.13
)-(
2.14
), has the following stochastic representation

(2.16)

u_{\alpha}(t)=\mathbb{E}\left[v(\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}(t))\right].

where
v(t)=T(t)f,t\geq 0,
is the unique solution to the abstract Cauchy problem

(2.17)

\displaystyle[\partial_{t}+A]v=0,\quad v(0)=f\in D(A),

with
\{T(t):t\geq 0\}
representing the
C_{0}
contraction semigroup on
\mathbb{H}
generated by
-A.

Proof.

By means of the spectral theorem, we can observe that
\widehat{v}(t,\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{-m(\xi)t},
where
\widehat{f}(\xi)\in L^{2}(\mathfrak{X},\Sigma,\mu),
solves

[\partial_{t}+m(\xi)]\widehat{v}(t,\xi)=0,\quad\widehat{v}(0,\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi),

and then
v(t)=U^{-1}\widehat{v}(t)
is the unique solution to the problem (
2.17
). Furthermore, by Lumer-Phillips Theorem (see, e.g., Theorem 3.3, pag.26, in
[
16
]
), it follows that
-A
is the generator of a
C_{0}
contraction semigroup
\{T(t):t\geq 0\}.
Therefore the Cauchy problem (
2.17
) is well-posed; that is the resolvent set
\rho(-A)
is non-empty and for each
f\in D(-A)
there is a unique solution
u:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to D(-A)
of (
2.17
) in
C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathbb{H}),
given by
v(t)=T(t)f,t\geq 0
(see, e.g., Theorem 1.2, pag.83, in
[
16
]
). We recall that the Laplace transform (with respect the variable
t
) of the density function
\ell_{\alpha}(x,t)
of
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}(t)
is given by (see (4.14) in
[
12
]
)

(2.18)

\tilde{\ell}_{\alpha}(x,s)=(s^{2\alpha-1}+2\lambda s^{\alpha-1})e^{-xs^{2\alpha}-2\lambda xs^{\alpha}},

where we denoted with
s
the Laplace parameter. Now, for each
\xi\in\mathfrak{X}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-st}\mathbb{E}(e^{-m(\xi)\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}(t)})\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-st}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-m(\xi)x}\ell_{\alpha}(x,t)\mathrm{d}x\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=(s^{2\alpha-1}+2\lambda s^{\alpha-1})\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x(m(\xi)+s^{2\alpha}+2\lambda s^{\alpha})}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{s^{2\alpha-1}+2\lambda s^{\alpha-1}}{s^{2\alpha}+2\lambda s^{\alpha}+m(\xi)}.

From (
2.8
) and the uniqueness of Laplace transform, it follows that

\widehat{u}_{\alpha}(t,\xi)=\mathbb{E}[\widehat{v}(\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}(t),\xi)]

and then we can conclude the proof by observing that
U^{-1}
is a linear operator independent by
t
.
∎

From the previous theorem immediately follows the next result.

Corollary 3
.

Let
-A
be the infinitesimal generator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathrm{d}x)
of a strong Markov process
\{X(t):t\geq 0\}
independent of
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}(t).
Then for
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}],

(2.19)

u_{\alpha}(t,x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[f(X(\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}(t)))\right],\quad t>0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

2.3.
Special case
\alpha=1/2

We recall that (see, e.g.,
[
17
]
)

(2.20)

E_{1/2,1}(t)=e^{t^{2}}(1+\text{erf}(t)),

where

\text{erf}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\tau^{2}}d\tau

is the error function. Therefore, in the case
\alpha=1/2
, we can represent the solution for the abstract fractional diffusion equation

(2.21)

\bigg[(D_{t}^{1/2})^{2}+2\lambda D_{t}^{1/2}+A\bigg]u=0

as follows

(2.22)

\Phi_{\frac{1}{2}}(t,x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}||x||^{\frac{d}{2}-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\frac{d}{2}}J_{\frac{d}{2}-1}(r||x||)\widehat{\Phi}_{\frac{1}{2}}(t,r)\mathrm{d}r,\quad t>0.

where

(2.23)

\displaystyle\widehat{\Phi}_{\frac{1}{2}}(t,\xi):=\frac{1}{2}\left[\bigg(1+\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{\lambda-m(\xi)}}\bigg)e^{r_{1}^{2}t}(1+\text{erf}(r_{1}\sqrt{t}))+\bigg(1-\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{\lambda-m(\xi)}}\bigg)e^{r_{2}^{2}t}(1+\text{erf}(r_{2}\sqrt{t}))\right],

and

\displaystyle r_{1}:=-\lambda+\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-m(\xi)},

\displaystyle r_{2}:=-\lambda-\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-m(\xi)}.

Therefore, as
t\to 0,

\widehat{\Phi}_{\frac{1}{2}}(t,\xi)\sim e^{(2\lambda^{2}-m(\xi))t}+\cosh(t\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-m(\xi)})+\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{\lambda-m(\xi)}}\sinh(t\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-m(\xi)})

This case can be particularly useful in order to obtain the asymptotic behavior of the solution as pointed out in
[
8
]
. Observe, moreover, that in general
(D_{t}^{1/2})^{2}\neq D_{t}
.

Furthermore, we can obtain an alternative interesting representation of (
2.10
) for
\alpha=1/2.

Propostion 1
.

Let
\{B(t):t\geq 0\}
be the standard Brownian motion. We get that

\displaystyle\widehat{\Phi}_{\frac{1}{2}}(t,\xi)=\mathbb{E}\left[g(|B(t)|,\xi)\right],

where

g(t,\xi):=e^{-\lambda t}\left[\cosh(t\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-m(\xi)})+\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{\lambda-m(\xi)}}\sinh(t\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-m(\xi)})\right].

Proof.

We observe that (see (4.3) in
[
33
]
)

E_{\frac{1}{2},1}(x)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-w^{2}-2xw}\mathrm{d}w.

and then

\displaystyle E_{\frac{1}{2},1}(r_{i}\sqrt{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-w^{2}+2r_{i}\sqrt{t}w}\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle=(2\sqrt{t}w=z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi t}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{4t}+r_{i}z}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{|B(t)|r_{i}}\right],\quad i=1,2,

where

represents the density function of
|B(t)|
for any

Therefore

\displaystyle\widehat{\Phi}_{\frac{1}{2}}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\bigg(1+\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{\lambda-m(\xi)}}\bigg)\mathbb{E}(e^{|B(t)|r_{1}})+\bigg(1-\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{\lambda-m(\xi)}}\bigg)\mathbb{E}(e^{|B(t)|r_{2}})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\bigg(1+\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{\lambda-m(\xi)}}\bigg)\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi t}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{4t}+r_{1}z}\mathrm{d}z+\bigg(1-\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{\lambda-m(\xi)}}\bigg)\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi t}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{4t}+r_{2}z}\mathrm{d}z\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi t}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{4t}-\lambda z}\left(e^{z\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-m(\xi)}}+e^{-z\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-m(\xi)}}\right)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi t}}\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{\lambda-m(\xi)}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{4t}-\lambda z}\left(e^{z\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-m(\xi)}}-e^{-z\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-m(\xi)}}\right)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi t}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{4t}-\lambda z}\left(\cosh(z\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-m(\xi)})+\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{\lambda-m(\xi)}}\sinh(z\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-m(\xi)})\right)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(e^{-\lambda|B(t)|}\left[\cosh(|B(t)|\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-m(\xi)})+\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{\lambda-m(\xi)}}\sinh(|B(t)|\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-m(\xi)})\right]\right).

∎

In the setting of Theorem

, by applying the inverse Fourier transform to

, we get the following stochastic representation of the fundamental solution of (

(2.24)

If

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{e^{-\lambda t}}{2}\left[\delta(x-t)+\delta(x+t)\right],

which represents the density function of the telegraph process

3.1.
The
d
-dimensional space-time fractional telegraph equation

The space-time fractional telegraph equation has been an object of many recent papers, we refer for example to
[
12
]
and
[
39
]
. The classical Laplace operator is replaced with its fractional version denoted by
-(-\Delta)^{\beta/2},\beta\in(0,2]
. We recall that by means of the spectral theorem, we can define the fractional Laplace operator
-(-\Delta)^{\beta/2}
as a Fourier multiplier with symbol
-||\xi||^{\beta},
where
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
; that is

(3.1)

(\mathcal{F}(-\Delta)^{\beta/2}f)(\xi)=||\xi||^{\beta}\hat{f}(\xi),

where
f\in
Dom
((-\Delta)^{\beta/2})=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathrm{d}x):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+||\xi||^{\beta})|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi<\infty\}
(see, e.g.,
[
25
]
for a detailed discussion on the fractional Laplace operator). Furthermore, we recall that
\{S_{d}^{\beta}:t\geq 0\}
is a
d
-dimensional isotropic stable process with characteristic function given by

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{i\langle\xi,S_{d}^{\beta}(t)\rangle}\right]=e^{-t||\xi||^{\beta}},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

In view of the general Theorem 1, we have the following result.

Proposition 3.1
.

The solution of the d-dimensional space-time fractional telegraph equation

\displaystyle\bigg[(D_{t}^{\alpha})^{2}+2\lambda D_{t}^{\alpha}+(-\Delta)^{\beta/2}\bigg]u=0,\quad t>0,\ \alpha\in(0,1],\beta\in(0,2],

\displaystyle u(0)=f,\ \alpha\in(0,1],

\displaystyle D_{t}^{\alpha}u(0)=0,\ \alpha\in(1/2,1],

is given by the convolution product
u_{\alpha}(t,x)=(f\ast\Phi_{\alpha}(t))(x),
for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},
where

\Phi_{\alpha}(t,x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}||x||^{\frac{d}{2}-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\frac{d}{2}}J_{\frac{d}{2}-1}(r||x||)\widehat{\Phi}_{\alpha}(t,r)\mathrm{d}r,\quad t>0

and

\displaystyle\widehat{\Phi}_{\alpha}(t,\xi):=\frac{1}{2}\left[\bigg(1+\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{\lambda-||\xi||^{\beta}}}\bigg)E_{\alpha,1}(r_{1}t^{\alpha})+\bigg(1-\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{\lambda-||\xi||^{\beta}}}\bigg)E_{\alpha,1}(r_{2}t^{\alpha})\right],

where

\displaystyle r_{1}:=-\lambda+\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-||\xi||^{\beta}},

\displaystyle r_{2}:=-\lambda-\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-||\xi||^{\beta}}.

Furthermore, for
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}],

u_{\alpha}(t,x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[f(S_{d}^{\beta}(\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}(t)))\right],\quad t>0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Proof.

As a matter of fact, this useful representation of the solution to the space-time fractional telegraph equation can be seen as a special case of Theorem 1. Indeed, from the well-known Fourier transform of the fractional Laplacian (
3.1
) and by observing that
m(\xi)=||\xi||^{\beta}
, we can immediately derive the statement of the theorem.

Since
\mathbb{E}(e^{i\langle\xi,S_{d}^{\beta}(t)\rangle})=e^{-t||\xi||^{\beta}}
is the characteristic function of the fundamental solution to the equation

[\partial_{t}+(-\Delta)^{\beta/2}]v=0,

from Corollary
3
follows the last result stated in the theorem, which essentially coincides with Theorem 4.1 in
[
12
]
(see also Remark
2.1
).
∎

Remark 3.1
.

It is worth mentioning that for
\beta=2,
the fractional Laplace operator coincides with the classical Laplace operator and
\{S_{d}^{2}(t):t\geq 0\}
is the
d
-dimensional standard Brownian motion. If
\beta=\frac{1}{2},
the process

represents the Cauchy process having density function

3.2.
The time fractional telegraph-type equation involving Bessel-Riesz operator

The Bessel-Riesz process is a
d
-dimensional Lévy process
\{Y_{d}^{\beta,\gamma}(t):t\geq 0\}
with joint characteristic function

(3.2)

\widehat{v}(t,\xi)=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{i\langle\xi,Y_{d}^{\beta,\gamma}(t)\rangle}\right]=e^{-t||\xi||^{\beta}(1+||\xi||^{2})^{\gamma/2}},

coinciding with the Fourier transform of the Green function of the equation

(3.3)

\partial_{t}u=-(-\Delta)^{\beta/2}(I-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}u,\quad,\gamma\geq 0,\beta\in(0,2],

where
(-\Delta)^{\beta/2}
and
(I-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}
are the inverses of the Riesz and the Bessel potential respectively. Therefore, the operator
-(-\Delta)^{\beta/2}(I-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}
can be defined as pseudo-differential operator in the space of Fourier transforms as done in the previous section; that is

(\mathcal{F}(-\Delta)^{\beta/2}(I-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}f)(\xi)=||\xi||^{\beta}(1+||\xi||^{2})^{\gamma/2}\hat{f}(\xi),

where
f\in
Dom
((-\Delta)^{\beta/2}(I-\Delta)^{\gamma/2})=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathrm{d}x):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}||\xi||^{\beta}(1+||\xi||^{2})^{\gamma/2}|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi<\infty\}
. The Bessel-Riesz operator and the related process have been studied in detail in
[
4
]
. In particular, it is possible to prove that
Y_{d}^{\beta,\gamma}(t)=B_{d}(L_{\beta,\gamma}(t)),
where
\{B_{d}(t):t\geq 0\}
is a
d
-dimensional Brownian motion and

Furthermore,
B_{d}(t)
and
L_{\beta,\gamma}(t)
are assumed independent.
In
[
3

The Fourier transform of the solution for the Cauchy problem (

An interesting stochastic interpretation of this result based on the Bessel-Riesz distribution has been provided in
[
3

Here we consider some new applications of Bessel-Riesz operators to generalized telegraph-type equations. The aim of this section is to consider analytical and probabilistic results about the generalized telegraph-type equation

\displaystyle(D_{t}^{\alpha})^{2}u+2\lambda D_{t}^{\alpha}u=-(-\Delta)^{\beta/2}(I-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}u,\quad,\gamma\geq 0,\beta\in(0,2],\alpha\in(0,1].

The solution of the fractional Cauchy problem involving the Bessel-Riesz operator

3.3.
The time-fractional telegraph-type relativistic diffusion

Following the recent paper by Shieh
[
37

, we recall that the time-fractional relativistic diffusion equation is the equation

(3.13)

\partial_{t}u=H_{\nu,m}u,\quad\alpha\in(0,1],

involving the operator

(3.14)

H_{\nu,m}:=m-(m^{\frac{2}{\nu}}-\Delta)^{\frac{\nu}{2}},

that is the relativistic diffusion operator with the spatial-fractional parameter
\nu\in(0,2)
and the normalized mass parameter
m>0
. We have that the relativistic operator admits Fourier multiplier
[m-(m^{\frac{2}{\nu}}+||\xi||^{2})^{\frac{\nu}{2}}]
; that is

(\mathcal{F}H_{\nu,m}f)(\xi)=[m-(m^{\frac{2}{\nu}}+||\xi||^{2})^{\frac{\nu}{2}}]\hat{f}(\xi),

where Dom
(H_{\nu,m})=\{f\in\ L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathrm{d}x):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}[m-(m^{\frac{2}{\nu}}+||\xi||^{2})^{\frac{\nu}{2}}]|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi<\infty\}.

Considering the Equation (
3.13
) with the initial condition
u(x,0)=f(x)
, we recall that the probabilistic meaning of the solution for the Cauchy problem (
3.13
) is given by (see
[
37
]
, Proposition 3)

(3.15)

u(t,x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[f(W_{d}^{\nu}(t))\right],

where
W_{d}^{\nu}(t),t\geq 0,
is the Brownian motion on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
subordinated by a
\nu/2
relativistic subordinator.
The relativistic subordinator
T(t),t\geq 0,
was introduced by Ryznar in
[
36
]
as a Lévy process with increasing sample paths with Laplace function given by

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-uT(t)}\right]=e^{-t((m^{\frac{2}{\nu}}-u)^{\frac{\nu}{2}}-m)},\quad u>0.

Assuming that the relativistic subordinator and the Brownian motion are independent, the subordinated process
W_{d}^{\nu}
comparing in (
3.15
) is a Lévy process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with characteristic function given by

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{i\langle\xi,W_{d}^{\nu}(t)\rangle}\right]=e^{-t((m^{\frac{2}{\nu}}+||\xi||^{2})^{\frac{\nu}{2}}-m)},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Furthermore, in
[
37
]
the time-fractional generalization of the equation (
3.13
) based on the replacement of the classical time-derivative with the Caputo derivative has been studied, while in
[
6
]
the authors dealt with a time-fractional generalized version of the equation (
3.13
). Here we consider the fractional telegraph-type relativistic diffusion equation.

.

The solution of the fractional telegraph-type relativistic diffusion

(3.16)

\displaystyle\bigg[(D_{t}^{\alpha})^{2}+2\lambda D_{t}^{\alpha}-H_{\nu,m}\bigg]u=0,\quad t>0,\ \alpha,\nu\in(0,1],

\displaystyle u(0)=f,\ \alpha\in(0,1],

(3.18)

\displaystyle D_{t}^{\alpha}u(0)=0,\ \alpha\in(1/2,1],

is given by the convolution product
u(t,x)=(f\ast\Phi_{\alpha}(t))(x),
for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

\displaystyle\widehat{\Phi}_{\alpha}(t,\xi)=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\bigg[\bigg(1+\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{\lambda-\theta(||\xi||)}}\bigg)E_{\alpha,1}(r_{1}t^{\alpha})+

\displaystyle+\bigg(1-\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{\lambda-\theta(||\xi||)}}\bigg)E_{\alpha,1}(r_{2}t^{\alpha})\bigg],

\theta(||\xi||)=(m^{\frac{2}{\nu}}+||\xi||^{2})^{\frac{\nu}{2}}-m

\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}],

4.
The Abstract Euler-Poisson-Darboux equation

4.1.
Stochastic solution to the Euler-Poisson-Darboux equation

The Euler-Poisson-Darboux (EPD) equation is an hyperbolic telegraph-type equation with singular coefficient whose classical form is given by

(4.1)

\bigg[\partial_{t}^{2}+\frac{2\lambda}{t}\partial_{t}\bigg]u(t,x)=\Delta u(t,x),\quad t>0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

There is a wide literature about this equation, that is still object of recent research, we refer for example to the recent paper
[
38
]
and the references therein. Random motions related to (
4.1
) have been studied in
[
15
]
.
As pointed out in the literature, the solution for the EPD equation (
4.1
) can be obtained by means of the fractional integral of the d’Alembert solution to the wave equation with the same initial data. In this section we consider the abstract EPD, showing the connection with the solution of the abstract wave equation. Then, we will get the stochastic interpretation in terms of the D’Alembert solution of the wave equation.

Let
A
be a positive and injective self-adjoint operator on a Hilbert space
\mathbb{H}
as in Section
2.1
. In particular the same spectral decomposition of
A
holds true; i.e. there exists a unitary group
U
and positive measurable function
m
such that (
2.1
) fulfills.
Let us deal with the following abstract Euler-Poisson-Darboux equation

(4.2)

\displaystyle\bigg[\partial_{t}^{2}+\frac{2\lambda}{t}\partial_{t}+A\bigg]u=0,\quad t>0,\lambda>0,

(4.3)

\displaystyle u(0)=f,\quad\partial_{t}u(0)=0,

where
t\mapsto u(t)\in\mathbb{H}
and
f\in D(A).
The unique solution of the abstract Cauchy problem (
4.2
)-(
4.3
) is given by
u(t)=U^{-1}\widehat{u}(t)
where
\widehat{u}(t,\xi)\in L^{2}(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu).

The main result of this section is contained in the next theorem.

Theorem 4
.

The function
\widehat{u}
has the following representations

(4.4)

\displaystyle\widehat{u}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2}{t\sqrt{m(\xi)}}\right)^{\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma\left(\lambda+\frac{1}{2}\right)J_{\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}\left(t\sqrt{m(\xi)}\right)\widehat{f}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{\widehat{f}(\xi)}{B(\lambda,\frac{1}{2})t}\int_{-t}^{t}\left(1-\frac{w^{2}}{t^{2}}\right)^{\lambda-1}\left(\frac{e^{i\sqrt{m(\xi)}w}+e^{-i\sqrt{m(\xi)}w}}{2}\right)\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{B(\lambda,\frac{1}{2})t}\int_{-t}^{t}\left(1-\frac{w^{2}}{t^{2}}\right)^{\lambda-1}\widehat{\bar{u}}(\xi,w)\mathrm{d}w,

where
\bar{u}

is the unique solution of the abstract wave equation

\left(\partial_{t}^{2}+A\right)u=0

with the initial conditions (
4.3
). Furthermore, the unique solution to the Cauchy problem (
4.2
) is given by

(4.5)

u(t)=\mathbb{E}[\bar{u}(t\sqrt{\mathfrak{X}})],

where
\mathfrak{X}
is a Beta r.v with parameters
\frac{1}{2}
and
\lambda.

Proof.

Since
A
is self-adjoint on
\mathbb{H}
and positive, there exists a unique positive self-adjoint operator
\sqrt{A}
such that
(\sqrt{A})^{2}=A.
First of all, let us consider the abstract wave equation

(4.6)

\left(\partial_{t}^{2}+A\right)\bar{u}(t)=\left(\partial_{t}+i\sqrt{A}\right)\left(\partial_{t}-i\sqrt{A}\right)\bar{u}(t)=0,

where

\bar{u}(t)=\frac{u_{1}(t)+u_{2}(t)}{2}.

Let be
u_{k}(t)=U^{-1}\widehat{u}_{k}(t),k=1,2,
be the unique solution of

(4.7)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u_{k}=(-1)^{k+1}i\sqrt{A}u_{k},\\
u_{k}(0)=f.\end{cases}

and for the spectral theorem

\widehat{u}_{k}(\xi,t),=e^{(-1)^{k+1}i\sqrt{m(\xi})t}\widehat{f}(\xi),k=1,2,
is the unique solution of

(4.8)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\widehat{u}_{k}(t,\xi)=(-1)^{k+1}i\sqrt{m(\xi)}\widehat{u}_{k}(t,\xi),\\
\widehat{u}_{k}(\xi,0)=\widehat{f}(\xi),\end{cases}

Under our assumptions the problem (
4.8
)-(
4.3
) is well-posed (see, e.g, Theorem 7.4 in
[
16
]
), and then we can say that
\bar{u}
is the unique solution to the abstract wave equation (
4.6
).

Going back to the abstract EPD equation, by using again the spectral theorem for the Cauchy problem we have that the self-adjoint operator can be treated as a scalar constant and then
u(t)=U^{-1}\widehat{u}(t),
where
\widehat{u}(t,\xi)\in L^{2}(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)
satisfies

(4.9)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}\widehat{u}(t,\xi)+\frac{2\lambda}{t}\partial_{t}\widehat{u}(t,\xi)+m(\xi)\widehat{u}(t,\xi)=0,\quad t>0,

(4.10)

\displaystyle\widehat{u}(0,\xi)=\widehat{f},\quad\partial_{t}\widehat{u}(0,\xi)=0.

By recalling the solution of the Bessel equation we have that (see, e.g.,
[
26
]
)

\displaystyle\widehat{u}(t,\xi)=\left(\frac{2}{t\sqrt{m(\xi)}}\right)^{\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma\left(\lambda+\frac{1}{2}\right)J_{\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}\left(t\sqrt{m(\xi)}\right)\widehat{f}(\xi)

The Poisson integral representation of the Bessel functions reads

(4.11)

J_{\mu}(z)=\frac{(z/2)^{\mu}}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\mu+\frac{1}{2})}\int_{-1}^{+1}(1-w^{2})^{\mu-\frac{1}{2}}\cos(zw)\mathrm{d}w

valid for
\mu>-\frac{1}{2},z\in\mathbb{R}
(see
[
26
]

By inserting (

\displaystyle\widehat{u}(t,\xi)

Remark 4.1
.

We recall that it is possible to get the solution
\bar{u}(t)
to the abstract wave equation (
4.6
), by means of the spectral functional calculus as follows

\bar{u}(t)=\cos(t\sqrt{A})f=\int_{0}^{\infty}\cos(t\sqrt{x})\mathrm{d}{\bf E}(x)f,

where
{\bf E}
represents the resolution of the identity for
\sqrt{A}
(see, e.g.,
[
16
]
). When
A
admits discrete spectrum, the above spectral integral reduces to the following Fourier series

\bar{u}(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\langle f,\phi_{k}\rangle\cos(t\sqrt{x_{k}})\phi_{k},

where
\{x_{k}:k\geq 1\}
is a sequence of positive eigenvalues and
\{\phi_{k}:k\geq 1\}
is an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors such that
A\phi_{k}=x_{k}\phi_{k}.

Remark 4.2

Theorem 4 proves that the solution of the Cauchy problem (

We recall that the Erdélyi-Kober fractional integral is defined as (see, e.g.,

(I_{\alpha}^{m}f)(x)=\frac{m}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{x}(x^{m}-y^{m})^{\alpha-1}y^{m-1}f(y)\mathrm{d}y,\quad\alpha>0,m>0.

Therefore, we can observe that (

u(t)=\frac{\Gamma(\lambda+\frac{1}{2})}{\sqrt{\pi}}I_{\lambda}^{1}(\bar{u}(t\sqrt{y})/\sqrt{y}).

Let

Finally, we suggest a heuristic construction of the solution to the EPD equation by means of a finite velocity random process.
Let
X_{\varepsilon}:=\{X_{\varepsilon}(t),t\geq 0\}
a telegraph process (starting its motion upward) defined as follows

Therefore (

5.
Conclusions

In this paper, we have studied the abstract time-fractional telegraph equation from both analytical and probabilistic viewpoints. We provide a general scheme for obtaining the exact solutions to applied problems, such as those involving the fractional Laplacian or the Bessel-Riesz operator. The second part of the paper considers the abstract Euler-Poisson-Darboux (EPD) equation. The connection to the first part is twofold: First, the EPD equation can be viewed as a telegraph equation with a variable (singular) rate. Second, it is well-known that the EPD solution is related to the D’Alembert solution of the classical wave equation via a fractional integral. In this work, we consider the abstract EPD and provide the analytical and probabilistic representation of its solution. We also show that the general theory can be applied to solve interesting mathematical problems, such as the space-fractional EPD equation.

The abstract fractional equations considered here involve time-fractional derivatives in the sense of Caputo. An interesting problem for future research is to study the general theory for abstract telegraph-type equations that involve integro-differential operators with different memory kernels. This research direction would follow recent work on anomalous diffusion involving general fractional derivatives with Sonin Kernels (see, e.g.,
[
24
]
,
[
40
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
1
]
and the references therein).

Another open question is to determine which fractional version of the EPD equation is most useful to consider. The approach based purely on time-fractional derivatives does not seem to be effective in this particular case. Indeed, since in the operator
(D_{t}^{\alpha})^{2}+\frac{2\lambda}{t}D_{t}^{\alpha}
the term
\frac{2\lambda}{t}
appears, the Laplace transform method is challenge. A possible alternative is to deal with fractional powers of the time operator that appears in the EPD equation.

Comment on: “Dynamic immune changes after bone marrow sparing VMAT in women with locally advanced cervical cancer treated with chemoradiotherapy.”
Authors: Preeti Lamba
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

Corbeau et al. report prospective immune profiling after bone marrow sparing (BMS) VMAT in women with locally advanced cervical cancer (LACC). The study is timely, given increasing efforts to integrate immunotherapy with chemoradiotherapy. However, several important methodological and translational limitations merit closer consideration.

Most notably, although pelvic bone marrow mean dose was reduced, treatment-related lymphopenia remained universal and prolonged. This raises concern regarding whether pelvic bone dose alone is an adequate surrogate for immune preservation. Recent analyses emphasize that radiation-induced lymphopenia correlates strongly with integral body dose and exposure of circulating lymphocytes rather than static marrow constraints alone [1], [2]. Without modeling circulating blood dose or incorporating functional imaging of active marrow compartments, it is difficult to conclude that BMS VMAT meaningfully mitigates immunologic injury.

Second, the exploratory comparison with a historical non-BMS cohort is inherently limited by substantial heterogeneity. Differences in planning margins, para-aortic irradiation frequency, adaptive planning techniques and immune assay timing may independently influence hematologic toxicity. Contemporary meta-analyses demonstrate that even modest variations in V10–V20 marrow volumes significantly alter toxicity risk [3] These confounders reduce the ability to attribute observed immune similarities or differences specifically to the BMS strategy.

Third, the study focuses exclusively on peripheral immune parameters. However, recent translational research highlights the critical role of intratumoral CD4+ T-cell subsets and tumor microenvironment remodeling in determining response to chemoradiotherapy and checkpoint inhibition [4]. Peripheral immune suppression does not necessarily equate to impaired tumor-directed immunity and without parallel tumor immune profiling, the clinical implications remain uncertain.

Finally, persistent lymphopenia was documented up to twelve months, yet correlations with oncologic outcomes were not explored. Accumulating evidence across solid tumors continues to demonstrate associations between radiation-induced lymphopenia and inferior survival [5]. Without linking immune kinetics to progression or survival endpoints, the clinical significance of these prolonged immune alterations remains speculative.

Taken together, the findings suggest that current BMS VMAT strategies may not sufficiently protect systemic immunity in LACC. Future studies should incorporate circulating blood dose modeling, functional marrow imaging, tumor microenvironment analysis and survival correlation to clarify whether advanced planning techniques truly translate into immunologic and clinical benefit.

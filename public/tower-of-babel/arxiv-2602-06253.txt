The proof theory and semantics of second-order (intuitionistic) tense logic
Justus Becker and Anupam Das and Sonia Marin and Paaras Padhiar

Abstract
. We develop a second-order extension of intuitionistic modal logic, allowing quantification over propositions, both syntactically and semantically. A key feature of second-order logic is its capacity to define positive connectives from the negative fragment. Duly we are able to recover the diamond (and its associated theory) using only boxes, as long as we include both forward and backward modalities (‘tense’ modalities). We propose axiomatic, proof theoretic and model theoretic definitions of ‘second-order intuitionistic tense logic’, and ultimately prove that they all coincide. In particular we establish completeness of a labelled sequent calculus via a proof search argument, yielding at the same time a cut-admissibility result. Our methodology also applies to the classical version of second-order tense logic, which we develop in tandem with the intuitionistic case.

2.
A second-order extension of tense logic

In this section we present the syntax of second-order tense logic and provide axiomatisations of its intuitionistic and classical theory.
One of the main points here is to show how diamonds may actually be defined in terms of the other connectives, even intuitionistically, allowing us to
recover
its theory, instead of providing arbitrary axioms therein.

We point the reader to, e.g.,
[
70
, Chapter 12]
for some useful background on second-order (intuitionistic) propositional logic.

2.1.
Syntax of second-order tense logic

Let us fix a set
\mathsf{Pr}
of
propositional symbols
, written
P,Q,R
etc., and a (disjoint) set
\mathsf{Var}
of
(formula/second-order) variables
, written
X,Y,Z
etc.
We shall work with second-order tense
formulas
, given by:

A,B,C,\dots\quad::=\quad P\in\mathsf{Pr}\ \mid\ X\in\mathsf{Var}\ \mid\ A\to B\ \mid\ \Box A\ \mid\ \Boxblack A\ \mid\ \forall XA

Write
\mathsf{Fm}
for the set of all formulas.
We shall frequently omit external brackets of formulas and internal brackets of long implications, understanding them as rightmost bracketed. I.e.
A\to B\to C=(A\to(B\to C))
and so on.

The set of
free variables
of a formula
A
, written
\mathsf{FV}(A)
, is defined as expected:

\begin{array}[]{r@{\ := \ }l}\mathsf{FV}(P)&\varnothing\\
\mathsf{FV}(X)&\{X\}\\
\mathsf{FV}(A\to B)&\mathsf{FV}(A)\cup\mathsf{FV}(B)\end{array}\qquad\begin{array}[]{r@{\ := \ }l}\mathsf{FV}(\Box A)&\mathsf{FV}(A)\\
\mathsf{FV}(\Boxblack A)&\mathsf{FV}(A)\\
\mathsf{FV}(\forall XA)&\mathsf{FV}(A)\setminus\{X\}\end{array}

Note that propositional symbols are
not
variables.
If
\mathsf{FV}(A)=\varnothing
then
A
is
closed

(or a
sentence
).
Otherwise it is
open
.

Remark 2.1

(Propositional symbols vs variables)
.

We could have worked without propositional symbols at all, using only variables.
However we take the current formulation so that we may safely deal with only closed formulas in the systems and semantics we present.
This choice allows us to avoid the need for explicit
environments
when interpreting syntax, lightening the notation therein.

It is well known that other propositional connectives and quantifiers can be

from our minimal syntax via

Remark 3.2

(Full vs Henkin)
.

A naive domain of sets is simply the full powerset

Such a structure is comprehensive by default, since it has

This is often referred to as the

7

7

Traditionally the nomenclature ‘second-order’ would be reserved for only such semantics, while our framework is perhaps more correctly dubbed ‘two-sorted first-order’. We shall refrain from rehashing this discussion here but refer the reader to REF for a comprehensive explanation. The current terminology ‘second-order’ has become standard in computational logic.

However such a semantics for second-order logic admits no complete proof systems, as its validities are not even analytical, let alone recursively enumerable.
It is more typical in proof theoretic investigations to admit the

Definition 3.7

(Birelational collapse)
.

Given a predicate structure
\mathfrak{P}
, as presented in

The set of worlds is

.

•

.

We better show that

satisfies the appropriate conditions of

Proposition 3.8
.

{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{P}}}
is a well-defined birelational structure.

Proof.

First we show that each

is upwards closed.
Suppose
(a,v)\leq_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{P}}}(b,v)
, WLoG, i.e.

. We have:

\begin{array}[]{r@{ \ \implies\ }ll}(a,v)\in V_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{P}}}&v\in V^{a}&\text{by definition of $V_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{P}}}$}\\
&v\in V^{b}&\text{by monotonicity of $V^{-}$}\\
&(b,v)\in V_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{P}}}&\text{by definition of $V_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{P}}}$}\end{array}

Now let us show that
R_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{P}}}
is a bisimulation on

:

•

Suppose
(a,v)R_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{P}}}(a,w)\leq_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{P}}}(b,w)
, WLoG, so
vR^{a}w
and
a\leq b
. Then:

–

(a,v)\leq_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{P}}}(b,v)
by definition of
\leq_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{P}}}
; and,

–

vR^{b}w
by monotonicity of
R^{-}
, so
(b,v)R_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{P}}}(b,w)
by definition of
R_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{P}}}
.

So we can choose
(b,v)
as the required world.

•

Suppose
(b,v)\geq_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{P}}}(a,v)R_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{P}}}(a,w)

b\geq a
and
vR^{a}w
. Then:

–

(b,w)\geq_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{P}}}(a,w)
by definition of
\leq_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{P}}}

–

vR^{b}w
by monotonicity of
R^{-}
, so
(b,v)R_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{P}}}(b,w)
by definition of
R_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{P}}}
.

So we can choose
(b,w)

We can exhibit a rather strong equivalence between the theories of

and

Proposition 3.9

(Equivalence)
.

Let
A(\vec{X})
be a formula, all free variables displayed, and
\vec{V}\in\mathcal{W}
with
|\vec{V}|=|\vec{X}|
.
Then
(a,v)\vDash_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{P}}}A(\vec{V})\iff a,v\vDash_{\mathfrak{P}}A(\vec{V}_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{P}}})
.

Proof.

By induction on the structure of
A(\vec{X})
:

•

If
A(\vec{X})=X
and
\vec{V}=V
then:

\begin{array}[]{r@{ \ \iff\ }ll}(a,v)\vDash_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{P}}}V_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{P}}}&v\in V^{a}&\text{by definition of $\vDash_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{P}}}$}\\
&a,v\vDash_{\mathfrak{P}}V&\text{by definition of $\vDash_{\mathfrak{P}}$}\end{array}

•

A=P
.
(a,v)\vDash_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{P}}}P

\Longleftrightarrow

(a,v)\in P_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{P}}}

\Longleftrightarrow

v\in P^{a}

\Longleftrightarrow

a,v\vDash_{\mathfrak{P}}P
.

•

A(\vec{X})=B(\vec{X})\to C(\vec{X})

and consider any

with

we have

•

A=\Box B(\vec{X})

\Longleftrightarrow

(b,w)\vDash_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{P}}}B(\vec{V})
for any

\overset{\text{I.H.}}{\Longleftrightarrow}

a\leq b
and

\Longleftrightarrow

a,v\vDash_{\mathfrak{P}}\Box B(\vec{V}_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{P}}})

•

A=\Boxblack B(\vec{X})
:
(a,v)\vDash_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{P}}}\Boxblack B(\vec{V})

\Longleftrightarrow

(b,w)\vDash_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{P}}}B(\vec{V})
for any
(a,v)\leq_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{P}}}(b,v)
and

3.3.
Two-sorted (uni)relational semantics under full comprehension

Finally let us adapt the semantics we have presented to the classical setting.
We can view
classical structures as special cases of either birelational or predicate structure where
\leq
is trivial, i.e.:

•

a birelational structure where all points are incomparable; or,

•

a predicate model with only one intuitionistic world.

For the sake of completeness, let us give a self-contained definition:

Definition 3.13

(Classical structures)
.

A
(uni)relational structure

\mathfrak{R}
includes the following data:

•

W
of

.

•

A class
\mathcal{W}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(W)
of

(or

•

for each

•

.

Now, let us temporarily expand the language of formulas by including each
V\in{\mathcal{W}}
as a propositional symbol, setting
V^{{\mathfrak{R}}}:=V
.
The judgement
v\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}A
, for
v\in W
, is defined by induction on the size of a closed formula

•

v\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}P
if
P_{{\mathfrak{R}}}v
.

•

v\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}A\to B
if, whenever
v\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}A
, we have
v\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}B
.

•

v\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}\Box A
if, whenever
vRw
, we have
w\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}A
.

•

v\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}\Boxblack A
if, whenever

, we have

.

•

v\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}\forall XA

, we have

.

We write simply
\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}A
if

for every

We say that
{\mathfrak{R}}
is

if, for each formula

(of the expanded language), it has a predicate

with

A
(uni)relational model
is a comprehensive relational structure.

Henceforth, let us reserve the metavariable
{\mathfrak{R}}
to vary over relational models.
Our main metalogical result for classical second-order tense logic, analogous to
3.3
in the intuitionistic case, is:

Main Theorem 3.14

(Soundness and Completeness)
.

\mathsf{K}\mathsf{t}2\vdash A\iff\forall{\mathfrak{R}}\,\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}A
.

Like the intuitionistic case completeness, the
\impliedby
direction, is factored through a proof theoretic presentation of the logic from
Section

4
.
Given how we have defined relational models, we can factor soundness, the
\implies
direction, into (a) already established soundness of
\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2
for birelational structures; and (b) verification of the additional axiom
\lnot\lnot A\to A
:

Theorem 3.15

(Soundness)
.

\mathsf{K}\mathsf{t}2\vdash A\implies\forall{\mathfrak{R}}\,\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}A
.

Proof sketch.

A relational model can be expanded into a birelational model by simply setting
\leq
to be equality on worlds.
Thus all the axioms and rules of
\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2
are already sound for relational models, by
Theorem

3.5
.
It remains to verify the double negation axiom,
\lnot\lnot A\to A
.
Suppose
v\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}\lnot\lnot A
, then either
v\not\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}A
or
v\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}\bot
. We consider each case separately:

\begin{array}[]{r@{\ \implies\ }ll}v\not\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}\lnot A&v\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}A\text{ and }v\not\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}\bot&\text{by definition of $\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}$ and $\lnot$}\\
&v\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}A\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr v\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}\bot&v\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}[A]&\text{since $\bot=\forall XX$ and by definition of $\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}$}\\
&v\in[A]&\text{by definition of $\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}$}\\
&v\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}A&\text{by definition of $[A]$}\qed\end{array}

4.
Proof theory: labelled systems

We shall now turn to a proof theoretic presentation of second-order tense logic.
As well as for self contained interest this will, as already mentioned, serve to factor our earlier stated axiomatic completeness results.
Unlike the previous two sections, we shall first present the classical system, before recovering the intuitionistic versions via appropriate constraints.
The only reason for this is brevity, allowing us to define all systems we consider without repeating rules.

4.1.
Labelled sequent calculi

Labelled deductive systems
were proposed by Gabbay
[
25
]
as a uniform proof-theoretic framework for a wide range of logics.
The idea has been applied to modal logic by using the strength of its (uni)relational semantics
[
60
]
in order to resolve the difficulty of designing proof systems for modal logic using standard Gentzen sequents.
They reason about formulas labelled by the world in which they are evaluated, while keeping track of a ‘control’ constraining the accessibility relation between worlds.
They were fully developed for intuitionistic modal logic by Simpson
[
66
]
, before being widely applied to e.g. classical modal logic with Horn
[
79
]
or coherent
[
49
]
extensions and beyond
[
50
]
,
justification logic
[
26
]
, non-normal modal logics
[
17
]
, conditional logics
[
30
]
,
first-order modal logic
[
51
]
, etc.

Let us now fix a set
\mathsf{Wl}
of
world symbols
, written
u,v,w
etc.
A
relational atom
is an expression
vRw
, where
v,w\in\mathsf{Wl}
.
A
labelled formula
is an expression
v:A
where
v\in\mathsf{Wl}
and
A
is a formula.
Write
\ell\mathsf{Fm}
for the set of labelled formulas.

A
(labelled) sequent
is an expression
\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
where
\mathbf{R}
is a set of relational atoms, called the
relational context
, and
\Gamma
and
\Delta
are multisets of labelled formulas, called the
(LHS) cedent
and
(RHS) cedent
, respectively.
‘
\,|\,
’ and ‘
\Rightarrow
’ here are just syntactic delimiters.
Informally, we may read sequents as “if all of the LHS holds, then some of the RHS is true”.
This intuition is developed more formally later in
Section

7
.

The system

The lower sequent of any inference step is the

Proofs

is nothing more than the extension of the labelled calculus for tense logic

15
]
by the usual rules for second-order quantifiers (see, e.g.,

[

).
The singleton RHS restriction defining

First let us state our main proof theoretic results:

(Hauptsatz)
.

We have the following:

(1)

\ell\mathsf{K}\mathsf{t}2\vdash A\implies\ell\mathsf{K}\mathsf{t}2\setminus\mathsf{cut}\vdash A

As for our soundness and completness results,

4.3.
Relating the two intuitionistic calculi

We mentioned earlier that there is no material difference between our two intuitionistic calculi, in terms of the logic they define.
Let us now make this formal:

Lemma 4.4
.

If
\mathsf{m}\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2(\setminus\mathsf{cut})\vdash\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
then there is some
w:A\in\Delta
s.t.
\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2(\setminus\mathsf{cut})\vdash\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma\Rightarrow w:A
(respectively).

Proof.

By induction on the proof of
\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
in
\mathsf{m}\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2
and a case analysis on the last rule applied in it:

•

\mathsf{id}
and right logical rules:
immediate as they have only one formula on the RHS.

•

Right weakening:

\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta

\scriptstyle\mathsf{w}_{r}\;

\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta,v:A

By inductive hypothesis, there is a
w:C
in
\Delta
such that
\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2\vdash\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma\Rightarrow w:C
; which is all we need.

•

Right contraction:

\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta,v:A,v:A

\scriptstyle\mathsf{c}_{r}\;

\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta,v:A

By inductive hypothesis, there either is a
w:C
in
\Delta
such that
\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2\vdash\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma\Rightarrow w:C
or
\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2\vdash\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma\Rightarrow v:A
; which is all we need.

•

Non-branching left rules:

\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma^{\prime}\Rightarrow\Delta

\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta

By inductive hypothesis there is
w:A\in\Delta
s.t.
\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2\vdash\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma^{\prime}\Rightarrow w:A
, by applying the same rule,

\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2\vdash\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma\Rightarrow w:A
.

•

Implication left rule:

\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta,v:A

\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma^{\prime},v:B\Rightarrow\Delta^{\prime}

\scriptstyle\to_{l}\;

\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma,\Gamma^{\prime},v:A\to B\Rightarrow\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}

By inductive hypothesis, either there is
w:C\in\Delta
s.t.
\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2\vdash\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma\Rightarrow w:C
, in which case, by weakening
\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2\vdash\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma,\Gamma^{\prime},v:A\to B\Rightarrow w:C
,
or
\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2\vdash\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma\Rightarrow v:A
and there is
w:C\in\Delta^{\prime}
s.t.
\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2\vdash\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma^{\prime},v:B\Rightarrow w:C
, in which case, by
\to_{l}
,
\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2\vdash\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma,\Gamma^{\prime},v:A\to B\Rightarrow w:C
.

•

\mathsf{cut}
:

\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta,v:A

\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma^{\prime},v:A\Rightarrow\Delta^{\prime}

\scriptstyle\mathsf{cut}\;

\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma,\Gamma^{\prime}\Rightarrow\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}

By inductive hypothesis, either there is
w:C\in\Delta
s.t.
\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2\vdash\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma\Rightarrow w:C
, in which case, by weakening
\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2\vdash\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma,\Gamma^{\prime}\Rightarrow w:C
,
or
\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2\vdash\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma\Rightarrow v:A
and there is
w:C\in\Delta^{\prime}
s.t.
\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2\vdash\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma^{\prime}\Rightarrow w:C
, in which case, by
\mathsf{cut}
,
\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2\vdash\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma,\Gamma^{\prime}\Rightarrow w:C
. ∎

From here we have immediately:

Proposition 4.5
.

\mathsf{m}\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2(\setminus\mathsf{cut})\vdash A\implies\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2(\setminus\mathsf{cut})\vdash A
(respectively).

Remark 4.6
.

The result above may seem surprising at first glance, as it does not typically hold for even (first-order) intuitionistic propositional logic (without modalities), in the presence of positive connectives.
For instance there is a multi-succedent intuitionistic proof of
A_{0}\lor A_{1}\Rightarrow A_{0},A_{1}
, but clearly no
A_{0}\lor A_{1}\Rightarrow A_{i}
is provable.
We avoid this issue as we do not work with native positive connectives, including
\lor
, only their impredicative encodings from
Eq.

2
.
Similarly note, in
Lemma

4.4
above, there is no requirement for
\Delta
to be nonempty: it is simply not possible to prove sequents with empty RHS in
\mathsf{m}\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2
, by analysis of its rules.

5.
Completeness via proof search: the classical case

One of the main technical contributions of the present work is the cut-free completeness of our sequent calculi via proof search.
For second (and higher) order (classical) predicate logic cut-admissibility remained an open problem since the late ’30s, what came to be known as
Takeuti’s conjecture
(see, e.g.,
[

In the late ’60s, the problem was finally resolved positively by Prawitz
[

and Tait
[

for second order logic, and extended to simple type theory (i.e. higher order logic) by Prawitz
[
55

and Takahashi
[

Both exploited Schütte’s technique of

[

[
62

or
[
73

for textbook presentations of the methodology for simple type theory.

This is the starting point for our argument, from which we must employ a number of adaptations.
We shall avoid working explicitly with the notion of partial valuation, rather only demonstrating the properties we need, but making due remarks to the classical methodology throughout.
In the intuitionistic setting our argument is rather more involved, with little prior related work.
For this reason we shall start with the classical case, in this section, showcasing the main ideas of the methodology before addressing the further technicalities necessary for the intuitionistic case in the next section.
Thus the main result of this section is:

Theorem 5.1

(Classical cut-free completeness)
.

\forall{\mathfrak{R}}\,\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}A\implies\ell\mathsf{K}\mathsf{t}2\setminus\mathsf{cut}\vdash A

As expected we shall approach this via contraposition, extracting a countermodel from failed proof search.
As already mentioned, our proof exploits ideas about partial valuations originating from
[

[

5.1.3.
Enumerating steps by activity

Now, fixing a conclusion sequent, note that the steps in
Eq.

9
are determined by the choice of principal and auxiliary labelled formulas, as well as which side the principal formula occurs on.
Let us call these data the

As activities are determined by only finite data, they are only countably many so may be enumerated by
\omega
.
The idea of our proof search algorithm will be to apply each of these steps in turn, so that every possible labelled formula on either side of a sequent is eventually principal.
To ensure we do not miss any possible steps, we shall employ an enumeration with sufficient redundancy:

Convention 5.2

(Adequate enumeration)
.

We assume an enumeration
(\alpha_{i})_{i<\omega}
of activities in which every possible activity occurs infinitely often.

Remark 5.3

(Activity vs inference step)
.

Note that distinct inference steps may have the same activity, as their contexts may be different, but once a concluding sequent is fixed the activity determines at most one inference step.
It may also determine no correct inference step at all, e.g. if the corresponding principal formula does not occur in the sequent, or only appears on the wrong side.

5.2.
The proof search branch

Let us set up some notation for convenience.
We shall write
\mathcal{S},\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
etc. to vary over labelled sequents.
If
\mathcal{S}=\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\mathbf{R}^{\prime}\,|\,\Gamma^{\prime}\Rightarrow\Delta^{\prime}

\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{\prime}

\mathbf{R}\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
,

\Delta\subseteq\Delta^{\prime}
.
For a set of sequents
\{\mathcal{S}_{i}=\mathbf{R}_{i}\,|\,\Gamma_{i}\Rightarrow\Delta_{i}\}_{i\in I}

\bigcup\limits_{i\in I}\mathbf{R}_{i}\,|\,\bigcup\limits_{i\in I}\Gamma_{i}\Rightarrow\bigcup\limits_{i\in I}\Delta_{i}

We shall typically reserve this union operation for when we take limits of chains of sequents under

For the remainder of this section let us fix a sequent

\ell\mathsf{K}\mathsf{t}2\not\vdash\mathcal{S}_{0}

Definition 5.4

(Proof search branch)
.

We extend

to a sequence

of unprovable sequents defined as follows:

•

•

Otherwise let

be the inference step with conclusion
\mathcal{S}_{i}
and activity
\alpha_{i}
.

•

By assumption that
\mathcal{S}_{i}
is unprovable, some premiss of

We set
\mathcal{S}_{i+1}
to be some unprovable premiss.

10

10

It does not matter which, but for concreteness, we may take the leftmost if there is a choice.

As mentioned earlier, since the macro rules of

Importantly we have:

Proposition 5.5

\Gamma_{\omega}\cap\Delta_{\omega}=\varnothing

Proof.

If
\Gamma_{\omega}

5.4.
Extracting a partial valuation

As in other countermodel constructions, the idea is to find a structure that forces all of

\Delta_{\omega}

Schütte dubbed such an assignment a

[

We stop short of working explicitly with partial valuations, for the sake of reducing the technical development, rather simply using predicates for truth and falsity induced by the LHS

\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr v:C\in\Delta_{\omega}&v\notin\mathtt{C}&\text{since $\mathtt{C}\geq_{\mathfrak{S}}C$}\\
&v\not\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}\mathtt{C}&\text{by definition of $\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}$}\end{array}

•

Suppose
A(\vec{X})=A_{0}(\vec{X})\to A_{1}(\vec{X})
, and fix
\vec{C}
and
\vec{\mathtt{C}}\geq_{\mathfrak{S}}\vec{C}
. We have:

\begin{array}[]{r@{\ \implies\ }ll}v:A(\vec{C})\in\Gamma_{\omega}&v:A_{0}(\vec{C})\in\Delta_{\omega}\text{ or }v:A_{1}(\vec{C})\in\Gamma_{\omega}&\text{by \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{partial-val-props-classical}}\\
&v\not\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}A_{0}(\vec{\mathtt{C}})\text{ or }v\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}A_{1}(\vec{\mathtt{C}})&\text{by IH}\\
&v\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}A(\vec{\mathtt{C}})&\text{by definition of $\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}$}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr v:A(\vec{C})\in\Delta_{\omega}&v:A_{0}(\vec{C})\in\Gamma_{\omega}\text{ and }v:A_{1}(\vec{C})\in\Delta_{\omega}&\text{by \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{partial-val-props-classical}}\\
&v\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}A_{0}(\vec{\mathtt{C}})\text{ and }v:\not\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}A_{1}(\vec{\mathtt{C}})&\text{by IH}\\
&v\not\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}A(\vec{\mathtt{C}})&\text{by definition of $\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}$}\end{array}

•

Suppose
A(\vec{X})=\Box A^{\prime}(\vec{X})
, and fix
\vec{C}
and
\vec{\mathtt{C}}\geq_{\mathfrak{S}}\vec{C}
. We have:

\begin{array}[]{r@{\ \implies\ }ll}v:A(\vec{C})\in\Gamma_{\omega}&\forall w\in\mathsf{Wl}\,(v\mathbf{R}_{\omega}w\implies w:A^{\prime}(\vec{C})\in\Gamma_{\omega})&\text{by \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{partial-val-props-classical}}\\
&\forall w\in\mathsf{Wl}\,(v\mathbf{R}_{\omega}w\implies w\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}A^{\prime}(\vec{\mathtt{C}}))&\text{by IH}\\
&v\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}A(\vec{\mathtt{C}})&\text{by definition of $\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}$}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr v:A(\vec{C})\in\Delta_{\omega}&\exists w\in\mathsf{Wl}\,(v\mathbf{R}_{\omega}w\text{ and }w:A^{\prime}(\vec{C})\in\Delta_{\omega})&\text{by \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{partial-val-props-classical}}\\
&\exists w\in\mathsf{Wl}\,(v\mathbf{R}_{\omega}w\text{ and }w\not\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}A^{\prime}(\vec{\mathtt{C}}))&\text{by IH}\\
&v\not\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}A(\vec{\mathtt{C}})&\text{by definition of $\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}$}\end{array}

•

The case when
A(\vec{X})=\Boxblack A^{\prime}(\vec{X})
is similar to the one above.

•

Suppose
A(\vec{X})=\forall XA^{\prime}(X,\vec{X})
, and fix
\vec{C}
and
\vec{\mathtt{C}}\geq_{\mathfrak{S}}\vec{C}
. We have:

\begin{array}[]{r@{\ \implies\ }ll}v:A(\vec{C})\in\Gamma_{\omega}&\forall C\in\mathsf{Fm}\,v:A^{\prime}(C,\vec{C})\in\Gamma_{\omega}&\text{by \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{partial-val-props-classical}}\\

&v\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}A(\vec{\mathtt{C}})&\text{by definition of $\vDash_{{\mathfrak{R}}}$}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr v:A(\vec{C})\in\Delta_{\omega}&\exists C\in\mathsf{Fm}\,v:A^{\prime}(C,\vec{C})\in\Delta_{\omega}&\text{by \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{partial-val-props-classical}}\\

6.
Completeness via proof search: the intuitionistic case

We now turn to the intuitionistic case for cut-free completeness.
As we have presented the classical case in detail, we shall focus in this section on how the intuitionistic case deviates from the classical one.
Let us summarise the key points:

•

Not all rules of
\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2
or
\mathsf{m}\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2
can be made invertible.
For the former this is because we lack right structural rules and so, e.g.,
\to_{l}
is not invertible.
For the latter this is because the right logical rules are not invertible.

•

As a result, instead of a proof search branch, we will construct a proof search
tree
. This is unsurprising given the shape of intuitionistic structures, which are partially ordered.

•

We will need to deal with limit sequents at each branching point of the countermodel construction, so our proof system must now work with genuinely
infinite sequents
.

The obtention of an appropriate proof search tree from which we can extract countermodels is facilitated by working directly with the calculus
\mathsf{m}\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2
, instead of
\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2
.
Cut-free completeness results for multi-succedent calculi have also appeared in
[
73
, pp. 52-59]
for first-order intuitionistic predicate logic over Kripke models, and in
[
56
]
for second-order intuitionistic predicate logic over Beth models.
Note that, in our setting we furthermore recover cut-free completeness of the single-succedent calculus
\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2
thanks to
Proposition

4.5
, even over all birelational models thanks to
Proposition

3.11
.
In summary, the main results of this section are:

Theorem 6.1

(Intuitionistic cut-free completeness)
.

\forall\mathfrak{P}\,\vDash_{\mathfrak{P}}A\implies\mathsf{m}\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2~\setminus~\mathsf{cut}~\vdash~A
.

Corollary 6.2
.

\forall\mathfrak{B}\,\vDash_{\mathfrak{B}}A\implies\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2~\setminus~\mathsf{cut}~\vdash~A
.

The remainder of this section is structured as the previous one.

6.1.
Setting up proof search

We now construe
\mathsf{m}\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2
over (possibly) infinite sequents
\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
, where some/all of
\mathbf{R},\Gamma,\Delta
may be (countably) infinite.
We say that such a sequent is
provable
in, or a
theorem
of,
\mathsf{m}\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2
if there are finite
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbf{R}
,
\Gamma^{\prime}\subseteq\Gamma
and
\Delta^{\prime}\subseteq\Delta
such that
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}\,|\,\Gamma^{\prime}\Rightarrow\Delta^{\prime}
is provable in
\mathsf{m}\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2
, in the usual sense.
Note that infinitude of sequents does not affect the fact that inference steps on them are still determined by activity of finite data, cf.
5.2
.

All the discussion of
Section

5.1
still applies when building proofs of
\mathsf{m}\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2
, however we shall consider only the left macro rules of
Eq.

9
, as the right ones are not derivable in
\mathsf{m}\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2
.
We thus restrict our enumeration of activities from
5.2
to ones with left principal formulas.
Our proof search algorithm will now alternate between two phases: the
LHS phase
, applying left rules, and the
RHS phase
, applying right rules.
We will again recast proof search as a countermodel construction, building a predicate structure where the LHS phase determines interpretations
within
an intuitionistic world and the RHS phase determines a tree structure inducing the corresponding partial order.

A bit more formally, the
LHS phase
initiates at some (possibly infinite) sequent
\mathcal{S}_{0}
unprovable in
\mathsf{m}\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2
and is defined just like the proof search branch in
Definition

5.4
, producing a branch
(\mathcal{S}_{i})_{i<\omega}
of unprovable sequents.
That is, at the
i
th
stage it applies the step with activity
\alpha_{i}
and conclusion
\mathcal{S}_{i}
(if it exists), setting
\mathcal{S}_{i+1}
to be some unprovable premiss (or
\mathcal{S}_{i}
, respectively).
Just like in
Section

5.2
this phase is monotone:
i\leq j\implies\mathcal{S}_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{j}
. Thus the steps applied are also invertible, by weakening.
Writing again
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}=\bigcup\limits_{i<\omega}\mathcal{S}_{i}
, note that, if
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}
were provable then so also would be
\mathcal{S}_{i}
for some
i<\omega
, by definition of provability of infinite sequents, so we have:

Observation 6.3
.

If
\mathsf{m}\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2\not\vdash\mathcal{S}_{0}
and
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}
is the limit of the LHS phase from
\mathcal{S}_{0}
, then also
\mathsf{m}\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2\not\vdash\mathcal{S}_{\omega}
.

The
RHS phase
initiates at a (possibly infinite) sequent
\mathcal{S}=\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
unprovable in
\mathsf{m}\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2
and simply applies, bottom-up, a single right logical step of
\mathsf{m}\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2
.
Such a step is determined by the choice of a principal formula in
\Delta
(up to renaming of fresh symbols).
Again, the (only) premiss must remain unprovable.
Note that, by inspection of the right logical steps, the RHS phase always ends at a sequent
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\mathbf{R}^{\prime}\,|\,\Gamma^{\prime}\Rightarrow\Delta^{\prime}
where
\Delta^{\prime}
is a
singleton
.
Note that the RHS phase is
not
monotone for the RHS, but remains monotone for the LHS, i.e. we still have

\mathbf{R}^{\prime}\supseteq\mathbf{R}
and
\Gamma^{\prime}\supseteq\Gamma
but not, in general,
\Delta^{\prime}\supseteq\Delta
.
Thus it is not, in general, invertible.

6.2.
The proof search tree

As already mentioned, branching in our proof search tree will occur in the RHS phase.
Since the RHS phase is determined by a choice of principal labelled formula in the RHS, we shall name the nodes of our tree structure accordingly.
Write
\sigma,\tau,
etc. to vary over
\ell\mathsf{Fm}^{<\omega}
.
We shall write
::
for concatenation of finite sequences.
Write
\sqsubseteq
for the prefix order on
\ell\mathsf{Fm}^{<\omega}
, i.e.
\sigma\sqsubseteq\tau
if
\tau
can be written as
\sigma::\sigma^{\prime}
.
Of course,
\sqsubseteq
is indeed a partial order.

For the remainder of this section we fix a (possibly infinite) sequent
\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}_{0}=\mathbf{R}^{\varepsilon}_{0}\,|\,\Gamma^{\varepsilon}_{0}\Rightarrow\Delta^{\varepsilon}_{0}
that is unprovable in
\mathsf{m}\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2
.

Definition 6.4

(Proof search tree)
.

The
proof search tree

\mathfrak{S}
consists of a tree
T\subseteq\ell\mathsf{Fm}^{<\omega}
and sequents
\{\mathcal{S}^{\sigma}_{i}=\mathbf{R}^{\sigma}\,|\,\Gamma^{\sigma}_{i}\Rightarrow\Delta^{\sigma}_{i}\}_{\sigma\in T,i<\omega}

defined as follows:

•

For any sequent
\mathcal{S}^{\sigma}_{0}
, apply the LHS phase to construct a chain
\mathcal{S}^{\sigma}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{\sigma}_{1}\subseteq\cdots
of unprovable sequents.
Define
\mathcal{S}^{\sigma}=\mathbf{R}^{\sigma}\,|\,\Gamma^{\sigma}\Rightarrow\Delta^{\sigma}
to be the limit
\mathcal{S}^{\sigma}_{\omega}
of this chain (which again must be unprovable, cf.
6.3
).

•

For each formula
v:A\in\Delta^{\sigma}
,
\sigma
has a child
\sigma::(v:A)
in
T
.
We set
\mathcal{S}^{\sigma::(w:A)}_{0}
to be the (unique) premisse of the (unique, up to renaming of fresh symbols) inference step with conclusion
\mathcal{S}^{\sigma}
and principal formula
v:A
.

Once again, since
\mathfrak{S}
has been constructed to include only unprovable sequents, like
Proposition

5.5
in the classical case we importantly have:

Proposition 6.5
.

\Gamma^{\sigma}\cap\Delta^{\sigma}=\varnothing
, for all
\sigma\in T
.

6.3.
Towards a countermodel: a pre-structure from proof search

Just like in
Section

5.3
, the proof search tree
\mathfrak{S}
we constructed gives rise to a ‘pre-structure’, i.e. a predicate structure lacking a domain of sets (and thus also an interpretation of propositional symbols):

Definition 6.6

(Pre-structure)
.

We define the predicate structure
\mathfrak{P}^{-}
by:

•

The set of intuitionistic worlds is just
T\subseteq\ell\mathsf{Fm}^{<\omega}
.

•

The partial order is just the restriction of
\sqsubseteq
to
T
.

•

The set of modal worlds is just
\mathsf{Wl}
.

•

The accessibility relation at
\sigma\in T
is just
\mathbf{R}^{\sigma}
, i.e.
v\mathbf{R}^{\sigma}w
if
vRw\in\mathbf{R}^{\sigma}
.

Note that
\sigma\sqsubseteq\tau\implies\mathbf{R}^{\sigma}\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{\tau}
, by monotonicity in the LHS and RHS phases.

6.4.
Extracting a partial valuation

This part of the argument is similar to the classical case, in
Section

5.4
, instead establishing bespoke local properties compatible with our predicate semantics:

Proposition 6.7

(Partial valuation)
.

We have the following:

(1)

v:A\to B\in\Gamma^{\sigma}\implies\forall\tau\sqsupseteq\sigma\,(v:A\in\Delta^{\tau}\text{ or }v:B\in\Gamma^{\tau})

(2)

v:A\to B\in\Delta^{\sigma}\implies\exists\tau\sqsupseteq\sigma\,(v:A\in\Gamma^{\tau}\text{ and }v:B\in\Delta^{\tau})

(3)

v:\Box A\in\Gamma^{\sigma}\implies\forall\tau\sqsubseteq\sigma\,\forall w\in\mathsf{Wl}\,(v\mathbf{R}^{\tau}w\implies w:A\in\Gamma^{\tau})

(4)

v:\Box A\in\Delta^{\sigma}\implies\exists\tau\sqsupseteq\sigma\,\exists w\in\mathsf{Wl}\,(v\mathbf{R}^{\tau}w\text{ and }w:A\in\Delta^{\tau})

(5)

v:\Boxblack A\in\Gamma^{\sigma}\implies\forall\tau\sqsupseteq\sigma\,\forall u\in\mathsf{Wl}\,(u\mathbf{R}^{\tau}v\implies u:A\in\Gamma^{\tau})

(6)

v:\Boxblack A\in\Delta^{\sigma}\implies\exists\tau\sqsupseteq\sigma\,\exists u\in\mathsf{Wl}\,(u\mathbf{R}^{\tau}v\text{ and }u:A\in\Delta^{\tau})

(7)

v:\forall XA\in\Gamma^{\sigma}\implies\forall\tau\sqsupseteq\sigma\,\forall C\in\mathsf{Fm}\ v:A[C/X]\in\Gamma^{\tau}

(8)

v:\forall XA\in\Delta^{\sigma}\implies\exists\tau\sqsupseteq\sigma\,\exists C\in\mathsf{Fm}\ v:A[C/X]\in\Delta^{\tau}

Proof.

We consider each case separately:

(1)

Let
\tau\sqsupseteq\sigma
. By monotonicity we have
v:A\to B\in\Gamma^{\tau}
, and so either
v:A\in\Delta^{\tau}
or
v:B\in\Gamma^{\tau}
, depending on which direction
\Gamma^{\sigma}
takes at the corresponding
\to_{l}
step.

(2)

If
v:A\to B\in\Delta^{\sigma}
then we can just set
\tau=\sigma::(v:A\to B)
.

(3)

Let
\tau\sqsupseteq\sigma
and
v\mathbf{R}^{\tau}w
. By monotonicity we have
v:\Box A\in\Gamma^{\tau}
, and so also
w:A\in\Gamma^{\tau}
by the corresponding
\Box_{l}
step.

(4)

If
v:\Box A\in\Delta^{\sigma}
, we can just set
\tau=\sigma::(v:\Box A)
and
w
the fresh world variable of the corresponding
\Box_{r}
step.

(5)

(Similar to (3)).

(6)

(Similar to (4)).

(7)

Let
\tau\sqsupseteq\sigma
and
C\in\mathsf{Fm}
. By monotonicity we have
v:\forall XA\in\Gamma^{\tau}
, and so also
v:A[C/X]\in\Gamma^{\tau}
by the corresponding
\forall_{l}
step.

(8)

If
v:\forall XA\in\Delta^{\sigma}
, we can just set
\tau=\sigma::(v:\forall XA)
and
C
the propositional eigenvariable of the corresponding
\forall_{r}
step. ∎

6.5.
A compatible countermodel via possible values

We continue to adapt the machinery of
Section

5
to the intuitionistic setting.
Recall that we have already fixed an unprovable sequent
\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}_{0}
and extended it to the proof search tree
\mathfrak{S}
, cf.
Definition

6.4
, in particular including the limit sequents
(\mathcal{S}^{\sigma})_{\sigma\in T}
.
We duly adapt
Definition

5.8
to the intuitionistic setting:

Definition 6.8

(Possible extensions)
.

A
possible extension
of a formula
C
(wrt
\mathfrak{S}
) is a family
\mathtt{C}=\{\mathtt{C}^{\sigma}\subseteq\mathsf{Wl}\}_{\sigma\in T}
such that:

•

\sigma\sqsubseteq\tau\implies\mathtt{C}^{\sigma}\subseteq\mathtt{C}^{\tau}
; and,

•

v:C\in\Gamma^{\sigma}\implies v\in\mathtt{C}^{\sigma}
; and,

•

v:C\in\Delta^{\sigma}\implies v\notin\mathtt{C}^{\sigma}
.

Let us write
\mathtt{C}\geq_{\mathfrak{S}}C
if
\mathtt{C}
is a possible extension of
C
, wrt
\mathfrak{S}
.

Note that every formula
C
still admits a
minimal (possible) extension

\lfloor{C}\rfloor
with
\lfloor{C}\rfloor^{\sigma}:=\{v\in\mathsf{Wl}\ |\ v:C\in\Gamma^{\sigma}\}
.
Note that monoticity wrt
\sqsubseteq
is inherited from monotonicity of LHSs during proof search: if
\sigma\sqsubseteq\tau
then
\Gamma^{\sigma}\subseteq\Gamma^{\tau}
, and so
\lfloor{C}\rfloor^{\sigma}\subseteq\lfloor{C}\rfloor^{\tau}
, for any formula
C
.
Again we obtain a predicate structure whose sets include all possible extensions:

Definition 6.9

(Countermodel)
.

We expand
\mathfrak{P}^{-}
into a structure
\mathfrak{P}
by including the following missing data:

•

The class
\mathcal{W}
of modal predicates include all possible extensions all formulas.
For each extension
\mathtt{C}
, the interpretation
\mathtt{C}^{\sigma}
is as defined in
Definition

6.8
.

•

We identify each
P\in\mathsf{Pr}
with the possible extension
\lfloor{P}\rfloor
.

Once again, we need a key compatibility result:

Lemma 6.10

(Compatibility)
.

For formulas
A(\vec{X})
and
\vec{C}
we have:

(1)

v:A(\vec{C})\in\Gamma^{\sigma}\implies\sigma,v\vDash_{\mathfrak{P}}A(\vec{\mathtt{C}})
whenever
\vec{\mathtt{C}}\geq_{\mathfrak{S}}\vec{C}
.

(2)

v:A(\vec{C})\in\Delta^{\sigma}\implies\sigma,v\not\vDash_{\mathfrak{P}}A(\vec{\mathtt{C}})
whenever
\vec{\mathtt{C}}\geq_{\mathfrak{S}}\vec{C}
.

Once again, before proving this, let us point out an immediate consequence.
Write
\sigma\vDash_{\mathfrak{P}}\mathbf{R}\,|\,\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
if
\mathbf{R}\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{\sigma}
and, for each
v:A\in\Gamma
(or
v:A\in\Delta
) we have
\sigma,v\vDash_{\mathfrak{P}}A
(or
\sigma,v\not\vDash_{\mathfrak{P}}A
, respectively).
As a special case of the
Lemma

6.10
above we have:

Proposition 6.11
.

\varepsilon\not\vDash_{\mathfrak{P}}\mathcal{S}^{\varepsilon}_{0}
.

Proof of
Lemma

6.10
.

By induction on
A(\vec{X})
:

•

Suppose
A(\vec{X})=X
, and fix
C
and
\mathtt{C}\geq_{\mathfrak{S}}C
. We have:

\begin{array}[]{r@{\ \implies\ }ll}v:C\in\Gamma^{\sigma}&v\in\mathtt{C}^{\sigma}&\text{since $\mathtt{C}\geq_{\mathfrak{S}}C$}\\
&\sigma,v\vDash_{\mathfrak{P}}\mathtt{C}&\text{by definition of $\vDash_{\mathfrak{P}}$}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr v:C\in\Delta^{\sigma}&v\notin\mathtt{C}^{\sigma}&\text{since $\mathtt{C}\geq_{\mathfrak{S}}C$}\\
&\sigma,v\not\vDash_{\mathfrak{P}}\mathtt{C}&\text{by definition of $\vDash_{\mathfrak{P}}$}\end{array}

•

Suppose
A(\vec{X})=A_{0}(\vec{X})\to A_{1}(\vec{X})

\vec{C}
and

Suppose

\vec{C}
and

. We have:

7.1.

A

As a consequence, it is connected, i.e., there exists exactly one path of undirected edges between any pair of distinct nodes.
A

Any labelled polytree sequent provable in

7.2.
Formula contexts

As the formula translation changes due to the position of the succedent formula, we define two kinds of “formula contexts”.
These are formulas of a certain shape with one unique atom
\{\}
, called the hole which can be substituted for a formula. The following kind of formula context has the shape of a left-hand-side interpretation.
We call the following a
conjunction context
.

F^{\land}\{\}::=\{\}\;|\;A\land F^{\land}\{\}\;|\;\Diamond F^{\land}\{\}\;|\;\Diamondblack F^{\land}\{\}

We write
F^{\land}\{A\}
when substituting for the hole in
F^{\land}\{\}
the formula
A
.
We also define the empty substitution
F^{\land}\{\varnothing\}

as
F^{\land}\{\top\}
.
We also write
F^{\land}
instead of
F^{\land}\{\varnothing\}
.

For the full formula interpretation, we define an
implication context

F^{\to}\{\}
:

F^{\to}\{\}::=\{\}\;|\;A\to F^{\to}\{\}\;|\;\Box F^{\to}\{\}\;|\;\Boxblack F^{\to}\{\}

Note that we can translate between these contexts as follows:

•

F^{\land}\{\}=\{\}
iff
F^{\to}\{\}=\{\}

•

F^{\land}\{\}=A\land F^{\land\prime}\{\}
iff
F^{\to}\{\}=A\to F^{\to\prime}\{\}

•

F^{\land}\{\}=\Diamond F^{\land\prime}\{\}
iff
F^{\to}\{\}=\Box F^{\to\prime}\{\}

•

F^{\land}\{\}=\Diamondblack F^{\land\prime}\{\}
iff
F^{\to}\{\}=\Boxblack F^{\to\prime}\{\}

Substitution for implication contexts
F^{\to}\{A\}
is defined by substituting the formula
A
for the hole (here, we do not need substitution for the empty context).

This allows us to translate these contexts into one another which will be helpful for the rules cut and
\to_{l}
which swap the label of the succedent formula.

Here we show that the formula interpretation of a sequent can be written in the form of contexts.

To prove

(

Lemma 7.17

(Soundness of interpretation)
.

If

then
\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2\vdash\mathtt{ifm}^{u}({\mathbf{R}}\,|\,\Gamma\Rightarrow w:A)

This is proved by induction on the proof of

in
\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2
, where both the base case and the inductive cases are provided by the following lemma.
Recall that we can assume that any sequent
{\mathbf{R}}\,|\,\Gamma\Rightarrow w:A
occurring in a proof is polytree labelled sequents; in particular labels in
\Gamma\cup\{w:A\}
are connected by
{\mathbf{R}}
.

Lemma 7.18

(Local soundness)
.

Let

\{{\mathbf{R}}\,|\,\Gamma_{i}\Rightarrow x_{i}:A_{i}\}_{i<n}

{\mathbf{R}}\,|\,\Gamma\Rightarrow x:A

be a rule instance of
\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2
with

,

then
\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2\vdash\mathtt{ifm}^{u}({\mathbf{R}}\,|\,\Gamma\Rightarrow x:A)
.

For most rules of
\ell\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2
, the proof is similar to the one for
nested sequents
for
\mathsf{IK}

[
69
]
, but over the current formula interpretation.
We highlight some of the technicalities of the proof.

The rule
\to_{l}
requires some attention in the way the context is interpreted into a formula
because the two premisses can have different labels on the RHS.
The RHS label determines how the formula interpretation is defined (

), in particular whether a relational atom

is read as a
\Box

belongs to the path from

to

\Diamond
(otherwise).
For this reason, the interaction axioms

and
\mathsf{I}_{\Diamondblack\Boxblack}:(\Diamondblack A\to\Boxblack B)\to\Boxblack(A\to B)

.

The rule
\Box_{l}
is more subtle to handle in the tense case than it usually is in the modal case
because the indicated relational atom
vRw
in the context can be read forwards or backwards when the formula interpretation is computed.
For this reason, the adjunction axioms
\mathsf{A}_{\Diamondblack\Box}:\Diamondblack\Box A\mathbin{\to}A

The rule

In particular the distributivity axiom

Lemma 7.26

(Soundness of
\Box_{r}
and
\Boxblack_{r}
)
.

If
\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2\vdash\mathtt{ifm}^{u}({\mathbf{R}},vRw\,|\,\Gamma\Rightarrow w:A)

\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2\vdash\mathtt{ifm}^{u}({\mathbf{R}}\,|\,\Gamma\Rightarrow v:\Box A)

Remark 7.27
.

Note that in almost all rules, we were able to derive a formula which is directly in correspondence to the rule which we were translating. This is for all rules, except for
\forall_{r}
which is directly corresponding to the rule of generalisation. In contrast, the cut-rule can also be derived as a single rule, as the next lemma shows.

Lemma 7.28
.

\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2\vdash\mathtt{ifm}^{u}({\mathbf{R}}\,|\,\Gamma\Rightarrow v:A)

\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2\vdash(F_{2}^{\and}\{A\to B\}\to F_{2}^{\mathbin{\to}}\{A\}\to F_{2}^{\and}\{B\}))\to((F_{3}^{\and}\{\top\}\to F_{3}^{\mathbin{\to}}\{C\})\to F_{3}^{\mathbin{\to}}\{C\})\to(F_{3}^{\and}\{\top\}\to F_{2}^{\mathbin{\to}}\{A\})\to(F_{2}^{\and}\{B\}\to F_{3}^{\mathbin{\to}}\{C\})\to(F_{2}^{\and}\{A\to B\}\to F_{3}^{\mathbin{\to}}\{C\})
.

By
Lemmas

7.14
and
7.15
and
\mathsf{mp}

\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2\vdash(F_{3}^{\and}\{\top\}\to F_{2}^{\mathbin{\to}}\{A\})\to(F_{2}^{\and}\{B\}\to F_{3}^{\mathbin{\to}}\{C\})\to(F_{2}^{\and}\{A\to B\}\to F_{3}^{\mathbin{\to}}\{C\})

7.11
and
7.12
and
\mathsf{mp}
, we also get

\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2\vdash F_{1}^{\mathbin{\to}}\{F_{3}^{\and}\{\top\}\to F_{2}^{\mathbin{\to}}\{A\}\}\to F_{1}^{\mathbin{\to}}\{F_{2}^{\and}\{B\}\to F_{3}^{\mathbin{\to}}\{C\}\}\to F_{1}^{\mathbin{\to}}\{F_{2}^{\and}\{A\to B\}\to F_{3}^{\mathbin{\to}}\{C\}\}
.
∎

9.
Conclusions

In this work we developed the axiomatics, semantics and proof theory of a second-order extension of tense logic, over both classical and intuitionistic bases.
We ultimately showed that several natural definitions of the intuitionistic or classical theory respectively coincide, showcasing the robustness of each logic.
Along the way we established fundamental metalogical results, namely soundness and completeness of axiomatisations with respect to certain (bi)relational semantics, and proof theoretic results, namely cut-admissibility for associated calculi based on labelled sequents.
We employed a
proof search
based approach to both of these results, establishing both simultaneously by way of our ‘grand tours’ in
Figs.

1(a)
and
1(b)
.
We conclude this work by discussing some further interesting directions of research.

Second-order logic has the capacity to define least and greatest
fixed points
of positive formulas, by encoding Knaster-Tarski style definitions (see, e.g.,
[
11
, Chapter 1]
or, at a higher level,
[
58
, Section 5.3]
).
In second-order logic with modalities we can do the same if we include a
global
modality, say
\Boxtimes
, where
\Boxtimes A

A

In particular, the least fixed point of the operator
X\mapsto A(X)
, where
X
appears only positively in
A(X)
, is given by:

\mu XA(X)\ :=\ \forall X(\Boxtimes(A(X)\to X)\to X)

This encoding already appears in
[
68
, Section 5]
, where it is observed that a second-order modal syntax can express all of the
modal
\mu
-calculus

[
40
]
.
It would be interesting to investigate the extension of our logics by a global modality according to the axiomatic, semantic and proof theoretic disciplines herein.
In the presence of tense modalities such an extension would subsume the
two-way
modal
\mu
-calculus
[
78
]
.
Note that this logic has recently received a proof theoretic treatment via
cyclic proofs

[
1
]
.

Finally it would be natural to recast second-order intuitionistic tense logic from a
proofs-as-programs
viewpoint, à la Curry-Howard (see, e.g.,
[
70
]
).
The logic
\mathsf{CK}
, a sublogic of
\mathsf{IK}
, and some extensions have already been studied from a proofs-as-programs perspective and translated into
modal lambda calculi

[
6
,
20
]
(see
[
38
]
for a survey).

Modal type theory
has independently emerged as a way to encapsulate computations with effects
[
48
]
, receiving categorical foundations in
[
32

65
]
, and has been implemented in mainstream programming languages
[
74
,
43
]
.
Recently adjoint modalities
\Diamondblack
and
\Box

[
39

To this end it would be interesting to develop a

\mathsf{IK}\mathsf{t}2

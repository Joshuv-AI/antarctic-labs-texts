Standard conjecture of Hodge type for powers of abelian varieties
Thomas Agugliaro

Abstract
. We prove that the standard conjecture of Hodge type holds for powers of abelian threefolds. Along the way, we also prove the conjecture for powers of simple abelian variety of prime dimension over finite fields, and in other related cases based on the notion of Frobenius rank of Lenstra–Zarhin. The main tool is a result comparing two real fiber functors on tannakian categories. A second tool is a new an explicit description of simple Lefschetz motives over finite fields, in terms of “enriched” Frobenius eigenvalues.



1.
Introduction

This paper is devoted to the study of the standard conjecture of Hodge type for powers of abelian varieties. The goal of this conjecture is to understand intersection forms on algebraic cycles, and more precisely the signature of such forms. The standard conjecture of Hodge type, as well as many other conjectures about algebraic cycles, is not stable under products, indeed there may be more algebraic cycles in a product of two varieties than in the varieties themselves. As an instance of our main result
Theorem

1.4
, we prove the conjecture for powers of abelian threefolds. This is the first result on the conjecture where all powers of a complete family are settled.

The standard conjecture of Hodge type have been proposed by Grothendieck
[
8
]
in
1968
, as a common generalization of the Hodge index theorem, the Hodge–Riemann bilinear relations and the positivity of the Rosati involution. The general conjecture is recalled in
3.9
, let us give here an instance of it. Let
X
be a smooth projective geometrically irreducible variety of even dimension
d
over a field
k
, and
\mathcal{Z}^{n}_{\mathrm{num}}(X)
denote the finite dimensional
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space of algebraic cycles modulo numerical equivalence, with
0\leqslant n\leqslant d
.

Conjecture 1.1
.

The intersection pairing

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}^{d/2}_{\mathrm{num}}(X)\times\mathcal{Z}^{d/2}_{\mathrm{num}}(X)

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{Q}

\displaystyle\alpha,\beta

\displaystyle\mapsto(-1)^{d/2}\alpha\cdot\beta

has signature
(s_{+},s_{-})
with

s_{+}=\rho_{d/2}-\rho_{d/2-1}+\rho_{d/2-2}-\dots+(-1)^{d/2}\rho_{0},

where
\rho_{n}=\dim(\mathcal{Z}^{n}_{\mathrm{num}}(X))
.

Apart when
X
is a surface or
X
is a complex algebraic variety, cases which were already known when the conjecture was formulated, the only results on the conjecture are rather recent. In
[
16
]
, Milne proved the conjecture for algebraic cycles which are linear combinations of intersections of divisors in abelian varieties (so called Lefschetz cycles). Ancona
[
2
]
proved the conjecture for abelian fourfolds, introducing techniques from
p
-adic Hodge theory in the picture. His tools were used by Koshikawa
[
11
]
to prove the conjecture for squares of simple abelian varieties of prime dimension over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
, as well as in
[
1
]
by the author to prove the conjecture for infinitely many abelian varieties in each dimension. Using different techniques, Ito–Ito–Koshikawa
[
9
]
proved the conjecture for
S\times S
, where
S
is any K3 surface, using the Kuga–Satake construction, and
\mathrm{CM}
-liftings to characteristic
0
.

1.1.
Main results

The first result concerns simple abelian varieties of prime dimension.

Theorem 1.2
.

The standard conjecture of Hodge type holds for powers of simple abelian varieties over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
of prime dimension.

An important property of the standard conjecture of Hodge type is its behavior under specialization: if a specialization of a variety
X
satisfies the conjecture, so does
X
. However, being simple is not a property which can be tested on specializations, so that one cannot extend
Theorem

1.2
to any field by a spreading out argument. Fortunately, we get the following corollary using a classification result on abelian threefolds
[
22
]
.

Corollary 1.3
.

The standard conjecture of Hodge type holds for powers of abelien threefolds over any field.

While studying the Tate conjecture for abelian varieties, Lenstra–Zarhin
[
23
]

[
12
]
, introduced an invariant of abelian varieties, called the Frobenius rank. Namely, given an abelian variety
A
which is geometrically simple over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, with Frobenius eigenvalues
\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{2g}
, the Frobenius rank is the rank of the multiplicative group generated by
\frac{\pi_{i}^{2}}{q}
,
1\leqslant i\leqslant 2g
. This invariant measures the complexity of the ring of Tate classes in an abelian variety, c.f.
Remark

4.8
. It has also been used by
[
11
]
to deduce results on the standard conjecture of Hodge type from
[
2
]
. The multiplicity of a simple abelian variety
A
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is the multiplicity of the roots in its characteristic polynomial. The following result is the one from which
Theorem

1.2
is deduced.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
A
be a geometrically simple abelian variety over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of dimension
g
, Frobenius rank
r
and multiplicity
m
. Assume that

(1)

m
is odd

(2)

r\geqslant\frac{g}{m}-1

(3)

there exists a totally real field
D
disjoint from the center
F
of
\End(A)\otimes\mathbb{Q}
such that
A
has
\mathrm{CM}
by
D\cdot F
.

Then all powers of
A
satisfy the standard conjecture of Hodge type.

When
m=1
,
D=\mathbb{Q}
will satisfy condition (3). As a consequence of
Theorem

1.4
and the constructions of
[
1
]
, we get

Corollary 1.5
.

For all prime numbers
p
and all even integers
g\geqslant 4
, there exists infinitely many simple abelian varieties of dimension
g
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
whose powers satisfy the standard conjecture of Hodge type, and such that there are Tate classes on
A
which are not Lefschetz, and that do not come from specializing Hodge classes of
\mathrm{CM}
-liftings.

In
[
1
]
, we construct many abelian varieties satisfying the hypothesis of
Theorem

1.4
with
m=1
. Using the same tools, it should be possible to construct abelian varieties satisfying the hypthesis for arbitrary
m>1
.

There are two main difficulties in
Theorem

1.4
. The first one is to prove the conjecture for
powers
of
A
, which may contain a lot more algebraic cycles than
A
itself, and even worse, it is not clear how the intersection forms of
A^{n}
are related to intersection forms on
A
. Moreover, factors in the motive of the abelian varieties we consider have in general very high ranks, making the use of
[
2
, Theorem 8.1]
impossible. This was the key in
[
2
]
, but also in
[
1
]
and
[
11
]
.

1.2.
Tools and idea of the proof of
Theorem

1.4

A first step is to reformulate the standard conjecture of Hodge type using tannakian categories. Namely, in
3.31
we write the intersection forms on algebraic cycles as realizations of quadratic forms defined on motives, or on Lefschetz motives (defined in
Section

2.4
).

Then, we define in
6.5
a tensor functor

R_{\infty}:\mathcal{LM}\mathrm{ot}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\to\mathrm{Isoc}_{\mathbb{R}},

on the category
\mathcal{LM}\mathrm{ot}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
of Lefschetz motives over the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
taking values in the archimedean tannakian category
\mathrm{Isoc}_{\mathbb{R}}
of real isocristals. This functor can be interpreted as a functorial archimedean cohomology theory for abelian varieties, or an archimedean Tate module. The caveat of this realization is that it is only well-defined on Lefschetz motives, or in other words, it is a cohomology theory without the cycle class map. In absence of the cycle class map, it becomes unclear that such a functor is useful to study intersections of algebraic cycles. This motivates
Theorem

6.9
and
6.11
, which are among the main novelties of this paper. These results allows one to compare the previous archimedean realization and the spaces of algebraic cycles in a Lefschetz motive.

Then, a clean classification of simple Lefschetz motives over a finite field is given in
4.14
. The simple Lefschetz motives are seen to be parametrized by
enriched eigenvalues
of abelian varieties, which are defined in
Definition

4.6
. This classification allows us to perform efficient dévissages in
Section

7
. Namely,
7.4
provides us with a decomposition of Lefschetz motives over finite fields. Then for orthogonality reasons, it suffices to prove a positivity result for each summand. By analyzing the decomposition, we see that the conjecture is already known for two of the factors, and
Theorem

7.2
gives a description of the other summands, which are called exotic.

From this description and
6.11
, we are left to prove a positivity statement for a simple exotic Lefschetz motives
M
of rank
2
over a finite field with a quadratic form
q
. Such a motive
M
does not lift to characteristic
0
, making the use of
[
2
, Theorem 8.1]
impossible. After an analysis of the situation, we see that
M^{m}
does lift (where
m
is the multiplicity). This allows us to use a theorem of Ancona–Marmora
[
3
, Theorem 3.2]
, which tells us that signature
(s_{+},s_{-})
of an auxiliary quadratic form
q^{\prime}
on
M^{m}
satisfies
4\mid s_{-}
. On the other hand, if the quadratic form
q
has signature
(2,0)
(resp.
(1,1)
or
(0,2)
), then
q^{\oplus m}
on
M^{m}
has signature
(2m,0)
(resp.
(m,m)
or
(0,2m)
). Finally, we use
6.11
to obtain that
q
and
q^{\prime}
have the same signature, and as
m
is odd, the only case where
4\mid s_{-}
is when
q
is positive definite. This was the tannakian reformulation, and this concludes the proof.

1.3.
Organization

This paper is organized as follows. In
Section

3
, we collect some basic properties on algebraic cycles, motives, tannakian categories and the standard conjecture of Hodge type. In
Section

4
, we define the group of enriched eigenvalues associated to an abelian variety, and we use it to give a classification of simple Lefschetz motives over finite fields. Various corollaries of

Section

Section

Namely, we define a functor from Lefschetz motives to real isocrystals, and we give results allowing one to compare quadratic forms in different realizations. Using the description of simple objects of the category of Lefschetz motives, in
Section

7
we describe a fine decomposition result for motives of abelian varieties satisfying the hypthesis of
Theorem

1.4
. Finally, in
Section

8
we prove
Theorem

1.4
, using the tannakian result
6.11
on the category of Lefschetz motives, and the input of
p
-adic Hodge theory from
[
3
]
.

Conventions

2.1.
Number fields and
p
-adic fields

A number field is a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}
. A totally real field

If

. Moreover,

2.2.
Motives

We will work with the category of homological motives

3.1.
Algebraic cycles and the standard conjecture of Hodge type

In this section, we will fix a field
k

H
a Weil cohomology for smooth projective varieties over
k
, satisfying the Hard Lefschetz property. We will also consider
X/k
a smooth projective, geometrically irreducible algebraic variety of dimension

, and
\mathcal{L}
an ample divisor on

Definition 3.1
.

For
0\leqslant n\leqslant d
, we denote by
\mathrm{CH}^{n}(X)
the Chow group of codimension
n
cycles of
X
. That is the free
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space generated by irreducible closed subvarieties of
X
, of codimension
n
, modulo rational equivalence. The intersection pairing

\mathrm{CH}^{n}(X)\times\mathrm{CH}^{d-n}(X)\to\mathrm{CH}^{d}(X)\xrightarrow{\deg}\mathbb{Q}

will be denoted
Z\cdot Z^{\prime}
.

Two cycles
Z_{1},Z_{2}\in\mathcal{\mathrm{CH}}^{n}(X)
are said to be numerically equivalent if for all
Z^{\prime}\in\mathrm{CH}^{d-n}(X)
,
Z_{1}\cdot Z^{\prime}=Z_{2}\cdot Z^{\prime}
. This defines an equivalence relation on
\mathrm{CH}^{n}(X)
, called numerical equivalence.

Two cycles
Z_{1},Z_{2}
are said to be equivalent with respect to the Weil cohomology
H
if
\mathrm{cl}(Z_{1})=\mathrm{cl}(Z_{2})
. This defines an equivalence relation on
\mathrm{CH}^{n}(X)
, called homological equivalence.

Definition 3.2
.

The quotient of
\mathrm{CH}^{n}(X)
by numerical equivalence (resp. homological equivalence) will be denoted
\mathcal{Z}^{n}_{\mathrm{num}}(X)
(resp.
\mathcal{Z}^{n}_{\mathrm{hom}}(X)
).

Homological equivalence is finer than numerical equivalence, so that we get a map

\mathcal{Z}^{n}_{\mathrm{hom}}(X)\to\mathcal{Z}^{n}_{\mathrm{num}}(X).

This map is compatible with the intersection products

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}^{n}_{\mathrm{num}}(X)

\displaystyle\times\mathcal{Z}^{m}_{\mathrm{num}}(X)\to\mathcal{Z}^{n+m}_{\mathrm{num}}(X),

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}^{n}_{\mathrm{hom}}(X)

\displaystyle\times\mathcal{Z}^{m}_{\mathrm{hom}}(X)\to\mathcal{Z}^{n+m}_{\mathrm{hom}}(X).

Theorem 3.3

(Hard Lefschetz)
.

Let
[\mathcal{L}]\in H^{2}(X)
be the image of
\mathcal{L}
by the cycle class map. The Lefschetz operator
\cup[\mathcal{L}]
induces isomorphisms for all
0\leqslant n\leqslant d

\cup[\mathcal{L}]^{d-n}:H^{n}(X)\to H^{2d-n}(X).

Definition 3.4
.

The
n
-th primitive part of
X
is the kernel
P^{n}_{\mathcal{L}}(X)
of

\cup[\mathcal{L}]^{d-n+1}:H^{n}(X)\to H^{2d-n+2}(X).

The following proposition is a consequence of
Theorem

3.3
, it shows that the whole cohomology of
X
can be expressed using primitive parts.

Proposition 3.5
.

For every
0\leqslant n\leqslant d
, the following map is an isomorphism

\begin{array}[]{rrcl}\bigoplus_{0\leqslant i\leqslant\frac{n}{2}}P^{n-2i}_{\mathcal{L}}(X)&\rightarrow&H^{n}(X)\\
(\alpha_{i})_{0\leqslant i\leqslant\frac{n}{2}}&\mapsto&\sum_{0\leqslant i\leqslant\frac{n}{2}}\alpha_{i}\cup[\mathcal{L}]^{i}\end{array}

Moreover, the induced decomposition on
H^{n}(X)
is orthogonal with respect to the intersection forms.

We are now able to state the main positivity result in characteristic
0
, see
[
21
, Theorem 6.32]
.

Theorem 3.6

(Hodge–Riemann relations)
.

Assume that
k=\mathbb{C}
and that
H
is singular cohomology with rational coefficients, endowed with its pure Hodge structure. Then for all
0\leqslant n\leqslant d
, the bilinear form

\begin{array}[]{rrcl}\eta_{\mathcal{L}}:&P^{n}_{\mathcal{L}}(X)\times P^{n}_{\mathcal{L}}(X)&\to&H^{2d}(X,\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathbb{Q}\\
&(\alpha,\beta)&\mapsto&i^{n}[\mathcal{L}]^{d-n}\cup\alpha\cup\beta\end{array}

is a polarization of Hodge structures.

The goal of the standard conjecture of Hodge type is to find a motivic version of
Theorem

3.6
. The same definitions as before also make sense in the context of algebraic cycles.

Definition 3.7
.

The
n
-th primitive parts of
X
is defined for
0\leqslant n\leqslant d/2
as the kernel
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{L}}^{n}(X)
of

\cdot\mathcal{L}^{d-2n+1}:\mathcal{Z}^{n}_{\mathrm{num}}(X)\to\mathcal{Z}^{d-n+1}_{\mathrm{num}}(X).

Remark 3.8
.

The primitive part in cohomology is denoted with a straight
P
, whereas the letter denoting primitive part in algebraic cycles is calligraphed. Hence, the cycle class map relates
\mathcal{P}^{n}_{\mathcal{L}}(X)
with

.

Conjecture 3.9

(Standard conjecture of Hodge type)
.

For any
0\leqslant n\leqslant d/2
, the pairing

\begin{array}[]{rrcl}\eta_{\mathcal{L}}:&\mathcal{P}^{n}_{\mathcal{L}}(X)\times\mathcal{P}^{n}_{\mathcal{L}}(X)&\to&\mathbb{Q}\\
&(\alpha,\beta)&\mapsto&(-1)^{n}\mathcal{L}^{d-2n}\cdot\alpha\cdot\beta\end{array}

is positive definite.

Remark 3.10
.

If

, then

3.6

\begin{array}[]{rrcl}\mathrm{cl}^{n}:\mathcal{Z}^{n}(X)\to H^{2n}(X)\end{array}

is compatible with intersection, and it has values in the

-part of the Hodge decomposition.

G=\underline{\Aut}^{\otimes}(\id_{\mathcal{T}})
the Tannaka group of
\mathcal{T}
, even when
\mathcal{T}
is non-neutral.

Definition 3.16
.

Given a tannakian category
\mathcal{T}
and
V
an object of
\mathcal{T}
, we denote by

the smallest full tannakian subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
containing

.

Remark 3.17
.

Alternatively, objects of
\langle V\rangle^{\otimes}
are subquotients of
V^{\otimes n}\otimes(V^{*})^{\otimes m}
for

and

in
\mathbb{Z}
.

Using the monoidal structures of tannakian categories, we can do most of the usual multilinear algebra.

Definition 3.18
.

Let
V
be an object of a tannakian category
\mathcal{T}
, and
\mathbb{L}
be an invertible object. A quadratic form on
V
, with values in
\mathbb{L}
is a symmetric map

q:V\otimes V\to\mathbb{L}.

More precisely, denote by
\tau
the automorphism of
V\otimes V
swapping the factors, we ask that the map
q
satisfies
q\circ\tau=q
.

A quadratic form is said to be non-degenerate if the map

V\to\underline{\Hom}(V,\mathbb{L})

obtained by adjunction is an isomorphism.

Another classical multilinear object is the determinant, we describe here how it is obtained in tannakian categories.

Definition 3.19
.

Assume that
K
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra, let
V
be an object of
\mathcal{T}
of dimension
d
. Consider the action of
S_{d}
on

which permutes the pure tensors. We denote by
\bigwedge^{d}V
the isotypical part of
V^{\otimes d}
relative to the signature representation of
S_{d}
. More precisely the action of
S_{d}
on
V^{\otimes d}
extends to a module structure for the algebra
K[S_{n}]
, and

\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\varepsilon(\sigma)[\sigma]\in K[S_{n}],

is an idempotent element. Hence it yields a projector of
V^{\otimes d}
, whose image is denoted
\bigwedge^{d}V
.

Lemma 3.20
.

The map

K\to\Hom(\wedge^{d}V,\wedge^{d}V)

mapping
1
to
\id_{\bigwedge^{d}V}
is an isomorphism, whose inverse is the trace.

Proof.

As
\wedge^{d}V
has dimension
1
, we have
\mathrm{Tr}(\id_{\wedge^{d}V})=1
, so that the trace is a left inverse of

c:K\to\Hom(\wedge^{d}V,\wedge^{d}V).

It suffices to check that
c
is an isomorphism after tensoring by a faithfully flat
K
-algebra, so that we can assume that
\mathcal{T}
has a
K
-linear fiber functor
\omega
. The composition

K\xrightarrow{c}\Hom(\wedge^{d}V,\wedge^{d}V)\xrightarrow{\omega}\Hom(\wedge^{d}\omega(V),\wedge^{d}\omega(V))

is an isomorphism. As
\omega
is faithful, we deduce that
c
is itself an isomorphism.
∎

Definition 3.21
.

Let
V
be an object of a
K
-linear tannakian category
\mathcal{T}
, and
u
an endomorphism of
V
. Let
d
denote the dimension of
V
, and
\bigwedge^{d}V
be the
d
-th exterior power of
V
. Then

—

u
induces an endomorphism
\bigwedge u\in\End(\bigwedge^{d}V)
. The scalar

\det(u)=\mathrm{Tr}(\bigwedge u)

is called determinant of
u
.

—

Consider the object
V_{[X]}=V\otimes_{K}K[X]
, in the
K[X]
-linear tannakian category
\mathcal{T}_{[X]}=\mathcal{T}\otimes_{K}K[X]
, and the endomorphism
X\id_{V_{[X]}}-u
. The determinant of this endomorphism is an element of

, which is denoted

\chi_{u}(X)=\det(X\id_{V}-u).

The previous definition is intrinsic, it only uses the monoidal structure.

Proposition 3.22
.

Given a tannakian category
\mathcal{T}
, and an endomorphism
u
of an object
V
of
\mathcal{T}
we have for any fiber functor
\omega:\mathcal{T}\to\mathrm{Vect}_{K}
:

(1)

\det(u)=\det(\omega(u))

(2)

\chi_{u}=\chi_{\omega(u)}

where in both of these equalities the left hand side is defined by
Definition

3.21
, and the right hand side coincides with the usual notion for linear endomorphisms.

Proof.

Fiber functors commute with

. As
Definition

3.21
only depends on the monoidal structure, the determinant and characteristic polynomial will commute with
\omega
. Moreover in
\mathrm{Vect}_{K}
, the usual determinant can be computed as in
Definition

3.21
.
∎

The following proposition will be useful to describe simple objects in tannakian categories.

Proposition 3.23
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a
F
-linear tannakian category, where
F
is a field of characteristic
0
, and
L/F
be a Galois extension with Galois group
G
. Assume that the Tannaka group of
\mathcal{T}
is an algebraic torus
T
, and that
T
is split by
L
. Then there is a bijection

\begin{array}[]{rrcl}\{\text{Simple object of }\mathcal{T}\}&\cong&\mathrm{Orb}_{G}(X^{\star}(T_{L}))\\
M&\mapsto&\{\text{characters for the action of }T\text{ on }M\}.\end{array}

Remark 3.24
.

In the previous proposition, it is tempting to write
X^{\star}(T_{L})/G
instead of
\mathrm{Orb}_{G}(X^{\star}(T_{L}))
, but one should be aware that the quotient is taken as sets, and not as abelian groups.

Proof.

The category
\mathcal{T}_{L}
has a split torus as Tannaka group. According to
[
17
, Chap 6, Prop 3.5.1]
there is a one-to-one correspondance between simple objects of
\mathcal{T}_{L}
and
X^{\star}(T_{L})
, sending an object
M
to the unique character of
T
acting non-trivially on
M
.

First, let us prove that the map defined in the statement is well defined, that is that
G
acts transitivelly on the characters of simple objects. Let
M
be a simple object of
\mathcal{T}
, and let
S
be the set of characters for the action of
T_{L}
on
M
. Assume that we have a partition
S=S_{1}\cup S_{2}
which respects the action of
G
. Then
M_{L}=M_{1}\oplus M_{2}
where characters of
M_{i}
are in
S_{i}
. By Galois descent of objects in Tannaka categories
[
17
, Chap 3, Prop. 1.2.3]
, we get a descent datum on
M_{L}
. Moreover, this descent datum stabilizes
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
(because there are no maps from
M_{1}
to
M_{2}
or from
M_{2}
to
M_{1}
). Hence
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
come from objects of
\mathcal{T}
, and as
M
is simple, either
S_{1}=\emptyset
or
S_{2}=\emptyset
.

Now, whenever one has two objects
M,N
of
\mathcal{T}
such that
\Hom(M,N)=0
, then

\Hom(M_{L},N_{L})=0.

It follows that the map (
3.23
) is injective.

For the surjectivity, we will again use Galois descent of objects in Tannaka categories. Consider
S
an orbit for the action of
G
on
X^{\star}(T_{L})
. Fix
\chi\in S
, and consider

N=\bigoplus_{g\in G}L_{\chi}^{g},

where
L_{\mu}
denotes the simple object of
\mathcal{T}_{L}
associated to
\mu\in X^{\star}(\mathcal{T})
. We endow
N
with the descent datum

\displaystyle N^{\sigma}

\displaystyle\cong N

\displaystyle(a_{g})_{g\in G}

\displaystyle\mapsto(a_{\sigma^{-1}g})_{g\in G}

under the identification of
(L_{\chi}^{g})^{\sigma}
with
L_{\chi}^{\sigma g}
. By Galois descent, there is an associated object
N^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{T}
such that
N^{\prime}_{L}\cong N
. In other words,
N^{\prime}
is the Weil restriction of scalars of
L_{\chi}
along the extension
L/F
. Let
M
be any simple component of
N^{\prime}
, as
L_{\chi}^{g}\cong L_{g\chi}
, the set of characters for the action of
T_{L}
on
M
is
S
.
∎

Remark 3.25
.

In the proof of the previous result, be aware that
M_{L}
might not be isomorphic to
\bigoplus_{\mu\in S}L_{\mu}
. There might be some
multiplicity
in the simple summands over
L
, for example this is the case for
\mathrm{Isoc}_{\mathbb{R}}
which we will describe now.

Definition 3.26
.

We denote by
\mathrm{Isoc}_{\mathbb{R}}
the category whose objects are finite dimensional
\mathbb{C}
-vector spaces
V
endowed with

(i)

a decomposition
V=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{n}
,

(ii)

a
\mathbb{C}
-antilinear automorphism
\sigma:V\to V
,

such that
\sigma
respects the decomposition and for any

\sigma_{|V_{n}}^{2}=(-1)^{n}\id_{V_{n}}
.

Morphisms in
\mathrm{Isoc}_{\mathbb{R}}
are the
\mathbb{C}
-linear maps which respect the structures. The
\Hom
spaces are
\mathbb{R}
-vector spaces, and we endow
\mathrm{Isoc}_{\mathbb{R}}
with the following monoidal structure:

(V\otimes W)_{n}=\bigoplus_{a+b=n}V_{a}\otimes W_{b}.

This makes
\mathrm{Isoc}_{\mathbb{R}}
into a
\mathbb{R}
-linear tannakian category.

Remark 3.27
.

In
\mathrm{Isoc}_{\mathbb{R}}
, the following object is simple:

V=\mathbb{C}^{2}=V_{1},

endowed with the automorphism
\sigma(z_{1},z_{2})=(-\overline{z_{2}},\overline{z_{1}})
. Indeed, one can compute that

is the quaternion algebra

.

But in the category

Hence there exist projectors exhibiting

as the square of a simple object of

.

3.3.
Motives and the standard conjecture of Hodge type

The monoidal categories that we will be considering in this article are categories of motives. Informally, they are the categories you obtain when you transform the category of algebraic varieties into a monoidal category. For a precise discussion, see
[
4
]
. As explained in the conventions, we will be working with motives with respect to homological equivalence.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a smooth projective scheme of relative dimension
d
over
S
satisfying the Künneth standard conjecture, where
S
is either a field or the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
of a
p
-adic field

. Let

be a relatively ample line bundle on

Definition 3.28
.

We say that
\mathcal{X}
satisfies the relative standard conjecture of Lefschetz type if the Lefschetz operator induces an isomorphism for all
0\leqslant n\leqslant d

\cup[\mathcal{L}]^{d-n}:\mathfrak{h}^{n}(\mathcal{X})\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}^{2d-n}(\mathcal{X})(d-n).

In any case, the
n
-th motivic primitive part is the kernel
\mathfrak{P}^{n}_{\mathcal{L}}(X)
of

\cup[\mathcal{L}]^{d-n+1}:\mathfrak{h}^{n}(\mathcal{X})\to\mathfrak{h}^{2d-n+2}(\mathcal{X})(d-n+1).

The motivic intersection form
\eta_{\mathcal{L},\mathrm{mot}}
is the composite of the following maps

\displaystyle\mathfrak{P}^{n}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{X})\otimes\mathfrak{P}^{n}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{X})\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}^{n}(\mathcal{X})\otimes\mathfrak{h}^{n}(\mathcal{X}),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}^{n}(\mathcal{X})\otimes\mathfrak{h}^{n}(\mathcal{X})\cong\mathfrak{h}^{n}(\mathcal{X})\otimes\mathfrak{h}^{2d-n}(\mathcal{X})(d-n),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}^{n}(\mathcal{X})\otimes\mathfrak{h}^{2d-n}(\mathcal{X})(d-n)\rightarrow\mathds{1}(-n).

Proposition 3.29
.

Assume that
\mathcal{X}
satisfies the relative standard conjecture of Lefschetz type. Then for all
0\leqslant n\leqslant d
, the map

\begin{array}[]{rrcl}\bigoplus\cup[\mathcal{L}]^{i}:\bigoplus_{0\leqslant i\leqslant\frac{n}{2}}\mathfrak{P}^{n-2i}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{X})(-i)&\rightarrow&\mathfrak{h}^{n}(\mathcal{X})\end{array}

is an isomorphism.

Remark 3.30
.

The functor
R_{Z}
from
Section

2.2
is lax–monoidal, so that whenever
\eta
is a quadratic form on

,

is a quadratic form on

.

Proposition 3.31
.

Let
S=\Spec(k)
,

a smooth projective variety over

,
\mathcal{L}
is an ample divisor on

,
\mathcal{P}^{n}_{\mathcal{L}}(X)

as in

\displaystyle R_{Z}(\mathfrak{P}^{2n}_{\mathcal{L}}(X)(n))

\displaystyle R_{Z}(\eta_{\mathcal{L},\mathrm{mot}}(n))

\displaystyle=\eta_{\mathcal{L}}.

The following would be a generalization of
Theorem

3.6
in a motivic setting.

There is a notion of polarization on motives (c.f.

3.33
)such that

4.
Enriched eigenvalues and Lefschetz motives

In this section, we define a new invariant of abelian varieties, which we call the group of enriched eigenvalues. Then, we use this invariant to give an explicit description of simple Lefschetz motives in
4.14
. Then we describe the dimension of a Lefschetz motive in terms of its associated enriched eigenvalues in
4.16
. Finally, we finish the section with two lemmas which are used in
Section

8
. First, let us recall the standard definitions.

4.1.
Enriched eigenvalues of abelian varieties

Let
p
be a prime number. Let
A
be an abelian variety of dimension
g
over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of characteristic
p
. Consider

\End^{0}(A)=\End(A)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}

and
F
the center of
\End^{0}(A)
. The geometric Frobenius
\pi
of
A
is a central endomorphism of
A
. Moreover by
[
20
, Theorem 2]
,
F=\mathbb{Q}(\pi)
.

Definition 4.1
.

When
A
is simple, the multiplicity of
A
is the integer
m
such that

m^{2}=\dim_{F}\End^{0}(A).

Remark 4.2
.

By
[
20
, Theorem 1]
,
m
is also the multiplicity of the roots of the characteristic polynomial of
\pi
, that is it satisfies
2g=m[F:\mathbb{Q}]
.

Let us go back to the case where
A
is not necessarily simple. Here
F
is a semisimple commutative algebra, finite over
\mathbb{Q}
, hence it is a product of number fields. Let
\tilde{F}
denote a Galois closure of a compositum of the factors of
F
, and
G=\mathrm{Gal}(\tilde{F}/\mathbb{Q})
.
The following notion of rank appears in
[
12
]
,
[
23
]
, and is useful to study Tate classes in powers of abelian varieties.

Definition 4.3
.

Denote by
\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{d}
the images of
\pi
in
\tilde{F}
along maps
F\to\tilde{F}
. Consider the subgroup
\Gamma
of
\tilde{F}^{\times}
generated by
\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{d}
. The Frobenius rank of
A
is

\mathrm{rk}(A):=\mathrm{rk}(\Gamma)-1=\mathrm{rk}(\Gamma/\langle p\rangle).

Remark 4.4
.

If
A
is simple with multiplicity
m
, then
d=\frac{2g}{m}
, and as
\pi_{i}\overline{\pi}_{i}=q=1
in
\tilde{F}^{\times}/\langle p\rangle
, we have

0\leqslant\mathrm{rk}(A)\leqslant\frac{g}{m}.

Notation 4.5
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We will denote by
F
the center of
\End^{0}(A)
(
F
is a product of number fields), by
\tilde{F}
a Galois closure of a compositum of the factors of
F
, and
r
the Frobenius rank of
A
. Moreover, when
A
is simple, we will denote by
m
the multiplicity of
A
, which is the multiplicity of the roots of the characteristic polynomial of
A
.

The following definition introduces the new invariant of this section.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, with Frobenius endomorphism
\pi\in F
, and let

\Pi=\{\sigma(\pi)\mid\sigma:F\to\tilde{F}\}.

The group
\mathrm{Eig}(A)
of enriched eigenvalues of
A
is the free abelian group generated by
\Pi\cup q
, denoted multiplicatively, modulo the relations
[\pi][\overline{\pi}]=[q]
with
\pi\in\Pi
.

Definition 4.7
.

The realization map is the map

\rho:\mathrm{Eig}(A)\to\tilde{F}^{\times}

induced by the inclusion
\Pi\cup q\subset\tilde{F}^{\times}
.

Remark 4.8
.

By
[
23
, Theorem 3.4.3]
, if
\rho
is injective, then all Tate classes in the cohomology of powers of
A
are Lefschetz classes. So that
\mathrm{Ker}(\rho)
measures the defect of Tate classes to be Lefschetz classes.

We will call
\mathrm{Ker}(\rho)
the group of nontrivial multiplicative relations between
\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{d}
, it is a free abelian group of rank
\frac{g}{m}-\mathrm{rk}(A)
.

4.2.
Simple Lefschetz motives

In this article, algebraic cycles come in two flavors:

(i)

the cycles for which we have to prove positivity in
3.9

(ii)

the cycles that we use to manipulate and decompose motives.

The point of using Lefschetz motives is to have a formalism that distinguishes between these two kinds of algebraic cycles. For more precisions, see
2.4
.

Remark 4.9
.

As homological equivalence coincides with numerical equivalence for Lefschetz cycles
[
13
, Prop 5.2]
, we have the usual realization functors which are well defined on Lefschetz motives. Hence, it makes the category of Lefschetz motive into a tannakian category. Moreover by
[
10
]
, the category of Lefschetz motive is semisimple.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, whose geometric endomorphisms are defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and
L_{A}
be the Tannaka group of
\langle A\rangle^{\otimes}
. Then
L_{A}
is an algebraic tori split by
\tilde{F}
(c.f.
4.5
), and there is a natural Galois-equivariant isomorphism of abelian groups

X^{\star}(L_{A,\tilde{F}})\cong\mathrm{Eig}(A).

Proof.

Let
A
be an abelian variety over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, with Tannaka group
L_{A}
. Assume that the geometric endomorphisms of
A
are defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then the Lefschetz group of
A
is the same as the Lefschetz group of
A_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
.

Let
\Pi
be the set of eigenvalues of
A
in a spliting field
\tilde{F}
of the characteristic polynomial. By
[
14
, Lemma 4.2]
, we have an identification

X^{*}(L_{A,\tilde{F}})=\{f:\Pi\to\mathbb{Z}\}/\{f\>|\;f=\iota f\text{ and }\sum_{\pi\in\Pi}f(\pi)=0\},

where
\iota
denote complex conjugation. In particular,
L_{A}
is split by
\tilde{L}
.
Consider the Galois equivariant map

\Pi\cup\{q\}\to X^{\star}(L_{A,\tilde{F}})

mapping
\pi\in\Pi
to the characteristic function
f_{\pi}
of
\pi
and
q
to
f_{\pi}+f_{\overline{\pi}}
for any
\pi
(in the quotient, this is independant of
\pi\in\Pi
).
It induces a unique map,

\mathrm{Eig}(A)\to X^{\star}(L_{A,\tilde{F}}),

by the universal propery of the free group with relations. This map is Galois equivariant, and surjective because any function
f:\Pi\to\mathbb{Z}
can be written as a sum of characteristic functions of elements. For injectivity, let

\lambda=[q]^{a}\cdot\prod_{\pi\in\Pi}[\pi]^{a_{\pi}}

be in the kernel. By replacing
[q]
by
[\pi_{0}][\overline{\pi_{0}}]
, we can assume that
a=0
. Then, the map

f:\pi\mapsto a_{\pi}

satisfies
f=\iota f
and
\sum_{\pi\in\Pi}f(\pi)=0
. In particular, for all
\pi
,
a_{\pi}=a_{\overline{\pi}}
, so that we can write

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle=\prod_{\{\pi,\overline{\pi}\}\in\Pi/\iota}([\pi][\overline{\pi}])^{a_{\pi}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\{\pi,\overline{\pi}\}\in\Pi/\iota}[q]^{a_{\pi}},

\displaystyle=[q]^{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\pi\in\Pi}a_{\pi}}

\displaystyle=1.

∎

Definition 4.11
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, whose geometric endomorphisms are defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and
M
be a Lefschetz motive of rank
r
in
\langle A\rangle^{\otimes}
. Let
\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{r}
be the characters for the action of
L_{A}
on
M
. We will call enriched eigenvalue of
M
, and denote
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r}
the elements of
\mathrm{Eig}(A)
corresponding to
\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{r}
via the identification of
4.10
.

Proposition 4.12
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, whose endomorphisms are defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and
M\in\langle A\rangle^{\otimes}
be a Lefschetz motive with enriched eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r}\in\mathrm{Eig}(A)
. Then the action of Frobenius on
R_{\ell}(M)
has eigenvalues
\rho(\lambda_{1}),\dots,\rho(\lambda_{r})
(where
\rho
is defined in
Definition

4.7
).

Remark 4.13
.

4.12
explains why we chose the name

eigenvalues: one can recover the usual eigenvalues from the enriched eigenvalues.

Proof.

Let
F
be the center of
\End^{0}(A)
. It is a product of
\mathrm{CM}
fields and totally real fields, denote by
\tilde{F}
a Galois closure of a compositum of these fields. Let
M\in\langle A\rangle^{\otimes}
, and denote
M_{\tilde{F}}\in\langle A\rangle^{\otimes}_{\tilde{F}}
the object obtained by extension of scalars. By
4.10
, the Lefschetz group
L_{A}
is split by
\tilde{L}
. Hence we can decompose
M_{\tilde{F}}
as a sum of its isotypical components

M_{\tilde{F}}=M_{\chi_{1}}\oplus\dots\oplus M_{\chi_{r}}

where
\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{r}
are the characters for the action of
L_{A}
on
M
.
Let
\ell\not=p
, and fix an embedding
\tilde{F}\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
. Now, we consider the
\ell
-adic realization

R_{\ell}(M_{\tilde{F}})=R_{\ell}(M_{\chi_{1}})\oplus\dots\oplus R_{\ell}(M_{\chi_{r}}).

The eigenvalues of Frobenius on
R_{\ell}(M)
are the same as the Frobenius eigenvalues on
R_{\ell}(M_{\tilde{F}})
, so that it suffices to show that the Frobenius eigenvalues on
R_{\ell}(M_{\chi_{i}})
is
\rho(\lambda_{i})
, where
\lambda_{i}
is associated to
\chi_{i}
via
4.10
.

In turn, it suffice to consider the simple Lefschetz motive
N_{\chi}
in
\langle A\rangle^{\otimes}_{\tilde{L}}
of type
\chi\in X^{\star}(L_{A,\tilde{F}})
(which are defined in
[
17
, Chap 6, Prop 3.5.1]
), and show that the eigenvalue on Frobenius on
R_{\ell}(N_{\chi})
is
\rho(\lambda)
, where
\lambda
is associated to
\chi
via
4.10
. The character associated to
N_{\chi}\otimes N_{\chi^{\prime}}
is
\chi+\chi^{\prime}
, and the associated eigenvalue of Frobenius is the product of the eigenvalues on
N_{\chi}
and on
N_{\chi^{\prime}}
. Hence it suffices to show the desired equality on a generating set of characters.

Such a set is given by the characters of
L_{A}
acting on
\mathfrak{h}^{1}(A)
. According to
[
14
, Remark 1.10]
, we have that

L_{A}(\mathbb{Q})=\{\alpha\in F\mid\alpha\cdot\overline{\alpha}\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}\},

and moreover the Frobenius
\pi\in F
corresponds to an element of
L_{A}(\mathbb{Q})
. This equality is such that
\pi
acts on
R_{\ell}(\mathfrak{h}^{1}(A))
as the geometric Frobenius acting by functoriality. Hence its eigenvalues are the eigenvalues of Frobenius. The other representation theoretic description of these eigenvalues is the following: they are the
\chi(\pi)
where
\chi
runs through the characters for the action of
L_{A}
on
\mathfrak{h}^{1}(A)
. These characters are just the restrictions of ring maps
F\to\tilde{F}
, so that the
\chi(\pi)\in\tilde{L}
is
\pi_{i}
for some
i
, which is the realization of
[\pi_{i}]
.
∎

Tate conjecture
3.37
predicts that there should be a nonzero map between two motives having an eigenvalue in common. The following proposition, which holds inconditionnaly, gives an analog of this with enriched eigenvalues. The main component of its proof is Tate’s theorem on divisors of abelian varieties
[
20
]
.

Proposition 4.14
.

Let
A/\mathbb{F}_{q}
be an abelian variety whose geometric endomorphisms are defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then the map

\begin{array}[]{rrcl}\{\text{Simple objects of }\langle A\rangle^{\otimes}\}&\to&\mathrm{Orb}_{G}(\mathrm{Eig}(A))\\
M&\mapsto&\{\text{enriched eigenvalues of }M\}\end{array}

is bijective.

Remark 4.15
.

As in
Remark

3.24
,
\mathrm{Orb}_{G}
denotes the set of orbits, and not the quotient as abelian groups.

Proof.

By
4.10
,
\langle A\rangle_{\tilde{F}}
has a split torus
L_{A,\tilde{L}}
as Tannaka group.
Hence, by
3.23
, we get a bijection

\begin{array}[]{rrcl}\{\text{Simple objects of }\langle A\rangle^{\otimes}\}&\cong&\mathrm{Orb}_{G}(X^{\star}(L_{A}))\\
M&\mapsto&\{\text{characters for the action of }L_{A,\tilde{L}}\text{ on }M\}.\end{array}

We conclude by using the isomorphism

\mathrm{Orb}_{G}(X^{\star}(L_{A}))\cong\mathrm{Orb}_{G}(\mathrm{Eig}(A))

provided by
4.10
.
∎

Proposition 4.16
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, whose geometric endomorphisms are defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and
M
be a simple Lefschetz motive in
\langle A\rangle^{\otimes}
with
S
as set of enriched eigenvalues. Assume that
M
is pure of weight
2n
, and that the Frobenius acts on
M
by multiplication by
q^{n}
, then
\dim(M)=\#S
.

Proof.

Let
Z
denote the center of
\End(M)
. Then
\End(M)
being a central simple
Z
-algebra, it has dimension
a^{2}
as a
Z
-vector space. The integer
a
is called multiplicity of
M
, and it is also the order of
[\End(M)]
in
\mathrm{Br}(Z)
. With these notations, we have
\dim(M)=a\deg(Z)
. Indeed, consider
L
a maximal commutative subalgebra of
\End(M)
, and
\tilde{L}
a Galois closure of
L
. Then
L
is an extension of
Z
of degree
a
, and
\deg(L)=a\deg(Z)
. Moreover, one has
\deg(L)=\dim(M)
, because
M_{\tilde{L}}
can be decomposed into a direct sum of characters indexed by embeddings
L\to\tilde{L}
.

In addition,
Z
can be computed as the fixed field of
\mathrm{Fix}_{G}(s)
for any
s\in S
, where

G=\mathrm{Gal}(\tilde{F}/\mathbb{Q}).

Hence
\deg(Z)=\#S
. To conclude, it suffices to prove that
a=1
. To do so, we can compute the local invariants of
\End(M)
as a central simple
Z
-algebra.

Consider the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear tannakian category
\langle M(n)\rangle
. For all
\ell\not=p
,
\ell
-adic cohomology defines a fiber functor on
\langle M(n)\rangle
. From this, we deduce that the local invariants at
\ell\not=p
vanish. Moreover, as the Frobenius acts on
M
by multiplication by
q^{n}
, it will act on
M(n)
by the identity. Hence the
F
-isocrystal associated to
M(n)
has slope
0
. The functor on Lefschetz motives extending

A\mapsto H_{\mathrm{crys}}(A\times_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}/W(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}))

maps
\langle M(n)\rangle
to
F
-isocrystals of slope
0
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. This category being equivalent to the category of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-vector spaces, crystalline cohomology defines a
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-linear fiber functor on
\langle M(n)\rangle
, so that the invariants at
p
also vanish. As
M(n)
has weight
0
, and using
6.7
,
R_{\infty}
defines a
\mathbb{R}
-linear fiber functor, so that the invariants at the infinite place vanish.
∎

We end this section with two lemmas on simple Lefschetz motives which will be used in

. They concern

Lefschetz motives as defined in

.

Lemma 4.17
.

Let
k
be a subfield of
\mathbb{C}
, and
N\in\mathcal{LM}\mathrm{ot}^{\mathrm{CM}}(k)
be a geometrically simple object of even weight which is not a Tate twist of the unit. Then
N
has complex multiplication by the field
L=\End(N)
, and there exists a quadratic form
\eta
on
N
such that

i)

for every
z\in L
, the adjoint of
z
with respect to
\eta
is
\overline{z}
.

ii)

R_{H}(\eta)
is a polarization of Hodge structure.

Proof.

By definition,
N
is a direct summand of the motive
\mathfrak{h}^{\star}(A)
associated to a
\mathrm{CM}
abelian variety
A
. Let
q
be a quadratic form on
\mathfrak{h}^{\star}(A)
such that
R_{H}(q)
is a polarization of Hodge structure. Then the restriction
\eta
of

to
N
is such that
R_{H}(\eta)
is a polarization on the Hodge structure

, in particular
\eta
is nondegenerate.

Adjunction with respect to
\eta
induces an involution
x\mapsto x^{\dagger}
on the commutative field

. As
R_{H}(\eta)
is a polarization, we get that
x\mapsto x^{\dagger}
is a positive involution, so that
L
is either a totally real field or a
\mathrm{CM}
field with complex conjugation
\dagger
. We conclude by
Lemma

4.18
, because

is not a Tate twist of the unit.
∎

Lemma 4.18
.

Let
k
be a subfield of
\mathbb{C}
, and
N
be a geometrically simple Lefschetz motive in
\mathcal{LM}\mathrm{ot}^{\mathrm{CM}}(k)
. Then either
N
is a Tate twist of the unit, or the field
\End(N)
is not totally real.

Proof.

Let
A
be an abelian variety over
k
of
\mathrm{CM}
type such that
N
is a submotive of
\mathfrak{h}^{1}(A)^{\otimes n}
for some
n
. We can assume that
k
is algebraically closed, and that
A=\prod_{i}A_{i}
has
\mathrm{CM}
by a
\mathrm{CM}
-algebra
L=\prod L_{i}
, with
\mathrm{CM}
type
\Phi\subset\Hom(L,k)
. Let
\tilde{L}
be the Galois closure in
k
of a compositum of the
L_{i}
’s. Then, we can identify
\Hom(L,k)
with
\Hom(L,\tilde{L})
, and we will denote
G=\mathrm{Gal}(\tilde{L}/\mathbb{Q})
and
\iota\in G
the complex conjugation.

In
[
14
, Proposition 2.5]
, Milne considers Galois orbits of
\mathrm{CM}
types. But according to
[
14
, Proposition 2.2]
, we have that
\Hom(L,\tilde{L})\cong\mathcal{C}
where
\mathcal{C}
is the Galois orbit of
\Phi
. We deduce from

that

corresponds to a conjugacy class

of elements in

X^{\star}(L(A))=\frac{\{f:\Hom(L,\tilde{L})\to\mathbb{Z}\}}{\{f\;|\;f=\iota f\text{ and }\sum_{\sigma\in\Hom(L,\tilde{L})}f(\sigma)=0\}}

for the action of

. Moreover, the endomorphism algebra of

is isomorphic to the fixed field of
\mathrm{Fix}_{G}([f])
for any
[f]\in C
. Hence,

is real if and only if
\iota\in\mathrm{Fix}_{G}([f])
. Let

be a lift of

as a function

. If
\iota[f]=[f]
, then there exists

with

and

. By applying

, so that

.

It follows that
[f]
corresponds to a Tate twist, because the element
\delta_{\sigma}+\iota\delta_{\sigma}\in X^{\star}(L(A))
is independant of

, and corresponds to the simple object

.
∎

6.
Archimedean fiber functors

A classical argument of Serre shows that there is no cohomology theory for varieties over finite fields which takes values in real vector spaces. But there is no known obstruction for having a cohomology theory taking values in
\mathrm{Isoc}_{\mathbb{R}}
as defined in
Definition

3.26
. The goal of this section is to exploit the existence of an archimedean fiber functor over the category of Lefschetz motives. It plays the role of a cohomology theory for abelian varieties, but without a cycle class map.

Definition 6.1
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, the object
\mathbb{R}(n)
of
\mathrm{Isoc}_{\mathbb{R}}
will be the graded vector space
\mathbb{C}
in degree
-2n
, with the complex conjugation as antilinear automorphism. For simplicity, we will denote
\mathbb{R}(0)
by
\mathbb{R}
.

Remark 6.2
.

The object denoted
\mathbb{R}
in the previous definition is the unit object of
\mathrm{Isoc}_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Definition 6.3
.

Let
(V,\sigma)
be an object of
\mathrm{Isoc}_{\mathbb{R}}
, pure of weight
n
, and

\eta:V\otimes V\to\mathbb{R}(n)

be symmetric if
n
is even, and antisymmetric if
n
is odd. We say that
\eta
is positive definite if for each
v\in V\setminus\{0\}
,

\eta(v,\sigma v)\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}.

In other words, we say that
\eta
is positive definite if the Hermitian form

\hat{\eta}:(v,w)\mapsto\eta(v,\sigma w),

is positive definite.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
\mathrm{Isoc}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\text{even}}
be the category of real isocrystals of even weight. The functor mapping an even weight isocrystal
(V,\sigma)
to the set of fixed points
V^{\sigma}=\{v\in V\mid\sigma(v)=v\}
is a fiber functor

\omega:\mathrm{Isoc}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\text{even}}\to\mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbb{R}}.

Moreover, for any quadratic form
\eta
on
V\in\mathrm{Isoc}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\text{even}}
,
\eta
is positive definite in the sense of
Definition

6.3
if and only if the quadratic form
\omega(\eta)
is positive definite on the
\mathbb{R}
-vector space
\omega(V)
.

Proof.

For for an object
(V,\sigma)
of even weight, we have
\sigma^{2}=\id_{V}
. Hence
\sigma
is a descent datum on
V
, and
V^{\sigma=\id}
is its underlying real vector space. Hence, the functor
\omega
is faithful and monoidal, so that it is a fiber functor. The last part of the proposition follows from the fact that a real quadratic form
q
on a real vector space
W
is positive definite if and only if the associated hermitian form
u,v\mapsto q(u,\overline{v})
is positive definite on
W_{\mathbb{C}}
.
∎

The following proposition is the combination of results of
[
16
]
and
[
7
]
.

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
k
be a finite field. There exists a
\otimes
-functor

R_{\infty}:\mathcal{LM}\mathrm{ot}(k)\to\mathrm{Isoc}_{\mathbb{R}},

which is compatible with weights, and such that for any abelian variety
A
with ample line bundle
\mathcal{L}
, bilinear form

R_{\infty}(\eta_{\mathcal{L}}):R_{\infty}(\mathfrak{P}^{n}(A))\otimes R_{\infty}(\mathfrak{P}^{n}(A))\to\mathbb{R}(-n),

is positive definite.
Moreover, for any
p
-adic field
K
with residue field
k
, and any

N\in\mathcal{LM}\mathrm{ot}^{\mathrm{CM}}(\mathcal{O}_{K})

with a bilinear form
\eta
such that
R_{H,\iota}(q)
is a polarisation of Hodge structure for some
\iota:K\to\mathbb{C}
, then
R_{\infty}(R_{\mathfrak{p}}(\eta))
is positive definite.

Remark 6.6
.

In the following proof, we make use of results of
[
16
]
in the form they were in the v2 version on arXiv, that we will cite as
[
15
]
.

Proof.

According to Milne
[
15
, Prop 1.5]
, there is a unique polarization
\Pi
on
\mathcal{LM}\mathrm{ot}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
which is compatible with the reduction functor

R_{p}:\mathcal{LM}\mathrm{ot}^{\mathrm{CM}}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})\to\mathcal{LM}\mathrm{ot}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}),

for a fixed
p
-adic prime of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.
That is, if one has a
\mathrm{CM}
Lefschetz motive
M
over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, with a bilinear form
\eta
such that
R_{H}(\eta)
is a polarization of Hodge structure, then
R_{p}(\eta)
is a polarization in the sense of
\Pi
. By
[
7
, Chap 2, Thm 5.20]
, one gets a tensor functor compatible with the Tate–triple structures

R_{\infty}:\mathcal{LM}\mathrm{ot}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})\to\mathrm{Isoc}_{\mathbb{R}}

such that
\eta
on
M\in\mathcal{LM}\mathrm{ot}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
is positive in the sense of
\Pi
if and only if
R_{\infty}(\eta)
is positive definite in the sense of
Definition

6.3
. More concretely
[
7
, §5]
,

being compatible with the Tate structures, means it is compatible with weights and sends the Tate object to the Tate object.

One gets a functor

R_{\infty}:\mathcal{LM}\mathrm{ot}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\to\mathrm{Isoc}_{\mathbb{R}}

by composing with the extension of scalars
\mathcal{LM}\mathrm{ot}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\to\mathcal{LM}\mathrm{ot}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
.

According to
[
15
, Prop 1.5]
, geometric Weil forms are positive for
\Pi
. We deduce from
[
15
, Remark 4.8]
that
\eta_{\mathcal{L}}
is positive for
\Pi
for any abelian variety
A
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with ample line bundle
\mathcal{L}
. Hence
R_{\infty}(\eta_{\mathcal{L}})
is positive definite in the sense of
Definition

6.3
.

Moreover, consider
N\in\mathcal{LM}\mathrm{ot}^{\mathrm{CM}}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
where
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is the ring of integers of a
p
-adic field, endowed with a bilinear form
\eta
. Assume that
R_{H,\iota}(\eta)
is a polarization for some
\iota:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
. By definition,
N
is a direct summand of the motive of an abelian variety
A
of
\mathrm{CM}
-type. Such abelian varieties are defined over number fields, that is there exists a number field
D\subset K^{\prime}
and an abelian variety
B
over

such that

A\times_{K}K^{\prime}\cong B\times_{D}K^{\prime},

where

is a finite extension of

.

Moreover, we can assume that the geometric endomorphisms of

are defined over

, so that

is also of
\mathrm{CM}
-type. The motive

is a direct summand of
\mathfrak{h}^{\star}(A)

. We get a motive

over

, by defining it as the image of

. Hence we have

Moreover, the bilinear form

on

gives rise to a bilinear form

on

, and we have
R_{H,\sigma}(\eta^{\prime})=R_{H,\iota}(\eta)
, which is a polarization of Hodge structure. Hence

is positive for

. But
R_{p}(\eta)_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}=R_{p}(\eta^{\prime})_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
, so that

is positive definite.
∎

Corollary 6.7
.

Let
k
be a finite field, and
\mathcal{LM}\mathrm{ot}(k)^{\text{even}}
be the category of Lefschetz motives of even weight. The composition
\omega_{\infty}=\omega\circ R_{\infty}
, where

is the functor from

, is a fiber functor

\omega_{\infty}:\mathcal{LM}\mathrm{ot}(k)^{\text{even}}\to\mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbb{R}}.

As the functor

is compatible with weights, it sends motives of even weights to objects of

of even weight. Hence

defines a real valued fiber functor on Lefschetz motives of even weights.
∎

Remark 6.8
.

In
[
17
, Prop 4.4.3.1]
, Saavedra proves a statement which is similar to the previous corollary for Grothendieck motives over finite fields, but conditionnaly on the standard conjectures.

The following theorem is a purely tannakian statement, which will be useful to compare quadratic forms.

Theorem 6.9
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a tannakian category over
\mathbb{R}
, endowed with two fiber functors

\omega,\omega^{\prime}:\mathcal{T}\to\mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbb{R}}.

Let

be an object of
\mathcal{T}
, endowed with two quadratic forms

\eta,\eta^{\prime}:{\mathrm{Sym}}^{2}M\to\mathds{1}.

Assume that
\omega(\eta)
and
\omega(\eta^{\prime})
are positive definite. Then
\omega^{\prime}(\eta)
and
\omega^{\prime}(\eta^{\prime})
have the same signature.

Proof.

As
\omega(\eta)
and
\omega(\eta^{\prime})
are positive definite, they are nondegenerate. This means that the maps

r_{\eta},r_{\eta^{\prime}}:M\to M^{*}

induced by
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
become isomorphisms after applying
\omega
, hence they are already isomorphisms. Hence
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
are nondegenerate. Let
u
be the unique endomorphism of
M
such that

\eta(x,uy)=\eta(ux,y)=\eta^{\prime}(x,y).

More precisely, this means that the following diagram is commutative
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\otimes M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\otimes M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\otimes M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\id\otimes u}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces u\otimes\id}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta}

Concretely,
u=r_{\eta}^{-1}\circ r_{\eta^{\prime}}
.

Let
\chi_{u}(X)
denote the characteristic polynomial of
u
(
Definition

3.21
). By
3.22
, we have that

\chi_{\omega(u)}(X)=\chi_{u}(X)=\chi_{\omega^{\prime}(u)}(X).

We have that
\omega(\eta)
and
\omega(\eta^{\prime})
are positive definite, and that

\omega(\eta^{\prime})(x,y)=\omega(\eta)(x,\omega(u)y)

for all
x,y\in\omega(M)
. We deduce that
\omega(u)
is a positive autoadjoint endomorphism of
\omega(M)
with respect to the scalar product
\omega(\eta)
. Hence the eigenvalues of
\omega(u)
are positive, so that the roots of
\chi_{u}(X)
are positive.

Hence the roots of
\chi_{\omega^{\prime}(u)}(X)
are also positive, and we deduce by
Lemma

that
\omega^{\prime}(\eta)
and
\omega^{\prime}(\eta^{\prime})
have the same signature. Indeed,

\omega(\eta^{\prime})(x,y)=\omega^{\prime}(\eta)(x,\omega^{\prime}(u)y)

for all
x,y\in\omega^{\prime}(M)
,
\omega^{\prime}(u)
is autoadjoint with respect to
\omega^{\prime}(\eta)
and has positive eigenvalues.
∎

Lemma 6.10
.

Let
V
be a
\mathbb{R}
-vector space,
\eta
be a nondegenerate quadratic form, and
u
an endomorphism of
V
which is autoadjoint with respect to
\eta
and has positive eigenvalues. Then
\eta
has the same signature as

x,y\mapsto\eta(x,uy).

Proof.

Consider
u_{\lambda}=\lambda u+(1-\lambda)\id_{V}
for
\lambda\in[0,1]
, and

\eta_{\lambda}:x,y\mapsto\eta(x,u_{\lambda}y).

u_{\lambda}
is autoadjoint with respect to
\eta
, so that
\eta_{\lambda}
is a symmetric bilinear form. Because the eigenvalues of
u
are positive, so are those of
u_{\lambda}
. Hence
u_{\lambda}
is invertible, and
\eta_{\lambda}
is nondegenerate.

Hence,
\lambda\mapsto\eta_{\lambda}
is a continuous path between
\eta
and
\eta_{1}
in the space of nondegenerate symmetric bilinear forms. As the space of nondegenerate symmetric bilinear forms with a fixed signature
(s_{+},s_{-})
is open for any
(s_{+},s_{-})
, it must also be closed, as its complement is a finite union of open subsets. Hence the signature of
\eta_{\lambda}
is constant, and
\eta_{0}
has the same signature as
\eta_{1}
.
∎

, which we will be using in the proof of
Theorem

5.2
.

Corollary 6.11
.

Let
N\in\mathcal{LM}\mathrm{ot}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
be a Lefschetz motive of weight
2n
, with

\mathrm{rk}(N)=\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}(R_{Z}(N(n))).

Let

\eta,\eta^{\prime}:N(n)\otimes N(n)\to\mathds{1}

be two quadratic forms, such that
R_{\infty}(\eta)
and
R_{\infty}(\eta^{\prime})
are positive definite. Then
R_{Z}(\eta)
and
R_{Z}(\eta^{\prime})
have the same signature.

Proof.

As
N
has weight
2n
,
N(n)
has weight
0
. As
\mathrm{rk}(N)=\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}(R_{Z}(N(n)))
, we have that
R_{Z}
defines a fiber functor

R_{Z}:\langle N(n)\rangle^{\otimes}\to\mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbb{Q}}.

By
6.7
, we have that
R_{\infty}
also induces a fiber functor

on

. We conclude by

.
∎

Remark 6.12
.

The assumption
\mathrm{rk}(N)=\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}(R_{Z}(N(n)))
does not force
N
to be
\mathds{1}(-n)^{\oplus\rho}
. This would be the case if
N
was an homological motive in
\mathcal{M}\mathrm{ot}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Indeed, for a Lefschetz motive to be isomorphic to
\mathds{1}(-n)^{\oplus\rho}
, it has to contain only Lefschetz classes. In other words, the assumption
\mathrm{rk}(N)=\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}(R_{Z}(N(n)))
says that
N
contains a lot of algebraic classes, but not necessarily Lefschetz classes.

7.
Decomposition of abelian motives

The proof of the standard conjecture of Hodge type for abelian fourfolds in
[
2
]
involves a decomposition result for abelian motives. The same decomposition was used in
[
5
]
to prove cases of the
\mathrm{hom=num}
conjecture. It is quite explicit in the sense that one can write down the algebraic cycles giving rise to the direct sum. In this article, the decompositions are finer and less explicit, they are abstracted through tannakian methods.

Definition 7.1
.

We say that a motive
M\in\mathcal{LM}\mathrm{ot}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is odd liftable if there exist an odd integer
m
, a
p
-adic field
K
with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, residue field
\mathbb{F}_{q^{a}}
, maximal ideal
\mathfrak{p}
, and a motive
N\in\mathcal{LM}\mathrm{ot}^{\mathrm{CM}}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
whose reduction satisfies
R_{\mathfrak{p}}(N)\cong M_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{a}}}^{\oplus m}
.

For a simple abelian variety
A/\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we consider its multiplicity
m
, its Frobenius rank
r
and its center
F
as in
4.5
. The notion of enriched eigenvalues is defined in
Definition

4.6
.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
A
be a simple abelian variety over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, of dimension
g
, Frobenius rank
r
and odd multiplicity
m
. Assume that

(1)

the geometric endomorphisms of
A
are defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,

(2)

r\geqslant\frac{g}{m}-1
,

(3)

there exists a totally real field
D
disjoint from
F
such that
A
has
\mathrm{CM}
by
L=D\cdot F
, i.e.
\deg D=m
and
D\cdot F\hookrightarrow\End(A)
.

Let
M
be a simple Lefschetz motive
M
of weight
2n
with enriched eigenvalue
\lambda\not=[q]^{n}
and
\rho(\lambda)=q^{n}
.
Then
M
has rank
2
and is odd liftable.

Proof.

Let
A
be such an abelian variety,
F
be the center of
\End^{0}(A)
,
\tilde{F}
be a Galois closure and

\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{g/m},\pi_{g/m+1}\dots,\pi_{2g/m}

be the eigenvalues of Frobenius in
\tilde{L}
, such that
\overline{\pi}_{i}=\pi_{g/m+i}
. Let us first describe the group
H
of
\lambda\in\mathrm{Eig}(A)
such that
\rho(\lambda)=q^{n}
for some
n
. Consider the
\mathrm{Gal}(\tilde{F}/\mathbb{Q})
-equivariant exact sequence

0\rightarrow H\rightarrow\mathrm{Eig}(A)\overset{\overline{\rho}}{\rightarrow}\tilde{F}^{\times}/q.

By definition,
r
is the rank of the image of
\overline{\rho}
. Moreover,
\mathrm{Eig}(A)
has rank
g/m+1
.

Hence
H
has rank
\frac{g}{m}+1-r
, which is at most
2
by the numerical assumption. We always have
[q]\in H
, hence
H
either has rank
1
or
2
. Moreover,
\mathrm{Eig}(A)
being torsion free, so is
H
. Assume that
H
has rank
1
, then any
\lambda
with
\rho(\lambda)=q^{n}
for some
n
is itself
[q]^{n}
. In this case, there is no motive
M
satisfying the assumptions.

Otherwise, there is
\mu=[q]^{\alpha_{0}}[\pi_{1}]^{\alpha_{1}}\dots[\pi_{g/m}]^{\alpha_{g/m}}
such that
H=[q]^{\mathbb{Z}}\cdot\mu^{\mathbb{Z}}
. Up to changing
\mu
, we will assume
\alpha_{0}=0
. Consider
\overline{H}=H/[q]
, which is generated by
\overline{\mu}
, and is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
. The group
\mathrm{Gal}(\tilde{F}/\mathbb{Q})
acts on
\overline{H}
, by a sign as
\mathrm{GL}_{1}(\mathbb{Z})=\{\pm 1\}
. Moreover,
\mathrm{Gal}(\tilde{F}/\mathbb{Q})
permutes transitively
\{\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{2g/m}\}
. Hence for any
i
,
\alpha_{i}=\pm\alpha_{1}
and up to changing
\pi_{i}
with
\overline{\pi}_{i}
, we can assume
\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{1}=\alpha
. Hence
\mu=([\pi_{1}]\dots[\pi_{g/m}])^{\alpha}
and

H=[q]^{\mathbb{Z}}\cdot([\pi_{1}]\dots[\pi_{g/m}])^{\alpha\mathbb{Z}}.

As
\mathrm{Gal}(\tilde{F}/\mathbb{Q})
acts on
\overline{H}
by a sign, we get that for each
\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}(\tilde{F}/\mathbb{Q})
,

\sigma\mu=\begin{cases}\mu\;\;\;\text{ or,}\\
[q]^{\alpha g/m}\mu^{-1}.\end{cases}

Now, let
M
be a motive as in the statement, with enriched eigenvalue
\lambda
. As
\rho(\lambda)=q^{n}
for some
n
, we have
\lambda\in H
, so that

\lambda=[q]^{i}([\pi_{1}]\dots[\pi_{g/m}])^{\alpha j}.

As
\rho(\lambda)=q^{n}
, we have by
4.16
that
\dim(M)
is the cardinal of the orbit of
\lambda
under
\mathrm{Gal}(\tilde{F}/\mathbb{Q})
. As
\lambda\not=[q]^{n}
, we see that
j\not=0
. Hence the orbit of
\lambda
is

\{[q]^{i}\mu^{j},[q]^{i+\frac{jg}{m}}\mu^{-j}\},

and
M
has rank
2
.

Let
D
be a totally real field of degree
m
disjoint from
F
, with a map
D\cdot F=L\to\End^{0}(A)
. Let
\tilde{L}
be a Galois closure of
L
and
G=\mathrm{Gal}(\tilde{L}/\mathbb{Q})
. Now, by
[
19
, Théorème 2]
there exists a
p
-adic field
K
with rings of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, residue field
\mathbb{F}_{q^{a}}
, and an abelian scheme
\mathcal{A}
over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
with complex multiplication by
L
such that
\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is isogenous to
A_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{a}}}
, moreover we can assume that the geometric endomorphisms of
\mathcal{A}
are defined over
K
. According to
[
14
, Prop 2.5]
and
3.23
, the simple objects of
\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle
are parametrized by
G
-orbits in

X^{\star}(L^{\Psi})=\frac{\{f:\Psi\to\mathbb{Z}\}}{\{f\;|\;f=\iota f\text{ and }\sum_{\psi\in\Psi}f(\psi)=0\}},

where
\Psi
is the Galois orbit of
\mathrm{CM}
-types associated to
\mathcal{A}_{K}
. Let us fix an embedding
\tilde{L}\to\overline{K}
, then by
[
14
, Proposition 2.2]
, we have that
\Psi\cong\Hom(L,\tilde{L})
. To a character
\chi:L\to\tilde{L}
of
L
, one can associate its characteristic function

\displaystyle f_{\chi}:\varphi\mapsto\begin{cases}1\text{ if }\chi=\varphi\\
0\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

As
D
and
F
are disjoint,
D\cdot F=D\otimes F
, hence there is a
\mathrm{Gal}(\tilde{L}/\mathbb{Q})
-equivariant identification

\Hom(L,\tilde{L})\cong\Hom(D,\tilde{L})\times\Hom(F,\tilde{L}).

Moreover, one has
\Hom(F,\tilde{L})\cong\{\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{g/m},\overline{\pi_{1}},\dots,\overline{\pi_{g/m}}\}
. Fix a embedding
\lambda\in\Hom(D,\tilde{L})
, and see
D
as a subfield of
\tilde{L}
. Consider the characters
\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{g/m}
of
L
which correspond to
(\lambda,\pi_{i})
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant g/m
. One has
\chi_{i}(\pi)=\pi_{i}
. By
[
14
, Theorem 5.4]
, reduction modulo
\mathfrak{p}
of the motive associated to

f=\alpha jf_{\chi_{1}}+\dots+\alpha jf_{\chi_{g/m}}

has
\mu^{j}=[\chi_{1}(\pi)]^{\alpha j}\dots[\chi_{g/m}(\pi)]^{\alpha j}
as enriched eigenvalue. Moreover,

\mathrm{Fix}_{\mathrm{Gal}(\tilde{L}/\mathbb{Q})}([f])=\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{Gal}(\tilde{L}/\mathbb{Q})}(\{\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{g/m}\}),

because
f=\sigma\cdot f+g
with
\iota g=g
if and only if

\{\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{g/m}\}=\{\sigma\cdot\chi_{1},\dots,\sigma\cdot\chi_{g/m}\}.

Moreover,

\mathrm{Stab}_{G}(\{\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{g/m}\})

has index
2
in
\mathrm{Gal}(\tilde{L}/D)
, which has index
m
in
G=\mathrm{Gal}(\tilde{L}/\mathbb{Q})
. Denote by
S
the
\mathrm{Gal}(\tilde{L}/\mathbb{Q})
-orbit of
[f]
and
N
the simple object associated, we conclude that
\dim(N)=\#S=2m
. Moreover,
R_{\mathfrak{p}}N(-i)
has
[q]^{i}\mu^{j}
as enriched eigenvalue, and for dimension reasons we have

R_{\mathfrak{p}}N(-i)\cong M_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{a}}}^{m}.

Hence
M
is odd liftable.
∎

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
A
be a simple abelian variety over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, of dimension
g
, Frobenius rank
r
and odd multiplicity
m
. Assume that
r\geqslant\frac{g}{m}-1
and that there exists a totally real field
D
disjoint from
F
such that
A
has
\mathrm{CM}
by
D\cdot F
. Then for any simple Lefschetz motive
M\in\langle A\rangle^{\otimes}
pure of weight
2n
with enriched eigenvalue
\lambda
, we have
3
possibilities

(1)

either
\lambda=[q]^{n}
, in which case
M=\mathds{1}(-n)

(2)

or
\lambda\not=[q]^{n}
and
\rho(\lambda)=q^{n}
, in which case
M
has rank
2
and is odd liftable.

(3)

or
\rho(\lambda)\not=q^{n}
, in which case
R_{Z}(M(n))=0
.

Proof.

If
\lambda=[q]^{n}
, then
M=\mathds{1}(-n)
by
4.14
, because
\mathds{1}(-n)
also has
[q]^{n}
as enriched eigenvalue. If
\lambda\not=[q]^{n}
, and
\rho(\lambda)=q^{n}
then
M
has rank
2
and is odd liftable by
Theorem

7.2
.

It remain to treat the case where
\rho(\lambda)\not=q^{n}
. According to
Remark

3.36
, algebraic classes in
M(n)
are homologically trivial in this case, hence numerically trivial. We conclude that
R_{Z}(M(n))=0
.
∎

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
A
be a simple abelian variety over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, of dimension
g
, Frobenius rank
r
and odd multiplicity
m
. Let
P
be a Lefschetz motive in
\langle A\rangle^{\otimes}
, pure of weight
2n
. Assume that
r\geqslant\frac{g}{m}-1
and that there exists a totally real field
D
disjoint from
F
such that
A
has
\mathrm{CM}
by
D\cdot F
. Then there are submotives
L,(E_{i})_{1\leqslant i\leqslant d}
and
T
of
P
such that

(1)

P=L\oplus E_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus E_{d}\oplus T

(2)

L
is isotypical of type
\mathds{1}(-n)

(3)

E_{i}
is isotypical of type
M_{i}
, where
M_{i}
has rank
2
and is odd liftable,

(4)

R_{Z}(T(n))=0
.

Moreover, the motives
M_{i}
are nonisomorphic and
M_{i}(n)^{\star}\cong M_{i}(n)
.

Proof.

Let
P
a motive in
\langle A\rangle^{\otimes}
, which is pure of weight
2n
. As
\langle A\rangle^{\otimes}
is a semisimple tannakian category, we have the following isotypical descomposition

\bigoplus_{i=0}^{s}M_{i}\otimes\Hom(M_{i},P)\xrightarrow{\sim}P,

where
M_{0},\dots,M_{s}
are nonisomorphic simple objects. As
P
is pure of weight
2n
,
M_{i}
is also pure of weight
2n
. Let
\lambda_{i}
be the enriched eigenvalue associated to
M_{i}
via
4.14
, it is also of weight
2n
.

According to
7.3
, there are three possibilities for each
M_{i}
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}

\displaystyle=[q]^{n}

\displaystyle\rho(\lambda_{1})

\displaystyle=\dots=\rho(\lambda_{d})=q^{n}

\displaystyle\rho(\lambda_{i})

\displaystyle\not=q^{n}\text{ for }i>d.

Hence the following subobjects of
P
will satisfy the desired properties

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=M_{0}\otimes\Hom(M_{1},P)

\displaystyle E_{i}

\displaystyle=M_{i}\otimes\Hom(M_{i},P)\text{ for }1\leqslant i\leqslant d

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{i>d}M_{i}\otimes\Hom(M_{i},P).

By definition, the
M_{i}
are not isomorphic. Moreover, for all

we have that
M_{i}(n)^{*}\cong M_{i}(n)
.
∎

, with odd multiplicity

.
Then for any Lefschetz motive

in
\langle A\rangle^{\otimes}
, which is pure of weight

, and any quadratic form

on

A,P

be as in the statement.
7.4
is about Lefschetz motive over

, hence we first need to find models for

R_{Z}({\eta_{0}}_{|L(n)})
and
R_{Z}({\eta_{0}}_{|E_{i}(n)})
are positive definite.

On the other hand,
R_{\infty}(\eta_{0})=R_{\infty}(\eta)
is positive definite. Consequently,
R_{\infty}({\eta_{0}}_{|L})
and
R_{\infty}({\eta_{0}}_{|E_{i}})
are positive definite. By
Lemma

8.2
and
Lemma

8.3
we conclude that
R_{Z}({\eta_{0}}_{|L})
and
R_{Z}({\eta_{0}}_{|E_{i}})
are positive definite as well.
∎

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
R_{\infty}
be the functor from
6.5
and
L
be a Lefschetz motive which is isotypical of type
\mathds{1}(-n)
with
n\in\mathbb{N}
. For any quadratic form
\eta
on
L(n)
, if
R_{\infty}(\eta)
is positive definite, then
R_{Z}(\eta)
is positive definite.

Proof.

As
L
is isotypical of type
\mathds{1}(-n)
,
L(n)
is isotypical of type
\mathds{1}
. That is,
L(n)
can be seen as a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space. Hence
R_{\infty}(L(n))=R_{Z}(L(n))
, and
R_{Z}(\eta)=R_{\infty}(\eta)
is positive definite.
∎

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
R_{\infty}
be the functor from
6.5
, and
E
be a Lefschetz motive of weight
2n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, which is isotypical of type
M
, where
M
is odd liftable of rank
2
. For any quadratic form
\eta
on
E(n)
, if
R_{\infty}(\eta)
is positive definite, then
R_{Z}(\eta)
is positive definite.

Proof.

Let
E
and
M
and
\eta
as in the statement. If
R_{Z}(M(n))=0
, then
R_{Z}(E(n))=0
and there is nothing to prove. As
M
is simple,
\End(M)
is a field. Moreover,

\End(M)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}=\End(M_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}),

and
M_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
is not simple (as the simple objects are characters) so that
\dim(\End(M))>1
. This shows that
R_{Z}(M(n))
cannot have dimension
1
, because the field
\End(M)
is acting on it. Hence,
\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}R_{Z}(M(n))=2
and
M
is odd liftable. Hence by
8.4
there exists a quadratic form
\eta^{\prime}
on
M_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{a}}}(n)
for some
a
such that
R_{Z}(\eta^{\prime})
and
R_{\infty}(\eta^{\prime})
are positive definite. Let us fix an isomorphism

E\cong M^{\oplus d}.

We have two quadratic forms
\eta
and
(\eta^{\prime})^{\oplus d}
on
E_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{a}}}(n)
, which are sent to positive definite forms by
R_{\infty}
. Then by
6.11
, we get that
R_{Z}(\eta)
and
R_{Z}(\eta^{\prime})^{\oplus d}
have the same signature. As
R_{Z}(\eta^{\prime})
is positive definite, we deduce that
R_{Z}(\eta)
is also positive definite, which concludes the proof.
∎

Proposition 8.4
.

Let
R_{\infty}
be the functor from
6.5
and
M
be an irreducible motive of rank
2
, of even weights in
\mathcal{LM}\mathrm{ot}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that

(i)

\dim R_{Z}(M)=2

(ii)

M
is odd liftable.

Then there exists a quadratic form
\eta
on
M_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{a}}}
, for some
a
, such that
R_{Z}(\eta)
and
R_{\infty}(\eta)
are positive definite.

Proof.

Let
M
as in the statement, and because
M
is odd liftable (
Definition

7.1
) we have a
p
-adic field
K
with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, residue field
\mathbb{F}_{q^{a}}
and
N\in\mathcal{LM}\mathrm{ot}^{\mathrm{CM}}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
with
\mathrm{CM}
by
L
such that
R_{\mathfrak{p}}(N)\cong M_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{a}}}^{\oplus m}
with
m
odd. Up to taking a finite extension, we can assume that
N
is geometrically simple. By
Lemma

4.17
, there exists a quadratic form
\eta_{0}
on
N
such that
R_{H}(\eta_{0})
is a polarization of Hodge structure and for every
z\in L
, the adjoint of
z
with respect to
\eta_{0}
is
\overline{z}
. Hence by
6.5
,
R_{\infty}(\eta_{0,\mathfrak{p}})
is positive definite.

Let
f:M\to N_{\mathfrak{p}}
be any nonzero map, and consider
\eta
the quadratic form on
M
obtained by restraining
\eta_{0,\mathfrak{p}}
to
M
via
f
. Then
R_{\infty}(\eta)
is the restriction of
R_{\infty}(\eta_{0,\mathfrak{p}})
, hence it is positive definite.

The quadratic forms
\eta^{\oplus m}
and
\eta_{0,\mathfrak{p}}
on
M^{\oplus m}\cong N_{\mathfrak{p}}
, are sent to positive definite forms by
R_{\infty}
. Hence
R_{Z}(\eta)^{\oplus m}
and
R_{Z}(\eta_{0,\mathfrak{p}})
have the same signature by
6.11
. We have
3
possibilities for
R_{Z}(\eta)
:

1)

If it has signature
(2,0)
then
R_{Z}(\eta_{0,\mathfrak{p}})
has signature
(2m,0)
,

2)

if it has signature
(1,1)
then
R_{Z}(\eta_{0,\mathfrak{p}})
has signature
(m,m)
,

3)

if it has signature
(0,2)
then
R_{Z}(\eta_{0,\mathfrak{p}})
has signature
(0,2m)
.

Let
(s_{+},s_{-})
be the signature of
R_{Z}(\eta_{0,\mathfrak{p}})
, according to
Theorem

3.34
we have
4|s_{-}
. As
m
is assumed to be odd, the only possibility is that
R_{Z}(\eta)
is positive definite, which concludes the proof. ∎

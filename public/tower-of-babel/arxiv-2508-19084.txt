Tunneling spectroscopy of the spinon-Kondo effect in one-dimensional Mott insulators
Rodrigo G. Pereira, Bruno F. Marquez, Karen Hallberg, Tim Bauer, Reinhold Egger

Abstract
We study the tunneling density of states (TDOS) in one-dimensional Mott insulators at energies below the charge gap. By employing nonlinear Luttinger liquid theory and density-matrix renormalization group (DMRG) simulations, we predict that in the presence of a magnetic impurity at the boundary, characteristic Fermi-edge singularity features can appear at subgap energies in the TDOS near the boundary. In contrast to the Kondo effect in a metal, these resonances are strongly asymmetric and of power-law form. The power-law exponent is universal and determined by the spinon-Kondo effect.



Introduction.—
It is well known that a spin-
1/2
magnetic impurity in a metal causes a Kondo resonance
pinned to the Fermi level, with the ground-state impurity spin fully screened by conduction electrons
Hewson 1993
.
The Kondo effect implies a robust zero-energy peak in the TDOS near the impurity that
can be observed in scanning tunneling spectroscopy (STS) or in transport experiments.
We consider here the TDOS near a magnetic impurity in a
Mott insulator
.
Since the state after a tunneling event must accomodate an extra electron or hole, which entails
both the spin and charge degrees of freedom,
a zero-energy Kondo peak is not expected because of the finite charge gap
\Delta
.
Can a magnetic impurity nonetheless induce subgap (energy
|E|<\Delta
) resonance features in a Mott insulator?
This question is of key importance for recent STS experiments conducted on two-dimensional spin liquid candidates, depositing spin-
1/2

Co atoms on 1T-TaSe
2

Chen et al. 2022
or spin-
3/2
molecules on 1T-NbSe
2

Zhang et al. 2024
.
Theoretical work
Law and Lee 2017
;
He et al. 2018
suggests that these materials may host a quantum spin liquid phase characterized by a spinon Fermi surface, with experimental evidence supporting this scenario
Ribak et al. 2017
;
Gomilšek et al. 2019
;
Murayama et al. 2020
;
Chen et al. 2025
. In such gapless quantum spin liquids, parton mean-field theories
Khaliullin and Fulde 1995
;
Kolezhuk et al. 2006
;
Florens et al. 2006
;
Ribeiro and Lee 2011
;
Zheng and Kargarian 2024
predict a spinon-Kondo effect, where spinons—rather than electrons—screen magnetic impurities. The observation of subgap features by STS
Chen et al. 2022
;
Zhang et al. 2024
has been attributed to a combination of this spinon-Kondo effect and interactions between spinons and charge degrees of freedom (“holons” or “doublons”), which together bind the fractional excitations into a localized electron-like mode
He and Lee 2022
.

We study a one-dimensional (1D) variant of this problem as sketched in Fig.
1
(a). The 1D case allows for nonperturbative analytical results by means of nonlinear Luttinger liquid theory
Imambekov et al. 2012
;
Schmidt et al. 2010
;
Pereira et al. 2012
;
Essler et al. 2015
, which we compare to numerical DMRG simulation
White 1992
results. In this manner, the uncontrolled approximations
introduced by parton mean-field theories in higher dimensions are avoided altogether. Moreover,
the 1D scenario can be experimentally realized, e.g., in nanographene chains
Jacob et al. 2021
;
Zhao et al. 2025
;
Su et al. 2025
, molecular chains
Sun et al. 2025
, and van der Waals materials
Park et al. 2024
, where spin-charge separation naturally emerges at low energy scales
Gogolin et al. 1998
. In fact, the existence of a Kondo screening cloud formed by spinons around a boundary impurity spin is well established in spin-
1/2
Heisenberg chains
Eggert and Affleck 1992
;
Wang 1997
;
Furusaki and Hikihara 1998
;
Laflorencie et al. 2008
;
Kattel et al. 2024
. An intuitive picture for how the properties of spinons in spin chains stem from a highly entangled ground state was put forward recently
Kulka et al. 2025
.
In order to address how spinons manifest in the TDOS and the corresponding STS experiments, however, one needs to include the charge degrees of freedom.

To that end, we consider a half-filled, particle-hole symmetric Hubbard chain with sites
j=1,\ldots,L
, hopping parameter
t_{0}
, and on-site interaction
U>0
. The chain is connected at the left boundary by a coupling
t^{\prime}
to an Anderson impurity with interaction
U_{d}>0
at site
j=0
; see Fig.
1
(a). The Hamiltonian reads

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-t_{0}\sum_{\sigma}\sum_{j=1}^{L-1}(c^{\dagger}_{j\sigma}c^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{j+1,\sigma}+\text{h.c.})

(1)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle U\sum_{j=1}^{L}\left(n_{j\uparrow}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(n_{j\downarrow}-\frac{1}{2}\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle t^{\prime}\sum_{\sigma}(c^{\dagger}_{0\sigma}c^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{1\sigma}+\text{h.c.})+U_{d}\left(n_{0\uparrow}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(n_{0\downarrow}-\frac{1}{2}\right),

with
n_{j\sigma}=c^{\dagger}_{j\sigma}c_{j\sigma}
and electron annihilation operators
c_{j\sigma}
for site
j
and spin projection
\sigma
. For arbitrary
U/t_{0}
, the charge gap in the bulk of an infinitely long Hubbard chain follows from the Bethe ansatz
Essler et al. 2005
;
Pereira et al. 2012
:

\Delta=\frac{8t_{0}^{2}}{U}\int_{1}^{\infty}dw\,\frac{\sqrt{w^{2}-1}}{\sinh(2\pi wt_{0}/U)}.

(2)

We study the site- and energy-dependent TDOS (
\hbar=1
),

\rho_{\sigma}(j,E)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{2\pi}\,e^{iEt}\langle\{c^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{j\sigma}(t),c^{\dagger}_{j\sigma}(0)\}\rangle,

(3)

with the anticommutator
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
. For the particle-hole symmetric model (
1
),

\rho_{\sigma}(j,-E)=\rho_{\sigma}(j,E)
, and because of spin SU(2) invariance,

\rho_{\uparrow}(j,E)=\rho_{\downarrow}(j,E)
. In particular, we define the impurity TDOS,
\rho_{\rm i}(E)=\rho_{\sigma}(j=0,E)
, and the boundary TDOS,
\rho_{\rm b}(E)=\rho_{\sigma}(j=1,E)
.
For
(t_{0},t^{\prime})\ll(U,U_{d})
,
the low-energy physics of Eq. (
1
) is captured by the effective spin model
Kattel et al. 2024

H_{\rm spin}=J_{\rm imp}\mathbf{S}_{0}\cdot\mathbf{S}_{1}+J\sum_{j\geq 1}\mathbf{S}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{S}_{j+1},

(4)

where
\mathbf{S}_{j}
are spin-
1/2
operators and the exchange couplings are
J=4t_{0}^{2}/U
and

J_{\rm imp}=8t^{\prime\,2}/(U+U_{d})
.
We focus on the parameter regime
U/t_{0}\gg 1
with
U_{d}\lesssim U
, including the relevant charge modes not contained in Eq. (
4
).

Main results.—
Our key results are summarized in Fig.
1
(b), which shows the schematic energy dependence of the boundary TDOS. For above-gap energies, we obtain a linear increase,
\rho_{\rm b}(E)\sim(|E|-\Delta)\Theta(|E|-\Delta)
, with the Heaviside step function
\Theta
.
Without the magnetic impurity, the boundary TDOS (
3
) vanishes for
|E|<\Delta
.
We predict characteristic subgap resonances in the presence of the impurity.
Instead of a zero-energy Kondo peak, we find strongly asymmetric Fermi-edge singularity peaks for
\Delta_{d}<|E|<\Delta
,

\rho_{\rm i,b}(E)\sim(|E|-\Delta_{d})^{-\alpha}\Theta(|E|-\Delta_{d}).

(5)

A rough estimate gives the threshold energy
\Delta_{d}\approx U_{d}/2
, but
more detailed estimates for
\Delta_{d}
are provided below. The singularity exhibits a universal exponent
\alpha=1/2
within an energy window
|E|-\Delta_{d}\ll T_{K}
, where
T_{K}
is the Kondo temperature from the spinon-Kondo effect. The subgap resonance features (
5
), see Fig.
1
(b), are observed in our DMRG simulations, and we find that the exponent extracted from numerical results for finite chains approaches the analytical prediction as we increase the coupling to the magnetic impurity.

Effective field theory.—
We first present analytical arguments based
on a nonlinear Luttinger liquid theory that describes threshold singularities of 1D Mott insulators
Pereira et al. 2012
;
Essler et al. 2015
.
We start with
t^{\prime}=0
. Within bosonization,

c_{j\sigma}
is expressed in terms of left- and right-moving charge and spin (
\nu=c,s
) boson fields

\varphi_{\nu,L/R}(x)

Gogolin et al. 1998
. Here we take the continuum limit,
ja\to x
, with
a
denoting the lattice spacing.
We impose open boundary conditions by folding bosons,
\varphi_{c,L}(x)=\varphi_{c,R}(-x)+\sqrt{\pi}
and
\varphi_{s,L}(x)=\varphi_{s,R}(-x)
, such that one
can work with chiral (say, right-moving) bosons,
\varphi_{\nu}(x)\equiv\varphi_{\nu,R}(x)
,
defined on the full line
-\infty<x<\infty
. They obey the algebra
[\varphi_{\nu}(x),\varphi_{\nu^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})]=\frac{i}{2}{\delta_{\nu\nu^{\prime}}\text{sgn}(x-x^{\prime})}
.
Using the chiral currents
J_{\nu}^{z}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi}}\partial_{x}\varphi_{\nu}
and

J_{\nu}^{\pm}=\frac{1}{2\pi}e^{\pm i\sqrt{4\pi}\varphi_{\nu}}
, we obtain the spin-charge-separated
low-energy Hamiltonian
Gogolin et al. 1998
;
Essler et al. 2005

H=\sum_{\nu=c,s}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\left(\frac{2\pi v_{\nu}}{3}\mathbf{J}_{\nu}^{2}-2\pi\lambda_{\nu}v_{\nu}\mathbf{J}_{\nu}(x)\cdot{\mathbf{J}}_{\nu}(-x)\right)
. For
|U|\ll t_{0}
, one finds
v_{c,s}=2t_{0}\pm\frac{U}{2\pi}
and
\lambda_{c,s}=\mp\frac{U}{2\pi t_{0}}

Essler and Tsvelik 2002
. For
U>0
, the spin coupling
\lambda_{s}
is marginally irrelevant, whereas
\lambda_{c}
is marginally relevant and causes a charge gap
\Delta\propto e^{-1/|\lambda_{c}|}
, consistent with Eq. (
2
).

The picture of gapped charge and gapless spin sectors remains qualitatively correct as the interaction strength increases. In the limit
U/t_{0}\gg 1
, the spin sector is described by a Heisenberg spin chain and charge excitations (holons) correspond to gapped spinless fermions
Penc et al. 1995
;
Matveev et al. 2007
.
Following Ref.
Pereira et al. 2012
, we describe holons as mobile charge impurities in
high-energy bands, restricting the Fock space to contain at most one holon.
Nonlinear Luttinger liquid theory
Pereira et al. 2012
;
Essler et al. 2015
then expresses the (say, spin-
\uparrow
) electron operator as
c^{\dagger}_{j,\uparrow}\sim d^{\dagger}_{c}(x)e^{i\sqrt{\pi}\varphi_{s}(x)}
, where

d^{\dagger}_{c}
creates a holon in the upper Hubbard band and the vertex operator
e^{i\sqrt{\pi}\varphi_{s}}
creates a low-energy spinon. In contrast with fermionic spinons in parton mean-field theories
He and Lee 2022
, here the vertex operator associated with a chiral spin-1/2 excitation has scaling dimension
1/4
and obeys semionic statistics
Pham et al. 2000
.
The dynamics is now captured by an effective mobile holon impurity model,

H_{\rm MIM}=\frac{v_{s}}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx(\partial_{x}\varphi_{s})^{2}+\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\,d^{\dagger}_{c}\left(\Delta-\frac{\partial_{x}^{2}}{2m_{c}}\right)d_{c}^{\phantom{\dagger}},

(6)

where
m_{c}\sim 1/(2t_{0})
is the holon mass and we impose the boundary condition
d_{c}(0)=0
.
Time reversal symmetry, which takes
\varphi_{s}\mapsto-\varphi_{s}
, implies that the leading bulk spin-charge coupling term is
\sim d^{\dagger}_{c}d^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{c}(\partial_{x}\varphi_{s})^{2}
. However, this irrelevant coupling neither generates contributions for
|E|<\Delta
nor modifies the leading energy dependence of the TDOS for
E\gtrsim\Delta
.
One can thus compute the TDOS directly using Eq. (
6
), where
\rho_{\rm b}(E)
follows by setting
d_{c}(a)\sim a\partial_{x}d_{c}(0)
. For
t^{\prime}=0
, we obtain
\rho_{\rm b}(E)\sim(|E|-\Delta)\,{\Theta(|E|-\Delta)},
see Fig.
1
(b).
A related calculation for the bulk of the chain (
j\gg 1
) instead gives
a step edge,
\rho_{\sigma}(j,E)\sim\Theta(|E|-\Delta)
, consistent with Ref.
Essler and Tsvelik 2002
.

Next, we add the impurity coupling
t^{\prime}\neq 0
, see Fig.
1
(a), and study the subgap regime
|E|<\Delta
. We assume for simplicity

J_{\rm imp}\ll U_{d}\ll U
, but comparison with DMRG results discussed below indicates that this assumption can be relaxed. In the low-energy subspace, the chain is at half-filling and the impurity site is singly occupied, see Eq. (
4
). Performing a Schrieffer-Wolff transformation for the model (
1
) to
lowest nontrivial order in
t^{\prime}/U\ll 1
while allowing for charge excitations
at the magnetic impurity site only, we arrive at the transformed low-energy Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{\rm eff}=U_{d}\left(\frac{1}{2}-2(S_{0}^{z})^{2}\right)+\frac{v_{s}}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\,(\partial_{x}\varphi_{s})^{2}

(7)

\displaystyle-2\pi v_{s}\lambda_{\parallel}S_{0}^{z}\partial_{x}\varphi_{s}(0)+\pi v_{s}\lambda_{\perp}\left(c^{\dagger}_{0\uparrow}c^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{0\downarrow}e^{-i\sqrt{4\pi}\varphi_{s}(0)}+\text{h.c.}\right),

with
S_{0}^{z}=(n_{0\uparrow}-n_{0\downarrow})/2
. The sector with an empty or doubly occupied impurity site (
S_{0}^{z}=0
) is separated from the sector with a singly occupied site (
S_{0}^{z}=\pm 1/2
) by the energy difference
U_{d}/2
.
The dimensionless Kondo couplings
\lambda_{\parallel,\perp}
are equal for the spin-isotropic model
(
1
),
\lambda_{\parallel}=\lambda_{\perp}=J_{\rm imp}/(\pi v_{s})
.
Moreover, the transformed electron operator at the boundary site
j=1
is
\tilde{c}_{1\sigma}=c_{1\sigma}+\frac{2t^{\prime}}{U+U_{d}}c_{0\sigma}
, up to higher orders in
t^{\prime}/U
. Boundary correlators such as
\langle\tilde{c}_{1\sigma}(t)\tilde{c}_{1\sigma}^{\dagger}(0)\rangle
therefore pick up the contribution
\left(\frac{2t^{\prime}}{U+U_{d}}\right)^{2}\langle c^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{0\sigma}(t)c^{\dagger}_{0\sigma}(0)\rangle
from the impurity site in the regime
t^{\prime}\ll U_{d},U
.

Momentarily neglecting the
\lambda_{\perp}
-term in Eq. (
7
),
we arrive at the standard Fermi-edge singularity problem
Gogolin et al. 1998
.
This is readily seen by applying the unitary transformation

W=\exp\left(-i\frac{4\gamma}{\sqrt{\pi}}S_{0}^{z}\varphi_{s}(0)\right)
,
which acts as
W^{\dagger}c_{0\sigma}W=c_{0\sigma}\,e^{-i\sigma\sqrt{4\pi}(\gamma/\pi)\varphi_{s}(0)}

and eliminates the
\lambda_{\parallel}
-term from Eq. (
7
) by choosing

\gamma=\frac{\pi^{3/2}}{2}\lambda_{\parallel}
. While
\lambda_{\parallel}\propto J_{\rm imp}
at weak coupling,
more generally
\gamma
corresponds to the exact scattering phase shift off the boundary since
W
is a boundary-condition-changing operator
Affleck and Ludwig 1994
. It is then straightforward to show

\rho_{\rm i}(E)\sim\frac{\Theta(|E|-\Delta_{d})}{(|E|-\Delta_{d})^{1-2(\gamma/\pi)^{2}}}.

(8)

At the same time, the boundary TDOS develops a subgap contribution,
\rho_{\rm b}(E)\sim\left(\frac{2t^{\prime}}{U+U_{d}}\right)^{2}\rho_{\rm i}(E)
. Here
\Delta_{d}\approx U_{d}/2
, but higher-order terms renormalize the value of
\Delta_{d}
.

Let us now include the
\lambda_{\perp}
term in Eq. (
7
). The unitary transformation
W
acts nontrivially on this term,

W^{\dagger}c^{\dagger}_{0\uparrow}c^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{0\downarrow}e^{-i\sqrt{4\pi}\varphi_{s}(0)}W=c^{\dagger}_{0\uparrow}c^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{0\downarrow}e^{-i\sqrt{4\pi}(1-2\gamma/\pi)\varphi_{s}(0)}
. Since
\gamma>0
, the vertex operator becomes more relevant.
In fact, within a perturbative RG approach restricted to the
(S_{0}^{z})^{2}=1/4
sector,
the Kondo couplings scale as

\frac{d\lambda_{\parallel}}{d\ell}=\lambda_{\perp}^{2}
and
\frac{d\lambda_{\perp}}{d\ell}=\lambda_{\perp}\lambda_{\parallel}
,
with
\lambda_{\perp}=\lambda_{\parallel}
in the SU(2)-symmetric case.
The strong-coupling fixed point corresponds to
\lambda_{\perp}=\lambda_{\parallel}\to\infty
with
phase shift
\gamma=\pi/2
. To verify this picture, we employ an argument similar to the Friedel sum rule in the bosonization approach to the X-ray edge problem
Gogolin et al. 1998
. We first recall that the spin-raising operator at the boundary corresponds to the chiral current
J_{s}^{+}(0)\sim e^{i\sqrt{4\pi}\varphi_{s}(0)}
. We denote the magnetization induced at the chain boundary as
S^{z}_{\rm b}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi}}\int_{-\epsilon}^{\epsilon}dx\,\partial_{x}\varphi_{s}(x)
,
where
\epsilon
is a short-distance scale and the prefactor is fixed by the relation

[S^{z}_{\rm b},J^{+}(0)]=J^{+}(0)
. Using
W^{\dagger}\partial_{x}\varphi_{s}(x)W=\partial_{x}\varphi_{s}(x)+\frac{4\gamma}{\sqrt{\pi}}S_{0}^{z}\delta(x)
,
we obtain
\langle S^{z}_{\rm b}\rangle=-\frac{2\gamma}{\pi}\langle S_{0}^{z}\rangle
. For
\gamma=\pi/2
,
we arrive at a strong-coupling Kondo singlet,
\langle S^{z}_{\rm b}+S^{z}_{0}\rangle=0
,
where a spinon has been removed from the chain in order to screen the impurity spin.
The exchange interaction in Eq. (
7
) thus leads to the spinon-Kondo effect.
Note that the transformed vertex operator then reduces to the identity. As a consequence,
Eq. (
8
) gives the correct result for the impurity TDOS in the strong-coupling limit. Inserting
\gamma=\pi/2
into Eq. (
8
), we arrive at Eq. (
5
) which holds close to the threshold,
0<|E|-\Delta_{d}\ll T_{K}
. For
J_{\rm imp}\ll J
, we have
T_{K}\sim e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{\frac{J_{\rm imp}}{J}}}

Laflorencie et al. 2008
;
Kattel et al. 2024
.
We note that higher-order terms in
\sim t^{\prime}/U
generate impurity contributions to the TDOS at sites
j>1
. As a consequence, the subgap TDOS weight is expected to decay exponentially with the distance from the boundary on a length scale
\sim v_{s}/\Delta
.

Strong-coupling fixed point.—
The square-root TDOS singularity (
5
) can be rationalized by the strong-coupling picture illustrated in Fig.
2
.
In addition to the energy cost
U_{d}/2
for adding an electron to the impurity site, the tunneling process entails breaking the Kondo singlet which costs an energy
\delta E_{s}\sim T_{K}
.
Furthermore, charge fluctuations due to
t^{\prime}\neq 0
also renormalize
\Delta_{d}
by an energy
\delta E_{c}
, resulting in
\Delta_{d}=U_{d}/2+\delta E_{s}+\delta E_{c}
. To estimate
\delta E_{c}

for
U\gg t_{0}
, we recall that holons are described by a tight-binding model of noninteracting spinless fermions
Penc et al. 1995
;
Matveev et al. 2007
.
For
U_{d}<U
, this model predicts a holon bound state localized near the boundary with energy
U_{d}/2+\delta E_{c}
,
where
\delta E_{c}\approx-4(t^{\prime})^{2}/(U-U_{d})<0
for
t^{\prime}\ll U-U_{d}
(see End Matter).
For
t^{\prime}\ll t_{0}
, we then expect
\Delta_{d}<U_{d}/2
because
\delta E_{s}\ll|\delta E_{c}|
. However, for larger
t^{\prime}
, perturbative expressions for

J_{\rm imp}
and
T_{K}
break down and
\delta E_{s}>|\delta E_{c}|
becomes possible, resulting in
\Delta_{d}>U_{d}/2
. In any case, near the strong-coupling fixed point, the excitation can be described by
c_{0\uparrow}^{\dagger}\sim d^{\dagger}_{\rm bs}e^{i\sqrt{\pi}\varphi_{s}(0)}
, where
d^{\dagger}_{\rm bs}
occupies the holon bound state and the vertex operator injects a spinon back into the chain, see Fig.
2
. Since the vertex operator has scaling dimension 1/4, its time correlations decay
\propto 1/\sqrt{t}
, and a Fourier transform gives the
1/\sqrt{|E|-\Delta_{d}}
singularity in Eq. (
5
). Importantly,
e^{\pm i\sqrt{\pi}\varphi_{s}}
is a nonlocal operator, which is why it does not appear in previous discussions of the Kondo effect in spin chains
Laflorencie et al. 2008
. However, it enters in the single-electron spectral function in combination with the operator
d^{\dagger}_{\rm bs}
acting in the charge sector. In a sense, the TDOS therefore probes the “local spinon spectral function” of a Mott insulator.

DMRG results.—
As independent approach, we have performed DMRG simulations to compute the TDOS for the model (
1
). The method is described in detail
in Ref.
Hallberg 2006
. Setting
t_{0}=1
, we focus on a Hubbard chain of length
L=23
with
U=10
, for which Eq. (
2
) gives
\Delta=3.27
, and fix
U_{d}=3
at the impurity site. To obtain smooth curves, we replace
\delta
-function peaks in the finite-chain TDOS by Lorentzians with broadening
\eta\sim 1/L
. Typical numerical results for the TDOS at sites
j=0,1,2
are shown in Fig.
3
. Clearly, the subgap feature is most pronounced in the impurity TDOS, but it also appears at the chain sites with a spectral weight that decays with distance from the boundary. Moreover, the TDOS is consistent with a linear energy dependence for
E\gtrsim\Delta
as predicted by field theory.
To quantitatively test the spinon-Kondo effect, we then analyze the subgap peak in
\rho_{\rm i}(E)
, taking into account broadening in the numerical data.
A convolution of the power law in Eq. (
5
) with a Lorentzian yields the fitting function (see End Matter)

f(E)=A\text{ Im}[(\Delta_{d}-E-i\eta)^{-\alpha}],

(9)

where
A
is a nonuniversal prefactor. To extract
\alpha
, we should in principle fit the energy dependence for
|E-\Delta_{d}|\ll T_{K}
. However, for small
t^{\prime}
, we may have
T_{K}\ll\eta
, such that the broadening obscures the threshold singularity. Equivalently, since
\eta\sim 1/L
, for
t^{\prime}\ll t_{0}
, the chain length is smaller than the size of the Kondo screening cloud
Laflorencie et al. 2008
and the RG flow to the strong-coupling point is cut off by finite-size effects. We then
expect an effective phase shift
\gamma<\pi/2
, resulting in a larger power-law exponent than the predicted value
\alpha=1/2
, see Eqs. (
5
) and (
8
). This consideration should be accounted for in STS experiments on relatively short chains
Jacob et al. 2021
;
Zhao et al. 2025
;
Su et al. 2025
.
For larger values of
t^{\prime}
, however,
T_{K}
increases and one thus expects the Kondo screening cloud to shrink. We therefore expect that
\alpha
approaches the predicted value

\alpha=1/2
as one increases
t^{\prime}
.
As shown in Fig.
4
(a), we find excellent agreement between DMRG data and Eq. (
9
). From the fits, we extract the values of
\Delta_{d}
and
\alpha
. First, we observe energy shifts
\Delta_{d}>U/2
for
t^{\prime}/t_{0}\geq 0.6
, where the line shape becomes more asymmetric with increasing
t^{\prime}
. Second, the
t^{\prime}
-dependence of
\alpha
is shown in Fig.
4
(b). Indeed, the numerical estimates for
\alpha
approach
\alpha=1/2

Discussion.—
The spinon-Kondo effect gives rise to a universal power-law singularity in the subgap TDOS of a 1D Mott insulator, different from a conventional Lorentzian Kondo resonance of width
T_{K}
around zero energy. In Ref.
He and Lee 2022
, a Lorentzian peak was found within a parton mean-field approach for the spinon spectral function of a U(1) quantum spin liquid in 2D. However, Ref.
He and Lee 2022
studied the regime
U_{d}>U
not discussed here. Beyond parton mean-field theory, 2D spinon Fermi surface states are strongly coupled non-Fermi liquid states
Ribeiro and Lee 2011
;
Lee 2009
;
Metlitski and Sachdev 2010
. We speculate that TDOS singularities for
U_{d}<U
could be used to probe their anomalous scaling dimensions
in analogy to the 1D spinons considered here.

Next we note that the TDOS obeys the sum rule
\int_{0}^{\infty}dE\,\rho_{\sigma}(j,E)=\frac{1}{2}
at half filling. In the regime where the subgap feature is well separated from bulk contributions, we measure the weight of the subgap peak as function of the distance from the impurity by computing the integral
I_{j}=\int_{0}^{\Delta}dE\,\rho_{\sigma}(j,E)
. As shown in Fig.
A1
, our numerical results indicate that
I_{j}
decays exponentially with
j
, confirming that the subgap feature is associated with a boundary bound state.

Finally, we note that for
j\geq 2
, the TDOS exhibits oscillations as a function of energy. In particular, for
j=2
in Fig.
3
, the TDOS is suppressed at one minimum with energy
E>\Delta
, but we find that the number of minima of
\rho_{\sigma}(j,E)
increases with
j

U=U_{d}=0
, where they can be traced back to the
\sim\sin(kja)
dependence of the electron wave function near the boundary, with the momentum
k
being governed by the energy of the electron injected into the chain. In the interacting case, we expect a similar behavior due to the wave function of holon scattering states near the boundary. More precisely, the TDOS involves nontrivial matrix elements between the exact eigenstates of the Hubbard model, but analytical expressions are not available for analyzing the resulting energy dependence in detail.

Fukaya A ∞ A_{\infty} -structure near infinity and the categorical formal completion
Yuan Gao

Abstract
. For a stopped Liouville manifold arising from a Liouville sector, we construct a symplectic analogue of the formal neighborhood of the stop on the level of Fukaya categories. This geometric construction is performed via Floer-theoretic methods by allowing wrappings in the negative direction. On the other hand, inspired by homological mirror symmetry for pairs, where the mirror is the formal neighborhood of a divisor in an ambient projective variety, there is a different approach by taking a ‘categorical formal completion’ introduced by Efimov. Our main results establishes equivalence of these two approaches, confirms computability of this new type of Floer theory by categorical and algebraic means, and indicates contributions from and to computations in homological mirror symmetry.



1.
Introduction

The goal of this paper is to construct a Fukaya
A_{\infty}
-categorical structure ‘at infinity’ for a
stopped Liouville manifold

(\bar{X},F_{0})

[
10
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
19
]

which arises from a corresponding
Liouville sector

X
.
This structure should be interpreted as a type of Fukaya categorical structure on the
‘formal neighborhood’
of the Liouville hypersurface
F_{0}\subset\partial_{\infty}\bar{X}
at infinity (where
F
is the Liouville completion of the domain
F_{0}
),
thus providing a symplectic analogue of the notion of the formal neighborhood of a divisor in an ambient variety in algebraic geometry.
The starting point of this study is wrapped Floer theory for Liouville sectors,
which is introduced and extensively studied in
[
10
]
,
and earlier in
[
19
]
for a certain class of stopped Liouville manifolds, or equivalently Liouville pairs.
In
[
11
]
, the theory is further extended to all
stopped Liouville sectors
,
defining the partially wrapped Fukaya category
\mathcal{W}(X,\mathfrak{g})
for an arbitrary stopped Liouville sector
(X,\mathfrak{g})
, where
\mathfrak{g}\subset\partial_{\infty}X
is a closed subset,
with objects being exact cylindrical Lagrangian submanifolds of
\partial X
disjoint from
\mathfrak{g}
at infinity,
and morphisms being Floer cochains after isotoping Lagrangians such that their boundaries at infinity are positively isotoped in
\partial_{\infty}X\setminus\mathfrak{g}

(such isotopies are called
positive wrappings
).

For our study, we shall be mainly interested in the case of a stopped Liouville manifold.
The goal of this paper is to construct a new Fukaya-type
A_{\infty}
-category using a variant of Floer theory,
which takes into account both
positive wrappings
not passing the stop,
as well as arbitrary
negative wrappings
which necessarily pass through the stop.
This type of Floer theory is to be defined using global Floer data on the target manifold,
but will capture local information around the stop.
A rough conceptual picture of this construction can be described as follows:
wrapping passing through the stop defines a self-functor of the partially wrapped Fukaya category,
and we can consider the nilpotents under this functor,
which form an inverse system of categories by the orders of nilpotency,
where the connecting functors are given by applying ‘wrapping past the stop’.
Eventually, we would hope to obtain the desired category by taking the limit of this inverse system.
However, this naive description is lack of sufficient ingredients for being a rigorous construction,
because of the following two reasons: first, the limit might not exist or not have nice properties, even if it exists;
second, for an arbitrary stop, it is not always possible to characterize the process of ‘wrapping past the stop’ in a precise quantitative way making the self-functor well-defined.

However, when the geometry of
(\bar{X},\mathfrak{f})
is reasonably nice,
there is a nice and clean way of implementing the above idea to develop such a Floer theory,
to be spelt out in §
3.5
.
This type of Floer theory is well-behaved when the stop
\mathfrak{f}
admits a
ribbon
;
that is to say,
\mathfrak{f}
is itself or the core of a Liouville hypersurface (with boundary)
F_{0}\subset\partial_{\infty}\bar{X}
.
Stopped Liouville manifolds of this kind naturally come from
sutured Liouville manifolds

(\bar{X},F_{0})
or
Liouville pairs

(\bar{X},F)
where
F
is the Liouville completion of
F_{0}
,
and each of them corresponds to a unique (up to deformation) Liouville sector of the form
\bar{X}\setminus\mathcal{N}\mathrm{bd}F_{0}
and vice versa, as shown by
[
10
]
.
Their partially wrapped Fukaya categories are all quasi-equivalent by
[
11
]
,

\mathcal{W}(\bar{X}\setminus\mathcal{N}\mathrm{bd}F_{0})\cong\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})\cong\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},\mathfrak{f}).

From now on, we will mostly take the stop to be
F_{0}
,
but sometimes interchangeably use the core
\mathfrak{f}
whenever the relevant results are easier to state.

To see a picture of what this new type of Floer theory does on objects,
consider a pair of exact cylindrical Lagrangians
K,L
whose boundaries at infinity
\partial_{\infty}K,\partial_{\infty}L
are Legendrian submanifolds disjoint from
F_{0}
.
The morphism space for this Floer theory is a chain complex
NC^{*}_{F}(K,L)
which can be thought of as generated by all the characteristics on the contact manifold
\partial_{\infty}\bar{X}
from Legendrians
\partial_{\infty}K
to
\partial_{\infty}L
not passing the ‘forbidden’
F_{0}
at infinity,
as well as all the characteristics from
\partial_{\infty}L
to
\partial_{\infty}K
that are allowed to pass through
F
.
In §
3.5
,
we equip these chain complexes with
A_{\infty}
-structures,
which are suitably packaged into an
A_{\infty}
-category which we shall name as the
negatively wrapped Fukaya category

(1.1)

\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0}).

Although the definition of this category relies on the existence of the ribbon
F
not just a stop
\mathfrak{f}
,
we also reserve the notation
\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},\mathfrak{f})
for the same category,
or simply
\mathcal{N}(X)
where
X
is the Liouville sector corresponding to the sutured Liouville manifold
(\bar{X},F_{0})
,
so that

(1.2)

\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})=\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},\mathfrak{f})=\mathcal{N}(X).

In the case of a Lefschetz fibration
\pi:\bar{X}\to{\mathbb{C}}
, this construction is essentially equivalent to one sketched in
[
18
]
.
A schematic geometric picture to interpret this point is as follows.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that the critical values are contained in the unit disk.
This category
\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})
is constructed by perturbing Lagrangians by a Hamiltonian vector field in a way such that their projections to
{\mathbb{C}}
are perturbed slightly in the counterclockwise direction in a large disk containing the unit disk,
where the projections are already linear in a collar neighborhood of the boundary of the disk,
and perturbed in arbitrary amounts in the clockwise direction outside that disk.

By construction, the category
\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})
comes with a natural functor from the partially wrapped Fukaya category
\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})
,
but this functor does not shed much light on the structures of
\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})
.
Generally, some information is lost when passing from
\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})
to
\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})
via this functor.
However, one of the main goals of the paper is to show that
\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})
is recoverable from
\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})
in some nice situations.
A first necessary condition for the situation to be considered as ‘nice’ is that there should be a good sense of objects in
\bar{X}
that are
‘supported on
F
’
.
A sufficient condition is that the core
\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{c}_{F}
is
mostly Legendrian
in the sense of
[
11
]
,
in which case the objects in
\bar{X}
‘supported on
F
’ can be taken to be
Lagrangian linking disks
,
bounded by small Legendrian linking spheres at smooth Legendrian points of
\mathfrak{f}
.
In general when
\mathfrak{f}
is not necessarily mostly Legendrian,
the subcategory of linking disks should be replaced by the full subcategory
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}(F)
of
\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})
whose objects are those in the image of the cup functor

(1.3)

i_{F}:\mathcal{W}(F)\to\mathcal{W}(F\times T^{*}[0,1])\to\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0}),

which is defined to be the composition of the Künneth stabilization functor and the pushforward functor induced by the canonical inclusion
T^{*}[0,1]\xhookrightarrow{}(\bar{X},F_{0})
.
In this case, we also need a stop removal condition.
We say the Liouville manifold
F

satisfies stop removal for the Liouville pair
(\bar{X},F)
(with the corresponding sutured Liouville manifold
(\bar{X},F_{0})
),
if the functor

(1.4)

\iota_{*}:\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})/\tilde{\mathcal{D}}(F)\to\mathcal{W}(\bar{X})

induced by pushforward
\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})\to\mathcal{W}(\bar{X})
is a quasi-equivalence.
We say the Liouville manifold
F

satisfies stop removal
,
if it satisfies stop removal for
(\bar{X},F)
for any
\bar{X}
.
Changing from the assumption that
\mathfrak{f}
is mostly Legendrian to the one that
F
satisfies stop removal is a very mild generalization.
In addition, a properness condition seems natural as well:
we say that the stop
F_{0}
(or
\mathfrak{f}
) is a
full stop
if
\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})=\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},\mathfrak{f})
is proper.

The conditions stated above ensure good control over the behavior of Floer theory,
allowing us to compute the new category
\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})
from the partially wrapped Fukaya category
\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})
using a certain purely algebraic machinery.
The relevant algebraic structure is that of the
categorical formal completion of a category along a subcategory
, which was introduced in
[
7
]
in the case of dg categories.
The definition receives a straightforward generalization,
giving rise to the notion of the
formal completion

\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}
of an
A_{\infty}
-category
\mathcal{C}
along a subcategory
\mathcal{T}
of
\mathcal{C}
(or of
\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod}
, the category of left modules over
\mathcal{C}
, or its derived category
\tilde{D}(\mathcal{C})
);
for the precise definition, see Definition
2.4
in §
2.2
.
Notably, the categorical formal completion is a categorical analogue of the more familiar algebro-geometric concept of the formal neighborhood
\hat{Y}_{D}
of a closed subscheme
D
in a given ambient (Noetherian, seperated) scheme
Y
.
To present a shortcut definition here, consider the case where
\mathcal{T}
is a full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
.
Let

(1.5)

Z_{\mathcal{T}}=im(y^{l}\circ i:\mathcal{T}^{op}\to\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod})

be the image of
\mathcal{T}^{op}
under the Yoneda embedding
\mathcal{T}^{op}\xhookrightarrow{}\mathcal{C}^{op}\to\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod}
.
Tautologically, this defines an
A_{\infty}
-bimodule over
(Z_{\mathcal{T}},\mathcal{C}^{op})
, by

(1.6)

(\hat{i},y^{l})^{*}(\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod})_{\Delta},

where
\hat{i}:Z_{\mathcal{T}}\to\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod}
is the inclusion of the subcategory and
y^{l}:\mathcal{C}^{op}\to\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod}
is the Yoneda embedding.
Such a bimodule gives rise to a module-valued functor

(1.7)

F_{\mathcal{T}}:\mathcal{C}\to Z_{\mathcal{T}}\mathrm{\!-\!mod}=\mathrm{mod\!-\!}Z_{\mathcal{T}}^{op}

and we define
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}
to be the essential image of
F_{\mathcal{T}}
.
For
\mathcal{T}
a subcategory of
\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod}
, define
Z_{\mathcal{T}}=\mathcal{T}
and perform a similar construction.
The formal completion is independent of the subcategory up to split-generation (when embedded in
\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod}
),
so is always quasi-equivalent to
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathrm{Perf}\mathcal{T}}
(Corollary
2.7
).
By construction, the formal completion comes with a natural functor
\mathcal{C}\to\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}
,
such that if
\mathcal{T}
is a full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
,
the restriction of the functor to
\mathcal{T}
gives a fully faithful embedding
\mathcal{T}\xhookrightarrow{}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}
(Lemma
2.12
).
In this way,
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}
can be interpreted as a formal neighborhood of
\mathcal{T}
in
\mathcal{C}
.
See §
2
for more details about the definitions and properties of
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}
.

Having introduced the necessary ingredients introduced above, we can now state the first main result about the category
\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})
as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any Liouville pair
(\bar{X},F)
,
let
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}(F)
be the subcategory of
\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})
whose objects are images under the cup functor (
1.3
),
and let

(1.8)

\widehat{\mathcal{W}}_{F}:=\widehat{\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})}_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}(F)}

denote the categorical formal completion of
\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})
along the subcategory
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}(F)
.

(i)

There is a canonical
A_{\infty}
-functor

(1.9)

\Psi:\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})\to\widehat{\mathcal{W}}_{F}.

(ii)

Suppose
\bar{X}
and
F
are both non-degenerate,
and
F
satisfies stop removal and is a full stop.
Then the functor
\Psi
(
1.9
) is a quasi-equivalence.

It is straightforward to check the conditions are satisfied for a symplectic Landau-Ginzburg model,
yielding the following consequence.

Corollary 1.2
.

Suppose
\pi:\bar{X}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is a symplectic Landau-Ginzburg model on a non-degenerate Liouville manifold
\bar{X}
with compact critical locus and Weinstein fiber
F
.
Then there is a canonical quasi-equivalence

\Psi:\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\widehat{\mathcal{W}}_{F}.

In fact, the quasi-equivalence between
\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})
and
\widehat{\mathcal{W}}_{F}
does not rely on the seemingly artificial non-degeneracy condition.
This is odd yet interesting because it suggests that the relation between these categories has its own intrinsic meaning independent of generation of wrapped Fukaya categories.
However, if we want to drop the non-degeneracy condition, we shall need a stronger version of properness assumption than
F
being a full stop.

Definition 1.3
.

We say the contact manifold
\partial_{\infty}\bar{X}
has bounded Reeb dynamics relative to a Liouville hypersurface
F
, if for any two Legendrians
\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{1}
, and any
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
,
the set of Reeb chords from
\Lambda_{0}
to
\Lambda_{1}
which intersect
F_{0}
transversely at most
m
times is finite.

This condition can be thought of as an open-string version of a common condition on Reeb orbits.
A standard consequence of the above bounded Reeb dynamics assumption is that the wrapped Floer cohomology between any pair of Lagrangians is degree-wise finite dimensional.
For the category
\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})
, it implies the following:

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose
F
satisfies stop removal for the pair
(\bar{X},F)
,
and that the contact boundary-at-infinity
\partial_{\infty}\bar{X}
has bounded Reeb dynamics relative to
F
, and that
\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})
is (homologically) smooth.
Then there is a quasi-equivalence

(1.10)

\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\widehat{\mathcal{W}}_{F}.

Some comments about Theorem
1.4
are in order.
Note that Theorem
1.4
does not directly establish (
1.9
) to be a quasi-equivalence as in the statement of Theorem
1.1
.
However, there is indeed a canonical functor defined by other means, which under some further technical argument can be compared to the functor (
1.9
).
Regarding the hypotheses of these two theorems, the implication of Theorem
1.4
is slightly stronger than Theorem
1.1
as we do not assume
\bar{X}
is non-degenerate.
Of course, when
F
is non-degenerate, and if we further assume that
\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})
admits a finite collection of split-generators,
then the assumption that
F
satisfies stop removal will imply
\bar{X}
is non-degenerate.
In
[
19
]
, it is shown that under some assumptions, including Abouzaid’s generation criterion
[
3
]
, such a non-degenerate
F
satisfies stop removal.
It is expected that any non-degenerate
F
satisfies stop removal, but the answer to the converse remains unclear.
As for Theorem
1.4
, the assumption that
F
satisfies stop removal is sufficient for our conclusion to be drawn.
In fact, Theorem
1.4
only requires
F
to satisfy stop removal for the particular pair
(\bar{X},F)
.
Lastly, smoothness of
\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})
is also an essential condition.
There are geometric conditions leading to smoothness of
\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})
: for example by
[
11
]
Corollary 1.19 if
(\bar{X},F)
is
weakly Weinstein
then
\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})
is smooth (since we already assume that
\mathfrak{f}
has a ribbon
F
).
However, that assumption is unnecessarily strong for Theorem
1.4
to hold.

Theorem
1.1
is closely related to the main result of
[
9
]
,
which concerns about the
Rabinowitz Fukaya category

\mathcal{RW}(\bar{X})
of a Liouville manifold.
When the stop
F
is empty, the category
\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},\varnothing)
recovers the Rabinowitz Fukaya category

\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},\varnothing)=\mathcal{RW}(\bar{X}).

The corresponding closed-string theory is more familiarly known as the
Rabinowitz Floer homology
of (the boundary of the domain completing to
\bar{X}
in)
\bar{X}
,
RFH^{*}(\bar{X})
, introduced in
[
4
]
,
whose algebra structures have received recent attention
[
6
,
5
]
.
For a general stop
\mathfrak{f}
that admits a ribbon
F
,
while it is natural to expect a closed-string theory for the category
\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})
(which nonetheless does not carry a ring structure) should exist, it is not the purpose of this paper to describe that construction.
However in the open-string case,
\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})
is a version of Rabinowitz theory for Liouville sectors,
which is much less functorial compared to the partially wrapped Fukaya category.
In particular, there is no pushforward functor associated to an arbitrary inclusion of stopped Liouville
sectors
.
However, it is still possible to find a certain restricted type of inclusions of stopped Liouville
manifolds
of the form
(\bar{X},F_{0})\xhookrightarrow{}(\bar{X},F^{\prime}_{0})
for an inclusion of a Liouville subdomain
F^{\prime}_{0}\subset F_{0}
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose
F^{\prime}_{0}\subset F_{0}
is a Liouville subdomain.
Then there is a pushforward functor

(1.11)

\iota_{\sharp}:\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})\to\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F^{\prime}_{0})

which is identity on objects, and on morphism spaces is given by the inclusion of subcomplexes.
If
\iota:(\bar{X},F_{0})\to(\bar{X},F^{\prime}_{0})
is a stop removal inclusion,
the pushforward functor is a localization.

On the other hand, Theorem
1.1
provides an effective tool for computing the category
\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})
directly algebraically from
\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})
and its subcategory
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}(F)
,
where in many instances the latter categories can be explicitly described using geometry, as addressed in
[
11
]
.

1.1.
Functoriality and variants

We have seen that the category
\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})
is related to the partially wrapped Fukaya category
\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})
in two ways:
first, there is a natural
A_{\infty}
-functor

(1.12)

j:\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})\to\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})

which is realized by chain-level inclusions (not necessarily injective on cohomology though);
second, the formal completion of
\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})
along a subcategory is quasi-equivalent to
\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})
.
There is yet one more interesting relation, which can be established by introducing certain ‘intermediate’ variants of the category
\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})
.
The first variant among these is an
A_{\infty}
-category

(1.13)

\mathcal{N}_{>-2}(\bar{X},F_{0})

defined in a way similar to
\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})
,
but by only taking negative wrappings of Lagrangians passing through the stop at most once.
This category plays a special role as it is expected to capture information on the stop.
In more detailed terms, the morphism spaces in
\mathcal{N}_{>-2}(\bar{X},F_{0})
are quotient spaces of the morphism spaces in
\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})
by certain subspaces
(see §
3.7
for more details).
In
[
18
]
, Seidel gave a different yet equivalent construction of
\mathcal{N}_{>-2}(\bar{X},F_{0})
in the case of a Lefschetz fibration
\pi:\bar{X}\to{\mathbb{C}}
, in which case the morphism spaces in that category are cones of continuation maps,
and proved that it is the fiber at
\infty
of a noncommutative pencil,
and is in turn quasi-equivalent to the Fukaya category of the fiber
F
.
In particular, there is a natural
A_{\infty}
-functor

(1.14)

\pi:\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})\to\mathcal{N}_{>-2}(\bar{X},F_{0})

which can be interpreted as the ‘restriction-to-the-fiber’ functor.

More generally, in §
3.7
we construct for each negative integer
m\leq-1
an
A_{\infty}
-category

(1.15)

\mathcal{N}_{>m}(\bar{X},F_{0})

whose objects the same as those in
\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})
,
and whose morphisms are Floer cochains for Lagrangian isotopies in the negative direction passing through the stop
\mathfrak{f}
by at most
-m-1
times.
These can be thought of as ‘infinitesimal thickenings’ of the category
\mathcal{N}_{>-2}(\bar{X},F_{0})
.
These fit into a diagram of
A_{\infty}
-categories (see §
3.8
) related by
A_{\infty}
-functors

(1.16)

\pi_{m,m^{\prime}}:\mathcal{N}_{>m}(\bar{X},F_{0})\to\mathcal{N}_{>m^{\prime}}(\bar{X},F_{0})

for any
m,m^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq-1}
with
m\leq m^{\prime}
,
which are roughly speaking ‘restrictions’ from negative wrappings which pass through the stop at most
-m-1
times to those which pass through the stop at most
-m^{\prime}-1
times.
This diagram of
A_{\infty}
-categories will be a key ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

These categories
\mathcal{N}_{>m}(\bar{X},F_{0})
come in company with natural
A_{\infty}
-functors

(1.17)

j_{m}:\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})\to\mathcal{N}_{>m}(\bar{X},F_{0})

and

(1.18)

\pi_{m}:\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})\to\mathcal{N}_{>m}(\bar{X},F_{0}),

which are analogues of
j
and
\pi
.
Intuitively,
j_{m}
is an ‘inclusion’, by allowing Lagrangians to be negatively wrapped past the stop at most
-m-1
times,
and
\pi_{m}
is a ‘restriction’, by restricting to negative wrappings which pass through the stop at most
-m-1
times.
By consistently choosing Floer data defining the various Fukaya categories,
we may construct the functors so that

\{j_{m}:\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})\to\mathcal{N}_{>m}(\bar{X},F_{0})\}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq-1}}

and

\{\pi_{m}:\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})\to\mathcal{N}_{>m}(\bar{X},F_{0})\}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq-1}}

form strictly compatible diagrams of functors.

1.2.
Relation with homological mirror symmetry

The idea of the construction of the category
\mathcal{N}(X,F_{0})
is inspired by homological mirror symmetry for pairs,
as pointed out in
[
18
]
.
Because of the connection with the results of
[
9
]
,
it is also a warning to the reader that some expository parts in this subsection share a substantial amount of overlaps with those,
but are explored with a different focus.

Suppose the Liouville pair
(\bar{X},F)
is mirror to a pair
(Y,D)
,
where
Y
is a smooth projective variety and
D
is an anti-canonical ample divisor.
Homological mirror symmetry predicts a Morita equivalence between
\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})
and
Coh(Y)
,
and one between
\mathcal{W}(\bar{X})
and
Coh(Y\setminus D)
,
such that the two equivalences are compatible with the natural functors.
That is,

(1.19)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Perf}\mathcal{W}(\bar{X})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Coh(Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Coh(Y\setminus D).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

The full mirror symmetry picture should also describe
D
as the mirror of the fiber
F
,
together with a Morita equivalence between

and the Fukaya category of

compatible with other two equivalences in the diagram (

From this point of view,

can be interpreted as a

of a ‘compactification’ of

when the relevant Fukaya categories are concerned.

In algebraic geometry, one can take the formal neighborhood of

in

,
\hat{Y}_{D}
.
whose category of (algebraizable) perfect complexes

via the categorical formal completion.
Going to the symplectic side, one can perform a similar construction to the partially wrapped Fukaya category, which yields (

By removing the divisor

in

which is not well-defined classically but has a well-defined category of perfect complexes due to

]

It turns out that the formal punctured neighborhood only depends on the divisor complement

Moreover, the construction of the formal punctured neighborhood of infinity can be generalized in a purely categorical way (also due to

The above symplecto-geometric interpretation for the formal punctured neighborhood of infinity also suggests a natural candidate for the symplectic mirror of the formal neighborhood of

in

By Theorem

Under HMS, this provides a purely algebraic formula for the category

Corollary 1.6
.

Let

Suppose

2.1.
Algebraic preliminaries

Fix a ground commutative ring
{\mathbf{k}}

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a (graded)
A_{\infty}
-category over
{\mathbf{k}}
.
For objects

The dg category of left (resp. right) modules over
\mathcal{C}
is defined to be the dg category of
A_{\infty}
-functors
\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod}=\mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{C},\mathrm{Ch}_{{\mathbf{k}}})

The Yoneda functors

are fully faithful, where by a fully faithful
A_{\infty}
-functor, we always mean it is cohomologically fully faithful.
Given two
A_{\infty}
-categories
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
, the dg category of
A_{\infty}
-bimodules over
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})
(or
\mathcal{C}-\mathcal{D}
bimodule) is the dg category of
A_{\infty}
-bilinear functors
\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod\!-\!}\mathcal{D}=\mathrm{Fun}^{bi}(\mathcal{C}^{op}\times\mathcal{D},\mathrm{Ch}_{{\mathbf{k}}})
.

Given a left (resp. right)
\mathcal{C}
-module
M
, its
linear dual

M^{\vee}=\hom_{{\mathbf{k}}}(M,{\mathbf{k}})
is a right (resp. left)
\mathcal{C}
-module, with grading specified by

(2.1)

(M^{\vee})^{*}(X)=\hom_{{\mathbf{k}}}(M^{-*}(X),{\mathbf{k}})

for every
X\in\mathrm{ob\ }\mathcal{C}
.

The convenience of working with
A_{\infty}
-modules and bimodules is based on the following two standard facts (good references include
[
17
]

[
16
]

[
13
]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Composition with any quasi-isomorphism of
\mathcal{C}
-modules (or
\mathcal{C}-\mathcal{D}
bimodules) induces quasi-isomorphism of morphism complexes in the category
\mathrm{mod\!-\!}\mathcal{C}
(or
\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod\!-\!}\mathcal{D}
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Any quasi-isomprhism of
\mathcal{C}
-modules (or
\mathcal{C}-\mathcal{D}
bimodules) has a homotopy inverse.

For an
A_{\infty}
-category
\mathcal{C}
, its

of right
A_{\infty}
-modules

is simply the homotopy category of right modules:

D(\mathcal{C})=H^{0}(\mathrm{mod\!-\!}\mathcal{C}),

as any quasi-isomorphisms of
A_{\infty}
-modules automatically has a homotopy inverse.
Similarly, the derived category of left
A_{\infty}
-modules is denoted by

\tilde{D}(\mathcal{C})=H^{0}(\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod}).

In practice, one could be more interested in a smaller derived category, for example the bounded derived category, which is defined to be the homotopy category of twisted complexes

(2.2)

D^{b}(\mathcal{C})=H^{0}(\mathrm{Tw}\mathcal{C}),

as well as its Karoubi completion (or split closure)

(2.3)

All these derived categories are triangulated, induced from the natural pre-triangulated structures on
\mathrm{mod\!-\!}\mathcal{C},\mathrm{Tw}\mathcal{C}
and
\mathrm{Perf}\mathcal{C}
.
We refer the reader to
[
17
]
for their constructions and basic properties.

Given a
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})
-bimodule
P
and a
(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E})
-bimodule

(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})
-bimodule with underlying cochain spaces

P\otimes_{\mathcal{D}}Q(X,Y)=\bigoplus_{Z_{0},\ldots,Z_{s}}P(X,Z_{0})\otimes\mathcal{D}(Z_{0},\ldots,Z_{s})\otimes Q(Z_{s},Y).

For a right
\mathcal{C}
-module
M
, and a
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})
-bimodule
P
, their (convolution) tensor product

is a
\mathcal{D}
-module with

M\otimes_{\mathcal{C}}P(X,Y)=\bigoplus_{Z_{0},\ldots,Z_{s}}M(X,Z_{0})\otimes\mathcal{D}(Z_{0},\ldots,Z_{s})\otimes P(Z_{s},Y).

For a
\mathcal{C}-\mathcal{C}
bimodule
P
, the
Hochschild cohomology cochain complex
of
\mathcal{C}
with coefficients in
P
is defined to be

(2.4)

\mathrm{CC}^{*}(\mathcal{C},P)=\prod_{X_{0},\ldots,X_{d}\in\mathcal{C}}\hom_{{\mathbf{k}}}(\mathcal{C}(X_{0},\ldots,X_{d}),P(X_{0},X_{d})),

with the differential

(2.5)

\delta(F)^{k}(x_{k},\ldots,x_{1})=\sum\mu^{r,s}_{P}(x_{k},\ldots,F^{k-r-s}(\cdots),x_{s},\ldots,x_{1})-\sum F^{k-j+1}(x_{k},\ldots,\mu^{j}_{\mathcal{C}}(\cdots),x_{i},\ldots,x_{1}).

The morphism space in the dg category of
A_{\infty}
-functors
\mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})
can be interpreted as a Hochschild cohomology cochain complex of
\mathcal{C}
with coefficients in the pullback diagonal bimodule
(F,G)^{*}\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}

(2.6)

\mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})(F,G)=\mathrm{CC}^{*}(\mathcal{C},(F,G)^{*}\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}).

Given a left
\mathcal{C}
-module
M
and a right
\mathcal{D}
-module

, their

defines a
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})
-bimodule,
with underlying chain complex

(2.7)

the differential given by the tensor product differential,
and bimodule structure maps induced from the the module structure maps
\mu^{k}_{M}
and
\mu^{k}_{N}

For a right
\mathcal{C}
-module

and a right
\mathcal{D}
-module
N
, the
space of linear homomorphisms
from

to

defines a
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})
-bimodule,
with underlying chain complex

(2.8)

\hom_{{\mathbf{k}}}(M,N)(X,Y)=\hom_{{\mathbf{k}}}(M(X),N(Y)),X\in\mathrm{ob\ }\mathcal{C},Y\in\mathrm{ob\ }\mathcal{D},

the differential given by the
\hom_{{\mathbf{k}}}
-differential,
and bimodule structure maps induced from the module structure maps for
M
and

2.2.
The categorical formal completion

The categorical formal completion of a compactly generated (by a set of objects) enhanced triangulated category
along a full thick essentially small triangulated subcategory is introduced in
[
7
]
via the notion of the ‘derived double centralizer’,
which in the case where the relevant categories come from algebraic geometry recovers the ordinary formal completion of a scheme along a closed subscheme in a purely categorical way without (but related to) limits.
The definitions carry over to the
A_{\infty}
-setting in an almost straightforward way, and in fact can be greatly simplified thanks to Lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a small
A_{\infty}
-category.
Consider a small full subcategory
\mathcal{T}
of the derived category of right
\mathcal{C}
-modules
D(\mathcal{C})
, not necessarily triangulated.
We wish to consider the notion of the
categorical formal completion
of
\mathcal{C}
along
\mathcal{T}
.
Most of the definitions and basic properties in this subsection are essentially due to
[
7
]
,
but we also include some new discussions in the favorable case where
\mathcal{T}
is an actual subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
,
including Lemma
2.9
, Corollary
2.10
, Proposition
2.11
which will be useful for computing the morphism spaces in the categorical formal completion.
Also, we should point out that we are performing the categorical completions along
left
modules while
[
7
]
performs the construction along
right
modules.
This is a technical point, but will be of importance to our construction in Fukaya categories.

First, following
[
7
]
(noting the difference in left and right modules), we make the following definition.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a small
A_{\infty}
-category over
{\mathbf{k}}
,
and
\mathcal{T}
a full small subcategory of
\tilde{D}(\mathcal{C})
.
Define
Z_{\mathcal{T}}
to be the
A_{\infty}
-category with
\mathrm{ob\ }Z_{\mathcal{T}}=\mathrm{ob\ }\mathcal{T}
,
and with morphisms

(2.9)

Z_{\mathcal{T}}(M,N)=\hom_{\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod}}(M,N).

That is,
Z_{\mathcal{T}}
is the full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod}
with objects in
\mathcal{T}
.

If
\mathcal{T}
itself is a subcategory of
\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod}
, we define
Z_{\mathcal{T}}=\mathcal{T}
.

The subcategory
\mathcal{T}
naturally gives rise to a
(Z_{\mathcal{T}},\mathcal{C}^{op})
-bimodule
P_{\mathcal{T}}
as follows.
For any
(M,X)\in\mathrm{ob\ }Z_{\mathcal{T}}\times\mathrm{ob\ }\mathcal{C}^{op}
, put

(2.10)

P_{\mathcal{T}}(M,X)=M(X).

The
A_{\infty}
-bimodule structure comes from the dg-structure on
Z_{\mathcal{T}}
(as a full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod}
) and the
A_{\infty}
-structure on
\mathcal{C}
, compatible with Yoneda embedding.
More concretely, this bimodule structure is related to the diagonal bimodule of the category
\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod}
in the following way.
Let

(\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod}},y^{r})^{*}(\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod})_{\Delta}=(\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod})_{\Delta}(-,y^{r}(-))=\hom_{\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod}}(y^{r}(-),-)

be the graph
(\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod},\mathcal{C}^{op})
bimodule of the right Yoneda embedding
y^{l}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod}
.
If
\hat{i}:Z_{\mathcal{T}}\xhookrightarrow{}\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod}
denotes the inclusion of the subcategory
Z_{\mathcal{T}}
of
\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod}
,
then there is a canonical map of
(Z_{\mathcal{T}},\mathcal{C}^{op})
-bimodules

(2.11)

P_{\mathcal{T}}\to(\hat{i},\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{C}^{op}})^{*}(id_{\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod}},y^{l})^{*}(\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod})_{\Delta}=(\hat{i},y^{l})^{*}(\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod})_{\Delta},

which on chain complexes consists of the canonical maps

(2.12)

M(X)\to\hom_{\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod}}(Y^{l}_{X},M),

for
M\in\mathrm{ob\ }Z_{\mathcal{T}}\subset\mathrm{ob\ }\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod},X\in\mathcal{C}
.
Fixing
X
,
P_{\mathcal{T}}(-,X)
defines a left
Z_{\mathcal{T}}
-module, or equivalently a right
Z_{\mathcal{T}}^{op}
-module.
Phrased in a more functorial way, we may say that the
(Z_{\mathcal{T}},\mathcal{C}^{op})
-bimodule
P_{\mathcal{T}}
defines a module-valued functor

(2.13)

F_{\mathcal{T}}=F(P_{\mathcal{T}}):(\mathcal{C}^{op})^{op}=\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{mod\!-\!}Z_{\mathcal{T}}^{op}.

Definition 2.4
.

The formal completion
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}
of
\mathcal{C}
along
\mathcal{T}
is defined to be the
A_{\infty}
-category with objects
\mathrm{ob\ }\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}=\mathrm{ob\ }\mathcal{C}
,
and morphisms

(2.14)

\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,Y)=\hom_{\mathrm{mod\!-\!}Z_{\mathcal{T}}^{op}}(P_{\mathcal{T}}(-,X),P_{\mathcal{T}}(-,Y)).

That is,
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}
is the essential image of
\mathcal{C}
in
\mathrm{mod\!-\!}Z_{\mathcal{T}}^{op}
under the module-valued functor
F_{\mathcal{T}}
(
2.13
).

Immediately by definition, there is a natural pushforward functor

(2.15)

\iota_{\mathcal{T}}:\mathcal{C}\to\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}

which is identity on objects, and is essentially the functor
F_{\mathcal{T}}
.
On the level of morphism spaces, it may be written as

\mathcal{C}(X,Y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle F_{\mathcal{T}}}}{{\to}}\hom_{\mathrm{mod\!-\!}Z_{\mathcal{T}}^{op}}(P_{\mathcal{T}}(-,X),P_{\mathcal{T}}(-,Y))=\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,Y),

which sends a morphism
c\in\mathcal{C}(X,Y)=\mathcal{C}^{op}(Y,X)
to the canonical pre-module homomorphism
P_{\mathcal{T}}(-,X)\to P_{\mathcal{T}}(-,Y)
(as right modules over
\mathcal{C}^{op}
) by post-composing with

using the right module action by
\mathcal{C}^{op}
via the bimodule maps
\mu^{k,1}_{P_{\mathcal{T}}}
.
Higher order maps are defined in a similar way, giving rise to pre-module homomorphisms by post-composing with composable chains of morphisms in
\mathcal{C}
using the higher order bimodule structure maps.

Lemma 2.5
.

If
\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2}
are two subcategories of
\tilde{D}(\mathcal{C})
which split-generate each other,
then the two formal completions
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}_{1}},\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}_{2}}

Proof.

Define a
(Z_{\mathcal{T}_{2}},Z_{\mathcal{T}_{1}})
-bimodule
S

(Z_{\mathcal{T}_{1}}^{op},Z_{\mathcal{T}_{2}}^{op})

be the bimodules defined as (

) for the two subcategories
\mathcal{T}_{1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{2}
respectively.
On the level of underlying cochain spaces, the bimodule

Now let us consider the convolution tensor product

S\otimes_{Z_{\mathcal{T}_{1}}}P_{\mathcal{T}_{1}}

of the
(Z_{\mathcal{T}_{1}},\mathcal{C})
-bimodule
P_{\mathcal{T}_{1}}
with
S
.
By the definition of
S
in (

(2.18)

because for every

\mathcal{C}
-module

\mathcal{T}^{op}
,

\hat{i}\circ j=y^{l}|_{\mathcal{T}^{op}}:\mathcal{T}^{op}\xhookrightarrow{}\mathcal{C}^{op}(\text{or }\mathrm{Tw}\mathcal{C}^{op}\text{ or }\mathrm{Perf}\mathcal{C}^{op})\to\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod},

which factors through
i^{op}
.
There is a graph
(\mathcal{T}^{op},\mathcal{C}^{op})
-bimodule for
i^{op}:\mathcal{T}^{op}\to\mathcal{C}^{op}
defined by

(2.20)

Q_{\mathcal{T}}=(i^{op},\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{C}^{op}})^{*}\mathcal{C}^{op}_{\Delta},

The relation between the bimodule
P_{\mathcal{T}}
and the diagonal bimodule
(\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod})_{\Delta}
is much simpler when
\mathcal{T}
is a full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Suppose
\mathcal{T}
is a full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
.
Then the bimodule map
P_{\mathcal{T}}\to(\hat{i},y^{l})^{*}(\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod})_{\Delta})
(
2.11
) is a quasi-isomorphism.

Proof.

The canonical map
M(X)\to\hom_{\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod}}(Y^{l}_{X},M)
(
2.12
) is a precisely the chain map for the Yoneda functor if
M
is a left Yoneda module.
∎

Lemma 2.9
.

Suppose
\mathcal{T}
is a full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
.
There is a quasi-isomorphism of
(\mathcal{T}^{op},\mathcal{C}^{op})
-bimodules

(2.21)

(j,\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{C}})^{*}P_{\mathcal{T}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}Q_{\mathcal{T}}.

Proof.

By the quasi-isomorphism (
2.11
), there is a quasi-isomorphism of
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{C})
-bimodule

(j,\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{C}})^{*}P_{\mathcal{T}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}(y^{l}|_{\mathcal{T}^{op}},y^{l})^{*}(\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod})_{\Delta}.

Yoneda lemma asserts that there is a quasi-isomorphism of
(\mathcal{C}^{op},\mathcal{C}^{op})
-bimodules from
\mathcal{C}^{op}_{\Delta}
to the pullback

(y^{l},y^{l})^{*}(\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod})_{\Delta}.

By the definition (
2.20
), the lemma follows by choosing a homotopy inverse of
\mathcal{C}^{op}_{\Delta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}(y^{l},y^{l})^{*}(\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod})_{\Delta}
.
∎

Corollary 2.10
.

Suppose
\mathcal{T}
is a full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
.
For each
X\in\mathrm{ob\ }\mathcal{C}
, there is a quasi-isomorphism of left
\mathcal{T}^{op}
-modules, or equivalently right
\mathcal{T}
-modules

(2.22)

j^{*}P_{\mathcal{T}}(-,X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}i^{*}Y^{r}_{X},

where
i:\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{C}
is the inclusion,
j:\mathcal{T}^{op}\to Z_{\mathcal{T}}
is the image of the left Yoneda functor
\mathcal{T}^{op}\to\mathcal{C}^{op}\to\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod}
.

Proof.

A direct computation shows

Q_{\mathcal{T}}(-,X)=(i^{op},\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{C}^{op}})^{*}\mathcal{C}^{op}_{\Delta}(-,X)=\mathcal{C}^{op}_{\Delta}(i^{op}(-),X)=\mathcal{C}^{op}(X,i^{op}(-))=\mathcal{C}(i(-),X)=i^{*}Y^{r}_{X}.

∎

This has the following consequence on computing morphism spaces in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}
:

Proposition 2.11
.

Suppose
\mathcal{T}
is a full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
.
Then for every
X,Y\in\mathrm{ob\ }\mathcal{C}=\mathrm{ob\ }\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}
, there is a quasi-isomorphism of chain complexes

(2.23)

\hom_{\mathrm{mod\!-\!}\mathcal{T}}(i^{*}Y^{r}_{X},i^{*}Y^{r}_{Y})\to\hom_{\mathrm{mod\!-\!}Z_{\mathcal{T}}^{op}}(P_{\mathcal{T}}(-,X),P_{\mathcal{T}}(-,Y)).

Moreover, this quasi-isomorphism is compatible with the product structure on the dg categories
\mathrm{mod\!-\!}\mathcal{T}
and
\mathrm{mod\!-\!}Z_{\mathcal{T}}^{op}
.
By the interpretation of
\hom
in the functor category as Hochschild cohomology cochain complexes given in (
2.6
), this is a quasi-isomorphism

(2.24)

\mathrm{CC}^{*}(\mathcal{T}^{op},\hom_{{\mathbf{k}}}(i^{*}Y^{r}_{X},i^{*}Y^{r}_{Y}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathrm{CC}^{*}(Z_{\mathcal{T}},\hom_{{\mathbf{k}}}(P_{\mathcal{T}}(-,X),P_{\mathcal{T}}(-,Y))),

which is compatible with the Yoneda products on Hochschild cohomology cochain complexes.
In particular, this provides a (partially-defined) quasi-equivalence from a subcategory of
\mathrm{mod\!-\!}\mathcal{T}
having objects being pullbacks of Yoneda modules associated to objects of
\mathcal{C}
to
\mathrm{mod\!-\!}Z_{\mathcal{T}}^{op}
with vanishing higher order terms.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{T}
is a full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
, the functor
j:\mathcal{T}^{op}\to Z_{\mathcal{T}}
is a quasi-equivalence.
By Corollary
2.10
, there is a quasi-isomorphism of
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{T})
-bimodules

(2.25)

\hom_{{\mathbf{k}}}(i^{*}Y^{r}_{X},i^{*}Y^{r}_{Y})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\hom_{{\mathbf{k}}}(j^{*}P_{\mathcal{T}}(-,X),j^{*}P_{\mathcal{T}}(-,Y))=(j,j)^{*}\hom_{{\mathbf{k}}}(P_{\mathcal{T}}(-,X),P_{\mathcal{T}}(-,Y)).

Thus, the quasi-isomorphism (
2.24
) follows from the general property of Hochschild cohomology,
and it respects the Yoneda products.
∎

If
\mathcal{T}
is a subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
, there is also a functor

(2.26)

\kappa:\mathcal{T}\to\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}

which is the restriction of
\iota_{\mathcal{T}}
(
2.15
) to
\mathcal{T}
.
In this case, the following lemma is a direct consequence of a computation of morphism spaces, with the help of Yoneda lemma.

Lemma 2.12
.

Suppose
\mathcal{T}
is a full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
.
Then the functor
\kappa:\mathcal{T}\to\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}
is fully faithful.

Proof.

When
\mathcal{T}
is a subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
, the category
Z_{\mathcal{T}}
is the image of
\mathcal{T}^{op}\xhookrightarrow{}\mathcal{C}^{op}\to\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod}
,
and the natural functor
j:\mathcal{T}^{op}\to Z_{\mathcal{T}}
is a quasi-equivalence.
By Corollary
2.10
, the pullback
j^{*}P_{\mathcal{T}}(-,K)
is quasi-isomorphic to
i^{*}Y^{r}_{K}=Y^{r}_{K}
for
K\in\mathrm{ob\ }\mathcal{T}\subset\mathrm{ob\ }\mathcal{C}
.
Thus, there are canonical quasi-isomorphisms

(2.27)

\mathcal{C}(K,L)=\mathcal{T}(K,L)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\hom_{\mathrm{mod\!-\!}\mathcal{T}}(Y^{r}_{K},Y^{r}_{L})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\hom_{\mathrm{mod\!-\!}Z_{\mathcal{T}}^{op}}(P_{\mathcal{T}}(-,K),P_{\mathcal{T}}(-,L))

for every
K,L\in\mathrm{ob\ }\mathcal{T}
,
where the first quasi-isomorphism is by Yoneda lemma for
\mathcal{T}
, and the second quasi-isomorphism is given Proposition
2.11
.
This implies that
\kappa
is fully faithful.
∎

Let us give two concrete examples to illustrate the above definitions, as a conclusion to this subsection.

Example 2.1
.

Consider an
A_{\infty}
-category with a single object
X
whose endomorphism space is an
A_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
.
Let
\mathcal{M}
be a left
A_{\infty}
-module over
\mathcal{A}
, which gives rise to a subcategory
\mathcal{T}
of the dg category of right modules over the given
A_{\infty}
-category, or equivalently the dg category of left modules over
\mathcal{A}
,
which has a single object
\mathcal{M}
.
In this case,

(2.28)

Z_{\mathcal{T}}=Z_{\mathcal{M}}:=\hom_{\mathcal{A}\mathrm{\!-\!mod}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M})=\prod_{k\geq 0}\hom_{{\mathbf{k}}}(\mathcal{A}^{\otimes k}\otimes\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M})[-k].

This is a dg algebra, and there is an obvious projection morphism
Z_{\mathcal{M}}\to\hom_{{\mathbf{k}}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M})
of dg algebras,
making
\mathcal{M}
naturally a right module over
Z_{\mathcal{M}}^{op}
.
The formal completion is given by the following formula

(2.29)

\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{T}}=\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{M}}:=\hom_{\mathrm{mod\!-\!}Z_{\mathcal{M}}^{op}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M}).

Example 2.2

(A corollary of Theorem 1.1 of
[
7
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\mathrm{Perf}(X)
be the dg category of perfect complexes over a smooth and proper scheme
X
over a field
{\mathbf{k}}
,
and
\mathcal{T}=\mathrm{Perf}_{Y}(X)
be the subcategory of perfect complexes with cohomology supported on a closed subscheme
Y\subset X
.
Define the formal neighborhood
\widehat{X}_{Y}
of
Y
in
X
, as well as the dg category of algebraizable perfect complexes
\mathrm{Perf}_{alg}(\widehat{X}_{Y})
by taking the homotopy limit over the
n
-th infinitesimal neighborhood
Y_{n}
of
Y
with respect to the obvious inclusions
i_{n,n+1}:Y_{n}\to Y_{n+1}
and
i_{n}:Y_{n}\to X
.
In this case, there is a natural quasi-equivalence

(2.30)

\mathrm{Perf}_{alg}(\widehat{X}_{Y})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}=\widehat{\mathrm{Perf}(X)}_{\mathrm{Perf}_{Y}(X)}.

2.3.
Relation to the categorical formal punctured neighborhood

There is another categorical construction, called the
categorical formal punctured neighborhood
of an
A_{\infty}
-category,
also due to Efimov
[
8
]
.
To give the definition, we first recall that the dg-category of Calkin complexes over
{\mathbf{k}}
,
\mathrm{Calk}_{{\mathbf{k}}}
,
is defined to be the dg-quotient of all chain complexes over
{\mathbf{k}}
by perfect chain complexes:

(2.31)

\mathrm{Calk}_{{\mathbf{k}}}=\mathrm{Ch}_{{\mathbf{k}}}/\mathrm{Perf}{\mathbf{k}}.

It comes with a natural projection
\mathrm{Ch}_{{\mathbf{k}}}\to\mathrm{Calk}_{{\mathbf{k}}}
,
so that for any
A_{\infty}
-category
\mathcal{B}
,
the Yoneda functor
y:\mathcal{B}\to\mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{B}^{op},\mathrm{Ch}_{{\mathbf{k}}})
further gives rise to a functor
\bar{y}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{B}^{op},\mathrm{Calk}_{{\mathbf{k}}})
.
More generally, we can define

(2.32)

\mathrm{Calk}_{\mathcal{B}}=\mathrm{mod\!-\!}\mathcal{B}/y(\mathcal{B})

the quotient of
\mathrm{mod\!-\!}\mathcal{B}
by the subcategory of representable modules, i.e. the image of the Yoneda functor
y:\mathcal{B}\to\mathrm{mod\!-\!}\mathcal{B}
.
Its idempotent split-closure is denoted by
\overline{\mathrm{Calk}}_{\mathcal{B}}
.

Definition 2.13
.

The categorical formal punctured neighborhood of a small
A_{\infty}
-category
\mathcal{B}
, denoted by
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}
,
is the essential image of the functor
\bar{y}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{B}^{op},\mathrm{Calk}_{{\mathbf{k}}})
.

For a smooth
\mathcal{B}
, this can be refined as follows.
We first define a functor
F_{\mathcal{B}}:\mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{B}^{op},\mathrm{Calk}_{{\mathbf{k}}})\to\overline{\mathrm{Calk}}_{\mathcal{B}}
to be the composition

(2.33)

F_{\mathcal{B}}:\mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{B}^{op},\mathrm{Calk}_{{\mathbf{k}}})\to\mathrm{Perf}(\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathrm{Calk}_{{\mathbf{k}}})\to\overline{\mathrm{Calk}}_{\mathcal{B}},

where smoothness ensures that the first functor
\mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{B}^{op},\mathrm{Calk}_{{\mathbf{k}}})\to\mathrm{Perf}(\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathrm{Calk}_{{\mathbf{k}}})
is a fully faithful embedding.
By definition the induced Yoneda functor
\bar{y}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{B}^{op},\mathrm{Calk}_{{\mathbf{k}}})
has image in
\ker(F_{\mathcal{B}})
.
Then we define
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}
to be the essential image of
\bar{y}:\mathcal{B}\to\ker(F_{\mathcal{B}})
.

In the following situation, the notion of the formal completion along a subcategory is related to the formal punctured neighborhood of infinity.
Consider a short exact sequence of small
A_{\infty}
-categories

(2.34)

\mathcal{T}\xhookrightarrow{}\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{B}.

To describe the relation between
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}
, we first give a more explicit description of
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}
in the presence of the short exact sequence (
2.34
).
The pullback functor on the categories of right modules
\mathrm{mod\!-\!}\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{mod\!-\!}\mathcal{T}
preserves proper modules, thus induces a functor

(2.35)

r_{\mathcal{T}}:\mathrm{Prop}\mathcal{C}=\mathrm{Perf}\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{Prop}\mathcal{T},

where
\mathrm{Prop}\mathcal{C}=\mathrm{Perf}\mathcal{C}
because
\mathcal{C}
is smooth and proper.
In particular, by restricting to
\mathcal{C}
we get a functor
\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{Perf}\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{Prop}\mathcal{T}
.
Since
\mathcal{T}
is proper, every perfect complex defines a proper module, i.e.
\mathrm{Perf}\mathcal{T}
embeds in
\mathrm{Prop}\mathcal{T}
under the Yoneda embedding, so we have a fully faithful embedding
\mathcal{T}\xhookrightarrow{}\mathrm{Perf}\mathcal{T}\xhookrightarrow{}\mathrm{Prop}\mathcal{T}
.
Then we take the quotient by
\mathcal{T}
to get a functor

(2.36)

\bar{r}_{\mathcal{T}}:\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{T}\cong\mathcal{B}\to\mathrm{Prop}\mathcal{T}/y(\mathcal{T})

where the first quasi-equivalence comes from the short exact sequence (
2.34
).
Since
y(\mathcal{T})
split-generates
\mathrm{Perf}\mathcal{T}
, the projection
\mathrm{Prop}\mathcal{T}/y(\mathcal{T})\to\mathrm{Prop}\mathcal{T}/\mathrm{Perf}\mathcal{T}
is a quasi-equivalence,
so that the above functor can be replaced by the following one

(2.37)

\bar{r}_{\mathcal{T}}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathrm{Prop}\mathcal{T}/\mathrm{Perf}\mathcal{T}.

Lemma 2.14
.

There is a quasi-equivalence

(2.38)

\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}ess-im(\bar{r}_{\mathcal{T}}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathrm{Prop}\mathcal{T}/\mathrm{Perf}\mathcal{T}).

Proof.

By applying
\overline{\mathrm{Calk}}
and
\mathrm{Fun}(-,\mathrm{Calk}_{{\mathbf{k}}})
to the categories in the short exact sequence, we obtain a commutative diagram.
By tracing the commutative diagram, Theorem 3.2 of
[
8
]
asserts that there a quasi-equivalence

\ker(F_{\mathcal{B}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\overline{\mathrm{Prop}\mathcal{T}/\mathrm{Perf}\mathcal{T}},

where
F_{B}
is as in (
2.33
), and
\overline{\mathrm{Prop}\mathcal{T}/\mathrm{Perf}\mathcal{T}}
is the idempotent split closure of
\mathrm{Prop}\mathcal{T}/\mathrm{Perf}\mathcal{T}
.
This is compatible with the natural functors
\mathcal{B}\to\ker(F_{\mathcal{B}})
and
\mathcal{B}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bar{r}_{\mathcal{T}}}}{{\to}}\mathrm{Prop}\mathcal{T}/\mathrm{Perf}\mathcal{T}
,
so we conclude that there is a quasi-equivalence

\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}ess-im(\bar{r}_{\mathcal{T}}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathrm{Prop}\mathcal{T}/\mathrm{Perf}\mathcal{T}).

∎

Theorem 2.15
.

Suppose we have a short exact sequence (
2.34
) in which
\mathcal{C}
is both smooth and proper,
so that
\mathcal{T}
is proper and
\mathcal{B}
is smooth.
Then there is a canonical functor

(2.39)

\hat{\pi}:\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}\to\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}

inducing a quasi-equivalence

(2.40)

\bar{\hat{\pi}}:\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}/\kappa(\mathcal{T})\to\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty},

where
\kappa
is the functor (
2.26
).

Proof.

To construct the desired functor
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}\to\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}
, it suffices to construct a functor
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}\to\mathrm{Prop}\mathcal{T}
,
and show that modulo the image of the fully faithful embedding
\iota_{\mathcal{T}}:\mathcal{T}\to\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}
Lemma
2.12
,
the image of the quotient functor lands in the essential image of
\mathcal{B}\to\mathrm{Prop}\mathcal{T}/\mathrm{Perf}\mathcal{T}
.

Since
\mathcal{T}
is proper, and for each
X\in\mathrm{ob\ }\mathcal{C}
the right Yoneda module
Y^{r}_{X}
is perfect and hence proper,
it follows that the right
\mathcal{T}
-module
i^{*}Y^{r}_{X}
is proper.
This allows us to define a functor

(2.41)

\tilde{\pi}:\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}\to\mathrm{Prop}\mathcal{T},

which on the level of objects sends each
X\in\mathrm{ob\ }\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}=\mathrm{ob\ }\mathcal{C}
, presented as the
Z_{\mathcal{T}}^{op}
-module
P_{\mathcal{T}}(-,X)
,
to
i^{*}Y^{r}_{X}\in\mathrm{ob\ }\mathrm{mod\!-\!}\mathcal{T}
,
and on the level of morphism spaces is given by the following composition of maps

(2.42)

\begin{split}\tilde{\pi}^{1}:\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,Y)=&\hom_{\mathrm{mod\!-\!}Z_{\mathcal{T}}^{op}}(P_{\mathcal{T}}(-,X),P_{\mathcal{T}}(-,Y))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j^{*}}}{{\to}}\hom_{\mathrm{mod\!-\!}\mathcal{T}}(j^{*}P_{\mathcal{T}}(-,X),j^{*}P_{\mathcal{T}}(-,Y))\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\hom_{\mathrm{mod\!-\!}\mathcal{T}}(i^{*}Y^{r}_{X},i^{*}Y^{r}_{Y}),\end{split}

where the second quasi-isomorphism is (
2.22
).
By the definition of the pullback of modules, the second order map of
\tilde{\pi}
is given by the second order map of
j^{*}
,
which in this case is zero as
j:Z_{\mathcal{T}}\xhookrightarrow{}\mathcal{C}\mathrm{\!-\!mod}
is a strict inclusion of subcategories.
Since both
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}
and
\mathrm{Prop}\mathcal{T}
are dg categories, there are no higher order terms,
so the construction of the functor
\tilde{\pi}:\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}\to\mathrm{Prop}\mathcal{T}
is complete.
By further projecting to the quotient, it induces a functor

(2.43)

\bar{\tilde{\pi}}:\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}\to\mathrm{Prop}\mathcal{T}/\mathrm{Perf}\mathcal{T}.

Next, recall that the functor
r_{\mathcal{T}}:\mathrm{Prop}\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{Prop}\mathcal{T}
(
2.35
) is the pullback functor for right modules,
so the induced functor
\bar{r}_{\mathcal{T}}:\mathcal{B}\cong\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{T}\to\mathrm{Prop}\mathcal{T}/\mathrm{Perf}\mathcal{T}
sends each
X\in\mathrm{ob\ }\mathcal{B}
, regarded as an object of
\mathcal{C}
via localization,
to the pullback of the right Yoneda module
i^{*}Y^{r}_{X}
over
\mathcal{T}
.
It follows that the image of
\bar{\tilde{\pi}}:\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}\to\mathrm{Prop}\mathcal{T}/\mathrm{Perf}\mathcal{T}
lands in the essential image of
\bar{r}_{\mathcal{T}}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathrm{Prop}\mathcal{T}/\mathrm{Perf}\mathcal{T}
.
Using the identification of
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}
with the essential image of
\bar{r}_{\mathcal{T}}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathrm{Prop}\mathcal{T}/\mathrm{Perf}\mathcal{T}
by Lemma
2.14
, we obtain the desired functor
\hat{\pi}:\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}\to\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}
(
2.39
).
Our construction immediately implies that the image of
\mathcal{T}
in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}
under
\kappa
is mapped to zero in
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}
by
\hat{\pi}
.
Hence, the functor
\hat{\pi}
induces
\bar{\hat{\pi}}:\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}/\mathcal{T}\to\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}
(
2.40
).

It remains to show that
\bar{\hat{\pi}}:\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}/\mathcal{T}\to\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}
is a quasi-equivalence.
Clearly, it is essentially surjective by the construction above, based on Lemma
2.14
.
To show fully-faithfulness, observe that the functor
\tilde{\pi}:\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}\to\mathrm{Prop}\mathcal{T}
(
2.41
) is fully faithful,
since its linear terms (
2.42
) are compositions of two quasi-isomorphisms by Proposition
2.11
.
Since
\kappa:\mathcal{T}\to\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}
is fully faithful by Lemma
2.12
, and its image under
\tilde{\pi}:\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}\to\mathrm{Prop}\mathcal{T}
is mapped to the subcategory
y(\mathcal{T})
consisting of Yoneda modules,
the induced quotient functor (
2.43
) is well-defined on the quotient
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}/\kappa(\mathcal{T})
and thus gives rise to a functor

(2.44)

\bar{\tilde{\pi}}:\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}/\kappa(\mathcal{T})\to\mathrm{Prop}\mathcal{T}/\mathrm{Perf}\mathcal{T},

which is fully faithful because it factors as
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}/\kappa(\mathcal{T})\to\mathrm{Prop}\mathcal{T}/y(\mathcal{T})\to\mathrm{Prop}\mathcal{T}/\mathrm{Perf}\mathcal{T}
.
This implies that
\bar{\hat{\pi}}:\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}/\kappa(\mathcal{T})\to\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}
(
2.40
) is fully faithful.
∎

Stating the last conclusion in Theorem
2.15
in a slightly different way, we have:

Corollary 2.16
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is both smooth and proper, the short exact sequence (
2.34
) induces a short exact sequence

(2.45)

\mathcal{T}\xhookrightarrow{}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{T}}\to\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\infty}.

Note that, in Theorem
2.15
and Corollary
2.16
, the assumption that
\mathcal{C}
is both smooth and proper is important.
The conclusions fail to hold if either assumption is dropped.

2.4.
The categorical formal completion as limits

In algebraic geometry, the formal completion is defined via limits, which we mentioned in §
1.2
.
The goal of this subsection is to present a purely categorical analogue of that construction.
Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
A_{\infty}
-category, and consider a diagram of
A_{\infty}
-categories

\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I}

indexed by some poset
I
(or more generally any small category),
together with a strictly compatible system of functors

\{f_{i}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I},

where strict compatibility means that the composition of
\iota_{i}
with any morphism
\mathcal{C}_{i}\to\mathcal{C}_{j}
in the diagram corresponding to a morphism
i\to j
in
I
is exactly equal to (not just homotopic to)
f_{j}
.
We can define the
homotopy limit
of the diagram

(2.46)

\mathrm{holim}_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i},

which comes with canonical functors

(2.47)

\pi_{i}:\mathrm{holim}_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i}\to\mathcal{C}_{i}

and

(2.48)

f:\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{holim}_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i},

such
\pi_{i}\circ f
agrees with
f_{i}
up to homotopy. An explicit model of the homotopy limit is given in Appendix
A
.

For each object
X_{i}\in\mathrm{ob\ }\mathcal{C}_{i}
, let
Y^{r}_{X_{i}}
be the associated (right) Yoneda module.
The image of
\mathcal{C}_{i}
under the (right) Yoneda embedding
y_{\mathcal{C}_{i}}:\mathcal{C}_{i}\to\mathrm{mod\!-\!}\mathcal{C}_{i}
, i.e. the subcategory whose objects are all Yoneda modules, is often referred to as the subcategory of
representable modules
.
The Yoneda embedding is compatible with pullback, in the sense that there is a canonical natural transformation

(2.49)

T_{F}:y_{\mathcal{C}}\to F^{*}\circ y_{\mathcal{D}}\circ F,

for any functor
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
.
Also recall that the category of
twisted complexes

\mathrm{Tw}\mathcal{C}_{i}
over
\mathcal{C}_{i}
is a pre-triangulated closure of
\mathcal{C}_{i}
,
such that the Yoneda embedding extends over and maps
\mathrm{Tw}\mathcal{C}_{i}
to the smallest pre-triangulated subcategory of

.
The Yoneda lemma implies that

,
whose triangulated closure is equivalent to the image of the homotopy category of twisted complexes over
\mathcal{C}

under the cohomology functor of the Yoneda embedding.
The pullback

of representable

-modules,
but will be if

is essentially surjective because of the natural transformation (

For the same reason, the categories

do not in general form a a diagram unlike

\mathcal{C}

(2.50)

\{f_{i}^{*}Y^{r}_{f_{i}(X)}\}_{i\in I},

with morphisms of modules in the diagram given by components of the natural transformation (

for a given morphism

.

-modules exactly because of the compatibility of the functors

with the functors

in the diagram.
In addition, there is a morphism

for every
i

\mathcal{C}
-modules above.
For each
\mathcal{C}
-module

(2.51)

\begin{split}&\lambda_{M}:M(X)\to\hom_{\mathrm{mod\!-\!}\mathcal{C}}(Y^{r}_{X},M)\\
&\lambda_{M}(m)^{k}(u,c_{k-1},\ldots,c_{1})=\mu^{k+1}_{M}(m,u,c_{k-1},\ldots,c_{1}),\end{split}

which is a generalization of the first order map of the Yoneda functor to an arbitrary module

.

Now consider the situation where all the categories
\mathcal{C}_{i}
have the same collection of objects and the functors

act as identity on objects.

Definition 2.17
.

In the situation as above, define the subcategory

3.
Various versions of Fukaya categories

In this section, we briefly review the definition of the partially wrapped Fukaya category,
and then introduce the main definition: the category
\mathcal{N}
and discuss its properties.
Although the framework of
[
10
]
which uses the wrapping categories and categorical localizations to define the partially wrapped Fukaya category is nice and clean,
for technical convenience we shall use concrete geometric perturbations by quadratic Hamiltonians as in
[
19
]
.
The convenience is in two places: the definition of the category
\mathcal{N}
and the construction of the functor in Theorem
1.1
.

3.1.
Liouville sectors and Liouville pairs

A Liouville sector of dimension
2n
is a Liouville manifold-with-boundary
(X,\omega=d\lambda,Z)
,
where
\lambda
is the Liouville form and
Z
is the Liouville vector field,
on which there exists a function, called the
defining function

I:\partial X\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that the Hamiltonian vector field
X_{I}
points outward along
\partial X
.
The defining function
I
provides an identification of a
cylindrical neighborhood

\mathcal{N}\mathrm{bd}^{Z}\partial X
of
\partial X
, i.e. a neighborhood that is invariant under the flow of
Z
), with
F\times{\mathbb{C}}_{\mathrm{Re}\geq 0}
,
where
F
is a Liouville manifold, called the
symplectic boundary
of
X
, which is the completion of a Liouville domain
F_{0}
sitting inside
\partial_{\infty}X
as a Liouville hypersurface with boundary.
The Liouville manifold
F
can be obtained from
\partial X
by symplectic reduction as the quotient by the integrable characteristic foliation, which indeed carries two Liouville forms
\lambda_{\pm\infty}
by embedding
F=I^{-1}(a)
as
a\to\pm\infty
.
Every Liouville sector
X
can be deformed such that
\lambda_{+\infty}=\lambda_{-\infty}
(Proposition 2.28 of
[
10
]
),
in which case we say that
X
has exact boundary.
There is an adequate supply of
cylindrical
almost complex structures
J
on
X
compatible with
\omega
making the projection
\pi:\mathcal{N}\mathrm{bd}^{Z}\partial X\to{\mathbb{C}}_{\mathrm{Re}\geq 0}
holomorphic.

Given a Liouville sector
X
as above, there is a Liouville manifold
\bar{X}
, called the
convex completion
of
X
, defined as gluing
F\times{\mathbb{C}}_{\mathrm{Re}\leq 0}
to
X
using the identification
\mathcal{N}\mathrm{bd}^{Z}\partial X\cong F\times{\mathbb{C}}_{\mathrm{Re}\geq 0}
,

(3.1)

\bar{X}=X\bigcup_{F\times{\mathbb{C}}_{0\leq\mathrm{Re}\leq\epsilon}}F\times{\mathbb{C}}_{\mathrm{Re}\leq\epsilon}

In this way we obtain a
sutured Liouville manifold
, meaning a pair
(\bar{X},F_{0})
where
\bar{X}
is a Liouville manifold which is the completion of a Liouville domain, and
F_{0}\subset\partial\bar{X}_{0}
is a Liouville hypersurface with boundary.
Conversely, given a sutured Liouville manifold
(\bar{X},F_{0})
, we may obtain a Liouville sector
X
by removing a standard neighborhood of
F_{0}
,
X=\bar{X}\setminus(F_{0}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{s\geq 0}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{|t|\leq\epsilon})^{\circ}
.
In fact, the above correspondence is unique in the homotopical sense:

Lemma 3.1

(Lemma 2.32 of
[
10
]
)
.

Every Liouville sector
X
comes from a unique (up to deformation) sutured Liouville manifold
(Y,F_{0})
.
In fact,
Y
must agree with the convex completion
\bar{X}
of
X
.

Note that
F_{0}\subset\partial_{\infty}X
is a closed subset,
so the pair
(\bar{X},F_{0})
is also called a
stopped Liouville manifold
following
[
11
]
.
The
core

\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{c}_{F}
of
F
is the set of all points which not escape to infinity under the Liouville flow.
It is a closed subset of
F_{0}
and therefore of
\partial_{\infty}\bar{X}
,
and therefore also a stop for
\bar{X}
.

A Lagrangian submanifold
L
of a Liouville sector
X
is understood to be properly embedded in the interior of
X
.

L
is
cylindrical
if away from a compact set,
L
is invariant under the flow of
Z
.
In particular, any closed Lagrangian submanifold is cylindrical.
From now on we shall only consider exact cylindrical Lagrangians.

A Hamiltonian
H:X\to{\mathbb{R}}
is
linear
(resp.
quadratic
) at infinity, if outside of a compact set it satisfies

dH(Z)=H(\text{ resp. }2H)

When constructing the partially wrapped Fukaya category using Hamiltonian perturbations, we shall instead go to the convex completion
\bar{X}
and consider quadratic Hamiltonians on
\bar{X}
satisfying certain conditions specified by the stop.

Definition 3.2

(Definition 2.14 of
[
19
]
)
.

A Hamiltonian
H:\bar{X}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is said to be compatible with the stop
F_{0}
if it satisfies

(i)

H
is positive;

(ii)

The Hamiltonian vector field
X_{H}
is tangent to
F_{0}
;

(iii)

d\theta(X_{H})
is non-negative in a cylindrical neighborhood of
F_{0}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{s>0}
in
\bar{X}
,
where
\theta
is the angular coordinate on
{\mathbb{R}}_{s\geq 0}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{|t|\leq\epsilon}
as a subset of the closed right half plane
{\mathbb{C}}_{\mathrm{Re}\geq 0}
with
{\mathbb{R}}_{s\geq 0}
identified with the non-negative real axis.

The last condition (iii) implies that the intersection of any Hamiltonian chord of
H
with the ray
F_{0}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{s>0}
is positive.
Quadratic Hamiltonians compatible with given stops are shown to always exist, by Lemma 2.14 of
[
19
]
.
In fact, the same argument, with very small changes of the numbers, can be used to derive the following fact:

Lemma 3.3
.

Linear and quadratic Hamiltonians compatible with the stop
F_{0}
exist.

3.2.
The partially wrapped Fukaya category

Let
X
be a Liouville sector, which corresponds to the sutured Liouville manifold
(\bar{X},F_{0})
.
Fix an at most countable collection
\bar{\mathbf{L}}
of exact cylindrical Lagrangian submanifolds of
\bar{X}
,
equipped with

and
Spin structures
,
such that for every

all Reeb chords on
\partial_{\infty}\bar{X}
from
\partial_{\infty}L_{i}

are non-degenerate.

Fix a positive quadratic Hamiltonian

that is compatible with the stop
F_{0}
.
For a pair
(L_{0},L_{1})
of Lagrangians in
\mathbf{L}
, let

be the set of all time-one chords for

from

to

The

is defined to be

(3.2)

CW^{i}(L_{0},L_{1};H)=\bigoplus_{x\in\chi(L_{0},L_{1}),\deg(x)=i}|o_{x}|_{{\mathbf{k}}}.

Let

.
Let
\bar{\mathcal{R}}^{k+1}
denote the Deligne-Mumford compactification of the moduli space of disks with

\bar{\mathcal{S}}^{k+1}\to\bar{\mathcal{R}}^{k+1}

The fiber over
[\Sigma]
is a nodal stable disk
\Sigma
.
For any

\Omega^{i}\to\bar{\mathcal{R}}^{k+1}
be the fibration with fiber at

being the space of
i
-forms
\Omega^{i}(\Sigma)

Make universal choices of
Floer data

(i)

compatible families of universal strip-like coordinates

\epsilon^{+}_{L_{0},\ldots,L_{k};j}:[0,+\infty)\times[0,1]\times\bar{\mathcal{R}}^{k+1}\to\bar{\mathcal{S}}^{k+1},

(3.4)

\epsilon^{-}_{L_{0},\ldots,L_{k};0}:(-\infty,0]\times[0,1]\times\bar{\mathcal{R}}^{k+1}\to\bar{\mathcal{S}}^{k+1},

(3.5)

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\nu_{j}\leq\nu_{0}

(ii)

compatible families of rescaling functions

(3.6)

\rho\in\Gamma(\bar{\mathcal{R}}^{k+1},\Omega^{0}_{\geq 1}),

which equals
\nu_{j}
in the strip-like coordinates.
Here

(iii)

compatible families of sub-closed one-forms,

Here sub-closedness means that

(iv)

compatible families of Hamiltonians

(3.8)

H:\bar{\mathcal{S}}^{k+1}\to\mathcal{H}(\bar{X})

up to a conformal rescaling factor near each of the chosen universal strip-like coordinates.

(v)

compatible families of cylindrical almost complex structures

(3.9)

J_{L_{0},\ldots,L_{k}}:\bar{\mathcal{S}}^{k+1}\to\mathcal{J}(\bar{X})

which makes the projection
\pi:F\times{\mathbb{C}}_{\mathrm{Re}\leq\epsilon}\to{\mathbb{C}}_{\mathrm{Re}\leq\epsilon}
holomorphic.

In all the above choices, compatibility is defined with respect to gluing of various boundary strata of
\bar{\mathcal{R}}^{k+1}
and
\bar{\mathcal{S}}^{k+1}

Let
S
be a disk with

Denote by

Consider
\mathcal{R}^{k+1}(\mathbf{x})
the moduli space of maps

satisfying

(3.10)

\begin{cases}&(du-X_{H_{S}}\otimes\beta_{S})^{0,1}=0,\\
&u(z)\in(\psi^{\rho_{S}(z)})^{*}L_{j}\text{ for }z\in\partial_{j}S,j=0,\ldots,k,\\
&\lim\limits_{s\to+\infty}u\circ\epsilon_{j}(s,\cdot)=(\psi^{\nu_{j}})^{*}x_{j},j=1,\ldots,k,\\
&\lim\limits_{s\to-\infty}u\circ\epsilon_{0}(s,\cdot)=(\psi^{\nu_{0}})^{*}x_{0}.\end{cases}

Define a map of degree

(3.11)

\begin{split}\mu^{k}_{\mathcal{W}(\bar{X})}:&CW^{*}(L_{k-1},L_{k};H)\otimes\cdots\otimes CW^{*}(L_{0},L_{1};H)\to CW^{*}(L_{0},L_{k};H)[2-k]\\

\deg(x_{0})=\deg(x_{1})+\cdots+\deg(x_{k})+2-k\end{subarray}}\sum_{u\in\mathcal{R}^{k+1}(\mathbf{x})}(-1)^{*_{k}}\mathcal{R}^{k+1}_{u}([x_{k}]\otimes\cdots\otimes[x_{1}]),\end{split}

It is by now a standard argument to show that

Definition 3.4
.

Fix an at most countable collection
\bar{\mathbf{L}}
of exact cylindrical Lagrangians of

,

-category with objects in

,

and

-structure maps given by

(

When no confusion may occur, we refer to this as the wrapped Fukaya category of

,
and denote it by

.

To define the partially wrapped Fukaya category, we shall now assume that the Hamiltonian is compatible with the stop
F_{0}
.
Take a sub-collection
\mathbf{L}\subset\bar{\mathbf{L}}
of Lagrangians that are disjoint from

at infinity.
For each

We can consider the following subspace:

.

3.3.
Some structural results

This subsection contains a brief summary of several structural results about partially wrapped Fukaya categories that are needed for relevant discussions in this paper, mostly due to
[

]
,
[

]

The most fundamental result underlying many various structural results of the partially wrapped Fukaya category of a stopped Liouville sector is an exact triangle describing the effect of wrapping through the stop.

Theorem 3.8

(Theorem 1.10 of
[
11
]
)
.

Let
(X,\mathfrak{f})
be a stopped Liouville sector, and

be a point near which
\mathfrak{f}
is a Legendrian submanifold.
Suppose

is a Lagriangian submanifold and

is obtained from

by a positive isotopy by passing

through

Let

be the small Lagrangian disk linking
\mathfrak{f}
at

.
Then there is an exact triangle

(3.17)

L^{w}\to L\to D_{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle[1]}}{{\to}}

in
\mathcal{W}(X,\mathfrak{f})
, where

is the continuation map induced by the isotopy

.

The existence of smooth Legendrian points on the stop

is important in many other results based on the wrapping exact triangle Theorem

.
Because of this, it is natural to make the following definition, following
[
11
]
.

Definition 3.9
.

A closed subset
\mathfrak{f}
of a contact manifold

is called mostly Legendrian if it admits a decomposition
\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{f}^{subcrit}\cup\mathfrak{f}^{crit}
,
where
\mathfrak{f}^{subcrit}
is closed and contained in the image of a manifold of dimension

and
\mathfrak{f}^{crit}\subset Y\setminus\mathfrak{f}^{subcrit}

Then there is a generation result for partially wrapped Fukaya categories of stopped Weinstein manifolds, whose proof crucially relies on Theorem

.

Theorem 3.10

(Theorem 1.10 of
[
11
]
)
.

Suppose

at infinity.
Then the partially wrapped Fukaya category

A special case where the stop is mostly Legendrian is given by a Lefschetz fibration

In that case, the Fukaya-Seidel category

This follows from another generation result (Theorem 1.16) of
[

]

for a stopped Liouville sector of the form

where

is a Liouville manifold, and
\Lambda\subset\partial_{\infty}(F\times{\mathbb{C}}_{\mathrm{Re}\geq 0})^{\circ}
is mostly Legendrian.

Another key ingredient for us is the stop removal formula.

Theorem 3.11

(Theorem 1.20 of
[
11
]
)
.

Suppose

are two stops for

The proof of Theorem

also relies on the wrapping exact triangle (Theorem

If

The special case for a localization relation between the Fukaya-Seidel category and the fully wrapped Fukaya category of the total space was earlier proved by Abouzaid-Seidel
[

]
and Sylvan
[

]
,
in somewhat different forms.

Lastly, we shall need the Künneth formula for partially wrapped Fukaya categories.

(Theorem 1.5 of
[

]
)

For Liouville sectors

and

-bilinear-functor

As an immediate consequence, Künneth formula shows that there is a stabilization operation in the partially wrapped content,

after fixing a choice of a functor

3.4.
The Rabinowitz Fukaya category

The new variant of Fukaya category for a Liouville sector

to be defined in the next subsection will be closely related to the
Rabinowitz Fukaya category
of its convex completion
\bar{X}
.
We briefly recall the relevant definitions here following
[
9
]
in order to fix notations needed for various Floer-theoretic constructions that will appear throughout the remaining subsections §

-§

.

Take a positive quadratic Hamiltonian
H:\bar{X}\to{\mathbb{R}}
.
Consider the negative Hamiltonian
-H
, and define the Floer cochain space for
(L_{0},L_{1})
with respect to
-H

(3.21)

CW^{i}(L_{0},L_{1};-H)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{-}\in\chi(L_{0},L_{1};-H)\\
\deg(x^{-})=i\end{subarray}}|o_{x^{-}}|_{{\mathbf{k}}}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\chi(L_{1},L_{0};H)\\
\deg(x)=n-i\end{subarray}}|o^{-}_{x}|_{{\mathbf{k}}}.

The second equality follows from a natural bijective correspondence between time-one
(-H)
-chords
x^{-}
from
L_{0}
to
L_{1}
and time-one
H
-chords
x
from
L_{1}
to
L_{0}
, by
x^{-}(t)=x(1-t)
,
which comes with an associated isomorphism of orientation lines
o_{x^{-}}\cong o_{x}^{-}
as thus an isomorphism of normalized orientation spaces

(3.22)

\displaystyle|o_{x^{-}}|_{{\mathbf{k}}}

\displaystyle\to|o_{x}^{-}|_{{\mathbf{k}}}

(3.23)

\displaystyle[x^{-}]

\displaystyle\mapsto[x]^{-},

where
[x]^{-}
is the negative orientation for

The Floer differential can be defined by counting inhomogeneous pseudoholomorphic strips in a similar way to the case of defining the Floer differential on
CW^{*}(L_{0},L_{1};H)
,
except that we no longer get a finite sum; and that is why we need the direct product in (
3.21
).
The Floer differential is still well-defined on the whole direct product by an action argument,
where the direct product can be interpreted as the completion with respect to a filtration on the direct sum given by putting upper bounds on the action.
Since our Lagrangians are oriented, we can make use of the Poincaré duality isomorphism

(3.24)

\bar{I}:CW^{*}(L_{0},L_{1};-H)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\to}}\hom_{{\mathbf{k}}}(CW^{n-*}(L_{1},L_{0};H),{\mathbf{k}}),

to give an alternative definition of the Floer complex for the negative quadratic Hamiltonian
-H
.

There is a
continuation map

(3.25)

c:CW^{*}(L_{0},L_{1};-H)\to CW^{*}(L_{0},L_{1};H),

which can be thought of as a continuation map induced by a monotone increasing homotopy from
-H
to
H
.
The well-definedness of the continuation map (
3.25
) on the direct product (
3.21
) follows from an action filtration argument similar to that for the well-definedness of the differential.
Alternatively, the map (
3.25
) can be defined as the dual of a
copairing
for the wrapped Floer cochain complexes with respect to the positive Hamiltonian
H
.
If the copairing is

(3.26)

\hat{c}:{\mathbf{k}}\to CW^{n-*}(L_{1},L_{0};H)\otimes CW^{*}(L_{0},L_{1};H),

then we set

(3.27)

c(\cdot)=(\bar{I}(\cdot)\otimes\mathrm{id})(\hat{c}).

The copairing
\hat{c}
is defined by counting rigid inhomogeneous pseudoholomorphic disks with no input and two outputs,
one of which is a time-one
H
-chord from
L_{0}
to
L_{1}
and the other a time-one
H
-chord from
L_{1}
to
L_{0}
;
for energy reasons such a count must be a finite, so a map of the form (
3.26
) is well-defined.
Details can be found in
[
9
]
.

The
Rabinowitz Floer complex
is defined to be the mapping cone of the continuation map (
3.25
),

(3.28)

\begin{split}RC^{*}(L_{0},L_{1})=&\mathrm{cone}(c:CW^{*}(L_{0},L_{1};-H)\to CW^{*}(L_{0},L_{1};H))\\
&=CW^{*}(L_{0},L_{1};-H)[1]\oplus CW^{*}(L_{0},L_{1};H).\end{split}

Furthermore, an
A_{\infty}
-structure can be constructed on the Rabinowitz Floer complexes by counting certain kinds of popsicles, adapted to the quadratic Hamiltonian framework.
Below we give a brief summary of the construction of the
A_{\infty}
-structure, and modify it to define an
A_{\infty}
-structure on for a corresponding chain complex in the partially wrapped setting.

Let
S
be a disk with
k+1
boundary punctures
z_{0},\ldots,z_{k}
.
For
1\leq j\leq k
, let
C_{j}
be the unique hyperbolic geodesic connecting the points at infinity
z_{0}
and
z_{j}
,
called
popsicle sticks
.
Recall from
[
2
]
that a popsicle structure on
S
of
flavor

\mathbf{p}:F\to\{1,\ldots,k\}
,
where
F
is a finite index set,
consists of a choice of a preferred point
\sigma_{f}\in C_{\mathbf{p}(f)}
,
called a
sprinkle
.
Assign a non-positive integer weight
w_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq 0}
to each puncture
z_{i}
satisfying

(3.29)

w_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(w_{i}+|\mathbf{p}^{-1}(i)|),

and

(3.30)

|\mathbf{p}^{-1}(i)|\leq-w_{i},i=0,1,\ldots,k.

We write

(3.31)

\mathbf{w}=(w_{0},\ldots,w_{k})

for the collection of weights.

Denote by
\mathcal{R}^{k+1,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{w}}
the moduli space of popsicles of flavor
\mathbf{p}
carrying weights
\mathbf{w}
,
and
\mathcal{S}^{k+1,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{w}}\to\mathcal{R}^{k+1,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{w}}
the universal family.
Each
\mathcal{R}^{k+1,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{w}}
is a copy of
\mathcal{R}^{k+1,\mathbf{p}}
, i.e. independent of
\mathbf{w}
,
and is a fiber bundle over
\mathcal{R}^{k+1}
with fiber
{\mathbb{R}}^{|F|}
.
The compactifications, by adding broken popsicles, are denoted by
\bar{\mathcal{S}}^{k+1,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{w}}\to\bar{\mathcal{R}}^{k+1,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{w}}
,
whose low dimensional boundary strata are products of moduli spaces of popsicles of other flavors.
For the purpose of counting popsicles to define the operations (
3.51
), we shall only restrict ourselves to injective popsicles, which are the ones with trivial
Aut(\mathbf{p})
,
or equivalently
|\mathbf{p}^{-1}(i)|\leq 1
for every
i=1,\ldots,k
.
In addition, we making the following constraints on the weights

(3.32)

w_{j}\in\{-1,0\},\forall j=0,1,\ldots,k.

Introduce a sign notation for every
j=0,\ldots,k
,

(3.33)

s_{j}=\begin{cases}+,&\text{ if }w_{j}=0,j>0\text{ or }w_{j}=-1,j=0\\
-,&\text{ if }w_{j}=-1,j>0\text{ or }w_{j}=0,j=0.\end{cases}

Equip
S
with strip-like ends
\{\epsilon_{j}\}_{j\in\{0,\dots,k\}}
determined by the weights
\mathbf{w}
in the following way.
Let
Z^{\pm}={\mathbb{R}}^{\pm}\times[0,1]
,
with standard coordinates
(s,t)
.
For
j=0,\ldots,k
, the
j
-th strip-like end near
z_{j}
is a holomorphic embedding

(3.34)

\epsilon_{j}:Z^{s_{j}}\to S.

These should satisfy
\epsilon_{j}^{-1}(\partial S)=\{t=0,1\}

and
\lim\limits_{s\to\pm\infty}u\circ\epsilon_{j}=z_{j}
for all
j=0,1,\ldots,k
, and have disjoint images.
A puncture
z_{j}
is called a negative (resp. positive) puncture if the strip-like end has domain
Z^{-}
(resp.
Z^{+}
).

Fix a
(k+1)
-tuple of Lagrangians
L_{0},\ldots,L_{k}\in\mathbf{L}
,
and assign
L_{j}
to the
j
-th boundary component of
S
between the punctures
z_{j}
and
z_{j+1}
.
Denote by
\mathbf{x}=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{k})
a collection of time-one chords for
s_{j}H
,
where
x_{0}\in\chi(L_{0},L_{d};s_{0}H)
and
x_{j}\in\chi(L_{j-1},L_{j};s_{j}H)
for
j=1,\ldots,k
.

Because of the changes we have made to the choices to the strip-like ends for a popsicle compared to those for an ordinary boundary-pointed disk,
we also need to change the conditions for a Floer datum.
In fact, the only condition we need to change is (
3.5
),
which now takes the form

(3.35)

\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{w_{j}}\nu_{j}\leq(-1)^{w_{0}}\nu_{0}.

Suppose we have made universal choices of Floer data for weighted popsicles.
Write down the inhomogeneous Cauchy-Riemann equation in the same form as (
3.10
),
but keep in mind that Floer data and asymptotic conditions should be changed accordingly.
The new equation is

(3.36)

\begin{cases}&(du-X_{H_{S}}\otimes\beta_{S})^{0,1}=0,\\
&u(z)\in(\psi^{\rho_{S}(z)})^{*}L_{j}\text{ for }z\in\partial_{j}S,j=0,\ldots,k,\\
&\lim\limits_{s\to s_{j}\infty}u\circ\epsilon_{j}(s,\cdot)=(\psi^{\nu_{j}})^{*}x_{j}^{s_{j}},j=1,\ldots,k,\\
&\lim\limits_{s\to-s_{0}\infty}u\circ\epsilon_{0}(s,\cdot)=(\psi^{\nu_{0}})^{*}x_{0}^{s_{0}}.\end{cases}

Let

(3.37)

\mathcal{R}^{k+1,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{x})

the moduli space of such popsicle maps satisfying (
3.10
) with the prescribed boundary conditions and asymptotic chords
x_{0},\ldots,x_{k}
.
The moduli space has virtual dimension

(3.38)

\dim\mathcal{R}^{k+1,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{x})=k-2+|F|+n(-w_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}w_{j})+(-1)^{w_{0}}\deg(x_{0})-\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{w_{j}}\deg(x_{j})

Counting rigid elements in moduli spaces of dimension zero yields multilinear maps of degree

(3.39)

\mu^{k,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{w}}:CW^{*}(L_{k-1},L_{k};s_{k}H)\otimes\cdots\otimes CW^{*}(L_{0},L_{1};s_{1}H)\to CW^{*}(L_{0},L_{k};s_{0}H)

which on basis elements take the form

(3.40)

\begin{split}&\mu^{k,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{w}}([x_{k}^{s_{k}}]\otimes\cdots\otimes[x_{1}^{s_{1}}])=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{0}\\
k-2+|F|+n(-w_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}w_{j})+(-1)^{w_{0}}\deg(x_{0})-\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{w_{j}}\deg(x_{j})=0\end{subarray}}\\
&\sum_{u\in\mathcal{R}^{k+1,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{x})}(-1)^{*_{k,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{w}}+\Diamond_{k,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{w}}}\mathcal{R}^{k+1,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{w}}_{u}([x_{k}^{s_{k}}]\otimes\cdots\otimes[x_{1}^{s_{1}}]),\end{split}

Here the signs

are

(3.41)

*_{k,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{w}}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}(j+w_{1}+\cdots+w_{j-1})\deg(x_{j}^{s_{j}})+\sum_{j=1}^{k}(k-j)w_{j},

(3.42)

\Diamond_{k,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{w}}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}|\mathbf{p}^{-1}(\{j+1,\ldots,k\})|(w_{j}+|\mathbf{p}^{-1}(j)|).

Whenever there are some
j
’s such that
w_{j}=-1
so the wrapped Floer complex
CW^{*}(L_{j-1},L_{j};-H)
are direct products (
3.21
),
which can be interpreted as the completion of the direct sum with respect to a filtration by putting upper bounds on the action,
the operations
\mu^{k,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{w}}
(
3.40
) can be extended to a well-defined map on the completion by an action filtration argument,
because
\mu^{k,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{w}}
increases the action.
Then we combine all these maps (

) to define

(3.43)

\mu^{k}_{\mathcal{RW}}:RC^{*}(L_{k-1},L_{k})\otimes\cdots\otimes RC^{*}(L_{0},L_{1})\to RC^{*}(L_{0},L_{k}),

which on a given component takes the form (
3.39
).
This has degree
2-k
for the reason of the degree shift in (
3.28
).

Definition 3.13
.

Fix an at most countable collection
\bar{\mathbb{L}}
of exact cylindrical Lagrangians in the Liouville manifold
\bar{X}
which contains all isotopy classes.
The Rabinowitz wrapped Fukaya category

is defined to be the

-category with objects being exact Lagrangian submanifolds in

,
morphism space being the Rabinowitz Floer complex

\hom_{\mathcal{RW}}(L_{0},L_{1})=RC^{*}(L_{0},L_{1})

and

-structure maps given by

(

).
When no confusion may occur, we refer to this as the Rabinowitz wrapped Fukaya category of

, and denote it by

.

Remark 3.14
.

When defining the complex
RC^{*}(L_{0},L_{1})
as in (

),
we interpret the generators for the

component as time-one
(-H)
-chords

from

to

.
However, when defining the

-structure maps

using the various moduli spaces of popsicle maps,
we use (

-chords
x
from

to
L_{0}
.
But the roles of inputs and outputs are interchanged, as (

Later, when we discuss about popsicle maps, the actual asymptotic chords are all

but keep in mind that an output

3.5.
The negatively wrapped Fukaya category

Now let us turn our attention to a Liouville sector
X
, whose corresponding sutured Liouville manifold is
(\bar{X},F_{0})
.
The goal of this subsection is to introduce a Floer-theoretic invariant for
X
or equivalently
(\bar{X},F_{0})
that is analogous to the the Rabinowitz Fukaya category.
This construction does not seem to carry over to general stopped Liouville sectors, contrasting the partially wrapped Fukaya category (
[
11
]
).
The key observation leading to the well-definedness is the following:

Lemma 3.15
.

Suppose
H:\bar{X}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a positive quadratic Hamiltonian which is compatible with the stop
F
.
Then the image of the continuation map
c
(
3.25
) is contained in the subspace
CW^{*}_{F}(L_{0},F_{1};H)
.

Proof.

Since the continuation map is defined as a copairing by counting certain disks with two outputs and no inputs,
we conclude by a standard homotopy argument that it factorizes through a map

(3.44)

c_{h}:CF^{*}(L_{0},L_{1};-h)\to CF^{*}(L_{0},L_{1};h)

for a small positive Hamiltonian
h
linear at infinity, in the sense that there is a commutative diagram:

(3.45)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces CW^{*}(L_{0},L_{1};-H)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces CW^{*}(L_{0},L_{1};H)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces CF^{*}(L_{0},L_{1};-h)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces CF^{*}(L_{0},L_{1};h)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c_{h}}

where the vertical maps are continuation maps induced by an increasing homotopy from
h
to
H
and an increasing homotopy from
-H
to
-h
.
For the homotopy from
-H
to
-h
, we define the continuation map in a similar way as (
3.25
) from the copairing (
3.26
).
We may choose
h
carefully so that it is also compatible with the stop in the sense of Definition
3.2
.

If the slope of the linear Hamiltonian
h
is sufficiently small, say less than the length of the shortest Reeb chord from
\partial_{\infty}L_{0}
to
\partial_{\infty}L_{1}
on
\partial_{\infty}X
,
the continuation images of any
h
-chord under the continuation map
CF^{*}(L_{0},L_{1};h)\to CW^{*}(L_{0},L_{1};H)
do not correspond to any Reeb chord on
\partial_{\infty}X
from
\partial_{\infty}L_{0}
to
\partial_{\infty}L_{1}
,
so must be some
H
-chords in the interior of the compact Liouville domain
\bar{X}_{0}
,
which never cross
F_{0}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{s>0}
.
It follows that the image of
CF^{*}(L_{0},L_{1};h)\to CW^{*}(L_{0},L_{1};H)
is contained in the subspace
CW^{*}_{F}(L_{0},L_{1};H)
.
∎

The proof of Lemma
3.15
in fact provides a more refined description of the image of the continuation map.
For this description, we introduce

(3.46)

CW^{*}_{int}(L_{0},L_{1};H)\subset CW^{*}(L_{0},L_{1};H)

the subspace generated by ‘interior chords’, i.e. the ones contained in a compact set in the Liouville domain
\bar{X}_{0}
which do not correspond to any Reeb chord from
\partial_{\infty}L_{0}
to
\partial_{\infty}L_{1}
.
Without loss of generality, we may choose
H
small enough in
C^{2}
-norm in the compact domain part,
such that all these interior chords are trivial, i.e. intersection points, and have zero action.
Therefore, the subspace
CW^{*}_{int}(L_{0},L_{1};H)
is a subcomplex.
By definition, this subcomplex is also contained in
CW^{*}_{F}(L_{0},L_{1};H)
, which is generated by all these interior chords,
as well as those Hamiltonian chords in the cylindrical end corresponding to some Reeb chords but not crossing
F_{0}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{s>0}
.
Note that the wrapped Floer complex
CW^{*}(L_{0},L_{1};-H)
also contains a subspace
CW^{*}_{int}(L_{0},L_{1};-H)
generated by interior chords (which may adjusted to be intersection points).
This is no longer a subcomplex, but is naturally identified with a quotient complex,
together with a projection map
CW^{*}(L_{0},L_{1};-H)\to CW^{*}_{int}(L_{0},L_{1};-H)
which is a chain map.
The more refined statement is

Corollary 3.16
.

The continuation map
c:CW^{*}(L_{0},L_{1};-H)\to CW^{*}(L_{0},L_{1};H)
(
3.25
) factors as

CW^{*}(L_{0},L_{1};-H)\to CW^{*}_{int}(L_{0},L_{1};-H)\to CW^{*}_{int}(L_{0},L_{1};H)\subset CW^{*}(L_{0},L_{1};H).

In particular, the image of
c
is contained in the subcomplex
CW^{*}_{int}(L_{0},L_{1};H)
generated by interior chords.

Lemma
3.15
allows us to define the following cochain complex:

(3.47)

\begin{split}NC^{*}_{F}(L_{0},L_{1})&:=\mathrm{cone}(c:CW^{*}(L_{0},L_{1};-H)\to CW^{*}_{F}(L_{0},L_{1};H))\\
&=CW^{*}(L_{0},L_{1};-H)[1]\oplus CW^{*}_{F}(L_{0},L_{1};H)),\end{split}

with the cone differential

(3.48)

\mu^{1}_{NC}=\begin{pmatrix}d_{-}&c[1]\\
0&\mu^{1}_{\mathcal{W}(X)}\end{pmatrix}

Now we want to define an
A_{\infty}
-structure on the complexes (
3.47
).
Since the continuation map (
3.25
) has image in
CW^{*}_{F}(L_{0},L_{1};H)
, there is an obvious inclusion

(3.49)

NC^{*}_{F}(L_{0},L_{1})\subset RC^{*}(L_{0},L_{1}),

which is a chain map.
In order for the maps
\mu^{k}_{\mathcal{RW}}
to descend to the subcomplexes
NC^{*}_{F}(L_{0},L_{1})
, we will need the following identity about intersection points of chords with
F_{0}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{s>0}
as well as those of inhomogeneous pseudoholomorphic curves with
F
.

Lemma 3.17
.

Consider any moduli space of popsicle maps
\mathcal{R}^{k+1,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{x})
,
which is among those defining the
A_{\infty}
-structure maps
\mu^{k}_{\mathcal{RW}}
(
3.43
).
Then the intersection numbers of various
H
-chords satisfy the following identity

(3.50)

(-1)^{w_{0}}x_{0}\cdot(F_{0}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{s>0})+u\cdot F=\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{w_{j}}x_{j}\cdot(F_{0}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{s>0}).

where
u
is any representative of an element in
\mathcal{R}^{k+1,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{x})
.

Proof.

This is just a variant of Sylvan’s argument
[
19
]
to show that the
A_{\infty}
-maps
\mu^{k}_{\mathcal{W}(\bar{X})}
(
3.11
) do not increase the intersection numbers of chords with
F_{0}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{s>0}
so that
\mu^{k}_{\mathcal{W}(X)}
(
3.14
) are well defined.

For convenience, we include the proof, which is quite straightforward.
The difference in the intersection numbers of all the outputs and those of all the inputs is precisely the winding number of
\partial u
around
F
,
which by topology is equal to
u\cdot F
.
∎

Proposition 3.18
.

Suppose for
j=1,\ldots,k
,
x_{j}
is either a time-one
H
-chord from
L_{j}
to
L_{j-1}
, in which case
w_{j}=-1
and
x_{j}
is an output,
or a time-one
H
-chord from
L_{j-1}
to
L_{j}
which does not intersect
F_{0}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{s>0}
, in which case

3.6.
Deformation invariance

The partially wrapped Fukaya category

is invariant under deformation of Liouville sectors by

We shall see that the category

also shares a similar property.

Proposition 3.21
.

A deformation of Liouville sectors
\{X_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
induces a quasi-equivalence
\mathcal{N}(X_{0})\cong\mathcal{N}(X_{1})
.

Proof.

The proof basically follows that of
[
10
]
.
The technical issue here is that in our setup,
\mathcal{N}(X)
is defined using Floer data on
\bar{X}
, so it is not automatic that an inclusion of sectors
X\xhookrightarrow{}X^{\prime}
always induces a functor
\mathcal{N}(X)\to\mathcal{N}(X^{\prime})
.
However, when there is a deformation of Liouville sectors
\{X_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
,
their convex completions are deformation equivalent,
and the whole deformation can be broken up into small pieces such that every small piece is a zigzag of trivial inclusions.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that the deformation is a trivial inclusion, of the form
X_{-a}\xhookrightarrow{}X
for
X_{-a}=e^{-aX_{I}}(X)
where
X_{I}
is the Hamiltonian vector field for the defining function
I
of
X
.
Looking at the corresponding sutured Liouville manifolds,
such an inclusion can be described as an inclusion

\bar{X}\setminus(F_{0}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{|t|\leq\epsilon^{\prime}}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{s\geq 0})^{\circ}\xhookrightarrow{}\bar{X}\setminus(F_{0}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{|t|\leq\epsilon}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{s\geq 0})^{\circ}

by deforming the neighborhood
F_{0}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{|t|\leq\epsilon}
of
F_{0}\subset\partial\bar{X}_{0}
to a bigger neighborhood for some
\epsilon^{\prime}>\epsilon
,
as long as there still exists a function
I_{F_{0}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{|t|\leq\epsilon^{\prime}}}:F_{0}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{|t|\leq\epsilon^{\prime}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
whose derivative along the Reeb vector field is positive, and the contact vector field for this function is outward pointing along
\partial(F_{0}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{|t|\leq\epsilon^{\prime}})
.
Under this description, there is an obvious functor
\mathcal{W}(X_{-a})\to\mathcal{W}(X)
which send every Lagrangian
L
to itself,
and is the identity on morphism spaces, since there is no newly created Reeb chord linking
F_{0}
when shrinking
F_{0}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{|t|\leq\epsilon^{\prime}}
to
F_{0}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{|t|\leq\epsilon}
.
This implies that the functor induces a functor
\mathcal{N}(X_{-a})\to\mathcal{N}(X)
,
which by the above analysis is indeed an equivalence.
∎

\mathrm{holim}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq-1}}\mathcal{N}_{>m}(\bar{X},F_{0})(s^{L_{0}},s^{L_{1}})=\mathrm{holim}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq-1}}\{\mathcal{N}_{>m}(L_{0},L_{1})\}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq-1}}.

The existence of such a map (
3.70
) is given in (
A.22
) in Appendix

.

The diagrams of functors (
3.67
) and (
3.68
) define natural functors

(3.72)

\Pi:\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})\to\mathrm{holim}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq-1}}\mathcal{N}_{>m}(\bar{X},F_{0})

and

(3.73)

J:\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})\to\mathrm{holim}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq-1}}\mathcal{N}_{>m}(\bar{X},F_{0}),

such that
J=\Pi\circ j
.
We shall show that
\Pi
(
3.72
) is fully faithful in favorable circumstances.

Proposition 3.32
.

Suppose
\partial_{\infty}\bar{X}
has bounded Reeb dynamics relative to
F
in the sense of Definition
1.3
.
Then the natural functor
\Pi:\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})\to\mathrm{holim}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq-1}}\mathcal{N}_{>m}(\bar{X},F_{0})
(
3.72
) is fully faithful.

Given the quasi-isomorphism (
3.70
) and the identification (
3.71
), it will be sufficient to show that the inverse system formed by the cohomology groups of the chain complexes also satisfy the degree-wise Mittag-Leffler condition.
This is the place where we need the assumption on the Reeb dynamics on
\partial_{\infty}\bar{X}
, in the sense of Definition
1.3
.
Recall that the morphism space in
\mathcal{N}_{>m}(\bar{X},F_{0})
is a quotient complex (
3.55
).

Lemma 3.33
.

Suppose
\partial_{\infty}\bar{X}
has bounded Reeb dynamics relative to
F
.
Then for each pair of Lagrangians
L_{0},L_{1}
, the inverse system of the cohomology groups

\{H^{*}(\mathcal{N}_{>m}(L_{0},L_{1}))\}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq-1}}

satisfies the degree-wise Mittag-Leffler condition.

Proof.

The key point is that for each
m
, the quotient complex
C_{0}/C_{m}
is finite-dimensional by the assumption that
\partial_{\infty}\bar{X}
has bounded Reeb dynamics relative to
F
.
We would like to show that, for every
m\leq-1
, there exists
m^{\prime}\leq m
such that for all
m^{\prime\prime}\leq m^{\prime}
, we have

(3.74)

\mathrm{Im}(H^{*}(C_{0}/C_{m^{\prime\prime}})\to H^{*}(C_{0}/C_{m}))=\mathrm{Im}(H^{*}(C_{0}/C_{m^{\prime}}\to H^{*}(C_{0}/C_{m})).

Let
[c]_{m}\in C_{0}/C_{m}
be any element, which we may assume to be a cocycle with respect to the induced quotient differential on
C_{0}/C_{m}
, hence representing a cohomology class.
Pick a representative
c\in C_{0}
of
[c]_{m}
, i.e. any element
c\in C_{0}
whose projection to
C_{0}/C_{m}
is
[c]_{m}
.
If
c
can be chosen such that
\mu^{1}_{\mathcal{N}}(c)=0
, then the equivalence class
[c]_{m^{\prime}}
of
c
in
C_{0}/C_{m^{\prime}}
for all
m^{\prime}\leq m
is also a cocycle with respect to the induced quotient differential on
C_{0}/C_{m^{\prime}}
.
By definition, the cohomology class of
[c]_{m^{\prime}}
is mapped to the cohomology class of
[c]_{m}
.
This implies that
H^{*}(C_{0}/C_{m^{\prime}})\to H^{*}(C_{0}/C_{m})
is surjective.
If
\mu^{1}_{\mathcal{N}}(c)\neq 0
for all representatives
c
, then there exists
m^{\prime}<m
such that the equivalence class
[c]_{m^{\prime}}
of
c
in
C_{0}/C_{m^{\prime}}
is not closed under the induced quotient differential on
C_{0}/C_{m^{\prime}}
.
Then, for any
m^{\prime\prime}<m^{\prime}
, the equivalence class
[c]_{m^{\prime\prime}}
is not closed under the differential on
C_{0}/C_{m^{\prime\prime}}
, which implies that both maps on cohomology

H^{*}(C_{0}/C_{m^{\prime}})\to H^{*}(C_{0}/C_{m})

and

H^{*}(C_{0}/C_{m^{\prime\prime}})\to H^{*}(C_{0}/C_{m})

have zero image.
Apply the same argument to all the vectors in a basis for the finite-dimensional
{\mathbf{k}}
-vector space
C_{0}/C_{m}
.
Since there are finitely many vectors, we may find the smallest
m^{\prime}
in the above argument, such that (
3.74
) holds for all
m^{\prime\prime}<m
.
∎

The degree-wise Mittag-Leffler condition for the inverse system formed by cohomology groups implies that
\varprojlim^{1}
vanishes for this inverse system, and therefore:

Corollary 3.34
.

The natural map

(3.75)

H^{*}(\varprojlim_{m\in Z_{\leq-1}}\hom_{\mathcal{N}_{>m}}(L_{0},L_{1}))\to\varprojlim_{m\in Z_{\leq-1}}H^{*}(\hom_{\mathcal{N}_{>m}}(L_{0},L_{1}))

is an isomorphism.

We have gathered all the ingredients and are ready to prove Proposition
3.32
.

Proof of Proposition
3.32
.

It suffices to show that the linear map
\Pi^{1}
for every pair
(L_{0},L_{1})
is a quasi-isomorphism.
We compose the map
H^{*}(\Pi^{1})
with the cohomology map of the quasi-isomorphism (
3.70
) and further with (
3.75
) to get a map

H^{*}(\mathcal{N}(L_{0},L_{1}))\to H^{*}(\varprojlim_{m\in Z_{\leq-1}}\mathcal{N}_{>m}(L_{0},L_{1}))\to\varprojlim_{m\in Z_{\leq-1}}H^{*}(\mathcal{N}_{>m}(L_{0},L_{1})).

By the universal property, this is the inverse limit of the diagram of maps

H^{*}(\pi_{m}):H^{*}(\mathcal{N}(L_{0},L_{1}))\to H^{*}(\mathcal{N}_{>m}(L_{0},L_{1})),

i.e. the unique lift of these maps to the inverse limit
\varprojlim_{m\in Z_{\leq-1}}H^{*}(\mathcal{N}_{>m}(L_{0},L_{1}))
.
It is straightforward to check that this lift is an isomorphism, using the descending filtration

\cdots\subset C_{m}\subset\cdots\subset C_{0}=\mathcal{N}(L_{0},L_{1}).

∎

In the homotopy limit
\mathrm{holim}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq-1}}\mathcal{N}_{>m}(\bar{X},F_{0})
,
there is the subcategory
\mathcal{S}(\{\mathcal{N}_{>m}(\bar{X},F_{0})\}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq-1}})
of constant sections defined in Definition
2.17
.
Finally we make the following observation about the functor

:

Lemma 3.35
.

The functor
\Pi:\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})\to\mathrm{holim}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq-1}}\mathcal{N}_{>m}(\bar{X},F_{0})
(
3.72
) has image landing in the subcategory
\mathcal{S}(\{\mathcal{N}_{>m}(\bar{X},F_{0})\}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq-1}})
.

Proof.

This is a statement on the level of objects, which immediately follows from the definitions of the diagram
\{\mathcal{N}_{>m}(\bar{X},F_{0})\}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq-1}}
and the functor
\Pi
.
∎

3.10.
Pushforward functors

An inclusion of Liouville sectors
\iota:X\xhookrightarrow{}X^{\prime}
is a proper exact embedding which is a diffeomorphism onto the image,
such that either
X=X^{\prime}
or
\iota(\partial X)\cap X^{\prime}=\varnothing
.
An inclusion of stopped Liouville sectors
\iota:(X,\mathfrak{f})\xhookrightarrow{}(X^{\prime},\mathfrak{f}^{\prime})
is an inclusion of Liouville sectors
\iota:X\xhookrightarrow{}X^{\prime}
such that
\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\cap(\partial_{\infty}X)^{\circ}=\mathfrak{f}
.
The partially wrapped Fukaya category is covariantly functorial with respect to inclusions of stopped Liouville sectors,
in particular for a single inclusion
\iota:X\xhookrightarrow{}X^{\prime}
, there is a natural pushforward functor

(3.76)

\iota_{*}:\mathcal{W}(X,\mathfrak{f})\to\mathcal{W}(X^{\prime},\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}).

The functor (
3.76
) is fully faithful if the inclusion is
forward stopped
(Corollary 8.17 of
[
11
]
).

The negatively wrapped Fukaya category
\mathcal{N}(X)=\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})
has similar functoriality under inclusions,
which is nonetheless more restricted due to the nature of our definition relying on the presence of a ribbon
F
.
We consider the situation where an inclusion of Liouville sectors is given by an inclusion of stops
\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\subset\mathfrak{f}
,
realized by an inclusion of the Liouville sub-domain
F^{\prime}_{0}\subset F_{0}
,
while the convex completions are the same,
\bar{X}=\bar{X}^{\prime}
.
Note that here we allow slightly general notions of Liouville domains and sub-domains:
F_{0}
may be disconnected and have several connected components,
and
F^{\prime}_{0}
is the union of several sub-domains in the connected components, some of which are allowed to be empty.

Proposition 3.36
.

In the case of a stop removal inclusion described as above, there is a pushforward functor

(3.77)

\iota_{\sharp}:\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})\to\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F^{\prime}_{0}),

which is identity on objects.

Proof.

The key point is that any quadratic Hamiltonian compatible with
F_{0}
is automatically compatible with
F^{\prime}_{0}
.
So we can define maps
\iota_{\sharp}^{k}
by letting the linear term
\iota_{\sharp}^{1}
be the obvious inclusion of subcomplexes,
and the higher order terms be zero.
It is straightforward to check that the maps defined this way satisfy the
A_{\infty}
-functor equations.
∎

3.11.
Fully-faithfulness

We conclude §
3
by studying the property of the functor
j
on a certain class of Lagrangians.
These Lagrangians serve as candidates of zero objects in the partially wrapped Fukaya category.

Definition 3.37
.

We say an (exact cylindrical) Lagrangian
L
in
\bar{X}
is nonessential,
if there is a Liouville hypersurface (with boundary)
S_{0}\subset\partial_{\infty}\bar{X}
as well as a sectorial inclusion
S\times{\mathbb{C}}_{\mathrm{Re}\geq 0}\xhookrightarrow{}\bar{X}
such that
L
is can be isotoped into the interior of the image of the inclusion.

In other words, a nonessential Lagrangian is one that can be isotoped into the complement of the sector
X^{\prime}
that is obtained by completing the Liouville domain
\bar{X}_{0}
(whose completion is
\bar{X}
) away from
S_{0}\subset\partial\bar{X}_{0}
.
The reason for the term ‘nonessential’ is the following:

Lemma 3.38

(Corollary 9.2 of
[
11
]
)
.

Let
L
be a nonessential Lagrangian of the Liouville manifold
\bar{X}
.
Then
L
is a zero object in
\mathcal{W}(\bar{X})
.
Furthermore, if there is such a Liouville hypersurface
S_{0}
such that
F_{0}\cap\partial_{\infty}(S\times{\mathbb{C}}_{\mathrm{Re}\geq 0})^{\circ}=\varnothing
,
then
L
is a zero object in
\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})
.

Nonessential Lagrangians are not necessarily zero objects in
\mathcal{W}(X)=\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})
.
Of course the second half of Lemma
3.38
gives a criterion for a nonessential Lagrangian
L
for being a zero object in
\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})
,
but that criterion relies on the existence of some
S_{0}
,
which is not a priori guaranteed for a given Lagrangian
L
.
The general expectation is that any Lagrangian which does not intersect the
relative core

\mathfrak{c}_{X,\mathfrak{f}}=\mathfrak{c}_{X}\cup(\mathfrak{f}\times{\mathbb{R}})
is a zero object;
in the real analytic case where the relative core is subanalytic singular isotropic, this generality follows from
[
12
]
.
However, for our purpose of studying the category
\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})
we just need the following result:

Proposition 3.39
.

The functor
j:\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})\to\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})
is fully faithful on the subcategory whose objects are nonessential Lagrangians.

The first half of Lemma
3.38
immediately implies that Proposition
3.39
holds automatically for the functor
\mathcal{W}(\bar{X})\to\mathcal{RW}(\bar{X})
, i.e. in the case where
F_{0}=\varnothing
.
In fact, we shall see that the general case follows by the same reasoning.

Proof of Proposition
3.39
.

It suffices to show that the induced map on cohomology is an isomorphism for each pair
(K,L)
of nonessential Lagrangians.
Let
L
be any nonessential Lagrangian.
By Lemma
3.38
,

L
is a zero object in the fully wrapped Fukaya category
\mathcal{W}(\bar{X})
,
and is also a zero object in the Rabinowitz Fukaya category
\mathcal{RW}(\bar{X})
.
By definition of the Rabinowitz complex (
3.28
),
it also follows that
H^{*}(CW^{*}(L,L;-H))=0
.
Therefore, the inclusion

CW^{*}_{F}(L,L;H)\subset NC^{*}_{F}(L,L)=\mathrm{cone}(c:CW^{*}(L,L;-H)\to CW^{*}_{F}(L,L;H))

is quasi-isomorphism.
The same argument can be applied to any pair
(K,L)
of nonessential Lagrangians.
∎

Proposition
3.39
together with Lemma
3.32
yields the following consequence.

Corollary 3.40
.

The functor
J:\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})\to\mathrm{holim}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq-1}^{op}}\mathcal{N}_{>m}(\bar{X},F_{0})
(
3.73
) is fully faithful on nonessential Lagrangians.

The proofs of Proposition
3.39
and its prerequisite Lemma
3.38
indeed imply a slightly stronger statement:

Proposition 3.41
.

Let
L
be a nonessential Lagrangian, and
K
any exact cylindrical Lagrangian, both disjoint from
F_{0}
at infinity.
Then the linear term of
j:\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})\to\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})
is a quasi-isomorphism for the pair
(L,K)
or
(K,L)
:

j^{1}:\hom_{\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})}(L,K)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\hom_{\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})}(j(L),j(K)),

j^{1}:\hom_{\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})}(K,L)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\hom_{\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})}(j(K),j(L)),

Proof.

The vanishing of
H^{*}(CW^{*}(L,L;-H))
implies the vanishing of
H^{*}(CW^{*}(L,K;-H))
or
H^{*}(CW^{*}(K,L;-H))
for any
K
.
∎

4.
The categorical equivalence

4.1.
Overview of the construction

Consider the cup functor

i_{F}:\mathcal{W}(F)\to\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})

Let
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}(F)
be the essential image of

.
Then we can define the categorical completion of

as in Definition
2.4
.

If

is a mostly Legendrian stop, there is alternative replacement of
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}(F)
along which we can perform the categorical formal completion.
Let
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{f})
the full subcategory of
\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})
with objects being small Lagrangian disks linking
\mathfrak{f}^{crit}
.
We can also define

(4.2)

If

is Weinstein, the cocores of

the subcategories
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{f})
and

are quasi-equivalent after passing to twisted complexes.
By Lemma
2.5
, in this case

(

) is quasi-equivalent to

(

).

The main goal of this section is to construct a canonical

-functor

(4.3)

\Psi:\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})\to\widehat{\mathcal{W}}_{F},

and prove the following:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(\bar{X},F)
be a Liouville pair such that
\bar{X}
and
F
are both non-degenerate Liouville manifolds.
Suppose
F
satisfies stop removal for
(\bar{X},F_{0})
, i.e. the functor (

) is a quasi-equivalence,
and that

is a full stop.
Then the functor
\Psi:\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})\to\widehat{\mathcal{W}}_{F}
(

) is a quasi-equivalence.

There are some special and interesting cases in which the hypotheses of Theorem

can be easily checked.
For example, if

is Weinstein,
\mathcal{W}(F)
has a collection of strong generators by cocore disks,
and has the core
\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{c}_{F}
being a mostly Legendrian stop,
so by Theorem
3.11

F
satisfies stop removal.
In addition, if
\bar{X}
is the total space of a symplectic Landau-Ginzburg model with compact critical locus with a regular fiber-at-infinity

being Weinstien,
such that
\bar{X}
itself is a non-degenerate Liouville manifold,
then
\mathcal{W}(\bar{X},F_{0})
is proper (Example 1.19 of
[
11
]
),
so that all the assumptions of Theorem
4.1
are satisfied.

We first give a construction of the functor for an arbitrary Liouville pair
(\bar{X},F)
independently of the hypotheses.
The components of the functor (
4.3
) are maps of the following form

(4.4)

\Psi^{k}:\hom_{\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})}(L_{k-1},L_{k})\otimes\cdots\otimes\hom_{\mathcal{N}(\bar{X},F_{0})}(L_{0},L_{1})\to\hom_{\widehat{\mathcal{W}}_{F}}(L_{0},L_{k}).

We find the following description of the morphism spaces in
\widehat{\mathcal{W}}_{F}
helpful in attempting to construct the functor
\Psi
:

Lemma 4.2
.

The category
\widehat{\mathcal{W}}_{F}
is quasi-equivalent to

.

Proof.

Recall from (

) that in the category

, the morphism is

\hom_{\widehat{\mathcal{W}}_{F}}(L_{0},L_{k})=\hom_{\mathrm{mod\!-\!}Z_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}(F)}^{op}}(P_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}(F)}(-,L_{0}),P_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}(F)}(-,L_{k})).

in fact implies that

4.2.
Moduli spaces of domains and maps

The maps (
4.5
) are constructed via the count of certain popsicles,
which are pseudoholomorphic maps from boundary-punctured disks (equipped with additional data) to the target stopped Liouville manifold subject to certain restricted types of Lagrangian boundary conditions.

We first describe the domains of these pseudoholomorphic maps.
Let

(4.7)

S=D^{2}\setminus\{z_{out},z^{\prime}_{l},\ldots,z^{\prime}_{1},z_{in},z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}\}

be a Riemann surface isomorphic to a disk with
k+l+2
boundary points removed.
The punctures are in the counterclockwise order along
\partial D^{2}
as indicated in (
4.7
),
where we shall call
z_{out}
the
0
-th puncture,

z^{\prime}_{j}
the
(l-j+1)
-th puncture for
j=1,\ldots,l
,

z_{in}
the
(l+1)
-th puncture,
and
z_{i}
the
(l+1+i)
-th puncture for
i=1,\ldots,k
.

Let
C_{i},C_{in}
and
C^{\prime}_{j}
be the unique hyperbolic geodesic connecting
z_{i},z_{in}
and respectively
z^{\prime}_{j}
to
z_{out}
.

Definition 4.3
.

We say a popsicle structure
\sigma
on
S
of flavor
\bar{\mathbf{p}}
is
\Psi
-adapted,
if the sprinkles can only lie on the geodesics
C_{i},i=1,\ldots,k
.

This flavor
\bar{\mathbf{p}}
can be regarded as the extension of a flavor
\mathbf{p}:F\to\{1,\ldots,k\}
by

(4.8)

\bar{\mathbf{p}}(f)=\mathbf{p}(f)+l+1.

Again, we shall only consider injective popsicles, meaning that

is trivial.
In addition, we want to equip the punctures with non-positive integer weights

w_{out}
for
z_{out}
,
w_{i}
for
z_{i}
,
w_{in}
for
z_{in}
and
w^{\prime}_{j}
for
z^{\prime}_{j}
,
which should satisfy

(4.9)

w_{out}=w^{\prime}_{j}=w_{in}=0,\text{ and }w_{i}\in\{-1,0\},i=1,\ldots,k.

Given these weight conditions, the standard conditions (
3.29
) and (
3.30
) on weighted popsicles become simplified to the following

(4.10)

|\bar{\mathbf{p}}^{-1}(i)|=-w_{i}\in\{0,1\},i=1,\ldots,k.

The strip-like ends for
S
near the punctures are chosen compatibly with the weights according to the rules (
3.34
).
In this case, the punctures

and

are positive punctures, equipped with positive strip-like ends

the puncture

is a negative puncture, equipped with a negative strip-like end

For the other punctures

, the strip-like ends are

\epsilon_{i}:Z^{s_{i}}\to S,

where the sign notation

is defined in (

).

A description of degenerations of

-adapted popsicle structures is given below,
which is essentially part of Lemma 3.1 of
[
9
]
, originally due to
[
18
]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Consider a weighted
\Psi
-adapted popsicle structure
\sigma
of flavor
\bar{\mathbf{p}}
on

as above.
Then for any broken popsicle
((S_{1},\sigma_{1}),(S_{2},\sigma_{2}))
of flavor
(\mathbf{p}_{1},\mathbf{p}_{2})
in a codimension one stratum of
\partial\bar{\mathcal{R}}^{k+l+2,\bar{\mathbf{p}}}
, exactly one of the following holds:

(i)

both
\sigma_{1}
and

are
\Psi
-adapted;

(ii)

\sigma_{2}
is
\Psi
-adapted, and

(iii)

\sigma_{1}
is
\Psi
-adapted, and
\sigma_{2}
is a trivial popsicle structure;

(iv)

neither

Proposition 4.12
.

Appendix A
Homotopy limits

The notion of homotopy limit is dual to homotopy colimit, where an explicit construction for the homotopy colimit of a diagram of
A_{\infty}
-categories is given in
[
11
]
using the Grothendieck construction of the diagram, adapted from
[
15
]
.
Although we work with
A_{\infty}
-categories whose properties are often referred to as ‘up to homotopy’,
in this construction we assume that diagrams are indexed by posets
I
,
and require that all commuting diagrams be strictly commuting.
For the homotopy limit (the lax limit), we can give an explicit model, via the category of sections of the Grothendieck construction.
For simplicity, all
A_{\infty}
-categories in this section are assumed to be strictly unital.
The reader should be warned that this Appendix consist of facts well-known to experts in category theory;
but we decide to spell them out here for the purpose of fixing conventions in this paper that exclusively fit into the framework of
A_{\infty}
-categories.

A.1.
Grothendieck construction

Let
I
be a poset, so that for every
i,j\in I
with
i\leq j
, there is a unique morphism
i\to j
, with the morphism
i\to i
being the identity morphism.
By a diagram
\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of
A_{\infty}
-categories indexed by a poset
I
, we mean a functor
I\to A_{\infty}-\mathrm{Cat}
which is strictly commuting, i.e. with identity natural transformation for compositions,
and satisfies
\mathcal{C}_{i}\to\mathcal{C}_{i}
is the identity functor.
Concretely, this consists the following data:

•

for each
i\in I
, an
A_{\infty}
-category
\mathcal{C}_{i}
,

•

for each morphism
i\to j
in
I
(i.e.
i\leq j
with a unique morphism), an
A_{\infty}
-functor
f_{ij}:\mathcal{C}_{i}\to\mathcal{C}_{j}
,

such that for every chain of morphisms
i\to j\to k
in
I
, we have
f_{jk}\circ f_{ij}=f_{ik}
.

First let us recall the Grothendieck construction
\mathrm{Gr}(\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I})
of a diagram
\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, following
[
15
]
,
[
11
]
.
Given a
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})
-bimodule
\mathcal{B}
, the
semi-orthogonal gluing

\langle\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C}\rangle_{\mathcal{B}}
of
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{C}
along
\mathcal{B}
is the
A_{\infty}
-category whose objects are
\mathrm{ob\ }\mathcal{C}\sqcup\mathrm{ob\ }\mathcal{D}
,
and morphisms are

(A.1)

\langle\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C}\rangle_{\mathcal{B}}(X,Y)=\begin{cases}\mathcal{D}(X,Y),&X,Y\in\mathrm{ob\ }\mathcal{D},\\
\mathcal{C}(X,Y),&X,Y\in\mathrm{ob\ }\mathcal{C},\\
\mathcal{B}(Y,X),&X\in\mathrm{ob\ }\mathcal{D},Y\in\mathrm{ob\ }\mathcal{C},\\
0,&X\in\mathrm{ob\ }\mathcal{C},Y\in\mathrm{ob\ }\mathcal{D}.\end{cases}

The Grothendieck construction of a finite directed system
\mathcal{C}_{0}\to\cdots\to\mathcal{C}_{k}
is defined inductively by
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{C})=\mathcal{C}
, and

(A.2)

\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{C}_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{01}}}{{\to}}\cdots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{k-1,k}}}{{\to}}\mathcal{C}_{k})=\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{C}_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{01}}}{{\to}}\cdots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{k-2,k-1}}}{{\to}}\langle\mathcal{C}_{k-1},\mathcal{C}_{k}\rangle_{(\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{C}_{k}},f_{k-1,k})^{*}(\mathcal{C}_{k})_{\Delta}}).

For any diagram
\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
indexed by a poset
I
,
define the Grothendieck construction
\mathrm{Gr}(\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I})
to be the
A_{\infty}
-category with objects
\sqcup_{i\in I}\mathrm{ob\ }\mathcal{C}_{i}
, with zero morphisms from objects in
\mathcal{C}_{i}
to those in
\mathcal{C}_{j}
unless there is a morphism
i\to j
in
I
(i.e.
i\leq j
),
such that for every chain of composable morphisms
i_{0}\to\cdots\to i_{p}
in
I
,
the full subcategory with objects
\sqcup_{j=0}^{p}\mathrm{ob\ }\mathcal{C}_{i_{j}}
is given by
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{C}_{i_{0}}\to\cdots\to\mathcal{C}_{i_{p}})
.

Define the class
A_{I}
of adjacent morphisms in
H^{0}(\mathrm{Gr}(\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I}))
to be those morphisms
X_{i}\to f_{ij}X_{i}
which correspond to the unit in
H^{0}\mathcal{C}_{j}(f_{ij}X_{i},f_{ij}X_{i})=H^{0}(\mathrm{Gr}(\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I})(X_{i},f_{ij}X_{i})
for
i\leq j
.

A.2.
The category of sections

Next, we define a new
A_{\infty}
-category
\Gamma\mathrm{Gr}(\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I})
.
The objects of
\Gamma\mathrm{Gr}(\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I})
are called
sections
of the Grothendieck construction
\mathrm{Gr}(\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I})
,
which are functors
I\to\mathrm{Gr}(\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I})
(not
A_{\infty}
-functors)
for which the composition
I\to\mathrm{Gr}(\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I})\to I
is the identity.
Concretely, an object
s
of
\Gamma\mathrm{Gr}(\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I})
consists of the following data:

•

for every object
i\in I
, an object
s(i)\in\mathcal{C}_{i}
,

•

for each morphism
i\to j
in
I
(unique for every
i\leq j
), a morphism
s_{ij}\in\mathcal{C}_{j}(f_{ij}(s(i)),s(j))
,

which are required to satisfy the following compatibility conditions

(i)

s_{ii}=1_{s(i)}\in\mathcal{C}_{i}(s(i),s(i))
(this makes sense as we assume
f_{ii}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{C}_{i}}
for all the diagrams we consider here),

(ii)

for morphism
i\to j\to k
(which exist when
i\leq j\leq k
), the associated morphisms
s_{ij},s_{jk},s_{ik}
should satisfy

(A.3)

\mu^{2}_{\mathcal{C}_{k}}(s_{jk},f_{jk}(s_{ij}))=s_{ik}.

Notice that the second condition is quite restrictive, but it is reasonable in our setting as we will only consider strictly commuting diagrams.
A morphism
T\in\Gamma\mathrm{Gr}(\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I})(s,s^{\prime})
is a natural transformation from
s
to
s^{\prime}
lying above the identity of
I
, which concretely consists of a morphism
T(i)\in\mathcal{C}_{i}^{g}(s(i),s^{\prime}(i))
for every
i\in I
,
satisfying compatibility conditions for natural transformations between functors
s,s^{\prime}:I\to\mathrm{Gr}(\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I})
, i.e.

(A.4)

\mu^{2}_{\mathcal{C}_{j}}(s^{\prime}_{ij},f_{ij}(T(i)))=\mu^{2}_{\mathcal{C}_{j}}(T(j),s_{ij}),

for every
i\to j
.
The
A_{\infty}
-structure on
\Gamma\mathrm{Gr}(\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I})
is induced by
A_{\infty}
-structures on
\mathcal{C}_{i}
for all
i
.
That is, for
T^{(j)}\in\Gamma\mathrm{Gr}(\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I})(s^{(j-1)},s^{(j)}),j=1,\ldots,k
, we let

(A.5)

T:=\mu^{k}_{\Gamma\mathrm{Gr}(\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I})}(T^{(k)},\ldots,T^{(1)})

be the natural transformation
T
such that

(A.6)

T(i)=\mu^{k}_{\mathcal{C}_{i}}(T^{(k)}(i),\ldots,T^{(1)}(i)).

This is well-defined because of (
A.3
) and (
A.4
).
The
A_{\infty}
-equations of
\Gamma\mathrm{Gr}(\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I})
immediately follow from
A_{\infty}
-equations of
\mathcal{C}_{i}
and the strictly commuting condition of the diagram
\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
.

A.3.
The homotopy limit

To define the homotopy limit, we first need to define the class of adjacent morphisms
B_{I}\subset H^{0}(\Gamma\mathrm{Gr}(\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I}))
.
These are induced by adjacent morphisms in
A_{I}\subset H^{0}(\mathrm{Gr}(\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I}))
.
That is to say, morphisms in
B_{I}
are defined to be those whose components are in
A_{I}
.

Definition A.1
.

We define the homotopy limit

(A.7)

\mathrm{holim}_{I}\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I}=\Gamma\mathrm{Gr}(\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I})[B_{I}^{-1}]

to be the localization of
\Gamma\mathrm{Gr}(\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I})
at the collection
B_{I}
of adjacent morphisms in
H^{0}(\Gamma\mathrm{Gr}(\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I}))
.

Remark A.2
.

At the beginning of §
A.1
, we assume the diagram is indexed by a poset
I
,
in which there is a unique morphism
i\to j
iff
i\leq j
.
All the above constructions also make sense for the opposite poset
I^{op}
, where there is a unique morphism
i\to j
iff
j\leq i
.

By construction, the homotopy limit comes with natural projections

(A.8)

\pi_{i}:\mathrm{holim}_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i}\to\mathcal{C}_{i}

for every
i
.
This is induced from a functor
\Gamma\mathrm{Gr}(\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I})\to\mathcal{C}_{i}
,
which on objects sends a section
s
to its evaluation
s(i)\in\mathrm{ob\ }\mathcal{C}_{i}
,
and on morphisms sends a natural transformation
T
to the morphism
T(i)
.

If
I
has a maximal element
\bar{i}
, then the homotopy colimit
\mathrm{hocolim}_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i}
is quasi-equivalent to
\mathcal{C}_{\bar{i}}
.
As for the homotopy limit, we have

Lemma A.3
.

If
I
has a minimal element
\underline{i}
, then the projection functor
\mathrm{holim}_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i}\to\mathcal{C}_{\underline{i}}
is a quasi-equivalence.

Proof.

It is not hard to construct an inverse to the projection, which is induced by a functor

\mathcal{C}_{\underline{i}}\to\Gamma\mathrm{Gr}(\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I})

sending every
X_{\underline{i}}\in\mathrm{ob\ }\mathcal{C}_{\underline{i}}
to the section
s
defined by

(A.9)

s(i)=f_{\underline{i},i}(X_{\underline{i}})

and for
i\to j
, the morphism
s_{ij}

is uniquely inductively defined by the insertion of
\underline{i}\to i\to j
and using (
A.3
),
with
s_{\underline{i},i}=1_{f_{\underline{i},i}(X_{\underline{i}})}\in\hom_{\mathcal{C}_{i}}(f_{\underline{i},i}(X_{\underline{i}}),f_{\underline{i},i}(X_{\underline{i}}))
.
By definition, such an
s
is a well-defined functor
I\to\mathrm{Gr}(\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I})
whose composition with the natural functor
\mathrm{Gr}(\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I})\to I
is the identity.

The maps on morphism spaces

(A.10)

\mathcal{C}_{\underline{i}}(X_{\underline{i},k-1},X_{\underline{i},k})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{C}_{\underline{i}}(X_{\underline{i},0},X_{\underline{i},1})\to\hom_{\Gamma\mathrm{Gr}(\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I})}(s_{0},s_{k})

are induced by
A_{\infty}
-multiplications with the elements from
\mathcal{C}_{\underline{i}}(X_{\underline{i},k-1},X_{\underline{i},k})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{C}_{\underline{i}}(X_{\underline{i},0},X_{\underline{i},1})
under the images of
f_{\underline{i},i_{k}}
.
In particular, the first order map induces an isomorphism on cohomology after passing to the quotient by adjacent morphisms.
And it is a direct computation to check the induced map on cohomology is inverse to the projection.
∎

Let
\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I},\{\mathcal{D}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be two diagrams indexed by the same poset
I
.
Suppose we have a diagram of strictly compatible
A_{\infty}
-functors
\{F_{i}:\mathcal{C}_{i}\to\mathcal{D}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
.
Then, the diagram of functors induces a functor on the homotopy limit

(A.11)

F=\mathrm{holim}_{i\in I}F_{i}:\mathrm{holim}_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i}\to\mathrm{holim}_{i\in I}\mathcal{D}_{i},

which is induced by the functor on the section categories
\Gamma\mathrm{Gr}(\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I})\to\Gamma\mathrm{Gr}(\{\mathcal{D}_{i}\}_{i\in I})
sending each
s
to the section
F\circ s
defined on the level of objects by
F\circ s(i)=F_{i}(s(i))
.
In particular, the usual
universal property
is a special consequence of the above construction:

Lemma A.4
.

If
\{F_{i}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a diagram of strictly compatible
A_{\infty}
-functors,
then there is an induced functor

(A.12)

F=\mathrm{holim}_{i\in I}F_{i}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{holim}_{i\in I}\mathcal{D}_{i}.

Moreover, if any other functor
G:\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{holim}_{i\in I}\mathcal{D}_{i}
satisfies
\pi_{i}\circ G=F_{i}
, then
G
is homotopic to
F
.

However, unlike the homotopy colimit, the homotopy limit is not functorial with respect to a diagram of functors covering an arbitrary functor between posets.
Nonetheless, we have the following special case where the diagram of functors is exactly induced by pullback of a functor of diagrams:

Lemma A.5
.

Let
\{\mathcal{D}_{j}\}_{j\in J}
be a diagram of
A_{\infty}
-categories, and
\alpha:I\to J
a functor of posets.
Let
\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be the pullback diagram defined by
\mathcal{C}_{i}=\mathcal{D}_{\alpha_{i}}
.
Then there is an induced functor

(A.13)

\alpha^{*}:\mathrm{holim}_{j\in J}\mathcal{D}_{j}\to\mathrm{holim}_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i}.

Proof.

On the section categories
\Gamma\mathrm{Gr}(\cdot)
, clearly there is a functor by pullback by
\alpha
.
It is straightforward to check that the adjacent morphisms in
H^{0}\Gamma\mathrm{Gr}(\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I})
are exactly the pullbacks of adjacent morphisms in
H^{0}\Gamma\mathrm{Gr}(\{\mathcal{D}_{j}\}_{j\in J})
under this pullback.
∎

A.4.
The natural transformation from the limit

Since the diagram
\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is strictly commuting and all the
A_{\infty}
-categories
\mathcal{C}_{i}
are small, we can also consider the ordinary limit

(A.14)

\lim_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i}

which is an
A_{\infty}
-category with set of objects being the following subset of
\prod_{I}\mathrm{ob\ }\mathcal{C}_{i}

(A.15)

\mathrm{ob\ }\lim_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i}=\{(X_{i})_{i\in I}:X_{i}\in\mathrm{ob\ }\mathcal{C}_{i},f_{ij}(X_{i})=X_{j}\text{ if there is a morphism }i\to j\}.

The morphism space
\lim_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i}((X_{i})_{i\in I},(Y_{i})_{i\in I})
is the following subspace of
\prod_{I}\mathcal{C}_{i}(X_{i},Y_{i})

(A.16)

\lim_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i}((X_{i})_{i\in I},(Y_{i})_{i\in I})=\{(c_{i})_{i\in I}:c_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{i}(X_{i},Y_{i}),f_{ij}(c_{i})=c_{j}\}.

The
A_{\infty}
-structure maps are defined component-wise, namely

(A.17)

\mu^{k}_{\lim_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i}}((c^{(k)}_{i})_{i\in I},\ldots,(c^{(1)}_{i})_{i\in I})=(\mu^{k}_{\mathcal{C}_{i}}(c^{(k)}_{i},\ldots,c^{(1)}_{i})_{i\in I}.

All of these are well-defined exactly because the diagram is strictly commuting.

Proposition A.6
.

There exists a natural transformation from
\lim
to
\mathrm{holim}
.
That is, for every such diagram there is a functor

(A.18)

H:\lim_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i}\to\mathrm{holim}_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i},

which is natural with respect to functors of diagrams.

Proof.

We will give the construction for a fixed diagram
\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, and naturality will easily follow from the construction.
On the level of objects, the functor
H
sends
(X_{i})_{i\in I}\in\mathrm{ob\ }\lim_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i}
to a section
s
such that
s(i)=X_{i}
on objects,
and for any morphism
i\to j
,
s_{ij}\in\mathcal{C}_{j}(f_{ij}(X_{i}),X_{j})=\mathcal{C}_{j}(X_{j},X_{j})
is given by the unit of
X_{j}
in
\mathcal{C}_{j}
.
On the level of morphisms,
H
sends
(c_{i})_{i\in I}\in\lim_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i}((X_{i})_{i\in I},(Y_{i})_{i\in I})
to the natural transformation
T
defined by
T(i)=c_{i}
.
With this definition, it is a straightforward computation to check the compatibility conditions (
A.3
) and (
A.4
).
Define higher order terms of
H
to be zero,
H^{k}=0
for
k\geq 2
.

It remains to check that
H
defined above is an
A_{\infty}
-functor.
Since all the higher order terms are zero, the only
A_{\infty}
-equation to verify is

(A.19)

\mu^{k}_{\mathrm{holim}_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i}}(H((c^{(k)}_{i})_{i\in I}),\ldots,H((c^{(1)}_{i})_{i\in I}))=H(\mu^{k}_{\lim_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i}}((c^{(k)}_{i})_{i\in I},\ldots,(c^{(1)}_{i})_{i\in I})).

Let
T^{(j)}=H((c^{(j)}_{i})_{i\in I}),j=1,\ldots,k
, which is the natural transformation such that
T^{(j)}(i)=c^{(j)}_{i}
.
By the definition of the
A_{\infty}
-structure of
\mathrm{holim}_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i}
,
\mu^{k}_{\mathrm{holim}_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i}}(T^{(k)},\ldots,T^{(1)})
is induced by
A_{\infty}
-structures of
\mathcal{C}_{i}
, by (
A.5
) and (

).
Thus we have

(A.20)

\mu^{k}_{\mathrm{holim}_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i}}(T^{(k)},\ldots,T^{(1)})(i)=\mu^{k}_{\mathcal{C}_{i}}(c^{(k)}_{i},\ldots,c^{(1)}_{i})

for every
i
,
verifying the
A_{\infty}
-functor equation.
∎

A special case of interest to us is where all the
\mathcal{C}_{i}
’s have the same collection of objects such that
f_{i,i+1}
acts as identity on objects.

Definition A.7
.

In the above situation, every object
X\in\mathrm{ob\ }\mathcal{C}_{i}
determines a unique object
(X_{i})_{i\in I}\in\mathrm{ob\ }\lim_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i}
where
X_{i}=X
for all
i
, which we call the constant sequence associated to
X
.

Under the functor
H:\lim_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i}\to\mathrm{holim}_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i}
, the corresponding section in
\mathrm{holim}_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i}
, denoted by
s^{X}
, is called the constant section associated to
X
.

Even more restrictively, suppose
\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is an inverse system (where
I={\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq-1}
).
The usual limit, a.k.a. the inverse limit in this case, is the
A_{\infty}
-category whose objects are constant sequences.
And the morphism space is by definition the inverse limit of cochain complexes

(A.21)

\lim_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i}((X)_{i\in I},(Y)_{i\in I})=\varprojlim_{i}\mathcal{C}_{i}(X,Y).

Thus we get for each pair of objects
X,Y\in\mathrm{ob\ }\mathcal{C}_{i}
, a cochain map

(A.22)

H:\varprojlim_{i}\mathcal{C}_{i}(X,Y)\to\mathrm{holim}_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i}(s^{X},s^{Y})=\mathrm{holim}_{i\in I}\{\mathcal{C}_{i}(X,Y)\}_{i\in I},

where the latter cochain complex is precisely the homotopy limit of the inverse system of cochain complexes
\{\mathcal{C}_{i}(X,Y)\}_{i\in I}
.
A natural question is whether this map is a quasi-isomorphism.
The answer depends on a condition on the inverse system, called the degree-wise
Mittag-Leffler
condition.

Definition A.8
.

An inverse system of cochain complexes
\{C_{i}\}_{i\in I}
with maps
f_{ij}:C_{i}\to C_{j},i\leq j
is said to satisfy the degree-wise Mittag-Leffler condition,
if for every
i
there exists
i^{\prime}\leq i
such that for all
i^{\prime\prime}\leq i^{\prime}
,
f_{i^{\prime\prime},i}(C_{i^{\prime\prime}})=f_{i^{\prime},i}(C_{i^{\prime}})
in each degree.

The following fact in homological algebra provides an answer to the question concerning the limits.
A standard reference is
[
21
]
.

Lemma A.9
.

Suppose an inverse system of cochain complexes
\{C_{i}\}_{i\in I}
satisfies the degree-wise Mittag-Leffler condition.
Then the natural map
H:\varprojlim_{i}C_{i}\to\mathrm{holim}_{i}C_{i}
is a quasi-isomorphism.

∎

Emergent structures in open EFTs
Perseas Christodoulidis

Abstract
Open effective field theories (EFTs) provide a systematic framework for describing systems coupled to an environment, where dissipation, noise, and modified conservation laws naturally arise. Working within the Schwinger–Keldysh formalism, we examine open extensions of well-studied theories describing a spontaneous symmetry breaking phase in the higher-form sense: the superfluid, Maxwell theory, and Einstein’s gravity. We demonstrate that to leading order breaking advanced symmetries while preserving the physical ones leads to (i) deformed conservation laws that hold at the level of expectation values for global symmetries and (ii) deformed noise constraints in gauge or gravitational theories.

Introduction

Understanding open quantum and classical systems is a central challenge in high-energy theory, condensed matter, and cosmology. When a system interacts with an environment, it typically exhibits non-unitary dynamics, which manifests itself as dissipation and stochastic noise, due to the exchange of energy and information. As standard quantum field theory techniques cannot capture these effects, one has to work within the Schwinger-Keldysh (SK) formalism
Schwinger:1960qe
;
Keldysh:1964ud
;
Feynman:1963fq
;
Sieberer:2015hba
;
Crossley:2015evo
;
Glorioso:2017fpd
;
Liu:2018kfw
;
Haehl:2016pec
(see also
Galley:2012hx
;
Galley:2014wla
for a classical formulation of nonconservative systems). This framework, which includes doubling the dynamical fields to describe evolution along a closed-time contour, enables the computation of in-in expectation values and the construction of effective actions for out-of-equilibrium processes. Such actions typically possess two types of symmetries: physical (diagonal) symmetries transform both fields identically in the two branches, while advanced symmetries act oppositely on the two field copies
Sieberer:2015svu
.

Building on the foundational work of
Crossley:2015evo
;
Liu:2018kfw
on dissipative effective field theories (EFTs), recent studies have applied these techniques in a variety of physical settings: time-translational symmetry breaking in flat spacetime
Hongo:2018ant
, open EFTs of inflation in cosmology
Salcedo:2024smn
;
Salcedo:2025ezu
, damping of pseudo-Goldstone modes
Delacretaz:2021qqu
;
Armas:2021vku
;
Baggioli:2023tlc

Akyuz:2023lsm
;
Akyuz:2025bco
and gravitational EFTs

. A central theme in all previous works is the explicit breaking of the advanced symmetry while the physical symmetry remains intact. Interestingly, in a recent open extension of Maxwell theory, it was shown that the effect of breaking the advanced symmetry only deforms the constraints of the theory
Salcedo:2024nex
; this is a consequence of invariance of the part of the action that mixes physical and advanced fields under a deformed gauge symmetry of the advanced field. This raises a question: when physical symmetries are preserved but advanced symmetries are broken, do open EFTs exhibit similar emergent structures?

In this work, we address this question in systems with gauge fields and gravity. Starting from the open EFT of a superfluid, we identify an emergent average current conservation, and in an open extension of Maxwell theory, formulated in terms of higher-form variables and coupled to background fields (see, e.g.
Gaiotto:2014kfa
;
Armas:2023tyx

Salcedo:2024nex
. Subsequently, we investigate gravitational analogues by considering open extensions of the Einstein–Hilbert action and establishing the existence of deformed noise constraints.

Open gauge theory

2.1
The open superfluid action revisited

Our starting point is the action describing the superfluid phase at non-zero temperature

\begin{aligned} S_{\rm cl}=\int\left(B_{\rm a0}B_{0}-c_{\rm s}^{2}B_{{\rm a}i}B_{i}+\dots\right)\end{aligned}\,,

3
Deformed constraints in gravity

The case of dissipative gravity is qualitatively different from that of gauge fields because of a variety of reasons. First, the standard formulation in the literature is valid in the semi-classical limit by treating the advanced field as a fluctuation field
Crossley:2015evo
;
Liu:2018kfw
, which implies the following form of advanced diffeomorphisms:
\delta g_{\rm a}^{\mu\nu}=\mathcal{L}_{\xi}g^{\mu\nu}
. This can be seen by writing the simplest closed action constructed from the Einstein-Hilbert term and noticing that it looks similar to the variation of the action

S_{\rm cl}=\int\sqrt{-g}\,\mathrm{d}^{4}xG_{\mu\nu}g_{\rm a}^{\mu\nu}\sim\int\sqrt{-g}\,\mathrm{d}^{4}xG_{\mu\nu}\delta g^{\mu\nu}\,.

(3.1)

The latter action has the usual diffeomorphism symmetry
\delta g^{\mu\nu}=\mathcal{L}_{\xi}g^{\mu\nu}
and the advanced symmetry mentioned before follows. Note also that physical diffeomorphisms affect the advanced metric, which is now viewed as a tensor living on the physical spacetime. Second, when introducing a dissipation vector, we reduce the symmetries of diffeomorphisms to a subgroup involving reparameterizations of the hypersurface orthogonal to the dissipation vector. This may generate new degrees of freedom or alter the constraint structure of the theory.

Recent work has highlighted the importance of noise constraints in correctly matching the number of advanced fields that are dynamically coupled to the physical ones after gauge fixing
Salcedo:2025ezu
. If more advanced fields exist than gauge-fixed physical fields, the equations of motion form an overdetermined system, potentially leading to inconsistencies; recall that the physical EOM are obtained by varying with respect to the advanced fields. For the theory to be consistent, identities must exist among these EOM to reduce the number of independent equations. We now demonstrate that such identities are present in simple open extensions, thus guaranteeing the consistency of open gravitational EFTs.

To examine the effect of open terms on the constraints of the theory we start with a modification of Einstein’s equations

E_{\mu\nu}=G_{\mu\nu}+\Delta_{\mu\nu}\,,

(3.2)

and encode diffeomorphism breaking via a non-covariantly conserved
\Delta_{\mu\nu}
leading to a non-zero source
J_{\nu}
,

\nabla_{\mu}E^{\mu}_{~\nu}=\nabla_{\mu}\Delta^{\mu}_{~\nu}\equiv J_{\nu}\,.

(3.3)

In certain cases the divergence equation can be interpreted as a vector, and we can construct a unit vector
u_{\nu}\propto J_{\nu}
which allows us to perform 1+3 split of spacetime (assuming it is not a null vector). The projector is

P_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}-\epsilon u_{\mu}u_{\nu}\,,

(3.4)

with
\epsilon=\pm 1
for spacelike/timelike unit vectors. Projecting the divergence equation on the orthogonal directions yields

P^{\nu}_{~\kappa}\nabla_{\mu}E^{\mu}_{~\nu}=0\,.

(3.5)

which provides us with three identities.

Upon initial inspection, it is not clear whether a fourth identity exists. In cases where
\Delta_{\mu\nu}
takes a simple form, an additional identity can be found by algebraic and differential manipulations of the EOM. For the simplest term that is built entirely from the metric and curvature tensor,

\Delta_{\mu\nu}=\Gamma Rg_{\mu\nu}\,,

(3.6)

we can combine the trace of (
3.2
)

E=(4\Gamma-1)R\,,

(3.7)

with the divergence equation (
3.2
) and find the
deformed identity

\nabla_{\mu}E^{\mu}_{~\nu}={\Gamma\over 4\Gamma-1}\left(E_{\alpha\beta}g^{\alpha\beta}\right)_{,\nu}\,,

(3.8)

for
\Gamma\neq 1/4
; for
\Gamma=1/4
the trace of the EOM vanishes which gives the fourth relation. Similarly to the superfluid and Maxwell theory, these identities can also be derived by requiring invariance of the dynamical part of the action under advanced deformed diffeomorphisms. Contracting with a vector
\xi^{\nu}
and integrating by parts yields the advanced diffeomorphism transformation

\delta g_{\rm a}^{\mu\nu}=-2\nabla^{(\mu}\xi^{\nu)}+g^{\mu\nu}{\Gamma\over 4\Gamma-1}\nabla_{\alpha}\xi^{\alpha}\,,

(3.9)

which reduces to a standard diffeomorphism when
\Gamma=0
.

With these identities at our disposal, we can derive noise constraints in gravity. Adding the noise part in the action

S_{\rm n}=\int\sqrt{-g}\,g_{\rm a}^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{N}_{\mu\nu\kappa\lambda}g_{\rm a}^{\kappa\lambda}\,,

(3.10)

and using the previous identities we find four noise constraints on the advanced metric components

\nabla^{\mu}\Xi_{\mu\nu}={\Gamma\over 4\Gamma-1}\left(g^{\alpha\beta}\Xi_{\alpha\beta}\right)_{,\nu}\,.

(3.11)

where
\Xi_{\mu\nu}\equiv\left(\mathcal{N}_{\mu\nu\kappa\lambda}+\mathcal{N}_{\mu\nu\kappa\lambda}^{\dagger}\right)g_{\rm a}^{\kappa\lambda}
. These relations ensure that only six advanced components dynamically couple to the physical metric components.

The previous example had the crucial property that the dissipative term is related through algebraic manipulations to Einstein’s tensor. Introducing additional structure in our theory, such as a unit timelike vector (
n^{\mu}
), can still allow for linear identities between the EOM when
\Delta_{\mu\nu}
is built from the Einstein tensor and the above ingredients, i.e. when considering the following dissipative term

\Delta_{\mu\nu}=Ag_{\mu\nu}+Bn_{\mu}n_{\nu}+\gamma_{3}n^{\kappa}G_{\kappa(\mu}n_{\nu)}\,,

(3.12)

with

A=\Gamma_{1}R+\Gamma_{2}G_{\rm nn}\,,\qquad B=\gamma_{1}R+\gamma_{2}G_{\rm nn}\,.

(3.13)

Any
\Delta_{\mu\nu}
built in this way can be related to
G_{\mu\nu}
via contractions with the metric or the normal vector. We will illustrate this for the more involved term
\gamma_{3}
and the other two can be treated similarly. This term resembles the Maxwell extension we explored in sec.

where the leading deformation is the projection of

along the dissipation vector

F

Setting
\Gamma_{1,2}

\gamma_{1,2}

G_{\rm nn}

E_{\rm nn}=(1-\gamma_{3})G_{\rm nn}\,.

(3.14)

Taking one projection along
n^{\mu}

n^{\kappa}E_{\kappa(\mu}n_{\nu)}={\gamma_{3}\over 2}G_{\rm nn}n_{\mu}n_{\nu}+\left(1-{\gamma_{3}\over 2}\right)n^{\kappa}G_{\kappa(\mu}n_{\nu)}\,,

(3.15)

hence, we obtain

\Delta_{\mu\nu}=\left(1-{\gamma_{3}\over 2}\right)^{-1}\gamma_{3}\left(n^{\kappa}E_{\kappa(\mu}n_{\nu)}-{\gamma_{3}\over 2(1-\gamma_{3})}E_{\rm nn}n_{\mu}n_{\nu}\right)\,,

(3.16)

for
\gamma_{3}\neq 1,2

Substituting this expression into the divergence equation (

Note that we obtain four noise constraints instead of three which is the number of allowed coordinate transformations on the hypersurface orthogonal to

Finally, it is worth considering what happens when
\Delta_{\mu\nu}
is an arbitrary function of
R_{\mu\nu},K_{\mu\nu},g_{\mu\nu},n_{\mu}
, where
K_{\mu\nu}\equiv P_{\mu}^{\kappa}P_{\nu}^{\lambda}\nabla_{\kappa}n_{\lambda}

\Delta_{\mu\nu}=K_{\mu\nu}
it is not possible to relate it to

What can be done in this case is to expand the EOM around a given background to linear order and separate the scalar from the vector degrees (the tensor modes are not present in the divergence equation) and derive two sets of equations for the scalar and vector modes with the goal of finding relations between them. The existence of relations between the EOM can be inferred by noticing that the scalar part of
\delta K_{ij}
can be expressed in terms of
\delta G_{\mu\nu}
through non-local expressions when accompanied with appropriate boundary conditions. These relations in combination with the EOM including the advanced fields imply on-shell constraints that reduce the number of advanced fields that couple to the dynamical variables and ensure the self-consistency of the SK action.

4
Conclusions

Our results suggest a unifying picture: when advanced symmetries are broken, but physical symmetries are preserved, open systems exhibit emergent structures. In the following, we summarise our main findings.

In sec.
2
we examined local open extensions of the superfluid and Maxwell action in the Schwinger-Keldysh (SK) framework. Following the standard approach of breaking the advanced symmetry but keeping the physical symmetry intact, we observe a deformation (compared to the closed theory) of conservation laws. These take the form of average current conservation in the superfluid case and a deformed identity between the equations of motion (EOM) in open Maxwell theory. In both cases, these structures emerge because of the way the dynamical part of the action becomes deformed once open terms are added.

Finally, in sec.
3
we explored open gravitational systems. Breaking the diffeomorphism symmetry introduces extra terms in the SK action, with a much richer structure than open Maxwell theory. In certain cases, we are able to derive the exact form of the deformed identities between the EOM. Although simple closed-form expressions may not exist for general dissipative terms, we argued that it is always possible to find relations between the EOM to linear order in metric perturbations. A promising direction for extending this work is to examine the role of these constraints in deriving the power spectrum of scalar perturbations in the open EFT of inflation beyond the decoupling limit
LopezNacir:2011kk
;
Salcedo:2024smn
.
We plan to address this in a forthcoming publication.

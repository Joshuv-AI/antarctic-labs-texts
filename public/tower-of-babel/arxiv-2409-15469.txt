Multisections of ( m + 3 ) (m+3) –dimensional m m –spun 3 3 –manifolds
Rudy Dissler

Abstract
A multisection, or n n –section, of an ( n + 1 ) (n+1) –dimensional manifold is a decomposition of this manifold into n n 1 1 –handlebodies of dimension n + 1 n+1 , such that all these handlebodies intersect along a closed surface, and every subcollection of k k handlebodies intersects along an ( n − k + 2 ) (n-k+2) –dimensional 1 1 –handlebody. This concept, due to Ben Aribi, Courte, Golla and Moussard [ BACGM23 ] , generalizes to any dimension Heegaard splittings and Gay–Kirby trisections. Any ( n + 1 ) (n+1) –manifold admits a multisection if n ≤ 4 n\leq 4 (see [ GK16 ] and [ BACGM23 ] ). But there are yet no general existence results for n ≥ 5 n\geq 5 . In this article, we provide a class of examples of multisected manifolds in all dimensions. We extend the concept of 4 4 –dimensional spun manifolds to any dimension, and construct multisections and their associated multisection diagrams for the class of m m –spun 3 3 –manifolds, of dimension m + 3 m+3 , for any m m . This allows us to give infinitely many examples of non-diffeomorphic multisected manifolds, in all dimensions.

Introduction

Throughout this article, we will refer to
1
–handlebodies (i.e. connected manifolds consisting in only

1

We will consider in this article an alternative notion of multisection, from Ben Aribi, Courte, Golla and Moussard

]

]

Remark 2.6
.

We can readily see from Definition

When constructing multisections of spun manifolds, we will first come up with a decomposition that matches all the criteria for a smooth multisection but one: the pieces are in fact handlebodies with smooth interior which are not submanifolds with corners, as their boundary presents some singularities not diffeomorphic to any type of corner. We now give a proper definition for such a decomposition.

Definition 2.7
.

For

, let

be an

–dimensional closed smooth manifold. An

•

•

of

Remark 2.8
.

An almost smooth multisection induces a stratification of its pieces:
Y_{I}=\sqcup_{J\supseteq I}\mathring{Y}_{J}
, as well as an almost smooth multisection of the boundary of a smoothed piece:
\partial(F_{I}(Y_{I},1))=\cup_{J\supset I}F_{I}(Y_{J},1)
.

Remark 2.9
.

A smooth multisection is an almost smoothed multisection, as shown in

, Section 2]

An interesting feature of multisections is that they can be described diagrammatically.

Definition 2.11

A multisection diagram is not unique, but each system of curves

H_{k}(S_{1}(M))=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}&\text{if $k=0,4$}\\
H_{1}(M)&\text{if $k=1$}\\
H_{1}(M)\oplus H_{2}(M)&\text{if $k=2$}\\
H_{2}(M)&\text{if $k=3$}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

Proof.

We have
H_{k}(M^{o})=H_{k}(M)
,
0\leq k\leq 2
, and
H_{k}(M^{o})=0
,
k\geq 3
. Then according to the Künneth Theorem, we have, for
m>2
:

H_{k}(M^{o}\times S^{m})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}&\text{if $k=0,m$}\\
H_{1}(M)&\text{if $k=1,m+1$}\\
H_{2}(M)&\text{if $k=2,m+2$}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

and also:

H_{k}(S^{2}\times S^{m})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}&\text{if $k=0,2,m,m+2$}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

Then we can use the Mayer–Vietoris sequence to compute the homology of
\mathcal{S}_{m}(M)=(M^{o}\times S^{m})\cup_{S^{2}\times S^{m}}(S^{2}\times B^{m+1})
:

H_{k}(S^{2}\times S^{m})\rightarrow H_{k}(S^{2})\oplus H_{k}(M^{o}\times S^{m})\rightarrow H_{k}(S_{m}M)\rightarrow H_{k-1}(S^{2}\times S^{m})

The case
m=1
and
m=2
can be dealt with in the same way, using:

H_{k}(M^{o}\times S^{1})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}&\text{if $k=0$}\\
H_{1}(M)\oplus\mathbb{Z}&\text{if $k=1$}\\
H_{1}(M)\oplus H_{2}(M)&\text{if $k=2$}\\
H_{2}(M)&\text{if $k=3$}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

and:

H_{k}(M^{o}\times S^{2})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}&\text{if $k=0$}\\
H_{1}(M)&\text{if $k=1,3$}\\
H_{2}(M)\oplus\mathbb{Z}&\text{if $k=2$}\\
H_{2}(M)&\text{if $k=4$}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

∎

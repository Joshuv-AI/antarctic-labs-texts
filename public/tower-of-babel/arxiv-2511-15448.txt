Continuity of total signature maps for Azumaya algebras with involution
Vincent Astier, Thomas Unger

Abstract
. In this paper we continue our investigation of signatures of hermitian forms over Azumaya algebras with involution over commutative rings. We show that the approach used in an earlier paper for central simple algebras can be extended to Azumaya algebras and leads to a natural way of choosing the signature of a hermitian form at a given ordering, producing total signatures of hermitian forms that are continuous functions on the real spectrum of the base ring.

16H05, 11E39, 13J30, 16W10, 06F25

1.
Introduction

In
[
6
]
we showed that signatures of hermitian forms can be
defined for Azumaya algebras with involution over a commutative ring
R
, and
used them to prove a version of Sylvester’s inertia theorem and, in case
R
is
semilocal, Pfister’s local-global principle. The definition of the
signature at
\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R
relies on the application of a Morita
equivalence which, when chosen differently, can change the sign of the result.
This is a consequence of a reduction to the central simple case, cf.

[
1
]
. There does not seem to be a direct canonical way to choose
this Morita equivalence, which presents a problem if we consider the total
signature map of a hermitian form, and want to obtain a continuous function
defined on
\operatorname{Sper}R
.

In this paper we show that the approach used in
[
1
]
for central
simple algebras with involution can be extended to Azumaya algebras with
involution: The sign of the signature can be chosen with the help of a
“reference form” (Section
5
), and this definition of
signature leads to total signatures of hermitian forms that are continuous
(Section
6
).

The results in this paper are very similar to those obtained for central simple
algebras with involution, but in many cases the existing proofs could not
simply be extended to Azumaya algebras. This required the introduction of new
tools, occasionally leading to simpler arguments. One such promising new tool
is obtained in Section
3.3
via a further investigation of a
pairing of hermitian forms, studied by Garrel in the central simple case, cf.

[
10
]
.

In the special case of central simple algebras with involution, we recall that

is such an algebra, then

A hermitian module over
(A,\sigma)
is a pair

is a finitely generated projective right

-module and

For

Note that not every hermitian form is isometric to a diagonal hermitian form.
We denote isometry of forms by

.

Let

, cf.

[

, and recall that it is additive and

-linear.
Furthermore,

[

also commutes with scalar

Lemma 2.4

(
[
6
, Lemma 4.1]
)
.

Let

be a commutative ring that contains

. Then for all

,

\Trd_{A}(a)\otimes_{R}1_{R^{\prime}}=\Trd_{A\otimes_{R}R^{\prime}}(a\otimes_{R}1_{R^{\prime}}).

We define the trace quadratic form of

as follows:

T_{A}:A\to Z(A),\ x\mapsto\Trd_{A}(x^{2}).

The associated symmetric bilinear form is
A\times A\to Z(A),(x,y)\mapsto\Trd_{A}(xy)
. We note that

is nonsingular (the proof uses the same
arguments as the proof of
[
6
, Lemma 4.3]
).

We denote the real spectrum of

\mathring{H}(r):=\{\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R\mid r>0\text{ at }\alpha\}=\{\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R\mid r\in\alpha\setminus-\alpha\}

We recall that there is a second, finer, topology on

(for

The space

[

Let

.
The support of

is the prime ideal

, and
we denote

•

by

•

by

,

•

and by

.

We also define

(A(\alpha),\sigma(\alpha)):=(A\otimes_{R}\kappa(\alpha),\sigma\otimes\id)

and

(A_{\alpha},\sigma_{\alpha}):=(A\otimes_{R}k(\alpha),\sigma\otimes\id).

Clearly,
(A_{\alpha},\sigma_{\alpha})=(A(\alpha),\sigma(\alpha))\otimes_{\kappa(\alpha)}k(\alpha)
.

Remark 2.5
.

(1)

Recall from
[
9
, Second paragraph of Section 1.4]

(2)

If

is a central simple

(in which case

(3)

In particular, we obtain from the

is a central simple

-algebra with involution and that

is a central simple

-algebra with involution, both in the sense

Since

3.
\mathscr{M}
-signatures

We recall the following definition from
[
6
]
:

Definition 3.1
.

Let
h
be a hermitian form over
(A,\sigma)
and let
\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R
.
Then
h\otimes\kappa(\alpha)
is a hermitian form over
the central simple algebra with involution
(A(\alpha),\sigma(\alpha))

and we define
the
\mathscr{M}
-signature
of
h
at
\alpha
by

\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}h:=\sign^{\mathscr{M}_{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\bar{\alpha}}(h\otimes_{R}\kappa(\alpha)),

where
\mathscr{M}_{\bar{\alpha}}
is a Morita equivalence as in

[
1
, Section 3.2]
; see

[
6
, Definition 3.1]
and the discussion following it.

Remark 3.2
.

The
\mathscr{M}
-signature
\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}
inherits all the properties from the
map
\sign^{\mathscr{M}_{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\bar{\alpha}}
. For instance, it is additive
and
\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}(q\otimes h)=\sign_{\alpha}q\cdot\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}h
if

q
is quadratic over
R
and
h
hermitian over
(A,\sigma)
, cf.

[
1
, Proposition 3.6]
.

The main drawback of the
\mathscr{M}
-signature at
\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R
is that
there is no canonical choice of Morita equivalence
\mathscr{M}_{\bar{\alpha}}
,
and that an arbitrary change in the sign of the signature of a form
can be obtained by taking a different Morita equivalence (cf.

[
1
, Proposition 3.4 and first paragraph of Section 3.3]
).
This is in particular a problem
if we hope to consider the total signature of a form as a continuous
function on
\operatorname{Sper}R
.
This problem is solved with the definition of
\eta
-signature in
Section
5
.

Remark 3.3
.

If there are several instances of
\mathscr{M}
-signatures of forms over
(A,\sigma)
in
a statement or formula, we use the same choice of Morita equivalence for each
of them.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
(M,h)
be a hermitian form over
(A,\sigma)
. There is
k\in\mathbb{N}
depending only on
A
and
M
, such that
|\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}h|\leq k
for every
\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R
.

Proof.

Let
S
be a finite set of generators of
M
over
A
. Since
A
is
projective over
R
, the rank function on
A
is continuous
(cf.
[
20
, p. 12]
)
and thus takes only
finitely many values
n_{1}^{2},\ldots,n_{t}^{2}
(which are all squares). Let
N=\mathrm{lcm}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{t})
.
The form
N\times h\otimes_{R}\kappa(\alpha)
is defined over
(A(\alpha),\sigma(\alpha))
, where
A(\alpha)
has degree
n_{i}
for some
1\leq i\leq t
. By

[
4
, Lemma 2.2]
(and since
n_{i}
, and thus
N
, are multiples of
the matrix size of
A(\alpha)
), there exist
r\in\mathbb{N}
and

a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\in\Sym(A(\alpha)^{\times},\sigma(\alpha))\cup\{0\}

such that
N\times h\otimes_{R}\kappa(\alpha)\simeq\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\rangle_{\sigma(\alpha)}
. By considering the underlying modules of both forms, it
follows that
r\leq\dim_{\kappa(\alpha)}(M^{N}\otimes_{R}\kappa(\alpha))\leq N\cdot|S|
, and thus

\displaystyle|\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}h|

\displaystyle\leq N\cdot|\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}h|

\displaystyle=N\cdot|\sign^{\mathscr{M}_{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\bar{\alpha}}h\otimes\kappa(\alpha)|

\displaystyle=|\sign^{\mathscr{M}_{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\bar{\alpha}}\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\rangle_{\sigma(\alpha)}|

\displaystyle\leq r\cdot n_{\bar{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq N\cdot|S|\cdot n_{\bar{\alpha}},

where the penultimate inequality comes from
[
3
, Proposition 4.4(iii)]
. The
result follows by the definition of
n_{\bar{\alpha}}
(cf.
Remark
2.5
or

[
3
, p. 352]
since clearly
n_{\bar{\alpha}}\leq n_{i}
for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
).
∎

We

\displaystyle\Nil[A,\sigma]

\displaystyle:=\{\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}(R)\mid\sign_{\alpha}^{\mathscr{M}}=0\}

\displaystyle\phantom{:}=\{\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}(R)\mid\sign^{\mathscr{M}_{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\bar{\alpha}}=0\}\text{ by \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{A-U-Az-PLG}{}{}, Lemma~3.7]}}.

Remark 3.6
.

We use the notation from Remark

For

\alpha\in\Nil[A,\sigma]
if and only if

D_{\alpha}=\begin{cases}(-1,-1)_{k(\alpha)}&\text{ if $\sigma_{\alpha}$ is orthogonal,}\\

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
F
be a field (of characteristic not
2
).

(1)

For
a,b\in F^{\times}
let

(a,b)_{F}
denote the quaternion
F
-algebra with generators
i,j

such that
i^{2}=a,j^{2}=b,ij=-ji=:k
.
If
A=(a,b)_{F}
with
a,b\in F^{\times}
, then

T_{A}\simeq\langle 2,2a,2b,-2ab\rangle.

(2)

If
A=M_{n}(F)
with
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then

T_{A}\simeq n\times\langle 1\rangle\perp\frac{n(n-1)}{2}\times\langle-1,1\rangle.

Proof.

The proof of these well-known facts is by straightforward computation, see

[
16
]
for example.

(1) Let
\alpha=x_{0}+x_{1}i+x_{2}j+x_{3}k\in A
. Then
\Trd_{A}(\alpha)=2x_{0}
.
The
F
-basis
\{1,i,j,k\}
of

is orthogonal for
T_{A}
and we get

T_{A}\simeq\langle 2,2a,2b,-2ab\rangle
.

(2) Let
\alpha=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\alpha_{ij}e_{ij}\in A
, where

\{e_{ij}\}_{i,j=1,\ldots,n}
is the standard matrix basis of

. Then

\Trd_{A}(\alpha)=\tr(\alpha)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{ii}
and

T_{A}\simeq n\times\langle 1\rangle\perp\frac{n(n-1)}{2}\times\langle-1,1\rangle
, where

comes from

, while the pairs

\{e_{ij},e_{ji}\}_{i\not=j}
contribute the
\frac{n(n-1)}{2}
hyperbolic
planes.
∎

Lemma 3.10

\displaystyle C_{5}

Proof.

The second statement is in
[

We could not find a reference for the first statement,

is finitely generated projective, there
are elements

[

Since the canonical map

[

M
is free (by replacing

by

). Let

be the matrix of

in a fixed basis of

over

h
be a hermitian form over
(S,\vartheta)
. The total signature map

\sign_{\bullet}h:\operatorname{Sper}R\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is continuous for the constructible topology on
\operatorname{Sper}R
.
Furthermore, if
h
is nonsingular, then
\sign_{\bullet}h
is continuous
for the Harrison topology on
\operatorname{Sper}R
.

Proof.

According to equation (
3.1
), we have
\sign_{\alpha}h=0
if

S\otimes_{R}\kappa(\alpha)\cong\kappa(\alpha)\times\kappa(\alpha)
, i.e., if

\alpha\in\Nil[S,\vartheta]
(since
\vartheta
is of the second kind by
definition). Since
\Nil[S,\vartheta]
is clopen (for the Harrison topology) by
Theorem
3.11
, it suffices to consider
\sign_{\bullet}h
on the
set
\operatorname{Sper}R\setminus\Nil[S,\vartheta]
. On this set
\sign_{\bullet}h
is
defined by equation (
3.2
) and the result follows by
Proposition
4.1
.
∎

As a consequence we obtain the following result for hermitian forms over

(A,\sigma)
:

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
h
be a hermitian form over
(A,\sigma)
. Then

|\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\bullet}h|=\frac{1}{\lambda_{\bullet}}\sqrt{\sign_{\bullet}(h*h)}

on
\operatorname{Sper}R
. In particular,
|\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\bullet}h|
is
continuous for the constructible topology on
\operatorname{Sper}R
,
and for the Harrison topology if
h
is nonsingular.

Proof.

The first statement follows directly from Theorem
3.13
.
The second statement from the fact that
\lambda_{\bullet}
is continuous
and Proposition
4.2
, since
h*h
is
a hermitian form over
(Z(A),\iota)
, which is nonsingular if
h
is
(as recalled in Section
3.3
).
∎

5.
Reference forms and
\eta
-signatures

As observed after Definition
3.1
, the sign of the
\mathscr{M}
-signature
at
\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R\setminus\Nil[A,\sigma]
can be changed by taking a
different Morita equivalence. One way to make a choice of sign is to fix a
hermitian form
\eta
whose
\mathscr{M}
-signature at
\alpha

is known to be nonzero, and to choose a Morita equivalence that makes the

\mathscr{M}
-signature at
\alpha
of
\eta
positive.
Therefore:

Definition 5.1
.

A hermitian form
\eta
over
(A,\sigma)
is called a

reference form for
(A,\sigma)
if

\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}\eta\not=0
for all
\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R\setminus\Nil[A,\sigma]
.

In this section we will show that reference forms always exist for
Azumaya algebras with involution over commutative rings.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
and let
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
be the field of fractions of

R/\mathfrak{p}
.
Any diagonal hermitian form over
(A\otimes_{R}\kappa(\mathfrak{p}),\sigma\otimes\id_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})})
is of
the form
h\otimes_{R}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
, for some
diagonal hermitian form
h
over
(A,\sigma)
.

Proof.

We write
\sigma(\mathfrak{p}):=\sigma\otimes\id_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}
.
It suffices to prove the statement for the form
\varphi:=\langle a\rangle_{\sigma(\mathfrak{p})}
,
where
a=\sum_{i}a_{i}\otimes({\overline{r_{i}}}/{\overline{s_{i}}})
and
\overline{r_{i}},\overline{s_{i}}
are in
(R/\mathfrak{p})\setminus\{0\}
.
After multiplying
a
by the (hermitian) square
1\otimes(\prod_{i}\overline{s_{i}})^{2}
,
we obtain
\varphi\simeq\langle\sum_{i}a_{i}\otimes\overline{r^{\prime}_{i}}\rangle_{\sigma(\mathfrak{p})}
for some
\overline{r^{\prime}_{i}}\in R/\mathfrak{p}
. Now
using that
1\otimes\overline{r^{\prime}_{i}}=r^{\prime}_{i}\otimes 1
, we obtain
\varphi\simeq\langle(\sum_{i}a_{i}r^{\prime}_{i})\otimes 1\rangle_{\sigma(\mathfrak{p})}=\langle\sum_{i}a_{i}r^{\prime}_{i}\rangle_{\sigma}\otimes\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
. ∎

Lemma 5.3
.

For every
\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R\setminus\Nil[A,\sigma]
, there is
a (diagonal) hermitian form
\eta_{\alpha}
over
(A,\sigma)
such that

\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}\eta_{\alpha}\not=0
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R\setminus\Nil[A,\sigma]
.
Then
\bar{\alpha}\not\in\Nil[A(\alpha),\sigma(\alpha)]
by

[
6
, Lemma 3.7]
.
By

[
1
, Theorem 6.4]

there is a diagonal
hermitian form
\varphi_{\alpha}
over
(A(\alpha),\sigma(\alpha))
such that

\sign^{\mathscr{M}_{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\bar{\alpha}}\varphi_{\alpha}\not=0
. Thus, by
Lemma
5.2
with
\mathfrak{p}=\Supp(\alpha)
,
there is a (diagonal) form
\eta_{\alpha}
over
(A,\sigma)
such that
\eta_{\alpha}\otimes_{R}\kappa(\alpha)=\varphi_{\alpha}
. Therefore,
\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}\eta_{\alpha}=\sign^{\mathscr{M}_{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\bar{\alpha}}\varphi_{\alpha}\not=0
.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
U\subseteq\operatorname{Sper}R
be clopen for the constructible topology. Then
there is a quadratic form
q
over
R
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\sign_{\bullet}q=0

on
U
and
\sign_{\bullet}q=2^{k}
on
(\operatorname{Sper}R)\setminus U
.

Proof.

By
[
11
, Theorem 3.4.5]
,

U
is a constructible subset of
\operatorname{Sper}R
, i.e., a
finite boolean combination of sets of the form
\mathring{H}(a)
for
a\in R
. We proceed by induction on this boolean combination:

a)

Let
U=\mathring{H}(a)
. Consider
q:=\langle a\rangle
. Then

\sign_{\bullet}q=1
on

\mathring{H}(a)
,
0
on
\{\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R\mid a\in\alpha\cap-\alpha\}
and
-1
on
\{\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R\mid a\in-\alpha\setminus\alpha\}
. Inspired by the observation that the polynomial
-x^{2}+x+2

takes value
0
at
x=-1
, and value
2
at
x=0
and
x=1
, we let

q^{\prime}:=-q\otimes q\perp q\perp\langle 1,1\rangle
. Then

\sign_{\bullet}q^{\prime}=0
on
\mathring{H}(a)
and
\sign_{\bullet}q^{\prime}=2
on

(\operatorname{Sper}R)\setminus\mathring{H}(a)
.

b)

Let
U=V_{1}\cup V_{2}
. By induction there are quadratic forms
q_{1},q_{2}

such that
\sign_{\bullet}q_{i}
equals
0
on
V_{i}
and
2^{k_{i}}
on

(\operatorname{Sper}R)\setminus V_{i}
for

i=1,2
. Let

q^{\prime}:=q_{1}\otimes q_{2}
. Then
\sign_{\bullet}q^{\prime}
equals
0
on
V_{1}\cup V_{2}
and
2^{k_{1}+k_{2}}
on
(\operatorname{Sper}R)\setminus(V_{1}\cup V_{2})
.

c)

Let
U=(\operatorname{Sper}R)\setminus V
. By induction there is a quadratic form

q
such that
\sign_{\bullet}q
equals
0
on
V
and
2^{k}
on
(\operatorname{Sper}R)\setminus V
.
Let
q^{\prime}:=2^{k}\times\langle 1\rangle\perp-q
. Then
\sign_{\bullet}q^{\prime}
equals

0
on
U
and
2^{k}

on
(\operatorname{Sper}R)\setminus U
.∎

Theorem 5.5
.

There exists a reference form
\eta
for
(A,\sigma)
such that

|\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\bullet}\eta|
is a constant nonzero function on
\operatorname{Sper}R
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R\setminus\Nil[A,\sigma]
. By Lemma
5.3
, there
exists a hermitian form
\eta_{\alpha}
over
(A,\sigma)
such that

s_{\alpha}:=|\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}\eta_{\alpha}|>0
. By
Corollary
4.3
, the set

U_{\alpha}:=|\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\bullet}\eta_{\alpha}|^{-1}(s_{\alpha})

is clopen in
\operatorname{Sper}R
for the constructible topology.
Therefore, by compactness of
\operatorname{Sper}R\setminus\Nil[A,\sigma]
(cf.
Theorem
3.11
), there are clopen sets
U_{i}
and hermitian forms

\eta_{i}
over
(A,\sigma)
, for
i=1,\ldots,k
(for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
) such that

\operatorname{Sper}R\setminus\Nil[A,\sigma]=U_{1}\cup\cdots\cup U_{k}
and

|\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\bullet}\eta_{i}|=:s_{i}>0
on
U_{i}
. Up to removing their
intersections, we can assume that
U_{1},\ldots,U_{k}
are disjoint. Moreover,
up to replacing each hermitian form
\eta_{i}
by sufficiently many copies of
itself, we may assume that
s_{1}=\cdots=s_{k}=:s
.

By Lemma
5.4
, there are quadratic forms
q_{i}
over
R
such that

\sign_{\bullet}q_{i}
equals
2^{k_{i}}
on
U_{i}
and
0
on
(\operatorname{Sper}R)\setminus U_{i}
. Again, up to replacing each quadratic form
q_{i}
by sufficiently many
copies of itself, we can assume that
\sign_{\bullet}q_{i}
equals
2^{k}
on

U_{i}
and
0
on
(\operatorname{Sper}R)\setminus U_{i}
.

Therefore the hermitian form
\displaystyle\midperp_{i=1}^{k}q_{i}\otimes\eta_{i}

has the required property, since its signature at any
\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R
is

2^{k}s
.
∎

Definition 5.6
.

Let
\eta
be a reference form for
(A,\sigma)
and let
h
be a hermitian form
over
(A,\sigma)
. For
\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R
we define the
\eta
-signature
of
h
at
\alpha
by

\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}h:=\sgn(\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}\eta)\cdot\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}h,

where
\sgn:\mathbb{Z}\to\{-1,1\}
denotes the sign function.

Remark 5.7
.

Observe that:

(1)

We have
\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}\eta>0
whenever
\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R\setminus\Nil[A,\sigma]
, and that
\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}
is a special case of

\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}
since it can be obtained from it for a well-chosen
Morita equivalence.

(2)

If
\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}h\not=0
, then

\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}h>0\Leftrightarrow(\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}\eta\text{ and }\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}h\text{ have the same sign}).

Proposition 5.8
.

With notation as in Definition
5.6
, the value of

\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}h

is independent of the choice of the Morita equivalence used in the computation
of
\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}h
.

Proof.

The statement is trivially true if
\alpha\in\Nil[A,\sigma]
, so we may assume
that

\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R\setminus\Nil[A,\sigma]
.
Let
\mathscr{N}
be another Morita equivalence in the definition of

\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}h
. Then, using that the signature is computed by
first extending scalars to
\kappa(\alpha)
, and then that

(A(\alpha),\sigma(\alpha))
is a central simple algebra with involution,
we
obtain by
[
1
, Lemma 3.8]
that

\displaystyle\sgn(\sign^{\mathscr{N}}_{\alpha}\eta)\cdot\sign^{\mathscr{N}}_{\alpha}h

\displaystyle=\sgn(\sign^{\mathscr{N}_{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\bar{\alpha}}(\eta\otimes\kappa(\alpha)))\cdot\sign^{\mathscr{N}_{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\bar{\alpha}}(h\otimes\kappa(\alpha))

\displaystyle=\sgn(\sign^{\mathscr{M}_{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\bar{\alpha}}(\eta\otimes\kappa(\alpha)))\cdot\sign^{\mathscr{M}_{\bar{\alpha}}}_{\bar{\alpha}}(h\otimes\kappa(\alpha))

\displaystyle=\sgn(\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}\eta)\cdot\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}h.\qed

Proposition 5.9
.

Assume we have ring morphisms
f:R\to L
and
g:R\to N

where
L
and
N
are real closed fields such that

f^{-1}(L_{\geq 0})=g^{-1}(N_{\geq 0})=:\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R
.
Then, for any hermitian form
h

over
(A,\sigma)
, we have

\sign^{\eta\otimes_{f}L}(h\otimes_{f}L)=\sign^{\eta\otimes_{g}N}(h\otimes_{g}N)

with respect to the unique orderings on
L
and
N
.

Proof.

Since

\Supp(\alpha)\subseteq\Ker f
,
f
induces a map
\bar{f}:\kappa(\alpha)\rightarrow L
and, similarly,
g
induces a map
\bar{g}:\kappa(\alpha)\rightarrow N
, so that both triangles in the following diagram commute:

\textstyle{R\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho_{\alpha}}
\scriptstyle{f}
\scriptstyle{g}
\textstyle{\kappa(\alpha)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\bar{f}}
\scriptstyle{\bar{g}}
\textstyle{L}
\textstyle{N}

By definition of signature, we have
\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}h=\sign^{\eta\otimes\kappa(\alpha)}_{\bar{\alpha}}(h\otimes_{R}\kappa(\alpha))
. Applying

[
7
, Theorem 2.9]
, we obtain

\sign^{\eta\otimes\kappa(\alpha)}_{\bar{\alpha}}(h\otimes_{R}\kappa(\alpha))=\sign^{\eta\otimes\kappa(\alpha)\otimes_{\bar{f}}L}\bigl((h\otimes_{R}\kappa(\alpha))\otimes_{\bar{f}}L\bigr)=\sign^{\eta\otimes_{f}L}(h\otimes_{f}L),

and

\sign^{\eta\otimes\kappa(\alpha)}_{\bar{\alpha}}(h\otimes_{R}\kappa(\alpha))=\sign^{\eta\otimes\kappa(\alpha)\otimes_{\bar{g}}N}\bigl((h\otimes_{R}\kappa(\alpha))\otimes_{\bar{g}}N\bigr)=\sign^{\eta\otimes_{g}N}(h\otimes_{g}N),

proving the result.
∎

6.
Continuity of the total signature map

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\eta
be a reference form for
(A,\sigma)
. Then for any hermitian form
h

over
(A,\sigma)
the total signature map
\sign^{\eta}_{\bullet}h:\operatorname{Sper}R\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is continuous with respect to the constructible topology
on
\operatorname{Sper}R
and the discrete topology on
\mathbb{Z}
.

If
\eta
and
h
are nonsingular, then the total signature map

\sign^{\eta}_{\bullet}h:\operatorname{Sper}R\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is continuous
with respect to the Harrison topology on
\operatorname{Sper}R
and the discrete topology
on
\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

We prove the first statement (for the constructible topology), the same proof

mutatis mutandis
gives the second one.

By definition of
\Nil[A,\sigma]
and since
\Nil[A,\sigma]
is clopen, cf.
Theorem
3.11
, it suffices to show that the map

\sign^{\eta}_{\bullet}h
is continuous on
\operatorname{Sper}R\setminus\Nil[A,\sigma]
. The
map
\sign^{\eta}_{\bullet}\eta
only takes a finite number of values on
\operatorname{Sper}R\setminus\Nil[A,\sigma]
(see Lemma
3.4
), say
k_{1},\ldots,k_{\ell}
, which are all positive by Remark
5.7
(1).
Let

\displaystyle O_{i}

\displaystyle:=\{\alpha\in(\operatorname{Sper}R)\setminus\Nil[A,\sigma]\mid\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}\eta=k_{i}\}

\displaystyle\phantom{:}=\{\alpha\in(\operatorname{Sper}R)\setminus\Nil[A,\sigma]\mid|\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}\eta|=k_{i}\}.

Then

O_{i}
is open
by Corollary
4.3
and
(\operatorname{Sper}R)\setminus\Nil[A,\sigma]

is
the disjoint union of the sets
O_{1},\ldots,O_{\ell}
. Therefore it suffices to
show that the map
\sign^{\eta}_{\bullet}h
is continuous from
O_{i}
to
\mathbb{Z}
.
But, for
\alpha\in O_{i}
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\displaystyle\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}h=r

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow k_{i}\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}h=k_{i}r

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}(k_{i}\times h)=\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}(r\times\eta)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}(k_{i}\times h-r\times\eta)=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow|\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}(k_{i}\times h-r\times\eta)|=0,

so that

(\sign^{\eta}_{\bullet}h)^{-1}(r)\cap O_{i}=\{\alpha\in O_{i}\mid|\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}(k_{i}\times h-r\times\eta)|=0\},

which is open by Corollary
4.3
.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

At the moment we do not know if it is always possible to find a reference form

\eta
such that
\sign_{\bullet}^{\eta}h
is continuous for the Harrison
topology, for all nonsingular hermitian forms
h
over
(A,\sigma)
(it is
certainly possible to do this in some special cases).
The following example shows that it is possible to have a reference form

\eta

such that
|\sign_{\bullet}^{\mathscr{M}}\eta|=k>0
on
\operatorname{Sper}R
, and a nonsingular
hermitian form
h
on
(A,\sigma)
such that
\sign_{\bullet}^{\eta}h
is not
continuous for the Harrison topology.

Example 6.3
.

Let
h
be a nonsingular hermitian form over
(A,\sigma)
. We define

\displaystyle\{h=0\}

\displaystyle:=\{\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R\mid\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}h=0\},\text{ and}

\displaystyle\{h\not=0\}

\displaystyle:=\{\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R\mid\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}h\not=0\}.

Since
h
is nonsingular, both
\{h=0\}
and
\{h\not=0\}
are clopen for
the Harrison topology on
\operatorname{Sper}R
, cf. Corollary
4.3
.

Let
U
be clopen for the constructible topology such that
U\cap\{h\not=0\}
is not clopen for the Harrison topology. (For instance, take
(A,\sigma)=(M_{n}(R),t)
and
h=\langle 1\rangle_{\sigma}
. Then
|\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}h|=n
for every

\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R
, and we can take for
U
any subset of
\operatorname{Sper}R
that is
clopen for the constructible topology, but not for the Harrison topology.)

We construct a reference form
\eta
such that
|\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\bullet}\eta|=k>0
on
\operatorname{Sper}R
, but
\sign^{\eta}_{\bullet}h
is not continuous for the
Harrison topology.

Let
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}
be the non-zero values taken by
|\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\bullet}h|
on
\operatorname{Sper}R
, and
O_{i}:=|\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\bullet}h|^{-1}(k_{i})
. Let
k:=k_{1}\cdots k_{r}
. By
[
18
, Theorem 3.2]
there are
s\in\mathbb{N}
and
quadratic forms
q_{1},\ldots,q_{r}
over
R
such that

\sign_{\bullet}q_{i}=2^{s}k/k_{i}
on
O_{i}
and
0
elsewhere.
If we define
q_{0}:=q_{1}\perp\ldots\perp q_{r}
we have

\sign_{\bullet}q_{0}>0\text{ and }|\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\bullet}(q_{0}\otimes h)|=2^{s}k\text{ on }\{h\not=0\}.

By Lemma
5.4
, there is a quadratic form
q
over
R
such that

\sign_{\bullet}q=2^{\ell}
on
U
and
-2^{\ell}
on
(\operatorname{Sper}R)\setminus U
.
Then
|\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\bullet}(q\otimes q_{0}\otimes h)|=k2^{s+\ell}
on
\{h\not=0\}
.
Let

\eta_{0}
be a hermitian form over
(A,\sigma)
such that
|\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\bullet}\eta_{0}|=c>0
on
\{h=0\}
and
0
on
\{h\not=0\}
(it can be obtained
by multiplying the form obtained in Theorem
5.5
by a suitable
quadratic form as in Lemma
5.4
). Defining

\eta:=c\times q\otimes q_{0}\otimes h\perp k2^{s+\ell}\times\eta_{0},

we have
|\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\bullet}\eta|=ck2^{s+\ell}
on
\operatorname{Sper}R
. In particular

\eta
is a reference form for
(A,\sigma)
.

Claim:

\sgn(\sign^{\eta}_{\bullet}h)
is not continuous for the Harrison
topology. In particular
\sign^{\eta}_{\bullet}h
is not continuous for the
Harrison topology.

Proof of the claim:

If
\alpha\in\{h\not=0\}\cap U
, we have

\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}\eta=c(\sign_{\alpha}q)(\sign_{\alpha}q_{0})(\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}h)=c^{\prime}\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}h,

for some
c^{\prime}>0
, so that
\sgn(\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}h)=1
by
Remark
5.7
(2).

If
\alpha\in\{h\not=0\}\setminus U
, we have

\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}\eta=c(\sign_{\alpha}q)(\sign_{\alpha}q_{0})(\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}h)=-c^{\prime}\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}h,

for some
c^{\prime}>0
,
so that
\sgn(\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}h)=-1
by Remark
5.7
(2).

Therefore
(\sgn(\sign^{\eta}_{\bullet}h))^{-1}(1)\cap\{h\not=0\}=U\cap\{h\not=0\}
, which is not clopen in the Harrison topology. This
proves the claim and completes the example.

We clarify some properties of the
\eta
-signature. For central simple
algebras with involution over a field these already appeared as

[
2
, Proposition 3.3]
in a slightly different context.
Proposition
6.4
is stated for the constructible topology on
\operatorname{Sper}R
, but the statements can easily be formulated
for the Harrison topology in cases where the total signature maps are
continuous for this topology.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
\eta
be a reference form for
(A,\sigma)
. The topology on
\operatorname{Sper}R
is
always the constructible topology in this proposition.

(1)

Let
h_{0}
be such that
\sign^{\eta}_{\bullet}h_{0}>0
on
\operatorname{Sper}R\setminus\Nil[A,\sigma]
. Then
\sign^{\eta}=\sign^{h_{0}}
.

(2)

Let
f
be a continuous map from
\operatorname{Sper}R
to
\{-1,1\}
(with the
discrete topology).
There exists a reference form
h_{f}
over
(A,\sigma)
such that

\sign^{h_{f}}=f\cdot\sign^{\eta}
.

(3)

Let
\eta^{\prime}
be another reference form for
(A,\sigma)
. There exists a
continuous map
f:\operatorname{Sper}R\to\{-1,1\}
such that

\sign^{\eta^{\prime}}=f\cdot\sign^{\eta}
.

Proof.

(1) Let
h
be a hermitian form over
(A,\sigma)
.
Considering that for a given
\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R
,
\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}
is a
special case of

\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}
, it follows from Definition
5.6
that

\sign^{h_{0}}_{\alpha}h=\sgn(\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}h_{0})\cdot\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}h=\begin{cases}\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}h&\text{if }\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R\setminus\Nil[A,\sigma],\\
0&\text{if }\alpha\in\Nil[A,\sigma].\end{cases}

(2)
By Lemma
5.4
there exists a quadratic form
q
over
R

such that
\sign q>0
on
f^{-1}(1)
and

\sign q<0
on
f^{-1}(-1)
.
Let
h_{f}=q\otimes\eta
. Then for any hermitian form
h
over
(A,\sigma)
we have
(since
\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}h_{f}=\sign_{\alpha}q\cdot\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}\eta
,
cf. Remark
3.2
),

\displaystyle\sign^{h_{f}}_{\alpha}h

\displaystyle=\sgn(\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}h_{f})\cdot\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}h

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}h&\text{if }\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}h_{f}>0,\text{ i.e., if }f(\alpha)=1\\
-\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}h&\text{if }\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}h_{f}<0,\text{ i.e., if }f(\alpha)=-1\\
\end{cases}

\displaystyle=f(\alpha)\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}h.

(3) Let
\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R\setminus\Nil[A,\sigma]
. By
[
1
, Proposition 3.4]

there exists
\delta\in\{-1,1\}
such that

\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}\eta=\delta\sign^{\eta^{\prime}}_{\alpha}\eta
. Thus,

\delta=\sgn\bigl(\sign^{\eta^{\prime}}_{\alpha}\eta\bigr)
,
since
\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}\eta>0
.
Define
f:\operatorname{Sper}R\to\{-1,1\}
by

f(\alpha)=\begin{cases}\sgn\bigl(\sign^{\eta^{\prime}}_{\alpha}\eta\bigr)&\text{if }\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R\setminus\Nil[A,\sigma],\\
1&\text{if }\alpha\in\Nil[A,\sigma].\end{cases}

Then
f
is continuous and
\sign^{\eta}=f\cdot\sign^{\eta^{\prime}}
.
∎

For
\alpha\in\operatorname{Sper}R
we consider

\displaystyle m_{\alpha}(A,\sigma)

\displaystyle:=\max\{\sign^{\mathscr{M}}_{\alpha}\langle a\rangle_{\sigma}\mid a\in\Sym(A^{\times},\sigma)\}

\displaystyle\phantom{:}=\max\{\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}\langle a\rangle_{\sigma}\mid a\in\Sym(A^{\times},\sigma)\}.

This function was of crucial importance in
[
6
]
to establish the
links between elements of maximal signature and positive semidefinite forms,
cf.
[
6
, Proposition 5.4, Theorem 6.1]
. However, we could only
determine its value for maximal elements of
\operatorname{Sper}R
, cf.

[
6
, Corollary 3.19]
. The following results clarify this.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
f:\operatorname{Sper}R\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
be continuous,
where
\operatorname{Sper}R
is equipped with the Harrison topology.
Let
\alpha\subseteq\beta\in\operatorname{Sper}R
. Then
f(\alpha)=f(\beta)
.

Proof.

Recall that
\alpha\subseteq\beta
is equivalent to
\beta\in\overline{\{\alpha\}}
, cf.

[
11
, Definition 3.3.6 and Proposition 3.3.7]
.
Let
k:=f(\beta)
and consider the open set

U=f^{-1}(k)
. Since
\beta\in U
, we have
\alpha\in U
. Hence,

f(\alpha)=k
.
∎

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
\eta
be a reference form for
(A,\sigma)
and assume that
\eta
is
nonsingular. Let
\alpha\subseteq\beta\in\operatorname{Sper}R
and let
h
be a
nonsingular hermitian form over
(A,\sigma)
. Then
\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}h=\sign^{\eta}_{\beta}h
.

Proof.

This follows from Lemma
6.5
and Theorem
6.1
.
∎

Corollary 6.7
.

Let
\eta
be a reference form for
(A,\sigma)
and assume that
\eta
is
nonsingular.
Let
\alpha\subseteq\beta\in\operatorname{Sper}R
. Then
m_{\alpha}(A,\sigma)=m_{\beta}(A,\sigma)
.

Proof.

Let
a\in\Sym(A^{\times},\sigma)
. By Proposition
6.6
, we have

\sign^{\eta}_{\alpha}\langle a\rangle_{\sigma}=\sign^{\eta}_{\beta}\langle a\rangle_{\sigma}
. The result
follows by taking the supremum over all such elements
a
.
∎

Structures of Moduli Spaces of Generalized Cantor Sets in ( 0 , 1 ) ℕ (0,1)^{\mathbb{N}}
Hiroshige Shiga

Abstract
. For each ω ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) ℕ \omega\in(0,1)^{\mathbb{N}} , we may construct a Cantor set E ⁡ ( ω ) ⊂ [ 0 , 1 ] E(\omega)\subset[0,1] called a generalized Cantor set for ω \omega . We study the moduli space of ω \omega denoted by ℳ ⁡ ( ω ) ⊂ ( 0 , 1 ) ℕ \mathcal{M}(\omega)\subset(0,1)^{\mathbb{N}} . It is the set of ω ′ \omega^{\prime} so that E ⁡ ( ω ′ ) E(\omega^{\prime}) is quasiconformally equivalent to E ⁡ ( ω ) E(\omega) . In this paper, we show that the set ℳ ⁡ ( ω ) \mathcal{M}(\omega) is measurable in ( 0 , 1 ) ℕ (0,1)^{\mathbb{N}} and we give a necessary condition for ω ′ \omega^{\prime} to belong to ℳ ⁡ ( ω ) \mathcal{M}(\omega) . By using this condition, we show that there are uncountably many moduli spaces in ( 0 , 1 ) ℕ (0,1)^{\mathbb{N}} . We also show that except for at most one moduli space, the volume of the moduli space with respect to the standard product measure of ( 0 , 1 ) ℕ (0,1)^{\mathbb{N}} vanishes.

1.
Introduction and main results

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a Cantor set in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
.
It is a totally disconnected perfect set in
\mathbb{C}
.
We consider the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
of
\mathcal{C}
, the set of Cantor sets
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
which are quasiconformally equivalent to
\mathcal{C}
.
Here, we say that a Cantor set
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{C}
is quasiconformally equivalent to
\mathcal{C}
if there exists a quasiconformal mapping
\varphi:{\mathbb{C}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\varphi(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})=\mathcal{C}
.

In a series of papers (
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
) , we have studied the quasiconformal equivalence of Cantor sets and their moduli spaces.
For example, we show that the limit set of a Schottky group and Cantor Julia sets of hyperbolic rational functions belong to the moduli space of the standard middle one third Cantor set
\mathcal{C}_{0}
.
Moreover, we show that the limit set of a generalized Schottky group with a rank one parabolic does not belong to the moduli space of
\mathcal{C}_{0}
.
Recently, R. Hidalugo (
[
5
]
) shows that the number of the moduli spaces of Cantor sets generated by finitely generated Kleinian groups is at most four.

In this paper, we consider generalized Cantor sets
E(\omega)
which are defined by
\omega=(q_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\Omega:=(0,1)^{\mathbb{N}}
.
The construction of

is a generalization of that of
\mathcal{C}_{0}
as follows.
To construct
E(\omega)
for
\omega=(q_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in(0,1)^{\mathbb{N}}
, we start with the closed unit interval
I=[0,1]
.
At the first step, we remove an open interval
J_{1}^{1}
of length
q_{1}(=q_{1}|I|)
from
I
so that
I\setminus J_{1}^{1}
consists of two closed intervals
I_{1}^{1}
and
I_{1}^{2}
of the same length.
We put
E_{1}(\omega)=I_{1}^{1}\cup I_{1}^{2}
.
We repeat this operation inductively.
Namely, for
E_{n-1}(\omega)=\cup_{j=1}^{2^{n-1}}I_{n-1}^{j}
, we remove open intervals
J_{n}^{2j-1}
of the length
q_{n}|I_{n-1}^{j}|
from

so that
I_{n-1}^{j}\setminus J_{n}^{2j-1}

Thus, we obtain

Then,

is a Cantor set and we call it the generalized Cantor set for
\omega=(q_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\Omega
.
Therefore, the set of generalized Cantor sets is identified with
\Omega
.

For a given
\omega\in\Omega
, we consider the moduli space of
\omega
denoted by
\mathcal{M}(\omega)
.
It is the set of
\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega
such that
E(\omega^{\prime})
is quasiconformally equivalent to
E(\omega)
.
In this paper, we study the structure of
\mathcal{M}(\omega)
in
\Omega
.
First, we show the following:

Theorem I
.

Under the standard product topology of
\Omega

(=(0,1)^{\mathbb{N}})
, the moduli space
\mathcal{M}(\omega)
is an
F_{\sigma}

\Omega
.

The space

(0,1)

Since each moduli space

is measurable, we may consider the volume

of
\mathcal{M}(\omega)
.

Theorem II
.

Except for at most one moduli space, the volume of any moduli space with respect to

is zero.

We consider conditions for
\omega^{\prime}
to belong to
\mathcal{M}(\omega)
.

Theorem III

Let
\omega=(q_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
\omega^{\prime}=(q_{n}^{\prime})_{n=1}^{\infty}

Suppose that both

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\log\frac{\log(1-q_{n})}{\log(1-q_{n}^{\prime})}\right|<\infty.

4.1.
The shift map and quasiconformal equivalence

Let

Namely, for

\sigma(\omega)=(q_{n+1})_{n=1}^{\infty}=(q_{2},q_{3},\dots).

The shift map

Actually, the followings are well-known.

Proposition 4.1
.

(1)

The shift map

Now, we establish a relationship between the shift map and the quasiconformal equivalence.

Therefore, we have

\ell_{X(\omega)}(\gamma_{k}^{j})\geq 2e^{-C(\delta)}\lambda(\Gamma_{0})\geq\frac{e^{-C(\delta)}\pi}{6\log 2-\log(1-q_{k})}.

Similarly, we have

\ell_{X(\omega^{\prime})}({\gamma^{\prime}}_{k}^{j})\geq\frac{e^{-C(\delta)}\pi}{6\log 2-\log(1-q^{\prime}_{k})}..

When
j
is odd, we see that the above argument still works and we obtain the same estimates as above.

Thus, we get the following estimates.

(5.8)

\frac{e^{-C(\delta)}\pi}{6\log 2-\log(1-q_{k})}\leq\ell_{X(\omega)}(\gamma_{k}^{j})\leq\frac{2\pi^{2}}{\log\left(1+\frac{2\delta}{1-q_{k}}\right)},

and

(5.9)

\frac{e^{-C(\delta)}\pi}{6\log 2-\log(1-q^{\prime}_{k})}\leq\ell_{X(\omega)}({\gamma^{\prime}}_{k}^{j})\leq\frac{2\pi^{2}}{\log\left(1+\frac{2\delta}{1-q_{k}^{\prime}}\right)}

for each
j\in\{1,2,\dots,2^{k}\}
.

We compare the left-hand side and the right-hand side of each other.

\displaystyle R(q_{k},q_{k}^{\prime})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi e^{C(\delta)}(6\log 2-\log(1-q^{\prime}_{k}))}{\log\left(1+\frac{2\delta}{1-q_{k}}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\pi e^{C(\delta)}\frac{\log(1-q_{k}^{\prime})}{\log(1-q_{k})}\cdot\frac{\frac{6\log 2}{\log(1-q_{k}^{\prime})}-1}{\frac{\log(2\delta+1-q_{k})}{\log(1-q_{k})}-1}.

and

\displaystyle R(q_{k}^{\prime},q_{k})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi e^{C(\delta)}(6\log 2-\log(1-q_{k}))}{\log\left(1+\frac{2\delta}{1-q_{k}^{\prime}}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\pi e^{C(\delta)}\frac{\log(1-q_{k})}{\log(1-q_{k}^{\prime})}\cdot\frac{\frac{6\log 2}{\log(1-q_{k}^{\prime})}-1}{\frac{\log(2\delta+1-q_{k}^{\prime})}{\log(1-q_{k}^{\prime})}-1}.

Thus, we have the following :

Lemma 5.3
.

If
q_{k},q_{k}^{\prime}\geq\delta>0
, then

\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}R(q_{k},q_{k}^{\prime})>0
if and only if

\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\log(1-q_{k}^{\prime})}{\log(1-q_{k})}>0

and
\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}R(q_{k}^{\prime},q_{k})>0
if and only if

\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\log(1-q_{k})}{\log(1-q_{k}^{\prime})}>0.

Remark 5.1
.

E. Kinjo (
[
6
]
) has estimated
\ell_{X(\omega)}(\gamma_{k}^{j})
from below.
Actually, she shows

\ell_{X(\omega)}(\gamma_{k}^{j})\geq 2\sinh^{-1}\left(1/\sinh\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{\log\left(\frac{1+q_{k}}{2q_{k}}\right)}\right)\right).

She has used a different method from ours, while the above estimate is weaker than ours.

Step 3 : Looking at the images of geodesics by quasiconformal maps.

Since we are supposing (
5.1
), there exists a subsequence
\{n_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
such that
\lim_{k\to\infty}q_{n_{k}}=\lim_{k\to\infty}q^{\prime}_{n_{k}}=1
.
In particular,
\lim_{k\to\infty}\ell({\gamma^{\prime}}_{n_{k}}^{j})=0
by (
5.9
).

Suppose that there exists a
K
-quasiconformal mapping
\varphi:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
with
\varphi(E(\omega^{\prime}))=E(\omega)
.
By Wolpert’s lemma (
[
12
]
), we have

\ell_{X(\omega)}([\varphi({\gamma}^{\prime}_{n_{k}})])\leq K\ell_{X(\omega^{\prime})}({\gamma^{\prime}}_{n_{k}}^{j}),

where
[\varphi(\gamma_{n_{k}}^{j})]
is the geodesic in
X(\omega)
homotopic to
\varphi({\gamma^{\prime}}_{n_{k}}^{j})
.

Hence,
\lim_{k\to\infty}\ell_{X(\omega)}([\varphi([\gamma_{n_{k}}^{j}])=0
and
\ell_{X(\omega)}([\varphi([\gamma_{n_{k}}^{j}])<d
if
k
is sufficiently large, where
d>0
is a constant given in Lemma 4.2.
Therefore, the geodesic
[\varphi({\gamma^{\prime}}_{n_{k}}^{j})]
cannot pass through any pair of pants of the pants decomposition of
X(\omega)
.
Thus, we obtain:

Lemma 5.4
.

If
k
is sufficiently large, then
[\varphi({\gamma^{\prime}}_{n_{k}}^{j})]
is a boundary curve of a pair of pants of the canonical pants decomposition of
X(\omega)
.

Step 3 : Comparing the lengths of geodesics.

From (
5.1
), the following two cases occurs.

\mbox{Case I : }\quad\frac{\log(1-q_{n_{k}})}{\log(1-q^{\prime}_{n_{k}})}\to 0\quad(k\to\infty)

or

\mbox{Case II : }\quad\frac{\log(1-q^{\prime}_{n_{k}})}{\log(1-q_{n_{k}})}\to 0\quad(k\to\infty).

Case I.

From Lemma
5.3
,
R(q_{n_{k}}^{\prime},q_{n_{k}})<K^{-1}
for a sufficiently large
k
.
This implies that

\displaystyle\ell_{X(\omega)}([\varphi({\gamma^{\prime}}_{n_{k}}^{j})])

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K\ell_{X(\omega^{\prime})}({\gamma^{\prime}}_{n_{k}}^{j})\leq\frac{2\pi^{2}K}{\log\left(1+\frac{2\delta}{1-q_{n_{k}}^{\prime}}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle KR(q^{\prime}_{n_{k}},q_{n_{k}})\cdot\frac{e^{C(\delta)}\pi}{6\log 2-\log(1-q_{k})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle KR(q^{\prime}_{n_{k}},q_{n_{k}})\ell_{X(\omega)}(\gamma_{n_{k}}^{m})<\ell_{X(\omega)}(\gamma_{n_{k}}^{m})

for any
j,m\in\{1,2,\dots,2^{n_{k}}\}
.

Moreover, since
(q_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is increasing, for any
n<n_{k}
,
q_{n}\leq q_{n_{k}}
and

0<-\log\left(1-q_{n}\right)\leq-\log\left(1-q_{n_{k}}\right)

holds.
Therefore, we also obtain

\displaystyle\ell_{X(\omega)}([\varphi({\gamma^{\prime}}_{n_{k}}^{j})])

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle KR(q^{\prime}_{n_{k}},q_{n_{k}})\cdot\frac{e^{C(\delta)}\pi}{6\log 2-\log\left(1-q_{n_{k}}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle KR(q^{\prime}_{n_{k}},q_{n_{k}})\cdot\frac{e^{C(\delta)}\pi}{6\log 2+\log\left(1-q_{n}\right)}<\ell_{X(\omega)}(\gamma_{n}^{m})

for
n<n_{k}
and
m\in\{1,2,\dots,2^{n}\}
.

Therefore, we see that the geodesic
[\varphi({\gamma^{\prime}}_{n_{k}}^{j})]
, which is a boundary of a pair of pants in the canonical pants decomposition for
\omega
, has to be in the inside of a topological disk bounded by
\gamma_{n_{k}}^{i}
of some
i\in\{1,2,\dots,2^{n_{k}}\}
.

Case II.
From Lemma
5.3
,
R(q_{n_{k}},q_{n_{k}}^{\prime})<K^{-1}
for a sufficiently large
k
.
By using the same argument as in Case I above, we obtain

\ell_{X(\omega)}([\varphi({\gamma^{\prime}}_{n_{k}}^{j})])>\ell_{X(\omega)}(\gamma_{n_{k}}^{m})

for any
j,m\in\{1,2,\dots,2^{n_{k}}\}
, and

\ell_{X(\omega)}([\varphi({\gamma^{\prime}}_{n_{k}}^{j})])>\ell_{X(\omega)}(\gamma_{n_{k}}^{m})

for
n>n_{k}
and
m\in\{1,2,\dots,2^{n}\}
.

Therefore, we see that the geodesic
[\varphi({\gamma^{\prime}}_{n_{k}}^{j})]
, has to be in the outside of any topological disk bounded by
\gamma_{n_{k}}^{i}

(i\in\{1,2,\dots,2^{n_{k}}\})
.

Step 4 : Using a topological argument to get a contradiction.

Let
D_{n_{k},j}
(resp.
D^{\prime}_{n_{k},j}
) be the disk bounded by
\gamma_{n_{k}}^{j}
(resp.
{\gamma^{\prime}}_{n_{k}}^{j}
).
We put
W_{n_{k},j}:=\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\cup_{j=1}^{2^{n_{k}}}\overline{D_{n_{k},j}}
and
W^{\prime}_{n_{k},j}:=\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\cup_{j=1}^{2^{n_{k}}}\overline{D^{\prime}_{n_{k},j}}
.
Both
W_{n_{k},j}
and
W^{\prime}_{n_{k},j}
are planar domains bounded by mutually disjoint
2^{n_{k}}
simple closed curves and

W_{n_{k},j}\cap E(\omega)=\emptyset,\quad W^{\prime}_{n_{k},j}\cap E(\omega^{\prime})=\emptyset.

Since
\varphi:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
is a homeomorphism and
\varphi(E(\omega^{\prime}))=E(\omega)
, the image
\varphi(W^{\prime}_{n_{k},j})
is also a domain bounded by
2^{n_{k}}
simple closed curves
\varphi({\gamma^{\prime}}_{n_{k}}^{j})

(j=1,2,\dots,2^{n_{k}})
and
\varphi(W^{\prime}_{n_{k},j})\cap E(\omega)=\emptyset
.

Let
\widehat{W}_{n_{k},j}
be a domain bounded by
[\varphi({\gamma^{\prime}}_{n_{k}}^{j})]

(j=1,2,\dots,2^{n_{k}})
.
Since
[\varphi({\gamma^{\prime}}_{n_{k}}^{j})]
is homotopic to
\varphi({\gamma^{\prime}}_{n_{k}}^{j})
,
\widehat{W}_{n_{k},j}
has the same property as that of
\varphi(W^{\prime}_{n_{k},j})
.
Namely,
\widehat{W}_{n_{k},j}\cap E(\omega)=\emptyset
.
Moreover,
[\varphi({\gamma^{\prime}}_{n_{k}}^{j})]
is a boundary of a pair of pants of the canonical pants decomposition of
X(\omega)
for a sufficiently large
k
(Lemma
5.4
).
Now, let us consider Case I in Step 3.

As we have seen there, each geodesic
[\varphi({\gamma^{\prime}}_{k}^{j})]
is in the inside of some
D_{n_{k},i}

(1\leq i,j\leq 2^{n_{k}})
.
Furthermore,
[\varphi({\gamma^{\prime}}_{k}^{j})]\not=\partial D_{n_{k},i}=\gamma_{n_{k}}^{i}
because
\ell_{X(\omega)}([\varphi({\gamma^{\prime}}_{n_{k}}^{j})])<\ell_{X(\omega)}(\gamma_{n_{k}}^{i})
.
Hence, the simply connected domain
D_{n_{k},i}
contains at least two geodesics in
\partial\widehat{W}_{n_{k},j}
, otherwise
\widehat{W}_{n_{k},j}\cap E(\omega)\not=\emptyset
.
This is absurd since both
W_{n_{k},j}
and
\widehat{W}_{n_{k},j}
are bounded by the same number of simple closed curves and they do not include any point in
E(\omega)
.

We may use the same argument for Case II and have a contradiction as well.
Thus, we have completed the proof of Theorem
III
.

6.
Proof of Theorem
IV

The proof of Theorem
IV
is done by giving an example.

For each
\alpha\in(1,+\infty)
, we put

q_{n}(\alpha)=1-\exp\left(-{n}^{\alpha}\right)

and
\omega(\alpha)=(q_{n}(\alpha))_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\Omega
.
Then,
\omega(\alpha)
is increasing and
\lim_{n\to\infty}q_{n}(\alpha)=1
.
Furthermore, for
1\leq\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha<+\infty
,

\frac{\log(1-q_{n}(\alpha))}{\log(1-q_{n}(\alpha^{\prime}))}=\left({n}\right)^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\to\infty\quad(n\to\infty).

Hence,
\mathcal{M}(\omega(\alpha))\not=\mathcal{M}(\alpha^{\prime})
from Theorem
III
and we conclude that
\{\mathcal{M}(\alpha)\}_{\alpha\in(1,+\infty)}
is uncountable.

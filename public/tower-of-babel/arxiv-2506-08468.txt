Vortices in Two-Dimensional Chiral Superfluids
Yan He, Wenxing Nie

Abstract
We study the orbital angular momentum (OAM) L z L_{z} of two-dimensional chiral ( p x + i ​ p y ) ν (p_{x}+ip_{y})^{\nu} -wave superfluids (SFs) in the presence of an axisymmetric multiply quantized vortex (MQV) with vorticity k k on a disk at zero temperature, in the framework of Bogoliubov-de Gennes (BdG) theory. Focusing on spectral asymmetry (or spectral flow), we find that L z = ( k + ν ) ​ N / 2 L_{z}=(k+\nu)N/2 for any integer ν \nu and k k in the Bose-Einstein Condensation (BEC) regime, where N N is the total number of fermions. While in the weak-pairing Bardeen-Cooper-Schrieffer (BCS) regime, only for chiral p + i ​ p p+ip -wave SF with k = ± 1 k=\pm 1 , L z = ( k + ν ) ​ N / 2 L_{z}=(k+\nu)N/2 still holds. For chiral SFs with ν ≥ 2 \nu\geq 2 or | k | ≥ 2 |k|\geq 2 in the BCS regime, the OAM L z L_{z} is remarkably reduced from its “full” value in the BEC regime. However, the deviations differ in these two cases. For chiral SFs with ν ≥ 2 \nu\geq 2 , L z L_{z} is sharply suppressed in this ideal setting with a specular wall, while the suppression caused by the | k | ≥ 2 |k|\geq 2 vortex is moderate, which is core-size dependent. Furthermore, for p + i ​ p p+ip -wave SF with k = − 1 k=-1 , the total OAM L z L_{z} is zero, but the distribution L z ​ ( r ) L_{z}(r) is nontrivial compared with that of vortex-free s s -wave SF, in which the total OAM is zero as well. For chiral SFs with ν ≥ 2 \nu\geq 2 and | k | ≥ 2 |k|\geq 2 , the effects of circulation due to vortex and chiral pairing can coexist, and hence depress the OAM simultaneously. These observations can be explained by spectral asymmetry and unpaired fermions in the ground state of the BdG Hamiltonian. We also investigate the spatial distribution of particle density, OAM, by solving the BdG equation.

II
chiral fermionic superfluids

In this section, we give a brief review of the ideal settings of the two-dimensional (2D) chiral superfluids (SFs) at zero temperature, in the framework of Bogoliubov-de Gennes (BdG) theory. We start with the Bardeen-Cooper-Schrieffer (BCS) mean-field Hamiltonian for chiral fermionic SF,

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{BCS}}=\int d\mathbf{r}\Big[\sum_{\sigma=\uparrow,\downarrow}\psi_{\sigma}^{{\dagger}}(\mathbf{r})\hat{T}\psi_{\sigma}(\mathbf{r})+\psi^{{\dagger}}_{\uparrow}(\mathbf{r})\hat{\Delta}\psi^{{\dagger}}_{\downarrow}(\mathbf{r})+\mbox{H.c.}\Big],

(1)

where
\psi_{\sigma}
(
\psi_{\sigma}^{\dagger}
) is the fermionic annihilation(creation) operator of spin
\sigma=\uparrow,\downarrow
.
For simplicity, the orbital quantization axis is assumed to be normal to the surface of the two-dimensional superfluid,
\mathbf{d}=(0,0,d_{z})
.
Then a symmetrized pairing operator
\hat{\Delta}
is given by
Ivanov 2001
;
Sauls 2011

\displaystyle\hat{\Delta}=\frac{1}{2}\Big[(\hat{p}_{x}+i\hat{p}_{y})^{\nu}\Delta(\mathbf{r})+\Delta(\mathbf{r})(\hat{p}_{x}+i\hat{p}_{y})^{\nu}\Big],

(2)

with momentum operators
\hat{p}_{x}=-i\partial_{x}
,
\hat{p}_{y}=-i\partial_{y}
. Since
\mathbf{r}
and
{\bf p}
do not commute with each other, we assume a symmetrized order for the pairing operator
\hat{\Delta}
as above. Recall that
\nu=1
represents the
p+ip
-wave pairing, and
\nu=2
corresponds to the
d+id
-wave pairing. For a uniform superfluid with rotation symmetry,
\Delta(r)
can be taken as a constant when neglecting the oscillation near the boundary. And it can be taken as
\Delta(r)=\Delta_{0}\tanh(r/\xi)
, when considering the influence of a vortex at the center, where
\xi
is the coherence length.

The kinematic energy operator is given by

\displaystyle\hat{T}=-\frac{\nabla^{2}}{2m_{0}}+V(\mathbf{r})-\mu,

(3)

where
V(\mathbf{r})
is some possible confining potential, and
\mu
is the chemical potential. In the presence of rotation symmetry, when
V(r)
describes the infinite circular well or a disk with a specular wall, the eigenfunction of
\hat{T}
is the Bessel function. If
V(r)
describes the harmonic trap, the eigenfunction of
\hat{T}
is the Laguerre Polynomial.

In this paper, we always assume that the populations of the two spin components are equal. The pairing potential is defined as

\displaystyle\Delta(\mathbf{r})=g\langle\psi_{\downarrow}(\mathbf{r})(\hat{p}_{x}-i\hat{p}_{y})^{\nu}\psi_{\uparrow}(\mathbf{r})\rangle,

(4)

where
g
is the bare coupling constant.

The BCS mean-field Hamiltonian in Eq. (
1
) can be diagonalized by the Bogoliubov transformation,

\displaystyle\psi_{\uparrow}(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{n}\Big[u_{n}(\mathbf{r})\gamma_{n,1}-v^{*}_{n}(\mathbf{r})\gamma^{\dagger}_{n,2}\Big],

(5)

\displaystyle\psi^{{\dagger}}_{\downarrow}(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{n}\Big[v_{n}(\mathbf{r})\gamma_{n,1}+u^{*}_{n}(\mathbf{r})\gamma^{\dagger}_{n,2}\Big],

(6)

in which
\gamma_{n,a},\gamma_{n,a}^{\dagger},(a=1,2)
are quasi-particle operators introduced by Bogoliubov transformation.
The coefficients of the above transformation satisfy the orthonormal conditions

\displaystyle\int d\mathbf{r}\Big[u^{*}_{m}(\mathbf{r})u_{n}(\mathbf{r})+v^{*}_{m}(\mathbf{r})v_{n}(\mathbf{r})\Big]=\delta_{mn}.

(7)

In terms of
\gamma
operator, the BCS Hamiltonian in Eq. (
1
) can be diagonalized as

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{BCS}}=E_{0}+\sum_{n,\sigma}E_{n}\gamma^{{\dagger}}_{n,\sigma}\gamma_{n,\sigma},

(8)

where
E_{0}
is the ground-state energy. With the above diagonalized Hamiltonian, one has the following commutation relations:

\displaystyle[\hat{H}_{\mathrm{BCS}},\gamma_{n,\sigma}]=-E_{n}\gamma_{n,\sigma},\quad[\hat{H}_{\mathrm{BCS}},\gamma^{{\dagger}}_{n,\sigma}]=E_{n}\gamma^{{\dagger}}_{n,\sigma}.

(9)

Substituting Eq. (
1
) and the inverse of Eq. (
5
) and (
6
) into the above commutation relations, and equating both sides, we arrive at the Bogoliubov-de Gennes equation of 2D chiral superfluid explicitly,

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\hat{T}&\hat{\Delta}\\
\hat{\Delta}^{\dagger}&-\hat{T}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u_{n}(\mathbf{r})\\
v_{n}(\mathbf{r})\end{array}\right)=E_{n}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u_{n}(\mathbf{r})\\
v_{n}(\mathbf{r})\end{array}\right).

The pair potential in Eq. (
4
) is in turn determined by
u_{n}(\mathbf{r})
and
v_{n}(\mathbf{r})
, the eigenfunctions of BdG equation as,

\displaystyle\Delta(\mathbf{r})=g\sum_{E_{n}<0}v^{*}_{n}(\mathbf{r})(\hat{p}_{x}-i\hat{p}_{y})^{\nu}u_{n}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle\quad-g\sum_{E_{n}>0}u_{n}(\mathbf{r})(\hat{p}_{x}-i\hat{p}_{y})^{\nu}v^{*}_{n}(\mathbf{r}).

(17)

Since we mainly focus on the topological properties, such as edge modes, spectral asymmetry (or spectral flow), and some other qualitative features, we will make almost no use of Eq. (
17
) in the rest of this paper. For this reason, we will not search for a self-consistent solution of the BdG equation in the current paper.

III
Vortex solutions

In this section, we further simplify the ideal setting of 2D chiral superfluid as one confined by a specular wall, considering a solution of the
p+ip
-wave or
d+id
-wave BdG equation with a multiply quantized vortex (MQV) located at the center of the disk, where the rotation axis is assumed to be perpendicular to the disk. Such confining potential is given by the infinite circular well,

\displaystyle V(\mathbf{r})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&(|\mathbf{r}|<R)\\
\infty,&(|\mathbf{r}|>R)\end{array}.\right.

It is convenient to use the polar coordinates
\mathbf{r}=(r,\theta)
in such system, where the Laplacian operator becomes,

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}=\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\Big(r\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\Big)+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial^{2}\theta},

(21)

and area element
d\mathbf{r}=rdrd\theta
. The
p+ip
-wave and
d+id
-wave pairing operators can be expressed in polar coordinates as

\displaystyle(\hat{p}_{x}\pm i\hat{p}_{y})^{\nu}=-ie^{\pm i\nu\theta}\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\pm\frac{i}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\Big)^{\nu},

(22)

where
\nu=1
for
p+ip
-wave pairing and
\nu=2
for
d+id
-wave pairing.

Considering a vortex with an integer vorticity
k
, which is located at the center of the disk, the gap function can be assumed in the following form,

\displaystyle\Delta(\mathbf{r})=\Delta(r)e^{ik\theta},

(23)

where
\Delta(r)=\Delta_{0}\tanh(r/\xi)
, and
\xi
is the coherence length. With the functional form of the gap function, we can expand
u_{n}(\mathbf{r})
and
v_{n}(\mathbf{r})
by the Bessel functions as follows,

\displaystyle u_{n}(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{l}u_{n}^{(l)}(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{l,m}U^{(l)}_{mn}\phi_{l+k+\nu,m}(\mathbf{r}),

(24)

\displaystyle v_{n}(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{l}v_{n}^{(l)}(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{l,m}V^{(l)}_{mn}\phi_{l,m}(\mathbf{r}),

(25)

where the expansion functions are given by

\displaystyle\phi_{l,m}(\mathbf{r})=N_{l,m}J_{l}\Big(z_{l,m}\frac{r}{R}\Big)\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{il\theta},

which is the wave function of a single electron in an infinite circular well.
Here
J_{l}(x)
is the first kind
j
th order Bessel function,
z_{lm}
is the
m
th zero point of the function
J_{l}(x)
, and
N_{l,m}=\sqrt{2}/\big(RJ_{l+1}(z_{l,m})\big)
is the normalization factor.
The expansion functions satisfy the boundary condition
\phi_{l,m}(R)=0
, which is required by the confining potential in Eq. (
III
).
They also satisfy the orthogonal relations as follows

\displaystyle\int d\mathbf{r}\,\phi^{*}_{l,m}(\mathbf{r})\phi_{l^{\prime},m^{\prime}}(\mathbf{r})=\delta_{ll^{\prime}}\delta_{mm^{\prime}}.

(26)

Then the BdG equation becomes a matrix eigenvalue equation. Since
l
is a good quantum number in such ideal settings with rotation symmetry, the matrix can be split into diagonal blocks for different values of
l
. For a given
l
, the BdG equation can be written as

\displaystyle\sum_{m^{\prime}}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}T_{l+k+\nu}&D_{l}\\
D_{l}^{\dagger}&-T_{l}\end{array}\right]_{mm^{\prime}}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}U^{(l)}_{m^{\prime}n}\\
V^{(l)}_{m^{\prime}n}\end{array}\right]=E^{(l)}_{n}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}U^{(l)}_{mn}\\
V^{(l)}_{mn}\end{array}\right].

Since the Bessel functions are the eigenfunctions of
\nabla^{2}
, we find that the kinematic term is simply a diagonal matrix given by

\displaystyle(T_{l})_{mm^{\prime}}=\Bigg[\frac{(z_{l,m}/R)^{2}}{2m_{0}}-\mu\Bigg]\delta_{mm^{\prime}},

(34)

where
m,m^{\prime}=1,2,\cdots,M
. The matrix elements of the pairing potential are given by

\displaystyle(D_{l})_{mm^{\prime}}=\int_{0}^{R}rdr\,\phi^{*}_{l+k+\nu,m}(\mathbf{r})\hat{\Delta}\phi_{l,m^{\prime}}(\mathbf{r}).

(35)

Making use of Eq.(
22
), we find that

\displaystyle(\hat{p}_{x}\pm i\hat{p}_{y})^{\nu}J_{l}\Big(z_{l,m}\frac{r}{R}\Big)e^{il\theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\pm i)^{\nu}(\frac{z_{lm}}{R})^{\nu}J_{l\pm\nu}\Big(z_{lm}\frac{r}{R}\Big)e^{i(l\pm\nu)\theta}.

(36)

Here we have used the recursion relations of the Bessel functions as follows

\displaystyle J^{\prime}_{n}(x)-\frac{n}{x}J_{n}(x)=-J_{n+1}(x),

(37)

\displaystyle J^{\prime}_{n}(x)+\frac{n}{x}J_{n}(x)=J_{n-1}(x).

(38)

After the
\theta
integral has been completed, the
D_{l}
matrix elements can be obtained by using the symmetrized pairing operator
\hat{\Delta}
in Eq. (
2
) as,

\displaystyle(D_{l})_{mm^{\prime}}=\frac{i^{\nu}}{2}N_{l+k+\nu,m}N_{l,m^{\prime}}\Bigg[\Big(\frac{z_{lm^{\prime}}}{R}\Big)^{\nu}\int_{0}^{R}rdr\Delta(r)J_{l+k+\nu}\Big(z_{l+k+\nu,m}\frac{r}{R}\Big)J_{l+\nu}\Big(z_{l,m^{\prime}}\frac{r}{R}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad+\Big(\frac{z_{l+k+\nu,m^{\prime}}}{R}\Big)^{\nu}\int_{0}^{R}rdr\Delta(r)J_{l+k}\Big(z_{l+k+\nu,m}\frac{r}{R}\Big)J_{l}\Big(z_{l,m^{\prime}}\frac{r}{R}\Big)\Bigg].

(39)

The integral in Eq. (
39
) can be calculated by Gaussian quadrature.
One can verify that the matrix
D_{l}
satisfies the following relation,

\displaystyle D_{l}=D_{-l-k-\nu}^{T},

(40)

so that the particle-hole symmetry is valid in our model. More explicitly, if we denote the BdG matrix of a given
l
as

\displaystyle H^{(l)}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}T_{l+k+\nu}&D_{l}\\
D_{l}^{\dagger}&-T_{l}\end{array}\right)_{mm^{\prime}},

the particle-hole symmetry will map
H^{(l)}
to
-H^{(-l-k-\nu)}
. Therefore, the energy spectrum is particle-hole symmetric about
l=-(k+\nu)/2
:

\displaystyle\left\{E^{(l)}_{m}\right\}_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}=\left\{-E^{(-l-k-\nu)}_{m}\right\}_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}.

(44)

Since level crossing largely happens in such a system with in-gap edge modes, during which process the spectral flow might appear, it is more convenient to treat the particles and holes on the same footing. Namely, we do not distinguish the particle or hole operator in the Bogoliubov transformation. Instead, the meaning of creation or annihilation is determined by the ground state (see below Eq. (
III
)).
To this end, we introduce the following generalized Bogoliubov transformation
Labonté 1974
as

\displaystyle\psi_{\uparrow}(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{n,l}u^{(l)}_{n}(\mathbf{r})\beta_{nl,1},

(45)

\displaystyle\psi^{\dagger}_{\downarrow}(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{n,l}v^{(l)}_{n}(\mathbf{r})\beta_{nl,2},

(46)

where
u^{(l)}_{n}
and
v^{(l)}_{n}
are defined in Eqs. (
24
) and (
25
), which are eigenfunctions for a given
l
.
Here
\beta_{nl,a}(a=1,2)
are quasi-particle Bogoliubov operators. The ground state expectation is given by

\displaystyle\langle\beta^{\dagger}_{nl,a}\beta_{nl,a}\rangle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&E^{(l)}_{n}>0\\
1,&E^{(l)}_{n}<0\end{array},\right.\quad\mbox{for}\quad a=1,2.

When
E^{(l)}_{n}>0
,
\beta_{nl,a}
is an annihilation operator. While
E^{(l)}_{n}<0
,
\beta_{nl,a}
is a creation operator.

By definitions of operators, generalized Bogoliubov transformation in Eq. (
45
), (
46
), and orthonormal conditions of
u_{n}^{(l)}(\mathbf{r})
and
v_{n}^{(l)}(\mathbf{r})
in Eq. (
7
), we can derive the expectation values of the following observables in the ground state. For example, the fermion density can be expressed as,

\displaystyle n(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{\sigma}\langle\psi_{\sigma}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{r})\psi_{\sigma}(\mathbf{r})\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{l}\Big[\sum_{E^{(l)}_{n}<0}|u^{(l)}_{n}(\mathbf{r})|^{2}+\sum_{E^{(l)}_{n}>0}|v^{(l)}_{n}(\mathbf{r})|^{2}\Big].

(50)

Since a vortex or chiral superfluid is accompanied by circulating currents, we can also evaluate the azimuthal mass current distribution,

\displaystyle J_{\theta}(\mathbf{r})=-i\frac{1}{m_{0}r}\sum_{\sigma}\langle\psi^{{\dagger}}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{r})\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\psi_{\sigma}(\mathbf{r})\rangle.

(51)

Similarly, we can also define the orbital angular momentum distribution as

\displaystyle L_{z}(\mathbf{r})=-i\sum_{\sigma}\langle\psi^{{\dagger}}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{r})\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\psi_{\sigma}(\mathbf{r})\rangle.

(52)

Because these two quantities are related by
L_{z}(\mathbf{r})=m_{0}rJ_{\theta}(\mathbf{r})
, in the following discussions, we will focus on the angular momentum distribution
L_{z}(\mathbf{r})
, which is equivalent to the circulating currents distribution
J_{\theta}(\mathbf{r})
.

In the same way, the ground state expectation value of orbital angular momentum distribution can be expressed as,

\displaystyle L_{z}(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{l}\Big[(l+\mu+\nu)\sum_{E^{(l)}_{n}<0}|u^{(l)}_{n}(\mathbf{r})|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-l\sum_{E^{(l)}_{n}>0}|v^{(l)}_{n}(\mathbf{r})|^{2}\Big].

(53)

We can integrate the local densities to obtain the total particle number and total orbital angular momentum as

\displaystyle N=\langle\hat{N}\rangle=\int d\mathbf{r}\,n(\mathbf{r}),\quad L_{z}=\langle\hat{L_{z}}\rangle=\int d\mathbf{r}\,L_{z}(\mathbf{r}),

(54)

where OAM operator is given by
\hat{L}_{z}=-i\int d\mathbf{r}\sum_{\sigma}\psi^{{\dagger}}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{r})\partial_{\theta}\psi_{\sigma}(\mathbf{r})
, and total fermion number operator corresponds to
\hat{N}=\int d\mathbf{r}\sum_{\sigma}\psi^{{\dagger}}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{r})\psi_{\sigma}(\mathbf{r})
.

Due to the pairing terms and the presence of vortices in the BCS Hamiltonian in Eq (
1
), neither
\hat{L}_{z}
nor
\hat{N}
commutes with
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{BCS}}
. Therefore, neither one is conserved separately. However, one can define a generalized orbital angular momentum,

\displaystyle\hat{Q}\equiv\hat{L}_{z}-\frac{k+\nu}{2}\hat{N},

(55)

and prove that
\hat{Q}
commutes with
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{BCS}}
, which implies the expectation value
Q=\langle\hat{Q}\rangle
is a conserved quantity

;
Volovik 2003
;
Prem et al. 2017
. Physically, the generalized OAM operator evaluates the deviation of OAM from its “full” value
L_{z}=(k+\nu)N/2
. So we can evaluate
Q
by the eigenvalues of
\hat{Q}
in the ground state of the BdG Hamiltonian, which are not trivial quantities.

By means of generalized Bogoliubov transformation in Eq. (
45
) and (
46
), we have proved in Appendix

that the expectation value

can be expressed by use of spectral asymmetry or spectral flow

;
Paranjape 1985
;
Niemi and Semenoff 1986
;
Stone and Gaitan 1987

\eta_{l}
as,

\displaystyle Q=-\sum_{l}\Big(l+\frac{k+\nu}{2}\Big)\frac{\eta_{l}}{2},

(56)

where
\eta_{l}
is defined as

\displaystyle\eta_{l}=\sum_{E^{(l)}_{n}>0}1-\sum_{E^{(l)}_{n}<0}1=\sum_{n}\mbox{sgn}E_{n}^{(l)}.

(57)

If one finds
\eta_{l}=0
for any

L_{z}=(k+\nu)N/2
. Otherwise, any nonzero spectral flow
\eta_{l}
introduces the suppression of OAM in the ground state.

Finally, we estimate the Fermi momentum and Fermi energy involved in this work. Integrating Eq. (
50
) over the whole disk gives the total particle number
N
of the Fermi superfluid. Assuming that the same amount of free fermions is contained in the same disk, then we find the following relation:

\displaystyle\frac{N}{\pi R^{2}}=\frac{2\pi k_{F}^{2}}{(2\pi)^{2}}

(58)

Therefore, the Fermi momentum on a disk is given by
k_{F}^{2}=2N/R^{2}
, and the corresponding Fermi energy is
E_{F}=k^{2}_{F}/(2m_{0})
. In the numerical calculations, we take
2m_{0}=1
for convenience.

IV
Result and discussion

In this section, we discuss the numerical solutions of BdG equations with a vortex inside the chiral superfluid. In solving the BdG equations in Eq. (
III
), we assume the radius dependence of the pairing potential is given by

\displaystyle\Delta(r)=\Delta_{0}\tanh\Big(\frac{r}{\xi}\Big),

(59)

where
\Delta_{0}
represents the overall scale of the gap amplitude, and
\xi=k_{F}/\Delta_{0}
is the coherence length. We do not seek a self-consistent solution in this paper at present, since the qualitative features of the solutions do not depend on the detailed function form of
\Delta(r)
. In this sense, it is good enough to stick to the assumptions like Eq. (
59
) to discuss the qualitative behaviors of vortices in chiral superfluids.

Finally, let us discuss why the OAM is suppressed in the presence of spectral flow. A general expression of the ground state of a BdG Hamiltonian is given by
|{\rm GS}\rangle\sim\otimes_{l}|{\rm GS}\rangle_{l}

Labonté 1974
,

\displaystyle|{\rm GS}\rangle_{l}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{M_{\uparrow}^{(l)}}\tilde{c}_{j,l+k+\nu,\uparrow}^{\dagger}\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{M_{\downarrow}^{(l)}}\tilde{c}_{j,-l,\downarrow}^{\dagger}\right)

\displaystyle\times\exp\left(\sum_{j>M_{\uparrow}^{(l)}}^{M}\sum_{j^{\prime}>M_{\downarrow}^{(l)}}^{M}\tilde{c}_{j,l+k+\nu,\uparrow}^{\dagger}F^{(l)}_{jj^{\prime}}\tilde{c}_{j^{\prime},-l,\downarrow}^{\dagger}\right)|0\rangle,

(60)

where
|0\rangle
is the vacuum state,
\tilde{c}_{j,l,\sigma}
is the linear superpositions of Fermion operator
c_{j,l,\sigma}
which is define by the expansion of field operator as
\psi_{\sigma}(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{j,l}c_{j,l,\sigma}\phi_{j,l}(\mathbf{r})
. The number of un-paired fermion is computed by
M_{\uparrow,\downarrow}^{(l)}=\mbox{max}(0,M-M_{+,-}^{(l)})
, where
M_{+,-}^{(l)}
is the number of positive (negative) eigenvalues of BdG matrix with given
l
, and
2M
is the dimension of the reduced BdG matrix
H^{(l)}
. When
M_{+}^{(l)}=M_{-}^{(l)}=M
, or
M_{\uparrow,\downarrow}^{(l)}=0
, Eq. (
60
) is reduced to the BCS ground state wavefunction, where all the fermions are paired into Cooper pairs. In general,
M_{+}^{(l)}
is not necessarily equivalent to
M_{-}^{(l)}
, especially in the system with in-gap edge modes, where level crossings largely happen. And the difference between them yielding the spectral asymmetry
\eta_{l}=M_{+}^{(l)}-M_{-}^{(l)}
. Furthermore, it can be proved that
\eta_{l}=2(M_{\downarrow}^{(l)}-M_{\uparrow}^{(l)})
, where nonzero
M_{\uparrow,\downarrow}^{(l)}
denotes the unpaired fermions in the energy levels after a generalized Bogoliubov transformation
Labonté 1974
;
Prem et al. 2017
. It implies that nonzero spectral flow signals the existence of unpaired fermions, which contribute to the reduction of the OAM
Tada et al. 2015
;
Prem et al. 2017
.

V
Conclusion

In conclusion, we have presented a detailed study of a variety of vortices in the chiral superfluid with different partial-wave pairing. It is found that in the
k=\pm 1
vortices of the
p+ip
chiral superfluid, there is no spectral asymmetry and the OAM exactly equals the contributions of all Cooper pairs. In the
k=-1
case, the spatial OAM distribution is nontrivial although the total OAM is exactly zero. In contrast, the spectral flows emerge when the vorticity is higher than
1
or the partial-wave pairing is higher than
p+ip
, which leads to the suppression of the total OAM. The effects of MQV or high partial-wave pairing on the spectral flows are almost independent of each other. In these cases, some counter-flow currents due to the unpaired fermions are also observed either in the vortex core or near the system boundary. In a qualitative sense, these counterflows explain the decrease of the OAM from the naive expected value.

Analytical soliton construction and dynamical transition analysis for the Kadomtsev-Petviashvili-Benjamin-Bona-Mahony model
Authors: Akhtar Hussain, Burak Oğul, Bushra R Al-Sinan, AMIRAH A AL-THAQFAN, Elkhateeb S Aly, Tarek F Ibrahim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn the present work, an extensive analytical analysis is carried out to obtain new exact soliton solutions for the (2+1)-dimensional Kadomtsev-Petviashvili-Benjamin-Bona-Mahony (KP-BBM) equation. The New Mapping Method (NMM), a recent, effective and robust technique for determining exact solutions of different nonlinear evolution equations is used. By empolying this technique, various new analytical solutions are obtained and represented by trigonometric and exponential functions. These solutions are new and different from those earlier documented in the literature. In addition, contour simulations are presented together with 2D and 3D graphical illustrations of the solution profiles showing that the solutions derived contain both singular and solitary wave forms. Graphical presentations of M-shape, kink, bell type, anti-bell type, singular wave, peakon and singular bell shape solutions for given parameter values are given. Finally, we apply the Galilean transformation to convert the ODE into a dynamical system. The behavior of the dynamical system is discussed using chaos and sensitivity analysis. To the best of present knowledge, the obtained solutions are important and can help in understanding new physical phenomena that are ruled by the KP-BBM equation.Keywords: New mapping method, (2+1)-dimensional Kadomtsev-Petviashvili-Benjamin-Bona-Mahony (KP-BBM)equation, Phase portrait, Periodic waves, Solitary wave solution, Kink wavesSubject terms: Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

Nonlinear partial differential equations (NLPDEs) are mathematically challenging and scientifically rewarding1. They exhibit complex behavior due to nonlinear terms that produce phenomena like shock waves, pattern formation, and multi-scale interactions. Over the last decade, NLPDEs have been used across disciplines to investigate topics ranging from neural dynamics in the brain to chemical reaction dynamics and interface evolution in materials science. Their inherently sensitive and unpredictable nature tends to demand the combination of sophisticated numerical algorithms, data-driven modeling, and qualitative analysis. Consequently, research into NLPDEs not only advances our theoretical insights but also stimulates innovation in predictive modeling and simulation methods across a wide range of sciences2–8.

Leading NLPDEs are the KP-BBM equation9, used in modeling shallow water waves and the Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) equation, instrumental in the theory of solitons10. Optical solitons in fiber optics are described by the nonlinear Schrödinger equation11, and the sine-Gordon equation is found in superconductivity and quantum field theory12. In recent research, these equations have been generalized into higher dimensions and with non-local effects. For example, the Benjamin-Bona-Mahony-Peregrine-Burgers (BBMPB) equation was recently used to simulate wave damping in porous media13, and the generalized hyperelastic rod wave (HERW) equation has been applied to the study of nonlinear elasticity14.

NLPDEs are frequently approached by a range of strong analytical tools intended to uncover exact or approximate closed-form solutions. A notable method is the variation of parameters method, which in conjunction with classical methods like the method of characteristics can simplify complicated NLPDEs into more manageable forms, at times transforming them into solvable ordinary differential equations (ODEs). This combination extends the list of NLPDEs that can be solved beyond what can be solved using each technique individually. Other popular analytical methods involve the variational iteration method15, which iteratively builds approximate solutions, and the inverse scattering transform16, best suited to integrable NLPDEs with soliton solutions. Expansion techniques like the (G'/G)-expansion method17, the F-expansion method18 and the extended Jacobi elliptic function method19 achieve systematically the exact traveling wave solutions through the transfer of the NLPDEs into solvable nonlinear ODEs.

In the present research, the NMM is extended to the KP-BBM equation for obtaining exact analytical solutions by converting the nonlinear equation to solvable auxiliary forms. The goal of this application is to broaden the KP-BBM equation’s solution space and offer a deeper insight into its nonlinear dynamics. The NMM, as presented by Zeng and Yong in20 is a remarkable improvement in the analytical solution of NLPDEs. In contrast with previous mapping methods, the new approach eliminates the previously imposed restrictions on the solution function form and permits more general, non-traveling wave solutions. As a particular case, this approach has successfully been applied to the Camassa-Holm nonlinear Schrödinger equation21 and (2+1)-dimensional Konopelchenko-Dubrovsky equation22 has given rise to a broad range of novel and more general exact solutions, such as soliton-like and periodic patterns. The power and versatility of the new mapping technique render it a highly potential instrument for examining a very large class of NLPDEs in applied mathematics and engineering.

The KP-BBM equation is a basic NLPDE that models multidimensional wave propagation with enhanced stability properties. Several analytical approaches have been applied with success to this equation to obtain exact solutions. Importantly, the generalized (G'/G)-expansion method has been employed for obtaining traveling wave solutions, whereas the new Kudryashov and generalized Arnous methods unveiled soliton and chaotic behaviors. Moreover, the (G'/G+G+A)-expansion method has been used to get wave solutions, and the enhanced bilinear neural network technique has yielded M-lump, lump-breather and N-soliton wave solutions. The Lie symmetry analysis23 has also been utilized for the reduction of the KP-BBM equation to derive new families of analytical solutions. These various methods in24–29 reflect the rich solution structure of the KP-BBM equation and establish a firm ground for using new analytical techniques. Dynamical analysis examines how a system evolves over time under varying conditions30. It explores the stability, periodicity, and potential chaotic behavior of nonlinear systems. By studying trajectories and equilibrium points, it reveals transitions between stable and unstable states. This analysis helps in understanding complex physical, biological, and engineering phenomena governed by differential equations31. The principal objective of this paper is to implement the NMM to the KP-BBM equation to obtain new exact analytical solutions. Special emphasis is given to revealing rich soliton structures, including bell-type, anti-bell type, M-shape, peakon and singular bell, as well as complex wave interactions such as kink and singular solutions. This strategy attempts to extend the accessible solution space of the KP-BBM equation and shed further light on its nonlinear wave dynamics. The research also illustrates the efficiency of the proposed mapping method in dealing with multidimensional NLPDEs with complex solution behaviors.

Physical interpretation of graphs and physical significance of parameters

This section presents the physical interpretation of the graphical solutions and the physical significance of the parameters involved in the obtained wave solutions. Different wave structures such as M-shaped waves, kink waves, peakon solitons, singular waves, anti-bell shapes, bell-shaped solitons, and singular bell waves are observed for different parameter values.

Physical interpretation of M-shape wave for

The graphical output of modeled under Eq. (13) exhibits an M-shaped wave profile for the parameter values

The M-shaped wave represents the interaction of two localized wave peaks separated by a shallow depression. Physically, this type of structure appears in nonlinear fluid waves, plasma wave interactions, and optical pulse propagation. The M-shape indicates the splitting of wave energy into two localized packets due to the combined effects of nonlinearity and dispersion. The negative value of contributes to wave inversion and interaction, which leads to the formation of the double-peak structure instead of a single bell-shaped soliton.

Physical interpretation of kink wave for

The graphical visualization of generated by Eq. (17) represents a kink-shaped wave for the parameter values

The kink wave represents a transition between two different equilibrium states. Physically, kink solutions describe phenomena such as magnetic domain walls, phase transition fronts, plasma double layers, and fluid bore waves. The kink structure indicates a permanent change in the state of the medium after the wave passes through it. Increasing the value of m increases nonlinear steepening, which transforms the M-shaped structure into a kink wave profile.

Physical interpretation of peakon soliton for

The solution generated by Eq. (14) produces a peakon soliton for the parameter values

A peakon soliton is a solitary wave with a sharp peak and a discontinuous derivative at the crest. Peakon waves occur in shallow water wave models, nonlinear elastic media, plasma physics, and nonlinear optical systems. The peak indicates strong nonlinearity dominating dispersion, producing a sharp localized energy packet that travels without changing shape.

Singular waves represent wave breaking, shock formation, or infinite amplitude spikes. Physically, such structures correspond to shock waves in fluids, plasma instabilities, rogue waves, and energy focusing phenomena. The very small value of and large value of s lead to instability and singularity formation in the wave profile.

Conclusions

In this research, the application of the NMM to the KP-BBM equation has yielded a rich variety of novel exact solutions. We find diverse soliton solutions like M-shape, kink, bell type, anti-Bell type, singular wave, peakon, and singular bell shape shown by Figs. 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, and 7. These solutions are graphically represented by vivid 2D and 3D graphical presentations, which expose intricate soliton structures and wave interactions. Contour plots also offer effective visualizations of the wave profiles and spatial behaviors, revealing the complex dynamics of the equation. The simultaneous application of 2D, 3D, and contour analyses not only confirms the results of the analysis but also deepens the insight into multidimensional nonlinear wave phenomena described by the KP-BBM equation. These results have applications to coastal wave propagation, wave channel experiments, small-amplitude tsunami evolution, long-crested waves with weak lateral spreading, long gravity waves in shallow water. It should be emphasized that singular solutions exhibiting blow-up behavior do not correspond to physically realizable shallow-water waves. The KP–BBM equation is derived under assumptions of weak nonlinearity, small amplitude, and long wavelength. Solutions that become unbounded violate these assumptions and therefore fall outside the model’s regime of physical validity. Such solutions should be interpreted as mathematical artifacts of the reduced model or as indicators of the breakdown of the asymptotic approximation, rather than physically observable wave phenomena. The complete methodology shows the robustness of the NMM in investigating rich solution patterns in higher-dimensional NLPDEs. In future work, the authors are interested to apply Deep Learning32–35, and Machine Learning36–38 for the analysis of desired model.

Abstract
. For graphs H H , we study the extremal function M H ​ ( n ) M_{H}(n) which is the maximum running time (until stabilisation) of an H H -bootstrap percolation process on n n vertices. Building on previous work in the clique case H = K k H=K_{k} , we develop a general framework of chain constructions . We demonstrate the flexibility of this framework by applying several variations of the method to give lower bounds on M H ​ ( n ) M_{H}(n) for a wide variety of different graphs H H including dense graphs, random graphs and complete bipartite graphs. In particular, we focus on the question of whether M H ​ ( n ) M_{H}(n) is (almost) quadratic or not and our lower bounds develop connections with additive combinatorics, utilising constructions of sets free of solutions to certain linear equations. Finally, our lower bounds are complemented by upper bounds which connect M H ​ ( n ) M_{H}(n) to other problems in extremal graph theory such as the Ruzsa-Szemerédi (6,3)-Theorem.

1.
Introduction

Given graphs
H
and
G
, let
n_{H}(G)
denote the number of copies of
H
in
G
. The
H
-bootstrap percolation process

(
H
-process
for short)
on a graph
G
is the sequence
(G_{i})_{i\geq 0}
of graphs defined by
G_{0}:=G
and

V(G_{i}):=V(G),\qquad\quad E(G_{i}):=E(G_{i-1})\cup\left\{e\in\binom{V(G)}{2}:n_{H}\left(G_{i-1}+e\right)>n_{H}(G_{i-1})\right\},

for
i\geq 1
.
We call
G
the
starting graph
of the process and
\tau_{H}(G):=\min\{t\in\mathbb{N}:G_{t}=G_{t+1}\}
the
running time
of the
H
-process on
G
, which is the point at which the process stabilises. Finally, we define
G_{\tau}
with
\tau=\tau_{H}(G)
to be the
final graph
of the process, and denote it
\langle G\rangle_{H}:=G_{\tau}
.

The
H
-process is an example of a cellular automaton and has been studied in various contexts
[
1
,
2
,
4
]
. In particular, over 50 years ago Bollobás
[
8
]
introduced the model through the notion of
weak saturation
, asking for the smallest number of edges an
n
-vertex graph
G
can have such that
\langle G\rangle_{H}=K_{n}
. Here our focus will be on a more recent extremal question, also raised by Bollobás, which asks for the maximum running time of an
H
-process on
n
vertices. We define

M_{H}(n):=\max\{\tau_{H}(G):v(G)=n\}

and we will be primarily interested in the asymptotics of
M_{H}(n)
. Note that trivially, one always has
M_{H}(n)=O(n^{2})
as the
H
-process on
G
has to add a new edge at each time step
1\leq t\leq\tau_{H}(G)
.

1.1.
Cliques

Initial exploration of
M_{H}(n)
focused on cliques
H=K_{k}
. Bollobás, Przykucki, Riordan and Sahasrabudhe
[
7
]
and independently Matzke
[
32
]
, showed that
M_{K_{4}}(n)=n-3
, in contrast to
K_{3}
for which
M_{K_{3}}(n)=\lceil\log(n-1)\rceil
. Both sets of authors also gave lower bounds for
M_{K_{k}}(n)
for larger
k
, with it being shown in
[
7
]
that there are constants
\lambda_{k}>0
which tend to
0
as
k
tends to infinity, such that
M_{K_{k}}(n)\geq n^{2-\lambda_{k}}
. However, the authors believed that the
K_{k}
-process would always fall short of lasting as long as the trivial upper bound, conjecturing that
M_{K_{k}}(n)=o(n^{2})
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Somewhat surprisingly, this was then refuted by Balogh, Kronenberg, Pokrovskiy and Szabó
[
3
]
who showed that
M_{K_{k}}(n)=\Omega(n^{2})
for all
k\geq 6
. This leaves open the question of whether the conjecture holds for
K_{5}
, which remains the most intriguing outstanding question in the field. Although they did not show that
M_{K_{5}}(n)
is quadratic, Balogh, Kronenberg, Pokrovskiy and Szabó
[
3
]
did show that
M_{K_{5}}(n)\geq n^{2-O(1/\sqrt{\log n})}=n^{2-o(1)}
by building a connection with subsets of the integers that are free of arithmetic progressions of size 3 and using a famous construction of Behrend
[
5
]
.

1.2.
Graphs with fast running time

In this series of papers on the running time of graph percolation processes, we study
M_{H}(n)
for different graphs
H
. In the first paper
[
16
]
, we addressed cycles showing the
M_{C_{k}}(n)
is logarithmic for all
3\leq k\in\mathbb{N}
. In fact, we determined
M_{C_{k}}(n)
exactly for large
n
, with the expression depending on the parity of
k
. The second paper
[
17
]
explored properties of
H
which lead to a maximum running time that is (sub-)linear. We showed for example that all trees
T
have constant running time
M_{T}(n)=O(1)
and that
M_{K_{2,s}}(n)
is linear in
n
for all
3\leq s\in\mathbb{N}
. We also proved general results exploring the relationship between
M_{H}(n)
with small values of certain graph parameters of
H
such as connectivity. We refer to the paper
[
17
]
for the details and will return briefly to discuss some of the results in Section
1.6
.
Suffice it to say here that our findings in the second paper
[
17
]
suggest that having a (sub-)linear running time
M_{H}(n)
requires
H
to have atypical and sparse structural properties, as with the examples listed above.

1.3.
Graphs with (almost) quadratic running time

In this current paper, we continue our exploration of
M_{H}(n)
and its dependence on properties of
H
. One of our main findings is that cliques of size at least
5
are far from unique in having maximum running time that is (almost) quadratic. In fact, graphs
H
with
M_{H}(n)=\Theta(n^{2})
are abundant. One indication of this is our first result which looks at the binomial random graph
H=G(k,p)
on
k
vertices with each edge present independently with probability
p=p(k)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
H=G(k,p)
. Then with probability tending to 1 as
k\to\infty
we have that

(1.1)

\displaystyle M_{H}(n)=

\displaystyle O(1)

if
p=o(\log k/k)
;

(1.2)

\displaystyle M_{H}(n)=

\displaystyle\Omega(n^{2})

if
p=\omega(\log k/k)
.

We remark that here, and throughout, the asymptotics of the function
M_{H}(n)
are in terms of the parameter
n

only
. The asymptotic dependence on
k
in the statement of Theorem
1.1
should be treated first and throughout this paper, the reader should think of
k=v(H)
as being fixed and
n
tending to infinity. For example, phrasing part (
1.2
) of Theorem
1.2
explicitly we have that for any
\varepsilon>0
and for any function
p(k)
such that
p(k)k/\log k
tends to infinity with
k
, there exists some
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that the following holds for all
k\geq k_{0}
. There is some
c=c(k)>0
such that with probability at least
1-\varepsilon
the graph
H=G(k,p)
satisfies
M_{H}(n)\geq cn^{2}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Note that Theorem
1.1
shows a stark phase transition in the behaviour of
H=G(k,p)
around the connectivity threshold
\log k/k
. Below this threshold, asymptotically almost surely
H(k,p)
will have an isolated edge which leads to a very fast percolation process no matter the starting graph. In contrast, as soon as
p(k)
is significantly above the connectivity threshold, asymptotically almost surely there is a choice of starting graph
G
such that the
H
-process with
H=G(k,p)
takes quadratically many steps before stabilising. This shows, in a very strong sense, that almost all graphs have quadratic maximum running time even when the average degree drops to (almost) logarithmic in
k
.

In relation to
minimum degree
conditions, we establish the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
5\leq k\in\mathbb{N}
and
H
is a graph with
v(H)=k
. We have that

(1.3)

\displaystyle M_{H}(n)\geq

\displaystyle\Omega(n^{2})

if
k\geq 6
and
\delta(H)>3k/4
;

(1.4)

\displaystyle M_{H}(n)\geq

\displaystyle n^{2-O(1/\sqrt{\log n})}=n^{2-o(1)}

if
\delta(H)\geq k/2+1
.

Note that Theorem
1.2
generalises the lower bounds for cliques given by Balogh, Kronenberg, Pokrovskiy and Szabó
[
3
]
. It turns out that Theorem
1.2
is tight in that there are
k
-vertex graphs
H
with
\delta(H)=k/2
which have much faster maximum running time. Indeed, the following example was shown in
[
17
]
to have linear running time. This shows that there is also a sharp phase transition when considering the behaviour of
M_{H}(n)
under extremal minimum degree conditions on
H
.

Figure 1.
An illustration of the graph
H^{\prime}_{7}
.

Example 1.3
.

Let
k\geq 3
and
H^{\prime}_{k}
be the
(2k-2)
-vertex graph composed by ‘gluing together’ two cliques of size
k
along a singular edge
e
and adding one more edge
e^{\prime}
between two non-adjacent vertices. (An illustration of
H^{\prime}_{7}
is given in Figure
1
). Then
M_{H^{\prime}_{k}}(n)=\Theta(n)
as shown in
[
17
]
.

Returning to Theorem
1.1
, we remark that
\log k/k
is also the threshold for the random graph
H=G(k,p)
to contain a
Hamilton cycle
, that is, a cycle covering all the vertices of
H
. It is not true that any graph containing a Hamilton cycle has a slow running time. Indeed, one can simply take the cycle itself which has logarithmic running time. However, it turns out that containing the
square
of a Hamilton cycle is enough to guarantee a running time that is almost quadratic. Here, the
square
of a graph
J
is obtained by adding all edges between vertices of distance 2 in
J
.

Theorem 1.4
.

If
H
with
k\geq 5
vertices contains the square of a Hamilton cycle, then

M_{H}(n)\geq n^{2-O(1/\sqrt{\log n})}=n^{2-o(1)}.

Note that Theorem
1.4
also generalises the result on
K_{5}
of Balogh, Kronenberg, Pokrovskiy and Szabó
[
3
]
as the square of a cycle of length 5 is
K_{5}
. Note also that by taking
H
itself to be a square of a cycle, Theorem
1.4
gives a sequence of extremely sparse
k
-vertex graphs for
k\geq 5
, that reach close to the maximum running time. Indeed, the square of a cycle is regular with vertex degrees just 4.

1.4.
The wheel graph

One question raised by the results above is whether the lower bounds of the form
n^{2-o(1)}
in Theorems
1.2
and
1.4
are tight or if they can be improved to prove quadratic running time. Of particular interest is the case
H=K_{5}
which, as mentioned above, was conjectured to have
M_{K_{5}}(n)=o(n^{2})
by Bollobás, Przykucki, Riordan and Sahasrabudhe
[
7
]
. Although Balogh, Kronenberg, Pokrovskiy and Szabó
[
3
]
disproved the conjecture for cliques of size at least 6, they stated that they tend to agree that the running time should be sub-quadratic for
K_{5}
.

Our next main result shows that it is possible to have a sub-quadratic running time that is
n^{2-o(1)}
.
The
wheel graph

W_{k}
is defined to be the graph with
k+1
vertices obtained by taking a cycle of length
k
and adding a new vertex that is adjacent to all vertices of the cycle.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
k\geq 7
be an odd integer.
The wheel graph
W_{k}
satisfies

M_{W_{k}}(n)\geq n^{2-o(1)}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad M_{W_{k}}(n)=o(n^{2}).

This suggests that such a running time could also be possible for
K_{5}
and the other graphs captured by Theorem
1.2
part (
1.4
) and Theorem
1.4
. We remark that the restriction to odd
k
is for somewhat technical reasons and is necessary for the upper bound only. We expect that the result also holds for even
k
but did not pursue this.

1.5.
Bipartite graphs

The known results for maximum running times for graphs
H
seem to indicate that apart from a few exceptions having maximum running times that are constant (trees), logarithmic (cycles) and linear (
K_{4}
,
K_{2,s}
for
s\geq 3
and the graphs
H^{\prime}_{k}
in Example
1.3
), all other running times are either quadratic or of the form
n^{2-o(1)}
. Indeed the results mentioned above cover a wide range of different graphs including graphs that are very sparse such as the square of a cycle and the wheel. This raises questions as to whether other types of running times are possible and whether there is some large class of graphs which have running times polynomially separated from the trivial upper bound
n^{2}
.

Our next result, whose proof is simple, gives the first example of an effective upper bound on running times that covers a general class of graphs, namely bipartite graphs. Recall that the
extremal number
(or
Turán number
) of a graph, denoted
\ex(n,H)
, is the maximum number of edges of an
n
-vertex graph that does not contain
H
as a subgraph.

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
H
be a graph with at least 2 edges. Then we have that

M_{H}(n)\leq 2\mathrm{ex}(n,H).

Proof.

Let
(G_{i})_{i\geq 0}
be an
H
-process with
\tau=\tau_{H}(G_{0})=M_{H}(n)
. For
1\leq i\leq\tau
, let
e_{i}\in E(G_{i})\setminus E(G_{i-1})
be an edge added at time
i
. Now consider the graph
G^{\prime}
with
V(G^{\prime})=V(G_{0})
and

E(G^{\prime})=\{e_{i}:i\in[\tau],i=1\mbox{ mod }2\}.

We claim that
G^{\prime}
has no copy of
H
. Indeed, if there is some copy
F
of
H
in
G^{\prime}
and
i_{*}\in[\tau]
is the maximal index such that
e_{i_{*}}\in E(F)
, then
E(F)\setminus\{e_{i_{*}}\}\subseteq E(G_{i_{*}-2})
and so
e_{i_{*}}
would be added by time
i_{*}-1
, contradicting the definition of
e_{i_{*}}
. Thus,
\tau/2\leq e(G^{\prime})\leq\ex(n,H)
as required.
∎

As a consequence of Proposition
1.6
and the celebrated Kővári-Sós-Turán theorem
[
26
]
, we get the following upper bound for bipartite graphs.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
H
be a bipartite graph such that the two partite sets of
H
have size
r
and
s
, respectively, where
1\leq r\leq s
. Then

M_{H}(n)=O(n^{2-1/r}).

Corollary
1.7
shows that for bipartite graphs
H
, the running time
M_{H}(n)
cannot reach quadratic length. However it could be that, as with our known examples of trees, cycles and
K_{2,s}
, the running time actually drops to linear. Our next result shows that this is not the case if the bipartite graph is sufficiently dense.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
3\leq r\leq s
and suppose
H
is a bipartite graph with parts
X,Y
with
|X|=r
,
|Y|=s
and such that
d(x)\geq s/2+1
for all
x\in X
and
d(y)\geq r/2+1
for all
y\in Y
. Then

M_{H}(n)\geq n^{3/2-o(1)}.

Theorem
1.8
can be seen as a bipartite analogue to Theorem
1.2
and in particular gives a running time of
M_{K_{r,s}}\geq n^{3/2-o(1)}
for all complete bipartite graphs
K_{r,s}
with
3\leq r\leq s
. This lower bound still falls short of the upper bound in Corollary
1.7
in all cases. For larger complete bipartite graphs, we can improve the lower bound, giving the following.

Theorem 1.9
.

For
3\leq r\leq s
, the maximum running time
M_{K_{r,s}}(n)
is bounded from below by

(1.5)

M_{K_{r,s}}(n)\geq n^{2-\tfrac{r+s-1}{r(s-1)-1}-o(1)}.

The bound in Theorem
1.9
becomes stronger than that of Theorem
1.8
when
s>\tfrac{3r-1}{r-2}
, in particular for
K_{r,r}
with
r\geq 5
. With
r
fixed and
s
growing, the bound of Theorem
1.9
approaches the upper bound given by Corollary
1.7
. Indeed
\tfrac{r+s-1}{r(s-1)-1}\rightarrow\tfrac{1}{r}
as
s\rightarrow\infty
.
Moreover, Theorem
1.9
implies infinitely many different possible running times.

Corollary 1.10
.

For every
3\leq r\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
s_{r}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

n^{2-1/({r-1})}\ll M_{K_{r,s_{r}}}(n)\leq O(n^{2-1/r}).

We remark that one can in fact prove a slightly better exponent in Theorem
1.9
, giving that
M_{K_{r,s}}(n)\geq n^{2-\tfrac{1}{r}-\tfrac{1}{s-1}-o(1)}
. The proof is similar but requires some additional technicalities. As we do not believe that either lower bound is tight, and the main motivation for Theorem
1.9
is Corollary
1.10
and showing that the bounds approach the upper bound of Corollary
1.7
as
s\rightarrow\infty
, we opt to prove the bound as in (
1.5
) and just sketch the improvement in the exponent after, see Remark
7.12
.

In general, it is unclear which of our bounds are closer to the truth for bipartite graphs and the picture is complicated by the fact that the extremal number is not known for many bipartite graphs
H
of interest, see for example
[
19
]
. We tend to think that the bound from Theorem
1.8
is close to being tight for small graphs such as
K_{3,3}
or the 3 dimensional cube graph
Q_{3}
. In fact, for the latter we can slightly improve the lower bound, removing the
o(1)
from the exponent.

Theorem 1.11
.

The running time of the cube
Q_{3}
has bounds

\Omega(n^{3/2})\leq M_{Q_{3}}(n)\leq O(n^{8/5}).

We remark that the bounds on
M_{Q_{3}}(n)
in Theorem
1.11
match the best known bounds on
\ex(n,Q_{3})
, see for example
[
19
]
. Indeed, the upper bound of Theorem
1.11
simply follows from Proposition
1.6
and the best known upper bound
\ex(n,Q_{3})=O(n^{8/5})
due to Erdős and Simonovits
[
12
]
. The lower bound will be proven in Section
6.3
.

1.6.
Inseparable graphs

The final topic covered in this paper returns to the main topic of our second paper
[
17
]
in this series which concentrated on properties of graphs
H
which have (sub-)linear running time. There we showed that all connected graphs
H
that have sub-linear running time and are not cycles, must have some vertex of degree 1. When considering graphs with linear running time this condition is no longer necessary as we know from examples such as
K_{4}
and
K_{2,s}
. To separate linear and super-linear running times, it turns out that the following notion of connectivity is useful, where we recall that a graph
F
is
k
-connected if
v(F)>k
and deleting any collection of at most
k-1
vertices (and their incident edges) from
F
leaves a connected graph.

Definition 1.12
.

A graph
H
is
(2,1)
-inseparable
if
H-e
is 3-connected for any choice of
e\in E(H)
.

Note that a
(2,1)
-inseparable graph cannot be separated by deleting at most 2 vertices and one edge, hence the terminology. We call a graph
(2,1)
-separable
if it is not
(2,1)
-inseparable.
Our last theorem of this paper shows that any graph
H
with a running time that is at most linear, must be
(2,1)
-separable.

Theorem 1.13
.

Any
(2,1)
-inseparable graph
H
with
v(H)=k
satisfies

M_{H}(n)=\Omega\left(n^{1+\tfrac{2}{3k-2}}\right).

Note that
(2,1)
-inseparability is not necessary for super-linear running time.
For example, the wheel graph
W_{k}
for odd
k\geq 7
, which has almost quadratic running time by Theorem
1.5
, can be disconnected by removing two vertices and an edge as it has vertices of degree three. However, in
[
17
]
we gave a partial converse to Theorem
1.13
showing that if a graph
H
is
(2,1)
-separable
and
it has the property that it
self-percolates
, that is,
\langle H\rangle_{H}=K_{v(H)}
, then
M_{H}(n)
is at most linear in
n
. In particular, this was used in
[
17
]
to show linear running time for the graphs
H^{\prime}_{k}
from Example
1.3
and for
K_{5}^{-}
which is the graph obtained by removing an edge from
K_{5}
.

1.7.
Methods and organisation

All of the lower bounds on running times stated above and proved in this paper follow from a general framework of
chain constructions
. We defer the full definition and motivation of these constructions to Section
4
and simply say here that it is useful to imagine a chain as a sequence of copies of
H
with consecutive copies intersecting at a single edge. The hope is to create an
H
-process based on a chain that will run through the copies with the completion of one copy triggering the completion of the next copy in the chain at the next time step. Under some technical conditions, principally that the copies of
H
in the chain do not induce some unwanted copy
F
of
H-e
for some
e\in E(H)
, one can show that the corresponding
H
-process behaves as desired and lasts as long as the sequence of copies of
H
.

The use of chains for constructions giving lower bounds for maximum running times dates back to the work of Bollobás, Przykucki, Riordan and Sahasrabudhe
[
7
]

on
M_{K_{k}}(n)
for
k\geq 5
who placed chains using a random process. The idea was then used again by Balogh, Kronenberg, Pokrovskiy and Szabó
[
3
]
who also introduced a method of linking chains for their lower bound on
M_{K_{5}}(n)
. Chain constructions have also featured in recent works looking at the analogous question for hypergraph cliques
[
15
,
20
,
34
]
. One of the key contributions of our work here is to build a general theory of chain constructions which unifies previous work and delves into the precise necessary conditions for chain constructions to work to give lower bounds on
M_{H}(n)
. Indeed, in Section
4
we will motivate the use of chains and introduce the concept of
proper chains
which are those for which we can control the corresponding
H
-process. Section
5
is then devoted to the process of linking chains, building on the initial idea present in
[
3
]
. In both these sections, we produce general lemmas that can be used for many different types of chain construction. We then demonstrate the applicability of this theory by giving three different types of chain construction, namely
ladder chains
(Section
6
),
dilation chains
(Section
7
) and
line chains
(Section
8
). The first of these builds on the construction of Balogh, Kronenberg, Pokrovskiy and Szabó
[
3
]
giving a quadratic lower bound on
M_{K_{k}}(n)
for
k\geq 6
. The second two constructions are distinct from previous constructions and introduce new ideas, in particular developing connections with other problems in additive number theory and extremal graph theory and utilising additive constructions free from solutions to certain equations and constructions of graphs of large girth.

The constructions of Sections
6
,
7
and
8
cover all of the new lower bounds in our results listed above. In fact, in some cases the results are more general and apply to classes of graphs that in particular cover some of the cases listed in the introduction. Hence, after some simple preliminaries in Section
2
and before delving into our theory of chains, in Section
3
we explore properties and connections between the different classes of graph
H
for which our methods apply. After our sections covering chain constructions, in Section
9
we give proofs of our upper bounds, principally the upper bound for the wheel in Theorem
1.5
which uses the Ruzsa-Szemerédi
(6,3)
-theorem
[
36
]
. Finally in Section
10
we give some concluding remarks.

Notation and preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

For a graph
H
and an edge
e\in E(H)
, we use the notation
H-e
to denote the graph with vertex set
V(H-e)=V(H)
and edge set
E(H-e)=E(H)\setminus\{e\}
. Similarly, for
e\in\binom{V(H)}{2}
(which may or may not be an edge of
H
) the notation
H+e
denotes the graph with the same vertex set
V(H+e)=V(H)
and edge set
E(H+e)=E(H)\cup\{e\}
. If
U
is some set of vertices (not necessarily a subset of
V(H)
), the graph
H-U

is the graph with vertex set
V(H-U)=V(H)\setminus(V(H)\cap U)
and all edges induced by
H
on this vertex set. When
U=\{u\}
is a single vertex, we drop the set brackets and simply write
H-u
. We also drop set brackets for edges unless necessary, writing for example
e=uw
, instead of the more cumbersome
e=\{u,w\}
.

Given a graph
H
, vertices
u,w\in V(H)
and vertex subsets
U,W\subset V(H)
, we have that
N_{H}(u,U)
denotes the set of neighbours of
u
in
U
. If
U=V(H)
, we simply write
N_{H}(u)
. Similarly, the degree of
u
in
U
is
d_{H}(u,U):=|N_{H}(u,U)|
and
d_{H}(u)
if
U=V(H)
. The set
E_{H}(U,W)
denotes all edges with one endpoint in
U
and the other in
W
(with edges in
H[U\cap W]
counted once) and
e_{H}(U,W):=|E_{H}(U,W)|
. Finally
\dist_{H}(u,w)
denotes the distance between
u
and
w
in
H
, that is, the number of edges in a shortest path from
u
to
w
in
H
. In all of the above, we omit the subscript if the graph
H
is clear from context.

Given a set of integers
A
in some abelian group and
t\in\mathbb{Z}
, the set
tA:=\{ta:a\in A\}
denotes all the dilations of elements in
A
by
t
. Finally, for
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{R}
we write
a=(b\pm c)d
as shorthand for the statement that
(b-c)d\leq a\leq(b+c)d
.

2.2.
Graph bootstrap processes

Recall that
\langle G\rangle_{H}
denotes the final graph of the
H
-process on
G
, that is, the graph at which the
H
-process stabilises.
The following observation can be proven easily by induction on
i\geq 0
, see
[
16
, Observation 2.1]

Observation 2.1
.

Let
\varphi:G\to G^{\prime}
be an injective graph homomorphism, and let
(G_{i})_{i\geq 0}
,
(G^{\prime}_{i})_{i\geq 0}
be the respective
H
-processes on
G
and
G^{\prime}
.
Then
\varphi
is also a homomorphism from
G_{i}
to
G_{i}^{\prime}
for every
i\geq 0
. In particular,
\varphi(\langle G\rangle_{H})\subseteq\langle G^{\prime}\rangle_{H}
.

An immediate consequence of Observation
2.1
is that if
G^{\prime}\subseteq G
and
(G^{\prime}_{i})_{i\geq 0}
and
(G_{i})_{i\geq 0}
are the respective
H
-processes on
G^{\prime}
and
G
,
then
G^{\prime}_{i}\subseteq G_{i}
for every
i\geq 0
.
We say that a graph
G
is
H
-stable
if
\langle G\rangle_{H}=G
and
we call
H

self-stable
if
\langle H\rangle_{H}=H
. Clearly cliques and complete bipartite-graphs are self-stable. Moreover the following are examples of self-stable graphs.

Observation 2.2
.

Let
H
be a wheel with at least
5
vertices or the cube graph
Q_{3}
. Then
\langle H\rangle_{H}=H
.

Proof.

In both cases, fixing
e_{1}\in\binom{V(H)}{2}\setminus E(H)
and any
e_{0}\in E(H)
, we have that
H-e_{0}+e_{1}
has a vertex of degree 4 and so is not a copy of
H
. This shows that
e_{1}
cannot complete a copy of
H
with edges of
H
and so
H
is indeed self-stable.
∎

By Observation
2.1
, any
H
-stable graph containing
G
must also contain every graph of the
H
-process on
G
. This implies the following observation.

Observation 2.3
.

If
G
is an
n
-vertex graph, then

\langle G\rangle_{H}=\bigcap\big\{G^{\prime}:G^{\prime}\mbox{ is a }H\mbox{-stable graph on }V(G)\mbox{ with }G\subseteq G^{\prime}\big\},

where here the intersection of a collection of
n
-vertex graphs refers to the graph obtained by intersecting the edge sets of the graphs in the collection.

2.3.
Additive constructions

In some of our constructions, we use sets of integers that are free to solutions of certain additive equations. Given an abelian group
\Gamma
and an equation
E
of the form

(2.1)

\sum_{i=1}^{h}\alpha_{i}x_{i}=0,

with
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
for
i\in[h]
, we say a solution
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{h})\in\Gamma^{h}
to the equation is
trivial
if there is some partition of
[h]
as
[h]=T_{1}\cup\cdots\cup T_{\ell}
such that for each
j\in[\ell]
we have that
\sum_{i\in T_{j}}\alpha_{i}=0
and
x_{i}=x_{i^{\prime}}
for all
i\neq i^{\prime}\in T_{j}
. We denote by
r_{E}(\Gamma)
the size of a largest subset of
\Gamma
that is free from non-trivial solutions to the equation
E
and similarly,
r_{E}(N)
denotes the size of the largest subset of
[N]
free from non-trivial solutions to
E
.

The problem of determining these extremal functions encapsulates many well studied problems in additive combinatorics. For example, when
E
is the equation
x_{1}+x_{2}-x_{3}-x_{4}=0
, the only trivial solutions are when
\{x_{1},x_{2}\}=\{x_{3},x_{4}\}
and a subset free of non-trivial solutions is called a
Sidon set
. It is known that the largest Sidon set
S\subset[N]
has size
r_{E}(N)=N^{1/2}(1+o(1))
with the upper bound due to Erdős and Turán
[
14
]
and the lower bound being a construction of Singer
[
38
]
. When the equation
E
is
x_{1}+x_{2}-2x_{3}=0
, non-trivial solutions are precisely 3-term arithmetic progressions with non-zero common difference. In this case, Behrend’s famous construction
[
5
]
gives that
r_{E}(N)\geq N^{1-O(1/\sqrt{\log N})}=N^{1-o(1)}
whilst recent breakthrough results
[
6
,
23
]
have shown that
r_{E}(N)\leq N^{1-\Omega(1/(\log N)^{8/9})}
. In general, Ruzsa
[
36
]
initiated a systematic study of the behaviour of
r_{E}(N)
and its dependence on the defining equation
E
. In Section
7.1
we will revisit the methods of Ruzsa to give sets simultaneously avoiding non-trivial solutions to several equations at once.

2.4.
Chernoff’s inequality

We will use Chernoff’s inequality which we give in the following form (see
[
22
, Theorem 2.1, Corollary 2.4 and Theorem 2.8]
).

Theorem 2.4

(Chernoff bounds)
.

Let
X
be the sum of a set of mutually independent Bernoulli random variables and let
\lambda=\mathbb{E}[X]
. Then for any
0<\delta<\tfrac{3}{2}
, we have that

\mathbb{P}[X\geq(1+\delta)\lambda]\leq e^{-\delta^{2}\lambda/3}\hskip 5.69054pt\mbox{ and }\hskip 5.69054pt\mathbb{P}[X\leq(1-\delta)\lambda]\leq e^{-\delta^{2}\lambda/2}.

Furthermore, if
x\geq 7\lambda
, then
\mathbb{P}[X\geq x]\leq e^{-x}
.

3.
Graph classes

Our chain constructions give lower bounds on maximum running times
M_{H}(n)
for a wide range of different graphs
H
. Indeed, some of our results discussed in the introduction are in fact more general than stated there and apply to larger classes of graphs
H
with certain properties. Although the definitions of these properties are tailored to our methods and what we are able to prove, the resulting graph classes are easy to define and have some interesting consequences and connections to other concepts.
In this section, we explore simple properties of the classes of graphs
H
we are interested in, first looking at
inseparable
graphs and then introducing and exploring
Behrendian
and
Sidonian
graphs. Finally, we derive properties of the random graph (which is simply a distribution on the class of all graphs) that we need to prove Theorem
1.1
.

3.1.
Inseparable graphs

Recall from Definition
1.12
that we say a graph
H
is
(2,1)
-inseparable if we cannot disconnect
H
by removing an edge and at most two vertices. We remark that it is implicit in the definition that a
(2,1)
-inseparable graph is necessarily non-empty and has
v(H)\geq 4
. Moreover, as deleting an edge can only decrease the connectivity, we have that
(2,1)
-inseparable graphs are themselves 3-connected. As the only 3-connected graph
H
with
v(H)=4
is
K_{4}
and deleting an edge from
K_{4}
gives a graph with a vertex cut of size 2, we in fact have that any
(2,1)
-inseparable graph
H
necessarily has
v(H)\geq 5
.

We also have that
any 4-connected graph is
(2,1)
-inseparable as if one could remove an edge and two vertices to get a separated graph, then removing those two vertices and an appropriate endpoint of the edge will also disconnect the graph. Thus the class of
(2,1)
-inseparable graphs lies between 3-connected and 4-connected graphs. In particular, this has the following consequences.

Observation 3.1
.

A graph
H
with
v(H)=k\geq 5
is
(2,1)
-inseparable if:

(i)

\delta(H)\geq k/2+1
; or

(ii)

H
contains the square of a Hamilton cycle.

Proof.

Let
U\subseteq V(H)
be an arbitrary subset of
k-3
vertices.
We will show that
H[U]
is connected and thus
H
is
4
-connected and
(2,1)
-inseparable.
In the case that
H
satisfies
(i)
, if
H[U]
is disconnected then there exists
W\subseteq U
such that
|W|\leq|U|/2
and
H[W]
is a connected component of
H[U]
.
However, then for any
u\in W
,

d_{H}(u)\leq d_{H[W]}(u)+3\leq|W|-1+3\leq\left\lfloor(k-3)/2\right\rfloor+2<k/2+1,

which contradicts our assumption on
\delta(H)
. In the case that
H
satisfies
(ii)
, for
u,w\in U
arbitrary, we have two paths between
u
and
w
given by traversing the Hamilton cycle in different directions. One of these paths, say
P
, has at most one vertex not in
U
and so there is a path from
u
to
w
in
H[U]
by traversing
P
using an edge between two vertices of distance 2 in
P
to pass over the vertex of
P
missing in
U
(if necessary). Thus
H[U]
is indeed connected.
∎

3.1.1.
(1,1,1)
-inseparable graphs

As discussed in the introduction, we are particularly interested in
bipartite
graphs as for such graphs we have upper bounds coming from the extremal number (Proposition
1.6
). In the context of inseparable graphs, many of our proofs, in particular our super-linear lower bound for inseparable graphs (Theorem
1.13
) extend to bipartite graphs (see Theorem
8.3
) under slightly weaker conditions. We make the following definition which captures this.

Definition 3.2

(
(1,1,1)
-inseparable)
.

A bipartite graph
H
is
(1,1,1)
-inseparable
if
it cannot be disconnected by removing an edge and at most one vertex from each partite set.

Again, implicit in the definition of
(1,1,1)
-inseparability is the fact that we only consider non-empty graphs to be
(1,1,1)
-inseparable. Moreover,
(1,1,1)
-inseparable graphs must have at least 2 vertices in each part (otherwise we disconnect the graph by removing just one vertex) and in fact all
(1,1,1)
-inseparable graphs
H
have
v(H)\geq 5
as the only candidate for such a 4-vertex graph is
C_{4}
which is not
(1,1,1)
-inseparable.

Note that the definition of
(1,1,1)
-inseparable matches Definition
1.12
except that in the case that we can disconnect by removing one edge and two vertices, in the bipartite case we will only consider this to be valid if the two chosen vertices are in different parts of the bipartition. There are graphs for which this makes a difference. In particular, both the 3-dimensional cube
Q_{3}
and the complete bipartite graph
K_{3,s}
with
s\geq 3
are
not

(2,1)
-inseparable as they have vertices with degree 3. On the other hand, all these graphs
are

(1,1,1)
-inseparable.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
3\leq r\leq s
and suppose
H
is a bipartite graph with parts
X,Y
with
|X|=r
,
|Y|=s
and such that
d(x)\geq s/2+1
for all
x\in X
and
d(y)\geq r/2+1
for all
y\in Y
. Then
H
is
(1,1,1)
-inseparable.

Proof.

Suppose
H^{\prime}
is obtained from
H
by removing at most one vertex from
X
to get
X^{\prime}
, at most one vertex from
Y
to get
Y^{\prime}
and an edge in
H
between
x^{\prime}\in X^{\prime}
and
y^{\prime}\in Y^{\prime}
. Now if
x_{1},x_{2}\in X^{\prime}\setminus\{x^{\prime}\}
, then as
d_{H^{\prime}}(x_{1}),d_{H^{\prime}}(x_{2})\geq s/2+1-|Y\setminus Y^{\prime}|>|Y^{\prime}|/2
, we have that
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
have a common neighbour in
H^{\prime}
and therefore lie in the same connected component. Similarly for any
y_{1},y_{2}\in Y^{\prime}\setminus\{y^{\prime}\}
, we have that
d_{H^{\prime}}(y_{1}),d_{H^{\prime}}(y_{2})>|X^{\prime}|/2
and all the vertices in
Y^{\prime}\setminus\{y^{\prime}\}
lie in the same connected component in
H^{\prime}
. Moreover, all the vertices in
Y^{\prime}\setminus\{y^{\prime}\}
lie in the same connected component as the vertices in
X^{\prime}\setminus\{x^{\prime}\}
because for
y\in Y^{\prime}\setminus\{y^{\prime}\}
, we have that
d_{H^{\prime}}(y)\geq r/2\geq 3/2
and so
y
has at least one neighbour in
X^{\prime}\setminus\{x^{\prime}\}
. Finally it remains to establish that
x^{\prime}
and
y^{\prime}
are connected to the remainder of
H^{\prime}
but this follows because
d_{H^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})\geq s/2+1-2>0
and similarly for
y^{\prime}
.
∎

The next lemma shows that if a starting graph
G_{0}
is bipartite and
H
is
(1,1,1)
-inseparable then the graphs
G_{i}
in the
H
-process will remain bipartite throughout.
In fact
H
being 2-edge-connected would suffice for this.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
H
be a
(1,1,1)
-inseparable graph. If
G
is a bipartite graph with partite sets
X,Y\subset V(G)
, so is
\langle G\rangle_{H}
.

Proof.

Fix parts
X,Y
of a bipartition of
G
and let
(G_{i})_{i\geq 0}
be the
H
-process on
G
. Suppose for a contradiction that the final graph
\langle G\rangle_{H}
is not bipartite and pick the smallest
i\in\mathbb{N}
for which
G_{i}
contains an edge
e
whose endpoints lie in the same part of
G
. Furthermore, let
F
be a copy of
H
in
G_{i}
completed by
e
.
Then as
H
is
(1,1,1)
-inseparable, we have that
F-e
is connected and so there exists a path between the endpoints of
e
in
F-e\subseteq G_{i-1}
. As this path connects two vertices from different parts of the bipartite graph
F-e
, the path must have odd length but this contradicts that
G_{i-1}
is a bipartite graph respecting the bipartition
X,Y
.
∎

3.1.2.
Edges in inseparable graphs

Finally we need the following easy consequences of inseparability.

Observation 3.5
.

Let
H
be a
(2,1)
-inseparable graph or a
(1,1,1)
-inseparable bipartite graph. Then for any
e\in E(H)
, there exists an
f\in E(H)
that is vertex-disjoint from
e
. In particular,
H
contains a pair of non-incident edges.

Proof.

If no such
f\in E(H)
existed, then we could disconnect
H
by removing
e
and its two vertices (using here that
v(H)\geq 4
). This would contradict the definition of inseparability.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
H
be a
(2,1)
-inseparable graph or a
(1,1,1)
-inseparable bipartite graph,
e\in E(H)
and
F=H-e
. Then for any pair of edges
f,f^{\prime}\in E(F)
, there is a cycle in
F
containing both
f
and
f^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Due to
H
being inseparable, the deletion of any one vertex from
F
leaves a connected graph, that is,
F
is 2-connected. Let
f=uv
and
f^{\prime}=xy
and suppose first that
v=y
. By Menger’s theorem (see for example
[
39
, Section 4.2]
), there are two internally-vertex-disjoint paths with endpoints
u
and
x
, one of which avoids
v=y
and forms a cycle with
f
and
f^{\prime}
. On the other hand, if
f
and
f^{\prime}
are vertex-disjoint then a well-known consequence of Menger’s theorem (see for example
[
39
, Exercise 4.2.9]
) gives that there are two vertex-disjoint paths with one endpoint in
\{u,v\}
and the other in
\{x,y\}
. Together with
f
and
f^{\prime}
, these paths give the desired cycle.
∎

3.2.
Higher inseparability

In Section
6.1
, we will explore a class of graphs
H
which have a higher level of inseparability.

Definition 3.7
.

We say that a graph
H
is
(\ell,1)
-inseparable
if it cannot be disconnected by the removal of an edge and at most
\ell
vertices.

By definition, we have that
(\ell,1)
-inseparable graphs are
(\ell^{\prime},1)
-inseparable for all
1\leq\ell^{\prime}\leq\ell
.

Remark 3.8
.

If
H
with
k=v(H)\geq 6
is
(\lceil k/2\rceil,1)
-inseparable then for any
e\in E(H)
and
F=H-e
, we have that
F[U]
is connected for all
U\subset V(F)
with
|U|

at least

\lfloor k/2\rfloor
.

In Theorem
6.5
we will show that any
(\lceil k/2\rceil,1)
-inseparable graph
H
with
k\geq 6
vertices has
M_{H}(n)=\Omega(n^{2})
. Note that this recovers the result of Balogh, Kronenberg, Pokrovskiy and Szabó
[
3
]
that
M_{K_{k}}(n)
is quadratic in
n
for all
k\geq 6
. Indeed, for any subset
U\subset V(K_{k})
with
|U|=\lfloor k/2\rfloor
, we have that
K_{k}[U]
is a clique of size at least 3 and so is 2-edge-connected. In fact, any graph
H
with a large enough minimum degree is
(\lceil k/2\rceil,1)
-inseparable.

Lemma 3.9
.

If
H
is a graph with
k:=v(H)\geq 6
vertices and minimum degree
\delta(H)>3k/4
. Then
H
is
(\lceil k/2\rceil,1)
-inseparable.

Proof.

It suffices to show that for any
U\subseteq V(H)
with
|U|=\lfloor k/2\rfloor
we have that
H[U]
is
2
-edge-connected. So suppose for a contradiction that there is some
U
for which this is not the case.
Then we can find
U^{\prime}\subset U
with
1\leq|U^{\prime}|\leq\lfloor|U|/2\rfloor
such that there is at most one edge between
U^{\prime}
and
U\setminus U^{\prime}
.
If
|U^{\prime}|\geq 2
we can pick a vertex
u\in U^{\prime}
without neighbours in
U\setminus U^{\prime}
and arrive at

\delta(H)\leq d(u)\leq k-1-|U\setminus U^{\prime}|\leq k-1-\left\lceil\frac{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}{2}\right\rceil\leq\left\lfloor\frac{3k}{4}\right\rfloor,

a contradiction.
If
U^{\prime}=\{u\}
is a single vertex then
u
has at most one neighbour in
U\setminus\{u\}
and so

\delta(H)\leq d(u)\leq k-|U|+1=\left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil+1\leq\left\lfloor\frac{3k}{4}\right\rfloor,

which is again a contradiction, where we used that
k\geq 6
in the final inequality.
∎

Theorem
1.2
part (
1.3
) is thus an immediate consequence of Lemma
3.9
and Theorem
6.5
mentioned above, which gives quadratic running times for all
(\lceil k/2\rceil,1)
-inseparable
k
-vertex graphs.
The connection between minimum degree and
(\lceil k/2\rceil,1)
-inseparable
k
-vertex graphs goes further.

Observation 3.10
.

If
H
is a
k
-vertex
(\lceil k/2\rceil,1)
-inseparable graph, then
\delta(H)\geq k/2+2
.

Proof.

Suppose that there is some vertex
v\in V(H)
with
d(v)\leq\lceil k/2\rceil+1
. Then we can take a set
v\in U\subset V(H)
such that
|U|=\lfloor k/2\rfloor
and
|U\cap N_{H}(v)|\leq 1
. Removing at most one edge in
U
separates
v
from the rest of
U
, implying that
H
is not
(\lceil k/2\rceil,1)
-inseparable.
∎

Therefore the class of
(\lceil k/2\rceil,1)
-inseparable
k
-vertex graphs is sandwiched between
k
-vertex graphs with minimum degree greater than
3k/4
and the class of graphs with minimum degree greater than
k/2+1
. Subject to the condition imposed by Observation
3.10
, the notion of
(\lceil k/2\rceil,1)
-inseparability captures many graphs
H
satisfying weak pseudorandom conditions. For example, we say a pair of disjoint vertex sets
A,B\subset V(H)
with
|A|=|B|=\ell
and
e_{H}(A,B)=0
is a
bipartite hole
of size
\ell
(these were introduced by McDiarmid and Yolov
[
33
]
in the context of Hamiltonicity). For
2\leq\ell\leq k/2
, one can show that any
k
-vertex graph
H
with
\delta(H)\geq k/2+\ell
and no bipartite hole of size
\ell
, is
(\lceil k/2\rceil,1)
-inseparable. Further natural examples of
k
-vertex
(\lceil k/2\rceil,1)
-inseparable graphs are complete tripartite graphs with no part larger than
\lfloor k/2\rfloor-2
and
t^{th}
powers of cycles for
t\geq k/4+1
, which are obtained by cyclically ordering the vertices and connecting each vertex to all vertices within distance
t
in the ordering. All these graphs have quadratic maximum running time, as we will show in Theorem
6.5
.

3.3.
Behrendian and Sidonian graphs

In Section
7
we will give a general construction for lower bounds on running times which are defined via a set of
dilations
.
When this set of dilations is generated using Behrend’s construction of arithmetic progression-free sets, the construction will allow us to prove lower bounds of the form
M_{H}(n)\geq n^{2-o(1)}
. It turns out that this works whenever we can guarantee that
H-e
is
Behrendian
for any
e\in E(H)
, with Behrendian graphs being defined as follows.

Definition 3.11

(Behrendian graphs)
.

We say a connected graph
H
is
Behrendian
if any colouring of
E(H)
which is non-monochromatic results in a non-monochromatic cycle in
H
which is the union of at most three monochromatic paths.

Remark 3.12
.

We restrict our attention to Behrendian graphs which are connected; any disconnected graph satisfying the condition would have one Behrendian component and all other components being isolated vertices.

At first sight, the definition of Behrendian graphs may appear somewhat mysterious. We will show that the notion can be thought of as yet another type of connectivity and encompasses many graphs of interest. For example, a graph
H
is
triangularly connected
if for any pair of distinct edges
e,f\in E(H)
, there is a sequence of triangles
T_{1},\ldots,T_{m}
in
H
with
e\in E(T_{1})
,
f\in E(T_{m})
and consecutive triangles in the ordering intersecting in an edge.
It is not hard to see that triangularly connected graphs are Behrendian. Indeed, if
E(H)
is coloured in a non-monochromatic fashion, there are edges
e,f\in E(H)
with distinct colours but if
H
is Behrendian, then there is no triangle that is
not
monochromatic, forcing all the triangles in the sequence
T_{1},\ldots,T_{m}
between
e
and
f
to be the same colour as
e
, a contradiction.
Triangularly connected graphs have been studied in the context of integer flows
[
18
,
21
,
31
]
and provide a large family of Behrendian graphs which includes any triangulation of a surface and the square of any connected graph (see e.g.
[
18

For us, the significance of Behrendian graphs comes in the applications of our dilation chain constructions which will give almost quadratic running times for graphs with minimum degree at least

Lemma 3.13
.

Consider the class
\mathcal{C}
of graphs obtained by the following rules:

(1)

H=K_{2}\in\mathcal{C}

(2)

If

(3)

If

and distinct vertices

such that
H=H_{1}\cup H_{2}\cup H_{3}

V(H_{1})\cap V(H_{3})=\{v\}
,
V(H_{2})\cap V(H_{3})=\{w\}
, and
H_{1},H_{2},H_{3}\in\mathcal{C}
, then

.

Then
\mathcal{C}
is precisely the class of Behrendian graphs.

Proof.

We first show that any graph in
\mathcal{C}
is a Behrendian graph. Connectivity follows easily from the rules as

is connected and inputs of connected

is as in (

both Behrendian (and thus connected) and let

.
If one of the restricted colourings

,

is non-monochromatic we can find the desired non-monochromatic cycle in

by the assumption that

and

are Behrendian.
Therefore we can assume that
\chi|_{E(H_{1})}
and

are both monochromatic with different colours.
Take distinct vertices

, a

-path

in
H_{1}
, and a
v_{1}v_{2}
-path

in
H_{2}
(using here that both

and

are connected).
If

is a cycle then it is the union of two monochromatic paths in different colours and we are done. If

is not a cycle, then there is a non-empty set of vertices

Again, if one of

It remains to show that any Behrendian graph lies in

to be Behrendian and
take the smallest

This is well-defined as

by (

We will show that

Suppose for a contradiction that

Any two of the

and

have two common vertices for some

and hence

In particular, the

are pairwise edge-disjoint.
Define an

by sending

to the unique

Since

If there exist

Assume then that there are distinct

Thus we see that

In particular, Observation

Lemma 3.15

(2)

Let

of

For case (

and

If

is a good choice. If there is no such

3.3.1.
Sidonian graphs

There are no bipartite graphs that satisfy Definition
3.11
of being Behrendian as if we colour a bipartite graph
H
in a
rainbow
fashion, that is giving each edge a unique colour, then any cycle in
H
has length at least 4 and therefore induces at least 4 colours. Nonetheless, we will be able to work with a somewhat analogous variant which we call
Sidonian

7
, we will be able to provide lower bounds on running times of the type
M_{H}(n)\geq n^{3/2-o(1)}
by using a set of dilations based on a variation of a Sidon set. We will show in Theorem
7.11
that this construction works whenever
H-e
satisfies the following property of being

, for any choice of

.

Definition 3.16
.

We say a bipartite graph
H
is
Sidonian
if any non-monochromatic colouring of
E(H)
results in a non-monochromatic copy of
C_{4}
.

The definition of Sidonian is perhaps not the most natural generalisation of Behrendian to a bipartite setting and is rather formulated to fit with our application to running times in Theorem
7.11
.
In particular, we do not get a characterisation as in Lemma
3.13
in this bipartite setting. Some consequences of the definition do carry over naturally though. Indeed, analogously to triangularly connected graphs being Behrendian, one can see that if there is a sequence of intersecting
C_{4}

H
, then it is necessarily Sidonian. This includes all
quadrangulations
of a surface, for example. For us, the notion will be used to prove Theorem

where we will need that all bipartite graphs with sufficiently large minimum degree conditions are Sidonian, even after deleting an edge.

Lemma 3.17
.

Let
3\leq r\leq s
and suppose

is a bipartite graph with parts

|X|=r
,

Proof.

Let

be arbitrary with

and

\chi
of

with no non-monochromatic copy of

. We will show that

is necessarily monochromatic and so

is Sidonian. Firstly, let

be the graph obtained from

by further deleting the endpoints

of

and all their incident edges. We claim that

is monochromatic under

with

and
\{u,w\}=f\cup f^{\prime}\setminus\{v\}
, we have that

(3.1)

d_{H-e}(u)+d_{H-e}(w)=d_{H}(u)+d_{H}(w)\geq\ell+2,

with

Hence there is some vertex

3.4.
Random graphs

We are interested in the binomial random graph
H=G(k,p)
with vertex set
[k]
and each pair of vertices

Fact 3.18

If

then a.a.s

Mostly, we are interested in the range

above the connectivity threshold. Here we will show that a.a.s.

[
9

Fact 3.19
.

If

then a.a.s.

is 4-connected and so is
(2,1)
-inseparable.

We will also use that

is self-stable. In order to deduce this, note that if

and a non-trivial

1

1

That is, not the identity map.

such that

and

4.
Chains

In this section, we introduce the concept of

4.1
) is given in generality and we then define a chain to be

(Definition

) if it satisfies extra conditions that allow us to control the running time of a construction based on the chain. Indeed, the key result of this section is Lemma
4.6

H
-process on a starting graph formed by a proper
H
-chain after removing certain edges. We remark that our methods here are closely related to those used by
[
3
,
7
]
in giving lower bounds for

with

as well as the constructions in
[

,

34
]
investigating

-processes for hypergraph cliques

. Indeed, all these previous constructions utilise starting graphs which are defined by certain chains and the proofs work by identifying properties of the chain that allow control of the running time for these constructions. In
[
7
]
, they use the terminology of

[
3
]
, they introduce an ordered hypergraph to characterise the chain and appeal to the key property of the hypergraph being

is analogous to these previous concepts but, in order to deal with general graphs

, we need to refine our definition. The resulting conditions are geared towards being widely applicable, as we will demonstrate in the remainder of the paper. Before introducing formally our definitions, we provide some motivation for the concept, discussing how chains appear in all
H

Consider an
H

and some integer

then triggers the completion of

in the next step. Indeed, such a sequence can be formed in reverse, starting with an edge

. Then there is some

-chain

When the graph

is clear from context, we will simply refer to a

.
of
length

\tau
is a sequence
(H_{i},e_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}
of copies
H_{i}
of
H
together with edges
e_{i}\in E(H_{i})
such that

e_{i}\in E(H_{i})\cap E(H_{i+1})

.
We call

The discussion above shows that any

(G_{i})_{i\geq 0}

Before discussing this approach in general, we now introduce the notion of a

Definition 4.2

(Simple chains)
.

Let
4\leq k\in\mathbb{N}
,

and suppose that
H

we have that
e_{i}=\varphi_{i}(f^{\prime})=\varphi_{i+1}(e^{\prime})=V(H_{i})\cap V(H_{i+1})

.

Remark 4.3
.

Note that the definition of a simple chain requires

In most cases though, the choice of valid ordering used to give the simple chains will have no bearing on the following arguments and any choice will suffice.

For a simple

(H_{i},e_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}
of length

n:=|V(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\tau}H_{i})|=\tau(v(H)-2)+2
vertices. For many graphs

can give a starting graph

with linear running time. This is the case, for example, with cliques of size at least 4 (as in Figure

and

).

4.1.
Proper chains

Our main aim with chain constructions is to provide
super-linear
bounds on running times. Therefore, we cannot require such a strict intersection condition as in Definition
4.2
and the copies
H_{i}
of
H
in our chain will have to overlap more significantly in order to give constructions achieving longer running times.
When considering such chains, we will need extra conditions that guarantee no additional edges are added that interfere with the desired percolation along the chain.
This leads us to the following definition of
proper
chains, recalling that
\langle G\rangle_{H}
denotes the graph at which the
H
-process on
G
stabilises.

Definition 4.4

(Proper chains)
.

An
H
-chain
(H_{i},e_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}
with an additional assigned edge
e_{0}\in E(H_{1})\setminus\{e_{1}\}
is called
proper
if:

(1)

e_{i}\notin E(\langle H_{j}\rangle_{H})
for
1\leq j<i\leq\tau
;

(2)

for any
e\in E(H)
and copy
F
of
H-e
in
\bigcup_{i=1}^{\tau}\langle H_{i}\rangle_{H}
, there is a unique
j\in[\tau]
such that
F\subseteq\langle H_{j}\rangle_{H}
. Furthermore,
F\not\subseteq\left(\bigcup_{i\in[\tau]\setminus j}\langle H_{i}\rangle_{H}\bigcup H_{j}\right)-\{e_{j-1},e_{j}\}
.

We will refer to the additional assigned edge
e_{0}
in a proper chain as the
origin
of the chain. This is an edge whose addition will kickstart the percolation along the chain. For the sake of brevity, we will often suppress the mention of the origin, especially if it has no bearing on the statement or argument in question. When referring to a proper chain or proving that a chain is proper or not, it is taken to be implicit that it has an origin defined. In particular, when using simple chains as in Definition
4.2
, we will always take a canonical choice of origin as
e_{0}:=v_{1}v_{2}=\varphi_{1}(e^{\prime})
. In Figure
2
, this choice is highlighted in blue. However, note that the chain
(H_{i},e_{i})_{i\in[4]}
in Figure
2
(and indeed any simple
K_{4}
-chain) is
not
actually proper as, for example, the vertex set
\{v_{1},v_{3},v_{4},v_{5}\}
hosts a copy of
K_{4}^{-}
that is not contained in one of the
H_{i}=\langle H_{i}\rangle_{K_{4}}
, violating condition
(2)
. This example shows that it is not necessary for an
H
-chain
(H_{i},e_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}
with underlying graph
G=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\tau}H_{i}
to be proper in order to have
\tau_{H}(G-\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{\tau}\})\geq\tau
. Indeed one can check that a simple
K_{4}
-chain
(H_{i},e_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}
as in Figure
2
adds the edge
e_{i}
at time step
i
for
i\in[\tau]
.

However, the conditions of proper chains are natural and allow more refined control over the percolation process, which will be essential for our purposes. Condition
(1)
ensures that an edge
e_{j}
is not added prematurely before step
j
in the process. Condition
(2)
of Definition
4.4
guarantees that the only edges added during the process are those that we expect from running the process individually on each
H_{i}
(see Lemma
4.5
below) so we can be sure that there are no unwanted edges being added that will accelerate the process. Finally the ‘furthermore’ part of condition
(2)
implies that for each
j\geq 2
, the process on
H_{j}
is not triggered until the addition of the edge
e_{j-1}
.

From this point onwards we will deal exclusively with proper chains and we begin by proving properties of the percolation processes based on these chains.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
(H_{i},e_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}
be a proper
H
-chain with underlying graph
G:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\tau}H_{i}
. Then we have that
\langle G\rangle_{H}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\tau}\langle H_{i}\rangle_{H}
.

Proof.

Certainly we have that
\langle H_{i}\rangle_{H}\subseteq\langle G\rangle_{H}
for all
i\in[\tau]
due to Observation
2.1
. For the other inclusion, note that
G\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{\tau}\langle H_{i}\rangle_{H}
and so by Observation
2.3
it suffices to establish that
\bigcup_{i=1}^{\tau}\langle H_{i}\rangle_{H}
is
H
-stable, which follows from condition
(2)
of Definition
4.4
. ∎

We now show that proper chains can be used to give lower bounds on running times.

Lemma 4.6

(Running times for proper chains)
.

Let
(H_{i},e_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}
be a proper
H
-chain with underlying graph
G:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\tau}H_{i}
.
Then the graph
G_{0}:=G-\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{\tau}\}
satisfies
\tau_{H}(G_{0})\geq\tau
, and therefore

M_{H}\left(v(G)\right)\geq\tau.

Proof.

The lemma follows from the fact that in the
H
-process
(G_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on
G_{0}=G-\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{\tau}\}
we have

E(G_{t})\cap\{e_{1},,\ldots,e_{\tau}\}=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{t}\}

for every
0\leq t\leq\tau
.
To prove this fact we induct on
t
to show the following stronger statement

(4.1)

E(G_{t})\cap\{e_{1},,\ldots,e_{\tau}\}=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{t}\}\quad\text{ and }\quad G_{t}\;\subseteq\;\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}\langle H_{i}\rangle_{H}\>\cup\>\bigcup_{i=t+1}^{\tau}H_{i}.

The case
t=0
follows immediately from the definition of
G_{0}
.
Let
t\geq 1
.
The induction hypothesis yields

(4.2)

E(G_{t-1})\cap\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{\tau}\}=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{t-1}\}

and

(4.3)

G_{t-1}\;\subseteq\;\bigcup_{i=1}^{t-1}\langle H_{i}\rangle_{H}\>\cup\>\bigcup_{i=t}^{\tau}H_{i}.

Now property
(1)
of Definition
4.1
implies that
E(H_{t})\cap\{e_{t},\ldots,e_{\tau}\}=\{e_{t}\}
and so
H_{t}-e_{t}\subseteq G_{0}+\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{t-1}\}\subseteq G_{t-1}
, where (
4.2
) was used in the second subgraph inclusion. Therefore we have that
e_{t}\in E(G_{t})
, as required.
Also property
(2)
of Definition
4.1
and (
4.3
) guarantee that every copy of
H
completed at time
t
lies in
\langle H_{j}\rangle_{H}
for some
j\in[\tau]
, while (
4.2
), (
4.3
) and the ‘furthermore’ part of property
(2)
imply that
j\leq t
for any such
j\in[\tau]
.
Therefore,
G_{t}\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}\langle H_{i}\rangle_{H}\cup\bigcup_{i=t+1}^{\tau}H_{i}
.

Finally, we have to make sure that
e_{i^{\prime}}\notin E(G_{t})
for
t<i^{\prime}\leq\tau
.
Suppose for a contradiction there was some
i^{\prime}\in[t+1,\tau]
with
e_{i^{\prime}}\in E(G_{t})
.
Then
e_{i^{\prime}}
lies in
E(G_{t})\setminus E(G_{t-1})
because of (
4.2
).
Let
H^{\prime}
be a copy of
H
completed by
e_{i^{\prime}}
at time
t
. In the previous paragraph, we showed that any copy of
H
completed at time
t
, in particular
H^{\prime}
, lies in
\langle H_{j}\rangle_{H}
for some
j\leq t
.
However, property
(1)
of being proper gives
e_{i^{\prime}}\notin E(\langle H_{j}\rangle_{H})
so we have arrived at the desired contradiction.
Thus
\{e_{t+1},\ldots,e_{\tau}\}\cap E(G_{t})=\varnothing
, concluding the induction step.
∎

Lemma
4.6
shows that proper chains can give lower bounds on running times. However it is not clear that proper
H
-chains (of arbitrary length) exist for all
H
. Indeed, for example, there are no such chains if
\delta(H)=1
because many unwanted copies of
H-e
can be found when
e
is taken to be the edge incident to the degree 1 vertex. We also saw that simple
K_{4}
-chains
as in Figure
2
are not proper. We close this section by showing that, for many graphs
H
, simple chains
do
give examples of proper chains. First, we show this under the assumption that
H
is inseparable. As simple chains exist for inseparable graphs (Observation
3.5
), this gives linear sized proper chains.

Lemma 4.7
.

If
H
is
(2,1)
-inseparable or
(1,1,1)
-inseparable then any simple
H
-chain is proper.

Proof.

Let
(H_{i},e_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}
be a simple
H
-chain with origin
e_{0}
, and let
\hat{G}:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\tau}\langle H_{i}\rangle_{H}
. Condition
(1)
of Definition
4.4
is clearly satisfied by the definition of the simple chain.
Now let
e\in E(H)
, and let
F
be a copy of
H-e
in
\hat{G}
.
Suppose that
V(F)
does not coincide with a single
V(H_{j})
.
Pick the smallest
j\in[\tau-1]
such that
V(F)\cap V(H_{j})\setminus(V(H_{j+1})\cup\ldots\cup V(H_{\tau}))\neq\varnothing
.
Since the intersection of
\bigcup_{i=1}^{j}\langle H_{i}\rangle_{H}
and
\bigcup_{i=j+1}^{\tau}\langle H_{i}\rangle_{H}
is precisely the edge
e_{j}
, we have that removing the two vertices of the edge
e_{j}
disconnects
F
, contradicting that
H
is inseparable. In the case that
H
is
(1,1,1)
-inseparable, we used here that
\hat{G}
is also bipartite by Lemma
3.4
and the embedding of
F
must respect the partition of
\hat{G}
, which in turn implies that the vertices of
e_{j}
are indeed in different parts of the bipartition of
F
. So
we must have that
V(F)\subseteq V(H_{j})
.
All edges in
\hat{G}[V(H_{j})]
lie in
\langle H_{j}\rangle_{H}
because the chain is simple, so
F\subseteq\langle H_{j}\rangle_{H}
, establishing the first part of condition
(2)
in Definition
4.4
.
Finally, in the graph
G^{\prime}:=\left(\bigcup_{i\in[\tau]\setminus j}\langle H_{i}\rangle_{H}\bigcup H_{j}\right)-\{e_{j-1},e_{j}\}
there are only
e(H)-2
edges with both endpoints in
V(H_{j})
.
Therefore we cannot have that
F\subset G^{\prime}
and the ‘furthermore’ part of condition
(2)
in Definition
4.1
is also satisfied. This completes the proof.
∎

We remark that the definitions of
(2,1)
-inseparable graphs (Definition
1.12
) and
(1,1,1)
-inseparable graphs (Definition
3.2
) stem precisely from Lemma
4.7
as the conditions that arise when trying to prove properness of simple chains.
However, even when a graph
H
is not inseparable, it is still possible to have proper chains. This is the case for the wheel graph, as we now show.

Lemma 4.8
.

For
7\leq k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
W_{k}
be the wheel graph with
V(W_{k})=[k+1]
such that
v\in\{3,\ldots,k-1\}
is a universal hub vertex and
W_{k}-v
is a cycle of length
k
containing edges
\{1,2\}
and
\{k,k+1\}
. Then for any
\tau\in\mathbb{N}
, the simple
H
-chain
(H_{i},e_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}
of length
\tau
is proper.

Proof.

Recall that
W_{k}
is self-stable (Observation
2.2
).
Now consider the simple chain
(H_{i},e_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}
as given by Definition
4.2
with
H=W_{k}
, the vertex ordering as in the statement of the lemma and the canonical choice of origin
e_{0}=\{v_{1},v_{2}\}
. We certainly have that
e_{i}\notin E(\langle H_{j}\rangle_{H})=E(H_{j})
for
1\leq j<i\leq\tau
by construction and so the chain satisfies condition
(1)
of being proper (Definition
4.4
). For condition
(2)
, fix some
e\in E(H)
and a copy
F
of
H-e
in
G=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\tau}\langle H_{i}\rangle_{H}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\tau}H_{i}
. We need to show that there is a unique
j\in[\tau]
such that
F\subseteq\langle H_{j}\rangle_{H}=H_{j}
and as
G[V(H_{j})]=H_{j}
, it suffices to show that
V(F)\subseteq V(H_{j})
. Let
v^{\prime}
be the vertex in
F
corresponding to the universal vertex
v
. As
d_{F}(v^{\prime})\geq d_{H}(v)-1\geq 6
, we have that there is some
j\in[\tau]
such that
v^{\prime}
is the universal vertex of
H_{j}
. Indeed all other vertices in
G
have
d_{G}(v)\leq 5
. If
v\notin e
then all other vertices of
F
are adjacent to
v^{\prime}
and so lie in
V(H_{j})
. If
v\in e
, we have that there is some
w\in V(F)
such that
F-v^{\prime}-w
is a path
P
with
k-1
vertices, all of which are adjacent to
v^{\prime}
in
G
. Thus
V(P)\subseteq V(H_{j})
and
P
is a subpath of the cycle
H_{j}-v^{\prime}
. This implies that
w\in V(H_{j})
as no vertex outside of
V(H_{j})
can complete a cycle with
P
. This proves that
V(F)\subseteq V(H_{j})
and so
F\subseteq H_{j}
. The ‘furthermore’ part of condition
(2)
for being proper follows because
(G-\{e_{j-1},e_{j}\})[V(H_{j})]
has
e(H)-2=2k-2
edges and so cannot contain a copy of
F
. ∎

5.
Linking collections of chains

In order to get lower bounds for running times of graph bootstrap processes, we can use Lemma
4.6
which reduces the question to exhibiting long proper chains. In this section we show how to construct such chains by
linking
a collection of smaller proper chains. The birth of this idea is due to Balogh, Kronenberg, Pokrovskiy and Szabó
[
3
]
who linked chains to prove their lower bound on the running time of the
K_{5}
-process. As with the previous section, our aim here is to axiomatise this procedure in order to produce general results with wide applicability. We begin by extending the definition of a proper chain (Definition
4.4
) to a collection of chains.

Definition 5.1

(Proper collection of chains)
.

Let
A
be an indexing set,
V
a vertex set and
\tau\in\mathbb{N}
. Suppose that for each
a\in A
we have a proper
H
-chain
(H^{a}_{i},e^{a}_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}
with origin
e_{0}^{a}
and underlying graph
G^{a}=\bigcup_{i\in[\tau]}H^{a}_{i}
on
V
. We say that the collection
\mathcal{H}=\{(H^{a}_{i},e^{a}_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}:a\in A\}
is
proper
if:

(i)

for any
a\neq b\in A
and
i\in\{0,\ldots,\tau\}
we have that
e_{i}^{a}\notin\langle G^{b}\rangle_{H}
;

(ii)

for any
e\in E(H)
and copy
F
of
H-e
in
\bigcup_{a\in A}\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H}
, there exists a unique
b\in A
such that
F\subseteq\langle G^{b}\rangle_{H}
.
Furthermore, for the unique
j\in[\tau]
such that
F\subseteq\langle H_{j}^{b}\rangle_{H}
, we have that
F\nsubseteq\left(\bigcup_{a\in A\setminus b}\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H}\bigcup_{i\in[\tau]\setminus j}\langle H_{i}^{b}\rangle_{H}\bigcup H^{b}_{j}\right)-\{e^{b}_{j-1},e^{b}_{j}\}
.

Note that due to Lemma
4.5
, we have that for each
a\in A
, the final graph of the chain
(H^{a}_{i},e^{a}_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}
has the form
\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\tau}\langle H_{i}^{a}\rangle_{H}
. Note also that in condition
(ii)
, we implicitly use that as
F\subseteq\langle G^{b}\rangle_{H}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\tau}\langle H_{i}^{b}\rangle_{H}
and the chain
(H^{b}_{i},e^{b}_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}
is proper, there is some unique
j\in[\tau]
such that
F\subseteq\langle H_{j}^{b}\rangle_{H}
.
We will soon see how to construct a single long proper chain from a proper collection of chains. First though, we derive some implications of the definition. We begin by showing that the second condition simplifies when
H
is self-stable.

Remark 5.2
.

If
\langle H\rangle_{H}=H
, then condition
(ii)
can be replaced by the following condition:

(iienumi)

for any
e\in E(H)
and copy
F
of
H-e
in
\bigcup_{a\in A}G^{a}
, there is a unique
b\in A
such that
F\subseteq G^{b}
.

Proof.

Using that
\langle H\rangle_{H}=H
, we have that
\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\tau}\langle H_{i}^{a}\rangle_{H}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\tau}H^{a}_{i}=G^{a}
for each
a\in A
.
Thus the first part of condition
(ii)
reduces to
(iienumi)
when
H
is self-stable. To see that the ‘furthermore’ part of condition
(ii)
is redundant when
\langle H\rangle_{H}=H
, note that if our copy
F
of
H-e
is contained in
\langle H^{b}_{j}\rangle_{H}=H_{j}^{b}
and
F\subseteq\left(\bigcup_{a\in A\setminus b}\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H}\bigcup_{i\in[\tau]\setminus j}\langle H_{i}^{b}\rangle_{H}\bigcup H^{b}_{j}\right)-\{e^{b}_{j-1},e^{b}_{j}\}
, then we have that
F\subseteq H^{b}_{j}-\{e^{b}_{j-1},e^{b}_{j}\}
which is impossible as
H^{b}_{j}-\{e^{b}_{j-1},e^{b}_{j}\}
does not contain enough edges.
∎

For some of our constructions, it will be convenient for us to work with stronger conditions. We make the following definition of being
strongly proper.

Definition 5.3

(Strongly proper collection of chains)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=\{(H^{a}_{i},e^{a}_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}:a\in A\}
be a collection of proper
H
-chains such that for each
a\in A
, the chain
(H^{a}_{i},e^{a}_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}
has underlying graph
G^{a}=\bigcup_{i\in[\tau]}H^{a}_{i}
. We say that the collection
\mathcal{H}
is
strongly proper
if the following hold:

(I)

the graphs
\{\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H}:a\in A\}
are pairwise edge-disjoint.

(II)

for any
e\in E(H)
and copy
F
of
H-e
in
\bigcup_{a\in A}\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H}
, there is a
b\in A
such that
F\subseteq\langle G^{b}\rangle_{H}
.

As hinted by our terminology, we now show that strongly proper collections are indeed proper.

Lemma 5.4
.

Suppose
\mathcal{H}=\{(H^{a}_{i},e^{a}_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}:a\in A\}
is a strongly proper collection of
H
-chains. Then
\mathcal{H}
is proper.

Proof.

We need to verify that the conditions
(I)
and
(II)
imply the conditions
(i)
and
(ii)
. So let us fix some strongly proper collection
\mathcal{H}=\{(H^{a}_{i},e^{a}_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}:a\in A\}
(and an implicit choice of origin
e_{0}^{a}
for each
a\in A
). Firstly, if
a\neq b\in A
and
i\in\{0,\ldots,\tau\}
then we certainly have that
e_{i}^{a}\in H_{i}^{a}\subset\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H}
(or
e_{i}^{a}\in H_{i+1}^{a}
if
i=0
) and so by
(I)
there is no other
b\in A\setminus a
such that
e_{i}^{a}\in\langle G^{b}\rangle_{H}
, verifying
(i)
.

Next, suppose that
e\in E(H)
and
F
is a copy of
H-e
in
\bigcup_{a\in A}\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H}
. Then by
(II)
, we have that there is a
b\in A
such that
F\subseteq\langle G^{b}\rangle_{H}
and this choice of
b\in A
is unique by
(I)
establishing the first part of
(ii)
.
For the ‘furthermore’ part of condition
(ii)
, taking the
j\in[\tau]
such that
F\subset\langle H_{j}^{b}\rangle
, we have that no edge of
F
lies in
\bigcup_{a\in A\setminus b}\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H}
by condition
(I)
. Therefore if the ‘furthermore’ condition of
(ii)
is violated, then
F\subset\left(\bigcup_{i\in[\tau]\setminus j}\langle H_{i}^{b}\rangle_{H}\bigcup H_{j}^{b}\right)-\{e^{b}_{j-1},e^{b}_{j}\}
which contradicts condition
(2)
of the chain
(H^{b}_{i},e^{b}_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}
being proper (Definition
4.4
). We therefore conclude that
\mathcal{H}
is indeed proper.
∎

5.1.
Long chains from proper collections

We are now ready to form long chains from proper collections of chains via linking. The idea here is simple. Taking a proper collection of chains
\mathcal{H}=\{(H_{i}^{a},e_{i}^{a})_{i\in[\tau]}:a\in A\}
on a vertex set
V
(with origins
e_{0}^{a}
), we introduce an order on
A
and for each pair of consecutive
a
and
b
in
A
, we introduce a new chain
(J^{a}_{\ell},e_{\ell})_{\ell\in[2k]}
which runs between
H^{a}_{\tau}
and
H^{b}_{1}
and whose vertices are new apart from at the edge that connects to
H^{a}_{\tau}
(which will be
e_{\tau}^{a}
) and the edge that connects to
H^{b}_{1}
(which will be
e_{0}^{b}
). Moreover, we guarantee that all the new vertices added for distinct
J_{\ell}^{a}
are disjoint. This results in one long chain with length
|A|\tau+2k\cdot(|A|-1)\geq|A|\tau
. We have also added roughly
2v(H)k|A|
new vertices for these linking chains but this will be
O(|V|)
in applications. Finally, we will use that
\mathcal{H}
was a proper collection to derive that this new long chain is also proper.

Lemma 5.5

(Long chains from proper collections)
.

Let
H
be an
(2,1)
-inseparable graph,
\tau\leq n\in\mathbb{N}
and
V
an
n
-vertex set. Suppose that
\mathcal{H}=\{(H^{a}_{i},e^{a}_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}:a\in A\}
is a proper collection of
H
-chains on the vertex set
V
.
Then there exists a proper
H
-chain of length at least
\tau|A|
on at most
2v(H)^{2}n
vertices.
Moreover, the same holds if
H
is
(1,1,1)
-inseparable and
\bigcup_{a\in A,i\in[\tau]}H^{a}_{i}
is bipartite on
V
.

Proof.

For
a\in A
, let
e_{0}^{a}
be the origin of the chain
(H_{i}^{a},e^{a}_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}
. We begin by (arbitrarily) introducing an order on
A
and so we can assume that
A=[s]
with
s=|A|\in\mathbb{N}
. We also fix
k:=v(H)
, let
e^{\prime},f^{\prime}\in E(H)
be a pair of disjoint edges in
H
(Observation
3.5
) and introduce an order on
V(H)=[k]
such that

e^{\prime}=\{1,2\}
and
f^{\prime}=\{k-1,k\}
. If
H
is bipartite, we ensure that vertices
1
and
k-1
are in the same part of
H
with vertices
2
and
k
in the other part.

For
a\in[s-1]
, let
U^{a}
be a new vertex set disjoint from
V
with
|U^{a}|=2k(k-2)-2
so that
U^{a}\cap U^{b}=\varnothing
for
a\neq b\in[s-1]
. We label
U^{a}
as
U^{a}=\{u^{a}_{3},\ldots,u^{a}_{2k(k-2)}\}
and also label the vertices of
e^{a}_{\tau}
as
\{u^{a}_{1},u^{a}_{2}\}
and the vertices of
e^{a+1}_{0}
as
\{u^{a}_{2k(k-2)+1},u^{a}_{2k(k-2)+2}\}
. If
G^{\prime}=\bigcup_{a\in A,i\in[\tau]}H_{i}^{a}
is bipartite (and thus
H
itself is bipartite), we ensure that the labelling of
e^{a}_{\tau}
and
e_{0}^{a+1}
is such that
u^{a}_{1}
and
u^{a}_{2k(k-2)+1}
are in the same part of the bipartition of
G^{\prime}
(and thus
u_{2}^{a}
and
u^{a}_{2k(k-2)+2}
are in the other part). Finally for
a\in[s-1]
and
\ell\in[2k]
we define
J^{a}_{\ell}
to be the image of the graph isomorphism
\varphi^{a}_{\ell}:H\rightarrow J^{a}_{\ell}
defined via
\varphi^{a}_{\ell}(j)=u^{a}_{(\ell-1)(k-2)+j}
for
j\in[k]
and fix
f^{a}_{\ell}:=\varphi^{a}_{\ell}(f^{\prime})=u^{a}_{\ell(k-2)+1}u^{a}_{\ell(k-2)+2}\in E(J^{a}_{\ell})
.

Note that for
a\in[s-1]
, the collection
(J_{\ell}^{a},f_{\ell}^{a})_{\ell\in[2k]}
defines a simple
H
-chain (with respect to the ordering on
V(H)=[k]
given above) if
e^{a}_{\tau}\cap e^{a+1}_{0}=\varnothing
. This is not the case if
e^{a}_{\tau}
and
e^{a+1}_{0}
intersect but we certainly have that both
(J_{\ell}^{a},f_{\ell}^{a})_{\ell=1}^{2k-1}
and
(J_{\ell}^{a},f_{\ell}^{a})_{\ell=2}^{2k}
are both simple
H
-chains.

We are now in a position to define our long chain
(H^{*}_{i},e^{*}_{i})_{i\in[\tau^{*}]}
with
\tau^{*}:=\tau s+2k(s-1)\geq\tau|A|
. This chain will run through each of the chains
(H^{a}_{i},e^{a}_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}
in order of the
a\in A=[s]
and in between
(H^{a}_{i},e^{a}_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}
and
(H^{a+1}_{i},e^{a+1}_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}
the chain
(H^{*}_{i},e^{*}_{i})_{i\in[\tau^{*}]}
will run through
(J^{a}_{\ell},f^{a}_{\ell})_{\ell\in[2k]}
. Formally we define

(5.1)

(H^{*}_{i},e^{*}_{i})=\begin{cases}(H^{a}_{i^{\prime}},e^{a}_{i^{\prime}}),&\text{ if }i=(a-1)(\tau+2k)+i^{\prime}\text{ with }a\in[s],i^{\prime}\in[\tau];\text{ or }\\
(J^{a}_{\ell},f^{a}_{\ell}),&\text{ if }i=(a-1)(\tau+2k)+\tau+\ell\text{ with }a\in[s-1],\ell\in[2k].\end{cases}

We also define
e_{0}^{*}
to be the origin
e_{0}^{1}
of the first chain
(H^{1}_{i},e^{1}_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}
in the collection. With this, it follows from the fact that each of the
(H^{a}_{i},e^{a}_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}
,
(J_{\ell}^{a},f_{\ell}^{a})_{\ell=1}^{2k-1}
and
(J_{\ell}^{a},f_{\ell}^{a})_{\ell=2}^{2k}
are themselves chains, that
e^{*}_{i}\in E(H_{i}^{*})\cap E(H^{*}_{i+1})
for each
i\in[\tau^{*}-1]
. Thus
(H^{*}_{i},e^{*}_{i})_{i\in[\tau^{*}]}
is indeed a chain of length
\tau^{*}\geq\tau s
with origin
e_{0}^{*}
. The underlying graph
G^{*}:=\bigcup_{i\in[\tau^{*}]}H_{i}^{*}
has vertex set
V\cup(\bigcup_{a=1}^{s-1}U^{a})
of size
n+(\tau-1)(2k(k-2)-2)\leq n+2(\tau-1)k^{2}\leq 2k^{2}n
using here that
\tau\leq n
. Moreover, note that if
H
is bipartite and so is
\bigcup_{a\in A,i\in[\tau]}H^{a}_{i}
, then
G^{*}
is also bipartite due to how we labelled the vertices of
H
and the edges
e_{\tau}^{a}
and
e_{0}^{a+1}
for
a\in[s-1]
.

It remains to show that this new chain is proper. For condition
(1)
of Definition
4.4
we fix some
1\leq j\neq i\leq\tau^{*}
and suppose that

e_{i}^{*}\in E(\langle H^{*}_{j}\rangle_{H}).

Then we need to show that
j>i
.
Suppose first that
e_{i}^{*}=f_{\ell}^{a}
for some
a\in[s-1]
and
\ell\in[2k-1]
. As
e_{i}^{*}\in E(\langle H^{*}_{j}\rangle_{H})
we must have that
H^{*}_{j}
is equal to
J^{a}_{\ell+1}
as this is the only copy of
H
amongst the
H_{i}^{*}
(other than
J^{a}_{\ell}
) whose vertices contain the edge
f_{\ell}^{a}
. Hence
j=i+1
and we are done.

If
e_{i}^{*}\neq f_{\ell}^{a}
for some
a\in[s-1]
and
\ell\in[2k-1]
, then we necessarily have that
e_{i}^{*}=e_{i^{\prime}}^{a}
for some
i^{\prime}\in\{0,\ldots,\tau\}
and
a\in[s]
. Therefore by property
(i)
of the collection being proper
e_{i}^{*}\notin\langle H^{b}_{i^{\prime\prime}}\rangle
for any
b\neq a
and
i^{\prime\prime}\in[\tau]
. In
particular, we have that
e_{i^{\prime}}^{a}\notin\{e^{b}_{0},e^{b}_{\tau}:b\neq a\}
.
Therefore as all of the
J^{a^{\prime}}_{\ell}
are either vertex disjoint from
V
or intersect
V
in some
e^{b}_{0}
or
e^{b}_{\tau}
, we have that if
H^{*}_{j}=J^{a^{\prime}}_{\ell}
for some
a^{\prime}\in[s-1]
,
\ell\in[2k]
then either
e_{i^{\prime}}^{a}=e_{0}^{a}
and
H^{*}_{j}=J^{a-1}_{2k}
or
e_{i^{\prime}}^{a}=e_{\tau}^{a}
and
H^{*}_{j}=J^{a}_{1}
. The former case is ruled out as this would imply that
j=i
and in the latter case
j=i+1
.
Finally, we have to consider the case that
H_{j}^{*}
is
H^{a}_{i^{\prime\prime}}
for some
i^{\prime\prime}\in[\tau]
. Then we have that
i^{\prime\prime}>i^{\prime}
on account of property
(1)
of the chain
(H^{a}_{i},e^{a}_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}
being proper (Definition
4.4
). Thus
j>i
and we are done.

To finish the proof, we need to establish condition
(2)
for the chain
(H^{*}_{i},e^{*}_{i})_{i\in[\tau^{*}]}
so fix some
e\in E(H)
and some copy
F
of
H-e
in the graph
\hat{G}:=\bigcup_{i\in[\tau^{*}]}\langle H^{*}_{i}\rangle_{H}
. We will begin by showing that either
V(F)\subset V
or
V(F)\subset U^{a}\cup W^{a}
for some
a\in[s-1]
and
W^{a}\in\{e^{a}_{\tau},e^{a+1}_{0}\}
. Indeed, suppose for a contradiction that this does not hold. Then as
V(F)\nsubseteq V
, there exists some
a\in[s-1]
and vertex
u\in V(F)\cap U^{a}
. Suppose first that
u\in U^{a}_{0}:=\{u_{3},\ldots,u_{k(k-2)+2}\}
. As
V(F)\nsubseteq U^{a}\cup\{e^{a}_{\tau}\}
, there is some vertex
y\in V(F)\cap(V(\hat{G})\setminus(U^{a}\cup\{e^{a}_{\tau}\}))
. We claim that
F-\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
is disconnected, with
u
and
y
in different components, recalling here that
e^{a}_{\tau}=\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
. Indeed, we have that

\dist_{\hat{G}-\{u_{1},u_{2}\}}(u,y)\geq\min\left\{\dist_{\hat{G}-\{u_{1},u_{2}\}}(u,w):{w\in e_{0}^{a+1}}\right\},

as any path from
u
to
y
in
\hat{G}-\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
has to go through
e_{0}^{a+1}
. The quantity on the right hand side of the inequality is at least
k
as any path from
y
to
w\in e_{0}^{a+1}
must contain at least one vertex from each set
V(J^{a}_{\ell})\setminus\{f^{a}_{\ell-1}\}
for
\ell=k,\ldots,2k
.
Therefore as
F-\{u_{1},u_{2}\}\subseteq\hat{G}-\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
, we indeed have that
F-\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
is disconnected, contradicting the
(2,1)
-inseparability of
H
. If
H
is
(1,1,1)
-inseparable, we use here that
\hat{G}
is also bipartite (from how we defined the underlying graph
G^{*}
and Lemma
3.4
) implying that if
\{u_{1},u_{2}\}\subseteq V(F)
, then the vertices
u_{1},u_{2}
lie in different parts of the bipartition of
V(F)
. The case that
u\in U^{a}\setminus U^{a}_{0}=\{u_{k(k-2)+3},\ldots,u_{2k(k-2)}\}
is analogous and one can see from the same argument that in such a case, removing the vertices of
e_{0}^{a+1}
from
F
disconnects
F
and contradicts the inseparability of
H
.

We now treat these two cases separately, assuming first that
V(F)\subseteq V
. As
\hat{G}[V]=\bigcup_{a\in A}\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H}
, by condition
(ii)
of the collection being proper, we have that there is a unique
b\in A
such that
F\subseteq\langle G^{b}\rangle_{H}
and so also a unique
j^{\prime}\in[\tau]
such that
F\subseteq\langle H^{b}_{j^{\prime}}\rangle_{H}
. Letting
j=(b-1)(\tau+2k)+j^{\prime}
, we then have that
j
is the unique index in
[\tau^{*}]
such that
F\subseteq\langle H^{*}_{j}\rangle_{H}
. Moreover, note that for any
i\in[\tau^{*}]\setminus j
, we have that there is some
a\in A
and
i^{\prime}\in[\tau]
such that
E(\langle H^{*}_{i}\rangle)\cap\binom{V}{2}\subseteq E(\langle H^{a}_{i^{\prime}}\rangle)
. Therefore, we have that
V[\bigcup_{i\in[\tau^{*}]\setminus j}\langle H^{*}_{i}\rangle_{H}\bigcup H^{*}_{j}]=\bigcup_{a\in A\setminus b}\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H}\bigcup_{i\in[\tau]\setminus j}\langle H_{i}^{b}\rangle_{H}\bigcup H^{b}_{j^{\prime}}
and the ‘furthermore’ part of condition
(2)
of being proper is implied by the ‘furthermore’ condition
(ii)
of the collection being proper.

Finally, suppose that there is some
a\in[s-1]
and
W^{a}\in\{e_{\tau}^{a},e_{0}^{a+1}\}
such that
V(F)\subseteq U^{a}\cup W^{a}
. In this case,
we claim that either
V(F)\cap\{u_{3},\ldots,u_{k-2}\}=\varnothing
or
V(F)\cap\{u_{(2k-1)(k-2)+3},\ldots,u_{2k(k-2)}\}=\varnothing
. Indeed, if this is not the case then
F-W^{a}
is disconnected and this contradicts the inseparability of
H
, using here that the shortest path in
\hat{G}[U^{a}]
between a vertex in

\{u_{3},\ldots,u_{k-2}\}
and a vertex in
\{u_{(2k-1)(k-2)+3},\ldots,u_{2k(k-2)}\}
is of length at least
2k>v(H)
. Hence, we have that either
V(F)\subseteq Y_{1}:=\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{2k-1}V(J_{\ell}^{a})
or
V(F)\subseteq Y_{2}:=\bigcup_{\ell=2}^{2k}V(J_{\ell}^{a})
. Due to the fact that
\hat{G}[Y_{1}]=\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{2k-1}\langle J_{\ell}^{a}\rangle_{H}
and
\hat{G}[Y_{2}]=\bigcup_{\ell=2}^{2k}\langle J_{\ell}^{a}\rangle_{H}
,
we have that condition
(2)
for
F
in
(G_{i}^{*},e_{i}^{*})_{i\in[\tau^{*}]}
follows from condition
(2)
holding for the chains
(J_{\ell}^{a},f_{\ell}^{a})_{\ell=1}^{2k-1}
and
(J_{\ell}^{a},f_{\ell}^{a})_{\ell=2}^{2k}
which are both proper as they are simple
H
-chains and
H
is inseparable (Lemma
4.7
).
∎

Remark 5.6
.

We remark that the condition that each chain in the proper collection has the same size
\tau
is unnecessary. Indeed the proof of Lemma
5.5
can be easily adapted to show that if
\tau_{a}\in\mathbb{N}
with
\tau_{a}\leq n
for
a\in A
and
\mathcal{H}=\{(H_{i}^{a},e_{i}^{a})_{i\in[\tau_{a}]}:a\in A\}
is a collection of chains which is proper (with the conditions of Definition
5.1
adapted to the different length chains in the obvious fashion), then there is a proper chain on at most
2v(H)^{2}n
vertices of length at least
\sum_{a\in A}\tau_{a}
.

Lemma
5.5
will be our main tool in proving lower bounds from collections of chains. However, in one of our key applications (namely Theorem
1.5
), our graph
H
is
(2,1)
-separable. In such a situation, we cannot guarantee that the new chains
(J^{a}_{\ell},e^{a}_{\ell})_{\ell\in[2k]}
introduced in the proof of Lemma
5.5
to link, do not create unwanted copies of
H-e
for some
e\in E(H)
. We therefore require some extra conditions on our collection, namely that the ends of each chain are disjoint from all of the other chains. This is the content of the following lemma.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
4\leq k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
H
be a graph with
V(H)=[k]
such that
e^{\prime}:=\{1,2\}
and
f^{\prime}:=\{k-1,k\}
are both edges of
H
. Moreover, fix
5k<\tau\leq n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
V
be an
n
-vertex set. Suppose that
\mathcal{H}=\{(H^{a}_{i},e^{a}_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}:a\in A\}
is a proper collection of simple
H
-chains, each with underlying graph
G^{a}=\bigcup_{i\in[\tau]}H^{a}_{i}
on
V
. Moreover, suppose that

(5.2)

\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}V(H^{a}_{i})\cap V(G^{b})=\varnothing\text{ and }\bigcup_{i=\tau-k}^{\tau}V(H^{a}_{i})\cap V(G^{b})=\varnothing\text{ for }a\neq b\in A.

Then there exists a proper
H
-chain of length at least
\tau|A|
on at most
2v(H)^{2}n
vertices.

Proof.

The proof here follows exactly the same scheme as that of Lemma
5.5
and the chain
(H^{*}_{i})_{i\in[\tau^{*}]}
is defined identically to how we defined it in that proof. In particular, we define the new vertex sets
U^{a}
and chains
(J^{a}_{\ell},f_{\ell}^{a})_{\ell\in[2k]}
for
a\in[s-1]
, using the ordering on
V(H)=[k]
(and edges
e^{\prime}
and
f^{\prime}
) in the statement of this lemma. For
a\in[s-1]
, let us also define
(J^{a}_{\ell},f_{\ell}^{a})=(H^{a}_{\tau+\ell},e^{a}_{\tau+\ell})
for
\ell=-k,\ldots,0
and
(J^{a}_{\ell},f_{\ell}^{a})=(H^{a+1}_{\ell-2k},e^{a+1}_{\ell-2k})
for
\ell=2k+1,\ldots,3k
. With this labelling, by choosing appropriately the labelling of
e_{\tau}^{a}
as
\{u_{0},u_{1}\}
and
e_{0}^{a+1}
as
\{u_{2k(k-2)+1},u_{2k(k-2)+2}\}
when defining the
J^{a}_{\ell}
, we can assume that
(J^{a}_{\ell},f^{a}_{\ell})_{\ell=-k}^{3k}
is a simple
H
-chain of length
4k+1
. Hence we have that the chain
(J^{a}_{\ell},f^{a}_{\ell})_{\ell=-k}^{3k}
is also proper, as the longer chains
(H^{a}_{i},e^{a}_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}
are simple and are proper by assumption.

Now condition
(1)
of
(G^{*}_{i},e^{*}_{i})_{i\in[\tau^{*}]}
being proper follows directly from the proof of Lemma
5.5
(where at no point did we use the
(2,1)
-inseparability of
H
). For condition
(2)
, take some
e\in E(H)
and a copy
F
of
H-e
in
\hat{G}:=\bigcup_{i\in[\tau^{*}]}\langle G^{*}_{i}\rangle_{H}
. If
V(F)\subset V
, we again refer to the proof of Lemma
5.5
which dealt with this case without appealing to the
(2,1)
-inseparability of
H
. So we are left with the case that there is some
a\in[s-1]
and vertex
y\in V(F)\cap U^{a}
. We then have that
V(F)\subseteq W:=U^{a}\bigcup_{i=\tau-k}^{\tau}V(H_{i}^{a})\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}V(H^{a+1}_{i})
as the
F
is connected (as otherwise simple proper
H
-chains would not exist) and all vertices that are within distance
k=v(H)
from
y
in
\hat{G}
are contained in
W
. Now property
(2)
of Definition
4.4
for
F
in the chain
(H^{*}_{i},e^{*}_{i})_{i\in[\tau^{*}]}
follows from the fact that
\hat{G}[W]=\bigcup_{\ell=-k}^{3k}\langle J^{a}_{\ell}\rangle_{H}
and from property
(2)
for
F
in the proper chain
(J^{a}_{\ell},f^{a}_{\ell})_{\ell=-k}^{3k}
. This completes the proof.
∎

6.
Ladder chains

Our first general construction of chains are
ladder chains
. This section builds on ideas of Balogh, Kronenberg, Pokrovskiy and Szabó
[
3
]
in their work showing a quadratic maximum running time for cliques with at least 6 vertices. They gave one long continuous chain that wrapped back onto itself whereas here we exhibit a collection of chains and appeal to Lemma
5.5
. This allows for more flexibility in the construction and also simplifies the exposition and analysis. The key idea is to use a bipartition to restrict the intersection of different chains (or in their case different segments of the long chain). We will partition the vertex set of
H
into two as
V(H)=U\cup W
and the vertex set
V
of our collection of chains will be composed of a sequence of copies
L_{1},\ldots,L_{M}
of
U
with consecutive copies joined at a single vertex as well as a sequence of copies
R_{1},\ldots,R_{M}
of
W
with consecutive copies linked at a single vertex. Now each chain
(H^{a}_{i},e_{i}^{a})_{i\in[\tau]}
in our collection will come with some
slope

\lambda_{a}\in\mathbb{N}
and the chain will be simple with
H^{a}_{i}
lying on the vertex set
L_{i}\cup R_{i+\lambda_{a}}
for
i\in[\tau]
. The reason that we refer to this as a collection of
ladder
chains is that we think of
\bigcup_{j\in[M]}L_{j}
as the left side,
\bigcup_{j\in[M]}R_{j}
as the right side and each of the chains
(H^{a}_{i},e_{i}^{a})_{i\in[\tau]}
for
a\in A
defining some
ladder
which uses these fixed sides and whose rungs are determined by the slope
\lambda_{a}
.

This construction allows us to place many chains on the same vertex set whilst maintaining a strong control on the intersections of the different chains. Indeed, crucial to our analysis is the fact that when considering the graph
\hat{G}=\bigcup_{a\in A,i\in[\tau]}\langle H_{i}^{a}\rangle_{H}
, we have that for each
j\in[M-1]
there is a single vertex at the intersection of
L_{j}
and
L_{j+1}
, whose removal separates
\hat{G}[\bigcup_{j\in M}L_{j}]
and similarly on the right side. Using this we can force that any copy of
H-e
in
\hat{G}
has a vertex set of the form
L_{j}\cup R_{j^{\prime}}
for some
j,j^{\prime}\in[M]
.
This works well in the case that
H
is highly connected and in this case we can derive quadratic bounds on maximum running times. This is demonstrated for
(\lceil k/2\rceil,1)
-inseparable
k
-vertex graphs in Section
6.1
and for random graphs in Section
6.2
. Finally in Section
6.3
we use ladder chains with slopes defined by a Sidon set to prove the a running time of
\Omega(n^{3/2})
when
H
is a three-dimensional cube.

We continue with the formal definition of ladder chains.

{const}

[Collection of ladder chains]
Let
H
be a graph with
k:=v(H)\geq 4
,
U\subset V(H)
,
W:=V(H)\setminus U
and
e^{\prime},f^{\prime}\in E_{H}(U,W)
vertex-disjoint edges. Furthermore, let

A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
some index set and fix
m:=\max A
. Then the collection of
H
-chains

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{Lad}}(H,U,e^{\prime},f^{\prime};A)=\{(H^{a}_{i},e^{a}_{i})_{i\in[m]}:a\in A\}

is defined as follows.

Fix
k_{U}:=|U|-1

Note that each

Before using these constructions to give lower bounds on running times, we provide some general lemmas that will be useful in our proofs.

Lemma 6.2

Let
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{Lad}}(H,U,e^{\prime},f^{\prime};A)
be some collection of ladder chains and let

. Then there is at most one

Proof.

Choose

and

The lemma then follows because the sets

∎

This has the following immediate consequence.

Corollary 6.3
.

If

Finally, we give the following general lemma about ladder chains.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{Lad}}(H,U,e^{\prime},f^{\prime};A)
be a collection of ladder chains as in Construction

(1)

;

(2)

Otherwise, letting

Proof.

For part (

), note that if

. If

with

for some

we see that

with

for some

by considering the graph isomorphism

.

For part (
2
), suppose that
j^{\prime}\neq j+4a
for any

and consider

with

and

or

(or both) which will establish the first part of (

). As

, we have that

for some

and

. Fixing such an

, we therefore have that

and

between

6.1.
Ladder chains for

-inseparable

Recall the definition of

-inseparable

to show that any

-inseparable

Theorem 6.5
.

Let

be a
(\lceil k/2\rceil,1)
-inseparable

. Then

.

Proof.

Let
k=v(H)\geq 6
and let

be a vertex subset such that
|U|=\lfloor k/2\rfloor
and
e_{H}(U,W)
is minimised, where

. Moreover, let

and

be some pair of non-incident edges in

. To see these exist, note that one can take some arbitrary

and

and use that

is 2-edge-connected to find some edge

and then again use that

is 2-edge-connected to find an edge

.

Next, for

sufficiently large, we let

and take

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{Lad}}(H,U,e^{\prime},f^{\prime};[m])=\{(H_{i}^{a},e^{a}_{i})_{i\in[m]}:a\in[m]\}

to be the collection of ladder chains on a vertex set

as defined in Construction

with

(with other notation as defined there).
We further define

As

is
(2,1)
-inseparable, each chain in

is proper (Observation

) and we will show
\mathcal{H}
is a proper collection of

-chains and so by Lemma
5.5
, we will get a proper

-chain of length at least

, on at most

vertices. Hence by Lemma
4.6
we have that

, as required.

Thus it remains to prove that
\mathcal{H}
is indeed a proper collection of
H
-chains and by Corollary
6.3
, we only need to establish condition
(ii)
of Definition
5.1
. To this end, we let
G^{a}:=\bigcup_{i\in[m]}H_{i}^{a}
for

and we fix some

and a copy

of

in

\hat{G}:=\bigcup_{a\in[m]}\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H}=\bigcup_{a\in[m],i\in[m]}\langle H^{a}_{i}\rangle_{H}.

We also define

and

.
We have to show that there is a unique

such that

. We proceed in stages
1
-
1
, with the outcomes of each stage highlighted before being followed by their respective proofs.

(1)

We have that
|U^{\prime}|=\left\lfloor{k/2}\right\rfloor
and
|W^{\prime}|=\left\lceil k/2\right\rceil
.

Suppose for a contradiction that
|U^{\prime}|>\lfloor k/2\rfloor
.
Let

be the minimum value such that

. As
|U^{\prime}|>|U|=|L_{j}|
, we have that
U^{\prime}\setminus L_{j}\neq\varnothing
. But then
F[U^{\prime}\setminus\{\ell_{(j+1)k_{U}}\}]
is disconnected, which contradicts that

is
(\lceil k/2\rceil,1)
-inseparable as
|U^{\prime}\setminus\{\ell_{(j+1)k_{U}}\}|\geq\lfloor k/2\rfloor
(see Remark

). Thus
|U^{\prime}|\leq\lfloor k/2\rfloor
and an analogous argument gives
|W^{\prime}|\leq\lceil k/2\rceil
. As
|U^{\prime}|+|W^{\prime}|=k
, we get

. In particular,
F[U^{\prime}]
and
F[W^{\prime}]
are connected by Remark

.

(1)

We have that either
U^{\prime}\cap L^{\mathrm{int}}\neq\varnothing
or

.

Suppose for a contradiction we have that

. Then as
F[U^{\prime}]
and
F[W^{\prime}]
are both connected, we must have that both

and
F[W^{\prime}]
are paths. However, we have that there is a single edge that can be added to
F
to obtain some copy

of

and so we have that either
F[U^{\prime}]=H^{\prime}[U^{\prime}]
or
F[W^{\prime}]=H^{\prime}[W^{\prime}]
(or both). Therefore at least one of

and

is 2-edge-connected, contradicting that they are both paths.

(1)

If
U^{\prime}\cap L^{\mathrm{int}}\neq\varnothing
then there exists
j_{R}\in\{0,\ldots,5m-1\}
such that
W^{\prime}=R_{j_{R}}
.

Assuming that

, there is some

such

where

. Now for any pair of vertices

, we have that

has size at least
\lfloor k/2\rfloor
and hence

is connected, and in particular at least one of

and
r^{\prime}
is adjacent in

to a vertex in
L^{\mathrm{int}}_{j_{*}}
.
Therefore at least one of
r
and
r^{\prime}
lies in
\mathcal{R}:=\{R_{j^{\prime}}:j^{\prime}=j_{*}+4a\mbox{ for some }a\in A=[m]\}
by Lemma
6.2
as the sets in
\mathcal{R}
are the only vertices that have neighbours in
L^{\mathrm{int}}_{j_{*}}
in
\hat{G}
. Repeating this for different choices of
\{r,r^{\prime}\}\subseteq R
, we get that there is some
W^{\prime\prime}\subseteq W^{\prime}
with
|W^{\prime\prime}|=|W^{\prime}|-1
such that
W^{\prime\prime}\subset\bigcup\mathcal{R}
. In fact, we cannot have vertices
u,v\in W^{\prime\prime}
in distinct sets in
\mathcal{R}
as
F[W^{\prime}]\subset\hat{G}
is connected and
W^{\prime}
is obtained from
W^{\prime\prime}
be adding a single vertex of
R
(which cannot have neighbours in two distinct sets in
\mathcal{R}
). Therefore, we have that
W^{\prime\prime}\subset R_{j_{R}}
for some
j_{R}\in\{0,\ldots,5m-1\}
. Now suppose for a contradiction that the vertex
r_{s}\in W^{\prime}\setminus W^{\prime\prime}
is such that
r_{s}\notin R_{j_{R}}
. If
s<j_{R}k_{W}
then, fixing

to be some vertex in
L^{\mathrm{int}}_{j_{*}}
we have that

has size at least
\lfloor k/2\rfloor
and thus
F[Y]
is connected and

and again by Lemma
6.2
, we get that

. As
r_{s}\notin R_{j_{R}}
, we get a contradiction as

is connected. The case that

is analogous.

(1)

If
W^{\prime}\cap R^{\mathrm{int}}\neq\varnothing
then there exists
j_{L}\in\{0,\ldots,5m-1\}
such that
U^{\prime}=L_{j_{L}}
.

The proof of this is identical to the proof of
1
with the sides reversed.

(1)

We have that
V(F)=V(H^{b}_{i^{\prime}})
for some unique

and

.

Firstly, we have
j_{L},j_{R}\in\{0,\ldots,5m-1\}
such that

and

. This follows from appealing to
1
and then either

followed by
1
if we are in the first case or vice versa if we are in the second.
We will appeal to Lemma
6.2
and show that
e_{\hat{G}}(U^{\prime},W^{\prime})\geq k
and so
j_{R}=j_{L}+4b
for some

and
U^{\prime}\cup W^{\prime}=L_{j_{L}}\cup R_{j_{R}}=V(H_{i^{\prime}}^{b})
with

. To count edges in

, note that

for all

as

is connected. In fact, for all

, we have

. Indeed, let

be the edge corresponding to

in the copy

of

and suppose

is not an endpoint of

with the other vertex of

in

(which happens for all but at most one

). Then letting

, we have that

has at least two neighbours in

(in both

). Moreover, taking

to be one of these neighbours, we have that

as required, using here that

.

(1)

We have that
F\subseteq\langle H^{b}_{i^{\prime}}\rangle_{H}\subseteq\langle G^{b}\rangle_{H}
.

This final stage follows from Lemma
6.2
part (

).

This completes the proof of the first part of condition

of Definition

. To finish the proof, we need to show the ‘furthermore’ part of condition

. As

by Lemma

), we have to show that

. If this was not the case, then we would have that

of

contradicting our choice of

which minimised the number of crossing edges between

and

. This completes the proof.
∎

6.2.

,

With

) in

as defined in Construction

), by Observation

By Corollary

, we have that as

7.
Dilation chains

In this section we introduce collections of
dilation chains
. We construct one chain
(H^{a}_{i},e^{a}_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}
for each integer
a
in a collection
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}
of
dilations
. The chain

(H^{a}_{i},e^{a}_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}
will be obtained by taking a simple chain with vertex set
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and dilating each of the vertices by a factor of
a
. We will use that
A
is free of (non-trivial) solutions to certain equations in order to show that there is no unwanted copy
F
of
H-e
for any
e\in E(H)
and therefore verify that the collection of chains is proper. We remark that although we reprove the result of Balogh, Kronenberg, Pokrovskiy and Szabó
[
3
]
showing that
M_{K_{5}}(n)=n^{2-o(1)}
in this section and both proofs use additive constructions, our chain constructions in this section are new and quite distinct from the one used in
[
3
]
. Indeed, their construction has more in common with the ladder chains of the previous section, splitting the vertex set into three parts instead of two and having slopes defined by a set of integers free of solutions to three-term arithmetic progressions (in a similar way to how we prove Theorem
1.11
using Sidon sets). Our construction here using dilation chains seems to be more flexible for proving general lower bounds on
M_{H}(n)
.

Before formally defining the chain construction and its properties, we need to revisit additive constructions and obtain sets free of non-trivial solutions to several equations at once. We collect what we need in Section
7.1
below, delaying proofs to the Appendix. Recall the definition of non-trivial solutions from Section
2.3
.

7.1.
Solution-free sets

Our main interest will be using constructions that avoid non-trivial solutions to all equations with bounded coefficients. We will use
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
as our host abelian group, which will be more convenient for our proofs.

Definition 7.1

(
(K,h)
-fold solution-free sets)
.

Let
K,h\in\mathbb{N}
and
p\in\mathbb{N}
be a prime. We say a set
A\subset\mathbb{Z}_{p}\setminus\{0\}
is
(K,h)
-fold solution-free
, if for any equation

(7.1)

\sum_{i=1}^{h}\alpha_{i}x_{i}=0

in
h
variables with
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
|\alpha_{i}|\leq K
for
i\in[h]
, there are no non-trivial solutions
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{h})\in A^{h}
. We denote by
r_{K,h}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
the size of a largest
(K,h)
-fold solution-free subset of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

Note that a
(K,h)
-fold solution-free set is also a
(K,h^{\prime})
-fold solution-free set for
1\leq h^{\prime}\leq h
as we allow for coefficients
\alpha_{i}
to be equal to
0
.
We also remark that one can find dense subsets
B\subset\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with
|B|\geq p/8h^{2}K^{2}
that avoid non-trivial solutions to all equations as in (
7.1
) such that
\sum_{i=1}^{h}\alpha_{i}\neq 0
, using a simple modular based construction (see Lemma
A.2
in Appendix
A
).
Moreover, using a well-known random translation trick (Lemma
A.3
),
we can adapt any set
A\subset\mathbb{Z}_{p}
avoiding some equations with
\sum_{i=1}^{h}\alpha_{i}=0
, to get a subset
A^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with
|A^{\prime}|\geq|A|/8h^{2}K^{2}
that additionally avoids all equations as in (
7.1
) with
\sum_{i=1}^{h}\alpha_{i}\neq 0
. Therefore, up to constants depending on
h
and
K
, the problem of lower bounding
r_{K,h}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
reduces to finding large sets avoiding all the equations
\sum_{i=1}^{h}\alpha_{i}x_{i}=0
with
\sum_{i=1}^{h}\alpha_{i}=0
.

For
h=1
this immediately gives dense
(K,1)
-fold solution-free sets
as when
\alpha=0
, any solution to
\alpha x=0
is trivial. Similarly, when
h=2
, we can get dense
(K,2)
-fold solution-free sets as
\alpha x_{1}-\alpha x_{2}=0
with
\alpha\neq 0
necessarily implies that
x_{1}=x_{2}
and the only solutions are trivial. When
h=3
, a
(K,3)
-fold solution-free set in particular avoids three-term arithmetic progressions and so can no longer be dense. On the other hand, it is well-known (see for example
[
37
, Theorem 1]
) that Behrend’s construction can be adapted to avoid equations of the form
\alpha_{1}x_{1}+\alpha_{2}x_{2}-(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})x_{3}=0
with
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
bounded. With the aforementioned random translation trick to avoid equations where the
\alpha_{i}
do not sum to zero, this gives the following, which is proven for completeness in Appendix
A
.

Theorem 7.2
.

For any
K\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
C>0
such that for any prime
p\in\mathbb{N}
,

r_{K,3}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\geq p^{1-C/\sqrt{\log p}}.

The construction in Theorem
7.2
will be used to give a lower bound on running times of the form
M_{H}(n)\geq n^{2-o(1)}
for Behrendian graphs in Theorem
7.7
. Analogously, our construction of similar chains for Sidonian graphs (Theorem
7.11
) which will be used to prove lower bounds on running times of the form
M_{H}(n)\geq n^{3/2-o(1)}
, requires dense
(K,4)
-fold solution-free sets.

As before, lower bounding
r_{K,4}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
(or the analogous question in the integers
[N]
) reduces to finding sets that avoid non-trivial solutions to all equations of the form
\sum_{i=1}^{4}\alpha_{i}x_{i}=0
with
\sum_{i=1}^{4}\alpha_{i}=0
and each
|\alpha_{i}|
bounded by
K
. Such sets are known as
K
-fold Sidon sets
. They were introduced formally by Lazebnik and Verstraëte
[
29
]
and studied by Cilleruelo and Timmons
[

]
. Lazebnik and Verstraëte
[

]
conjectured that there should exist

-fold Sidon sets in

of size

(which would lead to a lower bound of
r_{K,4}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\geq\Omega(p^{1/2})
) and verified the conjecture for

. Moreover, in
[
11
]
it is claimed that unpublished work of Axenovich and independently Verstraëte, establishes an asymptotic version of the conjecture, giving a lower bound of
N^{1/2-o(1)}
by adapting the methods of Ruzsa
[
36

. Unfortunately we could not recover this proof and we believe that there is a subtle issue that arises when simultaneously trying to avoid all the equations such that
\prod_{i=1}^{4}\alpha_{i}
is not a square in the integers. In lieu of such a lower bound construction for

-fold Sidon sets, it turns out that our methods can work for an adapted notion of
(K,4)
-fold solution-free sets where we additionally have a lower bound on the coefficients that feature and a condition on the parity of the coefficients. We prove the following in Appendix

by adapting the constructions of Ruzsa
[

]
.

Theorem 7.3
.

For all
K\in 2\mathbb{N}
and

with

.

\tau:=\lfloor\tfrac{p-3}{k-2}\rfloor
, the collection

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{Dil}}(H;A,p)=\{(H^{a}_{i},e^{a}_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}:a\in A\}

of
H
-chains is defined as follows.
Let
V=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{p-1}\}
and
W=\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{p-1}\}
be two vertex sets indexed by
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\setminus\{0\}
(with indices always considered modulo
p
). For

a\in A
and
i\in[\tau]
the graph
H_{i}^{a}
is the image of the graph isomorphism
\varphi^{a}_{i}:H\rightarrow H^{a}_{i}
defined via the map

(7.3)

\varphi^{a}_{i}(j)=\begin{cases}v_{a\cdot((i-1)(k-2)+j)},&\text{ if }k+2\leq i\leq\tau-k-2\\
&\text{ or }i=k+1,j\notin\{1,2\}\text{ or }i=\tau-k-1,j\notin\{k-1,k\};\\
w_{a\cdot((i-1)(k-2)+j)},&\text{ if }1\leq i\leq k\text{ or }\tau-k\leq i\leq\tau\\
&\text{ or }i=k+1,j\in\{1,2\}\text{ or }i=\tau-k-1,j\in\{k-1,k\},\end{cases}

for
j\in[k]=V(H)
. We further define
e^{a}_{i}=w_{a\cdot(i(k-2)+1)}w_{a\cdot(i(k-2)+2)}\in E(H^{a}_{i})
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\cup\{\tau-k-1,\ldots\tau\}
and
e^{a}_{i}=v_{a\cdot(i(k-2)+1)}v_{a\cdot(i(k-2)+2)}\in E(H^{a}_{i})
for
k+1\leq i\leq\tau-k-2
.

Note that each dilation chain
(H_{i}^{a},e_{i}^{a})_{i\in[\tau]}
is a
simple

H
-chain of length
\tau
on vertex set

V^{a}:=\{w_{a},\ldots,w_{a(k(k-2)+2)},v_{a(k(k-2)+3)},\ldots,v_{a(\tau-k-1)(k-2)},w_{a((\tau-k-1)(k-2)+1)},\ldots,w_{a(\tau(k-2)+2)}\}

as in Definition
4.2
, using here that the numbers
1,\ldots,\tau(k-2)+2
are all distinct modulo
p
and so their images under multiplication by
a\neq 0
modulo
p
are also distinct.
For
i\in[\tau-1]
we have that
e^{a}_{i}=\varphi^{a}_{i}(f^{\prime})=\varphi^{a}_{i+1}(e^{\prime})=V(H^{a}_{i})\cap V(H^{a}_{i+1})
and
e^{a}_{\tau}=\varphi^{a}_{\tau}(f)
. As usual with simple chains, we take the canonical choice of origin for the chain
(H_{i}^{a},e_{i}^{a})_{i\in[\tau]}
to be
e_{0}^{a}:=w_{a}w_{2a}=\varphi_{1}^{a}(e^{\prime})
.

The following observation is at the heart of Construction
7.2
and we will use it repeatedly.

Observation 7.4
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{Dil}}(H;A,p)
and let
g=u_{\lambda_{1}}u_{\lambda_{2}}
such that
u_{\lambda_{i^{\prime}}}\in\{v_{\lambda_{i^{\prime}}},w_{\lambda_{i^{\prime}}}\}
and
\lambda_{i^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\setminus\{0\}
for
i^{\prime}=1,2
. If
a\in A
is such that the chain
(H^{a}_{i},e^{a}_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}
is proper with underlying graph
G^{a}
and
g\in E(\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H})
, then there exists
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
with
|\alpha|\leq k
such that

\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}=\alpha a\mbox{ mod }p.

Proof.

Note that
\lambda_{1}\neq\lambda_{2}
as the vertex set
V^{a}
of
(H^{a}_{i},e_{i}^{a})_{i\in[\tau]}
has no pair of vertices
v_{\lambda},w_{\lambda}
with the same index. As the chain
(H_{i}^{a},e_{i}^{a})_{i\in[\tau]}
is proper, Lemma
4.5
gives that there is some
i_{*}\in[\tau]
such that
g\in E(\langle H_{i_{*}}^{a}\rangle)
. This in turn implies that there are
j_{1}\neq j_{2}\in[k]
such that
\lambda_{1}=a((i_{*}-1)(k-2)+j_{1})\mbox{ mod }p
and
\lambda_{2}=a((i_{*}-1)(k-2)+j_{2})\mbox{ mod }p
and so
\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}=a\alpha\mbox{ mod }p
for some
\alpha=j_{2}-j_{1}\neq 0
with
|\alpha|\leq k
.
∎

In particular, this has the following easy consequence.

Corollary 7.5
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{Dil}}(H;A,p)=\{(H^{a}_{i},e^{a}_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}:a\in A\}
be a collection of
H
-chains such that for each
a\in A
, the chain
(H^{a}_{i},e^{a}_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}
is proper and has underlying graph
G^{a}=\bigcup_{i\in[\tau]}H^{a}_{i}
. If
A
is
(k,2)
-fold solution-free then
the graphs
\{\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H}:a\in A\}
are pairwise edge-disjoint.

Proof.

Let
g=u_{\lambda_{1}}u_{\lambda_{2}}
such that
u_{\lambda_{i}}\in\{v_{\lambda_{i}},w_{\lambda_{i}}\}
and
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\setminus\{0\}
for
i=1,2
.
Then if
g\in E(\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H})\cap E(\langle G^{b}\rangle_{H})
for some
a\neq b\in A
, Observation
7.4
gives that there are
\alpha_{a},\alpha_{b}\neq 0
with
|\alpha_{a}|,|\alpha_{b}|\leq k
such that
\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}=\alpha_{a}a=\alpha_{b}b\mbox{ mod }p
.
Then
(a,b)
form a non-trivial solution to the equation
\alpha_{a}x_{1}-\alpha_{b}x_{2}=0
, contradicting that
A
is
(k,2)
-fold solution-free.
∎

The collection
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{Dil}}(H;A,p)
has vertex set
V\cup W
of size
2p-2
and each chain has length
\tau=\Omega(p)
. We will use Lemma
5.5
with appropriately chosen primes
p
to obtain lower bounds on running times
M_{H}(n)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. In fact, in our applications using Construction
7.2
, we will not always be working with
(2,1)
-inseparable
H
and so we have to appeal to Lemma
5.7
which allows us to derive lower bounds from proper collections of chains with the added condition that the start and end of each chain is disjoint from the vertices of all the other chains. This is precisely the reason for the additional vertex set
W
in Construction
7.2
which is imposed to separate the start and end of each chain and can be ignored (with all the vertices mapped to
V
in the natural way) in the case that Construction
7.2
is applied with
(2,1)
-inseparable
H
.

Lemma 7.6
.

Suppose that
H
is a
k
-vertex graph,
p\geq k^{3}
is a prime and
A\subset\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is
(2k^{2},2)
-fold solution-free. Then taking
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{Dil}}(H;A,p)=\{(H_{i}^{a},e_{i}^{a})_{i\in[\tau]}:a\in A\}
to be the collection of dilation
H
-chains, each with underlying graph
G^{a}=\bigcup_{i\in[\tau]}H^{a}_{i}
, we have that

(7.4)

\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}V(H^{a}_{i})\cap V(G^{b})=\varnothing\text{ and }\bigcup_{i=\tau-k}^{\tau}V(H^{a}_{i})\cap V(G^{b})=\varnothing\text{ for }a\neq b\in A.

Proof.

Note that for each
a\in A
, we have that

\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}V(H^{a}_{i})=\{w_{a},\ldots,w_{a(k(k-2)+2)}\}\subseteq\{w_{a\alpha}:\alpha=1,\ldots,k^{2}\}.

Similarly,

\bigcup_{i=\tau-k}^{\tau}V(H^{a}_{i})=\{w_{a((\tau-k-1)(k-2)+1)},\ldots,w_{a(\tau(k-2)+2)}\}\subseteq\{w_{a\alpha}:\alpha=-1,\ldots,-2k^{2}\},

as
(\tau-k-1)(k-2)+1\geq p-(k+2)(k-2)\geq p-2k^{2}
. Moreover, all other vertices in
V(G^{a})
are vertices in
V
. Therefore if
a\neq b
and there was some vertex lying in the intersection of
\cup_{i=1}^{k}V(H^{a}_{i})\cup_{i=\tau-k}^{\tau}V(H^{a}_{i})
and
V(G^{b})
, we would necessarily have that the vertex lies in
W
and is of the form
w_{\lambda}
such that
\lambda=\alpha a\mbox{ mod }p
and
\lambda=\beta b\mbox{ mod }p
with
|\alpha|,|\beta|\leq 2k^{2}
. This contradicts that
A
is
(2k^{2},2)
-fold solution-free.
∎

7.3.
Dilation chains for Behrendian graphs

We now show that when
H-e
is Behrendian for any
e\in E(H)
(Definition
3.11
) and
A
is a large
(K,3)
-fold solution-free set (Definition
7.1
) for an appropriate
K\in\mathbb{N}
, Construction
7.2
can be used to give lower bounds on running times of the form
M_{H}(n)\geq n^{2-o(1)}
.

Theorem 7.7
.

Suppose that
4\leq k\in\mathbb{N}
and
H
is a graph with
V(H)=[k]
such that for all
\tau\in\mathbb{N}
, the simple
H
-chain
(H_{i},e_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}
is proper.
If
H-e
is Behrendian for any choice of
e\in E(H)
, then we have that
M_{H}(n)\geq n^{2-O(1/\sqrt{\log n})}=n^{2-o(1)}
.

Before proving Theorem
7.7
, we discuss its implications. In Lemma
3.15
, we showed that if
H
is an odd wheel graph, contains the square of a Hamilton cycle, or
\delta(H)\geq k/2+1
, then
H-e
is Behrendian for any choice of
e\in E(H)
. In the latter two cases, the graphs are
(2,1)
-inseparable (Observation
3.1
) and so simple chains of arbitrary length exist for
H
by Observation
3.5
and Lemma
4.7
implies that these chains are proper. In the case that
H
is an odd wheel with at least
8
vertices, Lemma
4.8
gives the existence of proper simple chains of arbitrary length. Therefore in all these cases of
H
, the conditions of Theorem
7.7
are satisfied and thus Theorems
1.2
(part (
1.4
)),
1.4
and the lower bound of Theorem
1.5
are all corollaries.

Proof of Theorem
7.7
.

Let
H
be a graph with
V(H)=[k]
as in the statement of the theorem and let
n
be sufficiently large. Let
p
be a prime such that
n/8k^{2}\leq p\leq n/4k^{2}
(which exists by Bertrand’s postulate/Chebyshev’s Theorem). Next we take
A\subset\mathbb{Z}_{p}\setminus\{0\}
to be a
(2k^{2},3)
-fold solution-free subset of size
p^{1-O(1/\sqrt{\log p})}
, as guaranteed by Theorem
7.2
, and take

\mathcal{H}:=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{Dil}}(H;A,p)=\{(H^{a}_{i},e^{a}_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}:a\in A\},

to be the collection of chains given by Construction
7.2
, with vertex set
V\cup W
as in the construction. The chains in
\mathcal{H}
are simple and thus proper by assumption.
For each
a\in A
, let
G^{a}=\bigcup_{i\in[\tau]}H^{a}_{i}
be the underlying graph of the chain. Then we have that

(7.5)

\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}V(H^{a}_{i})\cap V(G^{b})=\varnothing\text{ and }\bigcup_{i=\tau-k}^{\tau}V(H^{a}_{i})\cap V(G^{b})=\varnothing\text{ for }a\neq b\in A,

by Lemma
7.6
and the fact that
A
is
(2k^{2},3)
-fold solution-free (and so
(2k^{2},2)
-fold solution-free). Therefore, by Lemma
5.7
, if
\mathcal{H}
is a proper collection of simple
H
-chains then there is a proper chain on at most
2k^{2}(2p-2)\leq n
vertices, of length at least

\tau|A|=\left\lfloor\frac{p-3}{k-2}\right\rfloor p^{1-O(1/\sqrt{\log p})}=n^{2-O(1/\sqrt{\log n})},

which in turn proves the theorem via an application of Lemma
4.6
.

Thus it remains to prove that
\mathcal{H}
is a proper collection of simple
H
-chains. We will in fact show that the collection is strongly proper (Definition
5.3
) which suffices by Lemma
5.4
. The collection satisfies property
(I)
(by Corollary
7.5
) of being strongly proper. We thus only need to show property
(II)
of Definition
5.3
and so we fix some
e\in E(H)
and a copy
F
of
H-e
in
\hat{G}:=\bigcup_{a\in A}\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H}
. We will show that there is a
b\in A
such that
F\subseteq\langle G^{b}\rangle_{H}
.

Colour the edges of
F
with colours from
A
such that each edge
g\in E(F)
receives the colour
a\in A
such that
g\in E(\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H})
, noting that there is just one choice of
a
due to Corollary
7.5
. Supposing for a contradiction that
F
is not contained in
\langle G^{b}\rangle_{H}
for some
b\in A
, we have that this colouring is non-monochromatic. Therefore, as
F
is Behrendian by assumption, there is some non-monochromatic cycle
C
which is a subgraph of
F
, such that
C
is the union at most three monochromatic paths.

Let
P_{1}
be some monochromatic path featuring in
C
with the edges of
P_{1}
belonging to
\langle G^{a_{1}}\rangle_{H}
. Suppose that
the endpoints of
P_{1}
are
u_{\rho}\in\{v_{\rho},w_{\rho}\}
and
u_{\mu}\in\{v_{\mu},w_{\mu}\}
with
\rho,\mu\in[p-1]
. Note that
\rho\neq\mu
as
P_{1}
is a path in
\langle G^{a_{1}}\rangle_{H}
which has vertex set
V^{a_{1}}
which contains no pairs
v_{\lambda},w_{\lambda}
with the same index. Now we claim that there is some
\beta_{1}\neq 0
with
|\beta_{1}|\leq k^{2}
such that
\mu-\rho=\beta_{1}a_{1}\mbox{ mod }p
.
Indeed, we can label the vertices of the path
P_{1}
as
u_{\lambda_{1}},\ldots,u_{\lambda_{\ell}}

with
u_{\lambda_{j}}\in\{v_{\lambda_{j}},w_{\lambda_{j}}\}
for
j=1,\ldots,\ell
and
\lambda_{1}=\rho,\lambda_{\ell}=\mu
.
Note that we necessarily have that
2\leq\ell\leq k
as
P
is a path contained in
F
. By Observation
7.4
, we have that for
j=1,\ldots,\ell-1
, there is some
\alpha_{j}
with
|\alpha_{j}|\leq k
such that
\lambda_{j+1}-\lambda_{j}=\alpha_{j}a_{1}\mbox{ mod }p
. Hence, again modulo
p
, we have that

\mu-\rho=\lambda_{\ell}-\lambda_{1}=(\lambda_{\ell}-\lambda_{\ell-1})+\ldots+(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})=(\alpha_{\ell-1}+\ldots+\alpha_{1})a_{1}.

Therefore, we can fix
\beta=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}\alpha_{j}
and we have that
|\beta_{1}|\leq(\ell-1)\max_{j}|\alpha_{j}|\leq(\ell-1)k\leq k^{2}
and
\beta_{1}\neq 0
as
\rho\neq\mu
.

If
C
is the union of two monochromatic paths, then we have some
a_{2}\neq a_{1}
and a path
P_{2}
in
\langle G^{a_{2}}\rangle_{H}
with endpoints
u_{\rho}
and
u_{\mu}
and so the above argument gives some
\beta_{2}\neq 0
with
|\beta_{2}|\leq k^{2}
such that
\mu-\rho=\beta_{2}a_{2}\mbox{ mod }p
. However, then we get that
(a_{1},a_{2})\in A^{2}
is a solution to the equation
\beta_{1}x_{1}-\beta_{2}x_{2}=0\mbox{ mod }p
. This solution is non-trivial as
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\neq 0
and
a_{1}\neq a_{2}
. This contradicts that
A
is
(k^{2},2)
-fold solution-free. Similarly, if
C
is the union of 3 monochromatic paths
P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}
, then we have 3 distinct indices
\rho,\mu,\nu\in[p-1]
and integer coefficients
\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}\neq 0
with
|\beta_{i}|\leq k^{2}
for
i\in[3]
, such that

\mu-\rho=\beta_{1}a_{1}\mbox{ mod }p,\qquad\rho-\nu=\beta_{2}a_{2}\mbox{ mod }p,\qquad\nu-\mu=\beta_{3}a_{3}\mbox{ mod }p.

This contradicts that
A
is
(k^{2},3)
-fold solution-free as we have that
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\in A^{3}
forms a solution to the equation
\beta_{1}x_{1}+\beta_{2}x_{2}+\beta_{3}x_{3}=0\mbox{ mod }p
, which is non-trivial due to the fact that the
a_{i}
are distinct and the
\beta_{i}
are non-zero. This shows that no such non-monochromatic cycle
C
can exist and so
F
must indeed be contained in
\langle G^{b}\rangle_{H}
for some
b\in A
, establishing property
(II)
of being a strongly proper collection, and completing the proof.
∎

7.4.
Constructing bipartite dilation chains

In the case that
H
is bipartite, we utilise the bipartite structure for our collection of chains. We emphasise that the two vertex sets
V_{X}
and
V_{Y}
appearing in the bipartite Construction
7.4
below are
not
analogous to the two sets
V
and
W
appearing in Construction
7.2
above. Indeed the sets
V_{X}
and
V_{Y}
correspond to the two parts of the bipartition of the graph
H
whilst the set
W
in Construction
7.2
was introduced to separate the ends of each chain from the other chains. We could add sets
W_{X}
and
W_{Y}
to Construction
7.4
below to achieve the same purpose. This would lead to a construction that can handle bipartite graphs
H
that are not
(1,1,1)
-inseparable but nonetheless have proper simple chains (via an application of Lemma
5.7
). However, as we will only apply the Construction
7.4
to
(1,1,1)
-inseparable graphs, we omit the extra sets
W_{X}
and
W_{Y}
for simplicity.

{const}

[Collection of dilation chains - Bipartite case]
Let
4\leq k\in\mathbb{N}
and suppose
H
is a
k
-vertex bipartite graph with vertex parts
X,Y
such that
V(H)=X\cup Y\subseteq[2k]
with
\{1,2k-1\}\subseteq X\subseteq\{1,3,\ldots,2k-1\}
,
\{2,2k\}\subseteq Y\subseteq\{2,4,\ldots,2k\}
and both
e^{\prime}:=\{1,2\}
and
f^{\prime}:=\{k-1,k\}
are edges of
H
. Moreover let
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,
p\in\mathbb{N}
a prime with
p\geq m+2k^{3}
and
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}\setminus{0}
a set of
dilations
. Then fixing
\tau:=\lfloor\tfrac{p-1-2k-m}{2k-2}\rfloor
, the collection

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{BipDil}}(H;A,p,m)=\{(H^{a}_{i},e^{a}_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}:a\in A\}

of
H
-chains is defined as follows.
Let
V_{X}=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{p-1}\}
and
V_{Y}=\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{p-1}\}
be two vertex sets indexed by
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\setminus\{0\}
(with indices always considered modulo
p
). For

a\in A
and
i\in[\tau]
the graph
H_{i}^{a}
is the image of the graph isomorphism
\varphi^{a}_{i}:H\rightarrow H^{a}_{i}
defined via the map

(7.6)

\varphi^{a}_{i}(j)=\begin{cases}x_{a\cdot((i-1)(2k-2)+j)},&\text{ if }j\in X\subseteq[2k];\\
y_{a\cdot(i(2k-2)+j+m)},&\text{ if }j\in Y\subseteq[2k].\end{cases}

We further define
e^{a}_{i}=x_{a\cdot(i(2k-2)+1)}y_{a\cdot((i+1)(2k-2)+2+m)}\in E(H^{a}_{i})
for
i\in[\tau]
.

As with the non-bipartite Construction
7.2
, we have that each dilation chain
(H_{i}^{a},e_{i}^{a})_{i\in[\tau]}
from Construction
7.4
is a simple
H
-chain of length
\tau
on vertex set
V_{X}^{a}\cup V_{Y}^{a}
where

V_{X}^{a}:=\{x_{a\ell}:\ell\in[\tau(2k-2)+2]\mbox{ and }\ell\mbox{ mod }(2k-2)\in X\}

and

V_{Y}^{a}:=\{y_{a(\ell+2k-2+m)}:\ell\in[\tau(2k-2)+2]\mbox{ and }\ell\mbox{ mod }(2k-2)\in Y\}.

We again take the canonical choice of origin for the chain
(H_{i}^{a},e_{i}^{a})_{i\in[\tau]}
to be
e_{0}^{a}:=x_{a}y_{a(2k+m)}=\varphi_{1}^{a}(e^{\prime})
. We also note that, taking
G^{a}=\bigcup_{i\in[\tau]}H^{a}_{i}
to be the underlying graph for each chain
(H_{i}^{a},e_{i}^{a})_{i\in[\tau]}
with
a\in A
, we have that
\bigcup_{a\in A}G^{a}
is bipartite, with each edge between a vertex in
V_{X}
and
V_{Y}
.

Finally, we remark about the shift in indices for the vertices in
V_{Y}
. Indeed, compared to their
V_{X}
counterparts, we have that the vertices in
V_{Y}
have all their indices translated by a factor of
2k-2+m
before then being multiplied by some
a\in A
. The reason for this is highlighted by the following observation, which is the bipartite counterpart of Observation
7.4
.

Observation 7.8
.

Suppose
H
is
(1,1,1)
-inseparable,
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{BipDil}}(H;A,p,m)
and
g=x_{\lambda}y_{\mu}
such that
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\setminus\{0\}
. If
G^{a}
is the underlying graph for the chain
(H^{a}_{i},e^{a}_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}\in\mathcal{H}
and
g\in E(\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H})
, then there exists
\alpha\in\{m+1,m+3,\ldots,m+4k-3\}
such that

\mu-\lambda=\alpha a\mbox{ mod }p.

Proof.

As
H
is
(1,1,1)
-inseparable, the simple chain
(H_{i}^{a},e_{i}^{a})_{i\in[\tau]}
is proper by Lemma
4.7
. Therefore Lemma
4.5
gives that there is some
i_{*}\in[\tau]
such that
g\in E(\langle H_{i_{*}}^{a}\rangle)
. This in turn implies that there are
j_{x}\in X\subseteq[2k]
and
j_{y}\in Y\subseteq[2k]
such that
\lambda=a((i_{*}-1)(2k-2)+j_{x})\mbox{ mod }p
and
\mu=a(i_{*}(2k-2)+j_{y}+m)\mbox{ mod }p
and so

\mu-\lambda=a(i_{*}(2k-2)+j_{y}+m)-a((i_{*}-1)(2k-2)+j_{x})=a((2k-2)+j_{y}-j_{x}+m)\mbox{ mod }p.

Now defining
\alpha:=(2k-2)+j_{y}-j_{x}+m
, we have that
\alpha\neq m\mbox{ mod }2
as
j_{y}\in Y\subseteq\{2,4,\ldots,2k\}
and
j_{x}\in X\subseteq\{1,3,\ldots,2k-1\}
have different parities. Moreover
\alpha\in\{m+1,m+3,\ldots,m+4k-3\}
as required because

-2k+3\leq j_{y}-j_{x}\leq 2k-1
with the minimum being achieved when
j_{y}=2
and
j_{x}=2k-1
and the maximum when
j_{y}=2k
and
j_{x}=1
.
∎

The shift of
2k-2+m
in the
V_{Y}
indices actually occurs for two reasons. Firstly, the shift of
2k-2
guarantees that edges that appear in
\langle H^{a}_{i}\rangle_{H}
for some
a\in A
and
i\in[\tau]
have
‘positive slope’
, that is, that the
\alpha
in Observation
7.8
is positive.
This turns out to be crucial in our analysis showing that there are no unwanted copies of
F=H-e
for any
e\in E(H)
, when proving that collections of dilation chains are proper. The reason for the extra shift by
m
is more technical and stems from our need to consider equations in which there is also a lower bound on the size of the coefficients as in Theorem
7.3
. We also remark that we place
X
on odd integers and
Y
on even integers, in order to have
\alpha\neq m\mbox{ mod }2
which is needed to apply Theorem
7.3
. For other applications of Construction
7.4
such as Theorem
1.9
or indeed if the sets
A
in Theorem
7.3
can be replaced by
(K,4)
-fold solution-free sets, we can simply take
m=0
when appealing to Construction
7.4
and take
V(H)=X\cup Y=[k]
, adjusting the construction accordingly.

As in Corollary
7.5
in the non-bipartite case, Observation
7.8
has the simple consequence that under mild assumptions on the dilation set
A
, the edges that feature in the process on these chains belong to a unique chain.

Corollary 7.9
.

Let
H
be
(1,1,1)
-inseparable and
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{BipDil}}(H;A,p,m)
a collection of
H
-chains such that for
a\in A
, the chain
(H^{a}_{i},e^{a}_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}
has underlying graph
G^{a}=\bigcup_{i\in[\tau]}H^{a}_{i}
. If
A
is
(m+4k,2)
-fold solution-free, then the graphs
\{\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H}:a\in A\}
are pairwise edge-disjoint.

Proof.

If we have an edge
g=x_{\lambda}y_{\mu}\in E(\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H})\cap E(\langle G^{b}\rangle_{H})
then Observation
7.8
implies that
\mu-\lambda=a\alpha=b\beta
for
\alpha,\beta\in\{m+1,\ldots,m+4k-3\}
. This is prohibited for
a\neq b
by the fact that
A
has no non-trivial solutions to
\alpha x-\beta b=0
.
∎

As was the case for the proof of Theorem
7.7
using the Construction
7.2
of dilation chains, proving lower bounds on running times using the bipartite Construction
7.4
will reduce to showing that certain configurations in the final graph
\hat{G}:=\bigcup_{a\in A}\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H}
lead to non-trivial solutions to equations. Before embarking on the full applications of Construction
7.4
, we show that using a set of dilations that is
(K,3)
-fold solution-free for an appropriate
K
already greatly restricts the types of
C_{4}
that we see in the final graph.

Lemma 7.10
.

Let
H
be
(1,1,1)
-inseparable and
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{BipDil}}(H;A,p,m)
such that for
a\in A
, the chain
(H^{a}_{i},e^{a}_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}\in\mathcal{H}
has underlying graph
G^{a}=\bigcup_{i\in[\tau]}H^{a}_{i}
. If
A
is
(2m+8k,3)
-fold solution-free, and
C
is a copy of
C_{4}
in
\hat{G}:=\bigcup_{a\in A}\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H}
, then either
C
is entirely contained in
\langle G^{b}\rangle_{H}
for some
b\in A
or each edge of
C
belongs to a distinct graph from
\{\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H}:a\in A\}
.

Proof.

Note that
\hat{G}
is bipartite with parts
V_{X}
and
V_{Y}
due to Lemma
3.4
as
\bigcup_{a\in A}G^{a}
is bipartite and
H
is
(1,1,1)
-inseparable.
Let
x_{\lambda_{1}},x_{\lambda_{2}}\in V_{X}
and
y_{\mu_{1}},y_{\mu_{2}}\in V_{Y}
be the vertices of
C
.
Further, for each choice of
i,j\in\{1,2\}
let
g_{ij}=x_{\lambda_{i}}y_{\mu_{j}}\in E(C)
and
a_{ij}\in A
be such that
g_{ij}\in\langle G^{a_{ij}}\rangle_{H}
, noting that the choice of
a_{ij}
is unique due to Corollary
7.9
. By Observation
7.8
, we have that for each choice of
i,j\in\{1,2\}
there is some
\alpha_{ij}\in[m+4k]
with
\mu_{j}-\lambda_{i}=\alpha_{ij}a_{ij}\mbox{ mod }p
. In turn, this gives that

(7.7)

\alpha_{11}a_{11}-\alpha_{12}a_{12}+\alpha_{22}a_{22}-\alpha_{21}a_{21}=(\mu_{1}-\lambda_{1})+(\lambda_{1}-\mu_{2})+(\mu_{2}-\lambda_{2})+(\lambda_{2}-\mu_{1})=0

with equalities being modulo
p
. If the values of
a_{ij}
are all the same or all different we are done, as this corresponds to the two situations in the statement of the lemma. For a contradiction then, suppose that there is some set
B\subseteq A
with
|B|\in\{2,3\}
and
a_{ij}\in B
for all
i,j\in\{1,2\}
. This implies that

(7.8)

\sum_{b\in B}\beta_{b}b=0\mbox{ with }\beta_{b}:=\sum\left\{(-1)^{i+j}\alpha_{ij}:(i,j)\in\{1,2\}^{2}\mbox{ and }a_{ij}=b\right\}\mbox{ for each }b\in B.

Now note that
\beta_{b}
is the sum of at most three values in
\{\alpha_{11},-\alpha_{12},-\alpha_{21},\alpha_{22}\}
, at most two of which have the same sign and so
|\beta_{b}|\leq 2\max_{ij}|\alpha_{ij}|\leq 2(2m+4k)=2m+8k
. Thus the collection
B
forms a solution to a two or three variable equation modulo
p
with coefficients bounded by
2m+8k
. This is a contradiction to the fact that
A
is
(2m+8k,3)
-fold solution-free, as long as the solution given by
B
is non-trivial. As all the values in
B
are distinct, for non-triviality it suffices to show that we do not have
\beta_{b}=0
for all
b\in B
. If there is some
b^{\prime}
such that
\beta_{b^{\prime}}\in\{\alpha_{11},-\alpha_{12},-\alpha_{21},\alpha_{22}\}
then certainly
\beta_{b^{\prime}}\neq 0
as all the
\alpha_{ij}
are non-zero. Therefore the remaining case to consider is that
|B|=2
and each element of
B
is the sum of two values in
\{\alpha_{11},-\alpha_{12},-\alpha_{21},\alpha_{22}\}
. Letting
b\in B
be such that
a_{11}=b
, we have that
\beta_{b}\in\{\alpha_{11}-\alpha_{12},\alpha_{11}-\alpha_{21},\alpha_{11}+\alpha_{22}\}
. Note that
\alpha_{11}-\alpha_{12}=\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\neq 0
as
\mu_{1}\neq\mu_{2}
. Similarly
\alpha_{11}-\alpha_{21}=\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}\neq 0
. Finally
\alpha_{11}+\alpha_{22}\neq 0
as both
\alpha_{11},\alpha_{22}\geq 1
. This shows that the solution given by
B
cannot be trivial and completes the proof.
∎

We remark that the final argument of the proof of the previous lemma, where we showed that
\alpha_{11}+\alpha_{22}\neq 0
, is precisely where we need the ‘positive slope’ property, namely that the
\alpha
values produced by Observation
7.8
are always positive. This is why we shift the indices for
V_{Y}
by
2k-2
and without this, we cannot guarantee that there are not copies of
C_{4}
whose edges alternate between some pair of graphs
\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H}
and
\langle G^{b}\rangle_{H}
. Such copies of
C_{4}
would then be troublesome in our proof showing that there are no copies
F
of
H-e
such that the edges of
F
span multiple graphs
\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H}
.

In general when appealing to Construction
7.4
, we will need to show that there are no such
F
spanning multiple
\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H}
. Whilst Lemma
7.10
will be able to rule out many of these, the lemma says nothing about ‘rainbow’ configurations of
F
or
C_{4}
, where each edge belongs to a distinct
\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H}
. In the next two sections, we use two different strategies to handle these. The first, for Sidonian graphs in Theorem
7.11
, uses a dilation set
A
that also avoids certain equations in four variables. The second approach, used for complete bipartite graphs in proving Theorem
1.9
, uses a random sparsification of the set of dilations in Lemma
7.10
.

7.5.
Dilation chains for Sidonian graphs

We now apply Construction
7.4
to get lower bounds on running times for graphs
H
such that
H-e
is Sidonian for any choice of
e\in E(H)
, recalling the definition of Sidonian graphs from Definition
3.16
.

Theorem 7.11
.

If
H
is
(1,1,1)
-inseparable and
H-e
is Sidonian for any choice of
e\in E(H)
, then we have that
M_{H}(n)\geq n^{3/2-o(1)}
.

Any bipartite graph with parts
X,Y
with
|X|=r\geq 3
,
|Y|=s\geq 3
such that
d(x)\geq s/2+1
for all
x\in X
and
d(y)\geq r/2+1
for all
y\in Y
, satisfies the conditions of Theorem
7.11
and so Theorem
1.8
follows as a corollary. Indeed the fact that such graphs are
(1,1,1)
-inseparable was shown in Lemma
3.3
and the Sidonian condition was shown in Lemma
3.17
.

Proof.

Let
k=v(H)
and
K=4k
. Moreover, let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary, and let
m\in\mathbb{N}
be the integer output by Theorem
7.3
with input
\varepsilon
.
Next, let
n
be sufficiently large and let
p
be a prime such that
n/8k^{2}\leq p\leq n/4k^{2}
(which exists by Bertrand’s postulate/Chebyshev’s Theorem). We take
A\subset\mathbb{Z}_{p}\setminus\{0\}
to be a
(2(m+K),3)
-fold solution-free subset of size
p^{1/2-\varepsilon}
, with no
non-trivial solutions to equations of the form

(7.9)

\alpha_{1}x_{1}+\alpha_{2}x_{2}+\alpha_{3}x_{3}+\alpha_{4}x_{4}=0

with
|\alpha_{i}|\in\{m+1,m+3,\ldots,m+K-1\}
for
i\in[4]
.
The existence of such a set
A
is guaranteed by Theorem
7.3
. Finally, we take

\mathcal{H}:=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{BipDil}}(H;A,p,m)=\{(H^{a}_{i},e^{a}_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}:a\in A\},

to be the collection of chains given by Construction
7.4
, with vertex set
V_{X}\cup V_{Y}
. Each chain in the collection
\mathcal{H}
is proper by Lemma
4.7
as
H
is
(1,1,1)
-inseparable and the chains are simple. For each
a\in A
, let
G^{a}=\bigcup_{i\in[\tau]}H^{a}_{i}
be the underlying graph of the chain corresponding to
a\in A
. By Lemma
5.5
, using that
H
is
(1,1,1)
-inseparable and
\bigcup_{a\in A}G^{a}
is bipartite, if
\mathcal{H}
is a proper collection of
H
-chains then there is a proper chain on at most
2k^{2}(2p-2)\leq n
vertices, of length at least

\tau|A|=\left\lfloor\frac{p-1-2k-m}{2k-2}\right\rfloor p^{1/2-\varepsilon}\geq n^{3/2-2\varepsilon},

using in the last inequality that
n
is sufficiently large in terms of
\varepsilon,m,k
. As
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, the theorem follows via an application of Lemma
4.6
.

As in the proof of Theorem
7.7
, we will show that
\mathcal{H}
is in fact strongly proper, which will conclude the proof. For property
(I)
, we can appeal to Corollary
7.9
as
A
is certainly
(m+4k,2)
-fold solution-free. For property
(II)
, we fix some
e\in E(H)
and a copy
F
of
H-e
in
\hat{G}:=\bigcup_{a\in A}\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H}
. Colour the edges
g\in E(F)
with elements in
A
such that
g
receives colour
a\in A
if
g\in E(\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H})
, noting that each edge receives one colour by Corollary
7.9
. If there is some
b\in A
such that all edges of
F
are coloured with
b
then we have that
F\subseteq\langle G^{b}\rangle_{H}
and we are done. Suppose for a contradiction then, that
F
is coloured in a non-monochromatic fashion. Therefore, as
F
is Sidonian by assumption, there is some copy
C
of
C_{4}
in
F
that is non-monochromatic. By Lemma
7.10
, as
A
is
(2m+8k,3)
-fold solution-free, we must have that
C
is
rainbow
, that is, each edge of
C
is coloured by a different
a\in A
. Label the vertices of
C
as
x_{\lambda_{1}},x_{\lambda_{2}}\in V_{X}
and
y_{\mu_{1}},y_{\mu_{2}}\in V_{Y}
, and edges of
C
as
e_{ij}=x_{\lambda_{i}}y_{\mu_{j}}\in E(C)
with
e_{ij}
coloured by
a_{ij}\in A
for
i,j\in\{1,2\}
. By Observation
7.8
, we have that for each choice of
i,j\in\{1,2\}
there is some
\alpha_{ij}\in\{m+1,m+3,\ldots,m+4k-3\}
such that
\mu_{j}-\lambda_{i}=\alpha_{ij}a_{ij}
. But then we have that

(a_{11},a_{12},a_{22},a_{21})\in A^{4}
form a solution to the equation

\alpha_{11}x_{1}-\alpha_{12}x_{2}+\alpha_{22}x_{3}-\alpha_{21}x_{4}=0\mbox{ mod }p,

which is necessarily non-trivial as each of the
a_{ij}
are distinct and the
\alpha_{ij}
are non-zero. This contradicts that
A
is free from non-trivial solutions to equations as in (
7.9
) and completes the proof.
∎

7.6.
Dilation chains for complete bipartite graphs

For our second application of Construction
7.4
, we prove Theorem
1.9
giving a lower bound on
M_{H}(n)
when
H
is a complete bipartite graph.

Proof of Theorem
1.9
.

Let
3\leq r\leq s
and let
H^{\prime}
be the graph obtained from
H=K_{r,s}
by removing a single edge. Fix
m:=0
,
k:=v(H)=r+s
and
let
n
be sufficiently large. Fix
p
to be a prime such that
n/8k^{2}\leq p\leq n/4k^{2}
and take
A\subset\mathbb{Z}_{p}\setminus\{0\}
to be a
(8k,3)
-fold solution-free subset of size
N:=p^{1-O(1/\sqrt{\log p})}
, as guaranteed by Theorem
7.2
. Moreover, let

\mathcal{H}:=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{BipDil}}(H;A,p,0)=\{(H^{a}_{i},e^{a}_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}:a\in A\}
be as in Construction
7.4
and let
G^{a}:=\bigcup_{i\in[\tau]}H^{a}_{i}
be the corresponding underlying graphs. Letting
\hat{G}:=\bigcup_{a\in A}\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H}
, we claim that there is some subset
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
such that

(1)

|A^{\prime}|\geq\tfrac{1}{4}N\left(\tfrac{N}{p^{k}}\right)^{\tfrac{1}{r(s-1)-1}}=p^{1+\tfrac{1-k}{r(s-1)-1}-O\left(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\log p}}\right)}
, and

(2)

For any copy
F
of
H^{\prime}
in
\hat{G}
, there is a collection
B\subseteq A^{\prime}
with
|B|<r(s-1)
such that
F\subseteq\bigcup_{b\in B}\langle G^{b}\rangle_{H}
.

Indeed, fix
q\in(0,1)
with
q=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\tfrac{N}{p^{k}}\right)^{\tfrac{1}{r(s-1)-1}}
and consider a random set
A_{q}\subseteq A
with each
a\in A
being a member of
A_{q}
independently with probability
q
. For each copy
F
of
H^{\prime}
in
\bigcup_{a\in A_{q}}\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H}
, let
B_{F}
be the collection of
b\in A_{q}
such that
F
has an edge belonging to
\langle G^{b}\rangle_{H}
, noting that each edge belongs to exactly one
\langle G^{b}\rangle_{H}
by Corollary
7.9
. Now let
A^{\prime}_{q}\subseteq A_{q}
be a subset of
A_{q}
obtained by deleting one
b\in B_{F}\subseteq A_{q}
for every copy
F
of
H^{\prime}
such that
|B_{F}|\geq r(s-1)
. For each
r(s-1)\leq t\leq rs-1
, letting
\mathcal{F}^{t}_{q}:=\{F\subseteq\bigcup_{a\in A_{q}}\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H}\mbox{ copies of }H^{\prime}:|B_{F}|=t\},
we have that

(7.10)

\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathcal{F}^{t}_{q}|\right]\leq p^{k}q^{t}\leq p^{k}q^{r(s-1)}=p^{k}\frac{1}{2^{r(s-1)}}\left(\frac{N}{p^{k}}\right)^{1+\tfrac{1}{r(s-1)-1}}\leq\frac{1}{4r}N\left(\frac{N}{p^{k}}\right)^{\tfrac{1}{r(s-1)-1}},

using that the number of copies of
F
in
\hat{G}
is at most
p^{k}
and each copy of
F
which spans exactly
t
of the graphs
\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H}
, lands in
\mathcal{F}_{q}^{t}
with probability
q^{t}
. Therefore we have that

(7.11)

\mathbb{E}[|A^{\prime}_{q}|]\geq\mathbb{E}[|A_{q}|]-\sum_{t=r(s-1)}^{rs-1}\mathbb{E}[|\mathcal{F}_{t}|]\geq q|A|-\frac{1}{4}N\left(\frac{N}{p^{k}}\right)^{\tfrac{1}{r(s-1)-1}}\geq\frac{1}{4}N\left(\frac{N}{p^{k}}\right)^{\tfrac{1}{r(s-1)-1}},

and so choosing an instance of
A_{q}
in which the lower bound of (
7.11
) is achieved and fixing
A^{\prime}=A^{\prime}_{q}
, we get that indeed
A^{\prime}
satisfies both properties (
1
) and (
2
).

Now with such an
A^{\prime}
fixed, we take

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}:=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{BipDil}}(H;A^{\prime},p,0)=\{(H^{a}_{i},e^{a}_{i})_{i\in[\tau]}:a\in A^{\prime}\}.

The chains in
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
are simple and thus proper by Lemma
4.7
as
H
is
(1,1,1)
-inseparable (Lemma
3.3
). By Lemma
5.5
, using that
H
is
(1,1,1)
-inseparable and
\bigcup_{a\in A}G^{a}
is bipartite, if
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is a proper collection of
H
-chains then there is a proper chain on at most
2k^{2}(2p-2)\leq n
vertices, of length at least

\tau|A|=\left\lfloor\frac{p-1-2k-m}{2k-2}\right\rfloor p^{1+\tfrac{1-k}{r(s-1)-1}-O\left(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\log p}}\right)}=n^{2-\tfrac{r+s-1}{r(s-1)-1}-o(1)},

and so the theorem follows via an application of Lemma
4.6
.

As in the proof of Theorem
7.11
, we show that
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is actually strongly proper and condition
(I)
follows immediately from Corollary
7.9
upon noticing that
A^{\prime}
is
(4k,2)
-fold solution-free as a subset of
A
. To finish, we show Condition
(II)
of being strongly proper and fix some copy
F
of
H^{\prime}
in
\bigcup_{a\in A^{\prime}}\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H}
. Let
F^{\prime}
be the graph obtained from
F
by deleting the vertex of degree
r-1
from the side of
F
of size
s
, so that
F^{\prime}
is a copy of
K_{r,s-1}
. Note that due to property (
2
) of the set
A^{\prime}
, we cannot have that all
r(s-1)
edges of
F^{\prime}
belong to distinct graphs in the collection
\{\langle G^{a}\rangle_{H}:a\in A^{\prime}\}
and so there exists some
b\in A^{\prime}
and two distinct edges
e_{1},e_{2}\in E(F^{\prime})
such that
e_{1},e_{2}\in E(\langle G^{b}\rangle_{H})
. We will show that
F\subseteq\langle G^{b}\rangle_{H}
and so
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
satisfies property
(II)
and is indeed strongly proper. To see this, first note that by Lemma
7.10
and the fact that
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
is
(8k,3)
-fold solution-free, we have the following

(1)

Any copy of
C_{4}
in
F
which contains two edges in
\langle G^{b}\rangle_{H}
, must be entirely contained in
\langle G^{b}\rangle_{H}
.

Fixing
C_{*}\subseteq F^{\prime}\subseteq F
to be some copy of
C_{4}
that contains
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
, we show that all edges of
F
are in
\langle G^{b}\rangle_{H}
by considering the following edges sequentially:

(i)

Edges of
C_{*}
:
This follows directly from applying
1
to
C_{*}
.

(ii)

Edges of
F^{\prime}
incident to
V(C_{*})
:
If
f=uv\in E(F^{\prime})
with
v\in V(C_{*})
and
u\notin V(C_{*})
, then let
v^{\prime}\in V(C_{*})\setminus\{v\}
on the same side as
v
in
F^{\prime}
and
u^{\prime}\in V(C_{*})
on the other side. Then
v,v^{\prime},u,u^{\prime}
span a
C_{4}
containing
f
and such that both
vu^{\prime}
and
v^{\prime}u^{\prime}
belong to
\langle G^{b}\rangle_{H}
as edges of
C_{*}
, using the previous step. Therefore by
1
,
f
also belongs to
\langle G^{b}\rangle_{H}
.

(iii)

Remaining edges of
F^{\prime}
:
If
g=xy\in E(F^{\prime})
with
x,y\notin V(C_{*})
, then let
x^{\prime}\in V(C_{*})
on the same side as
x
in
F
and
y^{\prime}\in V(F^{\prime})
some arbitrary vertex on the same side as
y
. Then
x,x^{\prime},y,y^{\prime}
span a
C_{4}
containing
g
and such that
x^{\prime}y
and
x^{\prime}y^{\prime}
are both in
\langle G^{b}\rangle_{H}
by the previous step. An application of
1
gives that
g
also belongs to
\langle G^{b}\rangle_{H}
.

(iv)

Edges of
F\setminus F^{\prime}
:
These edges are all incident to the vertex
z
of degree
r-1
in the part of
F
of size
s
. Choosing any two of these edges and some arbitrary further vertex in the part of size
s
gives a
C_{4}
in
F
with two edges in
F^{\prime}
, which belong to
\langle G^{b}\rangle_{H}
by the previous step, and so all edges incident to
z
also belong to
\langle G^{b}\rangle_{H}
by
1
.

This shows that
F
is entirely contained in
\langle G^{b}\rangle_{H}
and completes the proof that
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
satisfies property
(II)
of Definition
5.3
, verifying that
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is a strongly proper collection and completing the proof of the theorem.
∎

Remark 7.12
.

With a similar proof, one can actually slightly improve the exponent in Theorem
1.9
, showing that in fact
M_{K_{r,s}}(n)=n^{2-\tfrac{r+s-1}{r(s-1)}-o(1)}
. Indeed, in the proof of Theorem
1.9
above, we obtained
A_{q}^{\prime}
by deleting from
A_{q}
some
b\in B_{F}
for each ‘bad’ copy
F
of
H^{\prime}
with
|B_{F}|\geq r(s-1)
. This is somewhat wasteful. We could instead just delete a few edges of
\bigcup_{i\in[\tau]}\langle H^{b}_{i}\rangle_{H}
, killing
F
whilst leaving most of the chain
(H_{i}^{b},e_{i}^{b})_{i\in[\tau]}
intact. In more detail, we pick an edge
f_{b}
of
F
that lies in
\bigcup_{i\in[\tau]}\langle H^{b}_{i}\rangle_{H}
. Let
i_{*}\in[\tau]
be the maximum index
i\in[\tau]
such that
f_{b}\in E(\langle H^{b}_{i}\rangle_{H})
. Then we can ‘split’ the chain

(H_{i}^{b},e_{i}^{b})_{i\in[\tau]}
into at most two smaller
chains
(H_{i}^{b},e_{i}^{b})_{i=1}^{i_{*}-2}
and
(H_{i}^{b},e_{i}^{b})_{i=i_{*}+1}^{\tau}
which completely avoid the vertices of
f_{b}
. This splitting procedure maintains all of the relevant properties of the collection being (strongly) proper and we can still apply a variant of Lemma
5.5
(see Remark
5.6
) to link the chains of the new collection to get one long proper chain. As this linking procedure needs a constant number of new vertices for each link between two chains, we need that the number of chains in the collection is at most
O(n)
. Consequently, we need that the number of splittings we do, and so the number of bad
F
with
|B_{F}|\geq r(s-1)
, is at most
O(n)
. By considering the expected number of such bad
F
as in (
7.10
), we get that
n^{k-o(1)}q^{r(s-1)}
should be comparable to
n
, and thus
q=n^{\tfrac{1-k}{r(s-1)}-o(1)}
and
A_{q}
has expected size
n^{1+o(1)}q=n^{1-\tfrac{r+s-1}{r(s-1)}-o(1)}
. By taking such an
A_{q}
that achieves this size (as well as having at most expected upper bound on the number of bad
F
) and splitting chains that contain an edge of each bad copy
F
of
H^{\prime}
, we obtain a strongly proper collection of chains (of potentially different sizes) whose combined length is at least
n^{2-\tfrac{r+s-1}{r(s-1)}-o(1)}
, as required.

8.
Line chains

Our third and final construction of a chain collection simply places chains on edges of a hypergraph. We say a hypergraph
\mathcal{L}
is
linear
if for any pair of edges
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in E(\mathcal{L})
, we have that
|\ell_{1}\cap\ell_{2}|\leq 1
. The terminology stems from the geometric fact that two lines can intersect in at most one point and indeed many interesting examples of linear hypergraphs are generated by finite geometries. We proceed with the definition of our line chains.

{const}

[Collection of line chains]
Suppose that
4\leq k,L\in\mathbb{N}
,
H
is a graph with
V(H)=[k]
such that
e^{\prime}:=\{1,2\}
and
f^{\prime}=\{k-1,k\}
are edges of
H
and
\mathcal{L}
is an
L
-uniform hypergraph with vertex set
V
. Fixing
\tau:=\lfloor\tfrac{L}{4(k-2)}\rfloor
, a collection

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{Lin}}(H;\mathcal{L})=\{(H^{\ell}_{i},e_{i}^{\ell})_{i\in[\tau]}:\ell\in E(\mathcal{L})\}

of
H
-chains indexed by edges
\ell
of
\mathcal{L}
is a collection of
line chains
if for each
\ell\in E(\mathcal{L})
the chain
(H^{\ell}_{i},e_{i}^{\ell})_{i\in[\tau]}
is a simple
H
-chain on the vertices of
\ell
.

We do not specify exactly how to place the simple
H
-chains on each line of
\mathcal{L}
and in most cases this can be done arbitrarily, noting that there is plenty of space as a simple
H
-chain of length
\tau
has
\tau(k-2)+2\leq L/4+2
vertices. When
H
is bipartite, we show that this placement can be done in such a way that the union of the line chains is also bipartite.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
H
be a
k
-vertex bipartite graph and suppose
\mathcal{L}
is an
N
-vertex linear hypergraph with edges of size
L\geq 200\log N
. Then there is a collection
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{Lin}}(H;\mathcal{L})=\{(H^{\ell}_{i},e_{i}^{\ell})_{i\in[\tau]}:\ell\in E(\mathcal{L})\}
of line chains such that
G:=\bigcup_{\ell\in E(\mathcal{L}),i\in[\tau]}H_{i}^{\ell}
is bipartite.

Proof.

Consider a bipartition of
V=V(\mathcal{L})
as
V=V_{1}\cup V_{2}
where each vertex
v\in V
is added to
V_{j}
with probability
1/2
for
j\in[2]
, independently from the other vertices. For each
\ell\in E(\mathcal{L})
and
j=1,2
, we have that
\mathbb{E}[|\ell\cap V_{j}|]=L/2
. Moreover, by Chernoff’s theorem (Theorem
2.4
), we have that
\mathbb{P}[|\ell\cap V_{j}|\leq 3L/8]\leq\exp(-L/64)\leq N^{-3}
using our lower bound on
L
. Note also that
|E(\mathcal{L})|\leq\binom{N}{2}
as each pair of vertices in
V
lies in at most one edge of
\mathcal{L}
. Therefore, taking a union bound over
\ell\in E(\mathcal{L})
and
j=1,2
, we get that with positive probability there is some partition of
V
as
V_{1}\cup V_{2}
, such that
|V_{j}\cap\ell|\geq 3L/8
for all
j=1,2
and
\ell\in E(\mathcal{L})
. Fixing such a partition and using that a simple
H
-chain of length
\tau
has at most
L/4+2
vertices, for each
\ell\in E(\mathcal{L})
we can place a simple
H
-chain
(H^{\ell}_{i},e_{i}^{\ell})_{i\in[\tau]}
on the vertices of
\ell
such that
G^{\ell}:=\bigcup_{i\in[\tau]}H_{i}^{\ell}
is bipartite with one part in
\ell\cap V_{1}
and the other part in
\ell\cap V_{2}
. Consequently,
G=\bigcup_{\ell\in E(\mathcal{L})}G^{\ell}
is also bipartite.
∎

Our construction
8
will be applied with
\mathcal{L}
being a linear hypergraph with high
girth
. For our purposes we define a hypergraph
\mathcal{C}
to be a
cycle
if it has at least two edges and there are distinct vertices
v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}\in V(\mathcal{C})
and an ordering of
E(\mathcal{C})
as
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{r}
such that
v_{i}\in\ell_{i}\cap\ell_{i+1}
for
i=1,\ldots,r
(with
\ell_{r+1}=\ell_{1}
here). This is sometimes referred to as a
Berge
cycle in the literature. The
length
of the cycle is the number of edges
r
in
E(\mathcal{C})
and
the
girth
of a hypergraph
\mathcal{L}
is the length of the smallest cycle that is a subgraph of
\mathcal{L}
. Note that two edges of a hypergraph which intersect in at least two vertices form a cycle of length 2, so any hypergraph with girth at least 3 is necessarily linear.
We use the following construction of Lazebnik, Ustimenko and Woldar
[
27
]
.

Theorem 8.2
.

For
3\leq g\in\mathbb{N}
and
N
sufficiently large, there is an
L
-uniform hypergraph with
N/2^{2g}\leq|V(\mathcal{L})|,|E(\mathcal{L})|\leq N
, girth at least
g
and
L\geq\tfrac{1}{2}N^{\tfrac{2}{3g-5}}
.

The actual construction of Lazebnik, Ustimenko and Woldar
[
30
, Theorem 3.2]
is in the setting of bipartite graphs. They show that if
q
is a prime power,
s\in\mathbb{N}
is odd and
t:=\lfloor\tfrac{s+2}{4}\rfloor
, then there is a
q
-regular bipartite graph
G(q,s)
with no cycle of length at most
s+4
, and at most
2q^{s-t+1}
vertices. In following work, it was proven that
G(q,s)
has exactly
2q^{s-t+1}
vertices, first when
q
is odd
[
28
]
and then when
q
is even
[
30
]
. To derive Theorem
8.2
from this construction, given
g,N\in\mathbb{N}
, we fix
s=2g-5
,
t=\lfloor\tfrac{2g-3}{4}\rfloor
and let
q
be some prime power such that
\tfrac{1}{2}N^{\tfrac{1}{s-t+1}}\leq q\leq N^{\tfrac{1}{s-t+1}}
. Taking the bipartite graph
G(q,s)
from
[
30
, Theorem 3.2]
with parts
X,Y
, we define a hypergraph
\mathcal{L}
with vertex set
Y
and edges
\ell_{x}:=N_{G(q,s)}(x)
for each
x\in X
. We therefore have that
\mathcal{L}
has
q^{s-t+1}
vertices and edges and so the bounds in Theorem
8.2
for
|V(\mathcal{L})|
and
|E(\mathcal{L})|
are indeed satisfied.
Moreover, the edges of
\mathcal{L}
have size
q\geq\tfrac{1}{2}N^{\tfrac{2}{3g-5}}
, using that
s-t+1\leq(3g-5)/2
. Finally, note that a cycle of length
r<g
in
\mathcal{L}
gives rise to a cycle of length
2r<2g=s+5
in
G(q,s)
, and so we indeed have that the girth of
\mathcal{L}
is at least
g
.

With the hypergraph from Theorem
8.2
, we are ready to prove the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 8.3
.

Any
(2,1)
-inseparable or
(1,1,1)
-inseparable graph
H
with
v(H)=k
satisfies

M_{H}(n)\geq\Omega\left(n^{1+\tfrac{2}{3k-2}}\right).

In particular, note that Theorem
8.3
encompasses Theorem
1.13
which was stated just for
(2,1)
-inseparable graphs.

Proof of Theorem
8.3
. .

For
n
sufficiently large, fix
N:=\lfloor n/2k^{2}\rfloor
,
g:=k+1
and let
\mathcal{L}
be the hypergraph generated by Theorem
8.2
. We then take

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{Lin}}(H;\mathcal{L})=\{(H^{\ell}_{i},e_{i}^{\ell})_{i\in[\tau]}:\ell\in E(\mathcal{L})\}

to be a collection of line chains as in Construction
8
, with any ordering of
V(H)=[k]
such that both
e^{\prime}=\{1,2\}
and
f^{\prime}=\{k-1,k\}
are edges noting that such an ordering exists because
H
is inseparable (Observation
3.5
). In the case that
H
is bipartite, we place the simple
H
-chains in
\mathcal{H}
in such a way that
G:=\bigcup_{\ell\in E(\mathcal{L}),i\in[\tau]}H_{i}^{\ell}
is bipartite, using Lemma
8.1
. Each chain is proper by Lemma
4.7
.

We appeal to Lemma
5.5
, which states that if
\mathcal{H}
is a proper collection of chains, then there is a proper chain of length at least

\tau|E(\mathcal{L})|\geq\frac{LN}{4k}\geq\Omega\left(n^{1+\tfrac{2}{3g-5}}\right)=\Omega\left(n^{1+\tfrac{2}{3k-2}}\right),

on at most
n
vertices which in turn gives the desired lower bound on
M_{H}(n)
through an application of Lemma
4.6
. It remains then to prove that the collection
\mathcal{H}
is proper. For this, we again use Lemma
5.4
and in fact show that
\mathcal{H}
is strongly proper. Letting
G^{\ell}:=\bigcup_{i\in[\tau]}H^{\ell}_{i}
for
\ell\in E(\mathcal{L})
, we have that each edge of
\langle G^{\ell}\rangle_{H}
is contained in
\ell
. As
\mathcal{L}
is linear, this implies that the graphs
\{\langle G^{\ell}\rangle_{H}:\ell\in E(\mathcal{L})\}
are pairwise edge-disjoint and condition
(I)
of being strongly proper is satisfied.

It remains to verify condition
(II)
, so we fix some
e\in E(H)
, a copy
F
of
H-e
in
\bigcup_{\ell\in E(\mathcal{L})}\langle G^{\ell}\rangle_{H}
and suppose for a contradiction that there are
\ell^{\prime}\neq\ell^{\prime\prime}\in E(\mathcal{L})
and edges
f^{\prime},f^{\prime\prime}\in E(F)
with
f^{\prime}\in E(\langle G^{\ell^{\prime}}\rangle_{H})
and
f^{\prime\prime}\in E(\langle G^{\ell^{\prime\prime}}\rangle_{H})
. By Lemma
3.6
, there is some cycle
C\subseteq F
such that
f^{\prime},f^{\prime\prime}\in E(C)
. Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k^{\prime}}
be the vertices of
C
, noting that
k^{\prime}\leq k
as
C\subseteq F
and
F
is a copy of
H-e
. For each
i\in[k^{\prime}]
, we have that
x_{i}x_{i+1}
is an edge in
\bigcup_{\ell\in E(\mathcal{L})}\langle G^{\ell}\rangle_{H}
(taking
x_{k^{\prime}+1}=x_{1}
when
i=k^{\prime}
) and so there is some
\ell_{i}\in E(\mathcal{L})
such that
x_{i}x_{i+1}\in\langle G^{\ell_{i}}\rangle_{H}
and so
\{x_{i},x_{i+1}\}\subseteq\ell_{i}
.
The edges
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{k^{\prime}}
, which contain both
\ell^{\prime}
and
\ell^{\prime\prime}
, do not necessarily form a cycle, as there may be repeats among the edges. However it is true that there is a cycle of length at most
k^{\prime}
as a subgraph of the hypergraph formed by the edges
\{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{k^{\prime}}\}
. Indeed, consider the closed walk
x_{1}\ell_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{k^{\prime}}\ell_{k^{\prime}}
in the bipartite incidence graph between
V(\mathcal{L})
and
E(\mathcal{L})
. If there is some
x_{i}
such that
\ell_{i-1}=\ell_{i}
(taking
\ell_{i-1}=\ell_{k^{\prime}}
if

) then remove
x_{i}\ell_{i}
from the walk. This results in a circuit as all of the
x_{i}
are distinct and so the only possible repeated edges in the walk
x_{1}\ell_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{k^{\prime}}\ell_{k^{\prime}}
are of the form
\ell_{i-1}x_{i}
and
x_{i}\ell_{i}
. The resulting circuit then contains a cycle in the bipartite incidence graph, corresponding to a cycle in the hypergraph
\mathcal{L}
of length at most
k^{\prime}\leq k
. This contradicts that
\mathcal{L}
has girth at least
k+1
and verifies condition
(II)
of being strongly proper, completing the proof.
∎

9.
Upper bounds on running times

Here we prove the upper bounds on running times, where one has to show that the process terminates in a given number of steps, no matter what the
n
-vertex starting graph
G_{0}
is. The main content of this section is the proof of Theorem
1.5
giving an upper bound for the running time
M_{H}(n)
when
H
is an odd wheel graph.

9.1.
Sparse random graphs

We start with a quick and simple proof bounding the running time when
H
is a sparse random graph, establishing Theorem
1.1
(
1.1
). In fact, we show something slightly stronger, giving an explicit bound of just 3 for large enough
n
.

Proposition 9.1
.

The following holds a.a.s. for
H=G(k,p)
when
p=o(\log k/k)
. If
n\geq 2k
, then
M_{H}(n)\leq 3
.

Proof.

By Fact
3.18
, we have that a.a.s.
G(k,p)
is either empty or has an isolated edge. If the former holds, then we certainly have
M_{H}(n)=0
. If the latter holds then fixing
H
to be such an instance of
G(k,p)
and
G_{0}
to be an arbitrary
n
-vertex graph, we have that if
G_{1}\neq G_{0}
, then there is some copy
H_{1}
of
H
in
G_{1}
. Letting
e_{1}
be an isolated edge in
H_{1}
and
W=(V(G_{0})\setminus V(H))\cup e_{1}
, we have that
G_{2}[W]
is complete as any edge in
\binom{W}{2}
can replace
e_{1}
in a copy of
H
. Finally
G_{3}
is then complete as any remaining edge can complete a copy of
H
with some selection of
k-2
vertices of
W
.
∎

9.2.
The wheel graph

Before proving the upper bound of Theorem
1.5
, we need some preliminary lemmas. Firstly, we need the famous Ruzsa-Szemerédi
(6,3)
-problem/theorem. Answering a question of Brown, Erdős and Sós
[
10
]
, Ruzsa and Szemerédi
[
35
]
showed that any
n
-vertex
3
-uniform hypergraph with no
6
vertices inducing 3 or more edges, must have
o(n^{2})
edges. The solution of Ruzsa and Szemerédi
[
35
]
was one of the earliest applications of Szemerédi’s regularity lemma. The theorem has several equivalent formulations and applications. In particular, it can be used to give a proof of Roth’s theorem showing that sets in
[n]
that are free of three-term arithmetic progressions, have size
o(n)
. We will use the following equivalent formulation (see for example
[
25
, Theorem 3.2]
), where we say a matching
M
in a graph
G
is an
induced matching
if
E(G[V(M)])=E(M)
.

Theorem 9.2

(Ruzsa-Szemerérdi
(6,3)
-theorem)
.

For all
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
, there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that if
n\geq n_{0}
and
G
is an
n
-vertex graph which is the union of
n
induced matchings, then
e(G)\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}n^{2}
.

Secondly, we need the following simple result on
C_{k}
-processes which we proved in a previous paper
[
16
, Lemma 3.5]
.

Lemma 9.3
.

Let
k\geq 5
be odd and let
G
be
a connected graph of order at least
k+1
which contains a copy of
C_{k}
. Then
\langle G\rangle_{C_{k}}
is a clique.

We remark that Lemma
9.3
does not hold for even cycles as the final graph may be complete bipartite. It is for this reason that we focus here on wheel graphs
W_{k}
with
k
odd.
Finally we give a simple lemma showing how cliques grow during the
W_{k}
-process.

Lemma 9.4
.

Let
k\geq 7
be an odd integer and
H=W_{k}
the wheel graph with
k+1
vertices. Suppose that
(G_{i})_{i\geq 0}
is some
H
-process and
Y\subseteq V(G_{0})
with
|Y|\geq k+1
and
G_{0}[Y]
being a clique. If
F
is some copy of
H
in
G_{0}
with
2\leq|V(F)\cap Y|<k+1
, then there is a vertex
z\in V(F)\setminus(Y\cap V(F))
such that

G_{1}[Y\cup\{z\}]
is a clique. Moreover,
G_{k}[Y\cup V(F)]
is also a clique.

Proof.

We claim that there is some vertex
z\in V(F)\setminus Y
such that
|N_{G_{0}}(z,Y)|\geq 2
. Indeed, if the universal vertex
u
of
F
lies outside of
Y
, then we can take
z=u
and use that
|V(F)\cap Y|\geq 2
. If
u
lies in
Y
, then the cycle
C=F-u
has vertices both inside and outside of
Y
. Therefore, there is some vertex
z\in V(C)\setminus(V(C)\cap Y)
such that
|N_{C}(z,Y)|\geq 1
and as

also has

as a neighbour, we indeed have that
|N_{G_{0}}(z,Y)|\geq 2
. Now as the minimum degree of

is 3, for any

, such that

, we have that

completes some copy of
H
with

and vertices of

. Hence

is complete at time

, completing the proof.

The ‘moreover’ statement comes from repeated applications of the main statement of the lemma, growing the size of

at each time step to incorporate another vertex in
V(F)
. This then gives that all of

is in a clique with

after at most
k+1-|V(F)\cap Y|\leq k
steps.
∎

We are now in a position to prove the upper bound of Theorem
1.5
.

Proof of upper bound in Theorem

.

Let

be an odd integer,
H=W_{k}

-vertex graph

. We fix some
n

, suppose for a contradiction that

(G_{i})_{i=0}^{\tau}

It remains to prove the base case

Now we claim that we are done if for some

k+1
for some
\tau^{\prime}\leq\tau_{0}+\binom{k}{2}
. We are not quite done as there is no guarantee that
X
is the vertex set of some
H_{t_{0}}
with
1\leq t_{0}\leq\tau_{1}
. However there is some
t_{0}\in[\tau_{0}]
such that
G^{\prime}[X]
contains an edge of
V(H_{t_{0}})
(as
G^{\prime}
is simply the union of the
H_{t}
with
1\leq t\leq\tau_{0}
). Hence
|V(H_{t_{0}})\cap X|\geq 2
and by Lemma
9.4
, we have that
V(H_{t_{0}})
is a clique of size
k+1
at time
\tau^{\prime}+k\leq\tau_{1}
.

We can therefore assume that for all
v\in V(G_{0})
,

•

any connected component in
G^{\prime}[N_{v}]
that contains a copy of
C_{k}
has exactly
k
vertices.

Moreover, as
\tilde{G}\subseteq G^{\prime}
, we also have that

•

any connected component in
\tilde{G}[N_{v}]
that contains a copy of
C_{k}
has exactly
k
vertices.

This implies that for each
v\in V(G_{0})
, the graph
\tilde{G}_{v}
is a union of at most
K=\binom{k}{2}
matchings that are induced in
\tilde{G}
. Indeed, as
\tilde{G}_{v}
is simply a union of copies of
C_{k}
, we have that each non-empty connected component of
\tilde{G}_{v}
contains a copy of
C_{k}
and is contained in
\tilde{G}[N_{v}]
. Hence all non-empty connected components of
\tilde{G}_{v}
have exactly
k
vertices and we can use
K
colours to colour the edges of
\tilde{G}_{v}
such that no two edges in the same connected component receive the same colour. Each colour class is then a matching and it is induced in
\tilde{G}
as if it was not, then there would be a component of
\tilde{G}[N_{v}]
that violates (
\tilde{\dagger}
). As
\tilde{G}=\bigcup_{v\in V(G_{0})}\tilde{G}_{v}
, we have that
\tilde{G}
is the union of at most
Kn
induced matchings and thus, by Theorem
9.2
, we have that
\tilde{G}
has at most
\varepsilon^{\prime}n^{2}
edges.

Finally then, we will lower bound the number of edges of
\tilde{G}
to obtain a contradiction. Considering the edges
e_{t}\in E(H_{t})\cap E(H_{t+1})
for
1\leq t\leq\tau_{0}-1
, which are all distinct, if
(\tau_{0}-1)/2>\varepsilon^{\prime}n^{2}
of these lie in
\tilde{G}
, then we are done. Therefore, we can assume that that there are at least
(\tau_{0}-1)/2
steps
T\subseteq[\tau_{0}-1]
such that for each
t\in T
, we have that
e_{t}\notin E(Q_{t})\cup E(Q_{t+1})
. We claim that for all
t\in T
, we have that
e_{t}=u_{t}u_{t+1}
. Indeed, we must have that
e_{t}
is incident to both
u_{t}
and
u_{t+1}
as it is not in
Q_{t}
nor
Q_{t+1}
. Moreover, if
u_{t}=u_{t+1}
then
Q_{t}
and
Q_{t+1}
must intersect (as
e_{t}\setminus\{u_{t}\}\subseteq V(Q_{t})\cap V(Q_{t+1})
) and lie on distinct vertex sets (as
e_{t+1}\setminus\{u_{t}\}\nsubseteq V(Q_{t})
) and thus the component of
\tilde{G}
in
\tilde{G}[N_{u_{t}}]
containing
Q_{t}\cup Q_{t+1}
violates condition
(\tilde{\dagger})
.

So indeed, for all
t\in T
we have that
e_{t}=u_{t}u_{t+1}
and we fix
f_{t}=x_{t}u_{t+1}\in E(Q_{t})
, choosing
x_{t}
to be one of the two neighbours of
u_{t+1}
on
Q_{t}
. Note that we must have that
x_{t}\in V(Q_{t+1})
as otherwise
V(Q_{t+1})\cup\{x_{t}\}
is a connected graph in
G^{\prime}[N_{u_{t+1}}]
violating (
\dagger
), using here that
x_{t}u_{t}\in E(H_{t})\subseteq E(G^{\prime})
and
u_{t}\in V(Q_{t+1})
as
e_{t}\in E(H_{t+1})
.

Now we claim that the set
F:=\{f_{t}:t\in T\}\subseteq\tilde{G}
has size
|F|\geq(\tau_{0}-1)/2k!>\varepsilon^{\prime}n^{2}
which gives our desired contradiction. To see this, we show that each edge
g\in E(\tilde{G})
appears at most
k!
times as
f_{t}
for some
t\in T
.
Fixing some vertex
v\in g
, let
t_{0}\in T

be the first time that
e_{t}=g
and
u_{t+1}=v
(if such a time exists). Then every other time
t_{0}<t\in T
such that
g=f_{t}
with
u_{t+1}=v
, we must have that
V(Q_{t+1})=V(Q_{t_{0}+1})

as otherwise
Q_{t+1}\cup Q_{t_{0}+1}\subseteq\tilde{G}[N_{v}]
would violate (
\tilde{\dagger}
).
As there are at most
k!/2
ways to map a cycle to
k
vertices, we indeed get that
g
can appear as
f_{t}
at most
k!
times where the additional factor of two comes from considering the other vertex of
g
playing the role of
u_{t+1}
.
∎

10.
Concluding remarks

We close the paper with some remarks about future directions.

10.1.
Random graphs around the threshold

In this paper we proved that the binomial random graph
H=G(k,p)
has a phase transition around
\log k/k
with
M_{H}(n)=O(1)
a.a.s. when
p=o(\log k/k)
and
M_{H}(n)=\Omega(n^{2})
a.a.s. when
p=\omega(\log k/k)
, see Theorem
1.1
. It is natural to ask whether this threshold is sharper than presented here. The
0
-statement, asserting that
M_{H}(n)
is constant when
p
is small, can in fact be tightened easily. Indeed, the only property of
H=G(k,p)
that we used for the proof was the existence of an isolated edge, which a.a.s. occurs when
p\leq c_{0}\log k/k
, for any
c_{0}<1/2
, see for example
[
9
, Theorem 5.4]
. The
1
-statement, giving quadratic running time for
p
much larger than
\log k/k
, is more delicate and in particular, we use that
H=G(k,p)
is self-stable, applying a result of Kim, Sudakov and Vu
[
24
]
. Whilst it is believable that results on the asymmetry of
G(k,p)
can be strengthened to give that
H=G(k,p)
is self-stable as soon as
p>\log k/k
, we did not find such a result in the literature and it is not immediate how one could prove such a statement. Nonetheless, we believe it is likely that our proof can be pushed to give
M_{H}(n)=\Omega(n^{2})
a.a.s. already when
p>c_{1}\log k/k
for any
c_{1}>1
. The range
\log k/2k<p<\log k/k
seems to be the most interesting and it is unclear what to expect for the behaviour of
M_{H}(n)
with
H=G(k,p)
. In this range,
H
will a.a.s. have one giant component and some isolated vertices, which can be ignored from the perspective of running times. The giant component however will a.a.s. be
separable
in this range, even having vertices of degree one. This suggests that chain constructions will be useless for proving lower bounds on
M_{H}(n)
but it does not preclude the possibility of having a slow maximum running time. Indeed, in
[
17
]
we showed that there are graphs
H
with
\delta(H)=1
and
M_{H}(n)=\Omega(n^{2})
. It would also be interesting to determine
M_{H}(n)
for
H
being a random 3-regular graph, which we expect to be (almost) quadratic.

10.2.
The minimum degree threshold for quadratic running time

Another question that remains open from our work is to fully understand the behaviour of
M_{H}(n)
under minimum degree conditions on
H
. We proved in Theorem
1.2
that
M_{H}(n)
is of the form
n^{2-o(1)}
when
H
is a
k
-vertex graph with
\delta(H)\geq k/2+1
. This is tight as there are examples (
K_{k/2,k/2}
and the
H^{\prime}_{k/2+1}
in Example
1.3
) which have
\delta(H)=k/2
and running time
M_{H}(n)
polynomially separated from
n^{2}
(or even linear in the case of
H^{\prime}_{k/2+1}
). We also showed that in fact
M_{H}(n)=\Omega(n^{2})
when
\delta(H)>3k/4
. It would be interesting to determine the exact minimum degree threshold for quadratic running time and in particular, whether it is possible to have dense graphs
H
with
\delta(H)\geq k/2+1
that exhibit a running time like the wheel graph
W_{k}
, having
M_{H}(n)=o(n^{2})
.

10.3.
Bipartite graphs

The results of this paper show that bipartite graphs
H
have running time
M_{H}(n)
polynomially separated from
n^{2}
. Indeed, in Proposition
1.6
, we showed that
M_{H}(n)=O(\ex(n,H))
where
\ex(n,H)
is the extremal number of
H
. We expect that this is never tight.

Conjecture 10.1
.

For all bipartite graphs
H
, we have that

M_{H}(n)=o(\ex(n,H)).

It could well be that a stronger conjecture is true and there is always polynomial separation between
M_{H}(n)
and
\ex(n,H)
for all bipartite graphs
H
.

We also gave lower bounds for many different bipartite graphs
H
, in particular showing in Corollary
1.10
that for
r
fixed and
s
large, the maximum running time
M_{K_{r,s}}(n)
approaches the upper bound given by the extremal number (Corollary
1.7
). For smaller bipartite graphs, we expect that the bounds given by Proposition
1.6
are far from tight. In particular, for
K_{3,3}
and the cube graph
Q_{3}
, it would be very interesting to improve on any of the bounds

n^{3/2-o(1)}\leq M_{K_{3,3}}(n)\leq O(n^{5/3})\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\Omega(n^{3/2})\leq M_{Q_{3}}(n)\leq O(n^{8/5}),

and we tend to believe that the lower bounds are closer to the truth.

10.4.
Further applications of chain constructions

We expect that our framework for general chain constructions will have many further applications giving lower bounds for
M_{H}(n)
. We have already demonstrated the flexibility of the
ladder chains
(Construction
6
) and
dilation chains
(Constructions
7.2

and
7.4
) in giving general results for different classes of graphs
H
. For
line chains
, we explored applications using different linear hypergraphs
\mathcal{L}
but did not include these in the paper. In fact, one can achieve general results using these constructions but they are weaker than the constructions using additive constructions and dilation chains. Indeed, for example, taking a collection of line chains defined by a generalized quadrangle can give a lower bound of
M_{H}(n)=\Omega(n^{5/3})
for graphs
H
such that
H-e
is Behrendian for any
e\in E(H)
. In general it seems that the constructions coming from line chains are far from tight but allow to give very general results, for example giving super-linear bounds for all
(2,1)
-inseparable graphs
H
as we did here in Theorem
8.3
. We expect that they will also be useful for giving non-trivial general lower bounds on
M_{H}(n)
for higher uniformity hypergraphs
H
, a direction which we did not explore at all here but has already been studied for cliques
[
15
,
20
,
34
]
.

There are also other previous chain constructions that fit into our general framework and may find further applications in the future. Indeed, Bollobás, Przykucki, Riordan and Sahasrabudhe
[
7
]
used a randomised construction to give lower bounds on
M_{K_{k}}(n)
approaching quadratic as
k
grows. We expect that similar constructions could also be used to achieve the bipartite analogue (Corollary
1.10
) showing that
M_{K_{r,s}}(n)
approaches
n^{1-1/r}
as
s
grows but our approach of using dilation chains and alterations gives a less technical route. Finally, the proof of Balogh, Kronenberg, Pokrovskiy and Szabó
[
3
]
showing that
M_{K_{5}}(n)\geq n^{2-o(1)}
uses a chain construction that is similar to the ladder constructions given here, except that the vertex sets of the copies of
H=K_{5}
(as well as the vertices of the construction
G_{0}
) are split into three parts rather than two. Exploring this approach further could lead to interesting new classes of chain construction.

Appendix A
Additive constructions

In this appendix, we prove Theorems
7.2
and
7.3
giving large sets avoiding non-trivial solutions to several equations. In order to do so, we adapt classical constructions of Behrend
[
5
]
and Ruzsa
[
36
]
. We start with some notation. For
h,k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathcal{E}^{h}
denote the set of equations
E=E(x_{1},\ldots,x_{h})=\sum_{i=1}^{h}\alpha_{i}x_{i}
in at most
h
variables (we allow some of the
\alpha_{i}
to be zero).
The set
\mathcal{E}^{h}_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{E}^{h}
is then the equations
E=\sum_{i=1}^{h}\alpha_{i}x_{i}
with
|\alpha_{i}|\leq k
for all
i\in[h]
.
Finally,
\mathcal{E}_{k}^{h}(0)\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{k}^{h}
are the equations
E=\sum_{i=1}^{h}\alpha_{i}x_{i}\in\mathcal{E}_{k}^{h}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{h}\alpha_{i}=0
. For some collection
\mathcal{E}
of equations,
p
a prime and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
r_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
denotes the size of the largest subset of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
that has no non-trivial solutions modulo
p
to equations in
\mathcal{E}
, whilst
r_{\mathcal{E}}(n)
is the size of the largest subset of
[n]
which is free from non-trivial solutions (in the integers) to all equations in
\mathcal{E}
. When
\mathcal{E}
is a single equation, that is,
\mathcal{E}=\{E\}
, we drop the set brackets and simply write
r_{E}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
and
r_{E}(n)
.

A.1.
Preparatory lemmas

We now proceed with some simple lemmas.

Lemma A.1
.

Suppose that
h,k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{k}^{h}
is some collection of equations. Then for any prime
p\in\mathbb{N}
, fixing
n:=\lfloor(p-1)/hk\rfloor
, we have that

r_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\geq r_{\mathcal{E}}(n).

Proof.

Taking
A\subseteq[n]
to be a subset free of non-trivial solutions to equations in
\mathcal{E}
, that achieves the size
r_{\mathcal{E}}(n)
, we have that

|E(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})|\in\{1,\ldots,p-1\}
for any
E(x_{1},\ldots,x_{h})\in\mathcal{E}
and any
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})\in A^{h}
. Therefore
E(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})\neq 0\mbox{ mod }p
and considering
A
as a subset of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
in the natural way, we have that
A
is free from non-trivial solutions modulo
p
to equations in
\mathcal{E}
.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

For any
k,h\in\mathbb{N}
and sufficiently large prime
p\in\mathbb{N}
, taking
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}^{h}_{k}\setminus\mathcal{E}^{h}_{k}(0)
, we have that
r_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\geq p/8k^{2}h^{2}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
A.1
, it suffices to show that there is some set
B\subseteq[n]
with
n=\lfloor(p-1)/hk\rfloor\geq p/2hk
such that
|B|\geq n/4hk
and
B
has no non-trivial solutions (in the integers) to equations in
\mathcal{E}^{h}_{k}\setminus\mathcal{E}^{h}_{k}(0)
. We take
B^{\prime}\subseteq[n]
to be all integers
b\in[n]
such that
b=1\mbox{ mod }(hk+1)
. Then certainly
|B|\geq n/4hk
and for any
(b_{1},\ldots,b_{h})\in B^{h}
and any
E(x_{1},\ldots,x_{h})=\sum_{i=1}^{h}\alpha_{i}x_{i}\in\mathcal{E}^{h}_{k}\setminus\mathcal{E}^{h}_{k}(0)
, we have that modulo
(hk+1)
,

E(b_{1},\ldots,b_{h})=\sum_{i=1}^{h}\alpha_{i}\neq 0,

using that
E\notin\mathcal{E}^{h}_{k}(0)
. Therefore also in the integers
E(b_{1},\ldots,b_{h})\neq 0
and we are done.
∎

Lemma A.3
.

Suppose that
\ell,h,k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathcal{E}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{E}_{\ell}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{k}^{h}(0)
are collections of equations. Suppose further that
p\in\mathbb{N}
is a sufficiently large prime and that there are
\delta_{i}\in(0,1)
for
i\in[\ell]
such that
r_{\mathcal{E}_{i}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\geq\delta_{i}p
for all
i\in[\ell]
. Then for any
B\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, there is some
B^{\prime}\subseteq B
such that
|B^{\prime}|\geq\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\delta_{i}|B|
and
B^{\prime}
has no non-trivial solutions to any equation in
\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathcal{E}_{i}.

Proof.

For
i\in[\ell]
, let
A_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}
be a set free of non-trivial solutions to equations in
\mathcal{E}_{i}
and achieving the maximum size
r_{\mathcal{E}_{i}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. Note that for any
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, the subset
A_{i}+t:=\{a+t:a\in A_{i}\}
is also free of non-trivial solutions to equations in
\mathcal{E}_{i}
. Indeed, if
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})\in(A_{i}+t)^{h}
is a non-trivial solution to some equation
E(x_{1},\ldots,x_{h})=\sum_{i=1}^{h}\alpha_{i}x_{i}\in\mathcal{E}_{i}
, then

E(a_{1}-t,\ldots,a_{h}-t)=E(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{h}\alpha_{i}\right)t=0,

using that
E\in\mathcal{E}_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{E}^{h}_{k}(0)
and so
\sum_{i=1}^{h}\alpha_{i}=0
.
Therefore
(a_{1}-t,\ldots,a_{h}-t)\in A_{i}^{h}
and is a non-trivial solution to
E
, which is a contradiction.

We now consider
t_{1},\ldots,t_{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
to be independent uniform samples from
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and take the resulting random set

A_{*}=\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{\ell}(A_{i}+t_{i})\right)\cap B.

As
A_{*}
is a subset of
B
and
A_{i}+t_{i}
for each
i\in[\ell]
, we have that
A_{*}
has no non-trivial solutions to equations in
\mathcal{E}_{*}
. Moreover, for each
b\in B
, we have that

\mathbb{P}[b\in A_{*}]=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathbb{P}[b\in(A_{i}+t_{i})]=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{|A_{i}|}{p}\geq\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\delta_{i}.

Therefore, by linearity of expectation,
\mathbb{E}[|A_{*}|]\geq\left(\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\delta_{i}\right)|B|
. Taking
B^{\prime}
to be some outcome of
A_{*}
that achieves (at least) this expected size establishes the lower bound of the lemma.
∎

A.2.
Avoiding solutions to 3-variable equations

The next lemma is essentially due to Behrend.

Lemma A.4

(Behrend’s construction
[
5
]
)
.

Let
k\geq 2
and let
E=E(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k},y)=x_{1}+\ldots+x_{k}-ky\in\mathcal{E}^{k+1}_{k}(0)
. Then there exists some
c>0
such that for all sufficiently large primes
p\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that

r_{E}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\geq p^{1-c/{\sqrt{\log p}}}
.

Proof.

Fixing
n:=\lfloor(p-1)/k(k+1)\rfloor
, by Lemma
A.1
, it suffices to finds some subset
A\subseteq[n]
such that
A
has no solutions to
E
in the integers. Now for any selection of integers
q,d\geq 2
and
r
, we define the set

S_{r}(d,q):=\left\{a_{1}+a_{2}(kq-1)+\ldots+a_{d}(kq-1)^{d-1}:a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}\in[0,q-1],a_{1}^{2}+\ldots+a_{d}^{2}=r\right\}.

Note that for
r>d(q-1)^{2}
, we have that
S_{r}(d,q)=\varnothing
. We claim that for any choice of parameters
q,d
and
r
, the set
S_{r}(d,q)
has no non-trivial solutions to
E
.
Indeed, suppose there exist
a^{(1)},\ldots,a^{(k)},b\in S_{r}(d,q)
such that
a^{(1)}+\ldots+a^{(k)}=kb
.
For any
a\in\mathbb{N}
with digits
a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}
in base
kq-1
, let
\|a\|:=\sqrt{a_{1}^{2}+\ldots+a_{d}^{2}}
.
We have that

(A.1)

\|a^{(1)}+\ldots+a^{(k)}\|=\|kb\|=k\sqrt{r}=\|a^{(1)}\|+\ldots+\|a^{(k)}\|,

where we used here that
b\in S_{r}(d,q)
and so if
b_{1},\ldots,b_{d}
are the digits of
b
in base
kq-1
, then
kb_{1},\ldots,kb_{d}
are the digits of the expansion of
kb
in base
kq-1
. Now by (
A.1
), the
k
vectors
(a^{(j)}_{1},\ldots,a^{(j)}_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
j\in[k]
, must be pairwise linearly dependent as this is the only case in which the triangle inequality is an equality. As all the vectors have the same modulus (by definition of
S_{r}(d,q)
), they are all in fact equal and hence
a^{(1)}=\ldots=a^{(k)}=b
. This shows that the solution
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k},y)=(a^{(1)},\ldots,a^{(k)},b)
is trivial.

It remains to choose parameters
r
,
d
,
q
such that
|S_{r}(d,q)|
is as large as possible with
S_{r}(d,q)\subset[n]
. Note that for a fixed choice of
d
and
q
,

\sum_{r=0}^{d(q-1)^{2}}|S_{r}(d,q)|=q^{d}.

By averaging we can pick
r=r(d,q)
such that

|S_{r}(d,q)|\geq\frac{q^{d}}{d(q-1)^{2}+1}\geq\frac{q^{d-2}}{d}

With the choices
d:=\lfloor\sqrt{\log n}\rfloor
and
q:=\lfloor\frac{n^{1/d}}{k}\rfloor
we have both
S_{r}(d,q)\subseteq[n]
and
|S_{r}(d,q)|\geq n^{1-c^{\prime}/\sqrt{\log n}}\geq p^{1-c/\sqrt{\log p}}
for suitable constants
c^{\prime},c>0
, as required.
∎

This has the following corollary.

Corollary A.5
.

For any
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists some
c_{0}>0
such that for any sufficiently large prime
p\in\mathbb{N}
the following holds. Let
\mathcal{E}_{+}\subseteq\mathcal{E}^{4}_{3k_{0}}(0)
be some set of equations
E
of the form

(A.2)

E(x_{1},\ldots,x_{4})=\alpha_{1}x_{1}+\alpha_{2}x_{2}+\alpha_{3}x_{3}-(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3})x_{4},

with
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\in\{0,1,\ldots,k_{0}\}
. Then we have that

r_{\mathcal{E}_{+}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\geq p^{1-c_{0}/{\sqrt{\log p}}}
. In particular, we have that
r_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\geq p^{1-c_{0}/{\sqrt{\log p}}}
with
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}^{3}_{k_{0}}(0)
.

Proof.

For each
j\in\{1,\ldots,3k_{0}\}
, let
\mathcal{F}_{j}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{+}
be the collection of equations

(A.3)

E(x_{1},\ldots,x_{4})=\alpha_{1}x_{1}+\alpha_{2}x_{2}+\alpha_{3}x_{3}-(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3})x_{4}

such that
0\leq\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\leq k_{0}
and
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}=j
. Note that
\mathcal{E}_{+}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{3k_{0}}\mathcal{F}_{j}
.
By Lemma
A.4
, there is some
c_{j}>0
such that
r_{\mathcal{F}_{j}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\geq p^{1-c_{j}/\sqrt{\log p}}
. Indeed, any non-trivial solution
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{4})
to an equation as in (
A.3
), gives a non-trivial solution to the equation
E(x^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,x^{\prime}_{j},y)=x_{1}^{\prime}+\ldots+x^{\prime}_{j}-jy
by fixing
x^{\prime}_{i}=a_{1}
for
i=1,\ldots,\alpha_{1}
,
x^{\prime}_{i}=a_{2}
for
i=\alpha_{1}+1,\ldots,\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
,
x^{\prime}_{i}=a_{3}
for
i=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+1,\ldots,j
and
y=a_{4}
. The corollary then follows by fixing
c_{0}=\sum_{j=1}^{3k_{0}}c_{j}
and applying Lemma
A.3
with
B=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
to avoid all non-trivial solutions to equations in
\mathcal{E}_{+}=\bigcup_{j=0}^{k}\mathcal{F}_{j}
. The ‘in particular’ statement then follows because every equation
E(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathcal{E}^{3}_{k_{0}}(0)
has the form (
A.2
) with
\alpha_{3}=0
.
∎

The proof of Theorem
7.2
follows quickly, recalling the definition of
r_{K,3}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
from Definition
7.1
.

Proof of Theorem
7.2
.

Let
c_{0}>0
be the constant given by Corollary
A.5
with input
k_{0}=K
. Therefore
r_{\mathcal{E}^{3}_{K}(0)}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\geq p^{1-c_{0}/{\sqrt{\log p}}}
. Let
B\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}
be a subset free of non-trivial solutions to equations in
\mathcal{E}^{3}_{K}\setminus\mathcal{E}^{3}_{K}(0)
such that
|B|\geq p/72K^{2}
as guaranteed by Lemma
A.2
. Then applying Lemma
A.3
, we get a set
B^{\prime}\subseteq B
which is
(K,3)
-fold solution-free and of size

|B^{\prime}|\geq\frac{p^{1-c_{0}/{\sqrt{\log p}}}}{72K^{2}}\geq p^{1-C/{\sqrt{\log p}}},

for an appropriate
C>0
, as required.
∎

A.3.
Avoiding solutions to four-variable equations

In order to prove Theorem
7.3
, we need to find large sets avoiding non-trivial solutions to certain four-variable equations. We start with some lemmas looking at special cases of equations. Our first lemma builds on an approach of Ruzsa
[
36
, Theorem 7.3]
.

Lemma A.6
.

Let
k_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon_{1}>0
. Further let
\mathcal{E}_{=}\subseteq\mathcal{E}^{4}_{2k_{1}}(0)
be the collection of equations of the form

E(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})=\alpha_{1}x_{1}+\alpha_{2}x_{2}-\alpha_{1}x_{3}-\alpha_{2}x_{4},

with
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\{1,\ldots,k_{1}\}
. Then for sufficiently large primes
p\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
r_{\mathcal{E}_{=}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\geq p^{1/2-\varepsilon_{1}}
.

Proof.

Fix
n=\lfloor(p-1)/8k_{1}\rfloor
and let
q\in\mathbb{N}
be some prime between
\sqrt{n}/8k_{1}
and
\sqrt{n}/4k_{1}
. Further, fix some maximal set
B\subset\mathbb{Z}_{q}\setminus\{0\}
such that
B
has no non-trivial solutions to equations of the form

(A.4)

\beta_{1}x_{1}+\beta_{2}x_{2}-(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})x_{3},

with
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\{0,\ldots,2k_{1}\}
. By Corollary
A.5
, there is some
c_{0}>0
such that
|B|\geq q^{1-c_{0}/\sqrt{\log q}}
.

Next we fix

A:=\{b+(2k_{1}+1)qb^{\prime}:b\in B,b^{\prime}\in[q],b^{\prime}=b^{2}\mbox{ mod }q\}\subseteq[n].

We will show that
A
has no non-trivial solutions (in the integers) to equations in
\mathcal{E}_{=}
and so by Lemma
A.1
, we get that

r_{\mathcal{E}_{=}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\geq|A|=|B|\geq q^{1-c_{0}/\sqrt{\log q}}\geq p^{1/2-\varepsilon_{1}},

for
p
sufficiently large, as required. So suppose there is some equation
E(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})=\alpha_{1}x+\alpha_{2}x_{2}-\alpha_{1}x_{3}-\alpha_{2}x_{4}\in\mathcal{E}_{=}
with a non-trivial solution
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4})\in A^{4}
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\alpha_{1}\geq\alpha_{2}
. Letting
a_{i}=b_{i}+(2k_{1}+1)qb_{i}^{\prime}
with
b_{i}\in B
for
i\in[4]
, we get that

(A.5)

\alpha_{1}(b_{1}-b_{3})+\alpha_{2}(b_{2}-b_{4})+(2k_{1}+1)q\left(\alpha_{1}(b^{\prime}_{1}-b^{\prime}_{3})+\alpha_{2}(b^{\prime}_{2}-b^{\prime}_{4})\right)=0.

Taking (
A.5
) modulo
(2k_{1}+1)q
, we get that
D=\alpha_{1}(b_{1}-b_{3})+\alpha_{2}(b_{2}-b_{4})
is congruent to
0
modulo
(2k_{1}+1)q
. However we also have that
|D|\leq 2k_{1}q
and so we must in fact have that
D=0
in the non-modular setting, that is

(A.6)

\alpha_{1}(b_{1}-b_{3})=\alpha_{2}(b_{4}-b_{2}).

We can also now reduce (
A.5
) to

(A.7)

\alpha_{1}(b_{1}^{2}-b_{3}^{2})=\alpha_{2}(b_{4}^{2}-b_{2}^{2})\mbox{ mod }q.

If
b_{1}=b_{3}
or
b_{2}=b_{4}
then both equalities must hold and
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4})
is a trivial solution. Therefore we can divide the left and right hand side of (
A.7
) by
\alpha_{1}(b_{1}-b_{3})=\alpha_{2}(b_{4}-b_{2})\neq 0
to obtain that
b_{1}+b_{3}=b_{4}+b_{2}\mbox{ mod }q
. Using this and adding
\alpha_{1}(b_{1}+b_{3})
to (
A.6
), we get that

2\alpha_{1}b_{1}=(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})b_{4}+(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})b_{2}\mbox{ mod }q.

As
b_{2}\neq b_{4}
, this gives a non-trivial solution to an equation as in (
A.4
) with
\beta_{1}=\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}\geq 0
,
and
\beta_{2}=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
, contradicting the definition of
B
.
∎

Our next lemma tackles other 4 variable equations, building on ideas from
[
36
, Theorem 7.5]
.

Lemma A.7
.

Let
k_{2}\in 2\mathbb{N}
be fixed. Then for all
\varepsilon_{2}>0
, there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that the following holds. Let
\mathcal{E}_{\neq}\subseteq\mathcal{E}^{4}_{m+k_{2}}(0)
be the collection of equations of the form

E(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})=\alpha_{1}x_{1}+\alpha_{2}x_{2}-\alpha_{3}x_{3}-\alpha_{4}x_{4},

with
\alpha_{i}\in\{m+1,m+3,\ldots,m+k_{2}-1\}
for
i\in[4]
such that
\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\}\neq\{\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4}\}
and
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=\alpha_{3}+\alpha_{4}
. Then for sufficiently large primes
p\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
r_{\mathcal{E}_{\neq}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\geq p^{1-\varepsilon_{2}}
.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon^{\prime}=\varepsilon_{2}/k_{2}
. For
d\in\{m+2,\ldots,m+k_{2}\}
, let
\mathcal{F}_{d}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\neq}
be all equations as in the statement of the lemma such that
\min\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4}\}=d-1
. Note that
\mathcal{E}_{\neq}=\bigcup_{d=m+2}^{m+k_{2}}\mathcal{F}_{d}
(with the union here going over
d
such that
d=m\mbox{ mod }2
) and so by Lemma
A.3
(applied with
B=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
), it suffices to show that for each
d\in\{m+2,\ldots,m+k_{2}\}
, we have that
r_{\mathcal{F}_{d}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\geq p^{1-\varepsilon^{\prime}}
. So let us fix some
d\in\{m+2,m+4,\ldots,m+k_{2}\}
for the remainder of the proof.

Fix
n=\lfloor(p-1)/4(m+k_{2})\rfloor
and let
q\in\mathbb{N}
be some prime between
d/8k_{2}
and
d/4k_{2}
. We assume that
m
(and hence
d
and
q
) is sufficiently large so that there is a set
B\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{q}\setminus\{0\}
of size at least
q^{1-\varepsilon^{\prime}/4}
with
B
avoiding non-trivial solutions to equations in

(A.8)

\mathcal{E}_{+}:=\{\beta_{1}x_{1}+\beta_{2}x_{2}+x_{3}-(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}+1)x_{4}:\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\{1,\ldots,k_{2}\}\},

using Corollary
A.5
. Now we define
J:=\lfloor\log_{d}(n)\rfloor-2
and the set

A:=\left\{\sum_{j=0}^{J}b_{j}d^{j}:b_{j}\in B\mbox{ for }0\leq j\leq J\right\}\subseteq[n].

We will show that
A\subseteq[n]
has no non-trivial solutions (in the integers) to equations in
\mathcal{F}_{d}
and hence, by Lemma
A.1
, we get that

r_{\mathcal{F}_{d}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\geq|B|^{J+1}\geq\left(q^{1-\tfrac{\varepsilon^{\prime}}{4}}\right)^{\log_{d}(n)-2}\geq\left(\frac{d}{8k_{2}}\right)^{\log_{d}(n)\left(1-\tfrac{2}{\log_{d}(n)}\right)\left(1-\tfrac{\varepsilon^{\prime}}{4}\right)}.

Now
(d/8k_{2})^{\log_{d}(n)}=n^{1-\tfrac{1}{\log_{8k_{2}}(d)}}\geq n^{1-\varepsilon^{\prime}/4}
for
m
(and hence
d
) sufficiently large in terms of
k_{2}
. Therefore, we have that

r_{\mathcal{F}_{d}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\geq n^{\left(1-\tfrac{\varepsilon^{\prime}}{4}\right)^{2}\left(1-\tfrac{3}{\log_{d}(n)}\right)}\geq\left(\frac{p}{4(m+k_{2})}\right)^{1-\tfrac{3\varepsilon^{\prime}}{4}}\geq p^{1-\varepsilon^{\prime}},

for
p
(and hence
n
) sufficiently large.

It remains to show that
A
has no non-trivial solutions to equations in
\mathcal{F}_{d}
. Suppose to the contrary that there is some
E(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})=\alpha_{1}x_{1}+\alpha_{2}x_{2}-\alpha_{3}x_{3}-\alpha_{4}x_{4}\in\mathcal{F}_{d}
with a non-trivial solution
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4})\in A^{4}
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that

(A.9)

d-1=\alpha_{3}\mbox{ and }d+1\leq\alpha_{1}\leq\alpha_{2}<\alpha_{4}\leq m+k_{2},

due to the fact that
\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\}\neq\{\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4}\}
,
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=\alpha_{3}+\alpha_{4}
and
d-1=\min\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4}\}
. For
i\in[4]
, let

a_{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{J}b^{(i)}_{j}d^{j}
with
b^{(i)}_{j}\in B
for
0\leq j\leq J
. Let
j_{*}
be the minimum
j
with
0\leq j\leq J
such that not all the
b^{(i)}_{j}
,
i\in[4]
are equal. Note that such a
j_{*}
must exist, otherwise all the
a_{i}
are equal and the solution is trivial. Taking
E(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4})
modulo
d^{j_{*}+1}
, we get that

\alpha_{1}b^{(1)}_{j_{*}}d^{j_{*}}+\alpha_{2}b^{(2)}_{j_{*}}d^{j_{*}}-\alpha_{3}b^{(3)}_{j_{*}}d^{j_{*}}-\alpha_{4}b^{(4)}_{j_{*}}d^{j_{*}}=0\mbox{ mod }d^{j_{*}+1}.

Therefore considering
E(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4})/d^{j_{*}}
modulo
d
, we get that

\gamma_{1}b^{(1)}_{j_{*}}+\gamma_{2}d^{j_{*}}-\gamma_{3}b^{(3)}_{j_{*}}-\gamma_{4}b^{(4)}_{j_{*}}=0\mbox{ mod }d,

where
\gamma_{i}=\alpha_{i}\mbox{ mod }d
. Now note that
\gamma_{3}=-1
,
\gamma_{i}\geq 1
for
i=1,2,4
and
\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{3}-\gamma_{4}=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{3}-\alpha_{4}=0
. Therefore modulo
d
we have that
(b^{(1)}_{j_{*}},b^{(2)}_{j_{*}},b^{(3)}_{j_{*}},b^{(4)}_{j_{*}})\in B^{4}
is a solution to the equation
E_{*}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})=\gamma_{1}x_{1}+\gamma_{2}x_{2}+x_{3}-(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}+1)x_{4}
. Moreover, as
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\{1,\ldots,k_{2}\}
we have that
|E_{*}(b^{(1)}_{j_{*}},b^{(2)}_{j_{*}},b^{(3)}_{j_{*}},b^{(4)}_{j_{*}})|\leq(2k_{2}+1)q<d
and so
E_{*}(b^{(1)}_{j_{*}},b^{(2)}_{j_{*}},b^{(3)}_{j_{*}},b^{(4)}_{j_{*}})=0
in the non-modular setting (and so also modulo
q
). Therefore, as
E_{*}\in\mathcal{E}_{+}
(see (
A.8
)), we have that the solution is trivial and all of the
b^{(1)}_{j_{*}}
coincide. This contradicts the definition of
j_{*}
and concludes the proof.
∎

We are now in a position to prove Theorem
7.3
.

Proof of Theorem
7.3
.

Fix
\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon_{2}=\varepsilon/4
and let
m
be as output by Lemma
A.7
with input
k_{2}=K
and
\varepsilon_{2}
. Let
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}:=\mathcal{E}^{4}_{2(m+2K)}\setminus\mathcal{E}^{4}_{2(m+2K)}(0)
and fix
\mathcal{E}_{+}=\mathcal{E}_{3k_{0}}^{4}(0)
as in Corollary
A.5
with
k_{0}=2(m+2K)
. Further, fix
\mathcal{E}_{=}\subseteq\mathcal{E}^{4}_{2k_{1}}(0)
as in Lemma
A.6
with
k_{1}=2(m+K)
, and
\mathcal{E}_{\neq}\subseteq\mathcal{E}^{4}_{m+k_{2}}(0)
as in Lemma
A.7
with
m
as defined and
k_{2}=K
. Finally, take

\mathcal{E}:=\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\cup\mathcal{E}_{+}\cup\mathcal{E}_{=}\cup\mathcal{E}_{\neq}
. Due to Lemmas
A.2
,
A.3
,
A.6
and
A.7
as well as Corollary
A.5
, we have that for
p
sufficiently large,
r_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\geq p^{1/2-\varepsilon}
.

It remains to verify that all of the equations for which we want to avoid non-trivial solutions in Theorem
7.3
, are contained in
\mathcal{E}
. Firstly note that any set avoiding solutions to
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{E}_{+}
is certainly
(2(m+2K),3)
-fold solution-free. Moreover for any equation

E(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})=\alpha_{1}x_{1}+\alpha_{2}x_{2}+\alpha_{3}x_{3}+\alpha_{4}x_{4},

with
|\alpha_{i}|\in\{m+1,m+3,\ldots,m+K-1\}
for
i\in[4]
as in (
7.2
), we have that either
E\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
or
\sum_{i\in[4]}\alpha_{i}=0
. In the latter case, let
t
be the number of positive
\alpha_{i}
with
i\in[4]
and note that we can assume that
t\in\{2,3\}
as any solution to the equation
-E
also gives a solution to
E
.
If
t=3
, then we have that
E\in\mathcal{E}_{+}
. If
t=2
, then without loss of generality
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
are positive and
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=-\alpha_{3}-\alpha_{4}
.
If
\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\}=\{-\alpha_{3},-\alpha_{4}\}
,
then
E\in\mathcal{E}_{=}
, whilst if
\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\}\neq\{-\alpha_{3},-\alpha_{4}\}

then
E\in\mathcal{E}_{\neq}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Solving equations of signature ( p , p , 2 ) (p,p,2) with coefficients over number fields
Begum Gulsah Cakti, Erman Işık, Yasemin Kara, Ekin Özman

Abstract
. Using the modular method, we study solutions to the Diophantine equation A ​ a p + B ​ b p = C ​ c 2 Aa^{p}+Bb^{p}=Cc^{2} over number fields. 1.
Introduction

The proof of Fermat’s Last Theorem in 1995 marked a turning point in number theory, introducing the modular method—a powerful technique that connects elliptic curves and modular forms to solve Diophantine equations. Building on this foundation, many researchers have achieved significant progress in establishing asymptotic versions of Fermat’s result across various contexts. For totally real fields, work by Freitas and Siksek
[
14
]
demonstrated that solutions to the standard Fermat equation vanish for sufficiently large prime exponents, with Deconinck
[
7
]
later extending this to versions of the standard Fermat equation with coefficients. More recently, these asymptotic results have been generalized to arbitrary number fields under modularity assumptions, and analogous findings have emerged for generalized Fermat equations
[
20
]
.

Despite these advances, similar progress for other Fermat-type equations has been limited. While equations such as
x^{p}+y^{p}=z^{2}
have been extensively studied over the rationals by mathematicians including Darmon, Merel, Bennett, and Poonen,
[
6
,
29
,
1
]
, generalizations to higher-degree number fields remain scarce. Recent work has begun to address this gap, applying modular methods to prove asymptotic results for certain classes of solutions to equations like
x^{p}+y^{p}=z^{2}
and
x^{p}+y^{p}=z^{3}
over certain number fields in
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
and in
[
27
]
for totally real fields. Similarly, asymptotic results for particular types of solutions to the first equation with coefficients of specific forms over totally real fields are given in
[
26
,
25
]
. In
[
19
]
, the authors have extended the asymptotic results to the generalized equation
Aa^{p}+Bb^{p}=Cc^{3}
for number fields
K
satisfying a suitable
S
-unit condition.
These developments follow asymptotic approaches analogous to those used for the classical and generalized Fermat equations, thereby expanding our understanding of Diophantine problems beyond rational solutions.

In this paper, we prove that the equation
Aa^{p}+Bb^{p}=Cc^{2}
does not have asymptotic solutions for number fields
K
satisfying a suitable
S
-unit condition by combining and generalizing the approaches of
[
17
]
and
[
27
]
. In particular, we show that for certain real quadratic fields, this condition is satisfied.

Beyond asymptotic solutions, a natural question concerns effective bounds for equations with specific coefficients. This requires explicit form elimination, which is often not possible or feasible. However, for certain families of equations, this can be achieved. In this paper, we provide effective results regarding solutions to Diophantine equations of signature
(p,p,2)
and
(p,p,3)
with coefficients. More precisely, we show that for the equation

a^{p}+db^{p}=c^{2}

over certain real and imaginary quadratic fields, there exist explicit bounds (depending on
d
) such that there are no non-trivial solutions of a particular type whenever
p
exceeds these bounds.

2.
Frey Curve and Related Facts

In this section, we collect some facts related to the
Frey curve associated to a putative solution of Equation
1.1
and the associated Galois representation.

2.1.
Conductor of the Frey Curve

Let
G_{K}
be the absolute Galois group of a number field
K
, let
E/K
be an elliptic curve, and
\overline{\rho}_{E,p}
denote the residual Galois representation attached to
E

\overline{\rho}_{E,p}:G_{K}\longrightarrow\Aut(E[p])\cong\GL_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{p}).

We use
{\mathfrak{q}}
for an arbitrary prime of
K
, and
G_{\mathfrak{q}}
and
I_{\mathfrak{q}}
for a decomposition and inertia subgroups of
G_{K}
at
{\mathfrak{q}}
, respectively.

For a putative solution
(a,b,c)
to the equation
Ax^{p}+By^{p}=Cz^{2}
with a prime exponent
p\nmid ABC
, we associate the Frey elliptic curve,

(2.1)

E:=E_{(a,b,c,A,B,C)}:Y^{2}=X^{3}+2CcX^{2}+BCb^{p}X

whose arithmetic invariants are given by

\Delta_{E}=2^{6}AB^{2}C^{3}(ab^{2})^{p},\;\displaystyle j_{E}=2^{6}\frac{(4Aa^{p}+Bb^{p})^{3}}{AB^{2}(ab^{2})^{p}}

and

c_{4}(E)=16\left(4C^{2}c^{2}-3BCb^{p}\right)=2^{4}C(4Aa^{p}+Bb^{p}),\;c_{6}(E)=2^{6}C^{2}c(Bb^{p}-8Aa^{p}).

Let
{\mathcal{N}}_{E}
denote the conductor of
E
, and define

(2.2)

{\mathcal{N}}_{p}:={\mathcal{N}}_{E}\bigg/{\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathfrak{q}}\mid\mid{\mathcal{N}}_{E}\\
p\mid v_{\mathfrak{q}}(\Delta_{\mathfrak{q}})\end{subarray}}}{\mathfrak{q}},

where
\Delta_{\mathfrak{q}}
denotes the discriminant of a local minimal model of
E
at
{\mathfrak{q}}
. The following lemma collects the facts related to the Frey curve
E
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let E be the Frey curve defined in (
2.1
). Then:

(1)

The Frey curve
E
is semistable away from the primes dividing
2C
and has a
K
-rational point of order
2
. Moreover, the conductor
\mathcal{N}_{E}
attached to the Frey curve
E
is given by

\mathcal{N}_{E}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathfrak{P}}\mid 2C\end{subarray}}{\mathfrak{P}}^{r_{\mathfrak{P}}}\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathfrak{q}}\mid ABab\\
{\mathfrak{q}}\nmid 2\end{subarray}\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{q}\text{ and }{\mathcal{N}}_{p}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathfrak{P}}\in S_{K}\end{subarray}}{\mathfrak{P}}^{r^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{P}}},

where
0\leq r^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{P}}\leq r_{\mathfrak{P}}\leq 2+6v_{\mathfrak{P}}(2)
for the primes
{\mathfrak{P}}\in S_{K}
. In particular, the ideal
{\mathcal{N}}_{p}
can only be divisible by the primes of
K
dividing
2ABC
.

(2)

The Serre conductor
{\mathfrak{N}}_{E}
, which is the prime-to-
p
part of the Artin conductor of
\overline{\rho}_{E,p}
, is supported on the set
S_{K}
and belongs to a finite set depending only on the field
K
.

(3)

For large enough
p
(depending only on
A,B,C
and
K
), the Galois representation
\overline{\rho}_{E,p}
is finite flat at every prime
{\mathfrak{p}}
of
K
that lies above
p
. The determinant of the representation
\overline{\rho}_{E,p}
is the mod
p
cyclotomic character
\chi_{p}
.

Proof.

Recall that the invariants
c_{4}(E)
and
\Delta_{E}
are given by

c_{4}(E)=2^{4}C(4Aa^{p}+Bb^{p})\;\text{and}\;\Delta_{E}=2^{6}AB^{2}C^{3}(ab^{2})^{p}.

•

If
{\mathfrak{q}}\not\in S_{K}
does not divide
ab
, then
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(\Delta_{E})=0
. Therefore, the model is minimal and
E
has good reduction at
{\mathfrak{q}}
.

•

If
{\mathfrak{q}}
is an odd prime and
{\mathfrak{q}}\mid Aa
or
Bb
, then the given model is minimal and
E
has multiplicative reduction at
{\mathfrak{q}}
. Indeed, without loss of generality, assume that
{\mathfrak{q}}
is an odd prime and
{\mathfrak{q}}\mid Aa
. We then see that
{\mathfrak{q}}
does not divide
CBb
. It then follows that
c_{4}(E)=2^{4}C(4Aa^{p}+Bb^{p})
is not divisible by
{\mathfrak{q}}
, i.e.
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(c_{4}(E))=0
. Thus, the given model is minimal and
E
has multiplicative reduction at
{\mathfrak{q}}
. For each prime
{\mathfrak{P}}
dividing
2C
, the bounds on the exponents
r_{\mathfrak{P}}
and
r^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{P}}
follow from
[
33
, Theorem IV.10.4]
. It immediately follows from Equation
2.1
that the Frey curve
E
has a
2
-torsion point over
K
. This finishes the proof of (1).

Let
{\mathfrak{p}}
be a prime of
K
lying above
p
. We have

v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\Delta_{E})=v_{\mathfrak{p}}(A)+2v_{\mathfrak{p}}(B)+3v_{\mathfrak{p}}(C)+pv_{\mathfrak{p}}(a)+2pv_{\mathfrak{p}}(b).

As we assume

, we see that

. It then follows from
[
31
]
that
\overline{\rho}_{E,p}
is finite-flat at
{\mathfrak{p}}
. We can also deduce that
\overline{\rho}_{E,p}
is unramified at

for odd primes

of

. Therefore, for all primes

, we have

. In other words, the Serre conductor
{\mathfrak{N}}_{E}
is supported on the primes in

. As the Serre conductor
{\mathfrak{N}}_{E}
divides
{\mathcal{N}}_{E}
and is divisible only by the primes

, there can be only finitely many Serre conductors and they only depend on

∎

2.2.
Image of Inertia

Let
K
be a number field, and let
{\mathfrak{q}}
be a prime of
K
. In this section, we gather information about the image of inertia
\overline{\rho}_{E,p}(I_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}})

We use the following lemma for detecting when an elliptic curve has potentially multiplicative reduction.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
14
]
, Lemma 3.4)
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
K
with
j
-invariant

. Let

\mathfrak{q}\nmid p
be a prime
of

p\mid\#\overline{\rho}_{E,p}(I_{\mathfrak{q}})
if and only if

has potentially multiplicative

\mathfrak{q}
(i.e.
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(j_{E})<0
) and

.

By using the previous result we obtain:

Lemma 2.3
.

Fix
\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}\in T_{K}
and let
(a,b,c)\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{3}
be a non-trivial primitive solution to (
1.1
) with
\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}\mid b
and exponent
p>6v_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}}(2)
. Let
E
be the Frey curve as in (

) associated to
(a,b,c)
, and write

for its

-invariant. Then

has potentially multiplicative reduction at
\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}
and

, where

denotes an inertia subgroup of

at
\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}
.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}\in T_{K}
with
\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}\mid b
and exponent

. Then we have

\displaystyle v_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}}(j_{E})

\displaystyle=6v_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}}(2)+3v_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}}(4Aa^{p}+Bb^{p})-pv_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}}(ab^{2})

\displaystyle=12v_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}}(2)-2pv_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}}(b)

\displaystyle=2(6v_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}}(2)-pv_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}}(b)).

Since

, we have

has potentially multiplicative reduction at
\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}
(i.e.

), and

∎

3.
Modularity of the Frey Curve

In this section, we provide the theoretical background we need concerning modularity to prove our results.

If
E
is an elliptic curve over a number field
K
, we say that
E
is modular if there is an isomorphism of a compatible system of Galois representations

\rho_{E,p}\sim\rho_{{\mathfrak{f}},\varpi},

where
{\mathfrak{f}}
is an automorphic form over
K
of weight two with coefficient field
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\mathfrak{f}}
and
\varpi
is a prime in
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\mathfrak{f}}
lying above
p
.

3.1.
Totally real case

In the totally real case,
{\mathfrak{f}}
comes from a Hilbert eigenform of parallel weight two over
K
. The following result is due to Freitas, Le Hung and Siksek
[
12
, Theorems 1 and 5]
:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
K
be a totally real number field. There are at most finitely many
\overline{K}
-isomorphism classes of non-modular elliptic curves
E
over
K
. Moreover, if
K
is real quadratic, then all elliptic curves over
K
are modular.

Remark 3.2
.

All elliptic curves defined over totally real number fields are
conjectured to be modular. It was proven to hold for
cubic fields in
[
9
]
and for quartic fields not containing a square
root of
5
in
[
3
]
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
K
be a totally real number field, and fix
\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}\in T_{K}
. There is some constant
W(A,B,C,K)
, depending only on
K
and
A,B,C
, such that for any non-trivial primitive solution
(a,b,c)
to (
1.1
) with
\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}\mid b
and the exponent
p>W(A,B,C,K)
, the Frey curve
E
is modular.

Proof.

The proof follows very closely the proof of
[
27
, Lemma 26]
. It follows from Theorem
3.1
that there are at most finitely many possible
\overline{K}
-isomorphism classes of elliptic curves over
K
which are non-modular. Let
j_{1},\dots,j_{n}\in K
denote the
j
-invariants of these classes. Define
\lambda:=\frac{Aa^{p}}{Bb^{p}}
so that the
j
-invariant of
E
is

j(\lambda)=2^{6}(4\lambda+1)^{3}\lambda^{-1}.

We can assume that
\lambda\not\in\mathbb{Q}
, since these
\lambda
would lead to
j(\lambda)\in\mathbb{Q}
, and it is known that all rational elliptic curves are modular. Each equation
j(\lambda)=j_{i}
has at most three solutions in
K
. Thus there are values
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m}\in K
(where
m\leq 3n
) such that if
\lambda\neq\lambda_{k}
for all
k
, then the elliptic curve
E
with the
j
-invariant
j(\lambda)
is modular.

If
\lambda=\lambda_{k}
for some
k
, then
\tfrac{A}{B}\left(\tfrac{a}{b}\right)^{p}=\lambda_{k}
. Since
K
is totally real and
\lambda_{k}\notin\{0,\pm 1\}
, this equality holds if and only if
\frac{B}{A}\lambda_{k}\in(K^{\times})^{p}
. For each fixed
k
, the element
\frac{B}{A}\lambda_{k}\in K^{\times}
can be a
p
-th power for only finitely many primes
p
.
Thus, there exists a constant
V_{k}
such that for all primes
p>V_{k}
, the above equality cannot hold.

Let
W(A,B,C,K):=\max_{k}\{V_{k}\}
. Then for any prime

, we have
\lambda\neq\lambda_{k}
for all

. Therefore, the Frey curve

is modular.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

The finiteness part of Theorem
3.1
relies on Falting’s Theorem
[
11
]
. The bound in Falting’s Theorem is ineffective, and so is the bound

. Note that if

is quadratic or cubic we get

.

3.2.
General case

One needs generalized modularity theorems in order to run the modular approach to solve Diophantine equations. Because of the lack of their existence in the general case, we must assume them as conjectures. In this section, we give Conjecture
3.7
which is a special case of Serre’s modularity conjecture. First, we fix some notations.

Let
K
be an arbitrary number field (admitting possibly a complex embedding) with the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
and signature
(r,s)
. Let
{\mathfrak{N}}
be an ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
and consider the locally symmetric space
Y_{0}({\mathfrak{N}})
, defined as in
[
32
, §4]
.

For
i\in\{0,\dots,2r+3s\}
, consider the
i
-th cohomology group
H^{i}(Y_{0}({\mathfrak{N}}),{\mathbb{C}})
. For every prime
{\mathfrak{q}}
coprime to the level
{\mathfrak{N}}
, one can construct a Hecke operator

of
H^{i}(Y_{0}({\mathfrak{N}}),{\mathbb{C}})
and these operators commute with each other. We let
\mathbb{T}^{(i)}_{\mathbb{C}}({\mathfrak{N}})
denote the commutative
{\mathbb{Z}}
-algebra generated by these Hecke
operators inside the endomorphism algebra of
H^{i}(Y_{0}({\mathfrak{N}}),{\mathbb{C}})
.

Definition 3.5
.

A weight two complex eigenform
{\mathfrak{f}}
over

of degree

and level

is a ring homomorphism
{\mathfrak{f}}:\mathbb{T}^{(i)}_{\mathbb{C}}({\mathfrak{N}})\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}

Note that the values of

are algebraic integers and they generate a number field which we shall denote by
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\mathfrak{f}}
. We call a complex eigenform

if we have
{\mathfrak{f}}(T_{\mathfrak{q}})={\mathbb{N}}{\mathfrak{q}}+1
for all primes
{\mathfrak{q}}
coprime to the level. We call two complex eigenforms
{\mathfrak{f}},{\mathfrak{g}}
with possibly different degrees and levels
equivalent
if
{\mathfrak{f}}(T_{\mathfrak{q}})={\mathfrak{g}}(T_{\mathfrak{q}})
for almost all prime ideals
{\mathfrak{q}}
. A complex eigenform of level
{\mathfrak{N}}
is called
new
if it is not equivalent to one whose level is a proper divisor of
{\mathfrak{N}}
.

Let
p
be a rational prime unramified in
K
and coprime to
{\mathfrak{N}}
. The cohomology group
H^{i}(Y_{0}({\mathfrak{N}}),\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p})
also comes equipped with Hecke operators, still denoted

(we only consider the primes
{\mathfrak{q}}
coprime to
p{\mathfrak{N}}
). Denote the corresponding Hecke algebra by
\mathbb{T}^{(i)}_{\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}}({\mathfrak{N}})
.

Definition 3.6
.

A weight two mod
p
eigenform
\theta
over
K
of degree
i
and level
{\mathfrak{N}}
is a ring homomorphism
\theta:\mathbb{T}^{(i)}_{\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}}({\mathfrak{N}})\longrightarrow\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}
.

We say that a mod
p
eigenform
\theta
of level
{\mathfrak{N}}
lifts to a complex eigenform if there is a complex eigenform
{\mathfrak{f}}
of the same degree and level and a prime ideal
{\mathfrak{p}}
of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\mathfrak{f}}
over
p
such that for every prime
{\mathfrak{q}}
of
K
coprime to
p{\mathfrak{N}}
we have
\theta(T_{\mathfrak{q}})\equiv{\mathfrak{f}}(T_{\mathfrak{q}})\pmod{{\mathfrak{p}}}
.

For a detailed discussion concerning complex and mod
p
eigenforms over
K
, we refer the reader to
[
32
, Sections 2 and 3]
. The following conjecture is a special case of Serre’s modularity conjecture over number fields.

Conjecture 3.7

(
[
32
]
, Conjecture 3.1)
.

Let
\overline{\rho}:G_{K}\rightarrow\GL_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
be an odd, irreducible, continuous representation with Serre conductor
{\mathfrak{N}}
(prime-to-
p
part of its Artin conductor)
and such that
\det(\overline{\rho})=\chi_{p}
is the mod
p

Assume that
p
is unramified in

and that
\overline{\rho}\mid_{G_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{\mathfrak{p}}}}
arises from a finite-flat group scheme over
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}}
for every prime
{\mathfrak{p}}\mid p
. Then there is a weight two, mod
p
eigenform
\theta
over
K
of level
{\mathfrak{N}}
such that for all primes
{\mathfrak{q}}
coprime to
p{\mathfrak{N}}
, we have

{\rm Tr}(\overline{\rho}({\rm Frob}_{{\mathfrak{q}}}))=\theta(T_{{\mathfrak{q}}}),

where

denotes the Hecke operator at

.

Remark 3.8
.

Given a number field
K
, we obtain a
complex conjugation
for every real embedding

\sigma:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}
and every extension
\widetilde{\sigma}:\overline{K}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
of

as
\widetilde{\sigma}^{-1}\iota\widetilde{\sigma}\in G_{K}
where

is the usual complex conjugation. Recall that a representation

is

if the determinant of every complex conjugation is

. If the number field

is odd. It follows from Lemma

is odd.

4.
Properties of Galois Representations and Level Lowering

In this section, we first prove the irreducibility of the mod

Galois
representation,

, associated to the Frey elliptic curve and then we relate it with another representation of lower level via a new elliptic curve

whose conductor is independent of the solution.

4.1.
Irreducibility of the associated Galois representation

We need to prove the irreducibility of the mod

Galois
representation associated to the Frey elliptic curve. This is needed in the level lowering step, which will be proved in Section

.
The following well-known result about subgroups of

will be used to prove the irreducibility of the Galois representation

.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let

be an elliptic curve over a number field

of degree
d
and let

be the
image of the mod
p
Galois representation
\overline{\rho}_{E,p}
of

.
Then the following holds:

•

If
p\mid\#G
then either
\overline{\rho}_{E,p}
is reducible or
G
contains
{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
. In the latter case, we deduce that
\overline{\rho}_{E,p}
is absolutely irreducible.

•

If
p\nmid\#G
and
p>15d+1
then
G
is contained in a Cartan subgroup or
G
is contained in the normalizer of a Cartan subgroup but not the Cartan subgroup itself.

Proof.

For the proof, the main reference is
[
34
, Lemma 2]
. The version above including the proof of the second part is from
[
10
, Propositions 2.3 and 2.6]
.
∎

The following is Proposition 6.1 from Şengün and Siksek
[
32
]
. We include its statement for the convenience of the reader but we will omit its proof and refer to
[
32
]
instead.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
L
be a Galois number field and let
{\mathfrak{q}}
be a prime of
L
. There is a constant
B_{L,{\mathfrak{q}}}
such that the following is true. Let

be a rational prime. Let

be an elliptic curve that is semistable at all

and has potentially multiplicative reduction at
{\mathfrak{q}}
. Then
\overline{\rho}_{E,p}
is irreducible.

We apply this to our Frey curve given in (
2.1
) to obtain the surjectivity of
\overline{\rho}_{E,p}
.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
K
be a number field, and fix
\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}\in T_{K}
. There is a constant
D=D(A,B,C,K)
such that if
p>D
and

(a,b,c)
is a non-trivial primitive solution to (
1.1
) with
\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}\mid b
, then the Galois representation
\overline{\rho}_{E,p}
is surjective.

Proof.

It follows by Lemma
2.3
that if
p>6v_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}}(2)
, the Frey curve
E
has potentially multiplicative reduction at
\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}
. Also,
E
is semistable away from the primes dividing
2C
by Lemma
2.1
. Let
L
be the Galois closure of
K
, and let
\widetilde{\mathfrak{q}}
be a prime of
L
above
\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}
. Now, by applying Proposition
4.2
, we get a constant
B_{L,\widetilde{\mathfrak{q}}}
such that
\overline{\rho}_{E,p}
is irreducible whenever
p>B_{L,\widetilde{\mathfrak{q}}}
. Note that there are only finitely many choices of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{q}}
in
L
dividing
\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}
, and
L
only depends on
K
. Hence, we can obtain a constant depending only on
A,B,C,K
and we denote it by
D=D(A,B,C,K)
. We also enlarge
D
, if necessary, so that
D>6v_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{P}}}(2)
(since we applied Lemma
2.3
) and deduce that
\overline{\rho}_{E,p}
is irreducible when

by Proposition
4.2
.

Now, we apply Lemma
2.3
again and see that the image of
\overline{\rho}_{E,p}
contains an element of order
p
. By Theorem
4.1
any subgroup of
\GL_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{p})

having an element of order
p
is either reducible or contains
{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{p})
. As
p>D>6v_{{\mathfrak{P}}}(2)
, which implies that
\overline{\rho}_{E,p}
is irreducible, the image contains
{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{p})
. Finally, we can ensure that

by taking

large enough if needed. Hence,
\chi_{p}=\det(\overline{\rho}_{E,p})
is surjective giving the following short exact sequence

1\longrightarrow\SL_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{p})\longrightarrow\overline{\rho}_{E,p}(G_{K})\xrightarrow{\;\;\det\;\;}{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\times}\longrightarrow 1,

which completes the proof.
∎

4.2.
Level Lowering and Eichler-Shimura

For our asymptotic results, namely for Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
and
1.3

By [

(1)

6.9

\mathcal{N}_{\theta}

6.15

E

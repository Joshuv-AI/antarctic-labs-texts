On binomial edge ideals of corona of graphs
Buddhadev Hajra, Rajib Sarkar

Abstract
. For a simple graph G G , let J G J_{G} denote the corresponding binomial edge ideal. This article considers the binomial edge ideal of the corona product of two connected graphs G G and H H . The corona product of G G and H H , denoted by G ∘ H G\circ H , is a construction where each vertex of G G is connected (via the coning-off) to an entire copy of H H . This is a direct generalization of a cone construction. Previous studies have shown that for J G ∘ H J_{G\circ H} to be Cohen-Macaulay, both G G and H H must be complete graphs. However, there are no general formulae for the dimension, depth, or Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of J G ∘ H J_{G\circ H} for all graphs G G and H H . In this article, we provide a general formula for the dimension, depth and Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of the binomial edge ideals of certain corona and corona-type (somewhat a generalization of corona) products of special interests. Additionally, we study the Cohen-Macaulayness, unmixedness and related properties of binomial edge ideals corresponding to above class of graphs. We have also added a short note on the reduction of the Bolognini-Macchia-Strazzanti Conjecture to all graphs with a diameter of 3 3 .

1.
Introduction

In the past two decades, the dynamic intersection of combinatorics of a graph and the algebra of various ideals arising from it has become a vibrant area of research. One of the most extensively studied ideals in this context is the (monomial) edge ideal. Studying the Cohen-Macaulayness property of edge ideals is an important topic in this area. Though the complete classification of all simple graphs having Cohen-Macaulay edge ideal is not yet known, an exhaustive classification of bipartite graphs corresponding to the Cohen-Macaulay edge ideals is well understood.

In 2010, Herzog et al. in
[
HHHKR10
]
and Ohtani independently in
[
Oht11
]
introduced the concept of binomial edge ideals for simple graphs — the graphs having no loops or multiple edges. We refer the readers to the book
[
HHO18
]
and
[
Das22
,
SM18
]
for a detailed exploration of this topic. Consider a simple graph
G
with the vertex set
V(G)=[n]:=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
and the edge set
E(G)
. Let
S=\mathbf{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
be the polynomial ring in
2n
variables over an arbitrary field
\mathbf{k}
. Then, the binomial edge ideal corresponding to the graph
G
denoted by
J_{G}
is an ideal that is defined as follows:

J_{G}=\left\langle m_{ij}:\{i,j\}\in E(G),1\leq i<j\leq n\right\rangle\subseteq S,

where
m_{ij}
denotes the
(i,j)
-th
(2\times 2)
-minor
\det\begin{pmatrix}x_{i}&x_{j}\\
y_{i}&y_{j}\end{pmatrix}=x_{i}y_{j}-x_{j}y_{i}

of the following
(2\times n)
-matrix

M_{G}:=\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}&x_{2}&\cdots&x_{n}\\
y_{1}&y_{2}&\cdots&y_{n}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

The class of binomial edge ideals serves as a natural generalization of the
determinantal ideal

I_{2}
generated by all the
(2\times 2)
-minors of the
(2\times n)
-matrix
M_{G}
of indeterminates defined above. In particular, when
G
is the complete graph
K_{n}
, the binomial edge ideal
J_{G}
of
G
coincides with the ideal
I_{2}
.

Over the years, many researchers have paid attention to characterizing Cohen-Macaulay binomial edge ideals in the same spirit as in the case of classical edge ideals. Various endeavors have been made to address this challenge across different interesting families of graphs, viz., chordal, bipartite, (generalized) block, traceable, Hamiltonian, unicyclic, etc. Important contributions in this pursuit include the research in
[
BMRS24
,
BMS18
,
BMS22
,
EHH11
,
LMRR23
,
LRR24
,
RR14
,
Rin13
,
Rin19
,
SMK18
,
SS22
,
SS24
]
. Specifically, in
[
BMS22
]
, the authors introduced insightful combinatorial properties of Cohen-Macaulay binomial edge ideals, a contribution later leveraged in
[
LMRR23
]
to computationally classify all such ideals for graphs with up to
12
number of vertices. Recently, in
[
BMRS24
]
, the authors developed an algorithm to characterize Cohen-Macaulay binomial edge ideals for the graphs with up to
15
number of vertices. Despite these efforts, achieving a comprehensive classification of graphs with Cohen-Macaulay binomial edge ideals appears to be a formidable task. However, researchers have delved into a fundamental algebraic invariant, namely, the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of binomial edge ideals, employing the combinatorial intricacies of the underlying graph. This line of investigation has proven fruitful in the classification of specific Cohen-Macaulay binomial edge ideals, as evidenced in
[
ERT20
,
RS23
,
SMK18
]
. The exploration of both algebraic properties and numerical invariants of binomial edge ideals has received very significant attention in recent times.

Throughout this article, unless explicitly stated otherwise, we assume the graphs under discussion are connected. The
corona product
of two graphs, denoted as
G\circ H
, is defined as follows: take one copy of graph
G
and
|V(G)|
copies of graph
H
, connecting each vertex from the
i
-th copy of
H
with the
i
-th vertex of the graph
G
. The notation
H_{v}
refers to the copy of
H
connected to vertex
v
in
G\circ H
, and we use this notation quite often in the subsequent section. In particular, when
H=K_{1}
(a
point graph
),
G\circ H
is equivalent to the
whisker graph
, the graph which is obtained from a graph
G
by adding a whisker to each of its vertices. This fascinating construction of the corona of two graphs first appeared in
[
FH70
]
. As mentioned in
[
FH70
]
, it immediately
follows from the definition of the corona that

|V(G\circ H)|=|V(G)|(1+|V(H)|)
;

|E(G\circ H)|=|E(G)|+|V(G)|(|V(H)|+|E(H)|)
.

Simple examples illustrate that, in general,
G\circ H
is not equivalent to
H\circ G
. Furthermore, the associative law does not hold; i.e.,
G_{1}\circ(G_{2}\circ G_{3})
and
(G_{1}\circ G_{2})\circ G_{3}
represent distinct graphs for any three graphs
G_{1},G_{2},G_{3}
.

Since the class of corona of any two graphs appears to be interesting, it is important to check the Cohen-Macaulayness of the binomial edge ideal of the corona product. It can be easily observed by studying the cutsets that very often, the binomial edge ideal of a corona product fails to be Cohen-Macaulay. Therefore it is evident that in the case when
J_{G\circ H}
is indeed Cohen-Macaulay, the two graphs
G
and
H
are rather very special. In
[
KSM15
]
, Kiani and Saeedi Madani briefly studied the corona product of two graphs. They investigated the strong correlation between unmixedness and the Cohen-Macaulayness properties of the binomial edge ideals associated with the corona of two graphs. They proved that in the case of the corona product of two graphs, the two notions — unmixedness and Cohen-Macaulayness, in fact, are equivalent. One of the key results in this direction is — “
for any two graphs
G
and
H
with
|V(G)|\geq 2
, if
J_{G\circ H}
is Cohen-Macaulay (equivalently, unmixed), both the graphs
G
and
H
have to be complete graphs
” (cf.
[
KSM15
, Theorem 5.4]
). Since the unmixedness of a binomial edge ideal corresponding to any graph can be completely understood from the combinatorial data of the underlying graph, in the paper
[
KSM15
]
, the authors didn’t dig into the derivation of any formula for the well-known algebraic invariants such as depth, Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity, etc. of the binomial edge ideal corresponding to the corona product in order to study the Cohen-Macaulayness of the same.

A general formula for depth and Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity for the binomial edge ideals of the corona product purely in terms of those invariants of the two individual graphs would still be a very interesting but rather challenging question. In this article, we give a general formula for depth and Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity for the affine
\mathbf{k}
-algebra
S/J_{B\circ H}
, where
B
is a Cohen-Macaulay closed graph and
H
is an arbitrary graph.

Notation
.

Let
G
be a graph on
V(G)
. We reserve the notation
S
for the polynomial ring
\mathbf{k}[x_{u},y_{u}:u\in V(G)]
and for
v\in V(G)
,
S^{\prime}
for the polynomial ring
\mathbf{k}[x_{u},y_{u}:u\in V(G)\setminus\{v\}]
. If
H
is any other graph with the vertex set
V(H)
, then we set
S_{H}=\mathbf{k}[x_{u},y_{u}:u\in V(H)]
and for
w\in V(H)
, set
S_{H}^{\prime}=\mathbf{k}[x_{u},y_{u}:u\in V(H)\setminus\{w\}]
.

Convention
.

To be consistent, we reserve the following nomenclature throughout this article. A graph
G
is said to be unmixed (resp. Cohen-Macaulay) if
J_{G}
, the corresponding binomial edge ideal, is unmixed (resp. Cohen-Macaulay).

1.1.
Structure and main results of the paper

We have added the necessary preliminaries, both combinatorial and algebraic, in Section 2. The results obtained in the two subsequent sections (viz. Section 2 & Section 3) are of two different flavors. Firstly, we build the combinatorial understanding of unmixed/accessible/Cohen-Macaulay binomial edge ideals of the corona product and related graphs (see Section 3). We conclude Section 3 by adding a short note on the reduction of Bolognini-Macchia-Strazzanti Conjecture (B-M-S Conjecture, in short) to all simple graphs with diameter 3 (see Question
3.14
). Section 4 is a bit technical and is solely devoted to the computation of dimension, depth, and regularity formulae for certain binomial edge ideals of special interest.

We prove the following main results:

Theorem A

(Theorems
3.3
,
3.9
)
.

Let
G
and
H
be two graphs such that
|V(G)|>1
. For a non-empty subset
L
of
V(G)
, if
J_{G\circ_{L}H}
is unmixed (resp. accessible), then
J_{H}
is also unmixed (resp. accessible).

The converse to the above result is not true in general. For this, we gave an example.

The (generalized)
L
-corona product of any graph with the complete graph, i.e., the graphs
K_{n}\circ_{\ell}H
for all
n\geq 2
and any graph
H
are of special interest. We prove the following results:

Theorem B

(Theorem
3.12
)
.

Let
H
be any graph. For integers
n
and
\ell
such that
n\geq 2
and
1\leq\ell<n
,
K_{n}\circ_{\ell}H
is unmixed (or accessible or Cohen-Macaulay) if and only if
H
is unmixed (or accessible or Cohen-Macaulay, respectively).

Theorem C

(Theorem
4.1
)
.

Let
H
be any connected graph and
n\geq 1
any integer. Consider the graph
G:=K_{n}\circ_{\ell}H
with
1\leq\ell\leq n
. Then

\dim(S/J_{G})=n-\ell+1+\ell\dim(S_{H}/J_{H}),

where
|V(H)|=h
. Moreover, if
J_{H}
is unmixed then

\dim(S/J_{K_{n}\circ H})=n+nh+1

This dimension formula extends the result in
[
KSM15
, Corollary 5.2]
.

Theorem D

(Theorem
4.7
)
.

For any connected graph
H
and any integer
n\geq 1
, let
G:=K_{n}\circ H
. Then the following assertions hold.

(1)

0pt(S/J_{G})=\begin{cases}1+n\cdot 0pt(S_{H}/J_{H}),&\text{whenever }H\text{ is complete};\\
n\cdot 0pt(S_{H}/J_{H}),&\text{whenever }H\text{ is non-complete}.\end{cases}

(2)

\operatorname{reg}(S/J_{G})=\begin{cases}n+1,&\text{whenever }H\text{ is complete};\\
n\cdot\operatorname{reg}(S_{H}/J_{H}),&\text{whenever }H\text{ is non-complete}.\end{cases}

(3)

Assume that
H
is not a complete graph,
\operatorname{pd}(S_{H}/J_{H})=p_{H}
and
\beta_{p_{H},p_{H}+r_{H}}(S_{H}/J_{H})
is an extremal Betti number of
S_{H}/J_{H}
for
r_{H}\geq 2
. Then
\beta_{p,p+nr_{H}}(S/J_{G})
is an extremal Betti number if
n=2
and
\beta_{p,p+nr_{H}+1}(S/J_{G})
is an extremal Betti number if
n\geq 3
, where
p=\operatorname{pd}(S/J_{G})=2n+np_{H}.

More generally, we prove the following:

Theorem E

(Theorem
4.11
)
.

Let
B
be a Cohen-Macaulay closed graph and
H
any connected graph with
|V(B)|=b
. Consider the graph
G:=B\circ H
for
n\geq 1
. Then

(1)

0pt(S/J_{G})=\begin{cases}1+b\cdot 0pt(S_{H}/J_{H}),&\text{if }H\text{ is complete};\\
b\cdot 0pt(S_{H}/J_{H}),&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(2)

\operatorname{reg}(S/J_{G})=\begin{cases}b+1,&\text{if }H\text{ is complete};\\
b\cdot\operatorname{reg}(S_{H}/J_{H}),&\text{otherwise }.\end{cases}

(3)

Assume that
H
is not a complete graph,
\operatorname{pd}(S_{H}/J_{H})=p_{H}
and
\beta_{p_{H},p_{H}+r_{H}}(S_{H}/J_{H})
is an extremal Betti number of
S_{H}/J_{H}
for
r_{H}\geq 2
. Then
\beta_{p,p+br_{H}+1}(S/J_{G})
is an extremal Betti number, where
p=\operatorname{pd}(S/J_{G})=2b+bp_{H}.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Combinatorial Preliminaries

We recall some basic notation and definitions from graph theory which will be used throughout this article.

For a graph
G
, we denote the vertex set and edge set respectively by
V(G)
and
E(G)
. A subgraph
H
of
G
is said to be an
induced subgraph
if for
u,v\in V(H)
,
\{u,v\}\in E(H)
if and only if
\{u,v\}\in E(G)
. For a graph
G
, a
clique
is a complete subgraph of
G
. A vertex
v\in V(G)
is said to be a
simplicial vertex
if it belongs to exactly one maximal clique of
G
and a vertex is called
internal
if it is not simplicial. The set of internal vertices of
G
is denoted by
\operatorname{iv}(G)
.
Given
v\in V(G)
, define the
neighborhood of
v
in
G
as
N_{G}(v):=\{u~:~\{u,v\}\in E(G)\}\subset V(G)
.
For
v\in V(G)
, let
G_{v}
denote the graph with vertices
V(G)
and
E(G_{v})=E(G)\cup\{\{u,w\}~:~u,w\in N_{G}(v)\}
.

For any subset
A\subset V(G)
, by abuse of notation,
G\setminus A
denotes the induced subgraph of
G
on the vertex set
V(G)\setminus A
. In particular, for a vertex
v\in V(G)
, we denote by
G\setminus v
the induced subgraph of
G
on the vertex set
V(G)\setminus\{v\}
.

For any subset
T\subset V(G)
, we reserve the notation
\omega(G\setminus T)
to denote the number of connected components of the induced subgraph
G\setminus T
. A subset
T\subset V(G)
is called a
cutset
in
G
if either
T=\emptyset
or
T\neq\emptyset
with
\omega(G\setminus(T\setminus\{v\}))<\omega(G\setminus T)
for every vertex
v\in T
. The existence of a non-empty cutset is always guaranteed in every graph, which is different from a complete graph
K_{n}
. Let
\mathscr{C}(G)
denote the set of all cutsets in
G
.

We will say that
\mathscr{C}(G)
is an
accessible set system
if for every non-empty
T\in\mathscr{C}(G)
, there exists
t\in T
such that
T\setminus\{t\}\in\mathscr{C}(G)
. A graph
G
is called
accessible
if its binomial edge ideal
J_{G}
is unmixed and
\mathscr{C}(G)
is an
accessible set system
. These definitions are being used in
[
BMS22
]
.

2.2.
Algebraic Preliminaries

We now recall the definition of two important homological invariants, which can be computed directly from the Betti table. One of them is the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity, which measures the complexity of the structure of a graded module over a polynomial ring
A
. Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
A
-module and
\beta_{i,i+j}(M)
the graded Betti numbers. The
projective dimension
of
M
is denoted by
\operatorname{pd}_{A}(M)
(or, simply
\operatorname{pd}(M)
) and is defined as

\operatorname{pd}_{A}(M):=\max\{i:\beta_{i,i+j}(M)\neq 0\text{ for some }j\};

and the
Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity
(or simply,
regularity
) of
M
is defined as

\operatorname{reg}(M):=\max\{j:\beta_{i,i+j}(M)\neq 0\text{ for some }i\}.

The following formula due to Auslander and Buchsbaum is useful for our later discussion.

Theorem 2.1

(Auslander–Buchsbaum Formula)
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian local (or graded) ring and
M
is a non-zero finitely generated
R
-module such that
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M)<\infty
, then

\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M)+0pt(M)=0pt(R).

For the depth computation of binomial edge ideals, we need the following important result due to Ohtani.

Lemma 2.2

(Ohtani, cf.
[
Oht11
, Lemma 4.8]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph and
v\in V(G)
be a non-simplicial vertex. Then

J_{G}=(J_{G\setminus v}+(x_{v},y_{v}))\cap J_{G_{v}}.

Thus, we have the following short exact sequence:

(2.1)

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\frac{S}{J_{G}}\longrightarrow\frac{S}{(x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G\setminus v}}\oplus\frac{S}{J_{{G_{v}}}}\longrightarrow\frac{S}{(x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G_{v}\setminus v}}\longrightarrow 0,

and correspondingly, the long exact sequence of Tor modules:

(2.2)

\cdots\longrightarrow\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{S}\left(\frac{S}{J_{G}},\mathbf{k}\right)_{i+j}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{S}\left(\frac{S}{(x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G\setminus v}},\mathbf{k}\right)_{i+j}\oplus\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{S}\left(\frac{S}{J_{G_{v}}},\mathbf{k}\right)_{i+j}\\
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{S}\left(\frac{S}{(x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G_{v}\setminus v}},\mathbf{k}\right)_{i+j}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Tor}_{i-1}^{S}\left(\frac{S}{J_{G}},\mathbf{k}\right)_{i+j}\longrightarrow\cdots

We now recall the following basic properties of depth, which will be useful in the subsequent proofs. In the later proofs, we refer to this lemma as “Depth Lemma”.

Lemma 2.3

(
Depth Lemma).

Let
S
be a standard graded polynomial ring over a field
k
and let
M
,
N
and
P
be finitely generated graded
S
-modules. If

0\to M\xrightarrow{f}N\xrightarrow{g}P\to 0

is a short exact sequence with
f
,
g
graded homomorphisms of degree zero, then

(a)

0pt_{S}(M)\geq\min\{0pt_{S}(N),0pt_{S}(P)+1\}
;

(b)

0pt_{S}(M)=0pt_{S}(P)+1
, if
0pt_{S}(N)>0pt_{S}(P)
;

(c)

0pt_{S}(M)=0pt_{S}(N)
, if
0pt_{S}(N)<0pt_{S}(P)
.

(d)

0pt_{S}(N)\geq\min\{0pt_{S}(M),0pt_{S}(P)\}
;

(e)

0pt_{S}(P)\geq\min\{0pt_{S}(N),0pt_{S}(M)-1\}
;

(f)

0pt_{S}(P)=0pt_{S}(N)
, if
0pt_{S}(N)<0pt_{S}(M)
.

Likewise, we recall the following basic properties of regularity, which we will use in our later proofs. From now onward, we refer to this lemma as “Regularity Lemma”.

Lemma 2.4

(
Regularity Lemma).

Let
S
be a standard graded polynomial ring over a field
k
and let
M
,
N
and
P
be finitely generated graded
S
-modules. If

0\to M\xrightarrow{f}N\xrightarrow{g}P\to 0

is a short exact sequence with
f
,
g
graded homomorphisms of degree zero, then

(a)

\operatorname{reg}(M)\leq\max\{\operatorname{reg}(N),\operatorname{reg}(P)+1\}
;

(b)

\operatorname{reg}(M)=\operatorname{reg}(P)+1
, if
\operatorname{reg}(N)<\operatorname{reg}(P)
;

(c)

\operatorname{reg}(M)=\operatorname{reg}(N)
, if
\operatorname{reg}(N)>\operatorname{reg}(P)
.

The above two lemmas can be easily derived from the long exact sequence of
\operatorname{Tor}
and
\operatorname{Ext}
corresponding to the short exact sequence mentioned.

3.
Unmixedness, Cohen-Macaulayness, and Accessibility of Certain Corona Products

Let
G
and
H
be two graphs with
|V(G)|=m
and
|V(H)|=n
. Let
V(G)=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}\}
. Suppose,
H_{v_{i}}
(or simply
H_{i}
) denotes the copy of
H
which is joined to the vertex
v_{i}
of
G
in
G\circ H
for all
i=1,\ldots,m
. Thus

G\circ H:=G\cup(\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}v_{i}\ast H_{i}).

Modifying the above, we define for a non-empty subset
L\subset V(G)

G\circ_{L}H:=G\cup(\bigcup_{v\in L}v\ast H_{v}).

We call
G\circ_{L}H
the (generalized)
L
-corona product
of
G
with
H
. The definition of
G\circ_{L}H
heavily depends on the choice of
L
. Clearly,
G\circ_{L}H
coincides with the (usual) corona product
G\circ H
if and only if
L=V(G)
.

Whenever
G=K_{m}
, all subsets
L
of
V(G)
with
|L|=\ell
behave the same and thus we use the notation
K_{m}\circ_{\ell}H
for
K_{m}\circ_{L}H
to emphasize the fact that this is independent of the choice of
L
with
|L|=\ell
.

Every cutset
T
of
G\circ H
can be expressed as
T=T_{0}\cup(\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}T_{i})
, where
T_{0}\subset V(G)
and
T_{i}\subset V(H_{i})
for all
1\leq i\leq m
. The detailed description of a cutset for
G\circ H
or, more generally, for
G\circ_{L}H
with
L\subset V(G)
can be understood from the result below.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
G
and
H
be two graphs and
L
a non-empty proper subset of
V(G)
. Let
\emptyset\neq T\in\mathscr{C}(G\circ_{L}H)
with
T=T_{0}\cup(\bigcup_{v\in L}T_{v})
for
T_{0}\subset V(G)
and
T_{v}\subset V(H_{v})
for all
v\in L
. Then, the following assertions hold.

(1)

\emptyset\neq T_{0}\subsetneq V(G)
.

(2)

If
v\in L\setminus T_{0}
, then
T_{v}=\emptyset
.

(3)

If
v\in T_{0}
, then either
T_{v}=\emptyset
or
T_{v}\in\mathscr{C}(H_{v})
.

(4)

If
v\in L\cap T_{0}
such that
N_{G}(v)\subset T_{0}
, then
T_{v}\neq\emptyset
.

(5)

Let
N:=\{v\in L:T_{v}\neq\emptyset\}
. Then

\omega(G\circ_{L}H\setminus T)=\omega(G\setminus T_{0})+\sum\limits_{v\in N}\omega(H_{v}\setminus T_{v})+|T_{0}\cap L|-|N|.

(6)

If
(T_{0})_{G-sim}
denotes the set of simplicial vertices of
G
in
T_{0}
, then
(T_{0})_{G-sim}\subset L
.

(7)

If
T_{0}\cap L=\emptyset
, then
T=T_{0}\in\mathscr{C}(G)
and
(T_{0})_{G-sim}=\emptyset
.

Proof.

—
We prove the assertions as follows.

Proof of (1).
— If
T_{0}
is empty, then clearly
G\circ_{L}H\setminus T
remains connected. Thus
T
is not a cutset since
T
is assumed to be non-empty. Now suppose
T_{0}=V(G)
. Since
L\subsetneq V(G)
, assume
w\in V(G)\setminus L
. Then
G\circ_{L}H\setminus(T\setminus\{w\})=\{w\}\cup(\bigcup_{v\in L}(H_{v}\setminus T_{v}))

The proof is straightforward by using Corollary
3.6
followed by Theorem
3.10
.
∎

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
H

3.1.
A small remark on a conjecture of Bolognini-Macchia-Strazzanti

In
[
BMS22
]
, the authors made the following conjecture:

Conjecture 3.1

(Conjecture 1.1,
[
BMS22
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph. Then,
J_{G}
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
G
is accessible.

The combinatorial data of a graph captures a lot of information about the algebraic properties of the associated binomial edge ideal. For instance, we already know the unmixedness of a binomial edge ideal associated with a graph, which is completely understood by counting the size of every cutset and the number of connected components of the induced subgraph by deleting the cutset. So, the unmixedness of a binomial edge ideal is a combinatorial property, too. With a similar spirit, the main motivation behind the above conjecture is to investigate whether the Cohen-Macaulayness of a binomial edge ideal associated with a graph can also be described purely combinatorially or not.

In
[
BMS22
]
, the authors proved that if
J_{G}
is Cohen-Macaulay, then
G
is accessible. But the other direction is open, in general. In the same article, the authors settled the above conjecture if
G
is either a chordal or a traceable graph.

Let
\mathcal{G}
denote the class of all connected simple graphs. Define the BMS locus as follows:

{\rm BMS}(\mathcal{G}):=\{G\text{ satisfies B-M-S Conjecture }\ref{Conj: BMS}\}.

So the Conjecture
3.1
can be reformulated as — “
Is
{\rm BMS}(\mathcal{G})=\mathcal{G}
?
”

With respect to the standard distance metric
d
in a graph
G
, the diameter of
G
is denoted by
\operatorname{diam}(G)
and is defined as

\operatorname{diam}(G):=\max\{d(u,v):u,v\in V(G)\}.

Define for any positive integer
k
,

\mathcal{D}_{k}(\mathcal{G}):=\{G\in\mathcal{G}:\operatorname{diam}(G)\leq k\},

and further define for
k\geq 2
,

\mathcal{D}_{k}^{\circ}(\mathcal{G}):=\mathcal{D}_{k}(\mathcal{G})\setminus\mathcal{D}_{k-1}(\mathcal{G}).

Then there is a filtration:

\mathcal{D}_{1}(\mathcal{G})\subsetneq\mathcal{D}_{2}(\mathcal{G})\subsetneq\cdots,

such that
\mathcal{G}=\bigcup_{k\geq 1}\mathcal{D}_{k}(\mathcal{G})=\mathcal{D}_{1}(\mathcal{G})\cup\left(\bigcup_{k\geq 2}\mathcal{D}_{k}^{\circ}(\mathcal{G})\right)
.

We will prove here the following:

Theorem 3.13
.

The following assertions hold:

(1)

If
\mathcal{D}_{2}^{\circ}(\mathcal{G})\subseteq{\rm BMS}(\mathcal{G})
, then
{\rm BMS}(\mathcal{G})=\mathcal{G}
.

(2)

If
\mathcal{D}_{3}^{\circ}(\mathcal{G})\subseteq{\rm BMS}(\mathcal{G})
, then
{\rm BMS}(\mathcal{G})=\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

—
Let
H
be a (connected) accessible graph.

Proof of (1).
— Consider
\widetilde{H}:=\{w\}\sqcup H
, consisting of exactly two connected components—an isolated vertex
w
and
H
. Define
G:={\rm cone}(v,\widetilde{H})
for an external vertex
v
. Using
[
BMS22
, Theorem 4.8(3)]
, it follows that
G
is accessible. For any two distinct vertices
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
of
G
, observe that

{\rm dist}_{G}(v_{1},v_{2})=\begin{cases}1,\quad\text{if }v=v_{i}\text{ for some }i\in\{1,2\};\\
1,\quad\text{if }(v_{1},v_{2})\in V(H)\times V(H)\text{ with }\{v_{1},v_{2}\}\in E(H);\\
2,\quad\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

which implies that
\operatorname{diam}(G)=2
. Thus by the hypothesis,
G\in{\rm BMS}(\mathcal{G})
. Therefore,
J_{G}
is Cohen-Macaulay. Using
[
BMS22
, Theorem 4.8(4)]
, it follows that
J_{H}
is Cohen-Macaulay, as desired.

Proof of (2).
— Consider a (connected) complete graph
K_{3}
with three vertices namely
w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}
. Define
G:=K_{3}\circ_{\{w_{1},w_{2}\}}H
. Using Theorem
3.12
, it follows that
G
is accessible. For any two distinct vertices
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
of
G
, observe that

{\rm dist}_{G}(v_{1},v_{2})=\begin{cases}3,\quad\text{if }v_{1},v_{2}\in V(H_{w_{1}})\cup V(H_{w_{2}})\text{ such that}\\
\qquad(v_{1},v_{2})\notin(V(H_{w_{1}})\times V(H_{w_{1}}))\cup(V(H_{w_{2}})\times V(H_{w_{2}}));\\
2,\quad\text{if }(v_{1},v_{2})\in V(H_{w_{1}})\times V(H_{w_{1}})\text{ with }\{v_{1},v_{2}\}\notin E(H_{w_{1}}),\text{ or}\\
\qquad(v_{1},v_{2})\in V(H_{w_{2}})\times V(H_{w_{2}})\text{ with }\{v_{1},v_{2}\}\notin E(H_{w_{2}});\\
2,\quad\text{if }v_{1}=w_{3}\text{ with }v_{2}\in V(H_{w_{1}})\cup V(H_{w_{2}}),\text{ or}\\
\qquad v_{2}=w_{3}\text{ with }v_{1}\in V(H_{w_{1}})\cup V(H_{w_{2}});\\
1,\quad\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

which implies that
\operatorname{diam}(G)=3
. Thus by the hypothesis,
G\in{\rm BMS}(\mathcal{G})
. Therefore,
J_{G}
is Cohen-Macaulay. Once again, using Theorem
3.12
, it follows that
J_{H}
is Cohen-Macaulay, as desired.

This completes the proof.
∎

Therefore, related to Conjecture
3.1
, we are led to ask the following question:

Question 3.14
.

For a simple graph
G
with
\operatorname{diam}(G)=3
, if the corresponding binomial edge ideal
J_{G}
is Cohen-Macaulay, is
G
accessible?

4.
Dimension, Depth and Regularity of Certain Corona Products

In this section, we study the dimension, depth and Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of the binomial edge ideal of the corona product of
G
and
H
where
G
is either a complete graph or a Cohen-Macaulay closed graph (e.g. a path graph). We also prove the existence of extremal Betti numbers.

4.1.
Dimension of corona with complete graph

We will compute the dimension of
G=K_{n}\circ_{\ell}H
for any connected graph
H
and
1\leq\ell\leq n
. In the case of
\ell=n
, the graph
K_{n}\circ_{\ell}H
coincides with the usual corona product
K_{n}\circ H
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
H
be any connected graph and
n\geq 1
any integer. Consider the graph
G:=K_{n}\circ_{\ell}H
with
1\leq\ell\leq n
. Then

(4.1)

\dim(S/J_{G})=n-\ell+1+\ell\dim(S_{H}/J_{H}),

where
|V(H)|=h
.

Proof.

—
We use induction on
\ell
to prove this result. For
\ell=1
, let
v
be the vertex of
K_{n}
along which the copy of
H
is attached to
K_{n}
in
G
. Therefore,

G=K_{n}\circ_{1}H=K_{n}\cup_{v}(v\ast H)=v\ast(K_{n-1}\sqcup H)=v\ast H^{\prime},

where
H^{\prime}=K_{n-1}\sqcup H.
As
H^{\prime}
is disconnected,
\dim(S_{H^{\prime}}/J_{H^{\prime}})\geq|V(H^{\prime})|+2
and hence, it follows from
[
KS19
, Theorem 4.4]
that

\dim(S/J_{G})=\dim(S_{H^{\prime}}/J_{H^{\prime}})=n+\dim(S_{H}/J_{H}),

as desired. This establishes the base case of this induction process.

Choose any
\ell
with
1\leq\ell\leq n
. Now assume the formula in (
4.1
) for
K_{n}\circ_{t}H
for all
t
with
1\leq t<\ell
. Consider the graph
G=K_{n}\circ_{\ell}H
. Let
v\in V(K_{n})
be any vertex such that a copy of
H
is attached with
v
in
G
. Then

G_{v}=K_{n+h}\circ_{\ell-1}H;\quad G_{v}\setminus v=K_{n+h-1}\circ_{\ell-1}H;\quad G\setminus v=H\sqcup(K_{n-1}\circ_{\ell-1}H).

Therefore using the induction hypothesis, we get the following.

(4.2)

\left.\begin{aligned} \dim(S/J_{G_{v}})&=n+h-(\ell-1)+1+(\ell-1)\dim(S_{H}/J_{H});\\
\dim(S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G_{v}\setminus v}))&=n+h-1-(\ell-1)+1+(\ell-1)\dim(S_{H}/J_{H});\\
\dim(S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G\setminus v}))&=\dim(S_{H}/J_{H})+(n-1)-(\ell-1)+1+(\ell-1)\dim(S_{H}/J_{H})\\
&=\dim(S/J_{G_{v}})+\dim(S_{H}/J_{H})-(h+1)\\
&\geq\dim(S/J_{G_{v}}),\quad\text{ as }\dim(S_{H}/J_{H})\geq h+1.\end{aligned}\quad\right\}

Therefore, from the short exact sequence (
2.1
), we have

(4.3)

\max\{\dim(S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G\setminus v})),\dim(S/J_{G_{v}})\}=\max\{\dim(S/J_{G}),\dim(S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G_{v}\setminus v}))\}.

From (
4.2
), it is clear that
\dim(S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G_{v}\setminus v}))=\dim(S/J_{G_{v}})-1
and thus it follows from (
4.3
) that

\displaystyle\dim(S/J_{G})

\displaystyle=\dim(S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G\setminus v});

\displaystyle=n-\ell+1+\ell\dim(S_{H}/J_{H}),

as desired. This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

For any connected graph
H
and any integer
n\geq 1
,

(4.4)

\dim(S/J_{K_{n}\circ H})=n\dim(S_{H}/J_{H})+1,

where
|V(H)|=h
.

Proof.

—
Immediate from Theorem
4.1
.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
H
be any connected graph such that
J_{H}
is unmixed. Then for any integer
n\geq 1
,

(4.5)

\dim(S/J_{K_{n}\circ H})=n+nh+1,

where
|V(H)|=h
.

Proof.

—
Since
J_{H}
is unmixed,
\dim(S_{H}/J_{H})=h+1
. Plug this into (
4.4
) to obtain the desired result.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

The result of Corollary
4.3
was first observed in
[
KSM15
, Corollary 5.2]
.

4.2.
Depth and regularity of corona with complete graph

Our next aim is to compute the depth and regularity of
G=K_{n}\circ_{\ell}H
for any connected graph
H
and
1\leq\ell\leq n
. As noted earlier, in the case of
\ell=n
, the graph
K_{n}\circ_{\ell}H
coincides with the usual corona product
K_{n}\circ H
. But we will see that the depth and regularity formulae for
K_{n}\circ_{\ell}H
with
1\leq\ell<n
are slightly different from those with the case of
\ell=n
.

We first consider the base case when
\ell=1
. If
n=1
, then
G=v*H
, and hence, by virtue of
[
KS20
, Lemma 3.4]
,
0pt(S/J_{G})=0pt(S_{H}/J_{H})
. So we assume that
n\geq 2
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
H
be a connected graph with
|V(H)|=h
and
n\geq 2
be any integer. Consider the graph
G:=K_{n}\circ_{1}H
. Then the following assertions hold.

(1)

0pt(S/J_{G})=n+0pt(S_{H}/J_{H})
.

(2)

\operatorname{reg}(S/J_{G})=\begin{cases}2,\quad&\text{if }H\text{ is complete};\\
1+\operatorname{reg}(S_{H}/J_{H}),&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(3)

Assume that
H
is not a complete graph,
\operatorname{pd}(S_{H}/J_{H})=p_{H}
and
\beta_{p_{H},p_{H}+r_{H}}(S_{H}/J_{H})
is an extremal Betti number of
S_{H}/J_{H}
for
r_{H}\geq 2
. Then
\beta_{p,p+r_{H}}(S/J_{G})
is an extremal Betti number if
n=2
and
\beta_{p,p+r_{H}+1}(S/J_{G})
is an extremal Betti number if
n\geq 3
, where
p=\operatorname{pd}(S/J_{G})=n+p_{H}.

Proof.

—
We will prove the first two statements together.

Proof of (1) and (2).
—
Assume that
|V(H)|=h
. Let
S_{H}
be the polynomial ring over
\mathbf{k}
associated with the graph
H
.

Case 1.

Whenever
H=K_{h}
.

In this case, it follows from
[
EHH11
, Theorem 1.1]
that
G
is a block graph with
0pt(G)=|V(G)|+1=n+h+1=n+0pt(S_{H}/J_{H})
, as desired.

In this case, by
[
HR18
, Theorem 8]
,
\operatorname{reg}(S/J_{G})=\operatorname{iv}(G)+1=2
.

Case 2.

Whenever
H
is not complete.

Let
v
be the vertex of
K_{n}
along which
H
is attached to
K_{n}
in
G
.
Therefore,

G=K_{n}\cup_{v}(v\ast H)=v\ast(K_{n-1}\sqcup H).

From
[
KS20
, Theorem 3.9]
, it follows that
0pt(S/J_{G})=\min\{0pt(K_{n-1}\sqcup H),n+h+1\}
.

Clearly,
0pt(K_{n-1}\sqcup H)=n+0pt(S_{H}/J_{H})\leq n+h+1
, as
0pt(S_{H}/J_{H})\leq h+1
.
Hence, whenever
n\geq 2
,

0pt(S/J_{G})=\min\{n+0pt(S_{H}/J_{H}),n+h+1\}=n+0pt(S_{H}/J_{H}).

Again using
[
SMK18
, Theorem 2.1(a)]
, it immediately follows that

\operatorname{reg}(S/J_{G})=\max\{1+\operatorname{reg}(S_{H}/J_{H}),2\}=1+\operatorname{reg}(S_{H}/J_{H}),

since
H
being non-complete connected graph,
\operatorname{reg}(S_{H}/J_{H})>1
.

Proof of (3).
— Assume that
\operatorname{pd}(S_{H}/J_{H})=p_{H}
. Then by
[
KS20
, Lemma 3.3]
, there exists an integer
r_{H}\geq 2
such that
\beta_{p_{H},p_{H}+r_{H}}(S_{H}/J_{H})
is an extremal Betti number of
S_{H}/J_{H}
. Also, by part (1), we get
0pt(S/J_{G})=n+0pt(S_{H}/J_{H})
and hence, Auslander-Buchsbaum formula, we have
p:=\operatorname{pd}(S/J_{G})=n+p_{H}
. Since
v
is a non-simplicial vertex of
G
, we consider the short exact sequence
2.1
.
Observe that,

G_{v}=K_{n+h};\quad G_{v}\setminus v=K_{n+h-1};\quad G\setminus v=K_{n-1}\sqcup H.

Therefore,
\operatorname{pd}(S/J_{G_{v}})=n+h-1
,
\operatorname{pd}(S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G_{v}\setminus v}))=n+h
and
\operatorname{pd}(S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G\setminus v}))=n+p_{H}=p
. Hence,
\beta_{n+h-1,n+h}(S/J_{G_{v}})
and
\beta_{n+h,n+h+1}(S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G_{v}\setminus v}))
are respective extremal Betti numbers. If
n=2
, then
\beta_{p,p+r_{H}}(S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G\setminus v}))
and if
n\geq 3
, then
\beta_{p,p+r_{H}+1}(S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G\setminus v}))
is an extremal Betti number.
By
[
BN17
, Theorem B]
,
p_{H}\geq h-1
. If
p_{H}=h-1
, then considering the long exact sequence of
\operatorname{Tor}
(
2.2
) in homological degree
p
, we have

0\to\operatorname{Tor}^{S}_{p+1}\left(\frac{S}{(x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G_{v}\setminus v}},\mathbf{k}\right)_{p+j}\to\operatorname{Tor}_{p}^{S}\left(\frac{S}{J_{G}},\mathbf{k}\right)_{p+j}\to\operatorname{Tor}_{p}^{S}\left(\frac{S}{(x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G\setminus v}},\mathbf{k}\right)_{p+j}\to\cdots

Also if
p_{H}\geq h
, then we have the following isomorphism:

\operatorname{Tor}^{S}_{p}\left(\frac{S}{J_{G}},\mathbf{k}\right)_{p+j}\simeq\operatorname{Tor}^{S}_{p}\left(\frac{S}{(x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G\setminus v}},\mathbf{k}\right)_{p+j}\text{ for }j\geq 2.

This implies that if
n=2
, then
\beta_{p,p+r_{H}}(S/J_{G})\neq 0
and
\beta_{p,p+j}(S/J_{G})=0
for all
j\geq r_{H}+1
. Also, if
n\geq 3
, then
\beta_{p,p+r_{H}+1}(S/J_{G})\neq 0
and
\beta_{p,p+j}(S/J_{G})=0
for all
j\geq r_{H}+2
. Hence, either
\beta_{p,p+r_{H}}(S/J_{G})
or
\beta_{p,p+r_{H}+1}(S/J_{G})
is an extremal Betti number of
S/J_{G}
.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

For any connected graph
H
and any integer
n\geq 3
, consider the graphs
G:=K_{n}\circ_{\ell}H
for some
1\leq\ell<n
. Then the following assertions hold.

(1)

0pt(S/J_{G})=n-\ell+1+\ell\cdot 0pt(S_{H}/J_{H})
.

(2)

\operatorname{reg}(S/J_{G})=1+\ell\cdot\operatorname{reg}(S/J_{H})
.

(3)

Assume that
H
is not a complete graph,
\operatorname{pd}(S_{H}/J_{H})=p_{H}
and
\beta_{p_{H},p_{H}+r_{H}}(S_{H}/J_{H})
is an extremal Betti number of
S_{H}/J_{H}
for
r_{H}\geq 2
. Then
\beta_{p,p+\ell r_{H}}(S/J_{G})
is an extremal Betti number if
n=2
and
\beta_{p,p+\ell r_{H}+1}(S/J_{G})
is an extremal Betti number if
n\geq 3
, where
p=\operatorname{pd}(S/J_{G})=n+\ell-1+\ell p_{H}.

Proof.

—
Assume that
|V(H)|=h
.

Case 1.

Whenever
H=K_{h}
.

In this case,
G
is a block graph, and thus it follows from
[
EHH11
, Theorem 1.1]
and
[
HR18
, Theorem 8]

0pt(S/J_{G})=|V(K_{n}\circ_{\ell}K_{h})|+1=n+\ell h+1=n-\ell+1+\ell 0pt(S_{K_{h}}/J_{K_{h}})

and
\operatorname{reg}(S/J_{G})=\operatorname{iv}(G)+1=1+\ell
, respectively.

Case 2.

Whenever
H
is non-complete.

Let
\operatorname{pd}(S_{H}/J_{H})=p_{H}
. Then by Auslander-Buchsbaum formula,
0pt(S_{H}/J_{H})=2h-p_{H}
. To prove the assertion we show that
0pt(S/J_{G})\geq n-\ell+1+\ell 0pt(S_{H}/J_{H})
,
\operatorname{reg}(S/J_{G})=1+\ell\operatorname{reg}(S_{H}/J_{H})
. To prove
0pt(S/J_{G})\leq n-\ell+1+\ell 0pt(S_{H}/J_{H})
, we show that if
n=2
, then
\beta_{p,p+\ell r_{H}}(S/J_{G})
is an extremal Betti number and if
n\geq 3
, then
\beta_{p,p+\ell r_{H}+1}(S/J_{G})
is an extremal Betti number, where
p:=n+\ell-1+\ell p_{H}
. We proceed by induction on
\ell
. The base case
\ell=1
for the induction follows from Proposition
4.5
. Assume that
\ell\geq 2
.

Let
v
be one of the vertices along which
H
is attached to
K_{n}
in
G
.
Observe that,

G_{v}=K_{n+h}\circ_{(\ell-1)}H;\quad G_{v}\setminus v=K_{n+h-1}\circ_{(\ell-1)}H;\quad G\setminus v=H\sqcup(K_{n-1}\circ_{(\ell-1)}H).

Using the induction hypothesis, we get the following.

(4.6)

\left.\begin{aligned} 0pt(S/J_{G_{v}})&=n+h-\ell+2+(\ell-1)0pt(S_{H}/J_{H});\\
0pt(S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G_{v}\setminus v}))&=n+h-\ell+1+(\ell-1)0pt(S_{H}/J_{H});\\
0pt(S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G\setminus v}))&=n-\ell+1+\ell 0pt(S_{H}/J_{H});\end{aligned}\quad\right\}

and

(4.7)

\left.\begin{aligned} \operatorname{reg}(S/J_{G_{v}})&=1+(\ell-1)\operatorname{reg}(S_{H}/J_{H});\\
\operatorname{reg}(S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G_{v}\setminus v}))&=1+(\ell-1)\operatorname{reg}(S_{H}/J_{H});\\
\operatorname{reg}(S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G\setminus v}))&=1+\ell\operatorname{reg}(S_{H}/J_{H}).\end{aligned}\quad\right\}

By Auslander-Buchsbaum formula, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{pd}(S/J_{G_{v}})

\displaystyle=n+h-(\ell-1)-1+(\ell-1)p_{H}=p+h-1-p_{H};

\displaystyle\operatorname{pd}(S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G_{v}\setminus v}))

\displaystyle=p+h-2-p_{H};

\displaystyle\operatorname{pd}(S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G\setminus v}))

\displaystyle=p_{H}+n-1+\ell-1-1+(\ell-1)p_{H}=p-2.

Therefore

\displaystyle 0pt(S/J_{G_{v}}\oplus S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G\setminus v}))

\displaystyle=\min\{0pt(S/J_{G_{v}}),0pt(S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G\setminus v}))\}

\displaystyle=n-\ell+1+\ell 0pt(S_{H}/J_{H}),\quad(\text{since }0pt(H)\leq h+1);

\displaystyle 0pt(S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G_{v}\setminus v}))

\displaystyle=n+h-\ell+1+(\ell-1)0pt(S_{H}/J_{H});

and

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}(S/J_{G_{v}}\oplus S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G\setminus v}))

\displaystyle=\max\{\operatorname{reg}(S/J_{G_{v}}),\operatorname{reg}(S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G\setminus v}))\}

\displaystyle=1+\ell\operatorname{reg}(S_{H}/J_{H});

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}(S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G_{v}\setminus v}))

\displaystyle=1+(\ell-1)\operatorname{reg}(S_{H}/J_{H}).

Using Depth Lemma(a) and Regularity Lemma (c) on the short exact sequence (
2.1
), it immediately follows that

0pt(S/J_{G})\geq n-\ell+1+\ell 0pt(S_{H}/J_{H})\text{ and }\operatorname{reg}(S/J_{G})=1+\ell\operatorname{reg}(S_{H}/J_{H}).

Since
n+h-1\geq 3
, by induction hypothesis,

\beta_{p+h-1-p_{H},p+h-1-p_{H}+(\ell-1)r_{H}+1}(S/J_{G_{v}})\text{ and }\beta_{p+h-p_{H},p+h-p_{H}+(\ell-1)r_{H}+1}(S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G_{v}\setminus v}))

are respective extremal Betti numbers. Also, if
n=2
, then
\beta_{p,p+\ell r_{H}}(S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G\setminus v}))
and if
n\geq 3
, then
\beta_{p,p+\ell r_{H}+1}(S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G_{v}\setminus v}))
are extremal Betti numbers.
By
[
BN17
, Theorem B]
,
p_{H}\geq h-1
. If
p_{H}=h-1

Theorem 4.7
.

For any connected graph

and any integer

(2)

n\cdot\operatorname{reg}(S_{H}/J_{H}),&\text{whenever }H\text{ is non-complete}.\end{cases}

(3)

Assume that

\operatorname{pd}(S_{H}/J_{H})=p_{H}

Proof.

—
Assume that
|V(H)|=h
.

Case 1.

Whenever

.

In this case,

is a block graph and it follows from

\displaystyle 0pt(S/J_{G})=|V(K_{n}\circ K_{h})|+1=n+nh+1=1+n0pt(S_{K_{h}}/J_{K_{h}}).

Also, by

, Theorem 8]
,
\operatorname{reg}(S/J_{G})=\operatorname{iv}(G)+1=n+1.

Case 2.

Whenever

is non-complete.

In this case, in order to prove (1), (2) and (3) together, we apply induction on

. For the base case

of the induction, let

. Therefore the assertion (1), (2) and (3) follows from
[

, Theorem 3.4]
,
[

Assume that

Clearly,

and thus by applying Theorem
4.6
and the induction hypothesis, we get the following.

(4.8)

\left.\begin{aligned} 0pt(S/J_{G_{v}})&=h+2+(n-1)0pt(S_{H}/J_{H});\\

0pt(S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G\setminus v}))&=n0pt(S_{H}/J_{H});\end{aligned}\quad\right\}

and

(4.9)

\left.\begin{aligned} \operatorname{reg}(S/J_{G_{v}})&=1+(n-1)\operatorname{reg}(S_{H}/J_{H});\\
\operatorname{reg}(S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G_{v}\setminus v}))&=1+(n-1)\operatorname{reg}(S_{H}/J_{H});\\
\operatorname{reg}(S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G\setminus v}))&=n\operatorname{reg}(S_{H}/J_{H}).\end{aligned}\quad\right\}

\displaystyle 0pt(S/J_{G_{v}}\oplus S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G\setminus v}))

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}(S/J_{G_{v}}\oplus S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G\setminus v}))

\displaystyle\operatorname{pd}(S/J_{G_{v}})=2(n+nh)-(h+2+(n-1)(2h-p_{H}))

\displaystyle\operatorname{pd}(S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G_{v}\setminus v}))

\displaystyle\operatorname{pd}(S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G\setminus v}))

\beta_{p+h-1-p_{H},p+h-1-p_{H}+nr_{H}+1}(S/((x_{v},y_{v})+J_{G_{v}\setminus v}))

As

(

) in homological degree

This completes the proof.
∎

Definition 4.8
.

For a commutative Noetherian ring

and a finitely generated
R
-module

of

, is defined by
\dim(M)-0pt(M).
In particular, if

, then

is called

.

Corollary 4.9

Let

0&\text{if \ }\ell=n\text{ and }H\text{ is complete}.\end{cases}

Proof.

The proof is immediate following Theorems
4.1
,
4.6
and
4.7
.
∎

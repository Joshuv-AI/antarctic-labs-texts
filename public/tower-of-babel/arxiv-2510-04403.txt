Quasi-alternating surgeries on asymmetric L–space knots in the census Thanks: The author has been partially supported by JSPS KAKENHI Grant Number JP25K07004.
Masakazu Teragaito

Abstract
. In the SnapPy census, there are 9 9 asymmetric L–space knots. It is known that each of them admits exactly two quasi-alternating surgeries with the aid of a computer. The purpose of this article is to confirm these surgeries by the Montesinos trick.

Introduction

A knot
K
in the
3
–sphere is called an
L–space knot
if
K
admits a non-trivial Dehn surgery yielding an L–space.
If the symmetry group of the knot complement is trivial, then a knot is said to be
asymmetric
.
The first examples of asymmetric L–space knots were found by Baker and Luecke
[
4
]
.
Among them, the simplest one is the closure of a positive
12
–strand braid of length
249
, which has genus
119
,
as they mentioned in
[
4
, Example 1.7 and Figure 1]
.

Among
1267
hyperbolic knots in the SnapPy census, Dunfield
[
6
,
7
]
gave
630
L–space knots, leaving two as undetermined.
Later, these two are confirmed as L–space knots by
[
3
]
.
Thus there are exactly
632
hyperbolic L–space knots in the census at the moment.
In
[
1
]
, the computation of isometry groups by SnapPy
[
5
]
reveals that
there are exactly
9
asymmetric L–space knots among the census knots:

\begin{split}&t12533,t12681,o9\_38928,o9\_39162,o9\_40363,\\
&o9\_40487,o9\_40504,o9\_40582,o9\_42675.\end{split}

The simplest
t12533
is the closure of a
4
–braid of length
27

12
.
The remaining

Baker, Kegel and McCoy

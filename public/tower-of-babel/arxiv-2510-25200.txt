On Quasi-Modular Pseudometric Spaces and Asymmetric Uniformities
Philani Rodney Majozi

Abstract
We introduce and analyze quasi-modular pseudometric spaces : structures generated by left/right (generally non-symmetric) Luxemburg-type gauges coming from one-sided modulars. This relaxes symmetry in Chistyakov’s modular metric setting [ 1 , 2 ] and places it within the context of quasi-metrics and quasi-uniform spaces [ 10 , 12 , 15 ] . We develop the basic theory: induced (quasi-)topologies and quasi-uniformities; unit-ball characterizations; and Cauchy notions tailored to asymmetry. We give criteria for completeness, precompactness, and compactness, and prove bicompletion results. A symmetrization principle relates the asymmetric gauges to a canonical modular space; under mild Δ 2 / ∇ 2 \Delta_{2}/\nabla_{2} -type hypotheses, symmetrized and bicomplete constructions agree. Categorically, we treat these spaces as Lawvere-enriched objects and describe symmetrization as a Kan-style construction in the enriched setting [ 9 , 11 ] ; the induced entourages admit an Isbell-style uniform interpretation [ 8 ] . Applications cover weighted and generalized Orlicz models (including variable-exponent and double-phase growth), asymmetric function spaces built from positive/negative parts, and directed-graph energies with time-dependent costs. These examples yield modular Sobolev-type estimates and demonstrate fixed-point and stability principles for directional media. Overall, this setting unifies analytic, topological, and categorical perspectives for non-symmetric variational problems and provides practical criteria for compactness, duality, and well-posedness in asymmetric settings.

Introduction

Modular metric spaces, as initiated by Chistyakov
[
1
,
2
]
, have
proved to be a flexible bridge between classical metric geometry and modular
function-space theory. In the modular setting one measures displacement by a

scale–dependent
quantity
w_{\lambda}(x,y)
instead of a single distance

d(x,y)
; the parameter
\lambda>0
encodes resolution or tolerance and allows

distances of Orlicz/Musielak–Orlicz analysis. Recent work of
Mushaandja–Olela–Otafudu
[
13

clarified the induced

modular topology

The present paper develops the

systematically relaxing the symmetry requirement
w_{\lambda}(x,y)=w_{\lambda}(y,x)

Our guiding principle is that “asymmetry is geometry with direction”: directed
graphs, cost-to-go functions in control, preorder/causal structures, and
nonreversible diffusion all carry canonical one–sided notions of nearness. On
the topological side, this belongs naturally with
quasi-metric
and

quasi-uniform
spaces
[
10
,
12
,
15
]
and
the rich body of asymmetric topology summarized by Fletcher and Lindgren

[
4

[
11

models (quasi-)metrics as categories enriched over the
closed monoidal poset

, providing a
concise calculus for completions, Kan extensions, and symmetrizations; see
also Kelly’s monograph
[
9

Motivation

There are at least three reasons to allow asymmetry in the modular parameter:

(M1)

In many applications (one–way transport,
causal precedence, cost accumulation) the “effort” to go from
x
to
y
differs
from that of returning. A single symmetric modular cannot express such effects.

(M2)

Quasi-uniformities provide the intrinsic
uniform core of asymmetric spaces

,
15
]

scale–dependent modular should generate left/right quasi-uniformities

\mathcal{V}^{\pm}(w)

\tau^{\pm}(w)

(M3)

In

[
9
,
11
]
, a quasi-modular space is a small

\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
–category equipped with a family of hom-objects varying with

\lambda
. This enables compact proofs of completion/symmetrization results via
Kan extensions and Cauchy density.

2.1
Modulars, pseudomodulars, and convexity

Let
X
be a nonempty set. A mapping

w:(0,\infty)\times X\times X\longrightarrow[0,\infty],\qquad(\lambda,x,y)\mapsto w_{\lambda}(x,y),

is a
(metric) modular
on
X
if, for all
\lambda,\mu>0
and
x,y,z\in X
:

(M1)

x=y
iff
w_{\lambda}(x,y)=0
for all
\lambda>0

(separation)
;

(M2)

w_{\lambda}(x,y)=w_{\lambda}(y,x)

(symmetry)
;

(M3)

w_{\lambda+\mu}(x,y)\leq w_{\lambda}(x,z)+w_{\mu}(z,y)

(modular triangle)
.

If
(M1)
is relaxed to
w_{\lambda}(x,x)=0
(all
x,\lambda
), we speak of a

pseudomodular
. A modular (or pseudomodular) is called
convex
if

w_{\lambda+\mu}(x,y)\leq\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+\mu}w_{\lambda}(x,y)+\frac{\mu}{\lambda+\mu}w_{\mu}(x,y)\qquad(\lambda,\mu>0).

(1)

Equivalently,
\lambda\mapsto\lambda\,w_{\lambda}(x,y)
is nonincreasing

[
2
, Ch. 1]
. In particular, for
0<\lambda\leq\mu
,

w_{\mu}(x,y)\leq\frac{\mu}{\lambda}w_{\lambda}(x,y)\leq w_{\lambda}(x,y),

(2)

and whenever
w^{x,y}\not\equiv 0
one has

\lim_{\lambda\downarrow 0}w_{\lambda}(x,y)=\infty
, while

\lim_{\lambda\uparrow\infty}w_{\lambda}(x,y)=0
if
w^{x,y}\not\equiv\infty

[
2
, 1.2.3]
.

Right/left regularizations.

Define

(w_{+0})_{\lambda}(x,y):=\lim_{\mu\downarrow\lambda}w_{\mu}(x,y),\qquad(w_{-0})_{\lambda}(x,y):=\lim_{\mu\uparrow\lambda}w_{\mu}(x,y).

If
w
is a (pseudo)modular (strict/convex, resp.), then
w_{\pm 0}
is again a
(pseudo)modular with the same additional properties; moreover
w_{+0}
is
right–continuous and
w_{-0}
is left–continuous in
\lambda

[
2
, 1.2.5]
. In what follows we tacitly replace
w
by a
right–continuous representative
w_{+0}
when convenient.

2.2
From classical modulars and metrics to metric modulars

Two basic templates produce metric modulars:

Example 2.1

(
[
2
, 1.3.1–1.3.2]
)
.

Let
(X,d)
be a (pseudo)metric space and
g:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
.
Then
w_{\lambda}(x,y):=g(\lambda)\,d(x,y)
is a (pseudo)modular iff
g
is
nonincreasing; it is strict iff
g(\lambda)\neq 0
for all
\lambda>0
, and convex
iff
\lambda\mapsto\lambda g(\lambda)
is nonincreasing. The canonical choice

g(\lambda)=\lambda^{-1}
yields
w_{\lambda}=d/\lambda
.

Example 2.2

(
[
2
, 1.3.4–1.3.5]
)
.

Let
X
be a real linear space and
\rho:X\to[0,\infty]
a (convex) classical
modular. Then

w_{\lambda}(x,y):=\rho\!\left(\frac{x-y}{\lambda}\right)

is a (convex) metric modular. Conversely, if
w
is translation invariant and
positively homogeneous in the sense of
[
2
, 1.3.5]
, then

w_{\lambda}(x,y)=\rho((x-y)/\lambda)
with
\rho(\cdot)=w_{1}(\cdot,0)
.

2.3
The modular topology and uniformity (symmetric case)

Given a (pseudo)modular
w
on
X
, set

B_{w}(x,\lambda,\varepsilon):=\{\,y\in X:\ w_{\lambda}(x,y)<\varepsilon\,\}\qquad(\lambda,\varepsilon>0).

These form a neighborhood base of a topology
\tau(w)
on
X
(Hausdorff iff
w

is strict) and generate a uniformity via entourages

V(\lambda,\varepsilon):=\{(x,y):w_{\lambda}(x,y)<\varepsilon\}
; the induced
uniform topology coincides with
\tau(w)
. In the convex case
\tau(w)
is
metrizable by a Luxemburg-type distance (see below)

[
2
, Chs. 2–4]
; compare
[
8
]
.

2.4
Luxemburg-type gauges and first consequences

Two compressions of the scale family are useful throughout.

Definition 2.3
.

For
c\in(0,\infty)
define

d_{w}^{(c)}(x,y):=\inf\{\,\lambda>0:\ w_{\lambda}(x,y)\leq c\,\}.

If
w
is a
pseudomodular
, then
d_{w}^{(c)}
is a pseudometric for each
fixed
c>0
, and the associated topologies are equivalent for different
c
’s.
Moreover, if
w
is convex,
d_{w}^{(1)}
metrizes
\tau(w)
(and is symmetric by

(M2)
); see e.g.
[
2
, 2.2.3]
. In general, the family

\{(x,y)\mapsto w_{\lambda}(x,y)\}_{\lambda>0}
itself supplies a calibrated
(semi)metric family whose supremum metric is compatible with
\tau(w)
.

2.5
Quasi-modular pseudometrics: the asymmetric relaxation

Our target objects arise by removing symmetry
(M2)
and keeping the modular
triangle and scale monotonicity/regularity.

Definition 2.4
.

A family
w=\{w_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda>0}
with
w_{\lambda}:X\times X\to[0,\infty]

is a
quasi-modular pseudometric
if:

(QM1)

w_{\lambda}(x,x)=0
for all
x\in X
,
\lambda>0
;

(QM2)

w_{\lambda+\mu}(x,z)\leq w_{\lambda}(x,y)+w_{\mu}(y,z)
for all
x,y,z
and
\lambda,\mu>0
;

(QM3)

\lambda\mapsto w_{\lambda}(x,y)
is right–continuous and nonincreasing (after replacing
w
by its right regularization, if needed).

If, additionally,
w_{\lambda}(x,y)=0=w_{\lambda}(y,x)
for all
\lambda>0
implies

x=y
, we say
w
is
T_{0}
-separating
and drop “pseudo”.

Left/right basic balls and first countability.

Define

B^{+}_{w}(x;\lambda,\varepsilon):=\{y:\ w_{\lambda}(x,y)<\varepsilon\},\qquad B^{-}_{w}(x;\lambda,\varepsilon):=\{y:\ w_{\lambda}(y,x)<\varepsilon\}.

Right–continuity in
\lambda
and density of rationals yield countable local
bases at each point for the left and right topologies
\tau^{+}(w)
,
\tau^{-}(w)
.
These will match the quasi-uniform topologies generated by the entourages

V^{+}(\lambda,\varepsilon)=\{(x,y):w_{\lambda}(x,y)<\varepsilon\}
and

V^{-}(\lambda,\varepsilon)=\{(x,y):w_{\lambda}(y,x)<\varepsilon\}
(see
Section
3
); compare
[
10
,
12
]
.

Symmetrization

The
symmetrized
family

w^{\mathrm{sym}}_{\lambda}(x,y):=\max\{w_{\lambda}(x,y),\,w_{\lambda}(y,x)\}

is a (symmetric) pseudomodular whenever
w
is quasi-modular; this will control
relative compactness and total boundedness in Section
5
.

Luxemburg quasi-distances

Define the (generally asymmetric) Luxemburg gauge

d_{w}(x,y):=\inf\{\,\lambda>0:\ w_{\lambda}(x,y)\leq 1\,\}.

Then
d_{w}
is a quasi-pseudometric:

d_{w}(x,x)=0
and
d_{w}(x,z)\leq d_{w}(x,y)+d_{w}(y,z)
, by (QM2) and right–continuity.
Its symmetrization
d_{w}^{\!*}(x,y):=\max\{d_{w}(x,y),d_{w}(y,x)\}
is a pseudometric
compatible with
w^{\mathrm{sym}}
. Under mild calibration hypotheses, the left
(resp. right) topology of
d_{w}
is coarser than
\tau^{+}(w)
(resp.
\tau^{-}(w)
)
and coincides with it when the threshold
1
is stable across scales, as in the
convex case; see the symmetric discussion in
[
2
, Chs. 2–4]
.

2.6
Elementary constructions and examples

Example 2.5
.

Let
\rho
be a quasi-pseudometric on
X
(not necessarily symmetric). Then

w_{\lambda}(x,y):=\min\{\rho(x,y),\,\lambda\}

satisfies (QM1)–(QM3). This provides a canonical way to view any quasi-metric
as a quasi-modular pseudometric.

Example 2.6
.

Let
V
be a real vector space with an asymmetric sublinear gauge

p:V\to[0,\infty)
(
p(u+v)\leq p(u)+p(v)
,
p(\alpha v)=\alpha p(v)
for
\alpha\geq 0
; not
necessarily
p(v)=p(-v)
). Put
\rho(x,y):=p(y-x)
and set

w_{\lambda}(x,y):=\min\{\rho(x,y),\lambda\}
. Then
w
is quasi-modular, and is
modular iff
p
is symmetric.

Example 2.7
.

Let
G=(X,E)
be a directed graph with nonnegative edge weights
c
. Let

\rho(x,y)
be the shortest directed path cost (possibly
\infty
).
Then
w_{\lambda}(x,y):=\min\{\rho(x,y),\lambda\}
is a quasi-modular; asymmetry
encodes irreversibility of the dynamics.

Example 2.8
.

On a measure space
(\Omega,\mu)
, fix an increasing convex
\Phi
and define

\rho(f,g):=\int_{\Omega}\Phi\big((f-g)_{+}\big)\,d\mu
. Then

w_{\lambda}(f,g):=\min\{\rho(f,g),\lambda\}
is quasi-modular; replacing

(\cdot)_{+}
by
|\cdot|
recovers the symmetric classical case

[
14
,
16
]
.

2.7
Why asymmetry? Motivation

In many settings the “effort” to go from
x
to
y
differs from the return
effort (transport with drift, causal/temporal precedence, cost-to-go in control
or dynamic programming). The scale parameter
\lambda
allows one to interpolate
between coarse and fine resolutions, while the lack of symmetry places the
construction naturally within the theory of quasi-uniform and quasi-metric
spaces
[
10
,
12
]
. The enriched viewpoint over

(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},+,0,\Rightarrow)

[
9
,
11
]
provides a
concise setting for functorial constructions (opposite, symmetrization,
products), Kan extensions, and Cauchy/bicompletion procedures that we exploit in
Sections
4
and
5
.

3
Asymmetric Modular Topologies

Throughout let
X
be a set and
w:X\times X\times(0,\infty)\to[0,1)
be a

(quasi-)modular gauge
in the sense that for some (fixed) continuous

t
-conorm
\oplus
on
[0,1]
:

(W1)

w(x,y,t)=0\iff x=y
, and
w(x,y,t)<1
for all
t>0
;

(W2)

w(x,y,t)=w(y,x,t)
is
not
required (asymmetry allowed);

(W3)

w(x,z,t+s)\ \leq\ w(x,y,t)\ \oplus\ w(y,z,s)
(triangle);

(W4)

for each
x,y
, the map
t\mapsto w(x,y,t)
is continuous and
nonincreasing.

This covers the RGV–fuzzy metrics (via
\oplus
) and their classical
specialization (take
\oplus=\max
and
w(x,y,t)=d(x,y)/(t+d(x,y))
);
see
[
4
,
17
]
. We work in the (quasi-)uniform language
of entourages, following
[
15
, Chap. XII.4]
and the survey

[
10
]
.

3.1
Forward/backward quasi-uniformities and bitopologies

Definition 3.1
.

For
r\in(0,1)
and
t>0
set

E_{r,t}^{+}\ :=\ \{(x,y)\in X\times X:\ w(x,y,t)<r\},\qquad E_{r,t}^{-}\ :=\ \{(x,y):\ w(y,x,t)<r\}=(E_{r,t}^{+})^{-1}.

Let
\mathcal{V}^{+}(w)
(resp.
\mathcal{V}^{-}(w)
) be the family of subsets of

X\times X
containing some
E_{r,t}^{+}
(resp.
E_{r,t}^{-}
).

Theorem 3.2
.

For a (quasi-)modular gauge
w
as above, the families

\mathcal{V}^{+}(w)=\{\,V\subseteq X\times X:\exists r\in(0,1),\,t>0\text{ with }E_{r,t}^{+}\subseteq V\,\}

and
\mathcal{V}^{-}(w)
defined dually are quasiuniformities. Moreover, for every
basic entourage
E_{r,t}^{\pm}
there exist
r^{\prime}\in(0,r)
and
t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}>0
with

E_{r^{\prime},t^{\prime}}^{\pm}\circ E_{r^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}}^{\pm}\ \subseteq\ E_{r,t}^{\pm}.

Proof.

We give the argument for
\mathcal{V}^{+}(w)
; the proof for
\mathcal{V}^{-}(w)

is obtained by swapping the ordered pairs.

(QN1) Nonemptiness and diagonal.

Each
E_{r,t}^{+}
is nonempty. For any
t>0
and
r\in(0,1)
, if
x\in X
then

w(x,x,t)=0<r
, hence
(x,x)\in E_{r,t}^{+}
; thus
\Delta_{X}\subseteq E_{r,t}^{+}
.

(QN2) Upward closure.

By definition,
\mathcal{V}^{+}(w)
consists of all supersets of basic entourages

E_{r,t}^{+}
, so it is upward closed.

(QN3) Finite intersections.

Fix
r_{1},r_{2}\in(0,1)
and
t_{1},t_{2}>0
. Set

r^{\prime}:=\min\{r_{1},r_{2}\},\qquad t^{\prime}:=\min\{t_{1},t_{2}\}.

Let
(x,y)\in E_{r^{\prime},t^{\prime}}^{+}
. Since
t^{\prime}\leq t_{i}
and
t\mapsto w(x,y,t)
is
nonincreasing,
w(x,y,t_{i})\leq w(x,y,t^{\prime})<r^{\prime}\leq r_{i}
for
i=1,2
.
Thus
E_{r^{\prime},t^{\prime}}^{+}\subseteq E_{r_{1},t_{1}}^{+}\cap E_{r_{2},t_{2}}^{+}
, proving stability
under finite meets at the level of the base, hence for
\mathcal{V}^{+}(w)
.

(QN4) Small composites.

Fix
r\in(0,1)
and
t>0
. Since
\oplus
is a continuous
t
-conorm with
neutral element
0
and is increasing in each variable, there exists

r^{\prime}\in(0,r)
such that
r^{\prime}\oplus r^{\prime}<r
.
Choose any
t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}>0
with
t^{\prime}+t^{\prime\prime}\geq t
; for instance, take
t^{\prime}=t^{\prime\prime}=t
.

We claim that

E_{r^{\prime},t^{\prime}}^{+}\circ E_{r^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}}^{+}\ \subseteq\ E_{r,t}^{+}.

Indeed, if
(x,z)\in E_{r^{\prime},t^{\prime}}^{+}\circ E_{r^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}}^{+}
then there exists
y\in X

with
w(x,y,t^{\prime})<r^{\prime}
and
w(y,z,t^{\prime\prime})<r^{\prime}
. By the triangle axiom (W3),

w(x,z,t^{\prime}+t^{\prime\prime})\ \leq\ w(x,y,t^{\prime})\ \oplus\ w(y,z,t^{\prime\prime})\ <\ r^{\prime}\oplus r^{\prime}\ <\ r.

Since
t\leq t^{\prime}+t^{\prime\prime}
and
t\mapsto w(x,z,t)
is nonincreasing, we obtain

w(x,z,t)\leq w(x,z,t^{\prime}+t^{\prime\prime})<r
, hence
(x,z)\in E_{r,t}^{+}
as required.

Properties (QN1)–(QN3) and the small-composite property (QN4) are precisely the
Weil–Nachbin axioms for a quasi-uniformity. Therefore
\mathcal{V}^{+}(w)
is a
quasi-uniformity with the stated basic-composition refinement. The argument for

\mathcal{V}^{-}(w)
is identical, replacing
w(x,y,\cdot)
by
w(y,x,\cdot)
.
∎

Definition 3.3
.

The topologies induced by
\mathcal{V}^{\pm}(w)
are

\displaystyle\tau^{+}(w)

\displaystyle=\{\,M\subseteq X:\ \forall x\in M\ \exists\,r,t\ (E_{r,t}^{+}[x]\subseteq M)\,\},

\displaystyle\tau^{-}(w)

equivalently generated by the basic balls

and

.

Thus

is a canonical bitopological space attached to

[

,

]
.

3.2
Symmetrization and the join topology

Definition 3.4
.

Define
w^{\mathrm{sym}}(x,y,t):=w(x,y,t)\oplus w(y,x,t)
.
Let
\mathcal{U}(w^{\mathrm{sym}})
be the uniformity generated by the
entourages
E^{\mathrm{sym}}_{r,t}:=\{(x,y):w^{\mathrm{sym}}(x,y,t)<r\}=E_{r,t}^{+}\cap E_{r,t}^{-}
.
Write
\tau(w^{\mathrm{sym}})
for its topology and

B(x;r,t):=B^{+}(x;r,t)\cap B^{-}(x;r,t)
for its basic balls.

Proposition 3.5
.

\tau(w^{\mathrm{sym}})=\tau^{+}(w)\vee\tau^{-}(w)
, the least topology finer than
both
\tau^{+}(w)
and
\tau^{-}(w)
. If
w
is symmetric then

\tau^{+}(w)=\tau^{-}(w)=\tau(w^{\mathrm{sym}})

3.3
Continuity, completeness, and compactness

Definition 3.6
.

A map
f:(X,w)\to(Y,v)
is
forward (resp. backward) uniformly continuous if

\forall r,t\ \exists r^{\prime},t^{\prime}:\ (x,y)\in E_{r^{\prime},t^{\prime}}^{+,w}\Rightarrow(f(x),f(y))\in E_{r,t}^{+,v}\quad(\text{resp.\ with }-),

and
bi-uniformly continuous
if it is both.

Lemma 3.7
.

A map
f:X\to Y
is bi
-
uniformly continuous (i.e. forward and backward
uniformly continuous with respect to
w
and
v
) if and only if it is
uniformly continuous as a map

f:\bigl(X,\mathcal{U}(w^{\mathrm{sym}})\bigr)\longrightarrow\bigl(Y,\mathcal{U}(v^{\mathrm{sym}})\bigr).

Proof.

Recall from Definition
3.4
that the basic symmetric
entourages are

E^{\mathrm{sym},w}_{r,t}=E^{+,w}_{r,t}\cap E^{-,w}_{r,t},\qquad E^{\mathrm{sym},v}_{r,t}=E^{+,v}_{r,t}\cap E^{-,v}_{r,t}\quad(r\in(0,1),\,t>0).

(
\Rightarrow
).

Assume
f
is forward and backward uniformly continuous. Fix

r\in(0,1)
and
t>0
. By forward uniform continuity there exist

r_{1}\in(0,1)
,
t_{1}>0
with

(x,y)\in E^{+,w}_{r_{1},t_{1}}\ \Rightarrow\ (f(x),f(y))\in E^{+,v}_{r,t}.

By backward uniform continuity there exist
r_{2}\in(0,1)
,
t_{2}>0
with

(x,y)\in E^{-,w}_{r_{2},t_{2}}\ \Rightarrow\ (f(x),f(y))\in E^{-,v}_{r,t}.

Let
r^{\prime}:=\min\{r_{1},r_{2}\}
and
t^{\prime}:=\min\{t_{1},t_{2}\}
.
Then
E^{\mathrm{sym},w}_{r^{\prime},t^{\prime}}\subseteq E^{+,w}_{r_{1},t_{1}}\cap E^{-,w}_{r_{2},t_{2}}
,
hence for
(x,y)\in E^{\mathrm{sym},w}_{r^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
we have simultaneously

(f(x),f(y))\in E^{+,v}_{r,t}
and
(f(x),f(y))\in E^{-,v}_{r,t}
, i.e.

(f(x),f(y))\in E^{\mathrm{sym},v}_{r,t}
.
Thus

E^{\mathrm{sym},w}_{r^{\prime},t^{\prime}}\ \subseteq\ (f\times f)^{-1}\!\bigl(E^{\mathrm{sym},v}_{r,t}\bigr),

which is the basic entourage criterion for uniform continuity between the
symmetrized uniformities.

(
\Leftarrow
).

Assume
f
is uniformly continuous

(X,\mathcal{U}(w^{\mathrm{sym}}))\to(Y,\mathcal{U}(v^{\mathrm{sym}}))
.
Fix
r\in(0,1)
,
t>0
. Then there exist
r^{\prime}\in(0,1)
,
t^{\prime}>0
such that

E^{\mathrm{sym},w}_{r^{\prime},t^{\prime}}\ \subseteq\ (f\times f)^{-1}\!\bigl(E^{\mathrm{sym},v}_{r,t}\bigr).

Since
E^{+,w}_{r^{\prime},t^{\prime}}\subseteq E^{\mathrm{sym},w}_{r^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
and

E^{-,w}_{r^{\prime},t^{\prime}}\subseteq E^{\mathrm{sym},w}_{r^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
, it follows that

(x,y)\in E^{+,w}_{r^{\prime},t^{\prime}}\ \Rightarrow\ (f(x),f(y))\in E^{\mathrm{sym},v}_{r,t}\subseteq E^{+,v}_{r,t},

and similarly

(x,y)\in E^{-,w}_{r^{\prime},t^{\prime}}\ \Rightarrow\ (f(x),f(y))\in E^{\mathrm{sym},v}_{r,t}\subseteq E^{-,v}_{r,t}.

Hence
f
is forward and backward uniformly continuous.

Combining the two directions proves the equivalence.
∎

When

Definition 3.8

If

Proof.

Fix a basic symmetric entourage

with
r\in(0,1)
,

t>0

t
-conorm
\oplus
there exists

with
s\oplus s<r
. Put
t_{0}:=t/2
.

By forward precompactness (Definition

) there are points

F=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{m}\}\subseteq X
such that

X=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}B^{+}(x_{i};s,t_{0}).

By backward precompactness there are points
G=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\}\subseteq X

such that

X=\bigcup_{j=1}^{n}B^{-}(y_{j};s,t_{0}).

Hence the finite family of intersections

\mathcal{C}\ :=\ \bigl\{\,C_{ij}:=B^{+}(x_{i};s,t_{0})\cap B^{-}(y_{j};s,t_{0})\ \bigm|\ 1\leq i\leq m,\ 1\leq j\leq n\,\bigr\}

We claim that

C_{ij}\ \subseteq\ B(z_{ij};r,t)\qquad\text{for every nonempty }C_{ij}.

(3)

Indeed, let

∎

4
Categorical and Uniform Perspectives

In this section we recast quasi
-
modular gauges in the language of enriched
categories and relate the asymmetric (quasi
-
uniform) machinery to classical

as categories enriched over the quantale
\mathsf{V}=([0,\infty],\geq,+,0)

[
11
]
, together with the general calculus of
\mathsf{V}
-categories,
Kan extensions, and Cauchy completion as developed by Kelly
[
9
]
.
On the uniform side we use Isbell’s covering formulation and completion theory

[
8
]
. The two perspectives are contained in
(T,\mathsf{V})
–framework
of Clementino–Tholen
[
3
]

4.1
A quantalic base for quasi-modular gauges

Fix a continuous
t
-conorm
\oplus
on
[0,1]
.
Let
\mathsf{W}
be the complete lattice of all functions

\varphi:(0,\infty)\to[0,1]
ordered pointwise
decreasingly

(
\varphi\preceq\psi
iff
\varphi(t)\geq\psi(t)
for all
t
),
with monoidal product given by
convolution

(\varphi\star\psi)(u)\ :=\ \inf_{u=t+s}\ \big(\,\varphi(t)\ \oplus\ \psi(s)\,\big),\qquad\mathbf{0}(u):=0,\ \ \mathbf{1}(u):=1.

Then
(\mathsf{W},\preceq,\star,\mathbf{0})
is a commutative unital quantale:
\star
is
associative, commutative, monotone in each argument, and distributes over arbitrary
infima. (This is the standard “triangle–via–convolution” quantale; cf.
[
9
, §1.1–§1.6]
and
[
11
, §1]
for the enriched background.)

For a (quasi
-
)modular gauge
w
on
X
(assumptions (W1)–(W4) in
Section
3
), define for every
x,y\in X
the profile

W(x,y)(t)\ :=\ w(x,y,t)\in[0,1],\qquad t>0.

Then (W3) and (W4) translate exactly into the
\mathsf{W}
-enriched triangle
and unit inequalities:

W(x,z)\ \preceq\ W(x,y)\star W(y,z),\qquad\mathbf{0}\ \preceq\ W(x,x),

while (W1) is the
T_{0}
-separation at the underlying level. Thus:

Proposition 4.1
.

Every quasi
-
modular gauge
w
on
X
determines a small

\mathsf{W}
-category
X_{w}
with objects
X
and hom
-
objects
W(x,y)\in\mathsf{W}
.
A map
f:X\to Y
is forward (resp. backward) uniformly continuous in the
sense of Definition
3.6
iff it is
\mathsf{W}
-functorial
with respect to the forward (resp. backward) homs. If
w
is symmetric,

X_{w}
is a
\mathsf{W}
-category in the ordinary sense and bi
-
uniform
continuity is
\mathsf{W}
-functoriality.

Proof.

Let
\mathsf{W}
be the poset of all right–continuous, nonincreasing maps

\varphi:(0,\infty)\to[0,1]
, ordered by

\varphi\succeq\psi\iff\forall\,t\in(0,\infty)\ \forall\,r\in(0,1)\ \exists\,t^{\prime}>0,\ r^{\prime}\in(0,1):\ \bigl(\ \varphi(t^{\prime})<r^{\prime}\ \Rightarrow\ \psi(t)<r\ \bigr),

so that
\varphi\succeq\psi
expresses that every basic forward entourage defined by
\psi

is controlled by one defined by
\varphi
. Equip
\mathsf{W}
with the commutative,
associative, monotone tensor

(\varphi\otimes\psi)(t):=\inf\{\ \varphi(t^{\prime})\oplus\psi(t^{\prime\prime}):\ t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}>0,\ t^{\prime}+t^{\prime\prime}\geq t\ \},

whose unit is the constant-zero profile
\mathbf{0}

tensor (compare Theorem
3.2
). Define the hom–profiles of
X_{w}
by

W(x,y):t\mapsto w(x,y,t)
. Then
W(x,x)=\mathbf{0}
by (W1). For composition, fix

x,y,z\in X
,
t>0
,
r\in(0,1)
; choose
r^{\prime}<r
with
r^{\prime}\oplus r^{\prime}<r
(continuity and
monotonicity of
\oplus
). If
t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}>0
and
t^{\prime}+t^{\prime\prime}\geq t
with

w(x,y,t^{\prime})<r^{\prime}
and
w(y,z,t^{\prime\prime})<r^{\prime}
, the triangle axiom (W3) and monotonicity in

w(x,z,t)\ \leq\ w(x,z,t^{\prime}+t^{\prime\prime})\ \leq\ w(x,y,t^{\prime})\oplus w(y,z,t^{\prime\prime})\ <\ r^{\prime}\oplus r^{\prime}\ <\ r,

which is exactly the inequality
W(x,z)\preceq W(x,y)\otimes W(y,z)
. Thus

is a small

\mathsf{W}
-category. Now let
f:X\to Y
and let
v
be a quasi-modular gauge on
Y
.
By the definition of
\succeq
, the enriched functorial inequality

W_{X}(x,y)\succeq W_{Y}(fx,fy)
holds iff

\forall\,t,r\ \exists\,t^{\prime},r^{\prime}:\ w(x,y,t^{\prime})<r^{\prime}\ \Rightarrow\ v(fx,fy,t)<r,

i.e. iff
(x,y)\in E^{+,w}_{r^{\prime},t^{\prime}}\Rightarrow(f(x),f(y))\in E^{+,v}_{r,t}
, which is precisely
forward uniform continuity in the sense of Definition
3.6
;
the backward case is obtained by swapping the arguments in
w
and
v
.
If
w
is symmetric then

and the forward/backward hom–profiles coincide, so

is an ordinary (one-sided)
\mathsf{W}
-category and bi-uniform continuity is equivalent
to
\mathsf{W}
-functoriality.
∎

4.3
Cauchy completion: enriched vs. uniform

For a symmetric base
\mathsf{V}
, every
\mathsf{V}
-category admits a

Cauchy completion
obtained by splitting
\mathsf{V}
-Cauchy modules;
it is characterized by the universal property of extending (and detecting) absolute
colimits and by the Yoneda embedding
y:X\to\widehat{X}
with dense image

[
9
, §3, §4]
. In the metric case
\mathsf{V}=[0,\infty]
this recovers
the ordinary metric completion (up to isometry of the associated Lawvere spaces).

On the uniform side, every uniform space
(X,\mathcal{U})
admits a completion

(\widehat{X},\widehat{\mathcal{U}})
with the familiar universal property and

\widehat{X}
is compact iff
X
is complete and totally bounded

[
8
, Ch. II]
. Combining these with Proposition
4.1

and symmetrization yields:

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
w
be a quasi
-
modular gauge on
X
. Then the Cauchy completion of the
symmetric
\mathsf{W}
-category
X_{w^{\mathrm{sym}}}
coincides (up to unique
bi
-
uniform isomorphism) with the uniform completion of the symmetrized
uniform space
\bigl(X,\mathcal{U}(w^{\mathrm{sym}})\bigr)
.

Proof.

Write
u=\mathcal{U}(w^{\mathrm{sym}})
for the symmetric uniformity generated by the
two–sided entourages
E^{\mathrm{sym}}_{r,t}=E^{+}_{r,t}\cap E^{-}_{r,t}
, and let

X_{w^{\mathrm{sym}}}
denote the corresponding symmetric
\mathsf{W}
–category with
hom–profiles
W(x,y):t\mapsto w^{\mathrm{sym}}(x,y,t)
. Let

e:\ (X,u)\longrightarrow(\widehat{X},\widehat{u})\qquad\text{and}\qquad j:\ X_{w^{\mathrm{sym}}}\longrightarrow\overline{X}

be, respectively, the uniform completion of
u
(Isbell’s completion) and the

\mathsf{W}
–enriched Cauchy completion (Kelly’s completion). We claim there is a
unique bi–uniform isomorphism
\Phi:\overline{X}\!\to\!(\widehat{X},\widehat{u})
with

\Phi\circ j=e
on
X
.

First observe that the symmetric modular entourage base
\{E^{\mathrm{sym}}_{r,t}\}
is a
uniform entourage base; conversely, every basic
E^{\mathrm{sym}}_{r,t}
is the inverse
image of a basic entourage for a compatible family of pseudometrics (e.g. Luxemburg
gauges at fixed thresholds), hence
u
is exactly the uniformity determined by

w^{\mathrm{sym}}
(cf. the uniform entourage dictionary and weak uniformities in

[
8
, Ch. I]
, and the entourage cover in
[
15
, Ch. VIII]
).
Therefore uniformly continuous maps out of
(X,u)
are exactly
\mathsf{W}
–functors
out of
X_{w^{\mathrm{sym}}}
(bi–uniform continuity
\Leftrightarrow
enriched functoriality).

Both completions are characterized by the same universal property. On the uniform
side:
e
has dense image,
(\widehat{X},\widehat{u})
is complete and separated, and
for every uniformly continuous
f:(X,u)\to(Y,v)
with
(Y,v)
complete separated,
there is a unique uniformly continuous
\widehat{f}:\widehat{X}\to Y
with

\widehat{f}\circ e=f
(
[
8
, Ch. II]
). On the enriched side:
j
is

\mathsf{W}
–dense,
\overline{X}
is Cauchy complete and separated, and for every

\mathsf{W}
–functor
F:X_{w^{\mathrm{sym}}}\to Y
with
Y
Cauchy complete and separated,
there is a unique
\overline{F}:\overline{X}\to Y
with
\overline{F}\circ j=F

(
[
9
, §3–§4]
).

Apply these universal properties twice, using that a complete separated uniform
space is the same datum as a complete separated symmetric
\mathsf{W}
–category for
our base (the hom–profiles of
Y
encode its symmetric uniformity, and conversely).
From
j:X\to\overline{X}
and the uniform universal property (with
f=\mathrm{id}_{X}
),
there exists a unique uniformly continuous
\Phi:\overline{X}\to\widehat{X}
with

\Phi\circ j=e
. Dually, from
e:X\to\widehat{X}
and the enriched universal property
(with
F=\mathrm{id}_{X}
), there exists a unique
\Psi:\widehat{X}\to\overline{X}
with

\Psi\circ e=j
. Uniqueness in the two universal properties yields

\Phi\circ\Psi=\mathrm{id}_{\widehat{X}}
and
\Psi\circ\Phi=\mathrm{id}_{\overline{X}}
:
indeed, both composites agree with the identity on the dense image of
X
and are
endomaps of complete separated objects, hence equal. Consequently
\Phi
is a
uniform isomorphism; by construction it preserves and reflects the
\mathsf{W}
–hom
profiles, so it is bi–uniform and unique with
\Phi\circ j=e
.

In the metrizable subcase (e.g. when
u
has a countable base), the argument can
be seen concretely:
u
is generated by a single compatible metric (Isbell, Ch. V),
and the enriched Cauchy completion coincides with the ordinary metric completion;
this coincides with the uniform completion by construction. The general case follows
from the universal–property argument above. This proves that the Cauchy completion
of the symmetric
\mathsf{W}
–category
X_{w^{\mathrm{sym}}}
coincides, up to a unique
bi–uniform isomorphism, with the uniform completion of
(X,u)
.
∎

The forward and backward entourages

\mathsf{W}
-Cat.
The corresponding completions compare as follows.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\widetilde{X}
be any bicompletion of the pair

(X,\mathcal{V}^{+}(w),\mathcal{V}^{-}(w))
in the sense that both quasi
-
uniform
structures extend and the Yoneda images are dense. Then the symmetrized
uniform space
\bigl(\widetilde{X},\mathcal{U}(w^{\mathrm{sym}})\bigr)
is a
uniform completion of
\bigl(X,\mathcal{U}(w^{\mathrm{sym}})\bigr)
.

Proof.

Write
u:=\mathcal{U}(w^{\mathrm{sym}})
on
X
and
\widetilde{u}:=\mathcal{U}(w^{\mathrm{sym}})
on
\widetilde{X}
,
both generated by the symmetric basic entourages

E^{\mathrm{sym}}_{r,t}=E^{+}_{r,t}\cap E^{-}_{r,t}
(cf. Theorem
3.2
and
Proposition
3.5
). By hypothesis, the canonical map

e:X\to\widetilde{X}
has dense image for
\mathcal{V}^{+}(w)
and for
\mathcal{V}^{-}(w)
.
Since
E^{\mathrm{sym}}_{r,t}=E^{+}_{r,t}\cap E^{-}_{r,t}
, it follows that
e(X)
is dense for the

join
topology, hence for
\widetilde{u}
(the uniform topology of the
symmetrized uniformity), by Proposition
3.5
and the
general quasi/̄uniform to uniform
[
15
, Chap. XII.4]
;
see also
[
10
]
.

Completeness of
(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{u})
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a
\widetilde{u}
/̄Cauchy filter on
\widetilde{X}
. Then
\mathcal{F}
is
Cauchy for each quasi/̄uniformity
\mathcal{V}^{\pm}(w)
, because every

E^{\mathrm{sym}}_{r,t}
is contained in both
E^{\pm}_{r,t}
. Since
\widetilde{X}
is a
bicompletion, it is (left and right) quasi/̄uniformly complete; hence

\mathcal{F}
has a
\mathcal{V}^{+}(w)
/̄limit
x_{+}
and a
\mathcal{V}^{-}(w)
/̄limit
x_{-}
.
To see these coincide, fix a symmetric basic entourage

E^{\mathrm{sym}}_{r,t}
and pick

and
t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}>0
with

E^{+}_{r^{\prime},t^{\prime}}\circ E^{-}_{r^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}}\subseteq E^{\mathrm{sym}}_{r,t}
and

E^{-}_{r^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}}\circ E^{+}_{r^{\prime},t^{\prime}}\subseteq E^{\mathrm{sym}}_{r,t}

(Weil–Nachbin small/̄composite law, Theorem
3.2
). Since

\mathcal{F}
converges to
x_{+}
for
\mathcal{V}^{+}(w)
and to
x_{-}
for
\mathcal{V}^{-}(w)
,
there exists
F\in\mathcal{F}
with
F\subseteq E^{+}_{r^{\prime},t^{\prime}}[x_{+}]\cap E^{-}_{r^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}}[x_{-}]
.
Then for all

,
(x_{+},y)\in E^{+}_{r^{\prime},t^{\prime}}
and
(y,x_{-})\in E^{-}_{r^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}}
,
hence
(x_{+},x_{-})\in E^{\mathrm{sym}}_{r,t}
. As
E^{\mathrm{sym}}_{r,t}
was arbitrary, we get

w^{\mathrm{sym}}(x_{+},x_{-},t)=0
for all

, and, by separation
(\mathrm{W1})
,

x_{+}=x_{-}
. Thus every
\widetilde{u}
/̄Cauchy filter converges in
\widetilde{X}
, so

(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{u})
is complete.

Universal property.

Let
(Y,v)
be a complete separated uniform space and

f:(X,u)\to(Y,v)
a uniformly continuous map. Regard
Y
with the
(quasi/̄)uniform pair induced by

; then
f
is forward and backward
uniformly continuous. By the bicompletion property of
\widetilde{X}
, there exists
a unique forward/backward uniformly continuous
\tilde{f}:\widetilde{X}\to Y
with

\tilde{f}\circ e=f
. Since basic symmetric entourages are intersections of forward
and backward ones,
\tilde{f}
is uniformly continuous for
\widetilde{u}
. Uniqueness
holds in the uniform sense as well, because
e(X)
is
\widetilde{u}
/̄dense and

is separated.

We have shown that
e:(X,u)\to(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{u})
has dense image, that

(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{u})
is complete, and that every uniformly continuous

f:(X,u)\to(Y,v)
into a complete separated uniform space extends uniquely to

\tilde{f}:(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{u})\to(Y,v)
. Hence

(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{u})
is the uniform completion of
(X,u)
in the
sense of
[
8
, Chap. II]
. Equivalently, by

4.2
, it coincides (up to unique bi/̄uniform
isomorphism) with the completion of the symmetric
\mathsf{W}
/̄category

X_{w^{\mathrm{sym}}}
. This proves the claim.
∎

5.1
Cauchy notions and completeness

Definition 5.1
.

Let
(x_{i})_{i\in I}
be a net in
X
with

directed.

•

The net is
forward Cauchy
(for
\mathcal{V}^{+}(w)
) if for every
r\in(0,1)
and

there is
i_{0}
such that

(x_{i},x_{j})\in E^{+}_{r,t}\quad\text{whenever }i_{0}\leq i\leq j.

•

It is
backward Cauchy
(for
\mathcal{V}^{-}(w)
) if for every

there is
i_{0}
with

(x_{j},x_{i})\in E^{-}_{r,t}\quad\text{whenever }i_{0}\leq i\leq j.

•

It is
bi

Cauchy
if it is forward and backward Cauchy.

A net

i_{0}

for all

Definition 5.2
.

We say

is

(resp.

) if every forward (resp. backward) Cauchy net forward (resp. backward) converges. It is
bi
-
complete
if every bi
-
Cauchy net converges in the symmetrized topology
\tau(w^{\mathrm{sym}})
.

The following identifies the preceding with the classical uniform notion.

Theorem 5.3
.

(X,w)
is bi
-
complete iff the uniform space
\bigl(X,\mathcal{U}(w^{\mathrm{sym}})\bigr)
is complete.

Proof.

A net is Cauchy in
\mathcal{U}(w^{\mathrm{sym}})
iff it is eventually inside every two
-
sided ball
B(x;r,t)
; by definition this is equivalent to being both forward and backward Cauchy. Convergence in the uniform space is convergence in
\tau(w^{\mathrm{sym}})
. See the uniform Cauchy theory in
[
8
, Ch. II]
and the quasi
-
uniform to uniform in
[
10
,
15
]
.
∎

When
\mathcal{U}(w^{\mathrm{sym}})
has a countable base (e.g. in the convex/Luxemburg metricizable case), forward/backward Cauchy
sequences
suffice, and bi
-
completeness coincides with metric completeness for any compatible metric on
\mathcal{U}(w^{\mathrm{sym}})

[
8
]
.

5.2

Definition 5.4
.

(X,w)
is

Proposition 5.5
.

If

\bigl(X,\mathcal{U}(w^{\mathrm{sym}})\bigr)

Proof.

For each

and the cover by

-
sided balls cover

[
8
, Ch. II]
.
∎

Theorem 5.6
.

The following are equivalent:

(i)

(X,w)
is forward and backward precompact and bi

(ii)

\bigl(X,\mathcal{U}(w^{\mathrm{sym}})\bigr)
is compact;

(iii)

\bigl(X,\tau(w^{\mathrm{sym}})\bigr)

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow

and completeness by Theorem

+
totally bounded in uniform spaces

8
, Ch. II]

(iii) because a compact uniform space is compact in its induced topology and vice versa. (ii)

∎

Proposition 5.7
.

For

is compact in

(b)

Proof.

This is the standard characterization of relative (pre)compactness in uniform spaces, using the base of two

Let

If

6.1
Weighted Modulars

Definition 6.1
.

Let

\rho_{\varphi,w}(f):=\int_{\Omega}w(x)\,\varphi\!\big(x,|f(x)|\big)\,d\mu(x),\qquad\|f\|_{\varphi,w}:=\inf\{\lambda>0:\rho_{\varphi,w}(f/\lambda)\leq 1\}.

The weighted Musielak–Orlicz space is

L_{\varphi,w}(\Omega):=\{\,f\in L^{0}(\Omega):\exists\lambda>0,\ \rho_{\varphi,w}(f/\lambda)<\infty\,\}.

Remark 6.2
.

If
0<c\leq w(x)\leq C<\infty
a.e., then
\rho_{\varphi,w}\simeq\rho_{\varphi}
and

\|\cdot\|_{\varphi,w}\simeq\|\cdot\|_{\varphi}

with equivalent gauges
(see
[
2

[

, Ch. 3]

[

.

1.

\varphi(x,t)=t^{p(x)}

Then

classical operators (e.g. the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator) are bounded (cf.
[
6

2.

Double phase:

\varphi(x,t)=t^{p}+a(x)t^{q}

and

The weighted modular

captures both the soft/hard medium encoded by

[

6.2
Directed graphs and dynamic Orlicz energies

Let
G=(V,E)
be a locally finite
directed
graph with vertex measure
m:V\to(0,\infty)

and edge weights
\mu:E\to(0,\infty)
. For
e=(u\to v)
define the forward difference

\nabla^{+}f(e):=f(v)-f(u)
.

Definition 6.5
.

Let
e\mapsto\varphi(e,\cdot)
be an edgewise Musielak–Orlicz function. Define

\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}^{+}(f):=\sum_{e\in E}\mu(e)\,\varphi\!\big(e,|\nabla^{+}f(e)|\big),\qquad\|f\|_{\mathcal{E}^{+}_{\varphi}}:=\inf\{\lambda>0:\ \mathcal{E}_{\varphi}^{+}(f/\lambda)\leq 1\}.

A backward energy
\mathcal{E}^{-}_{\varphi}
is defined analogously using
\nabla^{-}f(u\to v):=f(u)-f(v)
.

Remark 6.6
.

In general
\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}^{+}\neq\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}^{-}
, yielding left/right gauges.
This fits the theory of quasi-uniform spaces: the forward quasi-pseudometric

d^{\to}(x,y)
given by minimal forward path cost induces entourages

U_{\varepsilon}:=\{(x,y):d^{\to}(x,y)<\varepsilon\}
generating a left quasi-uniformity,
while the backward one generates a right quasi-uniformity (see
[
10
,
15
]
).
Asymmetric distances on discrete structures are surveyed in
[
5
]
.

Example 6.7
.

Let
t\mapsto c_{t}(e)\in(0,\infty)
be a locally bounded edge–cost. The forward dynamic distance

d_{t}^{\to}(x,y)
is the infimum of
\sum c_{t}(e)
over forward paths
x\rightsquigarrow y
.
Choosing
\varphi_{t}(e,t^{\prime})=t^{\prime p}+a_{t}(e)\,t^{\prime q}
yields a double–phase
dynamic
energy

\mathcal{E}^{+}_{\varphi_{t}}
able to encode evolving anisotropy. Convergence in the

\mathcal{E}^{+}_{\varphi_{t}}
–gauge implies uniform smallness of forward oscillations on
U_{\varepsilon}^{t}
,
parallel to modular
\Rightarrow
uniform–type implications in
[
6
, §3.3]
.

6.3
Asymmetric function spaces via one–sided modulars

Definition 6.8
.

Let
\rho^{+}(f):=\int_{\Omega}\psi_{1}(x,(f(x))_{+})\,d\mu
and

\rho^{-}(f):=\int_{\Omega}\psi_{2}(x,(f(x))_{-})\,d\mu
,
with
(t)_{\pm}=\max\{\pm t,0\}
and
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}
Musielak–Orlicz functions.
Define

\|f\|_{+}:=\inf\{\lambda>0:\rho^{+}(f/\lambda)\leq 1\},\qquad\|f\|_{-}:=\inf\{\lambda>0:\rho^{-}(f/\lambda)\leq 1\},

and the symmetrized gauge
\|f\|_{\mathrm{sym}}:=\max\{\|f\|_{+},\|f\|_{-}\}

Lemma 6.9
.

For each side,
\|f\|_{\pm}\leq 1\iff\rho^{\pm}(f)\leq 1
.
Hence
\|f\|_{\mathrm{sym}}\leq 1
iff both
\rho^{+}(f)\leq 1
and
\rho^{-}(f)\leq 1
.

Proposition 6.10
.

If
\rho^{\pm}
satisfy modular triangle–type inequalities (as in
[
2
]
),
then

and
d_{-}(f,g):=\|f-g\|_{-}
are quasi–metrics.
They induce left/right topologies which together define a quasi–uniform structure on
L^{0}(\Omega)

whose symmetrization coincides with the topology of
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{sym}}

(
[
10
,
15
]
).

Example 6.11
.

Choosing
\psi_{1}(x,t)=t^{p(x)}
and
\psi_{2}(x,t)=t^{q(x)}
with

produces sign–asymmetric growth controlling positive and negative parts differently.
Such spaces are useful when forward/backward fluxes or one–sided constraints appear
(
[
6
, Ch. 7]
; cf. also the asymmetric distance perspective in
[
5
]
).

Sobolev–type

Define

W^{1,\varphi}(\Omega):=\{u\in L_{\varphi}(\Omega):\nabla u\in L_{\varphi}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\},\qquad\|u\|_{W^{1,\varphi}}:=\|u\|_{\varphi}+\|\nabla u\|_{\varphi}.

7
Closing Remarks and Open Problems

We developed a unified, modular perspective that accommodates classical and generalized
Orlicz growth, variable exponent and double–phase models, and
asymmetric
notion
arising from one–sided modulars and directed structures. This setting covers:
(i) weighted quasi–Orlicz modulars and their Luxemburg gauges;
(ii) directed-graph/dynamic energies with forward/backward (non-symmetric) costs;
(iii) asymmetric function spaces obtained from left/right modulars;
(iv) Sobolev-type constructions and compactness via modular Poincaré/Sobolev principles.
This unified treatment positions modular analysis alongside quasi-uniform methods and
asymmetric distances, and it interfaces naturally with reflexivity, separability,
and duality criteria from generalized Orlicz theory.

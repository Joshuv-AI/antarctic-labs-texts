Posterior concentration in spatio-temporal Hawkes processes miscouridou.xenia@ucy.ac.cy deborah.sulem@usi.ch
Xenia Miscouridou, Déborah Sulem

Abstract
We develop a Bayesian nonparametric framework for inference in multivariate spatio-temporal Hawkes processes, extending existing theoretical results beyond the purely temporal setting. Our framework encompasses modelling both the background and triggering components of the Hawkes process through Gaussian process priors. Under appropriate smoothness and regularity assumptions on the true parameter and the nonparametric prior family, we derive posterior contraction rates for the intensity function and the parameter, in the asymptotic regime of repeatedly observed sequences. Our analysis generalizes known contraction results for purely temporal Hawkes processes to the spatio-temporal setting, which allows to jointly model excitation and clustering effects across time and space. These results provide, to our knowledge, the first theoretical guarantees for Bayesian nonparametric methods in spatio-temporal point data.



Keywords:
Bayesian nonparametrics, Asymptotics, Gaussian processes

Introduction

Hawkes processes are point process models designed to capture sequences of events where the intensity of occurrence depends on the past history of the process. Their defining property is self-excitation: each past event increases the likelihood of future ones. Originally introduced by Alan G. Hawkes (1971) for modeling the clustering of earthquakes
(
17
)
, Hawkes processes have since been widely applied across disciplines, including seismology
(
34
)
, social and information networks
(
7
;
50
)
, neuroscience
(
39
;
45
)
, dynamic network analysis
(
47
;
13
)
, criminology
(
31
;
30
)
, and epidemiology
(
41
)
. More recently, Hawkes processes have also found applications in machine learning, where they are used to model temporal dependencies, perform causal discovery, and augment large language models with event-based dynamics and memory
(
29
;
52
;
19
;
18
)
.

A Hawkes process can be viewed as a non-homogeneous cluster Poisson point process and admits a self-exciting intensity function. It can also be represented as a branching or cluster process with a latent structure. This representation is particularly useful for simulation and interpretation, as the process can be viewed as a cascade of events, where each event is either exogeneously generated or endogeneously generated by a past event (parent). Hawkes processes can be univariate or multivariate. In the latter case, each component corresponds to a distinct type of event, and the process is equivalent to a marked point processes.

Originally, a Hawkes process was defined as a univariate temporal point process
(
17
)
, where each event at time
t_{i}
increases the probability of future events at times
t>t_{i}
. This temporal process does not allow to model spatial effects in spatio-temporal event data, or, in other words, it assumes that the influence of an event is homogeneous across space, which is often unrealistic in many applications. In practice, the excitation effect caused by an event may depend on both time and spatial proximity, making a spatio-temporal formulation more appropriate. Indeed, recent studies have emphasized spatio-temporal Hawkes processes in applications such as modeling wildfires
(
24
)
and terrorism
(
20
)
. Comprehensive overviews can be found in
38
and more recently in
2
. Despite these advances, there remains a significant gap between practical modeling approaches and theoretical understanding, particularly from a Bayesian perspective.

From a Bayesian viewpoint, establishing posterior contraction rates provides fundamental theoretical validation for a model’s ability to learn the true self-exciting mechanism as the amount of data increases. Existing work on posterior contraction for Hawkes processes has focused almost exclusively on temporal models.
12
derived posterior contraction rates for multivariate linear Hawkes processes in a nonparametric setting, while
44
extended the analysis to nonlinear Hawkes processes, accounting for inhibition effects. More general mathematical frameworks for posterior contraction in point processes are provided in
11
, and analogous results for inhomogeneous Poisson processes and using Gaussian process priors can be found in
23
and
15
.

On the frequentist side, likelihood-based inference for Hawkes models has a long history.
33
and
35
established consistency and asymptotic normality of the maximum likelihood estimator (MLE) for stationary, univariate, exponential and purely temporal Hawkes processes, while
26
extended these results to the multivariate case. For the purely temporal but nonstationary Hawkes model,
5
and
25
study the consistency of the MLE in an asymptotic setting closely related to ours. Broader results for MLE estimation in point processes can be found in Chapter 7 of
8
. Recent work has also derived non-asymptotic, finite-sample concentration inequalities for least-square estimation in multivariate temporal Hawkes processes, both in parametric and nonparametric settings
(
6
;
16
;
4
)
.

However, none of the existing Bayesian posterior contraction results or frequentist asymptotic results address the spatio-temporal setting, to our best knowledge. Theoretical guarantees for Bayesian inference in spatio-temporal Hawkes processes remain unexplored, despite their growing empirical importance. Partial advances have been made only recently, such as the flexible spatio-temporal modeling framework in
42
, but without asymptotic or contraction results. This gap motivates the present work, which provides a rigorous Bayesian nonparametric treatment of spatio-temporal, non-stationary Hawkes processes and establishes their posterior contraction properties.

To study these types of theoretical guarantees, different asymptotic setups are possible, such as repeated observations
(
9
)
or infinite domain
(
15
)
, and for each of these different Bernstein-type inequalities are needed.

Contribution.

We establish posterior contraction rates for non-stationary and spatio-temporal Hawkes processes within a Bayesian nonparametric framework in the setting of repeated observations. The nonparametric framework permits a flexible specification of the conditional intensity of the Hawkes process, using nonparametric prior families over functions of space and time for both the background rate and the triggering kernel. Under suitable regularity assumptions on the true parameter and mild conditions on the prior family, we derive explicit rates at which the posterior distribution concentrates around the truth. Our results hold for general classes of nonparametric priors, in particular encompassing Gaussian process priors, which provide a natural and widely used choice in modern Bayesian inference for point processes
(
48
;
27
;
28
)
. Our proofs have similar structure to those of papers of temporal Hawkes
(
12
;
44
)
but the extension to space-time is non-trivial and requires new concentration inequalities. Additionally, our work differs from the majority of previous point process papers as we consider the repeated observations settings rather than an infinite domain. Our analysis therefore extends existing posterior contraction results for temporal Hawkes to the general spatio-temporal and non-stationary setting.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section
2
gives the setup and introduces the multivariate spatio-temporal Hawkes process model and the Bayesian nonparametric formulation illustrated with Gaussian process priors on the background and triggering components. Section
3
presents the main theoretical results, establishing posterior contraction rates under suitable regularity conditions on the true intensity and the prior. Proofs of all main results can be found in Section
4
.

Setup and Methodology

2.1
Setup

We assume that we have repeated observations of a point process over a bounded spatio-temporal domain
S
. For simplicity we can assume
S=[0,1]\times[0,1]^{d}
(note that we can always rescale the events time to
[0,1]
)
1
1

1

We note that our methodology can easily be modified for general bounded domains by changing the support of the parameters.
. In many practical applications,
d=2
(latitude and longitude). The data thus consists of
n
i.i.d. sequences of events with spatio-temporal coordinates, i.e., sequence
i
is a set of
m_{i}
points
N^{i}=(t^{i}_{j},s^{i}_{j})_{j\leq m_{i}}
with
s_{j}^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

\displaystyle t^{i}_{1}<t^{i}_{2}<\dots<t^{i}_{m_{i}}

We denote by
N=(N^{i})_{i\leq n}
these sequences and model the latter as independent realisations of the same spatio-temporal Hawkes process
N(t,s)
defined on
S
as follows.

Definition 1
.

A spatio-temporal point process
N(t,s)
defined on a domain
S
is a spatio-temporal Hawkes process with parameter
f=(\mu,g)
where
\mu\geq 0
and
g\geq 0
are non-negative functions, respectively called the background rate and the triggering kernel, if for any
(t,s)\in S
, its conditional intensity function is

\displaystyle\lambda_{t,s}(f)=\lambda_{t,s}(\mu,g)

\displaystyle=\mu(t,s)+\int_{[0,t)\times[0,1]^{d}}g(t-t^{\prime},s-s^{\prime})dN(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\mu(t,s)+\sum_{(t_{j},s_{j})\in N,t_{j}<t}g(t-t_{j},s-s_{j}),

Note that a Hawkes process as defined in Definition
1
is non-stationary unless
\mu
is constant in time.

We denote by
\mathbf{P}_{f}
the law of the Hawkes process
N(t,s)
with parameter
f
and
\mathbb{E}_{f}
the corresponding expectation. For a subset
A\subset S
, we denote by
N(A)
the number of observations on
A
. We also make a finite-range assumption on the triggering kernel
g
, namely
g(t,s)=0
if
t<0
or
t>a
or
\|s\|_{\infty}>b
with
0<a<1/2,0<b<1/2
. This implies that we can re-write the intensity as

\displaystyle\lambda_{t,s}(f)

\displaystyle=\mu(t,s)+\int_{t-a}^{t-}\int_{s^{\prime}\in[0,1]^{d}:\|s-s^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq b}g(t-t^{\prime},s-s^{\prime})dN(t^{\prime},s^{\prime}).

(1)

We make another standard assumption that the branching ratio of
N(t,s)
is less than 1, implying that the process is non-explosive, i.e.,

\displaystyle\|g\|_{1}:=\int_{0}^{a}\int_{[0,1]^{d}\cap\{s:\|s\|_{\infty}\leq b\}}g(t,s)dtds<1.

Here the statistical goal is to estimate
f
from observations
N
. We first prove an identifiability result, which validates the feasibility of this estimation problem, under a mild assumption on the background rate.

Assumption 1
.

The background rate
\mu
verifies:

1.

\int_{0}^{1-a}\int_{[b,1-b]^{d}}\mu(t,s)>0
.

2.

\mu(t,s)<+\infty,\quad\forall(t,s)\in S
.

Assumption
1
ensures that the background rate is finite and that the probability of observing at least one event is non-null.

Proposition 1

(Identifiability)
.

Let
N
and
N^{\prime}
be two spatio-temporal Hawkes processes with respective parameters
f=(\mu,g)
and
f^{\prime}=(\mu^{\prime},g^{\prime})
verifying Assumption
1
. Then,

\displaystyle N\overset{d}{=}N^{\prime}\iff f=f^{\prime}.

The proof of Proposition
1
is found in Section
4
.

2.2
Methodology

We denote by
f_{0}=(\mu_{0},g_{0})
the true parameter and by
\mathbf{P}_{0}
and
\mathbb{E}_{0}
the law of the Hawkes process
N(t,s)
and its expectation respectively.

We now describe our Bayesian nonparametric estimation framework for the true parameter
f_{0}
of the spatio-temporal Hawkes process (Definition
1
). Here, we focus on prior families based on transformations of Gaussian processes (GP), though our theorerical results hold (Section
3
) for more general families such as mixture of beta densities or histogram priors (see, e.g.,
12
and
44
). Recall that our parameter of interest
f=(\mu,g)\in\mathcal{F}
where

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\{f=(\mu,g);\mu:S\to\mathbb{R}_{+},\>g:[0,a]\times[0,b]^{d}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}\}.

We define a prior distribution
\Pi
on
\mathcal{F}
which factorises over the background and triggering kernel, i.e.,

\displaystyle\Pi(f)=\Pi_{\mu}(\mu)\Pi_{g}(g),\qquad f\in\mathcal{F}.

The prior distributions
\Pi_{\mu},\Pi_{g}
are distributions on non-negative functions implicitly constructed via transformations of GP. Specifically,

\displaystyle\mu=\sigma(\nu),\qquad\nu\sim GP(0,k_{\nu})

\displaystyle g=\sigma(\phi),\qquad\phi\sim GP(0,k_{\phi}).

Above,
\nu
and
\phi
are latent functions and
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
is a known link function, typically a strictly increasing and bijective function on a large enough interval such as the softplus or the sigmoid function. Moreover,
k_{\nu}
and
k_{\phi}
are covariance functions (kernels) defined on the spatio-temporal domain
S
. For simplicity and without loss of generality, we choose a zero mean function in our GP prior.

GP priors are commonly used in Bayesian nonparametric methods for point processes, e.g., for inhomogeneous Poisson processes
(
1
;
27
;
22
;
36
;
15
;
32
)
as well as temporal Hawkes processes
(
48
;
28
)
. In
30
, a GP prior with exponential link function is used for estimating the spatio-temporal background rate of a Hawkes process. In constrast, here, both the background and the triggering kernel are estimated nonparametrically using GP priors. For an introduction to GPs, see, e.g.
37
.

In the rest of this section, we specify possible choices for the kernel functions and inference methodology. Let
u=(t,s)\in S
. Common choices of kernels include the squared exponential (RBF) and Matérn kernels defined as follows:

\displaystyle k_{RBF}(u,u^{\prime})=\sigma\exp\left(-\frac{\|u-u^{\prime}\|^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\right),

(2)

\displaystyle k_{Mat}(u,u^{\prime})=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{\Gamma(\tau)2^{\tau-1}}\left(\frac{\|u-u^{\prime}\|_{2}}{\ell}\right)^{\tau}B_{\nu}\left(\frac{\|u-u^{\prime}\|_{2}}{\ell}\right),

(3)

with hyperparameters
\sigma^{2},\ell,\tau>0
,
\Gamma
the Gamma function, and
B_{\tau}
the modified Bessel function of the second kind. We note that in the limit
\tau\to\infty
, the Matérn kernel is equivalent to the RBF kernel. Often, it is computationally convenient to use kernels that are separable in time and space and stationary, i.e.,

\displaystyle k_{r}(u,u^{\prime})=k_{r,t}(|t-t^{\prime}|)k_{r,s}(\|s-s^{\prime}\|_{2}),\quad r\in\{\nu,\phi\}.

Here as well, the RBF and Matérn kernels are common choices for the temporal and spatial kernels
k_{r,t}
and
k_{r,s}
. We note that a separable kernel does not imply that the latent function
\nu
or
\phi
(and a-fortiori
\mu
or
g
) are separable functions over space and time. Nonetheless, the choice of kernel and its hyperparameters determines the smoothness of the samples from the GP prior (see more on this in Section
3
).

Inference on
\mu
and
g
is then performed via the posterior distribution. Firstly, we define the likelihood of the set of observations
N=(N^{i})_{i=1,\dots,n}
as (see, e.g.,
8
)

\displaystyle L(N|f)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{m_{i}}\lambda^{i}_{t_{j}^{i},s_{j}^{i}}(f)\exp\left\{-\int_{S}\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f)dtds\right\},\qquad f\in\mathcal{F},

where, for each
i=1,\dots,n
,

\displaystyle\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f)=\mu(t,s)+\int g(t-t^{\prime},s-s^{\prime})dN^{i}(t^{\prime},s^{\prime}).

(4)

The posterior distribution is then defined as

\displaystyle\Pi(B|N)=\frac{\int_{B}L(N|f)d\Pi(f)}{\int_{\mathcal{F}}L(N|f)d\Pi(f)},\qquad B\subset\mathcal{F}.

In practice, a variational approximation of the posterior may only be computed, defined, e.g., as

\displaystyle\hat{Q}(f)=\arg\min_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}KL(Q||\Pi(f|N)),

(5)

where
KL
is the Kullback-Leibler divergence. Here, the minimum is taken over an approximating family of distributions
\mathcal{Q}
, for instance, Gaussian processes on
S
. This approach is used by
27
and
48
;
51
;
43
respectively in the context of Poisson and temporal Hawkes processes. In fact, since the posterior is non-conjugate here, sampling from the posterior using Monte-Carlo Markov Chain techniques is notoriously intensive.
Note that finding the minimiser in (
5
) is equivalent to maximising the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) defined as

\displaystyle ELBO(Q)=\mathbb{E}_{Q}[\log(L(N|f)\Pi(f))]-\mathbb{E}_{Q}[\log Q(f)].

3
Posterior concentration

3.1
General results

In this section we analyse the asymptotic properties of the posterior distribution as the number of observed sequences
n\to\infty
. Precisely, we establish general concentration rates for the posterior on the intensity function
\lambda_{t,s}(f)
and on the parameter
f

\displaystyle d_{S}(f,f^{\prime}):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{[0,1]}\int_{[0,1]^{d}}|\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f)-\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f^{\prime})|dtds=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\lambda^{i}(f)-\lambda^{i}(f^{\prime})\|_{1},

where
\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f)

4

\lambda^{i}(f)
denotes the corresponding function from

\mathbb{R}^{+}

Then we define the
L_{1}
-distance on the parameter as

\displaystyle\|f-f^{\prime}\|_{1}:=\|\mu-\mu^{\prime}\|_{1}+\|g-g^{\prime}\|_{1},\qquad f,f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}.

Our first result is the contraction of
\Pi(f|N)
on the true parameter
f_{0}
in terms of the stochastic distance, i.e.,

\displaystyle\Pi(d_{S}(f,f_{0})>M\epsilon_{n}|N_{n})\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathbb{P}_{0}}0.

(6)

where
\epsilon_{n}=o(1)
is called the contraction rate and

\bar{\epsilon}_{n}

To include in our theory the case of RBF kernel (see Section

such that
\epsilon_{n}\geq\bar{\epsilon}_{n}

Assumption 2

(Bounded parameter)
.

Recall that
f_{0}=(\mu_{0},g_{0})
. We assume that
\|g_{0}\|_{1}<1
and there exist
\underline{\mu},\bar{\mu},\bar{g}>0

such that for each

\displaystyle\underline{\mu}\leq\mu_{0}(t,s)\leq\bar{\mu}

\displaystyle 0\leq g_{0}(t,s)\leq\bar{g}.

Assumption 3

(Prior mass)
.

Let

\displaystyle B_{\infty}(\bar{\epsilon}_{n})=\{f=(\mu,g);\|\mu-\mu_{0}\|_{\infty}+\|g-g_{0}\|_{\infty}\leq\bar{\epsilon}_{n}\}.

There exists
c_{1}>0
such that
\Pi(B_{\infty}(\bar{\epsilon}_{n}))\geq e^{-c_{1}n\bar{\epsilon}_{n}^{2}}
.

Assumption 4

(Sieves)
.

Let
\Lambda_{0,2}:=\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\int(\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0}))^{2}dtds\right]
and

\displaystyle\kappa:=\frac{4\log 2}{\underline{\mu}}\left\{2+4\left(\frac{\bar{\mu}}{1-\|g_{0}\|_{1}}+\Lambda_{0,2}\right)\right\},

(7)

where
\underline{\mu},\bar{\mu}

\displaystyle C(\zeta_{0}\epsilon_{n},\mathcal{F}_{n},\|.\|_{1})\leq e^{c_{3}n\epsilon_{n}^{2}}.

Proposition 2

(Concentration in stochastic distance)

Under Assumptions

,
3
, and

3.2
Application to Gaussian process priors and Hölder smooth functions

Recall our GP-based prior construction from Section
2.2
with latent functions
\nu,\phi
, link function
\sigma
and covariance functions (kernels)
k_{\nu},k_{\phi}
. We demonstrate that under mild assumptions on
\sigma
and the kernels that Assumptions
3
and
4
are verified, and the concentration rate
\epsilon_{n}
is explicit in the smoothness of the true parameter
f_{0}
. Due to their popularity in practical applications, we focus in this section on the Mátern and the squared exponential kernels (defined in (
2

Before stating our assumptions, we introduce some notation. For
\alpha>0
, let
C^{\alpha}(S)
be the space of Hölder
\alpha
-smooth functions, i.e., functions which are
\lfloor\alpha\rfloor
-times differentiable and which
\lfloor\alpha\rfloor
-th derivative is
(\alpha-\lfloor\alpha\rfloor)
-continuous, i.e., for

\displaystyle|f^{\lfloor\alpha\rfloor}(x)-f^{\lfloor\alpha\rfloor}(y)|\leq|x-y|^{\alpha-\lfloor\alpha\rfloor}.

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
we denote by
\mathcal{S}^{\alpha}(S)
the Sobolev space of order
\alpha
, i.e., functions which (weak) derivatives

are squared integrable for any
\|\gamma\|_{1}\leq\alpha

Our first two assumptions are mild regularity and smoothness conditions on

Assumption 5
.

The link function

.

The functions

\mu_{0},g_{0}\in C^{\alpha}(S)

Remark 3.1
.

The softplus and the scaled sigmoid function

(Matérn covariance kernel)

Under Assumptions

,

Remark 3.2
.

Note that the above result is non-adaptive. Obtaining adaptive results for a GP prior with the Matérn kernel is particularly difficult.

Remark 3.3

(Separable kernel)
.

add references to papers using separable kernels in GP

In spatio-temporal data, it is common to choose a kernel function which factorises over temporal and spatial variables as it leads to computational acceleration. For instance, one can use a separable Matérn kernel with hyperparameters
(\tau_{t},\tau_{s})

. Recall the definitions of the tensor product Sobolev spaces:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{\gamma}([0,1])\otimes\mathcal{S}^{\gamma}([0,1]^{d})=\{f\in L_{2}([0,1]^{d+1}):D^{\alpha}f\in L_{2}([0,1]^{d+1}),\quad\forall\alpha,\|\alpha\|_{\infty}\leq\gamma\}.

In contrast to the Sobolev space

which requires that the (partial) derivatives of order

, which is a strictly stronger condition. Therefore,

(

. In fact,

contains functions that are finite linear combinations of product of functions in

and
\mathcal{S}^{\gamma}([0,1]^{d})
, i.e.,

\displaystyle f\in H^{\gamma}([0,1])\otimes H^{\gamma}([0,1]^{d})\iff f=\sum_{j}f_{tj}(t)f_{sj}(s).

In other words,

\sigma
is
L
-Lipschitz, there exists
L_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle\Pi_{g}(\|g-g_{0,n}\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon_{n}/4)\geq\Pi_{\phi}(\|\phi-\phi_{0,n}\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon_{n}/(4L))\geq e^{-L_{2}n\epsilon_{n}^{2}},

with
\Pi_{\phi}
the GP prior with Matérn kernel on
[0,a]\times[0,b]^{d}
and
\epsilon_{n}
as before.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\Pi(B_{\infty}(\epsilon_{n}))

\displaystyle\geq\Pi_{\nu}(\|\nu-\nu_{0}\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon_{n}/(2L))\Pi_{\phi}(\|\phi-\phi_{0,n}\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon_{n}/(4L))

\displaystyle\geq\Pi_{\nu}(\|\nu-\nu_{0}\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon_{n}/(4L))\Pi_{\phi}(\|\phi-\phi_{0,n}\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon_{n}/(4L))

\displaystyle\geq e^{-(L_{1}+L_{2})n\epsilon_{n}^{2}},

which demonstrates Assumption
3
with
c_{1}:=L_{1}+L_{2}
and
\bar{\epsilon}_{n}=\epsilon_{n}\asymp n^{-\tau/(2\tau+d+1)}
.

We now prove that Assumption
4
holds. For

, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{n}=M_{1}\epsilon_{n}\mathbb{B}_{1}+M_{2}\sqrt{n}\epsilon_{n}\mathbb{H}_{1},

with

are the unit balls respectively in
L_{\infty}(S)
and in
\mathcal{H}_{MAT}^{\tau}
(w.r.t. the corresponding Sobolev norm). Using Lemma B.2 in
15
, for any
\epsilon_{n}\to 0
such that
\epsilon_{n}\gtrsim n^{-\tau/(2\tau+d+1)}
and

, there exists
M_{1},M_{2}>0
and

such that

\displaystyle\Pi_{\nu}(\mathcal{M}_{n}^{c})\leq e^{-R_{1}n\epsilon_{n}^{2}}

\displaystyle\log C(\epsilon_{n},\mathcal{M}_{n},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\leq R_{2}n\epsilon_{n}^{2}.

(16)

Similarly we construct

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{n}=M_{1}\epsilon_{n}\mathbb{\bar{B}}_{1}+M_{2}\sqrt{n}\epsilon_{n}\mathbb{\bar{H}}_{1},

with
\mathbb{\bar{B}}_{1},\mathbb{\bar{H}}_{1}
the unit balls respectively in

and to the analog of

\displaystyle\Pi_{\phi}(\mathcal{N}_{n}^{c})\leq e^{-R_{1}n\epsilon_{n}^{2}}

(17)

\displaystyle\log C(\epsilon_{n},\mathcal{N}_{n},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\leq R_{2}n\epsilon_{n}^{2}.

(18)

We then construct the sieves as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{n}=\sigma(\mathcal{M}_{n})\times\sigma(\mathcal{N}_{n}).

We obtain

\displaystyle\Pi(\mathcal{F}_{n})=\Pi_{\mu}(\sigma(\mathcal{M}_{n}))\Pi_{g}(\sigma(\mathcal{N}_{n}))

\displaystyle\geq\Pi_{\nu}(\mathcal{M}_{n})\Pi_{\phi}(\mathcal{N}_{n})

\displaystyle\geq(1-e^{-R_{1}n\epsilon_{n}^{2}})^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 1-2e^{-R_{1}n\epsilon_{n}^{2}}\geq 1-e^{-\frac{R_{1}}{2}n\epsilon_{n}^{2}}.

Therefore, choosing

4
. Moreover, since

-Lipschitz,

\displaystyle C(\epsilon,\mathcal{F}_{n},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})

Therefore, using (

\displaystyle C(\epsilon_{n},\mathcal{F}_{n},\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\leq e^{2R_{2}L^{2}n\epsilon_{n}^{2}},

which proves the second part of Assumption
4
with
c_{3}:=2R_{2}L^{2}
and
\epsilon_{n}\asymp n^{-\tau/(2\tau+d+1)}
.

\displaystyle\Pi_{\nu}(\|\nu-\nu_{0}\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon_{n}/(2L))\geq e^{-\frac{n}{4L^{2}}\epsilon_{n}^{2}}.

5
Conclusion

The results of this work advance the theoretical understanding of Bayesian nonparametric inference for point processes by establishing posterior contraction rates for multivariate, non-stationary spatio-temporal Hawkes processes. By extending existing analyses for temporal Hawkes processes to the general spatio-temporal setting, we provide the first theoretical guarantees for Bayesian nonparametric learning of self-exciting mechanisms where both background and triggering components evolve across time and space. The framework is broadly relevant to applications involving complex dependency structures, such as seismic activity, neural spike trains, social or financial interactions, and information diffusion on networks, where events exhibit both temporal and spatial excitation.

Under mild regularity conditions on the true intensity and the prior, we show that flexible priors, particularly hierarchical Gaussian processes with squared-exponential kernel yield asymptotically optimal concentration rates. Future work could extend these results to multivariate or nonlinear Hawkes processes, where interactions among multiple latent components or nonlinear excitation effects introduce new theoretical and computational challenges. Additionally, the inclusion of covariates would be an interesting direction as it would enhance the spatial learning.

A.1
Bounds on stochastic distance

The next two lemmas provide lower and upper bounds on the stochastic distance using the
L_{1}
-norm, on the high probability event
\Omega_{n}
(defined in Lemma
4.1
).

Lemma A.1
.

On
\Omega_{n}
, for any
f,f^{\prime}
,

\displaystyle d_{S}(f,f^{\prime})\leq N_{0}\|f-f^{\prime}\|_{1}.

with
N_{0}=\bar{\mu}+\|g_{0}\|_{1}+1
.

Proof.

\displaystyle d_{S}(f,f^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}\int|\lambda^{i}_{f}(t,s)-\lambda^{i}_{f^{\prime}}(t,s)|dtds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}\int|\mu(t,s)-\mu^{\prime}(t,s)|dtds+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}\int\left|\sum_{t_{j}<t}g(t-t_{j},s-s_{j})-g^{\prime}(t-t_{j},s-s_{j})\right|dtds

\displaystyle\leq\|\mu(t,s)-\mu^{\prime}(t,s)\|_{1}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}\sum_{t_{j}<t}\int\left|g(t-t_{j},s-s_{j})-g^{\prime}(t-t_{j},s-s_{j})\right|dtds

\displaystyle\leq\|\mu(t,s)-\mu^{\prime}(t,s)\|_{1}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}\sum_{t_{j}<t}\|g-g^{\prime}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mu-\mu^{\prime}\|_{1}+\|g-g^{\prime}\|_{1}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}N^{i}[0,1]^{d+1}

\displaystyle\leq N_{0}(\|\mu-\mu^{\prime}\|_{1}+\|g-g^{\prime}\|_{1})=N_{0}\|f-f^{\prime}\|_{1},

with
N_{0}=\bar{\mu}+\|g_{0}\|_{1}+1
on
\Omega_{n}
.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

For any
f\in\mathcal{F}
, on
\Omega_{n}
,

\displaystyle-d_{S}(f,f_{0})+\|\mu_{0}\|_{1}+\|g_{0}\|_{1}\frac{e_{0}}{2}\leq\|\mu\|_{1}+\|g\|_{1}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}N^{i}(S)}{n}\leq\|\mu_{0}\|_{1}+\frac{3e_{0}}{2}\|g_{0}\|_{1}+d_{S}(f,f_{0})

(19)

Proof.

On one hand,

\displaystyle d_{S}(f,f_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{[0,1]}\int_{[0,1]^{d}}|\mu(t,x)-\mu_{0}(t,x)|dtdx+\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}N^{i}(S)}{n}\int_{[0,a]}\int_{[0,b]}|g(t,x)-g_{0}(t,x)|dtdx

\displaystyle\leq\|\mu-\mu_{0}\|_{1}+\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}N^{i}(S)}{n}\|g-g_{0}\|_{1}

(20)

with

\displaystyle\|\mu-\mu_{0}\|_{1}=\int_{[0,1]}\int_{[0,1]^{d}}|\mu(t,x)-\mu_{0}(t,x)|dtdx

\displaystyle\|g-g_{0}\|_{1}=\int_{[0,a]}\int_{[0,b]}|g(t,x)-g_{0}(t,x)|dtdx.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle d_{S}(f,f_{0})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{n}\left|\sum_{i}\int_{[0,1]}\int_{[0,1]^{d}}(\lambda^{i}_{t,x}(f)-\lambda^{i}_{t,x}(f_{0}))dtdx\right|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{n}\left|\sum_{i}(\|\mu\|_{1}+N^{i}(S)\|g\|_{1}-\|\mu_{0}\|_{1}-N^{i}(S)\|g_{0}\|_{1}\right|

from which we deduce that

\displaystyle-d_{S}(f,f_{0})+\|\mu_{0}\|_{1}+\|g_{0}\|_{1}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}N^{i}(S)}{n}\leq\|\mu\|_{1}+\|g\|_{1}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}N^{i}(S)}{n}\leq\|\mu_{0}\|_{1}+\|g_{0}\|_{1}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}N^{i}(S)}{n}+d_{S}(f,f_{0})

The previous inequality basically implies that if
d_{S}(f,f_{0})
is small, then also the
L_{1}
-norm of
(\mu,g)
is bounded by the
L_{1}
-norm of the true parameter, provided that
\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}N^{i}(S)}{n}
is concentrated around its expectation. On
\Omega_{n}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\underline{\mu}}{1-\|g_{0}\|_{1}}-\delta_{n}\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}N^{i}[0,1]^{d+1}\leq\frac{\bar{\mu}}{1-\|g_{0}\|_{1}}+\delta_{n},

thus with
e_{0}=\frac{\bar{\mu}}{1-\|g_{0}\|_{1}}
and
n
large enough, we obtain (
19
).

∎

A.2
Bernstein inequalities

The next two lemmas are two useful versions of Bernstein inequalities for point processes.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
N=(N^{i})_{i=1,\dots,n}

n
i.i.d. spatio-temporal Hawkes point processes on
[0,1]\times[0,1]^{d}
with parameter
f
satisfying Assumption
2
. Let
(S_{i})_{i=1,\dots,n}\subset[0,1]^{d+1}
possibly random independent subsets and
v>0
a deterministic constant such that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Lambda^{i}(S_{i})=\sum_{i}\int_{S_{1,i}}\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f)dtds\leq v,

where
\Lambda^{i}(S_{i})=\int_{S_{i}}\lambda_{t,s}^{i}(f)dtds
. Then for any
x>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}N^{i}(S_{i})-\Lambda^{i}(S_{i})\geq\sqrt{2vx}+\frac{x}{3}\right)\leq e^{-x},

(21)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}N^{i}(S_{i})-\Lambda^{i}(S_{i})\leq-\sqrt{2vx}-\frac{x}{3}\right)\leq e^{-x},

(22)

where
\Lambda^{i}(S_{i})=\int_{S_{i}}\lambda_{t,s}^{i}(f)dtds
.

Proof.

We prove the bound (
21
) on the right tail probability. The left tail probability (
22
) can be proven following the same strategy. This proof is structured in three main steps: 1) an exponential moment for a re-centered version of
\sum_{i}N^{i}(S_{i})
is established; 2) The Chernoff inequality is used to bound the tail probability; 3) a lower bound for any small enough value of the free parameter is applied to obtain the result.

Step 1

Let

\displaystyle E:=e^{\theta\sum_{i}(N^{i}(S_{i})-\Lambda^{i}(S_{i}))-\phi(\theta)\sum_{i}\Lambda^{i}(S_{i})}

with
\phi(x)=e^{u}-u-1
and for any
\theta>0
. We will prove that
\mathbb{E}[E]\leq 1
where we use the shortened notation
\mathbb{E}:=\mathbb{E}_{f}
. Firstly, since the variables

\displaystyle\theta(N^{i}(S_{i})-\Lambda^{i}(S_{i}))-\phi(\theta)\Lambda^{i}(S_{i})

are stochastically independent, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[E]=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[e^{\theta(N^{i}(S_{i})-\Lambda^{i}(S_{i}))-\phi(\theta)\Lambda^{i}(S_{i})}].

Moreover, since
\phi(\theta)\geq\frac{\theta^{2}}{2}+\frac{\theta^{3}}{6}=\frac{\theta^{2}}{2}(1+\frac{\theta}{3})
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[e^{\theta(N^{i}(S_{i})-\Lambda^{i}(S_{i}))-\phi(\theta)\Lambda^{i}(S_{i})}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[e^{\theta(N^{i}(S_{i})-\Lambda^{i}(S_{i}))-\frac{\theta^{2}}{2}(1+\frac{\theta}{3})\Lambda^{i}(S_{i})}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[e^{\theta(N^{i}(S_{i})-\Lambda^{i}(S_{i})(1+\frac{\theta}{2}(1+\frac{\theta}{3})))}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[e^{\theta(N^{i}(S_{i})-\Lambda^{i}(S_{i}))}]\mathbb{E}[e^{-\Lambda^{i}(S_{i})\frac{\theta^{2}}{2}(1+\frac{\theta}{3}))}]

(23)

using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in the last inequality. Under Assumption
2
,
N^{i}
is non-explosive and we can easily prove that it admits exponential moments. To see this, first observe that

\displaystyle N^{i}(S_{i})-\Lambda^{i}(S_{i})\leq N^{i}([0,1]^{d+1})-\Lambda^{i}([0,1]^{d+1})

and that
N^{i}([0,1]^{d+1})
is stochastically bounded by
\bar{N}([0,1])
where
\bar{N}
is a temporal point process with constant background
\bar{\mu}
, temporal kernel
\bar{g}(t)=\int_{s}g(t,s)ds
and compensator
\bar{\Lambda}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[e^{\theta(N^{i}(S_{i})-\Lambda^{i}(S_{i}))}]\leq\mathbb{E}[e^{\theta(N^{i}([0,1]^{d+1})-\Lambda^{i}([0,1]^{d+1}))}]\leq\mathbb{E}[e^{\theta(\bar{N}([0,1])-\bar{\Lambda}([0,1]))}].

By Theorem 2 in
3
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[e^{\theta(\bar{N}([0,1])-\bar{\Lambda}([0,1]))}]\leq 1,

which therefore implies that
\mathbb{E}[e^{\theta(N^{i}(S_{i})-\Lambda^{i}(S_{i}))}]\leq 1
. Moreover
\Lambda_{i}(S_{i})\geq 0
and
\theta>0
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[e^{-\theta\Lambda^{i}(S_{i})(1+\frac{\theta}{2}(1+\frac{\theta}{3}))}]\leq 1.

In light of (
23
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[e^{\theta(N^{i}(S_{i})-\Lambda^{i}(S_{i}))-\phi(\theta)\Lambda^{i}(S_{i})}]\leq 1,

and thus that
\mathbb{E}[E]\leq 1
as we wished to prove.

Step 2

Using the standard Chernoff inequality, for any
x>0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\theta\sum_{i}(N^{i}(S_{i})-\Lambda^{i}(S_{i}))-\phi(\theta)\sum_{i}\Lambda^{i}(S_{i})\geq x\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(E\geq e^{x}\right)\leq\mathbb{E}[E]e^{-x}\leq e^{-x}.

Additionally since
\phi(\theta)\leq\frac{\theta^{2}}{2(1-\theta/3)}
and by assumption,
\sum_{i}\Lambda^{i}(S_{i})\leq v
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\theta\sum_{i}(N^{i}(S_{i})-\Lambda^{i}(S_{i}))-\phi(\theta)\sum_{i}\Lambda^{i}(S_{i})\geq x\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i}N^{i}(S_{i})-\Lambda^{i}(S_{i})\geq\theta^{-1}(x+\phi(\theta)\sum_{i}\Lambda^{i}(S_{i}))\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i}N^{i}(S_{i})-\Lambda^{i}(S_{i})\geq\theta^{-1}x+\frac{\theta}{2(1-\theta/3)}v\right),

and therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i}N^{i}(S_{i})-\Lambda^{i}(S_{i})\geq\theta^{-1}x+\frac{\theta}{2(1-\theta/3)}v\right)\leq e^{-x}.

(24)

Step 3.

Note that the bound in (
24
) is valid for any
\theta>0
. But for any
\theta\in(0,3)
, we have

\displaystyle\theta^{-1}(x+\phi(\theta)v)\geq\sqrt{2vx}+\frac{x}{3}.

Therefore, together with (
24
) we can conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i}N^{i}(S_{i})-\Lambda^{i}(S_{i})\geq\sqrt{2vx}+\frac{x}{3}\right)\leq e^{-x}.

∎

Lemma A.4
.

Let
N=(N^{i})_{i=1,\dots,n}

n
i.i.d. spatio-temporal Hawkes point processes on
[0,1]\times[0,1]^{d}
with parameter
f
satisfying Assumption
2
. Let
(S_{i})_{i=1,\dots,n}\subset[0,1]^{d+1}
possibly random independent subsets. Then for any
x>0
, there exists
\sigma^{2},b>0
constants independent of
n
and
(S_{i})_{i}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}N^{i}(S_{i})-\Lambda^{i}(S_{i})\geq x\right)\leq e^{-\frac{nx^{2}}{2(\sigma^{2}+bx)}},

(25)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}N^{i}(S_{i})-\Lambda^{i}(S_{i})\leq-x\right)\leq e^{-\frac{nx^{2}}{2(\sigma^{2}+bx)}}.

(26)

Proof.

This proof is based on the moment version of the standard Bernstein inequality, together with the fact that
N^{i}
admits exponential moments, extending a result from
16
.

We first recall the standard Bernstein inequalities with the moment assumption: let
(Z_{i})_{i}
i.i.d. and centered random variables and
b,\sigma^{2}>0
such that for any
i\in[n]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Z_{i}^{2}]\leq\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Z_{i}^{k}]\leq\frac{1}{2}k!b^{k-2}\sigma^{2},\quad k\geq 2.

Then it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}Z_{i}\geq x\right)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{nx^{2}}{2(\sigma^{2}+bx)}\right)

(27)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}Z_{i}\leq-x\right)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{nx^{2}}{2(\sigma^{2}+bx)}\right).

(28)

Our goal is to apply the previous inequalities to
Z_{i}:=N^{i}(S_{i})-\Lambda^{i}(S_{i})
. By definition of
\Lambda^{i}
,
\mathbb{E}[N^{i}(S_{i})-\Lambda^{i}(S_{i})]=0
. Moreover since
S_{i}\subset[0,1]\times[0,1]^{d}
and using Proposition 2 in
16
, there exist constant
\theta,C>0
that can only depend on
f
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[e^{\theta_{i}N^{i}(S_{i})}]\leq\mathbb{E}[e^{\theta N^{i}([0,1]^{d+1})}]\leq C,

which, since
\Lambda(S_{i})\geq 0
, implies that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[e^{\theta_{i}(N^{i}(S_{i})-\Lambda(S_{i}))}]\leq C,

meaning that
N^{i}(S_{i})-\Lambda(S_{i})
admits exponential moments. From this we can deduce that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Z_{i}^{2}]\leq\frac{2}{\theta}\mathbb{E}[e^{\theta N^{i}(S_{i})}]\leq\frac{2C}{\theta}=:\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Z_{i}^{k}]\leq\frac{k!}{\theta^{k}}\mathbb{E}[e^{\theta N^{i}(S_{i})}]\leq\frac{k!C}{\theta^{k}}\leq\frac{1}{2}k!\sigma^{2}\frac{1}{\theta^{k-2}}=:\frac{1}{2}k!\sigma^{2}b^{k-2},

with
b:=\frac{1}{\theta}
. Therefore, applying the Bernstein’s inequality (
27
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}N^{i}(S_{i})-\Lambda^{i}(S_{i})\geq x\right)\leq e^{-\frac{nx}{2(\sigma^{2}+bx)}}.

Similarly, applying (
27
) we obtain the left tail probability in (
26
).
∎

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}\bar{N}^{i}[0,1]\geq\frac{\bar{\mu}}{1-\|g_{0}\|_{1}}+\delta_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}\bar{N}^{i}[0,1]-\mathbb{E}_{0}[\bar{N}^{i}[0,1]]\geq\delta_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\frac{n\delta_{n}^{2}}{2(\sigma^{2}+b\delta_{n})}}\leq e^{-\delta_{0}\log n/(4\sigma^{2})}=n^{-\delta_{0}/(4\sigma^{2})}\leq n^{-\alpha}

for
n
large enough and
\delta_{0}\geq 4\sigma^{2}\alpha
. Similarly,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}N^{i}[0,1]^{d+1}\leq\frac{\underline{\mu}}{1-\|g_{0}\|_{1}}-\delta_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}\underline{N}^{i}[0,1]\leq\frac{\underline{\mu}}{1-\|g_{0}\|_{1}}-\delta_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}\underline{N}^{i}[0,1]-\mathbb{E}_{0}[\underline{N}^{i}[0,1]]\leq-\delta_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\frac{n\delta_{n}^{2}}{2(\sigma^{2}+b\delta_{n})}}\leq e^{-\delta_{0}\log n/(4\sigma^{2})}=n^{-\delta_{0}/(4\sigma^{2})}\leq n^{-\alpha}.

Thus we can conclude that
\mathbb{P}_{0}(\Omega_{2}^{c})\leq 2n^{-\alpha}
which leads to

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{0}(\Omega_{n}^{c})\leq\mathbb{P}_{0}(\Omega_{1}^{c})+\mathbb{P}_{0}(\Omega_{2}^{c})\leq 3n^{-\alpha}.

∎

B.2
Proof of Lemma
4.2

Lemma B.2

(Kullback-Leibler)
.

Under Assumption
2
and if
\bar{\epsilon}_{n}=o((\log n)^{-2})
, there exist
b_{1},b_{2}>0
such that for any
f\in B_{\infty}(\bar{\epsilon}_{n})
,

\displaystyle KL(f,f_{0}):=\mathbb{E}_{0}[\log L(N|f_{0})-\log L(N|f)]\leq\kappa n\bar{\epsilon}_{n}^{2}(1+o(1))

(29)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{0}(\log L(N|f_{0})-\log L(N|f)>b_{1}n\bar{\epsilon}_{n}^{2})\leq\frac{b_{2}}{n\bar{\epsilon}_{n}^{2}},

(30)

with

\displaystyle\kappa:=\frac{4\log 2}{\underline{\mu}}\left\{2+4\left(\frac{\bar{\mu}}{1-\|g_{0}\|_{1}}+\Lambda_{0,2}\right)\right\}.

(31)

Proof.

We first prove the first statement (
29
). This proof is organised in 3 main steps. In the first step, we re-write the KL divergence as a single integral over the true conditional intensity measure and decompose it into two terms, considering the high-probability event
\Omega_{n}
and its complement
\Omega_{n}^{c}
. In the second and third steps, we control each of these terms. The first term can be controlled by the squared
\ell_{2}
-distance between the conditional intensities
\lambda_{t,s}^{1}(f)
and
\lambda_{t,s}^{1}(f_{0})
, which itself can be controlled by the squared
\ell_{2}
-distance on the parameter, i.e,
\|f-f_{0}\|_{2}^{2}
. We then control the second term using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and a bound on the fourth-moment of
N
. In the following, when we compute integrals over the spatio-temporal domain
[0,1]\times[0,1]^{d}
, we omit the bounds in the integral for ease of notation. We also note that since the domain is the
(d+1)
-dimensional hypercube, we use multiple times that
\int 1dtds=1
.

Let
f\in B_{\infty}(f_{0},\epsilon_{T})
. We have

\displaystyle KL(f,f_{0})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{0}[\log L(N|f_{0})-\log L(N|f)]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\sum_{i}\int-\log\frac{\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f)}{\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0})}dN_{t,s}^{i}+\int(\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f)-\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0}))dtds\right]

\displaystyle=n\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\int-\log\frac{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f)}{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})}\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})dtds+\int(\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f)-\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0}))dtds\right]

\displaystyle=n\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\int\psi(\frac{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f)}{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})})\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})dtds\right]

\displaystyle=n\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{n}}\int\psi(\frac{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f)}{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})})\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})dtds\right]}_{=:I_{1}}+n\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{n}^{c}}\int\psi(\frac{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f)}{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})})\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})dtds\right]}_{=:I_{2}},

where in the third equality we have defined
\psi(x)=-\log x+x-1\geq 0,x>0
.

Bound on
I_{1}

We use that
\psi(x)\leq-4\log(r)(x-1)^{2}
for any
x\geq r
and
r>0
. We thus find a lower bound
r
on the ratio
\frac{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f)}{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})}
on the event
\Omega_{n}
. First note that

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f)}{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f)-\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})}{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})}\geq 1-\frac{|\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f)-\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})|}{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})}.

We upper bound
\frac{|\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f)-\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})|}{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})}
using Assumption
2
and on
\Omega_{n}
. Since
f\in B_{\infty}(f_{0},\bar{\epsilon}_{n})
and
f_{0}
verifies Assumption
2
,

\displaystyle\mu(t,s)\geq\mu_{0}(t,s)-\|\mu-\mu_{0}\|_{\infty}\geq\underline{\mu}-\bar{\epsilon}_{n}\geq\underline{\mu}/2,

for any
t,s
and any
n
large enough, which implies that

\displaystyle\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f)\geq\mu(t,s)\geq\underline{\mu}/2.

(32)

Similarly we have

\displaystyle\mu(t,s)\leq\mu_{0}(t,s)+\|\mu-\mu_{0}\|_{\infty}\leq\bar{\mu}+\bar{\epsilon}_{n}\leq\bar{\mu}+1.

(33)

Moreover, on
\Omega_{n}
,
\sup_{i}\sup_{t\in[0,1]}N^{i}[t,t-A]\leq c_{\alpha}\log n
, therefore,

\displaystyle\left|\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})-\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\mu_{0}(t,s)-\mu(t,s)+\sum_{t_{i}^{1}\leq t}(g_{0}-g)(t-t_{i},s-s_{i})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\mu_{0}-\mu\|_{\infty}+\|g_{0}-g\|_{\infty}N^{1}(t,t-a)

\displaystyle\leq\|\mu_{0}-\mu\|_{\infty}+c_{\alpha}\|g_{0}-g\|_{\infty}\log n.

(34)

Since
f\in B_{\infty}(f_{0},\bar{\epsilon}_{n})
, this implies that

\displaystyle\frac{|\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f)-\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})|}{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\mu_{0}-\mu\|_{\infty}+c_{\alpha}\|g_{0}-g\|_{\infty}\log n}{\underline{\mu}}\leq\frac{\epsilon_{n}(1+c_{\alpha}\log n)}{\underline{\mu}}\leq\frac{1}{2},

for
n
large enough, using that by assumption
\bar{\epsilon}_{n}=o((\log n)^{-1})
, and thus,

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f)}{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})}\geq\frac{1}{2}.

(35)

Thus, with
r=\frac{1}{2}
, we obtain that
\psi(\frac{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f)}{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})})\leq 4\log(2)(\frac{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f)}{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})}-1)^{2}
which leads to

\displaystyle I_{1}=\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{n}}\int\psi(\frac{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f)}{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})})\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})dtds\right]

\displaystyle\leq 4\log(2)\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\int\left(\frac{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f)}{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})}-1\right)^{2}\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})dtds\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\log 2}{\underline{\mu}}\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\int(\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f)-\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0}))^{2}dtds\right],

under Assumption
2
. Using that
(x+y)^{2}\leq 2x^{2}+2y^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\int(\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f)-\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0}))^{2}dtds\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\mu-\mu_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+2\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\int\left(\sum_{t_{i}^{1}<t}g(t-t_{i}^{1},s-s_{i}^{1})-g_{0}(t-t_{i}^{1},s-s_{i}^{1})\right)^{2}dtds\right]

\displaystyle=2\|\mu-\mu_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+2\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\int\left(\int_{u:u\in[t-a,t)}\int_{v:\|v-s\|\leq b}(g(t-u,s-v)-g_{0}(t-u,s-v))dN^{1}_{u,v}\right)^{2}dtds\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\mu-\mu_{0}\|_{2}^{2}

(36)

\displaystyle\qquad+4\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\int\left(\int_{u:u\in[t-a,t)}\int_{v:\|v-s\|\leq b}(g(t-u,s-v)-g_{0}(t-u,s-v))(dN^{1}_{u,v}-\lambda^{1}_{u,v}(f_{0})dudv)\right)^{2}dtds\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+4\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\int\left(\int_{u:u\in[t-a,t)}\int_{v:\|v-s\|\leq b}(g(t-u,s-v)-g_{0}(t-u,s-v))\lambda^{1}_{u,v}(f_{0})dudv\right)^{2}dtds\right]

(37)

To bound the second and third terms in the RHS of (
37
), we will use the following identity (see, e.g., Theorem B12 in
21
): for any deterministic and squared integrable function
h
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\int\left(\int_{u:u\in[t-a,t)}\int_{v:\|v-s\|\leq b}h(t-u,s-v)(dN^{1}_{u,v}-\lambda^{1}_{u,v}(f_{0})dudv)\right)^{2}dtds\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\int\int_{u:u\in[t-a,t)}\int_{v:\|v-s\|\leq b}h^{2}(t-u,s-v)\lambda^{1}_{u,v}(f_{0})dudvdtds\right]

\displaystyle=\int\int_{u:u\in[t-a,t)}\int_{v:\|v-s\|\leq b}h^{2}(t-u,s-v)\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\lambda^{1}_{u,v}(f_{0})\right]dudvdtds.

(38)

We will also use an upper bound on
\max_{u,v}\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\lambda^{1}_{u,v}(f_{0})\right]
. Note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{0}[\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})]

\displaystyle=\mu_{0}(t,s)+\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\int_{u:u\in[t-a,t)}\int_{v:\|v-s\|\leq b}g_{0}(t-u,s-v)dN^{1}_{u,v}\right]

\displaystyle=\mu_{0}(t,s)+\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\int_{u:u\in[t-a,t)}\int_{v:\|v-s\|\leq b}g_{0}(t-u,s-v)\lambda^{1}_{u,v}(f_{0})dudv\right]

\displaystyle\leq\bar{\mu}+\|g_{0}\|_{1}\max_{u,v}\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\lambda^{1}_{u,v}(f_{0})\right],

which implies

\displaystyle\max_{t,s}\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})\right]\leq\frac{\bar{\mu}}{1-\|g_{0}\|_{1}}<\infty,

(39)

under Assumption
2
.
Using (
38
) with
h=g-g_{0}
and (
39
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\int\left(\int_{u:u\in[t-a,t)}\int_{v:\|v-s\|\leq b}(g(t-u,s-v)-g_{0}(t-u,s-v))(dN^{1}_{u,v}-\lambda^{1}_{u,v}(f_{0})dudv)\right)^{2}dtds\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\|g-g_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\frac{\bar{\mu}}{1-\|g_{0}\|_{1}}.

Moreover by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\int\left(\int(g-g_{0})(t-u,s-v)\lambda^{1}_{u,v}(f_{0})dudv\right)^{2}dtds\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\int\left(\int(g-g_{0})^{2}(t-u,s-v)dvdu\int(\lambda^{1}_{u,v}(f_{0}))^{2}dvdu\right)dtds\right]

\displaystyle\leq\|g-g_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\int\int(\lambda^{1}_{u,v}(f_{0}))^{2}dvdudtds\right]=\|g-g_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\Lambda_{0,2},

with

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0,2}:=\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\int(\lambda^{1}_{u,v}(f_{0}))^{2}dvdu\right].

We claim that
\Lambda_{0,2}<\infty
, since

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0,2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\mu_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+2\|g_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[(N[0,1]^{d+1})^{2}\right],

\|\mu_{0}\|_{2}^{2}<\infty
under Assumption
2
, and
\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[(N[0,1]^{d+1})^{2}\right]<\infty
. The existence of the second moments of
N[0,1]^{d+1}
comes from the fact that a spatio-temporal point process can be seen as a marked temporal point process (TPP) and any non-explosive TPP admits exponential moments on a finite domain, which implies that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[(N^{1}[0,1]^{d+1})^{k}\right]<\infty,\quad k>0.

(40)

Hence, given (
37
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\int(\lambda^{1}_{t,x}(f)-\lambda^{1}_{t,x}(f_{0}))^{2}dtdx\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\mu-\mu_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+4\left(\frac{\bar{\mu}}{1-\|g_{0}\|_{1}}+\Lambda_{0,2}\right)\|g-g_{0}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\{2+4\left(\frac{\bar{\mu}}{1-\|g_{0}\|_{1}}+\Lambda_{0,2}\right)\right\}\|f-f_{0}\|_{2}^{2},

(41)

and thus,

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\log 2}{\underline{\mu}}\left\{2+4\left(\frac{\bar{\mu}}{1-\|g_{0}\|_{1}}+\Lambda_{0,2}\right)\right\}\|f-f_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{4\log 2}{\underline{\mu}}\left\{2+4\left(\frac{\bar{\mu}}{1-\|g_{0}\|_{1}}+\Lambda_{0,2}\right)\right\}\bar{\epsilon}_{n}^{2}=\kappa\bar{\epsilon}_{n}^{2},

using that since
f\in B_{\infty}(f_{0},\bar{\epsilon}_{n})
,
\|f-f_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|f-f_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq\bar{\epsilon}_{n}^{2}
and with

\displaystyle\kappa=\frac{4\log 2}{\underline{\mu}}\left\{2+4\left(\frac{\bar{\mu}}{1-\|g_{0}\|_{1}}+\Lambda_{0,2}\right)\right\}.

We now prove that
I_{2}=o(\bar{\epsilon}_{n}^{2})
. One one hand, under Assumption
2
and using (
33
),

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})}{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f)}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})-\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f)}{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f)}\leq 1+\frac{|\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f)-\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})|}{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f)}\leq 1+2\frac{\|\mu-\mu_{0}\|_{\infty}+\|g-g_{0}\|_{\infty}N[0,1]^{d+1}}{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f)}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\frac{2\bar{\epsilon}_{n}(1+N[0,1]^{d+1})}{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f)}\leq 1+\frac{2\bar{\epsilon}_{n}(1+N[0,1]^{d+1})}{\underline{\mu}},

(42)

and on the other hand,

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f)}{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})}\leq 1+\frac{\bar{\epsilon}_{n}(1+N[0,1]^{d+1})}{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})}\leq 1+\frac{\bar{\epsilon}_{n}(1+N[0,1]^{d+1})}{\underline{\mu}}.

(43)

Thus, using that
\log x\leq x-1
,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{n}^{c}}\int\left\{-\log(\frac{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f)}{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})})+\frac{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f)}{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})}-1\right\}\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})dtds\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{n}^{c}}\int\left\{\log(\frac{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})}{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f)})+\frac{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f)}{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})}-1\right\}\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})dtds\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{n}^{c}}\int\left\{\log(1+\frac{2\epsilon_{n}(1+N^{1}[0,1]^{d+1})}{\underline{\mu}})+\frac{\epsilon_{n}(1+N^{1}[0,1]^{d+1})}{\underline{\mu}}\right\}\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})dtds\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{n}^{c}}\int\left\{\frac{2\epsilon_{n}(1+N^{1}[0,1]^{d+1})}{\underline{\mu}}+\frac{\epsilon_{n}(1+N^{1}[0,1]^{d+1})}{\underline{\mu}}\right\}\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})dtds\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{\bar{\mu}}\bar{\epsilon}_{n}\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{n}^{c}}\int\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})dtds\right]+\frac{3}{\bar{\mu}}\bar{\epsilon}_{n}\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{n}^{c}}N^{1}[0,1]^{d+1}\int\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})dtds\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\bar{\epsilon}_{n}\sqrt{\mathbb{P}_{0}(\Omega_{n}^{c})\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\int(\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0}))^{2}dtds\right]}+C\bar{\epsilon}_{n}\sqrt{\mathbb{P}_{0}(\Omega_{n}^{c})\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[(N^{1}[0,1]^{d+1})^{2}\int(\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})^{2}dtds\right]}

\displaystyle\leq C\bar{\epsilon}_{n}\sqrt{\mathbb{P}_{0}(\Omega_{n}^{c})\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\int(\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0}))^{2}dtds\right]}+C\bar{\epsilon}_{n}\sqrt{\mathbb{P}_{0}(\Omega_{n}^{c})\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[(N^{1}[0,1]^{d+1})^{4}\right]\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\int(\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0}))^{4}dtds\right]}}

with
C=\frac{3}{\bar{\mu}}
and using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in the last two inequalities. Moreover, using Lemma
4.1
,
\mathbb{P}_{0}(\Omega_{n}^{c})\leq n^{-\alpha}
for any
\alpha>0
.
From (
40
), we have
\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[(N^{1}[0,1]^{d+1})^{4}\right]<\infty
and thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[(\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0}))^{4}\right]\leq 8\mu_{0}(t,s)^{4}+8\|g_{0}\|_{\infty}^{4}\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[(N^{1}[0,1]^{d+1})^{4}\right]<\infty.

Thus,
I_{2}=O(n^{-\alpha/2})=o(\bar{\epsilon}_{n}^{2})
for any
\alpha>1
. We therefore conclude that for
n
large enough,

\displaystyle KL(f,f_{0})=n(I_{1}+I_{2})\leq\kappa n\bar{\epsilon}_{n}^{2}(1+o(1)),

which proves the first statement of Lemma
4.2
.

We now prove the second statement. The proof relies on bounding the variance of
\log L(f_{0}|N)-\log L(f|N)
, and applying Chebyshev’s inequality. To bound the variance of
\log L(f_{0}|N)-\log L(f|N)
, we will decompose it on
\Omega_{n}
and on
\Omega_{n}^{c}
. Recall that

\displaystyle\log L(f_{0}|N)-\log L(f|N)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int\log\frac{\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0})}{\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f)}dN_{t,s}^{i}+\int(\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f)-\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0}))dtds.

For any
i\in[n]
, let

\displaystyle Z_{i}:=\int\log\frac{\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0})}{\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f)}dN_{t,s}^{i}+\int(\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f)-\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0}))dtds.

Note that
\mathbb{E}_{0}[Z_{i}]=KL(f,f_{0})
. We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{0}[Z_{i}^{2}]=\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\left(\int\log\frac{\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0})}{\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f)}dN_{t,s}^{i}+\int(\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f)-\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0}))dtds\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\left(\int\log\frac{\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0})}{\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f)}\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0})dtds+\int\log\frac{\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0})}{\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f)}(dN_{t,s}^{i}-\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0})dtds)+\int(\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f)-\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0}))dtds\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\left(\int\psi\left(\frac{\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f)}{\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0})}\right)\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0})dtds+\int\log\frac{\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f)}{\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0})}(dN_{t,s}^{i}-\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0}))dtds)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\left(\int\psi\left(\frac{\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f)}{\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0})}\right)\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0})dtds\right)^{2}\right]+2\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\left(\int\log\frac{\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f)}{\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0})}(dN_{t,s}^{i}-\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0}))dtds)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\int\psi\left(\frac{\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f)}{\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0})}\right)^{2}(\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0}))^{2}dtds\right]+2\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\int(\log\frac{\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f)}{\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0})})^{2}\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0})dtds\right].

using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and (
38
) in the last inequality. We first bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{n}}\int\psi\left(\frac{\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f)}{\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0})}\right)^{2}(\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0}))^{2}dtds\right]\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{n}}\int(\log\frac{\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f)}{\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0})})^{2}\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0})dtds\right]

Recall from (
35
) that on
\Omega_{n}
and for
n
large enough,
\frac{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f)}{\lambda^{1}_{t,s}(f_{0})}\geq\frac{1}{2}
.
Thus, using again that
\psi(x)\leq-\log(r)(x-1)^{2}
for any
x\geq r>0
with
r=\frac{1}{2}
and under Assumption
2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\psi\left(\frac{\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f)}{\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0})}\right)^{2}(\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0}))^{2}\leq(\log 2)^{2}\frac{(\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0})-\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f))^{4}}{\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0})^{2}}\leq\frac{(\log 2)^{2}}{\underline{\mu}^{2}}(\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0})-\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f))^{4}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{n}}\int\psi\left(\frac{\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f)}{\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0})}\right)^{2}(\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0}))^{2}dtds\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(\log 2)^{2}}{\underline{\mu}^{2}}\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{n}}\int(\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0})-\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f))^{4}dtds\right]

Proof.

The main idea is to construct individual test functions

\displaystyle\phi(f_{1}):=\mathds{1}_{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}N^{i}(S_{1,i})-\Lambda^{i,0}(S_{1,i})>v_{n}}\vee\mathds{1}_{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}N^{i}(S_{1,i}^{c})-\Lambda^{i,0}(S_{1,i}^{c})>v_{n}}

) by balls of radius

a mimimal

-covering net of

Moreover,

\displaystyle d_{S}(f_{0},f_{j})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int|\lambda_{t,s}^{i}(f_{j})-\lambda_{t,s}^{i}(f_{0})|dtds

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int|\lambda_{t,s}^{i}(f)-\lambda_{t,s}^{i}(f_{0})|dtds

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int|\lambda_{t,s}^{i}(f_{j})-\lambda_{t,s}^{i}(f)|dtds

\displaystyle\geq j\epsilon_{n}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int|\lambda_{t,s}^{i}(f_{j})-\lambda_{t,s}^{i}(f)|dtds=j\epsilon_{n}-d_{S}(f,f_{j}),

since
f\in\mathcal{F}_{j}
. Besides, using Lemma
A.1
, on
\Omega_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle d_{S}(f,f_{j})\leq N_{0}\|f-f_{j}\|_{1}\leq N_{0}\zeta j\epsilon_{n},

therefore,

\displaystyle d_{S}(f_{0},f_{j})\geq j\epsilon_{n}\left(1-N_{0}\zeta\right)>0,

if
\zeta<N_{0}^{-1}
.

From (
47
) and (
48
), this implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}\Lambda^{i,f}(S_{1,i})-\Lambda^{i,0}(S_{1,i})

\displaystyle\geq-d_{S}(f,f_{j})+\frac{d_{S}(f_{0},f_{j})}{2}

\displaystyle\geq-N_{0}\zeta j\epsilon_{n}+\frac{j\epsilon_{n}(1-N_{0}\zeta)}{2}

\displaystyle\geq(1/2-3N_{0}\zeta/2)j\epsilon_{n}\geq j\epsilon_{n}/4,

by choosing

\displaystyle(1/2-3N_{0}\zeta/2)\geq\frac{1}{4}\iff\zeta\leq\frac{N_{0}}{6}.

Thus, with
v_{n}=nj\epsilon_{n}/8
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{f}[\mathds{1}_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{j}}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{n}}(1-\phi_{1,j}^{+})]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{f}[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{n}}\mathds{1}_{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}N^{i}(S_{1,i})-\Lambda^{i,f}(S_{1,i})<v_{n}-j\epsilon_{n}/4}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{f}[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{n}}\mathds{1}_{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}N^{i}(S_{1,i})-\Lambda^{i,f}(S_{1,i})<(1/8-1/4)j\epsilon_{n}}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{f}[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{n}}\mathds{1}_{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}N^{i}(S_{1,i})-\Lambda^{i,f}(S_{1,i})<-j\epsilon_{n}/8}].

(49)

To bound the RHS we use another form of Bernstein’s inequality stated in Lemma
A.3
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{f}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}N^{i}(S_{1,i})-\Lambda^{i}(S_{1,i})\leq-\sqrt{2vx}-\frac{x}{3}\right)\leq e^{-x},

for any
x>0
and with
v\geq\sum_{i}\int_{S_{1,i}}\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f)dtds=\sum_{i}\Lambda^{i,f}(S_{1,i})
. We first find such
v
. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\Lambda^{i,f}(S_{1,i})=\sum_{i}\int_{S_{1,i}}\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f)dtds\leq\sum_{i}\int\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f)dtds\leq n\|\mu\|_{1}+\|g\|_{1}\sum_{i}N^{i}[0,1]^{d+1}.

Moreover since
f\in\mathcal{F}_{j}
and on
\Omega_{n}
, using Lemma
A.2
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mu\|_{1}+\|g\|_{1}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}N^{i}[0,1]^{d+1}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mu_{0}\|_{1}+\|g_{0}\|_{1}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}N^{i}[0,1]^{d+1}+d_{S}(f,f_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\|\mu_{0}\|_{1}+e_{0}\|g_{0}\|_{1}+\|g_{0}\|_{1}+(j+1)\epsilon_{n}.

(50)

with
e_{0}=\frac{\bar{\mu}}{1-\|g_{0}\|_{1}}
. Thus, letting
C_{0}=\|\mu_{0}\|_{1}+\|g_{0}\|_{1}(e_{0}+1)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\Lambda^{i,f}(S_{1,i})\leq nC_{0}+(j+1)n\epsilon_{n}=:v.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{f}[\mathds{1}_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{j}}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{n}}(1-\phi^{+}(f_{j}))]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{f}[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{n}}\mathds{1}_{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}N^{i}(S_{1,i})-\Lambda^{i,f}(S_{1,i})<-\sqrt{2vx}-\frac{x}{3}}]

(51)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{f}\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}N^{i}(S_{1,i})-\Lambda^{i,f}(S_{1,i})<-\sqrt{2vx}-\frac{x}{3}\right]\leq e^{-x}.

(52)

We distinguish 2 cases:

•

j\epsilon_{n}\leq C_{0}+1
. Then we apply (
52
) with
x=x_{1}nj^{2}\epsilon_{n}^{2}
with

0<x_{1}\leq\frac{3}{56(C_{0}+1)}
. In particular,
x_{1}(C_{0}+1)\leq 1
thus
\sqrt{x_{1}(C_{0}+1)}\leq x_{1}(C_{0}+1)
. Then,

\displaystyle\sqrt{2vx}+\frac{x}{3}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2x_{1}vn}j\epsilon_{n}+\frac{x_{1}nj^{2}\epsilon_{n}^{2}}{3}

\displaystyle=\left(\sqrt{2x_{1}(C_{0}+(j+1)\epsilon_{n})}+\frac{x_{1}j\epsilon_{n}}{3}\right)nj\epsilon_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\left(2\sqrt{x_{1}(C_{0}+1)}+\frac{x_{1}(C_{0}+1)}{3}\right)nj\epsilon_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{7x_{1}(C_{0}+1)}{3}nj\epsilon_{n}\leq\frac{nj\epsilon_{n}}{8}.

Thus, with (
52
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{f}[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{n}}\mathds{1}_{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}N^{i}(S_{1,i})-\Lambda^{i,f}(S_{1,i})<-j\epsilon_{n}/8}]\leq\mathbb{E}_{f}[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{n}}\mathds{1}_{\sum_{i=1}^{n}N^{i}(S_{1,i})-\Lambda^{i,f}(S_{1,i})<-\sqrt{2vx}-\frac{x}{3}}]\leq e^{-nx_{1}j^{2}\epsilon_{n}^{2}}.

•

j\epsilon_{n}>C_{0}+1
. Then we apply (
52
) with
x=x_{0}nj\epsilon_{n}
with

x_{0}\leq\min(\frac{C_{0}+1}{32^{2}},\frac{3}{16})
. In particular
2\sqrt{\frac{x_{0}}{C_{0}+1}}\leq\frac{1}{16}
. We then have

\displaystyle\sqrt{2vx}+\frac{x}{3}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2x_{0}vnj\epsilon_{n}}+\frac{x_{0}nj\epsilon_{n}}{3}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\sqrt{2x_{0}(C_{0}+(j+1)\epsilon_{n})}}{j\epsilon_{n}}+\frac{x_{0}}{3}\right)nj\epsilon_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\left(2\frac{\sqrt{x_{0}}}{\sqrt{j\epsilon_{n}}}+\frac{x_{0}}{3}\right)nj\epsilon_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\left(2\frac{\sqrt{x_{0}}}{\sqrt{C_{0}+1}}+\frac{x_{0}}{3}\right)j\epsilon_{n}\leq\frac{nj\epsilon_{n}}{8}.

Thus, with (
49
) and (
52
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{f}[\mathds{1}_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{j}}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{n}}(1-\phi^{+}(f_{j}))]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{f}[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{n}}\mathds{1}_{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}N^{i}(S_{1,i})-\Lambda^{i,f}(S_{1,i})<-j\epsilon_{n}/8}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{f}[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{n}}\mathds{1}_{\sum_{i=1}^{n}N^{i}(S_{1,i})-\Lambda^{i,f}(S_{1,i})<-\sqrt{2vx}-\frac{x}{3}}]\leq e^{-x_{0}nj\epsilon_{n}}.

Note that the above bounds are independent of
f
and
f_{1}
. Therefore, we have proven that

\displaystyle\sup_{f_{1}\in\mathcal{N}_{j}}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{n},\|f-f_{1}\|\leq\zeta j\epsilon_{n}}\mathbb{E}_{f}[(1-\phi^{+}(f_{j}))\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{n}}\mathds{1}_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{j}}]\leq\begin{cases}e^{-x_{1}nj^{2}\epsilon_{n}^{2}}&\text{ if }j\epsilon_{n}\leq C_{0}+1\\
e^{-x_{0}nj\epsilon_{n}}&\text{ if }j\epsilon_{n}>C_{0}+1\end{cases}.

Now assume that
Case 2
holds. Then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}\int_{S_{1,i}^{c}}(\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{1})-\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0}))dtds\geq\frac{d_{S}(f_{0},f_{1})}{2}.

By applying the same computations with
S_{1,i}
replaced by
S_{1,i}^{c}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{f_{1}\in\mathcal{N}_{j}}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{n},\|f-f_{1}\|\leq\zeta j\epsilon_{n}}\mathbb{E}_{f}[\mathds{1}_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{j}}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{n}}(1-\phi^{-}(f_{j}))]\leq\begin{cases}e^{-x_{1}nj^{2}\epsilon_{n}^{2}}&\text{ if }j\epsilon_{n}\leq C_{0}+1\\
e^{-x_{0}nj\epsilon_{n}}&\text{ if }j\epsilon_{n}>C_{0}+1\end{cases}

Given (
46
), overall this implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq M}\sup_{f_{1}\in\mathcal{N}_{j}}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{n},\|f-f_{1}\|\leq\zeta j\epsilon_{n}}\mathbb{E}_{f}[\mathds{1}_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{j}}(1-\phi(f_{j}))\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{n}}]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{M\leq j\leq(C_{0}+1)\epsilon_{n}^{-1}}e^{-x_{1}^{2}nj^{2}\epsilon_{n}^{2}}+\sum_{j\geq(C_{0}+1)\epsilon_{n}^{2}}e^{-x_{0}nj\epsilon_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\min(x_{0},x_{1})nM\epsilon_{n}^{2}/2}=e^{-b_{2}n\epsilon_{n}^{2}}.

with
b_{2}=\frac{\min(x_{0},x_{1})M}{2}
and
b_{2}>c_{1}
for
M
large enough.

∎

B.4
Proof of Lemma
4.4

Lemma B.4
.

For any
f\in A_{n}^{c}
, there exists a constant
p_{0}>0
such that on
\Omega_{n}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{f}[Z_{1}]\geq p_{0}\|f-f_{0}\|_{1}.

Proof.

Recall that

\displaystyle Z_{1}:=\int_{0}^{t^{1}_{2}}\int_{[0,1]^{d}}|\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f)-\lambda^{i}_{t,s}(f_{0})|dtds,

where
t^{1}_{2}
is the time of the second event or
t_{2}^{1}=1
if there are less than 2 events. Define the event

\displaystyle\Omega_{0}:=\{N^{1}[0,1]^{d+1}=0\},

i.e.,
\Omega_{0}
is the event that the sequence has no events.
Then on
\Omega_{0}
,
t_{2}^{1}=1
and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{f}[Z_{1}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{0}}]=\mathbb{E}_{f}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{0}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{[0,1]^{d}}|\mu(t,s)-\mu_{0}(t,s)|dtds\right]=\|\mu-\mu_{0}\|_{1}\mathbb{P}_{f}[\Omega_{0}].

Moreover, with
\mathbb{Q}
the measure of a homogeneous Poisson point process with unit intensity on
[0,1]^{d+1}
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{f}[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{0}}]=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[\mathcal{L}_{f}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{0}}],

where
\mathcal{L}_{f}
is the likelihood process on
[0,1]^{d+1}
defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{f}=\exp\left(1-\int\lambda_{t,s}^{1}(f)dtds+\int\log(\lambda_{t,s}^{1}(f))dN_{t,s}\right)

On
\Omega_{0}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{f}=\exp\left(1-\int\mu(t,s)dtds\right)\geq\exp\left(1-\|\mu\|_{1}\right).

From (
19
), on
\Omega_{n}
,

\displaystyle\|\mu\|_{1}\leq\|\mu_{0}\|_{1}+\frac{3e_{0}}{2}\|g_{0}\|_{1}+d_{S}(f,f_{0})\leq\|\mu_{0}\|_{1}+\frac{3e_{0}}{2}\|g_{0}\|_{1}+M\epsilon_{n}

since
f\in A_{n}^{c}
. Thus, for
n
large enough, on
\Omega_{0}\cap\Omega_{n}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{f}\geq\exp\left(-\|\mu_{0}\|_{1}-\frac{3e_{0}}{2}\|g_{0}\|_{1}\right)=:\mathcal{L}_{0},

and thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{f}[Z_{1}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{0}}]\geq\mathbb{E}_{f}[Z_{1}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{0}\cap\Omega_{n}}]\geq\mathcal{L}_{0}\|\mu-\mu_{0}\|_{1}\mathbb{Q}(\Omega_{0}).

(53)

Since
\mathbb{Q}
is a homogeneous Poisson process with intensity one,

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}(\Omega_{0})=e^{-1}.

Therefore, we can conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{f}[Z_{1}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{0}}]\geq\mathcal{L}_{0}e^{-1}\|\mu-\mu_{0}\|_{1}.

Now let
\tau\in(0,1-2a)
and define the subspaces
S_{\tau}^{-}=[0,\tau]\times[0,1]^{d}
,
S_{\tau}=[\tau,\tau+a]\times[0,1]^{d}
,
S_{\tau}^{+}=[\tau+a,\tau+2a]\times[0,1]^{d}
and the event:

\displaystyle\Omega_{\tau}=\{N^{1}(S_{\tau}^{-})=0,N^{1}(S_{\tau})=1,N^{1}(S_{\tau}^{+})=0\}.

Note that on
\Omega_{\tau}
,
t_{2}^{1}>t_{1}^{1}+a
.
Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{f}[Z_{1}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\tau}}]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{f}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\tau}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{[0,1]^{d}}|\mu(t,s)-\mu_{0}(t,s)|dtds\right]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}_{f}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\tau}}\int_{t_{1}^{1}}^{t_{1}^{1}+a}\int_{[0,1]^{d}}|\mu(t,s)+g(t-t_{1}^{1},s-s_{1}^{1})-\mu_{0}(t,s)-g_{0}(t-t_{1}^{1},s-s_{1}^{1})|dtds\right]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{f}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\tau}}\int_{t_{1}^{1}}^{t_{1}^{1}+a}\int_{[0,1]^{d}}\left||\mu(t,s)-\mu_{0}(t,s)|-|g(t-t_{1}^{1},s-s_{1}^{1})-g_{0}(t-t_{1}^{1},s-s_{1}^{1})|\right|dtds\right]

(54)

Let first assume that

\displaystyle\|\mu-\mu_{0}\|_{1}\geq\frac{\mathcal{L}_{0}ae^{-2a-\tau}}{2}\|g-g_{0}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\implies\|\mu-\mu_{0}\|_{1}+\frac{\mathcal{L}_{0}ae^{-2a-\tau}}{2}\|\mu-\mu_{0}\|_{1}\geq\frac{\mathcal{L}_{0}ae^{-2a-\tau}}{2}\|f-f_{0}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\implies\|\mu-\mu_{0}\|_{1}\geq\frac{\mathcal{L}_{0}ae^{-2a-\tau}}{2+\mathcal{L}_{0}ae^{-2a-\tau}}\|f-f_{0}\|_{1}.

Then from (
53
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{f}[Z_{1}]\geq\mathbb{E}_{f}[Z_{1}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{0}}]\geq\frac{\mathcal{L}_{0}^{2}ae^{-2a-\tau-1}}{2+\mathcal{L}_{0}ae^{-2a-\tau}}\|f-f_{0}\|_{1}.

In the alternative case where

\displaystyle\|\mu-\mu_{0}\|_{1}<\frac{\mathcal{L}_{0}ae^{-2a-\tau}}{2}\|g-g_{0}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\implies\|f-f_{0}\|_{1}<\|g-g_{0}\|_{1}+\frac{\mathcal{L}_{0}ae^{-2a-\tau}}{2}\|g-g_{0}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\implies\|g-g_{0}\|_{1}\geq\frac{2}{2+\mathcal{L}_{0}ae^{-2a-\tau}}\|f-f_{0}\|_{1},

then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{f}[Z_{1}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\tau}}]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{f}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\tau}}\int_{t_{1}^{1}}^{t_{1}^{1}+a}\int_{[0,1]^{d}}|g(t-t_{1}^{1},s-s_{1}^{1})-g_{0}(t-t_{1}^{1},s-s_{1}^{1})|-|\mu(t,s)-\mu_{0}(t,s)|dtds\right]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{f}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\tau}}\int_{t_{1}^{1}}^{t_{1}^{1}+a}\int_{[0,1]^{d}}|g(t-t_{1}^{1},s-s_{1}^{1})-g_{0}(t-t_{1}^{1},s-s_{1}^{1})|dtds\right]-\|\mu-\mu_{0}\|_{1}\mathbb{E}_{f}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\tau}}\right].

Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{f}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\tau}}\int_{t_{1}^{1}}^{t_{1}^{1}+a}\int_{[0,1]^{d}}|g(t-t_{1}^{1},s-s_{1}^{1})-g_{0}(t-t_{1}^{1},s-s_{1}^{1})|dtds\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\mathcal{L}_{f}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\tau}}\int_{t_{1}^{1}}^{t_{1}^{1}+a}\int_{[0,1]^{d}}|g(t-t_{1}^{1},s-s_{1}^{1})-g_{0}(t-t_{1}^{1},s-s_{1}^{1})|dtds\right]

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{L}_{0}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\tau}}\int_{0}^{a}\int_{[0,1]^{d}}|g(u,s-s_{1}^{1})-g_{0}(u,s-s_{1}^{1})|duds\right]=\mathcal{L}_{0}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\tau}}\right]\|g-g_{0}\|_{1}.

We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\tau}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{Q}}\left[N^{1}(S_{\tau}^{-})=0\right]\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{Q}}\left[N^{1}(S_{\tau})=1\right]\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{Q}}\left[N^{1}(S_{\tau}^{+})=0\right]

\displaystyle=e^{-\tau}\times ae^{-a}\times e^{-a}=ae^{-2a-\tau}.

Thus we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{f}[Z_{1}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\tau}}]

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{L}_{0}ae^{-2a-\tau}\|g-g_{0}\|_{1}-\|\mu-\mu_{0}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\mathcal{L}_{0}ae^{-2a-\tau}}{2}\|g-g_{0}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\mathcal{L}_{0}ae^{-2a-\tau}}{2+\mathcal{L}_{0}ae^{-2a-\tau}}\|f-f_{0}\|_{1}.

Since
\Omega_{0}
and
\Omega_{\tau}
are disjoint events, we can conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{f}[Z_{1}]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{f}[Z_{1}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{0}}]+\mathbb{E}_{f}[Z_{1}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\tau}}]

\displaystyle\geq\min\left(\frac{\mathcal{L}_{0}ae^{-2a-\tau}}{2+\mathcal{L}_{0}ae^{-2a-\tau}},\frac{\mathcal{L}_{0}^{2}ae^{-2a-\tau-1}}{2+\mathcal{L}_{0}ae^{-2a-\tau}}\right)\|f-f_{0}\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{L}_{0}ae^{-2a-\tau}}{2+\mathcal{L}_{0}ae^{-2a-\tau}}\min\left(1,\mathcal{L}_{0}e^{-1}\right)\|f-f_{0}\|_{1}=\frac{\mathcal{L}_{0}^{2}ae^{-2a-\tau-1}}{2+\mathcal{L}_{0}ae^{-2a-\tau}}\|f-f_{0}\|_{1},

since
\mathcal{L}_{0}<1
. Therefore, with

\displaystyle p_{0}:=\frac{\mathcal{L}_{0}^{2}ae^{-2a-\tau-1}}{2+\mathcal{L}_{0}ae^{-2a-\tau}},

(55)

for any
\tau\in(0,1-2a)
, we obtain the result of Lemma
4.4
.
∎

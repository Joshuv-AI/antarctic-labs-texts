Gluon Wigner distributions with transverse polarization at non-zero skewness Journal: Physics Letters B
Sujit Jana, Kenil Solanki, Vikash Kumar Ojha

Abstract
We investigate the gluon Wigner distributions at non-zero skewness using light-front wave functions (LFWFs) in the dressed quark model, where the target state is a quark dressed with a gluon in the leading-order Fock space expansion. The analysis is for the configurations where either the gluon or the target is transversely polarized. We construct the Wigner distributions in a boost-invariant longitudinal space, characterized by the variable σ = 1 2 ​ b − ​ P + \sigma=\frac{1}{2}b^{-}P^{+} , which is conjugate to the skewness parameter ξ \xi . We observe a diffraction-like pattern in σ − \sigma- space, reminiscent of the single-slit interference phenomenon in optics, highlighting quantum interference effects in the gluon phase-space distribution.

Dressed Quark Model and Kinematics

We use the dressed quark model to investigate the gluon Wigner distributions. The dressed quark, modeled as a spin-
\frac{1}{2}
state consisting of a bare quark and a gluon in the leading-order light-front Fock space expansion, offers a simple yet insightful framework for studying gluon Wigner distributions. The model has been used in earlier studies to investigate the other relevant distribution functions, orbital angular momentum, and spin–orbit correlations of quarks and gluons
[
29
,
11
,
12
,
30
,
31
]
.

We adopt the light-front coordinate system
(x^{+},x^{-},\bm{x}_{\perp})
, where
x^{+}
is the light-front time and
x^{-}
is light-front longitudinal spatial coordinate, defined as
x^{\pm}=x^{0}\pm x^{3}

[
32
]
. The total squared momentum transfer to the target is given by
t=\Delta^{2}=(p-p^{\prime})^{2}
and the longitudinal momentum asymmetry between the initial and final target states is characterized by the skewness parameter
\xi
, defined as

\xi=\frac{\Delta^{+}}{2P^{+}},

where
P^{+}=\frac{p^{+}+p^{\prime+}}{2}
is the average longitudinal momentum of the target. Choosing a symmetric frame
[
33
]
, in which the average momentum is defined as
P=\frac{1}{2}(p+p^{\prime})
, the initial and final target states are parameterized as

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle=\left((1+\xi)P^{+},\,\frac{\bm{\Delta}_{\perp}}{2},\,\frac{m^{2}+\bm{\Delta}_{\perp}^{2}/4}{(1+\xi)P^{+}}\right),

(1)

\displaystyle p^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\left((1-\xi)P^{+},\,-\frac{\bm{\Delta}_{\perp}}{2},\,\frac{m^{2}+\bm{\Delta}_{\perp}^{2}/4}{(1-\xi)P^{+}}\right).

(2)

The momentum transfer is then given by

\Delta=p-p^{\prime}=\left(2\xi P^{+},\,\bm{\Delta}_{\perp},\,\frac{t+\bm{\Delta}_{\perp}^{2}}{2\xi P^{+}}\right),

and the invariant momentum transfer squared is expressed as

t=-\frac{4\xi^{2}m^{2}+\bm{\Delta}_{\perp}^{2}}{1-\xi^{2}}.

The dressed quark state with spin
\sigma
and momentum
p
can be expanded in Fock space up to the two-particle sector, incorporating the quark-gluon configuration
[
30
,
31
]

\displaystyle\Big|p^{+},p_{\perp},\sigma\Big\rangle=

\displaystyle\Phi^{\sigma}(p)b^{\dagger}_{\sigma}(p)|0\rangle+\sum_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}\int[dp_{1}]\int[dp_{2}]

\displaystyle\sqrt{16\pi^{3}p^{+}}\delta^{3}(p-p_{1}-p_{2})\Phi^{\sigma}_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}(p;p_{1},p_{2})

\displaystyle b^{\dagger}_{\sigma_{1}}(p_{1})a^{\dagger}_{\sigma_{2}}(p_{2})|0\rangle;

(3)

The state is expressed as a superposition of a single-quark state and a two-particle quark-gluon state, where the light-front wavefunctions (LFWFs) encapsulate the nonperturbative information. Here,
[dp]=\frac{dp^{+}d^{2}p_{\perp}}{\sqrt{16\pi^{3}p^{+}}}
,
\Phi^{\sigma}(p)
represents the single-particle wavefunction with momentum
p
and spin
\sigma
.
\Phi^{\sigma}_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}(p;p_{1},p_{2})
represents the two-particle light-front wavefunction and can be expressed in a boost-invariant form using the relation
\Psi^{\sigma}_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}(x,q_{\perp})=\Phi^{\sigma}_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}\sqrt{P^{+}}
.
The momentum variables
(x_{i},q_{i\perp})
are the Jacobi momenta, defined as

\displaystyle p_{i}^{+}=x_{i}p^{+},~~~~~~~~~~q_{i\perp}=k_{i\perp}+x_{i}p_{\perp},

(4)

and obey the following conditions

\displaystyle\sum_{i}x_{i}=1,~~~~~~~~~\sum_{i}q_{i\perp}=0.

(5)

Assigning
(x_{1},q_{1\perp})\equiv(x,q_{\perp})
to the quark, and
(x_{2},q_{2\perp})\equiv(x_{g},q_{\perp g})
to the gluon, we obtain

\displaystyle x+x_{g}=

\displaystyle 1\Rightarrow x_{g}=1-x,

(6)

\displaystyle\mathrm{and}~~~q_{\perp}+q_{\perp g}=

\displaystyle 0\Rightarrow q_{\perp g}=-q_{\perp}.

(7)

The longitudinal momentum fraction of the gluon with respect to the target is
x_{g}=k^{+}_{g}/P^{+}
, and the corresponding four-momentum of the gluon is

\displaystyle k_{g}\equiv\Big(x_{g}P^{+},k_{\perp g},k^{-}_{g}\Big).

(8)

3
Gluon Wigner distribution at non-zero skewness

We investigate the gluon Wigner distributions within the dressed quark model, focusing on scenarios with non-zero skewness. These distributions are constructed using the LFWFs and defined via the Fourier transform of gluon-gluon correlators in longitudinal boost-invariant space.
The general expression for the gluon Wigner distribution is given by
[
34
,
21
,
29
]
:

\displaystyle xW_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}(x,k_{\perp},\Delta_{\perp},\sigma)=\int\frac{d\xi}{2\pi}e^{i\sigma\cdot\xi}\int\frac{dz^{-}d^{2}z_{\perp}}{2(2\pi)^{3}p^{+}}e^{ik\cdot z}

\displaystyle\times\left\langle p^{+},\frac{\Delta_{\perp}}{2},\lambda^{\prime}\middle|\Gamma^{ij}F^{+i}\left(-\frac{z}{2}\right)\mathcal{W}F^{+j}\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)\mathcal{W}^{\prime}\middle|p^{+},\frac{\Delta_{\perp}}{2},\lambda\right\rangle\Bigg|_{z^{+}=0}.

(9)

Here, the skewness parameter
\xi
denotes the longitudinal momentum transfer to the target, and
\sigma
represents the longitudinal impact parameter space variable conjugate to
\xi

[
22
]
. The field strength tensors
F^{+i}
are evaluated at two spatially separated points and connected via Wilson lines
\mathcal{W}
and
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
, ensuring gauge invariance. However, as we adopt the light-front gauge
A^{+}=0
, the Wilson lines reduce to unity, and the field strength tensor simplifies to
F^{+i}=\partial^{+}A^{i}
.
The transverse components of the gauge field
A^{i}
are expressed in terms of creation and annihilation operators
[
31
]

\displaystyle A^{i}\Big(\frac{z}{2}\Big)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda}\int\frac{dk^{+}d^{2}k_{\perp}}{2k^{+}(2\pi)^{3}}\Big[\epsilon_{\lambda}^{i}(k)a_{\lambda}(k)e^{-\frac{i}{2}k.z}+\epsilon_{\lambda}^{*i}(k)a^{\dagger}_{\lambda}(k)e^{\frac{i}{2}k.z}\Big].

(10)

At twist-two, the gluon Wigner distributions are constructed using the polarization projectors
\Gamma^{ij}\in\{\delta^{ij}_{\perp},-i\epsilon^{ij}_{\perp},\Gamma^{RR},\Gamma^{LL}\}
, where
R
and
L
denote right- and left-handed polarizations respectively
[
24
]
.

In the dressed quark model, the target state is expanded in terms of Fock components, and we retain only the quark-gluon two-particle sector. The two-particle LFWFs are obtained using light-front Hamiltonian perturbation theory and expressed as
[
31
]

\displaystyle\Psi_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}^{\lambda a}(x,q_{\perp})=\frac{g}{\sqrt{2(2\pi)^{3}}}T^{a}\frac{\chi_{\sigma_{1}}^{\dagger}\,\mathcal{O}(x,q_{\perp},m)\,\chi_{\lambda}\,(\epsilon_{\perp\sigma_{2}})^{*}}{D(k_{\perp},x)},

(11)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(x,q_{\perp},m)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x}}\left[-2\frac{q_{\perp}}{1-x}-\frac{(\sigma_{\perp}\cdot q_{\perp})\sigma_{\perp}}{x}+im\sigma_{\perp}\frac{(1-x)}{x}\right]

denotes the spin-momentum structure, and

\displaystyle D(k_{\perp},x)=m^{2}-\frac{m^{2}+k_{\perp}^{2}}{x}-\frac{k_{\perp}^{2}}{1-x}

is the energy denominator.
Using the LFWFs, the gluon-gluon correlators for different choices of
\Gamma^{ij}
are expressed as overlaps of wave functions weighted by gluon polarization vectors. The correlators depend on the kinematic variables
x_{g}=1-x
, the skewness
\xi
, transverse momentum
k_{\perp g}
, and transverse momentum transfer to the target
\Delta_{\perp}
. The polarization vectors
\epsilon^{L}
and
\epsilon^{R}
are constructed from the Cartesian components via
\epsilon^{L(R)}=\epsilon^{1}\mp i\epsilon^{2}
.

The gluon-gluon correlator functions can be computed using the overlaps of two-particle LFWFs
[
21
]
. These correlators depend on the gluon polarization operator
\Gamma^{ij}
and the polarization of the target state. Explicitly, the correlators are given as follows:

(a) For the unpolarized case, corresponding to
\Gamma^{ij}=\delta^{ij}_{\perp}
,

\displaystyle W^{(\delta^{ij}_{\perp})}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}=-\sum_{\sigma_{1},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}\Psi^{*\,\sigma^{\prime}}_{\sigma_{1}\lambda_{1}}(x_{g}^{\prime},q^{\prime}_{\perp g})\,\Psi^{\sigma}_{\sigma_{1}\lambda_{2}}(y_{g},q_{\perp g})\left(\epsilon^{1}_{\lambda_{2}}\epsilon^{*1}_{\lambda_{1}}+\epsilon^{2}_{\lambda_{2}}\epsilon^{*2}_{\lambda_{1}}\right).

(12)

(b) For the longitudinally polarized case with
\Gamma^{ij}=-i\epsilon^{ij}_{\perp}
,

\displaystyle W^{(-i\epsilon^{ij}_{\perp})}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}=-i\sum_{\sigma_{1},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}\Psi^{*\,\sigma^{\prime}}_{\sigma_{1}\lambda_{1}}(x_{g}^{\prime},q^{\prime}_{\perp g})\,\Psi^{\sigma}_{\sigma_{1}\lambda_{2}}(y_{g},q_{\perp g})\left(\epsilon^{1}_{\lambda_{2}}\epsilon^{*1}_{\lambda_{1}}-\epsilon^{2}_{\lambda_{2}}\epsilon^{*2}_{\lambda_{1}}\right).

(13)

(c) For the right-handed circular gluon polarization
\Gamma^{ij}=\Gamma^{RR}
,

\displaystyle W^{(\Gamma^{RR})}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}=-\sum_{\sigma_{1},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}\Psi^{*\,\sigma^{\prime}}_{\sigma_{1}\lambda_{1}}(x_{g}^{\prime},q^{\prime}_{\perp g})\,\Psi^{\sigma}_{\sigma_{1}\lambda_{2}}(y_{g},q_{\perp g})\,\epsilon^{R}_{\lambda_{2}}\epsilon^{*R}_{\lambda_{1}}.

(14)

(d) For left-handed circular gluon polarization
\Gamma^{ij}=\Gamma^{LL}

\displaystyle W^{(\Gamma^{LL})}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}=-\sum_{\sigma_{1},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}\Psi^{*\,\sigma^{\prime}}_{\sigma_{1}\lambda_{1}}(x_{g}^{\prime},q^{\prime}_{\perp g})\,\Psi^{\sigma}_{\sigma_{1}\lambda_{2}}(y_{g},q_{\perp g})\,\epsilon^{L}_{\lambda_{2}}\epsilon^{*L}_{\lambda_{1}}.

(15)

where the kinematic variables are defined as follows:
x_{g}=1-x
is the gluon longitudinal momentum fraction,
x_{g}^{\prime}=x_{g}/(1-\xi)
, and
y_{g}=x_{g}/(1+\xi)
. The initial and final momenta of gluon are
(y_{g},q_{\perp g})
and
(x^{\prime},q^{\prime}_{\perp g})
respectively. The transverse momenta of the gluon in the initial and final states are given by

\displaystyle q_{\perp g}=-k_{\perp}+\frac{x_{g}\Delta_{\perp}}{2(1+\xi)},\quad q^{\prime}_{\perp g}=-k_{\perp}-\frac{x_{g}\Delta_{\perp}}{2(1-\xi)},

(16)

and the circular polarization vectors of the gluon are
\epsilon^{L(R)}_{\lambda}=\epsilon^{1}_{\lambda}\mp i\epsilon^{2}_{\lambda}

[
24
]
.

The gluon Wigner distributions in boost-invariant longitudinal space are obtained by taking the Fourier transform of correlators with respect to
\xi
:

\displaystyle\rho^{\Gamma}(x_{g},\sigma,\Delta_{\perp},k_{\perp};S)=\int_{0}^{\xi_{\text{max}}}\frac{d\xi}{2\pi}e^{i\sigma\cdot\xi}W^{\Gamma}(x_{g},\xi,\Delta_{\perp},k_{\perp};S),

(17)

where
S
denotes the polarization of the target state, and
\xi_{\text{max}}
is determined by the maximum allowed momentum transfer for fixed
-t
, given by

\displaystyle\xi_{\text{max}}=\frac{-t}{2m^{2}}\left(\sqrt{1+\frac{4m^{2}}{-t}}-1\right),\quad\text{with }-t=\frac{4\xi^{2}m^{2}+\Delta_{\perp}^{2}}{1-\xi^{2}}.

(18)

For the case of an unpolarized gluon and an unpolarized target, the Wigner distribution is defined as

\displaystyle\rho^{g}_{UU}(x_{g},\sigma,\Delta_{\perp},k_{\perp})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big[\rho^{\delta^{ij}_{\perp}}(x,\sigma,\Delta_{\perp},k_{\perp};+\hat{e}_{z})

\displaystyle+\rho^{\delta^{ij}_{\perp}}(x,\sigma,\Delta_{\perp},k_{\perp};-\hat{e}_{z})\Big].

(19)

Substituting the relevant expressions for the gluon-gluon correlator, we obtain an analytic form of the Wigner distribution

\displaystyle\rho^{g}_{UU}(x_{g},\sigma,t,k_{\perp})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\xi_{max}}\frac{d\xi}{4\pi}e^{i\sigma\cdot\xi}~\frac{\alpha_{g}}{x_{g}}\Big[-((4(1-\xi^{2})k^{2}_{\perp}

\displaystyle-x_{g}^{2}\Delta^{2}_{\perp}+4\xi x_{g}k_{\perp}\cdot\Delta_{\perp})((1+x_{g}^{2})+\xi

\displaystyle(2-3\xi))+4m^{2}((1-x_{g})^{2}-\xi^{2})^{2})\Big].

(20)

The integrand includes kinematic and mass-dependent contributions along with a factor

\displaystyle\alpha_{g}=\frac{N\sqrt{1-\xi^{2}}}{D(q_{\perp g},y_{g})D^{*}(q^{\prime}_{\perp g},x^{\prime}_{g})x_{g}((1-x_{g})^{2}-\xi^{2})^{3/2}}.

(21)

Here
N=\frac{g^{2}C_{F}}{2(2\pi)^{3}}
, with
g
being the strong coupling constant and
C_{F}
is the color factor.

5
Conclusion

We investigated the gluon Wigner distributions in a dressed quark model incorporating non-zero skewness, within the light-front Hamiltonian formalism. Using the two-particle LFWFs, we evaluated the gluon-gluon correlators for different polarization configurations—particularly emphasizing transverse polarization—and subsequently derived the associated Wigner distributions in the longitudinal boost-invariant impact parameter space (
\sigma
). Our results provide explicit expressions for the gluon Wigner distributions, including cases where the gluon and the target are polarized longitudinally or transversely.

We presented the numerical analysis for the unpolarized gluon distribution
\rho^{g}_{UU}
and selected polarization-dependent distributions such as
\rho^{gR}_{UT}
,
\rho^{gR}_{LT}
,
\rho^{g(R)x}_{TT}
, and
\rho^{gx}_{TL}
. The
\sigma
-space distributions exhibit a strong dependence on the momentum transfer
t
, with broader spatial profiles emerging at larger
-t
, in accordance with the uncertainty principle. Moreover, polarization-sensitive distributions reveal rich spatial structures that encode correlations between parton momentum and spatial degrees of freedom.

Our study demonstrates that even a simple perturbative model like the dressed quark framework captures essential qualitative features of gluon Wigner distributions and provides a tractable setting to explore the effects of non-zero skewness. These results serve as useful theoretical benchmarks and may offer insights for interpreting future experimental and lattice QCD studies of parton tomography. Further developments could extend this analysis to higher Fock sectors or more realistic non-perturbative models incorporating confinement effects.

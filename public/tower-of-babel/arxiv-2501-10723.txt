István Kovács 1 and Luka Šinkovec 2

Abstract
. Based on the earlier work of Li (European J. Combin. 1997) and Dobson (Discrete Math. 2008), in this paper we complete the classification of cyclic m m -DCI-groups and m m -CI-groups. For a positive integer m m such that m ≥ 3 m\geq 3 , we show that the group ℤ n \mathbb{Z}_{n} is an m m -DCI-group if and only if n n is not divisible by 8 8 nor by p 2 p^{2} for any odd prime p < m p<m . Furthermore, if m ≥ 6 m\geq 6 , then we show that ℤ n \mathbb{Z}_{n} is an m m -CI-group if and only if either n ∈ { 8 , 9 , 18 } n\in\{8,9,18\} , or n ∉ { 8 , 9 , 18 } n\notin\{8,9,18\} and n n is not divisible by 8 8 nor by p 2 p^{2} for any odd prime p < m − 1 2 p<\frac{m-1}{2} .

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a finite group and
S\subset G
be a subset not containing
the identity element of
G
. The
Cayley digraph

\cay(G,S)
is defined to have
vertex set
G
and arcs in the form
(g,sg)
, where
g\in G
and
s\in S
.
In the case when
S
is also inverse-closed, i.e., for every

x\in S
,
x^{-1}\in S
, the pair of arcs
(g,sg)
and
(sg,g)
can be identified
with the undirected edge
\{g,sg\}
, in which case
\cay(G,S)
is regarded as an undirected graph and referred to as a
Cayley graph
.
Every automorphism
\sigma\in\aut(G)

induces an isomorphism between the (di)graphs
\cay(G,S)
and
\cay(G,S^{\alpha})
, in which case
\sigma
is called a
Cayley isomorphism
.
The converse is not true in general, examples
of Cayley (di)graphs are known which are isomorphic but there exists no Cayley isomorphism mapping one onto the other.
A (di)graph
\cay(G,S)
is called a
CI-(di)graph
if for any

\cay(G,T)
, whenever
\cay(G,S)\cong\cay(G,T)
, we have

S^{\sigma}=T
for some
\sigma\in\aut(G)
.
For a positive integer
m
, the group
G
has the

m
-DCI-property
(
m
-CI-property
, resp.) if all Cayley digraphs
(Cayley graphs, resp.) on
G
of valency
m
are CI-digraphs (CI-graphs, resp.)
(see
[
11
, Definition 1.1]
). By the valency of a Cayley digraph

\cay(G,S)
we mean its in-valency, which is
equal to the cardinality
|S|
and coincides with its out-valency.
Furthermore,
G
is called an
m
-(D)CI-group
if it has the
i
-(D)CI property for every

i\leq m
and a
D(CI)-group
when it has the
i
-(D)CI property for every
positive integer
i
.

The study of finite groups with the
m
-(D)CI property was initiated by Li et al.
[
11
]
.
Both properties attracted considerable attention in the late 90’s,
in particular, it was shown that the finite
m
-DCI-groups belong to
a relatively restricted list of groups, and a similar list was also derived containing the finite
m
-CI-groups.
For the results from this period, we refer to the survey paper
[
10
]
.

In this paper, we aim to complete the classification of cyclic
m
-DCI- and
m
-CI-groups.
We review next the known results.
In 1967, Ádám
[
1
]
made a conjecture stating that every cyclic group is a
DCI-group. This conjecture was shown to be false soon after it has been published and
remarkably, the question as to which cyclic groups are DCI-groups was answered only 30 years later by Muzychuk
[
12
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
12
]
)
.

(1)

The group
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is a DCI-group if and only if
n=k
or
2k
, where
k
is square-free.

(2)

The group
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is a CI-group if and only if either
n\in\{8,9,18\}
, or

n=k
or
2k
, where
k
is square-free.

A few papers were devoted to cyclic
m
-(D)CI-groups for small
m
(see
[
3
,
4
,
7
,
8
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
), in particular,
Sun
[
14
]
showed that every cyclic group is a
2
-DCI-group and
Li
[
8
]
showed that every cyclic group is a
5
-CI-group.
At this point we would like to emphasize the difference between
the
m
-DCI- and the
m
-CI-property for cyclic groups, which lies in
the fact that by the former property one concerns digraphs, whereas by
the latter property one concerns only undirected graphs.
For example, it is known that
\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{8},\{1,2,5\})
is not a CI-digraph
[
6
]
, showing that
\mathbb{Z}_{8}
does not have the
3
-DCI-property.
On the other hand, as
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is a
5
-CI-group for every
n
,
\mathbb{Z}_{8}
has
the
3
-CI-property (this can be easily checked by simply looking at the possible Cayley
graphs on
\mathbb{Z}_{8}
of valency
3
).

In
[
9
]
, Li determined the positive integers
m
for which
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
has the
m
-DCI-property.
The following result was also shown, we present it here as it is stated in
[
10
, Theorem 7.2]
.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
10
]
)
.

Let
G
be a cyclic group, and let
G_{p}
be the Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
. Suppose that
G
has the
m
-DCI-property. If
|G|
is not a prime-square and
p+1\leq m\leq(|G|-1)/2
, then
G_{p}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
or

\mathbb{Z}_{4}
.

The above theorem provides necessary conditions for a cyclic group to be an
m
-DCI-group, but
it does not yield the complete classification of cyclic
m
-DCI-groups.
In his survey, Li wrote (see
[
10
, p. 319]
):

‘A natural question is, for a positive integer
m
,
which cyclic groups are
m
-DCI-groups, and which cyclic groups are

m
-CI-groups?’

Motivated by Theorem
1.2
, he also proposed
a conjecture, which contains sufficient conditions for a cyclic group to be an
m
-DCI- and
m
-CI-group, respectively (see
[
10
, Conjecture 7.3]
). This conjecture was proved later
by Dobson
[
5
]
and it reads as follows.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
5
]
)
.

Let
n=n_{1}n_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\gcd(n_{1},n_{2})=1
and
n_{1}
divides
4k
, where
k
is odd and square-free.

(1)

If
p>m
for every prime divisor
p
of
n_{2}
, then
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is an

m
-DCI-group.

(2)

If
p>2m
for every prime divisor
p
of
n_{2}
, then
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is a

2m
-CI-group.

However, it turns out that in order to classify the
cyclic
m
-DCI- and
m
-CI-groups, the sufficient conditions in Theorem
1.3
need to be strengthened. Our main result is the following generalization.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
n=n_{1}n_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\gcd(n_{1},n_{2})=1
and
n_{1}
divides
4k
, where
k
is odd and square-free.

(1)

If
p\geq m
for every prime divisor
p
of
n_{2}
, then
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is an

m
-DCI-group.

(2)

If
2p+1\geq m
for every prime divisor
p
of
n_{2}
, then
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is an

m
-CI-group.

Combining these stronger sufficient conditions with the known necessary conditions, we
complete the classification of cyclic
m
-DCI- and
m
-CI-groups.

Theorem 1.5
.

(1)

If
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
m\geq 3
, then the group
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is an
m
-DCI-group
if and only if
n
is not divisible by
8
nor by
p^{2}
for any odd prime
p<m
.

(2)

If
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
m\geq 6
, then the group
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is an
m
-CI-group
if and only if either
n\in\{8,9,18\}
, or
n\notin\{8,9,18\}
and it is not
divisible by
8
nor by
p^{2}
for any odd prime
p<\frac{m-1}{2}
.

Remark 1.6
.

The necessity part of Theorem
1.5
follows directly from known constructions of non-CI-digraphs and graphs
discovered in
[
2
,
6
,
11
]
. All these examples will appear in
the course of the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Remark 1.7
.

Comparing Theorem
1.5
with the classification of cyclic DCI- and CI-groups given
in Theorem
1.1
, we obtain that the group
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is an
m
-DCI-group for

m\geq 3
but not a DCI-group if there exist a prime
p
such that
p\geq m
and

p^{2}\mid n
; and also that
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is an
m
-CI-group for
m\geq 6
but not a CI-group
if
n\notin\{8,9,18\}
, and there exists a prime

such that

p\geq(m-1)/2
and

.

Dobson
[

]
obtained Theorem

using permutation group theoretical techniques.
Our proof of Theorem

is independent, it relies on a necessary
and sufficient criterion for two circulant digraphs to be isomorphic due to Muzychuk
[

]
. In the next section we give a short overview of Muzychuk’s criterion, Theorems

and
1.5
will then be derived in

.

2.
Isomorphic circulant digraphs

2.1.
Key spaces

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we use the symbol
[n]
to denote the set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
Let
n=p_{1}^{t_{1}}\cdots p_{l}^{t_{l}}
be the prime decomposition of
n
.
In what follows,
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
will be identified with the direct sum

\mathbb{Z}_{p_{1}^{t_{1}}}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{p_{l}^{t_{l}}}
. Therefore, an element

x\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
will also be represented as the
l
-tuple
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{l})
, where

x_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p_{i}^{t_{i}}}
for every
i\in[l]
.

Definition 2.1
.

The
key space

\mathbf{K}_{n}
is the direct product

\mathbf{K}_{n}:=\mathbf{K}_{p_{1}^{t_{1}}}\times\cdots\times\mathbf{K}_{p_{l}^{t_{l}}},

where for a prime power
p^{t}
,
t\geq 1
,

\mathbf{K}_{p^{t}}
consists of the
t
-tuples
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{t})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{t}
such that,

•

\forall i\in[t],~0\leq k_{i}<i
,

•

\forall i\in[t-1],~k_{i}\leq k_{i+1}
.

The elements of
\mathbf{K}_{n}
are called
keys
.
Thus a key
\mathbf{k}\in\mathbf{K}_{n}
is a two-dimensional integer array, which will be written as

\mathbf{k}=(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{l})=(k_{ij})_{i\in[l],j\in[t_{i}]},

where
\mathbf{k}_{i}=(k_{i1},\ldots,k_{it_{i}})\in\mathbf{K}_{p_{i}^{t_{i}}}
.

The
zero key

\mathbf{z}
in
\mathbf{K}_{n}
is defined as
\mathbf{z}=(z_{ij})_{i\in[l],j\in[t_{i}]}

with
z_{ij}=0
for every
i\in[l],j\in[t_{i}]
. If
n\equiv 4\pmod{8}
, then the

almost zero key

\mathbf{z}_{*}
in

\mathbf{K}_{n}
is defined as
\mathbf{z}_{*}=(z_{ij})_{i\in[l],j\in[t_{i}]}
with

z_{ij}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}~p_{i}^{t_{i}}=4~\text{and}~j=2,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

For two keys
\mathbf{k},\mathbf{m}\in\mathbf{K}_{n}
, we write
\mathbf{k}\leq\mathbf{m}
if

k_{ij}\leq m_{ij}
, for every
i\in[l]
and

j\in[t_{i}]
. The relation
\leq
is a partial order on
\mathbf{K}_{n}
.
The poset
(\mathbf{K}_{n},\leq)
is a lattice with meet
\wedge
and join
\vee

operations defined as

\forall i\in[l],\,\forall j\in[t_{i}],~(\mathbf{k}\wedge\mathbf{m})_{ij}=\min(k_{ij},m_{ij})~\text{and}~(\mathbf{k}\vee\mathbf{m})_{ij}=\max(k_{ij},m_{ij}).

2.2.
Key partitions

The set of all partitions of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
will be denoted by
\parti(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
.
For two partitions
\Sigma,\Delta\in\parti(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
,

\Delta
is said to be a
refinement
of
\Sigma
, denoted by

\Sigma\sqsubseteq\Delta
, if every class of
\Sigma
is union of classes of
\Delta
. The relation
\sqsubseteq
is a partial order on
\parti(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
.
The poset
(\parti(\mathbb{Z}_{n}),\sqsubseteq)
is a lattice. We write

\Sigma\wedge\Delta
for the meet and
\Sigma\vee\Delta
for the join
of the partitions
\Sigma
and
\Delta
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
n=p^{t}
for a prime
p
and let
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{t})\in\mathbf{K}_{p^{t}}
.
The
key partition

\Sigma(\mathbf{k})
is the partition of

\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}
defined as

\Sigma(\mathbf{k})=\big\{\{0\}\big\}\cup\big\{P_{k_{\alpha(x)}}+x\,\mid\,x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}\setminus\{0\}\big\},

where for
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}\setminus\{0\}
,
\alpha(x)
is the number in
[t]
defined by the
property that
p^{\alpha(x)}
is the order of
x
as an element in the group
(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}},+)
; and for
j\in\{0,1,\ldots,t\}
,
P_{j}
is the subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}
of order
p^{j}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be a number greater than
1
with prime decomposition
n=p_{1}^{t_{1}}\cdots p_{l}^{t_{l}}
and
let
\mathbf{k}=(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{l})\in\mathbf{K}_{n}
. Then the
key partition

\Sigma(\mathbf{k})
is the partition of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
defined as

\Sigma(\mathbf{k})=\big\{\,(S_{1},\ldots,S_{l})\,\mid\,S_{i}\in\Sigma(\mathbf{k}_{i}),i\in[l]\,\big\}.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
13
, Proposition 2.2]
)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbf{k},\mathbf{m}\in\mathbf{K}_{n}
. If
\mathbf{k}\leq\mathbf{m}
then
\Sigma(\mathbf{m})\sqsubseteq\Sigma(\mathbf{k})
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\Pi\in\text{Part}(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
be an arbitrary partition of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
.
The
key partition of

\Pi
is the coarsest key partition
\Sigma(\mathbf{m})
that
refines
\Pi
; or more formally,

•

\Pi\sqsubseteq\Sigma(\mathbf{m})
.

•

If
\Pi\sqsubseteq\Sigma(\mathbf{k})
for some
\mathbf{k}\in\mathbf{K}_{n}
, then

\Sigma(\mathbf{m})\sqsubseteq\Sigma(\mathbf{k})
.

The above key
\mathbf{m}
is referred to as the
key of
\Pi
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
S
be a nonempty subset of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
.
The
key
of
S
, denoted by
\mathbf{k}(S)
, is the key of the partition

\{S,\mathbb{Z}_{n}\setminus S\}
.

2.3.
Generalized multipliers

We consider first the generalized multipliers of cyclic groups of prime power order.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{N}
be a prime and
t\in\mathbb{N}
. A
generalized multiplier
of

\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}
is a
t
-tuple
\vec{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{t})\in\mathbb{N}^{t}
such that

\gcd(m_{i},p)=1
for every
i\in[t]
.

The set of all generalized multipliers of
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}
is denoted by
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}^{**}
.
We associate next a permutation of
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}
with any
generalized multiplier in
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}^{**}
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{N}
be a prime and
t\in\mathbb{N}
. For
\vec{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{t})\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}^{**}
, the
generalized multiplier function

f_{\vec{m}}
is the permutation of

\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}
, defined as

\forall x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}},~x^{f_{\vec{m}}}=\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}m_{t-i}x_{i}p^{i}\!\!\!\!\pmod{p^{t}},

where
\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}x_{i}p^{i}
is the
p
-adic decomposition of
x
, i.e.,
x_{i}\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}
for every
i\in\{0,\ldots,t-1\}
.

It follows from the above definition that, if
\vec{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{t})
and

\vec{m^{\prime}}=(m^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,m^{\prime}_{t})
are two generalized
multipliers of
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}
such that

\forall i\in[t],~m_{i}\equiv m^{\prime}_{i}\!\!\!\!\pmod{p^{i}},

then
f_{\vec{m}}=f_{\vec{m^{\prime}}}
. Thus, to have all generalized multiplier functions, it is sufficient to
consider only those generalized multipliers
\vec{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{t})
, which satisfy

m_{i}\in[p^{i}-1]
for every
i\in[t]
.

Fix a key
\mathbf{k}\in\mathbf{K}_{p^{t}}
. We define a subset
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}^{**}(\mathbf{k})\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}^{**}
as
follows. If
t>1
, then let

\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}^{**}(\mathbf{k})=\big\{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{t})\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}^{**}\,\mid\,\forall i\in[t-1],~m_{i+1}\equiv m_{i}\!\!\!\!\pmod{p^{i-k_{i+1}}}\,\big\};

and if
t=1
, then let
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{**}(\mathbf{k})=\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{**}
.

A
\Sigma(\mathbf{k})
-class different from
\{0\}
is in the form
P_{k_{i}}+x
, where
P_{k_{i}}
is the subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}
of order
p^{k_{i}}
, and
x
is a non-zero element of order
p^{i}
in
(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}},+)
. It is easy to see that for
every
\vec{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{t})\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}^{**}(\mathbf{k})
,
x^{f_{\vec{m}}}
has order
p^{i}
, and

(P_{k_{i}}+x)^{f_{\vec{m}}}=P_{k_{i}}+x^{f_{\vec{m}}}.

These show that
f_{\vec{m}}
induces a permutation of

\Sigma(\mathbf{k})
. Furthermore, one can show that, if
\vec{m^{\prime}}=(m^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,m^{\prime}_{t})
is another generalized multiplier from
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}^{**}(\mathbf{k})
such that

m^{\prime}_{i}\equiv m_{i}\!\!\pmod{p^{i-k_{i}}}
, then

(P_{k_{i}}+x)^{f_{\vec{m}}}=(P_{k_{i}}+x)^{f_{\vec{m^{\prime}}}}
.
In view of this, to have all permutations of
\Sigma(\mathbf{k})
induced by the generalized multipliers in
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}^{**}(\mathbf{k})
, it suffices to
consider only those generalized multipliers
\vec{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{t})\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}^{**}(\mathbf{k})
, which satisfy
m_{i}\in[p^{i-k_{i}}-1]
for every
i\in[t]
.
We refer to the latter generalized multipliers as
genuine
and denote their set by

\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}^{**}(\mathbf{k})^{\circ}
. More formally,

\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}^{**}(\mathbf{k})^{\circ}=\big\{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{t})\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}^{**}(\mathbf{k})\,\mid\,\forall i\in[t],~m_{i}\in[p^{i-k_{i}}-1]\,\big\}.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be an arbitrary number with prime decomposition
n=p_{1}^{t_{1}}\cdots p_{l}^{t_{l}}
, and let

\mathbf{k}=(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{l})\in\mathbf{K}_{n}
be an arbitrary key. The above concepts are generalized to
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{**}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{Z}_{p_{1}^{t_{1}}}^{**}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}_{p_{l}^{t_{l}}}^{**},

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{**}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{Z}_{p_{1}^{t_{1}}}^{**}(\mathbf{k}_{1})\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}_{p_{l}^{t_{l}}}^{**}(\mathbf{k}_{l}),

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{**}(\mathbf{k})^{\circ}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{Z}_{p_{1}^{t_{1}}}^{**}(\mathbf{k}_{1})^{\circ}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}_{p_{l}^{t_{l}}}^{**}(\mathbf{k}_{l})^{\circ}.

For
\vec{m}=(\vec{m_{1}},\ldots,\vec{m_{t}})\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{**}
, the generalized
multiplier function
f_{\vec{m}}
is the permutation of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
acting as

\forall(x_{1},\ldots,x_{l})\in\mathbb{Z}_{n},~(x_{1},\ldots,x_{l})^{f_{\vec{m}}}=\big(\,x_{1}^{f_{\vec{m_{1}}}},\ldots,x_{l}^{f_{\vec{m_{l}}}}\big).

Definition 2.9
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\mathbf{k}\in\mathbf{K}_{n}
. The
solving set

P(\mathbf{k})
is the
following set of permutations of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
:

P(\mathbf{k})=\{f_{\vec{m}}\,\mid\,\vec{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{**}(\mathbf{k})^{\circ}\}.

The example below is meant to illustrate all the concepts introduced in this section.

Example 2.10
.

We determine below the solving sets
P(\mathbf{k})
, where
\mathbf{k}\in\mathbf{K}_{p^{2}}
and
p
is a
fixed prime.

Due to Definition
2.1
,
\mathbf{K}_{p^{2}}
consists of two keys:
(0,0)
and
(0,1)
.
Let
\vec{m}=(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}^{**}((0,i))^{\circ}
, where
i\in\{0,1\}
.
Then

m_{1}\in[p-1],~m_{2}\in[p^{2-i}-1],~\gcd(m_{2},p)=1~\text{and}~m_{2}\equiv m_{1}\!\!\!\!\pmod{p^{1-i}}.

Due to Definition
2.8
,

(x_{0}+x_{1}p)^{f_{\vec{m}}}=m_{2}x_{0}+m_{1}x_{1}p
. Note that, if
i=0
, then

\displaystyle P((0,0))

\displaystyle=\big\{x\mapsto mx\,\mid\,m\in[p^{2}-1],\gcd(m,p)=1\big\}=\aut(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}),

(1)

\displaystyle P((0,1))

\displaystyle=\big\{x_{0}+x_{1}p\mapsto m_{2}x_{0}+m_{1}x_{1}p\,\mid\,m_{1},m_{2}\in[p-1]\big\}.

Proof.

Note that, for every

Due to Definition

(2)

2.4.
Muzychuk’s criterion

Now we are prepared to present Muzychuk’s criterion for two
circulant digraphs to be isomorphic.

Theorem 2.12

(
[
13
]
)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

and let

\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{n},T)
be two circulant
digraphs. Then we have the following.

(1)

\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{n},S)\ncong\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{n},T)

(2)

(i)

\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{n},S)\cong\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{n},T)

(ii)

There exists a permutation

such that

\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{n},S)^{f_{\vec{m}}}=\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{n},T).

(iii)

There exists a permutation
f_{\vec{m}}\in P(\mathbf{k})
such that

S^{f_{\vec{m}}}=T
.

Using the above theorem, we obtain the following sufficient condition
for a circulant digraph to be CI.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let

such that

\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{n},S)

Proof.

Suppose that
\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{n},S)\cong\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{n},T)
for some subset

.
We have to show that
S^{\sigma}=T
for some
\sigma\in\aut(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
.

By Theorem
2.12
,
\mathbf{k}(T)=\mathbf{k}(S)
and there exists

f_{\vec{m}}\in P(\mathbf{k})
, such that
S^{f_{\vec{m}}}=T
.
Let
n
have prime decomposition

.
Then
\mathbf{k}=(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{l})\in\mathbf{K}_{p_{1}^{t_{1}}}\times\cdots\times\mathbf{K}_{p_{l}^{t_{l}}}
, and
the action of

on

can be described as

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{l})^{f_{\vec{m}}}=\big(\,x_{1}^{f_{\vec{m_{1}}}},\ldots,x_{l}^{f_{\vec{m_{l}}}}\,\big),

If

On the other hand, as

In the later case, we see in (

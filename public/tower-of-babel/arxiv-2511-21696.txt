New Interval Calculus with Application to Interval Differential Equations
Wei Liu, Muhammad Aamir Ali, Yanrong An

Abstract




Abstract:
This paper presents a systematic study of the calculus of interval-valued functions and its application to interval differential equations.
To this end, first, we introduce new interval arithmetic operations. Under new operations, the space of interval numbers becomes a strict linear space, and indeed a Hilbert space, whereas the traditional interval arithmetic yields only a semilinear space with a defective algebraic structure.
Secondly, by basing derivative and integral of interval-valued functions on the proposed operations, we retain every essential property of classical calculus while seamlessly incorporating ideas from the multiplicative calculus. The resulting unified “hybrid” framework eliminates the tedious case-by-case inspection of switching points required by the gH-derivative, leading to a markedly streamlined computational procedure.
Finally, we establish an existence theorem for solutions of interval differential equations within the new calculus and corroborate its validity and practicality through representative examples. In contrast to the gH-derivative approach, the number of potential solutions does not explode doubly with additional switching points, ensuring robustness in both theory and computation.

Keywords:
Interval arithmetic operation; Interval-valued functions; Differentiability; Integrability;
Interval differential equations

Later in

In

However, due to the existence of two types of derivative, it is necessary to consider the problem of switching points during the differentiation process
[

. Furthermore, when solving differential equations, as the domain expands, the number of solutions to the equation may increase doubly.

After observing the shortcomings of previous literatures in interval analysis, particularly the algebraic deficiencies of traditional interval arithmetic operations, this work emerges to address several critical limitations.
The conventional approach renders the space of interval numbers
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
merely a semilinear space, creating substantial obstacles in developing a consistent calculus for interval-valued functions. These structural weaknesses manifest most prominently in differentiation and integration theories, where existing definitions lead to operational inconsistencies and computational complexities. This work aims to contribute to this growing body of knowledge by addressing key challenges and exploring new directions in the calculus of interval-valued functions.

Motivated by these challenges, we introduce novel arithmetic operations that restore complete linearity to
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
, transforming it into a proper Hilbert space equipped with a well-defined inner product “
\diamond
” and norm
\|\cdot\|
. This enhanced structure enables us to propose:

•

A new derivative concept unifying classical and multiplicative approaches;

•

An integral definition overcoming previous computational limitations;

•

A rigorous framework for interval differential equations.

The proposed calculus demonstrates superior computational efficiency compared to existing methods, while maintaining intuitive interpretability for applications ranging from uncertainty quantification to growth process modeling. Our results not only resolve long-standing theoretical inconsistencies, but also provide practical tools for working with interval-valued functions in scientific computing and engineering applications.

The rest of the paper are organized as follows.
In Section 2, we present new interval arithmetic operations, which lead to the space of all interval numbers being a linear space. Further, by introducing new distance, norm, and inner product formulas, we prove that the space of all interval numbers is a Hilbert space.
Section 3 introduces the interval-valued function and its continuity. Under the new norm, the space of continuous interval-valued functions is a Banach space.
Section 4 mainly introduces the new concept of the derivative of interval-valued functions and its related properties. The new derivative is a combination of the traditional derivative and the multiplicative derivative, which simplifies the differentiation of interval-valued functions and eliminates the need to consider the switching points. Compared to the gH-derivative, our derivative has advantages in computation.
In Section 5, we consider the Riemann-type integral of interval-valued functions and present many related properties, including linearity, monotonicity, subinterval integrability, and additivity. At the same time, the fundamental theorem of calculus, integration by parts formula, monotone convergence theorem, and dominated convergence theorem are also proved.
In Section 6, by using the well-known Schauder’s fixed point theorem, we present an existence theorem for the solutions of interval differential equations and demonstrate the effectiveness and practicality of our method through several specific examples.

The Space
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}

Let
\mathbb{R}
be the set of real numbers, and
a_{l},a_{r}\in\mathbb{R}
with
a_{l}<a_{r}
, the closed interval
\overline{a}=[a_{l},a_{r}]
is called an
interval number
.
Denote by
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
the set of all interval numbers, i.e.,

\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}=\{\overline{a}~|~\overline{a}=[a_{l},a_{r}],~a_{l}<a_{r}\}.

In this paper, real numbers are NOT considered as (degenerate) interval numbers.

For any interval number
\overline{a}=[a_{l},a_{r}]\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
, let

a_{c}=\frac{a_{l}+a_{r}}{2},\qquad a_{w}=\frac{a_{r}-a_{l}}{2}.

(2.1)

Then
a_{c}

(\in\mathbb{R})
is called the
center
of
\overline{a}
and
a_{w}

(>0)
is called the
radius
of
\overline{a}
. In fact, for any interval number, its center and radius are unique. In this sense, we can rewrite the interval number
\overline{a}=[a_{l},a_{r}]
by

\overline{a}\triangleq\langle a_{c};a_{w}\rangle.

This notation is also shown in many papers, such as

,

,

Definition 2.1
.

(

2
]
)
For any
\overline{a},\overline{b}\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}},

\overline{a}=\langle a_{c};a_{w}\rangle

\overline{b}=\langle b_{c};b_{w}\rangle,
we say that

(1)

(2)

\overline{a}<\overline{b}

or
a_{c}=b_{c}
and
a_{w}<b_{w}
.

(3)

\overline{a}\leq\overline{b}

\overline{a}<\overline{b}
or
\overline{a}=\overline{b}
.

Let

\overline{a}=\langle a_{c};a_{w}\rangle

\overline{b}=\langle b_{c};b_{w}\rangle,

\phi(\overline{a},\overline{b})=(a_{c}-b_{c})+\left(1-{\mathrm{sgn}(|a_{c}-b_{c}|)}\right)\left(\frac{a_{w}}{b_{w}}-1\right),

(2.2)

where
\mathrm{sgn}(.)
is the signum function.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that
\overline{a},~\overline{b}\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
, then

\overline{a}\leq\overline{b}
if and only if
\phi(\overline{a},\overline{b})\leq 0.

Proof.

(Sufficiency) Since
\phi(\overline{a},\overline{b})\leq 0,

if
a_{c}\neq b_{c}
, then

\phi(\overline{a},\overline{b})=a_{c}-b_{c}\leq 0,

hence,

a_{c}<b_{c},

which implies
\overline{a}<\overline{b}
;
if
a_{c}=b_{c}
, then

\phi(\overline{a},\overline{b})=\frac{a_{w}}{b_{w}}-1\leq 0,

i.e.,

a_{w}\leq b_{w},

which implies
\overline{a}\leq\overline{b}
.

(Necessity) If
\overline{a}=\overline{b}
, then

a_{c}=b_{c}
and
a_{w}=b_{w}
. Therefore

\phi(\overline{a},\overline{b})=0.

If
\overline{a}<\overline{b}
, then
a_{c}<b_{c}
or
a_{c}=b_{c}
and
a_{w}<b_{w}
. When
a_{c}<b_{c}
, one has

\phi(\overline{a},\overline{b})=a_{c}-b_{c}<0.

When
a_{c}=b_{c}
and
a_{w}<b_{w}
, one has

\phi(\overline{a},\overline{b})=\frac{a_{w}}{b_{w}}-1<0.

The proof is therefore complete.
∎

k\overline{a}\geq k\overline{b}
if
k<0
.

Proof.

(1)
1\overline{a}=\langle 1a_{c};a_{w}^{1}\rangle=\langle a_{c};a_{w}\rangle=\overline{a}
.

(2) Since

k(\overline{a}+\overline{b})=\langle k(a_{c}+b_{c});(a_{w}b_{w})^{k}\rangle

then

(3) Since

(k+l)\overline{a}=\langle(k+l)a_{c};a_{w}^{k+l}\rangle,

and

k\overline{a}+l\overline{a}=\langle ka_{c};a_{w}^{k}\rangle+\langle la_{c};a_{w}^{l}\rangle=\langle(k+l)a_{c};a_{w}^{k+l}\rangle,

then

(k+l)\overline{a}=k\overline{a}+l\overline{a}.

(4) Since

(kl)\overline{a}=\langle kla_{c};a_{w}^{kl}\rangle,

and

k(la)=k\langle la_{c};a_{w}^{l}\rangle=\langle kla_{c};a_{w}^{kl}\rangle,

then

(kl)\overline{a}=k(l\overline{a}).

(5) Since
\overline{a}\leq\overline{b}
, according to Definition
2.1
, we have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}a_{c}=b_{c},~a_{w}\leq b_{w},~\text{or}\\
a_{c}<b_{c}.\end{array}\right.

If
k\geq 0
, then we have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}ka_{c}=kb_{c},~a_{w}^{k}\leq b_{w}^{k},~\text{or}\\
ka_{c}<kb_{c},\end{array}\right.

which implies
k\overline{a}\leq k\overline{b}
.

If
k<0

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}ka_{c}=kb_{c},~a_{w}^{k}\geq b_{w}^{k},~\text{or}\\
ka_{c}>kb_{c},\end{array}\right.

which implies
k\overline{a}\geq k\overline{b}
.

Thus, we complete the proof.

Proposition 2.25
.

For any

(2)

(3)

Proposition 2.30
.

If
\overline{a},~\overline{b},~\overline{c}\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}},
then

(1)

(\overline{a}+\overline{b})\overline{c}=\overline{a}~\overline{c}+\overline{b}\overline{c};

(2)

(\overline{a}-\overline{b})\overline{c}=\overline{a}~\overline{c}-\overline{b}\overline{c};

(3)

k(\overline{a}\overline{b})=(k\overline{a})\overline{b}=\overline{a}(k\overline{b}),~k\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

(1)
(\overline{a}+\overline{b})\overline{c}=\langle a_{c}+b_{c};a_{w}b_{w}\rangle\langle c_{c};c_{w}\rangle=\langle(a_{c}+b_{c})c_{c};\mathrm{e}^{\ln(a_{w}b_{w})\ln c_{w}}\rangle.

\overline{a}~\overline{c}+\overline{b}\overline{c}=\langle a_{c}c_{c};\mathrm{e}^{\ln a_{w}\ln c_{w}}\rangle+\langle b_{c}c_{c};\mathrm{e}^{\ln b_{w}\ln c_{w}}\rangle=\langle a_{c}c_{c}+b_{c}c_{c};\mathrm{e}^{\ln a_{w}\ln c_{w}+\ln b_{w}\ln c_{w}}\rangle.

Hence,
(\overline{a}+\overline{b})\overline{c}=\overline{a}~\overline{c}+\overline{b}\overline{c}.

(2) The proof is similar to that of (1).

(3)
k(\overline{a}\overline{b})=k\langle a_{c}b_{c};\mathrm{e}^{\ln a_{w}\ln b_{w}}\rangle=\left\langle ka_{c}b_{c};\mathrm{e}^{k\ln a_{w}\ln c_{w}}\right\rangle
;

\overline{a}(k\overline{b})=\langle a_{c};a_{w}^{k}\rangle\langle kb_{c};b_{w}^{k}\rangle=\left\langle ka_{c}b_{c};\mathrm{e}^{\ln a_{w}\ln c_{w}^{k}}\right\rangle.

2.4.1
Distance

Definition 2.42
.

For any
\overline{a},\overline{b}\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
, we define

d(\overline{a},\overline{b})=\sqrt{(a_{c}-b_{c})^{2}+(\ln a_{w}-\ln b_{w})^{2}}.

By Definition
2.42
, it is easy to see that

Proposition 2.43
.

Assume that
\overline{a},\overline{b},\overline{c}\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
, then we have

(1)

d(\overline{a},\overline{b})\geq 0
, and
d(\overline{a},\overline{b})=0

\overline{a}=\overline{b}
;

(2)

d(k\overline{a},k\overline{b})=|k|d(\overline{a},\overline{b})
,

;

(3)

d(\overline{a},\overline{b})\leq d(\overline{a},\overline{c})+d(\overline{c},\overline{b})
.

Thus
d(.,.)
is a distance on
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
.
Hence,
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
is a distance space.

Definition 2.44

\overline{a}\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}

By Definition
2.44
, it is easy to see that

Proposition 2.45
.

Assume that
\overline{a},\overline{b}\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
, then we have

(1)

\|\overline{a}\|\geq 0
, and
\|\overline{a}\|=0

\overline{a}=\overline{0}
;

(2)

\|k\overline{a}\|=|k|\|\overline{a}\|
,

;

(3)

\|\overline{a}+\overline{b}\|\leq\|\overline{a}\|+\|\overline{b}\|

Thus
\|\cdot\|
is a norm on
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
.

Theorem 2.46
.

The space

Proof.

According to Proposition

, the theorem can be easily proved.

Lemma 2.47
.

For any
a,b\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
,

2\|\overline{a}\|+2\|\overline{b}\|=\|\overline{a}+\overline{b}\|^{2}+\|\overline{a}-\overline{b}\|^{2}.

Proof.

By Definition
2.44
, we have

\|\overline{a}+\overline{b}\|^{2}=(a_{c}+b_{c})^{2}+(\ln a_{w}+\ln b_{w})^{2},

and

\|\overline{a}-\overline{b}\|^{2}=(a_{c}-b_{c})^{2}+(\ln a_{w}-\ln b_{w})^{2}.

Thus,

\|\overline{a}+\overline{b}\|^{2}+\|\overline{a}-\overline{b}\|^{2}=2a_{c}^{2}+2b_{c}^{2}+2\ln^{2}a_{w}+2\ln^{2}b_{w}=2\|\overline{a}\|+2\|\overline{b}\|.

∎

Based on the norm, we give the definition of the limit in
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
.

Definition 2.48
.

Let
\overline{a}\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
and
\{\overline{a}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
, we say
\overline{a}
is the limit of the sequence
\{\overline{a}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
,
if

\|\overline{a}_{n}-\overline{a}\|\to 0,\text{ ~ as ~}n\to\infty,

and denoted by

\overline{a}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\overline{a}_{n}.

Theorem 2.49
.

Let
\overline{a}=\left\langle a_{c};a_{w}\right\rangle
and
\overline{a}_{n}=\left\langle(a_{n})_{c};(a_{n})_{w}\right\rangle
,
n=1,2,....
Then
\overline{a}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\overline{a}_{n}
if and only if

a_{c}=\lim_{n\to\infty}(a_{n})_{c},\text{~~~~ and~~~~}a_{w}=\lim_{n\to\infty}(a_{n})_{w}.

Proof.

(Necessity) Since
\overline{a}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\overline{a}_{n}
, one has

\|\overline{a}_{n}-\overline{a}\|=\sqrt{((a_{n})_{c}-a_{c})^{2}+(\ln(a_{n})_{w}-\ln a_{w})^{2}}\to 0,\text{ as }n\to\infty,

which implies that

(a_{n})_{c}\to a_{c},\qquad\ln(a_{n})_{w}\to\ln a_{w},\text{ as }n\to\infty,

i.e.,

a_{c}=\lim_{n\to\infty}(a_{n})_{c},\text{~~~~ and~~~~}a_{w}=\lim_{n\to\infty}(a_{n})_{w}.

(Sufficiency) The sufficiency is obvious according to the necessity.
∎

Theorem 2.50
.

\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
is a Banach space endowed with the norm
\|\cdot\|
.

Proof.

Let
\{\overline{a}_{m}|\overline{a}_{m}=\langle(a_{c})_{m};(a_{w})_{m}\rangle\},

m=1,2,3,...,
be a Cauchy sequence in
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
.
Thus, for all
\epsilon>0
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, when
m,n>N
, one has

\|\overline{a}_{m}-\overline{a}_{n}\|=\sqrt{((a_{m})_{c}-(a_{n})_{c})^{2}+(\ln(a_{m})_{w}-\ln(a_{n})_{w})^{2}}<\epsilon,

which implies
|(a_{m})_{c}-(a_{n})_{c}|<\epsilon
and
|\ln(a_{m})_{w}-\ln(a_{n})_{w}|<\epsilon
. Thus,

\{(a_{m})_{c}\}
and
\{\ln(a_{m})_{w}\}
are also Cauchy sequences in
\mathbb{R}
.
Therefore, there exist
b_{c},~b_{w}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\lim_{m\to\infty}(a_{m})_{c}=b_{c},\qquad\lim_{m\to\infty}\ln(a_{m})_{w}=b_{w}.

Hence, for arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
N>0
such that

|(a_{m})_{c}-b_{c}|<\varepsilon/\sqrt{2},\qquad|\ln(a_{m})_{w}-b_{w}|<\varepsilon/\sqrt{2},

when
m>N
.
Therefore, for
m>N
, we have

\|\overline{a}_{m}-\overline{b}\|\leq\sqrt{((a_{m})_{c}-b_{c})^{2}+(\ln(a_{m})_{w}-b_{w})^{2}}<\varepsilon,

where
\overline{b}=[b_{c}-\mathrm{e}^{b_{w}},b_{c}+\mathrm{e}^{b_{w}}]
. This implies that
\{\overline{a}_{m}\}
is convergent to
\overline{b}
in
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
. Therefore, the proof is completed.
∎

2.4.3
Inner Product

From Lemma
2.47
, we see that the norm in Definition
2.44
satisfies the parallelogram law, so the inner product on
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
can be given as follows.

Definition 2.51
.

For any
a,b\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
, we define

\overline{a}\diamond\overline{b}=\frac{1}{4}(\|\overline{a}+\overline{b}\|^{2}-\|\overline{a}-\overline{b}\|^{2})=a_{c}b_{c}+\ln a_{w}\ln b_{w}.

Proposition 2.52
.

Assume that
\overline{a},~\overline{b},~\overline{c}\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
,
k,~l\in\mathbb{R}
, then we have

(1)

Commutativity:
\overline{a}\diamond\overline{b}=\overline{b}\diamond\overline{a}
;

(2)

Linearity:
(k\overline{a}+l\overline{b})\diamond\overline{c}=k\overline{a}\diamond\overline{c}+l\overline{b}\diamond\overline{c}
;

(3)

Positive-Definiteness:
\overline{a}\diamond\overline{a}\geq 0
, and
\overline{a}\diamond\overline{a}=0\Leftrightarrow\overline{a}=\overline{0}
.

Proof.

(1) It is clear that

\overline{a}\diamond\overline{b}=a_{c}b_{c}+\ln a_{w}\ln b_{w}=b_{c}a_{c}+\ln b_{w}\ln a_{w}=\overline{b}\diamond\overline{a}.

(2) It follows that

\displaystyle(k\overline{a}+l\overline{b})\diamond\overline{c}

\displaystyle=\langle ka_{c}+lb_{c};{a_{c}}^{k}{b_{c}}^{l}\rangle\diamond\langle c_{c};c_{w}\rangle

\displaystyle=(ka_{c}+lb_{c})c_{c}+\ln({a_{c}}^{k}{b_{c}}^{l})\ln c_{w}

\displaystyle=ka_{c}c_{c}+k\ln{a_{c}}\ln c_{w}+lb_{c}c_{c}+l\ln{b_{c}}\ln c_{w}

\displaystyle=k\overline{a}\diamond\overline{c}+l\overline{b}\diamond\overline{c}.

(3) We observe that
\overline{a}\diamond\overline{a}=a_{c}^{2}+\ln^{2}a_{w}\geq 0
and
\overline{a}\diamond\overline{a}=0\Leftrightarrow\overline{a}=\overline{0}
.
∎

Thus, “
\diamond
” is an inner product on
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
.

Lemma 2.53
.

\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
is an inner product space under the inner product “
\diamond
”.

Theorem 2.54
.

\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
is a Hilbert space.

Proof.

By Definition
2.51
, we have
\|\overline{a}\|=\sqrt{\overline{a}\diamond\overline{a}}
for any
\overline{a}\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
. This implies that the norm is induced from the inner product. It follows from Theorem
2.50
that
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
is a Banach space endowed with the above norm
\|\cdot\|
. Thus, we complete the proof.
∎

3
The interval-valued function

Let
[a,b]\subset\mathbb{R}
,
a mapping
\overline{f}:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
is called an interval-valued function (IVF).
For any IVF
\overline{f}:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
, there exist two real functions
f_{l},f_{r}:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\overline{f}(x)=[f_{l}(x),f_{r}(x)],\qquad x\in[a,b],

where
f_{l},f_{r}
are called the left and right endpoint functions of
\overline{f}
, respectively.
Now let

f_{c}(x)=\frac{f_{l}(x)+f_{r}(x)}{2},\qquad f_{w}(x)=\frac{f_{r}(x)-f_{l}(x)}{2},\qquad x\in[a,b],

then
f_{c}
is called the
center function
of
\overline{f}
, and
f_{w}
is called the
radius function
of
\overline{f}
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\overline{f},\overline{g}:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
, we define

\overline{f}(x)\star\overline{g}(x):=\langle f_{c}(x);f_{w}(x)\rangle\star\langle g_{c}(x);g_{w}(x)\rangle,\qquad x\in[a,b],

where
\star\in\{+,-,\times,\div\}
.

.

Considering that
\overline{f}(x)=[0,2]-[-1,1]|x-2|
and
\overline{g}(x)=[0,2]\ominus_{gH}[-1,1]\odot|x-2|

.
Thus,
\overline{f}(x)=[0,2]
on [0,4] and

\displaystyle\overline{g}(x)=\begin{cases}[|x-2|,2-|x-2|],&x\in[0,1)\cup(3,4],\\
[2-|x-2|,|x-2|],&x\in[1,3].\end{cases}

see Figure
1
.

\overline{f}
and
\overline{g}
in Example

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\overline{f}:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
,
g:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
, we define

\overline{f}(x)\star g(x):=\overline{f}(x)\star(\overline{1}~g(x)),\qquad x\in[a,b],

g(x)\star\overline{f}(x):=(\overline{1}~g(x))\star\overline{f}(x),\qquad x\in[a,b],

where
\star\in\{+,-,\times,\div\}
.

Now we introduce the continuity of IVFs.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\overline{f}:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
be an interval-valued function, given a point
x\in[a,b]
, if for arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

d(\overline{f}(x),\overline{f}(y))<\varepsilon,

when

. Then
\overline{f}
is called continuous at

. Further,
\overline{f}
is called continuous on
[a,b]
if
\overline{f}
is continuous on every
x\in[a,b]
.

Denoted by
C([a,b],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}})
the space of all continuous IVFs on
[a,b]
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\overline{f}:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
be an interval-valued function. Then
\overline{f}
is continuous on
[a,b]
if and only if
f_{l}
and
f_{r}
are continuous on
[a,b]
.

Proof.

(Sufficiency) Since
f_{l}
and
f_{r}
are continuous on
[a,b]
, then

f_{c}=\frac{f_{l}+f_{r}}{2}
and
f_{w}=\frac{f_{r}-f_{l}}{2}
are also continuous on
[a,b]
, so is
\ln f_{w}
.
Let
x\in[a,b]
, then for arbitrary
\varepsilon_{1}>0
, there exists
\delta_{1}>0
such that

|f_{c}(x)-f_{c}(y)|\leq\varepsilon_{1},

where
y\in O(x,\delta_{1})\cap[a,b]
.
Also, for arbitrary
\varepsilon_{2}>0
, there exists
\delta_{2}>0
such that

|\ln f_{w}(x)-\ln f_{w}(y)|\leq\varepsilon_{2},

where
y\in O(x,\delta_{2})\cap[a,b]
.
Let
\delta=\min\{\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\}
,
\varepsilon=\max\{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\}
,
Thus,

d(\overline{f}(x),\overline{f}(y))=\sqrt{|f_{c}(x)-f_{c}(y)|^{2}+|\ln f_{w}(x)-\ln f_{w}(y)|^{2}}\leq\sqrt{\varepsilon_{1}^{2}+\varepsilon_{2}^{2}}\leq\sqrt{2}\varepsilon,

where

. By Definition

\overline{f}
is continuous on
[a,b]
.

(Necessity) Since
\overline{f}
is continuous on
[a,b]
, for arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

d(\overline{f}(x),\overline{f}(y))=\sqrt{|f_{c}(x)-f_{c}(y)|^{2}+|\ln f_{w}(x)-\ln f_{w}(y)|^{2}}\leq\varepsilon,~~x\in[a,b],

where
y\in O(x,\delta)\cap[a,b]
. Thus,

|f_{c}(x)-f_{c}(y)|\leq\varepsilon
, and
|\ln f_{w}(x)-\ln f_{w}(y)|\leq\varepsilon
,
which imply
f_{c}
and
\ln f_{w}
are also continuous on
[a,b]
, so is
f_{w}
.
Therefore,

f_{l}={f_{c}-f_{w}}
and
f_{r}={f_{c}+f_{w}}
are also continuous on
[a,b]
.
∎

Definition 3.6
.

For any
\overline{f}\in C([a,b],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}})
, we define

\|\overline{f}\|_{\infty}=\max_{x\in[a,b]}\|\overline{f}(x)\|.

By Definition
3.6
, it is easy to see that

Proposition 3.7
.

Assume that
\overline{f},\overline{g}\in C([a,b],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}})
,
k\in\mathbb{R}
, then we have

(1)

\|\overline{f}\|_{\infty}\geq 0
, and
\|\overline{f}\|_{\infty}=0
if and only if
\overline{f}=\overline{0}
;

(2)

\|k\overline{f}\|_{\infty}=|k|\|\overline{f}\|_{\infty}
;

(3)

\|\overline{f}+\overline{g}\|_{\infty}\leq\|\overline{f}\|_{\infty}+\|\overline{g}\|_{\infty}
.

Proof.

(1)

\|\overline{f}\|_{\infty}=\max_{x\in[a,b]}\|\overline{f}(x)\|=\max_{x\in[a,b]}\sqrt{f_{c}^{2}(x)+\ln^{2}f_{w}(x)}\geq 0.

Thus,
\|\overline{f}\|_{\infty}=0
if and only if
\overline{f}=\overline{0}
.

(2)
\|k\overline{f}\|_{\infty}=\max_{x\in[a,b]}\|k\overline{f}(x)\|=\max_{x\in[a,b]}\sqrt{k^{2}f_{c}^{2}(x)+k^{2}\ln^{2}f_{w}(x)}=|k|\|\overline{f}\|_{\infty}
.

(3)

\displaystyle\|\overline{f}+\overline{g}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\max_{x\in[a,b]}\sqrt{(f_{c}(x)+g_{c}(x))^{2}+(\ln f_{w}(x)+\ln g_{w}(x))^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{x\in[a,b]}\sqrt{\|\overline{f}\|_{\infty}^{2}+2f_{c}(x)g_{c}(x)+2\ln f_{w}(x)\ln g_{w}(x)+\|\overline{g}\|_{\infty}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\|\overline{f}\|_{\infty}^{2}+2\|\overline{f}\|_{\infty}\|\overline{g}\|_{\infty}+\|\overline{g}\|_{\infty}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\|\overline{f}\|_{\infty}+\|\overline{g}\|_{\infty}.

This ends the proof.
∎

According to Proposition
3.7
,
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
is a norm on
C([a,b],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}})
, and

Lemma 3.8
.

The space
C([a,b],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}})
endowed with the norm

is a normed space.

Further, we can prove that

Theorem 3.9
.

The space
C([a,b],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}})
endowed with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
is a Banach space.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Theorem
2.50
, so we omit it.
∎

4
Derivative of IVFs

In this section, we will present a new Derivative of IVFs based on the new subtraction given in Definition
2.21
, and
discuss its relationship with the classical derivative, the multiplicative derivative given in
[
1
]
, and the
gH
-derivative given in
[
37
]
, respectively.

4.1
Basic Definition and Properties

Definition 4.1
.

Suppose that the IVF
\overline{f}:[a,b]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
and
x_{0}\in[a,b]
and
x_{0}+h\in[a,b]
, then
\overline{f}
is said to be differentiable at
x_{0}
if there exists
\overline{f}^{\prime}(x_{0})\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
such that

\overline{f}^{\prime}(x_{0})=\lim\limits_{h\rightarrow 0}\frac{\overline{f}(x_{0}+h)-\overline{f}(x_{0})}{h}.

(4.1)

We say
\overline{f}
is differentiable on
[a,b]
if it is differentiable at each
x_{0}\in[a,b]
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\overline{f}:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
be an IVF with
\overline{f}(x)=\left\langle{f}_{c}(x);{f}_{w}(x)\right\rangle
. Then
\overline{f}
is differentiable at
x\in[a,b]
if and only if
{f}_{c}
is differentiable at
x
, and
{f}_{w}
is multiplicative differential at
x
. Moreover,

\overline{f}^{\prime}(x)=\left\langle{f}_{c}^{\prime}(x);{f}_{w}^{*}(x)\right\rangle,

where
{f}_{w}^{*}
is the multiplicative derivative of
f_{w}
on
[a,b]
(see details in
[
1
]
), i.e.,

f^{*}(x)=\lim_{h\to 0}\left(\frac{{f}(x+h)}{{f}(x)}\right)^{\frac{1}{h}}=\mathrm{e}^{(\ln f)^{\prime}(x)}.

(4.2)

Proof.

By Definition
4.1
,

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{\overline{f}\left({{x}}+h\right)-\overline{f}\left({{x}}\right)}{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{\langle f_{c}({{x}}+h);f_{w}({{x}}+h)\rangle-\langle f_{c}({{x}});f_{w}({{x}})\rangle}{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\left\langle\frac{f_{c}({{x}}+h)-f_{c}({{x}})}{h};\left(\frac{f_{w}(x+h)}{f_{w}({{x}})}\right)^{\frac{1}{h}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle{f}_{c}^{\prime}(x_{0});{f}_{w}^{*}(x_{0})\right\rangle.

This ends the proof.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Theorem
4.2
implies that the derivative of an IVF
\overline{f}
on
[a,b]
equals to the classical derivative of the center function
f_{c}
, and the multiplicative derivative of the radius function
f_{w}
.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
\overline{F},\overline{f}:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
, if

\overline{F}^{\prime}(x)=\overline{f}(x),~~x\in[a,b],

then

\overline{F}
is called a primitive of
\overline{f}
on
[a,b]
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Suppose that
\overline{f},\overline{g}:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
are differentiable on
[a,b]
. Then for any
x\in[a,b]
,

(1)

{{\left(c_{1}\overline{f}+c_{2}\overline{g}\right)}{{}^{\prime}}}\left({{x}}\right)=c_{1}{{\overline{f}}{{}^{\prime}}}\left({{x}}\right)+c_{2}{{\overline{g}}{{}^{\prime}}}\left({{x}}\right),
where
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}.

(2)

(\overline{f}~\overline{g})^{\prime}(x)=\overline{f}^{\prime}(x)\overline{g}(x)+\overline{f}(x)\overline{g}^{\prime}(x).

(3)

\left(\frac{~\overline{f}~}{\overline{g}}\right)^{\prime}(x)=\displaystyle\frac{\overline{f}^{\prime}(x)\overline{g}(x)-\overline{f}(x)\overline{g}^{\prime}(x)}{\overline{g}^{2}(x)}.

Proof.

(1)

\displaystyle{{\left(c_{1}\overline{f}+c_{2}\overline{g}\right)}{{}^{\prime}}}\left({{x}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{(c_{1}\overline{f}(x_{0}+h)+c_{2}\overline{g}(x_{0}+h))-(c_{1}\overline{f}(x_{0})+c_{2}\overline{g}(x_{0}))}{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\left(c_{1}\frac{\overline{f}(x_{0}+h)-\overline{f}(x_{0})}{h}+c_{2}\frac{\overline{g}(x_{0}+h)-\overline{g}(x_{0})}{h}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{1}{{\overline{f}}{{}^{\prime}}}\left({{x}}\right)+c_{2}{{\overline{g}}{{}^{\prime}}}\left({{x}}\right).

(2)

\displaystyle{{\left(\overline{f}(x)\overline{g}\right)}{{}^{\prime}}}\left({{x}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle f_{c}(x)g_{c}(x);\mathrm{e}^{\ln f_{w}(x)\ln g_{w}(x)}\rangle^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle f_{c}^{\prime}(x)g_{c}(x)+f_{c}(x_{0})g_{c}^{\prime}(x);\mathrm{e}^{(\ln f_{w})^{\prime}(x)\ln g_{w}(x)+\ln f_{w}(x)(\ln g_{w})^{\prime}(x)}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle f_{c}^{\prime}(x)g_{c}(x);\mathrm{e}^{\ln f_{w}^{\prime}(x)\ln g_{w}(x)}\rangle+\langle f_{c}(x)g_{c}^{\prime}(x);\mathrm{e}^{\ln f_{w}(x)(\ln g_{w})(x)}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle f_{c}^{\prime}(x);f_{w}^{*}(x)\rangle\langle g_{c}(x);g_{w}(x)\rangle+\langle f_{c}(x);f_{w}(x)\rangle\langle g_{c}^{\prime}(x);g_{w}^{*}(x)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{f}^{\prime}(x)\overline{g}(x)+\overline{f}(x)\overline{g}^{\prime}(x).

(3)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{~\overline{f}~}{\overline{g}}\right)^{\prime}(x)=

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{\frac{\overline{f}(x+h)}{\overline{g}(x+h)}-\frac{\overline{f}(x)}{\overline{g}(x)}}{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\left\langle\frac{\frac{f_{c}(x+h)}{g_{c}(x+h)}-\frac{f_{c}(x)}{g_{c}(x)}}{h};\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{h}\left(\frac{\ln f_{w}(x+h)}{\ln g_{w}(x+h)}-\frac{\ln f_{w}(x)}{\ln g_{w}(x)}\right)}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\frac{{f_{c}^{\prime}(x)}{g_{c}(x)}-{f_{c}(x)}{g_{c}^{\prime}(x)}}{g_{c}^{2}(x)};\mathrm{e}^{\frac{{(\ln f_{w})^{\prime}(x)}{\ln g_{w}(x)}-{\ln f_{w}(x)}{(\ln g_{w})^{\prime}(x)}}{(\ln g_{w}(x))^{2}}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left\langle{{f_{c}^{\prime}(x)}{g_{c}(x)}-{f_{c}(x)}{g_{c}^{\prime}(x)}};\mathrm{e}^{{{(\ln f_{w})^{\prime}(x)}{\ln g_{w}(x)}-{\ln f_{w}(x)}{(\ln g_{w})^{\prime}(x)}}}\right\rangle}{\overline{g}^{2}(x)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left\langle f_{c}^{\prime}(x)g_{c}(x);\mathrm{e}^{(\ln f_{w})^{\prime}(x)\ln g_{w}(x)}\right\rangle-\left\langle f_{c}(x)g_{c}^{\prime}(x);\mathrm{e}^{\ln f_{w}(x)(\ln g_{w})^{\prime}(x)}\right\rangle}{\overline{g}^{2}(x)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\overline{f}^{\prime}(x)\overline{g}(x)-\overline{f}(x)\overline{g}^{\prime}(x)}{\overline{g}(x)^{2}}.

The proof is therefore ended.
∎

Corollary 4.6
.

Suppose that
\overline{f}:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
are differentiable on
[a,b]
, and that
g:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
is differentiable on
[a,b]
. Then for any
x\in[a,b]
,

(1)

{{\left(c_{1}\overline{f}+c_{2}g\right)}{{}^{\prime}}}\left({{x}}\right)=c_{1}{{\overline{f}}{{}^{\prime}}}\left({{x}}\right)+c_{2}{{g}{{}^{\prime}}}\left({{x}}\right),
where
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}.

(2)

(\overline{f}~g)^{\prime}(x)=\overline{f}^{\prime}(x)g(x)+\overline{f}(x)g^{\prime}(x).

(3)

\left(\frac{~\overline{f}~}{g}\right)^{\prime}(x)=\displaystyle\frac{\overline{f}^{\prime}(x)g(x)-\overline{f}(x)g^{\prime}(x)}{g^{2}(x)}.

(4)

\left(\frac{~g~}{\overline{f}}\right)^{\prime}(x)=\displaystyle\frac{g^{\prime}(x)\overline{f}(x)-g(x)\overline{f}^{\prime}(x)}{\overline{f}^{2}(x)}.

Proof.

This is a direct conclusion of Proposition
4.5
and Definition
3.3
.
∎

Theorem 4.7
.

Suppose that
\overline{f}:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
is differentiable on
[a,b]
. Then for any
x\in[a,b]
,

{{\left(\overline{f}\circ g\right)}{{}^{\prime}}}\left({{x}}\right)={{\overline{f}}{{}^{\prime}}}\left({{y}}\right){{g}{{}^{\prime}}}\left({{x}}\right),

where
g:[a,b]\to[a,b]
is differentiable, and
{{y}}=g\left({{x}}\right)
.

Proof.

\displaystyle\left(\overline{f}\circ g\right)^{\prime}\left(x\right)=

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{\overline{f}(g(x+h))-\overline{f}(g(x))}{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\left\langle\frac{f_{c}(g(x+h))-f_{c}(g(x))}{h};\left(\frac{f_{w}(g(x+h))}{f_{w}(g(x))}\right)^{\frac{1}{h}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle f_{c}^{\prime}(y)g^{\prime}(x);\mathrm{e}^{\frac{f_{w}^{\prime}(y)}{f_{w}(y)}g^{\prime}(x)}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{{\overline{f}}{{}^{\prime}}}\left({{y}}\right){{g}{{}^{\prime}}}\left({{x}}\right).

This ends the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
\overline{f}:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
be an interval-valued function. If
\overline{f}
is differentiable on
[a,b]
, then
\overline{f}
is continuous on
[a,b]
.

Proof.

Since
\overline{f}
is differentiable on
[a,b]
, then by Theorem
4.2
,

\overline{f}^{\prime}\left(x\right)=\left\langle{f}_{c}^{\prime}(x);{f}_{w}^{*}(x)\right\rangle.

From (
4.2
), it follows that

{f}_{w}^{*}(x)=\mathrm{e}^{(\ln f_{w})^{\prime}(x)}=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{f_{w}^{\prime}(x)}{f_{w}(x)}},

which implies that

is differentiable on
[a,b]
.
Hence,
f_{l}=f_{c}-f_{w}
and
f_{r}=f_{c}+f_{w}
are also differentiable on
[a,b]
.
Thus,
f_{l}
and
f_{r}
are continuous on
[a,b]
, by Theorem
3.5
, so is
\overline{f}
.
∎

5
Integration of IVFs

In this subsection, we will introduce the concept of Riemann type integrals of interval-valued functions and its properties.

5.1
Basic Definition and Properties

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\overline{f}:\left[a,b\right]\to{{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathcal{I}}},\overline{A}\in{{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathcal{I}}}
. If for arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a constant
\delta>0
such that for any division
P
of
\left[a,b\right]
given by

a={{t}_{0}}<{{t}_{1}}<{{t}_{2}}<\cdots<{{t}_{n-1}}<{{t}_{n}}=b,\text{
and }(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},...,\xi_{n})

satisfies
t_{i-1}\leq\xi_{i}\leq t_{i}

and
t_{i}-t_{i-1}<\delta
for all
i
, one has

d\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\overline{f}\left({{\xi}_{i}}\right)\left({{t}_{i}}-{{t}_{i-1}}\right)},\overline{A}\right)<\varepsilon.

Then
\overline{f}
is said to be interval Riemann integrable to
\overline{A}
on
\left[a,b\right]
, and denoted by

\overline{A}=\left(IR\right)\int_{a}^{b}{\overline{f}\left(t\right)}\dd t.

Denote by
\mathcal{IR}([a,b],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}})
the space of all interval Riemann integrable IVFs
\overline{f}
on
[a,b]
. Also, denote by
\mathcal{R}([a,b],\mathbb{R})
the space of all Riemann integrable funcitons
f
on
[a,b]
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Suppose that
\overline{f}:\left[a,b\right]\to{{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathcal{I}}}
with
\overline{f}\left(t\right)=\left\langle{f}_{c}\left(t\right),{f}_{w}\left(t\right)\right\rangle,t\in\left[a,b\right]
, then
\overline{f}\in\mathcal{I}\mathcal{R}\left(\left[a,b\right],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}\right)
if and only if
{f}_{c},\ln{f}_{w}\in\mathcal{R}\left(\left[a,b\right],\mathbb{R}\right)
. Moreover,

\left(IR\right)\int_{a}^{b}{\overline{f}\left(t\right)}\dd t=\left\langle\left(R\right)\int_{a}^{b}{{f}_{c}\left(t\right)}\dd t;\left(R\right)\int_{a}^{b}{{f}_{w}\left(t\right)}^{\dd t}\right\rangle,

where
\left(R\right)\int_{a}^{b}{{f}_{w}\left(t\right)}^{\dd t}
is the multiplication integral of
{f}_{w}
on
[a,b]
(see details in
[
1
]
), i.e.,

\displaystyle(R)\int_{a}^{b}{{f}_{w}\left(t\right)}^{\dd t}=\mathrm{e}^{(R)\int_{a}^{b}(\ln f_{w})(t)\dd t}.

(5.1)

Proof.

By Definition
4.1
, one has

\displaystyle\left(IR\right)\int_{a}^{b}{\overline{f}\left(t\right)}\dd t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{\overline{f}\left({{\xi}_{i}}\right)\left({{t}_{i}}-{{t}_{i-1}}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{\langle f_{c}\left({{\xi}_{i}}\right);f_{w}(\xi_{i})\rangle\left({{t}_{i}}-{{t}_{i-1}}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}{\left\langle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}f_{c}\left({{\xi}_{i}}\right)\left({{t}_{i}}-{{t}_{i-1}}\right);\prod_{i=1}^{n}f_{w}(\xi_{i})^{\left({{t}_{i}}-{{t}_{i-1}}\right)}\right\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(R\right)\int_{a}^{b}{{f}_{c}\left(t\right)}\dd t;\mathrm{e}^{(R)\int_{a}^{b}(\ln f_{w})(t)\dd t}\right\rangle,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(R\right)\int_{a}^{b}{{f}_{c}\left(t\right)}\dd t;\left(R\right)\int_{a}^{b}{{f}_{w}\left(t\right)}^{\dd t}\right\rangle.

This ends the proof.

The interval Riemann integral has the following properties.

Proposition 5.4
.

(1)
(Monotonicity)
. Suppose that
\overline{f},\overline{g}\in\mathcal{I}\mathcal{R}\left(\left[a,b\right],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}\right)
. If
\overline{f}\left(t\right)\leq\overline{g}\left(t\right)
for any
t\in\left[a,b\right]
, then

\left(IR\right)\int_{a}^{b}{\overline{f}\left(t\right)}\dd t\leq\left(IR\right)\int_{a}^{b}{\overline{g}\left(t\right)}\dd t.

(2)
(Linearity)
. Suppose that
\overline{f},\overline{g}\in\mathcal{I}\mathcal{R}\left(\left[a,b\right],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}\right),\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
, then

\lambda_{1}\overline{f}+\lambda_{2}\overline{g}\in\mathcal{I}\mathcal{R}\left(\left[a,b\right],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}\right)
, and

\left(IR\right)\int_{a}^{b}{\left[\lambda_{1}\overline{f}\left(t\right)\ +\lambda_{2}\overline{g}\left(t\right)\right]}\dd t=\lambda_{1}\left(IR\right)\int_{a}^{b}{\overline{f}\left(t\right)}\dd t+\lambda_{2}\left(IR\right)\int_{a}^{b}{\overline{g}\left(t\right)}\dd t.

(3)
(Subinterval Integrability)
. If IVF
\overline{f}\in\mathcal{I}\mathcal{R}\left(\left[a,b\right],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}\right)
, then so
it is on a subinterval

\left[c,d\right]\subseteq\left[a,b\right]
.

(4)
(Additivity)
. Suppose that
\overline{f}\in\mathcal{I}\mathcal{R}\left(\left[a,b\right],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}\right)
, then for any
c\in\left[a,b\right]
, it holds

\left(IR\right)\int_{a}^{b}{\overline{f}\left(t\right)}\dd t=\left(IR\right)\int_{a}^{c}{\overline{f}\left(t\right)}\dd t+\left(IR\right)\int_{c}^{b}{\overline{f}\left(t\right)}\dd t.

Proof.

(1) By Definition
2.1
, there are three cases in “
\leq
”.

(I) If
\overline{f}\left(t\right)=\overline{g}\left(t\right)
,
t\in[a,b]
, then it is easy to see that

\left(IR\right)\int_{a}^{b}{\overline{f}\left(t\right)}\dd t=\left(IR\right)\int_{a}^{b}{\overline{g}\left(t\right)}\dd t.

(II) If
f_{c}\left(t\right)<g_{c}\left(t\right)
,
t\in[a,b]
, then one has

\left(R\right)\int_{a}^{b}{f_{c}\left(t\right)}\dd t<\left(R\right)\int_{a}^{b}{g_{c}\left(t\right)}\dd t,

that is,

\left(IR\right)\int_{a}^{b}{\overline{f}\left(t\right)}\dd t\leq\left(IR\right)\int_{a}^{b}{\overline{g}\left(t\right)}\dd t.

(III) If
f_{c}\left(t\right)=g_{c}\left(t\right)
,
f_{w}\left(t\right)<g_{w}\left(t\right)
,
t\in[a,b]
, then we have

\left(R\right)\int_{a}^{b}{\ln f_{w}\left(t\right)}\dd t<\left(R\right)\int_{a}^{b}{\ln g_{w}\left(t\right)}\dd t,

by (
5.1
), it holds

\left(IR\right)\int_{a}^{b}{\overline{f}\left(t\right)}\dd t\leq\left(IR\right)\int_{a}^{b}{\overline{g}\left(t\right)}\dd t.

(2) Since
\overline{f},\overline{g}\in\mathcal{I}\mathcal{R}\left(\left[a,b\right],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}\right)
, by Lemma
5.2
, the functions
f_{c},\ln f_{w},g_{c},\ln g_{w}\in\mathcal{R}\left(\left[a,b\right],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}\right),
so are
\lambda_{1}f_{c}+\lambda_{2}g_{c}
and
\lambda_{1}\ln f_{w}+\lambda_{2}\ln g_{w}
. Then

\lambda_{1}\overline{f}+\lambda_{2}\overline{g}=\left\langle\lambda_{1}f_{c}+\lambda_{2}g_{c};\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_{1}\ln f_{w}+\lambda_{2}\ln g_{w}}\right\rangle\in\mathcal{I}\mathcal{R}\left(\left[a,b\right],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}\right).

Moreover,

\displaystyle\left(IR\right)\int_{a}^{b}{\left[\lambda_{1}\overline{f}\left(t\right)\ +\lambda_{2}\overline{g}\left(t\right)\right]}\dd t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(IR)\int_{a}^{b}\left\langle\lambda_{1}f_{c}(t)+\lambda_{2}g_{c}(t);\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_{1}\ln f_{w}(t)+\lambda_{2}\ln g_{w}(t)}\right\rangle\dd t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle(R)\int_{a}^{b}\lambda_{1}f_{c}(t)+\lambda_{2}g_{c}(t)\dd t;\mathrm{e}^{(R)\int_{a}^{b}\lambda_{1}\ln f_{w}(t)+\lambda_{2}\ln g_{w}(t)\dd t}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\lambda_{1}(R)\int_{a}^{b}f_{c}(t)\dd t+\lambda_{2}(R)\int_{a}^{b}g_{c}(t)\dd t;\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_{1}(R)\int_{a}^{b}\ln f_{w}(t)\dd t+\lambda_{2}(R)\int_{a}^{b}\ln g_{w}(t)\dd t}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\lambda_{1}(R)\int_{a}^{b}f_{c}(t)\dd t;\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_{1}(R)\int_{a}^{b}\ln f_{w}(t)\dd t}\right\rangle+\left\langle\lambda_{2}(R)\int_{a}^{b}g_{c}(t)\dd t;\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_{2}(R)\int_{a}^{b}\ln g_{w}(t)\dd t}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\left(IR\right)\int_{a}^{b}{\overline{f}\left(t\right)}\dd t+\lambda_{2}\left(IR\right)\int_{a}^{b}{\overline{g}\left(t\right)}\dd t.

(3) Since
\overline{f}\in\mathcal{I}\mathcal{R}\left(\left[a,b\right],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}\right)
, by Lemma
5.2
, the functions
f_{c},\ln f_{w}\in\mathcal{R}\left(\left[a,b\right],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}\right).
Hence, for any
[c,d]\subseteq[a,b]
, the functions
f_{c},\ln f_{w}\in\mathcal{R}\left(\left[c,d\right],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}\right)
, thereby,
\overline{f}\in\mathcal{I}\mathcal{R}\left(\left[c,d\right],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}\right)
.

(4) Since
\overline{f}\in\mathcal{I}\mathcal{R}\left(\left[a,b\right],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}\right)
, by Lemma
5.2
, the functions
f_{c},\ln f_{w}\in\mathcal{R}\left(\left[a,b\right],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}\right).
Hence, for any
c\in[a,b]
, the functions
f_{c},\ln f_{w}\in\mathcal{R}\left(\left[a,c\right],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}\right)
, and also
f_{c},\ln f_{w}\in\mathcal{R}\left(\left[c,b\right],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}\right)
, thereby,

\displaystyle\left(IR\right)\int_{a}^{b}{\overline{f}\left(t\right)}\dd t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle(R)\int_{a}^{b}f_{c}(t)\dd t;\mathrm{e}^{(R)\int_{a}^{b}\ln f_{w}(t)\dd t}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle(R)\int_{a}^{c}f_{c}(t)\dd t+(R)\int_{c}^{b}f_{c}(t)\dd t;\mathrm{e}^{(R)\int_{a}^{c}\ln f_{w}(t)\dd t+(R)\int_{c}^{b}\ln f_{w}(t)\dd t}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle(R)\int_{a}^{c}f_{c}(t)\dd t;\mathrm{e}^{(R)\int_{a}^{c}\ln f_{w}(t)\dd t}\right\rangle+\left\langle(R)\int_{c}^{b}f_{c}(t)\dd t;\mathrm{e}^{(R)\int_{c}^{b}\ln f_{w}(t)\dd t}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(IR\right)\int_{a}^{c}{\overline{f}\left(t\right)}\dd t+\left(IR\right)\int_{c}^{b}{\overline{f}\left(t\right)}\dd t.

This ends the proof.
∎

and Definition

Fundamental Theorem of Calculus

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
\overline{f}\in\mathcal{IR}([a,b],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}})
, if
\overline{F}:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}

\overline{F}(t)=(IR)\int_{a}^{t}\overline{f}(s)\dd s,~~t\in[a,b],

\overline{F}

[a,b]
and
\overline{F}^{\prime}=\overline{f}
at all points
of continuity of
\overline{f}
.

Proof.

Since
\overline{f}\in\mathcal{I}\mathcal{R}\left(\left[a,b\right],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}\right)

\overline{F}(t)=(IR)\int_{a}^{t}\overline{f}(s)\dd s,~~t\in[a,b],

then for any

\displaystyle d(\overline{F}(t_{1}),\overline{F}(t_{2}))=

\displaystyle d\left((IR)\int_{a}^{t_{1}}\overline{f}(s)\dd s,(IR)\int_{a}^{t_{2}}\overline{f}(s)\dd s\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\left((R)\int_{t_{2}}^{t_{1}}f_{c}(s)\dd s\right)^{2}+\left((R)\int_{t_{2}}^{t_{1}}\ln f_{w}(s)\dd s\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle 0,\text{ as }t_{1}\to t_{2},

\overline{F}
is continuous on
[a,b]
.

\overline{f}
and

\displaystyle\overline{F}^{\prime}(x)=

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{\overline{F}(x+h)-\overline{F}(x)}{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{(IR)\int_{x}^{x+h}\overline{f}(s)\dd s}{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\overline{f}(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{f}(x).

This ends the proof.

(iii)
(IR)\int_{a}^{b}\overline{f}_{n}(t)\dd t
converges to
\overline{A}\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Then
\overline{f}\in\mathcal{IR}([a,b],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}})
and

(IR)\int_{a}^{b}\overline{f}(t)\dd t=\overline{A}.

Proof.

Let
\overline{f}(t)=\langle{f}_{c}(t);{f}_{w}(t)\rangle
,
\overline{f}_{n}(t)=\langle({f}_{n})_{c}(t);({f}_{n})_{w}(t)\rangle
,
t\in[a,b]
,
n=1,2,...,

\overline{A}=\langle A_{c};A_{w}\rangle
.
Since
\overline{f}_{n}\in\mathcal{IR}([a,b],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}})
,
n=1,2,...,
by Lemma
5.2
, one has

({f}_{n})_{c},\ln({f}_{n})_{w}\in\mathcal{R}([a,b],\mathbb{R}),~~n=1,2,....

It follows from (i), (iii) and Theorem
2.49
that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}({f}_{n})_{c}(t)={f}_{c}(t),~~~~\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}({f}_{n})_{w}(t)={f}_{w}(t),~~t\in[a,b],

and

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(R)\int_{a}^{b}({f}_{n})_{c}(t)\dd t=A_{c},~~~~\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(R)\int_{a}^{b}\ln({f}_{n})_{w}(t)\dd t=\ln A_{w}.

Moreover, by (ii) and Definition
2.4
, we have

({f}_{1})_{c}(t)\leq({f}_{2})_{c}(t)\leq...\leq({f}_{n})_{c}(t)\leq...,~~t\in[a,b],

and

({f}_{1})_{w}(t)\leq({f}_{2})_{w}(t)\leq...\leq({f}_{n})_{w}(t)\leq...,~~t\in[a,b],

i.e.,

\ln({f}_{1})_{w}(t)\leq\ln({f}_{2})_{w}(t)\leq...\leq\ln({f}_{n})_{w}(t)\leq...,~~t\in[a,b].

According to
[
16
, Theorem 4.1]
, it holds that
{f}_{c},{f}_{w}\in\mathcal{R}([a,b],\mathbb{R})
and

(R)\int_{a}^{b}{f}_{c}(t)\dd t=A_{c},~~~~(R)\int_{a}^{b}\ln{f}_{w}(t)\dd t=\ln A_{w}.

This implies that

(IR)\int_{a}^{b}\overline{f}(t)\dd t=\overline{A}.

Therefore, the proof is completed.
∎

Now we state and prove the dominated convergence theorem.

Theorem 5.16
.

If
\overline{f}_{n}\in\mathcal{IR}([a,b],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}})
,
n=1,2,...,
satisfying

(i)
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\overline{f}_{n}(t)=\overline{f}(t)
in
[a,b]
;

(ii)
\overline{g}(t)\preccurlyeq\overline{f}_{n}(t)\preccurlyeq\overline{h}(t)
for all
t\in[a,b]
and all
n
, where
\overline{g},\overline{h}\in\mathcal{IR}([a,b],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}})
.

Then
\overline{f}\in\mathcal{IR}([a,b],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}})
and

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(IR)\int_{a}^{b}\overline{f}_{n}(t)\dd t=(IR)\int_{a}^{b}\overline{f}(t)\dd t.

Proof.

Let Let
\overline{f}(t)=\langle{f}_{c}(t);{f}_{w}(t)\rangle
,
\overline{f}_{n}(t)=\langle({f}_{n})_{c}(t);({f}_{n})_{w}(t)\rangle
,
t\in[a,b]
,
n=1,2,...,

\overline{g}(t)=\langle{g}_{c}(t);{g}_{w}(t)\rangle
,
\overline{h}(t)=\langle{h}_{c}(t);{h}_{w}(t)\rangle
,
t\in[a,b]
.
Since
\overline{g},\overline{h},\overline{f}_{n}\in\mathcal{IR}([a,b],\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}})
,
n=1,2,...,
by Lemma
5.2
, one has

{g}_{c},\ln{g}_{w},{h}_{c},\ln{h}_{w},({f}_{n})_{c},\ln({f}_{n})_{w}\in\mathcal{R}([a,b],\mathbb{R}),~~n=1,2,....

Due to (i) and Theorem

2.49
, we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}({f}_{n})_{c}(t)={f}_{c}(t),

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\ln({f}_{n})_{w}(t)=\ln{f}_{w}(t),~~t\in[a,b].

Moreover, thanks to (ii) and Definition
2.4
, we get

{g}_{c}(t)\leq({f}_{n})_{c}(t)\leq{h}_{c}(t),

\ln{g}_{w}(t)\leq\ln({f}_{n})_{w}(t)\leq\ln{h}_{w}(t),~~t\in[a,b].

By
[
16
, Theorem 4.3]
, it holds that
{f}_{c},{f}_{w}\in\mathcal{R}([a,b],\mathbb{R})
and

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(R)\int_{a}^{b}({f}_{n})_{c}(t)\dd t=(R)\int_{a}^{b}{f}_{c}(t)\dd t,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(R)\int_{a}^{b}\ln({f}_{n})_{w}(t)\dd t=(R)\int_{a}^{b}\ln{f}_{w}(t)\dd t.

This implies that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(IR)\int_{a}^{b}\overline{f}_{n}(t)\dd t=(IR)\int_{a}^{b}\overline{f}(t)\dd t.

The proof is therefore completed.

Theorem 6.3

\displaystyle~\|\mathcal{T}(\overline{x})-\overline{x}_{0}\|_{\infty}=

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we jump out of the original framework of interval arithmetic operations, and present new ones. With the proposed arithmetic operations, the space
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
is a linear space, while it is a semilinear space with traditional operations. Moreover, new distance, norm, and inner product are presented to show that
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{I}}
is a Hilbert space. On this basis, novel definitions of derivative and integral for interval-valued functions are introduced, and their fundamental properties are systematically investigated. The proposed approach is particularly interesting due to its computational simplicity compared to existing methods. These new definitions simultaneously incorporate aspects of both the classical and multiplicative derivatives and integrals, offering a hybrid framework that retains the strengths of each. This dual-structure method not only simplifies calculations but also enhances the interpretability and applicability of the interval analysis. The classical derivative and integral are widely used in various applications. In contrast, the multiplicative derivative and integral are especially effective for modeling processes characterized by proportional or exponential growth, offering new perspectives in the analysis of interval-valued functions. Therefore, the physical interpretation of the newly introduced concepts of derivative and integral becomes more transparent and meaningful within this unified framework.

In the future, these results could be wildly applied in other mathematical branches, such as interval integration inequalities, interval differential equations, interval optimization, interval decision making and so on. Also, it is easy to extend the new framework to fuzzy numbers, and the calculus of fuzzy-valued functions. These could be of interest in many fields, such as finance, economics, environment, and engineering.

Partition function of 2D Coulomb gases with radially symmetric potentials and a hard wall
Matthias Allard, Peter J. Forrester, Sampad Lahiry, Bojian Shen

Abstract
The large N N asymptotic expansion of the partition function for the normal matrix model is predicted to have special features inherited from its interpretation as a two-dimensional Coulomb gas. However for the latter, it is most natural to include a hard wall at the boundary of the droplet. We probe how this affects the asymptotic expansion in the solvable case that the potential is radially symmetric and the droplet is a disk or an annulus. We allow too for the hard wall to be strictly inside the boundary of the droplet. It is observed the term of order log ⁡ N \log N , has then a different rational number prefactor to that when the hard wall is at the droplet boundary. Also found are certain universal (potential independent) numerical constants given by definite integrals, both at order N \sqrt{N} , and in the constant term.

2.4
Discussion

Highlighted in the Introduction was the universal term
(\chi/12)\log(N)
in the free energy expansion (
1.4
). It was commented that this is expected to be valid both the settings that the hard wall corresponds to the boundary of the droplet, and when the hard wall is removed altogether. Our result of
Theorem 2.1
, case

(
\eta=0
), indeed confirms the former scenario for the annulus droplet, with there being no terms proportional to
\log N
. Using this fact in
Theorem 2.3

then confirms that there is a term proportional to
\log N
, proportionality constant
-\frac{1}{12}
(the minus sign is due to the free energy being minus the logarithm of the partition function), again in agreement with predictions.

A striking feature of our expansions, beyond the cases covered by the conjectures from
Jancovici et al. 1994
, is that the
\log(N)
term remains universal — being independent of the potential
q(r)
and possibly
\beta
(although our study is restricted to
\beta=2
), albeit depending on whether
\eta
is zero, is between 0 and 1, is equal to 1, or is greater than 1. Specifically, for the annulus case
r_{1}>1
(
1>\eta>0
) of
Theorem 2.1
there is the term

-\frac{1}{4}\log(N)
; for the disk case
r_{1}>1
(
1>\eta>0
), substituting this fact in the result of
Theorem 2.3
shows that there is the term
-\frac{1}{3}\log(N)
; and for the annulus case with
r_{0}>1
(
\eta>1
) there is no
\log(N)
term, but for
r_{0}=1
(
\eta=1)
there is the term
-\frac{1}{4}\log(N)
as for the annulus case with
r_{1}>1
(
1>\eta>0
). How one might anticipate these findings remains an open question.

Let us then turn to

Substituting and proceeding with the change of variable

. By choosing

.

As

, we may expand the cubic term to obtain

\displaystyle I_{2}^{\rm out}\underset{N\to\infty}{\sim}\frac{e^{-NV_{\tau}(1)}}{c_{N}}\int_{-(\log(N))^{2}}^{0}

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{y}{c_{N}}\right)\left(1-\frac{NV_{\tau}^{\prime\prime\prime}(1)}{6}\frac{y^{3}}{c_{N}^{3}}\right)

(3.20)

\displaystyle\times e^{-N\left(V_{\tau}^{\prime}(1)\frac{y}{c_{N}}+\frac{V_{\tau}^{\prime\prime}(1)}{2}\frac{y^{2}}{c_{N}^{2}}\right)}\left(1+{\rm o}(Nc_{N}^{-4})\right)dy.

Moreover, since either

Fractional higher differentiability of solutions to the stationary Orlicz-Stokes system
Andrea Cianchi, Flavia Giannetti, Antonia Passarelli di Napoli & Christoph Scheven

Abstract
. This work concerns the stationary Stokes system subject to Orlicz type growth conditions. Fractional regularity properties of the symmetric gradient of local solutions are established, depending on a balance between the nonlinearity of the differential operator and the degree of integrability of the right-hand side in Orlicz spaces. This regularity is described via the membership of a non linear expression of the symmetric gradient in Besov spaces. The fractional regularity of the pressure term is also exhibited and is formulated in terms of Orlicz-Besov spaces. Parallel results for the symmetric gradient of local solutions to the associated plain elliptic system are also offered. A new version of a Poincaré-Sobolev inequality in Orlicz spaces, in modular form, on domains with finite measure plays a role in the proofs.

1.
Introduction

Stokes type systems model the motion of non-Newtonian fluids, namely
incompressible fluids
affected by a nonlinear relation between the deformation velocity and the deviatoric tensor stress. The stationary version of these systems takes the form

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-\mathrm{div}\,a(x,\mathcal{E}u)+\nabla\pi=f\\[2.58334pt]
\mathrm{div}\,u=0,\end{array}\right.

in an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
n\geq 2
. Here,
a:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\textup{sym}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\textup{sym}}
is a function satisfying ellipticity and growth conditions in the variable
in
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\textup{sym}}
, the space of symmetric
n\times n
matrices, and
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is measurable. The unknowns are the functions

u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\pi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
, and
\mathcal{E}u
denotes the symmetric gradient of
u
. In the physical problem,
u
stands for the velocity of the fluid,
\mathcal{E}u
represents its deformation velocity,
\pi
denotes its pressure,
a
is the deviatoric tensor stress, and
f
is an external force. We refer to
[
2
,
6
]
for a detailed description of the pertaining models.

The present paper is concerned with fractional differentiability properties of
\mathcal{E}u
and
\pi
for local solutions
(u,\pi)
to the system (
1.1
).
The major novelties amount to a precise balance assumption, in the general setting of Orlicz spaces, between the nonlinearity of
a(x,\mathcal{E}u)
in
\mathcal{E}u
and the degree of integrability of
f
, and to the lack of smoothness of
a(x,\mathcal{E}u)
in
x
.

The ellipticity and growth of the function
a(x,P)
in the symmetric gradient variable
P
and the integrability of the datum
f
are prescribed in terms of Young functions
\phi
and
\psi
, respectively, that are not necessarily powers. This allows for strongly nonlinear behaviors of the differential operator in (
1.1
), which need not be of polynomial type.
The Orlicz space
L^{\psi}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and the Orlicz-Sobolev space
W^{1,\phi}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
thus provide an appropriate functional framework for the datum and the solutions.
A fractional differentiability assumption in the variable
x
, in the spirit of
[
15
]
and
[
31
]
, is allowed on the function
a
. The regularity of
\mathcal{E}u
is described via the membership in a Besov space, of the form
B^{\sigma,2,\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
, of
the nonlinear expression
V(\mathcal{E}u)=\sqrt{\frac{\phi^{\prime}(|\mathcal{E}u|)}{|\mathcal{E}u|}}\,\mathcal{E}u
. A Besov space of the same kind is also well suited to account for the fractional regularity of
\pi
.
The use of the expression
V(\mathcal{E}u)
is critical in discussing differentiability properties of
\mathcal{E}u
, because of the possible degeneracy of the differential operator in (
1.1
). Analogous expressions, defined with

\mathcal{E}u

replaced with the full gradient
\nabla u
, are classically employed in
the analysis of (fractional) higher order differentiability properties of solutions to elliptic and parabolic problems involving

\nabla u
– see e.g. the monograph
[
29
]
and the more recent contributions
[
14
,
16
,
24
,
25
,
26
,
34
,
35
,
37
]
.

The results about the function
V(\mathcal{E}u)
for the Stokes system (
1.1
) have a parallel version for the plain symmetric gradient system

(1.2)

-\mathrm{div}\,a(x,\mathcal{E}u)=f.

They just follow via a simplification in the proof for (
1.1
), due to a more straightforward choice of test functions, which do not need to obey the divergence free constraint in the weak formulation of (
1.2
).

Loosely speaking, our conclusions assert that
V(\mathcal{E}u)
and, in the case of (
1.1
), also
\pi
, locally belong to suitable Besov spaces, as soon as the datum
f
lies in an Orlicz space
L^{\psi}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
which is smaller, in a qualified sense, than that required for weak solutions to be well defined. The latter assumption is precisely formulated via an interpolation inequality involving the functions
\psi
,
\phi
, and its sharp Sobolev conjugate
\phi_{n}
. The smoothness parameter
\sigma
in the relevant Besov spaces depends on the interpolation parameter, on the upper Simonenko index of
\phi^{\prime}
, and on the exponent in the fractional differentiability of
a
with respect to
x
. Thanks to classical embedding theorems for Besov spaces, these pieces of information on
V(\mathcal{E}u)
and
\pi
imply that they also belong to the fractional Sobolev spaces with suitable smoothness parameters.

These results encompass, generalize and improve those already available for some special instances.
The case of the
p
-Laplacian Stokes system, considered in
[
27
]
for
p\geq 2
, is recovered with the choice
\phi(t)=t^{p}
. It is also complemented and augmented, in that the range
1<p<2
is included and a fractional differentiability dependence of the function
a
in the
x
-variable is permitted. Systems governed by a Young function
\phi(t)
, possibly of power type
t^{p}
for any
p\in(1,\infty)
, are the subject of
[
28
]
. In that paper, however, only data
f\in L^{\widetilde{\phi}}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
are admitted, where
\widetilde{\phi}
denotes the Young conjugate of
\phi
. Furthermore, in
[
28
]

the assumptions on the dependence on
x
are more restrictive.
The result of
[
28
]
implies, in particular, that
V(\mathcal{E}u)\in W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
, provided that
\phi(t)=t^{p}
with
1<p<2
and
f\in L^{p^{\prime}}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. The same kind of regularity for local solutions to (
1.2
) and for the same range of values of
p
was established in
[
39
]
. In the recent paper
[
9
]
, the local Sobolev regularity of
V(\mathcal{E}u)
for local solutions to (
1.1
) has been shown to hold under the weaker assumption that
f\in L^{\frac{np}{n(p-1)+2-p}}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Such an assumption is known to be optimal even for an analogous
conclusion in the case of the standard
p
-Laplace equation – see
[
13
]
.
Related results on the
W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
regularity of
V(\mathcal{E}u)
for local solutions to the Stokes system (
1.1
), subject to diverse growth conditions on the differential operator and on the summability of the right-hand side, are contained in the papers
[
7
,
8
,
18
,
23
,
36
]
. They do not address, however, regularity issues about the function
\pi
.
Global
W^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
regularity of
V(\mathcal{E}u)
is proved in
[
3
]
for every
p\in(1,\infty)
and for solutions to homogeneous Dirichlet problems for the system (
1.2
), under the assumption
f\in W^{1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Partial results in this connection can also be found in
[
4
,
5
,
40
]
.

Results concerning other quantities than
V(\mathcal{E}u)
are available, although the regularity theory of systems depending of the symmetric gradient is still much less developed than its counterpart for the full gradient. For instance,
C^{1,\alpha}
–regularity of solutions to symmetric gradient
p
-Laplacian type systems and Stokes systems holds for
n=2

[
19
,
32
]
, but it is still an open question in higher dimension.
For
n\geq 3
, only partial
C^{1,\alpha}
–regularity of solutions to Stokes type systems has been established – see the papers
[
7
,
8
,
21
,
22
]
. In

[
1
]
, the same kind of regularity is exhibited for solutions to more general Stokes systems driven by an
x
–dependent power type nonlinearity in
\mathcal{E}u
. Maximal higher-integrability of the gradient of solutions to the Stokes system (
1.1
) is the topic of
[
18
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. We begin by recalling the notion and several specific properties of Young functions to be used in our proofs. This is the content of Section
2
. In the subsequent Section
3
, the function spaces entering our analysis are introduced and various pertaining embeddings and inequalities are collected. The main results are stated in Section
4
, where
they are illustrated by implementations to specific choices of the Young functions
\phi
and
\psi
. Section
5
is devoted to an ad hoc modular Poincaré-Sobolev inequality, in Orlicz-Sobolev spaces, tailored for our applications. In combination with techniques that are peculiar
to the Orlicz space realm, this is a critical step in our approach.
The proofs of the main results are accomplished in the remaining Sections
6
–
8
. A priori Besov space estimates for
V(\mathcal{E}u)
, under additional regularity assumptions on the function
a
and the datum
f
are established in Section
6
. They are removed in Section
7
via an approximation process. This double-step argument is needed because of the weakness of the assumptions imposed on the
x
–dependence of the function
a
.
The subject of Section
8
is the proof of the regularity of
\pi
. It is accomplished by regarding the first line in (
1.1
) as an equation where both
f
and
u
are assigned, and
\pi
is the sole unknown.

2.
Young functions

A Young
function
\phi
is a left-continuous convex function from
[0,\infty)
into
[0,\infty]
that vanishes
at
0
and is not constant in
(0,\infty)
. Any Young function
\phi
can be represented as

(2.1)

\phi(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\phi^{\prime}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\quad\quad\textup{for}\ t\in[0,\infty),

where
\phi^{\prime}
denotes the non-decreasing, left-continuous representative of the derivative of
\phi
.

If
\phi
is a Young function, then the function

(2.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\phi(t)}{t}\quad\text{is non-decreasing.}

Moreover,

(2.3)

\displaystyle k\phi(t)\leq\phi(kt)\quad\text{for $k\geq 1$ and $t\geq 0$.}

The Young conjugate of a Young function
\phi
is defined as

\displaystyle\widetilde{\phi}(t)=\sup\{st-\phi(s):s\geq 0\}\quad\text{for $t\geq 0$.}

One has that
\widetilde{\phi}
is also a Young function and

(2.4)

st\leq\phi(s)+\widetilde{\phi}(t)\qquad\mbox{for $s,t\geq 0$.}

Furthermore,

(2.5)

\displaystyle t\leq\phi^{-1}(t)\widetilde{\phi}^{-1}(t)\leq 2t\quad\text{for $t\geq 0$,}

where the inverses are defined in the generalized right-continuous sense.

The following inequalities hold:

(2.6)

\widetilde{\phi}\Big(\frac{\phi(t)}{t}\Big)\leq\phi(t)\leq\widetilde{\phi}\Big(\frac{2\phi(t)}{t}\Big)\qquad\text{for $t>0$.}

Moreover,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\phi}(\phi^{\prime}(t))\leq\phi(2t)\qquad\text{for $t>0$.}

A Young function
\phi
is said to dominate another Young function
\psi
globally if there exists a constant
c>0
such that

(2.8)

\displaystyle\psi(t)\leq\phi(ct)

for
t\geq 0
. The function
\phi
is said to dominate
\psi
near infinity
if there exists a constant
t_{0}>0
such that (
2.8
) holds for
t\geq t_{0}
.
We say that the functions
\phi
and
\psi
are equivalent globally [near infinity] if they dominate each other globally [near infinity]. The equivalence of Young functions in this sense will be denoted by
\phi\approx\psi
.

A function
\phi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is said to satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition – briefly,
\phi\in\Delta_{2}
– if there exists a constant
c\geq 2
such that

(2.9)

\displaystyle\phi(2t)\leq c\,\phi(t)\quad\text{for $t\geq 0$.}

The function
\phi
is said to satisfy the
\nabla_{2}
-condition – briefly,
\phi\in\nabla_{2}
–
if there exists a constant
c>2
such that

(2.10)

\displaystyle\phi(2t)\geq c\,\phi(t)\quad\text{for $t\geq 0$.}

The optimal constants
c

appearing in (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) will be denoted by
\Delta_{2}(\phi)
and
\nabla_{2}(\phi)
.

One has that
\phi\in\Delta_{2}
if and only if
\widetilde{\phi}\in\nabla_{2}
. Also, if
\phi\in\Delta_{2}
, then
\phi^{\prime}\in\Delta_{2}
.

Assume that the Young function
\phi
is strictly positive in
(0,\infty)
and
\phi\in C^{2}(0,\infty)
. We set

(2.11)

\displaystyle i_{\phi}=\inf_{t>0}\frac{t\phi^{\prime\prime}(t)}{\phi^{\prime}(t)}+1\quad\text{and}\quad s_{\phi}=\sup_{t>0}\frac{t\phi^{\prime\prime}(t)}{\phi^{\prime}(t)}+1.

Namely,
i_{\phi}
and
s_{\phi}
are the so called Simonenko indices of
\phi^{\prime}
, shifted by
1
.
One has that
\phi\in\Delta_{2}
if and only if
s_{\phi}<\infty
, and
\widetilde{\phi}\in\Delta_{2}
if and only if
i_{\phi}>1
. In what follows, when stating any property involving the indices
i_{\phi}
and
s_{\phi}
we shall tacitly assume that they satisfy the inequalities:

(2.12)

\displaystyle 1<i_{\phi}\leq s_{\phi}<\infty.

One can verify that

(2.13)

\displaystyle i_{\phi}\leq\frac{t\phi^{\prime}(t)}{\phi(t)}\leq s_{\phi}\quad\text{for $t>0$.}

Also,

(2.14)

\displaystyle s_{\phi}^{\prime}\leq\frac{t{\widetilde{\phi}}^{\prime}(t)}{\widetilde{\phi}(t)}\leq i_{\phi}^{\prime}\quad\text{for $t>0$,}

where
i_{\phi}^{\prime}
and
s_{\phi}^{\prime}
stand for the Hölder conjugates of
i_{\phi}

and
s_{\phi}
.

From (
2.13
), one has that

(2.15)

\displaystyle\min\{\lambda^{i_{\phi}},\lambda^{s_{\phi}}\}\phi(t)

\displaystyle\leq\phi(\lambda t)\leq\max\{\lambda^{i_{\phi}},\lambda^{s_{\phi}}\}\phi(t)\quad\text{for $t,\lambda\geq 0$}

and, from (
2.14
),

(2.16)

\displaystyle\min\{\lambda^{i_{\phi}^{\prime}},\lambda^{s_{\phi}^{\prime}}\}\widetilde{\phi}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{\phi}(\lambda t)\leq\max\{\lambda^{i_{\phi}^{\prime}},\lambda^{s_{\phi}^{\prime}}\}\widetilde{\phi}(t)\quad\text{for $t,\lambda\geq 0$.}

In particular, Equation (
2.15
) implies that

(2.17)

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(0)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}\phi^{\prime}(t)=\infty.

Coupling (
2.7
) with (
2.15
) yields

(2.18)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\phi}(\phi^{\prime}(t))\leq c(s_{\phi})\phi(t)\qquad\text{for $t>0$.}

Furthermore,

(2.19)

\phi(s+t)\leq c(s_{\phi})\big(\phi(s)+\phi(t)\big)\quad\text{for $s,t\geq 0$.}

Given a Young function
\phi
and
a\geq 0
, the shifted Young function
\phi_{a}
is defined, according to
[
17
]
(see also
[
16
]
for an earlier version), as

(2.20)

\displaystyle\phi_{a}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(\max\{s,{a}\})}{\max\{s,{a}\}}s\,\mathrm{d}s,

for
t\in\mathbb{R}
. The parameter
a
is called
the shift. Plainly,
\phi_{0}=\phi
. Moreover, if

a\eqsim b
, then
\phi_{a}(t)\eqsim\phi_{b}(t)
, with equivalence constants depending on
i_{\phi}
,
s_{\phi}
, and the equivalence constants in the relation
a\eqsim b
.
Here, and in what follows, the relation
\eqsim
between two expressions means that they are bounded by each other up to positive constants depending on appropriate quantities. This relation should not be confused with the relation
\approx

between Young functions introduced above.
We refer to the papers
[
16
,
17
]
for proofs of the properties of shifted Young functions recalled in this section.

If
\phi\in C^{2}(0,\infty)
, then
\phi_{a}\in C^{2}((0,\infty)\setminus\{a\})
, and

\frac{t\phi_{a}^{\prime\prime}(t)}{\phi_{a}^{\prime}(t)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}1&\mbox{ if $0\leq t<a$}\\[3.44444pt]
\displaystyle\frac{t\phi^{\prime\prime}(t)}{\phi^{\prime}(t)}&\mbox{ if $t>a$.}\end{array}\right.

Assume that
\phi
fulfills (
2.12
). Then,

(2.21)

\displaystyle\min\{\lambda^{i_{\phi}},\lambda^{2}\}\phi_{a}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\phi_{a}(\lambda t)\leq\max\{\lambda^{s_{\phi}},\lambda^{2}\}\phi_{a}(t)\quad\text{for $\lambda,t\geq 0$.}

Hence, by (
2.14
),

(2.22)

\displaystyle\min\{\lambda^{s_{\phi}^{\prime}},\lambda^{2}\}\widetilde{\phi_{a}}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{\phi_{a}}(\lambda t)\leq\max\{\lambda^{i_{\phi}^{\prime}},\lambda^{2}\}\widetilde{\phi_{a}}(t)\quad\text{for $\lambda,t\geq 0$.}

Equations (
2.4
),
(
2.21
),
and (
2.22
) ensure that, for
every
\delta>0
there exists
c_{\delta}=c_{\delta}(\delta,i_{\phi},s_{\phi})\geq 1
such
that

(2.23)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} s\,t&\leq\delta\,\phi_{a}(t)+c_{\delta}\,\widetilde{\phi_{a}}(s)\qquad\text{for $s,t,a\geq 0$.}\end{aligned}

One has that

(2.24)

\widetilde{\phi_{a}}(\phi_{a}^{\prime}(t))\leq c(s_{\phi})\phi_{a}(t)\quad\text{for $t\geq 0$.}

Moreover,

(2.25)

\phi_{a}(s+t)\leq c(s_{\phi})\big(\phi_{a}(s)+\phi_{a}(t)\big)\quad\text{for $s,t\geq 0$.}

There exists a constant
c=c(i_{\phi},s_{\phi})
such that

(2.26)

\displaystyle\phi_{a}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\delta\,\phi(a)+c\,\delta\,\phi(t/\delta),

for every
a\geq 0
,
t\geq 0
, and
\delta\in(0,1]
.

The following relation holds for every

P,Q\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
such that
|P|+|Q|>0
:

(2.27)

\phi_{|Q|}(|P-Q|)\eqsim\frac{\phi^{\prime}(\max\{|P|,|Q|\})}{\max\{|P|,|Q|\}}|P-Q|^{2},

with equivalence constants depending only on
i_{\phi}
and
s_{\phi}
.

Also,

(2.28)

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime\prime}(|P|+|Q|)|P-Q|

\displaystyle\eqsim\phi^{\prime}_{|P|}(|P-Q|)

(2.29)

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime\prime}(|P|+|Q|)|P-Q|^{2}

\displaystyle\eqsim\phi_{|P|}(|P-Q|)

with equivalence constants depending only on
i_{\phi}
and
s_{\phi}
.

Define the function
V:\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}

as

(2.30)

\displaystyle V(P)=\sqrt{\frac{\phi^{\prime}(|P|)}{|P|}}\,P\quad\text{for $P\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$.}

Then,
for every
\delta>0
there exists
c_{\delta}=c_{\delta}(\delta,i_{\phi},s_{\phi})
such that

(2.31)

\phi(t)\leq c_{\delta}\phi_{|P|}(t)+\delta|V(P)|^{2}\leq c_{\delta}\phi_{|P|}(t)+\delta{s_{\phi}}\,\phi(|P|)

for every
P\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, where
c_{\delta}=c_{\delta}(\delta,i_{\phi},s_{\phi})
.

One has that

(2.32)

\displaystyle\phi_{|{Q}|}\left(|{P-Q}|\right)\eqsim|{V(P)-V(Q)}|^{2}

for every
P,Q\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, and

(2.33)

\displaystyle|{V(Q)}|^{2}\eqsim\phi_{|{Q}|}(|{Q}|)\eqsim\phi(|{Q}|)

for every
P\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
, with
n\geq 2
, and let
\phi
be a Young function.
The Sobolev conjugate of
\phi
is the Young function
\phi_{n}
given by

(2.34)

\displaystyle\phi_{n}(t)=\phi(H_{n}^{-1}(t))\quad\text{for $t\geq 0$,}

where the function

H_{n}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)

is defined as

(2.35)

H_{n}(s)=\bigg(\int_{0}^{s}\Big(\frac{\tau}{\phi(\tau)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\mathrm{d}\tau\bigg)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\quad\text{for $s\geq 0$.}

Here,
H_{n}^{-1}
denotes the left-continuous inverse of
H_{n}
, and
\phi
is extended to
[0,\infty]
by setting
\phi(\infty)=\infty
.

The definition of
H_{n}
requires that

(2.36)

\displaystyle\int_{0}\Big(\frac{t}{\phi(t)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty.

This condition is not restrictive in view of our purposes.
In what follows, the function
\phi_{n}
is tacitly assumed to be defined with
\phi
modified near zero, if necessary, in such a way that (
2.36
) is satisfied. The choice of this possible modification of
\phi
is immaterial in our main results. Indeed, they depend on
\phi_{n}
only up to equivalence near infinity, and the latter depends on
\phi
only up to equivalence near infinity.

Note that, if

(2.37)

\int^{\infty}\Big(\frac{t}{\phi(t)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\mathrm{d}t<\infty,

then
H^{-1}(t)=\infty
for
t\geq t_{\infty}
, where we have set
t_{\infty}=\lim_{s\to\infty}H(s)
. Consequently,
\phi_{n}(t)=\infty
for
t\geq t_{\infty}
as well.

Also, there exist constants
c,t_{0}>0
such that

(2.38)

\displaystyle\phi(t)\leq\phi_{n}(ct)\quad\text{for $t\geq t_{0}$.}

3.
Function spaces and functional inequalities

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be an open set in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and let
v:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
. We define

\tau_{h}v:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
by

(3.1)

\begin{gathered}\tau_{h}v(x)=v(x+he_{i})-v(x),\end{gathered}

for

x\in\Omega
and

h\neq 0
such that
x+he_{i}\in\Omega
. Here,

e_{i}
, denotes the
i
-th coordinate unit vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.

Assume that the functions
v,w:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
are weakly differentiable. Then,

(\tau_{h}v)_{x_{i}}=\tau_{h}(v_{x_{i}}).

If the support of at least one of the functions
v
and

w
is compactly contained in
\Omega
, then

(3.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}v\,\tau_{h}w\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}w\,\tau_{-h}v\,\mathrm{d}x

for sufficiently small
|h|
.

Moreover,
the product rule for finite differences takes the form:

(3.3)

\tau_{h}(vw)(x)=v(x+he_{i})\tau_{h}w(x)+w(x)\tau_{h}v(x),

for
x\in\Omega
and
h
such that
x+he_{i}\in\Omega
.

Let
\phi
be a Young function and let
N\in\mathbb{N}
. The Orlicz space
L^{\phi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
is defined as

(3.4)

\displaystyle L^{\phi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})=\bigg\{v:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{N}:\exists\lambda>0\,\,\text{s.t.}\,\,\int_{\Omega}\phi\bigg(\frac{|v|}{\lambda}\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty\bigg\}.

The space
L^{\phi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
, equipped with the Luxemburg norm, is a Banach space. The definition of this norm will not be recalled, since norms in Orlicz spaces will not be used throughout this paper.
We also introduce the notation

L^{\phi}_{\perp}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})=\{v\in L^{\phi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N}):v_{\Omega}=0\},

where
v_{\Omega}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}v\mathrm{d}x
, the mean value of
v
over
\Omega
. When
N=1
, the target space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
in the notation of the Orlicz spaces and the other spaces introduced below will be omitted. Spaces of matrix-valued functions will also be considered and are defined analogously.

If
v\in L^{\phi}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then

(3.5)

\int_{B_{R}}\phi(|v(x+he_{i})|)\ \mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{B_{R+|h|}}\phi(|v(x)|)\ \mathrm{d}x

for every
R>0
and
h\neq 0
such that
B_{R+|h|}\Subset\Omega
. Here,
B_{R}
denotes a ball of radius
R
.

The Orlicz-Sobolev space
W^{1,\phi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
is given by

(3.6)

\displaystyle W^{1,\phi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})=\big\{v\in L^{\phi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N}):v\,\,\text{is weakly differentiable and}\,\,\nabla v\in L^{\phi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N\times n})\big\}.

The local versions
L^{\phi}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})

and
W^{1,\phi}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
of these spaces are defined as usual. Observe that
L^{\phi}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset L^{\psi}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
for some Young functions
\phi
and
\psi
if and only if
\phi
dominates
\psi
near infinity. A parallel characterization holds for inclusion relation between local Orlicz-Sobolev spaces.

The space
W^{1,\phi}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
of functions in
W^{1,\phi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
vanishing on
\partial\Omega
can be defined as

(3.7)

\displaystyle W^{1,\phi}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})=\big\{v\in W^{1,\phi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N}):\,\,\text{the extension of $v$ by $0$ to $\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega$ is weakly differentiable in $\mathbb{R}^{n}$}\big\}.

In what follows, functions in the space
W^{1,\phi}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
will be assumed to be defined as
0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\Omega
without further mentioning.

Let
\sigma\in(0,1]
.
We define the local fractional Orlicz-Sobolev space
W^{\sigma,\phi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
as

(3.8)

W^{\sigma,\phi}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})=\Bigg\{v\in L^{\phi}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N}):\forall\,\text{open set}\,\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega\,\exists\,\lambda>0\,\text{s.t.}\,\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\phi\left(\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|}{\lambda|x-y|^{\sigma}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y}{|x-y|^{n}}<\infty\Bigg\},

and the local Besov space
B^{\sigma,\phi,\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})

as

(3.9)

\displaystyle B^{\sigma,\phi,\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})=\bigg\{v\in L^{\phi}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N}):\forall\,B_{R}\Subset\Omega\,\exists\,\lambda,\delta>0\,\text{s.t.}\sup_{|h|<\delta}\int_{B_{R}}\phi\bigg(\bigg|\frac{\tau_{h}v}{\lambda|h|^{\sigma}}\bigg|\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty\bigg\}.

One has that,

(3.10)

B^{\sigma,\phi,\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset W^{\beta,\phi}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})\quad\text{if $0<\beta<\sigma<1$.}

The inclusion (
3.10
) follows from
[
28
, Lemma 2.13]
.
Moreover,
[
28
, Lemma 2.10]
implies that

(3.11)

\displaystyle W^{1,\phi}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset B^{1,\phi,\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N}),

and

(3.12)

\int_{B_{R}}\phi\left(\frac{|\tau_{h}v|}{|h|}\right)\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{B_{R+|h|}}\phi(|\nabla v|)\,\mathrm{d}x

for
v\in W^{1,\phi}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and
every
R>0
and
h\neq 0
such that
B_{R+|h|}\Subset\Omega
.

Notice that, although the lemmas from
[
28
]
quoted above are stated for Young functions
\phi
satisfying additional assumptions, the same arguments in their proofs yield a version of the results for arbitrary Young functions
\phi
.

A Sobolev type inequality in Orlicz spaces tells us that, if
\phi
is a Young function and
|\Omega|<\infty
, then

(3.13)

\displaystyle W^{1,\phi}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{\phi_{n}}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N}),

where
\phi_{n}
is defined as in (
2.34
),
see
[
11
]
(and
[
10
]
for an equivalent formulation). Here, and in what follows, the arrow
\to
denotes a continuous embedding.
If, in addition,
\Omega
is regular enough – a bounded Lipschitz domain, for instance – then

(3.14)

\displaystyle W^{1,\phi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{\phi_{n}}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Moreover, the target
L^{\phi_{n}}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
is optimal (i.e. smallest possible) in the embeddings (
3.13
) and (
3.14
) among all Orlicz spaces. As mentioned above, in these embeddings, the function
\phi_{n}
is defined with
\phi
modified near zero, if necessary, in such a way that the condition (
2.36
) is satisfied.

In the special case when
\phi(t)=t^{p}
for some
p\geq 1
, the spaces defined above recover the classical Lebesgue, Sobolev, Fractional Sobolev and Besov spaces, and are denoted simply by
L^{p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
W^{\sigma,p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and
B^{\sigma,p,\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

A Poincaré-Sobolev inequality for Besov spaces tells us that, if
0<\beta<\sigma<1
, then

B^{\sigma,2,\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset L^{\frac{2n}{n-2\beta}}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Moreover,
if
B_{R}\Subset\Omega
and
r<R
, then

(3.15)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{r}}|v|^{\frac{{2n}}{n-2\beta}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{n-2\beta}{2n}}\leq c(R-r)^{\sigma-\beta}\bigg(\sup_{0<h<\frac{R-r}{2}}\int_{B_{\frac{R+r}{2}}}\frac{|\tau_{h}v|^{2}}{|h|^{2\sigma}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{c}{(R-r)^{\beta}}\bigg(\int_{B_{R}}|v|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

for some constant
c=c(n,\beta,\sigma)
and for every
u\in B^{\sigma,2,\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
, see
[
27
, Lemma 2.8]
.

The symmetric gradient of a function
v\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
is defined as

(3.16)

\mathcal{E}v=\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\nabla v+(\nabla v)^{T}\big),

in the distributional sense. Define the symmetric gradient Orlicz-Sobolev space as

(3.17)

\displaystyle E^{1,\phi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})=\big\{v\in L^{\phi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n}):\,\,\mathcal{E}v\in L^{\phi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})\big\}.

Its subspace
E^{1,\phi}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
and the space
E^{1,\phi}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
can be defined in the same spirit as
W^{1,\phi}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
W^{1,\phi}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

A Korn type inequality on balls in Orlicz spaces asserts that, if
\phi\in\Delta_{2}\cap\nabla_{2}
, then

E^{1,\phi}_{0}(B_{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})\to W^{1,\phi}_{0}(B_{R},\mathbb{R}^{n}),

and there exists a constant
c
, depending on
n
,
\Delta_{2}(\phi)
,
and
\nabla_{2}(\phi)
such that

(3.18)

\int_{B_{R}}\phi(|\nabla u|)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c\int_{B_{R}}\phi(|\mathcal{E}u|)\mathrm{d}x

for every
u\in E^{1,\phi}_{0}(B_{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
, see
[
20
, Thms. 6.10]
. A variant of the Korn inequality in Orlicz spaces for functions
\phi
which do not satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
and
\nabla_{2}
conditions can be found in
[
12
]
.

An Orlicz space version of the
Bogovskiĭ Lemma on balls
B_{R}
is a consequence of
[
33
, Corollary 4.2]
, and tells us what follows. Assume that the Young function
\phi\in\Delta_{2}\cap\nabla_{2}
. Then there exists
a linear operator

(3.19)

\mathcal{B}:L^{\phi}_{\perp}(B_{R},\mathbb{R}^{N})\to W^{1,\phi}_{0}(B_{R},\mathbb{R}^{N})

such that

(3.20)

{\rm div}(\mathcal{B}v)=v\quad\mbox{in $B_{R}$}

for every
v\in L^{\phi}_{\perp}(B_{R},\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
Moreover, there exists a constant
c
depending on
n
,

\Delta_{2}(\phi)
, and
\nabla_{2}(\phi)
such that

(3.21)

\int_{B_{R}}\phi(|\nabla(\mathcal{B}v)|)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c\int_{B_{R}}\phi(|v|)\,\mathrm{d}x

and

(3.22)

\int_{B_{R}}\phi\left(\frac{|\tau_{h}\nabla(\mathcal{B}v)|}{|h|}\right)\mathrm{d}x\leq c\int_{B_{R}}\phi\left(\frac{|\tau_{h}v|}{|h|}+\frac{|\tau_{-h}v|}{|h|}+\frac{|v|}{R}\right)\mathrm{d}x

for every
v\in L^{\phi}_{\perp}(B_{R},\mathbb{R}^{N})
and every
h\neq 0
.
In the latter inequality, the functions
v
and
\mathcal{B}v
are continued by
zero outside of
B_{R}
.
Hence, in particular, if

v\in W^{1,\phi}_{0}(B_{R},\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then

(3.23)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R+|h|}}\phi\left(\frac{|\tau_{h}\nabla(\mathcal{B}v)|}{|h|}\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{B_{R}}\left[\phi(|\nabla v|)+\phi\left(\frac{|v|}{R}\right)\right]\mathrm{d}x

for some constant
c=c(n,\Delta_{2}(\phi),\nabla_{2}(\phi))
and
for every
h\neq 0
.
This follows from an estimate for the right-hand side of the inequality (
3.22
) by (
3.12
).

4.
Main results

Our hypotheses on the function
a
in (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) read as follows.
We assume
that
a:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\textup{sym}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\textup{sym}}
is a Carathéodory function such that, for a.e.
x,y\in\Omega
,

(4.1)

\displaystyle(a(x,P)-a(x,Q))\cdot(P-Q)

\displaystyle\geq\nu\phi^{\prime\prime}(|P|+|Q|)|P-Q|^{2},

(4.2)

\displaystyle|a(x,P)-a(x,Q)|

\displaystyle\leq L\phi^{\prime\prime}(|P|+|Q|)|P-Q|,

(4.3)

\displaystyle|a(x,P)|

\displaystyle\leq L\phi^{\prime}(|P|),

(4.4)

\displaystyle|a(x,P)-a(y,P)|

\displaystyle\leq(k(x)+k(y))|x-y|^{\alpha}\phi^{\prime}(|P|),

for every
P,Q\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\textup{sym}}
.
Here:

(4.5)

\phi\in C^{2}([0,\infty))\,\,\text{is a Young function satisfying \,\,(\ref{indices3});}

(4.6)

0<\nu\leq L
and
0<\alpha\leq 1
;

(4.7)

k\in L^{m}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)

for some
m\in(\tfrac{n}{\alpha},\infty]
,

and the dot
``\cdot"
stands for scalar product between vectors or matrices.

The assumptions (
4.1
)–(
4.3
) are the natural generalizations to the Orlicz ambit of standard conditions for nonlinear elliptic operators with a power type behavior.
In particular, Equations (
4.3
) and (
2.17
) imply that
a(x,0)=0
. Hence, the monotonicity property (
4.1
) implies the ellipticity condition

(4.8)

\displaystyle a(x,P)\cdot P

\displaystyle\geq\nu\phi^{\prime\prime}(|P|)|P|^{2}

for a.e.
x\in\Omega
and every
P\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\textup{sym}}
.

The assumption (
4.4
)
amounts to the membership of the function
a(\cdot,P)
in a Hajlasz-Sobolev type space of order
\alpha
and summability degree
m
. Especially, if
\alpha=1
, then (
4.4
) is equivalent to requiring that
a(\cdot,P)\in W^{1,m}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\textup{sym}})
for every
P\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\textup{sym}}
, and, in particular,

a(\cdot,P)\in{\rm Lip}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
when
m=\infty
.

A couple
(u,\pi)\in W^{1,\phi}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{\widetilde{\phi}}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
is called a local weak solution to
the system (
1.1
) if

\mathrm{div}\,u=0

and

(4.9)

\int_{\Omega}a(x,\mathcal{E}u)\cdot\mathcal{E}\varphi-\pi\,\mathrm{div}\,\varphi\,\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}f\cdot\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x

for every
\varphi\in W^{1,\phi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
with compact support in
\Omega
.

Clearly, such a definition requires that
the function
f\cdot\varphi
be locally integrable for every test function
\varphi
as above. Thanks to the embedding (
3.14
), this is guaranteed if

(4.10)

\displaystyle f\in L^{\widetilde{\phi_{n}}}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where
\widetilde{\phi_{n}}
denotes the Young conjugate of the function
\phi_{n}
defined by (
2.34
). As hinted in Section
1
, we are able to prove fractional differentiability of
V(\mathcal{E}u)
and
\pi
, provided that the integrability assumption (
4.10
) is slightly enhanced. Specifically, this amounts to requiring that

f\in L^{\psi}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{n})

for some Young function
\psi

fulfilling

(4.11)

\phi^{-1}(t)^{\theta}\phi_{n}^{-1}(t)^{1-\theta}\psi^{-1}(t)\leq ct\qquad\mbox{for}\,\,t\geq 1,

for some exponent
\theta\in(0,1]
and some constant
c
.

Thanks to (
2.5
), the condition (
4.11
) ensures that the function
\psi
lies, up to equivalence, near infinity between the functions
\widetilde{\phi_{n}}
and
\widetilde{\phi}
, which correspond to the values
\theta=0
and
\theta=1
, respectively.
The former choice is excluded, whereas the latter entails the strongest possible conclusions.
In particular, (
4.11
) ensures that

(4.12)

\displaystyle L^{\psi}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\subset L^{\widetilde{\phi_{n}}}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})

– see Remark
4.3
below – and hence that
weak solutions to the system (
1.1
)
are well defined.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
a:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\textup{sym}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\textup{sym}}
be a Carathéodory function satisfying (
4.1
)–(
4.4
).
Assume that
f\in L^{\psi}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
for some Young function

\psi
satisfying (
4.11
) for some
\theta\in(0,1]
and
c>0
.
Let
(u,\pi)
be a local weak solution to the system (
1.1
).

(i) One has that

(4.13)

\displaystyle V(\mathcal{E}u)\in B^{\sigma,2,\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}),

where
\sigma=\min\big\{\alpha,\theta,\theta s_{\phi}^{\prime}/2\big\}
. Moreover,

(4.14)

\displaystyle\sup_{|h|\leq{R}}\int_{B_{{R}}}\frac{|\tau_{h}V(\mathcal{E}u)|^{2}}{|h|^{2\sigma}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\bigg(\int_{B_{2R}}\psi(|f|)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\varkappa}+c_{2}\int_{B_{2R}}\phi(|\nabla u|)\,\mathrm{d}x+c_{3}

for every ball
B_{2R}\Subset\Omega
,
where the exponent
\varkappa
depends on the constants appearing in the assumptions, the constants
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}

also on
R
, and
c_{2}
also on

\|k\|_{L^{m}(B_{2R})}
.

(ii)
One has that

(4.15)

\displaystyle\pi\in B^{\varrho,\widetilde{\phi},\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega),

where
\displaystyle\varrho=\frac{\sigma}{\max\{1,i_{\phi}^{\prime}/2\}}
.
Moreover,

(4.16)

\displaystyle\sup_{|h|<R}\int_{B_{R}}\widetilde{\phi}\bigg(\frac{|\tau_{h}\pi|}{|h|^{\varrho}}\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\bigg(\int_{B_{2R}}\psi(|f|)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\varkappa}+c_{2}\Bigg[\bigg(\int_{B_{2R}}\phi(|\nabla u|)^{\varsigma}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\varsigma}}+\int_{B_{2R}}\widetilde{\phi}(|\pi|)\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigg]+c_{3}

for every ball
B_{2R}\Subset\Omega
,
where the exponent
\varkappa
and the constants
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}
depend on the same quantities as above, and the exponent

\varsigma
depends
on the constants appearing in the assumptions.

As explained in Section
1
, a result parallel to Part (i) of Theorem
4.1
holds for local solutions to the system (
1.2
). Recall that a function
u\in W^{1,\phi}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is said to be a weak solution to (
1.2
) if

(4.17)

\int_{\Omega}a(x,\mathcal{E}u)\cdot\mathcal{E}\varphi\,\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}f\cdot\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x

for every
\varphi\in W^{1,\phi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
with compact support in
\Omega
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume that
a
and
f
are as in Theorem
4.1
. Let
u
be a local weak solution to the system (
1.2
). Then, the same conclusions as in Part (i) of Theorem
4.1
hold.

The proof of Theorem
4.2

is omitted, as it just consists of a simplification of the proof of Theorem
4.1
. Indeed, as a close inspection of that proof reveals, the conclusions of Part (i) are derived via a suitable choice of divergence-free test functions
\varphi
in (
4.9
). They are constructed in two steps, the second one being needed to ensure that the divergence-free condition be fulfilled.
Thanks to the vanishing of
\mathrm{div}\,\varphi
, the equations (
4.9
)
and (
4.17
) agree. Since the latter is assumed to hold for non-necessarily divergence-free
\varphi
, one can just make use of the test functions
\varphi
obtained in the first step. This entails an integral identity of the same form as in the proof of Theorem
4.1
, save that some terms are now missing. The terms which are still present can be handled exactly in the same way.

Remark 4.3
.

By (
2.38
), the assumption (
4.11
) implies that

(4.18)

\displaystyle\phi_{n}^{-1}(t)\psi^{-1}(t)\leq ct\qquad\mbox{for}\,\,t\geq 1,

for some constant
c
. Therefore, owing to (

), there exist
c,t_{0}>0
such that

(4.19)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\phi_{n}}(t)\leq\psi(ct)\quad\text{for $t\geq t_{0}$.}

Hence, (
4.11
) guarantees that (
4.12
) holds, and hence any function
f
as in Theorems
4.1
–
4.2
fulfills (
4.10
).

Remark 4.4
.

Theorem
4.1
recovers, in particular, a special case of a result from
[
27
]
corresponding to
\phi(t)=t^{p}
, with
p\geq 2
and
m=\infty
. It also reproduces an instance from
[
28
]
, with the choice
\theta=1
and

.

Remark 4.5
.

The inclusion (
4.13
) implies, in particular, that

(4.20)

\displaystyle V(\mathcal{E}u)\in W^{\rho,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})

for every
\rho\in(0,\sigma)
. This is a consequence of (

).

Moreover, thanks to the embedding of

, the inclusion (

(4.21)

\displaystyle\phi(|\mathcal{E}u|)\in L^{\frac{n}{n-2\sigma},\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega).

Here,

Remark 4.6
.

Assume that the hypotheses of Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
hold with

\text{$\alpha=1$, \quad$\theta=1$, \quad$s_{\phi}\leq 2$}.

Then,
\sigma=1
and, since
B_{\rm loc}^{1,2,\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})=W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})

We conclude this section with an application of Theorem
4.1
to the case when
\phi
is a power, and, more generally, a power times a power of a logarithm. Let us emphasize that, even in the former case, the conclusions about the borderline regime corresponding to a power which agrees with the space dimension
n
are new.

Example 4.7
.

Assume that

\phi(t)=t^{p}
for some
p>1
.

If
1<p<n
, then

\phi_{n}(t)\approx t^{\frac{np}{n-p}}.

Hence, the condition (
4.11
) holds with

(4.22)

\displaystyle\psi(t)=t^{\frac{np}{({1-\theta})p+n(p-1)}}\quad\text{for $t\geq 0$.}

If
p=n
, then

\phi_{n}(t)\approx e^{t^{n^{\prime}}}-1\quad\text{near infinity.}

The condition (
4.11
) is thus fulfilled with

\psi(t)\approx t^{\frac{n}{n-\theta}}(\log t)^{\frac{({1-\theta})(n-1)}{n-\theta}}\quad\text{near infinity.}

If
p>n
, then

\phi_{n}(t)=\infty\quad\text{near infinity,}

and (
4.11
) holds with

\psi(t)\approx t^{\frac{p}{p-\theta}}\quad\text{near infinity.}

Example 4.8
.

Assume that
\phi(t)=t^{p}\log^{\beta}(1+t)
near infinity, for some
p>1
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
.

If
1<p<n
, then

\phi_{n}(t)\approx t^{\frac{np}{n-p}}(\log(1+t))^{\frac{n\beta}{n-p}}\quad\text{near infinity.}

The condition (
4.11
) therefore holds with

\psi(t)\approx t^{\frac{np}{({1-\theta})p+n(p-1)}}(\log(1+t))^{-\frac{n\beta}{({1-\theta})p+n(p-1)}}\quad\text{near infinity.}

If
p=n
and
\beta<n-1
, then

\phi_{n}(t)\approx e^{t^{\frac{n}{n-1-\beta}}}-1\quad\text{near infinity.}

Hence, the condition (
4.11
) is fulfilled with

\psi(t)\approx t^{\frac{n}{n-\theta}}(\log(1+t))^{\frac{({1-\theta})(n-1)-\beta}{n-\theta}}\quad\text{near infinity.}

If
p=n
and
\beta=n-1
, then

\phi_{n}(t)\approx e^{e^{t^{n^{\prime}}}}-e\quad\text{near infinity,}

and (
4.11
) holds with

\psi(t)\approx t^{\frac{n}{n-\theta}}(\log(1+t))^{-\frac{\theta(n-1)}{n-\theta}}(\log(\log(1+t)))^{\frac{({1-\theta})(n-1)}{n-\theta}}\quad\text{near infinity.}

If either
p=n
and
\beta>n-1
, or
p>n
, then

\phi_{n}(t)=\infty\quad\text{near infinity,}

whence (
4.11
) holds with

\psi(t)\approx t^{\frac{p}{p-\theta}}(\log(1+t))^{-\frac{\theta\beta}{p-\theta}}\quad\text{near infinity.}

5.
A modular Poincaré-Sobolev inequality on domains with finite measure

The definition (
2.34
) of the Sobolev conjugate
\phi_{n}
of a Young function
\phi
requires that the condition (
2.36
) be fulfilled. When dealing with embedding theorems on sets of finite measure, such as (
3.13
) and (
3.14
), and hence of norm inequalities, this is not a restriction. Indeed, as hinted above, replacing
\phi
with a Young function equivalent near infinity and satisfying (
2.36
) results in equivalent norms in
W^{1,\phi}_{0}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
W^{1,\phi}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

Our proofs require, however, a sharp Poincaré-Sobolev type inequality in integral form. The
replacement of
\phi
described above is not possible in this kind of inequalities without changing their form. In order to avoid the unnecessary restriction (
2.36
) on the function
\phi
,
we establish a Poincaré-Sobolev inequality, for functions in
W^{1,\phi}_{0}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, in open sets
\Omega
with finite measure, involving a variant of the function
\phi_{n}
, denoted by
\phi_{0,n}
. The crucial point is that this new function
\phi_{0,n}
is well defined for every Young function
\phi
, and is equivalent to
\phi_{n}
near infinity, independently of the specific modification of
\phi
near zero used in the definition of
\phi_{n}
. This property enables us to make use of
\phi_{0,n}
instead of
\phi_{n}
throughout our proofs. Indeed, since the key assumption
(
4.11
) only involves the behavior of
\phi_{n}
near infinity, it can be equivalently formulated with
\phi_{n}
replaced with
\phi_{0,n}
.

The function
\phi_{0,n}
is defined through the following steps.
Let
\phi
be any Young function such that
\phi(1)>0
. Of course, this is not a restriction, since this assumption is always satisfied, up to scaling. In particular, it is automatically fulfilled if
\phi\in\Delta_{2}
, an hypothesis which, however, is not required for the results of the present section.
Define the function
K_{n}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
as

(5.1)

K_{n}(t)=\chi_{[1,\infty)}(t)\bigg(\int_{1}^{t}\Big(\frac{\tau}{\phi(\tau)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\mathrm{d}\tau\bigg)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}\quad\text{for $t\geq 0$,}

and
\overline{\phi}_{0,n}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)

as

(5.2)

\overline{\phi}_{0,n}(t)=\chi_{[1,\infty)}(t)\bigg(\displaystyle\frac{\phi(K_{n}^{-1}(t-1))}{K_{n}^{-1}(t-1)}(t-1)\bigg)^{n^{\prime}}\quad\text{for $t\geq 0$,}

where
K_{n}^{-1}:[0,\infty)\to[1,\infty]
denotes the generalized right-continuous inverse of
K_{n}
. In particular, we have that
K_{n}^{-1}(0)=1
.

Notice that the function
\overline{\phi}_{0,n}
need not be convex. However, since
\overline{\phi}_{0,n}(t)/(t-1)
is non-decreasing, it is equivalent to the convex function

(5.3)

\displaystyle\phi_{0,n}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\overline{\phi}_{0,n}(\tau)}{\tau-1}\mathrm{d}\tau.

Indeed, one has that

(5.4)

\displaystyle\overline{\phi}_{0,n}(t/2)\leq\phi_{0,n}(t)\leq\overline{\phi}_{0,n}(t).

The equivalence of the function
\phi_{0,n}
to
\phi_{n}
near infinity is the content of the following result.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\phi
be a Young function such that
\phi(1)>0
and let
\phi_{0,n}
be the function given by (
5.3
).
Let

\phi_{n}
be defined as in (
2.34
), with
\phi
modified near
0
, if necessary, in such a way that (
2.36
) holds.
Then, there exist positive constants
c_{1},c_{2},t_{0}
such that

(5.5)

\displaystyle\phi_{n}(c_{1}t)\leq\phi_{0,n}(t)\leq\phi_{n}(c_{2}t)\quad\text{for $t\geq t_{0}$.}

Proof.

By the definition of
\phi_{0,n}(t)
and changes of variables, one has that

(5.6)

\displaystyle\phi_{0,n}(t)

\displaystyle=\chi_{[1,\infty)}(t)\phi_{0,n}(t)=\chi_{[1,\infty)}(t)\int_{0}^{t-1}\frac{\overline{\phi}_{0,n}(r+1)}{r}\mathrm{d}r=\chi_{[1,\infty)}(t)\int_{0}^{t-1}\bigg(\displaystyle\frac{\phi(K_{n}^{-1}(r))}{K_{n}^{-1}(r)}r\bigg)^{n^{\prime}}\frac{1}{r}\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\frac{\chi_{[1,\infty)}(t)}{n^{\prime}}\int_{1}^{K_{n}^{-1}(t-1)}\frac{\phi(s)}{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\qquad\mbox{for}\,\,t\geq 0.

By the property (
2.2
),

(5.7)

\phi(K_{n}^{-1}(t-1)/2)\leq\int_{1}^{K_{n}^{-1}(t-1)}\frac{\phi(s)}{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\phi({K_{n}^{-1}(t-1)})

if
t\geq K_{n}(2)+1
. We claim that

(5.8)

\displaystyle\frac{K_{n}^{-1}(t-1)}{2}\geq K_{n}^{-1}(t/4-1)\quad\text{if $t\geq t\geq 4(K_{n}(3/2)+1)$.}

To verify this claim, observe that, as
K_{n}
is a concave function in
[1,\infty)
, the function
B:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
, given by

B(t)=\chi_{[1,\infty)}(t)(K_{n}^{-1}(t-1)-1)\quad\text{for $t\geq 0$,}

is a Young function. By the property (
2.3
) applied to the function
B
,

(5.9)

\displaystyle\frac{K_{n}^{-1}(t-1)}{2}\geq K_{n}^{-1}(t/2-1)-1/2\quad\text{if $t\geq 2$.}

The property (
2.3
) again ensures that

(5.10)

\displaystyle K_{n}^{-1}(t/2-1)\geq 2K_{n}^{-1}(t/4-1)-1\quad\text{if $t\geq 4$.}

On the other hand,

(5.11)

\displaystyle 2K_{n}^{-1}(t/4-1)-1\geq\quad K_{n}^{-1}(t/4-1)+1/2\quad\text{if $t\geq 4(K_{n}(3/2)+1)$.}

Equation (
5.8
) follows from
(
5.9
)–(
5.11
).

Combining Equations (
5.6
) – (
5.8
) tells us that

(5.12)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}\phi(K_{n}^{-1}(t/4-1))\leq\phi_{0,n}(t)\leq\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}\phi(K_{n}^{-1}(t-1))

near infinity.

Now, observe that there exist positive constants
c_{1}

and
c_{2}
such that

(5.13)

\displaystyle H_{n}^{-1}(c_{1}t)\leq K_{n}^{-1}(t-1)\leq H_{n}^{-1}(c_{2}t)\quad\text{near infinity,}

where
H_{n}
is defined with
\phi
modified near zero, if necessary, in such a way that
the condition (
2.36
) is fulfilled. Indeed, Equation (
5.13
)
is equivalent to

(5.14)

\displaystyle H_{n}(s)/{c_{2}}\leq K_{n}(s)+1\leq H_{n}(s)/c_{1}\quad\text{near infinity,}

and these inequalities hold by the very definitions of
H_{n}
and
K_{n}
.
Equation (
5.5
) follows from (
5.12
), (
5.13
), and (
2.3
).
∎

The Poincaré-Sobolev inequality
this section is devoted to is stated in
Theorem
5.2
below. The critical step in its proof is an
interpolation
inequality provided by the subsequent Theorem
5.3
.
These results
are variants of those from
[
11
]
. As mentioned above, the novelty here is that the assumption (
2.36
) can be dispensed with in both inequalities, at the cost of an additional term, depending on the Lebesgue measure
|\Omega|
of
\Omega
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\Omega
be an open set in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
|\Omega|<\infty
and let
\phi
be a Young function such that
\phi(1)>0
. Then, there exists a constant
c=c(n)
such that

(5.15)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\phi_{0,n}\Bigg(\frac{c|u(x)|}{(|\Omega|+\int_{\Omega}\phi(|\nabla u|)\mathrm{d}y)^{1/n}}\Bigg)\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{\Omega}\phi(|\nabla u|)\mathrm{d}x

for every
u\in W^{1,\phi}_{0}(\Omega)
.

In what follows, given any
p\in(1,\infty)
, we denote by
\overline{\phi}_{0,p}
and
{\phi}_{0,p}
the functions defined as in (
5.2
) and (
5.3
), with
n
replaced with
p
. Moreover, given a measure space
(M,\mu)
and
p\in(1,\infty)
, we denote by
L^{p,1}(M,\mu)
the Lorentz space on
(M,\mu)
of those
\mu
-measurable functions
g:M\to\mathbb{R}
which make the norm

\|g\|_{L^{p,1}(M,\mu)}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(\{|g|>s\})^{\frac{1}{p}}\mathrm{d}s

finite.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
(M_{1},\mu_{1})
and
(M_{2},\mu_{2})
be measure spaces with
\mu_{1}(M_{1})<\infty
. Let
1<p<\infty
and let
T
be a sublinear operator such that

(5.16)

\displaystyle T:L^{1}(M_{1},\mu_{1})\to L^{p^{\prime}}(M_{2},\mu_{2}),

with norm
N_{0}
,
and

(5.17)

\displaystyle T:L^{p,1}(M_{1},\mu_{1})\to L^{\infty}(M_{2},\mu_{2}),

with norm
N_{1}
. Assume that
\phi
is a Young function such that
\phi(1)>0
.
Then, there exists a constant
c=c(p,N_{0},N_{1})
such that

(5.18)

\displaystyle\int_{M_{2}}\phi_{0,p}\Bigg(\frac{c|Tg(y)|}{(\mu_{1}(M_{1})+\int_{M_{1}}\phi(|g|)\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}(x))^{1/p}}\Bigg)\mathrm{d}\mu_{2}(y)\leq\int_{M_{1}}\phi(|g|)\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}(x).

Proof.

Given a function
g:M_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
and
t\geq 0
, define

g_{t}={\rm sign}(g)\min\{|g|,t\}\quad\text{and}\quad g^{t}=g-g_{t}.

To begin with, observe that

(5.19)

\displaystyle\|g_{t}\|_{L^{p,1}(M_{1})}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\mu_{1}(\{|g|>s\})^{\frac{1}{p}}\mathrm{d}s\leq\mu_{1}(M_{1})^{\frac{1}{p}}+\chi_{[1,\infty)}(t)\int_{1}^{t}\mu_{1}(\{|g|>s\})^{\frac{1}{p}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{1}(M_{1})^{\frac{1}{p}}+K_{p}(t)\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\phi(s)}{s}\mu_{1}(\{|g|>s\})\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\mu_{1}(M_{1})^{\frac{1}{p}}+K_{p}(t)\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi^{\prime}(s)\mu_{1}(\{|g|>s\})\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\mu_{1}(M_{1})^{\frac{1}{p}}+K_{p}(t)\bigg(\int_{M_{1}}\phi(|g|)\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\quad\text{for $t\geq 0$.}

On the other hand,

(5.20)

\displaystyle\|g^{t}\|_{L^{1}(M_{1})}

\displaystyle=\int_{t}^{\infty}\mu_{1}(\{|g|>s\})\mathrm{d}s=\frac{t}{\phi(t)}\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{\phi(t)}{t}\mu_{1}(\{|g|>s\})\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\frac{t}{\phi(t)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi^{\prime}(t)\mu_{1}(\{|g|>s\})\mathrm{d}s\leq\frac{t}{\phi(t)}\int_{M_{1}}\phi(|g|)\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}\quad\text{for $t\geq 0$.}

Now, let
k
be a positive constant to be chosen later. One has

(5.21)

\displaystyle\int_{M_{2}}\phi_{0,p}\Big(\frac{|Tg|}{4k}\Big)\,d\mu_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{\infty}\phi_{0,p}^{\prime}(t)\mu_{2}(\{|Tg|>4kt\})\mathrm{d}t\leq\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\overline{\phi}_{0,p}(2t)}{t}\mu_{2}(\{|Tg|>4kt\})\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\overline{\phi}_{0,p}(t)}{t}\mu_{2}(\{|Tg|>2kt\})\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=-\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\overline{\phi}_{0,p}(t)}{t^{p^{\prime}}}\frac{d}{dt}\bigg(\int_{t}^{\infty}\mu_{2}(\{|Tg|>2ks\})s^{p^{\prime}-1}\mathrm{d}s\bigg)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{d}{dt}\bigg(\frac{\overline{\phi}_{0,p}(t)}{t^{p^{\prime}}}\bigg)\bigg(\int_{t}^{\infty}\mu_{2}(\{|Tg|>2ks\})s^{p^{\prime}-1}\mathrm{d}s\bigg)\mathrm{d}t.

Note that the first inequality holds as
\phi_{0,p}^{\prime}(t)\leq\frac{\overline{\phi}_{0,p}(2t)}{t}
, since the function
\frac{\overline{\phi}_{0,p}(t)}{t-1}
is non-decreasing, and the last equality holds thanks to the fact that
\phi_{0,p}(1)=0
.

Set

\lambda(t)=K_{p}^{-1}(t-1)\quad\text{for $t\geq 1$.}

The sublinearity of
T
ensures that

\mu_{2}(\{|Tg|>2ks\})\leq\mu_{2}(\{|Tg_{\lambda(t)}|>ks\})+\mu_{2}(\{|Tg^{\lambda(t)}|>ks\})

for
s,t\geq 1
. Moreover, since the function
\frac{\overline{\phi}_{0,p}(t)}{t^{p^{\prime}}}
is non-decreasing, we have that
\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\frac{\overline{\phi}_{0,p}(t)}{t^{p^{\prime}}}\Big)\geq 0
for
t\geq 1
. Hence,

(5.22)

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\bigg(\frac{\overline{\phi}_{0,p}(t)}{t^{p^{\prime}}}\bigg)\bigg(\int_{t}^{\infty}\mu_{2}(\{|Tg|>2ks\})s^{p^{\prime}-1}\mathrm{d}s\bigg)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{d}{dt}\bigg(\frac{\overline{\phi}_{0,p}(t)}{t^{p^{\prime}}}\bigg)\bigg(\int_{t}^{\infty}\mu_{2}(\{|Tg_{\lambda(t)}|>ks\})s^{p^{\prime}-1}\mathrm{d}s\bigg)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{d}{dt}\bigg(\frac{\overline{\phi}_{0,p}(t)}{t^{p^{\prime}}}\bigg)\bigg(\int_{t}^{\infty}\mu_{2}(\{|Tg^{\lambda(t)}|>ks\})s^{p^{\prime}-1}\mathrm{d}s\bigg)\mathrm{d}t=I+J.

Owing to (
5.17
) and (
5.19
), with
t
replaced with
\lambda(t)
,

(5.23)

\displaystyle\|Tg_{\lambda(t)}\|_{L^{\infty}(M_{2})}\leq N_{1}\|g_{\lambda(t)}\|_{L^{p,1}(M_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq N_{1}\bigg(\mu_{1}(M_{1})^{\frac{1}{p}}+K_{p}(\lambda(t))\bigg(\int_{M_{1}}\phi(|g|)\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq N_{1}\bigg(\mu_{1}(M_{1})^{\frac{1}{p}}+t\bigg(\int_{M_{1}}\phi(|g|)\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\bigg)\quad\text{for $t\geq 1$.}

The choice

(5.24)

\displaystyle k=N_{1}\bigg(\mu_{1}(M_{1})^{\frac{1}{p}}+\bigg(\int_{M_{1}}\phi(|g|)\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\bigg)

entails that

(5.25)

\displaystyle ks\geq N_{1}\bigg(\mu_{1}(M_{1})^{\frac{1}{p}}+t\bigg(\int_{M_{1}}\phi(|g|)\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\bigg)

if
s\geq t\geq 1
. From (
5.23
) and (
5.25
) we deduce that

\mu_{2}(\{|Tg_{\lambda(t)}|>ks\})=0

if
s\geq t\geq 1
, whence

(5.26)

\displaystyle I=0.

By (
5.16
), we have that

(5.27)

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{t}^{\infty}\mu_{2}(\{|Tg^{\lambda(t)}|>ks\})s^{p^{\prime}-1}\mathrm{d}s\bigg)\leq\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\Big(\frac{N_{0}}{k}\Big)^{p^{\prime}}\bigg(\int_{\lambda(t)}^{\infty}\mu_{1}(\{|g|>s\})\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{p^{\prime}}.

Moreover, by (
5.20
) with
t
replaced with
\lambda(t),
and the
definitions of the latter and of
\overline{\phi}_{0,p}(t)
,

(5.28)

\displaystyle\frac{\overline{\phi}_{0,p}(t)}{t^{p^{\prime}}}\bigg(\int_{\lambda(t)}^{\infty}\mu_{1}(\{|g|>s\})\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\overline{\phi}_{0,p}(t)}{t^{p^{\prime}}}\bigg(\frac{\lambda(t)}{\phi(\lambda(t))}\bigg)^{p^{\prime}}\bigg(\int_{M_{1}}\phi(|g|)\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}\bigg)^{p^{\prime}}\leq\bigg(\int_{M_{1}}\phi(|g|)\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}\bigg)^{p^{\prime}}

for
t\geq 1
.
The following chain holds thanks to (
5.27
) and (
5.28
):

(5.29)

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\Big(\frac{N_{0}}{k}\Big)^{p^{\prime}}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{d}{dt}\bigg(\frac{\overline{\phi}_{0,p}(t)}{t^{p^{\prime}}}\bigg)\bigg(\int_{\lambda(t)}^{\infty}\mu_{1}(\{|g|>s\})\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{p^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\Big(\frac{N_{0}}{k}\Big)^{p^{\prime}}\bigg[\frac{\overline{\phi}_{0,p}(t)}{t^{p^{\prime}}}\bigg(\int_{\lambda(t)}^{\infty}\mu_{1}(\{|g|>s\})\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{p^{\prime}}\bigg|_{t=1}^{t=\infty}-\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\overline{\phi}_{0,p}(t)}{t^{p^{\prime}}}\frac{d}{dt}\bigg(\int_{\lambda(t)}^{\infty}\mu_{1}(\{|g|>s\})\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{p^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}t\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\Big(\frac{N_{0}}{k}\Big)^{p^{\prime}}\bigg[\bigg(\int_{M_{1}}\phi(|g|)\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}\bigg)^{p^{\prime}}+p^{\prime}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\overline{\phi}_{0,p}(t)}{t^{p^{\prime}}}\bigg(\int_{\lambda(t)}^{\infty}\mu_{1}(\{|g|>s\})\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{p^{\prime}-1}\mu_{1}(\{|g|>\lambda(t)\})\lambda^{\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg].

Note that

(5.30)

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\frac{\overline{\phi}_{0,p}(t)}{t^{p^{\prime}}}\bigg(\int_{\lambda(t)}^{\infty}\mu_{1}(\{|g|>s\})\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{p^{\prime}-1}\mu_{1}(\{|g|>\lambda(t)\})\lambda^{\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\overline{\phi}_{0,p}(\lambda^{-1}(\tau))}{\lambda^{-1}(\tau)^{p^{\prime}}}\bigg(\int_{\tau}^{\infty}\mu_{1}(\{|g|>s\})\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{p^{\prime}-1}\mu_{1}(\{|g|>\tau\})\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\overline{\phi}_{0,p}(\lambda^{-1}(\tau))}{\lambda^{-1}(\tau)^{p^{\prime}}}\bigg(\frac{\tau}{\phi(\tau)}\bigg)^{p^{\prime}-1}\bigg(\int_{\tau}^{\infty}\frac{\phi(s)}{s}\mu_{1}(\{|g|>s\})\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{p^{\prime}-1}\mu_{1}(\{|g|>\tau\})\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\phi(\tau)}{\tau}\bigg(\int_{\tau}^{\infty}\frac{\phi(s)}{s}\mu_{1}(\{|g|>s\})\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{p^{\prime}-1}\mu_{1}(\{|g|>\tau\})\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\bigg(\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\phi(s)}{s}\mu_{1}(\{|g|>s\})\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{p^{\prime}}\leq\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\bigg(\int_{M_{1}}\phi(|g|)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}\bigg)^{p^{\prime}}.

Combining the inequalities (
5.29
) and (
5.30
) yields

(5.31)

\displaystyle J\leq\frac{2}{p^{\prime}}\Big(\frac{N_{0}}{k}\Big)^{p^{\prime}}\bigg(\int_{M_{1}}\phi(|g|)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}\bigg)^{p^{\prime}},

whence, via (
5.21
) and (

) we obtain that

(5.32)

\displaystyle\int_{M_{2}}\phi_{0,p}\Big(\frac{|Tg|}{4k}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{2}\leq\frac{2}{p^{\prime}}\Big(\frac{N_{0}}{k}\Big)^{p^{\prime}}\bigg(\int_{M_{1}}\phi(|g|)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}\bigg)^{p^{\prime}}.

Since
k
is given by (
5.24
), the latter inequality implies that

(5.33)

\displaystyle\int_{M_{2}}\phi_{0,p}\Bigg(\frac{|Tg|}{4N_{1}\big(\mu_{1}(M_{1})^{\frac{1}{p}}+\big(\int_{M_{1}}\phi(|g|)\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\big)}\Bigg)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{2}\leq\frac{2}{p^{\prime}}\Big(\frac{N_{0}}{N_{1}}\Big)^{p^{\prime}}\int_{M_{1}}\phi(|g|)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}.

Hence, the inequality (
5.18
) follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.2
.

Given any function
u\in W^{1,\phi}_{0}(\Omega)
, denote by
u^{*}:[0,|\Omega|]\to[0,\infty]
its decreasing rearrangement. The Pólya-Szegö principle ensures that
u^{*}
is locally absolutely continuous, and

(5.34)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\phi(|\nabla u|)\,\mathrm{d}x\geq\int_{0}^{|\Omega|}\phi\big({n\omega^{\frac{1}{n}}}s^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}(-{u^{*}}^{\prime}(s)\big)\,\mathrm{d}s,

where
\omega_{n}
denotes the Lebesgue measure of the unit ball in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Moreover,

(5.35)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}{\overline{\phi}_{0,n}}(|u|)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{0}^{|\Omega|}{\overline{\phi}_{0,n}}(u^{*}(s))\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{|\Omega|}\overline{\phi}_{0,n}\bigg(\int_{s}^{|\Omega|}-{u^{*}}^{\prime}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Thanks to Equations (
5.34
) and (
5.35
), the inequality (
5.15
) will follow if we show that

(5.36)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{|\Omega|}\overline{\phi}_{0,n}\Bigg(\frac{c\int_{s}^{|\Omega|}r^{-1/n^{\prime}}g(r)\,\mathrm{d}r}{\big(|\Omega|+\int_{0}^{|\Omega|}\phi(g)dr\big)^{1/n}}\Bigg)\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\int_{0}^{|\Omega|}\phi(g)ds

for every measurable function
g:(0,|\Omega|)\to[0,\infty)
. One can verify that the operator
T
defined as

is sublinear and such that

T:L^{1}(0,|\Omega|)\to L^{n^{\prime}}(0,|\Omega|)\quad\text{and}\quad L^{n,1}(0,|\Omega|)\to L^{\infty}(0,|\Omega|),

with norms depending only on

. The inequality (

) is thus a consequence of Theorem
5.2
.

The following lemma from

,

Lemma A
.

Then,

(6.1)

\displaystyle a(x,Q)\cdot Q

for every

, and

Assume, in addition, that the function

(6.3)

\displaystyle|{a(x,P)-a(x,Q)}|

\displaystyle\leq c\phi_{|{Q}|}^{\prime}(|{P-Q}|),

(6.4)

\displaystyle(a(x,P)-a(x,Q))\cdot(P-Q)

\displaystyle\eqsim\phi_{|{Q}|}\left(|{P-Q}|\right).

for every

and a.e.

We shall also make use of a classical iteration lemma – see e.g.
[

First, assume that

in

in

\displaystyle|F_{P}(x)-F_{P}(y)|

\displaystyle|F_{P}(x)-F_{P}(y)|

depending at most on
n,m,\alpha,\beta,L,\mathrm{dist}(\Omega^{\prime},\partial\Omega)
and

.
An estimate in terms of the sharp fractional maximal function (see

[

\displaystyle|F_{P}(x)-F_{P}(y)|\leq c\big[(F_{P})_{\beta}^{\sharp}(x)+(F_{P})_{\beta}^{\sharp}(y)\big]|x-y|^{\beta}\leq c^{\prime}[k_{\beta}(x)+k_{\beta}(y)]|x-y|^{\beta}

for a.e.

, every

\displaystyle\leq c|h|^{\theta}\lambda|B_{\frac{R}{2}}|^{({1-\theta})/n}\bigg(\Xi_{\lambda}+\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}(\psi(|f|)+1)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)+c|h|^{\theta}\Xi_{\lambda}^{({1-\theta})/n}\bigg(\Xi_{\lambda}+\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}(\psi(|f|)+1)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg).

Fix
\kappa\in(0,1]
. By starting from (
8.4
) instead of (

) and arguing as in the proof of (

) one can deduce that

(8.5)

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq|h|^{\theta}\Bigg[c\kappa\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\phi(|\nabla w|)\,\mathrm{d}x+c_{\kappa}\bigg(\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}(\psi(|f|)+1)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{1+\frac{({1-\theta})i_{\phi}^{\prime}}{n}}+c_{\kappa}\bigg(\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}(\psi(|f|)+1)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{1+\frac{({1-\theta})s_{\phi}^{\prime}}{n}}\Bigg].

Now, consider
\mathrm{I}_{2}
. Observe that the exponent

defined in the statement
is such that

.
Recalling (

), (

) and using Lemma

, with

(8.6)

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\mathcal{A}_{h}\,|\mathcal{E}w|\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\mathcal{B}_{h}\,|\mathcal{E}w|\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c|h|^{\varrho}\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}(|\mathcal{E}(h)|)||\tau_{h}\mathcal{E}u|}{|h|^{\varrho}}|\mathcal{E}w|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+|h|^{\varrho}\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}({k_{\varrho}(x)+k_{\varrho}(x+he_{i})})\phi^{\prime}(|\mathcal{E}u|)|\mathcal{E}w|\,\mathrm{d}x=\mathrm{I}_{2,1}+\mathrm{I}_{2,2},

for some function

with
{m_{\varrho}}>\frac{n}{\varrho}
.
From (
2.28
), Young’s inequality, (
2.22
) and inequality (
2.24
), one can infer that

\displaystyle\mathrm{I_{2,1}}

\displaystyle\leq c{|h|^{\varrho}}\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\frac{\phi_{|\mathcal{E}u|}^{\prime}(|\tau_{h}\mathcal{E}u|)}{|h|^{\varrho}}\left|\mathcal{E}w\right|\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c_{\kappa}{|h|^{\varrho}}\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\widetilde{\phi_{|\mathcal{E}u|}}\left(\frac{\phi_{|\mathcal{E}u|}^{\prime}(|\tau_{h}\mathcal{E}u|)}{|h|^{\varrho}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\kappa{|h|^{\varrho}}\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\phi_{|\mathcal{E}u|}\left(|\mathcal{E}w|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c_{\kappa}\frac{{|h|^{\varrho}}}{|h|^{\varrho\max\{i_{\phi}^{\prime},2\}}}\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\phi_{|\mathcal{E}u|}(|\tau_{h}\mathcal{E}u|)\,\mathrm{d}x+\kappa{|h|^{\varrho}}\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\phi_{|\mathcal{E}u|}(|\mathcal{E}w|)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Notice that
\varrho\max\{i_{\phi}^{\prime},2\}=2\sigma
. Hence, thanks to
(
2.32
) and (
2.26
),

(8.7)

\displaystyle\mathrm{I_{2,1}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{\kappa}\frac{{|h|^{\varrho}}}{|h|^{2\sigma}}\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}|\tau_{h}V(\mathcal{E}u)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+c\kappa{|h|^{\varrho}}\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}(\phi(|\mathcal{E}u|)+\phi(|\mathcal{E}w|))\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c_{\kappa}|h|^{\varrho}\sup_{0<|h|<\frac{R}{2}}\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\frac{|\tau_{h}V(\mathcal{E}u)|^{2}}{|h|^{2\sigma}}\,\mathrm{d}x+c\kappa{|h|^{\varrho}}\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\phi(|\mathcal{E}u|)\,\mathrm{d}x+c\kappa|h|^{\varrho}\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\phi(|\mathcal{E}w|)\,\mathrm{d}x.

An application of Young’s and Hölder’s inequalities, (
2.16
), (
2.18
), and (
6.36
) yields:

(8.8)

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{2,2}

\displaystyle\leq|h|^{\varrho}\Bigg[\kappa\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\phi(|\mathcal{E}w|)\mathrm{d}x+c_{\kappa}\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\widetilde{\phi}\Big({(k_{\varrho}(x)+k_{\varrho}(x+he_{i}))}\phi^{\prime}(|\mathcal{E}u|)\Big)\mathrm{d}x\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq|h|^{\varrho}\Bigg[\kappa\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\phi(|\mathcal{E}w|)\mathrm{d}x+c_{\kappa}\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}(1+{k_{\varrho}(x)+k_{\varrho}(x+he_{i})})^{i_{\phi}^{\prime}}\widetilde{\phi}(\phi^{\prime}(|\mathcal{E}u|))\mathrm{d}x\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq|h|^{\varrho}\Bigg[\kappa\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\phi(|\mathcal{E}w|)\mathrm{d}x+c_{\kappa}\bigg(\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}(1+k_{\varrho}(x)+k_{\varrho}(x+he_{i}))^{{m_{\varrho}}}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{i_{\phi}^{\prime}}{{m_{\varrho}}}}\bigg(\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\big[\phi(|\mathcal{E}u|)\big]^{\frac{{m_{\varrho}}}{{m_{\varrho}}-i_{\phi}^{\prime}}}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{{m_{\varrho}}-i_{\phi}^{\prime}}{{m_{\varrho}}}}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq|h|^{\varrho}\Bigg[\kappa\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\phi(|\mathcal{E}w|)\mathrm{d}x+c_{\kappa}\bigg(\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\big[\phi(|\mathcal{E}u|)\big]^{\frac{{m_{\varrho}}}{{m_{\varrho}}-i_{\phi}^{\prime}}}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{{m_{\varrho}}-i_{\phi}^{\prime}}{{m_{\varrho}}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+c_{\kappa}\bigg(\int_{B_{R}}k_{\varrho}^{{m_{\varrho}}}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{i_{\phi}^{\prime}}{{m_{\varrho}}}}\bigg(\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\big[\phi(|\mathcal{E}u|)\big]^{\frac{{m_{\varrho}}}{{m_{\varrho}}-i_{\phi}^{\prime}}}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{{m_{\varrho}}-i_{\phi}^{\prime}}{{m_{\varrho}}}}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq{|h|^{\varrho}\Bigg[\kappa\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\phi(|\mathcal{E}w|)\mathrm{d}x+c_{\kappa,R}\bigg(\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\big[\phi(|\mathcal{E}u|)\big]^{\frac{{m_{\varrho}}}{{m_{\varrho}}-i_{\phi}^{\prime}}}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{{m_{\varrho}}-i_{\phi}^{\prime}}{{m_{\varrho}}}}}

\displaystyle{\qquad\qquad+c_{\kappa,R}\bigg(\int_{B_{{R}}}{k}^{{m}}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{i_{\phi}^{\prime}}{{m}}}\bigg(\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\big[\phi(|\mathcal{E}u|)\big]^{\frac{{m_{\varrho}}}{{m_{\varrho}}-i_{\phi}^{\prime}}}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{{m_{\varrho}}-i_{\phi}^{\prime}}{{m_{\varrho}}}}\Bigg]}.

Notice that the application of Hölder’s inequality is legitimate, as

{m_{\varrho}}>\frac{n}{\varrho}\geq i_{\phi}^{\prime}
. Also, the last integral is finite, since
\frac{{m_{\varrho}}}{{m_{\varrho}}-i_{\phi}^{\prime}}<\frac{n}{n-2\sigma}
.
Indeed, by (
3.15
), the inclusion
V(\mathcal{E}u)\in B^{\sigma,2,\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
implies that
V(\mathcal{E}u)\in L^{q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
for every

, and hence
\phi(|\mathcal{E}u|)\in L^{q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
for every
q<\frac{n}{n-2\sigma}
.
Equations (
8.6
)–(
8.8
) imply that

(8.9)

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\kappa|h|^{\varrho}\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\phi(|\mathcal{E}w|)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+c_{\kappa}|h|^{\varrho}\Bigg[\sup_{0<|h|<\frac{R}{2}}\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\frac{|\tau_{h}V(\mathcal{E}u)|^{2}}{|h|^{2\sigma}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{B_{R}}\phi(|\mathcal{E}u|)\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigg]+c_{\kappa,R}|h|^{\varrho}\Bigg[\bigg(\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\big[\phi(|\mathcal{E}u|)\big]^{\frac{{m_{\varrho}}}{{m_{\varrho}}-i_{\phi}^{\prime}}}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{{m_{\varrho}}-i_{\phi}^{\prime}}{{m_{\varrho}}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\bigg(\int_{B_{R}}{k}^{{m}}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{i_{\phi}^{\prime}}{{m}}}\bigg(\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\big[\phi(|\mathcal{E}u|)\big]^{\frac{{m_{\varrho}}}{{m_{\varrho}}-i_{\phi}^{\prime}}}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{{m_{\varrho}}-i_{\phi}^{\prime}}{{m_{\varrho}}}}\Bigg].

As far as the term
\mathrm{I}_{3}
is concerned, since
\|\nabla\zeta\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{c}{R}
, by Young’s inequality and (
3.12
), one has that

(8.10)

\displaystyle\mathrm{I_{3}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c|h|}{R}\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\phi^{\prime}(|\mathcal{E}u|)\,|\mathcal{E}w|\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c|h|\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\Big(c_{\kappa}\widetilde{\phi}\big(\phi^{\prime}(|\mathcal{E}u|)/R\big)+\kappa\phi(|\mathcal{E}w|)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c_{\kappa}|h|{\left(\frac{1}{R^{s_{\phi}^{\prime}}}+\frac{1}{R^{i_{\phi}^{\prime}}}\right)}\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\phi(|\mathcal{E}u|)\,\mathrm{d}x+c\kappa|h|\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\phi(|\mathcal{E}w|)\,\mathrm{d}x.

The term
\mathrm{I}_{4}
can be bounded similarly to
\mathrm{I}_{3}
as follows:

(8.11)

\displaystyle\mathrm{I_{4}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c|h|}{R}\int_{B_{\frac{R}{4}}}\phi^{\prime}(|\mathcal{E}u|)\,\frac{|\tau_{-h}w|}{|h|}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c|h|\int_{B_{\frac{R}{4}}}\left[c_{\kappa}\widetilde{\phi}\big(\phi^{\prime}(|\mathcal{E}u|/R)\big)+\kappa\phi\left(\frac{|\tau_{-h}w|}{|h|}\right)\right]\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c_{\kappa}|h|{\left(\frac{1}{R^{s_{\phi}^{\prime}}}+\frac{1}{R^{i_{\phi}^{\prime}}}\right)}\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\phi(|\mathcal{E}u|)+c\kappa|h|\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\phi\left(|{\nabla}w|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Exploiting the
estimates for
\mathrm{I}_{i}
,
i=1,\dots 4
, established above to bound the rightmost side of (
8.3
) yields:

(8.12)

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}

\displaystyle\leq c\kappa|h|^{\varrho}\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\phi(|\nabla w|)|)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+c_{\kappa}|h|^{\varrho}\Bigg[\sup_{0<|h|<\frac{R}{2}}\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\frac{|\tau_{h}V(\mathcal{E}u)|^{2}}{|h|^{2\sigma}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\bigg(\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}(\psi(|f|)+1)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{1+\frac{({1-\theta})i_{\phi}^{\prime}}{n}}+\bigg(\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}(\psi(|f|)+1)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{1+\frac{({1-\theta})s_{\phi}^{\prime}}{n}}\Bigg]

\displaystyle+c_{\kappa,R}|h|^{\varrho}\Bigg[\int_{B_{R}}\phi(|\mathcal{E}u|)\,\mathrm{d}x+\bigg(\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\big[\phi(|\mathcal{E}u|)\big]^{\frac{{m_{\varrho}}}{{m_{\varrho}}-i_{\phi}^{\prime}}}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{{m_{\varrho}}-i_{\phi}^{\prime}}{{m_{\varrho}}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\bigg(\int_{B_{R}}{k}^{{m}}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{i_{\phi}^{\prime}}{{m}}}\bigg(\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\big[\phi(|\mathcal{E}u|)\big]^{\frac{{m_{\varrho}}}{{m_{\varrho}}-i_{\phi}^{\prime}}}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{{m_{\varrho}}-i_{\phi}^{\prime}}{{m_{\varrho}}}}\Bigg].

Here, we have also made use of the fact
\varrho\leq\theta\leq 1
.
Owing to Young’s inequality, the inequalities (
3.12
) and (
2.16
), and the fact that
|h|\leq 1
, the term
\mathrm{II}
admits the following bound:

(8.13)

\displaystyle\mathrm{II}

\displaystyle\leq c|h|\int_{B_{\frac{R}{4}}}\frac{|\tau_{-h}w|}{|h|}\frac{|\pi|}{R}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq|h|\bigg(\kappa\int_{B_{\frac{R}{4}}}\phi\bigg(\frac{|\tau_{-h}w|}{|h|}\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}x+c_{\kappa}\int_{B_{\frac{R}{4}}}\widetilde{\phi}\bigg(\frac{|\pi|}{R}\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq|h|^{\varrho}\bigg(\kappa\int_{B_{\frac{R}{4}}}\phi(|\nabla w|)\,\mathrm{d}x+{c_{\kappa}\left(\frac{1}{R^{s_{\phi}^{\prime}}}+\frac{1}{R^{i_{\phi}^{\prime}}}\right)}\int_{B_{\frac{R}{4}}}\widetilde{\phi}(|\pi|)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg).

Now, combine Equations (
8.2
), (
8.12
), (
8.13
), and make use of
the inequality (
8.1
) to estimate the integrals involving
w
to deduce that

(8.14)

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{B_{{\frac{3}{4}R}}}

\displaystyle\frac{\tau_{h}(\zeta\pi)}{|h|^{\varrho}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg|\leq c\kappa\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\phi(|\varphi|)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+c_{\kappa}\Bigg[\sup_{0<|h|<\frac{R}{2}}\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\frac{|\tau_{h}V(\mathcal{E}u)|^{2}}{|h|^{2\sigma}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\bigg(\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}(\psi(|f|)+1)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{1+\frac{({1-\theta})i_{\phi}^{\prime}}{n}}+\bigg(\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}(\psi(|f|)+1)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{1+\frac{({1-\theta})s_{\phi}^{\prime}}{n}}\Bigg]

\displaystyle+c_{\kappa,R}\Bigg[\int_{B_{R}}\phi(|\mathcal{E}u|)\,\mathrm{d}x+\bigg(\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\big[\phi(|\mathcal{E}u|)\big]^{\frac{{m_{\varrho}}}{{m_{\varrho}}-i_{\phi}^{\prime}}}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{{m_{\varrho}}-i_{\phi}^{\prime}}{{m_{\varrho}}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\bigg(\int_{B_{R}}{k}^{{m}}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{i_{\phi}^{\prime}}{{m}}}\bigg(\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\big[\phi(|\mathcal{E}u|)\big]^{\frac{{m_{\varrho}}}{{m_{\varrho}}-i_{\phi}^{\prime}}}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{{m_{\varrho}}-i_{\phi}^{\prime}}{{m_{\varrho}}}}+\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\widetilde{\phi}(|\pi|)\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigg]

for every

.
Choose

\varphi=\frac{\xi}{|\xi|^{2}}\widetilde{\phi}\big(|\xi|\big),\quad\mbox{where }\,\,\,\xi=\frac{\tau_{h}(\zeta\pi)}{|h|^{\varrho}}.

We claim that

By our choice of

8.14

(8.16)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{3}{4}R}}

\displaystyle\widetilde{\phi}\bigg(\frac{|\tau_{h}(\zeta\pi)|}{|h|^{\varrho}}\bigg)\mathrm{d}x\leq c\kappa\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\widetilde{\phi}\bigg(\frac{|\tau_{h}(\zeta\pi)|}{|h|^{\varrho}}\bigg)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+c_{\kappa}\Bigg[\sup_{0<|h|<\frac{R}{2}}\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\frac{|\tau_{h}V(\mathcal{E}u)|^{2}}{|h|^{2\sigma}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\bigg(\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}(\psi(|f|)+1)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{1+\frac{({1-\theta})i_{\phi}^{\prime}}{n}}+\bigg(\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}(\psi(|f|)+1)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{1+\frac{({1-\theta})s_{\phi}^{\prime}}{n}}\Bigg]

\displaystyle+c_{\kappa,R}\Bigg[\int_{B_{R}}\phi(|\mathcal{E}u|)\,\mathrm{d}x+\bigg(\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\big[\phi(|\mathcal{E}u|)\big]^{\frac{{m_{\varrho}}}{{m_{\varrho}}-i_{\phi}^{\prime}}}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{{m_{\varrho}}-i_{\phi}^{\prime}}{{m_{\varrho}}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\bigg(\int_{B_{R}}{k}^{{m}}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{i_{\phi}^{\prime}}{{m}}}\bigg(\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\big[\phi(|\mathcal{E}u|)\big]^{\frac{{m_{\varrho}}}{{m_{\varrho}}-i_{\phi}^{\prime}}}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{{m_{\varrho}}-i_{\phi}^{\prime}}{{m_{\varrho}}}}+\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\widetilde{\phi}(|\pi|)\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigg].

Observe that
\tau_{h}(\zeta\pi)(x)=\tau_{h}\pi(x)
if

and

|h|\leq\frac{R}{16}
.
Hence, by setting
h_{1}=\min\{1,\frac{R}{16}\}
and
\kappa=\frac{1}{2c}

7.29
)
one obtains:

(8.17)

\displaystyle\sup_{|h|\leq h_{1}}

\displaystyle\int_{B_{{\frac{R}{16}}}}\widetilde{\phi}\bigg(\frac{|\tau_{h}\pi|}{|h|^{\varrho}}\bigg)\mathrm{d}x\leq\sup_{|h|\leq h_{1}}\int_{B_{\frac{3}{4}R}}\widetilde{\phi}\bigg(\frac{|\tau_{h}(\zeta\pi)|}{|h|^{\varrho}}\bigg)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq{c_{R}}\bigg(1+\bigg(\int_{B_{{{\frac{3}{2}R}}}}{k}^{{m}}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{{\frac{2m_{\sigma}}{{m(m_{\sigma}}-\frac{n}{\beta})}}}\bigg)\int_{B_{\frac{3}{2}R}}\phi(|\nabla u|)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+c_{R}\bigg(1+\bigg(\int_{B_{R}}{k}^{{m}}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{i_{\phi}^{\prime}}{{m}}}\bigg)\bigg(\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\big[\phi(|\mathcal{E}u|)\big]^{\frac{{m_{\varrho}}}{{m_{\varrho}}-i_{\phi}^{\prime}}}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{{m_{\varrho}}-i_{\phi}^{\prime}}{{m_{\varrho}}}}

\displaystyle\quad+c_{R}\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\widetilde{\phi}(|\pi|)\,\mathrm{d}x+c{\bigg(\int_{B_{\frac{3}{2}R}}(\psi(|f|)+1)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{1+\frac{({1-\theta})i_{\phi}^{\prime}}{n}}+c\bigg(\int_{B_{\frac{3}{2}R}}(\psi(|f|)+1)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{1+\frac{({1-\theta})s_{\phi}^{\prime}}{n}}}.

) with

\frac{R}{2}

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{3}{4}R}}\widetilde{\phi}\bigg(\frac{|\tau_{h}\pi|}{|h|^{\varrho}}\bigg)\mathrm{d}x\leq c\bigg(\frac{1}{R^{\varrho i_{\phi}^{\prime}}}+1\bigg)\int_{B_{\frac{3}{4}R}}\widetilde{\phi}\big(|\tau_{h}\pi|\big)\mathrm{d}x\leq c\bigg(\frac{1}{R^{\varrho i_{\phi}^{\prime}}}+1\bigg)\int_{B_{\frac{3}{2}R}}\widetilde{\phi}(|\pi|)\mathrm{d}x.

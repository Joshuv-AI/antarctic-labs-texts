Irreversible k k -Threshold Conversion Number of Corona Product and Double Corona Product Graphs
Eric Moon, Paul Flesher, Soumya Bhoumik

Abstract
This paper studies the irreversible k k -threshold process on graphs, where a vertex becomes colored if at least k k neighbors are colored and remains colored indefinitely. We investigate vertex sets that, when initially colored, lead to a completely colored graph. The graphs under initial consideration are constructed using the corona product of cyclic graphs and complete graphs. We then introduce and explore double corona product graphs (of cyclic graphs and complete graphs) to study more complex topologies and their impact on the coloring propagation. We further extend the theory by introducing a probabilistic approach to the coloring dynamics. Our findings provide insights into the interplay between graph structure and saturation dynamics by extending the theory to new families of graphs and introducing a probabilistic approach with potential applications in epidemiology and social influence modeling.

Introduction

The propagation of influence in networks is a fundamental topic in graph theory with applications in epidemiology, opinion dynamics, and social networks. The irreversible
k
-threshold process models such phenomena by coloring a vertex if at least
k
of its neighbors are colored, after which it remains colored permanently. A key problem in this area is the determination of initial sets that, if colored, lead to a completely colored graph under the irreversible
k
-threshold process and then determining the minimum size of such a set.

Numerous researchers have investigated this problem across various families of graphs. Dreyer and Roberts
[
1
]
were the first to explore this topic, analyzing the process in several well-known graph families, including regular graphs, cycles, paths, trees, grid graphs, and complete bipartite graphs. Building on this foundation, Abiad et al. extended the investigation to include certain product families of graphs, such as Cartesian, tensor, strong, and lexicographic products (see
[
5
]
). For readers interested in further developments, additional results can be found in
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
.

In this paper, we examine the
k
-threshold conversion process in corona product graphs
C_{n}\odot K_{p}
. These (corona) product graphs provide a natural framework for modeling structures found in epidemiology and social networking, yet their role in threshold dynamics has not been thoroughly investigated. In addition, we introduce a natural extension by defining and analyzing double corona graphs
C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{p}
that exhibit increased structural complexity and distinct spreading behaviors. We further extend the theory by introducing a probabilistic approach to analyzing the coloring dynamics on these families of graphs.

Our main results establish necessary and sufficient conditions for complete graph coloring in these graph families, identify the smallest sets of colored vertices that can color the entire graph, and demonstrate the impact of randomness on coloring dynamics. This study offers new insights into both theoretical and applied aspects of contagion dynamics by characterizing threshold conversion sets and extending the analysis to probabilistic settings. These findings contribute to a deeper understanding of influence propagation in structured networks, with implications for controlling disease spread, optimizing marketing strategies, and designing resilient network architectures.

We conclude with several key definitions, remarks, and results established elsewhere. The irreversible
k
-threshold conversion process on a graph
G(V,E)
is an iterative procedure that begins with an initial set
S_{0}\subseteq V
of colored vertices. At each step

k

k
-threshold conversion set (IkCS) of

,
S_{t}=V(G)
. The smallest possible size of an IkCS in
G
is called the irreversible
k
-threshold conversion number of
G
, denoted by
C_{k}(G)
. If
C_{k}(G)=|V(G)|
,
G
is called
k-
inconvertible.

Lemma 2.1

C_{k}(C_{n}\odot K_{p})=r\cdot n+C_{k-r}(C_{n}\odot K_{p-r})
where

Proof.

Naturally, when

must contain at least

Given this structure, whenever

C_{n}

k-p

Therefore, we obtain the recurrence:

C_{k}(C_{n}\odot K_{p})=\begin{cases}(k-1)n+C_{1}(C_{n}\odot K_{p-(k-1)}),&\text{if }k<p+1,\\
pn+C_{k-p}(C_{n}),&\text{if }k\geq p+1.\end{cases}

2.2
Probabilistic Approach

In this subsection, we introduce a probabilistic framework to analyze the likelihood of achieving a complete
k
-coloring in
C_{n}\odot K_{p}
under random initial conditions. Unlike traditional deterministic studies of graph coloring, our approach quantifies the success probability of the
k
-threshold process, offering new insights into the robustness and vulnerability of corona product graphs. By partitioning the problem into three distinct cases based on the relationship between
k
and
p
, we derive exact probabilities for successful colorings and, conversely, the graph’s resilience to incomplete propagation. This probabilistic perspective not only generalizes existing threshold results but also provides a tool for evaluating the structural stability of networks modeled by
C_{n}\odot K_{p}
.

Case 1.

k\leq p+1

Let us consider the necessary conditions for a completed graph coloring, by first examining the blocks. Naturally for
k\leq p
,
k-1
vertices are needed in each of the
n
blocks. Then there is one vertex that can be either in one of the blocks or the cycle.

The number of ways we can choose
k-1
vertices in every block and one in the cycle is:

\displaystyle n\binom{p}{k-1}^{n}

(1)

The number of ways we can choose
k-1
vertices in
n-1
blocks and
k
vertices in one of the
n
blocks is:

\displaystyle n\binom{p}{k-1}^{n-1}\binom{p}{k}

(2)

Since either one of these options would yield a successful graph, we know that the total number of successful initial coloring is:

\displaystyle n\binom{p}{k-1}^{n}+n\binom{p}{k-1}^{n-1}\binom{p}{k}

(3)

\displaystyle=\quad n\binom{p}{k-1}^{n-1}\binom{p+1}{k}

(4)

Then let us consider the total number of ways to pick the
(k-1)n+1
vertices from the
n(p+1)
vertices in the graph.

\displaystyle\binom{n(p+1)}{(k-1)n+1}

(5)

Thus, the probability of successful colorings is

\displaystyle P(\text{S})=\frac{n\binom{p}{k-1}^{n-1}\binom{p+1}{k}}{\binom{n(p+1)}{(k-1)n+1}}

(6)

Case 2.

k=p+2

From Lemma
2.1
, we know that we will need to start with all
p
vertices for each of the
n
blocks to have a successfully colored graph. The number of ways to do this is:

\displaystyle\binom{p}{p}^{n}=1

(7)

Additionally, we will need to alternate colored vertices in the cycle. Then we need a minimum of
\lceil{\frac{n}{2}}\rceil
cycle such that they alternate around the cycle for a successful coloring of the graph. If
n
is even, then you can either choose all of the odd labeled cycle nodes or all of the even labeled cycle nodes, hence only
2
options.

If
n
is odd, there must be two consecutive colored vertices in
S_{0}
with the rest of the vertices alternating. Let the
i^{th}
and
(i+1)^{st}
vertices be the consecutive colored vertices and note that the
(n+1)^{st}
vertex and the
1^{st}
vertex are the same. As such, there are
n
possible choices for the pair of consecutive colored vertices, hence there are
n
options when
n
is odd. Therefore, the number of ways to successfully pick the alternating vertices can be expressed as

\displaystyle 2+(n-2)[n\bmod 2)]

(8)

Then we can calculate the total number of ways to color
pn+\lceil{}{\frac{n}{2}}\rceil{}
vertices from the
n(p+1)
total vertices in the graph.

\displaystyle\binom{n(p+1)}{pn+\lceil{}{\frac{n}{2}}\rceil{}}

(9)

Thus the probability of successfully selecting initial vertices is

\displaystyle P(\text{S})=\frac{2+(n-2)[n\bmod 2)]}{\binom{n(p+1)}{pn+\lceil{}{\frac{n}{2}}\rceil{}}}

(10)

Expanding the Corona Product

We now introduce a new family of graph product which we call the
double corona product
, denoted
G_{1}\mathbin{\circledcirc}G_{2}
. To the best of our knowledge, this construction is novel. The double corona product of two graphs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
is formed by taking two identical copies of
G_{1}
, say
G_{1}
and
G_{1}^{\prime}
, and
n=|V(G_{1})|
copies of
G_{2}
. Then for each
i\in\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
, the
i^{\rm th}
vertex of both
G_{1}
and
G_{1}^{\prime}
is connected to every vertex in the
i^{\rm th}
copy of
G_{2}
. The formal definition is given below.

Definition 3.1

If
G_{1}
has vertex set
V(G_{1})=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n}\}
and
G_{2}
has vertex set
V(G_{2})
, then:

•

V(G_{1}\mathbin{\circledcirc}G_{2})=V(G_{1}^{{}^{\prime}})\cup V(G_{1}^{{}^{\prime\prime}})\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}V(G_{2}^{i})
, where
V(G_{2}^{i})
is the vertex set of the
i
-th copy of
G_{2}
.

•

E(G_{1}\mathbin{\circledcirc}G_{2})=E(G_{1}^{{}^{\prime}})\cup E(G_{1}^{{}^{\prime\prime}})\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}E(G_{2}^{i})\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\{(v_{i}^{{}^{\prime}},u),(v_{i}^{{}^{\prime\prime}},u)\mid u\in V(G_{2}^{i})\}
.

where
G_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}=\{v_{1}^{{}^{\prime}},v_{2}^{{}^{\prime}},\ldots,v_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}\},G_{1}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}=\{v_{1}^{{}^{\prime\prime}},v_{2}^{{}^{\prime\prime}},\ldots,v_{n}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\}
are copies of
G_{1}
.

In this paper, we focus on the double corona product
C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{p}
, which contains
n(p+2)
many vertices, and
n\left(\frac{(p+1)(p+2)}{2}+1\right)
many edges. The vertex set of
C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{p}
, the vertex set is given by
V=\{v_{i}|1\leq i\leq n\}\cup\{u_{i}^{j}|1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq p\}\cup\{w_{i}|1\leq i\leq n\}
, where
v_{i}
denotes the vertices of
C_{n}^{\prime}
(we refer them as inner vertices),
w_{i}
denotes the vertices of
C_{n}^{\prime\prime}
(we refer them as outer vertices), and where
u_{t}^{j}
represents the vertices of the copy of the
K_{p}
graph connected with the
v_{i}
vertex of
C_{n}^{\prime}
and the
w_{i}
vertex of
C_{n}^{\prime\prime}
. Within the context of double corona graphs, we redefine the
i^{th}
block to be
B_{i}=\{v_{i},w_{i},u_{i}^{j}|1\leq j\leq p\}
.

We will denote a block as
B
(blank) if there are no seed vertices,
C
(complete) if all vertices are either seed or converted,
O
(
I
respectively) if the seed vertex is
w_{i}
(
v_{i}
respectively),
M
(middle) if there is exactly one seed vertex among
\{u^{j}_{i}|1\leq j\leq p\}
in the block, and
T
(two) if there are exactly two seed vertices in the block.

Remark 3.2

From the definitions of corona and double corona graphs, it is clear that the graphs
C_{n}\odot K_{p}
and
C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{p-1}
have the same number of vertices and edges; however a different construction. We are interested in how this construction will affect the irreversible
k
-threshold process.

3.1
k
-threshold of
C_{k}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{p})

Lemma 3.3

Given any
k
-conversion set
S_{0}
of
C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{p}
, there exists a related
k
-conversion set
S^{\prime}_{0}
such that
|S^{\prime}_{0}|\leq|S_{0}|
with each block containing at most one seed vertex.

Proof.

We begin by making several observations.

•

Any block with two vertices either seed, converted, or a mixture of both complete the next iteration. As such, any irreversible 2-conversion set with any blocks containing more than 2 seeds is suboptimal and need not be considered in determining the minimal size.

•

B
converts if and only if there exists an iteration such that the two adjacent blocks are complete:
CBC
.

•

MOMMMMMM\ldots M
is an irreversible 2-conversion set without any blank blocks and with as many seed vertices as blocks.

Let
S_{0}
be an irreversible 2-conversion set with at least one blank block. We can consider
S_{0}
as a collection of strings of non-blank blocks book-ended with blank blocks (possibly the same blank block on both ends). If we can show that each string can be restructured so that the blocks are still converted with at most the same number of seed vertices and having at most one seed vertex in each block, we will have shown the result. We proceed with three cases. Let
N
be the number of non-blank blocks between two consecutive blank blocks.

Case
1.

Theorem 3.4

C_{2}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{p})=\lfloor\frac{3n+3}{4}\rfloor=n-\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\rfloor\text{ for }n\geq 3,\,p\geq 2
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.3
, we need only consider sets with at most one seed vertex in each block. Suppose that
S_{0}
is such an irreversible 2-conversion set and has more than
\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\rfloor
blank blocks. By the pidgeon-hole principle, there exists at least one string of four blocks with 2 blank blocks, but this necessitates two seed vertices in a single block which is a contradiction. As such, any irreversible 2-conversion set with at most one seed vertex in each block cannot have more than
\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\rfloor
many blank blocks meaning that there must be at least
n-\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\rfloor
many seed vertices.

To establish the result, we provide explicit constructions of such sets of minimal size.

\displaystyle n\,\equiv\,0\,(\rm mod\,4)

\displaystyle~~~~\underbrace{MOMB}_{1}\dots\underbrace{MOMB}_{\frac{n}{4}}

\displaystyle n\,\equiv\,1\,(\rm mod\,4)

\displaystyle~~~~~\underbrace{MOMB}_{1}\dots\underbrace{MOMB}_{\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\rfloor}M

\displaystyle n\,\equiv\,2\,(\rm mod\,4)

\displaystyle~~~~~\underbrace{MOMB}_{1}\dots\underbrace{MOMB}_{\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\rfloor}MO

\displaystyle n\,\equiv\,3\,(\rm mod\,4)

\displaystyle~~~~~\underbrace{MOMB}_{1}\dots\underbrace{MOMB}_{\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\rfloor}MOM

∎

Theorem 3.5

C_{2}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{1})=n\text{ for }n\geq 3
.

Proof.

We show that we can convert the entire graph with
n
many seed vertices by choosing
S_{0}
to be of the form
\{I,O,I,O,\cdots,I,O\}\,
or
\{I,O,I,O,\,\cdots,I,O,M\}
if
n
is even or odd respectively, which clearly proves that
C_{2}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{1})\leq n
. Thus, it remains to show that
C_{2}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{1})\geq n
. We will show that
C_{2}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{1})>n-1
. Let us assume that
|S_{0}|=n-1
. First, we make the obvious observation that in between two consecutive Blank blocks if there are no
C
or
T
(only
I
,
O
, and
M
) blocks, the graph will not be converted. Thus, there must be either
T
or
C
blocks, in between two consecutive Blank blocks, in order to make the conversion complete. However, with
n-1
seed vertices, if there are
m
many
T
or
C
blocks, then there will be at least
m+1
many Blank blocks, which implies that there will be two consecutive Blank blocks with no
T
block in between. Thus
n-1<C_{2}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{1})\leq n
, which implies

C_{2}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{1})=n.

By Lemma
3.3
, it is sufficient to consider 2-conversion sets in which each block of
C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{1}
contains at most one seed vertex. Suppose
S_{0}
is an irreversible 2-conversion set with fewer than
n
seed vertices.
Since there are
n
blocks and each block contains at most one seed, at least one block must be blank. Consider such a blank block. Since each adjacent block contains at most one seed, the blank block cannot simultaneously have both its inner and outer vertex be colored from the colored neighbors. Therefore, the blank block cannot become colored which is a contradiction, giving the lower bound:

C_{2}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{1})\geq n

A construction using exactly one seed per block of the form
\{I,O,I,O,\cdots,I,O\}\,
or

\{I,O,I,O,\,\cdots,I,O,M\}
if
n
is even or odd respectively, successfully colors the graph, hence

C_{2}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{1})\leq n.

Therefore,

C_{2}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{1})=n

∎

Theorem 3.6

C_{2}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{0})=2\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil\,,\,for\,n\geq 3

Proof.

Notice that
K_{0}
is a complete graph with no edges or vertices. From the definition of double corona graphs,
C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{0}
is constructed by taking two identical copies,
C_{n}^{\prime}
and
C_{n}^{\prime\prime}
and applying the corona product from them to a
K_{0}
(a complete graph with zero vertices). Hence, there are two disjoint cycles
C_{n}^{\prime}
and
C_{n}^{\prime\prime}
. Then
C_{2}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{0})=C_{2}(C_{n}^{\prime})+C_{2}(C_{n}^{\prime\prime})
. By Proposition
1.2
,
C_{2}(C_{n}^{\prime})=\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil=C_{2}(C_{n}^{\prime\prime})
. Therefore

C_{2}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{0})=2\lceil\tfrac{n}{2}\rceil.

∎

Lemma 3.7

C_{k}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{p})=\tau\cdot n+C_{k-\tau}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{p-\tau})
where
\tau=\min\{(k-2),p\}

Proof.

From Remark
1.1
, we know that
(k-2)n

M
nodes are necessary in
S_{0}
for the graph to be completely colored. As such, every vertex, regardless of whether it is in a block or in a cycle, is connected to at least
k-2
colored vertices. As such, we may consider the associated graph without these
(k-2)n
colored vertices and with the lowered threshold number of 2.

C_{k}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{p})=(k-2)n+C_{2}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{p-k+2}),\quad\text{ for }k\leq p+2

On the other hand, if
k>p+2
, all
p
vertices in each block are removed. The problem then reduces to completing the graph
C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{0}
under a threshold of
k-p
. Hence,

C_{k}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{p})=pn+C_{k-p}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{p-k+2}),\quad\text{ for }k>p+2

It follows that:

C_{k}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{p})=\begin{cases}(k-2)n+C_{2}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{p-(k-2)})&\text{if }k\leq p+2,\\
pn+C_{k-p}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{0})&\text{if }k>p+2.\end{cases}

∎

Theorem 3.8

For the graph
C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{p}
, the
k
-conversion number is given by

C_{k}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{p})=\begin{cases}(k-2)n+\lfloor\frac{3n+3}{4}\rfloor&\text{ if }k\leq p\\
pn&\text{ if }k=p+1\\
pn+2\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil&\text{ if }k=p+2\\
k\text{-inconvertible}&\text{ if }k\geq p+3\end{cases}

Proof.

We consider four cases based on the value of
k
.

Case
1.
k\leq p

As a direct consequence of Lemma
3.7
, we have:

C_{k}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{p})=(k-2)n+C_{2}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{p-(k-2)}).

Let
p^{*}=p-(k-2)
, so
p^{*}\geq 2
. Then:

C_{k}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{p})=(k-2)n+C_{2}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{p^{*}}).

By Theorem
3.4
,

C_{2}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{p^{*}})=\left\lfloor\frac{3n+3}{4}\right\rfloor,\quad\text{for }p^{*}\geq 2

Thus,

C_{k}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{p})=(k-2)n+\left\lfloor\frac{3n+3}{4}\right\rfloor,\quad\text{for }k\leq p.

Case
2.
k=p+1

By Lemma
3.7
,

C_{k}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{p})=(k-2)n+C_{2}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{1}).

By Theorem
3.5
:

C_{2}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{1})=n.

Therefore,

C_{k}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{p})=(k-2)n+n=pn,\quad\text{for }k=p+1

Case
3.
k=p+2

Again, by Lemma
3.7
,

C_{k}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{p})=pn+C_{2}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{0}).

From Theorem
3.6
, we obtain:

C_{2}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{0})=2\cdot C_{2}(C_{n})=2\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor.

Thus,

C_{k}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{p})=pn+2\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor,\quad\text{for }k=p+2.

Case
4.
k\geq p+3

Applying Lemma
3.7
once more,

C_{k}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{p})=pn+C_{k^{*}}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{0})=pn+C_{k^{*}}(C_{n}),

where
k^{*}\geq k-p
.

Since each vertex in
C_{n}
has degree 2, and by Remark
1.1
,
C_{k^{*}}(C_{n})
is
k^{*}
-inconvertible for all
k^{*}\geq 3
, it follows that:

C_{k}(C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{p})\text{ is }k\text{-inconvertible, for all }k\geq p+3.

∎

Conclusion

A significant advancement presented in our paper is the probabilistic analysis on
C_{n}\odot K_{p}
: We derived the exact probability that a seed set
S_{0}
with
k
-threshold vertices, chosen uniformly at random, fully colors the graph. To our knowledge, no prior work has addressed this random‐seeding method for corona products.

In future work, we aim to apply our probabilistic framework to the double‐corona graph family
C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{p}
. By comparing these probabilities against those for the standard corona
C_{n}\odot K_{p-1}
, since
C_{n}\mathbin{\circledcirc}K_{p}
and
C_{n}\odot K_{p-1}
share the same number of vertices and edges. This will isolate exactly how the double corona construction interferes with (or enhances) propagation under random seeding. This comparison will broaden our understanding of how both deterministic thresholds and random‐seeding resilience depend jointly on graph structure and construction.

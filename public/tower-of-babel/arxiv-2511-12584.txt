( − 1 ) (-1) -Shifted Darboux theorem of derived schemes in characteristic p > 2 p>2
Jiaqi Fu

Abstract
The derived geometry approach to Donaldson–Thomas theory (over ℂ \mathbb{C} ) is built on Pantev–Toën–Vezzosi–Vaquié’s existence theorem of ( − 1 ) (-1) -shifted symplectic forms [ PTVV13 ] and Brav–Bussi–Joyce’s shifted Darboux theorem [ BBJ19 ] . In this paper, we prove a Darboux theorem in characteristic p > 2 p>2 for the ( − 1 ) (-1) -shifted symplectic forms endowed with an infinitesimal structure . A key ingredient is Antieau’s derived infinitesimal cohomology [ Ant25 ] , which enjoys a Poincaré-type lemma. Our argument is in fact characteristic-free and provides a conceptual understanding of the Brav–Bussi–Joyce theorem. Moreover, we extend the existence theorem of Pantev–Toën–Vaquié–Vezzosi by constructing a de Rham ( − 1 ) (-1) -shifted symplectic form on Map k ⁡ ( X , Perf ¯ ) \map_{k}(X,\stperf) , where X X is a Calabi–Yau 3 3 -fold over a field k k in characteristic p > 2 p>2 . We conjecture that this ( − 1 ) (-1) -shifted symplectic form admits an infinitesimal structure.

Introduction

Donaldson–Thomas type (DT-type) invariants are virtual counts of (stable) sheaves on complex Calabi–Yau
3
-folds. Following work of Behrend
[
Beh09
]
and Pantev–Toën–Vaquié–Vezzosi
[
PTVV13
]
, classical DT theory can be regarded as a shadow of
(-1)
-shifted symplectic structures. Although introduced only recently,
(-1)
-shifted symplectic forms already appear implicitly in Thomas’s foundational work
[
Tho00
, page 8]
, in the guise of intersections of Lagrangians in a (holomorphic) symplectic manifold, each of which naturally carries a
(-1)
-shifted symplectic form
[
PTVV13
, §2.2]
. The simplest example can be given by considering the derived critical locus
\dcrit(f)
of a function
f:U\to\mathbb{C}
, i.e. the derived pullback of
U\xrightarrow{0}T^{*}U\xleftarrow{df}U
, where
T^{*}U
carries the tautological symplectic form. Invariants of DT-type then emanate from a shifted Darboux theorem of Brav–Bussi–Joyce stating that
(-1)
-shifted symplectic derived schemes are Zariski locally equivalent to the simplest Lagrangian intersections
\mathrm{Crit}(f)
(see
[
BBJ19
]
). For instance, classical DT-numbers are essentially Milnor numbers associated to the singularities of
f
.

The introduction of derived foliations
[
TV23a
]
by Toën–Vezzosi helps to revisit Brav–Bussi–Joyce’s result in a more conceptual way. Pantev–Toën pointed out that, on a
(-1)
-symplectic derived scheme
(X,\omega)
, the following data are equivalent (see
[
PT15
]
):

(1)

a Lagrangian derived foliation
\mathscr{F}
on
X
, and the exactness of
\omega
;

(2)

a symplectic identification of
X
to the derived critical locus
\dcrit(f)
for some function.

Hence, the shifted Darboux theorem can be interpreted as the local existence of Lagrangian derived foliations, which places derived foliation theory at centre stage.

This work extends Brav–Bussi–Joyce’s Darboux theorem to positive characteristics (
p>2
) via a foliation-theoretic approach, see Theorem
1.4
. We hope this work will initiate the investigation of Donaldson–Thomas theory in positive characteristics.

Poincaré-type lemma.

One of the main challenge in characteristic
p>0
is that de Rham cohomology admits too many cycles via the Cartier isomorphism, even in the derived setting. To remedy this, we instead consider a derived analogue of Grothendieck’s infinitesimal cohomology, namely the
Hodge-completed derived infinitesimal cohomology

\widehat{\mathbbb{\Pi}}
(Construction
3.1
), following Antieau
[
Ant25
]
. The universality of derived infinitesimal cohomology guarantees a comparison map

c:\widehat{F\mathbbb{\Pi}}(X/k)\to\widehat{F\mathrm{DR}}(X/k),

which respects the Hodge filtrations. In contrast to the derived de Rham cohomology, derived infinitesimal cohomology satisfies a Poincaré-type lemma (Theorem
4.1
):

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
B
is a simplicial commutative ring which is
almost of finite presentation
over
k
. Then there is a natural equivalence of derived commutative rings

\widehat{\mathbbb{\Pi}}\mathstrut_{B/k}\simeq\lim_{n}B^{(n)}\simeq\lim_{n}\pi_{0}B^{(n)},

where
B^{(n)}
denotes the Frobenius twist relative to
k
. In particular,
\pi_{i}\widehat{\mathbbb{\Pi}}\mathstrut_{B/k}=0
for
i\neq 0,-1
.

Remark 1.2
.

(1)

Antieau–Mathew independently discovered this Poincaré-type lemma in the context of quasisyntomic descent
[
Ant25
]
.

(2)

The graded pieces of
c:\widehat{F\mathbbb{\Pi}}(X/k)\to\widehat{F\mathrm{DR}}(X/k)
are given by

\lsym_{B}(\mathbb{L}_{B/k}[-1])\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Norm}}\wedge_{B}(\mathbb{L}_{B/k})[-1].

This difference is related to the higher
p
-power on the Lie side, see
[
Fu24
, Example 1.12]
.

The Hodge filtration induces a fibre sequence for every
q\geq 1
,

F_{[0,q-1]}\mathbbb{\Pi}(X/k)[-1]\xrightarrow{d_{\Inf}}\widehat{F_{q}\mathbbb{\Pi}}\mathstrut(X/k)\xrightarrow{\incl}\widehat{\mathbbb{\Pi}}\mathstrut(X/k).

In this context, an
infinitesimal closed
q
-form of degree
n
is defined as a cycle

\omega\in\pi_{-q-n}\widehat{F_{q}\mathbbb{\Pi}}\mathstrut(X/k),

and an
exact structure for
\omega
amounts to a lift
\lambda\in\pi_{1-q-n}F_{[0,q-1]}\mathbbb{\Pi}(X/k)
such that
d_{\Inf}\lambda=\omega
, or in other words, a null homotopy
\incl\omega\sim 0
in
\widehat{\mathbbb{\Pi}}\mathstrut(X/k)
.

Jet-Frobenius residue.

When
X=\spec B
is affine and of finite presentation, the Poincaré-type lemma shows that the obstructions to exactness live in

\pi_{-1}\widehat{F_{q}\mathbbb{\Pi}}\mathstrut_{B/k}[1]\cong\lim_{n}\mathstrut^{1}\pi_{0}B^{(n)}.

Furthermore, for each
N\geq\log_{p}(q)
, there is a
jet-Frobenius power map
(Construction
4.9
)

\mathcal{J}:\prod_{n\geq N}B^{(n)}\to\widehat{F_{q}\mathbbb{\Pi}}\mathstrut_{B/k}[1],

where
\incl\circ\pi_{0}\mathcal{J}:\prod_{n\geq N}\pi_{0}B^{(n)}\twoheadrightarrow\lim_{n}^{1}\pi_{0}B^{(n)}
agrees with the surjection in Milnor’s sequence. A
jet-Frobenius residue
of a
(-1)
-shifted
q
-form
\omega
is a cycle

\res_{JF}:=\mathcal{J}(g)

which satisfies
\incl\res_{JF}\sim\incl\omega
in
\widehat{\mathbbb{\Pi}}\mathstrut_{B/k}
. For an arbitrarily large
N
, there always exists a jet-Frobenius residue that lifts to Hodge filtration degree
N
.

Example 1.3
.

Consider
\widehat{\mathbbb{\Pi}}\mathstrut_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}
as
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-module, which can be modeled by a chain complex

\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]\xrightarrow{\partial_{0}:f\mapsto f\otimes 1-1\otimes f}\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]\widehat{\otimes}\mathstrut_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]\to\cdots,

where the Hodge filtration is given by the degreewise adic filtration. Here,
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]\widehat{\otimes}\mathstrut_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
is the
\infty
-jet of the affine line over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, and a jet-Frobenius residue is an infinite sum

f_{q}(x^{p^{q}})\otimes 1-1\otimes f_{q}(x^{p^{q}})+\cdots+f_{n}(x^{p^{n}})\otimes 1-1\otimes f_{n}(x^{p^{n}})+\cdots.

The jet-Frobenius residue is not uniquely defined, since the degree-zero copy of
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
lies in Hodge filtration degree
0<q
.

2.1
Filtrations and derived rings

Notation 2.1
.

Let
\mathbb{N}^{\geq}
and
\mathbb{N}^{dis}
be the
1

\geq

\infty

\infty

\fil\mathcal{C}:=\fun\big(N(\mathbb{N}^{\geq}),\mathcal{C}\big),\ \ \ \ \ \gr\mathcal{C}:=\fun\big(N(\mathbb{N}^{dis}),\mathcal{C}\big).

The functor of taking graded pieces
\gr:\fil\mathcal{C}\to\gr\mathcal{C}
maps every
(\ldots\to F_{2}C\to F_{1}C\to F_{0}C)
to
\{\gr_{n}C\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\gr_{n}C:=\mathrm{cofib}(F_{n+1}C\to F_{n}C)
. See
[

]
for a detailed discussion of filtrations in stable

For

, let

be the endo-functor of

truncating
(\ldots\to F_{2}C\to F_{1}C\to F_{0}C)

\ldots\to F_{n+1}C\to F_{n}C\to F_{n}C\to\ldots\to F_{n}C,

and denote
F_{[m,n]}:=\mathrm{cofib}(F_{\geq n}\to F_{\geq m})
for all
n\geq m\geq 0
. We also denote by
|-|:=F_{0}
the underlying module, and abbreviate
|F_{[m,n]}-|
as

if there is no risk of confusion.

Definition 2.2
.

If
\mathcal{C}
has sequential limits, we say a filtered object

is

\lim_{n}F_{n}C\simeq 0.

Let
\widehat{\fil}\mathcal{C}\subset\fil\mathcal{C}
be the full subcategory of complete filtered objects. This inclusion has a left adjoint

given by the assignment

We will mainly consider the case of

-modules.

Recollection 2.3

(Derived algebras)
.

Let
\vect_{\mathbb{Z}}
be the
1
-category of free abelian groups. The ordinary symmetric power

V\mapsto\oplus_{n\geq 0}(V^{\otimes n})_{\Sigma_{n}}

defines a monad on

are of finite degree and preserve

, finite free

-modules. Hence it can be canonically extended to a sifted-colimit-preserving monad
\lsym
on
\m_{\mathbb{Z}}

[

[

Let

be the

The functors

2.2
Frobenius

An advantage of
\dalg_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
compared with
\calg_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
is that there is a genuine Frobenius. For simplicity, we only discuss the Frobenius of connective algebras. Recall that, in the
\infty
-category
\scr_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\simeq\mathcal{P}_{\Sigma}(\mathrm{Poly}^{\omega}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}})
, the identity functor
id
admits an endotransformation, called
the absolute Frobenius map
,

\frob:id_{\scr_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}}\to id_{\scr_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}}

obtained by extending the Frobenius of (finitely generated)
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-polynomials. More generally, fixing a base ring
R\in\scr_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
, there is a
relative Frobenius twist

(-)^{(1)}:\scr_{R}\to\scr_{R}
defined by

B^{(1)}:=B\otimes_{R,\frob}R,

together with a relative Frobenius map
\varphi_{B}:B^{(1)}\to B
.

Definition 2.9
.

A simplicial commutative ring
R\in\scr_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
is said to be
perfect
if its Frobenius is an equivalence. The
left perfection functor
is given by the assignment

(-)_{\perf}:A\mapsto\colim\big(A\xrightarrow{\frob}A\xrightarrow{\frob}A\xrightarrow{\frob}\ldots\big).

The left perfection is always an ordinary ring.

Lemma 2.10
.

For an arbitrary
A\in\scr_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
, the natural transformation
\frob
induces

•

the ordinary absolute Frobenius
(-)^{p}:\pi_{0}A\to\pi_{0}A
in degree
0
;

•

null map on
\pi_{i}
for
i\geq 1
.

In particular, the natural map
(A)_{\perf}\to(\pi_{0}A)_{\perf}
is an equivalence.

Proof.

The derived transformation
\frob
induces a unary homotopy operation
\frob_{*}:\pi_{i}A\to\pi_{i}A
of degree
0
and weight
p
, which can be encoded by some cycle in
\pi_{i}\lsym^{p}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\mathbb{F}_{p}[i])
by the Yoneda lemma. However, the group

4.1

Theorem 4.1

(Poincaré type lemma)
.

Let

We are going to see that line (

Lemma 4.2

The cotangent complex

When

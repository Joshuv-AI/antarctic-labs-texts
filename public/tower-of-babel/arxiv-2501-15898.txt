Abstract
. The homotopy category of a model structure on a weakly idempotent complete additive category is proved to be equivalent to the additive quotient of the category of cofibrant-fibrant objects with respect to the subcategory of cofibrant-fibrant-trivial objects. A model structure on pointed category is fibrant, if every object is a fibrant object. Fibrant model structures is explicitly described by trivial cofibrations, and also by fibrations. Fibrantly weak factorization systems are introduced, fibrant model structures are constructed via fibrantly weak factorization systems, and a one-one correspondence between fibrantly weak factorization systems and fibrant model structures is given. Applications are given to rediscover the ω \omega -model structures and the 𝒲 \mathcal{W} -model structures, and their relations with exact model structures are discussed.



1.
Introduction

This paper is three fold.
First, Qullen’s homotopy category of a model structure on a weakly idempotent complete additive category
is expressed as the additive quotient of the category of cofibrant-fibrant objects with respect to
the subcategory of cofibrant-fibrant-trivial objects.

Second, a fibrant model structure
on a weakly idempotent complete additive category
is explicitly described by trivial cofibrations, and also by fibrations.
To construct fibrant model structures,
fibrantly weak factorization systems are introduced;
and a one-one correspondence between fibrant model structures and fibrantly weak factorization systems is given.
We will see that, even on exact categories,
fibrant model structures are in general not exact model structures.

Finally, as applications we rediscover two concrete fibrant model structures,
namely, the
\omega
-model structures on weakly idempotent complete exact categories,
and the
\mathcal{W}
-model structures on weakly idempotent complete additive categories.
Their relations with exact model structures are discussed and various examples are illustrated.

1.1.
The homotopy category of a model structure

The homotopy category is a main object of study.
If
\mathcal{A}
is a pointed model category, by introducing the loop (suspension) functor and (co)fibration sequences,
D. Quillen has found that the homotopy category
\operatorname{Ho}(\mathcal{A})
satisfies the similar axioms of a triangulated category ([Q1, p. 3.10]).
In fact, even if
\mathcal{A}
is an additive model category,
\operatorname{Ho}(\mathcal{A})
is not triangulated, in general,
but pretriangulated ([H1, 6.5], [BR, II, 1.1]).
Anyway, the most known triangulated categories arise as
\operatorname{Ho}(\mathcal{A})
.

The following result will be proved in Section 3.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{Fib},\operatorname{Weq})
be a model structure on a weakly idempotent complete additive category
\mathcal{A}
.
Then the homotopy category
{\rm Ho}(\mathcal{A})
is equivalent as an additive category to the quotient
(\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{F})/(\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{W})
, where
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{W}
are respectively the class of cofibrant objects,
the class of fibrant objects, and the class of trivial objects.

This result is known for exact model structures,
see J. Gillespie
[
G2
, Proposition 4.4]
;
and for the
\omega
-model structures on weakly idempotent complete exact categories,
see A. Beligiannis and I. Reiten
[
BR
, VIII, Theorem 4.2]
and
[
CLZ
, Theorem 1.1]
.
Moreover, if the exact model structure is given by a hereditary Hovey triple on a weakly idempotent complete exact category,
then
\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{F}
is a Frobenius category with
\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{W}
as the class of
the projective-injective objects, see J. Št́ovíček
[
Š
, Theorem 6.21]
(also H. Becker
[
Bec
, Proposition 1.1.14]
).

Quillen has introduced (left, right) homotopy relation on pointed model category
\mathcal{M}

(thus,
\mathcal{M}
has limits and colimits).
This gives an equivalent relation on
\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{F}
,
and the corresponding quotient category is just
{\rm Ho}(\mathcal{M})
([Q1, p.1.13]), up to an equivalence of category.
However, different from the proofs of the known cases mentioned above, here we can not use the homotopy relation, since
the existence of push-outs and pull-backs is not guaranteed in a weakly idempotent complete additive category.
Instead, one of the key steps is to use the fact that a kind of localization categories can be described as
additive quotient categories. See Theorem
3.1
and Lemma
2.2
.

1.2.
Descriptions of fibrant model structures

Abelian model structures on abelian categories have been introduced by
M. Hovey, and this has been extended to exact model structures on exact categories by J. Gillespie.
The Hovey correspondence gives a way of constructing exact model structures via
two complete cotorsion pairs (
[
H2
, Theorem 2.2]
; [G3, 3.3];
[
Š
, 6.9]
).
Many model structures known in algebra are exact (abelian).
A. Beligiannis and I. Reiten construct a
\omega
-model structure on a weakly idempotent complete exact category
(
[
BR
, Theorem 4.6]
,
[
CLZ
, Theorem 1.3]
), which uses one hereditary complete cotorsion pair, but is not exact in general.

A category is
pointed
([Q1]) if it has a zero object.
An additive category is
weakly idempotent complete
,
provided that every splitting monomorphism has a cokernel,
or equivalently,
every splitting epimorphism has a kernel (see e.g.
[
Bü
, 7.1, 7.2]
).

Let
(\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{Fib},\operatorname{Weq})
be a model structure on pointed category
\mathcal{A}
. We use the following notations. Put

\operatorname{TCoFib}=\operatorname{CoFib}\cap\operatorname{Weq}
and
\operatorname{TFib}=\operatorname{Fib}\cap\operatorname{Weq}
. Denote by
\mathcal{C}
the class of cofibrant objects
X
, i.e.,

0\longrightarrow X
is in
\operatorname{CoFib}
,
\mathcal{F}
the class of fibrant objects
X
, i.e.,

X\longrightarrow 0
is in
\operatorname{Fib}
, and
\mathcal{W}
the class of trivial objects
X
, i.e.,

0\longrightarrow X
is in
\operatorname{Weq}
, or equivalently,
X\longrightarrow 0
is in
\operatorname{Weq}
.
Put

{\rm T}\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{W}
and
{\rm T}\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{W}
.
We will also use these notations

\operatorname{CoFib},\ \ \operatorname{Fib},\ \ \operatorname{Weq},\ \ \operatorname{TCoFib},\ \ \operatorname{TFib},\ \ \mathcal{C},\ \ \mathcal{F},\ \ \mathcal{W},\ \ {\rm T}\mathcal{C},\ \ {\rm T}\mathcal{F}

even if
(\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{Fib},\operatorname{Weq})
has not yet been known a model structure, but it will be a candidate for a model structure.

A model structure on a pointed category will be said to be
fibrant
,
if every object is a fibrant object. Fibrant model structures
on weakly idempotent complete additive categories, can be explicitly described by the trivial cofibrations, and also by the fibrations,
as the following result shows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{Fib},\operatorname{Weq})
be a model structure on weakly idempotent complete additive category
\mathcal{A}
.
Then the followings are equivalent.

(1)
It is a fibrant model structure.

(2)

\operatorname{TCoFib}=\{\text{splitting monomorphism}\ f\ |\ \operatorname{Coker}f\in{\rm T}\mathcal{C}\}
.

(3)

\operatorname{Fib}=\{\text{morphism}\ f\ |\ f\ \mbox{is}\ \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}({\rm T}\mathcal{C},-)\mbox{-epic}\}
.

If this is the case, then the homotopy category
\operatorname{Ho}(\mathcal{A})\cong\mathcal{C}/{\rm T}\mathcal{C}
, as additive categories.

1.3.
Fibrantly weak factorization systems and construction of fibrant model structures

Weak factorization systems have been studied in
[
AHRT
]
and
[
Š
]
. See Definition
2.9
.
To construct fibrant model structures, we introduce
fibrantly weak factorization systems.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a pointed category.
A pair
({\rm L},{\rm R})
of classes of morphisms is a fibrantly weak factorization system on
\mathcal{A}
, provided that the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

({\rm L},{\rm R})
is a weak factorization system
(
cf. Definition
2.9
)
.

(2)
For
f:A\longrightarrow B
and
g:B\longrightarrow C
, if
f,gf\in{\rm R}
, then
g\in{\rm R}
.

(3)
The class
\mathcal{L}\cap\mathcal{R}
of objects is contravariantly finite in
\mathcal{A}
,
where

\mathcal{L}=\{X\ |\ 0\longrightarrow X\ \mbox{is in}\ {\rm L}\},\ \ \ \ \mathcal{R}=\{X\ |\ X\longrightarrow 0\ \mbox{is in}\ {\rm R}\}.

Assume that
(\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{TFib})
is a fibrantly weak factorization system on
\mathcal{A}
. We stress that, although here we use the notations

\operatorname{CoFib}
and
\operatorname{TFib}
, however, at this moment they are not assumed to be from a model structure. Put

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle=\{\mbox{object}\ X\ |\ \mbox{morphism}\ 0\longrightarrow X\ \mbox{is in}\ \operatorname{CoFib}\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}

\displaystyle=\{\mbox{object}\ X\ |\ \mbox{morphism}\ X\longrightarrow 0\ \mbox{is in}\ \operatorname{TFib}\}

\displaystyle{\rm T}\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{W}

\displaystyle\operatorname{Fib}

\displaystyle=\{\text{morphism}\ f\ |\ f\ \mbox{is}\ \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}({\rm T}\mathcal{C},-)\mbox{-epic}\}

\displaystyle\operatorname{TCoFib}

\displaystyle=\{\text{splitting monomorphism}\ f\ |\ \operatorname{Coker}f\in{\rm T}\mathcal{C}\}

\displaystyle\operatorname{Weq}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{TFib}\circ\operatorname{TCoFib}.

For a class
\mathcal{U}
of objects of category
\mathcal{A}
,
a morphism
f
of
\mathcal{A}
is said to be
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{U},-)
-epic
,
provided that
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(U,f)
is epic for any
U\in\mathcal{U}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a weakly idempotent complete additive category.
If
(\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{Fib},\operatorname{Weq})
is a fibrant model structure on
\mathcal{A}
, then

(\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{TFib})
is a fibrantly weak factorization system on
\mathcal{A}
, and

\operatorname{Fib}
and
\operatorname{TCoFib}
are given as in
(1.1)
.

Conversely, if
(\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{TFib})
is a fibrantly weak factorization system on
\mathcal{A}
, then
(\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{Fib},\operatorname{Weq})
is a fibrant model structure on
\mathcal{A}
,
where
\operatorname{Fib}
and
\operatorname{Weq}
are given as in
(1.1)
.

The second part of Theorem
1.4
can be regarded as a constructive result,
in sense that any fibrant model structure on a weakly idempotent complete additive category, can be constructed
from a fibrantly weak factorization system, where
\operatorname{Fib}
and
\operatorname{TCoFib}
are explicitly given in
(1.1)
.
This is sharpen by the following one-one correspondence
between fibrantly weak factorization systems and fibrant model structures.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a weakly idempotent complete additive category,

\Omega
the class of fibrantly weak factorization systems on
\mathcal{A}
, and
\Gamma
the class of fibrant model structures on
\mathcal{A}
. Then the map

\Phi:\Omega\longrightarrow\Gamma\ \ \ \mbox{given by}\ \ \ (\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{TFib})\mapsto(\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{Fib},\operatorname{Weq})

where
\operatorname{Fib}
and
\operatorname{Weq}
are given as in
(1.1)
, and the map

\Psi:\Gamma\longrightarrow\Omega\ \ \ \mbox{given by}\ \ \ (\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{Fib},\operatorname{Weq})\mapsto(\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{TFib})

give a one-one correspondence between
\Omega
and
\Gamma
.

This construction of fibrant model structures is on weakly idempotent complete additive categories. Even on exact categories,
they are not exact model structures,
and
(\mathcal{C},{\rm T}\mathcal{F})
and
({\rm T}\mathcal{C},\mathcal{F})
are not cotorsion pairs, in general. See Example
5.6
.

2.2.
Additive quotient categories

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an additive category, and
\mathcal{U}
a full additive subcategory (i.e., a full subcategory closed under finite coproducts).
Quotient category
\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{U}
has the same objects as
\mathcal{A}
, and

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{U}}(X,Y)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(X,Y)/\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(X,\mathcal{U},Y)

where
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(X,\mathcal{U},Y)
is the subgroup
\{f\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(X,Y)\ |\ f\ \text{factors \ through\ object \ in \ }\mathcal{U}\}
.
Then

\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{U}
is an additive category with the canonical additive functor
P:\mathcal{A}\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{U}
,
which sends objects in
\mathcal{U}
to the zero object, and has the universal property with this respect. That is, If

Q:\mathcal{A}\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}
is an additive functor also sending objects in
\mathcal{U}
to the zero object, then
there exists a unique additive functor
\bar{Q}:\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{U}\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}
such that
Q=\bar{Q}\circ P
.
This amounts that
P
sends morphisms in
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(X,\mathcal{U},Y)
to zero morphisms, and has the universal property with this respect.
If
\mathcal{U}
is closed under direct summands, then object
X\cong 0
in
\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{U}
if and only if
X\in\mathcal{U}
.

2.3.
Localization of categories

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a class of morphisms of a category
\mathcal{A}
.
By definition the localization category
{\mathcal{A}}[\mathcal{S}^{-1}]
of
\mathcal{A}
with respect to
\mathcal{S}

is a category together with localization functor
L:\mathcal{A}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{A}}[\mathcal{S}^{-1}]
,
which sends morphisms in
\mathcal{S}
to isomorphisms, and has the universal property with this respect.

Recall the construction of
{\mathcal{A}}[\mathcal{S}^{-1}]
,
via the path category
{\rm Path}Q(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{S})
of quiver

given by P. Gabriel and M. Zisman
[
GZ
]
. See also H. Krause
[

]
.
Vertices of

are the objects of
\mathcal{A}
.
For two vertices

and

the set of arrows of

from

to

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(X,Y)\ \overset{\bullet}{\bigcup}\ \mathcal{S}(Y,X)

.

\mathcal{A}

Then

the equivalent relations generated by the obvious relations:

(1) For each object

, one has

where

is the morphism
of

, and

is the morphism given by the path of length

at
X
;

(2) For composable morphisms

and

\mathcal{A}
, one has

where

Thus, if

Definition 2.3
.

([Q1], [Q2]) A model structure on a category
\mathcal{M}
is a triple

(\operatorname{CoFib}
,
\operatorname{Fib}
,
\operatorname{Weq})
of classes of morphisms,
where the morphisms in the three classes are respectively called
cofibrations, fibrations, and weak equivalences

satisfying the following axioms:

Two out of three axiom:
Let

be morphisms in
\mathcal{M}

are weak equivalences,
then so is the third one.

Retract axiom:

\operatorname{CoFib}
,
\operatorname{Fib}
,
\operatorname{Weq}
are closed under retract.

Lifting axiom:
Let

and

. If either
f\in\operatorname{Weq}
or
g\in\operatorname{Weq}
, then
f\ \square\ g
.

Factorization axiom:
Any morphism

admits factorizations

and
f=qj
, where
i\in\operatorname{CoFib}\cap\operatorname{Weq}
,
p\in\operatorname{Fib}
,

, and

.

The morphisms in

2.5.
Exact model structures and the Hovey correspondence

A model structure on exact category
\mathcal{A}
is
exact
([G2]), if
cofibrations are precisely inflations with cofibrant cokernel, and fibrations are precisely
deflations with fibrant kernel. If this is the case, trivial cofibrations are precisely inflations with trivially cofibrant cokernel,
and trivial fibrations are precisely deflations with trivially fibrant kernel.
If
\mathcal{A}
is abelian, then this goes to the notion of
abelian model structure
([H2]).

A Hovey triple
(
[
H2
]
,
[
G2
]
) in exact category
\mathcal{A}
is a triple
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{W})
of classes of objects such that

\mathcal{W}
is
thick
in
\mathcal{A}
(i.e.,
\mathcal{W}
is closed under direct summands,
and if two out of the three terms in an admissible exact sequence are in
\mathcal{W}
, then so is the third one); and that
both
(\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{W},\ \mathcal{F})
and
(\mathcal{C},\ \mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{W})
are complete cotorsion pairs.

Theorem 2.8
.

(The Hovey correspondence) ([H2, Theorem 2.2]; [G2, 3.3];
[
Š
, 6.9]
)
Let
\mathcal{A}
be a weakly idempotent complete exact category.
Then there is a one-to-one correspondence between exact model structures and Hovey triples in
\mathcal{A}
, given by

({\rm CoFib},\ {\rm Fib},\ {\rm Weq})\mapsto(\mathcal{C},\ \mathcal{F},\ \mathcal{W})
,
with the inverse
(\mathcal{C},\ \mathcal{F},\ \mathcal{W})\mapsto({\rm CoFib},\ {\rm Fib},\ {\rm Weq}).

A Hovey triple
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{W})
is
hereditary
if both the cotorsion pairs

(\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{W},\ \mathcal{F})
and
(\mathcal{C},\ \mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{W})
are hereditary.
An advantage of a hereditary Hovey triple is that the homotopy category of the corresponding exact model structure
is just the stable category of a Frobenius category, and hence triangulated.
J. Gillespie
[
G3
, Theorem 1.1]
has
constructed all the hereditary Hovey triples in an abelian category via two compatible hereditary complete cotorsion pairs.

5.
Two applications

Applying Theorem
1.4
to two special cases, we rediscover
\omega
-model structures and
\mathcal{W}
-model structures.
Both of them are fibrant, and not exact in general. We will analyze their relationships with exact model structures.

5.1.
\omega
-model structures

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a weakly idempotent complete exact category,

\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
full additive subcategories which are closed under direct summands and isomorphisms,
and
\omega=\mathcal{X}\cap\mathcal{Y}
.
Put

(5.1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{CoFib}_{\omega}

\displaystyle=\{\text{inflation}\ f\ |\ \operatorname{Coker}f\in\mathcal{X}\}

\displaystyle\operatorname{Fib}_{\omega}

\displaystyle=\{\text{morphism}\ f\ |\ f\ \mbox{is}\ \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(\omega,-)\mbox{-epic}\}

\displaystyle\operatorname{TCoFib}_{\omega}

\displaystyle=\{\text{splitting monomorphism}\ f\ |\ \operatorname{Coker}f\in\omega\}

\displaystyle\operatorname{TFib}_{\omega}

\displaystyle=\{\text{deflation}\ f\ |\ \operatorname{Ker}f\in\mathcal{Y}\}

\displaystyle\operatorname{Weq}_{\omega}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{TFib}_{\omega}\circ\operatorname{TCoFib}_{\omega}.

Theorem 5.1
.

With the same notations as above, then
({\rm CoFib}_{\omega},\ {\rm Fib}_{\omega},\ {\rm Weq}_{\omega})
given in
(5.1)
is a model structure with

\operatorname{TCoFib}_{\omega}=\operatorname{CoFib}_{\omega}\cap\operatorname{Weq}_{\omega}\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \operatorname{TFib}_{\omega}=\operatorname{Fib}_{\omega}\cap\operatorname{Weq}_{\omega}

if and only if

(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
is a hereditary complete cotorsion pair in
\mathcal{A}
, and
\omega
is contravariantly finite in
\mathcal{A}
.
If this is the case, then the class of cofibrant objects is
\mathcal{X},
the class of fibrant objects is
\mathcal{A},

and the class of trivial objects is
\mathcal{Y};
and
{\rm Ho}(\mathcal{A})\cong\mathcal{X}/\omega
as additive categories.

Theorem
5.1
is initiated by A. Beligiannis and I. Reiten [BR, VIII, Theorem 4.2] on abelian categories.
It was called
an
\omega
-model structure
. The present form is given in [CLZ].
Theorem
1.4
gives a shorter proof of the “if” part of Theorem
5.1
:
One can verifies that
(\operatorname{CoFib}_{\omega},\operatorname{TFib}_{\omega})
is a fibrantly weak factorization system on
\mathcal{A}
,
and then
({\rm CoFib}_{\omega},\ {\rm Fib}_{\omega},\ {\rm Weq}_{\omega})
is a fibrant model structure on
\mathcal{A}
, by Theorem
1.4
;
also,
{\rm Ho}(\mathcal{A})\cong\mathcal{X}/\omega
as additive categories, by Theorem
1.1
. For a complete proof we refer to
[
CLZ
, Theorem 1.1]
.

A fibrant model structure on an exact category is
weakly projective
if
cofibrations are exactly inflations with cofibrant cokernel, and each trivial fibration is a deflation; or equivalently,
trivial fibrations are exactly deflations with trivially fibrant kernel, and each cofibration is an inflation.
This is also equivalent to say that it is a fibrant model structure such that
(\mathcal{C},{\rm T}\mathcal{F})
is a complete cotorsion pair,
where
\mathcal{C}
is the class of cofibrant objects, and
\rm T\mathcal{F}
is the class of trivially fibrant objects.
See
[
CLZ
, Proposition 5.2]
.

As in abelian categories (
[
BR
, VIII, Theorem 4.6]
),

\omega
-model structures are precisely weakly projective model structures,
on a weakly idempotent complete exact category, as shown by the following correspondence of Beligiannis and Reiten.

Theorem 5.2
.

([CLZ, Theorem 1.3])
Let
\mathcal{A}
be a weakly idempotent complete exact category.
Then there is a one-to-one correspondence between hereditary complete cotorsion pairs with kernel
\omega
contravariantly finite in
\mathcal{A}
,
and weakly projective model structures on
\mathcal{A}
, given by

(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\mapsto(\operatorname{CoFib}_{\omega},\operatorname{Fib}_{\omega},\operatorname{Weq}_{\omega})
, with the inverse

For a class

\displaystyle=\{\text{morphism}\ f\ |\ f\ \mbox{is}\ \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{W},-)\mbox{-epic}\}

\displaystyle=\{\text{splitting monomorphism}\ f\ |\ \operatorname{Coker}f\in\mathcal{W}\}

\displaystyle=\{\text{splitting epimorphism}\ f\ |\ \operatorname{Ker}f\in\mathcal{W}\}

After we got Theorems
5.3
and Corollary

, we found that they have been discovered by T. Pirashvili
[

]
for abelian categories,
and by A. Beligiannis in
[

]
for idempotent complete additive categories.
Recall that an additive category is
idempotent complete
, if each idempotent morphism has a kernel.
Clearly, an idempotent complete additive category is weakly idempotent complete, but the converse is not true. See [Bü, Exercise 7.11].

5.3.
The relationships

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an exact category.
An exact model structure is
projective
(respectively,
injective
), if each object is fibrant (respectively, cofibrant).
An exact model structure on
\mathcal{A}
is projective (respectively, injective) if and only if

\mathcal{A}
has enough projective (respectively, injective) objects and
trivially cofibrant (respectively, trivially fibrant) objects coincide with projective (respectively, injective) objects. See [G2, 4.6].

An exact category is
Frobenius
, if it has enough projective objects and enough injective objects, and
the projective objects coincide with the injective objects.

An exact model structure on
\mathcal{A}
is
Frobenius
, if it is both projective and injective.
Thus, an exact model structure on
\mathcal{A}
is Frobenius if and only if

\mathcal{A}
is a Frobenius category and trivial objects coincide with the projective-injective objects of
\mathcal{A}
. See [G2, 4.7].

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a weakly idempotent complete exact category.

(1)
An
\omega
-model structure on
\mathcal{A}
is exact
if and only if
\mathcal{A}
has enough projective objects and
\omega=\mathcal{P}
, the class of projective objects of
\mathcal{A};

and if and only if this
\omega
-model structure is projective.

If this is the case, then

\displaystyle\operatorname{CoFib}_{\omega}=\{\text{inflation}\ f\ |\ \operatorname{Coker}f\in\mathcal{X}\},\ \ \ \ \ \ \operatorname{Fib}_{\omega}=\{\text{deflation}\}

\displaystyle\operatorname{TCoFib}_{\omega}=\{\text{splitting monomorphism}\ f\ |\ \operatorname{Coker}f\in\mathcal{P}\}

\displaystyle\operatorname{TFib}_{\omega}=\{\text{deflation}\ f\ |\ \operatorname{Ker}f\in\mathcal{Y}\},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \operatorname{Weq}_{\omega}=\operatorname{TFib}_{\omega}\circ\operatorname{TCoFib}_{\omega}.

(2)
A
\mathcal{W}
-model structure on
\mathcal{A}
is an
\omega
-model structure if and only if

\mathcal{A}
has enough injective objects and
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{I}
, the class of injective objects of
\mathcal{A}
.

If this is the case, then
\omega=\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{I}
is contravariantly finite in
\mathcal{A}
and

\displaystyle\operatorname{CoFib}_{\mathcal{W}}=\{\text{inflation}\},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \operatorname{Fib}_{\mathcal{W}}=\{f\ |\ f\ \mbox{is}\ \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{I},-)\mbox{-epic}\}

\displaystyle\operatorname{TCoFib}_{\mathcal{W}}=\{\text{splitting monomorphism}\ f\ |\ \operatorname{Coker}f\in\mathcal{I}\}

\displaystyle\operatorname{TFib}_{\mathcal{W}}=\{\text{splitting epimorphism}\ f\ |\ \operatorname{Ker}f\in\mathcal{I}\},\ \ \ \ \ \operatorname{Weq}_{\mathcal{W}}=\operatorname{TFib}_{\omega}\circ\operatorname{TCoFib}_{\omega}.

(3)
Let
(\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{Fib},\operatorname{Weq})
be a model structure on
\mathcal{A}
. Then the following are equivalent
:

{\rm(i)}

(\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{Fib},\operatorname{Weq})
is both an exact model structure and a
\mathcal{W}
-model structure
;

{\rm(ii)}

(\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{Fib},\operatorname{Weq})
is a Frobenius model structure
;

{\rm(iii)}

(\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{Fib},\operatorname{Weq})
is simultaneously an exact model structure, an
\omega
-model structure, and a
\mathcal{W}
-model structure.

If this the case, then
\omega=\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{I}
, and

\operatorname{CoFib}=\{\mbox{inflation}\},\ \ \ \operatorname{Fib}=\{\mbox{deflation}\},\ \ \ {\rm Weq}=\{\ f\ |\ \bar{f}\ \mbox{is an isomorphism in}\ \mathcal{A}/\mathcal{P}\}.

Proof.

(1)
If
\omega
-model structure
(\operatorname{CoFib}_{\omega},\operatorname{Fib}_{\omega},\operatorname{Weq}_{\omega})
is exact,
then by the Hovey correspondence
(\mathcal{X},\ \mathcal{A},\ \mathcal{Y})
is a Hovey triple,
thus
(\omega,\mathcal{A})
is a complete cotorsion pair, and hence
\mathcal{A}
has enough projective objects and
\omega=\mathcal{P}
.

Conversely, assume that
\mathcal{A}
has enough projective objects and
\omega=\mathcal{P}
. For any
f\in\operatorname{Fib}_{\omega}
, by definition
f:A\longrightarrow B
is
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(\omega,-)
-epic.
Taking a deflation
g:P\longrightarrow B
with
P\in\mathcal{P}
, then

g=fh
for some
h:P\longrightarrow A
. Since
\mathcal{A}
is weakly idempotent complete,
f
is a deflation. Thus

{\rm Fib}_{\omega}=\{f\ |\ f\ \mbox{is}\ \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(\omega,-)\mbox{-epic}\}=\{\text{deflation}\}.

Together with
\operatorname{CoFib}_{\omega}=\{\text{inflation}\ f\ |\ \operatorname{Coker}f\in\mathcal{X}\}
, by definition
(\operatorname{CoFib}_{\omega},\operatorname{Fib}_{\omega},\operatorname{Weq}_{\omega})
is exact.

(2)
If a
\mathcal{W}
-model structure is an
\omega
-model structure,
then
(\mathcal{C},{\rm T}\mathcal{F})=(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{W})
is a complete cotorsion pair, it follows that

\mathcal{A}
has enough injective objects and
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{I}
.

Conversely, assume that
\mathcal{A}
has enough injective objects and
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{I}
.
For any
f\in{\rm\operatorname{CoFib}}_{\mathcal{W}}
, by definition
f:A\longrightarrow B
is
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(-,\mathcal{I})\mbox{-epic}
.
Taking an inflation
g:A\longrightarrow I
with
I\in\mathcal{I}
, then

g=hf
for some
h:B\longrightarrow I
. Since
\mathcal{A}
is weakly idempotent complete,
f
is an inflation. Thus

{\rm CoFib}_{\mathcal{W}}=\{f\ |\ f\ \mbox{is}\ \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(-,\mathcal{I})\mbox{-epic}\}=\{\text{inflation}\}.

Since
\operatorname{TCoFib}_{\mathcal{W}}=\{\text{splitting monomorphism}\ f\ |\ \operatorname{Coker}f\in\mathcal{I}\}
, each trivial fibration is a deflation.
By definition,
(\operatorname{CoFib}_{\mathcal{W}},\operatorname{Fib}_{\mathcal{W}},\operatorname{Weq}_{\mathcal{W}})
is a weakly projective model structure, and hence an
\omega
-model structure.

(3)

{\rm(i)}\Longrightarrow{\rm(ii)}:
Since
\mathcal{W}
-model structures are exactly bifibrant model structures, this implication follows from the definition
of a Frobenius model structure.

{\rm(ii)}\Longrightarrow{\rm(iii)}:
If
(\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{Fib},\operatorname{Weq})
is a Frobenius model structure,
then it is a
\mathcal{W}
-model structure,
\mathcal{A}
has enough injective objects and
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{I}
.
By
(2)
, it is an
\omega
-model structure with
\omega=\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{I}.

{\rm(iii)}\Longrightarrow{\rm(i)}:
This is trivial.
∎

A model structure on pointed category
\mathcal{A}
is
non-trivial
, if
\operatorname{Ho}(\mathcal{A})\neq 0
.
We only interested in non-trivial model structures, and all examples of model structures below are non-trivial.

Examples 5.6
.

(1)
There are many examples of exact model structures which are also
\omega
-model structure. For example,
let
\mathcal{A}
be an abelian category with enough projective objects, and
{\rm Ch}^{-}(\mathcal{A})
the category of right bounded complexes of objects of
\mathcal{A}
.
Then
(\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{Fib},\operatorname{Weq})
is an abelian model structure on
{\rm Ch}^{-}(\mathcal{A})
,
where

\operatorname{CoFib}=\{\mbox{monomorphism}\ f\ |\ \operatorname{Coker}f\ \mbox{is a complex of projective objects}\}

\operatorname{Fib}=\{\mbox{epimorphism}\},\ \ \ \ \ \ \operatorname{Weq}=\{\mbox{quasi-isomorphism}\}.

See [Q1, page 1.2]. The class of cofibrant (fibrant, trivial, respectively) objects is given by

\mathcal{C}=\{\mbox{complex of projective objects}\},\ \ \ \mathcal{F}={\rm Ch}^{-}(\mathcal{A}),\ \ \ \mathcal{W}=\{\mbox{acyclic complex}\}.

By definition, it is a weakly projective model structure, and hence an
\omega
-model structure, by Theorem
5.2
. But it is not a
\mathcal{W}
-model structure.

In fact,
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{W})
is a hereditary and complete cotorsion pair in
{\rm Ch}^{-}(\mathcal{A})
, and

\omega:=\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{W}
is exactly the class of projective objects of
{\rm Ch}^{-}(\mathcal{A})
. See [EJX] and [G1].

(2)
Let
A
be an artin algebra,
A\mbox{-mod}
the category of finitely generated
A
-modules, and

\mathcal{I}
the full subcategory of injective modules.
Then
\mathcal{I}
is functorially finite in
A\mbox{-mod}
, and
(\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{Fib},\operatorname{Weq})
is both an
\omega
-model structure and a
\mathcal{W}
-model structure on
A\mbox{-mod}
,
where

\displaystyle{\rm\operatorname{CoFib}}=\{\text{monomorphism}\},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \operatorname{Fib}=\{\text{morphism}\ f\ |\ f\ \mbox{is}\ \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{I},-)\mbox{-epic}\}

\displaystyle\operatorname{TCoFib}=\{\text{splitting monomorphism}\ f\ |\ \operatorname{Coker}f\in\mathcal{I}\}

\displaystyle\operatorname{TFib}=\{\text{splitting epimorphism}\ f\ |\ \operatorname{Ker}f\in\mathcal{I}\},\ \ \ \ \ \ \operatorname{Weq}=\operatorname{TFib}\circ\operatorname{TCoFib}.

Cofibrant objects, fibrant objects, and trivial objects are respectively given by

\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{F}=A\mbox{-mod},\ \ \ \ \mathcal{W}=\mathcal{I}.

If
A
is not self-injective, then
(\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{Fib},\operatorname{Weq})
is not an abelian model structure since
(\mathcal{I},A\mbox{-mod})
is not a cotorsion pair.

If
A
is self-injective, then
(\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{Fib},\operatorname{Weq})
is a Frobenius model structure, i.e.,
it is simultaneously an
\omega
-model structure, a
\mathcal{W}
-model structure, and an abelian model structure.

(3)
Let
R
be a ring, and
{\rm Ch}(R)
the complex category of
R
-modules.
A complex
I
is dg
-injective
, if each
I^{n}
is an injective
R
-module and
{\rm Hom}^{\bullet}(E,I)
is acyclic for any acyclic complex
E
([AF]).
Denote by
{\rm dg}\mathcal{I}
the class of dg-injective complexes.
Then
(\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{Fib},\operatorname{Weq})
is an abelian model structure on
{\rm Ch}(R)
,
where

\operatorname{CoFib}=\{\mbox{monomorphism}\},\ \ \operatorname{Fib}=\{\mbox{epimorphism}\ f\ |\ \operatorname{Ker}f\in{\rm dg}\mathcal{I}\},\ \ \operatorname{Weq}=\{\mbox{quasi-isomorphism}\}.

See
[
H1
, Theorem 2.3.13]
. Cofibrant objects, fibrant objects, and trivial objects, are respectively given by

\mathcal{C}={\rm Ch}(R),\ \ \ \mathcal{F}={\rm dg}\mathcal{I},\ \ \ \mathcal{W}=\{\mbox{acyclic complex}\}.

Thus, it is not a fibrant model structure, and hence it is neither an
\omega
-model structure nor a
\mathcal{W}
-model structure.

(4)
Let
A
be an artin algebra.
For a module
M
, let
{\rm add}M
be the set of modules which are summands of finite direct sums of copies of
M
,
and
\widetilde{{\rm add}M}
the set of modules
X
such that there is an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow X\longrightarrow M^{0}\longrightarrow\ldots\longrightarrow M^{s}\longrightarrow 0

with each
M^{i}\in{\rm add}M
.

Let
T
be a tilting module, i.e.,
{\rm proj.dim.}T<\infty
,
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i}(T,T)=0
for
i\geq 1
, and
A\in\widetilde{{\rm add}T}
.
By
[
BR
, VIII, 4.13]
,
(\widetilde{{\rm add}T},\ T^{\perp_{\geq 1}})
is a hereditary complete cotorsion pair in
A\mbox{-mod}
with
\omega:=\widetilde{{\rm add}T}\cap T^{\perp_{\geq 1}}={\rm add}T
contravariantly finite in
A\mbox{-mod}
, where
T^{\perp_{\geq 1}}=\{M\in A\mbox{-mod}\ |\ \operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(T,M)=0,\ \forall\ i\geq 1\}
.
Then
(\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{Fib},\operatorname{Weq})
is an
\omega
-model structure on
A\mbox{-mod}
, where

\displaystyle{\rm\operatorname{CoFib}}=\{\text{inflation}\ f\ |\ \operatorname{Coker}f\in\widetilde{{\rm add}T}\},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \operatorname{Fib}=\{f\ |\ f\ \mbox{is}\ \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}({\rm add}T,-)\mbox{-epic}\}

\displaystyle\operatorname{TCoFib}=\{\text{splitting monomorphism}\ f\ |\ \operatorname{Coker}f\in{\rm add}T\}

\displaystyle\operatorname{TFib}=\{\text{deflation}\ f\ |\ \operatorname{Ker}f\in T^{\perp_{\geq 1}}\},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \operatorname{Weq}=\operatorname{TFib}\circ\operatorname{TCoFib}.

Cofibrant objects, fibrant objects, and trivial objects are respectively given by

\mathcal{C}=\widetilde{{\rm add}T},\ \ \ \ \mathcal{F}=A\mbox{-mod},\ \ \ \ \mathcal{W}=T^{\perp_{\geq 1}}.

Now, assume that
T
is not projective. Since
({\rm add}T,A\mbox{-mod})
is not a cotorsion pair in
A\mbox{-mod}
,

(\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{Fib},\operatorname{Weq})
is not an exact model structure.

Assume in addition that

\widetilde{{\rm add}T}\neq A\mbox{-mod}
, and hence
(\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{Fib},\operatorname{Weq})
is not a

-model structure.

(5)
Let
A

.

,

6.
The dual version: cofibrant model structures

We state the following dual version without proofs. A model structure on a pointed category is called
cofibrant
,
if every object is a cofibrant object.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
(\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{Fib},\operatorname{Weq})
be a model structure on weakly idempotent complete additive category
\mathcal{A}
.
Then the followings are equivalent.

(1)

(\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{Fib},\operatorname{Weq})
is a cofibrant model structure.

(2)

\operatorname{TFib}=\{\text{splitting epimorphism}\ f\ |\ \operatorname{Ker}f\in{\rm T}\mathcal{F}\}
.

(3)

\operatorname{CoFib}=\{\text{morphism}\ f\ |\ f\ \mbox{is}\ \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(-,{\rm T}\mathcal{F})\mbox{-epic}\}
.

If this is the case, then
\operatorname{Ho}(\mathcal{A})\cong\mathcal{F}/{\rm T}\mathcal{F}
, as additive categories.

To construct cofibrant model structure, we need

Definition 6.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a pointed category.
A pair
({\rm L},{\rm R})
of classes of morphisms is a cofibrantly weak factorization system on
\mathcal{A}
, provided that the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

({\rm L},{\rm R})
is a weak factorization system.

(2)
For
f:A\longrightarrow B
and
g:B\longrightarrow C
, if
g,gf\in{\rm L}
, then
f\in{\rm L}
.

(3)
The class
\mathcal{L}\cap\mathcal{R}
of objects is covariantly finite in
\mathcal{A}
,
where

\mathcal{L}=\{X\ |\ 0\longrightarrow X\ \mbox{is in}\ {\rm L}\},\ \ \ \ \mathcal{R}=\{X\ |\ X\longrightarrow 0\ \mbox{is in}\ {\rm R}\}.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
(\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{Fib},\operatorname{Weq})
be a cofibrant model structure. Then both
(\operatorname{TCoFib},\operatorname{Fib})
and
(\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{TFib})
are cofibrantly weak factorization systems.

Assume that
(\operatorname{TCoFib},\operatorname{Fib})
is a cofibrantly weak factorization system on
\mathcal{A}
. Put

(6.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle=\{\mbox{object}\ X\ |\ \mbox{morphism}\ X\longrightarrow 0\ \mbox{is in}\ \operatorname{Fib}\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}

\displaystyle=\{\mbox{object}\ X\ |\ \mbox{morphism}\ 0\longrightarrow X\ \mbox{is in}\ \operatorname{TCoFib}\}

\displaystyle{\rm T}\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{W}

\displaystyle\operatorname{CoFib}

\displaystyle=\{\text{morphism}\ f\ |\ f\ \mbox{is}\ \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(-,{\rm T}\mathcal{F})\mbox{-epic}\}

\displaystyle\operatorname{TFib}

\displaystyle=\{\text{splitting epimorphism}\ f\ |\ \operatorname{Ker}f\in{\rm T}\mathcal{F}\}

\displaystyle\operatorname{Weq}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{TFib}\circ\operatorname{TCoFib}.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a weakly idempotent complete additive category.
If
(\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{Fib},\operatorname{Weq})
is a cofibrant model structure on
\mathcal{A}
, then

(\operatorname{TCoFib},\operatorname{Fib})
is a cofibrantly weak factorization system on
\mathcal{A}
, and

\operatorname{CoFib}
and
\operatorname{TFib}
are given as in
(6.1)
.

Conversely, if
(\operatorname{TCoFib},\operatorname{Fib})
is a cofibrantly weak factorization system on
\mathcal{A}
, then
(\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{Fib},

\operatorname{Weq})
is a cofibrant model structure on
\mathcal{A}
,
where
\operatorname{CoFib}
and
\operatorname{Weq}
are given as in
(6.1)
.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a weakly idempotent complete additive category,
\Omega
the class of cofibrantly weak factorization systems on
\mathcal{A}
,
and
\Gamma
the class of cofibrant model structures on
\mathcal{A}
. Then the map

\Phi:\Omega\longrightarrow\Gamma\ \ \ \mbox{given by}\ \ \ (\operatorname{TCoFib},\operatorname{Fib})\mapsto(\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{Fib},\operatorname{Weq})

where
\operatorname{CoFib}
and
\operatorname{Weq}
are given as in
(6.1)
, and the map

\Psi:\Gamma\longrightarrow\Omega\ \ \ \mbox{given by}\ \ \ (\operatorname{CoFib},\operatorname{Fib},\operatorname{Weq})\mapsto(\operatorname{TCoFib},\operatorname{Fib})

give a one-one correspondence between
\Omega
and
\Gamma
.

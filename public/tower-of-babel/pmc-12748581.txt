Soliton dynamics of the fourth-order nonlinear Boussinesq equation arising in shallow-water via advanced analytical techniques
Authors: Sandeep Malik, Salah Mahmoud Boulaaras, Abdallah M Talafha, Fatma Nur Kaya Sağlam
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper focuses on the fourth-order nonlinear Boussinesq equation (FONBE), which describes the interaction mechanisms of solutions in shallow-water waves. The FONBE has broad applications across various fields of physics and engineering, including heat transfer, fluid convection, and modeling of underwater volcanic activity, ocean currents, seafloor movements, and other hydrothermal processes. The Kumar-Malik method (KMM) and the modified exponential function method (MEFM) are employed to derive different types of soliton solutions. Analytical forms expressed through Jacobi elliptic, hyperbolic, trigonometric, exponential, and rational functions are obtained. Graphical representations further support the analytical findings, providing a clear understanding of the solution behaviors. These results highlight the effectiveness of the proposed methodology in addressing nonlinear challenges in mathematics and engineering, offering improvements over previous studies. The applied techniques are robust, efficient, and adaptable to a wide class of nonlinear partial differential equations. The novelty of this work lies in the generation of several new soliton solutions through the application of analytical approaches that have not been previously applied to this equation.Keywords: Mathematical model, Fourth-order nonlinear Boussinesq equation, Soliton solutions, Analytical methods, Kumar-Malik method, Modified exponential function methodSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

Nonlinear partial differential equations (NLPDEs) are essential mathematical models found in a wide range of fields, such as physics, engineering, biology, economics, and others1–10 . These equations offer a more accurate representation of various real-world phenomena. Finding exact solutions to NLPDEs is crucial for practical applications and remains a key area of research for mathematicians, physicists, and engineers. Some standard models related to NLPDEs include the Navier–Stokes equations for fluid dynamics11 , the Hamilton–Jacobi equation used in optimal control theory, geometric optics, and dynamic games12 , the Korteweg–de Vries equation describing shallow water waves13 , and the Boussinesq equation, which is applied in fluid mechanics and wave propagation14 .

Due to the nonlinear nature of these equations, obtaining analytical solutions is often a challenging task. To tackle this, several methods have been developed, such as the improved modified extended tanh-expansion method15 , Hirota’s bilinear method16 , the new extended direct algebraic method17 , the first-integral method18,19 , the modified F-expansion method20 , the generalized unified method21 , the tanh-coth method22,23 , the new Kudryashov method24, the Riccati–Bernoulli sub-ODE technique25 , the modified Khater’s method26, the generalized projective Riccati equation method27, the -expansion method28, and the generalized exponential rational function method29 .

In fluid mechanics and hydrodynamics, the Boussinesq equation is a common form of NLPDEs. It is beneficial to explain that heat transfers through fluids and is influenced by convection behavior. It is also frequently used to represent hydrothermal processes such as seafloor motions, ocean currents, and undersea volcanoes. First, Wazwaz and Kaur studied this completely integrable Eq. The main motivation behind this study is the need for analytical methods that can solve the FONBE, which is essential for modeling complex nonlinear behaviors and shallow-water wave interactions. Several exact soliton solutions that capture the complicated structure of nonlinear dispersive waves have been obtained by using two robust analytical techniques: the KMM and MEFM. Compared to traditional analytical or numerical methods, which frequently fall incapable of revealing the full range of soliton frameworks and their physical characteristics, this hybrid methodological framework offers more flexibility and novelty. Handling the nonlinear and higher-order structure of the FONBE is one of the main issues this study tackles, and the suggested approaches show definite benefits in resolving these kinds of problems. This study is original because it is the first to apply the KMM and MEFM to the FONBE, leading to new and physically meaningful solutions.

Comparisons

This section takes into account several related studies to situate the current work within the framework of previous research. Using different analytical frameworks, researchers have studied Boussinesq-type equations in various forms. To investigate the Lie symmetries and associated vector fields of the tackled equation, Kumar et al. used the Lie symmetry approach38 . Wazwaz and Kaur used the Painlevé test to examine the full integrability of the governing equation and the simplified Hirota method, as well as exponential expansion methods to identify multiple soliton solutions45 . Farooq et al. used the three different methods to derive the soliton solutions of the considered model48 . Kumari et al. derived several exact solutions of the FONBE by symbolic computations49 . In contrast, the current study uses a novel combination of the MEFM and the KMM to study the FONBE. For the first time, this combined framework is applied to this equation, yielding a broader class of analytical solutions, including trigonometric, exponential, Jacobian elliptic, hyperbolic, and rational forms. These results have not been documented previously for the FONBE. By demonstrating the adaptability of the suggested approaches for sophisticated nonlinear systems, the obtained results not only build on those of earlier studies but also offer a deeper understanding of the nonlinear behavior of the FONBE.

Conclusion

In this study, several exact analytical soliton solutions of the FONBE were obtained using the KMM and the MEFM. These analytical solutions were expressed in Jacobi elliptic, trigonometric, hyperbolic, exponential, and rational forms, each corresponding to distinct physical wave structures. The reliability of these results was verified both analytically and graphically, confirming that the proposed methods yield consistent and precise outcomes. The obtained bright soliton solutions describe localized elevations of the wave profile that maintain their shape and amplitude during propagation, reflecting the stability and self-preserving nature of nonlinear solitary waves. In contrast, the dark soliton solutions represent localized depressions on the wave surface, typically forming when dispersion dominates over nonlinearity. The periodic and Jacobi elliptic solutions exhibit oscillatory behaviors and repeating wave trains, indicating a dynamic balance between nonlinear and dispersive effects. These various soliton types provide a comprehensive understanding of how wave patterns evolve under different parametric regimes. The parameters a, b, and c were found to play a crucial role in determining the qualitative nature of the solutions. Specifically, a influences the strength of the nonlinearity, b governs the dispersion, and c contributes to dissipation or energy loss mechanisms. Variations in these parameters affect the amplitude, velocity, and localization of soliton structures, leading to transitions between bright, dark, and periodic behaviors. These parametric relationships emphasize the sensitivity of nonlinear wave systems to small changes in governing coefficients, which can be crucial in predicting wave stability and energy propagation. The combination of the KMM and MEFM has proven to be a powerful analytical framework for deriving multiple families of soliton solutions. Compared to traditional analytical or numerical techniques, these methods require fewer assumptions, provide closed-form solutions, and allow direct control over the functional parameters that define solution types. Their complementary strengths make them adaptable to a wide range of nonlinear problems, demonstrating strong potential for further analytical generalization. Beyond the direct results, the developed approaches can be employed to analyze other nonlinear partial differential equations with higher-order or variable coefficients. The symbolic structure of the proposed framework can also be integrated with computational and machine learning-based optimization schemes to systematically identify new classes of soliton solutions of nonlinear wave solutions. In future work, the methodologies presented here can be extended to higher-dimensional and stochastic versions of nonlinear evolution equations, paving the way for a deeper exploration of soliton dynamics in complex physical systems.

Targeting autophagy in HCC treatment: exploiting the CD147 internalization pathway
Authors: Meirui Qian, Ziyu Wan, Xue Liang, Lin Jing, Huijie Zhang, Heyao Qin, Wenli Duan, Ruo Chen, Tianjiao Zhang, Qian He, Meng Lu, Jianli Jiang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground/AimsChemotherapy resistance in liver cancer is a major clinical issue, with CD147 playing a vital role in this process. However, the specific mechanisms underlying these processes remain largely unknown. This study investigates how CD147 internalization leads to cytoprotective autophagy, contributing to chemotherapy resistance in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC).MethodsUtilizing bioinformatics methods for KEGG pathways enrichment and screening key molecules associated with chemotherapy resistance through analyses of GEO and TCGA databases. An overexpression/knockdown system was used to study how CD147 internalization leads to autophagy in vitro and in vivo. The process was observed using microscopes, and molecular interactions and autophagy flux were analyzed. Analyzing the internalization of CD147 intracellular domains and the interaction with G3BP1 in clinical chemotherapy recurrence HCC tissues by immunohistochemistry, tissue immunofluorescence, and mass spectrometry. A tumor xenograft mice model was used to study cytoprotective autophagy induced by CD147 and test the effectiveness of combining cisplatin with an autophagy inhibitor in nude mice models.ResultsIn our study, we identified the tumor-associated membrane protein CD147, which implicated in chemoresistance lysosome pathways, by evaluating its protein degree value and betweenness centrality using Cytoscape. Our findings revealed that CD147 undergoes internalization and interacts with G3BP1 following treatment with cisplatin and methyl-β-cyclodextrin, forming a complex that is transported to lysosomes via Rab7A. Notably, higher doses of cisplatin enhanced CD147-mediated lysosomal transport while concurrently inhibiting SG assembly. The CD147-G3BP1 complex additionally inhibits mTOR activity, promoting autophagy and augmenting chemoresistance in hepatoma cells. In vivo studies investigations and analyses of clinical samples revealed that elevated internalization of CD147 is associated with chemotherapy recurrence in liver cancer and the maintenance of stem cells. Mice experiments found that the combined administration of cisplatin and hydroxychloroquine enhanced the efficacy of treatment.ConclusionsThis study reveals that CD147 internalization and CD147-G3BP1 complex translocation to lysosomes induce cytoprotective autophagy, reducing chemotherapy sensitivity by suppressing mTOR activity. It is also shown that chemotherapy drugs combined with autophagy inhibitors can improve the therapeutic effect of cancer, providing new insights into potential targeted therapeutic approaches in treating HCC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12964-024-01956-5.Keywords: CD147, Chemoresistance, Cytoprotective autophagy, G3BP1, SG

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the second most common cause of cancer-related death in China [1]. Resistance to platinum chemotherapy is a major problem in clinical treatment. The main mechanisms of platinum resistance in tumor cells include the following four aspects. First, the accumulation of deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) adducts is reduced [2]. Second, increased DNA damage tolerance and damage recognition defects lead to increased tolerance of the body to platinum–DNA complexes [3]. Third, apoptosis-related pathways are inhibited [4]. Finally, cytoprotective autophagy is induced [5, 6]. Recently, various studies have shown that autophagy is a key factor in tumor cell death and chemoresistance.

Autophagy (type II programmed cell death) plays a pivotal role in the different stages of cancer progression. Autophagy inhibits tumor progression in the early stages. As the tumor progresses to an advanced stage, accompanied by alterations in the tumor microenvironment (TME), autophagy—a cellular process responsible for protein degradation and recycling—facilitates the transport of damaged proteins and organelles to lysosomes for degradation via vesicular transport. This process supplies biological energy to the tumor, thereby promoting its proliferation and migration [7]. Hence, autophagy activation can be detected in colorectal cancer tissues of patients who experience recurrence after chemotherapy [8]. The mechanistic target of rapamycin complex 1 (mTORC1) is a vital complex, that includes mTOR, scaffold protein Raptor, and mLST8, which can inactivate the formed autophagy regulatory complex through phosphorylation, thereby inhibiting autophagy initiation [9]. The inhibition of mTORC1 can promote the activity of autophagy-related protein 13 (ATG13) and serine/threonine-protein kinase (ULK1), thereby initiating autophagy to promote the cytosolic macromolecules into autophagosomes fusing with lysosomes for degradation [10, 11]. Lysosomes, as the regulator "platform" for mTORC1, can recruit and activate mTORC1 to promote cell growth under the stimulation of nutrients and growth factors [12].

Cluster of differentiation 147 (CD147), also known as extracellular matrix metalloproteinase inducer (EMMPRIN) or basigin, is highly expressed on the surface of tumor cells from various epithelial sources, such as the liver, colon, and lung cancer [13]. Transmembrane CD147 is involved in neoplasm progression, metabolic reprogramming, epithelial-mesenchymal transformation (EMT), and chemotherapy resistance [14–16]. Our previous results showed that F-box protein 22 (FBXO22) mediates the polyubiquitination and degradation of CD147 by interacting with the CD147 intracellular domain, thereby reversing cisplatin resistance in tumor cells [17]. In addition, we demonstrated that CD147 undergoes an intramembranous cleavage by metalloproteinase domain-containing protein 10 (ADAM10) or γ-secretase to release its residual intracellular domain with the transmembrane region (△ECDs) and intracellular domains (ICDs), which promoted the activation of the Notch signaling pathway or enhances autophagy through the nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB)-trail-cysteine protease (caspase) 8-autophagy related 3 (ATG3) axis [18, 19]. HAb 18 blocks ADAM10/CD147 interaction to augment the efficacy of cisplatin. Nevertheless, the specific conditions under which the intracellular domain of residual CD147 undergoes internalization remain unclear. Furthermore, the study revealed that CD147 undergoes internalization and localizes within lysosomes; however, the mechanisms and functional significance of CD147 intracellular fragments in lysosomes remain to be elucidated.

In this study, we systematically explored the molecular mechanism by which the CD147-G3BP1 complex regulated mTORC1 activity to mediate autophagy through translocated lysosomes and affect the chemoresistance of tumor cells. Our findings provide a theoretical basis for developing tumor therapy models that target autophagy.

Materials and methods

Ethics statement

All experiments were performed according to the ethical policies in compliance with the ARRIVE guidelines and approved by the Laboratory Animal Center Research Ethics Committee of the Fourth Military Medical University (No. 2023-NTSCMM-ID006).

Bioinformatics analysis

From the GEO database, we downloaded RNA sequencing data (GSE206501) from liver cancer tissues resistant to and responsive to oxaliplatin, and compared gene expression characteristics of six samples. With the DESeq2 R package, gene expression levels with | Fold Change |> 8 and adjusted P value < 0.05 were plotted as heatmaps. Perform KEGG enrichment analysis on all genes showing differential expression in liver cancer tissues with oxaliplatin resistance and response. Select the differentially expressed genes in the Lysosome pathway with | Fold Change |> 2 to draw a heatmap for the top 20 pathways with the lowest P value. Using STRING (http://string-db.org/cgi/input.pl) as a source, the protein–protein interaction network (PPI) was plotted.

In addition, Cytoscape (version 3.7.1) was used to discern the PPI networks between the candidate proteins. Using GEPIA2 ( http://gepia2.cancer-pku.cn/)on the online website, we calculated the correlation between TCGA-LICH tumor transcriptome data and adjacent normal liver genomes, as well as GTEx.

Cell culture

Human hepatoma cells (Huh7, HepG2, and HCCLM3) were cultured in Dulbecco's modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM) medium (Sigma-Aldrich Corp. St. Louis, MO, USA) High-glucose medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum was obtained from Corning (NY, USA). Cells were incubated at 37 °C in a constant-temperature incubator with 5% CO2. The embryonic kidney 293 T cell line (HEK293T) was cultured in RPMI 1640 medium (Sigma-Aldrich Corp. St. Louis, MO, USA); and the other culture conditions were the same as mentioned above. The human hepatoma cell line Huh7 was obtained from the Japanese Collection of Research Bioresources Cell Bank (JCRB) Cell Bank in Japan, HepG2 cells were obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC) in the United States Cell Bank, and HEK293T cells were obtained from the Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shanghai, China).

Lentiviral vectors and transfection

Lentiviral vectors expressing intracellular segments of CD147 in the transmembrane region were purchased from GeneChem (Shanghai, China). The CRISPR/Cas9 lentiviral vector knockout G3BP1 was purchased from Beijing Tsingke Biotech Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). The cells were seeded into six-well plates at a density of 1 × 105 cells / well and transfected with the lentivirus for 48 h. Detailed information regarding the targeting sequences is provided in Table S3.

Plasmids

The following plasmids were used: eGFP-C1-CD147, pCMV-mCherry-GFP-LC3B (Beyotime, Shanghai, China), pLVX-GFP-G3BP1, pcDNA3.1-mCherry-△ECD, and pcDNA3.1-mCherry-ICD (Beijing Tsingke Biotech Co. Ltd., Beijing, China). Detailed information regarding the sequences is provided in Tables S2.

Immunoblotting

Total protein was extracted from cells using radioimmunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) lysis buffer (Beyotime) containing 1 mM phenylmethanesulfonyl fluoride. Proteins were separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis and transferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride membranes (Millipore, Bedford, MA, USA). The membranes were blocked with 5% skim milk at room temperature for 1 h and incubated with primary and secondary antibodies (1:5,000, Invitrogen 31460; 1:5,000, Pierce, 31430) diluted in TBST (150 mM NaCl, 120 mM Tris–HCl pH 7.4, and 0.05% Tween 20). Finally, the immunoblots were detected using Immobilon™ Western Chemiluminescent HRP Substrate (Millipore). The details of the primary antibodies used in this study are shown in Table S4. Full and uncropped western blots are shown in Figures S6-S9.

Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) and GST pulldown assays

It was performed using the Pierce™ Co-Immunoprecipitation Kit and GST Protein Interaction Pull-Down Kit (Thermo Scientific Pierce), according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Fifty micrograms of affinity-purified antibody (GFP or IgG antibody) were coupled to the resin. The antibody-agarose beads were mixed with the extracted protein (1 mg) and the mixture was rolled at 4 °C overnight. After washing with IP lysis/wash buffer six times, immunoprecipitated proteins were eluted and analyzed by western blotting. For the GST pulldown experiments, GST-△ECD peptides were synthesized by Beijing Tsingke Biotech. Total cell lysates were incubated with “bait” protein overnight at 4 °C and rinsed with washing buffer. Finally, proteins were visualized using immunoblotting.

Flow cytometry

After culturing the cells to logarithmic growth stage, digestion, and counting, 25–30 × 106 cells were counted. The lysosomal extraction Minute ™ Lysosome Isolation Kit for Mammalian Cells/Tissues (Invent Biotechnology, Minnesota, USA) and column centrifugation were used to extract lysosomes according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Fluorescent flow cytometry antibodies directly labeled with LAMP1 (10 μL / sample) and CD147 (20 μL / sample) were added, mixed, and incubated at 4 °C in the dark for 30 min. Lysosomes were washed twice with pre-cooled PBS, centrifuged at 13,000 g for 10 min, and the supernatant was discarded. The fluorescence intensity of CD147 on the lysosomal membrane was detected using BD Fortessa (Becton, Dickinson, and Company, USA). The results were analyzed using the FlowJo V10 software (Becton, Franklin Lakes, NJ, USA).

LC–MS/MS analyses

Proteins were digested using the Trypsin/Lys-C Mix (Promega, Madison, USA) at 37 °C for 16 h. The peptide mixtures were separated on a C18 column (Gemini; Phenomenex, Torrance, CA, USA). The peptide fractions were analyzed using a Thermo Scientific™ Orbitrap Eclipse™ Tribrid™ (Thermo Fisher, San Jose, USA) equipped with EASY-nLC-1200™ (Thermo Fisher, San Jose, USA). Raw MS files were uploaded to Proteome Discoverer (version 2.5, Thermo Fisher, MA, USA) for protein identification against the UniProt protein database.

Cell viability assay

Cell suspensions (containing 2.5 × 104 cells) were prepared and added to 96-well plates at a volume of 100 µL per well. The 96-well plates were then cultured in an incubator for 24 h (37 °C, 5%CO2). DDP and CQ (20 μM) were added to each well according to the concentration gradient (0, 1.25, 2.5, 5, and 10 µg / mL), and the cells were cultured in an incubator for 48 h. Next, 10 μL of the CCK-8 (Topscience) solution was added to each well and incubated for two hours. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader (Biotek Cytation, USA).

Immunofluorescence (IF) staining and image analysis

Cell suspensions were added to a culture dish (NEST, Wuxi, China), and the cells were allowed to grow to 60–70% confluency. After fixing with 1 mL paraformaldehyde for 20 min, punching with 0.3% Triton-100 for 5 min on ice, and sealing with goat serum for 30 min, cells were incubated with primary and secondary antibodies (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) and mounted with 4,6-diamino-2-phenyl indole (DAPI) (Abcam, Cat. # ab104139) was added for 5 min for observation under a laser confocal microscope (Nikon, Tokyo, Japan; Leica STELLARIS 5, Wetzlar, Germany). The primary antibodies used for IF are shown in Table S4. At least five images were taken for each experiment. Quantification of spots and SGs in cells was measured using particles and cell functions of Image J with FIJI plugin.

Super-resolution image acquisition

Super-resolution images were obtained using Elyra 7 with Lattice SIM2 (Zeiss, Oberkochen, Germany). z-stack images (55.278 μm interval; 16-bit image) were acquired using a ZEISS Elyra 7 and an objective lens (Plan-Apochromat 63 × /1.46 Oil DIC M27, Zeiss). For live imaging, cells were analyzed using an Elyra 7 microscope with a 63 × / 1.46 oil immersion objective, and images were processed with SIM2 using Zeiss ZEN 3.4 (blue edition) software. Videos were acquired at 1 s intervals for 1 min in Lattice SIM mode [20]. For image processing, the 3D-SIM wizard (Zen 3.0 SR software, Zeiss) was used with the following settings: processing, 3D; Input SNR, Low; Iterations, 10; Regularization Weight, 0.02; Processing Sampling, 2; Output Sampling, 2; and Filter, Median. This 3D SIM (Lattice SIM2) processing enables image acquisition with a diffraction limit of approximately 60 nm [21]. Quantitation of co-localization was performed by the Pearson coefficient with Zeiss ZEN 3.4 (blue edition) software. At least five images were taken for each experiment. SGs quantification was determined using spot and cell functions of Image J with FIJI plugin.

Surface plasmon resonance (SPR)

SPR analysis were performed using a Biacore T200 instrument. His-G3BP1 was purified and diluted in 10 mM sodium acetate (pH 4.5) and immobilized on a CM5 chip (Biacore, GE Healthcare). Synthesized peptides or antibody (as analytes) were injected over the chip at 30 µL / min with a 120 s association time and 150 s dissociation times. Multiple cycle kinetics were fit using a mathematical model of a simple 1:1 interaction in the Biacore T200 evaluation software (Biacore).

Immunohistochemistry (IHC) staining

Ten visual fields of the IHC staining results were randomly selected from each sample by using a digital slide scanner (3DHISTECH, Budapest, Hungary). Based on the HALO Multiplex IHC cell quantification method, we detected the percentage of positive cells, positive cytoplasms, and positive membranes in the tumor and stromal regions of whole tissue slices. The primary antibodies used for IHC are shown in Table S4.

Immunoelectron microscopy

The precipitate of Huh7 cells was fixed at 4 °C using 2.5% glutaraldehyde, dehydrated, and embedded to prepare sections. After washing with ultrapure water, sections were treated with 1% H2O2 for 10 min. After blocking with goat serum for 30 min, sections were incubated with the corresponding primary antibodies for 16 h at 4 °C. The next day, the sections were washed with PBS for 5 min and then incubated with gold colloidal labeling reagent (InnovaCoat® GOLD-20 nm Goat Anti-Rabbit IgG Conjugate, 219–1000, Expedeon; InnovaCoat® GOLD-10 nm Goat Anti-Mouse nanoparticles, 218–1000, Expedeon) at 25 °C for 1 h. Sections were then stained with 5% uranium acetate and lead acetate for 1 h at room temperature. The location of the protein was observed via immunoelectron microscopy (Hitachi, Japan) [22]. The primary antibodies used for immunoelectron microscopy are shown in Table S4.

In vivoantitumor efficacy study

Female NCG (NOD/ShiLtJ-Prkdc em26Cd52 Il2rg em26Cd22) mice (approximately 4.5 weeks old) and nude mice (approximately 4 weeks old) were purchased from GemPharmatech (Jiangsu, China) and acclimatized for 1 week. The animals were raised under specific pathogen-free (SPF) conditions and were used to construct a subcutaneous tumor model. NCG Mice were randomized into four groups and nude mice were randomized into six groups. Four mice per subgroup were subcutaneously implanted with 5 × 106 Huh7 CtrlGFP and Huh7 △ECD-GFP cells labeled with luciferase.The KODAK In Vivo imaging system was employed to detect luciferase fluorescence. Tumor size in NCG mice was assessed using in vivo small-animal imaging techniques five days following the administration of D-luciferin sodium. The mice were sacrificed via cervical dislocation 25 days after subcutaneous injection of tumor cells [23]. Human HCC transplanted subcutaneously in a nude mouse model was established and divided into three groups: control, cisplatin, cisplatin, and hydroxychloroquine. Five mice per subgroup were subcutaneously implanted with 2 × 105 HCCLM3 CtrlGFP or HCCLM3 △ECD-GFP cells. When the tumors reached a mean volume of 50 mm3, mice in each treatment group were injected with cisplatin (3 μg / g) every ten days. Hydroxychloroquine (40 μg / g) was orally administered daily. The Laboratory Animal Center Research Ethics Committee of the Fourth Military Medical University approved this study protocol.

Statistical analysis

GraphPad 8.0, Image Lab 5.1, Image J, and FlowJo V10.5.3 softwares were used for statistical analysis and drawing figures. Adobe Illustrator CS6 software was used for graphics. The results of all quantitative experiments were based on at least three independent biological samples. Differences were compared using a two-tailed Student’s t-test and a two-way ANOVA (or mixed model). P < 0.05 was regarded as statistically significant.

Results

Chemotherapy drugs induce CD147 internalization

Our analysis of two sets of differentially expressed RNA sequencing genes resistant and sensitive to oxaliplatin in the GEO database and enrichment of KEGG pathways showed upregulated genes enriched in pathways such as chemical carcinogenesis-reactive oxygen species pathway, cell adhesion molecules pathway, Efferocytosis pathway, phagosome pathway, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease pathway, lysosome pathway, and so on (Fig. 1A-C). Based on lysosomal pathways associated with autophagy, we screened 63 genes with differential expression of more than two-fold (Fig. 1D). We selected lysosomal tripeptidyl peptidase (TPP1) and CD147 (BSG) based on their protein degree value and betweenness centrality calculated using the topological network analysis software Cytoscape (Fig. 1E). In TCGA-LICH cancer and normal liver tissues, as well as GTEx, we found a significant correlation between TPP1 and CD147 (R = 0.46, Fig. 1F). In previous studies, CD147 could internalize and target lysosomes [18]. We speculate that clinical chemotherapy resistance is related to CD147 internalization targeting the lysosome pathway based on results of bioinformatics analysis. Hence, we verified that GFP-labeled CD147 co-localized with lysosomes in human embryonic kidney (HEK) HEK293T cells treated with treated with lysosomal inhibitor chloroquine (CQ) (20 μM) for 12 h (Fig. 1G, S1B). Cisplatin (DDP) was applied in different doses (0 μg / mL, 5 μg / mL, and 10 μg / mL) for 12 h in 293 T cells to investigate the internalization effect of platinum-based chemotherapy drugs on CD147. Interestingly, super-resolution images and living-cell images both showed that CD147 internalizes targeted lysosomes, and with increasing concentration, more green fluorescence occured in lysosomes (P = 0.0094; Fig. 1H, I; Movie 1). There was a significant difference in the co-localization of CD147 and lysosomes compared to the control group in 5 μg / mL and 10 μg / mL DDP (P = 0.0312, P < 0.0001; Figures S1C, D), suggesting that chemotherapy drugs could induce CD147 internalization.

CD147 intracellular segments internalized and localized on lysosomes

In previous studies, low cholesterol inhibitors have been shown to promote hydrolytic cleavage of the extracellular segment of CD147. Once the inhibitor was removed, the intracellular segment of CD147 could be internalized into the nucleus [18]. To observe this internalization phenomenon, we transfected the CD147-enhanced green fluorescent protein (eGFP) plasmid, in which GFP was fused at the C-terminus, into HEK293T cells and observed the localization of CD147 residual segments using laser scanning confocal microscopy. Following the withdrawal of methyl-β-cyclodextrin (MβCD) treatment, CD147 fluorescence on the cell membrane weakened, internalized, and diffused into the cytoplasm (Fig. 2A). Here, the expression of CD147-marked intracellular segments on the cell membrane was detected by flow cytometry (FC) in Huh7 (P = 0.0459, P = 0.0354, P = 0.088, respectively) and HCCLM3 (P < 0.0001, P = 0.0006, P = 0.0012, respectively). CD147 expression decreased on the cell membrane compared to that in the control group after treatment with the cholesterol inhibitor (Fig. 2B-E), and 10 mM concentration of MβCD resulted in a more obvious shedding effect on the extracellular segment of CD147 (Fig. 2C, E). The HAb18 monoclonal antibody was used to detect CD147 extracellular segment expression of Huh7 or HCCLM3 cells. Consistent with FC results, the expression of CD147 extracellular segments was significantly lower than that in the control group (P = 0.0141, P = 0.0295; Fig. 2F, G), which confirmed that low cholesterol levels enhanced the shedding of the extracellular segments and the internalization of intracellular segments of CD147. Simultaneously, 3D images and living cell images also showed significant co-localization between internalized CD147 intracellular domains and lysosomes, with localization distributed near nucleus (Fig. 2H, Movie 2, Figures S1A, 2C). These results confirmed that Low-concentration cholesterol contributed to the shedding of the extracellular segments of CD147 and the internalization of the intracellular domain of residual CD147.

To investigate the localization of the intracellular domain of residual CD147 followed by hydrolytic cleavage of the extracellular segments in hepatoma cells, we transfected lentiviral overexpressing the transmembrane and intracellular domains of CD147 (△ECD-GFP) into Huh7 and HepG2 cells and screened the stably transfected cell lines for puromycin resistance (Fig. 2I). △ECD-GFP was successfully transfected into hepatoma cells. A band of approximately 34 kDa was detected in both cell lines by western blotting using the CD147 C-terminal antibody (Fig. 2J). We found that the intracellular domain of residual CD147 co-localized with lysosomes in Huh7 and HepG2 cells (Fig. 2K, L, and N). After treatment with CQ, the co-localization of CD147 intracellular segments and lysosomes increased (Fig. 2M, N). The plot profile tool in Image J was used to analyze the coincidence of CD147 intracellular segments and lysosome-associated membrane protein 1 (LAMP1) at the same spatial position. The overlapping area of the red broken line and green broken line significantly increased after adding the lysosomal inhibitor CQ to Huh7 cells (Figure S2E). Immunoelectron microscopy clearly showed that the intracellular fragments of CD147 were not only located on the cytoplasmic side of the membrane in Huh7 CtrlGFP and Huh7 △ECD-GFP but were located on the surface of lysosomes in Huh7 △ECD-GFP (Figures S2F, G). These results confirmed the internalization of the intracellular domain of residual CD147, which was co-located with lysosomes after the shedding of the extracellular segments of CD147.

CD147 intracellular segments formed a complex with G3BP1 on lysosomes

To further confirm the localization of CD147 internalization on the surface of lysosomes, we extracted lysosomes from cells. The absence of expression of the Golgi membrane (GM) proteins GM130, and Lamin B1 indicated the high purity of the extracted lysosomes by column centrifugation (Figure S2A). Intracellular segments of CD147 were also identified in the total lysosomal protein extracted by mass spectrometry (MS) (Fig. 3A, S2D). The fluorescence of CD147 intracellular segments on the lysosomal membrane was detected using FC. Results showed that the fluorescence of the intracellular segments of CD147 in Huh7 △ECD-GFP was stronger than that in the control group (Fig. 3B, S2B), consistently, the ratio of CD147 positive lysosomes in Huh7 △ECD-GFP was significantly higher than that in the control group (P = 0.0126; Fig. 3C). Although the intracellular domain of residual CD147 internalizes induced by DDP, how the intracellular domain of residual CD147 is transferred from the cell membrane to lysosomes remains unknown. Proteins interacting with the intracellular domain of residual CD147 in lysosomes were detected by CO-IP and MS, and G3BP1 and Rab7A were screened (Table S1). G3BP1 is a core molecule usually located in stress granules (SGs) [24]. Rab7A, a member of the Rab family of small G proteins, regulates the translocation of early endosomes to late endosomes, and late endosomes to lysosomes [25]. Immunoblotting verified that CD147 intracellular segments interacted with G3BP1 and Rab7A (Fig. 3D, S3C). It was reported that inhibition of lysosomal function affected the transport of compound targets to lysosomes by Rab7A [26]. After CQ addition, the colocalization of △ECD-GFP and Rab 7A on lysosomes disappeared in Huh7 cells (Figure S3B). It was confirmed that the purified G3BP1 protein with His tag directly binds to the synthesized △ECD-GFP with GST tag by GST pulldown (Fig. 3E). Laser confocal images showed that overexpressed CD147 intracellular segments co-localized with G3BP1 in Huh7△ECD-GFP cells (Fig. 3F). However, the co-localization of G3BP1 and lysosomes appeared only when CD147 was overexpressed (Figure S3A). Immunoelectron microscopy images also showed that the intracellular domains of residual CD147 and G3BP1 were co-localized near lysosomes in Huh7△ECD-GFP cells (P = 0.0188, P = 0.0413, P = 0.0031; Fig. 3K-M). To verify that the combination was due to internalization of the CD147 intracellular domain, we transfected pmCherry-ΔECD and pGFP-G3BP1 into HEK293T cells and performed immunofluorescence staining of Rab7A. Laser confocal images showed that mCherry-△ECD, mCherry-ICD, GFP-G3BP1, and Rab7A co-localized in lysosomes respectively (Fig. 3O, S3D, and S3E). However, co-localization of GFP-G3BP1 and Rab7A was not observed in the absence of CD147 intracellular segments (Figure S3F). In order to further detect the morphology of CD147 intracellular segments that are bound to G3BP1, we transfected pmCherry-G3BP1 into Huh7 CtrlGFP and Huh7 △ECD-GFP. Super-resolution images showed that overexpressed CD147 intracellular segments colocalized with G3BP1 in similar vesicles compared with Huh7 CtrlGFP (Fig. 3N). Moreover, SPR results showed consistent results that CD147 △ECD directly binds to G3BP1 and the constant of combination was 3.20 × 10–8 mol/L (Fig. 3G-J). These results show that the intracellular domain of residual CD147 recruited G3BP1 after internalization that targeted lysosomes via Rab7A.

CD147 intracellular segments enhanced cytoprotective autophagy via inhibited mTOR activity

mTORC1 protein plays a crucial role in autophagy regulation on lysosomes. Previous literature reported that G3BP1 can transform the migration ability of breast cancer cells by recruiting tuberous sclerosis complex (TSC) regulated the activity of mTORC1 in lysosomes [27]. To verify whether CD147-G3BP1 complex affects the activity of mTOR, we examined mTOR phosphorylation. The expression of phosphorylated mTOR (p-mTOR) at the Ser2448 site was significantly reduced in Huh7 △ECD-GFP cells compared to that in Huh7 CtrlGFP (P = 0.005; Fig. 4A, B). During autophagy, sequestosome-1 (SQSTM1) / p62 carries ubiquitinated proteins, binds to light chain 3 (LC3), is encapsulated by autophagosomes, and enters lysosomes for degradation. Western blotting results showed that the expression of SQSTM1/p62 was reduced (P = 0.015), whereas that of LC3B/A was significantly increased (P = 0.0082) in Huh7 △ECD-GFP cells compared to that in Huh7 CtrlGFP, whereas the expression of LC3B/A was significantly increased (P = 0.0006) in Huh7 △ECD-GFP cells treated with CQ (Fig. 4C, D). The expression of SQSTM1/p62 increased during obstructed autophagy because degradation was inhibited by CQ (P = 0.0406; Fig. 4C, D). Laser confocal microscopy also showed that the co-localization of LC3 and LAMP1 was more conspicuous in the experimental group treated with CQ than without CQ (Fig. 4E). In Huh7 △ECD-GFP cells, the coincidence area of LC3 (red) and lysosomes (green) was greater than that in the control group, as analyzed using the plot profile tool in ImageJ (Fig. 4F). To observe the autophagosome more accurately, we transferred the LC3 double fluorescent plasmid pCMV-mCherry-GFP-LC3B into Huh7 CtrlGFP and Huh7 △ECD-GFP cells. Normally, plasmids can simultaneously produce yellow fluorescence under excitation. Once LC3B was fused with the lysosome, green fluorescence was gradually quenched in the acidic environment of the lysosome, leaving only red fluorescence. After Hanks balanced salt solution (HBSS) starvation for four hours, it was found that there were more red fluorescent dots in Huh7 △ECD-GFP cells than in Huh7 CtrlGFP cells using Image J analysis (P = 0.0216; Fig. 4G, H). Immunoelectron microscopic images showed that the number of autolysosomes increased in Huh7 △ECD-GFP cells compared to that in Huh7 CtrlGFP (P = 0.0109), similarly, the number of autolysosomes also increased significantly treated with HBSS than without HBSS in Huh7 △ECD-GFP cells (P = 0.0083; Fig. 4I-K). The above results indicate that CD147 intracellular segments enhance autophagy in the hepatoma cells.

To explore what effect of autophagy induced by the intracellular domain of residual CD147, we treated Huh7 CtrlGFP and Huh7 △ECD-GFP cells with different concentrations of DDP (0 μg / mL, 1.25 μg / mL, 2.5 μg / mL, 5 μg / mL, and 10 μg / mL) for 48 h. G3BP1 knockout abated CD147-induced autophagy

To investigate the effect of intracellular internalization of full-length CD147 on autophagy in HCC cells, we overexpressed full-length CD147 with a FLAG tag (Fig. 5A). We detected autophagic flux and phosphorylated mTOR expression in cells at different time points after being treated with MβCD (Fig. 5B). We found that LC3B/LC3A expression gradually increased over time, especially the eighth hour after withdrawal of MβCD (P = 0.0104; Fig. 5C, D). Furthermore, p-mTOR expression significantly decreased, especially in sixth, eighth, and tenth hour (P = 0.0086, P = 0.0065, P = 0.0091, respectively; Fig. 5C, F). The period of mTOR activity inhibition coincided with the period when autophagic flux began to increase. However, SQSTM1/p62 expression fluctuated considerably, decreasing at the sixth hour (P = 0.0364) and increasing at the eighth hour (P = 0.0161), This might be because the change in SQSTM1/p62 always lagged behind LC3B (Fig. 5C-E). These results suggest that the internalization of endogenous CD147 intracellular segments can enhance autophagic flux by inhibiting mTOR activity in hepatoma cells.

To verify the inhibitory effect of G3BP1 on mTORC1, we constructed HCC cell lines with high metastatic potential (HCCLM3) overexpressing the transmembrane and intracellular domains of CD147 and knocked down G3BP1 using three clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats (CRISPR) and CRISPR-associated protein 9 (Cas9) (CRISPR/Cas9) single guide sequences (Table S3). We found that shG3BP1-3 had better interference efficiencies than shG3BP1-1 and shG3BP1-1 (Fig. 5H, I). We chose shG3BP1-3 for the follow-up experiments. We transfected shNC or shG3BP1-3 into HCCLM3 CtrlGFP and HCCLM3 △ECD-GFP cells. Cisplatin-induced CD147 internalization formed a complex with G3BP1 accompanied by SGs depolymerization

Under chemotherapeutic stress, G3BP1 is activated to assemble stress granules (SGs) [28]. Laser confocal images showed that the number of SGs increased gradually in every 10 cells treated with an increased concentration of DDP in Huh7 CtrlGFP and Huh7 △ECD-GFP (P = 0.0142, P = 0.0063; Fig. 6A-C). However, compared with Huh7 CtrlGFP, the number of SG formed decreased at 2.5 μg / L and 5 μg / mL concentrations of DDP in Huh7 △ECD-GFP (P = 0.0062; Fig. 6C). To investigate whether CD147 intracellular segment overexpression was involved in SG formation under DDP, we transfected pmCherry-G3BP1 into HCCLM3 CtrlGFP and HCCLM3 △ECD-GFP which were observed by super-resolution microscopy. Super-resolution images showed that CD147 intracellular segments co-localized with G3BP1 at 0 μg / mL, 5 μg / mL and 10 μg / mL DDP in HCCLM3 △ECD-GFP, and the size of SGs assembled in △ECD-GFP bigger than CtrlGFP at 5 μg / mL DDP (Fig. 6D). To investigate whether CD147 affected SG depolymerization, we quantified the speckles area. The diameter of SG is usually greater than 100 nm. The images showed that as the concentration of DDP increased, the percentage of SGs gradually increased in HCCLM3 CtrlGFP and most of large volume SGs (area ≥ 5 μm2) appeared at 10 μg / mL DDP (Fig. 6D, E). However, compared to HCCLM3 CtrlGFP, large SGs of HCCLM3 △ECD-GFP appeared at 5 μg / mL DDP, depolymerized at 10 μg / mL DDP (Fig. 6D-G). For overexpressing CD147 intracellular segments, high-dose DDP resulted in the disappearance of large-sized SGs (Fig. 6D, F, and G). These results indicate that CD147 internalization and binds to G3BP1that might affect the assembly of SGs.

Combination administration enhances liver cancer treatment

To further demonstrate that overexpression of the CD147 intracellular segment could promote tumor chemotherapy resistance, we transfected Huh7 CtrlGFP and Huh7△ECD-GFP with luciferase virus and established a Huh7 xenograft model in non-obese diabetic (NOD) SCID gamma (NCG) mice. The fluorescence intensity of the tumors was observed after intraperitoneal injection of cisplatin on the fifth and 15th days (Fig. 7A). After the first injection of DDP, the intensity of fluorescence from tumors in Huh7 CtrlGFP was significantly reduced (P = 0.0028), but that of Huh7 △ECD-GFP was not significantly affected (Fig. 7B, C). Consistent with the above-mentioned results, the fluorescence intensity of tumors in the control group was reduced after the second injection of DDP (P = 0.02), whereas that in Huh7 △ECD-GFP remained unchanged (Fig. 7B, D). To further verify that cytoprotective autophagy leads to reduced sensitivity to chemotherapy, we established an HCCLM3 xenograft model in nude mice. The results showed that the tumor volume of HCCLM3 CtrlGFP mice treated with DDP alone (P = 0.0276) and dual administration groups (P = 0.0038; Fig. 7E, F) was significantly reduced compared to the saline group, while the tumor volume of HCCLM3 △ECD-GFP mice in DDP treatment alone was not significantly reduced. However, the tumor volume of HCCLM3 △ECD-GFP mice in the dual treatment group was significantly lower than that in the saline group (P = 0.0392) and the DDP administration alone group (P = 0.0302; Fig. 7E, F). HE and TUNEL assays showed tumor necrosis in HCCLM3 CtrlGFP mice treated with DDP alone and in combination (P = 0.0142), however, HCCLM3 △ECD-GFP mice treated with DDP alone had only slight tumor necrosis, while the necrotic area significantly increased in the dual administration groups (P = 0.0063; Figures S5A, B). Throughout the administration period, the weight of the mice steadily increased without any intergroup differences (Fig. 7G).

CD44 is a widely distributed glycoprotein on the cell surface that can specifically bind to the extracellular matrix to affect the mutual adhesion between cells and the matrix, thereby affecting the differentiation of mesenchymal stem cells. As a vital surface marker of stem cells, CD44 is significantly up-regulated by autophagy [29]. Immunohistochemical (IHC) staining showed that the expression of CD44 in HCCLM3 △ECD-GFP was enhanced considerably compared to that in HCCLM3 CtrlGFP in saline- and cisplatin-treated groups (P = 0.0291; P = 0.0013; Fig. 7H-J). In the dual administration group using HCQ and DDP, the expression of CD44 in HCCLM3 △ECD-GFP was lower than that in saline- and cisplatin-treated groups, whereas there was no significant difference in every group of HCCLM3 CtrlGFP (P = 0.0432; P = 0.0165; Fig. 7K, L). Moreover, we also collected 12 clinical tissues of relapsed liver cancer with chemotherapy and adjacent lesions, along with 12 tissues of untreated liver cancer and adjacent lesions. The IHC results showed significant internalization of the CD147 intracellular segment in recurrent liver cancer tissues after chemotherapy (P = 0.0144; Fig. 7M, S5C-S5H). In previous studies, we also found that patients with a high positive rate of CD147 intracellular fragment expression had a significantly poor overall survival compared with patients with a low positive rate of CD147 intracellular fragment expression in 102 cases of HCC tissues [19]. High-resolution MS revealed that the abundance of CD147 intracellular peptide segments in liver cancer tissues that relapsed after chemotherapy was higher than that in adjacent lesions (Figures S5I, S5J). IHF results also showed significant co-localization of internalized CD147 intracellular segments with G3BP1 in the cytoplasm (Fig. 7N). These results indicate that the internalization of CD147 intracellular segments reduced the chemosensitivity of cancer cells to cisplatin and maintained their stemness in HCC, and combination administration could enhance liver cancer treatment via inhibited autophagy.

Discussion

Autophagy, a cellular process typically associated with promoting tumor cell survival [30], can manifest in various forms, including protective, cytotoxic, and nonprotective autophagy, all of which can impact tumor progression [31]. Excessive or sustained autophagy activation may ultimately lead to cell death, however, in certain forms of cancer, the presence of protective autophagy is essential for tumor progression [32]. The protein complex mTORC1, which serves as a mediator between external stimuli and internal cellular reactions, plays a crucial role in enabling cells to adapt to changes in their surroundings [33]. In the context of pancreatic cancer, inhibiting mTORC1 activity can result in a temporary halt in cell growth [34]. Despite this, the inhibition of mTORC1 does not directly induce cellular aging but instead supports the survival of aging cells by modulating autophagy [35]. This pause can lead to the development of resistance to drugs commonly used in chemotherapy [36]. Hence, ongoing clinical trials are investigating tumor therapies that target autophagy. The involvement of endocytosis and the circulation of membrane proteins, particularly CD147, are believed to be crucial in malignant tumor progression [37]. Our previous research has shown that the intracellular domain of remaining CD147 is found in nucleus and can influence autophagy by facilitating the NF-κB pathway [18]. Nevertheless, the mechanisms underlying the internalization of the intracellular domain of residual CD147 and the potential impact of lysosomal localization on autophagy are still not fully understood. Cholesterol plays a crucial role as a lipid component necessary for the proliferation and survival of cancer cells [38]. Our study confirmed that MβCD can trigger the shedding of the extracellular domain of CD147, leading to the translocation of the intracellular domain of CD147 into the cytoplasm where it is trafficked to lysosomes. Interestingly, super-resolution live cell imaging showed that CD147 also exhibited similar manifestations which internalized and translocated to lysosome in tumor cells treated with DDP. As the concentration of DDP increased, more and more CD147 was internalized and translocated to lysosomes. We also found more phenomena of CD147 internalization in tissue samples of patients with recurrent liver cancer after chemotherapy, which suggests that DDP-induced CD147 internalization may be one of the reasons for tumor chemotherapy resistance.

G3BP1, a binding protein specific to the SH3 domain of the Ras GTPase-activating protein, is prominently expressed in various tumor tissues and cells, playing a role in the regulation of tumor proliferation, migration, invasion, and apoptosis [39]. Additionally, G3BP1 can bind to tumor suppressor molecules (mitochondrial H+- adenosine triphosphate (ATP) synthase subunit β, β-F1 ATPase) and downregulate their expression to enhance glycolysis in hepatoma cells [40]. Furthermore, Dou et al. observed a decrease in Slug expression in a nude mouse model following G3BP1 knockdown, leading to an increase in E-cadherin levels [41]. Previous study suggests that G3BP1 may function as a regulator of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) metastasis by modulating the Phosphoinositide 3-kinases (PI3K)/AKT/mTOR and Wnt signaling pathways, leading to the inhibition of proliferation, migration, and invasion of esophageal cancer cells [42]. These studies focused on the regulation of G3BP1expression but not the translocation. Previous studies focused on the regulation of G3BP1 expression but not the translocation. In this study during internalization, CD147 intracellular domain could interact with G3BP1, facilitating the localization of the complex to the lysosomal membrane via targeted lysosomal transport mediated by Rab7A. Furthermore, CD147-G3BP1 complex could activate autophagy by inhibiting mTORC1 activity, thereby contributing to the chemoresistance of HCC cells (Fig. 8). The knockout of G3BP1 weakened autophagy induced by the CD147-G3BP1 complex, indicating that inhibition of mTOR activity was mainly mediated by G3BP1, but lysosomal translocation of G3BP1 was mainly mediated by CD147.

The model of CD147 internalization induces autophagy and promotes tumor chemotherapy resistance by combining G3BP1-targeted lysosomes. Elevated levels of cisplatin have been shown to trigger the release of the extracellular domain of CD147, leading to the internalization of the intracellular domain into the cytoplasm where it interacts with lysosomes. This interaction results in the recruitment of G3BP1 by the CD147 intracellular domain, forming a complex that is subsequently localized to the lysosomal membrane through Rab7A-mediated transport and capable of inducing autophagy by suppressing mTORC1 activity, consequently enhancing resistance to chemotherapy in hepatocellular carcinoma cells

Additionally, G3BP1, as a key component of stress granules (SGs), aids in SG assembly to prevent the accumulation of damaged DNA and proteins under various stressful conditions, including exposure to arsenite, heat shock, hypoxia, viral infection, and chemotherapy [43]. It is important to note that the efficacy of stress-induced stress granule (SG) function may vary depending on the intensity and nature of the stress [44, 45]. Furthermore, cancer cells can develop resistance to chemotherapy and radiotherapy by forming excessive SGs [46, 47]. SGs formed by G3BP1 play a role in activating cytoprotective mechanisms in cancer cells and contributing to resistance to chemotherapy [28, 48]. In malignant breast tumors, G3BP1 is localized in lysosomes and suppresses mTORC1 activity in the absence of SGs. Deletion of G3BP1 leads to increased mTORC1 activity in cancer cells and correlates with a poorer prognosis in patients [27]. Phosphorylation and ubiquitination of G3BP1, acetylation of the N-terminal IDR region of RNA, and loss of heat shock protein can all lead to SG depolymerization [49–52]. In our study, high-dose DDP resulted in cytoplasmic translocation of CD147 intracellular segments that disrupted the formation of large SGs by G3BP1 in response to DDP stimulation. We also found phosphorylation modifications at the S149 and S232 sites of G3BP1 in MS data (not shown in the article). This phenomenon of SG depolymerization could potentially be attributed to the phosphorylation modification of G3BP1, thereby affecting the assembly of SGs.

The internalization of tumor-associated membrane proteins may play a significant role in the development of drug resistance in cancer cells, especially when combined with the release of hydrophobic drug adjuvants and chemotherapeutic agents. This finding clarifies the involvement of tumor-related membrane protein colocation in the regulation of autophagy and provides a robust theoretical basis for the development of targeted autophagy strategies in the clinical management of liver cancer.

However, there are still some limitations in this study. For instance, the mechanism by which DDP affects the internalization of CD147 necessitates further explore. Whether inhibitors targeting hydrolytic enzymes which cleaves CD147 can reverse chemotherapy resistance in tumor cells. Additonally, further verification is required to determine whether the similar compartment formed by CD147-G3BP1 is indeed a multivesicular body (MVB) as demonstrated by super-resolution microscopy (Fig. 3N). The mechanism by which CD147 internalization impacts SG assembly will be will be a crucial area for future research.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our investigation demonstrated that the intracellular domain of residual CD147 is internalized and interacts with G3BP1 to target lysosomes. The complex promotes autophagic activity and confers resistance to chemotherapy by suppressing mTORC1 function within lysosomes, simultaneously, the internalization of CD147 impedes the assembly of SGs, although further research is needed to fully understand this mechanism. In conclusion, this study reveals how CD147 internalization contributes to chemoresistance in HCC and suggests targeting autophagy as a potential treatment strategy.

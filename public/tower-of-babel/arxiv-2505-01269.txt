Verifying Parameterized Networks Specified by Vertex-Replacement Graph Grammars
Radu Iosif, Arnaud Sangnier, Neven Villani

Abstract
We consider the parametric reachability problem ( 𝖯𝖱𝖯 \mathsf{PRP} ) for families of networks described by vertex-replacement ( VR ) graph grammars, where network nodes run replicas of finite-state processes that communicate via binary handshaking. We show that the 𝖯𝖱𝖯 \mathsf{PRP} problem for VR grammars can be effectively reduced to the 𝖯𝖱𝖯 \mathsf{PRP} problem for hyperedge-replacement ( HR ) grammars, at the cost of introducing extra edges for routing messages. This transformation is motivated by the existence of several parametric verification techniques for families of networks specified by HR grammars, or similar inductive formalisms. Our reduction enables applying the verification techniques for HR systems to systems with dense architectures, such as user-specified cliques and multi-partite graphs.

Introduction

As pointed out in recent literature, “

”
[

. In this

such as access between point A and point B. Very often, the intended

typically known at early stages of network design.

The huge scale of present-day datacenters, of
\sim\!\!10^{4}
routers for a
regional hub and continuously growing, requires
parametric

rack, switches in a cluster, clusters in a layer, etc.
Despite decades of theoretical research and an impressive body
of results (see
[
7
]

[

) or

[
11
]
) families of graphs
with a common topological pattern.

The problem of verifying networks with user-specified architecture has
gained recent momentum, with the development of parametric
verification techniques for systems described using logic

[
5
]
or graph grammars

[
20

. In principle, logic-based graph specification

k

On the other hand, graph grammars are appealing for their constructive

Graph grammars are at the core of the theory of formal languages

[

Contribution

We leverage the idea of transforming
VR
grammars into

HR
grammars modulo elimination of
\epsilon
-edges to define a
reduction from the parametric reachability problem (
\mathsf{PRP}
)
for systems described by
VR
grammars and finite-state local
behaviors, with correctness properties given by unrestricted
arithmetic formulæ over a set of variables, tracking how many
processes are in a given local state, to the
\mathsf{PRP}
problem
for systems described by
HR
grammars, having the same
correctness property. This is motivated by several recent developments
in the verification of parametric systems with
HR
-style
architecture descriptions. In
particular,
[
10
]
reports on an inference
of structural invariants (
i.e.,
over-approximations of the reachable
set, that can be derived from the structure of the network) for
architectures described using a variant of Separation Logic with
inductive definitions (same expressivity as
HR
grammars)
Moreover,
[
9
]
gives an abstraction technique
that folds similar network nodes into a finite Petri net, whose
coverability problem over-approximates the original parametric
coverability problem. In this paper, we give a more general reduction
method, that preserves all safety properties specified by arithmetic
assertions, rather than the particular case of coverability.

Related Work

The literature on parametric verification is too large to be recalled
here. We point to
[
7
]

for a survey on the (un)decidability results, typically concerning
clique or ring architectures. The specification of network
architectures using inductive definitions can be traced back to the
work on

grammars

[
22
,
20
,
18
]
, that use
inductive rules to describe systems with linear (pipeline, token-ring)
architectures. These initial works target mainly safety properties, by
inferring network
invariants
[
23
,
21
]
. Pipeline
and clique architectures are also considered by methods based on the
theory of well-structured transition
systems
[
1
]
, such as monotonic
abstraction
[
2
,
3
]
.

A prominent exception, that considers the verification of clique-width
bounded architectures specified using Monadic Second Order Logic
(
\mathsf{MSO}
) against
\mathsf{CTL}^{*}\setminus\bigcirc
properties
is
[
5
]
. Their result uses a reduction
from the parametric model checking problem (
\mathsf{PMCP}
) to the decidability of

\mathsf{MSO}
over graphs of bounded
clique-width
[
13
]
. Our result is
orthogonal, because we consider
VR
grammars that strictly
subsume the
\mathsf{MSO}
-definable sets of bounded clique-width. It is,
however, an interesting question if our reduction can be used to
establish a more general decidability result. A promising lead would
be to use the decidability of
\mathsf{PMCP}
for
HR
systems
with local behaviors that mimick the passing of a pebble from one node
to another
[

]
.

Graphs

For simplicity reasons, in this paper we consider only networks whose
topologies are described by oriented binary graphs, where vertices
model processes and edges model a symmetric rendezvous between two
processes,
i.e.,
we do not distinguish the process initiating the
communication and consider that both participants have equal roles.

Let
\Lambda
and
\Delta
be finite disjoint alphabets of vertex and
edge labels, respectively. A
graph
is a tuple

\mathsf{G}=(\mathsf{V}_{\mathsf{G}},\mathsf{E}_{\mathsf{G}},\lambda_{\mathsf{G}})
, where

\mathsf{V}_{\mathsf{G}}
is a finite set of vertices,
\mathsf{E}_{\mathsf{G}}\subseteq\mathsf{V}_{\mathsf{G}}\times\Delta\times\mathsf{V}_{\mathsf{G}}
is a
set of edges, such that
(v,\delta,v^{\prime})\in\mathsf{E}_{\mathsf{G}}
implies

v\neq v^{\prime}
(
i.e.,
graphs have no self-loops) and
\lambda_{\mathsf{G}}:\mathsf{V}_{\mathsf{G}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{P}({\Lambda})}
assigns a set of labels
to each vertex. We do not distinguish graphs that are isomorphic.
We denote by
\mathcal{G}({\Lambda},{\Delta})
the set of graphs with
vertex and edge labels from
\Lambda
and
\Delta
, or simply

\mathcal{G}
, if
\Delta
and
\Lambda
are understood.

We shall consider parameterized networks described as infinite sets of
graphs. Albeit infinite, these sets have a finite description, given
by finitely many inductive rules stating how the graphs in the set are
built from smaller graphs. This constructive

3
Parameterized Systems

We aim at describing a family of networks by a
VR
-grammar. To
model the behavior of a network, we associate to each vertex in the graph
a
process type
, which is a finite-state machine represented as
a Petri net of a particular form. We denote by
\mathbb{P}
the fixed and
finite set of process types. In the rest of this paper, the alphabet
of vertex labels is assumed to be
\Lambda\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{def}}}}{{=}}\mathbb{P}\uplus\Pi
, where
\Pi=\biguplus_{\mathsf{p}\in\mathbb{P}}\Pi_{\mathsf{p}}

is a set of port labels partitioned according to the process types,

i.e.,
\Pi_{\mathsf{p}}
is the set of ports corresponding to the process
type
\mathsf{p}
. The intuition is that different vertices labeled with a
process type run copies of that process type, called

processes
. Neighbouring processes communicate by joining their
transitions in a symmetric synchronous rendezvous, represented by a
pair of transitions, that labels the edge between their host
vertices. Hence, the alphabet
\Delta
of edge labels is considered
to be the set of pairs of transitions from the process types

\mathbb{P}
.

3.3
The Parametric Reachability Problem

A
parametric

VR
(
resp.
HR
)
system
is
described by a
VR
(
resp.
HR
) grammar
\Gamma
. We
define our decision problem as a parametric reachability problem
asking if there exists an instance of a parametric system, described
by a grammar, whose behavior reaches an error configuration from a
given set. If the answer is negative, we have a proof that the
parametric system is correct.

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a fixed countably infinite set of variables,
interpreted over natural numbers. An arithmetic formula
\alpha
is a
(possibly quantified) first-order formula using variables in
\mathbb{X}
,
constants denoting natural numbers, the binary operations of addition
and multiplication and the (in)equality relation. We denote by

\mathbb{A}
the set of such formulæ. Let
\mathcal{N}
be
a PN and
\ell:\mathsf{Q}_{\mathcal{N}}\rightharpoonup\mathbb{X}
be a
partial function that labels certain places of
\mathcal{N}
with
variables. The boolean value
[\![{\alpha}]\!]_{\mathsf{m}}^{\ell}\in{\{{\mathrm{true},\mathrm{false}}\}}
of an arithmetic formula
\alpha

in a marking
\mathsf{m}:\mathsf{Q}_{\mathcal{N}}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
is
obtained by replacing each variable
x
that occurs free in
\alpha

by the integer value
\sum_{q\in\ell^{-1}(x)}\mathsf{m}(q)
,
i.e.,
the
total number of tokens from the places associated with
x
in

\mathsf{m}
. We further define
\mathit{Atoms}^{\ell}({\mathsf{m}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{def}}}}{{=}}{\{{\alpha\in\mathbb{A}\mid[\![{\alpha}]\!]_{\mathsf{m}}^{\ell}=\mathrm{true}}\}}
,
i.e.,
the set of arithmetic formulæ satisfied by
a given marking and a variable labeling. A PN
\mathcal{N}
satisfies
a reachability specification
\alpha
for a place labeling
\ell
,
written
(\mathcal{N},\ell)\models\alpha
, iff there exists a
finite firing sequence
\rho
leading to a reachable marking

\mathrm{init}_{\mathcal{N}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle{\rho}}}{{\leadsto}}\mathsf{m}^{\prime}
such that
\alpha\in\mathit{Atoms}^{\ell}({\mathsf{m}^{\prime}})
.

We specialize the satisfiability of a reachability specification
by an arbitrary PN to the satisfiability by the behavior of
a system
\mathsf{S}\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{P}})
and a labeling

\mathsf{L}:\mathsf{Q}_{\mathbb{P}}\rightharpoonup\mathbb{X}
of the places
from the process types
\mathbb{P}
with variables:

\displaystyle(\mathsf{S},\mathsf{L})\models\alpha\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{def}}}}{{\iff}}(\beta({\mathsf{S}}),\mathsf{L}\circ{}\!\downharpoonleft_{1})\models\alpha

where
{}\!\downharpoonleft_{1}
is the projection of a pair on its first component,

i.e.,
\mathsf{L}\circ{}\!\downharpoonleft_{1}
labels each pair
(q,v)\in\mathsf{Q}_{\beta({\mathsf{S}})}
by the variable
\mathsf{L}(q)
, if the latter
is defined.
Intuitively, we require of
\ell
that it assigns the same variable
to all “copies” of the same place
{\{{(q,v)\mid v\in\mathsf{V}_{\mathsf{S}}}\}}
.
Hence, the boolean value
[\![{\alpha}]\!]_{\mathsf{m}}^{\mathsf{L}\circ{}\!\downharpoonleft_{1}}
of an atomic proposition in a marking
\mathsf{m}
of

\beta({\mathsf{S}})
is obtained by mapping each free variable
x
from

\alpha
to the total number of tokens from a place
(q,v)\in\mathsf{Q}_{\beta({\mathsf{S}})}
such that
\mathsf{L}(q)=x
. This paper is
concerned with the following problem:

Definition 5

Translating
VR
to
HR
Systems

The existence of several verification techniques for parametric
systems with
HR
architectures and the scarsity of similar
results for
VR
systems motivates us to define a translation
from
VR
to
HR
systems, that preserves the answer of
the
\mathsf{PRP}
decision problem (
Definition

5
).
This translation uses expansion to encode dense graphs as sparse graphs
(see
Figure

2
).
Since labeled graphs are used as system models (
Definition

3
),
the behavior of a sparse graph must be equivalent to the behavior of its expansion,
on what concerns the satisfiability of reachability formulæ,
thus reducing each instance of the
\mathsf{PRP}
problem for a
VR

grammar to an instance of the same problem for an
HR
grammar.

Theorem 4.1

There exist computable
\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{H}}
and
\mathsf{L}^{\mathcal{H}}
,
process types and place labeling respectively,
such that for any
VR
-grammar
\Gamma
,
variable labeling
\mathsf{L}:\mathsf{Q}_{\mathbb{P}}\rightarrow\mathbb{X}

and reachability formula
\alpha
,
one can effectively build a
HR
-grammar
\Gamma^{\prime}

such that

\mathsf{PRP}_{\mathbb{P}}({\Gamma},{\alpha},{\mathsf{L}})\iff\mathsf{PRP}_{\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{H}}}({\Gamma^{\prime}},{\alpha},{\mathsf{L}^{\mathcal{H}}})

This result shows that it is possible to solve each instance
of the parametric model checking problem that takes a
VR

grammar as input by an effective reduction to an instance of the same
problem for a
HR
grammar, which, as previously mentioned, has
received more attention lately
[
9
]
.

Intuitively the translation works as follows:
vertices in the
HR
system are either
“real” vertices from the original
VR
system, or routing nodes.
A unique vertex of the
HR
system will act as representative of
all vertices currently
\pi
-ports in the
VR
system,
and communication between a
\pi
-port
v
and a
\pi^{\prime}
-port
v^{\prime}

in the
VR
system will in the
HR
system roughly take the form of
(1) a request from
v
to its representative
v_{\pi}
through a chain of routing nodes,
(2) similarly a request from
v^{\prime}
to its representative
v_{\pi^{\prime}}
,
(3) a direct communication between
v_{\pi}
and
v_{\pi^{\prime}}

(4) an acknowledgement from
v_{\pi}
to
v
following the inverse chain of routing nodes,
(5) an acknowledgement from
v_{\pi^{\prime}}
to
v^{\prime}
.
This is a simplification because in reality
steps (1–2) and (4–5) above may be interleaved,
and rather than a single representative there is
one representative per observable transition.

give an intuition of how they function,

4.1
Proof of
Theorem

4.1

The main idea is to use the
\epsilon_{t}
processes to define, for a

VR
-term
\theta
, a
HR
-term
\mathcal{H}(\theta)
that evaluates
to a sparse system, from which the system
\mathrm{val}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{VR}}}({\theta})
can be
retrived using graph expansion,
i.e.,
\mathrm{val}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{VR}}}({\theta})=\mathrm{exp}({\mathrm{val}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{HR}}}({\mathcal{H}(\theta)})})
(
Definition

1
).
Using the set of port labels

\Pi^{\mathcal{H}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{def}}}}{{=}}{\{{{\pi}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}},(\pi,t),\overline{(\pi,t)}\mid\pi\in\Pi,t\in\mathsf{T}^{\mathit{obs}}_{\mathrm{ptype}(\pi)}}\}}

where the process type of
{\pi}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}
is

{(\mathrm{ptype}(\pi))}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}
and the process type of
(\pi,t)
and

\overline{(\pi,t)}
is
\epsilon_{t}
,
we define
\mathcal{H}(\theta)
inductively on the structure of
\theta
.
That is, in addition to a
source label
{\pi}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}
for each port label
\pi\in\Pi

that occurs in
\theta
, we add two copies of each port label for each
observable transition. Intuitively,
(\pi,t)
will be the port
label denoting the root of the tree of
\epsilon_{t}
processes, and

\overline{(\pi,t)}
will be used as backup when we need a fresh
copy of
(\pi,t)
. We define a renaming function
\mathsf{fresh}:\Pi^{\mathcal{H}}\rightharpoonup\Pi^{\mathcal{H}}
by

\mathsf{fresh}(\overline{(\pi,t)})=(\pi,t)
, and undefined
everywhere else. That way
\mathsf{fresh}
substitutes each source for
its copy and forgets the original one.
We also use the renaming function

\mathsf{obs}:\Pi^{\mathcal{H}}\rightharpoonup\Pi^{\mathcal{H}}

which is the identity on
\Pi\times\mathsf{T}^{\mathit{obs}}_{\mathbb{P}}

and undefined everywhere else,

i.e.,
\mathsf{obs}
forgets every port label except the
(\pi,t)
labels.

5
Conclusions and Future Work

We consider the parametric reachability problem for families of
networks of finite-state processes specified using
VR
graph
grammars. Our approach is to reduce the problem to
HR
grammars,
for which several verification techniques have been developed
lately. The reduction is based on a construction of an
HR

grammar that expands to the language of a given
VR
grammar, by
elimination of epsilon-edges. We prove that this construction
preserves any reachability property expressed using first-order
arithmetic over variables that count the number of processes in each
state.

Future work involves an implementation of the proposed reduction,
based on existing tools for parametric verification of networks
specified using
HR
grammars
[
9

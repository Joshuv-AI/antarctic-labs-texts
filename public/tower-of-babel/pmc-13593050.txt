The safety and efficacy of different anti-tuberculosis regimens containing Lzd for RR/MDR/Pre-XDR-TB patients in China
Authors: Xian-Zhen Tang, Yao Ma, Shen-Jie Tang, Qing Chen, Li-Ping Zou, Xiao-Yan Fu, Tao Huang, Gui-Hui Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
China remains a high-burden country for rifampicin-resistant tuberculosis. The treatment of drug-resistant tuberculosis is expensive, with many adverse drug reactions and greater difficulty to cure. Searching for appropriate treatment regimens is urgent. A retrospective study was conducted on HIV-negative patients aged 18 to 60 years with rifampicin- or multidrug- or pre-extensively drug-resistant tuberculosis (RR/MDR/Pre-XDR-TB) at the Public Health Clinical Center of Chengdu, China from May 1, 2018 to July 31, 2022. Patients were divided into 3 groups: linezolid (Lzd) + bedaquiline (Bdq) containing group (regimen A), Lzd + delamanid (Dlm) containing group (regimen B), and Lzd + other drugs containing group (regimen C, not containing Bdq or Dlm). A total of 142 RR/MDR/Pre-XDR-TB patients were included (63 in regimen A, 34 in regimen B, and 45 in regimen C). At 2 months of treatment, 1 patient was lost to follow-up in regimen A and regimen C, respectively. At 6 months of treatment, 3 patients were lost to follow-up in regimen A and regimen C, respectively, and 2 patients were lost to follow-up in regimen B. The sputum culture conversion rates of RR/MDR/Pre-XDR-TB patients in regimen A, B, and C at the end of 2 and 6 months were (72.6% [45/62] vs 64.7% [22/34] vs 18.2% [8/44]) and (95.0% [57/60] vs 90.6% [29/32] vs 81.0% [34/42]), respectively (P < .05). 88.2% (30/34) of the Dlm group used Lzd-Dlm-clofazimine (Cfz)-cycloserine (Cs) containing regimens. 7.9% (5/63) of patients stopped using Bdq due to corrected QT interval by Fredericia >500 ms. 2.9% (1/34) of patients with corrected QT interval by Fredericia prolongation and an increase of 88 ms from baseline stopped Dlm. No patient experienced any malignant heart disease events. Due to related adverse events of Lzd, 14.1% (20/142) reduced Lzd dosage and 6.3% (9/142) had permanent discontinuation of Lzd. 6-month Lzd + (Bdq or Dlm) containing regimens were associated with favorable early culture conversion and showed good safety among HIV-negative patients aged 18 to 60 years with RR/MDR/Pre-XDR-TB. Lzd-Dlm-Cfz-Cs containing regimens may serve as promising candidate regimens for future research on rifampicin-resistant tuberculosis. Multicenter prospective studies with longer follow-up and definitive treatment outcomes are needed to establish comparative effectiveness or treatment superiority of these regimens.

1. Introduction

Tuberculosis (TB) is an infectious disease caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis infection, mainly transmitted through the respiratory tract. In the 2023 global tuberculosis report, it was estimated that there were 410,000 new multidrug-resistant/rifampicin-resistant tuberculosis (MDR/RR-TB) patients, with only about 2/5 of them receiving treatment.[1] The treatment success rate of MDR/RR-TB patients was globally 63% in 2020. Despite an increase from 60% in 2019,[1] the improvement is still slow. DR-TB poses a serious threat to public health. Previous diagnosis of TB and a history of anti-TB treatment are significant risk factors for MDR-TB.[2] Other common risk factors include low family income, human immunodeficiency virus infection, comorbidity with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, interrupted treatment, drug adverse reactions, poor compliance, and pulmonary cavity.[3–5] China remains a high-burden country for TB and MDR/RR-TB.[1] It is still urgent to search for effective and economical treatment regimens.

Linezolid (Lzd) is an oxazolidinone antimicrobial drug that inhibits bacterial protein synthesis in the early stages of translation.[6] Lzd presents antibacterial activity against both sensitive and DR-TB strains.[7] A study was previously conducted in vitro hollow fiber infection models and animal models, and the interaction between Lzd and bedaquiline (Bdq) was additive for bacterial killing in H37Rv and 18b strains.[8] Lzd also participates in the immunoregulation of infection.[9] Besides, Lzd exhibits good tissue penetration and is highly concentrated in the pulmonary cavity, caseous granuloma, and cerebrospinal fluid.[10] A systematic review and meta-analysis showed that the success rate of the Lzd-containing treatment regimen was 77.36%, and the culture conversion rate was 88.45%.[11] Additionally, Lzd has been used in the treatment of resistant TB and severe TB with tuberculous meningitis,[12] but the best treatment regimen is still worth exploring. Lzd was listed as the selected drug in group A according to the 2018 update of the WHO treatment guidelines for MDR/RR-TB.[13] New drug discoveries and advancements in clinical trials lead to evolving treatment options and drug selections. However, challenges arise due to diverse drug resistance, drug accessibility, and economic affordability for patients, complicating regimen formulation. Although WHO recommends the Bdq-pretomanid (Pa)-Lzd- moxifloxacin (Mfx) regimen where Pa is accessible, access to Pa remains restricted in many high TB-burden regions. Against such a context, the safety and effectiveness of different anti-TB regimens containing Lzd for RR/MDR/pre-extensively DR-TB (Pre-XDR-TB) patients in China were hereby explored.

2. Methods

2.1. Patients

A retrospective study was conducted on RR/MDR/Pre-XDR-TB patients diagnosed at the Public Health Clinical Center of Chengdu, China, from May 1, 2018 to July 31, 2022. As defined in the 2022 update of the WHO consolidated guidelines on TB (module 4: DR-TB treatment) in 2022,[14] Pre-XDR-TB is an infection caused by RR/MDR M tuberculosis strains that are resistant to any fluoroquinolones (FQs) (levofloxacin [Lfx] or Mfx) General demographic data, clinical characteristics, and chest imaging features were collected from patients on Lzd-containing anti-TB regimens. We recorded the time of sputum culture to negative and calculated the sputum negative conversion rate. Sputum culture conversion was defined as 2 consecutive negative sputum culture results, with a minimum interval of 28 days between each. The time of culture conversion was considered the time of the 1st negative sputum culture. The inclusion criteria were as follows: patients diagnosed with RR/MDR/Pre-XDR-TB according to bacteriological examination; RR/MDR/Pre-XDR-TB patients with positive baseline sputum culture; and HIV-negative patients aged between 18 and 60 years old. The exclusion criteria included patients with severe cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases, severe hepatic and renal dysfunction, pregnancy, or a malignant tumor. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Public Health Clinical Center of Chengdu, and informed consent was obtained from all patients involved. The safety and efficacy of different anti-TB regimens containing Lzd among RR/MDR/Pre-XDR-TB patients were observed for 6 months.

2.2. Tuberculosis bacteriological examination

M tuberculosis was cultured by the BACTEC MGIT 960 system of Becton, Dickinson and Company or Lowenstein–Jensen medium of Zhuhai Yinke Medical Engineering Co., Ltd, and drug sensitivity testing was conducted using the microplate method in vitro. The drug sensitivity test involved drugs including isoniazid, rifampicin, streptomycin, ethambutol, Lfx, Mfx, amikacin (Am), capreomycin, prothionamide, P-aminosalicylic acid, and clofazimine (Cfz). The testing results were used to determine RR/MDR/Pre-XDR-TB. Some patients were confirmed with RR-TB by GeneXpert MTB/RIF. Additionally, FQ resistance was diagnosed by the detection of mutations in the FQ resistance genes gyrA or gyrB. Due to limitations in conditions, the patients did not complete drug sensitivity testing with Lzd or Bdq, and this study about XDR-TB could not be clarified.

2.3. Antituberculosis regimens

The anti-TB regimens of optional drugs included Lzd, Bdq, Mfx, Lfx, cycloserine (Cs), Cfz, delamanid (Dlm), Am, capreomycin, prothionamide, P-aminosalicylic acid, pyrazinamide, and ethambutol, high-dose isoniazid, and amoxicillin–clavulanic acid. The anti-TB regimens were meticulously formulated, taking into account drug sensitivity test results, potential adverse reactions, and contraindications. Each regimen comprised at least 4 TB agents deemed likely to be effective. All patients’ anti-TB regimens involved Lzd in the study. Patients taking Bdq or Dlm were required to exclude respiratory failure, corrected QT interval by Fredericia (QTcF) >500 ms, with risks of QTcF prolongation, arrhythmias, and heart diseases. Patients taking Dlm were required to be excluded if their serum albumin was <2.8 g/dL. Patients taking Cs were required to be excluded if they had epilepsy, depression, severe anxiety, or other mental illnesses. Patients taking Cfz should be excluded if they had severe gastrointestinal diseases and were informed of adverse reactions to skin and mucosal staining. Their consent for use was obtained. The choice of anti-TB regimens was determined by a combination of multiple clinical factors, including results of drug susceptibility testing, prior TB treatment history, disease severity, patient tolerance, drug availability, and individual patient characteristics. Shared decision-making further integrated clinician and patient preferences into the final regimen choice. According to the anti-TB regimens, the patients were divided into 3 groups: the Lzd + Bdq containing group (regimen A), the Lzd + Dlm containing group (regimen B), and the Lzd + other drugs containing group (regimen C, not containing Bdq or Dlm). Lzd was used once a day, 600 mg each time.

2.4. Statistical analysis

Patient information about demographics, clinical characteristics, chest imaging, and sputum culture to negative condition was collected using an Excel table, and the statistical analysis was performed using IBM SPSS 27.0 (IBM Corporation). The measurement data were tested for normality, and their age and body mass index (BMI) did not conform to the normal distribution. Age and BMI were presented as median (quartile) (M [Q1, Q3]) and analyzed using the Kruskal–Wallis test. The enumeration data (gender, treatment history, diabetes, smoker, drinking, chest imaging, and Pre-XDR-TB) were expressed as a rate (%) and treated with the χ2 test or Fisher exact test. A P value < .05 indicates a statistically significant difference in the distribution of categorical variables between different groups. The time to sputum culture conversion was analyzed and visualized using Kaplan–Meier curves for the different treatment subgroups. The log-rank test was used to compare the statistical differences in sputum culture conversion rates between these groups.

3. Results

From May 1, 2018 to July 31, 2022, a total of 142 RR/MDR/Pre-XDR-TB patients were included in the experiment at the Public Health Clinical Center of Chengdu, China. 72 (50.7%) RR/MDR-TB and 70 (49.3%) Pre-XDR-TB patients were included. 38 (26.8%) patients were new cases, and 104 (73.2%) were retreated ones. Among those included, 21.1% (8/38) of new cases had a history of exposure to FQs (FQs used for >2 weeks), and 46.2% (48/104) of retreated cases had such a history (χ2 = 7.342, P = .007), presenting statistically significant differences (P < .05). Compared with RR/MDR-TB (20.8% [15/72]), the proportion of Pre-XDR-TB (58.6% [41/70]) with a history of exposure to FQs was higher (χ2 = 21.164, P < .001), exhibiting statistically significant differences (P < .05). Among the 3 anti-TB regimens containing Lzd, there were 63 patients in the Lzd + Bdq containing group, 34 in the Lzd + Dlm containing group, and 45 in the Lzd + other drugs containing group. No significant difference was observed among the 3 groups in age, gender, diabetes, smoking, drinking, and chest imaging (P > .05). Besides, there were significant differences in BMI, retreated cases, and Pre-XDR-TB among the 3 groups (P < .05), and the Lzd + Dlm containing group had the highest BMI (21.2 [19.2, 23.8] kg/m2) Among Pre-XDR-TB patients or retreated cases, the Lzd + Bdq containing group had a higher proportion (Table 1).

4. Discussion

Effective treatment of DR-TB relies on the joint application of several chemotherapeutic drugs and adequate courses. Although the full oral short course of Bdq-Pa-Lzd-Mfx treatment regimens is recommended by WHO based on existing evidence, there is a dearth of data from countries and regions beyond those where clinical trials were conducted.[14] Particularly, China lacks clinical data on related treatments due to limited accessibility to Pa. Therefore, effective and appropriate treatment regimens with accessible drugs should be necessarily delved into. How to combine old drugs with new ones and find the most suitable treatment regimens is still a clinical issue to be considered. This study preliminarily explored the safety and efficacy of regimens containing Lzd + Bdq or Lzd + Dlm, and Lzd + other drugs. Given that the conventional treatment course of Bdq and Dlm is 6 months, this study focused on evaluating the 6-month safety and efficacy outcomes in patients with RR/MDR/Pre-XDR-TB. The findings of this study provide preliminary evidence and a directional reference for further clinical research and optimal regimen selection in the future.

The addition of Bdq to an optimized background regimen showed a favorable outcome, improved sputum culture conversion, shortened sputum culture conversion time, and reduced TB-related mortality for DR-TB patients,[15,16] and presented a higher success rate in retreat cases.[17] In this study, the proportion of patients with bilateral lung lesions (77.8%), cavity (84.1%), retreat cases (85.7%), and Pre-XDR-TB (68.3%) was higher in the Lzd + Bdq containing regimen, and the treatments of these patients were refractory. However, our study found that in the early 2 months, the sputum culture conversion rate of the Lzd + Bdq containing regimen was the highest, reaching 72.6%, while that of the Lzd + other drug containing regimen was the lowest, accounting for only 18.2%. For 6 months, the sputum culture conversion rate was the highest in the Lzd + Bdq containing regimen. The Lzd + other drug containing regimen was formulated earlier, so traditional injection was mostly retained. Owing to the routine use of Am, which performed better in injection,[18] a certain effect was also achieved in the sputum culture conversion rate for 6 months, but it was not as good as the Lzd + Bdq containing regimen. Furthermore, it was evident that the more injection use group had a significant increase in ototoxicity. The use of BDQ and its combination with Lzd or Cfz or Cs were independent predictors of treatment success.[17] The selection of drugs for Cs and Cfz in the Lzd + Bdq containing regimen accounted for over 75%, further ensuring the treatment success rate. As observed, the Lzd + Bdq containing regimen was more advantageous, even in those refractory DR-TB patients.

There are limited reports on the treatment of RR-TB with Dlm. Dlm was reported to have early antibacterial activity in 14 days,[19] presenting a certain effect on RR-TB treatment in early clinical studies.[20] Whereas, the phase 3 trial found that the sputum culture conversion rates of Dlm plus optimized background regimen and placebo plus optimized background regimen had no statistically significant difference for 6 months (87.6% vs 86.1%).[21] So Dlm is currently in group C. However, the effectiveness of Dlm varied greatly in different studies, and the sputum culture conversion rates ranged from 67.6% to 96.6% at 6 months.[22] After all, Dlm is a new drug with a relatively low resistance rate,[23,24] and related regimens are still worth exploring. A Georgia observational study revealed that the 6-month sputum culture conversion rate for MDR-TB patients treated with the Bdq regimen was notably higher compared to the Dlm group (95% vs 74%).[25] This finding contrasted with the outcomes of the present study. Herein, it was found that RR-TB patients with or without FQ resistance achieved good sputum culture conversion rates of 95.0% and 90.6% for 6 months. Its effectiveness showed no significant statistical difference compared to the Lzd + Bdq group. Patients with bilateral lung lesions (76.5%), cavity (76.5%), and retreat case (64.7%) were prevalent in the study. The data showcased the beneficial effects of the Lzd + Dlm containing regimen. The success of this regimen might depend on the following factors: Firstly, the proportion of Cs and Cfz selected drugs was 94.1%, and most patients (88.2%) used a regimen containing Lzd-Dlm-Cs-Cfz, including 90.9% (10/11) Pre-XDR-TB. Secondly, all non-FQ resistant patients used Lfx in combination, which ensured better treatment outcomes. In order to reduce the impact on QTcF, Mfx was not selected. Thirdly, patients with HIV infection were not included. Finally, 79.4% (27/34) patients in the Lzd + Dlm regimens adopted fully oral regimens. It aligned better with WHO recommendations after 2022. The proportion of ototoxicity was also the lowest among the 3 groups, increasing patients’ tolerance to the regimen. Further and more extensive research is essential to validate whether the combination therapy containing Lzd-Dlm-Cs-Cfz could indeed represent a promising new option for RR-TB treatment. This study indicated that BMI was higher in the Dlm containing group at the baseline, which was attributed to Dlm’s requirements for nutritional status and albumin content. In recent years, there have been studies on the combination of Bdq and Dlm in the treatment of DR-TB, claiming to have achieved favorable efficacy and safety.[26] Moreover, the research on Dlm in children also demonstrated its good safety.[27] Exploring the development of Dlm containing drug-resistant regimens specifically for children is worthwhile. As a new drug, Dlm may be able to find a more optimized combination of DR-TB regimens to benefit DR-TB patients.

Adverse drug events are common in the treatment of DR-TB; most require intervention but can be managed. The most common adverse reactions to Lzd are myelosuppression, peripheral neuritis, and optic neuritis.[28] Our study found that 14.1% (20/142) of patients required a reduction in their Lzd dosage, and 6.3% (9/142) had to permanently discontinue Lzd usage due to adverse events experienced during the 6-month treatment period. The adverse reactions to Lzd are correlated with the dosage and duration of drug use. A daily dose of 600 mg seems suitable for balancing efficacy and toxicity.[29] A dosage of 300 mg/d can reduce adverse reactions but does not seem to reach the effective exposure dose.[30] More clinical data is needed to prove sufficient effectiveness. Mfx/Lfx, Bdq, Cfz, and Dlm are related to QTcF prolongation. This study indicated that the Bdq group (27.0% [17/63]) and Dlm group (8.8% [3/34]) showed a higher proportion of QTcF prolongation than the other group (2.2% [1/45]). The proportion of QTcF prolongation in the Bdq was 24.7%,[31] and that of Dlm was 2.4%[32] in some studies. Only 7.9% (5/63) of patients stopped using Bdq, and 2.9% (1/34) stopped using Dlm due to QTcF prolongation. No patient experienced any malignant heart disease events, indicating the relative safety and tolerance of the treatment regimens. As the proportion of patients experiencing QTcF prolongation was significantly lower in the Dlm group, it appeared that the risk of cardiac toxicity might be potentially lessened in the Dlm group when compared to the Bdq group. In this respect, the Dlm group seemed to have an advantage. Regular follow-up electrocardiograms were required at 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, and 24 weeks. Patients with significant QTcF prolongation experienced an increased frequency of electrocardiogram follow-up according to the situation. Before treatment, possible adverse and toxic reactions to each drug were introduced to the patients to enhance their preparation for potential adverse drug reactions, so that medical personnel could be promptly informed of their occurrence. Any adverse drug reaction should be closely monitored and properly and timely handled.

However, this study still had several limitations. Firstly, this is a retrospective, single-center study with a relatively small subgroup sample size and limited follow-up after 6 months, and there may be some other factors affecting the results. The single-center study design limits the external validity and generalizability of our findings. Patient baseline characteristics, drug resistance profiles, and combination treatment regimens vary across different cohorts, which may lead to heterogeneous treatment outcomes. Six-month sputum culture conversion is merely an important intermediate microbiological endpoint. However, it cannot be equated with WHO-defined treatment success, relapse-free cure, or long-term clinical prognosis. Secondly, in addition to the main drugs in the group, other drugs were also included, which may have a potential impact on the therapeutic effect. Thirdly, regimens for special populations such as people living with HIV, children, the elderly, and pregnant women were not explored. Since patients with severe cardiac, hepatic, or renal dysfunction were excluded at baseline, the safety profiles observed in the present cohort are only applicable to the studied population and cannot be generalized to all patients with DR-TB. Finally, the sample size was limited, so it was not completely divided into subgroups according to RR/MDR/Pre-XDR-TB, making it impossible to select treatment regimens more accurately for different types of DR-TB. More effective and well-tolerated new drugs should be developed in the future. Additionally, multicenter, prospective studies with larger sample sizes and longer follow-up periods should be conducted to identify the most suitable anti-TB treatment regimen for patients with RR/MDR/Pre-XDR-TB.

5. Conclusion

Both the 6-month Lzd + Bdq–containing regimen and the Lzd + Dlm–containing regimen were associated with a favorable early microbiological response in this cohort and showed good safety. The Lzd-Dlm-Cfz-Cs containing regimens may serve as promising candidate regimens for future research on RR-TB. Multicenter prospective studies with longer follow-up and definitive treatment outcomes are needed to establish comparative effectiveness or treatment superiority of these regimens. More rigorous research should be conducted to validate and explore their use in special populations, such as children and people living with HIV. Early drug resistance testing to comprehensively assess drug resistance, develop appropriate anti-TB treatment regimens, monitor adverse drug reactions throughout the treatment process, and improve patient compliance is of great significance to the success of DR-TB treatment. Furthermore, the advancement of new drugs and their accessibility are expected to promote the treatment of DR-TB.

a novel way of computing the shape derivative for a class of non-smooth pDEs and its impact on deriving necessary conditions for locally optimal shapes
L. Betz

Abstract
We derive necessary conditions for locally optimal shapes of a design problem governed by a non-smooth PDE. The main particularity of the state system is the lack of differentiability of the nonlinearity. We work in the framework of the functional variational approach (FVA) [ 39 ] , which has the capacity to transfer geometric optimization problems into optimal control problems, the set of admissible shapes being parametrized by a large class of continuous mappings. In the FVA setting, we introduce a sensitivity analysis technique that is novel even for smooth PDEs. We emphasize that we do not resort to extensions on the hold-all domain or any kind of approximation of the original PDE. The computation of the directional derivative of the state w.r.t. functional variations results in a new way of computing the shape derivative. The presented approach allows us to handle in the objective pointwise observation and derivatives of the state on an observation set as well as distributed observation terms. In addition, we introduce the concept of locally optimal shapes and we put into evidence its connection to locally minimizers of the corresponding control problem. With directional differentiability results for the control-to-state map at our disposal, we can then state necessary conditions for locally optimal shapes in general non-smooth settings.



keywords

Optimal control of non-smooth PDEs, shape and topology optimization, functional variational approach, Hamiltonian systems, pointwise observation, distributed observation, shape derivative, differential geometry.

We end the introduction by putting into evidence the differences between our technique and other comparable works. We start with the discrepancies with other papers in the context of the FVA. Though this particular method has often been used for differentiation purposes, the novelty in the present paper is that one does not alter the original PDE; we recall that our idea is to look at the sensitivity of the solution operator of (
1.3
) (with
\Omega=\Omega_{g}
) with respect to shape functions only, cf. (

[

,
26
,
25
,
24
]
, which yields an approximating PDE on the fixed domain. Other works
[

,
21
]
are based on the so-called Hamiltonian approach, which is more direct. Therein, an additional control variable defined on

is introduced so that the PDE is posed in the hold-all domain as well. However, the boundary condition appears as a state-control constraint and the constrained optimal control problem is in fact equivalent to the original shape optimization problem

,

We now turn our attention to the rest of the literature on the sensitivity analysis of problems on perturbed domains with emphasis on non-smooth problems

The typical idea is to introduce a smooth transformation of the domain, generated by a family of vector fields, which associates to each point in
\Omega
a unique point in the perturbed shape and viceversa. In this manner, the perturbed smooth domains are of the type

and has been used by numerous authors. We refer here in particular to

Another remarkable aspect about the aforementioned contributions is that, since they involve the computation of the so-called material derivative, they require the given right-hand side, in our case

). By contrast, we do not transfer the perturbed PDE to the original domain

Though our functional variational concept may seem different from the classical purely geometrical one

Finally, let us state an essential matter regarding the different perspective offered by the FVA when compared with the LSM.
In the LSM setting one employs the Hamilton Jacobi equation to update the level set function in numerical algorithms. This describes the evolution of

Assumption 7

(The non-smoothness)
.

1.

The function

is

2.

The mapping

\Big|\frac{\beta(y+\tau\,\delta y)-\beta(y)}{\tau}-\beta^{\prime}(y;\delta y)\Big|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau\searrow 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}0\quad\forall\,y,\delta y\in\mathbb{R}.

By Assumption
7
.
1
, it is straightforward to see that the Nemytskii operator

In addition,

\Big\|\frac{\beta(y+\tau\,\delta y)-\beta(y)}{\tau}-\beta^{\prime}(y;\delta y)\Big\|_{L^{\varrho}(\mathcal{A})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau\searrow 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}0\quad\forall\,y,\delta y\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{A}),\ \forall\,1\leq\varrho<\infty.

(3.3)

Lemma 19
.

Suppose that
g,h\in\mathcal{F}
and (
2.7
) is true. Then

\||q_{\lambda}-q|-m_{\lambda}\|_{L^{r}(\widetilde{\omega}_{\lambda})}\to 0,\quad\forall\,r\in[1,\infty),

where
\widetilde{\omega}_{\lambda}:=\{x\in\omega_{\lambda}:|q_{\lambda}-q|>m_{\lambda}\}

Proof.

Note that

q_{\lambda}\in W^{2,p}(\omega_{\lambda}),

by Definition
8
. In light of (
3.1
) (associated with

and
g+\lambda h
, respectively) and (

-\triangle(q_{\lambda}-q)+[\frac{[\beta(y_{g+\lambda h})-\beta(y_{g})]}{\lambda}-\beta^{\prime}(y_{g};q)]=0\quad\text{in }\omega_{\lambda}.

(3.27)

Observe that
(q_{\lambda}-q-m_{\lambda})^{+}\in H_{0}^{1}(\omega_{\lambda})
. This allows us to test the above equation with
(q_{\lambda}-q-m_{\lambda})^{+}
. We obtain

\int_{\omega_{\lambda}}[\nabla[q_{\lambda}-q-m_{\lambda}]^{+}]^{2}\,dx+\int_{\omega_{\lambda}}\underbrace{[\frac{[\beta(y_{g+\lambda h})-\beta(y_{g})]}{\lambda}-\beta^{\prime}(y_{g};q)]}_{=:A_{\lambda}}[q_{\lambda}-q-m_{\lambda}]^{+}\,dx=0.

(3.28)

Note that

A_{\lambda}=\frac{[\beta(y_{g+\lambda h})-\beta(y_{g}+\lambda q)]}{\lambda}+\frac{[\beta(y_{g}+\lambda q)-\beta(y_{g})]}{\lambda}-\beta^{\prime}(y_{g};q).

Due to the monotonicity of
\beta
(Assumption
7
.
1
) and since
q_{\lambda}-q>m_{\lambda}(>0)
implies

, we can conclude that the first term in

is non-negative on

This leads to

\int_{\omega_{\lambda}}[\nabla[q_{\lambda}-q-m_{\lambda}]^{+}]^{2}\,dx+\int_{\omega_{\lambda}}\Big[\frac{[\beta(y_{g}+\lambda q)-\beta(y_{g})]}{\lambda}-\beta^{\prime}(y_{g};q)\Big][q_{\lambda}-q-m_{\lambda}]^{+}\,dx\leq 0.

Since

can be extended by zero outside

(and it can thus be seen as an element in

), we can apply Poincaré’s inequality with a constant

\lambda
and obtain

\|[q_{\lambda}-q-m_{\lambda}]^{+}\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\lambda})}^{2}\leq c\,\Big\|\frac{[\beta(y_{g}+\lambda q)-\beta(y_{g})]}{\lambda}-\beta^{\prime}(y_{g};q)\Big\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\lambda})}\|[q_{\lambda}-q-m_{\lambda}]^{+}\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\lambda})}.

(3.30)

This yields

\|[q_{\lambda}-q-m_{\lambda}]^{+}\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\lambda})}\leq c\,\Big\|\frac{[\beta(y_{g}+\lambda q)-\beta(y_{g})]}{\lambda}-\beta^{\prime}(y_{g};q)\Big\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\lambda})}.

(3.31)

Adding
\|[q_{\lambda}-q-m_{\lambda}]^{+}\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\lambda})}^{2}
on both sides in (
3.29
) then leads to

\displaystyle\|[q_{\lambda}-q-m_{\lambda}]^{+}\|_{H^{1}(\omega_{\lambda})}^{2}

(3.32)

\displaystyle\leq\,\Big\|\frac{[\beta(y_{g}+\lambda q)-\beta(y_{g})]}{\lambda}-\beta^{\prime}(y_{g};q)\Big\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}(\omega_{\lambda})}\|[q_{\lambda}-q-m_{\lambda}]^{+}\|_{L^{r}(\omega_{\lambda})}

\displaystyle+\tilde{c}\|[q_{\lambda}-q-m_{\lambda}]^{+}\|_{L^{r}(\omega_{\lambda})}\|[q_{\lambda}-q-m_{\lambda}]^{+}\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\lambda})},

where

is arbitrary and

is the embedding constant of

; this is independent of
\lambda
, since
\mu(\omega_{\lambda})
is bounded by a constant independent of
\lambda
.
By using the same extension argument as above and the embedding
H^{1}(D)\hookrightarrow L^{r}(D),
as well as (
3.31
), we arrive at

\|q_{\lambda}-q-m_{\lambda}\|_{L^{r}(\omega_{\lambda}\cap\{q_{\lambda}-q>m_{\lambda}\})}\leq C\Big\|\frac{[\beta(y_{g}+\lambda q)-\beta(y_{g})]}{\lambda}-\beta^{\prime}(y_{g};q)\Big\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\lambda})},

(3.33)

where

is independent of
\lambda
.

In a similar manner, we obtain

\|q-q_{\lambda}-m_{\lambda}\|_{L^{r}(\omega_{\lambda}\cap\{q-q_{\lambda}>m_{\lambda}\})}\leq\widetilde{C}\Big\|\frac{[\beta(y_{g}+\lambda q)-\beta(y_{g})]}{\lambda}-\beta^{\prime}(y_{g};q)\Big\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{\lambda})},

(3.34)

where

is independent of
\lambda
, and we only sketch the arguments.

Testing (
3.27
) with

yields

\int_{\omega_{\lambda}}\nabla[q_{\lambda}-q]\nabla[q-q_{\lambda}-m_{\lambda}]^{+}\,dx+\int_{\omega_{\lambda}}\Big[\frac{[\beta(y_{g+\lambda h})-\beta(y_{g})]}{\lambda}-\beta^{\prime}(y_{g};q)\Big][q-q_{\lambda}-m_{\lambda}]^{+}\,dx=0,

(3.35)

which accounts to

(3.36)

Arguing as above we get that the first term in

is negative on

(since

Therefore

(3.37)

whence (
3.34

3.34
) and (

) finally gives the desired assertion.

4
Equivalence of the shape optimization problem with a control problem with admissible set consisting of functions

In this section we turn our attention to shape optimization problems governed by (

\left.\begin{aligned} \min_{\Omega\in\mathcal{O},E\subset\subset\Omega}\quad&\mathcal{J}(\Omega,y_{\Omega})\\

By arguing exactly as in the proof of

(4.1)

Note that the last condition in (

) ensures that

While the inclusion

[
7

7

In the sequel we abbreviate for simplicity

As in

\left.\begin{aligned} \min_{g\in\mathcal{F}_{E}}\quad&\mathcal{J}(\Omega_{g},y_{g})\\

Proposition 27
.

Assume that
\mathcal{J}
fulfills (

). Let
\Omega^{\star}\in\mathcal{O}_{E}
be an optimal shape of (

Ω

). Then, each of the functions

that satisfy

). Conversely, if

minimizes (

that contains
\bar{E}
is an optimal shape for (

P
Ω

).

Proof.

We follow the lines of the proof of
[
4
, Prop. 2.10]
.
Let
\Omega^{\star}\in\mathcal{O}
be an optimal shape for (

P
Ω

), i.e.,

\mathcal{J}(\Omega^{\star},y_{\Omega^{\star}})\leq\mathcal{J}(\Omega,y_{\Omega})\quad\forall\,\Omega\in\mathcal{O}_{E}.

Now, let

with

be fixed (note that, according to
[
4

Then,

Let

, be arbitrary and fixed; define

.

With the result in Proposition

Let

Proof.

Remark 31
.

The fundamental argument standing at the core of the proof of Theorem

is that if two parametrizations are "close" to each other, then their associated shapes will be "close" too. The reverse statement is however not true.
If two shapes are identical even, this does not mean that the same is true for their parametrizations, on the contrary:
for each

is a locally optimal shape for (

Ω

).

\{g^{\star}=0\}

in direction

In the next two subsections we investigate the directional differentiability of shape functionals

5.1
H^{2}
norms and pointwise observation on a given set

In this subsection we are concerned with shape functionals of the type

\mathcal{J}(y_{\Omega}):=\frac{1}{2}\|y_{\Omega}-y_{d}\|_{H^{2}(E)}^{2}+j_{obs}(y_{\Omega}),\quad E\subset\subset\Omega,

where

where

,

. Note that performance indices such as
j_{obs}
are of particular interest when the optimal shape must be chosen so that the state fits a desired value
y_{d}
which can be measured only pointwise (at
x^{k}_{obs}
) by a finite number of sensors
N
.

The corresponding reduced objective is

j(g)=\frac{1}{2}\|y_{g}-y_{d}\|_{H^{2}(E)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(y_{g}-y_{d})^{2}(x^{k}_{obs}),\quad g\in\mathcal{F}\text{ with }g<0\text{ in }\bar{E}.

We now intend to apply the differentiability result from Theorem
24
in order to obtain a formula for the directional derivative of

.

Proposition 33
.

Assume that
g,h\in\mathcal{F}
and (
2.7
) is true. Moreover, suppose that
\Omega_{g}\in\mathcal{O}_{E}
. Then
j
is directionally differentiable at
g
in direction
h
with

where

is the unique solution of (
3.25
)-(
3.26
) and

j_{obs}^{\prime}(g;h)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}(y_{g}-y_{d})(x^{k}_{obs})q(x^{k}_{obs}).

Proof.

The assumption
\Omega_{g}\in\mathcal{O}_{E}
implies
E\subset\subset\Omega_{g}
. From Theorem
24
we deduce that
\mathcal{S}:\mathcal{F}\to H^{2}(E)
is directionally differentiable with

, and in particular

\lim_{\lambda\searrow 0}q_{\lambda}(x^{k}_{obs})=q(x^{k}_{obs}),

where we use
H^{2}(E)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}(\bar{E})
and we abbreviate again
q_{\lambda}:=\frac{y_{g+\lambda h}-y_{g}}{\lambda}
. Applying the chain rule then leads to the desired statement.∎

With the result in the above theorem at hand we can provide a first-order necessary optimality condition in primal form for all locally optimal shapes of (

Proposition 34

5.2
Distributed observation

In this subsection, we are interested in the case

\mathcal{J}(\Omega,y_{\Omega}):=\int_{\Omega}J(y_{\Omega}(x))\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\psi(x)\,dx,

where
\psi\in\mathcal{C}(\bar{D})
and
J:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is Lipschitz continuous on bounded sets and directionally differentiable in the same sense as
\beta
. In particular, we suppose in the sequel that
for every
M>0
the estimate holds

\|J(y_{1})-J(y_{2})\|_{L^{r}(\mathcal{A})}\leq L_{J,M}\,\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|_{L^{r}(\mathcal{A})}\quad\forall\,y_{1},y_{2}\in\overline{B_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{A})}(0,M)},\ \forall\,1\leq r\leq\infty,

(5.4)

where
L_{J,M}>0
. In addition,

\Big\|\frac{J(y+\tau\,\delta y)-J(y)}{\tau}-J^{\prime}(y;\delta y)\Big\|_{L^{\varrho}(\mathcal{A})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau\searrow 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}0\quad\forall\,y,\delta y\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{A}),\ \forall\,1\leq\varrho<\infty.

(5.5)

Note that the above allows for tracking type functionals such as
J(y)=(y-y_{d})^{2},y_{d}\in\mathbb{R}.
Moreover,
J
might be non-smooth and typical examples entering this category are

J(y)=\max\{0,y-y_{d}\},\quad J(y)=|y-y_{d}|,\qquad y_{d}\in\mathbb{R}.

In this subsection, the reduced objective associated to (

P

) is given by

j(g)=\int_{\{g<0\}}J(\mathcal{S}(g)(x))dx+\underbrace{\int_{\{g<0\}}\psi(x)\,dx}_{=:\Psi(g)},\quad g\in\mathcal{F}

and, thanks to Theorem
26
, we have the following result.

Proposition 35
.

Assume that
g,h\in\mathcal{F}
and (
2.7
) is true. Moreover, suppose that
\Omega_{g}
is a domain. Then
j
is directionally differentiable at
g
in direction
h
with

j^{\prime}(g;h)=\int_{\{g<0\}}J^{\prime}(\mathcal{S}(g)(x);q(x))dx-\int_{\{g=0\}}{J(0)}\frac{h}{|\nabla g|}\,d\xi+\Psi^{\prime}(g;h),

where

is the unique solution of (
3.25
)-(
3.26
) and

\Psi^{\prime}(g;h)=-\int_{\{g=0\}}\psi\frac{h}{|\nabla g|}\,d\xi.

(5.6)

Proof.

By using
[
3

, we obtain

\frac{1}{\lambda}\Big\{\int_{\{g+\lambda h<0\}}\psi\,dx-\int_{\{g<0\}}\psi\,dx\Big\}\to-\int_{\{g=0\}}\psi\frac{h}{|\nabla g|}\,d\xi\quad\text{as }\lambda\searrow 0,

which yields (

We abbreviate for simplicity
y_{g+\lambda h}:=\mathcal{S}(g+\lambda h)
,
\lambda>0
small, and

. Then,
g+\lambda h\in\mathcal{F}
, cf. Lemma

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}{[j(g+\lambda h)-j(g)]}-\int_{\Omega_{g}}J^{\prime}(y_{g}(x);q(x))dx+\int_{g=0}J(\underbrace{y_{g}(\xi)}_{=0})\frac{h}{|\nabla g|}\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{g+\lambda h}\setminus\Omega_{g}}\frac{1}{\lambda}J(y_{g+\lambda h})\,dx-\int_{\Omega_{g+\lambda h}\setminus\Omega_{g}}\frac{1}{\lambda}J(0)\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega_{g}}\frac{1}{\lambda}(J(y_{g+\lambda h})-J(y_{g}+\lambda q))dx

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega_{g}}\frac{1}{\lambda}(J(y_{g}+\lambda q)-J(y_{g}))-J^{\prime}(y_{g};q)dx

\displaystyle\quad+\ {\int_{\Omega_{g+\lambda h}\setminus\Omega_{g}}\frac{1}{\lambda}J(0)\,d\xi+\int_{g=0}J(0)\frac{h}{|\nabla g|}\,d\xi}=:a_{\lambda}+b_{\lambda}+c_{\lambda}+d_{\lambda}.

Before we analize each term let us observe that, for
\lambda>0
small, it holds

\|y_{g+\lambda h}\|_{\mathcal{C}(D)},\|y_{g}+\lambda q\|_{L^{\infty}(D)}\leq M,

where

(as a result of (

,

From

(see Remark

\displaystyle|a_{\lambda}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{\Omega_{g+\lambda h}\setminus\Omega_{g}}|J(y_{g+\lambda h}(x))-J(y_{g}(x))|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq L_{J,M}\|q_{\lambda}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{g+\lambda h}\setminus\Omega_{g})}

\displaystyle\leq L_{J,M}\mu(\Omega_{g+\lambda h}\setminus\Omega_{g})^{\frac{\varrho-1}{\varrho}}\|q_{\lambda}\|_{L^{\varrho}(D)},\quad\varrho\in(1,\infty).

where in the second estimate we used (

Further, by employing again (

\displaystyle|b_{\lambda}|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega_{g}}\frac{1}{\lambda}|J(y_{g+\lambda h}(x))-J(y_{g}+\lambda q))|dx

\displaystyle|c_{\lambda}|\leq\int_{\Omega_{g}}\Big|\frac{J(y_{g}+\lambda q)-J(y_{g})}{\lambda}-J^{\prime}(y_{g};q)\Big|dx\to 0.

6
The FVA and the speed method

This final section aims at putting into evidence the strong connection between the FVA and the prominent speed method (also known as the velocity method)
[
36
]
. We present a rather formal approach which will lead us to the belief that the vector field
W
from Definition
14
generates the perturbations
\Omega_{g+\lambda h}
of the original domain
\Omega_{g}
in a similar way to the speed method. This supports our previous observation (Remark
16
) that (
3.25
)-(
3.26
) is the very same PDE that characterizes the shape derivative
[
36
, Eqs. (3.6), (3.8)]
.

Throughout this section,
g,h\in\mathcal{F}
are fixed and (
2.7
) is satisfied. For simplicity, we assume that
\Omega_{g}
is a (possibly multiply connected) domain. In all what follows,
\lambda_{0}>0
is fixed, small enough, so that the unicity of the approximating closed curves is guaranteed, cf. Proposition
4
.
Recall that
W:\{g=0\}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
was defined in subsection
3.2
as follows

W(x):=w((z^{-1}(x))\quad\forall\,x\in\{g=0\}^{\circ},\quad{\{g=0\}^{\circ}}\subset\{g=0\}

(6.1)

where
w\in\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{2})
is the unique solution of (
2.10
) and
z
describes the boundary of
\Omega_{g}
, that is, it solves (
2.2
).
For
\lambda\in[0,\lambda_{0})
we can analogously define
\mathcal{W}(\lambda,\cdot):\{g+\lambda h=0\}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}

\mathcal{W}(\lambda,x):=w(\lambda,(z_{\lambda}^{-1}(x))\quad\forall\,x\in\{g+\lambda h=0\}^{\circ},\quad{\{g+\lambda h=0\}^{\circ}}\subset\{g+\lambda h=0\}

(6.2)

where

w({\lambda},\cdot)=\lim_{\hat{\lambda}\to\lambda}\frac{z_{\hat{\lambda}}-z_{\lambda}}{\hat{\lambda}-\lambda}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{2})

(6.3)

is computed in a similar way to
w
for
\lambda>0
small enough, see the proof of
[
40
, Prop. 3.2]
. Hence, for fixed
t\in(0,\infty),

w(\lambda,t)
is the velocity vector at
z_{\lambda}(t)
of the trajectory
[0,\lambda_{0})\ni\lambda\mapsto z_{\lambda}(t)
. Note that
\mathcal{W}(0)=W
.

In the case of the speed method
[
36
, Sec. 2.9]
, the domain perturbations take place via
T_{\lambda}(\Omega_{g})
, where
T_{\lambda}:D\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the flow associated to a family of vector fields.
Assuming that there exists
\mathcal{W}(\lambda,\cdot)\in C^{0,1}_{c}(D;\mathbb{R}^{2})
an extension of
\mathcal{W}(\lambda,\cdot):\partial\Omega_{g+\lambda h}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
(denoted by the same symbol) so that
\lambda\mapsto\mathcal{W}(\lambda,\cdot)\in\mathcal{C}([0,\lambda_{0}),C^{0,1}_{c}(D;\mathbb{R}^{2}))
, let
T_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{W}}:D\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be its associated flow, i.e.,
T_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{W}}(X)=x(\lambda,X)
is the unique solution to

\frac{d}{d\lambda}x(\lambda,X)={\mathcal{W}}(\lambda,x(\lambda,X))\quad\lambda\in[0,\lambda_{0}),\quad x(0,X)=X\in D.

(6.4)

We are going to prove next that

\partial\Omega_{g+\lambda h}=T_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{W}}(\partial\Omega_{g}),\quad\quad\lambda\in[0,\lambda_{0}).

(6.5)

To this end, we introduce
another vector field which associates to each point on the boundary of
\Omega_{g}
a unique point on the boundary of the perturbed domain
\Omega_{g+\lambda h}
.
For
\lambda\in[0,\lambda_{0})
, this is given by the mapping
Z_{\lambda}:\{g=0\}\to\{g+\lambda h=0\}
defined as

Z_{\lambda}(x):=z_{\lambda}(z^{-1}(x))\quad\forall\,x\in\{g=0\}^{\circ},\quad{\{g=0\}^{\circ}}\subset\{g=0\}.

(6.6)

Clearly, it holds

Z_{\lambda}(z(t))=z_{\lambda}(t),\quad\forall\,t\in[0,T)

(6.7)

and
Z_{0}(x)=x.

For a point
X\in\partial\Omega_{g}
, we have with
t=z^{-1}(X)
the following

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\lambda}Z_{\lambda}(X)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\hat{\lambda}\to\lambda}\frac{Z_{\hat{\lambda}}(X)-Z_{\lambda}(X)}{\hat{\lambda}-\lambda}

(6.8)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\hat{\lambda}\to\lambda}\frac{z_{\hat{\lambda}}(t)-z_{\lambda}(t)}{\hat{\lambda}-\lambda}

(6.9)

\displaystyle=w(\lambda,t)={\mathcal{W}}(\lambda,Z_{\lambda}(X))\quad\lambda\in[0,\lambda_{0}),

(6.10)

where we use (
6.7
), (
6.2
) and (
6.3
).
Going back to (
6.4
), we see that

x(\lambda,X)=Z_{\lambda}(X)\quad\text{for }X\in\partial\Omega_{g},

since (
6.4
) is uniquely solvable.

Therefore, the points on the perturbed boundary
\partial\Omega_{g+\lambda h}
appear as flows of
\mathcal{W}
with initial condition on the original boundary.
We underline that our

is well-defined only on

(not in the whole

as in
[
36
]
); nevertheless, the entire domain is automatically perturbed, being the interior of a finite union of disjoint planar closed curves without self intersections (Proposition

Thus, we now have an additional (however rather formal) confirmation that the limit (

) we calculated in subsection

(Theorems

and

\lim_{\lambda\searrow 0}\frac{y_{T_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{W}}(\Omega_{g})}-y_{\Omega_{g}}}{\lambda},

that is, the shape derivative of

in direction
\mathcal{W}
.

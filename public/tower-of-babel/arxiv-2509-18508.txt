CaCuTe: Casual Cubic-Model Technique for Faster Optimization
Nazarii Tupitsa

Abstract
We establish a local 𝒪 ⁡ ( k − 2 ) \mathcal{O}(k^{-2}) rate for the gradient update x k + 1 = x k − ∇ f ​ ( x k ) / H ​ ‖ ∇ f ​ ( x k ) ‖ x^{k+1}=x^{k}-\nabla f(x^{k})/\sqrt{H\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|} under a 2 ​ H 2H -Hessian–Lipschitz assumption. Regime detection relies on Hessian–vector products, avoiding Hessian formation or factorization. Incorporating this certificate into cubic-regularized Newton (CRN) and an accelerated variant enables per-iterate switching between the cubic and gradient steps while preserving CRN’s global guarantees. The technique achieves the lowest wall-clock time among compared baselines in our experiments. In the first-order setting, the technique yields a monotone, adaptive, parameter-free method that inherits the local 𝒪 ⁡ ( k − 2 ) \mathcal{O}(k^{-2}) rate. Despite backtracking, the method shows superior wall-clock performance. Additionally, we cover smoothness relaxations beyond classical gradient–Lipschitzness, enabling tighter bounds, including global 𝒪 ⁡ ( k − 2 ) \mathcal{O}(k^{-2}) rates. Finally, we generalize the technique to the stochastic setting.

1
Introduction

In this work, we are interested in solving the unconstrained minimization problem
\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)

where
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is a twice-differentiable function with Lipschitz Hessian.

1.1
Motivation

The cubic regularized Newton (
CRN
) method
[
Griewank, 1981
]
naturally follows from the Lipschitz–Hessian assumption
[
Nesterov and Polyak, 2006
]
. Its update can be written implicitly as

H\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|(x^{k+1}-x^{k})=-\nabla f(x^{k})-\nabla^{2}f(x^{k})(x^{k+1}-x^{k})

where
H>0
is a constant,
{\bf I}
is the identity matrix.
When the quadratic term is negligible, i.e.

H\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{3}\gg\langle\nabla^{2}f(x^{k})(x^{k+1}-x^{k}),x^{k+1}-x^{k}\rangle

an approximate update formula reads as

H\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{3}\approx-\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),x^{k+1}-x^{k}\rangle\quad\text{or}\quad x^{k+1}\approx x^{k}-\frac{\nabla f(x^{k})}{\sqrt{H\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|}}

and raises a natural question:

Can we provably avoid Hessian evaluation and matrix inversion while retaining an
{\cal O}(k^{-2})
rate?

This affirmative result shows that the convergence is achievable with a first-order method and naturally leads to the next question.

How to utilize Hessian smoothness for the first order methods?

This question is central, since exact second-order steps are often infeasible in modern high-dimensional problems even when Hessian smoothness holds. Our approach leverages smoothness without forming or inverting Hessians. We also extend the framework to stochastic optimization, yielding practical algorithms for large-scale applications.

Notation and Main Assumptions

Our theory is based on the following assumption about second-order smoothness, which is also the key tool in proving the convergence of cubic Newton
[
Nesterov and Polyak, 2006
]
.

Assumption 2.1
.

There exists a constant
H>0
such that for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\displaystyle f(y)\leq f(x)+\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle\nabla^{2}f(x)(y-x),y-x\rangle+\frac{H}{3}\|y-x\|^{3},

(1)

\displaystyle\|\nabla f(y)-\nabla f(x)-\nabla^{2}f(x)(y-x)\|\leq H\|x-y\|^{2}.

(2)

Both inequalities hold if the Hessian of
f
is
2H
-Lipschitz, i.e.
\|\nabla^{2}f(x)-\nabla^{2}f(y)\|\leq 2H\|x-y\|
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The proof is given in
[
Nesterov and Polyak, 2006
, Lemma 1]
.

Assumption 2.2

(Convexity)
.

Function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is convex with, i.e.
\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
it holds

f(y)\geq f(x)+\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\rangle.

(3)

Assumption 2.3

(Bounded level set)
.

Let
x^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous. The sublevel set

\mathcal{L}_{0}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ f(x)\leq f(x^{0})\}

is bounded, i.e.,

D:=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{L}_{0}}\|x-x^{\star}\|<\infty,\qquad x^{\star}\in\arg\min f.

3
Descent Analysis

Since
f(x)
has
2H
-Lipschitz–Hessian, there exists
H_{k}\leq H
s.t.

\textstyle f(x^{+})\leq f(x^{k})+\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),x^{+}-x^{k}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle x^{+}-x^{k},\nabla^{2}f(x^{k})(x^{+}-x^{k})\rangle+\frac{H_{k}}{3}\lVert x^{+}-x^{k}\rVert^{3}.

By setting
x^{+}=x^{k}-\frac{\nabla f(x^{k})}{\sqrt{M_{k}\lVert\nabla f(x^{k})\rVert}}
for
M_{k}\geq H_{k}
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
we then obtain

\textstyle f\left(x^{k}-\frac{\nabla f(x^{k})}{\sqrt{M_{k}\lVert\nabla f(x^{k})\rVert}}\right)\leq f(x^{k})-\frac{2(1-\alpha)}{3\sqrt{M_{k}}}\lVert\nabla f(x^{k})\rVert^{3/2}\\
+\frac{1}{2M_{k}\lVert\nabla f(x^{k})\rVert}\left[\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),\nabla^{2}f(x^{k})\nabla f(x^{k})\rangle-\frac{4\alpha\sqrt{M_{k}}}{3}\lVert\nabla f(x^{k})\rVert^{5/2}\right].

(4)

The latter means that there exists

\textstyle M_{k}=\max{\left(H_{k},\frac{9\left\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),\nabla^{2}f(x^{k})\nabla f(x^{k})\right\rangle^{2}}{16\alpha^{2}\lVert\nabla f(x^{k})\rVert^{5}}\right)}

(5)

s.t.

\textstyle f\left(x^{k}-\frac{\nabla f(x^{k})}{\sqrt{M_{k}\lVert\nabla f(x^{k})\rVert}}\right)\leq f(x^{k})-\frac{2(1-\alpha)}{3\sqrt{M_{k}}}\lVert\nabla f(x^{k})\rVert^{3/2}.

(6)

Inequality (
5
) employs the Hessian–gradient product can be computed at (asymptotically) gradient cost, without forming the Hessian. Together with inequality (
6
), one can estimate local, directional Hessian–Lipschitz constant, which we do via first-order backtracking.

Case I (cubic-model decrease):
If
M_{k}=H_{k}
, then the above gives

\textstyle f(x^{k+1})\leq f(x^{k})-\frac{2(1-\alpha)}{3\sqrt{H_{k}}}\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|^{3/2},

(7)

which yields the usual cubic-model decrease and the
\mathcal{O}(k^{-2})
regime under
2.2
and
2.3

Case II (quadratic-model decrease):
If
M_{k}=\frac{9\left\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),\nabla^{2}f(x^{k})\nabla f(x^{k})\right\rangle^{2}}{16\alpha^{2}\lVert\nabla f(x^{k})\rVert^{5}}
then

\textstyle f\left(x^{k}-\frac{\nabla f(x^{k})}{\sqrt{M_{k}\lVert\nabla f(x^{k})\rVert}}\right)\leq f(x^{k})-\frac{8(1-\alpha)\alpha}{9L_{k}}\lVert\nabla f(x^{k})\rVert^{2},

(8)

where

\mathcal{O}(k^{-1})
regime under
2.2
and
2.3
and an additional smoothness assumption. Note that gradient

Algorithm

5
), a monotone, parameter-free, adaptive scheme.

Idea.
At each iteration,
CaCuAdGD
estimates the local Hessian smoothness
along the gradient direction
via a backtracking procedure that relies only on Hessian–vector products (no explicit Hessian computations). This yields an
automatic switch
between:
(i) a
bounded-Hessian
regime, where the quadratic model driven decrease holds; and (ii) a
Hessian-Lipschitz
(cubic) regime, where a cubic-model controls the remainder.

Remark 6.4
.

Since
\nabla^{2}f
is
2H
-smooth and, by monotonicity with
2.3
, the iterates stay in the compact sublevel set
\mathcal{L}_{0}
, we have

L\coloneqq\sup_{x\in\mathcal{L}_{0}}\|\nabla^{2}f(x)\|<\infty
,
so
\nabla f
is
L
-Lipschitz on
\mathcal{L}_{0}
.

6.4
Comparison with
AdGD

CaCuAdGD
uses gradient steps
x^{k+1}=x^{k}-\lambda_{k}\nabla f(x^{k})
with

\lambda_{k}=\min\Bigl\{\frac{16\alpha^{2}}{9L_{k}},\frac{1}{\sqrt{H_{k}\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|}}\Bigr\},

while
AdGD
employs

\lambda_{k}=\min\Bigl\{\frac{1}{2L_{k-1}},\sqrt{1+\theta_{k-1}}\lambda_{k-1}\Bigr\},\qquad\theta_{k-1}:=\frac{\lambda_{k-1}}{\lambda_{k-2}}.

Step size growth.
When the local smoothness improves sharply (i.e.,
L_{k}\ll L_{k-1}
),
AdGD
can increase
\lambda_{k}
only multiplicatively via
\sqrt{1+\theta_{k-1}}
, which may take several iterations to reach the new scale
L_{k}
. In contrast,
CaCuAdGD
adapts in a single step
H_{k}\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|
vanishes near a solution, so the step is governed by
L_{k}
(fast expansion to the local scale). Far from the solution, when
H_{k}\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|
is large, the cubic-model decrease (
7
) is guaranteed
which corresponds to a local
O(1/k^{2})
decay.

Choice of
\alpha
.
The parameter
\alpha\in(0,1)
control the trade-off between the two regimes. Smaller
\alpha
tightens
\tfrac{16\alpha^{2}}{9L_{k}}
and triggers the quadratic regime earlier (more conservative). Whereas larger
\alpha
loosens it, making the steps larger and lets the cubic regime act more often. At the same time, the decrease in the quadratic regime scales with
\alpha(1-\alpha)
(
8
), so increasing
\alpha
beyond
1/2
reduces this per-step guarantee. Because the time spent in each regime is problem dependent, selecting
\alpha
a priori is nontrivial. Our analysis allows any
\alpha\in(0,1)
; in practice, values slightly below
3/4
(i.e. 0.7) perform consistently well on various problems, making the method effectively parameter free.
Also, in the quadratic regime, the allowed step (the constant multiplying
1/L_{k}
) is larger for
CaCuAdGD
(
\approx 0.87
), compared with
AdProxGD

[
Malitsky and Mishchenko, 2024
]
(
1/\sqrt{2}
) and
AdGD
(
0.5
).

7
Stochastic Extension

In this section we consider

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)=\mathbb{E}_{\xi}f_{\xi}(x),

particularly, a finite sum minimization
f(x)=\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(x)

where each
f_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is a per-sample loss.
Here,
\xi
denotes a (multi)set of sampled indices
S\subseteq[n]
(a mini-batch), so
f_{\xi}(x)\equiv f_{S}(x)=\tfrac{1}{|S|}\sum_{i\in S}f_{i}(x)
.

Assumption 7.1
.

Stochastic gradient
g(x,\xi)
is an unbiased estimator of
\nabla f(x)
with bounded variance, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[g(x,\xi)]=\nabla f(x)
and for
\sigma_{g}\geq 0

\textstyle\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\left\lVert g(x,\xi)-\nabla f(x)\right\rVert^{2}\leq\sigma_{g}^{2},

(17)

stochastic Hessian
H(x,\xi)
has bounded third moment

\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\lVert H(x,\xi)-\nabla^{2}f(x)\rVert^{3}\leq\sigma_{H}^{3}.

(18)

Moreover,
f_{\xi}
is
L
-smooth, i.e.,
\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\textstyle\lVert H(x,\xi)\rVert\leq L.

(19)

Also,
f
and
f_{\xi}

2H
-smooth Hessian , i.e.,
\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\textstyle f_{(\xi)}(x)\leq f_{(\xi)}(y)+\left\langle\nabla f_{(\xi)}(y),x-y\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\nabla^{2}f_{(\xi)}(y)(x-y),x-y\right\rangle+\frac{H}{3}\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert^{3}.

(20)

These assumptions are standard
[
Gorbunov et al., 2020
,
Chayti et al., 2023
]
.

Remark 7.2
.

In the algorithm we
do not
form
H(x,\xi)
explicitly; we only require stochastic Hessian–gradient products
H(x,\xi)g(x,\xi)
. Since, to the best of our knowledge, there is no standard assumption tailored exactly to this product, we use the standard third-moment bound above. However, our analysis also suffices under the following non-standard second-moment bound:

\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\left[\|(\nabla^{2}f(x)-H(x,\xi))g(x,\xi)\|^{2}\right]\leq\sigma_{P}^{2}.

For brevity we write
g^{k}\coloneqq g(x^{k},\xi_{k})
and
H^{k}\coloneqq H(x^{k},\xi_{k})
. The stochastic extension is given by the following

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
7.1
holds. Assume that
\widehat{L}\geq L
and
\widehat{H}\geq H
. Then the method (
CaCuSGD
)

x^{k+1}=x^{k}-\frac{g^{k}}{\sqrt{M_{k}\lVert g^{k}\rVert}},\quad M_{k}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\widehat{L}^{2}{\lVert g^{k}\rVert}^{-1},&\text{if}\quad{4\langle g^{k},H^{k}g^{k}\rangle^{2}}{\lVert g^{k}\rVert^{-5}}\geq\widehat{H},\\
\widehat{H},&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}\quad\text{satisfies}

\displaystyle\text{ (i) }\mathbb{E}f(x^{k+1})\leq\mathbb{E}f(x^{k})-\frac{1}{4\widehat{L}}\mathbb{E}\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|^{2}+\Big(\frac{2L}{\widehat{L}^{2}}+\frac{2L^{2}}{3\widehat{L}^{3}}\Big)\sigma_{g}^{2}+\frac{\sigma_{H}^{3}}{6\widehat{H}^{2}},

\displaystyle\text{if}\quad{4\langle g^{k},H^{k}g^{k}\rangle^{2}}{\lVert g^{k}\rVert^{-5}}\geq\widehat{H},

\displaystyle\text{(ii) }\mathbb{E}f(x^{k+1})\leq\mathbb{E}f(x^{k})-\frac{1}{3\sqrt{\widehat{H}}}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|^{3/2}\right]+\frac{7}{4\sqrt{\widehat{H}}}\sigma_{g}^{3/2}+\frac{32}{3\widehat{H}^{2}}\sigma_{H}^{3},

\displaystyle\text{otherwise}.

Telescoping these one-step bounds is the only remaining step to obtain the standard-form result. However, because the iterates may switch regimes and the stochastic error terms mix, we state the per-iteration bounds as above.

The two regimes mirror the stochastic decrease results for standard
SGD

[
Gorbunov et al., 2020
]
and stochastic
CRN

[
Chayti et al., 2023
]
. The cubic phase provide irreducible but lower-power variance floor
\sigma_{g}^{3/2}
, while the quadratic phase reproduces the
SGD
behavior: increasing
\widehat{L}
(i.e., using a smaller stepsize) reduces the variance-driven term, enabling convergence to arbitrary accuracy. In practice, the cubic regime shows a faster transient toward the noise floor.

\bigl(f(x^{k})-f_{\star}\bigr)^{1/2}=\frac{3}{2}\frac{LD^{2}}{\sqrt{HD^{3}}}
is the unique solution of

\frac{2}{9LD^{2}}\bigl(f(x^{k})-f_{\star}\bigr)^{2}=\frac{1}{3\sqrt{HD^{3}}}\bigl(f(x^{k})-f_{\star}\bigr)^{3/2},

which determines which of the two decreases is smaller; the stated cases follow, as does the definition of
K_{1}
.

Detailed derivation.

If
(f(x^{k})-f_{\star})^{1/2}\geq\frac{3}{2}\frac{LD^{2}}{\sqrt{HD^{3}}}
then

f(x^{k+1})\leq f(x^{k})-\frac{2}{9L_{k}}\lVert\nabla f(x^{k})\rVert^{2}\leq f(x^{k})-\frac{2}{9L}\lVert\nabla f(x^{k})\rVert^{2}\leq f(x^{k})-\frac{2}{9LD^{2}}\left(f(x^{k})-f_{\star}\right)^{2}\\
\leq f(x^{k})-\frac{1}{3\sqrt{HD^{3}}}\left(f(x^{k})-f_{\star}\right)^{3/2}

coincides with

f(x^{k+1})\leq f(x^{k})-\frac{1}{3\sqrt{H_{k}}}\lVert\nabla f(x^{k})\rVert^{3/2}\leq f(x^{k})-\frac{1}{3\sqrt{H}}\lVert\nabla f(x^{k})\rVert^{3/2}\leq f(x^{k})-\frac{1}{3\sqrt{HD^{3}}}\left(f(x^{k})-f_{\star}\right)^{3/2}.

This implies that

f(x^{k+1})\leq f(x^{k})-\frac{1}{3\sqrt{HD^{3}}}\left(f(x^{k})-f_{\star}\right)^{3/2}

for any value of
M_{k}
.

Similarly if
(f(x^{k})-f_{\star})^{1/2}\leq\frac{3}{2}\frac{LD^{2}}{\sqrt{HD^{3}}}
then

f(x^{k+1})\leq f(x^{k})-\frac{2}{9L_{k}}\lVert\nabla f(x^{k})\rVert^{2}\leq f(x^{k})-\frac{2}{9L}\lVert\nabla f(x^{k})\rVert^{2}\leq f(x^{k})-\frac{2}{9LD^{2}}\left(f(x^{k})-f_{\star}\right)^{2}

and

f(x^{k+1})\leq f(x^{k})-\frac{1}{3\sqrt{H_{k}}}\lVert\nabla f(x^{k})\rVert^{3/2}\leq f(x^{k})-\frac{1}{3\sqrt{H}}\lVert\nabla f(x^{k})\rVert^{3/2}\leq f(x^{k})-\frac{1}{3\sqrt{HD^{3}}}\left(f(x^{k})-f_{\star}\right)^{3/2}\\
\leq f(x^{k})-\frac{2}{9LD^{2}}\left(f(x^{k})-f_{\star}\right)^{2}.

Thus for any
M_{k}

f(x^{k+1})\leq f(x^{k})-\frac{2}{9LD^{2}}\left(f(x^{k})-f_{\star}\right)^{2}.

∎

Theorem C.6
.

,

,

Or when the algorithm reaches non-accelerated regime (

Proof.

Let
\xi_{k}\coloneqq f(x^{k})-f_{\star}
. The inequalities (

) and (

\xi_{k}-\xi_{k+1}>0,

C.4
Relaxed smoothness

Lemma C.7

(Scalar logistic bound)
.

For all
s\in\mathbb{R}
,

\sigma(s)\sigma(-s)\leq\frac{2}{3\sqrt{3}}\sqrt{\log\bigl(1+e^{-s}\bigr)},

where
\sigma(s)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-s}}

Proof.

Let
t=e^{-s}>0
. Then

\sigma(s)\sigma(-s)=\frac{t}{(1+t)^{2}},\qquad\log\bigl(1+e^{-s}\bigr)=\log(1+t).

We use the elementary inequality

\log(1+t)\geq\frac{t}{1+t}\qquad\text{for all }t>0,

which follows from the convexity of
-\log
or by defining

\phi(t)=\log(1+t)-\frac{t}{1+t}
and noting
\phi^{\prime}(t)=\frac{t}{(1+t)^{2}}\geq 0
with
\phi(0)=0
.
Hence

\frac{\sigma(s)\sigma(-s)}{\sqrt{\log(1+t)}}=\frac{t}{(1+t)^{2}\sqrt{\log(1+t)}}\leq\frac{t}{(1+t)^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{t/(1+t)}}=\frac{\sqrt{t}}{(1+t)^{3/2}}\leq\frac{2}{3\sqrt{3}}.

Therefore,

\sigma(s)\sigma(-s)\leq\frac{2}{3\sqrt{3}}\sqrt{\log(1+t)}=\frac{2}{3\sqrt{3}}\sqrt{\log\bigl(1+e^{-s}\bigr)}.

∎

Example C.8

(Logistic function,
L_{0}=0
)
.

Let

By

\frac{\langle\nabla f,\nabla^{2}f\nabla f\rangle}{\lVert\nabla f\rVert^{2}}\leq\frac{2}{3\sqrt{3}}\lVert a\rVert^{2}\sqrt{f(x)}=\frac{2}{3\sqrt{3}}\lVert a\rVert^{2}\sqrt{f(x)-f_{\star}},

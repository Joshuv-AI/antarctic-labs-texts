Abstract
. We study interior estimates for solutions of the linear Poisson equation: △ ​ u = g ​ u + f \triangle u=gu+f where g g and f f belong to the Zygmund space L ​ ln ⁡ L L\ln L on a Riemann surface M M satisfying the isoperimetric inequality. As applications, we derive corresponding interior estimates, Harnack inequalities, and a global estimate.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbf{R}^{2}
. Using Moser’s iteration, one can derive the Harnack inequality for a linear uniformly elliptic equation

\operatorname{div}(A\nabla u)=gu+f~~{\rm in}~~\Omega

in the case that
g,f\in L^{q}(\Omega)
for some
q>1
. Here, no regularity assumption is made on the coefficients. For details, see
[
5
]
or
[
7
]
.

Let
M
be a Riemann surface. We say that
M
satisfies the isoperimetric inequality if there exists
A>0
such that

\displaystyle V(\Omega)\leq Al(\partial\Omega)^{2}

(1.1)

for any open domain
\Omega\subset M
with rectifiable boundary.

The isoperimetric inequality implies the Sobolev inequality (see
[
6
]
):

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{q\sqrt{A}}{2}V(\Omega)^{\frac{1}{q}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

(1.2)

for all
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,
q\geq 1
and all smooth domains
\Omega\subset M
.

One can check that the Harnack inequality in
[
5
]
can be generalized to a Riemann surface satisfying the isoperimetric inequality.

It is natural to ask whether we can apply Moser’s iteration to
g,f
in the Hardy space
\mathscr{H}^{1}
or the Zygmund class
L\ln L
to derive the corresponding Harnack inequality.
To the best of our knowledge, we have not found any result in this direction.

Take a smooth function
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{2})
with
0\leq\eta\leq 1
and
\eta\equiv 1
on
B_{1}
.
For
k>10
, set
y_{k}=(\frac{4}{k},\frac{4}{k})
and

\displaystyle f_{k}(x)=k^{2}\eta(k(x-y_{k}))-k^{2}\eta(k(x+y_{k})).

By the theory of
\mathscr{H}^{1}
-atoms (see
[
2
]
), we know that
\frac{f_{k}}{36\pi}
is a standard
\mathscr{H}^{1}
-atom in
B_{\frac{6}{k}}
and then

\|f_{k}\|_{\mathscr{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq 36\pi.

Set
A_{k}(x)=1+\eta(\frac{k(x+y_{k})}{2})
and let
u_{k}
be the solution to
A_{k}\triangle u_{k}(x)=f_{k}(x)
with
u_{k}(\infty)=0
.
Then from the choice of
\eta
, it is easy to see that

\triangle u_{k}(x)=\frac{f_{k}(x)}{A_{k}(x)}=k^{2}\eta(k(x-y_{k}))-\frac{k^{2}}{2}\eta(k(x+y_{k})),u_{k}(\infty)=0.

Now, straightforward computations yield

\displaystyle|u_{k}(0)|=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\ln|y|[k^{2}\eta(k(y-y_{k}))-\frac{k^{2}}{2}\eta(k(y+y_{k}))]dy\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k^{2}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\ln\frac{1}{|y|}\eta(k(y-y_{k}))dy

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{k^{2}}{2}\int_{|y-y_{k}|<\frac{1}{k}}\ln\frac{1}{|y|}dy

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2}\ln\frac{k}{5}.

Therefore, for a general linear uniformly elliptic equation with the nonhomogeneous term
f
in the Hardy space, we cannot expect to obtain an interior estimate if no regularity is assumed on the coefficients.
However, it is known (c.f.
[
4
]
) that if
u
is a solution of
\triangle u=f
with
f
in
\mathscr{H}^{1}
, then
u
is continuous and uniformly bounded.

Here we consider the following Poisson equation with potential

\triangle u=gu+f

(1.3)

on a Riemann surface
M
.

Before presenting the main theorems, we recall some well-known function spaces.

When the measure of
X
is finite, the Zygmund class
L\ln L(X)
consists of all functions
f
for which

\|f\|_{L\ln L(X)}=\int_{X}|f(x)|\max\{0,\ln|f(x)|\}dx<\infty.

L\ln L(X)
is a natural generalization of
L^{q}(X)
for
q>1
.

Note that
\|\cdot\|_{L\ln L(X)}
is not a norm. So, we need a suitable norm in
L\ln L(X)
.
Let
f^{*}
be the non-increasing rearrangement of
f
defined by

f^{*}(t)=\inf\{s\geq 0:|\{x:|f(x)|>s\}|\leq t\}

and

\displaystyle\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(X)}=\int^{\infty}_{0}f^{*}(t)\ln\frac{|X|}{t}dt.

By
[
1
, Theorem 6.30]
, we know that
\|\cdot\|^{*}_{L\ln L}
is a norm on
L\ln L(X)
. It is easy to see that
\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(X_{1})}\leq\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(X)}
if
X_{1}\subset X
.

Let
\Phi
be a function in the Schwartz space
S(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfying
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Phi(x)dx=1
and set
\Phi_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{t^{n}}\Phi(\frac{x}{t})
.
Following Stein
[
9
, p. 91]
, we can define the Hardy space
\mathscr{H}^{1}
as the space of all tempered distributions
f
satisfying

\|f\|_{\mathscr{H}^{1}}=\|\sup\limits_{t>0}|f\ast\Phi_{t}|\|_{L^{1}}<\infty.

Let
B_{r}(x)
be the geodesic disk in
M
with centre
x
and radius
r
. The bounded mean oscillation space
BMO(M)
consists of all functions
f
for which

\displaystyle\|f\|_{BMO}=\sup_{B_{r}(x)\subset M}\frac{1}{V(B_{r}(x))}\int_{B_{r}(x)}|f(y)-\frac{\int_{B_{r}(x)}f(z)dz}{V(B_{r}(x))}|dy<\infty.

Let
K_{M}
be the Gauss curvature of
M
. Sometimes we assume that there exist
p>1
and
A>0
which depend only on
M
such that

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{x\in M}\|K_{M}\|_{L^{p}(B_{1}(x))}\leq A.

(1.4)

For brevity, we have used the same letter
A
in this expression as in (
1.1
) to denote a positive constant. Indeed, provided

A
is chosen sufficiently large, we can always replace the constants in both (
1.1
) and (
1.4
) with a single universal constant
A
.

For the sake of convenience, we assume from now on that
u,f,g
are smooth in the relevant domains. Our main result is the following interior estimate.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
M
satisfies (
1.1
) and (
1.4
). If
B_{1}
is a unit geodesic ball in
M
,
f,g\in L\ln L(B_{1})
and
u
is a solution of (
1.3
),
then

\|u\|_{C(B_{\frac{1}{2}})}\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{1}(B_{1})}+\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}),

where
C
depends only on
A,p
and the structure of
g
.

Remark 1.
One can check that we can replace
f\in L\ln L(B_{1})
by
f\in\mathscr{H}^{1}_{at}(B_{1})
in this theorem where
\mathscr{H}^{1}_{at}(B_{1})
is the atomic Hardy space
defined in
[
2
]
. If
M=\mathbb{R}^{2}
, then the atomic Hardy space is the usual Hardy space.

As special cases of Theorem
1.1
, we can obtain the following theorems, which are interesting and new to us.

Proposition 1.2
.

Suppose that the surface
M
satisfies (
1.1
) and (
1.4
). If
B_{1}
is a unit geodesic ball in
M
,
f\in L\ln L(B_{1})
and

\triangle u(x)=f(x),\quad x\in B_{1},

then

\|u\|_{C(B_{\frac{1}{2}})}\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{1}(B_{1})}+\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}),

where
C
depends only on
A,p
.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
B_{1}
be the unit disk in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
f,g\in L\ln L(B_{1})
and

\triangle u(x)=g(x)u(x)+f(x),\quad x\in B_{1}.

Then

\|u\|_{C(B_{\frac{1}{2}})}\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{1}(B_{1})}+\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}),

where
C
depends only on the structure of
g
.

Remark 2.
In particular, if
g\in L^{q}(B_{1})
for some
q>1
, then we can take the constant
C
in Proposition
1.3
to be
C_{q}(1+\|g\|_{q})^{q_{1}}
for any
q_{1}>\frac{4q}{q-1}

which recovers the known result.

As applications, we can give a simple proof of the following Harnack inequality.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
B_{1}
be the unit disk in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. If
f\in L\ln L(B_{1})
,
u\geq 0
and

\triangle u(x)=f(x),\quad x\in B_{1},

then we have the following Harnack inequality:

\max\limits_{x\in B_{\frac{1}{2}}}u(x)\leq C(\min\limits_{y\in B_{\frac{1}{2}}}u(y)+\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}).

Since
f\in L\ln L(B_{1})
implies
f-\frac{\int_{B_{1}}fdx}{\pi}\in\mathscr{H}^{1}
(see
[
8
]
), we can extend Corollary
1.4
as follows:

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
B_{1}
be the unit disk in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. If
f\in\mathscr{H}^{1}(B_{1})
,
u\geq 0
and

\triangle u(x)=f(x),x\in B_{1},

then we have the following Harnack inequality:

\max\limits_{x\in B_{\frac{1}{2}}}u(x)\leq C(\min\limits_{y\in B_{\frac{1}{2}}}u(y)+\|f\|_{\mathscr{H}^{1}(B_{1})}).

Finally, we prove the following global estimate for Poisson equations on Riemann surfaces.

Theorem 1.6
.

Suppose that
M
satisfies (
1.1
) and (
1.4
). If
B_{1}
is a unit geodesic ball in
M
,
u\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{1})
,
f\in L\ln L(B_{1})
and

\triangle u(x)=f(x),x\in B_{1},

then

\|u\|_{C(B_{1})}\leq C\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})},

where
C
depends only on
A,p
.

Remark 3.
Unlike the case discussed in Remark 1, we cannot replace
f\in L\ln L(B_{1})
by
f\in\mathscr{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{at}}(B_{1})
in the proof of this theorem.

In Section 2 we derive some results for the surface
M
. We prove Theorem
1.1
in Section 3, two Harnack’s inequalities in Section 4 and
Theorem
1.6
in Section 5.

In what follows, the constant
C
denotes a generic positive constant that may change from line to line,
but depends only on
A
,
p
, and the structure of
g
.

2.
Some lemmas

For any
x_{0}\in M
, by the semi-geodesic coordinate grid around
x_{0}
we can write the first fundamental form as

dr^{2}+G^{2}(r,\theta)d\theta^{2}
where

r>0,0\leq\theta<2\pi,G(r,\theta)\geq 0,G(0,\theta)=0,\partial_{r}G(0,\theta)=1.

In this note,
G(r,\theta)
may vary for different
x_{0}
. But one can see that all the constants are uniformly in
x_{0}
. So we use the same symbol
G(r,\theta)
for all points.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
M
satisfies the isoperimetric inequality (
1.1
), then for any

x_{0}\in M,r>0
, there holds

\displaystyle V(B_{r}(x_{0}))\geq\frac{r^{2}}{4A},\quad l(\partial B_{r}(x_{0}))\geq\frac{r}{2A}.

(2.1)

Proof.

From (
1.1
), we have

\frac{d}{dr}V(B_{r}(x_{0}))=l(\partial B_{r}(x_{0}))\geq\sqrt{\frac{V(B_{r}(x_{0}))}{A}}

which yields that

\frac{d}{dr}\sqrt{V(B_{r}(x_{0}))}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{4A}}.

So, one obtain that
V(B_{r}(x_{0}))\geq\frac{r^{2}}{4A}
and
l(\partial B_{r}(x_{0}))\geq\frac{r}{2A}
from (
1.1
). This finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

If
M
satisfies (
1.4
), then for any
x_{0}\in M,0<r<1
, there holds

\displaystyle V(B_{r}(x_{0}))\leq(2\pi+A)^{p+1}r^{2},l(\partial B_{r}(x_{0}))\leq(2\pi+A)^{p+1}r.

(2.2)

Proof.

Note that
K_{M}(r,\theta)=-\frac{\partial^{2}_{rr}G(r,\theta)}{G(r,\theta)}
. When
r<1
, by (
1.4
) we have

\displaystyle l(\partial B_{r}(x_{0}))

\displaystyle=\int^{2\pi}_{0}G(r,\theta)d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\int^{2\pi}_{0}\int^{r}_{0}|\partial_{r}G(r,\theta)|dtd\theta

\displaystyle\leq\int^{2\pi}_{0}\int^{r}_{0}\left(1+\int^{t}_{0}|\partial^{2}_{rr}G(s,\theta)|ds\right)dtd\theta

\displaystyle=2\pi r+\int^{2\pi}_{0}\int^{r}_{0}|\partial^{2}_{rr}G(s,\theta)|(r-s)dsd\theta

\displaystyle\leq 2\pi r+r\int^{2\pi}_{0}\int^{r}_{0}|K_{M}(s,\theta)|G(s,\theta)dsd\theta

\displaystyle=2\pi r+r\|K_{M}\|_{L^{1}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}

\displaystyle\leq 2\pi r+ArV(B_{r}(x_{0}))^{1-\frac{1}{p}}.

(2.3)

Therefore, we can obtain that

\displaystyle V(B_{r}(x_{0}))

\displaystyle=\int^{r}_{0}l(\partial B_{t}(x_{0}))dt

\displaystyle\leq\int^{r}_{0}(2\pi t+AtV(B_{t}(x_{0}))^{1-\frac{1}{p}})dt

\displaystyle\leq\pi r^{2}+\frac{Ar^{2}V(B_{r}(x_{0}))^{1-\frac{1}{p}}}{2}.

When
V(B_{r}(x_{0}))\leq 1
, we have

V(B_{r}(x_{0}))\leq(\pi+\frac{A}{2})r^{2}.

When
V(B_{r}(x_{0}))>1
and
r<\frac{1}{\sqrt{A}}
, we can get that

V(B_{r}(x_{0}))\leq\pi r^{2}+\frac{Ar^{2}V(B_{r}(x_{0}))^{1-\frac{1}{p}}}{2}\leq\pi r^{2}+\frac{V(B_{r}(x_{0}))}{2}

which implies that

V(B_{r}(x_{0}))\leq 2\pi r^{2}.

If
V(B_{r}(x_{0}))>1
and
\frac{1}{\sqrt{A}}\leq r<1
, we obtain that

\displaystyle V(B_{r}(x_{0}))\leq\pi r^{2}+\frac{Ar^{2}V(B_{r}(x_{0}))^{1-\frac{1}{p}}}{2}\leq\pi+\frac{AV(B_{r}(x_{0}))^{1-\frac{1}{p}}}{2}.

Thus, when
V(B_{r}(x_{0}))>1
and
\frac{1}{\sqrt{A}}\leq r<1
, we have

\displaystyle V(B_{r}(x_{0}))\leq(\pi+\frac{A}{2})^{p}\leq A(\pi+\frac{A}{2})^{p}r^{2}.

So, for any
r<1
, we can always obtain that

V(B_{r}(x_{0}))\leq(2\pi+A)^{p+1}r^{2}.

Finally, for any
r<1
, by (
2.3
) we have

\displaystyle l(\partial B_{r}(x_{0}))\leq 2\pi r+Ar(2\pi+A)^{(p+1)(1-\frac{1}{p})}r^{2-\frac{2}{p}}\leq(2\pi+A)^{p+1}r.

This finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
M
satisfies (
1.1
). For any
x_{0}\in M,R>0
there holds

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|f(x)|\int^{R}_{d(x,x_{0})}\frac{1}{l(\partial B_{r}(x_{0}))}drdV\leq A\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{R}(x_{0}))}.

Proof.

Set
h(x)=\int^{R}_{d(x,x_{0})}\frac{1}{l(\partial B_{r}(x_{0}))}dr
. From (
1.1
) and the identity

\frac{d}{dr}V(B_{r}(x_{0}))=l(\partial B_{r}(x_{0}))
, we have

\displaystyle h(x)=

\displaystyle\int^{R}_{d(x,x_{0})}\frac{1}{l(\partial B_{r}(x_{0}))}dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{R}_{d(x,x_{0})}l(\partial B_{r}(x_{0}))^{-2}dV(B_{r}(x_{0}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int^{R}_{d(x,x_{0})}\frac{A}{V(B_{r}(x_{0}))}dV(B_{r}(x_{0}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A\ln\frac{V(B_{R}(x_{0}))}{V(B_{d(x,x_{0})}(x_{0}))}.

So we obtain that

h^{*}(V(B_{t}(x_{0})))\leq A\ln\frac{V(B_{R}(x_{0}))}{V(B_{t}(x_{0}))}

which yields that

h^{*}(t)\leq A\ln\frac{V(B_{R}(x_{0}))}{t}.

Thus, by
[
1
, Theorem 4.15]
we get that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|f(x)|\int^{R}_{d(x,x_{0})}\frac{1}{l(\partial B_{r}(x_{0}))}drdV=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|f(x)h(x)|dV

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int^{V(B_{R}(x_{0}))}_{0}f^{*}(t)h^{*}(t)dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle A\int^{V(B_{R}(x_{0}))}_{0}f^{*}(t)\ln\frac{V(B_{R}(x_{0}))}{t}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{R}(x_{0}))}.

This finishes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem

For convenience, we denote the center of
B_{1}

\displaystyle|\partial_{r}G(r,\theta)G(r,\varphi)-G(r,\theta)\partial_{r}G(r,\varphi)|

\displaystyle\int^{\rho}_{0}\int^{2\pi}_{0}|\frac{\partial}{\partial r}(\frac{G(r,\theta)}{l(\partial B_{r}(x_{0}))})|d\theta dr

\displaystyle C\int^{\rho}_{0}r^{-1}(2\pi+V(B_{r}(x_{0}))^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\|K_{M}\|_{L^{p}(B_{1}(x_{0}))})V(B_{r}(x_{0}))^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\|K_{M}\|_{L^{p}(B_{1}(x_{0}))}dr

\displaystyle C\int^{\rho}_{0}r^{-1}(1+r^{2-\frac{2}{p}})r^{2-\frac{2}{p}}dr

\displaystyle C\int^{\rho}_{0}r^{1-\frac{2}{p}}dr=C\rho^{2-\frac{2}{p}}.

(3.1)

By the divergence theorem, Lemma

\displaystyle|u(x_{0})-\frac{1}{l(\partial B_{\rho}(x_{0}))}\int^{2\pi}_{0}u(\rho,\theta)G(\rho,\theta)d\theta|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int^{\rho}_{0}\left(\frac{1}{l(\partial B_{r}(x_{0}))}\int^{2\pi}_{0}\partial_{r}u(r,\theta)G(r,\theta)d\theta-\int^{2\pi}_{0}u(r,\theta)\frac{\partial}{\partial r}(\frac{G(r,\theta)}{l(\partial B_{r}(x_{0}))})d\theta\right)dr\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C[\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))}+(\|g\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))}+\rho^{2-\frac{2}{p}})\|u\|_{C(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))}].

(3.2)

By the absolute continuity, when
g\in L\ln L(B_{1})
, we can find
\rho_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
depending only on

and the function

C(\|g\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{\rho_{0}}(x_{0}))}+\rho_{0}^{2-\frac{2}{p}})<\frac{1}{8}

for any

. Then from (

), for any
\rho<\rho_{0}
we get that

\displaystyle|u(x_{0})-\frac{1}{l(\partial B_{\rho}(x_{0}))}\int^{2\pi}_{0}u(\rho,\theta)G(\rho,\theta)d\theta|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C[\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))}+(\|g\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))}+\rho^{2-\frac{2}{p}})\|u\|_{C(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}+\frac{1}{8}\|u\|_{C(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))}.

Thus, for any
t<\rho_{0}
, integrating over
(0,t)
we obtain

\displaystyle V(B_{t}(x_{0}))|u(x_{0})|=\int^{t}_{0}l(\partial B_{\rho}(x_{0}))|u(x_{0})|d\rho

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}\int^{2\pi}_{0}|u(\rho,\theta)|G(\rho,\theta)d\theta d\rho+\int^{t}_{0}l(\partial B_{\rho}(x_{0}))[C\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}+\frac{1}{8}\|u\|_{C(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))}]d\rho

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{1}(B_{t}(x_{0}))}+V(B_{t}(x_{0}))(C\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}+\frac{1}{8}\|u\|_{C(B_{t}(x_{0}))}).

(3.3)

From (
2.1
) and (
3.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle|u(x_{0})|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\|u\|_{L^{1}(B_{t}(x_{0}))}}{V(B_{t}(x_{0}))}+C\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}+\frac{1}{8}\|u\|_{C(B_{t}(x_{0}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4A\|u\|_{L^{1}(B_{1})}}{t^{2}}+C\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}+\frac{1}{8}\|u\|_{C(B_{t}(x_{0}))}.

Clearly, for any
y_{0}\in B_{r}(x_{0}),r,t<\frac{\rho_{0}}{2}
, we can also get that

\displaystyle|u(y_{0})|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4A\|u\|_{L^{1}(B_{1})}}{t^{2}}+C\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}+\frac{1}{8}\|u\|_{C(B_{t}(y_{0}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4A\|u\|_{L^{1}(B_{1})}}{t^{2}}+C\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}+\frac{1}{8}\|u\|_{C(B_{r+t}(x_{0}))}.

(3.4)

Now we set
\omega(r)=\|u\|_{C(B_{r}(x_{0}))}
. Then for any
r,t<\frac{\rho_{0}}{2}
, (
3.4
) implies

\displaystyle\omega(r)\leq\frac{4A\|u\|_{L^{1}(B_{1})}}{t^{2}}+C\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}+\frac{1}{8}\omega(r+t).

(3.5)

Set

. Then we obtain the iteration

\omega(t_{k})\leq\frac{4^{k+2}A\|u\|_{L^{1}(B_{1})}}{\rho_{0}^{2}}+C\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}+\frac{1}{8}\omega(t_{k+1}).

By induction, we obtain

\omega(t_{1})\leq 8^{1-k}\omega(t_{k})+\sum\limits^{k-1}_{i=1}8^{1-i}(\frac{4^{i+2}A\|u\|_{L^{1}(B_{1})}}{\rho_{0}^{2}}+C\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}).

Letting

, we get that

\displaystyle\omega(\frac{\rho_{0}}{2})=

\displaystyle\omega(t_{1})\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}8^{1-k}\omega(\rho_{0})+\sum\limits^{\infty}_{i=1}8^{1-i}(\frac{4^{i+2}A\|u\|_{L^{1}(B_{1})}}{\rho_{0}^{2}}+C\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\frac{\|u\|_{L^{1}(B_{1})}}{\rho_{0}^{2}}+\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}).

Note that
\rho_{0}
depends only on
A,p
and the function
g
. Due to the arbitrariness of

, we can yield that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C(B_{\frac{1}{2}})}\leq\omega(\frac{\rho_{0}}{2})\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{1}(B_{1})}+\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}),

where
C
depends only on

and the function

. This finishes the proof.

4.
Proofs of Harnack inequalities

4.1.
Proof of Corollary
1.4

For any
x_{0}\in B_{\frac{1}{2}},\rho<\frac{1}{2}
, computations similar to (
3.2
) and Lemma
2.3
yield

\displaystyle|2\pi u(x_{0})-\int_{S^{1}}u(x_{0}+\rho\theta)d\theta|\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}|f(x)|\ln\frac{\rho}{|x-x_{0}|}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))}\leq C\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}.

Integrating over
(0,r)
for
r<\frac{1}{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\pi r^{2}u(x_{0})-\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u(x)dx|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int^{r}_{0}\rho|2\pi u(x_{0})-\int_{S^{1}}u(x_{0}+\rho\theta)d\theta|d\rho

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int^{r}_{0}\rho\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}d\rho

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Cr^{2}\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}

which implies that

\displaystyle|u(x_{0})-\frac{\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u(x)dx}{\pi r^{2}}|\leq C\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}.

For any
x_{1},x_{2}\in B_{\frac{1}{2}}
with
d(x_{1},x_{2})<\frac{1}{4}
, it is easy to see that
B_{\frac{1}{4}}(x_{2})\subset B_{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{1})
. So we get that

\displaystyle u(x_{2})\leq

\displaystyle\frac{16}{\pi}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{4}}(x_{2})}u(x)dx+C\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{16}{\pi}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{1})}u(x)dx+C\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4(u(x_{2})+C\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})})+C\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(u(x_{2})+\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}).

Thus, by iteration, for any
x,y\in B_{\frac{1}{2}}
, we have

\displaystyle u(x)\leq

\displaystyle C(u(y)+\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}).

This finishes the proof.

4.2.
Proof of Corollary
1.5

For any
x_{0}\in B_{\frac{1}{2}},\rho<\frac{1}{2}
, computations similar to (
3.2
) yield

|2\pi u(x_{0})-\int_{S^{1}}u(x_{0}+\rho\theta)d\theta|\leq|\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}f(x)\ln\frac{\rho}{|x-x_{0}|}dx|.

Note that
\ln\frac{\rho}{|x-x_{0}|}\chi_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}
is in
BMO
and its
BMO
-norm is independent of
\rho
. So, by the
\mathscr{H}^{1}
-
\mathrm{BMO}
duality, we get that

|2\pi u(x_{0})-\int_{S^{1}}u(x_{0}+\rho\theta)d\theta|\leq|\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}f(x)\ln\frac{\rho}{|x-x_{0}|}dx|\leq C\|f\|_{\mathscr{H}^{1}(B_{1})}.

Then, using the same proof as for Corollary
1.4
, for any
x,y\in B_{\frac{1}{2}}
we get that

\displaystyle u(x)\leq C(u(y)+\|f\|_{\mathscr{H}^{1}(B_{1})}).

This finishes the proof.

5.
Global estimate for Poisson equations on
M

We extend
f
to
B_{2}
by setting
f=0
in
B_{2}\setminus B_{1}
and let
v\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{2})
be the solution of
\triangle v=f
in
B_{2}
.

We claim the following John-Nirenberg inequality:

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2}}e^{\frac{|v(x)|}{2e\sqrt{A}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(B_{2})}}}dx\leq V(B_{2}).

(5.1)

We use a proof similar to that in
[
3
]
. It is well known that
k^{k}<(2e)^{k}k!
for any positive integer
k
. Then, by (
1.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2}}e^{\frac{|v(x)|}{2e\sqrt{A}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(B_{2})}}}dx=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2}}\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\frac{|v(x)|^{k}}{(2e\sqrt{A}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(B_{2})})^{k}k!}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\frac{\|v\|^{k}_{L^{k}(B_{2})}}{(2e\sqrt{A}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(B_{2})})^{k}k!}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}(\frac{k}{2}\sqrt{A})^{k}\frac{V(B_{2})\|\nabla v\|^{k}_{L^{2}(B_{2})}}{(2e\sqrt{A}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(B_{2})})^{k}k!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\frac{k^{k}}{(4e)^{k}k!}V(B_{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}2^{-k}V(B_{2})=V(B_{2}).

Thus, we have proved the claim (
5.1
).

Take a constant
C>2e\sqrt{A}
. For any
t>0
, from (
5.1
) we obtain

\displaystyle te^{\frac{v^{*}(t)}{C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(B_{2})}}}\leq

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}e^{\frac{v^{*}(s)}{C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(B_{2})}}}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int^{V(B_{2})}_{0}e^{\frac{v^{*}(s)}{C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(B_{2})}}}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2}}e^{\frac{|v(x)|}{C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(B_{2})}}}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle V(B_{2})

which implies that

\displaystyle v^{*}(t)\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(B_{2})}\ln\frac{V(B_{2})}{t}

(5.2)

for any
0<t\leq V(B_{2})
.

By
[
1
, Theorem 4.15]
and (
5.2
), we can obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2}}|v(x)f(x)|dV\leq

\displaystyle\int^{V(B_{2})}_{0}f^{*}(t)v^{*}(t)dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(B_{2})}\int^{V(B_{2})}_{0}f^{*}(t)\ln\frac{V(B_{2})}{t}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(B_{2})}\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}.

Now, from the assumptions that
v\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{2})
and
\triangle v=f
in
B_{2}
, we get that

\displaystyle\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{2})}=

\displaystyle|\int_{B_{2}}v(x)\triangle v(x)dV|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\int_{B_{2}}v(x)f(x)dV|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(B_{2})}\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}

which yields that

\displaystyle\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(B_{2})}\leq C\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}.

(5.3)

On the other hand, by (
1.2
), Lemma
2.2
and (
5.3
), we get that

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{1}(B_{2})}\leq CV(B_{2})\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(B_{2})}\leq C\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}.

(5.4)

Though
f\in C^{\infty}(B_{1})
,
f
is not smooth in
B_{2}
in general when we extend
f
to
B_{2}
by setting
f=0
in
B_{2}\setminus B_{1}
. Therefore, we cannot apply Theorem 1.1 on
v
directly.
Take
\eta_{n}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{1})
with
0\leq\eta_{n}\leq 1,\eta_{n}\equiv 1
on
B_{1-\frac{1}{n}}

and let
v_{n}\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{2})
be the solution of
\triangle v_{n}=\eta_{n}f
in
B_{2}
. It is easy to see that
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\|f-\eta_{n}f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}=0
.

Clearly, all the above computations remain valid if we replace
v,f
by
v_{n},\eta_{n}f
. (
5.4
) implies

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\|v-v_{n}\|_{L^{1}(B_{2})}\leq C\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\|f-\eta_{n}f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}=0.

As
\triangle(v_{n}-v_{m})=(\eta_{n}-\eta_{m})f
, by Theorem
1.1
and (
5.4
), we have

\displaystyle\|v_{n}-v_{m}\|_{C(B_{\frac{3}{2}})}\leq

\displaystyle C(\|v_{n}-v_{m}\|_{L^{1}(B_{2})}+\|(\eta_{n}-\eta_{m})f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|(\eta_{n}-\eta_{m})f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}.

Therefore,
\{v_{n}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
C(B_{\frac{3}{2}})
. As
v_{n}\to v
in
L^{1}(B_{2})
, we have
v_{n}\to v
in
C(B_{\frac{3}{2}})
.
Since
u-v
is harmonic in
B_{1}
, by the maximum principle, Theorem
1.1
,
the assumption
u\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{1})
and (
5.4
) for
v_{n}
, we can show that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C(B_{1})}\leq

\displaystyle\|v\|_{C(B_{1})}+\|u-v\|_{C(B_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|v\|_{C(B_{1})}+\|u-v\|_{C(\partial B_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|v\|_{C(B_{1})}+\|v\|_{C(\partial B_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\|v\|_{C(B_{1})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\|v_{n}\|_{C(B_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}(\|v_{n}\|_{L^{1}(B_{2})}+\|\eta_{n}f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\|\eta_{n}f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|^{*}_{L\ln L(B_{1})}.

This finishes the proof.

Single-cell transcriptomic profiling and machine learning identify S-nitrosylation-associated LAMC2 as a key prognostic driver in pancreatic cancer
Authors: Chenchen Yang, Hengfa Ge, Yunfan Yin, Jialing Zhang, Aifeng Gong
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPancreatic cancer (PC) remains one of the deadliest malignancies worldwide, characterized by late diagnosis, limited therapeutic options, and dismal prognosis. Post-translational modifications (PTMs) are increasingly recognized as key regulators of tumor biology, yet the role of S-nitrosylation—a redox-based cysteine modification—has not been systematically investigated in PC.MethodsWe integrated bulk and single-cell transcriptomic data from TCGA, ICGC, and GEO cohorts to comprehensively profile S-nitrosylation-related gene expression. A large-scale machine-learning framework incorporating 101 algorithmic combinations was used to construct an optimized prognostic model. Kaplan–Meier, ROC, and PCA analyses evaluated predictive power and stability across nine independent cohorts. The key hub gene LAMC2 was identified and subjected to single-gene bioinformatic analysis and functional validation through in-vitro experiments using MIA-PaCa-2 cells.ResultsS-nitrosylation exhibited the highest enrichment among all PTM types in pancreatic cancer and was mainly localized to malignant ductal and fibroblast cells. The StepCox(backward) + GBM model showed excellent prognostic performance (TCGA C-index = 0.827) and successfully stratified patients into high- and low-risk groups across multiple external datasets. High-risk patients displayed distinct transcriptional profiles and significantly worse survival outcomes (all p < 0.01). Among S-nitrosylation-related genes, LAMC2 showed the strongest correlation with risk score (r = 0.63) and was consistently upregulated in tumors. Cox and GSEA analyses linked LAMC2 to epithelial–mesenchymal transition, TGF-β, and hypoxia pathways. Functionally, LAMC2 knockdown suppressed migration and colony formation in pancreatic cancer cells.ConclusionsThis study establishes S-nitrosylation as a crucial PTM in pancreatic cancer and introduces a robust machine-learning-based prognostic model. LAMC2 is identified as a key oncogenic effector connecting S-nitrosylation dysregulation to tumor aggressiveness, providing novel prognostic and therapeutic insights for pancreatic cancer management.Keywords: Pancreatic cancer, S-nitrosylation, Single-cell RNA sequencing, Machine learning, LAMC2

Introduction

Pancreatic cancer (PC) is one of the most lethal malignancies worldwide, ranking among the top causes of cancer-related deaths [1–3]. Despite advances in diagnostic imaging, surgical resection, chemotherapy, and targeted therapy, the prognosis of pancreatic cancer remains dismal, with a five-year overall survival rate below 10% [4–6]. The disease is often diagnosed at an advanced stage due to its insidious onset and lack of specific symptoms, resulting in limited treatment options and poor clinical outcomes [7]. Therefore, it is of urgent importance to identify novel molecular biomarkers and develop reliable prognostic models to improve patient stratification and guide personalized therapeutic strategies.

Accumulating evidence has highlighted that post-translational modifications (PTMs) play essential roles in the regulation of protein function, localization, and stability, thereby influencing key cellular processes such as proliferation, apoptosis, autophagy, and immune responses [8]. PTMs, including phosphorylation, ubiquitination, acetylation, methylation, and S-nitrosylation, constitute an additional layer of complexity in tumor biology [9]. Dysregulation of PTMs has been implicated in tumor initiation and progression by altering signaling cascades and cellular homeostasis [10]. Among these, S-nitrosylation—a reversible covalent modification involving the attachment of a nitric oxide (NO) group to the thiol side chain of cysteine residues—has emerged as a crucial redox-based signaling mechanism in both physiological and pathological contexts [11]. We focused on S-nitrosylation because NO-dependent redox signaling is tightly coupled to hypoxia, inflammation, and stromal remodeling—hallmark processes in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma—yet the S-nitrosylation landscape has rarely been interrogated in a systematic and prognostically oriented manner in PC.

S-nitrosylation can modulate a broad spectrum of protein functions, including enzymatic activity, protein–protein interactions, and subcellular trafficking [12]. Aberrant S-nitrosylation has been associated with various cancers, including breast, lung, and hepatocellular carcinoma, by affecting pathways related to oxidative stress, cell survival, angiogenesis, and immune evasion [13]. In pancreatic cancer, recent studies have revealed that excessive nitric oxide production and aberrant S-nitrosylation may contribute to tumor cell proliferation, epithelial–mesenchymal transition, and resistance to apoptosis [14]. However, the comprehensive landscape of S-nitrosylation-related gene expression and its clinical significance in pancreatic cancer remain largely unexplored.

Machine learning (ML) has become a powerful tool in cancer research, offering innovative strategies to integrate high-dimensional omics data with clinical outcomes [15]. By leveraging algorithmic diversity, ML-based approaches can identify key molecular signatures and build robust prognostic models with superior accuracy and generalizability compared to traditional statistical methods [16]. In recent years, ensemble learning frameworks that combine multiple algorithms have demonstrated remarkable performance in predicting cancer prognosis and treatment response. However, few studies have systematically incorporated S-nitrosylation-related genes into ML-based prognostic modeling in pancreatic cancer. Moreover, most existing prognostic signatures for PC have been derived from bulk transcriptomic data alone and do not explicitly anchor model construction to a biologically defined PTM program or integrate single-cell information to refine candidate genes.

In this study, we performed a comprehensive bioinformatics analysis to elucidate the expression patterns and prognostic relevance of S-nitrosylation-related genes in pancreatic cancer. We integrated transcriptomic data and clinical information from multiple public databases and applied 101 combinations of machine learning algorithms to construct and optimize a prognostic risk model. This model demonstrated excellent predictive power for overall survival and effectively stratified patients into distinct risk subgroups with different molecular and immune characteristics. Furthermore, functional enrichment and immune microenvironment analyses were conducted to uncover the potential biological mechanisms linking S-nitrosylation to pancreatic cancer progression. Distinct from prior LAMC2-focused reports that primarily emphasized its role as an invasion marker, our work embeds LAMC2 within a S-nitrosylation-associated transcriptional network and provides both multicohort prognostic evidence and functional validation.

Overall, our findings reveal that S-nitrosylation-related genes play a pivotal role in the malignant progression of pancreatic cancer and have significant prognostic value. The proposed S-nitrosylation-based prognostic model not only provides new insights into the molecular underpinnings of pancreatic cancer but also offers potential targets for precision therapy and novel directions for future translational research.

Materials and methods

Data collection and preprocessing

The transcriptomic data and corresponding clinical information of pancreatic cancer (PC) patients were retrieved from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database and used as the training cohort. Independent validation datasets, including ICGC-AU, ICGC-CA, GSE28735, GSE57495, GSE62452, GSE78229, GSE79668, and GSE85916, were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. A concise overview was conducted of all cohorts, including platform, sample size, clinicopathological characteristics, and survival endpoints. All RNA-seq data were normalized and log2-transformed for downstream analyses. To mitigate potential batch effects, each cohort was processed and normalized independently without merging expression matrices across platforms, and the prognostic model trained in the TCGA cohort was applied to each external dataset separately. Risk scores were dichotomized using cohort-specific median cutoffs, thereby reducing the impact of cross-cohort distributional differences on risk-group classification.

To comprehensively assess the biological relevance of different post-translational modification (PTM) patterns, single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) was conducted using curated PTM-related gene sets. Among all PTM categories, the S-nitrosylation-related gene set exhibited the highest enrichment score, suggesting its close association with pancreatic cancer progression. Therefore, subsequent analyses focused on S-nitrosylation-associated genes.

Single-cell RNA sequencing analysis

Single-cell RNA-seq data from GSE155698, containing transcriptomic profiles of pancreatic cancer tissues [17], were processed using the Seurat (v4.3.0) package. Low-quality cells with fewer than 200 detected genes or more than 10% mitochondrial gene content were excluded. After normalization and principal component analysis (PCA), cell clustering was performed using the FindClusters function, and cell types were annotated based on canonical marker genes and reference databases.

To quantify S-nitrosylation activity at the single-cell level, we applied the AUCell algorithm to calculate the enrichment score of the S-nitrosylation-related gene set in each cell. Cells were stratified into high- and low-S-nitrosylation(SNO) groups according to the AUCell score distribution. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between the two groups were identified using the FindMarkers function with |log2FC| > 0.25 and adjusted p-value < 0.05. Additionally, the top 150 genes most correlated with S-nitrosylation scores were obtained by Spearman correlation analysis. The intersection between the DEGs and the top 150 correlated genes was defined as S-nitrosylation-associated candidate genes for further modeling. This strategy ensured that genes entering the prognostic modeling pipeline were both differentially expressed between SNO-high and SNO-low cells and tightly correlated with single-cell SNO activity, thereby enriching for functionally relevant candidates.

Machine learning–based prognostic model construction

A comprehensive machine-learning pipeline incorporating multiple algorithms and algorithmic combinations—including CoxBoost, Random Survival Forest (RSF), gradient boosting machine (GBM), SuperPC, survival support vector machine (survival-SVM), LASSO, Ridge, Elastic Net (Enet), partial least-squares Cox regression (plsRcox), and stepwise Cox regression (StepCox)—was applied to the TCGA cohort to develop prognostic models based on the S-nitrosylation-associated candidate genes. Input variables for all models were the candidate genes derived from the single-cell analysis, and each algorithm used its built-in feature-selection procedure (e.g., penalization or stepwise elimination) to identify informative predictors. For each algorithmic combination, we adopted an internal k-fold cross-validation strategy within the TCGA cohort, repeatedly partitioning the data into training and validation folds and calculating Harrell’s concordance index (C-index) and time-dependent ROC curves as performance metrics. The combination with the highest mean C-index across all cross-validation folds was selected as the final model.

The final model was validated in eight independent external cohorts (ICGC-AU, ICGC-CA, GSE28735, GSE57495, GSE62452, GSE78229, GSE79668, and GSE85916). Patients were divided into high-risk and low-risk subgroups based on the median risk score. The model’s robustness and generalizability were further evaluated by Kaplan–Meier survival analysis, univariate and multivariate Cox regression, ROC curve analysis, and principal component analysis (PCA) to visualize the distribution of risk groups in multidimensional space.

Functional and experimental validation of key gene LAMC2

To identify potential key regulators within the S-nitrosylation–based model, genes with the highest importance scores and consistent expression patterns across multiple cohorts were selected for further validation. Among them, LAMC2 was identified as a hub gene significantly associated with poor prognosis in pancreatic cancer patients. To bridge computational predictions with biological function, we next focused on experimental validation of LAMC2 as a representative S-nitrosylation-associated hub gene.

Functional experiments were performed using the human pancreatic cancer cell line MIA-PaCa2, which was obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC, Manassas, VA, USA). Small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) targeting LAMC2 were transfected into cells using Lipofectamine™ 3000 (Invitrogen, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The knockdown efficiency was confirmed by quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) and Western blot analysis.

Colony formation assay: After transfection, 800 cells per well were seeded into six-well plates and cultured for 10–14 days. Colonies were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, stained with crystal violet, and counted to evaluate clonogenic capacity.

Transwell migration and invasion assays: Cell migration and invasion abilities were assessed using Transwell chambers (8 μm pore size, Corning, USA). For invasion assays, chambers were precoated with Matrigel (BD Biosciences, USA). After 24–48 h of incubation, migrated or invaded cells were fixed, stained, and counted under a microscope.

All experiments were conducted in triplicate, and data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical significance was determined using Student’s t-test or one-way ANOVA, with p < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Results

Identification of S-nitrosylation as a key post-translational modification in pancreatic cancer

To comprehensively investigate the potential regulatory role of various post-translational modifications (PTMs) in pancreatic cancer, we first performed single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) on PTM-related gene sets across the TCGA cohort.

As shown in Fig. 1A, among the 15 types of PTMs analyzed, S-nitrosylation-related genes exhibited the highest mean ssGSEA score, suggesting that S-nitrosylation might play a predominant role in pancreatic cancer progression compared with other modification types such as ubiquitination, acetylation, or methylation. To further explore the cellular landscape of pancreatic cancer and the potential distribution of S-nitrosylation-related gene expression at single-cell resolution, we analyzed the single-cell RNA sequencing dataset GSE155698. Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) analysis revealed 16 distinct cellular clusters within the tumor microenvironment (Fig. 1B). Based on canonical marker genes, these clusters were annotated into eight major cell types, including myeloid cells, ductal cells, NK cells, T cells, acinar cells, B cells, stellate cells, fibroblasts, and endothelial cells (Fig. 1D). The expression profiles of representative genes corresponding to each PTM type were visualized in a dot plot (Fig. 1C), indicating that the expression of S-nitrosylation-related genes was particularly enriched in ductal and fibroblast populations. The relative proportions of each cell type across samples are shown in Fig. 1E, demonstrating marked intertumoral heterogeneity in the pancreatic cancer microenvironment. Furthermore, violin plot analysis (Fig. 1F) revealed that S-nitrosylation scores were significantly higher in ductal cells—the malignant epithelial compartment—compared to stromal or immune cell subsets, supporting the notion that dysregulated S-nitrosylation activity may be closely linked to pancreatic cancer cell aggressiveness and malignant phenotypes. Collectively, these results highlight S-nitrosylation as a key PTM signature in pancreatic cancer and provide the rationale for subsequent analyses focused on S-nitrosylation-related genes and their prognostic implications.

Intercellular communication and identification of S-nitrosylation-associated genes in pancreatic cancer

To further characterize the biological differences between the high- and low-S-nitrosylation (SNO) groups identified by AUCell scoring, we first evaluated their patterns of cell–cell communication using the CellChat analytical framework. As shown in Fig. 2A, both groups exhibited complex intercellular interaction networks among major cell types; however, the SNO-high group displayed markedly denser and stronger intercellular communication than the SNO-low group, particularly involving ductal, fibroblast, and stellate cells. This observation was quantitatively supported by the overall increase in both the number of inferred interactions and interaction strength in the SNO-high group (Fig. 2C). Scatter plots depicting outgoing versus incoming interaction strength (Fig. 2B) further indicated that ductal and fibroblast cells served as dominant signaling hubs within the SNO-high tumor microenvironment, suggesting that enhanced S-nitrosylation activity may potentiate crosstalk between malignant epithelial and stromal compartments. To pinpoint genes most associated with S-nitrosylation activity, we performed Spearman correlation analysis between SNO scores and gene expression across single cells. The ranked correlation curve (Fig. 2D) highlighted a subset of genes with strong positive correlations (r > 0.2), which were considered potential S-nitrosylation-associated candidates. Subsequently, the top 150 correlated genes were integrated with differentially expressed genes between the SNO-high and SNO-low groups to identify overlapping candidates for model construction. The information flow analysis (Fig. 2E) revealed that most of these overlapping genes exhibited higher regulatory information content in the SNO-high group, reflecting their functional enrichment in S-nitrosylation-activated pathways. Notably, several genes—such as LAMC2, MMP9, and COL1A1—stood out as major contributors to information flow within the SNO-high state, implicating them as central mediators linking S-nitrosylation signaling to extracellular matrix remodeling and tumor–stroma interaction. Together, these findings demonstrate that elevated S-nitrosylation activity reshapes the intercellular communication landscape of pancreatic cancer and highlight a set of key genes potentially driving this process, thereby providing a foundation for subsequent machine-learning-based prognostic modeling.

Construction and evaluation of S-nitrosylation-related prognostic models via multi-algorithm integration

To construct a robust and generalizable prognostic model based on S-nitrosylation-related genes, we systematically integrated 10 machine learning algorithms (CoxBoost, RSF, GBM, SuperPC, survivalSVM, Lasso, Ridge, Enet, plsRcox, and StepCox) into 101 algorithmic combinations. Each combination was evaluated across seven independent pancreatic cancer cohorts (TCGA, GSE28735, GSE57495, GSE62452, GSE78229, GSE79668, and GSE85916), and its predictive performance was assessed using Harrell’s concordance index (C-index) as the primary metric (Fig. 3). As shown in the heatmap, most models achieved satisfactory C-index values (≥ 0.6) across datasets, reflecting consistent predictive capacity. The StepCox(backward) + GBM model exhibited the highest predictive performance in the TCGA training cohort (C-index = 0.827), while maintaining a strong average C-index of 0.621 across all external validation cohorts. Similarly, StepCox(both) + GBM and StepCox(backward) + RSF models also demonstrated stable cross-cohort reproducibility, suggesting the robustness of these hybrid approaches. Notably, ensemble models combining stepwise Cox regression with boosting-based algorithms (GBM or CoxBoost) outperformed traditional single-method approaches such as Lasso or Ridge regression. This improvement likely arises from their enhanced ability to capture complex nonlinear interactions and synergistic effects among S-nitrosylation-associated genes. Based on both internal and external validation metrics, the StepCox(backward) + GBM model was identified as the optimal prognostic classifier. It demonstrated superior generalization, discrimination, and stability across multiple independent datasets, underscoring its potential clinical utility for risk stratification and outcome prediction in pancreatic cancer patients.

Construction of S-nitrosylation-related prognostic models via machine-learning integration. Heatmap of C-index values across 101 algorithmic combinations. The StepCox(backward)+GBM model exhibited optimal predictive performance in TCGA and consistent accuracy across validation cohorts

Validation of the S-nitrosylation-based prognostic model across independent cohorts

To verify the predictive power and generalizability of the S-nitrosylation-related prognostic model, we applied the StepCox(backward) + GBM classifier to the TCGA training cohort and eight independent external validation cohorts (ICGC-AU, ICGC-CA, GSE28735, GSE57495, GSE62452, GSE78229, GSE79668, and GSE85916). Patients in each dataset were stratified into high-risk and low-risk subgroups according to the median risk score derived from the optimal model. Kaplan–Meier survival analyses consistently demonstrated that high-risk patients had significantly shorter overall survival compared with low-risk patients in the majority of cohorts (Fig. 4A–I). In the TCGA cohort (Fig. 4A), the high-risk group exhibited a striking survival disadvantage (log-rank p < 0.0001), confirming the model’s strong discriminatory capacity in the training set. Similarly, significant prognostic separation was observed in both ICGC-AU (p = 0.01; Fig. 4B) and ICGC-CA (p = 0.002; Fig. 4C) validation cohorts, further demonstrating its transferability across different sequencing platforms and population backgrounds. Among the GEO datasets, high-risk patients showed consistently poorer survival in GSE57495 (p = 0.0053; Fig. 4E), GSE62452 (p = 0.0095; Fig. 4F), GSE78229 (p = 0.0013; Fig. 4G), GSE79668 (p = 0.0014; Fig. 4H), and GSE85916 (p < 0.0001; Fig. 4I). Although the GSE28735 cohort (Fig. 4D) did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.12), a clear downward survival trend was observed in the high-risk group, consistent with the overall pattern across other cohorts. Collectively, these results confirm that the S-nitrosylation-based risk signature is a robust and reproducible prognostic indicator in pancreatic cancer. Its stable performance across multiple independent datasets underscores its potential clinical utility for individualized risk assessment and survival prediction.

Kaplan–Meier survival analyses of the S-nitrosylation-based prognostic model. A–I Survival curves across TCGA, ICGC, and GEO cohorts, comparing overall survival between high- and low-risk subgroups

Time-dependent ROC analysis confirms the predictive accuracy of the S-nitrosylation-based prognostic model

To further evaluate the predictive performance and temporal stability of the S-nitrosylation-based prognostic model, time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were generated for 1-, 3-, and 5-year overall survival across the training and validation cohorts (Fig. 5A–I). In the TCGA training cohort (Fig. 5A), the model achieved excellent discrimination with AUCs of 0.88, 0.90, and 0.90 at 1, 3, and 5 years, respectively, indicating strong long-term predictive capability. In the ICGC-AU and ICGC-CA cohorts (Fig. 5B–C), the AUC values at 1–5 years ranged from 0.36 to 0.67, reflecting moderate but consistent predictive power across different populations and sequencing platforms. Among the GEO cohorts, the model also exhibited satisfactory and comparable predictive accuracy, with AUCs ranging from 0.59 to 0.84 at different time points. Specifically, GSE62452 (Fig. 5F) and GSE78229 (Fig. 5G) demonstrated relatively high AUCs at 3–5 years (0.78–0.84), suggesting that the model performs particularly well in predicting long-term outcomes. Even in smaller cohorts such as GSE28735 and GSE57495 (Fig. 5D–E), the AUCs remained above 0.6, supporting the generalizability of the prognostic signature. Collectively, these findings corroborate that the S-nitrosylation-based risk model maintains robust predictive performance across multiple datasets and time horizons, effectively distinguishing high-risk from low-risk pancreatic cancer patients and supporting its potential utility in clinical prognostication and personalized risk management.

Time-dependent ROC curves assessing predictive accuracy of the model. A–I ROC analyses for 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival across all nine cohorts, showing stable AUC values and consistent long-term predictive capability

Principal component analysis reveals distinct transcriptional profiles between high- and low-risk groups

To further explore the global transcriptomic differences captured by the S-nitrosylation-based prognostic model, principal component analysis (PCA) was performed across the training and all external validation cohorts (Fig. 6A–I). In the TCGA training cohort (Fig. 6A), patients in the high-risk and low-risk groups displayed clearly separated distributions along the first two principal components (PC1 and PC2), indicating that the risk score effectively distinguished patients based on overall gene expression patterns. Similarly, in the ICGC-AU (Fig. 6B) and ICGC-CA (Fig. 6C) cohorts, high- and low-risk patients occupied distinct transcriptional spaces, suggesting that the S-nitrosylation-related risk model captured consistent molecular variation across different populations and sequencing platforms. Comparable segregation trends were also observed in the GEO validation datasets (Fig. 6D–I), including GSE28735, GSE57495, GSE62452, GSE78229, GSE79668, and GSE85916. In these cohorts, the high-risk samples clustered more tightly and tended to shift toward one region of the PCA plot, reflecting shared molecular signatures associated with poor prognosis, whereas the low-risk samples were more dispersed, suggesting transcriptional heterogeneity among patients with favorable outcomes. Together, these PCA findings further support that the proposed S-nitrosylation-based risk model stratifies pancreatic cancer patients into two biologically distinct molecular subgroups, reinforcing its robustness and biological interpretability across multiple independent datasets.

Principal component analysis of high- and low-risk subgroups. A–I PCA plots illustrating distinct transcriptional distributions between high- and low-risk groups across all cohorts

Correlation between the S-nitrosylation-based risk score and gene expression profiles

To identify genes that contribute most strongly to the prognostic capability of the S-nitrosylation-based model, we examined the correlations between the calculated risk scores and the expression levels of S-nitrosylation-related genes within the TCGA cohort (Fig. 7). Pearson correlation analysis revealed that a substantial proportion of these genes exhibited significant associations with the model-derived risk score, suggesting their potential involvement in modulating S-nitrosylation-linked oncogenic processes. As illustrated in Fig. 7, several genes—including LAMC2, MMP9, COL1A1, SERPINB5, FN1, ITGA2, and S100A16—showed strong positive correlations with risk score (r > 0.4), indicating that their upregulation was associated with high-risk phenotypes and poor survival outcomes. Conversely, a smaller subset of genes, such as CDH1 and HNF1B, exhibited negative correlations, implying their relative suppression in aggressive tumors. Among all candidates, LAMC2 demonstrated the strongest correlation with the risk score (r = 0.63), highlighting it as a potential central regulator within the S-nitrosylation network. These findings underscore that the S-nitrosylation-based model captures biologically meaningful transcriptional programs, with LAMC2 emerging as a hub gene linking high S-nitrosylation activity to malignant progression and unfavorable prognosis in pancreatic cancer. Consequently, LAMC2 was selected for further functional and experimental validation in subsequent analyses.

Correlation between risk score and S-nitrosylation-related gene expression. Scatter plots showing Pearson correlations of representative genes with risk score. LAMC2 exhibited the strongest positive correlation (r = 0.63)

Single-gene analysis confirms LAMC2 as a key oncogenic regulator in pancreatic cancer

Given that LAMC2 exhibited the strongest correlation with the S-nitrosylation-based risk score, we further performed a comprehensive single-gene analysis to elucidate its expression pattern, prognostic relevance, and biological functions in pancreatic cancer (Fig. 8). Across multiple independent datasets—including GSE28735, GSE62452, GSE71729, and TCGA-PAAD—LAMC2 expression was significantly upregulated in tumor tissues compared with normal pancreatic samples (Fig. 8A–D, all p < 0.001 except TCGA-PAAD, p = 0.083). Similar trends were observed in the E-MTAB-6134 cohort, where LAMC2 expression was notably higher in TP53-mutant tumors compared with wild-type cases (p = 0.028; Fig. 8E), implying that TP53 dysregulation may contribute to the aberrant activation of LAMC2 in pancreatic cancer. To systematically assess its prognostic significance, we conducted Cox regression analyses across 16 independent cohorts covering overall survival (OS), disease-free survival (DFS), relapse-free survival (RFS), and disease-specific survival (DSS). As shown in Fig. 8 F, elevated LAMC2 expression was consistently associated with adverse survival outcomes across multiple datasets, with hazard ratios (HRs) greater than 1 in nearly all cohorts. These findings confirm LAMC2 as a robust and reproducible biomarker of poor prognosis in pancreatic cancer. Furthermore, gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) based on hallmark pathways revealed that tumors with high LAMC2 expression were enriched in several oncogenic and pro-invasive signatures, including epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT), E2F targets, G2/M checkpoint, hypoxia, and TGF-β signaling (Fig. 8G). These pathways are well-known drivers of tumor aggressiveness, cell cycle progression, and metastasis, suggesting that LAMC2 may act as a central effector linking aberrant S-nitrosylation signaling to malignant biological behavior. Collectively, these findings identify LAMC2 as a key S-nitrosylation-associated oncogene, characterized by its tumor-specific overexpression, correlation with TP53 mutation status, and consistent prognostic impact across multiple cohorts. Its enrichment in EMT and proliferative signaling pathways highlights its potential as both a prognostic biomarker and a therapeutic target in pancreatic cancer.

Experimental validation confirms the oncogenic role of LAMC2 in pancreatic cancer cells

To experimentally validate the functional role of LAMC2 in pancreatic cancer progression, we performed a series of in vitro assays using the MIA-PaCa-2 cell line. LAMC2 expression was silenced using two independent short hairpin RNAs (sh-LAMC2-1 and sh-LAMC2-2), with sh-control cells serving as the negative control. As shown in Fig. 9 A, Transwell migration assays revealed a marked reduction in the number of migrated MIA-PaCa-2 cells following LAMC2 knockdown compared with the control group, indicating that LAMC2 depletion significantly suppresses the migratory capacity of pancreatic cancer cells. Similarly, the colony formation assay (Fig. 9B) demonstrated that silencing LAMC2 markedly decreased the number and size of colonies formed after 10–14 days of culture, suggesting that LAMC2 promotes clonogenic survival and proliferative potential in vitro. Collectively, these functional experiments corroborate the bioinformatic findings and confirm that LAMC2 acts as a key oncogenic driver in pancreatic cancer, enhancing both migratory and proliferative phenotypes. These results further support its potential as a therapeutic target and as a mechanistic link between aberrant S-nitrosylation signaling and pancreatic tumor aggressiveness.

Discussion

Pancreatic cancer remains a formidable clinical challenge due to its complex molecular heterogeneity and late presentation [18]. In this study, we present a comprehensive multi-omics and machine-learning-driven framework that systematically elucidates the role of S-nitrosylation in pancreatic cancer and constructs a robust prognostic model with strong generalizability across multiple independent cohorts. Our results reveal that S-nitrosylation-related genes constitute a distinctive molecular program tightly associated with tumor aggressiveness, stromal activation, and poor survival outcomes. By anchoring model construction to a biologically defined S-nitrosylation gene set and refining candidate features using single-cell data, our work extends beyond conventional, genome-wide signatures and provides a PTM-centered perspective on PC prognosis.

The identification of S-nitrosylation as the most enriched post-translational modification among 15 PTM categories underscores its biological relevance in pancreatic cancer. Unlike irreversible modifications such as ubiquitination or methylation, S-nitrosylation is dynamically regulated by cellular redox status and nitric-oxide signaling, conferring a unique layer of regulatory flexibility. Our single-cell transcriptomic analysis demonstrated that S-nitrosylation activity was particularly elevated in ductal and fibroblast populations, two critical compartments in the pancreatic tumor microenvironment (TME). These findings suggest that aberrant S-nitrosylation may facilitate malignant epithelial–stromal crosstalk, consistent with prior reports linking nitric-oxide pathways to fibroblast activation and extracellular-matrix remodeling.

By applying a large-scale machine-learning strategy that integrated 10 algorithms and 101 combinations, we established a StepCox(backward) + GBM model with superior prognostic accuracy (C-index = 0.827 in TCGA). This ensemble approach outperformed single-method models such as LASSO or Ridge regression, highlighting the advantage of combining feature-selection and boosting mechanisms to capture nonlinear interactions among genes. The model’s predictive stability across nine independent cohorts—including ICGC and multiple GEO datasets—demonstrates its robustness and potential clinical applicability. Importantly, consistent survival separation and AUC values across diverse populations confirm the generalizability of the S-nitrosylation-based risk signature.

Principal-component analysis further revealed that high- and low-risk patients clustered into transcriptionally distinct groups, reinforcing the notion that our risk model reflects underlying biological heterogeneity rather than purely statistical separation. Correlation analyses identified several genes—most notably LAMC2, MMP9, and COL1A1—that strongly aligned with risk scores, implicating them as downstream mediators of S-nitrosylation-driven oncogenic signaling. Among these, LAMC2 emerged as the central hub gene, showing the highest positive correlation with the risk score and consistent overexpression in tumor tissues across multiple datasets.

LAMC2 encodes the γ2 chain of laminin-332, an extracellular-matrix glycoprotein that promotes cell adhesion, migration, and epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) [19–21]. Previous studies have identified LAMC2 as a hallmark of invasive cancer phenotypes, particularly in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, where it facilitates tumor–stroma interactions and metastasis [22]. Our single-gene analysis confirmed that LAMC2 expression was significantly elevated in pancreatic tumor tissues and correlated with TP53 mutation status, suggesting that its dysregulation may arise from impaired tumor-suppressor control. Multicohort Cox analyses consistently demonstrated that high LAMC2 expression predicts poor overall, disease-free, and relapse-free survival, establishing it as a robust prognostic biomarker. Moreover, GSEA revealed enrichment of EMT, TGF-β, and hypoxia pathways in LAMC2-high tumors, all of which are key drivers of pancreatic cancer aggressiveness and therapeutic resistance. These findings position LAMC2 not only as a marker of invasion but also as a nodal effector within a broader S-nitrosylation-associated transcriptional axis.

Although our data indicate that LAMC2 is tightly co-regulated with an S-nitrosylation-associated gene program, the precise mechanistic nature of this connection remains to be elucidated. One plausible scenario is that NO/S-nitrosylation signaling modulates extracellular-matrix and integrin pathways, thereby transcriptionally upregulating LAMC2 through hypoxia- and TGF-β–responsive transcription factors; conversely, LAMC2-driven extracellular-matrix remodeling may feed back to shape local redox status and NO gradients within the tumor microenvironment. These hypotheses are consistent with the enrichment of EMT, TGF-β, and hypoxia signatures in LAMC2-high tumors observed in our GSEA analysis and with previous reports linking nitric-oxide signaling to fibroblast activation and matrix dynamics, but they require direct mechanistic testing in future studies.

Functional experiments provided direct evidence of LAMC2’s oncogenic role. Silencing LAMC2 in MIA-PaCa-2 cells markedly reduced migration and colony-formation capacities, confirming its contribution to both invasive and proliferative behavior. These findings align with the bioinformatic prediction that LAMC2 acts as a mechanistic bridge between aberrant S-nitrosylation signaling and malignant phenotypes. From a translational perspective, targeting LAMC2 or modulating S-nitrosylation may represent promising therapeutic strategies to inhibit tumor progression and overcome chemoresistance.

Our study also highlights the potential of machine learning in precision oncology. By systematically screening 101 algorithmic combinations, we demonstrate a scalable framework for identifying the optimal prognostic model tailored to specific molecular processes. This approach not only enhances prediction accuracy but also ensures model interpretability by integrating biological priors—in this case, the S-nitrosylation gene network. The reproducibility of our model across diverse datasets further validates its utility in real-world clinical settings.

Nonetheless, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, all functional experiments in this study were conducted in a single pancreatic cancer cell line (MIA-PaCa-2) and were limited to in-vitro assays; additional validation in multiple genetically diverse cell lines and in vivo models will be necessary to generalize the role of LAMC2 and S-nitrosylation in pancreatic cancer progression. Second, although our analyses incorporated multiple large public datasets, prospective validation in multicenter clinical cohorts is required to confirm the clinical utility and implementability of the S-nitrosylation-based risk model. Third, while LAMC2 was functionally validated, the precise molecular mechanisms by which S-nitrosylation regulates LAMC2 expression or activity remain unclear; proteomic mapping of S-nitrosylated proteins combined with targeted perturbation of LAMC2 and its upstream regulators will be needed to address this question. Fourth, we primarily focused on survival outcomes, and the impact of S-nitrosylation-related signatures on therapeutic response—particularly to chemotherapy and immunotherapy—was not systematically evaluated. Moreover, the immunologic consequences of S-nitrosylation within the tumor microenvironment warrant further investigation, as NO-mediated redox modulation may affect immune evasion and therapeutic responsiveness.

Future work should therefore integrate prospective multi-center cohorts, quantitative S-nitrosylation proteomics, and in vivo functional studies of LAMC2 and related pathways, as well as systematic evaluation of how S-nitrosylation-based signatures relate to treatment response and immune checkpoint blockade in pancreatic cancer.

In summary, this study provides a comprehensive overview of the role of S-nitrosylation in pancreatic cancer and introduces a powerful machine-learning-based prognostic model with strong external validity. The identification of LAMC2 as a key oncogenic effector bridges molecular discovery and functional validation, offering novel insights into the redox-driven mechanisms underlying pancreatic cancer aggressiveness. Our findings lay the foundation for future translational efforts aimed at integrating S-nitrosylation signatures into precision diagnosis, prognostic evaluation, and targeted therapy development.

A full splitting algorithm for structured difference-of-convex programs Thanks: Submitted to editors DATE.
Radu Ioan Boţ, Rossen Nenov, Min Tao

Abstract
In this paper, we study a class of nonconvex and nonsmooth structured difference-of-convex (DC) programs, which contain in the convex part the sum of a nonsmooth linearly composed convex function and a differentiable function, and in the concave part another nonsmooth linearly composed convex function. Among the various areas in which such problems occur, we would like to mention in particular the recovery of sparse signals. We propose an adaptive double-proximal, full-splitting algorithm with a moving center approach in the final subproblem, which addresses the challenge of evaluating compositions by decoupling the linear operator from the nonsmooth component. We establish the subsequential convergence of the generated sequence of iterates to an approximate stationary point and prove its global convergence under the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz property. We also discuss the tightness of the convergence results and provide insights into the rationale for seeking an approximate KKT point. This is illustrated by constructing a counterexample showing that the algorithm can diverge when seeking exact solutions. Finally, we present a practical version of the algorithm that incorporates a nonmonotone line search, which significantly improves the convergence performance.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the following nonsmooth difference-of-convex (DC) program

\displaystyle\min_{{\mathbf{x}}\in S}F({\mathbf{x}}):=g(A{\mathbf{x}})+\varphi({\mathbf{x}})-f(K{\mathbf{x}}),

(1)

where
S\subseteq\R^{n}
is a nonempty, convex and compact set,
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{p}\rightarrow{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}:={\mathbb{R}}\cup\{{\pm}\infty\}
and
g:{\mathbb{R}}^{s}\rightarrow{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}
are proper, convex and lower semicontinuous functions,
A:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{s}
and
K:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{p}
are linear operators, and
\varphi:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is a differentiable function with
\ell_{\nabla\varphi}
-Lipschitz continuous gradient.

The study of this problem has attracted considerable interest in recent years due to its wide range of applications in various fields, most notably image processing
[
31
]
, machine learning
[
26
]
, optimal transport
[
20
]
, cryptography
[
27

and sparse signal recovery
[
23

. A more detailed overview of applications can be found in
[
28

.

DC programming is particularly challenging due to the nonsmooth and nonconvex nature of the objective function. One of the best known iterative methods for solving DC problems is the DC algorithm (DCA) introduced in
[
38
]
. DCA linearizes the concave part of the objective function using subgradients and solves the resulting convex subproblem in each iteration.

In general, DCA poses two main challenges. First, the resulting convex subproblem can be difficult to solve, especially in practical applications where the problem structure is complex or high-dimensional. In such cases, it may be necessary to use a specialized non-smooth nonlinear optimization solver to effectively deal with the subproblems that arise during DCA iterations. Secondly, it relies entirely on subgradients of the concave part, which can be disadvantageous due to the possible nonuniqueness of the subgradients and the highly discontinuous nature of the subdifferential.

Developments in DC programming have focused on enhancing both the efficiency and convergence properties of DCA. One advancement in this regard is the evaluation of the convex component of the objective function by its proximal point operator, which has been shown to improve the stability and the convergence rates of the algorithm
[
37
]
. Additionally, inexact proximal Newton methods have been applied to DC programming in
[
32
]
.
Another notable contribution is the development of the refined inertial DC algorithm
[
42
]
, which integrates inertia into the optimization process to accelerate convergence while maintaining stability. Furthermore, stochastic variants of DCA have been explored to tackle large-scale problems where deterministic approaches become computationally prohibitive
[
39
]
. Another recent direction is the use of Armijo’s backtracking linesearch rule
[
1
]
to improve convergence. Work in this direction can be found in
[
2
]
, which proposed the so-called Boosted DCA (BDCA) method for minimizing unconstrained smooth DC functions, and in
[
5
]
, which extended the former work for some classes of nonsmooth DC functions, and in
[
3
]
for linearly constrained DC programs.

In
[
25
,
24
,
33
]
, a family of DC bundle methods has been developed based on the idea of replacing the convex subproblems in DCA at each iteration by strictly convex quadratic programs. Another notable approach is Sequential Difference-of-Convex Programming (SDCP)
[
34
]
, which replaces the first component with a convex model tailored to the feasible set and objective structure, while directly handling the concave part to simplify the computation of critical points.

The work
[
9
]
represents a significant advance in this field by proposing a double-proximal gradient algorithm that evaluates both the convex and concave parts of the objective function using their respective proximal point operators. These evaluations differ from traditional methods, as they do not need to linearize either function. Furthermore, the inclusion of a linear operator in the concave part has been considered and evaluated in a forward manner similar to primal-dual full splitting methods.

This paper aims to extend this work by including a linear operator in the convex part of the objective function and developing a novel full splitting scheme. It relies only on gradient evaluation of the smooth component, proximal evaluation of the convex and concave parts separately, and conjugate unfolding to evaluate the linear operators by forward evaluation.

This paper is structured as follows. Section
2
introduces the necessary preliminary definitions and results, while Section
3
states the main assumptions and notions of stationarity. Section
4
introduces the conceptual full splitting scheme and discusses adaptive step size choices. Subsection
4.3
introduces the Adaptive DPFS
mc
algorithm and presents its asymptotic convergence results. Section
5
discusses the global convergence of the proposed algorithm under the assumption of the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz property, and Section
6
proves convergence rates and theoretical results for functions with Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz exponents. Finally, in Section
8
we perform numerical experiments demonstrating the effectiveness of our method for audio signal denoising.

Double proximal full splitting algorithm

4.1
Conceptual algorithmic framework

In this subsection, our aim is to formulate a first-order algorithm characterized by a “single-loop” structure, grounded in the overarching concept of full splitting as established in previous work

\varphi({\mathbf{x}}^{k+1})\leq\varphi({\mathbf{x}}^{k})+\langle{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k},\nabla\varphi({\mathbf{x}}^{k})\rangle+\frac{\ell_{\nabla\varphi}}{2}\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k}\|^{2}.

Adding the above two statements, yields

\displaystyle\ \varphi({\mathbf{x}}^{k+1})+\langle{\mathbf{z}}^{k},A{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}\rangle-\langle K{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1},{\mathbf{y}}^{k}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \varphi({\mathbf{x}}^{k})+\langle{\mathbf{z}}^{k},A{\mathbf{x}}^{k}\rangle-\langle K{\mathbf{x}}^{k},{\mathbf{y}}^{k}\rangle-\frac{{2\rho_{k}}-\ell_{\nabla\varphi}}{2}\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}\|^{2},

which, by invoking the definition of
\Psi
in (

), is nothing but

\displaystyle\Psi({\mathbf{x}}^{k+1},{\mathbf{y}}^{k},{\mathbf{z}}^{k})\leq\Psi({\mathbf{x}}^{k},{\mathbf{y}}^{k},{\mathbf{z}}^{k})-\frac{{2\rho_{k}}-\ell_{\nabla\varphi}}{2}\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}\|^{2}.

(14)

On the other hand, from (

) we get that

f^{*}({\mathbf{y}}^{k+1})-\langle K{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1},{\mathbf{y}}^{k+1}\rangle+\frac{\beta_{k}}{2}\|{\mathbf{y}}^{k+1}-{\mathbf{y}}^{k}\|^{2}\leq f^{*}({\mathbf{y}}^{k})-\langle K{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1},{\mathbf{y}}^{k}\rangle,

which, by invoking again the definition of
\Psi
, is nothing but

\displaystyle\Psi({\mathbf{x}}^{k+1},{\mathbf{y}}^{k+1},{\mathbf{z}}^{k})\leq\Psi({\mathbf{x}}^{k+1},{\mathbf{y}}^{k},{\mathbf{z}}^{k})-\frac{\beta_{k}}{2}\|{\mathbf{y}}^{k}-{\mathbf{y}}^{k+1}\|^{2}.

(15)

Further, from (
9c
) we have that

A{\mathbf{x}}^{k}-\alpha_{k-1}({\mathbf{z}}^{k}-\delta_{k-1}{\mathbf{z}}^{k-1})\in\partial g^{*}({\mathbf{z}}^{k}),

which leads to

\displaystyle-g^{*}({\mathbf{z}}^{k+1})+\langle{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1},A{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}\rangle\leq

\displaystyle\ -g^{*}({\mathbf{z}}^{k})+\langle{\mathbf{z}}^{k},A{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k},A{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}-\!(A{\mathbf{x}}^{k}-\alpha_{k-1}({\mathbf{z}}^{k}-\delta_{k-1}{\mathbf{z}}^{k-1}))\rangle.

(16)

We can regroup

\displaystyle\ \langle{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k},A{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}-(A{\mathbf{x}}^{k}-\alpha_{k-1}({\mathbf{z}}^{k}-\delta_{k-1}{\mathbf{z}}^{k-1}))\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \left\langle{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k},(A{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}-A{\mathbf{x}}^{k})+(\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha){\mathbf{z}}^{k}+\alpha({\mathbf{z}}^{k}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k-1})\right\rangle,

and estimate the individual parts as follows

\displaystyle\left\langle{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k},A{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}-A{\mathbf{x}}^{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sigma_{A}}{2(\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha)}\left\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha)\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}\|^{2},

\displaystyle(\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha)\left\langle{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k},{\mathbf{z}}^{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha)(\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}\|^{2}-\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}\|^{2}-\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}\|^{2}),

\displaystyle\alpha\left\langle{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k},{\mathbf{z}}^{k}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k-1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}\|^{2}+\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k-1}\|^{2}\right).

Plugging these inequalities into (
16
), and using again the definition of
\Psi
, it yields

\displaystyle\ \Psi({\mathbf{x}}^{k+1},{\mathbf{y}}^{k+1},{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1})-\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{k}-\alpha)\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \Psi({\mathbf{x}}^{k+1},{\mathbf{y}}^{k+1},{\mathbf{z}}^{k})-\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha)\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha}{2}(\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}\|^{2}+\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k-1}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k}\|^{2})+\frac{\sigma_{A}}{2(\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha)}\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha_{k})\left(\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}\|^{2}-\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}\|^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha_{k})\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}\|^{2}.

(17)

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle A{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}-\alpha_{k}{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}+\alpha{\mathbf{z}}^{k}\in\partial g^{*}({\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}),

\displaystyle A{\mathbf{x}}^{k}-\alpha_{k-1}{\mathbf{z}}^{k}+\alpha{\mathbf{z}}^{k-1}\in\partial g^{*}({\mathbf{z}}^{k}),

and the monotonicity of
\partial g^{*}
gives

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\left\langle{\mathbf{z}}^{k}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1},A{\mathbf{x}}^{k}-A{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}+\alpha_{k}{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}-\alpha_{k-1}{\mathbf{z}}^{k}+\alpha({\mathbf{z}}^{k-1}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle{\mathbf{z}}^{k}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1},A{\mathbf{x}}^{k}-\!A{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}+\!(\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{k-1}){\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}\!-\!\alpha_{k-1}({\mathbf{z}}^{k}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1})+\alpha({\mathbf{z}}^{k-1}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k})\right\rangle.

(18)

We estimate some of the above scalar products by using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\displaystyle\langle{{\mathbf{z}}^{k}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1},A{\mathbf{x}}^{k}-A{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sigma_{A}}{2(\alpha_{k}-\alpha)}\left\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{k}-\alpha)\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}\|^{2},

\displaystyle\alpha\left\langle{\mathbf{z}}^{k}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1},{{\mathbf{z}}^{k-1}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(\left\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}+\left\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k-1}\right\|^{2}\right),

and rewrite the others as

\displaystyle(\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{k-1})\left\langle{\mathbf{z}}^{k}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1},{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{k-1})\left(\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}\|^{2}-\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}\|^{2}-\left\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}\right),

\displaystyle-\alpha_{k-1}\left\langle{\mathbf{z}}^{k}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1},{\mathbf{z}}^{k}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\alpha_{k-1}\left\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}.

Plugging the above into (
18
) gives

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{k}-\alpha)\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}\|^{2}+\frac{\sigma_{A}}{2(\alpha_{k}-\alpha)}\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{k-1})\left[\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}\|^{2}-\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}\|^{2}\right]-\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}\|^{2}-\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k-1}\|^{2}\right),

and then after multiplication by
\frac{4\alpha}{\alpha_{k}-\alpha}

\displaystyle\frac{2\alpha^{2}}{\alpha_{k+1}-\alpha}\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{2\alpha^{2}}{\alpha_{k}-\alpha}\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k-1}\|^{2}+\frac{2\alpha\sigma_{A}}{(\alpha_{k}-\alpha)^{2}}\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle-\left(2\alpha-2\alpha^{2}\frac{\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{k+1}}{(\alpha_{k+1}-\alpha)(\alpha_{k}-\alpha)}\right)\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}\|^{2}+2\alpha\frac{\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{k-1}}{\alpha_{k}-\alpha}(\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}\|^{2}-\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}\|^{2}).

Adding the above inequality to (
17
) yields

\displaystyle\Psi({\mathbf{x}}^{k+1},{\mathbf{y}}^{k+1},{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1})-\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{k}-\alpha)\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}\|^{2}+\left(\frac{2\alpha^{2}}{\alpha_{k+1}-\alpha}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \Psi({\mathbf{x}}^{k+1},{\mathbf{y}}^{k+1},{\mathbf{z}}^{k})-\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha)\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}\|^{2}+\left(\frac{2\alpha^{2}}{\alpha_{k}-\alpha}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k-1}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\sigma_{A}\left(\frac{1}{2(\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha)}+\frac{2\alpha}{(\alpha_{k}-\alpha)^{2}}\right)\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle-\alpha\left(1-2\alpha\frac{\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{k+1}}{(\alpha_{k+1}-\alpha)(\alpha_{k}-\alpha)}\right)\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\left[2\alpha\frac{\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{k-1}}{\alpha_{k}-\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha_{k})\right](\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}\|^{2}-\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}\|^{2})+\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha_{k})\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}\|^{2}}_{\triangledown}.

We regroup the terms in
\triangledown
as follows

\displaystyle\triangledown=

\displaystyle\ \ \ \left(\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha_{k})-2\alpha\frac{\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{k-1}}{\alpha_{k}-\alpha}\right)\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{k-2}-\alpha_{k-1})-2\alpha\frac{\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha_{k-2}}{\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha}\right)\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{k-2}-\alpha_{k-1})+2\alpha\left(\frac{\alpha_{k-2}-\alpha_{k-1}}{\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha}-\frac{\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha_{k}}{\alpha_{k}-\alpha}\right)\right)\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}\|^{2}.

We recall the definition of the discrete energy function given in (
10
)

\displaystyle\psi_{k+1}:=

\displaystyle\ \Psi({\mathbf{x}_{k+1}},{\mathbf{y}}_{k+1},{\mathbf{z}}_{k+1})+\alpha\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{2\alpha}{(\alpha_{k+1}-\alpha)}\right)\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha)-2\alpha\frac{\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{k-1}}{\alpha_{k}-\alpha}\right)\left\|\mathbf{z}^{k+1}\right\|^{2}

and, by combining the previous inequality with (
14
) and (
15
), conclude

\displaystyle\psi_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\psi_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\left({2\rho_{k}}-\ell_{\nabla\varphi}-2\sigma_{A}\left(\frac{1}{2(\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha)}+\frac{2\alpha}{(\alpha_{k}-\alpha)^{2}}\right)\right)\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\beta_{k}}{2}\|{\mathbf{y}}^{k}-{\mathbf{y}}^{k+1}\|^{2}-\alpha\left(1-2\alpha\frac{\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{k+1}}{(\alpha_{k+1}-\alpha)(\alpha_{k}-\alpha)}\right)\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{k-2}-\alpha_{k-1})+2\alpha\left(\frac{\alpha_{k-2}-\alpha_{k-1}}{\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha}-\frac{\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha_{k}}{\alpha_{k}-\alpha}\right)\right)\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}\|^{2}.

This is nothing but (
13
).

4.2.1
Construction of dynamical step sizes to foster convergence

In the following, we outline a possible regime for the step size sequences arising in DPFS, which induces desirable convergence properties for the discrete energy sequence and consequently for the sequence of generated iterates.

Theorem 7
.

Let Assumption
3
be true, and
\gamma>0
,
\frac{\gamma}{2}>\alpha>0
and
\mu>0
. Suppose that the parameter sequences
(\alpha_{k})_{k\geq 0}
,
(\beta_{k})_{k\geq 0}
,
(\delta_{k})_{k\geq 0}
and
({\rho_{k}})_{k\geq 0}
in DPFS satisfy the following properties:

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}\alpha_{k}=\alpha\ \mbox{and}\ \alpha+\gamma\geq\alpha_{0}\geq\alpha_{k}\geq\alpha_{k+1}>\alpha\quad\forall k\geq 0,

(19)

with

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{k+1}}{(\alpha_{k+1}-\alpha)(\alpha_{k}-\alpha)}\leq\frac{1}{\gamma}\ \mbox{and}\ (\alpha_{k}-\alpha)(\alpha_{k+2}-\alpha)\geq(\alpha_{k+1}-\alpha)^{2}\quad\forall k\geq 0,

(20)

\displaystyle+\infty>{\overline{\beta}}\geq\beta_{k}\geq{\underline{\beta}}>0,\quad\forall k\geq 0,

(21)

\displaystyle\delta_{k}:=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha_{k}}\quad\forall k\geq 0,

(22)

and

\displaystyle\rho_{k}:={\frac{\ell_{\nabla\varphi}}{2}+\frac{3\sigma_{A}\gamma}{2(\alpha_{k}-\alpha)^{2}}+\mu}\quad\forall k\geq 0.

(23)

Let
({\mathbf{x}}^{k},{\mathbf{y}}^{k},{\mathbf{z}}^{k})_{k\geq 0}
be the sequence generated by (
9
). If
({\mathbf{z}}^{k})_{k\geq 0}
is bounded, then the following statements are true:

(i)

The sequence of
(\psi_{k})_{k\geq 2}
is bounded from below.

(ii)

The sequence of
(\psi_{k})_{k\geq 2}
is convergent,
i.e.,

\lim\limits_{k\to+\infty}\psi_{k}\in\R.

(iii)

The sequences of discrete velocities vanish, i.e.,

\mathop{\lim}\limits_{k\to+\infty}\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}\|=0,\quad\mathop{\lim}\limits_{k\to+\infty}\|{\mathbf{y}}^{k}-{\mathbf{y}}^{k+1}\|=0,\quad\mathop{\lim}\limits_{k\to+\infty}\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}\|=0.

Proof.

(i) Let
\ell\geq 0
be such that
\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}\|\leq\ell
for all
k\geq 0
. For all
k\geq 2
it holds

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{k-2}-\alpha)+2\alpha\frac{\alpha_{k-2}-\alpha_{k-1}}{\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha}=

\displaystyle\ (\alpha_{k-2}-\alpha)\left(\frac{1}{2}+2\alpha\frac{\alpha_{k-2}-\alpha_{k-1}}{(\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha)(\alpha_{k-2}-\alpha)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{2\alpha}{\gamma}\right)\leq\frac{3}{2}\gamma,

consequently,

-\left(\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{k-2}-\alpha)+2\alpha\frac{\alpha_{k-2}-\alpha_{k-1}}{\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha}\right)\left\|\mathbf{z}^{k}\right\|^{2}\geq-\frac{3}{2}\gamma\ell^{2}.

By (
9c
), we have for all
k\geq 1
that

\displaystyle g^{*}({\mathbf{z}}^{k+1})-\langle A{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1},{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq g^{*}({\mathbf{z}}^{1})-\langle A{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1},{\mathbf{z}}^{1}\rangle+\frac{\alpha_{k}}{2}\|{\mathbf{z}}^{1}-\delta_{k}{\mathbf{z}}^{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq g^{*}({\mathbf{z}}^{1})+\left\|A{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}\right\|\left\|{\mathbf{z}}^{1}\right\|+\alpha_{0}\left\|{\mathbf{z}}^{1}\right\|^{2}+\alpha_{0}\delta_{k}^{2}\left\|\mathbf{z}^{k}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq g^{*}({\mathbf{z}}^{1})+\ell\left\|A{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}\right\|+2\alpha_{0}\ell^{2}.

We define
{\cal C}:=g^{*}({\mathbf{z}}^{1})+2\alpha_{0}\ell^{2}+\sup_{{\mathbf{x}}\in S}\{g(A\mathbf{x})+\ell\left\|A{\mathbf{x}}\right\|\}
, which is well-defined since
S
is a compact set and
A(S)\subseteq\mathrm{ri}(\dom g)
. Therefore we have that for
all
k\geq 1

\displaystyle\Psi({\mathbf{x}}^{k+1},{\mathbf{y}}^{k+1},{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1})

\displaystyle\geq-g^{*}({\mathbf{z}}^{1})-2\alpha_{0}\ell^{2}-\ell\left\|A{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}\right\|-g(A{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1})

\displaystyle\quad+g(A{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1})+\varphi({\mathbf{x}}^{k+1})-\langle K{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1},{\mathbf{y}}^{k+1}\rangle+f^{*}({\mathbf{y}}^{k+1})

\displaystyle\geq F({\mathbf{x}}^{k+1})-{\cal C}\geq\underline{F}-{\cal C}>-\infty.

(24)

Therefore
(\psi_{k+1})_{k\geq 1}
is bounded from below and assertion (i) is fulfilled.

(ii) For all
k\geq 2
it holds

\displaystyle\frac{2\alpha}{(\alpha_{k}-\alpha)^{2}}+\frac{1}{2(\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha)}\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{2\alpha}{(\alpha_{k}-\alpha)^{2}}+\frac{1}{2(\alpha_{k}-\alpha)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{\gamma}{(\alpha_{k}-\alpha)^{2}}+\frac{\gamma}{2(\alpha_{0}-\alpha)(\alpha_{k}-\alpha)}<\frac{3\gamma}{2(\alpha_{k}-\alpha)^{2}},

(25)

from where we deduce that

c_{1}^{k}=\frac{1}{2}\left(2\rho_{k}-\ell_{\nabla\varphi}-2\sigma_{A}\left(\frac{1}{2(\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha)}+\frac{2\alpha}{(\alpha_{k}-\alpha)^{2}}\right)\right)>\mu>0.

Furthermore, for all
k\geq 2
,
c_{2}^{k}=\beta_{k}>0
and

c_{3}^{k}=\alpha\left(1-2\alpha\frac{\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{k+1}}{(\alpha_{k+1}-\alpha)(\alpha_{k}-\alpha)}\right)\geq\alpha\left(1-\frac{2\alpha}{\gamma}\right)>0.

By the given assumptions on the sequence
(\alpha_{k})_{k\geq 0}
, we have for all
k\geq 2

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha_{k-2}-\alpha_{k-1}}{\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha}\right)\;-\;\left(\frac{\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha_{k}}{\alpha_{k}-\alpha}\right)=

\displaystyle\;\frac{\alpha_{k-2}-\alpha}{\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha}\;-\;\frac{\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha}{\alpha_{k}-\alpha}\geq 0,

consequently,

\displaystyle 0\leq\omega_{k}\leq\left[\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{k-2}-\alpha_{k-1})+2\alpha\left(\frac{\alpha_{k-2}-\alpha_{k-1}}{\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha}-\frac{\alpha_{k-1}-\alpha_{k}}{\alpha_{k}-\alpha}\right)\right]\ell^{2}.

Summing these inequalities over
k=2\dots N
, we obtain

\displaystyle 0\leq\sum_{k=2}^{N}\omega_{k}

\displaystyle<\left[\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{0}+2\alpha\left(\frac{\alpha_{0}-\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{1}-\alpha}\right)\right]\ell^{2}.

Letting
N\to+\infty
, we derive that
\sum_{k=2}^{+\infty}\omega_{k}<+\infty
and
\mathop{\lim}\limits_{k\to+\infty}\omega_{k}=0
.
Now, invoking equation (
13
) and noting that the sequence
(\psi_{k})_{k\geq 2}
is bounded from below, by Lemma
2
, it follows that
\lim\limits_{k\to+\infty}\psi_{k}\in\R
and

\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{+\infty}\Big(c_{1}^{k}\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}\|^{2}+c_{2}^{k}\|{\mathbf{y}}^{k}-{\mathbf{y}}^{k+1}\|^{2}+c_{3}^{k}\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k}\|^{2}\Big)<+\infty.

(iii) Since

\varsigma:=\inf_{k\geq 0}\min(c_{1}^{k},c_{2}^{k},c_{3}^{k})\geq\min(\mu,\alpha(1-2\alpha/\gamma),\inf_{k\geq 0}\beta_{k})>0,

it yields

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\|{\mathbf{x}}^{k}-{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}\|^{2}<+\infty,\;\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\|{\mathbf{y}}^{k}-{\mathbf{y}}^{k+1}\|^{2}<+\infty,\;\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\|{\mathbf{z}}^{k}-{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}\|^{2}<+\infty.

Assertion (iii) follows directly.

Remark 8
(Explicit step size regime).

To simplify the arguments, let us choose
\alpha_{0}:=\alpha+\gamma
. Focusing on the assumptions stated in (
20
) for the sequence
(\alpha_{k})_{k\geq 0}
, we observe that for all
k\geq 0

\displaystyle 0\leq\frac{1}{\alpha_{k+1}-\alpha}-\frac{1}{\alpha_{k}-\alpha}=\frac{\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{k+1}}{(\alpha_{k+1}-\alpha)(\alpha_{k}-\alpha)}\leq\frac{1}{\gamma}.

Applying a telescoping sum over the indices
k=0,\dots,K
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha_{K+1}-\alpha}\leq\frac{K+1}{\gamma}+\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}-\alpha}=\frac{K+2}{\gamma},

for
K\geq 0
and see that
\alpha_{K+1}\geq\alpha+\frac{\gamma}{K+2}
has to hold. Moreover, the condition
(\alpha_{k}-\alpha)(\alpha_{k+2}-\alpha)\geq(\alpha_{k+1}-\alpha)^{2}
ensures that the sequence
(\alpha_{k}-\alpha)_{k\geq 0}
exhibits log-convexity. This property holds, for instance, for sequences of the form
(\frac{c}{(k+1)^{p}})_{k\geq 0}
with
p>0
. Thus it is easy to see that the conditions in (
19
) and (
20
) are satisfied for sequences of the form

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}=\alpha+\frac{\gamma}{(k+1)^{r}}

(26)

with
r\in(0,1]
for all
k\geq 0
.

Although the discrete velocities vanish, as seen in Theorem
7
(iii), this does yet not necessarily imply that the sequence generated by DPFS approaches the set of KKT points of (
1
). In the following we give an counterexample which illustrates that
may not be possible to achieve even subsequential convergence to an KKT point due to the decreasing step size sequence
(\frac{1}{\rho_{k}})_{k\geq 1}
.

4.2.2
Examining subsequential convergence to KKT points

We see in (
23
) that the step size
\frac{1}{\rho_{k}}
in the update (
9a
) of DFPS tends to zero as iterations increase. This can be expected to affect the convergence properties of the algorithm. In the following, we present an example that serves to demonstrate that, indeed, if the parameters of
\rho_{k}
,
\beta_{k}
,
\alpha_{k}
, and
\delta_{k}

are chosen according to the requirements of Theorem
7
, the subsequential convergence of DPFS to an exact KKT point may not be achieved.

Example 9
.

Consider the optimization problem (
1
) with
n=p=s=2
,
{S}:=\{{\mathbf{x}}\in\R^{2}:\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}\leq{200}\}
, the functions
g({\mathbf{x}})=\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{1}
,
\varphi({\mathbf{x}})=-\frac{1}{2}\|{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}^{2}
, and
f({\mathbf{x}})={\mathbf{c}}^{\top}{\mathbf{x}}
, where
{\mathbf{c}}=(2,2)^{\top}
, and
A=K=I
, the identity operator on
\R^{2}
. Then
g^{*}=\iota_{{\cal B}_{\infty}}
, where
{\cal B}_{\infty}
denotes the unit ball in the sup-norm in
\R^{2}
, and
f^{*}=\iota_{\{\mathbf{c}\}}
.

In accordance to Theorem
7
, we choose
\gamma=3
,
\alpha=1
,
\mu=\frac{1}{2}
,
\alpha_{k}=\alpha+\frac{\gamma}{k+1}
, and

{\rho_{k}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3}{2\gamma}(k+1)^{2}}+\mu=1+\frac{1}{2}(k+1)^{2}\ \forall k\geq 0.

The iterative scheme (
9
) is for all
k\geq 0
as follows

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}={\text{\rm Proj}}_{S}\left[(1+\frac{1}{\rho_{k}}){\mathbf{x}}^{k}-\frac{1}{\rho_{k}}{\mathbf{z}}^{k}+\frac{1}{\rho_{k}}{\mathbf{c}}\right],\\
{\mathbf{y}}^{k+1}={\mathbf{c}},\\
{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}=\proj_{{\cal B}_{\infty}}\left(\frac{{\mathbf{z}}^{k}+{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}}{\alpha_{k}}\right).\end{array}\right.

For the starting point we assume that
{\mathbf{x}}^{0}=({0,0})^{\top}
and
\|{\mathbf{z}}^{0}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
.
We are going to show that
(\mathbf{x}^{k})_{k\geq 0}
remains in the interior of
S
and that none of the accumulation points of
(\mathbf{x}^{k},\mathbf{y}^{k},\mathbf{z}^{k})_{k\geq 0}
is a KKT point of (
9
).

We denote
{\mathbf{u}}^{k}:=(1+\frac{1}{\rho_{k}}){\mathbf{x}}^{k}-\frac{1}{\rho_{k}}{\mathbf{z}}^{k}+\frac{1}{\rho_{k}}{\mathbf{c}}
for all
k\geq 0
and prove by induction that
\left\|{\mathbf{u}}^{k}\right\|_{2}<150
for all
k\geq 0
.
By our choice,
\left\|{\mathbf{u}}^{0}\right\|_{2}<150
holds. Assume that for
K\in\mathbb{N}
,
\left\|{\mathbf{u}}^{k}\right\|_{2}<150
holds for all
0\leq k\leq K
. This means that
{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}={\mathbf{u}}^{k}

Global convergence under the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz property

In this section, we discuss the convergence of the sequence
({\mathbf{x}}^{k},{\mathbf{y}}^{k},{\mathbf{z}}^{k})_{k\geq 0}
generated by Algorithm

in the framework of the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz (KL) property.

The framework in which we are going to do this is set out in the following assumption.

Assumption 16
.

Assume that
g
essentially strictly convex and
{\text{\rm int}}(\dom f)\neq\emptyset

We state the definition of the KL property.

Definition 17
.

A proper function
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}
is said to satisfy the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz (KL) property at a point
{\widehat{\mathbf{x}}}\in{\text{\rm dom }}\partial h
if there exist a constant
\eta\in(0,+\infty]
, an open neighborhood

U
of
{\widehat{\mathbf{x}}},
and a concave and continuous function
\phi:\;[0,\eta)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
with the properties

(i)

\phi(0)=0
;

(ii)

\phi
is continuously differentiable on
(0,\eta)
and
\phi^{\prime}{(\cdot)}>0
on
(0,\eta)
;

(iii)

for every
\mathbf{x}\in U
with
h({\widehat{\mathbf{x}}})<h(\mathbf{x})<h(\widehat{\mathbf{x}})+\eta
, it holds

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(h(\mathbf{x})-h(\widehat{\mathbf{x}})){\text{\rm{dist}}}(\mathbf{0},\partial h({\mathbf{x}}))\geq 1.

The class of so-called desingularization functions
\phi:[0,\eta)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
fulfilling (i)-(iii) is denoted by
\Phi_{\eta}
.

Remark 18
.

The classical Definition
17
of the Kurdyka–Łojasiewicz (KL) property assumes the function
h
is
proper and lower semicontinuous
on the whole
space. In the subsequent analysis we would like to assume that the merit function
\varpi
satisfies the KL property, however, since it contains the concave term

-g^{\ast}
, it can fail to be everywhere lower semicontinuous. On the other hand, by Assumption
16
,
-g^{\ast}
is differentiable on

\mathrm{int}(\dom g^{*})=\dom\partial g^{\ast}
, therefore,

\displaystyle\ \varpi\ \text{is lower semicontinuous on }\R^{n}\times\R^{p}\times\mathrm{int}(\dom g^{*})\times\R^{s}

and

\displaystyle\ \dom\partial\varpi\subseteq\R^{n}\times\R^{p}\times\mathrm{int}(\dom g^{*})\times\R^{s}.

Therefore, when assuming in the following that
\varpi
satisfies the KL property at a point
\widehat{\mathbf{w}}:=(\widehat{\mathbf{x}},\widehat{\mathbf{y}},\widehat{\mathbf{z}},\widehat{\mathbf{u}})\in\dom\partial\varpi
, in the following we will restrict the open neighborhood
U
of
\widehat{\mathbf{w}}
such that
U\subseteq\R^{n}\times\R^{p}\times\mathrm{int}(\dom g^{*})\times\R^{s}
, on which
\varpi
is lower semicontinuous.

The class of functions that satisfy the KL includes semialgebraic, real subanalytic, uniformly convex functions and convex functions satisfying a growth condition (
[
7
,
8
,
12

18

The following lemma can be found in

8
Application to audio signal processing

Consider an audio signal of duration
T
seconds, sampled at a rate of
f_{s}
samples per second. For simplicity, we consider a mono audio signal, which we can represent by a vector
\mathbf{x}\in\R^{N}
of size
N=T\cdot f_{s}
, whose entries are typically in the range of
[-1,1]
, where
-1
represents the minimum amplitude, and 1 represents the maximum amplitude of the audio signal.
The original audio signal
\overline{\mathbf{x}}
is assumed to be corrupted with a noise
\eta
such that only the resultant
\mathbf{u}=\overline{\mathbf{x}}+\eta
is known to us.

We aim to reconstruct the original audio signal
\overline{\mathbf{x}}
by considering a minimization problem that aims to find and estimate
{\mathbf{x}}
that is as close as possible to the original
\overline{\mathbf{x}}
. This typically involves formulating an objective function that consists of a fidelity term
\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{u}\right\|_{2}^{2}
and a regularization term that imposes specific priors or assumptions about the signal
\mathbf{x}
.

Audio signals usually are not sparse in their time domain, but their time-frequency representation obtained through the Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) or discrete Gabor Transform can exhibit sparsity. The STFT is defined as an injective linear operator
\mathcal{T}:\R^{N}\to\mathbb{C}^{M}
, which maps a signal into the time-frequency domain
[
21
]
.
We want to reconstruct the original signal
\mathbf{x}
by considering the minimization problem

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\R^{N}}\frac{r_{1}}{2}\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{u}\right\|_{2}^{2}+R(\mathcal{T}\mathbf{x}),

where we denote by
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{2}
the usual Euclidean norm,
r_{1}>0
is a regularization parameter and
R:\mathbb{C}^{M}\to\R
is a regularizing functional promoting sparsity.

8.1
Difference of Convex (DC) regularizer

It is widely recognized that
\|\cdot\|_{1}
minimization encourages sparsity. Thanks to this property—along with its convexity—it has become a popular tool for promoting sparse solutions in optimization problems, especially in areas like compressed sensing and sparse signal recovery. However, despite these advantages, convex regularizers have inherent limitations when it comes to capturing more intricate behaviors, which can lead to suboptimal performance in certain cases
[
35
]
.

Recently, more sophisticated sparsity-promoting regularizers have been explored, such as
\|\cdot\|_{1}-\|\cdot\|_{2}
and
\frac{\|\cdot\|_{1}}{\|\cdot\|_{2}}
. These regularizers effectively capture sparse behavior, as demonstrated in
[
41
]
. Nonetheless, their adoption has been constrained by the lack of fast and efficient algorithms.

In this section we will consider regularizers of the form
\|\cdot\|_{1}-{r_{2}}\|\cdot\|_{2}
(
0<r_{2}<1
). A simple interpretation of its sparsity-inducing properties is as follows: the sparser the vector, the closer its
\|\cdot\|_{1}
norm and
\|\cdot\|_{2}
norm are. This difference highlights the balance between the sparsity promotion of the
\|\cdot\|_{1}
norm and the error distribution balancing of the
\|\cdot\|_{2}
norm, making it a potentially powerful tool in optimization problems where both properties are desirable. Additionally, the parameter
{r_{2}}
provides further flexibility for various applications.

We will therefore consider the minimization problem

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{x}\in S}\varphi(\mathbf{x})+g(\mathcal{T}\mathbf{x})-f(\mathcal{T}\mathbf{x}),

(48)

where

\displaystyle g(\mathbf{x}):=\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{1}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{2}^{2},

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{x}):=r_{2}\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{2}^{2},

(49)

\displaystyle\varphi(\mathbf{x}):=\frac{r_{1}}{2}\left\|\mathbf{x}-{\mathbf{u}}\right\|_{2}^{2},

\displaystyle S:=\{\mathbf{x}\in\R^{N}:\left\|{\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{u}}\right\|_{2}\leq p\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{2}\},

(50)

\mathbf{u}\neq 0
is a fixed vector, corresponding to the corrupted measurement,
r_{1}
,
r_{2}
,
{\lambda}>0
are parameters, and
\mathcal{T}:\R^{N}\to\R^{2M}
is an injective linear operator. Here we use a simple zero sum trick to make
g
and
f
strongly convex, while keeping
g(\mathcal{T}\mathbf{x})-f(\mathcal{T}\mathbf{x})=\left\|\mathcal{T}\mathbf{x}\right\|_{1}-r_{2}\left\|\mathcal{T}\mathbf{x}\right\|_{2}
as desired. The constant
p\in(0,1)
serves as an upper bound on the relative noise level in
\mathbf{u}
, i.e.,
\frac{\left\|\eta\right\|_{2}}{\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{2}}
. In the absence of
a priori
knowledge, one can reasonably choose
p\leq\frac{1}{2}
which corresponds to the scenario where
\left\|\eta\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|\overline{\mathbf{x}}\right\|_{2}
. Recovering signals with higher noise levels is generally infeasible unless additional assumptions are imposed.

Note that
f
and
g
are strongly convex with modulus
\lambda
, and
\varphi
is strongly convex with modulus
r_{1}
. Moreover,
f
is differentiable with a locally Lipschitz continuous gradient over any compact convex set not containing the origin. Thus the conditions on
f
and
g
in Theorem
27
are met at every
{\mathbf{x}}\in S
.

8.2
KL exponent of the merit function

For
{\widetilde{\gamma}}=\widetilde{\alpha}-\alpha
, let

\displaystyle{F}_{{\widetilde{\gamma}}}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{G}_{g,\widetilde{\gamma}}({\cal T}{\mathbf{x}})+\varphi({\mathbf{x}})-{f}({\cal T}{\mathbf{x}}),

(51)

We want to prove that
{F}_{\widetilde{\gamma}}+\iota_{S}
satisfies the KL property at every critical point
\overline{\mathbf{x}}
with desingularization function
\phi(s)=\frac{\sigma}{1-\theta}s^{1-\theta}
with
\theta=\frac{1}{2}
for some
\sigma>0
. Then, by Theorem
29
, the corresponding merit function
{\varpi}
defined in (
29
) will satisfy the KL property at every critical point.

Indeed, for every critical point
(\overline{\mathbf{x}},\overline{\mathbf{y}},\overline{\mathbf{z}},\overline{\mathbf{u}})
of
\varpi
, it holds
\overline{\mathbf{u}}=\overline{\mathbf{z}}
, and
(\overline{\mathbf{x}},\overline{\mathbf{y}},\overline{\mathbf{z}})
satisfies (
47
). This means that
\overline{\mathbf{x}}
is a critical point of
F_{\widetilde{\gamma}}+\iota_{S}
, therefore, by Theorem
29
,
\varpi
satisfies the KL property at
(\overline{\mathbf{x}},\overline{\mathbf{y}},\overline{\mathbf{z}},\overline{\mathbf{u}})
with desingularization function
\phi(s)=2\sigma^{\prime}s^{\frac{1}{2}}
for some
\sigma^{\prime}>0
.

Lemma 30
.

Let
{\cal T}
be an injective linear operator and
\mu>0
be such that
\|{\cal T}\mathbf{x}\|_{2}\geq\mu
for every
\mathbf{x}\in S
. Then
{F}_{\widetilde{\gamma}}
is differentiable on
S
and it has a Lipschitz continuous gradient.
Moreover, if

r_{1}>\sigma_{\mathcal{T}}\left(\frac{2r_{2}}{\mu}+\lambda\right),

then
{F}_{\widetilde{\gamma}}
is strongly convex on
S
.

Proof.

Note that
{\bm{0}}\notin S
, therefore
{F}_{\widetilde{\gamma}}
is differentiable on
S
. For every
{\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}}\in S
it holds

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\mathbf{x}}{\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{2}}-\frac{\mathbf{y}}{\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{2}}\right\|_{2}\leq\frac{2}{\min_{\mathbf{x}\in S}\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{2}}\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\right\|_{2}.

Thus
f\circ\mathcal{T}
has a
\sigma_{\mathcal{T}}(\frac{2r_{2}}{\mu}+\lambda)
-Lipschitz continuous gradient on
S
. Since
\mathcal{G}_{g,\widetilde{\gamma}}
and
\varphi
have Lipschitz continuous gradient everywhere,
{F}_{\widetilde{\gamma}}
has Lipschitz continuous gradient on
S
.

Furthermore
-f\circ\mathcal{T}
is weakly convex on
S
with modulus
\sigma_{\mathcal{T}}(\frac{2r_{2}}{\mu}+\lambda)
.
Since
\mathcal{G}_{g,\widetilde{\gamma}}\circ\mathcal{T}
is convex and
\varphi
is strongly convex with modulus
r_{1}
, it yields that
{F}_{\widetilde{\gamma}}
is strongly convex on
S
if
r_{1}>\sigma_{\mathcal{T}}(\frac{2r_{2}}{\mu}+\lambda)
.

One could also leverage the strong convexity of
\mathcal{G}_{g,\widetilde{\gamma}}\circ\mathcal{T}
to relax the condition on
r_{1}
. However, its strong convexity modulus depends on
\widetilde{\gamma}=\widetilde{\alpha}-\alpha
, a quantity that varies with the iterates of the algorithm.

Theorem 31
.

Let
{\cal T}
be an injective linear operator and
\mu>0
be such that
\left\|{\cal T}\mathbf{x}\right\|_{2}\geq\mu
for all
\mathbf{x}\in S
. If
r_{1}>\sigma_{\mathcal{T}}(\frac{2r_{2}}{\mu}+\lambda)
, then
{F}_{\widetilde{\gamma}}+\iota_{S}
satisfies the KL property at every critical point
\mathbf{x}
with desingularization function
\phi(s)=2\sigma s^{\frac{1}{2}}
for some
\sigma>0
.

Proof.

Let
\overline{\mathbf{x}}
be a critical point of
F_{\widetilde{\gamma}}+\iota_{S}
. We distinguish two cases:

Case 1:

\overline{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathrm{int}(S)
.

By the strong convexity of
{F}_{\widetilde{\gamma}}
on
S
, there exists
\widetilde{\mu}>0
and a neighborhood
U\subseteq\mathrm{int}(S)
of
\overline{\mathbf{x}}
such that for every
\mathbf{x}\in U

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\widetilde{\mu}}\dist^{2}(\bm{0},\partial(F_{\widetilde{\gamma}}+\iota_{S})(\mathbf{x}))=\frac{1}{2\widetilde{\mu}}\left\|\nabla{F}_{\widetilde{\gamma}}(\mathbf{x})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq{F}_{\widetilde{\gamma}}(\mathbf{x})-{F}_{\widetilde{\gamma}}(\overline{\mathbf{x}})

Thus, the KL property holds in this case.

Case 2:

\overline{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathrm{bd}(S)
.

By the structure of
S
, the normal cone of
S
at
{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathrm{bd}(S)
is

N_{S}({\mathbf{x}})=\{\delta({\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{u})\mid\delta\geq 0\}.

Let
\overline{\delta}\geq 0
be such that
\overline{\xi}=\overline{\delta}(\overline{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{u})\in N_{S}(\overline{\mathbf{x}})
satisfies

\nabla{F}_{\widetilde{\gamma}}(\overline{\mathbf{x}})+\overline{\xi}=0.

Let
\mathbf{x}\in S
and every
\xi\in N_{S}(\mathbf{x})
. By the monotonicity of the normal cone operator, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\xi-\overline{\xi},\mathbf{x}-\overline{\mathbf{x}}\right\rangle\geq 0.

By the strong convexity of
{F}_{\widetilde{\gamma}}
on
S
, there exists
\widetilde{\mu}>0
such that

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla{F}_{\widetilde{\gamma}}(\mathbf{x})-\nabla{F}_{\widetilde{\gamma}}(\overline{\mathbf{x}}),\mathbf{x}-\overline{\mathbf{x}}\right\rangle\geq\frac{\widetilde{\mu}}{2}\left\|\mathbf{x}-\overline{\mathbf{x}}\right\|^{2}.

Combining the two, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\xi+\nabla{F}_{\widetilde{\gamma}}(\mathbf{x})\right\|\left\|\mathbf{x}-\overline{\mathbf{x}}\right\|

\displaystyle\geq\left\langle\xi+\nabla{F}_{\widetilde{\gamma}}(\mathbf{x}),\mathbf{x}-\overline{\mathbf{x}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\xi-\overline{\xi},\mathbf{x}-\overline{\mathbf{x}}\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla{F}_{\widetilde{\gamma}}(\mathbf{x})-\nabla{F}_{\widetilde{\gamma}}(\overline{\mathbf{x}}),\mathbf{x}-\overline{\mathbf{x}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\widetilde{\mu}}{2}\left\|\mathbf{x}-\overline{\mathbf{x}}\right\|^{2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\dist^{2}(\bm{0},\partial(F_{\widetilde{\gamma}}+\iota_{S})(\mathbf{x}))

\displaystyle=\inf_{\xi\in N_{S}(\mathbf{x})}\left\|\xi+\nabla{F}_{\widetilde{\gamma}}(\mathbf{x})\right\|^{2}\geq\frac{\widetilde{\mu}^{2}}{4}\left\|\mathbf{x}-\overline{\mathbf{x}}\right\|^{2}.

(52)

Denoting by
L>0
the Lipschitz constant of the gradient of
{F}_{\widetilde{\gamma}}
on
S
, for every
\mathbf{x}\in S
it holds

\displaystyle F_{\widetilde{\gamma}}(\mathbf{x})-F_{\widetilde{\gamma}}(\overline{\mathbf{x}})\leq\left\langle\nabla{F}_{\widetilde{\gamma}}(\overline{\mathbf{x}}),\mathbf{x}-\overline{\mathbf{x}}\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\left\|\mathbf{x}-\overline{\mathbf{x}}\right\|^{2}.

(53)

If
\overline{\delta}=0
, then
\nabla{F}_{\widetilde{\gamma}}(\overline{\mathbf{x}})=0
and the KL property follows directly from the two inequalities above.

If
\overline{\delta}>0
, then there exists a neighborhood
U
of
\overline{\mathbf{x}}
such that
{F}_{\widetilde{\gamma}}
is differentiable on
U
and
\left\|\nabla{F}_{\widetilde{\gamma}}({\mathbf{x}})\right\|>\epsilon
for some
\epsilon>0
and every
\mathbf{x}\in U
. Then, for every
\mathbf{x}\in U\cap\mathrm{int}(S)

\dist^{2}(0,\partial(F_{\widetilde{\gamma}}+\iota_{S})(\mathbf{x}))=\left\|\nabla{F}_{\widetilde{\gamma}}(\mathbf{x})\right\|^{2}>\epsilon^{2}.

For
\mathbf{x}\in U\cap\mathrm{bd}(S)
, note that
\left\|\overline{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{u}\right\|^{2}=\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{u}\right\|^{2}
, thus

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla{F}_{\widetilde{\gamma}}(\overline{\mathbf{x}}),\mathbf{x}-\overline{\mathbf{x}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\delta\left\langle-(\overline{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{u}),\mathbf{x}-\overline{\mathbf{x}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta}{2}\left\|\overline{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{u}\right\|^{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}\left\|\overline{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{x}\right\|^{2}-\frac{\delta}{2}\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{u}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta}{2}\left\|\overline{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{x}\right\|^{2}.

Thus, by (
52
) and (
53
), we can choose an open neighborhood
V\subseteq U
of
\overline{\mathbf{x}}
such that for every
\mathbf{x}\in V\cap S
it holds

\displaystyle\frac{2(L+\delta)}{\widetilde{\mu}^{2}}\dist^{2}(0,\partial(F_{\widetilde{\gamma}}+\iota_{S})(\mathbf{x}))\geq\frac{L+\delta}{2}\left\|\overline{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{x}\right\|^{2}\geq F_{\widetilde{\gamma}}(\mathbf{x})-F_{\widetilde{\gamma}}(\overline{\mathbf{x}}).

This concludes the proof.

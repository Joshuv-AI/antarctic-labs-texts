On Quantum Perceptron Learning via Quantum Search
Xiaoyu Sun, Mathieu Roget, Giuseppe Di Molfetta, and Hachem Kadri

Abstract
With the growing interest in quantum machine learning, the perceptron—a fundamental building block in traditional machine learning—has emerged as a valuable model for exploring quantum advantages. Two quantum perceptron algorithms based on Grover’s search , were developed in [ 18 ] to accelerate training and improve statistical efficiency in perceptron learning. This paper points out and corrects a mistake in the proof of [ 18 , Theorem 2] . Specifically, we show that the probability of sampling from a normal distribution for a D D -dimensional hyperplane that perfectly classifies the data scales as Ω ⁡ ( γ D ) \Omega(\gamma^{D}) instead of Θ ⁡ ( γ ) \Theta({\gamma}) , where γ \gamma is the margin. We then revisit two well-established linear programming algorithms—the ellipsoid method and the cutting plane random walk algorithm—in the context of perceptron learning, and show how quantum search algorithms can be leveraged to enhance the overall complexity. Specifically, both algorithms gain a sub-linear speed-up O ⁡ ( N ) O(\sqrt{N}) in the number of data points N N as a result of Grover’s algorithm and an additional O ⁡ ( D 1.5 ) O(D^{1.5}) speed-up is possible for cutting plane random walk algorithm employing quantum walk search.

IV
Weak Online Learning

IV-A

A General Framework of Weak Online Learning

In
[
18
]
, the authors suggested a weak online learning setup that effectively, examples are drawn uniformly from the training dataset
\{(\bm{x}_{1},y_{1}),\ldots,(\bm{x}_{N},y_{N})\}
, whereas in traditional online learning, the training data are fed to the algorithm one at a time in a streaming fashion. This weak online setup allows one to define a quantum analogue of the traditional perceptron algorithm, where a misclassified example (represented by quantum state) is drawn (measured) under a uniform superposition of the dataset; meanwhile, it also provokes a failure possibility of unsuccessful draws.
Under the context of convex optimization, the misclassified example is essentially a separation hyperplane, which is usually assumed to be provided by a separation oracle in most linear programming algorithms, such as ellipsoid and cutting plane algorithms.

Here we give a general description of the perceptron learning problem under the weak online learning setup. In the
t^{th}
round, a black box (a separation oracle) provides a misclassified example
(\bm{x}_{t}^{\prime},y_{t}^{\prime})
with success probability
\geq 1-\delta
(
\delta>0
), then the algorithm verifies whether the current perceptron model
\bm{w}_{t}
makes a mistake on it. If it is, update
\bm{w}_{t}
according to specified rules, e.g.,
\bm{w}_{t+1}=\bm{w}_{t}+\bm{x}_{t}^{\prime}y_{t}^{\prime}
for online perceptron; if it is not, re-query the black box for another misclassified data. Until the
r^{th}
round, a stop criterion is met and
\bm{w}_{r}\in\mathcal{VS}
. When the black box is a uniformly sampling machine,
O(N\log(1/\delta))
draws and classical queries (Eqn.(
1
)) need to be made to guarantee a failure possibility at most
\delta
in each round. When the black box is quantum Grover’s search subroutine, as described in the
online quantum perceptron
algorithm of
[
18
]
,
O(\sqrt{N}\log(1/\delta)
quantum queries (Eqn.(
2
)) suffice to find a misclassified example with a failure possibility at most
\delta
in each round. Let
\delta=O(\epsilon/r)
, by an argument of union bound,
O(r\sqrt{N}\log(r/\epsilon))
quantum queries ensures an overall failure possibility at most
\epsilon>0
.

Proposed in
[

, the cutting plane random walk algorithm provides another method to effectively reduce the volume in
\mathbb{R}^{D}
. In each round, the algorithm preserves an approximate centroid
\bm{z}_{t}
of current space, which we use as a perceptron model, similarly as proposed before in
[

. Initialized being the centre of
\mathcal{P}_{0}=B_{1}
, the perceptron updates its position by
\bm{z}_{t+1}\leftarrow\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\bm{w}^{\prime}_{j,t}
(Algorithm

\bm{w}^{\prime}_{j,t}
are uniformly sampled vectors over the remaining space
\{\bm{w}\mid(\bm{w}-\bm{z}_{t})^{T}\bm{x}_{t}^{\prime}y_{t}^{\prime}>0\}
cut by a misclassified example
(\bm{x}^{\prime}_{t},y^{\prime}_{t})
. When the sample size
N

1/3
with a high probability
[
2
]
. From Eqn.(
5
), one has
V(\mathcal{VS})/V(\mathcal{P}_{0})\geq\frac{\gamma^{D-1}}{\pi(D-1)}
, so letting

and
\bm{w}_{r}\in\mathcal{VS}
. A direct calculation yields

, hence the stop criterion can be

.

Similarly, we present an online quantum cutting plane algorithm by adopting Grover’s search subroutine and its extension of the quantum counting algorithm. By a straightforward use of Grover’s search, shown as
Qsearch
in Algorithm
2
, the overall queries complexity scales as
O(D\log{({\frac{1}{\gamma}})}\cdot\sqrt{N}\log(\frac{D\log(1/\gamma)}{\epsilon}))
, with a probability of failure at most
\epsilon
. For ease of analysing, we adapt the
hit-and-run

[
24
]

O^{*}(D^{3})
steps. In the
t^{th}

\bm{w}\in\mathcal{P}_{t}
and giving a

\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}_{t}}

queries to
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}_{t}}
leveraging binary search. A membership oracle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}_{t}}

\mathcal{P}_{t}

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}_{t}}(\bm{w})=g(\bm{w})\lor f_{\bm{w}}(\bm{x}^{\prime}_{1}y^{\prime}_{1},\bm{z}_{0})\lor\cdots\lor f_{\bm{w}}(\bm{x}^{\prime}_{t}y^{\prime}_{t},\bm{z}_{t-1}).

(6)

The functions

iff

,

\bm{w}\in\mathcal{P}_{t}
. Consequently, it takes

[
22
]
.

Additionally, there are

arithmetic operations by performing an affine transform in each round to ensure a warm start distribution. So in total, the computational complexity is
O^{*}(D\log{(\frac{1}{\gamma})}\cdot(\sqrt{N}+\log^{2}(N)\cdot D^{3}\cdot(\sqrt{t}+D^{2})))

O^{*}(D\log{(\frac{1}{\gamma})}(N+D^{5}\log{(\frac{1}{\gamma})}+D^{6}))

We note that it is proved in

V-A

Quantum Markov Chains

Here we start by briefly introducing some general concepts for quantum Markov chains
[
34
,
25
]
based on Szegedy’s quantum walk. In corresponds to an ergodic time-reversible Markov chain with finite state space
\operatorname{\Omega}
and transition matrix
P
, the Szegedy’s quantum walk is defined on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}
with basis states
\ket{x}\ket{y}
for
x,y\in\operatorname{\Omega}
,
|\operatorname{\Omega}|=d
. The quantum walk operator
W(P):=R_{\mathcal{B}}R_{\mathcal{A}}
, where
R_{\mathcal{I}}=2\operatorname{\Pi}_{\mathcal{I}}-\mathbb{1}
is the reflection about the
\mathcal{I}
subspace, with subspaces
\mathcal{A}=\textbf{span}(\ket{x}\ket{0}:x\in\operatorname{\Omega})
and
\mathcal{B}=\textbf{span}(\ket{0}\ket{x}:x\in\operatorname{\Omega})
.

For a classical distribution with probability density function
f
, one can define a quantum sample as
\ket{f}:=\sum_{x\in\operatorname{\Omega}}\sqrt{f(x)}\ket{x}
, with
\sum_{\bm{x}\in\operatorname{\Omega}}|f(\bm{x})|^{2}=1
. For a stationary distribution
\bm{\pi}_{t}
of an irreducible Markov chain with transition matrix
P_{t}
, its quantum sample accordingly is
\ket{\pi_{t}}^{\prime}=\sum_{x\in\operatorname{\Omega}}\sqrt{\pi_{t,x}}\ket{x}
, with
\ket{\pi_{t}}=\ket{\pi_{t}}^{\prime}\ket{0}\in\mathcal{H}
being a unique eigenvalue-
1
eigenvector of
W(P_{t})

[
34
,
36
]
.
A quantum update for
P_{t}
is defined as a unitary operator
U(P_{t})
that for all
x\in\operatorname{\Omega}
and
0\in\operatorname{\Omega}
,

U(P_{t})|x\rangle|0\rangle=\ket{x}\ket{p_{x}}=\ket{x}\sum_{y\in\operatorname{\Omega}}\sqrt{p_{xy}}\ket{y},

(7)

with
p_{xy}
being the transition probability from
x
to
y
. The quantum walk operator can be implemented as
W(P_{t})=U(P_{t})^{\dagger}SU(P_{t})R_{\mathcal{A}}U(P_{t})^{\dagger}SU(P_{t})R_{\mathcal{A}}
, where
S
denotes the swap operator. While the spectral gap of an ergodic time-reversible Markov chain
P
is defined by
\delta(P)=1-|\lambda_{1}|
, with
\lambda_{1}
being the second largest eigenvalues of
P
; a similar definition for a quantum walk
W(P)
is its phase gap
\operatorname{\Delta}(P)
, defined by the minimal angular distance of
1
to other eigenvalues in the complex plane, satisfies
\operatorname{\Delta}(P)\geq 2\sqrt{\delta(P)}
. For a classical Markov chain, the mixing time
\tau(P)=O(1/\delta(P))
describes how many steps before the state reaches a stationary distribution.

Double spacelike collinear limits from multi-Regge kinematics Preprint: BONN-TH-2025-21
Claude Duhr, Aniruddha Venkata, Chi Zhang (张驰)

Abstract
We study scattering amplitudes and form factors in planar 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 Super Yang-Mills theory in the limit where two pairs of gluons become collinear. We find that, when the virtualities of both collinear pairs are spacelike, the collinear factorisation of the amplitude involves a generalised splitting amplitude that correlates the two collinear directions, confirming a recent proposal in the literature. Remarkably, we find that our generalised splitting amplitude agrees with the Bern-Dixon-Smirnov (BDS) subtracted six-point amplitude in multi-Regge kinematics. The latter can be explicitly evaluated using integrability to all orders in the coupling. We also present compelling evidence for the universality of the generalised splitting amplitude, by showing that the same function governs the double spacelike collinear limit of scattering amplitudes with up to 8 particles and 3 loops and form factors with up to 6 particles and 2 loops.



I
Introduction

It is well known that scattering amplitudes develop singularities whenever subsets of massless partons become collinear.
From a phenomenological perspective, these collinear singularities govern the parton evolution in the initial and final states. In particular, the evolution of parton distribution functions (PDFs) in Deep Inelastic Scattering (DIS) is described by the celebrated Dokshitzer-Gribov-Lipatov-Altarelli-Parisi (DGLAP) equations

II
Amplitudes in planar
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM

Amplitudes in planar
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM are well studied (see
Arkani-Hamed:2022rwr
for a review). Many analytic results for multi-leg multi-loop amplitudes are available, due to the large number of symmetries of the theory. Besides the usual superconformal symmetry, the
S

\mathcal{N}=4
SYM is invariant under a dual superconformal symmetry
Drummond:2006rz
, which closes with the ordinary superconformal symmetry into a Yangian algebra
Drummond:2009fd
. It is convenient to consider superamplitudes, where each external state is characterised by a lightlike momentum
p_{i}
, an adjoint colour index
a_{i}
and a Graßmann variable
\eta_{i}
transforming in the fundamental representation of the
R
-symmetry group SU
(4)

Nair:1988bq
;
Arkani-Hamed:2008owk

\mathcal{A}_{N}=\!\!\!\sum_{\sigma\in S_{N}/\mathbb{Z}_{N}}\!\!\!\Tr(T^{a_{\sigma_{1}}}\cdots T^{a_{\sigma_{N}}})A_{N}(\Lambda_{\sigma_{1}},\ldots,\Lambda_{\sigma_{N}})\,,

(4)

where
T^{a}
are the generators of the gauge group SU
(N_{c})
,
A_{N}
are the partial amplitudes that depend on the cyclic ordering of the particles and
\Lambda_{i}:=(p_{i},\eta_{i})
, but are independent of the colour. As a consequence of dual (super-) conformal symmetry, the partial amplitude can be cast in the form
Drummond:2008vq

A_{N}({\bf\Lambda}_{N})=A_{\textrm{MHV}}^{\textrm{tree}}({\bf\Lambda}_{N})\,A_{N}^{\textrm{BDS}}\,R_{N}({\bf\Lambda}_{N})\,,

(5)

where
{\bf\Lambda}_{N}:=(\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{N})
,
A_{\textrm{MHV}}^{\textrm{tree}}
is the maximally helicity violating (MHV) tree superamplitude, and
A_{N}^{\textrm{BDS}}
is the BDS amplitude, defined as the exponential of the one-loop MHV amplitude (divided by the tree-level) weighted by the cusp and collinear anomalous dimensions
Bern:2005iz
.
The BDS amplitude captures infrared singularities to all orders in perturbation theory, so that the
BDS-subtracted amplitude

R_{N}
is finite and dual conformally invariant

;
Bern:2008ap
. In particular, its kinematic dependence can only be through dual conformally invariant cross-ratios,

u_{ijkl}=\frac{x_{ij}^{2}x_{kl}^{2}}{x_{ik}^{2}x_{jl}^{2}}\,,\quad x_{ij}=x_{i}-x_{j}=p_{i}+\cdots+p_{j-1}\,,

(6)

where the

are the dual coordinates; therefore,
R_{N}
is non-trivial only for
N\geq 6
. Explicit analytic results for BDS-subtracted amplitudes are available both for MHV and next-to-MHV (NMHV) for
N=6
up to
7

6

Caron-Huot:2019vjl
, for

4

Dixon:2016nkn
;

Li:2021bwg

Caron-Huot:2011zgw

Let us briefly comment on the behavior of
A_{N}
in the limit where a pair of particles

and

Moreover, since

is unity or an

(7)

where the subscript on

\pm

h_{1}+h_{2}-h_{P}=\pm 1

is the fraction of the parent momentum

in the strict collinear limit. Since the splitting amplitude is completely determined by

must tend to its tree value

Using the fact that amplitudes in this theory are
dual to Wilson loops
Caron-Huot:2010ryg

Basso:2013vsa

III
The generalised splitting amplitude

We now discuss the main result of this letter, namely the generalised splitting amplitude for two adjacent spacelike collinear pairs, say
p_{1}\parallel p_{2}
and
p_{3}\parallel p_{4}
, in planar
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM to all orders in the ’t Hooft coupling constant
a
. Note that the generalised splitting amplitudes, which require
P\cdot Q:=(p_{1}+p_{2})\cdot(p_{3}+p_{4})\neq 0
,
appear starting from
N=6
.

Before presenting the result, we first clarify what is meant by ‘spacelike’.
Amplitudes in planar
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM are typically computed in the Euclidean region where all invariants
x_{ij}^{2}
are negative and the amplitude is real. We refer to the region where all invariants are timelike, i.e., positive, as the
timelike Euclidean region
. It is identical to the Euclidean region up an overall phase (and the BDS-subtracted amplitude is independent of this phase). In the following, the term ‘spacelike’ refers to analytic continuation in some two-particle invariants
x^{2}_{i,i+2}

from the timelike Euclidean region to the region where these invariants are negative.

We are now in a position to state our claim about the generalised splitting amplitude, which consists of two points: (i) The
splitting amplitudes factorise in
all but two
distinct double spacelike collinear regions,
which, in addition to satisfying
s_{12}=x_{13}^{2}<0
and

\displaystyle\text{(I)}:

\displaystyle\quad P\cdot Q>0,\,s_{23}=x^{2}_{24}>0,\,\xi_{1}>1,\,\xi_{2}>1\>,

(8)

\displaystyle\text{(II)}:

\displaystyle\quad P\cdot Q<0,\,s_{23}=x^{2}_{24}<0,\,\xi_{1}>1,\,\xi_{2}>1\>,

(9)

respectively, and
\xi_{1}
and

(ii) Although the (component) amplitudes in these two regions do not factorise, they are compensated by

\displaystyle A_{N}

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{h,h^{\prime}}\splitti^{(2)}_{h}(-p_{1},p_{2};\xi_{1}+i\varepsilon)\,\splitti_{h^{\prime}}^{(2)}(\pm p_{3},\mp p_{4};\xi_{2}+i\varepsilon)\,

III.2
Universality of generalised splitting amplitudes

At this point, we would like to check that
\Delta_{\rm I/II}
is universal. It is independent of the hard scattering and is the same function for all higher point amplitudes and form factors. We now give compelling evidence that it is.

For the higher-point amplitudes, the main difference to the six-point case is that there may be several physical regions compatible with either DSL collinear region (
8
) or (
9
). For instance, physical regions compatible with both collinear regions can have either
x_{61}^{2}>0
or
x_{61}^{2}<0
for
N=7
. Through analytic continuation to various compatible regions, we have performed explicit computations of the DSL collinear limits for the symbols of seven- and eight-point MHV and NMHV amplitudes up to 3 loops
Caron-Huot:2010ryg
;
Drummond:2014ffa
;

;

III

\tau
and

are defined as in the six-point case with

The four-point MHV

;

, while the five- and six-point MHV form factors have been computed through two-loop order

In particular, the four-point form factor fails to factorise not only in regions I and II described in (

\tau

Based on our explicit computations, we conjecture that the generalised splitting amplitude is universal, and that the function
\Delta_{\rm I/II}
governs the behavior of any quantity in planar
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM in the limit where 2 spacelike pairs of particles become collinear.

Unlike other sources of collinear factorisation breaking considered in the literature
Catani:2011st
;
Henn:2024qjq
, this correlation between different spacelike collinear directions contributes already at leading color. While our analysis was restricted to the planar
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM theory, this is sufficient to establish the existence of correlations between multiple spacelike collinear directions at leading color. For the future, it would be tantalising to explore the implications for the factorisation of observables in hadronic collisions into PDFs multiplied by partonic cross sections.

Appendix A
Parametrisation of the double collinear limit

In this appendix, we describe how to parametrise the double collinear limit using momentum twistors
Hodges:2009hk
, which are convenient for the evaluation of amplitudes and form factors in
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM.

For amplitudes, the momentum twistors are defined as
Z_{i}:=(\lambda_{i}^{\alpha},x^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}_{i}\lambda_{i\alpha})
where
\lambda_{i}
are the usual spinor-helicity variables and
x_{i}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}:=x_{i}^{\mu}\sigma_{\mu}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}
. The double collinear limit can then be parametrised in terms of momentum twistors as

\displaystyle Z_{2}

\displaystyle\to Z_{1}+\epsilon Z_{4}-\epsilon\tau_{1}\frac{\langle 14\rangle}{\langle N1\rangle}Z_{N}-\epsilon^{2}(1-z)\tau_{2}\frac{\langle 14\rangle}{\langle 45\rangle}Z_{5}\,,

\displaystyle Z_{3}

\displaystyle\to Z_{4}+\epsilon Z_{1}-\epsilon\tau_{2}\frac{\langle 14\rangle}{\langle 45\rangle}Z_{5}+\epsilon^{2}\frac{(1-\bar{z})\tau_{1}}{\bar{z}}\frac{\langle 14\rangle}{\langle N1\rangle}Z_{N}\,.

Here, the collinear limit is taken by letting
\epsilon\to 0
, and the parameters
\tau_{i}
are related to the momentum fractions
\xi_{i}
through
\tau_{i}=(1-\xi_{i})/\xi_{i}
.

Compared with amplitudes, the momentum twistors for
N
-point form factors additionally have
Z_{N+i}=\mathsf{P}Z_{i}
with the transition matrix

\mathsf{P}=\begin{pmatrix}\delta_{\beta}^{\alpha}&0\\
q_{\beta}{}^{\dot{\alpha}}&\delta_{\beta}{}^{\dot{\alpha}}\end{pmatrix}\>,

such that
x_{N+i}-x_{i}=q
(note that
q=0
results in the amplitude case). For a comprehensive study, refer to e.g.
Basso:2023bwv
.

Appendix B
More details on the six-point case

In this appendix, we present more details on the DSL collinear limit for the six-point amplitudes.
Without loss of generality, we set the helicities of particles 1 through 4 as
\{-,+,+,-\}
, with particles 5 and 6 arbitrary (where all particles are taken to be outgoing).
In the DSL collinear limit
p_{1}\parallel p_{2}
and
p_{3}\parallel p_{4}
,
this amplitude behaves, according to eq. (
3
), as

\displaystyle A(1^{-},2^{+},3^{+},4^{-},5^{h_{5}},6^{h_{6}})\to

(15)

\displaystyle\sum_{h,h^{\prime}}\splitti^{(4)}_{h,h^{\prime}}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4};\xi_{1},\xi_{2})A(P^{h_{P}},5^{h_{5}},6^{h_{6}},Q^{h_{Q}})\>,

where the superscript
h_{i}
denotes the helicity of particle
i
,
h=h_{1}+h_{2}-h_{P}
and
h^{\prime}=h_{3}+h_{4}-h_{Q}
are the MHV degree of the two collinear pairs, while
\xi_{1,2}
denote the momentum fractions in the collinear limit through
p_{1}=\xi_{1}P
and
p_{4}=\xi_{2}Q
.

We first consider Region I, corresponding to the scattering process
1^{+}4^{+}\to 2^{+}3^{+}5^{h_{5}}6^{h_{6}}
. (Note that particle helicities are flipped when converting from outgoing to incoming, but the MHV degree of amplitudes is always computed treating all particles as outgoing.)
With the 2-particle splitting amplitudes defined in eq. (
7
), we can now define 4 ratios of these splitting functions:

\Delta_{\rm I}^{\pm\pm}:=\frac{\splitti^{(4)}_{\pm,\pm}(-p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},-p_{4};\xi_{1},\xi_{2})}{\splitti^{(2)}_{\pm}(-p_{1},p_{2};\xi_{1})\splitti^{(2)}_{\pm}(p_{3},-p_{4};\xi_{2})}\>,

(16)

By specifying
\{h_{5},h_{6}\}
as
\{+,+\}
and
\{-,-\}
, we can easily isolate the contributions to
\Delta_{\rm I}^{++}
and
\Delta_{\rm I}^{--}
, respectively:

\displaystyle\Delta_{\rm I}^{++}

\displaystyle=e^{i\delta_{6}}\mathsf{DCL}[R_{6}^{\text{MHV}}(1^{+}4^{+}\to 2^{+}3^{+}5^{+}6^{+})]\>,

(17)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\rm I}^{--}

\displaystyle=e^{i\delta_{6}}\mathsf{DCL}[R_{6}^{\overline{\text{MHV}}}(1^{+}4^{+}\to 2^{+}3^{+}5^{-}6^{-})]\>.

(18)

where
\mathsf{DCL}[R]
denotes the double collinear limit of the function
R
,
e^{i\delta_{6}}
arises from the BDS amplitude,
and
R^{\overline{\text{MHV}}}_{6}:=A^{\overline{\text{MHV}}}_{6}/(A_{6}^{\text{BDS}}A_{\overline{\text{MHV}}}^{\text{tree}})=R_{6}^{\text{MHV}}
. Then using the argument in Sec.
III
, we find

\Delta_{\rm I}^{++}=\Delta_{\rm I}^{--}=e^{i\delta_{6}}\mathcal{M}(R_{6}^{\text{MHV}})\>.

To obtain
\Delta_{\rm I}^{+-}
and
\Delta_{\rm I}^{-+}
, we need the six-point NMHV BDS-subtracted amplitude, which is conventionally written as

\displaystyle R_{6}^{\text{NMHV}}=((1)+(4))V_{1}+((1)-(4))\tilde{V}_{1}+\text{cyclic}\>.

(19)

Here
V_{i}
and
\tilde{V}_{i}
are some parity even and odd transcendental functions, respectively, and
(a):=[1\ldots\hat{a}\ldots 6]
is the usual
R
-invariant
Mason:2009qx
. In the double collinear limit, these
R
-invariants behave as

\displaystyle(6)\to\frac{-\bar{z}}{1-\bar{z}}(1)\>,\qquad(2)\to\frac{1}{1-\bar{z}}(1)\>,

(20)

\displaystyle(3)\to\frac{-z}{1-z}(4)\>,\qquad(5)\to\frac{1}{1-z}(3)\>.

(21)

Therefore, the
R_{6}^{\text{NMHV}}
in the double collinear limit can be organized as

\displaystyle\mathsf{DCL}[R_{6}^{\text{NMHV}}]

\displaystyle=(4)\biggl[\frac{1}{1-z}(V_{1}-\tilde{V}_{1}+V_{2}-\tilde{V}_{2})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\frac{-z}{1-z}(V_{1}-\tilde{V}_{1}+V_{3}+\tilde{V}_{3})\biggr]

\displaystyle\quad+(1)\biggl[\frac{1}{1-\bar{z}}(V_{1}+\tilde{V}_{1}+V_{2}+\tilde{V}_{2})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\frac{-\bar{z}}{1-\bar{z}}(V_{1}+\tilde{V}_{1}+V_{3}-\tilde{V}_{3})\biggr]

(22)

The effect of the multi-Regge limit on
V_{i}
and
\tilde{V}_{i}
is the same as that of the double collinear limit; however, the multi-Regge limit for the process
1^{+}4^{+}\to 2^{+}3^{+}5^{-}6^{+}
additionally requires
(4)\to 0
, while the multi-Regge limit for the process
1^{+}4^{+}\to 2^{+}3^{+}5^{+}6^{-}
additionally requires
(1)\to 0
. Meanwhile, the
R
-invariants
(1)
and
(4)
, together with the Parke-Taylor pre-factor, yield the product of the corresponding tree-level splitting functions and tree amplitudes in this double collinear limit. In this way, we conclude that

\displaystyle\Delta_{\rm I}^{-+}

\displaystyle=e^{i\delta_{6}}\mathcal{M}(R_{6}^{\text{NMHV}}(1^{+}4^{+}\to 2^{+}3^{+}5^{-}6^{+}))\>,

(23)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\rm I}^{+-}

\displaystyle=e^{i\delta_{6}}\mathcal{M}(R_{6}^{\text{NMHV}}(1^{+}4^{+}\to 2^{+}3^{+}5^{+}6^{-}))\>.

(24)

Note that, the MHV amplitudes in this double collinear limit only receive contributions from
\Delta_{\rm I}^{++}
,
while the NMHV amplitudes only receive contributions from
\Delta_{\rm I}^{++},\Delta_{\rm I}^{+-},\Delta_{\rm I}^{-+}
, and they first appear together in the seven-point NMHV amplitudes, all four terms become present starting from the eight-point N
2
MHV amplitudes.

The analysis for Region II is similar but now corresponds to the scattering process
1^{+}3^{-}5^{-h_{5}}\to 2^{+}4^{-}6^{h_{6}}
.

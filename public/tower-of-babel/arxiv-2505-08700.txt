A Smooth, Recurrent, Non-Periodic Viscosity Solution of the Hamilton-Jacobi Equation
CHARFI Skander

Abstract
Viscosity solutions of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation were introduced by Lions and Crandall. For Tonelli Hamiltonians, these solutions are generated by the Lax-Oleinik operator. It is known that this operator converges in the autonomous framework, but this convergence fails in the general cases. In this paper, we introduce a method to construct smooth, recurrent, non-periodic viscosity solutions on fixed compact manifolds M M of dimension 2 or higher. Additionally, we provide a detailed description of the non-wandering set of the Lax-Oleinik operator and identify its action on various omega-limit sets.

Let

This equation has a well posed Cauchy problem in the viscosity sense (see

[

]

defined on

In the autonomous Hamiltonian case, the asymptotic behaviour of the Lax-Oleinik operator

However, addressing the time-dependent framework reveals more challenging. According to A. Fathi and J.N. Mather

0.2
Main Result

In this work, we present a construction of a recurrent, non-periodic viscosity solution to the Hamilton-Jacobi equation (
0.1
), applicable to compact manifolds of dimension 2 or higher. The main idea is to make advantage of the correlation between periodic orbits within the Mather set and periodic viscosity solutions. Specifically, for each
\rho_{n}
-periodic orbit
\{x_{n}^{i}\}_{i}
within the Mather set, we consider the modified Peierls barriers
h^{\rho_{n}\infty}(x^{0}_{n},\cdot)
introduced in
[
FM00
]
, which is a
\rho_{n}
-periodic viscosity solution. The recurrent viscosity solution is of the form
u=\inf_{n\geq 0}\{h^{\rho_{n}\infty}(x^{0}_{n},\cdot)\}
. We carefully control the parameters of the constructed Hamiltonian to make sure that
u
is
\rho_{n}
-periodic near every point
x^{0}_{n}
. The recurrence, yet non-periodicity, of
u
is achieved by considering an infinite set of orbits with increasingly diverging periodicities
\rho_{n}
.

This idea allows the construction of numerous examples. However, achieving more than Lipschitz regularity for the recurrent solution reveals more intricate. To address this, adjustments are made to the Hamiltonian and the viscosity solution
u
to achieve
C^{\infty}
regularity with a new initial data of the form
u=\inf_{n\geq 0}\{c_{n}+h^{\rho_{n}}(x_{n},\cdot)\}
where
c_{n}
are constants to be chosen carefully.

More precisely, the weak-KAM theory demonstrates that
u_{c}
is already differentiable on the Mather set. Building on this result, we ensure that the boundary between realizations of the infimum defining
u_{c}
occurs within the Mather set. Additionally, symmetry conditions on the Hamiltonian are imposed to guarantee the
C^{\infty}
-regularity of the barriers
h^{\rho_{n}}(x_{n},\cdot)
outside of the Mather set.

Unfortunately, attaining higher regularity on the Mather set is significantly more challenging, as it often acts as an obstruction to achieving regularity higher than
C^{1,1}
. This is exemplified by the double covering of the pendulum (See
[
Cha25b
]
), where Peierls barriers are smooth everywhere except at one of the two points that shape the Mather set, at which they are never of
C^{2}
regularity (due to the hyperbolic nature of these points). To address this issue, we construct the Hamiltonian to induce slow parabolic negative time convergence in the unstable manifolds of points in the Mather set. This approach can result in a null higher derivatives of
u_{c}
at such points. This yields the following theorem.

Theorem 0.2
.

For any closed manifold
M
of dimension
d\geq 2
, there exists a
C^{\infty}
Tonelli Hamiltonian
H:\mathbb{T}^{1}\times T^{*}M\to\mathbb{R}
such that the Lax-Oleinik operator
\mathcal{T}
admits a
C^{\infty}
recurrent, non-periodic viscosity solution.

After the construction, our focus shifts to the asymptotic behaviour of the Lax-Oleinik operator for the obtained Tonelli Hamiltonian. We determine the omega-limit set
\omega(u)
of the constructed viscosity solution
u
and, more broadly, describe the whole non-wandering set
\Omega(\mathcal{T})
following a generalized representation formula derived from
[
Cha25a
]
. It is observed that when the Mather set consists of periodic orbits with integer periods,the Lax-Oleinik operator behaves like an odometer on its non-wandering set (see
[
HR79
]
,
[
Dow05
]
,
[
HV22
]
for surveys on odometers, also known as adding machines). This leads to the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Assume that the Mather set
\mathcal{M}
is the union of periodic orbits with integer periods. Then for all
v\in\Omega(\mathcal{T})
, the restriction of the Lax-Oleinik operator
\mathcal{T}
to the set
\omega(v)
is a factor of an odometer.

It is known that the Lax-Oleinik semigroup
\mathcal{T}
acts by isometries on its non-wandering set (See
[
Cha25a
]
). This imposes limitations on the possible dynamics that can occur on
\omega(u)
for any recurrent viscosity solution
u
. A natural question that arises is whether it is possible to construct an explicit example where
\mathcal{T}
exhibits more intricate dynamics than that of an odometer.

1.1
Tonelli Hamiltonians and the Lax-Oleinik Operator

We define the notion of Tonelli Hamiltonians, initially introduced in
[
Mat91
]
. These are Hamiltonians that are convex and superlinear in the fibres, serving as the tame framework for the weak-KAM theory.

Definition 1.1
.

A
1
-time-periodic Hamiltonian
H(t,x,p):\mathbb{T}^{1}\times T^{*}M\to\mathbb{R}
is said
Tonelli
if it satisfies the following classical hypotheses :

•

Regularity:
H
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
.

•

Strict convexity:
\partial_{pp}H(t,x,p)>0
for all
(t,x,p)\in\mathbb{T}^{1}\times T^{*}M
.

•

Superlinearity:
H(t,x,p)/|p|\to\infty
as
|p|\to\infty
for each
(t,x)\in\mathbb{T}^{1}\times M
.

•

Completeness: The Hamiltonian vector field
X_{H}(t,x,p)=(\partial_{p}H(t,x,p),-\partial_{x}H(t,x,p))
and hence its flow
\phi_{H}^{t,s}
is complete in the sense that the flow curves are defined for all times
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Under these assumptions, one can associate to
H(t,x,p)
a time-periodic Tonelli
Lagrangian

L(t,x,v):\mathbb{T}^{1}\times TM\to\mathbb{R}
given by

L(t,x,v)=\max_{p\in T_{x}^{*}M}\{p(v)-H(t,x,p)\}

(1.1)

which symmetrically gives

H(t,x,p)=\max_{v\in T_{x}M}\{p(v)-L(t,x,v)\}

(1.2)

The
Euler-Lagrange flow
also named
Lagrangian flow

\phi_{L}^{s,t}
is the conjugate to the Hamiltonian flow
\phi_{H}^{s,t}
by the
Legendre map
1
1

1

The Tonelli assumptions imply that for all time
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the Legendre map
\mathcal{L}
is a diffeomorphism between
\{t\}\times TM
and
\{t\}\times T^{*}M
(see
[
Fat08
]
for details).

\mathcal{L}(t,x,v)=\big(t,x,\partial_{v}L(t,x,v)\big)
. We adopt the notation
\phi_{L}^{t}
for
\phi_{L}^{0,t}
.

If
0\leq s\leq t
are two real times and
x
and
y
are two points of
M
, we define the following quantities

•

For all absolutely continuous curve
\gamma:[s,t]\to M
, the
action
of
\gamma
is

A_{L}(\gamma)=\int_{s}^{t}L(\tau,\gamma(\tau),\dot{\gamma}(\tau))\;d\tau

(1.3)

•

the
potential
between
(s,x)
and
(t,y)
is

h_{0}^{s,t}(x,y)=\inf\left\{A_{L}(\gamma)\;\left|\;\begin{matrix}\gamma:&[s,t]\to M\\
&s\mapsto x\\
&t\mapsto y\end{matrix}\right.\right\}

(1.4)

where the infimum is taken over such absolutely continuous curves
\gamma
.

Remark 1.2
.

The demanded absolute continuity of the curves enables to define this integral. All the curves and all the infimum over the curves that may be considered in this paper will be in the family of absolutely continuous curves. We will refrain from recalling it every time.

We now introduce the Lax-Oleinik operator that generates the viscosity solutions of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation

\partial_{t}u+H(t,x,\partial_{x}u)=\alpha_{0}

(1.5)

Definition 1.3
.

Fix two times
s<t
.

1.

The
Lax-Oleinik operator

\mathcal{T}_{0}^{s,t}:\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})\to\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})
is defined as

\begin{split}\mathcal{T}_{0}^{s,t}u_{0}(x)&=\inf_{\begin{matrix}\gamma:[s,t]\rightarrow M\\
t\mapsto x\end{matrix}}\left\{u_{0}(\gamma(s))+\int_{s}^{t}L(\tau,\gamma(\tau),\dot{\gamma}(\tau))\;d\tau\right\}\\
&=\inf_{y\in M}\left\{u_{0}(y)+h_{0}^{s,t}(y,x)\right\}\\
\end{split}

(1.6)

2.

The
Mañé critical value

\alpha_{0}
is defined as

\alpha_{0}=-\inf_{\mu}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{T}^{1}\times TM}L\;d\mu\right\}

(1.7)

where the infimum is taken over compact supported Borel probability measures
\mu
invariant by the Euler-Lagrangian flow corresponding to
L
.

3.

The
full Lax-Oleinik operator

\mathcal{T}^{s,t}:\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})\to\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})
is defined as

\mathcal{T}^{s,t}u_{0}(x)=\mathcal{T}_{0}^{s,t}u_{0}(x)+\alpha_{0}.(t-s)

(1.8)

We adopt the notations
\mathcal{T}^{t}
for
\mathcal{T}^{0,t}
and
\mathcal{T}
for
\mathcal{T}^{0,1}
.

One can verify that for all
s<t<\tau
,
\mathcal{T}^{t,\tau}\circ\mathcal{T}^{s,t}=\mathcal{T}^{s,\tau}
. Additionally, Since
L
is time-periodic,
\mathcal{T}^{t+1}=\mathcal{T}^{t}\circ\mathcal{T}
. Hence
\{\mathcal{T}^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a discrete semi-group acting on
\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})
, called the Lax-Oleinik semi-group. The main focus of this paper is the asymptotic behaviour of
\mathcal{T}
.

Remark 1.4
.

1.

By abuse of language, we will often refer to the initial condition
u_{0}
as a viscosity solution while referring in reality to the corresponding viscosity solution
u(t,x)=\mathcal{T}^{t}u_{0}(x)
.

2.

We will prove that in the case of the Mañé Lagrangians, heavily utilized in this article (see Subsection
2.1
), the Mañé critical value is null. In this case, the two operators
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{T}_{0}
do coincide.

Lemma 1.5
.

Let
v_{n}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}\times M,\mathbb{R})
be a sequence of viscosity solutions and let
u(t,x)=\inf_{n}\{v_{n}(t,x)\}
. Then
u
is a viscosity solution.

To a scalar map
u
of
\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})
, we associate its
\omega
-limit set

\omega(u):=\omega_{\mathcal{T}}(u)
by the operator
\mathcal{T}
as the set of limit points in the
C^{0}
-topology of the sequence
(\mathcal{T}^{n}(u))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. More precisely

\omega(u)=\{v\in\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})\;|\;\exists(k_{n})_{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}\text{ increasing sequence s.t }\|\mathcal{T}^{k_{n}}u-v\|_{\infty}\to 0\text{ as }n\to\infty\}

(1.9)

Definition 1.6
.

1.

If
\mathcal{T}u=u
, or equivalently if
u(t,\cdot)=\mathcal{T}^{t}u
is a
1
-periodic viscosity solution, then

is called a

of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation (
1.5
).

2.

The
recurrent set

\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{T})
of
\mathcal{T}
is the subset of recurrent elements of
\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})
i.e.
u\in\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})
such that
u

\omega(u)
.

3.

The

is the subset of elements

\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})
such that for all neighbourhood

The following proposition justifies the non-emptiness of these sets

Proposition 1.7
.

For all
(s,u)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})
, the family
\big(\mathcal{T}^{s,t}u=\mathcal{T}_{0}^{s,t}u+\alpha_{0}.(t-s)\big)_{t\geq s}

1.2
Existence of Minimizing Curves

We give classical results on minimizing curves that stem directly from the theory of variational calculus. We will avoid providing tedious proofs that can be found in the autonomous framework in
[
Fat08
]
and
[
CI99
]
and in the non-autonomous framework in Mañé ’s lecture notes
[
Mn91
]
.

Definition 1.8
.

A
minimizing curve

\gamma:I\to M
defined on an interval
I
is a curve such that for all
s<t
in
I
,

\int_{s}^{t}L(\tau,\gamma(\tau),\dot{\gamma}(\tau))\;d\tau=h_{0}^{s,t}(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))

(1.10)

Proposition 1.9
.

1.

If for some times
s<t
, the curve
\gamma
verifies (
1.10
), then it is minimizing on
[s,t]
.

2.

A minimizing curve
\gamma
is as regular as the Lagrangian
L
and it follows the Lagrangian flow
\phi_{L}
i.e for all time
\tau\in[s,t]
,
(\gamma(\tau),\dot{\gamma}(\tau))=\phi_{L}^{s,\tau}(\gamma(0),\dot{\gamma}(0))
. Moreover, it verifies the Euler-Lagrange equation

\partial_{x}L(\tau,\gamma(\tau),\dot{\gamma}(\tau))=\frac{d}{d\tau}\big(\partial_{v}L(\tau,\gamma(\tau),\dot{\gamma}(\tau))\big)

(1.11)

Remark 1.10
.

The first property is due to the fact that the quantity

\int_{s^{\prime}}^{t^{\prime}}L(\tau,\gamma(\tau),\dot{\gamma}(\tau))-h_{0}^{s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}(\gamma(s^{\prime}),\gamma(t^{\prime}))\;d\tau\geq 0

is non-negative and is increasing with the inclusion of the interval
[s^{\prime},t^{\prime}]\subset[s,t]
.

A main brick in the study of Tonelli Lagrangians is the existence of minimizing curves.

Theorem 1.11
.

(Tonelli’s Theorem)

be two real times and

and

be two points of

.

.

Let

and for all point

\gamma:[s,t]\to M

\gamma(t)=x\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{T}^{s,t}u(x)=u(\gamma(0))+A_{L}(\gamma)

1.4

Same as for minimizing curves in Remark

The Peierls barrier introduced by Mather in

For times

\begin{split}h^{s,t}(x,y)&=(t-s).\alpha_{0}+h_{0}^{s,t}(x,y)\\

&s\mapsto x\\
&t\mapsto y\end{matrix}\right.\right\}\end{split}

And we adopt the notation

for

and

Definition 1.25

and

1.6
The Mather Set

The Mather set, introduced by John N. Mather, is associated with the study of action-minimizing measures in Lagrangian and Hamiltonian systems.

Definition 1.30
.

1.

A measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{T}^{1}\times TM
is
a minimizing measure
if it is a Borel probability measure, invariant by the Euler-Lagrange flow
\phi_{L}
and it satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{1}\times TM}L\;d\mu=-\alpha_{0}

(1.27)

where
\alpha_{0}
is the Mañé critical value defined in (
1.7
) (and assumed to be null).

2.

The
Mather set

\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
is defined by

\tilde{\mathcal{M}}=\bigcup_{\mu}\supp(\mu)\subset\mathbb{T}^{1}\times TM

(1.28)

where the union is on minimizing measures
\mu
.

3.

The
projected Mather set

\mathcal{M}
is the projection of
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
to
\mathbb{T}^{1}\times M
.

4.

The
time-zero Mather set

\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
and its projected counterpart
\mathcal{M}_{0}
are the intersections

\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}:=\tilde{\mathcal{M}}\cap\big(\{0\}\times TM\big)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{M}_{0}:=\mathcal{M}\cap\big(\{0\}\times M\big)

(1.29)

seen respectively as subsets of
TM
and
M
.

Remark 1.31
.

1.

More explicitly, the invariant measures
\mu
featured in the definition are invariant by the maps
\Phi_{L}^{\tau}
, for all time
\tau>0
, given by

\begin{split}\Phi_{L}^{\tau}:\mathbb{T}^{1}\times TM&\longrightarrow\mathbb{T}^{1}\times TM\\
(t,x,v)&\longmapsto(t+\tau,\phi_{L}^{t,t+\tau}(x,v))\end{split}

(1.30)

2.

We easily see from these definitions that the different Mather sets are invariant by either the Euler-Lagrange flow or its time-one map.

The non-emptiness of this set has been proved by John N.Mather in the non-autonomous case. See proposition 4 of
[
Mat91
]

Proposition 1.32
.

The Mather set
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
is compact and non-empty.

The following proposition has been proved by R.Mañé in
[
Mn97
]
and generalized by J-M.Roquejoffre and P.Bernard in
[
BR04
]
to non-wandering viscosity solutions. However, for the purpose of this paper, we only require the result for periodic viscosity solutions.

Proposition 1.33
.

Let
\tilde{x}
be an element of the Mather set
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
and
x=\pi(\tilde{x})
be its projection in
\mathcal{M}_{0}
. Let
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to M
be the projection on
M
of the Lagrangian flow at
\tilde{x}
i.e.
\gamma(t)=\pi\circ\phi_{L}^{t}(\tilde{x})
. Then,

1.

Every periodic viscosity solution
u
is calibrated by
\gamma
and differentiable at
(t,\gamma(t))
.

2.

In particular, every weak-KAM solution
u
is calibrated by
\gamma
.

Remark 1.34
.

The application of Theorem
1.23
to the curves
\gamma
of
\mathcal{M}
reveals that the Mather set (and more generally, the Peierls set
\mathcal{A}_{0}
defined below) is a set of differentiability for all weak-KAM or periodic viscosity solutions. This fact can be extended to all non-wandering viscosity solutions of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation (see
[
BR04
]
), but we will not need such generality in this paper.

Proposition 1.35
.

The following inclusion holds

\mathcal{M}_{0}\subset\mathcal{A}_{0}

(1.31)

Proof.

Let
\mu
be a minimizing measure on
\mathbb{T}^{1}\times TM
. It is invariant by the map
\Phi_{L}^{1}
defined in (
1.30
). Then the Poincaré recurrence theorem shows that
\Phi_{L}^{1}
-recurrent points are dense in
\supp(\mu)
. If
(t,x,v)
is such a point, then so is
(\Phi_{L}^{t})^{-1}(t,x,v)=(0,(\phi^{t}_{L})^{-1}(x,v))
. Hence,
\Phi_{L}^{1}
-recurrent points are dense in
\supp_{0}(\mu):=\supp(\mu)\cap\{t=0\}
. Let
(x,v)
be a
\Phi_{L}^{1}
-recurrent element of
\supp_{0}(\mu)
where we omit the time variable
t=0
. Note that with this omission, the map
\Phi_{L}^{1}
coincides with
\phi_{L}^{1}
on
\supp_{0}(\mu)
.
Consider an increasing sequence of positive integers
k_{n}
such that
\phi_{L}^{k_{n}}(x,v)
converges to
(x,v)
. Let
u
be any weak-KAM solution, for instance
h^{\infty}(x,.)
(see Property
6
of Proposition
1.26
). From Proposition
1.33
, we know that the curve
x(t)=\pi\circ\phi_{L}^{t}(x,v)
is calibrated by
u
and

u(x(k_{n}))-u(x)=u(k_{n},x(k_{n}))-u(x)=k_{n}.\alpha_{0}+\int_{0}^{k_{n}}L(s,x(s),\dot{x}(s))\;ds\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty

where the limit holds due to the continuity of
u
. Thus, for any small
\varepsilon>0
and any large integer time
n_{0}>0
, closing the orbit
x(t)
enables to construct a loop
\gamma:[0,k_{n}]\to M
with
k_{n}>n_{0}
,
\gamma(0)=\gamma(k_{n})=x
and such that

k_{n}.\alpha_{0}+\int_{0}^{k_{n}}L(s,\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))\;ds\leq\varepsilon

Therefore, by the definition of the Peierls barrier and taking the liming on
k_{n}
, we get
h^{\infty}(x,x)\leq 0
. However, we already have from the non-negativity Property
7
of Proposition
1.26
the inverse inequality
h^{\infty}(x,x)\geq 0
. We conclude that
h^{\infty}(x,x)=0
.
∎

1.7
The Mañé Set

In this subsection, we introduce the final concept derived from the Aubry-Mather theory that holds significance for this article: the Mané set. This set will be used in the proof of the regularity of the recurrent viscosity solution of Section
4
.

The definition of the Mañé Set requires the introduction of a potential.

Definition 1.36
.

1.

The
Mañé potential

m:M\times M\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

m(x,y)=\inf_{n\geq 0}h^{n}(x,y)

(1.32)

with a time evolution counterpart
m:\{(s,t)\in\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\mathbb{T}^{1}\;|\;s\leq t\}\times M\times M\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

m^{s,t}(x,y)=\inf_{n\geq 0}h^{s,t+n}(x,y)

(1.33)

We use the notation
m^{t}
for
m^{0,t}
.

2.

A curve
\gamma:I\to\mathbb{R}
is said
semi-static
if for all times
s<t
in
I
, we have

m^{s,t}(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))=\alpha_{0}.(t-s)+\int_{s}^{t}L(\tau,\gamma(\tau),\dot{\gamma}(\tau))\;d\tau

(1.34)

3.

The
Mañé set

\tilde{\mathcal{N}}
is the subset of
\mathbb{T}^{1}\times TM
defined as

\tilde{\mathcal{N}}=\{(t,\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t))\;|\;\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to M\text{ is a semi-static curve }\}

(1.35)

Remark 1.37
.

1.

The first term of the infimum is the quantity
h^{0}
defined as

h^{0}(x,y)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }x=y\\
+\infty&\text{if }x\neq y\end{cases}

(1.36)

It follows that for any point
x
of
M
,
m(x,x)=0
. This also implies that the map
m
can exhibit discontinuity at the diagonal of
M\times M
.

2.

Away from the diagonal of
M\times M
, the Mañé potential
m^{s,t}
is continuous on its time and space variables.

3.

Note that a semi-static curve is minimizing as for any times
s<t
,

\displaystyle A_{L+\alpha_{0}}(\gamma)=m^{s,t}(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))\leq h^{s,t}(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))\leq A_{L+\alpha_{0}}(\gamma)

and we get equality everywhere.

4.

Analogously to Remarks
1.10
and
1.21
, if for fixed times
s<t
the identity (
1.34
) holds, then the curve
\gamma
is semi-static on
[s,t]
.

The regularity Proposition
1.18
for the finite-time potential
h^{t}
is reflected on the regularity of the Mañé potential
m^{s,t}
as stated below.

Proposition 1.38
.

For all positive real number
\varepsilon>0
and all times
s<t+\varepsilon
, the Mañé potential
m^{s,t}
is
\kappa_{\varepsilon}
-Lipschitz with
\kappa_{\varepsilon}
being the same Lipschitz value figuring in Proposition
1.18
.

Below is another characterization of semi-static curves. Only an implication is stated here, but the inverse statement will be shown in Proposition
1.45
.

Proposition 1.39
.

Let
u
be a weak-KAM solution of (
1.5
) and let
\gamma:I\to M
be a calibrated curve by
u
. Then the curve
\gamma
is semi-static.

Proof.

Let
s<t
be two times in
I
. We have by definition of weak-KAM solutions that for all integer
k
such that
s\leq t+k

\displaystyle u(t,\gamma(t))

\displaystyle=u(t+k,\gamma(t))+k.\alpha_{0}=\mathcal{T}^{s,t+k}u(s,\gamma(t))+k.\alpha_{0}

\displaystyle\leq u(s,\gamma(s))+h_{0}^{s,t+k}(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))+k.\alpha_{0}

\displaystyle=u(s,\gamma(s))+h^{s,t+k}(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))-\alpha_{0}.(t-s)

so that

\displaystyle A_{L+\alpha_{0}}(\gamma_{|[s,t]})=A_{L}(\gamma_{|[s,t]})+\alpha_{0}.(t-s)=u(t,\gamma(t))-u(s,\gamma(t))+\alpha_{0}.(t-s)\leq h^{s,t+k}(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))

And taking the infimum over the integers
k
, we get that
A_{L+\alpha_{0}}(\gamma_{|[s,t]})=m^{s,t}(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))
meaning that
\gamma
is a semi-static curve.
∎

Proposition 1.40
.

The following inclusion holds

\tilde{\mathcal{M}}\subset\tilde{\mathcal{N}}

(1.37)

Proof.

Let
(s,\tilde{x})
be an element of
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
and
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to M
be the curve of
M
defines as
\gamma(t)=\pi\circ\phi_{L}^{s,t}(\tilde{x})
. We need to show that
\gamma
is semi-static.

Fix a weak-KAM solution
u
. We know from Proposition
1.33
that
\gamma
is calibrated by
u
. Thus, for all integers
q<p
and
k\geq 1
, and for all curves
\sigma:[q,q+k]\to M
linking
\gamma(q)
to
\gamma(p)
, we have

\displaystyle A_{L+\alpha_{0}}(\gamma_{|[q,p]})

\displaystyle=A_{L}(\gamma_{|[q,p]})+\alpha_{0}.(p-q)

\displaystyle=u(p,\gamma(p))-u(q,\gamma(q))+\alpha_{0}.(p-q)

\displaystyle=u(q+k,\gamma(p))+\alpha_{0}.(q+k-p)-u(q,\gamma(q))+\alpha_{0}.(p-q)

\displaystyle=u(q+k,\gamma(p))-u(q,\gamma(q))+\alpha_{0}.k

\displaystyle\leq A_{L}(\sigma)+\alpha_{0}.k=A_{L+\alpha_{0}}(\sigma)

where we used the definition of weak-KAM solutions in the second line. Taking the infimum over such curves
\sigma
while varying

, this implies that

\displaystyle A_{L}(\gamma_{|[q,p]})+\alpha_{0}.(p-q)\leq m(\gamma(q),\gamma(p))\leq A_{L}(\gamma_{|[q,p]})+\alpha_{0}.(p-q)

We deduce equality everywhere. And since the integers
p
and
q
are arbitrary, we conclude from the last point of Remark
1.37
that the curve
\gamma
is semi-static and
(s,\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))=(s,\tilde{x})
belongs to the Mañé set
\tilde{\mathcal{N}}
.
∎

. To achieve this, we need to introduce additional concepts following

]
and
[

Definition 1.41
.

1.

We define the semi-distance
d_{1}:\mathcal{M}_{0}\times\mathcal{M}_{0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
as

d_{1}(x,y)=h^{\infty}(x,y)+h^{\infty}(y,x)

(1.38)

And set
\sim
to be the equivalence relation on
\mathcal{M}_{0}
given by

x\sim y\Longleftrightarrow d_{1}(x,y)=0

The

are the equivalence classes of the equivalence relation

. We denote by

the set of static classes.

2.

For a curve
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to M
, we define its integer-time

-limit set

and its integer-time
\omega
-limit set

by

\begin{split}\alpha_{k}(\gamma)&=\{z\in M\;|\;\exists(q_{n})_{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}};\;\lim_{n}q_{n}=+\infty,\;\lim_{n}\gamma(-k.q_{n})=z\}\\
\omega_{k}(\gamma)&=\{z\in M\;|\;\exists(q_{n})_{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}};\;\lim_{n}q_{n}=+\infty,\;\lim_{n}\gamma(k.q_{n})=z\}\end{split}

(1.40)

Then, we set a partial order
\preceq
on
\mathbb{M}
given by

i.

\preceq
is reflexive and transitive.

ii.

For all
x
and
y
in
\mathbb{M}
, if there exists a curve
\gamma
with lift
(\gamma,\dot{\gamma})
in the Mañé set
\tilde{\mathcal{N}}
such that its integer

\alpha_{1}(\gamma)\subset x
and its integer
\omega
-limit set
\omega_{1}(\gamma)\subset y
, then

.

Remark 1.42
.

Note that the semi-distance

is non-negative thanks to the triangular inequality property (

) and the non-negativity Property

of Proposition

. Indeed, for all

and

in

\displaystyle d_{1}(x,y)=h^{\infty}(x,y)+h^{\infty}(y,x)\geq h^{\infty}(x,x)\geq 0

It is a distance when restricted to the set
\mathbb{M}
.

Proposition 1.43
.

If
x
and
y
are two point of the Peierls set
\mathcal{A}_{0}
with the same static class in
\mathbb{M}
, then for all point

,

h^{\infty}(x,z)=h^{\infty}(x,y)+h^{\infty}(y,z)

(1.41)

Proof.

This is no more than an application of the triangular inequality (

) as follows

\displaystyle h^{\infty}(x,z)

\displaystyle\leq h^{\infty}(x,y)+h^{\infty}(y,z)

\displaystyle\leq h^{\infty}(x,y)+h^{\infty}(y,x)+h^{\infty}(x,z)

\displaystyle=d_{1}(x,y)+h^{\infty}(x,z)=h^{\infty}(x,z)

So, there is equality everywhere.
∎

The following proposition motivates the definition of the partial order

.

Proposition 1.44
.

Let
\gamma:I\to M
be a semi-static curve. Then, whenever the

and

and

Proof.

we only prove the first inclusion. Assume that
I
is unbounded in the negative direction. Let
z
be an element of
\alpha_{1}(\gamma)
and let

be an increasing sequence of positive integers such that

and
\gamma(-q_{n})
converges to

. We first prove that

belongs to
\mathcal{A}_{0}
. Fix a small time

t
to zero, we get

h^{\infty}(z,z)=\lim_{t\to 0}h^{\infty+t}(z,\sigma_{z}(t))=\lim_{t\to 0}m^{t}(z,\sigma_{z}(t))\leq\lim_{t\to 0}A_{L}(\sigma_{z|[0,t]})=0

(1.43)

where the first limit is due to the uniform continuity of

on the graph of
(t,\sigma_{z}(t))
for

. Hence, we deduce from the non-negativity Property
7
of Proposition

that

meaning that

belongs to the Peierls set
\mathcal{A}_{0}
.

Now we show that the
\alpha
-limit set
\alpha_{1}(\gamma)
belongs to a unique static class. Let
x
and
z
be two points of
\alpha_{1}(\gamma)
. Let
q_{n}
and
p_{n}
be two intertwined increasing sequences of integers such that
\gamma(-q_{n})
converges to
x
,
\gamma(-p_{n})
converges to
z
, for all

,
q_{n}<p_{n}<q_{n+1}
and
\lim\limits_{n}q_{n+1}-p_{n}=\lim\limits_{n}p_{n}-q_{n}=+\infty
. As done above, we construct two curves
\sigma_{z}
and
\sigma_{x}:[0,s]\to M
and we prove that

\displaystyle h^{\infty+t}(x,\sigma_{z}(t))=m^{t}(x,\sigma_{z}(t))\quad\text{and}\quad h^{t,\infty+2t}(\sigma_{z}(t),\sigma_{x}(2t))=m^{t,2t}(\sigma_{z}(t),\sigma_{x}(2t))

Thus, we get

\displaystyle h^{\infty+t}(x,\sigma_{z}(t))+h^{t,\infty+2t}(\sigma_{z}(t),\sigma_{x}(2t))

\displaystyle=m^{t}(x,\sigma_{z}(t))+m^{t,2t}(\sigma_{z}(t),\sigma_{x}(2t))

\displaystyle=\lim_{n}m^{t}\big(\gamma(-q_{n+1}),\gamma(t-p_{n})\big)+m^{t,2t}\big(\gamma(t-p_{n}),\gamma(2t-q_{n})\big)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n}A_{L+\alpha_{0}}(\gamma_{|[-q_{n+1},t-p_{n}]})+\lim_{n}A_{L+\alpha_{0}}(\gamma_{|[t-p_{n},2t-q_{n}]})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n}A_{L+\alpha_{0}}(\gamma_{|[-q_{n+1},2t-q_{n}]})=\lim_{n}m^{2t}\big(\gamma(-q_{n+1}),\gamma(2t-q_{n})\big)

\displaystyle=m^{2t}(x,\sigma_{x}(2t))

And taking

to
0
, we finally get

\displaystyle d_{1}(x,z)=h^{\infty}(x,z)+h^{\infty}(z,x)

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to 0}h^{\infty+t}(x,\sigma_{z}(t))+h^{t,\infty+2t}(\sigma_{z}(t),\sigma_{x}(2t))

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to 0}m^{2t}(x,\sigma_{x}(2t))\leq\lim_{t\to 0}A_{L+\alpha_{0}}(\sigma_{x|[0,2t]})=0

However, we already know from Remark
1.42
that
d_{1}(x,z)\geq 0
. We obtain
d_{1}(x,z)=0
and the two points
x
and

belong to the same static class
\bar{x}
.
∎

Proposition 1.45
.

Let
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to M
be a semi-static curve on
M
and let
\bar{x}
be the static class of
\alpha_{1}(\gamma)
. Then for all point
x
of
\bar{x}
, the curve
\gamma
is calibrated by the weak-KAM solution
h^{\infty}(x,\cdot)
.

Proof.

Fix two real times
s<t
. Let us first assume that
x
belongs to
\alpha_{1}(\gamma)
. Then there exists an increasing sequence of integers
q_{n}
such that
\gamma(-q_{n})
converges to
x
. We have from the definition of semi-static curves that

\displaystyle m^{t}(\gamma(-q_{n}),\gamma(t))

\displaystyle=A_{L+\alpha_{0}}(\gamma_{|[-q_{n},t]})=A_{L+\alpha_{0}}(\gamma_{|[-q_{n},s]})+A_{L+\alpha_{0}}(\gamma_{|[s,t]})

\displaystyle=m^{s}(\gamma(-q_{n}),\gamma(s))+A_{L+\alpha_{0}}(\gamma_{|[s,t]})

In order to take the limit, we need to make sure that we work in the domain of continuity of the Mañé potential
m^{s,t}
. It suffices to assume that
s
and
t\neq 0
in
\mathbb{T}^{1}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
. Then we can take the limit on
n
to get

\displaystyle m^{t}(x,\gamma(t))=m^{s}(x,\gamma(s))+A_{L+\alpha_{0}}(\gamma_{|[s,t]})

We need to replace

by
h^{\infty+t}
. To do so, remark that for all integer
n\geq 0
, we have from the triangular inequality (
1.24
) of the Peierls Barrier that

\displaystyle h^{\infty+t}(x,\gamma(t))=h^{\infty+t+n}(x,\gamma(t))\leq h^{\infty}(x,x)+h^{t+n}(x,\gamma(t))=h^{t+n}(x,\gamma(t))

where we used the inclusion

derived from Proposition
1.44
. And taking the infimum over

, we get the inequality

. And since the inverse inequality is immediate, we deduce the equality

. Doing the same for

\displaystyle h^{\infty+t}(x,\gamma(t))=h^{\infty+s}(x,\gamma(s))+A_{L+\alpha_{0}}(\gamma_{|[s,t]})

Now if for example

The extension of the result to the static class

1.43
.
∎

2
Construction of the Mañé Hamiltonian

In this section, we construct a Tonelli Hamiltonian
H:\mathbb{T}^{1}\times T^{*}M\to\mathbb{R}
whose Hamilton-Jacobi equation admits a smooth, recurrent, non-periodic viscosity solution. This construction is inspired by Mañé’s approach in the appendix of
[
Mn92
]
.

The idea of the construction involves creating regions in
M
where a selected viscosity solution is periodic with a prescribed minimal period. Taking these periods to infinity will make the solution non-periodic, and controlling the size of the regions facilitates the proof of the recurrence.

We will also highlight certain symmetries imposed on the Hamiltonian vector field, which are crucial for ensuring the existence of regular, recurrent, non-periodic viscosity solutions, as will be shown in Section
4
.

In the first subsection, we present the construction of the Hamiltonian, and in the second, we analyze the behavior of the
n
-Peierls barriers
h^{n\infty}
(see Definition
1.27
), which will be essential for building periodic viscosity solutions with prescribed minimal periods.

2.1
Construction of the Lagrangian
L

We construct the Lagrangian
L
following the examples constructed by Mañé in the appendix of
[
Mn92
]
. We will designate these Lagrangians as Mañé Lagrangians.

The idea is to select the desired dynamics of
f_{t}=\phi^{t}_{L}
on a specific submanifold of
TM
and subsequently extending it into a Lagrangian flow across the entire tangent bundle. From the Hamiltonian perspective, the initial submanifold corresponds to the zero-section of
T^{*}M
which yields the desired Hamiltonian dynamics.

We first need to fix a region of work in the manifold

2.1.2
The Isotopy

Now we define the isotopy

The Isotopy
g_{t}
.

Let (

The Isotopy

Let
\eta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth map, null on

, constant equal to

[1,+\infty)
and increasing on
[0,1]
. And consider for all integer

For all angles

The isotopy

is taken as

\mathcal{R}_{t}(x)=\begin{cases}R_{\frac{1}{\rho_{n}}\eta(t)}(x)&\text{if }x\in\overline{C_{n}}\\

on the sets

, and these angles decrease radially and progressively on

until they reach zero on

.

Computation shows that the map

.

The Isotopy

.

The desired isotopy

Remark 2.2
.

Symmetries of

and

.

Remark 2.3
.

1.

Computation shows that the smaller the quantities

2.1.3
The Mañé Lagrangian

The Mañé Lagrangian

The Vector Field

.

The first point of Remark

tells us that if

and

are small enough, we get for all times

is invertible for all times

This allows us to define the corresponding vector field
X(t,x)=X_{t}(x):\mathbb{R}\times M\to TM
by

X_{t}=\frac{df_{t}}{dt}\circ f_{t}^{-1}

(2.11)

We know that for all positive integer

\eta^{(k)}(0)=\eta^{(k)}(1)=0
. Thus,
X_{0}=X_{1}=0
and so are all of its time derivatives. This implies that the vector field
X_{t}
reduces to be a smooth time periodic vector field

.

Consider the time periodic vector fields
Y_{t}
relative to the isotopy
\mathcal{R}_{t}
and recall the relation between

and
g_{t}

Y_{t}=\frac{d\mathcal{R}_{t}}{dt}\circ\mathcal{R}_{t}^{-1}\quad\text{and}\quad Z=\frac{dg_{t}}{dt}\circ g_{t}^{-1}

(2.12)

Proposition 2.4
.

We have

X_{t}=Y_{t}+d\mathcal{R}_{t}.Z\circ\mathcal{R}^{-1}_{t}

(2.13)

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\frac{df_{t}}{dt}=\frac{d(\mathcal{R}_{t}\circ g_{t})}{dt}=\frac{d\mathcal{R}_{t}}{dt}\circ g_{t}+d\mathcal{R}_{t}.\frac{dg_{t}}{dt}

Then

\displaystyle X_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{df_{t}}{dt}\circ f_{t}^{-1}=\frac{d\mathcal{R}_{t}}{dt}\circ g_{t}\circ g^{-1}_{t}\circ\mathcal{R}^{-1}_{t}+d\mathcal{R}_{t}.\frac{dg_{t}}{dt}\circ g^{-1}_{t}\circ\mathcal{R}^{-1}_{t}

\displaystyle=Y_{t}+d\mathcal{R}_{t}.Z\circ\mathcal{R}^{-1}_{t}

∎

The Mañé Lagrangian
L
.

Following the appendix of
[
Mn92
]
, we define a smooth time-periodic Mañé Lagrangian
L:\mathbb{T}^{1}\times TM\to\mathbb{R}
by

L(t,x,v)=\frac{1}{2}\|v-X_{t}(x)\|^{2}

(2.14)

where
\|.\|
is the norm coming from the metric of

. It reduces to the
L^{2}
-norm when restricted to the chart where the dynamics are non trivial.

Proposition 2.6
.

The Mañé Hamiltonian
H:\mathbb{T}^{1}\times T^{*}M\to\mathbb{R}
associated to the Mañé Lagrangian
L
is given by

H(t,x,p)=\frac{1}{2}\|p+X_{t}(x)\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|X_{t}(x)\|^{2}

(2.15)

with vector field
X_{H}=(X_{H}^{x},X_{H}^{p})
given by

X_{H}^{x}(t,x,p)=p+X_{t}(x)\quad\text{and}\quad X_{H}^{p}(t,x,p)=-p.dX_{t}(x)

(2.16)

Proof.

Using the metric
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
induced by the norm
\|\cdot\|
, we identify elements of
v
of
T_{x}M
with elements
p
of
T^{*}_{x}M
as follows :
p=\langle v,\cdot\rangle
. Then, we have for all
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times M
and
(v,p)\in T_{x}M\times T^{*}_{x}M

\displaystyle 2.\big[H(t,x,p)+L(t,x,v)-p.v\big]

\displaystyle=\|p+X_{t}(x)\|^{2}-\|X_{t}(x)\|^{2}+\|v-X_{t}(x)\|^{2}-2p.v

\displaystyle=\|p\|^{2}+2p.X_{t}(x)+\|v\|^{2}-2v.X_{t}(x)+\|X_{t}(x)\|^{2}-2p.v

\displaystyle=\|p-v+X_{t}(x)\|^{2}\geq 0

with equality if and only if
p=v-X_{t}(x)=\partial_{v}L(t,x,v)
.

Direct computation yields

X_{H}^{x}(t,x,p)=\partial_{p}H(t,x,p)=p+X_{t}(x)\quad\text{and}\quad X_{H}^{p}(t,x,p)=-\partial_{x}H(t,x,p)=-p.dX_{t}(x)

∎

Proposition 2.7

Proof.

Regularity.

Strict Convexity.

It follows that
\partial^{2}_{vv}L(t,x,v)>0
for all
(t,x,v)\in\mathbb{T}^{1}\times TM
.

Superlinearity.
Since the norm is squared in the definition (
2.14
) of
L
, we get
\frac{L(t,x,v)}{\|v\|}\to\infty
as
\|v\|\to\infty
.

Completeness.
We will prove the completeness for the Hamiltonian flow
\phi_{H}
which is conjugated to the Lagrangian flow
\phi_{L}
by the Legendre map
\mathcal{L}
introduced in Subsection
1.1
. Note from the formula of the Hamiltonian vector field
X_{H}
expressed in (
2.16
), its time-periodicity and from the compactness of the manifold
M
that there exist two positive real numbers
C_{1}
and
C_{2}>0
such that for all
(s,x,p)\in\mathbb{T}^{1}\times T^{*}M
, we have

\|X_{H}(s,x,p)\|\leq C_{1}\|p\|+C_{2}

We work locally on charts in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Fix a time
T>0
. For all time
t
such that
|t-s|\leq T
and
\phi_{H}^{s,t}(x,p)
is well defined and belongs to the chart, we have

\left\|\frac{d}{dt}\phi_{H}^{s,t}(x,p)\right\|=\|X_{H}(t,\phi_{H}^{s,t}(x,p))\|\leq C_{1}\|\phi_{H}^{s,t}(x,p)\|+C_{2}

Hence, we get

\displaystyle\|\phi_{H}^{s,t}(x,p)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi_{H}^{s,t}(x,p)-(x,p)\|+\|(x,p)\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{s}^{t}X_{H}(\tau,\phi_{H}^{s,\tau}(x,p))\;d\tau\right\|+\|(x,p)\|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{s}^{t}\|X_{H}(\tau,\phi_{H}^{s,\tau}(x,p))\|d\tau+\|(x,p)\|

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\int_{s}^{t}\|\phi_{H}^{s,\tau}(x,p)\|d\tau+C_{2}.(t-s)+\|(x,p)\|

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\int_{s}^{t}\|\phi_{H}^{s,\tau}(x,p)\|d\tau+C_{2}T+\|(x,p)\|

Applying Grönwall lemma yields

\left\|\phi_{H}^{s,t}(x,p)\right\|\leq(C_{2}T+\|(x,p)\|).e^{C_{1}(t-s)}\leq(C_{2}T+\|(x,p)\|).e^{C_{1}T}

This compactness result for a fixed
T>0
allow to extend
\phi_{H}^{s,t}(x,p)
to all times
t
such that
|t-s|\leq T
. Since this holds for all
T>0
, we deduce the completeness of the Hamiltonian vector field
X_{H}
, and by conjugacy, the completeness of the Lagrangian vector field
X_{L}
.
∎

We recall some properties of the Lagrangian

•

The projection on

Proof.

The proof is segmented into two main parts. One for non-periodicity and another for recurrence. However, before delving into these steps, we need to determine the action of the Lax-Oleinik semigroup

3.1.1
Evaluation of
\mathcal{T}^{k}u

We evaluate the action of the Lax-Oleinik operator

Proposition 3.2
.

For all integer

\mathcal{T}^{k}u(x)=\inf_{n\geq 0}\{h^{\rho_{n}\infty+k}(x_{n},x)\}

(3.2)

Proof.

We use the following lemma

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

be a sequence of viscosity solutions and let
u(t,x)=\inf_{n}\{v_{n}(t,x)\}
. Then

is a viscosity solution.

Proof.

Fix two real times

. Then, for all

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{s,t}u(s,x)

\displaystyle=\inf_{y\in M}\big\{u(s,y)+h^{s,t}(y,x)\big\}

\displaystyle=\inf_{y\in M}\big\{\inf_{n\geq 0}\{v_{n}(s,y)\}+h^{s,t}(y,x)\big\}

\displaystyle=\inf_{n\geq 0}\;\inf_{y\in M}\Big\{v_{n}(s,y)+h^{s,t}(y,x)\Big\}

\displaystyle=\inf_{n\geq 0}\big\{\mathcal{T}^{s,t}v_{n}(s,x)\big\}=\inf_{n\geq 0}\{v_{n}(t,x)\}=u(t,x)

∎

We know from Proposition
1.28
that the maps

are viscosity solutions. Hence, the lemma immediately implies that

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{k}u(x)=\inf_{n\geq 0}\{\mathcal{T}^{k}h^{\rho_{n}\infty}(x_{n},x)\}=\inf_{n\geq 0}\{h^{\rho_{n}\infty+k}(x_{n},x)\}

∎

In order to determine the exact periodicity of

around every orbit

Proposition 3.4
.

For all integers

.

Proof.

The Proposition
1.28

-periodic viscosity solutions of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation (

(i)

h^{\rho_{n}\infty}(x_{n},x_{n})=0
.

(ii)

For all

\mathcal{T}^{k}h^{\rho_{n}\infty}(x_{n},x_{n})=h^{\rho_{n}\infty+k}(x_{n},x_{n})\not=0

(i)

For this case, One needs to follow the flow

starting at

. Let

be the curve

\displaystyle h^{\rho_{n}\infty}(x_{n},x_{n})=\liminf_{k}h^{\rho_{n}k}(x_{n},x_{n})

(ii)

Let

Since we assumed

Using the definition of

3.1.2

We proceed with the proof of the non-periodicity of

(ii)

The point

f_{t}

\partial B_{n}
towards

(see Figure

2.12

But before that, let us take a deeper look on the minimizing curves that realize

\gamma_{i}:[0,\rho_{m}q_{i}+k]\to M

\gamma_{i}(t_{i})\in\partial B_{n}

that there is a uniform Lipschitz value

for all minimizing curves. Hence, we have

\displaystyle d(\partial C_{n},\partial B_{n})\leq d(\gamma_{i}(s_{i}),\gamma_{i}(t_{i}))\leq M.(t_{i}-s_{i})

Set
\tau=\frac{d(\partial C_{n},\partial B_{n})}{M}
which is independent of

. The following holds.

Lemma 3.6
.

There exists a real number
\varepsilon_{n}>0
depending only on
\tau
such that

\inf\{h^{s,t}(x,y)\;|\;0\leq s<t,\;t-s\geq\tau,\;x\in\partial C_{n},\;y\in\partial B_{n}\}\geq\varepsilon_{n}

(3.3)

Proof.

We argue by contradiction. Assume that there exists a sequence of curves
\sigma_{i}:[s_{i},t_{i}]\to M
such that
t_{i}-s_{i}\geq\tau
,
x_{i}:=\sigma_{i}(s_{i})\in\partial C_{n}
,
y_{i}:=\sigma_{i}(t_{i})\in\partial B_{n}
and
h^{s_{i},t_{i}}(x_{i},y_{i})=A_{L}(\sigma_{i})\to 0
as

. The time periodicity of the Lagrangian allows, up to time translation, to assume that

for all

. And by compactness of
\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\partial C_{n}\times\partial B_{n}

(s,x,y)\in\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\partial C_{n}\times\partial B_{n}
.

If the times
t_{i}
are bounded, we can also assume that

converges to
t\geq s+\tau
. The continuity of the potential

results in the equality

h^{s,t}(x,y)=\lim\limits_{i}h^{s_{i},t_{i}}(x_{i},y_{i})=0

This gives rise to a minimizing curve

3.1.3
Recurrence of
u

Set for all
n\geq 0
, the integer
p_{n}=\prod\limits_{k=0}^{n}\rho_{k}
. The
\rho_{n}
-periodicity of the
\rho_{n}
-barriers
h^{\rho_{n}\infty}
tells that

\begin{split}\mathcal{T}^{p_{k}}u(x)&=\inf_{n\geq 0}\{h^{\rho_{n}\infty+p_{k}}(x_{n},x)\}\\
&=\min\big\{\inf_{0\leq n\leq k}\{h^{\rho_{n}\infty}(x_{n},x)\},\inf_{n>k}\{h^{\rho_{n}\infty+p_{k}}(x_{n},x)\}\big\}\end{split}

(3.6)

We see that the
k
first elements of the infimum defining
\mathcal{T}^{p_{k}}u
coincide with those in the infimum defining
u
. Our expectation is that the difference between these two maps diminishes as
k
approaches infinity.

We need to compare the two maps
h^{\rho_{n}\infty}(x_{n},x)
and
h^{\rho_{n}\infty+i}(x_{n},x)
for some
0\leq i<\rho_{n}
. A lemma in this direction is the following

Lemma 3.8
.

For all integers
n\geq 0
and
0\leq i<\rho_{n}
,

h^{\rho_{n}\infty+i}(x_{n},x)=h^{\rho_{n}\infty}(x^{i}_{n},x)

(3.7)

Proof.

We know that
h^{i}(x_{n},x^{i}_{n})=0
and
h^{\rho_{n}-i}(x^{i}_{n},x_{n})=0
as they are respectively realized by the curves
f_{t}(x_{n})
and
f_{t}(x^{i}_{n})
. Hence, applying the triangular inequality (
1.17
), we obtain

h^{\rho_{n}\infty+i}(x_{n},x)=\liminf_{j}h^{\rho_{n}j+i}(x_{n},x)\\
\leq\liminf_{j}\big[h^{i}(x_{n},x^{i}_{n})+h^{\rho_{n}j}(x^{i}_{n},x)\big]=\liminf_{j}h^{\rho_{n}j}(x^{i}_{n},x)=h^{\rho_{n}\infty}(x^{i}_{n},x)

and

h^{\rho_{n}\infty}(x^{i}_{n},x)=h^{\rho_{n}\infty+\rho_{n}}(x^{i}_{n},x)=\liminf_{j}h^{\rho_{n}j+\rho_{n}}(x^{i}_{n},x)\\
\leq\liminf_{j}\big[h^{\rho_{n}-i}(x^{i}_{n},x_{n})+h^{\rho_{n}j+i}(x_{n},x)\big]=\liminf_{j}h^{\rho_{n}j+i}(x_{n},x)=h^{\rho_{n}\infty+i}(x_{n},x)

∎

We proceed to the comparison. Recall from the definition of the
n
-Peierls barriers and from Proposition
1.26
that
h^{\rho_{n}\infty}
is
\kappa_{1}
-Lipschitz. Thus, we get for all integer
n>k
and all point
x
in
M
,

\displaystyle|h^{\rho_{n}\infty+p_{k}}(x_{n},x)-h^{\rho_{n}\infty}(x_{n},x)|

\displaystyle=|h^{\rho_{n}\infty}(x^{p_{k}}_{n},x)-h^{\rho_{n}\infty}(x_{n},x)|

\displaystyle\leq 2\kappa_{1}.d(x^{p_{k}}_{n},x_{n})\leq 2\kappa_{1}.2r_{n}\leq 4\kappa_{1}.r_{k}=:\varepsilon_{k}

Since we have equality
h^{\rho_{n}\infty+p_{k}}=h^{\rho_{n}\infty}
for all
n\leq k
, it follows that for all integer
n\geq 0
and all
x\in M

|h^{\rho_{n}\infty+p_{k}}(x_{n},x)-h^{\rho_{n}\infty}(x_{n},x)|\leq\varepsilon_{k}

(3.8)

In particular,

h^{\rho_{n}\infty}(x_{n},x)-\varepsilon_{k}\leq h^{\rho_{n}\infty+p_{k}}(x_{n},x)\leq h^{\rho_{n}\infty}(x_{n},x)+\varepsilon_{k}

Taking the infimum over
n
and using (
3.6
), we get

u(x)-\varepsilon_{k}\leq\mathcal{T}^{p_{k}}u(x)\leq u(x)+\varepsilon_{k}

or more precisely

\|\mathcal{T}^{p_{k}}u(x)-u(x)\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon_{k}\to 0\quad\text{as }k\to+\infty

This means that
\mathcal{T}^{p_{k}}u
converges to
u
in
\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})
, indicating that
u
belongs to its own
\omega
-limit set
\omega(u)
and is a recurrent viscosity solution.
∎

Construction of the conjugacy between

and

.
Let us define the bijective map

Proof.

Remark 4.17
.

1.

Lemma
4.14
can be understood as follows: The Mañé set
\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{Z}
corresponding to the autonomous component
Z
of
X_{t}
is symmetric with respect to the zero section of
TM
. The radial symmetry imposed on
Z
yields a Mañé set that, when viewed radially, closely resembles what is observed in one-dimensional systems, specifically the phase portrait of a pendulum. A radial section of
\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{Z}
around
r_{n}
is represented in Figure
3
.

Figure 3
:
Radial Section at angle
\theta=0
of the Mañé Set
\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{Z}
.

2.

Note that the obtained
C^{1}
regularity is highly unstable. A generic perturbation of the constants
c_{n}
induces irregularities. Specifically, if the equality
c_{n}+h^{\rho_{n}\infty}(x_{n},x)=h^{\infty}(z_{\infty},x)
occurs outside the Peierls set
\mathcal{A}_{0}
, then the loss of regularity is inevitable. In other words, the set of
C^{1}
recurrent viscosity solutions is sparse within the non-wandering set
\Omega(\mathcal{T})
of the Lax-Oleinik operator
\mathcal{T}
.

3.

Similarly, any perturbation of the Hamiltonian that disrupts its symmetries may also result in a loss of regularity, and such a Hamiltonian may not possess any
C^{1}
recurrent viscosity solution. There are examples of Hamiltonian systems with no regular elements in their non-wandering set
\Omega(\mathcal{T})
. The simplest example is the autonomous simple pendulum, where the unique element of
\Omega(\mathcal{T})
is a Lipschitz weak-KAM solution.

4.

Additionally, obtaining
C^{1,1}
recurrent viscosity solutions is significantly easier than obtaining
C^{2}
or more regular solutions. This is because if the infimum of two Peierls barriers occurs on the Peierls set,
C^{1,1}
regularity is guaranteed. Achieving higher regularity requires modifying the Hamiltonian to ensure that convergence to such points occurs only via parabolic trajectories.

5.

From a symplectic perspective, the smooth recurrent locally viscosity solution
u_{c}
gives rise to a Lagrangian submanifold, namely the graph of
du_{c}
in the cotangent bundle
T^{*}M
, which is (
C^{1}
-) recurrent under the Hamiltonian flow
\phi_{H}
.

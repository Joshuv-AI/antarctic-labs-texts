anti-associative dendriform algebras
Zafar Normatov

Abstract
. The general operadic approach to splitting algebraic operations was developed in [ 4 ] . By splitting the product in a given algebraic variety 𝒞 \mathcal{C} , notion of 𝒞 \mathcal{C} -dendriform algebras was systematically studied in [ 17 ] . This article aims to study “anti-associative dendriform algebras”, which offer an approach to addressing anti-associativity. These algebras are defined by two operations whose sum is anti-associative. Furthermore, the notion of 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators on anti-associative algebras is presented as a tool to interpret anti-associative dendriform algebras. Moreover, anti-associative algebras with nondegenerate Connes cocycles admit compatible anti-associative dendriform algebra structures.

Motivation

Lie algebras were introduced by Sophus Lie in the 1870s to formalize the study of infinitesimal transformations. Since then, they have become fundamental tools in a wide range of areas, including representation theory, differential geometry, algebraic geometry, number theory, quantum mechanics, and gauge theory.

A vector space
A
with a bilinear operation
\ast
is called a Lie admissible algebra if
(A,[-,-])
is a Lie algebra, where
[-,-]
is the commutator. In particular, an

associative algebra

(A,\cdot)
which is a vector space
A
with a bilinear operation
\cdot

x\cdot(y\cdot z)=(x\cdot y)\cdot z
is a Lie admissible algebra. Hence, there exist a functor from the category
As
of associative algebras to the category
Lie

\mathrm{\textbf{As}}\rightarrow\mathrm{\textbf{Lie}}.

Furthermore, pre-Lie algebras, also known by various names such as left-symmetric algebras, quasi-associative algebras, or Vinberg algebras, are also Lie-admissible algebras. These algebras were first introduced by A. Cayley in 1896 as a type of rooted tree algebra. Hence, one can define a functor from the category
Pre-Lie

Lie
of all Lie algebras. Thus, one has the following diagram

2.
anti-associative dendriform algebras

In this section, we prove that the diagram (
3
) is commutative. Moreover, using a bimodule structure on a vector space, we define an anti-associative algebra structure on the direct sum of the vector space with itself by using the operations of an anti-associative dendriform algebra.

Definition 2.1

(
[
6
]
)
.

An algebra
(A,\diamond)
over a field
\mathbb{F}
is called a Jacobi–Jordan algebra if it satisfies
the following two identities

\displaystyle x\diamond y-y\diamond x=0,

\displaystyle x\diamond(y\diamond z)+y\diamond(z\diamond x)+z\diamond(x\diamond y)=0,

for all
x,y,z\in A
.

In other words, Jacobi–Jordan algebras are commutative algebras which satisfy the
Jacobi identity. In the case of Lie algebras, the product is not commutative but anticommutative.

Recall that
(A,\circ)
is called a
Jacobi-Jordan-admissible
algebra

[
5
,
19
]
, where
A
is a vector space with a bilinear operation

\circ:A\otimes A\rightarrow A
, if the bilinear operation

[-,-]:A\otimes A\rightarrow A
defined by

[x,y]=x\circ y+y\circ x,\;\;\forall x,y\in A,

makes
(A,[-,-])
a Jacobi-Jordan algebra. In this case,
(A,[-,-])
is called the
sub-adjacent Jacobi-Jordan algebra
of

(A,\circ)
and denoted by
(\mathfrak{J}(A),[-,-])
. Obviously, an anti-associative algebra is a Jacobi-Jordan-admissible algebra.

Another example of Jacobi-Jordan-admissible algebras is pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebras.

Definition 2.2
.

[
5
]

A pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebra is a vector space
A
together with a bilinear multiplication
\cdot:A\otimes A\to A
, such that for any
x,y,z\in A
, the anti-associator
AAs(x,y,z):=(x\cdot y)\cdot z+x\cdot(y\cdot z)
is skew-symmetric in
x,y,
i.e.
AAs(x,y,z)=-AAs(y,x,z)
or equivalently

\displaystyle(x\cdot y)\cdot z+x\cdot(y\cdot z)=-(y\cdot x)\cdot z-y\cdot(x\cdot z).

We now give a definition analogous to that of an associative admissible algebra
[
11
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
A
be a vector space with two bilinear operations

\succ:A\otimes A\rightarrow A,\quad\prec:A\otimes A\rightarrow A.

Define a bilinear operation
\ast
as

x\ast y=x\succ y+x\prec y,\quad\forall x,y\in A.

(4)

The triple
(A,\succ,\prec)
is called an anti-associative admissible algebra if
(A,\ast)
is an anti-associative algebra.
In this case,
(A,\ast)
is called the associated anti-associative algebra of
(A,\succ,\prec
).

Remark 2.4
.

The triple
(A,\succ,\prec)
is an anti-associative admissible algebra if and only if the following
equation holds:

\begin{split}&(x\succ y)\succ z+(x\prec y)\succ z+(x\succ y)\prec z+(x\prec y)\prec z\\
&=-x\succ(y\succ z)-x\succ(y\prec z)-x\prec(y\succ z)-x\prec(y\prec z),\quad\forall x,y,z\in A.\end{split}

(5)

Definition 2.5
.

Let
A
be a vector space with two bilinear operations
\succ
and
\prec
. The triple

(A,\succ,\prec)
is called a
anti-associative dendriform algebra
if the following equations hold:

\displaystyle(x\ast y)\succ z

\displaystyle=-x\succ(y\succ z),

(6)

\displaystyle x\prec(y\ast z)

\displaystyle=-(x\prec y)\prec z,

(7)

\displaystyle x\succ(y\prec z)

\displaystyle=-(x\succ y)\prec z,\quad\forall x,y,z\in A,

(8)

where the bilinear operation
\ast
is defined by Eq. (
4
).

Example 2.6
.

Let
(A,\prec,\succ)
be a one-dimensional anti-associative dendriform algebra with basis element
e
.
Assume that
e\succ e=\alpha e
and

e\prec e=\beta e
, where
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}
.
Then, by equations (
6

8

\alpha(2\alpha+\beta)=0
and
\beta(2\beta+\alpha)=0

\alpha=\beta=0
, and every one-
dimensional anti-associative dendriform algebra is trivial.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
(A,\succ,\prec)
be an anti-associative dendriform algebra. Then the followings hold.

(i). Define a bilinear operation
\ast
by Eq. (
4
). Then
(A,\ast)
is an anti-associative algebra,
called the
associated anti-associative algebra
of
(A,\succ,\prec).

Furthermore,
(A,\succ,\prec)
is called a
compatible anti-associative dendriform algebra structure
on
(A,\ast).

(ii). The bilinear operation
\circ:A\otimes A\rightarrow A
given by

x\circ y=x\succ y+y\prec x,\ \ \forall x,y\in A,

defines a pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebra, called the
associated pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebra
of
(A,\succ,\prec).

(iii). Both
(A,\ast)
and
(A,\circ)
have the same sub-adjacent Jacobi-Jordan algebra
(\mathfrak{g}(A),[-,-])
defined by

[x,y]=x\succ y+x\prec y+y\succ x+y\prec x,\quad\forall x,y\in A.

Moreover, the commutative diagram (2) holds.

Proof.

(i) is straightforward.

(ii) Let
x,y,z\in A
. Then we have

\begin{split}(x\circ y)\circ z=&(x\succ y+y\prec x)\succ z+z\prec(x\succ y+y\prec x)\\
=&(x\succ y)\succ z+(y\prec x)\succ z+z\prec(x\succ y)+z\prec(y\prec x),\\
x\circ(y\circ z)=&x\succ(y\succ z+z\prec y)+(y\succ z+z\prec y)\prec x\\
=&x\succ(y\succ z)+x\succ(z\prec y)+(y\succ z)\prec x+(z\prec y)\prec x.\end{split}

By swapping
x
and
y
, we have

\begin{split}(y\circ x)\circ z=(y\succ x)\succ z+(x\prec y)\succ z+z\prec(y\succ x)+z\prec(x\prec y),\\
y\circ(x\circ z)=y\succ(x\succ z)+y\succ(z\prec x)+(x\succ z)\prec y+(z\prec x)\prec y.\end{split}

Using Eqs. (
6
)–(
8
), we obtain

\begin{split}x\circ(y\circ z)+y\circ(x\circ z)=&x\succ(y\succ z)+x\succ(z\prec y)+(y\succ z)\prec x+(z\prec y)\prec x\\
&+y\succ(x\succ z)+y\succ(z\prec x)+(x\succ z)\prec y+(z\prec x)\prec y\\
=&x\succ(y\succ z)+(z\prec y)\prec x+y\succ(x\succ z)+(z\prec x)\prec y\\
=&-(x\succ y+x\prec y)\succ z-z\prec(y\succ x+y\prec x)\\
&-(y\succ x+y\prec x)\succ z-z\prec(x\succ y+x\prec y)\\
=&-(y\circ x)\circ z-(x\circ y)\circ z.\end{split}

Moreover, we have

x\circ y+y\circ x=x\succ y+y\prec x+x\prec y+y\succ x=x\ast y+y\ast x,\quad\forall x,y\in A.

Thus
(A,\circ)
is a Jacobi-Jordan-admissible algebra and hence a pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebra.

(iii). It is straightforward.
Note that it also appears in the proof of
(ii).

Let
(A,\ast)
be an anti-associative algebra. Recall that a
bimodule
of
(A,\ast)
is a triple
(V,l,r)
consisting of a vector space
V
and linear maps

l,r:A\rightarrow\rm{End}_{\mathbb{F}}(V)
such that

l(x\ast y)v=-l(x)(l(y)v),\ \ r(x\ast y)v=-r(y)(r(x)v),\ \ l(x)(r(y)v)=-r(y)(l(x)v),\ \ \forall x,y\in A,v\in V.

In particular,
(A,L_{\ast},R_{\ast})
is a bimodule of
(A,\ast)
, where
L_{\ast},R_{\ast}:A\rightarrow{\rm End}_{\mathbb{F}}(A)
are two linear maps defined by

L_{\ast}(x)(y)=R_{\ast}(y)(x)=x\ast y
for all
x,y\in A
respectively.

Suppose that
(A,\ast)
is an anti-associative algebra. Let
V
be a vector space and
l,r:A\rightarrow{\rm End}_{\mathbb{F}}(V)
be linear maps.
Then
(V,l,r)
is a bimodule of
(A,\ast)
if and only if there is an anti-associative algebra structure on the direct sum
A\oplus V
of vector spaces with the following bilinear operation, still denoted by
\ast
:

(x,u)\ast(y,v)=(x\ast y,l(x)v+r(y)u),\quad\forall x,y\in A,u,v\in V.

We denote this anti-associative algebra by
A\ltimes_{l,r}V
.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
A
be a vector space with two bilinear operations
\succ
and
\prec
. Define a bilinear operation
\ast
by Eq. (
4
).
Then
(A,\succ,\prec)
is an anti-associative dendriform algebra if and only if
(A,\ast)
is an anti-associative algebra and
(A,L_{\succ},R_{\prec})
is a bimodule of
(A,\ast)
, where
two linear maps
L_{\succ},R_{\prec}:A\rightarrow{\rm End}_{\mathbb{F}}(A)
are defined by

L_{\succ}(x)(y)=x\succ y,\quad R_{\prec}(x)(y)=y\prec x,\quad\forall x,y\in A.

Proof.

From Eqs. (
5
), (
6
), (
7
) and (
8
) it follows that
(A,\succ,\prec)
is an anti-associative dendriform algebra if and only if
(A,\succ,\prec)
is an anti-associative admissible algebra, that is,
(A,\ast)
is an anti-associative algebra, and for all
x,y,z\in A
, the following equations hold:

x\succ(y\succ z)=-(x\cdot y)\succ z,\ (x\prec y)\prec z=-x\prec(y\cdot z),\ (x\succ y)\prec z=-x\succ(y\prec z).

Let
x,y,z\in A
. Then we have

\displaystyle L_{\succ}(x\ast y)(z)=-L_{\succ}(x)(L_{\succ}(y)z)\ \

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\ \ x\succ(y\succ z)=-(x\ast y)\succ z,

\displaystyle R_{\prec}(x\ast y)(z)=-R_{\prec}(y)(R_{\prec}(x)z)\ \

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\ \ (z\prec x)\prec y=-z\prec(x\ast y),

\displaystyle L_{\succ}(x)(R_{\prec}(y)z)=-R_{\prec}(y))(L_{\succ}(x)z)\ \

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\ \ x\succ(z\prec y)=-(x\succ z)\prec y.

Hence
(A,L_{\succ},R_{\prec})
is a bimodule.
∎

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
V

be a dual space of

Let

(i)

(ii)

(V,l,0),(V,0,r),(V^{*},r^{*},0)

(i)  Let

\begin{split}\langle l^{*}(x\ast y)u^{*},v\rangle=&\langle u^{*},l(x\ast y)v\rangle=-\langle u^{*},l(x)(l(y)v)\rangle=-\langle l^{*}(x)u^{*},l(y)v\rangle=-\langle l^{*}(y)(l^{*}(x)u^{*}),v\rangle,\\

3.
anti-associative dendriform algebras and
\mathcal{O}
-operators

M. Aguiar in 2000
[
1
]
was the first who noticed a relation between Rota–Baxter
algebras and dendriform algebras. The concept of an
\mathcal{O}
-operator, which generalizes the Rota–Baxter operator, was introduced by C. Bai, L. Guo, and K. Ni in 2010 [4]. They established that any dendriform algebra arises explicitly from an algebra admitting an
\mathcal{O}
-operator. By the motivation of their work, V. Yu. Gubarev and P. S. Kolesnikov proved that every dendriform dialgebra in
\operatorname{Var}
can be embedded into a
Rota–Baxter algebra of weight zero in the same variety
[
12
]
.

Similarly to the above works, the authors of
[
17
]
proved the following Proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
R
be a Rota–Baxter operator on an algebra
(A,\mu)

which belongs to
\mathcal{C}
. For
a,b\in A
, let
a\succ b:=R(a)b
and
a\prec b:=aR(b)
.
Then
(A,\succ,\prec)
is a
\mathcal{C}
-dendriform algebra.

In this section, for the case where
\mathcal{C}
is an anti-associative algebra, we prove that the result holds more generally for
\mathcal{O}
-operators , which we substitute for the Rota–Baxter operators in the construction.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
(A,\ast)
be an anti-associative algebra and
(V,l,r)
be a
bimodule. A linear map
T:V\rightarrow A
is called an
\mathcal{O}
-operator
of
(A,\ast)
associated to
(V,l,r)
if the following equation holds:

T(u)\ast T(v)=T\big(l(T(u))v+r(T(v))u\big),\quad\forall u,v\in V.

In particular, an

\mathcal{O}
-operator
T
of
(A,\ast)
associated to the
bimodule
(A,L_{\ast},R_{\ast})
is called an
Rota–Baxter
operator
, that is,
T:A\rightarrow A
is a linear map satisfying

T(x)\ast T(y)=T\big(T(x)\ast y+x\ast T(y)\big),\quad\forall x,y\in A.

(10)

In this case, we call
(A,T)
a
Rota–Baxter algebra
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(A,\ast)
be an anti-associative algebra and
(V,l,r)
be a
bimodule.
Suppose that
T:V\rightarrow A
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator of
(A,\ast)
associated to
(V,l,r)
.
Define two bilinear operations
\succ,\prec
on
V
respectively as

\displaystyle u\succ v=l(T(u))v,\quad u\prec v=r(T(v))u,\quad\forall u,v\in V.

(11)

Then
(V,\succ,\prec)
is an
anti-associative dendriform algebra. In this
case,
T
is a homomorphism of anti-associative algebras from the
associated anti-associative algebra
(V,\ast)
to
(A,\ast)
.
Furthermore, there is an induced anti-associative dendriform algebra structure
on
T(V)=\{T(u)~|~u\in V\}\subseteq A
given by

\displaystyle T(u)\succ T(v)=T(u\succ v),\quad T(u)\prec T(v)=T(u\prec v),\quad\forall u,v\in V,

(12)

and
T
is a homomorphism of anti-associative dendriform algebras.

Proof.

Let
u,v,w\in V
. Then we have

\begin{split}-(u\ast v)\succ w=&-(u\succ v+u\prec v)\succ w=-l(T\big(l(T(u))v\big))w-l(T\big(r(T(v))u\big))w=-l(T(u)\ast T(v))w\\
=&l(T(u))(l(T(v))w)=u\succ(v\succ w).\end{split}

Similarly, we have

(u\prec v)\prec w=-u\prec(v\ast w),\quad(u\succ v)\prec w=-u\succ(v\prec w).

Thus by
Definition
2.5
,
(V,\succ,\prec)
is an anti-associative dendriform algebra.

Moreover,

\displaystyle T(u\ast v)

\displaystyle=T(u\succ v+u\prec v)=T(u\succ v)+T(u\prec v)=T\big(l(T(u))v\big)+T\big(r(T(v))u\big)=T(u)\ast T(v),\ \ \forall u,v\in V,

shows that
T
is a homomorphism of anti-associative algebras from the associated anti-associative algebra
(V,\ast)
to
(A,\ast)

Furthermore, for any
u,v,w\in V,
we can obtain

\displaystyle T(u)\succ(T(v)\succ T(w))

\displaystyle=T(u\succ(v\succ w))=-T(u\succ v)\succ T(w)-T(u\prec v)\succ T(w)

\displaystyle=-(T(u)\succ T(v))\succ T(w)-(T(u)\prec T(v))\succ T(w)

\displaystyle=-(T(u)\succ T(v)+T(u)\prec T(v))\succ T(w).

Similarly, one can derive

\displaystyle(T(u)\prec T(v))\prec T(w)

\displaystyle=-T(u)\prec(T(v)\succ T(w)+T(v)\prec T(w)),

\displaystyle(T(u)\succ T(v))\prec T(w)

\displaystyle=-T(u)\succ(T(v)\prec T(w)),

which imply that
T(V)
is an anti-associative dendriform algebra. This
completes the proof.
∎

4.
anti-associative dendriform algebras and
Connes cocycles

In this section, we show that anti-associative dendriform algebras can be obtained from nondegenerate Connes cocycles of anti-associative algebras.

A
Connes cocycle
on an associative algebra
(A,\cdot)
is an
antisymmetric bilinear form
\mathcal{B}
satisfying

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(x\cdot y,z)+\mathcal{B}(y\cdot z,x)+\mathcal{B}(z\cdot x,y)=0,\quad\forall x,y,z\in A.

It corresponds to the original definition of cyclic cohomology by
Connes (
[
7
]
). Note that there is a close relation between
dendriform algebras and Connes cocycles (
[
3
]
). Next we
consider a Connes cocycle on anti-associative algebras.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(A,\ast)
be an anti-associative algebra. A bilinear form
\mathcal{B}
on
A
is called a
Connes cocycle
if

\mathcal{B}(a\ast b,c)+\mathcal{B}(b\ast c,a)+\mathcal{B}(c\ast a,b)=0,\quad\forall a,b,c\in A.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
(A,\ast)
be an anti-associative algebra and
\mathcal{B}
be a
nondegenerate Connes cocycle on
(A,\ast)
. Then
there exists a compatible anti-associative dendriform algebra structure
(A
,

\succ
,
\prec)
on
(A,\ast)
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(x\succ y,z)=\mathcal{B}(y,z\ast x),\ \ \mathcal{B}(x\prec y,z)=\mathcal{B}(x,y\ast z),\ \ \forall x,y,z\in A.

(13)

Proof.

Define a linear map
T:A\rightarrow A^{*}
by

\langle T(x),y\rangle=\mathcal{B}(x,y),\ \ \forall x,y\in A,

where,
A^{*}
is a dual vector space of
A
. By Lemma
2.9
,
(A^{*},R^{*},L^{*})
is a bimodule of
A
. Then
T
is invertible
and
T^{-1}

is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator of the anti-associative algebra
(A,\ast)
associated to the bimodule
(A^{*},R^{*},L^{*})
.
By Theorem
3.5
, there is a compatible anti-associative dendriform algebra structure
\succ,\prec
on
(A,\ast)
given by

x\succ y=T^{-1}\big(R^{*}(x)T(y)\big),\quad x\prec y=T^{-1}\big(L^{*}(y)T(x)\big),\quad\forall x,y\in A,

which gives exactly Eq. (
13
).
∎

Next, we turn to the double construction of Connes cocycles. Let
(A,\ast_{A})
be an anti-associative
algebra and suppose that there is a anti-associative algebra structure
\ast_{A^{*}}
on its dual space
A^{*}
. We
construct an anti-associative algebra structure on the direct sum
A\oplus A^{*}
of the underlying vector
spaces of
A
and
A^{*}

such that both
A
and
A^{*}
are subalgebras and the symmetric bilinear
form on
A\oplus A^{*}
given by equation

\mathcal{B}(x+a^{*},y+b^{*})=\langle x,b^{*}\rangle+\langle a^{*},y\rangle,\ \ \forall x,y\in A,a^{*},b^{*}\in A^{*}.

is a Connes cocycle on
A\oplus A^{*}
. Such a construction
is called a
double construction
of Connes cocycle associated to
(A,\ast_{A})
and
(A^{*},\ast_{A^{*}})
and we
denote it by
(T(A)=A\bowtie A^{*},\mathcal{B})
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(T(A)=A\bowtie A^{*},\mathcal{B})
be a double construction of Connes cocycle. Then
there exists a compatible anti-associative dendriform algebra structure
\succ,\prec
on
T(A)
defined by equation (
13
).
Moreover,
A
and
A^{*}
are anti-associative dendriform subalgebras with this product.

Proof.

The first half follows from Theorem
4.2
. Let
x,y\in A
. Set
x\succ y=a+b^{*}
, where
a\in A,\ b^{*}\in A^{*}
. Since
A
is an anti-associative subalgebra of
T(A)
and

\mathcal{B}(A,A)=\mathcal{B}(A^{*},A^{*})=0,

(14)

we have
\mathcal{B}(b^{*},A^{*})=0
and

\mathcal{B}(b^{*},A)=\mathcal{B}(x\succ y-a,A)\overset{\eqref{B(A,A)}}{=}\mathcal{B}(x\succ y,A)\overset{\eqref{non-comm}}{=}\mathcal{B}(y,A\ast x)\overset{\eqref{B(A,A)}}{=}0.

Therefore
b^{*}=0
due to the nondependence of
\mathcal{B}
. Hence
x\succ y=a\in A
. Similarly,
x\prec y\in A
.
Thus
A
is an anti-associative dendriform subalgebra of
T(A)
with the product
\succ,\prec
. By symmetry of
A
and
A^{*}
,

A^{*}
is also an anti-associative dendriform subalgebra.
∎

Definition 4.4
.

Let
(T(A_{1})=A_{1}\bowtie A_{1}^{*},\mathcal{B}_{1})
and
(T(A_{2})=A_{2}\bowtie A_{2}^{*},\mathcal{B}_{2})
be two double constructions of Connes cocycles. They are isomorphic if there exists an isomorphism of anti-associative
algebras
\phi:T(A_{1})\rightarrow T(A_{2})
satisfying the following conditions:

\phi(A_{1})=A_{2},\ \phi(A_{1}^{*})=A_{2}^{*},\ \mathcal{B}_{1}(x,y)=\phi^{*}\mathcal{B}_{2}(x,y)=\mathcal{B}_{2}(\phi(x),\phi(y)),\quad\forall x,y\in A_{1}.

(15)

Proposition 4.5
.

Two double constructions of Connes cocycles
(T(A_{1})=A_{1}\bowtie A_{1}^{*},\mathcal{B}_{1})

and
(T(A_{2})=A_{2}\bowtie A_{2}^{*},\mathcal{B}_{2})

isomorphism
\phi:T(A_{1})\rightarrow T(A_{2})
satisfying Eq. (
15
), where the anti-associative dendriform algebra
structures on
T(A_{1})
and

are given by Eq. (
13
) respectively.

Proof.

It is straightforward.
∎

5.
Isomorphism classes of two dimensional
anti-associative dendriform algebras

In this section, we classify two-dimensional anti-associative dendriform algebras. Moreover, we introduce the annihilator of anti-associative dendriform algebras and demonstrate its role in the construction of new algebras from old ones.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
(A,\succ,\prec)

be an anti-associative dendriform algebra. If one of the
binary operations
\succ,\ \prec
in
A
vanishes, then with respect to the second one, the algebra
A
is anti-associative.

Proof.

It is straightforward.
∎

Definition 5.2
.

An anti-associative dendriform algebra
(A,\succ,\prec)
is
2
-nilpotent if
(x*_{i_{1}}y)*_{i_{2}}z=x*_{i_{3}}(y*_{i_{4}}z)=0

for all
*_{i_{1}},*_{i_{2}},*_{i_{3}},*_{i_{4}}~\in~\{\succ,\prec\}
and
x,y,z\in A
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
(A,\succ,\prec)
be an anti-associative dendriform algebra satisfying
x\succ y+x\prec y=0
for all
x,y\in A
. Then
(A,\succ,\prec
) is a 2-nilpotent algebra.

Proof.

Let
x,y,z\in A
. Then, we obtain

\begin{split}&x\succ(y\succ z)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{def:id1}}}{{=}}-(x\succ y+x\prec y)\succ z=0,\\
&(x\prec y)\prec z\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{def:id2}}}{{=}}-x\prec(y\succ z+y\prec z)=0,\\
\end{split}

Using these results we can get

\begin{split}&x\prec(y\succ z)=x\prec(y\succ z)+x\succ(y\succ z)=0,\\
&(x\prec y)\succ z=(x\prec y)\succ z+(x\prec y)\prec z=0.\end{split}

Furthermore, we have

\begin{split}&(x\succ y)\succ z=(x\succ y)\succ z+(x\prec y)\succ z=(x\succ y+x\prec y)\succ z=0,\\
&x\prec(y\prec z)=x\prec(y\prec z)+x\prec(y\succ z)=x\prec(y\prec z+y\succ z)=0,\\
&(x\succ y)\prec z=(x\succ y)\prec z+(x\succ y)\succ z=0,\\
&x\succ(y\prec z)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{def:id3}}}{{=}}(x\succ y)\prec z=0.\end{split}

∎

Now, we classify anti-associative dendriform algebra structures on

2
-dimensional complex vector space.

Theorem 5.4
.

Any non-trivial two-dimensional anti-associative dendriform algebra can be
included in one of the following classes of algebras:

Rh_{1}:\quad e_{1}\prec e_{1}=e_{2}

Rh_{2}[\lambda]:\quad e_{1}\succ e_{1}=e_{2},\ e_{1}\prec e_{1}=\lambda e_{2},\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{C}.

Proof.

Let
A
be a two dimensional anti-associative dendriform algebra. Set

\begin{split}e_{i}\succ e_{j}=&\alpha_{ij1}e_{1}+\alpha_{ij2}e_{2},\\
e_{i}\prec e_{j}=&\beta_{ij1}e_{1}+\beta_{ij2}e_{2},\quad 1\leq i,j\leq 2.\end{split}

Now we define
e_{i}\ast e_{j}=e_{i}\succ e_{j}+e_{i}\prec e_{j}
. Then by Theorem
2.7
, the algebra
(A,\ast)
is anti-associative. As given in Examples in
[
19
]
, there are only two non-isomorphic anti-associative algebras i.e. abelian algebra
A_{1}
and the algebra
A_{2}
with non-zero multiplication
e_{1}\ast e_{1}=e_{2}
.

Let the algebra
(A,\ast)
be the algebra
A_{2}
. Assume that
(A,\succ,\prec)
is a compatible
anti-associative dendriform algebra structure on
(A,\cdot)
. Set
\alpha_{ij}:=\alpha_{ij1},\ \beta_{ij}:=\alpha_{ij2},\ 1\leq i,j\leq 2
.

Then we have

\begin{cases}e_{1}\prec e_{1}=e_{2}-\alpha_{11}e_{1}-\beta_{11}e_{2},\quad e_{1}\prec e_{2}=-\alpha_{12}e_{1}-\beta_{12}e_{2},\\
e_{2}\prec e_{1}=-\alpha_{21}e_{1}-\beta_{21}e_{2},\quad e_{2}\prec e_{2}=-\alpha_{22}e_{1}-\beta_{22}e_{2}.\end{cases}

Case
\alpha_{22}\neq 0
.

By considering Eqs. (
6
) and (
7
) for the triple
\{e_{2},e_{2},e_{2}\}
we obtain
\alpha_{21}=-\beta_{22},\alpha_{12}=-\beta_{22}
, respectively.

Comparing the coefficients of the basis
e_{1}
on the considering Eqs. (
6
) and (
7
) for the triple
\{e_{1},e_{1},e_{1}\}
we obtain
\beta_{22}=0
.

Again, considering Eq. (
6
) for the triple
\{e_{1},e_{1},e_{2}\}
we get
\alpha_{22}=0
which is a contradiction. Hence, there is no such a case.

Case
\alpha_{22}=0
.
By considering Eq. (
6
) for the triples
\{e_{2},e_{2},e_{1}\},\ \{e_{2},e_{2},e_{2}\}
and Eq. (
7
) for the triple
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{2}\}
we obtain
\alpha_{21}=0,\beta_{22}=0,\alpha_{12}=0
, respectively.

Again by considering Eq. (
6
) for the triples
\{e_{1},e_{1},e_{1}\},\ \{e_{1},e_{1},e_{2}\}
and Eq. (
7
) for the triple
\{e_{2},e_{1},e_{1}\}
we obtain
\alpha_{11}=0,\beta_{12}=0,\beta_{21}=0
, respectively. Then we have the anti-associative dendriform algebra with non-zero products given by

\begin{cases}e_{1}\succ e_{1}=\gamma e_{2}\\
e_{1}\prec e_{1}=(1-\gamma)e_{2}\end{cases}

Then, if
\gamma=0
we obtain

Rh(A)_{1}:\quad e_{1}\prec e_{1}=e_{2},

if
\gamma\neq 0
by the basis change
e^{\prime}_{1}=e_{1},e_{2}^{\prime}=\gamma e_{2}
we obtain

Rh(A)_{2}[\lambda]:\quad e_{1}\succ e_{1}=e_{2},\ e_{1}\prec e_{1}=\lambda e_{2},\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{C},\ \lambda\neq-1.

Let the algebra
(A,\ast)
be abelian algebra
A_{1}
. Then by
Proposition
5.3
the anti-associative dendriform algebra
(A,\succ,\prec)
associated to the abelian anti-associative algebra is
2
-nilpotent. Thus we have

(x\succ y)\succ z=x\succ(y\succ z)=0
which implies that
(A,\succ)
is an anti-associative
2

2

Rh_{2}[-1]
.

It is possible to consider the multiplication
\prec
as above. However, it is not difficult to show that the

∎

Definition 5.5
.

The following sets are called the annihilator of anti-associative and anti-associative dendriform algebras, respectively:

{\rm Ann}_{AAs}(A)=\{x\in A~|~x\ast y=y\ast x=0,\ \forall y\in A\},

{\rm Ann}_{AAsD}(A)=\{x\in A~|~x\succ y=x\prec y=y\succ x=y\prec x=0,\ \forall y\in A\}.

An ideal
I
of an anti-associative dendriform algebra
A
is a subalgebra of the algebra
A
that satisfies the
conditions:

x\succ y,\ x\prec y,\ y\succ x,\ y\prec x\in I,\ \ \mbox{for any}\ \ x\in A,\ y\in I.

It is obvious that the annihilator of an arbitrary anti-associative dendriform algebra is an ideal.

Now we prove the following Proposition which is a useful tool to construct higher dimensional anti-associative dendriform algebras.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
(A,\ast)
be an anti-associative algebra and let
(A,\succ,\prec)
be a compatible anti-associative dendriform
algebra structure on
(A,\ast)
. If the annihilator
(\rm{Ann}_{AAs}(A),\ast)
of
(A,\ast)
is the annihilator
(\rm{Ann}_{AAsD}(A),\succ,\prec)
of
(A,\succ,\prec)
, then
the quotient
(A/\rm{Ann}_{AAsD}(A),\succ,\prec
) is a compatible anti-associative dendriform algebra structure on
(A/\rm{Ann}_{AAs}(A),\ast)
.

Proof.

The Proposition can be described as follows

\begin{CD}(A,\succ,\prec)@>{\hskip 8.19447pt\hskip 8.19447ptx\ast y=x\succ y+x\prec y\hskip 8.19447pt\hskip 8.19447pt}>{}>(A,\ast)\\
@V{\bar{x}=x+\rm{Ann}_{AAsD}(A)}V{}V@V{}V{\bar{x}=x+\rm{Ann}_{AAs}(A)}V\\
(A/\rm{Ann}_{AAsD}(A),\succ,\prec)@>{}>{\hskip 8.19447pt\hskip 8.19447pt\bar{x}\ast\bar{y}=\bar{x}\succ\bar{y}+\bar{x}\prec\bar{y}\hskip 8.19447pt\hskip 8.19447pt}>(A/\rm{Ann}_{AAs}(A),\ast)\end{CD}

To show that
(\rm{Ann}_{AAsD}(A),\succ,\prec)
is a compatible anti-associative dendriform algebra structure on
(A/\rm{Ann}_{AAs}(A),\ast)
, it is
sufficient to show
\bar{x}\ast\bar{y}=\overline{x\ast y}
. The structure is given by
\bar{x}\ast\bar{y}=\bar{x}\succ\bar{y}+\bar{x}\prec\bar{y}
. Indeed,

\begin{array}[]{lll}\bar{x}\ast\bar{y}&=&\bar{x}\succ\bar{y}+\bar{x}\prec\bar{y}\\
&=&(x+\rm{Ann}_{AAsD}(A))\succ(y+\rm{Ann}_{AAsD}(A))+(x+\rm{Ann}_{AAsD}(A))\prec(y+\rm{Ann}_{AAsD}(A))\\
&=&(x\succ y+x\prec y)+\rm{Ann}_{AAs}(A)=\overline{x\ast y}.\end{array}

∎

In the following example, we show an application of Proposition
5.6
.

Example 5.7
.

Let
(A,\ast)

is the algebra

. Then, according to Proposition

\begin{cases}e_{1}\succ e_{1}=e_{2}+\alpha_{1}e_{3},\quad e_{1}\succ e_{2}=\alpha_{2}e_{3},\quad e_{2}\succ e_{1}=\alpha_{3}e_{3},\quad e_{2}\succ e_{2}=\alpha_{4}e_{3}\\

Using Eqs (
6
)-(
8
) for the triples
\{e_{1},e_{1},e_{1}\}
and
\{e_{2},e_{1},e_{1}\}

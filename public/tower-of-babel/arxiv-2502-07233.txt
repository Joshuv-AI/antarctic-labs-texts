Abstract
. We give a description of the minimal exponent of a hypersurface using higher direct images of suitably twisted sheaves of log forms on a log resolution.

Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth, irreducible,
n
-dimensional complex algebraic variety and let
Z
be a hypersurface in
X
. The
log canonical threshold

{\rm lct}(X,Z)
is an invariant of the singularities of
Z

that plays a key role in birational geometry. This can be described in terms of a log resolution of
(X,Z)
, as follows. Suppose that
\pi\colon Y\to X
is such a log resolution that we assume (for later purposes)
to be an isomorphism over
X\smallsetminus Z
. Therefore
\pi
is proper,
Y
is smooth, and
\pi^{*}(Z)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}E_{i}
is a simple normal crossing divisor. If we also write

K_{Y/X}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}E_{i}
(where
K_{Y/X}
is the relative canonical divisor, locally defined by the determinant of the Jacobian matrix of
\pi
), then

(1)

{\rm lct}(X,Z)=\min_{i=1}^{N}\tfrac{k_{i}+1}{a_{i}}.

One can also interpret the log canonical threshold in terms of the
multiplier ideals
of
Z
(see
[
Lazarsfeld
, Chapter 9.3.B]
). Recall that for every
\lambda\in{\mathbf{Q}}_{\geq 0}
,
the multiplier ideal
\mathcal{J}(X,\lambda Z)
is defined by

\mathcal{J}(X,\lambda Z)=\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\big(K_{Y/X}-\lfloor\lambda\pi^{*}(Z)\rfloor\big).

With this definition, it is straightforward to see that

(2)

{\rm lct}(X,Z)=\min\big\{\lambda\in{\mathbf{Q}}_{\geq 0}\mid\mathcal{J}(X,\lambda Z)\neq\mathcal{O}_{X}\big\}.

Our goal in this note is to give an analogous description for a refinement of the log canonical threshold, the
minimal exponent

\widetilde{\alpha}(Z)
(whenever the ambient variety
is not clear from the context, we write
\widetilde{\alpha}(X,Z)
). This invariant was defined by Saito
in
[
Saito-B
]
using the Bernstein-Sato polynomial of a local equation of
Z
. By a result of Lichtin and Kollár (see
[
Kollar
, Section 10]
), we have

{\rm lct}(X,Z)=\min\big\{\widetilde{\alpha}(Z),1\big\}.

Furthermore, it was shown in
[
Saito-B
]
that
\widetilde{\alpha}(Z)>1
if and only if
Z
has rational singularities.
Recently, there has been interest in the minimal exponent due to the fact that it can be used to characterize higher Du Bois and higher rational singularities
(see
[
MOPW
]
,
[
Saito_et_al
]
,
[
FL
]
,
[
MP
]
).

Part of the motivation for our main results comes from the desire to have a birational description of the minimal exponent that is analogous to (
1
).
We recall that if
Z
is reduced and the log resolution
\pi
has the property that the strict transform of
Z
is smooth, then it was shown in

[
MPV
, Corollary D]
that we always have the inequality

(3)

\widetilde{\alpha}(Z)\geq\min_{i;E_{i}\,{\rm exc}}\frac{k_{i}+1}{a_{i}},

where the minimum is over all
exceptional
divisors
E_{i}
.
Unfortunately, as pointed out by Kollár, one can’t hope to have equality in (
3
) since the right-hand side
depends on the log resolution. Therefore, describing the minimal exponent in terms of the numerical data of the resolution
remains problematic.
However, an outstanding open question in this direction is whether there is always an exceptional divisor
E_{i}

such that
\widetilde{\alpha}(Z)=\frac{k_{i}+1}{a_{i}}
.

The approach that we take in this paper goes in a slightly different direction: we aim for a cohomological characterization of the minimal exponent in terms of a log resolution
which extends the characterization (
2
) of the log canonical threshold via the triviality of multiplier ideals. In fact,
the minimal exponent can be characterized in terms of the
Hodge ideals

I_{p}(\lambda Z)
, ideals introduced and studied in
[
MPQ
]
. We note that for
p=0
, these recover the multiplier ideals:
we have
I_{0}(\lambda Z)=\mathcal{J}\big((\lambda-\epsilon)Z\big)
for
0<\epsilon\ll 1
. It was shown in
[
MPV
]
that for every nonnegative integer
p
and every
\alpha\in(0,1]\cap{\mathbf{Q}}
, we have

\widetilde{\alpha}(Z)\geq p+\alpha\quad\text{if and only if}\quad I_{p}(\alpha Z)=\mathcal{O}_{X}.

The Hodge ideals admit a description via a log resolution which extends the description of multiplier ideals, see
[
MPQ
, Section 8]
. However, this description is as the derived push-forward
of a complicated complex on the log resolution and thus hard to relate to more concrete geometric information on the log resolution. Our goal in this note is to give a more explicit description of the minimal
exponent that only involves the higher direct images of twists of certain sheaves of log differentials on the resolution.

Yet another description of the minimal exponent was given by Saito in
[
Saito-MLCT
]
in terms of his
microlocal multiplier ideals
, defined using the
V
-filtration with respect to a local equation of
Z
.
These ideals have been systematically studied recently in
[
SY
]
under the name of
higher multiplier ideals
.
Our approach in this article is closer in spirit to this point of view since we use the description
of the minimal exponent in terms of the
V
-filtration.

Let us describe our main results now. Let
\pi\colon Y\to X
be a log resolution as before and let
D=\pi^{*}(Z)
and
E=D_{\rm red}
. Our goal is to describe the condition
\widetilde{\alpha}(Z)>\gamma
, for some

\gamma\in{\mathbf{Q}}_{\geq 0}
,
using the higher direct images of the sheaves of differential forms with log poles along
E
, suitably shifted. If
Z
is not reduced, then
{\rm lct}(X,Z)<1
and thus
\widetilde{\alpha}(Z)={\rm lct}(X,Z)
.
Therefore, from now on, we assume that
Z
is reduced.

We treat the cases
\gamma\in{\mathbf{Z}}
and
\gamma\not\in{\mathbf{Z}}
separately.
If
\gamma=p\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{>0}
, then it was shown in
[
MP
]
and
[
FL
]
that the condition
\widetilde{\alpha}(Z)>p
is equivalent to
Z
having
(p-1)
-rational singularities. This is a condition that can be formulated
in terms of a resolution of singularities of
Z
. The following result rephrases this condition in terms of the log resolution of
(X,Z)
. Let us denote by
Z_{\rm sing}
the singular locus of
Z
.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
Z
is a reduced hypersurface in
X
and
p\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{>0}
, then
\widetilde{\alpha}(Z)>p
if and only if
the following two conditions hold:

We have

(4)

R^{q}\pi_{*}\Omega_{Y}^{i}({\rm log}\,E)=0\quad\text{for all}\quad q\geq 1,i\leq p.

We have
{\rm codim}_{Z}(Z_{\rm sing})\geq 2p
.

In fact, the proof will show that in ii) above, it is enough to assume that
{\rm codim}_{Z}(Z_{\rm sing})\geq\min\{3,2p\}
(see Remark
3.1
). The proof of Theorem
1.1

follows easily from the results in
[
MP
]
.

Our main contribution in this paper is to treat the case when
\gamma\not\in{\mathbf{Z}}
. In this case, we write
\gamma=p+\alpha
, with
p\in{\mathbf{Z}}
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
Z
be a reduced hypersurface in
X
. For every
\alpha\in(0,1)\cap{\mathbf{Q}}
and
p\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{\geq 0}
, the following hold:

i)

If
\widetilde{\alpha}(Z)\geq p
, then

(5)

R^{q}\pi_{*}\Omega_{Y}^{n-p}({\rm log}\,E)\big(-E-\lfloor\alpha D\rfloor\big)\to R^{q}\pi_{*}\Omega_{Y}^{n-p}{(\rm log}\,E)(-E)

is an isomorphism for all
q\neq p
and it is injective for
q=p
.

ii)

If
\widetilde{\alpha}(Z)>p
, then we have

\widetilde{\alpha}(Z)>p+\alpha
if and only if the morphism (
5
) is an isomorphism for
q=p
.

Note that if
p=0
, then
\Omega_{Y}^{n-p}({\rm log}\,E)(-E)=\omega_{Y}
, so the assertion in ii) says that
{\rm lct}(X,Z)>\alpha
if and only if
\mathcal{J}(X,\alpha Z)=\mathcal{O}_{X}
.

Rewriting the criterion in Theorem
1.2
via Grothendieck duality and using the vanishing in Theorem
1.1
gives the following reformulation:

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
Z
be a reduced hypersurface in
X
. If
\alpha\in(0,1)\cap{\mathbf{Q}}
and
p\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{\geq 0}
is such that
\widetilde{\alpha}(Z)>p
, then
\widetilde{\alpha}(Z)>p+\alpha
if and only if

R^{q}\pi_{*}\Omega_{Y}^{p}({\rm log}\,E)\big(\lfloor\alpha D\rfloor\big)=0\quad\text{for all}\quad q\geq 1.

Note that if in Corollary
1.3
we take
\alpha=1-\epsilon
, for
0<\epsilon\ll 1
, then we recover the fact that if
\widetilde{\alpha}(Z)>p
, then
we have
\widetilde{\alpha}(Z)\geq p+1
if and only if the canonical morphism

\gamma_{p}\colon\Omega_{Z}^{p}\to\underline{\Omega}_{Z}^{p}
is an isomorphism, where
\underline{\Omega}_{Z}^{p}
is the
p
th Du Bois complex of
Z
, see
Remark
5.5
below. In fact, it is known that
\widetilde{\alpha}(Z)\geq p+1
if and only if
\gamma_{i}
is an isomorphism for all
i\leq p
(that is,

Z
has
p
-
Du Bois singularities
), see
[
MOPW
]
and
[
Saito_et_al
]
.

While Corollary
1.3
can be used to compute the minimal exponent in some simple cases (most notably,
for ordinary singularities), it is not well-suited for computations (even the case of ordinary singularities requires considerable work).
We view this characterization of the minimal exponent as having rather theoretical interest.
For example, as an application of Corollary
1.3
, we prove
the constancy of the minimal exponent in a proper family of hypersurfaces that admit a simultaneous log resolution
(see Theorem
6.1
). This answers a question of Radu Laza.

is to describe the condition for having

This underlies a mixed Hodge module in the sense of Saito’s theory

.
In particular, it is a left module

Let
X

We denote by
\mathcal{D}_{X}

We begin by recalling some basic facts about
\mathcal{D}

[

\mathcal{D}

\mathcal{O}_{X}

F_{p-n}B_{f}^{r}=\bigoplus_{0\leq i\leq p}(\omega_{X}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{O}_{X}\partial_{t}^{i}\delta).

Since
\partial_{t}
acts on the right on

via the action of

on

on the left, it follows from condition ii) in the definition of the

-filtration
that the action of
\theta-\alpha
on
{\rm Gr}_{\alpha}^{V}(B_{f}^{r})
is nilpotent, where

is the Euler operator

.

Remark 2.1
.

We note that one has to be careful when switching to right
\mathcal{D}
-modules in the description of nearby cycles: the right
\mathcal{D}_{X}
-module
\psi_{f,-\alpha}(\omega_{X}):=\psi_{f,\alpha}(\mathcal{O}_{X})^{r}

is canonically isomorphic to
{\rm Gr}^{V}_{-\alpha}\iota_{+}(\omega_{X})
, which carries an induced Hodge filtration

from
\iota_{+}(\omega_{X})
.
However, since we do the switch left-right on an

-dimensional variety (while for

this was done on an
(n+1)
-dimensional variety), the Hodge filtration on
\psi_{f,-\alpha}(\omega_{X})
is given by

, that is

F_{p}\psi_{f,-\alpha}(\omega_{X})=F_{p-1}{\rm Gr}^{V}_{-\alpha}\iota_{+}(\omega_{X})\quad\text{for all}\quad p\in{\mathbf{Z}}.

Remark 2.2
.

As we have already mentioned, one can define
V
-filtrations with respect to
f
for more general
\mathcal{D}_{X}
-modules
{\mathcal{M}}
(for example, for all
\mathcal{D}_{X}
-modules
underlying Hodge modules on

). This is a filtration on
\iota_{+}(\mathcal{M})
that satisfies analogous properties to those we discussed in the case

.
For details, see
[
Saito-MHP
]
. We will only need this briefly in the proof of Proposition

below.

For future reference, we state here a well-known fact about the behavior of (graded de Rham complexes of) nearby cycles
under birational morphisms.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth, irreducible, complex algebraic variety and
f\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
nonzero. If
\pi\colon Y\to X
is a proper morphism
that is an isomorphism over the complement of the hypersurface defined by
f
, with
Y
smooth, and
g=f\circ\pi
, then

(10)

{\rm Gr}^{F}_{i}{\rm DR}_{X}\psi_{f,\alpha}(\mathcal{O}_{X})\simeq{\mathbf{R}}\pi_{*}\big({\rm Gr}^{F}_{i}{\rm DR}_{Y}\psi_{g,\alpha}(\mathcal{O}_{Y})\big)

for all
i\in{\mathbf{Z}}
and all
\alpha\in(0,1]\cap{\mathbf{Q}}
.

Proof.

Indeed,
on the one hand, if we consider
\pi\times\id\colon Y\times{\mathbf{A}}^{1}\to X\times{\mathbf{A}}^{1}
, then
we have isomorphisms of filtered
\mathcal{D}
-modules

{\rm Gr}_{V}^{\alpha}{\mathcal{H}}^{j}\big((\pi\times\id)_{+}B_{g}\big)\simeq\mathcal{H}^{j}(\pi_{+}{\rm Gr}_{V}^{\alpha}B_{g})\quad\text{for all}\quad j\in{\mathbf{Z}}

(see
[
Saito-MHP
, Proposition 3.3.17]
).
Note that here, on the left-hand side, we have the
V
-filtration with respect to a
\mathcal{D}
-module which is not the structure sheaf.
On the other hand, since
\pi
is an isomorphism over the complement of the hypersurface
Z
defined by
f
, we have

\mathcal{H}^{0}\big((\pi\times\id)_{+}(B_{g})\big)\simeq B_{f}\oplus\mathcal{M}
, where
\mathcal{M}
is a filtered
\mathcal{D}_{X\times{\mathbf{A}}^{1}}
-module supported on
X\times\{0\}
, while

\mathcal{H}^{j}\big((\pi\times\id)_{+}(B_{g})\big)
is supported on
X\times\{0\}
for all
j\neq 0
. Since
\alpha>0
, we have
{\rm Gr}_{V}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{N})=0
for every

\mathcal{D}_{X\times{\mathbf{A}}^{1}}
-module
\mathcal{N}
supported on
X\times\{0\}
by
[
Saito-MHP
, Lemme 3.1.3]
. We thus conclude that

(11)

\psi_{f,\alpha}(\mathcal{O}_{X})\simeq\pi_{+}\big(\psi_{g,\alpha}(\mathcal{O}_{Y})\big).

Using the compatibility of the graded de Rham complex with push-forward for (direct summands of) Hodge modules, which is a consequence
of Saito’s Strictness theorem (see
[
Saito-MHP
, Théorème 5.3.1]
), we obtain the formula in the proposition.
∎

We next recall the definition of Saito’s minimal exponent, see
[
Saito-B
]
.
Let
Z
be a nonempty hypersurface in
X
and suppose first that
Z
is defined by some
f\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
.
Recall that the Bernstein-Sato polynomial of
f
is the monic polynomial
b_{f}(s)
of minimal degree such that

b_{f}(s)f^{s}\in\mathcal{D}_{X}[s]\cdot f^{s+1}.

It is easy to check that since
f
is not invertible, we have
b_{f}(-1)=0
. The
minimal exponent

\widetilde{\alpha}(f)
is the negative of the largest root of
b_{f}(s)/(s+1)
(with the convention that this is
\infty
if

b_{f}(s)=s+1
). By a result of Kashiwara
[
Kashiwara
]
, all roots of
b_{f}(s)
are in
{\mathbf{Q}}_{<0}
. In particular, we have

\widetilde{\alpha}(f)\in{\mathbf{Q}}_{>0}\cup\{\infty\}
.

For an arbitrary hypersurface
Z
, we choose a finite open cover
X=\bigcup_{i\in I}U_{i}
such that
Z\cap U_{i}
is defined in
U_{i}
by some

f_{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(U_{i})
. In this case, the minimal exponent of
Z
is given by

\widetilde{\alpha}(Z)=\min_{i}\widetilde{\alpha}(f_{i}),

where the minimum is over the noninvertible
f_{i}
.
One can check that this is independent of the choice of cover and defining equations. By a result of Lichtin and Kollár
(see
[
Kollar
, Section 10]
), we have
{\rm lct}(X,Z)=\min\big\{\widetilde{\alpha}(Z),1\big\}
.

Remark 2.4
.

Actually, the definition in
[
Saito-B
]
concerns the
local minimal exponent

\widetilde{\alpha}_{P}(Z)
of
Z
at a point
P\in Z
. The definition is the same as above,
but using the
local
Bernstein-Sato polynomial
b_{f,P}(s)
of a local equation
f
of
Z
at
P
. It is well-known that if
Z
is defined by
f
, then

b_{f}(s)
is the least common multiple of the
b_{f,P}(s)
, for all
P\in Z
. Moreover, given any point
P\in Z
, there is an open neighborhood
U
of
P

such that
b_{f,P}(s)=b_{f|_{U}}(s)
. This implies that
\widetilde{\alpha}(Z)=\min_{P\in Z}\widetilde{\alpha}_{P}(Z)
and for every
P\in Z
,
there is an open neighborhood
U
of
P
such that
\widetilde{\alpha}_{P}(Z)=\widetilde{\alpha}(Z\cap U)
.

We will make use of the description of
\widetilde{\alpha}(Z)
in terms of the
V
-filtration, which we now recall.
Suppose that
Z
is defined by the nonzero
f\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
. The following result is due to Saito:

Theorem 2.5
.

If
\gamma\in{\mathbf{Q}}_{>0}
and we write

\gamma=p+\alpha
, where
p\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{\geq 0}
and
\alpha\in(0,1]\cap{\mathbf{Q}}
, then the following are equivalent:

i)

\widetilde{\alpha}(f)\geq p+\alpha
.

ii)

Around every point in
X
, there is a section of
V^{\alpha}B_{f}
of the form

\partial_{t}^{p}\delta+\sum_{i=1}^{p}h_{i}\partial_{t}^{p-i}\delta
.

iii)

\partial_{t}^{p}\delta\in V^{\alpha}B_{f}
.

iv)

F_{p+1}B_{f}\subseteq V^{\alpha}B_{f}
.

Proof.

For the equivalence between i) and ii), see
[
Saito-MLCT
, (1.3.8)]
(where the condition in ii) is formulated in terms of the microlocal
V
-filtration).
The implications iv)
\Rightarrow
iii)
\Rightarrow
ii) are trivial, hence it is enough to prove, by induction on
p
, the implication ii)
\Rightarrow
iv).
The case
p=0
is clear, hence we may assume
p\geq 1
. If
u=\partial_{t}^{p}\delta+\sum_{i=1}^{p}h_{i}\partial_{t}^{p-i}\delta\in V^{\alpha}B_{f}
, then

-\tfrac{1}{p}(t-f)u=\partial_{t}^{p-1}\delta+\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}g_{i}\partial_{t}^{p-1-i}\delta\in V^{\alpha}B_{f},

for suitable
g_{1},\ldots,g_{p-1}\in\mathcal{O}_{X}
. By induction, we get
F_{p}B_{f}\subseteq V^{\alpha}B_{f}
and together with the hypothesis in ii), we conclude that
F_{p+1}B_{f}\subseteq V^{\alpha}B_{f}
.
∎

We will make use of the following two immediate consequences of the above theorem:

Corollary 2.6
.

If
\alpha\in(0,1)\cap{\mathbf{Q}}
and
p\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{\geq 0}
,
then the following hold:

i)

If
\widetilde{\alpha}(f)\geq p
, then
{\rm Gr}^{F}_{i}\psi_{f,\alpha}(\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
for all
i\leq p
.

ii)

If
\widetilde{\alpha}(f)\geq p+\alpha
, then

\widetilde{\alpha}(f)>p+\alpha
if and only if
{\rm Gr}^{F}_{p+1}\psi_{f,\alpha}(\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
.

Proof.

Let us put
F_{q}V^{\beta}B_{f}:=F_{q}B_{f}\cap V^{\beta}B_{f}
. It follows from the definition of the induced filtration
that for every
i
, we have

{\rm Gr}^{F}_{i}\psi_{f,\alpha}(\mathcal{O}_{X})=F_{i}V^{\alpha}B_{f}/(F_{i-1}V^{\alpha}B_{f}+F_{i}V^{>\alpha}B_{f}).

The assertion in i) thus follows from the fact that since
\widetilde{\alpha}(f)\geq p
, by Theorem
2.5
we have

F_{p}B_{f}\subseteq V^{1}B_{f}\subseteq V^{>\alpha}B_{f}.

Suppose now that
\widetilde{\alpha}(f)\geq p+\alpha
. Using the theorem again, we see that
F_{p+1}B_{f}\subseteq V^{\alpha}B_{f}
. Since we also have

F_{p}B_{f}\subseteq V^{>\alpha}B_{f}
, we conclude that

{\rm Gr}^{F}_{p+1}\psi_{f,\alpha}(\mathcal{O}_{X})\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}/J,

where
J=\{h\in\mathcal{O}_{X}\mid h\partial_{t}^{p}\delta\in V^{>\alpha}B_{f}\}
. The assertion in ii) follows by one more application of the theorem.
∎

It is convenient to also give a version of the above corollary in terms of the graded de Rham complex of the nearby cycles:

Corollary 2.7
.

If
\alpha\in(0,1)\cap{\mathbf{Q}}
and
p\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{\geq 0}
is such that

\widetilde{\alpha}(f)\geq p
, then the following hold:

i)

{\rm Gr}^{F}_{i-n}{\rm DR}_{X}\psi_{f,\alpha}(\mathcal{O}_{X})=0\quad\text{for}\quad i\leq p
.

ii)

\mathcal{H}^{q}\big({\rm Gr}^{F}_{p+1-n}{\rm DR}_{X}\psi_{f,\alpha}(\mathcal{O}_{X})\big)=0\quad\text{for}\quad q\neq 0
.

iii)

\mathcal{H}^{0}\big({\rm Gr}^{F}_{p+1-n}{\rm DR}_{X}\psi_{f,\alpha}(\mathcal{O}_{X})\big)\simeq\omega_{X}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}{\rm Gr}^{F}_{p+1}\psi_{f,\alpha}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
.

In particular, if
\widetilde{\alpha}(f)\geq p+\alpha
, then we have
\widetilde{\alpha}(Z)>p+\alpha
if and only if

\mathcal{H}^{0}\big({\rm Gr}^{F}_{p+1-n}{\rm DR}_{X}\psi_{f,\alpha}(\mathcal{O}_{X})\big)=0.

Proof.

It follows from the definition of the graded de Rham complex that the complex
{\rm Gr}^{F}_{i-n}{\rm DR}_{X}\psi_{f,\alpha}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
involves

{\rm Gr}^{F}_{j}{\rm Gr}_{V}^{\alpha}B_{f}
, with
i-n\leq j\leq i
. In particular, it follows from assertion i) in the previous corollary that since

\widetilde{\alpha}(f)\geq p
, all terms in the complex
{\rm Gr}^{F}_{i-n}{\rm DR}_{X}\psi_{f,\alpha}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
are
0
if
i\leq p
.
Similarly, all terms in the complex
{\rm Gr}^{F}_{p+1-n}{\rm DR}_{X}\psi_{f,\alpha}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
that lie in nonzero degrees are 0, while
the term in degree
0
is
\omega_{X}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}{\rm Gr}^{F}_{p+1}\psi_{f,\alpha}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
. This gives the assertions in i), ii), and iii).
The last assertion in the corollary follows from iii) and the assertion ii) in the previous corollary.
∎

3.
The condition
\widetilde{\alpha}(Z)>p
for

In this section, we describe the condition for having
\widetilde{\alpha}(Z)>p
, where

is a positive integer, in terms of a log resolution. As in the previous sections,
we consider a smooth, irreducible,
n
-dimensional complex algebraic variety, and a nonempty hypersurface

in
X
. Let
\pi\colon Y\to X

be a log resolution of

that is an isomorphism over

Our goal is to prove Theorem

.

Remark 3.1
.

We will see in the proof of Theorem

given below that in ii) we may only require that
{\rm codim}_{Z}(Z_{\rm sing})\geq\min\{3,2p\}

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

As we will see,

[

]
. Let

Note first that the vanishing condition in (

[
MP3
, Corollary 31.2]
.
Therefore we may assume that

is a composition of blow-ups with smooth centers lying over

and let

be the restriction of

to the strict transform of

. We write

, where

is the sum of the

that

and

On the other hand, by
[

-rational singularities:
by definition, this means that, with the above notation, the canonical morphisms

\Omega_{Z}^{i}\to{\mathbf{R}}{\pi_{Z}}_{*}\Omega^{i}_{\widetilde{Z}}({\rm log}\,F|_{\widetilde{Z}})

We next note that if

In fact, this latter inequality also holds if we assume the condition in i) and the weak version of ii) mentioned in Remark

Indeed, this is clear if

Since

, we deduce using the isomorphisms (

This is almost the condition for having
(p-1)
-rational singularities: the only missing condition is that
the canonical maps

(14)

\Omega_{Z}^{i}\to{\pi_{Z}}_{*}\Omega^{i}_{\widetilde{Z}}({\rm log}\,F|_{\widetilde{Z}})

.
We are left with showing that this condition holds as well when i) and (the weak version of) ii) are satisfied.

This is clear if

, since in this case we assume

is normal. On the other hand, if

By

, the canonical morphism

is reflexive for

.

∎

4.
A filtered resolution of
\psi_{g,\alpha}(\mathcal{O}_{Y})
, when

is an SNC divisor

In this section, we work on a smooth, irreducible,
n
-dimensional complex algebraic variety
Y
. We assume that we have a nonzero
g\in\mathcal{O}_{Y}(Y)

that defines a divisor
D
supported on a reduced simple normal crossing divisor
E
. By Generic Smoothness, after possibly replacing
Y
by an open neighborhood
of
E
, we may and will assume that
g\colon Y\to{\mathbf{A}}^{1}
is smooth over
{\mathbf{A}}^{1}\smallsetminus\{0\}
.

We consider
{\rm dlog}(g)=\tfrac{dg}{g}\in\Gamma\big(Y,\Omega^{1}_{Y}({\rm log}\,E)\big)
and let

\Omega_{Y/{\mathbf{A}}^{1}}^{1}({\rm log}\,E)
be defined by the short exact sequence

(17)

0\to\mathcal{O}_{Y}\overset{j}{\longrightarrow}\Omega_{Y}^{1}({\rm log}\,E)\to\Omega_{Y/{\mathbf{A}}^{1}}^{1}({\rm log}\,E)\to 0,

where
j
is the injective map defined by the global section
{\rm dlog}(g)
.

Note that
\Omega_{Y/{\mathbf{A}}^{1}}^{1}({\rm log}\,E)
is a locally free sheaf of rank
n-1
. Indeed,
this is clear on
Y\smallsetminus E
, since on this open subset

is invertible, so

\Omega_{Y/{\mathbf{A}}^{1}}^{1}({\rm log}\,E)
is the sheaf of relative differentials for the morphism
Y\smallsetminus E\to{\mathbf{A}}^{1}\smallsetminus\{0\}
,
which we assume to be smooth.
On the other hand,
given
P\in E
, we can find an algebraic system of coordinates
y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}
in an affine neighborhood
U
of
P
, such that

is defined by
(y_{1}\cdots y_{r})
, for some

. In this case,

, with

{\rm dlog}(g)={\rm dlog}(u)+\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}{\rm dlog}(y_{i}).

It is then clear that
\Omega_{Y/{\mathbf{A}}^{1}}^{1}({\rm log}\,E)
is free in a suitable neighborhood of
P
, generated by the classes of
{\rm dlog}(y_{j})
, for
2\leq j\leq r
, and

, for

.

We put
\Omega_{Y/{\mathbf{A}}^{1}}^{p}({\rm log}\,E):=\wedge^{p}\Omega_{Y/{\mathbf{A}}^{1}}^{1}({\rm log}\,E)

.
Note that the short exact sequence (

, with the injective map being given by

Let us consider the log de Rham complex of the left
\mathcal{D}_{Y}
-module

with respect to

Recall that if

are local coordinates on

is given by

\nabla(\eta\otimes P)=d\eta\otimes P+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(dy_{i}\wedge\eta)\otimes\partial_{y_{i}}P.

Note that each term in the complex has a natural structure of right

-module induced by the right

-module structure of

and that the maps

-linear.
Therefore, this is a complex of right

-modules.

If

is an effective divisor supported on

Our description of

thus implies that
\overline{\sigma_{\alpha}}
gives an isomorphism

\mathcal{H}^{0}\big({\rm Gr}^{F}_{p}\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{D_{\alpha}}\big)\simeq{\rm Gr}^{F}_{p-1}V_{-\alpha}B_{g}^{r}.

In order to complete the proof of i), we still need to show that in the case of an arbitrary
Y
, the assertion also holds on
Y\smallsetminus E
.
We may thus assume that
E=0
, that is,
g
is invertible. By our running assumption, in this case

is a smooth morphism.
Passing through the analytic setting, as before, we reduce to the case when
Y={\mathbf{A}}^{n-1}\times\big({\mathbf{A}}^{1}\smallsetminus\{0\}\big)
and
g\colon Y\to{\mathbf{A}}^{1}

is the projection onto the last component. Note that if we denote the coordinates on
Y
by
y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}
, then

\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{D_{\alpha}}=\Omega_{Y/{\mathbf{A}}^{1}}^{\bullet}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\mathcal{D}_{Y}=\big(\Omega_{{\mathbf{A}}^{n-1}}^{\bullet}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbf{A}}^{n-1}}}\mathcal{D}_{{\mathbf{A}}^{n-1}}\big)[y_{n},y_{n}^{-1},\partial_{y_{n}}].

It is now clear that this is quasi-isomorphic to
\omega_{{\mathbf{A}}^{n-1}}[y_{n},y_{n}^{-1},\partial_{y_{n}}]
, which is (filtered) isomorphic via
\sigma_{\alpha}
to
\big(B_{g}^{r},F_{\bullet}[-1]\big)

(note that in this case we have
V_{-\alpha}B_{g}^{r}=B_{g}^{r}
).
This completes the proof of i).

Since we have a short exact sequence of complexes

0\to{\rm Gr}^{F}_{\bullet}(\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{D_{>\alpha}})\to{\rm Gr}^{F}_{\bullet}(\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{D_{\alpha}})\to{\rm Gr}^{F}_{\bullet}(\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{D_{\alpha}}/\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{D_{>\alpha}})\to 0,

by taking the long exact sequence in cohomology and applying i) for
\alpha
and
\alpha+\epsilon
(with
0<\epsilon\ll 1
), we obtain the assertion in ii).

We now prove the last assertion. Again, there are two cases to consider: when we are at a point in
E
and when we are on
Y\smallsetminus E
. We treat the former case and leave the latter one
as an exercise for the reader. For the case of a point in
E
,
arguing as before, we see that
it is enough to treat the case when
Y={\mathbf{A}}^{n}
and
g=y_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots y_{r}^{a_{r}}
. Note first that by what we have already proved,
\mathcal{H}^{0}(\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{D_{\alpha}})
is generated over
\mathcal{D}_{Y}
by the class
\overline{u}
of the section

u=\big(s_{\alpha}a_{2}{\rm dlog}(y_{2})\wedge\ldots\wedge a_{r}{\rm dlog}(y_{r})\wedge dy_{r+1}\wedge\ldots\wedge dy_{n}\big)\otimes 1

of

. Indeed, we know that
\overline{\sigma_{\alpha}}
gives an isomorphism

\mathcal{H}^{0}(\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{D_{\alpha}})\simeq V_{-\alpha}B^{r}_{g}
and
\sigma_{\alpha}(u)=s_{\alpha}\tfrac{a_{1}\cdots\alpha_{r}}{y_{1}\cdots y_{r}}dy\delta

by (

over

\mathcal{D}_{Y}
by (
29
).

Since both maps in iii) are
\mathcal{D}_{Y}
-linear, we see that it is enough to show that they take the same value on
\overline{u}
. In order to compute
N_{D_{\alpha}}(\overline{u})
, we apply the differential of
{\mathcal{C}}_{D_{\alpha}}
to

to get
s_{\alpha}\eta\cdot\big(\tfrac{\lceil\alpha a_{1}\rceil}{a_{1}}+\tfrac{1}{a_{1}}y_{1}\partial_{y_{1}}\big)

Since

we conclude that

On the other hand, the isomorphism induced by

5.
The description of the minimal exponent

Suppose that

We fix a log resolution

of

that is an isomorphism over

We put

and for every

Let

.
Since both sides of the formula in the theorem are supported on

X
by an open neighborhood of

to assume that

{\mathbf{A}}^{1}\smallsetminus\{0\}

4.1
for

and

With the notation in Remark

,
note that for every

For every

Example 5.4
.

If
p=0
, then we automatically have
\widetilde{\alpha}(Z)>0
. In this case, the condition

We deduce the dual version of Theorem
1.2

Proof of Corollary
1.3
.

Feasibility and Safety of Spinal Anaesthesia for Paediatric Herniotomy in a Resource-Limited Outreach Setting in Abia State, Southeast Nigeria.
Authors: Ikenna Stanley Ilo, Doris Ifeyinwa Ilo, Isaac Sunday Chukwu, Brendan Ejiofor Madu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Subarachnoid block (spinal anaesthesia) is a safe, cost-effective option for paediatric surgery, especially in resource-limited settings. In rural Nigeria, where surgical outreach programs provide vital care, this technique offers rapid onset, reliable analgesia, minimal airway manipulation, and fewer postoperative complications.Methodology:This prospective observational study was conducted over six months (September 2025–March 2026) during surgical outreach programs in Ebem Ohaofia, Abia State, and at Madonna Catholic Hospital and Isiala Ngwa North Hospital. Sixty-one consecutive paediatrics patients aged 2–8 years undergoing herniotomy under subarachnoid block were enrolled. Relevant perioperative data, including block characteristics, intraoperative hemodynamic parameters, and complications, were collected and analysed.Results:A total of 61 children aged 2–8 years underwent herniotomy under subarachnoid block (SAB). There was a male predominance (50 males, 11 females; ratio 4.5:1). No cases of failed block or conversion to general anaesthesia were recorded. Intraoperatively, no patient developed bradycardia, while hypotension occurred in one patient (1.6%), who was aged 8 years. Perioperative shivering was observed in five patients (8.2%) and was treated with pentazocine (0.25 mg/kg). The most common postoperative complaint was pain, reported in 16.4% (10) of patients.Conclusions:Subarachnoid block appears to be a practical anaesthetic technique for paediatric patients in surgical outreach programs in rural Abia State. Its advantages, including rapid onset, minimal equipment requirements, and favourable recovery profile, make it particularly suitable for resource-limited settings.

Introduction

Ambulatory surgery refers to a model of care in which patient admission, anaesthetic evaluation, surgical procedure, recovery, and discharge all occur within the same day. This approach offers several advantages, including reduced healthcare costs, minimal separation of patients from their families and social environment, and improved bed availability in healthcare facilities. Recent literature reports that ambulatory procedures now account for up to 60% of surgical workload in high-volume canters, reflecting the global expansion of day-case surgery practice. 1,2

Rural surgical outreach programs present unique challenges due to limited infrastructure, inadequate equipment, and resource constraints. Delivering safe anaesthesia in such settings requires techniques that are effective, reliable, and minimally dependent on sophisticated equipment. Studies from Nigeria have emphasized that anaesthetic safety in rural outreach depends on structured preoperative assessment, appropriate case selection, and the use of techniques that promote rapid recovery and early discharge.3,4 Careful preoperative evaluation and the use of anaesthetic techniques with rapid onset and minimal postoperative complications remain essential to facilitate ambulatory discharge in these environments.4,5

Paediatric spinal anaesthesia has emerged as an alternative to general anaesthesia, particularly for lower abdominal and lower limb surgeries in children. Recent studies have demonstrated its increasing safety profile, with advantages including reduced airway manipulation, stable intraoperative physiology, and rapid postoperative recovery.6 It is especially beneficial in low-resource settings, as it does not require expensive equipment and can be performed in peripheral healthcare centers where general anaesthesia infrastructure may be limited.5 Despite these benefits, its acceptance and utilization in paediatric practice remain poor, largely due to provider familiarity, technical challenges in younger children.7,1

This study therefore aims to evaluate the feasibility and safety of subarachnoid block in paediatric patients undergoing herniotomy in rural outreach programs in Abia State.

Methods

This was a prospective observational study conducted among paediatric patients undergoing surgical procedures during a surgical outreach programs in three different rural communities of Abia State, Nigeria, between September 2025 and March 2026. Anaesthetic evaluation was performed on the same day as surgical screening prior to the procedure.

Study population

The study included paediatric patients classified as American Society of Anaesthesiologists (ASA) physical status I and II, who were scheduled for herniotomy under subarachnoid block during the outreach program. All patients were accompanied by a responsible adult guardian who provided informed consent. The sample size was the number that met the inclusion criteria. A total of 61 children had SAB for herniotomies in the three rural outreaches.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Eligible participants with ASA I and II and aged between 2-8yrs. Patients were excluded if they had: an active upper respiratory tract infection, fever, diarrhoea. Any contraindication to spinal anaesthesia as determined during preoperative evaluation.

PREOPERATIVE ASSESSMENT

A preoperative anaesthetic assessment and outreach education were conducted for the patients in the morning before commencement of the program for the day. This includes a review of clinical history, fasting status, and baseline vital signs. The surgeries at the different outreach centres were conducted by paediatric surgeon deployed for the program. Anaesthesia was administered by a consultant Anaesthetist who has an interest in paediatric anaesthesia. Intravenous access was established prior to the procedure.

ANAESTHETIC TECHNIQUE

In the operating area, standard monitors were attached, and baseline vital signs were recorded. Patients were positioned and supported in the sitting position for the procedure.

Under aseptic conditions, lumbar puncture was performed in the lumbar interspace below the intercristal (Tuffier's) line, typically at the L3–L4 or L4–L5 interspace, using a short 27-gauge spinal needle. Subarachnoid block was achieved using hyperbaric bupivacaine 0.5% at a dose of 0.4mg/kg. Motor block was checked using the Bromage score. Surgery commenced once the Bromage score was 4.

All the patients were given intravenous diazepam 0.04mg/kg for mild sedation after the block.

INTRAOPERATIVE MONITORING AND MANAGEMENT

Intraoperative monitoring included continuous observation of blood pressure, heart rate, and oxygen saturation, with blood pressure and sedation score using Ramsay sedation score recorded every 2.5 minutes for the first 10 minutes following spinal injection and subsequently every 5 minutes until the end of surgery.

Hypotension was defined as a reduction in systolic blood pressure greater than 20% from baseline. Any occurrence of hypotension was managed with fluid administration and adjunct medications where necessary. Shivering was managed with a low dose of pentazocine.

POSTOPERATIVE CARE

At the conclusion of surgery, patients were transferred to the recovery room for monitoring until full motor function returned. Vital signs were continuously monitored during recovery.

Patients were discharged when clinically stable with adequate recovery of motor function, as these are day-care procedures. Postoperative analgesia was provided using oral ibuprofen syrup. Relatives were asked to report back to the health facilities if a headache occurs within 5 days of the surgery.

Data were collected prospectively using a structured data collection proforma designed at three key stages: preoperative, intraoperative and postoperative periods. The preoperative data included baseline demographic and clinical variables, including age, sex, weight, height, and American Society of Anaesthesiologists (ASA) physical status. Details of the anaesthetic technique and intraoperative course haemodynamic parameters, and occurrence of intraoperative complications. Postoperative recovery and outcomes were assessed in the recovery area and after discharge.

STATISTICAL ANALYSIS

Data were entered and analysed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Continuous variables were assessed for normality using visual inspection of histograms and expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Categorical variables were summarized as frequencies and percentages. A p-value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant where applicable.

Intraoperative haemodynamic parameters (mean arterial pressure and pulse rate) were analysed descriptively across time intervals. Changes over time were presented graphically.

ETHICAL CONSIDERATION

This study involved human participants and was approved by the Health Research Ethics Committee of the Federal Medical Centre, Umuahia, Abia State, Nigeria, with the number: FMC/QEH/G.596/Vol. 10/1002. Written consent was obtained from the parents.

RESULTS

A total of 61 paediatric patients underwent surgical procedures under spinal anaesthesia. There was a male predominance (82.0%), giving a male-to-female ratio of 4.5:1. The mean age was 4.1 ± 0.8 years, with a mean weight of 12.6 ± 1.1 kg and a mean height of 90.4 ± 6.3 cm. The majority of patients were classified as ASA I, 59 patients (96.7%). The mean duration of surgery was 23 ± 7 minutes, and the mean onset time of spinal block was 2.1 ± 0.4 minutes.

Subarachnoid block was successfully achieved in all patients (100%) with no cases of failed block or conversion to general anaesthesia. Intraoperatively, haemodynamic parameters remained within acceptable limits (Figure 1, 2). Hypotension occurred in 1 patient (1.6%), while transient tachycardia was observed in 5 patients (8.2%) (Table 1). Shivering was noted in 5 patients (8.2%) (Table 1). Spinal anaesthesia was successful in all patients, with no requirement for conversion to general anaesthesia. Sedation levels assessed using Ramsay score remained low and comparable across patients, including those who received pentazocine for shivering (Figure 3).

Discussion

This study evaluated the use of subarachnoid block (spinal anaesthesia) in paediatric patients undergoing surgical procedures during outreach programs in rural Abia State. The findings demonstrate that subarachnoid block can be a practical, anaesthetic technique in resource-limited settings, particularly where access to advanced airway equipment and inhalational anaesthetic agents may be constrained.8 Weight for age was noted to be low. Recent study confirm that stunting and underweight remain highly prevalent among children under five, especially in rural areas like Abia State, where food insecurity drives low weight-for-age scores.9

The high success rate observed in this study aligns with existing literature that supports the reliability of spinal anaesthesia in paediatric populations, especially for lower abdominal and lower limb procedures. In outreach settings, where patient turnover is often high and time efficiency is critical, the rapid onset and predictable depth of anaesthesia associated with subarachnoid block gives significant advantage. 10 Additionally, the avoidance of airway manipulation reduces the risk of perioperative respiratory complications, which is particularly relevant in environments lacking full resuscitative infrastructure.8,2

Another important consideration is the cost-effectiveness of spinal anaesthesia. Outreach programs typically operate under limited funding, and the reduced need for expensive anaesthetic agents, airway devices, and prolonged postoperative monitoring makes subarachnoid block an attractive option. This is especially pertinent in rural Nigerian settings, where healthcare resources are unevenly distributed and access to tertiary care facilities is limited.12

The hemodynamic stability observed among the paediatric patients in this study further supports the suitability of this technique. Although concerns have historically been raised regarding hypotension and bradycardia following spinal anaesthesia, particularly in adults, these effects tend to be less pronounced in children due to their physiological resilience and differences in autonomic regulation. 13 Our findings reinforce this observation, as clinically significant hemodynamic disturbances were minimal and easily managed.

Despite these advantages, the implementation of subarachnoid block in paediatric outreach programs is not without challenges. Technical difficulty, particularly in younger children, and the need for skilled anaesthesia providers remain important considerations. Furthermore, careful patient selection and adherence to dosing guidelines are essential to minimize the risk of complications.10,14

The demographic profile in this study showed a marked male predominance (82.0%), which is consistent with previous Nigerian outreach-based surgical studies where male children are more frequently presented for conditions such as inguinal hernia and hydrocele repair15,2 The mean age of 4.1 years aligns with reports by Olateju and coworkers, 17 who documented similar age distributions in paediatric regional anaesthesia studies in southwestern Nigeria. The predominance of ASA I patients (96.7%) reflects appropriate patient selection for outreach settings, where relatively healthy children are prioritized due to limited perioperative support infrastructure.

The mean onset time of spinal block (2.1 ± 0.4 minutes) observed in this study is comparable to findings by Eze, who reported rapid onset times in paediatric spinal anaesthesia, highlighting its advantage in high-turnover outreach programs.18 Rapid onset contributes to reduced anaesthetic time and improved efficiency, which is critical in resource-constrained environments.

Haemodynamic stability was well maintained intraoperatively, with only one case of hypotension (1.6%) and a small proportion of tachycardia (8.2%). These findings are consistent with other studies that have demonstrated minimal cardiovascular perturbations in children undergoing spinal anaesthesia,19,2,21 likely due to their relatively immature sympathetic nervous system. The low incidence of hypotension contrasts with adult populations, where sympathetic blockade often produces more pronounced effects.

Shivering was observed in 8.2% of patients, which is within the range reported in paediatric regional anaesthesia literature. 22 While not life-threatening, shivering may increase metabolic demand and discomfort, underscoring the importance of temperature monitoring and warming strategies, especially in outreach settings where environmental control may be suboptimal.

The 100% success rate of spinal anaesthesia without conversion to general anaesthesia is noteworthy and comparable to findings, who also reported high success rate.23 High success rate was also observed in caudal block in paediatric age group. 24 This may be attributed to careful patient selection, operator experience, and the relatively short duration of procedures performed. The absence of failed blocks further supports the feasibility of spinal anaesthesia as a primary technique in similar settings.

Postoperative pain prior to discharge was reported in 16.4% of patients, which is slightly higher than some reports17,2 Pain are generally manageable and may not necessitate major interventions. Importantly, no cases of post-dural puncture headache or backache were reported, corroborating findings from multiple paediatric studies that such complications are rare in children due to anatomical and physiological differences.18,20 Sedation with diazepam was use to keep the children calm, score because very minimum dose was used.

Overall, this study highlights the safety, efficacy, and practicality of subarachnoid block in paediatric surgical outreach programs in rural Nigeria. The technique offers significant advantages, including rapid onset, haemodynamic stability, and minimal complications, making it particularly suitable for low-resource environments. However, continued emphasis on training, monitoring, and standardized analgesic protocols is essential to further optimize outcomes.

Conclusion

Subarachnoid block represents a viable and beneficial anaesthetic option for paediatric surgical outreach programs in rural Abia State. Strengthening capacity building, improving perioperative monitoring infrastructure, and standardizing practice guidelines will further enhance its safety profile and expand its utility in similar low-resource settings.

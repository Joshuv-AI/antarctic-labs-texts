High TRA2B expression mediated by ncRNA correlates with adverse outcomes and tumor immune infiltration in hepatocellular carcinoma
Authors: Yanjun Li, Yefan Zhang, Qijun Li, Yuyang Gu, Yufen Xu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractHepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is one of the most frequent diagnosed malignancies globally with dismalprognosis and high mortality. Transformer 2 beta homolog (TRA2B), known as serine/arginine-rich splicing factor 10 (SRSF10), is a member of the serine/arginine (SR) protein superfamily that modulates gene expression by regulating mRNA splicing. The significance of TRA2B in cancer has received considerable attention in recent years, however, its specific role and molecular mechanisms in HCC remain unknown. This study aimed to analyze the expression of TRA2B in LIHC with the support of integrative bioinformatic analysis. The study conducted Kaplan-Meier and cox regression analyses and showed that the upregulated TRA2B was notably correlated with the dismal prognosis in HCC. TRA2B was identified as a potential proto-oncogene in HCC associated with poor prognosis. TRA2B expression in tumor tissues was found to be elevated using bioinformatics analysis. Following that, non-coding RNAs (ncRNA) related to TRA2B were extracted using series analysis. The CRNDE/LINC00511-miR-29c-3p axis was characterized as a potential upstream ncRNA-correlated signaling of TRA2B in HCC. Subsequently, the role of TRA2B in the tumor immune microenvironment (TIME) was investigated. In summary, this research provides novel insight into the potential role of TRA2B across various cancers and indicated that ncRNA-mediated overexpression of TRA2B is associated with the dismal prognosis in HCC.Keywords: TRA2B expression, Hepatocellular carcinoma, Clinical prognosis, Tumor immune infiltration

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) ranks as the sixth most prevalent cancer and the fourth leading cause of tumor-related mortality worldwide [1]. It is an inflammation-associated cancer related to chronic infection with hepatitis B and C [2, 3].The pathogenesis of HCC is driven by a complex interplay of molecular alterations within the tumor microenvironment. For instance, factors such as androgen receptor signaling have been implicated in creating a permissive environment for HCC development, particularly in men, highlighting the role of specific molecular pathways in disease progression [4]. At another level, the deregulation of non-coding RNAs and their interactions through competitive endogenous RNA (ceRNA) networks has emerged as a fundamental mechanism in HCC. This is exemplified by studies showing how circular RNAs (circRNAs) can promote HCC progression by sponging miRNAs [5]. Generally, HCC have a low mutational burden as well as few samples with microsatellite instability-high (MSI-H) status [6–9]. Despite significant advances in cancer immunotherapy over the past decade, many HCC patients do not benefit from the treatment. This underscores the persistent and critical need for the discovery of novel prognostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets. Indeed, recent efforts continue to highlight this need, such as the development of radiological coefficients to predict survival, demonstrating the ongoing search for better prognostic tools [10].

TRA2B, commonly known as serine/arginine-rich splicing factor 10 (SRSF10), is a member of the serine/arginine (SR) family and shares significant similarities with the other members of the family [11]. TRA2B has been found to be associated with various pathologies, including obesity, Alzheimer’s disease and so on [12, 13]. Recent studies have revealed that TRA2B plays an essential role in the pathogenesis of various cancers, including endometrial carcinoma, lung cancer, colon cancer, glioma, ovarian cancer and breast cancer [14–21]. Nonetheless, the specific function and regulation underlying molecular mechanisms of TRA2B in HCC remain unknown.

In this study, a high level of TRA2B expression has been found in HCC and is associated with a poor prognosis. Additionally, high TRA2B expression associated with aggressive clinicopathological characteristics, which lead to an increased metastatic potential as well as deteriorated prognoses. These findings suggested that TRA2B may act as a proto-oncogene in HCC. In addition, through bioinformatics ENCORI, we have previously identified TRA2B as a potential target gene of CRNDE/LINC00511-miR-29c-3p loop in HCC cells. Moreover, the relationship between the expression level of TRA2B and immune infiltration, markers of immune cells, and immune checkpoints in HCC was assessed. In conclusion, for the first time, this study explored the potential ncRNA-mediated regulatory mechanism of TRA2B in HCC, which may be linked to adverse outcomes and tumor infiltration.

Materials and methods

Expression pattern and survival analysis

We compiled the Table 1 to show the databases in this research more clearly. From TCGA, raw RNA sequence data and associated clinical data of HCC samples were downloaded. Based on mean TRA2B expression, samples were divided into two groups. Non-paired and paired t-tests were used to compare TRA2B expression in the tumor and adjacent normal tissues. Afterwards, among the two groups, Kaplan-Meier curves were used for comparing overall survival (OS), progression-free interval (PFI), and disease-specific survival (DSS). In addition, univariate and multivariate Cox analysis were performed to screen independent prognostic factors in HCC.

Association between TRA2B and clinicopathological characteristics

The association between TRA2B expression and clinical characteristics was also investigated. Independent-sample T-test was used to analysis the correlation between TRA2B expression and clinicopathologic characteristics (including age, gender, BMI, pathologic stage, histological stage, Tumor status and AFP level). Analyses and plots were created using R and ggplot. A P value < 0.05 was regarded as significant. Analysis were conducted using R packages such as tidyverse, ggplot2, reshape2, car, and rstatix. P < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Gene set enrichment analysis

RNA-seq data collected from the TCGA database were analyzed using GSEA to preliminarily classify the genes on the basis of their correlation with TRA2B expression. By GSEA, we carried out KEGG enrichment analysis to explore to underlying biological roles of TRA2B. To be statistically significant, enrichment results had to meet two following conditions: a false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.050 and a nominal p-value < 0.050.

Competing endogenous RNAs network construction

The online comprehensive database starBase is the optimal tool for depicting protein-RNA interaction [22]. It was utilized to predict potential binding miRNAs by seven prediction programs, which including miRmap, PicTar, TargetScan and so on. MiRNAs only satisfied with three or more of the aforementioned criteria were retained for further analysis. Subsequently, the potential lncRNAs binding to hsa-miR-29c-3p were extracted by the same process. StarBase was also used to measure the interactions between lncRNAs, miRNAs and target gene. In addtion, the Kaplan-Meier plotter was used to conduct the survival analysis of hsa-miR-29c-3p as well as GEPIA was used to estimate the prognostic value of candidate lncRNAs in HCC.

Correlation between TRA2B and immune infiltration

TIMER is a public website aimed to analyze the abundance of tumor-infiltrating immune cells (TIICs) across various cancers [23]. The correlation analyses are based on partial correlation, which automatically adjusts for tumor purity by default. This statistical control ensures that the observed associations between gene expression and immune cell abundance are independent of the proportion of tumor cells in the sample, thereby enhancing the validity of the results. It was utilized to investigate the association between TRA2B expression and immune cell infiltration as well as the expression level of immune checkpoints.

GEPIA portal is a commonly used interactive website for further analyzing the tumor samples from the GTEx and TCGA projects [24]. It was applied to investigate the relation between TRA2B and immune checkpoints. Simultaneous satisfaction of |R| greater than 0.1 and P < 0.05 was deemed statistically significant.

The expression analysis of TRA2B expression in HCC

To assess the status of TRA2B expression in HCC patients, we compared TRA2B expression level in tumors to normal tissues. The results demonstrated that TRA2B.

gene expression level was significantly higher in non-paired tumor tissues than in normal tissues (P = 7.1e-16, Fig. 1A). Similarly, in paired tumors, HCC tissues showed higher expression of TRA2B than normal tissues (P = 6.3e-07, Fig. 1B).

High TRA2B expression related to poor survival outcome in HCC

According to the expression level of TRA2B, samples were divided into high-expression group and low-expression group. We found that the highly expressed TRA2B was correlated with short OS [Hazard ratio (HR) = 1.49, P = 0.024], PFI(HR = 1.34, P = 0.047), DSS(HR = 1.58, P = 0.045)(Fig. 1C-E). In addition, the univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were performed to explore the prognostic value of clinicopathological parameters, particularly the TRA2B expression. In univariate analysis, T3&T4 stage (HR = 2.598, 95% CI = 1.826–3.697, P < 0.001), M1 stage (HR = 4.077, 95% CI = 1.281–12.973, P = 0.017), TRA2B expression (HR = 1.597, 95% CI = 1.160–2.199, P = 0.004), StageIII&StageIV (HR = 2.504, 95% CI = 1.727–3.631, P < 0.001) and Tumor status is with Tumor (HR = 2.317, 95% CI = 1.590–3.376, P< 0.001) were associated with OS. In multivariate analysis, only TRA2B expression (HR = 1.689, 95% CI = 1.116–2.556, P= 0.013) and Tumor status is with Tumor (HR = 1.892, 95% CI = 1.185–3.022, P= 0.008) were independent prognostic factors for HCC (Table 2).

Correlation of TRA2B expression levels with clinicopathological characteristics in HCC

The TRA2B expression was correlated with the clinicopathological features of patients with HCC to reveal the role of TRA2B in HCC progression. TRA2B expression level was significantly linked to AFP (P = 3.6e-06, Fig. 2A), age (P = 0.03, Fig. 2B), histologic grade (P = 5.5e-05, Fig. 2F), pathologic stages (P = 5.2e-03, Fig. 2G) and T stage (P = 9.9e-03, Fig. 2H). However, no significant correlation was observed between TRA2B expression and other clinicopathological features such as albumin(P = 0.4, Fig. 2C), BMI(P = 0.1, Fig. 2D), gender (P = 0.23, Fig. 2E) or prothrombin time(P = 0.56, Fig. 2I) .

Gene set enrichment analysis of TRA2B

In order to identify the TRA2B-related signaling pathways involved in HCC, we carried out GSEA analysis. The ressults showed that TRA2B was significantly related with cancer pathway, such as Pathways in cancer [normalized enrichment score (NES) = 1.830, adjusted P-value = 0.020, FDR = 0.012], Neuroactive Ligand-Receptor interaction (NES = 2.127, adjusted P-value = 0.020, FDR = 0.012), regulation of actin cytoskeleton(NES = 1.722, adjusted P-value = 0.020, FDR = 0.012), and focal adhesion(NES = 1.820, adjusted P-value = 0.020, FDR = 0.012). Notably, high TRA2B expression was significantly enriched in gene sets such as Cytokine-Cytokine Receptor Interaction (NES = 1.857, adjusted P-value = 0.020, FDR = 0.012), MAPK signaling pathway (NES = 1.645, adjusted P-value = 0.020, FDR = 0.012), WNT signaling pathway(NES = 1.945, adjusted P-value = 0.020, FDR = 0.012) and JAK-STAT Signaling Pathway (NES = 1.531, adjusted P-value = 0.048, FDR = 0.030). This suggests that TRA2B may be involved in regulating the expression of key molecules that govern immune cell communication and activation within the TIME.(Fig. 3A-I).

Screening and analysis of upstream MiRNAs of TRA2B in HCC

Benefiting from the technological advances in sequencing, a growing number of non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs) are being identified. A proportion of these is found as crucial regulators of gene expression. To define the regulated network of TRA2B, the potential upstream binding miRNAs of TRA2B were first predicted. Cytoscape was used to visualize TRA2B-miRNA regulate network (Fig. 4A). Only ten miRNAs met the screening criteria, with hsa-miR-29c-3p being significantly negatively correlated with TRA2B expression (R=-0.314, P = 6.98E-10, Fig. 4B). Furthermore, the expression of miR-29c-3p was downregulated in tumor tissues, while its upregulation(P < 0.05, Fig. 4C) was linked with better outcome in HCC(HR = 0.69, logrank P = 0.041, Fig. 4D). These findings suggested a potential regulatory relationship where miR-29c-3p might be the most potential upstream miRNA of TRA2B in HCC.

Further exploration of the upstream lncRNAs of hsa-miR-29c-3p

To further investigate the potential upstream binding lncRNAs of hsa-miR-29c-3p, the starBase database was used, and a total of 52 candidate lncRNAs were screened out. Among all the lncRNAs, the expression of CRNDE(P < 0.05, Fig. 5A), LINC00511(P < 0.05, Fig. 5B), and PVT1(P < 0.05, Fig. 5D) were increased in HCC tissues, while NEAT1 was highly expressed in normal liver tissue(P < 0.05, Fig. 5C). Further investigation revealed that HCC patients with CRNDE(HR = 1.5, Logrank P = 0.018, Fig. 5E) or LINC00511(HR = 1.7, Logrank P = 0.0032, Fig. 5F) overexpression had a dismal prognosis. In contrast, NEAT1(HR = 0.4, Logrank P = 0.39, Fig. 5G) or PTV1(HR = 1.2, Logrank P = 0.4, Fig. 5H) expression had no significant impact on outcome. The competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) hypothesis indicated that lncRNA could upregulate mRNA expression by competitively binding miRNA. Hence, to explore the potential ceRNA network, we further analyzed the expression correlations among these four lncRNAs, miR-29c-3p, and TRA2B using starBase in Table 3. Based on the differential expression analysis, survival analysis, and correlation analysis, CRNDE and LINC00511 may be the two most potential upstream lncRNAs of the miR-29c-3p/TRA2B axis in HCC.

Relationship between TRA2B with immune checkpoints in HCC

CD274, PDCD1, CTLA-4, and TIGIT have been demonstrated as critical immune checkpoints(ICIs) involved in the maintenance of immune homeostasis in HCC. The relationships between TRA2B with ICIs were investigated. TRA2B expression is positively associated with CD274 (partial correlation = 0.573,P = 4.08E-27, Fig. 6A), PDCD1 (partial correlation = 0.309,P = 4.82E-09,Fig. 6C), CTLA-4 (partial correlation = 0.275,P = 2.16E-07,Fig. 6E), and TIGIT (partial correlation = 0.377, P = 4.10E-13, Fig. 6G), which were regulated by purity. A general consistency between these results and GEPIA is evident. The analysis of the GEPIA database indicated a positive correlation between TRA2B with CD274(R = 0.32,P = 3.8E-15,Fig. 6B), PDCD1(R = 0.17,P = 0.0012,Fig. 6D), CTLA-4(R = 0.27,P = 1.1E-07,Fig. 6F), and TIGIT(R = 0.32,P = 3.1E-10,Fig. 6H). These findings may suggest that TRA2B has the potential to be a novel immunotherapeutic target that should be further investigated in combination with existing ICIs (Table 3).

Discussion

Despite significant advances in HCC treatment in recent years [25], HCC remains one of the most fatal malignancies [26]. The prognosis is particularly grave for patients with advanced disease, such as those with vascular invasion. A recent systematic review by Wang et al. underscored the complexity and poor outcomes associated with treating HCC patients with tumor thrombus in major vessels, reflecting the urgent need for better therapeutic strategies and stratification tools [27].

Finding promising therapeutic targets is critical as targeted therapeutics become the standard treatment of HCC [28]. Recently, the roles of TRA2B in various cancers have attracted increased attention [16–21]. However, as per our understanding, no research has been reported demonstrating the significance of TRA2B in HCC. In this study, we discovered that TRA2B was significantly expressed in HCC tissues and linked to a poor prognosis for HCC. There was a positive correlation between high TRA2B expression and multiple clinical features in HCC patients, including AFP, T stage, histological grade, and pathological stage. Thus, TRA2B has the potential to be a crucial biomarker in medical practice. Our work on TRA2B aligns with recent efforts to identify and validate non-traditional biomarkers, such as the tumor-associated lymphatic vessel density recently highlighted in a systematic review by Li et al., which also demonstrated significant prognostic value in hepatobiliary cancers [29]. The consistent emergence of these factors underscores the complexity of HCC prognosis and the need for multi-faceted biomarker panels.

Even though non-coding RNAs (ncRNA) have been already discovered for decades, it is only in the last decade that we have begun to understand the crucial role of ncRNA in cancer [30]. The ncRNA family consists mainly of miRNA, LncRNA, and circRNA [31]. The starbase database was used to explore the potential upstream regulation mechanism of TRA2B in HCC. The first step was to identify the upstream binding miRNAs of TRA2B. Seven target prediction algorithms were applied to predict candidate miRNAs, including PITA, miRmap, TargetScan, and others. A total of 10 miRNAs were finally extracted as predicted binding RNAs. Xiao et al. have proved that miR-29a-3p acts as a tumor suppressor gene and inhibits the proliferation and migration of HCC [32], while another research found that miR-29a-3p could suppress HCC by downregulating IGF1R [33]. MiR-29b-3p knockdown can enhance tumor invasion and metastasis [34]. MiR-29c-3p suppresses proliferation and tumor progression of HCC cells by targeting TRIM [35]. In addition, another research reported that miR-29c-3p overexpression repressed HCC cells proliferation, migration, and invasion [36]. The above three miRNAs were all negatively correlated with TRA2B, and the associations were statistically significant. The series analysis further identified miR-29c-3p as the most potential upstream binding miRNA of TRA2B in HCC.

According to the competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) hypothesis [37], the potential lncRNAs of miR-29c-3p/TRA2B axis should also be oncogenic and there should be negative correlation between lncRNAs and miRNA, similarly positive relationship between lncRNAs and mRNA. Hence, 52 potential candidates upstream lncRNAs were identified. Subsequently, expression, survival, and correlation analysis were performed, lncRNA colorectal neoplasia differentially expressed (CRNDE) and Linc00511 were identified as the most potential upstream binding lncRNAs. Previous studies have shown that lncRNA CRNDE could enhance HCC cell progression, migration, and invasion of HCC [38–40], as well as increased Linc00511 might be associated with adverse prognosis in HCC [41–43]. Taken together, lncRNA CRNDE and Linc00511/miR-29c-3p/TRA2B were considered the underlying pathways in HCC. The clinical translation of such molecular findings into reliable prognostic biomarkers is a critical goal. This endeavor is reflected in contemporary research, such as the prospective analysis by Chen et al. which identified proteins in extracellular vesicles with significant prognostic value for HCC patients [44]. Their work highlights the clinical need and feasibility of discovering non-tissue-based biomarkers. In this context, the components of our proposed axis could be explored in future studies as potential circulating biomarkers in liquid biopsies, offering a minimally invasive approach for patient stratification.

The tumor immune microenvironment is primarily consisted of tumor cells as well as infiltrated stromal and immune cells [45]. Previous studies have shown that tumor immune infiltration could exert a significant effect on tumor progression, immune evasion, and prognosis [46–48]. In HCC, TRA2B was found to be positively related to multiple immune infiltration cells, such as B cell, macrophage, CD8+ T cell, neutrophil, CD4+ T cell, and dendritic cell. This study also found that the expression of TRA2B was significantly associated with biomarkers of the immune infiltration cells. These findings partially revealed the TRA2B-mediated carcinogenesis in HCC.

Furthermore, the past decade has seen significant progress in ICIs development and large-scale clinical application [49]. Currently, pembrolizumab (PD-1), nivolumab (PD-1), and ipilimumab (CTLA-4) have been approved by FDA for second-line treatment in advanced HCC [50]. Nonetheless, two large phase-3 randomized, multicenter clinical trials CheckMate-459 and KEYNOTE-240 have revealed that the application of anti-PD1 agent did not result in a statistically significant improvement in OS among advanced HCC patients [51, 52].

According to our findings, higher TRA2B expression levels are positively related to CD274, CTLA4, PDCD1, and TIGIT in HCC, suggesting that TRA2B may potentially be developed as a novel anticancer target that can be used in combination with existing clinical immunotherapy treatment regimens. The results of the GSEA also showed that tumor-related pathways and immune activation-related pathways were enriched, including MAPK signaling pathway, WNT signaling pathway, JAK-STAT signaling pathway and so on. These findings suggest that TRA2B is significantly correlated with the composition of the immune microenvironment in HCC.

The potential to therapeutically target key nodes within the HCC signaling network to remodel the TIME is an area of intense investigation. For instance, Liu et al. demonstrated that the combination of the CDK4/6 inhibitor SHR6390 with cabozantinib effectively inhibited tumor progression in an HCC mouse model, an effect that was linked to alterations in the tumor microenvironment [53]. Previous studies have revealed that targeting splicing factors could regulate PD-1/PD-L1 expression in various cancers [54–56]. As a memeber of splicing family, the association between TRA2B and immune markers positions it as a potential candidate molecule whose modulation might similarly impact the HCC immune landscape, a hypothesis that merits future functional investigation.

In conclusion, this study has carried out the expression and prognosis analysis of TRA2B at the HCC for the first time. In HCC, highly expressed TRA2B was found to be closely associated with poor prognosis as well as clinical and pathologic features. We further predicted the possible upstream regulatory mechanism of TRA2B in HCC, named CRNDE/LINC00511-miR-29c-3p axis. In addition, our preliminary findings revealed that TRA2B plays an important role in the tumor immune microenvironment of HCC, which has the potential to be developed as a novel target for immunotherapy for HCC.

Despite the compelling associations identified in our study, it is crucial to acknowledge its inherent limitations. The findings derived from bioinformatic analyses are primarily correlative and do not establish direct causation. For instance, the predicted CRNDE/LINC00511-miR-29c-3p-TRA2B regulatory axis, while supported by expression correlations and survival data, requires experimental validation using techniques such as luciferase reporter assays, RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP), and gene knockdown/overexpression in vitro and in vivo to confirm the functional interactions. This will serve as the main focus for our ongoing research.

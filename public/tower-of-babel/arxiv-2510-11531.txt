On the negativity of the top Lyapunov exponent for stochastic differential equations driven by fractional Brownian motion
Alexandra Blessing Neamţu and Mazyar Ghani Varzaneh

Abstract
We provide sign information for the top Lyapunov exponent for a stochastic differential equation driven by fractional Brownian motion. To this aim we analyze the stochastic dynamical system generated by such an equation, obtain a random dynamical system and construct an appropriate invariant measure. Suitable estimates for its density together with Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem imply the negativity of the top Lyapunov exponent by increasing the noise intensity.



Keywords:
stochastic dynamical system, random dynamical system, fractional Brownian motion, negative Lyapunov exponent.

MSC (2020):
60G22, 60F99, 37H30.

Introduction

The main goal of this work is to develop a machinery that allows one to investigate the asymptotic behavior of stochastic differential equations perturbed by non-Markovian noise. To this aim, we consider the SDE

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&{\textnormal{d}}Y_{t}=F(Y_{t})\penalty\ {\textnormal{d}}t+\sigma{\textnormal{d}}B_{t}\\
&Y_{0}=x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
d\geq 1
and
(B_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a
d
-dimensional fractional Brownian motion (fbm). In particular, under suitable assumptions on the coefficients we prove that (
1.1
) has a negative Lyapunov exponent. To this aim, we first relate the stochastic dynamical systems (SDS) approach developed in
[
Hai05
]
with the random dynamical systems (RDS) one
[
Arn98
]
.

There has been a growing interest in the dynamics of stochastic systems with non-Markovian noise. Several results as averaging
[
HL20
,
LS22
]
, sample path estimates
[
EKN20
,
BBN25
]
, finite-time Lyapunov exponents
[
BNB25
]
, early warning signs for bifurcations
[
KLN22
]
, invariant manifolds and stability analysis
[
GVR25a
,
GVR25b
,
GVR25c
,
KN21
,
KN23
]
or traveling waves
[
SS25
]
have been established. Here we contribute to this aspect and illustrate how to obtain sign information for the top Lyapunov exponent of (
1.1
). In a forthcoming work we plan to investigate synchronization by noise in non-Markovian SDEs. For one-dimensional SDEs driven by fbm, weak synchronization was established in
[
FGS17b
, Section 4.1]
using that the
corresponding random dynamical system is order-preserving and strongly mixing.

As already stated, our approach involves the construction of a random dynamical system associated to (
1.1
). We mention that this framework is useful to define concepts as (in)stability, attractors, invariant manifolds, synchronization (by noise) or chaos. One key indicator for synchronization / chaos is given by the top Lyapunov exponent of the underlying system. This has been observed in different models arising from fluid dynamics such as Lagrangian flows
[
BBPS22a
]
or Galerkin truncations for 2D Navier-Stokes
[
BBPS22b
]
.

Here we are interested in the opposite phenomenon, i.e. when the top Lyapunov exponent is negative.
To the best of our knowledge, all results on Lyapunov exponents and RDS are formulated in a Markovian setting heavily relying on the invariant measure of (
1.1
) which solves a Fokker-Planck equation. All these tools break down for fbm. Therefore the main challenge of this work is to obtain an invariant measure for (
1.1
) based on the approach in
[
Hai05
]
together with a representation for its density. One of our main results, Theorem
4.1
establishes that the top Lyapunov exponent of (
1.1
) can become negative by taking the noise intensity
\sigma
sufficiently large. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work that provides sign information for Lyapunov exponents in a non-Markovian setting. The statement of Theorem
4.1
aligns with the results of
[
FGS17a
]
obtained for a similar type of SDE driven by a Brownian motion showing that its invariant measure flattens for large values of
\sigma
. This fact can be inferred from the corresponding Fokker-Planck equation satisfied by the invariant measure of the SDE. As already stated, such arguments are not available for SDEs driven by fbm.

Therefore we follow the following strategy: based on the results in
[
Hai05
]
one can construct a SDS associated to (
1.1
). Given this we show how to obtain a RDS and construct an invariant measure using disintegration. For its stationary density which can be expressed in terms of the Wiener-Lioville bridge, we can derive Gaussian upper and lower bounds similar to
[
LPS23
]
. A major difference in comparison to the results in
[
LPS23
]
is that in our case both drift and diffusion coefficients depend on a parameter and we have to quantify the dependence of the stationary density of a rescaled SDE on this parameter. In our case, the parameter is related to the intensity of the noise that has to be increased in order to make the top Lyapunov exponent of (
1.1
) negative. For Gaussian bounds of the density of (singular) SDEs with additive fractional Brownian motion we refer to
[
BKHT16
]
and
[
AGRT25
]
and emphasize that for our aims the density of the invariant measure of the corresponding SDE is relevant.

Our result on the negativity of the top Lyapunov exponent holds when the drift term
F
is eventually strictly monotone. We plan to extend these results to gradient type SDEs, i.e.
F=-\nabla V
, for a potential
V
and multiplicative noise where the results obtained in
[
ANPS24
]
can be helpful. Remarkably it was observed in
[
SS25
]
that large values of the Hurst parameter
H
imply higher stability of traveling waves. In
[
BNB25
,
BBN25
]
and here this is not the case, since we can always obtain stability, i.e. a negative top Lyapunov exponent by increasing the noise intensity. However, the proof of this statement is more technical for
H\in(1/2,1)
due to certain singularities that appear in zero, see Proposition
5.26
. Moreover, the results in
[
SS25
,
BNB25
,
BBN25
]
are valid on a finite-time interval whereas here we are interested in asymptotic statements and therefore need ergodicity which is a challenging task in a non-Markovian setting.

In conclusion, the connection between SDS and RDS developed in this work is of independent interest and can be applied to gain further dynamical insights for SDEs perturbed by fbm such as chaos, (singleton) attractors or invariant manifolds. We leave such aspects, in particular synchronization for (
1.1
) for a future work. Moreover, the sign of the Lyapunov exponent is related to several fundamental problems in dynamical systems. For example, having the invariant measure for (
1.1
), one can disintegrate it into probability measures to obtain a family of random measures on the state space as constructed in Theorem
3.11
. These random measures turn out to be closely connected to the sign of the first Lyapunov exponent. When the top Lyapunov exponent is negative, they are typically expected to become atomic, as we show in a forthcoming work. In contrast, when the first Lyapunov exponent is positive, the measures are expected to admit absolutely continuous conditional distributions on unstable manifolds, leading to the existence of Sinai-Ruelle-Bowen (SRB) measures. It is also worth noting that the non-Markovian nature of the system creates additional challenges. Nevertheless, we believe that the foundation established in this manuscript can serve as a basis for addressing further questions, such as obtaining a Furstenberg-type theorem for the top Lyapunov exponent.

This work is structured as follows. In Section
2
we collect important concepts from the theory of stochastic and random dynamical systems, the Mandelbrot van Ness representation of fractional Brownian motion together with a stationary noise process associated to it. Since this encodes the past of the noise we construct in Subsection
2.4
a two-sided fbm as required for the RDS approach. Based on these foundations we construct in Section
3
a RDS given a SDS. Since the SDS incorporates the past of the noise and the RDS its future, for the construction of the RDS given the SDS in Theorem
3.2
, we first define a space encoding the randomness which contains two components (the past and future of the noise) and an appropriate shift. In this setting we can verify the cocycle property.
Moreover, the SDS is defined on the product space of two Polish space
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{B}
, where
\mathcal{X}
stands for the phase space and
\mathcal{B}
is associated to the noise. In this setting one can construct a probability measure on
\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{B}
which is invariant for the SDS. Given this, we construct an invariant measure on the phase space
\mathcal{X}
for the RDS by disintegration together with a perfection-type argument in Theorem
3.11
to prove its invariance. This result is of independent interest and will be used in a forthcoming work on synchronization for (
1.1
).

In Section
4
we consider the SDE (
1.1
) for which one can obtain a SDS due
[
Hai05
]
. We apply the results stated in Section
3
to generate a RDS and compare this approach to the standard one, where one subtracts the noise from (
1.1
) obtaining an ODE with random coefficients
[
MS04b
]
. Here the model of the noise is described by the metric dynamical system
(C_{0}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{d});\mathcal{B}(C_{0}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{d}));\mathbb{P};(\theta_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}})
, where
C_{0}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{d})
denotes the space of continuous functions which are zero in zero,
\mathbb{P}
is the two-sided Wiener measure and
\theta
is the Wiener shift. In our setting, the space associated to the noise contains two components, both chosen from the space specified in Definition
2.17
whose choice is motivated by the Mandelbrot van Ness representation of fbm. Before analyzing the negativity of the top Lyapunov exponent, as a first step we investigate in Subsection
4.3
the linearized dynamics of (
1.1
). Provided that the top Lyapunov exponent is negative, we obtain the existence of a local stable manifold. A similar statement has been derived in the Markovian setting in
[
FGS17a
, Section 3.1]
. We employ novel tools based on the results in
[
GVR23

to obtain this kind of statement in the non-Markovian case.

In Section
5
we first rescale the SDE (
1.1
) and derive bounds for the density of the invariant measure of the corresponding rescaled SDE depending on
\sigma
. To this aim we follow the approach of
[
LPS23
]
, where in our situation we have to keep track of the dependence of
\sigma
on both drift and diffusion coefficient, whereas in
[
LPS23
]
only the drift term contains a parametric dependence. However, by the rescaling argument where the diffusion term lies on the unit sphere, we can effectively handle this additional dependence. Based on Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem we show that increasing

1.1

Preliminaries

2.1
Notations

In this section, we introduce some notations, definitions and auxiliary results which will frequently by used throughout the manuscript.

Lemma 2.1

Let
(X,\sigma(X))
and
(Y,\sigma(Y))
be two measure spaces,
T:X\to Y
be a measurable function and let
\mu
be a Borel measure on
X
. Then the pushforward measure
T_{\star}\mu
on
Y
is defined by

T_{\star}\mu(E):=\mu(T^{-1}(E)),\quad\forall E\in\sigma(Y).

In addition, for a measurable function
f:Y\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
, we have

\int_{X}f(T(x))\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)=\int_{Y}f(y)\,T_{\star}\mu(\mathrm{d}y).

The following result is widely used in this manuscript.

Lemma 2.2

(Fernique’s theorem)
Let
(E,\|\cdot\|)
be a real separable Banach space, and let
\mu
be a centered Gaussian measure on
E
. Then there exists a constant
\alpha>0
such that

\int_{E}\exp\bigl(\alpha\|x\|^{2}\bigr)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)<\infty.

Proof.

See
[
Led96
, Theorem 4.1]
and
[
FV10
, Theorem 15.33]
.

Definition 2.3

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space. Then we define

\mathrm{Pr}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\left\{\nu\text{ is a probability measure on }\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{B}\,\middle|\,\left(\Pi_{\mathcal{B}}\right)_{\ast}\nu=\mathbf{P}\right\},

where
\Pi_{\mathcal{B}}
denotes the projection onto
\mathcal{B}
.

Definition 2.4

For a path
f:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a parameter
\alpha\in(0,1]
, we define the Hölder seminorm

\|f\|_{\alpha;[0,T]}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,t\in[0,T]\\
s\neq t\end{subarray}}\frac{\lvert f(t)-f(s)\rvert}{\lvert t-s\rvert^{\alpha}},

and the supremum norm

\|f\|_{\infty;[0,T]}:=\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\lvert f(s)\rvert.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we say that
f\in C^{m,\alpha}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
if

•

f
is
m
-times continuously differentiable on
[0,T]
, and

•

its
m
-th derivative
f^{(m)}
has finite
\alpha
-Hölder seminorm.

We equip
C^{m,\alpha}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
with the norm

\|f\|_{C^{m,\gamma};[0,T]}:=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\|f^{(k)}\|_{\infty;[0,T]}\;+\;\|f^{(m)}\|_{\alpha;[0,T]}.

2.2
Stochastic and random dynamical systems

In this section, we provide a concise overview of the theory of stochastic dynamical systems (SDS) introduced in the seminal work
[
Hai05
]
. Furthermore, we outline key definitions and concepts from the theory of random dynamical systems (RDS) which goes back to
[
Arn98
]
. Even if these two theories are not equivalent, we show how to obtain a RDS given a SDS and how to apply well-established results from RDS theory in order to investigate non-Markovian dynamics.

Definition 2.5

(
[
Hai05
, Definition 2.1]
)
Let
\mathcal{B}
be a Polish space equipped with the Borel
\sigma
-algebra
\sigma(\mathcal{B})
. Then
\left(\mathcal{B},\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbf{P},\{\vartheta_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}\right)
is called a stationary noise process if it satisfies the following conditions.

(I)
We assume that
\mathbf{P}
is a probability measure on
(\mathcal{B},\sigma(\mathcal{B}))
.

(II)
For every
t\geq 0
, we assume that
P_{t}:\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}
and
\vartheta_{t}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}
are two measurable functions such that for every
\omega^{-}\in\mathcal{B}
, the random variables
P_{t}(\omega^{-},\cdot)
and
\vartheta_{t}
are independent. This means that for every
A,B\in\sigma(\mathcal{B})
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathbf{P}\left(\omega^{+}\in\mathcal{B}:P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})\in A,\vartheta_{t}(\omega^{+})\in B\right)\\
&=\mathbf{P}\left(\omega^{+}\in\mathcal{B}:P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})\in A\right)\mathbf{P}\left(\omega^{+}\in\mathcal{B}:\vartheta_{t}(\omega^{+})\in B\right).\end{split}

(2.1)

(III)
We assume that
\{\vartheta_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is a semiflow on
\mathcal{B}
, i.e.
\vartheta_{t+s}=\vartheta_{t}\circ\vartheta_{s}
for every
t,s\geq 0
. In addition, this flow is invariant with respect to
\mathbf{P}
meaning that
(\vartheta_{t})_{\star}(\mathbf{P})=\mathbf{P}
for every
t\geq 0
.

(IV)
For every
\omega^{-}\in\mathcal{B}
and
t\geq 0
, we define

\mathcal{P}_{t}(\omega^{-};\cdot):=(P_{t}(\omega^{-},\cdot))_{\star}(\mathbf{P}),

and assume that this family defines a Feller transition semigroup on
\mathcal{B}
. This means that for the family of probability measures

\mathcal{P}_{t}:\mathcal{B}\times\sigma(\mathcal{B})\to[0,1],

and for all
A\in\sigma(\mathcal{B})
and
t,s>0
, the following properties hold:

•

the map
\omega^{-}\mapsto\mathcal{P}_{t}(\omega^{-};A)
is measurable;

•

one has the semigroup property

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{t+s}(\omega^{-};A)=\int_{\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{P}_{t}(\omega^{+};A)\mathcal{P}_{s}(\omega^{-};\mathrm{d}\omega^{+});

(2.2)

•

for every
A\in\sigma(\mathcal{B})
and
\omega\in\mathcal{B}
it holds that
\mathcal{P}_{0}(\omega^{-},A)={\chi}_{A}(\omega^{-})
, where
\chi_{A}
denotes the indicator function of the set
A
;

•

for every
f\in C_{b}(\mathcal{B})
, the canonical map

\displaystyle f\mapsto\mathcal{P}_{t}f,\quad\mathcal{P}_{t}f(\omega^{-}):=\int_{\mathcal{B}}f(P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+}))\,\mathbf{P}(\mathrm{d}\omega^{+}),

belongs to the space of bounded and continuous functions on
\mathcal{B}
denoted by
C_{b}(\mathcal{B})
.

Furthermore, we assume that
\mathbf{P}
is the unique invariant measure associated with the transition semigroup
\{\mathcal{P}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
.

(V)
For every
\omega^{-},\omega^{+}\in\mathcal{B}
and
t,s\geq 0
, we assume that

\displaystyle\vartheta_{t}\circ P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})=P_{t}\left(\vartheta_{t}\omega^{-},P_{t}(\omega^{+},\omega^{-})\right)=\omega^{-},

\displaystyle P_{t+s}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})=P_{t}(P_{s}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+}),\vartheta_{s}\omega^{+}).

Remark 2.6

We note that properties
(I)
–
(IV)
are stated in
[
Hai05
, Definition 2.1]
. In addition, we require property
(V)
, which can easily be verified for the stationary noise process associated to fbm in Section
2.4
.

Now we define a stochastic dynamical system.

Definition 2.7

Let
\left(\mathcal{B},\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbf{P},\{\vartheta_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}\right)
be a stationary noise process and let
\mathcal{X}
be a Polish space. We call

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{X},\\
&(t,x,\omega)\rightarrow\varphi^{t}_{\omega}(x)\end{split}

(2.3)

a continuous stochastic dynamical system (SDS) if the following properties hold.

•

For every
T>0
,
\omega\in\mathcal{B}
, and
x\in\mathcal{X}
, for the map
\tilde{\varphi}_{T}(x,\omega):[0,T]\to\mathcal{X}
defined as

\tilde{\varphi}_{T}(x,\omega)(t):=\varphi^{t}_{\vartheta_{T-t}\omega}(x),

we have
\tilde{\varphi}_{T}(x,\omega)\in C\left([0,T],\mathcal{X}\right)
. Moreover, the map
(x,\omega)\mapsto\tilde{\varphi}_{T}(x,\omega)
is continuous from
\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{B}
to
C\left([0,T],\mathcal{X}\right)
.

•

For every
t,s\geq 0
,
\omega\in\mathcal{B}
and
x\in\mathcal{X}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\varphi^{0}_{\omega}(x)=x,\\
&\varphi^{t+s}_{\omega}(x)=\varphi^{s}_{\omega}\circ\varphi^{t}_{\vartheta_{s}\omega}(x).\end{split}

(2.4)

•

Additionally, if
\mathcal{X}
is a separable Banach space, we call the stochastic dynamical system
C^{k}
if for every
k\geq 1
,
t\geq 0
and
\omega\in\mathcal{B}
, the map

x\mapsto\varphi^{t}_{\omega}(x)

is
C^{k}
-Fréchet differentiable.

When referring to a continuous SDS, we omit specifying the stationary noise process and the underlying
Polish space
\mathcal{X}
whenever these are clear from the context.
The following result is taken from
[
Hai05
, Lemma 2.12]
.

Definition 2.8

Let
\varphi
be a continuous SDS. For
t\geq 0
and
\Gamma\in\sigma(\mathcal{X})\otimes\sigma(\mathcal{B})
, we set

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{t}\left((x,\omega^{-});\Gamma\right):=\int_{\mathcal{B}}{\chi}_{\Gamma}\left(\varphi^{t}_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}(x),P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})\right)\mathbf{P}(\mathrm{d}\omega^{+}).

(2.5)

Then the family
(\mathcal{Q}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
constitutes a Feller transition semigroup on
\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{B}
. In particular the Chapman–Kolmogorov equations hold, meaning that for every
t,s>0
,
(x_{1},\omega^{-})\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{B}
and
\Gamma\in\sigma(\mathcal{X})\otimes\sigma(\mathcal{B})
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{t+s}\left((x_{1},\omega^{-});\Gamma\right)=\int_{\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{Q}_{t}\left((x_{2},\omega^{+});\Gamma\right)\mathcal{Q}_{s}\left((x_{1},\omega^{-});\mathrm{d}(x_{2}\times\omega^{+})\right).

(2.6)

This leads to the following definition of an invariant measure for a SDS on the state space
\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{X}
.

Definition 2.9

Let
\varphi
be a continuous SDS. A probability measure
\mu
on
\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{X}
is called an invariant measure for
\varphi
, if it satisfies the following properties:

•

we have

\displaystyle(\Pi_{\mathcal{B}})_{\star}\mu=\mathbf{P},

(2.7)

•

the measure
\mu
is invariant with respect to the transition semigroup
(\mathcal{Q}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, i.e. for every
t\geq 0
and
\Gamma\in\sigma(\mathcal{X})\otimes\sigma(\mathcal{B})
it holds that

(\mathcal{Q}_{t})_{\star}\mu(\Gamma):=\int_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{Q}_{t}\left((x,\omega);\Gamma\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}(x,\omega)\right)=\mu(\Gamma).

Remark 2.10

Note that from Definition
2.5
, we can conclude that the transition semigroup
\{\mathcal{Q}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is stochastically continuous. This means that for every
(x,\omega)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{B}
and for every open ball of radius
\delta>0
around
(x,\omega)
denoted by
B((x,\omega),\delta)
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\mathcal{Q}_{t}\left((x,\omega);B((x,\omega),\delta)\right)=0.

The same claim holds for
(\mathcal{P}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.

Remark 2.11

From (
2.5
) we know that for
A\times B\in\sigma(\mathcal{X})\otimes\sigma(\mathcal{B})

\displaystyle(\Pi_{\mathcal{B}})_{\star}\mathcal{Q}_{t}\left((x,\omega);A\times B)\right)=\mathcal{P}_{t}(\omega;B).

(2.8)

In particular, this implies that the projected measure above evolves independently of the state space
\mathcal{X}
.
Moreover, from
(V)
, the transition semigroup
\{\mathcal{P}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
has the unique invariant measure
\mathbf{P}
. Consequently, if
\mu
is an invariant measure of
\{\mathcal{Q}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
, then condition (
2.7
) holds. Thus, to prove the uniqueness (which implies ergodicity) of the measure
\mu
, it suffices to consider the class of invariant measures of
\{\mathcal{Q}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
satisfying condition (
2.7
).
We also recall that by the ergodicity of
\mu
, we mean ergodicity with respect to the shift map on
C(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{B})
; see
[
DPZ96
, Chapter 2]
for further details.

We now introduce some concepts from the theory of random dynamical systems.

Definition 2.12

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
be measurable spaces and let
\mathbb{T}
be either
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{R}^{+}
, each equipped with its corresponding Borel
\sigma
-algebra.
A family
\theta=\{\theta_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{T}}
of maps from
\Omega
to itself is called a measurable dynamical system if the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

The map
(\omega,t)\mapsto\theta_{t}(\omega)
is
\mathcal{F}\otimes\sigma(\mathbb{T})/\mathcal{F}
-measurable.

(ii)

The identity condition holds:
\theta_{0}=\mathrm{Id}
.

(iii)

The semigroup property holds:
\theta_{s+t}=\theta_{s}\circ\theta_{t}
, for all
s,t\in\mathbb{T}
.

If
\mathbb{P}
is a probability measure on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
such that

(\theta_{t})_{\star}\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}\quad\text{for every }t\in\mathbb{T},

then the quadruple
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\theta)
is called a
measurable metric dynamical system
. We call
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\theta)

ergodic
if, for every
t\in\mathbb{T}\setminus\{0\}
, the map
\theta_{t}:\Omega\to\Omega

is ergodic. That is, if a set
A\in\mathcal{F}
satisfies
\mathbb{P}\big(A\triangle\theta_{t}^{-1}(A)\big)=0,

then

\mathbb{P}(A)=0\quad\text{or}\quad\mathbb{P}(A)=1.

Having a measurable metric dynamical system, one can define a random dynamical system.

Definition 2.13

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Polish space equipped with its Borel
\sigma
-algebra
\sigma(\mathcal{X})
. Then an (ergodic) measurable random dynamical system on
(\mathcal{X},\sigma(\mathcal{X}))
consists of an (ergodic) measurable metric dynamical system
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\theta)
together with a measurable map

\Phi\colon\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\Omega\times\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X},

which satisfies the cocycle property. Setting
\Phi^{t}_{\omega}(x):=\Phi(t,\omega,x)
we require

\Phi^{0}_{\omega}=\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal{X}}\quad\text{for all }\omega\in\Omega,

and

\Phi^{t+s}_{\omega}(x)=\Phi^{s}_{\theta_{t}\omega}\big(\Phi^{t}_{\omega}(x)\big),

for all
s,t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
x\in\mathcal{X}
, and
\omega\in\Omega
. The map
\Phi
is called a cocycle.
If additionally the map
\Phi_{\cdot}(\omega,\cdot)\colon\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
is continuous for every
\omega\in\Omega
, then
\Phi
is called a continuous random dynamical system.

When referring to the RDS
\Phi
we omit mentioning the underlying quadruple
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\theta)
and the Polish space
\mathcal{X}
, whenever these are clear from context.

Remark 2.14

In Definition
2.12
,
\mathbb{T}
may also be either
\mathbb{Z}
or
\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. In this case, we equip
\mathbb{T}
with its power set
P(\mathbb{T})
to form a measurable space. Similarly, in Definition
2.13
, one may replace
\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.

Let
\Phi
be a measurable RDS. Then we can define the associated skew-product for
t\geq 0
by

\displaystyle\Theta_{t}:\mathcal{X}\times\Omega\to\mathcal{X}\times\Omega,\quad(x,\omega)\mapsto\big(\Phi^{t}_{\omega}(x),\theta_{t}\omega\big).

(2.9)

It is straightforward to verify that the semigroup property holds, meaning that for all
s,t\geq 0
, we have

\Theta_{t+s}=\Theta_{s}\circ\Theta_{t}.

This leads to the following natural concept of an invariant measure for a random dynamical system.

Definition 2.15

Let
\Phi
be a measurable RDS over the metric dynamical system
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\theta)
and let
\Theta=\{\Theta_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be the associated skew-product (
2.9
). A probability measure
\nu
on the product space
\left(\mathcal{X}\times\Omega,\sigma(\mathcal{X})\otimes\mathcal{F}\right)

is called an invariant measure for the RDS
\Phi
if the following conditions are satisfied:

•

we have

(\Pi_{\Omega})_{\star}\nu=\mathbb{P};

•

the measure
\nu
is invariant under the skew-product map
\Theta
, i.e., for every
t\geq 0
,

(\Theta_{t})_{\star}\nu=\nu.

Similar to Definition
2.12
, the measure
\nu
is called
ergodic
if for every
t>0
, it is ergodic with respect to the transformation
\Theta_{t}
.

2.3
Fractional Brownian motion

In order to show that fractional Brownian motion generates a stationary noise process in the sense of Definition
2.5
, we rely on the Mandelbrot van Ness representation.

Definition 2.16

Let
(W(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
be a two-sided Wiener process with values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
H\in(0,1)
.
Then we have the following Mandelbrot van Ness representation of a two-sided fractional Brownian motion
(B^{H}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
given by

\displaystyle B^{H}_{t}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{H}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}(-r)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\mathrm{d}W(t+r)-\mathrm{d}W(r)\right),

(2.10)

where the stochastic integral is understood in the Itô sense and

\displaystyle\alpha_{H}:=\left(\frac{1}{2H}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\left((1+s)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}-s^{H-\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We mention that there also exists a Volterra-type representation for fractional Brownian motion. Since this does not lead to a stationary noise process, we rely on (
2.10
). Given this representation,
W
is referred to as the Wiener process associated to
B^{H}
.

Definition 2.17

For
0<H<1
, we define
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{-},\mathbb{R}^{d})
as the space of smooth, compactly supported functions
\omega:\mathbb{R}^{-}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\omega(0)=0
. For
\omega\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{-},\mathbb{R}^{d})
we set

\displaystyle\|\omega\|_{\mathcal{B}_{H}}:=\sup_{s,t\in\mathbb{R}^{-}}\frac{|\omega(t)-\omega(s)|}{\sqrt{1+|t|+|s|}|t-s|^{\frac{1-H}{2}}}.

(2.11)

The space
\mathcal{B}_{H}
is defined as the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{-},\mathbb{R}^{d})
with respect to the norm given above and is a separable Banach space.

Motivated by the representation (
2.10
) we state the following result.

Lemma 2.18

(
[
Hai05
, Lemma 3.6]
)
For
\omega\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{-},\mathbb{R}^{d})
the operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{H}\omega(t):=\frac{1}{\alpha_{H}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}(-r)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\dot{\omega}(t+r)-\dot{\omega}(r)\right)\,\mathrm{d}r,\quad t\leq 0,

(2.12)

can be canonically extended to
\mathcal{B}_{H}
. This means that for any sequence
\{\omega_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{-},\mathbb{R}^{d})
converging to
\omega\in\mathcal{B}_{H}
, the following limit

\mathcal{D}_{H}\omega:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{D}_{H}\omega_{n}

exists. Moreover,
\mathcal{D}_{H}\omega
takes values in
\mathcal{B}_{1-H}
and the mapping

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{H}:\mathcal{B}_{H}\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}_{1-H}

(2.13)

is continuous and admits a bounded inverse. Furthermore, there exists a constant
\gamma_{H}>0
, with
\gamma_{H}=\gamma_{1-H}
, such that the inverse of
\mathcal{D}_{H}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{H}^{-1}=\gamma_{H}\mathcal{D}_{1-H}:\mathcal{B}_{1-H}\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}_{H}.

(2.14)

We point out that there exists a Gaussian measure
\mathbf{P}
on
\mathcal{B}_{H}
such that the
canonical process associated to it is a time-reversed Brownian motion. Then, thanks to the properties of the operator
\mathcal{D}_{H}
, the pushforward measure
(\mathcal{D}_{H})_{\star}\mathbf{P}
defines a time-reversed fractional Brownian motion on
\mathcal{B}_{1-H}
with Hurst parameter
H
. Furthermore, using the operators
\mathcal{D}_{H}
and
\mathcal{D}_{1-H}
, one can switch between Wiener processes and fractional Brownian motions. We summarize these facts in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.19

(
[
Hai05
, Lemma 3.8]

For
H\in(0,1)
, there exists a unique Gaussian measure
\mathbf{P}
in
\mathcal{B}_{H}
, for which the associated canonical process is a time-reversed Brownian motion. In addition, the canonical process associated to the measure
(\mathcal{D}_{H})_{\star}\mathbf{P}
on
\mathcal{B}_{1-H}
is a time-reversed fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter
H
.

Remark 2.20

As we already mentioned, for the canonical Wiener process
\omega\in\mathcal{B}_{H}
, the process
\{\mathcal{D}_{H}\omega(t)\}_{t\leq 0}
defines a fractional Brownian motion for negative times. However, in the context of random dynamical systems, it is important to consider a two-sided fractional Brownian motion, i.e. we have to extend the previous construction to positive times. This is in contrast to the theory of stochastic dynamical systems, where it suffices to characterize the driving noise by its law.
We will extend in Lemma
2.23
the construction of the fractional Brownian motion to positive times.

2.4
Stationary noise process associated to fractional Brownian motion

We first construct a stationary noise process for a fractional Brownian motion.

Lemma 2.21

The quadrupel
\left(\mathcal{B}_{H},\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbf{P},\{\vartheta_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}\right)
is a stationary noise process in the sense of Definition
2.5
, where:

1.

the family of shift maps
\{\vartheta_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathcal{B}_{H}
is defined as

\displaystyle\vartheta_{t}\omega(s):=\omega(s-t)-\omega(-t),\ \ \ \forall s\leq 0,

2.

for every
t\geq 0
,
P_{t}:\mathcal{B}_{H}\times\mathcal{B}_{H}\to\mathcal{B}_{H}
is defined as

\displaystyle P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})(s):=\begin{cases}\omega^{+}(-s-t)-\omega^{+}(-t),&\text{for }-t\leq s\leq 0,\\
\omega^{-}(s+t)-\omega^{+}(-t),&\text{for }s\leq-t.\end{cases}

(2.15)

Figure 1
:
Visualization of
\vartheta_{t}\omega^{+}
and
P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})
for two paths
\omega^{-}
and
\omega^{+}
in
\mathcal{B}_{H}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{B}_{H}
can be canonically equipped with the Wiener measure
\mathbf{P}
, we can easily verify that
\left(\mathcal{B}_{H},\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbf{P},\{\vartheta_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}\right)
is a stationary noise process in the sense of Definition
2.5
, see
[
Hai05
, Lemma 3.10]
for more details. We only prove here the identities (
2.5
) which are straightforward. For example, using the definition of
\{\vartheta_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
and (
2.15
) we have

\displaystyle P_{t}\left(\vartheta_{t}\omega^{-},P_{t}(\omega^{+},\omega^{-})\right)(s)=\begin{cases}P_{t}(\omega^{+},\omega^{-})(-s-t)-P_{t}(\omega^{+},\omega^{-})(-t)=\omega^{-}(s),&\text{for }-t\leq s\leq 0,\\
\vartheta_{t}\omega^{-}(s+t)-P_{t}(\omega^{+},\omega^{-})(-t)=\omega^{-}(s),&\text{for }s\leq-t.\end{cases}

For
s\leq-t
we have since
\omega^{+}(0)=0
that

\vartheta_{t}\omega^{-}(s+t)-P_{t}(\omega^{+},\omega^{-})(-t)=\omega^{-}(s)=\omega^{-}(s+t-t)-\omega^{-}(-t)-\omega^{+}(-t+t)+\omega^{-}(-t)=\omega^{-}(s)

whereas for
-t\leq s\leq 0

P_{t}(\omega^{+},\omega^{-})(-s-t)-P_{t}(\omega^{+},\omega^{-})(-t)=\omega^{-}(s+t-t)-\omega^{-}(-t)-\omega^{-}(t-t)+\omega^{-}(-t)=\omega^{-}(s).

This verifies that
\mathcal{\vartheta}_{t}\circ P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})=P_{t}(\mathcal{\vartheta}_{t}\omega^{-},P_{t}(\omega^{+},\omega^{-}))=\omega^{-}
.

For our aims, we need the following result.

Lemma 2.22

For every
\omega^{-},\omega^{+}\in\mathcal{B}_{H}
and every
0\leq s\leq t
and
0\leq\tau\leq s
, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{H}P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})(\tau-t)-\mathcal{D}_{H}P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})(-t)=\mathcal{D}_{H}P_{s}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})(\tau-s)-\mathcal{D}_{H}P_{s}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})(-s).

(2.16)

Moreover, for all
r\leq 0
and
\omega^{-}\in\mathcal{B}_{H}
we have

\displaystyle(\mathcal{D}_{H}\vartheta_{t}\omega^{-})(r)=\mathcal{D}_{H}\omega^{-}(-t+r)-\mathcal{D}_{H}\omega^{-}(-t).

(2.17)

Proof.

We can assume that
\omega,\tilde{\omega}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{-},\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Otherwise, we can take two sequences
\{\omega_{n}^{-}\}_{n\geq 1}
and
\{\omega_{n}^{+}\}_{n\geq 1}
in
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{-},\mathbb{R}^{d})
that converge to
\omega^{-}
and
\omega^{+}
in
\mathcal{B}_{H}
and conclude the claim using the continuity of
P_{t}
,
P_{s}
and
\mathcal{D}_{H}
. From (
2.12
) it is sufficient to prove that for almost all
r\leq 0
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}r}\left[\left(P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})(\tau-t+r)-P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})(-t+r)\right)\right]=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}r}\left[\left(P_{s}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})(\tau-s+r)-P_{s}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})(-s+r)\right)\right],

which easily follows from (
2.15
). A similar argument can be used to prove the second claim.

Now, we extend the fractional Brownian motion obtained in Lemma
2.19
to positive times.

Lemma 2.23

For
H\in(0,1)
, let
\Omega_{H}:=\mathcal{B}_{H}\times\mathcal{B}_{H}
and define the probability space
(\Omega_{H},\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
by setting

\mathcal{F}:=\sigma(\mathcal{B}_{H})\otimes\sigma(\mathcal{B}_{H}),\quad\mathbb{P}:=\mathbf{P}\times\mathbf{P}.

Furthermore, we define

\displaystyle B_{t}^{H}:=\begin{cases}B_{t}^{H}\!\left(P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})\right)=-\bigl(\mathcal{D}_{H}P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})\bigr)(-t),&t\geq 0,\\[6.0pt]
\ \mathcal{D}_{H}\omega^{-}(t),&t\leq 0.\end{cases}

(2.18)

Then
(B_{t}^{H})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is a two-sided fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter
H
, adapted to the canonical filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{-\infty}^{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
of the Brownian motion, where

\mathcal{F}_{-\infty}^{t}:=\sigma\!\left(\bigcup_{s\leq t}\mathcal{F}_{s}^{t}\right)=\bigcup_{s\leq t}\mathcal{F}_{s}^{t}.

Proof.

From Definition
2.5
, for every
t\geq 0
we have
(P_{t})_{\star}(\mathbb{P})=\mathbf{P}
.
Moreover, from (
2.16
), for
s,t\geq 0
it follows that

B_{t}^{H}-B_{s}^{H}=-\mathcal{D}_{H}P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})(s-t).

Therefore, by combining Lemma
2.19
with Lemma
2.21
, one concludes that the process
\{B^{H}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is a fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter
H
, adapted to the filtration
\{\mathcal{F}^{t}_{-\infty}\}_{t\geq 0}
.
We further state the following result, which will be required in Section
5
.

Lemma 2.24

For every
T>0
and
\gamma\leq\frac{H}{2}
, we define the operator

\mathcal{P}:\mathcal{B}_{H}\longrightarrow C^{\gamma}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})

as the continuous extension of the mapping

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\omega)(t):=\frac{1}{\alpha_{H}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left[(t-r)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}-(-r)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}\right]\dot{\omega}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r,\quad\text{for all }t\in[0,T],

(2.19)

from
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{-},\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
\mathcal{B}_{H}
. In particular, the operator
\mathcal{P}
is well-defined and continuous.

Proof.

The proof closely follows the arguments in
[
Hai05
, Lemma 3.6]
and
[
DPT19
, Lemma 6.5]
. We provide it here for the sake of completeness.
Let
\omega\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{-},\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and let
t,s\in[0,T]
with
s<t
. Setting
h:=t-s
we have for every
r\leq 0
that

\displaystyle\left|(t-r)^{H-\frac{3}{2}}-(s-r)^{H-\frac{3}{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\frac{3}{2}-H\Big)(s-r)^{H-\frac{5}{2}}h.

(2.20)

Moreover, since
\omega(0)=0
,

\displaystyle\lvert\omega(r)\rvert\leq\|\omega\|_{\mathcal{B}_{H}}\,\sqrt{1+\lvert r\rvert}\,\lvert r\rvert^{\tfrac{1-H}{2}}.

(2.21)

Furthermore, for
s,t\in[0,T]
we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{-\infty}^{-h}(s-r)^{H-\tfrac{5}{2}}\sqrt{1+\lvert r\rvert}\,\lvert r\rvert^{\tfrac{1-H}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}r\;\lesssim\;h^{\tfrac{H}{2}-1},\\[3.99994pt]
&\Biggl\lvert\int_{-h}^{0}\Bigl((t-r)^{H-\tfrac{3}{2}}-(s-r)^{H-\tfrac{3}{2}}\Bigr)\lvert r\rvert^{\tfrac{1-H}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}r\Biggr\rvert\;\lesssim\;h^{\tfrac{H}{2}}.\end{split}

(2.22)

Therefore, by integration by parts and using (
2.20
)–(
2.22
), we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\bigl\lvert\mathcal{P}(\omega)(t)-\mathcal{P}(\omega)(s)\bigr\rvert=\frac{1}{\alpha_{H}}\Biggl\lvert\int_{-\infty}^{0}\Bigl((t-r)^{H-\tfrac{1}{2}}-(s-r)^{H-\tfrac{1}{2}}\Bigr)\dot{\omega}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\Biggr\rvert\\[3.00003pt]
&=\frac{\lvert H-\tfrac{1}{2}\rvert}{\alpha_{H}}\Biggl\lvert\int_{-\infty}^{0}\Bigl((t-r)^{H-\tfrac{3}{2}}-(s-r)^{H-\tfrac{3}{2}}\Bigr)\omega(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\Biggr\rvert\\[3.00003pt]
&\lesssim\Biggl\lvert\int_{-\infty}^{-h}\Bigl((t-r)^{H-\tfrac{3}{2}}-(s-r)^{H-\tfrac{3}{2}}\Bigr)\omega(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\Biggr\rvert+\Biggl\lvert\int_{-h}^{0}\Bigl((t-r)^{H-\tfrac{3}{2}}-(s-r)^{H-\tfrac{3}{2}}\Bigr)\omega(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\Biggr\rvert\\[3.00003pt]
&\lesssim h\,\|\omega\|_{\mathcal{B}_{H}}\int_{-\infty}^{-h}(s-r)^{H-\tfrac{5}{2}}\sqrt{1+\lvert r\rvert}\,\lvert r\rvert^{\tfrac{1-H}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}r\\
&\quad+\|\omega\|_{\mathcal{B}_{H}}\Biggl\lvert\int_{-h}^{0}\Bigl((t-r)^{H-\tfrac{3}{2}}-(s-r)^{H-\tfrac{3}{2}}\Bigr)\lvert r\rvert^{\tfrac{1-H}{2}}\sqrt{1+\lvert r\rvert}\,\mathrm{d}r\Biggr\rvert\\[3.00003pt]
&\lesssim(t-s)^{\tfrac{H}{2}}\,\|\omega\|_{\mathcal{B}_{H}}.\end{split}

Thus, by the definition of the Banach space
\mathcal{B}_{H}
and a density argument, one concludes that the previous estimate holds for every
\omega\in\mathcal{B}_{H}
. This completes the proof.

Remark 2.25

This lemma is a pathwise version, i.e. for every
\omega\in\mathcal{B}_{H}
of
[
DPT19
, Lemma 6.5]
, which provides less Hölder regularity in time for
\mathcal{P}
, i.e.
\gamma
for
\gamma\leq H/2
. This is natural, since we do not employ any probabilistic arguments which entail the
\gamma
-Hölder continuity of
\mathcal{P}
with
H<\gamma
.

We finally introduce the following Liouville operator.

Lemma 2.26

For every
T>0
, one can define the operator

\displaystyle\tilde{B}^{H}:\mathcal{B}_{H}\longrightarrow C([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})

(2.23)

as the continuous extension of the mapping

\displaystyle\tilde{B}^{H}(\omega)(t)=\tilde{B}^{H}_{t}(\omega):=-\frac{1}{\alpha_{H}}\int_{-t}^{0}(r+t)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}\dot{\omega}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r,\quad\text{for all }t\in[0,T]

from
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{-},\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
\mathcal{B}_{H}
. Moreover, there exists a constant
C_{T,H}>0
such that

\displaystyle\left|\tilde{B}^{H}_{t}(\omega)\right|\leq C_{T,H}\,t^{\tfrac{H}{2}}\,\|\omega\|_{\mathcal{B}_{H}}.

(2.24)

Proof.

Let
\omega\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{-},\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then we have

\displaystyle-\int_{-t}^{0}(r+t)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}\dot{\omega}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r=-\omega(-t)\,t^{H-\frac{1}{2}}+\left(H-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\int_{-t}^{0}(r+t)^{H-\frac{3}{2}}\bigl(\omega(r)-\omega(-t)\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}r.

This identity can be established directly by applying integration by parts to the left-hand side.
Therefore, using the definition of the norm of
\omega
in
\mathcal{B}_{H}
, we obtain the existence of a constant
C_{T,H}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha_{H}}\left|-\int_{-t}^{0}(r+t)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}\dot{\omega}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\right|\leq C_{T,H}t^{\frac{H}{2}}\|\omega\|_{\mathcal{B}_{H}},

as stated.

Remark 2.27

One can also define

\tilde{B}_{t}^{H}(\omega)=-\frac{1}{\alpha_{H}}\int_{-t}^{0}(r+t)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\omega(r)

without relying on any probabilistic tools.

Thanks to Lemma
2.24
and Lemma
2.26
, we can state the following result regarding the pathwise decomposition of fractional Brownian motion.

Lemma 2.28

Let
\omega^{-},\omega^{+}\in\mathcal{B}_{H}
. For
t\geq 0
, recall that

B^{H}_{t}\!\left(P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})\right)=-\bigl(\mathcal{D}_{H}P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})\bigr)(-t).

Then

B^{H}_{t}\!\left(P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})\right)=\mathcal{P}(\omega^{-})(t)+\tilde{B}^{H}_{t}(\omega^{+}).

(2.25)

Proof.

We first assume that

\omega^{-},\omega^{+}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{-},\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
In this case, the statement follows (
2.12
), (
2.19
) and (
2.23
).
For
\omega^{-},\omega^{+}\in\mathcal{B}_{H}
, we choose sequences

(\omega_{n}^{-})_{n\geq 1}
and
(\omega_{n}^{+})_{n\geq 1}
in

C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{-},\mathbb{R}^{d})
and conclude by a density argument.

Remark 2.29

Note that the previous lemma provides a decomposition of the fractional Brownian motion into two components: the history, represented by
\mathcal{P}(\omega^{-})(t)
, and the innovation process, given by
\tilde{B}^{H}_{t}(\omega^{+})
.

3
Construction of a random dynamical system given a stochastic dynamical system

Now that we can construct a stationary noise process for a fractional Brownian motion, let us return to the connection between stochastic and random dynamical systems.

Remark 3.1

Before generating a RDS from SDS we first point out some fundamental differences between the two theories. First of all, the abstract space
\Omega
in Definition
2.13
incorporates the future of the noise, in contrast to
\mathcal{B}
which incorporates the past of the noise. Moreover, in the RDS framework, the noise is frozen once an element
\omega\in\Omega
is fixed which is in contrast to the construction of the SDS.

The next results entails a random dynamical system given a stochastic dynamical system.

Theorem 3.2

Let
\varphi
be a continuous SDS. We consider the probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
where
\Omega:=\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{B}
,
\mathcal{F}:=\sigma(\mathcal{B})\otimes\sigma(\mathcal{B})
and
\mathbb{P}:=\mathbf{P}\times\mathbf{P}
and define a family of measurable functions

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathcal{X}\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{X},\\
&\left(t,x,(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})\right)\longrightarrow\Phi^{t}_{(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}(x):=\varphi^{t}_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}(x).\end{split}

(3.1)

Furthermore, we define the shift
\theta_{t}:\Omega\to\Omega
by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\theta_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+}):=\begin{cases}\left(P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+}),\vartheta_{t}\omega^{+}\right),&\text{for }t\geq 0,\\
\left(\vartheta_{-t}\omega^{-},P_{-t}(\omega^{+},\omega^{-})\right),&\text{for }t<0.\end{cases}\end{split}

(3.2)

For every
t,s\in\mathbb{R}
we have
\theta_{t+s}=\theta_{t}\circ\theta_{s}
. Additionally, this family preserves the probability measure, i.e.

\displaystyle(\theta_{t})_{\star}(\mathbf{P}\times\mathbf{P})=\mathbf{P}\times\mathbf{P},\quad\text{ for all }t\in\mathbb{R}.

(3.3)

In conclusion,
\Phi
is a continuous random dynamical system satisfying the cocycle property, i.e. for
t,s\geq 0
and
x\in\mathcal{X}
we have

\displaystyle\Phi^{t+s}_{(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}(x)=\Phi^{s}_{\theta_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}\circ\Phi^{t}_{(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}(x).

(3.4)

Proof.

From
(V)
in Definition
2.5
and the semiflow property of
\vartheta_{t}
we have for
t,s\geq 0

\displaystyle\begin{split}\theta_{t}\circ\theta_{s}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})&=\theta_{t}\left(P_{s}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+}),\vartheta_{s}\omega^{+}\right)=\left(P_{t}\left(P_{s}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+}),\vartheta_{s}\omega^{+}\right),\vartheta_{t}\circ\vartheta_{s}\omega^{+}\right)\\
&=\left(P_{t+s}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+}),\vartheta_{t+s}\omega^{+}\right)=\theta_{t+s}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+}).\end{split}

(3.5)

We now compute the inverse of
\theta_{t}
for
t\geq 0
. We observe

\displaystyle\theta_{t}\circ\theta_{-t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})=\theta_{t}\left(\vartheta_{t}\omega^{-},P_{t}(\omega^{+},\omega^{-})\right)=\left(P_{t}(\vartheta_{t}\omega^{-},P_{t}(\omega^{+},\omega^{-})),\vartheta_{t}P_{t}(\omega^{+},\omega^{-})\right)=(\omega^{-},\omega^{+}),

(3.6)

where we used the first property in
(V)
in the last step.
Similarly, we can prove that
\theta_{-t}\circ\theta_{-s}=\theta_{-t-s}
. Now, let
0\leq s\leq t
. Then, from (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) we have

\displaystyle\theta_{t}\circ\theta_{-s}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})=\theta_{t-s}\circ\theta_{s}\circ\theta_{-s}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})=\theta_{t-s}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+}).

The remaining cases can be addressed in a similar way. To prove (
3.3
), for simplicity, let
t\geq 0
and
A,C\in\sigma(\mathcal{B})
. From Definition
2.5
and (
3.2
)

\displaystyle(\theta_{t})_{\star}(\mathbf{P}\times\mathbf{P})(A\times C)

\displaystyle{\mathrel{\mathop{=}}}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\int_{\mathcal{B}}{\chi}_{A}(P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})){\chi}_{C}(\vartheta_{t}\omega^{+})\mathbf{P}(\mathrm{d}\omega^{+})\mathbf{P}(\mathrm{d}\omega^{-})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\hyperref@@ii[II]{\textbf{(II)}}}}{{\mathrel{\mathop{=}}}}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{B}}{\chi}_{A}(P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+}))\mathbf{P}(\mathrm{d}\omega^{+})\int_{\mathcal{B}}{\chi}_{C}(\vartheta_{t}\omega^{+})\mathbf{P}(\mathrm{d}\omega^{+})\right)\mathbf{P}(\mathrm{d}\omega^{-})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\hyperref@@ii[III]{\textbf{(III)}}}}{{\mathrel{\mathop{=}}}}\mathbf{P}(C)\int_{\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{P}_{t}(\omega^{-};A)\mathbf{P}(\mathrm{d}\omega^{-})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\hyperref@@ii[IV]{\textbf{(IV)}}}}{{\mathrel{\mathop{=}}}}\mathbf{P}(C)\mathbf{P}(A)=\mathbf{P}\times\mathbf{P}(A\times C).

Now, it remains to show the cocycle property. This can be verified as follows.

\displaystyle\Phi^{t+s}_{(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}(x)

\displaystyle{\mathrel{\mathop{=}}}\varphi^{t+s}_{P_{t+s}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}(x)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{cocycle_SDS}}}{{\mathrel{\mathop{=}}}}\phi^{s}_{P_{t+s}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}\circ\phi^{t}_{\vartheta_{s}\circ P_{t+s}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}(x)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\hyperref@@ii[V]{\textbf{(V)}}}}{{\mathrel{\mathop{=}}}}\phi^{s}_{P_{s}\left(P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+}),\vartheta_{t}\omega^{+}\right)}\circ\phi^{t}_{\vartheta_{s}\circ P_{s}\left(P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+}),\vartheta_{t}\omega^{+}\right)}(x)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\hyperref@@ii[V]{\textbf{(V)}}}}{{\mathrel{\mathop{=}}}}\phi^{s}_{P_{s}\left(P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+}),\vartheta_{t}\omega^{+}\right)}\circ\phi^{t}_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}(x)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{COCO}-\eqref{semigr}}}{{\mathrel{\mathop{=}}}}\Phi^{s}_{\theta_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}\circ\Phi^{t}_{(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}(x).

Remark 3.3

From the definition, it is clear that if
\varphi
is
C^{k}
for some
k\geq 1
, then
\Phi
is also
C^{k}
.

Assuming that
\varphi
admits an invariant measure
\mu
on
\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{X}
, it is possible to define a flow as in (

) on
\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{B}
that preserves the probability measure

.

Lemma 3.4

Let
\varphi
be a continuous SDS and suppose it admits an invariant measure
\mu
in the sense of Definition
2.9
. For every
t\geq 0
we set

\displaystyle\Theta_{t}:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{B},

\displaystyle\left(x,\omega^{-},\omega^{+}\right)\longrightarrow\left(\Phi^{t}_{(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}(x),P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+}),\vartheta_{t}\omega^{+}\right).

Then we have

1.

For all
t,s\geq 0
, it holds that

\displaystyle\Theta_{t+s}=\Theta_{t}\circ\Theta_{s}.

2.

This family of maps preserves the probability measure
\mu\times\mathbf{P}
, i.e.

\displaystyle(\Theta_{t})_{\star}(\mu\times\mathbf{P})=\mu\times\mathbf{P},\quad\forall t\in[0,\infty).

(3.7)

Proof.

The flow property is a simple consequence of Theorem
3.2
. It remains to prove (
3.7
). By definition for
\Gamma\times A\in\left(\sigma(\mathcal{X})\otimes\sigma(\mathcal{B})\right)\times\sigma(\mathcal{B})
, we have

\displaystyle(\Theta_{t})_{\star}(\Gamma\times A)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{B}}\int_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{B}}{\chi}_{\Gamma}\left(\varphi^{t}_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}(x),P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})\right){\chi}_{A}\left(\vartheta_{t}\omega^{+}\right)\mu(\mathrm{d}(x,\omega^{-}))\mathbf{P}(\mathrm{d}\omega^{+})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{B}}\int_{\mathcal{B}}{\chi}_{\Gamma}\left(\varphi^{t}_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}(x),P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})\right){\chi}_{A}\left(\vartheta_{t}\omega^{+}\right)\mathbf{P}(\mathrm{d}\omega^{+})\mu(\mathrm{d}(x,\omega^{-})).

Now, from
(II)
and
(III)
and the invariance of
\mu
, we conclude that

\displaystyle(\Theta_{t})_{\star}(\Gamma\times A)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}(A)\int_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{B}}\int_{\mathcal{B}}{\chi}_{\Gamma}\left(\varphi^{t}_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}(x),P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})\right)\mathbf{P}(\mathrm{d}\omega^{+})\mu(\mathrm{d}(x,\omega^{-}))

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}(A)\int_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{Q}_{t}\left((x,\omega^{-});\Gamma\right)\mu\left(\mathrm{d}(x,\omega^{-})\right)=\mathbf{P}(A)\mu(\Gamma)=(\mu\times\mathbf{P})(\Gamma\times A),

completing the proof.

This yields the following result.

Corollary 3.5

Let
\{\Theta_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be as in Lemma

. Then

\left(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{B},\;\sigma(\mathcal{X})\otimes\sigma(\mathcal{B})\otimes\sigma(\mathcal{B}),\;\mu\times\mathbf{P},\;\Theta\right),

is a metric dynamical system.

It is natural to expect a connection between the ergodicity of
\{\mathcal{Q}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
and that of
\{\Theta_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
. This is the content of the next corollary:

Corollary 3.6

Let
\mu
be the unique ergodic invariant measure for the transition semigroup
\{\mathcal{Q}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
, and suppose that, for every
t>0
, the map
\vartheta_{t}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}
is ergodic with respect to the measure

. Then, for every
t>0
, the map

\Theta_{t}:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{B}

is ergodic with respect to the product measure
\mu\times\mathbf{P}
.

Proof.

The statement follows directly from the uniqueness (and thus the ergodicity) of the invariant measure
\mu
associated with the transition semigroup

\{\mathcal{Q}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
.

The flow property of
\{\Theta_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
, together with the fact that the probability measure
\mu\times\mathbf{P}
is preserved under this flow, allows us to define another cocycle by linearizing
\Phi
.

Corollary 3.7

Suppose that
\mathcal{X}
is a separable Banach space and
\varphi
is a
C^{k}
-SDS for some
k\geq 1
. Let
\Phi
be the RDS given in Theorem
3.2
and define

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathcal{X}\times\left(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{B}\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{X},

\displaystyle\left(t,y,\left(x,\omega^{-},\omega^{+}\right)\right)\longrightarrow\mathcal{T}^{t}_{(x,\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}(y):={\textnormal{D}}_{x}\Phi^{t}_{(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}(y).

Then, for every
t,s\geq 0
and
(x,\omega^{-},\omega^{+})\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{B}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{t+s}_{(x,\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}=\mathcal{T}^{s}_{\Theta_{t}(x,\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}\circ\mathcal{T}^{t}_{(x,\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}.

In particular,
\mathcal{T}
is a linear continuous RDS over the metric metric dynamical system

\left(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{B},\,\sigma(\mathcal{X})\otimes\sigma(\mathcal{B})\otimes\sigma(\mathcal{B}),\,\mu\times\mathbf{P},\,\Theta\right).

Proof.

From (
3.4
) we have

\displaystyle\Phi^{t+s}_{(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}(x)=\Phi^{s}_{\theta_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}\circ\Phi^{t}_{(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}(x).

The claim follows by differentiating
\Phi
and using the chain rule.

Invariant measure for the RDS.

For stochastic Markov processes admitting an invariant measure, it is possible to construct a family of random measures that remain invariant under the associated cocycle of the process. This can be achieved by employing the martingale convergence theorem; see
[
Via14
, Chapter 5]
for further details. Since we are dealing with non-Markovian processes, this approach is not applicable. However, since the invariant measure
\mu
for a continuous SDS is defined on the product space
\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{X}
, we can instead employ the disintegration theorem to construct invariant random measures on the state space
\mathcal{X}
. This result will be used in a forthcoming work to investigate the connection between attractors and invariant measures in a non-Markovian setting.

Lemma 3.8

Suppose
\varphi
is a continuous SDS and
\mu
is an invariant measure for this SDS. Then, there exists a family of probability measures
\{\mu_{\omega^{-}}\}_{\omega^{-}\in\mathcal{B}}
on
\mathcal{X}
such that for every
A\times C\in\sigma(\mathcal{X})\otimes\sigma(\mathcal{B})
the following properties hold:

1)

\mu_{\omega^{-}}(\mathcal{X})=1
for
\mathbf{P}
-a.s.
\omega^{-}\in\mathcal{B}
.

2)

The function defined by
\omega^{-}\mapsto\mu_{\omega^{-}}(A)
is measurable.

3)

\mu(A\times C)=\int_{C}\mu_{\omega^{-}}(A)\mathbf{P}({\textnormal{d}}\omega^{-}).

4)

The family of measures
\{\mu_{\omega^{-}}\}_{\omega^{-}\in\mathcal{B}}
is
\mathbf{P}
-almost surely unique.
This means that if there exists another family of measures
\{\tilde{\mu}_{\omega^{-}}\}_{\omega^{-}\in\mathcal{B}}

satisfying the three conditions above, then for
\mathbf{P}
-almost every
\omega^{-}\in\mathcal{B}
we have

\tilde{\mu}_{\omega^{-}}=\mu_{\omega^{-}}.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{X}
are Polish spaces and
(\Pi_{\mathcal{X}})_{\star}\mu=\mathbf{P}
, the disintegration theorem entails the result. The uniqueness also follows from the disintegration theorem; see
[
VO16
, Proposition 5.1.7 and Theorem 5.1.11]
for more details.

Let
\Phi
be the RDS defined in Theorem
3.2
. Thanks to the previous lemma, if the SDS
\varphi
admits an invariant measure, then we can obtain a family of random measures on the state space
\mathcal{X}
. We naturally expect this family of random measures to be invariant for
\Phi
. This means that for every
t>0
, on a
\theta_{t}
-invariant subset
\tilde{\Omega}\subset\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{B}
of full measure, the following invariance property holds

\displaystyle(\Phi^{t}_{(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})})_{\star}\mu_{\omega^{-}}=\mu_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}.

(3.8)

However, there always exists a set of null measure that must be neglected, and this set typically depends on the parameter
t
. In the context of random dynamical systems, such dependence is undesirable. In what follows, we aim to address this issue by constructing a set of full measure in
\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{B}
that is
\theta
-invariant and on which the invariance property (
3.8
) holds. Our main contribution in this context is to generate such random measures in a non-Markovian setting, without imposing any additional assumptions on the underlying measure
\mu
. Before making these statements rigorous, we first show that these random measures are a natural choice (up to a set of full measure) for the invariance property (
3.8
).

Lemma 3.9

Assume that
\{\mu_{\omega^{-}}\}_{\omega^{-}\in\mathcal{B}}
is the family of random measures obtained in Lemma
3.8
. Then for every
t\geq 0
we can find a set of full measure
\mathcal{B}_{t}\subset\mathcal{B}
such that for every
\omega^{-}\in\mathcal{B}_{t}

\displaystyle(\varphi^{t}_{\omega^{-}})_{\star}(\mu_{\vartheta_{t}\omega^{-}})=\mu_{\omega^{-}}.

Proof.

First, we recall the basic fact that the composition of Borel measurable functions is again Borel measurable. This will be used throughout the argument, even when not stated explicitly.
Since
\varphi
is a continuous SDS, it follows that the map

\displaystyle(t,x,\omega^{-})\mapsto\varphi^{t}_{\omega^{-}}(x)

is jointly measurable. Let
A\times C\in\sigma(\mathcal{X})\otimes\sigma(\mathcal{B})
. Then from Definition
2.8
, Definition
2.9
and Lemma
3.8
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mu(A\times C)=\int_{\mathcal{B}}{\chi}_{C}(\omega^{-})\int_{\mathcal{X}}{\chi}_{A}(x)\mu_{\omega^{-}}(\mathrm{d}x)\mathbf{P}(\mathrm{d}\omega^{-})\\
&=(\mathcal{Q}_{t})_{\star}\mu(A\times C)=\int_{\mathcal{B}}\int_{\mathcal{X}}\int_{\mathcal{B}}{\chi}_{C}\left(P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})\right){\chi}_{A}(\varphi^{t}_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}(x))\mathbf{P}(\mathrm{d}\omega^{+})\mu_{\omega^{-}}(\mathrm{d}x)\mathbf{P}(d\omega^{-})\\
&=\int_{\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{B}}\int_{\mathcal{X}}{\chi}_{C}\left(P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})\right){\chi}_{A}(\varphi^{t}_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}(x))\mu_{\omega^{-}}(\mathrm{d}x)\ (\mathbf{P}\times\mathbf{P})(\mathrm{d}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})).\end{split}

(3.9)

We set

\displaystyle\tilde{R}(\omega^{-})=R(\omega^{-},\omega^{+}):=\int_{\mathcal{X}}{\chi}_{A}(\varphi^{t}_{\omega^{-}}(x)){\chi}_{C}(\omega^{-})\mu_{\vartheta_{t}\omega^{-}}(\mathrm{d}x).

Thus, from (
3.9
), Lemma
2.1
and (
3.3
), we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\mathcal{B}}{\chi}_{C}(\omega^{-})\int_{\mathcal{X}}{\chi}_{A}(x)\mu_{\omega^{-}}(\mathrm{d}x)\mathbf{P}(d\omega^{-})=\int_{\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{B}}R(\theta_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+}))(\mathbf{P}\times\mathbf{P})\mathrm{d}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})\\
&\hskip 10.00002pt=\int_{\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{B}}R(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})(\mathbf{P}\times\mathbf{P})\mathrm{d}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})=\int_{\mathcal{B}}{\chi}_{C}(\omega^{-})\int_{\mathcal{X}}{\chi}_{A}(\varphi^{t}_{\omega^{-}}(x))\mu_{\vartheta_{t}\omega^{-}}(\mathrm{d}x)\mathbf{P}({\textnormal{d}}\omega^{-})\\
&\hskip 20.00003pt=\int_{\mathcal{B}}{\chi}_{C}(\omega^{-})\int_{\mathcal{X}}{\chi}_{A}(x)(\varphi^{t}_{\omega^{-}})_{\star}(\mu_{\vartheta_{t}\omega^{-}})(\mathrm{d}x)\mathbf{P}(d\omega^{-}).\end{split}

(3.10)

Since
A\times C
was chosen arbitrarily, it follows from statetement 4) of Lemma
3.8
that on a set of full measure
\mathcal{B}_{t}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
, we have

\displaystyle(\varphi^{t}_{\omega^{-}})_{\star}(\mu_{\vartheta_{t}\omega^{-}})=\mu_{\omega^{-}}.

Remark 3.10

Let
t\geq 0
be fixed. Then, for every
C\in\sigma(\mathcal{B})
, from
(IV)
and the Fubini theorem, we have

\displaystyle(P_{t})_{\star}(\mathbf{P}\times\mathbf{P})(C)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}\times\mathbf{P}\left\{(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})\in\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{B}:\ P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})\in C\right)\}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{P}_{t}(\omega^{-},C)\mathbf{P}(\mathrm{d}\omega^{-})=\mathbf{P}(C).

Therefore, we can find a set of full measure
\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_{t}
such that, for every
\omega^{-}\in\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_{t}
, there exists another set of full measure
\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_{t,\omega^{-}}
with the property that if
\omega^{+}\in\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_{t,\omega^{-}}
, then

\displaystyle P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})\in\mathcal{B}_{t}.

In particular, this yields that

\displaystyle\varphi^{t}_{(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}(\mu_{\omega^{-}})=\mu_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})},

(3.11)

for
(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})\in\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_{t}\times\tilde{\mathcal{B}}_{t,\omega^{-}}
.

As noted in the previous remark, although the invariance property (
3.11
) holds, its validity is restricted to a set that may depend on
t
and
\omega^{-}
which is contradicts the RDS framework. Moreover, these sets are not necessarily invariant under the shift map
\theta
. In the next theorem, we rigorously resolve this problem. This result can be interpreted as a perfection-type result in the theory of random dynamical systems.

Theorem 3.11

There exist a set of full measure
\tilde{\mathcal{B}}
in
\mathcal{B}
and a family of random measures

\{\tilde{\mu}_{\omega^{-}}\}_{\omega^{-}\in\mathcal{B}}
satisfying the assumptions of Lemma
3.8
(i.e., another disintegration of
\mu
), such that for every
(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})\in\tilde{\mathcal{B}}\times\mathcal{B}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})\in\tilde{\mathcal{B}},\\
&\left(\varphi_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}^{t}\right)_{\star}\tilde{\mu}_{\omega^{-}}=\tilde{\mu}_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}.\end{split}

(3.12)

Proof.

Similar to Lemma
3.9
we use the fact that the composition of Borel measurable functions is again Borel measurable. For every
\omega^{-}\in\mathcal{B}
, we set

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}_{\omega^{-}}:=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{n}(\varphi^{s}_{\omega^{-}})_{\star}\mu_{\vartheta_{s}\omega^{-}}\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle\check{\mu}_{\omega^{-}}:=\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{n}(\varphi^{s}_{\omega^{-}})_{\star}\mu_{\vartheta_{s}\omega^{-}}\mathrm{d}s,

where this means that for every
C\in\sigma(\mathcal{B})

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}_{\omega^{-}}(C)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{n}(\varphi^{s}_{\omega^{-}})_{\star}\mu_{\vartheta_{s}\omega^{-}}(C)\mathrm{d}s,

and similarly for
\check{\mu}_{\omega^{-}}
.
First, note that these Lebesque integrals are well-defined. Indeed, for every
C\in\sigma(\mathcal{X})
, the following map is jointly measurable

\displaystyle(s,\omega^{-})\longrightarrow(\varphi^{s}_{\omega^{-}})_{\star}\mu_{\vartheta_{s}\omega^{-}}(C)=\int_{\mathcal{X}}{\chi}_{C}(\varphi^{s}_{\omega^{-}}(x))\mu_{\vartheta_{s}\omega^{-}}(\mathrm{d}x).

(3.13)

To establish this, we begin with the jointly measurable function

(s,x,\omega^{-})\mapsto{\chi}_{C}(\varphi^{s}_{\omega^{-}}(x)).

We then apply the monotone class lemma
[
Fol99
, Lemma 2.35]
, together with 2) in Lemma
3.8
and the fact that
\vartheta_{s}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}
is a measurable map, to conclude the measurability of the map defined in (
3.13
); see also
[
Fol99
, Proposition 2.34 and Theorem 2.36]
.
In particular, for every
\omega^{-}\in\mathcal{B}
, the map

s\mapsto(\varphi^{s}_{\omega^{-}})_{\star}\mu_{\vartheta_{s}\omega^{-}}(C)

is measurable. Therefore, the associated integrals are well-defined. Moreover, for every
n\geq 1
, the following map

\displaystyle\omega^{-}\longrightarrow\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{n}(\varphi^{s}_{\omega^{-}})_{\star}\mu_{\vartheta_{s}\omega^{-}}(C)\mathrm{d}s

is measurable. This in particular yields that
\hat{\mu}_{\omega^{-}}(C)
and
\check{\mu}_{\omega^{-}}(C)
are measurable (since the lim sup and lim inf are taken over a countable set). Additionally, note that from (
3.9
) and (
3.10
), for every
n\geq 1
, the family of
\left\{\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{n}(\varphi^{s}_{\omega^{-}})_{\star}\mu_{\vartheta_{s}\omega^{-}}\mathrm{d}s\right\}_{\omega^{-}\in\mathcal{B}}
satisfies the assumption in Lemma
3.8
.
Therefore, by Property 4) of this lemma, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a measurable subset
\mathcal{B}^{n}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
of full measure such that, for every
\omega^{-}\in\mathcal{B}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{n}(\varphi^{s}_{\omega^{-}})_{\star}\mu_{\vartheta_{s}\omega^{-}}\,\mathrm{d}s=\mu_{\omega^{-}}.

(3.14)

In particular, by setting
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}:=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{B}^{n}
, we obtain a measurable subset of
\mathcal{B}
with full measure such that (
3.14
) holds for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and every
\omega^{-}\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
. This implies that the families
\{\hat{\mu}_{\omega^{-}}\}_{\omega^{-}\in\mathcal{B}}
and
\{\check{\mu}_{\omega^{-}}\}_{\omega^{-}\in\mathcal{B}}
also satisfy the assumptions of Lemma
3.8
. Indeed, the first two conditions in Lemma
3.8
follow immediately. For the third condition, note that, using (
3.10
) and the Fubini theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{C}\mu_{\omega^{-}}(A)\,\mathbf{P}(\mathrm{d}\omega^{-})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{n}\int_{C}(\varphi^{s}_{\omega^{-}})_{\star}\mu_{\vartheta_{s}\omega^{-}}(A)\,\mathbf{P}(\mathrm{d}\omega^{-})\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{C}\bigg(\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{n}(\varphi^{s}_{\omega^{-}})_{\star}\mu_{\vartheta_{s}\omega^{-}}\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg)(A)\mathbf{P}(\mathrm{d}\omega^{-}).

Thus, since (
3.14
) holds for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
in
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
, we can apply the dominated convergence theorem to interchange the limit and the integral. This verifies the third condition for both families
\{\hat{\mu}_{\omega^{-}}\}_{\omega^{-}\in\mathcal{B}}
and
\{\check{\mu}_{\omega^{-}}\}_{\omega^{-}\in\mathcal{B}}
, which are equal on
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
.
The uniqueness statement in Lemma
3.8
indicates that for

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{B}}:=\{\omega^{-}\in\mathcal{B}:\hat{\mu}=\check{\mu}\},

we have
\mathbf{P}(\tilde{\mathcal{B}})=1
. Note that
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\subseteq\tilde{\mathcal{B}}
. Now, we prove the for every
\omega^{-}\in\tilde{B}
,
\omega^{+}\in\mathcal{B}
and
t\geq 0

\displaystyle P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})\in\tilde{\mathcal{B}}.

(3.15)

To this aim, first note that by the cocycle property of
\varphi
together with Property
(V)
in Definition
2.5
, we have

\varphi^{t+s}_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}=\varphi^{t}_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}\circ\varphi^{s}_{\omega^{-}}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{n}(\varphi^{t+s}_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})})_{\star}\mu_{\vartheta_{s}\omega^{-}}(C)\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{n}(\varphi^{s}_{\omega^{-}})_{\star}\mu_{\vartheta_{s}\omega^{-}}\left(\left(\varphi_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}^{t}\right)^{-1}(C)\right)\mathrm{d}s.

Since
\left(\varphi_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}^{t}\right)^{-1}(C)\in\sigma(\mathcal{X})
and
\omega^{-}\in\tilde{\mathcal{B}}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{n}(\varphi^{t+s}_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})})_{\star}\mu_{\vartheta_{s}\omega^{-}}(C)\mathrm{d}s,\ \ \ \text{exists}.

(3.16)

Note that due to (
2.5
) we have
\vartheta_{t+s}P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})=\vartheta_{s}P_{t}(\vartheta_{t}\omega_{-},P_{t}(\omega_{+},\omega_{-}))=\vartheta_{s}\omega^{-}
. This implies that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{n}(\varphi^{t+s}_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})})_{\star}\mu_{\vartheta_{s}\omega^{-}}(C)\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{n}(\varphi^{t+s}_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})})_{\star}\mu_{\vartheta_{t+s}P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}(C)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{t}^{n+t}(\varphi^{s}_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})})_{\star}\mu_{\vartheta_{s}P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}(C)\mathrm{d}s.

Therefore, form (
3.16
)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{n}(\varphi^{s}_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})})_{\star}\mu_{\vartheta_{s}P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}(C)\mathrm{d}s\ \ \ \text{exists}.

This yields (
3.15
). In particular

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}&\int_{0}^{n}(\varphi^{t+s}_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})})_{\star}\mu_{\vartheta_{s}\omega^{-}}(C)\mathrm{d}s=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{n}(\varphi^{s}_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})})_{\star}\mu_{\vartheta_{s}P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}(C)\mathrm{d}s\\
&=\check{\mu}_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}(C)=\hat{\mu}_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}(C).\end{split}

(3.17)

Now we prove that for
(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})\in\tilde{\mathcal{B}}\times\mathcal{B}
and
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left(\varphi_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}^{t}\right)_{\star}\hat{\mu}_{\omega^{-}}&=\hat{\mu}_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}\\
&=\left(\varphi_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}^{t}\right)_{\star}\check{\mu}_{\omega^{-}}=\check{\mu}_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}.\end{split}

(3.18)

First, note that by definition

\displaystyle\left(\varphi_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}^{t}\right)_{\star}\hat{\mu}_{\omega^{-}}(C)=\hat{\mu}_{\omega^{-}}\left(\left(\varphi_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}^{t}\right)^{-1}(C)\right)=

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{n}(\varphi^{s}_{\omega^{-}})_{\star}\mu_{\vartheta_{s}\omega}\left(\left(\varphi_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}^{t}\right)^{-1}(C)\right)\mathrm{d}s=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{n}(\varphi^{t+s}_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})})_{\star}\mu_{\vartheta_{s}\omega^{-}}(C)\mathrm{d}s.

The same arguments apply to
\check{\mu}
as well. Therefore, together with (
3.17
), we can deduce (
3.18
).

Now we can finish the proof by setting either
\tilde{\mu}_{\omega^{-}}=\hat{\mu}_{\omega^{-}}
or
\tilde{\mu}_{\omega^{-}}=\check{\mu}_{\omega^{-}}
for every

4
Stochastic differential equations driven by fractional Brownian motion

The aim of this section is to show how to obtain a stochastic dynamical system and thereafter a random dynamical system from stochastic differential equations driven by fractional Brownian motion. More precisely, we consider the following stochastic differential equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}Y_{t}=F(Y_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{t},\\
Y_{0}=x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{cases}

(4.1)

where
d\geq 1
and
(B^{H}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
denotes a
d

H\in(0,1)
.

Assumption 4.1

We make the following assumptions on the coefficients of (
4.1
).

1)

Let
\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an invertible matrix.

2)

For the vector field

, we assume the existence of constants

for

, such that for all

\displaystyle\langle F(\xi_{2})-F(\xi_{1}),\xi_{2}-\xi_{1}\rangle\leq\min\left\{C_{1}^{F}-C_{2}^{F}|\xi_{2}-\xi_{1}|^{2},C_{3}^{F}|\xi_{2}-\xi_{1}|^{2}\right\}.

3)

We further assume that
F
is differentiable and satisfies the following polynomial growth condition for some constant

and

\displaystyle|F(\xi)|\leq C_{F}(1+|\xi|)^{N},\quad|\mathrm{D}_{\xi}F|\leq C_{F}(1+|\xi|)^{N}\quad\text{for all }\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

4)

If

, we assume that the derivative of

is globally bounded.

4.1
Well-posedness and the generation of a stochastic dynamical system

For the sake of completeness, we first recall the solution concept of (

Definition 4.2

(

The SDE (

Lemma 4.4

4.2
Two approaches for the generation of RDS for SDEs with additive fractional noise

Let us now revisit the definition of a classical random dynamical system arising from the SDE (
4.1
) and compare it to the RDS constructed in Corollary
4.8
. As already stated, in the RDS framework one incorporates the future of the noise, while the SDS considers the past of the noise. In the following, we make this more precise.
The classical construction of an RDS associated with the SDE (
4.1
) proceeds as follows.

1.

One considers
\tilde{\Omega}:=C_{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{d})
endowed with the compact open topology and
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{B}(C_{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{d}))
. It is well-known that one can define a Gaussian measure
\mathbb{P}
such that the associated canonical process is a two-sided fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter
H\in(0,1)
. For every
\tilde{\omega}\in\tilde{\Omega}
and

, we define the measure-preserving transformation
\theta_{t}:\tilde{\Omega}\to\tilde{\Omega}

\displaystyle\theta_{t}\tilde{\omega}(s):=\tilde{\omega}(t+s)-\tilde{\omega}(t).

Moreover,
(\tilde{\Omega},\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},(\theta_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}))
is an ergodic metric dynamical system.

2.

Let
T>0
be fixed and let
\tilde{\omega}_{[0,T]}
denote the restriction of
\tilde{\omega}
to the interval
[0,T]
. For every
t\in[0,T]
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, define

\Psi^{t}_{\tilde{\omega}}(x):=\tilde{\Phi}_{T}(t,x,\tilde{\omega}_{[0,T]}).

In this setting, due to the uniqueness of the solution, one can verify the cocycle property, i.e. for all
t,s\geq 0

\Psi^{t+s}_{\tilde{\omega}}(x)=\Psi^{s}_{\theta_{t}\tilde{\omega}}\circ\Psi^{t}_{\tilde{\omega}}(x).

For the existence of RDS for S(P)DEs with additive fractional noise as sketched above, we refer to
[
MS04a
,
FGS17b
]
.
In the SDS setting, as seen above we consider the space
\Omega:=\mathcal{B}_{H}\times\mathcal{B}_{H}
and have that

\displaystyle\Psi^{t}_{(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}(x)=\varphi^{t}_{P_{t}(\omega^{-},\omega^{+})}(x),

where
\varphi
is the SDS associated to the SDE (
4.1
).

We mention some advantages of the formulation of RDS given by Corollary
4.8
which are exploited in this work.

1.

We are working with the space
\mathcal{B}_{H}
which can be canonically equipped with the Wiener measure
\mathbf{P}
and therefore incorporates Markovian dynamics.

2.

In order to compute Lyapunov exponents, we need ergodicity and information about the density of the invariant measure of (

5
Lyapunov exponents

In this section we prove that by increasing the intensity of the noise, we can ensure that the Lyapunov exponent of (

[

[

]

We consider the same SDE (

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\mathrm{d}Y_{t}^{\sigma}=F(Y_{t}^{\sigma})\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{t}\\
&Y_{0}^{\sigma}=x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{cases}

where we additionally keep track of the dependence of the solution

on the noise intensity

We first specify some assumptions.

Assumption 5.1

We assume that
F\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and that its derivative is globally bounded. Moreover,

is eventually strictly monotone, i.e. there exist constants

and

such that, for all

(5.2)

As before,

is an invertible matrix.

Remark 5.2

Note that Assumption
5.1
implies Assumption
4.1
. In particular

\displaystyle\langle F(\xi_{2})-F(\xi_{1}),\xi_{2}-\xi_{1}\rangle\leq\min\left\{C_{1}^{F}-C_{2}^{F}|\xi_{2}-\xi_{1}|^{2},\,C_{3}^{F}|\xi_{2}-\xi_{1}|^{2}\right\}

and

\displaystyle|F(\xi)|\leq C_{F}(1+|\xi|),\quad|{\textnormal{D}}_{\xi}F|\leq C_{F},

for all

) admits a unique invariant measure.

We further specify a restriction on

.

Definition 5.3

Let us consider the singular value decomposition of

as

\sigma=U\Sigma V^{T},

where

are orthogonal matrices, and

Then, for every

Note that in this case

and

. For

Throughout this section, the parameters

and

are fixed.

5.2
Negativity of the top Lyapunov exponent

The main goal of this subsection is to track the dependence of the measure
\pi^{\sigma}
(
\tilde{\pi}^{\sigma}
) on the parameter
\sigma
. As previously mentioned, this measure admits a density with respect to the Lebesgue measure. Our main contribution is to analyze how this density varies with respect to
\sigma
. More precisely, under Assumption
5.1
, we show that the top Lyapunov exponent of the SDE (
5.1
) becomes negative as the intensity of the noise
\sigma
increases. We first estimate the top Lyapunov exponent in terms of the invariant measure
\pi^{\sigma}
.

Lemma 5.10

Let
\lambda_{1}^{\sigma}
denote the top Lyapunov exponent of the SDE (
5.1
), as defined in Proposition
4.13
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
, depending only on the drift term
F
, such that

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{\sigma}\leq-C_{4}^{F}\pi^{\sigma}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(0,R)\right)+C\pi^{\sigma}\left(B(0,R)\right).

Proof.

Let
\Phi
denote the RDS associated with the SDE (
5.1
). From (
4.11
), it follows that for every
\tilde{\omega}=(\tilde{\omega}^{-},\tilde{\omega}^{+})\in\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{B}
and for all
x_{1},x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left|{\textnormal{D}}_{x_{1}}\Phi^{t}_{\tilde{\omega}}(x_{0})\right|^{2}=2\left\langle D_{\Phi^{t}_{\tilde{\omega}}(x_{1})}F\left(\frac{{\textnormal{D}}_{x_{1}}\Phi^{t}_{\tilde{\omega}}(x_{0})}{\left|{\textnormal{D}}_{x_{1}}\Phi^{t}_{\tilde{\omega}}(x_{0})\right|}\right),\frac{{\textnormal{D}}_{x_{1}}\Phi^{t}_{\tilde{\omega}}(x_{0})}{\left|{\textnormal{D}}_{x_{1}}\Phi^{t}_{\tilde{\omega}}(x_{0})\right|}\right\rangle\left|{\textnormal{D}}_{x_{1}}\Phi^{t}_{\tilde{\omega}}(x_{0})\right|^{2}.\end{split}

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left|{\textnormal{D}}_{x_{1}}\Phi^{t}_{\tilde{\omega}}(x_{0})\right|^{2}=2|x_{0}|^{2}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle D_{\Phi^{s}_{\tilde{\omega}}(x_{1})}F\left(\frac{{\textnormal{D}}_{x_{1}}\Phi^{s}_{\tilde{\omega}}(x_{0})}{\left|{\textnormal{D}}_{x_{1}}\Phi^{s}_{\tilde{\omega}}(x_{0})\right|}\right),\frac{{\textnormal{D}}_{x_{1}}\Phi^{s}_{\tilde{\omega}}(x_{0})}{\left|{\textnormal{D}}_{x_{1}}\Phi^{s}_{\tilde{\omega}}(x_{0})\right|}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}s\right).

From Proposition
4.13
, there exists
x_{0}\neq 0
such that for
\mu^{\sigma}\times\mathbf{P}
-almost every
(x_{1},\tilde{\omega})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{B}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lambda_{1}^{\sigma}&=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle D_{\Phi^{s}_{\tilde{\omega}}(x_{1})}F\left(\frac{({\textnormal{D}}_{x_{1}}\Phi^{s}_{\tilde{\omega}}(x_{0})}{\left|{\textnormal{D}}_{x_{1}}\Phi^{s}_{\tilde{\omega}}(x_{0})\right|}\right),\frac{({\textnormal{D}}_{x_{1}}\Phi^{s}_{\tilde{\omega}}(x_{0})}{\left|{\textnormal{D}}_{x_{1}}\Phi^{s}_{\tilde{\omega}}(x_{0})\right|}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\lambda^{+}(\Phi^{s}_{\tilde{\omega}}(x_{1}))\mathrm{d}s,\end{split}

(5.9)

where
\lambda^{+}(y):=\max_{|v|=1}\langle{\textnormal{D}}_{y}F(v),v\rangle
.
We define the function

\displaystyle\overline{\lambda}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{B}\to\mathbb{R},\quad\overline{\lambda}(x,\omega):=\lambda^{+}(x).

Thanks to Assumptions
5.1
, one can see that
\overline{\lambda}
is continuous and bounded.
From Corollary
3.6
, the family
\{\Theta_{t}\}_{t>0}
is ergodic with respect to the measure
\mu^{\sigma}\times\mathbf{P}
. Therefore, by (

), the monotone convergence theorem, and Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem, we conclude that for
\mu^{\sigma}\times\mathbf{P}
-almost every
(x_{1},\tilde{\omega})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{B}

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\overline{\lambda}(\Theta_{s}(x_{1},\tilde{\omega}))\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{B}}\overline{\lambda}(x,\omega)\,\mu^{\sigma}\times\mathbf{P}\left(\mathrm{d}(x,\omega^{-},\omega^{+})\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lambda^{+}(x)\,\pi^{\sigma}(\mathrm{d}x).

Consequently,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lambda_{1}^{\sigma}&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(0,R)}\lambda^{+}(x)\pi^{\sigma}(\mathrm{d}x)+\int_{B(0,R)}\lambda^{+}(x)\pi^{\sigma}(\mathrm{d}x)\\

(5.10)

where we used (

Therefore to make the first Lyapunov exponent
\lambda^{\sigma}_{1}
negative, it is sufficient to prove that as
\frac{1}{\|\sigma^{-1}\|}
grows, not too much mass of
\pi^{\sigma}
concentrates on

. We provide a well-known example that motivates this phenomenon.

Example 5.11

Recall that the fractional Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process is defined as

\tilde{x}_{t}^{\sigma}=\sigma\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)}\,\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{s},

where
(B^{H}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a fractional Brownian motion. This is a Gaussian process whose covariance matrix is given by

2H\exp(-t)\sigma\sigma^{T}\int_{0}^{t}s^{2H-1}\cosh(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Therefore, the density of the law
\mathcal{L}(\tilde{x}_{t}^{\sigma})
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
becomes flatter as
\|\sigma^{-1}\|
increases. In particular, its probability mass within any bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
becomes small. This is precisely what we aim to prove for the measure
\pi^{\sigma}
.

Let us briefly outline the strategy we will follow to show that, by increasing
\frac{1}{\|\sigma^{-1}\|}
, the Lyapunov exponent
\lambda^{\sigma}_{1}
becomes negative. According to Lemma
5.10
, it is sufficient to show that

\displaystyle\lim_{\|\sigma\|\rightarrow\infty}\pi^{\sigma}\left(B(0,R)\right)=0.

(5.11)

•

Step 1)
To prove this we work with the rescaled SDE (
5.3
). Thus, from Lemma (
5.8

\displaystyle\lim_{\|\sigma\|\rightarrow\infty}\tilde{\pi}^{\sigma}\left(\|\sigma\|^{-1}B(0,R)\right)=0.

(5.12)

•

Step 2)
We derive a uniform bound for the density of (
5.3
) when
\sigma
belongs to
T_{\theta,\kappa}
for every arbitrary but fixed value of

. To achieve this, we follow the approach in
[
LPS23
]
keeping track of the dependence of
\tilde{\pi}^{\sigma}
on
\sigma
.

Notation 5.12

Recall that

For simplicity, we write

We now focus on the density of the SDE (

Lemma 5.13

There exists a constant

Recall that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\mathrm{d}Z_{t}^{\sigma}=F^{\sigma}(Z_{t}^{\sigma})\,\mathrm{d}t+\,\|\sigma\|^{-1}\sigma\mathrm{d}B^{H}_{t}\\
&Z_{0}^{\sigma}=0\end{cases}

The goal is to obtain a bound for

in terms of a fractional Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process that is independent of
\sigma
. Let

denote the solution to the linear SDE (

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}|Z_{t}^{\sigma}-X_{t}^{\sigma}|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\left\langle F\left(X_{t}^{\sigma}+\left(Z_{t}^{\sigma}-X_{t}^{\sigma}\right)\right)-F(X_{t}^{\sigma}),Z_{t}^{\sigma}-X_{t}^{\sigma}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+2\langle F(X_{t}^{\sigma})+X_{t}^{\sigma},Z_{t}^{\sigma}-X_{t}^{\sigma}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{1}^{F}-2C_{2}^{F}\left|Z_{t}^{\sigma}-X_{t}^{\sigma}\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{C_{2}^{F}}\left|X_{t}^{\sigma}+F^{\sigma}(X_{t}^{\sigma})\right|^{2}+C_{2}^{F}\left|Z_{t}^{\sigma}-X_{t}^{\sigma}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-C_{2}^{F}\left|Z_{t}^{\sigma}-X_{t}^{\sigma}\right|^{2}+2C_{1}^{F}+\frac{1}{C_{2}^{F}}\left|X_{t}^{\sigma}+F^{\sigma}(X_{t}^{\sigma})\right|^{2}.

By Grönwall’s inequality and the growth condition
|F(X^{\sigma}_{t})|\leq C_{F}(1+|X^{\sigma}_{t}|)
, we conclude that there exist constants
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
depending on
F
(and also on
\kappa
assumed here to be equal to one for simplicity) such that

\displaystyle|Z_{t}^{\sigma}-X_{t}^{\sigma}|

\displaystyle\leq A_{1}+A_{2}{\sqrt{\int_{0}^{t}\exp\left(-C_{2}^{F}(t-\tau)\right)|X_{\tau}^{\sigma}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau}}.

(5.14)

For
C>0
and
\bar{X}\in C([0,t],\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we define the following norm

\|\bar{X}\|_{\mathcal{N}_{C}[0,t]}:=\left(\int_{0}^{t}\exp\bigl(-C(t-\tau)\bigr)\,|\bar{X}_{\tau}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right)^{\!1/2}.

Then, for
C=C_{2}^{F}
, it is clear that

\|X^{\sigma}\|_{\mathcal{N}_{C_{2}^{F}}[0,t]}\leq\|X^{I}\|_{\mathcal{N}_{C_{2}^{F}}[0,t]}.

Hence, by (
5.7
), it follows that for
Z_{t}^{\sigma}=Z_{t,0}^{\sigma}
and for every
\mathcal{R}>2A_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\left(\left|Z_{t}^{\sigma}\right|\geq\mathcal{R}\right)&\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\left|Z_{t}^{\sigma}-X_{t}^{\sigma}\right|\geq\frac{\mathcal{R}}{2}\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\left|X_{t}^{\sigma}\right|\geq\frac{\mathcal{R}}{2}\right)\\
&\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\|X^{I}\|_{\mathcal{N}_{C_{2}^{F}}[0,t]}\geq\frac{\frac{\mathcal{R}}{2}-A_{1}}{A_{2}}\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\left|X_{t}^{I}\right|\geq\frac{\mathcal{R}}{2}\right).\end{split}

(5.15)

Since
X^{I}
is a fractional Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process and hence Gaussian, it follows from
[
LPS23
, Lemma 2.7]
that there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
, independent of
\sigma
, such that

\displaystyle\max\left\{\sup_{t\geq 0}\mathbb{P}\left(\left\|X^{I}\right\|_{\mathcal{N}_{C_{2}^{F}}[0,t]}\geq\mathcal{R}\right),\ \sup_{t\geq 0}\mathbb{P}\left(\left|X_{t}^{I}\right|\geq\frac{\mathcal{R}}{2}\right)\right\}\leq C_{1}\exp(-C_{2}\mathcal{R}^{2}).

(5.16)

From (
5.7
), it follows that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}\!\left(\left|Z_{t}^{\sigma}\right|\geq\mathcal{R}\right)=\tilde{\pi}^{\sigma}\!\left(y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|y|\geq\mathcal{R}\right)\leq C_{1}\exp(-C_{2}\mathcal{R}^{2}),

proving the claim.

&\hskip 20.00003pt\ignorespaces\lesssim 1+t_{0}^{\eta}\left\|l\right\|_{C^{0,H-\frac{1}{2}+\eta}([0,{t_{0}}])}+t_{0}^{\eta}\left\|\mathcal{J}_{+}^{H-\frac{1}{2}}X^{z,t_{0}}\right\|_{C^{0,H-\frac{1}{2}+\eta}([0,{t_{0}}])}.\end{split}

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left\|\frac{\left\|\sigma\right\|\sigma^{-1}}{\rho_{H}}\left(\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}-H}F^{\sigma}\left(l+\frac{\rho_{H}}{\left\|\sigma\right\|}\sigma\mathcal{J}_{+}^{H-\frac{1}{2}}X^{z,t_{0}}\right)\right)\right\|_{\infty;[0,t_{0}]}\\

Theorem 5.30

Then for every

From Lemma
5.10

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{\sigma}\leq-C_{4}^{F}\pi^{\sigma}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(0,R)\right)+C\pi^{\sigma}\left(B(0,R)\right).

(5.61)

As already stated, we have to prove that increasing
\sigma

\pi^{\sigma}\big(B(0,R)\big)

\displaystyle\pi^{\sigma}\left(B(0,R)\right)=\tilde{\pi}^{\sigma}\left((\|\sigma\|^{-1}B(0,R)\right).

Since
\tilde{\pi}^{\sigma}
has the density function
\tilde{p}^{\sigma}_{\infty}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\pi^{\sigma}\big(B(0,R)\big)=\int_{\|\sigma\|^{-1}B(0,R)}\tilde{p}^{\sigma}_{\infty}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

Obviously as
\|\sigma\|

\|\sigma\|^{-1}B(0,R)
remains bounded, while its Lebesgue measure decreases to zero.
Moreover, for
\kappa_{1}>\kappa_{2}
we have the inclusion

\displaystyle T_{\theta,\kappa_{1}}\subset T_{\theta,\kappa_{2}}.

Thus, when
\theta\geq 1

become smaller. Consequently, Proposition
5.29

Appendix A
Auxiliary Lemma

The following elementary lemma is needed in the proof of Theorem
5.29
.

Lemma A.1

Let
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
|\xi|\leq M
. Then, for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant
\epsilon^{M}>0
such that for every
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle-|\xi+\zeta|^{2}\leq\epsilon|\zeta|^{2}-\epsilon^{M}|\xi|^{2}.

Proof.

The proof is straightforward, so we only outline the main idea. We proceed by contradiction. Assume that for some fixed
\epsilon>0
, and for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a pair
(\xi^{n},\zeta^{n})
such that
|\xi^{n}|\leq M
and

\displaystyle-|\xi^{n}+\zeta^{n}|^{2}>\epsilon|\zeta^{n}|^{2}-\frac{1}{n}|\xi^{n}|^{2}.

Then, thanks to the uniform boundedness of
(\xi^{n})_{n\geq 1}
, we can conclude that the sequence is precompact and obtain a contradiction.

Appendix B
Fractional calculus

In this section, we collect technical results related to fractional calculus which are used in Section
5
.

Definition B.1

Let
0<\alpha<1
. The
right-sided Riemann-Liouville fractional integral
is defined by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}_{+}:L^{1}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})&\longrightarrow L^{1}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d}),\\
(\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}_{+}f)(t)&:=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}f(s)(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\,\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

(B.1)

Moreover, it holds that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}_{+}f\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\frac{T^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(B.2)

It is well known that
\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}_{+}
increases the Hölder regularity of a function. More precisely, we have the following statement.

Theorem B.2

Let
\beta\in[0,1]
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that
\alpha+\beta\neq 1
. Then, the operator

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha}_{+}:C^{0,\beta}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow C^{\lfloor\alpha+\beta\rfloor,\,\alpha+\beta-\lfloor\alpha+\beta\rfloor}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d}),

\displaystyle(\tilde{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha}_{+}f)(t)

\displaystyle:=(\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}_{+}f)(t)-\frac{f(0)}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}t^{\alpha},

is continuous. In particular, there exists a constant
C_{\alpha,\beta}>0
such that for
t\in[0,T]
and every
f\in C^{0,\beta}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have

\big|(\tilde{\mathcal{J}}^{\alpha}_{+}f)(t)\big|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}\,t^{\alpha+\beta}\|f\|_{C^{0,\beta}([0,T])}.

Proof.

[
SKM93
, Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 1 in Chapter 1]
.

This entails the following corollary.

Corollary B.3

Let
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1)
and
\alpha+\beta<1
. Then there exists a constant
C_{\alpha,\beta}>0
such that for all
f\in C^{\beta}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
f(0)=0
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{J}^{\alpha}_{+}f\|_{\alpha+\beta;[0,T]}\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}\|f\|_{\beta;[0,T]}.

The right-sided Riemann-Liouville fractional derivative can be defined as follows

Definition B.4

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and define the domain

D(\mathcal{J}^{-\alpha}_{+}):=\big\{f\in L^{1}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d}):\mathcal{J}_{+}^{1-\alpha}f\in AC([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})\big\},

where
AC([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
denotes the space of absolutely continuous functions on
[0,T]
.
For any
f\in D(\mathcal{J}^{-\alpha}_{+})
, the
right-sided Riemann-Liouville fractional derivative
of order
\alpha
is defined for almost every
t\in[0,T]
by

(\mathcal{J}^{-\alpha}_{+}f)(t):=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{J}_{+}^{1-\alpha}f(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{0}^{t}f(s)(t-s)^{-\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}s.

From Theorem
B.2
we derive the following result.

Lemma B.5

For every
\beta\in(\alpha,1]
, we have the inclusion

C^{0,\beta}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset D(\mathcal{J}^{-\alpha}_{+}).

Moreover, there exists a
(\beta-\alpha)
-Hölder continuous function
\tilde{\mathcal{J}}^{-\alpha}_{+}f
and a constant
{C}_{\alpha,\beta}>0
such that for every
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(\mathcal{J}^{-\alpha}_{+}f)(t)=\tilde{\mathcal{J}}^{-\alpha}_{+}f(t)+\frac{f(0)(1-\alpha)}{\Gamma(2-\alpha)}t^{-\alpha},\\
&\left\|\tilde{\mathcal{J}}^{-\alpha}_{+}f\right\|_{C^{0,\beta-\alpha}([0,T])}\leq{C}_{\alpha,\beta}\|f\|_{C^{0,\beta}([0,T])}.\end{split}

(B.3)

Furthermore, the following estimate holds:

\displaystyle\left\|\tilde{\mathcal{J}}^{-\alpha}_{+}f\right\|_{\infty;[0,T]}\leq\tilde{C}_{\alpha,\beta}T^{\beta-\alpha}\|f\|_{C^{0,\beta}([0,T])},

(B.4)

for some constant
\tilde{C}_{\alpha,\beta}>0
.

Proof.

Since
\beta+1-\alpha>1
, Theorem
B.2
implies that

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{1-\alpha}_{+}f\in C^{1,\,\beta-\alpha}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d}),

and consequently,
f\in D(\mathcal{J}^{-\alpha}_{+})
. Again, from Theorem
B.2
, we have the decomposition

\displaystyle(\mathcal{J}^{1-\alpha}_{+}f)(t)=\tilde{\mathcal{J}}^{1-\alpha}_{+}f(t)+\frac{f(0)}{\Gamma(2-\alpha)}t^{1-\alpha},

(B.5)

where the function
\tilde{\mathcal{J}}^{1-\alpha}_{+}f
is such that its derivative

\tilde{\mathcal{J}}^{-\alpha}_{+}f(t):=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\tilde{\mathcal{J}}^{1-\alpha}_{+}f(t)

is Hölder continuous of order
\beta-\alpha
, and

\displaystyle|(\tilde{\mathcal{J}}^{1-\alpha}_{+}f)(t)|\leq C_{1-\alpha,\beta}\,t^{1-\alpha+\beta}\|f\|_{C^{0,\beta}([0,T])}.

This implies that
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big(\tilde{\mathcal{J}}^{1-\alpha}_{+}f\big)(0)=0
.
Differentiating the decomposition in (
B.5
) yields

(\mathcal{J}^{-\alpha}_{+}f)(t)=\tilde{\mathcal{J}}^{-\alpha}_{+}f(t)+\frac{f(0)(1-\alpha)}{\Gamma(2-\alpha)}t^{-\alpha}.

The second estimate in (

) follows directly from the regularity result in Theorem
B.2
applied to
\mathcal{J}^{1-\alpha}_{+}
. Moreover, the bound in (
B.4
) follows directly from (
B.3
), using the fact that
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big(\tilde{\mathcal{J}}^{1-\alpha}_{+}f\big)(0)=0
.

Inflation without an Inflaton
Daniele Bertacca, Raul Jimenez, Sabino Matarrese, Angelo Ricciardone

Abstract
We propose a novel scenario in which scalar perturbations, that seed the large scale structure of the Universe, are generated without relying on a scalar field (the inflaton). In this framework, inflation is driven by a de Sitter space-time (dS), where tensor metric fluctuations (i.e., gravitational waves) naturally arise from quantum vacuum oscillations, and scalar fluctuations are generated via second-order tensor effects. We compute the power spectrum of such scalar fluctuations and show it to be consistent with near scale-invariance. We derive the necessary conditions under which scalar perturbations become significant and much larger than the tensor modes, and we identify a natural mechanism to end inflation via a transition to a radiation-dominated phase. Our proposed mechanism could remove the need for a model-dependent scenario: the choice of a scalar field, as the inflaton, to drive inflation.

If the quantum picture of the dS metric can be described as a Hamiltonian process of scattering and decay of the gravitons composing the coherent state of gravitons, then the self-coupling of gravitons and their coupling with other relativistic particle species leads to metric processes quantum scattering and decay of the constituent gravitons of dS. In this case, the final quantum state cannot be described with coherent states and there will no longer be the dispersion relations of the free quanta propagating on a classical dS background. As pointed out in

[

This occurs because the geometry of the gravitational field is coupled to the energy-momentum tensor and this quantity, in turn, governs the dynamics of the gravitational field. Consequently, the quantum fluctuations of the vacuum or, equivalently, the effects of particle creation are linked to a background gravitational field.
If we use thermodynamic considerations, see
[

Indeed, considering a small fluctuation in the Hawking temperature of the horizon—similar to the case of a black hole—leads to a minor net heat exchange between the inner region and both the horizon and its surroundings.
The entire space is unstable to quantum/thermal fluctuations in its Hawking temperature, nucleating a sort of vacuum bubble at an arbitrary point, breaking global dS invariance
[

The aim of this letter is to introduce this novel mechanism and its main predictions. We derive the exact expressions for the second-order scalar potentials and the scalar power spectrum resulting from second-order tensor perturbations. We demonstrate that the latter agrees with the expected nearly scale-invariance from observations, opening the way for numerous potential follow-up studies and extensions.

II
Scalar perturbations from tensor modes

We work on a pure dS metric, which is defined in d-dimensions by the hyperboloid
(dS)_{\alpha}=-X_{0}^{2}+X_{1}^{2}+...+X_{d}^{2}
and in Cartesian coordinates,
ds^{2}=dt^{2}-e^{2t/\alpha}(dx^{2}+dy^{2}+dz^{2})

where
\alpha\equiv(3/\Lambda)^{1/2}
and
\Lambda
is the vacuum energy. We assume Einstein gravity.

For generality, on the RHS of Einstein’s equations, we allow for the presence of a stress-energy tensor which accounts for the sum of the cosmological constant driving our dS expansion plus a generic fluid, with energy density
\rho
, isotropic pressure
p
, four-velocity
u^{\mu}
and anisotropic stress tensor
\pi_{\nu}^{\mu}
, namely

T^{\mu}_{\nu}=-{\frac{\Lambda}{8\pi G}}\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}+(\rho+p)u^{\mu}u_{\nu}+p\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}+\pi^{\mu}_{\nu}\,,

(1)

As we will see below, the considered fluid unavoidably arises from
the vacuum expectation value of the second-order contribution to the Einstein’s tensor from gravitational waves (GW), which on sub-horizon scales leads to non-vanishing energy, pressure and anisotropic stress. Considering only the tensor contribution we have, in the comoving frame of such a GW fluid,

u^{\mu}=\frac{1}{a}\left(\delta^{\mu}_{0}+\frac{1}{2}v^{\mu}_{2}\right)\,,

(2)

from which we can compute
u_{\mu}
as:

\displaystyle u_{0}=g_{0\rho}u^{\rho}=a\left(-1-\frac{1}{2}\psi_{2}\right);u_{i}=\frac{1}{2}a\omega_{2i}+a\left(\frac{1}{2}v_{2i}\right)\,.

(3)

For the 00 component at second order we find

\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2}T^{0}_{0}=-\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2}\rho\,.

(4)

The other components of
T^{\mu}_{\nu}
are given by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2}T^{i}_{0}=-(1+w)\bar{\rho}\left[\frac{1}{2}v_{2}^{i}\right]\,,

(5)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2}T^{i}_{j}=+\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2}p\delta^{i}_{j}+\frac{1}{a^{2}}\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2}\pi^{i}_{j}\,,

(6)

where
w
is the equation of state at the background level and the anisotropic stress tensor can be decomposed into a trace-free scalar part,

\Pi
, a vector part,
\Pi_{i}
, and a tensor part,
\Pi_{ij}
, at each
order according to

\pi_{ij}=a^{2}\left[\Pi_{,ij}-\frac{1}{3}\nabla^{2}\Pi\delta_{ij}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\Pi_{i,j}+\Pi_{j,i}\right)+\Pi_{ij}\right]\;.

(7)

\frac{1}{a^{2}}\delta^{2}\pi_{ij}=\left[\Pi_{2,ij}-\frac{1}{3}\nabla^{2}\Pi_{2}\delta_{ij}+\frac{1}{3}\left(\Pi_{2i,j}+\Pi_{2j,i}\right)+\Pi_{2ij}\right]

(8)

we can split the peculiar velocity
v_{2}^{i}
as

v_{2i}=v_{2,i}+v_{2i}^{\perp}
, where
v_{2i}^{\perp}
is a solenoidal (divergence-free or transverse) vector.

Considering a graviton perturbation, with generic
w
and
c_{s}

\delta^{2}p=c_{s}^{2}\delta^{2}\rho
and manipulating the Einstein equations, we find the potential equation for
\phi_{2}
, i.e.

\displaystyle\phi_{2}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle+3\left(1+c_{s}^{2}\right)\mathcal{H}\phi_{2}^{\prime}+\left[2\mathcal{H}^{\prime}+\left(1+3c_{s}^{2}\right)\mathcal{H}^{2}\right]\phi_{2}-c_{s}^{2}\nabla^{2}\phi_{2}=8\pi Ga^{2}\left[2\mathcal{H}^{\prime}+3\left(1+c_{s}^{2}\right)\mathcal{H}^{2}\right]\Pi_{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{8\pi G}{3}a^{2}\nabla^{2}\Pi_{2}+8\pi Ga^{2}\mathcal{H}\Pi_{2}^{\prime}-\frac{3}{2}\left[2\mathcal{H}^{\prime}+\left(1+3c_{s}^{2}\right)\mathcal{H}^{2}\right]\nabla^{-4}\partial_{i}\partial^{j}{\cal A}^{i}_{j}-\frac{3}{2}\mathcal{H}\nabla^{-4}\partial_{i}\partial^{j}{{\cal A}^{i}_{j}}^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{-2}\partial_{i}\partial^{j}{\cal A}^{i}_{j}

\displaystyle-\left[2\mathcal{H}^{\prime}+\left(1+3c_{s}^{2}\right)\mathcal{H}^{2}\right]\nabla^{-2}\Bigg[-\frac{3}{4}\chi_{1}^{lk,m}\chi_{1kl,m}-\frac{1}{2}\chi_{1}^{kl}\nabla^{2}\chi_{1lk}+\frac{1}{2}\chi_{1,l}^{km}\chi_{1m,k}^{l}+\frac{1}{2}\chi_{1km}^{\prime}{\chi_{1km}^{\prime}}-\frac{3}{2}\nabla^{-2}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\left({\chi_{1k}^{i}}^{\prime}{\chi_{1kj}^{\prime}}\right)\Bigg]

\displaystyle+\frac{3}{8}\chi_{1,k}^{ml}\chi_{1lm}^{,k}-\frac{3}{8}{\chi_{1}^{kl}}^{\prime}{\chi_{1kl}^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{4}\chi_{1k,l}^{m}\chi_{1,m}^{lk}-\mathcal{H}\nabla^{-2}\Bigg[-\frac{3}{2}{\chi_{1}^{lk,m}}^{\prime}\chi_{1kl,m}+\frac{1}{2}{\chi_{1}^{kl}}^{\prime}\nabla^{2}\chi_{1lk}-\frac{1}{2}\chi_{1}^{kl}\nabla^{2}{\chi_{1lk}}^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}{\chi_{1,l}^{km}}^{\prime}\chi_{1m,k}^{l}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\chi_{1,l}^{km}{\chi_{1m,k}^{l}}^{\prime}-2\mathcal{H}{\chi_{1km}}^{\prime}{\chi_{1km}^{\prime}}\Bigg]+3\mathcal{H}\nabla^{-4}\left(-2\mathcal{H}{\chi_{1k,j}^{i}}^{\prime}{\chi_{1k,i}^{j}}^{\prime}+{\chi_{1k,i}^{j}}^{\prime}\nabla^{2}\chi_{1k,j}^{i}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{-2}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\left({\chi_{1k}^{i}}^{\prime}{\chi_{1kj}^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle+c_{s}^{2}\Bigg[-\frac{1}{8}{\chi_{1}^{ik}}^{\prime}\chi_{1ki}^{\prime}-\mathcal{H}\chi_{1}^{ik}\chi_{1ki}^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\chi^{mk}_{1}\nabla^{2}\chi_{1mk}+\frac{3}{8}\chi^{ml}_{1,k}\chi_{1lm}^{,k}-\frac{1}{4}\chi_{1k,l}^{m}\chi_{1,m}^{lk}\Bigg]\;,

(9)

where

\displaystyle{\cal A}^{i}_{j}=\frac{1}{2}\chi_{1}^{lk,i}\chi_{1kl,j}+\chi_{1}^{kl}\chi_{1lk,j}^{,i}-\chi_{1}^{kl}\chi_{1l,jk}^{i}-\chi_{1}^{kl}\chi_{1lj,k}^{,i}

\displaystyle+\chi_{1}^{kl}\chi_{1j,kl}^{i}+\chi_{1,l}^{ik}\chi_{1jk}^{,l}-\chi_{1,l}^{ki}\chi_{1j,k}^{l}\;.

(10)

Using the traceless part of the

\displaystyle\psi_{2}=\phi_{2}-8\pi Ga^{2}\Pi_{2}-{{\cal F}_{\chi}\over 4}\;,

Looking for the particular solution in which

is constant, i.e. on very large scales, and using the energy-momentum constraint equation we get

\displaystyle{8\pi G\over H_{\Lambda}^{2}}\Pi_{2}=\eta_{\rm in}^{2}\left[{8\pi G\over\eta_{\rm in}^{2}H_{\Lambda}^{2}}\Pi_{2{\rm in}}-\left(\phi_{2}-{1\over 4}{\cal F}_{\chi}\right)\right]\left({\eta\over\eta_{\rm in}}\right)^{\left(5+3c_{s}^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle+\left(\phi_{2}-{1\over 4}{\cal F}_{\chi}\right)\eta^{2}

\displaystyle\psi_{2}

\displaystyle=\left[-{8\pi G\over\eta_{\rm in}^{2}H_{\Lambda}^{2}}\Pi_{2{\rm in}}+\left(\phi_{2}-{1\over 4}{\cal F}_{\chi}\right)\right]\left({\eta\over\eta_{\rm in}}\right)^{3\left(1+c_{s}^{2}\right)}\;.

We can quickly verify that the previous two equations can be combined again to recover the usual relation

\displaystyle{8\pi G\over\eta_{\rm in}^{2}H_{\Lambda}^{2}}\Pi_{2}+\psi_{2}\left({\eta\over\eta_{\rm in}}\right)^{2}=\left(\phi_{2}-{1\over 4}{\cal F}_{\chi}\right)\left({\eta\over\eta_{\rm in}}\right)^{2}\;.

(16)

Note that if

\displaystyle\left(\phi_{2}-{1\over 4}{\cal F}_{\chi}\right)={8\pi G\over\eta_{\rm in}^{2}H_{\Lambda}^{2}}\Pi_{2{\rm in}}\;,

(17)

we will always have
\psi_{2}=0
and
\phi_{2}={\cal F}_{\chi}/4
; this could also be a solution in our scenario.

\eta_{\rm in}
is a suitable initial condition.

\eta_{\rm in}
is that initial moment in which the hypothesis that

is constant over time is still correct. Now, considering just a mode with a given comoving

If we Fourier transform all these variables we obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}_{2}

\displaystyle\simeq\left[-{8\pi G\over H_{\Lambda}^{2}}{c_{s}^{2}k^{2}}\tilde{\Pi}_{2{\rm in}}+\left(\tilde{\phi}_{2}-{1\over 4}\tilde{\cal F}_{\chi}\right)\right]\left(c_{s}k|\eta|\right)^{3\left(1+c_{s}^{2}\right)}\;.

Now, at superhorizon scales, still assuming

III
Power spectrum

As a simple analytical example, let us consider the case where
\psi_{2}=0
, i.e. when
\phi_{2}={\cal F}_{\chi}/4
and we focus only on the first terms in (
12
); the second term will give a similar dependence. Then the primordial power spectrum of scalar fluctuations
\phi
is

\langle\phi(\mathbf{k})\phi(\mathbf{k^{\prime}})\rangle=\frac{1}{4}\langle\phi_{2}(\mathbf{k})\phi_{2}(\mathbf{k^{\prime}})\rangle=(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf{k+k^{\prime}})\mathcal{P}_{\phi}(k)

(21)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\phi}(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{64(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{k^{4}}\int{\rm d}^{3}k_{1}{\rm d}^{3}k_{2}\;\delta^{(3)}[\mathbf{k}-(\mathbf{k}_{1}+{\mathbf{k}_{2}})]

\displaystyle\times\mathcal{K}_{h}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\mathbf{k}_{2},k^{2})\;P_{h}(k_{1})P_{h}(k_{2})\;,

(22)

where the kernel is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{h}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\mathbf{k}_{2},k^{2})

\displaystyle=\Bigg\{\bigg(k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2}+3\mathbf{k}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{k}_{2}\bigg)^{2}\bigg[\left(1-\hat{\mathbf{k}}_{1}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{k}}_{2}\right)^{4}+\left(1+\hat{\mathbf{k}}_{1}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{k}}_{2}\right)^{4}\bigg]

\displaystyle+8\left(\mathbf{k}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{k}_{2}\right)\left(k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2}+3\mathbf{k}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{k}_{2}\right)\left(\hat{\mathbf{k}}_{1}\crossproduct\hat{\mathbf{k}}_{2}\right)^{2}\left[3+\left(\hat{\mathbf{k}}_{1}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{k}}_{2}\right)^{2}\right]+8k_{1}^{2}k_{2}^{2}\left(\hat{\mathbf{k}}_{1}\crossproduct\hat{\mathbf{k}}_{2}\right)^{4}\left[1+\left(\hat{\mathbf{k}}_{1}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{k}}_{2}\right)^{2}\right]\Bigg\}\;,

(23)

defining
P_{h}(k)=2\pi^{2}\Delta^{2}_{h}(k)/k^{3}
, the tensor power spectrum in dS reads
[
30
]

\Delta^{2}_{h}(k)=\frac{16}{\pi}\left(\frac{H_{\rm inf}}{m_{\rm pl}}\right)^{2}\;.

(24)

Here
H_{\rm inf}=\sqrt{\Lambda/3}
is the Hubble constant during inflation.

Note that the kernel differs distinctly from that of standard single-clock inflation (which for this case is in the bispectrum). This difference will also affect the bispectrum of scalar perturbations, thereby imprinting a unique signature in the squeezed non-Gaussian features of the large-scale structure
[
12
]
.
As a consequence, this provides a means to observationally test our scenario. The power spectrum is nearly scale invariant; this can be seen easily as no uncompensated power of
4
terms of
\mathbf{k}
appear in the scaling of the momenta in (
23
).

IV
Discussion

We have shown that it is possible to generate nearly scale-invariant scalar adiabatic perturbations in pure de Sitter. This is a scenario where the inflaton does not exist, and thus opens up the possibility to provide a picture of inflation that is model independent. There are some interesting features in our calculation. First, note that the scalar fluctuations are generated outside the horizon from the tensor perturbations. Assuming these fluctuations are adiabatic, as in the standard picture of inflation, the perturbations induced in the potential
\phi
persist after the transition from the dS phase to a radiation dominated epoch through the decay of dS. Similarly to scalar modes, vector perturbations are also generated from second order tensor fluctuations. As expected, the fluctuations deviate from Gaussianity and exhibit a certain intrinsic level of non-Gaussian features, the quantification of which will be addressed in a future publication.
However, we note the following:
for any non-conformally invariant field (with mass
m\ll H
) the quantum generation of particles from the vacuum state will add extra contributions to the second-order scalar perturbation mode, hence contributing to a gaussianization by the central limit theorem.
In our scenario, the magnitude of fluctuations can be determined from eqs (
22
)–(
23
), which rely exclusively on dS features and potential physical mechanisms that enhance scalar perturbations. These factors suggest a possible explanation for why the fluctuations are at the
10^{-5}
level.

Cramer-Rao Bound for Angle of Arrival Estimates in True-Time-Delay Systems
Carl Collmann, Ahmad Nimr, Gerhard Fettweis

Abstract
In the context of joint communication and sensing (JC&S), the challenge of obtaining accurate parameter estimates is of interest. Parameter estimates, such as the AoA ( AoA ) can be utilized for solving the initial access problem, interference mitigation, localization of users or monitoring of the environment and synchronization of MIMO ( MIMO ) systems. Recently, TTD ( TTD ) systems have gained attention for fast beam training during initial access and mitigation of beam squinting. This work derives the CRB ( CRB ) for angle estimates in typical TTD systems. Properties of the CRB and the Fisher information are investigated and numerically evaluated. Finally, methods for angle estimation such as ML ( ML ) and established estimators are utilized to solve the angle estimation problem using a uniform linear array.

I
Introduction

In future 6G systems, joint communication and sensing will play an important role by transforming cell infrastructures into perceptive networks
[
7
]
.
In this context, parameter estimates such as
AoA
/
AoD
(
AoD
) derived from sensing are key enablers of smart, context aware mobile communication systems.
These parameter estimates can be used for beam selection, localization, object tracking or synchronization and calibration of increasingly large antenna arrays in
MIMO
systems
[
11
]
.

Recently, the use of
TTD
has gained interest for compensation of the beam squint phenomenon
[
8
]
, which is a significant issue for large relative bandwidths.
The frequency dependent response of
TTD
arrays can also be exploited for fast beam training
[
13
]
,
[
4
]
and angle estimation
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
.
The feasibility of utilizing
TTD
for angle estimation
[
2
]
and beam training
[
9
]
has been experimentally verified.
For angle estimation in
TTD
systems, various methods such as
OMP
(
OMP
),
ML
based estimation
[
1
]
,
LMMSE
(
LMMSE
)
[
5
]
or correlation and model based estimators
[
2
]
.
Previous works have compared the performance of simple model based estimators to common angle estimation methods
[
3
]
.
While super-resolution methods such as rootMUSIC provide greater angle estimation accuracy, even in the presence of impairments, simple model based estimators can still provide decent performance with
MAE
(
MAE
)
<$1\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolDegree}$

[
3
]
.
For fair comparison it should be noted that common angle estimation methods are limited by the assumption that the narrow-band criterion applies, while
TTD
based estimation relies on intentional violation of this criterion
[
3
]
.
Other works have extensively investigated the effect of hardware impairments in
TTD
systems, namely time and phase errors
[
1
]
.
In
[
1
]
a
CRB
is provided, however the bound is derived under nontrivial assumptions about linearization of hardware impairments and relative ratios of their magnitudes.

As the
CRB
can provide a useful method to judge the performance of estimation methods, derivation of the
CRB
for angle estimates in
TTD
systems is of interest.
This work derives an expression for the
CRB
of
AoA
in typical
TTD
systems to provide insights into limitations on angle estimation accuracy.
Common angle estimation methods such as
ML
estimation and model based estimators are compared to the derived
CRB
.

The paper is structured as follows:
Section
II
introduces the general system model for a
TTD
SIMO system.
In section
III
the
CRB
is derived and insights into components of the
FI
(
FI
) are provided.
Section
IV
describes the algorithms used for angle estimation and compares their performance with
MSE
(
MSE
) as the metric.
The paper is concluded in section
V

III
Cramer-Rao Bound for AoA Estimates in TTD Systems

The objective is to estimate the angle
\phi
from the received signal based on the previously established model.
Since the Cramer-Rao bound provides the minimum variance that any unbiased estimator can attain and is therefore of interest for evaluating angle estimation methods in
TTD
systems.
When considering
n
discrete samples/frequency bins at frequencies
f=f_{n}
, the observation is

\displaystyle Z[n]=Y[n,\phi]+V[n]=Z_{n}.

(9)

The likelihood function for the noisy received signal observation vector
\bm{Z}
is

\displaystyle p(\bm{Z};\phi)=\prod_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\pi\sigma_{v}^{2}}e^{-\frac{|Z_{n}-Y_{n}|^{2}}{\sigma_{v}^{2}}},

(10)

and the corresponding log-likelihood function

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\phi)=\ln p(\bm{Z};\phi)=-\ln{\pi\sigma_{v}^{2}}-\frac{1}{\sigma_{v}^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}|Z_{n}-Y_{n}|^{2}.

(11)

Taking the first derivative in regards to the phase yields

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}(\phi)}{\partial\phi}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sigma_{v}^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left[-\frac{\partial Y_{n}}{\partial\phi}^{*}(Z_{n}-Y_{n})-(Z_{n}-Y_{n})^{*}\frac{\partial Y_{n}}{\partial\phi}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\sigma_{v}^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{\partial Y_{n}}{\partial\phi}^{*}(Z_{n}-Y_{n})\right\}.

(12)

The second derivative in regard to the phase is

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}(\phi)}{\partial\phi^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\sigma_{v}^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathfrak{Re}\left\{\frac{\partial^{2}Y_{n}}{\partial\phi^{2}}^{*}(Z_{n}-Y_{n})-\frac{\partial Y_{n}}{\partial\phi}^{*}\frac{\partial Y_{n}}{\partial\phi}\right\}.

(13)

The
FI
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(\phi)=-\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}(\phi)}{\partial\phi^{2}}\right]=\frac{2}{\sigma_{v}^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left|\frac{\partial Y_{n}}{\partial\phi}\right|^{2}

(14)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\sigma_{v}^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left|X_{n}\left(-j2\pi(f_{n}+f_{\text{c}})\frac{d}{c}\cos{\phi}\right)\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}me^{-j\psi_{n}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\sigma_{v}^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left|X_{n}\right|^{2}\cdot\left|2\pi(f_{n}+f_{\text{c}})\frac{d}{c}\cos{\phi}\right|^{2}\cdot\underbrace{\left|\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}me^{-j\psi_{n}}\right|^{2}}_{\kappa_{n}}.

The sum over antenna elements
m
can be rewritten using the property of a geometric series and taking its first derivative

\displaystyle\kappa_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left|e^{-j\psi_{n}}-Me^{-jM\psi_{n}}+(M-1)e^{-j(M+1)\psi_{n}}\right|^{2}}{16\sin^{4}(\psi_{n}/2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-M\cos[(M-1)\psi_{n}]+(M-1)\cos[M\psi_{n}])^{2}}{16\sin^{4}(\psi_{n}/2)}

\displaystyle+\frac{(M\sin[(M-1)\psi_{n}]-(M-1)\sin[M\psi_{n}])^{2}}{16\sin^{4}(\psi_{n}/2)}.

(15)

For illustration the variable
\kappa(f,\phi)

The

is rewritten using a Riemann sum

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(\phi)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\sigma_{v}^{2}}\left(2\pi\frac{d}{c}\cos\phi\right)^{2}

(16)

\displaystyle\cdot\frac{1}{\Delta f}\int_{-B/2}^{B/2}|X(f)|^{2}(f+f_{\text{c}})^{2}\kappa(f)df.

Under the assumption that the energy of the signal can be written as

\displaystyle E_{s}=\int_{-B/2}^{B/2}|X(f)|^{2}df,

(17)

and that the spectrum of

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(\phi)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2E_{s}N}{\sigma_{v}^{2}}\left(2\pi\frac{d}{c}\cos\phi\right)^{2}\frac{1}{B}\left[\int_{-B/2}^{B/2}f^{2}\kappa(f)df\right.

\displaystyle+\left.2f_{\text{c}}\int_{-B/2}^{B/2}f\kappa(f)df+f_{\text{c}}^{2}\int_{-B/2}^{B/2}\kappa(f)df\right].

(18)

When treating the frequency as a uniformly distributed random variable

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[f\kappa]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{B}\int_{-B/2}^{B/2}f\kappa(f)df=\kappa_{1},

(20)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[f^{2}\kappa]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{B}\int_{-B/2}^{B/2}f^{2}\kappa(f)df=\kappa_{2}.

V
Conclusion

In this work, the

TTD
systems is derived.
Furthermore, the performance of
ML
and peak based angle estimation methods are compared to the

The

while the peak based method does not at any

This limitation of the peak method results from only considering the frequency bin with the maximum power for
AoA
estimation.
The spacing between frequency bins imposes a limit on the attainable angle estimation performance of this method.
A potential solution to this problem is to up-sample the observed signal vector with sinc-interpolation to improve the frequency resolution.
For the given parameters, a

(

Future works should also consider the trade-off between computational complexity and angle estimation performance for the

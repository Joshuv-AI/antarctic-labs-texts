Boosting quantum efficiency by reducing complexity
Giovanni Sisorio, Alberto Cappellaro, Luca Dell’Anna

Abstract
In the context of energy storage at the nanoscale, exploring the notion of quantum advantage implies walking on the thin line at the boundary between quantum mechanics and thermodynamics, which underpins our conventional understanding of battery devices. With no classical analogue, the Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev (SYK) model has emerged in the last years as a promising platform to boost charging and storage efficiency thanks to its highly-entangling dynamics. Here, we explore how the robustness of this setup by considering the sparse version of the SYK model, showing that, as long as chaos is not completely broken, reducing its complexity may lead to more efficient quantum batteries.



Introduction and motivations.

The quest for efficient energy storage and work extraction has been a cornerstone
of several technological breakthroughs,
dating back at least to the first industrial revolution, with its profound connection
to the development of thermodynamics as a backbone for our scientific understanding of Nature

Saggion et al. 2019
;
Leff and Rex 1990
. Nowadays, as material samples are brought to increasingly
lower temperature and smaller sizes, we are experiencing a sort of nanoscale industrial
revolution, where the challenge of energy storage takes on a quantum mechanical dimension,
where centuries-old thermodynamical concepts are in need to be revisited
in the face of non-classical
resources such as quantum coherence and entanglement

Halpern 2022
;
Kurizki and Kofman 2022
;
Strasberg 2024
. Indeed, the interplay between entanglement
generation and work extraction from many-body quantum states has spurred a sustained research
effort into quantum batteries
Alicki and Fannes 2013
;
Binder et al. 2015
;
Ferraro et al. 2018
;
Andolina et al. 2018
;
Liu et al. 2019
;
Rossini et al. 2019
;
Quach et al. 2022
;
Ahmadi et al. 2024
;
Campaioli et al. 2024
.

It is now clear that quantum battery engineering hinges on balancing
complexity and controllability.
For instance, while being very sensitive to noise and fluctuations,
the onset of a highly entangling dynamics can positively affect the battery performance,
especially when it comes to figures of merit such the amount of injected energy
or the work extraction power
Hovhannisyan et al. 2013
;
Caravelli et al. 2020
;
Campaioli et al. 2024
.
This tension motivates the exploration of minimal platforms where access to such observables
is amenable to analytical and computational analysis.
One of the most interesting candidates is certainly the Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev (SYK) model,
a paradigmatic example of a strongly interacting quantum system describing all-to-all
randomly interacting fermions
Sachdev and Ye 1993
;
Kitaev 2015a
;
Kitaev 2015b
;
Sachdev 2015
;
Gu et al. 2020
.
This peculiar structure supports the onset of maximal chaos,
such that the system qualifies as a fast scrambler, leading to a very efficient charging and
stable energy storage
Belyansky et al. 2020
;
Rossini et al. 2020
;
Rosa et al. 2020
, but also to significant
experimental and computational challenges.
The former are a direct consequence of
the all-to-all character of SYK couplings, ending up in significant practical bottleneck,
like the system’s size
Danshita et al. 2017
;
Chen et al. 2018
;
Can et al. 2019
;
Uhrich et al. 2023
;
Baumgartner et al. 2024
. In addition,
all-to-all interactions leads to volume-law entangled ground states, such that even powerful
techniques such tensor networks becomes significantly costly
Bettaque and Swingle 2024
,
with exact diagonalization seemingly limited to few tens of fermions due to the rapid
scaling of Hilbert space size
Fu and Sachdev 2016
;
Liu et al. 2018
;
Zhang 2022
.

Here, we investigate the sparse version of the SYK model, where some interaction terms
are removed with a certain probability
Xu et al. 2020
;
García-García et al. 2021
;
Orman et al. 2025
.
By
pruning
a fraction of the couplings, quantum chaotic features - instrumental for
efficient energy storage - are retained while mitigating its computational and
experimental overhead. Even more interestingly, our results demonstrate that such
sparsification can also enhance the battery performance, provided the system remains
sufficiently chaotic.

The sparse SYK model.

As mentioned in the introduction, the SYK model presented itself as a theory for strongly
coupled fermions with random (all-to-all) interactions

Sachdev and Ye 1993
;
Kitaev 2015a
;
Kitaev 2015b
. Now, up to the last years, most
of the existing efforts in elucidating the peculiar properties of this model focused on the
case with Majorana fermions. Here, on the other hand,
our starting point is the complex version of the SYK (cSYK) model

Sachdev 2015
;
Gu et al. 2020
for
N
spinless fermions, i.e.

\hat{H}_{\text{cSYK}}=\sum_{i,j,k,l=1}^{N}J_{ijkl}\;\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{j}\hat{c}_{k}\hat{c}_{l}

(1)

with the usual anticommutation relations

\{\hat{c}_{i},\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{j}\}=\delta_{ij}
and

\{\hat{c}^{(\dagger)}_{i},\hat{c}^{(\dagger)}_{j}\}=0
, while the
complex couplings satisfy
J_{ijkl}=J^{*}_{klij}
and
J_{ijkl}=-J_{jikl}=-J_{ijlk}
,
such that Eq. (
1
) is hermitian and anti-symmetrized. These couplings are distributed according to a zero-mean Gaussian density function
\mathcal{P}(J_{ijkl})
,
with variance
\overline{J^{2}_{ijkl}}=J^{2}/N^{3}
, the average over disorder
being defined as
\overline{\mathcal{O}(J_{ijkl})}=\int\mathcal{D}[J_{ijkl}]\mathcal{P}(J_{ijkl})O(J_{ijkl})
.
The sparse version of Eq. (
1
) is simply implemented by introducing,
for each coupling,
an additional random variable
x_{ijkl}
being equal to
1
with probability
p
and
0

otherwise, such that the coupling is retained with probability
p
or
pruned

(i.e. removed) with probability
1-p

Xu et al. 2020
;
García-García et al. 2021
.
Therefore, for a
q
-body interaction
(in the case of Eq. (
1
),
q=4
), the
pruning
procedure
reduces the number of interacting terms
\binom{N}{q}
by a factor
p
. In order to ensure
comparability with the energy scale of the original model (i.e.
p=1
),

\overline{J^{2}_{ijkl}}\longrightarrow\overline{J^{2}_{ijkl}}/p
.

Now, the complex SYK can be mapped onto a spin-
1/2
model by means of a Jordan-Wigner
transformation, providing us with a more convenient numerical platform for our later
purposed
García-Álvarez et al. 2017
;
Rossini et al. 2020
; by recalling

\hat{\sigma}^{\pm}_{i}=(\hat{\sigma}_{i}^{x}\pm i\hat{\sigma}_{i}^{y})/2
,
the transformation is defined by

\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i}=\hat{\sigma}_{i}^{+}\prod_{m=1}^{i-1}\hat{\sigma}^{z}_{m}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\hat{c}_{i}=\bigg(\prod_{m=1}^{i-1}\sigma_{m}^{z}\bigg)\hat{\sigma}_{i}\;.

(2)

As noticed in
Rosa et al. 2020
, this transformation make the highly nonlocal character
of the cSYK more transparent. As we aim to make clear later, this feature is
particularly relevant when we compare the sparse cSYK to other battery implementations,
such as, for instance, the Dicke-based one
Ferraro et al. 2018
.
Even in its complex version, it is important to recall that the
SYK model is maximally chaotic, saturating a general bound
Maldacena et al. 2016

on the Lyapunov exponent
\lambda_{L}
which controls the decay of out-of-time four-point correlators
as
\big\langle\hat{A}(t)\hat{B}(0)\hat{A}(t)\hat{B}(0)\big\rangle_{\text{th}}\sim 1-\alpha\exp(\lambda_{L}t_{s})
, with
\hat{A}
and
\hat{B}
some system’s operators,

\langle\bullet\rangle_{\text{th}}
the thermal average and
t_{s}
the so-called scrambling time.
The onset of maximal chaos implies that information is scrambled at the fastest rate enabled
by quantum mechanics and it is crucial for an efficient charging protocol

Rossini et al. 2020
;
Rosa et al. 2020
;
Belyansky et al. 2020
.
It is then natural to investigate the
relation between sparsity and quantum chaos, especially when one has to leverage the latter
to implement an efficient setup. Technically, the quantum chaotical character of the SYK model
reflects on specific features of its energy spectrum, which can be analyzed by means of tools
from random matrix theory (RMT)
Guhr et al. 1998
;
D’Alessio et al. 2016
;
Orman et al. 2025
.
Here, we first examine the behavior of the nearest-neighbor gap ratio, which is defined as

r=\bigg\langle\min\bigg(\frac{s_{i}}{s_{i+1}},\;\frac{s_{i+1}}{s_{i}}\bigg)\bigg\rangle\;,

(3)

with
s_{i}=E_{i}-E_{i-1}
the spacing between adjacent eigenvalues. The gap ration
quantifies the repulsion between energy levels at scales of the mean level spacing.
Consistently with predictions from RMT,
r
remains approximately constant for large

p
, while a sharp drop is observed as soon as
p
approaches a critical value
of sparsity usually labelled
p_{2}
, signaling the dramatic breakdown of spectral rigidity.

Implementing the battery: The charging protocol.

A quantum battery can be simply intended
as a collection of
N
quantum cells
Alicki and Fannes 2013
;
Binder et al. 2015
;
Campaioli et al. 2024
,
each acting as an individual unit of energy
storage, such that
\hat{H}_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\hat{h}_{i}
. Here, we focus on
the charging protocol, thereby assuming the battery is initially prepared
in its discharged state
(the lowest eigenstate of
\hat{H}_{0}
) up to
t\rightarrow 0^{-}
. Then, at
t=0

the charging Hamiltonian is turned on, realizing the following protocol

\hat{H}_{B}(t)=\hat{H}_{0}+\lambda(t)\hat{H}_{1}

(5)

where
\lambda(t)=1
when
0\leq t<\tau_{c}
, and
0
elsewhere, with
\tau_{c}

being the duration of the charging protocol. Now, within our implementation,
\hat{H}_{0}

is just an ensemble of
N
two-level systems (or spin-
1/2
particles, in the context of the JW transformation defined in Eq. (
2
)), while
the sparse cSYK is responsible for charging. With the convention
\hbar=1
,

\hat{H}_{0}=\omega_{0}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sigma^{y}_{i}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\hat{H}_{1}=\hat{H}^{\text{(sparse)}}_{\text{cSYK}}

(6)

where
\omega_{0}
is the level splitting and
\sigma_{y}^{i}
the usual Pauli matrix
for the
i^{\text{th}}
.

Now, in order to assess the performance of a battery, throughout the charging process,
it is worth examining specific figures of merit with particular attention to the role
played by the sparsity parameter.
In Fig.

the complexity emerging from an all-to-all interacting implementation. A first hint

A more complete insight into this trade-off can be acquired by investigating
a different figure of merit, ideally enconding additional information concerning
the work extraction at later times

(i.e. after the charging protocol is
turned off). This is the case for the so-called battery

defined as the ratio between the amount of extractable work and the energy stored

e(\tau_{c})=\overline{\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{E}_{N/2}(\tau_{c})}{E_{N/2}(\tau_{c})}\bigg)}

(9)

;

;

\mathcal{E}(t)\equiv\mathcal{E}(\rho(t))=\text{Tr}\big[\hat{H}_{0}\rho(t)\big]-\text{Tr}\big[\hat{H}_{0}\sigma_{\rho}\big]\;,

(10)

with
\sigma_{\rho}
being a completely passive passive state (thermal states are good candidates

), and
\rho(t)
the state evolved according to the charging Hamiltonian, cfr.
Eq. (
5
). Also notice that
in Eq. (
9

can be recast as a sum
of local terms (this is evident from our setup Eq. (
6
)), we just need to
restrict
\hat{H}_{0}
in defining

to the desired subset of elementary cells
(i.e. we stop the sum at
N/2
). Technically, this restriction reflects the reasonable assumption
that, in a realistic experimental platform, only a subset of the total number of qubits can
be accessed.

This is in line with the seminal observation made in

smaller batteries.
Now, as sparsity gradually increases some interactions are removed, thus reducing complexity and,
at the same time, crucially retaining the quantum chaotic character typical of the fully-connected
regime. We discussed this previously, in relation to important markers of quantum chaos such as the
nearest-neighbour ratio and the SFF dynamics (cfr. respectively
Fig.
2
and
3

intermediate regime of reduced complexity, the battery appears to operate more efficiently
likely because of the reduced interference between the interaction terms.
This is evident by looking, for instance, at the

(bottom panel in Fig.

from above, efficiency can be boosted by

more positively affected by an increased sparsity, when compared to smaller implementations

Finally, as we approach the critical value

, spectral rigidity breaks down and

and, consequently, the system’s ability to efficiently scramble information. Therefore,
the battery performance severely deteriorates in the regime

Experimental considerations.
It is
certainly relevant that all the crucial features of the fully-connected SYK model persist in
its sparse version, especially (at least for our goals) when it comes to the onset of maximal chaos.
Indeed, implementing a sparse model may enable experimentalists to overcome significant bottlenecks
displayed by the full version. Even within the framework of ultracold atomic gases,
where almost perfect isolation from the external environment and control on many physical parameters
are at hand, implementing the fully-connected SYK would require
4\cdot 10^{3}
lasers just for
the
N=16
fermions
Danshita et al. 2017
. A similar
N
is achieved by considering mesoscopic graphene
flakes at strong disorder and magnetic field, by considering the electrons in the lowest Landau level

Chen et al. 2018
. Here, the bottleneck is represented by the magnetic field strength, up to

B\sim 3\cdot 10^{3}
T for a flake with
5
nm radius (
2\cdot 10^{3}
carbon atoms mapping to the
N\lesssim 20
scenario). While larger flakes enables more realistic conditions
Brzezińska et al. 2023
,
implementing a sparse version represents an appealing alternative. This is even more compelling
when considering the recent advances with novel platforms based on
cavity quantum electrodynamical simulations
Uhrich et al. 2023
;
Baumgartner et al. 2024
. Starting from a single-mode
optical cavity filled with quasi-2D
6
Li atoms, through fast cycling and engineered time-dependent
disorder, it is possible to simulate the evolution of a random quantum circuit through discrete
steps, converging to the desired model. Remarkably, this hybrid digital-analog technique (resembling conventional Trotterization)
is able to target sparse version of the desired target model (the full SYK, in this case)
Baumgartner et al. 2024
.
In order to estimate sparseness, one can compute the
statistical distance
from the fully connected
model via the so-called Kullback-Leibler divergence (or relative Shannon entropy), namely

D_{KL}(\mathcal{J}||\mathcal{J}_{sp})=\int\mathcal{D}[\mathcal{J}]\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{J})\log(\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{J})/\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{J}_{sp}))
where

\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{J}=J_{ijkl})
is the distribution of the fully-connected model while

\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{J}_{sp})
described the sparse one.

Conclusions.
Our work underscores the potential of sparse quantum systems as a viable
path toward the goal of implementing robust quantum batteries, offering a blueprint for
harnessing quantum resources without succumbing to impractical complexity. More specifically,
we have first shown that, starting from the fully-connected SYK model, quantum chaotical features
persists up to a critical value (
p_{2}
) of the sparsity parameter, a threshold
where spectral rigidity breaks down. The stored energy saturates at this value and, more remarkably,
moving closer to
p_{2}
from above (cfr. Fig.

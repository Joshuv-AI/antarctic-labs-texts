Differential treatment effects across subgroups in immunomodulatory drugs for sepsis: protocol for a systematic review
Authors: Ling Ding, Chunyang Bai, Linge Jian, Dingji Hu, Jing Xiong, Zheng Zhang, Qiao He, Yuning Wang, Jiayue Xu, Long Ge, Wen Wang, Xin Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionSepsis, a life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection, may benefit from immunomodulatory drugs. Nevertheless, numerous clinical trials of these drugs have failed to demonstrate efficacy, partly due to substantial heterogeneous treatment responses. Subgroup analyses from these trials are frequently employed to investigate different treatment effects across subgroups. However, which drugs might have different effects across subgroups and how credible these findings are have not been well summarised and evaluated. Additionally, the differences in the characteristics and results of subgroup analyses based on whether the primary trial’s main effect is statistically significant remain unclear. We will conduct a systematic review to comprehensively address these questions.Methods and analysisWe will include randomised controlled trials (RCTs) evaluating immunomodulatory drugs for adult sepsis and exclude quasi-randomised trials, single-arm studies, animal research, conference abstracts, study protocols and non-English publications. To comprehensively search for subgroup analyses, we will search both RCTs and their published secondary analyses across PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, ClinicalTrials.gov and the Cochrane Library from their inception. Four reviewers will independently screen eligible studies and only one subgroup analysis will be selected for data extraction using standardised forms. The credibility of subgroup effects will be assessed using the Instrument for assessing the Credibility of Effect Modification Analyses. We will analyse the proportion and characteristics of subgroup analyses reported in trials. We will qualitatively summarise the results of subgroup analyses, focusing on findings with a subgroup-specific p value<0.05 or an interaction p value<0.05. If two or more studies examined the same drug within a subgroup, we will perform data synthesis using a random-effects model to estimate pooled subgroup effects. We will compare the differences based on whether the primary trial’s main effect was statistically significant using t-tests, Mann-Whitney U tests, χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate.Ethics and disseminationNo ethical approval is required because the data we will use do not include individual patient data. Findings will be disseminated through publication in a peer-reviewed journal.Trial registration numberCRD420251089737.

Introduction

Sepsis is a life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection,1 posing a significant global health burden.2 Global estimates from 2017 indicate approximately 48.9 million new sepsis cases and 11 million sepsis-related deaths worldwide, accounting for about 19.7% of global mortality that year.3 Although considerable progress has been made in elucidating sepsis pathophysiology in recent decades, clinical management continues to rely primarily on antimicrobial therapy and supportive care.4

Immunomodulatory drugs, which target the dysregulated host inflammatory and immune response in sepsis, represent a potential therapeutic approach.5 Nevertheless, multiple clinical trials investigating immunomodulatory therapies in sepsis have failed to demonstrate clinical efficacy.6 A fundamental explanation for this lies in the considerable interpatient heterogeneity of treatment responses in sepsis, while most clinical trials typically adopt a ‘one-size-fits-all’ approach.7 Consequently, the accurate identification of heterogeneous treatment responses to immunomodulatory agents and the precise targeting of responsive patient subgroups represent a promising therapeutic strategy.

Subgroup analysis remains the predominant method for identifying subgroup effects and is extensively used in clinical trial design. However, subgroup analyses frequently generate either spurious positive or false-negative findings owing to inadequate statistical power and multiple testing problems.8 Up to now, no study has systematically examined the issues related to the different treatment effects across subgroups in immunomodulatory drugs for sepsis.

Therefore, we will undertake a systematic review of randomised controlled trials (RCTs) and their secondary analyses to: (1) analyse the proportion of subgroup analyses reported in trials and describe the characteristics of subgroup analyses; (2) summarise the results of subgroup analyses, perform data synthesis if two or more studies examined the same drug within a subgroup and assess their credibility and (3) compare the differences in characteristics and results of subgroup analyses based on whether the primary trial’s main effect is statistically significant.

Methods and analysis

Standardised reporting and registration

We will prepare this systematic review in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines.9 The review protocol was prospectively registered at PROSPERO (registration number: CRD420251089737).

Eligibility criteria of trials

We will systematically review RCTs evaluating immunomodulatory drugs for adult sepsis. We will include studies that (1) enrol adult patients diagnosed with sepsis defined by the Sepsis-1,10 Sepsis-211 or Sepsis-3 criteria1 and (2) investigate immunomodulatory drugs with established or hypothesised immunomodulatory effects (see online supplemental file 1) for definitions12 13 as interventions. We will exclude the following studies: quasi-randomised trials, single-arm studies, animal research, conference abstracts, study protocols and non-English publications. Online supplemental file 2 provides additional details of our eligibility criteria.

Data sources and search strategy

To comprehensively search for subgroup analyses, we will search both randomised trials and their published secondary analyses. A comprehensive systematic search across PubMed, Embase, Web of Science and the Cochrane Library from their inception will be conducted. The search strategy will incorporate a combination of keywords and Medical Subject Headings terms. Additionally, we will search ClinicalTrials.gov and manually check the citing articles of eligible trials in Web of Science to identify additional trials and subgroup analyses that may be eligible for inclusion. Online supplemental file 3 presents the search strategies for all data sources. A subgroup analysis is defined as a statistical analysis that explores whether the effects of the intervention differ according to the status of a subgroup variable.14 No restrictions will be applied regarding publication date or status.

Study selection

Four reviewers will screen potentially eligible studies using Rayyan software (https://rayyan.ai), working in two pairs. Each pair (two reviewers) will independently screen studies, first by titles and abstracts, then by full-text assessment. Inter-reviewer discrepancies will be resolved through consensus discussion, with unresolved disagreements referred to a senior reviewer for final adjudication.

Selection of subgroup analyses for data extraction

When a trial reports only one subgroup analysis, we will include it. When trials report multiple subgroup analyses across different endpoints, we will include subgroup analyses for only one endpoint to avoid double-counting within the same trial. Specifically, we will prioritise the trial-defined primary endpoint: if a single primary endpoint is explicitly stated, we will use that endpoint. If the trial specifies multiple primary endpoints or does not clearly specify a primary endpoint, we will use the first endpoint reported in the abstract as a proxy primary endpoint.

Data extraction

Data will be extracted by a junior reviewer and independently checked by a senior reviewer for accuracy and completeness. Any discrepancies will be resolved by discussion, with adjudication by a third reviewer if necessary. All extracted data will be systematically recorded in Microsoft Excel using a standardised, pre-piloted extraction form.

We will extract the trial characteristics of trials reporting subgroup analyses, including the following: trial phase, geographical scope, number of trial centres, number of patients assessed for eligibility, number of eligible patients, number of patients randomised, number of patients analysed, what was the statistical significance of the main effect for the primary endpoint, whether the trial was pre-registered, whether the trial protocol was available, what was the funding source and whether a data monitoring committee was involved. Two reviewers will assess the risk of bias of included RCTs using the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 tool, with disagreements resolved by discussion and adjudication by a third reviewer if needed.

Then, we will extract characteristics of subgroup analyses, including the number of subgroup variables, whether any subgroup variables were measured postrandomisation, whether the hypothesis was specified a priori, whether point estimates and associated CIs were reported, whether p values were reported and whether results from an interaction test were reported.

Additionally, we will extract detailed results of subgroup analyses for each subgroup variable, including the categories of the subgroup variable and its results, including n (%) per subgroup category, point estimates and CIs, p-values for both subgroup categories and p value for interaction.

Assessment of subgroup analysis credibility

Two independent reviewers will evaluate the credibility of all investigator-reported subgroup analyses and pooled subgroup analyses using the Instrument for assessing the Credibility of Effect Modification Analyses (ICEMAN).15 Discrepancies between reviewers will be resolved through consensus discussion, with unresolved disagreements referred to a senior reviewer for final arbitration. Our credibility evaluation will specifically exclude subgroup analyses assessing post-randomisation variables, continuous effect modifier modelling, effect modelling and data-driven subgroup discovery algorithms.

Statistical analysis

We will calculate the proportion of trials reporting subgroup analyses and describe trial characteristics and subgroup-analysis characteristics. Continuous variables will be reported as means (SD) or medians (IQRs), and categorical variables as frequencies (percentages).

We will qualitatively describe the results of subgroup analyses, presenting findings stratified by whether the primary trial’s main effect was statistically significant. Statistical significance of the main effect will be determined as reported in the primary publication (p<0.05, or 95% CI excluding the null when p values are not reported). We will qualitatively summarise the results of all subgroup analyses, mainly focusing on findings with a subgroup-specific p value<0.05 or an interaction p value<0.05. For subgroup analyses without reported effect sizes or between-subgroup differences, we will calculate these using available original data—event counts for binary outcomes and means for continuous outcomes in both the intervention and control groups. Effect sizes will be computed using the Mantel-Haenszel method for dichotomous outcomes (presented as risk ratios and 95% CIs) and the inverse-variance method for continuous outcomes (presented as mean differences and 95% CIs). Between-subgroup differences will be assessed using χ² test for subgroup differences in Review Manager (RevMan). Where original data are unavailable, we will contact the corresponding authors of included studies to obtain these data.

If two or more studies examined the same drug within a subgroup, we will perform data synthesis using a random-effects model to estimate pooled subgroup effects. The Mantel-Haenszel method will be applied for dichotomous outcomes and the inverse variance for continuous outcomes. Dichotomous outcomes will be presented as risk ratios and 95% CIs, while continuous outcomes will be presented as mean differences and 95% CIs. We will perform a planned sensitivity analysis by restricting the pooled analysis to trials published in 2015 or later. Analysis will be performed using Review Manager 5.4. We will also describe the credibility of these investigator-reported subgroup analyses and pooled subgroup analyses.

We will compare the differences in characteristics, results and credibility of subgroup analyses based on whether the primary trial’s main effect was statistically significant. Continuous data will be compared using independent samples t-tests or Mann-Whitney U tests. Categorical variables will be analysed using either the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test. All tests will be two-sided, and significance (α) will be set at 0.05. Analyses will be performed using R V.4.4.1.

Discussion

This systematic review will provide a structured evaluation of both the results and methodological quality of studies examining differential treatment effects across subgroups of immunopharmacological treatments for sepsis. The findings will provide support for further research on precision immunopharmacological treatments.

This review has several strengths. Our transparent eligibility criteria and comprehensive search are intended to enhance completeness and reproducibility and to reduce the likelihood of missing relevant evidence. Independent screening and a piloted extraction process should minimise errors and improve the reliability of the synthesis. In addition, ICEMAN offers a structured framework to appraise the credibility of subgroup effects, reducing the risk of over-interpreting chance findings. A potential limitation is that this review will be based on reported subgroup analyses, and our findings may be vulnerable to under-reporting or selective reporting.

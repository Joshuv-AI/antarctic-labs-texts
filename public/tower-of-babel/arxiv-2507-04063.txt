On the rigidity of k k -step nilpotent graph Lie algebras
Josefina Barrionuevo † {\dagger} and Paulo Tirao † {\dagger} ‡ {\ddagger}

Abstract
. We thoroughly explore the class of k k -step nilpotent Lie algebras associated with a simple graph looking for k k -step nilpotent Lie algebras which are rigid in the variety of at most k k -step nilpotent Lie algebras. We find out that, besides the complete graph, the only examples arise for k = 2 k=2 and graphs of at most 4 vertices. A key tool to prove non- k k -rigidity in this context, is a general construction of non-trivial deformations for naturally graded nilpotent Lie algebras.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 17B30; Secondary 17B99

Rigidity problems about Lie algebras in general, and about nilpotent Lie algebras in particular, are challenging.
A major open problem is to understand whether there exist rigid nilpotent Lie algebras or not.
The so-called Vergne’s Conjecture predicts that the answer is no.

Some intriguing questions on the nilpotent case are:

(1)

Are there
k
-step nilpotent Lie algebras which are rigid in the variety of at most
k
-step nilpotent Lie algebras?

(2)

Are there
k
-step nilpotent Lie algebras which are rigid in the variety of at most
k+1
-step nilpotent Lie algebras?

(3)

Are there nilpotent Lie algebras which are rigid in the variety of nilpotent Lie algebras?

We know that the answer to the first questions is yes and we know only two families of such algebras (besides some isolated cases of dimension
<6
).
Namely, for every
k
the free
k
-step nilpotent Lie algebras, and Heisenberg Lie algebras for
k=2

[
BTS
]
.
We don’t know whether to expect many or few.

We don’t know any examples answering positively questions two and three, besides the exceptional cases in dimension
<6
,
due to the fact that there are only finitely many orbits (isomorphism classes) there.

In light of the above questions, we explore a class of
k
-step nilpotent Lie algebras that are associated with simple graphs.
Reasons for considering this class include the fact that they enjoy a pretty simple structure with a strong combinatorial flavor,
making them accessible for explicit algebraic computations.
And the fact that the free
k
-step nilpotent Lie algebras belong to this class; they are associated with complete graphs.

We find two new examples answering positively question one.
Both of them occur for
k=2
, and are associated with graphs of 4 vertices.
Moreover, we prove that these are the only ones within the class considered.
That is,
k
-step nilpotent graph Lie algebras are in general not
k
-rigid,
with the distinguished exceptions of the free
k
-step nilpotent Lie algebras.
Notice that the 3-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra is the free 2-step nilpotent Lie algebra
on 2 generators and it is a graph Lie algebra.

The fact that in general
k
-step graph Lie algebras are not
k
-rigid
follows as a particular instance of a more general result that we prove.
We show that a large class of
k
-step naturally graded nilpotent Lie algebras are not
k
-rigid
by constructing explicitly non-trivial deformations of them.

We’d like to mention that 2-step graph Lie algebras, and more generally, 2-step and 3-step graded Lie algebras have been considered within the framework of
deformations and contractions in
[
AAA
]
and
[
GR
]
respectively.

All Lie algebras in this paper are finite dimensional over an algebraic closed field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic zero.

1.
Preliminaries on
k
-step nilpotent graph Lie algebras

The central descending series of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is the filtration of ideals

\mathfrak{g}^{0}\supseteq\mathfrak{g}^{1}\supseteq\dots\supseteq\mathfrak{g}^{k}\supseteq\dots
defined recursively by:

\mathfrak{g}^{0}=\mathfrak{g}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{g}^{i+1}=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{i}],\quad\text{for $i\geq 1$}.

The associated graded Lie algebra is

\operatorname{gr}(\mathfrak{g})=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\mathfrak{g}^{i}/\mathfrak{g}^{i+1}.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is said to be
k
-step nilpotent if
\mathfrak{g}^{k}=0
and
\mathfrak{g}^{k-1}\neq 0

and it is said to be
naturally graded
if
\mathfrak{g}\simeq\operatorname{gr}(\mathfrak{g})
.

A Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with a graded decomposition

(1.1)

\mathfrak{g}=V_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus V_{k},

where
[V_{i},V_{j}]\subseteq V_{i+j}
, is at most
k
-step nilpotent.
In general, such a
\mathfrak{g}
may admit different graded decompositions.

If in addition it satisfies that

(1.2)

\mathfrak{g}^{i}=V_{i+1}\oplus\dots\oplus V_{k},

then it is
k
-step nilpotent and it is naturally graded.
Such a decomposition is called a
Carnot grading
and it is unique up to isomorphism.
Every
\mathfrak{g}
with a graded decomposition as in (
1.1
) admits a Carnot grading satisfying (
1.2
).

Remark 1.1
.

Having a Carnot grading and being naturally graded are equivalent.

The free
k
-step nilpotent Lie algebra on
m
generators,
L_{(k)}(m)
, admits a Carnot grading

L_{(k)}(m)=L_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus L_{k}
, where
L_{1}=\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\rangle
and
L_{2}=\Lambda^{2}L_{1}
.
See
[
BTS
]
for a thorough description, including Hall bases.

More general examples are the
k
-step nilpotent Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}(k,G)
associated with a simple graph,
considered by Dani and Maikar in
[
DM
]
for
k=2
and by Maikar in
[
M
]
for arbitrary
k
.
In this context,
L_{(k)}(m)=\mathfrak{g}(k,K_{m})
where
K_{m}
is the complete graph on
m
-vertices.

Given a simple graph

, with vertices

2.
On the rigidity of nilpotent Lie algebras

As a general setting we consider the variety
\mathcal{L}_{n}
of complex Lie algebras of dimension
n
,
the subvariety
\mathcal{N}_{n}
of nilpotent Lie algebras, and the subvarieties
\mathcal{N}_{n,k}
of nilpotent Lie algebras
which are at most
k
-step nilpotent.
Note that

\mathcal{L}_{n}\supseteq\mathcal{N}_{n}=\mathcal{N}_{n,n-1}\supseteq\mathcal{N}_{n,n-2}\supseteq\dots\supseteq\mathcal{N}_{n,1}.

A
\mu\in\mathcal{L}_{n}
is a Lie bracket in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and for
g\in GL(n,\mathbb{C})
,
g\cdot\mu
is the Lie bracket

g\cdot\mu(x,y)=g\mu(g^{-1}x,g^{-1}y).

The orbit of
\mu
under this action,
\mathcal{O}(\mu)
, is the isomorphism class of
\mu
.
If
\mathcal{O}(\mu)
is Zariski open in
\mathcal{L}_{n}
,
\mu
is said to be rigid.

This action preserves the subvarieties
\mathcal{N}_{n,k}
and a
\mu\in\mathcal{N}_{n,k}
is said to be
nilpotently rigid

if
\mathcal{O}(\mu)
is open in
\mathcal{N}_{n}
and it is said to be
k
-rigid
if
\mathcal{O}(\mu)
is open in
\mathcal{N}_{n,k}
.

Remark 2.1
.

On the varieties
\mathcal{L}_{n}
,
\mathcal{N}_{n}
and
\mathcal{N}_{n,k}
, in addition to the Zariski topology, one can also consider the finer Euclidean topology.
It is well known that an orbit is Euclidean open if and only if it is Zariski open.

Observe that if
\mu
is not rigid in
\mathcal{N}_{n,k}
, then it is not rigid in
\mathcal{N}_{n,k+i}
for
i\geq 1
, and also not rigid in
\mathcal{N}_{n}
and
\mathcal{L}_{n}
.

We will sometimes write, for convenience,
[x,y]
instead of
\mu(x,y)
.

2.2.
Linear deformations

The content of this subsection is from
[
BTS
]
.

Given a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with bracket
\mu
, if
\sigma
is a alternating bilinear map from
\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
such that

\mu_{t}=\mu+t\sigma

is a Lie bracket for all

\mu_{t}
is a linear deformation of
\mu
.
If for all
t\neq 0

\mu_{t}
is not isomorphic to
\mu
, then
\mu
is not rigid.

Given two independent elements
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}
, a complementary subspace
\mathfrak{h}
and
y\in\mathfrak{g}
,
consider the alternating bilinear map on
\mathfrak{g}
,
\sigma=\sigma^{a_{1},a_{2}}_{y}
, defined by:

\sigma(a_{1},a_{2})=y;\quad\sigma(a_{1},h)=0=\sigma(a_{2},h),\;\;\sigma(h,h^{\prime})=0,\;\forall h,h^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{h}.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
BTS
]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a nilpotent Lie algebra with bracket
\mu
. Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a codimension 2 subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
and let

be such that

\mathfrak{g}=\langle a_{1},a_{2}\rangle\oplus\mathfrak{h}
.
Then for every

\mu_{t}=\mu+t\sigma^{a_{1},a_{2}}_{y}
is a linear deformation of

.

3.
The rigidity problem for

-step nilpotent

graph Lie algebras, for

In this section we prove that for

, the

-step nilpotent graph Lie algebras are not

-rigid with the only exceptions of

-step nilpotent Lie algebras.

The following theorem, which provides sufficient conditions to ensure

Theorem 3.1
.

Let

is not

-rigid.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be the subalgebra
V_{1}^{\prime}\oplus V_{2}\oplus\dots\oplus V_{k}
, where
V_{1}^{\prime}
is a direct complement of
\langle a_{1},a_{2}\rangle
in

.
The elements

and

2.2
.
So that, if

is the bracket of

, we may consider the linear deformation of

\mu_{t}=\mu+t\sigma^{a_{1},a_{2}}_{y}.

It is straightforward that
\mu_{t}
is

We prove now that

is not naturally graded for all

is not

Supose that

The central descendent series of

\displaystyle a_{1}

\displaystyle=w_{1}+w_{2}+\ldots+w_{k}=w_{1}+b

\displaystyle a_{2}

\displaystyle=w_{1}^{\prime}+w_{2}^{\prime}+\ldots+w_{k}^{\prime}=w_{1}^{\prime}+c,

(3)

Since

\displaystyle ty

\displaystyle=[a_{1},a_{2}]_{t}

\displaystyle=[w_{1}+b,w_{1}^{\prime}+c]_{t}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{[w_{1},w_{1}^{\prime}]_{t}}_{\in W_{2}}+\underbrace{[w_{1},c]_{t}+[b,w_{1}^{\prime}]_{t}+[b,c]_{t}}_{\in W_{3}\oplus\ldots\oplus W_{k}}

Since
ty\in V_{k}=W_{k}
and
k\geq 3
, then
[w_{1},w_{1}^{\prime}]_{t}=0
and

\displaystyle ty

\displaystyle=[w_{1},c]_{t}+[b,w_{1}^{\prime}]_{t}+[b,c]_{t}

\displaystyle=[a_{1}-b,c]_{t}+[b,a_{2}-c]_{t}+[b,c]_{t}

\displaystyle=[a_{1},c]_{t}-[b,c]_{t}+[b,a_{2}]_{t}-[b,c]_{t}+[b,c]_{t}

\displaystyle=[a_{1},c]_{t}-[b,c]_{t}+[b,a_{2}]_{t}

\displaystyle=[a_{1},c]-[b,c]+[b,a_{2}]\,.

From this, we have
ty\in\mathfrak{g}^{(2)}+[a_{1},\mathfrak{g}^{1}]+[a_{2},\mathfrak{g}^{1}]
and then
t=0
.
∎

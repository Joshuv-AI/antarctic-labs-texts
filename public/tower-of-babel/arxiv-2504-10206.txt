Approximation by Neural Network Sampling Operators in Mixed Lebesgue Spaces
Arpan Kumar Dey, A. Sathish Kumar, P. Devaraj

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove the rate of approximation for the Neural Network Sampling Operators activated by sigmoidal functions with mixed Lebesgue norm in terms of averaged modulus of smoothness for a bounded measurable functions on bounded domain. In order to achieve the above result, we first establish that the averaged modulus of smoothness is finite for certain suitable subspaces of L p , q ​ ( ℝ × ℝ ) . L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}). Using the properties of averaged modulus of smoothness, we estimate the rate of approximation of certain linear operators in mixed Lebesgue norm. Then, as an application of these linear operators, we obtain the Jackson type approximation theorem, in order to give a characterization for the rate of approximation of neural network operators in-terms of averaged modulus of smoothness in mixed norm. Lastly, we discuss some examples of sigmoidal functions and using these sigmoidal functions, we show the implementation of continuous and discontinuous functions by neural network operators .

We begin with the definition of mixed Lebesgue spaces. For
1\leq p,q<\infty,
let
L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the space of all complex valued measurable functions

\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}

\|f\|_{L^{p,q}}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x,\mathbf{y})|^{q}d\mathbf{y}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

Further, let
L^{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the set of all complex valued measurable functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}

\|f\|_{L^{\infty,\infty}}:=ess\sup|f|<\infty

For

\ell^{p,q}(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d})

We denote by
\ell^{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}),
the space of all complex sequences on
\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}
such that

\|c\|_{\ell^{\infty,\infty}}:=\displaystyle\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}}|c(k)|<\infty.

We note that
L^{p,p}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
and
\ell^{p,p}(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d})=\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d+1})
for

Lemma 1
.

Let
\displaystyle f\in M\left([a,b]\times\prod_{i=1}^{d}[c_{i},d_{i}]\right),\ 1\leq q<p<\infty,\ \frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1,\ \frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=1.
Then, we have

(i)

\displaystyle\tau_{r}\left(f;\delta;M\left([a,b]\times\prod_{i=1}^{d}[c_{i},d_{i}]\right)\right)_{1,1}\leq K_{1}\displaystyle\tau_{r}\left(f;\delta;M\left([a,b]\times\prod_{i=1}^{d}[c_{i},d_{i}]\right)\right)_{p,q}

(ii)

\displaystyle\tau_{r}\left(f;\delta;M\left([a,b]\times\prod_{i=1}^{d}[c_{i},d_{i}]\right)\right)_{p,q}\leq K_{2}\displaystyle\left\{\tau_{r}\left(f;\delta;M\left([a,b]\times\prod_{i=1}^{d}[c_{i},d_{i}]\right)\right)_{1,1}\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}},

where

\displaystyle\displaystyle K_{1}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle(b-a)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d}(d_{i}-c_{i})\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle K_{2}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left\{\displaystyle 2^{r}\sup_{(x,\mathbb{y})\in[a,b]\times\prod_{i=1}^{d}(d_{i}-c_{i})}|f(x,\mathbb{y})|\right\}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}.

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0+}\tau_{r}\left(f;\delta;M\left([a,b]\times\prod_{i=1}^{d}[c_{i},d_{i}]\right)\right)_{p,q}=0

(a) The proof of the proposition follows from the following inequalities

(a)

\|g\|_{1,1}\leq(b-a)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d}(d_{i}-c_{i})\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\|g\|_{p,q}

(b)

\|g\|_{p,q}\leq\left\{\displaystyle\sup_{(x,\mathbb{y})\in[a,b]\times\prod_{i=1}^{d}(d_{i}-c_{i})}|g(x,\mathbb{y})|\right\}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\|g\|_{1,1}^{\frac{1}{p}}.

By applying Holder’s inequality twice, we obtain the inequality
(a)
. Now, we prove the second inequality. By using the definition of mixed Lebesgue norm, we have

\displaystyle\|g\|_{p,q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\int_{[a,b]}\left(\int_{\prod_{i=1}^{d}[c_{i},d_{i}]}\left|g(x,\mathbb{y})\right|^{q.{\frac{p}{q}}}d\mathbb{y}\right)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\left\{\displaystyle\sup_{(x,\mathbb{y})\in[a,b]\times\prod_{i=1}^{d}(d_{i}-c_{i})}|g(x,\mathbb{y})|\right\}^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\displaystyle\left(\int_{[a,b]}\left(\int_{\prod_{i=1}^{d}[c_{i},d_{i}]}\left|g(x,\mathbb{y})\right|d\mathbb{y}\right)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

Hence, we get the required estimate.

f\in M\left([a,b]\times\prod_{i=1}^{d}[c_{i},d_{i}]\right)
. Then f is Riemann integrable if and only if

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0+}\tau_{r}\left(f;\delta;M\left([a,b]\times\prod_{i=1}^{d}[c_{i},d_{i}]\right)\right)_{p,q}=0.

Proof.

By lemma 1, it is sufficient to show that

f
is Riemann integrable if and only if

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0+}\tau_{r}\left(f;\delta;M\left([a,b]\times\prod_{i=1}^{d}[c_{i},d_{i}]\right)\right)_{1,1}=0.

As
L^{1,1}
norm on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
acts like an
L^{1}
norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
and the rest of the proof follows in the same line as in
[

]
.
∎

Now, we define the integral moduli of smoothness in the mixed Lebesgue spaces.

Definition 7
.

(i)

Let
1\leq p,q<\infty
and
f\in;L^{p,q}\left((a,b)\times\prod_{i=1}^{d}(c_{i},d_{i})\right).
Then the integral moduli of smoothness is defined by

\displaystyle\omega_{r}(f;\delta;L^{p,q}\left((a,b)\times\prod_{i=1}^{d}(c_{i},d_{i})\right)

\displaystyle:=\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq h\leq\delta}\left\{\int_{(a,b-rh)}\left(\int_{\prod_{i=1}^{d}(c_{i},d_{i}-rh)}|(\Delta_{h,\mathbb{h}}^{r}f)(u,\mathbb{v})|^{q}d\mathbb{v}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}du\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(ii)

Similarly for
f\in L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
the integral moduli of smoothness is defined by

\omega_{r}(f;\delta;L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})=\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq h\leq\delta}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|(\Delta_{h,\mathbb{h}}^{r}f)(u,\mathbb{v})|^{q}d\mathbb{v}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}du\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Proposition 3
.

If
f\in L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}),r\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq p,q\leq\infty,
then we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\omega_{r}(f;\delta;L^{p,q}\left((-n,n)\times(-n,n)^{d}\right)=\omega_{r}(f;\delta;L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})).

Proof.

From the definition of integral moduli of smoothness, we have

\omega_{r}(f;\delta;L^{p,q}\left((-n,n)\times(-n,n)^{d}\right)\leq\omega_{r}(f;\delta;L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})),\forall n\in\mathbb{N},

which implies that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\omega_{r}(f;\delta;L^{p,q}\left((-n,n)\times(-n,n)^{d}\right)\leq\omega_{r}(f;\delta;L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})).

(2.1)

Again, from the definition of integral moduli of smoothness for
0\leq h\leq\delta
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\omega_{r}(f;\delta;L^{p,q}\left((-n,n)\times(-n,n)^{d}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq h\leq\delta}\left\{\int_{(-n,n-rh)}\left(\int_{(-n,n-rh)^{d}}|(\Delta_{h,\mathbb{h}}^{r}f)(u,\mathbb{v})|^{q}d\mathbb{v}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}du\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\{\int_{(-n,n-rh)}\left(\int_{(-n,n-rh)^{d}}|(\Delta_{h,\mathbb{h}}^{r}f)(u,\mathbb{v})|^{q}d\mathbb{v}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}du\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|(\Delta_{h,\mathbb{h}}^{r}f)(u,\mathbb{v})|^{q}d\mathbb{v}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}du\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}=\omega_{r}(f;\delta;L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})),

which implies

\omega_{r}(f;\delta;L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}))\leq\omega_{r}(f;\delta;L^{p,q}\left((-n,n)\times(-n,n)^{d}\right)

(2.2)

We obtain the desired result by combining the estimates (2.1) and (2.2).
∎

3.
Subspaces of
L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})

In this section, we prove various properties of the subspaces of
L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Lemma 3
.

A function
f\in M(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\in\Lambda^{p,q}
if and only if there exist a
\Delta_{*}>0
such that
\|f\|_{l^{p,q}(\Sigma)}<\infty
for each admissible sequence
\Sigma
with lower mesh size
\underline{\Delta}\geq\Delta_{*}
.

Proof.

By the definition of
\Lambda^{p,q}
, we see that if
f\in\Lambda^{p,q}
then
\|f\|_{l^{p,q}(\Sigma)}<\infty
for any admissible partition
\Sigma
so we are done. Now, We show the converse part.

Let
\Sigma=(x_{k},y_{j})_{{k\in\mathbb{Z}},{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
be an arbitrary admissible sequence with lower mesh size
\underline{\Delta}>\Delta_{*}
. Now we can choose
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
m\underline{\Delta}\geq\Delta_{*}
. So we have

\Delta_{*}\leq m\underline{\Delta}\leq(x_{mk}-x_{m(k-1)})\leq m\bar{\Delta}

\Delta_{*}\leq m\underline{\Delta}\leq(y_{i,mj_{i}}-y_{i,m(j_{i}-1)})\leq m\bar{\Delta},\ \forall i=1,2,...,d.

From the above estimates, we see that the subsequence
(x_{mk},y_{mj})_{{k\in\mathbb{Z}},{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
has lower mesh size greater than equal to
\Delta_{*}
. Furthermore, for
N=(N_{0},N_{y})
where
N_{y}=(N_{1},...,N_{d})
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbb{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sup_{\mathbb{y}\in Q_{j}}|f(z)|^{q}=\sum_{N_{1}=0}^{m-1}...\sum_{N_{d}=0}^{m-1}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sup_{\mathbb{y}\in Q_{mj-N_{y}}}|f(z)|^{q}.

(3.1)

For all such
N_{y}
’s we have
Q_{mj-N_{y}}\subseteq\widetilde{Q_{mj}}:=\prod_{i=1}^{d}[x_{{i},m(j_{i}-1)+N_{i}},x_{{i},{mj_{i}}}).

Now, from the above relation
(3.1)
, we get

\sum_{\mathbb{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sup_{\mathbb{y}\in Q_{j}}|f(z)|^{q}\leq\sum_{N_{1}=0}^{m-1}...\sum_{N_{d}=0}^{m-1}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sup_{\mathbb{y}\in\widetilde{Q_{mj}}}|f(z)|^{q}=m^{d}\sum_{\mathbb{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sup_{\mathbb{y}\in\widetilde{Q_{mj}}}|f(z)|^{q}.

For
\infty>p\geq q\geq 1
, we obtain

\left(\sum_{\mathbb{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sup_{\mathbb{y}\in Q_{j}}|f(z)|^{q}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\leq m^{\frac{dp}{q}}\left(\sum_{\mathbb{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sup_{\mathbb{y}\in\widetilde{Q_{mj}}}|f(z)|^{q}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}.

Hence, we have

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sup_{z\in Q_{jk}}|f(z)|^{q}\Delta_{jk}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\leq m^{\frac{dp}{q}+1}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{\mathbb{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sup_{\mathbb{y}\in\widetilde{Q_{mjk}}}|f(z)|^{q}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}<\infty,

where
\widetilde{Q_{mjk}}=\widetilde{Q_{mj}}\times(x_{(mk-1)+N_{0}},x_{mk})
and these cubes corresponds to the admissible sequence
(x_{mk},y_{mj})_{{k\in\mathbb{Z}},{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
. By the definition of
\Lambda^{p,q}
, we obtain the converse part for
p\geq q
. Now, from
[
26
]
, we have if
p_{1}>p_{2}>0
then
\|f\|_{L^{p_{2},p_{1}}}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p_{1},p_{2}}}

so we get the result when
p<q
. Thus, the proof is completed.
∎

Proposition 5
.

If
1\leq p,q<\infty
, then
\Lambda^{p,q}
is a proper subspace of
L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

For
f(x,\mathbb{y})\in\Lambda^{p,q}
, we have to show that

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x,\mathbf{y})|^{q}d\mathbb{y}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<+\infty.

Let us take the admissible sequence as
\Sigma=(x_{k},\mathbb{y}_{\mathbb{j}})_{k\in\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
, where
x_{k}=k
and
y_{\mathbb{j}}=\mathbb{j}
,
\mathbb{j}=(j_{1},j_{2},...,j_{d})
. Then the corresponding cube is given by

Q_{\mathbb{j}k}=[k-1,k]\prod_{i=1}^{d}[j_{i}-1,j_{i}]

with volume
\Delta_{\mathbb{j}k}=1
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z},\ \mathbb{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

For
p>q\geq 1
, we have

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x,\mathbf{y})|^{q}d\mathbb{y}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\leq\left(\sum_{\mathbb{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sup_{\mathbb{y}\in Q_{\mathbb{j}}}|f(x,\mathbf{y})|^{q}d\mathbb{y}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}.

In view of
\Lambda^{p,q}
and the above inequality, we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x,\mathbf{y})|^{q}d\mathbb{y}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}dx\leq\sum_{k\in Z}\left(\sum_{\mathbb{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sup_{\mathbb{y}\in Q_{\mathbb{j}}k}|f(x,\mathbf{y})|^{q}d\mathbb{y}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}<\infty.

Following (corollary 3.5.2) in
[
26
]
, we obtain the result for
p<q
. This completes the proof.
∎

Now we want to find some suitable subspaces of
L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
for which the
\tau-
modulus is finite. We consider the following subspaces.

Definition 8
.

Let
1\leq p,q<\infty
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
. Then we define the following spaces:

(i)

The space
\mathcal{F}^{p,q}
by

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}^{p,q}:=\Bigg\{&f\in M(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}):\mbox{there exists}\ \eta,\zeta\ \mbox{such that}\\
&f(x,\mathbb{y})=\mathcal{O}\left({(1+|x|)^{(\frac{-1}{p}-\eta)}}{{(1+\|\mathbb{y}\|)}^{(\frac{-1}{q}-\zeta)}}\right)\Bigg\}.\end{split}

(ii)

The space
\Omega^{p,q}
by

\begin{split}\Omega^{p,q}:=\Bigg\{&f\in M(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}):|f(x,\mathbb{y})|\leq g(x,\mathbb{y}),\ g\in L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}),\\
&g\geq 0,\ \mbox{even and non-increasing}\Bigg\}.\end{split}

(iii)

The Sobolev spaces
W^{r}(L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}))
by

\begin{split}W^{r}(L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})):=\Bigg\{&f\in L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}):f(x,\mathbb{y})=\phi(x,\mathbb{y})\ a.e.,\phi\in AC_{loc}^{r}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\ \\
&\mbox{and}\ D^{\beta}\phi\in L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\forall|\beta|\leq r\Bigg\},\end{split}

where
\ D^{\beta}=\frac{\partial^{n_{0}+|N|}}{\partial x^{n_{0}}\partial y_{1}^{n_{1}}\partial y_{2}^{n_{2}}...\partial y_{d}^{n_{d}}}
,
\beta=(n_{0},N),N=(n_{1},n_{2}...,n_{d})
and
|\beta|=\sum_{i=1}^{d}n_{i}.

Proposition 6
.

The following statement holds

(i)

\Omega^{p,q}
and
W^{r}(L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}))
are linear spaces.

(ii)

\mathcal{F}^{p,q}\subsetneq\Omega^{p,q}\subsetneq\Lambda^{p,q}

Proof.

By the definition of
\Omega^{p,q}
and
W^{r}(L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}))
it is clear that these two spaces are linear.
Let
f(x,\mathbb{y})\in\mathcal{F}^{p,q}
. Then one can see that
|f(x,\mathbb{y})|\in L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
and hence
f\in\Omega^{p,q}.
Let
f\in\Omega^{p,q}
. Then by definition of
\Omega^{p,q}
,
f\in M(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
and there exist a function
g\in L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
|f(x,\mathbb{y})|\leq g(x,\mathbb{y})
. Now let
\Sigma=(x_{k},y_{j})_{{k\in\mathbb{Z}},{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
be an arbitrary admissible partition of
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We have to show that
\|f\|_{l^{p,q}(\Sigma)}<\infty
. For
p\geq q
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sup_{z\in Q_{jk}}|f(z)|^{q}\Delta_{jk}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sup_{z\in Q_{jk}}|g(z)|^{q}\Delta_{jk}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\bar{\Delta})^{\frac{p}{q}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sup_{z\in Q_{jk}}|g(z)|^{q}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\bar{\Delta})^{\frac{p}{q}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|g(z)|^{q}d\mathbb{y}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}dx<\infty.

Similar result is true for
p\leq q
by Corollary 3.5.2 in
[
26
]
. This implies that
\Omega^{p,q}\subset\Lambda^{p,q}
.

∎

Proposition 7

Let

Similarly, the result is true for

(x,\mathbb{y})\in[-n,n]\times[-n,n]^{d}
. Since

is arbitrary, we obtain
f(x,\mathbb{y})=0
for all
(x,\mathbb{y})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
∎

Error Estimation And An Interpolation Theorem

We define the modified Steklov function for
f\in\Lambda^{p,q}
as follows

f_{r,h}=-\sum_{j=1}^{r}(-1)^{j}{\binom{r}{j}}F_{r,h_{j}}

where
h_{j}=\frac{jh}{r}
,
h>0
,
r\in\mathbb{N}
and the usual Steklov function

F_{r,h}(x,\mathbb{y})=\frac{1}{h^{r(d+1)}}\int_{[0,h]}...\int_{[0,h]}\int_{[0,h]^{d}}...\int_{[0,h]^{d}}f\left(x+\sum_{i=1}^{r}u_{i},\mathbb{y}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{v}_{i}\right)d\mathbb{v}_{1}...d\mathbb{v}_{r}\ du_{1}...du_{r},

here
u_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{v}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
i=1,2,...,r.

Proposition 11
.

If
f\in\Lambda^{p,q}
,
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
,
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
h>0
, then we have
F_{r,h}\in\Lambda^{p,q}
.

Proof.

By using Jensen’s inequality, we have

|F_{r,h}(x,\mathbb{y})|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h^{r(d+1)}}\int_{[0,h]}...\int_{[0,h]}\int_{[0,h]^{d}}...\int_{[0,h]^{d}}\left|f\left(x+\sum_{i=1}^{r}u_{i},\mathbb{y}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{v}_{i}\right)\right|^{q}d\mathbb{v}_{1}...d\mathbb{v}_{r}\ du_{1}...du_{r}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{q}}{h^{r(d+1)}}\int_{[0,h]}...\int_{[0,h]}\int_{[0,h]^{d}}...\int_{[0,h]^{d}}\left|f\left(x+\sum_{i=1}^{r}u_{i},\mathbb{y}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{v}_{i}\right)-f(x,\mathbb{y})\right|^{q}d\mathbb{v}_{1}...d\mathbb{v}_{r}\ du_{1}...du_{r}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{2^{q}}{h^{r(d+1)}}\int_{[0,h]}...\int_{[0,h]}\int_{[0,h]^{d}}...\int_{[0,h]^{d}}|f(x,\mathbb{y})|^{q}d\mathbb{v}_{1}...d\mathbb{v}_{r}\ du_{1}...du_{r}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}.

Now, for any admissible sequence
(x_{k},\mathbb{y}_{\mathbb{j}})_{k\in\mathbb{Z},\ \mathbb{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
we obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{q}}{h^{r(d+1)}}\int_{[0,h]}...\int_{[0,h]}\int_{[0,h]^{d}}...\int_{[0,h]^{d}}|\Delta_{\sum_{i=1}^{r}u_{i},\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{v}_{i}}^{1}f(x,\mathbb{y})|^{q}d\mathbb{v}_{1}...d\mathbb{v}_{r}\ du_{1}...du_{r}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{q}}{h^{r(d+1)}}\int_{[0,h]}...\int_{[0,h]}\int_{[0,h]^{d}}...\int_{[0,h]^{d}}[\omega_{1}(f,(x,\mathbb{y}),2(r+1)h,M(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})]^{q}d\mathbb{v}_{1}...d\mathbb{v}_{r}\ du_{1}...du_{r}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{q}\sup_{(x,\mathbb{y})\in Q_{\mathbb{j}k}}[\omega_{1}(f,(x,\mathbb{y}),2(r+1)h,M(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})]^{q}.

Now for
I_{2}
, we have

I_{2}\leq 2^{q}\sup_{(x,\mathbb{y})\in Q_{\mathbb{j}k}}|f(x,\mathbb{y})|^{q}.

Combining
I_{1}\ \mbox{and}\ I_{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{(x,\mathbb{y})\in Q_{\mathbb{j}k}}|F_{r,h}(x,\mathbb{y})|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{q}\sup_{(x,\mathbb{y})\in Q_{\mathbb{j}k}}[\omega_{1}(f,(x,\mathbb{y}),2(r+1)h,M(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})]^{q}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2^{q}\sup_{(x,\mathbb{y})\in Q_{\mathbb{j}k}}|f(x,\mathbb{y})|^{q}

Now, taking summation on both sides and using triangle inequality, we get

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{\mathbb{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sup_{(x,\mathbb{y})\in Q_{\mathbb{j}k}}|F_{r,h}(x,\mathbb{y})|^{q}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{p}\left(\sum_{\mathbb{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sup_{(x,\mathbb{y})\in Q_{\mathbb{j}k}}[\omega_{1}(f,(x,\mathbb{y}),2(r+1)h,M(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})]^{q}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2^{p}\left(\sum_{\mathbb{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sup_{(x,\mathbb{y})\in Q_{\mathbb{j}k}}|f(x,\mathbb{y})|^{q}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}.

Again, summing over all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
on both sides and in view of Proposition 6, we obtain
F_{r,h}\in\Lambda^{p,q}
.
∎

Lemma 4

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{h^{r(d+1)}}\int_{[0,h]}...\int_{[0,h]}\int_{[0,h]^{d}}...\int_{[0,h]^{d}}\sum_{j=1}^{r}(-1)^{j}{\binom{r}{j}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h^{r(d+1)}}\int_{[0,h]}...\int_{[0,h]}\int_{[0,h]^{d}}...\int_{[0,h]^{d}}\left\|\Delta_{\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{r}u_{i}}{r},\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{v}_{i}}{r}}^{1}f(x,\mathbb{y})\right\|_{p,q}

\displaystyle d\mathbb{v}_{1}...d\mathbb{v}_{r}\ du_{1}...du_{r}.

.
To prove the remaining two estimates, we represent the usual forward difference in terms of mixed differences as follows:

\Delta_{h,\mathbb{h}}^{\beta}=\Delta_{h_{0}}^{n_{0}}\Delta_{\mathbb{h}}^{|N|}=\Delta_{h_{0}}^{n_{0}}\Delta_{h_{1}}^{n_{1}}...\Delta_{h_{d}}^{n_{d}},

The proof of this claim follows from the representation of the finite difference as mentioned above and Proposition 21 (i). The representation allows us to use recursively one-dimensional estimate

Proposition 13

Let

Proof.

Let
\Sigma=(x_{k},y_{j})_{{k\in\mathbb{Z}},\ {j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}

\displaystyle\|\omega_{r}(f;.;2h;M(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}))\|_{l^{p,q}(\Sigma)}\leq c(r)\tau_{r}\left(f;h+\frac{\overline{\Delta}}{r};M(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\right)_{p,q}.

5.
Rate of Convergence of Neural Network Sampling Operators in Mixed Lebesgue Spaces

In this section, we establish the rate of approximation for Neural Network Sampling Operators in mixed Lebesgue spaces. Before discussing the approximation results, we shall define the algebraic and absolute moment as follows:

Let
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Then for any
\eta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},

n=(n_{1},n_{2})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
with
|n|=n_{1}+n_{2}=\eta,
we define the algebraic moments of order
\eta
as

\displaystyle m_{(n_{1},n_{2})}(\psi,u,v):=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\psi(u-k,v-j)(k-u)^{n_{1}}(j-v)^{n_{2}}

and the absolute moments are defined by

\displaystyle M_{(n_{1},n_{2})}(\psi):=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}|\psi(u-k,v-j)|\ |(k-u)|^{n_{1}}|(j-v)|^{n_{2}}.

We define

\displaystyle M_{\eta}(\psi):=\max_{|p|=\eta}\ M_{(n_{1},n_{2})}(\psi).
We note that
\displaystyle M_{\eta}(\psi)<\infty,
for every
0\leq\eta\leq\beta
in view of section 6 of (See[
[
22
]
]). We can easily see that for
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
\xi<\eta,

M_{\eta}(\chi)<\infty
implies that
M_{\xi}(\chi)<\infty.

Let
\psi(x,y)
be a sigmoidal function and let
I=[-1,1]
. For
f:I\times I\to\mathbb{R},
the Neural Network Sampling Operators are defined by

F_{n}f(x,y)=\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{k=-n}^{n}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}f{\left(\frac{k}{n},\frac{j}{n}\right)}\psi_{\sigma}(nx-k,ny-j)}{\displaystyle\sum_{k=-n}^{n}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}\psi_{\sigma}(nx-k,ny-j)}.

Now we shall recall some of the crucial properties enjoyed by
\displaystyle\psi_{\sigma}(x,y)
(See [
[
21
]
]).

Lemma 5
.

(i)

For every
u,v\in\mathbb{R}
, we have
\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\psi(u-k,v-j)=1.

(ii)

The series
\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\psi(u-k,v-j)
converges uniformly on the compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}.

(iii)

For
u,v\in I
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\frac{1}{\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\psi(nu-k,nv-j)}\leq\frac{1}{\psi_{\sigma}(1,1)}.

In view of the above Lemma, it is easy to see that the above NN operators
F_{n}
are well-defined. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle|F_{n}f(x,y)|\leq\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{k=-n}^{n}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}\left|f{\left(\frac{k}{n},\frac{j}{n}\right)}\right|\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(nx-k,ny-j)}{\displaystyle\sum_{k=-n}^{n}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(nx-k,ny-j)}\leq\frac{\|f\|_{\infty}}{\psi_{\sigma}(1,1)}M_{(0,0)}(\psi_{\sigma})<+\infty.

Lemma 6
.

Let
\psi(x,y)
be a multivariate sigmoidal function defined earlier,
f:I\times I\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded measurable function then,

\|F_{n}f\|_{p,q}\leq\|f\|_{\ell^{p,q}(\Sigma)}(\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(1,1))^{-\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q})},

where
\Sigma=\Sigma_{x}\times\Sigma{y}
is an admissible partition such that
\Sigma_{x}=\left\{\dfrac{k}{n}:k=-n,...,n\right\}
and
\Sigma_{y}=\left\{\dfrac{j}{n}:j=-n,...,n\right\}
.

Proof.

By the definition of the
L^{p,q}
norm and using Jensen’s inequality multiple times, we obtain

\displaystyle\|F_{n}f\|_{p,q}^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\left(\int_{-1}^{1}\left|\frac{\sum_{k=-n}^{n}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}f{(\frac{k}{n},\frac{j}{n})}\psi_{\sigma}(nx-k,ny-j)}{\sum_{k=-n}^{n}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}\psi_{\sigma}(nx-k,ny-j)}\right|^{q}dy\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\phi_{\sigma}(1)^{\frac{p}{q}}}\frac{\int_{-1}^{1}\left(\sum_{k=-n}^{n}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}\left|f{(\frac{k}{n},\frac{j}{n})}\right|^{q}\left(\int_{-1}^{1}\phi_{\sigma}(ny-j)dy\right)\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}dx}{\sum_{k=-n}^{n}\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\phi_{\sigma}(1)^{\frac{p}{q}+1}}\left(\sum_{k=-n}^{n}\left(\sum_{j=-n}^{n}\left|f{\left(\frac{k}{n},\frac{j}{n}\right)}\right|^{q}\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\frac{1}{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\|f\|_{\ell^{p,q}(\Sigma)}^{p}}{(\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(1,1))^{-\frac{1}{2}(\frac{p}{q}+1)}}.

By taking
p^{th}
square root on both sides of the above expression, we get the desired estimate.
∎

Now, we will estimate the approximation error in terms of the
\tau
-modulus of smoothness in
L^{p,q}
norm.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\sigma
be a sigmoidal function satisfying
(3)
with
\beta\geq 2\max{(p,q)}
,
1\leq q\leq p<\infty
. Then for any
f\in M(I\times I)
, we have

\|F_{n}f-f\|_{p,q}\leq C_{1}\tau_{1}\left(f,\frac{1}{n}\right)_{p,q}+C_{2}\tau_{2}\left(f,\frac{1}{n}\right)_{p,q},

where
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
C_{1},C_{2}
are positive constants.

Proof.

Let
0<h\leq 1
. Putting
r=2
in Proposition
12
we have,

\|F_{n}f-f\|_{p,q}\leq\|F_{n}f-F_{n}f_{2,h}\|_{p,q}+\|F_{n}f_{2,h}-f_{2,h}\|_{p,q}+\|f_{2,h}-f\|_{p,q}.

Now using Lemma
5
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|F_{n}f-F_{n}f_{2,h}\|_{p,q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|F_{n}(f-f_{2,h})\|_{p,q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f-f_{2,h}\|_{\ell^{p,q}(\Sigma)}(\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(1,1))^{-\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(1,1))^{-\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q})}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{\mathbb{j}\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\omega_{2}\left(f;\left(\frac{k}{n},\frac{j}{n}\right);2h\right)\right)^{q}\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{\frac{1}{q}+6}}{(\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(1,1))^{-\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q})}}\ \tau_{2}\left(f,h+\frac{1}{2n}\right)_{p,q}.

Now, in view of Proposition
12\ (iii)
, we get

\|f_{2,h}-f\|_{p,q}\leq K(r)\ \tau_{r}(f,2h)_{p,q}.

Further,
f_{2,h}\in W_{p,q}^{2}
. Using local Taylor formula for
f
at
(x,y)
, there exists a bounded function
\zeta
such that
\lim_{(t,v)\to(0,0)}\zeta(t,v)=0
and we have

\displaystyle f_{2,h}\left(\frac{k}{n},\frac{j}{n}\right)=

\displaystyle f_{2,h}(x,y)+\frac{\partial f_{2,h}}{\partial x}(x,y)\left(\frac{j}{n}-y\right)+\frac{\partial f_{2,h}}{\partial y}(x,y)\left(\frac{j}{n}-y\right)

\displaystyle+\ \frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}f_{2,h}}{\partial x^{2}}(x,y)\left(\frac{k}{n}-x\right)^{2}+\ \frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}f_{2,h}}{\partial y^{2}}(x,y)\left(\frac{j}{n}-y\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\ \frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial f_{2,h}}{\partial x\partial y}(x,y)\left(\frac{k}{n}-x\right)\left(\frac{j}{n}-y\right)

\displaystyle+\ \zeta\left(\frac{k}{n}-x,\frac{j}{n}-y\right)\left[\left(\frac{k}{n}-x\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{j}{n}-y\right)^{2}\right].

From the definition of the NN operator, we have

\displaystyle F_{n}f_{2,h}(x,y)=

\displaystyle f_{2,h}(x,y)+\frac{\frac{\partial f_{2,h}}{\partial x}(x,y)}{n}\frac{\sum_{k=-n}^{n}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}(k-nx)\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(nx-k,ny-j)}{\sum_{k=-n}^{n}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(nx-k,ny-j)}

\displaystyle+\frac{\frac{\partial f_{2,h}}{\partial y}(x,y)}{n}\frac{\sum_{k=-n}^{n}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}(j-ny)\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(nx-k,ny-j)}{\sum_{k=-n}^{n}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}\ \psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(nx-k,ny-j)}

\displaystyle+\frac{\frac{\partial^{2}f_{2,h}}{\partial x^{2}}(x,y)}{n^{2}}\frac{\sum_{k=-n}^{n}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}(k-nx)^{2}\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(nx-k,ny-j)}{\sum_{k=-n}^{n}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(nx-k,ny-j)}

\displaystyle+\frac{\frac{\partial^{2}f_{2,h}}{\partial y^{2}}(x,y)}{n^{2}}\frac{\sum_{k=-n}^{n}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}(j-ny)^{2}\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(nx-k,ny-j)}{\sum_{k=-n}^{n}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(nx-k,ny-j)}

\displaystyle+\frac{\frac{\partial^{2}f_{2,h}}{\partial x\partial y}(x,y)}{n^{2}}\frac{\sum_{k=-n}^{n}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}(k-nx)(j-ny)\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(nx-k,ny-j)}{\sum_{k=-n}^{n}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(nx-k,ny-j)}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{n^{2}}\frac{\sum_{k=-n}^{n}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}\zeta\left(\frac{k}{n}-x,\frac{j}{n}-y\right)\left[\left(\frac{k}{n}-x\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{j}{n}-y\right)^{2}\right]\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(nx-k,ny-j)}{\sum_{k=-n}^{n}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(nx-k,ny-j)}

\displaystyle=f(x,y)+A_{1}+A_{2}+A_{3}+A_{4}+A_{5}+R.

Using Minkowski inequality in the above expression, we get

\|F_{n}f_{2,h}-f_{2,h}\|_{p,q}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{5}\|A_{i}\|_{p,q}+\|R\|_{p,q}.

One can easily find the following estimates by using Jensen’s inequality to each
A_{i},\ i=1,...,5
with Theorem
12\ (iii)
.

\|A_{1}\|_{p,q}\leq\frac{M_{1,0}(\psi_{\bar{\sigma}})}{\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(1,1)}\frac{1}{n}\left\|\frac{\partial f_{2,h}}{\partial x}\right\|_{p,q}\leq C_{1}^{\prime}\frac{1}{n}h^{-1}\tau_{1}(f,h).

\|A_{2}\|_{p,q}\leq\frac{M_{0,1}(\psi_{\bar{\sigma}})}{\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(1,1)}\frac{1}{n}\left\|\frac{\partial f_{2,h}}{\partial y}\right\|_{p,q}\leq C_{2}^{\prime}\frac{1}{n}h^{-1}\tau_{1}(f,h).

\|A_{3}\|_{p,q}\leq\frac{M_{2,0}(\psi_{\bar{\sigma}})}{\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(1,1)}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}f_{2,h}}{\partial x^{2}}\right\|_{p,q}\leq C_{3}^{\prime}\frac{1}{n^{2}}h^{-2}\tau_{2}(f,h).

\|A_{4}\|_{p,q}\leq\frac{M_{0,2}(\psi_{\bar{\sigma}})}{\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(1,1)}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}f_{2,h}}{\partial y^{2}}\right\|_{p,q}\leq C_{4}^{\prime}\frac{1}{n^{2}}h^{-2}\tau_{2}(f,h).

\|A_{5}\|_{p,q}\leq\frac{M_{1,1}(\psi_{\bar{\sigma}})}{\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(1,1)}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x\partial y}\right\|_{p,q}\leq C_{5}^{\prime}\frac{1}{n^{2}}h^{-2}\tau_{2}(f,h).

Now, we will estimate the norm for the remainder as follows:

\displaystyle\|R\|_{p,q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{n^{2}}\frac{\sum_{k=-n}^{n}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}\zeta\left(\frac{k}{n}-x,\frac{j}{n}-y\right)\left[\left(\frac{k}{n}-x\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{j}{n}-y\right)^{2}\right]\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(nx-k,ny-j)}{\sum_{k=-n}^{n}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(nx-k,ny-j)}\right\|_{p,q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle D_{1}+D_{2}.

where,
D_{1}
is estimated by

\displaystyle D_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{n^{2}}\frac{\sum_{k=-n}^{n}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}\zeta\left(\frac{k}{n}-x,\frac{j}{n}-y\right)\left(\frac{k}{n}-x\right)^{2}\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(nx-k,ny-j)}{\sum_{k=-n}^{n}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(nx-k,ny-j)}\right\|_{p,q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\zeta\|_{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\left\|\frac{\sum_{k=-n}^{n}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}\left(\frac{k}{n}-x\right)^{2}\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(nx-k,ny-j)}{\sum_{k=-n}^{n}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(nx-k,ny-j)}\right\|_{p,q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\zeta\|_{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\frac{M_{2,0}(\psi_{\bar{\sigma}})}{\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(1,1)}.

Similarly, we obtain

D_{2}\leq\|\zeta\|_{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\frac{M_{0,2}(\psi_{\bar{\sigma}})}{\psi_{\bar{\sigma}}(1,1)}.

Since
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
are independent of
f
and
\zeta
, so for sufficiently large
n
, we obtain that
D_{1},D_{2}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. This implies that
R\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Hence, by combining all the estimates, we get the desired estimate.
∎

7.
Conclusion

The rate of approximation of neural network operators in-terms of averaged modulus of smoothness is proved in this paper. In order to achieve our approximation, the properties of averaged modulus of smoothness has been discussed in mixed norm. Further, suitable subspaces
\Lambda^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}),
of
L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})
are identified and established that averaged modulus of smoothness is finite for all functions in

\Lambda^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}).
Using the properties of averaged modulus of smoothness, the rate of approximation of certain linear operators in mixed Lebesgue norm is estimated. As an application of these linear operators, rate of approximation of neural network operators in-terms of averaged modulus of smoothness in mixed norm has been proved. Finally, some examples of sigmoidal functions has been discussed. Using these sigmoidal functions, implementation of continuous and discontinuous functions by neural network operators has been provided.

Determination of the spatial patterns and influencing factors of agricultural cultural heritages in the six provinces of central China via GIS
Authors: Hui Xiang, Lelin Zhang, Baofa Peng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractUnderstanding the spatial pattern of agricultural cultural heritages (ACHs) is the prerequisite and foundation for their innovative and development. Based on GIS technologies, this paper analyzes the spatial pattern of ACHs in the six provinces of central China, explores the main influencing factors, and draws the following conclusions: (1) The quantity of ACHs has grown rapidly in batches, and they mainly consist of planting and non-planting categories. The fourth and seventh batches grew the fastest, increasing by 300% and 500% respectively. The proportion of planting is 43.59%, and that of non-planting is 48.72%. (2) The ACHs in the study area is generally scattered, which is more in the east and south, and less in the west and north, forming a tripartite spatial pattern. The NNI in most provinces are greater than 1, indicating a dispersed state. There are 25 ACHs in the eastern and southern, with two higher and one medium density zones. (3) The key influencing factors are rivers, topography, economy, and transportation. 56.42% of ACHs are distributed within 0-1 km of rivers, 71.80% within 0-4 km, and 92.31% within 0-8 km. Elevation has a negative impact on the distribution of ACHs, with more pronounced effects in the west and north. Economy has a significant positive impact on the distribution of ACHs, with economically developed regions exhibiting a higher number, while economically backward areas fewer. 92.30% ACHs are concentrated in highways, 79.48% in national highways, 94.87% in provincial highways, and 76.92% in railways. (4) It should focus on the development of ACHs in the Yangtze River Basin, integrating the resources , improving the accessibility of transportation networks, and establishing a differentiated policy incentive system.Keyword: The six provinces in central China, ACHs, Spatial pattern, Influence factorsSubject terms: Ecology, Ecology, Environmental social sciences, Geography, Geography

Introduction

In July 2022, Xi Jinping emphasized that “protecting ACHs is a common responsibility of humanity” and called for “adhering to the protection in excavation and inheritance in utilization”1. ACHs refer to the unique agricultural landscapes2 formed through long-term collaborative evolution and dynamic adaptation between rural areas and the environments3. They represent an important asset and valuable essence created by humans through long-term agricultural practices4, embodying the crystallization of ancient farming wisdom5 and carrying unique, regional6, and diverse culture and humanistic spirit7. Therefore, the study of ACHs holds significant value in inheriting agricultural knowledge and culture8, and enhancing national cultural confidence9.

With the increasing emphasis on the utilization and protection of ACHs, its related research has been continuously increasing10. Relevant literature is primarily based on fields ofcultural studies11, tourism12, agronomy13, sociology14, and interdisciplinary15. Research methods have gradually shifted from qualitative analysis to a combination of qualitative and quantitative methods16. Qualitative methods include literature analysis, logical reasoning, historical comparison, et al17. These methods have the advantages of simplicity and practicality18, but lack case studies19. Quantitative methods include survey interview20, comprehensive indicator calculations21, structural equation modeling22, and geographic information technology22. The results are scientific, reasonable, and objective23, which can compensate for the shortcomings of qualitative analysis24. Therefore, the combination of qualitative and quantitative analysis has become the mainstream methods. Existing research often focuses on the development, utilization, and protection of ACHs from a macro scale25, concentrating on large-scale regions such as countries and continents26, with a lack of research at the medium and small scales27. Research content mainly focuses on the inheritance and protection, ecological value, landscape characteristics28, tourism development potential29, and development suitability30. Understanding the spatial distribution pattern of ACHs is the premise and foundation for their innovative and scientific exploitation31, which can avoid blindness and arbitrariness32. However, research on such topics is rare. ACHs, a new type of cultural heritage, combines natural and humanistic characteristics, and its spatial distribution is also influenced by various natural and human factors. However, there is a greater lack of literature exploring multiple influencing factors.

The six provinces in central China have superior natural conditions, a large farming population, and a long agricultural history. Through long-term farming practices, they have formed a rich and diverse ACHs33. With the continuous improvement of transportation and the implementation of policies integrating culture and tourism, the development of ACHs has gained increasing attention34. However, ACHs industries face severe challenges, including sensitive and fragile ecological environment, uneven spatial distribution of resources, and significant pressure on their transformation35,36. In view of this, we select the six provinces in central China as typical case, analyze the spatial patterns, explore the main influencing factors, and discuss measures for the inheritance and development of ACHs. This research has important theoretical value and practical significance: firstly, it can deepen the research content of ACHs, construct interdisciplinary research ideas, expand related research fields37, and provide methodological references for future research38; secondly, it is conducive to scientifically planning, innovating and inheriting of Chinese traditional culture39, and facilitating the rational utilization of ACHs, the deep integration of culture and tourism industries, and the orderly development of rural economy and society21.

The study area

The six provinces in central China are located in the hinterland of Chinese Mainland (Fig. 1), including Henan, Shanxi, Hubei, Anhui, Hunan, and Jiangxi. This region has obvious geographical advantages, being at the core position connecting the east and west, and the south and north. The area is about 1.028 × 106 km2, accounting for 10.7% of China’s total area, and the population is 368 million, accounting for 25% of the total. The agricultural production is naturally superior, and it is a concentrated area of agricultural provinces. At the same time, this region has a prominent strategic position in agricultural development, and it is an important agricultural production base for grain, fishery, and medicinal materials. In addition, this region has a long agricultural history, with diverse types of agricultural products and obvious differences. Therefore, the ACHs in the study area are unique, diverse, and heterogeneous.

Data sources

ACHs data in the study area is sourced from the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs of the People’s Republic of China (http://www.moa.gov.cn). Statistics show that there are 39 ACHs in the six provinces. Among them, the numbers in Anhui, Henan, Hubei, Hunan, Jiangxi, and Shanxi are 8, 5, 5, 9, 8, and 4, respectively. The spatial geographic coordinate data of ACHs were obtained through the coordinate system (API) of Amap (https://www.amap.com/), and manual verification and identification correction were adopted. The data of administrative boundaries, rivers, DEM, and transportation lines were sourced from the website of Chinese scientific research departments (http://www.resdc.cn/). Socioeconomic data is sourced from statistical yearbooks, statistical annual reports, and socioeconomic development bulletins of the six provinces. In the above data, DEM is raster data (30 m), while the rest are vector data.

Agriculture is categorized into farming, animal husbandry, forestry and fruit industry, fishery, and complex agriculture, each of which has formed its corresponding type of ACHs. Statistics reveal that the ACHs in the study area consists of three categories: planting, non-planting (including animal husbandry, forestry, fishery, et al.), and complex agriculture. Therefore, ACHs are classified into planting, non-planting, and complex categories. The statistical results in batches are shown in Table 1.

The number of ACHs in the six provinces of central China has shown a rapid growth trend across different batches. Factors affecting the spatial pattern of ACHs

Rivers

Agricultural development is inseparable from water, and the distribution of ACHs is also closely related to it (Fig. 5). The ACHs in the research area are concentrated in the Yangtze River (29), followed by the Yellow River (9) and the Huai River (1) (Fig. 5a). Therefore, the Yangtze River Basin is the core area of ACHs, covering the northwestern Hunan, southern Anhui, and northern Jiangxi. 16 items are distributed along the main streams or first tributaries (Fig. 5b), 28 items along second and above tributaries (Fig. 5c), 37 items along third and above tributaries (Fig. 5d), and the remaining 2 items along lower tributaries (Fig. 5a). The above data indicates that ACHs in the six provinces of central China is concentrated in the main streams and tributaries of rivers. Therefore, the ACHs in the case site has hydrophilicity.

The rivers and ACHs in the six provinces of central China.

22 ACHs (accounting for 56.42%) are distributed within a range of 0-1 km from the river (Fig. 6a), 28 (71.80%) within 0–4 km (Fig. 6b), and 36 (92.31%) within 0-8 km (Fig. 6c). Therefore, the majority ACHs in the study area are distributed within 8 km from the river. Water is an important agricultural resource, areas close to water have favorable natural conditions, laying a good foundation for the formation of rich and diverse ACHs.

Elevation

The six provinces in central China are located on the third tier of China, including the Loess Plateau, North China Plain, the Middle and Lower Yangtze Valley Plain, and the Jiangnan Hills. The terrain is higher in the west and lower in the east, with vast plain areas. The GWR results reveal (Fig. 7a) that the influence of elevation is negative and has spatial characteristics of higher in the north and west, and lower in the south and east. The above results indicate that elevation has a negative impact on the distribution of ACHs, that is, as elevation increases, the quantity of ACHs decreases. Meanwhile, there are spatial differences in its influence, with more pronounced effects in the west and north. The reason is that the western and northern parts have higher terrain, making it more difficult in agricultural cultivation. Therefore, the distribution of ACHs in the study area has a clear lower-altitude orientation, with significant differences in the number of ACHs in different topographies, and a general decreasing trend with increasing altitude.

Economy

The results of GWR indicate that, except for a few regions (Hubei and Jiangxi), economy in the majority areas has a positive impact on ACHs (Figure 7). Areas with higher economic level, there are also more ACHs, and vice versa. Hunan and Anhui are exhibiting a most positive impact, they are areas both per capita GDP and the number of ACHs are higher than the average value. The formation of ACHs requires a large amount of material, financial, and technological investment, which are closely related to the economy. The economy in Shanxi and Hunan has moderate influence, meaning a lower level of economic development and a small number of ACHs. The formation of ACHs are constrained by economic conditions, and economic backwardness is not conducive to the formation and development of ACHs. Hubei and Jiangxi are the negative impact types, meaning a trade-off relationship between the two indicators. On the whole, empirical data from most provinces (66.67%) in the study area indicates that there is a correlation between economy and the number of ACHs. Economically developed regions have more ACHs, while less developed areas fewer, so economy has a positive effect on the formation of ACHs.

Transportation

Agricultural activities rely on transportation, making transportation a key factor influencing ACHs (Fig. 8). To reduce transportation time and costs, ACHs are always close to the transportation lines. At the same time, convenient transportation bring a large flow of travellers, materials, and information, expanding the customer market and influence of ACHs. As shown in Fig. 8, ACHs exhibit a characteristic of clustering or expanding along transportation or at intersections of multiple routes. Among them, 92.30% ACHs are clustered near highways (Fig. 8a), 79.48% near national roads, 94.87% near provincial roads, and 76.92% near railways. In all, transportation influences the distribution of ACHs, with highways and provincial roads having the greatest impact, followed by national roads and railways. Therefore, transportation is important driving forces, and areas with denser transportation lines also have more ACHs.

Discussion

Research implications

Research implications

ACHs are valuable asset inhuman history4, which not only embodies the wisdom and creativity of agricultural populations, but also carries rich biodiversity8, ecosystems9, and landscape values53. Under the impact of globalization and modernization, the inheritance and development of ACHs face many challenges. This study found that there is a lack of complex ACHs, so it is necessary to strengthen the management of such type. At the same time, we found ACHs concentrated in the Yangtze River Basin, the southern part of the study area. The Yangtze River Basin has developed economy, dense population, and convenient transportation, and the consumption potential is huge. In addition, the study confirmed that lower altitude areas and economically developed regions are conducive to the formation and development of ACHs. The possible reason is that the lower altitude areas have superior natural conditions and agricultural resources, providing a good foundation for the formation of ACHs. Therefore, attention should be paid to the mountainous areas, and corresponding policy incentives and financial support should be provided.

In the future, we should focus on the development of ACHs in the Yangtze River Basin. Firstly, integrating the resources of the Yangtze River Basin. Building innovation centers in economically developed areas such as Hunan and Hubei, establishing a mechanism for sharing scientific research achievements and cultural industries. Developing technologies for cultural heritage protection and restoration, virtual reality, and innovative design to enhance technological innovation capabilities. Secondly, increasing investment in infrastructure. Improving the accessibility of transportation networks, to reduce circulation costs and market barriers. Finally, establishing a differentiated policy incentive system. For areas with abundant ACHs but backward economy and fragile ecology, it is important to develop ecological function of ACHs. Encouraging local residents to adopt the concept of reasonable protection and scientific development through ecological compensation, organic agriculture, and ecotourism. And areas with good ecology and rich ACHs, it should develop leisure agriculture and rural tourism, to promote the employment and income of local residents. At the same time, support policies such as financial projects, land priority, technological assistance, and talent cultivation will be provided to enhance the overall competitiveness of cultural industry.

(2)Comparison with existing research

Comparison with existing research

This article believed that the quantity of ACHs in the research area is growing rapidly, and this conclusion is consistent with other regions of China54,55. In recent years, China has implemented strategies of rural cultural revitalization and cultural heritage protection, which have promoted the protection, innovation, and inheritance of Chinese traditional culture15–17. At the same time, it also indicates that China has shifted from the stage of economic high-speed growth11 to that of high-quality development of economic and cultural synergy14,17.

This article also found that elevation has a negative impact on ACHs, which is similar to the conclusions of existing research56,57. Existing literature has confirmed that Christian cultural heritage is concentrated in the plains of western Europe56, while world cultural heritage sites are concentrated near coastal areas of lower altitude57. Possible reasons may be that lower altitude areas have fertile soil8, abundant water, and concentrated populations10, making it easier for human civilization to form53. In addition, this article found that economy has a positive impact on cultural heritage. Financial support are conducive to the formation of ACHs55, and economically developed regions receiving more financial support56.

In all, the conclusions of this study are scientific and reasonable.

(3)Contributions and prospects

Contributions and prospects

The spatial analysis and mapping techniques of geography can achieve the spatial quantification of information44, and are widely applied in numerous fields58. However, these methods are rarely applied in research on ACHs44. Therefore, we employed GIS techniques, including kernel density, buffer zone, graphic mapping and GWR, to examine the spatial characteristics of ACHs in the six provinces of central China and identify the influencing factors. Our research provides methodological guidance and technical references for studies on geography, culture, and agronomy. Furthermore, this work contributes to the scientific management of cultural resources and the high-quality development of China’s cultural industries.

ACHs are also an important tourism resource. Developing tourism is a crucial way for achieving sustainable economic development, innovative inheritance, and scientific protection of ACHs. However, this article does not delve into tourism development of which. Future research could focus on the spatial heterogeneity, barriers, and development models of ACHs tourism, and further explore these aspects.

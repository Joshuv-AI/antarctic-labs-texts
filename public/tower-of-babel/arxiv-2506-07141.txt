Highly efficient linear energy stable methods for preserving the original energy dissipation law of the incompressible Navier-Stokes equation
Zihan Weng, Qi Hong, Yuezheng Gong \comma \corrauth

Abstract
In this paper, we introduce a comprehensive computational framework to construct highly efficient linear energy stable methods for the incompressible Navier-Stokes equation, which preserve the original energy dissipation law. By multiplying the convection term by an identity-one term and incorporating a zero stabilization term, we recast the original model as a strongly equivalent system, while ensuring the retention of the original energy dissipation law. Such nonlinear system is then discretized in time based on the Crank-Nicolson schemes and the backward differentiation formulas, resulting in highly efficient time-discrete schemes. The proposed schemes are designed to preserve the original energy dissipation law while requiring only the solutions of three linear Stokes systems and a 2 × 2 2\times 2 linear system at each time step. The finite difference approximation on a staggered grid is employed for the time-discrete systems to derive fully discrete energy stable schemes, which are proven to preserve the original energy dissipation law and be uniquely solvable. We present the efficient implementation of these methods. Various numerical experiments are carried out to verify the accuracy, efficacy, and advantageous performance of our newly developed methods.

Introduction

The incompressible Navier-Stokes(NS) equations play an important role in today’s fluid dynamics simulations. In this paper, we consider the dimensionless incompressible Navier-Stokes equations

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\bm{u}_{t}-\nu\Delta\bm{u}+\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\bm{u}+\nabla p=\bm{f},&\ \mathrm{in}\ \Omega\times(0,T],\\[4.26773pt]
\nabla\cdot\bm{u}=0,&\ \mathrm{in}\ \Omega\times(0,T],\end{array}\right.

where the velocity
\bm{u}(\bm{x},t)
subject to the initial condition
\bm{u}(\bm{x},0)=\bm{u}_{0}(\bm{x})
and the Dirichlet boundary conditions or the periodic boundary conditions.
\bm{x}
and
t
are the spatial coordinates and time, respectively and in (
1
),
\Omega
is an open bounded domain in
R^{d}(d=2,3)
. The notation
p(\bm{x},t)
is the pressure and
\nu
is the kinematic viscosity which means the inverse of the Reynolds number
Re
.
\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\bm{u}
is the nonlinear convection and
\bm{f}
is an external force.

There are various numerical methods for the incompressible NS equations, and one class of the most popular schemes is the projection method, which was originally proposed by Chorin
[
4
]
and Temam
[
27
]
. The projection methods consist of a prediction step for the viscous effects and a projection step for the divergence-free conditions, which successfully decouple the momentum equations and arrive at efficient calculation. Whereas, artificial boundary conditions are introduced for the pressure which can lead to boundary layers and reduce the convergence rates
[
13
,
7
,
8
]
. To tackle this issue, the gauge formulation was developed by using the Helmholtz-Hodge decomposition to reformulate (
1
) in terms of two auxiliary variables
[
9
,
15
]
. The main advantages of this formulation are that one can choose the boundary conditions flexibly and arrive at a decoupled system by treating the convection term explicitly. However, it still remains challenging for the schemes of gauge system to achieve energy stability.

It is known that the incompressible NS equations satisfy the energy dissipation law with the periodic or the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions

(3)

\displaystyle\bm{u}|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

Given the kinetic energy
E[\bm{u}]=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\bm{u}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\bm{x}
in a smooth domain
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and ignoring the external force, system (
1
) has the energy dissipation law

(4)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E[\bm{u}]=-\nu\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\bm{u}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\bm{x}.

By rewriting the convection term into an antisymmetry form and applying the Crank-Nicolson method, one can arrive a second-order scheme which maintains the energy dissipation law
[
10
]
unconditionally. However, such scheme is not efficient for it needs to solve a coupled nonlinear system with iteration technique at each time step. Recently, the scalar auxiliary variable (SAV) schemes were proposed for the gradient flow
[
24
,
25
]
which can be extended to the incompressible NS equations
[
20
,
18
,
19
]
and the rotating dynamics
[
29
]
. The SAV scheme reformulates system (
1
) by introducing a time-dependent auxiliary variables to transform the energy functional into a quadratic form. The schemes derived from new system allow for an explicit treatment of the convection terms and maintain a modified energy dissipation law. Additionally, the implementation of the SAV scheme is efficient for that it only includes solving two generalized Stokes equations and a nonlinear algebraic equation. Simultaneously, inspired by the application of Lagrange multiplier technique on the gradient flow
[
3
]
, a Lagrange multiplier was introduced into a dynamic equation for the original kinetic energy in
[
31
]
. The proposed schemes are combined with the projection method and satisfy the energy dissipation law with respect to the introduced Lagrange multiplier and some pressure gradient terms. Moreover, by treating the convection terms explicitly, the implementation also consists of two generalized Stokes equations and a nonlinear algebraic equation. In addition to that, Doan et al. modified the systems in
[
6
]
to ensure the uniqueness solution of the Lagrange multiplier and avoid iteration for solving the multiplier. For the incompressible NS equations with penalty terms, Yang et al. proposed an auxiliary variable viscosity splitting(AVVS) idea
[
26
,
30
]
, and constructed energy stable method for the simulation of fluid–solid interactions on a uniformed mesh.

Besides the schemes above, notice that the convection and the stress term satisfy a
so-called ”zero-energy-contribution” feature. To be specific, when deducing the energy dissipation law, after applying the inner products of the momentum equation with velocity, the results of the convection and velocity, together with the results of the gradient of pressure and velocity will completely cancel out. Therefore, by applying this property and combining the projection method, Yang et al. developed fully decoupled linear schemes for the phase field model (the DIEQ scheme in
[
32
]
and the DSAV scheme in
[
35
,
33
,
36
,
34
]
). These schemes maintain a modified energy dissipation law containing the pressure gradient terms, the auxiliary terms and the stabilized terms which allow for calculation with large time steps.

Although most of the energy stable schemes mentioned above are efficient to implement, which require solving several generalized Stokes equations (for the coupled approach) or a sequence of Poisson-type equations (for the decoupled approach) at each time step, it still remains unknown that how to construct a class of schemes for the incompressible NS equations which could maintain all the following properties: (i)An unconditionally original energy dissipation law can be satisfied; (ii)The schemes are linear and can be implemented efficiently; (iii)The schemes can be proved uniquely solvable.

In this paper, we aim to develop a class of linear schemes for the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations to overcome the challenges mentioned above. At first, by introducing a zero stabilization term, we reformulate the original model (
1
) into an equivalent system. First- and second-order schemes based on the Crank-Nicolson method and backward differentiation formula are obtained with the explicit treatment of the nonlinear convection term. It holds that our schemes are linear and unconditionally maintain the original energy dissipation law. The unique solvability and fully discrete energy dissipation law are proved with the spatial discretization on the staggered grid
[
11
,
14
,
17
]
. Additionally, our schemes are easy to implement consisting of three generalized Stokes systems and a
2\times 2
linear system at each time step. We also propose a fast calculation technique of the Stokes systems under periodic boundary conditions. Convergence and energy dissipation properties are tested with a forced flow and the Taylor-Green vortex problem, respectively. Moreover, our schemes can endure high Reynolds number and capture dynamical evolution in various practical examples like the shear layer flow and Lid-driven cavity flow, which are proved with two classical problems. To be specific, the lid-driven cavity flow and Kelvin-Helmholtz instability problem.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows: We reformulate the equations into an equivalent system Section
2
. The first- and second-order temporal discretization with unconditionally energy dissipation law are proposed in Section
3
with a brief description of the implementation. Additionally, section
4
demonstrates the fully discrete schemes with the staggered-grid finite difference method, following the proofs of the discrete energy dissipation law and the unique solvability of our schemes. Details of a fast calculation technique are also specified in section
4
. Moreover, in Section
5
, several numerical tests are carried out to demonstrate the accuracy and stability of our scheme. Finally, we give some conclusions in Section
6
.

2
Robust equivalent model with a zero stabilization term

In this section, we present a robust equivalent model with a zero stabilization term for the incompressible NS equation. It serves as an elegant platform for devising highly efficient linear energy stable schemes while preserving the original energy dissipation law. Throughout this paper, we denote the standard
L^{2}
inner product by
(\cdot,\cdot)
and the corresponding
L^{2}
norm by

\|\cdot\|
.

Noticing that
\nabla\cdot\bm{u}=0
, one can derive the following identity under homogeneous Dirichlet or periodic boundary conditions

(5)

\displaystyle(\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\bm{u},\bm{u})=0.

We introduce a stabilization function
\bm{F}(\bm{u})
that satisfies the following condition

(6)

\big(\bm{F}(\bm{u}),\bm{u}\big)\neq 0,\quad\forall\bm{u}\neq\bm{0}.

Let

(7)

\bm{G}(\bm{u})=\frac{\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\bm{u}}{\big(\bm{F}(\bm{u}),\bm{u}\big)},\quad\forall\bm{u}\neq\bm{0},

and

(8)

\displaystyle\bm{B}(\bm{u},\bm{v})=\big(\bm{F}(\bm{u}),\bm{v}\big)\bm{G}(\bm{u})-\big(\bm{G}(\bm{u}),\bm{v}\big)\bm{F}(\bm{u}).

It is important to highlight that
\bm{B}(\bm{u},\bm{v})
is linear with respect to the second variable
\bm{v}
. Next we introduce an useful lemme.

Lemma 2.1
.

Under homogeneous Dirichlet or periodic boundary conditions, it holds for
\bm{u}\neq\bm{0}
and
\nabla\cdot\bm{u}=0
that

(9)

\bm{B}(\bm{u},\bm{u})=\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\bm{u},

and

(10)

\big(\bm{B}(\bm{u},\bm{v}),\bm{v}\big)=0,\quad\forall\bm{v}.

Proof.

According to Eqs. (
5
) and (
7
), we can obtain

\big(\bm{F}(\bm{u}),\bm{u}\big)\bm{G}(\bm{u})=\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\bm{u},

and

\big(\bm{G}(\bm{u}),\bm{u}\big)=\frac{(\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\bm{u},\bm{u})}{\big(\bm{F}(\bm{u}),\bm{u}\big)}=0,

which can directly lead to

(11)

\bm{B}(\bm{u},\bm{u})=\big(\bm{F}(\bm{u}),\bm{u}\big)\bm{G}(\bm{u})-\big(\bm{G}(\bm{u}),\bm{u}\big)\bm{F}(\bm{u})=\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\bm{u}.

Moreover, taking the
L^{2}
inner product of (
8
) with
\bm{v}
, we arrive at

(12)

\big(\bm{B}(\bm{u},\bm{v}),\bm{v}\big)=\big(\bm{F}(\bm{u}),\bm{v}\big)\cdot\big(\bm{G}(\bm{u}),\bm{v}\big)-\big(\bm{G}(\bm{u}),\bm{v}\big)\cdot\big(\bm{F}(\bm{u}),\bm{v}\big)=0.

∎

By utilizing the relationship (
9
) to replace the convective term, we can rewrite the NS equation (
1
) into the following robust equivalent model

(13a)

\displaystyle\bm{u}_{t}-\nu\Delta\bm{u}+\bm{B}(\bm{u},\bm{u})+\nabla p=\bm{f},

(13b)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\bm{u}=0.

To design highly efficient energy stable schemes based on the robust equivalent model, we first revisit the energy dissipation law.

Theorem 2.1
.

Under homogeneous Dirichlet or periodic boundary conditions, and in the absence of external force
\bm{f}
, the robust equivalent model (
13
) satisfies the energy dissipation law

(14)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E=-\nu\|\nabla\bm{u}\|^{2},

where the energy is given by

E=\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{u}\|^{2}.

Proof.

Under homogeneous Dirichlet or periodic boundary conditions, it is readily to check that

(\Delta\bm{u},\bm{u})=-\|\nabla\bm{u}\|^{2}\leq 0,\quad(\nabla p,\bm{u})=-(p,\nabla\cdot\bm{u})=0,

where
\nabla\cdot\bm{u}=0
was used. According to Lemme
2.1
, it is clear that

\big(\bm{B}(\bm{u},\bm{u}),\bm{u}\big)=0.

For the external force
\bm{f}=\bm{0},
taking the
L^{2}
inner product of (
13a
) with
\bm{u}
, we obtain

(\bm{u}_{t},\bm{u})=-\nu\|\nabla\bm{u}\|^{2}.

Consequently, we have

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E=(\bm{u}_{t},\bm{u})=-\nu\|\nabla\bm{u}\|^{2}.

∎

Remark 2.1
.

We note that although
\big(\bm{G}(\bm{u}),\bm{u}\big)\bm{F}(\bm{u})
vanishes in the continuous case, it is generally non-zero in the discrete setting and plays a crucial role in developing highly efficient linear energy stable schemes. Therefore, we refer to it as the zero stabilization term. Furthermore, there are various options for the stabilization function
\bm{F}(\bm{u})
, but for simplicity, we choose
\bm{F}(\bm{u})=\bm{u}
in this paper.

Remark 2.2
.

For general inhomogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions
\bm{u}|_{\partial\Omega}=\bm{u}_{b}
, it holds for
\nabla\cdot\bm{u}=0
that
[
6
]

(15)

\displaystyle(\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\bm{u},\bm{u})=\frac{1}{2}(\bm{u}_{b}\cdot\bm{n},|\bm{u}_{b}|^{2})_{\partial\Omega},

where
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\partial\Omega}
denotes the
L^{2}
inner product on
\partial\Omega
and
\bm{n}
represents the outward unit normal vector to
\partial\Omega.
In this case, it suffices to modify
\bm{B}(\bm{u},\bm{v})
as

(16)

\bm{B}(\bm{u},\bm{v})=\big(\bm{F}(\bm{u}),\bm{v}\big)\bm{G}(\bm{u})-\frac{(\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\bm{u},\bm{v})-\frac{1}{2}(\bm{u}_{b}\cdot\bm{n},|\bm{u}_{b}|^{2})_{\partial\Omega}}{\big(\bm{F}(\bm{u}),\bm{u}\big)}\bm{F}(\bm{u}),

while the numerical methods proposed in this paper remain valid.

Highly efficient time-discrete schemes

In this section, we present some highly efficient time-discrete schemes that rigorously preserve the original energy dissipation law. Let us next assume a uniform partition of the time interval
[0,T]:~0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}<t_{n+1}<\cdots<t_{N_{t}}=T
with the time step size
\tau=T/N_{t}
and consider the time discretization of the robust equivalent model (

3.1
Crank-Nicolson schemes

Firstly, we develop a first-order linear scheme based on Crank-Nicolson (CN) method, that reads as follows.

Scheme 3.1

(CN1)
.

Given the initial condition
\bm{u}^{0}
, we compute
\bm{u}^{n+1}
for

via

(17a)

\displaystyle\frac{\bm{u}^{n+1}-\bm{u}^{n}}{\tau}-\nu\Delta\bm{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}+\bm{B}(\bm{u}^{n},\bm{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})+\nabla p^{n+\frac{1}{2}}=\bm{f}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\bm{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}=0,

where

To construct the second-order linear CN scheme, we assume

\bm{u}^{n}

Then we develop the following second-order linear CN scheme.

Scheme 3.2

\displaystyle E^{n}=\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{u}^{n}\|^{2}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\frac{E^{n+1}-E^{n}}{\tau}=\left(\frac{\bm{u}^{n+1}-\bm{u}^{n}}{\tau},\bm{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\right)=-\nu\|\nabla\bm{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\|^{2}.

\displaystyle\alpha=\big(\bm{F}(\overline{\bm{u}}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}),\bm{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\big),\quad\beta=\big(\bm{G}(\overline{\bm{u}}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}),\bm{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\big).

\displaystyle\frac{2(\bm{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}-\bm{u}^{n})}{\tau}-\nu\Delta\bm{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}+\alpha\bm{G}(\overline{\bm{u}}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})-\beta\bm{F}(\overline{\bm{u}}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})+\nabla p^{n+\frac{1}{2}}=\bm{f}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},

(23b)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\bm{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}=0,

where
\bm{u}^{n+1}=2\bm{u}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}-\bm{u}^{n}

It is readily to check that the unknown quantities

3.2
Backward differentiation formula schemes

By applying the first-order backward differentiation formula (BDF) method to the robust equivalent model (

Scheme 3.3

(BDF1)
.

Given the initial condition
\bm{u}^{0}
, we compute
\bm{u}^{n+1}
for

(28a)

\displaystyle\frac{\bm{u}^{n+1}-\bm{u}^{n}}{\tau}-\nu\Delta\bm{u}^{n+1}+\bm{B}(\bm{u}^{n},\bm{u}^{n+1})+\nabla p^{n+1}=\bm{f}^{n+1},

.

\bm{f}

\displaystyle\frac{E^{n+1}-E^{n}}{\tau}=-\nu\|\nabla\bm{u}^{n+1}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\tau}\|\bm{u}^{n+1}-\bm{u}^{n}\|^{2},

Therefore, we have

satisfies either the homogeneous Dirichlet or periodic boundary conditions.

Theorem 3.3
.

Under homogeneous Dirichlet or periodic boundary conditions, and in the absence of external force
\bm{f}
, the BDF2 scheme preserves the following discrete energy dissipation law

(32)

\displaystyle\frac{\widehat{E}^{n+1}-\widehat{E}^{n}}{\tau}=-\nu\|\nabla\bm{u}^{n+1}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{4\tau}\|\bm{u}^{n+1}-2\bm{u}^{n}+\bm{u}^{n-1}\|^{2},

where the discrete energy at
t=t_{n}
is defined as

(33)

\displaystyle\widehat{E}^{n}=\frac{1}{4}\Big(\|\bm{u}^{n}\|^{2}+\|2\bm{u}^{n}-\bm{u}^{n-1}\|^{2}\Big).

Proof.

Similar to the proof of Theorem
2.1
, taking the
L^{2}
inner product of (
31a
) with
\bm{u}^{n+1}
, one can arrive at

\displaystyle\left(\frac{3\bm{u}^{n+1}-4\bm{u}^{n}+\bm{u}^{n-1}}{2\tau},\bm{u}^{n+1}\right)=-\nu\|\nabla\bm{u}^{n+1}\|^{2},

where (
10
) was used.
It is readily to check that

\displaystyle\left(3\bm{u}^{n+1}-4\bm{u}^{n}+\bm{u}^{n-1},2\bm{u}^{n+1}\right)=

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}^{n+1}\|^{2}-\|\bm{u}^{n}\|^{2}+\|2\bm{u}^{n+1}-\bm{u}^{n}\|^{2}-\|2\bm{u}^{n}-\bm{u}^{n-1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\bm{u}^{n+1}-2\bm{u}^{n}+\bm{u}^{n-1}\|^{2}.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\widehat{E}^{n+1}-\widehat{E}^{n}}{\tau}=-\nu\|\nabla\bm{u}^{n+1}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{4\tau}\|\bm{u}^{n+1}-2\bm{u}^{n}+\bm{u}^{n-1}\|^{2}.

∎

(34)

\displaystyle\alpha=\big(\bm{F}(\overline{\bm{u}}^{n+1}),\bm{u}^{n+1}\big),\quad\beta=\big(\bm{G}(\overline{\bm{u}}^{n+1}),\bm{u}^{n+1}\big).

4
Fully discrete schemes

In this section, we employ the finite difference approximation on a staggered grid to the time-discrete systems proposed in the previous section, thereby deriving the corresponding fully discrete schemes. For clarity, our discussion centers on the two-dimensional scenario with periodic boundary conditions. However, it is important to mention that the findings of this study are equally relevant to both two- and three-dimensional instances with either homogeneous Dirichlet or periodic boundary conditions. The proposed fully discrete schemes are demonstrated to not only uphold the energy dissipation law at the fully discrete level but also guarantee unique solvability, all while maintaining computational efficiency.

4.1
The spatial discretization

Let
\Omega=[0,L_{x}]\times[0,L_{y}]
denote a two-dimensional rectangular domain. Given positive integers
N_{x}
and
N_{y}
, the mesh sizes are defined as
h_{x}=L_{x}/N_{x}
and
h_{y}=L_{y}/N_{y}
. We further define the following two-dimensional point sets:

\displaystyle\Omega_{ew}=\left\{(x_{i+\frac{1}{2}},y_{j})|i=0,\ldots,N_{x}-1,\;j=1,\ldots,N_{y}\right\},

\displaystyle\Omega_{ns}=\left\{(x_{i},y_{j+\frac{1}{2}})|i=1,\ldots,N_{x},\;j=0,\ldots,N_{y}-1\right\},

\displaystyle\Omega_{c}=\left\{(x_{i},y_{j})|i=1,\ldots,N_{x},\;j=1,\ldots,N_{y}\right\},

where
x_{l}=(l-1/2)h_{x}
,
y_{l}=(l-1/2)h_{y}
with
l
taking either integer or half-integer values. The arrangement of unknown variables within
\Omega_{ew}
,
\Omega_{ns}
and
\Omega_{c}
is depicted on a staggered grid in Figure
1
. In this schematic,
\Omega_{ew}
corresponds to blue squares,
\Omega_{ns}
to red triangles, and
\Omega_{c}
to black circles. Then we define the following discrete function spaces
[
11
,
14
]

\displaystyle V_{ew}=\left\{U|U=\{u(x_{i+\frac{1}{2}},y_{j})|(x_{i+\frac{1}{2}},y_{j})\in\Omega_{ew}\}\right\},

\displaystyle V_{ns}=\left\{V|V=\{v(x_{i},y_{j+\frac{1}{2}})|(x_{i},y_{j+\frac{1}{2}})\in\Omega_{ns}\}\right\},

\displaystyle V_{c}=\left\{P|P=\{p(x_{i},y_{j})|(x_{i},y_{j})\in\Omega_{c}\}\right\}.

For any two matrices
A
and
B
of the same size, we define

\displaystyle(A,B)_{h}=h_{x}h_{y}\mathrm{Tr}(A^{T}B),\quad\|A\|_{h}=\sqrt{(A,A)_{h}},

as the discrete
l^{2}
inner product and norm.

Figure 1:
Staggered grid
(N_{x}=N_{y}=4)
with positions of unknowns
\bm{u}=(u,v)^{T}
and
p
.

Next, we introduce some differentiation matrices as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}(N)=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1&1&&&\\
&1&1&&\\
&&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&&&1&1\\
1&&&&1\\
\end{pmatrix}_{N\times N},\quad\mathbb{D}_{1}(N,h)=\frac{1}{2h}\begin{pmatrix}0&1&&&-1\\
-1&0&1&&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&&-1&0&1\\
1&&&-1&0\\
\end{pmatrix}_{N\times N},

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{2}(N,h)=\frac{1}{h}\begin{pmatrix}-1&1&&&\\
&-1&1&&\\
&&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&&&-1&1\\
1&&&&-1\\
\end{pmatrix}_{N\times N},\quad\mathbb{D}_{3}(N,h)=\frac{1}{h^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}-2&1&&&1\\
1&-2&1&&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&&1&-2&1\\
1&&&1&-2\\
\end{pmatrix}_{N\times N}.

Based on the above definitions, we denote

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}_{1}=\mathbb{C}(N_{x}),\quad\mathbb{A}_{2}=\mathbb{D}_{1}(N_{x},h_{x}),\quad\mathbb{A}_{3}=\mathbb{D}_{2}(N_{x},h_{x}),\quad\mathbb{A}_{4}=\mathbb{D}_{3}(N_{x},h_{x}),

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}_{1}=\mathbb{C}(N_{y}),\quad\mathbb{B}_{2}=\mathbb{D}_{1}(N_{y},h_{y}),\quad\mathbb{B}_{3}=\mathbb{D}_{2}(N_{y},h_{y}),\quad\mathbb{B}_{4}=\mathbb{D}_{3}(N_{y},h_{y}).

Then the discrete divergence operator
\nabla_{d}\cdot(\bullet)
:
[V_{ew};V_{ns}]\rightarrow V_{c}
is define as

\displaystyle\nabla_{d}\cdot\bm{U}=\mathbb{A}_{3}U+V\mathbb{B}_{3}^{T},\quad\forall\bm{U}=[U;V]\in[V_{ew};V_{ns}].

And the discrete gradient operator
\nabla_{D}(\bullet)
:
V_{c}\rightarrow[V_{ew};V_{ns}]
is defined as

\displaystyle\nabla_{D}P=[-\mathbb{A}_{3}^{T}P;-P\mathbb{B}_{3}],\quad\forall P\in V_{c}.

Additionally, the discrete Laplacian operator
\Delta_{h}(\bullet)
:
V_{ew}\cup V_{ns}\cup V_{c}\rightarrow V_{ew}\cup V_{ns}\cup V_{c}
is defined as

\displaystyle\Delta_{h}W=\mathbb{A}_{4}W+W\mathbb{B}_{4}^{T},\quad\forall W\in V_{ew}\cup V_{ns}\cup V_{c}.

For
\bm{U}=[U;V]\in[V_{ew};V_{ns}]
, we define
\bm{F}(\bm{U})=\bm{U}
and
\bm{G}(\bm{U})=[G_{1}(\bm{U});G_{2}(\bm{U})]
with elements

\displaystyle G_{1}(\bm{U})=\frac{U\odot(\mathbb{A}_{2}U)-\big((\mathbb{A}_{1}^{T}V)\odot(U\mathbb{B}_{3})\big)\mathbb{B}_{1}^{T}}{(\bm{F}(\bm{U}),\bm{U})_{h}},\quad G_{2}(\bm{U})=\frac{V\odot(V\mathbb{B}_{2}^{T})-\mathbb{A}_{1}\big((\mathbb{A}_{3}^{T}V)\odot(U\mathbb{B}_{1})\big)}{(\bm{F}(\bm{U}),\bm{U})_{h}},

where the notation
\odot
denotes the Hadamard product of any two matrices. Let

\displaystyle\bm{B}(\bm{U},\bm{V})=\big(\bm{F}(\bm{U}),\bm{V}\big)_{h}\bm{G}(\bm{U})-\big(\bm{G}(\bm{U}),\bm{V}\big)_{h}\bm{F}(\bm{U}).

We note that
\bm{F}(\bm{U})
,
\bm{G}(\bm{U})
and
\bm{B}(\bm{U},\bm{V})
represent the spatial discretization approximations of
\bm{F}(\bm{u})
,
\bm{G}(\bm{u})
and
\bm{B}(\bm{u},\bm{v})
, respectively. Then we have the following lemme.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
\bm{U},~\bm{V}\in[V_{ew};V_{ns}]
and
P\in V_{c},
there exist the following identities

(40)

\big(\bm{B}(\bm{U},\bm{V}),\bm{V}\big)_{h}=0,\quad(\nabla_{D}P,\bm{U})_{h}=-(P,\nabla_{d}\cdot\bm{U})_{h}.

Furthermore, it holds for
\nabla_{d}\cdot\bm{U}=\bm{0}
that

(41)

(\nabla_{D}P,\bm{U})_{h}=0.

Proof.

Through a straightforward calculation, we can deduce

\displaystyle\big(\bm{B}(\bm{U},\bm{V}),\bm{V}\big)_{h}=\big(\bm{F}(\bm{U}),\bm{V}\big)_{h}\cdot\big(\bm{G}(\bm{U}),\bm{V}\big)_{h}-\big(\bm{G}(\bm{U}),\bm{V}\big)_{h}\cdot\big(\bm{F}(\bm{U}),\bm{V}\big)_{h}=0.

For any matrices
A
,
B
,
X
and
Y
, we have

(42)

\displaystyle(AX,Y)_{h}=h_{x}h_{y}\mathrm{Tr}((AX)^{T}Y)=h_{x}h_{y}\mathrm{Tr}(X^{T}A^{T}Y)=(X,A^{T}Y)_{h},

(43)

\displaystyle(XB,Y)_{h}=(B^{T}X^{T},Y^{T})_{h}=(X^{T},BY^{T})_{h}=(X,YB^{T})_{h}.

Using (
42
) and (
43
) yields

\displaystyle(\nabla_{D}P,\bm{U})_{h}=

\displaystyle(-\mathbb{A}_{3}^{T}P,U)_{h}+(-P\mathbb{B}_{3},V)_{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-P,\mathbb{A}_{3}U)_{h}+(-P,V\mathbb{B}_{3}^{T})_{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(P,\nabla_{d}\cdot\bm{U})_{h}.

Therefore, we further arrive at (
41
) for the case where
\nabla_{d}\cdot\bm{U}=\bm{0}
. This completes the proof.
∎

\displaystyle\nabla_{d}\cdot\bm{U}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}=\bm{0},

Theorem 4.1
.

In the absence of the external force, both the fully discrete CN1 scheme (
44
) and the fully discrete CN2 scheme (

\frac{E_{h}^{n+1}-E_{h}^{n}}{\tau}=\nu(\Delta_{h}\bm{U}^{n+\frac{1}{2}},\bm{U}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})_{h},

Similar to the proof of Theorem

Theorem 4.2
.

For
\nu,~\tau>0
, under the solvability condition
(P^{n+\frac{1}{2}},1)_{h}=0
, both fully discrete CN schemes are uniquely solvable.

Proof.

Here, we only prove the existence and uniqueness of the solution for Scheme
4.2
. For Scheme
4.1

(48a)

\displaystyle\frac{2(\bm{U}-\bm{U}^{n})}{\tau}-\nu\Delta_{h}\bm{U}+\bm{B}(\overline{\bm{U}},\bm{U})+\nabla_{D}P=\mathscr{F},

(48b)

\displaystyle\nabla_{d}\cdot\bm{U}=\bm{0},

whose homogeneous linear system reads as

(49a)

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\tau}\bm{U}-\nu\Delta_{h}\bm{U}+\bm{B}(\overline{\bm{U}},\bm{U})+\nabla_{D}P=\bm{0},

(49b)

\displaystyle\nabla_{d}\cdot\bm{U}=\bm{0}.

[
11
]
, we need only to prove that the homogeneous linear system (

\displaystyle\alpha=(\bm{F}(\overline{\bm{U}}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}),\bm{U}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})_{h},\quad\beta=(\bm{G}(\overline{\bm{U}}^{n+\frac{1}{2}}),\bm{U}^{n+\frac{1}{2}})_{h},

0\leq n\leq N_{t}-1
via

(58a)

\displaystyle\frac{\bm{U}^{n+1}-\bm{U}^{n}}{\tau}-\nu\Delta_{h}\bm{U}^{n+1}+\bm{B}(\bm{U}^{n},\bm{U}^{n+1})+\nabla_{D}P^{n+1}=\mathscr{F}^{n+1},

(58b)

\displaystyle\nabla_{d}\cdot\bm{U}^{n+1}=\bm{0},

where
\mathscr{F}^{n+1}
represents the spatial discretization of the external force
\bm{f}^{n+1}
.

Scheme 4.4

(BDF2)
.

Given
\bm{U}^{0}\in[V_{ew};V_{ns}]
, and
\bm{U}^{1}\in[V_{ew};V_{ns}]
is obtained using the fully discrete BDF1 scheme, we compute
\bm{U}^{n+1}\in[V_{ew};V_{ns}]
for
1\leq n\leq N_{t}-1
via

(59a)

\displaystyle\frac{3\bm{U}^{n+1}-4\bm{U}^{n}+\bm{U}^{n-1}}{2\tau}-\nu\Delta_{h}\bm{U}^{n+1}+\bm{B}(\overline{\bm{U}}^{n+1},\bm{U}^{n+1})+\nabla_{D}P^{n+1}=\mathscr{F}^{n+1},

(59b)

\displaystyle\nabla_{d}\cdot\bm{U}^{n+1}=\bm{0},

where
\overline{\bm{U}}^{n+1}=2\bm{U}^{n}-\bm{U}^{n-1}
.

Theorem 4.3
.

In the absence of the external force, the fully discrete BDF1 scheme (
58
) preserves the discrete energy dissipation law

(60)

\frac{E_{h}^{n+1}-E_{h}^{n}}{\tau}=\nu(\Delta_{h}\bm{U}^{n+1},\bm{U}^{n+1})_{h}-\frac{1}{2\tau}\|\bm{U}^{n+1}-\bm{U}^{n}\|^{2}_{h},

where the fully discrete energy of BDF1 scheme at
t=t_{n}
is defined as

(61)

\displaystyle E_{h}^{n}=\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{U}^{n}\|^{2}_{h}.

Additionally, the fully discrete BDF2 scheme (
59
) satisfies the discrete energy dissipation law

(62)

\frac{\widehat{E}_{h}^{n+1}-\widehat{E}_{h}^{n}}{\tau}=\nu(\Delta_{h}\bm{U}^{n+1},\bm{U}^{n+1})_{h}-\frac{1}{4\tau}\|\bm{U}^{n+1}-2\bm{U}^{n}+\bm{U}^{n-1}\|^{2}_{h},

where the fully discrete energy of BDF2 scheme at
t=t_{n}
is defined as

(63)

\displaystyle\widehat{E}_{h}^{n}=\frac{1}{4}\left(\|\bm{U}^{n}\|^{2}_{h}+\|2\bm{U}^{n}-\bm{U}^{n-1}\|^{2}_{h}\right).

Proof.

Here, we present the proof of the discrete energy dissipation law for the fully discrete BDF2 scheme. The proof for the fully discrete BDF1 scheme follows analogously, and thus the details are omitted for brevity.

Taking the discrete inner product of (
59a
) with
\bm{U}^{n+1}
and using Lemma
4.1
, one can arrive at

\displaystyle\left(\frac{3\bm{U}^{n+1}-4\bm{U}^{n}+\bm{U}^{n-1}}{2\tau},\bm{U}^{n+1}\right)_{h}=\nu(\Delta_{h}\bm{U}^{n+1},\bm{U}^{n+1})_{h}.

By applying the following identity

\displaystyle\left(3\bm{U}^{n+1}-4\bm{U}^{n}+\bm{U}^{n-1},2\bm{U}^{n+1}\right)_{h}=

\displaystyle\|\bm{U}^{n+1}\|^{2}_{h}-\|\bm{U}^{n}\|^{2}_{h}+\|2\bm{U}^{n+1}-\bm{U}^{n}\|^{2}_{h}-\|2\bm{U}^{n}-\bm{U}^{n-1}\|^{2}_{h}

\displaystyle+\|\bm{U}^{n+1}-2\bm{U}^{n}+\bm{U}^{n-1}\|^{2}_{h},

we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\widehat{E}_{h}^{n+1}-\widehat{E}_{h}^{n}}{\tau}=\nu(\Delta_{h}\bm{U}^{n+1},\bm{U}^{n+1})_{h}-\frac{1}{4\tau}\|\bm{U}^{n+1}-2\bm{U}^{n}+\bm{U}^{n-1}\|^{2}_{h}\leq 0.

∎

Theorem 4.4
.

For
\nu,~\tau>0
, under the solvability condition
(P^{n+1},1)_{h}=0
, both fully discrete BDF schemes are uniquely solvable.

Proof.

We also only prove the existence and uniqueness of the solution for Scheme
4.4
and omit the similar proof of the version of Scheme
4.3
. For brevity, we denote
\bm{U}:=\bm{U}^{n+1},~\overline{\bm{U}}:=\overline{\bm{U}}^{n+1},
etc. Then the linear system (
59
) can be written as

(64a)

\displaystyle\frac{3\bm{U}-4\bm{U}^{n}+\bm{U}^{n-1}}{2\tau}-\nu\Delta_{h}\bm{U}+\bm{B}(\overline{\bm{U}},\bm{U})+\nabla_{D}P=\mathscr{F},

(64b)

\displaystyle\nabla_{d}\cdot\bm{U}=\bm{0},

whose homogeneous linear system reads as

(65a)

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2\tau}\bm{U}-\nu\Delta_{h}\bm{U}+\bm{B}(\overline{\bm{U}},\bm{U})+\nabla_{D}P=\bm{0},

(65b)

\displaystyle\nabla_{d}\cdot\bm{U}=\bm{0}.

Similar to the proof of Theorem
4.2
, we need only to prove that the homogeneous linear system (
65
) with
(P,1)_{h}=0
admits only the zero solution. Taking the discrete inner product of (
65a
) with
\bm{U}
and using Lemma
4.1
, we have

(66)

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2\tau}\|\bm{U}\|^{2}_{h}-\nu(\Delta_{h}\bm{U},\bm{U})_{h}=0.

Since
\Delta_{h}
is a semi-negative definite operator and
\nu,~\tau>0
, it follows that

\bm{U}=\bm{0}.

Furthermore, we can derive from (
65a
) that

\nabla_{D}P=\bm{0}.

Noticing the solvability condition
(P,1)_{h}=0
, we obtain

P=\bm{0}.

This completes the proof.
∎

In what follows, we present an explicit and effcient implementation of the fully discrete BDF2 scheme. The fully discrete BDF1 scheme can be solved similarly, and the details are omitted for brevity. We denote

\displaystyle\alpha=(\bm{F}(\overline{\bm{U}}^{n+1}),\bm{U}^{n+1})_{h},\quad\beta=(\bm{G}(\overline{\bm{U}}^{n+1}),\bm{U}^{n+1})_{h},

and then have

\displaystyle\bm{B}(\overline{\bm{U}}^{n+1},\bm{U}^{n+1})=\alpha\bm{G}(\overline{\bm{U}}^{n+1})-\beta\bm{F}(\overline{\bm{U}}^{n+1}).

Therefore, the linear system (
59
) can be reformulated as

(67a)

\displaystyle\frac{3\bm{U}^{n+1}-4\bm{U}^{n}+\bm{U}^{n-1}}{2\tau}-\nu\Delta_{h}\bm{U}^{n+1}+\alpha\bm{G}(\overline{\bm{U}}^{n+1})-\beta\bm{F}(\overline{\bm{U}}^{n+1})+\nabla_{D}P^{n+1}=\mathscr{F}^{n+1},

(67b)

\displaystyle\nabla_{d}\cdot\bm{U}^{n+1}=\bm{0}.

Similar to the semi-discrete system, we decompose the solutions as follows

(68a)

\displaystyle\bm{U}^{n+1}=\alpha\bm{U}^{n+1}_{1}+\beta\bm{U}^{n+1}_{2}+\bm{U}^{n+1}_{3},

(68b)

\displaystyle P^{n+1}=\alpha P^{n+1}_{1}+\beta P^{n+1}_{2}+P^{n+1}_{3},

where
\bm{U}^{n+1}_{i}
and
P^{n+1}_{i}\ (i=1,2,3)
satisfy the following three subsystems

(69a)

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2\tau}\bm{U}^{n+1}_{i}-\nu\Delta_{h}\bm{U}^{n+1}_{i}+\nabla_{D}P^{n+1}_{i}=\bm{M}^{n}_{i},

(69b)

\displaystyle\nabla_{d}\cdot\bm{U}^{n+1}_{i}=\bm{0},

with

(70)

\displaystyle\bm{M}^{n}_{1}=-\bm{G}(\overline{\bm{U}}^{n+1}),\quad\bm{M}^{n}_{2}=\bm{F}(\overline{\bm{U}}^{n+1}),\quad\bm{M}^{n}_{3}=\frac{1}{2\tau}\left(4\bm{U}^{n}-\bm{U}^{n-1}\right)+\mathscr{F}^{n+1}.

Applying the discrete divergence operator
\nabla_{d}\cdot(\bullet)
to (
69a
) and utilizing (
69b
), we obtain

(71)

\displaystyle\Delta_{h}P^{n+1}_{i}=\nabla_{d}\cdot\bm{M}^{n}_{i}.

It is noted that the system (
71
) under the condition
(P^{n+1}_{i},1)_{h}=0
is unique solvable and can be efficiently solved using the FFT algorithm. Once
P_{i}^{n+1}
is determined, we can compute
\bm{U}^{n+1}_{i}
via

(72)

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{3}{2\tau}-\nu\Delta_{h}\Big)\bm{U}^{n+1}_{i}=\bm{M}^{n}_{i}-\nabla_{D}P^{n+1}_{i},

which is also uniquely solvable and can be efficiently solved using the FFT algorithm. Once
\bm{U}^{n+1}_{i}~(i=1,2,3)
are obtained, the coefficients
\alpha
and
\beta
can be computed by solving the following
2\times 2
linear system

(73)

\displaystyle Ax=b,

where

\displaystyle A=\begin{pmatrix}1-(\bm{F}(\overline{\bm{U}}^{n+1}),\bm{U}^{n+1}_{1})_{h}&-(\bm{F}(\overline{\bm{U}}^{n+1}),\bm{U}^{n+1}_{2})_{h}\\
-(\bm{G}(\overline{\bm{U}}^{n+1}),\bm{U}^{n+1}_{1})_{h}&1-(\bm{G}(\overline{\bm{U}}^{n+1}),\bm{U}^{n+1}_{2})_{h}\end{pmatrix},\quad x=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha\\
\beta\end{pmatrix},\quad b=\begin{pmatrix}(\bm{F}(\overline{\bm{U}}^{n+1}),\bm{U}^{n+1}_{3})_{h}\\
(\bm{G}(\overline{\bm{U}}^{n+1}),\bm{U}^{n+1}_{3})_{h}\end{pmatrix}.

After solving
\alpha
and
\beta
,
\bm{U}^{n+1}
and
P^{n+1}
can be updated by utilizing (
68a
) and (
68b
), respectively.
Algorithm

is proposed to summarize the implementation of our fully discrete BDF2 scheme.

Remark 4.1
.

It is noteworthy that all the proposed fully discrete schemes can be straightforwardly extended to accommodate Dirichlet boundary conditions. Specifically, under homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions, these schemes still preserve the corresponding energy dissipation law. For more general Dirichlet boundary conditions, they remain uniquely solvable. In such cases, the generalized Stokes systems can be efficiently solved by employing the preconditioned iterative solvers described in Ref.
[
6
]
.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, a class of linear unconditionally energy stable approaches are proposed for solving the incompressible NS equations. By introducing a zero stabilization term, we reformulate the incompressible NS equations into an equivalent system and derive the first- and second-order schemes based on the CN and BDF methods. Unlike the existing linear methods based on the auxiliary variables, it is theoretically proved that our schemes preserve the original energy dissipation law unconditionally. Additionally, the fully discrete schemes are obtained with the staggered-grid finite difference method which maintain the unique solvability. Moreover, our schemes are linear and the implementation contains only three generalized Stokes equations and a
2\times 2
linear system. Notice that our schemes allow for explicit computation with the discrete Fourier algorithm when considering the periodic boundary conditions. In addition to that, various numerical experiments are carried out to demonstrate the convergence, efficiency, and the energy dissipation law of our schemes. Ultimately, we plan to generalize our schemes to other models, such as the NS equations coupled with phase field model.

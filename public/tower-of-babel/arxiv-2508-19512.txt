Abstract
. In recent work with Raum the authors considered congruences for the ordinary partition function p ⁡ ( n ) p(n) of the form p ⁡ ( ℓ ​ Q r ​ n + β ) ≡ 0 ( mod ℓ ) p(\ell Q^{r}n+\beta)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell} where ℓ , Q ≥ 5 \ell,Q\geq 5 are prime and r ∈ { 1 , 2 } r\in\{1,2\} , and proved a number of results which show that such congruences are scarce in a precise sense. Here we improve one of our results when r = 1 r=1 ; in particular we prove (outside of trivial cases) that the set of primes Q Q such that there exists β ∈ ℤ \beta\in\mathbb{Z} with p ⁡ ( ℓ ​ Q ​ n + β ) ≡ 0 ( mod ℓ ) p(\ell Qn+\beta)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell} for all n n has density zero. The proof involves a modification of part of our previous argument and an application of a recent theorem of Dicks regarding modular forms of half-integral weight and level one modulo ℓ \ell .



1.
Introduction

We consider linear congruences for the partition function, where
\ell
is a prime number:

(1.1)

\displaystyle p(mn+\beta)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\quad\text{for all $n$}.

The theory of such congruences has a rich history which goes back to the three famous congruences of Ramanujan:

(1.2)

\displaystyle p(\ell n+\beta)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\quad\text{for }\ell=5,7,11\text{,}\quad\beta=\tfrac{1}{24}\pmod{\ell}.

We briefly describe some of the important results which shape our understanding of such congruences.
Radu
[
Rad12
]
proved a conjecture of Subbarao by showing that there are no congruences of the form (
1.1
) when
\ell=2,3
. In later work
[
Rad13
]
, Radu proved a conjecture of the first author and Ono by showing that for any congruence (
1.1
), we have

\ell\mid m
and
\left(\frac{1-24\beta}{\ell}\right)\in\{0,-1\}
.

Let
\ell\geq 5
be prime. We will consider congruences of the form

(1.3)

\displaystyle p(\ell Q^{r}n+\beta)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell},\quad r=0,1,2,\dots.

When
r=0
, work of Kiming and Olsson
[
KO92
]

and the first author and Boylan
[
AB03
]

shows that the three congruences (
1.2
) are the only examples.
At the other extreme, for any
r\geq 4
the work of Ono
[
Ono00
]
and its generalizations

[
Ahl00
,
AO01
]

show that there are infinitely many primes
Q
which lead to (
1.3
). The same is true generally for weakly holomorphic modular forms by
the work of Treneer
[
Tre06
,
Tre08
]
.

The situation is more interesting when
r=3
; here Atkin
[
Atk68
]
proved a handful of
examples for primes
\ell\leq 31
(Johansson
[
Joh12
]

added
22
billion examples for these primes). Recently the first author with Allen, and Tang
[
AAT22
]
has showed that there are infinitely many such congruences for any prime
\ell\geq 5
(this has been generalized to a wide class of modular forms in
[
AAD24
]
).

It remains to consider congruences (
1.3
) when
r=1,2
. No examples are known in these cases,
and in
work with Raum
[
ABR23
]

we proved a number of results which show that such congruences can exist only in very particular circumstances (if they exist at all). Here we briefly describe only some of those results.
Our basic approach
involves analyzing certain half-integral weight modular forms
f_{\ell,\delta}

(see (
2.3
) below) with

\displaystyle f_{\ell,\delta}\equiv\sum_{\left(\frac{-n}{\ell}\right)=\delta}p\left(\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{n+1}{24}$}}\right)q^{\frac{n}{24}}\pmod{\ell},\quad\delta\in\{0,-1\}.

When
r=2
, we showed
[
ABR23
, Thm. 1.6]

that the congruence
(
1.3
) (outside of trivial cases) implies the restriction
Q\mid\mid 24\beta-1
,
as well as congruence relations involving

f_{\ell,\delta}\mid U_{Q^{2}}
,
f_{\ell,\delta}\mid V_{Q^{2}}
, and
f_{\ell,\delta}\otimes\chi_{Q}
with
\delta=\left(\frac{1-24\beta}{\ell}\right)
. These relations enabled us to computationally rule out instances of (
1.3
) with
r=2
for
17\leq\ell\leq 1,000
and
5\leq Q\leq 10,000
.

When
r=1

[
ABR23
, Thm. 1.4]
shows that either
the set
S
of primes
Q
for which there is a congruence (
1.3
) has density 0,
or the modular form
f_{\ell,\delta}

(with

\delta=\left(\frac{1-24\beta}{\ell}\right)
) has very particular properties. For example,

f_{\ell,\delta}
is annihilated mod
\ell
by all Hecke operators
T_{Q^{2}}
with
Q\equiv-1\pmod{\ell}
.
This allows us to show that
S
has density zero for
17\leq\ell<10,000
.
In
[
ABR23
]
we also show that
if there is a congruence (
1.3
)
with
r=1
at
Q
then the
Fourier expansion of
f_{\ell,\delta}

vanishes modulo
\ell
on one of the square classes modulo
Q
. This opens the door to ruling out
Q
using an efficient sieve-like computation. As a result we show that
there is no such congruence (apart from the trivial examples arising from (
1.2
))
for
\ell<10,000
and
Q<10^{9}
.

Our goal here is to improve

[
ABR23
, Thm. 1.4]

by replacing a technical part of the proof
with an alternate argument
which allows us to rule out the second possibility described above.
This argument
relies on a recent result of Dicks
[
Dic21
]
which
describes precisely the situations in which a level one half-integral weight modular form can have its Fourier expansion supported on finitely many square classes modulo
\ell
.
We prove the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
\ell\geq 5
is prime,
and if
\ell\in\{5,7,11\}
suppose further that
\beta\not\equiv 1/24\pmod{\ell}
.
Then the set of primes
Q
for which there exists a congruence of the form

(1.4)

\displaystyle p(\ell Qn+\beta)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\quad\text{for all $n$}

has density 0.

In Section 2 we give some brief background material and we describe the result of Dicks as well as several fundamental results which limit the existence of a possible congruence (
1.3
) with
r=1
.
In Section 3, we use these results to give a proof of Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Background

2.1.
Modular forms with the eta multiplier

The Dedekind eta function is defined by

\displaystyle\eta(\tau):=q^{\frac{1}{24}}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{n})\text{,}

where we use the notation

\displaystyle q:=e(\tau)=e^{2\pi i\tau},\ \ \ \tau\in\mathbb{H}.

The eta function is a modular form of weight
1/2
on
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
; in particular, there is a multiplier
\nu_{\eta}
with

(2.1)

\displaystyle\eta(\gamma\tau)=\nu_{\eta}(\gamma)(c\tau+d)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\eta(\tau),\qquad\gamma=\left(\begin{matrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{matrix}\right)\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\text{.}

where
\nu_{\eta}(\gamma)
is the 24th root of unity given by the explicit formula in
[
Kno70
, §4.1]
.

If
f
is a function on the upper half-plane,
k\in\mathbb{Z}/2
, and
\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Q})
,
we define

\displaystyle\left(f\big|_{k}\gamma\right)(\tau):=(\det\gamma)^{\frac{k}{2}}(c\tau+d)^{-k}f(\tau).

Throughout the paper,
\ell\geq 5
will denote a fixed prime number.
Given
k\in\mathbb{Z}/2
, a positive integer
N
, and a multiplier system
\nu
on
\Gamma_{0}(N)
, we denote by
S_{k}(N,\nu)
the space of cusp forms of weight
k
and multiplier
\nu
on
\Gamma_{0}(N)

whose Fourier coefficients are algebraic numbers which are integral at all primes above
\ell
.
Forms
f
in this space satisfy the transformation law

\displaystyle f\big|_{k}\gamma=\nu(\gamma)f\ \ \ \text{for}\ \ \ \gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma_{0}(N),

vanish at the cusps of
\Gamma_{0}(N)
,
and have
Fourier expansions of the form

f(\tau)=\sum_{n\equiv r\pmod{24}}a(n)q^{\frac{n}{24}}.

For
Q\geq 1
we have the operators
U_{Q}
and
V_{Q}
whose action on
f=\sum a(n)q^{\frac{n}{24}}
is given by

\displaystyle f\big|U_{Q}:=\sum a(Qn)q^{\frac{n}{24}}\quad\text{and}\quad f\big|V_{Q}:=\sum a(n)q^{\frac{Qn}{24}}

2.2.
Fundamental results on congruences

As mentioned above, our method involves analyzing modular forms
f_{\ell,\delta}
whose Fourier coefficients modulo
\ell
capture values of
p(n)
in
square-classes. To be precise, we showed in (1.8)-(1.9) of
[
ABR23
]
that for
\delta\in\{0,-1\}
, there is a cusp form

(2.2)

\displaystyle f_{\ell,\delta}\in\begin{cases}S_{\frac{\ell^{2}-2\ell}{2}}\left(1,\nu_{\eta}^{-1}\right)\ \ &\text{if $\delta=0$}\text{,}\\
S_{\frac{\ell^{2}-2}{2}}\left(1,\nu_{\eta}^{-1}\right)\ \ &\text{if $\delta=-1$}\text{,}\end{cases}

with

(2.3)

\displaystyle f_{\ell,\delta}=\sum a_{\ell,\delta}(n)q^{\frac{n}{24}}\equiv\sum_{\left(\frac{-n}{\ell}\right)=\delta}p\left(\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{n+1}{24}$}}\right)q^{\frac{n}{24}}\pmod{\ell}.

We recall several important results. The first is a theorem of Radu
which shows that
congruences for
p(n)
propagate across square classes. Given a positive integer
m
and an integer
\beta
, define the set

\displaystyle S_{m,\beta}:=\{\beta^{\prime}\!\!\pmod{m}\ :\ 24\beta^{\prime}-1\equiv a^{2}(24\beta-1)\!\!\pmod{m}\ \ \text{for some $a$ with $(a,6m)=1$}\}.

Then we have

Theorem 2.1
.

[
Rad12
, Thm. 5.4]

Suppose that
m
and
\ell
are positive integers and that for some integer
\beta
we have a congruence
p(mn+\beta)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}
.
Then for all
\beta^{\prime}\in S_{m,\beta}
we have a congruence
p(mn+\beta^{\prime})\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}
.

The next result of Radu shows that congruences can only occur on arithmetic progressions in certain square classes.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
Rad13
, Thm. 1.1]

Suppose that
\ell\geq 5
is prime. Then a congruence (
1.4
) can occur only if

\left(\frac{1-24\beta}{\ell}\right)\in\{0,-1\}
.

Finally, we require one of the main results of
[
ABR23
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
ABR23
, Thm 1.5]

Suppose that
\ell,Q\geq 5
are distinct primes.

(1)

A congruence (
1.4
) cannot occur with
\left(\frac{1-24\beta}{Q}\right)=0
.

(2)

Fix
\delta\in\{0,-1\}
and
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
. If there is a congruence of the form (
1.4
)
with

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1-24\beta}{\ell}\right)=\delta\ \ \text{and}\ \ \left(\frac{1-24\beta}{Q}\right)=\varepsilon\text{,}

then

\displaystyle f_{\ell,\delta}\big|U_{Q}\equiv-\varepsilon\left(\frac{12}{Q}\right)Q^{-1}\,f_{\ell,\delta}\big|V_{Q}\pmod{\ell}.

2.3.
A mod
\ell

version of a theorem of Vignéras

We recall a recent theorem of Dicks which can be
viewed as a mod
\ell
, level one version of a theorem of Vignéras
[
Vig77
]
(the result of Vignéras guarantees that any half integral weight modular form whose Fourier expansion is supported on finitely many square classes must be a linear combination of theta series).
Dicks
[
Dic21
]
gives a precise mod
\ell
version of this result for half integral weight modular forms of level one whose weights are sufficiently small with respect to
\ell
. Here we state the result only in the case when the coefficient ring is
\mathbb{Z}
.

Theorem 2.4
.

[
Dic21
, Thm. 1.3]

Suppose that
\ell\geq 5
is prime
and that
\lambda
is a non-negative integer with
\lambda+\frac{1}{2}<\frac{\ell^{2}}{2}
. Suppose that
r
is a positive integer with
(r,6)=1
and that
f\in S_{\lambda+\frac{1}{2}}(1,\nu_{\eta}^{r})\cap\mathbb{Z}[\![q^{\frac{r}{24}}]\!]
satisfies

f\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a(t_{i}^{2}n^{2})q^{\frac{t_{i}n^{2}}{24}}\not\equiv 0\pmod{\ell},

where each
t_{i}
is a square-free positive integer. Then one of the following is true.

(1)

f\equiv a(1)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{12}{n}\right)n^{\lambda}q^{\frac{n^{2}}{24}}\pmod{\ell}
. In this case
r\equiv 1\pmod{24}
and
\lambda
is even.

(2)

f\equiv a(\ell)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{12}{n}\right)q^{\frac{\ell n^{2}}{24}}\pmod{\ell}.
In this case
r\equiv\ell\pmod{24}
and
\lambda\equiv\frac{\ell-1}{2}\pmod{\ell-~1}
.

(3)

f\equiv a(1)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{12}{n}\right)n^{\lambda}q^{\frac{n^{2}}{24}}+a(\ell)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{12}{n}\right)q^{\frac{\ell n^{2}}{24}}\pmod{\ell}
, where
a(1)\not\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}
and
a(\ell)\not\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}
. In this case,
r\equiv\ell\equiv 1\pmod{24}
and
\lambda\equiv\frac{\ell-1}{2}\pmod{\ell-1}
.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

By the results of
[
KO92
]
and
[
AB03
]
we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle f_{\ell,\delta}\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\,\iff\,\ell\in\{5,7,11\}\quad\text{and}\quad\delta=0.

From Theorem
2.2
and the first part of Theorem
2.3

we see that in order to prove
Theorem
1.1
it will suffice to establish the following:

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose that
\ell\geq 5
is prime and that

(\delta,\varepsilon)
is a pair with
\delta\in\{0,-1\}
and
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
.
Then, outside of the three exceptional cases in (
3.1
), the set of primes
Q
for which there is a congruence (
1.4
) with
\left(\frac{1-24\beta}{\ell}\right)=\delta
and

\left(\frac{1-24\beta}{Q}\right)=\varepsilon

has density zero.

To prove the proposition, fix
such
a choice of

(\delta,\varepsilon)

and let
f_{\ell,\delta}\not\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}
be as in (
2.2
)– (
2.3
). Let
P_{\delta,\varepsilon}
be the set of primes
Q\geq 5
for which there is a congruence (
1.4
) for some
\beta
with

\left(\frac{1-24\beta}{\ell}\right)=\delta
and
\left(\frac{1-24\beta}{Q}\right)=\varepsilon
. Our goal is to show that
P_{\delta,\varepsilon}
has density 0, i.e., that

\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{\#\{Q\in P_{\delta,\varepsilon}:Q<X\}}{X/\log X}.

Let
Q\in P_{\delta,\varepsilon}
. From Theorem
2.1
, we have
p(\ell Qn+\beta^{\prime})\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}
for all
\beta^{\prime}\in S_{\ell Q,\beta}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Recalling the definition of
S_{\ell Q,\beta}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle p(N)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\quad\text{if}\quad\left(\frac{1-24N}{\ell}\right)=\delta\quad\text{and}\quad\left(\frac{1-24N}{Q}\right)=\varepsilon,

or in other words that

\displaystyle p\left(\frac{n+1}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\quad\text{if}\quad\left(\frac{-n}{\ell}\right)=\delta\quad\text{and}\quad\left(\frac{-n}{Q}\right)=\varepsilon.

From this we conclude that

(3.2)

f_{\ell,\delta}\equiv\sum_{\left(\frac{-n}{Q}\right)=-\varepsilon}a_{\ell,\delta}(n)q^{\frac{n}{24}}+\sum a_{\ell,\delta}(Qn)q^{\frac{Qn}{24}}\pmod{\ell}.

On the other hand, from the second part of Theorem
2.3
we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\sum a_{\ell,\delta}(Qn)q^{\frac{n}{24}}\equiv-\varepsilon\left(\frac{12}{Q}\right)Q^{-1}\sum a_{\ell,\delta}(n)q^{\frac{Qn}{24}}\pmod{\ell}.

By (
3.3
) we see that

a_{\ell,\delta}(Qn)\not\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\implies

Q\mid n
,
which leads to

(3.4)

\sum a_{\ell,\delta}(Qn)q^{\frac{Qn}{24}}\equiv\sum a_{\ell,\delta}(Q^{2}n)q^{\frac{Q^{2}n}{24}}\pmod{\ell}.

Finally, combining (
3.2
) and (
3.4
), we deduce that for every
Q\in P_{\delta,\varepsilon}
, we have

f_{\ell,\delta}\equiv\sum_{\left(\frac{-n}{Q}\right)=\varepsilon}a_{\ell,\delta}(n)q^{n/24}+\sum a_{\ell,\delta}(Q^{2}n)q^{Q^{2}n/24}.

In other words,

(3.5)

a_{\ell,\delta}(n)\not\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\ \implies\ \left(\frac{-n}{Q}\right)=\varepsilon\quad\text{ or }\quad Q^{2}\mid n

We now claim that there is no
finite sequence of squarefree integers
t_{i}
such that

(3.6)

f_{\ell,\delta}\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{\ell,\delta}(t_{i}n^{2})q^{\frac{t_{i}n^{2}}{24}}\pmod{\ell}.

To prove this we recall that the weight
\lambda+1/2
of
f_{\ell,\delta}
is given in (
2.2
) and is less than
\ell^{2}/2
, so we may apply Theorem
2.4
to
f_{\ell,\delta}
. By (
2.2
) we have
r=-1
, so cases (1) and (3) of Theorem
2.4
are not possible.
We also find that case (2) is not possible, since

\lambda=\begin{cases}\frac{\ell^{2}-2\ell-1}{2}\quad&\text{if}\quad\delta=0,\\
\frac{\ell^{2}-3}{2}\quad&\text{if}\quad\delta=-1,\end{cases}

and in both cases we have

\lambda\equiv-1\pmod{\ell-1}
.
This establishes the claim.

It follows that there is an infinite sequence of positive square free numbers
t_{1}<t_{2}<\cdots
such that for each
i
there exists some
n_{i}
with
a_{\ell,\delta}(t_{i}n_{i}^{2})\not\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}
.
Suppose that
Q\in P_{\delta,\varepsilon}
and that
Q\mid n_{i}
.
From the second part of Theorem
2.3
we have

\displaystyle f_{\ell,\delta}\big|U_{Q^{2}}\equiv-\varepsilon\left(\frac{12}{Q}\right)Q^{-1}\,f_{\ell,\delta}\pmod{\ell}.

So we can conclude that
a_{\ell,\delta}\left(t_{i}n_{i}^{2}/Q^{2}\right)\not\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}
. Removing additional factors of
Q^{2}
if necessary we can assume that for all
Q\in P_{\delta,\varepsilon}
and all
i
we have
Q\nmid n_{i}
.
This gives an infinite sequence
t_{1}<t_{2}<\cdots
of positive squarefree integers such that

(3.7)

\left(\frac{-t_{i}}{Q}\right)=\varepsilon\quad\text{for all}\quad Q\in P_{\delta,\varepsilon}.

We may then choose a subsequence
t_{i_{1}}<t_{i_{2}}<\cdots
with the property that
for every
k\geq 1
,
t_{i_{k}}
is divisible by a prime
p_{k}
which does not divide any of
t_{i_{1}},\dots,t_{i_{k-1}}
.

Fix
k\geq 1
and consider the
sum

(3.8)

S_{k}(X):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\ \text{prime}\\
Q\leq X\end{subarray}}\left(1+\varepsilon\left(\frac{-t_{i_{1}}}{Q}\right)\right)\cdots\left(1+\varepsilon\left(\frac{-t_{i_{k}}}{Q}\right)\right).

From (
3.7
) we see that

(3.9)

S_{k}(X)\geq 2^{k}\cdot\#\left\{Q\in P_{\delta,\varepsilon}\ :\ Q\leq X\right\}.

If
t
is not a square then
\left(\frac{t}{\bullet}\right)
corresponds to a non-trivial Dirichlet character and it follows from Dirichlet’s theorem on primes in progression that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\ \text{prime}\\
Q\leq X\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{t}{Q}\right)=o\left(\frac{X}{\log X}\right).

Expanding out the products in (
3.8
) and using this fact along with our assumption on the
t_{i_{k}}
gives

S_{k}(X)\sim\frac{X}{\log X}.

Combining this with (
3.9
)
we obtain

\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{\#\left\{Q\in P_{\delta,\varepsilon}\ :\ Q\leq X\right\}}{X/\log X}\leq 2^{-k}.

Since
k
is arbitrary this shows that
P_{\delta,\varepsilon}
has density zero. This establishes Proposition
3.1
and with it Theorem
1.1
.

On the gravity-capillary water waves with point vortices
Lizhe Wan

Abstract
. We consider the two-dimensional deep gravity-capillary water waves with point vortices. We first formulate the question in the holomorphic coordinates. Then, we derive an a priori energy estimate for water waves, and show that the water wave system has a unique solution for initial data in ℋ s × Ω t N {\mathcal{H}}^{s}\times\Omega_{t}^{N} , s > 3 2 s>\frac{3}{2} . Finally, we show that if there are only two vortices, and vortices, initial velocity, and initial surfaces are symmetric with respect to the vertical axis, then the solution of pure capillary water waves with two point vortices has an extended cubic lifespan.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the Cauchy problem of two-dimensional deep gravity-capillary water waves with point vortices.
This system solves a special kind of free boundary incompressible Euler equations with appropriate boundary conditions, and the fluid vorticity is irrotational everywhere except for finitely many point vortices.

Suppose that an incompressible, homogeneous, and inviscid fluid occupies a time-dependent unbounded domain
\Omega_{t}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
at each time
t\geq 0
.
We assume that the domain
\Omega_{t}
has infinite depth, and its upper free boundary
\Gamma_{t}
is asymptotically flat at infinity.
Let the fluid velocity at time
t\geq 0
be
\textbf{v}(t,\cdot):\Omega_{t}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
The vorticity of the fluid is defined to be

\omega:=\nabla^{\perp}\cdot\textbf{v},\quad\nabla^{\perp}:=(-\partial_{y},\partial_{x}).

While most of the results in water waves assume zero or constant vorticity, we will consider in this paper that the vorticity consists of a finite sum of point vortices.
A
point vortex
for vorticity is the case where
\omega(t)=\lambda\delta_{z(t)}.

In other words, the vorticity is a Dirac measure supported at
z(t)\in\Omega_{t}
with
vortex strength

\lambda
.
For two-dimensional fluid, the vorticity
\omega
satisfies the transport equation
(\partial_{t}+\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla)\omega=0.

The transport term
\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla\omega
is not well-defined at the vortex center
z(t)
.
To make the transport equation well-defined, we require
\mathbf{v}
to be a weak solution to the Euler equations away from
z(t)
.
Assuming there are
N
point vortices with vortex strength
\lambda_{j}
at vortex center
z_{j}(t)
for
1\leq j\leq N
, then inside the domain
\Omega_{t}
, the fluid satisfies the incompressible Euler equations

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathbf{v}_{t}+\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{v}=-\nabla p-g\textbf{e}_{y}&&\text{in }\Omega_{t}\backslash\cup_{j=1}^{N}\{z_{j}(t)\},\\
&\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}=0&&\text{in }\Omega_{t},\\
&\omega=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_{j}\delta_{z_{j}(t)}&&\text{in }\Omega_{t},\end{aligned}\right.

for gravitational constant
g\geq 0
, and pressure
p(t,\cdot):\Omega_{t}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.
Since this is a free boundary problem, on the upper surface
\Gamma_{t}
, we have the kinematic boundary condition

\partial_{t}+\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla\text{ is tangent to }\bigcup\Gamma_{t},

as well as the dynamic boundary condition

p=-\sigma{\bf H}\ \ \text{ on }\Gamma_{t}.

Here,
{\bf H}
is the mean curvature of the boundary, and
\sigma>0
is the coefficient of the surface tension.
The position of each point vortex centered at
z_{j}(t)
is governed by the
Helmholtz–Kirchhoff
model

(1.2)

\frac{d}{dt}z_{j}(t)=\textbf{v}(t,z_{j}(t))=\textbf{u}(t,z_{j}(t))-\sum_{k\neq j}\frac{\lambda_{k}}{2\pi}\nabla^{\perp}\log{|{z_{j}(t)-z_{k}(t)}|},

where
\mathbf{u}
is the irrotational part of the velocity field such that
\nabla\times\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{0}
, and
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0
.
The second term on the right-hand side of (
1.2
) vanishes in the case of a single vortex.
A rigorous derivation of the Helmholtz–Kirchhoff model can be found in Marchioro and Pulvirenti
[
35
]
, and Chapter
4
of the textbook
[
36
]
.

For free boundary incompressible Euler equations with nonzero vorticity, see the work of Beyer-Gunther
[
14
]
, Ogawa-Tani
[
39
]
, Lindblad
[
34
]
, Coutand-Shkoller
[
18
]
, Shatah-Zeng
[
42
,
41
]
, Zhang-Zhang
[
54
]
, Bieri-Miao-Shahshahani-Wu
[
15
]
, Ifrim-Tataru
[
31
]
, Christodoulou-Lindblad
[
17
]
, Wang-Zhang-Zhao-Zheng
[
51
]
, Ifrim-Pineau-Tataru-Taylor
[
27
]
, and the references therein.

However, the results mentioned in the previous paragraph do not apply in the setting of point vortices.
Here, we first recall some results in fluid dynamics with point vortices in the fixed domain.
Gallay in
[
22
]
considered Navier–Stokes equations with increasingly concentrated vorticity.
He showed that the vanishing viscosity limit for smooth solutions is just Euler equations with point vortices.
Glass, Munnier, and Sueur in
[
23
]
showed that the motion of a point vortex inside a domain can be obtained as the limit of the motion of a rigid body immersed in the fluid when the body shrinks to a massless point particle with fixed circulation.

As for the water waves with point vortices, there are many results on the existence and stability of solitary waves, see Ter-Krikorov
[
45
]
, Filippov
[
20
,
21
]
, and Chen-Varholm-Walsh-Wheeler
[

for the gravity water waves, and Shatah-Walsh-Zeng
[
40
]
, Varholm
[
46
]
, and Varholm-Wahlén-Walsh
[
47
]
for the gravity-capillary water waves.
To the author’s knowledge, the only well-posedness result for the Cauchy problem is the work of Su
[
43
]
on the gravity water waves with point vortices.
Su also considered in
[
44
]
the transition of the Rayleigh-Taylor instability for gravity water waves with a pair of point vortices.
No well-posedness result for gravity-capillary water waves with point vortices was known before.

1.1.
Water waves in holomorphic coordinates

Instead of solving the Euler equations (
1.1
) in a moving domain, one can reformulate the problem and work in a fixed domain instead.
In Section
5
of
[
47
]
, Varholm, Wahlén, and Walsh rewrote the water waves with a single point vortex.
This formulation was previously used to study irrotational water waves by, for instance, Alazard, Burq, and Zuily
[
11
,
7
,
8
,
10
,
9
]
.
In
[
43
,
44
]
, Su reexpressed the gravity water waves with point vortices using a mix of Lagrangian and conformal formulation as in the framework of Wu
[
52
,
53
]
for irrotational gravity water waves.

While both two formulations are feasible for studying the Cauchy problem of gravity-capillary water waves with point vortices,
we will follow the formulation in Hunter-Ifrim-Tataru
[
25
]
, and work in the
holomorphic coordinates
.
We remark that the use of holomorphic coordinates has been successfully applied in many water wave models by Ifrim, Tataru, and their collaborators, see
[
25
,
29
,
24
,
5
,
28
,
4
,
30
,
31
]
.

In this paper, we will slightly abuse notation and write holomorphic functions either as functions on the lower half of the complex plane or as their restriction to the real line.
Let
{\mathbf{W}}(t,\alpha)
be the differentiated holomorphic position of the free surface,
R(t,\alpha)
be the irrotational part of the complex conjugate of the complex velocity on the surface, and
z_{j}(t):=x_{j}(t)+iy_{j}(t)\in\Omega_{t}
be the complex position of the
j
-th point vortex.
The water wave system can be reexpressed in terms of unknowns
({\mathbf{W}},R,\{z_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N})
.
Following the idea of Appendix
A
of Hunter-Ifrim-Tataru
[
25
]
and Ifrim-Tataru
[
30
]
, the gravity-capillary water waves with point vortices are given by the system

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &(\partial_{t}+b\partial_{\alpha}){\mathbf{W}}+\frac{(1+{\mathbf{W}})R_{\alpha}}{1+\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}}=(1+{\mathbf{W}})M-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\lambda_{j}i}{2\pi}\frac{1+\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}}{(\overline{Z-z_{j}})^{2}}\\
&(\partial_{t}+b\partial_{\alpha})R-(\partial_{t}+b\partial_{\alpha})\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\lambda_{j}i}{2\pi}\frac{1}{Z-z_{j}}-i\frac{g{\mathbf{W}}-a}{1+{\mathbf{W}}}=\frac{2\sigma}{1+{\mathbf{W}}}\mathbf{P}\Im\left[\frac{{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{J^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+{\mathbf{W}})}\right]_{\alpha}\\
&\frac{d}{dt}z_{j}(t)=\mathcal{U}(t,z_{j}(t))+\sum_{k\neq j}\frac{\lambda_{k}i}{2\pi}\frac{1}{\overline{z_{j}(t)-z_{k}(t)}},\quad\forall 1\leq j\leq N,\end{aligned}\right.

where
J:=|1+{\mathbf{W}}|^{2}
is the Jacobian,
\mathbf{P}=\frac{1}{2}(I-iH)
is the holomorphic projection,
Z_{\alpha}=1+{\mathbf{W}}
,
\mathcal{U}(t,z)
is the irrotational part of velocity at the point
z
, which is given by

(1.4)

\mathcal{U}(t,z):=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{(1+{\mathbf{W}})\cdot\bar{R}(t,\alpha)}{Z(t,\alpha)-z}\,d\alpha,

and auxiliary functions
a
,
b
and
M
are defined in (
2.41
), (
2.34
) and (
2.37
) respectively.
The detailed computations and derivations of the water wave system (
1.3
) will be carried out in Section
2.3
.

When there are no point vortices, the irrotational gravity-capillary water waves are locally well-posed for
({\mathbf{W}}_{0},R_{0})\in H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for
s>1
, see
[
48
]
.
In this paper, we will also use the functional space

{\mathcal{H}}^{s}:=H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R}),

and consider
({\mathbf{W}}(t),R(t),\{z_{j}(t)\}_{j=1}^{N})\in{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\times\Omega_{t}^{N}
.

1.2.
Main results

The first main result of this paper is the energy estimate for water waves (
1.3
).
Before stating the energy estimate, we first introduce the control norms.
Let
\epsilon>0
be an arbitrarily small positive constant, and fix time
t
, define

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}_{0}}:=\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C^{\epsilon}_{*}}+\|R\|_{C^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}_{*}}+\|Z-\alpha\|_{L^{\infty}}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\lambda_{j}|+\max_{j\neq k}\frac{|\lambda_{j}|}{|z_{j}-z_{k}|},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}_{1}}:=\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C^{1+\epsilon}_{*}}+\|R\|_{C_{*}^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}}+\|Z-\alpha\|_{C^{1}_{*}}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\lambda_{j}|+\max_{j\neq k}\frac{|\lambda_{j}|}{|z_{j}-z_{k}|},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}_{\frac{3}{2}}}:=\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C^{\frac{3}{2}+\epsilon}_{*}}+\|R\|_{C^{1+\epsilon}_{*}}+\|Z-\alpha\|_{C^{\frac{3}{2}}_{*}}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\lambda_{j}|+\max_{j\neq k}\frac{|\lambda_{j}|}{|z_{j}-z_{k}|}.

Here,
C^{s}_{*}
is the Zygmund space that will be defined in Section
2.1
.
Each control norm in
{\mathcal{A}_{1}}
has
1
more derivative than the corresponding term in
{\mathcal{A}_{0}}
, and each control norm in
{\mathcal{A}_{\frac{3}{2}}}
has
\frac{3}{2}
more derivatives than the corresponding term in
{\mathcal{A}_{0}}
.
In addition, if the term

\lim_{t\nearrow T^{*}}\max_{j\neq k}\frac{|\lambda_{j}|}{|z_{j}(t)-z_{k}(t)|}=+\infty,

then at least two point vortices collide at time
T^{*}
.
The model (
1.1
) is no longer true and we will not consider this situation.

The first main result in this paper is the a priori energy estimate for the system (
1.3
).

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
s>0
, suppose
({\mathbf{W}},R,\{z_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N})\in C([0,T];{\mathcal{H}}^{s})\times C^{1}([0,T];\Omega_{t})^{N}
solve the water waves (
1.3
).
Then, there exists an energy functional
E_{s}
which has the following properties:

(1)

Norm equivalence:

(1.5)

E_{s}\approx_{\mathcal{A}_{0}}\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}+\|Z-\alpha\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\lambda_{j}|^{2}.

(2)

Energy estimate:

(1.6)

\frac{d}{dt}E_{s}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}{\mathcal{A}_{\frac{3}{2}}}E_{s}.

As a consequence of the a priori energy estimate, one can prove the local well-posedness result of water waves (
1.3
).

Theorem 1.2
.

For
s>\frac{3}{2}
, if the initial datum
({\mathbf{W}}_{0},R_{0})\in{\mathcal{H}}^{s}
, and disjoint point vortices
z_{j}(0)\in\Omega_{0}
,
\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{R}

for
1\leq j\leq N
, then the system (
1.3
) has a unique solution
({\mathbf{W}},R)\times\{z_{j}(t)\}_{j=1}^{N}\in C([0,T];{\mathcal{H}}^{s})\times C^{1}([0,T];\Omega_{t})^{N}
for some time
0<T<T^{*}
.
Moreover,
T^{*}
is the maximal lifespan such that either
T^{*}=+\infty
or the solution blows up at
T^{*}
:

\lim_{\tau\nearrow T^{*}}\|{\mathcal{A}_{1}}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}[0,\tau)}+\|{\mathcal{A}_{\frac{3}{2}}}(t)\|_{L^{1}_{t}[0,\tau)}=+\infty.

Remark 1.1
.

(1)

For the blow-up criterion of gravity water waves with point vortices in
[
43
]
, the author uses Sobolev norms to characterize the blow-up of solutions.
Here, in Theorem
1.1
, we prove the tame energy estimate that involves Zygmund control norms
{\mathcal{A}_{0}},{\mathcal{A}_{1}},{\mathcal{A}_{\frac{3}{2}}}
.
This is why the blow-up criterion in Theorem
1.2
does not involve the Sobolev control norms.

(2)

An interesting question one may ask is whether it is possible to further show that
z_{j}(t)\in C^{2}([0,T];\Omega_{t})
for each
j
as in the corresponding result in
[
43
]
?
In order to estimate
|\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}z_{j}|
, one will have to estimate
\|R_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}},
which is bounded when
s>2
(or
s>\frac{7}{4}
if further using the Strichartz estimate).
It may not be bounded in the low regularity setting
\frac{3}{2}<s\leq\frac{7}{4}
.
Hence, we only prove that
z_{j}(t)\in C^{1}([0,T];\Omega_{t})
.

(3)

For reference, the local well-posedness of two dimensional irrotational gravity-capillary water waves was established in, for instance, Alazard-Burq-Zuily
[
7
]
, Ifrim-Tataru
[
30
]
, de Poyferré-Nguyen
[
19
]
, Nguyen
[
38
]
, and Ai
[

]
.
The local well-posedness of two-dimensional irrotational gravity water waves was established in Wu
[
52
]
, Lannes
[
33
]
, Alazard-Burq-Zuily
[
8
,

,
11
]
, Hunter-Ifrim-Tataru
[

]
, Ai
[

,

]
, and Ai-Ifrim-Tataru
[

]
for the low regularity well-posedness.

The dynamics of point vortices when
N\geq 3
can be highly complex.
Su in Section 3.3 in
[
43
]
has a brief discussion on some possible situations for the dynamics of two vortices
N=2
.

When there are no point vortices and no gravity, the first global well-posedness result belongs to Ifrim-Tataru
[
30
]
.
In their work, Ifrim-Tataru proved a low regularity global well-posednss result, but also showed that the solutions do enjoy a cubic lifespan, as well as, scale-invariant energy estimates which both findings allowed them to work close to the regularity threshold.
This is the first result of this type for capillary water waves in 2D.
This result was followed, later on, by a global well-posedness result of Ionescu-Pusateri
[
32
]
, however obtained in a much higher regularity setting.

For water waves with point vortices (
1.3
), since the terms with point vortices are not quadratic, in general, one can only expect quadratic lifespans for solutions.
However, our next main result shows that when
N=2
, and these two point vortices are symmetric with respect to the vertical axis, small initial data leads to the extended cubic lifespan.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
s>\frac{3}{2}
, and the gravity is zero
g=0
, we assume that the water wave system (
1.3
) satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

2 point vortices are symmetric, and are not close to the surface: The number of vortices
N=2
, with position
z_{1}(t)=-x(t)+iy(t)
,
z_{2}(t)=x(t)+iy(t)
and strength
\lambda
,
-\lambda
, where
x(t)>0
,
y(t)<0
, and
\lambda
satisfies either
\lambda<0
,
|y(0)|\gtrsim 1
or
\lambda>0
,
|y(0)|\gg\epsilon^{-2}
.

(2)

The free upper surface and the complex velocity field on the surface are symmetric: Following the definition of variables in Section
2.3
,
(\Re{\mathbf{W}}_{0},\Im R_{0},\Im Z_{0})
are even functions and
(\Im{\mathbf{W}}_{0},\Re R_{0},\Re Z_{0}-\alpha)
are odd functions.

(3)

The initial datum of (
1.3
) and vortex strength are small such that

\|({\mathbf{W}}_{0},R_{0},Z_{0}-\alpha)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\times L^{2}}\leq\epsilon,\quad|\lambda|^{2}+|\lambda x(0)|\leq\epsilon

for some small constant
0<\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}\ll 1
.

(4)

The initial rotational part of the velocity dominates the irrotational part of the velocity:
\frac{|\lambda|}{x(0)}\geq M
, for some constant
M\gg 1
.

Then the solution of (
1.3
) has an extended cubic lifespan
T\geq\delta\epsilon^{-2}
for some constant
\delta>0
that depends on
\epsilon_{0}
.
Moreover, the energy of the solution is bounded:

(1.7)

\|({\mathbf{W}}(t),R(t),Z(t)-\alpha)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\times L^{2}}\lesssim\epsilon,\quad\forall t\in[0,\delta\epsilon^{-2}].

A detailed energy inequality will be given in Theorem
5.1
.

Remark 1.2
.

These conditions ensure that two point vortices either move downward or move upward but their positions are far from the surface.
In either case, the terms caused by point vortices are under control.

For the proof of Theorem
1.3
, the strategy is to show that point vortices have bounded vertical velocities, and their horizontal movements are also controlled, so that the rotational terms are small.
For the irrotational part of the system, we use the normal form analysis in the spirit of
[
30
]
.
As a consequence, we will prove the modified energy estimate Theorem
5.1
, which will then lead to the cubic lifespan of the solution.

1.3.
The organization of the paper

Section
2
is devoted to basic estimates and derivation of the water wave system (
1.3
).
In Section
2.1
, we define the function spaces we will use in this paper.
In Section
2.2
, we define paradifferential operators and list paradifferential estimates that will be used in the analysis.
We then follow the derivation in
[

]
to derive the system of equations governing the evolution of water waves in holomorphic coordinates in Section

.

Next, in Section

, we derive some water wave estimates including estimates of

, the rotational part of the complex velocity, and the time derivatives of point vortices.
We also compute the para-material derivatives
T_{D_{t}}:=\partial_{t}+T_{b}\partial_{\alpha}
of
{\mathbf{W}},R
and
J^{s}
.

In the next section, we consider the local well-posedness of water waves.
In Section
4.1
, we derive the modified energy estimate Theorem
1.1
by constructing appropriate cubic energy corrections.
Later, in Section
4.2
, we prove Theorem
1.2
by building a sequence of approximate solutions to show that this sequence converges to the solution of water waves (

).

Finally, in Section

We make the bootstrap assumption on the Sobolev norms of

.

(

2.
Preliminaries and derivation of water waves

2.1.
Norms and function spaces

In this section, we list the definitions of norms and function spaces we will need for the analysis.

In two space dimensions, we identify
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
\mathbb{C}
, so that a point
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
is equivalent to a complex number
x+iy\in\mathbb{C}
.
In the following, we will write
\mathbb{R}^{2}
vectors as complex numbers to simplify the computation.

Let
H
be the Hilbert transform on the real line:

Hf(x):=p_{.}v_{.}\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{f(y)}{x-y}\,dy.

The Hilbert transform can also be represented as a Fourier multiplier

\widehat{Hf}(\xi)=-i\mbox{sgn}(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi).

As a consequence,
H^{2}=-I
.
An important property of the Hilbert transform is the convolution identity

(2.1)

H\left[uH[v]+vH[u]\right]=H[u]H[v]-uv,\quad\forall u,v\in L^{2},

We define
\mathbf{P}
as the projection onto negative frequencies

\widehat{{\mathbf{P}}f}(\xi):=\mathbf{1}_{(-\infty,0)}\hat{f}(\xi)

The operator
{\mathbf{P}}
can also be expressed using the Hilbert transform

\mathbf{P}=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{I}-iH).

A complex-valued function
f
on the real line that can be extended to be holomorphic in the lower half-plane satisfies

{\mathbf{P}}f=f,

which means that its real and imaginary parts satisfy the relations

(2.2)

\Re f=H\Im f,\quad\Im f=-H\Re f.

We will see later in the derivation of water waves, unknowns
{\mathbf{W}}
,
R
, and
Z-\alpha
are all holomorphic functions, and therefore satisfy the above properties.
On the other hand, a complex-valued function
f
on the real line that can be extended to be holomorphic in the upper half-plane satisfies
\bar{{\mathbf{P}}}f=f
.

Consider the Littlewood-Paley frequency decomposition,

I=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}P_{k},

where for each
k\geq 1
, the symbols of the operator
P_{k}
are smooth and localized at
2^{k}
, and
P_{0}
selects the low frequency components
|\xi|\leq 1
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
, and
p,q\in[1,\infty]
, the non-homogeneous Besov space
B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})
is defined as the space of all tempered distributions
u
such that

\|u\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}}:=\left\|(2^{ks}\|P_{k}u\|_{L^{p}})_{k=0}^{\infty}\right\|_{l^{q}}<+\infty.

When
p=q=\infty
, the Besov space
B^{s}_{\infty,\infty}
becomes the
Zygmund space

C^{s}_{*}
.
When
p=q=2
, the Besov space
B^{s}_{2,2}
becomes the
Sobolev space

H^{s}
.

Let
1\leq p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq\infty
,
1\leq r_{1}\leq r_{2}\leq\infty
, then for any real number
s
,

B^{s}_{p_{1},r_{1}}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow B^{s-(\frac{1}{p_{1}}-\frac{1}{p_{2}})}_{p_{2},r_{2}}(\mathbb{R}).

As a special case when
p_{1}=r_{1}=2
and
p_{2}=r_{2}=\infty
,

H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow C^{s}_{*}(\mathbb{R})\quad\forall s.

The Sobolev space
H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})
can be embedded into the Zygmund space
C^{s}_{*}(\mathbb{R})
.

The Zygmund space
C^{s}_{*}(\mathbb{R})
is just the usual Hölder space
W^{s,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
when
s\in(0,\infty)\backslash\mathbb{N}
.
One has the embedding properties

\displaystyle C_{*}^{s}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}),\quad s>0;\qquad L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow C_{*}^{s},\quad s<0;

\displaystyle C_{*}^{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow C_{*}^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}),\quad H^{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow H^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}),\qquad s_{1}>s_{2}.

2.2.
Paradifferential estimates

In this section, we recall some paradifferential estimates that will be used in the rest part of the paper.

Definition 2.1
.

(1)

Let
\rho\in[0,\infty)
,

2.3.
Derivation of water waves in holomorphic coordinates

In this section, we derive the water wave system with point vortices in holomorphic coordinates following closely the work of Hunter-Ifrim-Tataru

This is essentially a system of nonlinear dispersive partial differential equations coupled with
N

When there is no point vortex, the system is reduced to the irrotational water waves studied in

]
.

We first use complex analysis to transform the lower half-plane
\mathbb{H}:=\{\alpha+i\beta|\beta\leq 0\}
to the fluid domain
\Omega_{t}
.
At each time
t
, by the Riemann mapping theorem, there exists a unique bijective conformal map
h(t,\cdot):\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\Omega_{t}
that maps the lower half-plane
\mathbb{H}
to the fluid domain
\Omega_{t}

The conformal map

satisfies

h(t,\alpha,\beta)-(\alpha+i\beta)\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{as }\alpha+i\beta\rightarrow\infty.

The real and imaginary parts of the conformal map
x(t,\alpha,\beta)
and
y(t,\alpha,\beta)
satisfy the Cauchy-Riemann equations

(2.26)

x_{\alpha}=y_{\beta},\quad x_{\beta}=-y_{\alpha}.

For the complex fluid velocity
v=v_{1}+iv_{2}
in
\Omega_{t}
, it can be decomposed into the irrotational part
v^{ir}
and the rotational part
v^{rot}:=\sum_{j=1}^{N}v^{rot}_{j}
caused by point vortices:

v=v^{ir}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}v^{rot}_{j},\quad v^{rot}_{j}(t,z):=\frac{\lambda_{j}i}{2\pi}\frac{1}{\overline{z-z_{j}(t)}},\quad\text{for }z\neq z_{j}(t).

Then there exists a velocity potential
\phi
of the fluid such that it can be further decomposed into the rotational and irrotational parts:

\phi=\phi^{ir}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\phi_{j}^{rot},\quad(\partial_{x}+i\partial_{y})\phi^{ir}=v^{ir},\quad(\partial_{x}+i\partial_{y})\phi^{rot}_{j}=v^{rot}_{j}.

Let
\psi=\phi\circ h:\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the conformal velocity potential

\psi(t,\alpha,\beta)=\phi(t,x(t,\alpha,\beta),y(t,\alpha,\beta)).

Again, we can decompose the fluid potential
\psi
into the irrotational and rotational parts
\psi=\psi^{ir}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\psi_{j}^{rot}.

Using the chain rule,

\psi_{\alpha}=\phi_{x}x_{\alpha}+\phi_{y}y_{\alpha},\quad\psi_{\beta}=\phi_{x}x_{\beta}+\phi_{y}y_{\beta},

and the Cauchy-Riemann relations (
2.26
), the real and imaginary parts of the complex fluid velocity
v=v_{1}+iv_{2}=\phi_{x}+i\phi_{y}
are given by

(2.27)

v_{1}=\frac{1}{j}(x_{\alpha}\psi^{ir}_{\alpha}+x_{\beta}\psi^{ir}_{\beta})+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Re\{v^{rot}_{j}\circ h\},\quad v_{2}=\frac{1}{j}(y_{\alpha}\psi^{ir}_{\alpha}+y_{\beta}\psi^{ir}_{\beta})+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Im\{v^{rot}_{j}\circ h\},

for the Jacobian
j=x^{2}_{\alpha}+y^{2}_{\alpha}.

We parametrize the free surface using the conformal variables
X(t,\alpha):=x(t,\alpha,0)
, and
Y(t,\alpha):=y(t,\alpha,0)
.
Since
(x-\alpha)+i(y-\beta)
is holomorphic in the lower half-plane and vanishes at infinity, we have by choosing
\beta=0
,

(2.28)

X-\alpha=HY,\quad Y=-H(X-\alpha),

where

Due to the incompressibility condition for the irrotational part of the velocity
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0
,
\phi^{ir}
and therefore
\psi^{ir}
are harmonic.
One can find
\theta^{ir}(t,\alpha,\beta)
as the harmonic conjugate of
\psi^{ir}
such that they satisfy the Cauchy-Riemann equations,

\psi^{ir}_{\alpha}=\theta^{ir}_{\beta},\quad\psi^{ir}_{\beta}=-\theta^{ir}_{\alpha}.

Let
\Psi(t,\alpha)=\psi^{ir}(t,\alpha,0)
denote the boundary value of the conformal irrotational velocity potential,
and
\Theta(t,\alpha)=\theta^{ir}(t,\alpha,0)
be the boundary value of its harmonic conjugate.
Then,

(2.29)

\psi^{ir}_{\beta}|_{\beta=0}=-\Theta_{\alpha}=H\Psi_{\alpha}.

We write
Z(t,\alpha)=h(t,\alpha,0)=X(t,\alpha)+iY(t,\alpha)
.
The equations (

(2.30)

\frac{d}{dt}z_{j}(t)=\mathcal{U}(t,z_{j}(t))+\sum_{k\neq j}\frac{\lambda_{k}i}{2\pi}\frac{1}{\overline{z_{j}(t)-z_{k}(t)}},\quad\forall 1\leq j\leq N,

where
\mathcal{U}(t,z)
is the irrotational part of the complex velocity at

, and is given using the Cauchy’s integral formula and (

\mathcal{U}(t,z)=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{\Gamma_{t}}\frac{(v_{1}^{ir}+iv_{2}^{ir})(t,Z)}{Z-z}\,dZ=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{Z_{\alpha}\cdot(\Psi_{\alpha}-i\Theta_{\alpha})(t,\alpha)}{\bar{Z}_{\alpha}(Z(t,\alpha)-z)}\,d\alpha.

In the following, we will derive the equations representing the kinematic and dynamic boundary conditions on the free surface.

The kinematic boundary condition says that the normal component of the velocity of the surface
\Gamma_{t}
is equal to the normal component of the fluid velocity.
In other words,

(X_{t},Y_{t})\cdot(-Y_{\alpha},X_{\alpha})=(v_{1},v_{2})\cdot(-Y_{\alpha},X_{\alpha})\quad\text{on }\Gamma_{t}.

Applying (

) and (

(2.31)

X_{\alpha}Y_{t}-Y_{\alpha}X_{t}=-\Theta_{\alpha}+\frac{X_{\alpha}}{2\pi}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Re\left\{\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\overline{Z-z_{j}(t)}}\right\}+\frac{Y_{\alpha}}{2\pi}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Im\left\{\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\overline{Z-z_{j}(t)}}\right\}.

Note that both
h_{t}
and
h_{\alpha}
are holomorphic functions on the lower complex plane, then
\frac{h_{t}}{h_{\alpha}}
is also holomorphic and decays at infinity.
The boundary value of

\frac{X_{\alpha}X_{t}+Y_{\alpha}Y_{t}}{J}+i\frac{X_{\alpha}Y_{t}-Y_{\alpha}X_{t}}{J},\quad J:=X_{\alpha}^{2}+Y^{2}_{\alpha}=|Z_{\alpha}|^{2}.

\displaystyle iZ_{\alpha}\left(\frac{Y_{\alpha}}{2\pi J}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Im\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\overline{Z-z_{j}}}-iH\left[\frac{Y_{\alpha}}{2\pi J}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Im\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\overline{Z-z_{j}}}\right]+\frac{X_{\alpha}}{2\pi J}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Re\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\overline{Z-z_{j}}}-iH\left[\frac{X_{\alpha}}{2\pi J}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Re\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\overline{Z-z_{j}}}\right]\right)

If

is a time-dependent function, then for the composition
g(t,\cdot):=f(t,\cdot)\circ h:\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, one has the chain rule

f_{t}=g_{t}-x_{t}f_{x}-y_{t}f_{y}.

Using this property of the composition and (
2.27
), (
2.31
), (
2.32
), we compute

\displaystyle\phi_{t}|_{\beta=0}=\Psi_{t}+\Psi^{rot}_{t}-(v_{1}|_{\beta=0}X_{t}+v_{2}|_{\beta=0}Y_{t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi_{t}+\Psi^{rot}_{t}-\frac{X_{\alpha}X_{t}\Psi_{\alpha}+X_{\beta}X_{t}\Psi_{\beta}}{J}-\frac{Y_{\alpha}Y_{t}\Psi_{\alpha}+Y_{\beta}Y_{t}\Psi_{\beta}}{J}-X_{t}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Re\{v_{j}^{rot}\circ Z\}

\displaystyle-Y_{t}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Im\{v_{j}^{rot}\circ Z\}+H\left[\frac{Y_{\alpha}}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Re\{v_{j}^{rot}\circ Z\}\right]\Psi_{\alpha}-H\left[\frac{X_{\alpha}}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Im\{v_{j}^{rot}\circ Z\}\right]\Psi_{\alpha}

\displaystyle-\frac{Y_{\alpha}}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Re\{v_{j}^{rot}\circ Z\}\Theta_{\alpha}+\frac{X_{\alpha}}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Im\{v_{j}^{rot}\circ Z\}\Theta_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi_{t}+\Psi^{rot}_{t}+H\left[\frac{\Theta_{\alpha}}{J}\right]\Psi_{\alpha}-\frac{1}{J}\Theta^{2}_{\alpha}-\Re\left\{\bar{Z}_{t}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{j}^{rot}\circ Z\right\}+2\Im\left\{{\mathbf{P}}\Im\left[\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{j}^{rot}\circ Z}{Z_{\alpha}}\right]Q_{\alpha}\right\}.

Here, we use the computation

\displaystyle H\left[\frac{Y_{\alpha}}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Re\{v_{j}^{rot}\circ Z\}-\frac{X_{\alpha}}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Im\{v_{j}^{rot}\circ Z\}\right]\Psi_{\alpha}-\left[\frac{Y_{\alpha}}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Re\{v_{j}^{rot}\circ Z\}-\frac{X_{\alpha}}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Im\{v_{j}^{rot}\circ Z\}\right]\Theta_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=H\Re\left[\frac{Y_{\alpha}+iX_{\alpha}}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{j}^{rot}\circ Z\right]\Psi_{\alpha}-\Re\left[\frac{Y_{\alpha}+iX_{\alpha}}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{j}^{rot}\circ Z\right]\Theta_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\Im\left[\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{j}^{rot}\circ Z}{Z_{\alpha}}\right]\Theta_{\alpha}-H\Im\left[\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{j}^{rot}\circ Z}{Z_{\alpha}}\right]\Psi_{\alpha}=\Re\left\{-i(1-iH)\Im\left[\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{j}^{rot}\circ Z}{Z_{\alpha}}\right]Q_{\alpha}\right\}

\displaystyle=2\Im\left\{{\mathbf{P}}\Im\left[\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{j}^{rot}\circ Z}{Z_{\alpha}}\right]Q_{\alpha}\right\}.

For the second term of (
2.38
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\phi(t,\cdot,0)|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}(u^{2}+v^{2})|_{\beta=0}=\frac{1}{2J}(\Psi^{2}_{\alpha}+\Theta^{2}_{\alpha})+\frac{1}{2}\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{j}^{rot}\circ Z\Big|^{2}+\Re\left\{R\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{j}^{rot}\circ Z\right\}.

In holomorphic coordinates, the mean curvature is a function of
Z_{\alpha}
, see
[
30
]
,

\mathbf{H}=\frac{-i}{2(Z_{\alpha}+\bar{Z}_{\alpha})}\left(\frac{Z_{\alpha}-\bar{Z}_{\alpha}}{|Z_{\alpha}|}\right)_{\alpha}=-\frac{i}{2}\left(\frac{Z_{\alpha\alpha}}{J^{\frac{1}{2}}Z_{\alpha}}-\frac{\bar{Z}_{\alpha\alpha}}{J^{\frac{1}{2}}\bar{Z}_{\alpha}}\right).

Using above computations, the Bernoulli’s equation (

\displaystyle\Psi_{t}+H\left[\frac{\Theta_{\alpha}}{J}\right]\Psi_{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2J}(\Psi_{\alpha}^{2}-\Theta^{2}_{\alpha})+gY+\Re\left\{b\bar{Z}_{\alpha}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{j}^{rot}\circ Z\right\}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left|\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{j}^{rot}\circ Z\right|^{2}+\Psi^{rot}_{t}+2\Im\left\{{\mathbf{P}}\Im\left[\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{j}^{rot}\circ Z}{Z_{\alpha}}\right]Q_{\alpha}\right\}+\frac{i\sigma}{2}\left(\frac{Z_{\alpha\alpha}}{J^{\frac{1}{2}}Z_{\alpha}}-\frac{\bar{Z}_{\alpha\alpha}}{J^{\frac{1}{2}}\bar{Z}_{\alpha}}\right)=0.

Applying the operator
I-iH
to the above equation of
\Psi
, and using the convolution identity of the Hilbert transform (

), we obtain the complex-valued equation

(2.39)

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+b\partial_{\alpha})Q+2{\mathbf{P}}\Psi_{t}^{rot}-ig(Z-\alpha)-{\mathbf{P}}\left|\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{j}^{rot}\circ Z\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{{\mathbf{P}}}\left[\frac{|Q_{\alpha}|^{2}}{J}\right]+\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{j}^{rot}\circ Z}{Z_{\alpha}}Q_{\alpha}+2\sigma{\mathbf{P}}\Im\left[\frac{Z_{\alpha\alpha}}{J^{\frac{1}{2}}Z_{\alpha}}\right].

Note that
2{\mathbf{P}}\Psi^{rot}_{\alpha}
term can be written as

\displaystyle 2{\mathbf{P}}\Psi^{rot}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=(1-iH)\Psi^{rot}_{\alpha}=\psi_{\alpha}^{rot}|_{\beta=0}-i\psi_{\beta}^{rot}|_{\beta=0}

\displaystyle=\psi_{x}^{rot}|_{\beta=0}X_{\alpha}+\psi_{y}^{rot}|_{\beta=0}Y_{\alpha}-i(\psi_{x}^{rot}|_{\beta=0}X_{\beta}+\psi_{y}^{rot}|_{\beta=0}Y_{\beta})

\displaystyle=\psi_{x}^{rot}|_{\beta=0}(X_{\alpha}+iY_{\alpha})-i\psi_{y}^{rot}|_{\beta=0}(X_{\alpha}+iY_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=(\psi_{x}^{rot}-i\psi_{y}^{rot})|_{\beta=0}(X_{\alpha}+iY_{\alpha})=Z_{\alpha}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\overline{v_{j}^{rot}\circ Z}.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\frac{2}{1+{\mathbf{W}}}\partial_{t}{\mathbf{P}}\Psi^{rot}_{\alpha}=\partial_{t}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\overline{v_{j}^{rot}\circ Z}+\frac{{\mathbf{W}}_{t}}{1+{\mathbf{W}}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\overline{v_{j}^{rot}\circ Z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\overline{v_{j}^{rot}\circ Z}+\left(\frac{\bar{R}_{\alpha}}{1+{\mathbf{W}}}-\frac{b{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{1+{\mathbf{W}}}+\frac{1}{1+{\mathbf{W}}}\partial_{\alpha}\sum_{j=1}^{N}v^{rot}_{j}\circ Z-b_{\alpha}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{N}\overline{v_{j}^{rot}\circ Z}.

Taking the
\alpha
-derivative of the equation (
2.39
), using (
2.35

(\partial_{t}+b\partial_{\alpha})R=\frac{1}{1+{\mathbf{W}}}(\partial_{t}+b\partial_{\alpha})Q_{\alpha}-\frac{R}{1+{\mathbf{W}}}(\partial_{t}+b\partial_{\alpha}){\mathbf{W}},

we get that

satisfies the equation

(2.40)

(\partial_{t}+b\partial_{\alpha})R-(\partial_{t}+b\partial_{\alpha})\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\lambda_{j}i}{2\pi}\frac{1}{Z-z_{j}}-i\frac{g{\mathbf{W}}-a}{1+{\mathbf{W}}}=\frac{2\sigma}{1+{\mathbf{W}}}\mathbf{P}\Im\left[\frac{{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{J^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+{\mathbf{W}})}\right]_{\alpha},

where

is the real

defined by

(2.41)

a:=i\left(\bar{{\mathbf{P}}}[(\bar{R}+v^{rot}\circ Z)\partial_{\alpha}(R+\overline{v^{rot}\circ Z})]-{\mathbf{P}}[(R+\overline{v^{rot}\circ Z})\partial_{\alpha}(\bar{R}+v^{rot}\circ Z)]\right),

and

(2.42)

v^{rot}(t,z)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}v^{rot}_{j}(t,z)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\lambda_{j}i}{2\pi}\frac{1}{\overline{z(t)-z_{j}(t)}}.

Equations (
2.30
), (
2.36
) and (
2.40
) form the water wave system (
1.3
).
Once we solve unknowns
({\mathbf{W}},R,\{z_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N})
at each time

Z(t,\alpha)=\alpha+\int_{-\infty}^{\alpha}{\mathbf{W}}(t,y)\,dy,

which determines the fluid domain at each time

The irrotational and the rotational parts of the complex fluid velocity at point

inside the domain are given by (
1.4
) and (
2.42
).
As a consequence, we then solve the incompressible free boundary Euler equations (
1.1
) with the kinematic and dynamic boundary conditions.

3.
Water waves related estimates

In this section, we first derive some estimates that are related to the water wave system (

of
{\mathbf{W}},R
, and

These estimates will later be needed when we obtain the a priori energy estimate for water waves.

We start by computing estimates for the rotational part of the complex velocity on the surface.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
z_{j}\in\Omega_{t}
, and let
h(t,\cdot)
be the Riemann mapping in Figure
1
, we set
c_{j}:=h^{-1}(t,z_{j})
.
Then, we have the following identities:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\bar{{\mathbf{P}}}\frac{1}{Z(t,\alpha)-z_{j}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h_{z}(t,c_{j})(\alpha-c_{j})},

(3.2)

\displaystyle\bar{{\mathbf{P}}}\frac{1}{(Z(t,\alpha)-z_{j})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h_{z}(t,c_{j})^{2}(\alpha-c_{j})^{2}}.

As a consequence, for the rotational part of the velocity
v^{rot}
(
2.42
) on the surface,

(3.3)

{\mathbf{P}}(v^{rot}\circ Z)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\lambda_{j}i}{2\pi}\frac{1}{\overline{h_{z}(t,c_{j})(\alpha-c_{j})}}.

{\mathbf{P}}(v^{rot}\circ Z)\in H^{s}
for all
s\geq 0
.

Proof.

Recall that
Z(t,\alpha)-z_{j}
is the boundary value of
h(t,\alpha,\beta)-z_{j}
in the lower half plane.
Since
h(t,\cdot)
is a bijective map,
h(t,\alpha,\beta)-z_{j}
has a unique zero
c_{j}=h^{-1}(t,z_{j})
.
Hence
\frac{1}{Z(t,\alpha)-z_{j}}
is a meromorphic function with a pole
h^{-1}(t,z_{j})
of multiplicity one.
For
z
in a small neighborhood of
c_{j}
, we have the Taylor series expansion

h(t,z)-z_{j}=h_{z}(t,c_{j})(z-c_{j})+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}c_{n,j}(z-c_{j})^{n}.

Therefore,
\frac{1}{Z(t,\alpha)-z_{j}}-\frac{1}{h_{z}(t,c_{j})(\alpha-c_{j})}
is a holomorphic function on
\mathbb{H}
, and we have

\bar{{\mathbf{P}}}\frac{1}{Z(t,\alpha)-z_{j}}=\bar{{\mathbf{P}}}\frac{1}{h_{z}(t,c_{j})(\alpha-c_{j})}.

Since
\frac{1}{h_{z}(t,c_{j})(\alpha-c_{j})}
can be extended to be holomorphic in the upper half-plane,
\bar{{\mathbf{P}}}\frac{1}{h_{z}(t,c_{j})(\alpha-c_{j})}=\frac{1}{h_{z}(t,c_{j})(\alpha-c_{j})}
, and this gives the identity (
3.1
).
Similarly,

\frac{1}{(Z(t,\alpha)-z_{j})^{2}}-\frac{1}{h_{z}(t,c_{j})^{2}(\alpha-c_{j})^{2}}

is a holomorphic function on
\mathbb{H}
, so that

\bar{{\mathbf{P}}}\frac{1}{(Z(t,\alpha)-z_{j})^{2}}=\bar{{\mathbf{P}}}\frac{1}{h_{z}(t,c_{j})^{2}(\alpha-c_{j})^{2}}=\frac{1}{h_{z}(t,c_{j})^{2}(\alpha-c_{j})^{2}}.

As for
{\mathbf{P}}(v^{rot}\circ Z)
, it is given by

{\mathbf{P}}(v^{rot}\circ Z)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\lambda_{j}i}{2\pi}{\mathbf{P}}\frac{1}{\overline{Z(t,\alpha)-z_{j}}}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\lambda_{j}i}{2\pi}\frac{1}{\overline{h_{z}(t,c_{j})(\alpha-c_{j})}}.

Since points
z_{j}
are in the interior of
\Omega_{t}
and
h(t,\cdot)
is bijective,
|\alpha-c_{j}|>0
, and
\frac{1}{\alpha-c_{j}}\in L^{2}
.
Therefore,
{\mathbf{P}}(v^{rot}\circ Z)\in H^{s}\cap C^{s}_{*}
for all
s\geq 0
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
s>0
, then for the rotational part of the complex velocity on the surface,

(3.4)

\|\bar{{\mathbf{P}}}(v^{rot}\circ Z)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{{\mathcal{A}_{0}}}\|Z-\alpha\|_{H^{s}},\quad\|\bar{{\mathbf{P}}}(v^{rot}\circ Z)\|_{C^{s}_{*}}\lesssim_{{\mathcal{A}_{0}}}\|Z-\alpha\|_{C_{*}^{s}}.

Hence,
v^{rot}\circ Z
can be decomposed into the smooth holomorphic part and the antiholomorphic part that can be controlled by
Z-\alpha
in some appropriate norm.

Proof.

We express

\bar{{\mathbf{P}}}(v^{rot}\circ Z)=v^{rot}\circ Z-{\mathbf{P}}(v^{rot}\circ Z)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\lambda_{j}i}{2\pi}\left(\frac{1}{\overline{Z-z_{j}}}-\frac{1}{\overline{h_{z}(t,c_{j})(\alpha-c_{j})}}\right).

When
Z(t,\alpha)-\alpha=0
, the fluid domain
\Omega_{t}=\mathbb{H}
, so that
h_{z}=1
, and
c_{j}=z_{j}
.
Hence,

\frac{1}{\overline{Z-z_{j}}}-\frac{1}{\overline{h_{z}(t,c_{j})(\alpha-c_{j})}}=0,\quad\text{when }Z=\alpha.

Using Moser-type estimates (
2.15

2.16

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{\overline{Z-z_{j}}}-\frac{1}{\overline{h_{z}(t,c_{j})(\alpha-c_{j})}}\right\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{{\mathcal{A}_{0}}}\|Z-\alpha\|_{H^{s}},

This gives the proof of (

∎

Lemma 3.6

The time derivatives of

\left|\frac{d}{dt}z_{j}(t)\right|\leq\|R(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}+(N-1)\max_{j\neq k}\frac{|\lambda_{k}|}{2\pi}\frac{1}{|z_{j}(t)-z_{k}(t)|}\lesssim{\mathcal{A}_{1}},

Proof.

According to (

\left|\frac{d}{dt}z_{j}(t)\right|\leq|\mathcal{U}(t,z_{j})|+\sum_{k\neq j}\frac{|\lambda_{k}|}{2\pi}\frac{1}{|z_{j}(t)-z_{k}(t)|}.

∎

4.1.
A priori energy estimate for water waves

Following the more recent paradifferential approach introduced by Alazard-Burq-Zuily

[

In this section, we prove the a priori energy estimate for water waves Theorem
1.1

Recall that in Section

, we compute the para-material derivatives of
({\mathbf{W}},R)
:

(4.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &T_{D_{t}}{\mathbf{W}}=-\partial_{\alpha}T_{(1+{\mathbf{W}})(1-\bar{{\mathcal{Y}}})}R+G\\
&T_{D_{t}}R=-i\sigma T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-{\mathcal{Y}})^{2}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha\alpha}+3i\sigma T_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-{\mathcal{Y}})^{3}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}+K,\end{aligned}\right.

where the perturbative source terms

\|(G,K)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}{\mathcal{A}_{\frac{3}{2}}}\left(\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}+\|Z-\alpha\|_{H^{s+1}}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\lambda_{j}|\right),\quad s>0.

A natural attempt for the energy would be choosing

\int\sigma|\langle D\rangle^{s+{\frac{1}{2}}}{\mathbf{W}}|^{2}+|\langle D\rangle^{s}R|^{2}+|Z-\alpha|^{2}\,d\alpha+\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\lambda_{j}|^{2}.

1.5

However, its time derivative contains non-perturbative terms that cannot be bounded by the right-hand side of (
1.6

We need to add appropriate energy corrections in order to satisfy the energy estimate (
1.6

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We choose the energy

\displaystyle E_{s}:=

\displaystyle\int\sigma T_{J^{-\frac{3}{2}}}\langle D\rangle^{s+{\frac{1}{2}}}{\mathbf{W}}\cdot\langle D\rangle^{s+{\frac{1}{2}}}\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}+|\langle D\rangle^{s}R|^{2}+|Z-\alpha|^{2}\,d\alpha+\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\lambda_{j}|^{2}

\displaystyle+9\sigma\Re\int iT_{{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}T_{J^{-\frac{3}{2}}(1-{\mathcal{Y}})}\langle D\rangle^{s-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbf{W}}\cdot\langle D\rangle^{s+\frac{1}{2}}\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}\,d\alpha

\displaystyle+\left(3s-\frac{15}{2}\right)\Im\int T_{{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}\langle D\rangle^{s-1}R\cdot T_{1-{\mathcal{Y}}}\langle D\rangle^{s}\bar{R}\,d\alpha.

Here, the para-coefficient
T_{J^{-\frac{3}{2}}}
is added to the first term of the energy in order to cancel the leading term of the time derivative of
E_{s}
:

(4.2)

J^{-\frac{3}{2}}(1-\bar{{\mathcal{Y}}})(1+{\mathbf{W}})=J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-\bar{{\mathcal{Y}}})^{2}.

The last two integrals of
E_{s}
are energy corrections whose time derivatives eliminate sub-leading terms of the time derivative of the first integral of
E_{s}
.

Due to (
3.24
) for
J^{-\frac{3}{2}}
and (
2.9
), (
2.10
), (
3.10
), we estimate

\displaystyle\left|\int T_{J^{-\frac{3}{2}}-1}\langle D\rangle^{s+{\frac{1}{2}}}{\mathbf{W}}\cdot\langle D\rangle^{s+{\frac{1}{2}}}\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}\,d\alpha\right|\lesssim\|J^{-\frac{3}{2}}-1\|_{L^{\infty}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\lesssim{\mathcal{A}_{0}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}^{2},

\displaystyle\left|\Re\int iT_{{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}T_{J^{-\frac{3}{2}}(1-{\mathcal{Y}})}\langle D\rangle^{s-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbf{W}}\cdot\langle D\rangle^{s+\frac{1}{2}}\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}\,d\alpha\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C^{\epsilon}_{*}}\|J^{-\frac{3}{2}}(1-{\mathcal{Y}})\|_{C^{\epsilon}_{*}}\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\lesssim{\mathcal{A}_{0}}(1+{\mathcal{A}_{0}})\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}^{2},

\displaystyle\left|\Im\int T_{{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}\langle D\rangle^{s-1}R\cdot T_{1-{\mathcal{Y}}}\langle D\rangle^{s}\bar{R}\,d\alpha\right|\lesssim\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C^{\epsilon}_{*}}(1+\|{\mathcal{Y}}\|_{L^{\infty}})\|R\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\lesssim{\mathcal{A}_{0}}(1+{\mathcal{A}_{0}})\|R\|_{H^{s}}^{2}.

Hence, the energy

satisfies the norm equivalence (
1.5

It suffices to prove the energy estimate (
1.6

We compute the time derivative of
E_{s}
, and replace some time derivatives with para-material derivatives.

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}E_{s}=

In the next subsection, we will prove the unique existence of solutions by using the iteration.
At each step of iteration, we will consider the following system of linear differential equations:

(4.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &(\partial_{t}+T_{\tilde{b}}\partial_{\alpha}){\mathbf{W}}+\partial_{\alpha}T_{1+\tilde{A}}R=\tilde{G}\\
&(\partial_{t}+T_{\tilde{b}}\partial_{\alpha})R+i\sigma T_{1+\tilde{B}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha\alpha}-3i\sigma T_{\tilde{C}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}=\tilde{K},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\tilde{b},\tilde{A},\tilde{B},\tilde{C}
and
\tilde{G},\tilde{K}
are fixed functions.
We show that (
4.3
) has a unique solution, provided that these functions satisfy the appropriate regularity conditions.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
s>0
, and time
T>0
.
Suppose the initial condition
({\mathbf{W}}_{0},R_{0})\in{\mathcal{H}}^{s}
, the fixed functions satisfy
(\tilde{G},\tilde{K})\in C([0,T];{\mathcal{H}}^{s})
,
\tilde{b}\in C([0,T];C^{1+\epsilon}_{*})
,
\tilde{A},\tilde{B}\in C([0,T];C^{\frac{3}{2}+\epsilon}_{*})
,
\tilde{C}\in C([0,T];C^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}_{*})
,
\tilde{A}_{t},\tilde{B}_{t}\in C([0,T];C^{\epsilon}_{*})
, and
\tilde{b},\frac{1+\tilde{B}}{1+\tilde{A}}
are real-valued functions.
Then the linear equation (
4.3
) has a unique solution
({\mathbf{W}},R)\in C([0,T];{\mathcal{H}}^{s})
.
Moreover, there exists a modified energy
\tilde{E}_{s}
such that

(4.4)

\tilde{E}_{s}\approx\sigma\|{\mathbf{W}}\|^{2}_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}+\|R\|_{H^{s}}^{2},\quad\frac{d}{dt}\tilde{E}_{s}\lesssim\tilde{E}_{s}+\|(G,K)\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{s}},

where the implicit constant depends on Zygmund norms of
\tilde{b}
,
\tilde{A},\tilde{B}
,
\tilde{C}
,
\tilde{A}_{t}
and
\tilde{B}_{t}
.

Sketch of the proof.

The existence of the solution follows from the standard theory of linear PDEs, see for example Section
5
in
[
52
]
.
The difference of two solutions of (
4.3
) satisfies (
4.3
) with zero initial data and zero source term.
Hence, the solution is unique by applying Gronwall’s inequality and the modified energy estimate (
4.4
).

For the modified energy estimate, we choose

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{s}=

\displaystyle\int\sigma T_{\frac{1+\tilde{B}}{1+\tilde{A}}}\langle D\rangle^{s+{\frac{1}{2}}}{\mathbf{W}}\cdot\langle D\rangle^{s+{\frac{1}{2}}}\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}+|\langle D\rangle^{s}R|^{2}\,d\alpha+\Re\int iT_{\tilde{D}}\langle D\rangle^{s-\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbf{W}}\cdot\langle D\rangle^{s+\frac{1}{2}}\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}\,d\alpha

\displaystyle+\Im\int T_{\tilde{E}}\langle D\rangle^{s-1}R\cdot\langle D\rangle^{s}\bar{R}\,d\alpha,

where functions
\tilde{D},\tilde{E}\in C([0,T];C^{-1}_{*})
are chosen such that

-2i(1+\tilde{A})\Im\tilde{D}-2\sigma(1+\tilde{B})\Re\tilde{E}=(2s+3)\frac{1+\tilde{B}}{1+\tilde{A}}\tilde{A}_{\alpha}-6\bar{\tilde{C}}+(2s-3)\bar{\tilde{B}}_{\alpha}.

These choices of

and

ensure that the sub-leading terms of

are removed, and the leading term of
\frac{d}{dt}\tilde{E}_{s}
is eliminated with the para-coefficient

.
Then the modified energy estimate (
4.4
) is virtually the same as the proof of Theorem
1.6
, and we omit it.
∎

4.2.

As in (

We now consider the class of approximate solutions that satisfy the following three properties:

(4.10)

\displaystyle\|({\mathbf{W}},R,Z-\alpha)(t)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\times L^{2}}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\lambda_{j}|\leq C\left(\|({\mathbf{W}}_{0},R_{0},Z_{0}-\alpha)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\times L^{2}}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\lambda_{j}|\right),

for some fixed constant

Assume that the

To check the distance property (

\displaystyle|z^{(n+1)}_{j}(t)-z^{(n+1)}_{k}(t)|\geq|z^{(n+1)}_{j}(0)-z^{(n+1)}_{k}(0)|-|z^{(n+1)}_{j}(t)-z^{(n+1)}_{j}(0)|-|z^{(n+1)}_{k}(t)-z^{(n+1)}_{k}(0)|

For the distance property (

As a by-product of (

for some constant

Hence, the

Next, we show that the approximate solutions

form a Cauchy sequence.
Define the difference between two approximate solutions

Then

\left\{\begin{aligned} &T_{D_{t}}^{(n)}\hat{{\mathbf{W}}}^{(n)}=-\partial_{\alpha}T_{(1+{\mathbf{W}}^{(n)})(1-\bar{{\mathcal{Y}}}^{(n)})}\hat{R}^{(n)}+\hat{G}^{(n)}\\

where the source terms

\displaystyle\hat{G}^{(n)}=

\displaystyle+3i\sigma T_{(J^{(n)})^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-{\mathcal{Y}}^{(n)})^{3}{\mathbf{W}}^{(n)}_{\alpha}-(J^{(n-1)})^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-{\mathcal{Y}}^{(n-1)})^{3}{\mathbf{W}}^{(n-1)}_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}^{(n)}_{\alpha}.

\displaystyle\left(\|({\mathbf{W}}_{0},R_{0},Z_{0}-\alpha)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\times L^{2}}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\lambda_{j}|\right)\cdot

\displaystyle\|(\hat{{\mathbf{W}}}^{(n-1)},\hat{R}^{(n-1)},\hat{Z}^{(n-1)})\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{s-\frac{3}{2}}\times L^{2}}.

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\neq j}\frac{|\lambda_{k}|}{2\pi}\left|\frac{\hat{z}^{(n-1)}_{k}(t)-\hat{z}^{(n-1)}_{j}(t)}{\left(z_{j}^{(n)}(t)-z_{k}^{(n)}(t)\right)\left(z_{j}^{(n-1)}(t)-z_{k}^{(n-1)}(t)\right)}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\|({\mathbf{W}}_{0},R_{0},Z_{0}-\alpha)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\times L^{2}}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\lambda_{j}|\right)\left(\|(\hat{{\mathbf{W}}}^{(n-1)},\hat{R}^{(n-1)},\hat{Z}^{(n-1)})\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{s-\frac{3}{2}}\times L^{2}}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\hat{z}^{(n-1)}_{j}|\right),

5.
Cubic lifespan of solutions for 2 vortices with small symmetrized data

Recall that for holomorphic functions
f
on the real line,
\Re f=H\Im f
.
Since the Hilbert transform
H
maps even functions to odd functions, and vice versa, we can define the space of complex even and odd functions in
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}})
by

\displaystyle H^{s}_{e}:=\{f\in H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}):\Re\{f\}\text{ is odd, and }\Im\{f\}\text{ is even}\},

\displaystyle H^{s}_{o}:=\{f\in H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}):\Re\{f\}\text{ is even, and }\Im\{f\}\text{ is odd}\}.

If the initial data is symmetric with respect to the vertical axis, then
R_{0}\in H^{s}_{e}
,
Z_{0}-\alpha\in H^{s+\frac{3}{2}}_{e}
and
{\mathbf{W}}_{0}\in H_{o}^{s+\frac{1}{2}}
.
The following result asserts that the solution keeps this symmetry.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
s>\frac{3}{2}
.
If the initial data is symmetric about the vertical axis in the sense that

(1)

The initial surface and velocity are symmetric:
({\mathbf{W}}_{0},R_{0})\in H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{o}\times H^{s}_{e}
.

(2)

The number of vortices is even, and the initial positions and strengths of vortices are symmetric.
The initial vorticity
\omega_{0}
can be represented by

\omega_{0}=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{0}}(\lambda_{j}\delta_{z_{j,1}(0)}-\lambda_{j}\delta_{z_{j,2}(0)}),

where
\lambda_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
z_{j,1}(0),z_{j,2}(0)\in\Omega_{0}
for
1\leq j\leq N_{0}
.
Moreover,
\Re\{z_{j,1}(0)\}=-\Re\{z_{j,2}(0)\}
,
\Im\{z_{j,1}(0)\}=\Im\{z_{j,2}(0)\}
.

Then the water wave system (
1.3
) admits a unique solution
\left({\mathbf{W}},R,\{z_{j,1}\}_{j=1}^{N_{0}},\{z_{j,2}\}_{j=1}^{N_{0}}\right)\in C([0,T];H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}_{o}\times H^{s}_{e})\times C^{1}([0,T];\Omega_{t}^{2N_{0}})
, and
\Re\{z_{j,1}(t)\}=-\Re\{z_{j,2}(t)\}
,
\Im\{z_{j,1}(t)\}=\Im\{z_{j,2}(t)\}
for
1\leq j\leq N_{0}
.

The proof of this proposition is virtually the same as Theorem
5
in
[
43
]
, and we ask the interested readers to check there.

In the rest of this section, we will choose
T=\delta\epsilon^{-2}
, for some positive constant
\delta
.
We will take into account the symmetry of the initial data and prove Theorem
1.3
.
For the simplicity of the computation, we will write the coefficient of the surface tension
\sigma=1
in the following.
We consider the control norms defined by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}_{0}^{\sharp}}:=\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C^{\epsilon}_{*}}+\|R\|_{C^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}_{*}},\quad{\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\sharp}}:=\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C^{1+\epsilon}_{*}}+\|R\|_{C^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}_{*}},\quad{\mathcal{A}^{\sharp}_{\frac{3}{2}}}:=\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{C^{\frac{3}{2}+\epsilon}_{*}}+\|R\|_{C^{1+\epsilon}_{*}}.

These control norms do not contain the vortices interaction terms, as we will show that two vortices won’t be too close on
[0,T]
.
The key part of the proof is the following energy estimate:

Theorem 5.1
.

Under the conditions in Theorem
1.3
, there exists an energy functional
\mathcal{E}_{s}
associated with the solution that satisfies the following properties:

(1)

Norm equivalence:

(5.1)

\mathcal{E}_{s}\approx_{\mathcal{A}_{0}^{\sharp}}\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}.

(2)

Energy estimate:

(5.2)

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}_{s}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}_{0}^{\sharp}}\left({\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\sharp}}{\mathcal{A}^{\sharp}_{\frac{3}{2}}}+(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\right)\mathcal{E}_{s}+\epsilon^{2}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}.

In order to prove Theorem
1.3
, we make the following bootstrap assumption for the solution:
For some large constant
\tilde{M}\gg 2
,

(5.3)

\|({\mathbf{W}},R,Z-\alpha)(t)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{s}\times L^{2}}\leq\tilde{M}\epsilon,\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

The rest of this section is organized as follows.
In Section

, we derive estimates that are related to the point vortices.
Next, in Section

Then, in Section

, we construct a modified energy and derive the energy estimate Theorem

.
At the end, in Section

.

5.1.
Preliminary estimates

In this section, we consider some estimates that are related to terms of vortices.
We show that many of these bounds are related to some negative powers of
d_{S}(t)
(
3.6
), and also obtain a lower bound for
d_{S}(t)
.

We first estimate
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{U}_{z}
in
L^{\infty}
.
By the maximum principle for holomorphic functions and Sobolev embedding,

(5.4)

\|\mathcal{U}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}=\|\mathcal{U}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{t})}=\|R(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\tilde{M}\epsilon.

\bar{\mathcal{U}}_{z}(t,\cdot)
is a holomorphic function in
\Omega_{t}
with boundary value
\frac{\bar{R}_{\alpha}}{\bar{Z}_{\alpha}}
.
Again by the maximum principle,

(5.5)

\|\mathcal{U}_{z}(t,z)\|_{L^{\infty}}=\left\|\frac{R_{\alpha}}{Z_{\alpha}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|R_{\alpha}\|_{L^{\infty}}(1+\|{\mathcal{Y}}\|_{L^{\infty}})\lesssim 2\tilde{M}\epsilon.

Next, we control the growth of real and imaginary part of
z(t)
,
x(t):=\Re z(t)
and
y(t):=\Im z(t)
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume the conditions in Theorem
1.3
and (
5.3
), then for
0\leq t\leq T
:

(5.6)

C_{1}\frac{\lambda}{x(0)}\leq\frac{d}{dt}y(t)\leq C_{2}\frac{\lambda}{x(0)},\quad\frac{1}{C_{3}}\leq\frac{x(t)}{x(0)}\leq C_{3},

for some constants
C_{2}>C_{1}>0
,
C_{3}>1
.
In other words, point vortices mostly move in the vertical direction.
In addition, the distance of the vortices to the surface
d_{S}(t)
(
3.6
) satisfies

(5.7)

d_{S}(t)\gtrsim 1+t,\quad\text{when }\lambda<0;\quad d_{S}(t)\gg\epsilon^{-2},\quad\text{when }\lambda>0.

Proof.

By taking the imaginary part of
z_{2}(t)
,
y(t)
solves the equation

\frac{d}{dt}y(t)=\Im\mathcal{U}(t,z_{2}(t))+\frac{\lambda}{4\pi x(t)}.

Using the bootstrap assumption (
5.3
) and assumption
(4)
in Theorem
1.3
,

|\Im\mathcal{U}(t,z_{2}(t))|<|\mathcal{U}(t,z_{2}(t))|\leq\|\mathcal{U}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})}=\|R(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\epsilon\ll\frac{|\lambda|}{x(0)}.

Hence, the estimate for
\frac{d}{dt}y(t)
holds as long as one can prove the estimate for
x(t)
in (
5.6
).

Since
\Re\mathcal{U}
is an odd function,
\Re\{\mathcal{U}(t,iy)\}=0
.
By the mean value theorem,

\frac{d}{dt}x(t)=\Re\mathcal{U}(t,x(t)+iy(t))-\Re\mathcal{U}(t,iy(t))=\Re\mathcal{U}_{z}(t,\tilde{x}(t)+iy(t))x(t),

for some
\tilde{x}(t)\in(0,x(t))
.
Without loss of generality, one can assume that
\Re\mathcal{U}_{z}(t,\tilde{x}(t)+iy(t))\geq 0
, and
x(t)
is increasing on
0\leq t\leq T
.
Using the bound (
5.5
), we get

\frac{x(t)}{x(0)}\leq e^{2\tilde{M}\epsilon t},\quad\frac{1}{x(t)}\geq\frac{e^{-2\tilde{M}\epsilon t}}{x(0)},\quad t\in[0,T],

which is not enough to prove the bound (
5.6
).
To obtain a better estimate for
\frac{x(t)}{x(0)}
, one needs to get a refined bound for
|\Re\mathcal{U}_{z}(t,z)|
.

We first prove a weaker bound for
d_{S}(t)
:

(5.8)

d_{S}(t)\gtrsim 1+\epsilon t,\quad\text{when }\lambda<0;\quad d_{S}(t)\gg\epsilon^{-2},\quad\text{when }\lambda>0.

Since
\frac{|\lambda|}{x(0)}\geq M\gg 1
in the assumption of Theorem
1.3
,

\displaystyle y(t)-y(0)\leq\tilde{M}\epsilon t+\lambda\int_{0}^{t}\frac{e^{-2\tilde{M}\epsilon\tau}}{4\pi x(0)}\,d\tau\leq\tilde{M}\epsilon t-\frac{M}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2\tilde{M}\epsilon\tau}\,d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{M}\epsilon t-\frac{M}{8\epsilon\tilde{M}\pi}(1-e^{-2\tilde{M}\epsilon t})\leq-C\epsilon t,

for some constant
C>0
.
If
\lambda>0
, then
\frac{1}{x(t)}\leq\frac{1}{x(0)}
since we assume that
x(t)
is increasing, so that

y(t)-y(0)\leq\tilde{M}\epsilon t+\frac{|\lambda|}{4\pi x(0)}t.

Therefore,
|y(t)|\gg\epsilon^{-2}
on
[0,T]
.
Because
d_{S}(t)\geq|y(t)|-\|Z(t)-\alpha\|_{L^{\infty}}
, we obtain the estimate (
5.8
).

Returning back to the proof of the bound for
\frac{x(t)}{x(0)}
, we estimate
|\Re\mathcal{U}_{z}(t,\tilde{x}(t)+iy(t))|
.
Taking the
z
-derivative in (
1.4
),

\partial_{z}\mathcal{U}(t,z)=-\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{(1+{\mathbf{W}})\cdot\bar{R}(t,\alpha)}{(Z(t,\alpha)-z)^{2}}\,d\alpha,

we get using (
3.8
), for
t\in[0,T]
, and
\lambda<0
,

\displaystyle|\Re\mathcal{U}_{z}(t,\tilde{x}(t)+iy(t))|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}(1+\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{L^{\infty}})\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{|\tilde{x}(t)+iy(t)-Z(t,\alpha)|^{4}}\,d\alpha\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|R\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\epsilon\left(\inf_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}}\min_{1\leq j\leq N}|Z(t,\alpha)-\tilde{x}(t)-iy(t)|\right)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\lesssim\epsilon(1+\epsilon t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}.

Hence, we have

\frac{d}{dt}\ln\frac{x(t)}{x(0)}\lesssim\epsilon(1+\epsilon t)^{-\frac{3}{2}},

so that for some constant
C>0
,

x(t)\leq x(0)\exp\left\{C\epsilon\int_{0}^{+\infty}(1+\epsilon t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\,dt\right\}=x(0)\exp\left\{2C\right\}.

When
\lambda>0
,

\ln\frac{x(t)}{x(0)}\lesssim\int_{0}^{T}\epsilon(\epsilon^{-2})^{-\frac{3}{2}}\,dt\lesssim\delta\epsilon^{2}<2C.

Choosing the constant
C_{3}=\exp\{2C\}
gives the proof for
\frac{x(t)}{x(0)}
, which leads to the estimate (
5.6
).
Integrating
\frac{d}{dt}y(t)
, we get

|y(t)-y(0)|\geq\min\{C_{1},C_{2}\}\frac{|\lambda|}{x(0)}t\gtrsim t.

Again using
d_{S}(t)\geq|y(t)|-\|Z(t)-\alpha\|_{L^{\infty}}
, we get the estimate for
d_{S}(t)
(
5.7
).
∎

Under the assumption of Theorem
1.3
, we can write
v^{rot}\circ Z
as

v^{rot}\circ Z=\sum_{j=1}^{2}v_{j}^{rot}\circ Z=-\frac{i\lambda}{\pi}\frac{x(t)}{\overline{(Z-z_{1})(Z-z_{2})}}.

Then we have the following estimates due to Lemma 6.4 in
[
43
]
.

(
[

]
)

With the assumption in Theorem

, and the bootstrap assumption (

\displaystyle\|v^{rot}\circ Z\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\epsilon d_{S}(t)^{-\frac{3}{2}},

(5.10)

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{2}\frac{\lambda_{j}i}{2\pi}\frac{1}{(Z(t,\alpha)-z_{j}(t))^{2}}\right\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\epsilon d_{S}(t)^{-\frac{5}{2}}.

With the above estimates in hand, we estimate one of the terms in

.

Lemma 5.4
.

With the condition in Theorem
1.3
, and the bootstrap assumption (

), for
s>\frac{3}{2}
,
\lambda_{1}=\lambda
,
\lambda_{2}=-\lambda

(5.11)

\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{2}\frac{\lambda_{j}i}{2\pi}\frac{\frac{d}{dt}z_{j}(t)}{(Z(t,\alpha)-z_{j}(t))^{2}}\right\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\epsilon d_{S}(t)^{-\frac{5}{2}}+\epsilon^{2}d_{S}(t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}.

Proof.

Using the equation for

:

\frac{d}{dt}z_{j}(t)=\frac{\lambda i}{4\pi x(t)}+\mathcal{U}(t,z_{2}(t)),

we can write

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{2}\frac{\lambda_{j}i}{2\pi}\frac{\frac{d}{dt}z_{j}(t)}{(Z(t,\alpha)-z_{j}(t))^{2}}=-\sum_{j=1}^{2}\frac{\lambda_{j}\lambda}{8\pi^{2}x(t)}\frac{1}{(Z(t,\alpha)-z_{j}(t))^{2}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(t,z_{1}(t))\sum_{j=1}^{2}\frac{\lambda_{j}i}{2\pi}\frac{1}{(Z(t,\alpha)-z_{j}(t))^{2}}+\frac{\lambda i(\mathcal{U}(t,z_{1})-\mathcal{U}(t,z_{2}))}{2\pi}\frac{1}{(Z(t,\alpha)-z_{2}(t))^{2}}.

Note that
\mathcal{U}(t,z_{j}(t))
only depends on

), (

), (
5.10
), and the assumption

to estimate,

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{2}\frac{\lambda_{j}\lambda}{8\pi^{2}x(t)}\frac{1}{(Z-z_{j})^{2}}\right\|_{H^{s}}+\left\|\mathcal{U}(t,z_{1}(t))\sum_{j=1}^{2}\frac{\lambda_{j}i}{2\pi}\frac{1}{(Z-z_{j})^{2}}\right\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{3}|\lambda|}{4\pi x(0)}\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{2}\frac{\lambda_{j}i}{4\pi}\frac{1}{(Z-z_{j})^{2}}\right\|_{H^{s}}+\|\mathcal{U}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{2}\frac{\lambda_{j}i}{4\pi}\frac{1}{(Z-z_{j})^{2}}\right\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\epsilon d_{S}(t)^{-\frac{5}{2}},

∎

Similarly, we have

(5.14)

\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{2}\frac{\lambda_{j}i}{2\pi}\frac{1+\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}}{(\overline{Z-z_{j}})^{2}}\right\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim\left(1+\|{\mathbf{W}}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{2}\frac{\lambda_{j}i}{2\pi}\frac{1}{(Z-z_{j})^{2}}\right\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim\epsilon d_{S}(t)^{-\frac{5}{2}}.

Finally, we obtain a refined estimate for the advection velocity

.

.

Two parts of the advection velocity
b
satisfy the estimates, for

,

(5.15)

\|b_{1}\|_{C^{\epsilon}_{*}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}_{0}^{\sharp}}\|R\|_{C^{\epsilon}_{*}},\quad\|b_{1}\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}_{0}^{\sharp}}\|R\|_{H^{s}},\quad\|b_{2}\|_{C^{\epsilon}_{*}}+\|b_{2}\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}_{0}^{\sharp}}\epsilon d_{S}(t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}.

Proof.

As in the proof of (
3.11
), it suffices to consider the estimate
b_{2}
using (

\displaystyle\|T_{1-{\mathcal{Y}}}\bar{{\mathbf{P}}}(v^{rot}\circ Z)\|_{C^{\epsilon}_{*}}+\|\bar{{\mathbf{P}}}\Pi({\mathcal{Y}},\bar{{\mathbf{P}}}(v^{rot}\circ Z))\|_{C^{\epsilon}_{*}}\lesssim(1+\|{\mathcal{Y}}\|_{L^{\infty}})\|\bar{{\mathbf{P}}}(v^{rot}\circ Z)\|_{C^{\epsilon}_{*}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}_{0}^{\sharp}}\epsilon d_{S}(t)^{-\frac{3}{2}},

In summary, all terms in the water wave system that involve point vortices satisfy the bound (

5.2.
Normal form variables

We proceed further with a normal form idea which by now is commonly used in water wave systems, beginning with the work of Hunter-Ifrim-Tataru
[
25
]
and Alazard-Delort
[
12
]
. More recently, the normal form performed in a paradifferential setting was a key ingredient in the work of Ai-Ifrim-Tataru
[
5
,
4
]
, where it permitted to achive a close-to-scaling well-posedness theory.
Here, we follow this later approach and proceed a similar fashion as in
[
5
,
4
]
.
From the analysis in Section
3
and Section
5.1
, we can write
({\mathbf{W}}_{t},R_{t})
as the sum of leading terms of order
\frac{3}{2}
plus other source terms:

(5.16)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}{\mathbf{W}}=-T_{(1+{\mathbf{W}})(1-\bar{{\mathcal{Y}}})}R_{\alpha}+G_{1}+G_{2}+G_{3}\\
&\partial_{t}R=-iT_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-{\mathcal{Y}})^{2}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha\alpha}+K_{1}+K_{2}+K_{3},\end{aligned}\right.

where
(G_{1},K_{1})
are non-perturbative low-high terms given by

\displaystyle G_{1}

\displaystyle=-T_{b}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}-T_{{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}(1-\bar{{\mathcal{Y}}})}R+T_{(1+{\mathbf{W}})(1-\bar{{\mathcal{Y}}})^{2}\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\alpha}}R-T_{R_{\alpha}+\bar{R}_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}},

\displaystyle K_{1}

\displaystyle=-T_{b}R_{\alpha}-T_{R_{\alpha}}R+3iT_{J^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-{\mathcal{Y}})^{3}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}+\frac{5}{2}iT_{{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}-\frac{i}{2}T_{\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\alpha\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}},

and
(G_{2},K_{2})
are non-perturbative balanced terms given by

\displaystyle G_{2}=

\displaystyle{\mathbf{P}}\partial_{\alpha}\Pi(R,\bar{{\mathbf{W}}})-{\mathbf{P}}\partial_{\alpha}\Pi(R+\bar{R},{\mathbf{W}}),

\displaystyle K_{2}=

\displaystyle-{\mathbf{P}}\Pi(\bar{R},R_{\alpha})-\Pi(R_{\alpha},R)+\frac{5}{2}i\Pi\left({\mathbf{W}},{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha\alpha}\right)+\frac{3}{2}i\Pi\left({\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha},{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{i}{2}{\mathbf{P}}\Pi\left(\bar{{\mathbf{W}}},{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha\alpha}\right)-\frac{i}{2}{\mathbf{P}}\Pi\left(\bar{{\mathbf{W}}}_{\alpha\alpha},{\mathbf{W}}\right).

These two source terms satisfy the Zygmund estimates for
s>0

(5.17)

\|(G_{1},K_{1})\|_{C^{s}_{*}}\lesssim\left({\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\sharp}}+\epsilon d_{S}(t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\right)\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{C^{s+1}_{*}},\quad\|(G_{2},K_{2})\|_{C^{s}_{*}}\lesssim{\mathcal{A}^{\sharp}_{\frac{3}{2}}}\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{C^{s}_{*}}.

The rest source terms
(G_{3},K_{3})
are either cubic or higher perturbative terms or source terms that involve point vortices such as
{\mathbf{P}}[a{\mathcal{Y}}]
.
These terms satisfy the bounds

(5.18)

\|(G_{3},K_{3})\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{s}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{A}_{0}^{\sharp}}{\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\sharp}}{\mathcal{A}^{\sharp}_{\frac{3}{2}}}\|({\mathbf{W}},R)\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{s}}+\epsilon d_{S}(t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}.

We remark that many terms in
(G_{1},K_{1})
and
(G_{2},K_{2})
do not have the para-coefficients as in Lemma
3.1
of
[
48
]
.

[

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\mathbf{W}}^{[1]}+T_{(1-\bar{{\mathcal{Y}}})(1+{\mathbf{W}})}\partial_{\alpha}R^{[1]}+\text{cubic, higher, or perturbative terms}

For low-high holomorphic terms, holomorphic bilinear forms satisfy the system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\partial_{\alpha}A^{h}_{lh}(R,{\mathbf{W}})-B^{h}_{lh}(R_{\alpha},{\mathbf{W}})-iC^{h}_{lh}(R,{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha\alpha})=T_{R_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}&\\

-iA^{h}_{lh}({\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha\alpha},{\mathbf{W}})+i\partial_{\alpha}^{2}B^{h}_{lh}({\mathbf{W}},{\mathbf{W}})-iD^{h}_{lh}({\mathbf{W}},{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha\alpha})=-3iT_{{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha}-\frac{5}{2}iT_{{\mathbf{W}}_{\alpha\alpha}}{\mathbf{W}}.&\end{array}\right.

Define the symbol
\chi_{1}(\xi,\eta)
that selects the low-high frequencies (

).
By taking the Fourier transform, the symbols
\mathfrak{a}^{h}_{lh}
,
\mathfrak{b}^{h}_{lh}
,
\mathfrak{c}^{h}_{lh}
,
\mathfrak{d}^{h}_{lh}
of low-high holomorphic bilinear forms

then solve the system

5.4.

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem

We need to close the bootstrap assumption (

Applying the Gronwall’s inequality for the energy estimate (
5.2

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{s}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left\{C_{1}\int_{0}^{t}{\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\sharp}}(s){\mathcal{A}^{\sharp}_{\frac{3}{2}}}(s)+(1+s)^{-\frac{3}{2}}ds\right\}

\displaystyle\cdot

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{E}_{s}(0)+C_{2}\int_{0}^{t}\epsilon^{2}(1+s)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\exp\left\{-C_{3}\int_{0}^{s}{\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\sharp}}(\tau){\mathcal{A}^{\sharp}_{\frac{3}{2}}}(\tau)+(1+\tau)^{-\frac{3}{2}}d\tau\right\}ds\right),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\sharp}}(t)\leq{\mathcal{A}^{\sharp}_{\frac{3}{2}}}(t)\lesssim\epsilon,\quad\forall t\in[0,T],

For the bound of

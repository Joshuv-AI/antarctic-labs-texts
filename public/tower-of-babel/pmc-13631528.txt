Utilization of stakeholder engagement to inform the identification of leading indicators for clinical research enrollment performance in a large integrated health care system: The VA clinical research enrollment summit
Authors: Marcus R Johnson, Aliya Asghar Uddin, Michael Wininger, Jennifer Romesser, Kandi Velarde, Jeff Newcomb, Margaret Tiktin, Susan Alton Dailey, Cindy Liu, Grant D Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractStudy participant enrollment remains the ubiquitous rate-limiter in the successful completion of clinical trials. Enrollment efficiency is a principal determinant of study performance and outcomes; and the nature of the enrolled cohort has major implications for study generalizability. Thus, all stakeholders have a role, and responsibility, in attaining enrollment targets, and each process partner has a unique vantage-point in measuring and troubleshooting enrollment barriers. The Cooperative Studies Program (CSP) is the intramural research program for multi-site clinical trials and epidemiological studies within the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs (VA). The CSP's designated research site support infrastructure is the Network of Dedicated Enrollment Sites (NODES), which convenes annual plenary meetings to benchmark institutional performances, evaluate and update programmatic best practices, and strategize innovative approaches in clinical trial execution. The VA Clinical Research Enrollment Summit Workgroup (CREW) identifies and brings together individuals that have a vested interest in the performance of CSP and VA sponsored research (stakeholders) and this group includes representation from across CSP, invited keynote speakers, and panelists from across the VA community, yielding an evolving portrait of how clinical trialists should endeavor to motivate, manage and measure study enrollment. This manuscript focuses on the successes, challenges, and lessons learned from the VA Clinical Research Enrollment Summit Workgroup (CREW) during the development and implementation of this forum, as well as outcomes from these summits to date.Keywords: Department of veterans affairs, CSP, NODES, Clinical trial enrollment, Stakeholder engagement

Introduction

Participant enrollment remains one of the most complex and challenging components of clinical research, and its importance cannot be understated due to its impact on a study's duration and cost, quality of its results, and generalizability of its findings [[1], [2], [3], [4]]. Given this context, it is paramount that the various stakeholders within and across clinical research ecosystems (sponsors, networks, sites, patient representatives) work together collectively to better understand the multi-factorial nature of barriers to participant enrollment success (as defined by established study and/or site and network performance metrics) and develop strategies to address them. Furthermore, particular interest and focus on the leading indicators (input-oriented) of clinical trial success, i.e., metrics that predict future conditions and/or performance, may be beneficial in developing solutions to improve lagging indicators (output-oriented) of a given study's overall success, including participant enrollment [[5], [6], [7]].

The work discussed in this manuscript was conducted in the Veterans Health Administration (VHA). VHA is the largest integrated health care system in the United States with 1380 health care facilities, including 170 VA Medical Centers (VAMCs) and 1193 VA outpatient clinics that serve over 9 million Veterans enrolled in the VA health care program [[8], [9], [10]]. Within VHA, the Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) Cooperative Studies Program (CSP) is the foundation for VA's clinical trials enterprise and is housed within the VA Office of Research and Development (ORD). This clinical research infrastructure was established to provide coordination for, and enable cooperation on, VA's multi-site clinical trials and epidemiological studies [11,12]. CSP's infrastructure is comprised of several Coordinating Centers responsible for planning and conducting these types of studies. Additionally, CSP established a consortium of 20 VA Medical Centers (VAMCs) called the Network of Dedicated Enrollment Sites (NODES) that have teams (Nodes) to provide site-level expertise and innovative approaches in addressing challenges to clinical trial execution [[13], [14], [15], [16], [17]].

Though published literature is supportive of various strategies that were designed to address participant enrollment challenges in clinical research, they are diffuse and reflect spectra across the many dimensions of contemporary clinical trial design [[18], [19], [20], [21], [22]]. On the other hand, the VA provides a unique environment in which to explore and validate solutions to more effectively address challenges to study enrollment, due to its reduction of the complexity that exists in other settings (heterogenous patient populations, varied clinical and research operations and policy, etc.) [23,24].

Given NODES’ expertise and experience in executing multi-site clinical research within the VA, it was well poised to lead efforts to bring together stakeholder representation from across the broader CSP to collectively develop, implement, and evaluate strategies to improve study enrollment performance across the program (patient, site, network, study, and sponsor levels). In FY 2022, NODES initiated the VA Clinical Research Enrollment Summit which is a series of annual meetings that have a primary focus on bringing together key stakeholders from across the 3 Cooperative Studies Program (CSP) pillars (CSP Coordinating Centers, CSP Epidemiology Centers, NODES) to share best practices related to study participant enrollment, and to foster the development of new strategies to address barriers/obstacles to achieving success in this area. This manuscript focuses on the successes, challenges, and lessons learned from the VA Clinical Research Enrollment Summit Workgroup (CREW) during the development and implementation of this forum, as well as outcomes from these summits to date.

Methods

VA clinical research enrollment summit background

The NODES Executive Committee (EC) provides leadership for strategic initiatives, policies, and standards to ensure proper coordination and efficient management of the overall NODES program, along with other NODES and CSP Central Office leadership. CSP NODES leadership submitted a white paper to the NODES Executive Committee in December 2021 that provided an overview of obstacles and barriers associated with clinical research participant enrollment in the VA, as well as proposed strategies for reducing their impact and/or eliminating them (Appendix A). One of those proposed strategies included the coordination of a recruitment summit meeting that was inclusive of representation from the various VA ORD departments that conduct human subjects research, and selected stakeholders (both internal and external to VA) with the goals of sharing best practices related to participant recruitment and fostering new strategies to address recruitment challenges.

The NODES EC and CSP leadership approved the establishment of the “VA Clinical Research Enrollment Summit (hereinafter referred to as “the Summit”)” in February 2022, and planning for the meeting was initiated shortly thereafter. It was intended that these summits would have a primary focus on CSP operations and policy, and improving participant enrollment in CSP-funded studies, but that any knowledge generated, lessons learned, and/or successful practices would be shared with other VA research groups and the clinical research community more broadly (non-VA federal entities, academic healthcare and research institutions, etc.) for awareness and potential adoption where applicable. It should also be noted that the naming of the event was transitioned from “Recruitment Summit” to “Enrollment Summit” shortly after approval for the meeting was received, acknowledging that these terms represent two distinct phases of human subjects research with recruitment more generally representing the initial exchange of meaningful information with a study participant and them deciding whether or not to enroll in a research study, while enrollment is related to a participant (or their legally authorized representative) agreeing to actually participate in a study following completion of the informed consent process [25,26].

Cross-functional workgroup development

The VA Clinical Research Enrollment Summit Workgroup (CREW) is a cross-functional workgroup that was initially comprised of CSP Coordinating Center (CSPCC) staff (Center Biostatistician, Center Project Manager), CSP Epidemiology Center (CSPEC) staff (Center Program Manager), CSP NODES staff (NODES Directors and Associate Directors – Operations (ADOs), CSP NODES Leadership), and program management representation from a VA Precision Oncology Program (POP) clinical research network (The NCI and VA Interagency Group to Accelerate Trials Enrollment (NAVIGATE)) [27,28]. These CREW members were identified and invited to participate on this effort by CSP NODES Leadership, and they all represent core functional and operational areas of CSP and VA research. Their engagement, feedback, and input were critical in advancing these enrollment summits from concept into actual practice. The CREW was convened in March 2022 and met over the course of nine months (virtually through teleconferencing) to plan the inaugural summit, which included the development of the summit agenda.

Results

VA clinical research enrollment summits

The inaugural Summit was held in November 2022 as an in-person meeting, and attendees included additional representation from the CSP and VA research groups represented in the CREW, as well as other key stakeholders in CSP research including NODES Directors and ADOs, CSPCC Directors and Deputy Directors, CSPEC Associate Directors and Program/Project Managers, CSP research site staff (Local Site Investigator and Study Coordinators), VA facility research leadership (Associate Chiefs of Staff – Research), and National Study Investigators (CSP Study Chairs) and Coordinators. The meeting agenda template is attached with specific attendee information replaced by the attendees’ respective role (Appendix B).

The first meeting day included a keynote talk by CSP/ORD leadership which highlighted leadership support for the summit initiative and demonstrated the importance of clinical research, including study enrollment, for the broader VA healthcare system and the health and well-being of the nation's Veterans. A summary of CSP study enrollment performance (FY21-22) was then presented which provided a current state of enrollment performance in the program and set the foundation for the remainder of the day. Additional Day One meeting topics included two panel discussions, including one composed of research site representatives and the other including representatives from the sponsor (CSP) to discuss their perspectives of major challenges/barriers and success as it relates to study participant enrollment. These panel discussions resulted in the identification of three core opportunity areas that were proposed as “leading indicators (LE-Is)” of CSP study participant enrollment performance as follows: LE-I #1: Information Sharing and Collaboration Between CSP Pillars, LE-I #2: Research Staff Training, and LE-I #3:Veteran Engagement, meaning that attendees considered work and improvement in these areas as determinants for study enrollment performance (lagging indicator) (Table A). The second day of the meeting included attendees having the chance to join one of three breakout groups (each focused on one of the three LE-Is) based on interest and expertise, and the remainder of the day was devoted to brainstorming and development of proposed strategies aimed at improving the respective breakout group topic (LE-I). Several breakout group members also agreed to take the lead in creating workgroups (one for each LE-I) including seeking out additional representation from stakeholder groups within CSP to round out those membership rosters.

Leading indicator (LE-I) workgroups

LE-I #1: Information Sharing and Collaboration Between CSP pillars workgroup (ISC-WG)

Following the inaugural Summit (December 2022), the cross-functional Information Sharing and Collaboration Workgroup (ISC-WG) was established including representation from key program stakeholder groups and pillars including NODES (Directors and ADOs), the CSPCCs (Biostatistician and Project Manager) and a National Study Coordinator. The ISC-WG's goals were (1) to strengthen cross-pillar collaboration to support the dissemination of best practices, identification of operational barriers, and sharing of lessons learned across CSP studies, and (2) to improve bi-directional information flow between study sponsors, including CSPCCs, CSPECs, NODES, and study site teams to enhance study execution and overall operational efficiency.

ISC-WG leads were appointed, and the group convened monthly throughout the year and between the inaugural and subsequent enrollment summits. During their initial meetings, the ISC-WG developed a set of Objectives and Key Results (OKRs) to guide the selection of priority areas, define specific deliverables, and establish metrics for monitoring progress [29]. Four major projects were subsequently implemented and are in various stages of execution and evaluation (Integration of NODES Representation in CSP Study Governance (Completed: 03/31/2026), Development of a CSP-Wide Best Practices Repository (Completed: 02/10/2025), Enhanced Support for CSP Study Sites (In Progress), and Communication of Study Operational Updates to Study Sites (In Progress)) (Table C).

LE-I #2: Research Staff Training workgroup

The Research Study Staff Training Workgroup (RSST – WG) was established following feedback from the 2022 Summit, which acknowledged the need to enhance the knowledge and competence of CSP clinical research teams to improve participant enrollment, data quality, and study execution. The RSST-WG, composed of representation from CSP NODES (ADOs, Clinical Research Nurse, Operations Manager), CSPECs (Program Manager), and CSP National Study Chairs Office (CSP National Study Coordinator), began monthly meetings to develop strategies for addressing these gaps. Two priorities were identified for further evaluation and strategy development: site mentorship and staff training.

For the priority of site mentorship, the group referenced a previously implemented framework (NODES Site Mentorship Model) that was associated with improved enrollment at low-performing sites in an individual CSP clinical trial [30]. One component of that mentorship project included a site mentorship checklist that was developed to assess site-specific needs, barriers, and performance challenges. The RSST-WG updated the checklist to incorporate clearer form guidance and purpose, removed study specific information and replaced those areas with general verbiage applicable to all studies, and incorporated an additional question for the Investigator and Study Coordinator with regards to their meeting frequency (Completed: 06/13/2024). The NODES mentorship model was then implemented for two additional CSP clinical trials to determine and evaluate its potential association with study participant enrollment, using the revised site mentorship checklist. A manuscript describing that work is currently under journal review.

Concurrently, the RSST-WG initiated development of a staff training program, selecting informed consent/Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) compliance and data collection/management as initial focus areas. This decision was largely based on their relevance to Good Clinical Practice (GCP) and importance to clinical research study execution, as well their significance to CSP in terms of having been recognized as opportunities for improvement during program site monitoring and auditing efforts [[31], [32], [33], [34], [35]]. To guide program design, a survey was distributed to CSP study coordinators (site) and NODES staff to assess research experience, learning preferences, and preferred training delivery methods. Survey responses were analyzed (n = 90), and existing resources were identified for repurposing to create targeted educational sessions. Development of these modules is ongoing, with plans for pilot implementation this fall with the first training session being scheduled for 10/22/2026.

LE-I #3: Veteran Engagement workgroup

The Veteran Engagement Workgroup (VE-WG) began meeting monthly after the CSP Enrollment Summit with the intention of exploring the feasibility of integrating Veteran engagement strategies into CSP studies. The VE-WG has also presented annually at the enrollment summits with updates on their progress related to various activities. The broad aims of this workgroup were to increase engagement of Veterans in the design, planning, and execution of CSP studies as a strategy to integrate research options into VA clinical care; to broaden access to VA research generally; and to maximize the impact of CSP research by focusing on health care concerns that are important to Veterans. The workgroup began by reviewing and summarizing literature around participant engagement in clinical trials and identifying different models or strategies for engagement, primarily focusing on an organization-level standing panel versus a study-specific panel or having Veterans integrated into the study team [[36], [37], [38], [39]]. Over the course of the first 20 months of work, the VE-WG also consulted with various experts and study teams that were implementing strategies of Veteran engagement across VA research divisions, i.e., non-CSP VA research groups (11/2022-7/2024). These consultations provided the workgroup with opportunities to see how different Veteran engagement models worked and to understand more about associated logistics and costs related to their implementation.

From this preliminary research (consultation feedback and observations), the group developed key recommendations for CSP leadership related to the engagement of Veterans in CSP study design and management activities, which included a more significant review of CSP's institutional needs and readiness around Veteran engagement. Thus in 2024, the VE-WG began consulting with GROVE (Growing Rural Outreach through Veteran Engagement), a group well-versed in Veteran engagement [40]. GROVE put together a proposal for CSP leadership that included an environmental scan to assess institutional readiness prior to identifying a strategy that would work best for the organization to achieve VE-specific program objectives.

This proposal was presented to CSP and CSPCC leadership in the fall of 2024, and specific objectives related to Veteran engagement in the context of CSP operations are currently under development. Additionally, several alternatives to the initial GROVE proposal have also been presented to these leadership groups that addressed feedback received during the initial proposal presentation (6/17/2025 & 4/27/2026). This work contributed to receiving buy-in and support for planning a centralized and independent CSP VE committee as an approach to improve Veteran engagement and participation across the CSP research lifecycle (planning, start-up, enrollment, follow-up, etc.) (In Progress - Planning Started 12/2025). One of the highlights of this workgroup, and overall effort, was the inclusion of Veteran participation at our annual Summit starting with our 2024 event (12/3-12/4/2024) and continued at our most recent meeting (12/9-12/10/2025). The VE-WG also added a Veteran workgroup member on 7/19/2025.

Discussion

Clinical trial success

The notion of “success” in a clinical research program or organization is contextual and varies by setting. In the pharmaceutical industry, a study is successful if the therapy is progressed to the subsequent clinical trial phase, or on to a regulatory filing [[43], [44], [45]]. However, from its founding as an intramural research program within the VA's Medical Research Service, CSP serves the mission of advancing the health and care of Veterans, with collateral impact on national health care challenges [46,47]. The CSP model is to conduct definitive studies that produce outcomes aimed at steering and advancing clinical practice [11]. Indeed, to ensure that CSP investigators are not biased in cultivating a specific therapeutic platform, study proponents are expected to endorse clinical equipoise at the time of study proposal [11,47]. Especially when the end-product optimizes the “Iron Triangle” of cost, time, and quality, the success of a CSP study is one that achieves its enrollment target [48]. The Summit has facilitated the identification of LE-Is that all have an impact on the cost, time, and quality of our CSP studies and therefore, we consider this effort overall as a success for our program.

The Summit initiative represents an important step toward systematically addressing the longstanding and multifactorial challenges associated with participant enrollment in VA Cooperative Studies Program (CSP) clinical trials. Enrollment barriers in clinical research are well recognized, yet solutions often remain diffuse, inconsistently applied, or insufficiently integrated across complex research infrastructures. By organizing diverse stakeholders from across the CSP pillars (Coordinating Centers, Epidemiology Centers, NODES teams), and other CSP research stakeholders, e.g., National Study Leadership, research site staff, and Veteran-focused programs-the Summit created a structured opportunity for collaborative problem-solving designed to strengthen study operations and improve challenges related to research study participant enrollment.

A key contribution of this work is the formal identification of three leading indicators (LE-Is)—Information Sharing and Collaboration, Research Staff Training, and Veteran Engagement—of participant enrollment success (Table B). The VA, being an integrated healthcare system with established research infrastructure, was uniquely positioned for addressing enrollment challenges for CSP. The Summit and subsequent CREW workgroups operationalized these LE-Is by translating high-level challenges into targeted initiatives that improved program-wide communication pathways, enhanced staff training resources, and included Veteran feedback into study design and implementation.

The work of the ISC-WG highlights the need for a more consistent and transparent operational environment. Fragmented communication across research communities is a common barrier to efficient trial execution [49]. By establishing objectives, creating shared governance processes, and developing mechanisms such as a CSP-wide best practices repository and enhanced site support structures, the ISC-WG made progress toward improving bi-directional information flow and allowing earlier identification of operational break-down (Appendix C) (Appendix D). These efforts are particularly relevant in a system where multi-site coordination is critical, and any operational delays can significantly affect research study timelines and enrollment success.

Similarly, the RSST-WG addressed a major gap frequently cited across clinical research settings: variability in research staff experience, training, and access to mentorship [50]. The adaptation and expansion of the NODES mentorship model for multiple CSP studies, coupled with efforts to design standardized training modules focused on informed consent, HIPAA compliance, and data management, demonstrate how structured educational programs can help in staff competency which may contribute to enhanced participant engagement, more efficient study start-up, and better adherence to protocol-driven activities—all of which are linked to enrollment performance.

The VE-WG brings forward another critical dimension: integrating Veteran perspectives into CSP research planning and execution. Early discussions showed that meaningful engagement strategies offer real benefits but are challenging to implement at scale. The development of recommendations for CSP leadership and the initiation of readiness assessments around Veteran engagement reflect a thoughtful, phased approach to ensuring that future engagement efforts are sustainable and aligned with our organizational mission. CSP research studies are designed and implemented for Veterans to improve their well-being and to address their healthcare needs. Stakeholder perspectives directly shape the relevance and feasibility of study procedures; continuous and inclusive engagement is essential for ensuring that trials are designed and implemented effectively [51]. The momentum toward establishing a centralized CSP Veteran Engagement committee is a notable milestone, as Veteran engagement and their feedback play a significant role in improving study relevance, trust, communication, and ultimately, participant enrollment.

Formal inquiry into the summit activities and subsequent changes in CSP enrollment is beyond the scope of this paper, however descriptive trends highlight the potential influence of system-wide operational improvements. Enrollment performance fluctuated over time period (FY21–25) as shown in Figure A, due to variations in total number of studies, number of sites and other possible contextual factors (such as site-level challenges) related to study execution. Still, the observed improvements following periods of intensive CREW activity, leadership engagement, and LE-I workgroup implementation may suggest that coordinated, structured, and stakeholder-driven initiatives hold promises for supporting enrollment success. Continued monitoring and prospective evaluation will be essential to better understanding these associations, and the VA Clinical Research Enrollment Summit, including reporting from the established workgroups to further explore the impact of our work in the context of the identified LE-Is, will be continued. This approach also allows for the potential identification of additional opportunities for improvement during the annual meeting series, as well as through workgroup reporting given that those groups are working directly with various national CSP study teams, research staff, and other functional groups within the organization on a regular basis for the projects and activities that have been highlighted. In addition to study enrollment performance, we envision exploring associations between our work and the cost, time, and quality of our CSP studies as additional focus areas given their importance to and impact on overall clinical research success.

Several limitations of this work should be acknowledged. As an operational improvement initiative, the work was not designed to formally test causal relationships or quantify the direct impact of individual strategies on enrollment outcomes. As an operational initiative, our focus was on process exploration and not comparative effectiveness research; thus, insights presented here are inferential but not definitive. The variability in study designs, study site capacity, and specific site-level challenges across fiscal years also obscure interpretation of program-level trends. Additionally, while representation across stakeholder groups was significant, ensuring sustained engagement and equitable input across all CSP sites, and study leadership will remain an ongoing challenge. Lastly, we recognize that healthcare monoliths are idiosyncratic: each system poses carries its own challenges and opportunities. Our work here provides insight into a model developed in just one healthcare system; we emphasize that it is not our specific findings, but rather our framework of self-assessment that would be of greatest interest to readers outside of the Department of Veterans Affairs. While it would likely prove beneficial for research sponsor leadership and stakeholders across the broader research enterprise to evaluate operations in their respective institutions in the context of the leading indicators that our VA group identified as impacting study enrollment performance (LE-I #1: strengthening communication pathways between key organizational stakeholder groups; LE-I #2: improving training for research site and sponsor staff; and LE-I #3: further engaging patient populations and communities of interest), we would implore other groups to more closely focus on the framework of self-assessment that we employed for this effort when thinking about this work in the context of their institutions, i.e., identifying individuals within the organization that have a vested interest in clinical research performance and working together to collectively determine priority areas for improvement (lagging indicators) and what factors impact performance (leading indicators).

Despite these limitations, this initiative reflects a proactive and collaborative approach to strengthening clinical research operations within the nation's largest integrated healthcare system. By focusing on leading indicators of enrollment success and building infrastructure to support continuous learning, CSP is well positioned to improve participant recruitment, enhance study execution, and advance high-quality research that benefits Veterans.

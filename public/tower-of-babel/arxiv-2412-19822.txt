Relation between Exponential Moment problem and Classical Moment problem
O. Kounchev, H. Render, Ts. Tsachev

Abstract
The main result of the paper is an interesting relation between the solution of the truncated Exponential Moment problem and truncated Classical Moment problem, considered on the half-line or on a compact interval. AMS Classification, MSC2010 : 44A60, 30E05, 42A82. Key words : exponential polynomials, moment problem.

Exponential polynomials

Exponential polynomials in a general setting have been given a special
attention e.g. in
[

For simplicity sake, in the present paper we will assume that the vector of
the ”frequencies”

imaginary parts of the constants

. We
assume that
\mathbb{S}
is a linear functional on the linear span

U_{N}=\left\langle e^{\lambda_{0}x},....,e^{\lambda_{N}x}\right\rangle_{\operatorname{lin}}\text{ with }N=2n-1,

(1)

The function (sometimes called fundamental)

\Phi^{\left(j\right)}\left(0\right)=0\qquad\text{for }j=0,1,...,N-1;\qquad\Phi^{\left(N\right)}\left(0\right)=1.

For the proof that the system of functions

we refer to
[

[

is defined by the finite number of ”exponential
moments”:

\widehat{c}_{j}:=\widehat{c}_{N,j}:=\int j!\cdot\Phi_{\left(\lambda_{0},....,\lambda_{N}\right)}^{\left(N-j\right)}\left(x\right)d\mu\left(x\right)=\int b_{j}\left(x\right)d\mu\left(x\right)\text{ for }j=0,...,N.

(2)

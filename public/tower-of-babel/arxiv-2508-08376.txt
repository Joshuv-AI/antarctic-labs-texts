Continuous topological phase transition between ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} topologically ordered phases
Qi Zhang, Wen-Tao Xu

Abstract
Topological phase transitions beyond anyon condensation remain poorly understood. A notable example is the transition between the toric code (TC) and double semion (DS) phases, which has two distinct ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} topological orders in ( 2 + 1 ) (2+1) D. Previous studies reveal that the transition between them can be either first order or via an intermediate phase, thus the existence of a directly continuous transition between them remains a long-standing problem. Motivated by the fact that both phases can arise from condensing distinct anyons in the ℤ 4 \mathbb{Z}_{4} topological order, we introduce a perturbed ℤ 4 \mathbb{Z}_{4} quantum double (QD) model to study the TC-DS transition. We confirm the existence of a continuous ( 2 + 1 ) (2+1) D XY* transition between the TC and DS phases by mapping it to a two-coupled quantum Ising model. Importantly, using the condensation order parameters and the area law coefficients of the Wilson loops, we further reveal that ℤ 4 \mathbb{Z}_{4} anyons, fractionalized from the ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} topological orders, become deconfined at the transition between ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} topologically ordered phases. Our results open a path toward developing a theoretical framework for topological phase transitions beyond anyon condensation.

However, topological phase transitions that are
not
induced by anyon condensation remain poorly understood. A notable example is the transition between the toric code (TC)
Kitaev 2003
and double semion (DS) phases
Levin and Wen 2005
, which represent two distinct
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
topological orders with incompatible anyons.
By interpolating fixed TC and DS string-net models using a one-parameter Hamiltonian, the transition has been studied using exact diagonalization
Morampudi et al. 2014
and quantum Monte Carlo
Dupont et al. 2021a
;
Dupont et al. 2021b
, the former method indicates a first-order transition, while the latter method suggests the existence of an intermediate gapless phase, as shown in Fig.
1
a.
Additionally, a fine-tuned transition between the TC and DS phases can be realized using deformed projected entangled pair states (PEPS) with some tuning parameters
Duivenvoorden et al. 2017b
;
Iqbal et al. 2018b
;
Xu and Zhang 2018
;
Liu et al. 2024
. Furthermore, a field-theoretic argument has been proposed for a continuous transition between the TC and DS phases in the presence of SU(2) symmetry
Barkeshli 2013
.
Despite these efforts, how to concretely realize a direct continuous transition between the TC and DS phases using a generic Hamiltonian remains a long-standing open question.
If such a continuous transition exists, the nature of the critical point, particularly how the incompatible anyons of the two
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
topological orders reconcile at the critical point, is unclear.

In this letter, we confirm the existence of a direct continuous TC-DS transition and provide new insights into its nature. Rather than considering a model interpolating directly between fixed points of the TC and DS phases
Morampudi et al. 2014
;
Dupont et al. 2021a
;
Dupont et al. 2021b
, we propose a perturbed
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
quantum double (QD) model based on the observation that both phases can emerge from the
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
topological order via anyon condensation
Duivenvoorden et al. 2017a
;
Iqbal et al. 2018a
;
Ellison et al. 2022
.
To determine its phase diagram and the quantum criticality of the continuous TC-DS transition, we introduce a duality transformation that maps the perturbed
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
QD model to a two-coupled
(2+1)
D quantum Ising model and approximate its ground states with variationally optimized infinite PEPS (iPEPS)
Corboz 2016
;
Vanderstraeten et al. 2016
based on automatic differentiation
Liao et al. 2019
.

Using the condensation order parameters and the area law coefficients of the Wilson loop expectation values, we identify that the continuous TC-DS transition belongs to the XY* universality class. Interestingly, as shown in Fig.
1
b, the continuous TC-DS transition resembles the deconfined quantum critical point
Senthil et al. 2004a
;
Senthil et al. 2004b
—a direct continuous transition between two phases that spontaneously break different symmetries and exhibit fractionalized excitations—in the following aspects: First, the perturbed
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
QD model has two different 1-form symmetries, which are loop-like generalized symmetries. One 1-form symmetry is broken spontaneously in the TC phase, while the other one is broken spontaneously in the DS phase.
Second, some anyons in the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
topological orders are fractionalized and become deconfined at the continuous TC-DS transition point.
Our results suggest that at the critical point between topological phases that cannot be connected via anyon condensation, fractionalized anyons that are confined in both adjacent phases can emerge. This opens a path toward developing a general theoretical framework for topological phase transitions beyond anyon condensation.

Interestingly, the continuous TC-DS transition exhibits features that resemble deconfined quantum critical points
Senthil et al. 2004a
;
Senthil et al. 2004b

exhibit fractionalized excitations—in the following aspects: First, from Fig.

\langle\boldsymbol{e}^{2}\rangle=0
in the DS phase while
\langle\boldsymbol{e}^{2}\boldsymbol{m}^{2}\rangle=0
in the TC phase, implying the gapped TC and DS phases spontaneously break two different 1-form symmetries,
\prod_{e\in C}Z_{e}^{2}
and
\prod_{e\in\hat{C}}W_{e}^{2}

\boldsymbol{m}
and
\boldsymbol{m}^{3}

\boldsymbol{m}
and
\boldsymbol{m}^{3}

\prod_{e\in C}Z_{e}^{2}
and
\prod_{e\in\hat{C}}W_{e}^{2}
do not have mixed anomalies.

Discussions and outlook.

In this letter, we propose a perturbed
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
QD model to study the topological phase transitions between the TC and DS phases, from which we find that the direct continuous phase transition exists between these two
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
topologically ordered phases.
We identify that the continuous TC-DS transition belongs to the XY* universality class, where deconfined
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
anyons emerge. Our findings imply that if we consider a model without any explicit
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
structure but exhibiting a continuous TC-DS transition, fractionalized anyons can emerge at the transition.
We emphasize that the TC-DS transition is not a fine-tuned result since it is a continuous transition line in the plane defined by
h_{x}<0.8
and
h_{z}=h_{w}
, and we further verified this conclusion through the analysis of O(2) field theory
app
.
Since the TC and DS phases are dual to two different
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry protected topological (SPT) phases in
(2+1)
D
Levin and Gu 2012
, the multi-critical point can also be interpreted as a topological critical point between two
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
SPT phases.
Our results shed light on constructing a general theoretical framework for continuous topological phase transitions beyond anyon condensation, i.e. considering emergent anyons to reconcile two incompatible topological orders.

Since the TC–DS transition can be simulated on a digital quantum computer
Liu et al. 2024
, it would be interesting to experimentally realize this transition and measure the corresponding anyonic order parameters. In addition, one may consider adding more perturbations like
-h_{x}^{\prime}\sum_{e}X_{e}+\text{h.c.}
,
-h_{z}^{\prime}\sum_{e}Z_{e}+\text{h.c.}
, to the perturbed
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
QD model in Eq. (
Continuous topological phase transition between
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
topologically ordered phases
).
These terms explicitly break the exact 1-form symmetries
\prod_{e\in C}Z_{e}^{2}
,
\prod_{e\in\hat{C}}X_{e}^{2}
, hence the duality transformation and non-local order parameters do not work anymore. In such cases, the Fredenhagen–Marcu order parameter can be used to detect anyon condensation
Fredenhagen and Marcu 1983
;
Marcu 1986
;
Xu et al. 2025a
, and an adiabatic approach can be used to investigate confinement
Xu et al. 2025b
.
Alternatively, the broken exact 1-form symmetry could be restored via quantum error correction
Liu et al. 2025
.

An interesting question regarding the duality transformation is: How can we explicitly construct the operator that implements the duality transformation?
We can think about this question from the fact that two categories
\text{Vec}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})
and
\text{Vec}^{\omega_{\text{II}}}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2})
are Morita equivalent
de Wild Propitius 1995

Williamson et al. 2017

\omega_{\text{II}}

\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}

Specifically, we can apply a finite depth unitary circuit (FDUC) to transform the perturbed
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
QD model to a perturbed type-II cocycle twisted
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
QD model
Lootens et al. 2022

Then, via a quantum circuit whose depth grows at least linearly on the system size (LDQC)

\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
QD model to the perturbed but untwisted
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
QD model, whose topological order is simply two copies of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
topological order. Finally, applying the tensor product of two Wegner duality (duality
⊗2
) transformations on the perturbed but untwisted
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
quantum double model, we arrive at the two-coupled Ising model. The effect of applying these transformations on the fixed point
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
QD model can be summarized as:

\penalty\ \mbox{Vec}(\mathbb{Z}_{4})\xrightarrow{\text{FDQC}}\mbox{Vec}^{\omega_{II}}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2})\xrightarrow{\text{LDQC}}\mbox{Vec}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2})\xrightarrow{\text{duality}^{\otimes 2}}\mbox{
Paramagnet},

(S9)

and the transformation for the perturbed
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
QD model is the same one. Interestingly, it is known that the DS phase has the intrinsic sign problem
Smith et al. 2020
;
Zhang et al. 2020
, but the duality transformation maps it to a paramagnetic phase without the intrinsic sign problem. The underlying reason is that the intrinsic sign problem has been removed by the LDQC. It would be interesting to explicitly work out the transformations shown in Eq. (
S9
) in the future.

SIV

O(2)

In this section, we show how to predict the critical exponents of the condensation order parameters and the area law coefficients at the TC-DS transition point, i.e. the multi-critical point where four phases meet.
A natural candidate for effective Ginzburg-Landau field theory at the multi-critical point of the two-coupled Ising model is the O(2)
\phi^{4}
model described by the Lagrangian:

\mathcal{L}=\left(\partial\boldsymbol{\phi}\right)^{2}/2+m^{2}\boldsymbol{\phi}^{2}/2+g\boldsymbol{\phi}^{4}/(4!)+\cdots,

(S10)

where
m
is the mass and
g
is the coupling strength, and
\boldsymbol{\phi}=(\phi_{0},\phi_{1})
is a two-component order parameter field corresponding respectively to the two local order parameters
\langle\tau^{z}\sigma^{x}\rangle
and
\langle\tau^{z}\rangle

\phi^{4}
model belongs to the XY universality class
Wilson and Fisher 1972
;
Henriksson 2023
. However, when
\mathcal{L}
describes the physics near the multi-critical point of the perturbed
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
QD model,
\boldsymbol{\phi}=(\phi_{0},\phi_{1})
corresponds to the two condensation order parameters
\langle\prod_{e\in\tilde{C}}W^{2}_{e}\rangle
and
\langle\prod_{e\in C}Z^{2}_{e}\rangle
, and the Wilson-Fisher fixed point of
\mathcal{L}
belongs to so-called XY* universality class. The starred and non-starred versions of the Ginzburg-Landau-Wilson (GLW) theory differ in the following aspects
Schuler et al. 2016
;
Schuler et al. 2023
:
First, in the starred version
\phi_{i}
can only be created in pairs on a torus just like anyons; Second,
\phi_{i}
and
-\phi_{i}
are indistinguishable in the started version, which is so-called orbifording, so that both periodic and anti-periodic boundary conditions have to be considered; Third, the starred version GLW theory describes the higher-form symmetry breaking transition, while the non-stared version describing the unusual symmetry breaking transition. Nevertheless, the scaling dimensions of certain fields in the XY and XY* universality classes are the same.

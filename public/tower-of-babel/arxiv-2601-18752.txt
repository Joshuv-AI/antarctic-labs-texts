Role of the symmetry energy on hybrid stars
H. Güven, K. Bozkurt, E. Khan, J. Margueron

Abstract
The impact of the symmetry energy on the properties of compact stars is analyzed considering constraints from nuclear physics and astrophysics. A compact star can be a neutron star composed only of nuclear matter or a hybrid star with a quark core. Two typical models (soft and stiff) are considered for the nuclear equation of state, and for the hybrid one, a parameterized first-order phase transition approach, completed with a linear quark matter equation of state, is implemented. We show that the phase transition reduces the tension between GW170817 and NICER observations, and we illustrate the impact of the symmetry energy for the understanding of the nature of the binary system in GW170817. We also confirm our previous findings that the GW170817 waveform is best described as a binary HS with a low-density onset of stiff quark matter. This could also be interpreted as a quarkyonic cross-over.

Introduction

Neutron stars (NSs) are laboratories for the properties of supra-dense matter and large isospin asymmetries (excess of neutrons over protons), requiring expertise from nuclear physics, particle physics, and astrophysics

In order to make progress on the understanding of supra-dense matter existing in nature, a theoretical approach, combining the complementary nuclear physics knowledge with the multi-messenger astrophysical observations

The gravitational wave event GW170817, also known as the "golden event", contributes to better constraining the dense matter equation of state (EoS)

In our study, we adopt two typical nucleon EoS: a soft one represented by the Skyrme SLy5 Hamiltonian
Chabanat et al. 1998
and a stiff one represented by the relativistic PKDD Lagrangian
Long et al. 2004
. The soft nucleon model is compatible with recent chiral effective field theory predictions for the symmetry energy

We implement the constraints from gravitational wave signals, radio astronomy, and nuclear physics within a Bayesian framework, similar to our previous analysis in Ref.
Güven et al. 2023

We explore three cases associated with the binary systems in the GW170817 observation: i) two neutron stars (BNS), which implies that QM occurs at sufficiently large density for the binary system to remain on the nucleonic branch for the GW170817 observation, ii) a hybrid star (HS) together with a neutron star (HSNS), using the fact that the two systems in GW170817 can have different masses, the low mass system being associated to a NS and the high mass one to a HS, or iii) two-hybrid stars (BHS), which implies a low value for the QM onset density

II
Theoretical Framework

We detail the modeling that reflects the properties of dense matter (crust EoS, nucleon meta-model, and FOPT to QM) as well as the general relativity equations to obtain CS’s properties. In this way, EoSs are linked to global CS’s properties such as masses, radii, and tidal deformabilities.

II.1
Crust and outer core

The Douchin-Haensel EoS

P. 2001

II.2
First order phase transition and quark matter equation of state

The EoS in QM is provided by an approximation providing an analytic representation of modern EoS, see Ref.
Zdunik, J. L. and Haensel,
P. 2013
and references therein. Inspired by the MIT bag model
Chodos et al. 1974
, QM is represented by a linear EoS, i.e., the pressure
p
is a linear function of the energy density
\epsilon
. The energy density in nuclear and quark matter reads
Zdunik, J. L. and Haensel,
P. 2013
;
Alford et al. 2013
;
Chamel, N. et al. 2013
,

\varepsilon(p)=\begin{cases}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{NUC}}(p)\hbox{, if }p<p_{\mathrm{PT}},\\
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{QM}}(p)=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{NUC}}(p_{\mathrm{PT}})+\Delta\varepsilon_{\mathrm{PT}}+(p-p_{\mathrm{PT}})/\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}\,,\end{cases}

(11)

otherwise, where
\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}
is related to the sound speed in QM
c_{s,\mathrm{PT}}
as
\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}=(c_{s,\mathrm{PT}}/c)^{2}
and
c
is the speed of light in the vacuum. Since the phase transition is of first order and the density is an order parameter, it is discontinuous through the phase transition and jumps from the density at which the first QM drop appears, hereafter referred to as
n_{\mathrm{PT}}
, and the maximum density of the phase coexistence region,
n_{\mathrm{tr}}
. The density step in the FOPT is defined as,

\Delta n_{\mathrm{PT}}=n_{\mathrm{tr}}-n_{\mathrm{PT}}\,,

(12)

producing the latent heat through the phase transition,

\Delta\epsilon_{\mathrm{PT}}=\epsilon_{\mathrm{QM}}(n_{\mathrm{tr}})-\epsilon_{\mathrm{NUC}}(n_{\mathrm{PT}})\,.

(13)

In addition, the following equalities are imposed between the pressure and the chemical potential at the boundaries of the FOPT:
p_{\mathrm{NUC}}(n_{\mathrm{PT}})=p_{\mathrm{QM}}(n_{\mathrm{tr}})=p_{\mathrm{PT}}
and
\mu_{\mathrm{NUC}}(n_{\mathrm{PT}})=\mu_{\mathrm{QM}}(n_{\mathrm{tr}})=\mu_{\mathrm{PT}}
. Note that
p_{\mathrm{PT}}
is a model parameter, while
\mu_{\mathrm{PT}}
depends on the nuclear symmetry energy
e_{\mathrm{sym}}
.

In QM, the linear EoS (
11
) implies that the sound speed is constant, so the linear EoS is also referred to as the constant sound speed (CSS) approach. Note, however, that the QM sound speed is not necessarily constant: in the approach we adopt, it is sufficient that the QM sound speed is approximately constant close to the phase transition, and more precisely for a density range going from
n_{\mathrm{tr}}
up to the maximal density probed by NS,
n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
, or in terms of pressure from
p_{\mathrm{PT}}
up to
p_{\mathrm{TOV}}
. Typical values for
n_{\mathrm{PT}}
and
n_{\mathrm{tr}}
are 2-3
n_{\mathrm{sat}}
. The precise value of
p_{\mathrm{TOV}}
depends on the considered EoS. Above the maximal pressure
p_{\mathrm{TOV}}
, variations of the sound speed from the constant approximation should not impact the properties of CS in the stable branch, which is where CSs are observed, nor the dynamical collapse to black holes when the unstable branch is explored, see discussion from Ref.
Ujevic et al. 2024
for instance.

In the present approach, the description of the FOPT and the quark phase requires only three parameters: the pressure
p_{\mathrm{PT}}
at the onset of the phase transition, the shift in energy density
\Delta\varepsilon_{\mathrm{PT}}
, reflecting the latent heat, and finally, the sound speed
c_{s,\mathrm{PT}}
in QM, which controls the stiffness of the quark EoS above the FOPT.

In QM, the energy density
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{QM}}
can be re-expressed from Eq. (
11
) as

\epsilon_{\mathrm{QM}}(p)=\epsilon_{\mathrm{QM}}^{*}+\frac{p}{\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}}\,,

(14)

with

\epsilon^{*}_{\mathrm{QM}}=\epsilon_{\mathrm{NUC},\mathrm{PT}}+\Delta\varepsilon_{\mathrm{PT}}-p_{\mathrm{PT}}/\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}\,,

(15)

where
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{NUC},\mathrm{PT}}=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{NUC}}(p_{\mathrm{PT}})
.

In the following, the QM thermodynamic functions are expressed in terms of the baryon density
n_{b}
, which is associated with the conservation of the baryon number, i.e.,
n_{b}=(n_{u}+n_{d}+n_{s})/3
for u-d-s QM. Note that the following relations are identical to the ones provided in the original Refs.
Zdunik, J. L. and Haensel,
P. 2013
;
Alford et al. 2013
;
Chamel, N. et al. 2013
.

Since the sound speed parameter
\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}\equiv dp_{\mathrm{QM}}/d\epsilon_{\mathrm{QM}}=(n_{b}/\mu_{\mathrm{QM}})dn_{b}/d\mu_{\mathrm{QM}}
, where the QM chemical potential
\mu_{\mathrm{QM}}\equiv d\epsilon_{\mathrm{QM}}/dn_{b}
and the baryon density
n_{b}\equiv dp_{\mathrm{QM}}/d\mu_{\mathrm{QM}}
, we obtain

\mu_{\mathrm{QM}}(n_{b})=\mu^{*}_{\mathrm{QM}}\left(\frac{n_{b}}{n^{*}_{\mathrm{QM}}}\right)^{\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}}\,,

(16)

where
\mu^{*}_{\mathrm{QM}}
and
n^{*}_{\mathrm{QM}}
are parameters for QM EoS.

Since
\epsilon_{\mathrm{QM}}=n_{b}\mu_{\mathrm{QM}}-p_{\mathrm{QM}}
(Euler relation), the pressure in QM can be expressed as,

p_{\mathrm{QM}}(n_{b})=\varepsilon^{*}_{\mathrm{QM}}\frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}}{1+\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}}\left[\left(\frac{n_{b}}{n^{*}_{\mathrm{QM}}}\right)^{1+\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}}-1\right],

(17)

and using Eq. (
14
), the energy density in QM reads,

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{QM}}(n_{b})=\varepsilon^{*}_{\mathrm{QM}}\left\{1+\frac{1}{1+\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}}\left[\left(\frac{n_{b}}{n^{*}_{\mathrm{QM}}}\right)^{1+\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}}-1\right]\right\}\,.

(18)

The QM parameters
\varepsilon^{*}_{\mathrm{QM}}
,
\mu^{*}_{\mathrm{QM}}
, and
n^{*}_{\mathrm{QM}}
are interpreted as the energy density, chemical potential, and the baryon density at zero QM pressure:
p_{\mathrm{QM}}=0
. Therefore, we obtain from the Euler relation, the following relation between these QM parameters,

n^{*}_{\mathrm{QM}}=\varepsilon^{*}_{\mathrm{QM}}/\mu^{*}_{\mathrm{QM}}\,,

(19)

showing that fixing two of them is enough to determine the QM EoS. To parameterize the FOPT, the parameter
\mu^{*}_{\mathrm{QM}}
can be employed instead of the latent heat
\Delta\varepsilon_{\mathrm{PT}}
since,

\Delta\varepsilon_{\mathrm{PT}}=\frac{p_{\mathrm{PT}}}{\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}}\left[1+\frac{1+\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}}{\left[\frac{\mu_{\mathrm{NUC},\mathrm{PT}}}{\mu^{*}_{\mathrm{QM}}}\right]^{\frac{1+\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}}{\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}}}-1}\right]-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{NUC},\mathrm{PT}}\,,

(20)

using Eqs. (
15
) and (
24
), see hereafter.
In Eq. (
20
),
\Delta\varepsilon_{\mathrm{PT}}
is expressed as a function of the FOPT parameters
p_{\mathrm{PT}}
and
\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}
, the QM parameter
\mu_{\mathrm{QM}}^{*}
, and the nuclear matter properties
\mu_{\mathrm{NUC},\mathrm{PT}}
and
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{NUC},\mathrm{PT}}
, which depend on the symmetry energy
e_{\mathrm{sym}}
.

In the following, the FOPT model is described in terms of the following three parameters:
p_{\mathrm{PT}}
,
\mu_{\mathrm{QM}}^{*}
, and
\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}
. These parameters have a clear interpretation. For a given nuclear EoS, the parameter
p_{\mathrm{PT}}
is related to the density onset
n_{\mathrm{PT}}
, the parameter
\mu^{*}_{\mathrm{QM}}
is related to the QM EoS (for two-flavor color-superconducting model,
\mu^{*}_{\mathrm{QM}}\in[950,1000]
MeV, and for color-flavor-locked superconducting model
\mu^{*}_{\mathrm{QM}}\in[1080,1150]
MeV
Agrawal 2010
), and finally, the parameter
\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}
reflects the quark interaction: without interaction, one expects
\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}=1/3
(conformal limit), and quark interaction is repulsive for
\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}>1/3
.

Let us now invert Eq. (
17
) to express the baryon density
n_{b}
as a function of the pressure
p
:

n_{b}(p)=n^{*}_{\mathrm{QM}}\left[1+\frac{1+\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}}{\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}}\frac{p}{\epsilon^{*}_{\mathrm{QM}}}\right]^{1/(1+\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}})}\,,

(21)

from where one can obtain the QM parameter,

n_{\mathrm{QM}}^{*}=\frac{n_{\mathrm{PT}}+\Delta n_{\mathrm{PT}}}{\left[1+\frac{1+\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}}{\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}}\frac{p_{\mathrm{PT}}}{\epsilon^{*}_{\mathrm{QM}}}\right]^{1/(1+\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}})}}\,,

(22)

where
n_{\mathrm{PT}}
is fixed by
p_{\mathrm{PT}}
for a given nuclear EoS, and
\Delta n_{\mathrm{PT}}
is defined from Eq. (
20
). Therefore, the QM parameter
n_{\mathrm{QM}}^{*}
is impacted by the symmetry energy
e_{\mathrm{sym}}
. Injecting Eq. (
21
) into Eq. (
16
), the QM chemical potential becomes:

\mu_{\mathrm{QM}}(p)=\mu^{*}_{\mathrm{QM}}\left[1+\frac{1+\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}}{\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}}\frac{p}{\varepsilon^{*}_{\mathrm{QM}}}\right]^{\frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}}{1+\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}}}\,,

(23)

recovering the expression for the chemical potential given in Ref.
Zdunik, J. L. and Haensel,
P. 2013
. Since
\mu_{\mathrm{PT}}\equiv\mu_{\mathrm{QM}}(p_{\mathrm{PT}})
, one obtains from Eq. (
23
), the following expression for the QM parameter
\varepsilon^{*}_{\mathrm{QM}}
:

\varepsilon^{*}_{\mathrm{QM}}=p_{\mathrm{PT}}\frac{1+\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}}{\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}}\left[\left(\frac{\mu_{\mathrm{PT}}}{\mu^{*}_{\mathrm{QM}}}\right)^{\frac{1+\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}}{\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}}}-1\right]^{-1}\,,

(24)

where
\varepsilon^{*}_{\mathrm{QM}}
is defined from the FOPT parameters
p_{\mathrm{PT}}
,
\mu^{*}_{\mathrm{PT}}
,
\alpha_{\mathrm{PT}}
and the nuclear matter property
\mu_{\mathrm{NUC},\mathrm{PT}}\equiv\mu_{\mathrm{PT}}
. We conclude that the three QM parameters
\epsilon^{*}_{\mathrm{QM}}
,
\mu^{*}_{\mathrm{QM}}
, and
n^{*}_{\mathrm{QM}}
are entirely controlled by the FOPT parameters and the nuclear EoS, in particular, the nuclear symmetry energy. Therefore, the nuclear symmetry energy has an important role in the determination of the QM EoS.

The NS global properties such as masses, radii, and tidal deformabilities are deduced from the nuclear EoS assuming general relativity (TOV and pulsation equation)

Oppenheimer and Volkoff 1939

Hinderer 2008

Tolman 1939
;
Oppenheimer and Volkoff 1939

\displaystyle\frac{dm(r)c^{2}}{dr}=4\pi r^{2}\epsilon(r),

\displaystyle\frac{dp(r)}{dr}=-\epsilon(r)\Bigg(1+\frac{p(r)}{\epsilon(r)}\Bigg)\frac{d\Phi(r)}{dr},

(25)

\displaystyle\frac{d\Phi(r)}{dr}=\frac{Gm(r)}{c^{2}r^{2}}\Bigg(1+\frac{4\pi p(r)r^{3}}{m(r)c^{2}}\Bigg)\Bigg(1-\frac{2Gm(r)}{rc^{2}}\Bigg)^{-1}\hskip-8.5359pt,

(26)

where

(27)

If

The tidal deformability

\Lambda_{i}=\frac{2k_{2,i}}{3C_{i}^{5}},

Before discussing the role of the nuclear symmetry energy on CSs, we first present the uncertainty quantification of the transition to QM and the implementation of nuclear and astrophysical constraints.

III
Uncertainty quantification and constraints on the quark matter phase

The quantification of the uncertainty of the parameters governing the transition to QM and the implementation of the nuclear and astrophysical constraints are performed within the Bayesian approach.

III.1
Bayesian approach

The Bayesian approach allows a statistical exploration of the uncertainties in the model parameters as well as the limitations in this exploration originating from a set of constraints.

The core of the Bayesian analysis lies in Bayes’ theorem, expressing the probability associated with a given model, represented here by its parameters
\{a_{i}\}
, to reproduce a set of data. The posterior probability distribution function (PDF)
P(\{a_{i}\}\mid\textrm{data})
is obtained as
Sivia and Skilling 2006
:

P(\{a_{i}\}\mid\textrm{data})\sim P(\textrm{data}\mid\{a_{i}\})\times P(\{a_{i}\}),

(33)

where

P(\textrm{data}\mid\{a_{i}\})
is the likelihood probability determined from the comparison between the model and the measurements, and
P(\{a_{i}\})
is the prior which represents the
a priori
knowledge on the model parameters.

The marginal one- and two-parameter probabilities are defined as
Sivia and Skilling 2006
:

\displaystyle P(a_{j}\mid\textrm{data})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigg\{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{N}\int da_{i}\Bigg\}P(\{a_{i}\}\mid\textrm{data})\,,

(34)

\displaystyle P(a_{j},a_{k}\mid\textrm{data})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigg\{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j,k\end{subarray}}^{N}\int da_{i}\Bigg\}P(\{a_{i}\}\mid\textrm{data})\,,

(35)

where
N
is the number of model parameters which are varied for the uncertainty quantification. These marginal probabilities also represent the posterior PDF for one parameter (
34
), and the correlation between two model parameters (
35
).

The likelihood probability defines the ability of the model to reproduce the data and contains limitations in the model parameter space due to the constraints. It is expressed as:

P(\textrm{data}\mid\{a_{i}\})=w_{\textrm{filter}}\times p_{\tilde{\Lambda}}\,,

(36)

where
w_{\textrm{filter}}(\{a_{i}\})
is a pass-band type filter reflecting the constraint limitations, and the probability
p_{\tilde{\Lambda}}
represents the ability of the model to describe the tidal deformability from the GW170817 event
Abbott et al. 2019
. These two terms can be expressed as the following constraints:

(C1)

Each viable EoS shall satisfy causality, stability, and positiveness of the symmetry energy, see Ref.
Margueron et al. 2018b
, and the associated
M_{\mathrm{TOV}}
should be larger than the largest observed mass
M_{\max}^{\mathrm{obs}}\approx 2M_{\odot}

Antoniadis et al. 2013
. These constraints shall be satisfied by all EoS up to the density corresponding to the maximum density of the stable branch, denoted
n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
.

(C2)

The probability
p_{\tilde{\Lambda}}
in Eq. (
36
) is defined in the following way:

p_{\tilde{\Lambda}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{M}p_{\tilde{\Lambda}}^{k}\,,

(37)

where the index
k
runs over a set of binary configurations
\{M_{1,k},\Lambda_{1,k},M_{2,k},\Lambda_{2,k}\}
for a given EoS (we consider six masses
M_{i}
) and respecting
0.7\leq q\leq 1
and
M_{\text{chirp}}=1.1975^{+0.0001}_{-0.0001}M_{\odot}
for GW170817. In other words, to be compatible with GW170817, the masses
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
should be distributed between 1.1 and 1.6
M_{\odot}
. Large masses
M_{1}>1.6M_{\odot}
CSs exist in nature (since
\sim 2M_{\odot}
CSs have been detected), but they are not constrained by GW170817. The internal probability
p_{\tilde{\Lambda}}^{k}
is obtained by comparing the model prediction and the measurement for GW170817.

Another choice, different from (C2), to calculate
p_{\tilde{\Lambda}}
could be to assign to
p_{\tilde{\Lambda}}
the maximum probability obtained for the effective tidal deformability
\tilde{\Lambda}
,
p_{\tilde{\Lambda}}=\max_{k}p_{\tilde{\Lambda}}^{k}
. In the present analysis, we do not explore this other choice to determine
p_{\tilde{\Lambda}}
. It would, however, be interesting to explore the role of the different prescriptions for
p_{\tilde{\Lambda}}
on the results.

There are three independent GW analyses of GW170817 providing different measurements for
\tilde{\Lambda}
: i) the tidal deformability from Ref.
Coughlin et al. 2019
includes
GW, Electromagnetic (EM), and Gamma Ray Burst (GRB) signal, and predict a single peak distribution located around
\tilde{\Lambda}_{\textrm{max}}\approx 600

Abbott et al. 2019

IV
Conclusions

In this study, we have explored nuclear and hybrid EoS, where the transition to QM is of first order and the QM EoS is linear. This general approach accurately approximates modern QM models. We have shown that the FOPT approach still depends on the nuclear EoS, in particular, on the nuclear symmetry energy. Two representative models have been considered, the Skyrme SLy5 models for the soft nuclear EoS and the PKDD relativistic Lagrangian for the stiff nuclear EoS.

The uncertainties quantification for the FOPT parameters and the nuclear physics and astrophysics constraints are implemented within the Bayesian statistical approach. All EoS respect causality, stability, and the observational evidence that stable CS TOV masses are above
2M_{\odot}
. In this way, a total of about
400\ 000
priors are considered.

We observe that the tension between models and data (NICER and GW) existing for nuclear models can be solved by considering hybrid EoSs built from the FOPT approach.

We found that the nuclear symmetry energy impacts hybrid EoS and the nature of CS in GW170817: both soft and stiff nuclear EoS can produce hybrid EoS, suggesting that GW170817 is a BHS, while only soft nuclear EoS predicts GW170817 to be a BNS or NSHS system. In other words, if the nuclear EoS is stiff, GW170817 can only be a BHS. In addition, the
\tilde{\Lambda}
measurement from GW170817 is best reproduced for HS built on hybrid stiff PKDD EoS.

Detailed analyses are performed in this study, and we show that the hybrid EoS based on stiff nuclear EoS requires softening, while the one based on soft nuclear EoS can be soft or stiff and still reproduce GW170817. We have compared globally our predictions to NICER measurements, but to simplify the discussion, we have not considered NICER measurements in our constraints.

Finally, we have shown that the correlation between
p_{\mathrm{PT}}
and
\mu^{*}_{\mathrm{QM}}
, as well as between
p_{\mathrm{PT}}
and
n_{\mathrm{PT}}
FOPT properties, is impacted by the nuclear symmetry energy.

We conclude on the important role played by the nuclear symmetry energy on the properties of HS. Note that it was already well documented for NS, but little was done for HS. In the future, it would be interesting to explore the same question with a larger set of nuclear EoS and analyze in more detail for role of the symmetry energy (characterized, for instance, by different values of the curvature of the symmetry energy
K_{\mathrm{sym}}
) on hybrid EoS.

In summary, this work underlines the role of the nuclear symmetry energy for HS. A precise determination of the symmetry energy, particularly its density dependence, is crucial for interpreting astrophysical observations and constraining the phase diagram of dense matter. We plan, for instance, to explore more widely the symmetry energy by varying, e.g., the curvature of the symmetry energy
K_{\mathrm{sym}}
. Future work will benefit from incorporating a broader set of nuclear EoS and considering pulsar timing measurements (NICER, for instance) in the Bayesian constraints to further refine our understanding of the hadron-quark phase transition in neutron stars.

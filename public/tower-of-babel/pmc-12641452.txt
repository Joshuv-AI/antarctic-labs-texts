The Japanese Lead Extraction Registry (J‐LEX): Annual Report in 2024
Authors: Masahiko Goya, Morio Shoda, Kengo Kusano, Haruka Matsuura, Koshiro Kanaoka, Nobuhiro Nishii, Katsuhiko Imai, Yoji Okamoto, Yuji Narita, Katsuhide Hayashi, Hitoshi Minamiguchi, Masahiko Takagi, Yoshihiro Miyamoto, Mitsuru Wada, Akihiko Nogami, Wataru Shimizu, Hiroshi Tada, J‐LEX Registry Investigators
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe Japanese Heart Rhythm Society established a nationwide, mandatory, multi‐center, prospective, observational registry of transvenous lead extraction (TLE) named the Japanese lead extraction registry (J‐LEX) in 2018. As of the end of 2024, a cumulative total of approximately 5400 cases has been registered across 140 participating centers since 2018. The registry includes all consecutive transvenous extractions, excluding surgical‐only cases. The indication of TLE was an infection in 54.5%, and the guideline classification was class I in 59.8% and class IIa in 21.8%. As for non‐infectious indications, TLE is performed for abandoned leads in case of lead failure or device upgrade, and for lead‐related trouble such as pain, vessel stenosis or occlusion, too many leads, tricuspid valve regurgitation, and difficulty with radiation therapy. In 2024, the TLE procedure was attempted on patients with a median age of 71.3 years, female in 32.7%, in the hybrid operating room in 73.3%, and in a standard OR with a C‐arm fluoroscope in 11.5%. The average implantation duration of the target leads was 8.5 years. Complete removal was achieved in 96.7% of the target leads, and clinical success in 97.3% of the patients. Perioperative complications were observed in 4.8% of the patients. Three patients died during a TLE operation, and 11 patients died in‐hospital (cardiac death in three patients and non‐cardiac death in eight patients).

The Japanese Heart Rhythm Society established a nationwide, mandatory, multi‐center, prospective, observational registry of transvenous lead extraction (TLE) named the Japanese lead extraction registry (J‐LEX) in 2018. This registration has been conducted according to the methods of the previously published design paper [1], under the approval from the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of the National Cardiovascular Center (M29‐146, February 23, 2018), along with the IRBs of all participating hospitals. We have published the annual report of J‐LEX for 2019 and 2020 [2, 3]. From 2018 through the end of 2024, the number of participating centers had increased to 140, and a cumulative total of approximately 5400 cases had been registered. The TLE procedure was attempted to 1910 leads among 1038 patients in 2024 (Figure 1). TLE procedure was attempted on the patients from January to December in 2024, with a median age of 71.3 years old, female in 32.7%, and body mass index (BMI) smaller than previous reports from western countries [4, 5] (Table 1, Figure 2). The TLE procedure was performed in the Hybrid Operating Room (HBOR) in 73.3%, in the standard operating room with a C‐arm fluoroscope in 11.5%, and in the Catheterization Laboratory (Cath Lab) in 15.2% (Figure 3). The method of anesthesia was mainly general anesthesia in 83.9% (Figure 3). The indication of TLE was an infection in 54.5% and the guideline classification [6] was class I in 59.8%, class IIa in 21.8%, and class‐IIb in 18.3% (Figure 3). The average number of extracted leads per patient was 1.8 (Figure 3). The type of extracted leads were pacemaker lead in 78.4%, implantable cardioverter‐defibrillator lead in 15.2%, and cardiac resynchronization left ventricular lead in 5.9% (Table 1). The average implantation duration of target leads was 8.5 ± 6.9 years. Multiple methods of lead extraction were used in many cases: locking devices were used in 76.9% of leads, laser sheaths were used for 61.7% of leads, rotational mechanical sheaths for 43.8% of leads, and mechanical sheaths for 26.7% of leads. Also, snaring techniques were used for 26.3% of the leads (Table 1). Complete removal, defined as the extraction of all lead components, was achieved in 96.7% of the target leads. Clinical success—defined as complete or partial removal (≤ 4 cm of residual lead material) without compromising the clinical objective—was obtained in 97.3% of patients (Table 1). Perioperative complications were observed in 4.8% of the patients (Table 1). Open surgical procedures were performed for lead remnants, infected vegetations, or bleeding complications for 36 leads (Table 1). Three patients died during a TLE operation and the other 11 patients died in‐hospital (cardiac death in three patients and non‐cardiac in eight patients) (Table 1). These success and complication rates are broadly comparable to those reported in the ELECTRa or LExICon registry [4, 5].

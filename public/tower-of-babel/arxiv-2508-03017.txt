SA-3DGS: A Self-Adaptive Compression Method for 3D Gaussian Splatting
Liheng Zhang, Weihao Yu, Zubo Lu, Haozhi Gu, Jin Huang

Abstract
Recent advancements in 3D Gaussian Splatting have enhanced efficient and high-quality novel view synthesis. However, representing scenes requires a large number of Gaussian points, leading to high storage demands and limiting practical deployment. The latest methods facilitate the compression of Gaussian models but struggle to identify truly insignificant Gaussian points in the scene, leading to a decline in subsequent Gaussian pruning, compression quality, and rendering performance. To address this issue, we propose SA-3DGS, a method that significantly reduces storage costs while maintaining rendering quality. SA-3DGS learns an importance score to automatically identify the least significant Gaussians in scene reconstruction, thereby enabling effective pruning and redundancy reduction. Next, the importance-aware clustering module compresses Gaussians attributes more accurately into the codebook, improving the codebook’s expressive capability while reducing model size. Finally, the codebook repair module leverages contextual scene information to repair the codebook, thereby recovering the original Gaussian point attributes and mitigating the degradation in rendering quality caused by information loss. Experimental results on several benchmark datasets show that our method achieves up to 66× compression while maintaining or even improving rendering quality. The proposed Gaussian pruning approach is not only adaptable to but also improves other pruning-based methods (e.g., LightGaussian), showcasing excellent performance and strong generalization ability.

Introduction

We propose SA-3DGS, which significantly reduces the storage overhead of 3DGS by an order of magnitude while maintaining excellent rendering quality.

Novel View Synthesis (NVS) aims to generate new viewpoints or views from a set of input images, thereby reconstructing a 3D representation of the scene. It is widely used in fields such as simultaneous localization and mapping
(
Rosinol, Leonard, and Carlone 2022
)
, medical image reconstruction
(
Corona-Figueroa et al. 2022
)
, and autonomous driving
(
Turki et al. 2023
;
Wu et al. 2023
)
. Despite the existence of technologies such as Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF)
(
Mildenhall et al. 2020
)
in NVS, the slow training and rendering speeds, along with excessive memory consumption, severely limit its practical use in real-world scenarios. To address these challenges, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)
(
Kerbl et al. 2023
)
offers a more efficient alternative.
3DGS represents scenes using Gaussian points in 3D space, enabling efficient rendering through rasterization, which significantly improves rendering speed and reduces memory consumption compared to NeRF, while maintaining high-quality output. However, the requirement for a large number of Gaussian points to represent scenes leads to increased computational and storage overhead, limiting the practical applicability of the method in resource-constrained environments.

Recent advancements in 3DGS have focused on improving rendering quality, with methods such as Mip-Splatting
(
Yu et al. 2024
)
and GOF
(
Yu, Sattler, and Geiger 2024
)
achieving notable improvements. However, these advances are still constrained by high memory consumption, limiting practical deployment. To address this, researchers have explored various compression techniques, such as vector quantization and codebook-based compression
(
Papantonakis et al. 2024
;
Lee et al. 2024
;
Navaneet et al. 2024
)
, to reduce storage overhead while retaining scene details. A common strategy to reduce storage costs is pruning Gaussian points such as LightGaussian
(
Fan et al. 2024
)
, Mini-Splatting
(
Fang and Wang 2024
)
, and RadSplat
(
Niemeyer et al. 2025
)
. These methods calculate an importance score for each Gaussian point and prune those below a predefined threshold. However, as shown in Fig.
1
, these methods can lead to rendering results with missing regions, severely impacting visual quality. This issue arises from their reliance on heuristic formulas for calculating importance scores, which often fail to generalize across different scenes. For example, LightGaussian defines a positive correlation between opacity and importance score, which can lead to incorrect pruning of large-volume but low-opacity Gaussian points in the background. In addition, these methods struggle to accurately identify all insignificant points, limiting the compression potential of the model. While pruning reduces the number of Gaussian points, the storage overhead remains substantial due to the large number of attributes each Gaussian point contains. Some methods address this with codebook-based compression by clustering Gaussians, but they either assign equal weights to all Gaussians when computing cluster centers
(
Papantonakis et al. 2024
;
Lee et al. 2024
;
Navaneet et al. 2024
)
, which fails to emphasize the influence of high-importance points, or restrict clustering to low-importance Gaussians
(
Fan et al. 2024
)
, limiting further compression potential. Moreover, although codebook compression reduces storage overhead, it introduces a challenge in rendering quality. The irreversible nature of compression leads to information loss, compromising the precise representation of critical attributes such as position and color. In summary, these methods fail to fully exploit the model’s compression potential, and rendering quality remains suboptimal.

To address these issues, we propose a self-adaptive 3D Gaussian Splatting (SA-3DGS) method. First, we analyze the limitations of pruning methods that rely on empirical formulas in various reconstruction scenarios and propose an adaptive pruning module that automatically learns the importance of Gaussian points based on each scene. Notably, this pruning module not only enables the model to achieve higher compression rates but is also compatible with pruning modules from previous works, improving their rendering quality. We also introduce a novel importance-aware clustering module that stores all Gaussian points and leverages importance scores to enhance the codebook’s expressive accuracy, instead of separately storing only high-importance Gaussian points. Compared with other codebook compression methods, this approach offers higher compression rates and better rendering quality. Finally, in contrast to existing methods that directly use the codebook to replace 3DGS attributes for image rendering, we introduce a codebook repair module that leverages contextual information to repair the compressed Gaussian point attributes. This restoration process helps recover important details lost during codebook compression, ensuring that the final output maintains visual accuracy. Through these techniques, SA-3DGS is able to significantly reduce the size of the model while maintaining rendering quality. Our main contributions are as follows.

•

We introduce a self-adaptive Gaussians pruning module that dynamically assigns importance scores to Gaussian points, enabling higher-quality pruning.

•

We propose an importance-aware clustering module that ensures key information is preserved during compression while simultaneously enhancing the model’s compression rate.

•

To the best of our knowledge, we are the first to introduce a codebook repair mechanism in the context of 3DGS compression. We propose a residual MLP repair method to compensate for information loss during compression.

Through experiments, we demonstrate that our method significantly reduces the storage requirements (e.g., from 664MB to 10MB) while maintaining or even improving rendering quality (e.g., PSNR increases by 0.54dB on DeepBlending). Furthermore, our Gaussians pruning module has good generalization ability and can enhance the performance of various 3DGS compression methods. In summary, our research provides an efficient and scalable compression and rendering solution for 3D scene reconstruction.

3
Method

Overview.
Conventional pruning methods rely on predefined importance scoring functions to compute the importance of Gaussian points, which often struggle to generalize across different reconstruction scenes. To address this, we propose a self-adaptive module that automatically learns the importance of Gaussian points for each scene. To preserve critical outliers while reducing storage cost, we propose an importance-aware clustering method that uses learned importance scores to derive a more representative codebook. To mitigate information loss from quantization, we introduce a lightweight residual prediction module to repair lost details. The framework of our method is illustrated in Fig.
2
.

Self-Adaptive Gaussians Pruning

Pruning was first explored in voxel-based neural radiance fields to remove empty or low-information regions for efficiency. This approach is naturally extended to Gaussian representations, where pruning is guided by importance scores assigned to each Gaussian point. However, methods that rely on fixed formulas to calculate importance scores have clear limitations. They rely on empirical heuristics and struggle to adapt to different scenes, where color, geometry, or opacity may vary in importance. These fixed criteria can incorrectly prune Gaussian points that are important from certain viewpoints.

To overcome these limitations, we propose a self-adaptive Gaussian pruning method that automatically estimates the significance of each Gaussian point based on scene-specific content. Specifically, each Gaussian point
G_{i}
is assigned a learnable importance score
\alpha_{i}\in(0,1)
, which is normalized via a sigmoid activation during training. This score is optimized jointly with the photometric loss, allowing the network to infer the contribution of each point to the final image quality. Points with lower importance scores are considered less informative and can be safely removed. During pruning, we define a threshold
\tau_{sc}
and remove all Gaussian points whose importance scores fall below this threshold:

G^{\prime}=\{G_{i}\in G\mid\alpha_{i}\geq\tau_{sc}\},

(1)

where
G
is the original set of Gaussian points and
G^{\prime}
is the pruned subset. To further enhance sparsity, we introduce a score regularization term to penalize high importance values:

\mathcal{L}_{sc}=\lambda_{sc}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}

(2)

where
\lambda_{sc}
is a regularization coefficient and
N
is the total number of Gaussian points. To leverage the original 3DGS framework’s opacity-based pruning mechanism, we apply an opacity regularization term to encourage sparsity:

\mathcal{L}_{op}=\lambda_{op}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sigma_{i}

(3)

where
\sigma_{i}
is the opacity of the Gaussian point and
\lambda_{op}
controls the strength of this penalty.
During training, we perform pruning at each predefined pruning iteration. Specifically, a Gaussian point is removed if its importance score
\alpha_{i}
falls below a score threshold
\tau_{sc}
, or if its opacity
\sigma_{i}
is below an opacity threshold
\tau_{op}
. Since the importance scores are used only during training, they are discarded afterward, introducing no additional storage overhead in the final model.

Importance-Aware Clustering

To reduce storage without compromising rendering quality, we selectively apply vector quantization to the SH features of Gaussian points. Due to their high dimensionality, SH coefficients dominate the memory footprint of 3DGS and are therefore an ideal target for compression. In contrast, geometric attributes such as position, scale, size, and orientation are critical to spatial fidelity and are retained in full precision (float32) to prevent visual artifacts caused by quantization errors.

To further improve the effectiveness of SH compression, we introduce an importance-aware clustering strategy. Conventional clustering methods treat all Gaussians equally when computing cluster centers, which can cause high-impact outliers to be poorly represented, degrading the final rendering quality. To address this, we incorporate learned importance scores into the clustering process. Specifically, we define an importance-weighted distance function to guide k-means clustering:

D_{ij}=\left\|{f}^{\text{ori}}_{i}-c_{j}\right\|_{2}^{2}\cdot\alpha_{i}^{2},

(4)

where
{f}^{\text{ori}}_{i}
denotes the original SH feature vector of the
i
-th Gaussian and
c_{j}
is the
j
-th cluster center. This weighting ensures that more important Gaussians exert stronger influence on cluster formation, preserving critical features while enabling efficient compression across the point cloud.

To validate this strategy, we analyze the distribution of Gaussian points during clustering. As shown in Fig.
3
, we apply PCA to the SH features and observe that outliers often lie near spatial boundaries and tend to maintain high importance throughout training. While many normal points are gradually assigned low importance scores as training progresses, outliers consistently retain high scores. This indicates that outliers capture critical visual information that cannot be easily substituted by more common points, underscoring their essential role in accurate scene representation.

Fig.
4
shows renderings using only high-importance Gaussians. The model provides a coarse reconstruction of complex textures such as the train surface, whereas low-frequency regions like the sky require only a few Gaussians. This demonstrates that the model effectively distinguishes between visually complex and structurally simple regions. By contrast, skipping vector quantization would require separately storing high-importance Gaussians for the sky while still using the codebook for the rest, leading to inefficiencies in storage and compression.

Codebook Repair

The codebook-based 3DGS compression introduces irreversible information loss when Gaussian attributes are quantized into discrete codebook entries, potentially leading to degraded rendering quality. Inspired by restoration techniques in image processing, where lightweight neural networks are used to recover details in tasks such as denoising and super-resolution, we adopt a residual correction strategy to compensate for this loss. To restore the lost high-frequency details, we design a shallow MLP that predicts residuals over compressed SH features. Specifically, we construct the input vector
z_{i}
by concatenating the compressed SH features
\hat{f}_{i}
, the 3D center position of the Gaussian
p_{i}
, and the view-dependent vector
v_{i}
, which consists of the unit direction vector
d_{i}
from the camera center to
p_{i}
, and the corresponding Euclidean distance
r_{i}
:

z_{i}=[\hat{f}_{i};\ p_{i};\ v_{i}]

(5)

The MLP takes
z_{i}
as input and predicts a residual vector
\Delta f_{i}
, which is added to the compressed feature to obtain the corrected representation:

f_{i}=\hat{f}_{i}+\Delta f_{i}

(6)

To guide this correction, we apply a photometric loss on the rendered images and introduce an auxiliary loss
\mathcal{L}_{\text{re}}
to encourage the corrected feature
f_{i}
to approximate the original uncompressed SH coefficients
f_{i}^{\text{ori}}
:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{re}}=\lambda_{\text{re}}\cdot\left\|f_{i}-f_{i}^{\text{ori}}\right\|_{1}

(7)

where
\lambda_{\text{re}}
controls the strength of this residual supervision.

Loss Function Design

We jointly optimize the learnable parameters and the residual correction MLP using a combination of loss terms. The total loss is defined as:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{RGB}}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{L1}}+\lambda_{\text{SSIM}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{SSIM}}+\mathcal{L}_{\text{LPIPS}}

(8)

\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{RGB}}+\mathcal{L}_{\text{sc}}+\mathcal{L}_{\text{op}}+\mathcal{L}_{\text{re}}

(9)

Here,
\mathcal{L}_{\text{L1}}
denotes the per-pixel L1 loss between the rendered and reference images,
\mathcal{L}_{\text{SSIM}}
encourages structural similarity
(
Wang et al. 2004
)
, and
\mathcal{L}_{\text{LPIPS}}
promotes perceptual fidelity via learned patch-wise feature distances
(
Zhang et al. 2018
)
.

Experiments

Baseline and Implementation

Our method is built on top of the 3DGS
(
Kerbl et al. 2023
)
framework, which leverages PyTorch and a CUDA for efficient differentiable Gaussian rasterization. All experiments were run on a single NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU using the original training hyperparameters without modification. We initiate importance score learning at iteration 15K, apply pruning every 1K iterations between 15K and 20K, and apply importance-aware compression from iteration 20K to 25K. Inspired by CompGS
(
Navaneet et al. 2024
)
, we reduce the frequency of cluster assignments by updating them every 100 iterations to mitigate training overhead. We apply vector quantization only to the SH features, with separate codebooks (size 4096) for the DC component and the higher-order component. Residual correction is performed via two lightweight MLPs starting at iteration 20K and continuing until 30K: one for the DC component and another for the higher-order component. Both MLPs consist of two layers with ReLU activation, with hidden dimensions of 64 and 148. The loss of SH repair is weighted by
\lambda_{\text{re}}=1\times 10^{-2}
. Regularization based on importance and opacity is applied between iterations 15K and 20K using weights
\lambda_{\text{sc}}=1.5\times 10^{-8}
and
\lambda_{\text{op}}=1\times 10^{-7}
, respectively. Additionally, the loss of LPIPS is weighted by
\lambda_{\text{LPIPS}}=1\times 10^{-2}
applied from iterations 20K to 25K.

3DGS achieves competitive 3D scene reconstruction through a large number of Gaussian points, but its high storage overhead limits its practical use. Additionally, while other methods reduce storage overhead, they still have untapped potential in terms of compression ratio and reconstruction quality. In contrast, our method significantly reduces storage requirements while maintaining rendering quality comparable to or even higher than 3DGS. Our high compression ratio is largely attributed to the self-adaptive pruning module and the importance-aware codebook, which effectively remove redundant Gaussian points while replacing Gaussian point attributes with a high-quality codebook, thereby enhancing compression efficiency. The high reconstruction quality is a result of the self-adaptive pruning module’s accurate identification of redundant Gaussian points, the codebook’s high capacity to represent Gaussian point attributes, and the codebook repair module that restores the original Gaussian point attributes. Compared to other methods, our approach achieves superior compression efficiency while maintaining excellent visual quality. Specifically, the compression ratio ranges from 36x to 66x, highlighting the greater compression potential of our method.

Conclusion

We introduced SA-3DGS, which enables scene rendering under resource constraints through self-adaptive pruning, importance-aware clustering, and codebook repair modules. Our self-adaptive pruning and importance-aware clustering effectively identify and prune the least important Gaussian distributions generated during densification, clustering the remaining Gaussian points into the most representative codebook. The codebook repair module effectively restores the codebook to the original Gaussian point attributes of the scene. The experimental results demonstrate that our method reduces 3DGS storage cost by up to 66x, while maintaining high-quality rendering, providing an enhanced solution for other importance-based 3DGS compression methods.

NDUFB11 and NDUFS3 regulate arterial atherosclerosis and venous thrombosis: Potential markers of atherosclerosis and venous thrombosis
Authors: Yan-Hong Ma, Yin Yang, Jing-Hui Li, Bo-Chen Yao, Qing-Liang Chen, Lian-Qun Wang, Zhi-Gang Guo, Su-Zhi Guo
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Atherosclerosis is a chronic disease that thickens the blood vessel walls and narrows the lumen. Venous thrombosis is a blood clot that forms in the body’s deep veins or pulmonary arteries. However, the relationship between NDUFB11 and NDUFS3 and atherosclerosis and venous thrombosis is unclear. We employed data files that combined atherosclerosis and chronic stress groups. Subsequently, we conducted differential gene expression analysis (DEGs) and performed weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA). We constructed and analyzed a protein-protein interaction (PPI) network. Further analyses included functional enrichment analysis, gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA), gene expression heatmaps, immune infiltration analysis, and mRNA analysis. By comparing our findings with the Comparative Toxicogenomics Database (CTD), we identified the most relevant diseases associated with the core genes. Additionally, we utilized TargetScan to screen for miRNAs regulating the central DEGs. To validate our results, we conducted Western Blot experiments at the cellular level. A total of 1747 DEGs were co-identified. According to the Gene Ontology (GO) analysis of differentially expressed genes, they were primarily enriched in mitochondrial gene expression, mitochondrial envelope, organelle membrane, and mitochondrial inner membrane categories. Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analysis revealed that the target cells were mainly enriched in metabolic pathways, ribosomes, and histidine metabolism. The intersection of enriched terms from both GO and KEGG analyses showed significant enrichment in mitochondrial gene expression, mitochondrial envelope, organelle inner membrane, ribosomal structural constituents, histidine metabolism, and oxidative phosphorylation. Eight core genes were identified, including NDUFS5, UQCRQ, COX6C, COX7B, ATP5ME, NDUFS3, NDUFA3, and NDUFB11. The gene expression heatmap demonstrated that core genes (NDUFB11 and NDUFS3) were downregulated in atherosclerosis with venous thrombosis samples and upregulated in normal samples. CTD analysis revealed that the core genes NDUFB11 and NDUFS3 were associated with pain, arterial diseases, atherosclerosis, arteritis, venous thrombosis formation, and venous thromboembolism. We added Western Blot basic cell experiment for verification. NDUFB11 and NDUFS3 are downregulated in atherosclerosis and venous thrombosis, associated with poorer prognosis, and may serve as potential biomarkers for both diseases.

1. Introduction

Atherosclerosis is one of the leading causes of cardiovascular diseases worldwide, affecting a wide population.[1] Major risk factors for this disease include high cholesterol, hypertension, smoking, obesity, and lack of exercise. Its hallmark is the accumulation of lipid deposits in the arterial intima, gradually forming plaques known as atheromatous plaques.[2,3] These plaques can lead to thickening and hardening of the arterial walls, ultimately resulting in narrowing of blood vessels and luminal obstruction. Atherosclerosis typically progresses without noticeable symptoms, a condition referred to as asymptomatic. However, when these plaques impede blood supply, symptoms such as angina (chest pain), myocardial infarction (heart attack), stroke, and peripheral artery disease may occur.[4] Pathological features include plaque formation, thickening of the vessel wall, inflammatory responses, fibrosis, and calcification.[5] Atheromatous plaques are composed of cholesterol, collagen, inflammatory cells, and other cell types. Atherosclerosis is a major contributor to cardiovascular diseases and can lead to severe complications like heart disease, stroke, peripheral artery disease, and even life-threatening events.[6,7]

Venous thrombosis refers to the formation of blood clots within the deep venous system of the body, typically located in the lower limbs. It can be further classified into Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT) and Pulmonary Embolism (PE).[8,9] DVT occurs when a clot forms within the deep veins, while PE is a complication that arises when a DVT clot dislodges and travels through the bloodstream to reach the pulmonary arteries. Venous thrombosis is quite common, especially in older individuals, post-surgery patients, those who are bedridden for extended periods, pregnant individuals, those using contraceptive pills, or individuals with a genetic predisposition.[10] Epidemiological data indicates a yearly increase in the incidence of venous thrombosis. Excessive activity of coagulation factors in the blood leads to the clotting of blood, typically occurring within veins. It commonly occurs in the deep veins of the lower limbs but can also occur in other locations.[11,12] Venous thrombosis may not manifest obvious symptoms, but some common signs and indications include swelling, pain, redness, and localized temperature elevation. If a clot dislodges and triggers a pulmonary embolism, patients may experience shortness of breath, chest pain, and coughing. As these clots gradually enlarge within the veins due to the aggregation of platelets and fibrin, they can lead to vascular narrowing or blockage, and severe cases can be life-threatening.[13]

The exact causes of atherosclerosis and venous thrombosis are not clear, but factors such as genetic factors, chromosomal abnormalities, and gene fusion may be related to their development. Therefore, in-depth research into their molecular mechanisms is crucial for a better understanding of their etiology and prevention.

NDUFB11 is a protein and a subunit of mitochondrial respiratory complex I (CI), also known as NADH dehydrogenase (ubiquinone) subunit B11. It plays a crucial role in mitochondrial energy production, particularly in the electron transfer process.[14,15] NDUFS3 is a subunit of mitochondrial respiratory CI as well, also referred to as NADH dehydrogenase (ubiquinone) subunit S3. The mitochondrial respiratory chain (MRC) is a group of protein complexes within cells involved in converting energy from food into adenosine triphosphate (ATP) that cells can use. NDUFS3 is a part of this complex and plays a critical role in mitochondrial energy production.[16,17] Both of these genes encode subunits of mitochondrial respiratory CI, participating in energy metabolism and cellular respiration chain functions, but the specific mechanisms are not yet fully understood.

Therefore, we aim to employ bioinformatics to uncover the core genes involved in atherosclerosis and venous thrombosis compared to normal tissues. Subsequently, we will conduct enrichment analysis and pathway analysis. We will validate the significance of NDUFB11 and NDUFS3 in atherosclerosis and venous thrombosis using publicly available datasets and confirm their roles through in vitro experiments with endothelial cells.

2. Method

2.1. Venous thromboembolism and atherosclerosis datasets

In this study, the datasets for Venous Thromboembolism (VTE) - GSE48000 and Atherosclerosis - GSE57691 were downloaded from the GEO database (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). GSE48000 consists of 40 VTE samples and 25 normal samples, while GSE57691 consists of 9 atherosclerosis samples and 10 normal samples. These datasets were used to identify Differentially Expressed Genes (DEGs) associated with VTE and Atherosclerosis.

2.2. Selection of differentially expressed genes (DEGs)

The R package “limma” was utilized for probe summarization and background correction of the GSE48000 and GSE57691 matrices. The Benjamini-Hochberg method was employed to adjust the raw p-values. The fold change was calculated using False Discovery Rate (FDR). The cutoff criteria for DEGs were set at P < .05 and fold change > 1.3. A volcano plot was created for visualization.

2.3. Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA)

Firstly, the Median Absolute Deviation of each gene was calculated based on the gene expression profiles. The lower 50% of genes with the smallest median absolute deviation values were removed. The “goodSamplesGenes” function from the R package WGCNA was used to remove outlier genes and samples. WGCNA was then applied to construct a scale-free co-expression network. Pearson correlation matrices were computed for all gene pairs, and the power function a_mn = |c_mn|^β (c_mn = Pearson correlation between gene m and gene n, a_mn = adjacency between gene m and gene n) was used to build the weighted adjacency matrix. β is a soft-thresholding parameter that emphasizes strong correlations and weakens weak correlations. After selecting a power of 9, the adjacency matrix was transformed into a Topological Overlap Matrix. Modules of highly correlated genes were identified using hierarchical clustering, with a minimum module size of 30. A sensitivity of 3 was set. To further analyze the modules, module eigengenes were correlated with phenotypes, and a cutoff value of 0.25 for module dissimilarity (1-topological overlap matrix) was used to merge modules. The gray module was considered a module of unassigned genes.

2.4. Protein-protein interaction (PPI) network construction and analysis

The PPI network for DEGs was constructed using the STRING database and analyzed using Cytoscape software. Specifically, the DEG list was input into the STRING database to construct a PPI network with a confidence score > 0.4. Cytoscape software was then used for visualization and identification of core genes within the PPI network. The MCODE algorithm was employed to find the most relevant modules within the network. Additionally, 4 algorithms (maximum clique centrality, maximum neighborhood component, density of maximum neighborhood component, edge percolated component) were used to calculate the top genes’ relevance and the intersection was taken. The resulting core gene list was visualized and exported.

2.5. Functional enrichment analysis

Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analyses were performed to assess the functional roles and biological pathways associated with the DEGs. The latest KEGG Pathway gene annotations were obtained from the KEGG REST API (https://www.kegg.jp/kegg/rest/keggapi.html) and used as the background. The DEGs were mapped to this background, and enrichment analysis was conducted using the R package clusterProfiler (version 3.14.3) to obtain enriched gene sets. The GO annotations for genes were obtained from the org.Hs.e.g..db package (version 3.1.0). A minimum gene set size of 5 and a maximum gene set size of 5000, with a P value cutoff of <.05 and an FDR of < 0.25, were considered statistically significant.

Additionally, Metascape was used for comprehensive gene list annotation and analysis, with visualization of the results.

2.6. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA)

For Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA), the samples were divided into 2 groups, disease and normal, based on the phenotypes of VTE and Atherosclerosis. The c2.cp.kegg.v7.4.symbols.gmt gene set was downloaded from the Molecular Signatures Database. GSEA was conducted to identify relevant pathways and molecular mechanisms based on gene expression profiles and phenotype grouping. A minimum gene set size of 5, a maximum gene set size of 5000, 1000 permutations, and P value < .05 and FDR < 0.25 were used as the criteria for statistical significance. GO and KEGG analyses for the entire genome were also performed.

2.7. Gene expression heatmaps

Heatmaps of core gene expression in GSE48000 and GSE57691 samples were created using the R package heatmap. The expression differences of core genes in VTE and normal samples, as well as Atherosclerosis and normal samples, were visualized.

2.8. Immune infiltration analysis

CIBERSORT, a widely used method for calculating immune cell infiltration, was applied to the GSE48000 and GSE57691 matrices. LM22 gene files were used to define 22 immune cell subtypes. The CIBERSORT package was used to estimate immune cell abundances in the gene expression matrix. Samples with a confidence score (P value) < .05 were selected as having sufficient confidence.

2.9. CTD analysis

The Comparative Toxicogenomics Database (CTD) integrates data on interactions between chemicals, genes, phenotypes, and diseases, providing valuable insights into the relationships between genes and diseases. Core gene lists were input into the CTD website to find diseases most related to the core genes. Radar plots were created using Excel to visualize the expression differences for each gene.

2.10. Western blotting (WB)

Western blotting, also known as immunoblotting, is a method to detect the expression of a certain protein in complex samples according to the specific binding of antigens and antibodies, and can qualitatively and semiquantitatively analyze proteins. Total protein was extracted and the protein content was determined. After SDS-PAGE electrophoresis and membrane transfer, the protein samples were blocked with 5% skim milk for 1 hour at room temperature, shaken with Tris Buffered Saline Tween at high speed on a shaker, washed for 5 minutes, and repeated 3 times. The primary antibody was added and incubated overnight at 4°C, followed by TBST shaking 3 times (5 minutes each time) and TBST shaking 3 times (5 minutes each time). The results were analyzed after chemiluminescence development.

4. Discussion

The seriousness of atherosclerosis is profound, as it stands as a major cause of numerous cardiovascular diseases, posing a significant threat to health and life. Atherosclerosis can result in the narrowing or blockage of coronary arteries, which can trigger a heart attack, known as myocardial infarction.[6] Plaque formation and arterial narrowing can also impact cerebral blood supply, increasing the risk of stroke.[18] Atherosclerosis can affect arteries in other parts of the body, such as the legs, leading to peripheral artery disease, which may result in intermittent claudication, gangrene, and, in severe cases, amputation. Atherosclerosis can also give rise to other complications, including arterial aneurysms (dilation of blood vessel walls), kidney disease, and retinal damage.[19] The molecular mechanisms underlying this process are intricate.[20]

One of the most dangerous complications of venous thrombosis is pulmonary embolism, where a blood clot dislodges from the deep venous system and migrates to the pulmonary artery, obstructing blood flow to the lungs.[21] This can lead to symptoms such as shortness of breath, chest pain, coughing, hemoptysis (coughing up blood), and even life-threatening situations. Untreated venous thrombosis can also result in chronic venous conditions, such as chronic venous insufficiency or chronic venous ulcers.[22] These conditions can lead to symptoms like leg swelling, pain, ulcers, and skin changes. The molecular mechanisms of venous thrombosis involve Virchow triad, which includes abnormalities in blood composition (such as hypercoagulability), reduced blood flow or stasis (such as prolonged immobility), and endothelial injury (such as damage to the inner wall of veins).[23] It also involves factors related to platelets, coagulation factors, fibrin, and the dissolution mechanisms.[11]

Both of these diseases are related to the vascular system, but their pathogenic mechanisms and associated factors are different. Atherosclerosis is closely linked to lipid deposition and inflammatory responses within the arterial walls, while venous thrombosis is typically associated with the imbalances in Virchow triad and the coagulation-anticoagulation mechanisms. The primary findings of this study indicate that NDUFB11 and NDUFS3 are downregulated in atherosclerosis and venous thrombosis, and lower expression of NDUFB11 and NDUFS3 is associated with worse prognosis, possibly playing a role in one of the processes mentioned above.

Ndufb11 (NADH Dehydrogenase [Ubiquinone] 1 Beta Subcomplex) is a protein subunit found within the mitochondria, and it belongs to the NADH dehydrogenase complex. NADH dehydrogenase is a crucial enzyme complex in the MRC responsible for electron transfer and proton pumping, ultimately generating chemical energy (ATP) within the cell. Ndufb11 plays a significant role within CI, contributing to the production of energy in the mitochondria[24] the survival and normal function of cells.[25,26] Particularly, diseases related to mitochondrial dysfunction may be associated with mutations or functional abnormalities in Ndufb11. These diseases may include mitochondrial disorders, neuromuscular diseases, among others, and research into these conditions helps to better understand the role of Ndufb11 in mitochondrial function.[14,27]

Our previous study[11] revealed that NDUFB11 and NDUFS3 are downregulated in atherosclerosis with chronic stress, while they are upregulated in the normal group. In a study by Reinson[27] it was found that the NDUFB11 gene encodes a relatively small integral membrane protein, NDUFB11, which is crucial for the assembly of active CI. Mutations in the NDUFB11 gene may indicate conditions such as tissue-like cardiomyopathy or congenital sideroblastic anemia, and the clinical manifestations of the same mutation can be highly variable. Since cardiomyocytes are closely related to atherosclerosis, NDUFB11 likely plays an important role in atherosclerosis.

Amate-García’s results[14] demonstrated the existence of a novel splice variant, c.338G > a, in the X-linked NDUFB11 gene. This gene encodes the NADH:ubiquinone oxidoreductase subunit B11 of the MRC CI. Molecular and functional analysis conducted in the target tissues of affected individuals, namely skeletal muscle and myocardium, showed biochemical disruptions in the MRC, suggesting that this variant is pathogenic.

Venous thrombosis is a condition related to blood coagulation and the circulation of blood within blood vessels. The formation of thrombi consumes more energy, which can lead to inadequate oxygen and nutrient supply to local tissues, affecting cellular energy metabolism. Cellular energy metabolism processes involve the role played by mitochondria, and the subunit NDUFB11 in the MRC is typically associated with cellular energy metabolism. This indirect influence of the NDUFB11 gene contributes to varying prognostic outcomes in venous thrombosis. Within cellular energy metabolism, mitochondrial respiration is predominantly governed by NDUFB11. Results from McLaughlin study[28] suggest that proteins most closely associated with respiratory conductance are PPM1K, NDUFB11, and C15orf61. Downregulation of these proteins may lead to specific deficiencies in mitochondrial complexes I and IV, thereby restricting their respiratory function. Based on this, a lower expression of NDUFB11 is associated with poor prognosis in venous thrombosis. Therefore, we speculate that NDUFB11 plays a significant role in both atherosclerosis and venous thrombosis.

Ndufs3 is a subunit of the NADH Dehydrogenase CI, and its role within CI is to accept and transfer electrons, aiding in the process of electron transfer. This complex is responsible for gradually transferring electrons from NADH to the proton pump in the inner mitochondrial membrane through a series of enzymatic reactions (respiratory chain Complexes III and IV), resulting in the creation of a proton gradient.[16] This proton gradient is essential for ATP synthesis, the primary molecule for storing and transferring energy within the cell. Ndufs3’s position within CI allows it to collaborate with other subunits during the electron transfer process, ensuring the smooth transfer of electrons from NADH to the respiratory chain. Mitochondrial dysfunction or defects in CI can lead to a range of diseases collectively known as mitochondrial disorders or respiratory chain deficiencies. Some mitochondrial diseases are associated with mutations or functional abnormalities in Ndufs3, which can result in impaired ATP production within cells, thereby affecting the function of multiple tissues and organs.[29,30]

Samanta[16] in their study demonstrated the presence of compound heterozygous variants in the NDUSF3 gene. Electron microscopy analysis of skeletal muscle fibers revealed an increase in mitochondrial quantity, varying degrees of mitochondrial enlargement, irregularities in shape, and a stacked arrangement perpendicular to the muscle fibers. These findings indicate abnormalities in the mitochondrial ultrastructure in individuals with related disorders. Structural abnormalities in NDUFS3 may exacerbate the symptoms of atherosclerosis. Atherosclerosis typically involves lipid accumulation, inflammatory responses, fibrous plaque formation, and calcification within arteries. Dysregulation of NDUFS3 control over mitochondria in each of these processes may lead to adverse prognostic outcomes. Moreover, there is a close connection between venous thrombosis and atherosclerosis, involving processes such as vascular inflammation and endothelial cell damage, which are linked to cellular energy metabolism. Impairment of the MRC due to NDUFS3 influence, with reduced mobility leading to its enrichment in less active subassemblies, may contribute to poorer prognosis in this disease.

The literature review aligns with our results, indicating that NDUFB11 and NDUFS3 are downregulated in atherosclerosis and venous thrombosis, and their reduced expression correlates with poorer prognosis. Finally, we conducted animal experiments involving gene overexpression and gene knockout to further validate their functions. The study results confirm our analyses, demonstrating that NDUFB11 and NDUFS3 are downregulated in atherosclerosis and venous thrombosis compared to normal tissues.

In summary, it can be concluded that the expression of Ndufb11 and Ndufs3 is downregulated in atherosclerosis and venous thrombosis, and this downregulation is associated with a poorer prognosis. Therefore, Ndufb11 and Ndufs3 hold promise as potential molecular targets for precision treatment of atherosclerosis and venous thrombosis. These findings provide a strong direction for further research into the mechanisms of these 2 diseases.

Expression and clinical significance of miR-34a-5p in lumbar disc herniation
Authors: Boyu Song, Rui Dong, Tingting Liu, Xu Yang, Zhili Zhang, Haixia Feng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AimsThis study aimed to investigate the expression, clinical significance, and underlying molecular mechanism of miR-34a-5p in Lumbar disc herniation (LDH).MethodsA total of 112 patients with LDH and 112 healthy controls were enrolled. Serum miR-34a-5p expression was measured by RT-qPCR, and its diagnostic value was assessed using receiver operating characteristic curve analysis. The correlations of miR-34a-5p with clinical indicators and inflammatory factors were analyzed. In vitro, an IL-1β-induced inflammatory injury model of human nucleus pulposus cells (HNPCs) was established to verify the function of miR-34a-5p.ResultsSerum miR-34a-5p was markedly upregulated in patients with LDHmiR-34a-5p was positively correlated with disease severity, the Visual Analogue Scale, Oswestry Disability Index, Pfirrmann grade, TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, and negatively correlated with Japanese Orthopaedic Association score and IL-10. In vitro, IL-1β upregulated miR-34a-5p in HNPCs; miR-34a-5p overexpression aggravated the inhibition of cell viability and the inflammatory response, whereas its knockdown alleviated these effects. Mechanistically, miR-34a-5p directly targeted and suppressed ZBTB20 mRNA expression.ConclusionsmiR-34a-5p is highly expressed in LDH and may serves as a promising diagnostic biomarker. It promotes HNPC inflammatory injury and LDH progression by targeting ZBTB20.

Introduction

Lumbar Disc Herniation (LDH) is one of the most common disorders encountered in orthopedic spine surgery, with low back pain, radiating leg pain, numbness, and lower-extremity dysfunction as its main clinical manifestations [1]. It severely impairs patients’ quality of life and working capacity, imposing a substantial medical and economic burden at the personal, familial and social levels [2]. In line with population aging and changes in living habits, the incidence of LDH has been rising annually, with a progressively younger age of onset, which has rendered this disease a pressing public health concern [3]. Intervertebral disc degeneration is the core pathological basis, manifested as nucleus pulposus cell loss, extracellular matrix imbalance and persistent inflammatory response [4, 5]. Surgical treatment can relieve neurological compression but cannot reverse disc degeneration, and it is associated with postoperative complications [6, 7]. Therefore, exploring the underlying molecular regulatory mechanisms and identifying effective therapeutic targets are of great clinical significance.

Accumulating evidence has highlighted the pivotal roles of non‑coding RNAs, including microRNAs, small interfering RNAs, and circular RNAs, in the pathophysiology, diagnosis, and management of various musculoskeletal conditions, such as tendon injuries [8, 9], osteoarthritis [10], rheumatoid arthritis [11], and osteoporosis [12, 13]. Recent studies have revealed that miRNAs play vital regulatory roles in intervertebral disc degeneration and LDH pathogenesis [14], and the expression of certain miRNAs is closely associated with LDH severity. For instance, the upregulation of miR-17 has been closely associated with postoperative radicular pain [15], and miR-93-5p expression is markedly correlated with the severity of LDH [16]. In addition, the highly expressed miR-146a participates in the progression of intervertebral disc degeneration by regulating the IL-6/STAT3 signaling pathway [17]. miR-34a-5p, a core member of the miR-34 family, is recognized to modulate pathological changes in numerous human diseases, and its dysregulated expression has been identified in tumors [18, 19], cardiovascular diseases [20], and neurological diseases [21]. Existing data have demonstrated a high abundance of miR-34a-5p in degenerative nucleus pulposus, which promotes the senescence and apoptosis of local cells and accelerates the progression of disc degeneration [22, 23]. However, the relationships among miR-34a-5p expression, its diagnostic potency, and its clinical application prospects in LDH still await further systematic verification.

Therefore, this study aimed to explore the expression and clinical significance of miR-34a-5p in LDH through clinical specimen detection, cellular experiments, and molecular biological assays.

Materials and methods

Study subjects

A total of 112 patients diagnosed with LDH and 112 age- and gender-matched healthy controls were enrolled in this single-center observational study. The diagnosis of LDH was confirmed by clinical manifestations, physical examination, and lumbar magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). Patients with other spinal diseases, severe systemic diseases, infections, tumors, or those who had received anti-inflammatory, immunosuppressive, or surgical treatment within 3 months were excluded. Healthy controls were individuals without LDH or other spinal disorders, confirmed by physical examination and MRI. All subjects signed informed consent forms, and the study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Hospital of Qinhuangdao.

Baseline demographic and clinical data of all subjects were collected. The 112 LDH patients were further divided into three subgroups according to disease severity, which was comprehensively evaluated by two independent senior spine surgeons based on clinical symptoms, VAS score, ODI, JOA score, and Pfirrmann grading of intervertebral disc degeneration. Peripheral venous blood samples were collected from all subjects in the morning under fasting conditions.

Real-time quantitative PCR

Total RNA was isolated from human nucleus pulposus cells and serum specimens via Trizol reagent (Invitrogen, USA). RNA concentration and purity were assessed with NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer, and samples with A260/A280 values ranging from 1.8 to 2.0 were regarded as eligible for subsequent experiments. Targeted reverse transcription was performed with matched commercial kits: miRNA-specific reverse transcription reagents (Takara, Japan) were used to synthesize the cDNA of miR-34a-5p, whereas a general reverse transcription kit from the same manufacturer was used to obtain ZBTB20 mRNA cDNA. RT-qPCR amplification was carried out on StepOnePlus fluorescence quantitative PCR instrument by employing SYBR Green reaction mixture (Takara, Japan). U6 small nuclear RNA served as the endogenous control for miR-34a-5p, and GAPDH was selected as the reference gene for normalization of ZBTB20 expression. Relative gene expression was calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method. All experiments were performed in three independent biological replicates to ensure the reliability of the results.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

Serum concentrations of pro-inflammatory mediators including TNF-α, IL-1β and IL-6, as well as the anti-inflammatory cytokine IL-10, were quantified by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay. All assays were performed using commercially available ELISA kits (R&D Systems, USA) in strict accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions. The concentration of each cytokine was determined according to the corresponding standard curve.

HNPC culture and inflammatory injury model establishment

Human NPCs (HNPCs) were purchased from ScienCell (Carlsbad, CA, USA). Cells were cultured in DMEM/F12 medium supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin-streptomycin in a 37 °C incubator with 5% CO₂. Cells at passages 3–5 were used for subsequent experiments. To establish the inflammatory injury model, HNPCs were treated with different concentrations of IL-1β (0, 5, 10, 20 ng/mL) for 24 h [24]. Cell viability was detected using the CCK-8 assay (Beyotime, China): cells were seeded in 96-well plates, treated with IL-1β, then 10 µL CCK-8 solution was added to each well and incubated for 2 h. The absorbance at 450 nm was measured to evaluate cell viability. The optimal concentration of IL-1β for subsequent experiments was determined based on the results.

Cell transfection

The miR-34a-5p mimic, miR-34a-5p inhibitor, and their respective negative control (NC) constructs were acquired from GenePharma Co., Ltd. (China). HNPCs were plated into 6-well culture plates and cultured until they reached 70%-80% confluence. Transfection was performed using Lipofectamine 3000 reagent (Invitrogen, USA). At 48 h after transfection, the transfection efficiency was verified by RT-qPCR through detection of miR-34a-5p expression.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

Four bioinformatics databases (TargetScan, miRDB, ENCORI, and miRWalk) were used to jointly predict the potential target genes of miR-34a-5p, and ZBTB20 was selected as the candidate target gene. The wild-type (WT) 3′-UTR sequence of ZBTB20 containing the complementary binding site of miR-34a-5p and the mutant (MUT) 3′-UTR sequence (with the binding site mutated) were synthesized and cloned into the pmirGLO luciferase reporter vector (Promega, USA), designated pmirGLO-ZBTB20-WT and pmirGLO-ZBTB20-MUT, respectively. HNPCs were co-transfected with pmirGLO-ZBTB20-WT/MUT and miR-34a-5p mimic/inhibitor/NC using Lipofectamine 3000. After 48 h of transfection, the luciferase activity was detected using the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Firefly luciferase activity was normalized to Renilla luciferase activity, and each experiment was performed in triplicate.

RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) assay

HNPCs at 80–90% confluence were harvested and lysed in ice-cold RIP lysis buffer supplemented with protease inhibitor cocktail and RNase inhibitor. The cell lysates were then incubated with magnetic beads pre-conjugated with anti-Ago2 antibody or isotype-matched IgG antibody (as a negative control) at 4 °C for 12 h with gentle rotation. After thorough washing, the immunoprecipitated complexes were treated with proteinase K to degrade the bound proteins, and the co-precipitated RNA was isolated using TRIzol reagent. The enrichment of miR-34a-5p and ZBTB20 mRNA in the immunoprecipitated RNA was quantified by RT-qPCR, and the relative enrichment was normalized to the input group (10% of the cell lysate before immunoprecipitation).

Statistical analysis

All data were analyzed using SPSS 26.0 statistical software. Measurement data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation. Comparisons between two groups were performed using t-test, and among multiple groups using one-way ANOVA. Correlation analysis was conducted using Spearman correlation coefficient. ROC curve analysis was used to evaluate the diagnostic value of miR-34a-5p.

Expression and diagnostic value of miR-34a-5p in LDH patients

The relative expression of miR-34a-5p was first compared between the control group and patients with LDH (Fig. 1A). miR-34a-5p expression was markedly upregulated in the LDH group compared with the control group (P < 0.001). ROC curve analysis revealed that serum miR‑34a‑5p exhibited favorable diagnostic efficacy for distinguishing patients with LDH from control subjects, with an AUC of 0.834 (95% CI 0.782–0.887). The optimal cut‑off value was determined as 1.19, corresponding to a sensitivity of 81.3% and a specificity of 75.0% (Fig. 1B). Subsequent subgroup analysis stratified by LDH severity (mild, moderate, and severe groups) revealed a stepwise increase in miR-34a-5p expression with advancing disease severity: miR-34a-5p levels were markedly higher in the moderate group than in the mild group (P < 0.01), and were further elevated in the severe group compared with the moderate group (P < 0.001) (Fig. 1C).

Correlation between miR-34a-5p expression and clinical indicators in LDH

Correlation analyses were performed to further characterize the relationships between miR-34a-5p expression and key clinical and molecular parameters in patients with LDH. miR-34a-5p expression showed significant positive correlations with the VAS score (r = 0.654), Pfirrmann grade of intervertebral disc degeneration (r = 0.625), and ODI (r = 0.569), and a significant negative correlation with the JOA score (r = −0.540) (Fig. 2A). In addition, miR-34a-5p expression was positively correlated with the TNF-α (r = 0.600), IL-1β (r = 0.615), and IL-6 (r = 0.597), and negatively correlated with the anti-inflammatory cytokine IL-10 (r = −0.563) (Fig. 2B).

Regulatory effects of miR-34a-5p on IL-1β-induced HNPC function

To verify the functional role of miR-34a-5p in LDH, an IL-1β-induced inflammatory injury model of HNPCs was established. First, HNPCs were treated with different concentrations of IL-1β, which inhibited HNPC in a concentration-dependent manner (P < 0.05, Fig. 3A). Subsequently, HNPCs were treated with 10 ng/mL IL-1β, and the secretion of inflammatory factors was measured. After IL-1β treatment, the secretion levels of the pro-inflammatory factors TNF-α and IL-6 were markedly increased, whereas the secretion level of the anti-inflammatory factor IL-10 was markedly decreased (P < 0.01, Fig. 3B). Furthermore, miR-34a-5p expression was markedly upregulated in IL-1β-stimulated HNPCs (P < 0.01, Fig. 3C).

miR-34a-5p overexpression and knockdown models were then established in IL-1β-induced HNPCs. The miR-34a-5p mimic markedly increased miR-34a-5p expression, whereas the miR-34a-5p inhibitor markedly reduced it (P < 0.01, Fig. 3D). Functional experiments showed that overexpression of miR-34a-5p further inhibited the viability of IL-1β-induced HNPCs, whereas knockdown of miR-34a-5p exerted the opposite effect (Fig. 3E, P < 0.01). With regard to inflammatory factors, overexpression of miR-34a-5p further promoted the secretion of TNF-α and IL-6 (P < 0.01), and further inhibited the secretion of IL-10 (P < 0.01), whereas knockdown of miR-34a-5p markedly suppressed the secretion of TNF-α and IL-6 (P < 0.01) and restored the secretion of IL-10 (P < 0.01) (Fig. 3F–H).

miR-34a-5p targets ZBTB20 to regulate the inflammatory injury of HNPCs

Four bioinformatics databases were used for cross-prediction of potential target genes of miR-34a-5p. Venn diagram analysis revealed 40 overlapping candidate genes predicted by all four databases, among which ZBTB20 was selected as a high-confidence target for further validation (Fig. 4A). Sequence alignment confirmed the presence of a conserved complementary binding site for hsa-miR-34a-5p in the 3′-UTR of ZBTB20 (Fig. 4B). In HNPCs co-transfected with the ZBTB20-WT, miR-34a-5p mimic markedly reduced luciferase activity, whereas the miR-34a-5p inhibitor markedly increased it (P < 0.01). In contrast, no significant changes in luciferase activity were observed in cells transfected with the ZBTB20-MUT (P>0.05) (Fig. 4C). RIP assay results showed that both miR-34a-5p and ZBTB20 mRNA were notably enriched in the anti-Ago2 antibody group compared with the IgG group (P < 0.001, Fig. 4D). The regulatory effect of miR-34a-5p on ZBTB20 expression was further examined by RT-qPCR. IL-1β treatment markedly inhibited the mRNA expression of ZBTB20 in HNPCs; overexpression of miR-34a-5p further downregulated the expression of ZBTB20, whereas knockdown of miR-34a-5p markedly restored ZBTB20 mRNA expression (P < 0.01, Fig. 4E).

Discussion

Dysregulated miRNA expression has been extensively reported to be implicated in the progression of intervertebral disc degeneration [25, 26]. The present study demonstrated that serum miR-34a-5p is markedly upregulated in patients with LDH, which is consistent with previous studies confirming that the upregulation of miR-34a-5p is associated with intervertebral disc degeneration [27, 28]. ROC curve analysis demonstrated good diagnostic efficacy of miR-34a-5p for LDH (AUC = 0.834), supporting its potential as a diagnostic biomarker. More importantly, our findings indicated that the expression level of miR-34a-5p was positively correlated with the severity of LDH, suggesting its involvement in both disease onset and progression, which provides a potential indicator for evaluating LDH severity and prognosis.

The VAS, Pfirrmann grade, ODI and JOA scores separately assess pain severity, disc degeneration and lumbar function, respectively [29, 30]. Correlation analyses showed that miR-34a-5p levels were associated with these indicators, suggesting that elevated miR-34a-5p is associated with more severe pain, aggravated intervertebral disc degeneration, and impaired lumbar function in patients with LDH. Inflammation is a key pathological process in the progression of LDH progression, and the imbalance between pro-inflammatory and anti-inflammatory factors is an important feature of the disease [31, 32]. In this study, miR-34a-5p was found to be positively associated with pro-inflammatory cytokines and negatively associated with the anti-inflammatory cytokine IL-10. Given the roles of these cytokines in disc degeneration and inflammatory injury, miR-34a-5p may mediate LDH progression by disrupting inflammatory homeostasis.

To verify the functional role of miR-34a-5p in LDH, an IL-1β-induced inflammatory injury model of HNPCs was established, which is a classic model for simulating the inflammatory microenvironment of LDH [33]. IL-1β inhibited HNPC viability in a concentration-dependent manner, promoted the secretion of pro-inflammatory factors, and suppressed the secretion of anti-inflammatory factors, consistent with the pathological characteristics of LDH. Notably, IL-1β treatment markedly upregulated miR-34a-5p expression in HNPCs, consistent with clinical sample results. miR-34a-5p overexpression and knockdown models were subsequently established in IL-1β-induced HNPCs. Overexpression of miR-34a-5p aggravated the inhibition of HNPC viability, promoted TNF-α and IL-6 secretion, and suppressed IL-10 secretion, whereas its knockdown reversed the IL-1β-induced inhibition of HNPC viability, reduced pro-inflammatory factor secretion, and restored IL-10 secretion. These results clearly indicate that miR-34a-5p plays a pro-degenerative and pro-inflammatory role in LDH.

To elucidate the molecular mechanism underlying miR-34a-5p-mediated LDH progression, bioinformatics prediction identified ZBTB20 as the primary target gene of miR-34a-5p. As a zinc finger and BTB domain-containing protein, ZBTB20 modulates cell proliferation, differentiation and inflammation. It is markedly downregulated in degenerative nucleus pulposus tissues, and its overexpression relieves nucleus pulposus cell degeneration [34]. Compared to previous studies, miR-34a-5p has been shown to participate in the progression of intervertebral disc degeneration (IDD) by targeting various downstream molecules, including SIRT1 [22], which mediates nucleus pulposus cell apoptosis and degeneration, and GDF5 [27], which regulates chondrogenesis and extracellular matrix homeostasis. This study is the first to identify ZBTB20 as a novel direct downstream target of miR-34a-5p in LDH. The discovery reveals a previously unexplored miR-34a-5p/ZBTB20 regulatory axis, thereby expanding the molecular network through which miR-34a-5p promotes LDH, and provides a new potential therapeutic target for understanding and intervening in LDH. Moreover, ZBTB20 inhibits chondrocyte inflammation and apoptosis via the NF-κB pathway [35]. Consistent with these reports, our results showed that IL-1β stimulation suppressed ZBTB20 levels in HNPCs, miR-34a-5p overexpression further reduced ZBTB20 expression, and that miR-34a-5p silencing reversed this inhibition. Collectively, these findings indicate that miR-34a-5p exerts its pro-degenerative and pro-inflammatory effects in LDH by directly targeting and suppressing ZBTB20 expression, and that inhibition of miR-34a-5p may represent a potential therapeutic strategy for LDH.

This study has several strengths. First, the expression profile and clinical value of miR-34a-5p in LDH were systematically clarified, and its favorable diagnostic potential as a novel biomarker was validated. Second, cellular experiments confirmed that miR-34a-5p alleviates HNPC inflammatory injury, indicating a promising therapeutic target for LDH. Third, the miR-34a-5p/ZBTB20 regulatory axis was identified, which further complements the molecular mechanism underlying LDH pathogenesis.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, this was a single-center investigation with limited sample size, and multi-center large-cohort studies are required for external validation. Second, the regulatory interaction between miR-34a-5p and ZBTB20 was only verified in vitro, and further in vivo animal experiments are needed. Third, this study focused only on HNPC inflammatory injury, and the roles of miR-34a-5p in other pathological processes of LDH remain to be explored.

In conclusion, miR-34a-5p is upregulated in patients with LDH, and its expression level is associated with disease severity, clinical indicators, and inflammatory response. It exerts pro-degenerative and pro-inflammatory effects in LDH by targeting and inhibiting ZBTB20, thereby providing a new theoretical basis for the molecular diagnosis and targeted therapy of LDH.

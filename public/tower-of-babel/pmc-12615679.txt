Treatment of initially unresectable local advanced pancreatic adenocarcinoma with FOLFIRINOX: A prospective study YCOG1403 (C-FLAP study)
Authors: Noritoshi Kobayashi, Yasuhiro Yabushita, Ryutaro Mori, Tomoaki Takahashi, Kentaro Miyake, Yu Sawada, Yuki Homma, Ryusei Matsuyama, Naoki Okubo, Eriko Katsuta, Kensuke Kubota, Shoji Yamanaka, Yasushi Ichikawa, Itaru Endo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aimed to provide evidence regarding the treatment of patients with unresectable pancreatic cancer. We conducted a prospective single-arm phase II trial using the FOLFIRINOX regimen. After completing 4–8 cycles, patients underwent surgical resection when feasible. The primary endpoint was R0 resection rate. Fifteen patients were enrolled in this study. A median of six courses of FOLFIRINOX chemotherapy was administered, and a partial response or R0 resection was achieved in 26.7% and 33% of the patients, respectively. Severe adverse events due to chemotherapy and major surgical complications were observed in 33.3% and 6.7% of patients, respectively. The median overall survival of patients who underwent R0 resection or with R1 or unresectable disease was 47.8 months (95% confidence interval (CI), 22.5–73.1) or 14.5 months (95% CI, 11.8–17.2), respectively (P = 0.031). Well-selected patients with unresectable locally advanced pancreatic cancer treated with FOLFIRINOX achieved relatively high R0 resection rates and prolonged survival. Therefore, induction with FOLFIRINOX is feasible and well tolerated for locally advanced, initially unresectable pancreatic cancer and may be effective in facilitating R0 resection and prolonging survival.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-23608-5.Keywords: Unresectable locally advanced pancreatic cancer, FOLFIRINOX, R0 resectionSubject terms: Cancer, Gastroenterology

Introduction

Approximately 60,430 new diagnoses of pancreatic cancer (PC) were expected in the United States in 20211. The incidence of PC is increasing at a rate of 0.5–1.0% per year, and PC is projected to become the second leading cause of cancer-related deaths in the United States by 20301,2. Most patients with PC exhibit nonspecific symptoms even at an advanced stage, which results in a late diagnosis and the tumor not being amenable to curative surgery1. At the time of diagnosis, almost 50% of the patients had metastatic lesions, and 30% were diagnosed with locally advanced PC3. Resectability, defined as the ability to completely remove cancer cells, was assessed to select a treatment strategy for localized PC4. The American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) classification of tumors, nodes, and metastases was used to assess prognosis5. For practical treatment planning, localized PCs are categorized as either resectable pancreatic cancer (R-PC), borderline resectable PC (BR-PC), or unresectable locally advanced pancreatic cancer (URLA-PC), based on the degree of tumor contact and invasion into major vessels such as the superior mesenteric artery, hepatic artery, or celiac vasculature6.

There is growing interest in evaluating neoadjuvant systemic therapy for R-PC and BR-PC. Neoadjuvant or pre-operative therapy can eradicate occult metastatic disease and increase the number of patients eligible for surgery. A recent randomized prospective trial (Southwest Oncology Group S1505 trial) for R-PC demonstrated high completion rates, with 84% of patients receiving total of six courses neoadjuvant and adjuvant FOLFIRINOX and 73% undergoing resection. Median overall survival (OS) was 23.2 months (95% confidence interval (CI), 17.6–45.9 months) and median disease-free survival after resection was 10.9 months7. In a phase II trial, 34 of 43 (79%) patients with BR-PC received eight cycles of preoperative FOLFIRINOX chemotherapy followed by chemoradiotherapy and the median progression-free survival (PFS) among all eligible patients was 14.7 months, and the median OS was 37.7 months. R0 resection was achieved in 31 of the 48 eligible patients (65%; 95% CI, 49–78%). Among patients who underwent resection, the median PFS was 48.6 months, and the median OS was not reached8. Another advantage is the potential downstaging before surgery, which facilitates margin-negative (R0) resection of BR-PC8,9. A multi-institutional trial demonstrated improved R0 resection rates in 58 patients with BR-PC receiving neoadjuvant therapy compared to initial surgery (82.4% vs. 33.3%; P = 0.01)10.

However, in URLA-PC, the tumor is by definition unable to be resected without compromising surgical margins, thus, systemic treatments are mainly focused on survival benefits rather than conversion surgery. Only a small fraction of patients undergo surgical resection with curative intent. Planning R0 surgical resection for patients with URLA-PC is challenging. The initial treatment for conversion surgery typically consists of chemotherapy regimens such as FOLFIRINOX or albumin-bound paclitaxel and gemcitabine11. A systematic review of 11 studies involving 315 patients with URLA-PC revealed a pooled median OS of 24.2 months12. An observational study including 101 patients with URLA-PC who were treated with FOLFIRINOX as induction therapy showed that 29% of the patients had a > 30% reduction in tumor size, and half of the patients who experienced a reduction in tumor size underwent resection13. Among patients who underwent resection, 55% achieved R0 resection.

The classification of ‘unresectable locally advanced pancreatic cancer’ is not used in the latest version of the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) Guidelines for Pancreatic Adenocarcinoma Version 2.2025 and has been changed to ‘locally advanced’14. This is thought to be due to the fact that unresectable is a misleading name, unlike unresectable by definition and technically unresectable. The latest guidelines suggest that clinical trials, systemic therapy, or induction chemotherapy followed by chemoradiation or stereotactic body radiotherapy should initially be recommended for locally advanced disease with a good or intermediate performance status (PS). Conversion surgical resection is considered for patients with no disease progression and good or intermediate PS after treatment, because favorable OS and relapse-free survival can be expected.

However, no prospective studies have investigated the surgical conversion rate after FOLFIRINOX chemotherapy in patients with URLA-PC. Therefore, we conducted a prospective single-arm phase II trial of URLA-PC with FOLFIRINOX chemotherapy followed by surgical resection.

Methods

Study design

The Conversion therapy of FOLFIRINOX in patients with unresectable locally advanced pancreatic cancer (C-FLAP) was an open-label, single-arm, phase II trial conducted by the Yokohama Clinical Oncology Group (YCOG1403). The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Yokohama City University (B140508029). This study was registered with the Japanese Clinical Trials Registry (under UMIN000014039. (http://www.umin.ac.jp/ctr/index.htm). The patients provided written informed consent before enrollment in the study. The first registration date was 22/05/2014. The primary endpoints were R0 resection rate and possibility of transfer to resection due to the therapeutic efficacy of FOLFIRINOX therapy.

Inclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria were: (1) patients with histologically or cytologically confirmed pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma between 2014 and 2022 at Yokohama City University Hospital; (2) the absence of distant metastases (confirmed using whole-body enhanced computed tomography, fluorodeoxyglucose-positron emission tomography, and laparoscopic examination; no previous treatment for PC; 3) aged ≥ 20 years and ≤ 75 years; expected to survive for at least eight weeks from the date of enrollment; 4) presence of measurable lesions (RECIST v1.1); 5) at least one of the following URLA-PC criteria for primary lesions (NCCN Guidelines v1.2013): cancerous stenosis of more than half the circumference of the superior mesenteric artery or celiac artery, unreconstructable superior mesenteric vein occlusion, portal vein occlusion, aortic invasion, or cancerous stenosis of the aorta; 6) imaging evaluations before and after FOLFIRINOX therapy were performed by an independent radiologist according to RECIST v1.1; and 7) patient laboratory data showed adequate hematologic, liver, and renal function (hemoglobin, > 9.0 g/dL; neutrophil cell count, 1,500/mm3; platelet count, > 100,000/mm3; aspartate aminotransferase, alanine transaminase and total bilirubin, < 2.5 × the upper limit of each institution’s normal range; serum creatinine, < 1.2 mg/dL; and C-reactive protein, < 2.0 mg/dL). All laboratory tests were assessed within seven days prior to the start of treatment.

Exclusion criteria

The exclusion criteria were: (1) history of severe drug hypersensitivity or allergy; (2) patients who were expected to experience severe toxicity from irinotecan with two UGT1A1 genetic polymorphisms (UGT1A1*6 and UGT1A1*28), such as homozygotes (UGT1A1*6/*6 and UGT1A1*28/*28) or both heterozygotes (UGT1A1*6/*28); (3) patients with active cancer which was either concurrent and metachronous with a disease-free period of ≤ 5 years other than PC, however, carcinoma in situ and lesions equivalent to carcinoma in mucosa that were considered to be curable by local treatment were exempt; (4) serious comorbid disease that is difficult to control (heart disease, lung disease, severe diabetes, severe infection, intestinal paralysis, and intestinal obstruction); (5) pregnant, potentially pregnant, or breastfeeding women; (6) patients with psychosis or psychiatric symptoms and who were considered to have difficulty participating in this study; (7) patients who received blood transfusions, blood products, or hematopoietic factor preparations, such as granulocyte colony-stimulating factor, within 7 days before enrollment in the study; (8) patients with large amounts of body cavity fluid (pleural fluid, ascites, and pericardial effusion); and (9) patients with jaundice and experiencing diarrhea (however, for patients with a colostomy, when diarrhea interfered with daily life). We also excluded vulnerable patients with poor general health status. Because the original FOLFIRINOX regimen has relatively high toxicity, we excluded patients who were predicted to have prolonged decreased oral intake and bone marrow function, making it difficult to continue treatment and causing severe adverse events. Patients who were administered atazanavir sulfate were also excluded.

Treatment protocol

The FOLFIRINOX regimen used in our study comprised 85 mg/m2 oxaliplatin (L-OHP), 200 mg/m2 leucovorin (l-LV), 180 mg/m2 irinotecan (CPT-11), and 400 mg/m2 5-fluorouracil (FU) (bolus), followed by continuous intravenous infusion of 2400 mg/m2 5-FU for 46 h. Treatment was administered every 2 weeks. The bolus infusion of 5-FU was omitted, and the initial dose of CPT-11 was reduced to 150 mg/m2 after a protocol amendment in 2021.

The clinical trial design is shown in Fig. 1a. The decision for conversion to surgery was made at completion of every two courses after four courses, up to eight courses. If the tumor was determined to be unresectable after completion of eight courses, the treatment protocol was discontinued. Patients judged to have technically resectable tumors underwent surgery within four weeks after the end of FOLFIRINOX therapy. Although not prescribed in a treatment protocol, adjuvant chemotherapy with S-1 alone was recommended for six months after surgery. Treatment after the discontinuation of FOLFIRINOX was not prescribed. However, continuation of FOLFIRINOX was recommended in cases where the tumor was judged to be unresectable, but an efficacy of stable disease (SD) or higher persisted after eight courses of FOLFIRINOX.

Depending on the severity of adverse events that occurred during the previous course, the dosages of L-OHP, CPT-11, and 5-FU were reduced. Adverse events in this study were evaluated in accordance with the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) v4.0. Dose reduction was required for any of the following events: (1) grade 4 neutropenia, (2) febrile neutropenia, (3) grade 4 thrombocytopenia, (4) any other grade 3 or 4 toxicity, or (5) delayed recovery from treatment-related toxicity for more than 2 weeks. Chemotherapy was discontinued in patients with interstitial pneumonitis of any grade or grade 3 peripheral sensory neuropathy.

Dose reduction was performed simultaneously according to dosage variation standards for L-OHP, CPT-11, and 5-FU, whereas l-LV was not reduced. The initial dose of L-OHP was 85 mg/m2, −1 level was 65 mg/m2, and − 2 level was 50 mg/m2. The initial dose of CPT-11 was 180 mg/m2, −1 was 150 mg/m2, and − 2 was 120 mg/m2. The initial dose of 5-FU was 400 mg/m2 as a bolus injection and 2400 mg/m2 as a continuous infusion. The − 1 level omitted the bolus injection and included 2000 mg/m2 as a continuous infusion. The − 2 level omitted the bolus injection and included 1600 mg/m2 as a continuous infusion. In patients who had been reduced to the − 1 level according to the above criteria, when further toxicity was observed, L-OHP, CPT-11, and 5-FU were reduced to the − 2 level. If further toxicity was observed after the − 2 level of 5-FU reduction, the treatment protocol was discontinued. In cases where further toxicity was observed after a −2 level reduction of L-OHP, L-OHP was discontinued and FOLFIRI treatment was continued. Similarly, in cases where further toxicity was observed after the − 2 level of CPT-11, CPT-11 was discontinued and FOLFOX therapy was continued. The treatment was discontinued when both L-OHP and CPT-11 were discontinued. However, when the adverse event resolved after a single dose reduction, the dose was increased. Dose reductions were not double counted for multiple reasons. For example, if two toxicities, e.g. grade 3 diarrhea and grade 3 mucositis, were observed in the previous course, the reduction in the next course was one level. All the patients routinely received palonosetron, aprepitant, and dexamethasone as prophylaxis during each cycle.

Decision for conversion surgery

Treatment effect judgments were performed by independent radiologists in accordance with RECIST v1.1. Based on these results, the decision to resect was ultimately made based on an independent evaluation by three surgeons of the imaging evaluation committee. The feasibility of resection was judged for the patients whose FOLFIRINOX efficacy was SD or higher. It was decided based on an anatomical standpoint in addition to tumor shrinkage, that is, the degree of involvement of the tumor with critical adjacent vessels such as the celiac artery, superior mesenteric artery, common hepatic artery, superior mesenteric vein, and portal vein. Thus, the decision to proceed with surgery was made based on whether the findings of the aforementioned URLA-PC criteria were improved based on an imaging study by the Diagnostic Imaging Review Committee, which consisted of three surgical experts. Each committee member made an independent decision, and in cases where two or more members judged the tumor to be resectable, the clinician proceeded with the resection; otherwise, the tumor was considered unresectable.

Statistical analysis

The target number of patients was set as follows: prospective reports investigating resection rates after chemoradiotherapy or chemotherapy reported an R0 resection rate of 7–29%15–19, and the trial threshold R0 resection rate was 10%. In addition, studies on resection feasibility after FOLFIRINOX therapy have reported R0 resection rates of 8–36%20–23. This was a prospective study with the R0 resection rate as the primary endpoint. Therefore, the expected R0 resection rate in the present study was 25%. Using SWOG Statistical Tools: One Arm Binominal24 with a threshold R0 resection rate of 10%, an expected R0 resection rate of 25%, and a bilateral α = 0.05 and β = 0.2, the required number of cases was 49. Considering the expected small number of dropouts, the target number of patients included in this study was 50.

Survival analysis was performed using the Kaplan–Meier method, and differences in survival between groups were assessed using the log-rank test. The null hypothesis was no difference in the survival probabilities between the groups at any time point. A two-sided significance level of 5% was adopted. This analysis included ad hoc comparisons that were not based on predefined hypotheses, and so the results should be interpreted with caution.

A waterfall plot was created based on the tumor size at the best response. In cases where the patient underwent surgery, the time of evaluation was immediately before surgical resection; in other cases, it was at the end of either the 4th, 6th, or 8th course of treatment, whenever the best response was obtained. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 28 software (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA).

Results

Patients

Among the 443 patients with PC treated from 2014 to 2022 at Yokohama City University Hospital, 120 were diagnosed with unresectable PC (Fig. 1b). Of these, 60 were diagnosed with URLA-PC. Based on the aforementioned exclusion criteria, 45 patients were excluded, and 15 patients were enrolled. The major reason for exclusion was previous chemotherapy at other institutions. Other reasons included advanced age, vulnerability to FOLFIRINOX, lack of a definitive histological diagnosis, refusal to participate, lack of laparoscopic examination, UGT1A1 polymorphisms, active multiple cancers, serious complications, and thrombocytopenia. This enrollment did not meet the initial target number of patients calculated by the power analysis for statistical significance.

Patient demographics at enrollment are summarized in Table 1. There were 12 male and three female patients, with a median age of 64 years (range, 44–73 years). The Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) Performance Status of all patients was 0. Five patients (33.3%) had diabetes mellitus. The median CA19-9 level was 44 mL/IU (range, 1.2–13549 mL/IU). Tumors were found in the pancreatic head and body/tail in 9 and 6 patients, respectively. Four patients (26.7%) underwent biliary stent placement. The median tumor diameter was 35 mm (range, 24–72 mm) and all tumors were defined as URLA-PCs based on arterial invasion. Twelve (80%) tumors invaded the portal vein.

Efficacy of chemotherapy

The efficacy of chemotherapy is summarized in Table 2. FOLFIRINOX chemotherapy was administered for a median of six cycles (range, 3–8 cycles) in the designed protocol. Partial response (PR) was achieved in 26.7% (4/15) of the patients, SD in 66.7% (10/15), and progressive disease (PD) in 6.7% (1/15). The disease control rate was 93.3% (14/15). In 2021, the protocol was revised to stop the bolus administration of 5-FU and to change the CPT-11 dose to 150 mg/m², as this modified FOLFIRINOX regimen became widely accepted in Japan. Only one patient was enrolled after the protocol revision, and the best response in this case was SD.

Surgery

The surgical procedures and outcomes are summarized in Table 4. 40% (6/15) of patients underwent surgery, and R0 resection was achieved in 33.3% (5/15) of patients and 81.3% (5/6) of the patients who underwent surgery (Figure S2). Surgical procedures, including pancreaticoduodenectomy and distal pancreatectomy, were performed in five and one cases, respectively. The median operative time was 646 min (range, 534–1033 min). Arterial resection was performed in three cases (50%), and portal vein resection was performed in four cases (66.7%). The median blood loss was 1600 mL (range, 400–8853 mL). Well and moderately differentiated tumor pathological types were found in two and four cases, respectively. Evans grades 1, 2, and 3 were observed in three, two and one cases, respectively. The most severe complication after surgical resection was a pancreatic fistula (n = 1). Diarrhea (n = 5), delayed gastric emptying (n = 3), abdominal abscesses (n = 1), and ascites (n = 1) were also observed after surgical resection. Five patients received S-1 chemotherapy and one patient received gemcitabine and albumin-bound paclitaxel combination chemotherapy after surgical resection. Four patients had recurrent disease, and two patients did not have recurrence at the last follow-up, with long survival. The therapeutic effect of chemotherapy in the two patients who did not relapse was PR in one patient and SD in the other. The preoperative tumor markers of the patient with PR were CEA 16.9 ng/mL and CA19-9 1 U/mL, while the patient who achieved SD had preoperative CEA 1.8 ng/mL and CA19-9 23 U/mL.

The four patients with recurrent disease suffered local recurrence and peritoneal disseminated recurrence. Relapse-free survival time after R0 resection was 25.7 months (95% CI, 11.3–40.1).

Overall survival

Swimmer plots for all patients receiving FOLFIRINOX treatment are shown in Figure S3. The median OS of all cases was 21.3 months (95% CI, 9.15–33.4 months) (Fig. 2a). OS of R0 resection cases was significantly longer than that of other cases (47.8 months [95% CI, 22.5–73.1 months] vs. 14.5 months [95% CI, 11.8–17.2 months]; log-rank test, P = 0.031) (Fig. 2b). OS of R0 and R1 resection cases was longer than that of other cases (31.6 months [95% CI, 10.4–52.7 months] vs. 14.5 months [95% CI, 14.2–14.8 months]; log-rank test, P = 0.054) (Fig. 2c).

Discussion

We conducted a prospective single-arm study of URLA-PCs to determine the conversion rate to surgery after FOLFIRINOX therapy. R0 resection was achieved in 33.3% of the patients with URLA-PC. The OS of patients who underwent R0 resection was 47.8 months, which is extremely long among patients with URLA-PC. There are many prospective studies on neoadjuvant chemotherapies, including FOLFIRINOX, and chemoradiotherapy for R-PC or BR-PC; however, no prospective study has evaluated the resectability rate following pre-operative chemotherapy using FOLFIRINOX. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to evaluate the R0 resection rate after pre-operative chemotherapy using the FOLFIRINOX regimen in patients with URLA-PC.

The R0 resection rate in this study was 33%, which is similar to the previous retrospective studies and meta-analysis involving conversion surgery after FOLFIRINOX treatment12,25–27. In a retrospective study by Blazer et al., R0 resection was performed in 10 of 25 (40%) patients with URLA-PC25. The meta-analysis of 13 studies involving 253 patients by Petrelli et al. reported that R0 resection was performed in 22.5% (95% CI, 13.3–35.4) of patients with URLA-PC26. According to another meta-analysis, 91 of 325 patients (28.0%) underwent resection after FOLFIRINOX for URLA-PC. The pooled percentage of resection in a random-effects model was 25.9% (95% CI, 20.2%–31.9%; I2 = 24%)12.

The OS rate was similar to those reported in previous retrospective studies and meta-analyses12,27. Pooled analysis of 315 patients revealed a follow-up duration of 12.3 (8.0–20.5) months, PFS of 15.0 months and OS of 24.2 months12. Among the 437 patients with URLA-PC who underwent radical pancreatic surgery, median OS was 33.4 months (95% CI, 29.0–36.3 months).

The number of chemotherapy cycles before conversion surgery varied across studies; however, a previous meta-analysis demonstrated that this may not have a major impact28. In a retrospective multicenter study involving 97 patients with URLA-PC in Japan, conversion surgery was more beneficial for patients with > 8 months of pre-operative therapy than for those with < 8 months of pre-operative therapy29. Compared with this retrospective study, our study had a shorter duration of chemotherapy. However, this retrospective study mainly used gemcitabine-based chemotherapy (91.4%), and albumin-bound paclitaxel was not approved for insurance support at the time of that study in Japan. Therefore, the optimal treatment duration of each regimen may vary.

On the other hand, it has been reported that a significant fraction of patients with PC do not respond to neoadjuvant FOLFIRINOX but do respond to neoadjuvant albumin-bound paclitaxel and gemcitabine30. Although researchers have intensively sought useful predictive biomarkers for PC chemotherapy, there are currently no practical biomarkers. Therefore, it is important to proceed with second-line treatment if the tumor does not respond to FOLFIRINOX treatment.

Some studies have shown a survival benefit of additional radiotherapy after FOLFIRINOX treatment31. However, the biologically effective dose and the protocols for fractionated administration of radiotherapy are not standardized; the method of administration varies depending on the combination with chemotherapy, and there is no rigorous evidence of superiority of the additional radiotherapy compared to chemotherapy alone and so we conducted this study with chemotherapy alone.

The difficulty in conversion surgery is that the decision to perform surgical resection does not necessarily correspond to treatment efficacy. In our study, there were patients in which resection was performed despite the treatment not achieving PR and, conversely, patients in which resection was not performed after achieving a PR. The decision to perform conversion surgery was based on a comprehensive judgment by experienced experts, such as reducing the length and circumference of contact between the tumor and important arteries rather than shrinking the tumor, increasing the CT value of the adipose tissue around the tumor, and normalizing the tumor markers. We achieved a relatively high rate of R0 resection, and these patients had long-term survival. Because PC is a fatal disease, appropriate patient selection for surgical resection is extremely important for achieving the best oncologic outcomes.

In terms of statistical significance, this may be a controversial study, as it did not meet the initial statistically set number of cases. The number of patients was originally set based on the power calculation; however, we were only able to enroll 15 eligible patients in eight years. The major reason was a small proportion of the URLA-PC, which accounted for 13.5% of all PC cases in our study. Furthermore, this study had significant selection bias, being a single-arm, non-randomized study in which a subjectively selected subset of patients underwent surgical resection after neoadjuvant FOLFIRINOX treatment. Therefore, the favorable outcomes of patients who ultimately underwent resection may be attributable to the better biology of their tumors rather than the effect of chemotherapy.

This was a prospective study conducted in a homogeneous population, not conducted after radiotherapy, and allowed a pure evaluation of the role of FOLFIRINOX therapy. The R0 resection rate was relatively high, the OS was long, especially in patients who underwent resection, and the side effects of chemotherapy were considered acceptable. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to evaluate the R0 resection rate after pre-operative chemotherapy using the FOLFIRINOX regimen in patients with URLA-PC. R0 resection was achieved in 33.3% of the patients with URLA-PC. Long-term survival can be achieved by surgical resection in very well-selected cases of URLA-PC. Even in cases where resection is not achieved, chemotherapy, such as FOLFIRINOX, is considered extremely important for extending the survival period.

It was also clear from this study that not all patients with URLA-PC benefited from FOLFIRINOX therapy. Based on many reports and guidelines, it is deemed that patients who receive FOLFIRINOX therapy should be those who have a good PS before the start of treatment and who maintain adequate bone marrow function or cases where BRCA1/2 or PALB2 mutations are found by genetic analysis14. With appropriate patient selection, we showed that FOLFIRINOX therapy can enable surgical resection and prolong the prognosis of patients with URLA-PC. Further large-scale studies are needed to confirm the utility of FOLFIRINOX as preoperative therapy for URLA-PC.

SC2Arena and StarEvolve: Benchmark and Self-Improvement Framework for LLMs in Complex Decision-Making Tasks
Pengbo Shen, Yaqing Wang, Ni Mu, Yao Luan, Runpeng Xie, Senhao Yang, Lexiang Wang, Hao Hu, Shuang Xu, Yiqin Yang, Bo Xu

Abstract
Evaluating large language models (LLMs) in complex decision-making is essential for advancing AI’s ability for strategic planning and real-time adaptation. However, existing benchmarks for tasks like StarCraft II fail to capture the game’s full complexity, such as its complete game context, diverse action spaces, and all playable races. To address this gap, we present SC2Arena, a benchmark that fully supports all playable races, low-level action spaces, and optimizes text-based observations to tackle spatial reasoning challenges. Complementing this, we introduce StarEvolve, a hierarchical framework that integrates strategic planning with tactical execution, featuring iterative self-correction and continuous improvement via fine-tuning on high-quality gameplay data. Its key components include a Planner-Executor-Verifier structure to break down gameplay, and a scoring system for selecting high-quality training samples. Comprehensive analysis using SC2Arena provides valuable insights into developing generalist agents that were not possible with previous benchmarks. Experimental results also demonstrate that our proposed StarEvolve achieves superior performance in strategic planning. Our code, environment, and algorithms are publicly available.

Introduction

The rapid advancement of large language models (LLMs)
(
Achiam et al. 2023
;
Guo et al. 2025
)
has accelerated research towards artificial general intelligence
(
Mon-Williams et al. 2025
;
Zheng et al. 2025
)
. A critical milestone in this pursuit is the development of agents capable of strategic planning and real-time adaptation in complex environments.
StarCraft II, a renowned real-time strategy game, provides an ideal testbed with its profound strategic depth, diverse unit types and interactions, vast decision space, and inherent demands for world modeling, as illustrated in Fig.
1
.
These characteristics align closely with the LLMs’ core strengths
(
Wei et al. 2022
;
Yao et al. 2023
;
Wang et al. 2023b
)
, making it particularly suitable for LLM evaluation.
Therefore, establishing benchmarks in StarCraft II to assess LLM performance is critical.
However, existing LLM benchmarks in StarCraft II
(
Ma et al. 2024
;
Li et al. 2024
)
often fail to capture the original game’s complexity, lacking support for full-length game context, complete low-level action spaces, comprehensive race configuration, and agent-vs-agent mechanisms.
This limits their ability to evaluate the strategic reasoning and adaptability of LLM agents.

Figure 1:
An illustration of StarCraft II.
StarCraft II is a highly complex and competitive real-time strategy game, where players choose one of three distinct races (Terran, Zerg, Protoss) to gather resources, construct buildings, produce units, and defeat enemies.
Each race has unique traits and can produce race-specific buildings and military units.

To bridge this gap, we propose
SC2Arena
, a comprehensive StarCraft II benchmark for evaluating LLMs’ decision-making ability.
With a standardized JSON interface, it supports ① full-length game context, ② all playable races (Terran, Protoss, Zerg), and ③ complete low-level actions, preserving the game’s strategic depth.
Crucially, SC2Arena addresses inherent challenges of text-based agents by optimizing observations: proximity-based unit ordering captures units’ spatial relationships, and worker aggregation reduces information overload.
Additionally, it enables agent-vs-agent gameplay, facilitating direct competition and an agent ranking mechanism to better evaluate performance and strategic adaptability.

Furthermore, we introduce
StarEvolve
, a closed-loop agent framework that enables self-correction and self-improvement for strategic decision-making.
It employs a hierarchical architecture: a Planner generates high-level strategic commands, while an Executor converts them into precise low-level actions.
Both components integrate Verifier modules that perform iterative self-correction to ensure syntactic correction and feasibility.
Crucially, StarEvolve achieves continuous self-improvement by collecting gameplay data and performing supervised fine-tuning (SFT).
We design an RL-inspired scoring function to prioritize high-quality training samples, which quantifies action impact through discounted value increments across key game-state metrics.
Utilizing these data, we fine-tune the Planner, Executor, and Verifiers to continuously improve their performance.

In summary, our contributions are twofold:
(1) We propose SC2Arena, a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating LLMs in StarCraft II, supporting full-length game context, all races, full action spaces, and agent-vs-agent gameplay.
(2) We propose StarEvolve, a closed-loop agent framework, which integrates strategic planning with tactical execution. It achieves self-correction with iterative decision refinement, and also continuous self-improvement via SFT, enabling LLM agents to defeat challenging opponents.

Benchmark: SC2Arena

In this section, we propose SC2Arena, a structured text-based benchmark designed to evaluate LLM agents in StarCraft II, with standardized observation and action interfaces.
SC2Arena supports full-length game contexts, complete low-level action spaces, all three races and agent-vs-agent gameplay, thus preserving the original game’s strategic depth and complexity.

3.1
Introduction to SC2Arena

Overview of StarCraft II.

StarCraft II is a highly complex and competitive real-time strategy game, where players control one of three distinct races (Terran, Zerg, Protoss) to gather resources, construct buildings, produce units, and command armies to defeat opponents.
As shown in Fig.
1
, the game demands proficiency in macro-management, like resource management and base-building, micro-level unit control, and strategic decision-making, all under real-time constraints and partial information.
Its observation space can reach up to 1,200 dimensions, while the action space can extend to 300 dimensions.
Professional matches typically last 20–30 minutes, with players executing hundreds of actions per minute.
This complexity makes it both a popular AI benchmark and a globally renowned e-sport.

Each race in StarCraft II offers a distinct playstyle and unique mechanics, as shown in Fig.
1
:

Terrans
are a human race with mechanized, repairable units.
Their unique Bio-ball Micro-Tactic involves tightly grouped armies supported by Medivacs (flying medical transport units).
By optimizing unit positioning and treating injured units, this strategy maximizes firepower and survivability.

Zerg
are a swarm-based alien race with rapidly breeding biological units to overwhelm opponents.
Their unique Creep Spread Mechanism enhances unit speed and vision, enabling aggressive expansion and superior map control.

Protoss
are an advanced alien race with regenerating shielded units. Their unique Warp-in Deployment mechanic allows armies to instantly arrive at strategic locations within their Pylon network, enabling precise timing attacks.
Appendix
A.1
offers further details.

3.2
Benchmark Design

To enable LLM agents to play StarCraft II effectively, game states must be converted into textual observations, which presents two key challenges:
(1)
Spatial reasoning difficulty
: Agents struggle to infer positional relationships between units based solely on text
(
Sharma 2023
)
.
This hinders agents’ ability to identify attached and interactable unit structures, such as adjacent Barracks and Tech Labs, thereby reducing their effectiveness.
(2)
Information overload
: Raw observations often include excessive low-value data, such as repetitive worker unit statuses, which overwhelms agents and distracts them from strategic decision-making
(
Adejumo and Johnson 2024
)
.

To address these issues, SC2Arena introduces two key techniques, as illustrated in Fig.
2
:
(1)
Proximity-based unit ordering
:
We enhance spatial reasoning via a greedy nearest-neighbor unit ordering, which explicitly encodes spatial relationships into the observation text.
Specifically, starting from the Command Center, each unit is listed after its nearest neighbor unit.
This heuristic helps agents identify interactable structures effectively.
(2)
Unit aggregation
:
We reduce information overload by aggregating routine worker units into a single representation, preserving core workers’ information while removing redundant details.
As validated in Section
5.3
, these techniques improve agent performance.

Observation and Action Interface.

As shown in Fig.
2
, SC2Arena provides a standardized and extensible text-based interface for StarCraft II.
Observations are formatted as a hierarchical text summary, categorized into six key types: resource levels, unit states, building statuses, ongoing activities, visible enemy units/structures, and technological progress.
Actions are defined in a unified JSON format, enabling agents to specify tasks such as unit production, structure construction, and tactical maneuvers.
Each action includes parameters like unit IDs, target locations, or specific abilities, allowing precise in-game control.
Appendix
A.2
provides detailed examples of observations and actions.

Implementation details.

At each iteration of the gameplay loop, the agent processes the textual game state, constructs a prompt, and queries the LLM for a decision. The environment extracts the valid JSON from the LLM’s output, parses it into an executable action, and executes it. This design maintains a clear separation between the agent and the environment. Appendix
A.3
provides more details.

3.3
Evaluation Metrics

SC2Arena supports competitive interactions between agents and various opponents, including built-in AI, rule-based adversaries, or other agents. We provide a comprehensive set of metrics to assess both performance and efficiency across these competitive settings.

Performance metrics:

(1) Elo Rating (ELO): A widely adopted metric in competitive environments for quantifying agents’ relative levels. It is derived from agent-vs-agent match outcomes and updates after each contest.
(2) Win Rate (WR): Percentage of games won, typically against built-in AIs.
(3) Time Cost of Winning (TCW): Average duration of winning games, reflecting strategic efficiency.
(4) Supply Block Ratio (SBR): Percentage of game time during which a player cannot produce new units due to reaching the supply cap. This metric evaluates a player’s ability to manage resources and sustain continuous military production.
(5) Resource Utilization Ratio (RUR): Average minerals and gas spent per game timestep, reflecting economic development.

Efficiency metrics:

(6) Tokens Per Decision (TPD): Average number of LLM tokens per decision.
(7) Valid Action Ratio (VAR): Percentage of valid and executable decisions made by the agent.
Detailed formulations are provided in Appendix
A.4
.

4.1
Hierarchical Self-Correction Framework

Deploying LLM agents for StarCraft II presents challenges, due to the game’s vast low-level action space and the need for precise unit selection in JSON syntax, which often results in invalid actions, distracting agents from high-level strategy
(
Tam et al. 2024
)
.
To address this, StarEvolve employs a two-tier architecture: a high-level
Planner
generates strategic commands, and a low-level
Executor
translates them into JSON actions.
However, the Planner may produce commands violating resource constraints or referencing invalid units, while the Executor can generate syntactically incorrect or infeasible actions.
To mitigate these errors, StarEvolve integrates
Verifiers
for both components, enabling iterative self-correction.
The Planner’s Verifier validates strategic commands against expert rules, while the Executor’s Verifier ensures action syntax and feasibility.
Fig.
3
depicts this process.

Planner.

The Planner generates high-level strategic commands
c
in natural language, such as “Build a Barracks”, based on the game observations
o
from SC2Arena.
To enhance command quality, the Planner’s Verifier, also an LLM, evaluates each command.
If any rule is violated, the Verifier offers structured feedback and suggestions
f_{\text{p}}
, allowing the Planner to iteratively refine its output.
This design is highly extensible and supports seamless integration of additional expert knowledge.
Appendix
B.1
provides detailed prompts and rule lists.

Executor.

The Executor translates the Planner’s natural language commands
c
into executable, low-level JSON actions
a
.
To ensure the feasibility of actions, the Executor’s Verifier validates each JSON output by checking (1) JSON syntax correction, (2) parameter and action validity, and (3) resource feasibility.
Invalid actions also trigger structured feedback
f_{\text{e}}
, enabling iterative self-correction.
Appendix
B.3
provides implementation details.

4.2
Self-Improvement via Fine-tuning

In this subsection, we design a self-improvement loop based on SFT as in Fig.
3
, which enables the agent to extract strategic insights from its own high-performing behaviors and enhance its performance.
We introduce the self-improvement loop from the following perspectives:

SFT Data Construction.

We construct the SFT dataset from winning game trajectories, where Chain-of-Thought (CoT) naturally emerges through in-game prompting.
To ensure dataset quality, we design a scoring function inspired by reinforcement learning.
Each action
a_{t}
at timestep
t
is evaluated based on its discounted future impact on core game metrics, including the converted mineral/vespene values of all units and structures, as well as the counts of army and mining units.
Formally, the score aggregates temporally discounted gains over a 20-step horizon (30 seconds), where
M
denotes the metric set and
V_{m}(o_{t})
represents the value of metric
m
on the game observation
o_{t}
:

\text{score}(a_{t})=\sum_{k=1}^{20}\gamma^{k}\sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}}\left(V_{m}(o_{t+k})-V_{m}(o_{t})\right),~\gamma=0.95

(1)

To ensure comparability, raw metrics are normalized to zero mean and unit variance.
Actions with scores above a predefined threshold are retained, prioritizing those that optimize both short-term tactical benefits and long-term strategic impact.
Appendix
A.4
provides the full evaluated metric list and more details.

Fine-tuning Planner and Executor.

To enhance performance, we train the Planner and Executor with verifier-guided rejection sampling.
Specifically, the Planner is trained on
\langle
observation
o_{t}
, verified high-level command
c_{t}
\rangle
pairs, while the Executor is trained on
\langle
(
observation
o_{t}
, high-level command
c_{t}
),
verified JSON action
a_{t}
\rangle
pairs.
Crucially, the SFT loss is computed solely on the final output of each module’s self-correction chain, masking all intermediate refinement steps.
This approach mimics rejection sampling: the Verifier iteratively discards suboptimal outputs, accepting only the terminal responses as training targets, thereby learning the optimal decisions.

Fine-tuning Planner’s Verifier.

To improve verification efficiency, we train the Planner’s Verifier using self-correction trajectories.
For each successfully verified high-level command
c^{+}
, we construct training data using the final accepted command and its immediate rejected predecessor
c^{-}
, in the form of
\langle
rejected command
c^{-}
+ feedback
f_{\text{p}}^{-}

\rightarrow
accepted command
c^{+}
+ feedback
f_{\text{p}}^{+}

\rangle
.
This method performs implicit contrastive learning, enabling the Verifier to better capture the boundary between valid and invalid commands compared to learning from isolated examples, thus enhancing learning effectiveness.

Implementation details.

We collect high-quality gameplay data to fine-tune the Planner, Executor, and Planner’s Verifier. Specifically, these data are merged into a single, unified dataset to train an LLM, enabling it to learn both decision-making and self-correction strategies. Details on the SFT process are provided in Appendix
B.4
.

5.2
Main Results

Agent-vs-Agent.

To evaluate the performance of StarEvolve against baseline agents, we conduct pairwise matches across 5 games each of Terran vs. Terran (TvT), Zerg vs. Zerg (ZvZ), and Protoss vs. Protoss (PvP), using the outcomes to calculate Elo ratings.
As shown in Fig.
4
, StarEvolve (SFT) achieves the highest Elo rating of 1211 for Terran, outperforming DeepSeek.
For Protoss and Zerg, StarEvolve (SFT) also achieves competitive ratings of 1080 and 1096.
These results highlight three key insights:
① SC2Arena effectively supports Elo-based agent ranking;
② StarEvolve demonstrates strong performance, outperforming most baselines;
and ③ fine-tuning significantly enhances performance, enabling StarEvolve to match or surpass DeepSeek in specific scenarios.

Agent-vs-Built-In AI.

Furthermore, we evaluated the performance of StarEvolve without SFT using Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Qwen3-8B (no_think), DeepSeek-V3-0324, and fine-tuned StarEvolve with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, following the fine-tuning process in Section
4.2
.
We use metrics in Section
3.3
, excluding Elo, for evaluation.
As shown in Table
2
, StarEvolve consistently outperforms challenging built-in AIs across diverse LLMs, demonstrating its robustness.
Fine-tuning further improves performance: for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, fine-tuning increases the win rate from 55% to 71% at LV6 and reduces token consumption from 2298 to 1451.
At the more challenging LV7, the fine-tuned Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct model achieves a 73% win rate, approaching DeepSeek’s performance.
These findings demonstrate that StarEvolve is effective across models and scenarios, with significant improvements after fine-tuning.

Comparison with prior work.

We compare our method with
Ma et al. 2024
in the Protoss vs. Zerg setting, the primary focus of their study, using 20 matches.
As shown in Table
5
, despite leveraging stronger GPT-based LLMs,
Ma et al. 2024
achieves lower win rates than our approach.
Notably,
Ma et al. 2024
simplifies the problem: their agent generates only high-level decisions, while low-level actions are executed via predefined scripts, thereby reducing game complexity.
In contrast, our method outperforms this baseline with a smaller model while addressing the more challenging full low-level action space.

5.3
Ablation Study

SC2Arena’s optimized observation.

To evaluate the impact of our observation optimization techniques, we conducted ablation experiments by removing two key components: unit ordering and aggregation.
We performed 20 TvT, ZvZ, and PvP matches for each configuration and analyzed the results.
As shown in Table
8
in Appendix
E
, removing these techniques significantly degrades performance.
Specifically, removing unit ordering impairs spatial relationship modeling or aggregation, reducing the Valid Action Ratio (VAR).
This suggests the critical role of both techniques in enabling efficient decision-making within StarCraft II’s complex environment.

Self-correction mechanism and the Planner.

We ablated the Verifier and Planner modules to assess their contributions by conducting 20 TvT, ZvZ, and PvP matches for each configuration.
As shown in Table
3
, disabling the Verifier module reduced the win rate by 18% and VAR by 30%.
Removing the Planner module led to a 15% win rate in all matches.
This shows the essential role of hierarchical self-correction and strategic planning in robust decision-making.

To evaluate the effectiveness of the scoring function, we trained on all winning matches instead of using the function to filter experimental data.
We conducted 20 TvT matches and analyzed the results.
As shown in Table

, removing the scoring function significantly decreases performance, with win rates dropping from 73% to 41% and VAR decreasing by 33%.
These results highlight that, even when distilling high-quality data from high-performance models like DeepSeek, selecting the best data remains essential.
Our scoring function effectively identifies key game indicators, ensuring better data selection.

A.1
Introduction to StarCraft II

In this subsection, we provide a concise overview of StarCraft II, a landmark real-time strategy (RTS) game developed by Blizzard Entertainment. Renowned for its strategic complexity, tactical depth, and asymmetrical balance between three distinct races, StarCraft II has become a benchmark for AI research in dynamic, partially observable environments requiring real-time decision-making.

Basic Interface.

The game features a top-down view of the battlefield, with a primary map display, a minimap for global awareness, and mouse-and-keyboard controls. Players use the mouse to select units, issue commands, and navigate the map, while the keyboard enables rapid actions through hotkeys, such as unit production and camera movement. The minimap allows monitoring of multiple locations simultaneously, facilitating quick responses. Resources, including minerals and vespene gas, are displayed alongside a command panel that provides access to unit and building functions.

Game Objectives and Core Gameplay.

The objective is to defeat opponents by destroying their bases while protecting one’s own. Success requires a combination of strategic planning and tactical execution:

•

Strategy:

Players manage resource collection, base expansion, and technological progression to unlock advanced units and upgrades. Securing additional resource nodes is essential for sustained production, while efficient resource allocation ensures competitiveness over time.

•

Tactics:

Combat relies on precise unit control, such as positioning, micro-management (e.g., dodging attacks or optimizing unit abilities), and multi-tasking across multiple fronts. The fog of war, which obscures unscouted areas, adds to the challenge by limiting available information, requiring adaptive decision-making under time constraints.

Race Mechanisms.

The game’s three asymmetric races—Terran, Zerg, and Protoss—offer distinct playstyles while maintaining competitive balance:

•

Terran:

A versatile, mechanically focused race with strong defensive capabilities. Terran units and buildings can be repaired, and structures like bunkers and siege tanks excel at controlling key areas. Add-ons and mobile buildings enhance adaptability, enabling positional advantages.

•

Zerg:

An aggressive, swarm-based race that emphasizes rapid production and map control. Units are spawned from larvae at hatcheries, requiring efficient resource management and expansion. Zerg structures must be built on creep, an organic terrain that spreads dynamically, encouraging offensive gameplay.

•

Protoss:

A technologically advanced race featuring powerful but costly units. Protoss structures require energy supplied by pylons, and units are warped in rather than produced conventionally. Shields that regenerate over time promote hit-and-run tactics, while warp gates allow rapid reinforcement, favoring decisive engagements.

The combination of economic strategy, technological progression, and tactical combat, along with the unique characteristics of each race, creates a highly dynamic and competitive environment. StarCraft II epitomizes the challenges of real-time multi-agent systems, making it an enduring platform for AI research.

A.2
Formulation of SC2Arena’s Observation and Action

SC2Arena provides a standardized text-based interface designed for LLM agents. Observations are presented as hierarchical textual summaries of the game state, employing two key optimizations to minimize cognitive load while retaining essential information:

•

Unit aggregation
, which groups units performing similar tasks (e.g., mining SCVs), and

•

Proximity-based unit ordering
, inspired by the Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP), which sequences units and buildings based on spatial proximity, starting from the Command Center. This structure forms an explicit topological map, aiding LLMs in understanding functional dependencies (e.g., Barracks-TechLab) and battlefield positions without processing raw game data. Actions are encoded in a unified JSON format, allowing for precise multi-unit commands.

Observation Example.

The textualized observation encompasses the following information: current game settings (time, race, map), resource status (minerals, vespene, supply), the state of allied units, abilities of allied units/buildings, the status of visible enemy units/buildings, descriptions of allied abilities, history of allied actions, and the map resources distribution.

SC2Arena is built on the widely used Python-sc2 library, which provides a Python API for interacting with the StarCraft II game client via its raw API.

Running experiments in SC2Arena involves two key components: the agent (LLM) and the environment (game client).

Agent (LLM Server)
: Our experiments used a server with an AMD EPYC 64-Core CPU, 503GB RAM, and six NVIDIA A100 (80GB) GPUs, running Ubuntu 18.04, Python 3.9+, and necessary deep learning frameworks. Detailed environmental requirements are provided in our anonymous code repository.

Environment (Game Client)
: The StarCraft II game client can run on a standard desktop or laptop computer. A local installation of StarCraft II on a compatible operating system (Windows or macOS) is required.

A.4
SC2Arena’s Performance Metrics

In this subsection, we describe the evaluation metrics used for SC2Arena.
First, we explain the calculation of the Elo rating.
Then, we introduce the other metrics and provide their formulation.

Elo Rating System.

We employ the standard Elo rating system to quantify agent performance in agent-vs-agent comparisons. Each agent starts with an initial rating of
R_{0}=1000
. After a match between agent
A
(rating
R_{A}
) and agent
B
(rating
R_{B}
), their ratings update as:

1.

Expected Score Calculation:

A
’s expected score is derived from the rating difference:

E_{A}=\frac{1}{1+10^{(R_{B}-R_{A})/400}}

B
’s expected score is
E_{B}=1-E_{A}
.

2.

Actual Score Assignment:

The outcome
S_{A}
is assigned as:

\displaystyle S_{A}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1.0&\text{win}\\
0.5&\text{tie}\\
0.0&\text{loss}\end{cases},

\displaystyle S_{B}

\displaystyle=1-S_{A}.

3.

Rating Update:

New ratings are calculated using:

\displaystyle R^{\prime}_{A}

\displaystyle=R_{A}+K\cdot(S_{A}-E_{A}),

\displaystyle R^{\prime}_{B}

\displaystyle=R_{B}+K\cdot(S_{B}-E_{B}),

where
K=32
is the update sensitivity factor.

Matches are processed in random order. Ratings update sequentially after each match, with new agents initialized to
R_{0}
upon first appearance. Final rankings are determined by descending order of converged ratings.

Other metrics.

Then, we introduce the other metrics and provide their formulation.

1.

Win Rate (WR)
: The proportion of games won against a fixed set of opponents (e.g., built-in AIs). It reflects overall effectiveness in achieving victory under standardized conditions.

\mathrm{WR}=\frac{\text{Number of Wins}}{\text{Total Games Played}}\times 100\%

2.

Time Cost of Winning (TCW)
: Average duration of victorious games, measured in game seconds. It indicates strategic efficiency; lower TCW implies faster execution of winning strategies.

\mathrm{TCW}=\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{wins}}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{wins}}}T_{i}

where
T_{i}
is the duration of the
i
-th winning game.

3.

Supply Block Ratio (SBR)
: Fraction of game time during which unit production is halted due to insufficient supply capacity. It evaluates macro-management skill; lower SBR denotes optimal resource allocation and production continuity.

\mathrm{SBR}=\frac{\text{Time Supply Capped}}{\text{Total Game Time}}\times 100\%

4.

Resource Utilization Ratio (RUR)
: Average resources (minerals + gas) spent per game timestep. It quantifies economic efficiency; higher RUR indicates effective resource conversion into units/upgrades.

\mathrm{RUR}=\frac{\text{Total Resources Spent}}{\text{Total Game Timesteps}}

5.

Tokens Per Decision (TPD)
: Average output token count consumed by the LLM to generate a single in-game decision. It measures computational efficiency of the LLM agent; lower TPD implies leaner prompt engineering and faster inference.

\mathrm{TPD}=\frac{\text{Total Tokens Processed}}{\text{Total Decisions Made}}

6.

Valid Action Ratio (VAR)
: Proportion of agent decisions that are syntactically and semantically executable in the game environment. It assesses action robustness; higher VAR indicates better alignment with game rules and context awareness.

\mathrm{VAR}=\frac{\text{Valid Decisions}}{\text{Total Decisions Attempted}}\times 100\%

B.4
Supervised Fine-tuning

The supervised fine-tuning data selection process uses a scoring function controlled by two hyperparameters: the forward step size (
K
) and the discount factor (
\gamma
).
Specifically,
K
is set to 20, corresponding to a temporal window of approximately 30 seconds, chosen to capture the immediate effects of actions, while minimizing the influence of longer-term confounding factors.
The discount factor
\gamma
is adjusted such that the cumulative discount over this window is approximately 0.36, i.e.,
\gamma^{20}\approx 0.36
.
Scores are then Z-score normalized independently for each 30-second interval, and data points with standardized scores greater than 0.1 are selected for fine-tuning.

B.5
Implementation Details

The decision-making timing in StarEvolve is controlled by a two-tiered temporal trigger.
The primary trigger activates whenever the agent’s mineral supply exceeds 170, with decisions made at fixed intervals of 10 game iterations (approximately 1.5 seconds).
To ensure responsiveness, a secondary failsafe trigger guarantees that a decision is made at least once every 100 iterations, regardless of the primary trigger’s activation.
After each execution of the failsafe trigger, it is rescheduled for 100 iterations later.

In StarCraft II, workers constitute a significant portion of controllable units.
Delegating their tedious resource-gathering tasks to an LLM agent would hinder its decision-making capabilities.
To address this, StarEvolve employs a rule-based approach that periodically assigns workers to the nearest resource nodes based on the current levels of Minerals and Vespene Gas.
Additionally, units are programmed to automatically attack, prioritizing enemies with the lowest health.

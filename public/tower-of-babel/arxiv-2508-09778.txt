Recurrence for pretentious systems along generalized Pythagorean triples Thanks: The authors were supported by the Hellenic Foundation for Research and Innovation ELIDEK HFRI-NextGenerationEU-15689.
Nikos Frantzikinakis and Andreas Mountakis

Abstract
. We establish multiple recurrence results for pretentious measure-preserving multiplicative actions along generalized Pythagorean triples, that is, solutions to the equation a ​ x 2 + b ​ y 2 = c ​ z 2 ax^{2}+by^{2}=cz^{2} . This confirms the ergodic-theoretic form of the generalized Pythagorean partition regularity conjecture in this critical case of structured measure-preserving actions. As a consequence of our main theorem, any finite coloring of ℕ \mathbb{N} generated by the level sets of finitely many pretentious completely multiplicative functions, must contain a monochromatic generalized Pythagorean triple.

1.
Introduction and main results

1.1.
Introduction

A central principle in arithmetic Ramsey theory asserts that any finite
partition of the natural numbers must contain at least one cell exhibiting
significant internal structure. Here, by structure we mean the
existence of nontrivial solutions to algebraic equations or systems of
equations, with all variables lying in the same cell of the partition.

Definition 1.1
.

An equation
P(x,y,z)=0
is
partition regular
if, for every finite
partition of
\mathbb{N}
, there exist distinct
x,y,z
in the same cell that
satisfy the equation.

The partition regularity of linear equations is classically illustrated by the
results of Schur
[
25
]
and van der Waerden
[
26
]
,
and was substantially
extended by Rado
[
24
]
, who gave a complete classification of
systems of linear equations that are partition regular.

Definition 1.2
.

We say that
(a,b,c)\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
is a
Rado triple
if
a=c
,

b=c
, or
a+b=c
.

In the case of a single linear equation, Rado’s criterion asserts that

(1.1)

ax+by=cz

is partition regular if and only if
(a,b,c)
is a Rado triple.

Polynomial equations pose far greater challenges in the study of partition
regularity. The most prominent example is the long-standing open problem of
Erdős and Graham
[
16
,
17
]
, which asks whether the
Pythagorean equation
x^{2}+y^{2}=z^{2}
is partition regular.
More generally, one may ask about the partition regularity of broader classes
of homogeneous quadratic equations in three variables. This problem captures
the essential structural difficulties of the nonlinear setting and has become
a focus of considerable recent research. In this direction we state the
following conjecture:

Conjecture 1
.

If
a,b,c\in\mathbb{N}
, then

(1.2)

ax^{2}+by^{2}=cz^{2}

is partition regular if and only if
(a,b,c)
is a Rado triple.

The necessity of the Rado triple condition follows from the fact that
partition regularity of (
1.2
) implies partition regularity of its
linear counterpart (
1.1
). The sufficiency, however, is far less
understood: no instance of a Rado triple
(a,b,c)
is currently known for which
partition regularity of (
1.2
) has been established. Depending on
the coefficients, the problem exhibits a range of distinct and often
deeply challenging behaviors.
In
[
12
]
, among other results, the authors
prove partition regularity with respect to the pair
(x,y)
for the equation

16x^{2}+9y^{2}=z^{2}
; that is, for every finite partition of
\mathbb{N}
there
exist distinct
x,y
in the same cell and
z\in\mathbb{N}
such that the
equation is satisfied. In
[
14
]
, partition regularity with respect
to
(x,y)
and with respect to
(y,z)
is established for the Pythagorean equation

x^{2}+y^{2}=z^{2}
. This result was later extended in
[
13
]
to
partition regularity of (
1.2
) with respect to
(x,y)
and
(y,z)

whenever
a=c
or
b=c
. For further results on the partition regularity of quadratic
equations, see

[
1
,
3
,
4
,
7
,
9
,
10
,
19
,
20

21

The techniques developed in

14
]
are
inherently limited to pair configurations and do not extend to partition
regularity problems involving triples. To overcome this obstacle, we employ the
framework of ergodic theory to formulate sufficient conditions for the partition

Definition 1.3
.

A
multiplicative action
is a quadruple
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T_{n})
,
where
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu)
is a probability space and, for

n\in\mathbb{N}

T_{n}\colon X\to X
are invertible,
measure-preserving, and satisfy
T_{1}=\mathrm{id}
and

T_{m}\circ T_{n}=T_{mn}
for all

. This uniquely
determines a
(\mathbb{Q}_{>0},\times)
-action on
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu)

defined by
T_{m/n}:=T_{m}\circ T_{n}^{-1}

Using a variant of Furstenberg’s correspondence
principle
[
15

for
dilation-invariant densities from
[
2
]

together with the fact that
partition regularity of homogeneous equations follows from the existence of
solutions in every multiplicatively syndetic subset of the integers,
Conjecture
1
reduces to the following multiple recurrence statement:

Conjecture 2

(Ergodic variant of Conjecture
1
)
.

Let
(a,b,c)
be a Rado triple. Then for every multiplicative action

(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T_{n})
and every set
A\in\mathcal{X}
with

X=\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}T_{j}^{-1}A

distinct

such that

\mu\bigl(T_{x}^{-1}A\cap T_{y}^{-1}A\cap T_{z}^{-1}A\bigr)>0\quad\text{and}\quad ax^{2}+by^{2}=cz^{2}.

If
a+b\neq c
, simple examples show this multiple recurrence can fail for certain positive measure sets
A
. For instance, if
a=b=c=1
, take the multiplicative action by dilations on
\mathbb{T}
with the Haar measure

T_{n}x=n^{2}x\pmod{1}
, and
A=[1/3,2/3]
.

1.2.
Main results

In this paper we establish Conjecture
2
for a
class of structured systems the
pretentious multiplicative actions
. Although not the
most technically demanding case, they are the most plausible setting for
counterexamples to nonlinear partition regularity of homogeneous equations,
making them a critical test case. Verifying the
conjecture in this setting provides strong evidence for both
Conjectures
1
and
2
.

Pretentious systems in multiplicative dynamics play a role analogous to that of
rational Kronecker systems in the additive setting. The analogy is useful but
imperfect: in Kronecker systems, multiple recurrence is typically straightforward,
whereas pretentious systems possess a richer structure, making their analysis
substantially more delicate. The notation for the next definition is given in
Sections
2.2
and
2.4
.

Definition 1.4
.

A multiplicative action
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n})
is
pretentious
if the spectral
measure of every
F\in L^{2}(\mu)
is supported on the set of pretentious
multiplicative functions.
1

1

Given a measure space
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu)

\mu(X\setminus E)=0

The simplest example of a pretentious multiplicative action is a rotation by a
pretentious multiplicative function: if
f\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
is pretentious
and

carries the Haar measure, define

(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n})

Our main result, stated below, remains nearly as difficult to prove even in the
simpler setting of multiplicative rotations by several pretentious multiplicative
functions. We choose to work in the broader framework of pretentious multiplicative actions,
as this is likely to be a necessary intermediate step toward establishing
Conjecture

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(a,b,c)
be a Rado triple. For every pretentious multiplicative action

(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n})
and every set
A\in{\mathcal{X}}
with
\mu(A)>0
, the following
holds: for any
\varepsilon>0
there exist distinct
x,y,z\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\mu\bigl(A\cap T^{-1}_{x}A\cap T^{-1}_{y}A\cap T^{-1}_{z}A\bigr)\geq(\mu(A))^{4}-\varepsilon\quad\text{and}\quad ax^{2}+by^{2}=cz^{2}.

Remark 1
.

Our argument extends, without substantive changes, to the setting of three
pretentious multiplicative actions
T_{n},S_{n},R_{n}
acting on the same
probability space
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu)
, without assuming commutativity. Moreover, we
show that the conclusion holds for many triples
x,y,z\in{\mathbb{N}}
; precise
formulations appear in Theorems
4.1
and
5.1
.

For a general multiplicative action
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n})
, the functions

F\in L^{2}(\mu)
whose spectral measure is supported on pretentious
multiplicative functions form the
pretentious factor
of the system (see

[
11
, Theorem 2.4]
). Theorem
1.1
therefore establishes
multiple recurrence along generalized Pythagorean triples within this factor.

We deduce the following corollary, which confirms
Conjecture
1
in the case where the partition is defined by the level
sets of finitely many pretentious multiplicative functions.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
(a,b,c)
be a Rado triple, and let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{s}\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}

be pretentious completely multiplicative functions. Then for every open arc
I

of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
containing
1
there exist distinct
x,y,z\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

ax^{2}+by^{2}=cz^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad f_{j}(x),f_{j}(y),f_{j}(z)\in I\quad\text{for }j=1,\ldots,s.

Proof.

Let

(1.3)

I_{\delta}:=\{e^{2\pi it}\colon t\in(-\delta,\delta)\}

for some

\delta\in(0,1/4)

I_{2\delta}\subset I
.
Let
X:=(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{s}
, let
{\mathcal{X}}
be the Borel
\sigma

X
, and
let
\mu
denote the Haar measure on
X
. For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
z_{1},\ldots,z_{s}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}

T_{n}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{s}):=(f_{1}(n)z_{1},\ldots,f_{s}(n)z_{s}).

We claim that
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n})
is a pretentious multiplicative action.
Indeed, the spectral measure of each character of
X
is supported on the set

\{f_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots f_{s}^{k_{s}}\colon k_{1},\ldots,k_{s}\in{\mathbb{Z}}\},

which consists entirely of pretentious, completely multiplicative functions.
Since linear combinations of characters are dense in

Let
A:=I_{\delta}\times\cdots\times I_{\delta}

\mu(A)>0

provides distinct

Consequently, for each

This completes the proof.

1.3.
Proof strategy

We begin with a rough outline of our general proof strategy; for the
Pythagorean equation, a more detailed sketch appears in
Section
3
.

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we work with solutions of

ax^{2}+by^{2}=cz^{2}
in the parametric form

x=k\,P_{1}(m,n),\quad y=k\,P_{2}(m,n),\quad z=k\,P_{3}(m,n),\quad k,m,n\in{\mathbb{N}},

where
P_{j}
,
j\in\{1,2,3\}
, are binary quadratic forms that will be explicitly described later.
The key averages to analyze are
(averaging notation is given in Section
1.5
):

(1.4)

{\mathbb{E}}_{(m,n)\in[N]^{2}}\int F\cdot T_{P_{1}(m,n)}F\cdot T_{P_{2}(m,n)}F\cdot T_{P_{3}(m,n)}F\,d\mu.

When the action is ergodic and finitely generated, we restrict the averaging
to a two-dimensional grid
(Qm+1,Qn)
, where
Q\in{\mathbb{N}}
is taken to be highly
divisible.
Applying the spectral theorem together with the concentration estimates for
multiplicative functions given in Propositions
2.1

and
2.2
, we find that for at least two indices

j\in\{1,2,3\}
, the term
T_{P_{j}(Qm+1,Qn)}F
in
(
1.4
) can be replaced by
F
, while the third term can
be replaced either by
F
itself or by
T_{aQn}F
for some
a\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Taking a multiplicative average over
Q
then yields a grid for which the following lower bound is achieved

\int F\,d\mu\cdot\int F^{3}\,d\mu-\varepsilon\ \geq\ \Big(\int F\,d\mu\Big)^{4}-\varepsilon,

completing the proof.

This outline describes the argument in the finitely generated case.
For general actions, two significant new difficulties arise:

(i)

Archimedean characters.

Non-concentration phenomena appear for functions whose spectral measures
are supported on Archimedean characters. As in earlier work, we address
this by introducing a cutoff
S_{\delta}
(see (
2.39
)) that,
for sufficiently small
\delta
, localizes the
Archimedean characters, so the corresponding component of the functions becomes concentrated around itself.

(ii)

1
-pretentious oscillating multiplicative functions.

A greater challenge comes from functions whose spectral measures are
supported on
1
-pretentious oscillating multiplicative functions (see the example after
Proposition

2.1
).
Our new crucial idea here is to average over a grid
(Qm+1,Qn+v)
, carefully
choosing
Q
and
v=v(Q_{1},Q_{2},Q_{3})
to satisfy congruences such as those in

Lemma

4.2
. This allows us to factor out highly divisible,
pairwise coprime terms
Q_{j}
from each
P_{j}(Qm+1,Qn+v)
for
j\in\{1,2,3\}
.
We then perform a triple multiplicative averaging over the
Q_{j}
,
thereby eliminating the contribution from the oscillating component of
F
.
The resulting bound is

\int F\cdot F_{1}\cdot F_{2}\cdot F_{3}\,d\mu-\varepsilon,

where for
j\in\{1,2,3\}
the functions
F_{j}
are projections of
F
onto suitable
factors. The proof is completed by applying the estimate in

Proposition

2.9
.

The fact that
(a,b,c)
is a Rado triple plays a crucial role in the
preceding arguments. It ensures that we can parametrize the solutions to
(
1.2
) so that at least two of the quadratic forms

P_{j}
,
j\in\{1,2,3\}
, have leading coefficient
1
with respect to the variable
m
.
This property is essential even in the finitely generated case, as it allows
us to replace
T_{P_{j}(Qm+1,Qn+v)}F
by
F
for at least two values of
j
.
In the general case, it is also used to guarantee that the sets
S_{\delta}

have positive upper density.

To illustrate with a concrete example why our method fails
when
(a,b,c)
is not a Rado triple, consider the equation

x^{2}+y^{2}=4z^{2}
, which admits the parametrization

x=k\,2(m^{2}-n^{2}),\quad y=k\,4mn,\quad z=k\,(m^{2}+n^{2}),\quad k,m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

This equation is partition regular with respect to each of the pairs

(x,y)
,
(y,z)
, and
(x,z)
(see
[
13
, Theorem 1.2]
), but it
is not partition regular with respect to
(x,y,z)
since
(1,1,4)
is not a
Rado triple.
If the action is finitely generated and ergodic, our argument yields a lower
bound of the form

\int F\,d\mu\cdot\int F^{2}\cdot T_{2}F\,d\mu,

and the presence of the term
T_{2}F
in place of
F
prevents positivity, as
expected.

The periodic completely multiplicative functions are precisely the

, which we typically denote by
\chi
. For our context, we define the
conductor
of
\chi
to be its smallest period.
We let

{\mathcal{M}}:=\{f\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}\;:\;f\text{ is completely multiplicative}\}

and endow
{\mathcal{M}}
with the topology of pointwise
convergence, under which
{\mathcal{M}}
is a compact metric space.

Following the terminology of Granville and Soundararajan

18

, given two multiplicative functions

f,g\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{U}}
, we define their
pretentious distance

between two real numbers

by

(2.7)

{\mathbb{D}}(f,g;A,B):=\Big(\sum_{A<p\leq B}\frac{1}{p}\big(1-\Re\big(f(p)\,\overline{g(p)}\big)\Big)^{1/2}.

We set
{\mathbb{D}}(f,g):=\lim\limits_{X\to\infty}{\mathbb{D}}(f,g;1,X)
, and say
that
f

pretends
to be
g
, denoted

, if

{\mathbb{D}}(f,g)<+\infty
.

Definition 2.1
.

A multiplicative function

\chi
and a real number

We denote by

.

By

.

Given

2.3.
Spectral measures

Note that
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-valued completely multiplicative functions on

{\mathbb{Q}}_{>0}
are uniquely determined by their values on
{\mathbb{N}}
, so we may
identify
{\mathcal{M}}
with the Pontryagin dual of the discrete group

({\mathbb{Q}}_{>0},\times)
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n})
be a multiplicative action and let
F\in L^{2}(\mu)
.
The map

({\mathbb{Q}}_{>0},\times)\to{\mathbb{C}},\quad r/s\mapsto\int_{X}T_{r}F\cdot T_{s}\overline{F}\,d\mu

is well-defined and positive definite. By the Bochner-Herglotz theorem,
there exists a finite Borel measure
\sigma_{F}
on
{\mathcal{M}}

(called the
spectral measure
of
F
) such that

\int_{X}T_{r}F\cdot T_{s}\overline{F}\,d\mu=\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}f(r)\cdot\overline{f(s)}\,d\sigma_{F}(f)\quad\text{for all }r,s\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{>0}.

From the above, we can easily deduce the following identity, which we will use frequently:

(2.12)

\Bigg\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{l}c_{k}T_{r_{k}}F\Bigg\rVert_{L^{2}(\mu)}=\Bigg\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{l}c_{k}f({r_{k}})\Bigg\rVert_{L^{2}(\sigma_{F}(f))}

for all
l\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
c_{1},\ldots,c_{l}\in{\mathbb{C}}
,

r_{1},\ldots,r_{l}\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{>0}
, where, for
f\in{\mathcal{M}}
and
r=m/n\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{>0}
,

f(r)=f(m)/f(n)=f(m)\overline{f(n)}
.
From (
2.12
), we immediately obtain that for all
r\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{>0}
,

(2.13)

\lVert T_{r}F-F\rVert_{L^{2}(\mu)}=\lVert f({r})-1\rVert_{L^{2}(\sigma_{F}(f))}.

\text{ for all but finitely many

If

Lemma 2.3

and

{\mathcal{M}}_{p,\textup{fin.supp.},P}

.

Proof.

We first prove that
{\mathcal{M}}_{p,\textup{fin. supp.}}

Given

The function

countable infimum of measurable functions.
3
3

3

Each series in this

It follows that

For each

Since the set of Dirichlet characters is countable, we deduce that

{\mathcal{M}}_{p,\textup{fin.supp.}}
is Borel measurable.

If, in the previous argument, we instead define

then the same reasoning shows that
{\mathcal{M}}_{p,\textup{fin. supp.},P}

also Borel measurable.
This completes the proof.

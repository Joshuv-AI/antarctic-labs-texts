Multi-Particle Invariant Mass — Standard Expressions and Corrections to Order ( m / E ) 4 (m/E)^{4}
M.P. Fewell ARC Centre of Excellence for Dark-Matter Particle Physics and Department of Physics, Adelaide University, Adelaide, South Australia 5005, Australia

Abstract
In collider-based particle physics, invariant mass refers to the magnitude of the total-momentum 4-vector of a system of particles. An expression for the invariant mass of a 2-particle system is well known; it assumes that both the total energy E E and the transverse momentum p T p_{\mathrm{T}} of each particle in the system greatly exceed its mass m m . This note explores these assumptions by computing correction terms in powers of m / E m/E up to order ( m / E ) 4 (m/E)^{4} . The assumptions are found to be robust: not only is the leading correction quadratic in m / E m/E , but also cancellations reduce its coefficient and that of the next-to-leading correction, which is of order ( m / E ) 4 (m/E)^{4} . Three- and four-particle systems are also treated and the generalisation to larger numbers of particles indicated. The zeroth-order expressions for these multi-particle systems are remarkably simple; they deserve to be better known.

Two particles

2.2
Three and four particles, and more

From eq. (
1
), the invariant mass of a system of three particles is

m^{2}_{123}=\sum_{\mu}(p^{\mu}_{1}+p^{\mu}_{2}+p^{\mu}_{3})(p_{\mu 1}+p_{\mu 2}+p_{\mu 3}).

(9)

Expanding this out gives

m^{2}_{123}=(E_{1}+E_{2}+E_{3})^{2}-(p_{\mathrm{x}1}+p_{\mathrm{x}2}+p_{\mathrm{x}3})^{2}\\
-(p_{\mathrm{y}1}+p_{\mathrm{y}2}+p_{\mathrm{y}3})^{2}-(p_{\mathrm{z}1}+p_{\mathrm{z}2}+p_{\mathrm{z}3})^{2}.

Applying the approximations
E\gg m
and
p_{\mathrm{T}}\gg m
:

\displaystyle m^{2}_{123}=(

\displaystyle p_{\mathrm{T}1}\cosh\eta_{1}+p_{\mathrm{T}2}\cosh\eta_{2}+p_{\mathrm{T}3}\cosh\eta_{3})^{2}

\displaystyle-(p_{\mathrm{T}1}\sinh\eta_{1}+p_{\mathrm{T}2}\sinh\eta_{2}+p_{\mathrm{T}3}\sinh\eta_{3})^{2}

\displaystyle-(p_{\mathrm{T}1}\cos\phi_{1}+p_{\mathrm{T}2}\cos\phi_{2}+p_{\mathrm{T}3}\cos\phi_{3})^{2}

\displaystyle-(p_{\mathrm{T}1}\sin\phi_{1}+p_{\mathrm{T}2}\sin\phi_{2}+p_{\mathrm{T}3}\sin\phi_{3})^{2},

which, on multiplying out, reduces to

m^{2}_{123}=m^{2}_{12}+m^{2}_{13}+m^{2}_{23}\,.

(10)

A similar analysis gives the square of the invariant mass of a system of four particles as the sum of the squared invariant masses of the pairwise combinations:

m^{2}_{1234}=m^{2}_{12}+m^{2}_{13}+m^{2}_{14}+m^{2}_{23}+m^{2}_{24}+m^{2}_{34}\,.

(11)

The generalisation to five and more particles is clear.

3.2
Expanding the surd

The surd in eq. (
4

\sqrt{p^{2}_{\mathrm{T}}+m^{2}}=p_{\mathrm{T}}\sqrt{1+\left(\frac{m}{p_{\mathrm{T}}}\right)^{2}}.

Now

p_{\mathrm{T}}=p\sin\theta=\sqrt{E^{2}-m^{2}}\sin\theta.

Hence, in a form suitable for the application of Taylor’s theorem,

\begin{split}\sqrt{p^{2}_{\mathrm{T}}+m^{2}}&=p_{\mathrm{T}}\sqrt{1+\frac{(m/E)^{2}}{\sin^{2}\theta\,[1-(m/E)^{2}]}}\\[5.69046pt]
&\approx p_{\mathrm{T}}\sqrt{1+\frac{m^{2}}{E^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}\left[1+\left(\frac{m}{E}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{m}{E}\right)^{4}\right]}\\[5.69046pt]

\begin{split}\sqrt{p^{2}_{\mathrm{T}}+m^{2}}&\approx p_{\mathrm{T}}\left[1+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{m}{E\sin\theta}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{m^{4}}{E^{4}\sin^{2}\theta}-\frac{1}{8}\left(\frac{m}{E\sin\theta}\right)^{4}\right]\\[4.26773pt]

as the sum of
m^{2}_{1,ij}
values. However, counting terms in the expression for the sum
(E_{1}+E_{2}+E_{3})^{2}
reveals a complication: the sum has only nine terms of order
(m/E)^{2}
, but each
m^{2}_{1,ij}
contains four such terms (eq.
19
), so a sum of three requires twelve terms. The remedy is to add and subtract the three extra terms needed, giving

m^{2}_{1,123}=m^{2}_{1,12}+m^{2}_{1,13}+m^{2}_{1,23}-\sum_{i=1}^{3}p_{\mathrm{T}i}^{2}\cosh^{2}\eta_{i}\left(\frac{m_{i}}{E_{i}}\right)^{2}.

(21)

The form of eq. (
21
) suggests that the leading correction to the three-particle invariant mass is, in fractional terms, smaller than that for two particles, since the three terms subtracted are positive-definite.

3.4.3
Four and More Particles

Like for three particles, counting correction terms provides a pointer to the result for four particles. Writing as before

m^{2}_{1234}\approx m^{2}_{0,1234}+m^{2}_{1,1234}\,,

(22)

the zeroth-order expression, eq. (
11
), contains six pairwise combinations of the four particles. A sum of six corresponding combinations of eq. (
19
) furnishes 24 correction terms, but
(E_{1}+E_{2}+E_{3}+E_{4})^{2}
provides only sixteen terms of order
(m/E)^{2}
. Explicit evaluation shows that the eight additional terms required have analogous form to the three additional terms in eq. (
21
). The result is

m^{2}_{1,1234}=m^{2}_{1,12}+m^{2}_{1,13}+m^{2}_{1,14}+m^{2}_{1,23}+m^{2}_{1,24}+m^{2}_{1,34}\\
-2\sum_{i=1}^{4}p_{\mathrm{T}i}^{2}\cosh^{2}\eta_{i}\left(\frac{m_{i}}{E_{i}}\right)^{2}.

(23)

This expression suggests a furtherance of the surmise in the comment following eq. (
21
): the first-order correction to the four-particle value seems smaller again, fractionally, than that for three particles; for here eight positive-definite terms are subtracted from a sum of six two-particle corrections, compared to three from three in eq. (
21
).

Comparison of eqs (
21
) and (
23
) leads to the conjecture that the correction term for the invariant mass of a five-particle system may be

m^{2}_{1,12345}=m^{2}_{1,12}+m^{2}_{1,13}+m^{2}_{1,14}+m^{2}_{1,15}+m^{2}_{1,23}+m^{2}_{1,24}+m^{2}_{1,25}\\
+m^{2}_{1,34}+m^{2}_{1,35}+m^{2}_{1,45}-3\sum_{i=1}^{5}p_{\mathrm{T}i}^{2}\cosh^{2}\eta_{i}\left(\frac{m_{i}}{E_{i}}\right)^{2},

(24)

based on the patterns of the algebra in the derivations of eqs (
21
) and (
23
) and on counts of correction terms. However, readers requiring the result should check explicitly.

3.4.4
Sensitivity to Polar Angle

In §
2.1
it is surmised that the corrections should show sensitivity to momenta directed nearly parallel to the z axis; that is, that the correction terms should involve
\eta
. This does not refer to the
\cosh\eta
factors in eqs (
19
), (
21
), (
23
) and (
24
); for, although
\cosh\eta\rightarrow e^{|\eta|}
as
\eta\rightarrow\pm\infty
, these factors are always multiplied by a
p_{\mathrm{T}}
, all of which go to zero in the same limit. Indeed, it is apparent from eq. (
15
) that, to zeroth order —  which is sufficient in this context —
p_{\mathrm{T}}\cosh\eta=E
. Hence the leading-order correction has no explicit dependence on
\eta
.

3.5
Corrections of Order
(m/E)^{4}

Since the leading correction does not show the surmised sensitivity to
\eta
, we look for it in the term of order
(m/E)^{4}
. Returning to the two-particle result of eq. (
17
), but this time keeping all terms in eqs (
15
) and (
16
):

\begin{split}(E_{1}+&E_{2})^{2}-(p_{\mathrm{z}1}+p_{\mathrm{z}2})^{2}\\[2.84544pt]
&\approx\left\{p_{\mathrm{T}1}\cosh\eta_{1}\left[1+\frac{m_{1}^{2}}{2E_{1}^{2}}+\frac{m_{1}^{4}}{8E_{1}^{4}}\left(3+\frac{4\cos^{3}\theta_{1}}{\sin^{4}\theta_{1}}\right)\right]\right.\\[4.26773pt]
&\left.\mspace{80mu}\mathrel{+}p_{\mathrm{T}2}\cosh\eta_{2}\left[1+\frac{m_{2}^{2}}{2E_{2}^{2}}+\frac{m_{2}^{4}}{8E_{2}^{4}}\left(3+\frac{4\cos^{3}\theta_{2}}{\sin^{4}\theta_{2}}\right)\right]\right\}^{2}\\[4.26773pt]
&-\left[p_{\mathrm{T}1}\sinh\eta_{1}\left(1+\frac{m_{1}^{4}}{2E_{1}^{4}}\;\frac{\cos\theta_{1}}{\sin^{4}\theta_{1}}\right)+p_{\mathrm{T}2}\sinh\eta_{2}\left(1+\frac{m_{2}^{4}}{2E_{2}^{4}}\;\frac{\cos\theta_{2}}{\sin^{4}\theta_{2}}\right)\right]^{2}.\\[2.84544pt]
\end{split}

Extending eq. (
18
) to include corrections
m^{2}_{2,12}
of order
(m/E)^{4}
:

m^{2}_{12}\approx m^{2}_{0,12}+m^{2}_{1,12}+m^{2}_{2,12}

(25)

and using (
5
) to replace
\theta
by
\eta
, one finds from the equation above that

\begin{split}m^{2}_{2,12}&=\left(p_{\mathrm{T}1}\cosh\eta_{1}+p_{\mathrm{T}2}\cosh\eta_{2}\right)\\[1.42271pt]
&\mspace{80mu}\times\left[\frac{p_{\mathrm{T}1}\cosh\eta_{1}}{4}\left(\frac{m_{1}}{E_{1}}\right)^{4}\left(3+4\sinh^{3}\eta_{1}\cosh\eta_{1}\right)\right.\\[2.84544pt]
&\left.\mspace{120mu}\mathrel{+}\frac{p_{\mathrm{T}2}\cosh\eta_{2}}{4}\left(\frac{m_{2}}{E_{2}}\right)^{4}\left(3+4\sinh^{3}\eta_{2}\cosh\eta_{2}\right)\right]\\
&\mathrel{+}\left[\frac{p_{\mathrm{T}1}\cosh\eta_{1}}{2}\left(\frac{m_{1}}{E_{1}}\right)^{2}+\frac{p_{\mathrm{T}2}\cosh\eta_{2}}{2}\left(\frac{m_{2}}{E_{2}}\right)^{2}\right]^{2}\\
&\mathrel{-}\left(p_{\mathrm{T}1}\sinh\eta_{1}+p_{\mathrm{T}2}\sinh\eta_{2}\right)\left[p_{\mathrm{T}1}\sinh^{2}\eta_{1}\cosh^{3}\eta_{1}\left(\frac{m_{1}}{E_{1}}\right)^{4}\right.\\
&\left.\mspace{250mu}\mathrel{+}p_{\mathrm{T}2}\sinh^{2}\eta_{2}\cosh^{3}\eta_{2}\left(\frac{m_{2}}{E_{2}}\right)^{4}\right].\end{split}

(26)

At first sight, eq. (
26
) has explicit
\eta
dependence in the terms where the combined power of
\sinh\eta
and
\cosh\eta
exceeds the power of
p_{\mathrm{T}}
, but the following analysis suggests that this is somewhat illusory.

Momenta directed nearly parallel to the z axis correspond to
\eta\rightarrow\pm\infty
, in which limit
\cosh\eta\rightarrow e^{|\eta|}
and
\sinh\eta\rightarrow\sgn\eta\,e^{|\eta|}
. In this limit,
p_{\mathrm{T}}\cosh\eta\approx~E
and
p_{\mathrm{T}}\sinh\eta\approx\sgn\eta\,E
. Applying this to both particles, eq. (
26
) approaches

\begin{split}m^{2}_{2,12}&\rightarrow\left(E_{1}+E_{2}\right)\left[\sgn\eta_{1}\>E_{1}\,e^{4|\eta_{1}|}\left(\frac{m_{1}}{E_{1}}\right)^{4}+\sgn\eta_{2}\>E_{2}\,e^{4|\eta_{2}|}\left(\frac{m_{2}}{E_{2}}\right)^{4}\right]\\
&\mspace{24mu}\mathrel{-}\left(\sgn\eta_{1}\>E_{1}+\sgn\eta_{2}\>E_{2}\right)\left[E_{1}\,e^{4|\eta_{1}|}\left(\frac{m_{1}}{E_{1}}\right)^{4}\right.\\[-7.11317pt]
&\left.\mspace{340mu}\mathrel{+}E_{2}\,e^{4|\eta_{2}|}\left(\frac{m_{2}}{E_{2}}\right)^{4}\right].\end{split}

(27)

This is plainly zero if both particles are travelling in the same direction (i.e. when
\sgn\eta_{1}=\sgn\eta_{2}
); remarkably, it is also zero if they are travelling in opposite directions. That is, the looked-for
\eta
dependence is not apparent where it might be expected to be largest, namely at large
\eta
.

The opposite limit is
\eta\rightarrow 0
, in which
\cosh\eta\rightarrow 1
and
\sinh\eta\rightarrow 0
. Applying this limit to both particles, one finds that eq. (
26
) approaches

\begin{split}m^{2}_{2,12}\rightarrow\>p^{2}_{\mathrm{T}1}\left(\frac{m_{1}}{E_{1}}\right)^{4}+\,p^{2}_{\mathrm{T}2}\left(\frac{m_{2}}{E_{2}}\right)^{4}&+\frac{3p_{\mathrm{T}1}p_{\mathrm{T}2}}{4}\left[\left(\frac{m_{1}}{E_{1}}\right)^{4}+\left(\frac{m_{2}}{E_{2}}\right)^{4}\right]\\[1.42271pt]
&\mathrel{+}\frac{p_{\mathrm{T}1}p_{\mathrm{T}2}}{2}\left(\frac{m_{1}}{E_{1}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{m_{2}}{E_{2}}\right)^{2}.\end{split}

(28)

This is clearly not zero. It therefore amounts to an identification of
\eta
dependence, since the previous paragraph shows that, in this same case of the limit applying to both particles,
m^{2}_{2,12}\rightarrow 0
as
\eta\rightarrow\pm\infty
.

The extension of this analysis to three and more particles is left as an exercise for the reader who needs it (or is curious).

4
Summary and Conclusion

Section
2
of this note recapitulates the standard derivations of expressions for the invariant mass of two-, three- and four-particle systems in terms of the measurable quantities
p_{\mathrm{T}}
,
\eta
and
\phi
. The two-particle expression is widely known; an internet search for the three- and four-particle expressions failed to find any result, suggesting that these are not as well known as their simplicity warrants.

The standard derivations make the assumptions
E\gg m
and
p_{\mathrm{T}}\gg m
. Section
3
explores corrections to these assumptions by expanding expressions in Taylor series in
m/E
, finding that the assumptions are more robust than might be naively expected:

•

The leading correction is of order
(m/E)^{2}
, rather than linear in
m/E
, and the next-to-leading correction is of order
(m/E)^{4}
.

•

The expressions furnish two sources of corrections which partly cancel to leading order, rendering the net correction for two particles smaller in magnitude that would be the case if either source acted alone.

•

The leading correction for three particles is smaller, in fractional terms, than that for two particles, and those for four and more particles smaller again.

•

The assumption
p_{\mathrm{T}}\gg m
suggests that the corrections should show explicit
\eta
dependence. There is none to leading order. At next-to-leading order, the
\eta
dependence is modest; in particular, it goes to zero in the situation where it might naively be expected to be largest, namely as
\eta\rightarrow\pm\infty
, i.e. when the momenta of the particles in a collider experiment are nearly aligned to the beam direction.

The centre-of-mass energy delivered by the Large Hadron Collider is so much greater than the mass of any known particle that the analysis of §
3
is entirely irrelevant to the practical analysis of experimental data. Nevertheless it it interesting to find that the assumptions underlying the standard derivation are robust: the corrections are smaller than one would expect at first sight.

DEEP: Edge-based Dataflow Processing with Hybrid Docker Hub and Regional Registries
Narges Mehran 1, Zahra Najafabadi Samani 3, Reza Farahani 4, Josef Hammer 5, Dragi Kimovski 4

Abstract
Reducing energy consumption is essential to lessen greenhouse gas emissions, conserve natural resources, and help mitigate the impacts of climate change. In this direction, edge computing, a complementary technology to cloud computing, extends computational capabilities closer to the data producers, enabling energy-efficient and latency-sensitive service delivery for end users. To properly manage data and microservice storage, expanding the Docker Hub registry to the edge using an AWS S3-compatible MinIO -based object storage service can reduce completion time and energy consumption. To address this, we introduce Docker rEgistry-based Edge dataflow Processing ( DEEP ) to optimize the energy consumption of microservice-based application deployments by focusing on deployments from Docker Hub and MinIO-based regional registries and their processing on edge devices. After applying nash equilibrium and benchmarking the execution of two compute-intensive machine learning (ML) applications of video and text processing, we compare energy consumption across three deployment scenarios: exclusively from Docker Hub, exclusively from the regional registry, and a hybrid method utilizing both. Experimental results show that deploying 83 % 83\text{\,}\mathrm{\%} of text processing microservices from the regional registry improves the energy consumption by 0.34 % 0.34\text{\,}\mathrm{\%} ( ≈ \approx 18 J 18\text{\,}\mathrm{J} ) compared to microservice deployments exclusively from Docker Hub.

I
Introduction

Edge computing addresses the need to reduce energy consumption by extending computational capabilities closer to where the data is produced. This reduces the energy required for data transfers to centralized cloud servers, enabling more energy-efficient and latency-sensitive services for end users. Moreover, edge computing allows non-time-critical tasks to be offloaded to the cloud, balancing local processing and centralized resource optimization. This combination supports sustainable and efficient service delivery while contributing to global efforts to reduce energy use and environmental impact.

To effectively support edge computing, Docker Hub
1
1

1

https://hub.docker.com/
serves as a widely used container registry for storing, managing, and sharing Docker images. It leverages a network of cloud data centers and content delivery networks (CDNs) to guarantee low latency and scalability for developers. While the locations of Docker Hub’s servers remain undisclosed, its CDN-based distribution model enables Docker images to be served geographically closer to end users, reducing download times.
This model aligns with other large-scale cloud services, combining centralized servers for data management with distributed point of presence to enhance delivery performance. Moreover, recently,
regional computing

[
1
]
was presented as a strategy for data processing and storage during
on-peak network hours
, with the ability to migrate processed data to cloud servers during
off-peak hours
, reducing network congestion.

Traditional methods
[
2
,
3
]
often rely on cloud-native Docker registries, which typically result in higher latency and energy consumption while overlooking the potential benefits of regional registries. Although some efforts have designed MinIO-based regional Docker registries
[
4
,
5
]
and caching mechanisms
[
6
,
7
]
to minimize download times, they rarely focus on incorporating energy-efficient microservice scheduling strategies.
To solve these challenges, we introduce
DEEP
, which investigates energy efficiency by addressing both the microservice image deployment from Docker registries and their dataflow processing on edge devices.
DEEP
explores the potential of a regional Docker registry in boosting environmental sustainability and energy efficiency.
More specifically,
DEEP
applies a
nash equilibrium
model to improve the energy consumption of compute-intensive ML applications, modeled as a directed acyclic graph (DAG) deployed from Docker registries. Therefore, the primary contributions of
DEEP
are:

•

Design and develop a regional registry for image retrieval;

•

Formulate a nash-equilibrium game-based model for microservice deployment on edge devices;

•

Benchmark and compare the energy consumption of the microservice deployments from two Docker registries and executions on a physical edge-based testbed.

II
Literature Review

Cloud-native Docker registry

Zerouali et al.
[
3
]
investigated which source registries host the most frequently used images, such as Busybox, MySQL, Nginx, Postgres, and Alpine, with the expectation that most stem from Docker Hub, with over
10
million images. Their findings show that Docker Hub is the most popular registry, GitHub is the second most popular, and Quay
[
8
]
is the third most popular.

Edge-driven regional Docker registry

Makris et al.
[
4
]
introduced a data placement method that minimizes completion time by leveraging an edge-driven Docker registry tailored for extended reality applications. With the development of augmented and virtual reality technologies, this method proposes an edge-located Docker registry that caches Docker images closer to the application’s users, reducing reliance on cloud-native registries that may impose extra delays.

Edge-driven cache-based scheduling

Khan et al.
[
6
]
introduced a task allocation strategy in fog computing, which bridges the gap between cloud and edge devices by leveraging caching mechanisms to reduce latency. This scheme stores frequently accessed data closer to the edge, minimizing repeated data transfers and improving response times. Hua et al.
[
7
]
proposed a data caching scheme that allocates fog resources closer to users by integrating proactive and reactive scheduling methods, enhancing resource efficiency and reducing latency.

Energy-aware ML application deployment

Mokhtari et al.
[
9
]
proposed a heuristics-based deployment method for ML applications on heterogeneous edge devices. This method minimizes task completion time while adhering to energy constraints for edge deployment, particularly latency-sensitive Internet of Things-based applications, e.g., SmartSight.

Research gap

Existing methods have focused on either solely cloud-native or regional registry

]
-

[

III-A

Application model

Dataflow processing application

is a DAG
\mathcal{A}=\left(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{E}\right)
consisting of
microservices
interdependent through
dataflows
:

1.

Microservices

\mathcal{M}=\left\{\left(m_{i},\mathtt{Size}_{m_{i}}\right)|0\leq i<\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{M}}\right\}
where
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{M}}
denotes the number of microservices in application
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathtt{Size}_{m_{i}}
is the
\mathrm{GB}
size of each containerized
m_{i}
;

2.

Dataflows

\mathcal{E}=\{\left(m_{u},m_{i},\mathit{df}_{ui}\right)
|
\left(m_{u},m_{i}\right)\in\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{M}\}
with
\mathtt{Size}_{ui}
(in
\mathrm{MB}
), transferring from an
upstage
microservice
m_{u}\in\mathcal{A}
to a
downstage
one
m_{i}\in\mathcal{A}
.

Requirements

\mathtt{req}\left(m_{i}\right)
for processing dataflows
\mathit{df}_{ui}
by a microservice
m_{i}
is a triple representing the minimum number of cores, memory, and storage sizes (in
\mathrm{GB}
):

\mathtt{req}\left(m_{i}\right)=\langle\mathtt{CORE}\left(m_{i}\right),\mathtt{CPU}\left(m_{i}\right),\mathtt{MEM}\left(m_{i}\right),\mathtt{STOR}\left(m_{i}\right)\rangle,

where
\mathtt{CPU}\left(m_{i}\right)
represents the minimum processing load, measured in millions of instructions (
\mathrm{M}\mathrm{I}
), required to process dataflows
\mathit{df}_{ui}
using
m_{i}
.

III-B

Device and network model

We model the set of heterogeneous capacity-constrained devices interconnected via network channels:

1.

Devices
\mathcal{D}=\left\{d_{j}|0\leq j<\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{D}}\right\}
represent a set of
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{D}}
physical edge devices, each characterized by
\mathtt{CORE}_{j}

cores
,
\mathtt{MEM}_{j}

memory
and
\mathtt{STOR}_{j}

storage
(in
\mathrm{GB}
) and
\mathtt{CPU}_{j}

processing speed
(in million of instructions per second (
\mathrm{M}\mathrm{I}\mathrm{/}\mathrm{s}
)):
d_{j}=\left(\mathtt{CORE}_{j},\mathtt{CPU}_{j},\mathtt{MEM}_{j},\mathtt{STOR}_{j}\right)
.

2.

Network channels
\mathcal{H}=\{h_{kj}\ |\ 0\leq k,j<\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{D}}\}
interconnect the devices, where each channel
h_{kj}=\mathtt{BW}_{kj}
models the network bandwidth
\mathtt{BW}_{kj}
for transferring a data unit between the devices
d_{k}
and
d_{j}
. For simplicity, this work neglects the network round-trip time (RTT), focusing exclusively on bandwidth for data transfer.

III-C

Registry model

We model the Docker image registries
\mathcal{R}=\left\{r_{g}|0\leq g<\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{R}}\right\}
to store the containerized microservices
m_{i}\in\mathcal{M}
where
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{R}}
shows the number of registries.
\mathtt{BW}_{gj}
represents the network bandwidth for transferring containerized microservices between the registry server
r_{g}
and device
d_{j}
, hosting microservice
m_{i}
.

III-D

Dataflow processing model

Dataflow processing consists of the following key metrics:

1.

Completion time

\mathtt{CT}\left(m_{i},r_{g},d_{j}\right)
for a dataflow
\mathit{df}_{ui}
processed by a microservice
m_{i}
, retrieved from a registry
r_{g}
, and scheduled on a device
d_{j}
, is the sum of

deployment
,
dataflow transmission
and
dataflow processing
times:

\mathtt{CT}\left(m_{i},r_{g},d_{j}\right)=\frac{\mathtt{Size}_{m_{i}}}{\mathtt{BW}_{gj}}+\frac{\mathtt{Size}_{ui}}{\mathtt{BW}_{kj}}+\frac{\mathtt{CPU}\left(m_{i}\right)}{\mathtt{CPU}_{j}},

where deployment time (
T_{d}
) is determined by downloading a containerized microservice
m_{i}
of size
\mathtt{Size}_{m_{i}}
not already existing on a device
d_{j}
from a Docker registry (remote or regional) and deploying it on
d_{j}
while
\mathtt{BW}_{gj}
defines the bandwidth from Docker registry
r_{g}
to a device
d_{j}
. The dataflow transmission time (
T_{c}
) is defined based on the transfer between microservices
m_{u}
and
m_{i}
executing on corresponding devices
d_{k}
and
d_{j}
. Finally, the
dataflow processing time (
T_{p}
)
is computed as the ratio of the processing requirements to the device’s capacity
\mathtt{CPU}_{j}
. Note that we utilized the application’s DAG to model its completion time
[
10
]
.

2.

Energy consumption
\mathtt{EC}\left(m_{i},r_{g},d_{j}\right)
is the sum of the
active
and
static
energy consumed
\mathtt{EC}\left(m_{i},d_{j}\right)
during each microservice (non-concurrently) execution:

\mathtt{EC}\left(m_{i},r_{g},d_{j}\right)=E_{a}\left(m_{i},r_{g},d_{j}\right)+E_{s}\left(d_{j}\right),

where
E_{a}\left(m_{i},r_{g},d_{j}\right)
represents
active energy
consumed during microservice
m_{i}
deployment from
r_{g}
on device
d_{j}
to process a dataflow
\mathit{df}_{ui}
, which is directly related to
\mathtt{CT}\left(m_{i},r_{g},d_{j}\right)
. Moreover,
E_{s}\left(d_{j}\right)
denotes
static energy
consumed for maintaining the device
d_{j}
and running its background tasks. The total energy
\mathtt{EC}_{\text{total}}\left(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{R},\mathcal{D}\right)
consumed by an application
\mathcal{A}
is the sum of energy consumed by all its microservices during dataflow processing
[
11
]
:

\mathtt{EC}_{\text{total}}\left(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{R},\mathcal{D}\right)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{i}\in\mathcal{A}\ \land\\
d_{j}=\mathtt{sched}\left(m_{i}\right)\ \land\\
r_{g}=\mathtt{regist}\left(m_{i}\right)\end{subarray}}\mathtt{EC}\left(m_{i},r_{g},d_{j}\right).

III-E

Problem definition

We model the dataflow processing problem in the context of the Docker registry-based microservice image storage services using the
prisoner dilemma
model within the nash equilibrium
[
12
]
to optimize energy consumption through cooperation between microservices and devices. The game investigates the solutions for retrieving the microservices from a Docker registry with minimal energy consumption during deployment from registries
r_{g}=\mathtt{regist}\left(m_{i}\right)
and executing them on the least power-hungry edge devices
d_{j}=\mathtt{sched}\left(m_{i}\right)
:

\displaystyle\min\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathtt{sched}(\mathcal{M})=\mathcal{D}\ \land\ \mathtt{regist}(\mathcal{M})=\mathcal{R}\end{subarray}}\mathtt{EC}_{\text{total}}\left(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{R},\mathcal{D}\right).

VI
Conclusion

We presented
DEEP
, an edge-based dataflow processing method deploying microservices from hybrid Docker Hub and regional registries.
DEEP
enables the reduction of edge devices’ energy consumption and yields more energy-efficient dataflow processing, especially in the text processing application. The results show that
DEEP
reduces energy consumption by
0.34\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
, compared to exclusively deploying from Docker Hub, by retrieving
83\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
of the text processing microservices from the regional registry.
We also observe that the microservice’s image location plays no significant role in energy consumption for heavyweight video processing.
We plan to extend this energy-aware nash-based model to schedule the computation between cloud and edge.

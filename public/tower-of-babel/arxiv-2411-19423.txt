Bounds on the Global Attractor of 2D Incompressible Turbulence in the Palinstrophy–Enstrophy–Energy Space
Pedram Emami, John C. Bowman

Abstract
. Analytic bounds on the projection of the global attractor of 2D incompressible turbulence in the palinstrophy–enstrophy plane [Dascaliuc, Foias, and Jolly 2005, 2010] are observed to vastly overestimate the values obtained from numerical simulations. This is due to the lack of a good estimate for the inner product ( ℬ ⁡ ( 𝒖 , 𝒖 ) , A 2 ​ 𝒖 ) (\mathcal{B}({\bm{u}},{\bm{u}}),A^{2}{\bm{u}}) of the advection term and the biLaplacian. Sobolev inequalities like Ladyzhenskaya or Agmon’s inequalities yield an upper bound that we show is not sharp. In fact, for statistically isotropic turbulence, the expected value of ( ℬ ⁡ ( 𝒖 , 𝒖 ) , A 2 ​ 𝒖 ) (\mathcal{B}({\bm{u}},{\bm{u}}),A^{2}{\bm{u}}) is zero. The implications for estimates on the behaviour of the global attractor are discussed.

1.
Introduction

One of the most recent approaches in studying turbulence incorporates functional analysis tools to study
turbulence on a solid mathematical basis. The Navier–Stokes equation is the generally accepted governing
partial differential equation of turbulence. Unlike empirical or heuristic formulations, it provides a
solid foundation for studying important intrinsic characteristics of turbulence.

Building on the work of
Dascaliuc et al. (2010)
that characterized the bounds on the global attractor of
2D homogeneous incompressible turbulence in the energy–enstrophy and enstrophy–palinstrophy planes for
constant forcing, we extended in
(
Emami & Bowman, 2018
)
the analysis to handle random forcing, which is more
applicable to both laboratory and numerical experiments. For white-noise random forcing, corresponding
bounds on the global attractor were obtained on the energy–enstrophy plane and demonstrated with direct
numerical simulations. While it turned out that the same form of the upper bound found for constant
forcing also holds for random forcing, it was observed that the projected upper bound of the global
attractor on the enstrophy–palinstrophy plane vastly overestimates numerically computed trajectories.

These observations motivated us to study the upper bound of the projection of the global attractor onto
the enstrophy–palinstrophy plane in more detail. The result is a much tighter upper bound that, together
with the upper bound on the energy–enstrophy plane, provides a better understanding of the global
attractor as well as a more discerning criteria for validating heuristic engineering subgrid models.

2.
Definitions and preliminaries

One of the simplest contexts in which to pose the turbulence problem is 2D incompressible
homogeneous isotropic turbulent flow in a bounded domain with periodic boundary conditions and zero
mean velocity and forcing. One close realization of this ideal form of turbulence in laboratories
is a very thin layer of turbulent fluid far downstream from a flow passing over a net of wires.

Looking at this ideal form of turbulence deterministically involves using the incompressible
Navier–Stokes and continuity equations expressed as a set of integro-differential equations, with
zero mean flow and forcing and constant density
\rho=1
:

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial{\bm{u}}}{\partial t}-\nu{\nabla}^{2}{\bm{u}}+{\bm{u}}{\bm{\cdot}}\bm{\nabla}{\bm{u}}+\bm{\nabla}p={\bm{F}},

(1)

\displaystyle\bm{\nabla}{\bm{\cdot}}{\bm{u}}=0,\quad\int_{\Omega}{{\bm{u}}\,d{\bm{x}}}=\bm{0},\quad\int_{\Omega}{{\bm{F}}\,d{\bm{x}}}=\bm{0},

(2)

\displaystyle{\bm{u}}({\bm{x}},0)={\bm{u}}_{0}({\bm{x}}),

(3)

with
\Omega={\left[0,L\right]}\times{\left[0,L\right]}
and periodic boundary conditions on
\partial\Omega
. This
problem can be considered in a specific Hilbert space

applied forcing:

H(\Omega)\doteq\mathop{\rm cl}\nolimits\left\{{\bm{u}}\in(C^{2}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}(\Omega))^{2}\ |\ \bm{\nabla}{\bm{\cdot}}{\bm{u}}=0,\ \int_{\Omega}{{\bm{u}}\,d{\bm{x}}=0}\right\},

From now on, we will drop the term ‘extended’ from both inner product and the resulting norm since

The above problem can then be expressed as

\dfrac{d{\bm{u}}}{dt}-\nu{\nabla}^{2}{\bm{u}}+{\bm{u}}{\bm{\cdot}}\bm{\nabla}{\bm{u}}+\bm{\nabla}p={\bm{F}},\qquad{\bm{u}}(t)\in H(\Omega).

In terms of these definitions, (

3.
Stokes operator
A

The operator
A=\mathcal{P}(-{\nabla}^{2})
is positive-semidefinite and self-adjoint
in
H(\Omega)
, with a compact inverse and eigenvalues

\lambda=k_{0}^{2}{\bm{k}}{\bm{\cdot}}{\bm{k}},\qquad{\bm{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{\bm{0}\},

where
k_{0}=2\pi/L
.
The eigenvalues of this positive-definite infinite-dimensional linear operator can
be arranged as

0<\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<\cdots,\qquad\lambda_{0}={\left(\dfrac{2\pi}{L}\right)}^{2}.

The corresponding eigenvectors
\bm{w}_{i},\ i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
form an orthonormal basis for the
Hilbert space
H
upon which we can define any power of
A
:

A^{\alpha}\bm{w}_{j}=\lambda_{j}^{\alpha}\bm{w}_{j},\qquad\alpha\in\mathbb{R},\quad j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Having the above orthonormal basis, we can use our inner product
to define new spaces
V^{2\alpha}~\subset~H

for any real value of
\alpha
:
(
Temam, 1995
)

V^{2\alpha}=D(A^{\alpha})\doteq\left\{{\bm{u}}\in H\ |\ \sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}^{2\alpha}({\bm{u}},\bm{w}_{j})^{2}<\infty\right\}.

Among all possible values of
\alpha
, we are especially interested in the spaces
V^{0},V^{1}
,
V^{2}
, and

V^{3}
corresponding to
\alpha=0,\alpha=1/2
,
\alpha=1
, and
\alpha=3/2
motivated by the definition
of energy, enstrophy, palinstrophy, and hyperpalinstrophy. It is readily shown that

\displaystyle{\bm{u}}\in V^{0}

\displaystyle\iff\left\lVert{\bm{u}}\right\rVert<\infty,

\displaystyle{\bm{u}}\in V^{1}

\displaystyle\iff\left\lVert A^{1/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert<\infty,

\displaystyle{\bm{u}}\in V^{2}

\displaystyle\iff\left\lVert A{\bm{u}}\right\rVert<\infty,

\displaystyle{\bm{u}}\in V^{3}

\displaystyle\iff\left\lVert A^{3/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert<\infty.

For any solution
{\bm{u}}
of the 2D Navier–Stokes equation, we
define the
\zeta
th-order
polystrophy

E_{\zeta}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert A^{\zeta/2}{\bm{u}}(t)\right\rVert^{2}.

The special polystrophy values
E_{0}
,
Z\doteq E_{1}
,
P\doteq E_{2}
,

P_{2}\doteq E_{3}
are respectively known as the
energy
,

enstrophy
,
palinstrophy
, and
hyperpalinstrophy
(
Rondoni & Segre, 1999
;
Al & Ohkitani, 2012
)
.

Just as energy is proportional to the mean-squared velocity, enstrophy is proportional to the
mean-squared vorticity and therefore provides a measure of the
rotational energy in a flow. It is easily shown that the rate at
which energy is dissipated is proportional to the enstrophy. Likewise,
enstrophy is dissipated at a rate proportional to the palinstrophy.

In all of our numerical simulations of randomly forced isotropic
turbulence, we noticed that the triplet term is negligible.
The robustness of this observation motivated us to prove the following
result.

Theorem 1
.

For incompressible homogeneous statistically isotropic 2D turbulence

(\mathcal{B}({\bm{u}},{\bm{u}}),A^{\zeta}{\bm{u}})=0\qquad\forall\zeta\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof
.

Since for 2D turbulence, the stream function
\psi(x,y)
is well
defined, taking into account that
A
is self-adjoint, we know that

\displaystyle u=\psi_{y},v=-\psi_{x}\quad({\bm{u}}\cdot\bm{\nabla}){\bm{u}}=\begin{bmatrix}\psi_{y}\psi_{yx}-\psi_{x}\psi_{yy}\\
\psi_{x}\psi_{xy}-\psi_{y}\psi_{xx}\\
\end{bmatrix},\quad A^{\zeta}{\bm{u}}=\begin{bmatrix}A^{\zeta}u\\
A^{\zeta}v\end{bmatrix}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle({\bm{u}}\cdot\bm{\nabla}){\bm{u}}\cdot A^{\zeta}{\bm{u}}

\displaystyle=A^{\zeta}\psi_{y}(\psi_{y}\psi_{yx}-\psi_{x}\psi_{yy})-A^{\zeta}\psi_{x}(\psi_{x}\psi_{xy}-\psi_{y}\psi_{xx}).

Since
A
is an isotropic operator, the ensemble average of the above
expression will vanish for isotropic turbulence. That is,

\displaystyle\left\langle({\bm{u}}{\bm{\cdot}}\bm{\nabla}){\bm{u}}{\bm{\cdot}}A^{\zeta}{\bm{u}}\right\rangle=0;

this implies that

\displaystyle{\left(\mathcal{B}({\bm{u}},{\bm{u}}),A^{\zeta}{\bm{u}}\right)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left\langle{\left({\bm{u}}\cdot\bm{\nabla}{\bm{u}}\right)}\cdot A^{\zeta}{\bm{u}}\right\rangle\,d{\bm{x}}+\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\bm{\nabla}p\cdot A^{\zeta}{\bm{u}}\right\rangle\,d{\bm{x}}\>

\displaystyle=0-\left\langle\int_{\Omega}pA^{\zeta}\bm{\nabla}{\bm{\cdot}}{\bm{u}}\,d{\bm{x}}\right\rangle=0,

using incompressibility. Therefore,
{\left(\mathcal{B}({\bm{u}},{\bm{u}}),A^{\zeta}{\bm{u}}\right)}=0
and in particular,
{\left(\mathcal{B}({\bm{u}},{\bm{u}}),A^{2}{\bm{u}}\right)}=0
.

\blacksquare

inequality, we find

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\dfrac{d}{dt}\left\lVert A^{\zeta/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon_{\zeta}-\nu k_{0}^{2}\left\lVert A^{\zeta/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}\overset{\text{Gronwall inequality}}{\Rightarrow}

\displaystyle\left\lVert A^{\zeta/2}{\bm{u}}(t)\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-2\nu k_{0}^{2}t}\left\lVert A^{\zeta/2}{\bm{u}}(0)\right\rVert^{2}+{\left(\frac{1-e^{-2\nu k_{0}^{2}t}}{\nu k_{0}^{2}}\right)}\epsilon_{\zeta}.

Thus, for every
{\bm{u}}\in\mathcal{A}
, we would expect to have

\left\lVert A^{\zeta/2}{\bm{u}}(t)\right\rVert^{2}\leq\dfrac{\epsilon_{\zeta}}{\nu k_{0}^{2}}.

(25)

7.
Bounds on the projection of the global attractor to the space of three
consecutive polystrophies

Let
{\bm{u}}(t)
be a solution such that

{\bm{u}}(t)\neq\bm{0}
on some interval
(t_{1},t_{2}]
. Then the function

\chi(t)\doteq\frac{\left\lVert A^{\zeta/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}}{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert}
for any
\zeta,\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\sigma<\zeta
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{d\chi}{dt}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\dfrac{d\left\lVert A^{\zeta/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}}{dt}\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert-\left\lVert A^{\zeta/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}\dfrac{d\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert}{dt}}{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}}

(26)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{2{\left(\epsilon_{\zeta}-\nu\left\lVert A^{(\zeta+1)/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}\right)}}{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert}-\dfrac{\left\lVert A^{\zeta/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}{\left(\epsilon_{\sigma}-\nu\left\lVert A^{(\sigma+1)/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}\right)}}{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{3}}.

On defining,

\lambda(t)\doteq\frac{\chi(t)}{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert}=\frac{\left\lVert A^{\zeta/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}}{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}},

we see that (
26
) can be written as

\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert\frac{d\chi}{dt}=-2\nu\left\lVert A^{(\zeta+1)/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}+2\epsilon_{\zeta}-\lambda\epsilon_{\sigma}+\nu\lambda\left\lVert A^{(\sigma+1)/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}.

(27)

In the last equation we observe that if
\zeta=\sigma+1
, (
27
) can be simplified to

\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert\frac{d\chi}{dt}=-2\nu\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2+1}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}+2\epsilon_{\zeta}-\lambda\epsilon_{\sigma}+\nu\lambda\left\lVert A^{\zeta/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}.

(28)

Thus, on introducing
{\bm{v}}=(A-\lambda)A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}-\frac{A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{f}}}{2\nu}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\bm{v}}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert(A-\lambda)A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}-\dfrac{A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{f}}}{2\nu}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2+1}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}-2\lambda\left\lVert A^{\zeta/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}-\dfrac{\epsilon_{\zeta}}{\nu}+\dfrac{\lambda\epsilon_{\sigma}}{\nu}+\lambda^{2}\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}+\dfrac{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{f}}\right\rVert^{2}}{4\nu^{2}},

so that

\displaystyle-2\nu\left\lVert{\bm{v}}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=-2\nu\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2+1}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}+4\nu\lambda\left\lVert A^{\zeta/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}+2\epsilon_{\zeta}-2\lambda\epsilon_{\sigma}-2\nu\lambda^{2}\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\dfrac{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{f}}\right\rVert^{2}}{2\nu}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{{\left(2\epsilon_{\zeta}-2\nu\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2+1}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}\right)}-\lambda\epsilon_{\sigma}+\nu\lambda\left\lVert A^{\zeta/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}}_{\displaystyle\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert\dfrac{d\chi}{dt}}+\underbrace{\nu\lambda\left\lVert A^{\zeta/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}}_{\displaystyle\nu\chi^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad-\lambda\epsilon_{\sigma}-\dfrac{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{f}}\right\rVert^{2}}{2\nu}.

Thus

\displaystyle-\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert\dfrac{d\chi}{dt}

\displaystyle=2\nu\left\lVert{\bm{v}}\right\rVert^{2}+\nu\chi^{2}-\lambda\epsilon_{\sigma}-\dfrac{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{f}}\right\rVert^{2}}{2\nu}.

On introducing a real constant
\alpha
whose value will be determined later, and considering the
fact that
\chi=\lambda\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert
we may write

\displaystyle\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert\dfrac{d}{dt}(\alpha-\chi)

\displaystyle=2\nu\left\lVert{\bm{v}}\right\rVert^{2}+\nu(\alpha-\chi)^{2}-\nu\alpha^{2}+2\nu\alpha\chi-\lambda\epsilon_{\sigma}-\dfrac{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{f}}\right\rVert^{2}}{2\nu}\quad

\displaystyle=2\nu\left\lVert{\bm{v}}\right\rVert^{2}+\nu(\alpha-\chi)^{2}-\nu\alpha^{2}+{\left(2\nu\alpha-\dfrac{\epsilon_{\sigma}}{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert}\right)}\chi

\displaystyle\quad-\dfrac{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{f}}\right\rVert^{2}}{2\nu}.

On defining
\beta=2\nu\alpha-\dfrac{\epsilon_{\sigma}}{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert}
,
the above result may be rewritten as

\displaystyle\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert\dfrac{d}{dt}(\alpha-\chi)

\displaystyle=2\nu\left\lVert{\bm{v}}\right\rVert^{2}+\nu(\alpha-\chi)^{2}-\beta(\alpha-\chi)+\alpha\beta-\nu\alpha^{2}-\dfrac{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{f}}\right\rVert^{2}}{2\nu}.

Thus, if
\alpha
is such
that

\displaystyle\beta=2\nu\alpha-\dfrac{\epsilon_{\sigma}}{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert}>0\quad\text{and}\quad\nu\alpha^{2}-\dfrac{\alpha\epsilon_{\sigma}}{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert}-\dfrac{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{f}}\right\rVert^{2}}{2\nu}>0,

(29)

we can introduce

\displaystyle\phi\doteq 2\nu\left\lVert{\bm{v}}\right\rVert^{2}+\nu(\alpha-\chi)^{2}+\alpha\beta-\nu\alpha^{2}-\dfrac{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{f}}\right\rVert^{2}}{2\nu}>0

to express the above first-order differential equation as

\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert\dfrac{d}{dt}(\alpha-\chi)+\beta(\alpha-\chi)=\phi.

(30)

The solution to this equation is given by

\displaystyle\alpha-\chi(t)

\displaystyle=(\alpha-\chi(t_{0}))\exp{\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t}{-\dfrac{\beta}{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert}\,ds}\right)}

\displaystyle+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}{\dfrac{\phi}{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert}\exp{\left(-\int_{\tau}^{t}{\dfrac{\beta}{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert}\,ds}\right)}\,d\tau}.

Taking
t_{0}\to-\infty
, and
t=0
, results in
{\left(\alpha-\chi(0)\right)}\geq 0
. Now taking
t_{0}=0
, and

t\to\infty
, one finds that
\alpha-\chi(t)\geq 0
for all
t\in(-\infty,\infty)
. Thus

0\leq\chi\leq\alpha\quad\Rightarrow\quad\left\lVert A^{\zeta/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\alpha\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert.

(31)

To get the above result we need to check conditions (
29
). Working on these
inequalities, one can show

\displaystyle\nu\alpha^{2}-\dfrac{\alpha\epsilon_{\sigma}}{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert}-\dfrac{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{f}}\right\rVert^{2}}{2\nu}=0\Rightarrow

(32)

\displaystyle\alpha_{1,2}=\dfrac{\dfrac{\epsilon_{\sigma}}{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert}\pm\sqrt{\dfrac{\epsilon^{2}_{\sigma}}{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}}+2\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{f}}\right\rVert^{2}}}{2\nu}\Rightarrow\begin{cases}\alpha_{1}<0,\\
\alpha_{2}\geq\dfrac{\epsilon_{\sigma}}{\nu\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert}.\end{cases}

Therefore,

\displaystyle\nu\alpha^{2}-\dfrac{\epsilon_{\sigma}\alpha}{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert}-\dfrac{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{f}}\right\rVert^{2}}{2\nu}>0\iff\alpha>\alpha_{2}\geq\dfrac{\epsilon_{\sigma}}{\nu\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert}\text{
or }\alpha<\alpha_{1}<0,

which implies that

\displaystyle\beta=2\nu\alpha-\dfrac{\epsilon_{\sigma}}{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert}>0

and, using (
25
) (which implies that

\epsilon_{\sigma}\geq 0
),

\alpha\geq\dfrac{\epsilon_{\sigma}}{\nu\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert}\geq k_{0}\sqrt{\dfrac{\epsilon_{\sigma}}{\nu}}.

(33)

If we set
\alpha=k_{0}\sqrt{\dfrac{\epsilon_{\sigma}}{\nu}}
, (
31
)
leads to a useful bound for polystrophies of order
\sigma
and
\zeta=\sigma+1
:

\left\lVert A^{(\sigma+1)/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}\leq k_{0}\sqrt{\dfrac{\epsilon_{\sigma}}{\nu}}\,\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert.

(34)

Using (
25
) and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,
we also find

k_{0}\sqrt{\nu\epsilon_{\sigma}}\leq\dfrac{\epsilon_{\sigma}}{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert}=\frac{({\bm{f}},A^{\sigma}{\bm{u}})}{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert}\leq\frac{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{f}}\right\rVert\,\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert}{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert}=\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{f}}\right\rVert.

(35)

It is convenient to use the lower bound for
\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{f}}\right\rVert
found in
(
35
) to define a Grashof number for white-noise forcing:

G=\dfrac{\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{f}}\right\rVert}{\nu^{2}k_{0}^{2}}\geq\tilde{G}\doteq\dfrac{\sqrt{\nu\epsilon_{\sigma}}}{\nu^{2}k_{0}},

allowing us to express (
34
) more compactly:

Theorem 2
.

Let
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
and
{\bm{u}}\in\mathcal{A}
be a non-trivial solution of
(
6
) driven by a random forcing with
\sigma
-order polystrophy injection rate
\epsilon_{\sigma}
. On choosing
\tilde{G}\doteq\sqrt{\nu\epsilon_{\sigma}}/(\nu^{2}k_{0})
, and introducing the
normalized velocity
{\bm{v}}={\bm{u}}/(\tilde{G}\nu)
,

\left\lVert A^{(\sigma+1)/2}{\bm{v}}\right\rVert^{2}\leq k_{0}^{2}\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{v}}\right\rVert.

The above result has the same form as found in
(
Emami & Bowman, 2018
)
in the

E
–
Z
plane. We can go one step further and apply the above theorem twice to any three normalized
consecutive polystrophies:

\left\lVert A^{(\sigma+2)/2}{\bm{v}}\right\rVert^{2}\leq k_{0}^{2}\,\left\lVert A^{(\sigma+1)/2}{\bm{v}}\right\rVert\quad\text{and}\quad\left\lVert A^{(\sigma+1)/2}{\bm{v}}\right\rVert^{2}\leq k_{0}^{2}\,\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{v}}\right\rVert.

Therefore,

\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2+1}{\bm{v}}\right\rVert^{4}\leq k_{0}^{6}\,\left\lVert A^{\sigma/2}{\bm{v}}\right\rVert.

(36)

These generalized bounds are presented in Fig.
4
, along with a projection of the trajectory
of a numerical solution of 2D turbulence onto the energy–enstrophy–palinstrophy space.

Remark
.

It is important to observe that the inequality (
36
) is quite special in the sense that it
cannot be directly derived by applying our analysis for
\sigma
and
\sigma+2
order polystrophies.

Remark
.

In particular, for
\sigma=0
:

\frac{2P}{(\nu\tilde{G})^{2}}\leq k_{0}^{2}\sqrt{\frac{2Z}{(\nu\tilde{G})^{2}}}\leq k_{0}^{6}\sqrt[4]{\frac{2E}{(\nu\tilde{G})^{2}}}.

(37)

Observation 1
.

It is important to observe that these relations are global on the additive subgroup of real
numbers that is homomorphic with the subgroup generated by
1
.

8.
The inverse cascade and the necessity of hypoviscosity

In 3D turbulence, Kolmogorov argued that a uniform amount of energy
cascades from the forcing scales down to molecular scales, where it
gets dissipated
(
Kolmogorov, 1941
)
.
In 2D turbulence, Fjørtoft showed instead that a uniform amount of
enstrophy cascades down to the small scales, while a uniform amount of
energy cascades up to the large scales
(
Fjrtoft, 1953
)
.
In numerical simulations of 2D turbulence in a bounded domain with doubly
periodic boundary conditions, this
inverse cascade
causes the energy to
pile up at the large scales.
Although there is no pure realization of 2D turbulence in nature,
geophysicists believe that 2D turbulence can be roughly realized in high altitude atmospheric flow. It is
believed that the inverse energy cascade towards the large scales will be extracted from this realization
of 2D turbulence via interaction with gravity
waves. However, in pseudospectral simulations of
2D turbulence, where the boundary conditions are periodic,
there is no natural mechanism for extracting energy out
of these large scales. For this reason, numericists typically introduce
an artificial
hypoviscosity
to take energy out of the flow
at large scales. Since there are various ways of implementing
hypoviscosity, it is crucial to understand how they affect the bounds
on the global attractor.

In this section we will analyze one of the simplest implementations of
hypoviscosity: a constant artificial friction
\gamma

applied at all scales. The
governing equation for the incompressible 2D turbulence with random
forcing, (
6
), becomes

\frac{\partial{\bm{u}}}{\partial t}+\nu A{\bm{u}}+\mathcal{B}({\bm{u}},{\bm{u}})+\gamma{\bm{u}}={\bm{f}}.

(38)

Consequently, the dynamical system equations (
7
), (
8
), and
(
9
) will change to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert{\bm{u}}(t)\right\rVert^{2}+\nu\left\lVert A^{1/2}{\bm{u}}(t)\right\rVert^{2}+(\mathcal{B}({\bm{u}},{\bm{u}}),{\bm{u}})+\gamma\left\lVert{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}=({\bm{f}},{\bm{u}}(t)),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert{A^{1/2}}{\bm{u}}(t)\right\rVert^{2}+\nu\left\lVert A{\bm{u}}(t)\right\rVert^{2}+(\mathcal{B}({\bm{u}},{\bm{u}}),A{\bm{u}})+\gamma\left\lVert{A^{1/2}}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}=({\bm{f}},A{\bm{u}}(t)),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert A{\bm{u}}(t)\right\rVert^{2}+\nu\left\lVert A^{3/2}{\bm{u}}(t)\right\rVert^{2}+(\mathcal{B}({\bm{u}},{\bm{u}}),A^{2}{\bm{u}})+\gamma\left\lVert A{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}=({\bm{f}},A^{2}{\bm{u}}(t)).

Among the above equations, we are mainly interested in the energy and enstrophy equations. Taking into
account the bilinear identities, we find

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(\mathcal{B}({\bm{u}},{\bm{u}}),{\bm{u}})=(\mathcal{B}({\bm{u}},{\bm{u}}),A{\bm{u}})=0,\\
\epsilon=({\bm{f}},{\bm{u}}),\quad\eta=({\bm{f}},A{\bm{u}}),\end{cases}

and therefore, we obtain the following system of differential equations:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}+\nu\left\lVert A^{1/2}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}+\gamma\left\lVert{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}=\epsilon,\\[14.22636pt]
\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert{A^{1/2}}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}+\nu\left\lVert A{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}+\gamma\left\lVert{A^{1/2}}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}=\eta.\end{cases}

(39)

Using the Poincaré inequalities

k_{0}^{2}\left\lVert{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\left\lVert{A^{1/2}}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad k_{0}^{2}\left\lVert{A^{1/2}}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\left\lVert A{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2},

we then obtain the following results for energy and enstrophy, respectively:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon-(\nu k_{0}^{2}+\gamma)\left\lVert{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}\overset{\text{Gronwall inequality}}{\Rightarrow}

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\bm{u}}(t)\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-2(\nu k_{0}^{2}+\gamma)t}\left\lVert{\bm{u}}(0)\right\rVert^{2}+{\left(\frac{1-e^{-2(\nu k_{0}^{2}+\gamma)t}}{\nu k_{0}^{2}+\gamma}\right)}\epsilon

(40)

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert{A^{1/2}}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\eta-(\nu k_{0}^{2}+\gamma)\left\lVert{A^{1/2}}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}\overset{\text{Gronwall inequality}}{\Rightarrow}

\displaystyle\left\lVert{A^{1/2}}{\bm{u}}(t)\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-2(\nu k_{0}^{2}+\gamma)t}\left\lVert{A^{1/2}}{\bm{u}}(0)\right\rVert^{2}+{\left(\frac{1-e^{-2(\nu k_{0}^{2}+\gamma)t}}{\nu k_{0}^{2}+\gamma}\right)}\eta.

(41)

So for every
{\bm{u}}\in\mathcal{A}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\bm{u}}(t)\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon}{\nu k_{0}^{2}+\gamma},

(42)

\displaystyle\left\lVert{A^{1/2}}{\bm{u}}(t)\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\eta}{\nu k_{0}^{2}+\gamma}.

(43)

From (
42
), we see that

\sqrt{\nu\epsilon k_{0}^{2}+\epsilon\gamma}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{\left\lVert{\bm{u}}\right\rVert}=\frac{({\bm{f}},{\bm{u}})}{\left\lVert{\bm{u}}\right\rVert}\leq\frac{\left\lVert{\bm{f}}\right\rVert\left\lVert{\bm{u}}\right\rVert}{\left\lVert{\bm{u}}\right\rVert}=\left\lVert{\bm{f}}\right\rVert.

On introducing
{\bm{f}}^{\prime}={\bm{f}}-\gamma{\bm{u}}
, which we call the
reduced forcing
, the above differential
equations can be simplified to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert{\bm{u}}(t)\right\rVert^{2}+\nu\left\lVert A^{1/2}{\bm{u}}(t)\right\rVert^{2}=({\bm{f}}^{\prime},{\bm{u}}(t)),

(44)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\lVert{A^{1/2}}{\bm{u}}(t)\right\rVert^{2}+\nu\left\lVert A{\bm{u}}(t)\right\rVert^{2}=({\bm{f}}^{\prime},A{\bm{u}}(t)).

(45)

On defining
\epsilon^{\prime}:=({\bm{f}}^{\prime},{\bm{u}})=\epsilon-\gamma\left\lVert{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}
, and applying the argument in Section
7
for

\zeta=1,\sigma=0
, and considering
{\bm{f}}^{\prime}
instead of
{\bm{f}}
, the calculation of
\alpha
and
\beta

remains unchanged. The first inequality in (
33
) then becomes

\alpha\geq\frac{\epsilon^{\prime}}{\nu\left\lVert{\bm{u}}\right\rVert}.

(46)

Since
\epsilon\geq\epsilon^{\prime}
, we can enforce (
46
) by
imposing

\alpha\geq\frac{\epsilon}{\nu\left\lVert{\bm{u}}\right\rVert}\geq\frac{\sqrt{\nu\epsilon k_{0}^{2}+\epsilon\gamma}}{\nu}.

Thus, on choosing
\alpha=\sqrt{(\nu\epsilon k_{0}^{2}+\epsilon\gamma)}/\nu
, (
31
) becomes

\left\lVert{A^{1/2}}{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{\sqrt{\nu\epsilon k_{0}^{2}+\epsilon\gamma}}{\nu}\left\lVert{\bm{u}}\right\rVert.

(47)

Therefore, the presence of a constant friction
\gamma
changes the definition of

\tilde{G}
:

G=\frac{\left\lVert{\bm{f}}\right\rVert}{\nu^{2}k_{0}^{2}}\geq\tilde{G}\doteq\frac{\sqrt{\nu\epsilon k_{0}^{2}+\epsilon\gamma}}{\nu^{2}k_{0}^{2}}.

This motivates the velocity normalization

{\bm{v}}(t)={\bm{u}}(t)/(\tilde{G}\nu)
, so that

\left\lVert{A^{1/2}}{\bm{v}}\right\rVert^{2}\leq k_{0}^{2}\left\lVert{\bm{v}}\right\rVert.

Thus, the presence of a constant friction (which we apply in our
numerical simulations to remove energy from the inverse cascade)
leads to the same form for the bounds on the projection of
the global attractor onto the
E
–
Z
plane.

Remark
.

The same argument with minor changes establishes that constant
forcing will not affect the form of the projection of the bounds of
the global attractor onto the
Z
–
P
plane, as well as other
consecutive polystrophy pairs.

9.
Pseudospectral numerical simulation

To illustrate the theoretical results in this work,
we use a dealiased pseudospectral simulation to solve (
38
)
in a doubly periodic square box from zero initial conditions,
respecting the incompressibility condition
\bm{\nabla}{\bm{\cdot}}{\bm{u}}=0
.
For efficiency, we evolve the scalar
vorticity

\omega=\hat{\bm{z}}{\bm{\cdot}}\bm{\nabla}{\bm{\times}}{\bm{u}}
:

\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial t}+(\hat{\bm{z}}{\bm{\times}}\bm{\nabla}\nabla^{-2}\omega{\bm{\cdot}}\bm{\nabla})\omega=-\gamma{\bm{u}}+\nu_{H}\nabla^{2}\omega+f,

(48)

where
\hat{\bm{z}}
is the unit normal to the flow plane,
\nu_{H}
is the
molecular viscosity, and
f
is a white-noise random forcing.
As discussed in Section
8
,
the friction term
\gamma
prevents the energy injected
by
f
from piling up at the largest scales.

Upon Fourier transforming, (
48
) becomes

\frac{\partial\omega_{{\bm{k}}}}{\partial t}=S_{\bm{k}}-\nu_{H}k^{2}\omega_{{\bm{k}}}-\gamma\omega_{\bm{k}}+f_{{\bm{k}}},

(49)

where
S_{\bm{k}}=\sum_{\bm{q}}\hat{\bm{z}}{\bm{\cdot}}{\bm{k}}{\bm{\times}}{\bm{q}}\,\omega_{{\bm{k}}-{\bm{q}}}\omega_{{\bm{q}}}/q^{2}
represents the advective convolution.
Although the theoretical treatment in Section
8
assumes
\gamma
is constant, some
numericists replace it with a wavenumber-dependent hypoviscosity
H(k_{L}-k)\nu_{L}
, where
H
is the
Heaviside unit step function, which acts only at wavenumbers lower than
k_{L}
. This is to avoid
introducing unwanted dissipation in the inertial range, consistent with Kolmogorov’s phenomenological
view of turbulence
(
Kolmogorov, 1941
)
. Most of our numerical results used a finite
k_{L}
set
to the lowest forced wavenumber, but we verified numerically that replacing
the wavenumber-dependent friction with a constant friction
\gamma=\nu_{L}
does not qualitatively
change the results, except that it steepens the inertial-range power
law of the energy spectrum well below the expected
k^{-3}
power law obtained with the Heaviside cutoff.

We use
1023\times 1023
dealiased Fourier modes to evolve (
49
) in a doubly periodic square box
of size
L=2\pi
; this is equivalent to
1534\times 1534
spatial modes. In Fourier space, the turbulence
is driven by a white-noise forcing
f_{{\bm{k}}}
limited to an annulus of mean radius
k_{f}=4
and width

\delta_{f}=1
. The parameters
\nu_{H}=4\times 10^{-5}
,
\nu_{L}=0.15
, and
k_{L}=3.5
were chosen to give a
steady-state energy spectrum with a wide inertial range extending over roughly one decade of wavenumber
space. As described in
(
Emami & Bowman, 2018
)
, the Novikov theorem
(
Novikov, 1964
)
allows us to control the
average rate
\eta
of enstrophy injection by the random forcing. We adopt the value
\eta=1
. An
adaptive time step was used to evolve the simulation from zero initial conditions to a fully developed
turbulent statistically stationary state. As is usual in numerical simulations of turbulence, we assume
that the ergodic theorem is sufficiently applicable so that ensemble averages may be approximated by
temporal averages.

The simulations were performed with the numerical code
DNS

https://github.com/dealias/dns/tree/master/2d
written by Professor John C. Bowman. The code,
written in C++, is comprised of a kernel called
TRIAD
that uses adaptive differential equation
solvers for ODEs and PDEs. The package contains a set of different numerical integrators and exploits the

FFTW++
library
(
Bowman & Roberts, 2010
)
for calculating hybrid dealiased convolutions
(
Murasko & Bowman, 2024
)
under
Hermitian symmetry. Advanced computer memory management, such as implicit padding, memory alignment, and
dynamic moment averaging, allow
DNS
to attain its extreme performance. It uses the formulation
proposed by
Basdevant (1983)
to reduce the number of FFTs required for 2D (3D) incompressible

Discussion

In previous work
(
Emami & Bowman, 2018
)
, we showed that the upper bound in
the
E
–
Z
plane obtained by
Dascaliuc et al. (2005)
for
constant forcing also works for white-noise random forcing under the
proper normalization. Unfortunately, the bounds that

Dascaliuc et al. (2010)
obtained in the
Z
–
P
plane vastly
overestimate actual values from numerical simulations.
In this work,
we show this bound can be greatly tightened by exploiting isotropy.
The key difficulty in refining these bounds is the lack of a good
estimate for the triplet term
({\mathcal{B}}({\bm{u}},{\bm{u}}),A^{2}{\bm{u}})
.
However, for statistically isotropic turbulence with arbitrary forcing,
we have shown for any real
\zeta
that the expected value of the
triplet
({\mathcal{B}}({\bm{u}},{\bm{u}}),A^{\zeta}{\bm{u}})
is zero.
Moreover, if the turbulence is driven by
a white-noise random forcing, one can make use of the Novikov theorem

(
Novikov, 1964
)
to go further and obtain the same
form of the upper bounds for
P
vs.
Z
as we derived for
Z
vs.
E

in
(
Emami & Bowman, 2018
)
. In addition, we saw that enstrophy can be used as
an intermediary to provide new
P
vs.
E
bounds, using transitivity.
Combining these results with the corresponding Poincaré inequalities
leads to six bounds in the
E
–
Z
–
P
space.
Likewise, the general inequality we derived for consecutive polystrophies
can be used to obtain relations between any polystrophy pair.

Furthermore, we showed that a slightly modified normalization can be
used to extend all of these results to account for the large-scale
friction (hypoviscosity) that is normally used by numericists to
remove energy at the large scales and thereby ensure isotropy.
We showed that realistic numerical simulations of forced–dissipative
turbulence respects these bounds. We also confirmed that

{\mathcal{B}}({\bm{u}},{\bm{u}})
and
A^{2}{\bm{u}}
are statistically independent and showed that

({\mathcal{B}}({\bm{u}},{\bm{u}}),A^{2}{\bm{u}})
is negligible compared to the dissipative term of the
governing equation.

The analytical bounds we have derived for 2D turbulence driven by
random forcing provides a mathematical framework to evaluate various heuristic
turbulent subgrid (and supergrid) models by characterizing the
behaviour of the global attractor under these models.

Appendix A
Inequalities

Among many inequalities in the field of functional analysis, we now list those that are used in this
work. The reader who is interested in studying their proofs and related inequalities, especially

Sobolev inequalities
, can refer to any standard functional analysis resource.

In this appendix we use the following notation:

•

\Omega
: the domain of definition of functions;

•

{\bm{u}}
:
{\bm{u}}({\bm{x}}),\quad{\bm{x}}\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, or
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
;

•

{\bm{u}}_{{\bm{k}}}
: the discrete Fourier transform of
{\bm{u}},\quad{\bm{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\backslash\{\bm{0}\}
, or
{\bm{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\backslash\{\bm{0}\}
;

•

A=-{\nabla}^{2}
: a self-adjoint linear operator.

For the sake of simplicity, we are going to ignore
\Omega
in our
notation; for example, we would write
H^{2}
instead of
H^{2}(\Omega)
.

Appendix B
Agmon inequalities

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
{\bm{u}}\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. Then the 3D Agmon inequalities state
that there exists a constant
C_{A}
such that

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\bm{u}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{A}\left\lVert{\bm{u}}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{1/2}\left\lVert A{\bm{u}}\right\rVert_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{1/2},

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\bm{u}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{A}\left\lVert{\bm{u}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1/4}\left\lVert A{\bm{u}}\right\rVert_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{3/4}.

When
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
{\bm{u}}\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, the 2D Agmon inequality states that there exists a constant
C_{A}
such that

\left\lVert{\bm{u}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C_{A}\left\lVert{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{1/2}\left\lVert A{\bm{u}}\right\rVert^{1/2}.

(50)

It must be noted here that
C_{A}
is a universal constant that
depends only on the space
\Omega
, so in order to make use of this inequality for numerical simulations,
having an explicit value for
C_{A}
is crucial. In the next section we
are going to evaluate this constant for the two-dimensional periodic case.

Appendix C
Universal constant of the Agmon inequality in a 2D periodic domain

The main reason for finding an explicit value for the constant in the Agmon inequality is to obtain some
useful bounds for our direct numerical simulation of 2D turbulence. As the numerical simulation is done
on a lattice, we focus on the discrete form of the Agmon inequality,
where the integrals are replaced by summations. Because our simulations are performed in Fourier space, we make use
of the Fourier representation of the Agmon inequality.

We consider continuous functions on a bounded domain. The numerical simulations are done on a lattice on
this domain, where suprema reduce to maxima. That is,

\left\lVert{\bm{u}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=\mathop{\rm max}_{\Omega}|{\bm{u}}|.

It is worth mentioning that
pseudospectral
numerical simulations of turbulence in a periodic
domain use the
N
-point discrete Fourier transform

\hat{\bm{u}}_{k}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}{\bm{u}}_{j}e^{-\frac{2\pi ijk}{N}},

which has the inverse

{\bm{u}}_{j}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\hat{\bm{u}}_{k}e^{\frac{2\pi ijk}{N}}.

The orthogonality relationship underlying this transform pair is elucidated on substituting
z=e^{2\pi i(j+{\mathchoice{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\kern 0.89824pt\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt\kern 1.12283pt\crcr\kern 1.3pt\cr$\displaystyle{\hbox{$\displaystyle j\mkern 2.0mu$}}$\crcr}}}{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\kern 0.89824pt\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt\kern 1.12283pt\crcr\kern 1.3pt\cr$\displaystyle{\hbox{$\textstyle j\mkern 2.0mu$}}$\crcr}}}{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\kern 0.6152pt\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.76903pt\crcr\kern 1.3pt\cr$\displaystyle{\hbox{$\scriptstyle j\mkern 2.0mu$}}$\crcr}}}{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\kern 0.48387pt\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.60486pt\crcr\kern 1.3pt\cr$\displaystyle{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle j\mkern 2.0mu$}}$\crcr}}}\mkern-2.0mu}{})/N}
:

\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}e^{2\pi i(j+{\mathchoice{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\kern 0.89824pt\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt\kern 1.12283pt\crcr\kern 1.3pt\cr$\displaystyle{\hbox{$\displaystyle j\mkern 2.0mu$}}$\crcr}}}{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\kern 0.89824pt\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt\kern 1.12283pt\crcr\kern 1.3pt\cr$\displaystyle{\hbox{$\textstyle j\mkern 2.0mu$}}$\crcr}}}{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\kern 0.6152pt\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.76903pt\crcr\kern 1.3pt\cr$\displaystyle{\hbox{$\scriptstyle j\mkern 2.0mu$}}$\crcr}}}{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\kern 0.48387pt\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt\kern 0.60486pt\crcr\kern 1.3pt\cr$\displaystyle{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle j\mkern 2.0mu$}}$\crcr}}}\mkern-2.0mu}{})k/N}=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}z^{k}=\begin{cases}N&\text{if}\quad j+{\mathchoice{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\kern 1.1603pt\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt\kern 1.4504pt\crcr\kern 1.3pt\cr$\displaystyle{\hbox{$\displaystyle j\mkern 2.0mu$}}$\crcr}}}{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\kern 1.1603pt\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt\kern 1.4504pt\crcr\kern 1.3pt\cr$\displaystyle{\hbox{$\textstyle j\mkern 2.0mu$}}$\crcr}}}{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\kern 0.9649pt\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt\kern 1.20616pt\crcr\kern 1.3pt\cr$\displaystyle{\hbox{$\scriptstyle j\mkern 2.0mu$}}$\crcr}}}{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\kern 0.86937pt\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt\kern 1.08673pt\crcr\kern 1.3pt\cr$\displaystyle{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle j\mkern 2.0mu$}}$\crcr}}}\mkern-2.0mu}{}=mN,\quad m\in\mathbb{Z},\\
\frac{1-z^{N}}{1-z}=0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Taking into account the above definitions, one can easily obtain the convolution theorem for the product
of two functions
f\text{ and }g
:

\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}f_{j}g_{j}e^{-\frac{2\pi ikj}{N}}=\dfrac{1}{N\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}e^{-\frac{2\pi ikj}{N}}\sum_{p=0}^{N-1}\hat{f_{p}}e^{-\frac{2\pi ipj}{N}}\sum_{q=0}^{N-1}\hat{g_{q}}e^{-\frac{2\pi iqj}{N}}.

Using the orthogonality condition

\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}e^{-\frac{2\pi i(p+q-k)j}{N}}=N\delta_{p+q-k,0},

yields

\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}f_{j}g_{j}e^{-\frac{2\pi ikj}{N}}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\hat{f_{p}}\hat{g}_{k-p}.

So, taking
k=0
and
g=f
, we obtain the
Parseval identity

\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}f_{j}^{2}=\sum_{p=0}^{N-1}\hat{f}_{p}\hat{f}_{-p}=\sum_{p=0}^{N-1}\left|\hat{f}_{p}\right|^{2}.

(51)

Now using the fact that

{\left(\mathop{\rm max}_{\Omega}|{\bm{u}}|\right)}^{2}\leq\sum_{{\bm{x}}}{|{\bm{u}}|^{2}}
and
\sqrt{2}\leq\sqrt{1+k^{2}}
for
{\bm{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\backslash\{\bm{0}\}
, and applying the
Parseval identity (
51
) and
Hölder inequality
, we find

\displaystyle{\left(\mathop{\rm max}_{\Omega}|{\bm{u}}|\right)}^{2}\leq\sum_{{\bm{x}}}{|{\bm{u}}|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\bm{k}}}{|{\bm{u}}_{{\bm{k}}}|^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{{\bm{k}}}{|{\bm{u}}_{{\bm{k}}}|^{2}\sqrt{1+k^{2}}}\ \overset{\text{H\"{o}lder Ineq.}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}{\left(\sum_{{\bm{k}}}{|{\bm{u}}_{{\bm{k}}}|^{2}}\right)}^{1/2}{\left(\sum_{{\bm{k}}}{|{\bm{u}}_{{\bm{k}}}|^{2}(1+k^{2}})\right)}^{1/2}.

(52)

Consequently,

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm max}_{\Omega}|{\bm{u}}|\leq\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[4]{2}}{\left(\sum_{{\bm{k}}}{|{\bm{u}}_{{\bm{k}}}|^{2}}\right)}^{1/4}{\left(\sum_{{\bm{k}}}{|{\bm{u}}_{{\bm{k}}}|^{2}(1+k^{2}})\right)}^{1/4}.

(53)

Comparing to (
50
), we thus see that

C_{A}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[4]{2}}.

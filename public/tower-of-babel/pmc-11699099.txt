Analyzing trends and factors influencing price changes in public pooled drugs procurement system in Malaysia: Exploring market competition
Authors: Farahwahida Mohd Kasim, Ernieda Hatah, Lokhman Hakim Osman, Adliah Mhd Ali, Zaheer-Ud-Din Babar
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPooled procurement is key to managing drug spending amid rising healthcare costs, but conclusive evidence on its sustained impact is lacking, driven by market competition.ObjectiveThis study aims to evaluate the trends and factors influencing the price changes of drugs awarded for pooled procurement systems utilizing a drug price index analysis.MethodsThis retrospective study involved a review of secondary data on public pooled procurement of drugs in Malaysia. Drugs were selected through purposive sampling and focused on eight therapeutic subgroups that showed significant patterns of expenditure and consumption. The observed data are retrieved from centralized contracts that were managed through a tendering process spanning from 2010 to 2021. A price index was computed to assess fluctuations in pharmaceutical pricing and market competition. To assess the influence of potential variables on market competition, quantified through the drug price index, a multiple linear regression analysis was utilized. The variables studied include product age, quantity index, bidding periods, therapeutic subgroups, and market competition elements such as types and number of competing products, dosage form, and types of procurements. The significance level was set at p < 0.05.ResultsThe median price index, derived from the analysis of 88 drugs involved in 335 tenders over 10 years was 89.28 (range between 1.91 and 123.81). The observed trends indicated a consistent decline in the median drug price index throughout the bidding periods. The regression model, encompassing 19 selected variables, demonstrated the ability to predict the drug price index shown by the F-statistic (F (19, 315) = 68.022, p < 0.0005) with adj. R2 = 0.79. The coexistence of innovative or reference products and their generic or biosimilar equivalents in a market may promote competition. Five to eight competing products resulted in remarkably low drug prices.ConclusionPooled procurement is one of the approaches to cost containment that is potentially subject to the dynamics of market competition. Time can be a limiting factor that hinders the realization of greater savings. Therefore, it is important to strike a delicate balance when considering regulatory measures, such as patents and barriers to market entry, in order to maintain a fair and competitive landscape within the industry.Keywords: Drug, Public’s pooled procurement, Centralized contracts, Bidding, Tendering, Competition, Malaysia

Introduction

Global healthcare expenditure has exhibited a sustained increase since the past twenty years (WHO, 2021), posing significant challenges for many countries. This upward trend includes the increased share of government health spending in upper-middle and high-income countries (WHO, 2021). In 2019, global healthcare spending reached a staggering $8.5 trillion, equivalent to 9.8 % of the world's gross domestic product (WHO, 2021). As a result, pharmaceutical spending is projected to hit $1.6 trillion by 2025 with an annual growth rate of approximately 3 % to 6 % excluding expenses related to COVID-19 vaccines (IQVIA Institute For Human Data Science, 2021). The increase in pharmaceutical spending can be attributed to high procurement prices, increased utilization, or a combination of both factors (Luz et al., 2017). To curb pharmaceutical spending, various strategies are currently being employed in different countries. These strategies include pooled procurement, competitive bidding, price negotiation, and the utilization of quality-assured generics and biosimilars (WHO, 2020).

Pooled procurement is emerging as a potential solution to effectively manage drug spending. Pooled procurement aims to increase availability by maintaining or reducing costs, improving quality, and increasing procurement efficiency by pooling resources and negotiating collectively (Parmaksiz et al., 2022). Others may include strategies that may offer the potential for achieving economies of scale by increasing the purchase volume and consolidating multiple buyers into a single entity (Waning et al., 2009). The implementation process of pooled procurement varies and can be carried out at global, regional, national, subnational and organizational levels (Parmaksiz et al., 2022). A recent systematic review on pooled procurement of drugs revealed a consistent pattern of price reduction following the adoption of pooled procurement strategies (Parmaksiz et al., 2022). However, it is worth noting that this study identified a potential bias, as most of the reviewed papers predominantly featured examples of successfully implemented pooled or centralized procurement practices (Seidman and Atun, 2017, Parmaksiz et al., 2022).

In Malaysia, the Ministry of Health (MOH) has been operating drugs' pooled procurement systems for over two decades. These procurement systems encompass two primary approaches: concession-based and centralized contracts (Pharmacy Practice & Development Division 2020). These methods involve competitive bidding, including open tender processes, as well as negotiated tenders between the government and contracted suppliers. By leveraging these procurement strategies, significant cost advantages are realized through economies of scale and enhanced inventory security. The pooled procurement process involves either pooling quantities within MOH facilities or inter-ministerially across MOH, the Ministry of Defense (MOD), and the Ministry of Higher Education (MOHE). The MOH plays a pivotal role in healthcare financing, contributing a substantial share of health expenditure in Malaysia, which comprises 86 % of the total public sector health spending (Malaysia National Health Accounts Section 2021). In the year 2021, the MOH allocated RM 2,552.18 million towards drugs, reflecting a slight year-on-year increment of 1.01 % as compared to 2020 (Pharmaceutical Services Programme 2021). Within this expenditure, a significant portion of 86 % was obtained through the drugs' pooled procurement system. This amounted to RM 1,111 million (44 %) procured through a concessionaire and RM 1,070 million (42 %) through centralized contracts (Pharmaceutical Services Programme 2021). Nevertheless, despite efforts in price containment through public procurement strategies in Malaysia, the cost of drugs continues to persist at levels notably higher, often ranging from 1.5 to three times than international reference prices (IRPs) (Babar et al., 2007, Wong et al., 2019, Hamzah et al., 2020). This gap between local drug pricing and established international reference pricing underscores the need for a comprehensive assessment to better address pricing disparities. The dynamics of market competition must be evaluated to understand the effectiveness of procurement strategies.

Despite ongoing research efforts to explore the potential advantages of pooled procurement, there persist notable gaps in the existing body of knowledge. These gaps are particularly pronounced in terms of understanding the dynamics of market interactions between purchasers and sellers, as well as their relationship with market competition (Dubois et al., 2021). Prior studies have underscored the importance of further investigating factors such as the degree of competition, variations between pharmaceutical molecules, the number of market competitors, and their medium- to long-term repercussions (García-Goñi et al., 2021). To date, limited evidence exists regarding the determinants of price fluctuations in long-term pooled procurement strategies, especially in upper-middle-income countries like Malaysia. Additionally, employing a price index as a key metric in assessing the impact on drug prices over time offers advantages, including increased transparency, reduced susceptibility to external factors such as fluctuating market exchange rates, and adaptability to domestic market conditions. Therefore, the primary objective of this study was to analyze trends and factors influencing competitive markets in the determination of awarded prices within the framework of pooled procurement, specifically centralized contracts, in the Malaysian public healthcare sector through employing a drug price index.

Materials and methods

Study design

A retrospective observational study of secondary data of public’s pooled procurement of pharmaceutical products in Malaysia. Data was collected from the MOH for the public health care sector.

Data sources and selection

Procurement data for drugs were purposefully sampled in this study. Drugs were specifically chosen based on the Ministry of Health Medicines Formulary (MOHMF) list which specifically includes essential drugs regularly procured by the MOH in Malaysia. The trends and factors influencing changes in drug prices within the context of pooled procurement were focused solely on products under centralized contracts, which are entirely administered by the MOH. Initially, all drugs were categorized and organized according to the Anatomical Therapeutic Chemical (ATC) system at hierarchy level two. The ATC system is a standardized classification method used for comparing drug usage across various product levels, ranging from levels one to five (WHO, 2023). Typically, the ATC at level five is employed to establish reference prices, as it pertains to pharmaceuticals with identical chemical substances (WHO, 2020). As of December 31, 2021, the MOHMF encompasses a total of 87 therapeutic subgroups and includes 1,640 different drugs in its inventory.

The examination of drugs that significantly impact expenditure and consumption was carried out following the data from the Malaysian Statistics on Medicines Report 2016. This analysis revealed that sixteen therapeutic subgroups, comprising a total of 658 drugs, stood out as the top ten in terms of spending and the top ten in terms of usage within the public sector in 2016 (Pharmaceutical Services Programme 2020). Out of these sixteen therapeutic subgroups identified as contributing significantly to both high spending and high utilization, further in-depth analysis was focused on eight ATC level two therapeutic subgroups. These eight therapeutic areas encompass drugs used for specific purposes: diabetes (A10), antithrombotic agents (B01), agents acting on the renin-angiotensin system (C09), lipid-modifying agents (C10), antibacterials for systemic use (J01), antineoplastic agents (L01), immunosuppressants (L04), and psycholeptics (N05). Within these eight therapeutic subgroups, a total of 420 drugs were classified. Of these, 232 were identified as items traditionally procured under concession or centralized contracts. The list was refined to include 159 drugs with annual expenditures of more than RM4 million at ATC level five between 2017 and 2021. Of the 159 drugs, 91 drugs were historically purchased through centralized contracts. To conduct a comprehensive trend and factor analysis, drugs that fulfil the inclusion and exclusion criteria were selected for the study. The criteria for including data in the selection process were: The pharmaceutical products or drugs must be registered with the Drug Control Authority (DCA) in Malaysia, the products offered by the bidder must contain the same active pharmaceutical ingredients or be therapeutic equivalents, ensuring comparability, the drug must have been offered and awarded to the successful bidder at least twice during the data collection period. Any incomplete data and bidders who did not fulfil the mandatory requirements in the technical or specification evaluation were excluded from the analysis.

The procurement data were obtained through the acquisition of information from bidding reports and contract databases managed by the Pharmaceutical Services Programme, MOH. The dataset revolves around the bidding and awarding phases for a select range of pharmaceutical products. Bidding involves the submission of price offers and product specifications by participating bidders during the tendering process. In each tender, the specifications were based on the MOHMF, which is established using drug generic names. Conversely, an award signifies the formal recognition granted to a successful tenderer or bidder, designating them as the chosen supplier for a specific contract, which remains valid for a predetermined duration. Observational data related to bids and awards for the selected drugs were collected spanning the years 2010 to 2021. These observational data functions serve as observed prices in the price index, as independent variables, and as reference points for the subsequent factor analysis. Baseline data on the first awarded bids from 1999 to 2019 was used for the computation of the price index. The collected bidding data encompasses the bidding number, bidding year, bidder code, and product information. This product information includes essential attributes like active ingredients, product name, strength, quantity, dosage form, and pack size. The award data includes data collection on elements like the contract number, pricing details, active ingredients, product name, strength, dosage form, quantity, and pack size pertinent to the contracts that have been successfully awarded. Significantly, all pricing data in this study were documented and reported in Ringgit Malaysia, aligning with the contracts granted in the local currency during their respective bidding periods. In the present investigation, the awarded price inherently encompasses the assumption that the bid price took into account the prevailing inflation rate at the time of analysis.

To ensure an adequate sample size for factor analysis, we employed a guideline based on various suggestions in the literature. According to Kyriazos (2018), different rules of thumb have been proposed, such as considering N > 200 or > 300 as adequate, with specific values like 50 considered extremely poor, 100 as poor, 200 as fair, and 300 as acceptable. For regression analysis, the sample size should consider the variance in the data. In cases with very low variance, a minimum sample size of N = 8 is considered informative, while in situations with higher variance, a minimum of N = 25 samples per factor is required (Jenkins and Quintana-Ascencio 2020).

Data analyses

An econometric model was developed to determine the variables affecting changes in drug prices in pooled procurement under the public health system in Malaysia. The variables were selected based on their theoretical relevance and importance in the field. The variables selected for trends and factor analysis as follows:

Price Index = β0 + (β1 · Quantity Index) + (β2· Product Age) + (β3 · Bidding Periods) + (β4 · Therapeutic subgroups) + (β5 · Types of Procurement) + (β6 · Types of Competing Products) + (β7 · Number of Competing Products) + (β8 · Dosage Form) + ε

In this study, the simple or elementary price index was employed to assess the relative changes in the prices awarded during specific bidding periods. To facilitate this analysis, observation and base prices were set for drugs that exhibited similarity in terms of their active ingredients and strength or therapeutic equivalence. These established prices were subsequently adjusted to align with the standardized packaging across all bidding periods to ensure uniformity and fairness in pricing comparisons. A single drug price index (PI) was calculated as follows (Dorin et al., 2020):

PI=Pi,tPi,0x100

Pi, t = Price of the drug in the observation period; t bid.

Pi, t = Price of the drug in the observation period; t bid.

Pi, 0 = Price of the drug in the base period; first bid.

Pi, 0 = Price of the drug in the base period; first bid.

A quantity index (QI) was employed to assess how price fluctuations were influenced by variations in the quantity of drugs awarded during a specific bidding period. The calculation of this quantity index followed a straightforward and unweighted approach as the drugs that were evaluated were considered homogeneous in terms of their characteristics, quality, and significance, making a weighted index unnecessary for the analysis. This formula is based on a direct comparison between the awarded quantity, the observed quantity, and the base quantity as follows:

QI=Qi,tQi,0x100

Qi, t = Quantity of the drug in the observation period; t bid.

Qi, t = Quantity of the drug in the observation period; t bid.

Qi, 0 = Quantity of the drug in the base period; first bid.

Qi, 0 = Quantity of the drug in the base period; first bid.

The base period represents the initial reference point within the bidding periods. The term “first bid” denotes the accepted offer submitted by a supplier in response to the initial tender in a sequence of tenders. During this first bid, both the price index and quantity index for each drug were set at a value of 100. If there was an increase in price or quantity over the observation period, the price index and quantity index would exceed 100. Conversely, if there was a decrease in price or quantity during the observation period, the price or quantity index would be less than 100. Importantly, the baseline data from the first bid were not included in the regression analysis. This decision was made in response to the presence of constant values or a lack of variability in a particular variable, which can potentially lead to multicollinearity issues in regression analysis. Addressing this concern aims to improve the stability and interpretability of the model.

Product age served as an independent variable to investigate the influence of the length of time a product has been available in the market on the price index. The study explored the relationship between the age of award-winning products, considering the time since their registration with the DCA, and the price index. The time was measured in years. The term “bidding periods” represents a chronological sequence of bidding events. These bidding periods are specific time frames in which bids were submitted during the bidding process. Regardless of the number of products bid upon in each case, the variables were labelled as “first bid,” “second bid,” and so on to denote the sequence in which the bids were submitted. For instance, “second bid” indicates the bidding period of the second tender, which subsequently received the contract. Similarly, “third bid” were submitted during the bidding period of the third tender and were also awarded the contract.

The therapeutic subgroups considered in the analyses encompassed A10, B01, C09, C10, J01, L01, L04, and N05. Regarding the procurement type, the study encompassed negotiated tenders and open tenders for centralized contracts pooled exclusively within MOH facilities, and centralized contracts pooled jointly between the three ministries: MOH, MOD, and MOHE. Throughout the bidding period, open tenders served as the primary method for MOH facility contracts, while negotiated tenders or closed tenders were utilized for directly negotiating with a supplier offering a sole product from 2012 to 2016. In 2019, new initiatives were introduced, which included open tender pooled quantities for items commonly used by all three ministries. The competing products were innovator and generic products, or reference and biosimilar products. The “number of competing products” represents the group of products that actively participate in each bidding period. It is important to note that, due to potential withdrawals of products, disqualifications, or determinations of ineligibility during the evaluation process, the actual count of competing products may be fewer than the total number of products initially offered by the bidders. Dosage form refers to oral and parenteral products. Oral products included were immediate-release tablets, modified-release tablets, and solutions. Parenteral products involved were intravenous, intramuscular, and subcutaneous.

Data collection was carried out using Excel worksheets. A thorough check was performed to identify and rectify any data entry errors against the source documents. Each variable, whether entered manually or computed, underwent scrutiny to ensure value consistency, reinforcing the reliability and accuracy of the data. Descriptive statistics, such as the median, were employed to illustrate the data's trends and characteristics. The analysis was conducted using IBM's Statistical Package for Social Science (SPSS®) software version 25.0. Multiple linear regression with backward elimination regression was applied to evaluate whether the studied variables significantly predicted changes in the price index. The independent variables that made the most substantial contributions to a high mean price index were established as reference points for each group variable. The criteria for removal were set if the probability of F was greater than or equal to 0.1. The model sought to identify the most effective combination of predictors that contributed significantly to the prediction equation. Variables were considered significant if their p-values were less than 0.05.

Preliminary analyses were conducted to confirm that there were no violations of the assumptions of independence of observations, multicollinearity, linearity, and normality. The Durbin-Watson test was used to check for autocorrelation or independence of observations, and Variance Inflation Factor (VIF) for multicollinearity (Hair et al., 2019). There was no evidence of multicollinearity if the VIF value was less than 10 (Hair et al., 2019). A plot of the unstandardized residual values versus the unstandardized predicted values was visualized to ensure the residuals are approximately constant or homoscedastic (Hair et al., 2019). Visual inspections of the studentized residuals versus the unstandardized expected values should reveal no obvious patterns, indicating that the assumption of homoscedasticity was satisfied (Hair et al., 2019). Partial regression plots and a plot of studentized residuals against predicted values were used to indicate linearity (Hair et al., 2019).

The residuals of the regression line are also inspected to ensure they are approximately normally distributed. Significant outliers or influential cases were examined through standard deviation, leverage points, and cook distance (Hair et al., 2019). The potential outliers were identified if the regression output obtained a standard deviation from the mean more or less than +/- 3. The influential points were assessed using Cook's Distance, with values less than one indicating no influential points (Hair et al., 2019). The leverage of less than 0.2 as safe, 0.2 to less than 0.5 as risky, and values of 0.5 and above as dangerous (Huber 1981). This is to ensure that the accuracy of predictions from the model developed is not detrimentally affected.

Results

During the period from 2010 to 2021, a total of 89 drugs, which met the specified inclusion and exclusion criteria, were selected from eight distinct therapeutic subgroups. These drugs were chosen to represent the pooled procurement system within the Malaysian public healthcare setting. The analysis encompassed a total of 335 successfully awarded tenders with 830 bid submissions. Of the total awarded tenders, 58.51 % (n = 196) were from bidders who offered only innovator or reference products. The analysis reveals that a significant portion of the awarded tenders (58.81 %, n = 197), involved competition with only one product. Furthermore, the majority of these tenders (69.25 %, n = 232), pertained to oral products. In addition, procurement was primarily conducted through open tender within the MOH (88.06 %, n = 295). The median price index was recorded at 89.28. The range of price movements within the ten years exhibited the highest level of price reduction at 98.09 % and the highest price increase at 23.81 % (price index; min:max = 1.91:123.81). The descriptive statistics for all independent variables related to the price index can be found in Supplementary 1.

A downward trend in the median price index was observed from the second to the last bid, with the price index range decreasing from 99.90 (IQR: 100.00 − 96.98) to 48.76 (IQR: 69.34 − 17.28). Notably, the median price index for the second bid was only 0.10 % lower than the base price index (100.00) of the first bid. The median of the quantity index displayed an increasing trend, with the range expanding from 161.21 (IQR: 208.60 − 118.20) to 1,382.88 (IQR: 2,850.74 − 530.32), indicating an increase in demand with greater variability over time. The overall median of the quantity index is 384.39 (IQR: 831.24 − 177.23), with the highest median at 11,903.58 and the lowest at 8.11. Fig. 1 shows the progression of the median price index and the median quantity index across various bidding periods. The overall age of awarded products has a median value of 10 years (IQR: 14.00 − 6.00) and ranges from less than one year to 27 years. The observed age of awarded products and bidding periods show a consistent increase when an innovative or biological reference product participated in the tender. For a single product participating in a tender (n = 197), the median price index decreases only at the eighth to twelfth bids (78.73, n = 21). In multiple linear regression, preliminary analysis confirmed no violation of the assumptions of multicollinearity, linearity, normality, and homoscedasticity. The result of the Durbin-Watson statistic is 2.070, showing that there is no autocorrelation. All cases have safe leverage below 0.2, and only two cases have leverage considered risky. No leverage value above 0.5 is considered dangerous. This is to ensure the reliability of analysis and prediction accuracy. The regression model with the backward selection method is significantly explains a substantial portion of the variability in the drug price index, F (19, 315) = 68.022, p < 0.0005 with adj. R2 = 0.79. Within the set of examined variables, the bidding periods, particularly the sixth (β = − 14.28, p < 0.0005), and seventh bids (β = − 13.10, p < 0.0005), as well as the eighth to twelfth bids (β = − 19.07, p < 0.0005) exert a significant influence on drugs prices compared to the earlier bidding periods. Conversely, the third bid (β = − 0.09, p = 0.976), fourth bid (β = − 1.29, p = 0.688), and fifth bid (β = − 5.27, p = 0.054) are not statistically significant in predicting the price index relative to the second bid.

By therapeutic subgroups, according to strength of the effect, J01 (β = − 28.15, p < 0.0005), C09 (β = − 13.98, p < 0.0005), N05 (β = − 12.31, p < 0.0005), L01 (β = − 8.53, p = 0.004), and A10 (β = − 8.97, p = 0.007) exhibit significantly lower price indices compared to the reference group (B01 and L04). The therapeutic subgroups B01 (β = − 2.87, p = 0.471) and C10 (β = − 6.72, p = 0.078) do not demonstrate statistically significant associations with the price index when compared to the reference group (L04). The bidding processes that involved two (β = − 17.425, p < 0.0005), three (β = − 18.26, p < 0.0005), and four (β = − 26.95, p < 0.0005) competing products, as well as those with five to eight (β = − 39.16, p < 0.0005) competing products were significantly reduce drug prices when compared to a single product in the bidding process. In addition, tenders that involved a combination of innovator & generic or reference and biosimilar products (β = − 26.68, p < 0.0005), and those participated in by generic or biosimilar products only (β = − 20.30, p < 0.0005) have been found to have lower prices compared to tenders participated in solely by innovator or reference products. Noticing oral products (β = − 5.73, p = 0.010) have been found to have lower prices compared to parenteral products. Quantity index has a small effect (β = − 0.002, p = 0.041) although significantly influences drug prices. While others have a negative relationship with the price index, product age has a positive relationship (β = 0.53, p = 0.005). Open tender for MOH only (β = − 3.78, p = 0.457) and open tender for MOH, MOD and MOHE (β = − 3.55, p = 0.550) are not statistically significantly different from negotiated tender in terms of price index. Discussion

This study elaborated the dynamics and determinants of competitive markets influencing the awarded prices in pooled procurement systems within the public healthcare sector in Malaysia, utilizing a drug price index. Our empirical findings underscore the pivotal role played by market competition in shaping the fluctuations of the drug price index. The observed evolution of the price index reveals that competitive markets tend to exert downward pressure on price levels over time. This is driven by the interplay of supply and demand forces. In contrast, less competitive markets may have some degree of market power to potentially sustain prevailing price levels or, in some cases, facilitate price increases. A majority of the study's findings suggest that procuring drugs through centralized contracts or tendering can result in favorable pricing outcomes, contributing to cost-efficiency in the public healthcare system (Dubois et al., 2021, Li et al., 2021, Seidman and Atun, 2017, Wouters et al., 2019). Nevertheless, our results showed that the effect and strength of the centralized contract to reduce drug prices potentially depend on variables such as bidding periods, type and number of competing products participating in the tender, dosage form, age of awarded products, and quantity purchased.

The results of this study have shown that price reduction in a pooled tender occurs in several steps. First, the more tenders opened for bidding, the more intense the competition. This provides more opportunities for a greater number of competitors to participate in the tender. The current tendering process by the Malaysian MOH may partly contribute to the bidders' offers. The bids and awarded value are officially announced to the public to ensure a fair and transparent system. In this context, the previous contracted price often serves as a benchmark for bidders to competitively propose in new tenders, despite the various tactics they might employ. The findings of our study demonstrate that a significant price reduction was notably achieved starting from the sixth bid. Consequently, if the bidding period occurs every two to three years, and a substantial price reduction is only attainable after the sixth bid, then it would take about 12 years or more on average to realize significant cost savings within this pooled procurement system. This could be due to the expiration of patents, which leads to more products becoming available in the market, thereby significantly driving prices down. Alternatively, delays in market entry due to factors such as data exclusivity or protection can also impact the number of available products and influence price competition. This scenario becomes even more apparent when there are limited competitors in the market, as was the case in our study where most samples involved single competing products in the early bidding period. Even in cases where there are multiple bidders competing for a sole product, price competition may still be limited compared to when there are multiple competing products. This aligns with other research suggesting that centralized procurement alone may not be sufficient to lower drug prices when the supply side is highly concentrated (Dubois et al., 2021).

Second, several conditions must align to establish a competitive environment. These conditions include the presence of numerous competitors, participants sharing a common understanding of market potential, and a framework that allows for free entry and exit (Mankiw, 2018; OECD, 2021). In this context, the primary market structure such as monopoly, oligopoly, monopolistic competition and perfect competition plays an important role in shaping the price formation. The main characteristics of monopolists are cost determiner and hence have the power to raise prices and restrict supply volume (Qaqaya and Lipimile, 2008). Conversely, in perfect competition, each vendor sells identical products, and individual sellers have no influence over pricing. However, real-world competition often falls along the spectrum between these two extremes, making definitive categorization challenging. In microeconomics, the interaction is justified through the theory of consumers, which clarifies how buyers prioritize maximizing their satisfaction (utility), while the theory of firms elucidates how companies prioritize maximizing their profits (V. Eastin and L. Arbogast, 2011). This principle comes into play when established firms start turning a profit with their products; it tends to attract new competitors into the market (Mankiw, 2018). Therefore, over time, the market has seen an increasing number of products that can potentially participate in bidding, leading to a more competitive environment that exerts downward pressure on drug prices. The correlation between the number of products on the market and their impact on prices has been extensively studied. Numerous research studies have confirmed a statistically significant inverse relationship between the number of manufacturers, products, or competitors and the percentage change in drug prices (Dubois et al., 2021, Helble and Aizawa, 2016, Li et al., 2018). Our study's results align with these findings, showing a consistent pattern of price reduction gained by the buyer as soon as two competing products are involved in the bidding process. This downward price trend continues to intensify as competition increases, particularly when there are five to eight competing products in the mix.

Third, the study underscores that higher-frequency tenders, featuring a diverse range of competing products, lead to lower prices and result in substantial cost savings. The most intense level of competition was observed when competitors included innovator-generic or reference-biosimilar bidders. The presence of both innovator and generic products or reference and biosimilar products potentially enhances competition and offers consumers a broader spectrum of choices to cater to their specific needs. This diversity serves as an incentive for innovators and reference product manufacturers to develop strategies that allow them to maintain their market share in a competitive landscape. These strategies often revolve around establishing a brand or product image and identity, a concept known as market positioning. Effective market positioning influences consumer perceptions of a brand or product about its competitors, thereby influencing purchasing decisions (Saqib, 2021). This finding is relevant to our own, which suggests that older products tend to receive awards over time. Moreover, our research has unveiled a trend where the price of a product gradually increases as it remains on the market for an extended period. This phenomenon can be attributed to the fact that more than half of the awarded tenders analyzed in our study feature innovative or reference products that are older than 15 years. The dominance of such well-established products may contribute to a static or high price index, even when the product has been in the market for a prolonged period. Furthermore, buyers might perceive certain innovative or reference products as non-interchangeable with generics, which gives an advantage to these long-standing products. Consequently, product age emerges as a critical factor in evaluating the maturity, relevance, and competitiveness of a product within its respective industry or market niche.

Fourth, according to economic theory, demand, and manufacturing costs are the most important considerations in drug pricing (Peterson et al., 1998). In economies of scale, the long-run average total cost experiences a decline as the quantity of output increases (Mankiw, 2018). This signifies that as a firm expands its scale of operations, it becomes more cost-efficient, resulting in a reduction in the average cost per unit of output over the long term. This cost per unit of output yet depends on various factors such as fixed and variable cost in production (Nwokoye and Ilechukwu, 2018). Hence, while a large quantity-based contract potentially influences the reduction of drug prices (Li et al., 2021). In this context, it is crucial to recognize that economies of scale aren't the sole factor that determines which companies can provide lower prices. Large purchase quantities do not always result in lower prices for certain drugs (Waning et al., 2009). Additionally, the effectiveness of volume-price contract strategies in reducing unit prices can vary based on the specific drug and supplier (Li et al., 2021). This study confirms that quantity does play a role in price reduction, but its impact is relatively smaller compared to other variables.

Fifth, standard economic theory posits that competition represents a static end-state in which firms cannot consistently overcharge and generate abnormal profits (OECD, 2021). In equilibrium, competition is dormant and only becomes active in disequilibrium (Nutter and Moore, 1976). In an equilibrium state, competition lies dormant and only becomes active when there is a disturbance in the equilibrium. The conditions of equilibrium are marked by a balance in the number of known quantities and the prices to be determined (Stingler, 1942). In contrast, disequilibrium arises from either a surplus or a shortage in the market. If a company finds it advantageous to lower its prices while accepting its competitors' prices as given, it indicates that the market has not yet reached equilibrium (Farm, 2017). Therefore, the more a market deviates from perfect competition, the more likely prices are to be higher (OECD, 2018). In this study, a general trend of decreasing prices was observed throughout the bidding periods. However, the thresholds for price changes vary across different therapeutic subgroups. This suggests that variations in the drug price index within pooled procurement, among different therapeutic subgroups and dosage forms, may be attributed to distinct states of equilibrium within those markets. Certain types of drugs and dosage forms, such as oral medications, may experience greater price decreases compared to others, like parenteral medications. This is likely because more products in the market are produced in these more common dosage forms, consequently, increased competition among manufacturers typically leads to observable price reductions.

Overall, this study focuses on the microeconomics of how a purchaser makes decisions based on firms’ behavior over time in specific pharmaceutical markets. The present study also highlights that market competition within therapeutic subgroups in the Malaysian public pooled procurement system has not yet reached equilibrium. There are no significant differences in price effects based on the types of procurement. This is because most single products are still procured through open or competitive bidding, where competition among suppliers retains the potential to drive down drug prices. Moreover, each procurement types takes place at different bidding stages, contingent on the level of participation by market competitors. It is important to note that the selection of companies into the bidding process and the eventual winner may not solely depend on medicine price alone. Stakeholders may need to consider other crucial factors such as the company’s financial profile, product complaint history, previous supply performance, delivery period, and so forth when selecting the winner. However, decision-making not related to price may not fully reveal the extent of competition considerations during the selection of winning bidders, as price remains a central element in determining competition within any market (WHO, 2020). One of the strengths of this study lies in its focus on the long-term implications of the standardized bidding process, offering insights into the dynamics of market competition and factors that may influence it unlike the previous studies which predominantly concentrated on assessing the short or long-term effects of price in pooled procurement or tendering (Dubois et al., 2021, Kim and Skordis-Worrall, 2017, Li et al., 2021, Wouters et al., 2019).

Understanding the factors that contribute to lower drug prices can enhance the procurement process and establish more efficient approaches for drug price reduction. Pharmaceutical policies related to pricing mechanisms should be adapted to the local market, emphasizing transparency, stability, and predictability to succeed (Nguyen et al., 2015). Therefore, it is crucial to critically evaluate and implement regulatory or government policies that, unintentionally, may hinder market competition, including any exclusivity like patents or other barriers to market entry (Gurgula, 2020). Encouraging generic competition can enhance market competition. To have a level playing field, policies that foster a competitive market environment by leveraging the number of suppliers, such as supporting new players and small and medium-sized pharmaceutical enterprises, should be facilitated. Complex regulatory requirements that increase the timeliness of evaluation can be supplemented through a collaborative network that promotes drug regulatory convergence and harmonization (Feng et al., 2024). The inclusion of ATC drug groups in our regression analysis helps identify specific therapeutic areas where price reductions have or have not occurred, guiding policymakers in targeting specific groups of drugs for further analysis and possible intervention. Continuous market assessment will help further understand the market competition landscape.

This study is subject to several limitations. Firstly, it assesses factors from the standpoint of public purchasers, and its findings may not encompass supplier perspectives or apply to other settings that might have distinct practices in price-setting mechanisms due to nuanced differences or heterogeneity. While this study focuses on Malaysia, the methodologies and analytical frameworks employed can serve as valuable templates for similar studies in other countries or regions. Researchers in different contexts can adapt and extend our approaches to investigate purchaser-driven determinants of drug prices within their respective healthcare systems, thereby enhancing the comparability and generalizability of findings across different settings. Secondly, the analysis might not encompass all factors influencing the awarded price, like individual product differentiation such as specific clinical outcomes, and other market dynamics. Thirdly, the study did not separately evaluate the inflation rate from other drug costs. This is important since the bidding price set by pharmaceutical companies may have already incorporated this factor in their pricing strategies. Thirdly, this study may face an endogeneity issue, where certain variables, such as quantity, could be correlated with the error term. However, in the Malaysian public healthcare system, the tendering process typically relies on predetermined specifications, including quantity, years of awarded tender, and the location of supply, thus minimizing the endogeneity issue. Companies often consider these factors when formulating their pricing strategies before the bidding stage. Fourthly, the current study utilized a single drug price index across bidding periods which is a less commonly employed method compared to Laspeyres, Paasche, and Fisher indexes. However, using a single drug price index offers specificity, allowing in-depth analysis to track a drug's pricing changes over time in response to market fluctuations. This approach becomes particularly valuable in scenarios where computing a comprehensive basket price index is impractical due to varying time frames across bidding periods. The calculation of a price index also only includes prices of goods that have reached the purchase stage (the winning bid) to reflect actual transaction prices, which represent the real economic cost to purchasers and suppliers. Therefore, the non-winning bids that may not result in actual sales or accurately reflect actual purchasing behavior or market conditions in a pooled procurement system were not included. Finally, this study focuses on actual competition, which is entirely dependent on companies' decisions to participate in the tender. A more ideal approach would be to consider potential competition based on the types and numbers of product registries in the market, rather than focusing solely on the products actively participating in the bidding.

Conclusion

The fluctuations in drug prices within Malaysia’s public healthcare pooled procurement settings are closely associated with various factors such as the types and number of competing products in bidding, the bidding periods, therapeutic subgroups, dosage form, quantity, and awarded product age during the bidding process. Furthermore, the study reveals that the presence of both innovator or reference products and their generic or biosimilar equivalents in the market may foster a competitive environment, leading to favorable pricing outcomes. Conversely, the delay in achieving substantial cost savings within the pooled procurement system is linked to the number of bidding cycles, particularly evident in the sixth bid, warranting further investigation. Factors influencing this delay, such as government regulations or policies, may need to be studied more comprehensively. Future studies may examine drug price dynamics in different procurement methods in the public healthcare setting in Malaysia. This integrated analysis can contribute to a more comprehensive understanding of market behavior and the factors influencing pricing trends, aiding in the formulation of effective policies and strategies.

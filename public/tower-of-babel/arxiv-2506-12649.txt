Beyond Dicke superradiance: Universal scaling of the peak emission rate
Raphael Holzinger, Susanne F. Yelin

Abstract
In quantum optics, superradiance is a phenomenon in which a system of N N fully excited quantum emitters radiate intense flashes of light during collective decay. However, computing its peak intensity for many spatially separated emitters remained challenging due to the exponential growth of the underlying Hilbert space with system size N N . Derived from exact numerics, we present an analytically compact and easily computable formula for the peak photon emission rate of fully excited quantum emitter systems undergoing superradiant decay. The result applies to emitter configurations in any geometry and reveals a universal behavior: spatially extended quantum systems have a geometry-dependent optimal emitter number and always reach a peak photon emission rate that increases only linearly with N N . Our results encompass quantum systems ranging from cold atomic gases and neutral-atom arrays to molecular aggregates and solid-state materials.

To address this, we derive a new, general formula for computing the peak photon emission rate, solely based on the properties of the initial state. It can be efficiently evaluated and circumvents the need for computing the early time evolution of the system. Remarkably, it requires only the evaluation of the second-order correlation function of the initial fully excited state, which itself depends simply on the dissipative dipole-dipole couplings between emitters, i.e. the geometric arrangement and radiative environment

Robicheaux 2021

These couplings encapsulate the collective nature of decay and can be computed directly from the electromagnetic Green’s function, independent of the ensemble’s specific configuration or emitter type. We demonstrate that, upon appropriate normalization, peak emission rates across a wide range of geometries and system sizes collapse onto a single, universal scaling curve

Introduced here for the first time, this empirical function, based on exact numerical data, defines a universal law for superradiant peak emission across diverse platforms, dramatically simplifying its prediction. Interestingly, we find that there exists a geometry-dependent optimal emitter number at which the peak emission is maximized before transitioning to linear scaling. This result offers a powerful and general tool for predicting superradiant peak emission in extended, fully excited quantum systems, enabling large-scale modeling of correlated decay in quantum optics, condensed matter, and other correlated quantum systems.

Conclusions and outlook
– We have shown that the exponentially hard problem of determining the peak emission rate from an initially fully excited ensemble of dipole emitters admits an unexpectedly compact solution. By recognizing that the two-photon correlation of the initial state maps any quantum system coupled to a 3D photonic environment onto a universal scaling curve, we derived an analytic expression applicable to arbitrary configurations—ordered or disordered.

This result provides two key insights into collective decay. First, superlinear scaling of the peak emission rate occurs exclusively in the Dicke limit, where emitters are perfectly indistinguishable. Second, a superradiant peak persists only up to a finite, spacing-dependent emitter number, beyond this threshold, the emission rate necessarily crosses over to a universal linear scaling with
N
. Since our formula expresses
R_{\text{peak}}
in terms of the readily computed quantity in Eq. (
3
), it eliminates the need for exponentially costly simulations of early-time dynamics.

These findings carry immediate implications. They establish fundamental limits on the size and brightness of superradiant bursts in large quantum emitter ensembles and yield a practical design principle: to maximize
R_{\text{peak}}
, one should choose the optimal emitter number
N
and spacing
a
—or, in disordered systems, the appropriate emitter density
Holzinger and Yelin 2025
.

Experimentally, verifying the predicted linear scaling in the largest accessible arrays and mapping the dependence of the optimal size
N_{\text{opt}}
on lattice constant and dimensionality is within reach of current platforms. Complementary investigations in single-mode waveguides, where our Supplemental Material predicts a superlinear
N^{3/2}
scaling, could further clarify how the photonic density of states reshapes scaling laws. On the theoretical side, our approach can be extended to structured or chiral reservoirs
Liedl et al. 2024
;
Lodahl et al. 2017
, as well as to realistic array imperfections
Rubies-Bigorda et al. 2023
. Finally, while we have assumed collection of all emitted light, extending the framework to directional detection, i.e. monitoring emission within a specific solid angle, should be achievable by modifying the second-order correlation expression in Eq. (
3
) accordingly
Masson et al. 2024
;
Robicheaux 2021
.

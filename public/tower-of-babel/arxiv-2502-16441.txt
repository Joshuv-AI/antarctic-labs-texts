Introduction

0.1.
Affine Lusztig varieties and affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties

The primary focus of this paper is on the affine analogues of the Lusztig varieties, specifically the affine Lusztig varieties. For simplicity, the introduction is limited to discussing split groups in the equal characteristic case. However, the main body of the paper extends the study to arbitrary reductive groups in both equal and mixed characteristic settings.

Let
L
be the field of Laurent series over an algebraically closed field and
\mathbf{G}
be a connected reductive group split over
L
. Let
\breve{G}=\mathbf{G}(L)
be the corresponding loop group and
\mathcal{K}=\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O}_{L})
be its hyperspecial subgroup. The affine Lusztig varieties in the affine Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}=\breve{G}/\mathcal{K}
and the affine flag variety
\mathrm{Fl}=\breve{G}/\mathcal{I}
are defined by

\displaystyle Y^{\mathbf{G}}_{\mu}(\gamma)=\{g\mathcal{K}\in\mathrm{Gr};g^{-1}\gamma g\in\mathcal{K}t^{\mu}\mathcal{K}\},

\displaystyle Y_{w}^{\mathbf{G}}(\gamma)=\{g\mathcal{I}\in\mathrm{Fl};g^{-1}\gamma g\in\mathcal{I}\dot{w}\mathcal{I}\}.

Here
\gamma
is a regular semisimple element in
\breve{G}
,
\mu
is a dominant coweight of
\breve{G}
, and
w
is an element in the Iwahori–Weyl group of
\breve{G}
. It was first introduced by Lusztig in
[
20
]
. In the affine Grassmannian case, it was also studied by Kottwitz and Viehmann
[
19
]
. In the literature, affine Lusztig varieties are also referred to as generalized affine Springer fibers or Kottwitz–Viehmann varieties.

Affine Lusztig varieties are locally closed subschemes of the affine Grassmannian and the affine flag variety, equipped with a reduced scheme structure. They naturally arise in the study of orbital integrals of spherical Hecke algebras and Iwahori–Hecke algebras, and they serve as building blocks for the (conjectural) theory of affine character sheaves.

Affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties are defined in a similar way. Let
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}
be a connected reductive group split over
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\!(\epsilon)\!)
and
L^{\prime}=\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(\!(\epsilon)\!)
. Let
\breve{G}^{\prime}=\mathbf{G}^{\prime}(L^{\prime})
and
\sigma
be the Frobenius morphism on
\breve{G}^{\prime}
. Let
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
be a
\sigma
-stable Iwahori subgroup of
\breve{G}^{\prime}
. Let
b\in\breve{G}^{\prime}
and
w
be an element in the Iwahori–Weyl group of
\breve{G}^{\prime}
. The affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties are defined by

\displaystyle X^{\mathbf{G}}_{\mu}(b)=\{g\mathcal{K}^{\prime};g^{-1}b\sigma(g)\in\mathcal{K}^{\prime}t^{\mu}\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\},

\displaystyle X_{w}^{\mathbf{G}^{\prime}}(b)=\{g\mathcal{I}^{\prime};g^{-1}b\sigma(g)\in\mathcal{I}^{\prime}\dot{w}\mathcal{I}^{\prime}\}.

Here
b\in\breve{G}^{\prime}
,
\mu
is a dominant coweight of
\breve{G}^{\prime}
, and
w
is an element in the Iwahori–Weyl group of
\breve{G}^{\prime}
. The notion of an affine Deligne–Lusztig variety was first introduced by Rapoport
[
24
]
and plays an important role in arithmetic geometry and the Langlands program.

Despite the similar definitions of affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties and affine Lusztig varieties, there is no direct geometric connection between these two families of varieties. However, the main result of
[
10
]
establishes the following numerical connection between these varieties:

Suppose that the group
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}
over
F^{\prime}
is associated with the group
\mathbf{G}
over

and the regular semisimple conjugacy class of
\gamma
in
\breve{G}
is associated with the
\sigma
-conjugacy class of
b

\breve{G}^{\prime}

w\in\tilde{W}
,

•

Y^{\mathbf{G}}_{w}(\gamma)\neq\emptyset
if and only if
X^{\mathbf{G}^{\prime}}_{w}(b)\neq\emptyset
;

•

In this case
\dim Y^{\mathbf{G}}_{w}(\gamma)=\dim X^{\mathbf{G}^{\prime}}_{w}(b)+\dim Y^{\mathbf{G}}_{\gamma}
.

The notions of associated groups and associated conjugacy classes were introduced in
[
10
]
(see also Theorem
1.1

associated with

0.2.
Main result

For a scheme
X
, we denote by
\Sigma^{{\rm top}}(X)
the set of irreducible components of
X
of dimension equals to
\dim(X)
. For any regular semisimple element
\gamma\in\breve{G}
, its centralizer
Z_{\breve{G}}(\gamma)
acts on
Y^{\mathbf{G}}_{w}(\gamma)
and on
\Sigma^{{\rm top}}(Y^{\mathbf{G}}_{w}(\gamma))
. Similarly, for any
b\in\breve{G}^{\prime}
, the
\sigma
-centralizer
J_{b}
acts on
X^{\mathbf{G}^{\prime}}_{w}(b)
and on
\Sigma^{{\rm top}}(X^{\mathbf{G}^{\prime}}_{w}(b))
.

The main result of this paper is to enumerate the irreducible components of affine Lusztig varieties. The description is given in terms of reduction paths, a notion introduced in
[
14
]
to encode the Deligne-Lusztig reduction steps. We refer to §
2.2
for the precise definition.

We first recall the description of the irreducible components of affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties.

(a)
\sharp\bigl(J_{b}\backslash\Sigma^{{\rm top}}(X^{\mathbf{G}^{\prime}}_{w}(b))\bigr)
equals to the number of reduction paths in a reduction tree of
w
that correspond to the
\sigma
-conjugacy class
[b]
and have the correct lengths.

We may rewrite it in a slightly different way as

(a’)
\sharp\bigl(J_{b}\backslash\Sigma^{{\rm top}}(X^{\mathbf{G}^{\prime}}_{w}(b))\bigr)=\sum_{\underline{p}}1
, where
\underline{p}
runs over the reduction paths in a reduction tree of
w
that correspond to the
\sigma
-conjugacy class
[b]
and have the correct lengths.

Now we state our main result.

Theorem 0.1
.

Let
\gamma
be a regular semisimple element in
\breve{G}
. Then for any
w\in\tilde{W}
, we have

\sharp\bigl(Z_{\breve{G}}(\gamma)\backslash\Sigma^{{\rm top}}(Y^{\mathbf{G}}_{w}(\gamma))\bigr)=\sum_{\underline{p}}n_{\underline{p},\gamma},

where
\underline{p}
runs over the reduction paths in a reduction tree of
w
that correspond to the conjugacy class
\{\gamma\}
and have the correct lengths and
n_{\underline{p},\gamma}
is the number of
Z_{\breve{G}}(\gamma)
-orbits on the irreducible components of affine Springer fibers associated with the pair
(\gamma,\underline{p})
.

We refer to §
2.5
and Theorem
2.8
for the definitions and the precise statement.

0.3.
Comparison theorem on the affine Lusztig varieties and affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties

It is worth pointing out that the number
n_{\underline{p},\gamma}
does not equal to
1
in general. It was shown by Tsai in
[
27
]
that for split groups, when
\gamma
is “close enough” to the identity element, the number of
Z_{\breve{G}}(\gamma)
-orbits on the set of top dimensional irreducible components of the affine Springer fibers associated with
\gamma
in the affine flag equals to the cardinality of the Weyl group.

However, we discovered that for the split group and the regular semismimple element
\gamma
with integral Newton point, when
w
becomes “larger”, the difference between the irreducible components of the affine Lusztig varieties and the affine Deligne-Lusztig variety disappears. This leads to our second main result.

Theorem 0.2
.

Suppose that
\mathbf{G}
is split over
L
and
\gamma
has integral Newton vector. Let
(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},[b])
be the pair associated with
(\mathbf{G},\{\gamma\})
, then for “almost all”
w\in\tilde{W}
, we have

\sharp\bigl(Z_{\breve{G}}(\gamma)\backslash\Sigma^{{\rm top}}(Y^{\mathbf{G}}_{w}(\gamma))\bigr)=\sharp\bigl(J_{b}\backslash\Sigma^{{\rm top}}(X^{\mathbf{G}^{\prime}}_{w}(b))\bigr).

(0.1)

Here “almost all” means that the exceptional set for the pairs
(w,\{\gamma\})
is small with respect to measure, not cardinality. We refer to Theorem
3.6
for the precise statement.

As a special case, we deduce the explicit description on the irreducible components of affine Lusztig varieties in the affine Grassmannian for regular semisimple elements with integral Newton vector in split groups, verifying a conjecture of Chi
[
2
]
for integral Newton points.

Theorem 0.3
.

If
\mathbf{G}
is split over
L
and the Newton vector of
\gamma
is integral, then for any dominant coweight
\mu
such that
Y_{\mu}(\gamma)\neq\emptyset
, we have

\sharp\bigl(Z_{\breve{G}}(\gamma)\backslash\Sigma^{{\rm top}}(Y^{\mathbf{G}}_{\mu}(\gamma))\bigr)=\dim V_{\mu}(\lambda_{\gamma}),

where
V_{\mu}(\lambda_{\gamma})
is certain weight space of the irreducible representation
V_{\mu}
of the Langlands dual group of
\mathbf{G}
.

0.4.
Strategy

We first provide a brief review of the strategy and the main ingredients in
[
10
]
to establish the comparison theorem on the dimension of affine Lusztig varieties and affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties.

In
[
10
]
, the basic strategy involves two parts:

•

base case: here
w
is in the Weyl group of a standard parahoric subgroup
\mathcal{K}_{1}
of
\breve{G}
. In this case, the affine Lusztig variety
Y^{\mathbf{G}}_{w}(\gamma)
maps to the affine Springer fiber
Y
associated with
\gamma
in the partial flag variety
\breve{G}/\mathcal{K}_{1}
, and the fibers are the (classical) Lusztig varieties.

–

The affine Springer fiber
Y
consists of two parts: the regular locus and the non-regular locus;

–

By the work of Kazhdan and Lusztig
[
16
]
on the regular locus of
Y
and a detailed analysis on the classical Lusztig varieties, the subvariety of
Y^{\mathbf{G}}_{w}(\gamma)
over
Y^{{\mathrm{reg}}}
has the desired dimension.

•

Inductive step: We apply Deligne-Lusztig reduction
[
7
]
and Hodge-Newton decomposition
[
4
]
,
[
10
]
to reduce to the case where
w
is in a(n extended) standard parahoric subgroup of
\tilde{W}
.

It is worth noting that the reduction paths in the inductive step are rather complicated and that there is no good way to keep track of them.

However, for the affine Lusztig varieties and affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties, they share the same reduction paths. This makes it possible to compare the dimensions of these two families of schemes.

Following a similar strategy, we prove Theorem
0.1
. The proof of Theorem
0.2
is more subtle. As mentioned above, the comparison result (
0.1
) is not valid for all

. Thus one needs to carefully choose the base case to start with.

•

base case : here
\mathbf{G}
is split over
L
and
\gamma
is an element in
\mathcal{I}
.

–

By the works of Ngo
[
22
]
and Chi
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
, the centralizer
Z_{\breve{G}}(\gamma)
acts transitively on the regular locus of affine Springer fiber associated with
\gamma
in the affine Grassmannian;

–

By
[
11
]
, the classical Lusztig varieties associated with the elliptic Weyl group element and regular element in the group is irreducible.

Combining these works, the base case for the irreducible components is where
w
is an elliptic element in the hyperspecial subgroup of
\tilde{W}
.

•

Inductive step:

–

By the work of
[
15
]
and
[
23
]
on the conjecture of X. Zhu and M. Rapoport for the
J_{b}
-action on the affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties in the affine Grassmannian. These works provide crucial information on the structure of affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties associated with
w_{0}t^{\mu}
in the affine flag variety.

–

Partial Conjugation Method in
[
6
]
: This technique allows us to handle some part of the reduction paths more effectively and to extend the results from the elements of the form

to ”almost all” elements in
\tilde{W}

Using these strategies and tools, we can establish the main comparison theorem on the irreducible components of affine Lusztig varieties and affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties for a broad class of elements in
\tilde{W}
.

1.5.
Dimension formula

In
[

, Theorem 5.6]
, we established the following comparison result on the dimension formula of affine Lusztig varieties and affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties.

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose that
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}
is associated with
\mathbf{G}
. Let
\gamma

(1)

if and only if

(2)

If

, then
\dim Y_{w}(\gamma)=\dim X_{w}(b)+\dim Y_{\gamma}.

Combining Theorem
1.4
with our previous knowledge on the affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties, we obtain the explicit description on the nonemptiness pattern and dimension formula of the affine Lusztig varieties in most cases. We first recall the definitions of the defect and virtual dimension.

For
b\in\breve{G}^{\prime}
, the
defect
of
b
is defined as
\operatorname{def}(b)=\rank_{F}\mathbf{G}^{\prime}-\rank_{F}\mathbf{J}_{b}
, where for a reductive group
\mathbf{H}
defined over
F

is the
F
-rank of the group
\mathbf{H}
. Note that any element in
\tilde{W}
can be written in a unique way as

, where

,

is a dominant coweight such that

. We set

. For any

and

is defined as

Here

For

We have the following results on the nonemptiness pattern and dimension formula of affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties and affine Lusztig varieties for almost all cases.

[

.

[

Let

2.2.
Reduction trees

The reduction trees for

was introduced in
[

. The reduction tree encodes the Deligne-Lusztig reduction for the affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties associated to

and the (oriented) edges are of the form

For
x,y\in\tilde{W}

if there exists

and

with

and

The reduction tree of a minimal length in its conjugacy class consists of the element itself and no edges. Suppose that
w
is not of minimal length in its
\sigma
-conjugacy class of
\tilde{W}
and that a reduction tree is given for any

with

. By Theorem

, there exists

and

and the reduction tree for

and

. We write

. The

of the reduction path

is the number of edges in
\underline{p}

We denote by

the set of reduction paths in

. For any

the set of reduction paths

in

with

. Similarly, for any

, we denote by

the set of reduction paths

in
\mathcal{T}
with

.

The following connection between the reduction trees and the affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties is given in

2.3.
Enumeration of irreducible components of affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties

We first give a description of the irreducible components of affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties in terms of reduction paths.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
w\in\tilde{W}
and
\mathcal{T}
be a reduction tree of
\mathcal{T}
. Then for any

with

\sharp\bigl(J_{b}\backslash\Sigma^{{\rm top}}(X_{w}(b))\bigr)=\sharp\{\underline{p}\in{\rm RP}_{[b]}(\mathcal{T});\ell(\underline{p})+\ell(\text{end}(\underline{p}))-\langle\bar{\nu}_{\text{end}(\underline{p})},2\rho\rangle=\dim X_{w}(b)\}.

Remark 2.6
.

It is shown in
[

, Theorem 2.19]
that the number of

-orbits on

equals the leading coefficient of the corresponding class polynomial of the associated affine Hecke algebra. One may deduce the above formula from loc.cit. For the convenience of the readers, below we give a proof without referring to the class polynomials.

Proof.

We first consider the case where

is a minimal length element in its conjugacy class in
\tilde{W}
. In this case, by
[
7
, Theorem 4.8]
,

. Also by definition,

consists of only one vertex, the element

itself. The statement holds in this case.

Now we consider the general case. Let

\displaystyle\Sigma^{{\rm top}}(X_{w}(b))

\displaystyle=\bigsqcup_{\underline{p}\in{\rm RP}_{[b]}(\mathcal{T});\ell(\underline{p})+\dim X_{\text{end}(\underline{p})}(b)=d}\Sigma^{{\rm top}}(X_{(\underline{p})}(b))

\displaystyle\cong\bigsqcup_{\underline{p}\in{\rm RP}_{[b]}(\mathcal{T});\ell(\underline{p})+\dim X_{\text{end}(\underline{p})}(b)=d}\Sigma^{{\rm top}}(X_{\text{end}(\underline{p})}(b))

2.4.

We consider the group
\breve{G}

Lemma 3.3

Since

and

for any

We first show that there exists a sequence

of length

Let
w_{0}(yx)^{-1}=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{l}}
be a reduced expression. Then we have
\ell(s_{i_{j+1}}\cdots s_{i_{l}}yx)=l-j+\ell(yx)
for any

\ell(s_{i_{j+1}}\cdots s_{i_{l}}yxt^{\mu})=l-j+\ell(yx)+\ell(t^{\mu})
and

\ell(s_{i_{j+2}}\cdots s_{i_{l}}yxt^{\mu}s_{i_{j+1}})=l-(j+1)+\ell(yx)+\ell(t^{\mu})-1=\ell(s_{i_{j+1}}\cdots s_{i_{l}}t^{\mu})-2.

So
s_{i_{j+1}}\cdots s_{i_{l}}t^{\mu}\rightharpoonup s_{i_{j+2}}\cdots s_{i_{l}}t^{\mu}
for any
j
. Thus we obtain a sequence

w_{0}t^{\mu}=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{l}}yxt^{\mu}\rightharpoonup s_{i_{2}}\cdots s_{i_{l}}yxt^{\mu}\rightharpoonup\cdots\rightharpoonup yxt^{\mu}

of length
\ell(w_{0})-\ell(yx)
.

Let

be a reduced expression. Then we have

\ell(s_{k_{j}}\cdots s_{k_{m}}xt^{\mu}s_{k_{1}}\cdots s_{k_{j-1}})=\ell(s_{k_{j}}\cdots s_{k_{m}}x)+\ell(t^{\mu})-(j-1)

and

\displaystyle\ell(s_{k_{j+1}}\cdots s_{k_{m}}xt^{\mu}s_{k_{1}}\cdots s_{k_{j}})

\displaystyle=\ell(s_{k_{j+1}}\cdots s_{k_{m}}x)+\ell(t^{\mu})-j

\displaystyle\leqslant\ell(s_{k_{j}}\cdots s_{k_{m}}x)+1+\ell(t^{\mu})-j

\displaystyle=\ell(s_{k_{j}}\cdots s_{k_{m}}xt^{\mu}s_{k_{1}}\cdots s_{k_{j-1}}).

So we have a sequence

\displaystyle yxt^{\mu}

\displaystyle=s_{k_{1}}\cdots s_{k_{m}}xt^{\mu}s_{k_{2}}\cdots s_{k_{m}}xt^{\mu}s_{k_{1}}\cdots

\displaystyle xt^{\mu}s_{k_{1}}\cdots s_{k_{m}}=xt^{\mu}y.

In this sequence, each arrow either preserves the lengths of the elements or decrease the length by

equals to
\frac{1}{2}(\ell(yxt^{\mu})-\ell(xt^{\mu}y))=\frac{1}{2}(\ell(yx)-\ell(x)+\ell(y))
. By deleting the edges (and the corresponding elements) which preserves the lengths, we obtain a sequence

yxt^{\mu}\rightharpoonup\cdots\rightharpoonup xt^{\mu}y

of length
\frac{1}{2}(\ell(yx)-\ell(x)+\ell(y))
.

The statement follows by combining the sequence (a) with the sequence (b).

Lemma 3.4

and
\mu\in X_{*}(T)^{+}_{\Gamma_{0}}

\ell(t^{\mu}s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{j}})=\ell(t^{\mu})-j

. By definition, there exists a reduction tree
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
of
w_{0}t^{\mu}
which contains the sequence
w_{0}t^{\mu}\rightharpoonup w_{1}\rightharpoonup\cdots\rightharpoonup w
followed by the reduction tree
\mathcal{T}
as a subtree of
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
. For any reduction path
\underline{p}:w\rightharpoonup x_{1}\rightharpoonup\cdots\rightharpoonup x_{m}
in
\mathcal{T}
, we set
\underline{p}^{\prime}:w_{0}t^{\mu}\rightharpoonup w_{1}\rightharpoonup\cdots\rightharpoonup w\rightharpoonup x_{1}\rightharpoonup\cdots\rightharpoonup x_{m}
. Then
\underline{p}^{\prime}
is a reduction path in
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
. Note that
d_{w_{0}t^{\mu}}(b)-d_{w}(b)=\ell(w_{0})-\frac{1}{2}(\ell(x)-\ell(y)+\ell(yx))
. If
\underline{p}
is a cordial reduction path in
\mathcal{T}
, then
\underline{p}^{\prime}
is a cordial reduction path in
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
. By §
3.2
,
\underline{p}^{\prime}
is a very special reduction path in
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
. As the definition of the reduction paths only depends on the end point,
\underline{p}
is again a very special reduction path in
\mathcal{T}
. This finishes the proof of the statement when
w
has the regular translation part.

When
w
is in the antidominant chamber, similar argument works by using Lemma
3.4
instead of Lemma
3.3
.

3.5.
Comparison between the irreducible components

In this section, we study the cases where the irreducible components of the affine Lusztig varieties and the affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties “match”. To do this, we need the following deep result on the regular locus of the affine Springer fibers.

Theorem 3.5
.

Suppose that
\mathbf{G}
is split over
L
and
\gamma\in\mathcal{I}
is a regular semisimple element of
\breve{G}
. Then
Z_{\breve{G}}(\gamma)
acts transitively on the regular locus
Y^{{\mathrm{reg}}}_{\mathbb{S}_{0}}(\gamma)
of the affine Springer fiber in the affine Grassmannian.

This was first proved by Ngo
[
22
, Proposition 3.7.1]
in the context of equal characteristic for Lie algebras. For loop groups in equal characteristic, Chi obtained the result by reducing it to the Lie algebra case, as shown in
[
2
, Corollary 3.8.2]
. In the setting of mixed characteristic for loop groups, the result was obtained by Chi in
[
3
, Corollary 7.6]
. However, similar results do not hold for ramified groups, as demonstrated by
[
3
, Example 7.7 & Example 7.8]
.

Note that any element in
\breve{G}
is conjugate to an element in
\breve{I}\dot{w}\breve{I}
for a straight element
w\in\tilde{W}
. We say that
\gamma

has an integral Newton point
if
\gamma
is conjugate to an element in
\breve{I}\dot{w}\breve{I}
for
w=t^{\mu}
for some
\mu\in X_{*}(T)_{\Gamma_{0}}
. In this case,
f_{\mathbf{G},\mathbf{G}^{\prime}}({\gamma})=[t^{\mu}]
. In particular, all elements in
\mathcal{I}
have integral Newton points. It is also worth noting that many elements with integral Newton points are ramified elements.

Now we prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a split group over
L
and
\gamma
be a regular semisimple element of
\breve{G}
with integral Newton point. Suppose that
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}
is associated with
\mathbf{G}
and
[b]=f_{\mathbf{G},\mathbf{G}^{\prime}}(\{\gamma\})\in B_{\sigma}(\mathbf{G}^{\prime})
. Let
w\in\tilde{W}
with
\dim X_{w}(b)=d_{w}(b)
. If
w
is in the antidominant chamber or has the regular translation part, then

\sharp\bigl(Z_{\breve{G}}(\gamma)\backslash\Sigma^{{\rm top}}(Y_{w}(\gamma))\bigr)=\sharp\bigl(J_{b}\backslash\Sigma^{{\rm top}}(X_{w}(b))\bigr).

Proof.

By definition,
[b]=[t^{\mu}]
for some
\mu\in X_{*}(T)_{\Gamma_{0}}
. As
[t^{\mu}]=[t^{x(\mu)}]
for any
x\in W_{0}
, we can also assume that
\mu
is dominant. Let
\mathbf{M}
be the Levi subgroup generated by
\mathbf{T}
and
\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}
for
\langle\alpha,\mu\rangle=0
. Then
\mathbf{M}
is standard Levi subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
and
\mathbf{M}
splits over
L
. In this case, the very special parahoric subgroups of
\breve{M}
are the hyperspecial subgroups.

By definition, we have
{\rm RP}_{[b]}(\mathcal{T})={\rm RP}_{\{\gamma\}}(\mathcal{T})
. Moreover, by Theorem
1.4
, we have
\dim Y_{w}(\gamma)=d_{w}(b)+\dim Y_{\gamma}
. Then for
\underline{p}\in{\rm RP}_{\{\gamma\}}(\mathcal{T})={\rm RP}_{[b]}(\mathcal{T})
,
\ell(\underline{p})+\ell(\text{end}(\underline{p}))-\langle\bar{\nu}_{\text{end}(\underline{p})},2\rho\rangle+\dim Y_{\gamma}=\dim Y_{w}(\gamma)
if and only if
\ell(\underline{p})+\ell(\text{end}(\underline{p}))-\langle\bar{\nu}_{\text{end}(\underline{p})},2\rho\rangle=\dim X_{w}(b)
, that is,
\underline{p}\in{\rm RP}_{[b]}(\mathcal{T})
is cordial.

By Theorem
3.5
for
\mathbf{M}
and Proposition
3.1
,
n_{\underline{p},\gamma}=1
for any cordial reduction path in
{\rm RP}_{\{\gamma\}}(\mathcal{T})
. Now the statement follows from Theorem
2.8
and Proposition
2.5
.
∎

3.6.
Affine Lusztig varieties in the affine Grassmannian

Let
\widehat{\mathbf{G}}
be the Langlands dual of
\mathbf{G}
over the complex number field
\mathbb{C}
. Let
\widehat{\mathbf{T}}
be the maximal torus dual to the maximal torus
\mathbf{T}
of
\mathbf{G}
. Suppose that the group
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}
over
F^{\prime}
is associated with the group
\mathbf{G}
and
[b]=f_{\mathbf{G},\mathbf{G}^{\prime}}(\{\gamma\})
. Then by definition, we may identify the Newton point of
\gamma
with the Newton point of
b
. In particular, if moreover
\gamma
has the integral Newton point
\lambda_{\gamma}
, then under this identification, we have
\lambda_{\gamma}=\lambda_{b}
. We have the following result, which verifies a conjecture of Chi
[
2
]
when
\gamma
has an integral Newton point.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a split group over
L
and
\gamma
be a regular semisimple element of
\breve{G}
with an integral Newton point
\lambda_{\gamma}
. Let
\mu
be a dominant coweight with
Y_{\mu}(\gamma)\neq\emptyset
. Then

\sharp\bigl(Z_{\breve{G}}(\gamma)\backslash\Sigma^{\rm top}(Y_{\mu}(\gamma))\bigr)=\dim V_{\mu}(\lambda_{\gamma}).

Proof.

Set
Y=\bigsqcup_{w\in W_{0}t^{\mu}W_{0}}Y_{w}(\gamma)
. By
[
10
, Proof of Theorem 6.9]
,
Y
is a
Z_{\breve{G}}(\gamma)
-equivariant fibration over
Y_{\mu}(\gamma)
with fibers isomorphic to
\mathcal{K}^{\mathrm{sp}}/\mathcal{I}
. In particular, we have a
Z_{\breve{G}}(\gamma)
-equivariant bijection
\Sigma^{\rm top}(Y)\Sigma^{\rm top}(Y_{\mu}(\gamma))
.

Let
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}
be a group over
F^{\prime}
that is associated with the group
\mathbf{G}
and
[b]=f_{\mathbf{G},\mathbf{G}^{\prime}}(\{\gamma\})
. By Theorem
1.4
, for any
w
, we have either
X_{w}(b)=Y_{w}(\gamma)=\emptyset
or
\dim Y_{w}(\gamma)=\dim X_{w}(b)+\dim Y_{\gamma}
. By
[
7
, Theorem 9.1]
, for
w\in W_{0}t^{\mu}W_{0}
and
w\neq w_{0}t^{\mu}
, we have either
X_{w}(b)=\emptyset
or
\dim X_{w}(b)<\dim X_{w_{0}t^{\mu}}(b)
. Thus for
w\in W_{0}t^{\mu}W_{0}
and
w\neq w_{0}t^{\mu}
, we have either
Y_{w}(\gamma)=\emptyset
or
\dim Y_{w}(\gamma)<\dim Y_{w_{0}t^{\mu}}(\gamma)
. The inclusion
Y_{w_{0}t^{\mu}}(\gamma)Y
induces a
Z_{\breve{G}}(\gamma)
-equivariant bijection
\Sigma^{\rm top}(Y_{w_{0}t^{\mu}}(\gamma))\Sigma^{\rm top}(Y)
. By
[
15
, Proposition 2.4.10]
, we have a
J_{b}
-equivariant bijection
\Sigma^{\rm top}(X_{w_{0}t^{\mu}}(b))\Sigma^{\rm top}(X_{\mu}(b))
. By Theorem
3.6
and Theorem
3.2
(1), we have

\displaystyle\sharp\bigl(Z_{\breve{G}}(\gamma)\backslash\Sigma^{\rm top}(Y_{\mu}(\gamma))\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sharp\bigl(Z_{\breve{G}}(\gamma)\backslash\Sigma^{\rm top}(Y)\bigr)=\sharp\bigl(Z_{\breve{G}}(\gamma)\backslash\Sigma^{\rm top}(Y_{w_{0}t^{\mu}}(\gamma))\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sharp\bigl(J_{b}\backslash\Sigma^{\rm top}(X_{w_{0}t^{\mu}}(b))\bigr)=\sharp\bigl(J_{b}\backslash\Sigma^{\rm top}(X_{\mu}(b))\bigr)

\displaystyle=\dim V_{\mu}(\lambda_{\gamma}).

The theorem is proved.
∎

As we have seen from the proof,
\sharp\bigl(Z_{\breve{G}}(\gamma)\backslash\Sigma^{\rm top}(Y_{\mu}(\gamma))\bigr)=\dim V_{\mu}(\lambda_{\gamma})
if and only if
\sharp\bigl(Z_{\breve{G}}(\gamma)\backslash\Sigma^{\rm top}(Y_{w_{0}\mu}(\gamma))\bigr)=\sharp\bigl(J_{b}\backslash\Sigma^{\rm top}(X_{w_{0}t^{\mu}}(b))\bigr)
. By Theorem
2.8
and Proposition
2.5
, the above condition is equivalent to the condition that
n_{\underline{p},\gamma}=1
for all cordial reduction paths in
{\rm RP}_{\{\gamma\}}(\mathcal{T})
for a reduction tree
\mathcal{T}
of
w_{0}t^{\mu}
. However, as we have seen in
[
3
, Example 7.7 & Example 7.8]
, such condition is not satisfied for the affine Grassmannians of ramified groups.

3.7.
Irreducibility

The enumeration of the (top dimensional) irreducible components of affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties in the affine flag is a challenging problem. We refer to the recent work of
[
14
]
and
[
26
]
for certain cases where
J_{b}
acts transitively on
\Sigma^{{\rm top}}(X_{w}(b))
and to the work
[
25
]
for the description of
J_{b}\backslash\Sigma^{{\rm top}}(X_{w}(b))
in terms of double Bruhat graphs. By Theorem
3.6
, we may deduce similar results for affine Lusztig varieties.

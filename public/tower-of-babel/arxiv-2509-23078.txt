Introduction

All graphs considered in this paper are finite simple and undirected. For such a graph
G
, we denote its vertex set and edge set by
V(G)
and
E(G)
, respectively. For a vertex
u\in V(G)
, let
N_{G}(u)
and
d_{G}(u)
denote the neighbourhood and degree of
u
, respectively; thus
N_{G}(u)=\{v\in V(G)~:~uv\in E(G)\}
and
d_{G}(u)=|N_{G}(u)|
. The minimum degree of
G
is written as
\delta(G)
. For a vertex subset
X
and a vertex
v
of
G
, we define
d_{X}(u)=|N_{G}(u)\cap X|
. Moreover,
G[X]
denotes the subgraph of
G
induced by
X
. For a fixed graph
H
,
G
is said to be
H
-free
if it does not contain
H
as a subgraph (not necessarily induced). For terminology and notation not explicitly defined in this paper, we refer the reader to
[
2
]
.

Many fundamental and applied problems in graph theory revolve around the concept of graph partitioning, where the vertex set of a graph is divided into parts satisfying prescribed structural or combinatorial properties; among these, one of the most extensively studied directions involves partitions subject to minimum degree constraints. To provide a concise overview of results relevant to this study, we first introduce the following notions.

Let
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
denote the set of non-negative integers. For a function
f\colon V(G)\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
defined on the vertex set of an arbitrary graph
G
, a subset
X\subseteq V(G)
is said to be
f
-feasible
if
d_{X}(u)\geq f(u)
for every
u\in X
. Similarly, given two functions
a,b\colon V(G)\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, a pair
(A,B)
of non-empty disjoint subsets of
V(G)
is called
(a,b)
-feasible
if
A
is
a
-feasible and
B
is
b
-feasible. An
(a,b)
-feasible pair satisfying
A\cup B=V(G)
is referred to as an
(a,b)
-feasible partition
.

Throughout this paper,
K_{4}-e
denotes the graph obtained by deleting a single edge from the complete graph
K_{4}
, while
K_{2,3}
denotes the complete bipartite graph with partite sets of cardinalities
2
and
3
, respectively.

For positive integers
s
and
t
, let
g(s,t)
denote the minimum integer such that
every graph
G
with minimum vertex degree
\delta(G)\geq g(s,t)
admits a vertex partition

(S,T)
satisfying
\delta(G[S])\geq s
and
\delta(G[T])\geq t
.
In 1983, Thomassen
[
11
]
conjectured that

g(s,t)=s+t+1,

and thirteen years later this conjecture was confirmed by Stiebitz
[
10
]
.

Theorem A

(
[
10
]
)
.

For a graph
G
and
a,b\colon V(G)\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
suppose that
d_{G}(u)\geq a(u)+b(u)+1
holds for every
u\in V(G)
. Then
G
has an
(a,b)
-feasible partition.

In subsequent work, a series of Stiebitz-type results has appeared. The degree condition in Theorem
A
can be relaxed when we impose restrictions on the (local) structure of the target graphs.
For instance, Kaneko
[
7
]
showed that

g(s,t)\leq s+t

holds whenever
G
is triangle-free. Next, Hou et al.
[
6
]
established the following results.

Theorem B

(
[
6
]
)
.

For a
K_{2,3}
-free graph
G
and
a,b\colon V(G)\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, if
d_{G}(u)\geq a(u)+b(u)
holds for every

u\in V(G)
, then
G
admits an
(a,b)
-feasible partition.

Theorem C

(
[
6
]
)
.

For a graph
G
with at least five vertices and
a,b\colon V(G)\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, suppose that
G
has no subgraph
obtained by connecting a single vertex to exactly two vertices of
K_{4}-e
and
d_{G}(u)\geq a(u)+b(u)
holds for every

u\in V(G)
. Then
G
admits an
(a,b)
-feasible partition.

These results are followed by additional findings that, while less central to the focus of the present paper, provide useful insights into related problems; in the following, we highlight the following.

In 2000, Diwan
[
3
]
proved that

g(s,t)\leq s+t-1

holds for graphs with girth at least five whenever
\min\{s,t\}\geq 2
. Gerber and Kobler
[
5
]
generalized Diwan’s result to a specific function-type setting, not elaborated here.
Another noteworthy contribution is due to Bazgan, Tuza and Vanderpooten
[
1
]
, who presented polynomial-time algorithms to find
(a,b)
-feasible partitions satisfying the results of Stiebitz, Kaneko and Diwan, respectively. Ma et al.
[
9
]
further extended Diwan’s result by showing that

g(s,t)\leq s+t-1

holds for
C_{4}
-free graphs whenever
\min\{s,t\}\geq 2
.
Recently, Zeng and Zu
[
12
]
proved that a graph in which quadrangles do not share edges with triangles and other quadrangles admits an
(a,b)
-feasible partition whenever
d_{G}(u)\geqslant a(u)+b(u)-1
holds for every
u\in V(G)
.

We proceed with a slightly extended approach introduced by Furuya et al.
[
4
]
. Let
J(G)
be the set of pairs
(u_{1}u_{2}\ldots u_{s},v_{1}v_{2}\ldots v_{t})
of vertex sequences of
G
such that (a)
\{u_{i}:1\leq i\leq s\}\neq\{v_{i}:1\leq i\leq t\}
, (b)
s\in\{3,4\}
and
t\in\{3,4\}
, (c)
(u_{1},u_{2})=(v_{1},v_{2})
and (d) each sequence induces a cycle. Let
S_{1}(G)=\{u_{1},u_{2}~:~(u_{1}u_{2}\ldots u_{m},v_{1}v_{2}\ldots v_{n})\in J(G)\}
and
S_{0}(G)=V(G)\setminus S_{1}(G)
.

Theorem D

(
[
4
]
)
.

For a graph
G
and
a,b\colon V(G)\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, suppose that
d_{G}(u)\geq a(u)+b(u)-1+2h(u)
and
\min\left\{a(u),b(u)\right\}\geq 2(1-h(u))
for
u\in S_{h(u)}(G)
, with
h(u)\in\left\{0,1\right\}
. Then
G
admits an
(a,b)
-feasible partition.

By analyzing the preceding theorem, one may expect that a graph
G
admits an
(a,b)
-feasible partition
whenever the vertices associated with a given subgraph have comparatively large degrees.
This observation motivates the main result of the present work.

The following notation will be used throughout the remainder of the paper without further explicit reference.
If
H
is a prescribed subgraph of
G
, let
T_{1}(G)
denote the subset of
V(G)
such that every vertex of
T_{1}(G)
belongs to at least one subgraph isomorphic to
H
. Set
T_{0}(G)=V(G)\setminus T_{1}(G)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For a graph
G
with at least five vertices and
a,b\colon V(G)\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, suppose that
d_{G}(u)\geq a(u)+b(u)+h(u)
holds for
u\in T_{h(u)}(G)
, with
h(u)\in\left\{0,1\right\}
. Then
G
admits an
(a,b)
-feasible partition whenever one of the following holds:

(i)

\min\left\{a(u),b(u)\right\}\geq 1-h(u)
for
u\in T_{h(u)}(G)
and
H
is obtained by connecting a single vertex to exactly two vertices of
K_{4}-e
;

(ii)

\min\left\{a(u),b(u)\right\}\geq 2-h(u)
for
u\in T_{h(u)}(G)
and
H\cong K_{2,3}
.

Evidently, Theorem
1.1
extends the result of Theorem
A
, as it requires a weaker initial assumption,
d_{G}(u)\geq a(u)+b(u)+h(u)

instead of

d_{G}(u)\geq a(u)+b(u)+1,

while introducing an additional condition specified in (i) or (ii). Moreover, part (i) (resp. part (ii)) permits the existence of subgraphs isomorphic to a single vertex joined by two edges to
K_{4}-e
(
K_{2,3}
), thereby extending the results of Theorem
C
(Theorem
B
). This highlights how Theorem
1.1
compares to the previous results by relaxing the degree requirement and allowing specific subgraph configurations that were not covered in earlier theorems.

The initial framework, together with relevant results, notions and notation, is presented in Section
2
. The proof of Theorem
1.1
is provided in Section
3
.

Preparation

Everything stated in this section will be used in the next one. In particular, the initial assumptions given after the next lemma remain in force throughout the remainder of the paper.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
10
]
)
.

For a graph
G
and
a,b\colon V(G)\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
G
admits an
(a,b)
-feasible partition if and only if
G
admits an
(a,b)
-feasible pair.

Henceforth, we assume that
G
has no
(a,b)
-feasible partition, and therefore

G
has no
(a,b)
-feasible pair, by the previous lemma. Next, we choose

and

so that

\sum_{u\in V(G)}\big(a(u)+b(u)\big)

is as large as possible, subject to the non-existence of
(a,b)
-feasible pairs. For the sake of simplicity, for
h\in\{0,1\}
, we set
T_{h}\coloneqq T_{h}(G)
.

We proceed with the following notation and notions.

1.

For
X\subseteq V(G)
, let
B_{1}(X)=\left\{u\in X~:~d_{X}(u)\leq a(u)\right\}
and
B_{2}(X)=\left\{u\in X~:~d_{X}(u)\leq b(u)+h(u)-1\right\}
.

2.

For
i\in\left\{1,2\right\}
, a non-empty subset
X_{i}
of
V(G)
is called
i
-good
if
B_{i}(X_{i})=\emptyset
.

3.

For
i\in\left\{1,2\right\}
, a non-empty subset
X_{i}
of
V(G)
is called
i
-meager
if
B_{i}(X^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
holds for every non-empty subset
X^{\prime}
of
X_{i}
.

4.

A partition
(X_{1},X_{2})
of
V(G)
is called
degenerate
if
X_{i}
is
i
-meager for each
i\in\{1,2\}
.

5.

A non-empty subset
X
of
V(G)
is called
a
-degenerate
if for every non-empty subset
X^{\prime}
of
X
there exists a vertex
x\in X^{\prime}
such that
d_{X^{\prime}}(x)\leq a(x)
.

6.

A non-empty subset
X
of
V(G)
is called
a
-nice
if
d_{X}(x)\geq a(x)
holds for every
x\in X
.

The subsets that are

-degenerate or

-nice are defines analogously. Clearly, if
X_{1}
is
1
-good, then
X_{1}
is
a
-nice. Similarly, if
X_{2}
is
2
-good, then
X_{2}
is
b
-nice.

For two vertices

, let

denote the number of edges incident with each. Clearly,
e(u,v)\in\{0,1\}
. For
X\subseteq V(G)
,

denotes the number of edges with each end in

.

For a partition
(X_{1},X_{2})
of
V(G)
, a

is defined as

\displaystyle\omega(X_{1},X_{2})=e(X_{1})+e(X_{2})+\sum_{u\in X_{1}}b(u)+\sum_{v\in X_{2}}a(v).

(1)

For

,

, a straightforward algebraic calculation shows that

\displaystyle\omega(X_{1}\setminus\{u\},X_{2}\cup\{u\})-\omega(X_{1},X_{2})

\displaystyle=d_{X_{2}}(u)-d_{X_{1}}(u)+a(u)-b(u)

\displaystyle\geq(b(u)+h(u))-a(u)+a(u)-b(u)

(2)

\displaystyle=h(u),

and

\displaystyle\omega(X_{1}\cup\{v\},X_{2}\setminus\{v\})-\omega(X_{1},X_{2})

\displaystyle=d_{X_{1}}(v)-d_{X_{2}}(v)+b(v)-a(v)

\displaystyle\geq(a(v)+1)-(b(v)+h(v)-1)+b(v)-a(v)

(3)

\displaystyle=2-h(v).

The exchange of
u
and

yields a new partition:
X_{1}^{\prime}=X_{1}\cup\{v\}\setminus\{u\}
,
X_{2}^{\prime}=X_{2}\cup\{u\}\setminus\{v\}
. It follows from (

) and (

) that

\displaystyle w(X_{1}^{\prime},X_{2}^{\prime})-\omega(X_{1},X_{2})

\displaystyle=\left(d_{X_{2}}(u)-d_{X_{1}}(u)+a(u)-b(u)\right)+\left(d_{X_{1}}(v)-d_{X_{2}}(v)-a(v)+b(v)\right)-2e(u,v)

\displaystyle\geq\,2-h(v)+h(u)-2e(u,v).

(4)

Note that in the previous computation, the value of

is predetermined and depends solely on whether

; that is, it is independent of whether

lies in
X_{1}
or

. In the next section, we proceed within the same framework assuming a partition

, which uniquely determines the value of

for every

. The graph

3
Proof

In light of Theorems
B
and
C
, we may suppose that
T_{0}\neq\emptyset
and
T_{1}\neq\emptyset
.

In what follows, we prove a sequence of claims, each under the assumptions introduced after Lemma
2.1
. Taken together, these establish the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The first three claims examine the vertices of
G
.

Claim 3.1
.

For every vertex
u\in V(G)
,
d_{G}(u)=a(u)+b(u)+h(u)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
d_{G}(u)\neq a(u)+b(u)+h(u)
for some
u\in V(G)
. Then,
d_{G}(u)\geq a(u)+b(u)+h(u)+1
. Let
a^{\prime}\colon V(G)\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
be a function satisfying

\displaystyle a^{\prime}(v)=\begin{cases}a(v)+1,&v=u\\
a(v),&v\neq u.\end{cases}

From
d_{G}(u)\geq a(u)+b(u)+h(u)+1=a^{\prime}(u)+b(u)+h(u)
, we deduce that
a^{\prime}
and
b
satisfy

\sum_{v\in V(G)}(a^{\prime}(v)+b(v))>\sum_{v\in V(G)}(a(v)+b(v)).

However, this contradicts the maximality of
\sum_{v\in V(G)}(a(v)+b(v))
.
∎

Claim 3.2
.

For every vertex
u\in V(G)
,
\min\{a(u),b(u)\}\geq 1
.

Proof.

If
\min\{a(u),b(u)\}\geq 2-h(u)
, then this claim holds. Otherwise,
\min\{a(u),b(u)\}\geq 1-h(u)
. By symmetry, suppose that
a(u)\leq 0
. Hence,
1-h(u)\leq a(u)\leq 0
, and so
h(u)\geq 1
. This, together with definition of
h(u)
, forces
h(u)=1
and
a(u)=0
.

Let
X_{1}=\{u\}
and
X_{2}=V(G)\setminus\{u\}
. Then

d_{X_{2}}(v)\geq a(v)+b(v)+h(v)-e(u,v)\geq 1-h(v)+b(v)+h(v)-1=b(v)

holds for every vertex
v\in X_{2}
. Therefore,
(X_{1},X_{2})
is an
(a,b)
-feasible partition of
G
, which contradicts the non-existence of such a partition (assumed in the previous section).
∎

Claim 3.3
.

For every vertex
u\in V(G)
,
V(G)\setminus\left\{u\right\}
is
2
-good.

Proof.

For every vertex
v\in V(G)\setminus\left\{u\right\}
, Claim
3.2
ensures

\displaystyle d_{V(G)\setminus\left\{u\right\}}(v)\geq(a(v)+b(v)+h(v))-e(v,u)\geq b(v)+1+h(v)-1=b(v)+h(v).

Since
v
is arbitrary,
V(G)\setminus\left\{u\right\}
is
2
-good, as desired. ∎

We now show the existence of a degenerate partition.

Claim 3.4
.

There exists a degenerate partition of
V(G)
.

Proof.

By Claim
3.3
, there exists a proper subset of
V(G)
that is
2
-good. Let
X_{2}
be a 2-good subset of
V(G)
with the minimum cardinality. By the minimality of
X_{2}
, every proper non-empty subset of
X_{2}
is 2-meager. Let
X_{1}=V(G)\setminus X_{2}
. Since
X_{2}
is a proper subset of
V(G)
,
X_{1}\neq\emptyset
. If there exists a
1
-good subset
X_{1}^{\prime}
of
X_{1}
, then
(X_{1}^{\prime},X_{2})
is an
(a,b)
-feasible pair of
G
, which is impossible. Hence,
X_{1}
is 1-meager.

The minimality of
X_{2}
yields the existence of a vertex
u\in X_{2}
with
d_{X_{2}}(u)=b(u)+h(u)
. Indeed, if
d_{X_{2}}(w)\geq b(w)+h(w)+1
holds for every vertex
w\in X_{2}
, then
X_{2}\setminus\{w\}
would also be a
2
-good subset of
V(G)
.

We set
X_{1}^{\prime\prime}=X_{1}\cup\left\{u\right\}
and
X_{2}^{\prime\prime}=X_{2}\setminus\left\{u\right\}
. Obviously,
(X_{1}^{\prime\prime},X_{2}^{\prime\prime})
is a partition of
V(G)
, and to conclude the proof it is sufficient to show that
X_{i}^{\prime\prime}
is
i
-meager for
i\in\{1,2\}
. Since

\left|X_{2}\right|\geq d_{X_{2}}(u)+1=b(u)+h(u)+1\geq 1-h(u)+h(u)+1=2,

X_{2}^{\prime\prime}
is non-empty. Hence,
X_{2}^{\prime\prime}
is
2
-meager. On the other hand,
d_{X_{1}^{\prime\prime}}(u)=d_{X_{1}}(u)=a(u)
by Claim
3.1
. Since
X_{1}
is
1
-meager,
X_{1}^{\prime\prime}
is also
1
-meager, and we are done.
∎

Let
\mathcal{P}
denote the family of degenerate partitions
(X_{1},X_{2})
of
V(G)
satisfying the following two conditions:

1.

\omega(X_{1},X_{2})
is maximum;

2.

\left|X_{1}\right|
is minimum, subject to the previous item.

By Claim
3.4
,
\mathcal{P}\neq\emptyset
. In addition,
X_{1}
is
(a-1)
-degenerate or
X_{2}
is
(b-1)
-degenerate, as
G
does not admit a feasible partition.

Claim 3.5
.

If
(X_{1},X_{2})\in\mathcal{P}
, then
\min\{|X_{1}|,\left|X_{2}\right|\}\geq 2
.

Proof.

Let
u\in B_{2}(X_{2})
. By Claim
3.1
, we have

\left|X_{1}\right|\geq e(u,X_{1})\geq d_{G}(u)-(b(u)+h(u)-1)\geq a(u)+1\geq 2.

To complete the argument it suffices to establish the same inequality for
|X_{2}|
. Assume that
|X_{2}|=1
, i.e.
X_{2}=\{u\}
. We begin by deriving a collection of structural constraints. Using these constraints, we then construct one or more
(a,b)
-feasible partitions which contradict the assumption, thereby excluding the case
|X_{2}|=1
.

If
\min\left\{a(v),b(v)\right\}\geq 2-h(v)
holds for every

, then

d_{X_{1}}(v)\geq a(v)+b(v)+h(v)-e(u,v)\geq a(v)+2-h(v)+h(v)-e(u,v)\geq a(v)+1,

for every vertex
v\in X_{1}
. However, this contradicts the assumption
(X_{1},X_{2})\in\mathcal{P}
. Therefore, the assumption (ii) of Theorem

is not satisfied, meaning the defining graph of the set
T_{1}
, denoted by
H
, is as in (i): obtained by connecting a single vertex to exactly two vertices of

.

Since
X_{1}
is
1
-meager, there is a vertex
x\in B_{1}(X_{1})
. Since
d_{X_{2}}(x)\geq b(x)+h(x)\geq 1
, by Claim

, we have
ux\in E(G)
. If
(X_{1}\setminus\{x\},X_{2}\cup\{x\})
is a degenerate partition, then the inequality (

) implies

\omega(X_{1}\setminus\{x\},X_{2}\cup\{x\})-\omega(X_{1},X_{2})\geq h(x)\geq 0,

which contradicts the minimality of
|X_{1}|
. Thus, the partition is not degenerate, which implies that
X_{2}\cup\{x\}
is
2
-good, and so

and

1=d_{\{u,x\}}(x)\geq b(x)+h(x)
. Hence,
b(u)=b(x)=1
and
h(u)=h(x)=0
, by Claim

. This, in particular, means that

. In addition, for every

, from

and

, by employing Claim

.

From

and (again)

, we have

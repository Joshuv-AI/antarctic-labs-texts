Feasibility study in quantitative identification of fresh and old osteoporotic vertebral compression fracture by vertebral CT value and CT value difference
Authors: Ningning Feng, Shibo Zhou, Xing Yu, Jianbin Guan, Wenhao Li, Kaitan Yang, Xinliang Yue, Ziye Qiu, Guozheng Jiang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study aims to assess the diagnostic utility of vertebral CT value and CT value difference in distinguishing between fresh and old osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures (OVCF).MethodsA retrospective analysis was conducted on 118 patients with OVCF who underwent both MRI and CT examinations at our hospital. The nature of the fractured vertebra was determined according to MRI. The CT value of the fractured vertebrae and the mean CT value of the adjacent normal vertebrae were measured separately, and the differences between these values were calculated. Independent samples t-tests were used to compare CT value and CT value difference among all groups. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was employed to determine the optimal cut-off value for both CT value and CT value difference in differentiating fresh and old fractures.ResultsThe study included a total of 163 fractured vertebrae from 118 patients. The CT value of fresh fractured vertebrae was significantly higher than those of adjacent normal vertebrae, which was statistically different (P < 0.001). In contrast, the difference between CT value of old fractured vertebrae and those of adjacent normal vertebrae was not statistically significant (P > 0.05). There were significant differences in CT value and CT value difference between fresh fractured vertebrae and old fractured vertebrae (P < 0.001). The ROC curve analysis showed that the optimal cut-off value of CT value for fresh fractures and old fractures was 103.40 HU. The optimal cut-off value of CT value difference was 39.81 HU.ConclusionVertebral CT value and CT value difference offer a certain reference value for distinguishing between fresh and old OVCF. These parameters can serve as a rapid diagnostic tool when MRI is unavailable or impractical, aiding in the timely assessment of OVCF.Keywords: Vertebral CT value, CT value difference, Osteoporotic vertebral compression fracture, Fresh and old fracture

Background

Osteoporotic vertebral compression fracture (OVCF) occurs with minimal or no trauma and is the most common type of fragility fracture [1–3]. As a serious complication of osteoporosis, its incidence is increasing year by year with the aging of population [4–6]. Timely surgical intervention is often necessary for patients with fresh fractures. Delayed diagnosis and treatment can lead to complications such as nonunion or severe vertebral wedge deformities, significantly impacting the patient’s quality of life. Percutaneous Vertebral Augmentation (PVA) [7–11] is a common method for treating fresh OVCF on the basis of systematic anti-osteoporosis therapy. It can effectively relieve pain while restoring the height of the vertebral body, so that patients can resume their activities as early as possible and reduce various bedridden complications. After more than 20 years of clinical practice, its efficacy has been recognized by most scholars. However, for patients with old fractures, where the vertebral structure has stabilized over time, treatment is usually unnecessary [12–14]. A key challenge in clinical practice is quickly and accurately identifying whether a vertebral fracture is fresh or old, to guide appropriate treatment. MRI is considered the gold standard for diagnosing fresh OVCF [15]. However, MRI is expensive and has long appointment times. For patients with obvious contraindications to MRI (claustrophobia, metal implants, etc.), the nature of fractured vertebra cannot be determined by MRI. CT examination has the advantages of being widely available, easy operation and relative economy. Vertebral CT value can effectively reflect vertebral bone mineral density (BMD) and is less affected by spinal degeneration. In recent years, it has been widely used in the diagnosis of osteoporosis and the prediction of fracture occurrence [16–22]. At present, there are no reports on the use of vertebral CT value to distinguish fresh or old OVCF. In this study, the feasibility of quantitative diagnosis of fresh and old OVCF by vertebral CT value and CT value difference was discussed based on MRI diagnosis results. The research results are reported as follows.

Measurement methods

MRI

Scanning was performed using a 3.0 T Philips MRI machine (Germany) with a slice thickness of 4 mm and a slice spacing of 0.4 mm. The scanning parameters were as follows: TSE sequence: T1WI (TR = 600 ms, TE = 8 ms), T2WI (TR = 2700 ms, TE = 110 ms), and STIR sequence (TR = 2500 ms, TE = 60 ms, TI = 150 ms). The nature of OVCF was determined by two senior imaging physicians combined with MRI findings. Fresh OVCF was diagnosed when bone marrow edema was present in the vertebral body (low signal on T1WI, high signal on T2WI and STIR), whereas old OVCF was diagnosed in the presence of vertebral wedge deformity without bone marrow edema.

CT

CT scanning was conducted using a Siemens dual-source CT machine under the following conditions: tube voltage of 120 kV, tube current of 355 mA, slice thickness of 5 mm, and slice spacing of 5 mm. The bone window settings were a width of 1500 HU and a position of 500 HU. Imaging measurements were performed using the PACS system (GE, USA). The CT value of the fractured vertebra and the upper and lower normal adjacent vertebra were measured under the conventional bone window (window width 1500Hu, window position 500Hu). Measurements were taken at three axial levels: the lower layer of the upper endplate, the middle layer of the vertebra, and the upper layer of the lower endplate. For each level, an oval region of interest (ROI) was drawn within the cancellous bone, avoiding the pericortical bone and any areas of local hyperplasia. CT values within the ROI were automatically recorded, and the average of the three levels was calculated as the CT value of the vertebral body(Fig. 1).

Statistical methods

Data analysis and visualization were performed using SPSS 26.0 and GraphPad Prism 9. All continuous variables in this study conformed to normal distribution and were expressed as Mean ± SD. Independent sample t-test was used between CT value of fresh fractured vertebrae and adjacent normal vertebrae, old fractured vertebrae and adjacent normal vertebrae, fresh fractured and old fractured vertebrae. ROC curve analysis was performed on CT value and CT value difference between fresh and old fractured vertebrae to obtain the area under curve (AUC), sensitivity, specificity and optimal cut-off value. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Clinical demographic data

A total of 118 patients with OVCF were included in this study, including 31 males and 87 females. The mean age of the patients was 73.84 ± 6.89 years (62–89 years). Among the 118 patients, a total of 163 fractured vertebrae were identified, including 43 thoracic fractures and 122 lumbar fractures, along with 278 adjacent normal vertebrae. Based on the MRI results, 91 vertebrae were diagnosed as fresh fractures, and 72 as old fractures. Thirty-one patients presented with both fresh and old fractures.

Measurements of vertebral CT value

The average CT value of 91 fresh fractured vertebra was 152.65 ± 43.44 HU, and the average CT value of adjacent normal vertebra was 67.35 ± 30.31 HU, there was statistically significant difference between them (P < 0.001, Fig. 2). The average CT value of 72 old fractured vertebrae was 68.83 ± 24.35 HU, and the average CT value of adjacent normal vertebrae was 63.28 ± 14.58 HU, there was no statistically significant difference between them (P > 0.05, Fig. 2; Table 1).

Discussion

This study is the first to explore the diagnostic significance of vertebral CT value and CT value difference in distinguishing between fresh and old compression fractures. A total of 118 patients with OVCF were included in the study, including 31 males and 87 females, with a mean age of 73.84 ± 6.89 years. Based on the MRI results, 91 fresh fractured vertebrae and 72 old fractured vertebrae were diagnosed. There was no significant difference in gender and age between the two groups (P > 0.05). Measurement results showed that CT values of fresh fractured vertebrae were significantly higher than those of adjacent normal vertebrae and old vertebrae (123.06 ± 29.10 HU VS 62.48 ± 31.39 HU VS 61.79 ± 13.48 HU, P < 0.001). There was also significant difference in CT value difference between fresh fractured vertebrae and old vertebrae (P < 0.001). In contrast, there was no statistical difference between the CT values of the old fractured vertebrae and those of the adjacent normal vertebrae (P > 0.05).

With the advent of aging society, OVCF has become the most common bone disease in middle-aged and elderly people. Studies have shown that one in two women and one in five men aged over 50 years will suffer an osteoporotic fracture in their lifetime [23, 24]. OVCF is a serious complication of osteoporosis and should be treated aggressively once it is clearly diagnosed [25, 26]. PVA [27–30] has been shown to effectively relieve patients’ pain, reduce bone loss and prevent bed-ridden complications. The development of OVCF is often a gradual process. Due to a lack of awareness about the condition, middle-aged and elderly patients tend to seek medical attention only when symptoms worsen or fractures recur. X-rays of these patients frequently reveal multiple wedge-shaped vertebrae, necessitating the identification of whether the fractures are fresh or old. Studies have shown [31] that fresh fractured vertebrae can be identified by local spinous process tenderness. But patients with OVCF often have extensive thoracolumbar and back tenderness due to decreased BMD, and the wedge-shaped vertebrae are often adjacent vertebrae, which is difficult to accurately locate by physical examination alone. Currently, the differentiation between fresh and old OVCF is mainly based on MRI [15, 32, 33] or Single-Photon Emission Computed Tomography (SPECT) [34, 35]. MRI is a highly sensitive imaging examination of haemorrhage, oedema and fatty tissue. It can well show the morphology, signal changes and surrounding soft tissue changes of the target vertebra, and is the preferred method to distinguish between fresh and old compression fractures [36]. Fresh fractures show low signal in T1W1 image and localised high signal in T2W1 image and STIR image on MRI. In contrast, in old fractures, because the intravertebral haemorrhage and oedema have been absorbed, the vertebral signal is often consistent with that of adjacent normal vertebrae. SPECT identifies abnormal bone metabolism or skeletal disease based on abnormal accumulation or sparsity of the radiopharmaceutical in the body. It has significant advantages in the early diagnosis of benign bone diseases [37]. Fresh fractures have a significantly higher uptake of radiopharmaceutical, whereas old fractures have a normal or slightly increased uptake of radiopharmaceutical [38, 39]. However, because SPECT is based on planar imaging, it is difficult to accurately locate for some patients with both old and new spinal injuries. In addition, metastatic tumors and infected lesions can also cause abnormal bone metabolism, which reduce the sensitivity of whole-body bone imaging [34, 40, 41]. The appointment time of MRI and SPECT is long, and some patients cannot complete the MRI or SPECT examination due to their own reasons (claustrophobia, metal implants, skeletal imaging agent allergy, etc.) [41, 42]. Therefore, it is important to find a simple and rapid method that can identify the nature of OVCF in addition to MRI and SPECT examinations.

CT examination has high clinical utilization rate and low cost. The vertebral CT value is the value that corresponds to the X-ray attenuation coefficient for each tissue in the CT image [43], which accurately reflects the BMD changes in vertebral cancellous bone [44]. Studies have shown that vertebral CT value can effectively distinguish patients with normal bone quality or osteoporosis [45–49], and can also identify the occurrence of osteoporotic fractures [50–54]. Due to the choice of different measuring vertebrae in various studies, there is no unified standard for the cut-off value of vertebral CT value in the diagnosis of OVCF. In clinical studies about vertebral CT value, Chen [55] and Lai [56] found that the difference between CT value of fresh fractured vertebrae and those of adjacent normal vertebrae was statistically significant. Our results also support this view. We believe that CT value of fresh OVCF is significantly higher than those of adjacent normal vertebrae and old OVCF, mainly due to trabecular bone disruption and local bone overlap at the fracture site. Additionally, bone marrow hemorrhage and edema, although their impact is minimal, can contribute to increased CT values [57]. In contrast, the old OVCF has a long history of injury, and the haemorrhage and oedema in the vertebra have been absorbed. Under the action of spinal biomechanics, vertebrae occur bone remodeling, and return to normal cancellous bone microstructure. Therefore, the CT value of old OVCF is similar to that of the adjacent normal vertebra [58].

Previous studies [55, 56] only observed significant differences between CT values of fresh fractured vertebrae and adjacent normal vertebrae. Our study also investigated the differences between CT values of old fractured and adjacent normal vertebrae. The results of the study showed that there was no significant difference between the CT values of the old fractured vertebrae and those of the adjacent normal vertebra. Based on this, we believe that vertebral CT values may have some diagnostic value for fresh or old fractures. In our study, the ROC curve analysis was further used to identify the optimal cut-off value for fresh and old OVCF. The results of the study showed that vertebral CT value of 103.40 HU was the optimal cut-off value to identify fresh and old OVCF (AUC = 0.93, Sensitivity = 83.50%, Specificity = 94.40%). However, during our measurements, we observed that some patients with severe osteoporosis exhibited negative vertebral CT value. This may lead to greater variability in CT value of fresh fractured or normal adjacent vertebrae in such patients compared with other patients. And there may be greater individual variability in the identification of fresh or old OVCF using vertebral CT value alone. Therefore, we attempted to reduce the influence of individual variability on the results by using the CT value difference between the fractured vertebrae and those of the adjacent normal vertebrae for assessment. The result of the study showed that the optimal cut-off value of CT value difference was 39.81 HU(AUC = 0.97, Sensitivity = 92.30%, Specificity = 97.20%). Compared to vertebral CT value, CT value difference has better diagnostic efficacy for fresh or old OVCF.

This study has some limitations. First, it is a retrospective study with a small number of included cases and is only a single-center study, so it is still necessary to increase the number of cases and conduct multi-center studies in the future. At the same time, the different degree of osteoporosis and the degree of fracture compression may have some influence on the vertebral CT value, which needs further research and exploration in the future.

Conclusion

In summary, vertebral CT value and CT value difference offer a certain reference value for distinguishing between fresh and old OVCF. By comparing the CT value and CT value difference between the fractured vertebrae and the adjacent normal vertebrae, it is possible to differentiate between fresh and old fractures to a certain extent. At the same time, for fractures that are difficult to identify clinically, vertebral CT value combined with MRI or SPECT can be tried to improve their diagnostic accuracy.

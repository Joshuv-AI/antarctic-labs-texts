Improving women football tactics analysis by using extreme learning and accumulated optimization algorithm
Authors: Jun Li, Mohammad Khishe, Banar Fareed Ibrahim
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe research will fill the existing gaps in the current approaches to the analysis of the strategies of women football by offering a new deep-learning framework and creating the Accumulated Chimp Optimization Algorithm (ACHOA). Traditional approaches like Deep Convolutional Neural Networks (DCNN) are very processing-intensive and thus they are not effective in real-time applications. Moreover, the random factor selection that is typical of the existing approaches involving the use of Deep Extreme Learning Machines (DELM) makes the reliability and accuracy of such models in terms of forecasting less than satisfactory. In order to overcome these deficiencies, we propose a new ACHOA-enhanced DELM model that enhances the performance of the model by making the parameters stable. In a way our method is a supplement to video recordings, as it makes use of the continuous frames to determine how effective a player is, how many skills may be estimated to be under the hood, and where he/she makes positional errors, which enables a more dramatically tactical goal that could not be reached before. One of the main contributions of this study is the ACHOA itself that enhances the reliability of the DELM model. The model has been trained and tested on video data from the 2021–2022 UEFA Women’s Champions League and the model has been validated using this real-world data. It achieved a classification accuracy of over 95%, and there was better agreement between the factors and the evaluations made by the experts. Further, the possibility of real-time monitoring helps to make tactical shifts, which enhances pre-game measures and develops performance metrics in women’s soccer. Keywords: Women’s football, Convolutional neural networks, Refined chimp optimization, Extreme learning machineSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The growth of expert systems and artificial intelligence has increased group methods evaluation in sporting activities, particularly female football. To get a one-upmanship trainer, experts should recognize the subtleties of group play1. Deep learning, especially DCNN, has become a powerful tool for determining and examining patterns in various other fields, such as sporting activities2. Nonetheless, the procedure of enhancing DCNNs remains a challenging job as a result of the substantial variety of criteria entailed, as well as the need to strike a delicate balance between design intricacy and the capability to generalize3.

Football is a team game and teamwork is very important. Nevertheless, power, speed, and ability are other factors that play a significant role in the game. Female football players may have particular physical strength restrictions due to the notable physiological disparities between them and their male counterparts4. Hence, female football players need to have their abilities honed and cultivated more thoroughly to receive effective instruction. Moreover, the time interval between daily training and competition should be managed properly to improve real-time training. In this framework, this paper introduces and explores a CNN-based method of providing accurate training feedback as efficiently as possible. The objective is to assist women’s football teams in executing pre-match tactical training, effectively handling various opponents during gameplay, and ultimately enhancing the game’s success rate.

CNNs have proven to be incredibly successful in a vast number of computer vision tasks and have become a crucial component of the market5–7. However, conventional training methods of DCNNs, which include movement slope descent with Backpropagation (BP) are generally criticized due to their high requirement on the adjustment of the criterion and slow convergence8. The hybrid of DELM and DCNN designs has been viewed by researchers as one of the potential solutions to these issues9.

The DELM has been recommended as an alternative to conventional DCNN training techniques because of its quick understanding of price and durable consolidation capacities. By randomly selecting input weights and analytically calculating the output weights, DELM can save a lot of learning time and processing resources10. In addition, the DELM is an ideal choice in real-time tasks like real-time diagnosis of COVID-19 using X-ray images11 and lane detection12 in autonomous vehicles, because it can solve the network weights in a single iteration.

Although DELM has benefits, its efficiency is highly dependent on the first facility of weights as well as predispositions, which could be an obstacle in accomplishing high-precision13. DELM specifications have been changed, and nature-inspired optimization techniques have been used to solve this problem. The CHOA has shown promise in improving the accuracy and reliability of the DELM by adjusting the original weights and biases, and it is motivated by chimpanzee hunting strategies14.

In recent years, some modern metaheuristic algorithms have shown good performance in deep learning optimization. Examples include adaptive-acceleration-empowered collaborative particle swarm optimization15, gradient informed metaheuristics16, An improved sparrow search algorithm with multi-strategy integration17, multi-strategy gravitational search algorithm18, Starfish optimization algorithm19, Orangutan Optimization Algorithm20, and large-scale evolutionary learning frameworks with surrogate modeling for high dimensional spaces21. These newer approaches, including adaptive Fireworks-Swarm hybrids22, improved Reptile Search variants23, multi-phase Harris Hawks frameworks24, dynamic Slime Mould derivatives25 and hybrid exploration-exploitation learning approaches26, are aimed at speeding up the convergence while making them less sensitive to initial parameters. Although these algorithms are promising in complex optimization problems, many of them have a large computational overhead or require multiple control parameters. In contrast, CHOA-based optimizers have a competitive performance with a simpler structure and a strong global search ability and stable behavior in tuning DELM parameters. These strengths drove the invention of the ACHOA, that adds chimp-inspired search dynamics with accumulated weighting to get better reliability and convergence efficiency with the proposed framework.

The CHOA is a reliable option when it comes to optimizing DCNN because it does not get trapped in local optima and has a good balance between exploration and exploitation. Nevertheless, a modified version of CHOA that uses weighting methods has been suggested to make it perform even better. Sine-Cosine Chimp Optimization Algorithm (SChoA) is an improved CHOA that solves the problem of the low convergence rate and the local minima trapping of the chimp. This enhancement is attained by incorporating sine-cosine features to upgrade its search formulas27.

As compared to various other formulas influenced by nature, the enhanced CHOA displays a remarkable merging rate, incredible success in recognizing international optima, and also accomplishes a much more optimal equilibrium between exploration and exploitation28. These growths are vital for applications such as electronic filter style and also various other technical applications that call for high accuracy as well as real-time handling29.

To sum up, a promising technique to increase the efficiency of DCNNs is to incorporate DELM with nature-inspired formulas, especially the enhanced CHOA. By integrating the durable optimization given by CHOA with the quick-discovery abilities of DELM, it is feasible to produce DCNN designs that are a lot more exact, effective, and appropriate for several real-world applications30,31.

The outcomes show the efficiency of incorporating DCNN with ACHOA techniques to accomplish a high level of category precision and minimize family member mistakes in song-style classification jobs. In addition, the recommended technique shows substantial renovations in training effectiveness, highlighting its capacity to raise MGCs in real-world circumstances.

DCNNs have become a crucial aspect in the research study of expert systems, especially for determining and discovering items and photos. This need has boosted the study of different training techniques that can increase understanding and also decrease dependency on substantial quantities of information32.

The merging of DELM and DCNN reveals a pledge in this Sect33. ELM is a robust formula of discovery that can significantly reduce the time of learning and information requirements by computing network weights between the output and the hidden layers within a single model. The scientists have shown the capacity to maintain effectiveness while dramatically lowering the time needed for learning by recommending a split DELM framework within the DCNN style. This approach effectively discovers weights for networks by utilizing the BP technique to compute the targets of the covert layer12.

Extra progress was achieved by presenting a new DCNN design and training method predicated on DELM. It is a layer-by-layer learning algorithm that combines semi-supervised convolutional filters and random filters. This approach is used to solve a nonlinear random projection-based convex optimization problem quicker and requiring less human effort than conventional BP-based training. This method is as accurate as deep feature-based methods as benchmarks confirm in object and character recognition12. CNN-DELM model is a random feature learning model that can identify and classify pedestrians and has been tested on widely used datasets. The model has shown a training speed that is up to 200 times faster than the previous models and its classification accuracy is similar or better than deep convolutional neural networks trained with backpropagation. This improvement demonstrates the way DELM can solve the problem of a local minimum, slow learning, and poor generalization that emerges due to traditional DCNN training methods10.

Furthermore, scientists have discovered that using orthogonal bipolar vectors (OBV) enhances the learning rate of DCNNs. The advised method has revealed a quicker discovery contour by integrating DCNN and DELM after a particular number of books without endangering the category price34.

Also, the application of DEL to DCNNs has been applied to specific fields, including the detection of military objects. In order to improve the performance at a lower resource consumption and without loss of accuracy, scholars have incorporated the DELM classifying layer with the convolution extractor35.

A hybrid classifier of RDELM (regularized DELM) with a genetic algorithm and DCNN has been proposed. DCNN-RDELM classifier has been found to be superior to the DCNN and RDELM separately. It takes the best features of the two methods and makes the testing quicker and learning more available. There is a reference to back this finding36.

Notwithstanding these remarkable achievements, significant improvement is required in the performance of DCNN. Nature-inspired algorithms can contribute a lot to this need. Nature-inspired algorithms like enhanced chimp optimization algorithm may provide new ways of optimizing the DCNN training process. New methods can be used to enhance the search of the solution space by using natural processes and behaviors to inspire these algorithms. Such techniques may help to make DCNNs learning more accurate and efficient in complex and changing environments where the conventional methods may not be as useful.

In short, DELM is a convenient method of minimizing handling cost and maximizing the efficiency of the understanding in the process of DCNN training. The requirement for cutting-edge strategies to enhance deep learning formulas is highlighted by the ability of the nature-motivated strategy to improve this procedure even more. More reputable and reliable AI systems will likely be feasible as the advancements in these techniques result from these partnerships.

Deep Convolution neural network (DCNN)

A DCNN is a very specialized deep-learning method that is precisely fit for examining aesthetic input such as pictures and spectrograms. This architecture is built on the human visual system and it is designed to learn spatial feature hierarchies on raw input data in an independent and flexible manner. A DCNN is made of convolutional, pooling and fully connected layers. Convolutional layers rely on filters or kernels to extract local features of the input data by means of convolution. Pooling layers reduce the spatial dimensions of the feature maps of the convolutional layer, preserving the information that is significant. Entirely connected layers accomplish the process of combining extracted features and performing classification tasks37.

The processing architecture includes three convolution layers C1, C3, and C5 between layers S2 and S4 as shown in Fig. 1. The ultimate output layer is denoted as F6. In a feature map architecture, the convolutional layer’s neurons are linked to the local receptive fields of the subsampling layer and vice versa. Consequently, neurons with identical feature mappings (FMs) get information from distinct places when processing the entire input with the same weights38.

The architecture of the LeNet-5.

The feature maps in the sub-sampling layer are subjected to spatial down-sampling with a ratio of 2. A feature map in layer C3 can be subsampled to produce a similar feature map in layer S4, which is 5 × 5. The final layer is F6 where the categorization process takes place. The feature maps are generated by convolution of mappings of the previous layer with the proper kernels and linear filters39.

Deep extreme learning machine (DELM)

The DELM is a one-layer neural network method that is famous due to its fast learning speed and low computation. Unlike conventional neural networks, DELM has a random value as the input weights and relies solely on a system of linear equations to adjust the output weights during training. This makes the training time shorter and the computation less complex, which makes DELM applicable to classification and regression in various parts. The prevalent fostering of this technique in functional circumstances, including big datasets, can be because of its simplicity, performance, and solid generalization capacity. Improving the stability of DELM using ACHOA

Though DELMs take shorter time to train compared to the conventional FC layer, the input layer biases and weights are randomly selected, hence unstable and cannot be applied in real-world engineering issues. So, we keep running in real-time while using ACHOA to adjust the input layers’ weights and biases in DELM, making the model more stable (ACHOA-DELM) and reliable. When using an optimization strategy to tune a deep model, there are usually two main obstacles to overcome. In a nature-based method, the search agents (real solutions) should consider the parameters of the structure before defining the objective function based on the problem’s interest.

Findings and discussion on simulation results

The primary objective of the hybrid method is to increase the diagnostic efficacy of the classical DCNN with the help of the ACHOA and DELM learning algorithms, as explained above. The maximum iteration of the DELM-ACHOA experiment is 100, and the population size is 30. The learning rate parameter of the DCNN is set to 0.001 and the batch size is set to 5. Each evaluation also takes into consideration an epoch count of between one and twenty. All the input photos were resized to 31 31 dimensions before being added to DCNNs. The research was done using MATLAB-R2022a on a Microsoft Windows 11 personal computer with an Intel Core i7 central processing unit, 32 gigabytes of random-access memory, and ten different execution times. The provided data set includes a wide range of attacking and defending scenarios, and a complete set of image classes related to football games. The game of football can be comprehended in a better way when it is divided into its components, each one of which has its own actions and perspectives. Whether it is the offensive techniques such as the use of the strategy of shooting in front of the door or the defensive techniques such as the use of the slide tackle, the classification gives a vivid description of the complexities that are played out during football games.

The dataset is organized in such a way that it is possible to analyze shooting techniques, defensive tactics, and goal-scoring opportunities in detail. Such classes as Quick counterattack goal and Long-range shot (40 degrees) demonstrate how various teams score and Slide tackle and Breakthrough demonstrate how various teams defend.

The balanced offensive and defensive classes in the data set also guarantee a perfect understanding of the dynamics of football. The presence of various types of pictures will enable scholars and industry experts to develop more efficient algorithms and models to assess football matches, player statistics, and team strategies.

The results of the simulation confirm that the ACHOA-DELM-DCNN model can be used to improve the diagnostic capabilities of the traditional DCNNs to analyze football matches. With the ACHOA and DELM learning algorithms, the model can produce excellent results in the visual feature classification, and the accuracy rates exceed 95%.

The total analysis of its training and preparation shows the stability and consistency of the model in many games with an accuracy that is regularly above 92% and loss values that are small. These results indicate that the model is stable in terms of identifying and training movements and angles of football goals.

The application of the ACHOA-DELM-DCNN architecture in the selection of the tactics of women football teams further proves its flexibility and effectiveness in the evaluation of the performance of players and tactical arrangements. The ability of the model to assess individual latent abilities such as observation, judgment, control and coordination gives vital tactical assessment and player development information.

The positioning errors of the players also prove the usability of the model in real-time tracking and error detection in matches. Team performance can be enhanced with a speedy rate by coaches who can correct the faults of individual players by analyzing them continuously and in real time.

The results of the simulation based on the discussion of the combined dataset show that the proposed ACHOA-DELM-DCNN model is a giant leap in the direction of women football teams in the process of analyzing matches, player evaluation, and tactical optimization.

Despite the fact that the ACHOA-DELM-DCNN model demonstrates a number of strong points in enhancing the analysis of football matches and player evaluation, a number of limitations are also present:

Limited generalizability is the restricted use of the results of a research to a broader population or other contexts. The training and evaluation of the model are mainly focused on a dataset of women football games in Hangzhou. The narrowness of the study may be a weakness of the study in terms of its applicability to other leagues, styles of play and competition. The effectiveness of the model in various football contexts remains doubtful unless it is validated in a number of datasets.

The model is mainly based on visual characteristics that are gathered through picture input to be classified and analyzed. This method is appropriate to record basic actions and movements, but it might not record details of games, e.g., the position of the player, off-the-ball movement and tactical formations. These are factors that need to understand the environment more and they are not just what the eyes can see.

The ACHOA-DELM-DCNN model is susceptible to the quality of the images, lighting, camera angles and other environmental factors. The variation in the picture features of different matches or camera settings may cause biases or errors in the model predictions, and this decreases the robustness and reliability of the model.

The strategy mainly focuses on the examination of the actions of individual players and tactical formations through the use of visual inputs. It, however, does not have the ability to include contextual information, e.g., match dynamics, team strategy, player relationships, and situational awareness. These aspects play a key role in carrying out a comprehensive match analysis and decision-making.

The ACHOA-DELM-DCNN system has a lot of optimization algorithms and deep learning structures, which makes it more complex to train, deploy, and maintain. Moreover, the training and inference computational power, especially when dealing with large datasets, may make the application in the real-life football environment difficult.

These deficiencies need additional research and development to solve them. This assessment involves diversification of the data, incorporation of contextual data, resilience of the model, and computational efficiency. Moreover, the validation and testing of the model in different football situations are also important to assess the efficiency of the model and its possibility to be used in other cases.

Convergence analysis

In this section, we provide a detailed comparison of the ACHOA with some well-known optimization algorithms, namely Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO)48, Genetic Algorithm (GA)49, Simulated Annealing (SA)50, Differential Evolution (DE)51, and two modified versions of the original CHOA, namely DLFCHOA and RULCHOA. We assess the performance of these algorithms based on three well-known benchmark functions, namely Rosenbrock, Rastrigin and Sphere, as well as a practical problem, the analysis of women’s football tactics problem. The performance of each algorithm was tested for 100 iterations with the error (objective function value) calculated at each iteration. The convergence of the algorithms was quantified by how fast the algorithms decreased the error. The results for all the benchmark functions and the women’s football problem are presented in Fig. 10.

Convergence comparison.

Figure 10 shows the comparison of convergence of ACHOA, CHOA, DLFCHOA, RULCHOA, PSO, GA, SA and DE for the benchmark functions and the women’s football problem. ACHOA was consistently the fastest and most stable in terms of convergence, with the lowest error values in all tasks, including the analysis of the tactics of women’s football in real time. DLFCHOA and RULCHOA, although a bit slower than ACHOA, exhibited a good performance with smoother and more stable convergence than PSO, GA, and SA, which had more oscillations and slower convergence. PSO and GA were good but were hampered by premature convergence and oscillatory behavior, especially on complex functions such as Rastrigin. SA had fast initial convergence but slowed considerably and DE showed steady but slower convergence. Overall, ACHOA turned out to be the most efficient, and particularly for real-time applications such as tactical analysis in women’s football.

The DELM-DCNN model without any further optimization algorithm proves to have the fastest training time (30.1 s) and evaluation time (0.015 s), due to its simple architecture. The model, however, does not have advanced optimization capabilities, which means that it converges more slowly than more sophisticated methods. PSO-DELM-DCNN adds PSO, which slightly increases both the training time (35.5 s) and the evaluation time (0.025 s), but achieves a moderate optimization effect compared to the base DELM-DCNN model. Despite its slight increase in complexity, due to the ACHOA optimization, this hybrid model offers great improvements in accuracy and convergence rate, making it very effective for real-time applications in which fast decision-making is important.

The NCHOA-DELM-DCNN and DLFCHOA-DELM-DCNN models have similar performance to ACHOA-DELM-DCNN, with slightly longer training times (42.8 and 44.1 s, respectively) and evaluation times. However, they are still a good balance between accuracy and computational efficiency, albeit they are not as fast to converge as ACHOA-DELM-DCNN. Models such as Fuzzy-CHOA-DELM-DCNN, WCHOA-DELM-DCNN, and RULCHOA-DELM-DCNN have slower convergence, with training times taking more than 47 s and evaluation times taking more than 0.025 s, which indicates that these models are more complex but not necessarily more efficient than the other models. These models tend to exhibit tradeoffs in their performance against the cost of computations. The GA-DELM-DCNN model has the longest training and evaluation times with GA-DELM-DCNN taking 52.1 s for training and 0.035 s for evaluation. These algorithms have slower rates of convergence, which makes them unsuitable for real-time applications.

Overall, the ACHOA-DELM-DCNN hybrid model is the most balanced in terms of efficiency and performance. Its slight increase in complexity is more than offset by the dramatic improvement in accuracy, rate of convergence and real-time applicability that makes it the best choice for large-scale, dynamic tasks, such as the analysis of women’s football tactics.

Ablation study

To measure the contribution of each component in the proposed ACHOA-DELM-DCNN framework better, an ablation study was performed by testing four model configurations in the same training conditions. These configurations are: (1) the baseline DCNN using backpropagation training, (2) DCNN using DELM replacing fully connected layers in the network, (3) DCNN using CHOA optimized DELM, and (4) the proposed ACHOA-DELM-DCNN model. This experimental setup enables us to separate the incremental performance improvements brought by DELM as a fast classifier replacement as well as the further improvements brought by ACHOA over the standard CHOA optimizer. The evaluation metrics include classification accuracy, RMSE and average convergence iterations which give a balanced view of the predictive performance, stability and optimization efficiency. Conclusion

In this study, an ACHOA-DELM-DCNN framework for enhancing tactical analysis and player evaluation in women’s football was introduced. By combining DELM as a fast and stable classifier and boosting CHOA with the proposed ACHOA optimizer, the classification accuracy of the model was more than 95% in the case of the UEFA Women’s Champions League video data. The system is effective in extracting visual features, assessing latent abilities of players and identifying positional errors, which has a practical value for real-time decision-making and pre-match tactical preparation. The empirical results show that ACHOA helps to improve the optimization stability and convergence, and DELM helps to improve the computational efficiency compared with traditional DCNN training. The model also correlates well with expert assessments, supporting the use of the model in performance assessment and tactical planning. Future work will focus on expanding the data set to a different number of leagues and environments, implementing contextual and tracking data, as well as optimizing devices in terms of computational needs for real-time use cases for a live match context. Incorporating multimodal information and studying transfer learning techniques may enhance the generalizability and the analytical capability of the model.

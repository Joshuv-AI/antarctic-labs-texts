Effect of the year-round simple swallowing training program in community-dwelling older people with mild oropharyngeal dysphagia: study protocol for a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Chen Zhang, Ying Yang, Xiaofeng Zhu, Hongyan Liu, Teng Zuo, Shu Yan, Weijia Zhao, Siyu Luo, Hongji Zeng, Xi Zeng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOropharyngeal Dysphagia (OD) significantly affects the nutritional status and dysphagia-specific quality of life in older adults, and essentially increases the risk of aspiration. The Simple Swallowing Training Program (SSTP) is a user-friendly and safe behavioral intervention strategy. However, its long-term effects on swallowing and nutritional outcomes remain unclear. It is also unknown whether the SSTP can reduce the incidence of aspiration pneumonia and aspiratory cough during daily eating. This study aims to explore the effects of the SSTP on community-dwelling individuals aged 60 years and older with mild OD.MethodsThis randomized controlled trial will involve 472 community-dwelling individuals aged 60 years and older. Participants will be randomized to either the SSTP or an active control group and will receive the respective intervention for one year. The primary outcome is swallowing function at 12 months, assessed using the Swallowing Outcome Severity Scale. Secondary outcomes include (1) airway protection at 12 months, assessed using the Penetration-Aspiration Scale; (2) malnutrition risk at 6 and 12 months, assessed using the Nutritional Risk Screening 2002; (3) dysphagia-specific quality of life; (4) self-reported OD risk at the first, sixth, and twelfth month, assessed using the Dysphagia Handicap Index and the 10-item Eating Assessment Tool; and (5) aspiratory cough and (6) aspiration pneumonia within 12 months post-randomization.DiscussionThe findings of this study will identify the short-term and long-term effects of the SSTP among community-dwelling older adults, and they will provide evidence-based support for the popularization of the SSTP to improve mild OD in the older population.Trial registrationClinicalTrials.gov. Introduction

Oropharyngeal Dysphagia (OD) is common among community-dwelling older adults [1]. It affects at least 30% of independently living older adults and is considered a geriatric syndrome [2]. OD and its related events, such as aspiration, malnutrition mental problems, and asphyxiation [2–4]., are common and burdensome among the older population. This makes OD a public health concern for older adults [2].

Swallowing function typically undergoes degenerative changes with age [5]. Older people frequently lack awareness of the swallowing problems [6]. Affected individuals typically seek medical assistance only when complications arise or when the condition has severely progressed [7, 8]. This highlights the importance of prevention. In clinical practice, OD treatment typically includes rehabilitative approaches [2]. These strategies are usually individualized to effectively address specific swallowing issues [2, 9, 10]. However, this can lack cost-effectiveness and preventive value in community settings [11, 12]. To address these challenges, several community-based OD interventions have been developed and a review has indicated that exercise-based interventions are the most widely studied strategy for preventing and improving OD in community-dwelling older adults [11]. In China, calisthenics is one of the most common forms of daily exercise among older adults and can easily be combined with swallowing behavior interventions. Based on this, the Simple Swallowing Training Program (SSTP) was developed [13]. It consists of a warm-up and 11 other sections. The SSTP is general, user-friendly, easy to promote, and requires no equipment. Our pilot study has shown that the short-term SSTP is safe and can significantly improve swallowing function and dysphagia-specific quality of life [13].

However, there are still some pending issues. First, to our knowledge, the gold standard for OD assessment, the Video Fluoroscopic Swallowing Study (VFSS) [14], is rarely used in community-based swallowing intervention studies [11]. Various OD screenings may fail to accurately assess and quantify functional outcomes [15]. Second, the outcomes are typically limited to swallowing function and quality of life, which is considered to overlook certain impacts of OD on physical health. According to the white paper from the European Society for Swallowing Disorders, relevant reviews, and our clinical experience, OD primarily poses nutritional risks and threatens airway protection, thereby affecting overall health [2, 10, 16]. Moreover, intra-individual variability might decrease the stability within single outcome assessments of airway protection. It is well known that penetration and aspiration typically begin with coughing while eating, known as overt aspiration [10, 16]. Silent aspiration, on the other hand, necessitates tracking through aspiration pneumonia or imaging materials [10, 16]. Therefore, the effect of intervention can be supplementarily assessed through long-term follow-up of aspiratory cough and aspiration pneumonia. Given that follow-up durations in previous related studies were typically less than six months [11], the seasonal and periodic characteristics of the Chinese diet, and that significant changes in nutritional risk typically require at least three months, a year-round follow-up period is considered necessary. These issues have also yet to be addressed in previous SSTP studies [13, 17].

To address these gaps, a randomized controlled trial is designed to investigate the effects of year-round SSTP on community-dwelling older adults with mild OD. Those with moderate-to-severe OD will be excluded because their urgent conditions are incompatible with the preventive focus of the SSTP. The intervention will be delivered online via the internet. The outcomes of interest will include swallowing function, nutritional risk, airway protection, dysphagia-specific quality of life, aspiratory cough, and aspiration pneumonia. We will also monitor adverse events throughout the trial. The purpose of this study is to explore: (1) effects of the year-round SSTP on swallowing function, nutritional status, and aspiration symptoms; (2) the non-inferiority hypothesis regarding the safety of the SSTP compared with non-swallowing exercises.

Methods

Study design and patient and public involvement

This is a pragmatic, parallel-arm, randomized controlled study. Participants will be randomized 1:1 into the SSTP group or the active control group to receive swallowing or non-swallowing training for one year, respectively. Participants will have home-based training and receive training demonstrations, supervision, and guidance through online video conferencing. The study design is shown in Fig. 1. This protocol was designed and reported based on the CONSORT and SPIRIT 2025 checklists [18, 19], while referencing the TIDieR statement [20]. Feedback and comments from public and patients who have previously tried the SSTP have been considered in the trial design, study rationale, and content of the intervention (Appendix S1).

Trial design

Participants

We plan to recruit participants in Zhengzhou and Suzhou, China, through social media and community posters. The inclusion criteria are: (A) Community-dwelling Chinese-speaking individuals aged 60 years or older; (B) Full per-oral nutrition; (C) In the past six months, no history of hospitalization or participation in any swallowing-related intervention; (D) Clear consciousness and ability to cooperate with questionnaires and training; and (E) Mild OD, which is firstly screened using the Volume-Viscosity Swallow Test, and then confirmed using the Video Fluoroscopic Swallowing Study (VFSS) with the Dysphagia Outcome and Severity Scale (DOSS) level between four and six [2, 21, 22]. The exclusion criteria are: (A) Self-reported medical condition precluding exercise; (B) Physical disabilities; (C) Tracheostomized individuals; (D) Neuromuscular disorders; (E) Diagnosis of aspiration pneumonia; (F) Structural abnormalities of the oral cavity, pharynx, or esophagus; and (G) Cognitive impairment, which is assessed using the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study criteria and the threshold is ≤ 11 for the total scores of situational memory, orientation, and executive function [23]. Preliminary eligibility screening will be conducted over the phone for the inclusion and exclusion criteria A-E. Potential subjects will be invited to the First Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University or the People’s Hospital of Suzhou New District to sequentially perform face-to-face information confirmation, complete the eligibility screening including VFSS, provide written informed consent, and participate in baseline assessments.

Randomization and blinding

After signing the informed consent and completing baseline assessments, participants will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to the SSTP and active control groups, with block sizes of two and four. Spouses or partners will be assigned to the same group to avoid contamination. Randomization will be conducted using SAS v9.4 and carried out respectively in the two cities by independent researchers. Randomization information will be securely stored by independent personnel from the Clinical Research Methodology Key Unit and kept confidential from unauthorized individuals. After randomization, the participants will receive a phone card and key from independent researchers to communicate intervention details privately and ensure confidentiality. This phone card and key will also be used for subsequent logins to the online platform to receive intervention demonstrations. They will also receive a training manual corresponding to the group, to facilitate familiarity with the training content at any time. The appearance of the manuals is identical to prevent unblinding. Participants will be instructed to minimize discussions about the training content or group assignments with friends and neighbors to reduce intervention contamination.

Participants and interventionists will not be blinded. For questionnaire-based outcomes, the assessors are the participants themselves, so they are not blinded. For instrument-based outcomes, the assessors will be blinded.

Intervention

The intervention period will last for one year (365 days) following randomization and will be conducted only on weekdays. Each weekday is scheduled for two intervention sessions, one in the morning and one in the afternoon. Each session lasts approximately 20 min. Depending on the group assignment, participants will receive either the SSTP or active control intervention.

Participants will join online meetings (Tencent Meeting platform) using phone numbers and keys assigned during randomization via phones, computers, or tablets. Upon logging in, they will be directed by the system to the meeting corresponding to their groups. Each meeting room will contain ≤ 5 participants and one guide from the research team. The participants and guides will be randomly assigned to different meeting rooms for each intervention to reduce the investigator bias and intervention contamination. The morning interventions are planned for 10:45 and 11:15, while the afternoon interventions are scheduled for 16:45 and 17:15. Participants only need to select one session from the morning and another one from the afternoon to reduce absenteeism. This arrangement is considered relatively flexible and cost-effective.

In each meeting room, the guide will instruct the participants to perform the corresponding training and observe via camera whether participants’ movements meet the standards, providing real-time verbal encouragement and guidance. Participants can choose whether to enable their camera and voice features to communicate with the guides. The meetings will be randomly screenshot to irregularly check that the attendees are the actual participants. These screenshots will be completely deleted every two weeks. The guides are all speech therapists with at least three years of experience. They will be trained in advance to ensure the appropriateness of their actions and communications. The guides will record participants’ compliance and maintain attendance records. Staff will remind participants with low frequency of participation (< 66.67%) during monthly phone follow-ups. This minimal recommended training frequency of the SSTP has been explored in previous studies [17]. Both the active control and SSTP groups will adhere to this frequency. The SSTP and active control interventions do not require equipment, involve jumping, or include moderate-intensity exercise, making them suitable for older adults.

The SSTP includes a warm-up and 11 sections, as shown in Appendix S2 [13]. The active intervention group will receive an equally timed and intensive active non-swallowing intervention. This intervention is designed to balance the confounding effects of physical activity and to avoid direct engagement of the swallowing muscles, as shown in Appendix S3. Its sections are derived from the common facial massage exercises, which have been verified as sufficiently safe by geriatric specialists (n = 12) and confirmed to avoid affecting swallowing function by dysphagia experts (n = 19). To enhance adherence to the intervention, some flexibility will be allowed in the warm-up content during each training session.

Outcomes

We defined several assessment time points as follows: baseline (T0), one month after the intervention begins (T1), six months after the intervention begins (T2), and after the intervention period ends (T3). All VFSS assessments will be conducted only twice due to radiation risks and the limited number of machines. Nutritional risk will not be assessed at T1, as its significant changes typically require at least three months to manifest. The SPIRIT participant timeline is shown in Fig. 2 [19].

Primary outcome

The severity of OD will be assessed using the DOSS at T0 and T3. It is based on VFSS and follows a standardized and recognized modified barium swallow protocol [24]. Specifically, participants will be seated and swallow contrast-agent boluses of varying consistency including thin liquid, nectar liquid, thin honey liquid, pudding/paste, and shortbread cookies. A 30-frame X-ray system will be used to record both lateral and frontal views of the swallowing process simultaneously. Independent certified Speech-Language Pathologists will be blinded and conduct anonymous assessments. The DOSS consists of seven levels, with higher levels indicating better swallowing function [21]. Levels 4–6 are defined as mild OD, where affected individuals can have full per-oral nutrition without the need for assistance or continuous supervision.

Secondary outcomes

(1) Airway protection will be assessed using the Penetration-Aspiration Scale (PAS) at T0 and T3 [25]. It is based on the modified barium swallow records in the primary outcome. The PAS and DOSS will be assessed by different blinded assessors to ensure objectivity. The total PAS score can range from 1 to 8, with higher scores indicating poorer airway protection. (2) The risk of malnutrition will be assessed using the Nutrition Risk Screening 2002 (NRS-2002) at T0, T2, and T3 [26]. The total score can range from 0 to 7, with a score ≥ 3 indicating the presence of malnutrition risk. The assessment will be conducted by blinded researchers from the Department of Geriatrics or Department of Rehabilitation Medicine. (3) Dysphagia-specific quality of life will be assessed using the self-administered Dysphagia Handicap Index (DHI) at T0, T1, T2, and T3. The total score can range from 0 to 100 and a higher total score indicates worse dysphagia-specific quality of life [27]. (4) Self-reported risk of dysphagia will be assessed using the self-administered 10-item Eating Assessment Tool (EAT-10) at T0, T1, T2 and T3 [28]. It represents perceived swallowing function, with a total score ranging from 0 to 40. A higher total score indicates poorer perceived swallowing function.

(5) Aspiratory cough will be tracked throughout the study based on self-reports. Aspiratory cough is defined as coughing caused by the aspiration of water or food into the airway during daily eating or drinking. Participants can choose to complete a 12-month aspiratory cough calendar in paper form or through an online survey. Each record of aspiratory cough will include three details: the date, the food that triggered the cough, and whether it is resolved through individual behaviors like back patting or coughing. Resolution of an aspiratory cough is defined as the disappearance of subjective discomfort. All aspiratory cough events will be followed up by blinded research assistants via phone. (6) Aspiration pneumonia will be tracked throughout the study, and participants will be instructed to report any hospital diagnosis of aspiration pneumonia during the study period. Since a participant may experience multiple times of aspiration pneumonia within a single year, repeat reports within one month for the same participant will be excluded [29].

Adverse events

Adverse events will be tracked throughout the study period, and both researchers (through observation) and participants (through experience) will be required to report any unexpected intervention-related outcomes, such as falls, cardiac events, or seizures. If an adverse event occurs during the SSTP or active non-swallowing training, the participant will stop the corresponding intervention and take safety measures, such as resting or seeking medical attention. Any adverse events will be continuously monitored, graded based on the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events, and reported to the principal investigator for etiology analysis. Any serious adverse events will be additionally reported to the Data Monitoring Committee, which will convene within 24 h to stay tuned.

Data collection and management

For eligibility screening, information from the preliminary phone assessment will be verified on-site. For objective assessment-based outcomes, assessors are trained in advance to ensure consistency in the assessments. Both T0 and T3 assessments will be conducted on-site, with objective assessments scheduled between the questionnaire assessments to reduce negative responses caused by boredom. In terms of the questionnaire assessments, some items will be randomly shuffled. Telephone follow-ups are planned to encourage compliance among participants who are absent for ≥ 3 consecutive days or who have an overall participation rate < 66.67% [17]. Participants will receive irregular calls to verify study-related information, including aspiratory cough and potential adverse events, at a frequency of ≥once per month. In addition, participants will be reminded not to have additional training or to attempt the training content of the other group.

We plan to use the clinical trial database managed by Zhengzhou University to store the participant information. All generated paper documents and electronic databases will only have access granted to the research team. All publications related to the study results will use de-identified data. All demographic information will be stored in a separate file. After the study ends, access will only be granted to the principal investigator.

Statistical methods

Repeated measures data, which are normally distributed and have equal variance, will be analyzed using repeated measures ANOVA, while other repeated measures data will be analyzed using Generalized Estimating Equations with autocorrelation matrix structures. These analyses will include time, between-group, and interaction effects. Significant interaction effects indicate significant effects of differentiated interventions on the outcomes over time. The number of aspiratory coughs and aspiration pneumonia per participant-year will be analyzed using negative binomial regression to assess between-group differences in these outcomes. Supplementary analyses will include (1) using instrumental variable regression to analyze the average causal effect of participants who completed at least two-thirds of the SSTP on the DOSS, and (2) conducting simple effects analyses using t-tests or Mann-Whitney U tests to assess between-group differences for each outcome at each time point. An intention-to-treat approach will be adopted, and we plan not to impute missing data on the primary outcomes [31].

In addition, intervention adherence will be assessed using attendance records from the online platform, calculated as the number of completed sessions divided by the number of scheduled sessions. Completion is defined as finishing at least 8 SSTP sections or 5 control sections. This result will be used as a supplementary analysis to adjust all available outcome analyses. Experienced medical statistics experts will oversee the entire statistical analysis process. Given that there are seven outcomes, the significance level α has been adjusted from 0.05 to 0.007. Statistical analyses will be conducted using Stata v18.0 MP.

Ethical considerations and monitoring

This protocol has been approved by the Ethics Committee of the Zhengzhou University First Affiliated Hospital (Code: 2025-KY-0718) and registered at ClinicalTrials.gov. (Unique identifier: NCT07373028). This study will follow the Declaration of Helsinki. All participants will be given sufficient time to decide whether to sign the informed consent form. Written informed consent must be obtained from each participant prior to randomization. The Data Monitoring Committee is an independent multidisciplinary team of experts that operates independently of the sponsors and funders. The focus of oversight is on the incidence, relatedness, and severity of adverse events and serious adverse events to promptly identify safety risks. Safety and feasibility will be the primary considerations for the committee when making recommendations for termination of the study.

Discussion

This study has practical implications as it will address the public health concern of OD in older adults. Global ageing has increased the importance of addressing OD-related burdens [1, 2]. To our knowledge, this is the first rigorous trial to evaluate the effect and safety of year-round community-based swallowing training programs for older adults with OD. Moreover, this will be the first long-term SSTP-related study. This study was initiated in February 2026. As of April 2026, participant recruitment is still ongoing. The intervention is scheduled to be launched between May and June 2026, with the study expected to conclude in June 2027.

This trial adopts pragmatic design, which reflects daily living conditions, flexible online delivery, and routine adherence patterns, which enhances the external validity and generalizability of the SSTP for future community‑wide implementation. The parallel‑arm structure is expected to support clear evaluation of long‑term intervention effects, reduce intervention contamination, and simplify implementation, monitoring, and statistical analysis of repeated‑measure outcomes. This protocol has adopted other various strategies to address intervention contamination, which is a significant concern in community-based randomized controlled trials, such as assigning couples to the same group and verifying their identities during online meetings. Although it is an open-label study, relevant measures are still designed to decrease assessment biases, including blinding the VFSS assessors and shuffling the order of questionnaire items. Additionally, interventionists and participants are not long-term bound to reduce the investigator bias.

Community-based swallowing trials are typically short-term and designed without the gold standard of dysphagia assessment [11, 14]. The current protocol is expected to solve this issue. Another strength is that various outcomes are considered. Based on the white paper of the European Union Geriatric Medicine Society, we focused on two important swallowing-related outcomes: nutrition and safety [2]. Given the characteristics of presbyphagia and the consistency with previous studies [5, 11], we still concentrated on general swallowing function and dysphagia-specific quality of life. A noteworthy feature is that aspiratory cough will be traced. This was inspired by fall-related studies [32], as unresolved aspiration cough is often the start for significant declines in the condition of older adults [10], similar to falls. Three indicators will be recorded, including the type of food that caused the cough and whether it was resolved. This is speculated to reveal whether the SSTP can fundamentally impact the occurrence of aspiratory cough or influence cough ability to increase the likelihood of safely clearing the aspirated materials. Multiple follow-up points were designed to facilitate cross-study comparisons and to explore temporal variations of the outcomes.

The SSTP is safe, low-cost, easy to promote, user-friendly, and is not restricted by training environments or formats [13]. If proven effective, these characteristics will make the SSTP an ideal intervention strategy for community-dwelling older adults with mild OD, enabling its rapid integration into primary elderly health services. In the future, exploration can be conducted on the large-scale promotion of the SSTP, such as the development of digital self-training tools or the adaptive adjustments for populations with moderate-to-severe OD.

Dispersive estimates for discrete Klein-Gordon equations on one-dimensional lattice with quasi-periodic potentials
Zhiqiang Wan, Heng Zhang

Abstract
. We prove ℓ 1 → ℓ ∞ \ell^{1}\!\to\!\ell^{\infty} dispersive estimates for the discrete Klein–Gordon equation on ℤ \mathbb{Z} with small real-analytic quasi-periodic potentials, showing that the time-decay rate persists as ( 1 3 ) − (\tfrac{1}{3})^{-} . As applications, we derive the corresponding Strichartz estimates and establish small-data global well-posedness for the associated nonlinear discrete Klein–Gordon equation.



1.
Introduction

We consider the Klein–Gordon equation with quasi-periodic potentials on the one-dimensional lattice
\mathbb{Z}
:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\bigl(\partial_{tt}+G_{\theta}+m^{2}\bigr)u(n,t)=0,\qquad\qquad\quad(n,t)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\left(0,\infty\right),\\
u(\cdot,0)=\varphi(\cdot)\in\ell^{1}\left(\mathbb{Z}\right),\quad\partial_{t}u(\cdot,0)=\psi(\cdot)\in\ell^{1}\left(\mathbb{Z}\right),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z},\\
\end{cases}

where
G_{\theta}
is defined as

(G_{\theta}u)(n):=-\Delta_{\text{disc}}u(n)+V(\theta+n\omega)u(n),\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z},

and the discrete Laplacian is given by

\Delta_{\text{disc}}u(n):=u(n+1)+u(n-1)-2u(n)
.
Here
\theta\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, the potential
V\colon\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is a real-analytic with
d\geq 1
, and the frequency vector
\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
Diophantine
; i.e., there exist constants
\gamma>0
and
\eta>d-1
such that

(1.2)

\inf_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigl|\langle k,\omega\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}-j\pi\bigr|>\frac{\gamma}{|k|^{\eta}},\qquad\forall\,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\},

where
\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
denotes the standard scalar product on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We assume that
V
admits a bounded analytic complex extension to
\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{d}:\left|{\rm Im}z\right|<r\right\}
. We also denote

\varepsilon_{0}:=\left|V\right|_{r}=\sup_{\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{d}:\left|{\rm Im}z\right|<r\right\}}\left|V\left(z\right)\right|.

In the literature, one often sets
H_{\theta}=G_{\theta}-2
to be the
quasi-periodic Schrödinger operator
. We are interested in the dispersive estimates for the equation (
1.1
), namely the
\ell^{1}\!\to\!\ell^{\infty}
estimates of the form

(1.3)

\left\|u(\cdot,t)\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}\leq C\langle t\rangle^{-\tau}\bigl(\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})}+\left\|\psi\right\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})}\bigr),\qquad\forall\,\varphi,\psi\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}),

where
\langle t\rangle:=\sqrt{1+t^{2}}
is the Japanese bracket,
C>0
is a constant independent of
t
and
\tau
is so called dispersive decay rate.

Motivation
The goal of this work is to understand to what extent dispersive properties of lattice Klein–Gordon dynamics are stable under introducing a quasi-periodic medium. On the one hand, the free discrete Klein–Gordon equation on
\mathbb{Z}
is known to exhibit a precise dispersive decay rate (see
[
23
]
), which plays a central role in both the linear and nonlinear theory. On the other hand, quasi-periodic Schrödinger operators
H_{\theta}
are canonical models for wave propagation in quasicrystals and are known, in the small analytic and Diophantine regime, to have purely absolutely continuous spectrum of Cantor type (see for example
[
2
,
1
]
). From a dispersive PDE viewpoint, this spectral picture is highly singular and lies completely outside the standard setting of the fine continuous spectrum given by a finite union of intervals, so it is a priori unclear whether any robust
\ell^{1}\to\ell^{\infty}
decay should survive.

Our main result states as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
\varepsilon_{0}<\varepsilon_{*}
, with
\varepsilon_{*}
as in Lemma
3.1
. Then for any given
0<\tau<\frac{1}{3}
, there exists
K_{1}=K_{1}(\varepsilon_{0},\tau)
such that for any
\theta\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
and any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

(1.4)

\left\|u\left(\cdot,t\right)\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{Z}\right)}\leq K_{1}\langle t\rangle^{-\tau}(\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\ell^{1}\left(\mathbb{Z}\right)}+\left\|\psi\right\|_{\ell^{1}\left(\mathbb{Z}\right)})\ ,\quad\forall\ \varphi,\psi\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})\ .

For our Theorem
1.1
, we have the following remarks.

(
\mathbf{R}_{1}
)

Stefanov and Kevrekidis
[
23
]
first established a sharp dispersive decay rate

\frac{1}{3}

for the free discrete Klein-Gordon equation (i.e., equation (
1.1
) with
V=0
) on
\mathbb{Z}
via Van der Corput’s lemma.
Recently, Borovyk and Goldberg
[
7
]
derived the corresponding rate on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, namely
\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{3}{4}}
. Later, Cuenin and Ikromov
[
10
]
derived the rates on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for
d=3,4
, namely
\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{7}{6}}\ (d=3)
, and

\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{3}{2}}\log\bigl(2+|t|\bigr)\ (d=4)
. The obstacle to extending Cuenin-Ikromov to higher dimensions is that the finite classification of stable phase singularities (Thom’s catastrophes), which is essential for deriving sharp decay rates, breaks down in dimensions
d\geq 5
. Our result can be seen as a perturbation result for Stefanov and Kevrekidis, which shows that the decay rate
(\frac{1}{3})^{-}
persists under small quasi-periodic perturbations of the potentials.

(
\mathbf{R}_{2}
)

While several studies (e.g.,
[
9
,
13
,
21
,
18
,
19
]
) have addressed dispersive equations with potentials whose continuous spectrum is a union of disjoint intervals, generic behavior of the operator
H_{\theta}
is markedly different: its spectrum is purely absolutely continuous and forms a Cantor set; see, e.g.,
[
2
,
1
,
12
]
. Our approach builds heavily on recent advances in the spectral theory of quasi-periodic Schrödinger operators; see, for example,
[
25
]
. Specifically, we employ a KAM-based perturbative construction to approximate the rotation number and decompose the spectrum into many small intervals. Using a modified spectral transform, we represent the solution as an oscillatory integral and then apply Van der Corput’s lemma on each interval to establish dispersive decay. We emphasize that a similar strategy appears in the study of ballistic motion and dispersive estimates for discrete Schrödinger equations with quasi-periodic potentials; see, e.g.,
[
4
,
25
]
.

(
\mathbf{R}_{3}
)

For discrete wave equation with quasi-periodic potentials (i.e., equation (
1.1
) with
m=0
), our method does not work. In fact, there does not exist
\ell^{1}\to\ell^{\infty}
estimates for fundamental solution of the free wave equation. We point out the following development regarding the dispersive estimate for the free discrete wave equation. This phenomenon also holds on
\mathbb{R}^{1}
. Schultz
[
22
]
proved dispersive estimates for the wave equation on lattice graphs
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for
d=2,3
, namely
\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{3}{4}}\ (d=2)
and
\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{7}{6}}\ (d=3)
. Combining the singularity theory
with results in uniform estimates of oscillatory integrals, Bi-Chen-Hua
[
5
]
extended this result to
d=4
: they proved that the optimal
time decay rate is
|t|^{-\frac{3}{2}}\log|t|
. By tools from Newton polyhedra, Bi-Chen-Hua
[
6
]
further extended the result to
d=5
: the sharp decay rate of the fundamental solution of the wave equation on
\mathbb{Z}^{5}
is
|t|^{-\frac{6}{11}}
.

A typical application for dispersive estimates is the derivation of Strichartz inequalities. For the inhomogeneous discrete Klein-Gordon equation,

(1.5)

\begin{cases}\bigl(\partial_{tt}+G_{\theta}+m^{2}\bigr)u(n,t)=F(n,t),&(n,t)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\left(0,\infty\right),\\[5.69054pt]
u(n,0)=\varphi(n),\quad\partial_{t}u(n,0)=\psi(n),&n\in\mathbb{Z},\end{cases}

we have the following mixed space-time estimates via the standard Keel-Tao argument. For convenience, we say
a pair of exponents
(q,r)
with

2\leq q,r\leq\infty

is called
\tau
–admissible
if

\frac{2}{q}=\tau\Bigl(1-\frac{2}{r}\Bigr).
Here
0<\tau<\tfrac{1}{3}
is fixed as in Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Fix

0<\tau<\tfrac{1}{3}
and
F\in L^{\tilde{q}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R};\ell^{\tilde{r}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{Z}))
. Let
(q,r)
and
(\tilde{q},\tilde{r})
be two
\tau
–admissible pairs with
q,\tilde{q}<\infty.
Then the Cauchy
problem (
1.5
) has a unique global solution

u\in C(\mathbb{R};\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}))

and

(1.6)

\|u\|_{L^{q}_{t}(\mathbb{R};\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z}))}\;\leq\;C\Bigl(\|\varphi\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}+\|\psi\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}+\|F\|_{L^{\tilde{q}^{\prime}}_{t}(\mathbb{R};\ell^{\tilde{r}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{Z}))}\Bigr),

where
\tilde{q}^{\prime}
and
\tilde{r}^{\prime}
denote the Hölder conjugates of

\tilde{q}
and
\tilde{r}
, respectively, and the constant
C
is
independent of
\theta
,
\varphi
,
\psi
and
F
.

Using Strichartz estimates, we establish the global well-posedness of the nonlinear Klein-Gordon equation

(1.7)

\begin{cases}\bigl(\partial_{tt}+G_{\theta}+m^{2}\bigr)u(n,t)=\pm|u|^{p-1}u,&(n,t)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0},\\[5.69054pt]
u(n,0)=\varphi(n),\quad\partial_{t}u(n,0)=\psi(n),&n\in\mathbb{Z},\end{cases}

with small initial data.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
p>7
. There exist
\varepsilon>0
and a constant
C
, so that whenever

\|\varphi\|_{\ell^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}\right)}
and
\|\psi\|_{\ell^{2}\left(\mathbb{Z}\right)}\leq\varepsilon
, there exists a unique global solution to (
1.7
), satisfying

\|u\|_{L^{q}_{t}(\mathbb{R};\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z}))}\leq C\varepsilon,

for all admissible pairs
(q,r)
. In particular,
\|u(n,t)\|_{\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z})}\to 0
as
t\to\infty
for every
r>2
.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section
2

1.1
by analysing a specific oscillatory integral on the spectrum. In Section

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Schrödinger cocycle and fibered rotation number

The spectral problem
H_{\theta}u=Eu
is equivalent to the following first-order system, known as the Schrödinger cocycle
(\omega,A_{0}+F_{0})
:

(2.1)

\begin{pmatrix}u(n+1)\\
u(n)\\
\end{pmatrix}=(A_{0}(E)+F_{0}(\theta+n\omega))\begin{pmatrix}u(n)\\
u(n-1)\\
\end{pmatrix}\ ,

with
A_{0}(E):=\begin{pmatrix}-E&-1\\
1&0\\
\end{pmatrix}
and
F_{0}(\theta):=\begin{pmatrix}V(\theta)&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}
.

We begin with recalling the definition of the fibered rotation number,
originally introduced by Herman
[
15
]
. The following exposition follows

[
14
]
.

Let

A+F:\theta\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\longmapsto\begin{pmatrix}a(\theta)&b(\theta)\\
c(\theta)&d(\theta)\end{pmatrix}\in SL(2,\mathbb{R}),

and consider the induced map

T_{(\omega,A+F)}:(\theta,\varphi)\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{T}\longmapsto(\theta+\omega,\,\phi_{(\omega,A+F)}(\theta,\varphi))\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{T},

where

\phi_{(\omega,A+F)}(\theta,\varphi)=\arctan\!\left(\frac{c(\theta)+d(\theta)\tan\varphi}{a(\theta)+b(\theta)\tan\varphi}\right).

Assume that
a,b,c,d
are continuous on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
and that the cocycle
A+F(\theta)

is homotopic to the identity. Then the same holds for
T_{(\omega,A+F)}
,
which consequently admits a continuous lift

\widetilde{T}_{(\omega,A+F)}:(\theta,\varphi)\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\longmapsto(\theta+\omega,\,\tilde{\phi}_{(\omega,A+F)}(\theta,\varphi))\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}

such that

\tilde{\phi}_{(\omega,A+F)}(\theta,\varphi)\bmod\pi=\phi_{(\omega,A+F)}(\theta,\varphi\bmod\pi).

The function

(\theta,\varphi)\longmapsto\tilde{\phi}_{(\omega,A+F)}(\theta,\varphi)-\varphi

is
(2\pi)^{d}
-periodic in
\theta
and
\pi
-periodic in
\varphi
. Set

\rho(\phi_{(\omega,A+F)}):=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\bigl(p_{2}\circ\widetilde{T}^{\,n}_{(\omega,A+F)}(\theta,\varphi)-\varphi\bigr)\in\mathbb{R},

where
p_{2}(\theta,\varphi)=\varphi
.
This limit exists for all
(\theta,\varphi)\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
,
and the convergence is uniform in both variables
(see
[
15
]
for details). The equivalence class of
\rho(\phi_{(\omega,A+F)})
in
\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{T}
,
which is independent of the chosen lift, is called the

fibered rotation number
of the skew-product system

(\omega,A+F):(\theta,y)\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\longmapsto(\theta+\omega,\;(A+F(\theta))y)\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{2},

and is denoted by
\rho_{(\omega,A+F)}
.

We review here the KAM theory of Eliasson
[

and Hadj Amor

[
14

for the reducibility of the Schrödinger cocycle
(\omega,A_{0}+F_{0}(\cdot))

[

and Moser-Pöschel
[

[

and define the sequences as in

\varepsilon_{j+1}=\varepsilon_{j}^{1+\sigma},\quad N_{j}=4^{j+1}\sigma|\ln\varepsilon_{j}|,\quad j\geq 0.

We also define

(2.2)

\langle k\rangle_{\omega}:=\frac{\langle k,\omega\rangle}{2},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},

and denote by
|\cdot|_{\mathcal{C}_{W}^{k}(S)}
the
\mathcal{C}^{k}

\mathbb{T}^{d}
(or

\mathbb{T}^{d}
(or
2\mathbb{T}^{d}
) and the
\mathcal{C}_{W}^{k}

S

S
is a union of finitely many intervals, we omit the subscript
W
in these norms.

Furthermore, we denote the fibered rotation number of the Schrödinger cocycle

(\omega,A_{0}+F_{0})
by
\rho\equiv\rho_{(\omega,A_{0}+F_{0})}
. Set
\Sigma
to be the spectrum of
H_{\theta}
.
We mention that
\Sigma
is independent of
\theta
and
\rho:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\pi]
is a non-decreasing function with

\rho(E)\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}=0\ ,&E\leq\inf\Sigma\\
\in(0,\pi)\ ,&E\in(\inf\Sigma,\sup\Sigma)\\
=\pi\ ,&E\geq\sup\Sigma\end{array}\right.\ ,

By the gap-labeling theorem
[
16
]
,
\rho
is constant in a gap of
\Sigma
(i.e., an interval on
\mathbb{R}
in the resolvent set of
H_{\theta}
), and each gap is labeled with

k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that
\rho=\langle k\rangle_{\omega}
mod
\pi
in this gap.

The following theorem constitutes the foundational result of the KAM (Kolmogorov-Arnold-Moser) iteration scheme in the infinite limit. It establishes that for sufficiently small potentials, the Schrödinger cocycle is reducible on a full-measure subset of the spectrum. By characterizing the spectrum
\Sigma
as a Cantor set and providing the existence of the limit conjugacy matrices, this theorem defines the global landscape on which the dynamics occur. Its primary role in the subsequent analysis is to rigorously control the measure of the resonant sets excluded during the iteration. These measure estimates are indispensable for the approximation arguments in Lemma
3.1
, ensuring that the integral over the fractal Cantor set
\Sigma
is well-defined and can be approximated by integrals over finite unions of intervals with controllable error.

Let

\mathcal{S}(u(t)):=\left(\begin{matrix}g_{1}(E,t)\\
g_{2}(E,t)\end{matrix}\right)

\mathcal{S}\varphi=\left(g_{1}^{\varphi},g^{\varphi}_{2}\right)^{T},\quad\mathcal{S}\psi=\left(g_{1}^{\psi},g_{2}^{\psi}\right)^{T}

transforms for initial data
\varphi

It follows from the functional calculus that for a.e.

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}g_{1}(E,t)\\
g_{2}(E,t)\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\cos(t\Omega(E))\begin{pmatrix}g_{1}^{\varphi}(E)\\

g_{2}^{\varphi}\end{pmatrix}+\frac{1}{{\rm i}\Omega(E)}\begin{pmatrix}g_{1}^{\psi}\\
g_{2}^{\psi}\end{pmatrix}\right]+\frac{e^{-{\rm i}\Omega(E)t}}{2}\left[\begin{pmatrix}g_{1}^{\varphi}\\
g_{2}^{\varphi}\end{pmatrix}-\frac{1}{{\rm i}\Omega(E)}\begin{pmatrix}g_{1}^{\psi}\\
g_{2}^{\psi}\end{pmatrix}\right],

where

By (
2.13
), we have

(3.1)

|u(n,t)|\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\left|\int_{\Sigma}\left(g_{1}(E,t){{\mathcal{K}}}_{n}(E)+g_{2}(E,t){{\mathcal{J}}}_{n}(E)\right)\rho^{\prime}\mathrm{d}E\right|+\varepsilon_{0}^{\frac{\sigma^{2}}{10}}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{\ell^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{Z}\right)}\ .

To estimate

\varepsilon_{0}
to be small. Substituting the expression of
\mathcal{S}(u(t))
into the integral, and recalling the definitions of

and

, we obtain

(3.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\left(g_{1}(E,t){{\mathcal{K}}}_{n}(E)+g_{2}(E,t){{\mathcal{J}}}_{n}(E)\right)\,\rho^{\prime}\mathrm{d}E

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}e^{{\rm i}\Omega(E)t}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\varphi(k)+\frac{\psi(k)}{{\rm i}\Omega(E)}\right)\left({{\mathcal{K}}}_{k}(E){{\mathcal{K}}}_{n}(E)+{{\mathcal{J}}}_{k}(E){{\mathcal{J}}}_{n}(E)\right)\,\rho^{\prime}\mathrm{d}E

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}e^{-{\rm i}\Omega(E)t}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\varphi(k)-\frac{\psi(k)}{{\rm i}\Omega(E)}\right)\left({{\mathcal{K}}}_{k}(E){{\mathcal{K}}}_{n}(E)+{{\mathcal{J}}}_{k}(E){{\mathcal{J}}}_{n}(E)\right)\,\rho^{\prime}\mathrm{d}E\ .

Note that the term

\sum_{k_{\Delta},n_{\Delta}}\beta_{k,k_{\Delta}}\beta_{n,n_{\Delta}}\cos(k_{\Delta}-n_{\Delta})\rho(E)\ .

\left|\mathbb{I}^{J}\left(\Sigma\right)-\sum_{j=0}^{J}\mathbb{I}^{J}(\Lambda^{\left(J\right)}_{j};k_{\Delta},n_{\Delta},\rho_{J})\right|\leq 61\varepsilon_{J}^{\frac{3\sigma}{4}}+100\left|k_{\Delta}-n_{\Delta}\right|\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{10}}_{J}.

Proof.

By the definitions of
\mathbb{I}^{J}\left(\Sigma\right)
and
\sum_{j=0}^{J}\mathbb{I}^{J}(\Lambda^{\left(J\right)}_{j};k_{\Delta},n_{\Delta},\rho_{J})
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{I}^{J}\left(\Sigma\right)-\sum_{j=0}^{J}\mathbb{I}^{J}(\Lambda^{\left(J\right)}_{j};k_{\Delta},n_{\Delta},\rho_{J})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\sum_{j=J+1}^{\infty}\mathbb{I}^{J}\left(\Sigma_{j}\right)\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\sum_{j=0}^{J}\int_{\Sigma_{j}\cap\Lambda_{j}^{\left(J\right)}}\beta^{J}_{k,k_{\Delta}}\beta^{J}_{n,n_{\Delta}}e^{-{\rm i}t\Omega}\Big(\rho^{\prime}\cos\big((k_{\Delta}-n_{\Delta})\rho-\rho_{J}^{\prime}\cos\big((k_{\Delta}-n_{\Delta})\rho_{J}\Big)\mathrm{d}E\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\sum_{j=0}^{J}\int_{\Sigma_{j}\setminus\Lambda_{j}^{\left(J\right)}}\beta^{J}_{k,k_{\Delta}}\beta^{J}_{n,n_{\Delta}}e^{-{\rm i}t\Omega}\rho^{\prime}\cos\big((k_{\Delta}-n_{\Delta})\rho\Big)\mathrm{d}E\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\sum_{j=0}^{J}\int_{\Lambda_{j}^{\left(J\right)}\setminus\Sigma_{j}}\beta^{J}_{k,k_{\Delta}}\beta^{J}_{n,n_{\Delta}}e^{-{\rm i}t\Omega}\rho_{J}^{\prime}\cos\big((k_{\Delta}-n_{\Delta})\rho_{J}\Big)\mathrm{d}E\right|

\displaystyle:=\mathbf{I}_{1}+\mathbf{I}_{2}+\mathbf{I}_{3}+\mathbf{I}_{4}.

Below we turn to give the estimates about
\{\mathbf{I}_{l}\}_{1\leq l\leq 4}
.

a. Estimate
\mathbf{I}_{1}

Combining (
2.15
) and (
3.7

\left|\beta^{J}_{k,k_{\Delta}}\beta^{J}_{n,n_{\Delta}}\right|_{\Sigma_{j}}\leq\left(1+\varepsilon_{0}^{\frac{1}{4}}+\varepsilon_{0}^{\sigma}\right)^{2}+10\leq 19\quad{\rm for}\;j\geq J+1.

By (

\mathbf{I}_{1}\leq\sum_{j=J}^{\infty}19\cdot 3\left|\ln\varepsilon_{j}\right|^{2d}\varepsilon_{j}^{\sigma}\leq 60\varepsilon_{J}^{\frac{3\sigma}{4}}.

b. Estimate
\mathbf{I}_{2}

We need to estimate

(3.12)

\displaystyle\left|\rho^{\prime}\cos\big((k_{\Delta}-n_{\Delta})\rho\big)-\rho_{J}^{\prime}\cos\big((k_{\Delta}-n_{\Delta})\rho_{J}\big)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\rho^{\prime}-\rho^{\prime}_{J}\right|+\left|\rho^{\prime}_{J}\big(\cos\left(k_{\Delta}-n_{\Delta}\right)\rho_{J}-\cos\left(k_{\Delta}-n_{\Delta}\right)\rho\big)\right|

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{1}+\mathbf{M}_{2}.

For any
0\leq j\leq J
, on

, we derive

\left|\sin\xi_{J}\right|\geq\left|\sin\xi\right|-\left|\sin\xi-\sin\xi_{J}\right|\geq\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{J}^{\frac{1}{20}},

by Proposition
2.2
(
S2
). On
\Sigma_{j}\cap\Lambda_{j}^{\left(J\right)}
, using Proposition
2.2
(
S2
) (
S3
),

we have

(3.13)

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{1}=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\left({\rm tr}A_{J}\right)^{\prime}}{2\sin\xi_{J}}-\frac{\left({\rm tr}B\right)^{\prime}}{2\sin\xi}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\left|\sin\xi_{J}\sin\xi\right|}\left(\left|\left({\rm tr}A_{J}\right)^{\prime}\left(\sin\xi-\sin\xi_{J}\right)\right|+\left|\left(\left({\rm tr}A_{J}\right)^{\prime}-\left({\rm tr}B\right)^{\prime}\right)\sin\xi_{J}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{J}^{-\frac{1}{10}}\left(1\cdot\varepsilon_{J}^{\frac{1}{4}}+2N_{j}^{10\tau}\cdot\varepsilon_{J}^{\frac{1}{4}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{10}}_{J},

and

(3.14)

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{2}=

\displaystyle\left|\rho^{\prime}_{J}\big(\cos\left(k_{\Delta}-n_{\Delta}\right)\rho_{J}-\cos\left(k_{\Delta}-n_{\Delta}\right)\rho\big)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\left({\rm tr}A_{J}\right)^{\prime}}{2\sin\xi_{J}}\right|\cdot 2\left|\sin\Big(\frac{1}{2}\left(k_{\Delta}-n_{\Delta}\right)\left(\rho_{J}-\rho\right)\Big)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4N_{j}^{10\tau}\varepsilon_{J}^{-\frac{1}{20}}\left|k_{\Delta}-n_{\Delta}\right|\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}_{J}.

By (
2.16
) and (
2.17
), we have

(3.15)

\left|\beta^{J}_{k,k_{\Delta}}\beta^{J}_{n,n_{\Delta}}\right|_{\Gamma^{\left(J\right)}_{j}}\leq\left(1+\varepsilon_{0}^{\frac{1}{4}}+\varepsilon_{0}^{3\sigma}\right)^{2}\leq 9\quad{\rm for}\;0\leq j\leq J.

Combining (
3.15

3.13

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{2}\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{J}\int_{\Sigma_{j}\cap\Lambda^{\left(J\right)}_{j}}9\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{10}}_{J}+4N_{j}^{10\tau}\varepsilon_{J}^{-\frac{1}{20}}\left|k_{\Delta}-n_{\Delta}\right|\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}_{J}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 9\Theta_{1}\varepsilon_{J}^{\frac{1}{10}}\left(1+\left|k_{\Delta}-n_{\Delta}\right|\right).

c. Estimate
\mathbf{I}_{3}

For any

via the fact in Proposition

(

\left|\sin\xi_{J}\right|\leq\left|\sin\xi_{j}-\sin\xi\right|+\left|\sin\xi\right|\leq\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}_{J}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{20}}_{J}\leq\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{20}}_{J}.

Thus combining (

2.2
(S4), we have

\mathbf{I}_{3}\leq 9\sum_{j=0}^{J}\int_{\Sigma_{j}\setminus\Lambda_{j}^{\left(J\right)}}\rho^{\prime}\mathrm{d}E\leq 9\rho(\{(\inf\Sigma,\sup\Sigma):|\sin\xi_{J}|\leq\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon_{J}^{\frac{1}{20}}\})\leq 9\varepsilon_{J}^{\frac{1}{24}}.

d. Estimate

(S4), for any

\mathbf{I}_{4}\leq 9\sum_{j=0}^{J}\rho_{J}\left(\Gamma^{\left(J\right)}_{j}\setminus\Sigma_{j}\right)\leq 9\left(J+1\right)\varepsilon^{\frac{7\sigma}{8}}_{J}.

e. Back to estimate

By the upper bound for

\displaystyle\leq

This completes the proof.
∎

We set

\widetilde{\Lambda}^{(J)}_{j}:=\left\{E\in\Gamma^{\left(J\right)}_{j}:\left|\sin\xi_{J}\right|>\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{20}}_{J}\right\}\quad{\rm for}\;0\leq j\leq J.

and denote

S_{1}:=\bigcup_{j=0}^{J}\Lambda^{\left(J\right)}_{j}\setminus\bigcup_{j=0}^{J}\widetilde{\Lambda}^{\left(J\right)}_{j}\quad,\quad S_{2}:=\bigcup_{j=0}^{J}\Lambda^{\left(J\right)}_{j}\cap\bigcup_{j=0}^{J}\widetilde{\Lambda}^{\left(J\right)}_{j}\;.

For the corresponding integral on
S_{1}
, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

For any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\left|\mathbb{I}^{J}(S_{1};k_{\Delta},n_{\Delta},\rho_{J})\right|\leq 54|\ln\varepsilon_{0}|^{2J^{2}d}\,\varepsilon_{J}^{\frac{1}{20}}.

Proof.

By definition, we have

S_{1}\subset\Bigl\{E\in\bigcup_{j=0}^{J}\Gamma_{j}^{(J)}:\ |\sin\xi_{J}(E)|\leq\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon_{J}^{\frac{1}{20}}\Bigr\}.

Hence, by (
3.15
),

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{I}^{J}(S_{1};k_{\Delta},n_{\Delta},\rho_{J})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{S_{1}}\beta^{J}_{k,k_{\Delta}}(E)\beta^{J}_{n,n_{\Delta}}(E)\,\cos\bigl((k_{\Delta}-n_{\Delta})\rho_{J}(E)\bigr)\,e^{-it\Omega(E)}\,\rho_{J}^{\prime}(E)\,\mathrm{d}E\right|

\displaystyle\leq 9\int_{S_{1}}\rho_{J}^{\prime}(E)\,\mathrm{d}E.

Now we decompose
\bigcup_{j=0}^{J}\Gamma_{j}^{(J)}
into its (open) connected components:

\bigcup_{j=0}^{J}\Gamma_{j}^{(J)}=\mathcal{R}\sqcup\Bigl(\bigsqcup_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}\Bigr),

where
\mathcal{R}
is a finite set and each
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
is an open interval. On each
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
, the function
\rho_{J}
is smooth and strictly monotone, and moreover

\rho_{J}=\xi_{J}+\sum_{l=0}^{J-1}\langle k_{l}\rangle
with the sum constant on
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
.
Hence on
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
the condition
|\sin\xi_{J}(E)|\leq\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon_{J}^{\frac{1}{20}}

is equivalent to

|\sin(\rho_{J}(E)-c_{\nu})|\leq\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon_{J}^{\frac{1}{20}}

for some constant
c_{\nu}
.
Perform the change of variables
y=\rho_{J}(E)
on
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
, then

\int_{S_{1}\cap\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}\rho_{J}^{\prime}(E)\,\mathrm{d}E=|\rho_{J}(S_{1}\cap\mathcal{L}_{\nu})|\leq\left|\left\{y\in\rho_{J}(\mathcal{L}_{\nu}):\ |\sin(y-c_{\nu})|\leq\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon_{J}^{\frac{1}{20}}\right\}\right|.

For any interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
and any
\delta\in(0,1)
, one has the elementary estimate

\left|\{y\in I:\ |\sin y|\leq\delta\}\right|\leq 4\delta,

since on each period
[0,\pi]
the set
\{|\sin y|\leq\delta\}
consists of two subintervals
near
0
and
\pi
of total length
\leq 4\delta
.
Applying this with
\delta=\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon_{J}^{\frac{1}{20}}
gives

\int_{S_{1}\cap\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}\rho_{J}^{\prime}(E)\,\mathrm{d}E\leq 6\,\varepsilon_{J}^{\frac{1}{20}}.

Summing over all components,

\int_{S_{1}}\rho_{J}^{\prime}(E)\,\mathrm{d}E\leq\sum_{\nu}\int_{S_{1}\cap\mathcal{L}_{\nu}}\rho_{J}^{\prime}(E)\,\mathrm{d}E\leq 6|\ln\varepsilon_{0}|^{2J^{2}d}\,\varepsilon_{J}^{\frac{1}{20}}.

Thus,

\left|\mathbb{I}^{J}(S_{1};k_{\Delta},n_{\Delta},\rho_{J})\right|\leq 54\,|\ln\varepsilon_{0}|^{2J^{2}d}\,\varepsilon_{J}^{\frac{1}{20}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Note that there are at most
2
intervals contained in each connected component of
\bigcup_{j=0}^{J}\Lambda^{\left(J\right)}_{j}
. For the corresponding integral on
S_{2}
, we obtain estimate as follows.

Lemma 3.6
.

For any
J\geq 1
, there exists a constant
C(m)
such that

\left|\mathbb{I}^{J}(S_{2};k_{\Delta},n_{\Delta},\rho_{J})\right|\leq C\left(m\right)\left|\ln\varepsilon_{0}\right|^{2J^{2}d}\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{3}}.

Proof.

We change variables first. By Proposition
2.2
(
S3
), the set

\bigcup_{j=0}^{J}\left\{E\in\Gamma_{j}^{\left(J\right)}:\left|\sin\xi_{J}\right|\geq\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{20}}_{J}\right\}

consists of at most
2\left|\ln\varepsilon_{0}^{2J^{2}d}\right|
connected components. Here and below, we set

\bigcup_{j=0}^{J}\left\{E\in\Gamma_{j}^{\left(J\right)}:\left|\sin\xi_{J}\right|\geq\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{20}}_{J}\right\}=\mathcal{T}\sqcup\left(\bigsqcup_{\kappa}\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}\right),

where
\mathcal{T}

\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}
are open intervals. By definition, we have

and for any fixed
\kappa
,

\mathbb{I}^{J}(\mathcal{L}_{\kappa};k_{\Delta},n_{\Delta},\rho_{J})=\int_{\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}}\beta^{J}_{k,k_{\Delta}}\beta^{J}_{n,n_{\Delta}}\cos\big((k_{\Delta}-n_{\Delta})\rho_{J}\left(E\right)\big)e^{-{\rm i}t\Omega\left(E\right)}\cdot\rho_{J}^{\prime}\mathrm{d}E.

We can denote the inverse of
\rho_{J}=\rho_{J}\left(E\right)
be

E=E\left(\rho_{J}\right)\quad{\rm on\;interval}\;\left(\rho_{J}\left(\inf\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}\right),\rho_{J}\left(\sup\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}\right)\right),

since in each
\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}
,
\rho_{J}
is strictly increasing. Thus, we derive

(3.16)

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}^{J}(\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}\cap S_{2};k_{\Delta},n_{\Delta},\rho_{J})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\rho_{J}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}\cap S_{2}\right)}\beta^{J}_{k,k_{\Delta}}\left(E\left(\rho_{J}\right)\right)\beta^{J}_{n,n_{\Delta}}\left(E\left(\rho_{J}\right)\right)\cos\big((k_{\Delta}-n_{\Delta})\rho_{J}\big)e^{-{\rm i}t\Omega\left(E\left(\rho_{J}\right)\right)}\mathrm{d}\rho_{J}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\rho_{J}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}\cap S_{2}\right)}\beta^{J}_{k,k_{\Delta}}\left(E\left(\rho_{J}\right)\right)\beta^{J}_{n,n_{\Delta}}\left(E\left(\rho_{J}\right)\right)e^{-{\rm i}t\Omega\left(E\left(\rho_{J}\right)\right)}\left(e^{{\rm i}(k_{\Delta}-n_{\Delta})\rho_{J}}+e^{-{\rm i}(k_{\Delta}-n_{\Delta})\rho_{J}}\right)\mathrm{d}\rho_{J}.

Now we deal with the oscillatory integrals.

We briefly analyze on the amplitude function. Using (
2.16
) and (
2.17
), we derive

\left|\beta^{J}_{k,k_{\Delta}}\right|_{\mathcal{C}^{1}\left(\Gamma_{0}^{(J)}\right)}\leq 2\quad{\rm and}\quad\left|\beta^{J}_{k,k_{\Delta}}\right|_{\mathcal{C}^{1}\left(\Gamma_{j+1}^{(J)}\right)}\leq\varepsilon_{j}^{3\sigma}\quad{\rm for}\;0\leq j\leq J-1.

and by the fact that

, we have
\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}E}{\mathrm{d}\rho_{J}}\right|<3

\left|\beta^{J}_{k,k_{\Delta}}\left(E\left(\cdot\right)\right)\right|_{\mathcal{C}^{1}\left(\rho_{J}\left(\Gamma_{0}^{(J)}\right)\right)}\leq 6\quad{\rm and}\quad\left|\beta^{J}_{k,k_{\Delta}}\left(E\left(\cdot\right)\right)\right|_{\mathcal{C}^{1}\left(\rho_{J}\left(\Gamma_{j+1}^{(J)}\right)\right)}\leq 3\varepsilon_{j}^{3\sigma}\quad{\rm for}\;0\leq j\leq J-1.

For any
\kappa

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}}{\mathrm{d}\rho^{3}_{J}}\mathcal{H}_{t}\left(\rho_{J}\right)=\frac{3t}{8\Omega(E\left(\rho_{J}\right))^{5}}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}E}{\mathrm{d}\rho_{J}}\right)^{3}-\frac{3t}{4\Omega\left(E\left(\rho_{J}\right)\right)^{3}}\frac{\mathrm{d}E}{\mathrm{d}\rho_{J}}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}E}{\mathrm{d}\rho^{2}_{J}}+\frac{t}{2\Omega\left(E\left(\rho_{J}\right)\right)}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}E}{\mathrm{d}\rho^{3}_{J}}.\\
\end{cases}

Moreover, using the definition of

we have

(3.18)

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}}{\mathrm{d}\rho^{3}_{J}}E\left(\rho_{J}\right)=\frac{3\left(\rho^{\prime\prime}\right)^{2}}{\left(\rho^{\prime}_{J}\right)^{5}}-\frac{\rho_{J}^{\prime\prime\prime}}{\left(\rho^{\prime}_{J}\right)^{4}}.\\

We begin with assuming
\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}\subset\Gamma_{0}^{\left(J\right)}
. In this case, noting that

A_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}-E&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}

and by the fact that

\left|A_{J}-A_{0}\right|_{\mathcal{C}^{3}\left(\Gamma_{0}^{\left(J\right)}\right)}\leq\varepsilon_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}},

we deduce

\left|\left({\rm tr}A_{J}\right)^{\prime}+1\right|_{\Gamma_{0}^{\left(J\right)}},\;\left|\left({\rm tr}A_{J}\right)^{\prime\prime}\right|,\;\left|\left({\rm tr}A_{J}\right)^{\prime\prime\prime}\right|\leq\varepsilon_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

On
\Gamma_{0}^{(J)}
, by the definition of
\rho_{J}
, we have
\rho_{J}=\xi_{J}

3.17

\begin{cases}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}\rho^{2}_{J}}\mathcal{H}_{t}\left(\rho_{J}\right)=-\frac{t}{\Omega\left(E\left(\rho_{J}\right)\right)^{3}}\left(\frac{\sin\rho_{J}}{\left({\rm tr}A_{J}\right)^{\prime}}\right)^{2}-\frac{t}{\Omega\left(E\left(\rho_{J}\right)\right)}\left(\frac{2\left({\rm tr}A_{J}\right)^{\prime\prime}\sin^{2}\rho_{J}}{\left(\left({\rm tr}A_{J}\right)^{\prime}\right)^{3}}+\frac{\cos\rho_{J}}{\left({\rm tr}A_{J}\right)^{\prime}}\right),\\

Using these identities we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}\rho^{2}_{J}}\mathcal{H}_{t}\left(\rho_{J}\right)-\left(-\frac{t}{\Omega\left(E\left(\rho_{J}\right)\right)^{3}}\sin^{2}\rho_{J}+\frac{t}{\Omega\left(E\left(\rho_{J}\right)\right)}\cos\rho_{J}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{t}{C\left(m\right)}\left(12\varepsilon_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}+2\varepsilon_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}+16\varepsilon_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)=\frac{30t\varepsilon_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{C\left(m\right)},

where
C\left(m\right):=\min\left\{\inf\Omega\left(E\left(\rho_{J}\right)\right)^{3},\inf\Omega\left(E\left(\rho_{J}\right)\right)\right\}
and

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}}{\mathrm{d}\rho^{3}_{J}}\mathcal{H}_{t}\left(\rho_{J}\right)-\left(\frac{3t}{\Omega\left(E\left(\rho_{J}\right)\right)^{5}}\sin^{3}\rho_{J}-\frac{3t}{\Omega\left(E\left(\rho_{J}\right)\right)^{3}}\sin\rho_{J}\cos\rho_{J}-\frac{t}{\Omega\left(E\left(\rho_{J}\right)\right)}\sin\rho_{J}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

Proof of Theorem

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
T
be a bounded self-adjoint operator on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
with finite range:
there exists
R\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\langle\delta_{n},T\delta_{k}\rangle=0
whenever

|n-k|>R
. Assume also

M:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\big|\langle\delta_{n},T\delta_{k}\rangle\big|<\infty.

Let
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\sigma(T)
and set
\delta:=\mathrm{dist}(z,\sigma(T))>0
.
Then there exist constants

C,c>0
depending only on
R
and
M
such that

\big|\langle\delta_{n},(T-z)^{-1}\delta_{k}\rangle\big|\leq\frac{C}{\delta}\exp\!\Big(-c\,\frac{\delta}{1+\delta}\,|n-k|\Big),\qquad\forall\,n,k\in\mathbb{Z}.

Proof.

Fix
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\sigma(T)
and set
\delta:=\mathrm{dist}(z,\sigma(T))>0
.
Since
T
is self-adjoint, the spectral theorem gives

\|(T-z)^{-1}\|_{\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}}\leq\delta^{-1}.

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
, define the bounded multiplication operator

W_{\alpha,N}
on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
by

(W_{\alpha,N}f)(n):=e^{\alpha\,\eta_{N}(n)}f(n),\qquad\eta_{N}(n):=\begin{cases}n,&|n|\leq N,\\
N,&n\geq N,\\
-N,&n\leq-N.\end{cases}

Then
W_{\alpha,N}
is invertible with inverse
W_{\alpha,N}^{-1}=W_{-\alpha,N}
, and we
introduce the conjugated operator

T_{\alpha,N}:=W_{\alpha,N}TW_{\alpha,N}^{-1}\quad\text{on }\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}).

Because
T
has range
R
, the matrix elements satisfy
T_{nk}=0
whenever
|n-k|>R
,
and hence

(T_{\alpha,N})_{nk}=e^{\alpha(\eta_{N}(n)-\eta_{N}(k))}T_{nk}.

In particular, if
|n-k|\leq R
then
|\eta_{N}(n)-\eta_{N}(k)|\leq|n-k|\leq R
, so

\big|(T_{\alpha,N}-T)_{nk}\big|=\big|e^{\alpha(\eta_{N}(n)-\eta_{N}(k))}-1\big|\cdot|T_{nk}|\leq\big(e^{|\alpha|R}-1\big)\,|T_{nk}|.

Define
A_{\alpha,N}:=T_{\alpha,N}-T
. Using the assumption

M=\sup_{n}\sum_{k}|T_{nk}|<\infty
and the preceding pointwise bound,

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|(A_{\alpha,N})_{nk}|\leq\big(e^{|\alpha|R}-1\big)\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|T_{nk}|\leq M\big(e^{|\alpha|R}-1\big).

Since
T
is self-adjoint,
|T_{nk}|=|T_{kn}|
, hence the same estimate holds for the
column sums:

\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|(A_{\alpha,N})_{nk}|\leq M\big(e^{|\alpha|R}-1\big).

By Schur’s test on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
we conclude that

(4.1)

\|T_{\alpha,N}-T\|_{\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}}=\|A_{\alpha,N}\|_{\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}}\leq M\big(e^{|\alpha|R}-1\big),

uniformly in
N
.

Now choose a constant
a_{0}>0
(depending only on
R
and
M
) such that

2MRa_{0}\leq\frac{1}{2},\qquad\text{and}\qquad a_{0}\leq\min\Bigl\{1,\frac{1}{R}\Bigr\}.

Set

|\alpha|:=a_{0}\,\frac{\delta}{1+\delta}.

Then
|\alpha|R\leq 1
, so
e^{|\alpha|R}-1\leq 2|\alpha|R
, and (
4.1
) yields

\|T_{\alpha,N}-T\|\leq M\big(e^{|\alpha|R}-1\big)\leq 2MR|\alpha|=2MRa_{0}\,\frac{\delta}{1+\delta}.

Therefore,

\|(T_{\alpha,N}-T)(T-z)^{-1}\|\leq\|T_{\alpha,N}-T\|\cdot\|(T-z)^{-1}\|\leq 2MRa_{0}\cdot\frac{1}{1+\delta}\leq 2MRa_{0}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Hence
T_{\alpha,N}-z
is invertible and the resolvent identity gives

(T_{\alpha,N}-z)^{-1}=(T-z)^{-1}\Bigl(I+(T_{\alpha,N}-T)(T-z)^{-1}\Bigr)^{-1},

so the geometric-series expansion implies the uniform bound

(4.2)

\|(T_{\alpha,N}-z)^{-1}\|_{\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}}\leq\frac{2}{\delta},

valid for both
\alpha=+|\alpha|
and
\alpha=-|\alpha|
.

Finally we estimate matrix elements. Since
W_{\alpha,N}
is invertible,

(T_{\alpha,N}-z)^{-1}=W_{\alpha,N}(T-z)^{-1}W_{\alpha,N}^{-1},

hence for all
n,k\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\langle\delta_{n},(T-z)^{-1}\delta_{k}\rangle=e^{-\alpha(\eta_{N}(n)-\eta_{N}(k))}\langle\delta_{n},(T_{\alpha,N}-z)^{-1}\delta_{k}\rangle.

Using
|\langle\delta_{n},(T_{\alpha,N}-z)^{-1}\delta_{k}\rangle|\leq\|(T_{\alpha,N}-z)^{-1}\|
and (
4.2
), we obtain the two bounds

\big|\langle\delta_{n},(T-z)^{-1}\delta_{k}\rangle\big|\leq\frac{2}{\delta}\exp\!\bigl(-|\alpha|(\eta_{N}(n)-\eta_{N}(k))\bigr)\quad(\alpha=+|\alpha|),

and

\big|\langle\delta_{n},(T-z)^{-1}\delta_{k}\rangle\big|\leq\frac{2}{\delta}\exp\!\bigl(+|\alpha|(\eta_{N}(n)-\eta_{N}(k))\bigr)\quad(\alpha=-|\alpha|).

Taking the minimum of the right-hand sides yields

\big|\langle\delta_{n},(T-z)^{-1}\delta_{k}\rangle\big|\leq\frac{2}{\delta}\exp\!\bigl(-|\alpha|\,|\eta_{N}(n)-\eta_{N}(k)|\bigr).

Letting
N\to\infty
(for fixed
n,k
, once
N\geq\max\{|n|,|k|\}
we have

\eta_{N}(n)=n
and
\eta_{N}(k)=k
) gives

\big|\langle\delta_{n},(T-z)^{-1}\delta_{k}\rangle\big|\leq\frac{2}{\delta}\exp\!\bigl(-|\alpha|\,|n-k|\bigr)=\frac{2}{\delta}\exp\!\Bigl(-a_{0}\,\frac{\delta}{1+\delta}\,|n-k|\Bigr).

Thus the claim holds with
C=2
and
c=a_{0}
, where
c
depends only on
R
and
M
.
∎

]
:

\mathbf{A}^{-1}=T^{-1/2}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-1/2}(T+\lambda)^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\qquad\text{in }\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})).

Pairing this identity with
\delta_{n},\delta_{k}
gives

\langle\delta_{n},\mathbf{A}^{-1}\delta_{k}\rangle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-1/2}\langle\delta_{n},(T+\lambda)^{-1}\delta_{k}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\lambda,

and using (
4.3
) we obtain the pointwise kernel bound

\big|\langle\delta_{n},\mathbf{A}^{-1}\delta_{k}\rangle\big|\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{-1/2}}{c+\lambda}\exp\!\Big(-c_{1}\,\frac{c+\lambda}{1+c+\lambda}\,|n-k|\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda.

Denote
K(n,k):=\langle\delta_{n},\mathbf{A}^{-1}\delta_{k}\rangle
.
For each fixed
\lambda\geq 0
, set

a(\lambda):=c_{1}\,\frac{c+\lambda}{1+c+\lambda}.

Since
a(\lambda)\geq c_{1}\frac{c}{1+c}>0
, the discrete exponential is uniformly summable:
there exists a constant
C_{2}
depending only on
c_{1}
and
c
such that

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-a(\lambda)|j|}\leq C_{2},\qquad\forall\,\lambda\geq 0.

Therefore, for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|K(n,k)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{-1/2}}{c+\lambda}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-a(\lambda)|n-k|}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{1}C_{2}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{-1/2}}{c+\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda=\frac{C_{1}C_{2}}{\pi}\cdot\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{c}}=\frac{C_{1}C_{2}}{\sqrt{c}}.

Taking the supremum over
n
gives

B_{1}:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|K(n,k)|\leq\frac{C_{1}C_{2}}{\sqrt{c}}<\infty.

Since
\mathbf{A}^{-1}
is self-adjoint on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
,
K(n,k)=\overline{K(k,n)}
, so also

B_{2}:=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|K(n,k)|=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|K(k,n)|\leq B_{1}<\infty.

Now let
f\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
. The operator
\mathbf{A}^{-1}
acts by

(\mathbf{A}^{-1}f)(n)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}K(n,k)f(k),

and the Schur test implies that
\mathbf{A}^{-1}
extends boundedly to
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
with

\|\mathbf{A}^{-1}\|_{\ell^{p}\to\ell^{p}}\leq B_{1}^{1-\frac{1}{p}}B_{2}^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\max\{B_{1},B_{2}\}\leq\frac{C_{1}C_{2}}{\sqrt{c}}.

All constants depend only on
m
and
\|V\|_{\ell^{\infty}}
(through
c
, and through the
constants in Lemma
4.1
applied to
T
), and are therefore independent of
\theta
.
This proves the lemma.
∎

We use the famous result from Keel-Tao.

Theorem 4.1

(Keel and Tao, Theorem 1.2 in
[
17
]
)
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space,
(X,\mathrm{d}x)
be a measure space and

U(t):H\to L^{2}(X)
be a one-parameter family of mappings, which obey the energy estimate

\|U(t)f\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H}

and the decay estimate

\|U(t)(U(s))^{*}g\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\lesssim\langle t-s\rangle^{-\tau}\|g\|_{L^{1}(X)},

for some
\sigma>0
. Then,

\displaystyle\|U(t)f\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H},

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\int(U(t))^{*}F(t,\cdot)\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr\|_{H}\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}},

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\int^{t}_{0}U(t)(U(s))^{*}F(s,\cdot)\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr\|_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{\tilde{q}^{\prime}}_{t}L^{\tilde{r}^{\prime}}_{x}},

where exponent pairs
(q,r)
and
(\tilde{q},\tilde{r})
are
\tau
–admissible.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We indeed proved the dispersive estimate for the half-wave group in Section
3
:

(4.4)

\|e^{it\mathbf{A}}f\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}\leq C\langle t\rangle^{-\tau}\|f\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})},\qquad\forall\,t\in\mathbb{R},

with
C
independent of
\theta
.

Now set
X:=\mathbb{Z}
with counting measure,
H_{0}:=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, and define
U(t):H_{0}\to L^{2}(X)=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
by

U(t):=e^{it\mathbf{A}}
. Since
\mathbf{A}
is self-adjoint on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
,
U(t)
is unitary:

\|U(t)f\|_{\ell^{2}}=\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}.

Moreover,
U(t)U(s)^{*}=U(t-s)
and (
4.4
) implies

\|U(t)U(s)^{*}g\|_{\ell^{\infty}}=\|e^{i(t-s)\mathbf{A}}g\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\leq C\langle t-s\rangle^{-\tau}\|g\|_{\ell^{1}}.

Therefore Theorem
4.1
implies for any
\tau
–admissible pair
(q,r)
with
q<\infty
,

(4.5)

\|e^{it\mathbf{A}}f\|_{L^{q}_{t}(\mathbb{R};\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z}))}\lesssim\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})},

and for any two
\tau
–admissible pairs
(q,r)
and
(\tilde{q},\tilde{r})
with
q,\tilde{q}<\infty
,

(4.6)

\Bigl\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\mathbf{A}}G(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr\|_{L^{q}_{t}(\mathbb{R};\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z}))}\lesssim\|G\|_{L^{\tilde{q}^{\prime}}_{t}(\mathbb{R};\ell^{\tilde{r}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{Z}))}.

All implicit constants are independent of
\theta
.

We now consider the inhomogeneous Klein–Gordon equation

\partial_{tt}u+\mathbf{A}^{2}u=F,\qquad u(0)=\varphi,\quad\partial_{t}u(0)=\psi,

whose Duhamel formula is

(4.7)

u(t)=\cos(t\mathbf{A})\varphi+\mathbf{A}^{-1}\sin(t\mathbf{A})\psi+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\sin\bigl((t-s)\mathbf{A}\bigr)F(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Since
\mathbf{A}^{-1}
is a Borel function of
\mathbf{A}
, it commutes with
e^{it\mathbf{A}}
, and

\cos(t\mathbf{A})=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(e^{it\mathbf{A}}+e^{-it\mathbf{A}}\bigr),\qquad\mathbf{A}^{-1}\sin(t\mathbf{A})=\frac{1}{2i}\bigl(e^{it\mathbf{A}}-e^{-it\mathbf{A}}\bigr)\mathbf{A}^{-1},

\mathbf{A}^{-1}\sin\bigl((t-s)\mathbf{A}\bigr)=\frac{1}{2i}\Bigl(e^{i(t-s)\mathbf{A}}-e^{-i(t-s)\mathbf{A}}\Bigr)\mathbf{A}^{-1}.

Thus
u
can be written as a sum of two half-wave evolutions and two half-wave Duhamel terms:

u(t)=e^{it\mathbf{A}}f_{+}+e^{-it\mathbf{A}}f_{-}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\mathbf{A}}G(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(t-s)\mathbf{A}}\widetilde{G}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,

where

f_{\pm}:=\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\varphi\mp i\mathbf{A}^{-1}\psi\Bigr),\qquad G(s):=\frac{1}{2i}\mathbf{A}^{-1}F(s),\qquad\widetilde{G}(s):=-\frac{1}{2i}\mathbf{A}^{-1}F(s).

Applying (
4.5
) gives

\|e^{it\mathbf{A}}f_{+}\|_{L^{q}_{t}\ell^{r}}+\|e^{-it\mathbf{A}}f_{-}\|_{L^{q}_{t}\ell^{r}}\lesssim\|f_{+}\|_{\ell^{2}}+\|f_{-}\|_{\ell^{2}}\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{\ell^{2}}+\|\psi\|_{\ell^{2}},

using
\|\mathbf{A}^{-1}\|_{\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}}\leq c^{-1/2}
. For the inhomogeneous terms, by
(
4.6
) and Lemma
4.2
with
p=\tilde{r}^{\prime}
,

\Bigl\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\mathbf{A}}G(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr\|_{L^{q}_{t}\ell^{r}}\lesssim\|G\|_{L^{\tilde{q}^{\prime}}_{t}\ell^{\tilde{r}^{\prime}}_{n}}\lesssim\|\mathbf{A}^{-1}F\|_{L^{\tilde{q}^{\prime}}_{t}\ell^{\tilde{r}^{\prime}}_{n}}\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{\tilde{q}^{\prime}}_{t}\ell^{\tilde{r}^{\prime}}_{n}},

and similarly for the term with
\widetilde{G}
. Combining these estimates yields (
1.6
).

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
∎

4.2.
Nonlinear equation and proof of Theorem
1.3

We consider the nonlinear discrete Klein–Gordon equation

(4.8)

\begin{cases}\bigl(\partial_{tt}+G_{\theta}+m^{2}\bigr)u(n,t)=\pm|u(n,t)|^{p-1}u(n,t),&(n,t)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R},\\[2.84526pt]
u(n,0)=\varphi(n),\quad\partial_{t}u(n,0)=\psi(n),&n\in\mathbb{Z},\end{cases}

with
p>7
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Fix

\tau:=\frac{2}{p-1}<\frac{1}{3}.

Then
(q,r)=(p+1,p+1)
is
\tau
–admissible.
Let

S:=L^{p+1}_{t}(\mathbb{R};\ell^{p+1}(\mathbb{Z})),\quad\text{and}\quad\|u\|_{S}:=\|u\|_{L^{p+1}_{t}\ell^{p+1}_{n}}.

Step 1. Fixed point in
S
: global existence and uniqueness in
S
.

Write (
4.8
) in abstract form as

\partial_{tt}u+\mathbf{A}^{2}u=\pm N(u),\qquad N(u):=|u|^{p-1}u.

The Duhamel formula reads

(4.9)

u(t)=\cos(t\mathbf{A})\varphi+\mathbf{A}^{-1}\sin(t\mathbf{A})\psi\pm\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\sin\bigl((t-s)\mathbf{A}\bigr)N(u(s))\,\mathrm{d}s.

Define

\Phi(u)(t):=\cos(t\mathbf{A})\varphi+\mathbf{A}^{-1}\sin(t\mathbf{A})\psi\pm\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\sin\bigl((t-s)\mathbf{A}\bigr)N(u(s))\,\mathrm{d}s.

By Theorem
1.2
with
(q,r)=(p+1,p+1)
and

(\tilde{q},\tilde{r})=(p+1,p+1)
, we have

(4.10)

\|\Phi(u)\|_{S}\leq C\Bigl(\|\varphi\|_{\ell^{2}}+\|\psi\|_{\ell^{2}}+\|N(u)\|_{L^{\frac{p+1}{p}}_{t}(\mathbb{R};\ell^{\frac{p+1}{p}}(\mathbb{Z}))}\Bigr).

Moreover,

\|N(u(t))\|_{\ell^{\frac{p+1}{p}}}=\|u(t)\|_{\ell^{p+1}}^{p}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\|N(u)\|_{L^{\frac{p+1}{p}}_{t}\ell^{\frac{p+1}{p}}_{n}}=\|u\|_{S}^{p}.

Hence

(4.11)

\|\Phi(u)\|_{S}\leq C\Bigl(\|\varphi\|_{\ell^{2}}+\|\psi\|_{\ell^{2}}+\|u\|_{S}^{p}\Bigr).

Similarly, using

|N(u)-N(v)|\leq C_{p}\bigl(|u|^{p-1}+|v|^{p-1}\bigr)|u-v|

and Hölder in
(t,n)
,

(4.12)

\|N(u)-N(v)\|_{L^{\frac{p+1}{p}}_{t}\ell^{\frac{p+1}{p}}_{n}}\leq C\bigl(\|u\|_{S}^{p-1}+\|v\|_{S}^{p-1}\bigr)\|u-v\|_{S}.

Applying Theorem
1.2
again yields

(4.13)

\|\Phi(u)-\Phi(v)\|_{S}\leq C\bigl(\|u\|_{S}^{p-1}+\|v\|_{S}^{p-1}\bigr)\|u-v\|_{S}.

Set
\varepsilon:=\|\varphi\|_{\ell^{2}}+\|\psi\|_{\ell^{2}}
.
Take
R:=2C\varepsilon
and
B_{R}:=\{u\in S:\|u\|_{S}\leq R\}
.
If
\varepsilon>0
is sufficiently small so that
C(2C\varepsilon)^{p-1}\leq\frac{1}{2}
,
then
\Phi
maps
B_{R}
into itself by (
4.11
) and is a strict contraction
by (
4.13
). Hence there exists a unique global fixed point

u\in S
solving (
4.9
). Moreover,

(4.14)

\|u\|_{S}\leq C\varepsilon.

Step 2. Energy regularity:
u\in C(\mathbb{R};\ell^{2})
and
\partial_{t}u\in C(\mathbb{R};\ell^{2})
.

Fix any finite interval
I=[-T,T]
. Since
u\in S
, we have

N(u)\in L^{\frac{p+1}{p}}_{t}(\mathbb{R};\ell^{\frac{p+1}{p}})
, and because

\frac{p+1}{p}<2
we have
\ell^{\frac{p+1}{p}}(\mathbb{Z})\hookrightarrow\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, hence

N(u)\in L^{\frac{p+1}{p}}_{t}(I;\ell^{2})\subset L^{1}_{t}(I;\ell^{2})
.

We interpret (
4.9
) as an identity in
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
The maps
t\mapsto\cos(t\mathbf{A})\varphi
and
t\mapsto\mathbf{A}^{-1}\sin(t\mathbf{A})\psi
are continuous in
\ell^{2}
.
For the Duhamel term, using
\|\mathbf{A}^{-1}\sin((t-s)\mathbf{A})\|_{\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}}\leq\|\mathbf{A}^{-1}\|

and dominated convergence, we conclude
u\in C(I;\ell^{2})
, hence

(4.15)

u\in C(\mathbb{R};\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})).

Moreover, since
N(u)\in L^{1}(I;\ell^{2})
, differentiating (
4.9
) in
\ell^{2}
for
t\in I

gives

(4.16)

\partial_{t}u(t)=-\mathbf{A}\sin(t\mathbf{A})\varphi+\cos(t\mathbf{A})\psi\pm\int_{0}^{t}\cos\bigl((t-s)\mathbf{A}\bigr)N(u(s))\,\mathrm{d}s\quad\text{in }\ell^{2}.

The right-hand side is continuous in
\ell^{2}
on
I
, hence

(4.17)

\partial_{t}u\in C(\mathbb{R};\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})).

Step 3. Energy conservation and uniqueness in the energy class.

We first note that the nonlinearity
N(u)=|u|^{p-1}u
is locally Lipschitz on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
:
for
u,v\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
,

\|N(u)-N(v)\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq C_{p}\bigl(\|u\|_{\ell^{2p}}^{p-1}+\|v\|_{\ell^{2p}}^{p-1}\bigr)\|u-v\|_{\ell^{2p}}\leq C_{p}\bigl(\|u\|_{\ell^{2}}^{p-1}+\|v\|_{\ell^{2}}^{p-1}\bigr)\|u-v\|_{\ell^{2}},

where we used
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})\hookrightarrow\ell^{2p}(\mathbb{Z})
for
p\geq 1
.

Since
\mathbf{A}^{2}
is bounded on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, the first-order system

\partial_{t}\binom{u}{\partial_{t}u}=\binom{\partial_{t}u}{-\mathbf{A}^{2}u\pm N(u)}

is an ODE on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})\times\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
with locally Lipschitz right-hand side.
Together with (
4.15
)–(
4.17
), this implies in particular

(4.18)

u\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R};\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})).

Hence the energy identity below is justified by classical differentiation, and the energy-class
solution is unique by the Picard–Lindelöf theorem (see
[
8
]
) as long as it exists.

Define the energy

(4.19)

E(t):=\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{A}u(t)\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\mp\frac{1}{p+1}\|u(t)\|_{\ell^{p+1}}^{p+1}.

Since
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})\hookrightarrow\ell^{p+1}(\mathbb{Z})
for
p\geq 1
,
E(t)
is well-defined.
Using (
4.18
) and taking the
\ell^{2}
inner product of

\partial_{tt}u+\mathbf{A}^{2}u=\pm N(u)
with
\partial_{t}u
, we obtain

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{A}u(t)\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\Bigr)=\pm\langle N(u(t)),\partial_{t}u(t)\rangle_{\ell^{2}}.

On the other hand,

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{p+1}\|u(t)\|_{\ell^{p+1}}^{p+1}\Bigr)=\langle N(u(t)),\partial_{t}u(t)\rangle_{\ell^{2}}.

Therefore
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E(t)=0
and hence

(4.20)

E(t)=E(0),\qquad\forall\,t\in\mathbb{R}.

Step 4. Uniform
\ell^{2}
bounds for small data (both signs).

Set

Y(t):=\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}+\|\mathbf{A}u(t)\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}.

By energy conservation,

E(t)=\frac{1}{2}Y(t)-\kappa\frac{1}{p+1}\|u(t)\|_{\ell^{p+1}}^{p+1}=E(0),\qquad\kappa\in\{+1,-1\}.

Since
\|u\|_{\ell^{p+1}}\leq\|u\|_{\ell^{2}}
and
\|u(t)\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq\underline{m}^{-1}\|\mathbf{A}u(t)\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq c^{-\frac{1}{2}}Y(t)^{1/2}
,

(4.21)

\|u(t)\|_{\ell^{p+1}}^{p+1}\leq C_{*}\,Y(t)^{\frac{p+1}{2}},\qquad C_{*}:=c^{-\frac{p+1}{2}}.

In defocusing case
\kappa=-1
we have

E(t)=\frac{1}{2}Y(t)+\frac{1}{p+1}\|u(t)\|_{\ell^{p+1}}^{p+1}\geq\frac{1}{2}Y(t),

hence
Y(t)\leq 2E(0)
for all
t
.

In Focusing case
\kappa=+1
, we use (
4.21
),

E(t)\geq\frac{1}{2}Y(t)-\frac{C_{*}}{p+1}Y(t)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}.

Choose
\delta>0
so that
\frac{C_{*}}{p+1}\delta^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\leq\frac{1}{4}
. Then

Y\leq\delta
implies
E(t)\geq\frac{1}{4}Y(t)
. Assume the data are small so that

Y(0)\leq\delta
and
4E(0)<\delta
. By continuity
Y(t)\leq 4E(0)
for all
t\geq 0
, and by time reversibility for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

In both cases,

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}Y(t)\leq CE(0)\leq C\bigl(\|\varphi\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}+\|\psi\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\bigr),

and hence

(4.22)

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\bigl(\|u(t)\|_{\ell^{2}}+\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{\ell^{2}}\bigr)\leq C\bigl(\|\varphi\|_{\ell^{2}}+\|\psi\|_{\ell^{2}}\bigr).

Step 5. Bounds for all
\tau
–admissible pairs.

Let
(q,r)
be any
\tau
–admissible pair with
q<\infty
.
Apply Theorem
1.2
to (
4.9
) with this
(q,r)
and with

(\tilde{q},\tilde{r})=(p+1,p+1)
. Using
\|N(u)\|_{L^{\frac{p+1}{p}}_{t}\ell^{\frac{p+1}{p}}_{n}}=\|u\|_{S}^{p}

and (
4.14
), we get

(4.23)

\|u\|_{L^{q}_{t}(\mathbb{R};\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z}))}\leq C\Bigl(\|\varphi\|_{\ell^{2}}+\|\psi\|_{\ell^{2}}+\|u\|_{S}^{p}\Bigr)\leq C(\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{p})\leq C\varepsilon,

for
\varepsilon
small.
For the endpoint
(q,r)=(\infty,2)
, the estimate follows from (
4.22
).
Therefore the claimed bound holds for all
\tau
–admissible pairs
(q,r)
.

Step 6. Decay
\|u(t)\|_{\ell^{r}}\to 0
for every
r>2
.

Fix
r>2
. Let
(q_{r},r)
be the
\tau
–admissible pair; then
q_{r}<\infty
.
By (
4.23
), we have
u\in L^{q_{r}}_{t}(\mathbb{R};\ell^{r})
and

f_{r}(t):=\|u(t)\|_{\ell^{r}}\in L^{q_{r}}(0,\infty)
.

We show
f_{r}(t)\to 0
as
t\to+\infty
. Since
r\geq 2
,
\|g\|_{\ell^{r}}\leq\|g\|_{\ell^{2}}
, hence
for
0\leq s<t
,

\displaystyle|f_{r}(t)-f_{r}(s)|

\displaystyle\leq\|u(t)-u(s)\|_{\ell^{r}}\leq\|u(t)-u(s)\|_{\ell^{2}}=\Bigl\|\int_{s}^{t}\partial_{\tau}u(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\Bigr\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{s}^{t}\|\partial_{\tau}u(\tau)\|_{\ell^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq C\varepsilon\,|t-s|,

by (
4.22
). Thus
f_{r}
is uniformly continuous on
[0,\infty)
.
A bounded uniformly continuous function in
L^{q_{r}}(0,\infty)
must satisfy
f_{r}(t)\to 0

as
t\to\infty
. Hence
\|u(t)\|_{\ell^{r}}\to 0
for every
r>2
.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
∎

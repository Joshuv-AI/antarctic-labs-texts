Abstract
. One standard way to prove existence for deterministic, highly nonlinear PDEs is to use the Schauder–Tychonoff fixed-point theorem. In what follows, we introduce and verify a stochastic variant of the Schauder–Tychonoff theorem. We apply our existence result to nonlinear stochastic diffusion equations with non-Lipschitz perturbations.

When passing from deterministic to stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs), one quickly encounters a number of difficulties that are not present in the
classical setting. Fundamental identities that hold deterministically may fail in the stochastic framework, for instance, expectations do not in general
factorise, so that
\mathbb{E}[uv]\neq\mathbb{E}[u]\mathbb{E}[v]
, and
integrability assumptions such as
\mathbb{E}\big[\|v\|\big]<\infty
does not automatically
imply higher moments. Moreover, many structural tools commonly used in
deterministic analysis such as local monotonicity methods or maximum principles may
no longer apply in the presence of randomness. Uniform a priori bounds, while
still obtainable in many situations, often require more delicate arguments, and
non-Lipschitz nonlinearities typically need to be handled via suitable
interpolation or cut-off procedures.

Despite these additional challenges, a general strategy for establishing the
existence of probabilistic weak solutions has emerged and is now widely used (see
[
12
,
53
]
).
One usually begins by constructing a sequence of approximate solutions, either
through a Galerkin scheme or a time-discretisation method. These approximations
must satisfy uniform bounds that are strong enough to guarantee the tightness of the
induced probability laws. Once tightness is established, the Skorokhod
representation theorem plays a central role: it provides a new probability space
on which a subsequence of the approximations converges almost surely, with
respect to appropriately “reconstructed” Wiener processes. The final and often
most delicate step consists of analysing the limiting objects and showing that
the limit indeed satisfies the original SPDE in the desired weak or
martingale sense. For this particular part, we combine a standard identification procedure with the approach given in
[
53
]
.

In the present work, we take a different route. Instead of relying on a
discretisation-based compactness argument, we formulate the existence theory as a
fixed-point problem on the level of probability laws. By applying the
Schauder–Tychonoff theorem directly to the laws of appropriately parametrised
solution candidates, we obtain a modular and conceptually transparent framework that is independent of the particular approximation scheme. This approach
isolates the essential probabilistic and topological ingredients and clarifies
the exact analytical requirements needed for the fixed-point argument to close.
As a consequence, the method applies to a broad class of nonlinear stochastic
systems of type (
1.1
) without the need to verify discretisation-specific
properties, thereby simplifying the existence theory while retaining full
generality.

Before turning to the main result of this article, we first clarify the notion of

compactness cannot be interpreted as a solution in the classical (probabilistic
strong) sense. Instead, one must reconstruct a probability space on which both
the solution and the corresponding Wiener processes are defined.

\displaystyle\quad+2\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}\big((\Sigma(u_{1}(s))-\Sigma(u_{2}(s)))dW(s),u_{1}(s)-u_{2}(s)\big)_{H}\bigg|.

We focus on the first term on the right-hand side of (
3

\displaystyle\bigg|2\int_{0}^{T}\big(\nabla^{-1}(\xi_{1}^{[\frac{1}{2}]}(s)-\xi_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}(s)),\nabla^{-1}(u_{1}(s)-u_{2}(s))\big)\,ds\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq CT^{\frac{m}{m+1}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}|\xi_{1}(s)-\xi_{2}(s)|_{V}^{m+1}\,ds\bigg)^{\frac{1}{m+1}}+C\int_{0}^{T}|u_{1}(s)-u_{2}(s)|_{H}^{2}\,ds.

For the final term appearing in the right-hand side of (
3

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}\big((\Sigma(u_{1}(s))-\Sigma(u_{2}(s)))dW(s),u_{1}(s)-u_{2}(s)\big)_{H}\bigg|\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|u_{1}(t)-u_{2}(t)|_{H}^{2}\bigg]+C\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}|\Sigma(u_{1}(s))-\Sigma(u_{2}(s))|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}({{\mathcal{O}}}),H)}^{2}\,ds\bigg].

Combining the above facts with (
3
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|u_{1}(t)-u_{2}(t)|_{H}^{2}\bigg]+\frac{1}{2^{m-2}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}|u_{1}(s)-u_{2}(s)|_{V}^{m+1}\,ds\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq CT^{\frac{m}{m+1}}\bigg\{\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}|\xi_{1}(s)-\xi_{2}(s)|_{V}^{m+1}\,ds\bigg]\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{m+1}}+C\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}|u_{1}(s)-u_{2}(s)|_{H}^{2}\,ds\bigg].

An application of Gronwall’s inequality in the above inequality yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|u_{1}(t)-u_{2}(t)|_{H}^{2}\bigg]+\frac{1}{2^{m-2}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}|u_{1}(s)-u_{2}(s)|_{V}^{m+1}\,ds\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq CT^{\frac{m}{m+1}}\bigg\{\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}|\xi_{1}(s)-\xi_{2}(s)|_{V}^{m+1}\,ds\bigg]\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{m+1}},

and hence the continuity holds.

Step (IV)

Verification of Condition (
iv
)
. In this step, we establish that the operator

From the energy estimate (

that means the compact containment condition holds.

Now, we prove the solution’s time regularity.

.
Applying the Itô formula to the process
|u(s)-u_{0}|_{H}^{2}
, we obtain for all

(with any

),
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.,

\displaystyle|u(s)-u_{0}|_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle=-2\int_{0}^{s}\big(u^{[m]}(r)-u^{[m]}_{0},u(r)-u_{0}\big)dr+2\int_{0}^{s}\big(u^{[m]}_{0},u(r)-u_{0}\big)dr

\displaystyle\quad+2\int_{0}^{s}\langle\xi^{[\frac{1}{2}]}(r),u(r)-u_{0}\rangle dr+\int_{0}^{s}|\Sigma(u(r))|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}({{\mathcal{O}}}),H)}^{2}dr

(3.18)

\displaystyle\quad+2\int_{0}^{s}\big(\Sigma(u(r))dW(r),u(r)-u_{0}\big)_{H}.

From the above expression, we obtain for all

,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.,

\displaystyle|u(s)-u_{0}|_{H}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{s}\big(u^{[m]}(r)-u^{[m]}_{0},u(r)-u_{0}\big)dr

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{s}\big(u^{[m]}_{0},u(r)-u_{0}\big)dr+2\int_{0}^{s}\langle\xi^{[\frac{1}{2}]}(r),u(r)-u_{0}\rangle dr

(3.19)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{s}|\Sigma(u(r))|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}({{\mathcal{O}}}),H)}^{2}dr+2\int_{0}^{s}\big(\Sigma(u(r))dW(r),u(r)-u_{0}\big)_{H}.

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\bigg|\int_{0}^{s}\big(u^{[m]}_{0},u(r)-u_{0}\big)dr\bigg|\bigg]
and estimate it as follows

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\bigg|\int_{0}^{s}\big(u^{[m]}_{0},u(r)-u_{0}\big)dr\bigg|\bigg]

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\bigg|\int_{0}^{s}\langle\xi^{[\frac{1}{2}]}(r),u(r)-u_{0}\rangle dr\bigg|\bigg]

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|u(s)-u_{0}|_{H}^{2}\bigg]+\frac{1}{2^{m-1}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}|u(r)-u_{0}|_{V}^{m+1}dr\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq 2t^{\frac{m}{m+1}}\mathbb{E}\big[|u_{0}|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}\big]^{\frac{m}{m+1}}\bigg\{\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}|u(r)-u_{0}|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}dr\bigg]\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{m+1}}

\displaystyle\quad+Ct^{\frac{m}{m+1}}R^{\frac{1}{m+1}}+C\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}|u(r)-u_{0}|_{H}^{2}dr\bigg]+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|u(s)-u_{0}|_{H}^{2}\bigg]

\displaystyle\quad+C\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}|\Sigma(u(r))-\Sigma(u_{0})|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}({{\mathcal{O}}}),H)}^{2}dr\bigg]+C\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}|\Sigma(u_{0})|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{2}(U,H)}^{2}dr\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq 2t^{\frac{m}{m+1}}\mathbb{E}\big[|u_{0}|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}\big]^{\frac{m}{m+1}}\bigg\{\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}|u(r)-u_{0}|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}dr\bigg]\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{m+1}}

\displaystyle\quad+Ct^{\frac{m}{m+1}}R^{\frac{1}{m+1}}+C\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}|u(r)-u_{0}|_{H}^{2}dr\bigg]+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|u(s)-u_{0}|_{H}^{2}\bigg]

\displaystyle\quad+C\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}|u(r)-u_{0}|_{H}^{2}dr\bigg]+Ct\mathbb{E}\big[|u_{0}|_{H}^{2}\big].

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|u(s)-u_{0}|_{H}^{2}\bigg]+\frac{1}{2^{m-2}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}|u(r)-u_{0}|_{V}^{m+1}dr\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(4t^{\frac{m}{m+1}}\mathbb{E}\big[|u_{0}|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}\big]^{\frac{m}{m+1}}\bigg\{\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}|u(r)-u_{0}|_{L^{m+1}}^{m+1}dr\bigg]\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{m+1}}

\displaystyle\qquad+Ct^{\frac{m}{m+1}}R^{\frac{1}{m+1}}+Ct\mathbb{E}\big[|u_{0}|_{H}^{2}\big]\bigg)e^{Ct}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(t^{\frac{m}{m+1}}+t\big)\leq Ct^{\frac{m}{m+1}}.

From the above expression, we obtain a positive constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\|u\|_{\mathbb{W}^{\alpha}_{2}(0,T;H)}^{2}\bigg]\leq C,\ \text{ for }\ \alpha<\frac{m}{2(m+1)}.

Using the above fact and the energy estimate (
3.4
), we know that the solution
u\in\mathbb{X}_{1}:=L^{m+1}(0,T;V)\cap\mathbb{W}^{\alpha}_{2}(0,T;H)\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;H)

[
25

(cf.
[
68
]

in

is compact. In particular, for any

is compact in

. This completes the verification of Assumption (
iv
) of Theorem
2.5

We only have to verify the last assumption of Theorem

Thus, we verified all the assumptions of Theorem

Appendix A
Extension of a probability space

To construct the Wiener process, it is necessary to introduce additional random variables on an auxiliary probability space and then extend the original probability space by means of this auxiliary space.
In what follows, we make precise the notion of an extension of a probability space (compare with
[
45
]
).

Definition A.1
.

We say a probability space
(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathscr{F}},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})
with a filtration
\{\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is an extension of a probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
with a filtration
\{\mathscr{F}_{t}\}
, if there exists a mapping
\pi:\widetilde{\Omega}\longrightarrow\Omega
which is
\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}/\mathscr{F}
-measurable such that

(i)
\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}_{t}\supset\pi^{-1}\left(\mathscr{F}_{t}\right)
,

(ii)
\mathbb{P}=\pi(\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})(:=\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}\circ\pi^{-1}),
and

(iii) for every
X(\omega)\in\mathscr{L}_{\infty}(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})

\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\left(\widetilde{X}(\widetilde{\omega})\mid\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}_{t}\right)=\mathbb{E}\left(X\mid\mathscr{F}_{t}\right)(\pi\widetilde{\omega}),\quad\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}\text{-a.s., }

where we set
\widetilde{X}(\widetilde{\omega})=X(\pi\widetilde{\omega})
for
\widetilde{\omega}\in\widetilde{\Omega}
.

Definition A.2
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space with filtration
\{\mathscr{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
. Let
\left(\Omega^{\prime},\mathscr{F}^{\prime},{\mathbb{P}}^{\prime}\right)
be another probability space and set

\widetilde{\Omega}=\Omega\times\Omega^{\prime},\quad\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}=\mathscr{F}\times\mathscr{F}^{\prime},\quad\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}=\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{P}^{\prime},

and

\pi\widetilde{\omega}=\omega\quad\text{ for }\quad\widetilde{\omega}=\left(\omega,\omega^{\prime}\right)\in\widetilde{\Omega}.

If
\{\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is a filtration on
(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathscr{F}},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})
such that
\mathscr{F}_{t}\times\mathscr{F}^{\prime}\supset\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}_{t}\supset\mathscr{F}_{t}\times\left\{\Omega^{\prime},\emptyset\right\}
, then
(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathscr{F}},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})
with
\{\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is called a standard extension of
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
with filtration
\{\mathscr{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
.

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\Big(\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|W_{n}(t)-W_{n-1}(t)|_{U}^{2}>a_{n}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\Bigg(\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\Big(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi_{i}\xi_{k,n}S_{k,n}(t),\sum_{i^{\prime}\in\mathbb{I}}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi_{i^{\prime}}\xi_{k,n}S_{k,n}(t)\Big)>a_{n}^{2}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\Bigg(\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\lambda_{i}|\psi_{i}|_{H}^{2}|\xi_{k,n}|^{2}|S_{k,n}(t)|^{2}>a_{n}^{2}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\lambda_{i}2^{-(n+1)}\sup_{k}|\xi_{k,n}|^{2}>a_{n}^{2}\Big)\ \Big(\text{using orthonormality of }\psi_{i},(i)\text{ for }S_{k,n}\Big)

(C.1)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{k}|\xi_{k,n}|^{2}>\frac{2^{n+1}a_{n}^{2}}{\sum\limits_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\lambda_{i}}\right)\quad\Big(\text{using Claim \ref{claim6.3}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{C_{1}}{\pi}}2^{n/2-1}a_{n}^{-1}e^{-\frac{2^{(n+1)}a_{n}^{2}}{C_{1}}},\qquad\Big(\text{where }C_{1}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\lambda_{i}\Big).

Let us choose the constant
a_{n}
in such a way that

(C.2)

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{C_{1}}{\pi}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}2^{n/2-1}a_{n}^{-1}e^{-\frac{2^{(n+1)}a_{n}^{2}}{C_{1}}}<\infty,\text{ and }\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}a_{n}<\infty.

Let us choose
a_{n}=\big(n2^{-(n+1)}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Then, conditions change to

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{C_{1}}{2\pi}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{2^{n}}{n^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{n}}<\infty,\text{ and }\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{n^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2^{n/2}}<\infty

The condition (
C.2
)
1
and the Borel-Cantelli lemma ensure that the convergence is almost surely. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|W_{n}(t)-W_{n-1}(t)|_{U}\leq a_{n},\text{ for all large enough }n;

the second condition (
C.2
)
2
guaranties that
W_{n}
converge uniformly almost surely to a limit function
W
, therefore,
W
is continuous.

From the above arguments, we obtained that
W_{n}
converges uniformly to some continuous limit
W
. Now, we need to show that the limit
W
is a centred Gaussian process with covariance structure
\mathbb{E}\big[\langle W(t),u\rangle\langle W(s),v\rangle\big]=t\langle Qu,v\rangle,
for
s\leq t
. Note that,
W_{n}
is a centred Gaussian process, thus the vector form
\big(W_{n}(t_{1}),W_{n}(t_{2}),\dots,W_{n}(t_{k})\big)
is also a centred Gaussian process and converges almost surely to
\big(W(t_{1}),W(t_{2}),\dots,W(t_{k})\big)
, which also has a zero-mean Gaussian law. Moreover, the limit of covariances of
W_{n}
gives the covariance of
W

Let us move to the proof of covariance. We know that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\langle W_{n}(t),u\rangle\langle W_{n}(s),v\rangle\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\Big\langle\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\ \sum_{(k,m)\in S_{m}}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}\psi_{i}\ \xi_{k,m}S_{k,m}(t),u\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\times\Big\langle\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\ \sum_{(k,m)\in S_{m}}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}\psi_{i}\ \xi_{k,m}S_{k,m}(s),v\Big\rangle\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\Big\langle\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}\psi_{i},u\Big\rangle\Big\langle\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}\psi_{i},v\Big\rangle\Bigg(\sum_{m=0}^{n}\ \sum_{(k,m)\in S_{m}}S_{k,m}(s)S_{k,m}(t)\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\langle\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}\psi_{i},u\rangle\langle\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}\psi_{i},v\rangle\Bigg(\sum_{m=0}^{n}\ \sum_{(k,m)\in S_{m}}S_{k,m}(s)S_{k,m}(t)\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}\langle\sqrt{Q}\psi_{i},u\rangle\langle\sqrt{Q}\psi_{i},v\rangle\Bigg(\sum_{m=0}^{n}\ \sum_{(k,m)\in S_{m}}S_{k,m}(s)S_{k,m}(t)\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\langle Qu,v\rangle\Bigg(\sum_{m=0}^{n}\ \sum_{(k,m)\in S_{m}}S_{k,m}(s)S_{k,m}(t)\Bigg)

where we used the independence of
\xi_{k,n}
. The right-hand side of the above expression converges to

\displaystyle\langle Qu,v\rangle\sum_{(k,m)\in S_{m}}S_{k,m}(s)S_{k,m}(t)=\int_{0}^{1}\mathds{1}_{[0,s]}(r)\mathds{1}_{[0,t]}(r)dr=\min\{t,s\},

since
\displaystyle S_{k,m}(t)=\int_{0}^{1}\mathds{1}_{[0,t]}(r)h_{k,n}(r)dr
is the Fourier coefficient of
h_{k,n}
in the representation of
\mathds{1}_{[0,t]}
in terms of complete orthonormal system
\{h_{k,n}:(k,n)\in S\}
.
∎

Claim C.3
.

To show

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg(\sup_{k\in[0,2^{n}],k\text{ odd}}|\xi_{k,n}|^{2}>\frac{2^{(n+1)}a_{n}^{2}}{C_{1}}\bigg)\simeq\sqrt{\frac{C_{1}}{\pi}}2^{n/2-3/2}a_{n}^{-1}e^{-\frac{2^{(n+1)}a_{n}^{2}}{C_{1}}}.

Proof.

We know that
\xi_{k,n}\in\mathcal{N}(0,1)
, thus the square of a standard normal random variable
|\xi_{k,n}|^{2}
follows a
\chi_{1}^{2}
distribution whose CDF is given by

\displaystyle F_{\chi_{1}^{2}}(x)=\mathbb{P}\Big(|\xi_{k,n}|^{2}\leq x\Big)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{x}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-t/2}\,dt.

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(|\xi_{k,n}|^{2}>x\Big)=1-F_{\chi_{1}^{2}}(x).

Using the union bound, we can approximate the probability of the supremum exceeding
\frac{2^{(n+1)}a_{n}^{2}}{C_{1}}
. For any permissible
k
, the probability that
|\xi_{k,n}|^{2}
exceeds
\frac{2^{(n+1)}a_{n}^{2}}{C_{1}}
is

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(|\xi_{k,n}|^{2}>\frac{2^{(n+1)}a_{n}^{2}}{C_{1}}\Big)=1-F_{\chi_{1}^{2}}\Big(\frac{2^{(n+1)}a_{n}^{2}}{C_{1}}\Big).

Note that
\xi_{k,n}
are independent, the probability that none of
|\xi_{k,n}|^{2}
exceeds
\frac{2^{(n+1)}a_{n}^{2}}{C_{1}}
is

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\sup_{k\in[0,2^{n}],k\text{ odd}}|\xi_{k,n}|^{2}\leq\frac{2^{(n+1)}a_{n}^{2}}{C_{1}}\Big)=\prod_{k\in[0,2^{n}],k\text{ odd}}\mathbb{P}\Big(|\xi_{k,n}|^{2}\leq\frac{2^{(n+1)}a_{n}^{2}}{C_{1}}\Big).

Using the independence, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\sup_{k\in[0,2^{n}],k\text{ odd}}|\xi_{k,n}|^{2}\leq\frac{2^{(n+1)}a_{n}^{2}}{C_{1}}\Big)=\Big[F_{\chi_{1}^{2}}\Big(\frac{2^{(n+1)}a_{n}^{2}}{C_{1}}\Big)\Big]^{2^{n-1}},

since
k
is odd, there are
2^{n-1}
odd indices in the range
[0,2^{n}]
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\sup_{k\in[0,2^{n}],k\text{ odd}}|\xi_{k,n}|^{2}>\frac{2^{(n+1)}a_{n}^{2}}{C_{1}}\Big)=1-\Big[F_{\chi_{1}^{2}}\Big(\frac{2^{(n+1)}a_{n}^{2}}{C_{1}}\Big)\Big]^{2^{n-1}}.

Note that, for large
n
, the term
2^{n-1}
grows exponentially. If
F_{\chi_{1}^{2}}\Big(\frac{2^{(n+1)}a_{n}^{2}}{C_{1}}\Big)
is close to
1
, we can approximate the complementary probability using the union bound, that is,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\sup_{k\in[0,2^{n}],k\text{ odd}}|\xi_{k,n}|^{2}>\frac{2^{(n+1)}a_{n}^{2}}{C_{1}}\Big)

\displaystyle\eqsim 2^{n-1}\mathbb{P}\bigg(|\xi_{k,n}|^{2}>\frac{2^{(n+1)}a_{n}^{2}}{C_{1}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}\bigg[1-F_{\chi_{1}^{2}}\Big(\frac{2^{(n+1)}a_{n}^{2}}{C_{1}}\Big)\bigg].

We know that, for large
x
, the tail of
\chi_{1}^{2}
distribution can be approximated as

\displaystyle 1-F_{\chi_{1}^{2}}(x)\sim\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi x}}e^{-x/2}.

Using the above fact, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\sup_{k\in[0,2^{n}],k\text{ odd}}|\xi_{k,n}|^{2}>\frac{2^{(n+1)}a_{n}^{2}}{C_{1}}\Big)

\displaystyle\eqsim\frac{2^{n-1}}{\sqrt{\frac{\pi 2^{(n+1)}a_{n}^{2}}{2C_{1}}}}e^{-\frac{2^{(n+1)}a_{n}^{2}}{C_{1}}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{C_{1}}{\pi}}2^{n/2-1}a_{n}^{-1}e^{-\frac{2^{(n+1)}a_{n}^{2}}{C_{1}}},

which completes the proof.
∎

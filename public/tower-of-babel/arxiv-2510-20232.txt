1.
Introduction

A basic task in algebraic number theory is to describe the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
of a number field
K
. We call
K

monogenic
if there exists an algebraic integer
\theta
with
\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathbb{Z}[\theta]
, so that every element of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
can be expressed as a polynomial in a single generator, forming a
power integral basis
. This paper focuses the
pure
family
K=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
with
\alpha^{n}=m
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. We say that
K
is
\alpha
-monogenic
if
\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]=\mathcal{O}_{K}
. A natural question then arises: when is
K

\alpha
-monogenic? For example, in degree
2
the answer depends on congruence conditions: for
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{a})
one has

\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{a}]\quad\text{if }a\equiv 2,3\pmod{4},\qquad\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathbb{Z}\!\left[\frac{1+\sqrt{a}}{2}\right]\quad\text{if }a\equiv 1\pmod{4}

(see, e.g.,
[
Neu99
, Ch.I]
). Our paper gives an elementary criterion for a pure number field to be
\alpha
-monogenic, together with its natural density consequences.

1.1.
Main results

Our first contribution is a
one-page
proof, relying only on Dedekind’s index theorem, which determines
\alpha
-monogeneity in every pure degrees.

Criterion for
\alpha
-monogeneity.

Let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
with
\alpha^{n}=m
,
n\geq 2
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
. Then

\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \text{$m$ is square-free and }\nu_{p}(m^{p}-m)=1\ \text{for every prime }p\mid n.

The proof applies Dedekind’s index theorem to
f(X)=X^{n}-m
and, writing
n=p^{r}s
with
(p,s)=1
, compares
f
with
g^{\,p^{r}}
for
g(X)=X^{s}-m
modulo
p
.
A single
p
-adic evaluation in
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})[X]/(g)
shows that
p\mid[\mathcal{O}_{K}:\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]]
iff
p^{2}\mid(m^{p^{r}}-m)
, which is equivalent to
\nu_{p}(m^{p}-m)\geq 2
, so the square-free condition controls primes dividing
m
. This yields, as immediate corollaries, the familiar residue tests in small degrees (
n=3,4,5,6
) without any integral-basis computation.

Our second contribution is a clean distribution theory for
\alpha
-monogenic pure fields.
Let
\mathcal{E}_{p}=\{m\in\mathbb{Z}:\ m^{p}\equiv m\pmod{p^{2}}\}
. By Hensel, among units modulo
p^{2}
the “bad” classes are exactly the
(p-1)
Teichmüller lifts.
Conditioning on square-freeness, a proportion
1/(p+1)
of
m
lie in
\mathcal{E}_{p}
, and the events
\{m\in\mathcal{E}_{p}\}
are independent across primes dividing
n
(Chinese Remainder + uniform square-free distribution in classes).
Consequently, for the set of
m
with
x^{n}-m
irreducible (a density-
1
restriction by Eisenstein), the natural density of
\alpha
-monogeneity is

\delta_{n}=\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}\prod_{p\mid n}\frac{p}{p+1},

a product depending only on
\mathrm{rad}(n)
.
We refine this density in arithmetic progressions (with explicit local factors), exhibit progressions on which
100\%
of square-free
m
are
\alpha
-monogenic, and count
\alpha
-monogenic pure fields by discriminant:

N_{n}(Y)\sim\begin{cases}\delta_{n}\,(Y/n^{n})^{1/(n-1)},&n\ \text{odd},\\
2\delta_{n}\,(Y/n^{n})^{1/(n-1)},&n\ \text{even}.\end{cases}

Related results

There is an extensive literature on monogeneity, integral bases, and pure fields, among others, see Gaál’s monograph
[
Gaá19
]
for a comprehensive treatment and his recent survey
[
Gaá24
]

to generate a power integral basis. When specialized to
L=\mathbb{Q}

, this yields exactly the two local conditions in Theorem
2.6
(square-freeness of

\nu_{p}(m^{p}-m)=1
for all

)
[
Smi21
]
. Our proof has the advantage of being uniform and short in the pure case (one

-

[
Gas17
,
EF21
]
for earlier formulations in the case

and the associated Wieferich
-
type obstruction
a^{p}\equiv a\pmod{p^{2}}
.

A companion paper by the first-named author studies the full
p
-adic shape of explicit integral bases for pure fields and proves that, for each
p^{e}\parallel n
, the local shape is
determined by
a\bmod p^{e+1}
, yielding a minimal global period
M(n)=\prod_{p^{e}\parallel n}p^{e+1}=n\cdot\mathrm{rad}(n)
. In that framework the same local invariant
r_{p}(a)=\nu_{p}(a^{p-1}-1)-1

governs denominators and gives a Wieferich-type threshold at
p\mid n
, which is exactly the

\alpha
-monogeneity criterion
\nu_{p}(m^{p}-m)=1
. See
[
ND25
]

for details.

The density of square-free integers in progressions and basic
p
-adic features of Frobenius (Teichmüller lifts) will play a role in our distributional results, for accessible references see Brown
[
Bro21
]
and standard sources such as Cohen
[
Coh93
]
or Koblitz
[
Kob84
]
. For pure cubics, monogeneity (not necessarily via

[
AN21
]
. In the monic coefficient space, Bhargava–Shankar–Wang
[
BSW22
,
BSW25
]
proved that a positive density

of polynomials (f) satisfy
\mathbb{Z}[x]/(f)=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}[x]/(f)}
, and they determined the density of squarefree discriminants. They further extend these results to binary forms and resolve the arithmetic Bertini case of

2.
A criterion for
\alpha
-monogenic pure fields

To begin, we recall basic settings throughout the paper.

Definition 2.1
.

An algebraic number field
K
is called a
pure number field
if
K=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
, where
\alpha
is a root of an irreducible polynomial
f(X)=X^{n}-m
over
\mathbb{Q}
with
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
n\geq 2
.

Definition 2.2
.

If
\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]=\mathcal{O}_{K}
for
K=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
, then
K
is said to be
\alpha
-monogenic
.

For example, when
K=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
with
\alpha\in\{\sqrt{-2},\sqrt[3]{3},\sqrt[5]{6}\}
, one checks that
\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]
. We record a version of Dedekind’s index theorem as follows.

Theorem 2.3

(Dedekind’s index theorem)
.

Let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
, where
\alpha
is integral over
\mathbb{Z}
with monic minimal polynomial
f\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
. For a prime
p
, write the factorization of
\overline{f}
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[X]
as

\overline{f}(X)=\overline{\pi}_{1}(X)^{e_{1}}\cdots\overline{\pi}_{g}(X)^{e_{g}},

with pairwise distinct monic irreducibles
\overline{\pi}_{j}
. Let
\pi_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
be monic lifts of
\overline{\pi}_{j}
and define
F\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
by

f(X)=\pi_{1}(X)^{e_{1}}\cdots\pi_{g}(X)^{e_{g}}+p\,F(X).

Then
p\mid(\mathcal{O}_{K}:\mathbb{Z}[\alpha])
if and only if
\overline{\pi}_{j}\mid\overline{F}
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[X]
for some
j
with
e_{j}\geq 2
.

We also use the discriminant–index identity.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
K
be a number field and let
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{m}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
–basis of a sublattice
N\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
of finite index. Then

D(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{m})=\bigl(\mathcal{O}_{K}:N\bigr)^{2}\,\mathrm{disc}(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathbb{Z}).

In particular, if
K=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
with
\alpha
integral over
\mathbb{Z}
and minimal polynomial
f
, then

(2.1)

\mathrm{disc}(f)=\bigl(\mathcal{O}_{K}:\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]\bigr)^{2}\,\mathrm{disc}(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathbb{Z}).

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
with minimal polynomial
f(X)=X^{n}-m
. Then every prime dividing
\bigl(\mathcal{O}_{K}:\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]\bigr)
divides
mn
. Equivalently,
K
is
\alpha
-monogenic if and only if no prime
p\mid mn
divides
\bigl(\mathcal{O}_{K}:\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]\bigr)
.

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{disc}(f)=(-1)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}n^{n}m^{\,n-1}
, the claim follows from (
2.1
).
∎

We are now in a position to state and prove the main criterion of our work.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
with
\alpha
a root of the irreducible polynomial
f(X)=X^{n}-m\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
. Then
K
is
\alpha
-monogenic, i.e.
\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]=\mathcal{O}_{K}
, if and only if

•

m
is square-free, and

•

\nu_{p}(m^{p}-m)=1
for every prime
p
dividing
n
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.5
, only primes dividing
mn
can divide the index.

Case
p\mid m
.

Reducing
f
modulo
p
gives
\overline{f}(X)\equiv X^{n}
. Take
\pi(X)=X
(multiplicity
e=n\geq 2
) and write

f(X)=X^{n}+pF(X)\quad\text{with}\quad F(X)=-\frac{m}{p}\in\mathbb{Z}[X].

By Theorem
2.3
, we have
p\mid(\mathcal{O}_{K}:\mathbb{Z}[\alpha])
if and only if
\overline{\pi}\mid\overline{F}
, i.e. iff
F\equiv 0\pmod{p}
, which holds exactly when
\nu_{p}(m)\geq 2
. Thus no such
p
divides the index if and only if
m
is square-free.

Case
p\mid n
.

Write
n=p^{r}s
with
(p,s)=1
and set
g(X)=X^{s}-m
. Over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
we have

\overline{f}(X)=X^{n}-m\equiv\bigl(X^{s}-m\bigr)^{p^{r}}=\overline{g}(X)^{p^{r}},

so every irreducible factor appears with multiplicity
\geq 2
. Define

F(X)=\frac{f(X)-g(X)^{p^{r}}}{p}\in\mathbb{Z}[X],

which is integral since
\binom{p^{r}}{k}\equiv 0\pmod{p}
for
0<k<p^{r}
. Dedekind’s index theorem gives

p\mid(\mathcal{O}_{K}:\mathbb{Z}[\alpha])\iff\overline{\pi}\mid\overline{F}

for some irreducible factor
\overline{\pi}
of
\overline{g}
. Let
\alpha_{0}
be any root of
\overline{g}
(hence of
\overline{f}
). In the ring
A=(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})[X]/(g)
we have
X^{s}=m
, so

\frac{f(X)-g(X)^{p^{r}}}{p}\equiv\frac{X^{n}-m}{p}\equiv\frac{m^{p^{r}}-m}{p}\pmod{pA}.

Reducing modulo
p
and evaluating at
\alpha_{0}
yields

\overline{F}(\alpha_{0})\equiv\frac{m^{p^{r}}-m}{p}\pmod{p}.

Consequently,
p\mid(\mathcal{O}_{K}:\mathbb{Z}[\alpha])
iff
p^{2}\mid(m^{p^{r}}-m)
, i.e. iff
\nu_{p}(m^{p^{r}}-m)\geq 2
, and hence

p\nmid(\mathcal{O}_{K}:\mathbb{Z}[\alpha])\iff\nu_{p}(m^{p^{r}}-m)=1.

Finally, if
p\mid m
with
\nu_{p}(m)=1
then
m^{p^{r}}-m=m(m^{p^{r}-1}-1)
has
p
-adic valuation
1
. If
p\nmid m
, then

\nu_{p}(m^{p^{r}}-m)=\nu_{p}\bigl(m(m^{p^{r}-1}-1)\bigr)=\nu_{p}(m^{p^{r}-1}-1)=\nu_{p}(m^{p-1}-1)=\nu_{p}(m^{p}-m),

since
p\nmid(p^{r}-1)
and the valuation is constant on exponents prime to
p
. Thus the condition is independent of
r
, and the two bullets in the statement are equivalent to
(\mathcal{O}_{K}:\mathbb{Z}[\alpha])=1
.
∎

Remark 2.7

(On the reduction from
p^{r}
to
p
)
.

In the proof we used that, for any prime
p\mid n
and
n=p^{r}s
with
(p,s)=1
,

\nu_{p}(m^{p^{r}}-m)=\nu_{p}(m^{p}-m).

Indeed, if
p\nmid m
then

\nu_{p}(m^{p^{r}}-m)=\nu_{p}\!\big(m(m^{p^{r}-1}-1)\big)=\nu_{p}\!\big(m^{p^{r}-1}-1\big)=\nu_{p}\!\big(m^{p-1}-1\big)=\nu_{p}(m^{p}-m),

since
p\nmid(p^{r}-1)
and by the standard lifting-the-exponent (LTE) argument (see, e.g.,
[
Coh93
, Ch. 4]
or
[
Kob84
, §I.5]
).
If
p\mid m
and
m
is square-free at
p
, then
m^{p^{r}}-m=m\big(m^{p^{r}-1}-1\big)\equiv-m\not\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2}}
, whence
\nu_{p}(m^{p^{r}}-m)=1=\nu_{p}(m^{p}-m)
.
This justifies the final reduction used in the proof.

We derive the following immediate consequences.

Corollary 2.8
.

If
n=p^{r}
with
p
prime and
r\geq 1
, then
K
is
\alpha
-monogenic if and only if
m
is square-free and
\nu_{p}(m^{p}-m)=1
.

Corollary 2.9
.

•

If
n=3
then
K
is
\alpha
-monogenic iff
m
is square-free and
m\not\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}
.

•

If
n=4
then
K
is
\alpha
-monogenic iff
m
is square-free and
m\not\equiv 1\pmod{4}
.

•

If
n=5
then
K
is
\alpha
-monogenic iff
m
is square-free and
m\not\equiv 1,7,18,24\pmod{25}
.

•

If
n=6
then
K
is
\alpha
-monogenic iff
m
is square-free and
m\not\equiv 1\pmod{4}
and
m\not\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}
.

3.
Natural density for
\alpha
-monogenic pure fields

In this section we compute the natural density of integers
m
for which

\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]
when
\alpha^{n}=m
is pure. Throughout,
n\geq 2
is fixed.

Notation and preliminaries

For an integer
q\geq 1
and
a\in\mathbb{Z}
, write

\mathcal{A}(q,a)=\{m\in\mathbb{Z}:\ m\equiv a\pmod{q}\}.

A set
E\subset\mathbb{Z}
has
natural density

\delta(E)
if the limit

\delta(E):=\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{1}{X}\#\{m\in E:\ 1\leq m\leq X\}

exists (equivalently, the two-sided limit with
|m|\leq X
exists and has the same value for the congruence- and squarefreeness-defined sets we consider). We write
\mu
for the Möbius function, and
\mu^{2}
for the indicator of square-free integers.

We will also use the following elementary observations.

Lemma 3.1

(Square-free density in arithmetic progressions)
.

Let
q\geq 1
and
a\in\mathbb{Z}
with
\gcd(a,q)=1
. Then the set of square-free integers in the residue class
a\bmod q

has natural density

\delta\big(\{m:\ m\equiv a\bmod q,\ m\ \text{square-free}\}\big)=\frac{1}{q}\cdot\frac{1}{\zeta(2)}\cdot\prod_{\ell\mid q}\frac{1}{1-\ell^{-2}}.

In particular, this density is the same for every
a
coprime to
q
.

Proof.

Use
\mu^{2}(m)=\sum_{d^{2}\mid m}\mu(d)
and interchange sums:

\#\{m\leq X:\ m\equiv a\bmod q,\ \mu^{2}(m)=1\}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq\sqrt{X}\\
\gcd(d,q)=1\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\ \#\{m\leq X:\ m\equiv a\bmod q,\ d^{2}\mid m\}.

If
\gcd(d,q)>1
and
\gcd(a,q)=1
, the inner count is zero. Otherwise, with
\gcd(d,q)=1
,
the two conditions are equivalent to a single congruence modulo
qd^{2}
by the Chinese Remainder Theorem,
contributing
X/(qd^{2})+O(1)
. Hence

\#\{m\leq X:\ m\equiv a\bmod q,\ \mu^{2}(m)=1\}=\frac{X}{q}\sum_{\gcd(d,q)=1}\frac{\mu(d)}{d^{2}}+O(\sqrt{X}).

Since
\sum_{\gcd(d,q)=1}\mu(d)d^{-2}=\prod_{\ell\nmid q}(1-\ell^{-2})=\zeta(2)^{-1}\prod_{\ell\mid q}(1-\ell^{-2})^{-1}
, division by
X
and passage to the limit yield the claim.
∎

Lemma 3.2

(Fixed points of Frobenius modulo
p^{2}
)
.

For a prime
p
the congruence
x^{p}\equiv x\pmod{p^{2}}
has exactly
p
solutions modulo
p^{2}
:
one is
0
and the others are the
(p-1)
Teichmüller lifts in
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
.
Moreover, each solution modulo
p
lifts uniquely to a solution modulo
p^{k}
for all
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

Let
g(x)=x^{p}-x
. In
\mathbb{F}_{p}
the roots are all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
, at each root mod
p
one has
g^{\prime}(x)=px^{p-1}-1\equiv-1\pmod{p}
,
which is invertible. By Hensel, each root lifts uniquely to
p^{k}
for all
k
, so
\#\{x\bmod p^{2}:\ g(x)\equiv 0\}=p
.
∎

Remark 3.3

(Teichmüller lifts and
\mathcal{E}_{p}
)
.

The
(p-1)
unit solutions to
x^{p}\equiv x\pmod{p^{2}}
are precisely the Teichmüller lifts of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
. Thus
\mathcal{E}_{p}
is the union of the single class
0\bmod p^{2}
and these
(p-1)
unit classes (for
p=2
, interpret modulo
4
).

3.1.
Local exclusions among square-free integers

For a fixed prime
p
, define the set of
p
-exceptional
integers

\mathcal{E}_{p}:=\{m\in\mathbb{Z}:\ m^{p}\equiv m\pmod{p^{2}}\}.

By Lemma
3.2
,
\mathcal{E}_{p}
is the union of the single class
0\bmod p^{2}
and the
(p-1)
unit-classes
in
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
fixed by Frobenius. Intersecting with the square-free set automatically discards the class
0\bmod p^{2}
.

Proposition 3.4

(Local loss at a prime
p
)
.

Among square-free integers, the proportion that lie in
\mathcal{E}_{p}
equals
\dfrac{1}{p+1}
.
Equivalently, the
surviving
proportion at
p
equals
\dfrac{p}{p+1}
.

Proof.

Among units modulo
p^{2}
there are exactly
(p-1)
“bad” residue classes (the Teichmüller lifts).
By Lemma
3.1
, each such class contributes density

\frac{1}{p^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{\zeta(2)}\cdot\frac{1}{1-p^{-2}}=\frac{1}{\zeta(2)}\cdot\frac{1}{p^{2}-1}

inside the square-free set. Summing over
(p-1)
classes, the lost fraction among square-free integers is

\frac{p-1}{p^{2}-1}=\frac{1}{p+1},

so the surviving fraction is
1-\frac{1}{p+1}=\frac{p}{p+1}
.
∎

3.2.
Global density via independence across primes

Let
S
denote the set of square-free integers. For a finite set of primes
P
write

\mathcal{B}_{P}\ :=\ S\cap\bigcap_{p\in P}\mathcal{E}_{p}.

Note.
The set
\bigcap_{p\in P}\mathcal{E}_{p}
is a disjoint union of
\prod_{p\in P}(p-1)
residue classes modulo
Q_{P}:=\prod_{p\in P}p^{2}
(by the Chinese Remainder Theorem). Intersecting with
S
is then handled by Lemma
3.1
, applied class-by-class and summed.

Hence

(3.1)

\delta(\mathcal{B}_{P})=\frac{1}{\zeta(2)}\prod_{p\in P}\frac{p-1}{p^{2}-1}.

Now inclusion–exclusion over
P\subseteq\{p:\ p\mid n\}
shows that the set

\mathcal{G}_{n}\ :=\ S\ \setminus\ \bigcup_{p\mid n}\mathcal{E}_{p}

has density

\delta(\mathcal{G}_{n})=\frac{1}{\zeta(2)}\prod_{p\mid n}\left(1-\frac{p-1}{p^{2}-1}\right)=\frac{1}{\zeta(2)}\prod_{p\mid n}\frac{p}{p+1}.

This is the key combinatorial identity underlying the main density theorem below.

We also record that the restriction
x^{n}-m
irreducible is asymptotically harmless as follows.

Lemma 3.5
.

The set
\{m\in\mathbb{Z}:\ x^{n}-m\ \text{is irreducible over }\mathbb{Q}\}
has natural density
1
.

Proof.

If
m
has a prime factor
p
with
\nu_{p}(m)=1
, then
x^{n}-m
is Eisenstein at
p
, hence irreducible.
Thus the complement is contained in
\{\pm 1\}
and the set of
squarefull
integers
m=\pm\prod p_{i}^{e_{i}}
with all
e_{i}\geq 2
.
The number of squarefull integers
\leq X
is
O(X^{1/2})
(e.g. each squarefull
m
can be written as
a^{2}b^{3}
, summing over
b\leq X^{1/3}
gives

The following result describes the failure distribution and independence phenomena.

Proposition 3.16
.

Let
P=\{p:\ p\mid n\}
. Among square-free
m

\{m\in\mathcal{E}_{p}\}

Hence, for any subset

\displaystyle\delta\big(\{m:\ x^{n}-m\ \text{irr.},\ \mathcal{O}_{K}\neq\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]\ \text{and the set of failing primes is exactly }S\}\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}\Big(\prod_{p\in S}\frac{1}{p+1}\Big)\Big(\prod_{p\in P\setminus S}\frac{p}{p+1}\Big).

In particular, the expected number of failing primes (among those dividing

\sum_{p\mid n}\frac{1}{p+1}

Proof.

By (
3.1
) with CRT, for any finite

\delta\!\left(S\cap\bigcap_{p\in P}\mathcal{E}_{p}\right)=\frac{1}{\zeta(2)}\prod_{p\in P}\frac{p-1}{p^{2}-1}=\left(\frac{1}{\zeta(2)}\right)\prod_{p\in P}\left(\frac{1}{p+1}\cdot\frac{p}{p-1}\cdot\frac{p-1}{p}\right)=\delta(S)\prod_{p\in P}\frac{1}{p+1},

(Conditional local laws at a fixed prime)

Let

Among square-free

\begin{cases}1,&a\equiv 0\ (\bmod p),\\[4.0pt]
\frac{p-1}{p},&a\not\equiv 0\ (\bmod p).\end{cases}

Consequently, conditioning on

If

If

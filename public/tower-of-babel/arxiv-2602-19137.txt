Derivation Depth as an Information Metric: Axioms, Coding Theorems, and Storage–Computation Tradeoffs
Jianfeng Xu

Abstract
We propose derivation depth as a computable, premise-relative metric of online inferential cost in logical query answering systems. We formalize information as a two-domain object ( S O , S C ) (S_{O},S_{C}) linked by an effective realization mechanism, and separate intrinsic semantic content from operational shortcuts by extracting a canonical irredundant core Atom ​ ( S O ) \text{Atom}(S_{O}) . Given any finite available premise base B B , we define base-relative derivation depth Dd ( ⋅ ∣ B ) \text{Dd}(\cdot\mid B) via a computable predecessor operator and prove its well-definedness and computability. By encoding derivation traces and combining a richness condition with incompressibility, we establish coding-type bounds showing that, for generic information-rich queries, conditional Kolmogorov complexity satisfies K ⁡ ( q ∣ ⟨ B ⟩ ) = Θ ⁡ ( Dd ​ ( q ∣ B ) ​ log ⁡ ( | B | + Dd ​ ( q ∣ B ) ) ) K(q\mid\langle B\rangle)=\Theta\!\bigl(\text{Dd}(q\mid B)\log(|B|+\text{Dd}(q\mid B))\bigr) up to constant factors and an unavoidable logarithmic addressing term; for conjunctive workloads we provide a step-accurate BCQ instantiation. We then derive a frequency-weighted storage–computation tradeoff with a break-even scale f c = Θ ⁡ ( ρ ​ log ⁡ ( m + d ) ) f_{c}=\Theta(\rho\log(m+d)) , formulate budgeted caching as submodular maximization, and extend the framework to noisy premise bases with loss/pollution.



I
Introduction

Modern information systems increasingly operate under a coupled constraint:
they must answer rich logical queries over large knowledge bases with low latency, while facing tight budgets on
persistent storage, bandwidth, and online computation.
In practice, this constraint is managed via a continuum of mechanisms—from rule compilation and
materialized views to memoization and cache hierarchies—all of which can be summarized as a single design question:

which information should be stored (and in what form), and which information should be derived on demand,
so as to minimize total resource cost under the system’s access pattern?

This question arises in database view selection and physical design
[
12
,
19
]
,
in caching and paging systems
[
39
,
31
,
3
]
,
and more recently in retrieval-augmented architectures for large models
[
29
,
24
]
.
It also appears, in a broader form, in neuro-symbolic AI as a recurring tension between storing explicit symbolic
structures and recomputing (or approximating) them via learned computation
[
17
,
28
]
.

I-A

Problem setting and motivating tension

A query answering system implicitly maintains two kinds of “knowledge”:
(i)
semantic premises
that constitute an irredundant core of what is known, and
(ii)
operational shortcuts
that are stored to reduce online inference.
When the query workload changes, shortcuts may become wasteful; when the base grows, recomputation becomes expensive.
A satisfactory theory should therefore separate at least three notions:

(i)

Intrinsic content:
what the system logically entails from a minimal semantic core;

(ii)

Operational cost:
how expensive it is to answer queries under the currently stored shortcuts;

(iii)

Economic tradeoff:
when it is beneficial to pay storage to reduce online inference, given query frequencies.

To formalize these, we model knowledge bases and queries inside a fixed effective logical substrate
(Section
II
and Section
II-A
), define a canonical irredundant semantic core

\text{Atom}(S_{O})
(Definition
2.9
), and measure online inference by a base-relative derivation depth

\text{Dd}(\cdot\mid B)
(Definition
2.11
).
This supports two baselines: the
intrinsic
baseline
B=A:=\text{Atom}(S_{O})
and the

operational
baseline
B=S_{O}
, yielding intrinsic vs. operational depths
(Definition
2.12
and Remark
2.5
).

II
System Model and Axiomatic Foundations

This section fixes the formal substrate on which our information metrics and
optimization problems are built. The model consists of:
(i) a fixed logical language
\mathcal{L}
and an effective proof system;
(ii) a two-domain state description
(S_{O},S_{C})
with computable realization;
(iii) a notion of
irredundant semantic core

\text{Atom}(S_{O})
that separates

intrinsic
inferential content from
operational
shortcut effects; and
(iv) a computable predecessor operator
P_{O}
used solely to define and compute
base-relative derivation depth
\text{Dd}(\cdot\mid B)
.

II-A

Underlying Logical System
\mathcal{L}
and Effective State Sets

Throughout we fix a formal logical system
\mathcal{L}
taken to be

first-order logic with least fixed-point operators

\mathrm{FO(LFP)}

[
21
]
, extended with multiple sorts including at least

\mathsf{Obj}
(entities),
\mathsf{Time}
(time points), and
\mathsf{Carrier}
(carriers).
We restrict attention to
finite
structures over a discrete, bounded time domain

T=\{t_{0},\ldots,t_{n}\}
.
Under this restriction,
\mathcal{L}
is consistent, its satisfaction relation

\mathcal{M}\models\varphi
is decidable for every finite structure
\mathcal{M}
and formula

\varphi\in\mathcal{L}
, and—by the Immerman–Vardi theorem

[
33
,
42
]
—
\mathrm{FO(LFP)}
captures exactly the class of
polynomial-time computable properties over ordered finite structures.

Definition 2.1

(Expressible and effectively representable state sets)
.

A state set
S(X,T)
is
expressible in
\mathcal{L}
if there exists a finite

\mathcal{L}
-signature
\Sigma
and a finite
\mathcal{L}
-structure whose domain includes (encodings of)

X
and
T
, such that membership
s\in S(X,T)
and all relations used to describe the state dynamics
are definable by formulas of
\mathcal{L}
over
\Sigma
.
It is
effectively representable
if each
s\in S(X,T)
admits a finite encoding from which the
relevant
\mathcal{L}
-predicates can be evaluated effectively.

[
35
]
.

Henceforth, all state sets are assumed expressible and effectively representable in
\mathcal{L}
.

Assumption 2.1

(Fixed effective proof system and deductive closure)
.

We fix an effective proof system
\mathsf{PS}
for
\mathcal{L}
such that proof checking is decidable.
We write
\Gamma\vdash_{\mathcal{L}}\varphi
to denote derivability of a well-formed formula
\varphi

from a
finite
set of formulas
\Gamma
in
\mathsf{PS}
, and define the deductive closure operator

\text{Cn}(\Gamma)\;:=\;\{\varphi:\ \Gamma\vdash_{\mathcal{L}}\varphi\}.

Remark 2.1

(On decidability of
\text{Cn}(\cdot)
)
.

Assumption
2.1
guarantees only that
verifying
a purported proof is decidable.
In general, the membership predicate
\varphi\in\text{Cn}(\Gamma)
may be undecidable (and is typically only
semi-decidable). Whenever we require decidability of specific instances of redundancy/entailment, we state it
explicitly (e.g., Assumption
2.4
) and work in fragments where it is satisfied.

II-B

Computable Information Axioms

An
information system
consists of a semantic (ontological) state set
S_{O}
and a carrier
(physical) state set
S_{C}
, each equipped with its own time domain and representation.
A computable
enabling
(realization) mechanism links semantic states to carrier states
[
23
]
.

Axiom 2.1

(Binary attribute (two-domain information))
.

Information is modeled by two state domains
(S_{O},S_{C})
, where
S_{O}
is the semantic/ontological
state set and
S_{C}
is the carrier/physical state set.

Axiom 2.2

(Existence duration (domain-wise time and precedence))
.

There exist time domains
(T_{O},\prec_{O})
and
(T_{C},\prec_{C})
and time-index maps

\mathsf{time}_{O}:S_{O}\to T_{O}
and
\mathsf{time}_{C}:S_{C}\to T_{C}
.
Moreover, there is a fixed cross-domain precedence relation
\prec
such that whenever a carrier
state
s_{c}
is an enabled realization of a semantic state
s_{o}
, we have

\mathsf{time}_{O}(s_{o})\prec\mathsf{time}_{C}(s_{c}).

Axiom 2.3

(State representation (effective encodability))
.

There exist finite binary representations
\text{enc}_{O}:S_{O}\to\{0,1\}^{*}
and
\text{enc}_{C}:S_{C}\to\{0,1\}^{*}
such that:

(R1)

Decidable identity:
equality of encoded states is decidable.

(R2)

Effective predicate evaluation:
all relations/predicates used to describe membership and
structure on
S_{O}
and
S_{C}
in the fixed logic
\mathcal{L}
are effectively evaluable from the encodings.

Axiom 2.4

(Enabling mapping (computable totality and surjective coverage))
.

There exists a relation
R_{\mathcal{E}}\subseteq S_{O}\times S_{C}
and the induced set-valued map

\mathcal{E}:S_{O}\Rightarrow S_{C}
,
\mathcal{E}(s_{o}):=\{s_{c}\in S_{C}:(s_{o},s_{c})\in R_{\mathcal{E}}\}
, such that:

(E1)

Totality on semantic states:
for every
s_{o}\in S_{O}
,
\mathcal{E}(s_{o})\neq\varnothing
.

(E2)

Surjective coverage of carrier reality:
for every
s_{c}\in S_{C}
, there exists
s_{o}\in S_{O}

with
s_{c}\in\mathcal{E}(s_{o})
. Equivalently,
\bigcup_{s_{o}\in S_{O}}\mathcal{E}(s_{o})=S_{C}
.

(E3)

Computable enabling selector:
there exists a partial computable function

e:\{0,1\}^{*}\to\{0,1\}^{*}
such that for every
s_{o}\in S_{O}
,
e(\text{enc}_{O}(s_{o}))
halts and equals

\text{enc}_{C}(s_{c})
for some
s_{c}\in\mathcal{E}(s_{o})
.

(E4)

Temporal precedence of realization:
if
s_{c}\in\mathcal{E}(s_{o})
, then

\mathsf{time}_{O}(s_{o})\prec\mathsf{time}_{C}(s_{c})
.

By Axiom

, there exists a relation

We write the induced set-valued enabling (realization) map

\mathcal{E}:S_{O}\Rightarrow S_{C},\;\mathcal{E}(s_{o}):=\{\,s_{c}\in S_{C}:\ (s_{o},s_{c})\in R_{\mathcal{E}}\,\}.

(2.1)

(Computable enabling (realization) mechanism)
.

A relation
R_{\mathcal{E}}\subseteq S_{O}\times S_{C}
(equivalently, the induced
\mathcal{E}
in
(
2.1
)) is called a
computable enabling mechanism
if it satisfies all clauses

(E1)
–
(E4)
of Axiom
2.4
.

Definition 2.3

(Information instance)
.

An
information instance
is a tuple

\mathcal{I}\;=\;\langle S_{O},T_{O},S_{C},T_{C},\mathsf{time}_{O},\mathsf{time}_{C},R_{\mathcal{E}}\rangle,

where
(S_{O},S_{C})
are the two state domains (Axiom
2.1
),

\mathsf{time}_{O},\mathsf{time}_{C}

2.2
),
and

).

II-D

Synonymous State Sets and Ideal Information

Definition 2.4

(Compositional interpretation between sub-signatures)
.

Let
\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}
be sub-signatures of
\mathcal{L}
.
A
compositional interpretation

\tau:\Sigma_{1}\hookrightarrow\Sigma_{2}
in
\mathcal{L}
consists of:

(i)
for each sort
s
of
\Sigma_{1}
, a domain formula
\delta_{s}(y)
over
\Sigma_{2}
;

(ii)
for each relation symbol
R
of
\Sigma_{1}
, a formula
\tau_{R}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})
over
\Sigma_{2}
.
The map
\tau
extends to all formulas of
\Sigma_{1}
by the standard recursive clauses:

\displaystyle\tau(\neg\varphi)

\displaystyle\equiv\neg\tau(\varphi),

\displaystyle\tau(\varphi\land\psi)

\displaystyle\equiv\tau(\varphi)\land\tau(\psi),

\displaystyle\tau(\varphi\lor\psi)

\displaystyle\equiv\tau(\varphi)\lor\tau(\psi),

\displaystyle\tau(\exists x\!:\!s.\;\varphi)

\displaystyle\equiv\exists y.\,\bigl[\delta_{s}(y)\land\tau(\varphi[x/y])\bigr].

Definition 2.5

(Synonymous state sets)
.

State sets
S_{1}(X_{1},T_{1})
and
S_{2}(X_{2},T_{2})
, each expressible in
\mathcal{L}
as an interpretation of a
WFF family
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
(assumed finite or effectively enumerable) over sub-signatures

\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
, are
synonymous relative to
\mathcal{L}
, written

S_{1}\equiv_{\mathcal{L}}S_{2}
, if there exist:

(i)

compositional interpretations
\tau_{12}:\Sigma_{1}\hookrightarrow\Sigma_{2}
and

\tau_{21}:\Sigma_{2}\hookrightarrow\Sigma_{1}
in
\mathcal{L}
, and

(ii)

a bijection
\sigma:\Phi_{1}\to\Phi_{2}
,

such that for every
\varphi\in\Phi_{1}
,

\vdash_{\mathcal{L}}\;\sigma(\varphi)\leftrightarrow\tau_{12}(\varphi),

and round-trip coherence holds on generators (bi-interpretability).

Remark 2.2

(Relation to interpretability)
.

Definition
2.5
is a formulation of mutual interpretability (bi-interpretability) at the
level of generating formula families; see standard references in model theory and finite model theory,
e.g.,
[
20
,
16
]
.

Definition 2.6

(Ideal information)
.

An information instance
\mathcal{I}
is
ideal
(with respect to
\mathcal{L}
) if

S_{O}\equiv_{\mathcal{L}}S_{C}.

Noisy Information

Assumption 2.2

(Common semantic universe for set operations)
.

There exists a fixed ambient set
\mathbb{S}_{O}
such that all semantic state sets considered satisfy

S_{O}\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}
.
Moreover,
\mathbb{S}_{O}
is effectively representable in the sense that
\text{enc}_{O}
and
\mathsf{time}_{O}
extend to

\mathbb{S}_{O}
, and

Definition 2.7

(Noisy semantic base (set perturbation))
.

Let
S_{O}\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}
be the intended semantic state set.
A
noisy semantic base
associated with
S_{O}
is any set of the form

\tilde{S}_{O}:=(S_{O}\setminus S_{O}^{-})\cup S_{O}^{+},

where
S_{O}^{-}\subseteq S_{O}
(lost states) and
S_{O}^{+}\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}\setminus S_{O}
(spurious states).

Lemma 2.1

(Semantic transferability under signature isomorphism)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\mathrm{FO(LFP)}
be the underlying logical system over finite relational structures.
For any state set
S(X,T)
expressible in
\mathcal{L}
over a sub-signature
\Sigma=\{R_{1},\ldots,R_{n}\}
,
and any arity-matching
\Sigma^{\prime}=\{R^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,R^{\prime}_{n}\}
, there exists

expressible over
\Sigma^{\prime}

such that
S\equiv_{\mathcal{L}}S^{\prime}
.

Uniformly rename symbols

and transport the generating formula family; this yields a

∎

Definition 2.8

(Noisy information (semantic description relative to a fixed carrier))
.

Fix a carrier domain
S_{C}
.
A
noisy information
associated with
S_{C}
is an abstract object

\tilde{\mathcal{I}}=\langle\tilde{S}_{O},\,S_{C},\,(\tau_{OC},\tau_{CO},\sigma)\rangle,

where

witnesses

.

(Existence of carrier-fixed noisy information)
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be an information instance with semantic domain
S_{O}
and carrier domain
S_{C}
.
Then there exists a noisy semantic base
\tilde{S}_{O}
of the form

\tilde{S}_{O}=(S_{O}\setminus S_{O}^{-})\cup S_{O}^{+}
such that

II-F

Semantic Dependencies, Irredundant Cores, and Derivation Depth

This subsection introduces two logically distinct ingredients:
(i) an
irredundant semantic core

\text{Atom}(S_{O})
used to separate intrinsic vs. operational inference;
(ii) a computable predecessor operator
P_{O}
used to define and compute derivation depth
\text{Dd}(\cdot\mid B)
.
Crucially,
\text{Atom}(S_{O})
is defined via the deductive closure
\text{Cn}(\cdot)
rather than via
P_{O}
.

Available premise bases.

A finite set
B\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}
is called an
available premise base
if its elements are treated as
depth-
0
premises when measuring derivation depth from
B
.

Assumption 2.3

(Finite and effectively listable knowledge bases)
.

The knowledge bases
S_{O}
considered in this paper are finite and effectively listable under a fixed canonical order.

Assumption 2.4

(Effective redundancy test (core extractability))
.

For the knowledge bases
S_{O}
considered, the predicate

s\in\text{Cn}(\Gamma)

is decidable whenever
\Gamma\subseteq S_{O}
is finite and
s\in S_{O}
.
(Equivalently: redundancy of a stored formula relative to the remaining stored formulas is decidable.)

Remark 2.3

(When Assumption
2.4
is reasonable)
.

Assumption
2.4
is strong for unrestricted logics, but it is satisfied in many
practically relevant decidable fragments used for knowledge bases and rule systems (e.g., Datalog/Horn-style
settings and bounded-domain theories), where entailment and redundancy checking are decidable and often tractable;
see, e.g.,
[
1
,
15
]
.

Definition 2.9

(Semantic atomic core (canonical irredundant generating set))
.

Let
S_{O}
be a finite knowledge base.
Define
\text{Atom}(S_{O})
to be the output of the following fixed deterministic
irredundantization
procedure:

(i)

Initialize
A\leftarrow S_{O}
.

(ii)

Scan elements of
S_{O}
in the fixed canonical order; for each
s\in S_{O}
, if

s\in\text{Cn}(A\setminus\{s\})
, then set
A\leftarrow A\setminus\{s\}
.

(iii)

Output
A
and denote it by
\text{Atom}(S_{O})
.

Proposition 2.2

(Core correctness: equivalence and irredundancy)
.

Under Assumptions
2.3
and
2.4
, for every finite knowledge base
S_{O}
,
the set
A:=\text{Atom}(S_{O})
satisfies:

(i)

Closure equivalence:

\text{Cn}(A)=\text{Cn}(S_{O})
.

(ii)

Irredundancy:
for every
a\in A
,
a\notin\text{Cn}(A\setminus\{a\})
.

(iii)

Canonicality:

\text{Atom}(S_{O})
is uniquely determined by
S_{O}
and the fixed canonical order.

Each removal step deletes some
s
with
s\in\text{Cn}(A\setminus\{s\})
, hence
\text{Cn}(A)=\text{Cn}(A\setminus\{s\})
.
By induction over the finite scan, the final set
A
has
\text{Cn}(A)=\text{Cn}(S_{O})
.
Irredundancy holds because any
a\in A
was not removable at its scan time, and subsequent removals only shrink
the conditioning set, which cannot create a new derivation of
a
from an even smaller set without having existed
already (monotonicity of
Cn
). Canonicality follows from determinism.
∎

Definition 2.10

(Intrinsic vs. operational premise bases)
.

For a knowledge base
S_{O}
, define:

\displaystyle A:=\text{Atom}(S_{O})\quad\text{(core premises)},

\displaystyle J:=S_{O}\setminus A\quad\text{(stored shortcuts)}.

We call
A
the
intrinsic
premise base and
A\cup J=S_{O}
the
operational
premise base.

Predecessor structure for depth.

We model single-step inferential dependency by a predecessor operator
P_{O}
.

Axiom 2.5

(Finite and computable predecessors)
.

For every
s\in\mathbb{S}_{O}
, the set
P_{O}(s)
is finite and effectively computable from
\text{enc}_{O}(s)
.

Axiom 2.6

(Well-foundedness of predecessor unfolding)
.

For every
s\in\mathbb{S}_{O}
, the backward unfolding of
s
along
P_{O}
contains no infinite chain.

Proposition 2.3

(Finite, acyclic, and computable predecessor unfolding)
.

Under Axioms
2.5
and
2.6
,
for any
s\in\mathbb{S}_{O}
, the backward unfolding of
s
along
P_{O}
is a finite directed acyclic graph (DAG),
and is effectively traversable from
\text{enc}_{O}(s)
.

Finite branching follows from Axiom
2.5
.
If the unfolding were infinite, König’s lemma
[
27
]
would yield an infinite backward chain, contradicting
Axiom
2.6
. Computable traversal follows from computability of
P_{O}(\cdot)
.
∎

Assumption 2.5

(Alignment of dependency and inference (relative to a premise base))
.

Fix a finite available premise base
B\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}
.
For every state/formula
s\in\mathbb{S}_{O}\setminus B
, the predecessor set
P_{O}(s)
coincides with the set of
immediate premises in a fixed normal-form single-step inference concluding
s
in the chosen proof system for
\mathcal{L}

(soundness and completeness at one step). Elements of
B
are treated as available premises and need not be inferred.

Definition 2.11

(Base-relative derivation depth)
.

Let
B\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}
be a finite set of
available premises
.
Under Assumption
2.5
, the
derivation depth
of
s\in\mathbb{S}_{O}
from
B
is

\text{Dd}(s\mid B):=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }s\in B,\\[1.72218pt]
1+\displaystyle\max_{s^{\prime}\in P_{O}(s)}\text{Dd}(s^{\prime}\mid B),&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(2.2)

with the convention that the maximum over an empty set is
0
.

Remark 2.4

(Nullary inference steps)
.

The convention
\max\varnothing=0
allows
\text{Dd}(s\mid B)=1
when
s\notin B
and
P_{O}(s)=\varnothing
,
which corresponds to admitting nullary (axiom) inference steps in the fixed normal form.
If a given proof system has no such steps, one may equivalently restrict attention to instances where

P_{O}(s)\neq\varnothing
for all
s\notin B
.

Theorem 2.1

(Well-definedness and computability of derivation depth)
.

Let
B\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}
be a finite, effectively decidable premise base.
Under Assumptions
2.5
and Axioms
2.5
–
2.6
,
for every
s\in\mathbb{S}_{O}
,
\text{Dd}(s\mid B)
is a unique, finite, and computable non-negative integer.

By Proposition
2.3
, the predecessor unfolding is a finite computable DAG.
Thus the recursion (
2.2
) is well-founded. A bottom-up dynamic program computes
\text{Dd}(s\mid B)

using computability of
P_{O}(\cdot)
and decidability of membership in
B
.
∎

Definition 2.12

(Intrinsic and operational derivation depths)
.

Let
S_{O}
be a knowledge base with core
A:=\text{Atom}(S_{O})
.
For any query/formula
q
derivable from
A
(hence from
S_{O}
), define

n_{\mathrm{int}}(q):=\text{Dd}(q\mid A),\qquad n_{\mathrm{op}}(q):=\text{Dd}(q\mid S_{O}).

Remark 2.5

(Monotonicity and interpretation)
.

Since
A\subseteq S_{O}
, enlarging the premise base cannot increase derivation depth; hence

n_{\mathrm{op}}(q)\leq n_{\mathrm{int}}(q).

The intrinsic depth
n_{\mathrm{int}}
captures inferential content relative to an irredundant core,
whereas the operational depth
n_{\mathrm{op}}
captures actual online cost under stored shortcuts.
This separation is the basis of the storage–computation optimization in later sections.

Definition 2.13

(Semantic atomicity measure)
.

The
semantic atomicity
of an information instance
\mathcal{I}
with semantic space
S_{O}
is

\mathsf{A}(\mathcal{I})\;:=\;\lvert\text{Atom}(S_{O})\rvert.

Remark 2.6

(Connection to logical depth)
.

The depth functional
\text{Dd}(\cdot)
is related to Bennett’s logical depth
[
4
]
.
Later sections connect intrinsic/operational derivation depth to conditional logical depth under explicit simulation assumptions.

III
Derivation Depth as an Information Metric

This section develops the ideal (noise-free) theory: we show that, for generic information-rich queries,
base-relative derivation depth provides a quantitative information-theoretic proxy for conditional
algorithmic complexity. We also introduce a BCQ-oriented
compositional
(sum-rule)
depth functional
\text{Dd}_{\Sigma}(\cdot\mid B)
for conjunctive/combined queries, while keeping

\text{Dd}(\cdot\mid B)
as the paper’s primary depth notion.

A key point is that all depths are measured
relative to a finite available premise base

B\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}
: elements of
B
are treated as depth-
0
premises
(Definition
2.11
).

For readability, throughout this section we write
\mathcal{P}
for the fixed effective proof
system
\mathsf{PS}
in Assumption
2.1
.

Remark 3.1

(Logarithm convention)
.

Throughout Sections
III
–
IV
,
\log
denotes
\log_{2}

(bit-length scale), while
\ln
denotes the natural logarithm.

III-A

Derivation Entropy and the Information-Rich Regime

Assumption 3.1

(Finite premise bases for asymptotic analysis)
.

In Sections
III
–
IV
, we consider finite available
premise bases
B\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}
that are effectively listable.
We write
m:=|B|
and fix a canonical computable encoding
\langle B\rangle\in\{0,1\}^{*}

(e.g., a length-prefixed list of encodings in a fixed order).

Fix a premise base
B
satisfying Assumption
3.1
, and consider queries

q\in\text{Cn}(B)
derivable in
\mathcal{P}
over
\mathcal{L}
.

Definition 3.1

(Derivation entropy)
.

Let
B
be a premise base and
q\in\text{Cn}(B)
a derivable query.
The
derivation entropy
of
q
given
B
is

H_{\mathrm{derive}}(q\mid B):=\text{Dd}(q\mid B)\cdot\ln 2,

(3.1)

where
\text{Dd}(q\mid B)
is the base-relative derivation depth from Definition
2.11
.

Remark 3.2

(Terminology)
.

The quantity
H_{\mathrm{derive}}(q\mid B)
is an
entropy-like
rescaling of an online derivation-cost functional;
it is not a Shannon entropy unless a query distribution is specified (cf. Remark
3.4
).

Definition 3.2

(Conditional algorithmic entropy)
.

Fix a universal Turing machine
U
and the encoding convention
\langle\cdot\rangle
.
For a finite premise base
B
and a query
q
, define

H_{K}(q\mid B):=K\bigl(q\mid\langle B\rangle\bigr)\cdot\ln 2.

Remark 3.3

(Prefix-free complexity convention)
.

Throughout,
K(\cdot\mid\cdot)
denotes
prefix-free
(self-delimiting) conditional Kolmogorov complexity
with respect to the fixed universal machine
U
and encoding convention
\langle\cdot\rangle
.
This convention aligns with the self-delimiting encodings used later for derivation traces.

Remark 3.4

(Relation to Shannon entropy (in expectation))
.

Let
Q
be a random query drawn from a computable distribution
P
over a finite query set.
Standard results (for a prefix-free variant of
K
) imply

\mathbb{E}_{P}\!\left[K\!\left(Q\mid\langle B\rangle\right)\right]\leq H_{P}\!\left(Q\mid\langle B\rangle\right)+O(1),

where
H_{P}(\cdot\mid\cdot)
is Shannon conditional entropy.
Thus, while our development is individual-sequence (distribution-free), it is compatible with the
Shannon-theoretic viewpoint in expectation under computable query sources.

Assumption 3.2

(Computable query source)
.

Let
Q
be a random query supported on a finite set of encodings.
We assume the conditional distribution
P_{Q\mid\langle B\rangle}
is computable uniformly in
\langle B\rangle
,
i.e., there exists an algorithm that, given
\langle B\rangle
, outputs the probability mass function of
Q

(or samples from it) to arbitrary precision.

Proposition 3.1

(Expected Kolmogorov complexity matches Shannon entropy up to
O(1)
)
.

Under Assumption
3.2
, let
K(\cdot\mid\cdot)
be prefix-free conditional Kolmogorov
complexity (Remark
3.3
). Then

\mathbb{E}\!\left[K\!\left(Q\mid\langle B\rangle\right)\right]=H_{P}\!\left(Q\mid\langle B\rangle\right)+O(1),

where the
O(1)
term depends only on the fixed universal machine
U
, the encoding conventions, and the
fixed algorithm witnessing computability of
P_{Q\mid\langle B\rangle}
, but is independent of the particular
realization of
\langle B\rangle
and of the sample
Q
.
Standard references include, e.g.,
[
30
]
.

Since
P_{Q\mid\langle B\rangle}
is computable uniformly from
\langle B\rangle
and the support is finite,
there exists a prefix code
C_{\langle B\rangle}
(e.g., Shannon–Fano or arithmetic coding) computable from

\langle B\rangle
such that the code length
\ell(q)
satisfies

\ell(q)\leq-\log P(q\mid\langle B\rangle)+O(1)
.
Hence
K(q\mid\langle B\rangle)\leq\ell(q)+O(1)\leq-\log P(q\mid\langle B\rangle)+O(1)
.
Taking expectation gives

\mathbb{E}[K(Q\mid\langle B\rangle)]\leq H_{P}(Q\mid\langle B\rangle)+O(1)
.

For the reverse direction, prefix-free complexities induce (up to an additive constant) valid prefix code lengths,
so Kraft’s inequality implies that any such lengths have expected value at least the Shannon entropy, yielding

\mathbb{E}[K(Q\mid\langle B\rangle)]\geq H_{P}(Q\mid\langle B\rangle)-O(1)
.
∎

Remark 3.5

(Compatibility with the individual-sequence viewpoint)
.

Proposition
3.1
is used only to translate our individual-query statements into
distributional (expected) ones. All main coding theorems below remain distribution-free.

Definition 3.3

(Complexity factor)
.

For a premise base
B
with
m=|B|
and a query
q
, for any nonnegative integer complexity parameter

n
representing the
online derivation cost
(depth or step complexity) of
q
from
B
, define

L_{B}:=\log(m+n).

Definition 3.4

(Information-rich regime)
.

Let
B
be a premise base with
m:=|B|
.
We say that a family of instances
(q,B)
lies in the
information-rich regime
if

K\bigl(q\mid\langle B\rangle\bigr)=\omega(\log m),

with asymptotics taken along the family with
m\to\infty
.

Remark 3.6

(Meaning of the condition)
.

The term
\log m
is the indexing overhead: the number of bits required merely to name
one premise among
m
candidates. The information-rich regime excludes queries whose conditional
description is essentially a constant number of premise indices (pure lookups), and focuses on queries
whose specification carries substantially more information than naming.

III-B

Derivation Traces, BCQs, and Encoding Length

The main bridge to Kolmogorov complexity is constructed at the level of
derivation traces
.
For conjunctive/combined queries (BCQs), we additionally introduce a compositional (sum-rule) depth
functional
\text{Dd}_{\Sigma}(\cdot\mid B)
that directly models aggregation cost.

III-B
1
Derivation traces and shortest-trace length

Definition 3.5

(Derivation trace and length (base-parametric))
.

Fix a finite premise base
B
with
m:=|B|
and a proof system
\mathcal{P}
with a finite rule set
[
13
]
.
A (sequential)
derivation trace
of a query
q
from
B
is a pair

\pi=\bigl((r_{1},\vec{p}_{1}),\ldots,(r_{\ell},\vec{p}_{\ell});\ o\bigr),

where each
r_{i}
is a rule identifier, each
\vec{p}_{i}
is a vector of premise pointers (indices),
and
o
is an
output pointer
(an index into the final pool), such that the following deterministic
replay procedure outputs
q
:

(i)

Initialize the pool
P_{0}
as an ordered list consisting of the
m
premises
B
in the canonical
order induced by
\langle B\rangle
.

(ii)

For
i=1,\ldots,\ell
, interpret
\vec{p}_{i}
as indices into the current pool
P_{i-1}
, apply rule

r_{i}
of
\mathcal{P}
to the referenced premises (in the prescribed order), obtain a conclusion formula

f_{i}
, and append it to obtain
P_{i}:=P_{i-1}\,\|\,\langle f_{i}\rangle
.

(iii)

Output the formula stored at index
o
in
P_{\ell}
.

The trace is
valid
if each rule application is checkable in
\mathcal{P}
(hence decidable), and it

derives
q
if the replay output equals
q
. Its
length
is
|\pi|:=\ell
.

Definition 3.6

(Base premises referenced by a derivation trace)
.

Let
\pi=((r_{1},\vec{p}_{1}),\ldots,(r_{\ell},\vec{p}_{\ell});\ o)
be a derivation trace of
q
from
B

(Definition
3.5
).
Define
\text{Atoms}(\pi)\subseteq B
as the set of base premises referenced by
\pi
, namely those premises
whose indices in the initial pool
P_{0}
are referenced by some premise pointer in
\pi
, or by the
output pointer when
\ell=0
.

Definition 3.7

(Minimal derivation-trace length (base-parametric))
.

For a derivable query
q\in\text{Cn}(B)
, define the minimal derivation-trace length

\displaystyle N(q\mid B):=\min\bigl\{

\displaystyle|\pi|:\ \pi\text{ is a valid derivation trace}

\displaystyle\text{deriving }q\text{ from }B\bigr\}.

(We allow
|\pi|=0
, in which case the trace outputs
q
by selecting it from the initial pool
P_{0}
.)

III-B
2
BCQs and compositional (sum-rule) depth

Definition 3.8

(Boolean conjunctive query (BCQ))
.

A
Boolean conjunctive query (BCQ)
is a formula of the form

q\;=\;\bigwedge_{j=1}^{t}a_{j},

where each
a_{j}
is an atomic formula of
\mathcal{L}
(or a designated atomic query form) and
t\geq 1
.
We write
\mathrm{len}(q):=t
.

Definition 3.9

(Compositional (sum-rule) derivation depth for BCQs)
.

Fix a premise base
B
and the proof system
\mathcal{P}
.
For a BCQ
q
derivable from
B
, define its
compositional
(sum-rule) depth by

\text{Dd}_{\Sigma}(q\mid B)\;:=\;N(q\mid B).

Remark 3.7

(Role of
\text{Dd}_{\Sigma}
and why Section IV still uses
Dd
)
.

The base-relative depth
\text{Dd}(\cdot\mid B)
from Definition
2.11
is the paper’s
primary inferential depth notion (height of a dependency unfolding).
For BCQs, the online computation cost is often governed by
aggregation
across conjuncts; this motivates
the compositional depth
\text{Dd}_{\Sigma}(\cdot\mid B)
, which is step-accurate by construction.
In Section
IV
, we keep
Dd
as the default cost symbol, and provide a BCQ
instantiation by replacing
Dd
with
\text{Dd}_{\Sigma}
when the query workload is BCQ-dominated.

Lemma 3.1

(BCQ aggregation overhead is linear in the number of conjuncts)
.

Assume
\mathcal{P}
contains a conjunction-introduction rule allowing one to derive

\varphi\land\psi
from
\varphi
and
\psi
in
O(1)
trace steps.
Let
q=\bigwedge_{j=1}^{t}a_{j}
be a BCQ derivable from
B
. Then

\text{Dd}_{\Sigma}(q\mid B)\;\leq\;\sum_{j=1}^{t}\text{Dd}_{\Sigma}(a_{j}\mid B)\;+\;O(t),

where each
a_{j}
is treated as a one-conjunct BCQ.

Take shortest traces
\pi_{j}
deriving each
a_{j}
from
B
, concatenate them (reusing the same base pool),
and then conjoin the obtained atoms using
t-1
conjunction-introduction steps under a fixed parenthesization.
This yields a valid trace for
q
of length
\sum_{j}|\pi_{j}|+O(t)
, hence the bound.
∎

III-B
3
Derivation encoding length

Lemma 3.2

(Derivation encoding)
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a proof system with finite rule set
R
and maximum rule arity
k

(i.e., each rule has at most
k
premises), where
|R|
and
k
are constants independent of the
premise base size. For any derivation trace
\pi
of length
n:=|\pi|
from a premise base
B
with

m=|B|
, there exists an encoding satisfying

\displaystyle|\text{enc}(\pi)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle k\cdot n\cdot\log(m+n+1)\!+\!O(n)\!+\!O(\log(m+n+1)),

(3.2)

where the
O(n)
term is linear in
n
with a constant depending only on
|R|
and
k
.

By Assumption
2.1
, proof checking in
\mathcal{P}
is decidable.
Moreover, by Axiom
2.3
, semantic formulas/states admit finite encodings and all
predicates needed to check rule applicability are effectively evaluable.
Hence each derivation step admits a finite verifiable description.

Write
\pi=((r_{1},\vec{p}_{1}),\ldots,(r_{n},\vec{p}_{n}))
. We encode
\pi
by concatenating a self-delimiting header
together with per-step information.

Header.
Encode
m
and
n
using a self-delimiting integer code, requiring
O(\log m+\log n)
bits.

Step
i
.
At step
i
, premises are selected from the pool consisting of the
m
initial premises
together with the
i-1
previously derived formulas, hence pool size
<m+n
.
Encode:
(i) the rule identifier
r_{i}\in R
, costing
\lceil\log|R|\rceil=O(1)
bits; and
(ii) the premise pointers
\vec{p}_{i}
, at most
k
indices into a set of size
<m+n
, costing at most

k(\log(m+n)+1)
bits.

Output pointer.
Encode the final output index
o
as an integer in a range of size
<m+n+1
,
costing
O(\log(m+n+1))
bits.

Summing over
n
steps yields (
3.2
), see e.g. Li–Vitányi
[
30
]
for self-delimiting encodings.
∎

III-C

Richness and Incompressibility

Lower bounds rely on a combinatorial condition ensuring that there are many distinct queries at a given
online cost scale. For our coding arguments, the natural cost scale is shortest-trace length (steps);
BCQs are covered by
\text{Dd}_{\Sigma}=N
.

Definition 3.10

(Richness condition (trace-length form))
.

A proof system
\mathcal{P}
satisfies the
richness condition
with parameter
\delta_{0}>0
if there exists

m_{0}>0
such that for all
m\geq m_{0}
, all integers
n
with
\log m\leq n\leq\operatorname{poly}(m)
, and all premise bases

B
with
|B|=m
,

\bigl|\{\,\langle q\rangle:N(q\mid B)=n\,\}\bigr|\geq 2^{(1-\delta_{0})\,n\log(m+n)}.

(3.3)

The cardinality is taken over distinct encodings
\langle q\rangle
under the fixed encoding conventions.

Remark 3.8

(Scope of richness)
.

Definition
3.10
is stated uniformly over all premise bases
B
of size
m
for clarity.
For the main incompressibility arguments, it suffices that the lower bound (
3.3
) holds
on the
class of bases
under consideration (e.g., the atomic-fact bases in
Proposition
3.2
), rather than for every possible
B
.

Remark 3.9

(Compatibility with
Dd
and with BCQs)
.

Definition
3.10
is stated in terms of step complexity
N(\cdot\mid B)
, which is the quantity
directly controlled by Lemma
3.2
.
For general queries, Assumption
3.3
(introduced below) allows transferring results to

\text{Dd}(\cdot\mid B)
up to constant factors.
For BCQs,
\text{Dd}_{\Sigma}(\cdot\mid B)=N(\cdot\mid B)
by definition, so richness immediately supports the BCQ
(sum-rule) instantiation.

III-C
1
A BCQ richness witness in a Horn/Datalog fragment

Proposition 3.2

(Instance-wise richness for BCQs under compositional depth)
.

Assume
\mathcal{P}
contains binary conjunction introduction (as in Lemma
3.1
) and,
moreover, that in the chosen trace normal form, each single trace step can introduce
at most one

new occurrence of the connective
\land
in the derived formula.
Let the premise base
B
consist of
m
pairwise distinct atomic formulas
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\}
in
\mathcal{L}
.

Fix any integer
n\geq 1
and define the BCQ family

\mathcal{Q}_{n}^{\mathrm{BCQ}}:=\left\{\begin{aligned} q_{\mathbf{i}}=&a_{i_{0}}\land a_{i_{1}}\land\cdots\land a_{i_{n}}\;:\;\\
&\mathbf{i}=(i_{0},\ldots,i_{n})\in[m]^{n+1}\\
&\text{with all }i_{j}\text{ distinct}\end{aligned}\right\},

with a fixed left-associative parenthesization (so distinct index sequences yield distinct encodings).
Then:

(i)

Every
q\in\mathcal{Q}_{n}^{\mathrm{BCQ}}
satisfies

\text{Dd}_{\Sigma}(q\mid B)=N(q\mid B)=n.

(ii)

|\mathcal{Q}_{n}^{\mathrm{BCQ}}|=m\cdot(m-1)\cdots(m-n)
, hence

\log|\mathcal{Q}_{n}^{\mathrm{BCQ}}|=(n+1)\log m-O(n)
.

Consequently, if there exists a fixed constant
C\geq 1
such that
n\leq m^{C}
in the asymptotic regime,
then the richness inequality (
3.3
) holds (for these bases) with any choice of

\delta_{0}\;\geq\;1-\frac{1}{C+1},

for all sufficiently large
m
.

(i) For any
q_{\mathbf{i}}\in\mathcal{Q}_{n}^{\mathrm{BCQ}}
, each
a_{i_{j}}\in B
is available at depth
0
.
A left-associative construction uses exactly
n
binary conjunction-introduction steps, hence

N(q_{\mathbf{i}}\mid B)\leq n
.
On the other hand,
q_{\mathbf{i}}
contains exactly
n
occurrences of
\land
under the fixed
parenthesization. By the normal-form assumption that each trace step can introduce at most one new
\land
,
any derivation trace producing
q_{\mathbf{i}}
must have length at least
n
.
Therefore
N(q_{\mathbf{i}}\mid B)=n
, and since
\text{Dd}_{\Sigma}=N
for BCQs,
\text{Dd}_{\Sigma}(q_{\mathbf{i}}\mid B)=n
.

(ii) The counting formula for
|\mathcal{Q}_{n}^{\mathrm{BCQ}}|
is immediate.

For richness, when
n\leq m^{C}
, we have
\log(m+n)\leq(C+1)\log m
for all sufficiently large
m
, so

\displaystyle\log|\{q:N(q\mid B)=n\}|

\displaystyle\;\geq\;

\displaystyle\log|\mathcal{Q}_{n}^{\mathrm{BCQ}}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n+1)\log m-O(n)

\displaystyle\;\geq\;

\displaystyle(1-\delta_{0})n\log(m+n)

for any
\delta_{0}\geq 1-\frac{1}{C+1}
and all sufficiently large
m
, after absorbing the lower-order
O(n)
term.
∎

III-C
2
Incompressibility at the
n\log(m+n)
scale

Lemma 3.3

(Derivation incompressibility (step-length form))
.

Let
B

Upper bound (all queries):

(ii)

satisfies

(3.6)

(iii)

) with parameter

,

\delta

(3.7)

Equivalently, at least a

satisfy

Part (i).

Let
\pi
be a shortest derivation trace of

from

, so

.
Consider a program that contains
\text{enc}(\pi)

y

III-D

Main Theorem: Depth as an Information Metric

The coding argument is naturally stated for step complexity
N(q\mid B)
(shortest trace length).
To keep
\text{Dd}(\cdot\mid B)
as the paper’s primary notion, we relate
Dd
and
N
via a
serializability assumption.

Assumption 3.3

(Serializable depth witnesses)
.

For the class of premise bases
B
and queries
q
considered, whenever
q\in\text{Cn}(B)
and

\text{Dd}(q\mid B)<\infty
, there exists a derivation trace
\pi
of
q
from
B
such that

|\pi|=\Theta\!\bigl(\text{Dd}(q\mid B)\bigr).

Equivalently, there exist constants
c_{\min},c_{\max}>0
such that for all such
(q,B)
,

c_{\min}\,\text{Dd}(q\mid B)\leq N(q\mid B)\leq c_{\max}\,\text{Dd}(q\mid B).

Remark 3.10

(On the strength of Assumption
3.3
)
.

Assumption
3.3
is an
instance-class
hypothesis: in general, the unfolding height

\text{Dd}(q\mid B)
and the shortest sequential trace length
N(q\mid B)
need not be within constant factors.
When the assumption fails, all coding and incompressibility statements remain valid at the step scale
N(q\mid B)
,
and the BCQ instantiation via
\text{Dd}_{\Sigma}=N
(Corollary
3.1
) provides a step-accurate alternative.

Theorem 3.1

(Derivation depth as an information metric (base-parametric))
.

Let
B
be a finite premise base with
m=|B|
, and let
q\in\text{Cn}(B)
be derivable with

d:=\text{Dd}(q\mid B)\geq 1.

Define

L_{B}:=\log(m+d),\qquad L:=d\cdot L_{B}.

Assume
\log m\leq d\leq\operatorname{poly}(m)
, Assumption
3.1
, and
Assumption
3.3
.

(i)

Upper bound (all queries).

There exists a constant
c_{1}>0
(depending only on
\mathcal{P}
,
U
, the encoding conventions, and
the serializability constants) such that

K\bigl(q\mid\langle B\rangle\bigr)\leq c_{1}\cdot d\,\log(m+d)\;=\;c_{1}\cdot L.

(3.9)

Equivalently,

\text{Dd}(q\mid B)\ \geq\ \frac{K(q\mid\langle B\rangle)}{c_{1}\,\log(m+\text{Dd}(q\mid B))}.

(ii)

Generic lower bound (under richness).

Suppose
\mathcal{P}
satisfies the richness condition (Definition
3.10
) with parameter
\delta_{0}>0
.
Let
n:=N(q\mid B)
and assume
q
is generic/incompressible at its step-length scale so that

K(q\mid\langle B\rangle)\geq(1-\delta)\,n\log(m+n)
for some
\delta>\delta_{0}

(cf. Lemma
3.3
(iii)).
Then there exists a constant
c_{2}>0
such that

\text{Dd}(q\mid B)\ \leq\ \frac{K(q\mid\langle B\rangle)}{c_{2}\,(1-\delta)\,\log(m+\text{Dd}(q\mid B))}.

Consequently, for generic/incompressible queries (in the above sense),

\text{Dd}(q\mid B)=\Theta\!\left(\frac{K(q\mid\langle B\rangle)}{\log(m+\text{Dd}(q\mid B))}\right),

and equivalently

H_{\mathrm{derive}}(q\mid B)=\Theta\!\left(\frac{H_{K}(q\mid B)}{\log(m+\text{Dd}(q\mid B))}\right).

Equivalently, for generic/incompressible queries,

K\bigl(q\mid\langle B\rangle\bigr)=\Theta\bigl(\text{Dd}(q\mid B)\,\log(m+\text{Dd}(q\mid B))\bigr).

Let
d=\text{Dd}(q\mid B)
and
n=N(q\mid B)
.

(i) Upper bound.

By Assumption
3.3
,
n\leq c_{\max}d
.
Let
\pi
be a shortest trace, so
|\pi|=n
. By Lemma
3.2
,

|\text{enc}(\pi)|\leq k\,n\,\log(m+n)+O(n)=O\!\bigl(n\log(m+n)\bigr).

Hence
K(q\mid\langle B\rangle)\leq O(n\log(m+n))
.
Using
n\leq c_{\max}d
and
\log(m+n)=O(\log(m+d))
yields

K(q\mid\langle B\rangle)\leq c_{1}d\log(m+d)
.

(ii) Generic lower bound.

By Lemma
3.3
(iii), generic
q
at step-length
n
satisfy

K(q\mid\langle B\rangle)\geq(1-\delta)n\log(m+n)
.
By Assumption
3.3
,
n\geq c_{\min}d
, and again
\log(m+n)=\Theta(\log(m+d))
.
Rearranging gives the stated upper bound on
d
with some constant
c_{2}>0
.
∎

Corollary 3.1

(BCQ instantiation (sum-rule depth without serializability))
.

Let
q
be a BCQ and define its compositional (sum-rule) depth by

\text{Dd}_{\Sigma}(q\mid B)
(Definition
3.9
).
Then all statements of Theorem
3.1
remain valid if we replace

\text{Dd}(q\mid B)
by
\text{Dd}_{\Sigma}(q\mid B)
and drop Assumption
3.3
.

For BCQs,
\text{Dd}_{\Sigma}(q\mid B)=N(q\mid B)
by definition, so the coding and incompressibility arguments
apply directly at the step-length scale.
∎

Corollary 3.2

(Intrinsic vs. operational depth as two instantiations)
.

Let
S_{O}
be a knowledge base and let
A:=\text{Atom}(S_{O})
be its irredundant semantic core
(Definition

).
For any query
q
derivable from
A
(hence from

), define

n_{\mathrm{int}}(q):=\text{Dd}\bigl(q\mid A\bigr),\qquad n_{\mathrm{op}}(q):=\text{Dd}(q\mid S_{O}).

Then
n_{\mathrm{op}}(q)\leq n_{\mathrm{int}}(q)
(Remark
2.5
).

Moreover, whenever the hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
hold,
it applies both to
(q,B)=(q,A)
(intrinsic analysis) and to
(q,B)=(q,S_{O})
(operational analysis),
with base sizes
m_{\mathrm{int}}:=|A|
and
m_{\mathrm{op}}:=|S_{O}|
, respectively.

Remark 3.11

(Interpretation)
.

Theorem
3.1
connects descriptional complexity (
K(\cdot\mid\cdot)
) with inferential
depth (
\text{Dd}(\cdot\mid\cdot)
). The logarithmic factor
\log(m+\text{Dd})
represents the addressing cost of selecting
premises from a pool of size
\lesssim m+\text{Dd}
in derivation encodings.

III-E

Connection to Bennett’s Logical Depth

We now relate base-relative derivation depth (Definition
2.11
) to Bennett’s logical depth.
Since
\text{Dd}(\cdot\mid B)
measures the height of the predecessor unfolding, while Bennett depth is defined via
running time of near-minimal programs, we connect the two by (i) serializability and (ii) simulation assumptions.

Definition 3.11

(Bennett’s logical depth
[
4
,
30
]
)
.

For strings
x
and
y
and a significance level
t\geq 0
, the logical depth of
x
at significance
t

conditional on
y
is

\displaystyle\mathrm{depth}_{t}(x\mid y):=\min\bigl\{T(p):\;

\displaystyle|p|\leq K(x\mid y)+t,

\displaystyle U(p,y)=x\bigr\},

(3.10)

where
U
is a fixed universal Turing machine,
T(p)
is the running time of
U(p,y)
, and
K(\cdot\mid\cdot)

is (prefix-free) conditional Kolmogorov complexity under
U
.

Lemma 3.4

(Depth is bounded by trace length)
.

Fix a finite premise base
B
. Assume Assumption
2.5
(so that the predecessor operator
P_{O}

matches the fixed one-step normal form of the proof system
\mathcal{P}
used to define derivation traces).
Let
\pi
be any valid derivation trace (Definition
3.5
) that derives
q
from
B
.
If
|\pi|=\ell
, then

\text{Dd}(q\mid B)\leq\ell.

Let
\pi=((r_{1},\vec{p}_{1}),\ldots,(r_{\ell},\vec{p}_{\ell});\ o)
and let
f_{i}
be the conclusion produced at step
i

during replay, so that
q
is selected from the final pool
P_{\ell}
.

We claim by induction on
i
that every formula in the pool
P_{i}
has derivation depth at most
i
from
B
.
For
i=0
,
P_{0}
consists exactly of
B
, hence all elements have depth
0
.
For the inductive step,
P_{i}
is obtained from
P_{i-1}
by appending
f_{i}
.
By validity of the trace and Assumption
2.5
, the immediate premises used to derive
f_{i}

are exactly
P_{O}(f_{i})
, and each predecessor in
P_{O}(f_{i})
is an element of
P_{i-1}
.
By the induction hypothesis, each such predecessor has depth at most
i-1
, hence

\text{Dd}(f_{i}\mid B)=1+\max_{s^{\prime}\in P_{O}(f_{i})}\text{Dd}(s^{\prime}\mid B)\leq 1+(i-1)=i.

Thus every element of
P_{i}
has depth at most
i
. In particular, the output
q
selected from
P_{\ell}

satisfies
\text{Dd}(q\mid B)\leq\ell
.
∎

Theorem 3.2

(Derivation depth as conditional logical depth)
.

Let
S_{O}
be a knowledge base with intrinsic base
A:=\text{Atom}(S_{O})
, and assume
Assumption
3.3
. Let
U
be a universal Turing machine and
\langle\cdot\rangle
the fixed encoding.

Assume the following two properties hold for the class of instances under consideration:

(i)

Efficient proof simulation (EPS):

there exists a constant
a_{1}>0
such that, given
\langle A\rangle
and a derivation trace encoding
\text{enc}(\pi)

of length
|\pi|=\ell
,
U
can verify
\pi
and output
\langle q\rangle
in time at most
a_{1}\cdot\ell
.

(ii)

No proof shortcut (NPS):

there exists a constant
a_{2}>0
such that if some program
p
outputs
\langle q\rangle
in time
T(p)

given oracle access to
\langle A\rangle
, then there exists a derivation trace
\pi
of
q
from
A

with
|\pi|\leq a_{2}\cdot T(p)
.

Then for every derivable query
q\in\text{Cn}(A)
with
m=|A|
and
d=\text{Dd}(q\mid A)\geq 1
,
letting
L:=d\log(m+d)
, there exists a constant
c_{t}>0
such that

\mathrm{depth}_{c_{t}L}\bigl(\langle q\rangle\mid\langle A\rangle\bigr)=\Theta\!\bigl(\text{Dd}(q\mid A)\bigr).

Let
m=|A|
and
d=\text{Dd}(q\mid A)
.

Upper bound.

By Assumption
3.3
, there exists a derivation trace
\pi
deriving
q
from
A

with length
|\pi|=\ell\leq c_{\mathrm{ser}}\,d
for some constant
c_{\mathrm{ser}}>0
.
By Lemma
3.2
,

\displaystyle|\text{enc}(\pi)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle k\,\ell\,\log(m+\ell)+O(\ell)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O\bigl(d\log(m+d)\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O(L).

Let
p
be a program that contains
\text{enc}(\pi)
, uses
\langle A\rangle
as conditional input, runs the EPS
verification/simulation, and outputs
\langle q\rangle
.
Since
|p|=O(L)
and
K(\langle q\rangle\mid\langle A\rangle)\leq|p|+O(1)
, choosing
c_{t}
large enough ensures

|p|\leq K(\langle q\rangle\mid\langle A\rangle)+c_{t}L
.
By (EPS),
T(p)\leq a_{1}\ell\leq a_{1}c_{\mathrm{ser}}d
, hence

\mathrm{depth}_{c_{t}L}(\langle q\rangle\mid\langle A\rangle)=O(d)
.

Lower bound.

Let
T^{*}:=\mathrm{depth}_{c_{t}L}(\langle q\rangle\mid\langle A\rangle)
.
By Definition
3.11
, there exists a program
p^{*}
of admissible length at significance
c_{t}L

that outputs
\langle q\rangle
in time
T^{*}
given
\langle A\rangle
.
By (NPS), there exists a derivation trace
\pi^{*}
of
q
from
A
with
|\pi^{*}|\leq a_{2}T^{*}
.
By Lemma
3.4
(with base
A
),

d=\text{Dd}(q\mid A)\leq|\pi^{*}|\leq a_{2}T^{*}
,
hence
T^{*}\geq d/a_{2}
. Therefore

\mathrm{depth}_{c_{t}L}(\langle q\rangle\mid\langle A\rangle)=\Omega(d)
.

Combining the two bounds yields

\mathrm{depth}_{c_{t}L}(\langle q\rangle\mid\langle A\rangle)=\Theta(d)=\Theta(\text{Dd}(q\mid A))
.
∎

Remark 3.12

(On the time model in Theorem
3.2
)
.

Assumption (EPS) treats one trace step (including addressing/pointer handling) as
O(1)
time on the underlying machine.
On standard bit-cost Turing machines, replaying a trace typically incurs an additional
\Theta(\log(m+\ell))
addressing
overhead per step, leading to a natural
\Theta(\text{Dd}(q\mid A)\log(m+\text{Dd}(q\mid A)))
time scale.
This matches the “addressing cost” factor that already appears in the coding bounds of
Theorem
3.1
.

III-F

Tightness and Locality Refinements

We first show the asymptotic characterization is tight up to constant factors.
Then we discuss refinements exploiting semantic locality.

III-F
1
Tightness analysis

Theorem 3.3

(Tightness of the asymptotic characterization)
.

There exists a proof system
\mathcal{P}
and an infinite family of pairs
(q,B)
with

m=|B|
and
d=\text{Dd}(q\mid B)
satisfying
d\to\infty
,
d=o(m)
, and the
information-rich condition (Definition
3.4
), such that

\text{Dd}(q\mid B)=\Theta\!\left(\frac{K\bigl(q\mid\langle B\rangle\bigr)}{\log\bigl(|B|+\text{Dd}(q\mid B)\bigr)}\right).

We give an explicit family over a
fixed
finite relational signature in the standard descriptive-complexity setting.

Fixed signature and numerals.

Fix a finite relational signature

\Sigma_{0}:=\{A(\cdot),\,<,\,\mathrm{BIT}(\cdot,\cdot)\},

where
<
is a built-in linear order on the object sort and
\mathrm{BIT}(x,y)
is the standard bit predicate
available on ordered finite structures (cf. descriptive complexity references such as
[
21
,
16
]
).
For each integer
i\geq 0
, we use the standard shorthand
\underline{i}
(“the numeral
i
”) to denote
a
\Sigma_{0}
-definable element with binary value
i
. Formally, one may read an atom
A(\underline{i})
as the
closed formula

a_{i}\;:=\;\exists x\;\bigl(\mathrm{Num}_{i}(x)\land A(x)\bigr),

where
\mathrm{Num}_{i}(x)
is a
\Sigma_{0}
-formula that holds exactly on the element encoding
i
in the
built-in number representation;
\mathrm{Num}_{i}
can be chosen with length
O(\log i)
under the usual BIT-based
encodings.

Proof system and one-step predecessor normal form.

Let
\mathcal{P}
contain the binary conjunction-introduction rule: from
\varphi
and
\psi
, derive

\varphi\land\psi
.
We work in a one-step normal form for this rule, so under Assumption
2.5
we have

P_{O}(\varphi\land\psi)=\{\varphi,\psi\}.

Premise bases.

For each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, define the premise base over the
same
signature
\Sigma_{0}
by

B_{m}:=\{a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{m-1}\},\qquad\text{where }a_{i}:=A(\underline{i}).

Then
|B_{m}|=m
, and all instances live in the fixed logical language
\mathcal{L}=\mathrm{FO(LFP)}
over the
fixed signature
\Sigma_{0}
.

Parameters.

Let
n:=\lfloor\sqrt{m}\rfloor
for
m\geq 4
. Then
n\to\infty
and
n=o(m)
.

Queries (canonical encoding).

For each subset
I\subseteq[m]
with
|I|=n+1
, write
I=\{i_{0}<i_{1}<\cdots<i_{n}\}
and define the left-associated
conjunction

q_{I}:=(\cdots((a_{i_{0}}\land a_{i_{1}})\land a_{i_{2}})\cdots)\land a_{i_{n}}.

Under the fixed parenthesization and increasing order, distinct
I
’s yield distinct encodings
\langle q_{I}\rangle
.

Derivation depth.

Since each
a_{i_{j}}\in B_{m}
is depth
0
, there exists a derivation trace of
q_{I}
from
B_{m}
of length
n
,
hence by Lemma
3.4
we have
\text{Dd}(q_{I}\mid B_{m})\leq n
.
Conversely, because
P_{O}(\varphi\land\psi)=\{\varphi,\psi\}
, the predecessor unfolding of the left-associated
conjunction contains a chain of length
n
through the intermediate conjunctions, so
\text{Dd}(q_{I}\mid B_{m})\geq n
.
Therefore
\text{Dd}(q_{I}\mid B_{m})=n
.

Kolmogorov complexity bounds.

Given
\langle B_{m}\rangle
, specifying
q_{I}
amounts to specifying the subset
I
.
There are
\binom{m}{n+1}
choices, hence by the standard counting bound for prefix-free Kolmogorov complexity
(see, e.g.,
[
30
]
),

K\bigl(q_{I}\mid\langle B_{m}\rangle\bigr)\geq\log\binom{m}{n+1}-O(1).

For
n+1\leq m/2
(true for all sufficiently large
m
),
\binom{m}{n+1}\geq\left(\frac{m}{n+1}\right)^{n+1}
,
so
K(q_{I}\mid\langle B_{m}\rangle)=\Omega(n\log m)
.

On the other hand, Lemma
3.2
(with
k=2
) yields an encoding of a length-
n
conjunction-building trace of

q_{I}
from
B_{m}
of size
\leq 2n\log(m+n)+O(n)
, hence

K(q_{I}\mid\langle B_{m}\rangle)=O(n\log(m+n))
.
Since
n=o(m)
,
\log(m+n)=\Theta(\log m)
, so

K(q_{I}\mid\langle B_{m}\rangle)=\Theta(n\log m).

Finally,
\text{Dd}(q_{I}\mid B_{m})=n
and
\log(m+\text{Dd}(q_{I}\mid B_{m}))=\Theta(\log m)
, giving

\text{Dd}(q_{I}\mid B_{m})=\Theta\!\left(\frac{K(q_{I}\mid\langle B_{m}\rangle)}{\log(m+\text{Dd}(q_{I}\mid B_{m}))}\right).

Moreover
K(q_{I}\mid\langle B_{m}\rangle)=\Theta(n\log m)=\omega(\log m)
, so the family lies in the information-rich regime.
∎

III-F
2
Semantic locality and effective complexity

The bounds in Theorem
3.1
treat all base premises uniformly via

L_{B}=\log(m+n)
, where
m=|B|
.
For semantic knowledge bases
S_{O}
, the most relevant instantiation is the intrinsic base

A:=\text{Atom}(S_{O})
. For semantically local queries that effectively depend on only a small subset of
A
,
one can tighten the logarithmic factor by replacing
|A|
with an
effective

Definition 3.12

(Essential core premises and effective parameters)
.

Fix a knowledge base
S_{O}
with intrinsic base
A:=\text{Atom}(S_{O})
.
For a derivable query
q\in\text{Cn}(A)
, let
\Pi^{*}(q)
denote the set of
shortest

derivation traces of
q
from
A
, i.e.,

\Pi^{*}(q):=\{\pi:\ \pi\text{ derives }q\text{ from }A,\ |\pi|=N(q\mid A)\},

where
N(q\mid A)
is defined in Definition
3.7
. Define:

(i)

Essential atom set:

\operatorname{Ess}(q\mid S_{O}):=\bigcap_{\pi\in\Pi^{*}(q)}\text{Atoms}(\pi)\ \subseteq\ A.

(ii)

Extended essential atom set:

\operatorname{Ess}^{+}(q\mid S_{O}):=\bigcup_{\pi\in\Pi^{*}(q)}\text{Atoms}(\pi)\ \subseteq\ A.

(iii)

Effective knowledge-base size:

m_{\mathrm{eff}}(q):=|\operatorname{Ess}^{+}(q\mid S_{O})|
.

(iv)

Effective complexity factor:

L_{\mathrm{eff}}(q):=\log\bigl(m_{\mathrm{eff}}(q)+\text{Dd}(q\mid A)\bigr).

(v)

Semantic locality coefficient:

\lambda(q):=m_{\mathrm{eff}}(q)/|A|\in(0,1]
.

Remark 3.13

(Well-definedness and relation to
Dd
)
.

Since
q
is derivable from
A
,
\Pi^{*}(q)\neq\varnothing
.
Moreover, Lemma
3.4
gives
\text{Dd}(q\mid A)\leq N(q\mid A)
, while
Assumption
3.3
implies
N(q\mid A)=O(\text{Dd}(q\mid A))
in the regimes considered.
Hence
N(q\mid A)=\Theta(\text{Dd}(q\mid A))
, so defining locality via shortest traces is compatible with using

\text{Dd}(\cdot\mid A)
as the main depth parameter.

Proposition 3.3

(Effective derivation-depth characterization under locality)
.

Let
S_{O}
be a knowledge base with
A:=\text{Atom}(S_{O})
.
Let
q\in\text{Cn}(A)
be a derivable query with
n:=\text{Dd}(q\mid A)\geq 1
and

m_{\mathrm{eff}}:=m_{\mathrm{eff}}(q)
(Definition
3.12
).
Assume
\log m_{\mathrm{eff}}\leq n\leq\operatorname{poly}(m_{\mathrm{eff}})
, and Assumption
3.3
.
Suppose the proof system
\mathcal{P}
satisfies the richness condition (Definition
3.10
)
relative to premise bases of size
m_{\mathrm{eff}}
.
If
q
is generic/incompressible with respect to
\langle\operatorname{Ess}^{+}(q\mid S_{O})\rangle
(at the corresponding
depth scale), then

\text{Dd}(q\mid A)=\Theta\!\left(\frac{K\bigl(q\mid\langle\operatorname{Ess}^{+}(q\mid S_{O})\rangle\bigr)}{L_{\mathrm{eff}}(q)}\right).

(3.11)

Let
B:=\operatorname{Ess}^{+}(q\mid S_{O})
. By definition of
\operatorname{Ess}^{+}
, there exists a shortest trace
\pi^{*}
of
q
from
A

that references only premises in
B
, hence
q\in\text{Cn}(B)
.
Apply Theorem
3.1
with premise base
B
, whose size is
m_{\mathrm{eff}}
.
∎

Corollary 3.3

(Improvement from locality)
.

For queries with
\lambda(q)<1
, the effective complexity factor satisfies

L_{\mathrm{eff}}(q)<\log(|A|+\text{Dd}(q\mid A))
, yielding a tighter characterization than
Theorem
3.1
instantiated with
B=A
.
Quantitatively, when
n\ll m_{\mathrm{eff}}\ll|A|
,

\frac{\log(|A|+n)}{L_{\mathrm{eff}}(q)}\approx\frac{\log|A|}{\log m_{\mathrm{eff}}}=\frac{\log|A|}{\log|A|+\log\lambda(q)}.

(3.12)

When
n\ll m_{\mathrm{eff}}\ll|A|
, we have
\log(|A|+n)\approx\log|A|
and

L_{\mathrm{eff}}(q)=\log(m_{\mathrm{eff}}+n)\approx\log m_{\mathrm{eff}}
.
Since
m_{\mathrm{eff}}=\lambda(q)\,|A|
, the displayed approximation follows.
∎

Example 3.1

(Locality improvement)
.

Consider a knowledge base with
|A|=10^{6}
atomic core facts and a query
q
depending on only

m_{\mathrm{eff}}=10^{3}
core facts, with intrinsic derivation depth
n=100
.
Then
\log(|A|+n)\approx\log(10^{6})\approx 19.9
bits, while

L_{\mathrm{eff}}=\log(10^{3}+100)\approx 10.1
bits, giving an improvement factor

\log(|A|+n)/L_{\mathrm{eff}}\approx 1.97
.

Remark 3.14

(Computational considerations)
.

Computing
\operatorname{Ess}^{+}(q\mid S_{O})
exactly may be intractable if the set of shortest traces is large.
However, any single shortest trace
\pi^{*}
yields a certified lower bound

|\text{Atoms}(\pi^{*})|\leq m_{\mathrm{eff}}(q)
.
In practice one may work with an efficiently computed upper bound
\hat{m}\geq m_{\mathrm{eff}}(q)
,
e.g., by taking unions of premise sets from multiple near-shortest traces found by heuristic search.

IV
Storage–Computation Tradeoff and Optimization

Section
III
links online inference cost to conditional algorithmic information:
for generic information-rich queries, the step cost (and, under Assumption
3.3
, also the depth cost
Dd
)
satisfies

\text{Dd}(q\mid B)\;=\;\Theta\!\left(\frac{K(q\mid\langle B\rangle)}{\log(|B|+\text{Dd}(q\mid B))}\right)

up to constant factors. In this section we turn this correspondence into operational design rules:
when to derive on demand, when to cache, and how to allocate a finite storage budget across cache items.

Two baselines: intrinsic vs. operational.

Let
S_{O}
be a finite knowledge base and let
A:=\text{Atom}(S_{O})
be its irredundant semantic core
(Definition
2.9
). Write
S_{O}=A\cup J
, where
J:=S_{O}\setminus A
are stored shortcuts.
For any query
q\in\text{Cn}(A)=\text{Cn}(S_{O})
, define

n_{\mathrm{int}}(q):=\text{Dd}(q\mid A),\qquad n_{\mathrm{op}}(q):=\text{Dd}(q\mid S_{O}),

with
n_{\mathrm{op}}(q)\leq n_{\mathrm{int}}(q)
(Remark
2.5
).

IV-A

Frequency-Weighted Tradeoff Analysis

Caching enlarges the available premise base, thereby turning expensive derivations into depth-
0
premises.

IV-A
1
Caching semantics as premise-base augmentation

Definition 4.1

(Augmented-base derivation depth (baseline-parametric))
.

Fix a baseline available premise base
B_{0}\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}
(finite and decidable).
For any finite cache set
S\subseteq\text{Cn}(B_{0})
, define the augmented available premise base
B_{0,S}:=B_{0}\cup S
.
For any query
q\in\text{Cn}(B_{0,S})
, define

\text{Dd}(q\mid B_{0}\cup S)\;:=\;\text{Dd}(q\mid B_{0,S}).

Remark 4.1

(Monotonicity under caching)
.

If
S_{1}\subseteq S_{2}
, then
\text{Dd}(q\mid B_{0}\cup S_{2})\leq\text{Dd}(q\mid B_{0}\cup S_{1})
.

IV-A
2
Cost model and strategies

Definition 4.2

(Cost model)
.

Consider a system with two resources:

(i)

Storage cost
(bits): storing
b
bits incurs cost
\alpha\cdot b
for some
\alpha>0
.

(ii)

Computation cost
(derivation steps): executing
t
steps incurs cost
\beta\cdot t
for some
\beta>0
.

Let
\rho:=\alpha/\beta
be the relative price of storage vs. computation. By choosing units we normalize
\beta=1
.
We report online computation in the depth notation
Dd
, justified by Assumption
3.3
.
For BCQs, one may use
\text{Dd}_{\Sigma}
as a step-accurate replacement.

Definition 4.3

(Query distribution and access frequency)
.

Let
Q
be a finite set of queries and
P_{Q}
a probability distribution over
Q
.
For a horizon of
N
total queries, define the expected access count (frequency)

f_{q}:=N\cdot P_{Q}(q).

Definition 4.4

(On-demand derivation (baseline
B_{0}
))
.

Fix a baseline premise base
B_{0}
.
The on-demand strategy stores no additional query-specific information and answers
q
by deriving it from
B_{0}
.
Its cost per access is

\operatorname{Cost}_{\mathrm{derive}}(q;B_{0}):=\text{Dd}(q\mid B_{0}).

Definition 4.5

(Caching a query as a premise (baseline
B_{0}
))
.

Fix a baseline premise base
B_{0}
.
The caching strategy stores a persistent description that makes
q
directly available as an additional premise,
i.e., it adds
q
to the cache set
S
so that
q\in B_{0}\cup S
and thus
\text{Dd}(q\mid B_{0}\cup S)=0
.

Let
\ell_{q}(B_{0})
denote the number of stored bits required to cache
q
given that
B_{0}
is always available.
Information-theoretically,
\ell_{q}(B_{0})
is lower bounded (up to
O(1)
) by
K(q\mid\langle B_{0}\rangle)
and can be
matched within constant slack under standard coding conventions. We use an information-theoretic benchmark: there exists a prefix-free code length

L(q\mid\langle B_{0}\rangle)
such that

K\bigl(q\mid\langle B_{0}\rangle\bigr)\;\leq\;L\bigl(q\mid\langle B_{0}\rangle\bigr)\;\leq\;K\bigl(q\mid\langle B_{0}\rangle\bigr)+O(1),

where the
O(1)
term depends only on the fixed universal machine and encoding conventions.
We take

\ell_{q}(B_{0}):=L\bigl(q\mid\langle B_{0}\rangle\bigr).

(4.1)

Definition 4.6

(Amortized cost per access (baseline
B_{0}
))
.

Fix a baseline premise base
B_{0}
and a query
q
with frequency
f_{q}>0
.

(i)

Caching:
caching
q
requires storing
\ell_{q}(B_{0})
bits and (at most) one-time population cost.
The amortized cost per access is modeled as

\operatorname{Cost}_{\mathrm{cache}}(q;B_{0})\!:=\!\frac{\rho\cdot\ell_{q}(B_{0})}{f_{q}}+\frac{\text{Dd}(q\!\mid\!B_{0})}{f_{q}}+c_{\mathrm{hit}},

(4.2)

where
c_{\mathrm{hit}}=O(1)
accounts for the (model-dependent) per-access cache lookup overhead.

(ii)

On-demand:
the amortized cost equals

\operatorname{Cost}_{\mathrm{derive}}(q;B_{0}):=\text{Dd}(q\mid B_{0}).

(4.3)

IV-A
3
Main tradeoff theorem and critical frequency

Theorem 4.1

(Frequency-weighted storage–computation tradeoff (baseline-parametric))
.

Fix a baseline premise base
B_{0}
with
m:=|B_{0}|
, and let
q\in\text{Cn}(B_{0})
be derivable with

d:=\text{Dd}(q\mid B_{0})\geq 1
.
Let
K_{q}:=K(q\mid\langle B_{0}\rangle)
.
Assume
\log m\leq d\leq\operatorname{poly}(m)
and Assumption
3.3
.
Assume moreover that
(q,B_{0})
lies in the information-rich regime (Definition
3.4
)
and that
q
is generic/incompressible at its cost scale … so that Theorem
3.1

applies to
(q,B_{0})
. Let
f_{q}\geq 1
be the access frequency.

Define the optimal amortized cost

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cost}^{*}(q;B_{0})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\min\{\operatorname{Cost}_{\mathrm{cache}}(q;B_{0}),\ \operatorname{Cost}_{\mathrm{derive}}(q;B_{0})\}.

Then:

(i)

Asymptotic characterization:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cost}^{*}(q;B_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\!\left\{\frac{\rho\!\cdot\!K_{q}}{f_{q}}\!+\!O\!\left(\!\frac{d}{f_{q}}\!\right)\!+\!O(1),\Theta\!\left(\!\frac{K_{q}}{\log(m\!+\!d)}\!\right)\!\right\},

(4.4)

(ii)

Critical frequency:
the two strategies have equal asymptotic cost when

f_{q}=f_{c}\quad\text{with}\quad f_{c}=\Theta\!\bigl(\rho\cdot\log(m+d)\bigr).

(4.5)

(iii)

Optimal strategy:

•

If
f_{q}\gg f_{c}
, caching is optimal and
\operatorname{Cost}^{*}(q;B_{0})=O(1)
.

•

If
f_{q}\ll f_{c}
, on-demand derivation is optimal and
\operatorname{Cost}^{*}(q;B_{0})=\text{Dd}(q\mid B_{0})
.

•

If
f_{q}=\Theta(f_{c})
, both strategies have comparable amortized cost.

By Definition
4.5
and (
4.1
), we have

\ell_{q}(B_{0})=K_{q}+O(1)
.
Therefore, by Definition
4.6
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cost}_{\mathrm{cache}}(q;B_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\rho\,(K_{q}+O(1))}{f_{q}}+\frac{d}{f_{q}}+O(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\rho K_{q}}{f_{q}}+O\!\left(\frac{d}{f_{q}}\right)+O(1).

By Theorem
3.1
applied to
(q,B_{0})
in the generic regime,

\operatorname{Cost}_{\mathrm{derive}}(q;B_{0})=d=\Theta\!\left(\frac{K_{q}}{\log(m+d)}\right).

Taking the minimum yields (
(i)
).

For the break-even scale, compare the leading storage-amortization term
\rho K_{q}/f_{q}
with the on-demand term

\Theta(K_{q}/\log(m+d))
, which gives
f_{q}=\Theta(\rho\log(m+d))
.
At this scale, the additional population term satisfies

\frac{d}{f_{q}}=\Theta\!\left(\frac{K_{q}/\log(m+d)}{\rho\log(m+d)}\right)=\Theta\!\left(\frac{K_{q}}{\rho\,\log^{2}(m+d)}\right),

which is lower order than the two compared leading terms. Hence the critical frequency remains

f_{c}=\Theta(\rho\log(m+d))
.
∎

IV-A
4
BCQ instantiation: constant-window
f_{c}
and entropy form

Corollary 4.1

(Constant-window break-even frequency for generic BCQs)
.

Fix a baseline premise base
B_{0}
with
m:=|B_{0}|
and let
q
be a BCQ.
Measure online computation by compositional depth
d_{\Sigma}:=\text{Dd}_{\Sigma}(q\mid B_{0})
.
Let
L_{B}:=\log(m+d_{\Sigma})
and
K_{q}:=K(q\mid\langle B_{0}\rangle)
.

Assume the generic/incompressible condition at the cost scale holds so that the two-sided bounds induced by
Theorem
3.1
apply with

replaced by
\text{Dd}_{\Sigma}

(cf. Corollary
3.1
), i.e.,

\frac{K_{q}}{c_{1}L_{B}}\;\leq\;d_{\Sigma}\;\leq\;\frac{K_{q}}{(1-\delta)L_{B}}

for some
\delta>\delta_{0}
.
Then the caching-vs-derivation comparison admits the following sufficient conditions:

(i)

(
Caching certainly wins
) If
f_{q}\geq\rho\,c_{1}\,L_{B}
, then

\operatorname{Cost}_{\mathrm{cache}}(q;B_{0})\leq\operatorname{Cost}_{\mathrm{derive}}(q;B_{0})+O(1)
when

is replaced by
\text{Dd}_{\Sigma}
.

(ii)

(
On-demand certainly wins
) If
f_{q}\leq\rho\,(1-\delta)\,L_{B}
, then

\operatorname{Cost}_{\mathrm{derive}}(q;B_{0})\leq\operatorname{Cost}_{\mathrm{cache}}(q;B_{0})+O(1)
when
Dd
is replaced by
\text{Dd}_{\Sigma}
.

In particular, the BCQ critical frequency satisfies

f_{c}=\Theta\!\bigl(\rho\log(m+d_{\Sigma})\bigr).

Corollary 4.2

(Entropy-form expected on-demand cost for BCQs)
.

Fix
B_{0}
and consider a random BCQ
Q
drawn from a computable conditional source
(Assumption
3.2
). Measure computation by
\text{Dd}_{\Sigma}(Q\mid B_{0})
.
Assume the instance-wise generic condition holds with high probability over
Q
(e.g., by
Lemma
3.3
(iii) under a richness guarantee).

Then, up to an additive constant,

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\text{Dd}_{\Sigma}(Q\mid B_{0})\right]=\Theta\!\left(\frac{\mathbb{E}\!\left[K(Q\mid\langle B_{0}\rangle)\right]}{\log\!\bigl(m+\mathbb{E}[\text{Dd}_{\Sigma}(Q\mid B_{0})]\bigr)}\right),

and by Proposition
3.1
,

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\text{Dd}_{\Sigma}(Q\mid B_{0})\right]=\Theta\!\left(\frac{H_{P}(Q\mid\langle B_{0}\rangle)}{\log\!\bigl(m+\mathbb{E}[\text{Dd}_{\Sigma}(Q\mid B_{0})]\bigr)}\right).

Remark 4.2

(Interpretation: bits-per-step and its role in
f_{c}
)
.

For generic BCQs, Theorem
3.1
with

implies that each unit of online computation
(one trace step) corresponds to
\Theta(\log(m+\text{Dd}_{\Sigma}))
bits of conditional algorithmic information.
This explains why the break-even access frequency depends essentially only on
\rho\log(m+\cdot)
.

IV-B

System-Wide Allocation and Submodular Optimization

We allocate a finite storage budget across many potential cache items to reduce operational online depth.
By default in this subsection we take the operational baseline
B_{0}:=S_{O}
.

Remark 4.3

(Notational warning)
.

In this subsection,
K
denotes the number of queries in the finite query set
Q=\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{K}\}
.
This is distinct from Kolmogorov complexity
K(\cdot\mid\cdot)
.

Remark 4.4

(Notational warning: budget vs. premise bases)
.

Earlier sections use
B
to denote an available premise base in
\text{Dd}(\cdot\mid B)
.
In this subsection we reserve
\mathcal{B}>0
for the storage
budget
(in bits) to avoid overloading.

IV-B
1
Problem formulation

Consider a knowledge system with:

•

a knowledge base
S_{O}
with intrinsic core
A:=\text{Atom}(S_{O})
;

•

a finite query set
Q=\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{K}\}\subseteq\text{Cn}(A)
with distribution
P_{Q}
, where
p_{i}:=P_{Q}(q_{i})>0
;

•

a candidate cache ground set
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\text{Cn}(A)\setminus S_{O}
;

•

a storage cost function
\sigma:\mathcal{U}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
(bits);

•

a storage budget
\mathcal{B}>0
(bits).

For any allocation
S\subseteq\mathcal{U}
, define
\sigma(S):=\sum_{u\in S}\sigma(u)
.

For each query
q_{i}
and allocation
S
, define the operational depth under caching

n_{i}(S):=\text{Dd}(q_{i}\mid S_{O}\cup S).

Definition 4.7

(Expected operational depth and reduction)
.

For
S\subseteq\mathcal{U}
, define the expected online derivation cost

\bar{n}(S):=\mathbb{E}_{q\sim P_{Q}}\!\bigl[\text{Dd}(q\mid S_{O}\cup S)\bigr]=\sum_{i=1}^{K}p_{i}\,n_{i}(S),

and the expected cost reduction

\Delta(S):=\bar{n}(\varnothing)-\bar{n}(S)=\sum_{i=1}^{K}p_{i}\,[n_{i}(\varnothing)-n_{i}(S)]\geq 0.

The budgeted allocation problem is

\max_{S\subseteq\mathcal{U}}\ \Delta(S)\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\sigma(S)\leq\mathcal{B}.

Remark 4.5

(Choosing storage costs
\sigma(\cdot)
)
.

A canonical choice consistent with Section
IV-A
is

\sigma(u)=\ell_{u}(S_{O})
, where
\ell_{u}(S_{O})
is the (prefix-free) code length used to store
u
given the always-available base

\langle S_{O}\rangle
(cf. Definition
4.5
).
In practice,
\sigma(u)
can be instantiated by a fixed compression pipeline that upper bounds this benchmark length.

IV-B
2
Submodularity of depth reduction

Definition 4.8

(Marginal reduction)
.

For
S\subseteq\mathcal{U}
and
u\in\mathcal{U}\setminus S
, define the marginal gain

\Delta_{u}(S):=\Delta(S\cup\{u\})-\Delta(S)=\bar{n}(S)-\bar{n}(S\cup\{u\}).

Theorem 4.2

(Submodularity of expected depth reduction)
.

Assume that for each query
q_{i}
and each
u\in\mathcal{U}
, the depth reduction exhibits diminishing returns:
for all
A\subseteq B\subseteq\mathcal{U}
and
u\in\mathcal{U}\setminus B
,

n_{i}(A)-n_{i}(A\cup\{u\})\geq n_{i}(B)-n_{i}(B\cup\{u\}).

Then
\Delta
is normalized, monotone, and submodular.

Normalization and monotonicity are immediate.
For submodularity, for
A\subseteq B
and
u\notin B
,

\displaystyle\Delta_{u}(A)-\Delta_{u}(B)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{K}p_{i}\Bigl(\![n_{i}(A)\!-\!n_{i}(\!A\!\cup\!\{u\}\!)]\!-\![n_{i}(B)\!-\!n_{i}(B\!\cup\!\{u\})]\!\Bigr)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 0.

∎

Remark 4.6

(On the diminishing-returns hypothesis)
.

The condition in Theorem
4.2
is an instance/workload assumption.
It is not implied by monotonicity of
\text{Dd}(\cdot\mid\cdot)
alone, but can be validated empirically
(e.g., by sampling marginals

) and is often a reasonable approximation in cache/view selection workloads.

IV-C

Semantic Clustering for Practical Deployment

The ground set
\mathcal{U}
can be extremely large, and evaluating marginal gains
\Delta_{u}(S)
can be expensive.
This subsection introduces a semantic clustering layer to:
(i) reduce the effective candidate set,
(ii) expose shared derivation structure across queries, and
(iii) provide scalable heuristics for allocation.
Throughout, clustering is used only for
candidate generation
; the final selection is still performed
against the operational objective
\Delta(\cdot)
via Algorithm
1
.

Intrinsic structure, operational objective.

We cluster queries using intrinsic dependence on core premises (via
\operatorname{Ess}^{+}(q\mid S_{O})
from
Definition
3.12
, which is defined using shortest traces from
A=\text{Atom}(S_{O})
).
However, the optimization objective
\Delta(S)
is operational: it reduces depths

\text{Dd}(q\mid S_{O}\cup S)
. This matches deployment: clustering is a heuristic to shrink
\mathcal{U}
,
while the depth oracle remains operational.

IV-C
1
Semantic distance and cohesive clustering

Definition 4.9

(Semantic distance)
.

For queries
q_{i},q_{j}\in\text{Cn}(\text{Atom}(S_{O}))
, let

A_{i}:=\operatorname{Ess}^{+}(q_{i}\mid S_{O})
and
A_{j}:=\operatorname{Ess}^{+}(q_{j}\mid S_{O})
.
Define the semantic distance (Jaccard distance) as

d_{\mathrm{sem}}(q_{i},q_{j}\mid S_{O}):=\begin{cases}0,\!&\text{if }A_{i}\!=\!A_{j}\!=\!\varnothing,\\[1.29167pt]
1,\!&\text{if exactly one}\\
&\text{of }A_{i},A_{j}\text{ is }\\
&\text{empty},\\[1.29167pt]
1-\dfrac{|A_{i}\cap A_{j}|}{|A_{i}\cup A_{j}|},\!&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(4.6)

Remark 4.7

(Approximation of
\operatorname{Ess}^{+}
in deployment)
.

Computing
\operatorname{Ess}^{+}(q\mid S_{O})
exactly may be hard if shortest derivations are not unique or hard to find.
In deployment one may replace it by
\widehat{\operatorname{Ess}}^{+}(q\mid S_{O})
obtained from one or several certified (near-)short traces.
All definitions below remain meaningful under this replacement by interpreting
A_{i}
and
A_{j}
as such approximations.

Lemma 4.1

(Metric properties)
.

The function
d_{\mathrm{sem}}(\cdot,\cdot\mid S_{O})
is a pseudometric on
\text{Cn}(\text{Atom}(S_{O}))
.

Jaccard distance is a metric on finite sets (with the empty-set conventions above).
Composing a metric with a possibly non-injective map
q\mapsto\operatorname{Ess}^{+}(q\mid S_{O})
yields a pseudometric.
∎

Definition 4.10

(
\delta_{\mathrm{clust}}
-cohesive clustering)
.

Let
Q\subseteq\text{Cn}(\text{Atom}(S_{O}))
be finite.
A
\delta_{\mathrm{clust}}
-cohesive clustering is a partition
\mathcal{C}=\{Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{r}\}
such that
for every cluster
Q_{k}
and all
q_{i},q_{j}\in Q_{k}
,

d_{\mathrm{sem}}(q_{i},q_{j}\mid S_{O})\leq\delta_{\mathrm{clust}}.

Remark 4.8

(Constructing cohesive clusters is heuristic)
.

Definition
4.10
specifies a target property; computing such a partition exactly is not required
for our guarantees (which are stated relative to the reduced ground set).
In practice, one can form approximate cohesive clusters using similarity search pipelines such as MinHash/LSH
to bucket queries by approximate Jaccard similarity
[
7
,
22
]
, followed by
lightweight post-processing (e.g., splitting large buckets).

2
Core sets: what is guaranteed, and what is not

Definition 4.11

(Cluster core and extended atom sets)
.

\displaystyle:=\bigcap_{q\in Q_{k}}\operatorname{Ess}^{+}(q\mid S_{O}),

(4.9)

(Cluster centrality)

For a cluster

Lemma 4.2

(Two-query core coverage)
.

If

V
Extensions to Noisy Information

Sections
II
–
IV
develop an ideal (noise-free) theory in which all
computational costs are measured by
base-relative
derivation depth
\text{Dd}(\cdot\mid B)
with respect
to a chosen finite available premise base
B
(Definition
2.11
).
This section extends the framework to systems operating with
noisy information
, where the

available premise base itself
is perturbed by loss (unavailable premises) and/or pollution
(spurious added premises).
Throughout, we maintain the standing requirement that any noisy base
\tilde{B}
used to evaluate
\text{Dd}(\cdot\mid\tilde{B})

is
finite
and has
decidable membership
, so that the computability guarantee of
Theorem
2.1
continues to apply.

A key design requirement is
contextual consistency
with Section
IV
:
in particular, caching always acts by
enlarging
the available premise base, while noise can either
shrink it (loss) or enlarge it (pollution). In deployment, both effects may coexist; operationally we will
evaluate costs under augmented noisy bases of the form
\tilde{B}_{0}\cup S
.

All depth, entropy, and optimization statements below are therefore formulated in a
baseline-parametric
manner.

V-A

Setup: premise perturbations and base-relative depth

Baseline premise bases.

Recall the two natural baselines from Section
IV
:
the intrinsic baseline
B_{0}=A:=\text{Atom}(S_{O})
and the operational baseline
B_{0}=S_{O}
.
Under Assumption
2.3
, both are finite available premise bases with decidable membership.
Section
IV
is operational by default (baseline
B_{0}=S_{O}
), while still comparing to the intrinsic baseline
A
.

Definition 5.1

(Noisy premise base (baseline-parametric))
.

Fix a finite baseline available premise base

.
A

is any finite set of the form

(5.1)

where

is a finite set of

and

is a finite set of

.

Remark 5.1

(Effectivity requirements for noisy bases)
.

When we write

, we implicitly assume membership in

is decidable
(e.g.,
\tilde{B}_{0}
is given by an explicit list or by a decidable index set).
Under this effectivity assumption and the finiteness in Definition

,
Theorem
2.1
applies with premise base
\tilde{B}_{0}
.

Remark 5.2

(Noisy derivation depth)
.

Given a noisy base
\tilde{B}_{0}
, the noisy depth of a query

is
\text{Dd}(q\mid\tilde{B}_{0})
in the sense of
Definition

. This is the only notion of derivation cost used in this section.
(Noise may also affect semantic correctness; here we focus on cost, while robustness mechanisms are introduced later.)

Remark 5.3

(Consistency with Sections
III
–
IV
)
.

In the ideal regime, the premise base is simply the chosen baseline
B_{0}
(intrinsic
A
or operational
S_{O}
).
Section
IV
already treats caching as base augmentation
B_{0}\mapsto B_{0}\cup S

(Definition

); Section

keeps exactly the same semantics
but allows

itself to be perturbed into
\tilde{B}_{0}
.

V-B

Derivation Entropy under Noise

Assumption 5.1

(Bounded and effectively describable noise)
.

Let
B_{0}
be a baseline premise base with
m:=|B_{0}|
, and let
\tilde{B}_{0}
be as in
Definition
5.1
. Write
\mathcal{N}:=|B_{0}^{-}|+|B_{0}^{+}|
.
We assume:

(i)

(
Polynomial naming universe
) There exists a computable family of universes

\{\mathcal{V}_{s}\}_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{O}
with canonical indexing such that

|\mathcal{V}_{s}|\leq\operatorname{poly}(s)
and

B_{0}\cup B_{0}^{+}\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{m+\mathcal{N}}.

Consequently, every element of
B_{0}\cup B_{0}^{+}
can be named by an index using

O(\log(m+\mathcal{N}))
bits.

(ii)

(
Two-way effective noise description
) Given either
\langle B_{0}\rangle
or
\langle\tilde{B}_{0}\rangle
,
the finite sets
B_{0}^{-}
and
B_{0}^{+}
are effectively describable (and membership is decidable) by
index lists over
\mathcal{V}_{m+\mathcal{N}}
of total length

O(\mathcal{N}\log(m+\mathcal{N}))
under the fixed encoding conventions.

Definition 5.2

(Derivation entropy under a premise base)
.

For a finite premise base
B
and a query
q\in\text{Cn}(B)
, define

H_{\mathrm{derive}}(q\mid B):=\text{Dd}(q\mid B)\cdot\ln 2.

Remark 5.4

(Conditional algorithmic entropy under noise)
.

For any finite premise base
B
, define

H_{K}(q\mid B):=K\!\left(q\mid\langle B\rangle\right)\cdot\ln 2.

In particular,
H_{K}(q\mid\tilde{B}_{0})=K(q\mid\langle\tilde{B}_{0}\rangle)\cdot\ln 2
.

Lemma 5.1

(Base-conversion description length)
.

Let
B_{0}
be a baseline base and
\tilde{B}_{0}=(B_{0}\setminus B_{0}^{-})\cup B_{0}^{+}
a noisy base.
Let
m:=|B_{0}|
and
\mathcal{N}:=|B_{0}^{-}|+|B_{0}^{+}|
.
Under Assumption
5.1
,

\displaystyle K\left(\langle\tilde{B}_{0}\rangle\mid\langle B_{0}\rangle\right)=O\bigl(\mathcal{N}\log(m+\mathcal{N})\bigr),

\displaystyle K\left(\langle B_{0}\rangle\mid\langle\tilde{B}_{0}\rangle\right)=O\bigl(\mathcal{N}\log(m+\mathcal{N})\bigr).

We describe each base from the other by specifying the symmetric difference using index lists over the
polynomial naming universe
\mathcal{V}_{m+\mathcal{N}}
.

From
\langle B_{0}\rangle
to
\langle\tilde{B}_{0}\rangle
.

Given
\langle B_{0}\rangle
, encode the finite sets
B_{0}^{-}
and
B_{0}^{+}
as index lists in

\mathcal{V}_{m+\mathcal{N}}
. By Assumption
5.1
(i), each index costs

O(\log(m+\mathcal{N}))
bits, and by (ii) the total description length is

O(\mathcal{N}\log(m+\mathcal{N}))
.
From
\langle B_{0}\rangle
and these lists we can compute
\langle\tilde{B}_{0}\rangle
effectively.

From
\langle\tilde{B}_{0}\rangle
to
\langle B_{0}\rangle
.

Similarly, given
\langle\tilde{B}_{0}\rangle
, provide the same two index lists for
B_{0}^{-}
and
B_{0}^{+}

(over
\mathcal{V}_{m+\mathcal{N}}
); Assumption
5.1
(ii) guarantees such a two-way effective description.
Then
B_{0}
is recovered by adding
B_{0}^{-}
and removing
B_{0}^{+}
, and
\langle B_{0}\rangle
is computable
from
\langle\tilde{B}_{0}\rangle
plus this description.

In both directions, the program overhead is
O(1)
, so the conditional Kolmogorov complexities satisfy the stated bounds.
∎

Proposition 5.1

(Noisy derivation-entropy characterization and entropy perturbation)
.

Let
B_{0}
be a baseline base,
\tilde{B}_{0}
a noisy base as in Definition
5.1
,

m:=|B_{0}|
,
\tilde{m}:=|\tilde{B}_{0}|
, and
\mathcal{N}:=|B_{0}^{-}|+|B_{0}^{+}|
.
Assume Assumption
5.1
and Assumption
3.3
.

Let
q
be a query derivable from
\tilde{B}_{0}
, with noisy depth
\tilde{n}:=\text{Dd}(q\mid\tilde{B}_{0})

satisfying
\log\tilde{m}\leq\tilde{n}\leq\operatorname{poly}(\tilde{m})
.
Assume
(q,\tilde{B}_{0})
lies in the information-rich regime and is generic/incompressible at its depth scale
so that Theorem
3.1
applies with premise base
\tilde{B}_{0}
.
Then:

(i)

Noisy derivation-entropy characterization:

H_{\mathrm{derive}}(q\mid\tilde{B}_{0})=\Theta\!\left(\frac{H_{K}(q\mid\tilde{B}_{0})}{\log(\tilde{m}+\tilde{n})}\right).

(5.2)

(ii)

Algorithmic-entropy perturbation:

\bigl|H_{K}(q\mid\tilde{B}_{0})-H_{K}(q\mid B_{0})\bigr|\leq O\!\bigl(\mathcal{N}\log(m+\mathcal{N})\bigr).

(5.3)

(i) Apply Theorem
3.1
with premise base
\tilde{B}_{0}
.

(ii) Using the conditioning inequality

K(x\mid y)\leq K(x\mid z)+K(z\mid y)+O(1)
and Lemma

, we have

K(q\mid\langle B_{0}\rangle)\leq K(q\mid\langle\tilde{B}_{0}\rangle)+O(\mathcal{N}\log(m+\mathcal{N})),

and symmetrically with
B_{0}
and
\tilde{B}_{0}

\ln 2
.
∎

V-C

Noise Effects on Tradeoff Parameters

We quantify how premise loss/pollution perturbs the parameters that govern the tradeoffs and optimization
problems in Section
IV
. The key asymmetry is monotonic:

adding premises cannot increase depth
, while
removing premises cannot decrease depth
.

Lemma 5.2

(Monotonicity in the premise base)
.

If
B_{1}\subseteq B_{2}
are finite available premise bases, then for every
s\in\mathbb{S}_{O}
,

\text{Dd}(s\mid B_{2})\leq\text{Dd}(s\mid B_{1}).

This follows by well-founded recursion on the predecessor unfolding using (
2.2
):
the base case set
\{s:s\in B\}
only grows with
B
, and the recursive clause takes a maximum over predecessors.
∎

Definition 5.3

(Reconstruction depth from preserved premises)
.

Fix a baseline
B_{0}
and loss set
B_{0}^{-}\subseteq B_{0}
. Let
B_{\cap}:=B_{0}\setminus B_{0}^{-}
be the preserved base.
Define the (worst-case) reconstruction depth of lost premises from preserved premises as

d_{\mathrm{rec}}:=\max_{b\in B_{0}^{-}}\text{Dd}\!\bigl(b\mid B_{\cap}\bigr),

with the convention
d_{\mathrm{rec}}=\infty
if some lost premise
b\in B_{0}^{-}
is not derivable from
B_{\cap}
,
i.e.,
b\notin\text{Cn}(B_{\cap})
.

Remark 5.5

(Why reconstruction depth is meaningful)
.

An element is depth-
0
only relative to the chosen premise base (Definition
2.11
).
Thus a premise
b\in B_{0}
can be atomic/available under
B_{0}
(depth
0
) yet have positive reconstruction
depth when removed from the available base.

Lemma 5.3

(Noise perturbation of depth and description length)
.

Let
B_{0}
be a baseline base,
\tilde{B}_{0}=(B_{0}\setminus B_{0}^{-})\cup B_{0}^{+}
a noisy base, and let
q
be derivable
from both
B_{0}
and
\tilde{B}_{0}
.
Let
m:=|B_{0}|
,
\tilde{m}:=|\tilde{B}_{0}|
,
n:=\text{Dd}(q\mid B_{0})
, and
\tilde{n}:=\text{Dd}(q\mid\tilde{B}_{0})
.
Under Assumption
5.1
:

(i)

Base-size perturbation:

|\tilde{m}-m|\leq\mathcal{N}
.

(ii)

Loss-driven depth degradation:

letting
B_{\cap}:=B_{0}\setminus B_{0}^{-}
and
d_{\mathrm{rec}}
be as in Definition
5.3
,

\text{Dd}(q\mid B_{\cap})\leq n+d_{\mathrm{rec}}.

(5.4)

Moreover, since
B_{\cap}\subseteq\tilde{B}_{0}
, pollution cannot increase depth:

\tilde{n}=\text{Dd}(q\mid\tilde{B}_{0})\leq\text{Dd}(q\mid B_{\cap}).

In particular,
\tilde{n}\leq n+d_{\mathrm{rec}}
.

(iii)

Algorithmic-entropy perturbation:

\bigl|H_{K}(q\mid\tilde{B}_{0})-H_{K}(q\mid B_{0})\bigr|\leq O\!\bigl(\mathcal{N}\log(m+\mathcal{N})\bigr).

(5.5)

(i) Immediate from
\tilde{B}_{0}=(B_{0}\setminus B_{0}^{-})\cup B_{0}^{+}
.

(ii) Let
B_{\cap}:=B_{0}\setminus B_{0}^{-}
. Assume
d_{\mathrm{rec}}<\infty
; otherwise the bound is trivial.
We prove the stronger claim that for all
s\in\mathbb{S}_{O}
,

\text{Dd}(s\mid B_{\cap})\leq\text{Dd}(s\mid B_{0})+d_{\mathrm{rec}}.

(5.6)

Consider the backward predecessor unfolding DAG of
s
(Proposition
2.3
) and proceed in a
topological order (equivalently, by well-founded recursion on the unfolding height).

If
s\in B_{\cap}
, then
\text{Dd}(s\mid B_{\cap})=0\leq\text{Dd}(s\mid B_{0})+d_{\mathrm{rec}}
.

If
s\in B_{0}^{-}
, then
\text{Dd}(s\mid B_{0})=0
and by definition of
d_{\mathrm{rec}}
we have

\text{Dd}(s\mid B_{\cap})\leq d_{\mathrm{rec}}
, so (
5.6
) holds.

Otherwise
s\notin B_{0}
, and by (
2.2
),

\text{Dd}(s\mid B_{\cap})=1+\max_{s^{\prime}\in P_{O}(s)}\text{Dd}(s^{\prime}\mid B_{\cap}).

By the induction hypothesis, for every
s^{\prime}\in P_{O}(s)
,

\text{Dd}(s^{\prime}\mid B_{\cap})\leq\text{Dd}(s^{\prime}\mid B_{0})+d_{\mathrm{rec}}
, hence

\displaystyle\text{Dd}(s\mid B_{\cap})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1+\max_{s^{\prime}\in P_{O}(s)}\bigl(\text{Dd}(s^{\prime}\mid B_{0})+d_{\mathrm{rec}}\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Dd}(s\mid B_{0})+d_{\mathrm{rec}},

which is (
5.6
). Taking
s=q
yields (
5.4
).

Finally, since
B_{\cap}\subseteq\tilde{B}_{0}
, Lemma
5.2
gives

\text{Dd}(q\mid\tilde{B}_{0})\leq\text{Dd}(q\mid B_{\cap})
, and thus
\tilde{n}\leq n+d_{\mathrm{rec}}
.

(iii) This is Proposition
5.1
(ii).
∎

Remark 5.6

(No two-sided bound on
|\tilde{n}-n|
without restricting pollution)
.

Without structural restrictions on
B_{0}^{+}
, pollution can arbitrarily shorten derivations (e.g., by adding
q
itself),
so
|\tilde{n}-n|
cannot be controlled solely by
\mathcal{N}
or loss-only parameters. This is why

5.3
gives robust one-sided “no-worse-than” bounds for loss and monotonicity for pollution.

Corollary 5.1

(Loss-only noise tolerance (depth stability))
.

Assume loss-only noise
B_{0}^{+}=\varnothing
, so
\tilde{B}_{0}=B_{0}\setminus B_{0}^{-}
.
Assume
q
remains derivable and
d_{\mathrm{rec}}<\infty
satisfies
d_{\mathrm{rec}}=o(n)
where
n=\text{Dd}(q\mid B_{0})
.
Then

H_{\mathrm{derive}}(q\mid\tilde{B}_{0})=H_{\mathrm{derive}}(q\mid B_{0})\cdot(1+o(1)).

Loss-only implies
\text{Dd}(q\mid\tilde{B}_{0})\geq\text{Dd}(q\mid B_{0})=n
(removing premises cannot decrease depth).
Lemma
5.3
(ii) gives
\text{Dd}(q\mid\tilde{B}_{0})\leq n+d_{\mathrm{rec}}
.
Thus
\text{Dd}(q\mid\tilde{B}_{0})=n(1+o(1))
, and multiply by
\ln 2
.
∎

Proposition 5.2

Let

be the access frequency.

Let
\tilde{m}:=|\tilde{B}_{0}|
and
\tilde{n}:=\text{Dd}(q\mid\tilde{B}_{0})

Assume
(q,\tilde{B}_{0})
lies in the information-rich regime and is generic/incompressible at its depth scale so that

applies to

Define the noisy cache-length benchmark

\displaystyle\tilde{\operatorname{Cost}}_{\mathrm{cache}}(q;\tilde{B}_{0}):=\frac{\rho\cdot\tilde{\ell}_{q}}{f_{q}}+\frac{\text{Dd}(q\mid\tilde{B}_{0})}{f_{q}}+c_{\mathrm{hit}},

Then:

(i)

\displaystyle\tilde{\operatorname{Cost}}^{*}(q;\tilde{B}_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\!\left\{\!\frac{\rho\!\cdot\!\tilde{\ell}_{q}}{f_{q}}\!+\!O\!\left(\!\frac{\tilde{n}}{f_{q}}\!\right)\!+\!O(1),\Theta\!\left(\!\frac{\tilde{\ell}_{q}}{\log(\tilde{m}\!+\!\tilde{n})}\!\right)\!\right\}.

(5.7)

(ii)

(5.8)

(iii)

let

be the ideal optimal amortized cost under baseline

as in Theorem

Under Assumption

\displaystyle\tilde{\operatorname{Cost}}^{*}(q;\tilde{B}_{0})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cost}^{*}(q;B_{0})\!+\!O\!\left(\!\frac{\rho\!\cdot\!\mathcal{N}\log(m\!+\!\mathcal{N})}{f_{q}}\!\right)\!+\!O(d_{\mathrm{rec}}),

Noisy system-wide allocation and submodularity

Fix an operational candidate set
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\text{Cn}(\text{Atom}(S_{O}))\setminus S_{O}
and a query set

Q=\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{K}\}
with distribution
P_{Q}
as in Section
IV-B
.
In noise, the baseline premise base becomes
\tilde{B}_{0}
(typically
\tilde{B}_{0}=\tilde{S}_{O}
operationally,
or
\tilde{B}_{0}=\tilde{A}
intrinsically), and caching enlarges
\tilde{B}_{0}
to
\tilde{B}_{0}\cup S
.

Definition 5.4

(Noisy expected derivation cost and reduction)
.

Given a noisy baseline
\tilde{B}_{0}
, define for
S\subseteq\mathcal{U}
:

\displaystyle\tilde{\bar{n}}(S):=\sum_{i=1}^{K}p_{i}\,\text{Dd}(q_{i}\mid\tilde{B}_{0}\cup S),

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}(S):=\tilde{\bar{n}}(\varnothing)-\tilde{\bar{n}}(S).

Proposition 5.3

(Preservation of submodularity under noise)
.

Assume diminishing returns on the noisy base: for all
A_{1}\subseteq A_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{U}
and

u\in\mathcal{U}\setminus A_{2}
,

\displaystyle\text{Dd}(q_{i}\mid\tilde{B}_{0}\cup A_{1})-\text{Dd}(q_{i}\mid\tilde{B}_{0}\cup A_{1}\cup\{u\})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\text{Dd}(q_{i}\mid\tilde{B}_{0}\cup A_{2})-\text{Dd}(q_{i}\mid\tilde{B}_{0}\cup A_{2}\cup\{u\})

for each
i
.
Then
\tilde{\Delta}
is normalized, monotone, and submodular on
2^{\mathcal{U}}
.

Identical to Theorem
4.2
, with
\bar{n}
replaced by
\tilde{\bar{n}}
.
∎

V-D
3
Pollution exposure and a robust objective

In pollution-dominated regimes it can be desirable to avoid cache items whose usefulness relies heavily on
spurious premises
B_{0}^{+}
, even if they reduce depth. We encode this as a modular penalty.

Definition 5.5

(Pollution exposure score)
.

Fix the noisy base
\tilde{B}_{0}=(B_{0}\setminus B_{0}^{-})\cup B_{0}^{+}
.
For a candidate cache item
u\in\mathcal{U}
, define its pollution exposure as

\pi_{\mathrm{poll}}(u):=\frac{\bigl|\operatorname{Ess}^{+}(u\mid\tilde{B}_{0})\cap B_{0}^{+}\bigr|}{\max\{1,\ |\operatorname{Ess}^{+}(u\mid\tilde{B}_{0})|\}}\in[0,1],

where
\operatorname{Ess}^{+}(\cdot\mid\tilde{B}_{0})
is defined analogously to Definition
3.12
with baseline
\tilde{B}_{0}
.

Definition 5.6

(Robustified noisy objective)
.

For penalty weight
\lambda\geq 0
, define for
S\subseteq\mathcal{U}

\tilde{\Delta}_{\lambda}(S):=\tilde{\Delta}(S)-\lambda\sum_{u\in S}\sigma(u)\,\pi_{\mathrm{poll}}(u).

Proposition 5.4

(Submodularity is preserved under pollution penalties)
.

If
\tilde{\Delta}
is normalized and submodular, then
\tilde{\Delta}_{\lambda}
is normalized and submodular for every
\lambda\geq 0
.
(Monotonicity may be lost.)

The penalty term is modular in
S
, hence submodular; subtracting a modular function preserves submodularity.
∎

Remark 5.7

(Monotonicity and algorithms)
.

Because
\tilde{\Delta}_{\lambda}
may be non-monotone, Algorithm
1
does not retain its
(1-1/e)
guarantee
when applied directly. One may instead use approximation algorithms for
non-monotone
submodular maximization;
see, e.g.,
[
9
]
.

V-D
4
Loss-dominated regimes: compensation-then-optimize

When loss dominates, a small number of missing premises can dramatically increase depth.
A natural response is to first allocate storage to
restore
(cache) critical lost premises, and then
optimize remaining storage for depth reduction.

Definition 5.7

(Critical and reconstructible lost premises (baseline-parametric))
.

Fix a baseline
B_{0}
, its noisy version
\tilde{B}_{0}
, and a finite query set
Q
.
Define:

(i)

Critical lost premises:

\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{crit}}:=B_{0}^{-}\cap\bigcup_{q\in Q}\operatorname{Ess}^{+}(q\mid B_{0}).

(ii)

Reconstructible critical premises:

\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{rec}}:=\{b\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{crit}}:\ b\in\text{Cn}(\tilde{B}_{0})\}.

(iii)

Irrecoverable critical premises:

\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{irr}}:=\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{crit}}\setminus\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{rec}}.

Remark 5.8

(Caching lost premises during compensation)
.

Although Section
IV-B
typically uses
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\text{Cn}(\text{Atom}(S_{O}))\setminus S_{O}
,
in the compensation phase we allow caching elements
b\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{rec}}\subseteq B_{0}
as well:
adding
b
to the available premise base simply makes it depth-
0
again under Definition
2.11
.
Formally, define the extended ground set

\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{ext}}:=\mathcal{U}\cup\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{rec}}.

Definition 5.8

(Depth-threshold service constraint (
\mathrm{SLA}_{h}
, noisy baseline))
.

Fix a depth threshold
h\in\mathbb{N}
and a storage budget
\mathcal{B}>0
(bits).
An allocation
S\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{ext}}
is
\mathrm{SLA}_{h}
-feasible under noisy baseline
\tilde{B}_{0}
if

\sigma(S)\leq\mathcal{B}\quad\text{and}\quad\max_{1\leq i\leq K}\text{Dd}(q_{i}\mid\tilde{B}_{0}\cup S)\leq h.

(5.10)

Denote the feasible family by
\mathcal{F}_{h}(\mathcal{B})
.

Assumption 5.2

(SLA repair by compensation)
.

Let
S_{\mathrm{comp}}:=\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{rec}}
and
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{comp}}:=\sigma(S_{\mathrm{comp}})
.
Assume
\mathcal{B}\geq\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{comp}}
and the compensation cache alone meets the depth SLA:

\max_{1\leq i\leq K}\text{Dd}(q_{i}\mid\tilde{B}_{0}\cup S_{\mathrm{comp}})\leq h.

Assumption 5.3

(Mandatory compensation under
\mathrm{SLA}_{h}
)
.

Assume that every
\mathrm{SLA}_{h}
-feasible allocation must contain the compensation set:

\forall S\in\mathcal{F}_{h}(\mathcal{B}),\qquad S_{\mathrm{comp}}\subseteq S.

Lemma 5.4

(Compensation cache size bound)
.

Assume
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{irr}}=\varnothing
.
Then the total description size needed to store
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{rec}}
as cached premises satisfies

\displaystyle\sigma(S_{\mathrm{comp}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{rec}}|\cdot O(\log(m+\mathcal{N}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\cdot O(\log(m+\mathcal{N})),

where
m:=|B_{0}|
and
\mathcal{N}:=|B_{0}^{-}|+|B_{0}^{+}|
.

Each element of
B_{0}
can be named by an index in a pool of size
O(m+\mathcal{N})
, costing
O(\log(m+\mathcal{N}))
bits.
Moreover, under Assumption
5.1
(i) and the fixed representation conventions
(Axiom
2.3
), such an index effectively specifies the corresponding encoded premise,
so it can be stored and re-introduced as an available premise when cached.
Since
|\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{rec}}|\leq|B_{0}^{-}|\leq\mathcal{N}
, the bound follows.
∎

Proposition 5.5

(Two-phase strategy:
(1-1/e)
guarantee under
\mathrm{SLA}_{h}
)
.

Assume
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{irr}}=\varnothing
, and Assumptions
5.2
and
5.3
.
Let
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}:=\mathcal{B}-\sigma(S_{\mathrm{comp}})
be the remaining budget.

Define the incremental objective after compensation (on the noisy baseline):

\tilde{\Delta}_{\mathrm{add}}(S):=\tilde{\bar{n}}(S_{\mathrm{comp}})-\tilde{\bar{n}}(S_{\mathrm{comp}}\cup S),\qquad S\subseteq\mathcal{U}.

Assume
\tilde{\Delta}_{\mathrm{add}}
is normalized, monotone, and submodular (e.g., by diminishing returns on
\tilde{B}_{0}\cup S_{\mathrm{comp}}
).

Let
S_{\mathrm{greedy}}\subseteq\mathcal{U}
be the output of Algorithm
1
applied to
\tilde{\Delta}_{\mathrm{add}}
with budget
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
,
and set
S_{\mathrm{2ph}}:=S_{\mathrm{comp}}\cup S_{\mathrm{greedy}}\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{ext}}
.
Then
S_{\mathrm{2ph}}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}(\mathcal{B})
, and if

S_{h}^{\star}\in\arg\max\{\tilde{\Delta}(S\cap\mathcal{U}):S\in\mathcal{F}_{h}(\mathcal{B})\},

we have

\tilde{\Delta}(S_{\mathrm{2ph}}\cap\mathcal{U})\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{e}\right)\tilde{\Delta}(S_{h}^{\star}\cap\mathcal{U}).

(5.11)

Feasibility holds because adding premises cannot increase derivation depth.
Under Assumption
5.3
, any
\mathrm{SLA}_{h}
-feasible solution must include
S_{\mathrm{comp}}
,
so the remaining choice reduces to monotone submodular maximization under a knapsack constraint on
\mathcal{U}
,
to which Theorem
4.3
applies.
∎

VI
Conclusion

This paper develops an axiomatic and information-theoretic framework for analyzing

inference cost
and
storage–computation tradeoffs
in knowledge-based query answering systems.
Our starting point is a two-domain view of information
(S_{O},S_{C})
together with a computable realization mechanism
(Section
II
), and a canonical separation between
intrinsic
semantic content and

operational
shortcut effects via the irredundant core
\text{Atom}(S_{O})

(Section
II-F
).

Main results

We introduce
base-relative derivation depth

\text{Dd}(\cdot\mid B)
as a computable, premise-parametric cost functional.
By encoding derivation traces (Lemma
3.2
) and combining richness with incompressibility
(Definition
3.10
and Lemma
3.3
), we show that for generic information-rich queries,

\text{Dd}(\cdot\mid B)
is tightly coupled to conditional algorithmic information up to an unavoidable logarithmic addressing factor
(Theorem
3.1
).
For conjunctive workloads, the compositional depth
\text{Dd}_{\Sigma}
provides a step-accurate instantiation
(Definition
3.9
and Corollary
3.1
).
Building on the depth–information correspondence, we derive a frequency-weighted storage–computation tradeoff with a break-even
frequency scale
f_{c}=\Theta(\rho\log(m+d))
in the generic regime (Theorem
4.1
and
Corollary
4.1
), and formulate system-wide caching as a budgeted optimization problem that admits standard
approximation guarantees under submodularity assumptions (Section
IV-B
).
Finally, we extend the framework to noisy premise bases (Section
V
) and derive baseline-parametric,
noise-aware tradeoffs and allocation objectives.

Limitations

Several limitations reflect the gap between idealized information-theoretic models and deployed systems.
First, the depth-as-information theorems rely on explicit structural assumptions, including alignment between the predecessor
operator and single-step inference (Assumption
2.5
), serializability relating
Dd
to shortest trace length

N
(Assumption
3.3
), and a richness condition guaranteeing sufficiently many distinct queries at each cost
scale (Definition
3.10
).
Characterizing when these assumptions hold for concrete logics, proof systems, and workloads remains an open task.
Second, our semantic-core extraction
\text{Atom}(S_{O})
relies on a decidable redundancy test
(Assumption
2.4
) and assumes finite, effectively listable knowledge bases
(Assumption
2.3
).
Third, the noise model in Section
V
is premise-set based (loss/pollution) and does not yet capture richer
corruption modes (e.g., structured drift in rules or semantics-changing perturbations).
Finally, while our results use conditional Kolmogorov complexity as an information-theoretic benchmark, it is not computable; any
operational deployment must instantiate the description-length terms via concrete encodings or computable proxies.

Future directions

The framework suggests several directions for further study:
(i) develop testable sufficient conditions (or diagnostics) for richness and serializability in common proof fragments, and quantify
the constant factors relating
Dd
,
N
, and execution time;
(ii) design scalable methods to approximate semantic locality (e.g.,
\operatorname{Ess}^{+}(q\mid S_{O})
) and exploit locality-refined parameters
in caching decisions;
(iii) extend the optimization layer to nonstationary query distributions and online adaptation;
(iv) move beyond set perturbations to richer noise and robustness models, including adversarial pollution; and
(v) integrate the proposed metrics into concrete reasoning/caching engines to study end-to-end behavior and system interactions.

In summary, derivation depth provides a principled bridge between inference cost and information, supporting both individual-query
tradeoffs and system-wide storage allocation.

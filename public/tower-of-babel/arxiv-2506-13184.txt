Iterated finite group actions on closed connected aspherical manifolds
Jordi Daura Serrano

Abstract
. In this paper we use free iterated actions and the iterated discrete degree of symmetry introduced in [ DS25b ] to obtain rigidity results on aspherical manifolds. We also introduce the concept of the length of an iterated action and we study it for nilmanifolds, solvmanifold and locally symmetric spaces.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 57S17, 54H15

1.
Introduction

Let
M
be a closed connected manifold. Can we determine
M
up to homeomorphism if we know the collection of finite group which acts effectively on
M
? The answer to this question is clearly negative since there exist many closed connected asymmetric manifolds (manifolds which do not admit any effective group action of a non-trivial finite group) which are not homeomorphic. Indeed, it is conjectured in
[
Pup07
]
and
[
Sch81
]
that "most" manifolds are asymmetric. The question becomes interesting if we assume that
M
admits effective group actions of large finite groups in some sense. A way to make this question precise is to use the discrete degree of symmetry introduced in
[
MiR24a
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Given a manifold
M
let

\mu(M)=\{r\in\mathbb{N}:\text{ $M$ admits an effective action of $(\mathbb{Z}/a)^{r}$ for arbitrarily large $a$}\}.

More explicitly,
r\in\mu(M)
if there exists an increasing sequence of natural number
\{a_{i}\}
and effective group actions of
(\mathbb{Z}/a_{i})^{r}
on
M
for each
i
.

The discrete degree of symmetry of a manifold
M
is

\operatorname{disc-sym}(M)=\max(\{0\}\cup\mu(M)).

The discrete degree of symmetry was introduced in
[
MiR24a
]
, and it can be considered an analogue for finite group actions of the toral degree of symmetry, which is defined as

\operatorname{tor-sym}(M)=\max(\{0\}\cup\{r\in\mathbb{N}:T^{r}\text{ acts effectively on $M$}\}),

and which has been widely studied (see
[
Hsi12
, Chapter VII. §2]
,
[
LR10
, §11.7, §11.8]
and the survey
[
Gro02
]
). Recall that if
M
is a closed connected
n
-dimensional manifold, then
\operatorname{tor-sym}(M)\leq n
and the equality holds if and only if
M
is homeomorphic to
T^{n}
(see
[
MiR24a
, 12.2]
). A natural question to ask is whether this result is still true if we replace
\operatorname{tor-sym}(M)
by
\operatorname{disc-sym}(M)
:

Question 1.2
.

[
MiR24b
, Question 3.4, Question 3.5]

Let
M
be a closed connected
n
-dimensional manifold. Is
\operatorname{disc-sym}(M)\leq n
? If
\operatorname{disc-sym}(M)=n
, is
M
homeomorphic to
T^{n}
?

These questions have been affirmatively answered for some classes of manifolds:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
M
be a closed connected
n
-dimensional manifold. Then:

(1)

[
MiR24a
, Theorem 1.3]
If
M
is oriented and there exists a non-zero degree map
M\longrightarrow T^{n}
then
\operatorname{disc-sym}(M)\leq n
and if the equality holds and
\pi_{1}(M)
is virtually solvable then
M\cong T^{n}
.

(2)

[
DS25a
, Theorem 1.6, Theorem 1.9]
If
M
is aspherical and there exists a constant
C
such that every finite subgroup
G
of
\operatorname{Out}(\pi_{1}(M))
satisfies
|G|\leq C
, then
\operatorname{disc-sym}(M)\leq n
and the equality holds if and only if
M\cong T^{n}
.

Remark 1.4
.

Following the notation introduced in
[
DS25a
]
, we will say that a group
\mathcal{G}
is Minkowski if there exists a constant
C
such that every finite subgroup
G\leq\mathcal{G}
satisfies
|G|\leq C
. This name is motivated by a classical result of Hermann Minkowski which states that
\operatorname{GL}(n,\mathbb{Z})
is Minkowski. The Minkowski property was studied under the name of bounded finite subgroups property in
[
Pop11
,
PS14
,
Pop18
,
Gol23
,
BZ24
]
.

The discrete degree of symmetry is useful to detect when a closed connected manifold is homeomorphic to
T^{n}
. However, it falls short to obtain rigidity results for other classes of manifolds. A class of manifolds which generalize tori are nilmanifolds, which are coset spaces
N/\Gamma
where
N
is a simply connected nilpotent Lie group and
\Gamma
is a lattice of
N
. An example of nilmanifold in dimension
3
are Heisenberg manifolds, which can be obtained as the total space of a non-trivial principal
S^{1}
-bundle over
T^{2}
. It is straightforward to prove that their discrete degree of symmetry is equal to
1
. However, there exist many other
3
-dimensional manifolds with discrete degree of symmetry equal to 1 which are not homeomorphic to a Heisenberg manifold (see
1.9
below and
[
DS25a
]
).

Motivated by the fact that nilmanifolds are precisely the total space of iterated principal
S^{1}
-bundles (see
[
Bel20
]
), we introduced free iterated actions of finite groups and the iterated discrete degree of symmetry in
[
DS25b
]
to generalize
1.3
.(2). Iterated group actions were previously studied with other purposes by other authors, see
[
BBS01
,
FOM12
,
vL18
,
vL21
,
BK23
,
QSW24
]
.

Let us briefly recall some of the concepts introduced in
[
DS25b
]
.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=\{G_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,n}
be a collection of groups and let
X
be a topological space. An iterated action of
\mathcal{G}
on
X
(denoted by
\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright X
) is:

1.

A sequence of surjections of topological spaces

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X=X_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{n},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{3}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{n}}

2.

and a collection of group actions
\{\Phi_{i}:G_{i}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Homeo}(X_{i-1})\}_{i=1,\dots,n}
,

such that the maps
p_{i}:X_{i-1}\longrightarrow X_{i}
are the orbit maps of the action of
G_{i}
on
X_{i-1}
. If all actions are free we say that the iterated action is free.

Let
\mathcal{G}=\{G_{i}\}_{i=1,...,n}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=\{G^{\prime}_{i}\}_{i=1,...,n^{\prime}}
be two collections of finite groups which act freely on
M
. Let
p=p_{n}\circ\cdots\circ p_{1}
and
p^{\prime}=p^{\prime}_{n^{\prime}}\circ\cdots\circ p^{\prime}_{1}
. We say that the iterated actions
\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright M
and
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\curvearrowright M
are equivalent (and we denote it by
\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright M\sim\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\curvearrowright M
) if there exist homeomorphisms
f:M_{n}\longrightarrow M_{n^{\prime}}
and
\overline{f}:M\longrightarrow M
such that
p^{\prime}\circ\overline{f}=f\circ p
(that is,
p
and
p^{\prime}
are isomorphic coverings).

The equivalence class will be denoted by
[\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright M]
. If there exists a finite group
G
such that
\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright M
is equivalent to
\{G\}\curvearrowright M
then we say that
\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright M
is simplifiable.

Given a free iterated action
\mathcal{A}\curvearrowright M
of finite abelian groups, the rank of the iterated action is

\operatorname{rank}_{ab}(\mathcal{A}\curvearrowright M)=\min\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{rank}A^{\prime}_{i}:\{A_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,A_{n}^{\prime}\}\curvearrowright M\in[\mathcal{A}\curvearrowright M]\text{, $A_{i}^{\prime}$ abelian for all $i$}\}.

We define
\mu_{2}(M)
as the set of all pairs
(f,b)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
which satisfy:

1.

There exist an increasing sequence of prime numbers
\{p_{i}\}
, a sequence of natural numbers
\{a_{i}\}
and a collection of free iterated actions
\{(\mathbb{Z}/p_{i}^{a_{i}})^{f},(\mathbb{Z}/p_{i})^{b}\}\curvearrowright M
for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

2.

\operatorname{rank}_{ab}(\{(\mathbb{Z}/p_{i}^{a_{i}})^{f},(\mathbb{Z}/p_{i})^{b}\}\curvearrowright M)=f+b
for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

Consider the lexicographic order in
\mathbb{N}^{2}
, that is
(a,b)\geq(c,d)
if
a>c
, or
a=c
and
b\geq d
. Define the iterated discrete degree of symmetry of
M
as

\operatorname{disc-sym}_{2}(M)=\max\{(0,0)\cup\mu_{2}(M)\}.

The iterated discrete degree of symmetry of a manifold is a refinement of the degree of symmetry of a manifold. The next theorem, proved in
[
DS25b
]
, is a generalization of
1.3
.(1).

Theorem 1.6
.

[
DS25b
]

Let
M
be a closed connected orientable
n
-dimensional manifold admitting a non-zero degree map
M\longrightarrow N/\Gamma
to a
2
-step nilmanifold satisfying that
\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{Z}\Gamma=f
. Then
\operatorname{disc-sym}_{2}(M)\leq(f,n-f)
. Moreover, if
\operatorname{disc-sym}_{2}(M)=(f,n-f)
and
\pi_{1}(M)
is virtually solvable then
M\cong N/\Gamma
.

Thus, the iterated discrete degree of symmetry is a suitable tool to study rigidity problems on manifolds related with 2-step nilmanifolds. The main goal of this paper is to generalize
1.3
.(2). In particular, we prove:

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
M
be a closed connected
n
-dimensional aspherical manifold such that
\mathcal{Z}\pi_{1}(M)
and
\mathcal{Z}\operatorname{Inn}\pi_{1}(M)
are finitely generated, and
\operatorname{Aut}(\operatorname{Inn}\pi_{1}(M))
and
\operatorname{Out}(\operatorname{Inn}\pi_{1}(M))
are Minkowski. Assume that
\operatorname{disc-sym}_{2}(M)=(f,b)
with
f+b=n
. Then
M
is homeomorphic to a 2-step nilmanifold
N/\Gamma
satisfying that
\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{Z}\Gamma=f
.

It is an interesting question whether all closed connected aspherical manifolds satisfy the hypothesis of
1.7
.

No closed aspherical manifold with
\mathcal{Z}\pi_{1}(M)
not finitely generated is known (see
[
LR10
, Remark 3.1.19.]
). In
[
BHM22
]
, Belk, Hyde and Matucci constructed a finitely presented group
G
such that
\mathcal{Z}G\cong(\mathbb{Q},+)
, which is not finitely generated (see also
[
Hou07
, Theorem II]
). We do not know if
G
could be the fundamental group of a closed connected aspherical manifold (we do not even know if
G
is torsion-free or the cohomology of
G
is finite and satisfies Poincaré duality).

Regarding the conditions on the automorphism and outer automorphism groups, we prove:

Proposition 1.8
.

Let
\Gamma
be a lattice of a connected Lie group
G
, then
\operatorname{Aut}(\operatorname{Inn}\Gamma)
and
\operatorname{Out}(\operatorname{Inn}\Gamma)
are Minkowski.

1.8
can be deduced using the same arguments as in the proof of
[
DS25a
, Thereom 1.9]
. Thus, we can use
1.7
on locally homogeneous spaces
H\setminus G/\Gamma
, where
H
is a connected Lie group,
H
is a maximal compact subgroup of
G
and
\Gamma
is a cocompact torsion-free lattice of
G
.

We also compute the iterated discrete degree of symmetry for closed connected aspherical 3-manifolds. In the following theorem,
K
denotes the Klein bottle and
SK
denotes the total space of the non-trivial principal
S^{1}
-bundle over
K
.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
M
be a
3
-dimensional closed connected aspherical manifold. Then:

1.

\operatorname{disc-sym}_{2}(M)=(3,0)
if
M\cong T^{3}
.

2.

\operatorname{disc-sym}_{2}(M)=(2,0)
if
M\cong K\times S^{1}
or
M\cong SK
.

3.

\operatorname{disc-sym}_{2}(M)=(1,2)
if
M
is a Heisenberg manifold.

4.

\operatorname{disc-sym}_{2}(M)=(1,0)
if
\mathcal{Z}\pi_{1}(M)\cong\mathbb{Z}
and
\operatorname{Inn}\pi_{1}(M)
is centreless.

5.

\operatorname{disc-sym}_{2}(M)=(0,0)
if
M
does not belong to any of the above 4 cases.

In
1.7
and
1.9
, we only consider free iterated actions of two finite groups. We can study, instead, free iterated actions on manifolds of many finite groups. To do so, we define the following invariant:

Definition 1.10
.

Given a collection of finite groups
\mathcal{G}=\{G_{1},\dots,G_{n}\}
, we define
l(\mathcal{G})=n
. Given a free iterated action
\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright M
, the length of the iterated action is

l(\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright M)=\min\{l(\mathcal{G}^{\prime}):\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\curvearrowright M\in[\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright M]\}.

The iterated length of a manifold
M
is

l(M)=\max\{l(\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright M):\text{ free iterated action }\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright M\}.

With this notation, a free iterated action
\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright X
is simplifiable if and only if
l(\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright X)=1
and all free iterated actions on
X
are simplifiable if and only if
l(X)=1
. The next result gives a bound for the iterated length of a nilmanifold.

Theorem 1.11
.

Let
N/\Gamma
be a
c
-step nilmanifold. There exists a constant
C
such that any free iterated action
\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright N/\Gamma
is equivalent to a free iterated action
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\curvearrowright N/\Gamma
where
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=\{A_{1},...,A_{c},G^{\prime}\}
,
A_{i}
are finite abelian groups such that
\operatorname{rank}(A_{i})\leq b_{i}
and
|G^{\prime}|\leq C
. In particular,
l(N/\Gamma)\leq c+1
.

Given a closed connected manifold
M
, it is an interesting question to study when
l(M)
is bounded. If no bound exists then we will write
l(M)=\infty
. We have seen that
l(M)
is bounded if
M
is a nilmanifold. Another case where the iterated length is bounded is the following:

Proposition 1.12
.

Given a locally symmetric space
H\setminus G/\Gamma
, there exists
C
depending on
\Gamma
such that
l(M)\leq C
.

However, not all closed connected aspherical manifolds have bounded iterated length, as the next result shows.

Theorem 1.13
.

1.

There exists a closed solvmanifold
M
such that
l(M)=\infty
.

2.

There exist a closed connected aspherical locally homogeneous space
H\setminus G/\Gamma
such that the solvable radical of
G
is abelian and
l(H\setminus G/\Gamma)=\infty
.

This paper is divided as follows. In the first section we recall some preliminary group theory results which will be widely used to prove the main results of this paper. The second section is devoted to prove
1.7
. In section three we prove
1.9
. Finally, in the forth section we study the length of free iterated actions on aspherical manifolds, proving
1.12
,
1.13
and
1.11
.

2.2.
Infranilmanifolds and almost crystallographic groups

Let
N
be a simply connected nilpotent Lie group and
C
be a maximal compact subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(N)
. A discrete cocompact subgroup
\Gamma
of
N\rtimes C
is called an almost-crystallographic group (abbreviated as AC-group). Moreover,
\Gamma
is said to be almost-Bieberbach if it is torsion-free. If
\Lambda
be a finitely generated torsion-free nilpotent group, an extension
1\longrightarrow\Lambda\longrightarrow\Gamma\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow 1
in which
G
is finite is said to be essential if
\Lambda
is a maximal nilpotent subgroup of
\Gamma
.
Note that crystallographic groups are an example of AC-groups. Bieberbach theorems for crystallographic groups have been generalized to almost-crystallographic groups.

Theorem 2.9
.

[
Dek06
, Chapter 2]

Let
N
be a simply connected nilpotent Lie group and
C
a maximal compact subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(N)
.

1.

(Generalized 1st Bieberbach theorem) Let
\Gamma\leq N\rtimes C
be an AC-group of
N
. The subgroup
N\cap\Gamma=\Lambda
is a lattice of
N
,
\Lambda
is the unique normal maximal nilpotent subgroup of
\Gamma
and
\Gamma/\Lambda
is a finite group.

2.

(Generalized 2nd Bieberbach theorem) Let
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
be AC-groups of
N
. Assume that there exists an isomorphism
f:\Gamma\longrightarrow\Gamma^{\prime}
. Then
f
can be realized as a conjugation by an element of
\operatorname{Aff}(N)=N\rtimes\operatorname{Aut}(N)
.

3.

(Generalized 3rd Bieberbach theorem) Let
\Lambda
be a lattice of
N
. There are only finitely many essential extensions
1\longrightarrow\Lambda\longrightarrow\Gamma\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow 1
.

If
\Gamma
is an almost-Bieberbach group of
N
then
\Gamma
acts freely on
N
and the orbit space
N/\Gamma
is a closed connected aspherical manifold which is finitely covered by the nilmanifold
N/\Lambda
. These manifolds are known as infranilmanifolds, and just like flat manifolds, they also have a geometric characterization, see
[
Gro78
,
Ruh82
]
. The algebraic characterization of crystallographic groups (see
[
Dek16
, Theorem 2.1.4]
) can also be generalized to AC-groups, but we need some further definitions to state it.

Definition 2.10
.

[
Dek06
, Definition 2.3.3]

Let
\Gamma
be a virtually polycyclic group. The Fitting subgroup
\operatorname{Fitt}(\Gamma)
is the unique maximal normal nilpotent subgroup of
\Gamma
. The Fitting subgroup
\operatorname{Fitt}(\Gamma)
is unique.

Theorem 2.11
.

[
Dek06
, Theorem 3.4.6]

Let
\Gamma
be a virtually polycyclic group. The following are equivalent:

1.

\Gamma
is an AC-group.

2.

\operatorname{Fitt}(\Gamma)
is torsion-free, maximal nilpotent and of finite index in
\Gamma
.

3.

\Gamma
contains a torsion-free nilpotent subgroup

of finite index such that

is torsion-free.

4.

\Gamma
contains a nilpotent subgroup of finite index and
\Gamma
does not contain any non-trivial finite normal subgroup.

Corollary 2.12
.

If
\Gamma
is a torsion-free virtually polycyclic group then
\Gamma
is an AC-group if and only if it contains a nilpotent subgroup of finite index.

for its definition and main properties. Let

Lemma 3.3

Assume that

and

are Minkowski.

Proof.

We consider the central short exact sequence

The group

Remark 3.4
.

Note that if

is 2-step Minkowski if and only if

Proposition 3.5

4.
Free iterated actions on closed aspherical 3-dimensional manifolds

If
M
is a closed
3
-dimensional aspherical manifold with an effective

action, then

can be one of the following four cases (see
[
LR10
, §14.4]

1.

M\cong T^{3}
.

2.

M
is homeomorphic to
K\times S^{1}
or
SK
, where
K
denotes the Klein bottle and
SK
the non-trivial principal
S^{1}
-bundle over
K
.

3.

M\cong H/\Gamma
, where
H
is the
3
-dimensional Heisenberg group and
\Gamma
is a lattice of
H

4.

\mathcal{Z}\pi_{1}(M)\cong\mathbb{Z}
, and
\operatorname{Inn}\pi_{1}(M)\cong\pi_{1}(M)/\mathcal{Z}\pi_{1}(M)
is centreless.

Note that in all cases, we have a central extension

1\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow\pi_{1}(M)\longrightarrow Q\longrightarrow 1

where
Q
acts effectively, properly and cocompactly on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. If
M
is a closed connected aspherical 3-manifold then
\operatorname{Out}(\pi_{1}(M))
is Minkowski by
[
Koj84
]
and
\operatorname{tor-sym}(M)=\operatorname{disc-sym}(M)=\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{Z}\pi_{1}(M)
by
[
Gab92
, Corollary 8.3]
and
[
CJ94
, Theorem 1.1]
. Thus, it is straightforward to show that
\operatorname{disc-sym}(T^{3})=3
,
\operatorname{disc-sym}(K\times S^{1})=\operatorname{disc-sym}(SK)=2
,
\operatorname{disc-sym}(H/\Gamma)=1
, and
\operatorname{disc-sym}(M)=1
if
M
belongs to the case 4. Moreover if
M
is a closed connected aspherical 3-manifold which does not belong to one of the four cases above, then
\operatorname{tor-sym}(M)=\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{Z}\pi_{1}(M)=0
and hence
\operatorname{disc-sym}(M)=0
.

We will compute the iterated discrete degree of symmetry and show that it can be used to distinguish the four cases of the above classification. We start by providing a different proof that
\operatorname{Out}(\pi_{1}(M))
is Minkowski if
M
belongs to one of these four cases.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
M
be a closed
3
-dimensional aspherical manifold with an effective
S^{1}
-action. Then
\mathcal{Z}\pi_{1}(M)
is finitely generated and
\operatorname{Out}(\pi_{1}(M))
is Minkowski.

Proof.

In the first three cases the fundamental group is polycyclic and therefore
\operatorname{Out}(\pi_{1}(M))
is Minkowski (this is a consequence of Minkowski’s lemma and
[
Weh94
]
). We only need to check the case where we have a short exact sequence
1\longrightarrow\mathcal{Z}\pi_{1}(M)\cong\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow\pi_{1}(M)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Inn}(\pi_{1}(M))\longrightarrow 1
and
\operatorname{Inn}(\pi_{1}(M))
is centreless. Since
\mathcal{Z}\pi_{1}(M)
is a characteristic subgroup of
\pi_{1}(M)
then there are short exact sequences

1\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow\operatorname{Out}(\pi_{1}(M))\longrightarrow\operatorname{Out}(\operatorname{Inn}\pi_{1}(M))\longrightarrow 1

and

1\longrightarrow\overline{H}^{1}(\operatorname{Inn}\pi_{1}(M),\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}(1,\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow 1.

Recall that
\overline{H}^{1}(\operatorname{Inn}\pi_{1}(M),\mathbb{Z})\cong Z^{1}(\operatorname{Inn}\pi_{1}(M),\mathbb{Z})/\overline{B}^{1}(\operatorname{Inn}\pi_{1}(M),\mathbb{Z})
and

\overline{B}^{1}(\operatorname{Inn}\pi_{1}(M),\mathbb{Z})\cong p^{-1}(\mathcal{Z}\operatorname{Inn}\pi_{1}(M)\cap C_{\pi_{1}(M)}(\mathcal{Z}\pi_{1}(M)))/\mathcal{Z}\pi_{1}(M)

where
p:\pi_{1}(M)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Inn}\pi_{1}(M)
is the quotient map. Since
\mathcal{Z}\operatorname{Inn}\pi_{1}(M)
is trivial, the group
\overline{B}^{1}(\operatorname{Inn}\pi_{1}(M),\mathbb{Z})
is also trivial and

\overline{H}^{1}(\operatorname{Inn}\pi_{1}(M),\mathbb{Z})\cong Z^{1}(\operatorname{Inn}\pi_{1}(M),\mathbb{Z})\cong H^{1}(\operatorname{Inn}\pi_{1}(M),\mathbb{Z}).

The group
\operatorname{Out}(\pi_{1}(M))
will be Minkowski if
\operatorname{Out}(\operatorname{Inn}\pi_{1}(M))
and

are Minkowski. Since
\operatorname{Inn}\pi_{1}(M)
acts effectively, properly and cocompactly on

,
\operatorname{Inn}\pi_{1}(M)
is a subgroup of isometries of the Euclidean plane or the hyperbolic plane. In both cases

is a finitely generated abelian group and therefore Minkowski.

If
\operatorname{Inn}\pi_{1}(M)
is a subgroup of isometries of the Euclidean plane then it is virtually abelian and therefore
\operatorname{Out}(\operatorname{Inn}\pi_{1}(M))
is Minkowski.
If
\operatorname{Inn}\pi_{1}(M)
is a subgroup of isometries of the hyperbolic plane, then it contains a centreless torsion-free Fuchsian subgroup

Theorem 5.9
.

There exists a
3
-dimensional solvmanifold
M
such that
l(M)=\infty
.

Proof.

Consider the 3-dimensional solvable Lie group
R=\operatorname{Sol}^{3}
. Explicitly,
R\cong\mathbb{R}^{2}\rtimes_{\psi}\mathbb{R}
, where

\psi(t)=\left(\begin{matrix}e^{-t}&0\\
0&e^{t}\end{matrix}\right).

Any lattice of
R
is isomorphic to a semi-direct
\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
, where
\phi:\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
satisfies
\operatorname{tr}(\phi(1))>2
(see
[
LT15
, §2]
). Two of these lattices
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are isomorphic if and only if
\phi^{\prime}(1)
is conjugate to
\phi(1)
or
\phi(1)^{t}
.

We take the matrix

A=\left(\begin{matrix}5&2\\
2&1\end{matrix}\right)

and the lattice
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
of
R
where
\phi(1)=A
. We are going to see that the solvmanifold
M=R/\Gamma
satisfies
l(M)=\infty
. We start by studying the lattice
\Gamma
and some of its sublattices.

For an integer
k\geq 0
we define the group
\Gamma_{k}=2^{k}(\mathbb{Z})^{2}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
. We have a series
\Gamma=\Gamma_{0}\geq\Gamma_{1}\geq\Gamma_{2}\geq\dots
. Firstly, we note that the group morphism
f_{k}:\Gamma\longrightarrow\Gamma_{k}
such that
f_{k}(v,t)=(2^{k}v,t)
is an isomorphism for all
k\geq 0
, which implies that
R/\Gamma
and
R/\Gamma_{k}
are diffeomorphic for all
k\geq 0
.

We claim now that the normalizer
N_{\Gamma}(\Gamma_{k})=\Gamma_{k-1}
. Firstly, a computation shows that:

1.

If
(v,t)\in\Gamma
then
(v,t)^{-1}=(-A^{-t}v,-t)
.

2.

If
(v,t),(w,s)\in\Gamma
then
(v,t)(w,s)(v,t)^{-1}=((Id-A^{s})v+A^{t}w,s)
.

Therefore, the normalizer takes the form

N_{\Gamma}(\Gamma_{k})=\{(v,t)\in\Gamma:(Id-A^{s})v\in 2^{k}\mathbb{Z}^{2}\text{ for all $s$}\}.

The matrix
A
is of the form
A=Id+B
with

B=\left(\begin{matrix}4&2\\
2&0\end{matrix}\right)\in M_{2\times 2}(2\mathbb{Z})

which implies that
Id-A^{s}=-\sum_{i=1}^{s}\binom{s}{i}B^{s}\in M_{2\times 2}(2\mathbb{Z})
for all
s\geq 1
(and we have a similar form for
A^{-s}
). Let
(v,t)\in N_{\Gamma}(\Gamma_{k})
with
v=(v_{1},v_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Since
(Id-A^{s})v\in 2^{k}\mathbb{Z}^{2}
for all
s
, in particular
-Bv=(Id-A)v\in 2^{k}\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. We obtain that
2v_{1},2v_{2}\in 2^{k}\mathbb{Z}
and therefore
v_{1},v_{2}\in 2^{k-1}\mathbb{Z}
. Moreover, if
v\in 2^{k-1}\mathbb{Z}^{2}
then
(Id-A^{s})v\in 2^{k}\mathbb{Z}^{2}
for all
s
. Thus, we have seen that

N_{\Gamma}(\Gamma_{k})=\{(v,t)\in\Gamma:v\in 2^{k-1}\mathbb{Z}^{2}\}=\Gamma_{k-1}.

In consequence,
\Gamma_{k}\trianglelefteq\Gamma_{k-1}
and
\Gamma_{k}\ntrianglelefteq\Gamma_{k-i}
for
i>1
. In addition,
\Gamma_{k-1}/\Gamma_{k}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2
. If
\pi_{k}:\Gamma_{k-1}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}/2\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2
denotes the quotient map, then we can define new lattices

\Gamma_{k}^{(1,0)}=\pi_{k}^{-1}(\langle(1,0)\rangle)=(2^{k}\mathbb{Z}\times 2^{k-1}\mathbb{Z})\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z},

\Gamma_{k}^{(0,1)}=\pi_{k}^{-1}(\langle(0,1)\rangle)=(2^{k-1}\mathbb{Z}\times 2^{k}\mathbb{Z})\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z},

\Gamma_{k}^{(1,1)}=\pi_{k}^{-1}(\langle(1,1)\rangle)=\{((v_{1},v_{2}),t)\in\Gamma_{k-1}:v_{1}+v_{2}\in 2^{k}\mathbb{Z}\}.

Analogous computations show that
N_{\Gamma}(\Gamma_{k}^{(i,j)})=\Gamma_{k-1}^{(i,j)}
and
\Gamma_{k}^{(i,j)}/\Gamma_{k-1}^{(i,j)}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2
for all
(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}/2\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2
, where we set
\Gamma_{k}^{(0,0)}=\Gamma_{k-1}
.

We consider the regular
\mathbb{Z}/2\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2
self-covering
p:R/\Gamma_{1}\cong M\longrightarrow R/\Gamma\cong M
. We can obtain a tower of regular self-coverings
p^{k}:R/\Gamma_{k}\cong M\longrightarrow R/\Gamma\cong M
, which has associated a free iterated action of
\mathcal{G}_{k}=\{\mathbb{Z}/2\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2,\dots,\mathbb{Z}/2\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2\}
with
l(\mathcal{G}_{k})=k
on
M
.

Let
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=\{G_{1}^{\prime},\dots,G_{m}^{\prime}\}\curvearrowright M
be a free iterated action equivalent to
\mathcal{G}_{k}\curvearrowright M
. Then we have a subnormal series
\Gamma_{k}=\Lambda_{0}\trianglelefteq\Lambda_{1}\trianglelefteq\cdots\trianglelefteq\Lambda_{m}=\Gamma
such that
\Lambda_{i}/\Lambda_{i-1}\cong G_{i}^{\prime}
. We have
\Lambda_{1}\leq N_{\Gamma}(\Gamma_{k})
and therefore
\Lambda_{1}=\Gamma_{k}^{(i,j)}
for some
(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}/2\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2
. In consequence,
|G_{1}^{\prime}|\leq 4
. We can repeat the same process with
\Lambda_{1}=\Gamma_{k}^{(i,j)}
, since
N_{\Gamma}(\Gamma_{k}^{(i,j)})=\Gamma_{k-1}^{(i,j)}
. Repeating this process we find that all the subgroups of the subnormal series are of the form
\Gamma_{a}^{(i,j)}
. This implies that
|G_{i}^{\prime}|\leq 4
for all
1\leq i\leq m
.

Finally, if
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\curvearrowright M
satisfies that
m=l(\mathcal{G}_{k}\curvearrowright M)
then
[\Gamma:\Gamma_{k}]=4^{k}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}|G_{i}|\leq 4^{m}
, which implies that
l(\mathcal{G}_{k}\curvearrowright M)=k
. Since
k
can be chosen to be arbitrarily large, we obtain that
l(M)=\infty
.
∎

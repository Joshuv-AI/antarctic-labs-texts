On the distribution of shapes of pure quartic number fields
Sudipa Das, Sushant Kala, Arunabha Mukhopadhyay, Anwesh Ray

Abstract
. The shape of a number field is a subtle arithmetic invariant arising from the geometry of numbers. It is defined as the equivalence class of the lattice of integers with respect to linear operations that are composites of rotations, reflections, and positive scalar dilations. For a number field of degree n n , the shape is a point in the space of shapes 𝒮 n − 1 \mathcal{S}_{n-1} , which is the double quotient GL n − 1 ⁡ ( ℤ ) \ GL n − 1 ⁡ ( ℝ ) / GO n − 1 ⁡ ( ℝ ) \operatorname{GL}_{n-1}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\operatorname{GL}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})/\operatorname{GO}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R}) . In this paper, we investigate the distribution of shapes in the family of pure quartic fields K m = ℚ ⁡ ( m 4 ) K_{m}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{m}) . We prove that the shape of K m K_{m} lies on one of ten explicitly described torus orbits in 𝒮 3 \mathcal{S}_{3} , determined by the sign and residue class of m mod 32 m\bmod 32 . It is shown that the shape on a given torus orbit is completely determined by two parameters, one of which varies continuously, while the other takes values in a discrete set. As a result, the distribution of shapes in this family is governed by a product of a continuous and a discrete measure. Our results shed new light on a question posed by Manjul Bhargava and Piper H concerning the distribution of shapes in families of non-generic number fields of fixed degree. Notably, the limiting distribution in our case does not arise as the restriction of the natural measure on 𝒮 3 \mathcal{S}_{3} induced by Haar measure on GL 3 ⁡ ( ℝ ) \operatorname{GL}_{3}(\mathbb{R}) .

1.1.
Motivation and historical context

The shape of a number field is a subtle arithmetic invariant which arises from the geometry of numbers. Given an inner product space
V\simeq\mathbb{R}^{r}
, the
shape
of a full rank lattice
\Lambda\subset V
is an invariant which is unchanged by rotation, reflection and positive scalar dilations. Formally, the space of all such shapes is given by the double coset space

\mathcal{S}_{r}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{R})/\operatorname{GO}_{r}(\mathbb{R}),

where
\operatorname{GO}_{r}(\mathbb{R})\subset\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{R})
denotes the group of matrices that preserve the standard inner product up to a positive scalar multiple.
Let
K
be a number field of degree
n
and ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. One can embed
K
into its Minkowski space

j\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}K\rightarrow K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{n}

and thus associate a lattice
j(\mathcal{O}_{K})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
K
. Here,
K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}
is endowed with a natural inner product which arises from the trace form on
K
. The
shape

K
is defined to be the shape of orthogonal projection of
j(\mathcal{O}_{K})
onto the hyperplane perpendicular to the vector
j(1)
.

All quadratic number fields have the same shape since all rank
1
lattices are dilations of a fixed lattice. For cubic number fields
K
, the associated rank
2
lattice is represented by a point in the complex upper half plane
\mathbb{H}
. The shape of
K
is represented by a point in the fundamental domain

\mathcal{F}_{2}=\{x+iy\in\mathbb{H}\mid x\in[0,1/2),\quad x^{2}+y^{2}\geq 1\},

which comes equipped with the hyperbolic measure, which is induced from the Haar measure on
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
D. Terr first introduced the study of the shape of a number field in his PhD thesis
[
Ter97
]
, written under the supervision of H. Lenstra. He proved that the shapes of cubic fields are equidistributed in the space of shapes of rank-
2
lattices. Furthermore, he showed that the shape associated to any Galois cubic field is always hexagonal. M. Bhargava and A. Shnidman
[
BS14
]
classified the shapes of cubic orders whose automorphism group is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
, and also established asymptotics for the number of such number fields. In a related development, G. Mantilla-Soler and M. Monsurró
[
MSM16
]
determined the shapes of cyclic number fields of prime degree
\ell
.

M. Bhargava and Piper H. extended Terr’s result to
S_{n}
number fields of degree
n=4,5
and conjectured that such equidistribution results hold for all
n\geq 6
as well. In greater detail, let
\mu
denote the measure on
\mathcal{S}_{n-1}
induced from the Haar measure on
\operatorname{GL}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})
. An
n
-ic field is a number field
K/\mathbb{Q}
of degree
n
. Given an
n
-ic field
K
, denote by
\widetilde{K}
its Galois closure over
\mathbb{Q}
and set

\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(\widetilde{K}/\mathbb{Q}).

We note that
\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})
acts faithfully on the
n
-embeddings
K\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
and may thus be viewed as a subgroup of
S_{n}
. For

and a positive real number

-ic extensions of

\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N_{n}^{(i)}(X,Y)}{N_{n}^{(i)}(X)}=\frac{\mu(Y)}{\mu(\mathcal{S}_{n-1})}.

1.2.
Main result

In this article, we study the distribution of shapes of the rings of integers of pure quartic number fields
K_{m}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{m})
, where
m

\pm

(1)

\rm{I}\pm

(2)

1.3.
Organization

Including the introduction, this article is divided into four sections. In §
2
, we review foundational concepts related to the shape of a number field. In §
3
, we compute the Gram matrices associated to pure quartic fields, using the explicit integral bases constructed in
[
Fun84
]
. This leads to a complete classification of the shapes of pure quartic fields into ten distinct classes. The main distribution results are established in §

2.
Shapes of number fields

This section introduces notation and reviews background material on the shapes of number fields. Fix an integer
r\geq 2
, and let
M_{r}(\mathbb{R})
denote the space of real
r\times r
matrices. The group
\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{R})
consists of all invertible elements of
M_{r}(\mathbb{R})
, and denote by
\operatorname{I}_{r}
the identity matrix. For any matrix
M\in M_{r}(\mathbb{R})
, write
M^{T}
for its transpose. Let
V\simeq\mathbb{R}^{r}
be an inner product space with standard orthonormal basis
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{r}\}
, so that

(e_{i},e_{j})=\delta_{i,j}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }i=j,\\
0&\text{ if }i\neq j.\end{cases}

Let
\operatorname{GO}_{r}(\mathbb{R})
be the subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{R})
consisting of matrices
M
satisfying
M^{T}M=\lambda\operatorname{I}_{r}
for some scalar
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
; these are precisely the invertible linear transformations preserving the inner product up to a positive scaling.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Lambda\subset V
be a lattice. The
shape
of
\Lambda
, denoted
\operatorname{sh}_{\Lambda}
, is its equivalence class under the action of rotations, reflections, and positive scalar dilations. The space of all such shapes is the double coset space

\mathcal{S}_{r}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{R})/\operatorname{GO}_{r}(\mathbb{R}).

Given a basis
\{b_{1},\dots,b_{r}\}
for
\Lambda
, we express each
b_{i}
in terms of the standard basis as
b_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}a_{i,j}e_{j}
. The matrix
(a_{i,j})\in\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{R})
then determines a point in
\mathcal{S}_{r}
, namely the shape of
\Lambda
. This space is equipped with a natural measure
\mu_{\mathcal{S}_{r}}
induced from Haar measure on
\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{R})
.

Given any measurable subset
Y\subset\mathcal{S}_{r}
, we write
\mu(Y)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mu_{\mathcal{S}_{r}}(Y)
for its measure.

It is often convenient to describe
\mathcal{S}_{r}
as the quotient of a discrete group acting on a homogenous space
\mathcal{H}_{r}
. Let
\mathcal{P}_{r}
denote the space of positive definite
r\times r
symmetric real matrices. The group
\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{Z})
acts on
\mathcal{P}_{r}
by
G\mapsto M^{T}GM
, while
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}
acts by scaling,
G\mapsto\lambda^{2}G
. Set
\mathcal{H}_{r}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathcal{P}_{r}/\mathbb{R}^{\times}
and note that
\mathcal{H}_{r}
inherits a natural
\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{Z})
-action. We identify
\mathcal{H}_{r}
with a subset of affine space
\mathbb{A}^{r^{2}}=\operatorname{M}_{r}(\mathbb{R})
as follows:

\mathcal{H}_{r}=\{M\in\operatorname{M}_{r}(\mathbb{R})\mid M\text{ is positive definite and symmetric with }\det M=1\}.

There is a natural bijection
\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{R})/\operatorname{GO}_{r}(\mathbb{R})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{H}_{r}
given by
M\mapsto|\det M|^{-2/r}M^{T}M
, inducing an identification
\mathcal{S}_{r}\simeq\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathcal{H}_{r}
. This identification can be made explicit as follows. If
\Lambda
is a lattice with basis
\{b_{1},\dots,b_{r}\}
, its Gram matrix
\operatorname{Gr}(\Lambda)
is defined by
(\langle b_{i},b_{j}\rangle)_{i,j}
, and the shape of
\Lambda
corresponds to the class of
\operatorname{Gr}(\Lambda)
in
\mathcal{S}_{r}
. This class is invariant under change of basis by
M\in\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{Z})
and rescaling by
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}
, as is seen from the following relation

\operatorname{Gr}(\lambda M\cdot\Lambda)=\lambda^{2}M^{T}\operatorname{Gr}(\Lambda)M,

demonstrating that
\operatorname{sh}_{\Lambda}
is indeed well defined.

The description of
\mathcal{H}_{2}
is quite simple, since it consists of matrices
\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
b&c\\
\end{array}}\right)\in\operatorname{M}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
with
a>0
and
ac-b^{2}=1
. Let
\mathbb{H}
denote the upper half plane in
\mathbb{C}
. Let
\operatorname{GL}_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{R})
be the subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
consisting of invertible matrices with positive determinant. There is a natural action of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathbb{H}
by fractional linear transformations:

\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\\
\end{array}}\right)(z)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left(\frac{\alpha z+\beta}{\gamma z+\delta}\right).

This action is transitive and the stabilizer of

\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})/\operatorname{GO}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{H}

\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\\
\end{array}}\right)\mapsto\left(\frac{\alpha z+\beta}{\gamma z+\delta}\right)

\end{array}}\right)\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}y^{1/2}&0\\

\end{array}}\right),

\end{array}}\right)=MM^{T}=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}(y+y^{-1}x^{2})&y^{-1}x\\

\end{array}}\right).

There is a

\mathcal{H}_{2}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{H}

\end{array}}\right)

\mathcal{F}_{2}=\{x+iy\mid x\in[0,1/2),\quad y>0,\quad x^{2}+y^{2}\geq 1\}.

For

\operatorname{GL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})

\mathcal{H}_{3}

\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})

The resulting fundamental domain

can be viewed as a higher-rank generalization of the classical fundamental domain for the
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-action on the upper half-plane. To realize
\mathcal{H}_{3}
as an analogue of an upper half-space, we adopt Iwasawa coordinates, expressing each point

0&0&y_{3}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&x_{1}&x_{2}\\
0&1&x_{3}\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},

where

A fundamental domain for the action of

on

\mathcal{M}_{3}=\left\{Y=(y_{ij})\in\mathcal{H}_{3}\,\middle|\,\begin{aligned} &y_{11}\leq y_{22}\leq y_{33},\quad 0\leq y_{12}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}y_{11},\\

&e^{t}Ye\geq y_{33}\quad\text{for all }e=(\pm 1,\pm 1,\pm 1)\end{aligned}\right\}.

It follows that

(2.1)

0\leqslant x_{1}\leqslant\frac{1}{2},\quad\left|x_{2}\right|\leqslant\frac{1}{2},\quad 0\leqslant y_{1}x_{1}x_{2}+y_{2}x_{3}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\left(y_{1}x_{1}^{2}+y_{2}\right),

Hence, we get

\mathcal{F}_{3}=\{v=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},y_{1},y_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{5}\ \ \mid\ \ v\ \text{ satisfies \eqref{FD1} and \eqref{FD2}}\ \}.

Now we discuss the shape of a number field
K
with
[K\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Q}]=n
and ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Let
\{1,\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n-1}\}
be an integral basis. Let
\{\sigma_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
denote the set of embeddings of
K
into
\mathbb{C}
. The Minkowski embedding

\displaystyle j\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}K

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{C}^{n}

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle\mapsto\big(\sigma_{1}(\alpha),\dots,\sigma_{n}(\alpha)\big)

identifies
K
with an
n
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The real span of the image, denoted
K_{\mathbb{R}}
, inherits a natural inner-product structure, and the restriction of
j
to
\mathcal{O}_{K}
yields a rank-
n
lattice in
K_{\mathbb{R}}
. Although one might be inclined to define the shape of
K
as the shape of this lattice, such a definition presents difficulties when studying the distribution of shapes across a family of number fields. This is because each lattice necessarily contains the vector
j(1)
, this introduces a nontrivial constraint, disrupting randomness in the distribution of shapes. To circumvent this issue, we instead define the shape of
K
in terms of a sublattice obtained by projecting
j(\mathcal{O}_{K})
onto the orthogonal complement of
j(1)
. This is accomplished via the map

\alpha^{\perp}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=n\alpha-\operatorname{tr}(\alpha),

which sends elements of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
to those of trace zero. The set
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\perp}
, consisting of the images of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
under this map, forms a rank
r\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(n-1)
submodule of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
.

Definition 2.2
.

We define the shape of
K
, denoted
\operatorname{sh}_{K}
, to be the shape of the lattice
j(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\perp})
in
\mathcal{S}_{r}
.

Given a basis
\{1,\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n-1}\}
of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, the corresponding basis for
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\perp}
is given by
\{\alpha_{1}^{\perp},\dots,\alpha_{n-1}^{\perp}\}
. As we discussed above, the Gram matrix is given as follows:

(2.3)

\operatorname{Gr}\left(j\left(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\perp}\right)\right)=\big(\langle j(\alpha_{i}^{\perp}),j(\alpha_{j}^{\perp})\rangle\big).

Proposition 3.3
.

The Gram matrix of the basis
\mathcal{B}_{\rm{II}}=\{1,\alpha,\beta,\gamma\}
is given by :

\mathcal{P}_{\rm{II},+}=\begin{pmatrix}4&0&0&0\\
0&4\alpha^{2}&0&0\\
0&0&4\beta^{2}&0\\
0&0&0&4\gamma^{2}\end{pmatrix},\ \ \mathcal{P}_{\rm{II},-}=\begin{pmatrix}4&0&0&0\\
0&4|\alpha|^{2}&0&0\\
0&0&4|\beta|^{2}&0\\
0&0&0&4|\gamma|^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

Proof.

Let
m>0
. Then we have:

(1)

j_{1}(1)=(1,1,1,1)
,

(2)

j_{1}(\alpha)=(\alpha,\alpha,-\alpha,-\alpha)
,

(3)

j_{1}(\beta)=(\beta,-\beta,i\beta,-i\beta)
,

(4)

j_{1}(\gamma)=(\gamma,-\gamma,-i\gamma,i\gamma)
.

Taking the standard Hermitian inner product on
\mathbb{C}^{4}
, we compute:

\langle j_{1}(\lambda),j_{1}(\lambda)\rangle=4\lambda^{2}

for each
\lambda\in\{1,\alpha,\beta,\gamma\}
, and

\langle j_{1}(\lambda_{1}),j_{1}(\lambda_{2})\rangle=0

for all distinct
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\{1,\alpha,\beta,\gamma\}
. That is, the vectors
j_{1}(1),j_{1}(\alpha),j_{1}(\beta),j_{1}(\gamma)
form an orthogonal set, and the corresponding Gram matrix is diagonal with entries
4\lambda^{2}
on the diagonal, as claimed.

Now suppose
m<0
. Then:

(1)

j_{2}(1)=(1,1,1,1)
,

(2)

j_{2}(\beta)=(\beta,i\beta,-i\bar{\beta},\bar{\beta})
,

(3)

j_{2}(\alpha)=\left(\frac{\beta^{2}}{bc},-\frac{\beta^{2}}{bc},-\frac{\bar{\beta}^{2}}{bc},\frac{\bar{\beta}^{2}}{bc}\right)
,

(4)

j_{2}(\gamma)=\left(\frac{\beta^{3}}{bc^{2}},i\frac{\beta^{3}}{bc^{2}},-i\frac{\bar{\beta}^{3}}{bc^{2}},\frac{\bar{\beta}^{3}}{bc^{2}}\right)
.

It is easy to see that the vectors
j_{2}(\alpha),j_{2}(\beta),j_{2}(\gamma)
are mutually orthogonal with respect to the standard Hermitian inner product. The squared norm of
j_{2}(\alpha)
is:

\displaystyle\langle j_{2}(\alpha),j_{2}(\alpha)\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\left(\frac{\beta^{2}}{bc},-\frac{\beta^{2}}{bc},-\frac{\bar{\beta}^{2}}{bc},\frac{\bar{\beta}^{2}}{bc}\right),\left(\frac{\beta^{2}}{bc},-\frac{\beta^{2}}{bc},-\frac{\bar{\beta}^{2}}{bc},\frac{\bar{\beta}^{2}}{bc}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(bc)^{2}}\left(|\beta^{2}|^{2}+|\beta^{2}|^{2}+|\bar{\beta}^{2}|^{2}+|\bar{\beta}^{2}|^{2}\right)=4\left(\frac{|\beta|^{2}}{bc}\right)^{2}=4|\alpha|^{2}.

A similar computation gives:

\langle j_{2}(\beta),j_{2}(\beta)\rangle=4|\beta|^{2},\quad\langle j_{2}(\gamma),j_{2}(\gamma)\rangle=4|\gamma|^{2}.

Hence, the Gram matrix of the set
\{j_{2}(1),j_{2}(\alpha),j_{2}(\beta),j_{2}(\gamma)\}
is diagonal with entries
4|\lambda|^{2}
, and the vectors are pairwise orthogonal as desired.

∎

Distribution results

For the sake of discussion, assume that
m>0
and

. The shape
\operatorname{sh}_{m}
of
j(\mathcal{O}_{K_{m}})^{\perp}\in\mathcal{S}_{3}
, is given by

\operatorname{sh}_{m}=\left({\begin{array}[]{ccc}\alpha^{2}&&\\
&\beta^{2}&\\
&&\gamma^{2}\\
\end{array}}\right)=\left({\begin{array}[]{ccc}\beta^{4}/(bc)^{2}&&\\
&\beta^{2}&\\
&&\beta^{6}/(bc^{2})^{2}\\
\end{array}}\right).

After permuting the variables, we have that:

\operatorname{sh}_{m}=\left({\begin{array}[]{ccc}\gamma_{1}^{2}&&\\
&\gamma_{2}^{2}&\\
&&\gamma_{3}^{2}\\
\end{array}}\right)

where
\gamma_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\beta
,
\gamma_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\beta^{2}/bc
and
\gamma_{3}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\beta^{3}/bc^{2}
.
We scale this matrix by dividing by
(abc)
. Let
\lambda_{i}=\gamma_{i}^{2}/(abc)
, we find that
\operatorname{sh}_{m}=\operatorname{diag}\left(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}\right)
,

\begin{split}&\lambda_{1}^{2}=\frac{\beta^{4}}{(abc)^{2}}=\frac{ab^{2}c^{3}}{a^{2}b^{2}c^{2}}=\frac{c}{a},\\
&\lambda_{2}=\frac{\beta^{4}}{(bc)^{2}(abc)}=\frac{ab^{2}c^{3}}{ab^{3}c^{3}}=\frac{1}{b},\\
&\lambda_{3}^{2}=\frac{\beta^{12}}{(bc^{2})^{4}(abc)^{2}}=\frac{a^{3}b^{6}c^{9}}{a^{2}b^{6}c^{10}}=\frac{a}{c}.\end{split}

Since
\beta
is real and
a,b,c>0
, we find that
\lambda_{i}>0
for
i=1,2,3
, and therefore,

\lambda_{1}=\sqrt{\frac{c}{a}},\quad\lambda_{2}=\frac{1}{b}\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda_{3}=\sqrt{\frac{a}{c}}.

We find that

\operatorname{sh}_{m}=\left({\begin{array}[]{ccc}\sqrt{\frac{c}{a}}&&\\
&\frac{1}{b}&\\
&&\sqrt{\frac{a}{c}}\\
\end{array}}\right),

we refer to the pair

(4.1)

\lambda(m)=(\lambda_{1}(m),\lambda_{2}(m))=\left(\sqrt{\frac{c}{a}},\frac{1}{b}\right)

as the

.

In the more general case, suppose that

is of Type
\ast
. Assume that the conditions (i’)-(iv’) are satisfied and set

\lambda(m)=(\lambda_{1}(m),\lambda_{2}(m))=\left(\sqrt{\frac{c}{|a|}},\frac{1}{b}\right).

The shape of

is then given by the projection of the matrix

C_{\ast}\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&0\\
0&\sqrt{c/|a|}&0&0\\
0&0&1/b&0\\
0&0&0&\sqrt{|a|/c}\end{pmatrix}C_{\ast}^{T}

to the orthogonal complement of the vector
(1,1,1,1)
.

Let
\mathcal{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a closed and bounded region whose boundary is rectifiable and has total length
L
. The result below is often attributed to Lipschitz, with an early reference appearing in the work of Bachmann
[
Bac04
]
, who credits Lipschitz as the source (see also
[
Dav51
]
).

Lemma 4.1

(Lipschitz Principle)
.

Let
\mathcal{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be as above. Then the number of integer lattice points contained in
\mathcal{R}
satisfies

\left|\#(\mathcal{R}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2})-\operatorname{Area}(\mathcal{R})\right|\leq 4(L+1).

Consider the torus orbit
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{H}_{3}
consisting of diagonal matrices of the form

D(x_{1},x_{2})=x_{2}^{1/3}\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}^{-1}&&\\
&x_{2}^{-1}&\\
&&x_{1}\end{pmatrix}.

Define

\psi(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\lfloor x\rfloor}\alpha(n),\quad\text{where}\quad\alpha(n)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}n^{-2/3}\displaystyle\prod_{\ell\mid n}\left(\dfrac{\ell-1}{\ell+1}\right)&\text{if }n\text{ is squarefree},\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

and the product is over prime divisors
\ell
of
n
. Let
\hat{\mu}
denote the measure
\frac{\alpha(\lfloor x_{2}\rfloor)}{x_{1}}\,dx_{1}\,dx_{2}
on
\mathcal{T}
.

Given a pair
(R_{1},R_{2})
, define the region

W(R_{1},R_{2})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left\{D(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathcal{T}\;\middle|\;x_{1}^{2}\in[1,R_{1}],\;x_{2}\in[1,R_{2}]\right\}.

Then we find that

\hat{\mu}\left(W(R_{1},R_{2})\right)=\int_{1}^{R_{2}}\int_{1}^{\sqrt{R_{1}}}\frac{\alpha(\lfloor x_{2}\rfloor)}{x_{1}}\,dx_{1}\,dx_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\log R_{1}\cdot\psi(R_{2}).

Let
\mathcal{R}(N,R_{1},R_{2})
be defined as follows:

\mathcal{R}(N,R_{1},R_{2})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{(a,b,c)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}_{\geq 1}\mid ab^{2/3}c<N,\quad\frac{a}{c}\in[1,R_{1}]\quad\text{and }\quad b\in[1,R_{2}]\}.

Let
\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{Z}^{3}}(N,R_{1},R_{2})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathcal{R}(N,R_{1},R_{2})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{3}
. For
M,R\geq 1
, define

\mathcal{S}(M,R)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left\{(a,c)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}^{2}\,\middle|\,ac<M,\;\frac{a}{c}\in[1,R]\right\},

and let
\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}(M,R)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathcal{S}(M,R)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Then we have the identity

(4.2)

\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{Z}^{3}}(N,R_{1},R_{2})=\bigsqcup_{b=1}^{\lfloor R_{2}\rfloor}\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\left(\frac{N}{b^{2/3}},R_{1}\right),

where the union is disjoint.

Lemma 4.2
.

The area of
\mathcal{S}(M,R)
is given by:

\operatorname{Area}\left(\mathcal{S}(M,R)\right)=\begin{cases}\frac{M}{2}\log R-\frac{(R-1)}{2}&\text{ if }M\geq R;\\
\frac{M}{2}\log M-\frac{(M-1)}{2}&\text{ if }M<R.\\
\end{cases}

Proof.

Note that for any fixed
c\in[1,\sqrt{M}]
, the condition
ac<M
and
\frac{a}{c}\in[1,R]
together imply that

a\in\left[c,\min(Rc,M/c)\right].

We begin by analyzing the case
M\geq R
. Accordingly, we split the region of integration into two parts:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Area}(\mathcal{S}(M,R))

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{\sqrt{M/R}}\int_{c}^{Rc}\,da\,dc+\int_{\sqrt{M/R}}^{\sqrt{M}}\int_{c}^{M/c}\,da\,dc

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{\sqrt{M/R}}(R-1)c\,dc+\int_{\sqrt{M/R}}^{\sqrt{M}}\left(\frac{M}{c}-c\right)dc

\displaystyle=\frac{R-1}{2}\left(\frac{M}{R}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(M-\frac{M}{R}\right)+\frac{M}{2}\log R

\displaystyle=\frac{M}{2}\log R-\frac{R-1}{2}.

Next, suppose
M<R
. In this case, for all
c\in[1,\sqrt{M}]
, we have
M/c\leq Rc
, so the upper bound of the inner integral is simply
M/c
. Thus,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Area}(\mathcal{S}(M,R))

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{\sqrt{M}}\int_{c}^{M/c}\,da\,dc=\int_{1}^{\sqrt{M}}\left(\frac{M}{c}-c\right)dc

\displaystyle=M\log\sqrt{M}-\frac{1}{2}(M-1)=\frac{M}{2}\log M-\frac{M-1}{2}.

∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
L=L(M,R)
be the length of the boundary of
\mathcal{S}(M,R)
. Then, we have that

L\leq\left(\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{1+R^{2}}\right)(\sqrt{M}-1)+(R-1).

Proof.

Assume that
M\geq R
. Then the total boundary length is given by
L=L_{1}+L_{2}+L_{3}+L_{4}
, where the individual contributions are as follows:

•

L_{1}=\operatorname{length}\{(c,c)\mid c\in[1,\sqrt{M}]\}=\sqrt{2}(\sqrt{M}-1)
,

•

L_{2}=\text{length}\{(c,Rc)\mid c\in[1,\sqrt{M/R}]\}=\sqrt{1+R^{2}}(\sqrt{M/R}-1)
,

•

L_{3}=\operatorname{length}\{(c,M/c)\mid c\in[\sqrt{M/R},\sqrt{M}]\}=\int_{\sqrt{M/R}}^{\sqrt{M}}\sqrt{1+\left(\frac{M}{c^{2}}\right)^{2}}\,dc\leq\sqrt{1+R^{2}}\left(\sqrt{M}-\sqrt{M/R}\right)
,

•

L_{4}=R-1
is the length of the vertical segment from
(1,1)
to
(1,R)
.

Thus, the total length is given by

L\leq\left(\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{1+R^{2}}\right)(\sqrt{M}-1)+(R-1).

Similar analysis applies in the case when
M<R
to give the result.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

We have that

\#\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{Z}^{3}}(N,R_{1},R_{2})=\frac{N}{2}\log R_{1}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\lfloor R_{2}\rfloor}n^{-2/3}\right)+O_{R_{1},R_{2}}(\sqrt{N}).

Proof.

From Lemma
4.1
and Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
, it follows that

\left|\#\left(\mathcal{S}(M,R)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2}\right)-\frac{M}{2}\log R\right|\leq 4(L+1)\leq CR\sqrt{M},

where
C>0
is an absolute constant.
Noting that

\#\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{Z}^{3}}(N,R_{1},R_{2})=\sum_{b=1}^{\lfloor R_{2}\rfloor}\#\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}(N/b^{2/3},R_{1}),

we deduce that

\left|\#\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{Z}^{3}}(N,R_{1},R_{2})-\frac{N}{2}\left(\sum_{b=1}^{\lfloor R_{2}\rfloor}b^{-2/3}\right)\log R_{1}\right|\leq CR_{1}\sqrt{R_{2}}\sqrt{N}.

∎

Remark 4.5
.

Higher-dimensional analogues of Lipschitz’s principle, due to Davenport
[
Dav51
]
, allow for effective volume estimates by bounding the lower-dimensional projections (or "shadows") of a region containing the lattice points; see, for example,
[
Hol22

(a,c)
-plane is too large to yield meaningful results.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
\ell

\ell

(a,b,c)

ab
,
bc
, and
ca
is squarefree. Equivalently,
(a,b,c)
is carefree if and only if it is
\ell

\ell
.

Given a positive integer

n

\ell

n
. Fix a residue class

Lemma 4.7
.

For any odd prime
\ell
, we have

\#\mathcal{A}_{\ell}=(\ell-1)^{3}(\ell^{3}+3\ell^{2}),\qquad\text{and hence}\qquad\mathfrak{d}_{\ell}=\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell}\right)^{3}\left(1+\frac{3}{\ell}\right).

Proof.

The number of tuples
(\bar{a},\bar{b},\bar{c})\in(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{2}\mathbb{Z})^{3}
such that
\ell\nmid\bar{a}\bar{b}\bar{c}
is
\ell^{3}(\ell-1)^{3}
. The number of triples for which exactly one coordinate is divisible by
\ell
is
3\ell^{2}(\ell-1)^{3}
. In total,
\#\mathcal{A}_{\ell}=(\ell-1)^{3}(\ell^{3}+3\ell^{2})
.
∎

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
n
be an odd squarefree natural number which is divisible by all primes
\ell\leq R_{2}
and
\tau
be a residue class mod
32
such that
8\nmid\tau
. Then for
N>n^{4}R_{1}R_{2}
,

\#\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\tau}(N,R_{1},R_{2})=\frac{N}{2\times 32^{2}}\prod_{\ell|n}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\right)\log R_{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1,\\
b\,\square\text{-free}\end{subarray}}^{\lfloor R_{2}\rfloor}b^{-2/3}n_{\tau}(b)\prod_{\ell|b}\left(\frac{\ell-1}{\ell+1}\right)+O_{n,R_{1},R_{2}}(\sqrt{N}),

where
O_{n,R_{1},R_{2}}(\cdot)
indicates that the constant in the error bound depends on
R_{1}
,
R_{2}
and
n
.

Proof.

Let
\Lambda_{n}
be the lattice
(32n^{2}\mathbb{Z})^{3}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{3}
. Note that
\Omega_{n}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{3}/\Lambda_{n}
choose a set of representatives
\widetilde{\Omega}_{n}\subset([0,32n^{2})\cap\mathbb{Z})^{3}
. We find that

\begin{split}\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\tau}(N,R_{1},R_{2})=&\bigsqcup_{z\in\widetilde{\Omega}_{n}}(z+\Lambda_{n})\cap\mathcal{R}(N,R_{1},R_{2}),\\
=&\bigsqcup_{z\in\widetilde{\Omega}_{n}}\Lambda_{n}\cap\left(\mathcal{R}(N,R_{1},R_{2})-z\right).\end{split}

Observe that

\#\left(\Lambda_{n}\cap\left(\mathcal{R}(N,R_{1},R_{2})-z\right)\right)=\#\left(\mathbb{Z}^{3}\cap\left(\frac{1}{32n^{2}}\cdot\mathcal{R}(N,R_{1},R_{2})-\frac{z}{32n^{2}}\right)\right)

and we estimate

\begin{split}&\#\left(\mathbb{Z}^{3}\cap\left(\frac{1}{32n^{2}}\cdot\mathcal{R}(N,R_{1},R_{2})-\frac{z}{32n^{2}}\right)\right)\\
=&\#\left\{(a+z_{1}/32n^{2},b+z_{2}/32n^{2},c+z_{3}/32n^{2})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{3}\mid ab^{2/3}c<\frac{N}{(32n^{2})^{8/3}},\,a/c\in[1,R_{1}]\,\text{ and }b\in\left[\frac{1}{32n^{2}},\frac{R_{2}}{32n^{2}}\right]\right\}\\

=&\sum_{b^{\prime}=1}^{\lfloor(R_{2}+z_{2})/32^{2}n^{4}\rfloor}\#\left(\mathcal{S}\left(\frac{N}{32^{2}n^{4}(32n^{2}b^{\prime}-z_{2})^{2/3}},R_{1}\right)-\left(\frac{z_{1}}{32n^{2}},\frac{z_{3}}{32n^{2}}\right)\right)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2}\\
=&\sum_{b^{\prime}=1}^{\lfloor(R_{2}+z_{2})/32n^{2}\rfloor}\operatorname{Area}\left(\mathcal{S}\left(\frac{N}{32^{2}n^{4}(32n^{2}b^{\prime}-z_{2})^{2/3}},R_{1}\right)\right)+O\left(\sum_{b^{\prime}=1}^{\lfloor(R_{2}+z_{2})/32n^{2}\rfloor}L\left(\frac{N}{32^{2}n^{4}(32n^{2}b^{\prime}-z_{2})^{2/3}},R_{1}\right)\right),\end{split}

where in the above chain of equalities, we set
b^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=b+z_{2}/32n^{2}
. Note that in the sum,
b
does not take integer values, but
b^{\prime}
does. From Lemma
4.2
, we find that the area of
\mathcal{S}(M,R)
is given by:

\operatorname{Area}\left(\mathcal{S}(M,R)\right)=\begin{cases}\frac{M}{2}\log R-\frac{(R-1)}{2}&\text{ if }M\geq R;\\
\frac{M}{2}\log M-\frac{(M-1)}{2}&\text{ if }M<R.\\
\end{cases}

Since

, we have that

\begin{split}&\#\left(\mathbb{Z}^{3}\cap\left(\frac{1}{32n^{2}}\cdot\mathcal{R}(N,R_{1},R_{2})-\frac{z}{32n^{2}}\right)\right)\\
=&\frac{N\log R_{1}}{2}\frac{1}{32^{2}n^{4}}\left\{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1,\\
b\equiv-z_{2}\mod{32n^{2}}\end{subarray}}^{\lfloor R_{2}\rfloor}b^{-2/3}\right\}+O_{n,R_{1},R_{2}}(\sqrt{N})\\
\end{split}

Thus we find that

\begin{split}\#\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\tau}(N,R_{1},R_{2})=&\frac{N\log R_{1}}{2}\frac{1}{32^{2}n^{4}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1,\\
b\,\square\text{-free}\end{subarray}}^{\lfloor R_{2}\rfloor}\frac{\#\{z\in\widetilde{\Omega}_{n}\mid z_{2}=32n^{2}-b\}}{b^{2/3}}+O_{n,R_{1},R_{2}}(\sqrt{N})\\
=&\frac{N\log R_{1}}{2\times 32^{2}}\frac{1}{n^{4}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1,\\
b\,\square\text{-free}\end{subarray}}^{\lfloor R_{2}\rfloor}\frac{n_{\tau}(b)\times\prod_{\ell|b}(\ell^{2}-\ell)^{2}\times\prod_{\ell|n,\ell\nmid b}\ell^{2}(\ell^{2}-1)}{b^{2/3}}+O_{n,R_{1},R_{2}}(\sqrt{N})\\
=&\frac{N}{2\times 32^{2}}\prod_{\ell|n}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\right)\log R_{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1,\\
b\,\square\text{-free}\end{subarray}}^{\lfloor R_{2}\rfloor}n_{\tau}(b)b^{-2/3}\prod_{\ell|b}\left(\frac{\ell-1}{\ell+1}\right)+O_{n,R_{1},R_{2}}(\sqrt{N}).\\
\end{split}

Lemma 4.9
.

,

(2)

\ell^{2}|b
,

(3)

\ell^{2}|c

and

,

\ell|a
and

\ell|b

+&\sum_{b=1}^{\lfloor R_{2}\rfloor}O\left(L\left(\{(a^{\prime},c)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{2}\mid a^{\prime}c\leq\frac{N}{\ell^{2}b^{2/3}},\quad a^{\prime}/c\in[1/\ell^{2},R_{1}/\ell^{2}]\}\right)\right)\\
=&O_{R_{1},R_{2}}(N/\ell^{2}),\end{split}

where the area and length calculation is similar to that in Lemmas

.
We find that

\begin{split}\#\mathcal{F}_{2}(N,R_{1},R_{2})=&\sum_{b^{\prime}=1}^{\lfloor R_{2}/\ell^{2}\rfloor}\#\{(a,c)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{2}\mid ac\leq\frac{N}{\ell^{4/3}b^{\prime 2/3}},\quad a/c\in[1,R_{1}]\}\\
=&\sum_{b^{\prime}=1}^{\lfloor R_{2}/\ell^{2}\rfloor}O\left(\operatorname{Area}\left(\{(a,c)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{2}\mid ac\leq\frac{N}{\ell^{4/3}b^{\prime 2/3}},\quad a/c\in[1,R_{1}]\}\right)\right)\\
+&\sum_{b^{\prime}=1}^{\lfloor R_{2}/\ell^{2}\rfloor}O\left(L\left(\{(a,c)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{2}\mid ac\leq\frac{N}{\ell^{4/3}b^{\prime 2/3}},\quad a/c\in[1,R_{1}]\}\right)\right)\\
=&O_{R_{1},R_{2}}\left(\frac{N}{\ell^{4/3}}\right)+O_{R_{1},R_{2}}(\sqrt{N}),\\
\#\mathcal{F}_{3}(N,R_{1},R_{2})=&\sum_{b=1}^{\lfloor R_{2}\rfloor}\#\{(a,c^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{2}\mid ac^{\prime}\leq N/\ell^{2}b,\quad a/c^{\prime}\in[1,\ell^{2}R_{1}]\}\\
=&O_{R_{1},R_{2}}(N\log\ell/\ell^{2})+O_{R_{1},R_{2}}(\sqrt{N}),\\
\#\mathcal{F}_{4}(N,R_{1},R_{2})=&\sum_{b^{\prime}=1}^{\lfloor R_{2}/\ell\rfloor}\#\{(a^{\prime},c)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{2}\mid a^{\prime}c\leq N/\ell^{5/3}b^{\prime},\quad a^{\prime}/c\in[1,R_{1}/\ell]\}\\
=&O_{R_{1},R_{2}}(N/\ell^{5/3})+O_{R_{1},R_{2}}(\sqrt{N}),\\
\#\mathcal{F}_{5}(N,R_{1},R_{2})=&\sum_{b=1}^{\lfloor R_{2}\rfloor}\#\{(a^{\prime},c^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{2}\mid a^{\prime}c^{\prime}\leq N/\ell^{2}b,\quad a^{\prime}/c^{\prime}\in[1,R_{1}]\}\\
=&O_{R_{1},R_{2}}(N/\ell^{2})+O_{R_{1},R_{2}}(\sqrt{N}),\\
\#\mathcal{F}_{6}(N,R_{1},R_{2})=&\sum_{b^{\prime}=1}^{\lfloor R_{2}/\ell\rfloor}\#\{(a,c^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{2}\mid ac^{\prime}\leq N/\ell^{5/3}b^{\prime},\quad a/c^{\prime}\in[1,\ell R_{1}]\}\\
=&O_{R_{1},R_{2}}\left(N\log\ell/\ell^{5/3}\right)+O_{R_{1},R_{2}}(\sqrt{N}).\\
\end{split}

Thus putting it all together, one finds that

\frac{\#\mathcal{C}_{\ell}^{\prime}(N,R_{1},R_{2})}{N}=\sum_{i=1}^{6}\frac{\mathcal{F}_{i}(N,R_{1},R_{2})}{N}=O\left(\ell^{-4/3}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right).

∎

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
Y>0
be a positive constant and let
\mathcal{D}_{Y}(N,R_{1},R_{2})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigcup_{\ell>Y}\mathcal{C}_{\ell}^{\prime}(N,R_{1},R_{2})
. Then we find that

\frac{\#\mathcal{D}_{Y}(N,R_{1},R_{2})}{N}=O\left(\sum_{\ell>Y}\ell^{-4/3}+\frac{1}{\log N}\right).

Proof.

Clearly
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}^{\prime}(N,R_{1},R_{2})=0
unless
\ell\ll\sqrt{N}
. Thus from Lemma
4.9
,

\frac{\#\mathcal{D}_{Y}(N,R_{1},R_{2})}{N}=O\left(\sum_{\ell>Y}\ell^{-4/3}+\frac{\pi(\sqrt{N})}{\sqrt{N}}\right).

The result follows from the prime number theorem.
∎

Proposition 4.11
.

We have that

\begin{split}\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{C}^{\tau}(N,R_{1},R_{2})}{N}=&\frac{1}{2048\zeta(2)}\log R_{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1,\\
b\,\square\text{-free}\end{subarray}}^{\lfloor R_{2}\rfloor}n_{\tau}(b)b^{-2/3}\prod_{\ell|b}\left(\frac{\ell-1}{\ell+1}\right),\\
=&\frac{1}{2048\zeta(2)}\log R_{1}\psi_{\tau}(R_{2}),\end{split}

where

\psi_{\tau}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\lfloor x\rfloor}n_{\tau}(n)\alpha(n).

Proof.

Let
n
be a squarefree natural number which is divisible by all primes
\ell\leq R_{2}
. Then for
N>n^{4}R_{1}R_{2}
, Proposition
4.8
asserts that

\#\mathcal{C}_{n}^{\tau}(N,R_{1},R_{2})=\frac{N}{2048}\prod_{\ell|n}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\right)\log R_{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1,\\
b\,\square\text{-free}\end{subarray}}^{\lfloor R_{2}\rfloor}n_{\tau}(b)b^{-2/3}\prod_{\ell|b}\left(\frac{\ell-1}{\ell+1}\right)+O_{n,R_{1},R_{2}}(\sqrt{N}).

For
Y>0
, set
n_{Y}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{\ell\leq Y}\ell
. We find that

\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{C}^{\tau}(N,R_{1},R_{2})}{N}\leq\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{C}_{n_{Y}}^{\tau}(N,R_{1},R_{2})}{N}=\frac{1}{2048}\prod_{\ell\leq Y}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\right)\log R_{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1,\\
b\,\square\text{-free}\end{subarray}}^{\lfloor R_{2}\rfloor}n_{\tau}(b)b^{-2/3}\prod_{\ell|b}\left(\frac{\ell-1}{\ell+1}\right).

Taking the limit of the right hand side as
Y\rightarrow\infty
, we find that

\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{C}^{\tau}(N,R_{1},R_{2})}{N}\leq\frac{1}{2048\zeta(2)}\log R_{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1,\\
b\,\square\text{-free}\end{subarray}}^{\lfloor R_{2}\rfloor}n_{\tau}(b)b^{-2/3}\prod_{\ell|b}\left(\frac{\ell-1}{\ell+1}\right).

On the other hand, since

\mathcal{C}_{n_{Y}}^{\tau}(N,R_{1},R_{2})\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\tau}(N,R_{1},R_{2})\cup\mathcal{D}_{Y}(N,R_{1},R_{2}),

we find that

\begin{split}\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{C}^{\tau}(N,R_{1},R_{2})}{N}&\geq\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{C}_{n_{Y}}(N,R_{1},R_{2})}{N}-\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{D}_{Y}(N,R_{1},R_{2})}{N}\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2048}\prod_{\ell\leq Y}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\right)\log R_{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1,\\
b\,\square\text{-free}\end{subarray}}^{\lfloor R_{2}\rfloor}n_{\tau}(b)b^{-2/3}\prod_{\ell|b}\left(\frac{\ell-1}{\ell+1}\right)-C\sum_{\ell>Y}\ell^{-4/3}\end{split}

for some positive constant
C>0
. Taking the limit of the right hand side with
Y\rightarrow\infty
, we find that

\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{C}^{\tau}(N,R_{1},R_{2})}{N}\geq\frac{1}{2048\zeta(2)}\log R_{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1,\\
b\,\square\text{-free}\end{subarray}}^{\lfloor R_{2}\rfloor}n_{\tau}(b)b^{-2/3}\prod_{\ell|b}\left(\frac{\ell-1}{\ell+1}\right).

Therefore, we have shown that

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{C}^{\tau}(N,R_{1},R_{2})}{N}=\frac{1}{2048\zeta(2)}\log R_{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1,\\
b\,\square\text{-free}\end{subarray}}^{\lfloor R_{2}\rfloor}n_{\tau}(b)b^{-2/3}\prod_{\ell|b}\left(\frac{\ell-1}{\ell+1}\right).

∎

We now give a proof of the main result.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Assume that
m>0
and is of Type
\mathrm{II}
. The argument in the remaining cases proceeds similarly. Write
m=ab^{2}c^{3}
, where
(a,b,c)
is a carefree triple with
a,b,c>0
, and suppose further that
c/a>1
. This condition ensures that the isomorphism class of the number field
K_{m}
is uniquely determined. Recall from Corollary
3.2
that the discriminant of
K_{m}
is given by
\Delta_{m}=-2^{8}a^{3}b^{2}c^{3}
. Setting
X=2^{8}N^{3}
, the condition
|\Delta_{m}|\leq X
becomes equivalent to

ab^{2/3}c\leq N.

Consider the four rectangles:

\begin{split}\mathcal{R}_{1}&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=[1,R_{1}]\times[1,R_{2}],\\
\mathcal{R}_{2}&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=[1,R_{1}]\times[1,R_{2}^{\prime}],\\
\mathcal{R}_{3}&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=[1,R_{1}^{\prime}]\times[1,R_{2}],\\
\mathcal{R}_{4}&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=[1,R_{1}^{\prime}]\times[1,R_{2}^{\prime}],\end{split}

so that by inclusion–exclusion,

\int_{\mathcal{R}}=\int_{\mathcal{R}_{1}}-\int_{\mathcal{R}_{2}}-\int_{\mathcal{R}_{3}}+\int_{\mathcal{R}_{4}}.

It therefore suffices to prove the theorem under the simplifying assumption
R_{1}^{\prime}=R_{2}^{\prime}=1
, in which case the claim follows directly from Proposition
4.11
.
∎

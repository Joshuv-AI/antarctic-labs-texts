Archit Agarwal

Abstract
. Ramanujan recorded five q q -series identities at the end of his second notebook and an unified generalization of these identities obtained by Bhoria, Eyyunni and Maji. Recently, Dixit and Patel gave a finite analogue of the identity of Bhoria et. al. which in turn gives finite analogue of all the aforementioned identities of Ramanujan. In this paper, one of our main goals is to obtain a one-variable generalization of the identity of Bhoria et. al. along with its finite analogue, which naturally generalizes the result of Dixit and Patel. Utilizing these newly established identities, we derive one-variable generalizations for each of the five entries by Ramanujan and their corresponding finite analogues.



1.
Introduction

Ramanujan catalogued many
q
-series identities in Chapter XVI of his second notebook
[
19
]
. He
[
19
, pp. 302–303]
further listed five
q
-series identities at the end of his second notebook.
Proofs of these five identities can be found in
[
7
, pp. 262–265]
. In order to state these identities, we first introduce certain essential notations. For any complex number
q
with
|q|<1
, the
q
-Pochhammer symbol is defined as

\displaystyle(a)_{0}:=(a;q)_{0}=1,

\displaystyle(a)_{n}:=(a;q)_{n}=(1-a)(1-aq)\cdots(1-aq^{n-1}),~n\geq 1,

\displaystyle(a)_{\infty}:=(a;q)_{\infty}:=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(a;q)_{n},~\textrm{for}~|q|<1.

These notations are fundamental to the theory of
q
-series. Below, we present the five identities given by Ramanujan.

Entry 1:
Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
a\neq 0,b\neq q^{-n}
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{(-aq)_{\infty}}{(bq)_{\infty}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-b/a)_{n}a^{n}q^{n(n+1)/2}}{(q)_{n}(bq)_{n}}.

(1.1)

Ona can also find the above identity in the Lost Notebook
[
20
, p. 370]
.

Entry 2:
Let
a\neq q^{-n}
for any natural number
n
, then one has

\displaystyle(aq)_{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{na^{n}q^{n^{2}}}{(q)_{n}(aq)_{n}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}a^{n}q^{n(n+1)/2}}{1-q^{n}}.

(1.2)

Entry 3:
For
a\neq 0
, and
b\neq q^{-n},n\geq 0
, the following identity is true:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(b/a)_{n}a^{n}}{\left(1-q^{n}\right)(b)_{n}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a^{n}-b^{n}}{1-q^{n}}.

(1.3)

Letting
a\rightarrow 0
, and replacing
b
by
zq
gives the next identity.

Entry 4:
For
z\neq q^{-n}
for any
n\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}z^{n}q^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}}{\left(1-q^{n}\right)(zq)_{n}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}q^{n}}{1-q^{n}}.

(1.4)

The above identity was rediscovered by Uchimura
[
21
, Equation (3)]
and Garvan
[
14
]
. The last identity is as follows:

Entry 5:
If
a\neq q^{-n}
for any
n\geq 0
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a^{n}(q)_{n-1}}{\left(1-q^{n}\right)(a)_{n}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{na^{n}}{1-q^{n}}.

(1.5)

In 1919, Kluyver
[
18
]
found a special case of (
1.4
), namely, for
z=1
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}q^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}}{\left(1-q^{n}\right)(q)_{n}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n}}{1-q^{n}}.

(1.6)

Fine
[
13
, p. 14, Equation (12.4), (12.42)]
, Uchimura
[
21
, Theorem 2]
and Zudilin
[
22
, P. 4]
also rediscovered (
1.6
). Uchimura gave a new expression to (
1.6
), mainly, he showed that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nq^{n}(q^{n+1})_{\infty}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}q^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}}{\left(1-q^{n}\right)(q)_{n}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n}}{1-q^{n}}.

Over the years, this identity has gained significant attention from numerous mathematicians and has been generalized in various ways. Readers interested in exploring these generalizations can see

In 2020, Dixit and Maji
[
10

obtained a one variable generalization of (

), namely, for complex numbers
a,~b,~c

|a|<1

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(b/a)_{n}a^{n}}{(1-cq^{n})(b)_{n}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(b/c)_{n}c^{n}}{(b)_{n}}\left(\frac{aq^{n}}{1-aq^{n}}-\frac{bq^{n}}{1-bq^{n}}\right).

(1.7)

In the same paper, they also derived a more general form of (

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}z^{n}q^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}}{(1-cq^{n})(zq)_{n}}=\frac{z}{c}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(zq/c)_{n-1}}{(zq)_{n}}(cq)^{n}.

(1.9)

In the same paper, Dixit and Maji further explored many beautiful partition theoretic interpretations arising from (

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\begin{bmatrix}N\\

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}N\\
n\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}N\\

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\begin{bmatrix}N\\
n\end{bmatrix}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}z^{n}q^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}(q)_{n}}{(1-cq^{n})(zq)_{n}}=\frac{z}{c}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\begin{bmatrix}N\\
n\end{bmatrix}\frac{(cq)_{N-n}(zq/c)_{n-1}(q)_{n}(cq)^{n}}{(cq)_{N}(zq)_{n-1}},

(1.11)

For
|z|<1
, the
q-
binomial theorem is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a)_{n}z^{n}}{(q)_{n}}=\frac{(az)_{\infty}}{(z)_{\infty}}.

(3.1)

Basic hypergeometric series
{}_{r+1}\phi_{r}

\displaystyle{}_{2}\phi_{1}\left[\begin{matrix}a_{1},&a_{2},&\cdots,&a_{r+1}\\
b_{1},&b_{2},&\cdots,&b_{r}\end{matrix};q;z\right]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a_{1})_{n}(a_{2})_{n}\cdots(a_{r+1})_{n}}{(b_{1})_{n}(b_{2})_{n}\cdots(b_{r})_{n}}\frac{z^{n}}{(q)_{n}}.

We recall famous Heine transformation
[
15
, p. 359, (III.1)]

\displaystyle{}_{2}\phi_{1}\left[\begin{matrix}a,&b\\
&c\end{matrix};q;z\right]=\frac{(az)_{\infty}(b)_{\infty}}{(z)_{\infty}(c)_{\infty}}{}_{2}\phi_{1}\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{c}{b},&z\\
&az\end{matrix};q;b\right].

(3.2)

The
q
-Gauss sum is

\displaystyle{}_{2}\phi_{1}\left[\begin{matrix}a,&b\\
&c\end{matrix};q;\frac{c}{ab}\right]=\frac{(c/a)_{\infty}(c/b)_{\infty}}{(c)_{\infty}(c/ab)_{\infty}}.

(3.3)

We shall also recall two
{}_{3}\phi_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\big(\frac{E}{A}\big)_{\infty}\big(\frac{DE}{BC}\big)_{\infty}}{\big(E\big)_{\infty}\big(\frac{DE}{ABC}\big)_{\infty}}{}_{3}\phi_{2}\left[\begin{matrix}A,&\frac{D}{B},&\frac{D}{C}\\
&D,&\frac{DE}{BC}\end{matrix}\,;q,\frac{E}{A}\right],

(3.4)

\displaystyle{}_{3}\phi_{2}\left[\begin{matrix}q^{-N},&B,&C\\

Affine semigroups without consecutive small elements
J. C. Rosales, R. Tapia-Ramos, A. Vigneron-Tenorio

Abstract
An 𝒜 \mathcal{A} -semigroup is a numerical semigroup without consecutive small elements. This work generalizes this concept to finite-complement submonoids of an affine cone 𝒞 \mathcal{C} . We develop algorithmic procedures to compute all 𝒜 \mathcal{A} -semigroups with a given Frobenius element (denoted by 𝒜 ⁡ ( f ) \mathcal{A}(f) ), and with fixed Frobenius element and multiplicity. Moreover, we analyze the 𝒜 ⁡ ( f ) \mathcal{A}(f) -systems of generators. Furthermore, we study 𝒜 \mathcal{A} -numerical semigroups with maximal embedding dimension, fixed Frobenius number and multiplicity, providing an algorithm for their computation and a graphical classification.

Affine semigroup, Apéry set, Arf numerical semigroup,
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup, Frobenius element,
\operatorname{MED}
-numerical semigroup, numerical semigroup, saturated numerical semigroup, tree of semigroups.

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of all non-negative integers, and consider
[k]
as the set
\{1,\ldots,k\}
. Given
p\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
, an affine semigroup
S
in
\mathbb{N}^{p}
is a non-finite subset of
\mathbb{N}^{p}
closed under addition, containing the zero element, and such that there exists a finite subset
A=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\}\subset\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
S=\{\sum_{i\in[k]}\lambda_{i}a_{i}\mid\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{N}\}
,
this set is denoted by
\langle A\rangle
, and it is named a generating set of
S
.
If this set is minimal with respect to inclusion, it is the unique minimal generating set of
S
and is denoted by
\operatorname{msg}(S)
. The minimal generating set exists for any affine semigroup (see
[
13
]
), and its cardinality, called the embedding dimension of
S
, is denoted by
\operatorname{e}(S)
.

Given
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{p}
a non-negative integer cone, an affine semigroup is a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup if the minimal integer cone containing
S
is
\mathcal{C}
, and
\mathcal{H}(S)=\mathcal{C}\setminus S
is a finite set. We assume that the cone has at least
p
extremal rays. The canonical basis of
\mathbb{N}^{p}
is
\Lambda=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{p}\}
. In case
p=1
, then
S
is a numerical semigroup.

Several invariants of
\mathcal{C}
-semigroups are needed for this work. Consider a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup
S
, and a fixed monomial order
\preceq
on
\mathbb{N}^{p}
, that is, a total order on
\mathbb{N}^{p}
compatible with addition, where
0\preceq x
for any
x\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
(see
[
3
]
), the Frobenius element of
S
is defined as
\operatorname{Fb}(S)=\max_{\preceq}\mathcal{H}(S)
. When
\mathcal{H}(S)
is empty,
\operatorname{Fb}(S)=-\sum_{i\in[p]}e_{i}
. Any
x\in S
is called a small element of
S
if
x\prec\operatorname{Fb}(S)
, and the set of all small elements of
S
is denoted by
\operatorname{N}(S)
. The multiplicity of
S
is
\operatorname{m}(S)=\min_{\preceq}(S\setminus\{0\})
, and its ratio
\operatorname{r}(S)=\min_{\preceq}\big(S\setminus\langle\operatorname{m}(S)\rangle\big)
.

The concept of
\mathcal{A}
-numerical semigroup was introduced in
[
12
]
: a numerical semigroup
S
is called an
\mathcal{A}
-semigroup if, for any
x\in\mathbb{N}
where
x<f
, the condition
\{x,x+1\}\not\subset S
holds. This work expands this definition to
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup as: a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup
S\subseteq\mathcal{C}
is an
\mathcal{A}
-semigroup when, for every
s\in S
and
e\in\Lambda
, the set
\{s,s+e\}
is not contained in
\operatorname{N}(S)
. Given
x,y\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
, we say that
x
and
y
are consecutive if
y-x\in\Lambda
, or
x-y\in\Lambda
. Thus, an
\mathcal{A}
-semigroup is a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup without consecutive small elements. Fixed a cone
\mathcal{C}
, the set of all
\mathcal{C}
-semigroups with Frobenius element
f
is denoted by
\mathcal{A}(f)
.

One of the main objectives of this work is to study the fundamental properties of the set
\mathcal{A}(f)
. We prove that
\mathcal{A}(f)
is a covariety, and we use it to provide some algorithmic methods to compute it. In particular, fixed
f\in\mathcal{C}
, a finite tree containing all the
\mathcal{A}
-semigroups with Frobenius
f
is introduced. These
\mathcal{A}
-semigroups are obtained by joining some special gaps to a given
\mathcal{A}
-semigroup. To improve this computation, we propose an approach to get these special gaps from their father’s special gaps.

A partition of
\mathcal{A}(f)
is defined via the subsets
\mathcal{A}(f,m)=\{S\in\mathcal{A}(f)\mid\operatorname{m}(S)=m\}
. It is shown that
\mathcal{A}(f)
is the finite union of such subsets, where each value of
m
satisfies specific properties. Since the set
\mathcal{A}(f,m)
is a ratio-covariety, we construct an associated tree and provide an algorithm to compute it. Furthermore, we introduce an additional partition of
\mathcal{A}(f,m)
based on certain subsets of
\mathcal{C}
determined by the parameters
f
,
m
, and the fixed monomial order. The above different partitions allow us to develop other algorithms for computing
\mathcal{A}(f)
.

A set
X\subset\mathcal{C}
is an
\mathcal{A}(f)
-set if there is a
S\in\mathcal{A}(f)
such that
X\subseteq\operatorname{N}(S)\setminus\{0\}
. We show that given
X
an
\mathcal{A}(f)
-set, there exists the smallest element
S
in
\mathcal{A}(f)
containing
X
. This
\mathcal{A}
-semigroup is denoted by
\mathcal{A}(f)[X]
and
X
is called an
\mathcal{A}(f)
-system of generators of
S
. We prove that every semigroup in
\mathcal{A}(f)
admits a unique minimal
\mathcal{A}(f)
-system of generators. The cardinality of this minimal
\mathcal{A}(f)
-system of generators is known as the
\mathcal{A}(f)
-rank of
S
. The
\mathcal{A}
-semigroups with
\mathcal{A}(f)
-rank equal to zero, one, or two are also characterized in this work.

Focus on numerical semigroups; it is well-known that the embedding dimension of a numerical semigroup is upper-bounded by
\operatorname{m}(S)
. The numerical semigroups whose embedding dimension is equal to their multiplicity are called semigroups with maximal embedding dimension, and are denoted as

-semigroups. In connection with this class of semigroups, a long list of publications exists where
1
-dimensional local analytically irreducible domains are studied via their value semigroups. From this approximation, several properties of such rings have been considered, including being an
\operatorname{MED}
-semigroup, Arf numerical semigroup or saturated (see
[

,
[

-semigroup, Arf numerical semigroup, and saturated numerical semigroup were born. Following the terminology introduced in
[

, an
\operatorname{\mathcal{A}MED}
-semigroup is a numerical

-semigroup which is also
\mathcal{A}
-semigroup. Denoting by

The set

Let
\Lambda=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{p}\}
be the set of canonical basis vectors of
\mathbb{N}^{p}

\preceq
be a monomial order on
\mathbb{N}^{p}

\mathcal{A}
-semigroup

is a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup such that for every

, then

\mathcal{A}(f)=\{S\text{ is a }\mathcal{A}\text{-semigroup}\mid\operatorname{Fb}(S)=f\}.

This section analyses the set
\mathcal{A}(f)

Since

\mathcal{A}(f)
also does, as shown in the following example.

Example 1
.

Consider the positive cone
\mathcal{C}
spanned by
\{(1,0),(1,1),(1,2)\}

\mathcal{C}
-semigroup

\mathcal{H}(S)=\{(1,0),(1,1),(2,0),(2,1),(2,2),(3,1),(4,0),(4,1),(4,2),(5,0)\}.

aM\preceq_{lex}bM

\operatorname{N}_{\preceq_{glex}}(S)=\{(0,0),(1,2),(2,3),(3,0),(3,2),(5,1),(6,0)\},

In accordance with the terminology established in
[
8
]
, a covariety
V
is a non-empty family of
\mathcal{C}
-semigroups satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

There exists a minimal element in
V
with respect to inclusion, denoted by
\min_{\subseteq}V
.

(ii)

If
S,T\in V
, then
S\cap T\in V
.

(iii)

If

, then
S\setminus\{\operatorname{m}(S)\}\in V
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{0\}
. Notice that,

\Delta(f)=\operatorname{min}_{\subseteq}\bigl(\mathcal{A}(f)\bigr)=\{0\}\cup\{x\in\mathcal{C}\mid x\succ f\}.

In the context of numerical semigroups,
\Delta(f)
is known as a half-line semigroup (see
[
14
]
). At the same time, in a more general setting, it is referred to as an ordinary semigroup (see
[
16
]
).

Proposition 2
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{0\}
. Then, the set
\mathcal{A}(f)
is a covariety.

Proof.

It suffices to check the last two properties to show that
\mathcal{A}(f)
is a covariety.
The intersection of two
\mathcal{C}
-semigroups
S
and
T
is itself a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup, and its Frobenius element corresponds to
\max_{\preceq}\{\operatorname{Fb}(S),\operatorname{Fb}(T)\}
. Moreover, the property of being an
\mathcal{A}
-semigroup is compatible with the intersection.
Finally, for any
S\in\mathcal{A}(f)\setminus\{\Delta(f)\}
, we obtain that
S\setminus\{\operatorname{m}(S)\}
is also a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup, and since
S
is an
\mathcal{A}
-semigroup, removing
\operatorname{m}(S)
preserves the
\mathcal{A}
-semigroup structure. Thus,
\mathcal{A}(f)
is a covariety.
∎

The properties of covarieties allow us to describe the set
\mathcal{A}(f)
through an associated directed graph
G(\mathcal{A}(f))
, whose vertex set is
\mathcal{A}(f)
, and where an ordered pair

A graph is a tree if there exists a vertex
R
(the root) such that any vertex
S
is connected to
R
by a unique sequence of edges

(S_{0},S_{1}),(S_{1},S_{2}),\ldots,(S_{n-1},S_{n}),

S_{0}=S

S_{n}=R

We establish the following result by extending the scope of
[
8

Proposition 3
.

The graph
G(\mathcal{A}(f))
is a tree with root
\Delta(f)
.

Proof.

Let
S\in\mathcal{A}(f)\setminus\{\Delta(f)\}
, and consider the sequence
\{S_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
given by
S_{0}=S
and, for each
i\in\mathbb{N}

S_{i+1}=S_{i}\setminus\{\operatorname{m}(S_{i})\}
if
S_{i}\neq\Delta(f)
, and
S_{i+1}=\Delta(f)
otherwise.
Since the sequence is stationary, it defines a sequence of edges that connect
S
to
\Delta(f)
. The uniqueness of this sequence follows from the fact that each
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup has a unique multiplicity, ensuring no cycles occur.
∎

For any
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup

, we say that an element

As an immediate consequence of the above proposition, and using

Proposition 4

Let

Suppose that

Throughout this section, an element

\preceq

Furthermore, given an
\mathcal{A}(f)

Since

Proposition 8
.

Let
X
be an
\mathcal{A}(f)
-set. Then,
\mathcal{A}(f)[X]
is the smallest element of
\mathcal{A}(f)
containing
X
, with respect to inclusion.

Given an
\mathcal{A}(f)
-set

, we say that

is an
\mathcal{A}(f)
-generator system of

\mathcal{A}(f)
-set and

. Moreover, if there is no proper subset

such that
\mathcal{A}(f)[Y]=S
, we say that

is a minimal
\mathcal{A}(f)
-system of generators of
S
.
If there is a unique minimal
\mathcal{A}(f)
-system of generators of

, we denote it by
\operatorname{msg}_{\mathcal{A}(f)}(S)
, and the cardinality of it, called the

The main goal of this section is to prove that every

admits a unique minimal
\mathcal{A}(f)
-system of generators, which we explicitly determine. Subsequently, we characterize those
\mathcal{A}
-semigroups of

-rank equal to zero, one, and two.

We state the following result based on

Theorem 9

Let
S\in\mathcal{A}(f)

\operatorname{msg}_{\mathcal{A}(f)}(S)=\operatorname{N}(S)\cap\operatorname{msg}(S).

Proof.

Let
S\in\mathcal{A}(f)
and consider
X=\operatorname{N}(S)\cap\operatorname{msg}(S)
. By definition,
X
is trivially an
\mathcal{A}(f)
-set. Let us see that
X
is a
\mathcal{A}(f)
-system of generators of

X\subset S
, then
\mathcal{A}(f)[X]\subseteq S
.
For the other inclusion, observe that
X\subset\mathcal{A}(f)[X]
and
\mathcal{A}(f)[X]\in\mathcal{A}(f)
, which implies that
X\sqcup\Delta(f)\subseteq\mathcal{A}(f)[X]
. Consequently,
\operatorname{msg}(S)\subset\mathcal{A}(f)[X]
, and thus
S\subseteq\mathcal{A}(f)[X]
. To complete the proof, we show that if
Y
is an
\mathcal{A}(f)
-set such that
\mathcal{A}(f)[Y]=S
, then necessarily
X\subseteq Y
. Suppose that
X\not\subseteq Y
, then
x\in X\setminus Y
exists. Since
x\in\operatorname{msg}(S)
and
S\in\mathcal{A}(f)
, we have
S\setminus\{x\}\in\mathcal{A}(f)
and
Y\subset S\setminus\{x\}
. Applying Proposition
8
,
S=\mathcal{A}(f)[Y]\subseteq S\setminus\{x\}
, which is false. Hence,
X
is the unique minimal
\mathcal{A}(f)
-system of generators of
S
.
∎

As a consequence of Theorem
9
, we obtain the following corollary, which in particular caracterizes the elements
S\in\mathcal{A}(f)
with
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{A}(f)}(S)=0
, and gives the key fact for those with
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{A}(f)}(S)=1
.

Corollary 10
.

Let
S\in\mathcal{A}(f)
. Then, the following conditions hold:

•

\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{A}(f)}(S)\leq\operatorname{e}(S)
.

•

\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{A}(f)}(S)=0
if and only if
S=\Delta(f)
.

•

If
S\neq\Delta(f)
, then
\operatorname{m}(S)\in\operatorname{msg}_{\mathcal{A}(f)}(S)
.

•

\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{A}(f)}(S)=1
if and only if
\operatorname{msg}_{\mathcal{A}(f)}(S)=\{\operatorname{m}(S)\}
.

Next, we provide a characterization of the elements
S\in\mathcal{A}(f)
with
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{A}(f)}(S)=1
. Recall that
\Lambda
is the canonical basis of
\mathbb{N}^{p}
. Note that if
\operatorname{m}(S)\in\Lambda
, then
\operatorname{m}(S)
and
2\operatorname{m}(S)
are consecutive. So, when
\operatorname{m}(S),2\operatorname{m}(S)\in\operatorname{N}(S)
, we deduce that
S
is not an
\mathcal{A}
-semigroup. Therefore, any element
S
in
\mathcal{A}(f)
with
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{A}(f)}(S)=1
and
\operatorname{m}(S)\in\Lambda
is
\langle m\rangle\cup\Delta(f)
, for some
m\in\Lambda
and
m\prec f\prec 2m
.

Theorem 11
.

Let
S
be a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup and
m,f\in\mathcal{C}
with
m\notin\Lambda
. Then,
S\in\mathcal{A}(f)
with
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{A}(f)}(S)=1
if and only if

S=\langle m\rangle\cup\Delta(f),

(2)

where
m\prec f
and
f\notin\langle m\rangle
.

Proof.

Suppose that
S\in\mathcal{A}(f)
and
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{A}(f)}(S)=1
. By Corollary
10
, we have
S=\mathcal{A}(f)[\{\operatorname{m}(S)\}]
and
S\neq\Delta(f)
. Therefore,
\operatorname{m}(S)\prec f
and
f\notin\langle\operatorname{m}(S)\rangle
, which implies that
S=\langle\operatorname{m}(S)\rangle\cup\Delta(f)
. Conversely, consider the
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup
S
described in (
2
). Clearly,
S\in\mathcal{A}(f)
,
S\neq\Delta(f)
, and any element of
\mathcal{A}(f)
that contains
\{m\}
also contains
S
. By Proposition
8
, we deduce that
S=\mathcal{A}(f)[\{m\}]
, and applying Corollary
10
, we obtain
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{A}(f)}(S)=1
.
∎

The
\mathcal{A}
-semigroup presented in (
2
) can be described in greater detail. For this purpose, we introduce some notation related to monomial orders on
\mathbb{N}^{p}
. Any such order
\preceq
can be defined using
p
distinct maps
\pi_{i}:\mathbb{N}^{p}\longrightarrow\mathbb{N}
, where each
\pi_{i}
is defined via
\pi_{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{p})=\sum_{j\in[p]}a_{ij}x_{j}
, such that
a_{ij}
are positive real numbers for every
i,j\in[p]
, and the matrix
M=(a_{ij})
is non-singular (for further details, see
[
3
,
11
]
). Some results in this work require that the set
\{x\in\mathcal{C}\mid x\preceq k\}
is finite for any
k\in\mathcal{C}
, especially when
\mathcal{C}=\mathbb{N}^{p}
. Hence, we assume that the fixed monomial order satisfies such a property, and that the coefficients of
\pi_{1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{p})
are all strictly positive (integer) numbers, that is, the entries of the first row of
M
are all strictly positive. For example, a graded monomial order can be used.

From now on,
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
denotes the floor function, which rounds down to the nearest integer. Therefore, using the above terminology, the
\mathcal{A}
-semigroup given in (
2
) corresponds with the set

S=\bigg\{\operatorname{m}(S),2\operatorname{m}(S),\ldots,\left\lfloor\tfrac{\pi_{1}(f)}{\pi_{1}\left(\operatorname{m}(S)\right)}\right\rfloor\operatorname{m}(S)\bigg\}\cup\Delta(f),

which leads to the following result for numerical semigroups. We denote by
\operatorname{g}(S)
the genus of
S
, that is, the cardinality of
\mathcal{H}(S)=\mathcal{C}\setminus S
.

Corollary 12
.

If
S\in\mathcal{A}(f)
is a numerical semigroup with
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{A}(f)}(S)=1
, then
\operatorname{g}(S)=f-\left\lfloor\frac{f}{\operatorname{m}(S)}\right\rfloor
.

Example 13
.

Let
S=\mathcal{A}(14)[\{5\}]=\{0,5,10,15,\longrightarrow\}
, where the symbol
\longrightarrow
indicates that every integer greater than
15
belongs to the set. Hence,
\operatorname{g}(S)=14-\left\lfloor\tfrac{14}{5}\right\rfloor=12
.

Consider now the case where
S\in\mathcal{A}(f)
and
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{A}(f)}(S)=2
. We structure our discussion analogously to the case
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{A}(f)}(S)=1
, and provide an explicit description of the
\mathcal{A}
-semigroups with
\mathcal{A}(f)
-rank equal to two. Additionally, for those that are also numerical semigroups, we present a pseudo-formula for their genus.

Recall that for any
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup
S
, the ratio of

is given by
\operatorname{r}(S)=\min_{\preceq}\big(\operatorname{msg}(S)\setminus\{\operatorname{m}(S)\}\big)
. The next result is a direct consequence of Theorem
9
.

Corollary 14
.

Let
S\in\mathcal{A}(f)
. Then, the following conditions are equivalent:

•

\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{A}(f)}(S)=2
.

•

\{\operatorname{m}(S),\operatorname{r}(S)\}
is an
\mathcal{A}(f)
-set, and
S=\mathcal{A}(f)\big[\{\operatorname{m}(S),\operatorname{r}(S)\}\big]
.

•

\operatorname{r}(S)\prec f
and
S=\langle\operatorname{m}(S),\operatorname{r}(S)\rangle\cup\Delta(f)
, where if there exist two consecutive elements of
\langle\operatorname{m}(S),\operatorname{r}(S)\rangle
, then at least one of them is greater than
f
.

To characterize the elements
S\in\mathcal{A}(f)
with
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{A}(f)}(S)=2
, we briefly explain the conditions under which
\langle m,r\rangle
is a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup for any
m,r\in\mathcal{C}
. The conditions above are outlined as follows.

•

For
p\geq 3
,
\langle m,r\rangle
is not a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup.

•

For
p=2
,
S=\langle m,r\rangle
is a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup if and only if
S=\mathcal{C}
. Since a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup
S=\langle m,r\rangle\subseteq\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{2}
has embedding dimension two if and only if the set
\mathbb{N}^{2}\cap\{\lambda_{1}a_{1}+\lambda_{2}a_{2}\mid 0\leq\lambda_{i}\leq 1,\mbox{ and }a_{i}=\min_{\leq_{\mathbb{N}^{2}}}(\tau_{i}\cap\mathcal{C})\}=\{0,m,r,m+r\}
(see
[
4
]
).

•

For
p=1
,
\langle m,r\rangle
is a numerical semigroup if and only if
m
and
r
are coprime (see
[
14
, Lemma 2.1]
).

We state the following theorem, which provides a characterization of the elements
S\in\mathcal{A}(f)
with
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{A}(f)}(S)=2
in the case where
\langle m,r\rangle
is not a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup. From now on, we assume that for any
m,r,f\in\mathcal{C}
, if two consecutive elements of
\langle m,r\rangle
exist, then one of them is greater than
f
.

Theorem 15
.

Let
S
be a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup and let
m,r,f\in\mathcal{C}
such that
\langle m,r\rangle
is not a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup,
m\prec r\prec f
,
r\notin\langle m\rangle
, and
f\notin\langle m,r\rangle
. Then,

S=\langle m,r\rangle\cup\Delta(f)\in\mathcal{A}(f)\text{ and }\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{A}(f)}(S)=2.

Moreover, every
\mathcal{A}
-semigroup with
\mathcal{A}(f)
-rank equal to two, such that its multiplicity and ratio do not generate a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup, is of this form.

Proof.

Clearly,
S\in\mathcal{A}(f)
. By Theorem
9
, it follows that
\operatorname{msg}_{\mathcal{A}(f)}(S)=\{m,r\}
. So,
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{A}(f)}(S)=2
. Suppose now that
T\in\mathcal{A}(f)
with
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{A}(f)}(T)=2
such that
\langle\operatorname{m}(T),\operatorname{r}(T)\rangle
is not a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup. By Corollary
14
, we have
T=\langle\operatorname{m}(T),\operatorname{r}(T)\rangle\cup\Delta(f)
. Trivially,
\operatorname{m}(T)\prec\operatorname{r}(T)\prec f
,
\operatorname{r}(T)\notin\langle\operatorname{m}(T)\rangle
and,
f\notin\langle\operatorname{m}(T),\operatorname{r}(T)\rangle
.
∎

Theorem
15
does not analyze all numerical semigroups with
\mathcal{A}(f)
-rank equal to two. Specifically, the case where
m
and
r
are coprime (i.e., when
\langle m,r\rangle
is a numerical semigroup) has not been considered. Notice that any numerical semigroup
S
with multiplicity equal to two is minimally generated by
\{2,\operatorname{Fb}(S)+2\}
. Thus,
S
is an
\mathcal{A}
-semigroup with
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{A}(\operatorname{Fb}(S))}(S)=1
. Moreover, the multiplicity of any numerical
\mathcal{A}
-semigroup with
\mathcal{A}(f)
-rank equal to two is greater than or equal to three. In general, affine semigroups require more consideration due to the greater number of cases that distinguish them from numerical semigroups. However, as we have seen, the situation is reversed. This is why we rely on the Apéry set to address all the possibilities for numerical semigroups.

The following two propositions are formulated specifically for them. We recall a well-known characterization of the Apéry set of a numerical semigroup.

Proposition 16
.

[
14
, Lemma 2.4]
.
Let
S
be a numerical semigroup and
b\in S\setminus\{0\}
. Then,

\operatorname{Ap}(S,b)=\{0=w(0),w(1),\ldots,w(b-1)\},

where
w(i)
is the least element of
S
such that
w(i)\equiv i\mod b
, for all
i\in\{0\}\cup[b-1]
.

Proposition 17
.

Let
S
be a numerical semigroup,
b\in S\setminus\{0\}
,
\operatorname{Ap}(S,b)=\{0=w(0),w(1),\ldots,w(b-1)\}
and
X=\{i\in[b-1]\mid w(i)<\operatorname{Fb}(S)\}
. Then,
S\in\mathcal{A}(f)
if and only if
X\cup\{0,b\}
does not contain consecutive natural numbers.

Proof.

Suppose that
S
is a numerical semigroup such that
S\notin\mathcal{A}(f)
. Therefore, there exists
s,s+1\in\operatorname{N}(S)\cap\operatorname{Ap}(S,f+1)
. By Proposition
16
, we have
s=w(i)
and
s+1=w(j)
for some
i,j\in[f]\cup\{0\}
, which implies that
i,f\in X\cup\{0,f\}
. Since
w(j)=w(i)+1\equiv w(i+1)\mod(f+1)
, we conclude that
j=i+1
.
Conversely, assume that there exist
i,i+1\in X\cup\{0,b\}
, and that
w(b)=w(0)
. We have
w(i)+1\equiv w(i+1)
and
w(i+1)-1\equiv w(i)\mod b
. If
w(i+1)<w(i)
, then
w(i)+1\in S
. Otherwise,
w(i+1)-1\in S
. Hence,
S\notin\mathcal{A}(f)
.
∎

The following theorem provides an analogous result to Theorem
15
for those numerical semigroups whose multiplicity and ratio are coprime.

Theorem 18
.

Let
m,r,f\in\mathbb{N}
with
3\leq m<r<f
, and such that
\langle m,r\rangle
is a numerical semigroup,
f\notin\langle m,r\rangle
, and
X=\left\{\lambda r\mod m\mid\lambda\in\big[\lfloor\tfrac{f-1}{r}\rfloor\big]\right\}\cup\{0,m\}
. If
X
does not contain consecutive natural numbers, then

S=\langle m,r\rangle\cup\{f+1,f+2,\longrightarrow\}\in\mathcal{A}(f)\text{ with }\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{A}(f)}(S)=2.

Moreover, every numerical
\mathcal{A}
-semigroup with
\mathcal{A}(f)
-rank equal to two, such that its multiplicity and ratio generate a numerical semigroup, is of this form.

Proof.

Let
S=\langle m,r\rangle\cup\{f+1,f+2,\longrightarrow\}
. Since
\langle m,r\rangle
is a numerical semigroup, it follows that
\operatorname{m}(S)=m
and
\operatorname{r}(S)=r
. By
[
14
, Example 2.22]
we obtain that

\displaystyle\{x\in\operatorname{Ap}(S,m)\mid x<f\}

\displaystyle=\{x\in\operatorname{Ap}\big(\langle m,r\rangle,m\big)\mid x<f\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\lambda r\mid\lambda\in\{0\}\cup[\lfloor\tfrac{f-1}{r}\rfloor]\right\}.

Therefore,
X\cup\{0,m\}=\{i\in[m-1]\mid w(i)<f\}\cup\{0,m\}
. By hypothesis,
X\cup\{0,m\}
does not contain consecutive natural numbers. Applying Proposition
17
, we deduce that
S\in\mathcal{A}(f)
. Finally, by Theorem
9
, we obtain that
\operatorname{msg}_{\mathcal{A}(f)}(S)=\{m,r\}
, and thus
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{A}(f)}(S)=2
. Suppose now that
T\in\mathcal{A}(f)
with
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{A}(f)}(T)=2
, and that
\langle\operatorname{m}(T),\operatorname{r}(T)\rangle
is a numerical semigroup. By Corollary
14
, we have
T=\langle\operatorname{m}(T),\operatorname{r}(T)\rangle\cup\{f+1,\longrightarrow\}
. Trivially,
\operatorname{m}(T)<\operatorname{r}(T)<f
,
\operatorname{r}(T)\notin\langle\operatorname{m}(T)\rangle
and,
f\notin\langle\operatorname{m}(T),\operatorname{r}(T)\rangle
.
∎

, which does not contain consecutive natural numbers. Since the conditions given in Theorem

hold, we deduce that

At the end of the section, we provide a pseudo-formula for computing the genus of any numerical semigroup with
A(f)
-rank equal to two. It is a classical result that the Apéry set of a numerical semigroup, with respect to a non-zero element, allows us to compute its genus, as stated below.

Proposition 21
.

[
14
, Proposition 2.12]
.
Let
S
be a numerical semigroup and
b\in S\setminus\{0\}
. Then,
\operatorname{g}(S)=\frac{1}{b}(\sum_{w\in\operatorname{Ap}(S,b)}w)-\frac{b-1}{2}
.

From the previous result, our objective is reduced to study the Apéry set. The next proposition describes explicitly the set
\operatorname{Ap}(S,\operatorname{m}(S))
. Let
B
be the set
\big\{\lambda\operatorname{r}(S)\mid\lambda\in\{0\}\cup[l]\big\}
, where

l=\big\lfloor\frac{\operatorname{Fb}(S)-1}{\operatorname{r}(S)}\big\rfloor
if
\operatorname{m}(S)
and
\operatorname{r}(S)
are coprime, or
l=\max\left\{k\in\big[\tfrac{\operatorname{m}(S)}{\gcd\left(\operatorname{m}(S),\operatorname{r}(S)\right)}-1\big]\mid k\operatorname{r}(S)<\operatorname{Fb}(S)\right\}
, otherwise.

Proposition 22
.

Let
S\in\mathcal{A}(f)
be a numerical semigroup with
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{A}(f)}(S)=2
. Then,
\operatorname{Ap}(S,\operatorname{m}(S))
is the disjoint union of
B
and the set

\bigcup_{i\in[\operatorname{m}(S)]}\big\{\operatorname{Fb}(S)+i\mid\operatorname{Fb}(S)+i\not\equiv b\mod\operatorname{m}(S),\,\forall b\in B\big\}.

Proof.

Let
S\in\mathcal{A}(f)
be a numerical semigroup with
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{A}(f)}(S)=2
. By Theorems
15
and
18
,

S=\langle\operatorname{m}(S),\operatorname{r}(S)\rangle\cup\{\operatorname{Fb}(S),\longrightarrow\}.

For the sake of simplicity, we write
m,r
and
f
instead of
\operatorname{m}(S),\operatorname{r}(S)
and
\operatorname{Fb}(S)
, respectively.
By Proposition
16
, we know that
\operatorname{Ap}(S,m)=\{0=w(0),w(1),\ldots,w(m-1)\}
, where
w(i)
is the least element of
S
such that
w(i)\equiv i\mod m
, for all
i\in\{0\}\cup[m-1]
. Suppose that
\langle\operatorname{m}(S),\operatorname{r}(S)\rangle
is not a numerical semigroup and let
d=\gcd(m,r)
. Notice that for every natural number
k
,

(k\tfrac{m}{d}+i)r\equiv k\tfrac{m}{d}d\tfrac{r}{d}+ir\equiv km\tfrac{r}{d}+ir\equiv ir\mod m,

for all
i\in\{0\}\cup[\tfrac{m}{d}-1]
. Thus, defining
l=\max\left\{k\in\left[\tfrac{m}{d}-1\right]\mid kr<f\right\}
, we obtain that
B=\big\{\lambda\operatorname{r}(S)\mid\lambda\in\{0\}\cup[l]\big\}\subset\operatorname{Ap}(S,m)
.
Now, let
x\in\operatorname{Ap}(S,m)\setminus B
. So,
x>\operatorname{Fb}(S)
, which implies that
x=\operatorname{Fb}(S)+i
for some
i\in[m]
. We conclude that

\operatorname{Ap}(S,m)=B\sqcup\big\{\operatorname{Fb}(S)+i\mid\operatorname{Fb}(S)+i\not\equiv b,\text{ for any }b\in B\big\}.

If
\langle m,r\rangle
is a numerical semigroup, then by Proposition
17
and Theorem
18
, it follows that the Apéry set admits the same decomposition with
l=\left\lfloor\tfrac{f-1}{r}\right\rfloor
.
∎

A partition of
\mathcal{A}(f)

Let
m,f\in\mathcal{C}
. We denote by
\mathcal{A}(f,m)
the set of all possibles
\mathcal{A}
-semigroups
S
such that
\operatorname{Fb}(S)=f
and
\operatorname{m}(S)=m
.
In this section, we provide an alternative procedure to compute
\mathcal{A}(f)
, using the sets
\mathcal{A}(f,m)
. Moreover, we show that
\mathcal{A}(f,m)
can be arranged in a rooted tree. Again, fix a monomial order
\preceq
on
\mathbb{N}^{p}
.

Given
f
and
m
in a cone
\mathcal{C}
with
f\notin\langle m\rangle
, we define

\Delta(f,m)=\langle m\rangle\cup\Delta(f).

The following result determines under what conditions
\mathcal{A}(f,m)\neq\emptyset
.

Proposition 25
.

Let
m,f\in\mathcal{C}
. The set
\mathcal{A}(f,m)\neq\emptyset
if and only if

•

m\prec f
, or there does not exist any
z\in\mathcal{C}
such that
f\prec z\prec m
.

•

f\notin\langle m\rangle
.

Proof.

Assume
S\in\mathcal{A}(f,m)
. Since
f
is the Frobenius element of
S
,
f\notin\langle m\rangle
follows. If
m\prec f
, we are done. Otherwise, suppose there exists
z\in\mathcal{C}
such that
f\prec z\prec m
. By the definition of the Frobenius element, it follows that
z\in S
, which contradicts the definition of multiplicity. Conversely, suppose that
f\notin\langle m\rangle
. If
m\prec f
, then
\Delta(f,m)\in\mathcal{A}(f,m)
by Theorem
11
. If there does not exist
z\in\mathcal{C}
such that
f\prec z\prec m
, then
\Delta(f)\in\mathcal{A}(f,m)
.
∎

Fix
f\in\mathcal{C}
. We define,

\operatorname{M}(f)=\{m\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{0\}\mid m\prec f,\text{ or }\nexists\,z\in\mathcal{C}\text{ such that }f\prec z\prec m\}.

(3)

Observe that
\mathcal{A}(f)=\cup_{m\in\operatorname{M}(f)}\mathcal{A}(f,m)
, and thus the family
\{\mathcal{A}(f,m)\mid m\in\operatorname{M}(f)\}
forms a partition of
\mathcal{A}(f)
.

Example 26
.

Consider the monomial order, the element
f\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
, and the cone
\mathcal{C}
given by Example
7
. Figure

illustrates the set
\Delta(f)
. The empty circles represent the gaps of
\Delta(f)
, the blue squares denote its minimal generators, and the red circles represent some elements in it.

\Delta(f)
.

So, the set
\operatorname{M}(f)
is
\{(2,1),(3,1),\ldots,(8,1),(3,2),\ldots,(6,2),(6,3)\}

We adopt the terminology established in
[

, extending it from numerical semigroups to

Proposition 32
.

Let

be a
\mathcal{C}
-semigroup,

\mathcal{A}
-semigroup if and only if these conditions hold:

C1.

If

with

C2.

for every

Proof.

Consider the
\mathcal{A}
-semigroup
S
. Hence, if there exists some

, with

for some

, then

. Thus,

, and

\operatorname{Sat}(f)\subseteq\operatorname{Arf}(f)\subseteq\mathcal{A}(f)

is a chain of covarieties such that
\Delta(f)=\min_{\subseteq}(\operatorname{Sat}(f))=\min_{\subseteq}(\operatorname{Arf}(f))=\min_{\subseteq}(\mathcal{A}(f))
.

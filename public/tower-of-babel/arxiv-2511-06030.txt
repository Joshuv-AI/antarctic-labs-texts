Asymptotic behavior of solutions to a space fractional diffusion equation
Barbara Łupińska, Piotr Rybka

Abstract
We improve the time decay estimates of solutions to the one-dimensional fractional diffusion equation involving the Caputo derivative. The equation is considered on the half-line. Depending on the boundary condition, we show that solutions converge in L p L^{p} , p > 1 p>1 to a multiple of the self-similar solutions or decay to zero. The convergence rate is provided.



Key words:
Caputo derivative, space-fractional diffusion operator, fundamental solution, decay of solutions

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification.
Primary: 35R11, Secondary: 35A08

Introduction

It is well-known that solutions to the heat equation on the line with an initial condition decaying at infinity converge as time goes to infinity to a multiple of the fundamental solution, (see
[
2
, Theorem 1.1.4]
),

\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u(\cdot,t)-mG_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}=0,

(1)

where
m=\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(x,0)\,dx
and
G_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{2}(\pi t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\exp\big(-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}\big)
.
The proof offered in
[
2
]
is based on the representation of solutions in terms of convolution with the fundamental solution,
G_{t}
.

In the present paper we prove an analogue of (
1
) for the space fractional diffusion

\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x}D^{\alpha}_{xC}u=0,&(x,t)\in(0,\infty)^{2},\\
u(x,0)=g(x),&x>0,\end{array}

(2)

where
g,\ yg(\cdot)\in L^{1}(0,\infty)
and

D^{\alpha}_{xC}
is the fractional Caputo derivative of order
\alpha\in(0,1)
with respect to the spacial variable
x
, see Section 2 for the definition of the operator
D^{\alpha}_{xC}
.

Problem (
2
) has to be augmented with the boundary condition at
x=0
. We choose the Dirichlet data

u(0,t)=0,\qquad t>0

(3)

or the fixed slope condition,

u_{x}(0,t)=0,\qquad t>0.

(4)

Here is our main result. Let us suppose that
w
is a solution to (
2
)–(
3
), then Theorem
1
(a) states that

\sup_{t\geq 1}t^{\frac{2-1/p}{1+\alpha}}\|w(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{p}}<\infty

for all
p\in(1,\infty].
We see that unexpectedly the Dirichlet boundary condition leads to extinguishing the solution. However, if
v
is a solution to (
2
), (
4
), then its behavior is consistent with (
1
), because we can show in Theorem
1
(b) that

\sup_{t\geq 1}t^{\frac{2-1/p}{1+\alpha}}\|v(\cdot,t)-2m{\mathscr{E}}_{t}\|_{L^{p}}<\infty

for all
p\in(1,\infty].
We see that the case
p=1
is excluded. In fact, we can only prove weak type
(1,1)
estimates, see Lemma
5
.

In the above formula we set
m=\int_{0}^{\infty}g(y)\,dy
and
{\mathscr{E}}_{t}
is the fundamental solution of (
2
1
), see Section 2 for the definition of
{\mathscr{E}}_{t}
and its properties.

Our method of proving these facts is based on the representation formulas of solutions in terms of the convolution of data with the fundamental solution. These formulas were derived in
[
4
]
, we will recall them in Section
4
. However, in order to make them work we have to establish estimates on the decay of the fundamental solution to (
2
),
{\mathscr{E}}_{t},
and its derivatives, see Section
3
for a series of Lemmas. These bounds are an improvement in comparison with
[
4
]
and they give us hope of lifting the restriction of the compact support of
g
. However, we need the boundedness of
\hbox{supp}\,g
in the proof of Theorem
1
.

The organization of the paper is as follows.
In Section 2 we recall the basic facts from the fractional calculus. Section 3 is devoted to the proof of estimates on
{\mathscr{E}}_{t}
and its derivatives. The representation formulas, see (
14
) and (
15
) are recalled in Section 4, where the main theorem is proved with the help of Marcinkiewicz Interpolation Theorem.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Fractional operators

First of all, we recall the notions of the Caputo and Riemann-Liouville fractional derivatives. We begin with the definition of the Riemann-Liouville integral and the derivative of fractional order
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Here they are

[I^{\alpha}_{a+}f](x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{a}^{x}{\frac{f(\tau)d\tau}{(x-\tau)^{1-\alpha}}},

D^{\alpha}_{xRL}f(x)=\frac{d}{dx}[I^{1-\alpha}_{a+}f],

while the Caputo fractional derivative of order
\alpha
has the form

D^{\alpha}_{xC}f(x)=[I^{1-\alpha}_{a+}f^{\prime}](x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{a}^{x}{\frac{f^{\prime}(\tau)d\tau}{(x-\tau)^{\alpha}}}.

Remark 1

(see
[
3
]
)
.

The operator
I^{\alpha}_{a+}f
is well defined for
f\in L^{p}(a,b),\ p\geq 1
and
D^{\alpha}_{xRL}f,\ D^{\alpha}_{xC}f
are well defined, for example, for an absolutely continuous functions. This approach is sufficient for our purposes.

The following formula illustrates the relationship between the two types of derivatives for a general function
f\in AC[0,L]
,

D^{\alpha}_{xRL}f(x)=D^{\alpha}_{xC}f(x)+\frac{x^{-\alpha}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}f(0).

Let us notice that if
f(0)=0
, then

D^{\alpha}_{xRL}f(x)=D^{\alpha}_{xC}f(x).

Moreover, if
\alpha\in(0,1)
, then we have

D^{\alpha}_{xRL}I^{\alpha}_{a+}f(x)=f(x),

but

I^{\alpha}_{a+}D^{\alpha}_{xRL}f(x)=f(x)-f(a),

(5)

for absolutely continuous functions. For more details about fractional operators, we refer to
[
3
]
.

2.2
The fundamental solution

The authors of
[
4
]
derived a formula for
{\mathscr{E}}
, a self-similar solution to (
2
). Namely, its definition follows

{\mathscr{E}}_{t}(x)={\mathscr{E}}(x,t)=a_{0}t^{-\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}E_{\alpha,1+1/\alpha,1/\alpha}\left(-\frac{x^{1+\alpha}}{(1+\alpha)t}\right),

(6)

where

E_{\alpha,1+1/\alpha,1/\alpha}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}

and

b_{n}=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha i+i+2)}{\Gamma(\alpha(i+1)+i+2)},\quad n>0,\qquad b_{0}=1.

In general,

E_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
is the three parameter Mittag-Leffler function, see
[
3
, formula (1.9.19)]
for the definition. In particular,
E_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
is an entire function.

The constant
a_{0}
is determined by the condition

\frac{1}{a_{0}}=2\int_{0}^{\infty}E_{\alpha,1+1/\alpha,1/\alpha}\left(-x^{1+\alpha}/(1+\alpha)\right)dx.

Since
{\mathscr{E}}
plays the role of the fundamental solution and it is used to represent solutions, see Proposition
2
below, we collect here its main properties. They are shown in
[
4
, Theorem 1]
:

Proposition 1
.

Let us suppose
{\mathscr{E}}
is the fundamental solution defined above. Then:

(1)
{\mathscr{E}}\in C^{2}(0,\infty)^{2}
;

(2)
{\mathscr{E}}
is positive for all
x,\ t>0
;

(3) for all
t>0
function
{\mathscr{E}}(\cdot,t)
is decreasing;

(4) for all
t>0
function
{\mathscr{E}}(\cdot,t)
is in
L^{1}(0,\infty)
and
\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathscr{E}}(x,t)dx=\frac{1}{2}.}

Let us set

\Phi(x)=E_{\alpha,1+1/\alpha,1/\alpha}\left(-\frac{x^{1+\alpha}}{1+\alpha}\right),

(7)

we will call it the profile function.
We can write
{\mathscr{E}}
shortly as follows

{\mathscr{E}}_{t}(x)=a_{0}t^{-\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}\Phi\left(\frac{x}{t^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}}\right).

(8)

What is important for the derivation of the properties of
\Phi
is not the series defining the Mittag-Leffler function, but the differential equation
\Phi
satisfied.
We recall:

Lemma 1
.

(see
[
4
, Proposition 1]
)

The function
\Phi
defined in (
7
) satisfies the following fractional differential equation, when
\alpha\in(0,1)

D^{\alpha}_{xC}v(x)=-\frac{1}{1+\alpha}xv(x),\;\;x>0,\;\;v(0)=1.

(9)

3
The decay properties of the profile function
\Phi

The goal of this subsection is to establish a number of estimates for solutions to (
9
). First, we recall a known result.

Lemma 2
.

(see
[
4
, Lemma 8]
)
Let us suppose that
\Phi
given by (
7
). Then, for all
x>0
we have

0<x\Phi(x)\leq 4.

Let us stress that for
x\in[0,1]
the above inequality is far from being useful. Indeed, monotonicity of
\Phi
guaranteed by Proposition
1
(3) and
\Phi(0)=1
imply that
\Phi(x)\leq 1
for
x\in[0,1]
.

Now, we will present step-by-step improvements of the above estimate. The goal is to show how fast
\Phi
and
\Phi^{\prime}
decay at infinity.
Here is our first observation:

Lemma 3
.

For all
x>1
, the profile function
\Phi
defined in (
7
) satisfies the following inequality

\Phi(x)\leq\Gamma(\alpha+2)\left(\frac{2}{x}\right)^{\alpha+1}.

(10)

Proof.

From Lemma
1
, we have

D^{\alpha}_{xC}\Phi(x)=-\frac{1}{1+\alpha}x\Phi(x),\ x>0,\qquad\Phi(0)=1.

(11)

After we apply the fractional Riemann-Liouville integral operator,
I^{\alpha},
to both sides of (
11
) and take into account (
5
), we obtain

\Phi(x)=1-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)(1+\alpha)}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{s\Phi(s)ds}{(x-s)^{1-\alpha}}\geq 0,\qquad x>0.

Therefore, using the monotonicity of the function
\Phi
, we get

\Gamma(\alpha)(1+\alpha)\geq\int_{0}^{x}\frac{s\Phi(s)ds}{(x-s)^{1-\alpha}}=\int_{0}^{\frac{x}{2}}\frac{s\Phi(s)ds}{(x-s)^{1-\alpha}}+\int_{\frac{x}{2}}^{x}\frac{s\Phi(s)ds}{(x-s)^{1-\alpha}}

\geq\int_{\frac{x}{2}}^{x}\frac{s\Phi(s)ds}{(x-s)^{1-\alpha}}\geq\frac{x}{2}\Phi(x)\int_{\frac{x}{2}}^{x}(x-s)^{\alpha-1}ds=\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{\alpha+1}\Phi(x),

which ends the proof.
∎

After establishing an improved decay estimate for
\Phi
we are in a position to prove an estimate for
\Phi^{\prime}
.
The lemma below plays a crucial role in estimating the solutions to (
2
).

Lemma 4
.

For all
x>0
, the derivative of the profile function
\Phi
can be estimated as follows,

|\Phi^{\prime}(x)|\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}x^{\alpha}&x\in[0,1],\\
\frac{C_{1}(\alpha)}{x}&x>1,\end{array}\right.

(12)

where

C_{1}(\alpha)=\alpha 2^{1+\alpha}\max\bigg\{\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}\frac{du}{u^{1+\alpha}(1-u)^{1-\alpha}},2\bigg\}.

Proof.

We begin with the fractional differential equation presented in Lemma
1

D^{\alpha}_{xC}\Phi(x)=-\frac{1}{1+\alpha}x\Phi(x),\;\;x>0,\;\;\Phi(0)=1.

(13)

After we apply the Riemann-Liouville derivative
D^{1-\alpha}_{xRL}
to both sides, we obtain

\Phi^{\prime}(x)=-\frac{1}{1+\alpha}D^{1-\alpha}_{xRL}[x\Phi(x)]=-\frac{1}{1+\alpha}D^{1-\alpha}_{xC}[x\Phi(x)]=-\frac{1}{1+\alpha}I^{\alpha}[x\Phi(x)]^{\prime}<0.

Using the definition of the Riemann-Liouville integral, we have

0<I^{\alpha}[x\Phi(x)]^{\prime}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{x}{\frac{(\Phi(s)+s\Phi^{\prime}(s))ds}{(x-s)^{1-\alpha}}},\;\;\;x>0.

Of course, due to monotonicuty of
\Phi
we have

0<\Phi(x)+x\Phi^{\prime}(x)=\Phi(x)-x|\Phi^{\prime}(x)|,\quad x>0.

If
x\in[0,1]
, then the above inequality implies that

|\Phi^{\prime}(x)|\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)(1+\alpha)}\int_{0}^{x}{\frac{\Phi(s)ds}{(x-s)^{1-\alpha}}}\leq\frac{\alpha}{\Gamma(2+\alpha)}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{(x-s)^{1-\alpha}}\,ds=\frac{x^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(2+\alpha)}.

Now, for
x>1
, we proceed differently. We have

|\Phi^{\prime}(x)|=-\Phi^{\prime}(x)=\frac{\alpha}{\Gamma(2+\alpha)}\bigg(\int_{0}^{ax}+\int_{ax}^{x}\bigg)\frac{(s\Phi^{\prime}(s)}{(x-s)^{1-\alpha}}\,ds=:I_{1}+I_{2},

where
a\in(0,1)
is arbitrary and we will take
a=\frac{1}{2}
. In order to estimate
I_{2}
we take into account that
\Phi^{\prime}(x)<0
and we use Lemma
3
,

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(2+\alpha)}{\alpha}I_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{ax}^{x}\frac{\Phi(s)+s\Phi^{\prime}(s)}{(x-s)^{1-\alpha}}\,ds\leq\int_{ax}^{x}\frac{\Phi(s)}{(x-s)^{1-\alpha}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{ax}^{x}\frac{\Gamma(2+\alpha)2^{1+\alpha}\,ds}{s^{1+\alpha}(x-s)^{1-\alpha}}=\frac{\Gamma(2+\alpha)2^{1+\alpha}}{x}\int_{a}^{1}\frac{du}{u^{1+\alpha}(1-u)^{1-\alpha}}.

Hence, for
a=\frac{1}{2}
we have

I_{2}\leq\alpha 2^{1+\alpha}\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}\frac{du}{u^{1+\alpha}(1-u)^{1-\alpha}}\frac{1}{x}.

In order to estimate
I_{1}
we will integrate by parts

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(2+\alpha)}{\alpha}I_{1}

\displaystyle=(\alpha-1)\int^{ax}_{0}\frac{s\Phi(s)}{(x-s)^{2-\alpha}}+\frac{s\Phi(s)}{(x-s)^{1-\alpha}}\bigg|_{s=0}^{s=ax}\leq\frac{ax\Phi(ax)}{(1-a)^{1-\alpha}x^{1-\alpha}}.

We dropped the integral because it is negative. Finally, Lemma
3
yields for
a=\frac{1}{2}
,

I_{1}\leq\frac{\alpha 2^{2+\alpha}}{x}

and

|\Phi^{\prime}(x)|\leq C_{1}(\alpha)\frac{1}{x},

where
C_{1}(\alpha)=\alpha 2^{1+\alpha}\max\{\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}\frac{du}{u^{1+\alpha}(1-u)^{1-\alpha}},2\}.

Our claim follows.
∎

Remark 2
.

Certainly, the above estimate is far from being optimal, since integration of the estimate of
\Phi^{\prime}
provided above yields a logarithmic bound on
\Phi
which is much worse than the result of Lemma
3
.

4
The convergence result

In this section, we show our convergence result.
For this purpose we recall the representation formulas.

Proposition 2
.

(see
[
4
, Theorem 4.1]
)
Let us extend
{\mathscr{E}}
to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
by the following formula

{\mathscr{E}}(x,t)={\mathscr{E}}(|x|,t)\chi_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}(t),\qquad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Let us suppose that
g\in L^{p}(0,\infty),
where
p\in[1,\infty)
and
g
has compact support, (resp.
g\in C^{0}_{c}([0,\infty))

and we set
{\mathscr{E}}_{t}(x)={\mathscr{E}}(x,t).

We define functions
w_{1}
,
w_{2}
by the following formulas,

w_{1}(x,t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}({\mathscr{E}}_{t}(x-y)-{\mathscr{E}}_{t}(x+y))g(y)\,dy,

(14)

w_{2}(x,t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}({\mathscr{E}}_{t}(x-y)+{\mathscr{E}}_{t}(x+y))g(y)\,dy.

(15)

Then,

(a) For all
t>0
functions
w_{1}(\cdot,t)
and
w_{2}(\cdot,t)
belong to
C^{1+\alpha}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Moreover,
w_{1}
(resp.
w_{2}
) is a classical solution to (
2
), (
3
) (resp. (
2
), (
4
)).

(b) The functions
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
belong to
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))
if
p<\infty
(resp.
w_{1},w_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};C(\mathbb{R}_{+}))
, when
g\in C^{0}_{c}([0,\infty))
).
Moreover,

\lim_{t\to 0}\|w_{1}(\cdot,t)-g\|_{L^{p}}=0,\qquad(\hbox{resp. }\lim_{t\to 0}\|w_{2}(\cdot,t)-g\|_{L^{\infty}}=0).

When
g
is continuous, we require
g(0)=0
in case of the Dirichlet boundary condition, (
3
).

Our asymptotic analysis depends in a crucial manner on this representation formula. Here is the result which follows from (
14
) and (
15
).

Theorem 1
.

Let us suppose that
g,\ yg(\cdot)\in L^{1}(0,\infty)
have a compact support,
\theta
is any fixed number from the interval
(0,1)
,

m=\int_{0}^{\infty}g(y)dy,

(16)

and
w_{1}
, (resp.
w_{2}
), is defined by (
14
), (resp. (
15
)).
Then,

(a) there is
C_{1}>0
such that

\|w_{1}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1/(1-\theta)}}\leq C_{1}t^{-(1+\theta)/(1+\alpha)}\|yg(\cdot)\|_{L^{1}},\qquad t>1;

(b) there is
C_{2}>0
such that

\|w_{2}(\cdot,t)-2m{\mathscr{E}}_{t}(\cdot)\|_{L^{1/(1-\theta)}}\leq C_{2}t^{-(1+\theta)/(1+\alpha)}\|yg(\cdot)\|_{L^{1}},\qquad t>1;

(c) for
C_{2}(\alpha)
defined in Lemma
5
we have

\|w_{2}(\cdot,t)-2m{\mathscr{E}}_{t}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}},\quad\|w_{1}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{C_{2}(\alpha)}{t^{2/(1+\alpha)}}\|yg(\cdot)\|_{L^{1}},\qquad t>1.

Before we start the proof we notice that the two key objects we have to study are

v^{+}(x,t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathscr{E}}_{t}(x+y)g(y)\,dy,\qquad v^{-}(x,t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathscr{E}}_{t}(x-y)g(y)\,dy.

(17)

Here is our main observation.

Lemma 5
.

Let us suppose that
g
satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
1
,
m
is given by (
16
) and
v^{+}
,
v^{-}
are defined in (
17
). Then,

|v^{\pm}(x,t)-m{\mathscr{E}}_{t}(x)|\leq\frac{C_{2}(\alpha)}{t^{1/(1+\alpha)}}\|yg(\cdot)\|_{L^{1}}\bigg(\frac{1}{t^{1/(1+\alpha)}}\chi_{[0,t^{1/(1+\alpha)}]}(x)+\frac{1}{x}\chi_{(t^{1/(1+\alpha)},\infty)}(x)\bigg)\qquad\hbox{for }t>1,

where
C_{2}(\alpha)=2a_{0}C_{1}(\alpha)
and
C_{1}(\alpha)
is defined in Lemma
4
.

Proof.

We will first estimate
v^{+}-{\mathscr{E}}_{t}
.
Let us notice that due to the definitions of
{\mathscr{E}}_{t}
and
m
, see (
8
) and (
16
), we have

\displaystyle v^{+}(x,t)-m{\mathscr{E}}_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{0}}{t^{1/(1+\alpha)}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\Phi\bigg(\frac{x+y}{t^{1/(1+\alpha)}}\bigg)-\Phi\bigg(\frac{x}{t^{1/(1+\alpha)}}\bigg)\bigg)g(y)\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{0}}{t^{1/(1+\alpha)}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{y}{t^{1/(1+\alpha)}}\Phi^{\prime}\bigg(\frac{x+sy}{t^{1/(1+\alpha)}}\bigg)\,dsg(y)\,dy=:I.

We consider two cases:
\frac{x}{t^{1/(1+\alpha)}}<1
and
\frac{x}{t^{1/(1+\alpha)}}\geq 1
.

When
\frac{x}{t^{1/(1+\alpha)}}<1
we set
y_{0}=t^{1/(1+\alpha)}-x
and
\xi=\frac{x+sy}{t^{1/(1+\alpha)}}
. We calculate

I=\frac{a_{0}}{t^{2/(1+\alpha)}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{y_{0}}+\int_{y_{0}}^{\infty}\bigg)\int_{0}^{1}y\Phi^{\prime}\big(\xi\big)\,dsg(y)\,dy=I_{1}+I_{2}.

We first estimate
|I_{1}|
by noticing that
|\xi|\leq 1
and
x+y_{0}=t^{1/(1+\alpha)}
,

\displaystyle|I_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{0}}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)t^{2/(1+\alpha)}}\int_{0}^{y_{0}}\int_{0}^{1}y|\xi|^{\alpha}\,ds|g(y)|\,dy\leq\frac{a_{0}}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)t^{2/(1+\alpha)}}\int_{0}^{y_{0}}y|g(y)|\,dy,

where we applied Lemma
4
to bound
\Phi^{\prime}.

Now, we estimate
I_{2}
and again we will use Lemma
4
,

\displaystyle|I_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{0}C_{1}(\alpha)}{t^{2/(1+\alpha)}}\int_{y_{0}}^{\infty}\bigg(\int_{0}^{s_{0}(y)}\bigg(\frac{x+sy}{t^{1/(1+\alpha)}}\bigg)^{\alpha}\,ds+\int_{s_{0}(y)}^{1}\frac{t^{1/(1+\alpha)}}{x+sy}\,ds\bigg)y|g(y)|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{0}C_{1}(\alpha)}{t^{2/(1+\alpha)}}\int_{y_{0}}^{\infty}y|g(y)|\,dy,

where we set
s_{0}(y)=(t^{1/(1+\alpha)}-x)/y
for
y\geq y_{0}
. Hence,

|I|\leq\frac{a_{0}C_{1}(\alpha)}{t^{2/(1+\alpha)}}\int_{0}^{\infty}y|g(y)|\,dy

for
x<t^{1/(1+\alpha)}
.

Now, we take care of the second case, i.e.
\frac{x}{t^{1/(1+\alpha)}}\geq 1
. If this happens, then the argument of
\Phi^{\prime}
is always greater than 1. Hence, we proceed as in the estimate of
I_{2}
above, however,
y_{0}=0
. Thus,

|I|\leq\frac{a_{0}C_{1}(\alpha)}{xt^{1/(1+\alpha)}}\int_{0}^{\infty}y|g(y)|\,dy.

Our claim follows for
v^{+}
.

Now, we turn our attention to
v^{-}-m{\mathscr{E}}_{t}
. We notice

v^{-}(x,t)-m{\mathscr{E}}_{t}(x)=\frac{a_{0}}{t^{1/(1+\alpha)}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\Phi\bigg(\frac{x-y}{t^{1/(1+\alpha)}}\bigg)-\Phi\bigg(\frac{x}{t^{1/(1+\alpha)}}\bigg)\bigg)g(y)\,dy.

We also see that
\Phi
extended to negative arguments is differentiable at
0
due to (
12
). Hence,

v^{-}(x,t)-m{\mathscr{E}}_{t}(x)=-\frac{a_{0}}{t^{2/(1+\alpha)}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}y\Phi^{\prime}\bigg(\frac{x-sy}{t^{1/(1+\alpha)}}\bigg)\,dsg(y)\,dy=:I.

We must take into account the possibility that the argument of the integrand is negative. We recall that our datum
g
has a compact support, we may assume that
\hbox{supp}\,g\subset[0,R]
. Since we are interested in the time assymptotics we may consider only
t>R^{1+\alpha}.

We will come up with different estimates for
|v^{-}(x,t)-m{\mathscr{E}}_{t}(x)|
when
x<2R
and
x\geq 2R
.

Let us consider
x<2R
. Then we notice

\bigg|\Phi^{\prime}\bigg(\frac{x-sy}{t^{1/(1+\alpha)}}\bigg)\bigg|\leq C_{1}(\alpha).

Indeed, if
\xi
denotes the argument of
\Phi^{\prime}
, then
Lemma
4
yields
|\Phi^{\prime}(\xi)|\leq\frac{|\xi|^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}\leq 1
, when
|\xi|\leq 1
and
|\Phi^{\prime}(\xi)|\leq\frac{C_{1}(\alpha)}{|\xi|}\leq C_{1}(\alpha)
for
|\xi|>1
. Hence

|v^{-}(x,t)-m{\mathscr{E}}_{t}(x)|\leq C_{1}(\alpha),\qquad\hbox{for }x\leq\max\{2R,t^{1/(1+\alpha)}\}.

When
x>\max\{2R,t^{1/(1+\alpha)}\}
, then for
s\in[0,1]
and
y\in[0,R]
we have

|x-sy|/t^{1/(1+\alpha)}>1
. Hence,

\bigg|\Phi^{\prime}\bigg(\frac{x-sy}{t^{1/(1+\alpha)}}\bigg)\bigg|\leq t^{1/(1+\alpha)}\frac{C_{1}(\alpha)}{x-sy}.

As a result, we have

\displaystyle|I|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{0}C_{1}(\alpha)}{t^{1/(1+\alpha)}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{y}{x-sy}\,ds|g(y)|\,dy\leq\frac{a_{0}C_{1}(\alpha)}{t^{1/(1+\alpha)}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{y}{x-y}|g(y)|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2a_{0}C_{1}(\alpha)}{t^{1/(1+\alpha)}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{y}{x}|g(y)|\,dy,

where we also used
1/(x-y)\leq 2/x
for
x>2R.

∎

We are now ready for
the proof of Theorem
1
.

We are going to establish the estimates for
v^{\pm}-m{\mathscr{E}}_{t}
.
For this purpose we will use the above Lemma and the following Marcinkiewicz Interpolation Theorem:

Proposition 3
.

(see
[
1
, Theorem 6.0.2]
,
[

, Theorem 1]
)

Let
(M.\Sigma,\mu)
and
(N,\Xi,\nu)
be two
\sigma
-finite measure spaces. Let
1\leq p_{0}\leq q_{0}\leq\infty
,
1\leq p_{1}\leq q_{1}\leq\infty
and

T:L^{p_{0}}(M,\mu)+L^{p_{1}}(M,\mu)
into the
\Xi
-measurable functions on
N
so that
T
is of the weak types
(p_{0},q_{0})
and
(p_{1},q_{1})
with constants
C_{0}
and
C_{1}
, respectively. Then, for any
\theta\in(0,1)
there is a constant
C_{\theta}
depending only on
\theta,

p_{j}
,
q_{j}
,
C_{j}
,
j=0,1
, so that

\|Tf\|_{q_{\theta}}\leq KC_{0}^{1-\theta}C_{1}^{\theta}\|f\|_{p_{\theta}},

where

\frac{1}{p_{\theta}}=\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}+\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}},\qquad\frac{1}{q_{\theta}}=\frac{\theta}{q_{1}}+\frac{1-\theta}{q_{0}}

and
K
is independent of
f
, it is bounded if
p_{0}
,
p_{1},

q_{0}
,
q_{1}
are fixed and
\theta
stays away from
0
and
1
.

Lemma
5

gives us two types of results. First we note that

(M,\Sigma,\mu)=(\mathbb{R}_{+},{\mathscr{A}},y\mathcal{L}^{1}),\qquad(N,\Sigma,\nu)=(\mathbb{R}_{+},{\mathscr{A}},\mathcal{L}^{1}),

where
{\mathscr{A}}
is the
\sigma
-field of Lebesgue measurable sets,
\mathcal{L}^{1}
is the one-dimensional Lebesgue measure. For a fixed
t>0
we set

T^{\pm}g=v^{\pm}(t,\cdot)-m{\mathscr{E}}_{t}
. For a fixed
\theta
we set

p_{0}=1=p_{1},\qquad q_{0}=1,\ q_{1}=\infty.

We claim that
T^{\pm}
are of type
(1,\infty)
with constant
C_{1}=C_{2}(\alpha)t^{-2/(1+\alpha)}
, i.e.,

\|v^{\pm}-m{\mathscr{E}}_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{2}(\alpha)t^{-2/(1+\alpha)}\|yg(\cdot)\|_{L^{1}}=C_{2}(\alpha)t^{-2/(1+\alpha)}\|g\|_{L^{1}_{\mu}}.

Indeed, for
x<t^{1/(1+\alpha)}
, we have

|v^{\pm}(x,t)-m{\mathscr{E}}_{t}(x)|\leq C_{2}(\alpha)t^{-2/(1+\alpha)}\|g\|_{L^{1}_{\mu}}.

For

|(T^{\pm}g)(x)|\leq C_{2}(\alpha)t^{-1/(1+\alpha)}x^{-1}\|yg(\cdot)\|_{L^{1}}\leq C_{2}(\alpha)t^{-2/(1+\alpha)}\|g\|_{L^{1}_{\mu}}.

We are going to check that the mapping
T^{\pm}
is of the weak type
(1,1)
, i.e. we will check that

\sup_{\sigma>0}\sigma|\{x:|T^{\pm}g(x)|>\sigma\}|\leq C_{2}(\alpha)t^{-1/(1+\alpha)}\|g\|_{L^{1}_{\mu}}.

Indeed, this immediately follows from Lemma
5
, moreover
C_{0}=C_{2}(\alpha)t^{-1/(1+\alpha)}
.

Now, we are going to use the Marcinkiewicz Interpolation Theorem, Proposition
3
, with

p=1,\qquad q=1/(1-\theta)

and

C_{0}^{1-\theta}C_{1}^{\theta}=t^{-(1+\theta)/(1+\alpha)}.

Hence, for
\theta\in(0,1)
we have

\|v^{\pm}(x,t)-m{\mathscr{E}}_{t}(x)\|_{L^{1/(1-\theta)}}\leq KC_{2}(\alpha)t^{-(1+\theta)/(1+\alpha)},

where

different from
0
and

Thus,

\displaystyle\|w_{1}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1/(1-\theta)}}

\displaystyle\leq\|v^{+}(x,t)-m{\mathscr{E}}_{t}(x)\|_{L^{1/(1-\theta)}}+\|v^{-}(x,t)-m{\mathscr{E}}_{t}(x)\|_{L^{1/(1-\theta)}}

\displaystyle\leq 2KC_{2}(\alpha)t^{-(1+\theta)/(1+\alpha)}\|g\|_{L^{1}_{\mu}}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\|w_{2}(\cdot,t)-2m{\mathscr{E}}_{t}(\cdot)\|_{L^{1/(1-\theta)}}

\displaystyle\leq\|v^{+}(x,t)-m{\mathscr{E}}_{t}(x)\|_{L^{1/(1-\theta)}}+\|v^{-}(x,t)-m{\mathscr{E}}_{t}(x)\|_{L^{1/(1-\theta)}}

\displaystyle\leq 2KC_{2}(\alpha)t^{-(1+\theta)/(1+\alpha)}\|g\|_{L^{1}_{\mu}}.

Part (c) follows immediately from Lemma
5
. ∎

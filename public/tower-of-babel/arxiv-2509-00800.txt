SWAGSplatting: Semantic-guided Water-scene Augmented Gaussian Splatting 179 Journal: TOG CCS: Computing methodologies Reconstruction
Zhuodong Jiang, Haoran Wang, Guoxi Huang, Brett Seymour, Nantheera Anantrasirichai

Abstract
. Accurate 3D reconstruction in underwater environments remains a complex challenge due to issues such as light distortion, turbidity, and limited visibility. AI-based techniques have been applied to address these issues, however, existing methods have yet to fully exploit the potential of AI, particularly in integrating language models with visual processing. In this paper, we propose a novel framework that leverages multimodal cross-knowledge to create semantic-guided 3D Gaussian Splatting for robust and high-fidelity deep-sea scene reconstruction. By embedding an extra semantic feature into each Gaussian primitive and supervised by the CLIP extracted semantic feature, our method enforces semantic and structural awareness throughout the training. The dedicated semantic consistency loss ensures alignment with high-level scene understanding. Besides, we propose a novel stage-wise training strategy, combining coarse-to-fine learning with late-stage parameter refinement, to further enhance both stability and reconstruction quality. Extensive results show that our approach consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods on SeaThru-NeRF and Submerged3D datasets across three metrics, with an improvement of up to 3.09 dB on average in terms of PSNR, making it a strong candidate for applications in underwater exploration and marine perception.

1.
Introduction

Underwater exploration supports a wide range of applications, including marine ecology, archaeology, and robotics. These tasks rely heavily on effective 3D reconstruction for interpretation and navigation. High-quality 3D visuals are particularly critical when deployed on VR headsets, as in educational and creative industry applications. However, underwater environments present unique challenges: limited visibility, colour distortion, sparse viewpoints, and noise due to light attenuation and scattering. Additionally, data acquisition is costly and often sparse, making reconstruction in such settings especially challenging.

Recent advances in Neural Rendering have opened new possibilities for 3D reconstruction under limited input, especially through Novel View Synthesis (NVS) methods. The Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF)
(
Mildenhall et al. 2021
)
achieves high-quality results but requires long training times and lacks flexibility. In contrast, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)
(
Kerbl et al. 2023
)
offers real-time rendering and higher refresh rates using explicit point-based scene representations. However, most NeRF and 3DGS-based methods are built based on the assumption of clear media, and their performance degrades severely in turbid underwater conditions
(
Liu et al. 2024
)
.

Previous methods for underwater 3D reconstruction
(
Levy et al. 2023
;
Jiang et al. 2025
)
typically treat all regions uniformly; however, in underwater environments, salient objects—which usually appear in the foreground—deserve greater attention. Without explicit guidance, gradients from these regions are diluted by the background during training, leading to reconstructions where salient objects appear blurred and lack fidelity. This also provides an advantage for peripheral vision in VR applications.

2.
Preliminaries

3D Gaussian Splatting.

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)
(
Kerbl et al. 2023
)
represents a scene as a set of 3D anisotropic Gaussians, each defined by its position
\bm{\mu}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, covariance
\Sigma_{i}
, view-dependent colour
c_{i}
, and opacity
\alpha_{i}
. To render an image, Gaussians
G
are projected onto the 2D image plane, where their contribution to pixel
\bm{x}
is given by:

(1)

G_{i}(\bm{x})=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}(\bm{x}-\bm{\mu}_{i})^{\top}\Sigma_{i}^{-1}(\bm{x}-\bm{\mu}_{i})\right).

The final colour is computed via alpha blending:

(2)

C=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}c_{i}(\mathbf{v})\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}(1-\alpha_{j}),

where
c_{i}(\mathbf{v})
denotes the SH-based view-dependent appearance.

Underwater image formation.

Underwater scenes are strongly affected by light scattering and absorption, which significantly degrade image quality and thus the reconstruction performance
(
Tang et al. 2024
)
. The observed colour
I_{c}
at a camera pixel can be modelled as:

(3)

I_{c}=J\cdot T^{D}+B^{\infty}\cdot(1-T^{B}),

where
J
is the true scene radiance,
B^{\infty}
is background light, and
T^{D}
,
T^{B}
represent the transmission of direct and backscattered light:

(4)

T^{D}=\exp(-\beta^{d}\cdot z),\quad T^{B}=\exp(-\beta^{b}\cdot z),

with
\beta^{d}
,
\beta^{b}
denoting attenuation coefficients and
z
the depth. These effects are crucial to model when adapting 3DGS to underwater environments.

The detailed structure of the proposed framework is presented in Fig.
2
. Inspired by RUSplatting
(
Jiang et al. 2025
)
, which utilises an MLP to estimate the RGB decouple underwater medium, employing a frame interpolation mechanism with the uncertainty parameter and integrating an edge-aware smooth loss into the optimisation. We further advance this model by adding a semantic-guided feature into the Gaussians and applying a stage-wise optimisation strategy to better emphasise the underwater object instead of the medium itself.

3.1.
Semantic-guided Gaussians

To improve the reconstruction quality of underwater scenes, we incorporate semantic guidance into the 3DGS framework. Each Gaussian is augmented with a learnable semantic feature vector
f_{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
d
is the projected dimension of the semantic embedding space. Unlike spatial or appearance attributes,
f_{s}
is optimised with external supervision to capture high-level semantic information.

To obtain reference embeddings, we first generate textual descriptions for each scene using BLIPo3
(
Chen et al. 2025
)
, which guide Grounded-SAM
(
Ren et al. 2024
)
to capture regions of interest in the input image
I_{img}
.

(5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle=\text{Grounded-SAM}(I_{img},\text{caption}),

\displaystyle I_{\mathcal{R}}

\displaystyle=I_{img}[\mathcal{R}],

\displaystyle f_{\text{ref}}

\displaystyle={\text{CLIP}}(I_{\mathcal{R}}),

where
\mathcal{R}
is the bounding box of the detected object and
f_{\text{ref}}
denotes the CLIP embedding of the detected region.

During training, all Gaussians whose projections fall inside the region
\mathcal{R}
are encouraged to align their semantic features with
f_{\text{ref}}
via the following loss:

(6)

L_{s}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{R}}\|f_{s}^{(i)}-f_{\text{ref}}\|_{2}^{2}.

This semantic alignment enforces object-level consistency, allowing the model to preserve meaningful structure even under sparse-view or noisy underwater conditions, thereby enhancing both the fidelity and interpretability of the reconstructed scenes.

3.2.
Stage-wise Optimisation Strategy

The overall training follows a two-stage schedule, guided by a composite loss function. Our training loss follows the design of RUSplatting
(
Jiang et al. 2025
)
, consisting of a weighted combination of reconstruction loss
L_{\text{Rec}}
, depth-supervised loss
L_{\text{Depth}}
, grey-world assumption loss
L_{g}
and the edge-aware smoothness loss
L_{\text{Smooth}}
. We further improve optimisation by integrating our proposed semantic loss
L_{s}
and introducing an addition pixel-wise MSE loss
\ell_{2}
loss
L_{2}
. The final loss function is calculated as,

(7)

L_{\text{final}}=L_{\text{Rec}}+L_{\text{Depth}}+L_{g}+L_{\text{Smooth}}+\lambda_{s}L_{s}+\lambda_{2}L_{2},

where
\lambda_{s}
and
\lambda_{2}
are the weights for the semantic loss and the
\ell_{2}
loss, respectively. Accordingly, the loss function for the interpolated frame remains as,

(8)

L_{\text{total}}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\gamma\cdot L_{\text{total}}-\frac{1}{2}\cdot\alpha\cdot\log(\gamma),

where
\gamma
is the learned uncertainty and
\alpha
acts as the regularisation term.

To ensure training robustness in noisy and sparse-view underwater scenes, we adopt a stage-wise optimisation strategy. During the initial 60% of iterations, we focus on coarse structure and robustness by emphasising the
\ell_{1}
-based reconstruction loss and enforcing semantic alignment. While in the final 40%, we freeze the geometric and semantic parameters for the Gaussians, namely position
f_{xyz}
, rotation
f_{\text{rotation}}
, scale
f_{\text{scale}}
, and semantic feature
f_{s}
, and switch focus to fine appearance refinement and the colour restoration. Furthermore, in the final stage, we reduce the weight of
\ell_{1}
, which is embedded into the
L_{\text{Rec}}
and increase the weight of the
\ell_{2}
loss term. This term promotes sharper detail reconstruction and fine-tuning of opacity and appearance parameters. By adopting such a coarse-to-fine stage-wise strategy, the model achieves high fidelity and quality reconstruction.

4.1.
Experiment

The experiments are conducted on a single RTX 4090 GPU. All experiments are run for 20,000 iterations, hence, the second stage of the training starts at the 12,000
th
iteration. The
f_{\text{ref}}
is computed at the beginning of the training and cached, in order to be computationally efficient. The projection of the CLIP embedding dimension
d
is set to 32. Additionally, we adopt a fixed weight of 0.1 for the interpolation frame instead of applying adaptive weights.

Evaluation

Two datasets were used: SeaThru-NeRF
(
Levy et al. 2023
)
and Submerged3D
(
Jiang et al. 2025
)

(

)
, SeaThru-NeRF
(

)
, 3DGS
(
Kerbl et al. 2023

4.3.
Results

Quantitative Comparisons.

Tab.
1
summarises the quantitative comparison with the other six state-of-the-art methods, showing average rendering performance across all scenes in each dataset. Our SWAGSplatting consistently surpasses all baseline models across the three metrics. In terms of PSNR, it achieves improvements of 2.70 and 3.48 dB on SeaThru-NeRF and Submerged3D, respectively. Additionally, SWAGSplatting yields improvement on SSIM and LPIPS, with an increase on SSIM of 11.42% and a decrease on LPIPS of 34.80% on SeaThru-NeRF. On Submerged3D, it gains 18.30% on SSIM and reduces 23.03% on LPIPS.

Ablation Study.

To evaluate the effectiveness of each component in SWAGSplatting, we conducted ablation studies. The configurations are listed in Tab.
2
, and the results are shown in Fig.

Comparing M1 with M3 and M2 with SWAGSplatting, removing this component leads to average drops of 0.91% and 1.62% in PSNR and SSIM, respectively, and an increase of 1.13% in LPIPS, indicating that semantic guidance yields more perceptually faithful results. (ii) Although the stage-wise optimisation component brings a smaller gain than the semantic-guided one, it still contributes positively: removing it degrades performance, as evidenced by decreases from M2 to M1 and from SWAGSplatting to M3.

5.
Conclusions

This paper presents an enhanced semantic-guided 3D Gaussian Splatting framework tailored for robust underwater scene reconstruction. To be more detailed, we integrate an extra semantic feature into each Gaussian point. By introducing the semantic loss through the CLIP-supervised semantic feature into the loss function, we enhance the model’s ability in high-level structural consistency and superior reconstruction quality. Additionally, we adopt a stage-wise optimisation strategy to further improve training stability and visual fidelity, combining coarse-to-fine optimisation with late-stage refinement via
\ell_{2}
fine-tuning and parameter freezing. These improvements collectively enable more accurate, consistent, and visually realistic reconstructions of complex underwater environments.

Transverse-momentum resummation at mixed QCD ⊗ \otimes QED NNLL accuracy for 𝒁 Z boson production at hadron colliders Preprint: FTUV-25-0731.8337
Andrea Autieri, Stefano Camarda, Leandro Cieri, Giancarlo Ferrera, and German F. R. Sborlini

Abstract
We consider the transverse momentum ( q T q_{T} ) distribution of neutral charged bosons at hadron colliders. We perform the resummation of the logarithmically-enhanced effects due to simultaneous QCD and QED initial-state radiation, up to mixed next-to-next-to-leading logarithmic (NNLL) accuracy. We study the impact of such mixed QCD ⊗ \otimes QED resummed contributions on top of pure QCD corrections, finding percent-level effects.

2
QCD transverse-momentum resummation formalism

In this section, we address the transverse-momentum resummation formalism of Refs.
Catani:2000vq
;
Bozzi:2005wk
for vector boson production at hadron colliders. We consider the following process:

h_{1}(p_{1})+h_{2}(p_{2})\rightarrow V(M,q_{T})+X

(1)

where
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
are the momenta of the colliding hadrons,
M
and
q_{T}
are the invariant mass and the transverse momentum of the triggered final state, respectively. Here,
X
denotes undetected radiation under which we are totally inclusive.

According to the factorization theorem
Collins:1989gx
, the
q_{T}
-differential cross-section can be written as:

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma_{h_{1}h_{2}\rightarrow V}}{dq_{T}^{2}}(q_{T},M,s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a_{1},a_{2}}\int dx_{1}dx_{2}\,f_{a_{1}/h_{1}}(x_{1},\mu_{F}^{2})\,f_{a_{2}/h_{2}}(x_{2},\mu_{F}^{2})\,

(2)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\frac{d\hat{\sigma}_{a_{1}a_{2}\rightarrow V}}{dq_{T}^{2}}(q_{T},M,\hat{s};\mu_{F}^{2})\,,

where
\sqrt{s}
is the hadronic centre-of-mass energy (
s=(p_{1}+p_{2})^{2}\simeq 2p_{1}p_{2}
),
f_{a_{i}/h_{i}}(x_{i},\mu_{F}^{2})
are the parton distribution functions (PDF) at the factorization scale
\mu_{F}
,
\hat{\sigma}_{a_{1}a_{2}}
is the partonic cross section,
\hat{s}=x_{1}x_{2}s
is the partonic centre-of-mass energy and
\mu_{R}
is the renormalization scale. The partonic cross-section can thus be calculated using perturbation theory, whereas the non-perturbative dependence of the hadron process is universal and embodied within the PDFs.

Higher-order perturbative contributions to the partonic cross-section are enhanced in the small
q_{T}
region (i.e.
q_{T}\ll M
) by logarithmic terms proportional to

(\alpha_{\rm S}^{n}/q_{T}^{2})\log^{m}(M^{2}/q_{T}^{2})\,.

(3)

Therefore, we explicitly split the partonic cross-section as follows:

\frac{d\hat{\sigma}_{a_{1}a_{2}\rightarrow V}}{dq_{T}^{2}}=\frac{d\hat{\sigma}^{\text{res.}}_{a_{1}a_{2}\rightarrow V}}{dq_{T}^{2}}+\frac{d\hat{\sigma}^{\text{fin.}}_{a_{1}a_{2}\rightarrow V}}{dq_{T}^{2}},

(4)

where
d\sigma^{\text{res.}}
contains the large logarithmic terms while the finite remainder
d\hat{\sigma}^{\text{fin.}}
fulfills

\lim_{Q_{T}\rightarrow 0}\int_{0}^{Q_{T}^{2}}dq_{T}^{2}\frac{d\hat{\sigma}^{\text{fin.}}}{dq_{T}^{2}}=0\ ,

(5)

and is, by definition, free of logarithmically-enhanced contributions. In this article, we deal only with the resummed part, which is dominant in the small
q_{T}
-region
Bozzi:2010xn
.

In order to impose transverse-momentum conservation, the resummation is performed in impact parameter space
b
, which is the Fourier-Bessel conjugated to
q_{T}
. Thus, we have

\frac{d\hat{\sigma}_{a_{1}a_{2}\rightarrow V}^{\rm{res.}}}{dq_{T}^{2}}(q_{T};M,\hat{s};\alpha_{\rm S}(\mu_{R}^{2}),\mu_{F}^{2},\mu_{R}^{2})=\frac{M^{2}}{\hat{s}}\int_{0}^{\infty}db\frac{b}{2}J_{0}(bq_{T})

\times\mathcal{W}_{a_{1}a_{2}}^{V}(b;M,\hat{s},\alpha_{\rm S}(\mu_{R}^{2}),\mu_{F}^{2},\mu_{R}^{2}),

(6)

with
J_{0}(x)
the 0th-order Bessel function. Then, we consider the Mellin
N
-moments with respect to
z=M^{2}/\hat{s}
at fixed
M
:

\mathcal{W}^{V}_{ab,N}(b,M;\alpha_{\rm S}(\mu_{R}^{2}),\mu_{R}^{2},\mu_{F}^{2})=\int_{0}^{1}dz\,z^{N-1}\mathcal{W}^{V}_{ab}(b,M,\hat{s}=M^{2}/z;\alpha_{\rm S}(\mu_{R}^{2}),\mu_{R}^{2},\mu_{F}^{2}).

(7)

In the simplified flavour diagonal case
(a,b)=(q,\bar{q})
in the Mellin space, we have an exponentiation of the logarithmic terms according to:

\mathcal{W}_{N}^{V}(b,M;\alpha_{\rm S}(\mu_{R}^{2}),\mu_{R}^{2},\mu_{F}^{2})=\mathcal{H}_{N}^{V}(M,\alpha_{\rm S}(\mu_{R}^{2});M^{2}/\mu_{R}^{2},M^{2}/\mu_{F}^{2},M^{2}/Q^{2})

\times\exp\left\{\mathcal{G}_{N}(\alpha_{\rm S}(\mu_{R}^{2}),L;M^{2}/\mu_{R}^{2},M^{2}/Q^{2})\right\},

(8)

where

L=\log\left(\frac{Q^{2}b^{2}}{b_{0}^{2}}\right),

(9)

Q
is the resummation scale (introduced to parametrize the arbitrariness of the factorization between the fixed order and resummed contributions) and
b_{0}=2e^{-\gamma_{E}}
, being
\gamma_{E}=0.5772\ldots
the Euler-Mascheroni constant.

Studying the ingredients of Eq. (
8
),
\exp\{{\mathcal{G}}_{N}\}
contains and resums all the logarithmic enhanced terms while
\mathcal{H}^{V}
includes hard-virtual contributions that behave like constants as
b\rightarrow\infty
. The exponent
\mathcal{G}_{N}
can be systematically expanded as:

\mathcal{G}_{N}(\alpha_{\rm S},L;M^{2}/\mu_{R}^{2},M^{2}/Q^{2})=L\,g_{N}^{(1)}(\alpha_{\rm S}L)+g_{N}^{(2)}(\alpha_{\rm S}L)+\sum_{n=3}^{+\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha_{\rm S}}{\pi}\right)^{n-2}g_{N}^{(n)}(\alpha_{\rm S}L)\,.

(10)

Therefore Eq. (
8
) constitutes a perturbative expansion in the parameter
\alpha_{\rm S}L
:
Lg^{(1)}
resums the
leading logarithmic
(LL) contributions
\alpha_{\rm S}^{n}L^{n+1}
,
g_{N}^{2}
together with
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{V(1)}
control the
next-to-leading logarithmic
(NLL) contributions
\alpha_{\rm S}^{n}L^{n}
,
g_{N}^{3}
together with
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{V(2)}
collect the
next-to-next-to-leading logarithmic
(NNLL) terms
\alpha_{\rm S}^{n}L^{n-1}
and so on.

The functions
g_{N}^{(n)}
can be explicitly obtained from the integral representation of
\mathcal{G}_{N}
:

\mathcal{G}_{N}\big(\alpha_{\rm S}(\mu_{R}^{2}),L;M^{2}/\mu_{R}^{2},M^{2}/Q^{2}\big)=-\int_{b_{0}^{2}/b^{2}}^{Q^{2}}\frac{dq^{2}}{q^{2}}\left[A(\alpha_{\rm S}(q^{2}))\log\frac{M^{2}}{q^{2}}+\widetilde{B}_{N}(\alpha_{\rm S}(q^{2}))\right]

(11)

where
A(\alpha_{\rm S})
and
\widetilde{B}_{N}(\alpha_{\rm S})
are perturbative series in the strong coupling that can be obtained from the logarithmic terms in the perturbative expansion of the
q_{T}
distribution at the
n
-th relative order in
\alpha_{\rm S}

Bozzi:2005wk
.

The explicit expression of the
g_{N}^{(1)}
,
g_{N}^{(2)}
and
g_{N}^{(3)}
coefficients can be found in Ref.
Bozzi:2005wk
,
g_{N}^{(4)}
in Ref.
Bizon:2017rah
for the related case of direct transverse momentum space resummation and
g_{N}^{(5)}
in Ref.
Camarda:2023dqn
.

The hard-virtual coefficient
\mathcal{H}_{N}^{V}
, can be expanded as:

\mathcal{H}_{N}^{V}=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha_{\rm S}}{\pi}\right)^{n}\mathcal{H}_{N}^{V(n)}\,,

(12)

with
\mathcal{H}^{V(n)}
depending on the process. These coefficients can be written in terms of
H^{V}(\alpha_{\rm S})
(which is associated to finite contribution of the multi-loop virtual scattering amplitude) and the process-independent functions
C(\alpha_{\rm S})
related to the collinear emissions from the initial-state partons according to
Catani:2012qa
;
Catani:2013tia
:

\mathcal{H}_{q\bar{q}\rightarrow ab}^{V}(z;\alpha_{\rm S})=H_{q}^{V}(\alpha_{\rm S})\int_{0}^{1}dz_{1}\int_{0}^{1}dz_{2}\,\delta(z-z_{1}z_{2})C_{qa}(z_{1};\alpha_{\rm S})C_{\bar{q}b}(z_{2};\alpha_{\rm S})\,.

(13)

The hard-virtual factor can be expanded in perturbative QCD series as

H^{V}(\alpha_{\rm S})=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}H^{V(n)},

(14)

and the collinear coefficient functions,

C(\alpha_{\rm S})=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha_{\rm S}}{\pi}\right)^{n}C^{(n)}.

(15)

At NLL+NLO we include the functions
g_{N}^{(1)}
,
g_{N}^{(2)}
and
{\cal H}_{N}^{V(1)}
,
at NNLL+NNLO we also include the functions
g_{N}^{(3)}
and
{\cal H}_{N}^{V(2)}

Catani:2012qa
;
Gehrmann:2012ze
, at N
3
LL+N
3
LO the functions
g_{N}^{(4)}
and
{\cal H}_{N}^{V(3)}

Luo:2019szz
;
Ebert:2020yqt
and finally at N
4
LL+N
3
LO the function
g_{N}^{(5)}
and
{\cal H}_{N}^{V(4)}
.

3
Combined QED and QCD transverse-momentum resummation formalism

When simultaneously dealing with QCD and QED corrections, the resummed component in Mellin and b-space can be written as
Autieri:2023xme
;
Cieri:2018sfk
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{N}^{{}^{\prime}V}(b,M;\mu_{F})=

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}_{V}^{(0)}(M)\,\mathcal{H}_{N}^{{}^{\prime}V}(\alpha_{\rm S},\alpha;M^{2}/\mu_{R}^{2},M^{2}/\mu_{F}^{2},M^{2}/Q^{2})

(16)

\displaystyle\times\exp\left\{\mathcal{G}_{N}^{\prime}(\alpha_{\rm S},\alpha,L;M^{2}/\mu_{R}^{2},M^{2}/Q^{2})\right\},

where the resummed form factor and the hard-collinear coefficient functions have a double perturbative expansion in
\alpha_{\rm S}
and
\alpha
, respectively. Thus we write:

\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{N}(\alpha_{\rm S},\alpha,L)=&\mathcal{G}_{N}(\alpha_{\rm S},L)+L\,g^{\prime(1)}(\alpha L)+g^{\prime(2)}_{N}(\alpha L)+\sum\limits_{n=3}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{n-2}g^{\prime(n)}_{N}(\alpha L)\\[10.0pt]
&+g^{\prime(1,1)}(\alpha_{\rm S}L,\alpha L)+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m=1\\
n+m\neq 2\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha_{\rm S}}{\pi}\right)^{n-1}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{m-1}g^{\prime(n,m)}_{N}(\alpha_{\rm S}L,\alpha L)\,,\end{array}

(17)

and

\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathcal{H}^{\prime V}_{N}(\alpha_{\rm S},\alpha)=&\mathcal{H}^{V}_{N}(\alpha_{\rm S})+\dfrac{\alpha}{\pi}\mathcal{H}^{{}^{\prime}V(1)}_{N}+\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(\dfrac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{n}\mathcal{H}^{{}^{\prime}V(n)}_{N}\\[10.0pt]
&+\sum\limits_{n,m=1}^{\infty}\left(\dfrac{\alpha_{\rm S}}{\pi}\right)^{n}\left(\dfrac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{m}\mathcal{H}^{\prime V(n,m)}_{N}\,.\end{array}

(18)

The pure QED functions
g^{{}^{\prime}(1)}
and
g^{{}^{\prime}(2)}
, as well as the first non-vanishing mixed QCD
\otimes
QED

term,
g^{{}^{\prime}(1,1)}
, are known in literature
Autieri:2023xme
;
Cieri:2018sfk
. The functions
g^{{}^{\prime}(2,1)}
,
g^{{}^{\prime}(1,2)}
and
g^{{}^{\prime}(3)}
are required to consistently achieve full mixed QCD
\otimes
QED NNLL accuracy. The computation of these three functions constitutes an original result of this paper.

Moving forward to the extension of the
q_{T}
-resummation formalism, the integral representation of
\mathcal{G}_{N}
including QCD
\otimes
QED corrections is given by

\mathcal{G}_{N}=-\int_{b_{0}^{2}/b^{2}}^{Q^{2}}\frac{dq^{2}}{q^{2}}\left[A(\alpha_{\rm S}(q^{2}),\alpha(q^{2}))\log\left(\frac{M^{2}}{q^{2}}\right)+\widetilde{B}_{N}(\alpha_{\rm S}(q^{2}),\alpha(q^{2}))\right],

(19)

where
A(\alpha_{\rm S}(q^{2}),\alpha(q^{2}))
and
\widetilde{B}_{N}(\alpha_{\rm S}(q^{2}),\alpha(q^{2}))
are double perturbative expansions in
\alpha_{\rm S}
and
\alpha
, defined according to

A(\alpha_{\rm S},\alpha)=\frac{\alpha_{\rm S}}{\pi}A^{(1)}+\left(\frac{\alpha_{\rm S}}{\pi}\right)^{2}A^{(2)}+\sum_{n=3}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha_{\rm S}}{\pi}\right)^{n}A^{(n)}

+\frac{\alpha}{\pi}A^{{}^{\prime}(1)}+\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{2}A^{{}^{\prime}(2)}+\sum_{n=3}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{n}A^{{}^{\prime}(n)}

+\frac{\alpha_{\rm S}}{\pi}\frac{\alpha}{\pi}A^{(1,1)}+\left(\frac{\alpha_{\rm S}}{\pi}\right)^{2}\frac{\alpha}{\pi}A^{(2,1)}+\frac{\alpha_{\rm S}}{\pi}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{2}A^{(1,2)}+\sum_{n,m=2}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha_{\rm S}}{\pi}\right)^{n}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{m}A^{(n,m)}\,,

(20)

and

\widetilde{B}_{N}(\alpha_{\rm S},\alpha)=\frac{\alpha_{\rm S}}{\pi}\widetilde{B}_{N}^{(1)}+\left(\frac{\alpha_{\rm S}}{\pi}\right)^{2}\widetilde{B}_{N}^{(2)}+\sum_{n=3}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha_{\rm S}}{\pi}\right)^{n}\widetilde{B}_{N}^{(n)}

+\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\widetilde{B}_{N}^{{}^{\prime}(1)}+\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{2}\widetilde{B}_{N}^{{}^{\prime}(2)}+\sum_{n=3}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{n}\widetilde{B}_{N}^{{}^{\prime}(n)}

+\frac{\alpha_{\rm S}}{\pi}\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\widetilde{B}_{N}^{(1,1)}+\left(\frac{\alpha_{\rm S}}{\pi}\right)^{2}\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\widetilde{B}_{N}^{(2,1)}+\frac{\alpha_{\rm S}}{\pi}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{2}\widetilde{B}_{N}^{(1,2)}+\sum_{n,m=2}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha_{\rm S}}{\pi}\right)^{n}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{m}\widetilde{B}_{N}^{(n,m)}\,.

(21)

In the QCD
\otimes
QED formulation, we must consistently include the mixed QCD
\otimes
QED contributions to the running of the QCD and QED couplings simultaneously. As a consequence, the renormalization group equations (RGE) are written as

;
Billis:2019evv
:

\frac{d\mathrm{log}\left(\alpha_{\rm S}(\mu^{2})\right)}{d\mathrm{log}\left(\mu^{2}\right)}=\beta(\alpha_{\rm S},\alpha)=-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n}\left(\frac{\alpha_{\rm S}}{\pi}\right)^{n+1}-\sum_{n,m+1=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n,m}\left(\frac{\alpha_{\rm S}}{\pi}\right)^{n+1}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{m}\,,

(22)

\frac{d\mathrm{log}\left(\alpha(\mu^{2})\right)}{d\mathrm{log}\left(\mu^{2}\right)}=\beta^{\prime}(\alpha_{\rm S},\alpha)=-\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\beta^{\prime}_{n}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{n+1}-\sum_{m,n+1=0}^{\infty}\beta^{\prime}_{m,n}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{m+1}\left(\frac{\alpha_{\rm S}}{\pi}\right)^{n}\,,

(23)

with the inclusion of the mixed QCD
\otimes
QED
\beta
functions:
\beta_{n,m}
and
\beta^{\prime}_{n,m}
. The known coefficients useful for our discussion are
Cieri:2018sfk
;
Billis:2019evv
:

\beta_{1,1}=\frac{1}{16}(C_{F}-2C_{A})T_{F}N_{q}^{(2)}\,,

(24)

\beta_{0}^{\prime}=-\frac{N^{(2)}}{3},\,\beta^{\prime}_{1}=-\frac{N^{(4)}}{4},\,\beta^{\prime}_{1,0}=-\frac{C_{F}C_{A}N_{q}^{(2)}}{4},\,\beta^{\prime}_{1,1}=\frac{-C_{F}N_{c}}{16}N_{q}^{(2)}\,,

(25)

\beta_{0,2}=\frac{1}{64}\left(\frac{44}{9}T_{F}N_{q}^{(2)}N^{(2)}+2\,T_{F}N_{q}^{(4)}\right)\,,

(26)

\beta^{\prime}_{1}=-\frac{N^{(4)}}{4}\,,\beta^{\prime}_{2,0}=-\frac{1}{64}\left(\left(\frac{133}{18}C_{A}-C_{F}\right)2C_{F}N_{c}N_{q}^{(2)}-\frac{44}{9}C_{F}T_{F}N_{f}N_{c}N_{q}^{(2)}\right)\,,

(27)

\beta^{\prime}_{2}=-\frac{1}{64}\left(\frac{44}{9}N^{(4)}N^{(2)}+2N^{(6)}\right),

(28)

where we introduced the short-hand definitions

N^{(n)}=N_{c}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{f}}e_{q_{i}}^{n}+\sum_{l=1}^{n_{l}}e_{l}^{n},

(29)

N_{q}^{(n)}=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{f}}e_{q_{i}}^{n}\,,

(30)

with

the number of colors,

(

) the number of quark (lepton) flavours and

(

) the quark (lepton) electric charges.

At the lowest order in perturbation theory, RGE equations are decoupled. Then, the solution is given by

\alpha_{\rm S}^{\rm LO}(Q^{2})=\frac{\alpha_{\rm S}(\mu_{R}^{2})}{y}\,,

(31)

with

\lambda_{\mu}=\log(Q^{2}/\mu^{2})

(32)

and

y=1-\lambda_{\mu}\beta_{0}\alpha_{\rm S}(\mu_{R}^{2})\,,

(33)

where we explicitly introduced the renormalization scale
\mu_{R}
. Starting from the next perturbative order, the equations are coupled and the evolution of QCD coupling presents QED-induced corrections. If we use the expansion

\alpha_{\rm S}\equiv\sum_{i,j=0}\delta\alpha_{\rm S}^{(i,j)}\,,

(34)

with
\alpha_{\rm S}^{\rm LO}\equiv\alpha_{\rm S}^{(0,0)}
, we can write the NLO QCD corrections as

\delta\alpha_{\rm S}^{(1,0)}(Q^{2})=-\alpha_{\rm S}^{2}(\mu_{R}^{2})\frac{\beta_{1}\beta^{\prime}_{0}\mathrm{log}\left(y\right)+\beta_{0,1}\beta_{0}\mathrm{log}\left(y^{\prime}\right)+\beta_{0}^{2}\beta_{0}^{\prime}\mathrm{log}\left(\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu_{R}^{2}}\right)}{\beta_{0}\beta^{\prime}_{0}y^{2}}\,,

(35)

where QED effects manifest through the mixing
\beta
-coefficients and

y^{\prime}=1-\lambda_{\mu}\beta^{\prime}_{0}\alpha(\mu_{R}^{2})\,.

(36)

The mixed NLO QCD+LO QED correction reads

\displaystyle\delta\alpha_{S}^{(1,0)}(Q^{2})=\alpha(\mu_{R}^{2})\alpha_{\rm S}^{2}(\mu_{R}^{2})\frac{1}{\beta_{0}^{2}\beta_{0}^{{}^{\prime}2}y^{3}y^{\prime}}\biggl((\beta_{1,1}\beta_{0}-\beta_{1}\beta_{0,1})\beta_{0}^{{}^{\prime}2}y\,y^{\prime}\mathrm{log}\left(y\right)

\displaystyle+(\beta_{0,1}\beta_{0}^{2}\beta^{\prime}_{1}y+\beta_{0}\beta^{\prime}_{0}(\beta_{0,1}\beta^{\prime}_{1,0}-\beta_{1,1}\beta^{\prime}_{0})y\,y^{\prime}+\beta_{1}\beta_{0,1}\beta_{0}^{{}^{\prime}2}y^{{}^{\prime}2})\mathrm{log}\left(y^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle+\beta_{0}^{2}y\left((\beta_{0,1}\beta^{\prime}_{1}-\beta_{0,2}\beta_{0}^{\prime})(y^{\prime}-1)+\beta_{0,1}\beta_{0}^{{}^{\prime}2}\mathrm{log}\left(\frac{Q^{2}}{\mu_{R}^{2}}\right)\right)\biggl)\,,

5
Conclusions

In this paper, we have considered the combined QCD
\otimes
QED transverse-momentum resummation formalism
Cieri:2018sfk
;
Autieri:2023xme
;
Buonocore:2024xmy
for the neutral current Drell-Yan (DY) process. Specifically, we dealt with the mixed QCD
\otimes
QED logarithmically-enhanced contributions, starting from the known
q_{T}
-subtraction coefficients of Ref.
Cieri:2020ikq
and the mixed renormalization group equations (RGE) for the QCD and QED coupling
Cieri:2018sfk
;
Billis:2019evv
.

Here, we present for the first time all the required ingredients to consistently and simultaneously resum logarithmically enhanced contributions originating from QCD and QED emissions up to NNLL accuracy. In particular, we have obtained the resummed form factor including up to mixed QCD
\otimes
QED NNLL and pure QED NNLL effects for initial-state radiation. Although pure QED contributions were directly obtained by Abelianization
deFlorian:2018wcj
;
AH:2019pyp
;
Cieri:2020ikq
of QCD counterparts
Bozzi:2005wk
, mixed QCD
\otimes
QED terms were computed from first principles. Specifically, we relied on the integral representation of the Sudakov form factor, and the solution of RGE equations for QCD and QED coupling at different perturbative orders in a simultaneous QCD
\otimes
QED expansion.

Then we applied the formalism to study the phenomenology at LHC (13 TeV) and Tevatron (1.96 TeV). For this purpose, we implemented the mixed higher-order resummation coefficients in the code
DYTURBO

Camarda:2019zyx
;
Camarda:2021ict
;
Camarda:2021jsw
;
Camarda:2023dqn
. We studied the
q_{T}
spectrum for
Z
/
\gamma^{*}
production, comparing LL QED, mixed NLL QCD
\otimes
QED and mixed NNLL QCD
\otimes
QED resummation effects with respect to pure QCD corrections up to N
3
LL. In all the cases, we found that NLL/NNLL QCD
\otimes
QED are smaller than LL QED corrections and they tend to stabilize for increasing
q_{T}
. Regarding the error bands induced by scale variations, we found that they are mainly driven by pure QCD uncertainties, and higher-order mixed QCD
\otimes
QED terms are more stable than LL QED ones.

Besides this, the suppression of gluon-initiated processes at Tevatron leads, as expected, to an enhancement of pure QED and mixed QCD
\otimes
QED contributions w.r.t. LHC simulations.

Also, we considered the corrections induced in the invariant mass distribution. In this case, we found that LL QED effects reduce up to
0.2\,\%
the distribution around the peak, and mixed NLL and NNLL go in the opposite direction: they increase the distribution approximately by
0.1\,\%
and
0.2\,\%
, respectively. A similar behavior was found when considering the rapidity distribution (both at LHC and Tevatron), although the corrections were
{\cal O}(0.1\,\%)
.

In conclusion, we have shown the impact of adding higher-order mixed QCD
\otimes
QED
q_{T}
resummation effects for
Z/\gamma*
production to obtain more stable results, which is crucial in the context of the current high-precision physics program at colliders.

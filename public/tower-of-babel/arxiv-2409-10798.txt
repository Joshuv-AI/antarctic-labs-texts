Shot Noise near Quantum-Criticality Thanks: Visiting Scholar
Srinivas Raghu, Chandra M. Varma

Abstract
Shot-noise measures the correlations of fluctuations of current for a voltage applied much larger than the temperature and reveals aspects of correlations in fermions beyond those revealed in the conductivity. Recent measurements of shot-noise in the quantum-critical region of the heavy-fermion compound YbRh 2 Si 2 (YRS) have presented a conceptual challenge to old theory and those devised following the experiments. Since the measured resistivity and the specific heat in YRS follow the predictions of marginal Fermi liquid (MFL) theory, we use it to calculate noise using the method developed by Nagaev. We get fair agreement with the magnitude and temperature dependence in the experiments using parameters from resistivity measurements. To achieve this, we find it necessary that the collisions between fermions by exchanging the MFL fluctuations conserve energy but lose momentum through Umklapp scattering and that the fermions and their fluctuations are locally in mutual equilibrium. At low temperatures, impurity scattering determines the noise and at high temperatures the MFL scattering. We show that the noise for MFL scattering for high T alone is the same as the Johnson-Nyquist noise, which in this case is temperature independent. Therefore the Fano factor crosses over to 0 0 at high temperatures independent of the voltage applied.

A variety of quasi-2-dimensional metals have resistivity proportional to

\displaystyle 2\frac{W^{2}}{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx^{\prime}\langle\delta J(x,t)\delta J(x^{\prime},0)\rangle

A fundamental issue on which the magnitude and temperature dependence of the calculated noise depends is whether the fluctuations that couple to fermions to give marginal Fermi-liquid are the fluctuations of the same fermions responsible for the electrical conduction, or are they of some other degrees of freedom. In the former case the total energy of the fermions is conserved in the inelastic collisions induced by the fluctuations. This is not so generally in the latter case.

Sources of current relaxation -
Current correlations may occur due to diverse physical processes besides the thermal noise. We shall not concern ourselves with noise when scattering by phonons is important because that is irrelevant to the recent experiments. For current fluctuations due to elastic impurity scattering, calculations in two opposite limits are available: 1) impurity scattering alone or 2) impurity scattering supplemented by frequency independent particle-particle scattering (pps) in the strong scattering limit. In the former, the shot-noise has been derived using transfer-matrix methods by Beenakker and Buttiker
[

The same result was derived by Nagaev
[

\displaystyle F_{elastic}(V,0)=\frac{1}{3}.

(4)

In the other limit, it is assumed that pps is strong enough that it leads to local thermodynamic equilibrium of the carriers on a length scale much smaller than the meso-scopic sample size in which the experiment is done, so that a local chemical potential
\mu(x)
and a local temperature

The scattering rate for any inelastic pps tends to

We will show below that the issue of lack of local equilibrium does not arise in the experimental conditions of Ref.

As mentioned above, at high enough temperatures, the inelastic pps of MFL dominates over the impurity scattering in determining the scattering rate so that the conductivity is

Shot noise from the kinetic equation -
Our work rests on extensions of the methods devised by Nagaev

\left(\dot{\bm{r}}\nabla_{\bm{r}}+\dot{\bm{k}}\nabla_{\bm{k}}\right)f(\bm{r},\bm{k},T)=I_{coll}+{\mathcal{L}}.

The collision integral is a sum of elastic scattering due to impurities and inelastic scattering from exchange by fermions of fluctuations giving MFL.

\displaystyle I_{coll}=(I_{el}+I_{inel}).

We consider a geometry as in the experiment with a sample of length

large compared to its transverse dimensions with a voltage

across the two ends in the direction

\dot{\bf k}=eE\hat{x}

is (even, odd) with respect to
{\bm{k}}
. Dropping the Langevin term for the moment, the Boltzmann equation then takes the form

v_{F}\left(\partial_{x}+eE_{x}\partial_{\epsilon}\right)(f_{e}(x,\epsilon,T)+f_{o}(x,\epsilon,T))=I_{coll}.

(7)

Since the energy is relative to the local chemical potential, the distribution function depends on the quantity
\epsilon+eEx
, and the two partial derivatives above can be expressed as the total derivative with respect to x

We can divide the collision integral into even and odd parts and write by symmetry that

\displaystyle v_{F}\frac{d}{dx}f_{o}(x,\epsilon,T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{coll,e}(x,\epsilon,T)

\displaystyle v_{F}\frac{d}{dx}f_{e}(x,y(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{coll,o}(\epsilon,T))=-\frac{1}{\tau(x,E,T)}f_{o}(x,\epsilon,T)

The last equality introduces the momentum relaxation time, and relates the odd and the even distribution functions:

We therefore have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}D(x,\epsilon,T)\frac{d}{dx}f_{o}(x,\epsilon,T)=I_{coll,e}(x,\epsilon,T).

(11)

where

\displaystyle D(x,\epsilon,T)=\frac{1}{2}v_{F}^{2}\tau(x,\epsilon,T).

(12)

\tau^{-1}(x,\epsilon,T)

[

,

]

We predict similar shot noise suppression in all materials with quantum-critical regions with linear in T resistivity, like

There have been recent works

The calculations in these papers have not been compared with the measured magnitude and temperature dependence of noise.

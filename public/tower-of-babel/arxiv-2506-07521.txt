Abstract
. We consider a power-type mild singular perturbation of a Dirichlet semilinear critical problem settled in an open and bounded set in a Carnot group. Here, the term critical has to be understood in the sense of the Sobolev embedding. We aim to prove the existence of two positive weak solutions: the first one is obtained by means of the variational Perron’s method, while for the second one we adapt a classical argument relying on proper estimates of a family of functions which mimic the role of the classical Aubin-Talenti functions in the Euclidean setting. Our results fall in the framework of semilinear PDEs in Carnot group but, as far as we know, are the first ones dealing with singular perturbations of power-type.

-
PDEs on Carnot groups
. To begin with, it is worth mentioning that, if
\mathbb{G}
is a Carnot group and if
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
is a sub-Laplacian on
\mathbb{G}

(see Section
2
for the relevant definitions), then
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
is a second-order differential operator with

non-negative characteristic form
, and thus it falls in the class of

degenerate-elliptic operators.

These degenerate operators
have appeared in the literature since the early 1900s, due to their appearance
in models of theoretical physics and of diffusion processes; however,
the lack of
regularizing properties
(caused by the lack of ellipticity)
creates several difficulties and prevents the application of several techniques.

In this perspective, a major difference between
general

degenerate-elliptic operators and the sub-Laplacians on Carnot groups
is that
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}

is a
sum of squares
of vector fields satisfying
the celebrated

Hörmander hypoellipticity condition
, see
[
32
]
.
Starting from this fact, and by taking benefit of the underlying geometry
attached to
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
induced by
\mathbb{G}
,
G.B. Folland, L.P. Rothschild and E.M. Stein developed in the 70’s the

singular integral theory in nilpotent Lie
groups
(see, e.g., Folland’s survey

[
19
]
for a torough discussion of the contributions
of Stein in this context). More precisely, in 1975 Folland
[
18
]

accomplished a functional
analytic study of sub-Laplacians on Carnot Lie groups and proved
the existence of an associated
well-behaved global fundamental solution
.
One year later
Rothschild and Stein
[
51
]
proved their celebrated

lifting theorem

enlightening the fundamental role played by the sub-Laplacians in the theory of second order PDEs which are sum of squares of vector fields; this remarkable result paved the way for a deep study of PDEs on Carnot groups.

In this perspective, we refer e.g. to

The situation may a priori change when considering structures different from Heisenberg groups. In

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we collect all the relevant notations, definitions and preliminaries needed in the rest of the paper.

2.1.
Carnot groups

A Carnot group
\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{R}^{N},\diamond)
of step
k
is a connected, simply connected Lie group whose finite dimensional Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of left-invariant (w.r.t.
\diamond
) vector fields admits a stratification of step
k
, namely there exist
k
linear subspaces
\mathfrak{g}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{g}_{k}
such that

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{k},\qquad[\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{i}]=\mathfrak{g}_{i+1},\qquad\mathfrak{g}_{k}\neq\{0\},\qquad\mathfrak{g}_{i}=\{0\}\textrm{ for all }i>k.

In particular, this implies that Carnot groups are a special instance of graded groups.

We call
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
the horizontal layer. We denote by
X_{1},\ldots,X_{N}
a basis of left-invariant vector fields of
\mathfrak{g}
such that the following holds:

•

X_{1},\ldots,X_{m_{1}}
is a basis of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
;

•

for every
1<i\leq k
,
X_{m_{i-1}+1},\ldots,X_{m_{i}}
is a basis of of
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
;

•

m_{0}=0
and
n_{i}:=m_{i}-m_{i-1}=\dim\mathfrak{g}_{i}
for every
1\leq i\leq k
;

•

m_{1}+\ldots+m_{k}=N
.

We notice that
N
is the topological dimension of
\mathbb{G}
, but we can also define its
homogeneous dimension

Q
as follows

(2.1)

Q:=\sum_{i=1}^{k}i\,n_{i}.

We notice that
N\leq Q
and that
N=Q
if and only if
\mathbb{G}
is the classical Euclidean group
(\mathbb{R}^{N},+)
. In particular, this is the only possible case whenever
Q\leq 3
.

Since the exponential map is a one-to-one diffeomorphism from
\mathfrak{g}
to
\mathbb{G}
, any point
g\in\mathbb{G}
can be uniquely written in exponential coordinates as

g=g_{1}X_{1}+\ldots+g_{N}X_{N}=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{N}).

Being a graded group, every Carnot group
\mathbb{G}
possess a family of anisotropic dilations
\delta_{\lambda}:\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{G}
defined as

(2.2)

\delta_{\lambda}(g)=\left(\lambda^{\alpha_{1}}g_{1},\ldots,\lambda^{\alpha_{N}}g_{N}\right),\quad\textrm{ for every }\lambda>0,

where
\alpha_{j}=i
if
m_{i-1}<j\leq m_{i}
.
We notice that
Q=\alpha_{1}+\ldots+\alpha_{N}
.

An explicit expression of the group operation
\diamond
can be then determined by means of the Campbell-Baker-Hausdorff formula, see e.g.
[
5
]
for more details. The null element of
\diamond
is the identity
0=(0,\ldots,0)
and the inverse of a certain
g\neq 0
is usually denoted by
g^{-1}
.

The group operation
\diamond
can also be used to define a further family of automorphisms of
\mathbb{G}
known as
left translations

\tau:\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{G}
. More precisely, given a base point
h\in\mathbb{G}
, we define

(2.3)

\tau_{h}(g):=h\diamond g.

It is possible to endow a Carnot group
\mathbb{G}
with a richer structure. Firstly, it is possible to define a scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}
such that the basis
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{m_{1}}\}
of the horizontal layer becomes orthonormal, and this provides a sub-Riemannian structure over
\mathbb{G}
. We notice that one can also provide a purely Riemannian structure defining a scalar product on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
making the entire basis
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{N}\}
orthonormal.
The sub-Riemannian Carnot group
\mathbb{G}
can also be endowed with an intrinsic metric structure by means of the so called
Carnot-Carathéodory
(
CC
in short) distance. It is well known that with this distance, these spaces are not Riemannian at any scale, see e.g.
[
52
]
.

From a more analytic point of view, we can define several differential operators modelled on the horizontal vector fields

\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{m_{1}}\}
.
First, given a smooth horizontal vector field
V=v_{1}X_{1}+\ldots+v_{m_{1}}X_{m_{1}}
, we define its horizontal divergence as

(2.4)

\mathrm{div}_{\mathbb{G}}V:=X_{1}v_{1}+\ldots+X_{m_{1}}v_{m_{1}}.

Moreover, given a smooth enough scalar-valued function
u:\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{R}
, we can define
the horizontal gradient of
u
as

(2.5)

\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u:=(X_{1}u,\ldots,X_{m_{1}}u),

and the sub-Laplacian of
u
as

(2.6)

\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}u:=\mathrm{div}_{\mathbb{G}}(\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u)=X_{1}^{2}u+\ldots+X_{m_{1}}^{2}u,

We point out that both the divergence
\mathrm{div}_{\mathbb{G}}
and the horizontal gradient
\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}
(and
a fortiori
the sub-Laplacian
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
)
are independent of the choice of the base on
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
: in fact, they are intrisically
associated to the sub-Riemannian structure on
\mathbb{G}
.

We notice that both the horizontal gradient
\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}
and the sub-Laplacian
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
are left-invariant operators, i.e.

(2.7)

\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}(u\circ\tau_{h})=(\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u)\circ\tau_{h}\quad\textrm{ and }\quad\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}(u\circ\tau_{h})=(\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}u)\circ\tau_{h},\quad\textrm{ for every }h\in\mathbb{G},

and they are, respectively, homogeneous of degree one and two w.r.t. the family of dilations
\delta_{\lambda}
defined in (
2.2
), namely

(2.8)

\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}(u\circ\delta_{\lambda})=\lambda(\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u)\circ\delta_{\lambda}\quad\textrm{ and }\quad\lambda^{2}(\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}u)\circ\delta_{\lambda},\quad\textrm{ for every }\lambda>0.

The Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}^{N}
coincides with the Haar measure of
\mathbb{G}
and hence is left-invariant and satisfies the following scaling property:

(2.9)

\mathcal{L}^{N}(\delta_{\lambda}(E))=\lambda^{Q}\mathcal{L}^{N}(E)\quad\textrm{ for every measurable set }E\subset\mathbb{G}.

Every integral in this manuscript has to be understood
with respect to the Haar measure, unless otherwise stated.

Moreover, the homogeneity of the
X_{i}
’s implies that the (formal)
adjoint of
X_{i}
in the Lebesgue space
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
is precisely
-X_{i}
(for
i\leq i\leq m_{1}
), that is,

(2.10)

\int_{\mathbb{G}}(X_{i}\varphi)\psi=-\int_{\mathbb{G}}\varphi(X_{i}\psi)\quad\text{for
every $1\leq i\leq m_{1}$}.

In particular,
-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
is a
self-adjoint operator
.

Every Carnot group can be endowed with several homogeneous norms. A homogeneous (quasi)norm
\rho:\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{R}
is a non-negative function further satisfying the following properties:

•

\rho(g)=0
if and only if
g=0
;

•

\rho(\delta_{\lambda}(g))=\lambda\,\rho(g)
for every
g\in\mathbb{G}
and for every
\lambda>0
;

•

\rho(h\diamond g)\leq C\left(\rho(h)+\rho(g)\right)
for every
g,h\in\mathbb{G}
and for some constant
C\geq 1
.

The importance of such objects is witnessed by a famous result of Folland: in
[
18
]
, he showed that there exists a homogeneous norm
|\cdot|_{\mathbb{G}}
on
\mathbb{G}
and a positive constant
C_{Q}>0
, depending only on
Q
, such that the function

(2.11)

\Gamma_{h}(g):=\dfrac{C_{Q}}{|h^{-1}\diamond g|^{Q-2}_{\mathbb{G}}},\quad\textrm{ with }Q\geq 3,

is a fundamental solution of
-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
with pole at
h\in\mathbb{G}
.
Moreover, homogeneous norms can be used to define distances, different from the
CC
-distance, as follows:

d_{\rho}(g,h):=\rho(h^{-1}\diamond g).

In any case, all these norms (and the relative distances) are equivalent and they all induce on
\mathbb{G}
the Euclidean topology. For our purposes, we prefer to work with the homogeneous norm
|\cdot|_{\mathbb{G}}
(and the associated distance
d_{\mathbb{G}}
) which provides the fundamental solution defined in (
2.11
). In particular, we will denote by

B_{r}(g_{0}):=\{g\in\mathbb{G}:d_{\mathbb{G}}(g,g_{0})=|g_{0}^{-1}\diamond g|_{\mathbb{G}}<r\},

the open ball of radius
r>0
and center
g_{0}\in\mathbb{G}
.

2.2.
Folland-Stein spaces and critical PDEs

Let
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathbb{G}
be an open set.
For every
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathcal{O})
there exists a positive constant
C_{Q}>0
depending only on the homogeneous dimension
Q
such that the following Sobolev inequality holds true

(2.12)

\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2_{Q}^{\star}}(\mathcal{O})}\leq C_{Q}\,\||\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f|\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})},

where

(2.13)

2_{Q}^{\star}:=\dfrac{2Q}{Q-2},

denotes the (sub-elliptic) critical Sobolev exponent, resembling the classical Euclidean
one

2^{\star}=\frac{2N}{N-2}.

Thanks to (
2.12
),

\||\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f|\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
provides a norm on the space
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
.
We define the Folland-Stein space
S^{1}_{0}(\mathcal{O})
as the completion of
C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathcal{O})
w.r.t. the above norm, and we set

\|u\|_{S^{1}_{0}(\mathcal{O})}=\||\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u|\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}\quad\text{for every $u\in S^{1}_{0}(\mathcal{O})$}.

We explicitly observe that, owing to (
2.12
), we have

(2.14)

S_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{O})=\big\{u\in L^{2_{Q}^{\star}}(\mathcal{O}):\,\text{$X_{i}u\in L^{2}(\mathcal{O})$
for all $1\leq i\leq m_{1}$}\big\},

where
X_{1}u,\ldots,X_{m_{1}}u
are meant in the sense of distributions, that is (see also (
2.10
)),

\int_{\mathcal{O}}(X_{j}u)\varphi=-\int_{\mathcal{O}}uX_{j}\varphi\quad\text{for all $\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})$}.

In the particular case when
\mathcal{O}

is bounded
(which the is case we are mainly
interested in, together with the case
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{G}
),
the space
S_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{O})
enjoys the following classical properties.

(1)

S_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{O})
is endowed with a structure of real Hilbert space by the inner product

\langle u,v\rangle_{S_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{O})}=\int_{\mathcal{O}}\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u,\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}\qquad(u,v\in S_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{O})),

whose associated norm is precisely
\|\cdot\|_{S_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{O})}
.

(2)

By density, the Sobolev inequality (
2.12
) actually holds for every function
u\in S_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{O})
. As a consequence,
S_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{O})
is
continuously embedded
into
L^{p}(\mathcal{O})
for every
1\leq p\leq 2_{Q}^{\star}
.
Furthermore, this embedding turns out to be
compact
when
1\leq p<2_{Q}^{\star}
.

(3)

If
u\in S_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{O})
, then
u_{+}=\max\{u,0\},\,u_{-}=\max\{-u,0\}\in S_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{O})
, and

\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u_{+}=\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u\cdot\chi_{\{u>0\}}\quad\textrm{ and }\quad\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u_{-}=\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u\cdot\chi_{\{u<0\}}.

In particular, for every
c\in\mathbb{R}
, we derive

(2.15)

\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u=0\quad\textrm{a.e. on any level set }\{u=c\}.

We refer, e.g., to
[
21
]
for good approximation results in terms of smooth function, allowing to prove the above facts.

Remark 2.1
.

On account of the above properties of
S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, it is possible to prove
the following
convergence result
, which will be repeatedly used in the sequel.

Assume that
\{u_{k}\}_{k}\subseteq S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
is a
bounded sequence
. Since
S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
is a real
Hilbert space
, and since we have already pointed out that the embedding

S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega)

is compact for every
1\leq p<2^{\star}_{Q}
, as in the classical case we deduce that there exists a function
u\in S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
such that (up to a subsequence, and as
k\to+\infty
)

•

u_{k}\to u

weakly in
S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
;

•

u_{k}\to u

strongly
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
for every
1\leq p<2^{\star}_{Q}
;

•

u_{k}\to u

pointwise a.e. in
\Omega
.

On the other hand, since the embedding
S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2^{*}Q}(\Omega)
is
continuous
, we have that
u\in L^{2^{\star}_{Q}}(\Omega)
, and
\{u_{k}\}_{k}
is
bounded also in
L^{2^{\star}_{Q}}(\Omega)
; in particular, setting

p_{0}=\frac{2^{\star}_{Q}}{2^{\star}_{Q}-1}\in(1,2^{\star}_{Q}),

we have that
\{v_{k}=u_{k}^{2^{\star}_{Q}-1}\}_{k}
is bounded in
L^{p_{0}}(\Omega)
. As a consequence of this fact, and since we know that
u_{k}\to u
pointwise a.e. in
\Omega
, we deduce that
\{v_{k}\}_{k}

converges weakly to
u^{2^{\star}_{Q}-1}

(as
k\to+\infty)
, and up to a subsequence) in
L^{p_{0}}(\Omega)
, that is,

\int_{\Omega}u_{k}^{2^{\star}_{Q}-1}\varphi\,dx\to\int_{\Omega}u^{2^{\star}_{Q}-1}\varphi\,dx\quad\text{for every $\varphi\in L^{p_{0}^{\prime}}(\Omega)=L^{2^{\star}_{Q}}(\Omega)\supseteq S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)$}.

In
[
27
]
Garofalo and Vassilev adapted the celebrated
concentrantion-compactness principle
of Lions showing that the best Sobolev constant in (
2.12
) can be achieved and it is characterized as follows

(2.16)

S_{\mathbb{G}}:=\inf_{f\in S^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{G})}\dfrac{\||\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f|\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{G})}}{\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2_{Q}^{\star}}(\mathbb{G})}}.

As in the Euclidean case, this fact has immediate consequences at the level of the critical PDE

(2.17)

-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}u=u^{2^{\star}_{Q}-1}\quad\textrm{in }\mathbb{G}.

We list here below the most important results concerning (
2.16
) and (
2.17
).

•

Every minimizing sequence of (
2.16
) is relatively compact in
S^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{G})
, after possibly translating and dilating each of its elements. In particular, the minimum in (
2.16
) is achieved and (
2.17
) admits a non-negative and non-trivial solution
U\in S^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{G})
, see
[
27
, Theorem 6.1]
.

•

By Bony’s maximum principle
[
7
]
, every non-negative solution of (
2.17
) is actually positive.

•

If
T\in S^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{G})
is a positive solution of (
2.17
), then there exists a positive constant
M_{1}>0
such that

(2.18)

T(g)\leq M_{1}\,\min\{1,|g|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2-Q}\},\quad\textrm{ for every }g\in\mathbb{G},

see
[
6
, Theorem 3.4]
.

•

If
T\in S^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{G})
is a positive solution of (
2.17
), then there exists a positive constant
M_{2}>0
such that

(2.19)

T(g)\geq M_{2}\,\dfrac{|B_{1}(0)|}{(1+|g|_{\mathbb{G}})^{Q-2}},\quad\textrm{ for every }g\in\mathbb{G},

see
[
39
, Lemma 3.2]
.

Let now
T\in S^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{G})
be a minimizer of (
2.16
). For every
\varepsilon>0
define the rescaled function

(2.20)

T_{\varepsilon}(g):=\varepsilon^{(2-Q)/2}T\left(\delta_{1/\varepsilon}(g)\right).

Let further be
R>0
such that
B_{R}(0)\subset\Omega
and let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{R}(0))
be a cut-off function such that
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
and
\varphi\equiv 1
in
B_{R/2}(0)
. Define the function

(2.21)

U_{\varepsilon}(g):=\varphi(g)T_{\varepsilon}(g),\quad g\in\mathbb{G}.

The following holds:

•

Up to multiplicative constants,
T_{\varepsilon}
is a solution of (
2.17
).

•

Due to scaling invariance, we can set
T
as to have

(2.22)

\||\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}T_{\varepsilon}|\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{G})}=\|T_{\varepsilon}\|^{2^{\star}_{Q}}_{L^{2_{Q}^{\star}}(\mathbb{G})}=S_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q/2}.

•

The function
U_{\varepsilon}
satisfies the following estimates as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}

(2.23)

\displaystyle\||\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}U_{\varepsilon}|\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{G})}

\displaystyle=S_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q/2}+O(\varepsilon^{Q-2})

(2.24)

\displaystyle\|U_{\varepsilon}\|^{2^{\star}_{Q}}_{L^{2_{Q}^{\star}}(\mathbb{G})}

\displaystyle=S_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q/2}+O(\varepsilon^{Q}),

see
[
39
, Lemma 3.3]
.

2.3.
Strong Maximum Principle and Harnack inequality

Now we have reviewed
the basic concepts concerning the Carnot groups setting, and before starting
our study of problem (

P

)
λ
, for a future reference
we explicitly
state here below a
Weak Harnack inequality
for

\text{$L=-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}+c(x)$ (with $c\leq 0$)},

from which we will also derive a
Strong Maximum Principle.

These results will be fundamental throughout the rest of the paper.

Proposition 2.2

(Weak Harnack inequality for
-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}+c
)
.

Let
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set, and let

c\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O}),\,c\geq 0.

Moreover, let
u\in S_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{O})
be a
weak supersolution

of the equation

(2.25)

\text{$-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}u+cu=0$ in $\mathcal{O}$},

that is

(
see also the subsequent Definition
2.5
)
,

\int_{\mathcal{O}}\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u,\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varphi\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}+\int_{\mathcal{O}}cu\varphi\geq 0\quad\text{
for every $\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathcal{O}),\,\varphi\geq 0$}.

If, in addiction,
u\geq 0
a.e. in
\mathcal{O}
, there exist
constants
c_{0}>0
and
p_{0}\in(0,1)
,

independent of
u
, such that,
for every
g_{0}\in\mathcal{O},\,r>0
with
B_{4r}(g_{0})\Subset\mathcal{O}
, we have

(2.26)

\Big(-\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int_{B_{3r}(g_{0})}|u|^{p_{0}}\Big)^{1/p_{0}}\leq c_{0}\,\inf_{B_{r}}u.

Proof.

The proof of this proposition is contained in the proof of
[
29
, Theorem 4.1]
,
where the Authors establish a
full Harnack inequality

for the
non-negative weak solutions
u
of

(2.27)

\text{$Lu=0$ in $\Omega$},

and
L
is a general
X
-elliptic operator (of which
-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}+c(\cdot)
is a particular
case).
Indeed,
to prove the cited Harnack inequality for a given non-negative weak solution
u

of (
2.27
), the Authors in
[
29
]
use
an adaptation of the Moser iteration technique, and they show that

i)

since
u
is, in particular, a
weak subsolution
of (
2.27
), then

\sup_{B_{r}(g_{0})}u\leq C_{1}\Big(-\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int_{B_{2r}(g_{0})}|u|^{s}\Big)^{1/s},

for some constants
C_{1}>0,\,s>0
independent of
u
;

ii)

since
u
is, in particular, a
weak supersolution
of (
2.27
), then

\inf_{B_{r}(g_{0})}u\geq C_{2}\Big(-\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int_{B_{3r}(g_{0})}|u|^{-p_{0}}\Big)^{-1/p_{0}},

for some constant
C_{2}>0
and every
p_{0}
small enough;

iii)

there exists a constant
C_{3}>0
, only depending on
p_{0}
, such that

\Big(-\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int_{B_{3r}(g_{0})}|u|^{p_{0}}\Big)^{1/p_{0}}\leq C_{3}\Big(-\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int_{B_{3r}(g_{0})}|u|^{-p_{0}}\Big)^{-1/p_{0}},

provided that
B_{4r}(g_{0})\Subset\mathcal{O}
. Thus, since the proofs of i) - iii) are mutually independent,
if
u
is just a weak supersolution of (
2.25
) (which is a particular
case of (
2.27
)), the demonstration of assertion ii)
in
[
29
]
gives exactly the desired estimate (
2.26
).
∎

From Proposition
2.2
, and using a classical covering argument, we obtain the following

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathbb{G}
be a bounded open set,
and let
c\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O}),\,\text{$c\geq 0$}
. Furthermore, let

u\in S_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{O})
be a weak supersolution of
equation (
2.25
) satisfying

\text{$u>0$ a.e.\,on every open ball $B=B_{r}(g_{0})\Subset\Omega$}.

Then, for every open set
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\Subset\mathcal{O}
there exists
C=C(\mathcal{O}^{\prime},u)>0
such that

\text{$u\geq C(\mathcal{O}^{\prime},u)>0$ a.e.\,in $\mathcal{O}^{\prime}$}.

As anticipated, Proposition
2.2
implies the following
Strong Maximum Principle
.

Proposition 2.4

(Strong Maximum Principle for
L
)
.

Let
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathbb{G}
be a
bounded and connected

open set, and let

u\in S^{1}_{0}(\mathcal{O}),\,u\geq 0
be a
weak supersolution
of (
2.25
).
Then,

(2.28)

\text{either $u\equiv 0$ or $u>0$ a.e.\,in $\mathcal{O}$}.

Proof.

We assume that there exists a set
\mathcal{Z}\subseteq\mathcal{O}
of
positive Lebesgue measure
such that
u\equiv 0
(pointwise) on
\mathcal{Z}
, and we prove that in this case we have
u\equiv 0
a.e. on
\mathcal{O}
.

To this end we first observe that, since
|\mathcal{Z}|>0
, we can find
r_{0}>0
such that

\mathcal{Z}_{0}=\mathcal{Z}\cap\{g\in\mathcal{O}:\,d_{\mathbb{G}}(g,\partial\mathcal{O})\geq r_{0}\}

has
positive Lebesgue measure
; moreover, since
K=\{g\in\mathcal{O}:\,d_{\mathbb{G}}(g,\partial\mathcal{O})\geq r_{0}\}\subseteq\Omega

is compact (recall that
d_{\mathbb{G}}
induces
the Euclidean topology), there exist
g_{1},\ldots,g_{p}\in K
and
r_{1},\ldots,r_{p}>0
(for some
p\in\mathbb{N}
) such that
B_{4r_{i}}(g_{i})\Subset\mathcal{O}
(for
1\leq i\leq p
) and

\mathcal{Z}_{0}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{p}(\mathcal{Z}_{0}\cap B_{r_{i}}(g_{i})).

As a consequence, there exists a
d_{\mathbb{G}}
-ball
B_{0}=B_{r_{i_{0}}}(g_{i_{0}})

(for some
1\leq i_{0}\leq p
) such that

(2.29)

|\mathcal{Z}_{0}\cap B_{r_{i_{0}}(g_{i_{0}})}|>0.

In particular, since
u\geq 0
a.e. in
\mathcal{O}
and
u=0
on
\mathcal{Z}_{0}
, we have

(2.30)

\inf_{B_{r_{i_{0}}}(g_{i_{0}})}u=\inf_{\Omega}u=0.

With (
2.29
)-(
2.30
)
at hand, we then define the set

S=\{g\in\mathcal{O}:\,\text{there exists a ball
$B=B_{r}(g)\subseteq\mathcal{O}$
s.t.\,$u=0$ a.e.\,on $B$}\},

and we prove that
S=\mathcal{O}
by a
connection argument
:

-
S\neq\emptyset
. Let
g_{i_{0}}\in\mathcal{O}
be as in (
2.29
)-(
2.30
). Since
u
is a non-negative weak supersolution of equation (
2.25
), and since

B=B_{4r_{i_{0}}(g_{i_{0}})}\Subset\mathcal{O}
, we can apply the Weak Harnack Inequality in Proposition
2.2
: this gives the following estimate

\Big(-\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int_{B_{3r_{i_{0}}}(g_{i_{0}})}|u|^{p_{0}}\Big)^{1/p_{0}}\leq c\,\inf_{B_{r_{i_{0}}}(g_{i_{0}})}u=0,

from which we derive that
u=0
a.e. in
B_{3r_{i_{0}}}
. Hence,
g_{i_{0}}\in S
.

-
S
is open
. Assume that
g\in S
, and let
B=B_{r}(g)\subseteq\mathcal{O}
be a

d_{\mathbb{G}}
-ball such that

\text{$u=0$ a.e.\,in $B$}.

Given any
g^{\prime}\in B
, if we choose
\rho>0
in such a way that
B_{\rho}(g^{\prime})\subseteq B
, we obviously have

\text{$u=0$ a.e.\,on $B_{\rho}(g^{\prime})\subseteq B$},

and this proves that
g^{\prime}\in S
.
By the arbitrariness of
g^{\prime}\in B_{r}(g)
, we conclude that
B_{r}(g)\subseteq S
, and hence
S
is open, as desired.

-
S
is closed
. Assume that
(g_{k})_{k}
is a sequence of points in
S
which converges (as
k\to+\infty
) to some
g\in\mathcal{O}
, and let
\rho>0

be such that
B_{4\rho}(g)\Subset\mathcal{O}
. Since
g_{k}\to g
as
k\to+\infty
, there
exists some
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
g_{k_{0}}\in B_{\rho}(g)
; on the other hand, since

g_{k_{0}}\in S
, it is possible to find a suitable
d_{\mathbb{G}}
-ball
B=B_{r}(g_{k_{0}})

such that
B\subseteq B_{\rho}(g)
and

\text{$u=0$ a.e.\,in $B$}.

Hence, using once again the
Weak
Harnack Inequality in Proposition
2.2
, we get

\Big(-\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int_{B_{3\rho}(g)}|u|^{p_{0}}\Big)^{1/p_{0}}\leq c\,\inf_{B_{\rho}(g)}u=0,

and thus
u=0
a.e. in
B_{3\rho}(g)
, that is,
g\in S
. Hence,
S
is closed.

Gathering the above facts, and recalling that
\mathcal{O}
is connected, we then conclude that
\mathcal{O}=S
, and this obviously implies that

u=0
a.e. in
\mathcal{O}
, as desired.
∎

2.4.
Weak sub/supersolutions of
\eqref{eq:Main_Problem}_{\lambda}

We are now ready to properly set the definition of weak sub/supersolution of (

P

)
λ
.
However, we prefer to provide such definitions for the following slightly more general singular Dirichlet problem:

(2.31)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}u=\dfrac{\lambda}{u^{\gamma}}+f(x,u)&\textrm{ in }\Omega,\\
u>0&\textrm{ in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\textrm{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
\gamma\in(0,1)
,
\lambda>0
and
f:\Omega\times(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is a Carathéodory function satisfying the following critical growth condition: there exists a positive constant
K_{f}>0
such that

(2.32)

|f(x,t)|\leq K_{f}(1+t^{2^{\star}_{Q}-1})\quad\textrm{ for a.e. }x\in\Omega\textrm{ and for every }t>0.

Here and throughout,
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{G}
is a fixed
bounded and
connected open set
(as in (

P

)).

Definition 2.5
.

Let
f:\Omega\times(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be a Carathéodory function satisfying the growth condition (
2.32
). We say that a function
u\in S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is a weak subsolution (resp. supersolution) of (
2.31
) if it satisfies the following properties:

(i)

u>0
in
\Omega
and
u^{-\gamma}\in L^{1}_{\textrm{loc}}(\Omega)
.

(ii)

For every
0\leq\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
, it holds that

(2.33)

\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u,\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varphi\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}\leq(\textrm{resp. }\geq)\lambda\int_{\Omega}u^{-\gamma}\varphi+\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)\varphi.

Finally, we say that
u\in S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is a weak solution of (
2.31
) if it is both a weak subsolution and a weak supersolution of (
2.31
) without the non-negativity condition on
\varphi
.

Remark 2.6
.

We now list here below some comments concerning the above Definition
2.5
. In what follows,
we tacitly understand that
f:\Omega\to(0,+\infty)
is a Carathéodory function satisfying
the growth
assumption (
2.32
).

1)  If
u\in S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is a weak sub/supersolution of problem
(
2.31
), all the integrals appearing in (
2.33
)

exist and are finite
.
Indeed, by Hölder’s inequality, for every

\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega),\,\text{$\varphi\geq 0$ in $\Omega$}
,
we obtain

(2.34)

\left|\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u,\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varphi\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}\right|\leq\|u\|_{S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}\|\varphi\|_{S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}

Moreover, using (
2.32
) and the Sobolev inequality, we also get

(2.35)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)\,|\varphi|&\leq K_{f}\Big(\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2^{\star}_{Q}-1}|\varphi|\,dx\Big)\\
&(\text{using H\"{o}lder's inequality})\\
&\leq K_{f}\big(\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{L^{2^{\star}_{Q}}(\Omega)}^{2^{\star}_{Q}-1}\cdot\|\varphi\|_{L^{2^{\star}_{Q}}(\Omega)}\big)\\
&\leq K_{f}\big(\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+S_{\mathbb{G}}^{-2^{\star}_{Q}/2}\|u\|_{S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2^{\star}_{Q}-1}\cdot\|\varphi\|_{S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}\big)\\
&(\text{again by H\"{o}lder's inequality and Poincaré inequality})\\
&\leq C\|\varphi\|_{S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}\big(1+\|u\|_{S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2^{\star}_{Q}-1}\big)<+\infty,\end{split}

where
S_{\mathbb{G}}>0
is the best Sobolev constant in
\mathbb{G}
, and
C>0
depends on
Q,\,f,\,|\Omega|
.
Finally, since
we are assuming that
u^{-\gamma}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
, we obviously
have

0\leq\int_{\Omega}u^{-\gamma}\varphi\leq\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\int_{\mathrm{supp}(\varphi)}u^{-\gamma}<+\infty.

We explicitly stress that this last estimate
(which is related with the
singular term

u^{-\gamma}
) is the unique estimate involving
the
L^{\infty}
-norm of the test function
\varphi
; on the contrary,
estimates (
2.34
)-(
2.35
) only involve
the
S_{0}^{1}
-norm
of
\varphi
.

2)  If
u\in S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is a weak
solution
of
problem
(
2.31
), and if
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is a non-negative
test function (that is,
\varphi\geq 0
in
\Omega
), by (
2.34
)-(
2.35
)
we have

\displaystyle 0\leq\int_{\Omega}u^{-\gamma}\varphi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\Big(\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u,\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varphi\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}+\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)\varphi\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\|\varphi\|_{S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}\big(1+\|u\|_{S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2^{\star}_{Q}-1}\big);

from this, by using a standard
density argument
, we can easily prove the following facts:

a)

u^{-\gamma}\varphi\in L^{1}(\Omega)

for every
\varphi\in S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
;

b)

identity (
2.33
)
actually holds
for every
\varphi\in S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

3)  Let
u\in S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
be a weak
solution
of problem (
2.31
).
Since, in particular, we know that

u>0
a.e. in
\Omega
(and
u\equiv 0
a.e. in
\mathbb{G}\setminus\Omega
), it is quite easy
to recognize that
u
is a
weak supersolution of the equation

-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}u=0\quad\text{in $\Omega$},

in the sense of Definition
2.5
.
As a consequence, we are entitled to apply
[
29
]
, ensuring that

\begin{gathered}\text{for every $\mathcal{O}\Subset\Omega$ there exists $c(\mathcal{O},u)>0$
s.t.\,$u\geq c(\mathcal{O},u)>0$ a.e.\,in $\mathcal{O}$}.\end{gathered}

2.5.
Singular problem

The last preliminary result
we aim to present concerns the

unperturbed
, purely singular version of problem (

P

)
λ
, that is,

(2.36)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}u=\dfrac{\lambda}{u^{\gamma}}&\textrm{ in }\Omega,\\
u>0&\textrm{ in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\textrm{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
\gamma\in(0,1)
and
\lambda>0
. We say that
u\in S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is a weak solution of (
2.36
) accordingly to Definition
2.5
with
f\equiv 0
.
In this context, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{G}

be a bounded open set. Moreover, let
\gamma\in(0,1)
and
\lambda>0
.

Then, there exists a unique weak solution
w_{\lambda}\in S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
to (
2.36
). Moreover,
w_{\lambda}
is a global minimizer in the
S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
-topology
of the functional

(2.37)

J_{\lambda}(u):=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u|^{2}-\dfrac{\lambda}{1-\gamma}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{1-\gamma}.

Finally, we also have that
J_{\lambda}(w_{\lambda})<0
.

Proof.

The proof of this theorem is very similar
to that of
[
54
, Theorem 1.1]
(for the solvability of
(
2.36
)), and exploits a classical scheme of Stampacchia (for the
global boundedness of
w_{\lambda}
); however, we present it
here in detail for the sake of completeness.

To ease the readability, we split the proof into five steps.

Step I).
In this first step we prove that
J_{\lambda}
possesses a

global and strictly negative minimum
, which is attained by
a function
w_{\lambda}\in S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}

such that
w_{\lambda}\geq 0
a.e. in
\Omega
.

To this we first observe that, since
S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
is a Hilbert
space and since
0<1-\gamma<1
, the functional
J_{\lambda}
is weakly
lower semicontinuous on
S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
(notice that
\||\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u|\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

is precisely the
norm
of
S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
). Moreover, owing to the Sobolev inequality
(
2.12
) and using Hölder’s inequality, we have

J_{\lambda}(u)\geq\dfrac{\|u\|^{2}_{S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}}{2}-\dfrac{\lambda C}{1-\gamma}\|u\|^{1-\gamma}_{S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

(for some positive constant
C
only depending on
Q
and
\Omega
),
and this proves that
J_{\lambda}
is
coercive
on
S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.
Gathering these facts, we then derive that

J_{\lambda}

attains a
global minimum
, that is, there exists
a function
w_{\lambda}\in S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)

such that

J_{\lambda}(w_{\lambda})=\min_{u\in S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}J_{\lambda}(u)=m_{\lambda}.

We now observe that, if
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is a fixed non-vanishing
function, we have

J_{\lambda}(t\varphi)=\frac{t^{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varphi|^{2}-\dfrac{\lambda t^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma}\int_{\Omega}|\varphi|^{1-\gamma}\to-\infty\quad\text{as $t\to 0^{+}$}

(recall that
0<1-\gamma<1
); as a consequence, we have

m_{\lambda}<0
, and thus
w_{\lambda}\not\equiv 0
.

Finally, taking into account that
J_{\lambda}(|u|)=J_{\lambda}(u)
for every
u\in S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
,
by possibly replacing the function
w_{\lambda}
with
|w_{\lambda}|
, we can assume that

w_{\lambda}\geq 0
a.e. in
\Omega
.

Step II).
In this second step we prove that the global, non-negative
minimizer
w_{\lambda}\in S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)

obtained in the previous step is actually strictly positive (a.e.) in
\Omega
.

To this end, we arbitrarily fix a
non-negative
function
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)

and we notice that, since
the function
w_{\lambda}
is a global minimizer of
J_{\lambda}
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{t\to 0^{+}}\Big\{\frac{1}{t}(J_{\lambda}(w_{\lambda}+t\varphi)-J_{\lambda}(w_{\lambda}))\Big\}\leq\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w_{\lambda},\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varphi\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}},

and thus
-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}u\geq 0
in the weak sense on
\Omega
;
from this, by using the Strong Maximum Principle in Proposition

2.4
(and since that
w_{\lambda}\not\equiv 0
), we deduce that

w_{\lambda}>0
a.e. in
\Omega
.

Step III).
In this third step, we prove that the function
w_{\lambda}\in S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)

(which we have already proved to be a global and strictly positive minimzer of

J_{\lambda})
is a weak solution of

(2.38)

-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}w_{\lambda}=\frac{\lambda}{w_{\lambda}^{\gamma}}\quad\text{in $\Omega$},

and hence of the whole problem (
2.36
).

To this end we first observe that, since
w_{\lambda}
is a global minimizer of
J_{\lambda}
, the point
t_{0}=0
is a

global minimum
for the (smooth) map
h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

h(t)=J_{\lambda}\big((1+t)w_{\lambda})\big)=\frac{(1+t)^{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w_{\lambda}|^{2}-\frac{\lambda(1+t)^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma}\int_{\Omega}|w_{\lambda}|^{1-\gamma};

as a consequence, we have

(2.39)

0=h^{\prime}(0)=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w_{\lambda}|^{2}-\lambda\int_{\Omega}|w_{\lambda}|^{1-\gamma}.

Moreover, again by the minimality of
J_{\lambda}(w_{\lambda})
, we also get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq J_{\lambda}(w_{\lambda}+t\varphi)-J_{\lambda}(w_{\lambda})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w_{\lambda},\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varphi\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}+\frac{t^{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varphi|^{2}-\frac{\lambda}{1-\gamma}\int_{\Omega}\big[(w_{\lambda}+t\varphi)^{1-\gamma}-w_{\lambda}^{1-\gamma}\big]

for every
non-negative function
\varphi\in S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and every
t>0
;
thus, by letting
t\to 0^{+}
with the aid of the Fatou Lemma
(and by the arbitrariness of
\varphi
),
we obtain

(2.40)

\int_{\Omega}\big(\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w_{\lambda},\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varphi\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}-{\lambda}w_{\lambda}^{-\gamma}\varphi\big)\geq 0,\quad\text{for every
$\varphi\in S_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\,\varphi\geq 0$}.

With estimates (
2.39
)-(
2.40
) at hand,
we can easily complete the demonstration of this step.

Indeed, given any

\psi\in S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, by applying
(
2.40
) to

\varphi=(w_{\lambda}+\varepsilon\psi)_{+}\in S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)

(and with
\varepsilon>0
arbitrarily fixed), and by using (
2.39
), we get

\displaystyle 0\leq\int_{\Omega}\big(\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w_{\lambda},\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varphi\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}-{\lambda}w_{\lambda}^{-\gamma}\varphi\big)

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{\{w_{\lambda}+\varepsilon\psi\geq 0\}}\big(\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w_{\lambda},\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}(w_{\lambda}+\varepsilon\psi)\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}-{\lambda}w_{\lambda}^{-\gamma}(w_{\lambda}+\varepsilon\psi)\big)

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{\Omega}\big(\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w_{\lambda},\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}(w_{\lambda}+\varepsilon\psi)\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}-{\lambda}w_{\lambda}^{-\gamma}(w_{\lambda}+\varepsilon\psi)\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\int_{\{w_{\lambda}+\varepsilon\psi<0\}}\big(\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w_{\lambda},\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}(w_{\lambda}+\varepsilon\psi)\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}-{\lambda}w_{\lambda}^{-\gamma}(w_{\lambda}+\varepsilon\psi)\big)

\displaystyle\qquad=\Big(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w_{\lambda}|^{2}-\lambda\int_{\Omega}|w_{\lambda}|^{1-\gamma}\Big)+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\big(\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w_{\lambda},\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\psi\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}-{\lambda}w_{\lambda}^{-\gamma}\psi\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\int_{\{w_{\lambda}+\varepsilon\psi<0\}}\big(\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w_{\lambda},\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}(w_{\lambda}+\varepsilon\psi)\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}-{\lambda}w_{\lambda}^{-\gamma}(w_{\lambda}+\varepsilon\psi)\big)

\displaystyle\qquad(\text{here we use \eqref{eq:operatorPartzero}})

\displaystyle\qquad=\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\big(\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w_{\lambda},\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\psi\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}-{\lambda}w_{\lambda}^{-\gamma}\psi\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\int_{\{w_{\lambda}+\varepsilon\psi<0\}}\big(\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w_{\lambda},\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}(w_{\lambda}+\varepsilon\psi)\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}-{\lambda}w_{\lambda}^{-\gamma}(w_{\lambda}+\varepsilon\psi)\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\big(\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w_{\lambda},\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\psi\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}-{\lambda}w_{\lambda}^{-\gamma}\psi\big)-\varepsilon\int_{\{w_{\lambda}+\varepsilon\psi<0\}}\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w_{\lambda},\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\psi\rangle,

where we have used the fact that
w_{\lambda}>0
a.e. in
\Omega
.
In view of this fact, we also recognize that the Lebesgue measure of the set
A_{\varepsilon}=\{w_{\lambda}+\varepsilon\psi<0\}

goes to
0
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
: indeed, since

\psi<-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}w_{\lambda}<0\quad\text{on $A_{\varepsilon}$},

and since
\Omega\supseteq A_{\varepsilon}
has finite measure, we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}|A_{\varepsilon}|=\Big|\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}A_{\varepsilon}\Big|=|\{w_{\lambda}\leq 0\}|=0.

Hence, from the above estimate
(and by the arbitrariness of
\varepsilon>0
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\big(\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w_{\lambda},\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\psi\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}-{\lambda}w_{\lambda}^{-\gamma}\psi\big)\geq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\{w_{\lambda}+\varepsilon\psi<0\}}\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w_{\lambda},\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\psi\rangle=0.

This, together with the arbitrariness of

\psi\in S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, allows us to finally conclude that

w_{\lambda}
is a weak solution of equation
(
2.38
), as desired.

Step IV).
In this fourth step we show that
w_{\lambda}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
To this end we first observe that, since we
already know
that

w_{\lambda}
is a weak solution
of (
2.36
), for every
k>1
we have

\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w_{\lambda},\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}[(w_{\lambda}-k)_{+}]\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}-\lambda\int_{\Omega}w_{\lambda}^{-\gamma}(w_{\lambda}-k)_{+}=0;

from this, by using the Hölder inequality and the weighted Young inequality, together
with the continuous embedding of
S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
into
L^{2_{Q}^{\star}}(\Omega)
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}[(w_{\lambda}-k)_{+}]|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lambda}{k^{\gamma}}\int_{A_{k}}(w_{\lambda}-k)_{+}\leq\frac{\lambda}{k^{\gamma}}|A_{k}|^{1-1/2_{Q}^{\star}}\Big(\int_{\Omega}|(w_{\lambda}-k)_{+}|^{2_{Q}^{\star}}\Big)^{1/2_{Q}^{\star}}

\displaystyle\leq c|A_{k}|^{1-1/2_{Q}^{\star}}\Big(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}[w_{\lambda}-k)_{+}]|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq c(\varepsilon)|A_{k}|^{2(1-1/2_{Q}^{\star})}+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}[w_{\lambda}-k)_{+}]|^{2},

where
c(\varepsilon)>0
is a suitable constant depending on
\varepsilon
, and

A_{k}=\{w_{\lambda}\geq k\}.

From this, by choosing
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, we get

(2.41)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}[(w_{\lambda}-k)_{+}]|^{2}\leq c|A_{k}|^{2(1-1/2_{Q}^{\star})}.

With estimate (
2.41
) at hand,
we can easily complete the proof of this step (and of the whole
theorem). Indeed, owing to the cited (
2.41
), for every

1<k<h
we have

\displaystyle(h-k)^{2}|A_{h}|^{2/2_{Q}^{\star}}

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{A_{h}}(w_{\lambda}-k)_{+}^{2_{Q}^{\star}}\Big)^{2/2_{Q}^{\star}}\leq\Big(\int_{A_{k}}(w_{\lambda}-k)_{+}^{2_{Q}^{\star}}\Big)^{2/2_{Q}^{\star}}

\displaystyle(\text{here we use once again \eqref{eq:Sobolev_Ineq}})

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}[(w_{\lambda}-k)_{+}]|^{2}\leq c|A_{k}|^{2(1-1/2_{Q}^{\star})},

for some constant
c>0
independent of
k,h
; as a consequence, we get

|A_{h}|\leq\frac{c}{(h-k)^{2_{Q}^{\star}}}|A_{k}|^{2_{Q}^{\star}-1}\quad\text{for all $1<k<h$}.

Since, obviously,
q=2_{Q}^{\star}-1>1
, we can apply
[
36
, Lemma B.1]
: this gives

|A_{d}|=|\{w_{\lambda}\geq d\}|=0

for some
d>0
, and thus
w_{\lambda}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, as desired.

Step V)
. In this last step, we prove that
w_{\lambda}
is
the
unique
weak solution of problem (
2.36
). To this end, let us suppose that
z_{\lambda}\in S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
is another
weak solution of the same problem; we then choose a smooth function
\theta\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying the following properties

•

\theta(t)=0
for
t\leq 0
and
\theta(t)=1
for
t\geq 1
;

•

\theta
is non-decreasing on
\mathbb{R}
;

and we define (for every
\varepsilon>0
)

\theta_{\varepsilon}(t):=\theta\left(\frac{t}{\varepsilon}\right).

Now, since
\varphi_{\varepsilon}\in S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, and since

w_{\lambda},z_{\lambda}
solve (
2.36
), we get

\displaystyle(\ast)\,\,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w_{\lambda},\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}(w_{\lambda}-z_{\lambda})\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}\,\theta^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(w_{\lambda}-z_{\lambda})-\lambda\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{\theta_{\varepsilon}(w_{\lambda}-z_{\lambda})}{w_{\lambda}^{\gamma}}=0;

\displaystyle(\ast)\,\,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}z_{\lambda},\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}(w_{\lambda}-z_{\lambda})\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}\,\theta^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(w_{\lambda}-z_{\lambda})-\lambda\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{\theta_{\varepsilon}(w_{\lambda}-z_{\lambda})}{z_{\lambda}^{\gamma}}=0.

As a consequence, by subtracting the above identities, we obtain

(2.42)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(z_{\lambda}-w_{\lambda})|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}\,\theta^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(w_{\lambda}-z_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\lambda\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{1}{z_{\lambda}^{\gamma}}-\frac{1}{w_{\lambda}^{\gamma}}\Big)\theta_{\varepsilon}(w_{\lambda}-z_{\lambda}).

From this, by letting
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
we derive that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\dfrac{1}{z_{\lambda}^{\gamma}}-\dfrac{1}{w_{\lambda}^{\gamma}}\right)\theta_{\varepsilon}(w_{\lambda}-z_{\lambda})=\int_{\{w_{\lambda}>z_{\lambda}\}}\left(\dfrac{1}{z_{\lambda}^{\gamma}}-\dfrac{1}{w_{\lambda}^{\gamma}}\right)\leq 0,

from which we derive that

|\{w_{\lambda}>z_{\lambda}\}|=0
, that is,

w_{\lambda}\leq z_{\lambda}
a.e. on
\Omega
. Finally, by interchanging the role of
z_{\lambda}
and
w_{\lambda}
,
we conclude that

\text{$w_{\lambda}\equiv z_{\lambda}$ a.e.\,in $\Omega$},

and this proves the uniqueness of
w_{\lambda}
.
∎

3.
Existence of the first solution

To begin with, we define

(3.1)

\Lambda:=\sup\{\lambda>0:\eqref{eq:Main_Problem}_{\lambda}\textrm{ admits a weak solution}\}.

We then turn to prove in this first part of the section the following facts:

a)
\Lambda
is well-defined and
\Lambda<+\infty
;

b)  problem (

P

)
λ
admits a weak solution for every
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
.

We begin by proving assertion a).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Lambda
be as in (
3.1
). Then
\Lambda\in(0,+\infty)
.

Proof.

We consider the functional

(3.2)

I_{\lambda}(u):=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u|^{2}-\dfrac{\lambda}{1-\gamma}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{1-\gamma}-\dfrac{1}{2^{\star}_{Q}}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2^{\star}_{Q}},\quad u\in S^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

First of all, by combining Hölder’s and Sobolev’s inequalities, we have

(3.3)

\begin{split}&\mathrm{a)}\,\,\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2^{\star}_{Q}}\leq C\||\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u|\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2^{\star}_{Q}};\\
&\mathrm{b)}\,\,\int_{\Omega}|u|^{1-\gamma}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2^{\star}_{Q}}(\Omega)}^{(1-\gamma)/2^{\star}_{Q}}\leq C\||\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u|\|^{1-\gamma}_{L^{2}(\Omega)};\end{split}

as a consequence, denoting by

B_{r}:=\left\{u\in S^{1}_{0}(\Omega):\|u\|_{S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq r\right\},

the above estimates (
3.3
-a) imply the existence of
r_{0}>0
and
\delta_{0}>0
such that

(3.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\tfrac{1}{2}\||\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u|\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2^{\star}_{Q}}\|u\|^{2^{\star}_{Q}}_{L^{2^{\star}_{Q}}(\Omega)}\geq 2\delta_{0}&\textrm{for all }u\in\partial B_{r_{0}},\\[5.69046pt]
\tfrac{1}{2}\||\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u|\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2^{\star}_{Q}}\|u\|^{2^{\star}_{Q}}_{L^{2^{\star}_{Q}}(\Omega)}\geq 0&\textrm{for all }\in B_{r_{0}},\end{array}\right.

hence, again by (
3.3
-b) we conclude that there exists
\lambda_{\star}>0

such that

(3.5)

\begin{split}I_{\lambda}\big|_{\partial B_{r_{0}}}&\geq 2\delta_{0}-\frac{\lambda\,C}{1-\gamma}r_{0}^{1-\gamma}\geq\delta_{0},\quad\textrm{for every }\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{\star}].\end{split}

We now define
c_{\star}:=\inf_{B_{r_{0}}}I_{\lambda_{\star}}
, and we notice that
c_{\star}<0
. Indeed, for every
v\not\equiv 0
, it holds that

I_{\lambda_{\star}}(tv)=t^{2}\||\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v|\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\dfrac{\lambda_{\star}}{1-\gamma}t^{1-\gamma}\int_{\Omega}|v|^{1-\gamma}-\dfrac{t^{2^{\star}_{Q}}}{2^{\star}_{Q}}\int_{\Omega}|v|^{2^{\star}_{Q}},

which becomes negative for
t>0
small enough because
0<1-\gamma<1
. The argument is now pretty standard. We first consider a minimizing sequence
\{u_{j}\}_{j}\subset B_{r_{0}}
related to
c_{\star}
and we know that there exists
u_{\star}
such that, up to subsequences,

•

u_{j}\to u_{\star}
as
j\to+\infty
weakly in
S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
;

•

u_{j}\to u_{\star}
as
j\to+\infty
strongly in
L^{r}(\Omega)
for every
r\in[2,2^{\star}_{Q})
;

•

u_{j}\to u_{\star}
as
j\to+\infty
pointwise a.e. in
\Omega
.

Moreover, since
I_{\lambda}(|u|)=I_{\lambda}(u)
for every
\lambda>0
, we may also assume that
u_{j}\geq 0
.
Combining (
3.5
) with
c_{\star}<0
, we realize that there exists a positive, and independent of

\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that

(3.6)

\|u_{j}\|_{S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq r_{0}-\varepsilon_{0}.

Now, by combining the
algebraic inequality
(a+b)^{p}\leq a^{b}+b^{p}
(holding true for
all
a,b\geq 0
and
0<p<1
) with the
Hölder inequality, as

we have

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}u_{j}^{1-\gamma}&\leq\int_{\Omega}u_{\star}^{1-\gamma}+\int_{\Omega}|u_{j}-u_{\star}|^{1-\gamma}\\
&\leq\int_{\Omega}u_{\star}^{1-\gamma}+C\|u_{j}-u_{\star}\|^{1-\gamma}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\int_{\Omega}u_{\star}^{1-\gamma}+o(1),\end{split}

and similarly

\int_{\Omega}u_{\star}^{1-\gamma}\leq\int_{\Omega}u_{j}^{1-\gamma}+\int_{\Omega}|u_{j}-u_{\star}|^{1-\gamma}=\int_{\Omega}u_{j}^{1-\gamma}+o(1),

which in turn implies

(3.7)

\int_{\Omega}u_{j}^{1-\gamma}=\int_{\Omega}u_{\star}^{1-\gamma}+o(1),\quad\textrm{as }j\to+\infty.

By Brezis-Lieb lemma, see
[
9
]

(3.8)

\|u_{j}\|^{2^{\star}_{Q}}_{L^{2^{\star}_{Q}}(\Omega)}=\|u_{\star}\|^{2^{\star}_{Q}}_{L^{2^{\star}_{Q}}(\Omega)}+\|u_{j}-u_{\star}\|^{2^{\star}_{Q}}_{L^{2^{\star}_{Q}}(\Omega)}+o(1),\quad\textrm{as }j\to+\infty,

and

(3.9)

\||\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u_{j}|\|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}=\||\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u_{\star}|\|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}+\||\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}(u_{j}-u_{\star})|\|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}+o(1),\quad\textrm{as }j\to+\infty.

By combining (

) and (

),
it follows that

and that

for big enough

) on

.
Using now (

)-(

, we find

\displaystyle c_{\star}

\displaystyle=I_{\lambda_{\star}}(u_{j})+o(1)

\displaystyle=I_{\lambda_{\star}}(u_{\star})+\dfrac{1}{2}\||\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}(u_{j}-u_{\star})|\|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}-\dfrac{1}{2^{\star}_{Q}}\|u_{j}-u_{\star}\|^{2^{\star}_{Q}}_{L^{2^{\star}_{Q}}(\Omega)}+o(1)

\displaystyle\geq I_{\lambda_{\star}}(u_{\star})+o(1)\geq c_{\star}+o(1),

which proves that
u_{\star}\geq 0
,
u_{\star}\not\equiv 0
is a local minimizer of

in the
S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

From this, by arguing exactly as in the proof

is actually a weak solution of (

P

)
λ
,
and hence we get that

\Lambda\geq\lambda_{\star}>0.

Let us now prove that

. Following
[
30
]
, we consider
the
first

of the operator

\mu_{1}=\min\big\{\||\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}|\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}:\,\text{$u\in S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)$ and $\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1$}\big\},

The proof of the existence of such a function

Now, assuming that there exists

(

)
λ

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

be a

[
resp.

]

and a

of problem (

P

)
λ
, respectively.

[

[

We limit ourselves to consider only the case when

P

)

is a

To begin with, we arbitrarily fix a bounded open set

)

Lemma 3.4
.

\text{$\underline{u}\geq C$ a.e.\,in $\mathcal{O}$}.

of (

\text{$\underline{u}(g)\leq u(g)\leq\overline{u}(g)$ for a.e. $g\in\Omega$}.

Proof.

We adapt to our setting the proof of
[
30

.
We consider the set

M:=\left\{u\in S^{1}_{0}(\Omega):\underline{u}\leq u\leq\overline{u}\textrm{ a.e. in }\Omega\right\},

Step 1:
we claim that there exists a relative minimizer

of

on

It is enough to show that
I_{\lambda}
is w.l.s.c. on

\Omega

Thanks to the continuous embedding of

\int_{\Omega}\overline{u}^{2^{\star}_{Q}}<+\infty\quad\int_{\Omega}\overline{u}^{1-\gamma}<+\infty,

where in the latter we used first Hölder inequality.
Hence, by dominated convergence, we have that, as

\|u_{j}\|^{2^{\star}_{Q}}_{L^{2^{\star}_{Q}}(\Omega)}\to\|u\|^{2^{\star}_{Q}}_{L^{2^{\star}_{Q}}(\Omega)}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\int_{\Omega}|u_{j}|^{1-\gamma}\to\int_{\Omega}|u|^{1-\gamma}.

Therefore,

\liminf_{j\to+\infty}I_{\lambda}(u_{j})\geq I_{\lambda}(u),

as desired.

Step 2:
we prove that
u_{\lambda}
is a weak solution of (

P

)
λ
. Just in this paragraph

We take
\varphi\in S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and

\varphi^{\varepsilon}:=(u+\varepsilon\varphi-\overline{u})_{+}\qquad\varphi_{\varepsilon}:=(u+\varepsilon\varphi-\underline{u})_{-}.

Since

(3.10)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\dfrac{I_{\lambda}(u+t(v_{\varepsilon}-u))-I_{\lambda}(u)}{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u,\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}(v_{\varepsilon}-u)\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}-\lambda\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{v_{\varepsilon}-u}{u^{\gamma}}-\int_{\Omega}u^{2^{\star}_{Q}-1}(v_{\varepsilon}-u).

We omit the details concerning the second integral, we refer to
[

]
for the details.

Set now

and

With this notation at hand, we can write (

\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u,\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varphi\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}-\lambda\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{\varphi}{u^{\gamma}}-\int_{\Omega}u^{2^{\star}_{Q}-1}\varphi\geq\dfrac{E^{\varepsilon}-E_{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}.

We want to show that

\dfrac{E^{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}\geq o(1)\quad\textrm{and }\quad\dfrac{E_{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}\leq o(1),\quad\textrm{as }\varepsilon\to 0^{+}.

We will show only the first one, being the second very similar.
Firstly, we define the sets

\displaystyle\Omega^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle:=\left\{g\in\Omega:u(g)+\varepsilon\varphi(g)\geq\overline{u}(g)>u(g)\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}\Omega^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle:=\{g\in\Omega:u(g)+\varepsilon\varphi(x)<\overline{u}(g)\}.

and notice that, being

and
\overline{u}
measurable functions,

Indeed,
\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}\Omega^{\varepsilon}=\varnothing
, and
as

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}|\Omega^{\varepsilon}|=\left|\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}\Omega^{\varepsilon}\right|=0.

Therefore, following
[
30
]
, we have that

(3.11)

\displaystyle\dfrac{E^{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\left(\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}(u-\overline{u}),\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varphi^{\varepsilon}\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}+\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\overline{u},\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varphi^{\varepsilon}\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}-\int_{\Omega}(\lambda u^{-\gamma}+u^{2^{\star}_{Q}-1})\varphi^{\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}(u-\overline{u}),\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varphi^{\varepsilon}\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}+\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}}(\lambda\overline{u}^{-\gamma}+\overline{u}^{2^{\star}_{Q}-1}-\lambda u^{-\gamma}-u^{2^{\star}_{Q}-1})\varphi^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}(u-\overline{u}),\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varphi^{\varepsilon}\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}-\dfrac{\lambda}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}}|\overline{u}^{-\gamma}-u^{-\gamma}||\varphi|.

Now, owing to (
2.15
) and since
\varphi^{\varepsilon}=0
on
\mathcal{C}\Omega^{\varepsilon}
, we have that

(3.12)

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}(u-\overline{u}),\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varphi^{\varepsilon}\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}=\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}}\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}(u-\overline{u}),\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varphi^{\varepsilon}\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}}|\nabla(u-\overline{u})|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}+\int_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}}\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}(u-\overline{u}),\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varphi\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}}\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}(u-\overline{u}),\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varphi\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}=o(1),\qquad\textrm{as }\varepsilon\to 0^{+}.

Combining (
3.11
) and (
3.12
), we finally get

(3.13)

\dfrac{E^{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}\geq o(1),\quad\textrm{as }\varepsilon\to 0^{+}.

Similarly

(3.14)

\dfrac{E_{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}\leq o(1),\quad\textrm{as }\varepsilon\to 0^{+}.

Combining (
3.13
) and (
3.14
), we get that

\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u,\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varphi\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}-\int_{\Omega}(\lambda u^{-\gamma}-u^{2^{\star}_{Q}-1})\varphi\geq o(1)\quad\textrm{as }\varepsilon\to 0^{+}.

Taking now
-\varphi
, and passing to the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, one closes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.5

Lemma 3.6

Proof.

By contradiction, suppose

\displaystyle A_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\overline{w}_{j}|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}+\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\overline{u},\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\overline{w}_{j}\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{\overline{S}_{j}}\Big\{\frac{\lambda}{1-\gamma}(|\overline{u}+\overline{w}_{j}|^{1-\gamma}-|\overline{u}|^{1-\gamma})+\frac{1}{2^{\star}_{Q}}(|\overline{u}+\overline{w}_{j}|^{2^{\star}_{Q}}-|\overline{u}|^{2^{\star}_{Q}})\Big\}

\displaystyle(\text{by the Mean Value Theorem, for some $\theta\in(0,1)$})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\overline{w}_{j}|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}+\int_{\overline{S}_{j}}(\lambda\overline{u}^{-\gamma}+\overline{u}^{2^{\star}_{Q}-1})\overline{w}_{j}\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{\overline{S}_{j}}\big\{\lambda(\overline{u}+\theta\overline{w}_{j})^{-\gamma}\overline{w}_{j}+(\overline{u}+\theta\overline{w}_{j})^{2^{\star}_{Q}-1}\overline{w}_{j}\}dx

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\overline{w}_{j}|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}-\int_{\overline{S}_{j}}\big((\overline{u}+\theta\overline{w}_{j})^{2^{\star}_{Q}-1}-\overline{u}^{2^{\star}_{Q}-1}\big)\overline{w}_{j}\,dx

\displaystyle(\text{again by the Mean Value Theorem})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\overline{w}_{j}|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}-C\int_{\overline{S}_{j}}(\overline{u}^{2^{\star}_{Q}-2}+\overline{w}_{j}^{2^{\star}_{Q}-2})\overline{w}_{j}^{2},

where
C>0
is a suitable constant only depending on the dimension
n
.
From this, by exploiting Hölder’s and Sobolev’s inequalities, we obtain

(3.27)

\begin{split}A_{j}&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\overline{w}_{j}|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}-C\int_{\overline{S}_{j}}(\overline{u}^{2^{\star}_{Q}-2}+\overline{w}_{j}^{2^{\star}_{Q}-2})\overline{w}_{j}^{2}\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\overline{w}_{j}|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}\left\{1-\hat{C}\Big(\int_{\overline{S}_{j}}\overline{u}^{2^{\star}_{Q}}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{2^{\star}_{Q}-2}{2^{\star}_{Q}}}-\hat{C}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\overline{w}_{j}|^{2}_{\mathbb{G}}\right)^{(2^{\star}_{Q}-2)/2}\right\},\end{split}

where
\hat{C}>0
is another constant depending on
Q
.

With (
3.27
) at hand, we are finally ready to complete the proof.
Indeed, taking into account the above (
3.23
), we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\Big(\int_{\overline{S}_{j}}\overline{u}^{2^{\star}_{Q}}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{2^{\star}_{Q}-2}{2^{\star}_{Q}}}=0;

moreover, since
u_{j}\to u_{\lambda}
in
S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
as
j\to+\infty
, one also get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\overline{w}_{j}|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}=\int_{\overline{S}_{j}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}(u_{j}-\overline{u})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|u_{j}-u_{\lambda}\|_{S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{2}+2\int_{\overline{S}_{j}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}(u_{\lambda}-\overline{u})|^{2}\to 0\qquad\text{as $j\to+\infty$}.

Gathering these facts, we then infer the existence of some
j_{0}\geq 1
such that

A_{j}\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\overline{w}_{j}|_{\mathbb{G}}^{2}\left\{1-\hat{C}\Big(\int_{\overline{S}_{j}}\overline{u}^{2^{\star}_{Q}}\Big)^{\frac{2^{\star}_{Q}-2}{2^{\star}_{Q}}}-\hat{C}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\overline{w}_{j}|^{2}_{\mathbb{G}}\right)^{(2^{\star}_{Q}-2)/2}\right\}\geq 0\quad\forall\,\,j\geq j_{0}.

By arguing in a very similar way, one can prove that
B_{j}\geq 0
for every
j\geq j_{0}

(by possibly enlarging
j_{0}
if needed); as a consequence, from
(
3.26
) we get

I_{\lambda}(u_{j})\geq I_{\lambda}(u_{\lambda})+A_{j}+B_{j}\geq I_{\lambda}(u_{\lambda}),

but this is contradiction with property ii) of the sequence
\{u_{j}\}_{j}
.
∎

For this purpose, we will apply the Ekeland’s variational principle

For any

)

4.1.
First case

)

We then choose

By construction

Since it is

moreover, since

to the functional

on

providing us with a sequence

(4.2)

\begin{split}\mathrm{i)}&\,\,I_{\lambda}(v_{k})\leq I_{\lambda}(u_{k})\leq I_{\lambda}(u_{\lambda})+1/k^{2},\\
\mathrm{ii)}&\,\,\|v_{k}-u_{k}\|_{S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq 1/k,\\
\mathrm{iii)}&\,\,I_{\lambda}(v_{k})\leq I_{\lambda}(u)+1/k\,\|v_{k}-u\|_{S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\quad\text{for every $u\in X$}.\end{split}

We now observe that, since

is
bounded

in

there exists

\begin{split}\mathrm{i)}&\,\,\text{$v_{k}\to v_{\lambda}$ weakly in $S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)$};\\
\mathrm{ii)}&\,\,\text{$v_{k}\to v_{\lambda}$ strongly in $L^{p}(\Omega)$ for every $1\leq p<2^{\star}_{Q}$};\\
\mathrm{iii)}&\,\,\text{$v_{k}\to v_{\lambda}$ pointwise
a.e.\,in $\Omega$}.\end{split}

where we have also used the

Lemma 4.1
.

The function

)

Proof.

We fix

By setting

with

Lemma 4.3

Appendix A
Generalized directional derivative

We introduce the generalized directional derivative. Consider
the convex cone
H_{\lambda}\subseteq S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
defined
at (
4.1
), and
consider any continuous and locally Lipschitz functional
I\colon{H_{\lambda}}\to\mathbb{R}
.
Moreover, to simplify the notation, in what follows we set

\|u\|=\|u\|_{S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}\quad\text{for every $u\in H_{\lambda}$}.

The
generalized directional derivative
at

x,v\in H_{\lambda}
is defined as

(A.1)

I^{0}(x,y):=\limsup_{\|h\|\to 0,\ \rho\to 0^{+}}\frac{I(x+h+\rho y)-I(x+h)}{\rho},

Lemma A.1
.

For fixed
x
, the map
I\mapsto I^{0}(x,v)
is subadditive with respect to
v
.

Proof.

We use the notation
I^{*}_{x,h,v}(\rho):=I(x+h+\rho v)-I(x+h)
.
Therefore,

I^{*}_{x,h,v+w}(\rho)=I^{*}_{x,h+\rho v,w}(\rho)+I^{*}_{x,h,v}(\rho).

Then, the lemma follows by observing that

\limsup_{\|h\|\to 0,\ \rho\to 0^{+}}I^{*}_{x,h+\rho v,w}(\rho)=\limsup_{\|h\|\to 0,\ \rho\to 0^{+}}I^{*}_{x,h,w}(\rho)

because any
h^{\prime}
sufficiently small can be represented
as
h+\rho v
for
h
and
\rho
sufficiently small.∎

Moreover, we suppose that
I
respects property (
4.18
)
and we apply the Ekeland’s principle to the continuous functional

\Phi(\eta):=\max_{t\in[0,1]}I(\eta(t)),

setting
\ell:=\inf_{\eta\in\Gamma_{\lambda}}\Phi(\eta)
.
As a direct consequence, there exists
a sequence
\{\eta_{k}\}_{k}\subset\Gamma_{\lambda}

verifying

(A.2)

\displaystyle\Phi(\eta_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\ell+\frac{1}{k}

(A.3)

\displaystyle\Phi(\eta_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\Phi(\eta)+\frac{1}{k}d(\eta_{k},\eta)\ \ \ \forall\eta\in\Gamma_{\lambda}

Lemma A.2
.

For every
k
consider
\Lambda_{k}:=\{t\in(0,1):\ I(\eta_{k}(t))=\max_{s\in[0,1]}I(\eta_{k}(s))\}
,
then for every
k
there exists
t_{k}\in\Lambda_{k}
such that,
v_{k}:=\eta_{k}(t_{k})
,
we have

(A.4)

I^{0}\left(v_{k};\ \frac{w-v_{k}}{\max(1,\|w-v_{k}\|)}\right)\geq-\frac{1}{k}\ \ \ \forall w\in H_{\lambda}.

Proof.

Observe that
\Lambda_{k}
is always a compact subset of
(0,1)
.
We star by considering the case when
\Lambda_{k}
is a single point
t_{k}
.

We argue by contradiction supposing that there exists
\omega\in H_{\lambda}

such that

(A.5)

I^{0}\left(\eta_{k}(t_{k});\ \frac{\omega-\eta_{k}(t_{k})}{\max(1,\|\omega-\eta_{k}(t_{k})\|}\right)<-\frac{1}{k}.

Consider a continuous cut-off function
g\colon[0,1]\to[0,1]
such that

in a neighborhood
of

g(0)=g(1)=0

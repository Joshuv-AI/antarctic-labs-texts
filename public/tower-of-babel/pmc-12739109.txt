Equity of total health expenditure in China, 2015–2021
Authors: Minghua Zhou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo analyze the equity of total health expenditure in China from 2015 to 2021 and to provide a scientific basis for promoting the healthy development of total health expenditure in China. Total health expenditure was divided into three components: government health expenditure, social health expenditure, and out-of-pocket health payments. Data from 2015 to 2021 were extracted, and the concentration index and Kakwani index were used to evaluate the equity. Mainland China (excluding Hong Kong, Macau, and Taiwan) was divided into the northeast, east, central, and west regions, and the health resource density index (HRDI) of the China average was used as the reference standard to calculate the deficiency and deficiency ratio in each region. The stepwise regression analysis was used to analyze the influencing factors of per capita total health expenditure in China. The average percentages of government health expenditure, social health expenditure, and out-of-pocket health payments were 28.26%, 43.94%, and 27.79%, respectively, from 2015 to 2021. The concentration indices of total health expenditure, government health expenditure, social health expenditure, and out-of-pocket health payments were all greater than 0. The Kakwani index of total health expenditure, government health expenditure, and out-of-pocket health payments was less than 0, while the Kakwani index of social health expenditure was greater than 0 overall but became less than 0 in 2021. Both GDP and total health expenditure in the northeast region moved toward lower percentages, and GDP and total health expenditure in the east region moved toward higher percentages. Taking the HRDI of China’s average as the reference standard, the HRDI for total health expenditure, government health expenditure, social health expenditure, and out-of-pocket health payments in the east and central regions were higher than the reference standard, and the health expenditure in the east was more than 50% of the reference standard and the central region was more than 4% of the reference standard. The HRDI of total health expenditure, government health expenditure, social health expenditure, and out-of-pocket health payments in the west region was less than the reference standard, and the deficiency ratio of health expenditure in the west region was more than 46%. The HRDI of out-of-pocket health payments in the northeast region was higher than the reference standard from 2015 to 2020, and the HRDI of total health expenditure, government health expenditure, and social health expenditure in the northeast region was lower than the reference standard. Per capita disposable income and the number of health technicians per 1,000 population were the main factors influencing per capita total health expenditure in China from 2015 to 2021. The health financing structure was inappropriate, with a low percentage of government health expenditure and a high percentage of out-of-pocket health payments. Total health expenditure, government health expenditure, social health expenditure, and out-of-pocket health payments, all of the health expenditure tended to regions with higher levels of economic development. Total health expenditure, government health expenditure, and out-of-pocket health payments were regressive, and social health expenditure was progressive overall. Health expenditure was more adequate in the east and central regions and more inadequate in the west region. Per capita disposable income and the number of health technicians per 1,000 population had a greater impact on per capita total health expenditure in China.Keywords: Total health expenditure, Concentration index, Kakwani index, Health resource density index, ChinaSubject terms: Health care economics, Health policy, Health services

Introduction

Total health expenditure is the total monetary amount of health resources that a country or region raises from society as a whole to provide health services in a given period of time1. It reflects the level of importance and expenditure on health by the government, society and individuals under certain economic conditions, as well as the main features of the health financing model and the equity and reasonableness of health financing. According to the source method of total health expenditure, China divides the total health expenditure into three components: government health expenditure, social health expenditure, and out-of-pocket health payments. Government health expenditure reflects the main responsibility of the government in health financing2, while social health expenditure and out-of-pocket health payments play a complementary role to government health expenditure3, which can better meet the people’s multi-level and diversified demand for medical services. To analyze the equity of total health expenditure and explore the path of healthy development of total health expenditure in China, these are of great significance to promote the high-quality development of health in China.

As socialism with Chinese characteristics enters a new era, the main social contradiction in China has become the contradiction between people’s growing need for a better life and unbalanced and inadequate development, which requires more attention to the public welfare and equity of health care4. At present, the research on total health expenditure in China mainly focuses on analyzing the spatial–temporal evolution of total health expenditure using Bayesian space–time model5, predicting the growth of total health expenditure using ARIMA model1, the trends and determinants of catastrophic health expenditure in China6, evaluating the equity of health expenditure for patients with rare diseases in China7, using the super-SBM model and the Malmquist productivity index to measure the health expenditure efficiency in rural China8, using multiple linear regression models to evaluate the relationship between out-of-pocket health payments and government health expenditure in China and OECD countries9, and using gray theory to analyze China’s total health expenditure and the driving factors10. There are fewer related studies that analyze the equity of total health expenditure and its components, and fewer that evaluate the deficiency of total health expenditure in the four regions of China. In order to explore the path for the healthy development of total health expenditure in China, this study used the concentration index and the Kakwani index to analyze the equity of total health expenditure and its components in China, used the health resource density index (HRDI) of the China average as the reference standard to calculate the deficiency and deficiency ratio in the northeast, east, central, and west regions, respectively, and used the stepwise regression analysis to analyze the influencing factors of per capita total health expenditure in China, in order to provide a reference basis for promoting the healthy development of total health expenditure, and improving the equity of total health expenditure in China.

Materials & methods

Data sources

Data on total health expenditure in China were obtained from the "China Health Statistical Yearbook" (2017–2023), and data on population, geographical area, gross domestic product (GDP), urbanization rate and per capita disposable income were obtained from the "China Statistical Yearbook" (2016–2022). The data for this study cover 31 provinces, municipalities and autonomous regions in mainland China (excluding Hong Kong, Macau and Taiwan) from 2015 to 2021.

According to the source method of total health expenditure in China, total health expenditure is composed of three components: government health expenditure, social health expenditure, and out-of-pocket health payments. Government health expenditure is the funding of all levels of government for health services, medical insurance subsidies, health and medical insurance administration, population and family planning affairs expenditures and other projects. Social health expenditure is the community’s financial investment in health in addition to government expenditure, including social medical insurance expenditure, commercial medical insurance premiums, social medical expenditure, social donation support, administrative fee income, etc. Out-of-pocket health payments is the cash payments made by urban and rural residents when they receive various types of medical services, including out-of-pocket expenses when seeking medical treatment for residents who are covered by various medical insurance systems. According to the general situation in China, the ratio of the total health expenditure structure is 3:4:3, i.e., social health expenditure is 40%, and government health expenditure and out-of-pocket health payments are 30% each. In order to reduce the proportion of out-of-pocket health payments, in 2016, according to the planning targets of the "Healthy China 2030" planning outline, the proportion of out-of-pocket health payments in the total health expenditure will be about 25% by 2030. With the development of the economy and the soundness of the social security system, the total health expenditure is composed of government health expenditure, social health expenditure and out-of-pocket health payments in the structural ratio of 6:2:2 recommended by the World Health Organization, which has a low personal risk.

In order to further analyze the influencing factors of health expenditure in China, comprehensively considered the influence of population, economy, and medical resource allocation on health expenditure, population density, urbanization rate, per capita GDP, per capita disposable income, number of beds per 1,000 population, and number of health technicians per 1,000 population were selected as the evaluation indexes of the influencing factors.

Setting

According to the new situation of accelerated economic and social development, Mainland China (excluding Hong Kong, Macau and Taiwan) has been divided into the northeast, east, central and west regions. Northeast region (Heilongjiang, Jilin and Liaoning), east region (Beijing, Tianjin, Hebei, Shandong, Jiangsu, Shanghai, Zhejiang, Fujian, Guangdong and Hainan), central region (Shanxi, Henan, Hunan, Hubei, Jiangxi and Anhui) and west region (Chongqing, Sichuan, Guangxi, Yunnan, Guizhou, Shaanxi, Gansu, Inner Mongolia, Qinghai, Ningxia, Xinjiang and Tibet).

Research methods

Descriptive analysis was used to evaluate the basic situation of total health expenditure, government health expenditure, social health expenditure and out-of-pocket health payments, and concentration index and Kakwani index were used to evaluate the equity of total health expenditure, government health expenditure, social health expenditure and out-of-pocket health payments. Taking the health resource density index(HRDI) of China’s average as the reference standard, the deficiency and deficiency ratio were calculated in the northeast, east, central and west regions, respectively. The stepwise regression analysis was used to analyze the influencing factors of per capita total health expenditure in China.

Results

Basic information of total health expenditure in China

The total health expenditure, government health expenditure, social health expenditure, and out-of-pocket health payments in China have increased from RMB 4111.561 billion, RMB 1227.952 billion, RMB 1660.911 billion and RMB 1222.698 billion in 2015 to RMB 7697.426 billion, RMB 2045.336 billion, RMB 3625.702 billion and RMB 2026.388 billion in 2021, respectively. The percentage of government health expenditure showed a decreasing, then increasing and then decreasing trend, and the percentage was the lowest value of 26.57% in 2021. The percentage of social health expenditure showed an increasing trend overall, reaching a maximum value of 47.10% in 2021. The percentage of out-of-pocket health payments showed a decreasing trend, with a minimum value of 26.33% in 2021. The average percentages of government health expenditure, social health expenditure and out-of-pocket health payments were 28.26%, 43.94%, and 27.79%, respectively, from 2015 to 2021 (Table 1).

Discussion

The average percentages of government health expenditure, social health expenditure and out-of-pocket health payments were 28.26%, 43.94%, and 27.79%, respectively, from 2015 to 2021. According to the requirement of 6:2:2 for the structure of government health expenditure, social health expenditure and out-of-pocket health payments recommended by the World Health Organization, this indicated that there was an unreasonable health financing structure, with a low proportion of government health expenditure and a high proportion of social health expenditure and out-of-pocket health payments. The proportion of government health expenditure was low because China’s medical and health care industry developed late, and although the government has increased its financial investment in the health system in recent years, there was still a considerable gap with major developed countries. With the development of China’s economy entering the “new normal”, GDP and fiscal revenue are gradually shifting to low growth rates, which will inevitably affect the government’s ability and capacity to invest in health. Inadequate government health expenditure affects the realization of the government’s primary responsibility for health development and has an important impact on reducing the burden of health care on society and individuals17. Based on the "Health China 2030" planning outline, the proportion of out-of-pocket health payments to total health expenditure will be about 25% by 2030, and there was room for further reduction in out-of-pocket health payments. According to the World Health Organization’s recommendations, the percentage of out-of-pocket health payments was 15% to 20% to better protect the people from health financing risks. Therefore, it is necessary to establish and improve a long-term and stable government health investment mechanism, implement the main responsibility of financing at all levels in the medical and health care system18, increase public financial expenditure on medical services in order to realize the need for medical care to achieve the desired health outcome19, further expand the financing channels and protection levels of social insurance and commercial insurance, and continuously reduce the proportion of out-of-pocket health payments to build a more reasonable health care financing structure.

Total health expenditure, government health expenditure, social health expenditure and out-of-pocket health payments, all of the health expenditure tended to regions with higher economic levels, indicating that health expenditure was closely related to the level of economic development. The economically developed regions were able to provide higher health expenditure, while the economically relatively backward regions had a lack of health expenditure20. We believe there are three reasons for this. First, the level of economic development was the economic basis for health expenditure, the more economically developed regions have relatively higher financial income, and the more sufficient financial resources will be invested in the development of health care21. Second, regions with higher levels of economic development had a more complete construction of health care systems, better development of the social medical services, active medical services market, which could attract the people to make reasonable use of medical and health services, so as to meet the people’s multi-level and diversified health care needs. Third, people in regions with higher levels of economic development had a greater awareness of health care and were more willing and able to pay for health care services22. Therefore, it is necessary to further develop the social economy and lay a solid economic foundation for health expenditure, and to further improve the health care system and form a complete medical service market23. It is necessary to strengthen the special treatment of major diseases among the poor, increase the reimbursement rate for medical expenses for major diseases, and gradually raise the level of medical protection. The government should adhere to the concept of “big health”, try to categorize and treat major and chronic diseases, strengthen disease prevention and control, and promote the advancement of the health gateway to poverty alleviation.

Compared with social health expenditure and out-of-pocket health payments, the equity of government health expenditure was relatively better, indicating that the government has fully fulfilled the primary responsibility of health investment and actively regulated the direction of health expenditure, and played a positive role in the equity of health expenditure. Regions with a higher level of economic development have a more concentrated population, a relatively well-developed medical and health care system, and a stronger social and individual ability to pay, resulting in relatively less equitable social health expenditure and out-of-pocket health payments24. Regions with a lower level of economic development have an inadequate health care system, which combined with the low expenditure on health and the loss of health care personnel, has led to further inequities in the use of health care services25. Regions with lower levels of economic development rely more on government investment in health and promote the construction of a basic public health service system in order to promote access to health services for local residents. Therefore, it is necessary to maintain the primary responsibility of government health expenditure26, increase transfer payments to economically backward and remote regions, gradually narrow the treatment gap between different regions and different groups of people, strengthen the basic and bottom-up protection of the impoverished masses, improve the equity of health expenditure, and promote the goal of basic medical and health services for all.

The Kakwani index of total health expenditure, government health expenditure, and out-of-pocket health payments was less than 0 when calculated using health expenditure in 31 regions, indicating that total health expenditure, government health expenditure, and out-of-pocket health payments were regressive and that the growth of health expenditure was lower than the growth of income. It has been influenced by the transition to low economic growth, which has affected the government’s ability to invest in health. At the same time, the real beneficiaries of government health investment in health are often those who have the ability to actually use health services, and the poor will have limited use of health services and often have no actual ability to use them due to lack of compensation, making government health expenditure to be regressive. Out-of-pocket health payments are regressive, indicating that out-of-pocket health payments are less equitable and are not conducive to access to basic health services for low-income populations, especially with a greater impact on health service utilization for the poor27. When households happen to have higher health service utilization, they are vulnerable to poverty or catastrophic health expenditures due to illness28. Therefore, it is important to focus on the principle of equity in health financing, gradually increase government health expenditure, and take measures to ensure that the beneficiaries are low-income people, and also to expand the channels of health financing by increasing external health expenditures, such as social donations, and reducing out-of-pocket health payments, so as to improve the people’s health status and well-being29.

The Kakwani index of social health expenditure was greater than 0 (except in 2021), indicating that social health expenditure was progressive overall and that social health expenditure has increased more than income. With the liberalization of the medical service market, social medicine has developed vigorously, and social investment in health expenditure has gradually increased30. With the popularization of the health concept and economic development, the people’s willingness and ability to pay have become stronger, social medical insurance has gradually been fully covered, and the purchasing power of commercial medical insurance has increased, which has overall contributed to the overall growth of social health expenditure31. Therefore, it is necessary to increase public health financing, further improve the construction of the social medical insurance system, expand the coverage of social medical insurance, control the unreasonable increase of medical expenses, and ensure that all people have access to basic medical and health services. Actively develop social medical services as an important part of the medical services market, and promote the prosperity of the medical services market. Actively play the supplementary protection role of commercial medical insurance, increase the proportion of government and social compensation for out-of-pocket health payments, and increase the utilization rate of medical services for the people.

The health expenditure in the east and central regions was higher than the average in China, and the health expenditure in the east region was more than 50% of the average in China, and the health expenditure in the central region was more than 4% of the average in China, indicating that the health expenditure in the east and central regions was more adequate. The health expenditure in the west region was lower than the average in China, and the deficiency ratio in the west region was more than 46%, indicating that the health expenditure in the west region was more inadequate. The different distribution of health expenditure among the four regions in China has led to significant disparities, which has severely limited the equity of health expenditure32. The east region has a high population density, a higher level of economic development, and relatively adequate health expenditure, with GDP and total health expenditure fluctuating toward higher percentages. The west region has a large geographical area and sparse population, with the overall GDP moving in the direction of higher percentages and the overall total health expenditure moving in the direction of lower percentages, and although the level of socio-economic development has gradually improved, the health expenditure has been relatively inadequate33. The imbalance in economic development in the east and west regions has led to a large disparity in health expenditure in different regions, and people in the west region have difficulty in enjoying the same level of health services as those in the east region, thus increasing the inequity in health expenditure34. Both GDP and total health expenditure in the northeast region have been moving toward lower percentages, indicating that the level of the economy and health expenditure in the northeast region have been facing a lack of growth. Therefore, it is necessary to strengthen the transfer payments from the central government, increase the support for the west region and other regions where health expenditure is relatively inadequate, and build a medical security system with basic medical insurance as the mainstay, medical aid as the backbone, and supplementary medical insurance, commercial medical insurance, charitable donations, and medical mutual aid as a common development, so as to gradually improve the equity of health expenditure on the basis of guaranteeing access to basic medical services for all.

Stepwise regression analysis showed that per capita disposable income and the number of health technicians per 1,000 population had a significant impact on per capita total health expenditure in China. We believe there are two reasons for this. On the one hand, a higher level of per capita disposable income means that individuals have a greater ability to pay for medical expenses, and the people have more funds available for health expenditures, thus bringing about a considerable scale of health expenditures35. The higher the per capita disposable income, the greater the people’s awareness of health care, leading to increased demand for health care services, which in turn leads to an influx of patients into urban and large-scale health care institutions, contributing to the growth of health care expenditure. On the other hand, the increase in the number of health technicians has led to an increase in the supply of health services, thus enriching the health market and meeting the people’s multi-level and diversified demand for health services, with a direct impact on health expenditure36. With the growth of health technicians, the level of medical technology has become more sophisticated, expanding the scope of medical and health services, and the development of new technologies and businesses, which has led to an increase in the health expenditure. Therefore, it is necessary to develop the economy through multiple channels, promote increased income for the people, and raise disposable income levels, thereby improving health services throughout the life cycle. It is also necessary to optimize the deployment of health technicians, improve the accessibility of quality medical resources, and expand the coverage of medical technology services37, which will indirectly reduce the cost of medical services and increase the utilization of health services.

Limitations

Although we used the concentration index and the Kakwani index to evaluate the equity of total health expenditure, government health expenditure, social health expenditure, and out-of-pocket health payments, took the HRDI of the China average as the reference standard to calculate the deficiency and deficiency ratio in the northeast, east, central, and west regions, respectively, and used the stepwise regression analysis to analyze the influencing factors of per capita total health expenditure in China, there are still some limitations in this study. First, the equity research method used in this paper is based on the common methods used in similar studies. Second, this study focused on the regional equity analysis of health expenditure and did not address the analysis in terms of household-level data, which can be specifically analyzed in the next step of the study to analyze the burden of health care expenditure at the household level. Third, we did not conduct a comparative analysis of the equity of total health expenditure in the same type of countries or regions.

Conclusion

In this study, the concentration index and Kakwani index were used to evaluate the equity of total health expenditure, government health expenditure, social health expenditure, and out-of-pocket health payments, the HRDI of China’s average was used as the reference standard to calculate the deficiency and deficiency ratio in the northeast, east, central and west regions respectively, and the stepwise regression analysis was used to analyze the influencing factors of per capita total health expenditure in China. The study found that China’s health financing structure was inadequate, with a low proportion of government health expenditure and a high proportion of out-of-pocket health payments. Total health expenditure, government health expenditure, social health expenditure, and out-of-pocket health payments tended to regions with higher levels of economic development. Compared with social health expenditure and out-of-pocket health payments, the equity of government health expenditure was relatively better. Based on health expenditure in 31 regions, total health expenditure, government health expenditure and out-of-pocket health payments were regressive and social health expenditure was progressive. Both GDP and total health expenditure in the northeast region moved toward lower percentages, and GDP and total health expenditure in the east region moved toward higher percentages. Taking the HRDI of China’s average as the reference standard, the health expenditure was more adequate in the east and central regions and more inadequate in the west region. Per capita disposable income and the number of health technicians per 1,000 population were the main factors influencing per capita total health expenditure in China from 2015 to 2021. We suggest that we should adhere to the primary responsibility of government health expenditure, increase transfer payments to economically backward and remote areas such as the west and northeast regions, and improve the equity of health expenditure. To build a medical security system with basic medical insurance as the mainstay, medical aid as the backbone, and supplementary medical insurance, commercial medical insurance, charitable donations, and medical mutual aid as co-development. It is important to further improve the health care system, build a complete medical service market, and safeguard basic medical and health services for all.

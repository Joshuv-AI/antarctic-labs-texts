Abstract
. In this note, we give a concrete realization of the Jacquet-Langlands correspondence for non-Eichler orders of indefinite quaternion algebras defined over ℚ \mathbb{Q} . To be more precise, we consider a special type of index-two suborder of the Eichler order of level N N in the quaternion algebra with an even discriminant D D .



1.
Introduction and statements of results

Let
\mathcal{B}
be an indefinite quaternion algebra of discriminant
D
over

\mathbb{Q}
. Up to conjugation, there is a unique embedding
\iota
from
\mathcal{B}
into
M(2,\mathbb{R})
. Let
\mathcal{O}
be an order in
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{O}^{1}
be its norm-one group. Then
\Gamma(\mathcal{O}):=\iota(\mathcal{O}^{1})
is a discrete subgroup
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
of first kind and acts on the upper half plane
\mathbb{H}
via the linear fractional transformations. When
\mathcal{B}\neq M(2,\mathbb{Q})
, we let
X(\mathcal{O})
be the compact Riemann surface
\Gamma(\mathcal{O})\backslash\mathbb{H}
. To ease notation, we will also use
\Gamma(\mathcal{O})
to indicate the norm one group
\mathcal{O}^{1}
in
\mathcal{B}
.

For a positive squarefree integer
D
with an even number of
prime divisors and a positive integer
N
relatively prime to
D
,
we let
\mathcal{O}(D,N)
denote the Eichler order of level
N
in the
quaternion algebra
\mathcal{B}_{D}
of discriminant
D
over
\mathbb{Q}

and
\Gamma(D,N)
be its norm-one group. For a positive even integer
k
, let
S_{k}(\Gamma(D,N))
be the space of modular forms of weight
k
on
\Gamma(D,N)
. Also, for a positive integer
M
, let
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(M))
denote the space of modular forms of weight
k
on
\Gamma_{0}(M)
. Then the classical Jacquet-Langlands correspondence for
\mathcal{O}(D,N)
can be stated in the following form.

Theorem A

(
[
6
,
10
]
)
.

Let
D>1
be a positive squarefree integer with an even number of prime divisors and
N
be a positive integer relatively prime to
D
. For a positive integer
n
relatively prime to
DN
, let
T_{n}
denote the Hecke operator on
S_{k}(\Gamma(D,N))
or
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(DN))
. We have

\operatorname{tr}(T_{n}|S_{k}(\Gamma(D,N)))=\operatorname{tr}(T_{n}|S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(DN)^{D\text{-new}})).

Here for a positive integer
L
and a positive divisor
M
of
L
, we let

S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(L))^{M\text{-new}}:=\bigoplus_{M^{\prime}|L,M|M^{\prime}}\{g(m\tau):g\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(M^{\prime}))^{\text{new}},m|(L/M^{\prime})\},

where
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(M))^{\text{new}}
denotes the newform subspace of
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(M))
.

A natural question to ask is whether analogous correspondences exist for non-Eichler orders of
\mathcal{B}_{D}
.
In view of automorphic representations, such correspondences for non-Eichler orders exist. However, Hecke operators in the case of non-Eichler orders may not have a clean description as in the case of Eichler orders. Also,
it is hard to match spaces of modular forms on a non-Eichler order to spaces of classical modular forms on some congruence subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
. As far as we know, there are few explicit realizations of Jacquet-Langlands correspondence for non-Eichler orders in an indefinite quaternion algebra known in literature (see
[
4
,
5
,
15
, et al.]
for local consideration, and
[
8
,
12
,
14
,
16
, et al.]
for definite quaternion algebra cases). The purpose of this paper is to provide such an example.

We first describe the non-Eichler orders we are interested in. Throughout the paper, we assume that
D
is even. In this case,
the function
w:\alpha\to\frac{1}{2}v_{2}(\operatorname{nrd}(\alpha))
defines a discrete
valuation on the division algebra
\mathcal{B}_{D}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}_{2}
, where
v_{2}
is the
2
-adic valuation and
\operatorname{nrd}(\alpha)
denotes the reduced norm of
\alpha
. Then the
maximal
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-order
R=\mathcal{O}(D,N)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is equal to the
valuation ring of
\mathcal{B}_{D}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}_{2}
with respect to
w
. Let
P
be
the unique maximal (two-sided) ideal of
R
. We have
R/P\simeq\mathbb{F}_{4}

(see
[
16
, Theorem 13.1.6]
). Then
\mathbb{Z}_{2}+P
is a suborder
of index
2
of
R
.
It follows that, by the local-global correspondence for orders in
\mathcal{B}_{D}
, the Eichler order
\mathcal{O}(D,N)
has a suborder
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}(D,N)
of index
2
such
that
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}(D,N)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}=\mathcal{O}(D,N)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
for odd prime
p

and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}(D,N)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{2}
has index
2
in
\mathcal{O}(D,N)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. The following is an explicit example of such an order.

Example 1
.

Let
D=2p_{1}\ldots p_{r}
, where
p_{1},\ldots,p_{r}
are all
congruent to
3
modulo
4
and
r
is odd. Then
\mathcal{O}(D,1)
and

\mathcal{O}^{\prime}(D,1)
can be realized as

\mathcal{O}(D,1)=\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}i+\mathbb{Z}j+\mathbb{Z}\frac{1+i+j+ij}{2},\qquad\mathcal{O}^{\prime}(D,1)=\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}i+\mathbb{Z}j+\mathbb{Z}ij,

where
i^{2}=-1
and
j^{2}=p_{1}\ldots p_{r}
. In the case of
D=6
, the group
\Gamma(\mathcal{O}(6,1))/\{\pm 1\}
is generated by the elements

\gamma_{2}=i,\quad\gamma_{3}=(1-3i+j-k)/2,\quad\gamma_{4}=(1-3i-j-k)/2,\quad\gamma_{1}=(\gamma_{2}\gamma_{3}\gamma_{4})^{-1}=2i+j,

where
\gamma_{1}^{2}=\gamma_{2}^{2}=\gamma_{3}^{3}=\gamma_{4}^{3}=-1
. The group
\Gamma(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}(6,1))/\{\pm 1\}
is generated by

\gamma_{3}^{m}\gamma_{1}\gamma_{3}^{-m}\mbox{and}\quad\gamma_{3}^{m}\gamma_{2}\gamma_{3}^{-m},\quad m=0,1,2.

We let
\Gamma(D,N)
and
\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N)
denote the groups of norm-one
elements in
\mathcal{O}(D,N)
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}(D,N)
, and
X(D,N)
and
X^{\prime}(D,N)

denote the Shimura curves associated to

\mathcal{O}(D,N)
and
O^{\prime}(D,N)
, respectively. Also, we let
S_{k}(\Gamma(D,N))

and
S_{k}(\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N))
be the spaces of modular forms of weight

k
on
\Gamma(D,N)
and
\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N)
, respectively.
On the space
S_{k}(\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N))
, we can define Hecke operators in the same way as
S_{k}(\Gamma(D,N))
.
Namely, for a positive integer
n
relatively prime to
DN
, let
M(n)
be the set of elements of reduced norm
n
in
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}(D,N)
(by Lemma
6
(4),
M(n)
is nonempty). Then the Hecke operator
T_{n}
on
S_{k}(\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N))
can be defined by

T_{n}:f\longmapsto n^{k/2-1}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N)\backslash M(n)}f\big|_{k}\gamma.

We have the following Jacquet-Langlands correspondence for the non-Eichler order

\mathcal{O}^{\prime}(D,N)
.

Theorem 1
.

With
D
and
N
given as above, we have, for all
positive integer
n
such that
(n,DN)=1
and all positive even
integers
k
,

\operatorname{tr}(T_{n}\big|S_{k}(\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N)))=\operatorname{tr}(T_{n}\big|S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(DN))^{D\text{-new}})+2\operatorname{tr}(T_{n}\big|S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(2DN))^{2D\text{-new}}).

Theorem
1
can be refined as follows.
The group
\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N)
is a
normal subgroup of index
3
of
\Gamma(D,N)
(see Lemma
6
). Thus,
\Gamma(D,N)
acts on the space

S_{k}(\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N))
.
For a character
\chi
of the quotient group

\Gamma(D,N)/\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N)
, we let

S_{k}(\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N),\chi):=\{f\in S_{k}(\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N)):f\big|_{k}\alpha=\chi(\alpha)f\text{ for all }\alpha\in\Gamma(D,N)\}.

Thus, we have a direct sum decomposition

S_{k}(\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N))=\bigoplus_{\chi}S_{k}(\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N),\chi).

It is easy to see that this is an orthogonal decomposition
with respect to the Petersson inner product and each summand is invariant under Hecke operators.
When
\chi
is the trivial character
\chi_{0}
,

S_{k}(\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N),\chi_{0})
is the same as
S_{k}(\Gamma(D,N))
.
In view of the classical Jacquet-Langlands correspondence for Eichler orders, it is
natural to guess that

(1)

\operatorname{tr}(T_{n}|S_{k}(\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N),\chi))=\operatorname{tr}(T_{n}|S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(2DN))^{2D\text{-new}}

for a nontrivial character
\chi
.
The next theorem shows that this is indeed the case.

Theorem 2
.

Let
D
and
N
be as above. Let
\chi
be a nontrivial
character of the group
\Gamma(D,N)/\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N)
. Then (
1
) holds for all positive integers
n
with

(n,DN)=1
. Equivalently, for all
positive even integers
k
, the two spaces

S_{k}(\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N),\chi)
and
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(2DN))^{2D\text{-new}}

are isomorphic as Hecke modules.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Optimal embeddings and CM-points

Since the trace formulas involve CM-points, we briefly review the
notion of CM-points and formulas for the number of CM-points on a
modular curve or a Shimura curve in this section.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be an indefinite quaternion algebra of discriminant
D
over

\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathcal{O}
be an order in
\mathcal{B}
. We fix an embedding

\iota
of
\mathcal{B}
into
M(2,\mathbb{R})
. In order for a quadratic number
field
K
to be embeddable into
\mathcal{B}
, the necessary and
sufficient condition is
\left(\frac{K}{p}\right)\neq 1
for any prime divisor
p
of

D
, where
\left(\frac{K}{p}\right)
is the Kronecker symbol. Now suppose that
K
can
be embedded into
\mathcal{B}
, say,
\sigma:K\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}
is an
embedding. Then
\sigma(K)\cap\mathcal{O}=\sigma(R)
for some quadratic order

R
in
K
. Let
d
be the discriminant of
R
. Then we say
\sigma

is an
optimal embedding
of discriminant
d
into
\mathcal{O}
. We let

\mathrm{Emb}(d;\mathcal{O})
denote the set of all such embeddings. Note that if

\sigma\in\mathrm{Emb}(d;\mathcal{O})
, then
\gamma\sigma\gamma^{-1}
also
belongs to
\mathrm{Emb}(d;\mathcal{O})
for
\gamma\in\mathcal{O}^{1}
, where
\mathcal{O}^{1}

denotes the group of norm-one elements in
\mathcal{O}
.

Now if
K
is an imaginary quadratic number field, then

\iota(\sigma(K))
has a common fixed point
\tau_{\sigma}
on

\mathbb{H}
. This point is called a
CM-point
of discriminant
d
.
It is clear that for
\gamma\in\mathcal{O}^{1}
, we have

\tau_{\gamma\sigma\gamma^{-1}}=\iota(\gamma)\tau_{\sigma}
. Thus, each
conjugacy class in
\mathrm{Emb}(d;\mathcal{O})
by
\mathcal{O}^{1}
determines a unique point on

X(\mathcal{O})
.

Notation 2
.

For a modular curve or a Shimura curve
X
and a
negative discriminant
d
, we let
\mathrm{CM}(d;X)
denote the set of
CM-points of discriminant
d
on
X
.

Note, however, that the correspondence between
\mathrm{Emb}(d;\mathcal{O})/\mathcal{O}^{1}
and

\mathrm{CM}(d;X(\mathcal{O}))
is not one-to-one. This is due to the fact that
if
\sigma\in\mathrm{Emb}(d;\mathcal{O})
, then
\overline{\sigma}:K\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}
defined by

\overline{\sigma}(a):=\sigma(\overline{a})
is also an optimal embedding
of discriminant
d
with the same fixed point

\tau_{\overline{\sigma}}=\tau_{\sigma}
.
To get a one-to-one correspondence, we consider the
(2,1)
-entry of

\iota(\sigma(\sqrt{d}))
. A simple computation shows that the

(2,1)
-entries of
\iota(\sigma(\sqrt{d}))
are either all positive or
all negative for
\sigma
in a given conjugacy class of optimal
embeddings. We say
\sigma
is
positive
(respectively,

negative
) and write
\sigma>0
(respectively,
\sigma<0
) if
the
(2,1)
-entry of
\iota(\sigma(\sqrt{d}))
is positive
(respectively, negative). (In some literature, a positive embedding is
called normalized instead.) We let

\mathrm{Emb}^{+}(d;\mathcal{O}):=\{\sigma\in\mathrm{Emb}(d;\mathcal{O}):\sigma>0\}
. Then the
correspondence between
\mathrm{Emb}^{+}(d;\mathcal{O})/\mathcal{O}^{1}
and
\mathrm{CM}(d;X(\mathcal{O}))
is
one-to-one.

The determination of the cardinality of
\mathrm{CM}(d;X(\mathcal{O}))
is usually
done locally. For a prime
p
, we let
\mathcal{O}_{p}:=\mathcal{O}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and
similarly let
\mathrm{Emb}(d;\mathcal{O}_{p})
denote the set of optimal embeddings of
discriminant
d
into
\mathcal{O}_{p}
.

Lemma 3

(
[
16
, Theorem 30.7.3]
)
.

With the notations given as above, let

e(d;\mathcal{O}_{p})=|\mathrm{Emb}(d;\mathcal{O}_{p})/\mathcal{O}_{p}^{\times}|
. Then

|\mathrm{CM}(d;X(\mathcal{O}))|=h(d)\prod_{p}e(d;\mathcal{O}_{p}),

where
h(d)
is the class number of the order of discriminant
d
in
K
.

Define the Eichler symbol
\left\{\frac{d}{p}\right\}
by

\left\{\frac{d}{p}\right\}=\begin{cases}\left(\frac{d_{0}}{p}\right),&\text{if }p\nmid f,\\
1,&\text{if }p|f,\end{cases}

where
\left(\frac{d_{0}}{p}\right)
is the Kronecker symbol. We now record formulas for
e(d;\mathcal{O}_{p})
relevant to our discussion.

Lemma 4

(
[
3
, Proposition 5, Chapter II]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{O}(D,N)
be an Eichler order of level
N
in the indefinite
quaternion algebra of discriminant
D
over
\mathbb{Q}
(
D=1
allowed).
Given a negative discriminant
d
, we write
d
as

d=f^{2}d_{0}
, where
d_{0}
is a fundamental discriminant and
f
is a
positive integer.

(1)

If
p|D
, then
e(d;\mathcal{O}(D,N)_{p})=1-\left\{\frac{d}{p}\right\}
.

(2)

If
p\|N
, then
e(d;\mathcal{O}(D,N)_{p})=1+\left\{\frac{d}{p}\right\}
.

The case
p^{2}|N
is more complicated. For our purpose, we only need
the formula for the case
p=2
and
4\|N
.

Lemma 5

(
[
13
, Theorem 2]
)
.

Let
M
be an odd positive integer. Given a negative
discriminant
d
, write
d
as
d=f^{2}d_{0}
, where
d_{0}
is a
fundamental discriminant and
f
is a positive integer. Then

e(d;\mathcal{O}(1,4M)_{2})=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }4|d_{0},2\nmid f,\\
3,&\text{if }4|d_{0},2|f,\\
1+\left(\frac{d_{0}}{2}\right),&\text{if }d_{0}\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ 4,2\nmid f,\\
3+\left(\frac{d_{0}}{2}\right),&\text{if }d_{0}\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ 4,2\|f,\\
3,&\text{if }d_{0}\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ 4,4|f.\end{cases}

2.2.
The Shimura curve
X^{\prime}(D,N)

In this section, we collect some properties of the group

\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N)
and the Shimura curve
X^{\prime}(D,N)
that will be
needed later on.

Lemma 6
.

(1)

An element
\alpha
of
\mathcal{O}(D,N)
is contained in
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}(D,N)

if and only if the reduced trace
\operatorname{trd}(\alpha)
is even. Also, an element
\alpha

of
\Gamma(D,N)
is contained in
\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N)
if and only if

\operatorname{nrd}(\alpha-1)
is even.

(2)

We have
\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N)\lhd\Gamma(D,N)
and

[\Gamma(D,N):\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N)]=3
.

(3)

Let
\mathcal{O}(D,N)^{\times}
be the unit group of
\mathcal{O}(D,N)
. Then
\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N)\lhd\mathcal{O}(D,N)^{\times}
and
\mathcal{O}(D,N)^{\times}/\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N)
is cyclic of order
6
.

(4)

The reduced norm map
\operatorname{nrd}:\mathcal{O}^{\prime}(D,N)\to\mathbb{Z}
is surjective.

(5)

Let
p
be a prime not dividing
DN
. Suppose that
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
are two elements of reduced norm
p
in
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}(D,N)
. Then there are elements
\alpha
and
\beta
in
\Gamma(D,N)
such that
\gamma_{1}=\alpha\gamma_{2}\beta
and
\alpha\beta\in\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N)
.

Proof.

Recall that the unique division quaternion algebra over

\mathbb{Q}_{2}
can be realized as
\left(\frac{-1,-1}{\mathbb{Q}_{2}}\right)
. The unique maximal

\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-order of
\left(\frac{-1,-1}{\mathbb{Q}_{2}}\right)
is

R:=\mathbb{Z}_{2}+\mathbb{Z}_{2}i+\mathbb{Z}_{2}j+\mathbb{Z}_{2}(1+i+j+ij)/2
. Its maximal ideal is

P=(i+j)=\{a_{0}+a_{1}i+a_{2}j+a_{3}ij:a_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2},a_{0}+\cdots+a_{3}\text{ is
even}\}
and the suborder
\mathbb{Z}_{2}+P
of index
2
in the maximal
order is
R^{\prime}:=\mathbb{Z}_{2}+\mathbb{Z}_{2}i+\mathbb{Z}_{2}j+\mathbb{Z}_{2}ij
. Therefore, we
have
\mathcal{B}_{D}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}_{2}\simeq\left(\frac{-1,-1}{\mathbb{Q}_{2}}\right)
, and
the images of
\mathcal{O}(D,N)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}(D,N)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{2}

under the isomorphism are
R
and
R^{\prime}
, respectively. From this, we
immediately see that an element
\alpha
of
\mathcal{O}(D,N)
is contained
in
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}(D,N)
if and only if
\operatorname{trd}(\alpha)
is even.

Now suppose
\alpha\in\Gamma(D,N)
. We have

\operatorname{nrd}(\alpha-1)=\operatorname{nrd}(\alpha)-\operatorname{trd}(\alpha)+1=2-\operatorname{trd}(\alpha)
. By the
characterization of elements of
\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N)
above,
we see that
\alpha
is in
\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N)
if and only if

\operatorname{nrd}(\alpha-1)
is even.

We now prove Part (2). We regard
\mathcal{O}(D,N)
as a subring of
R
. Observe that
R/P\simeq\mathbb{F}_{4}
and the only elements
a
of
\mathbb{F}_{4}
such that
\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{4}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}(a)=0
are those elements in
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Consequently, by Part (1), an element
\gamma
of
\mathcal{O}(D,N)
is in
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}(D,N)
if and only if
\gamma\equiv 0,1\ \mathrm{mod}\ P
. In particular,
an element
\gamma
of
\Gamma(D,N)
is in
\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N)
if and only if

\gamma\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ P
. In other words,
\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N)
is the kernel
of the reduction homomorphism

\Gamma(D,N)\longrightarrow(R/P)^{\times}\quad\mbox{defined
by}\quad\alpha\mapsto\alpha\ \mathrm{mod}\ P.

Thus,
\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N)\lhd\Gamma(D,N)

and the index of
\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N)
in
\Gamma(D,N)
is either
1
or

3
. In view of Part (1), we only need to show that
\Gamma(D,N)

has an element of odd trace.

Recall that, as a consequence of the strong approximation
for Eichler orders in an indefinite quaternion algebra over
\mathbb{Q}
,
the reduction map
\Gamma(D,N)\mapsto(\mathcal{O}(D,N)/2\mathcal{O}(D,N))^{1}
is
surjective (see Theorem 28.2.11 of
[
16
]
), where

(\mathcal{O}(D,N)/2\mathcal{O}(D,N))^{1}
denotes the group of elements

\alpha+2\mathcal{O}(D,N)
such that
\operatorname{nrd}(\alpha)\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ 2
. Now it is
easy to see that the embedding
(\mathcal{O}(D,N)/2\mathcal{O}(D,N))^{1}\hookrightarrow(R/2R)^{1}
is actually an isomorphism. Since
R
has an element

(1+i+j+ij)/2
of norm
1
and trace
1
, we see that
\Gamma(D,N)

has an element of odd trace. This completes the proof of the lemma.

To prove Part (3), we first note that, by Part (2),
[\mathcal{O}(D,N)^{\times}:\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N)]=[\mathcal{O}(D,N)^{\times}:\Gamma(D,N)][\Gamma(D,N):\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N)]=6
. Moreover, using the characterization of elements of
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}(D,N)
given in Part (1), we easily see that
\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N)
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}(D,N)^{\times}
are both normal subgroups of
\mathcal{O}(D,N)^{\times}
. Now the proof of Part (2) can also be used to show that
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}(D,N)^{\times}
is a subgroup of
\mathcal{O}(D,N)^{\times}
of index
3
.
Thus,
\mathcal{O}(D,N)^{\times}/\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N)
is a group of order
6
having
and a normal subgroup
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}(D,N)/\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N)
of index
3
. Therefore,
\mathcal{O}(D,N)^{\times}/\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N)
is cyclic of order
6
.

We next prove Part (4).
By the strong approximation theorem for the Eichler order
\mathcal{O}(D,N)
, the reduced norm map
\operatorname{nrd}:\mathcal{O}(D,N)\to\mathbb{Z}
is surjective for
\mathcal{O}(D,N)
.
For an integer
n
, let
\gamma
be an element of reduced norm
n
in
\mathcal{O}(D,N)
. Let
\alpha
be an element of
\Gamma(D,N)
not in
\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N)
. Then
1
,
\alpha
, and
\overline{\alpha}
form a complete set of coset representatives of
\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N)
in
\Gamma(D,N)
. By Part (1),
\operatorname{trd}(\alpha)
is odd.
Thus,
1+\alpha+\overline{\alpha}

. Then the standard properties of the Petersson inner product imply that

\begin{split}\langle f\big|_{k}\gamma_{1},g\rangle&=\langle f\big|_{k}\alpha\gamma_{2}\beta,g\rangle=\chi(\alpha)\langle f\big|_{k}\gamma_{2}\beta,g\rangle=\chi(\alpha)\langle f\big|_{k}\gamma_{2},g\big|_{k}\beta^{-1}\rangle\\
&=\chi(\alpha)\chi(\beta)\langle f\big|_{k}\gamma_{2},g\rangle=\langle f\big|_{k}\gamma_{2},g\rangle.\end{split}

This proves (
25
). Consequently,
we have

\langle T_{p}f,g\rangle=(p+1)\langle f\big|_{k}\gamma,g\rangle,\qquad\langle f,T_{p}g\rangle=(p+1)\langle f,g\big|_{k}\gamma\rangle

for any element
\gamma
of reduced norm
p
in
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}(D,N)
. Here
p+1=|\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N)\backslash M(p)|
.
Now we have
\langle f\big|_{k}\gamma,g\rangle=\langle f,g\big|_{k}\overline{\gamma}\rangle
, where
\overline{\gamma}\gamma=nI
.
Since
\gamma
and
\overline{\gamma}
are both elements of reduced norm
p
in
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}(D,N)
, by (
25
), we have
\langle f,g\big|_{k}\gamma\rangle=\langle f,g\big|_{k}\overline{\gamma}\rangle
.
It follows that
T_{p}
is self-adjoint on
S_{k}(\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N),\chi)
and the proof of the lemma is complete.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
2
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Let
\chi
be a nontrivial character of
\Gamma(D,N)/\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N)
. By Lemma
13
, Hecke operators
T_{n}
,
(n,DN)=1
, are commuting self-adjoint linear operators on
S_{k}(\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N),\chi)
and
S_{k}(\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N),\overline{\chi})
. Thus, the two spaces of modular forms have bases consisting of simultaneous eigenforms for all Hecke operators.
Moreover, by Lemma
12
, if
f
is a Hecke eigenform in
S_{k}(\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N),\chi)
, then
f^{c}
is a Hecke eigenform in
S_{k}(\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N),\overline{\chi})
and the eigenvalues are related by
T_{n}f^{c}=\overline{\lambda_{n}(f)}f^{c}
, where
\lambda_{n}(f)
is the eigenvalue of
T_{n}
corresponding to
f
.
Now by Lemma
13
, all eigenvalues
\lambda_{n}(f)
are real. Therefore, the eigenvalue of
T_{n}
corresponding to
f^{c}
is the same as that corresponding to
f
. It follows that

\operatorname{tr}(T_{n}|S_{k}(\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N),\chi))=\operatorname{tr}(T_{n}|S_{k}(\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N),\overline{\chi}).

Finally, by the classical Jacquet-Langlands correspondence for Eichler orders and Theorem
1
, we have

\operatorname{tr}(T_{n}|S_{k}(\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N)),\chi)+\operatorname{tr}(T_{n}|S_{k}(\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N),\overline{\chi})=2\operatorname{tr}(T_{n}|S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(2DN))^{2D\text{-new}}).

From this, we conclude that

\operatorname{tr}(T_{n}|S_{k}(\Gamma^{\prime}(D,N),\chi))=\operatorname{tr}(T_{n}|S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(2DN))^{2D\text{-new}}).

This completes the proof of Theorem
2
.
∎

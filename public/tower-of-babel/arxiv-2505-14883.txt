𝔏 {{\mathfrak{L}}} -resolvents of symmetric linear relations in Pontryagin spaces
Volodymyr Derkach

Abstract
. Let A A be a closed symmetric operator with the deficiency index ( p , p ) (p,p) , p < ∞ p<\infty , acting in a Hilbert space ℌ {\mathfrak{H}} and let 𝔏 {\mathfrak{L}} be a subspace of ℌ {\mathfrak{H}} . The set of 𝔏 {\mathfrak{L}} -resolvents of a densely defined symmetric operator in a Hilbert space with a proper gauge 𝔏 ( ⊂ ℌ ) {\mathfrak{L}}(\subset{\mathfrak{H}}) was described by Kreĭn and Saakyan. The Kreĭn–Saakyan theory of 𝔏 {\mathfrak{L}} -resolvent matrices was extended by Shmul’yan and Tsekanovskii to the case of improper gauge 𝔏 ( ⊄ ℌ ) {\mathfrak{L}}(\not\subset{\mathfrak{H}}) and by Langer and Textorius to the case of symmetric linear relations in Hilbert spaces. In the present paper we find connections between the theory of boundary triples and the Kreĭn–Saakyan theory of 𝔏 {\mathfrak{L}} -resolvent matrices for symmetric linear relations with improper gauges in Pontryagin spaces. We extend the known formula for the 𝔏 {\mathfrak{L}} -resolvent matrix in terms of boundary operators to this class of relations. The results are applied to the minimal linear relation generated by a canonical system.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Linear relations in Pontryagin spaces

Let us recall some definitions from
[
8
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
1
]
,
[
4
]
.
An indefinite inner product space

({\mathfrak{H}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{{\mathfrak{H}}})
, see
[
8
]
, is called a Kreĭn space
if it can be decomposed into a direct orthogonal sum

(2.1)

{\mathfrak{H}}={\mathfrak{H}}_{+}[+]{\mathfrak{H}}_{-}

of two subspaces
{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}
and
{\mathfrak{H}}_{-}
such that
({\mathfrak{H}}_{\pm},\pm[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{H}})

are Hilbert spaces.
The operator
J=P_{+}-P_{-}
, where
P_{\pm}
are orthogonal projections in
{\mathfrak{H}}
onto
{\mathfrak{H}}_{\pm}
, is called
the fundamental symmetry
of
{\mathfrak{H}}
. It induces a Hilbert inner product in
{\mathfrak{H}}
by the formula

(2.2)

(f,g)_{\mathfrak{H}}^{2}=[Jf,g]_{{\mathcal{K}}},\quad f,g\in{\mathfrak{H}}.

and turns
{\mathfrak{H}}
into a Hilbert space

({\mathfrak{H}},(\cdot,\cdot)_{{\mathfrak{H}}})
.
A Kreĭn space
({\mathfrak{H}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{{\mathfrak{H}}})
with a finite negative index
\kappa_{-}({\mathfrak{H}}):=\dim{\mathfrak{H}}_{-}=\kappa
is called a
Pontryagin space
with negative
index
\kappa
(or shortly,
\pi_{\kappa}
-space).

A linear relation
T
in a Pontryagin space

({\mathfrak{H}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{{\mathfrak{H}}})

is a linear subspace of
{\mathfrak{H}}\times{\mathfrak{H}}
.
The
domain
, the
range
, the
kernel
, and the
multivalued part
of a linear relation
T
are defined as follows:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\dom{T}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{f\colon\begin{bmatrix}f\\
g\end{bmatrix}\in T\right\},

\displaystyle\ran{T}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{g\colon\begin{bmatrix}f\\
g\end{bmatrix}\in T\right\},

(2.4)

\displaystyle\ker{T}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{f\colon\begin{bmatrix}f\\
0\end{bmatrix}\in T\right\},

\displaystyle\mul{T}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{g\colon\begin{bmatrix}0\\
g\end{bmatrix}\in T\right\}.

The
adjoint
linear relation
T^{[*]}
is defined by

(2.5)

T^{[*]}\coloneqq\left\{\begin{bmatrix}u\\
f\end{bmatrix}\in{\mathfrak{H}}\times{\mathfrak{H}}\colon\langle f,v\rangle_{{\mathfrak{H}}}=\langle u,g\rangle_{{\mathfrak{H}}}\ \text{for any}\ \begin{bmatrix}v\\
g\end{bmatrix}\in T\right\}.

A linear relation
T
in
{\mathfrak{H}}
is called
closed
if
T
is closed as a subspace of
{\mathfrak{H}}\times{\mathfrak{H}}
.
The set of all closed linear operators (relations) is denoted by
\mathcal{C}({\mathfrak{H}})
(
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}({\mathfrak{H}})
).
Identifying a linear operator
T\in\mathcal{C}({\mathfrak{H}})
with its graph one can consider
\mathcal{C}({\mathfrak{H}})
as a part of
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}({\mathfrak{H}})
.

Let
T
be a closed linear relation,
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, then

(2.6)

T-\lambda I\coloneqq\left\{\begin{bmatrix}f\\
g-\lambda f\end{bmatrix}\colon\begin{bmatrix}f\\
g\end{bmatrix}\in T\right\}.

A point
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\ker{\left(T-\lambda I\right)}=\{0\}
and
\ran{\left(T-\lambda I\right)}={\mathfrak{H}}
is called a
regular point
of the linear relation
T
. Let
\rho(T)
be the set of regular points.
The
point spectrum

\sigma_{p}(T)
of the linear relation
T

is defined by

(2.7)

\sigma_{p}(T)\coloneqq\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\colon\ker(T-\lambda I)\neq\{0\}\},

A linear relation
A
is called
symmetric
in a Pontryagin space

({\mathfrak{H}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{{\mathfrak{H}}})
if
A\subseteq A^{[*]}
.
A point
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
is called a
point of regular type
(and is written as
\lambda\in\widehat{\rho}(A)
) for a closed symmetric linear relation
A
, if
\lambda\notin\sigma_{p}(A)
and the subspace
\ran(A-\lambda I)
is closed in
H
.
For
\lambda\in\widehat{\rho}(A)
let us set

{\mathfrak{N}}_{\lambda}(A^{[*]})\coloneqq\ker(A^{[*]}-\lambda I)
and

(2.8)

\widehat{{\mathfrak{N}}}_{\lambda}(A^{[*]})\coloneqq\left\{\widehat{f}_{\lambda}=\begin{bmatrix}f_{\lambda}\\
\lambda f_{\lambda}\end{bmatrix}\colon f_{\lambda}\in{\mathfrak{N}}_{\lambda}(A^{[*]})\right\}.

As is known, see
[
19
, Theorem 6.1]
, the numbers
\dim{{\mathfrak{N}}}_{\lambda}
take a constant value
n_{+}(A)
for all
\lambda\in\widehat{\rho}(A)\cap{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
, and
n_{-}(A)
for all
\lambda\in\widehat{\rho}(A)\cap{\mathbb{C}}_{-}
, where

(2.9)

{\mathbb{C}}_{\pm}=\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}:\,\pm\text{Im }\lambda>0\}.

The numbers
n_{\pm}(A)
are called the
defect numbers
of
A
.

2.2.
{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}
-families

Recall that a Hermitian kernel
{\mathsf{K}}_{\omega}(\lambda):\Omega\times\Omega\to{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times m}
is said to
have
\kappa

negative squares

if for every positive integer
n
and every choice of
\omega_{j}\in\Omega

and
u_{j}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}

(j=1,\dots,n)
the matrix

\left[u_{k}^{*}\mathsf{K}_{\omega_{j}}(\omega_{k})u_{j}\right]_{j,k=1}^{n}

has at most
\kappa
negative eigenvalues and for some choice of

\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n}\in\Omega
and
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
exactly

\kappa
negative
eigenvalues. In this case we write

\mbox{sq}_{-}{\mathsf{K}}=\kappa.

For a mvf
f(\lambda)

we shall use the symbol

{\mathfrak{h}}_{f}
for the domain of holomorphy of
f
in
{\mathbb{C}}

and set
f^{\#}(\lambda)=f(\overline{\lambda})^{*}
, for

\overline{\lambda}\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{f}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
p\times p
mvf
Q(\lambda)
meromorphic on

{\mathbb{C}}_{+}\cup{\mathbb{C}}_{-}
is said to belong to the class
{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}^{p\times p}
if:

(a)

the kernel

{\mathsf{N}}^{Q}_{\omega}(\lambda):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\displaystyle Q(\lambda)-Q(\omega)^{*}}{\displaystyle\lambda-\overline{\omega}}&\quad\textrm{for }\lambda,\,\omega\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{Q},\ \lambda\neq\overline{\omega}\\
Q^{\prime}(\lambda)&\quad\textrm{for }\lambda,\,\omega\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{Q},\ \lambda=\overline{\omega}\end{array}\right.

has
\kappa
negative squares on
{\mathfrak{h}}_{Q}
;

(b)

Q^{\#}(\lambda)=Q(\lambda)
for all
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{Q}
.

The class
{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}^{p\times p}
was introduced by
M. G. Kreĭn and H. Langer in
[
26
]
. In the case
\kappa=0
it coincides with the class
{\mathcal{R}}^{p\times p}:={\mathcal{N}}_{0}^{p\times p}
of mvf’s meromorphic on

{\mathbb{C}}_{+}\cup{\mathbb{C}}_{-}
which satisfy the assumption (b) in Definition
2.1
and have non-negative imaginary part
\text{Im }Q(\lambda)
in
{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
, see
[
21
]
.
A mvf
M\in{\mathcal{R}}^{p\times p}
is said to belong to the class
{\mathcal{R}}_{u}^{p\times p}

if
\det\operatorname{Im}{M({i})}\neq 0
.

Definition 2.2
.

A family
\tau(\lambda)=\ran\begin{bmatrix}\varphi(\lambda)\\
\psi(\lambda)\end{bmatrix}
where
\varphi
and
\psi
are
p\times p
mvf’s holomorphic on

{\mathbb{C}}_{+}\cup{\mathbb{C}}_{-}
will be called an
{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}^{p\times p}
-family if:

(i)

the kernel

{\mathsf{N}}_{\omega}^{\varphi\psi}(\lambda):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\displaystyle\varphi(\omega)^{*}\psi(\lambda)-\psi(\omega)^{*}\varphi(\lambda)}{\displaystyle\lambda-\overline{\omega}}&\quad\textrm{for }\lambda,\,\omega\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}\cup{\mathbb{C}}_{-},\ \lambda\neq\overline{\omega}\\
\varphi^{\#}(\lambda)\psi(\lambda)-\psi(\lambda)^{\#}\varphi(\lambda)&\quad\textrm{for }\lambda,\,\omega\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}\cup{\mathbb{C}}_{-},\ \lambda=\overline{\omega}\end{array}\right.

has
\kappa
negative squares on
{\mathbb{C}}_{+}\cup{\mathbb{C}}_{-}
;

(ii)

\varphi^{\#}(\lambda)\psi(\lambda)-\psi^{\#}(\lambda)\varphi(\lambda)
for all
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}\cup{\mathbb{C}}_{-}
;

(iii)

\ker\varphi(\lambda)\cap\ker\psi(\lambda)=\{0\}

for all
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}\cup{\mathbb{C}}_{-}
.

The set of
{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}^{p\times p}
-families is denoted by
\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}^{p\times p}
.

Two

-families

and

\ran\text{\rm col}\{\varphi_{2}(\lambda),\psi_{2}(\lambda)\}

\varphi_{2}(\lambda)=\varphi_{1}(\lambda)\chi(\lambda)

and
\psi_{2}(\lambda)=\psi_{1}(\lambda)\chi(\lambda)

for some mvf
\chi(\lambda)
,
which is holomorphic and invertible on
{\mathbb{C}}_{+}\cup{\mathbb{C}}_{-}
.

Every
{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}^{p\times p}
-family
\tau=\ran\text{\rm col}\{\varphi(\lambda),\psi(\lambda)\}

can be treated as a family of linear relations in
{\mathbb{C}}^{p}
.
In the case when
\det\varphi(\lambda)\not\equiv 0

it has at most
\kappa
zeros and
the
{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}^{p\times p}
-family
\tau

{\mathfrak{h}}_{\varphi^{-1}}
with
the graph of
{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}^{p\times p}
-function

.
Since every mvf from
{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}^{p\times p}
admits such a representation
(see
[
5
]
),
the set
{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}^{p\times p}
can be identified with a subset of

\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}^{p\times p}

.

A pair
\begin{bmatrix}C(\lambda)&D(\lambda)\end{bmatrix}

Definition 2.7

3.
Rigged Pontryagin spaces and generalized resolvents

In this section we present the construction of the rigging

of a Pontryagin space associated with a symmetric linear relation
A
and look at the properties of
extended generalized resolvents of
A
and extended boundary triples.

3.1.
Rigged Pontryagin spaces.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A
is a closed symmetric linear relation with equal
defect numbers
n_{\pm}(A)=p<\infty
in a
\pi_{\kappa}
-space

({\mathfrak{H}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{{\mathfrak{H}}})
. Then there exists a pair
{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}
and
{\mathfrak{H}}_{-}
of Hilbert spaces with the duality
\langle\mathfrak{f},h\rangle_{-,+}
for
\mathfrak{f}\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{-}
and
h\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}
such that

(i)

{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}=\dom A^{[*]}(\subset{\mathfrak{H}})
;

(ii)

{\mathfrak{H}}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}_{-}
and for all
f\in{\mathfrak{H}}
and
h\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}
we have

\langle{f},h\rangle_{-,+}=[f,h]_{\mathfrak{H}}
.

Moreover, the norm in
{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}
can be defined by

(3.1)

\|f\|^{2}_{{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}}=\|f\|_{\mathfrak{H}}^{2}+\|P_{{\mathfrak{H}}_{0}}Jf^{\prime}\|_{\mathfrak{H}}^{2}\quad\mbox{for all $\begin{bmatrix}f\\
f^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}\in A^{[*]}$}

where
J
is a fundamental symmetry in
({\mathfrak{H}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{{\mathfrak{H}}})
and

P_{{\mathfrak{H}}_{0}}
is the orthogonal projection onto
{\mathfrak{H}}_{0}:=\overline{\dom A}

in the Hilbert space
({\mathfrak{H}},[J\cdot,\cdot]_{{\mathfrak{H}}})
.

Proof.

Let us set
S:=JA
for the symmetric linear relation in the Hilbert space
({\mathfrak{H}},(\cdot,\cdot)_{{\mathfrak{H}}})
with the inner product

(\cdot,\cdot)_{{\mathfrak{H}}})=({\mathfrak{H}},[J\cdot,\cdot]_{{\mathfrak{H}}})
.
The proof is splited into 2 steps.

Step 1: Construction of a rigging for the linear relation
S
.

If
\mul S\neq\{0\}
, then the linear relation
S
admits the representation

(3.2)

S=\gr(S_{\rm op})\widehat{\oplus}\,S_{\rm mul},

where
S_{\rm op}
is a single-valued symmetric operator in the Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}_{\rm op}:={\mathfrak{H}}\ominus\mul S
,
while
S_{\rm mul}:=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
\mul S\end{bmatrix}
is a purely multi-valued linear relation in the subspace
{\mathfrak{H}}_{\rm mul}:=\mul S
, called
the multi-valued part
of
S
.

Let us consider
S_{\rm op}
as an operator from the Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}_{0}:=\overline{\dom S}
to the Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}_{\rm op}
and denote it by
S_{0}
and let
S_{0}^{*}\in{\mathcal{C}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{H}}_{0})
be the adjoint to the operator
S_{0}\in{\mathcal{C}}({\mathfrak{H}}_{0},{\mathfrak{H}})
. Then
S_{0}^{*}
is a single-valued operator.
Denote by
{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,+}
the linear space
{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,+}:=\dom S_{0}^{*}
endowed with the inner product

(3.3)

(f,g)_{0,+}:=(f,g)_{{\mathfrak{H}}}+(S_{0}^{*}f,S_{0}^{*}g)_{\mathfrak{H}},\quad f,g\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,+}=\dom S_{0}^{*}.

Since the operator
S_{0}^{*}
is closed,
{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,+}
is a Hilbert space with the graph norm

(3.4)

\|\widehat{f}\|^{2}_{0,+}=\|f\|_{\mathfrak{H}}^{2}+\|S_{0}^{*}f\|_{\mathfrak{H}}^{2}\quad f\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,+}=\dom S_{0}^{*}

such that
{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,+}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}_{\rm op}
. If
{\mathfrak{H}}_{0}\subsetneq{\mathfrak{H}}
then
S_{\rm op}^{*}

is a linear relation

S_{\rm op}^{*}=\mbox{gr }S_{0}^{*}\widehat{\oplus}\,\begin{bmatrix}0\\
{\mathfrak{H}}_{1}\end{bmatrix},\quad\text{where}\quad{\mathfrak{H}}_{1}={\mathfrak{H}}_{\rm op}\ominus{\mathfrak{H}}_{0}.

By
[
6
, Section 1.1.1]
,
there exist a dual Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}_{-}
of bounded conjugate linear functionals on
{\mathbb{C}}^{p}_{0,+}

and an isometric operator
V_{0}
from
{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,-}
onto
{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,+}
such that
{\mathfrak{H}}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}_{-}
and

(3.5)

[f,h]_{\mathfrak{H}}=(V_{0}f,h)_{{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}}\quad\text{for all }\quad f\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{\rm op},\quad h\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,+}.

The embedding
{\imath}:{\mathfrak{H}}_{\rm op}\hookrightarrow{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,-}
is realized by the identification of
any vector
f\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{\rm op}
with the functional

(3.6)

{\imath}f:h\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,+}\mapsto{\imath}f(h)=(f,h)_{\mathfrak{H}}.

and the space
{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,-}
can be realized as the completion of
{\mathfrak{H}}_{\rm op}
with respect to the “negative norm”

\|{\imath}f\|_{{\mathfrak{H}}_{-}}=\sup_{h\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,+}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|[f,h]_{\mathfrak{H}}|}{\|h\|_{{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,+}}}.

For
f\in{\mathfrak{H}}
we will identify
{\imath}f
with
f
. This gives the inclusion
{\mathfrak{H}}_{\rm op}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,-}
and we will use the notation

(3.7)

\langle\mathfrak{f},h\rangle^{(0)}_{-,+}:=\mathfrak{f}(h)\quad\textrm{for \ $\mathfrak{f}\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,-}$ \ and \quad$h\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,+}$},

for the duality between
{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,-}
and
{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,+}
. In view of (
3.6
)
the expression
\langle\mathfrak{f},h\rangle_{-,+}
can be viewed as an extension of the inner product in the Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}_{\rm op}
.

The triple

{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,+}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}_{\rm op}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,-}

is called a
rigged Hilbert space

{\mathfrak{H}}_{\rm op}
, cf.,
[
6
]
.
Setting

(3.8)

{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}={\mathfrak{H}}_{0,+}\oplus\mul S,\quad{\mathfrak{H}}_{-}={\mathfrak{H}}_{0,-}\oplus\mul S,\quad\widehat{V}:=V\oplus I_{\mul S}:\,\widehat{\mathfrak{H}}_{-}\to\widehat{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}

we obtain a rigged Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}

(3.9)

{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}_{-}

with the duality between
{\mathfrak{H}}_{-}
and
{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}
given by

\langle\mathfrak{f},h\rangle^{(S)}_{-,+}:=\langle\mathfrak{f}_{0},h_{0}\rangle^{(0)}_{-,+}+(f_{1},h_{1})_{\mathfrak{H}}

for

\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{f}_{0}+f_{1},\quad{h}=h_{0}+h_{1},\quad\mathfrak{f}_{0}\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,-},\quad h_{0}\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,+},\quad f_{1},h_{1}\in\mul S.

Then it follows from (
3.7
) and (
3.6
) that

(3.10)

\langle\mathfrak{f},h\rangle^{(S)}_{-,+}=(f,h)_{\mathfrak{H}}\quad\text{ for all $f\in{\mathfrak{H}}$ and $h\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}$}.

Since
S^{*}=S_{\rm op}^{*}\widehat{\oplus}\,\begin{bmatrix}0\\
\mul S\end{bmatrix}

we obtain from (
3.4
)

(3.11)

\|f\|^{2}_{{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}}=\|f\|_{\mathfrak{H}}^{2}+\|P_{{\mathfrak{H}}_{0}}f^{\prime}\|_{\mathfrak{H}}^{2}\quad\mbox{for all $\begin{bmatrix}f\\
f^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}\in S^{*}$}.

Step 2: Construction of a rigging for the linear relation
A
.

Let
{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}_{-}
be the rigging constructed for the linear relation
S
.
Then (i) holds since
S^{*}=JA^{[*]}
and hence
{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}=\dom S^{*}=\dom A^{[*]}
.
The formula (
3.1
) follows from (
3.11
).

Let us define the duality between
{\mathfrak{H}}_{-}
and
{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}
by

\langle\mathfrak{f},h\rangle_{-,+}:=\langle\mathfrak{f},Jh\rangle^{(S)}_{-,+}\quad\text{ for $\mathfrak{f}\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{-}$ and $h\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}$}.

Then, by (
3.10
), we get for all
f\in{\mathfrak{H}}
and
h\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}

\langle{f},h\rangle_{-,+}=\langle\mathfrak{f},Jh\rangle^{(S)}_{-,+}=(f,Jh)_{\mathfrak{H}}=[f,h]_{\mathfrak{H}}.

This proves (ii).
∎

The triple
{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}_{-}
constructed in Lemma
3.1
is called a
rigged Pontryagin space
.

Let
{\mathbb{C}}^{p}
be an auxiliary Hilbert space.
For a linear operator
T\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathfrak{H}}_{-},{\mathbb{C}}^{p})
we denote by
T^{\langle*\rangle}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{C}}^{p},{\mathfrak{H}}_{+})
its adjoint with respect to the duality
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{-,+}
, i.e.,

(3.12)

\langle\mathfrak{f},T^{\langle*\rangle}\xi\rangle_{-,+}=(T\mathfrak{f},\xi)_{{\mathbb{C}}^{p}}=\xi^{*}T\mathfrak{f}\quad\xi\in{\mathbb{C}}^{p},\quad\mathfrak{f}\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{-}.

For an operator
T\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathbb{C}}^{p})
the operator

defined by the equality (

) coincides with
T^{[*]}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{C}}^{p},{\mathfrak{H}})
.

Similarly, the adjoint to

with respect to the duality

is defined as the operator

such that

(3.13)

\langle G\xi,h\rangle_{-,+}=(\xi,G^{\langle*\rangle}h)_{{\mathbb{C}}^{p}}\quad\xi\in{\mathbb{C}}^{p},\quad h\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}.

In particular, for the operator
\gamma(\overline{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{C}}^{p},{\mathfrak{H}}_{+})

its adjoint
\gamma(\overline{\lambda})^{\langle*\rangle}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathfrak{H}}_{-},{\mathbb{C}}^{p})
is defined by

(3.14)

\langle\mathfrak{f},\gamma(\overline{\lambda})\xi\rangle_{-,+}=(\gamma(\overline{\lambda})^{\langle*\rangle}\mathfrak{f},\xi)_{{\mathbb{C}}^{p}}\quad\mathfrak{f}\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{-},\quad\xi\in{\mathbb{C}}^{p}.

In what follows we also use the notation

(3.15)

\langle h,\mathfrak{f}\rangle_{-,+}:=\langle\mathfrak{f},h\rangle_{-,+}^{*},\quad\text{for}\quad h\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{+},\quad\mathfrak{f}\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{-}.

3.2.
Extended generalized resolvents

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
A
be a closed symmetric linear relation with equal defect numbers,
and let
{\mathbf{R}}_{\lambda}
be a generalized resolvent of
A
with a minimal representing relation
\widetilde{A}
,
\lambda\in\rho(\widetilde{A})
. Then

(i)

\ran{\mathbf{R}}_{\lambda}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}
and

\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{R}}_{\lambda}f\\
(I_{\mathfrak{H}}+\lambda{\mathbf{R}}_{\lambda})f\end{bmatrix}\in A^{[*]}
for all
f\in{\mathfrak{H}}
.

(ii)

{\mathbf{R}}_{\lambda}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{H}}_{+})
.

(iii)

The operator
{\mathbf{R}}_{\lambda}
admits a continuation to an operator

\widetilde{\mathbf{R}}_{\lambda}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathfrak{H}}_{-},{\mathfrak{H}})
called extended generalized resolvent.

(iv)

For every selfadjoint extension
A_{0}
of
A
the extended resolvent
\widetilde{R}_{\lambda}^{0}
satisfies the identity

(3.16)

\widetilde{R}_{\lambda}^{0}-\widetilde{R}_{\mu}^{0}=(\lambda-\mu)R_{\lambda}^{0}\widetilde{R}_{\mu}^{0},\quad\lambda,\mu\in\rho(A_{0}),

and hence

\widetilde{R}_{\lambda}^{0}-\widetilde{R}_{\mu}^{0}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathfrak{H}}_{-},{\mathfrak{H}}_{+})
.

Proof.

(i) For all
f\in{\mathfrak{H}}
and
\begin{bmatrix}h\\
h^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}\in A
we obtain

[(I_{\mathfrak{H}}+\lambda{\mathbf{R}}_{\lambda})f,h]_{\mathfrak{H}}-[{\mathbf{R}}_{\lambda}f,h])_{\mathfrak{H}}\\
=[f,h]_{\mathfrak{H}}-[f,{\mathbf{R}}_{\overline{\lambda}}(h^{\prime}-\overline{\lambda}h)]_{\mathfrak{H}}=[f,h]_{\mathfrak{H}}-[f,h]_{\mathfrak{H}}=0

Here we used the inclusion
{\mathbf{R}}_{\overline{\lambda}}(h^{\prime}-\overline{\lambda}h)=h
.
This proves (i).

(ii) Let
f_{n}\to 0
in
{\mathfrak{H}}
. Then
{\mathbf{R}}_{\lambda}f_{n}\to 0
and, by
(
3.1
),

\|{\mathbf{R}}_{\lambda}f_{n}\|^{2}_{{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}}=\|{\mathbf{R}}_{\lambda}f_{n}\|_{\mathfrak{H}}^{2}+\|P_{{\mathfrak{H}}_{0}}J(f_{n}+\lambda{\mathbf{R}}_{\lambda}f_{n})\|_{\mathfrak{H}}^{2}\to 0.

(iii) Since
{\mathbf{R}}_{\overline{\lambda}}^{\langle*\rangle}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathfrak{H}}_{-},{\mathfrak{H}})
, (iii) follows from the inclusion
{\mathbf{R}}_{\lambda}\subset{\mathbf{R}}_{\overline{\lambda}}^{\langle*\rangle}
.

(iv) follows from the Hilbert identity

R_{\overline{\lambda}}^{0}-R_{\overline{\mu}}^{0}=({\overline{\lambda}}-{\overline{\mu}})R_{\overline{\lambda}}^{0}R_{\overline{\mu}}^{0}

by taking the adjoint with respect to the duality
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{-,+}

and using the equalities
(R_{\overline{\lambda}}^{0})^{\langle*\rangle}=\widetilde{R}_{\lambda}^{0}
,

(R_{\overline{\mu}}^{0})^{\langle*\rangle}=\widetilde{R}_{\mu}^{0}
.
∎

The operator
{\mathbf{R}}
defined by

(3.17)

{\mathbf{R}}=\frac{1}{2}(R_{i}^{0}+R_{-i}^{0}).

has the properties

(1)

{\mathbf{R}}={\mathbf{R}}^{*}
,
{\mathbf{R}}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{H}}_{+})
and
\widetilde{\mathbf{R}}:={\mathbf{R}}^{\langle*\rangle}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathfrak{H}}_{-},{\mathfrak{H}})
.

(2)

For every extended generalized resolvent
\widetilde{\mathbf{R}}_{\lambda}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathfrak{H}}_{-},{\mathfrak{H}})
we have

\widetilde{\mathbf{R}}_{\lambda}-\widetilde{\mathbf{R}}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathfrak{H}}_{-},{\mathfrak{H}}_{+})
.

the first claim follows from Lemma
3.2
(ii)–(iii) and the second claim follows from the equality

\widetilde{\mathbf{R}}_{\lambda}-\widetilde{\mathbf{R}}=(\widetilde{R}_{\lambda}^{0}-\widetilde{\mathbf{R}})-\gamma(\lambda)(M(\lambda)+\tau(\lambda))^{-1}\gamma(\overline{\lambda})^{\langle*\rangle},

and the relations
\gamma(\lambda)\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{C}}^{p},{\mathfrak{H}}_{+})
and
\gamma(\overline{\lambda})^{\langle*\rangle}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathfrak{H}}_{-},{\mathbb{C}}^{p})
, see (

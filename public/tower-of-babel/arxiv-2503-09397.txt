3
The initial-boundary value problem
\beta^{T}_{\rm v}

In this section we establish properties of the solution to the system
\beta^{T}_{\rm v}
, (
9
)–(
11
). We show that there exists a continuous matrix-valued function

, defined for

u^{f_{\rm v}}_{\beta}(x,t)=f_{\rm v}(t-x)+\int_{x}^{t}w(x,s)f_{\rm v}(t-s)ds,\quad x\in[0,\infty),\ t\in[0,T],

(16)

under the agreement that

is continued to the negative half-line by zero, is the solution of the system

.

The kernel

is a generalized solution of the following Goursat problem:

\displaystyle w_{tt}(x,t)-w_{xx}(x,t)+q(x)w(x,t)=0,

\displaystyle t>0,\ x\in(0,t),

(17)

\displaystyle w(0,t)=0,

\displaystyle t\geqslant 0,

(18)

\displaystyle w(x,x)=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{x}q(s)ds,

\displaystyle x\geqslant 0,

(19)

in the sense that it is the solution of the corresponding integral equation. In what follows we derive this equation in the same way as it was done in
[
1
]
and show that it has the unique continuous solution. Then we prove that
u^{f_{\rm v}}_{\beta}
defined by (
16

9
)–(
11
).

To reduce the Goursat problem to an integral equation let us change the variables,

\xi=t-x,\quad\eta=t+x,\quad v(\xi,\eta):=w\left(\frac{\eta-\xi}{2},\frac{\eta+\xi}{2}\right).

Then (
17
)–(
19

\displaystyle v_{\xi\eta}(\xi,\eta)+q\left(\frac{\eta-\xi}{2}\right)\frac{v(\xi,\eta)}{4}=0,

\displaystyle\eta>0,\ \xi\in(0,\eta),

(20)

\displaystyle v(\xi,\xi)=0,

\displaystyle\xi\geqslant 0,

(21)

\displaystyle v(0,\eta)=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{\eta}{2}}q(s)ds,

\displaystyle\eta\geqslant 0.

(22)

Formally solving the inhomogeneous differential equation
v_{\xi\eta}(\xi,\eta)=g(\xi,\eta):=-q\left(\frac{\eta-\xi}{2}\right)\frac{v(\xi,\eta)}{4}
gives
v(\xi,\eta)=\int_{0}^{\xi}d\xi_{1}\int_{0}^{\eta}d\eta_{1}g(\xi_{1},\eta_{1})+v_{1}(\xi)+v_{2}(\eta)
with some functions
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
that can be determined from (
21
) and (
22
). This leads to the integral equation

v(\xi,\eta)=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\frac{\xi}{2}}^{\frac{\eta}{2}}q(s)ds-\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{\xi}d\xi_{1}\int_{\xi}^{\eta}d\eta_{1}q\left(\frac{\eta_{1}-\xi_{1}}{2}\right)v(\xi_{1},\eta_{1}).

(23)

Denote

v_{0}(\xi,\eta):=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\frac{\xi}{2}}^{\frac{\eta}{2}}q(s)ds,\quad\widetilde{v}:=Vv,

where
V
is the operator acting as

(Vv)(\xi,\eta):=-\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{\xi}d\xi_{1}\int_{\xi}^{\eta}d\eta_{1}q\left(\frac{\eta_{1}-\xi_{1}}{2}\right)v(\xi_{1},\eta_{1}).

Then the integral equation (
23
) can be written as
v=v_{0}+Vv
and its solution is
v=(I-V)^{-1}v_{0}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}V^{n}v_{0}
, if one shows that the series converge in a suitable sense. Let us fix
T>0
and consider it in the space
\mathcal{C}(\{(\xi,\eta)\,|\,\eta\in[0,T],\xi\in[0,\eta]\})
. One has

\|v_{0}(\xi,\eta)\|_{\mathbb{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{\eta}{2}}\|q(s)\|_{\mathbb{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}}ds=:S(\eta),\quad\forall\xi\geqslant 0,

\|(Vv_{0})(\xi,\eta)\|_{\mathbb{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}}\leqslant\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{\xi}d\xi_{1}\int_{\xi}^{\eta}d\eta_{1}S(\eta_{1})\left\|q\left(\frac{\eta_{1}-\xi_{1}}{2}\right)\right\|_{\mathbb{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}}\\
=S(\eta)\int_{0}^{\xi}d\xi_{1}\int_{\xi}^{\eta}d\eta_{1}S^{\prime}(\eta_{1}-\xi_{1})\leqslant S^{2}(\eta)\xi,

analagously

\|(V^{2}v_{0})(\xi,\eta)\|_{\mathbb{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}}\leqslant\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{\xi}d\xi_{1}\int_{\xi}^{\eta}d\eta_{1}S^{2}(\eta_{1})\xi_{1}\left\|q\left(\frac{\eta_{1}-\xi_{1}}{2}\right)\right\|_{\mathbb{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}}\\
=S^{2}(\eta)\int_{0}^{\xi}d\xi_{1}\xi_{1}\int_{\xi}^{\eta}d\eta_{1}S^{\prime}(\eta_{1}-\xi_{1})\leqslant\frac{S^{3}(\eta)\xi^{2}}{2},

and, by induction,

\|(V^{n}v_{0})(\xi,\eta)\|_{\mathbb{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}}\leqslant\frac{S^{n+1}(\eta)\xi^{n}}{n!}
.
Therefore the series converge and the resulting solution

belongs to
\mathcal{C}(\{(\xi,\eta)\,|\,\eta\in[0,T],\xi\in[0,\eta]\})
for every

and satisfies the estimate

\|v(\xi,\eta)\|_{\mathbb{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}}\leqslant S(\eta)e^{\xi S(\eta)}.

Looking at the equality (

v_{\xi}(\xi,\eta)=\frac{1}{4}q\left(\frac{\xi}{2}\right)-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\xi}^{\eta}q\left(\frac{\eta_{1}-\xi}{2}\right)v(\xi,\eta_{1})d\eta_{1}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{\xi}q\left(\frac{\xi-\xi_{1}}{2}\right)v(\xi_{1},\xi)d\xi_{1},

v_{\eta}(\xi,\eta)=-\frac{1}{4}q\left(\frac{\eta}{2}\right)-\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{\xi}q\left(\frac{\eta-\xi_{1}}{2}\right)v(\xi_{1},\eta)d\xi_{1}.

Moreover,

v_{\xi\eta}(\xi,\eta)=-\frac{1}{4}q\left(\frac{\eta-\xi}{2}\right)v(\xi,\eta)

holds for fixed
\xi
and a. e.
\eta
and for fixed

and a. e.

, which gives (

Solution

v_{0_{\xi}}(\xi,\eta)=\frac{1}{4}q\left(\frac{\xi}{2}\right),\quad v_{0_{\eta}}(\xi,\eta)=-\frac{1}{4}q\left(\frac{\eta}{2}\right),

\widetilde{v}_{\xi}(\xi,\eta)=-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\xi}^{\eta}q\left(\frac{\eta_{1}-\xi}{2}\right)v(\xi,\eta_{1})d\eta_{1}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{\xi}q\left(\frac{\xi-\xi_{1}}{2}\right)v(\xi_{1},\xi)d\xi_{1}\\
=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{\eta-\xi}{2}}q(\tau)v(\xi,\xi+2\tau)d\tau+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{\xi}{2}}q(\tau)v(\xi-2\tau,\xi)d\tau,

(24)

\widetilde{v}_{\eta}(\xi,\eta)=-\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{\xi}q\left(\frac{\eta-\xi_{1}}{2}\right)v(\xi_{1},\eta)d\xi_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\frac{\eta-\xi}{2}}^{\frac{\eta}{2}}q(\tau)v(\eta-2\tau,\eta)d\tau.

(25)

Taking
v
from the equation (
23
) we get:

\frac{d}{d\xi}v(\xi,\xi+2\tau)=\frac{1}{4}\left(q\left(\frac{\xi}{2}\right)-q\left(\frac{\xi}{2}+\tau\right)\right)\\
+\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{0}^{\frac{\xi}{2}}q(\sigma)v(\xi-2\sigma,\xi)d\sigma-\int_{0}^{\tau}q(\sigma)v(\xi,\xi+2\sigma)d\sigma\right.\\
\left.-\int_{\tau}^{\tau+\frac{\xi}{2}}q(\sigma)v(\xi+2\tau-2\sigma,\xi+2\tau)d\sigma\right),

\frac{d}{d\eta}v(\eta-2\tau,\eta)=\frac{1}{4}\left(q\left(\frac{\eta}{2}-\tau\right)-q\left(\frac{\eta}{2}\right)\right)\\
+\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{0}^{\frac{\eta}{2}-\tau}q(\sigma)v(\eta-2\tau-2\sigma,\eta-2\tau)d\sigma-\int_{0}^{\tau}q(\sigma)v(\eta-2\tau,\eta-2\tau+2\sigma)d\sigma\right.\\
\left.-\int_{\tau}^{\frac{\eta}{2}}q(\sigma)v(\eta-2\sigma,\eta)d\sigma\right).

Substitution to (
24
) and (
25
) gives:

\widetilde{v}_{\xi\xi}(\xi,\eta)=\frac{q((\eta-\xi)/2)v(\xi,\eta)+q(\xi/2)v(0,\xi)}{4}\\
+\frac{1}{8}\left(\int_{0}^{\frac{\eta-\xi}{2}}q(\tau)(q(\xi/2+\tau)-q(\xi/2))d\tau+\int_{0}^{\frac{\xi}{2}}q(\tau)(q(\xi/2-\tau)-q(\xi/2))d\tau\right)\\
+\frac{1}{4}\left(\int_{0}^{\frac{\eta-\xi}{2}}d\tau q(\tau)\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}d\sigma q(\sigma)v(\xi,\xi+2\sigma)-\int_{0}^{\frac{\xi}{2}}d\sigma q(\sigma)v(\xi-2\sigma,\xi)\right.\right.\\
\left.\left.+\int_{\tau}^{\tau+\frac{\xi}{2}}d\sigma q(\sigma)v(\xi+2\tau-2\sigma,\xi+2\tau)\right)\right.\\
+\int_{0}^{\frac{\xi}{2}}d\tau q(\tau)\left(\int_{0}^{\frac{\xi}{2}-\tau}d\sigma q(\sigma)v(\xi-2\tau-2\sigma,\xi-2\tau)\right.\\
\left.\left.-\int_{0}^{\tau}d\sigma q(\sigma)v(\xi-2\tau,\xi-2\tau+2\sigma)-\int_{\tau}^{\frac{\xi}{2}}d\sigma q(\sigma)v(\xi-2\sigma,\xi)\right)\right),

(26)

\widetilde{v}_{\eta\eta}(\xi,\eta)=\frac{q((\eta-\xi)/2)v(\xi,\eta)-q(\eta/2)v(0,\eta)}{4}\\
+\frac{1}{8}\int_{\frac{\eta-\xi}{2}}^{\frac{\eta}{2}}q(\tau)(q(\eta/2)-q(\eta/2-\tau))d\tau\\
+\frac{1}{4}\left(\int_{\frac{\eta-\xi}{2}}^{\frac{\eta}{2}}d\tau q(\tau)\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}d\sigma q(\sigma)v(\eta-2\tau,\eta-2\tau+2\sigma)\right.\right.\\
\left.\left.-\int_{0}^{\frac{\eta}{2}-\tau}d\sigma q(\sigma)v(\eta-2\tau-2\sigma,\eta-2\tau)+\int_{\tau}^{\frac{\eta}{2}}d\sigma q(\sigma)v(\eta-2\sigma,\eta)\right)\right),

(27)

and

\widetilde{v}_{\xi\eta}(\xi,\eta)=-\frac{q((\eta-\xi)/2)v(\xi,\eta)}{4}.

Returning to the function
w
we can accordingly write it in the form
w=w_{0}+\widetilde{w}
with

w_{0}(x,t)=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\frac{t-x}{2}}^{\frac{t+x}{2}}q(s)ds.

For the second summand
\widetilde{w}
let us find the second order derivatives

\widetilde{w}_{tt}((\eta-\xi)/2,(\eta+\xi)/2)=\widetilde{v}_{\xi\xi}(\xi,\eta)+\widetilde{v}_{\eta\eta}(\xi,\eta)+2\widetilde{v}_{\xi\eta}(\xi,\eta)\\
=\frac{q(\xi/2)v(0,\xi)-q(\eta/2)v(0,\eta)}{4}\\
+\frac{1}{8}\left(q(\eta/2)\int_{\frac{\eta-\xi}{2}}^{\frac{\eta}{2}}q(\tau)d\tau-q(\xi/2)\int_{0}^{\frac{\eta-\xi}{2}}q(\tau)d\tau-q(\xi/2)\int_{0}^{\frac{\xi}{2}}q(\tau)d\tau\right.\\
\left.+\int_{0}^{\frac{\eta-\xi}{2}}q(\tau)q(\xi/2+\tau)d\tau+\int_{0}^{\frac{\xi}{2}}q(\tau)q(\xi/2-\tau)d\tau-\int_{\frac{\eta-\xi}{2}}^{\frac{\eta}{2}}q(\tau)q(\eta/2-\tau)d\tau\right)\\
+\widehat{w}((\eta-\xi)/2,(\eta+\xi)/2),

where the function
\widehat{w}
contains all the remaining integral terms and is continuous. Returning to the original variables we have

\widetilde{w}_{tt}(x,t)=\frac{1}{4}q\left(\frac{t-x}{2}\right)w\left(\frac{t-x}{2},\frac{t-x}{2}\right)-\frac{1}{4}q\left(\frac{t+x}{2}\right)w\left(\frac{t+x}{2},\frac{t+x}{2}\right)\\
+\frac{1}{8}\left(q\left(\frac{t+x}{2}\right)\int_{t}^{\frac{t+x}{2}}q(\tau)d\tau-q\left(\frac{t-x}{2}\right)\int_{0}^{t}q(\tau)d\tau\right.\\
\left.-q\left(\frac{t-x}{2}\right)\int_{0}^{\frac{t-x}{2}}q(\tau)d\tau+\int_{0}^{x}q(\tau)q\left(\frac{t-x}{2}+\tau\right)d\tau\right.\\
\left.+\int_{0}^{\frac{t-x}{2}}q(\tau)q\left(\frac{t-x}{2}-\tau\right)d\tau-\int_{x}^{\frac{t+x}{2}}q(\tau)q\left(\frac{t+x}{2}-\tau\right)d\tau\right)+\widehat{w}(x,t).

(28)

At the same time, using (
21
),

\widetilde{w}_{xx}((\eta-\xi)/2,(\eta+\xi)/2)=\widetilde{v}_{\xi\xi}(\xi,\eta)+\widetilde{v}_{\eta\eta}(\xi,\eta)-2\widetilde{v}_{\xi\eta}(\xi,\eta)\\
=\widetilde{w}_{tt}((\eta-\xi)/2,(\eta+\xi)/2)-4\widetilde{v}_{\xi\eta}(\xi,\eta)\\
=\widetilde{w}_{tt}((\eta-\xi)/2,(\eta+\xi)/2)+q((\eta-\xi)/2)v(\xi,\eta),

and thus

\widetilde{w}_{xx}(x,t)=\widetilde{w}_{tt}(x,t)+q(x)w(x,t).

The following lemma is a consequence of the Fubini’s theorem.

Lemma 1
.

If
q\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\rm loc}([0,\infty);\mathbb{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}})
, then the integrals
\int_{0}^{\frac{t-x}{2}}q(\tau)q\left(\frac{t-x}{2}-\tau\right)d\tau
,
\int_{x}^{\frac{t+x}{2}}q(\tau)q\left(\frac{t+x}{2}-\tau\right)d\tau
and
\int_{0}^{x}q(\tau)q\left(\frac{t-x}{2}+\tau\right)d\tau
as functions of
x
and
t
are locally summable in both variables: for every
t>0
they belong to
\mathcal{L}_{1}([0,t];\mathbb{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}})
as functions of
x
, and for every
x>0
to
\mathcal{L}_{1,\rm loc}([x,\infty);\mathbb{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}})
as functions of
t
.

Proof.

The first integral equals
p((t-x)/2)
, where

p(x):=\int_{0}^{x}q(\tau)q(x-\tau)d\tau.

Take
T>0
. Then

\int_{0}^{T}\|p(x)\|_{\mathbb{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}}dx\leqslant\int_{0}^{T}dx\int_{0}^{x}d\tau\|q(\tau)\|\|q(x-\tau)\|\\
=\int_{0}^{T}d\tau\|q(\tau)\|\int_{\tau}^{T}dx\|q(x-\tau)\|=\int_{0}^{T}d\tau\|q(\tau)\|\int_{0}^{T-\tau}d\sigma\|q(\sigma)\|\\
\leqslant\int_{0}^{T}\|q(\tau)\|d\tau\int_{0}^{T}\|q(\sigma)\|d\sigma<\infty,

which justifies the change of the order of integration. Hence
p\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\rm loc}([0,\infty);\mathbb{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}})
, and the assertion for the first integral follows.

The second integral can be written as the difference of

\int_{0}^{\frac{t+x}{2}}q(\tau)q\left(\frac{t+x}{2}-\tau\right)d\tau=p\left(\frac{t+x}{2}\right)

and
\int_{0}^{x}q(\tau)q\left(\frac{t+x}{2}-\tau\right)d\tau
. It therefore remains to consider
\int_{0}^{x}q(\tau)q\left(\frac{t+x}{2}-\tau\right)d\tau
and
\int_{0}^{x}q(\tau)q\left(\frac{t-x}{2}+\tau\right)d\tau
. For every
t\geqslant 0
one has

\int_{0}^{t}dx\int_{0}^{x}d\tau\|q(\tau)\|\left\|q\left(\frac{t+x}{2}-\tau\right)\right\|=\int_{0}^{t}d\tau\|q(\tau)\|\int_{\tau}^{t}dx\left\|q\left(\frac{t+x}{2}-\tau\right)\right\|\\
=2\int_{0}^{t}d\tau\|q(\tau)\|\int_{\frac{t-\tau}{2}}^{t-\tau}d\sigma\|q(\sigma)\|\leqslant 2\int_{0}^{t}\|q(\tau)\|d\tau\int_{0}^{t}\|q(\sigma)\|d\sigma<\infty,

\int_{0}^{t}dx\int_{0}^{x}d\tau\|q(\tau)\|\left\|q\left(\frac{t-x}{2}+\tau\right)\right\|=\int_{0}^{t}d\tau\|q(\tau)\|\int_{\tau}^{t}dx\left\|q\left(\frac{t-x}{2}+\tau\right)\right\|\\
=2\int_{0}^{t}d\tau\|q(\tau)\|\int_{\tau}^{\frac{t+\tau}{2}}d\sigma\|q(\sigma)\|\leqslant 2\int_{0}^{t}\|q(\tau)\|d\tau\int_{0}^{t}\|q(\sigma)\|d\sigma<\infty.

For every
x\geqslant 0
and
T>x
one has:

\int_{x}^{T}dt\int_{0}^{x}d\tau\|q(\tau)\|\left\|q\left(\frac{t+x}{2}-\tau\right)\right\|=\int_{0}^{x}d\tau\|q(\tau)\|\int_{x}^{T}dt\left\|q\left(\frac{t+x}{2}-\tau\right)\right\|\\
=2\int_{0}^{x}d\tau\|q(\tau)\|\int_{x-\tau}^{\frac{T+x}{2}-\tau}d\sigma\|q(\sigma)\|\leqslant 2\int_{0}^{x}\|q(\tau)\|d\tau\int_{0}^{\frac{T+x}{2}}\|q(\sigma)\|d\sigma<\infty,

\int_{x}^{T}dt\int_{0}^{x}d\tau\|q(\tau)\|\left\|q\left(\frac{t-x}{2}+\tau\right)\right\|=\int_{0}^{x}d\tau\|q(\tau)\|\int_{x}^{T}dt\left\|q\left(\frac{t-x}{2}+\tau\right)\right\|\\
=2\int_{0}^{x}d\tau\|q(\tau)\|\int_{\tau}^{\frac{T-x}{2}+\tau}d\sigma\|q(\sigma)\|\leqslant 2\int_{0}^{x}\|q(\tau)\|d\tau\int_{0}^{\frac{T+x}{2}}\|q(\sigma)\|d\sigma<\infty.

From this the assertion for the second and the third integrals follows, which completes the proof.
∎

We have proven the following result.

Lemma 2
.

Let
q\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\rm loc}([0,\infty);\mathbb{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}})
. Then

1. For every
t\geqslant 0
one has
\widetilde{w}_{tt}(\cdot,t)\in\mathcal{L}_{1}([0,t];\mathbb{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}})
.

2. For every
x\geqslant 0
one has
\widetilde{w}_{tt}(x,\cdot)\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\rm loc}([x,\infty);\mathbb{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}})
.

3. For every
t\geqslant 0
the equation
\widetilde{w}_{tt}(x,t)-\widetilde{w}_{xx}(x,t)+q(x)w(x,t)=0
holds for a. e.
x\in[0,t]
.

4. If a representative of the equivalence class
q
is chosen, then for a. e.
x\geqslant 0
the equation
\widetilde{w}_{tt}(x,t)-\widetilde{w}_{xx}(x,t)+q(x)w(x,t)=0
holds for a. e.
t\in[x,\infty)
.

Remark 1
.

The equation
w_{tt}(x,t)-w_{xx}(x,t)+q(x)w(x,t)=0
can hold nowhere in the sense of an equality of partial derivatives.

Theorem 1
.

Let
q\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\rm loc}([0,\infty);\mathbb{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}})
,
f_{\rm v}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\rm loc}([0,\infty);\mathbb{C}^{n})
and
{\rm supp\,}f_{\rm v}\subset(0,\infty)
. Then the function

u^{f_{\rm v}}(x,t)=f_{\rm v}(t-x)+\int_{x}^{t}w(x,s)f_{\rm v}(t-s)ds

(29)

solves the system

\displaystyle u_{tt}(x,t)-u_{xx}(x,t)+q(x)u(x,t)=0,

\displaystyle t>0,\text{ a.\,e. }x\in(0,t),

(30)

\displaystyle u(x,t)=0,

\displaystyle x\geqslant t,

(31)

\displaystyle u(0,t)=f_{\rm v}(t),

\displaystyle t\geqslant 0.

(32)

Besides that one has

u^{f_{\rm v}}(\cdot,t)\in\mathcal{W}^{2}_{1}([0,t];\mathbb{C}^{n}),\quad-u^{f_{\rm v}}_{xx}(\cdot,t)+q(\cdot)u^{f_{\rm v}}(\cdot,t)\in\mathcal{C}([0,t];\mathbb{C}^{n})

for every
t\geqslant 0
.

Proof.

Firstly, one can differentiate twice the equality (
29
) in
t
and get

u^{f_{\rm v}}_{tt}(x,t)=f_{\rm v}^{\prime\prime}(t-x)+\int_{x}^{t}w(x,s)f_{\rm v}^{\prime\prime}(t-s)ds.

Note that from this
u^{f_{\rm v}}_{tt}(\cdot,t)\in\mathcal{C}([0,t];\mathbb{C}^{n})
for every
t\geqslant 0
. As one can see, for every
x
the functions
\widetilde{w}(x,\cdot)
and
\widetilde{w}_{t}(x,\cdot)
are absolutely continuous. Thus one can integrate by parts:

u^{f_{\rm v}}_{tt}(x,t)=f_{\rm v}^{\prime\prime}(t-x)+\int_{x}^{t}w_{0}(x,s)f_{\rm v}^{\prime\prime}(t-s)ds+\int_{x}^{t}\widetilde{w}(x,s)f_{\rm v}^{\prime\prime}(t-s)ds\\
=f_{\rm v}^{\prime\prime}(t-x)+\int_{x}^{t}w_{0}(x,s)f_{\rm v}^{\prime\prime}(t-s)ds+\widetilde{w}_{t}(x,x)f_{\rm v}(t-x)+\int_{x}^{t}\widetilde{w}_{tt}(x,s)f_{\rm v}(t-s)ds,

since
\widetilde{w}(x,x)=0
. Differentiate now (
29
) in
x
,

u^{f_{\rm v}}_{xx}(x,t)=f_{\rm v}^{\prime\prime}(t-x)+\left(\int_{x}^{t}w_{0}(x,s)f_{\rm v}(t-s)ds\right)_{xx}\\
+\left(\int_{x}^{t}\widetilde{w}(x,s)f_{\rm v}(t-s)ds\right)_{xx}=f_{\rm v}^{\prime\prime}(t-x)+\left(\int_{x}^{t}w_{0}(x,s)f_{\rm v}(t-s)ds\right)_{xx}\\
-\widetilde{w}_{x}(x,x)f_{\rm v}(t-x)+\int_{x}^{t}\widetilde{w}_{xx}(x,s)f_{\rm v}(t-s)ds,

again using
\widetilde{w}(x,x)=0
. Then for every
t\geqslant 0
and a. e.
x\in(0,t)

u^{f_{\rm v}}_{tt}(x,t)-u^{f_{\rm v}}_{xx}(x,t)=\int_{x}^{t}w_{0}(x,s)f_{\rm v}^{\prime\prime}(t-s)ds-\left(\int_{x}^{t}w_{0}(x,s)f_{\rm v}(t-s)ds\right)_{xx}\\
+(\widetilde{w}_{t}(x,x)+\widetilde{w}_{x}(x,x))f_{\rm v}(t-x)+\int_{x}^{t}(\widetilde{w}_{tt}(x,s)-\widetilde{w}_{xx}(x,s))f_{\rm v}(x,s)ds\\
=\int_{x}^{t}w_{0}(x,s)f_{\rm v}^{\prime\prime}(t-s)ds-\left(\int_{x}^{t}w_{0}(x,s)f_{\rm v}(t-s)ds\right)_{xx}\\
-q(x)\int_{x}^{t}\widetilde{w}(x,s)f_{\rm v}(x,s)ds,

where we used the equality
\widetilde{w}_{t}(x,x)+\widetilde{w}_{x}(x,x)=0
. Consider the first and the second terms separately:

\int_{x}^{t}w_{0}(x,s)f_{\rm v}^{\prime\prime}(t-s)ds=w_{0}(x,x)f_{\rm v}^{\prime}(t-x)+\int_{x}^{t}w_{0_{t}}(x,s)f_{\rm v}^{\prime}(t-s)ds\\
=-\frac{f_{\rm v}^{\prime}(t-x)}{2}\int_{0}^{x}q(\tau)d\tau-\frac{1}{4}\int_{x}^{t}\left(q\left(\frac{s+x}{2}\right)-q\left(\frac{s-x}{2}\right)\right)f_{\rm v}^{\prime}(t-s)ds

and

\left(\int_{x}^{t}w_{0}(x,s)f_{\rm v}(t-s)ds\right)_{xx}=\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{x}^{t}ds\int_{\frac{s-x}{2}}^{\frac{s+x}{2}}d\tau q(\tau)f_{\rm v}(t-s)\right)_{xx}\\
=\frac{1}{2}\left(\left(\int_{0}^{x}q(\tau)d\tau\right)f_{\rm v}(t-x)-\int_{x}^{t}\left(q\left(\frac{s+x}{2}\right)+q\left(\frac{s-x}{2}\right)\right)f_{\rm v}(t-s)ds\right)_{x}\\
=\frac{q(x)f_{\rm v}(t-x)}{2}-\frac{f_{\rm v}^{\prime}(t-x)}{2}\int_{0}^{x}q(\tau)d\tau\\
-\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{x}^{\frac{t+x}{2}}q(\sigma)f_{\rm v}(t+x-2\sigma)d\sigma+\int_{0}^{\frac{t-x}{2}}q(\sigma)f_{\rm v}(t-x-2\sigma)d\sigma\right)_{x}\\
=q(x)f_{\rm v}(t-x)-\frac{f_{\rm v}^{\prime}(t-x)}{2}\int_{0}^{x}q(\tau)d\tau\\
-\frac{1}{2}\int_{x}^{\frac{t+x}{2}}q(\sigma)f^{\prime}(t+x-2\sigma)d\sigma+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{t-x}{2}}q(\sigma)f^{\prime}(t-x-2\sigma)d\sigma\\
=q(x)f_{\rm v}(t-x)-\frac{f_{\rm v}^{\prime}(t-x)}{2}\int_{0}^{x}q(\tau)d\tau\\
-\frac{1}{4}\int_{x}^{t}\left(q\left(\frac{s+x}{2}\right)-q\left(\frac{s-x}{2}\right)\right)f_{\rm v}^{\prime}(t-s)ds,

thus one has

\int_{x}^{t}w_{0}(x,s)f_{\rm v}^{\prime\prime}(t-s)ds-\left(\int_{x}^{t}w_{0}(x,s)f_{\rm v}(t-s)ds\right)_{xx}=-q(x)f_{\rm v}(t-x)

and

u^{f_{\rm v}}_{tt}(x,t)-u^{f_{\rm v}}_{xx}(x,t)\\
=-q(x)\left(f_{\rm v}(t-x)+\int_{x}^{t}w(x,s)f_{\rm v}(t-s)ds\right)=-q(x)u^{f_{\rm v}}(x,t).

We see that (
30
) holds. For every
t\geqslant 0
one has
u^{f_{\rm v}}_{xx}(\cdot,t)\in\mathcal{L}_{1}([0,t];\mathbb{C}^{n})
, which means that
u^{f_{\rm v}}(\cdot,t)\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{1}([0,t];\mathbb{C}^{n})
and that
-u^{f_{\rm v}}_{xx}(x,t)+q(x)u^{f_{\rm v}}(x,t)=-u^{f_{\rm v}}(x,t)
is a continuous function of
x
. Condition (
31
) is obviously satisfied and (
32
) holds, because
w(0,t)\equiv 0
.
∎

4
Smooth waves

In this section we show that the solution
u^{f_{\rm v}}_{\beta}(x,t)
to the system
\beta^{T}_{\rm v}
is at the same time a solution of the system
\alpha^{T}_{\rm v}
. Since the latter is known to be unique, see (
13
), this means that
u^{f_{\rm v}}_{\alpha}=u^{f_{\rm v}}_{\beta}
.

Theorem 2
.

Let a locally summable Hermitian
\mathbb{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}
-valued potential
q
be such that the operator
L_{0}
is positive definite. Then
u^{f_{\rm v}}_{\beta}(x,t)
given by (
16
) is the solution of the system
\alpha^{T}_{\rm v}
, (
5
)–(
8
).

Proof.

By Theorem
1
the solution
u^{f_{\rm v}}_{\beta}
satisfies (
5
), clearly (
7
) and (
8
) are satisfied as well. It remains to see that (
6
) holds. This means we need to show that the derivative
\ddot{u}^{f_{\rm v}}_{\beta}
in
\mathscr{H}
exists and coincides with the partial derivative
(u^{f_{\rm v}}_{\beta})_{tt}
. This follows from the next lemma (applied twice).
∎

Lemma 3
.

If
f_{\rm v}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,\infty);\mathbb{C}^{n})
and
{\rm supp\,}f_{\rm v}\subset(0,\infty)
, then
\dot{u}^{f_{\rm v}}_{\beta}=(u^{f_{\rm v}}_{\beta})_{t}
.

Proof.

The assertion follows from a direct estimate. Let
h\in(-1,1)\backslash\{0\}
. Since

(u^{f_{\rm v}}_{\beta})_{t}(x,t)=f_{\rm v}^{\prime}(t-x)+\int_{x}^{t}w(x,s)f_{\rm v}^{\prime}(t-s)ds=u^{f^{\prime}_{\rm v}}_{\beta}(x,t)

and
f_{\rm v}^{\prime}
is again from
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,\infty);\mathbb{C}^{n})
with
{\rm supp\,}f^{\prime}_{\rm v}\subset(0,\infty)
, one can differentiate any number of times, which means that
u^{f_{\rm v}}(x,\cdot)
is a smooth function. Using the Taylor formula

g(x+h)=g(x)+g^{\prime}(x)h+\frac{1}{2}\int_{x}^{x+h}(x+h-t)g^{\prime\prime}(t)dt

for a smoooth function
g
, which gives the estimate

\left\|\frac{g(x+h)-g(x)}{h}-g^{\prime}(x)\right\|_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\leqslant\frac{|h|}{2}\max_{t\in[x,x+h]}\|g^{\prime\prime}(t)\|_{\mathbb{C}^{n}},

we obtain for
u^{f_{\rm v}}(x,\cdot)
the following estimates, assuming
|h|<1
:

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\|\frac{u^{f_{\rm v}}(x,t+h)-u^{f_{\rm v}}(x,t)}{h}-u^{f_{\rm v}}_{t}(x,t)\right\|^{2}_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}dx\\
\leqslant\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\max_{t_{1}\in[t,t+h]}\left\|u^{f_{\rm v}}_{tt}(x,t_{1})\right\|_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}^{2}dx\\
=\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{t+h}\max_{t_{1}\in[t,t+h]}\left\|f_{\rm v}^{\prime\prime}(t_{1}-x)+\int_{x}^{t_{1}}w(x,s)f_{\rm v}^{\prime\prime}(t_{1}-s)ds\right\|_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}^{2}dx\\
\leqslant\frac{h^{2}}{2}\max_{t_{1}\in[0,t+1]}\|f_{\rm v}^{\prime\prime}(t_{1})\|_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}^{2}(t+1)\Big[1+\max_{t_{1}\in[0,t+1],x_{1}\in[0,t_{1}]}\|w(x_{1},t_{1})\|_{\mathbb{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}}^{2}(t+1)\Big]^{2}.

This means that
\frac{u^{f_{\rm v}}(\cdot,t+h)-u^{f_{\rm v}}(\cdot,t)}{h}\to u^{f_{\rm v}}_{t}(\cdot,t)
as
h\to 0
in the space
\mathscr{H}=\mathcal{L}_{2}([0,\infty);\mathbb{C}^{n})
.
∎

The next corollary follows immediately.

Corollary 1
.

The function
u^{f}(x,t)=u^{f_{\rm v}}_{\beta}(x,t)
, where
f=\Lambda^{T}f_{\rm v}
, is the solution of the system
\alpha^{T}
, (
1
)–(
4
). The closure of the operator

is an isomorphism from
\mathscr{F}^{T}
to
\mathscr{U}^{T}
and
\mathscr{U}^{T}=\mathcal{L}_{2}([0,T];\mathbb{C}^{n})
.

5
The control operator and the Sobolev norm

The operator
W_{\rm v}^{T}:f_{\rm v}\mapsto u^{f_{\rm v}}_{\beta}(\cdot,t)
defined by (

\mathcal{L}_{2}([0,T];\mathbb{C}^{n})
can be at the same time considered as an operator acting in the Sobolev space
\mathcal{H}^{2}([0,T];\mathbb{C}^{n})
. One can see that it is a bounded operator in the norm of this space. We show that it is also boundedly invertible in this norm.

Theorem 3
.

If
q\in\mathcal{L}_{2}([0,T];\mathbb{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}})
, then the operator
W_{\rm v}^{T}
given by (
15
) and restricted to the space
\mathcal{H}^{2}([0,T];\mathbb{C}^{n})
is an isomorphism in this space.

Proof.

Let
Y^{T}:f_{\rm v}(\cdot)\mapsto f_{\rm v}(T-\cdot)
be the reflection operator in
\mathcal{H}^{2}([0,T];\mathbb{C}^{n})
.
The property of being an isomorphism in
\mathcal{H}^{2}([0,T];\mathbb{C}^{n})
holds simultaneously for
W_{\rm v}^{T}
and for the operator
\tilde{W}^{T}:=W_{\rm v}^{T}Y^{T}=I+A^{T}
where the operator
A^{T}
acts in
\mathcal{H}^{2}([0,T];\mathbb{C}^{n})
by the rule

(A^{T}f_{\rm v})(x)=(\mathcal{A}f_{\rm v})(x):=\int_{x}^{T}\left(\widetilde{w}(x,s)-\int_{\frac{s-x}{2}}^{\frac{s+x}{2}}\frac{q(\tau)}{2}d\tau\right)f_{\rm v}(s)ds.

One has:

(A^{T}f_{\rm v})^{\prime}(x)=\left(\int_{0}^{x}\frac{q(\tau)}{2}d\tau\right)f_{\rm v}(x)\\
+\int_{x}^{T}\left(\widetilde{w}_{x}(x,s)-\frac{q\left(\frac{s+x}{2}\right)+q\left(\frac{s-x}{2}\right)}{4}\right)f_{\rm v}(s)ds,

(A^{T}f_{\rm v})^{\prime\prime}(x)=(q(x)-\widetilde{w}_{x}(x,x))f_{\rm v}(x)+\left(\int_{0}^{x}\frac{q(\tau)}{2}d\tau\right)f_{\rm v}^{\prime}(x)\\
+\left(\frac{q\left(\frac{T-x}{2}\right)-q\left(\frac{T+x}{2}\right)}{4}\right)f_{\rm v}(T)\\
+\int_{x}^{T}\widetilde{w}_{xx}(x,s)f_{\rm v}(s)ds-\int_{x}^{T}\left(\frac{q\left(\frac{s+x}{2}\right)-q\left(\frac{s-x}{2}\right)}{4}\right)f_{\rm v}^{\prime}(s)ds.

From these equalities it is immedately clear that
A^{T}
is a bounded operator in
\mathcal{H}^{2}([0,T];\mathbb{C}^{n})
.

The expression
\mathcal{A}
defines several operators in different spaces. In
\mathcal{L}_{2}([0,T];\mathbb{C}^{n})
it defines the operator
A^{T}_{\mathcal{L}}
which is a Volterra integral operator, thus
(I+A^{T}_{\mathcal{L}})^{-1}
exists, is bounded and the Neumann’s series converges in
\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{L}_{2}([0,T];\mathbb{C}^{n}))
:

(I+A^{T}_{\mathcal{L}})^{-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}(A^{T}_{\mathcal{L}})^{n}=I-A^{T}_{\mathcal{L}}+(A^{T}_{\mathcal{L}})^{2}-(A^{T}_{\mathcal{L}})^{3}(I+A^{T}_{\mathcal{L}})^{-1}.

(33)

Let us show that the expression
\mathcal{A}
defines bounded operators

1.

from
\mathcal{L}_{2}([0,T];\mathbb{C}^{n})
to
\mathcal{C}([0,T];\mathbb{C}^{n})
,

2.

from
\mathcal{C}([0,T];\mathbb{C}^{n})
to
\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T];\mathbb{C}^{n})
,

3.

form
\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T];\mathbb{C}^{n})
to
\mathcal{H}^{2}([0,T];\mathbb{C}^{n})
.

These operators and
A^{T}
can be regarded as restrictions of
A^{T}_{\mathcal{L}}
.
Denote

\displaystyle a_{1}

\displaystyle:=1/2\|q\|_{\mathcal{L}_{1}([0,T];\mathbb{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}})},

\displaystyle a_{2}

\displaystyle:=\|q\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}([0,T];\mathbb{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}})},

\displaystyle b_{1}

\displaystyle:=\|\widetilde{w}\|_{\mathcal{C}(\{(x,t)\,|\,t\in[0,T],x\in[0,t]\};\mathbb{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}})},

\displaystyle b_{2}

\displaystyle:=\|\widetilde{w}_{x}\|_{\mathcal{C}(\{(x,t)\,|\,t\in[0,T],x\in[0,t]\};\mathbb{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}})},

\displaystyle b_{3}

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{T}dx\left(\int_{x}^{T}\|\widetilde{w}_{xx}(x,t)\|_{\mathbb{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}}dt\right)^{2}.

We need to show that
b_{3}
is finite. Since
\widetilde{w}_{xx}(x,t)=\widetilde{w}_{tt}(x,t)+q(x)w(x,t)
,
w
is continuous and
q\in\mathcal{L}_{2}([0,T];\mathbb{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}})
, we need to prove that

\int_{0}^{T}dx\left(\int_{x}^{T}\|\widetilde{w}_{tt}(x,t)\|_{\mathbb{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}}dt\right)^{2}<\infty.

It is enough to show that
\int_{x}^{T}\|\widetilde{w}_{tt}(x,t)\|_{\mathbb{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}}dt
is a bounded function of
x\in[0,T]
. Looking at (
28
) and (
26
)–(
27
) to estimate
\hat{w}
, with

b_{4}:=\|w\|_{\mathcal{C}(\{(x,t)\,|\,t\in[0,T],x\in[0,t]\};\mathbb{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}})},

for every
x\in[0,T]
one can write, using the estimates from the proof of Lemma
1
:

\int_{x}^{T}\|\widetilde{w}_{tt}(x,t)\|_{\mathbb{M}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}}dt\leqslant 2a_{1}b_{4}+\frac{3a_{1}^{2}}{4}+\frac{4a_{1}^{2}+2a_{1}^{2}}{8}+\frac{9a_{1}^{2}b_{4}}{4}.

From this one has
b_{3}<\infty
. Then under corresponding assumptions about
f_{\rm v}
the following estimates hold:

i.

\|A^{T}f_{\rm v}\|_{\mathcal{C}}\leqslant(a_{1}+b_{1})\|f_{\rm v}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{1}}\leqslant(a_{1}+b_{1})\sqrt{T}\|f_{\rm v}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}
,

ii.

\|(A^{T}f_{\rm v})^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{C}}\leqslant a_{1}\|f_{\rm v}\|_{\mathcal{C}}+b_{2}T\|f_{\rm v}\|_{\mathcal{C}}+2a_{1}\|f_{\rm v}\|_{\mathcal{C}}=(3a_{1}+b_{2}T)\|f_{\rm v}\|_{\mathcal{C}}
,

\|A^{T}f_{\rm v}\|_{\mathcal{C}}\leqslant(a_{1}+b_{1})\|f_{\rm v}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{1}}\leqslant(a_{1}+b_{1})T\|f_{\rm v}\|_{\mathcal{C}}
,

iii.

\|(A^{T}f_{\rm v})^{\prime\prime}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}\leqslant(a_{2}+b_{2}\sqrt{T})\|f_{\rm v}\|_{\mathcal{C}}+a_{1}\sqrt{T}\|f_{\rm v}^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{C}}+2a_{1}\|f_{\rm v}\|_{\mathcal{C}}+\sqrt{b}_{3}\|f_{\rm v}\|_{\mathcal{C}}+2a_{1}\|f_{\rm v}^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{C}}\leqslant(4a_{1}+a_{2}+(a_{1}+b_{2})\sqrt{T}+\sqrt{b_{3}})\|f_{\rm v}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{1}}
,

\|A^{T}f_{\rm v}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}\leqslant\sqrt{T}\|A^{T}f_{\rm v}\|_{\mathcal{C}}\leqslant(a_{1}+b_{1})T^{\frac{3}{2}}\|f_{\rm v}\|_{\mathcal{C}}\leqslant(a_{1}+b_{1})T^{\frac{3}{2}}\|f_{\rm v}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{1}}
.

This means that the claims 1.–3. hold true. Therefore
(A^{T}_{\mathcal{L}})^{3}
can be viewed as a bounded operator from
\mathcal{L}_{2}([0,T];\mathbb{C}^{n})
to
\mathcal{H}^{2}([0,T];\mathbb{C}^{n})
. Since the embedding operator
J_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}
of
\mathcal{H}^{2}([0,T];\mathbb{C}^{n})
into
\mathcal{L}_{2}([0,T];\mathbb{C}^{n})
is bounded, from (
33
) one can see that the restriction
(I+A^{T}_{\mathcal{L}})^{-1}\upharpoonright\mathcal{H}^{2}([0,T];\mathbb{C}^{n})
is a bounded operator in
\mathcal{H}^{2}([0,T];\mathbb{C}^{n})
:

(I+A^{T}_{\mathcal{L}})^{-1}\upharpoonright\mathcal{H}^{2}([0,T];\mathbb{C}^{n})=I-A^{T}+(A^{T})^{2}-(A^{T}_{\mathcal{L}})^{3}(I+A^{T}_{\mathcal{L}})^{-1}J_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}.

Together with the fact that
I+A^{T}=(I+A^{T}_{\mathcal{L}})\upharpoonright\mathcal{H}^{2}([0,T];\mathbb{C}^{n})
this implies the equalities

(I+A^{T}_{\mathcal{L}})^{-1}\upharpoonright\mathcal{H}^{2}([0,T];\mathbb{C}^{n})(I+A^{T})=I_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}=(I+A^{T})(I+A^{T}_{\mathcal{L}})^{-1}\upharpoonright\mathcal{H}^{2}([0,T];\mathbb{C}^{n}),

which means that

(I+A^{T}_{\mathcal{L}})^{-1}\upharpoonright\mathcal{H}^{2}([0,T];\mathbb{C}^{n})=(I+A^{T})^{-1}.

It follows that
\tilde{W}^{T}=I+A^{T}
and hence
W^{T}_{\rm v}
are isomorphisms in
\mathcal{H}^{2}([0,T];\mathbb{C}^{n})
. This completes the proof.
∎

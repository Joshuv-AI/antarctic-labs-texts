Pharmaceutical development of composition of effervescent tablets with acetylsalicylic acid, paracetamol, and ascorbic acid
Authors: Oksana Panysheva
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aims to develop and optimize a stable composition of effervescent tablets containing acetylsalicylic acid (300 mg), paracetamol (200 mg), and ascorbic acid (300 mg) for the Ukrainian pharmaceutical market. A systematic pharmaceutical development approach was employed, utilizing dispersion and regression analysis to evaluate the impact of various functional groups of excipients on twelve critical quality indicators. The research included a comprehensive assessment of the tableting blend's pharmaco-technological properties (bulk/tapped density, Carr's index, flowability, and angle of repose) and the finished tablets' quality (pressing process, appearance, mass uniformity, hardness, friability, and disintegration). Organoleptic attributes of the resulting solution, including transparency, odor, and taste, were also evaluated. The initial screening phase identified the most effective types of fillers, disintegrants, and lubricants for direct compression. Experimental data scientifically established the optimal stoichiometric ratio between anhydrous citric acid and sodium bicarbonate as 1:1.312. Subsequent optimization using a utility function and regression analysis allowed for the selection of the best glidants, sweeteners, and flavorings. The interaction between the quantities of a co-processed binder and a sorbitol-based filler was proved to be critical for achieving the required tablet hardness and rapid disintegration. The finalized composition, containing an optimized matrix of anhydrous citric acid, sodium bicarbonate, lactose, polyvinylpyrrolidone, crospovidone, sorbitol, sodium saccharin, lemon flavoring, and sodium stearyl fumarate, successfully met all pharmacopoeial requirements and exceeded the criteria of the Quality Target Product Profile. Long-term stability studies confirmed a 24-month shelf life in aluminum-polymer packaging. The study resulted in a scientifically validated composition that provides a fast-acting, consumer-friendly, and cost-effective domestic alternative to imported analgesic and antipyretic combinations, significantly enhancing patient compliance and accessibility within the local market.Keywords: Pharmaceutical development, Effervescent tablets, Quality by design, Dispersion analysis, Regression analysis

Introduction

The evolution of oral dosage forms has consistently aimed to balance patient convenience with optimal pharmacokinetic performance. According to the Food and Drug Administration and the European Pharmacopoeia, effervescent tablets are defined as solid dosage forms designed to dissolve or disperse in water before use, typically containing acidic substances and carbonates that react rapidly to release carbon dioxide [1,2]. This category of pharmaceuticals is experiencing a significant surge in popularity among clinicians and patients due to several biopharmaceutical advantages. The primary benefit is the accelerated release of the active pharmaceutical ingredient (API), bypassing the rate-limiting disintegration step required for conventional tablets [3,4]. This results in faster absorption and an earlier onset of therapeutic action, which is critical for acute conditions such as pain and fever [5,6]. Furthermore, administering the drug in a pre-dissolved state minimizes the localized irritation of the gastrointestinal mucosa, a common drawback of solid acetylsalicylic acid (ASA) [7,8]. From a clinical perspective, effervescent forms enhance patient compliance, particularly in pediatric and geriatric populations where swallowing large tablets (dysphagia) is a major barrier to adherence [4,9]. They also facilitate the conversion of poorly soluble compounds into solution via salt formation and carbon dioxide-induced solubilization, while simultaneously allowing for the correction of unpleasant organoleptic properties through sophisticated flavoring systems [8,10,11].

The manufacturing of effervescent tablets is a sophisticated technological process requiring a complex multicomponent system, typically involving 5 to 15 functional ingredients [12,13]. The core functionality is driven by a gas-forming mixture (acidic and alkaline fractions), which usually constitutes 80%-95% of the total tablet mass [10,13]. While anhydrous citric acid and sodium bicarbonate remain the most common agents, recent advances explore organic acids like fumaric, malic, or tartaric acid to optimize reaction kinetics and the final solution's pH [7,14]. To prevent premature interaction between these highly hygroscopic fractions during storage, the role of stabilizing agents is paramount. High-molecular compounds, such as polyvinylpyrrolidone (Povidone® K25) and various polyethylene glycols (PEGs) (PEG 6000), function as essential binders that also provide a protective barrier against moisture [6,10]. A critical requirement for this technology is the selection of fillers with high water solubility and superior compressibility for direct compression [11,15]. Functional excipients like anhydrous lactose (SuperTab® 22AN), lactose monohydrate (Tablettose® 80), and polyols such as sorbitol (Neosorb® P60W, Parteck® SI400) and mannitol (Perlitol® 300DC) play a dual role as structure-formers and dissolution promoters [[3], [4], [5]]. Specialized fillers, including dextrose (Compri-Zuger®), sugar spheres (Scerinerts® 50), and microcrystalline cellulose micropellets (Cellets® 100), are often utilized to enhance tablet hardness, which is frequently a weak point in effervescent systems [6,11]. To achieve rapid disintegration, the multifunctional co-processed excipient Ludipress® or superdisintegrants like crospovidone (Polyplasdone® XL-10) are vital for accelerating the matrix breakup [10]. Furthermore, technological challenges like blend adhesion to the tablet press necessitate water-soluble lubricants; hence, traditional magnesium stearate is strictly avoided due to its hydrophobicity and soapy taste, replaced by sodium stearyl fumarate or sodium lauryl sulfate to ensure solution transparency [8,10,13]. The fluidity of these hygroscopic blends is further maintained by advanced glidants like Aerosil® 200 or magnesium aluminosilicate (Neuselin® US2), while the organoleptic profile is refined using high-intensity sweeteners like sodium saccharin or aspartame and fruit flavorings (orange, lemon, etc.) [2,9,14].

The therapeutic rationale for this study is rooted in the well-documented clinical synergy of ASA, paracetamol, and ascorbic acid. This triad provides a dual-action analgesic and antipyretic mechanism (acting on both central and peripheral pathways) while supporting the immune system during inflammatory processes [16,17]. Internationally, this combination is a gold standard for treating flu, colds, and migraines, marketed under prestigious brands such as Afebryl® (Belgium, Portugal), Isofebril® vitamine C (France), Gripral + C ratiopharm® (Germany), Neo-Nisidina® C (Italy), and Thomapyrin® mit Ascorbinsäure (Austria, Bulgaria). Despite its global success, this specific therapeutic combination is currently absent from the Ukrainian pharmaceutical market, representing a significant therapeutic gap [12,16]. In this research, this triad was intentionally selected as a “research object of maximum complexity” to investigate the technological boundaries of direct compression [15,18,19]. These three APIs possess vastly different physical properties – varying in solubility, particle morphology, and compressibility – making the achievement of blend homogeneity and stable flow a rigorous test for modern excipient matrices [20]. Developing a domestic product carries substantial socio-economic value, offering a more cost-effective and accessible alternative to imported analogs while simplifying the treatment regimen for local patients [12,21]. By utilizing a Quality by Design (QbD) approach with dispersion and regression analysis, we aim to overcome the inherent instability of ascorbic acid and the hygroscopicity of the effervescent base [14].

Consequently, the objective of this study is to develop a scientifically validated composition and direct compression technology for obtaining stable effervescent tablets containing 300 mg of ASA, 200 mg of paracetamol, and 300 mg of ascorbic acid. The research focuses on ensuring high-quality appearance, solution transparency, and pleasant organoleptic properties while strictly adhering to pharmacopoeial requirements for mass uniformity, mechanical hardness, friability, and rapid disintegration.

Materials and methods

Materials

Powders of ASA, paracetamol, and ascorbic acid were used as active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) for the development of effervescent tablets. The study utilized ASA manufactured by Shandong Xihua Pharmaceutical Co. Ltd. (China), paracetamol by Hebei Jiheng (Group) Pharmaceutical Co. Ltd. (China), and ascorbic acid by DSM Nutritional Products (Switzerland). The ASA powder is a white, odorless crystalline powder; however, in a humid environment (e.g., contact with air), it acquires an acetic acid odor. It consists of oval-shaped particles with smooth edges (D10 = 219 μm, D50 = 703 μm, D90 = 1330 μm). The ASA powder is characterized by a bulk density of 0.6667 ± 0.0230 g/ml, tapped density of 0.8571 ± 0.0571 g/ml, Carr's index of 24.84% ± 2.30%, flowability of 7.7 ± 1.5 s/100 g, and an angle of repose of 37.2 ± 2.9°. The paracetamol is a white amorphous powder with elongated particles (D10 = 5.84 μm, D50 = 40.4 μm, and D90 = 208 μm), providing a bulk density of 0.3850 ± 0.0127 g/ml, tapped density of 0.5934 ± 0.0101 g/ml, Carr's index of 54.20% ± 2.48%. The powder exhibits no flowability (0 s/100 g) and has an angle of repose of (11.6 ± 4.6) ° of the powder. The ascorbic acid is a white crystalline powder consisting of cubic crystals (D10 = 72.8 μm, D50 = 265 μm, and D90 = 523 μm). It is characterized by a bulk density of 0.9329 ± 0.0074 g/ml, tapped density of 1.1545 ± 0.0115 g/ml, Carr's index of 23.76% ± 0.23%, flowability of 11.4 ± 0.8 s/100 g, and angle of repose of 38.3 ± 2.7°. One tablet contains 300 mg ASA, 200 mg paracetamol, and 300 mg ascorbic acid.

The following excipients were investigated: sodium bicarbonate, anhydrous citric acid, anhydrous lactose (SuperTab® 22AN), lactose monohydrate (Tablettose® 80), sorbitol (Neosorb® P60W and Parteck® SI400), mannitol (Perlitol® 300DC), dextrose (Compri-Zuger® B, M3, and O), sugar spheres (Scerinerts® 50), microcrystalline cellulose micropellets (Cellets® 100), polyvinylpyrrolidone (Povidone® K25), crospovidone (Polyplasdone® XL-10), sodium croscarmellose, and Ludipress® (93% lactose, 3.5% polyvinylpyrrolidone (Kollidon® 30), and 3.5% crospovidone (Kollidon® CL)). Additional components included sodium lauryl sulfate, sodium stearyl fumarate (Pruv®), PEG 6000, colloidal anhydrous silicon dioxide (Aerosil® 200 and Aeroperl® 300), magnesium aluminosilicate (Neuselin® US2), sodium saccharin, aspartame, and flavorings (lemon, orange, lemon-lime). All materials were of pharmaceutical grade.

Packaging utilized pharmaceutical-grade blister packages manufactured from aluminum-polymer laminate foils (cold-form foil) and aluminum foil, ensuring superior moisture and light barriers in compliance with current pharmacopoeial standards.

The list of excipients for the reference products was compiled from official instructions for use and summary of product characteristics. Statistical planning and data processing, including dispersion and regression analyses, were performed in accordance with established pharmaceutical guidelines [18,19].

Method of dispersion analysis

Dispersion analysis was employed to statistically identify the influence of various factors and their interactions on the variability of the studied pharmaceutical features. The initial screening phase for the effervescent tablet formulation utilized a randomized 3х3 Greek-Latin square design. This orthogonal matrix was selected for its high efficiency in multivariable optimization, allowing for the simultaneous evaluation of three independent factors at three levels through only 9 experimental runs. This significantly reduced material consumption compared to the 27 runs required for a full factorial (33) design.

To ensure statistical reliability and reproducibility, each experimental run was performed in duplicate (n = 2), and the order of the runs was fully randomized to eliminate systematic bias. The criteria for selecting factor levels were justified by a pre-formulation market analysis of reference products and preliminary compressibility studies. The inherent balance of the design ensured that each factor level appeared exactly once in combination with others, providing an independent estimation of each ingredient's effect and preventing the confounding of variables. Furthermore, the systematic distribution of factors effectively mitigated background noise from environmental fluctuations, such as minor variations in ambient humidity, which is critical for moisture-sensitive effervescent mixtures.

Experimental data were subjected to dispersion analysis using STATISTICA® 10.0 (StatSoft Inc., USA) and Microsoft Excel 2019. Factors were considered statistically significant in cases where Fexperimental > Ftabular (p = 0.05). For all significant factors, a ranked series of advantages was constructed to determine their specific influence on the critical pharmaco-technological indicators of the tableting blend, effervescent tablets, and the resulting solution [18,19].

Utility function

The utility function was employed to identify the optimal excipients by summing the ordinal numbers of the factor levels within a ranked series of advantages [19]. In this approach, each quality indicator was treated as a Critical Quality Attribute (CQA) with equal statistical weight. This methodology was selected for the screening and optimization phases to ensure a robust balance across all studied parameters, including pharmacopoeial standards (mass uniformity, hardness, friability, and disintegration) and organoleptic properties (taste and odor). The final selection was strictly restricted to formulations that met the predefined mandatory limits for each critical parameter, ensuring that no single quality attribute was compromised during the optimization process.

Method of regression analysis

Quantitative characteristics of the excipients and the interrelationships between factors were investigated using regression analysis. For this purpose, a randomized symmetric rotatable composite second-order design was employed in two distinct stages. At the initial stage, four quantitative factors were studied using a matrix of 20 experimental runs, which included 4 replicates at the central (basic) level to determine the variance of reproducibility and ensure statistical precision. During the subsequent optimization stage, two quantitative factors were evaluated using a matrix of 10 experimental runs, with 2 replicates at the central level. The sequence of all experiments across both stages was fully randomized to eliminate systematic bias. Regression analysis enabled the development of mathematical models in the form of polynomial equations. The model coefficients, variance, standard error, and statistical significance were calculated using Student's t-test (t5 = 2.571; p = 0.05). Model adequacy was verified via the Fisher criterion (F-test), where F0.05;15;3 = 8.7 for the four-factor quantitative study and F0,1;6;1 = 58.204 for the two-factor composition optimization. Significant coefficients were used to construct the final regression equations. A regression model was considered adequate if the calculated F-value did not exceed the tabular value for the selected significance level (p < 0.05 or p < 0.01). To assess factor interactions, graphical univariate dependencies were constructed by stabilizing other factors at a constant level. Furthermore, contour plots were generated in the plane of intersection of two factors to visually identify a compromise solution for the optimization problem. To determine the optimal excipient ratios, regression equations were transformed into canonical expressions. Provided that bii>0 and |bi|-∑|bij|>2|bii|, the value + 1 was entered into the model for xi if bі was positive. The coordinates of the intersection of the lines derived from the transformed equations correspond to the quantities of excipients providing the optimal quality indicators [18,19].

Tablet preparation

The effervescent tablets, containing 300 mg of ASA, 200 mg of paracetamol, and 300 mg of ascorbic acid, were manufactured using the direct compression method. Accurate weighing of all components was ensured using an electronic balance PB8001-S (Mettler Toledo, Switzerland). All ingredients were blended in a specific sequence using a container blender CML 10 (Glatt, Germany) to ensure optimal homogeneity of the multicomponent mixture. The resulting blend was compressed into tablets with a diameter of 17 mm and an average weight of 1500 mg using a rotary tablet press XL 100 (Korsch, Germany), with compression forces ranging from 10 kN to 25 kN. The finished effervescent tablets were then packed into blisters using a Fantasy blister machine (Fantasy Standard OMAR S.r.l., Italy) to provide a robust barrier against environmental factors, ensuring the stability of the moisture-sensitive formulation.

Pharmaco-technological and phisico-chemical properties

To evaluate the pharmaco-technological properties of the powders, the tableting blend was tested for bulk density, tapped density, and Carr's index using a tapped density tester SVM 202 (ERWEKA GmbH, Germany). Flowability and the angle of repose were determined using a flow tester GTB (ERWEKA GmbH, Germany). According to the established gradation, a Carr's index value in the range of 12%-16% corresponds to good flowability, 18%-21% to fair/passable, and 23%-35% to poor flowability [8,15,22]. Flowability and the angle of repose were expressed as the average of three independent measurements [22]. A complex indicator on a five-point scale, accounting for matrix filling uniformity, adhesion (sticking), and extrusion force, was utilized to evaluate the compression process. The finished effervescent tablets were characterized by appearance, mass uniformity, hardness, friability, and disintegration time. The appearance was determined visually in daylight on a white background, assessing the surface integrity, smoothness, glossiness, and edge evenness. These attributes, along with the presence of inclusions, were rated on a five-point scale. Quality attributes were studied using an analytical balance AB54-S (Mettler Toledo, Switzerland), a tablet hardness tester Easy Check Pro (ERWEKA GmbH, Germany), and a friability tester TAR 200 (ERWEKA GmbH, Germany). All pharmaco-technological evaluations were performed in accordance with pharmacopoeial requirements [22]. Disintegration was defined as the time until the cessation of effervescence (absence of fizzing) in a beaker containing 200 ml of water at (20 ± 2) °C [22]. The target pharmacopoeial limits were: mass uniformity ≤ ±5%, hardness ≥50 N (for 17 mm diameter), friability ≤1%, and disintegration ≤5 min [22]. Organoleptic evaluation of the resulting solution was conducted using a five-point scoring system for transparency, odor, and taste. A score of 5 points was assigned for a perfectly clear solution with a distinct pleasant aroma and a harmonious sweet-sour taste. A score of 4 points indicated slight opalescence and an acceptable taste with a faint medicinal aftertaste. Lower scores (3 to 1 points) characterized increasing turbidity, medicinal or repulsive odors, and pronounced bitterness or heavy precipitation, rendering the solution unsuitable for use. For the stability study, the tablets were subjected to long-term storage conditions at (25 ± 2) °C and (60 ± 5) % relative humidity [23].

The physico-chemical attributes of the optimized formulation, including related substances (degradation products), uniformity of dosage units, and API assay, were determined using High-Performance Liquid Chromatography on an Agilent 1260 Infinity system (Agilent Technologies, USA). For the simultaneous determination of ASA, paracetamol, and their primary degradation products – salicylic acid and 4-aminophenol, as well as other and total impurities – crushed tablets were dissolved in a mixture of water and acetonitrile (80:20 v/v). The analysis was performed using a Mediterranea SEA18 column (4.6 × 250 mm, 5 μm). The mobile phase consisted of a buffer solution (pH 3.0) and acetonitrile (80:20 v/v) at a flow rate of 1.0 ml/min. Detection was performed at 225 nm with an injection volume of 10 μl and a total run time of 20 min. For the determination of ascorbic acid, detection was conducted at 210 nm using a SphereClone NH2 column (4.6 × 250 mm, 5 μm). The mobile phase was adjusted to a phosphate buffer–acetonitrile mixture (35:65 v/v). The uniformity of dosage units was assessed according to pharmacopoeial requirements [22] via the direct High-Performance Liquid Chromatography method under the same chromatographic conditions as the API assay [24].

For the stability study, tablets were stored under long-term conditions at (25 ± 2) °C and (60 ± 5) % RH for 24 months. Samples were analyzed every 3 months in the first year and every 6 months in the second year for all CQAs mentioned above [23].

Results and discussion

Qualitative composition screening

Comprehensive analysis of the excipient profiles in reference marketed products

An analysis of registered products with identical active ingredient dosages (300 mg ASA, 200 mg paracetamol, and 300 mg ascorbic acid), such as Afebryl®, Isofebril® vitamine C, Gripрal + C ratiopharm®, Neo-Nisidina® C, and Thomapyrin® mit Ascorbinsäure, revealed that a high-quality effervescent form requires a balanced combination of specific functional groups. Specifically, Afebryl® (Laboratórios Azevedos – Indústria Farmacêutica, S.A.) contains sodium carbonate, anhydrous citric acid, 83 mg of sorbitol, 94 mg of lactose monohydrate, sodium saccharin, lemon essence, and 8 mg of povidone. Isofebril® vitamine C (Plus Pharmacie SA) includes sodium carbonate, citric acid, lactose, sodium saccharin, lemon essential oil powder, sorbitol, and 8 mg of povidone. Gripрal + C ratiopharm® (Ratiopharm) utilizes sodium carbonate, citric acid, sorbitol, Povidone® K25, lactose monohydrate, lemon oil, and sodium saccharin. Neo-Nisidina® C (Boehringer Ingelheim Italia s.p.a.) is formulated with sodium carbonate, citric acid, lactose, lemon flavoring, sodium saccharin, polyvinylpyrrolidone, and fumaric acid. Thomapyrin® mit Ascorbinsäure (Boehringer Ingelheim RCV GmbH & Co KG) contains 200 mg of lactose monohydrate, 1550 mg of sodium carbonate, 2.6 mg of fructose, sodium saccharin, citric acid, Povidone® K25, fumaric acid, and lemon flavoring. It was established that these industrial formulations typically consist of a gas-forming mixture (such as 1550 mg of sodium carbonate with citric or fumaric acid), fillers (94-200 mg of lactose monohydrate, 83 mg of sorbitol, or 2.6 mg of fructose), disintegrants or binding agents (Povidone® K25, polyvinylpyrrolidone), sodium saccharin, and lemon flavoring. In this study, the choice of sodium bicarbonate and anhydrous citric acid as the primary gas-forming couple was based on their dominant use in these analogs and their ability to provide the optimal intensity of carbon dioxide release. The prioritized use of anhydrous forms was necessitated by the need to minimize moisture content within the tablet matrix to prevent premature autocatalytic reactions.

Evaluation of the influence of excipient nature on the CQAs of the tableting blend, effervescent tablets, and resulting solution using dispersion analysis

At the first stage of research, the influence of excipients from the groups of fillers, disintegrants, and lubricants on the quality indicators of the tableting blend, effervescent tablets, and solution was investigated. The selection of these specific excipients was strategically guided by their established suitability for direct compression and their chemical compatibility with effervescent matrices, derived from the market analysis described above. To systematically evaluate the impact of different chemical structures, each group included three excipients of diverse nature. The fillers (factor A), such as anhydrous lactose and sorbitol, were selected based on their prevalence in commercial analogs (such as Afebryl® and Thomapyrin®) and their specialized grades for direct compression, ensuring high water solubility, low hygroscopicity, and superior compressibility. Fructose, which is found in Thomapyrin® but is rarely used in effervescent tablets, was substituted with mannitol as a more chemically stable and versatile choice for modern effervescent matrices. For disintegrants (factor B), Povidone® K25 was chosen as a benchmark due to its presence in market formulations, where it functions not only as a binder but also as a soluble disintegrant that contributes to masking the medicinal taste of APIs. To enhance the disintegration process, it was evaluated alongside modern superdisintegrants with different functional mechanisms: crospovidone and sodium croscarmellose. Crospovidone provides rapid wicking (capillary action) without significant swelling, which is beneficial for maintaining the structural integrity of the effervescent matrix during gas evolution, while sodium croscarmellose offers high swelling capacity to accelerate tablet breakup. This combination allowed for a comparative analysis of how varying disintegration kinetics affect the dissolution rate of the multi-component ASA-paracetamol-ascorbic acid system. Regarding lubricants (factor C), the selection was strictly limited to hydrophilic or water-soluble substances commonly used in the manufacture of effervescent tablets. Sodium lauryl sulfate, sodium stearyl fumarate, and PEG 6000 were selected as technically compatible alternatives that ensure a clear and palatable solution. This systematic selection, based on the QbD approach, allowed for the construction of a robust 3x3 Greco-Latin square experimental design, ensuring that each factor level was scientifically justified by prior knowledge and the technological requirements of the dosage form. A mixture of 159 mg (10.6%) sodium bicarbonate and 135 mg (9.0%) anhydrous citric acid was used as the gas-forming mixture. Quantitative composition development

Determination of the stoichiometric ratio for the gas-forming mixture (citric acid/sodium bicarbonate)

The selection of an optimal ratio for the gas-forming mixture is a critical stage in the formulation of effervescent tablets. In the initial experimental batches (1-9), a mixture of 135 mg (9.0%) anhydrous citric acid and 159 mg (10.6%) sodium bicarbonate was utilized, representing a mass ratio of 1:1.178. While this combination ensured compliance with pharmacopoeial disintegration standards (ranging from 0.28 to 3.42 min), it was primarily empirical and required further refinement to ensure chemical efficiency.

To achieve a scientifically justified balance and ensure a complete chemical reaction without unreacted residues, a stoichiometric calculation was performed. Based on the molar masses of anhydrous citric acid (C6H8O7, 192.123 g/mol) and sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO3, 84.01 g/mol), the interaction follows the equation: 3NaHCO3+ HOC-CH3-CH3-(COOH)3 = HOC-CH3-CH3-(COONa)3+ 3H2O + 3CO2. According to this stoichiometry, 192.123 g of C6H8O7 reacts equivalently with 252.03 g of NaHCO3 (3х84.01 g), yielding a precise mass ratio of 1:1.312. This theoretically justified ratio was subsequently implemented in all following experimental series to maximize effervescence efficiency and ensure the pH stability of the resulting solution. The transition from the 1:1.178 ratio to the stoichiometric 1:1.312 ratio allowed for a more controlled and reproducible disintegration process.

Selection of excipient concentrations that ensure superior pharmaco-technological properties

As a result of the experimental series, the following excipient quantities providing the optimal pharmaco-technological indicators for the tableting blend and effervescent tablets were selected: 105 mg of anhydrous citric acid, 153 mg of sodium bicarbonate, 30 mg of Polyplasdone® XL-10, and 37.5 mg of Pruv®. The mixture exhibited good compressibility, and the appearance of the effervescent tablets was rated at 4 points. The obtained tablets demonstrated a mass uniformity of ±0.41%, hardness of 57.17 N, and a rapid disintegration time of 28 s. However, the friability (2.0%) exceeded pharmacopoeial limits, and the resulting solution was cloudy with visible suspensions, possessing an unpleasant bitter taste.

Formulation optimization

Analysis of additional excipients to enhance the technological and organoleptic properties (glidants, sweeteners, and flavorings) via dispersion analysis

To further optimize the tableting blend and enhance the consumer attributes of the effervescent tablets, the next stage of research focused on flowability and organoleptic properties. To improve the fluidity of the multicomponent mixture and prevent the adhesion of granules to the punch surfaces, three glidants (factor A) were investigated: colloidal anhydrous silicon dioxide (а1 – Aerosil® 200 and а2 – Aeroperl® 300) and а3 – magnesium aluminosilicate (Neuselin® US2). These excipients were selected for their high specific surface area and their ability to minimize interparticulate friction, which is critical for maintaining a stable flow during the direct compression of hygroscopic effervescent components. To effectively mask the characteristic bitter taste of paracetamol and ASA, the formulation was supplemented with sweeteners (factor B) and flavorings (factor C), based on the organoleptic profiles of market analogs (e.g., Afebryl®, Thomapyrin®). Sodium saccharin (b1) and aspartame (b2) were selected as high-intensity sweeteners, while b3 – dextrose (Compri-Zuger® B) was included as a multifunctional agent providing both sweetness and structural bulk for direct compression. The choice of flavorings (с1 – lemon, с2 – orange, and с3 – lemon-lime) was guided by their superior ability to complement the natural acidity of the effervescent base, providing a pleasant sensory experience upon dissolution. Based on the preliminary results, which showed rapid disintegration within 1 min, the quantity of the superdisintegrant Polyplasdone® XL-10 was adjusted to 8 mg per tablet to balance the disintegration rate with the mechanical strength of the tablet. To achieve a target average weight of 1500 mg, varying quantities of Compri-Zuger®B were added to each batch as a bulking agent. Final formulation and validation

Experimental validation of the optimized composition

Based on the multi-stage experimental design, an optimal composition for effervescent tablets was proposed, containing 300 mg of ASA, 200 mg of paracetamol, and 300 mg of ascorbic acid. The matrix also included 105 mg of anhydrous citric acid, 153 mg of sodium bicarbonate, 193.7 mg of Ludipress®, 178.4 mg of sorbitol (Parteck® SI400), 5.4 mg of sodium saccharin, 27 mg of lemon flavoring, and 37.5 mg of sodium stearyl fumarate (Pruv®). The experimental reproduction of this formulation confirmed its superior technological properties: bulk density was 0.675 g/ml, tapped density 0.880 g/ml, Carr's index 26.2%, flowability 15.3 s/100 g, and the angle of repose was 40.5°. The pressing process and the appearance of the tablets were rated at 5 points. The finished tablets demonstrated excellent mechanical and pharmacopoeial characteristics, including mass uniformity (±0.6%), hardness (53 N), friability (0.6%), and rapid disintegration (1 min 12 s). The resulting solution had a pleasant odor and a pleasant sweet-sour taste. The obtained indicators met all pharmacopoeial requirements and complied with the criteria of the Quality Target Product Profile.

Long-term stability testing and final characterization of the dosage form

Since ascorbic acid is an inherently unstable compound that undergoes rapid oxidation and degradation under high temperature and humidity [20], accelerated stability studies were not conducted. Instead, to determine the shelf life, the tablets were packed in blister packages manufactured from aluminum-polymer laminate foils and aluminum foil [21]. This choice of packaging is scientifically justified by the need for a high moisture and light barrier, as the multi-layer aluminum laminate (cold-form foil) provides near-zero water vapor transmission rates, protecting the hygroscopic effervescent couple and preventing the catalytic degradation of ASA and ascorbic acid. The tablets were subjected to long-term storage at (25 ± 2) °C and (60 ± 5) % relative humidity [23]. Monitoring was performed over 24 months, with sampling every 3 months in the first year and every 6 months in the second year. Critical parameters such as appearance, average weight, related substances, disintegration, uniformity of dosage units, and API assay were evaluated (Table 11). The results prove that at 25 °C, the formulation remains within the established specification limits for the entire 24-month period, confirming the absence of detrimental interactions between the APIs and excipients and ensuring the product's commercial viability.

Limitation

In the present study, a stable composition and direct compression technology for effervescent tablets containing acetylsalicylic acid, paracetamol, and ascorbic acid were successfully developed. However, certain technical limitations must be considered for industrial scale-up. Specifically, for the proposed formulation, maintaining the compression force within the established range (10–25 kN) is critical. Deviations beyond these limits can significantly affect the CQAs of the final product, such as mechanical hardness and friability. Furthermore, due to the high hygroscopicity of the effervescent components, the environmental humidity during the manufacturing process must be strictly controlled to prevent premature degradation. Therefore, continuous monitoring of tablet hardness and mass uniformity is essential during the compression stage to ensure the reproducibility of the optimized formulation.

Conclusion

A stable and scientifically substantiated composition for effervescent tablets containing acetylsalicylic acid, paracetamol, and ascorbic acid was successfully developed and experimentally validated. Using a systematic QbD approach, including dispersion and regression analyses, the influence of the nature and concentration of various excipients on the pharmaco-technological and organoleptic characteristics of the tableting blend and finished tablets was comprehensively investigated. The optimal combination of anhydrous citric acid, sodium bicarbonate, Ludipress®, sorbitol (Parteck® SI400), sodium saccharin, lemon flavoring, and sodium stearyl fumarate (Pruv®) was justified. The resulting formulation ensures a high-quality consumer appearance, solution transparency, and pleasant taste, while strictly adhering to all pharmacopoeial requirements. The developed formulation provides a fast-acting and accessible domestic alternative for fever reduction, pain relief, and immune support, particularly for headaches, toothaches, and symptoms of flu and colds. The study demonstrated that the integrated use of statistical methods, such as the utility function and response surface methodology, provides a robust algorithm for the pharmaceutical development of complex multicomponent dosage forms.

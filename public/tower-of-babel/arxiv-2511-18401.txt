Periodicity characterization by capacities for star-shaped domains
Jean Gutt, Vinicius G. B. Ramos, Shira Tanny

Abstract
We complete the spectral characterization of Besse and Zoll Reeb flows on the standard contact sphere S 2 ​ n − 1 S^{2n-1} initiated by Ginzburg–Gürel–Mazzucchelli. Roughly speaking, it states that a Reeb flow on the boundary of any star-shaped domain in ℝ 2 ​ n {\mathbb{R}}^{2n} is Besse if and only if it has n n coinciding Ekeland–Hofer capacities, and that it is Zoll if and only if the first n n capacities coincide.

Introduction and results

A closed contact manifold
(Y,\alpha)
is called
Besse
if all its Reeb orbits are closed, and
Zoll
if, in addition, all orbits have the same minimal period. On
Y=S^{2n-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2n}
with its standard contact structure, contact forms are in bijection with star-shaped domains in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
. Here and throughout a star-shaped domain is a contractible domain containing the origin, whose boundary is smooth and transverse to the radial vector field

In other texts, such domains are sometimes called “strictly star-shaped” or domains whose boundary is a “restricted contact type hypersurface”.

In recent years there have been several works aimed at characterizing and classifying Besse or Zoll flows. In fact, it is an open question whether on
S^{2n-1}
with the standard structure, there are other Besse contact forms beyond boundaries of ellipsoids and Zoll contact forms beyond the round sphere
[
14

. In dimension 3, classification results for Besse and Zoll contact forms (on the sphere as well as on other contact manifolds) were obtained by Cristofaro-Gardiner and Mazzucchelli

. For convex domains, they obtained a complete characterization: The flow is periodic if and only if there are

For non-convex star-shaped domains they use the Ekeland–Hofer capacities

Theorem

(

If

We study the characterization of periodicity via equivariant symplectic homology capacities, which we denote by

Theorem 1

Let

c_{k}(\alpha)=\cdots=c_{k+n-1}(\alpha)=\mathcal{T}.

In fact,

Theorem

Theorem 2
.

Let
W\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
be a Besse star-shaped domain and suppose that the manifold of (parametrized) Reeb orbits of period
T
is of dimension
2d+1
. Then

c_{k}(\alpha)=\cdots=c_{k+d}(\alpha)=T\quad\text{for}\quad k=\frac{1}{2}(\mu_{-}(S_{T})-n+1).

This result implies that the capacity-spectrum of a Besse star-shaped domain coincides with the usual spectrum (i.e., the set of periods) and the number of repetitions of each period depends on the dimension of the space of orbits of that period.
We also show that coincidence of the first
n
capacities implies the Zoll property.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose that
W\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
is a star-shaped domain such that

c_{1}(W)=\cdots=c_{n}(W).

Then, the Reeb flow on
\partial W
is Zoll and
c_{1}(W)
is the minimal period.

Together with

stated above, Theorems

Finally, our methods also allow us to determine the topological structure of the Morse–Bott families of Reeb orbits on Besse star-shaped domains.

Theorem 4
.

Let
W\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
be a Besse star-shaped domain and denote by
\varphi^{t}:\partial W\rightarrow\partial W
its Reeb flow. For any
T
in the spectrum of the Reeb flow, the fixed point set

Y_{T}=\{z:\varphi^{T}z=z\}\quad\text{is an ${\mathbb{F}}_{2}$-homology sphere, and in particular is connected.}

2.1
The Conley–Zehnder index in the Morse–Bott setting

2.1.1
Morse–Bott star-shaped domains

Let
W
be a star-shaped domain whose Reeb flow
\varphi^{t}
is Besse, namely
\varphi^{\mathcal{T}}=\operatorname{id}
for some
\mathcal{T}>0
.
Any Besse contact form is Morse–Bott nondegenerate, and its flow induces an almost-free
S^{1}
action on
\partial W
. We denote by
\operatorname{Spec}(\partial W)\subset{\mathbb{R}}
the set of periods of periodic orbits. This set is rank-1 (i.e., contained in a rescaling of
{\mathbb{Z}}
) when the flow is Besse.

After rescaling
W
we may assume that
\varphi^{1}=\operatorname{id}
. We will work under this normalization assumption from now on.
For any period
T\in\operatorname{Spec}(\partial W)
of an orbit of
\varphi^{t}
, the set

Y_{T}=\{z:\varphi^{T}z=z\}\quad\text{is a smooth contact manifold}

and
d\varphi^{t}
is nondegenerate in the directions transverse to
Y_{T}
.

Remark 2.1

(Finiteness)
.

For each
T
,
Y_{T}
has finitely many connected components, as it is a smooth manifold. Moreover, the Morse–Bott nondegeneracy guarantees that period spectrum is discrete, and therefore there are finitely many periods up to the common period of the flow (
T\leq 1
). For any period
T>1
, the manifold
Y_{T}
contains no simple orbits.
Overall this implies that there are finitely many connected components of manifold of (parametrized) orbits that contain simple orbits.

The quotient of
Y_{T}
under the almost-free circle action induced by
\varphi^{t}
, denoted by

S_{T}:=\left\{p\in\partial W:\varphi^{T}(p)=p\right\}/\{\varphi^{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}\quad\text{is a symplectic orbifold.}

We sometimes refer to it as the
T
-critical manifold, but will mostly work with the manifold
Y_{T}
, rather than the orbifold quotient.
We will often denote its dimension by
2d(S_{T})
, or simply
2d
when the critical manifold is clear from the context.

2.1.2
Indices of paths of symplectic matrices

Let
\widetilde{\operatorname{Sp}}(2n-2)
be the universal cover of
{\operatorname{Sp}}(2n-2)
. We think of elements of
\widetilde{\operatorname{Sp}}(2n-2)
as paths in
{\operatorname{Sp}}(2n-2)
starting at the identity, up to homotopy with fixed ends.

The Robbin–Salamon index of a possibly degenerate path
\Phi\in\widetilde{\operatorname{Sp}}(2n-2)
of symplectic matrices is a half-integer

\operatorname{RS}(\Phi)\in\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{Z}}\quad\text{such that}\quad|\operatorname{RS}(\Phi)-\operatorname{RS}(\Phi^{\prime})|\leq\frac{1}{2}\dim\ker\Phi(1)

(1)

for any path
\Phi^{\prime}
close enough to
\Phi
. For a nondegenerate path it coincides with the Conley–Zehnder index,
\operatorname{CZ}(\Phi)
.
We define the extremal indices
\mu_{\pm}(\Phi)
to be smallest and largest indices of nondegenerate perturbations

\mu_{-}(\Phi):=\liminf_{\Phi^{\prime}\rightarrow\Phi}\operatorname{RS}(\Phi^{\prime}),\qquad\mu_{+}(\Phi):=\limsup_{\Phi^{\prime}\rightarrow\Phi}\operatorname{RS}(\Phi^{\prime}),

where the limits are taken over nondegenerate perturbations
\Phi^{\prime}
of
\Phi
. Then clearly,

\mu_{-}(\Phi)\leq\operatorname{RS}(\Phi)\leq\mu_{+}(\Phi)\quad\text{and}\quad|\mu_{\pm}(\Phi)-\operatorname{RS}(\Phi)|\leq\frac{1}{2}\dim\ker\Phi(1).

(2)

Actually,
\mu_{\pm}(\Phi)=\operatorname{RS}(\Phi)\pm\frac{1}{2}\dim\ker\Phi(1)
. This follows from the index jump bound (
1
), as well as the fact that, for small enough
\epsilon>0
,
\operatorname{RS}(\{e^{\pm\epsilon J_{0}t}\Phi(t)\}_{t})=\operatorname{RS}(\Phi)\pm\frac{1}{2}\dim\ker\Phi(1)
.
We say that the path
\Phi\in\widetilde{\operatorname{Sp}}(2n-2)
is dynamically convex if

\mu_{-}(\Phi)\geq n+1.

The mean index of a path is

\hat{\mu}(\Phi):=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\operatorname{RS}(\Phi^{k})}{k}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mu_{\pm}(\Phi^{k})}{k},

where
\Phi^{k}
is the composition of
\Phi
on itself
k
times. The mean index has two properties that will be useful to us:

1.

[
6
, eq. (3.2)]
: For any
\Phi
,

\hat{\mu}(\Phi)-n+1\leq\mu_{-}(\Phi)\leq\mu_{+}(\Phi)\leq\hat{\mu}(\Phi)+n-1.

(3)

2.

When
\Phi
is maximally degenerate, all eigenvalues of
\Phi
are equal to 1, then

\operatorname{RS}(\Phi^{k})=k\cdot\operatorname{RS}(\Phi)\quad\text{and hence}\quad\operatorname{RS}(\Phi)=\hat{\mu}(\Phi).

(4)

2.1.3
Indices of closed orbits and Morse–Bott families.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
W
be a star-shaped domain such that the Reeb flow on
\partial W
is Besse. For any Reeb orbit
x
on
\partial W
, there is an element

\Phi\in\widetilde{\operatorname{Sp}}(2n-2)

well-defined up to conjugation by a constant matrix, that represents the linearized flow along
x
, and:

1.

The element corresponding to the
k
-th iterate of
x
is
\Phi^{k}
.

2.

If

x_{0},x_{1}
are Reeb orbits lying in the same connected component of a Morse–Bott family, then their linearized flows give the same element:

\Phi_{0}=\Phi_{1}\quad\text{in}\quad\widetilde{\operatorname{Sp}}(2n-2).

Proof.

Suppose that
W
is a star-shaped domain, then all Reeb orbits are contractible and
c_{1}(\xi)=0
for
\xi\subset T(\partial W)
the contact distribution. Given a Reeb orbit
x\subset\partial W
, let
D
be a disk bounding it, and let

\tau_{D}:\xi|_{D}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{2n-2}\quad\text{be a symplectic trivialization of the contact structure.}

We define

\Phi(t):=\tau_{D}\circ d\varphi^{t}(x(0))\circ\tau_{D}^{-1}

to be the element of
\widetilde{\operatorname{Sp}}(2n-2)
corresponding to
x
.
Up to a global change of basis of the target
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n-2}
(or, in other words, conjugation by a fixed matrix), this element is independent of the choice of
D
and
\tau_{D}
, since any two such disks are homotopic and the corresponding trivializations will induce homotopic paths of matrices with fixed ends.

Denote by
S
the connected component of the manifold
Y_{T}
of fixed points of
\varphi^{T}
, that contains
x_{0},x_{1}
. Consider the sub-bundle

\xi_{S}:=\xi|_{S}\cap TS.

The dimension of this sub-bundle is
2d=\dim(S_{T})
. The time-
T
linearized flow satisfies:

d\varphi^{T}|_{\xi_{S}}=\operatorname{id}_{\xi_{S}}\quad\text{and}\quad(d\varphi^{T})^{k}=\operatorname{id}_{\xi}\text{ for any $k$ such that }kT\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(5)

This implies that
d\varphi^{T}_{x_{0}(0)}
is diagonalizable over
{\mathbb{C}}
:

d\varphi^{T}_{x_{0}(0)}\cong\operatorname{id}_{d,{\mathbb{C}}}\oplus_{j=1}^{n-1-d}\ e^{i\theta_{j}}\cdot\operatorname{id}_{1,{\mathbb{C}}},\quad\text{for }\theta_{j}\in\left\{\frac{2\pi m}{k}:m=1,\dots,k-1\right\}.

(6)

Fix a trivialization
\tau(t):\xi|_{x_{0}(t)}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{2n-2}
that extends over a disk
D
bounding
x_{0}
.
After a constant change of coordinates on the target
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n-2}
, we may assume that

\tau(0)\circ d\varphi^{T}_{x_{0}(0)}\circ(\tau(0))^{-1}=\operatorname{id}_{2d}\oplus_{j=1}^{n-1-d}\ e^{i\theta_{j}}.

Moreover, it follows from the Morse–Bott condition that
\theta_{j}\neq 0\mod 2\pi
.

Now, given
x_{1}
in the same component
S
, choose a path
x_{s}(0)
in
S
connecting
x_{0}(0)
to
x_{1}(0)
and set

(s,t)\mapsto x_{s}(t):=\varphi^{t}x_{s}(0).

Extend
\tau(t)
to a trivialization
\tilde{\tau}(s,t):\xi|_{x_{s}(t)}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{2n-2}
such that

\tilde{\tau}(s,0)\circ d\varphi^{T}\circ(\tilde{\tau}(s,0))^{-1}=\operatorname{id}_{d,{\mathbb{C}}}\oplus_{j=1}^{n-1-d}\ e^{i\theta_{j}^{s}}\cdot\operatorname{id}_{1,{\mathbb{C}}}.

But, since
\theta_{j}^{s}
is continuous in
s
and takes values in the finite set
\left\{\frac{2\pi m}{k}:m=1,\dots,k-1\right\}
, then it must be constant in
s
:
\theta_{j}^{s}=\theta_{j}
.
We see that under the trivialization
\tilde{\tau}
the linearized flows along
x_{0},x_{1}
are homotopic with fixed ends, so they represent the same element in
\widetilde{\operatorname{Sp}}(2n-2)
.
∎

2.2
Symplectic homology

Given a nondegenerate star-shaped domain
W
, the symplectic homology
SH(W;\mathbb{F})
is a direct limit of Hamiltonian Floer homology of
certain admissible Hamiltonians (these are
C^{2}
–small on
W
and grow linearly in the Liouville coordinate outside of
W
). The chain complexes for such Hamiltonians have two generators

\hat{x},\check{x}\quad\text{in degrees}\quad|\hat{x}|=\operatorname{CZ}(x)+1,\quad|\check{x}|=\operatorname{CZ}(x)

for any periodic Reeb orbit
x
on
\partial W
, and a single critical point
p
in degree
n
, representing the homology of
W
. The action filtration is given by the action (or Reeb period) for the generators
\hat{x},\check{x}
and zero for the critical point
p
.
For any
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the filtered homology
SH^{t}(W;\mathbb{F})
is the homology of the sub-complex of generators with action
<t
.
Given an interval
I=(t^{\prime},t)\subset{\mathbb{R}}
,
SH^{I}(W;{\mathbb{F}})
is the homology of the quotient of chain complex
SC^{t}(W;{\mathbb{F}})
by the image of
SC^{t^{\prime}}(W;{\mathbb{F}})
.

This filtration induces the structure of a persistence module on symplectic homology. By the structure theorem for persistence modules,
SH(W;\mathbb{F})
decomposes uniquely as a direct sum of graded interval modules
\mathbb{F}(a,b]
, and the resulting multiset of intervals is the barcode

\mathcal{B}(W;\mathbb{F}).

The degree
\deg(I)
of a bar
I=(a,b]
is the homological degree in which the corresponding interval module lives.

For every bar
I=(a,b]
, there exist closed Reeb orbits
x,y
such that

\mathcal{A}(x)=a,\qquad\mathcal{A}(y)=b,\qquad\deg(I)\in[\mu_{-}(x),\mu_{+}(x)+1],\quad\deg(I)\in[\mu_{-}(y)-1,\mu_{+}(y)].

In particular this means that
\mu^{-}(y)-\mu^{+}(x)\leq 2
and when equality holds one has
\deg(I)=\mu^{+}(x)+1=\mu^{-}(y)-1
. Bars with
a=0
start at the extra generator
p
.

Remark 2.5

(Smith inequality)
.

Let
W
be a Liouville domain. The Smith inequality (
[
15
,
16
]
, see also
[
6
, Theorem 2.5]
) states that

\dim SH^{pI}(W;\mathbb{F}_{p})\geq\dim SH^{I}(W;\mathbb{F}_{p}).

(7)

Together with the universal coefficients theorem this implies that for all
t>0
and for any field
\mathbb{F}
,

\dim SH^{t}(W;\mathbb{F})\geq\dim H(W;\mathbb{F}).

See
[
6
, eq. (4.12)]
for a proof.

Definition 2.6

(equivariant SH capacities)
.

Let
W\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
be star-shaped domain. As a graded vector space, its positive
S^{1}
–equivariant symplectic homology is

CH_{*}(W)=\begin{cases}\mathbb{F},&*=n-1+2k,\ k\in{\mathbb{N}}\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

It admits an action filtration. The equivariant SH capacities are defined to be

c_{k}(W):=\inf\left\{L:\operatorname{im}\Big(CH_{n-1+2k}^{L}(W))\rightarrow CH_{n-1+2k}(W)\Big)\neq 0\right\}\in(0,\infty]

The equivariant SH capacities admit several useful properties, but we will only use the (graded) spectrality property.

Graded spectrality:

If
\alpha=\lambda|_{\partial W}
is nondegenerate, then

c_{k}(W)={\mathcal{A}}(\gamma)\quad\text{for some Reeb orbit $\gamma$ with}\quad\operatorname{CZ}(\gamma)=n-1+2k.

When
W
is Morse–Bott, there exists a connected component
S
of a Morse–Bott family
S_{T}
such that

c_{k}(W)=T\quad\text{and}\quad n-1+2k\in[\operatorname{RS}(S)-d,\operatorname{RS}(S)+d].

3
Besse domains are dynamically convex

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that the Reeb flow on
\partial W
is Besse of minimal common period
1
. Then
\operatorname{RS}(S_{1})>0
.

Proof.

For any Reeb orbit
x
on
\partial W
, its mean index is a positive multiple of
\hat{\mu}(S_{1})=\operatorname{RS}(S_{1})
. Therefore, if
\operatorname{RS}(S_{1})\leq 0
then all orbits have non-positive mean indices. By (
3
), this implies

\mu_{+}(x)\leq\hat{\mu}(x)+n-1\leq n-1.

By the definition of
\mu_{+}
, there are nondegenerate perturbations of
W
for which all Conley–Zehnder indices of all orbits are bounded by
n-1
. This contradicts the fact that
c_{k}(W)
are finite and satisfy the graded spectrality property.
∎

For Besse contact forms, the Robbin–Salamon index of any orbit is an integer. The following fact from
[

]
determines the parity of the index and will be useful.

Lemma 3.2

(
[
4

Let

be a Besse star-shaped domain and

a connected component of a Morse–Bott family of dimension

\operatorname{RS}(S)\equiv n-1-d\mod 2\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mu_{-}(S)\equiv n-1\mod 2.

Proof.

This is a simple consequence of the arguments in
[

. More accurately,
[

states that

\operatorname{RS}(S)\equiv n-1-d\mod 2
.
Moreover,
[

states that when perturbing a Besse contact form via a Morse function on

\mu_{-}(S)\equiv\operatorname{RS}(S)-d\equiv n-1\mod 2.\qed

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

SH_{*+\mu_{-}(S_{T})}^{[T-\epsilon,T+\epsilon]}(W;{\mathbb{F}}_{2})=H_{*}(Y_{T};{\mathbb{F}}_{2}).

The next proposition is a main new ingredient in the proof of the main results of this paper.

Proposition 3.4
.

Any Besse star-shaped domain is dynamically convex.

Proof.

We need to show that for any connected component
S
of a Morse–Bott family,

\mu_{-}(S)\geq n+1.

By Lemma
3.1
,
\hat{\mu}(S_{1})=\operatorname{RS}(S_{1})>0
. Since all mean indices of other orbits are positive multiples of
\operatorname{RS}(S_{1})
, they must be positive as well and by (
3
)

\mu_{-}(x)\geq-n+1\quad\text{for all Reeb orbits }x.

Assume that
W
is not dynamically convex, then the above lower bound guarantees that there is an orbit of minimal index. Let
k\leq n
be the minimal value of
\mu_{-}
over all Reeb orbits. Among all (connected components of) Morse–Bott families with
\mu_{-}=k
, let
S\subset S_{T}
be the one with the largest action.
By Lemma
3.3
,

SH_{k}^{[T-\epsilon,T+\epsilon]}(W;{\mathbb{F}}_{2})\neq 0\quad\text{while}\quad SH_{k}(W;{\mathbb{F}}_{2})=0.

This means that there must be a bar
I
starting at action
T
with degree
k
, and ending at some action
T^{\prime}>T
(alternatively - after a perturbation there must be an orbit coming from a Morse–Bott family
S_{T^{\prime}}
, “killing” the generator in degree
k
). Notice that for any connected component
S^{\prime}
of
S_{T^{\prime}}
,

\mu_{-}(S^{\prime})>k,\text{since $T$ is the largest action in the minimal degree }k.

On the other hand, by Lemma
3.2

k=\mu_{-}(S)\equiv n-1\equiv\mu_{-}(S^{\prime})\mod 2,

so we must have
\mu_{-}(S^{\prime})\geq k+2
, in contradiction to
k=\deg(I)\geq\mu_{-}(S^{\prime})-1
(alternatively, after a perturbation there could not be an orbit from
S_{T^{\prime}}
of degree
k+1
).
∎

Symplectic Homology of Besse contact forms

u\subset\mathcal{N}
.

Denote by
u_{T}=\pi\circ u
the projection of
u
to
I\times Y_{T}
. Note that
u_{T}
is also a solution of the same Floer equation since
J
respects the splitting
V_{T}\oplus V_{N}
and commutes with
d\pi
:

\displaystyle\partial_{s}u_{T}-J(\partial_{t}u_{T}-X_{H_{\delta}})

\displaystyle=d\pi\partial_{s}u-J(d\pi\partial_{t}u-X_{H_{\delta}})

\displaystyle=d\pi\partial_{s}u-J(d\pi\partial_{t}u-d\pi X_{H_{\delta}})

\displaystyle=d\pi\Big(\partial_{s}u-J(\partial_{t}u-X_{H_{\delta}})\Big)=d\pi(0).

Here we used the fact that
X_{H_{\delta}}\in T(I\times Y_{T})
and hence
d\pi
is the identity on it. Consider the energy of
u
, it splits as

E(u)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}}\|\partial_{s}u\|_{J}^{2}\ ds\ dt=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\times S^{1}}\|d\pi\partial_{s}u\|_{J}^{2}+\|(\operatorname{Id}-d\pi)\partial_{s}u\|_{J}^{2}\ ds\ dt=:E(u_{T})+E_{N}.

However, by the energy identity of Floer solutions, since both
u
and
u_{T}
solve the same equation and have the same ends, their energies coincide:

E(u_{T})+E_{N}=E(u)={\mathcal{A}}(\gamma_{p})-{\mathcal{A}}(\gamma_{q})=E(u_{T})\quad\text{which implies}\quad E_{N}=0.

In particular
\partial_{s}u
has no components in
\ker d\pi
.
Note that
u
is
J
-holomorphic in the
V_{N}
directions:

(\operatorname{Id}-d\pi)\big(\partial_{s}u+J\partial_{t}u\big)=0.

Therefore
\partial_{t}u
has no components in
\ker d\pi
as well and
u
is constant in the normal directions. Since the ends lie in
Y_{T}
, we conclude that
\operatorname{image}(u)\subset I\times Y_{T}
.
∎

Lemma A.5
.

Let
J
as in Setup
A.2
and let
p,q
be critical points of
f
of index difference
\leq 1
. For small enough
\delta
, there is a bijection between Floer solution
u\subset I\times Y_{T}
from
\gamma_{p}
to
\gamma_{q}
and Morse negative-gradient flow lines of
\delta f
from
p
to
q
.

Proof.

We start with the
C^{2}
-small, autonomous Morse Hamiltonian
\delta\cdot f
. For
C^{2}
small autonomous Morse Hamiltonians, all Floer solutions are Morse flow lines (cf.,
[
1
, Proposition 10.1.7.]
). As explained in the setup above, the pair
(\delta f,g_{J})
is Morse-Smale, and the linearized operator of the Floer equation is surjective
[
1
, Section 10]
.

The Reeb flow gives an
S^{1}
action on
I\times Y_{T}
, so we can define the following map:

\Phi:u\mapsto\bar{u},\quad\quad\bar{u}(s,t):=\varphi^{-T\cdot t}u(s,t).

(12)

Then,

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\bar{u}=

\displaystyle d\varphi^{-T\cdot t}\partial_{s}u,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bar{u}=

\displaystyle-T\cdot R\circ\varphi^{-T\cdot t}u+d\varphi^{-T\cdot t}\partial_{t}u.

Since
u
satisfies the Floer equation for
H_{\delta}=T\cdot\rho+\delta\cdot f\circ\varphi^{-T\cdot t}
in
\mathcal{N}
, then
\bar{u}
satisfies the Floer equation for the Hamiltonian

\delta\cdot f
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\bar{u}=

\displaystyle\ d\varphi^{-T\cdot t}\partial_{s}u=-d\varphi^{-T\cdot t}J(\partial_{t}u-X_{H_{\delta}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-Jd\varphi^{-T\cdot t}\big(\partial_{t}u-T\cdot R-d\varphi^{T\cdot t}X_{\delta f}\circ\varphi^{-T\cdot t}\circ u\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-J\big(d\varphi^{-T\cdot t}\partial_{t}u-T\cdot R-X_{\delta f}\circ\varphi^{-T\cdot t}\circ u\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-J\big(\partial_{t}\bar{u}+T\cdot R-T\cdot R-X_{\delta f}\circ\bar{u}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-J\big(\partial_{t}\bar{u}-X_{\delta f}\circ\bar{u}\big).

We see that the map (
12
) gives a bijection between Floer solutions of
H_{\delta}
between
\gamma_{q}
and
\gamma_{p}
and those of
\delta\cdot f
between
q,p
, which are simply Morse negative-gradient flow-lines.
∎

The fact that the linearized operator
L_{(H_{\delta},J)}
associated to
(H_{\delta},J)
is surjective at
u
connecting
\gamma_{q}
and
\gamma_{p}
follows from the surjectivity of the linearized operator for
(\delta f,J)
, together with the fact that
L_{(\delta f,J)}=L_{(H_{\delta},J)}\circ\Phi^{-1}
where
\Phi
is the map from (
12
).

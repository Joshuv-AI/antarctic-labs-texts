Primal-dual algorithm for weakly convex functions under sharpness conditions
Ewa M. Bednarczuk, The Hung Tran, Monika Syga

Abstract
We investigate the convergence of the primal-dual algorithm for composite optimization problems when the objective functions are weakly convex. We introduce a modified duality gap function, which is a lower bound of the standard duality gap function. Under the sharpness condition of this new function, we identify the area around the set of saddle points where we obtain the convergence of the primal-dual algorithm. We give numerical examples and applications in image denoising and deblurring to demonstrate our results.

Introduction

Our interest is the following saddle point problem,

\min_{x\in X}\max_{y\in Y}f(x)+\langle Lx,y\rangle-g^{*}(y),

(1)

where
X
and
Y
are Hilbert,
f:X\to\left(-\infty,+\infty\right]
is proper lsc weakly convex and
g:Y\to\left(-\infty,+\infty\right]
is proper lsc convex,
L:X\to Y
is a bounded linear operator and the conjugate
g^{*}(y)=\sup_{w\in Y}\{\langle y,w\rangle-g(w)\}
. When
g
is convex, problem (
1
) is equivalent to the composite optimization problem in Hilbert spaces
(
Bednarczuk and Syga, 2022
)
,

\inf_{x\in X}f\left(x\right)+g\left(Lx\right).

(2)

Problem (
2
) encompasses many optimization problems and applications by using appropriate functions in the modeling process. For example, in the convex case, one can use indicator functions to model constrained problems or use total variation for image processing problems, e.g.
Rudin et al. (1992)
;
Chambolle and Pock (2011)
.

When
f
and
g
are convex functions, there have been many primal-dual methods and convergence discussions of problem (
2
) for the last two decades
(
Chambolle and Pock, 2011
;
Condat, 2013
;
He and Yuan, 2012
;
Vũ, 2013
)
.
For nonconvex settings, there are several attempts to analyze the primal-dual algorithms for saddle point problem (
1
).
For example, in the series of work
(
Clason et al., 2021
;
Mazurenko et al., 2020
;
Valkonen, 2014
)
, the authors consider a smooth nonlinear coupling term instead of
\langle Lx,y\rangle
in problem (
1
), while in
Hamedani and Aybat (2021)
, an accelerated primal-dual algorithm with backtracking is proposed to solve the nonlinear saddle point problem.
On the other hand, in
(
Guo et al., 2023
;
Li et al., 2022
;
Sun et al., 2018
;
Zhu et al., 2024
)
, the convergence of primal-dual methods for nonconvex objective functions is investigated by relying on Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz (KŁ) inequality, which is satisfied by a large class of functions
(
Bolte et al., 2007
)
.
Note that KŁ inequality is equivalent to other conditions such as quadratic growth condition, metric sub-regularity condition, or error bound condition, e.g.
(
Drusvyatskiy and Lewis, 2018
;
Cuong and Kruger, 2022
;
Bai et al., 2022
)
. These conditions are usually used to improve numerical algorithms’ convergence rate or achieve convergence in the nonconvex setting.

Our main contribution is the convergence analysis of primal-dual algorithms, i.e., Algorithm
1
and
2
for solving problem (
1
) under the assumption of sharpness or error-bound condition of the new duality gap function (Definition
5
) and sharpness of objective function f problem (
2
) (Definition
6
).
Our problem setting refers to the case where the objective functions are weakly convex. It is a large class of functions, including convex and differentiable functions with Lipschitz continuous gradient. It has been shown that for weakly convex functions, the proximal subgradient is well-defined and global. In
Jourani (1996)
, such property is called globalization property. Recent results (
Bednarczuk et al. (2023a)
) show that the proximal subgradient enjoys the calculus sum rule, so we can analyze the primal-dual algorithm in terms of the proximal subgradient.
From the works of
Davis et al. (2018)
;
Bednarczuk et al. (2023b)
, weakly convex functions with sharpness condition give us a linear convergence as well as specify a starting area where the convergence is guaranteed.
Moreover, it was observed in
Mollenhoff et al. (2015)
;
Shen and Gu (2018)
;
Liu et al. (2019)
that by including a weakly convex regularizer into the model, one can improve the performance of the model.

Sharpness conditions are usually imposed on the objective function, see, e.g.,
Davis et al. (2018)
;
Bednarczuk et al. (2023b)
;
Johnstone and Moulin (2020)
. For the saddle point problem, the role of objective is played by the Lagrangian and duality gap functions appear in investigating the convergence rate of primal-dual methods.
However, duality gap functions are not often used to establish convergence for primal-dual algorithms. It is effective when the domains of the saddle point problem are bounded.
On the other hand, one uses the gradient/subgradient to measure the distance to criticality by using the KKT condition as a criterion (
(
Rockafellar and Wets, 2009
, Example 11.41)
).
Recently, in
Lu and Yang (2022)
, the authors introduce the infimal-subdifferential size, which uses subgradient to measure the distance of the iterates toward the saddle points for the primal-dual algorithm.

For our setting, when problem (
1
) is defined on general Hilbert spaces
X
and
Y
, the standard duality gap function (
17
) is not a good choice for investigating convergence of primal-dual algorithm. Observe that in
Chambolle and Pock (2011)
, the restricted duality gap function is introduced to achieve convergence.
Instead, we introduce a modified gap function (see Definition
5
below), which is a lower bound of the standard duality gap function, to formulate our main requirement for convergence.
This function comes naturally from the structure of the primal-dual algorithm, albeit more restrictive than the standard gap function. Combined with the sharpness condition, we use this new modified gap function for obtaining the convergence to the set of saddle points for problems with unbounded saddle point sets (Theorem
2
and
3
).

The structure of the paper is as follows.
In Section
2
, we give the definition of weakly convex functions, and their properties, together with proximal subgradient and calculus sum rule for weakly convex functions. In Section
3
, we introduce the Lagrange saddle point problem as well as the standard duality gap function and the new modified duality gap function.
We also give definitions of sharpness for the duality gap functions and supporting examples.
Section
4
and
5
are devoted to the convergence analysis of the primal-dual Algorithm
1
and
1
which can be considered as a weakly convex counterpart of the Chambolle-Pock algorithm (
Chambolle and Pock (2011)
).
We show that, under sharpness condition (Definition
5
), both primal and dual iterates converge to a saddle point provided that the iterates start sufficiently close to the set of saddle points.
Since Algorithm
1
, is not symmetric with respect to the primal and dual updates, we formulate Algorithm
2
by changing the order of updates, and investigate its convergence in Theorem
3
.
Section
6
is dedicated to the analysis of the primal-dual algorithm when only problem (
2
) is sharp. Then, the dual iterate provides auxiliary information on how close we are to the set of minimizers of problem (
2
).
Finally, in Section
7
, we perform numerical tests for our theory on Algorithm
1
and
2
. We test against the
\ell_{1}
-regularization problem with a fully weakly convex problem. We also extend our model to image denoising and deblurring problems.

Preliminaries

Let
X
be Hilbert space with the inner product
\langle,\rangle
and the norm
\|x\|=\sqrt{\langle x,x\rangle}
.
A function
f:X\to\left(-\infty,+\infty\right]
is proper if its domain, denoted by
\text{dom }f=\left\{x\in X:f\left(x\right)<+\infty\right\}
is nonempty.
We start with the definition of
\rho
-weak convexity (also known as
\rho
-paraconvexity, see
Jourani (1996)
;
Rolewicz (1979)
or
\rho
-semiconvexity, see
Cannarsa and Sinestrari (2004)
).

Definition 1

(
\rho
-weak convexity
)
.

Let
X
be a Hilbert space. A function
h:X\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]
is said to be
\rho
-weakly convex if there exists a constant
\rho\geq 0
such that for
\lambda\in[0,1]
the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\quad(\forall(x,y)\in X^{2})

\displaystyle h(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)\leq\lambda h(x)+(1-\lambda)h(y)+\lambda(1-\lambda)\frac{\rho}{2}\|x-y\|^{2}.

(3)

We refer to
\rho
as the modulus of weak convexity of the function
h
.

Equivalently in Hilbert space, a
\rho
-weak convexity
f
means that
f+\frac{\rho}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}
is convex (see
(
Cannarsa and Sinestrari, 2004
, Proposition 1.1.3)
). A function of the form
f(x)=-a\|x\|^{2}
is
2a
-weakly convex for
a>0
. Below is another example of a weakly convex function.

Example 1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty)
be defined as
f(x)=|(x-a)(x-b)|
, where
(a,b)\in\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,|\,a<b\}
. Function
f
is
2
-weakly convex (
(
Bednarczuk et al., 2023b
, Example 1)
).

Function
f
from
Example 1
is a simple example of a non-smooth weakly convex function. Despite its simplicity, it can be used as a modeling function in various applications, as we will show in the numerical section.

We also give a definition of a convex conjugate of a proper function
F:X\to(-\infty,+\infty]

Definition 2
.

Let
X
be Hilbert,
F:X\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be proper, its conjugate
F^{*}:X\to(-\infty,+\infty]
is defined as

F^{*}(u)=\sup_{x\in X}\ \langle u,x\rangle-F(x).

For subdifferentials, since the weakly convex function is general nonconvex, its Moreau subdifferentials can be empty (e.g. Example (
1
) for
a<x<b
). However, its proximal subgradient is nonempty, giving us an important global property of a weakly convex function. We introduce the proximal subgradient below.

Definition 3

(Global proximal
{\varepsilon\text{-subdifferential}}
)
.

Let
\varepsilon\geq 0
. Let
X
be a Hilbert space. The global proximal
{\varepsilon\text{-subdifferential}}
of a function
F:X\to(-\infty,+\infty]
at
x_{0}\in\text{dom }F
for
C\geq 0
is defined as follows:

\partial^{\,\varepsilon}_{C}F(x_{0})=\left\{v\in X\,|\,F(x)-F(x_{0})\geq\langle v\ |\ x-x_{0}\rangle-C\|x-x_{0}\|^{2}-\varepsilon\ \ \forall x\in X\right\}.

(4)

For
\varepsilon=0
, we have

\partial_{C}F(x_{0})=\{v\in X\,|\,F(x)\geq F(x_{0})+\langle v\ |\ x-x_{0}\rangle-C\|x-x_{0}\|^{2},\;\forall x\in X\}.

(5)

In view of (
5
),
\partial_{0}F(x)
denotes the subdifferential in the sense of convex analysis. The elements of
\partial^{\,\varepsilon}_{C}F(x)
are called proximal
\varepsilon
-subgradients.

By
(
Jourani, 1996
, Proposition 3.1)
, for a proper lsc
\rho
-weakly convex
F
, the global proximal subdifferential
\partial_{{\rho}/{2}}F(x_{0})
defined by (
5
) coincides with the set of local proximal subgradients which satisfy (
5
) locally in a neighbourhood of
x_{0}
. This important property is called
the globalisation property
, see
e.g.
[56]. It also coincides with Clarke subdifferential
\partial_{c}h(x_{0})
(see
Jourani (1996)
;
Atenas et al. (2023)
).
In this work, we only consider proper lsc
\rho
-weakly convex functions, we will use
\partial_{\rho}F(x_{0})
instead of
\partial_{\rho/2}F(x_{0})
for proximal subdifferentials of
F
at
x_{0}
. The next proposition describes the domain of proximal subdifferentials for weakly convex function
F
.

Proposition 1

(
(
Bednarczuk et al., 2023a
, Proposition 2)
)
.

Let
X
be a Hilbert space, and
F
is proper lsc
\rho
-weakly convex on
X
with
\rho\geq 0
. Then

\text{dom}\,\partial_{\rho}F\subset\text{dom}\,F,

and for every
\varepsilon>0
we have the equality
\text{dom}\,\partial_{\rho}^{\,\varepsilon}h=\text{dom}\,h
.

Moreover, we also have the sum rule for proximal subdifferentials for weakly convex functions. This is an important property when analyzing primal-dual algorithm below.

Theorem 1
.

(
Bednarczuk et al., 2023a
, Theorem 2)

Let
X
be a Hilbert space.
For
i=0,1
, let function
f_{i}:X\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]
be proper lower semicontinuous and
\rho_{i}
-weakly convex on
X
with
\rho_{i}\geq 0
. Then, for all
x\in\text{dom}\,f_{0}\cap\text{dom}\,f_{1}
and for all
\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{1}\geq 0
we have

\partial_{\varepsilon_{0},\rho_{0}}f_{0}(x)+\partial_{\varepsilon_{1},\rho_{1}}f_{1}(x)\subseteq\partial_{\varepsilon,{\rho}}(f_{0}+f_{1})(x)

(6)

for all
\varepsilon\geq\varepsilon_{0}+\varepsilon_{1}
and for all
\rho\geq\rho_{0}+\rho_{1}
.
The equality

\partial_{\varepsilon,\rho_{0}+\rho_{1}}(f_{0}+f_{1})(x)=\bigcup_{\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{1}\,|\,\varepsilon_{0}+\varepsilon_{1}\leq\varepsilon}\partial_{\varepsilon_{0},\rho_{0}}f_{0}(x)+\partial_{\varepsilon_{1},\rho_{1}}f_{1}(x)

(7)

holds when
\text{dom}\,f_{0}\cap\text{dom}\,f_{1}
contains a point at which either
f_{0}+{\rho_{0}}/2\|\cdot\|^{2}
or
f_{1}(\cdot)+{\rho_{1}}/2\|\cdot\|^{2}
is continuous.

3
Primal-Dual relationship of Composite Minimization Problem with weakly convex function

In this section, we introduce the Lagrangian dual problem corresponding to (
2
) and define the modifeid duality gap function, which has a global minimizers at the saddle point of Lagrange saddle point problem. We also define sharpness condition, which will be our main tool for the convergence analysis below.

For problem (
2
), the following holds

\inf_{x\in X}\sup_{y\in Y}f(x)+\langle Lx,y\rangle-g^{*}(y)=\inf_{x\in X}f(x)+g(Lx),

(8)

where the function
f:X\to(-\infty,+\infty]
is a proper lsc weakly convex defined on Hilbert space
X
,
g:Y\to(-\infty,+\infty]
is a proper lsc convex function on Hilbert space
Y
and
L:X\to Y
is a bounded linear operator with its adjoint
L^{*}:Y\to X
.
The LHS can be considered as Lagrange primal problem (
8
). Noted that, in general, Lagrange primal is not equivalent to the primal problem (
2
) unless function
g
is convex
(
Bednarczuk and Syga, 2022
, Proposition 5.1)
.
The Lagrange dual problem with respect to (
8
) is

\sup_{y\in Y}\inf_{x\in X}\mathcal{L}(x,y),

(9)

where
\mathcal{L}:X\times Y\to(-\infty,+\infty]
is the Lagrangian defined as

\mathcal{L}(x,y):=f(x)+\langle Lx,y\rangle-g^{*}(y).

(10)

In the next result, we show that the fully weakly convex case (
f,g
weakly convex) in the form of problem (
2
) can be reduced to our current setting (
f
weakly convex,
g
convex).

Lemma 1
.

Let
X,Y
be Hilbert spaces, consider the functions
f:X\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be proper lsc
\rho_{f}
-weakly convex and
g:Y\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be proper lsc
\rho_{g}
-weakly convex, the problem (
2
)

\inf_{x\in X}f(x)+g(Lx),

is equivalent to

\inf_{x\in X}F(x)+G(Lx),

(11)

where
F:X\to(-\infty,+\infty]
is proper lsc weakly convex and
G:Y\to(-\infty,+\infty]
is proper lsc convex.

Proof.

Since
g
is
\rho_{g}
-weakly convex, we can turn (
2
) into

\inf_{x\in X}f(x)-\frac{\rho_{g}}{2}\|Lx\|^{2}+\frac{\rho_{g}}{2}\|Lx\|^{2}+g(Lx).

Denoting
F(x)=f(x)-\frac{\rho_{g}}{2}\|Lx\|^{2}
and
G(y)=g(y)+\frac{\rho_{g}}{2}\|y\|^{2}
, we obtain (
11
) where
F
is
\rho_{f}+\rho_{g}\|L\|^{2}
-weakly convex (see
(
Bednarczuk and Tran, 2023
, Proposition 4.5)
) and the function
G
is convex.
The conjugate dual problem with respect to functions
F
and
G
is

\sup_{y\in Y}\inf_{x\in X}F\left(x\right)+\left\langle Lx,y\right\rangle-G^{*}\left(y\right),

(12)

with the respective Lagrangian

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{FG}(x,y)=F\left(x\right)+\left\langle Lx,y\right\rangle-G^{*}\left(y\right).

(13)

Now let us consider the Lagrange primal problem with respect to (
13
),

\inf_{x\in X}\sup_{y\in Y}\mathcal{L}_{FG}(x,y)=\inf_{x\in X}\sup_{y\in Y}F\left(x\right)+\left\langle Lx,y\right\rangle-G^{*}\left(y\right)=\inf_{x\in X}F\left(x\right)+G^{**}\left(Lx\right).

Because
G
is lsc convex,
G^{**}=G

(
Bauschke and Combettes, 2017
, Theorem 13.37)
so we finish the proof.

\hbox to0.0pt{$\sqcap$\hss}\sqcup

By the fact that the conjugate function is a convex function, the Lagrangian is convex with respect to the second variable
y\in Y
. On the other hand, it is nonconvex (weakly convex) in the primal variable
x\in X
.

Let us consider
\mathcal{K}:X\times Y\to(\infty,+\infty]
be a proper function. Recall that
(x^{*},y^{*})
is a saddle point
(
Bauschke and Combettes, 2017
, Definition 19.16)
of
\mathcal{K}(x,y)
if it satisfies

(\forall(x,y)\in X\times Y)\qquad\sup_{y\in Y}\mathcal{K}(x^{*},y)\geq\mathcal{K}(x^{*},y^{*})\geq\inf_{x\in X}\mathcal{K}(x,y^{*}).

(14)

Combining with the general minimax relationship i.e.

\inf_{x\in X}\sup_{\hat{y}\in Y}\mathcal{K}\left(x,\hat{y}\right)-\sup_{y\in Y}\inf_{\hat{x}\in X}\mathcal{K}\left(\hat{x},y\right)\geq 0,

(15)

one can define a gap function
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{K}}:X\times Y\to[0,\infty)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{K}}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=\sup_{(\overline{x},\overline{y})\in X\times Y}\mathcal{K}(x,\overline{y})-\mathcal{K}(\overline{x},y).

(16)

This is a standard definition of duality gap function which comes from variation inequality, first appearing in
(
Auslender, 1973
)
and is widely used for general saddle point problem
(
Chambolle and Pock, 2011
;
Tran-Dinh and Cevher, 2014
;
Davis, 2015
)
.
From (
16
), it is clear that
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{K}}(x,y)=0
if and only if
(x,y)
is a saddle point
(x,y)
. For the Lagrangian (
10
), the gap function can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=f(x)+g^{*}(y)+\sup_{(\hat{x},\hat{y})\in X\times Y}\langle Lx,\hat{y}\rangle-\langle L\hat{x},y\rangle-f(\hat{x})-g^{*}(\hat{y})

(17)

\displaystyle=f(x)+g^{*}(y)+g^{**}(Lx)-f^{*}(-L^{*}y).

Observe that
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)
can take
+\infty
when
f(\hat{x})=-\infty
or
g^{*}(y)=+\infty
.
Since
f
is proper lsc weakly convex and
g
is proper lsc convex, the gap function
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)
, according to (
17
),
is weakly convex with respect to
x
, and convex with respect to
y
. It is also jointly weakly convex with respect to
(x,y)
thanks to its structure.
Indeed, let
\lambda\in[0,1],(x_{1},y_{1}),(x_{2},y_{2})\in X\times Y
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{L}}(x_{1}+(1-\lambda)x_{2},y_{1}+(1-\lambda)y_{2})

\displaystyle=f(x_{1}+(1-\lambda)x_{2})+g^{*}(y_{1}+(1-\lambda)y_{2})

\displaystyle+\sup_{(\hat{x},\hat{y})\in X\times Y}\langle L(x_{1}+(1-\lambda)x_{2}),\hat{y}\rangle-\langle L\hat{x},y_{1}+(1-\lambda)y_{2}\rangle-f(\hat{x})-g^{*}(\hat{y})

\displaystyle\leq f(x_{1})+g^{*}(y_{1})+(1-\lambda)(f(x_{2})+g^{*}(y_{2}))+\sup_{(\hat{x},\hat{y})\in X\times Y}\langle Lx_{1},\hat{y}\rangle-\langle L\hat{x},y_{1}\rangle-f(\hat{x})-g^{*}(\hat{y})

\displaystyle+(1-\lambda)\sup_{(\hat{x},\hat{y})\in X\times Y}\langle Lx_{2},\hat{y}\rangle-\langle L\hat{x},y_{2}\rangle-f(\hat{x})-g^{*}(\hat{y})+\lambda(1-\lambda)\frac{\rho_{f}}{2}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{G}(x_{1},y_{1})+(1-\lambda)\mathcal{G}(x_{2},y_{2})+\lambda(1-\lambda)\frac{\rho_{f}}{2}\|(x_{1},y_{1})-(x_{2},y_{2})\|^{2}_{X\times Y},

(18)

where we use weak convexity of
f
with modulus
\rho_{f}
, convexity of
g^{*}
, and the norm of the Cartesian product
X\times Y
is defined as

\|(x,y)\|_{X\times Y}=\sqrt{\|x\|_{X}^{2}+\|y\|_{Y}^{2}}.

Hence, the standard duality gap function
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{L}}
is jointly weakly convex with modulus
\rho_{f}
.

3.1
Sharpness

Let us introduce the sharpness condition for the Lagrangian, which measures how far we are from the saddle point in terms of Lagrangian values.

Definition 4

(Sharpness)
.

Let us denote
z=(x,y)\in X\times Y=Z
, and let
S\subset Z
be the nonempty set of saddle point of
\mathcal{K}:Z\to(-\infty,+\infty]
. We say that
\mathcal{K}
is sharp if there exists a positive constant
\mu>0
such that

(\forall z\in Z)\quad\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{K}}(z)\geq\mu\text{dist}\left(z,S\right),

where the distance function is defined as

\text{dist}(z,S)=\inf_{\bar{z}\in Z}\|z-\bar{z}\|=\inf_{(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in S}\sqrt{\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}_{X}+\|y-\bar{y}\|^{2}_{Y}}.

Definition
4
coincides with sharpness condition of a proper function
F:X\to(-\infty,+\infty]
with respect to its minimizer, in view of the fact
\mathrm{argmin}\ \mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{K}}=S
,

F(x)-\inf_{\bar{x}\in X}F(\bar{x})\geq\mu\text{dist}(x,\arg\min F).

Sharpness condition is also known as error bound condition and is often used to speed up the convergence of algorithms
Roulet and d’Aspremont (2017)
;
Colbrook (2022)
;
Fercoq (2023)
.
In a nonconvex setting, sharpness is used to investigate the convergence of algorithm
Davis et al. (2018)
;
Li et al. (2019)
;
Chen et al. (2021)
. For necessary and sufficient conditions for error bound condition, the reader is referred to the works of
Drusvyatskiy and Lewis (2018)
;
Drusvyatskiy et al. (2021)
;
Cuong and Kruger (2022)
.

In the context of primal-dual problems, there are several ways of introducing sharpness conditions.
For example,
Applegate et al. (2023)
introduces the normalized duality gap function and proves that it is sharp for linear programming problems. They exploit its properties to obtain a linear convergence of the primal-dual algorithm with restart.
With an analogous approach,
Xiong and Freund (2023)
gives two new measures and condition to study the convergence of primal-dual algorithm based on normalized dual gap function, namely limiting error ratio and LP sharpness.
Conversely,
Fercoq (2023)
use smoothed dual gap function with quadratic growth condition (or sharpness of order
2
) to investigate linear convergence of primal-dual hybrid gradient for convex optimization problem.
Another line of work comes from
Colbrook (2022)
, where the author uses sharpness condition on the primal objective of the linear inverse problem (instead of the dual gap function) and study the linear convergence of the primal-dual algorithm.
To the best of our knowledge, these are the only works in the literature that use a concept analogous to the sharpness in the sense of Definition
4
.

Nevertheless, the duality gap function
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{K}}
is not often used to achieve convergence of primal-dual algorithm, even in the fully convex case. Instead, one can obtain the convergence by utilizing the gap function restricted on bounded set which contain the saddle points
(
Chambolle and Pock, 2011
, Theorem 1)
.
To investigate the convergence of the primal-dual Algorithm
1
and
2
, we introduce inf-sharpness condition below based on a modified gap function, which will be crucial in the convergence analysis, see, e.g., Theorem
2
and Theorem
3
.
The modified gap function
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{K}}:Z\to(-\infty,+\infty]
is defined as

\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{K}}(x,y):=\inf_{(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in S}\left\{\mathcal{K}\left(x,\bar{y}\right)-\mathcal{K}\left(\bar{x},y\right)\right\},

(19)

where
S
is the set of saddle points of
\mathcal{K}
.
Observe that
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{K}}(x,y)\geq 0
for any
(x,y)\in Z
and if
(x,y)\in S
then
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{K}}(x,y)=0
. The converse is not true, as we will see in Example
7
below. Moreover, we have

\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{K}}(x,y)\geq\sup_{(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in S}\left\{\mathcal{K}\left(x,\bar{y}\right)-\mathcal{K}\left(\bar{x},y\right)\right\}\geq\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{K}}(x,y)\geq\inf_{(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in X\times Y}\left\{\mathcal{K}\left(x,\bar{y}\right)-\mathcal{K}\left(\bar{x},y\right)\right\}.

(20)

Now, we are ready to introduce sharpness condition for function
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{K}}
which we will call Inf-Sharpness.

Definition 5

(Inf-Sharpness)
.

Let
S\subset Z
be the nonempty set of saddle points of the
\mathcal{K}
.
We say that the
\mathcal{K}
is
inf-sharp
if there exists a positive constant
\mu>0
such that,

(\forall z\in Z)\quad\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{K}}(z)\geq\mu\text{dist}\left(z,S\right).

(21)

Observe that, from (
20
), if the
\mathcal{K}
is inf-sharp, then we also have
\mathcal{K}
sharpness in the sense of Definition
4
with the same constant
\mu>0
.

In the case of Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}
defined in (
10
), if the functions
f
and
g^{*}
are strongly convex, the set of minimizers of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{L}}
coincides with the set of saddle points of problem (
2
). Indeed, let
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)=0
and
(x^{*},y^{*})\in S
be the saddle point such that the infimum is attained for
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)
, we have

0=\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)=\left(f(x)-f(x^{*})+\langle Lx,y^{*}\rangle\right)+\left(g^{*}(y)-g^{*}(y^{*})-\langle Lx^{*},y\rangle\right)\geq\frac{\rho_{f}}{2}\|x-x^{*}\|^{2}+\frac{\rho_{g^{*}}}{2}\|y-y^{*}\|^{2},

where
\rho_{f},\rho_{g^{*}}>0
are the strongly convex modulus of
f
and
g^{*}
, respectively.

In the fully weakly convex case, one can follow the approach of
Fercoq (2023)
and define the following function

\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{K}}(x,y):=\inf_{(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in S}\left\{\mathcal{K}(x,y^{\prime})-\mathcal{K}(x^{\prime},y)+\frac{\beta}{2}\left(\|x-x^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|y-y^{\prime}\|^{2}\right)\right\}\geq\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{K}}(x,y),

where
\beta>0
is the scaling parameter. Its minimizers are the saddle points of
\mathcal{K}
, and
\beta
can be taken to be a scaling parameter.
The use of the function
\mathcal{I}
, in the convergence analysis of Algorithm
1
and
2
, would require more constraints on the stepsizes.

4
Primal-Dual algorithm with Dual Update

Let us recall the Lagrange saddle point problem (
8
)

\inf_{x\in X}\sup_{y\in Y}f(x)+\langle Lx,y\rangle-g^{*}(y)=\inf_{x\in X}f(x)+g(Lx),

where
f
is proper lsc
\rho
-weakly convex function and
g
is proper lsc convex defined on Hilbert spaces
X
and
Y
, respectively, and
L:X\to Y
is a bounded linear operator with the adjoint operator
L^{*}:Y\to X
.
Our standing assumption in this section is that
\text{dom}\,f\cap\text{dom}\,g\circ L\neq\emptyset
and the set of saddle points
S
is nonempty.

For convex functions
f
and
g
, the analogous of Algorithm
1
has been studied in
Chambolle and Pock (2011)
.
In
He et al. (2014)
, the authors consider Algorithm
1
for convex case with no relaxation (
\theta=0
). They emphasize that the algorithm may diverge even if the Lagrangian is convex-concave.

In our setting, under the inf-sharpness condition defined in Definition
5
, we investigate the convergence of Algorithm
1
where function
f
is weakly convex and
g
is convex (see Theorem
2
).
For weakly convex function
f
, we use proximal subdifferentials. It has been proved that for this particular class of functions, the proximal subdifferential can be globalized (see
(
Jourani, 1996
, Proposition 3.1)
).

1:

Initialize:

x_{0}\in\text{dom}\,f\cap\text{dom}\,g\circ L
and
\bar{y}_{0}=y_{0}

2:

Set:

\tau,\sigma>0,\sigma\rho<1,\theta\in[0,1],\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|<1

3:

for

n\in\geq 0

do

4:

y_{n+1}=\text{argmin}_{y\in Y}g^{*}(y)+\frac{1}{2\tau}\|y-y_{n}-\tau Lx_{n}\|^{2}

\triangleright
(dual update)

5:

\bar{y}_{n+1}=y_{n+1}+\theta(y_{n+1}-y_{n})

\triangleright
(
\theta
relaxation step)

6:

x_{n+1}=\text{argmin}_{x\in X}f(x)+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\|x-x_{n}+\sigma L^{*}\bar{y}_{n+1}\|^{2}

\triangleright
(primal update)

7:

end

for

Algorithm 1

Primal-Dual Algorithm

In each step of Algorithm
1
, we have to solve two minimization problems where the objectives are convex thanks to the appropriate choice of stepsize
\sigma\rho<1
,

\displaystyle 0\in\partial\left(g^{*}+\frac{1}{2\tau}\|\cdot-y_{n}-\tau Lx_{n}\|^{2}\right)(y_{n+1}),

(25)

\displaystyle 0\in\partial\left(f+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\|\cdot-x_{n}+\sigma L^{*}\bar{y}_{n+1}\|^{2}\right)(x_{n+1}),

(26)

where
\partial
is the subdifferential in the sense of convex analysis.
We can apply the convex subdifferentials sum rule to the dual update (
25
) as both functions are convex
(
Bauschke and Combettes, 2017
, Corollary 16.48)
.
Equivalently, since
\sigma\rho<1
and
f
is weakly convex, we utilize the sum rule for proximal subdifferentials for weakly convex function in Theorem
1
with
\varepsilon=0
to (
26
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{y_{n}-y_{n+1}}{\tau}+Lx_{n}

\displaystyle\in\partial g^{*}\left(y_{n+1}\right),

(27)

\displaystyle\frac{x_{n}-x_{n+1}}{\sigma}-L^{*}\bar{y}_{n+1}

\displaystyle\in\partial_{\rho}f\left(x_{n+1}\right).

(28)

Without the condition
\sigma\rho<1
, the above equivalence between (
26
) and (
28
) do not hold.
For a detailed discussion of the splitting rule of weakly convex functions, the reader is referred to the work of
Bednarczuk et al. (2023a)
.

Let us estimate the Lagrangian values for the iterates generated by Algorithm
1
for the Lagrangian given in (
10
).

Proposition 2
.

Let
X,Y
be Hilbert spaces,
f:X\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be proper lsc
\rho
-weakly convex function and
g:Y\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be proper lsc convex defined on Hilbert spaces
X
and
Y
, respectively, and
L:X\to Y
be a bounded linear operator. Let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be the primal and dual sequences generated according to formulas in Algorithm
1
. Then for any
(x,y)\in X\times Y
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\left(x,y_{n+1}\right)-\mathcal{L}\left(x_{n+1},y\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1-\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\tau}\left\|y-y_{n+1}\right\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\tau}\left\|y-y_{n}\right\|^{2}+\frac{1-\theta\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\tau}\left\|y_{n}-y_{n+1}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1-\sigma\rho-\theta\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\sigma}\left\|x-x_{n+1}\right\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\sigma}\left\|x-x_{n}\right\|^{2}+\frac{1-\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\sigma}\left\|x_{n}-x_{n+1}\right\|^{2}.

(29)

Proof.

From the update of Algorithm
1
and (
28
), by definitions of subdifferentials, we have

\displaystyle(\forall x\in X)\ f(x)-f(x_{n+1})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\sigma}\langle x_{n}-x_{n+1},x-x_{n+1}\rangle-\langle L^{*}\bar{y}_{n+1},x-x_{n+1}\rangle-\frac{\rho}{2}\|x-x_{n+1}\|^{2},

\displaystyle(\forall y\in Y)\ g^{*}(y)-g^{*}(y_{n+1})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\tau}\langle y_{n}-y_{n+1},y-y_{n+1}\rangle+\langle Lx_{n},y-y_{n+1}\rangle,

Summing the above inequalities and taking into account the Lagrangian given in (
10
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\left(x,y_{n+1}\right)-\mathcal{L}\left(x_{n+1},y\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left\langle\frac{y_{n}-y_{n+1}}{\tau},y-y_{n+1}\right\rangle+\left\langle\frac{x_{n}-x_{n+1}}{\sigma},x-x_{n+1}\right\rangle-\frac{\rho}{2}\left\|x-x_{n+1}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\langle L(x_{n}-x_{n+1}),y-y_{n+1}\rangle+\langle L(x-x_{n+1}),y_{n+1}-\bar{y}_{n+1}\rangle,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\frac{y_{n}-y_{n+1}}{\tau},y-y_{n+1}\right\rangle+\left\langle\frac{x_{n}-x_{n+1}}{\sigma},x-x_{n+1}\right\rangle-\frac{\rho}{2}\left\|x-x_{n+1}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\langle L\left(x_{n}-x_{n+1}\right),y-y_{n+1}\rangle+\theta\langle L\left({x}-x_{n+1}\right),y_{n}-y_{n+1}\rangle

(30)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left\langle\frac{y_{n}-y_{n+1}}{\tau},y-y_{n+1}\right\rangle+\left\langle\frac{x_{n}-x_{n+1}}{\sigma},x-x_{n+1}\right\rangle-\frac{\rho}{2}\left\|x-x_{n+1}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle-\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|\left(\frac{\|x_{n}-x_{n+1}\|^{2}}{2\sigma}+\frac{\|y-y_{n+1}\|^{2}}{2\tau}+\theta\frac{\|x-x_{n+1}\|^{2}}{2\sigma}+\theta\frac{\|y_{n}-y_{n+1}\|^{2}}{2\tau}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1-\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\tau}\left\|y-y_{n+1}\right\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\tau}\left\|y-y_{n}\right\|^{2}+\frac{1-\theta\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\tau}\left\|y_{n}-y_{n+1}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1-\sigma\rho-\theta\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\sigma}\left\|x-x_{n+1}\right\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\sigma}\left\|x-x_{n}\right\|^{2}+\frac{1-\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\sigma}\left\|x_{n}-x_{n+1}\right\|^{2},

which is (
29
).

\hbox to0.0pt{$\sqcap$\hss}\sqcup

Noted that we only require
\sigma\rho<1
in Proposition
2
to solve a convex minimization sub-problem in the primal update of Algorithm
1
.
To investigate the convergence of Algorithm
1
, we will utilize the inf-sharpness assumption and estimation (
29
).
We show that if
(x_{n},y_{n})
are close enough to the solution set
S
, then we have decreasing property for the distance of
(x_{n},y_{n})
to the set
S
.

Proposition 3
.

Let
X
and
Y
be Hilbert,
f:X\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be a proper lsc
\rho
-weakly convex function,
g:Y\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be proper lsc convex and
L:X\to Y
be a bounded linear operator. Let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be the sequences generated by Algorithm
1
. Let us assume that the stepsizes
\tau,\sigma
satisfy the inequality
\sigma\rho+\theta\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|<1
, where
\theta\in[0,1]
, and the Lagrangian is inf-sharp in the sense of Definition
5
with respect to the set of saddle point
S\neq\emptyset
. At iteration
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\text{dist}((x_{n},y_{n}),S)\leq\frac{\mu}{\max\left\{\frac{1}{2\sigma},\frac{1}{2\tau}\right\}-A}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{dist}((x_{n+1},y_{n+1}),S)\leq\frac{\mu}{\max\left\{\frac{1}{2\sigma},\frac{1}{2\tau}\right\}-A},

(31)

where
A:=\min\left\{\frac{1-\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\tau},\frac{1-\sigma\rho-\theta\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\sigma}\right\}
.
Moreover,

\text{dist}((x_{n},y_{n}),S)\geq\text{dist}((x_{n+1},y_{n+1}),S).

(32)

Proof.

Let us prove (
32
) first. By (
29
) from Proposition
2
, for any
(x,y)\in X\times Y
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\left(x,y_{n+1}\right)-\mathcal{L}\left(x_{n+1},y\right)+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\left\|x-x_{n}\right\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\tau}\left\|y-y_{n}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1-\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\tau}\left\|y-y_{n+1}\right\|^{2}+\frac{1-\theta\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\tau}\left\|y_{n}-y_{n+1}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1-\sigma\rho-\theta\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\sigma}\left\|x-x_{n+1}\right\|^{2}+\frac{1-\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\sigma}\left\|x_{n}-x_{n+1}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1-\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\tau}\left\|y-y_{n+1}\right\|^{2}+\frac{1-\sigma\rho-\theta\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\sigma}\left\|x-x_{n+1}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle A\inf_{(x,y)\in S}\left\|x-x_{n+1}\right\|^{2}+\left\|y-y_{n+1}\right\|^{2}=A\text{dist}^{2}((x_{n+1},y_{n+1}),S),

(33)

where
A:=\min\left\{\frac{1-\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\tau},\frac{1-\sigma\rho-\theta\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\sigma}\right\}>0
thanks to the assumption on the stepsizes.
The last inequality in (
33
) holds as we can take any
(x,y)\in S
.
Rearrange both sides of (
33
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sigma}\left\|x-x_{n}\right\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\tau}\left\|y-y_{n}\right\|^{2}\geq

\displaystyle A\text{dist}^{2}((x_{n+1},y_{n+1}),S)+\mathcal{L}\left(x_{n+1},y\right)-\mathcal{L}\left(x,y_{n+1}\right).

(34)

Let us take infimum on both sides of (
34
) with respect to
(x,y)\in S
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\max\left\{\frac{1}{2\sigma},\frac{1}{2\tau}\right\}\text{dist}^{2}((x_{n},y_{n}),S)

\displaystyle\geq A\text{dist}^{2}((x_{n+1},y_{n+1}),S)+\underbrace{\inf_{(x,y)\in S}\left\{\mathcal{L}\left(x_{n+1},y\right)-\mathcal{L}\left(x,y_{n+1}\right)\right\}}_{=\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{L}}(x_{n+1},y_{n+1})}.

(35)

We apply sharpness assumption to (
35
) and obtain

\max\left\{\frac{1}{2\sigma},\frac{1}{2\tau}\right\}\text{dist}^{2}((x_{n},y_{n}),S)\geq A\text{dist}^{2}((x_{n+1},y_{n+1}),S)+\mu\text{dist}((x_{n+1},y_{n+1}),S).

(36)

To obtain (
32
), we need

\displaystyle A\text{dist}^{2}((x_{n+1},y_{n+1}),S)+\mu\text{dist}((x_{n+1},y_{n+1}),S)

\displaystyle\geq\max\left\{\frac{1}{2\sigma},\frac{1}{2\tau}\right\}\text{dist}^{2}((x_{n+1},y_{n+1}),S),

which holds when

\text{dist}((x_{n+1},y_{n+1}),S)\leq\frac{\mu}{\max\left\{\frac{1}{2\sigma},\frac{1}{2\tau}\right\}-A}.

(37)

The quantity on RHS of (
37
) is well-defined as

\max\left\{\frac{1}{2\sigma},\frac{1}{2\tau}\right\}\geq\min\left\{\frac{1}{2\sigma},\frac{1}{2\tau}\right\}>\min\left\{\frac{1-\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\tau},\frac{1-\sigma\rho-\theta\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\sigma}\right\},

thanks to the assumptions on the stepsizes
\tau,\sigma
. Next, we will prove that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}

\text{dist}((x_{n},y_{n}),S)\leq\frac{\mu}{\max\left\{\frac{1}{2\sigma},\frac{1}{2\tau}\right\}-A}\Rightarrow\text{dist}((x_{n+1},y_{n+1}),S)\leq\frac{\mu}{\max\left\{\frac{1}{2\sigma},\frac{1}{2\tau}\right\}-A}.

Let us start from (
36
)

\displaystyle\max\left\{\frac{1}{2\sigma},\frac{1}{2\tau}\right\}\frac{\mu^{2}}{\left(\max\left\{\frac{1}{2\sigma},\frac{1}{2\tau}\right\}-A\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\max\left\{\frac{1}{2\sigma},\frac{1}{2\tau}\right\}\text{dist}^{2}((x_{n},y_{n}),S)

\displaystyle\geq A\text{dist}^{2}((x_{n+1},y_{n+1}),S)+\mu\text{dist}((x_{n+1},y_{n+1}),S).

(38)

Inequality (
38
) becomes a quadratic inequality with respect to
\text{dist}((x_{n+1},y_{n+1}),S)
. The quadratic equation taken from (
38
) has two distinct solutions, which implies that

0\leq\text{dist}((x_{n+1},y_{n+1}),S)\leq\frac{\mu}{\max\left\{\frac{1}{2\sigma},\frac{1}{2\tau}\right\}-A}.

\hbox to0.0pt{$\sqcap$\hss}\sqcup

Remark 1
.

When the iterates are close enough to the solution
S
, the sharpness of order
1
implies the sharpness of order
2
, which is known as the quadratic growth condition. It has been shown in
(
Liao et al., 2024
, Theorem 3.1)
that for a weakly convex function (see formula (
18
) for the weak convexity of the duality gap function
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{L}}
), quadratic growth condition is equivalent to subdifferential error bound and PŁ inequality, which are well-known regularity conditions used to achieve linear convergence rate.
On the other hand, inf-sharpness of function
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{L}}
(Definition
5
) implies sharpness of order
1
of function
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{L}}
.

When inequality in (
31
) is strict, we obtain convergence of the distance function.

Corollary 1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be Hilbert,
f:X\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be a proper lsc
\rho
-weakly convex function,
g:Y\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be proper lsc convex and
L:X\to Y
be a bounded linear operator. Let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be the sequences generated by Algorithm
1
. Let us assume that
\sigma\rho+\theta\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|<1
and the Lagrangian is inf-sharp in the sense of Definition
5
with respect to the set of saddle points
S\neq\emptyset
. If the starting point
(x_{0},y_{0})
satisfies

\text{dist}((x_{0},y_{0}),S)<\frac{\mu}{\max\left\{\frac{1}{2\sigma},\frac{1}{2\tau}\right\}-A},

where
A=\min\left\{\frac{1-\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\tau},\frac{1-\sigma\rho-\theta\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\sigma}\right\}
.
Then
\text{dist}((x_{n},y_{n}),S)
tends to zero as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

By assumption and Proposition
3
,
\text{dist}((x_{n},y_{n}),S)
is non-increasing and

\text{dist}((x_{n},y_{n}),S)<\frac{\mu}{\max\left\{\frac{1}{2\sigma},\frac{1}{2\tau}\right\}-A},

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\left(A+\frac{\mu}{\text{dist}((x_{0},y_{0}),S)}\right)\text{dist}^{2}((x_{n+1},y_{n+1}),S)

\displaystyle\leq\left(A+\frac{\mu}{\text{dist}((x_{n+1},y_{n+1}),S)}\right)\text{dist}^{2}((x_{n+1},y_{n+1}),S)

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\frac{1}{2\sigma},\frac{1}{2\tau}\right\}\text{dist}^{2}((x_{n},y_{n}),S).

(39)

Inequality (
39
) implies that

\text{dist}^{2}((x_{n+1},y_{n+1}),S)\leq(B)^{n-1}\text{dist}^{2}((x_{0},y_{0}),S),

(40)

where

B:=\frac{\max\left\{\frac{1}{2\sigma},\frac{1}{2\tau}\right\}}{A+\frac{\mu}{\text{dist}((x_{0},y_{0}),S)}}.

By assumption on
\text{dist}((x_{0},y_{0}),S)
, we have
0<B<1
since

A+\frac{\mu}{\text{dist}((x_{0},y_{0}),S)}>\max\left\{\frac{1}{2\sigma},\frac{1}{2\tau}\right\}.

As (
40
) holds for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we let
n\to\infty
and we infer that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\text{dist}((x_{n},y_{n}),S)=0.

\hbox to0.0pt{$\sqcap$\hss}\sqcup

Next, we prove the convergence of
(x_{n},y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
to a saddle point in
S
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
X
and
Y
be Hilbert,
f:X\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be a proper lsc
\rho
-weakly convex function,
g:Y\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be proper lsc convex and
L:X\to Y
be a bounded linear operator. Let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be the sequences generated by Algorithm
1
. Let us assume that the stepsizes
\tau,\sigma
satisfy
\sigma\rho+\theta\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|<1
, and the Lagrangian is inf-sharp in the sense of Definition
5
with respect to the set of saddle point
S\neq\emptyset
. If the starting point
(x_{0},y_{0})
satisfies

\text{dist}((x_{0},y_{0}),S)<\frac{\mu}{\max\left\{\frac{1}{2\sigma},\frac{1}{2\tau}\right\}-A},

where
A=\min\left\{\frac{1-\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\tau},\frac{1-\sigma\rho-\theta\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\sigma}\right\}.

Then
(x_{n},y_{n})
converges to a saddle point.

Proof.

By (
29
) from Proposition
2
and the inf-sharpness of Lagrangian, we have

\displaystyle\max\left\{\frac{1}{2\sigma},\frac{1}{2\tau}\right\}\text{dist}^{2}((x_{n},y_{n}),S)

\displaystyle\geq A\text{dist}^{2}((x_{n+1},y_{n+1}),S)+\mu\text{dist}((x_{n+1},y_{n+1}),S)

\displaystyle+\frac{1-\theta\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\tau}\left\|y_{n}-y_{n+1}\right\|^{2}+\frac{1-\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\sigma}\left\|x_{n}-x_{n+1}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\min\left\{\frac{1-\theta\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\tau},\frac{1-\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\sigma}\right\}\|(x_{n},y_{n})-(x_{n+1},y_{n+1})\|^{2}_{X\times Y}.

(41)

Taking square root on both sides of (
41
) and summing up till
N\in\mathbb{N}
, combining with the result from Corollary

, we obtain

\displaystyle C\sum_{i=0}^{N}B^{\frac{i-1}{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=0}^{N}\|(x_{i},y_{i})-(x_{i+1},y_{i+1})\|_{X\times Y},

(42)

where

B=\frac{\max\left\{\frac{1}{2\sigma},\frac{1}{2\tau}\right\}}{A+\frac{\mu}{\text{dist}((x_{0},y_{0}),S)}},\quad C:=\sqrt{\frac{\max\left\{\frac{1}{2\sigma},\frac{1}{2\tau}\right\}}{\min\left\{\frac{1-\theta\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\tau},\frac{1-\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\sigma}\right\}}}\text{dist}((x_{0},y_{0}),S).

From the proof of Corollary
1
we have
0<B<1
. Letting
N\to\infty
, the LHS of (
42
) is summable which implies that

\sum_{i}\|(x_{i},y_{i})-(x_{i+1},y_{i+1})\|_{X\times Y}<+\infty.

As
X,Y
are Hilbert, so is
X\times Y
. By
(

, Theorem 1)
,
(x_{n},y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is Cauchy , so it converges. Combining this with the result obtained in Corollary

which is

,
(x_{n},y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
must converge to a saddle point in

.

6
Solution to the Primal Problem

While the inf-sharpness in the sense of Definition
5
helps us analyze the convergence of Algorithm
1
and Algorithm
2
, one needs to know the set of saddle points (both the primal and dual solutions) to calculate
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{K}}(x,y)
.
It is interesting to solve problem (
2
) with only knowledge of the primal objective; hence, we ask ourselves how much we know about the convergence of Algorithm
1
or
2
. Is it possible to assume sharpness condition only on the primal objective functions instead of on the Lagrangian, and how is it related to Definition
4
and Definition
5
?

In this section, we consider the same setting as in Section
3
, where
f
is
\rho
-weakly convex and
g
is convex. We analyze the gap function
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)
given by (
17
) with the Lagrangian given in (
10
),

\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)=\sup_{\overline{x}\in X,\overline{y}\in Y}\mathcal{L}(x,\overline{y})-\mathcal{L}(\overline{x},y)=f(x)+g^{**}(Lx)+g^{*}(y)+\sup_{\overline{x}\in X}\{-\langle L\overline{x},y\rangle-f(\overline{x})\}.

Since
g
is convex, we have
g=g^{**}
. Let us take

\displaystyle\inf_{y\in Y}\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\inf_{y\in Y}\left\{(f+g\circ L)(x)+\sup_{\overline{x}\in X}\{-\langle L\overline{x},y\rangle-f(\overline{x})+g^{*}(y)\}\right\}

\displaystyle=(f+g\circ L)(x)-\sup_{y\in Y}\inf_{\overline{x}\in X}\left\{f(\overline{x})+\langle L\overline{x},y\rangle-g^{*}(y)\right\}

\displaystyle\geq(f+g\circ L)(x)-\inf_{\overline{x}\in X}\sup_{y\in Y}\mathcal{L}(\overline{x},y)

\displaystyle=(f+g\circ L)(x)-\inf_{\overline{x}\in X}(f+g\circ L)(\overline{x}),

(47)

where we use weak duality in the third inequality, and the last equality is the equivalence between Lagrangian primal (
8
) and primal problem (
2
) thanks to the convexity of
g
.

Before discussing the convergence of the algorithm, let us give a proper definition of sharpness for problem (
2
).

Definition 6

(Sharpness of Primal Objective)
.

We say that the objective function
f+g\circ L
is sharp with respect to the set of minimizers
S_{P}=\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}{}\end{subarray}}{\mathrm{argmin}}\;\;(f+g\circ L)\neq\emptyset
if there exists a positive constant
\mu
such that

(\forall x\in X)\quad(f+g\circ L)(x)-\inf_{z\in X}(f+g\circ L)(z)\geq\mu\text{dist}\left(x,S_{P}\right).

(48)

Similarly, we can also define sharpness for the Lagrangian dual problem (
9
) as follow: there exists
\eta>0
such that

(\forall y_{0}\in Y)\quad\sup_{y\in Y}\inf_{x\in X}\mathcal{L}(x,y)-\inf_{x\in X}\mathcal{L}(x,y_{0})\geq\eta\text{dist}(y_{0},S_{D}),

(49)

where
S_{D}=\text{argmax}_{y\in Y}\{\inf_{x\in X}\mathcal{L}(x,y)\}
is the solution to the dual problem.

The relationship between the sharpness of the primal problem and the sharpness of Lagrangian is described in the next proposition.

Proposition 6
.

Let us consider the composite problem (
2
) and its corresponding Lagrangian given in (
10
).
If the Lagrangian is sharp in the sense of Definition
4
then the primal objective function is sharp as in Definition
6
.
On the other hand, if the objective primal
f+g\circ L
is sharp according to Definition
6
and the respective Lagrange dual is sharp as in (
49
) then there exists
\xi>0
such that

\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)\geq\xi(\text{dist}(x,S_{P})+\text{dist}(y,S_{D})),

where
S_{P},S_{D}
are the solution sets of the primal (
6
) and dual (
49
) problems, respectively.

Proof.

By Definition
4
,
we have

\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)=f(x)+g^{*}(y)+\sup_{(\hat{x},\hat{y})\in X\times Y}\langle Lx,\hat{y}\rangle-\langle L\hat{x},y\rangle-f(\hat{x})-g^{*}(\hat{y})\geq\mu\text{dist}((x,y),S),

where
S
is the set of saddle points.
The RHS of the above inequality can be written

\displaystyle\text{dist}((x,y),S)

\displaystyle=\inf_{(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in S}\|(x,y)-(\bar{x},\bar{y})\|_{X\times Y}=\inf_{(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in S}\sqrt{\|x-\bar{x}\|_{X}^{2}+\|y-\bar{y}\|^{2}_{Y}}

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in S}\|x-\bar{x}\|_{X}\geq\text{dist}(x,S_{P}).

(50)

On the other hand, observe that

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=f(x)+g^{*}(y)+\sup_{(\hat{x},\hat{y})\in X\times Y}\left\{\langle Lx,\hat{y}\rangle-\langle L\hat{x},y\rangle-f(\hat{x})-g^{*}(\hat{y})\right\}

\displaystyle=f(x)+g^{*}(y)+\sup_{\hat{x}\in X}\left\{-\langle L\hat{x},y\rangle-f(\hat{x})\right\}+\sup_{\hat{y}\in Y}\left\{\langle Lx,\hat{y}\rangle-g^{*}(\hat{y})\right\}

\displaystyle=f(x)+g^{*}(y)+g^{**}(Lx)+\sup_{\hat{x}\in X}\left\{-\langle L\hat{x},y\rangle-f(\hat{x})\right\}

\displaystyle=f(x)+g(Lx)+\sup_{\hat{x}\in X}\left\{-\langle L\hat{x},y\rangle-f(\hat{x})+g^{*}(y)\right\}.

(51)

Combining (
50
) and (
51
)

f(x)+g(Lx)+\sup_{\hat{x}\in X}\left\{-\langle L\hat{x},y\rangle-f(\hat{x})+g^{*}(y)\right\}\geq\mu\text{dist}(x,S_{P}).

(52)

Since (
52
) holds for any
(x,y)\in X\times Y
, we fix
y=y^{*}
as a saddle point and obtain

\displaystyle(f+g\circ L)(x)-\inf_{\hat{x}\in X}\sup_{\hat{y}\in Y}\left\{\langle L\hat{x},\hat{y}\rangle+f(\hat{x})-g^{*}(\hat{y})\right\}

\displaystyle\geq(f+g\circ L)(x)-\inf_{\hat{x}\in X}\left\{f(\hat{x})+\langle L\hat{x},y^{*}\rangle-g^{*}(y^{*})\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\mu\text{dist}(x,S_{P}).

Thanks to the convexity of
g
, the Lagrange primal has the same value as problem (
2
). Hence, we obtain

(f+g\circ L)(x)-\inf_{\hat{x}\in X}(f+g\circ L)(\hat{x})\geq\mu\text{dist}(x,S_{P}).

For the second statement, let us assume that
f+g\circ L
is sharp, then there exists
\mu\geq 0
such that

(f+g\circ L)(x)-\inf_{\hat{x}\in X}f(\hat{x})+g(L\hat{x})\geq\mu\text{dist}(x,S_{P}),

for any
x_{0}\in X
and
S_{P}=\text{argmin}f+g\circ L
.
We use (
47
) and obtain

\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)\geq\inf_{y\in Y}\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)\geq(f+g\circ L)(x)-\inf_{\overline{x}\in X}(f+g\circ L)(\overline{x})\geq\mu\text{dist}(x,S_{P}).

(53)

We recall that the dual problem has the form

\sup_{y\in Y}\inf_{x\in X}\mathcal{L}(x,y)=\sup_{y\in Y}-f^{*}(-L^{*}y)-g^{*}(y),

then sharpness for the objective of the dual problem as in (
49
) is

(\exists\eta>0)(\forall y\in Y)\quad\sup_{\hat{y}\in Y}-f^{*}(-L^{*}\hat{y})-g^{*}(\hat{y})+f^{*}(-L^{*}{y})+g^{*}({y})\geq\eta\text{dist}(y,S_{D}).

With the same argument with the primal problem as above, we get the following

\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)\geq\eta\text{dist}(y,S_{D}).

(54)

Summing (
53
) and (
54
),

\displaystyle 2\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)\geq\mu\text{dist}(x,S_{P})+\eta\text{dist}(y,S_{D})

\displaystyle\geq\min\{\mu,\eta\}\left(\text{dist}(x,S_{P})+\text{dist}(y,S_{D})\right).

(55)

Denoting
\xi=\min\{\mu,\eta\}/2
, we finish the proof.

where

\mu>0

\eta

x^{*}\in S_{P}

\displaystyle-\inf_{\overline{x}\in X}(f+g\circ L)(\overline{x})=-\left(f+g\circ L\right)\left(x^{*}\right)

corresposding to

\displaystyle\left(f+g\circ L\right)\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}\left(x,y^{*}\right)+g\left(Lx\right)+g^{*}\left(y^{*}\right)-\left\langle Lx,y^{*}\right\rangle.

\displaystyle\left(f+g\circ L\right)\left(x\right)-\inf\left(f+g\circ L\right)

(

\mu\text{dist }\left(x,S_{P}\right)\leq\left(f+g\circ L\right)\left(x\right)-\left(f+g\circ L\right)\left(x^{*}\right)\leq\mathcal{L}\left(x,y^{*}\right)-\mathcal{L}\left(x^{*},y\right)+\left\langle Lx-Lx^{*},\overline{y}-y^{*}\right\rangle,

\mu\text{dist }\left(x,S_{P}\right)+\inf_{\left(x^{*},y^{*}\right)\in S}-\left\langle Lx-Lx^{*},\overline{y}-y^{*}\right\rangle\leq\inf_{\left(x^{*},y^{*}\right)\in S}\mathcal{L}\left(x,y^{*}\right)-\mathcal{L}\left(x^{*},y\right)=\mathcal{H}\left(x,y\right)

On the other hand, when both

\displaystyle\mu\text{dist }\left(x,S_{P}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{L}\left(x,y^{*}\right)-\mathcal{L}\left(x^{*},y\right)+\left\langle Lx-Lx^{*},\overline{y}-y^{*}\right\rangle-\frac{1}{2\eta}\|y-y^{*}\|^{2}-\frac{\eta}{2}\|Lx-Lx^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}\left(x,y^{*}\right)-\mathcal{L}\left(x^{*},y\right)-\frac{1}{2\eta}\|y-y^{*}\|^{2}-\frac{\eta}{2}\|Lx-Lx^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left(\alpha\|Lx-Lx^{*}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\|\overline{y}-y^{*}\|^{2}-\|\sqrt{\alpha}{Lx-Lx^{*}}-\frac{\overline{y}-y^{*}}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{L}\left(x,y^{*}\right)-\mathcal{L}\left(x^{*},y\right)+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|\overline{y}-y^{*}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\eta}\|y-y^{*}\|^{2}+\frac{\alpha-\eta}{2}\|Lx-Lx^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{L}\left(x,y^{*}\right)-\mathcal{L}\left(x^{*},y\right)+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|\overline{y}-y^{*}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\eta}\|y-y^{*}\|^{2}.

where
\eta>\alpha>0
. Letting
y=\overline{y}
and taking the infimum with respect to
(x^{*},y^{*})\in S
, and we finish the proof.

\hbox to0.0pt{$\sqcap$\hss}\sqcup

Before using primal sharpness to discuss convergence of primal-dual algorithm, we need to transform the Lagrangian into primal objective. For
(x,y),(x_{0},y_{0})\in X\times Y
, we consider

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(x,y_{0})-\mathcal{L}(x_{0},y)

\displaystyle=f(x)-f(x_{0})+g^{*}(y)-g^{*}(y_{0})+\langle Lx,y_{0}\rangle-\langle Lx_{0},y\rangle

\displaystyle\geq f(x)-f(x_{0})-g^{*}(y_{0})+\langle Lx,y_{0}\rangle-g^{**}(Lx_{0})

\displaystyle=(f+g\circ L)(x)-(f+g\circ L)(x_{0})-g^{*}(y_{0})+\langle Lx,y_{0}\rangle-g(Lx),

(58)

where we utilize the convexity of
g
in the last equality. Let us fix
(x,y)
and take
y_{0}\in\partial g(Lx)
, then for any
x_{0}\in X
, (
58
) becomes

\mathcal{L}(x,y_{0})-\mathcal{L}(x_{0},y)\geq(f+g\circ L)(x)-(f+g\circ L)(x_{0}).

(59)

Inequality (
59
) indicates that the choice of
y_{0}
depends on
x
. For primal-dual algorithms, one often arrive at the term
\mathcal{L}(x_{n+1},y_{0})-\mathcal{L}(x_{0},y_{n+1})
for any
(x_{0},y_{0})\in X\times Y
, and so we need to know
x_{n+1}
before choosing
y_{0}
. In this case, Algorithm
2
is more suitable for us as we perform the primal update before the dual. Therefore, we focus on Algorithm
2
in this section. This will be made clearer in the following result.

Proposition 7
.

Let
X
and
Y
be Hilbert,
f:X\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be a proper lsc
\rho
-weakly convex function,
g:Y\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be proper lsc convex and
L:X\to Y
be a bounded linear operator. Let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be the sequences generated by Algorithm
2
. Let us assume that the stepsizes
\tau,\sigma
satisfy the inequality
\sigma\rho+\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|<1
, and primal objective is sharp as in Definition
6
with respect to the set of
S_{P}=\arg\min(f+g\circ L)\neq\emptyset
. Then we have the following,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sigma}\text{dist}^{2}(x_{n},S_{P})+\frac{1}{2\tau}\text{dist}^{2}(y_{n},\partial g(Lx_{n+1}))

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1-\sigma\rho-\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\sigma}\text{dist}^{2}(x_{n+1},S_{P})+\mu\text{dist}(x_{n+1},S_{P}).

(60)

Proof.

Let us recall (
43
) in Proposition
4
, for any
(x,y)\in X\times Y
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\left(x,y_{n+1}\right)-\mathcal{L}\left(x_{n+1},y\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1-\theta\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\tau}\left\|y-y_{n+1}\right\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\tau}\left\|y-y_{n}\right\|^{2}+\frac{1-\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\tau}\left\|y_{n}-y_{n+1}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1-\sigma\rho-\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\sigma}\left\|x-x_{n+1}\right\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\sigma}\left\|x-x_{n}\right\|^{2}+\frac{1-\theta\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\sigma}\left\|x_{n}-x_{n+1}\right\|^{2}.

(61)

We utilise (
59
) to obtain

\mathcal{L}(x_{n+1},y_{0})-\mathcal{L}(x,y_{n+1})\geq(f+g\circ L)(x_{n+1})-(f+g\circ L)(x),

(62)

for any
x\in X
and
y=y^{*}_{n+1}\in\partial g(Lx_{n+1})
. Combining (
62
), sharpness condition (
48
) and take
x^{*}\in S_{P}
, (
61
) becomes

\displaystyle-\mu\text{dist}\left(x_{n+1},S_{P}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1-\theta\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\tau}\left\|y^{*}_{n+1}-y_{n+1}\right\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\tau}\left\|y^{*}_{n+1}-y_{n}\right\|^{2}+\frac{1-\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\tau}\left\|y_{n}-y_{n+1}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1-\sigma\rho-\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\sigma}\left\|x^{*}-x_{n+1}\right\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\sigma}\left\|x^{*}-x_{n}\right\|^{2}+\frac{1-\theta\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\sigma}\left\|x_{n}-x_{n+1}\right\|^{2}.

As
y^{*}_{n+1}\in\partial g(Lx_{n+1})
and
x^{*}\in S_{P}
can be taken arbitrarily, we obtain

\displaystyle-\mu\text{dist}\left(x_{n+1},S_{P}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1-\theta\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\tau}\text{dist}^{2}(y_{n+1},\partial g(Lx_{n+1}))-\frac{1}{2\tau}\text{dist}^{2}(y_{n},\partial g(Lx_{n+1}))

\displaystyle+\frac{1-\sigma\rho-\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\sigma}\text{dist}^{2}(x_{n+1},S_{P})-\frac{1}{2\sigma}\text{dist}^{2}(x_{n},S_{P})

\displaystyle+\frac{1-\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\tau}\left\|y_{n}-y_{n+1}\right\|^{2}+\frac{1-\theta\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\sigma}\left\|x_{n}-x_{n+1}\right\|^{2}.

By assumption on the stepsizes
\tau,\sigma
, we obtain (
60
).

\hbox to0.0pt{$\sqcap$\hss}\sqcup

This shows that the convergence of
(\text{dist}\left(x_{n},S_{P}\right))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
depends on the distance of the dual sequence
(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
to
\partial g(Lx_{n+1})
. This occurs since we only make assumption on the primal objective, and do not have additional information on the dual variable
y\in Y
like in Definition
5
.
Hence, one can think of the term
(\text{dist}\left(y_{n},\partial g(Lx_{n+1}\right))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
as the inexactness produce by the algorithm, despite the fact that proximal calculation is exact. For simplicity, we denote
\varepsilon_{n}:=\text{dist}(y_{n},\partial g(Lx_{n+1}))\geq 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This quantity can be checked after the primal update and with
\varepsilon_{n}
small enough, we can obtain convergence, as we will see below.

Lemma 3
.

Let
X
and
Y
be Hilbert,
f:X\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be a proper lsc
\rho
-weakly convex function,
g:Y\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be proper lsc convex and
L:X\to Y
be a bounded linear operator. Let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be the sequences generated by Algorithm
2
. Let us assume that the stepsizes
\tau,\sigma
satisfy the inequality
\sigma\rho+\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|<1
, where
\theta\in[0,1]
, and primal objective is sharp as in Definition
6
with respect to the set of
S_{P}=\arg\min(f+g\circ L)\neq\emptyset
. If
\sigma,\tau
satisfy the following

(\forall n\in\mathbb{N})\quad\mu^{2}\sigma\tau>(\sigma\rho+\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|)\varepsilon_{n}^{2},\ \varepsilon_{n}=\text{dist}(y_{n},\partial g(Lx_{n+1})),

and at iteration
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\text{dist}\left(x_{n},S_{P}\right)\leq E^{+}_{n}\quad

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\quad\text{dist}(x_{n+1},S_{P})\leq E^{+}_{n},

(63)

\displaystyle\text{dist}\left(x_{n},S_{P}\right)\leq E^{-}_{n}\quad

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\quad\text{dist}(x_{n+1},S_{P})\leq E^{-}_{n},

(64)

where

E_{n}^{+}:=\frac{\sigma\mu+\sigma\sqrt{\mu^{2}-\frac{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}(\sigma\rho+\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|)}{\sigma\tau}}}{\sigma\rho+\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|},\quad E_{n}^{-}:=\frac{\sigma\mu-\sigma\sqrt{\mu^{2}-\frac{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}(\sigma\rho+\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|)}{\sigma\tau}}}{\sigma\rho+\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}.

Moreover, if

E_{n}^{-}\leq\text{dist}(x_{n+1},S_{P})\leq E_{n}^{+},

(65)

Then
\text{dist}(x_{n+1},S_{P})\leq\text{dist}(x_{n},S_{P})
.

Proof.

From (
60
), we have

\frac{1}{2\sigma}\text{dist}^{2}(x_{n},S_{P})\geq\frac{1-\sigma\rho-\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\sigma}\text{dist}^{2}(x_{n+1},S_{P})+\mu\text{dist}(x_{n+1},S_{P})-\frac{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}{2\tau}.

(66)

We would like the RHS of (
66
) satisfies

\frac{1-\sigma\rho-\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\sigma}\text{dist}^{2}(x_{n+1},S_{P})+\mu\text{dist}(x_{n+1},S_{P})-\frac{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}{2\tau}\geq\frac{1}{2\sigma}\text{dist}^{2}(x_{n+1},S_{P}),

which is equivalent to solving the following quadratic inequality

\frac{\sigma\rho+\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\sigma}z^{2}-\mu z+\frac{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}{2\tau}\leq 0.

Thanks to the assumption
\mu^{2}\sigma\tau>(\sigma\rho+\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|)\varepsilon_{n}^{2}
, we obtain the solution

\frac{\sigma\mu-\sigma\sqrt{\mu^{2}-\frac{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}(\sigma\rho+\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|)}{\sigma\tau}}}{\sigma\rho+\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}\leq\text{dist}(x_{n+1},S_{P})\leq\frac{\sigma\mu+\sigma\sqrt{\mu^{2}-\frac{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}(\sigma\rho+\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|)}{\sigma\tau}}}{\sigma\rho+\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|},

which is (
65
). If (
65
) holds, we obtain
\text{dist}(x_{n+1},S_{P})\leq\text{dist}(x_{n},S_{P})
.

Let us prove (
63
), assume that for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
\text{dist}(x_{n},S_{P})\leq E_{n}^{+}
. By (
66
), we have

\frac{1}{2\sigma}(E_{n}^{+})^{2}\geq\frac{1-\sigma\rho-\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\sigma}\text{dist}^{2}(x_{n+1},S_{P})+\mu\text{dist}(x_{n+1},S_{P})-\frac{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}{2\tau}.

(67)

By solving (
67
), we obtain,

0\leq d(x_{n+1},S_{P})\leq E_{n}^{+}.

The proof for (
64
) can be done in the same way.

\hbox to0.0pt{$\sqcap$\hss}\sqcup

Lemma
3
describes the behavior between
\text{dist}(x_{n+1},S_{P})
and
E_{n}^{+}
. Condition (
65
) is quite restrictive as we need to bound
\text{dist}(x_{n+1},S_{P})
on both sides. In the next result ,we give a relaxed estimation of
(\text{dist}(x_{n},S_{P}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
X
and
Y
be Hilbert,
f:X\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be a proper lsc
\rho
-weakly convex function,
g:Y\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be proper lsc convex and
L:X\to Y
be a bounded linear operator. Let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be the sequences generated by Algorithm
2
. Let us assume that the stepsizes
\tau,\sigma
satisfy the inequality
\sigma\rho+\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|<1
, and primal objective is sharp as in Definition
6
with respect to the set of
S_{P}=\arg\min(f+g\circ L)\neq\emptyset
. If
\sigma,\tau
satisfy the following

(\forall n\in\mathbb{N})\quad\mu^{2}\sigma\tau>(\sigma\rho+\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|)\varepsilon_{n}^{2},

and at iteration
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\text{dist}\left(x_{n},S_{P}\right)\leq E^{+}_{n}.

Then there exists
\xi_{n}\geq 1
such that

\xi_{n+1}\left[\text{dist}^{2}(x_{n+1},S_{P})-(E_{n}^{-})^{2}\right]\leq\text{dist}^{2}(x_{n},S_{P})-(E_{n}^{-})^{2}.

(68)

On the other hand, if
E_{0}^{-}\leq\text{dist}(x_{0},S_{P})\leq E_{0}^{+}
and
\varepsilon_{0}>0,\sum\varepsilon_{n}^{2}<+\infty
then
\text{dist}(x_{n},S_{P})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

From the proof of Lemma
3
, we know that
E_{n}^{+},E_{n}^{-}
are solutions of the following equation

\frac{\sigma\rho+\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\sigma}z^{2}-\mu z+\frac{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}{2\tau}=0.

We subtract
(E_{n}^{-})^{2}/2\sigma
on both sides of (
60
),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sigma}\left[\text{dist}^{2}(x_{n},S_{P})-(E_{n}^{-})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1-\sigma\rho-\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\sigma}\left[\text{dist}^{2}(x_{n+1},S_{P})-(E_{n}^{-})^{2}\right]+\mu(\text{dist}(x_{n+1},S_{P})-E_{n}^{-})

\displaystyle=\xi_{n+1}\left[\text{dist}^{2}(x_{n+1},S_{P})-(E_{n}^{-})^{2}\right],

where

\xi_{n+1}:=\frac{1-\sigma\rho-\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\sigma}+\frac{\mu}{\text{dist}(x_{n+1},S_{P})+E_{n}^{-}}>0.

Notice that
\xi_{n+1}\geq 1/2\sigma
if
\text{dist}(x_{n+1},S_{P})\leq E_{n}^{+}
which is satisfied by the result in Lemma
3
.

For the second statement, by assumption

E_{0}^{-}\leq\text{dist}(x_{0},S_{P})\leq E_{0}^{+}<E^{+}:=\frac{2\sigma\mu}{\sigma\rho+\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}.

Hence, from (
60
), we have

\frac{1}{2\sigma}\left[\text{dist}^{2}(x_{n},S_{P})-\text{dist}^{2}(x_{n+1},S_{P})\right]+\frac{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}{2\tau}\geq\text{dist}(x_{n+1},S_{P})\left[\mu-\frac{\sigma\rho+\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\sigma}\text{dist}(x_{n+1},S_{P})\right].

(69)

The RHS of (
69
) is nonnegative thanks to assumption on
\text{dist}(x_{0},S_{P})
. Indeed, from Lemma
3
,
\text{dist}(x_{1},S_{P})\leq E_{0}^{+}
. If
E_{0}^{-}\leq\text{dist}(x_{0},S_{P})\leq\text{dist}(x_{1},S_{P})\leq E_{0}^{+}
then
\text{dist}(x_{0},S_{P})=\text{dist}(x_{1},S_{P})
, otherwise
\text{dist}(x_{1},S_{P})\leq E_{0}^{-}<E^{+}
. By induction,
\text{dist}(x_{n},S_{P})
is is bounded away from
E^{+}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Taking the sum of (
69
), we obtain

+\infty>\frac{1}{2\sigma}\text{dist}^{2}(x_{0},S_{P})+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}{2\tau}\geq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\text{dist}(x_{n+1},S_{P})\left[\mu-\frac{\sigma\rho+\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\sigma}\text{dist}(x_{n+1},S_{P})\right],

which implies
\text{dist}(x_{n+1},S_{P})\left[\mu-\frac{\sigma\rho+\sqrt{\sigma\tau}\|L\|}{2\sigma}\text{dist}(x_{n+1},S_{P})\right]\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Since
\text{dist}(x_{n},S_{P})
is bounded away from
E^{+}
,
\text{dist}(x_{n},S_{P})
musts tend to zero.

\hbox to0.0pt{$\sqcap$\hss}\sqcup

This result shows that if
\text{dist}(y_{n},\partial g(Lx_{n+1}))\to 0
fast enough then
\text{dist}(x_{n},S_{P})\to 0
as well.
If one know
\partial g(Lx)
then we can choose
\varepsilon_{n}
small enough so
\text{dist}(x_{n},S_{P})
converges.
The assumption on the stepsizes can be realized, for example: assume that
\max\varepsilon_{n}^{2}=0.1,\rho=2,\mu^{2}=1,\|L\|=1
. Choosing
\tau=0.5
and
0.0555<\sigma<0.304806
will satisfied the condition in Theorem
4
.

The inf-sharp condition is quite restrictive, as we need to test for Lagrangian. It is much easier to verify sharpness condition (

(70)

A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
is a random transformation matrix and
b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is the noisy data.
Our job is to recover the original signal
x^{*}

70

(
Combettes and Wajs, 2005

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\|x\|^{2}-\|x^{*}\|^{2}|+\frac{1}{2}\|Ax-b\|^{2},

70

) and (
71

2

(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

For proximal calculation, the function
\|y-b\|^{2}/2

The function
\|\|x\|^{2}-c|,c>0

Bednarczuk et al., 2023b

\frac{\sqrt{c}x_{n}}{\left\|x_{n}\right\|}&\left(1-2\sigma\right)^{2}c\leq\left\|x_{n}\right\|^{2}\leq\left(1+2\sigma\right)^{2}c,\\
\frac{x_{n}}{1-2\sigma}&\left\|x_{n}\right\|^{2}<\left(1-2\sigma\right)^{2}c.\end{cases}

Image Deblurring

We use a similar model and comparison in the previous subsection to Image Deblurring.

\displaystyle\min_{x}\frac{1}{2}\|Ax-b\|^{2}+\|x\|_{1},

(Convex model)

\displaystyle\min_{x}|\|x\|^{2}-\|x_{0}\|^{2}|+\frac{1}{2}\|Ax-b\|^{2}.

(Weakly Convex model)

Here
x_{0}
is the original image, the matrix
A
is a Gaussian blurring matrix, we extract
A
by applying Gaussian filter with standard deviation
\sigma=4
to the identity matrix. The quantity
b
will be the blurred image corrupted with additive noise from normal distribution,
b=Ax+\varepsilon\mathcal{N}(0,1)
where
\varepsilon=0.01
.

For the setting of the algorithm, we keep the same stepsizes as in the previous example with zeros initialization. We only run the algorithm for
N=1000
steps. To measure the result, we calculate the peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) with respect to the original image. The higher PSNR implies a better result from the process.

The images are taken from the dataset BSD68 of
Github
. The images are then rescaled to the value between
[0,1]
and resized into
256\times 256
. The details for preprocessing steps can be found in
Peyré (2011)
or (
Numerical Tours
). We test for several images (number
007,011,013,044
in the dataset) and show the results in Figure (
11
). The result shows that the convex model for this application does not work well. The weakly convex model gives improved results compared to the blurred images, as one can recognize the shapes and objects. This is because, in the weakly convex model, we also include the information of the solution
x_{0}
and the noisy data.

7.3
Image Denoising with Total Variation

A typical application of the primal-dual algorithm would be Total Variation for image denoising. We consider the following total variation problems and their modification with weakly convex terms,

\displaystyle\min_{x\in X}\frac{\lambda}{2}\|x-b\|^{2}+\|\nabla x\|_{1},

(Convex model)

\displaystyle\min_{x\in X}\lambda|\|x\|^{2}-b|+\|\nabla x\|_{1},

(WC-1)

\displaystyle\min_{x\in X}|\|x\|^{2}-\|x_{0}\|^{2}|+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|x-b\|^{2}+\|\nabla x\|_{1},

(WC-2)

\displaystyle\min_{x\in X}\|x-x_{0}\|_{1}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|x-b\|^{2}+\|\nabla x\|_{1},

(WC-3)

\displaystyle\min_{x\in X}\|x^{2}-x_{0}^{2}\|_{1}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|x-b\|^{2}+\|\nabla x\|_{1},

(WC-4)

where
x^{2}
is taken element-wise. All the weakly convex models are sharp except the first model. If we replace
b
with
\|x_{0}\|^{2}
in the model (WC-1), then we have no information on the noisy image, which does not make sense. That is why we include
x_{0}
and
b
in all other models. We use Algorithm
2
to compare these different models.

The general problem can be written in the form

\min_{x\in X}f(x)+\|\nabla x\|_{1},

where
\nabla:\mathbb{R}^{n^{2}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2n^{2}}
is the discrete gradient operator, which can be calculated as
(\nabla x)_{i,j}=((\nabla x)^{1}_{i,j},(\nabla x)^{2}_{i,j}),

where

(\nabla x)^{1}_{i,j}=\begin{cases}u_{i+1,j}-u_{i,j}&i<n,\\
0&i=n.\end{cases},\qquad(\nabla x)^{2}_{i,j}=\begin{cases}u_{i,j+1}-u_{i,j}&j<n,\\
0&j=n.\end{cases}

We use
g(Lx)=\|\nabla x\|_{1}
as the composite function. The dual problem will have the form

\max_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{2n^{2}}}\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n^{2}}}f(x)-\langle x,\mathrm{div}y\rangle-\delta_{P}(y),

where
\delta_{P}(y)
is the indicator function on the set
P=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{2n^{2}}:\|y\|_{\infty}\leq 1\}
with

\|y\|_{\infty}=\max_{i,j}|y_{i,j}|,\qquad|y_{i,j}|=\sqrt{(y_{i,j}^{1})^{2}+(y_{i,j}^{2})^{2}}.

The function
\mathrm{div}y
is the discrete divergence operator. All the explicit forms of the gradient and divergence can be found in
(
Chambolle, 2004
;
Chambolle and Pock, 2011
)
, as well as an explicit form for the proximal operator. The proximal calculation for
f
can be made using the explicit form of the previous examples. The proximal for
\ell_{1}
norm is the softmax function which is available on
(Prox-repository)
.

Setting:

We use the same stepsizes as in the previous example with zeros initialization, and the maximum iteration is
N=2000
. We use (PSNR) to measure the error with respect to the original image.
The images will be the same as in the previous example, corrupted with additive noise in the form
b=x_{0}+0.1*\mathcal{N}(0,1)
, where
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
is the matrix with standard normal distribution. The results are illustrated in Figure
12
.

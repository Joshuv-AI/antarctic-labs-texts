Commutative rings behind divisible residuated lattices
Cristina Flaut, Dana Piciu

Abstract
Divisible residuated lattices are algebraic structures corresponding to a more comprehensive logic than Hajek’s basic logic with an important significance in the study of fuzzy logic. The purpose of this paper is to investigate commutative rings whose the lattice of ideals can be equipped with a structure of divisible residuated lattice. We show that these rings are multiplication rings. A characterization, more examples and their connections to other classes of rings are established. Furthermore, we analyze the structure of divisible residuated lattices using finite commutative rings. From computational considerations, we present an explicit construction of isomorphism classes of divisible residuated lattices (that are not BL-algebras) of small size n n ( 2 ≤ n ≤ 6 2\leq n\leq 6 ) and we give summarizing statistics. Keywords: multiplication ring, ideal, divisible residuated lattice AMS Subject Classification 2010: 03G10, 03G25, 06A06, 06D05, 08C05, 06F35.

Residuated lattices were introduced in

Since BL-algebras are MTL-algebras satisfying divisibility property the

Definition 1

3
Divisible residuated lattices and Multiplication rings

Lemma 6

Let
(L,\vee,\wedge,\odot,\rightarrow,0,1)
be a residuated lattice. The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)
L
is divisible;

(ii)   For every
x,y,z\in L,

z\rightarrow(x\odot(x\rightarrow y))=z\rightarrow(x\wedge y).

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii).
Obviously.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i).
For
z=

x\wedge y
we obtain
(x\wedge y)\rightarrow(x\odot(x\rightarrow y))=1,
so
L
is divisible, since in a
residuated lattice
x\odot(x\rightarrow y)\leq x\wedge y.

Unitary commutative rings for which the lattice of ideals is an MV-algebra,
a BL-algebra or an MTL-algebra are called MV-rings, BL-rings and
respectively MTL-rings and are introduced in
[
7
]
,
[
18
]

and
[

Definition 7

(

J\subseteq I

J=I\otimes K.
The

Multiplication rings have been studied in
[
15
]
,
[
16
]

and
[
25

. It is well known that a multiplication ring is a subring
of a cartesian product of Dedekind domains and special primary rings, see

[

Using Proposition
4

Theorem 8

A unitary commutative ring is a multiplication ring if an only if
its lattice of ideals is a divisible residuated lattice.

Using Lemma
6
we obtain a new characterization for
multiplication rings:

Proposition 9

Let
A
be a commutative unitary
ring. The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)
A
is a multiplication ring;

(ii)
I\otimes(J:I)=I\cap J,
for every
I,J\in Id(A);

(iii)
((I\otimes(J:I)):K)=((I\cap J):K),
for every
I,J,K\in Id(A).

We recall that a residuated lattice
(L,\vee,\wedge,\odot,\rightarrow,0,1)
in which

is called a

, see
[

[

In

was proved that unitary commutative rings for which the
semiring of ideals, under ideal sum and ideal product, are Heyting algebras
are exactly Von Neumann regular rings, i.e. commutative rings
A

for every element
x\in A
there exists an element

x=a\cdot x^{2}.

Since MV-algebras, BL-algebras and Heyting algebras are divisible residuated
lattices we have the following result:

Proposition 10

MV-rings, BL-rings and Von Neumann regular rings are
multiplication rings.

Proposition 11

A commutative unitary ring is:

(i) a BL-ring if and only if it is an MTL-ring and a multiplication ring;

(ii) an MV-ring if and only if it is an multiplication MTL-ring and
Ann(Ann(I))=I,
for every
I\in Id\left(A\right).

Proof.
(i) Obviously, since a BL-algebra is an MTL-algebra satisfyng the
divisibility property.

(ii) MV-algebras are BL-algebras in which
x^{\ast\ast}=x,
for every
x
.

Theorem 12

(i) A commutative ring which is a principal ideal domain
is a multiplication ring;

(ii) A ring factor of a principal ideal domain is a multiplication ring.

(iii) A finite commutative unitary ring of the form

(i).

Let

\backslash\{0\}

Proposition 14

If
A
is a multiplication ring, then for every
I,J,K\in Id\left(A\right)
we have:

(c_{1})

Ann(Ann(Ann(I))\rightarrow I)=\{0\};

(c_{2})

Ann(Ann(I\rightarrow J))=Ann(Ann(I))\rightarrow Ann(Ann(J));

(c_{3})

Ann(Ann(I\otimes J))=Ann(Ann(I))\otimes Ann[Ann(Ann(I))\cap Ann(J)],

(c_{4})

Ann(Ann(I\cap J))=Ann(Ann(I))\cap Ann(Ann(J));

(c_{5})

Ann(J)\subseteq I\Rightarrow I\rightarrow Ann(Ann(I\otimes J))=Ann(Ann(J));

(c_{6})

I\otimes(J\cap K))=(I\otimes J)\cap(I\otimes K);

(c_{7})

I\cap(J+K)=(I\cap J)+(I\cap K).

Proof.

(c_{1}).
From
Ann(I)\subseteq Ann(Ann(I))\rightarrow I\Rightarrow Ann(Ann(Ann(I))\rightarrow I)\subseteq Ann(Ann(I))\Rightarrow Ann(Ann(Ann(I))\rightarrow I)=Ann(Ann(I))\cap Ann(Ann(Ann(I))\rightarrow I)

\overset{(\mathop{\mathrm{d}iv})}{=}Ann(Ann(I))\otimes[Ann(Ann(I))\rightarrow Ann(Ann(Ann(I))\rightarrow I)]=

Ann(Ann(I))\otimes Ann[(Ann(Ann(I))\rightarrow I))\otimes Ann(Ann(I))]\overset{(\mathop{\mathrm{d}iv})}{=}Ann(Ann(I)\otimes Ann(Ann(Ann(I))\cap I)=Ann(Ann(I))\otimes Ann(I)=\mathbf{0}.

(c_{2}).
We have
Ann(Ann(I\rightarrow J))\subseteq Ann(Ann(I))\rightarrow Ann(Ann(J))=I\rightarrow Ann(Ann(J)).
Also,
(I\rightarrow Ann(Ann(J)))\rightarrow Ann(Ann(I\rightarrow J))=

Ann(Ann(I\rightarrow Ann(Ann(J))))\rightarrow Ann(Ann(I\rightarrow J))\supseteq

Ann(Ann[(I\otimes(I\rightarrow Ann(Ann(J))))\rightarrow J])\overset{(\mathop{\mathrm{d}iv})}{=}Ann(Ann[(I\cap Ann(Ann(J)))\rightarrow J])\supseteq Ann(Ann[Ann(Ann(J))\rightarrow J])=A

\Rightarrow(I\rightarrow Ann(Ann(J)))\rightarrow Ann(Ann(I\rightarrow J))=A\Rightarrow I\rightarrow Ann(Ann(J))\subseteq Ann(Ann(I\rightarrow J))

\Rightarrow Ann(Ann(I\rightarrow J))=I\rightarrow Ann(Ann(J))=Ann(Ann(I))\rightarrow Ann(Ann(J)).

(c_{3}).
From
Ann(Ann(I\otimes J))\subseteq Ann(Ann(I))\Rightarrow

Ann(Ann(I\otimes J))=Ann(Ann(I\otimes J))\cap Ann(Ann(I))=Ann[J\rightarrow Ann(I)]\cap Ann(Ann(I))=

Ann(Ann(I))\otimes Ann[Ann(Ann(I))\cap Ann(J)].

(c_{4}).
Obviously,
Ann(Ann(I\cap J))\subseteq Ann(Ann(I)),Ann(Ann(J)).

Let
K\in Id(A)
such that
K\subseteq Ann(Ann(I)),Ann(Ann(J)).
Then
Ann(Ann(K))\subseteq Ann[Ann(I)+Ann(J)].
But
K\subseteq Ann(Ann(K))
and
Ann[Ann(I)+Ann(J)]=Ann(Ann(I))\cap Ann(Ann(J))\Rightarrow K\subseteq Ann(Ann(I))\cap Ann(Ann(J)).

(c_{5}).

I\rightarrow Ann(Ann(I\otimes J))=Ann(I\otimes J)\rightarrow Ann(I)=Ann[I\otimes(I\rightarrow Ann(J))]=Ann(I\cap Ann(J))=Ann(Ann(J)).

(c_{6}).
Clearly
I\otimes(J\cap K)\subseteq(I\otimes J)\cap(I\otimes K).

Also,
(I\otimes J)\cap(I\otimes K)=(I\otimes J)\otimes[(I\otimes J)\rightarrow(I\otimes K)]=I\otimes[J\otimes(J\rightarrow(I\rightarrow(I\otimes K)))]=

I\otimes[J\cap(I\rightarrow(I\otimes K))]=

I\otimes[(I\rightarrow(I\otimes K))\otimes((I\rightarrow(I\otimes K))\rightarrow J)].

But
K\subseteq I\rightarrow(I\otimes K)\Rightarrow(I\rightarrow(I\otimes K))\rightarrow J\leq K\rightarrow J,
so
(I\otimes J)\cap(I\otimes K)\subseteq I\otimes(J\cap K)).

(c_{7}).
Clearly,
I\cap(J+K)\supseteq(I\cap J)+(I\cap K).
Also, we
have
I\cap(J+K)=(J+K)\otimes[(J+K)\rightarrow I]=[J\otimes((J+K)\rightarrow I)]+[K\otimes((J+K)\rightarrow I)]\subseteq[J\otimes(J\rightarrow I)]+[K\otimes(K\rightarrow I)]=(I\cap J)+(I\cap K).

\subseteq
,

Definition 21

Let
A
be a commutative unitary ring.

(i) The ideal
M
of the ring
A
is maximal if it is maximal
amongst all proper ideals of the ring
A
. From here, we have there are no
other ideals different from
A
containing
M
. The ideal
J
of the ring
A

is a minimal ideal if it is a nonzero ideal which contains no other
nonzero ideals.

(ii) A commutative local ring
A
is a ring with a unique maximal
ideal.

(iii) Let
P

\not=A
be an ideal in the ring
A
. Let
a,b\in A

such that
ab\in P
. If we have
a\in P
or
b\in P
, therefore
P
is called a prime ideal of
A
.

Definition 22

(i) (
[
18
]
) A commutative ring
A
is called a Noetherian
ring if the condition of ascending chain is satisfied, that means every
increasing sequence of ideals
I_{1}\subseteq I_{2}\subseteq...\subseteq I_{r}\subseteq...
is stationary, that means there is
q
such that
I_{q}=I_{q+1}=...
.

(ii) A commutative ring
A
is called an Artinian ring if the
condition of descending chain is satisfied, that means every decreasing
sequence of ideals
I_{1}\supseteq I_{2}\supseteq...\supseteq I_{r}\supseteq...
is stationary, that means there is
q
such that
I_{q}=I_{q+1}=...
.

Hyperbolic distortion and conformality at the boundary Thanks: § Partially supported by the Alexander von Humboldt Foundation.
Pavel Gumenyuk, Maria Kourou §, Annika Moucha §, Oliver Roth

Abstract
. We characterize two classical types of conformality of a holomorphic self-map of the unit disk at a boundary point — existence of a finite angular derivative in the sense of Carathéodory and the weaker property of angle preservation — in terms of the non-tangential asymptotic behaviour of the hyperbolic distortion of the map. These characterizations are given purely with reference to the intrinsic metric geometry of the unit disk. In particular, we relate the classical Julia –Wolff – Carathéodory theorem with the case of equality in the Schwarz – Pick lemma at the boundary. We also provide an operator-theoretic characterization of the existence of a finite angular derivative based on Hilbert space methods. As an application we study the backward dynamics of discrete dynamical systems induced by holomorphic self-maps, and characterize the regularity of the associated pre-models in terms of a Blaschke-type condition involving the hyperbolic distortion along regular backward orbits.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we relate the Julia – Wolff – Carathéodory Theorem to the case of asymptotic equality at the boundary in the Schwarz – Pick lemma.
We deal with two classical notions of boundary conformality for holomorphic self-maps of the unit disk
\mathbb{D}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
:

(
\text{C}_{\text{w}}
)

The non-tangential conformality, in the sense of angle preservation, at a boundary point
{\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}}
;

(
\text{C}_{\text{s}}\;
)

The existence of a finite angular derivative at a boundary point
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
.

We refer to
(
\text{C}_{\text{w}}
)
as
conformality at
\sigma
in the weak sense
and to
(
\text{C}_{\text{s}}
)
as
conformality at
\sigma
in the strong sense
(see Section
2
for the precise definitions). It should be mentioned that both types of boundary conformality of a holomorphic self-map
{\varphi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}}
guarantee that the angular limit
\varphi(\sigma):=\angle\lim_{z\to\sigma}\varphi(z)
of
\varphi
at
\sigma
does exist and belongs to
\partial\mathbb{D}
.

The notion of weak conformality at the boundary is a fundamental and well-developed concept in complex analysis. Some authors call it
isogonality
(see
[
67
]
) or
semiconformality
(see
[
22
,
53
,
62
,
53
]
). In contrast to strong conformality, it has a clear geometric meaning: every smooth curve in
\mathbb{D}
which lands non-tangentially at
\sigma
is mapped onto a smooth curve landing at some point
\omega\in\partial\mathbb{D}
, where
\omega
is independent of the choice of the curve, and the angles between two such curves are preserved, see e.g.
[
67
, Sections 4.3, 11.3 and  11.4]
or
[
44
, Sections V.5 and  V.6]
. For this reason, this concept arises naturally in geometric function theory, e.g. for studying the well-known angular derivative problem for conformal mappings
[
22
,
44
,
53
,
67
,
69
]
. It has found various recent applications, for instance in the study of discrete and continuous iteration in hyperbolic domains, in particular for the investigation of so-called pre-models associated to repulsive fixed points, see
[
64
,
65
]
and
[
26
, Section 13.2]
, and in connection with commuting self-maps
[
34
, Section 8]
.

The notion of strong conformality at the boundary, that is,
the existence of a finite angular derivative in the sense of Carathéodory, is also rooted in classical geometric function theory. It is relevant for several areas of recent research such as composition operators
[
35
,
75
]
,
boundary behaviour of conformal mappings
[
44
,
67
]
, continuous semigroups of holomorphic self-maps
[
26
]
, and discrete iteration
[
4
]
.
The angular derivative has also been considered from an operator-theoretic point of view (see
[
9
,
41
,
42
,
43
,
71
,
72
]
) and for holomorphic functions of several complex variables (
[
1
,
2
,
8
,
13
,
49
,
70
]
).

Starting with the classical works of Julia, Wolff and Carathéodory in the 1920s, the concept of angular derivative has been closely tied to a boundary version of the Schwarz lemma, known as Julia’s lemma or the Julia – Wolff lemma; see
[
4
, Chapter 2]
for an up-to-date account and
[
3
,
19
,
40
]
for recent multi-point versions of these classical results.
One of the goals of the present paper is to establish a different connection between conformality at the boundary and the Schwarz lemma. This connection is based on a study of the finer aspects of the
cases of equality
in the Schwarz lemma at the boundary.

In fact, the topic of “Schwarz lemmas at the boundary”, in particular with a focus on the case of equality and in conjunction with rigidity properties of holomorphic maps, has been widely studied from various perspectives in the past decades.
For maps that are smooth up to the boundary, the underlying idea can be traced back to the famous work of Loewner
[
60
, Hilfssatz I]
introducing the celebrated Loewner differential equation. The basic result in this direction without any assumption about regularity at the boundary was obtained by Burns and Krantz in their pioneering paper
[
30
]
. Their work has stimulated much activity in recent years in boundary rigidity results for holomorphic mappings in one and several complex variables, see e.g.
[
17
,
28
,
29
,
33
,
37
,
39
,
59
,
61
,
76
,
78
,
83
]
.

The main objective of the present paper is to demonstrate that each of the conditions
(
\text{C}_{\text{w}}
)
and
(
\text{C}_{\text{s}}
)
corresponds exactly to a certain natural “non-tangential” case of equality in the invariant Schwarz lemma at the boundary point
\sigma
. This leads to conformally invariant characterizations of both types of boundary conformality entirely in terms of the natural metric geometry of the disk. Thereby, our results link the classical Julia – Wolff – Carathéodory theorem on the angular derivative of holomorphic self-maps
\varphi
of the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
with
the circle of ideas around the boundary Schwarz lemma of Burns and Krantz. We also show that conformality in the strong sense corresponds precisely to a natural “non-tangential” case of equality at the boundary in the Cauchy – Schwarz inequality for the de Branges – Rovnyak space
\mathcal{H}(\varphi)
associated to
\varphi
. In fact, we give an independent proof of this equivalence by operator-theoretic methods. As an application of our approach, we investigate a problem for the discrete dynamical system induced by iterating a holomorphic self-map
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
. Specifically, we characterize the regularity of the pre-model for
\varphi
at a repulsive fixed point
{\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}}
in terms of the hyperbolic distortion of
\varphi
evaluated along a regular backward orbit. This extends the main result of the recent paper
[
48
]
on holomorphic semiflows in
\mathbb{D}
to the discrete setting.

2.
Main results

The hyperbolic metric
\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(z)\,|\!\di z|
of the unit disk
\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\colon|z|<1\}
is defined by

\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(z):=\frac{2}{1-|z|^{2}}\,,

and we denote the associated hyperbolic distance between two points
z,w\in\mathbb{D}
by

{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)
.
The invariant Schwarz lemma or Schwarz – Pick lemma is concerned with the set
{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
of holomorphic self-maps of
\mathbb{D}
. It says that every
\varphi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})

is a weak contraction of the metric space
(\mathbb{D},{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}})
, that is

(2.1)

{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}\big(\varphi(z),\varphi(w)\big)\leqslant{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}\big(z,w\big),\quad z,w\in\mathbb{D}.

Moreover, equality holds for two distinct points
z,w\in\mathbb{D}
— and hence for all
z,w\in\mathbb{D}
— if and only if
\varphi
belongs to the group
{\sf Aut}(\mathbb{D})
of all conformal automorphisms (or biholomorphisms) of
\mathbb{D}
. Equivalently,
the
hyperbolic distortion

\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{h}}\!\varphi:\mathbb{D}\to[0,+\infty)
defined by

(2.2)

\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{h}}\!\varphi(z):=\lim\limits_{w\to z}\frac{{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}\big(\varphi(z),\varphi(w)\big)}{{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)}=\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)\frac{|\varphi^{\prime}(z)|}{1-|\varphi(z)|^{2}}

satisfies

(2.3)

\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{h}}\!\varphi(z)\leqslant 1

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
with equality for one — and hence for every —
{z\in\mathbb{D}}
if and only if
{\varphi\in{\sf Aut}(\mathbb{D})}
; see
[
19
, Def. 5.1]
.

As usual, we use the symbol
\,\,\angle\lim\,\,
to denote the
non-tangential
or
angular limit
, see e.g.
[
4
, Section 2.2]
.
The concrete point of departure of this paper has been the following question posed in
[
29
, Problem 5.2]
:
let
\varphi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
,
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
, and suppose that

(2.4)

\angle\lim_{z\to\sigma}\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{h}}\!\varphi(z)\penalty\ =\penalty\ 1.

Does this imply that
\varphi
has an angular limit at
\sigma
?

Our first main result implies an affirmative answer to the above question and, in fact, tells much more. In order to properly formulate this result, we need to introduce some terminology.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. We call
\varphi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})

conformal at
\sigma
in the strong sense
if
\varphi
possesses a finite angular derivative in the sense of Carathéodory at
\sigma
, i.e. if there exists
\omega\in\partial\mathbb{D}
such that the angular limit

(2.5)

\varphi^{\prime}(\sigma):=\angle\lim_{z\to\sigma}\frac{\varphi(z)-\omega}{z-\sigma}

is finite.
We call
\varphi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})

conformal at
\sigma
in the weak sense
if

(2.6)

\varphi(\sigma):=\angle\lim_{z\to\sigma}\varphi(z)\,\in\,\partial\mathbb{D}

and

(2.7)

\angle\lim_{z\to\sigma}\arg\frac{1-\overline{\varphi(\sigma)}\varphi(z)}{1-\overline{\sigma}z}=0.

We emphasize that the limit value
\varphi(\sigma)
in (
2.6
) is required to lie on the unit circle
\partial\mathbb{D}
.

Remark 2.2
.

If
\varphi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
is conformal at
\sigma
in the strong sense, then
\varphi
is clearly also conformal at
\sigma
in the weak sense, with
{\varphi(\sigma)=\omega}
. The converse is not true, see
[
84
, Example 2.2]
and also Section
8.4
below. The angular limit
\varphi^{\prime}(\sigma)
in (
2.5
) exists (finitely or infinitely) for any
{\sigma,\omega\in\partial\mathbb{D}}
and any
{\varphi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})}
; see e.g.
[
4
, Theorem 2.3.2]
. Moreover,
\varphi
is conformal at
\sigma
in the strong sense if and only if (
2.6
) holds and the angular limit
{\angle\lim_{z\to\sigma}\varphi^{\prime}(z)}
exists and is finite, see e.g.
[
4
, Proposition 2.3.1]
,
[
67
, Proposition 4.7]
, or
[
75
, Section 4.2]
. Geometrically, condition (
2.7
) means that for smooth curves in
\mathbb{D}
tending non-tangentially to
\sigma
, the angles at which they meet
\partial\mathbb{D}
are preserved by
\varphi
.

We are now prepared to state our first main result.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose
\varphi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
and
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. Then the following conditions are pairwise equivalent:

(a)

{\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{h}}\!\varphi(r\sigma)\to 1}
as
{r\to 1^{-}}
.

(b)

\displaystyle\angle\lim\limits_{z,w\to\sigma}\frac{{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}\big(\varphi(z),\varphi(w)\big)}{{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}\big(z,w\big)}=1
.

(c)

The self-map
\varphi
is conformal at
\sigma
in the weak sense.

In condition
(b)
, we adopt the convention that we identify the hyperbolic difference quotient
{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}(\varphi(z),\varphi(w))/{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)
for
w=z
with
\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{h}}\!\varphi(z)
. This is in accordance with (
2.2
). Theorem
1
shows that equality in the Schwarz – Pick inequalities (
2.1
) and (
2.3
) is attained at a boundary point
{\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}}
in the sense that the hyperbolic distortion

\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{h}}\!\varphi(z)
tends to
1
resp. the

quotient

\,{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}(\varphi(z),\varphi(w))/{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)
tends to
1
as
z
and
w
approach
\sigma
non-tangentially if and only if the self-map
\varphi
is conformal at
\sigma
in the weak sense. We wish to emphasize that one of the main contributions of the present paper lies in proving that condition
(a)
implies condition
(c)
.

In fact, there are equivalent analogues to each of the three conditions in the above theorem. In order to state them, we need the following definition: a sequence
(z_{n})
in
\mathbb{D}
is said to be of
bounded (hyperbolic) step
if there is a constant
{M>0}
such that
{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}\big(z_{n},z_{n+1}\big)\leqslant M
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}:=\{1,2,\dots\}
.

Addendum 1

(
Further conditions equivalent to conformality at the boundary in the weak sense
)
.

A self-map
\varphi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
is conformal at the point
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
in the weak sense if and only if one — and hence each — of the following conditions holds:

(a’)

There is a sequence
(z_{n})\subseteq\mathbb{D}
of bounded step converging non-tangentially to
\sigma
such that

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{h}}\!\varphi(z_{n})=1\,.

(b’)

There is a sequence
(z_{n})\subseteq\mathbb{D}
of bounded step converging non-tangentially to
\sigma
such that

\frac{{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}\big(\varphi(z_{n}),\varphi(z_{n+1})\big)}{{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}\big(z_{n},z_{n+1}\big)}\to 1\quad\text{as\penalty\ $\penalty\ n\to+\infty$}\,.

(c’)

The following two angular limits exist:

\angle\lim\limits_{z\to\sigma}\varphi(z)\in\partial\mathbb{D}\quad\text{and}\quad\angle\lim\limits_{z\to\sigma}\arg\varphi^{\prime}(z)\in\mathbb{R}\,.

Remark 2.3
.

The equivalence of
(c’)
and
(c)
follows from the results obtained by Yamashita in
[
80
]
, who considered the more general situation that
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
is holomorphic and non-constant, see Remark
A.3
for the details. Since, in general,
\varphi^{\prime}
is
not
required to be non-vanishing in
\mathbb{D}
, it is however necessary to explain in which sense the existence of
\angle\lim_{z\to\sigma}\arg\varphi^{\prime}(z)
is to be understood. This part of condition
(c’)
means that for each Stolz region
{S\subseteq\mathbb{D}}
with vertex at
\sigma
, the following holds: there exists
{\varepsilon>0}
such that
{\varphi^{\prime}(z)\neq 0}
for all
{z\in S_{\varepsilon}}:={\{z\in S\colon|z-\sigma|<\varepsilon\}}
, and the limit of
\arg\varphi^{\prime}(w)
as
{S_{\varepsilon}\ni z\to\sigma}
exists in the topology of
{\mathbb{R}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z})}
.

Remark 2.4

(Weak Conformality and the Visser – Ostrowski condition)
.

Under the additional assumptions that
\angle\lim_{z\to\sigma}\varphi(z)=:\varphi(\sigma)
exists and
{\varphi(\sigma)\in\partial\mathbb{D}}
, the conformality of
\varphi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
at
\sigma
in the weak sense is equivalent to the classical
Visser – Ostrowski
condition:

(2.8)

\angle\lim\limits_{z\to\sigma}\frac{(z-\sigma)\varphi^{\prime}(z)}{\varphi(z)-\varphi(\sigma)}=1\,.

This is a rather simple fact, which immediately follows, e.g., from Lemma
A.1
included in the Appendix. Up to our best knowledge, it has not been, however, known before. It is also worth mentioning that without the hypotheses
\varphi(\mathbb{D})\subset\mathbb{D}
and
\varphi(\sigma)\in\partial\mathbb{D}
, condition (
2.8
) is weaker than isogonality, i.e. it does not imply the existence of
\angle\lim_{z\to\sigma}\arg\varphi^{\prime}(z)
; see Section
8.1
and Remark
A.3
for more details.

Note also that the above Visser – Ostrowski condition easily implies that
\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{h}}\!\varphi(z)
tends to
1
if
{z\to\sigma}
radially, i.e. condition
(a)
in Theorem
1
.

Using Theorem
1
, we are further able to characterize conformality in the strong sense of a self-map
\varphi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
in terms of the non-tangential boundary behaviour of the hyperbolic distortion
\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{h}}\!\varphi(z)
. In particular, this gives a complete answer to
[
29
, Problem 5.3]
. Similarly to Theorem
1
, we also provide an equivalent two-point condition in terms of the hyperbolic distances. Moreover, we can add an operator-theoretic condition in terms of the non-tangential boundary behavior of the reproducing kernel functions
k_{z}^{\varphi}
in the de Branges – Rovnyak space induced by
\varphi
(for the precise definitions and terminology used in condition
(c)
of the statement, see Section
6
).

Theorem 2
.

Let
\varphi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
and
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
. Then the following conditions are pairwise equivalent:

(a)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\big(1-\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{h}}\!\varphi(r\sigma)\big)\,\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(r\sigma)\di r\penalty\ <+\infty\penalty\
.

(b)

\displaystyle\angle\lim\limits_{z,w\to\sigma}\big(\,{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}\big(z,w\big)-{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}\big(\varphi(z),\varphi(w)\big)\,\big)\,=\,0
.

(c)

\displaystyle\angle\lim_{z,w\to\sigma}\frac{\left|\langle k_{z}^{\varphi},k_{w}^{\varphi}\rangle_{\varphi}\right|}{\left\|k_{z}^{\varphi}\right\|_{\varphi}\left\|k_{w}^{\varphi}\right\|_{\varphi}}=1
.

(d)

The self-map
\varphi
is conformal at the point
\sigma\penalty\
in the strong sense.

As with Theorem
1
, one can add further conditions equivalent to those listed in Theorem
2
.

Addendum 2

(
Further conditions equivalent to conformality at the boundary in the strong sense
)
.

A self-map
\varphi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
is conformal at the point
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
in the strong sense if and only if one — and hence both — of the following conditions holds:

(b’)

{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}\big(r\sigma,s\sigma\big)-{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}\big(\varphi(r\sigma),\varphi(s\sigma)\big)\to 0\penalty\
as
\penalty\ (0,1)\ni r,s\to 1
.

(c’)

\displaystyle\angle\liminf_{z,w\to\sigma}\frac{\left|\langle k_{z}^{\varphi},k_{w}^{\varphi}\rangle_{\varphi}\right|}{\left\|k_{z}^{\varphi}\right\|_{\varphi}\left\|k_{w}^{\varphi}\right\|_{\varphi}}>0
.

Remark 2.5
.

The major aspect of Theorem
2
is probably the equivalence between
(a)
and
(d)
.
A proof of this equivalence can also be found in a recent preprint of O. Ivrii and M. Urbański
[
52

.
The approach developed in
[

]
is of strong geometric flavour; in particular, it uses the relation of the hyperbolic distortion
\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{h}}\!\varphi
to the geodesic curvature of the image
{\varphi([0,\sigma))}
of the geodesic ray
{[0,\sigma)}
. Our approach is different, and dual in the sense that we work with the

of
{[0,\sigma)}
. As we shall see in Section

, the key role in our proof that condition

implies strong conformality is played by several properties of

which follow directly from the weak conformality, see Proposition

. In fact, apart from the operator-theoretic aspect, Theorem

appears to be a fairly direct consequence of the more general Theorem

and the techniques we employ in its proof.

The equivalence

in Theorem

is a statement purely in terms of the path-metric space

. The same remark applies to conditions

and

Remark 2.10
.

One may wonder whether the value of the integral

I(\varphi,\sigma):=\int_{0}^{1}\big(1-\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{h}}\!\varphi(r\sigma)\big)\,\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(r\sigma)\di r

in condition
(a)
of Theorem
2
is related to the value
\varphi^{\prime}(\sigma)
of the angular derivative of
\varphi
at
\sigma
. In fact, it is easy to see that

I(\varphi,\sigma)\leqslant 2\log|\varphi^{\prime}(\sigma)|\,,

cf. the proof of Proposition
5.1
below. However, an estimate of the form
I(\varphi,\sigma)\geqslant c\log|\varphi^{\prime}(\sigma)|+d
with constants
c
and
d
independent of the function
\varphi
cannot hold. This can be seen by taking the degree two Blaschke products

\varphi_{a}(z):=z\frac{z-a}{1-az}\,,\quad 0\leqslant a<1\,.

Then
|\varphi_{a}^{\prime}(1)|=2/(1-a)
, but
I(\varphi_{a},1)=\log(1+a)+\log 2
. The authors thank Artur Nicolau for pointing this out to them.

In the special case of a univalent function mapping
\mathbb{D}
onto a symmetric subdomain of
\mathbb{D}
, the equivalence between conditions
(a)
and
(d)
in Theorem
2
was recently established in

.
In the same work, the equivalence between conditions
(a)
and
(d)
in Theorem

was also proved for a special class of univalent holomorphic self-maps of

associated to the backward dynamics of holomorphic semiflows in

; see
[

.
Motivated by the latter result, we apply our methods to study and solve a problem arising in the classical field of backward dynamics in discrete iteration of holomorphic self-maps in the disk.

Indeed, the notion of conformality at a boundary point, as introduced in Definition
2.1
, turns out to be highly relevant for the study of holomorphic dynamical systems. In the theory of holomorphic iteration in the unit disk, points
{\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}}
at which condition (
2.6
) holds are referred to as
contact points
, and those of them with
{\varphi(\sigma)=\sigma}
are called
boundary fixed points
. Furthermore, boundary points
\sigma
at which
\varphi
is conformal in the strong sense are known as
regular

3.1.
Hyperbolic distortion in the unit disk

Throughout this paper we assume familiarity with standard facts of hyperbolic geometry which can be found e.g. in
[
4
,
20

Recall that the

(3.1)

\varrho_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)=\left|\frac{z-w}{1-\overline{w}z}\right|\,,\qquad z,w\in\mathbb{D}\,,

of the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
is related to the
hyperbolic distance

{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}
by

{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}\big(z,w\big)=\log\frac{1+\varrho_{\mathbb{D}}\big(z,w\big)}{1-\varrho_{\mathbb{D}}\big(z,w\big)}\,.

Let
\varphi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
and

\varphi^{*}(z,w)=\frac{1-\overline{w}z}{z-w}\frac{\varphi(z)-\varphi(w)}{1-\overline{\varphi(w)}\,\varphi(z)}

is called the
hyperbolic difference quotient
of
\varphi
, see
[
20
]
.
Note that if
\varphi\not\in{\sf Aut}(\mathbb{D})
, then
\varphi^{*}(\cdot,v)
extends, for each fixed
{v\in\mathbb{D}}
, to a holomorphic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
; hence the Schwarz – Pick lemma implies

(3.2)

\varrho_{\mathbb{D}}\big(\varphi^{*}(z,v),\varphi^{*}(w,v)\big)\leqslant\varrho_{\mathbb{D}}\big(z,w\big)\,

for all
z,w\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\{v\}
. This is the three-point Schwarz -- Pick lemma of Beardon and Minda
2
2

2

They use the hyperbolic distance instead of the pseudo-hyperbolic one.

[
19

{{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)}-{{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}(\varphi(z),\varphi(w))\geqslant 0}

is a consequence of (

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\varphi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
,

and

e^{-{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)}\sinh\big({\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)\big)\delta\leqslant{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)-{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}\big(\varphi(z),\varphi(w)\big)\leqslant e^{{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)}\sinh\big({\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)\big)\delta\,.

Proof.

Clearly, we can assume that
\varphi\not\in{\sf Aut}(\mathbb{D})
, so
\delta>0
.
Fix
z\in\mathbb{D}
.
In terms of the hyperbolic derivative of
\varphi
at
w
, which is defined as

\varphi^{h}(w):=\lim\limits_{v\to w}\varphi^{*}(v,w)=\left(1-|w|^{2}\right)\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(w)}{1-|\varphi(w)|^{2}}\,,

the three-point Schwarz – Pick inequality (
3.2
) for

takes the form

\varrho_{\mathbb{D}}\big(\varphi^{*}(z,w),\varphi^{h}(w)\big)\leqslant\varrho_{\mathbb{D}}\big(z,w\big)\,.

Since
\varrho_{\mathbb{D}}(|a|,|b|)\leqslant\varrho_{\mathbb{D}}(a,b)
and
\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{h}}\!\varphi(a)=|\varphi^{h}(a)|
for all

\left|\frac{|\varphi^{*}(z,w)|-\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{h}}\!\varphi(w)}{1-\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{h}}\!\varphi(w)|\varphi^{*}(z,w)|}\right|\leqslant\varrho_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)\,.

This results in two inequalities, namely

\frac{|\varphi^{*}(z,w)|-\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{h}}\!\varphi(w)}{1-\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{h}}\!\varphi(w)|\varphi^{*}(z,w)|}\leqslant\varrho_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{h}}\!\varphi(w)-|\varphi^{*}(z,w)|}{1-\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{h}}\!\varphi(w)|\varphi^{*}(z,w)|}\leqslant\varrho_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)\,.

Solving for
|\varphi^{*}(z,w)|
and using
|\varphi^{*}(z,w)|=\varrho_{\mathbb{D}}\big(\varphi(z),\varphi(w)\big)/\varrho_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)

\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{h}}\!\varphi(w)-\varrho_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)}{1-\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{h}}\!\varphi(w)\varrho_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)}\varrho_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)\leqslant\varrho_{\mathbb{D}}\big(\varphi(z),\varphi(w)\big)\leqslant\frac{\varrho_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)+\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{h}}\!\varphi(w)}{1+\varrho_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{h}}\!\varphi(w)}\varrho_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)\,.

In terms of the hyperbolic distance

instead of the pseudohyperbolic distance

or

-\log\big(1-\delta e^{-{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)}\sinh{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)\big)\leqslant{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)-{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}\big(\varphi(z),\varphi(w)\big)\leqslant\log\big(1+\delta e^{{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)}\sinh{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{D}}(z,w)\big)\,.

Taking into account that

7.
Applications to Holomorphic Dynamical Systems

7.1.
Iteration theory and backward dynamics

In this subsection we recall basic definitions and facts from the theory of holomorphic iteration in the unit disk. For a complete up-to-date overview of the topic and proofs of the statements given below, we refer interested readers to
[
4
]
and in particular, to Chapter 4 of that monograph. A fundamental result concerning forward dynamics of a holomorphic self-map
{\varphi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})}
, known as the Denjoy – Wolff Theorem, states that except for the trivial case of non-euclidean rotations
\varphi={\psi^{-1}\circ(z\mapsto e^{i\theta}z)\circ\psi}
,
{\psi\in{\sf Aut}(\mathbb{D})}
,
{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}
, the sequence
(\varphi^{\circ n})
formed by the iterates
{\varphi^{\circ 0}:={\sf id}_{\mathbb{D}}}
,
{\varphi^{\circ n}:=\varphi\circ\varphi^{\circ(n-1)}}
for
{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, converges locally uniformly in
\mathbb{D}
to a constant function
{g\equiv\tau_{\varphi}\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}}
. The point
\tau_{\varphi}
is called the
Denjoy – Wolff
point of
\varphi
.

If
\tau_{\varphi}\in\mathbb{D}
, then it is immediate to see that
\tau_{\varphi}
is a fixed point of
\varphi
. The same can be stated in the case where
{\tau_{\varphi}\in\partial\mathbb{D}}
, if we extend the notion of a fixed point to the boundary as follows:
{\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}}
is said to be a
boundary fixed point
for
\varphi
if the angular limit
{\angle\lim_{z\to\sigma}\varphi(z)}:=\varphi(\sigma)
exists and coincides with
\sigma
. In contrast to interior fixed points, which a holomorphic self-map
{\varphi\neq{\sf id}_{\mathbb{D}}}
has at most one, there can be many — and even infinitely many — boundary fixed points. Moreover, it is known that the angular derivative
\varphi^{\prime}(\sigma):={\angle\lim_{z\to\sigma}\big(\varphi(z)-\sigma\big)/(z-\sigma)}
exists at any boundary fixed point
\sigma
; however, it can be infinite. More precisely,
\varphi^{\prime}(\sigma)\in{(0,+\infty)\cup\{\infty\}}
.

If
{\varphi^{\prime}(\sigma)=\infty}
, then the boundary fixed point
\sigma
is called
super-repulsive
. Otherwise, i.e. in case
\varphi^{\prime}(\sigma)\in{(0,+\infty)}
, the boundary fixed point
\sigma
is said to be
regular
. If
{\tau_{\varphi}\in\partial\mathbb{D}}
, then
{\varphi^{\prime}(\tau_{\varphi})\in(0,1]}
. At the same time, for any boundary regular fixed point
\sigma
other than the Denjoy – Wolff point
\tau_{\varphi}
, we have
{\varphi^{\prime}(\sigma)>1}
. Such points are often referred to as
repulsive fixed points
.

Observe that in our terminology introduced in Section
2
, a boundary fixed point is regular if and only if the self-map is conformal at that point in the strong sense. Therefore, it seems rather natural to introduce the following definition as well.

Definition 7.1

A boundary fixed point

\varphi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
is said to be

\varphi
is conformal at
\sigma
in the weak sense.

Remark 7.2
.

8.
Concluding remarks

8.1.
A word about terminology

The two notions of boundary conformality for holomorphic self-maps, which we consider in this paper, have counterparts for conformal mappings of
\mathbb{D}
. Unfortunately, there is no common consent in the literature regarding terminology. In the terminology introduced by Pommerenke in
[
67
]
, which we prefer to follow,
{f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}}
is said to be
conformal
at
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
if the angular limit
f(\sigma):=\angle\lim_{z\to\sigma}f(z)
exists finitely and
f
has at
\sigma
finite non-vanishing angular derivative
f^{\prime}(\sigma):={\angle\lim_{z\to\sigma}\big(f(z)-f(\sigma)\big)/(z-\sigma)}
. In the earlier monograph
[
66
]
by the same author, as well as in some other sources, e.g.
[
44
, §V.5]
, the term “conformality” is used for another, a priori and a posteriori weaker property which, instead of asking for finite non-vanishing angular derivative, is analytically described by the existence of the finite angular limit of
{\arg\big(f(z)-f(\sigma)\big)/(z-\sigma)}
. Geometrically, this is equivalent to a certain angle preservation property. By this reason, following
[
67
]
, we refer to this weaker property as
isogonality
. Another term, which appears often in the literature (e.g.
[
26
,
53
]
) in the same meaning as isogonality, is
semi-conformality
.

Clearly, both conformality and isogonality can be extended to the case of infinite angular limit
{f(\sigma)=\infty}
by replacing
f
with
1/f
. Similarly, these properties can be reformulated in the “strip setting”, i.e. for conformal mappings of an infinite strip at one of its boundary points at
\infty
; see e.g.
[
22
,
69
,
53
]
.
Moreover, Yamashita
[
80
]
studied isogonality for
non-injective
holomorphic functions in
\mathbb{D}
, using the term “conformality” instead. In the same paper, the Visser – Ostrowski condition (see e.g.
[
67
, §11.3]
) is also investigated and referred to as “semi-conformality”. Note that in general, the latter condition is much weaker than isogonality, but they become equivalent if
f
is a holomorphic self-map and the value of the angular limit at the boundary point we consider belongs again to the boundary; this follows, for example, from our Lemma
A.1
.

By the very definition (see Section
2
) for a holomorphic self-map
\varphi\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D})
, the conformality at
\sigma\in\partial\mathbb{D}
in the weak (resp., strong) sense is equivalent to isogonality (resp., conformality) at
\sigma
, combined with the requirement that
\varphi(\sigma)\in\partial\mathbb{D}
.

Moreover,

\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\big(f(w)-w\big)=\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}f(\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}w)+\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\int\limits_{\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}w}^{w}\!\big(f^{\prime}(\zeta)-1\big)\,\di\zeta\quad\text{for any\penalty\ $w\in\mathbb{S}$.}

Bounding the integral above by
|\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}w|\cdot\max\big\{|f^{\prime}(\zeta)-1|\colon\zeta\in[\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}w,\,w]\big\}
, we conclude that
\angle\lim_{w\to+\infty}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\big(f(w)-w\big)=0
, which proves
(i)(2)
.
∎

There is a one-to-one relation between the isogonality of a simply connected domain and isogonality of the corresponding Riemann map, see e.g.
[
67
, §11.3]
or
[
44
, §V.5]
. Moreover, for holomorphic self-maps, not necessarily univalent, the following result holds, which we believe is known to specialists and which we prove here because of lack of a suitable reference for the non-univalent case.

Proposition A.6
.

Suppose that
F\in{\sf Hol}(\mathbb{H},\mathbb{H})
has a boundary fixed point at
\infty
and that it is conformal in the weak sense at
\infty
. Then there exists a simply connected domain
U\subseteq\mathbb{H}
satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

U
is isogonal at
\infty
.

(ii)

F
is univalent in
U
.

(iii)

W:=F(U)
is also isogonal at
\infty
.

(iv)

The inverse function
G:=(F|_{U})^{-1}:W\to U
satisfies

\angle\lim_{\zeta\to\infty}G(\zeta)=\infty,\quad\penalty\ \angle\lim_{\zeta\to\infty}\arg\frac{G(\zeta)}{\zeta}=0,\quad\text{\penalty\ and}\quad\angle\lim_{\zeta\to\infty}\frac{G^{\prime}(\zeta)}{|G^{\prime}(\zeta)|}=1.

The angular limits in
(iv)
above are to be understood in the sense of Definition
A.5
.

Proof.

Construction of a domain
U
satisfying
(i)
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, denote
\theta_{n}:=\pi/2-1/2^{n}
. According to Lemma
A.1

(i)
, for each
{n\in\mathbb{N}}
there exists
R_{n}>0
such that

(A.4)

F^{\prime}(\zeta)\neq 0\quad\text{and}\quad\big|\arg F^{\prime}(\zeta)\big|<1/2^{n+1}\quad\text{for all\penalty\ }\penalty\ \zeta\in\mathbb{C}\cap\overline{A_{\theta_{n},R_{n}}}.

Note that

U_{n}:=\big\{\zeta\in\mathbb{H}\colon|\arg\zeta|<\theta_{n}\big\}\,\bigcap\,\big\{\zeta\in\mathbb{H}\colon\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\zeta>R_{n},\penalty\ |\arg(\zeta-R_{n})|<\theta_{n+1}\big\}\penalty\ \subseteq\penalty\ A_{\theta_{n},R_{n}}.

Clearly, the union
U:=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}U_{n}
is a domain of the form
\{u+iv:u>R_{1},\penalty\ |v|<f(u)\}
, where
{f:[R_{1},+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)}
is an increasing continuous function with
{f(R_{1})=0}
. Therefore,
U
is simply connected. Moreover,
(i)
holds since
{U_{n}\supset A_{\theta_{n},\rho_{n}}}
for any
{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, where
\rho_{n}:={R_{n}\sin\theta_{n+1}/\sin(\theta_{n+1}-\theta_{n})}
.

Proof of

(ii)
. Fix two distinct points
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\in U
and choose a pair of integers
{n\geqslant 1}
and
{m\geqslant n}
such that
{\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\in U_{n,m}}:={U_{n}\cup U_{m}}
.
By construction,

U_{n,m}=\big\{u+iv\colon u>R_{n},\penalty\ |v|<f_{n,m}(u)\big\},

where
f_{n,m}
is an increasing function in
[R_{n},+\infty)
with
f_{n,m}(R_{n})=0
. Moreover,

(A.5)

\big|f_{n,m}(u_{2})-f_{n,m}(u_{1})\big|\leqslant C_{m}|u_{2}-u_{1}|,\quad C_{m}:=\tan\theta_{m+1},\qquad\text{for any\penalty\ $\penalty\ u_{1},u_{2}\geqslant R_{n}$.}

In particular, if
{\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\zeta_{2}=\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\zeta_{1}}
, then the line segment joining
\zeta_{1}
and
\zeta_{2}
lies entirely in
U_{n,m}
. Since
{\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}F^{\prime}>0}
in the closure of
U_{n,m}
, arguing as in the proof of the classical Noshiro – Warschawski Theorem (see e.g.
[
38
, Theorem 2.16]
) one can easily see that
{F(\zeta_{2})\neq F(\zeta_{1})}
.

So we may suppose that
{\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\zeta_{2}>\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\zeta_{1}}
and additionally, that the line segment
I
joining
\zeta_{1}
with
\zeta_{2}
does not lie in the closure of
U_{n,m}
. From the Lipschitz property (
A.5
), this is only possible in the case of
{|a|<C_{m}}
, where
{a\in\mathbb{R}}
stands for the slope of
I
, meaning that for a suitable
{b\in\mathbb{R}}
the segment
I
is contained in the graph of
{g(u):=au+b}
. Clearly,
{a\neq 0}
. Thanks to the symmetry, we may suppose without loss of generality that
{a>0}
.
Write
{\zeta_{k}=:u_{k}+iv_{k}}
,
{k=1,2}
. Since
{\zeta_{1}\in U_{n,m}}
, we have
{g(u_{1})>-f_{n,m}(u_{1})}
and, due to monotonicity,
{g(u)>-f_{n,m}(u)}
for all
{u\in[u_{1},u_{2}]}
. Hence,
I
can have intersection points with
\partial U_{n,m}
only in the upper half-plane
{\{\zeta:\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\zeta>0\}}
. It follows that the arc
\Gamma:=\{u+ih(u):u\in[u_{1},u_{2}]\}
of the graph of the continuous function
h(u):={\min\{g(u),f_{n,m}(u)\}}
joining
\zeta_{1}
to
\zeta_{2}
is contained in the closure of
U_{n,m}
. Taking into account that
h
is Lipschitz continuous with the same constant
C_{m}
and using (
A.4
), it is not difficult to see that
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}F(\zeta)
changes strictly monotonically as
\zeta
moves along
\Gamma
. In particular,
{\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}F(\zeta_{2})>\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}F(\zeta_{1})}
.

Proof of

(iii)
is completely standard. We use the notation introduced on the previous step. Setting
{F(\infty):=\infty}
, the function
F
extends to a continuous injective map of the closure of
U_{n+1}
regarded as a Jordan domain on the Riemann sphere for any
{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
Taking that by the hypothesis,
{\arg\big(F(\zeta)/\zeta\big)\to 0}
as
{\zeta\to\infty}
non-tangentially, we may conclude that
{F(U_{n+1})\supset A_{\theta_{n},P_{n}}}
for some
{P_{n}>0}
large enough. It follows that

F(U)\penalty\ =\penalty\ \bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}F(U_{n})\penalty\ \supset\penalty\ \bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}F(U_{n+1})\penalty\ \supset\penalty\ \bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}A_{\theta_{n},P_{n}}

and hence,

is isogonal at
\infty
.

Proof of

(iv)
. We follow the same argument as in the previous step. Namely, given
{\theta\in(0,\pi/2)}
choose some
{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
{\theta_{n}>\theta}
. Then
{A_{\theta,P}\subseteq W_{n}:=F(U_{n})}
for some
{P>0}
. Therefore, for any
\zeta\in A_{\theta,P}
, we have
G(\zeta)=(F|_{U_{n}})^{-1}(\zeta)
. By Carathéodory’s Extension Theorem (see e.g.
[
66
, Theorem 9.10]
),
F|_{U_{n}}
extends to a homeomorphism
F_{n}
of the closure of
U_{n}
onto the closure of
W_{n}
in
{\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}}
. Taking into account that
{F(x)\to\infty}
as
{x\to+\infty}
, we have
F_{n}(\infty)=\infty
. Therefore,
{w:=G(\zeta)\to F_{n}^{-1}(\infty)=\infty}
as
{W_{n}\supset A_{\theta,P}\ni\zeta\to\infty}
. It further follows that

\lim_{A_{\theta,P}\ni\zeta\to\infty}\arg\frac{G(\zeta)}{\zeta}=\lim_{U_{n}\ni w\to\infty}\arg\frac{w}{F(w)}=0\quad\text{and\penalty\ }\penalty\ \lim_{A_{\theta,P}\ni\zeta\to\infty}\frac{G^{\prime}(\zeta)}{|G^{\prime}(\zeta)|}=\lim_{U_{n}\ni w\to\infty}\frac{|F^{\prime}(w)|}{F^{\prime}(w)}=1,

where the last equality holds by Lemma
A.1

(i)
.
∎

We conclude the Appendix with two rather elementary lemmas, which are used in the proofs of our main results and which we include for the sake of completeness.
Let us denote by
{\mathrm{B}_{\mathbb{H}}}(\zeta_{0},R)\coloneqq\{\zeta\in\mathbb{H}\,:\,{\mathrm{k}}_{\mathbb{H}}(\zeta,\zeta_{0})\leqslant R\}
the closed hyperbolic disk in

Lemma A.7
.

For any

and any

\max_{\zeta\in{\mathrm{B}_{\mathbb{H}}}(1,R)}\big|F^{\prime}(\zeta)-1\big|\,<\,\varepsilon.

Remark A.8
.

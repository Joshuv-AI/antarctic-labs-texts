Abstract
. A new conjecture on characters of finite groups, related to the McKay conjecture, was proposed recently by the first and third authors. In this paper, we prove it for p p -solvable groups when p p is odd.

1.
Introduction

A new conjecture on characters of finite groups was proposed recently by the first and the third authors
[
6
]
.
The simplest form of this conjecture asserts the following:
if
G
is a finite group,
p
is a prime, and
x\in G
is a
p
-element which lies in a unique
Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
of
G
(we say that
x
is
picky
in
G
), then there is a bijection
{\rm Irr}^{x}(G)\rightarrow{\rm Irr}^{x}({\bf N}_{G}(P))

satisfying certain strong conditions, where
{\rm Irr}^{x}(G)
is the set of the irreducible complex characters
\chi
of
G

such that
\chi(x)\neq 0
. These
picky
elements are shown to be fairly abundant in
[
4
]
. The purpose of this paper is to prove the following strong form of the conjecture for
p
-solvable groups for odd primes. Namely:

THEOREM A
.

Let
G
be a finite
p
-solvable group, where
p
is an odd prime, and let
x\in G
be a
p
-element which lies in a unique
Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
of
G
. Then there is a bijection
{}^{*}:{\rm Irr}^{x}(G)\rightarrow{\rm Irr}^{x}({\bf N}_{G}(P))

and a sign
\epsilon_{x}
such that
\chi(x)=\epsilon_{x}\chi^{*}(x)
and
\chi(1)_{p}=\chi^{*}(1)_{p}
for all

\chi\in{\rm Irr}^{x}(G)
.

In order to prove this theorem we need to use deep results from the character theory of finite groups, like the Dade–Turull character correspondence (see Section 8.3 of
[
7
]
, for instance).
Unfortunately, our methods are not sufficient to handle the case
p=2
. As we briefly explain in the final section of the paper, this limitation is related, among other things, to the behavior of the Weil character associated with symplectic groups when evaluated on
2
-elements of order at least
4
.

We refer the reader to
[
3
,
5
]
for progress on the conjecture for group of Lie type and symmetric groups, respectively.

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose that
G
is a finite group and that
L\leq K\leq G
are normal subgroups of
G
such that

K/L
is a
p^{\prime}
-group. Suppose that
H\leq G
is such that
G=KH
and write
K\cap H=C
.
Let
x\in H
such that
xL\in H/L
is a
p
-element and suppose that
H/L
has a normal Sylow

-subgroup. Then
x^{g}\in H

{\bf C}_{K/L}(x)\subseteq C/L
. Moreover, if this happens then
\mu^{G}(x)=\mu(x)
for all characters
\mu
of

.

Proof.

Let
k\in K
. Notice that
k^{x}L=kL
if and only if
x^{k}L=xL
.
Suppose that
x^{g}\in H
implies
g\in H
for any
g\in G
. Let
kL\in K/L
centralized by
x
.
Then
x^{k}\in H
and thus
k\in H\cap K=C
, by hypothesis.

Conversely, assume that
{\bf C}_{K/L}(x)\subseteq C/L
. Since
C
is normal in
H
, then
{\bf C}_{K/L}(x^{h})={\bf C}_{K/L}(x)^{h}\subseteq C/L
for all
h\in H
.
Now, suppose that
x^{k}\in H
for some
k\in K
. Then
x^{-1}x^{k}\in H
. But
x^{-1}k^{-1}xk\in K
.
Thus
x^{-1}x^{k}\in C
. Therefore

and it is a

is a

\mu^{G}(x)={1\over|H|}\sum_{g\in G\atop g^{-1}xg\in H}\mu(g^{-1}xg)={1\over|H|}\sum_{g\in H}\mu(g^{-1}xg)=\mu(x)\,,

∎

Although we shall not need it, the following elementary fact might perhaps be useful in the future.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
G
be a finite group, let
H\leq G
and let
x\in H
be a
p
-element. Then
(1_{H})^{G}(x)=1
if and only if

H
contains all the
p
-Sylow normalizers that contain
x
.

Proof.

Suppose that
H
contains all the
p
-Sylow normalizers that contain
x
.
In particular,
|G:H|
is
p^{\prime}
. Suppose that
x\in P\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(H)
.
Let
g\in G
be such that
x^{g}\in H
. Then
x^{g}\in P^{h}
for some
h\in H
.
Thus
x\in P^{hg^{-1}}
. Hence,
P^{hg^{-1}}\subseteq H
, by hypothesis.
Therefore,
P^{hg^{-1}}=P^{h_{1}}
for some
h_{1}\in H
. We have that
hg^{-1}h_{1}^{-1}
normalizes
P
,
and therefore is contained in
H
. We conclude that
g\in H
. Therefore

(1_{H})^{G}(x)=1
, as wanted.

Conversely, suppose that
(1_{H})^{G}(x)=1
. Then, if
x^{g}\in H
for some
g\in G
we have that
g\in H
.
Let
Q
be a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
H
containing
x
.
Let
P\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(G)
such that
P\cap H=Q
. If
Q<P
, then
Q<{\bf N}_{P}(Q)
. Let
y\in{\bf N}_{P}(Q)-Q
.
Then
x^{y}\in Q
but
y\not\in H
, a contradiction. So we may assume that
Q\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(G)
.
Suppose that
x\in R\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(G)
. Let
y\in{\bf N}_{G}(R)
.
Then
R^{g}=Q
for some
g\in G
. Then
x^{g}\in H
and therefore

g\in H
. Also,
x^{yg}\in H
, and thus
yg\in H
. Hence

, as wanted.
∎

The Key result

We start by proving the following:

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that
G
is a finite group,
p
is an odd prime,
L\leq K\leq G
are normal subgroups of
G
such that

K/L
is a
p^{\prime}
-group and
L\subseteq{\rm\textbf{Z}}(G)
. Suppose that
V=PK
is normal in
G
, where
P\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(G)
.
Let
H={\bf N}_{G}(P)
. Note that
G=KH
and write
K\cap H=C
.
Let
\theta\in{\rm Irr}(K)
be
G
-invariant, and let
\varphi\in{\rm Irr}(C)
be the relative
P
-Glauberman correspondent
of
\theta
. Then there exists a bijection

f:{\rm Irr}(G|\theta)\rightarrow{\rm Irr}(H|\varphi)

such that

\chi(1)_{p}=f(\chi)(1)_{p}
for every
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G|\theta)
. Furthermore, if
x\in P
is such that
xL
is picky in
G/L
, then

\chi(x)=\varepsilon f(\chi)(x)
, where
\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}
is the unique sign such that

[\theta_{C},\varphi]\equiv\varepsilon
mod
p
.

Proof.

Since
L
is central in
G
,
K
has a central Sylow
p
-subgroup, say
L_{p}
, and
it follows that
K=X\times L_{p}
for a unique
X\trianglelefteq\,G
.
It follows that
\theta=\theta_{p^{\prime}}\times\theta_{p}
, and
\varphi=\varphi_{p^{\prime}}\times\theta_{p}
.
Notice that
{\bf C}_{X}(P)=C\cap X
and that
\varphi_{p^{\prime}}
is the
P
-Glauberman correspondent of
\theta_{p^{\prime}}
.
Notice that
\theta_{p^{\prime}}
has a canonical extension
\widehat{\theta_{p^{\prime}}}

to
V
, and
\varphi_{p^{\prime}}
has a canonical extension
\widehat{\varphi_{p^{\prime}}}=\varphi_{p^{\prime}}\times 1_{P}
to
U=V\cap H
.
It is well-known that there is a character triple isomorphism

(G,X,\theta_{p^{\prime}})\rightarrow(H,L_{p^{\prime}},\varphi_{p^{\prime}})
that sends
\widehat{\theta_{p^{\prime}}}

to
\widehat{\varphi_{p^{\prime}}}
(see the proof of Lemma 7.3 of
[
9
]
). Let

{}^{*}:{\rm Irr}(G|\theta_{p^{\prime}})\rightarrow{\rm Irr}(H|\varphi_{p^{\prime}})

be the bijection associated to the character triple isomorphism. Let
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G|\theta)
. By Gallagher’s correspondence, we can write
\chi_{V}=\delta\widehat{\theta_{p^{\prime}}}
,
where
\delta
is a character of
V/X
all of whose irreducible constituents lie over
\theta_{p}
.
By the character triple isomorphism, we have that

(\chi^{*})_{U}=\delta\widehat{\varphi_{p^{\prime}}}
, where we identify
\delta
with the corresponding character
via the natural isomorphism
V/X\rightarrow U/L_{p^{\prime}}
. Hence
\chi^{*}
lies over
\theta_{p}
and hence the restriction of
∗
to
{\rm Irr}(G|\theta)
gives a bijection

{}^{*}:{\rm Irr}(G|\theta)\rightarrow{\rm Irr}(H|\varphi).

We next prove that this is the desired bijection. First since character triple isomorphisms preserve ratios of character degrees we have that
\chi(1)_{p}=\chi^{*}(1)_{p}
. Now, let
Y=K\langle x\rangle
, and notice that
Y/X
is a
p
-group. Let
\tau\in{\rm Irr}(Y)
be the canonical extension of
\theta_{p^{\prime}}
to
Y
. Now if
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G|\theta)
we have that
\chi_{Y}=\xi\tau
for some character
\xi
of
Y/X
and then
\chi(x)=\xi(x)\tau(x)
. Our goal now is to show that
\chi^{*}(x)=\xi(x)\tau^{*}(x)
. By the proof of Lemma 5.17 of
[
7
]
, we have that
\chi^{*}(x^{*})=\tau^{*}(x^{*})\xi^{*}(x^{*})=\tau^{*}(x^{*})\xi(x)
. Since the isomorphism of character triples is associated to the natural map
gX\rightarrow gL_{p^{\prime}}
we have that
x^{*}=x
and hence
\chi^{*}(x)=\xi^{*}(x)\tau^{*}(x)=\xi(x)\tau^{*}(x)
, as wanted.
Now, we consider the action of
S=\langle x\rangle
on
X
. Since
xL
is picky in
G/L
we have that

{\bf C}_{X}(x)/L\subseteq{\bf C}_{X/L}(xL)\subseteq{\bf N}_{X/L}(PL/L)=(C\cap X)/L={\bf C}_{X}(P)/L

and hence
{\bf C}_{X}(S)={\bf C}_{X}(P)
. Therefore the
S
-Glauberman correspondent of
\theta_{p^{\prime}}
is also
\varphi_{p^{\prime}}
. Now, by Theorem 13.6 of
[
2
]
applied to the action of
S=\langle x\rangle
on
X
, we have that
\tau(x)=\varepsilon\varphi_{p^{\prime}}(1)
where
\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}
. Also, notice that, since
L\langle x\rangle/L_{p^{\prime}}
is a
p
-group and
L_{p^{\prime}}
is central, we have that
L\langle x\rangle=L_{p^{\prime}}\times W
for
W\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(L\langle x\rangle)
. Now
L\langle x\rangle/L
is normal in
C\langle x\rangle/L
and hence

is normal in
C\langle x\rangle
. Therefore

and since
\tau^{*}
is the canonical extension, we have that
\tau^{*}=\varphi_{p^{\prime}}\times 1_{W}
and then
\tau^{*}(x)=\varphi_{p^{\prime}}(1)
. Then

\chi(x)=\varepsilon\varphi_{p^{\prime}}(1)\xi(x)=\varepsilon\chi^{*}(x).

To finish the proof, we need to show that
\varepsilon
is the unique sign such that
[\theta_{C},\varphi]\equiv\varepsilon
mod
p
. By Theorem 13.14 of
[
2

we have that
\varepsilon\equiv[(\theta_{p^{\prime}})_{C\cap X},\varphi_{p^{\prime}}]
mod

[(\theta_{p^{\prime}})_{C\cap X},\varphi_{p^{\prime}}]=f
. Following Theorem E of
[
8
]

\theta_{C}=e\varphi+p\Delta,

and

(\theta_{p^{\prime}})_{C\cap X}=f\varphi_{p^{\prime}}+p\Delta^{\prime},

where
\Delta

are 0 or characters of

\theta=\theta_{p^{\prime}}\times\theta_{p}
and
\varphi=\varphi_{p^{\prime}}\times\theta_{p}
. Then

\theta_{C}=(\theta_{p^{\prime}})_{C\cap X}\times\theta_{p}=f(\varphi_{p^{\prime}}+p\Delta^{\prime})\times\theta_{p}=f\varphi\times pf(\Delta^{\prime}\times\theta_{p})

and hence,

mod

\varepsilon
is uniquely determined, as wanted.

K/L
is a
p^{\prime}
-group. Suppose that
V=PK
is normal in
G

P\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(G)
.
Let
H=L{\bf N}_{G}(P)
and write

.
Let
\theta\in{\rm Irr}(K)
be
G
-invariant, and let
\varphi\in{\rm Irr}(C)
be the relative
P
-Glauberman correspondent
of
\theta
. Let
\xi\in{\rm Irr}(L)
under
\varphi
, and suppose that
\xi
is
G

f:{\rm Irr}(G|\theta)\rightarrow{\rm Irr}(H|\varphi)

such that

\chi(1)_{p}=f(\chi)(1)_{p}
for every
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G|\theta)
. Furthermore, if
x\in P
is such that
xL
is picky in

\chi(x)=\varepsilon f(\chi)(x)

\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}

[\theta_{C},\varphi]\equiv\varepsilon

By the theory of character triples (see Corollary 5.9 of
[

(G^{*},L^{*},\xi^{*})
be a character triple isomorphic to
(G,L,\xi)
with

and, with a slight abuse of notation, let

{}^{*}:{\rm Irr}(J|\xi)\rightarrow{\rm Irr}(J^{*}|\xi^{*})

be the associated bijection of characters, where
L\leq J\leq G
and
J/L\cong J^{*}/L^{*}
under the corresponding isomorphism of groups. By the definition of character triple isomorphisms (see Definition 5.7(a) of
[
7
]
, for instance),
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G|\theta)
if and only if
\chi^{*}\in{\rm Irr}(G^{*}|\theta^{*})
(and the same with

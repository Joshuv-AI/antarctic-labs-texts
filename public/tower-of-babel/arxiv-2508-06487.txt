Weak approximation of stochastic differential equations with sticky boundary conditions
A. Sharma

Abstract
Sticky diffusion models a Markovian particle experiencing reflection and temporary adhesion phenomena at the boundary. Numerous numerical schemes exist for approximating stopped or reflected stochastic differential equations (SDEs), but this is not the case for sticky SDEs. In this paper, we construct and analyze half-order and first-order numerical schemes for the weak approximation of stochastic differential equations with sticky boundary conditions. We present the algorithms in general setting such that they can be used to solve general linear parabolic partial differential equations with second-order sticky boundary condition via the probabilistic representations of their solutions. Since the sticky diffusion spends non-zero amount of time on boundary, it poses extra challenge in designing the schemes and obtaining their order of convergence. We support the theoretical results with numerical experiments. Key words. sticky diffusion, second-order boundary condition, mesh-free Monte Carlo methods for parabolic partial differential equations, Feynman-Kac formula. AMS subject classifications. 65C30, 60H35, 60H10, 37H10

. Splitting the terms, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\varphi(X_{\chi})Y_{\chi}+Z_{\chi}-\mathbb{E}[\varphi(X(T))Y(T)+Z(T)]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\sum_{k=0}^{\chi-1}(u_{k+1}Y_{k+1}+Z_{k+1}-u^{{}^{\prime}}_{k+1}Y^{{}^{\prime}}_{k+1}-Z_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}(u^{{}^{\prime}}_{k+1}Y^{{}^{\prime}}_{k+1}+Z^{{}^{\prime}}_{k+1}-u_{k}Y_{k}-Z_{k}\;|\;X_{k}).

It can be shown via Taylor’s expansion that the following holds under Assumptions
2.1
-
2.5
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(u_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}}Y_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}}+Z_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}}-u_{k}Y_{k}-Z_{k}\;|\;X_{k})I(t_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}}<T)\leq CY_{k}h^{2},

(4.27)

where
C>0
is independent of
h
. The proof of above one-step error estimate is available in
[
28
]
(also see
[
20
, Lemma 3.2]
). Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}\varphi(X_{\chi})Y_{\chi}+Z_{\chi}-\mathbb{E}[\varphi(X(T))Y(T)+Z(T)]|

\displaystyle\leq\bigg|\mathbb{E}\sum_{k=0}^{\chi-1}(u_{k+1}Y_{k+1}+Z_{k+1}-u^{{}^{\prime}}_{k+1}Y^{{}^{\prime}}_{k+1}-Z_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}})\bigg|

\displaystyle\;\;\;+Ch^{2}\mathbb{E}\sum_{k=0}^{\chi-1}Y_{k},

where
C>0
is independent of
h
. We split the term as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\chi-1}|u_{k+1}Y_{k+1}

\displaystyle+Z_{k+1}-u_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}}Y_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}}-Z_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}}|I_{\bar{G}^{c}}(X_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=|u_{k+1}Y_{k+1}+Z_{k+1}-u_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}}Y_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}}-Z_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}}|I_{\bar{G}^{c}}(X_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}})I(t^{{}^{\prime}}_{k+1}\geq T)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=0}^{\chi-1}|u_{k+1}Y_{k+1}+Z_{k+1}-u_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}}Y_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}}-Z_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}}|I_{\bar{G}^{c}}(X_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}})I(t^{{}^{\prime}}_{k+1}<T)

\displaystyle=|u_{k+1}Y_{k+1}+Z_{k+1}-u_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}}Y_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}}-Z_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}}|I_{\bar{G}^{c}}(X_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}})I(t^{{}^{\prime}}_{k+1}\geq T)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=0}^{\chi-2}|u_{k+1}Y_{k+1}+Z_{k+1}-u_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}}Y_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}}-Z_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}}|I_{\bar{G}^{c}}(X_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}})I(t^{{}^{\prime}}_{k+1}<T)I(t_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}<T)

\displaystyle\quad+|u_{k+1}Y_{k+1}+Z_{k+1}-u_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}}Y_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}}-Z_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}}|I_{\bar{G}^{c}}(X_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}})I(t^{{}^{\prime}}_{k+1}<T)I(t_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\geq T).

Using Lemma
4.2
, Lemma
4.3
and Lemma
4.4
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\chi-1}|u_{k+1}Y_{k+1}+Z_{k+1}

\displaystyle-u_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}}Y_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}}-Z_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}}|I_{\bar{G}^{c}}(X_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}})\leq ChY_{\chi-1}+Ch\sum_{k=0}^{\chi-2}r_{k+1}I_{\bar{G}^{c}}(X_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}})Y_{k}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}\varphi(X_{\chi})Y_{\chi}+Z_{\chi}-\mathbb{E}[\varphi(X(T))Y(T)+Z(T)]|\leq Ch\mathbb{E}Y_{\chi-1}+Ch\mathbb{E}\sum_{k=0}^{\chi-2}r_{k+1}Y_{k}I_{\bar{G}^{c}}(X_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}})+Ch^{2}\mathbb{E}\sum_{k=0}^{\chi-1}Y_{k}.

From (
3.3
), (
3.10
), (
3.13
) and (
3.17
), it is not difficult to infer that there exist
K,\tilde{K}>0
independent of
h
such that

\displaystyle Y_{k}

\displaystyle\leq Y_{k-1}(1+Kr_{k}+\tilde{K}h)\leq Y_{k-1}(1+Kr_{k}I_{\bar{G}^{c}}(X_{k}^{{}^{\prime}}))(1+\tilde{K}h)\leq Y_{0}e^{\tilde{K}T}\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(1+Kr_{i}I_{\bar{G}^{c}}(X_{i}^{{}^{\prime}})).

This implies

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}\varphi(X_{\chi})Y_{\chi}+Z_{\chi}-\mathbb{E}[\varphi(X(T))Y(T)+Z(T)]|

\displaystyle\leq Ch\mathbb{E}\prod_{k=0}^{\chi-2}Y_{k}+Ch^{2}\mathbb{E}\sum_{k=0}^{\chi-1}\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(1+Kr_{i}I_{\bar{G}^{c}}(X_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}))

\displaystyle\quad+Ch\mathbb{E}\sum_{k=0}^{\chi-2}r_{k+1}I_{\bar{G}^{c}}(X_{k+1}^{{}^{\prime}})\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(1+Kr_{i}I_{\bar{G}^{c}}(X_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}))

which on applying Lemma
4.6
and Corollary
4.1
give the first-order of convergence.
∎

5
Convergence of Projected Euler scheme for sticky SDEs

We first state the main theorem of convergence for projected Euler scheme i.e. Algorithm
2
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Under Assumptions
2.1
-
2.5
, the weak order of convergence of
Algorithm
2
is
\mathcal{O}(h^{1/2})
i.e.

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}(\varphi(X_{\chi})Y_{\chi}+Z_{\chi})-u(t_{0},X_{0})|\leq Ch^{1/2},

(5.1)

where
u(t,x)
is solution of (
1.3
)-(
1
), and
C
is a positive constant independent of
h
.

We now prove one-step lemma for error near the boundary.

Lemma 5.2

(One-step lemma for Algorithm
2
)
.

Let Assumptions
2.1
-
2.5
hold. Then the one-step error of Algorithm
2
when
X_{k}\notin\bar{G}
is given by

\displaystyle|u_{k+1}Y_{k+1}+Z_{k+1}-u_{k}Y_{k}-Z_{k}|\leq Cr_{k+1}^{2}Y_{k},\;\;\;k=0,\dots,\chi-2,\;\;\;a.s.

where
C
is a positive constant independent of
h
and
Y_{k}
.

Proof.

We split the terms as follows:

\displaystyle u_{k+1}Y_{k+1}+Z_{k+1}-u_{k}Y_{k}-Z_{k}=(u_{k+1}-u_{k})Y_{k+1}+u_{k}(Y_{k+1}-Y_{k})+Z_{k+1}-Z_{k}.

(5.2)

Using Taylor’s expansion, we obtain

\displaystyle u_{k+1}=u(t_{k},X_{k+1})+h_{k}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(t_{k},X_{k+1})+\frac{h_{k}^{2}}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}u(t_{k}+\epsilon_{1}h_{k},X_{k+1}),

where
\epsilon_{1}\in(0,1)
and recall
h_{k}=\mu(X_{k+1})r_{k}
. Again, using Taylor’s expansion, we ascertain

\displaystyle u_{k}

\displaystyle=u(t_{k},X_{k+1}-r_{k}\nu_{k+1})=u(t_{k},X_{k+1})-r_{k}(\nabla u(t_{k},X_{k+1})\cdot\nu_{k+1})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{r_{k}^{2}}{2}D^{2}u(t_{k},X_{k+1}-\epsilon_{2}r_{k}\nu_{k+1})[\nu_{k+1},\nu_{k+1}],

where
\epsilon_{2}\in(0,1)
and
\nu_{k+1}:=\nu(X_{k+1})
. Hence,

\displaystyle(u_{k+1}

\displaystyle-u_{k})Y_{k+1}=\Big(h_{k}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(t_{k},X_{k+1})+\frac{h_{k}^{2}}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}u(t_{k}+\epsilon_{1}h_{k},X_{k+1})+r_{k}(\nabla u(t_{k},X_{k+1})\cdot\nu_{k+1})

\displaystyle-\frac{r_{k}^{2}}{2}\nabla^{2}u(t_{k},X_{k+1}-\epsilon_{2}r_{k}\nu_{k+1})[\nu_{k+1},\nu_{k+1}]\Big)(Y_{k}+r_{k}c(t_{k},X_{k+1})Y_{k}+r_{k}\gamma(t_{k},X_{k+1})Y_{k}\Big).

(5.3)

Since
X_{k+1}\in\bar{G}
, using (
1.3
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(t_{k},X_{k+1})

\displaystyle=-(b(t_{k},X_{k+1})\cdot\nabla u(t_{k},X_{k+1}))-\big(a(t_{k},X_{k+1}):\nabla^{2}u(t_{k},X_{k+1})\big)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;-c(t_{k},X_{k+1})u(t_{k},X_{k+1})-g(t_{k},X_{k+1}).

(5.4)

Combining (
5.3
) and (
5.4
) yields

\displaystyle(u_{k+1}

\displaystyle-u_{k})Y_{k+1}=\bigg(-r_{k}\mu(X_{k+1})\Big((b(t_{k},X_{k+1})\cdot\nabla u(t_{k},X_{k+1}))-\big(a(t_{k},X_{k+1}):\nabla^{2}u(t_{k},X_{k+1})\big)\Big)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+r_{k}Du(t_{k},X_{k+1})[\nu_{k+1}]\bigg)Y_{k}-r_{k}\mu(X_{k+1})c(t_{k},X_{k+1})u(t_{k},X_{k+1})Y_{k}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;-r_{k}\mu(X_{k+1})g(t_{k},X_{k+1})Y_{k}+\mathcal{O}(r_{k}^{2}Y_{k})

\displaystyle=-r_{k}\gamma(t_{k},X_{k+1})u(t_{k},X_{k+1})Y_{k}+r_{k}\psi(t_{k},X_{k+1})Y_{k}-r_{k}\mu(X_{k+1})c(t_{k},X_{k+1})u(t_{k},X_{k+1})Y_{k}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;-r_{k}\mu(X_{k+1})g(t_{k},X_{k+1})Y_{k}+\mathcal{O}(r_{k}^{2}Y_{k}),

(5.5)

where we have used (
1
). From (
3.24
), we have

\displaystyle u_{k}(Y_{k+1}-Y_{k})

\displaystyle=(u(t_{k},X_{k+1})-r_{k}Du(t_{k},X_{k+1}-\epsilon_{3}r_{k}\nu_{k+1})[\nu_{k+1}])

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\times\big(r_{k}\gamma(t_{k},X_{k+1})Y_{k}+r_{k}c(t_{k},X_{k+1})Y_{k}\big),

(5.6)

where
\epsilon_{3}\in(0,1)
. Adding (
5.5
) and (
5.6
) gives

\displaystyle u_{k+1}Y_{k+1}-u_{k}Y_{k}=r_{k}\psi(t_{k},X_{k+1})Y_{k}-r_{k}\mu(X_{k+1})g(t_{k},X_{k+1})Y_{k}+\mathcal{O}(r_{k}^{2}Y_{k}),

(5.7)

which on using the expression for
(Z_{k+1}-Z_{k})
from (
3.25
) gives the desired bound.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\varphi(X_{\chi})Y_{\chi}+Z_{\chi}-u(t_{0},x))=\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\chi-1}u_{k+1}Y_{k+1}+Z_{k+1}-u_{k}Y_{k}-Z_{k})\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\chi-1}\big(u_{k+1}Y_{k+1}+Z_{k+1}-u_{k}Y_{k}-Z_{k})\big)I_{\bar{G}}(X_{k})\Big)+\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\chi-1}\big(u_{k+1}Y_{k+1}+Z_{k+1}-u_{k}Y_{k}-Z_{k})\big)I_{\bar{G}^{c}}(X_{k})\Big).

Note that due to uniform boundedness of the terms involved inside summation, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\Big(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\chi-1}\big(u_{k+1}Y_{k+1}+Z_{k+1}-u_{k}Y_{k}-Z_{k})\big)I_{\bar{G}}(X_{k})\Big)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\big((u_{k+1}Y_{k+1}+Z_{k+1}-u_{k}Y_{k}-Z_{k})I_{\bar{G}}(X_{k})\big),

where we assign
X_{k+1}=X_{k}
,
t_{k+1}=t_{k}
,
Y_{k+1}=Y_{k}
, and
Z_{k+1}=Z_{k}
when
k>\chi
.
From
[
20
]
, we know that
\mathbb{E}\big(u_{k+1}Y_{k+1}+Z_{k+1}-u_{k}Y_{k}-Z_{k}|X_{k}\in\bar{G}\big)\leq CY_{k}h^{2}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\big((u_{k+1}Y_{k+1}+Z_{k+1}-u_{k}Y_{k}-Z_{k})I_{\bar{G}}(X_{k})\big)=\mathbb{E}\Big(\mathbb{E}\big(u_{k+1}Y_{k+1}+Z_{k+1}-u_{k}Y_{k}-Z_{k}|X_{k}\in\bar{G}\big)I_{\bar{G}}(X_{k})\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big(\mathbb{E}\big(u_{k+1}Y_{k+1}+Z_{k+1}-u_{k}Y_{k}-Z_{k}|X_{k}\in\bar{G}\big)I_{\bar{G}}(X_{k})I(\chi>k)\Big)\leq Ch^{2}\mathbb{E}(Y_{k}I_{\bar{G}}(X_{k})I(\chi>k)),

which implies

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\big((u_{k+1}Y_{k+1}+Z_{k+1}-u_{k}Y_{k}-Z_{k})I_{\bar{G}}(X_{k})\big)\leq Ch^{2}\mathbb{E}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}(Y_{k}I_{\bar{G}}(X_{k})I(\chi>k))\leq Ch^{2}\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\chi-1}Y_{k}\Big).

From Lemma
5.2
, we have

\displaystyle|u_{k+1}Y_{k+1}+Z_{k+1}-u_{k}Y_{k}-Z_{k}|I_{\bar{G}^{c}}(X_{k})\leq Cr_{k}^{2}Y_{k}I_{\bar{G}^{c}}(X_{k}),

(5.8)

which implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\chi-1}\big(u_{k+1}Y_{k+1}+Z_{k+1}-u_{k}Y_{k}-Z_{k})\big)I_{\bar{G}^{c}}(X_{k})\Big)\leq Ch^{1/2}\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\chi-1}r_{k}Y_{k}I_{\bar{G}^{c}}(X_{k})\Big).

(5.9)

Using (
3.20
) and (
3.24
), we can deduce that

\displaystyle Y_{k}\leq Y_{k-1}(1+Kr_{k-1}I_{\bar{G}^{c}}(X_{k-1}))(1+\tilde{K}h)=Y_{0}e^{\tilde{K}T}\prod\limits_{i=0}^{k}(1+Kr_{i}I_{\bar{G}^{c}}(X_{i})),

where
K,\tilde{K}>0
are independent of
h
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\varphi(X_{\chi})Y_{\chi}+Z_{\chi}-u(t_{0},x))

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}\mathbb{E}\sum_{k=0}^{\chi}\prod\limits_{i=0}^{k}(1+Kr_{i}I_{\bar{G}^{c}}(X_{i}))

\displaystyle\quad+Ch^{1/2}\mathbb{E}\sum_{k=0}^{\chi}r_{k}I_{\bar{G}^{c}}(X_{k})\prod\limits_{i=0}^{k}(1+Kr_{i}I_{\bar{G}^{c}}(X_{i})).

(5.10)

Using the same set of arguments as in Lemma
4.6
and Corollary
4.1
, it can be shown that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sum_{k=0}^{\chi}r_{k}I_{\bar{G}^{c}}(X_{k})\prod\limits_{i=0}^{k}(1+Kr_{i}I_{\bar{G}^{c}}(X_{i}))\leq C,\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{E}\sum_{k=0}^{\chi}\prod\limits_{i=0}^{k}(1+Kr_{i}I_{\bar{G}^{c}}(X_{i}))\leq C/h,

where
C>0
is independent of
h
. Using the above bounds in (
5.10
), we get the desired result.
∎

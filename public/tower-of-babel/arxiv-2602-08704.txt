Friedkin–Johnsen Social Influence Dynamics on Networks: A Boundary-Value Formulation and Influenceability Measures
Moses Boudourides

Abstract
This article presents a rigorous mathematical analysis of the Friedkin–Johnsen model of social influence on networks. We frame the opinion dynamics as a discrete boundary-value problem on a network, emphasizing the role of stubborn (boundary) and susceptible (interior) agents in shaping opinion evolution. This perspective allows for a precise analysis of how network structure, stubborn agents (boundary), and susceptible agents (interior) collectively determine the evolution of opinions. We derive the transient and steady-state solutions using two distinct but related approaches: a general resolvent-based method applicable to agents with heterogeneous susceptibilities, and a spectral method valid for the special case of homogeneous susceptibility. We further establish quantitative convergence rates to the steady state, derive explicit sensitivity formulas with respect to susceptibility parameters, and prove perturbation bounds under changes in the influence matrix. Moreover, we formally define a set of influenceability measures and prove some of their basic properties. Finally, we provide a Monte Carlo illustration on the Zachary karate club graph, showing how the proposed opinion broadcasting centralities and centralizations behave under random susceptibility profiles and how they relate to classical network centralities.

Introduction

Problem formulation and contributions

We study discrete-time Friedkin–Johnsen opinion dynamics on a graph with a nonempty set of boundary (stubborn) nodes whose opinions are fixed (Dirichlet boundary conditions). The resulting interior dynamics form an affine system driven by a random-walk operator. Our contributions are:
(i) an explicit block-matrix reduction of the dynamics to the interior, yielding existence, uniqueness, and a closed-form resolvent-based representation of the steady state (Green’s operator);
(ii) resolvent-based influenceability measures quantifying boundary-to-interior influence; (iii) quantitative convergence-rate bounds in terms of
\rho(S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega})
(and a sharper
Dirichlet-spectral rate in the undirected homogeneous specialization);
(iv) explicit sensitivity formulas for the steady state with respect to susceptibilities and perturbation
bounds with respect to the influence matrix blocks.

The study of how opinions and attitudes spread through social systems is a central theme in computational social science. The Friedkin–Johnsen (FJ) model of social influence provides a foundational framework for this process, positing that an individual’s opinion evolves as a convex combination of their initial, private belief and the opinions of their peers
[
9
,
12
,
10
]
. The balance between these two forces is governed by an individual’s susceptibility to social influence.

At its core, the FJ model is a linear dynamical system on a graph. This structure invites an analogy to the discrete diffusion or heat equation, a, perspective that allows the powerful tools of spectral graph theory and the theory of partial differential equations (PDEs) to be brought to bear on the problem. Our aim in this article is to develop this PDE-inspired approach with mathematical rigor.

The paper is structured as follows. Section 2 establishes the graph-theoretic notation and presents the main theoretical results, including the IBVP formulation and the derivation of the solutions. Section 3 formally defines the influenceability measures. Section 4 presents a Monte Carlo illustration on the Zachary karate club graph, and Section 5 concludes.

2
Preliminaries: Graph Notation and Influence Dynamics

Standing assumptions

We consider a finite set of
n
agents indexed by
V=\{1,\dots,n\}
.
Let
W\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be a row-stochastic matrix encoding interpersonal
influence among agents in
V
. We define the underlying graph
G=(V,E)
to be the
support graph
of
W
, that is,

(i,j)\in E\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad W_{ij}>0.

In general,
G
is directed and
W
is asymmetric
[
19
]
.
The undirected graph case corresponds to a symmetric edge set (and, thus, it arises from a symmetric matrix
W
) and will be treated as a special case.

No global connectivity assumption is imposed. Whenever structural assumptions are
required, they are stated explicitly and formulated in terms of reachability
properties in the support graph.

2.1
Undirected graph specialization and Laplacians

When the support graph
G
of
W
is undirected, let
A\in\{0,1\}^{n\times n}
be
the adjacency matrix of
G
. The degree matrix is
D=\mathrm{diag}(\deg(1),\ldots,\deg(n))
.
I
denotes the
n
-dimensional identity operator.

Definition 2.1

(Random-Walk Matrix)
.

The
random-walk matrix
is the matrix
N=D^{-1}A
, which is row-stochastic.

Definition 2.2

(Graph Laplacians)
.

The
combinatorial Laplacian
is the matrix
L=D-A
. The
symmetric normalized Laplacian
is the matrix
\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{sym}}=D^{-1/2}LD^{-1/2}=I-D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2}
.

Definition 2.3

(Dirichlet Laplacian
[
5
]
)
.

For any
\Omega\subset V
, the
Dirichlet Laplacian
on
\Omega
is the principal submatrix

\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{sym},\,\Omega\Omega}
of the symmetric normalized Laplacian

\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{sym}}
, obtained by restricting to the rows and columns
indexed by
\Omega
.

All the above operators are only defined in the undirected (symmetric) specialization and
are not used in the general directed setting.

2.2
The DeGroot and Friedkin–Johnsen models

The classical DeGroot model of opinion dynamics describes a linear averaging
process
[
6
]
:

v^{t+1}=Wv^{t},

(1)

where
v^{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
denotes the vector of opinions at time
t
.

The Friedkin–Johnsen model
[
9
,
12
,
10
,
11
]
extends the DeGroot dynamics by allowing
agents to retain attachment to their initial opinions:

v^{t+1}=SWv^{t}+(I-S)v^{0},

(2)

where
S=\mathrm{diag}(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})
is the diagonal susceptibility matrix with

s_{i}\in[0,1]
, and
v^{0}
denotes the vector of initial opinions.

When spectral expansions are used, we restrict for simplicity to homogeneous
susceptibility
S=sI
with
s\in(0,1)
.

2.3
IBVP Formulation and Block-Matrix Reduction

We partition the vertex set
V
into an interior
\Omega=\{i\in V:s_{i}>0\}
and a boundary
\partial\Omega=\{i\in V:s_{i}=0\}
. We assume
\partial\Omega
is non-empty and that every node in
\Omega
has a path to a node in
\partial\Omega
. Let the initial opinions be
v^{0}|_{\Omega}=\phi
and the fixed boundary opinions be
v^{t}|_{\partial\Omega}=\psi
.

By ordering the vertices such that
V=\Omega\cup\partial\Omega
, we write

W=\begin{pmatrix}W_{\Omega\Omega}&W_{\Omega\partial}\\
W_{\partial\Omega}&W_{\partial\partial}\end{pmatrix},\quad S=\begin{pmatrix}S_{\Omega}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad v^{t}=\begin{pmatrix}v_{t}^{\Omega}\\
\psi\end{pmatrix}.

(3)

The block
W_{\Omega\Omega}
describes influence among interior nodes (i.e., malleable agents), while
W_{\Omega\partial}
captures influence from
boundary to interior nodes (i.e., from stubborn to malleable agents). The blocks
W_{\partial\Omega}
and

W_{\partial\partial}
correspond to boundary dynamics and are irrelevant under
Dirichlet boundary conditions. The diagonal matrix
S=\mathrm{diag}(s_{i})
collects node susceptibilities, with

s_{i}\in[0,1]
. Its restriction
S_{\Omega}
denotes the
|\Omega|\times|\Omega|

diagonal matrix associated with interior nodes; boundary nodes are assumed to
have zero susceptibility.

Finally,
v^{t}
denotes the opinion vector at time
t
, decomposed into the
interior opinion vector
v_{t}^{\Omega}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\Omega|}
and the fixed
boundary opinion vector
\psi\in\mathbb{R}^{|\partial\Omega|}
.

Theorem 2.4

(Interior Affine Dynamics)
.

Denoting by
I_{\Omega}
the
|\Omega|\times|\Omega|
identity matrix acting on interior-state vectors indexed by
\Omega
,
the opinion dynamics on the interior
\Omega
are governed by the affine recursion:

v_{t+1}^{\Omega}=S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega}v_{t}^{\Omega}+S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\partial}\psi+(I_{\Omega}-S_{\Omega})\phi.

(4)

Proof.

Substituting the block matrices into the FJ model (
2
) yields:

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}v_{t+1}^{\Omega}\\
\psi\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}S_{\Omega}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}W_{\Omega\Omega}&W_{\Omega\partial}\\
W_{\partial\Omega}&W_{\partial\partial}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}v_{t}^{\Omega}\\
\psi\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}I_{\Omega}-S_{\Omega}&0\\
0&I_{\partial}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\phi\\
\psi\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}S_{\Omega}(W_{\Omega\Omega}v_{t}^{\Omega}+W_{\Omega\partial}\psi)\\
0\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}(I_{\Omega}-S_{\Omega})\phi\\
\psi\end{pmatrix}.

The top block of this matrix equation gives the desired result. The bottom block confirms
\psi=\psi
, consistent with the boundary condition.
∎

2.4
General Solution via Resolvent Method (Heterogeneous Susceptibility)

We now solve for the transient and steady-state behavior of the interior dynamics (
4
).

Theorem 2.5

(Existence and uniqueness of steady state
[
21
]
)
.

If the iteration matrix satisfies

\rho(S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega})<1,

(5)

then there exists a unique steady-state opinion vector
v_{*}^{\Omega}
given by

v_{*}^{\Omega}=(I_{\Omega}-S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega})^{-1}\Big(S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\partial}\psi+(I_{\Omega}-S_{\Omega})\phi\Big).

(6)

Proof.

The interior dynamics (
4
) define an affine map on
\mathbb{R}^{|\Omega|}
with linear part
S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega}
.
Condition (
5
) implies that
I_{\Omega}-S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega}
is invertible (equivalently, the Neumann series

\sum_{k\geq 0}(S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega})^{k}
converges), yielding existence and uniqueness of the fixed point (
6
).
∎

Proposition 2.6

(Necessity of boundary reachability/damping)
.

Assume that boundary reachability fails, i.e., there exists a nonempty subset

C\subseteq\Omega
such that no node in
C
has a directed path (in the support
graph of
W
) to any boundary node, and such that
C
is closed under the interior
dynamics, meaning that

(W_{\Omega\Omega})_{ij}=0\quad\text{for all }i\in C,\ j\in\Omega\setminus C.

Then the submatrix
W_{CC}
is row-stochastic and satisfies
\rho(W_{CC})=1
.
In particular, if
s_{i}=1
for all
i\in C
, then
S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega}

contains
W_{CC}
as a principal submatrix, and hence

\rho(S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega})\geq 1.

Therefore the spectral-radius condition (
5
)
fails, and the Dirichlet steady state (
6
) need not exist
uniquely.

Proof.

Closedness implies that
W_{CC}
is itself row-stochastic, so it has eigenvalue
1
with a nonnegative
eigenvector, hence
\rho(W_{CC})=1
. If
s_{i}=1
on
C
, then
S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega}
restricts to

W_{CC}
on that class, giving
\rho(S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega})\geq 1

[
21
]
.
∎

Remark 2.7

(Example: a closed interior class)
.

Let
\Omega=\{1,2\}
,
\partial\Omega=\{3\}
and take

W_{\Omega\Omega}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad W_{\Omega\partial}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\end{pmatrix}.

Then
C=\{1,2\}
is closed and
\rho(W_{\Omega\Omega})=1
. If
S_{\Omega}=I
, then

\rho(S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega})=1
and (
5
) fails.

Definition 2.8

(Resolvent / Green’s Function)
.

The matrix
G_{S}=(I_{\Omega}-S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega})^{-1}
is the resolvent of the system, which acts as the discrete Green’s function for this problem. It can be expressed via the Neumann series
G_{S}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega})^{k}
.

Remark 2.9

(Absorbing Markov chain interpretation)
.

The resolvent
(I_{\Omega}-S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega})^{-1}
is closely related to the fundamental matrix of an absorbing Markov chain obtained by making the boundary nodes
\partial\Omega
absorbing. In this interpretation, the Neumann series
\sum_{k\geq 0}(S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega})^{k}
encodes expected discounted visit counts to interior states before absorption.

Theorem 2.10

(Spectral characterization via cycle damping)
.

Let
W
be a row-stochastic matrix supported on a directed graph and

S_{\Omega}=\mathrm{diag}(s_{i})
with
s_{i}\in[0,1]
.
Assume boundary reachability.
Then

\displaystyle\rho(S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega})<1\quad\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\text{every directed cycle in the interior subgraph}

(7)

\displaystyle\text{contains at least one node }i\text{ with }s_{i}<1.

Proof.

If there exists a directed cycle in
\Omega
on which
s_{i}=1
for all nodes, then the
product of transition weights along the cycle yields an invariant nonnegative vector,
implying
\rho(S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega})\geq 1
.
Conversely, if every directed cycle is damped by at least one factor
s_{i}<1
, then
standard results on nonnegative matrices imply that all cycle gains are strictly
contractive, hence
\rho(S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega})<1
.
∎

Example 1

(Asymmetric directed cycle)
.

Let
\Omega=\{1,2,3\}
and
\partial\Omega=\{4\}
, with directed edges

1\to 2\to 3\to 1
and
3\to 4
. Let
s_{1}=s_{2}=1
and
s_{3}\in(0,1)
.
Although
W_{\Omega\Omega}
contains a directed cycle, the presence of damping at
node
3
ensures
\rho(S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega})<1
and hence invertibility of

I-S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega}
.

Theorem 2.11

(Transient Solution)
.

The transient opinion vector on the interior is given by:

v_{t}^{\Omega}=(S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega})^{t}\phi+\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}(S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega})^{k}\Big(S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\partial}\psi+(I_{\Omega}-S_{\Omega})\phi\Big).

(8)

Proof.

This result is obtained by unrolling the recursion in (
4
).
∎

Proposition 2.12

(Error recursion and exact convergence formula)
.

Assume
\rho(S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega})<1
and let
v_{\Omega}^{\ast}
be the unique steady state
(
6
). Then, for all
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

v_{\Omega}^{t}-v_{\Omega}^{\ast}\;=\;(S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega})^{t}\,(\phi-v_{\Omega}^{\ast}).

(9)

Consequently, for any submultiplicative matrix norm
\|\cdot\|
and the corresponding vector norm,

\|v_{\Omega}^{t}-v_{\Omega}^{\ast}\|\;\leq\;\|(S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega})^{t}\|\,\|\phi-v_{\Omega}^{\ast}\|.

(10)

Proof.

Let
b:=S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\partial}\psi+(I_{\Omega}-S_{\Omega})\phi
.
The affine recursion for the interior opinions reads

v_{\Omega}^{t+1}=S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega}v_{\Omega}^{t}+b
.
Since
v_{\Omega}^{\ast}
satisfies
v_{\Omega}^{\ast}=S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega}v_{\Omega}^{\ast}+b
,
subtracting gives the linear error recursion

(v_{\Omega}^{t+1}-v_{\Omega}^{\ast})=S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega}\,(v_{\Omega}^{t}-v_{\Omega}^{\ast}).

Iterating from
t=0
(with
v_{\Omega}^{0}=\phi
) yields (
9
). Taking norms and using
submultiplicativity gives (
10
).
∎

Theorem 2.13

(Quantitative convergence rate via spectral radius)
.

Assume
\rho(S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega})<1
and let
A:=S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega}
.
Then for every
\delta>0
there exists a constant
C_{\delta}\geq 1
such that for all
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\|v_{\Omega}^{t}-v_{\Omega}^{\ast}\|\;\leq\;C_{\delta}\,(\rho(A)+\delta)^{t}\,\|\phi-v_{\Omega}^{\ast}\|.

(11)

Moreover,

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\ \|v_{\Omega}^{t}-v_{\Omega}^{\ast}\|^{1/t}\;\leq\;\rho(A).

(12)

Proof.

By Proposition
2.12
,
\|v_{\Omega}^{t}-v_{\Omega}^{\ast}\|\leq\|A^{t}\|\,\|\phi-v_{\Omega}^{\ast}\|
.
For any submultiplicative norm, Gelfand’s formula gives

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|A^{t}\|^{1/t}=\rho(A)
; see, e.g.,
[
14
,
13
]
.
Hence for any
\delta>0
there exists
t_{0}=t_{0}(\delta)
such that

\|A^{t}\|^{1/t}\leq\rho(A)+\delta
for all
t\geq t_{0}
, i.e.

\|A^{t}\|\leq(\rho(A)+\delta)^{t}
for
t\geq t_{0}
.
Let

C_{\delta}:=\max_{0\leq t\leq t_{0}}\ \frac{\|A^{t}\|}{(\rho(A)+\delta)^{t}},

so that
\|A^{t}\|\leq C_{\delta}(\rho(A)+\delta)^{t}
for all
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Substituting into Proposition
2.12
yields (
11
).
Finally, (
12
) follows by taking
\limsup
of
t
th roots in (
10
).
∎

Corollary 2.14

(Sharpened rate in the undirected homogeneous specialization)
.

Assume the undirected random-walk specialization
W=N=D^{-1}A
and homogeneous susceptibility

S_{\Omega}=sI_{\Omega}
with
s\in(0,1)
, as in Corollary 2.12.
Let
\lambda_{\max}=\lambda_{\max}(N_{\Omega\Omega})
denote the spectral radius of the Dirichlet
restriction
N_{\Omega\Omega}
. Then, for every
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\|v_{\Omega}^{t}-v_{\Omega}^{\ast}\|_{2}\;\leq\;(s\,\lambda_{\max})^{t}\,\|\phi-v_{\Omega}^{\ast}\|_{2},

(13)

and
s\,\lambda_{\max}<1
.

Proof.

In this specialization,
A=S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega}=sN_{\Omega\Omega}
.
By similarity,
N_{\Omega\Omega}
is similar to the symmetric matrix

D_{\Omega}^{1/2}N_{\Omega\Omega}D_{\Omega}^{-1/2}
, hence it is diagonalizable with real spectrum
and
\|N_{\Omega\Omega}^{t}\|_{2}=\lambda_{\max}^{t}
.
Therefore
\|A^{t}\|_{2}=s^{t}\|N_{\Omega\Omega}^{t}\|_{2}=(s\lambda_{\max})^{t}
.
Insert this into Proposition
2.12
. Finally, since
\rho(A)=s\lambda_{\max}

and
\rho(A)<1
(by Theorem 2.5 assumptions), we have
s\lambda_{\max}<1
.
∎

Theorem 2.15

(Sensitivity of the steady state to a susceptibility parameter)
.

Assume
\rho(S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega})<1
and write

v_{\Omega}^{\ast}(s)=(I_{\Omega}-S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega})^{-1}\Bigl(S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\partial}\psi+(I_{\Omega}-S_{\Omega})\phi\Bigr).

Let
k\in\Omega
and let
E_{k}:=e_{k}e_{k}^{\top}
be the coordinate projector on the
k
th interior index.
Then
v_{\Omega}^{\ast}
is differentiable in
s_{k}
and

\frac{\partial v_{\Omega}^{\ast}}{\partial s_{k}}\;=\;G_{S}\,E_{k}\Bigl(W_{\Omega\Omega}v_{\Omega}^{\ast}+W_{\Omega\partial}\psi-\phi\Bigr),\qquad G_{S}=(I_{\Omega}-S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega})^{-1}.

(14)

Equivalently,

\frac{\partial v_{\Omega}^{\ast}}{\partial s_{k}}\;=\;\Bigl(\bigl(W_{\Omega\Omega}v_{\Omega}^{\ast}+W_{\Omega\partial}\psi-\phi\bigr)_{k}\Bigr)\,G_{S}e_{k}.

(15)

Proof.

Let
A(s):=I_{\Omega}-S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega}
and

b(s):=S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\partial}\psi+(I_{\Omega}-S_{\Omega})\phi
so that
v_{\Omega}^{\ast}=A^{-1}b
.
Differentiate with respect to
s_{k}
using

\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{k}}(A^{-1}b)=A^{-1}\frac{\partial b}{\partial s_{k}}-A^{-1}\frac{\partial A}{\partial s_{k}}A^{-1}b.

Since
\frac{\partial S_{\Omega}}{\partial s_{k}}=E_{k}
, we have

\frac{\partial A}{\partial s_{k}}=-E_{k}W_{\Omega\Omega}
and

\frac{\partial b}{\partial s_{k}}=E_{k}(W_{\Omega\partial}\psi-\phi)
.
Substituting and using
A^{-1}b=v_{\Omega}^{\ast}
gives (
14
).
Identity (
15
) follows from
E_{k}u=u_{k}e_{k}
for any vector
u
.
∎

Theorem 2.16

(Perturbation bound with respect to the influence matrix blocks)
.

Fix
S_{\Omega}
and the data
(\psi,\phi)
and consider two influence matrices
W
and
\widetilde{W}

sharing the same partition
V=\Omega\cup\partial\Omega
.
Assume
\rho(S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega})<1
and
\rho(S_{\Omega}\widetilde{W}_{\Omega\Omega})<1
and denote

A:=I_{\Omega}-S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega},\qquad\widetilde{A}:=I_{\Omega}-S_{\Omega}\widetilde{W}_{\Omega\Omega},\qquad b:=S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\partial}\psi+(I_{\Omega}-S_{\Omega})\phi,\qquad\widetilde{b}:=S_{\Omega}\widetilde{W}_{\Omega\partial}\psi+(I_{\Omega}-S_{\Omega})\phi.

Then, for any compatible submultiplicative norm,

\|v_{\Omega}^{\ast}(W)-v_{\Omega}^{\ast}(\widetilde{W})\|\;\leq\;\|A^{-1}\|\,\|S_{\Omega}\|\,\|W_{\Omega\Omega}-\widetilde{W}_{\Omega\Omega}\|\,\|\widetilde{A}^{-1}\|\,\|\widetilde{b}\|\;+\;\|\widetilde{A}^{-1}\|\,\|S_{\Omega}\|\,\|W_{\Omega\partial}-\widetilde{W}_{\Omega\partial}\|\,\|\psi\|.

(16)

Proof.

Write
v_{\Omega}^{\ast}(W)=A^{-1}b
and
v_{\Omega}^{\ast}(\widetilde{W})=\widetilde{A}^{-1}\widetilde{b}
.
Then

A^{-1}b-\widetilde{A}^{-1}\widetilde{b}=(A^{-1}-\widetilde{A}^{-1})\,\widetilde{b}+A^{-1}(b-\widetilde{b}).

Using the resolvent identity

A^{-1}-\widetilde{A}^{-1}=A^{-1}(\widetilde{A}-A)\widetilde{A}^{-1}
(see, e.g.,
[
14
,
13
]
)
and
\widetilde{A}-A=S_{\Omega}(W_{\Omega\Omega}-\widetilde{W}_{\Omega\Omega})
, we obtain

\|(A^{-1}-\widetilde{A}^{-1})\,\widetilde{b}\|\leq\|A^{-1}\|\,\|S_{\Omega}\|\,\|W_{\Omega\Omega}-\widetilde{W}_{\Omega\Omega}\|\,\|\widetilde{A}^{-1}\|\,\|\widetilde{b}\|.

Moreover,
b-\widetilde{b}=S_{\Omega}(W_{\Omega\partial}-\widetilde{W}_{\Omega\partial})\psi
, hence

\|A^{-1}(b-\widetilde{b})\|\leq\|A^{-1}\|\,\|S_{\Omega}\|\,\|W_{\Omega\partial}-\widetilde{W}_{\Omega\partial}\|\,\|\psi\|.

Combining the two bounds yields (
16
).
∎

2.5
Special Case: Spectral Solution with Homogeneous Susceptibility

In this subsection we assume the undirected random-walk specialization
W=N=D^{-1}A
(equivalently,
D
is reversible with respect to
D
). Moreover, in the special case where susceptibility is homogeneous for all malleable agents, i.e.,
S_{\Omega}=sI_{\Omega}
for some scalar
s\in(0,1]
, the analysis simplifies, and we can obtain a true spectral decomposition.

Corollary 2.17

(Spectral representation in the undirected random-walk case)
.

Assume that the support graph
G
of
W
is undirected and that

W=N:=D^{-1}A

is the random-walk matrix of
G
. Let
\Omega\subset V
denote the interior set
and assume homogeneous susceptibility
S_{\Omega}=sI_{\Omega}
with
s\in(0,1)
.
Then the Green operator

G_{S}:=(I_{\Omega}-sN_{\Omega\Omega})^{-1}

admits the spectral representation

G_{S}=\sum_{k=1}^{|\Omega|}\frac{1}{1-s\,\lambda_{k}}\,u_{k}u_{k}^{\top},

where
\{\lambda_{k}\}
and
\{u_{k}\}
are the eigenvalues and orthonormal eigenvectors
of the symmetric matrix

D_{\Omega}^{1/2}N_{\Omega\Omega}D_{\Omega}^{-1/2}=I_{\Omega}-\mathscr{L}_{\Omega\Omega},

and
\mathscr{L}_{\Omega\Omega}
denotes the Dirichlet restriction of the symmetric
normalized Laplacian.

Proof.

When
S_{\Omega}=sI_{\Omega}
, the iteration matrix becomes
sW_{\Omega\Omega}
. This matrix is not symmetric, but it is similar to the symmetric matrix
s(I-\mathscr{L}_{\Omega\Omega})
. The spectral properties of
sW_{\Omega\Omega}
are therefore determined by the eigenvalues of the Dirichlet Laplacian
\mathscr{L}_{\Omega\Omega}
, allowing for a valid spectral decomposition of the resolvent and the transient solution
[
15
]
.
∎

3
Influenceability Measures

3.1
The Notion of Influenciability

We fix a
source
boundary vertex
s_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and an interior initial opinion vector

\phi_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\Omega|}
. For emphasis, we introduce the notation

i(s_{0}):=s_{0}\in\partial\Omega

for the (distinguished) boundary vertex at which the boundary input is applied. We consider the
corresponding temporal solution
\{v^{t}(s_{0},\phi_{0})\}_{t\geq 0}
of the Friedkin–Johnsen IBVP
from the previous sections, and we denote by

\overline{v}(s_{0},\phi_{0})

its steady state (cf. (
6
)).

Directed distances.

Recall that throughout the manuscript
G=(V,E)
is a directed graph (arising from the generally asymmetric
influence matrix
W
). For
i,j\in V
we define the
directed geodesic distance

d(i,j):=\min\{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\ \exists\ \text{a directed path }i\to j\text{ of length }\ell\},

(17)

with the convention
d(i,j)=+\infty
if there is no directed
i\to j
path. We also set
d(i,i)=0
.

Definition 3.1

(Opinion kick–off time and germinated opinion)
.

For each vertex
i\in V
, the
opinion kick–off time

T_{i}^{0}=T_{i}^{0}(s_{0},\phi_{0})

is defined by

T_{i}^{0}:=\inf\bigl\{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\;:\;v_{i}^{t}(s_{0},\phi_{0})\neq 0\bigr\},

with the convention
\inf\emptyset=+\infty
. The corresponding
germinated opinion
of
i
is

v_{i}^{T_{i}^{0}}:=v_{i}^{T_{i}^{0}}(s_{0},\phi_{0}),

interpreting
v_{i}^{T_{i}^{0}}=0
when
T_{i}^{0}=+\infty
(i.e., when
v_{i}^{t}\equiv 0
for all
t
).

Definition 3.2

(Stabilization time and steady–state opinion)
.

For a prescribed precision
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
, the (approximate)
opinion stabilization time

of vertex
i
is

T_{i}^{\infty}(\varepsilon):=\inf\bigl\{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\;:\;\lvert v_{i}^{t}(s_{0},\phi_{0})-\overline{v}_{i}(s_{0},\phi_{0})\rvert<\varepsilon\bigr\}.

The limiting value
\overline{v}_{i}=\overline{v}_{i}(s_{0},\phi_{0})
is called the

steady–state opinion
of
i
.

Proposition 3.3

(Kick–off times and directed distance)
.

For every vertex
i\in V
,

T_{i}^{0}=d\bigl(i(s_{0}),i\bigr).

In particular,
T_{i}^{0}=0
if and only if
i=i(s_{0})
, and
T_{i}^{0}=+\infty
if and only if
i
is not reachable
from
i(s_{0})
in
G
.

Proof.

At time
t=0
, the only imposed nonzero opinion is at the source boundary vertex
i(s_{0})
.
Under the graph–local update rule (i.e., a node can only be affected through incoming arcs from its
in-neighborhood), influence can traverse at most one directed edge per time step. Hence if

d(i(s_{0}),i)>t
then necessarily
v_{i}^{t}(s_{0},\phi_{0})=0
, so
T_{i}^{0}\geq d(i(s_{0}),i)
.
Conversely, if
d(i(s_{0}),i)=\ell<\infty
, then along any directed path of length
\ell
from
i(s_{0})
to
i
,
the boundary input produces a nonzero contribution at time
\ell
, implying
v_{i}^{\ell}(s_{0},\phi_{0})\neq 0
and
thus
T_{i}^{0}\leq\ell
. Therefore
T_{i}^{0}=\ell=d(i(s_{0}),i)
. If
d(i(s_{0}),i)=+\infty
, then
i
is unreachable
from the source, so
v_{i}^{t}(s_{0},\phi_{0})=0
for all
t
, and by convention
T_{i}^{0}=+\infty
.
∎

Proposition 3.4

(Ordering of kick–off times)
.

For any
i,j\in V
,

T_{i}^{0}\leq T_{j}^{0}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d\bigl(i(s_{0}),i\bigr)\leq d\bigl(i(s_{0}),j\bigr),

with the understanding that finite values are always
\leq+\infty
.
Moreover, if
d(i(s_{0}),i)<\infty
and
\overline{v}_{i}(s_{0},\phi_{0})>0
, then

T_{i}^{\infty}(\varepsilon)\ \geq\ T_{i}^{0}\qquad(0<\varepsilon\ll 1).

Proof.

The equivalence follows immediately from Proposition
3.3
.
For the second claim: if
d(i(s_{0}),i)<\infty
then
v_{i}^{t}(s_{0},\phi_{0})=0
for all
t<T_{i}^{0}
and

v_{i}^{T_{i}^{0}}(s_{0},\phi_{0})\neq 0
, while
\overline{v}_{i}>0
by assumption. Hence

\lvert v_{i}^{t}-\overline{v}_{i}\rvert=\overline{v}_{i}
for all
t<T_{i}^{0}
, which is not
<\varepsilon
for all
sufficiently small
\varepsilon
. Therefore
T_{i}^{\infty}(\varepsilon)\geq T_{i}^{0}
.
∎

Proposition 3.5

(Monotonicity of opinions)
.

Assume nonnegative weights and a nonnegative boundary input at
i(s_{0})
. Then, for all
i\in V
,

0\leq v_{i}^{T_{i}^{0}}\leq\overline{v}_{i},

and
t\mapsto v_{i}^{t}(s_{0},\phi_{0})
is nondecreasing. In particular, for vertices that are reachable from

i(s_{0})
, earlier kick–off cannot exceed the steady state:

d(i(s_{0}),i)<\infty\ \Longrightarrow\ 0<v_{i}^{T_{i}^{0}}\leq\overline{v}_{i}.

Proof.

Under the stated hypotheses, the IBVP update is an order–preserving affine map. Hence the iterates remain
nonnegative and increase monotonically toward the steady state, yielding

0\leq v_{i}^{T_{i}^{0}}\leq\overline{v}_{i}
and monotonicity in
t
. If
i
is reachable, then
T_{i}^{0}<\infty

(Proposition
3.3
) so
v_{i}^{T_{i}^{0}}\neq 0
by definition.
∎

Interpretation.

The quadruple
\bigl(T_{i}^{0},\,v_{i}^{T_{i}^{0}},\,T_{i}^{\infty}(\varepsilon),\,\overline{v}_{i}\bigr)

characterizes the
influenceability
of vertex
i
relative to the source
i(s_{0})
:
highly influenceable vertices react early (small
T_{i}^{0}
), receive a strong initial “germ”
(large
v_{i}^{T_{i}^{0}}
), and stabilize rapidly (small
T_{i}^{\infty}(\varepsilon)
) at a comparatively
large steady–state opinion
\overline{v}_{i}
.

Next we define steady–state measures of influence based on the IBVP solution.

Definition 3.6

(Influence Matrix)
.

Let
v_{*}^{\Omega}(j,\psi,\phi)
be the steady-state opinion of node
j\in\Omega
when the boundary data
at node
k\in\partial\Omega
is
\psi
and the interior initial opinions are
\phi
.
The
influence matrix

U\in\mathbb{R}^{|\Omega|\times|\partial\Omega|}
is defined by its columns:

U_{:,k}=v_{*}^{\Omega}(k,e_{k},0),

(18)

where
e_{k}
is the standard basis vector for the
k
-th boundary node, and where all other boundary nodes
and all interior initial opinions are
0
.

The influence matrix
U
encodes the fundamental mechanism of social influence in the Friedkin–Johnsen model. Specifically, the entry
U_{jk}
quantifies the
steady-state response
of interior node
j
to a unit opinion imposed at boundary node
k
, with all other boundary nodes held at zero opinion and all interior nodes starting from neutral opinion.

More precisely, under the boundary condition where only node
k\in\partial\Omega
has fixed opinion
\psi_{k}=1
and all other boundary nodes have opinion
0
, the steady-state opinion at interior node
j\in\Omega
is exactly
U_{jk}
. This represents the
total influence
that node
k
exerts on node
j
through the network, accounting for both direct connections and indirect paths of influence propagation.

The matrix
U
thus serves as a
linear response operator
: it maps boundary conditions to interior steady states in a way that captures the full complexity of the network structure and the heterogeneous susceptibilities of the agents. This perspective is analogous to the Green’s function in the theory of partial differential equations, where boundary data is translated into interior solutions via a fundamental solution operator.

Proposition 3.7
.

From the steady-state solution (
6
), the influence matrix

U
admits the representation

U=(I_{\Omega}-S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega})^{-1}S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\partial}=G_{S}S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\partial}.

Proposition 3.8

(Boundary-wise influence decomposition)
.

Let
G=(I_{\Omega}-S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega})^{-1}
and let

W_{\Omega\partial}
denote the interior–boundary block of
W
.
Then the interior steady state admits the decomposition

v_{*}^{\Omega}=\sum_{j\in\partial\Omega}\beta^{(j)}\,\psi_{j}+G(I_{\Omega}-S_{\Omega})\phi_{\Omega},\qquad\beta^{(j)}:=S_{\Omega}GW_{\Omega\partial}e_{j},

where
e_{j}
denotes the canonical basis vector in

\mathbb{R}^{|\partial\Omega|}
associated with boundary node
j
.
Each vector
\beta^{(j)}
quantifies the marginal influence of boundary node
j
on the interior steady state.

Proposition 3.9

(Non-negativity and bounds on
U
)
.

Assume
S_{\Omega}
is diagonal with
S_{\Omega,ii}\in(0,1]
for all
i\in\Omega
, and
W_{\Omega\Omega},W_{\Omega\partial}
have non-negative entries. Then:

(i)

U
has non-negative entries:
U_{jk}\geq 0
for all
j\in\Omega,k\in\partial\Omega
.

(ii)

Each entry is bounded:
0\leq U_{jk}\leq 1
for all
j,k
.

(iii)

The row sums satisfy
0<\sum_{k\in\partial\Omega}U_{jk}\leq 1
for all
j\in\Omega
.

Proof.

(i)
Since
S_{\Omega}
and
W_{\Omega\Omega},W_{\Omega\partial}
are non-negative, the Neumann series representation

G_{S}=(I_{\Omega}-S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega})^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega})^{k}

is a sum of non-negative matrices, hence non-negative. Therefore
U=G_{S}S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\partial}
is also non-negative.

(ii)
To see that
U_{jk}\leq 1
, note that the steady-state opinion
\overline{v}_{j}(k,e_{k},0)
at node
j
when node
k
is the sole boundary source with unit opinion cannot exceed the maximum possible opinion value of
1
. More formally, by the affine structure of the dynamics and the fact that all entries of
S_{\Omega}
and
W
are at most
1
, the steady state satisfies
0\leq\overline{v}_{j}\leq 1
.

(iii)
The row sum
\sum_{k\in\partial\Omega}U_{jk}
represents the total steady-state opinion of node
j
when the entire boundary is set to uniform unit opinion. By the linearity of the IBVP, this equals the steady-state opinion of node
j
under the boundary condition
\psi\equiv 1
. Since the dynamics preserve opinions in the interval
[0,1]
and the boundary input is
1
, we have
\sum_{k\in\partial\Omega}U_{jk}\leq 1
. The strict positivity follows from the assumption that every interior node has a path to the boundary (boundary reachability), ensuring that the boundary influence reaches every interior node.
∎

Remark 3.10

(Interpretation of row sums)
.

The row sum
r_{j}:=\sum_{k\in\partial\Omega}U_{jk}
quantifies the
total influenceability
of node
j
. It represents the steady-state opinion that node
j
would adopt if all boundary nodes simultaneously imposed unit opinion. Nodes with larger row sums are more susceptible to external influence from the boundary, while nodes with smaller row sums are more resistant (either due to high internal susceptibility
s_{j}
close to
1
, or due to being far from the boundary in the network).

Remark 3.11

(Interpretation of column sums)
.

The column sum
c_{k}:=\sum_{j\in\Omega}U_{jk}
quantifies the
total influence exerted
by boundary node
k
. It represents the cumulative effect of node
k
’s opinion on all interior nodes. Nodes with larger column sums are more influential broadcasters, while nodes with smaller column sums have limited reach through the network.

Proposition 3.12

(Monotonicity in susceptibility)
.

If
s_{j}\leq s_{j}^{\prime}
for all
j\in\Omega
(i.e., agents become more susceptible), then the corresponding influence matrices satisfy
U\leq U^{\prime}
entry-wise.

Proof.

Increasing susceptibility increases the weight placed on peer opinions relative to initial beliefs. This amplifies the influence of the boundary. Formally, since
G_{S}=(I_{\Omega}-S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\Omega})^{-1}
and
U=G_{S}S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\partial}
, increasing
S_{\Omega}
increases both
G_{S}
and the product
S_{\Omega}W_{\Omega\partial}
, hence
U
increases entry-wise.
∎

Remark 3.13

(Extreme cases)
.

When
S_{\Omega}\to 0
(agents become completely stubborn), we have
U\to 0
, meaning the boundary has no influence on the interior. Conversely, when
S_{\Omega}\to I
(agents become maximally susceptible), the influence matrix approaches its maximum possible values, reflecting the fact that all agents are equally influenced by the boundary.

3.2
All–Vertex Influenceability Matrices

Up to now, the source of influence was assumed to belong to a fixed boundary

\partial\Omega
. We next “scan” the network by allowing
any
vertex to
act as the unique source, while keeping the same underlying susceptibility
profile for the remaining vertices.

Definition 3.14

(Source–dependent susceptibility and initial condition)
.

Fix a susceptibility vector
s\in[0,1]^{V}
. For each

i\in V
we define the modified susceptibility vector
s[i]\in[0,1]^{V}
by

s[i]_{j}:=(1-\delta_{ij})\,s_{j}=\begin{cases}0,&j=i,\\
s_{j},&j\neq i,\end{cases}

(19)

where
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta. We also define the (full) initial
opinion vector
\phi[i]\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
by

\phi[i]_{j}:=\delta_{ij},\qquad j\in V.

(20)

Thus, in the source–indexed IBVP corresponding to
i
, the vertex
i
is fully stubborn
(
s[i]_{i}=0
) and carries unit opinion (
\phi[i]_{i}=1
), while every other vertex
j\neq i
has susceptibility
s[i]_{j}=s_{j}\in[0,1]

and starts from neutral opinion (
\phi[i]_{j}=0
). In particular, if
s_{j}=0
then

j
is also stubborn, whereas if
s_{j}>0
then
j
is susceptible.

Definition 3.15

(Opinion response matrices)
.

For each
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we define the
opinion response matrix

\mathscr{U}^{t}=\{\mathscr{U}^{t}_{i,j}\}_{i,j\in V}\in\mathbb{R}^{V\times V}
by

\mathscr{U}^{t}_{i,j}:=v_{j}^{t}\bigl(s[i],\phi[i]\bigr),\qquad i,j\in V,

(21)

where
v^{t}(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the unique solution of the source–indexed IBVP from the previous sections.

Definition 3.16

(Four canonical all–vertex matrices)
.

For each pair
i,j\in V
and each
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
, we define:

\displaystyle\mathscr{U}^{\infty}_{i,j}

\displaystyle:=\overline{v}_{j}\bigl(s[i],\phi[i]\bigr),

(22)

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}_{i,j}

\displaystyle:=T_{j}^{0}\bigl(s[i],\phi[i]\bigr),

(23)

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{i,j}

\displaystyle:=v_{j}^{\,T_{j}^{0}(s[i],\phi[i])}\bigl(s[i],\phi[i]\bigr),

(24)

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}(\varepsilon)_{i,j}

\displaystyle:=T_{j}^{\infty}\bigl(\varepsilon;s[i],\phi[i]\bigr).

(25)

Proposition 3.17

(Directed distance interpretation of
\mathscr{T}
)
.

Assume that the baseline susceptibility vector satisfies
s_{j}>0
for all
j\in V
.
For each
i\in V
, consider the source–indexed susceptibility vector
s[i]
defined by

s[i]_{i}=0
and
s[i]_{j}=s_{j}
for
j\neq i
(so that
i
is the unique stubborn vertex in the
corresponding IBVP). Then, for every
i,j\in V
,

\mathscr{T}_{i,j}=d(i,j),

where
d(i,j)
denotes the directed geodesic distance from
i
to
j
in
G
, with the convention that

d(i,j)=+\infty
(and hence
\mathscr{T}_{i,j}=+\infty
) when
j
is not reachable from
i
in
G
.
In particular,
\mathscr{T}
is generally
not
symmetric.

Proof.

Fix
i\in V
and consider the source–indexed IBVP defined by
(s[i],\phi[i])
.
By the same locality argument as in Proposition
3.3
, influence emitted at
i

can traverse at most one directed edge per time step. Hence
v_{j}^{t}(s[i],\phi[i])=0
whenever
d(i,j)>t
,
so
T_{j}^{0}(s[i],\phi[i])\geq d(i,j)
. Conversely, if
d(i,j)=\ell<\infty
, along any directed
i\to j
path
of length
\ell
one obtains
v_{j}^{\ell}(s[i],\phi[i])\neq 0
, implying
T_{j}^{0}(s[i],\phi[i])\leq\ell
.
Therefore
\mathscr{T}_{i,j}=T_{j}^{0}(s[i],\phi[i])=d(i,j)
. If
d(i,j)=+\infty
, then
v_{j}^{t}(s[i],\phi[i])=0

for all
t
and
\mathscr{T}_{i,j}=+\infty
.
∎

Proposition 3.18

(Row sums of
\mathscr{T}
and out–closeness)
.

Let
n:=|V|
and define the
out–reachability set
of
i
by

R^{+}(i):=\{\,j\in V\setminus\{i\}:d(i,j)<\infty\,\}.

Define the
out–closeness
of
i
by

c_{\mathrm{close}}^{+}(i):=\begin{cases}\dfrac{|R^{+}(i)|}{\sum_{j\in R^{+}(i)}d(i,j)},&|R^{+}(i)|>0,\\[4.73611pt]
0,&|R^{+}(i)|=0.\end{cases}

(26)

Then

\sum_{j\in R^{+}(i)}\mathscr{T}_{i,j}=\sum_{j\in R^{+}(i)}d(i,j)=\frac{|R^{+}(i)|}{c_{\mathrm{close}}^{+}(i)}.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.17
,
\mathscr{T}_{i,j}=d(i,j)
for all
j\in R^{+}(i)
.
The identity follows by direct substitution into (
26
). (See

[
20
,
8
,
22
,
17
]
for classical closeness notions and
directed variants.)
∎

Proposition 3.19

(Bounds on steady–state responses)
.

Assume
s\in[0,1]^{V}
and the source–indexed IBVP boundary value at the source satisfies
v_{i}^{t}\equiv 1
.
Then, for all
i,j\in V
,

0\leq\mathscr{U}^{\infty}_{i,j}\leq 1,\qquad\mathscr{U}^{\infty}_{i,i}=1.

Proof.

Order preservation and convexity of the Friedkin–Johnsen update imply that the state remains in the convex hull
of the stubborn values, hence in
[0,1]
. The identity
\mathscr{U}^{\infty}_{i,i}=1
follows from stubbornness
at the source.
∎

Definition 3.20

(Volumes of opinion broadcasting and reception)
.

For each
i\in V
, define its
volume of opinion broadcasting
and

volume of opinion reception
, respectively, by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{u}^{\infty,\mathrm{out}}_{i}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{j\in V\setminus\{i\}}\mathscr{U}^{\infty}_{i,j},

(27)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{u}^{\infty,\mathrm{in}}_{i}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{j\in V\setminus\{i\}}\mathscr{U}^{\infty}_{j,i}.

(28)

We also define the
graph influenceability
by

\mathscr{I}:=\sum_{i\in V}\mathfrak{u}^{\infty,\mathrm{out}}_{i}=\sum_{i\in V}\mathfrak{u}^{\infty,\mathrm{in}}_{i}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in V\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}\mathscr{U}^{\infty}_{i,j}.

(29)

Proposition 3.21

(Balance and extremal bounds)
.

For every
i\in V
,

0\leq\mathfrak{u}^{\infty,\mathrm{out}}_{i}\leq n-1,\qquad 0\leq\mathfrak{u}^{\infty,\mathrm{in}}_{i}\leq n-1,

and the balance identity (

) holds. In particular,

0\leq\mathscr{I}\leq n(n-1).

Proof.

The bounds follow from Proposition

. The balance identity is equality of two ways
of summing the off–diagonal entries of

.
∎

3.3
Influence–Based Centralities on the Broadcasting Graph

To quantify the influence potential of each node, we now define a new weighted, directed graph, the broadcasting graph, where edge weights represent the steady-state influence between nodes when each acts as a source.

Broadcasting graph.

Define the
broadcasting graph

G^{\infty}=(V,E,w^{\infty})
by assigning to each directed edge

(i,j)\in E
the weight

w^{\infty}_{ij}:=\mathscr{U}^{\infty}_{i,j}\in[0,1],\qquad(i,j)\in E,

(30)

and setting
w^{\infty}_{ij}=0
when
(i,j)\notin E
. Let
W^{\infty}:=(w^{\infty}_{ij})_{i,j\in V}
.
Also let
N^{+}(i):=\{j\in V:(i,j)\in E\}
denote the out–neighborhood of
i
.

Definition 3.22

(Opinion broadcasting degree centrality)
.

For each vertex
i\in V
, define the
opinion broadcasting degree centrality
by

c_{\mathrm{obdeg}}(i):=\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{j\in N^{+}(i)}\mathscr{U}^{\infty}_{i,j}.

(31)

Definition 3.23

(Opinion broadcasting closeness centrality)
.

Let
R^{+}(i)
be as in Proposition
3.18
. Define

\nu:=\min\{\,\mathscr{U}^{\infty}_{i,j}:\ i\neq j,\ d(i,j)<\infty\,\},

with
\nu:=0
if the set is empty. The
opinion broadcasting closeness centrality
of
i
is

c_{\mathrm{obclose}}(i):=\begin{cases}\dfrac{|R^{+}(i)|\,\nu}{\sum_{j\in R^{+}(i)}\mathscr{U}^{\infty}_{i,j}\,d(i,j)},&|R^{+}(i)|>0\ \text{and}\ \nu>0,\\[5.16663pt]
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(32)

Definition 3.24

(Opinion broadcasting betweenness centrality)
.

For every edge
(i,j)\in E
with
w^{\infty}_{ij}>0
define its
log–length
by

\ell^{\infty}_{ij}:=-\log w^{\infty}_{ij}\in[0,\infty),

(33)

and set
\ell^{\infty}_{ij}=+\infty
when
w^{\infty}_{ij}=0
.
For a directed path
p:i=i_{0}\to i_{1}\to\cdots\to i_{m}=j
let

L^{\infty}(p):=\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}\ell^{\infty}_{i_{r}i_{r+1}}
.
Define the induced directed
log–distance
on
G^{\infty}
by

d_{\log}^{\infty}(i,j):=\inf\{\,L^{\infty}(p)\,:\ p\text{ is a directed path from }i\text{ to }j\,\}.

(34)

For
a\neq b
let
\sigma_{ab}
be the number of directed
d_{\log}^{\infty}
–geodesics from
a
to
b
and

\sigma_{ab}(k)
the number of those geodesics passing through
k
as an interior vertex.
The
opinion broadcasting betweenness centrality
is

c_{\mathrm{obbet}}(k):=\frac{1}{(n-1)(n-2)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b\in V\\
a\neq b,\ a\neq k,\ b\neq k\end{subarray}}\frac{\sigma_{ab}(k)}{\sigma_{ab}},

(35)

with
\sigma_{ab}(k)/\sigma_{ab}:=0
when
\sigma_{ab}=0
.

Definition 3.25

(Opinion broadcasting eigenvector centrality)
.

For
\eta>0
, define the strictly positive matrix

W^{\infty}_{\eta}:=W^{\infty}+\eta\,\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top},

where
\mathbf{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
denotes the all–ones column vector (so that
\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}
is the all–ones matrix). This is a “teleportation / regularization” step, directly analogous to the one used in the classical definition of PageRank.
Let
x^{(\eta)}
be the Perron eigenvector of
W^{\infty}_{\eta}
:

W^{\infty}_{\eta}x^{(\eta)}=\lambda_{\max}^{(\eta)}x^{(\eta)},\qquad x^{(\eta)}\in\mathbb{R}^{V}_{>0}.

The
opinion broadcasting eigenvector centrality
is the normalized Perron vector

c_{\mathrm{obeig}}(i):=\frac{x^{(\eta)}_{i}}{\sum_{k\in V}x^{(\eta)}_{k}}.

(36)

(If
W^{\infty}
is irreducible, one may take
\eta=0
.)

Definition 3.26

(Opinion broadcasting PageRank)
.

Let
P^{\infty}
be the row–stochastic matrix obtained by normalizing outgoing weights of
W^{\infty}
:
for
i\in V
,

P^{\infty}_{ij}:=\begin{cases}\dfrac{w^{\infty}_{ij}}{\sum_{m\in V}w^{\infty}_{im}},&\sum_{m\in V}w^{\infty}_{im}>0,\\[5.16663pt]
\dfrac{1}{n},&\sum_{m\in V}w^{\infty}_{im}=0.\end{cases}

(37)

For
\alpha\in(0,1)
, the
opinion broadcasting PageRank
vector
\pi^{\infty}
is the unique probability
vector satisfying

(\pi^{\infty})^{\top}=\alpha(\pi^{\infty})^{\top}P^{\infty}+(1-\alpha)\frac{\mathbf{1}^{\top}}{n},

(38)

and we set

c_{\mathrm{obpr}}(i):=\pi^{\infty}_{i}.

(39)

Proposition 3.27

(Product characterization of
d_{\log}^{\infty}
)
.

For all
i,j\in V
,

d_{\log}^{\infty}(i,j)=-\log\Bigl(\sup_{p:i\leadsto j}\ \prod_{(u\to v)\in p}w^{\infty}_{uv}\Bigr),

where the supremum ranges over directed paths
p
from
i
to
j
(and equals
0
if no such path exists,
so that
d_{\log}^{\infty}(i,j)=+\infty
in that case).

Proof.

For any directed path
p
, by (
33
) we have

L^{\infty}(p)=-\log\bigl(\prod_{(u\to v)\in p}w^{\infty}_{uv}\bigr).

Taking the infimum over paths is equivalent to taking the supremum of the multiplicative path weights
and applying
-\log(\cdot)
.
∎

Proposition 3.28

(Normalization and basic bounds)
.

For all
i\in V
,

0\leq c_{\mathrm{obdeg}}(i)\leq\frac{\deg^{+}(i)}{n-1}\leq 1,\qquad 0\leq c_{\mathrm{obclose}}(i)\leq c_{\mathrm{close}}^{+}(i)\leq 1,

where
\deg^{+}(i):=|N^{+}(i)|
. Moreover,
c_{\mathrm{obeig}}(i)\geq 0
and
\sum_{i\in V}c_{\mathrm{obeig}}(i)=1

Broadcasting centralizations across Monte Carlo runs

Interpretation of Figure

The distributions are visibly nondegenerate, confirming that susceptibility heterogeneity induces genuine
run–to–run variability in graph–level influence concentration, even on a fixed connected topology. The
degree and PageRank broadcasting centralizations are comparatively concentrated, reflecting the relative
stability of local and random–walk–type measures under stochastic attenuation. In contrast, broadcasting
closeness (log–metric) and broadcasting betweenness (log–metric) show broader spreads, as expected from
path–dependent indices: small changes in susceptibility can substantially reweight effective shortest
routes (in the log–metric sense) and hence alter the concentration of mediated influence.

Eigenvector broadcasting centralization also shows substantial variability, which is consistent with the
global nature of spectral centrality: modest perturbations in the effective propagation matrix can shift the
mass of the Perron vector among medium–central nodes, thereby affecting graph–level concentration even
when the identity of the most central broadcasters remains stable on average.

4.5
Summary of the worked Monte Carlo illustration

Overall, the karate club simulations support two complementary conclusions. First, broadcasting
centralities behave as susceptibility–attenuated analogues of classical centralities on connected toy
networks: the network topology largely determines the identity of major broadcasters, while the random
susceptibility profile controls the scale and variability of influence propagation. Second, the
graph–level centralizations exhibit nontrivial dispersion across runs, indicating that heterogeneity in
susceptibility may act as a mechanism that stochastically amplifies or dampens influence inequality in a
population, even when the underlying social topology is fixed.

5
Conclusion

We developed a mathematically rigorous framework for Friedkin–Johnsen social influence on graphs by
formulating the dynamics as a discrete initial–boundary value problem and by introducing a family of
vertexwise and graphwise
influenceability
measures derived from characteristic times, steady–state
responses, and all–vertex scanning constructions.
Beyond existence and representation, we obtained quantitative rate, sensitivity, and stability results
that make the resolvent formulation a robust tool for analysis and computation.
In particular, the matrices

\mathscr{U}^{\infty}
,
\mathscr{T}
,
\mathscr{E}
, and
\mathscr{S}(\varepsilon)
provide a compact summary of
how influence propagates from any chosen source through the network, and they induce the broadcasting graph

G^{\infty}
on which influence–based centralities and centralizations can be defined consistently.

The Monte Carlo study on the Zachary karate club network illustrates how these broadcasting indices relate
to classical centralities on a connected benchmark graph. Across
R=100{,}000
random susceptibility draws,
the nodewise Monte Carlo means of broadcasting centralities remain strongly correlated with their standard
topological analogues (Table
1
), confirming that the proposed measures preserve the
dominant structural signatures of influence in a network. At the same time, rank–based comparisons and the
empirical distributions of broadcasting centralizations (Figure
4
)
show that susceptibility heterogeneity introduces systematic attenuation and nontrivial variability in
graph–level influence concentration, especially for path–dependent indices such as closeness and
betweenness under the log–metric.

Several directions are suggested by the present framework. First, the all–vertex scanning matrices admit
reception–based analogues and can be extended to directed and weakly connected graphs using directed
reachability distances and log–metric path weights. Second, the same constructions can be adapted to
weighted, multilayer, and time–varying networks, as well as to nonlinear influence dynamics, thereby
enabling a general theory of influenceability and influence centralization beyond the classical linear
setting.

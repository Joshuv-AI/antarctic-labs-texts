Real-World Upadacitinib Persistence and Clinical Outcomes Among Canadian Patients with Inflammatory Arthritis from a Patient Support Program: The UPRAISE Study
Authors: Janet E Pope, Jayeshkumar Patel, Christopher D Saffore, Siran Fang, Al Rasymas, Dalinda Liazoghli, Olga Ziouzina
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionRheumatoid arthritis (RA), psoriatic arthritis (PsA), and axial spondyloarthritis (axSpA) impact quality of life. Medication persistence is a key determinant of long-term effectiveness in chronic inflammatory diseases. This study evaluated treatment persistence, function, and program satisfaction among Canadian patients with RA, PsA, and axSpA receiving upadacitinib enrolled in the AbbVie Care patient support program (PSP).MethodsData were collected from adults with RA, PsA, or axSpA enrolled in the Canadian PSP between January 2020 and July 2025. Demographic characteristics, first prescription fill rates, medication persistence, Health Assessment Questionnaire Disability Index (HAQ-DI) scores, and patient satisfaction with the PSP (subset of RA patients) were assessed.ResultsA total of 16,075 patients enrolled in the PSP were assessed (RA: N = 11,449; PsA: N = 2751; axSpA N = 1875). Over 90% of all patients filled their first upadacitinib prescription within 7 days. Among patients with RA, medication persistence was 73%, 63%, 56%, and 51% at 12, 24, 36, and 48 months, respectively. In PsA, persistence was 68%, 54%, and 47% at 12, 24, and 36 months, and with axSpA, persistence was 63% and 50% at 12 and 24 months. Patients with RA had a mean reduction from baseline in HAQ-DI of − 0.40 (N = 777; SD: 0.77; P < .001) and most patients (62%) achieved minimal clinically important difference (MCID; improvement ≥ 0.22[RA] or ≥ 0.35[PsA]), with 28% having normative values (HAQ-DI ≤ 0.25). Among patients with PsA, the mean reduction from baseline in HAQ-DI was − 0.65 (N = 153; SD: 0.66; P < 0.001); 73% of patients achieved MCID and 26% had normative values. Among 200 RA patients surveyed, nearly 90% were extremely/very satisfied with the PSP.ConclusionsHigh first prescription fill and long-term persistence rates were observed in patients with RA, PsA, and axSpA enrolled in a single national PSP, along with substantial improvements in physical function.Keywords: Axial spondyloarthritis, Clinical outcomes, Medication persistence, Patient support program, Physical function (HAQ), Psoriatic arthritis, Real-world experience (RWE), Rheumatoid arthritis, Upadacitinib

Key Summary Points

Why carry out this study?Medication persistence is a recognized challenge in the management of chronic inflammatory diseases, and while patient support programs (PSPs) have demonstrated benefits for older advanced therapies in rheumatic conditions, data for newer agents like upadacitinib remain limited.This study examined persistence and outcomes of patients with rheumatoid arthritis (RA), psoriatic arthritis (PsA), and axial spondyloarthritis (axSpA) enrolled in the upadacitinib PSP in Canada.What was learned from this study?High medication persistence rates were observed across all diseases, with most patients enrolled in the PSP continuing upadacitinib treatment for 1 to 4 years.The majority of patients enrolled in the PSP reported being very or extremely satisfied with PSP services provided by the program.Patients with RA and PsA who participated in the PSP showed significant improvement in physical function, as demonstrated by the mean change from baseline in the Health Assessment Questionnaire-Disability Index.

Introduction

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA), psoriatic arthritis (PsA), and axial spondyloarthritis (axSpA) are chronic inflammatory diseases that substantially impair patient-reported function, work capacity, and health-related quality of life (HRQoL) [1, 2]. Optimal management relies not only on efficacious medications but also on persistence with prescribed therapy, as discontinuation is associated with poorer outcomes and escalation of care needs [3]. Medication persistence remains a challenge in real-world rheumatology practice, especially in chronic diseases requiring lifelong treatment.

Several advanced treatment options are available for patients with RA, PsA, and axSpA, including tumor necrosis factor (TNF) and interleukin (IL) inhibitors (e.g., IL-6, IL-17, etc.), but differ in administration, dosing schedule, and patient experience [1, 4, 5].

Upadacitinib, an oral Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitor, was approved as a first-line advanced therapy for the treatment of moderate-to-severe RA (December 2019) and PsA (June 2021) among Canadian patients with inadequate response or intolerance to methotrexate or other conventional synthetic disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs) [6–8]. Upadacitinib was also approved for the treatment of patients with moderate-to-severe axSpA (July 2022) and non-radiographic (nr)-axSpA (May 2023) with an inadequate response to biologic (b)DMARDs [9, 10].

Although substantial real-world evidence exists regarding medication persistence and long-term functional outcomes for established therapies, data remain scarce for newer agents such as upadacitinib. Much of the available real-world evidence is derived from patient support programs (PSP) such as AbbVie Care® that have been developed to assist patients with treatment access and adherence. Several PSP models have demonstrated improved adherence and reduced treatment discontinuation in patients receiving established advanced therapies like adalimumab; however, these are usually focused on older agents or limited to specific indications [11–24]. Results from the COMPANION studies showed that calls from AbbVie Care wellness case managers had a positive impact on medication adherence for adalimumab at 1 and 3 years and on clinical outcomes among patients enrolled in the PSP [20–23]. Similarly, a study of patients enrolled in the adalimumab PSP demonstrated that enrollment in the PSP was associated with greater adherence, lower rates of treatment discontinuation, and reduced risk of hospitalizations for up to 3 years of follow-up [24].

However, these findings highlight a significant gap in understanding the factors that influence sustained medication persistence, particularly in the context of novel treatments. To address this, the current study evaluated patient characteristics, medication persistence, and physical function among Canadian patients with RA, PsA, and axSpA and program satisfaction in patients with RA enrolled in the AbbVie Care upadacitinib PSP. By including a large cohort of patients with various inflammatory arthritis conditions, this study offers new insights into real-world persistence and treatment outcomes with upadacitinib, complementing efficacy data from clinical trials.

Methods

Study Design and Participants

The UPRAISE non-interventional observational study used patient-level data from adult patients diagnosed with RA, PsA, or axSpA collected through the Canadian upadacitinib AbbVie Care PSP between January 2020 and July 2025, reflecting Canadian approval dates for RA (December 2019), PsA (June 2021), axSpA (July 2022), and nr-axSpA (May 2023). This is consistent with FDA and EMA definitions of real-world data and real-world evidence when analyzed using fit‑for‑purpose scientific methods [25, 26]. Patients had a physician-confirmed diagnosis of RA, PsA, or axSpA, were prescribed upadacitinib, and consented to PSP enrollment and for their data to be used for research purposes. Patients enrolled in the program, but who did not start upadacitinib, were excluded. Patients were followed from the date they enrolled in the PSP to their most recently recorded follow-up visit, upadacitinib discontinuation date, or program disenrollment date, whichever occurred first. PSP services included financial coverage navigation, pharmacy coordination, and personalized support such as goal setting and care coach calls.

Outcomes

Demographic and clinical characteristics of patients enrolled in the PSP, including age, diagnosis, and treatment history, were recorded. Prior treatments included in this report were JAK inhibitors (tofacitinib and baricitinib), TNF inhibitors (adalimumab, certolizumab, etanercept, golimumab, and infliximab), IL-6 inhibitors (sarilumab and tocilizumab), IL-17 inhibitors (ixekizumab and secukinumab), and an IL-12/23 inhibitor (ustekinumab). Information on medication adherence, including prescription filling and refilling of upadacitinib, was collected directly from patients and/or physicians. First prescription fill and treatment persistence were derived from these self-reported data. The rate of first prescription fill was derived as the proportion of patients who filled their first upadacitinib prescription within 7 days of enrolling into the PSP among all patients enrolled in the PSP. There is currently no consensus on the optimal time window for defining primary adherence or first prescription fill in observational studies. Prior research has used a wide range of thresholds; for example, some studies have evaluated prescription fill rates using 30- or 90-day windows to define primary adherence. In the present study, we selected a 7-day cutoff as a more stringent and conservative measure, given the structured nature of the PSP and its goal of facilitating rapid treatment initiation. This shorter window provides a sensitive indicator of timely medication uptake within the PSP framework. Persistence was defined as the proportion of patients remaining on upadacitinib after initiation and was assessed up to 48 months post-treatment initiation among patients with RA, up to 36 months in patients with PsA, and up to 24 months in patients with axSpA. For context on patient experience, satisfaction with the PSP was measured in two surveys rounds of 100 RA patients each (June 2020, January 2021; before approval for PsA and axSpA), reporting on aspects of support and service. Physical function was assessed via the Health Assessment Questionnaire-Disability Index (HAQ-DI) for patients with RA and PsA [27]. In this study, HAQ-DI assessments were collected via telephone interviews at upadacitinib initiation and during voluntarily follow-up. Consequently, HAQ-DI scores were not available for all patients at all time points, and the most recent available score was used for each patient. Alternatively, results from HAQ-DI collected at 12 months ± 3 months after starting upadacitinib (9–15 months) are also reported. Minimal clinically important differences (MCIDs) for HAQ-DI were defined as ≥ 0.22- or ≥ 0.35-point reductions from baseline for patients with RA or PsA, respectively; normative value was defined as a score ≤ 0.25 [28–31].

Statistical Analysis of Data

Demographic and clinical characteristics were assessed descriptively, including rates of first prescription (re)fill, medication persistence, and patient satisfaction. Change from baseline (upadacitinib initiation) in HAQ-DI was assessed and compared using a paired t test across the entire RA or PsA cohort. Improvements in HAQ-DI and persistence rates were also stratified, within each cohort, by prior b/targeted synthetic (ts)DMARD treatment exposure (i.e., bio-naïve vs. b/tsDMARD-experienced).

Ethical Statement

This study was conducted in accordance with the ethical principles that have their origin in the current Declaration of Helsinki and was consistent with International Conference on Harmonization Good Clinical Practice, Good Epidemiology Practices, and applicable regulatory requirements. All participants provided consent to enroll in the upadacitinib AbbVie Care program and for their demographic and clinical information, as well as questionnaire answers, to be used for research purposes. None of the patients analyzed had their identity or medical records disclosed for the purposes of this study, and only anonymized patient-level data were accessed. Because no identifiable protected health information was extracted or accessed during the course of this study, no institutional review board review or approval was required [32].

Results

Patient Demographics and Clinical Characteristics

Overall, 17,598 patients provided informed consent for inclusion in the PSP and were prescribed upadacitinib. A total of 16,075 patients (≥ 18 years old, known upadacitinib start date, not in discontinued-temporary therapy status, and, when applicable, a discontinuation date ≥ start date) were included in this analysis (11,449 patients with RA, 2751 patients with PsA, and 1875 patients with axSpA; Table 1). Among patients with RA, the median age was 62 years (interquartile range [IQR]: 52, 70), and 77% were women. Approximately one-third of patients (29%) were bio-naïve and 38% of patients had received ≥ 2 prior b/tsDMARD therapies. Over half of patients with RA (54%) had previously received treatment with an anti-TNF therapy, 21% had received a different JAK inhibitor, and 15% had received an IL-6 inhibitor. Of the 11,449 patients with RA, 777 patients (6.8%) had a HAQ-DI score both at baseline and during treatment, with a mean baseline score of 1.37 (SD: 0.76).

Patients with PsA were younger than patients with RA with a median age of 56 years (IQR: 46, 64) and 64% were women. One-fifth of patients with PsA were bio-naïve (20%) and over half (51%) had received ≥ 2 b/tsDMARD therapies. Approximately two-thirds of patients with PsA had previously received an anti-TNF therapy (60%), 38% had received an IL-17 inhibitor, and 11% had received a different JAK inhibitor. Among those with HAQ-DI score both at baseline and during treatment (N = 153; 5.6%), the mean HAQ-DI score at baseline was 1.46 (SD: 0.59).

Patients with axSpA were younger with a median age of 48 years (IQR: 39, 57) and the proportion of women/men was more balanced (58% women). The vast majority (93%) of patients with axSpA had used at least one b/tsDMARD. Most of them had used an anti-TNF therapy (83%) and 40% had prior IL-17 inhibitor exposure; only 2.7% had prior JAK inhibitor use. HAQ-DI scores were not collected/assessed among patients with axSpA due to a lack of clinical significance of the measure in this population.

First Prescription Fill and Medication Persistence Over Time

After enrolling in the PSP, a very high proportion of patients filled their upadacitinib prescription within 7 days (RA: 94%; PsA: 93%; axSpA: 91%) (Fig. 1).

First prescription fill rate among patients with rheumatoid arthritis, psoriatic arthritis, and axial spondyloarthritis within 7 days of enrollment in the upadacitinib patient support program. Denominator includes all patient enrolled in the patient support program for rheumatoid arthritis, psoriatic arthritis, or axial spondyloarthritis who provided informed consent and were prescribed upadacitinib, including patients who enrolled in the patient support program but did not initiate upadacitinib

The rates of medication persistence over time were assessed among patients with RA, PsA, and axSpA in the PSP (Fig. 2). Overall, among all patients with RA, rates of medication persistence were 73%, 63%, 56% at 12, 24, and 36 months, respectively, with 51% still using upadacitinib at 48 months. Similarly, among all patients with PsA, 68% and 54% of patients were still using upadacitinib at 12 and 24 months, respectively, and 47% at 36 months. The medication persistence rate was 63% and 50% among patients with axSpA at 12 and 24 months, respectively. In patients with axSpA, persistence was better in patients using upadacitinib as first-line advanced therapy (bio-naïve) than in b/tsDMARDs-experienced patients at 12 (74% vs. 62%) and 24 months (64% vs. 49%). However, these results should be interpreted with caution as only a small proportion of patients with axSpA were bio-naïve (6.3%).

Mean Change from Baseline in HAQ-DI

Patients enrolled in the PSP showed significant improvement in HAQ-DI scores from baseline to follow-up (Table 2). Among patients with RA who had available data (N = 777), the mean change in HAQ-DI was − 0.40 (SD: 0.77; P < 0.001) with a mean time between baseline and the most recent HAQ-DI value of 542.2 days (1.5 years). When further stratified by b/tsDMARD exposure, bio-naïve patients had greater improvements in HAQ-DI score than b/tsDMARD-experienced patients (− 0.50 [0.73] vs. − 0.35 [0.78]) even though both groups had significant improvements compared to baseline (P < 0.001). When only HAQ-DI values obtained between 9 and 15 months of follow-up were considered, higher mean (SD) changes from baseline were observed than when all HAQ-DI values were considered (all RA patients: − 0.54 [0.67]; bio-naïve: − 0.63 [0.64]; b/tsDMARD-experienced: − 0.50 [0.68]). Most patients with RA (62%) achieved MCID (i.e., ≥ 0.22-point reduction) in HAQ-DI at follow-up, with 67% of bio-naïve patients reaching that threshold (Fig. 3a). Only 12% of patients with RA recorded normative values (i.e., HAQ-DI ≤ 0.25) at the time of starting upadacitinib and this increased to 28% at follow-up, with 36% of bio-naïve patients achieving normative values (Fig. 3b).

When stratified by prior exposure to b/tsDMARD therapies, bio-naïve patients and b/tsDMARD-experienced patients had similar improvements (− 0.61 [0.77] vs. − 0.66 [0.64]). Discussion

This study provides a large, real-world evidence that high and sustained persistence with upadacitinib is achievable across RA, PsA, and axSpA. Over 90% of patients with RA, PsA, and axSpA enrolled in the AbbVie Care PSP initiated upadacitinib rapidly (within 7 days), and persistence rates remained high overtime: 73% at 12 months, 63% at 24 months, 56% at 36 months, and 51% at 48 months for RA; for PsA, 68% at 12 months, 54% at 24 months, and 47% at 36 months were persistent; and for axSpA, 63% at 12 months and 50% at 24 months continued therapy.

In axSpA, higher persistence was found in bio-naïve patients (64%) vs. b/tsDMARD-experienced patients (49%). Patients with RA and PsA demonstrated significant improvement in physical functioning irrespective of whether patients had previously used a different b/tsDMARD or not.

In this study, we showed high rates of primary adherence, with 94% of patients filling their first prescription within 7 days of PSP enrollment. This finding was markedly greater than that seen in previous studies of patients newly prescribed medications. A recent study conducted in the Canadian population using administrative claims data showed that 17% of prescriptions were never filled, all indications combined [33]. Similarly, an American study showed that, of more than 82,000 prescriptions for new medications, only 72% of these prescriptions were filled based on filled claims [34]. Kan et al. demonstrated using administrative claims data that approximately 37% of patients with RA newly prescribed methotrexate failed to initiate therapy within 2 months, and 41% prescribed biologics or tofacitinib failed to start therapy within 3 months [35]. It is important to note that, as opposed to our study, these data were not derived from patients in a PSP. Indeed, a study of patients with various inflammatory diseases receiving adalimumab treatment showed that PSP enrollment was associated with a decreased likelihood of medication abandonment [36]. Moreover, patients in the PSP were 36% more likely to have a subsequent medication refill than those not participating in a PSP [36].

Higher rates of medication persistence have been highlighted as a benefit of PSP participation [13, 37]. In this study, we also found that patients with RA enrolled in the PSP had persistence rates of 73% and 63% at 12 and 24 months, respectively. The COMPANION study, which assessed persistence among patients initiating adalimumab, showed that PSP enrollment, and especially calls received from care coaches (i.e., registered nurses), resulted in a greater likelihood of medication adherence over 3 years [20]. In the present study, all patients enrolled into the PSP received care coach calls. The persistence rates observed in this study in patients with RA enrolled in a PSP were generally higher than those reported in a similar population of patients in real-world, non-PSP settings. Indeed, an American retrospective study based on pharmacy claims reported upadacitinib medication persistence rates of 55% at 12 months as compared with 40% to 50% of patients receiving comparators (e.g., adalimumab, baricitinib, tofacitinib) [38]. Similarly, an Australian study using electronic medical records reported an upadacitinib persistence rate of 64% at 15 months [39]. However, another recent retrospective observational study using data from the national Services Australia Pharmaceuticals Benefits Scheme has reported an upadacitinib persistence rate (72%) closer to the persistence rates obtained in the current study [40].

In addition to the fact that our study collected data through a PSP, another reason why these persistence rates may be lower than those determined in this study is the data source; our study captured discontinuation data directly from patients and/or their providers, and thus these rates are more reliable than claims data. Other studies assessing medication persistence among patients participating in PSPs further highlight the benefit of such programs. In the PASSION study evaluating patients with RA using adalimumab, 75% of PSP users, versus 58% of non-users, remained on treatment after 78 Weeks [41]. Similarly, patients in an adalimumab PSP had consistently greater medication persistence compared with adalimumab-treated patients not enrolled in the PSP [13, 24].

The 12-month persistence rates were 68% and 63% for PsA and axSpA, respectively. Twelve-month persistence rates for various bDMARDs and data sources were reported by other groups and ranged from 42 to 69% in PsA and from 34 to 72% in axSpA [42–47]. One study using data from a registry reported combined 12-month persistence rates for 4 JAK inhibitors (tofacitinib, baricitinib, upadacitinib, and filgotinib) of 61% in PsA and 83% in axSpA [44]. However, direct comparisons are limited, as persistence can vary substantially between individual agents.

Aside from medication persistence, in other analyses, PSP enrollment was also associated with reduced risk of hospitalization [24] and lower all-cause and disease-related medical costs compared with those patients not participating in the PSP [13]. Medication persistence, especially among patients with chronic diseases, may also significantly impact therapeutic efficacy. Indeed, in a study of patients with axSpA receiving adalimumab, those receiving care coach calls as a part of their PSP enrollment were significantly more likely to achieve disease control within 6–18 months compared to those not receiving care coach support [23].

Several pharmaceutical companies have offered PSPs as a means of providing education and adherence support for patients (in addition to aiding in medication approval, bridging drug coverage and helping with copayments). PSPs may impact clinical, psychological, and economic outcomes for patients [13, 19]. There are very little data regarding assessment of HAQ-DI among patients participating in PSPs. However, real-world studies of b/tsDMARD-experienced patients have demonstrated that a poor score on HAQ-DI is associated with greater healthcare resource use and higher total medical costs, which suggests that improving functional status in patients with PsA may reduce the economic burden [48]. Moreover, improvements in physical functioning have also been shown to positively impact patient HRQoL and treatment satisfaction [31, 49]. In this study, the mean baseline HAQ-DI score was 1.39 (SD: 0.73), and over 60% of all patients with RA or PsA reported clinically meaningful improvement in HAQ-DI and more than a quarter of patients achieved normative values (i.e., HAQ-DI ≤ 0.25). The mean change from baseline, regardless of b/tsDMARD exposure status, was greater than the recognized MCID (i.e., ≥ 0.22-point decrease in RA; ≥ 0.35-point decrease in PsA) for all groups [28, 29].

A limitation of the current study is that it assessed only those patients enrolled in the AbbVie Care program; thus, we could not compare outcomes with patients not enrolled in the PSP. However, the PASSION study, which focused on patients with RA enrolled in the AbbVie Care program for adalimumab, also showed that patients enrolled in the PSP demonstrated greater improvements in HAQ-DI starting at Week 24 (− 0.47 vs. − 0.42) compared to non-users; differences between groups were also significant at Week 78 (− 0.53 vs. − 0.39, P = 0.011); this value is in line with the − 0.54-point improvement at 9–15 months observed among RA patients receiving upadacitinib in this study [41]. Likewise, greater proportions of patients using the PSP vs. non-users achieved MCID at Weeks 24 and 78 in the PASSIONS study [41]. In the COMPANION study, patients with axSpA or Crohn’s disease had a higher likelihood of achieving controlled disease (axSpA) or remission (Crohn’s disease) at 6–18 months [22, 23].

Strengths of this study include its ability to obtain data on a large sample of patients (> 16,000) with rheumatic conditions, including primary adherence and longer-term medication persistence. Data regarding treatment discontinuation were reported directly by the patient and/or physician and are therefore more reliable than data obtained from administrative claims data. Similarly, this study was able to obtain detailed information on all previous advanced lines of therapy patients received. Limitations of the study are that, given the nature of the study design, there was no comparison group for the HAQ-DI or persistence outcomes data. We acknowledge a possible selection bias as patients who enroll in PSPs may differ from those who do not, as participants are often more engaged in their disease and its management, which may contribute to higher rates of prescription initiation and longer treatment persistence. Another response/selection bias could be introduced by the use of voluntary telephone interviews to collect HAQ-DI data and a small proportion of patients with complete longitudinal data. No analysis of bias due to missing data was performed. Also, HAQ-DI assessments were not required for patients with axSpA and thus HAQ-DI outcomes were not assessed among patients with axSpA in this study. As this is an observational study, not all confounders could be adjusted for (e.g., disease severity, comorbidities, socioeconomic status, prior adherence, concomitant medications) and thus it is possible that bias may be introduced. The results reflect outcomes within the PSP framework and cannot establish causality for the program’s impact. The representation of the PSP cohort relative to the Canadian population is unknown and therefore generalization of these study results may not apply to broader RA, PsA and axSpA populations, both within and outside of Canada.

Conclusions

Our analysis provides a large real-world evaluation of persistence and treatment outcomes across multiple disease indications—RA, PsA, and axSpA—within a single PSP for the same therapy, offering complementary evidence to clinical trial efficacy data. High rates of first prescription fills in the first 7 days of PSP enrollment were observed and over half of patients persisted with upadacitinib treatment up to 24 months post-enrollment. The majority of patients were satisfied with services provided by the PSP as shown by a survey. Upadacitinib treatment improved function and showed reasonable persistence in RA, PsA, and axSpA.

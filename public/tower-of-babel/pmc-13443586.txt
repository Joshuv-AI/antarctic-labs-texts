Association between loneliness trajectories and chronic diseases as well as chronic comorbidities among middle - aged and elderly chinese: based on the group - based trajectory modeling method
Authors: Yihang Pang, Yongrong Jiang, Naiqian Sa, Ge Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study aims to investigate the associations between loneliness trajectories and chronic diseases and chronic comorbidities among middle aged and elderly Chinese individuals. This study included 8,935 participants aged ≥ 45 years from the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (2011–2020). Loneliness was assessed using a single item 4 point scale. Group based trajectory modeling (GBTM) was applied to identify loneliness trajectories, and logistic regression was used to examine their associations with chronic diseases and comorbidities. Three distinct loneliness trajectories were identified: low (45.55%), moderate (18.15%), and high (36.30%). In the core model, loneliness trajectories were significantly associated with hypertension, chronic pulmonary disease, heart disease, stroke, kidney disease, digestive diseases, mental and neurological disorders, memory-related diseases, arthritis/rheumatism, and asthma. For chronic comorbidities, compared with the high loneliness group, the moderate and low loneliness groups showed significantly lower risks with OR (95%CI) of 0.74 (0.65–0.83) and 0.58 (0.53–0.64), respectively. Results from the extended model were similar. Loneliness trajectories are significantly associated with multiple chronic diseases and chronic comorbidities in middle aged and elderly Chinese adults. These findings highlight the important role of loneliness as a psychosocial factor in chronic disease burden and support its integration into public health strategies for healthy aging. Introduction

As the country with the largest elderly population in the world, China is projected to have 400 million residents aged 65 years and older by 2050, thereby facing substantial challenges associated with population aging1.Chronic diseases are the leading causes of death and disability among middle aged and elderly populations worldwide, imposing an unsustainable burden on health care systems across the globe2.An epidemiological study found that more than 75.8% of Chinese residents aged 60 and above suffer from at least one chronic disease3.Chronic diseases have emerged as a major global health challenge. The negative consequences of poor health, disability, and death impose an increasingly heavy economic burden, particularly in low and middle income countries4,5.

With the development of the economy and society, loneliness has become more prevalent among middle aged and elderly populations. Loneliness is defined as a subjective feeling of lacking adequate social relationships in terms of either quantity or quality6.Loneliness is a major social risk factor for many adverse physical and mental health outcomes7,8.Previous studies have demonstrated the associations between loneliness and diseases in adults, such as diabetes, pain, and cardiovascular diseases9–11.However, relatively few studies have taken into account the impact of longitudinal changes in loneliness on the diseases of middle aged and elderly people. A cohort study from China has shown a significant association between loneliness trajectories and the risk of developing chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)12.

Based on this, we conducted an analysis using the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study database (CHARLS). Our objective was to examine the associations between baseline loneliness, different longitudinal loneliness trajectory patterns, multiple chronic diseases, and the chronic comorbidity model. This study will clarify the long term impacts of persistent loneliness on physical health, provide reliable population level evidence for the role of psychosocial factors in the development of chronic diseases, and offer unique insights into clinical risk stratification, targeted psychological interventions, and the formulation of public health strategies for healthy aging in China.

Materials and methods

Data sources and study population

This study utilized data collected by CHARLS in 2011, 2013, 2015, and 2020. CHARLS is a nationally representative cohort study. Baseline data were collected in 2011 through a multi - stage sampling method. The study covered 28 provinces, 150 counties (districts), and 450 rural or urban communities in China. Follow up surveys were conducted in 2013, 2015, 2018, and 2020, aiming to comprehensively evaluate the health status of the elderly population in China and its related factors. This study defined the middle aged and elderly population as those aged 45 and above. This definition fully aligns with the target population design of the CHARLS and is also a common standard for epidemiological research on aging and chronic diseases both domestically and internationally13. This study was approved by the Biomedical Ethics Review Committee of Peking University (IRB00001052–11015), and all participants provided written informed consent14.Considering that the sample size was only 5,273 after including the participants in 2018, and there was an issue of insufficient fitting of the trajectory model, this analysis utilized data from four surveys conducted between 2011, 2013, 2015, and 2020, with the baseline survey in 2011 serving as the initial reference point. Eligible participants were required to have valid demographic information and at least three measurements of loneliness symptoms. The participant screening process is detailed in Fig. 1.

Definition of loneliness

The assessment of loneliness was derived from one item of the Center for Epidemiologic Studies Depression Scale (CES - D)15.A specific item in this scale was designed to quantify the frequency with which an individual experienced loneliness in the past week. The response options were “rarely or none (< 1 day)”, “some or a little (1–2 days)”, “occasionally (3–4 days)” and “most of the time (5–7 days)”, coded as 0–3 respectively. Higher scores corresponded to a high level of loneliness. This assessment has been used as a feasible and effective measure of loneliness16.

Assessment of chronic diseases

The outcome variables were chronic diseases and chronic comorbidities. In the 2011 CHARLS survey, data on these 14 chronic diseases were collected through self - reports of doctor - diagnosed conditions. Each participant was asked about their status (yes or no) regarding hypertension, dyslipidemia, diabetes, cancer, chronic pulmonary diseases, liver diseases, heart diseases, stroke, kidney diseases, digestive disorders, mental illnesses, memory - related diseases, arthritis, and asthma. Chronic comorbidity was defined as an individual having two or more chronic diseases17.

Assessment of covariates

Covariates reflecting the participants’ conditions were collected through questionnaires, including age, gender (Male and Female), BMI, Residence (Village and Urban), educational level (Illiterate, Primary school, Junior high school, and Senior high school and above), marital status [Be married and others (Married but not living with spouse temporarily for reasons such as work, Separated, Divorced, Widowed, and Never married)], and employment status (Be employed and Be unemployed). Blood biochemical data included White Blood Cell (WBC), Mean Corpuscular Volume (MCV), Blood Urea Nitrogen (BUN), Total Cholesterol (TC), Triglyceride (TG), High - Density Lipoprotein Cholesterol (HDL - C), Low - Density Lipoprotein Cholesterol (LDL - C), C - Reactive Protein (CRP), and Uric Acid (UA). The original missing proportions of each variable can be seen in Supplementary Fig. 4.

Statistical analysis

Group based trajectory models were employed to identify possible loneliness trajectories between 2011 and 2020. GBTM assumes that there are different development trajectories within the population and uses maximum likelihood estimation to identify clusters of individuals with similar development trajectories. It can simultaneously estimate the probabilities of multiple trajectories and then assign participants to groups based on the participants’ posterior probabilities of belonging to each latent trajectory. This is an appropriate method for describing loneliness trajectories using time based data. Since loneliness at a single time point is a continuous variable, a censored normal model was adopted. To determine the most appropriate number and shape of trajectories, we fitted the model with 1 to 5 groups of trajectories and tested the significance of the intercept, linear, and quadratic time terms. Model fit was determined by statistics such as the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC), Akaike Information Criterion (AIC), and the Consistent Akaike Information Criterion (CIC). The lower the absolute value, the better the fit. In addition, we used the Average Posterior Probability (AvePP) to examine the consistency between the fitted model and the data. An AvePP of not less than 0.7 for each trajectory group was considered an indication of a good fit18,19.

Binary logistic regression models were used to explore the associations between loneliness trajectories and chronic diseases as well as chronic comorbidities. Linear regression analysis was performed to examine the association between loneliness trajectories and the number of chronic diseases. Three models were constructed to evaluate the stability of the results. Model 1 was unadjusted. Model 2, defined as the core model in this study, was adjusted for residence, education level, marital status, gender, age, BMI, and employment status. Model 3, regarded as the extended model, was further adjusted for WBC, MCV, BUN, TC, TG, HDL-C, LDL-C, CRP, and UA. To investigate the associations between loneliness trajectories and chronic diseases in different subgroups, we conducted subgroup analyses in terms of Residence, Education, Martial status, Gender, Age, BMI, and Employment status. To demonstrate the stability of the results, we carried out multiple sensitivity analyses, mainly including: (1) including participants with complete loneliness scores for four times; (2) using the inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) model to balance the differences in covariates among different trajectory groups; (3) using the chronic disease outcomes in 2020 to explore the association between Loneliness trajectories and chronic diseases. In the IPTW model, the generalized boosted model (GBM) in the “twang” package of R software was used to estimate the propensity score (PS) for each subject20,21.This model is based on a non - parametric machine learning approach. Through multiple iterations, it achieves optimal balance between treatment groups, thus achieving the effect of randomization. The standardized mean difference (SMD) was used to evaluate the inter group balance of all covariates. A smaller SMD indicates less inter group variation in covariates, and an SMD < 0.1 suggests better balance22. Furthermore, this study is an observational cohort study, and the writing and reporting strictly adhered to the STROBE guidelines. All statistical analysis were performed using R (version 4.4.1). The GBTM model is implemented using the GBTM package.P values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant and every P value was two sided.

Results

Loneliness trajectories

The results of group based trajectory modeling (GBTM) showed that the absolute values of BIC, AIC, and CIC decreased as the number of trajectory groups increased. In addition, the average posterior probability (AvePP) of all trajectory groups was greater than 0.7. We further used the predicted probability of group membership (≥ 10%) as an auxiliary criterion for model selection. The results indicated that only the 3 trajectory model satisfied all criteria. Therefore, the 3 trajectory model was determined as the optimal fitting model, and detailed results are presented in Supplementary Table 1. Figure 2 shows three different loneliness trajectories, namely, low loneliness(n = 4070, 45.55%), moderate loneliness (n = 1622, 18.15%), and high loneliness (n = 3243, 36.30%). The low loneliness group is characterized by maintaining a low level of loneliness in all four follow ups, with an average loneliness score of less than 1.2. The moderate loneliness group has a loneliness level higher than that of the low loneliness group in all four follow ups, with an average loneliness score between 1.2 and 1.6.The high loneliness group has a loneliness level higher than 2 in all four follow ups.

Baseline characteristics

A total of 8,935 participants were included in the final analysis. Of these, 36.30% were classified into the high loneliness group, 18.15% into the moderate loneliness group, and 45.55% into the persistent low loneliness group. We compared the distribution of covariates across the three loneliness trajectory groups. Compared with the high and moderate loneliness groups, participants in the low loneliness group had higher BMI, a higher proportion of urban residence (9.3%), higher educational level, higher marriage rate (92.7%), larger proportion of males (52.7%), lower proportion of employment (55.8%), lower HDL-C level, and higher UA level. In addition, the prevalence of chronic lung diseases, heart diseases, stroke, kidney diseases, stomach or other digestive diseases, emotional, nervous, or psychiatric disorders, arthritis or rheumatism, and asthma was lower in the low loneliness group. The number of chronic diseases was also lower in this group. Detailed results are presented in Table 1.

The association between loneliness trajectories and chronic diseases

As shown in Table 2, three models were constructed to examine the associations. In the core model (Model 2), loneliness trajectories were significantly associated with hypertension, chronic lung diseases, heart diseases, stroke, kidney diseases, stomach or other digestive diseases, emotional, nervous, or psychiatric disorders, memory-related diseases, arthritis or rheumatism, and asthma. For hypertension, compared with the high loneliness group, the odds ratio (OR) with 95% confidence interval (CI) was 0.94 (0.81–1.08) for the moderate loneliness group and 0.82 (0.73–0.93) for the low loneliness group. For chronic lung diseases, the OR (95% CI) was 0.83 (0.69–1.01) for the moderate loneliness group and 0.71 (0.60–0.82) for the low loneliness group. For heart diseases, the OR (95% CI) was 0.76 (0.63–0.92) for the moderate loneliness group and 0.64 (0.55–0.75) for the low loneliness group. For stroke, the OR (95% CI) was 0.69 (0.45–1.04) for the moderate loneliness group and 0.54 (0.39–0.74) for the low loneliness group. For kidney diseases, the OR (95% CI) was 0.71 (0.55–0.88) for the moderate loneliness group and 0.54 (0.44–0.65) for the low loneliness group. For stomach or other digestive diseases, the OR (95% CI) was 0.68 (0.59–0.79) for the moderate loneliness group and 0.59 (0.53–0.66) for the low loneliness group. For emotional, nervous, or psychiatric disorders, the OR (95% CI) was 0.41 (0.22–0.72) for the moderate loneliness group and 0.57 (0.38–0.84) for the low loneliness group. For memory-related diseases, the OR (95% CI) was 0.66 (0.37–1.10) for the moderate loneliness group and 0.49 (0.32–0.76) for the low loneliness group. For arthritis or rheumatism, the OR (95% CI) was 0.79 (0.70–0.90) for the moderate loneliness group and 0.61 (0.55–0.68) for the low loneliness group. For asthma, the OR (95% CI) was 0.76 (0.56–1.03) for the moderate loneliness group and 0.64 (0.50–0.82) for the low loneliness group. However, no significant associations were observed between loneliness trajectories and dyslipidemia, diabetes, cancer, or liver disease. Consistent findings were also observed in the extended model (Model 3).

The association between loneliness trajectories and the number of chronic diseases

As shown in Supplementary Table 4, linear regression analysis was performed to examine the association between loneliness trajectories and the number of chronic diseases. In the core model, loneliness trajectories were significantly associated with the number of chronic diseases. Compared with the high loneliness group, the β (95% CI) was − 0.27 (− 0.35, − 0.19) for the moderate loneliness group and − 0.43 (− 0.50, − 0.37) for the low loneliness group. Logistic regression analysis was used to explore the associations of loneliness trajectories with the presence of chronic diseases and multiple chronic comorbidities. For the presence of one or more chronic diseases, compared with the high loneliness group, the OR (95% CI) was 0.76 (0.66–0.87) for the moderate loneliness group and 0.61 (0.55–0.68) for the low loneliness group. For the presence of two or more chronic diseases, the OR (95% CI) was 0.74 (0.65–0.84) for the moderate loneliness group and 0.58 (0.53–0.64) for the low loneliness group. For the presence of three or more chronic diseases, the OR (95% CI) was 0.67 (0.58–0.78) for the moderate loneliness group and 0.53 (0.47–0.60) for the low loneliness group. Consistent results were also observed in the extended model (Model 3). Detailed findings are presented in Table 3.

Exploratory analysis of the association between loneliness trajectory and chronic diseases

To explore the associations between loneliness trajectories and chronic diseases across different subgroups, we performed subgroup analyses stratified by residence, education, marital status, gender, age, BMI, and employment status, with adjustment for all other covariates. For the presence of one or more chronic diseases, significant interactions were observed between loneliness trajectories and age as well as BMI subgroups. In participants aged ≥ 60 years, compared with the high loneliness group, the OR (95% CI) was 0.86 (0.70–1.07) for the moderate loneliness group and 0.70 (0.59–0.83) for the low loneliness group, with a P interaction value of 0.022. In the subgroup with BMI < 18.5 kg/m², the OR (95% CI) was 0.74 (0.43–1.30) for the moderate loneliness group and 0.53 (0.36–0.80) for the low loneliness group. In participants with BMI ≥ 24 kg/m², the OR (95% CI) was 0.87 (0.69–1.10) for the moderate loneliness group and 0.72 (0.60–0.86) for the low loneliness group, with a P interaction value of 0.014. For the presence of two or more chronic diseases, a significant interaction was found between loneliness trajectories and age (P interaction = 0.007). For the presence of three or more chronic diseases, loneliness trajectories also showed a significant interaction with age (P interaction = 0.010). Detailed results are illustrated in Fig. 3.

Sensitivity analysis of the association between loneliness trajectories and chronic diseases

To verify the stability of the association between loneliness trajectories and chronic diseases, we performed several sensitivity analyses. First, we excluded participants with incomplete loneliness data across all four waves. A total of 8,423 participants with complete loneliness measurements were retained, and group based trajectory modeling was conducted to identify their loneliness trajectories, as shown in Supplementary Fig. 1 and Supplementary Table 2. In the fully adjusted Model 3, significant associations between loneliness trajectories and chronic diseases remained, and the results are presented in Supplementary Table 5. Second, given the significant baseline imbalance across trajectory groups, we employed an IPTW model to balance the distribution of covariates. The standardized mean difference (SMD) plots before and after weighting are displayed in Supplementary Fig. 2. In the core Model 2, loneliness trajectories were still significantly associated with chronic diseases, as shown in Supplementary Table 6. Finally, we defined chronic disease status in 2020 as the outcome variable. A total of 7,439 participants were included in this analysis, and the corresponding loneliness trajectory model is shown in Supplementary Fig. 3 and Supplementary Table 3. In the core Model 2, significant associations between loneliness trajectories and chronic diseases were observed, and consistent findings were obtained in the extended Model 3, as presented in Supplementary Table 7. Overall, the results of all sensitivity analyses were consistent with those of the main analysis.

Disscussion

Using a representative sample, a 9 year follow up survey was conducted on Chinese adults aged 45 and above. Three different loneliness trajectories were identified: Low loneliness, moderate loneliness, and high loneliness. Loneliness trajectories were significantly associated with hypertension, chronic lung diseases, heart problems, stroke, kidney disease, stomach or other digestive diseases, emotional, nervous, or psychiatric problems, memory - related diseases, arthritis or rheumatism, and asthma. Compared with the high loneliness trajectory, both the low loneliness and moderate loneliness groups were significantly associated with chronic comorbidities in middle aged and elderly people. This study is the first to explore the relationship between loneliness trajectories and the risk of chronic comorbidities in middle aged and elderly people, highlighting the unique role of psychosocial factors in the management of chronic diseases in this population.

The formation of different trajectories is the result of the combined effects of multiple factors such as social support, health status, psychological adaptability, and the external environment, and there is significant group heterogeneity. The formation of the low loneliness trajectory benefits from a stable family and social support network, positive psychological adjustment ability, and a good health and economic foundation. These factors together construct multiple barriers against loneliness23; The other two groups, demonstrating moderately increasing or highly increasing levels of loneliness, may be trapped in a maladaptive regulatory cycle of succumbing to loneliness. Those with high loneliness are likely to experience the most severe form of maladaptation. Individuals showing a growing sense of loneliness may also exhibit certain characteristics or genetic vulnerabilities24, These characteristics or genetic vulnerabilities tend to make them overly sensitive to social information, perceiving it as a threat. This sets up a self reinforcing cycle that leads to persistent loneliness25. After adjusting for all covariates, a significant association was found between loneliness trajectories and multiple chronic diseases. Moreover, both the group with low loneliness and the group with moderate loneliness were significantly associated with chronic comorbidities. This study suggests that in the middle aged and elderly population, special attention should be paid to those in the moderate to high trajectory groups, and more social support should be provided to them.

The significant association between loneliness trajectories and chronic comorbidities can be explained as follows. On one hand, long term high level loneliness acts as a chronic psychological stressor, constantly activating the hypothalamic pituitary adrenal axis, which leads to the excessive secretion of stress hormones such as cortisol26, The continuously elevated cortisol suppresses the activity of immune cells in the body, reduces the levels of anti - inflammatory factors, promotes the release of pro - inflammatory factors, disrupts the balance of the immune system, and thus triggers a chronic low grade inflammatory response27; This chronic inflammation is not only the core pathological basis for the occurrence and development of various chronic diseases such as diabetes, hypertension, and cardiovascular diseases. It also accelerates vascular endothelial damage, metabolic disorders, and organ function decline, ultimately increasing the risk of chronic comorbidities. On the other hand, high loneliness trajectories are often accompanied by social isolation and psychological estrangement. People in this category are more likely to engage in unhealthy behavior patterns such as smoking, excessive alcohol consumption, lack of exercise, and irregular eating habits28, Meanwhile, they have lower compliance in aspects such as disease prevention screening and treatment following medical advice, which further increases the probability of multiple chronic diseases co - existing. Conversely, the positive psychological state and stable social support corresponding to the low loneliness or moderate loneliness trajectories can not only alleviate stress responses and maintain immune homeostasis, but also positively guide the formation and maintenance of healthy behaviors, thus playing a protective role against chronic comorbidities29.

In the subgroup analysis, we found that for one, two, or three or more chronic diseases, there were significant interactions between loneliness trajectories and the age subgroup. Compared with the high loneliness trajectory group, both the low loneliness group and the moderate loneliness group could significantly reduce the risk of chronic comorbidities, and this protective effect was more prominent in people under 60 years old. The potential mechanisms for this result may be as follows. On one hand, compared with people aged 60 and above, the physiological functions of adults under 60 are still in a relatively active state. The body has a stronger buffering and repair capacity against psychological stress. The positive psychological state brought about by low loneliness or moderate loneliness can more effectively inhibit chronic stress related neuro-endocrine disorders, reducing the risk of inflammatory factor release and metabolic abnormalities30, Consequently, it reduces the probability of chronic comorbidities. On the other hand, for people aged 60 and above, they often face multiple issues such as physiological decline, an increased number of chronic diseases, and a shrinking social support network. The impact of loneliness on health is easily diluted or masked by these confounding factors. Therefore, the protective effects of low or moderate loneliness trajectories are relatively weakened31.

The innovation of this study lies in using large scale, nationally representative data to investigate, for the first time, the relationships between loneliness trajectories, the risks of 14 chronic diseases, and chronic comorbidities. Additionally, the stability of the results was demonstrated through numerous sensitivity analyses. The study suggests that more social support should be provided to middle aged and elderly people with moderate to high levels of loneliness. In addition, by exploring the association between chronic diseases in 2011 and loneliness trajectories, we aim to better predict the future development of loneliness among people with chronic diseases, providing a public health reference for potential changes in loneliness.However, this study has several limitations. First, the diagnosis of chronic diseases and covariates relied on self reported data, which may lead to recall bias. Some participants may have undiagnosed or subclinical diseases, affecting the accuracy of our research results. Future studies should incorporate objective diagnostic measures, including biomarkers and clinical evaluations, to improve reliability. Second, as the study population was mainly from China, the results may not be fully generalizable to populations with different demographic and epidemiological characteristics. Further validation in diverse populations is necessary. In this study, the use of a single item measure of loneliness is applicable to long term trajectory classification at the population level and public health screening, rather than for precise psychological diagnosis at the clinical or individual level. As such, the research findings may have certain limitations when it comes to interpretation at the individual level. In addition, the survey in 2020 was affected by the COVID − 19 pandemic, which might have introduced potential biases in self reported chronic diseases. Although we minimized this impact by focusing on long term trends. Finally, due to the limitations of the survey based research, this study did not explore the longitudinal association between loneliness trajectories and chronic comorbidities. There may be a reverse causality or bidirectional interaction between loneliness and chronic diseases. Loneliness may influence health behaviors and physiological functions, while chronic diseases could lead to social withdrawal and exacerbate loneliness. This study cannot distinguish the direction of influence. Future research should use more rigorous longitudinal data to explore the risks between loneliness trajectories and chronic comorbidities.

Conclusion

In this large scale national survey study, loneliness trajectories were found to have a significant association with multiple chronic diseases and chronic comorbidities. These findings suggest that loneliness could serve as a powerful marker for chronic diseases, which can be used for the risk stratification of chronic diseases in the elderly population.

Existence of Non-Decaying Solutions to the Generalized Surface Quasi-Geostrophic Equations
Zachary Radke

Abstract
. We establish the short-time existence and uniqueness of non-decaying solutions to the generalized Surface Quasi-Geostrophic equations in Hölder-Zygmund spaces C r ​ ( ℝ 2 ) C^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2}) for r > 1 r>1 and uniformly local Sobolev spaces H u ​ l s ​ ( ℝ 2 ) H_{ul}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2}) for s > 2 s>2 .



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

We study non-decaying solutions of the generalized surface quasi-geostrophic equations. This model arises as an interpolation between the 2D Euler and surface quasi-geostrophic equations. The 2D Euler equations are

(
E
)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla p=0\hskip 14.22636pt&\text{in }[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
\text{div }u=0\hskip 14.22636pt&\text{in }[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
u|_{t=0}=u^{0}\hskip 14.22636pt&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{2},\end{cases}

where
u:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the velocity and
p:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is the scalar pressure. In vorticity form the 2D Euler equations are

(
E_{\omega}
)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\omega+u\cdot\nabla\omega=0\hskip 14.22636pt&\text{in }[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
u=\mathcal{K}\ast\omega=\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta)^{-1}\omega\hskip 14.22636pt&\text{in }[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
\omega|_{t=0}=\omega^{0}\hskip 14.22636pt&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{2},\end{cases}

where
\omega=\partial_{1}u_{2}-\partial_{2}u_{1}
. The surface quasi-geostrophic equations can be written as

(
SQG
)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\theta+u\cdot\nabla\theta=0\hskip 14.22636pt&\text{in }[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
u=\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta)^{-1/2}\theta\hskip 14.22636pt&\text{in }[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
\theta|_{t=0}=\theta^{0}\hskip 14.22636pt&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{2}.\end{cases}

We see that (

E
ω

) and (

⁢
S
Q
G

) are very similar in that the scalar quantity of interest is being transported by a divergence-free vector field, but differ in that the velocity
u
in
(

E
ω

)
gains one more spatial degree of regularity than
\omega
. This is in contrast to (

⁢
S
Q
G

) where the velocity and scalar have roughly the same spatial regularity.

In this paper we interpolate between
(

E
ω

)
and
(

⁢
S
Q
G

)
by defining the velocity as
u=\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta)^{-1+\beta/2}
for
\beta\in(0,1)
. Here
(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}
is the fractional Laplacian which is defined as

(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}f(x)=C_{d,s}PV\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{|x-y|^{d+s}}dy,

for
s>0
and
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. This gives the generalized SQG equation

(
gSQG_{\beta}
)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\theta+u\cdot\nabla\theta=0\hskip 14.22636pt&\text{in }[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
u=\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta)^{-1+\beta/2}\theta\hskip 14.22636pt&\text{in }[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
\theta|_{t=0}=\theta^{0}\hskip 14.22636pt&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{2}.\end{cases}

Note that when
\beta=0
we recover (

E
ω

) and when
\beta=1
we recover (

⁢
S
Q
G

). We also see that we can write the velocity as

u=\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta)^{-1}(-\Delta)^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\theta=\mathcal{R}^{\perp}(-\Delta)^{\frac{\beta-1}{2}}\theta,

where
\mathcal{R}
is the Riesz transform defined as

\mathcal{R}f=\nabla(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}f.

Moreover, one can write
u=\nabla^{\perp}(\Phi_{\beta}\ast\theta)
where
\Phi_{\beta}
is the fundamental solution of
(-\Delta)^{1-\frac{\beta}{2}}
defined as

\Phi_{\beta}(x)=\frac{C_{\beta}}{\mid\!x\!\mid^{\beta}}.

In a rough sense, we see that the velocity
u
in (

⁢
g
S
Q

G
β

) gains
1-\beta
degrees of spatial regularity more than the transported quantity
\theta

1.3.
Known Results

Global existence of smooth solutions to (

⁢
S
Q
G

) is a very challenging open question. Since this question is so difficult, one idea was to regularize the velocity field in some manner. One of the earliest papers to take this approach is
[
3
]
, where the authors study a logarithmic generalization of the velocity given by

u=\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta)^{-1}(\log(1+\log(1-\Delta)))^{\beta}\theta,

which is more singular than the velocity in (

L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}\cap B_{q,\infty}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Furthermore, they provide a blow-up criterion. In particular, they assume for
\beta\in[0,1],\theta_{0}\in C^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
\sigma>1
,
q>1
, that if
\theta
satisfies, for
T>0
:

\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\theta(\cdot,t)\right\rVert_{C^{\beta}}dt<\infty,

then
\theta
remains in
C^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
on the time interval
[0,T]
.

It is also natural to ask what happens the velocity
u
is more singular than the scalar
\theta
. This problem is investigated in
[
3
]
where the authors study the generalization of the velocity field given by
u=\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1+\beta}{2}}
where
\beta\in(1,2)
. The authors establish local well-posedness for (

⁢
g
S
Q

G
β

) given
\theta_{0}\in H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
m\geq 4
an integer. Existence for global weak solutions on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}
is shown provided that
\theta
is mean zero, i.e.
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\theta dx=0
.

There has also been work done for the ill-posedness for (

⁢
g
S
Q

G
β

) when
\beta\in(1,2)
. Very recently, in
[
6

, Córdoba and Martínez-Zoroa establish strong ill-posedness in classical Hölder Spaces. In this setting, for

, the authors also construct solutions with initial data in
C^{k,\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
that instantaneously leave
C^{k,\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, but lie in some Sobolev space of sufficiently small order.

For the study of non-decaying solutions to fluid equations one of the earliest results can be found in
[
13
]
where Serfati studies solutions to (

E

) with initial velocity and vorticity in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and potentially lacking spatial decay. One of the main obstacles in this setting is that the constitutive law typically ceases to make sense. We thus adapt the constitutive law to hold in a weaker sense. This is often achieved by applying a cut-off function to the kernel
\mathcal{K}
to split the kernel into a near-field and far-field term. Then integrating the far-field term by parts yields a constitutive law which makes sense for functions without decay. Such an identity is often referred to as the Serfati identity
[
13

.

For more regular solutions than those considered in
[
13
]
, the authors in
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
establish global well posedness to the 2D Navier Stokes equations with initial velocity in

[
12
]
. In
[
15
]
Wu studies solutions to (

⁢
S
Q
G

) in Hölder spaces
C^{k,\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, but assumes some integrability condition on
\theta
so the constitutive law makes sense. In
[
1
]
the authors remove the integrability conditions and replace the constitutive law with a Serfati-type identity. In
[
1
]
, the authors establish well-posedness to both (

E

) and (

⁢
S
Q
G

) in Hölder-Zygmund and uniformly local Sobolev spaces
H_{ul}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
m\geq 3
an integer.

In this work, we expand upon
[
1
]
by establishing a similar result for (

⁢
g
S
Q

G
β

) in Hölder-Zygmund spaces
C^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and uniformly local Sobolev spaces
H^{s}_{ul}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
s>2
, where
s
is not necessarily an integer. Since fractional derivatives are nonlocal, working in

ODE Lemmas

We make extensive use of Grönwall’s Lemma which we state below.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
T>0
and
u,\alpha,\beta
be nonnegative, continuous functions on
[0,T]

\alpha

2.2.
Littlewood-Paley Theory

Before defining our function spaces, we begin with a brief overview of the Littlewood-Paley operators. We closely follow
[
5
]
.

It is well known that if
\mathcal{C}
is the annulus of center
0
, short radius
3/5
, and long radius
5/3
, then there exists two positive radial functions
\hat{\chi},\hat{\varphi}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{5/6}(0))
and
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathcal{C})
respectively such that, if for
j\in\mathbb{Z}
we set
\varphi_{j}(x)=2^{jd}\varphi(2^{j}x)
, then

\hat{\chi}+\sum_{j\geq 0}\hat{\varphi}_{j}=\hat{\chi}+\sum_{j\geq 0}\hat{\varphi}(2^{-j}\cdot)\equiv 1.

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}
we define
\chi_{n}
via its Fourier transform as

\hat{\chi}_{n}(\xi)=\hat{\chi}(\xi)+\sum_{j\leq n}\hat{\varphi}_{j}(\xi)\text{ for }\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

For
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we define the operator
S_{n}
as

S_{n}f=\chi_{n}\ast f.

Formally,
S_{n}
can be thought of as a projection in Fourier space into the ball of radius of order
2^{n}
. For
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we define the inhomogeneous Littlewood-Paley operators
\Delta_{j}
by

\Delta_{j}f=\begin{cases}0,\hskip 28.45274pt\text{if }j<-1\\
\chi\ast f,\hskip 28.45274pt\text{if }j=-1\\
\varphi_{j}\ast f,\hskip 28.45274pt\text{if }j\geq 0,\end{cases}

and for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
we define the homogeneous Littlewood-Paley operators
\dot{\Delta}_{j}
by

\dot{\Delta}_{j}f=\varphi_{j}\ast f.

One can formally think of
\dot{\Delta}_{j}
as a projection in Fourier space into the annulus of inner and outer radius of order
2^{j}
. For any
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have that
u=\lim_{n\to\infty}S_{n}u
where the limit holds in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
; equivalently

u=\sum_{j\geq-1}\Delta_{j}u\text{ in }\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

One benefit of using Littlewood-Paley operators can be seen in Bernstein’s Lemma. Heuristically, Bernstein’s Lemma states that if the support of
\hat{u}
is included in some ball
B_{\lambda}(0)
, then a derivative of
u
costs no more than
\lambda
. This will be made precise in Lemma
2.2
below. We let
\mathcal{C}_{a,b}(0)
denote the annulus with inner radius
a
and outer radius
b
.

Lemma 2.2
.

(Bernstein’s Lemma) Let
(r_{1},r_{2})
be a pair of strictly positive real numbers such that
r_{1}<r_{2}
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
, such that, for every integer
k
, every pair of real numbers
(a,b)
satisfying
b\geq a\geq 1
, and every function
u\in L^{a}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(1)

If
\supp{\hat{u}}\subset B_{r_{1}\lambda}(0)
then

\sup_{\mid\!\alpha\!\mid=k}\left\lVert\partial^{\alpha}u\right\rVert_{L^{b}}\leq C^{k}\lambda^{k+d(\frac{1}{a}-\frac{1}{b})}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{a}}.

(2)

Furthermore, if
\supp{\hat{u}}\subset\mathcal{C}_{r_{1}\lambda,r_{2}\lambda}(0)
then

C^{-k}\lambda^{k}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{a}}\leq\sup_{\mid\!\alpha\!\mid=k}\left\lVert\partial^{\alpha}u\right\rVert_{L^{a}}\leq C^{k}\lambda^{k}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{a}}.

2.3.
Function Spaces

We begin by defining the Bessel potential, allowing us to make sense of Sobolev spaces for non-integer valued exponents.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
. We define the Bessel potential of order
s
to be

J^{s}=(I-\Delta)^{s/2},

where

(I-\Delta)^{s/2}f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\widehat{G}^{s}\widehat{f})=G^{s}\ast f,

and

G^{s}(x)=C_{n}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(1+4\pi^{2}\mid\!\xi\!\mid^{2}\right)^{s/2}(x).

We have the following commutator estimate on the Bessel potential due to Kato and Ponce
[
10
]
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Suppose that
s>0
and
p\in(1,\infty)
. Then for
f,g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we have

\left\lVert J^{s}(fg)-fJ^{s}g\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert J^{s-1}g\right\rVert_{L^{p}}+\left\lVert J^{s}f\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}.

We remark that Proposition
2.4
holds for functions in spaces of which the Schwartz class is dense.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
. We define the Bessel Potential spaces
H_{p}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\dot{H}_{p}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to be

\displaystyle H_{p}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\left\lVert J^{s}f\right\rVert_{L^{p}}<\infty\},

\displaystyle\dot{H}_{p}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\left\lVert(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}f\right\rVert_{L^{p}}<\infty\},

equipped with the norms

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H_{p}^{s}}=\left\lVert J^{s}f\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\text{ and }\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\dot{H}_{p}^{s}}=\left\lVert(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}f\right\rVert_{L^{p}}.

For the case of
p=2
we define
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=H_{2}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\dot{H^{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\dot{H}_{2}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Remark 2.6
.

(Equivalent
H^{s}

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{s}}=\left(\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{2js}\left\lVert\Delta_{j}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

A proof of equivalence can be found in
[
5

Definition 2.10

Let

(2)

Proposition 2.11
.

The norms
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{W_{ul}^{s,2}}
and
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{H_{ul}^{s}}

Proof.

In view of (
1
) and (
2
), we must show that the
W^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
norms are equivalent. This is fairly standard so we only provide an outline of the proof. We begin by recalling that the fractional Laplacian for
(-\Delta)^{\sigma}u
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
for
\sigma\in(0,1)
is defined as

(-\Delta)^{\sigma}u(x)=C_{d,\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{\mid\!x-y\!\mid^{2+2\sigma}}dy.

Note by Plancherel’s theorem

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}u(-\Delta)^{\sigma}udx=\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{\sigma}}^{2}.

But we also see that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}u(-\Delta)^{\sigma}udx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}u(x)\left(C_{\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{\mid\!x-y\!\mid^{2+2\sigma}}dy\right)dx

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}u(y)\left(C_{\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{u(y)-u(x)}{\mid\!x-y\!\mid^{2+2\sigma}}dx\right)dy

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{\sigma}}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\mid\!u(x)-u(y)\!\mid^{2}}{\mid\!x-y\!\mid^{2+2\sigma}}dydx.

We then note

(3)

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{\sigma}}^{2}=\frac{C_{\sigma}}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\mid\!u(x)-u(y)\!\mid^{2}}{\mid\!x-y\!\mid^{2+2\sigma}}dydx=[u]_{W^{\sigma,2}}^{2}.

Observing that

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H^{m}}^{2}=\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla^{m}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(1+\mid\!\xi\!\mid^{2m})\mid\!\hat{u}(\xi)\!\mid^{2}d\xi,

and using (
3

\displaystyle\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{W^{s,2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{W^{m,2}}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla^{m}u\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{\sigma}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(1+\mid\!\xi\!\mid^{2m}+\mid\!\xi\!\mid^{2s})\mid\!\hat{f}(\xi)\!\mid^{2}d\xi.

Then we note that there exists two positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

C_{1}\leq\frac{1+\mid\!\xi\!\mid^{2m}+\mid\!\xi\!\mid^{2s}}{(1+\mid\!\xi\!\mid^{2})^{s}}\leq C_{2},

which shows that the two norms are equivalent.
∎

Definition 2.12
.

For
r\in\mathbb{R}
we define the Hölder-Zygmund space
C^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to be the set of tempered distributions

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{C^{r}}=\sup_{j\geq-1}2^{jr}\left\lVert\Delta_{j}f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}<\infty.

We also define the homogeneous Hölder-Zygmund space
\dot{C}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with norm

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\dot{C}^{r}}=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\lVert\dot{\Delta}_{j}f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}.

Definition 2.13

For
m\geq 0
an integer, we define the Hölder space

\tilde{C}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\{f:D^{\alpha}f\in\tilde{C}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\text{ for all }|\alpha|\leq m\text{ and }\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\tilde{C}^{m}}<\infty\},

where

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\tilde{C}^{m}}=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\left\lVert D^{\alpha}f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}.

It is important to note that here

\tilde{C}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\{f\in\tilde{C}^{\lfloor r\rfloor}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\tilde{C}^{r}}<\infty\},

where

(4)

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\tilde{C}^{r}}=\sum_{0\leq|\alpha|\leq\lfloor r\rfloor}\left\lVert D^{\alpha}f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\sum_{|\beta|=\lfloor r\rfloor}\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{\mid\!D^{\beta}f(x)-D^{\beta}f(y)\!\mid}{\mid\!x-y\!\mid^{r-\lfloor r\rfloor}}.

It is well known that when

(see for example

Theorem 2.14
.

(Sobolev Embedding) Suppose that
s>d/2
is a real number. For any

H_{ul}^{j+s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\tilde{C}^{j}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Proof.

Let

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\lVert\phi_{x}f\right\rVert_{\tilde{C}^{j}}\lesssim\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\lVert\phi_{x}f\right\rVert_{H^{j+s}}=\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{j+s}_{ul}}.

If
j
is not an integer, observe that

\displaystyle\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\tilde{C}^{j}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\lfloor j\rfloor}\left\lVert D^{\alpha}f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\sum_{|\beta|=\lfloor j\rfloor}\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{\mid\!D^{\beta}f(x)-D^{\beta}f(y)\!\mid}{\mid\!x-y\!\mid^{j-\lfloor j\rfloor}}

\displaystyle\leq 3\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\lfloor j\rfloor}\left\lVert D^{\alpha}f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\sum_{|\beta|=\lfloor j\rfloor}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{\sup_{y\in B_{1}(x)}\frac{\mid\!D^{\beta}f(x)-D^{\beta}f(y)\!\mid}{\mid\!x-y\!\mid^{j-\lfloor j\rfloor}}\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\lfloor j\rfloor}\left\lVert D^{\alpha}(\phi_{x,2}f)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\sum_{|\beta|=\lfloor j\rfloor}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{\sup_{y\in B_{1}(x)}\frac{\mid\!D^{\beta}(\phi_{x,2}f)(x)-D^{\beta}(\phi_{x,2}f)(y)\!\mid}{\mid\!x-y\!\mid^{j-\lfloor j\rfloor}}\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\lfloor j\rfloor}\left\lVert D^{\alpha}(\phi_{x,2}f)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\sum_{|\beta|=\lfloor j\rfloor}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{\mid\!D^{\beta}(\phi_{x,2}f)(x)-D^{\beta}(\phi_{x,2}f)(y)\!\mid}{\mid\!x-y\!\mid^{j-\lfloor j\rfloor}}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\lVert\phi_{x,2}f\right\rVert_{\tilde{C}^{j}}\lesssim\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\lVert\phi_{x}f\right\rVert_{\tilde{C}^{j}}.

In the first inequality we used that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{\mid\!D^{\beta}f(x)-D^{\beta}f(y)\!\mid}{\mid\!x-y\!\mid^{j-\lfloor j\rfloor}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{y\in B_{1}(x)}\frac{\mid\!D^{\beta}f(x)-D^{\beta}f(y)\!\mid}{\mid\!x-y\!\mid^{j-\lfloor j\rfloor}}+\sup_{\mid\!x-y\!\mid\geq 1}\frac{\mid\!D^{\beta}f(x)-D^{\beta}f(y)\!\mid}{\mid\!x-y\!\mid^{j-\lfloor j\rfloor}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{y\in B_{1}(x)}\frac{\mid\!D^{\beta}f(x)-D^{\beta}f(y)\!\mid}{\mid\!x-y\!\mid^{j-\lfloor j\rfloor}}+2\left\lVert D^{\beta}f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}.

If
j
is an integer we repeat the previous argument without the semi-norm estimate.
Thus we have shown
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\tilde{C^{j}}}\lesssim\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H_{ul}^{s}}
which concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma A.1
.

If
r>0
and

, then

\left\lVert\nabla^{\perp}(a\Phi_{\beta})\ast f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{C^{r}}.

Proof.

First, by the Leibniz rule we note

\left\lVert\nabla^{\perp}(a\Phi_{\beta})\right\rVert_{L^{1}}\lesssim\left\lVert\Phi_{\beta}\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\supp{a})}\left\lVert\nabla a\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\left\lVert\nabla\Phi_{\beta}\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\supp{a})}\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}.

Since

,
\Phi_{\beta}\in W^{1,1}(\supp{a})
. Applying Young’s convolution inequality we have that

\left\lVert\nabla^{\perp}(a\Phi_{\beta})\ast f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{C^{r}}.

∎

Lemma A.2

\displaystyle\left\lVert\dot{\Delta}_{j}\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta)^{-1+\frac{\beta}{2}}f\right\rVert_{L^{p}}

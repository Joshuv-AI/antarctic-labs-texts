Qualitative Estimates of Topological Entropy for Non-Monotone Contact Lax-Oleinik Semiflow Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author.
Wei Cheng, Jiahui Hong, Zhi-Xiang Zhu ∗

Abstract
. For the non-monotone Hamilton-Jacobi equations of contact type, the associated Lax-Oleinik semiflow ( T t , C ⁡ ( M ) ) (T_{t},C(M)) is expansive. In this paper, we provide qualitative estimates for both the lower and upper bounds of the topological entropy of the semiflow.

35F21,37L05,37B40,49L25

1.
Introduction

Let
M
be a compact and connected manifold without boundary. A function
H=H(x,p,u):T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is called a
Tonelli Hamiltonian of contact type
if it is of class
C^{2}
and satisfies the following conditions:

(H1)

H_{pp}(x,p,u)>0
,
\forall(x,p,u)\in T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}
;

(H2)

There exist a super-linear continuous function
\theta^{*}_{1}:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
and a constant
c_{0}\geqslant 0
such that

\displaystyle H(x,p,0)\geqslant\theta^{*}_{1}(|p|_{x})-c_{0},\qquad\forall(x,p)\in T^{*}M;

(H3)

There exists
\kappa>0
such that

\displaystyle|H_{u}(x,p,u)|\leqslant\kappa,\qquad\forall(x,p,u)\in T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}.

Consider the Hamilton-Jacobi equation of contact type:

(1)

\begin{cases}D_{t}u+H(x,Du(x),u(x))=0,&\quad t>0,x\in M,\\
u(0,x)=\phi(x),&\quad x\in M,\end{cases}

where
\phi\in C(M)
, the space of continuous functions on
M
. Using Herglotz’ variational principle (
[
4
,
3
]
) or the implicit variational principle (
[
13
]
), the (unique) solution of (
1
) has a representation formula. More precisely,

\displaystyle u(t,x)=T_{t}\phi(x),\quad(x\in M,t\geqslant 0).

The Lax-Oleinik operators
\{T_{t}\}
are defined as follows:

\displaystyle(T_{t}\phi)(x)=\inf_{\xi\in\mathcal{A}_{t,x}}\left\{\phi(\xi(0))+\int_{0}^{t}L(\xi(s),\dot{\xi}(s),u_{\xi}(s))\,ds\right\},

where
L:TM\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is the associated Tonelli Lagrangian of contact type defined by

\displaystyle L(x,v,u):=\sup_{p\in T^{*}_{x}M}\{p(v)-H(x,p,u)\},\qquad x\in M,v\in T_{x}M,u\in\mathbb{R},

\mathcal{A}_{t,x}
is the family of absolutely continuous arcs
\xi:[0,t]\to M
with
\xi(t)=x
, and
u_{\xi}:[0,t]\to\mathbb{R}
uniquely solves the Carathéodory equation:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{u}_{\xi}(s)=L(\xi(s),\dot{\xi}(s),u_{\xi}(s)),&\quad s\in[0,t],\\
u_{\xi}(0)=\phi(\xi(0)).&\end{cases}

For more details on the semigroup
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
, see Section
2.2
. A typical example of a contact-type Hamilton-Jacobi equation is the discounted equation with
H(x,p,u)=\lambda u+H(x,p)
, where
\lambda>0
.

To understand the dynamics of the semiflow
(T_{t},C(M))
generated by (
1
), especially when
\lambda<0
in the discounted equation or
H_{u}<0
in general, many authors have studied the dynamics in a deterministic way, such as through Lyapunov stability and periodicity (
[
8
,
14
]
). In the current paper, we will concentrate on certain statistical behaviors, specifically the topological entropy
h_{\rm top}(T_{t})
(see Section
2.1
for the definition) of the semiflow
\{T_{t}\}
. Recall that one can easily deduce
h_{\rm top}(T_{t})=0
for the classical Lax-Oleinik semigroup from the qualitative estimate of the Kolmogorov
\varepsilon
-entropy in the space
W^{1,1}(M)
, which was obtained in
[
2
,
1
]
.

When discussing the contact-type Lax-Oleinik semigroup, we contrast the case
h_{\rm top}(T_{t})=0
when
H_{u}>\lambda>0
, due to the contraction property of
\{T_{t}\}
, with the expansion case when
H_{u}<0
. In the latter case, we will primarily focus on giving a qualitative estimate of
h_{\rm top}(T_{t})
.

For the lower bound of
h_{\rm top}(T_{t})
, we have the following result: under the assumptions (H1)-(H3), if there exists
0<\lambda\leqslant\kappa
such that
\lambda\leqslant L_{u}(x,v,u)\leqslant\kappa
for all
(x,v,u)\in TM\times\mathbb{R}
, then
h_{\rm top}(T_{t})\geqslant\lambda
(see Theorem
3.1
). The estimate of the upper bound for
h_{\rm top}(T_{t})
is more difficult. We will focus on the discounted Hamiltonian in the form

\displaystyle H(x,p,u)=h(p)-\lambda u,\quad(x,p,u)\in T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R},

where
h=h(p):T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Tonelli Hamiltonian and
\lambda>0
is a constant. We prove that for such a Hamiltonian,
h_{\rm top}(T_{t})=\lambda
(Theorem
3.2
).

We remark that it is still an interesting problem whether the developed theory in infinite-dimensional dynamical systems (see, for instance,
[
9
,
11
,
10
,
7
]
) can be applied to study the expansive Lax-Oleinik semiflow
(T_{t},C(M))
for (
1
), with positive topological entropy, and its potential consequences on the complicated dynamical behavior.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Topological entropy

Let
(X,d)
be a complete metric space, and let
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
be a continuous semigroup action on
X
. That is,
T(t,x)=T_{t}(x):[0,+\infty)\times X\to X
is continuous,
T_{0}=\text{id}
, and
T_{s}\circ T_{t}=T_{s+t}
for any
t,s\geqslant 0
. For any
x\in X
,
t\geqslant 0
, and
\varepsilon>0
, we define the
dynamical ball
of center
x
with length
t
and radius
\varepsilon>0
as

\displaystyle B(x,t,\varepsilon)=\left\{y\in X\ \middle|\ d(T_{s}(y),T_{s}(x))<\varepsilon\ \text{for all}\ s\in[0,t]\right\}.

It is immediate that
B(x,0,\varepsilon)=B(x,\varepsilon)
. Let
K\subset X
be a compact subset. A set
E\subset X
is called a
(t,\varepsilon)
-generating set for
K
if

\displaystyle K\subset\bigcup_{x\in E}B(x,t,\varepsilon),

and
E\subset K
is a
(t,\varepsilon)
-separated set if the dynamical ball
B(x,t,\varepsilon)
for each
x\in E
contains no other element of
E
. For any compact subset
K
and
\varepsilon>0
, let
g_{t}(\varepsilon,K)
be the smallest cardinality of a
(t,\varepsilon)
-generating set of
K
, and
s_{t}(\varepsilon,K)
the largest cardinality of a
(t,\varepsilon)
-separated set
E\subset K
. Then, we define

\displaystyle g(K)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}g(K,\varepsilon)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log g_{t}(\varepsilon,K),

\displaystyle s(K)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}s(K,\varepsilon)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log s_{t}(\varepsilon,K).

It is known that
g(K)=s(K)
for any compact set
K\subset X
. We now define the topological entropy of the semigroup
\{T_{t}\}
as

\displaystyle h(T_{t})=h_{\rm top}(T_{t})=\sup_{K\subset X}g(K)=\sup_{K\subset X}s(K),

where the supremum is taken over all compact subsets
K
of
X
.

Recall a semigroup
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
is called
uniformly continuous
if for any
t>0
and
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle d(x,y)<\delta\Longrightarrow d(T_{s}(x),T_{s}(y))<\varepsilon\ \text{for every}\ s\in[0,t].

Proposition 2.1

(
[
12
]
)
.

If the semi-flow
\{T_{t}\}
is uniformly continuous then its topological entropy
h_{\rm top}(T_{t})
coincides with the topological entropy of its time-
\tau
map for any
\tau>0
.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
12
]
)
.

If the semigroup
\{T_{t}\}
is non-expansive, i.e.,

\displaystyle d(T_{t}(x),T_{t}(y))\leqslant d(x,y),\qquad\forall t\geqslant 0,x,y\in X,

then for any compact subset
K
of
X
,
g(K)=0
. In particular,
h_{\rm top}(T_{t})=0
.

In fact, the topological entropy
h_{\rm top}(T_{t})
is independent of the action of the semigroup
T_{t}
in finite time. More precisely, for any
x\in X
,
t\geqslant t_{0}>0
, and
\varepsilon>0
, we define the dynamical ball over time
[t_{0},t]
as

\displaystyle B_{t_{0}}(x,t,\varepsilon)=\left\{y\in X\ \middle|\ d(T_{s}(x),T_{s}(y))<\varepsilon\text{ for all }s\in[t_{0},t]\right\}.

For any compact subset
K\subset X
,
t\geqslant t_{0}>0
, and
\varepsilon>0
, let

\displaystyle g_{t_{0},t}(\varepsilon,K)=\min\left\{\#(E)\ \middle|\ E\subset X,\ \bigcup_{x\in E}B_{t_{0}}(x,t,\varepsilon)\supset K\right\}.

Proposition 2.2
.

(1)

For any compact subset
K\subset X
,
t\geqslant t_{0}>0
,
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\displaystyle g_{t_{0},t}(\varepsilon,K)\leqslant g_{t}(\varepsilon,K),\quad g_{t_{0}}(\varepsilon,K)\cdot g_{t_{0},t}(\varepsilon,K)\geqslant g_{t}(2\varepsilon,K).

(2)

For any compact subset
K\subset X
,
t_{0}>0
, we have

\displaystyle g(K)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log g_{t_{0},t}(\varepsilon,K).

Proof.

By definition, we know that

\displaystyle B(x,t,\varepsilon)\subset B_{t_{0}}(x,t,\varepsilon),\quad\forall x\in X,

which implies directly

\displaystyle g_{t_{0},t}(\varepsilon,K)\leqslant g_{t}(\varepsilon,K).

For the second inequality, we consider two finite covers of the compact set
K
:

\displaystyle\bigcup_{i=1}^{g_{t_{0}}(\varepsilon,K)}B(x_{i},t_{0},\varepsilon)\supset K,\quad\bigcup_{j=1}^{g_{t_{0},t}(\varepsilon,K)}B_{t_{0}}(y_{j},t,\varepsilon)\supset K.

If
B(x_{i},t_{0},\varepsilon)\cap B_{t_{0}}(y_{j},t,\varepsilon)\neq\varnothing
, we choose
z_{ij}\in B(x_{i},t_{0},\varepsilon)\cap B_{t_{0}}(y_{j},t,\varepsilon)
. It follows from the definition of the dynamical ball that

\displaystyle B(z_{ij},t,2\varepsilon)\supset B(x_{i},t_{0},\varepsilon)\cap B_{t_{0}}(y_{j},t,\varepsilon).

Thus, we have

\displaystyle g_{t}(2\varepsilon,K)\leqslant\#\{z_{ij}\}\leqslant g_{t_{0}}(\varepsilon,K)\cdot g_{t_{0},t}(\varepsilon,K).

Now we turn to prove statement (2). Using the result in (1), we obtain

\displaystyle g(K)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log g_{t}(2\varepsilon,K)

\displaystyle\leqslant\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\left(g_{t_{0}}(\varepsilon,K)\cdot g_{t_{0},t}(\varepsilon,K)\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log g_{t_{0},t}(\varepsilon,K)

\displaystyle\leqslant\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log g_{t}(\varepsilon,K)=g(K).

This completes the proof.
∎

2.2.
Herglotz’ variational principle

Let
M
be a compact and connected manifold without boundary and
H:T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
a Tonelli Hamiltonian of contact type. Its associated Tonelli Lagrangian of contact type
L=L(x,v,u):TM\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is of class
C^{2}
and satisfies the following conditions:

(L1)

L_{vv}(x,v,u)>0
,
\forall(x,v,u)\in TM\times\mathbb{R}
;

(L2)

There exist a super-linear continuous function
\theta_{0}:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
and a constant
c_{0}\geqslant 0
such that

\displaystyle L(x,v,0)\geqslant\theta_{0}(|v|_{x})-c_{0},\qquad\forall(x,v)\in TM;

(L3)

There exists
\kappa>0
such that

\displaystyle|L_{u}(x,v,u)|\leqslant\kappa,\qquad\forall(x,v,u)\in TM\times\mathbb{R}.

To introduce the Lax-Oleinik evolution of contact type, let us recall the relevant Herglotz’ variational principle. For any
x,y\in M
and
t>0
, let
\Gamma^{t}_{x,y}
be the set of absolutely continuous arcs
\xi:[0,t]\to M
connecting
x
to
y
, and
\mathcal{A}_{t,x}
the set of absolutely continuous arcs
\xi:[0,t]\to M
such that
\xi(t)=x
. We define the Lax-Oleinik evolution of contact type as a operator
T_{t}
,
t>0
, acting on a function
\phi:M\to\mathbb{R}
, as

\displaystyle(T_{t}\phi)(x)=\inf_{\xi\in\mathcal{A}_{t,x}}\Big\{\phi(\xi(0))+\int^{t}_{0}L(\xi(s),\dot{\xi}(s),u_{\xi}(s))\ ds\Big\},

where
u_{\xi}:[0,t]\to\mathbb{R}
uniquely solves the Carathéodory equation

(2)

\begin{cases}\dot{u}_{\xi}(s)=L(\xi(s),\dot{\xi}(s),u_{\xi}(s)),\qquad s\in[0,t],\\
u_{\xi}(0)=\phi(\xi(0)).\end{cases}

Let
C(M)
the space of continuous real-valued functions on
M
endowed with the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|=\max_{x\in M}|f(x)|.

The space
(C(M),\|\cdot\|)
is a complete metric space. We can conclude from the following Lemma
2.2
that

\displaystyle T_{t}:C(M)\to C(M),\qquad\phi\mapsto T_{t}\phi

is a continuous semigroup with
T_{0}=\text{id}
. For the proof, one can refer to
[
4
,
3
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

(1)

For any
\phi\in C(M)
,
t>0
and
x\in M
there exists
\xi\in\mathcal{A}_{t,x}
such that

\displaystyle T_{t}\phi(x)=\phi(\xi(0))+\int^{t}_{0}L(\xi(s),\dot{\xi}(s),u_{\xi}(s))\ ds=u_{\xi}(t),

where
u_{\xi}
is uniquely determined by (
2
).

(2)

For any
\phi\in C(M)
,
(t,x)\mapsto T_{t}\phi(x)
is a continuous real-valued function on
[0,+\infty)\times M
, and it is locally semiconcave on
(0,+\infty)\times M
.

(3)

T_{s}\circ T_{t}\phi=T_{s+t}\phi
for all
s,t\geqslant 0
and
\phi\in C(M)
.

(4)

We have

\displaystyle\|T_{t}\phi-T_{t}\psi\|\leqslant e^{\kappa t}\|\phi-\psi\|,\qquad\forall t\geqslant 0,\phi,\psi\in C(M).

In particular, if
L_{u}(x,v,u)\leqslant 0
for all
(x,v,u)\in TM\times\mathbb{R}
, then

\displaystyle\|T_{t}\phi-T_{t}\psi\|\leqslant\|\phi-\psi\|,\qquad\forall t\geqslant 0,\phi,\psi\in C(M).

3.
Qualitative estimate of topological entropy

3.1.
Lower bound

Now we can estimate the topological entropy
h_{\rm top}(T_{t})
. By the non-expansivity property of the semigroup
\{T_{t}\}
in the case
L_{u}\leqslant 0
, the following consequence can be easily obtained from Lemma
2.2
(4) and Lemma
2.1
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Under the assumption
(L1)-(L3)
, if

\displaystyle L_{u}(x,v,u)\leqslant 0,\qquad\forall(x,v,u)\in TM\times\mathbb{R},

then
h_{\rm top}(T_{t})=0
.

The following result provides a lower bound of
h_{\rm top}(T_{t})
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Under the assumptions
(L1)-(L3)
and that there exists
0<\lambda\leqslant\kappa
such that

\displaystyle\lambda\leqslant L_{u}(x,v,u)\leqslant\kappa,\qquad\forall(x,v,u)\in TM\times\mathbb{R}.

(1)

For any compact subset of the form
K=\left\{\phi=\phi_{0}+\delta\ \middle|\ \delta\in[0,a]\right\}\subset C(M)
, where
\phi_{0}\in C(M)
and
a>0
, it holds that
g(K)\geqslant\lambda
.

(2)

h_{\rm top}(T_{t})\geqslant\lambda
.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and
t>0
. Let
\phi_{m}=\phi_{0}+2me^{-\lambda t}\varepsilon
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
. We claim

(3)

\|T_{t}\phi_{m}-T_{t}\phi_{m-1}\|\geqslant 2\varepsilon,\qquad\forall m\in\mathbb{N}.

Given
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in M
, let
\xi\in\mathcal{A}_{t,x}
be a minimizer for
T_{t}\phi_{m}(x)
. That is

\displaystyle T_{t}\phi_{m}(x)=\phi_{m}(\xi(0))+\int^{t}_{0}L(\xi(s),\dot{\xi}(s),u_{\xi}(s))\ ds=u_{\xi}(t),

where
u_{\xi}:[0,t]\to\mathbb{R}
is determined by

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{u}_{\xi}=L(\xi,\dot{\xi},u_{\xi}),\\
u_{\xi}(0)=\phi_{k}(\xi(0))=\phi_{0}(\xi(0))+2me^{-\lambda t}\varepsilon.\end{cases}

On the other hand,

\displaystyle T_{t}\phi_{m-1}(x)\leqslant\phi_{m-1}(\xi(0))+\int^{t}_{0}L(\xi(s),\dot{\xi}(s),v_{\xi}(s))\ ds=v_{\xi}(t),

with
v_{\xi}:[0,t]\to\mathbb{R}
solving the equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{v}_{\xi}=L(\xi,\dot{\xi},v_{\xi}),\\
v_{\xi}(0)=\phi_{m-1}(\xi(0))=\phi_{0}(\xi(0))+2(m-1)e^{-\lambda t}\varepsilon.\end{cases}

Set
w_{\xi}=u_{\xi}-v_{\xi}
, then we have

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{w}_{\xi}=L(\xi,\dot{\xi},u_{\xi})-L(\xi,\dot{\xi},v_{\xi})=\widehat{L_{u}}\cdot w_{\xi},\\
w_{\xi}(0)=u_{\xi}(0)-v_{\xi}(0)=2e^{-\lambda t}\varepsilon,\end{cases}

where

\displaystyle\widehat{L_{u}}(s)=\int^{1}_{0}L_{u}(\xi(s),\dot{\xi}(s),u_{\xi}(s)+\theta(u_{\xi}(s)-v_{\xi}(s)))\ d\theta,\quad s\in[0,t].

It follows

\displaystyle w_{\xi}(t)=e^{\int^{t}_{0}\widehat{L_{u}}\ ds}\cdot 2e^{-\lambda t}\varepsilon\geqslant e^{\lambda t}\cdot 2e^{-\lambda t}\varepsilon=2\varepsilon,

and

\displaystyle T_{t}\phi_{m-1}(x)\leqslant v_{\xi}(t)=u_{\xi}(t)-w_{\xi}(t)\leqslant T_{t}\phi_{m}(x)-2\varepsilon,

which implies our claim (
3
).

Thus, for any
\phi\in C(M)
the dynamical ball
B(\phi,t,\varepsilon)
contains only a unique
\phi_{m}
with
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Then,

\displaystyle g_{t}(\varepsilon,K)\geqslant\#\{\phi_{m}\in K\}\geqslant\left\lfloor\frac{a}{2e^{\lambda t}\varepsilon}\right\rfloor+1\geqslant\frac{a}{2e^{\lambda t}\varepsilon}.

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle g(K)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log g_{t}(\varepsilon,K)\geqslant\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\frac{a}{2e^{\lambda t}\varepsilon}=\lambda,

and this completes the proof of (1).

Item (2) is immediate from the definition of
h_{\rm top}(T_{t})
together with (1).
∎

3.2.
Upper bound

Let
M
be a compact and connected manifold without boundary. A function
l=l(x,v):TM\to\mathbb{R}
is called a Tonelli Lagrangian if it is of class
C^{2}
and satisfies the following conditions:

(l1)

l_{vv}(x,v)>0
,
\forall(x,v)\in TM
;

(l2)

There exist a super-linear continuous function
\theta_{0}:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
and a constant
c_{0}\geqslant 0
such that

\displaystyle l(x,v)\geqslant\theta_{0}(|v|_{x})-c_{0},\qquad\forall(x,v)\in TM.

In this section, we will estimate the topological entropy
h_{\rm top}(T_{t})
in the case

\displaystyle L(x,v,u)=l(x,v)+\lambda u,\quad(x,v,u)\in TM\times\mathbb{R},

where
l=l(x,v):TM\to\mathbb{R}
is a Tonelli Lagrangian and
\lambda>0
is a constant. It is well known that this type of contact system is equivalent to a classical non-autonomous system, which has some properties in the following Lemma
3.1
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose
M
is a compact manifold,
l(x,v):TM\to\mathbb{R}
is a Tonelli Lagrangian, and
\lambda>0
is a constant. For any
t>0
,
y,x\in M
, let

\displaystyle A(t,y,x)=\inf_{\xi\in\Gamma^{t}_{y,x}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda s}l(\xi(s),\dot{\xi}(s))ds.

(1)

For any
t>0
,
y,x\in M
, there exists a minimizer
\xi
for
A(t,y,x)
. Moreover, there exists
K_{1}>0
such that if
t\geqslant 1
, we have

\displaystyle|\dot{\xi}(s)|\leqslant K_{1},\quad\forall s\in[0,t].

(2)

There exists
K_{0}>0
such that for all
t\geqslant 1
,
y\in M
, the function
A(t,y,\cdot)
is
e^{-\lambda t}K_{0}
-Lipschitz.

(3)

If
M=\mathbb{T}^{d}
and
l(x,v)=l(v)
is independent of
x
, then for all
t\geqslant 1
,
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, the function
A(t,\cdot,x)
is
e^{-\lambda t}K_{0}
-Lipschitz.

Proof.

The result in (1) is well known. One can refer to
[
6
]
for the proof.

For any
x\in M
and minimizer
\xi
for
A(t,y,x)
, the result (1) implies

\displaystyle|p(t)|=|e^{-\lambda t}l_{v}(\xi(t),\dot{\xi}(t))|\leqslant e^{-\lambda t}K_{0}.

Combining this with the relation (see
[
5
]
)

\displaystyle D^{+}_{x}A(t,y,x)=\text{co}\left\{p(t)=e^{-\lambda t}l_{v}(\xi(t),\dot{\xi}(t))\ \middle|\ \xi\text{ is a minimizer}\right\},

where
“co”
stands for the convex hull, we conclude that
A(t,y,\cdot)
is
e^{-\lambda t}K_{0}
-Lipschitz continuous.

Now, we turn to prove statement (3). Under this condition, for any
y\in M
and minimizer
\xi
for
A(t,y,x)
, the Euler-Lagrange equation implies

\displaystyle\dot{p}(s)=e^{-\lambda t}l_{x}(\dot{\xi}(s))=0,\quad\forall s\in[0,t].

This leads to
|p(0)|=|p(t)|\leqslant e^{-\lambda t}K_{0}
. Additionally, we have

\displaystyle D^{+}_{y}A(t,y,x)=-\text{co}\left\{p(0)=e^{-\lambda t}l_{v}(\xi(0),\dot{\xi}(0))\ \middle|\ \xi\text{ is a minimizer}\right\}.

Therefore, we conclude that
A(t,\cdot,x)
is
e^{-\lambda t}K_{0}
-Lipschitz continuous.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose
M
is a compact manifold,
l(x,v):TM\to\mathbb{R}
is a Tonelli Lagrangian,
\lambda>0
is a constant. Let

\displaystyle L(x,v,u):TM\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R},\quad L(x,v,u)=l(x,v)+\lambda u.

(1)

For any
\varphi\in C^{0}(M)
,
t>0
,
x\in M
, we have

\displaystyle T_{t}\varphi(x)=\inf_{y\in M}\{e^{\lambda t}\varphi(y)+D(t,y,x)\},

where

\displaystyle D(t,y,x)=\inf_{\xi\in\Gamma^{t}_{y,x}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\lambda(t-s)}l(\xi(s),\dot{\xi}(s))ds.

(2)

If
M=\mathbb{T}^{d}
and
l(x,v)=l(v)
is independent of
x
, then there exists
K_{0}>0
such that for all
t\geqslant 1
,
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, the function
D(t,\cdot,x)
is
K_{0}
-Lipschitz continuous.

Proof.

By definition, we have

\displaystyle T_{t}\phi(x)=\inf_{y\in M}\inf_{\xi\in\Gamma_{y,x}^{t}}\left(\phi(y)+\int_{0}^{t}\big\{l(\xi(s),\dot{\xi}(s))+\lambda u_{\xi}(s)\big\}\,ds\right)

where
u_{\xi}(s)
satisfies the differential equation

(4)

\begin{cases}\dot{u}_{\xi}(s)=l(\xi(s),\dot{\xi}(s))+\lambda u_{\xi}(s),&\quad s\in[0,t],\\
u_{\xi}(0)=\phi(y).&\end{cases}

Solving (
4
), we obtain

\displaystyle u_{\xi}(t)=e^{\lambda t}\phi(y)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{\lambda(t-s)}l(\xi(s),\dot{\xi}(s))\,ds.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle T_{t}\phi(x)

\displaystyle=\inf_{y\in M}\inf_{\xi\in\Gamma_{y,x}^{t}}\left(e^{\lambda t}\phi(y)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{\lambda(t-s)}l(\xi(s),\dot{\xi}(s))\,ds\right)

\displaystyle=\inf_{y\in M}\left(e^{\lambda t}\phi(y)+D(t,y,x)\right),

where
D(t,y,x)
is the integral term. This completes the proof of (1).

To prove (2), for any
t\geqslant 1
,
x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, we have

\displaystyle D(t,y,x)=\inf_{\xi\in\Gamma^{t}_{y,x}}e^{\lambda t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda s}l(\xi(s),\dot{\xi}(s))\,ds=e^{\lambda t}A(t,y,x).

Combining this with Lemma
3.1
(3), we know that
D(t,y,x)
is
K_{0}
-Lipschitz with respect to
y
.
∎

For any compact subset
V\subset C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, we try to get a finite estimate of
g_{t}(\varepsilon,V)
. That is, we need to construct finite functions
\psi\in C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
such that

\displaystyle\bigcup_{\psi}B(\psi,t,\varepsilon)\supset V.

So we hope
T_{t}\psi
is determined by the values of
\psi
at finite points
z_{i}
’s, see the following Lemma
3.2
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Under the assumption of Proposition
3.2
(2), suppose
\{z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
is a set of finite points on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. For any function
\psi:\{z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
, let

\displaystyle K_{\psi}=\max\{K_{0},\mbox{\rm Lip}\,[\psi]\},

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R},\quad\tilde{\psi}(x)=\min_{i=1,\ldots,m}\psi(z_{i})+K_{\psi}d(z_{i},x).

(1)

\tilde{\psi}
is
K_{\psi}
-Lipschitz, and

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}(z_{i})=\psi(z_{i}),\quad i=1,\ldots,m.

(2)

We have

\displaystyle T_{t}^{-}\tilde{\psi}(x)=\min_{i=1,\ldots,m}e^{\lambda t}\psi(z_{i})+D(t,z_{i},x),\quad\forall t\geqslant 1,\ x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.

Proof.

Given
x,y\in M
, there exists
z_{i}
such that

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}(x)

\displaystyle=\psi(z_{i})+K_{\psi}\,d(z_{i},x),

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}(y)

\displaystyle\leqslant\psi(z_{i})+K_{\psi}\,d(z_{i},y).

This implies

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}(y)-\tilde{\psi}(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant K_{\psi}\,d(z_{i},y)-K_{\psi}\,d(z_{i},x)\leqslant K_{\psi}\,d(x,y).

Similarly, we have
\tilde{\psi}(x)-\tilde{\psi}(y)\leqslant K_{\psi}\,d(x,y)
, which shows that
\tilde{\psi}
is a Lipschitz continuous function with Lipschitz constant
K_{\psi}
. Recalling that
K_{\psi}\geqslant\mathrm{Lip}[\psi]
, for any
i,j=1,\ldots,m
, we have

\displaystyle\psi(z_{i})

\displaystyle\leqslant\psi(z_{j})+\text{Lip}(\psi)\cdot d(z_{j},z_{i})

\displaystyle\leqslant\psi(z_{j})+K_{\psi}\cdot d(z_{j},z_{i}).

It follows that
\psi(z_{i})=\tilde{\psi}(z_{i})
for all
i=1,\ldots,m
.

Now, we turn to the proof of (2). For any
y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, there exists
z_{i}
such that
\tilde{\psi}(y)=\psi(z_{i})+K_{\psi}d(z_{i},y)
. Now we have

\displaystyle e^{\lambda t}\psi(z_{i})+D(t,z_{i},x)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\,e^{\lambda t}(\tilde{\psi}(y)-K_{\psi}d(z_{i},y))+D(t,y,x)+K_{0}d(z_{i},y)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\,e^{\lambda t}\tilde{\psi}(y)+D(t,y,x),\quad\forall t\geqslant 1,\ x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.

This together with (1) implies

\displaystyle T_{t}\tilde{\psi}(x)

\displaystyle=\inf_{y\in\mathbb{T}^{n}}e^{\lambda t}\psi(y)+D(t,y,x)

\displaystyle=\min_{i=1,\ldots,m}e^{\lambda t}\psi(z_{i})+D(t,z_{i},x),\quad\forall t\geqslant 1,\ x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.

∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Under the assumption of Proposition
3.2
(2), for any compact subset
V\subset C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, there holds
g(V)\leqslant\lambda
. Moreover, we have
h_{\rm top}(T_{t})=\lambda
.

Proof.

By compactness of
V
, there exists
R>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{C^{0}}\leqslant R,\quad\forall\phi\in V.

Fix any
\varepsilon>0
, there exist finite points
\{z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
such that

\displaystyle\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}B(z_{i},\varepsilon)=\mathbb{T}^{d}.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
k=0,1,\ldots,\lfloor\frac{2Re^{\lambda n}}{\varepsilon}\rfloor
, let

\displaystyle U_{n,k}=\left\{-R+ke^{-\lambda n}\varepsilon+l\cdot e^{-\lambda j}\varepsilon\ \middle|\ j=1,\ldots,n,\ l=0,1,\ldots,\left\lceil\frac{K_{0}}{\varepsilon}\right\rceil+1\right\}.

Consider the set of functions

\displaystyle F_{n}=\left\{\psi:\{z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}\to U_{n,k}\ \middle|\ \ k=0,1,\ldots,\left\lfloor\frac{2Re^{\lambda n}}{\varepsilon}\right\rfloor\right\},

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{n}=\left\{\tilde{\psi}\ \middle|\ \psi\in F_{n}\right\},

where
\tilde{\psi}
is defined by Lemma
3.2
. We claim that

(5)

\bigcup_{\tilde{\psi}\in\tilde{F}_{n}}\tilde{B}(\tilde{\psi},n,(1+K_{0})\varepsilon)\supset V,

where

\displaystyle\tilde{B}(\tilde{\psi},n,(1+K_{0})\varepsilon)=\left\{\phi\in C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\ \middle|\ \|T_{k}\varphi-T_{k}\tilde{\psi}\|_{C^{0}}<(1+K_{0})\varepsilon,\ \forall k=1,\ldots,n\right\}.

The relation (
5
) implies

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{n}(V,(1+K_{0})\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leqslant\#(\tilde{F}_{n})=\#(F_{n})=\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{2Re^{\lambda n}}{\varepsilon}\right\rfloor}\#^{m}(U_{n,k})

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\frac{2Re^{\lambda n}}{\varepsilon}+1\right)n^{m}\left(\frac{K_{0}}{\varepsilon}+3\right)^{m},

where

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{n}(V,(1+K_{0})\varepsilon)=\min\left\{\#(E)\ \middle|\ E\subset C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),\bigcup_{\psi\in E}\tilde{B}(\psi,n,(1+K_{0})\varepsilon)\supset V\right\}.

It follows from Lemma
2.2
(4), Proposition
2.1
and Proposition
2.2
that

\displaystyle g(V)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\tilde{g}_{n}(V,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\tilde{g}_{n}(V,(1+K_{0})\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leqslant\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\frac{2Re^{\lambda n}}{\varepsilon}+1\right)n^{m}\left(\frac{K_{0}}{\varepsilon}+3\right)^{m}

\displaystyle=\lambda.

By the arbitrariness of
V
, we have

\displaystyle h_{\rm top}(T_{t})=\sup_{V\subset C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}g(V)\leqslant\lambda.

Combining this with Theorem
3.1
, we conclude that

\displaystyle h_{\rm top}(T_{t})=\lambda.

Finally, we only need to prove the claim (
5
). In fact, for any
\phi\in V
, there exists a unique
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,\lfloor\frac{2Re^{\lambda n}}{\varepsilon}\rfloor\}
such that

\displaystyle-R+ke^{-\lambda n}\varepsilon\leqslant\min_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}}\phi(x)<-R+(k+1)e^{-\lambda n}\varepsilon.

Let
\psi:\{z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\psi(z_{i})=\max\left\{c\in U_{n,k}\ \middle|\ c\leqslant\phi(x),\forall x\in B(z_{i},\varepsilon)\right\}.

Then
\psi\in F_{n}
and
\tilde{\psi}\in\tilde{F}_{n}
. Now we only need to prove

\displaystyle\phi\in\tilde{B}(\tilde{\psi},n,(1+K_{0})\varepsilon),

that is,

(6)

\|T_{j}\tilde{\psi}-T_{j}\phi\|_{C^{0}}<(1+K_{0})\varepsilon,\quad\forall j=1,\ldots,n.

For any
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, there exists
y_{x}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
such that

\displaystyle T_{j}\phi(x)=e^{\lambda j}\phi(y_{x})+D(j,y_{x},x).

Choose
z_{i}
such that
y_{x}\in B(z_{i},\varepsilon)
. By the choice of
\psi
, we have
\psi(z_{i})\leqslant\phi(y_{x})
. Using Proposition
3.2
, we know that

(7)

\begin{split}T_{j}\tilde{\psi}(x)&\leqslant e^{\lambda j}\psi(z_{i})+D(j,z_{i},x)\\
&\leqslant e^{\lambda j}\phi(y_{x})+D(j,y_{x},x)+(D(j,z_{i},x)-D(j,y_{x},x))\\
&\leqslant T_{j}\phi(x)+K_{0}\varepsilon.\end{split}

On the other hand, the choice of
k
and
\psi
implies that there exists
z_{i_{0}}
such that

\displaystyle\psi(z_{i_{0}})=\min_{1=1,\ldots,m}\psi(z_{i})=-R+ke^{-\lambda n}\varepsilon.

Lemma
3.2
implies that there exists
z_{i_{x}}
such that

\displaystyle T_{j}\tilde{\psi}(x)=e^{\lambda j}\psi(z_{i_{x}})+D(j,z_{i_{x}},x)\leqslant e^{\lambda j}\psi(z_{i_{0}})+D(j,z_{i_{0}},x),

which leads to

(8)

\displaystyle\psi(z_{i_{x}})

\displaystyle\leqslant\psi(z_{i_{0}})+e^{-\lambda j}(D(j,z_{i_{0}},x)-D(j,z_{i_{x}},x))

\displaystyle\leqslant-R+ke^{-\lambda n}\varepsilon+K_{0}e^{-\lambda j}.

Now, by the definition of
U_{n,k}
and the choice of
\psi
, we know that there exists
y\in B(z_{i_{x}},\varepsilon)
such that

\displaystyle\psi(z_{i_{x}})\leqslant\phi(y)<\psi(z_{i_{x}})+e^{-\lambda j}\varepsilon.

If it were not the case, Pigeonhole principle yields

\psi(z_{i_{x}})\geq-R+ke^{-\lambda n}\varepsilon+\left(\left\lceil\frac{K_{0}}{\varepsilon}\right\rceil+1\right)\cdot e^{-\lambda j}\varepsilon>-R+ke^{-\lambda n}\varepsilon+K_{0}e^{-\lambda j},

which contradicts (
8
).
Thus we have that

(9)

\begin{split}T_{j}\tilde{\psi}(x)&=e^{\lambda j}\psi(z_{i_{x}})+D(j,z_{i_{x}},x)\\
&\geqslant e^{\lambda j}(\phi(y)-e^{-\lambda j}\varepsilon)+D(j,y,x)+(D(j,z_{i_{x}},x)-D(j,y,x))\\
&\geqslant e^{\lambda j}\phi(y)-\varepsilon+D(j,y,x)-K_{0}d(z_{i_{x}},y)\\
&\geqslant T_{j}\phi(x)-(1+K_{0})\varepsilon.\end{split}

It is easy to see that (
6
) follows directly from (
7
) and (
9
). This completes the proof of our claim (
5
).
∎

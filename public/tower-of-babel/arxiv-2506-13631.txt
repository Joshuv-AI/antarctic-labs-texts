Anomalous Superfluid Density in Pair-Density-Wave Superconductors
Ke Wang, Qijin Chen, Rufus Boyack, K. Levin

Abstract
We study the superfluid density n s ​ ( T ) n_{s}(T) of pair-density-wave (PDW) superconductors; this determines where they are unstable and establishes signatures of their unusual order. Here we compute n s ​ ( T ) n_{s}(T) for the 2D unidirectional PDW superconducting phases that emerge from a microscopic model (with nearest neighbor attraction). With gapped and gapless bands in the Bogoliubov quasiparticle dispersion, this theory provides self-consistently determined wavevectors 𝐐 \mathbf{Q} for the spatially modulated pairing. We report a negative n s n_{s} for a significant fraction of the purportedly physical phase diagram. Independent of the microscopic model, we can generally understand that the implied instability in these superconductors arises from the variationally large | 𝐐 | |\mathbf{Q}| . In the stable regime, the finite-temperature superfluid density n s ​ ( T ) n_{s}(T) displays unusual temperature dependences originating from the gapless Bogoliubov bands which reflect Van Hove-singularity-induced Sommerfeld contributions.These gapless bands, as well as the negative contributions from the Higgs mode, additionally lead to an anomalously small (highly anisotropic) superfluid density. This will then yield an unexpectedly large finite-frequency weight in the optical conductivity.

EM response: the superfluid density.
We consider a mean

Finite-temperature: Role of Fermi Arcs.
As temperature is
raised, we observe that, rather than reflecting gap symmetries
through power laws (as is typically found for the temperature
dependence of the superfluid density), the temperature dependence in

coming from Bogoliubov band excitations is dominated by

current-current correlation functions can be quantified in a similar

7

2

(10)

\displaystyle n^{ii}_{0}(T)-n^{ii}_{0}(0)\simeq-\frac{\pi^{2}T^{2}}{6}D^{\prime\prime}_{i}(0),\quad\frac{T}{\Delta}\ll 1.

Conclusions.
The central qualitative observations of this paper
are expected to hold for a bi-directional PDW as well, although this case
might be even more constrained than the unidirectional PDW superconductors considered
here. Among these central results is our observation, based on the superfluid density,
that stable PDW superconductors are restricted to reasonably small
values of
Q
and that, as a consequence, their stability requires
that the PDW momentum vary over a rather narrow range from
0.25\pi

to
0.44\pi
. Fortunately in much of the literature it is assumed
that
Q\approx\pi/4
, which is compatible with these stability
requirements.

There has been some emphasis in the literature on viewing the PDW state as only existing
above
T_{c}

Lee 2014
;
Setty et al. 2023
. In the Supplementary Materials

app
, we discuss a fluctuating version of the PDW
phase we address here. There is also interest in an accompanying
charge density wave state which we find (and which is supported by the
literature
Loder et al. 2010
) to be of lesser importance as a sub-leading effect.

We end by noting that our paper
should serve as a caution about the necessity of computing the superfluid density when investigating novel
superconducting phases. Indeed there has been a proliferation of recent theory papers
suggesting exciting candidate phases which are thought to appear in exotic superconductors such as, for example,
in moire and rhombohedral graphene.
However, without a study of the superfluid density we have no assurance that these new phases
are robust enough to be observed experimentally and, even more important, that they are stable.

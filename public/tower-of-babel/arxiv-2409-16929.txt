One-body correlations and momentum distributions of trapped one-dimensional Bose gases at finite temperature
Attila Takács, Yicheng Zhang, Pasquale Calabrese, Jerôme Dubail, Marcos Rigol, Stefano Scopa

Abstract
We introduce a general approximate method for calculating the one-body correlations and the momentum distributions of one-dimensional Bose gases at finite interaction strengths and temperatures trapped in smooth confining potentials. Our method combines asymptotic techniques for the long-distance behavior of the gas (similar to Luttinger liquid theory) with known short-distance expansions. We derive analytical results for the limiting cases of strong and weak interactions and provide a general procedure for calculating one-body correlations at any interaction strength. A step-by-step explanation of the numerical method used to compute Green’s functions (needed as input to our theory) is included. We benchmark our method against exact numerical calculations and compare its predictions to recent experimental results.

The momentum distribution
f({\bf p})

f({\bf p})=\int d{\bf x}\int d{\bf y}\;e^{i{\bf p}({\bf x}-{\bf y})}g_{1}({\bf x},{\bf y}),

(1)

\hat{\Psi}^{\dagger}({\bf x})

{\bf x}

\hbar=1

\hbar

,

There also exist well-known asymptotic results for the OBDM of 1D bosonic gases. In particular, in homogeneous (i.e., translational invariant) ground states, the
long-distance
asymptotic behavior of
g_{1}(x,y)
is predicted by Luttinger liquid theory
[
30
,
31
,
32
,
33
]
to be of the general form:

\frac{g_{1}(x,y)}{n}\underset{|x-y|\gg d(n)}{\simeq}\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{B_{m}2\cos(2mk_F|x-y|)}{(n|x-y|)^{2m^{2}K+\frac{1}{2K}}},

(3)

where
n
is the 1D atom density,
k_{F}=\pi n
is the associated Fermi wavevector, and
K
is the dimensionless Luttinger parameter, which depends on the strength of the interaction between the bosons.
d(n)
is the typical length scale of the microscopic problem, thereafter taken as the maximum between the interparticle distance
n^{-1}
, and the healing length
\xi_{\text{heal}}\propto 1/\sqrt{n}
, namely
d(n)=\max(1/n,\xi_{\text{heal}})
. The sum in Eq. (
3
) runs over integer numbers
m\geq 0
, physically encoding low-energy momentum
\pm 2mk_{F}
processes
[
32
]
. Each of these terms comes with a dimensionless amplitude
B_{m}
that depends on the microscopic details of the model
[
34
,
35
]
. It follows from Eq. (
3
) that the momentum distribution of the ground state of an infinitely long 1D Bose gas exhibits a peak
f(p)\sim|p|^{\frac{1}{2K}-1}
for
p\to 0
, as well as weaker singularities about integer multiples of
2k_{F}
,
f(p)\sim|p\mp 2mk_{F}|^{\frac{1}{2K}+2m^{2}K-1}
when
p\rightarrow\pm 2mk_{F}
.

Asymptotic results are also available for the
short-distance
behavior of
g_{1}(x,y)
. In particular, for the ground state of the 1D Bose gas with
\delta
-interaction, also known as the Lieb-Liniger model
[
36
]
(see Sec.
II
for details)
[
37
,
38
,
39
]
:

\frac{g_{1}(x,y)}{n}\underset{|x-y|\ll d(n)}{\simeq}1+\sum_{q>1}C_{q}(n|x-y|)^{q}

(4)

where the dimensionless coefficients
C_{q}
can be expressed in terms of local thermodynamic quantities in the gas.

. In the limit of infinite repulsion (hard-core bosons), this observation dates back to the seminal work of Vaidya and Tracy

The previous observations for the homogeneous ground state motivate the analysis in this paper. We use asymptotic results for the long- and short-distance behavior of the OBDM at finite temperature (see also Refs.
[
40
,
41

For intermediate interactions strengths, which are not exactly solvable, we benchmark our results against those of quantum Monte Carlo simulations.

[
45

IV

VI
.

II
Lieb-Liniger model

Throughout this paper, we focus on 1D gases of bosons with repulsive contact interactions. In the absence of an external potential, the corresponding model was introduced and solved by Lieb and Liniger
[
36
]
:

\hat{H}_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\frac{L}{2}}^{\frac{L}{2}}dx\ \hat{\Psi}^{\dagger}(x)\left[-\partial^{2}_{x}+c\hat{\Psi}^{\dagger}(x)\hat{\Psi}(x)\right]\hat{\Psi}(x),

(5)

where
\hat{\Psi}^{\dagger}(x)
and
\hat{\Psi}(x)
are bosonic creation and annihilation operators, respectively, at position
x
in a ring of length
L
. We set the mass of the bosons
m=1
, and
c>0
is the strength of the repulsive contact interaction.

The Lieb-Liniger model can be solved using the Bethe ansatz
[
36
,
47
,
48
]
. Focusing on the sector with
N
bosons, the Hamiltonian (
5
) can be written in the first-quantized form

\hat{\cal H}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{j}^{2}}+c\sum_{1\leq j<\ell\leq N}\delta(x_{j}-x_{\ell}),

(6)

with associated many-body eigenstates

\hat{\cal H}\,\chi(\bm{\lambda},\vec{x})=E(\bm{\lambda})\,\chi(\bm{\lambda},\vec{x})

(7)

\vec{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})
, whose explicit expression can be found, e.g., in Refs.
[
36
,
47
,
48
]
. Importantly, these eigenstates are labeled by a set of real spectral parameters
\bm{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N})
(or rapidities) whose allowed values are the solutions of the Bethe equations:

e^{i\lambda_{j}L}=\prod_{\ell\neq j}\frac{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{\ell}+ic}{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{\ell}+ic},\quad j=1,\dots,N,

(8)

or, equivalently, in logarithmic form

\lambda_{j}+\frac{1}{L}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}2\arctan\frac{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{\ell}}{c}=\frac{2\pi}{L}I_{j}.

(9)

Equation (
9
) uniquely specifies a rapidity set
\bm{\lambda}
[hence, an eigenstate of the Hamiltonian (
5
)] for a set of distinct integers (half-integers)
I_{1},\,\dots,\,I_{N}
for
N
even (odd). In fact, one may interpret the r.h.s. of Eq. (
9
) as the set of momenta of a noninteracting Fermi gas, and thus one can think of the corresponding rapidities as imposing a nontrivial quantization condition due to the contact interactions. For instance, the ground state set
\bm{\lambda}_{\text{GS}}
is obtained from the equally spaced configuration
I_{j}=-\frac{N+1}{2}+j
, i.e., by filling a Fermi sea for the associated noninteracting system.

In the thermodynamic limit,
N\to\infty
and
L\to\infty
at fixed density
n=N/L
, it is convenient to replace the rapidity set
\bm{\lambda}
with a smooth density distribution
\rho(\lambda_{j})=\lim_{N,L\to\infty}1/[L(\lambda_{j+1}-\lambda_{j})]
. In the ground state, the latter satisfies the following integral equation

2\pi\rho(\lambda)=1+\int_{-\lambda_{F}}^{\lambda_{F}}d\lambda^{\prime}\frac{2c\rho(\lambda^{\prime})}{c^{2}+(\lambda-\lambda^{\prime})^{2}},

(10)

with
\lambda_{F}
fixed by
n=\int_{-\lambda_{F}}^{\lambda_{F}}d\lambda\ \rho(\lambda)
. For later convenience, we also introduce the dressing operation of a generic function of rapidities
h
, defined as the solution to the integral equation
[
49
]

h^{\rm dr}(\lambda)=h(\lambda)+\int_{-\lambda_{F}}^{\lambda_{F}}\frac{d\lambda^{\prime}}{2\pi}\frac{2c}{c^{2}+(\lambda-\lambda^{\prime})^{2}}h^{\rm dr}(\lambda^{\prime}),

(11)

in terms of which
\rho(\lambda)\equiv\left[1/(2\pi)\right]^{\rm dr}
. It is also possible to express Eq. (
10
) in terms of dimensionless variables
\alpha=c/\lambda_{F}
and
g(u)\equiv\rho(\lambda_{F}u)
:

2\pi g(u,\alpha)=1+\int_{-1}^{1}du^{\prime}\frac{2\alpha g(u^{\prime},\alpha)}{\alpha^{2}+(u-u^{\prime})^{2}},

(12)

normalized such that
\gamma\int_{-1}^{1}du\ g(u,\alpha)=\alpha
. As a result, one finds that the equilibrium properties of the Lieb-Liniger gas depend uniquely on the dimensionless reduced coupling

\gamma=c/n,

(13)

with
\gamma\to 0
and
\gamma\to\infty
corresponding to the limits of weak and strong interactions, respectively.

Although the Bethe ansatz approach provides an exact understanding of the spectrum of the Lieb-Liniger model (
5
), the calculation of correlation functions within this framework is a formidable challenge. Since determinant formulas for the field form-factors
\langle\bm{\lambda}|\hat{\Psi}^{\dagger}(0)|\bm{\mu}\rangle
between two generic Bethe-ansatz eigenstates have been determined
[
50
]
, one may express the OBDM (
2
) as

g_{1}(x,0)=\sum_{\{\bm{\mu}\}}\frac{\langle\bm{\lambda}|\hat{\Psi}^{\dagger}(x)|\bm{\mu}\rangle\langle\bm{\mu}|\hat{\Psi}(0)|\bm{\lambda}\rangle}{\langle\bm{\lambda}|\bm{\lambda}\rangle\langle\bm{\mu}|\bm{\mu}\rangle},

(14)

where
\bm{\lambda}
is the rapidity set of the reference state (e.g., the ground state). The evaluation of Eq. (
14
) requires the summation over the intermediate Bethe-ansatz states
\ket{\bm\mu}
, which beyond few-particle systems is a challenging task that needs to be tackled using sophisticated numerical algorithms, see, e.g., Ref.
[
25
]
. The numerical evaluation of Eq. (
14
) for the ground state, reported in Ref.
[
24
]
, yielded the results plotted in Fig.
1
.

II.1
Long-distance asymptotics of the OBDM

Alternatively, a universal description of the system’s correlations can be obtained at low energies using Luttinger liquid theory
[
31
,
32
]
. The main idea of this effective low-energy theory is to encode low-energy quantum fluctuations in terms of fluctuating bosons on top of the Fermi sea
\lambda\in[-\lambda_{F},\lambda_{F}]
obtained through the thermodynamic Bethe ansatz. Following this approach, it is possible to write the so-called harmonic fluid expansion of the field operator
[
31
,
32
,
33
,
15
]

\hat{\Psi}^{\dagger}(x)\!=\!\sqrt{n-\frac{\partial_{x}\hat{\phi}}{\pi}}\!\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\!\left[\sqrt{B_{|m|}n^{-\Delta_{m}}}e^{im[k_{F}x+\hat{\phi}(x)]}\right]\!e^{-i\hat{\theta}(x)},

(15)

with the density
\partial_{x}\hat{\phi}
and phase
\hat{\theta}
fluctuating fields satisfying
[\partial_{x}\hat{\phi}(x),\hat{\theta}(x^{\prime})]=-i\pi\delta(x-x^{\prime})
. In Eq. (
15
),
B_{m}\equiv B_{m}(\gamma)
are dimensionless nonuniversal amplitudes associated to Umklapp scattering, whose values are obtained from field form factors in the thermodynamic limit, as detailed in Appendix
A
(see also Refs.
[
34
,
35
,
44
,
46
]
). The properties of such fluctuating bosons are determined by the Luttinger Hamiltonian

\hat{\mathscr{H}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{L}dx\left(vK\left[\partial_{x}\hat{\theta}(x)\right]^{2}+\frac{v}{K}\left[\partial_{x}\hat{\phi}(x)\right]^{2}\right),

(16)

where
v
is the sound velocity and
K
is the Luttinger parameter, respectively. For the Lieb-Liniger model (
5
), these parameters are not independent,
v=\pi n/K
, with
K=[1^{\rm dr}(\lambda_{F})]^{2}\geq 1
for repulsive interactions.

By establishing the two-point correlation of the fluctuating fields via Eq. (
16
), the Luttinger liquid theory enables the calculation of higher-order correlators by means of Wick’s theorem. For the specific case of
g_{1}(x,y)
, this leads to Eq. (
3
)
[
32
,
33
,
15
]
. When evaluating the sum in Eq. (
3
), one can use the fact that each harmonic
m
contributes to the expansion in Eq. (
15
) as a short-distance correction that scales like
n^{-\Delta_{m}}
, with
\Delta_{m}=2m^{2}K+\frac{1}{2K}

[
32
]
. Hence, by truncating the sum at its leading order (
m=0
), one obtains the long-distance asymptotics of the OBDM [valid for
|x|\gg d(n)
]

\frac{g_{1}(x,0)}{n}\approx\frac{B_{0}n^{-\frac{1}{2K}}}{|x|^{\frac{1}{2K}}},

(17)

with
|x|
replaced by
L\sin(\pi|x|/L)/\pi
in finite-size systems. Furthermore, the Luttinger liquid theory allows one to account for small thermal fluctuations. By incorporating the effect of a finite temperature
T
in the low-energy description of fluctuating fields, one obtains:

\frac{g_{1}(x,0)}{n}\approx\frac{B_{0}n^{-\frac{1}{2K}}}{\left[\xi_{T}\sinh\left(\frac{|x|}{\xi_{T}}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2K}}},

(18)

valid for a translationally invariant gas when
\xi_{T}\ll d(n)\ll|x|
, with thermal length
\xi_{T}=\pi T/v
(we set the Boltzmann constant
k_{B}=1
). See, e.g., Secs. 3 and 4 of Ref.
[
32
]
or the appendices of Ref.
[
33
]
for a derivation of Eq. (
18
).

II.2
Short-distance asymptotics of the OBDM

The long-distance asymptotics in Eq. (
3
) exhibits an ultraviolet divergence in the limit
|x-y|\to 0
. This singularity, absent in the microscopic model, is inherent to the Luttinger liquid description and must be regularized in order to obtain the momentum distribution via Fourier transform [cf. Eq. (
1
)]. To this end, the short-distance expansion of
g_{1}(x,y)
for
|x-y|\ll d(n)
, reported in Eq. (
4
) and derived in Refs.
[
37
,
38
,
39
]
, can be used. Retaining only the lowest orders, one has

\frac{g_{1}(x,0)}{n}\overset{x\to y}{\approx}1+C_{2}(n|x|)^{2}+C_{3}(n|x|)^{3}+C_{4}(n|x|)^{4},

(19)

with the coefficients expressed in terms of thermodynamic Bethe ansatz quantities

C_{2}(\gamma)=-\frac{\epsilon_{2}(\gamma)-\gamma\epsilon_{2}^{\prime}(\gamma)}{2},

(20)

C_{3}(\gamma)=\frac{\gamma^{2}\epsilon_{2}^{\prime}(\gamma)}{12},

(21)

and

C_{4}(\gamma)=\frac{18\epsilon_{2}^{2}-9\epsilon_{4}-2\epsilon_{2}(2+3\epsilon_{2}^{\prime})\gamma+\gamma[2\epsilon_{4}^{\prime}+\epsilon_{2}^{\prime}\gamma(2+\gamma)]}{24}.

(22)

Here we defined the functions

\epsilon_{2k}(\gamma)=\frac{\int_{-1}^{1}du\ g(u,\alpha_{\gamma})u^{2k}}{\left[\int_{-1}^{1}du\ g(u,\alpha_{\gamma})]\right]^{2k+1}}.

(23)

Strictly speaking, Eq.(
19
) is valid for the ground state of the Lieb-Liniger model. However, while finite but low temperatures qualitatively change the long-distance decay of the OBDM from algebraic to exponential [cf. Eqs. (
17
) and (
18
)], they do not significantly affect its short-distance behavior. The validity of Eq. (
19
) for the finite-temperature gas was tested against quantum Monte Carlo simulations in Refs.
[
41
,
40
]
.

As discussed in the introduction, Fig.
1
shows the ground-state OBDM for a translationally-invariant gas of size
L
, obtained by combining the asymptotic results of Eqs. (
17
) and (
19
). For the long-distance behavior, we retained only the leading-order term (
m=0
), although we verified that subleading corrections (
m=\pm 1
) do not significantly affect the momentum distribution within the range of momenta that are shown. The matching at intermediate scales
|x-y|\sim d(n)
is done by taking the minimum of the two asymptotic curves. While improved results could be obtained through, e.g., a polynomial interpolation between the two asymptotic regimes, we find that our approach provides accurate results without the need of further manipulations.

III
Trapped 1D Bose gases

In experiments with ultracold bosonic gases in 1D geometries, which can be realized using 2D optical lattices
[
3
,
15
]
or atom chips
[
51
]
, a confining potential
V(x)
is present, so the corresponding 1D gases are modeled using the Hamiltonian:

\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{0}+\int_{-\frac{L}{2}}^{\frac{L}{2}}dx\ V(x)\hat{\Psi}^{\dagger}(x)\hat{\Psi}(x).

(24)

The confining potential breaks the Bethe ansatz solvability of the model. Yet, one can use the local density approximation (LDA) to describe local quantities in the inhomogeneous system using the corresponding Bethe ansatz results for the homogeneous gas with a local chemical potential
\mu-V(x)
, see, e.g., Refs.
[
3
,
15
]
. This simple approach provides an accurate description of inhomogeneous gases whenever the length scale associated to the changes in the density due to the external potential is much longer than the interparticle distance, namely, whenever
n^{-1}(x)\ll|\partial_{x}\log n(x)|
.

In what follows, we assume that
V(x)=V(-x)
so that in the ground state the gas is confined in a region
x\in[-R,R]\subseteq[-L/2,L/2]
, with
R
being the “radius” of the atomic cloud. The generalization to non-symmetric traps is straightforward.

In the Bethe ansatz description, LDA is implemented through a position-dependent rapidity cutoff
\lambda_{F}(x)
in Eqs. (
10
) and (
11
), fixed such that
n_{\text{LDA}}(x)=\int_{-\lambda_{F}(x)}^{\lambda_{F}(x)}d\lambda\ \rho(\lambda)
. Adopting a grand-canonical description for the gas locally allows one to determine
\lambda_{F}(x)
from
V(x)
via the self-consistent equation
e^{\rm dr}[\pm\lambda_{F}(x)]=0
, with the dressed energy
e^{\rm dr}(\lambda)
satisfying

e^{\rm dr}(\lambda)=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}-\mu+V(x)+\int_{-\lambda_{F}(x)}^{\lambda_{F}(x)}\frac{d\lambda^{\prime}}{2\pi}\frac{2c\ e^{\rm dr}(\lambda^{\prime})}{c^{2}+(\lambda-\lambda^{\prime})^{2}}.

(25)

In Eq. (
25
), the chemical potential
\mu
is chosen so that the inhomogeneous gas contains exactly
N=\int dx\ n_{\text{LDA}}(x)
particles.

Implementing the LDA in the Bethe ansatz framework results in a position-dependent Fermi surface, on top of which low-energy fluctuations can be incorporated like in the standard Luttinger liquid theory reviewed in Sec.
II
. This leads to the so-called inhomogeneous Luttinger liquid Hamiltonian
[
52
,
53
,
43
,
42
,
54
,
46
,
44
,
55
,
56
,
57
,
58
,
59
,
60
]

\hat{\mathscr{H}}_{\text{inh}}=\int_{0}^{L}\frac{dx\ v_{\text{LDA}}(x)}{2\pi}\left(K(x)\left[\partial_{x}\hat{\theta}(x)\right]^{2}+\frac{\left[\partial_{x}\hat{\phi}(x)\right]^{2}}{K(x)}\right).

(26)

Here,
K(x)=[1^{\rm dr}(\lambda_{F}(x)]^{2}
is the local Luttinger parameter and
v_{\text{LDA}}=\pi n_{\text{LDA}}(x)/K(x)
.

Notice that the Hamiltonian (
26
) is still quadratic in the fluctuating fields. Thus, exploiting Wick’s theorem, it is possible to derive a generic expression for the long-distance asymptotics of the OBDM in the presence of confining potentials

g_{1}(x,y)=e^{G_{\theta\theta}(x,y)}\prod_{z=x,y}\frac{\sqrt{B_{0}(z)}n_{\text{LDA}}(z)^{\frac{2K(z)-1}{4K(z)}}}{[{\cal T}v_{\text{LDA}}(z)]^{\frac{1}{4K(z)}}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}G_{\theta\theta}(z)},

(27)

at leading order in the harmonic expansion (
m=0
), and valid for
|x-y|\gg\max[d_{\text{LDA}}(x),d_{\text{LDA}}(y)]
, with
d_{\text{LDA}}(x)=d(n_{\text{LDA}}(x))
. Here,
B_{0}(x)=B_{0}(c/n_{\text{LDA}}(x))
is the local nonuniversal amplitude, and
G_{\theta\theta}(x)
and
G_{\theta\theta}(x,y)
are the Green’s functions of the phase fluctuating field
\hat{\theta}(x)
. We also introduced the timescale

{\cal T}=\int_{-R}^{R}\frac{dx}{v_{\text{LDA}}(x)}

(28)

associated to the inhomogeneous Luttinger liquid model (
26
), which is the time needed by an excitation with velocity
v_{\text{LDA}}(x)
to propagate from one edge to the other of the atomic cloud.

We stress that
\langle\hat{\theta}(x)\hat{\theta}(y)\rangle
is the phase-phase expectation value computed on the inhomogenous equilibrium state (at either zero or finite temperature) of the trapped Lieb-Liniger gas (
24
), while
G_{\theta\theta}(x,y)=\langle\hat{\theta}[s(x)]\hat{\theta}[s(y)]\rangle
is the corresponding correlation after the change of coordinate
x\to s(x)
that maps the modulated Fermi surface onto one with unit sound velocity and local Luttinger parameter
K[s(x)]
(see, e.g., Refs.
[
54
,
61
,
42
]
and the discussion below). One then needs to define a regularized Green’s function for phase-phase correlations occurring at same position
x

[
44
,
46
]

G_{\theta\theta}(x)=\lim_{x\to x^{\prime}}\left[G_{\theta\theta}(x,x^{\prime})-G_{\theta\theta}^{\text{hom}}(x-x^{\prime})\right]

(29)

where ultraviolet divergences are removed by exploiting the known result for the homogeneous gas, namely
G_{\theta\theta}^{\text{hom}}(x)=-K[s(x)]/4\log|s(x)|^{2}

[
32
]
.

Equation (
27
) readily provides the long-distance asymptotics of the OBDM in terms of
G_{\theta\theta}(x,y)
. In general, analytical results for
G_{\theta\theta}(x,y)
are not available, so we treat this function as an input to our theory. In Sec.
IV
, we discuss an efficient numerical implementation to obtain
G_{\theta\theta}(x,y)
for arbitrary potentials, and at finite interaction strengths and temperature. Conversely, the limits of strong and weak interactions are exactly solvable and are discussed in the following paragraphs.

Lastly, we note that Eq. (
27
) has singularities in the limit
x\to y
, which parallel those discussed in Sec.
II
for the homogeneous case. Therefore, the long-distance asymptotics given in Eq. (
27
) must be complemented with a short-distance expansion for
|x-y|\ll\min[d_{\text{LDA}}(x),d_{\text{LDA}}(y)]

\displaystyle g_{1}(x,y)=n_{\text{LDA}}(\zeta)\left(1+C_{2}(\zeta)\left[n_{\text{LDA}}(\zeta)|x-y|\right]^{2}\right.

(30)

\displaystyle\left.\quad+C_{3}(\zeta)\left[n_{\text{LDA}}(\zeta)|x-y|\right]^{3}+C_{4}(\zeta)\left[n_{\text{LDA}}(\zeta)|x-y|\right]^{4}\right),

with
\zeta=(x+y)/2
and the coefficients
C_{q}(x)\equiv C_{q}(c/n_{\text{LDA}}(x))
obtained as simple LDA extensions of the results discussed in Sec.
II
. Combining together the two asymptotic results of Eqs. (
27
) and (
30
), we approximate the OBDM at all distances to determine the momentum distribution of the trapped gas (
24
).

III.1
Finite-temperature weakly interacting bosons

in a harmonic trap

In the quasicondensate regime
\gamma\to 0^{+}
, namely, at weak repulsive interactions
c\to 0
and high density
n(x)=\bar{n}(x)/c
such that
\bar{n}(x)
is finite, analytical results can be derived for the OBDM in a harmonic trap
V(x)=\frac{1}{2}\omega^{2}x^{2}
, see also Ref.
[
52
]
. In this regime, our formalism below is equivalent to Bogoliubov theory, see, e.g., Ref.
[
62
]
.

Starting from the equation of state,
\mu=cn
(see, e.g., Ref.
[
63
]
), using the LDA one finds the local density

n_{\text{LDA}}(x)=\frac{\omega^{2}R^{2}}{2c}\left(1-\frac{x^{2}}{R^{2}}\right),

(31)

where
R=\sqrt{2\mu}/\omega
. Equivalently,
R
can be related to the number of particles through

N=\frac{2\omega^{2}R^{3}}{6c}.

(32)

In the quasicondensate regime, Bogoliubov theory predicts a divergent Luttinger parameter given by
[
33
]

K(x)\overset{\gamma\to 0^{+}}{\simeq}\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{\gamma(x)}}=\pi\sqrt{\frac{n_{\rm LDA}(x)}{c}},

(33)

while the sound velocity is

v_{\text{LDA}}(x)\overset{\gamma\to 0^{+}}{\simeq}\sqrt{c\ n_{\rm LDA}(x)}.

(34)

In the special case of a harmonic potential, the Luttinger liquid Hamiltonian (
26
) can be diagonalized using the following mode expansion
[
52
,
64
,
57
]

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\omega\sqrt{\frac{c}{2}}\sum_{p>0}\frac{{\mathfrak{a}}_{p}(x/R)}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{p}}}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{p}-\hat{a}_{p}),

(35)

\displaystyle\hat{\phi}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi\omega}{\sqrt{2c}}\sum_{p>0}\frac{{\mathfrak{b}}_{p}(x/R)}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{p}}}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{p}+\hat{a}_{p}).

(36)

with
[\hat{a}_{p},\hat{a}_{q}^{\dagger}]=\delta_{p,q}
,
[\hat{a}_{p},\hat{a}_{q}]=0
, and the phonon dispersion in the trap

\varepsilon_{p}=\omega\sqrt{\frac{p(p+1)}{2}}.

(37)

Importantly, the mode amplitudes
{\mathfrak{a}}_{p}(u)
and
{\mathfrak{b}}_{p}(u)
entering in Eqs. (
35
) and (
36
) have a known analytical expression in terms of the Legendre polynomials
{\cal L}_{p}(u)

[
52
,
64
,
57
]

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{a}}_{p}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{p+\frac{1}{2}}{\cal L}_{p}(u),

(38)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{b}}_{p}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sqrt{\frac{p+1/2}{2p(p+1)}}(1-u^{2}){\cal L}_{p}^{\prime}(u).

(39)

These functions satisfy the differential equation

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\partial_{u}\\
-\frac{1-u^{2}}{2}\partial_{u}&0\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}{\mathfrak{a}}_{p}\\
{\mathfrak{b}}_{p}\end{array}\right)\,=\,\sqrt{\frac{p(p+1)}{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}{\mathfrak{a}}_{p}\\
{\mathfrak{b}}_{p}\end{array}\right),

(40)

and are normalized such that

\int_{-1}^{1}du\ {\mathfrak{a}}_{p}(u){\mathfrak{a}}_{q}(u)=\int_{-1}^{1}du\ \frac{2{\mathfrak{b}}_{p}(u){\mathfrak{b}}_{q}(u)}{1-u^{2}}=\delta_{p,q}.

(41)

Using Eqs. (
35
) and (
36
), the Hamiltonian (
26
) becomes diagonal in the mode operators and reads (up to an additive constant)

\hat{\mathscr{H}}_{\text{inh}}=\sum_{p>0}\varepsilon_{p}\ \hat{a}^{\dagger}_{p}\hat{a}_{p}.

(42)

The two-point correlation functions of Luttinger fields computed in the ground state in harmonic traps thus have the following analytical expressions

\displaystyle\langle\hat{\theta}(x)\hat{\theta}(y)\rangle=\frac{c\omega^{2}}{2}\sum_{p>0}\frac{{\mathfrak{a}}_{p}(x/R){\mathfrak{a}}_{p}(y/R)}{\varepsilon_{p}},

(43)

\displaystyle\langle\hat{\phi}(x)\hat{\phi}(y)\rangle=\frac{\pi^{2}\omega^{2}}{2c}\sum_{p>0}\frac{{\mathfrak{b}}_{p}(x/R){\mathfrak{b}}_{p}(y/R)}{\varepsilon_{p}},

(44)

\displaystyle\langle\hat{\phi}(x)\hat{\theta}(y)\rangle=i\frac{\pi\omega^{2}}{2}\sum_{p>0}\frac{{\mathfrak{b}}_{p}(x/R){\mathfrak{a}}_{p}(y/R)}{\varepsilon_{p}}.

(45)

Notice that the sum entering in Eqs. (
43
)–(
45
) is fast converging in
p
. These formulas can be generalized straightforwardly to finite temperatures by replacing the ground state expectation values
\left<\hat{a}_{p}\hat{a}_{p}^{\dagger}\right>=1
and
\left<\hat{a}_{p}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{p}\right>=0
by the thermal equilibrium ones,
\tr(\hat{\rho}_\text{th}\hat{a}_p\hat{a}^\dagger_p)=1+n_{\text{BE}}(\varepsilon)
and
\tr(\hat{\rho}_\text{th}\hat{a}^\dagger_p\hat{a}_p)=n_{\text{BE}}(\varepsilon)
, for a the density matrix
\rho_{\text{th}}\propto\exp\big(-\beta\hat{\mathscr{H}}_\text{inh}\big)
. The thermal occupation of a bosonic mode is
n_{\text{BE}}(\varepsilon)=1/(e^{\varepsilon/T}-1)
. One finds that, at finite temperature, Eqs. (
43
)-(
44
) become

\displaystyle\langle\hat{\theta}(x)\hat{\theta}(y)\rangle=\frac{c\omega^{2}}{2}\sum_{p>0}\frac{{\mathfrak{a}}_{p}(x/R){\mathfrak{a}}_{p}(y/R)}{\varepsilon_{p}}[1+2n_{\text{BE}}(\varepsilon_{p})];

(46)

\displaystyle\langle\hat{\phi}(x)\hat{\phi}(y)\rangle=\frac{\pi^{2}\omega^{2}}{2c}\sum_{p>0}\frac{{\mathfrak{b}}_{p}(x/R){\mathfrak{b}}_{p}(y/R)}{\varepsilon_{p}}[1+2n_{\text{BE}}(\varepsilon_{p})],

(47)

while
\langle\hat{\phi}(x)\hat{\theta}(y)\rangle
in Eq. (
45
) remains unchanged.

Equation (
46
), together with the known result for the nonuniversal amplitude
B_{0}\simeq 1
for
\gamma\to 0^{+}

[
44
]
, gives direct access to the asymptotic long-distance behavior of the OBDM (
24
) in the quasicondensate regime for a harmonic potential. Explicitly,

\displaystyle g_{1}(x,x^{\prime})=\exp\left(\frac{c\omega^{2}}{2}\sum_{p>0}{\mathfrak{a}}_{p}(x){\mathfrak{a}}_{p}(x^{\prime})\frac{[1+2n_{\text{BE}}(\varepsilon_{p})]}{\varepsilon_{p}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\prod_{z=x,x^{\prime}}\frac{n_{\text{LDA}}(z)^{\frac{2K(z)-1}{4K(z)}}}{\exp\left(\frac{c\omega^{2}}{4}\sum_{p>0}{\mathfrak{a}}^{2}_{p}(z)\frac{[1+2n_{\text{BE}}(\varepsilon_{p})]}{\varepsilon_{p}}\right)}.

(48)

III.2
Finite-temperature hard-core bosons in a trap

In the hard-core boson limit (
\gamma\to\infty
, also known as the Tonks-Girardeau limit
[
20
]
), analytical results can be derived for the OBDM of the inhomogeneous gas. The zero-temperature case was discussed in Refs.
[
43
,
46
,
61
]
, so here we focus on the finite temperature
T=1/\beta
regime. The key feature of the Tonks-Girardeau limit that makes possible an analytical treatment is that the Luttinger parameter is constant and equal to its free-fermionic value,
K(\infty)=1
, regardless of the local value of density. This allows us to reduce the inhomogeneous Luttinger Hamiltonian (
26
) to the 2D conformal field theory of a compact free boson. In terms of density fluctuating fields, the corresponding action reads
[
32
,
33
,
65
]

\mathscr{S}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\beta/{\cal T}}d\tau\int_{0}^{1}ds\left(\nabla\phi\right)^{2},

(49)

where
\nabla=(\partial_{s},\partial_{\tau})
is the gradient in 2D Euclidean spacetime. Here
\tau
is the dimensionless (i.e., rescaled by
1/{\cal T}
) imaginary time, and the field
\phi
is periodic in that imaginary time direction,
\phi(x,\tau+\beta/{\cal T})=\phi(x,\tau)
. The spatial coordinate
x
has been replaced with the stretched coordinate
[
43
,
44
,
46
]

s(x)={\cal T}^{-1}\int_{-R}^{x}\frac{dx^{\prime}}{v_{\text{LDA}}(x^{\prime})},

(50)

which can be interpreted as the (dimensionless) time needed for an excitation traveling from the left boundary to the point
x
. The field
\phi
has Dirichlet boundary conditions at the two boundaries,
\phi(0,\tau)=\phi(1,\tau)
. The action (
49
) is then the one of a free boson living on a cylinder of width
1
and circumference
\beta/{\cal T}
. After some technical manipulations detailed in Appendix
B
, one obtains the following result for the finite-temperature Green’s function of the phase fields

G_{\theta\theta}(x,y)=-\frac{1}{2}\log{ \left| \frac{\vartheta_1 \!\left( \frac{s(x) - s(y)}{2} \left|\frac{i\beta}{2\mathcal{T}} \!\right. \right) \vartheta_1 \!\left( \frac{s(x) + s(y)}{2} \left| \frac{i\beta}{2\mathcal{T}} \!\right.\right)}{\left[\partial_z \vartheta_1 \!\left( 0 \left|\frac{i\beta}{2\mathcal{T}} \!\right. \right) \right]^2 } \right| }

(51)

where

\vartheta_{1}(z|\tau)=-i\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{Z}}+\frac{1}{2}}(-1)^{r-\frac{1}{2}}e^{2\pi riz}e^{2\pi\frac{r^{2}}{2}i\tau}

(52)

is the Jacobi
\theta
function. The regularized Green’s function at equal points is then

\displaystyle G_{\theta\theta}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{x^{\prime}\rightarrow x}\left[G_{\theta\theta}(x,x^{\prime})+\frac{1}{4}\log|s(x)-s(x^{\prime})|^{2}\right]

(53)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\log{ \left| \frac{ \frac{\pi}{2} \vartheta_1\!\left(s(x) \left|\frac{i\beta}{2\mathcal{T}}\!\right.\right)}{\partial_z \vartheta_1\!\left( 0 \left|\frac{i\beta}{2\mathcal{T}}\!\right.\right) } \right| }.

IV
Finite-temperature Green’s functions of the inhomogeneous Luttinger liquid

In this section we discuss the numerical method used to determine the equilibrium two-point correlation functions of the Luttinger fields
\hat{\theta}(x)
and
\hat{\phi}(x)
in the Hamiltonian (
26
), valid for arbitrary strengths of the contact interaction at finite temperature. We follow Ref.
[
45
]
, where a numerical method is described for the zero-temperature Green’s functions (see also Ref.
[
46
]
), and extend that method to finite temperature.

Our starting point is the Hamiltonian (
26
), which for convenience we express in terms of the stretched coordinate
s(x)
in Eq. (
50
), with
v_{\text{LDA}}(x)=\pi n_{\text{LDA}}(x)/K(x)
,

\hat{\mathscr{H}}_{\text{inh}}=\frac{1}{2\pi{\cal T}}\int_{0}^{1}ds\left(\pi^{2}K(s)\hat{\Pi}^{2}(s)+\frac{\left[\partial_{s}\hat{\phi}(s)\right]^{2}}{K(s)}\right).

(56)

Here, we introduced the canonically conjugated momentum
\hat{\Pi}(s)=\partial_{s}\hat{\theta}(s)/\pi
such that
[\hat{\Pi}(s),\hat{\phi}(s)]=-i\delta(s-s^{\prime})
. This Hamiltonian can be readily discretized as follows

\hat{\mathscr{H}}_{\text{inh}}=\frac{\pi}{2M{\cal T}}\sum_{j=1}^{M}K_{j}\hat{\Pi}_{j}^{2}+\frac{M}{\pi{\cal T}}\sum_{j=1}^{M+1}\frac{(\hat{\phi}_{j}-\hat{\phi}_{j-1})^{2}}{K_{j}+K_{j-1}},

(57)

where
M\gg 1
is the number of sampling points in the unit interval, and
K_{j}\equiv K(s_{j})
is the discretized Luttinger parameter. The Luttinger fields are replaced with their discretized version
\hat{\Pi}_{j}
,
\hat{\phi}_{j}
satisfying
[\hat{\Pi}_{j},\hat{\phi}_{j^{\prime}}]=-i\delta_{j,j^{\prime}}
. Open boundary conditions are imposed on the chain, implying that
\hat{\phi}_{0}=\hat{\phi}_{M+1}=0
and
K_{0}=K_{M+1}=1
. In matrix form,

\hat{\mathscr{H}}_{\text{inh}}=\hat{\bm{\Phi}}^{\text{T}}\ h\ \hat{\bm{\Phi}},

(58)

where

\bm{\Phi}^{\text{T}}=\begin{pmatrix}\hat{\phi}_{1},\dots,\hat{\phi}_{M},\hat{\Pi}_{1},\dots,\hat{\Pi}_{M}\end{pmatrix},

(59)

and
h
is the
2M\times 2M
Hamiltonian matrix having nonvanishing elements

\displaystyle h_{i,j}=\frac{M}{\pi{\cal T}}\left[\frac{\delta_{i,j}-\delta_{i,j+1}-\delta_{i+1,j}}{K_{j}+K_{j-1}}+\frac{\delta_{i,j}}{K_{j+1}+K_{j}}\right];

(60)

\displaystyle h_{i+M,j+M}=\frac{\pi}{2M{\cal T}}\delta_{i,j}K_{j},

(61)

for
i,j=1,\dots,M
. It is convenient to change the operator basis from
\hat{\bm{\Phi}}
to the bosonic modes

\hat{b}^{\pm}_{j}=\frac{\hat{\phi}_{j}\pm i\hat{\Pi}_{j}}{\sqrt{2}},

(62)

satisfying
[\hat{b}^{+}_{j},\hat{b}^{-}_{j^{\prime}}]=\delta_{j,j^{\prime}}
and commuting otherwise, that we collect in the
2M
-vector

\hat{\bm{b}}^{\dagger}\equiv\begin{pmatrix}\hat{b}^{-}_{1},\dots,\hat{b}^{-}_{M},\hat{b}^{+}_{1},\dots,\hat{b}^{+}_{M}\end{pmatrix}=\hat{\bm{\Phi}}^{\text{T}}W^{\dagger}

(63)

with
2M\times 2M
matrix
W
having nonvanishing elements for
j=1,\dots,M

\displaystyle W_{j,j}\equiv W_{j,j+M}=1/\sqrt{2},

\displaystyle W_{j+M,j}=i/\sqrt{2},\quad W_{j+M,j+M}=-i/\sqrt{2}.

(64)

This notation allows us to recast the Hamiltonian (
57
) in the quadratic form

\hat{\mathscr{H}}_{\text{inh}}=\hat{\bm{b}}^{\dagger}\left(W^{\dagger}\ h\ W\right)\hat{\bm{b}},

(65)

which can be diagonalized by a further unitary transformation
U
. Denoting
\hat{\bm{\eta}}=U\hat{\bm{b}}
, one has

\displaystyle\hat{\mathscr{H}}_{\text{inh}}

\displaystyle=\hat{\bm{\eta}}^{\dagger}U\left(W^{\dagger}\ h\ W\right)U^{\dagger}\hat{\bm{\eta}}=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\varepsilon_{j}(\hat{\eta}^{\dagger}_{j}\hat{\eta}_{j}+\hat{\eta}_{j}\hat{\eta}_{j}^{\dagger}),

(66)

with
\hat{\bm{\eta}}^{\dagger}=(\hat{\eta}^{\dagger}_{1},\dots,\hat{\eta}^{\dagger}_{M},\hat{\eta}_{1},\dots,\hat{\eta}_{M})
and eigenvalues
\varepsilon_{j}\equiv\varepsilon_{j+M}
for
j=1,\dots,M
, following from the symplectic structure of
U
required to preserve the canonical commutation relations of
\hat{b}^{\pm}_{j}
operators
[
45
]
.

Given the structure of the Fock space, it is convenient to consider the associated matrix

\tilde{h}=\begin{pmatrix}\text{Id}&0\\
0&-\text{Id}\end{pmatrix}\left(W^{\dagger}\ h\ W\right),

(67)

where
Id
is the
M\times M
identity matrix and projects on the negative eigenvalue spectrum of
\tilde{h}
such that the degeneracy of the spectrum is removed.

Denoting as
\gamma_{j}
the eigenvectors, and as
\omega_{j}<0
the eigenvalues, of
\tilde{h}
restricted to the negative energy subspace, the two-point correlation matrix can be written as

\langle\hat{b}^{+}_{i}\hat{b}^{-}_{j}\rangle=\gamma^{\dagger}_{i}\ [{\cal P}_{T}(\omega)]_{ij}\ \gamma_{j},

(68)

where
{\cal P}(T)
is a
M\times M
diagonal matrix that projects onto the target state over which the expectation value is computed. In a thermal state, it gives Bose-Einstein weights to the bosonic modes

\displaystyle[{\cal P}_{T}(\omega)]_{i,j}=\frac{\delta_{i,j}}{1-e^{-\omega_{j}/T}}.

(69)

Equation (
68
) fixes the structure of the desired correlation function up to a normalization of the eigenvectors
\gamma_{j}
. In order to fix it, we consider the auxiliary (Hermitian) matrix

O_{ij}=\gamma_{i}^{\dagger}\begin{pmatrix}\text{Id}&0\\
0&-\text{Id}\end{pmatrix}\gamma_{i}

(70)

with eigenvectors
u_{j}
, and we construct the normalized eigenvectors as
[
45
,
46
]

v_{j}=\frac{u_{j}}{\sqrt{u_{j}^{\dagger}\begin{pmatrix}-\text{Id}&0\\
0&\text{Id}\end{pmatrix}u_{j}}},\quad j=1,\dots,M.

(71)

Finally, we consider the
2M\times 2M
canonical transformation implemented by

\Omega=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc|}v^{\dagger}_{1}(M+1)&\dots&v^{\dagger}_{1}(2M)\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
v^{\dagger}_{M}(M+1)&\dots&v^{\dagger}_{M}(2M)\\[4.0pt]
v^{\dagger}_{1}(1)&\dots&v^{\dagger}_{1}(M)\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
v^{\dagger}_{M}(1)&\dots&v^{\dagger}_{M}(M)\\
\end{array}\begin{array}[]{ccc}v_{1}&\dots&v_{M}\end{array}\right),

(72)

in terms of which

\Omega^{\dagger}\ \tilde{h}\ \Omega=\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon_{1}\\
&\ddots\\
&&\varepsilon_{M}\\[4.0pt]
&&&\varepsilon_{1}\\
&&&&\ddots\\
&&&&&\varepsilon_{M}\end{pmatrix}.

(73)

By returning to the Luttinger fields (
59
), we are now in the position of obtaining the desired correlation simply as

\langle\hat{\bm{\Phi}}^{\text{T}}\hat{\bm{\Phi}}\rangle=W\ \Omega\begin{pmatrix}\text{Id}+{\cal P}_{T}(2\varepsilon)&\\
&{\cal P}_{T}(2\varepsilon)\end{pmatrix}\Omega^{\dagger}\ W^{\dagger},

(74)

with the factor
2
in the thermal state projectors following by our choice of normalization. Explicitly, this matrix contains the Green’s functions

\langle\hat{\bm{\Phi}}^{\text{T}}\hat{\bm{\Phi}}\rangle=\begin{pmatrix}\left[\langle\hat{\phi}_{i}\hat{\phi}_{j}\rangle\right]_{i,j=1}^{M}&\left[\langle\hat{\phi}_{i}\hat{\Pi}_{j}\rangle\right]_{i,j=1}^{M}\\[8.0pt]
\left[\langle\hat{\Pi}_{i}\hat{\phi}_{j}\rangle\right]_{i,j=1}^{M}&\left[\langle\hat{\Pi}_{i}\hat{\Pi}_{j}\rangle\right]_{i,j=1}^{M}\end{pmatrix},

(75)

so we perform the numerical integration

{\cal C}=A\ \langle\hat{\bm{\Phi}}^{\text{T}}\hat{\bm{\Phi}}\rangle\ A^{\text{T}},

(76)

with

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|ccccc}\text{Id}\\
\hline\cr&1\\
&\frac{\pi}{M}&1\\
&\frac{\pi}{M}&\frac{\pi}{M}&1\\
&\vdots&&\ddots&\ddots\\
&\frac{\pi}{M}&\dots&&\frac{\pi}{M}&1\end{array}\right),

(77)

yielding the Green’s functions for density and phase fluctuating fields,
x_{j}\equiv x(s_{j})
,

{\cal C}=\begin{pmatrix}\left[G_{\phi\phi}(x_{i},x_{j})\right]_{i,j=1}^{M}&\left[G_{\phi\theta}(x_{i},x_{j})\right]_{i,j=1}^{M}\\[8.0pt]
\left[G_{\theta\phi}(x_{i},x_{j})\right]_{i,j=1}^{M}&\left[G_{\theta\theta}(x_{i},x_{j})\right]_{i,j=1}^{M}\end{pmatrix}.

(78)

Lastly, the regularized Green’s functions are obtained from those in Eq. (
78
) by removing ultraviolet divergences affecting the correlation at coincindent points. Focusing on
G_{\theta\theta}(x,x^{\prime})
:

G_{\theta\theta}(x_{j})=G_{\theta\theta}(x_{j+1},x_{j-1})+\frac{K_{j}}{4}\log\left(\frac{2}{2+M}\right)^{2}

(79)

for
j\neq 1,M
, and at the boundaries

\displaystyle G_{\theta\theta}(x_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{\theta\theta}(x_{1},x_{2})+\frac{K_{2}}{4}\log\!\left(\frac{1}{2+M}\right)^{2}\!,

(80)

\displaystyle G_{\theta\theta}(x_{M})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{\theta\theta}(x_{M},x_{M-1})+\frac{K_{M+1}}{4}\log\!\left(\frac{1}{2+M}\right)^{2}\!\!.\quad\ \ \

(81)

The regularization of the other Green’s function
G_{\phi\phi}
is done in a similar way.

The phase-phase Green’s function in Eq. (

) [together with its regularization in Eqs. (

VI
Conclusion

We introduced a general approximate framework for estimating the one-body correlations and the momentum distributions of trapped 1D Bose gases at finite temperature and arbitrary interaction strengths. Our framework builds on results for the large-distance asymptotics of the OPDM derived using the inhomogeneous Luttinger liquid method
[
33
,
42
,
43
,
44
,
45
,
46
]
, combined with established short-distance expansions for the Bose gas
[
37
,
38
,
39
]
. Analytical results for the long-distance asymptotics were discussed in the asymptotic limits of weak repulsion in a harmonic trap (Eq. (
48
), derived previously in Refs.
[
64
,
52
]
), and strong repulsion in an arbitrary trapping potential [Eq. (
54
)]. At finite interaction strength, the finite-temperature Green’s functions of the inhomogeneous Luttinger liquid that enters the general formula for the OPDM [Eq. (
27
)] needs to be computed numerically. We explained in detail the numerical method for obtaining these Green’s function in Sec.
IV
. The calculations are then carried out using Eq. (
27
) to evaluate the long-distance behavior of the OPDM and Eq. (
30
) for the short-distance behavior. This allows to estimate the momentum distribution of the gas after carrying out a Fourier transform.

We benchmarked our framework against exact numerical calculations in the hard-core limit and quantum Monte Carlo simulations for finite repulsive interaction strengths. We also showed that our framework provides a good description of recent experimental results. The latter is remarkable because of the small numbers of particles in many of the 1D gases and the relatively high temperatures involved. This is promising as our approach is computationally inexpensive (our calculations can be carried out within a few minutes on a laptop) while the quantum Monte Carlo simulations require hundreds of CPU hours and need to be carried out in computing clusters.

A crucial advantage of the approach we introduced here, which we plan to exploit next, is that unlike quantum Monte Carlo simulations it is not restricted to equilibrium situations. Our next goal is to determine how accurately it can describe the evolution of the momentum distribution following trap quenches as those studied in Refs.
[
9
,
11
]
. Computing the dynamics of such momentum distributions is out of the reach of any existing theoretical method —except in the asymptotic regime of hard-core bosons where exact results are available
[
61
]
and numerical calculations have already been compared to the experimental results
[
8
,
14
]
.

Appendix A
Calculation of the nonuniversal amplitudes

Here we summarize the procedure used to calculate the nonuniversal amplitudes
B_{m}
entering in the long-distance asymptotics of the OBDM in Eq. (
3
). We refer to, e.g., Refs.
[
34
,
35
,
44
,
46
]
for detailed studies of these amplitudes.

By exploiting the operator-state correspondence between vertex operators in the underlying effective field theory and the low-energy excited states of the microscopic model, we write the field amplitude for a homogeneous system of
N
bosons on a ring of size
L
as
[
44
,
46
]

\displaystyle\sqrt{B_{m}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{N,L\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{L}{2\pi}\right)^{\Delta_{m}}\times

\displaystyle\frac{\left|\bra{\{J_j^{(m)}\}^{N-1}_{j = 1}}\hat{\Psi}(0)\ket{\{ I_i\}^{N}_{i = 1}}\right|}{\sqrt{\innerproduct{\{J_j^{(m)}\}^{N-1}_{j = 1}}{\{J_j^{(m)}\}^{N-1}_{j = 1}}}\sqrt{\innerproduct{\{I_i\}^{N}_{i = 1}}{\{I_i\}^{N}_{i = 1}}}},

(84)

where the matrix elements can be efficiently computed using the determinant formula of Ref.
[
50
]
. The state
\ket{\{I_i\}^{N}_{i = 1}}
is the ground state of the gas, specified by the set of Bethe integers

I_{i}=-\frac{N+1}{2}+i,\qquad i=1,\dots,N,

(85)

while
\ket{\{J_j^{(m)}\}^{N-1}_{j = 1}}
is a low-energy excited state corresponding to the Bethe integers

J_{j}^{(m)}=-\frac{N}{2}+j+m,\qquad j=1,\dots,N-1.

(86)

We recall that the set of rapidities specifying the Bethe states above is obtained from Eqs. (
85
) and (
86
) by solving the Bethe equations (
9
). Finally, the nonuniversal field amplitude (
84
) requires taking the thermodynamic limit
N,L\to\infty
at fixed density
n=N/L
and interaction
c
, i.e., at fixed reduced coupling
\gamma
. In practice, one can compute the amplitude for fixed values of
N
and
L
and extrapolate the thermodynamic limit with a polynomial fit in
1/N
. The result of this procedure is shown in Fig.

Appendix B
Analytical expression for the Green’s function of trapped hard-core bosons at finite temperature

In this Appendix we derive Eq. (
51
). We start from the mode decomposition of the Luttinger liquid Hamiltonian at
K=1
with Dirichlet boundary conditions on both sides [i.e.
\hat{\phi}(s=0)=\hat{\phi}(s=1)=0
],

\displaystyle\hat{\mathscr{H}}_{\text{inh}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{ds}{2\pi{\cal T}}\left[(\partial_{s}\hat{\theta})^{2}+(\partial_{s}\hat{\phi})^{2}\right]=\frac{\pi}{\mathcal{T}}\sum_{n\geq 1}[\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{n}\hat{a}_{n}+\frac{1}{2}],

where
\mathcal{T}
is the time needed for a gapless excitation to travel from the left to the right boundary, and

\displaystyle\hat{\phi}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n}\sin( \pi n s )(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{n}+\hat{a}_{n})\,,

(87)

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{i\,n}\cos( \pi n s)(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{n}-\hat{a}_{n})\,,

(88)

with the canonical commutation relation

[\hat{a}_{n},\hat{a}_{m}^{\dagger}]\,=\,n\,\delta_{n,m}\,,

(89)

such that
[\hat{\phi}(s),\frac{1}{\pi}\partial\hat{\theta}(s^{\prime})]=[\hat{\phi}(s),\hat{\Pi}(s^{\prime})]=i\delta(s-s^{\prime})
for
s,s^{\prime}\in(0,1)
. The finite-temperature Green’s function is

\displaystyle\left<\hat{\theta}(s)\hat{\theta}(s^{\prime})\right>_{\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{n}\frac{\cos( \pi n s )\cos( \pi n s' )}{n^{2}}\left<(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{n}-\hat{a}_{n})^{2}\right>_{\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{n}\frac{\cos( \pi n s )\cos( \pi n s' )}{n^{2}}\left<\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{n}\hat{a}_{n}+\frac{n}{2}\right>_{\beta}.

We then use

\left<\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{n}\hat{a}_{n}\right>_{\beta}\,=\,\frac{{\rm tr}\left[e^{-\beta H}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{n}\hat{a}_{n}\right]}{{\rm tr}\left[e^{-\beta H}\right]}\,=\,n\,\frac{\sum_{p\geq 0}pq^{np}}{\sum_{p\geq 0}q^{np}}\,,

(90)

with

q=\exp\left(-\pi\beta/\mathcal{T}\right),

(91)

and the following identity

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n}u^{n}\frac{\sum_{p\geq 0}(p+\frac{1}{2})q^{np}}{\sum_{p\geq 0}q^{np}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n}u^{n}\frac{1+q^{n}}{1-q^{n}}

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p\geq 0}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n}u^{n}(1+q^{n})q^{pn}

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p\geq 0}-\log\left(1-uq^{p}\right)-\log\left(1-uq^{p+1}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=-\log\left[(u;q)_{\infty}\right]\,+\,\frac{1}{2}\log\left(1-u\right),

where
(u;q)_{\infty}\,=\,\prod_{p\geq 0}(1-uq^{p})
is known as the ‘
q
-Pochhammer symbol’. This leads to

\displaystyle\langle\hat{\theta}(s)\hat{\theta}(s^{\prime})\rangle_{\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\log\left[\left(e^{i\pi(s-s^{\prime})},q\right)_{\infty}\left(e^{-i\pi(s-s^{\prime})},q\right)_{\infty}\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\log\left[\left(e^{i\pi(s+s^{\prime})},q\right)_{\infty}\left(e^{-i\pi(s+s^{\prime})},q\right)_{\infty}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\log\left[\left(1-e^{i\pi(s-s^{\prime})}\right)\left(1-e^{-i\pi(s-s^{\prime})}\right)\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\log\left[\left(1-e^{i\pi(s+s^{\prime})}\right)\left(1-e^{-i\pi(s+s^{\prime})}\right)\right].

Reorganizing the terms in the infinite products, one arrives at

\displaystyle\langle\hat{\theta}(s)\hat{\theta}(s^{\prime})\rangle_{\beta}=

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{4}\log\left[\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\frac{\sin\frac{\pi(s-s^{\prime}-in\frac{\beta}{\mathcal{T}})}{2}\sin\frac{\pi(s+s^{\prime}-in\frac{\beta}{\mathcal{T}})}{2}}{\left(\sin\frac{\pi(s-in\frac{\beta}{\mathcal{T}})}{2}\sin\frac{\pi(s^{\prime}-in\frac{\beta}{\mathcal{T}})}{2}\right)^{2}}\right|^{2}\right],

(92)

where one can see the periodicity of the result under
s\rightarrow s+i\frac{\beta}{\mathcal{T}}
. This result can be rewritten in terms of the Jacobi’s
\theta
function
\vartheta_{1}(z|\tau)
[defined in Eq. (

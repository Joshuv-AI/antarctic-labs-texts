Hybrid model integrating LeViT transformer and distillation techniques for pattern detection and dance classification
Authors: Yanyan Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Artificial intelligence (AI) has become an integral part of modern life, extending its impact into the preservation of cultural heritage. This study applies state-of-the-art vision transformer models for the classification of traditional Chinese dance types. The main aim is to introduce a lightweight Vision Transformer (LeViT) architecture integrated with distillation mechanism to capture complex cues and visual patterns from images. It is evaluated alongside the standard Vision Transformer (ViT) and a baseline Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) model, which remains a benchmark in computer vision research. The dataset comprises images of three Chinese dance forms that follows standard preprocessing techniques including noise removal, geometric augmentation, and contrast enhancement. After feature extraction and supervised training, the proposed LeViT model demonstrated superior performance in terms of accuracy and stability compared with ViT and CNN. The findings confirm that transformer-based architectures, particularly LeViT, effectively capture subtle motion cues and fine-grained spatial patterns within dance imagery, offering a promising framework for future research in AI-driven cultural preservation and visual arts classification.

Introduction

Culture has long been an integral part of human civilization, shaping traditions, identities, and artistic expressions. Among these, dance stands as one of the most profound and dynamic forms of cultural expression, reflecting historical narratives, regional diversity, and artistic creativity1,2. People engage with dance in various ways, from performance and entertainment to therapy, education, and ritualistic practices. As societies evolve, dance continues to serve as an essential medium for storytelling and emotional expression, influencing domains such as performing arts, social events, and even rehabilitation therapy3. It serves as a universal language that exceeds barriers, enhance social cohesion and emotional linking4. Dance is deeply embedded in cultural traditions, preserving history and storytelling across generations5. Moreover, it is a tool for education, therapy, and social activism, enabling individuals to express emotions, challenge societal norms, and advocate for change6.

With the rise of digital media and globalization, preserving, analyzing, and classifying different dance forms has become an important endeavor, fostering cultural appreciation and ensuring that traditional practices are not lost over time7. The growing interest in dance classification has led to the development of computational techniques to systematically analyze and categorize dance movements8. Traditionally, classification relied on human expertise, where choreographers, dance instructors, or historians manually identified distinct styles based on movement patterns, attire, and music synchronization. However, these conventional approaches are inherently subjective, time-consuming, and require extensive domain knowledge9. With the advancement of AI, particularly in the fields of computer vision and deep learning, automated systems can now accurately classify dance styles with minimal human intervention10.

Deep learning models and AI-based methods, in general, have shown substantial promise in identifying and categorizing11 numerous artistic and physical actions, including dance. Initial machine learning systems were based on manual features and classical computer vision algorithms, and it was usually restricted to represent the complex motion patterns12. In more recent times, convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have been used with great success in dance recognition tasks, with learners being able to more effectively learn hierarchical features of images and videos. Nonetheless, CNN-based techniques continue to have generalization and scalability issues with large and heterogeneous data13. Vision Transformers (ViTs) have been proposed to solve these shortcomings, becoming an effective alternative to dance classification. In contrast to CNNs, which pay attention to local spatial relationships, ViTs include self-attention mechanisms to obtain global context, and thus, they are most effective with tasks that require complex visual representations14. Recent papers have shown that transformer-based models are more powerful than classical CNN models used in image classification tasks, such as human activity recognition and artistic style classification. Being capable of capturing long-range correlations and storing complex information, Vision Transformers are a perfect solution to the problem of classifying dance forms with high accuracy and high strength15.

A dataset used in this study based on variations of Chinese dance and the divided into three classes, which are China Fan Dance, Sword Dance, and Dragon Dance. The sample will consist of 3815 images that will record the various movement patterns and postures of each style of dance. To form a baseline of the performance, traditional machine learning models were taken as the baseline classifiers and then a large-scale comparison to various versions of Vision Transformers was performed. This aims to determine the performance of transformer-based architecture in learning and separation of minor differences between dance styles, both of which will ultimately enhance automated dance recognition.

Related work

The use of deep learning techniques in dance classification has highly evolved to the point where the identification of dance styles and movements just from images has made great progress. To improve accuracy and efficiency of dance classification, several researchers have investigated different architectures, including CNN architectures, DNN architectures, geometric deep learning, and vision transformer-based studies, etc. In the study of, Li et al.16 proposed a DNN based approach to folk dance training and classification to deep learning to improve movement identification from image data with the help of Enhancing feature extraction. However, this approach made only limited generality to other dance forms as it primarily worked with folk dances. In Dewan et al.17, they proposed a deep learning pipeline to Indian dance style classification which includes CNN based architectures to increase accuracy. Though their results were effective, their observation is important for the real world, where the data variability is higher and the applicability of the study of dance classification in a controlled dataset is raised. Naik et al.18 also made another great contribution by applying ResNet34 to classify Indian classical dance images. The main contribution of their study was to show the capability of CNNs to process complex visual forms observed in dance postures. Nevertheless, their analysis was limited to the dataset’s small size and variety, which may limit the generalization of their results to other dance forms. In the same way, Karumuri et al.19 investigated classifying emotional expressions in dance motions through CNNs. In their study, they encoded the full body motion capture (Mo-cap) data into an 8-bit RGB image format which demonstrates the potential of learning affective features from dance using CNNs. The study was innovative, yet as all dance applications may not have access to Mo-cap data, this study relied on it. In his study, Wang et al.20 integrate deep learning with the Internet of Things (IoT) for investigating high level dance movements. The intersection of over here is between AI and smart technology in dance analysis research, they underscored. The feasibility of large-scale implementation of ICT is, however, compromised by the need for IoT integration. In Castro et al.21, did a study of dance classification in video data where they study online dance videos, and the challenges of availability and quality of the dataset. Current large scale video datasets lack sufficient annotated dance movements for deep learning models, and they pointed out the drawbacks of such datasets. Other than the image-based approaches, Lin et al.22 had also used the geometric deep learning technique for classifying 3D Indian classical point cloud data. While this approach enhanced depth-aware feature extraction, it did not make the data acquisition process easily available to popular use. Mohanty et al.23 also took another approach to recognize movement of dance in controlled laboratory settings using Kinect using deep learning. However, their system, Nrityabodha, effectively extracted features from dance postures but today’s Kinect devices were required by their system to be only used in tickets conditions. In Feng et al.24 focused on dance movement arrangement where they used deep learning to improve the movement classification accuracy by incorporating a super pixel segmentation. Although their approach increases the precision of segmenting, the classification performance of complex dance sequences is still unclear. Similarly, Mohanty et al.25combined affective computing to analyze emotional recognition in Indian classical dance videos using the techniques of deep learning to class emotions presented in dance performances.

In a hybrid system using CNN and SVM Gupta et al.26, classified respectively Classical, Bhangra, Popping, Hip Hop and Ballet with an accuracy of 94.2%. This is why the high performance of their model shows that combining deep learning with classic machine learning classifiers works well. Another study27 proposed a fuzzy deep learning technique with IoT for dancing pose recognition. While encouraging, the study did not offer explicitly defined accuracy metrics that could support comparison of performance. In Jain et al.12, the authors enhanced the architectures of traditional CNNs for classifying Indian classical dance forms with an accuracy of 95.6%. Their findings demonstrated that image thresholding was a pre-processing technique that produced improved classification accuracy. Mulyanto et al.28 performed an experiment on the use of a Video Vision Transformer with Tubelet Embedding for traditional dance style classification. The study had also shown improvement over the conventional CNN based methods in processing features in temporal dimension; this was in the cases of dance sequences. A hybrid model based on a transformer encoder with CNN was proposed by Qu et al.29 to evaluate dance movement quality but lacked high quality annotated datasets. In Fan et al.15 introduced a cross-modal transformer for dance style transfer, which aimed to adjust movements from one style to another while making them suitable to classify. Like this, in their work of choreographic quality transforms for dance performance assessment, Kim et al.30 use kinematic data using visual attention mechanisms, their approach classified the movement quality but with scalability due to the dependency on the sensor-based input. Zhong et al.31 developed DanceMVP, a self-supervised transformer model for multi-task primitive-based dance assessment. Among other choreographies, their study also included Urban, Jazz, and K–Pop, which show the versatility of transformers in multi modal learning. Nevertheless, data collection was challenging due to their reliance on large pretraining datasets. In study32, FCTNet is introduced, a fusion model of 3D CNNs and transformers applied to dance action recognition. Although it was very useful for discerning complex movements, the demands on computation made it infeasible for real time applications. To generate partner dance motion in virtual reality environments, Wu et al.33 employed a transformer-based model and synthesized complementary movements. But this approach that trained itself could be used for training and had not been validated with real world data.

Similarly, in Pan et al.34, they employed a transformer controller for various dance synthesis by synthesizing key frame-based movements with good fidelity. Nevertheless, the method lacked generalization on other dance styles. In fact, Yin et al.35 presented a multimodal dance style transfer model with vision transformers for better classification and extraction. The study also demonstrated the advantage of using transformer-based motion adaptation, yet classification loss in the domain remained a big challenge. In this work, Neethu et al.36 use a fused Vision Transformer and CNN approach to understand and classify facial expressions in Kathakali dance. While their work showed the effectiveness of transformers in expression recognition, they needed a significant number of labeled datasets for optimization. Transformer based; In a work by Kritis et al.37 DanceConv’s dance motions are synthesized from music using a Transformer-based audio encoder. What they showed was the potential of transformers in music driven dance generation but were missing work on accuracy of movement synchronization. Likewise, DanceFormer38 is a parametric motion transformer for music conditioned 3D dance generation. Whilst this method improved movement coherence and fluidity that was hard for it to adapt to various dance styles. In these studies, the transformer is shown to be an effective tool for making predictions in dance classification and generation, but lessons are drawn to continue the investigation on real-time processing, dataset generalization, and computational efficiency39. More studies prove that deep learning is effective in various fields of medical and agricultural diagnostics. Extensive testing with CNN- and ViT-based models on the SIPaKMeD pap-smear dataset has demonstrated that ViT architectures, with data augmentation and ensemble learning, are better than conventional CNNs, and hold promising prospects of clinical application, allowing detection of cancer at an early and accurate stage40. Likewise, in the agricultural field, the MetaFormer architecture has been deployed to perform olive leaf disease detection, combining the separable self-attention with the lightweight architecture to attain state-of-the-art performance on two benchmark data sets and outperform state-of-the-art models Swin, MaxViT, and ResNet5041. These papers emphasize that models based on transformers combined with effective architectural design can also improve the development of computer-aided diagnostics in the fields by ensuring the balance of accuracy, interpretability, and computation efficiency.

Most of these methods, despite the state of the art in the existing literature on this research area of classifying dances with the help of CNNs, RNNs, and transformer-based models have drawbacks related to scalability, generalization, and efficiency. Transformer-based neural networks used to handle fine-grained variations of poses, but they require large, labeled datasets and are computationally expensive and restrict their practical use to real-time applications. CNN-based methods do not handle fine-grained variations of poses, and their high computational cost makes them unsuitable for real-time applications. Additionally, some studies use artificial or manipulated datasets, instead of the real-life data of cultural dance, and this makes them less applicable to practice. Notably, previous studies have mostly failed to consider lightweight architectures that are balanced accuracy and yet are computationally efficient. These gaps demonstrate the necessity of a model that can be useful in identifying the presence of complex visual cues in dance and be viable to be used in educational and cultural preservation settings. The proposed LeViT with knowledge distillation framework is designed to address this unmet need by improving accuracy, reducing computational overhead, and adapting to real-world Chinese dance datasets.

Proposed research methodology

In this study, the adopted research methodology is a systematic approach to achieve the effective recognition and classification of dance with Vision Transformer based models. This methodology includes data collection, preprocessing, model selection, training, evaluation and performance validation, as flow defined in Fig. 1. First, preprocessing steps, including image normalization, and resizing, are employed to improve model generalization. Since, the study uses Vision Transformer (ViT) models with the use of self-attention mechanisms to increase the accuracy in feature extraction and classification. Various standard classification metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score were used to evaluate performance, and a thorough model effectiveness was assessed. Finally, computational efficiency, as well as the inference speed and real time applicability are also analyzed to evaluate if these models can be deployed into the retail and industrial environments.

The novelty of this work lies in integration of Levit + distillation which was not explored in the existing studies of relevant literature. The main contribution with integrating the computational efficiency of the LeViT transformer with a specially designed distillation strategy, which passes knowledge of a larger, high-capacity teacher model to a lightweight student model. This design accomplishes two distinct innovations, specifically: (i) it allows powerful performance on complex and fine-grained Chinese dance motions where subtle visual signals are essential, and (ii) it demands much lower training and inference expenses than the standard ViT and CNN baselines, which makes it more feasible in practice in terms of real-time cultural preservation and learning. Therefore, the suggested LeViT + distillation model is not simply an incremental one, but it includes a new architecture, whose accuracy, efficiency, and cultural domain specificity are balanced.

Traditional CNN model

Among deep learning models for image-based tasks, Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) are the most popular model used. CNNs are particularly suitable for extracting spatial hierarchies in images (feature extraction), that can recognize the presence of logos, colors and positioning of text (elements in packaging)46. The next step is to use the pooling layer (e.g. max pooling) which has reduce the dimension of spatial space and maintain key features that keep good computational computation efficiency and ward off overfitting. The output of the extracted features prior to the final stage of FC layers is flattened and passed through a SoftMax activation function for classification47. CNNs work exceptionally well to recognize images in image recognition tasks such as consumer attention modeling and packaging analysis, but the drawbacks of CNNs with long range dependencies and contextual relationships of images make it challenging to learn.

Vision transformer

Another model, vision Transformer (ViT), utilized as baseline for dance type classification, an image of input is flattened as a sequence of patches, and the patch are embedded linearly to a lower dimensional space. The latter sends these patch embeddings, together with position encodings to maintain spatial information, into the standard Transformer encoder. It has multiple layers of multi-head self-attention and feed forward NN layers which facilitates the model in performing global patch pairs relationships and understanding of intricate patterns of the image48. So, a special classification token is preceded to the sequence and the final representation of this sequence after passing through the encoder form its input is fed into a fully connected layer to predict the dance type49. ViT takes advantage of the Transformer’s capacity to learn the long-ranged temporal dependency structure at different levels of pooling, hence, it effectively classes dances based on both local and global visual features in the image50.

Experimental setup

In this section experimental setup has been discussed to use a structured dataset and standardized evaluation metrics to evaluate the performance using standard measures of Accuracy, precision, recall and F1_score, are utilized to evaluate the performance of the model, and confusion matrix analysis, confidence distribution for model robustness and misclassification trends.

Dataset

The dataset used is for image classification of various traditional dance utilized to capture the fine steps, complex dance movements and distinctive features of a dance. It includes 3815 images limited to three dance categories, China Fan dance (1315 images), Sword Dance (1250 images) and Dragon Dance (1250 images). The dataset is of dancers in various poses, gestures, forms, including costumes, prop, and dynamic movement sequences. For model training and evaluation, the dataset is split into a 70:30 ratio into training and testing sets, ensuring a balanced distribution of images across all categories. Results and discussion

The performance is evaluated of the proposed LeViT model with baseline architectures. Finally, this analysis is performed based upon the accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and computational efficiency. Compared with conventional CNN models, the proposed model does better in predicting the classification and feature extraction at the same time, as well as in the aspect of better generalization. The model is also evaluated in terms of training convergence, loss trends, real time inference speed, and confidence scores and to predict whether it is robust enough and can be deployed in dance type recognition applications. The results in Table 4 indicate evident discrepancies between CNN and ViT and the suggested LeViT-based framework in the Chinese dance type recognition scenario. CNN has the lowest overall performance, with an accuracy of 89% and F1-score 89% meaning that the network is not very good at generalizing between dance classes. Whereas, CNN is relatively high in recall of some classes like China Fan Dance (98%), its precision is low (81%), which indicates that the method has a tendency of giving false positives. This is an imbalance that indicates that CNN has not always recorded the finer details of the cultural dances, whose minute differences between posture and costumes are essential in proper classification. ViT model is based on CNN with the added benefit of self-attention, which results in an overall accuracy of 93% and equal precision, recall, and F1-scores. As an illustration, it achieves almost perfect accuracy when it comes to Sword Dance (100%), and high F1-scores when it comes to Dragon Dance (97%). Nonetheless, the inconsistency between classes also indicates that ViT, despite being effective, may also fail to cope with domain-specific complexity of dance images, especially in the case of small datasets. The fact that it needs large-scale training information to be optimal means that it is not always well suited to real-world applications where annotated cultural datasets are typically scarce.

Conversely, the introduced LeViT with knowledge distillation has the highest performance of 96% accuracy and 93% F1-score on all the classes. It does not have the same precision recall imbalance as CNN and in comparison, to ViT, it has consistently high results on China Fan, Sword, and Dragon Dance categories. This stability shows the two-fold advantage of the efficient transformer architecture of LeViT and the distillation approach that complement each other to support strong recognition of subtle motions of a dance with lower computational costs. The proposed model, therefore, supports accuracy, precision and efficiency and becomes a more viable and domain-oriented model in solving real world dance classification tasks.

The cross-validation outcomes presented in Table 5 and substantiated with the confusion matrices shown in Fig. 13 give a comparison between CNN, ViT, and the proposed LeViT model to classify Chinese dance. The CNN baseline was the least performing in general, with an accuracy of 87% and balanced and moderate precision, recall, and F1-scores. The confusion matrix (Fig. 6a) that corresponds to the results shows that CNN has the tendency to misclassify visually similar classes, including Dragon Dance and China Fan Dance (6.2 out of 100), as well as Sword Dance (6.8 out of 100). This implies that CNN is dependent on local feature extraction thereby restricting its capability to recover the intricate fine-grained spatial dependencies that play a vital role in differentiating traditional dance movements. ViT model was a better approach as it reached 90% accuracy and better precision (93%), recall (93%), and F1-score (93%). ViT minimizes the misclassification of classes, as demonstrated in Fig. 13b, over CNN, especially in Dragon Dance, in which recall increased to 91.9%. Nevertheless, there is still an unresolved ambiguity between China Fan Dance and Sword Dance (5.1% and 5.0% respectively), suggesting that although ViT takes advantage of global self-awareness to learn long-range dependencies, it is also to some extent susceptible to differences in pose and costume in a limited set of data.

The proposed LeViT model using distillation had the best overall performance, with an accuracy of 94% precision, recall, and F1-score at 93 consistently. The confusion matrix in Fig. 13c indicates that LeViT does not only reduce the errors in class level but also produces very reliable predictions regardless of the type of dance. As an example, Sword Dance is learned with a recall of 95.5 and slight overlap with other classes which reflects the capability of the model to learn the unique motion cues and cultural patterns within the fine-grained recognition of dance. In comparison to CNN and ViT, LeViT is more cost-efficiency and has strong accuracy, which makes it effective to be implemented in practice in cultural conservation and learning systems. The findings combined indicate that the proposed LeViT with distillation can be considered significant progress in the field of fine-grained dance classification as it allows to enhance the separability of classes, decrease the rate of misclassification, and provide consistent folds. The quantitative data are supported by the visual evidence of the confusion matrices (Fig. 13) that testify to the fact that the model serves its purpose in extracting the subtle visual and contextual aspects of traditional Chinese dance.

The hyperparameter settings for the dance classification models were carefully selected to optimize performance and ensure effective training, display in Table 6. A batch size was selected for computational efficiency while remaining stable for convergence of the models. The architecture derived the model depth and based on this, CNNs used deep convolutional layers for feature extraction and ViT used transformer encoders. To make it consistent with models, the input image size was standardized. The non-linearity was applied with ReLU, and so was the activation, and SoftMax was utilized in the last classification layer. A tradeoff between the computational and feature granularity was made with the best inputs being a batch size of 32 and input image size of 224 × 224. The cosine decay-based learning rate of 0.00001 became smooth in convergence but a dropout rate of 0.1 became effective in countering overfitting. The weight decay parameter and optimizer Adam (Beta1 = 0.9, Beta2 = 0.999, epsilon = 1e-6) have been optimized to trade off the training and generalization. Data augmentation strategies, including width height shift and the change in brightness also helped to augment the resilience against image variability. The early stopping parameter was also 5 epochs, and the parameter did not overfit but maintained its efficiency. The hyperparameters were optimized by experimental trial and error over the proposed LeViT model, and the model was put into a converged point, and a stable performance.

To improve model robustness, data augmentation techniques like width and height shift, horizontal flip, brightness range and contrast modification were used. Long range dependency was captured by attention mechanisms in ViT, and hierarchical feature learning was made by feature extraction layers in CNNs. Parameter sharing and skip connections were used to build models, for the purpose of making learning more efficient. Using the optimized kernel size, pooling type, number of filters, and stride convolutions, feature extraction efficiency was achieved with a parameter efficiency balanced between model complexity and performance. This hyperparameter sets the computational resource for model training, as LeViT model has good computational efficiency and balance in training and validation accuracy as shown in Fig. 15. Conclusion and future work

This study proposed a hybrid neural architecture that integrates the LeViT transformer with knowledge distillation for classifying Chinese dance types from image data. The model achieved superior accuracy compared to ViT and CNN while maintaining greater stability, reduced overfitting, and computational efficiency. These results demonstrate that lightweight transformer models, when enhanced with distillation, provide a practical balance between performance and efficiency, offering meaningful implications for real-world applications such as cultural preservation, dance education, and performance analysis. Beyond reporting performance improvements, this work underscores critical insights. CNN models showed difficulty in distinguishing visually similar classes, while ViT, though effective, exhibited fluctuations that limit reliability. The proposed LeViT framework consistently captured fine-grained patterns in traditional dance, confirming its suitability for domains where interpretability and efficiency are equally important. This study is limited to static image-based recognition, which does not capture the temporal dynamics of dance movements. Since dance involves continuous sequences, rhythm, and transitions, relying solely on individual frames may overlook critical motion information. Future research can extend this work toward video-based approaches that integrate temporal modeling through 3D CNNs, recurrent or transformer-based sequence models, or pose trajectory analysis to more effectively represent the evolving structure of dance. Additionally, lightweight AI models optimized for real-time applications would enable deployment in mobile applications, virtual reality (VR), and interactive dance learning platforms, expanding the practical impact of AI in the field of dance recognition.

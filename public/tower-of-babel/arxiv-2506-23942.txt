C 4 C_{4} -free subgraphs of high degree with geometric applications
Zach Hunter, Aleksa Milojević, Benny Sudakov, István Tomon

Abstract
The Zarankiewicz problem, a cornerstone problem in extremal graph theory, asks for the maximum number of edges in an n n -vertex graph that does not contain the complete bipartite graph K s , s K_{s,s} . While the problem remains widely open in the case of general graphs, the past two decades have seen significant progress on this problem for various restricted graph classes – particularly those arising from geometric settings – leading to a deeper understanding of their structure. In this paper, we develop a new structural tool for addressing Zarankiewicz-type problems. More specifically, we show that for any positive integer k k , every graph with average degree d d either contains an induced C 4 C_{4} -free subgraph with average degree at least k k , or it contains a d d -vertex subgraph with Ω k ​ ( d 2 ) \Omega_{k}(d^{2}) edges. As an application of this dichotomy, we propose a unified approach to a large number of Zarankiewicz-type problems in geometry, obtaining optimal bounds in each case.



1
Introduction

In 1953, Zarankiewicz
[
52
]
asked for the maximum number of edges in an
n
-vertex graph
G
that contains no copy of
K_{s,s}
, the complete bipartite graph with two parts of size
s
. Just a year later, Kővári, Sós, and Turán
[
34
]
proved that such graphs have at most
O_{s}(n^{2-1/s})
edges—a bound that remains unimproved to this day, although it is known to be tight only for
s\in\{2,3\}
. On the other hand, a simple application of the probabilistic deletion method yields a lower bound of
\Omega_{s}(n^{2-2/(s+1)})
for
s\geq 4
(see, e.g.,
[
2
]
), which, up to polylogarithmic factors, is the best known to date.

Over the past two decades, Zarankiewicz-type problems have been extensively studied in various restricted graph families, motivated by questions from both combinatorial geometry and structural graph theory. In many geometric incidence problems, it is natural to study
K_{s,s}
-free graphs, as this condition often corresponds to an inherent geometric constraint. For instance, the incidence graph of points and lines in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is always
K_{2,2}
-free, while in point-hyperplane incidence graphs, avoiding
K_{s,s}
reflects a natural non-degeneracy assumption
[
3
,
42
]
.

More generally, refined bounds for Zarankiewicz-type problems have been obtained in a variety of settings: for intersection graphs of geometric objects
[
19
,
20
,
22
,
31
,
51
]
, incidence graphs involving points and geometric shapes
[
8
,
23
,
31
,
42
]
, semialgebraic and semilinear graphs
[
5
,
23
,
42
]
, graphs of bounded VC-dimension
[
23
,
30
]
, and for visibility graphs
[
1
,
15
]
. In all these cases, the classical Kővari-Sós-Turán bound can be substantially improved. For example, it was shown in
[
23
]
that a
K_{s,s}
-free graph of VC-dimension
d
has at most
O_{d,s}(n^{2-1/d})
edges – a significant improvement over the
O(n^{2-1/s})
bound when
d\ll s
. In some cases, the bound is even linear: for example,
[
19
]
showed that a
K_{s,s}
-free intersection graph of
n
curves in the plane has at most
O_{s}(n)
edges. Beyond merely improving the bounds, these works also bring together a rich toolkit of techniques such as partitioning methods, variants of
\varepsilon
-nets and separator theorems, that have deepened our understanding of the underlying geometry of these problem.

These results reveal an interesting general phenomenon: in many restricted graph families, the extremal behavior is not governed by
s
, as it is in general graphs, but instead reflects intrinsic structural parameters such as the ambient dimension or the VC-dimension, while
s
may only appear in the constant factor.

In parallel with this line of work, a similar phenomenon has been observed in structural graph theory. One of the first results in this direction is due to Kühn and Osthus
[
35
]
, who showed that if a
K_{s,s}
-free
n
-vertex graph
G
does not contain an induced subdivision of a fixed graph
H
, then
G
has at most
O_{H,s}(n)
edges. A
subdivision
of the graph
H
is any graph which can be obtained by replacing the edges of
H
by disjoint paths. Thus, the answer to Zarankiewicz’s problem changes substantially in the family of graphs avoiding induced subdivisions of
H
.

Since then, analogous behavior has been observed in other families of interest, such as graphs avoiding an induced copy of a tree
T

[
29
,
46
]
, and families defined by forbidding an induced bipartite graph
[
7
,
29
]
.
In fact, the general phenomenon can be described more abstractly, as follows. If
\mathcal{F}
is a family of graphs closed under taking induced subgraphs and
\mathcal{F}
excludes at least one bipartite graph
H
, then there exists
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\mathcal{F})>0
such that every
K_{s,s}
-free
n
-vertex graph in
\mathcal{F}
has at most
O_{s}(n^{2-\varepsilon})
edges. This was implicitly proven by
[
7
,
23
,
27
]
, and the value of
\varepsilon
was later improved for various families
\mathcal{F}
by
[
4
,
29
]
.

In this paper, we focus on Zarankiewicz-type problems in geometrically defined graphs, where the extremal number is expected to be linear or nearly linear. We uncover a new strucutural result about graphs avoiding high degree induced
C_{4}
-free subgraphs, which we use to improve and unify a wide range of Zarankiewicz-type results for geometric graph families, obtaining tight bounds in each case.

1.1
Intersection graphs of curves

The
intersection graph
of a family of sets or geometric objects
\mathcal{F}
is the graph whose vertices correspond to the elements of
\mathcal{F}
, with an edge between any two sets that intersect. A particularly interesting class of intersection graphs are
string graphs
, which are defined as the intersection graphs of families of planar curves (which are sometimes also called strings). Here, a
planar curve
stands for the image of a continuous function
\phi:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

The study of string graphs dates back to the 1960s, when they were introduced independently by Benzer
[
6
]
, in the context of genetic mutations, and by Sinden
[
47
]
, in connection with the layout of integrated circuits. Both works posed the basic question of determining which graphs can be represented as string graphs. It is elementary to show that all planar graphs are string graphs, and Even, Erlich and Tarjan
[
16
]
observed that a subdivision of any non-planar graph is not a string graph. However, giving a complete characterization of string graphs is difficult. The main issue is that the involved curves can be arbitrarily complex, making it hard to show that the recognition problem is even decidable. Remarkably, the decidability was established
[
43
,
44
]
only in 2002, and the problem was later shown to be NP-complete
[
45
]
. Despite – or perhaps because of – these results, understanding the structure of string graphs remains an active area of research.

A good example of such a structural result about string graphs is the extension of the classical Lipton–Tarjan separator theorem. This theorem states that any planar graph on
n
vertices has a balanced separator of size
O(\sqrt{n})
, where a
balanced separator
is a set of vertices whose removal splits the graph into two connected components of roughly equal size. In
[
20
]
, Fox and Pach showed that every string graph with
m
edges admits a balanced separator of size at most
O(m^{3/4}\log m)
. This result has several interesting applications; for instance, it implies that a
K_{s,s}
-free string graph on
n
vertices has at most
2^{O(s)}n
edges. Since string graphs do not contain subdivisions of non-planar graphs as induced subgraphs, and in particular no subdivision of
K_{5}
, the theorem of Kühn and Osthus
[
35
]
discussed above directly shows that the number of edges in
K_{s,s}
-free string graphs is
O_{s}(n)
. However, the point of the work of Fox and Pach, together with the subsequent works, is to pinpoint the correct dependence on
s
.

The dependence on
s
in this bound was subsequently improved to
s(\log s)^{O(1)}
by Fox and Pach
[
22
]
, using a refined bound on the size of balanced separators due to Matoušek
[
40
]
. Ultimately, the optimal bound of
O(s\log s)
was established by Lee
[
37
]
, alongside optimal bounds on the size of balanced separators in string graphs.

Although the existence of small separators is a powerful tool, with a wide range of theoretical and algorithmic applications, it is only available for a limited selection of graph families. In this paper, we provide an alternative proof of the optimal
O(s\log s)
bound which relies on our key graph-theoretic tool, Theorem
1.9
, and avoids the use of separators altogether.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex
K_{s,s}
-free string graph. Then
G
has
O(s(\log s)n)
edges.

For certain restricted families of curves, this bound can be further improved. A family of curves is called
k
-intersecting
if any two curves intersect in at most
k
points. We further impose standard non-degeneracy conditions: no curve is self-intersecting, no three curves meet at a common point, and if two curves intersect, one properly passes to the other side of the other (i.e. there are no touching pairs of curves).

Fox and Pach
[
20
]
showed that a
K_{s,s}
-free intersection graph of an
n
-element
k
-intersecting family of curves has at most
O_{k}(sn)
edges. Beyond separators, a key component of their proof is a result of Fox, Pach, and Tóth
[
26
]
, which states that dense intersection graphs of
k
-intersecting families contain bicliques of linear size. Here, a
biclique
is a complete bipartite graph with equal sized vertex classes. We prove a strengthening of this result, carrying it over to the bipartite setting, and give an alternative proof of the bound
O_{k}(sn)
. More precisely, we assume that
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
are families of curves which are individually
k
-intersecting, and we make no assumptions on the number of intersections between a curve from
\mathcal{A}
and a curve from
\mathcal{B}
. To state the following result, let us define the notion of the
bipartite intersection graph
of two families
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
, which is the bipartite graph with vertex classes
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
, where there is an edge between
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
b\in\mathcal{B}
if
a
and
b
intersect.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
be
n
-element
k
-intersecting families of curves. If the bipartite intersection graph of
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
has at least
cn^{2}
edges, then it contains a biclique of size
\Omega_{k,c}(n)
.

We note that a similar result to Theorem
1.2
is proved by Korándi, Pach, and Tomon
[
33
]
, under the addional assumption that
\mathcal{B}
is
1
-intersecting and that all curves are graphs of functions
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
. Also, recently, Fox, Pach, and Suk
[
24
]
proved the
k=1
case of Theorem
1.2
under the stronger assumption that
\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}
is 1-intersecting. Therefore, Theorem
1.2
also greatly strengthens both of these results. We can use Theorem
1.2
and our main graph theoretical lemma to give a simple proof of several results, including the
O_{k}(sn)
bound for the number of edges in
K_{s,s}
-free intersection graphs of
k
-intersecting families.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G
be the intersection graph of a
k
-intersecting family of curves. If
G
is
K_{s,s}
-free, then
G
has
O_{k}(sn)
edges.

Furthermore, the case when
G
is the intersection graph of the union of two families of disjoint curves is also interesting – for example, it includes the intersection graphs of vertical and horiztonal segments in the plane, which have been recently studied in
[
9
,
31
]
. In this case, we also obtain a tight bound.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
be two
n
element families of pairwise disjoint curves, and let
G
be the intersection graph of
\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}
. If
G
is
K_{s,s}
-free, then
G
has
O(sn)
edges.

Note that the difference between Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
is that the curves
a\in\mathcal{A},b\in\mathcal{B}
may intersect an arbitrary number of times in Theorem
1.4
.
Finally, we can establish a similar result in the case of intersection graphs of convex sets.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
G
be the intersection graph of a family of convex sets in the plane. If
G
is
K_{s,s}
-free, then
G
has
O(sn)
edges.

1.2
Pseudo-disks

Given a ground set
X
and a family of its subsets
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{X}
, the
incidence graph
of
(X,\mathcal{F})
is the bipartite graph with vertex classes
X
and
\mathcal{F}
, with an edge between
x\in X
and
F\in\mathcal{F}
if
x\in F
. Recently, Chan and Har-Peled
[
8
]
presented a number of Zarankiewicz-type results for geometric incidence graphs, including incidence graphs of points and boxes, halfspaces, and pseudo-disks. A family of
pseudo-disks
is a family of simple closed Jordan regions in the plane such that the boundaries of any two intersect in at most two points. Disks form a natural example, and more generally, any family of homothets of a fixed convex shape also constitutes a pseudo-disk family. In
[
8
]
, it is proved that a
K_{s,s}
-free incidence graph of
n
points and
n

y
-monotone pseudo-disks has at most
O(sn\log\log n)
edges. They further posed the problem of improving this bound to
O_{s}(n)
. Here, a pseudodisk is called
y
-monotone if its intersection with any vertical line is either empty or it is an interval.

Soon afterward, Keller and Smorodinsky
[
31
]
improved their results as follows. Firstly, they showed that the number of edges in a
K_{s,s}
-free incidence graph of points and pseudodisks is bounded by
O(s^{6}n)
. Furthermore, they extended this result by showing that if
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are two
n
-element families of pseudo-disks, and the bipartite intersection graph
G
between
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
is
K_{s,s}
-free, then
e(G)=O(s^{6}n)
. In the case of
y
-monotone pseudo-disks, we further improve these result by establishing the optimal dependence on
s
as well.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
be two
n
-element families of
y
-monotone pseudodisks. If the biparite intersection graph
G
between
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
is
K_{s,s}
-free, then
G
has
O(sn)
edges.

1.3
Semilinear graphs

A graph
G
is
semialgebraic
of description complexity
(d,D,m)
if the vertices of
G
are points in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and whether
x
and
y
are joined by an edge is decided by a Boolean combination of
m
inequalities
\{f_{i}(x,y)\leq 0\}
for
i\in[m]
, where
f_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a polynomial of total degree at most
D
.
Semilinear graphs form the special subfamily of semialgebraic graphs in which the defining polynomials are linear functions, that is,
D=1
. The study of Zarankiewicz problems for semilinear graphs was initiated by Basit, Chernikov, Starchenko, Tao, and Tran
[
5
]
, and have recieved significant interest since then
[
8
,
31
,
50
,
51
]
. In this paper, we establish optimal bounds for this problem, sharpening all previous results.

Formally, a bipartite graph
\Gamma
is
semilinear
of dimension
(d_{x},d_{y})
and complexity
(h,t)
if the following holds. The vertex classes of
\Gamma
are
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}
and
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{d_{y}}
, and there are
ht
linear functions
f_{i,j}:\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{y}}\to\mathbb{R}
, where
(i,j)\in[h]\times[t]
, such that two vertices
x\in X
and
y\in Y
are joined by an edge in
\Gamma
if and only if

\bigvee_{j\in[t]}\bigwedge_{i\in[h]}\{f_{i,j}(x,y)\leq 0\}=\mbox{true}.

This definition might seem somewhat technical at first, so let us provide some examples. One of the most interesting families of semilinear graphs of bounded dimension and complexity are the incidence graphs of points and axis-parallel boxes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Indeed, given a point
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})
and an axis parallel box
B=[a_{1},b_{1}]\times\dots\times[a_{d},b_{d}]
, we have
x\in B
if
a_{i}-x_{i}\leq 0
and
x_{i}-b_{i}\leq 0
for every
i\in[d]
. Therefore, we can represent the boxes using
2d
-dimensional vectors, and so incidence graphs of points and boxes are semilinear of dimension
(d,2d)
and complexity
(2d,1)
. Further examples include interval graphs, shift graphs, circle graphs, and intersection graphs of boxes.

Another semilinear graph of interest is the incidence graph of points and
corners
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where a corner is a set of the form
[-\infty,b_{1}]\times\dots\times[-\infty,b_{d}]
. These graphs are semilinear of dimension
(d,d)
and complexity
(d,1)
. It is not difficult to show that a semilinear graph of dimension
(d_{1},d_{2})
and complexity
(h,t)
is the union of
t
incidence graphs of points and corners in
\mathbb{R}^{h}
. Basit, Chernikov, Starchenko, Tao and Tran
[
5
]
and Tomon and Zakharov
[
51
]
proved that if the point-corner incidence graph has
n
points and is
K_{s,s}
-free, then it has
O_{h,s}\big(n(\log n)^{h}\big)
edges, which implies a similar bound for the number of edges of
K_{s,s}
-free
n
-vertex semilinear graph of complexity
(h,t)
.

In the case of incidence graphs of points and boxes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the resulting upper bound
O_{d,s}(n(\log n)^{2d})
was improved by Chan and Har-Peled
[
8
]
to
O_{d}(sn(\log n/\log\log n)^{d-1})
, who also established that this bound is the best possible for
s=2
, see also
[
50
]
for a matching lower bound construction. They used this result to show that if an
n
-vertex
K_{s,s}
-free semilinear graph
\Gamma
of complexity
(h,1)
can be defined with linear functions
f_{1,1},\dots,f_{h,1}
such that there are
\delta
non-parallel halfspaces among the halfspaces
\{y:f_{i,1}(0,y)\leq 0\}
, then
\Gamma
has
O_{\delta}(sn(\log n/\log\log n)^{\delta-1})
edges. We greatly improve these results by showing that the exponent of the
\log
term can be bounded by the dimension alone.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\Gamma
be an
n
-vertex
K_{s,s}
-free semilinear graph of dimension
(d_{x},d_{y})
, where
d_{x}\geq d_{y}\geq 1
, and complexity
(h,t)
. Then

e(\Gamma)\leq O_{d_{x},h}\left(tsn\Big(\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}\Big)^{d_{y}-1}\right).

The bound in Theorem
1.7
is very close to optimal. Let
m=2n/s
, then as mentioned above, there exist
K_{2,2}
-free
m
-vertex incidence graphs of points and boxes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\Omega_{d}(m(\log/\log\log m)^{d-1})
edges. Such graphs are semilinear of dimension
(d,2d)
and complexity
(2d,1)
. Repeating each point and box
s/2
times, and perturbing them slightly to avoid repetitions, we get an
n
-vertex
K_{s,s}
-free incidence graph with
\Omega_{d}\Big(s^{2}m\big(\frac{\log m}{\log\log m}\big)^{d-1}\Big)=\Omega_{d}\Big(sn\big(\frac{\log n/s}{\log\log n/s}\big)^{d-1}\Big)
edges.

1.4
Polygon visibility graphs

The study of the following geometric Zarankiewicz problem was recently initiated by Du and McCarty
[
15
]
. For a (closed) Jordan curve
K
and a set of points
P\subset K
, the
visibility graph
of
P
with respect to
K
is the graph with vertex set
P
, in which
x,y\in P
are joined by an edge if the straight line segment connecting
x
and
y
is completely contained in the region bounded by
K
, which we denote by
K^{*}
. Du and McCarty
[
15
]
conjecture that the maximum number of edges in a
K_{s,s}
-free polygon visibility graph is
O_{s}(n)
. This is partially motivated by the result of Davies, Krawczyk, McCarty, and Walczak
[
12
]
that this family is
\chi
-bounded. The notion of
\chi
-boundedness is central in structural graph theory, where a family
\mathcal{F}
is said to be
\chi
-bounded if the chromatic number
\chi(G)
of any graph
G\in\mathcal{F}
is bounded by a function of the clique number
\omega(G)
, i.e. if there exists a function
f:\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\to\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
\chi(G)\leq f(\omega(G))
for all
G\in\mathcal{F}
.

The question of Du and McCarty is still open, but Ackerman and Keszegh
[
1
]
established a number of partial results towards it. Say that a Jordan curve is
x
-monotone
, if every vertical line intersects
K
in at most 2 points, and say that it is
star-shaped
, if there is a point
c\in K^{*}
such that every half-line starting at
c
intersects
K
in one point. In
[
1
]
, it is proved that if
G
is an
n
-vertex visibility graph with respect to an
x
-monotone or star-shaped curve, and
G
is
K_{s,s}
-free, then
G
has at most
O_{s}(n)
edges. We improve both of these results as follows.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex visibility graph with respect to a Jordan curve
K
that is either
x
-monotone or star shaped. If
G
contains no
K_{s,s}
, then
G
has
O(sn)
edges.

In order to prove this theorem, we establish general results about
K_{s,s}
-free ordered graphs avoiding some fixed ordered matching, which might be of independent interest.

1.5
Key graph-theoretic tool: finding
C_{4}
-free subgraphs or dense patches

Our main theoretical contribution, and the unifying theme of this work, is the following statement, which says that every graph
G
either contains a
C_{4}
-free induced subgraph with large average degree or a dense subgraph, whose size is at least the average degree of
G
.

Theorem 1.9
.

For every positive integer
k
there exists
c=c(k)>0
such that the following holds. Every graph
G
of average degree at least
d
either contains an induced
C_{4}
-free subgraph with average degree at least
k
, or it contains a subgraph on
d
vertices with at least
cd^{2}
edges.

In a typical application, we attempt to bound the average degree of a
K_{s,s}
-free graph from some geometrically defined class by
d\leq O(s)
, which corresponds to bounding the number of edges by
O(sn)
. To do this, the first step is to bound the number of edges of a
C_{4}
-free graph from this family by
Cn
, for some absolute constant
C
, which corresponds to addressing the case when
s=2
. Then, given a
K_{s,s}
-free graph
G
from this geometric family, we apply our Theorem
1.9
with any
k>C
, and observe that, since
G
does not contain
C_{4}
-free subgraphs of average degree at least
k
, it must contain a subgraph on
d
vertices with
cd^{2}
edges. The second step is to show that in such dense graphs one can find bicliques of size
\Omega(d)
. In particular, this means that
s\geq\Omega(d)
, i.e. that
d\leq O(s)
.

Beyond the geometric applications discussed so far, Theorem
1.9
has a tight connection with the notion of degree-boundedness in structural graph theory. A hereditary family
\mathcal{F}
is called
degree-bounded
if every
K_{s,s}
-free
n
-vertex member of
\mathcal{F}
has
O_{\mathcal{F},s}(n)
edges, or equivalently, average degree
O_{\mathcal{F},s}(1)
. For example, an equivalent way to phrase the aforementioned theorem of Kühn and Osthus
[
35
]
is that the family of graphs without induced
H
-subdivisions is degree-bounded. As we previously discussed, degree-bounded families appear in a number of places, including the family of graphs avoiding an induced copy of a tree
T

[
29
,
45
]
, string graphs
[
19
,
20
,
22
]
, incidence graphs of points and halfspaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

[
8
]
, and visibility graphs with respect to certain Jordan curves
[
1
]
.

This has lead to significant interest in understanding general properties of degree-bounded families. The first result in this direction is due to McCarty
[
41
]
, who showed the following, perhaps surprising result. If
\mathcal{F}
is a hereditary family for which there exists a positive integer
k
such that every
C_{4}
-free member of
\mathcal{F}
has average degree at most
k
, then
\mathcal{F}
is degree-bounded. This was later strengthened by Girão and Hunter
[
27
]
(see also the related independent work by Bourneuf, Bucić, Cook and Davies
[
7
]
), who showed that if
\mathcal{F}
is degree-bounded, then there exists
c=c(\mathcal{F})>0
such that every
K_{s,s}
-free member of
\mathcal{F}
has average degree at most
O_{\mathcal{F}}(s^{c})
. In other words, these works say that (1) in order to show that
\mathcal{F}
is degree-bounded, it is enough to consider
C_{4}=K_{2,2}
-free members of
\mathcal{F}
, and (2) if
\mathcal{F}
is degree-bounded, then it is automatically
polynomially
degree-bounded.

In light of these results, Theorem
1.9
is equivalent with the statement that if
\mathcal{F}
is a degree-bounded family, then there exist
c=c(\mathcal{F})>0
such that if
G\in\mathcal{F}
has average degree
d
, then
G
contains a subgraph with
d
vertices and edge density at least
c
. Here, the edge-density of a graph
G
is defined as
e(G)/\binom{|V(G)|}{2}
, i.e. as the number of edges of
G
divided by maximum number of edges in a graph with the same vertex set.

We now describe from another perspective how to apply Theorem
1.9
to get sharp bounds on Zarankiewicz problems. Say that a family
\mathcal{F}
of graphs is
weakly degree-bounded
if there exists
k
such that every
C_{4}
-free member of
\mathcal{F}
has average degree at most
k
. As we pointed out, for hereditary families being weakly degree-bounded is equivalent to being degree-bounded. However, we introduce this weaker notion to emphasize that it is enough to consider
C_{4}
-free members of the family. Furthermore, say that a family of graphs
\mathcal{F}
has the
density-EH property
(where EH is an abbreviation for Erdős-Hajnal) if for every
c>0
there exists
\delta=\delta(c)>0
such that for every
G\in\mathcal{F}
, where
G
has edge density at least
c
,
G
contains a biclique of size at least
\delta v(G)
.

Corollary 1.10
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a hereditary family that is both weakly degree-bounded and has the density EH-property. Then there exists a constant
C=C(\mathcal{F})>0
such that if
G\in\mathcal{F}
is
K_{s,s}
-free, then the average degree of
G
is at most
Cs
.

Proof.

As
\mathcal{F}
is weakly degree-bounded, there exists
k
such that every
C_{4}
-free member of
\mathcal{F}
has average degree less than
k
. Let
G\in\mathcal{F}
be a
K_{s,s}
-free graph, and let
d
be the average degree of
G
. If
c=c(k)>0
is the constant promised by Theorem
1.9
, then
G
contains an induced subgraph
H
on
d
vertices with at least
cd^{2}
edges. But as
\mathcal{F}
has the density-EH propery, there exists
\delta=\delta(c)>0
such that
H
contains a biclique of size at least
\delta v(H)=\delta d
. Therefore,
s>\delta d
, which gives
d\leq s/\delta
, showing that the theorem holds with
C=1/\delta
.
∎

Corollary
1.10
turns out to be highly applicable in geometric settings. One reason for this is that semialgebraic graphs have the density-EH property. It is proved by Fox, Gromov, Lafforgue, Naor and Pach
[
17
]
that for any fixed triple
(d,D,m)
, the family of semialgrabraic-graphs of description complexity
(d,D,m)
has the density-EH property. This provides one half of the requirements of Theorem
1.10
. However, these families are not necessarily weakly degree-bounded (e.g. incidence graphs of points and lines), so this property depends more on the specific geometric structure.

1.6
Graphs without dense subgraphs

Finally, we highlight a strengthening of Theorem
1.9
, which can be used to settle a recent conjecture of Fox, Nenadov, and Pham
[
18
]
. A natural relaxation of
K_{s,s}
-freeness, introduced in
[
18
]
, is the notion of
(c,t)
-sparsity. A graph
G
is said to be
(c,t)
-sparse
if for all subsets
A,B\subseteq V(G)
with
|A|,|B|\geq t
, there are at most
e(A,B)\leq(1-c)|A||B|
edges between them. It is easy to see that a graph is
K_{s,s}
-free if and only if it is
(1/s^{2},s)
-sparse. Another motivation for studying
(c,t)
-sparse graphs is that random graphs
G(n,p)
are
(c,t)
-sparse for any
c<1-p
and
t=\Omega_{p,c}(\log n)
.

In
[
18
]
, the authors showed that if
c>0
is a constant and
T
is a tree, then every
(c,t)
-sparse graph with at least
Ctn
edges contains an induced copy of
T
, where
C=C(T,c)
is sufficiently large with respect to
T
and
c
. They conjectured that the same statement holds if instead of a tree
T
, we seek an induced subdivision of a fixed graph
H
. We prove this conjecture.

Theorem 1.11
.

For every
c>0
and every graph
H
, there exists a constant
C
such that if
G
is a
(c,t)
-sparse graph with average degree at least
Ct
, then
G
contains an induced subdivision of
H
.

In particular, this theorem immediately follows from the following strengthening of Theorem
1.9
, after recalling that
C_{4}
-free graphs of sufficiently large average degree contain and induced subdivision of
H
(Kühn and Osthus
[
35
]
). In Section
3
, we show that the next theorem indeed implies Theorem
1.9
.

Theorem 1.12
.

Let
k\geq 1,c>0
, then there exists
C=C(k,c)
such that the following holds. Let
G
be a
(c,t)
-sparse graph
G
with average degree at least
Ct
. Then
G
contains a bipartite
C_{4}
-free induced subgraph of average degree at least
k
.

Organization.

In Section
2
, we begin by presenting how Theorem
1.9
can be used to give short proofs of tight bounds for the Zarankiewicz problem for (1) intersection graphs of horizontal and vertical segments in the plane, (2) point-halfspace incidence graphs and (3) string graphs. Then, in Section
3

1.9
and Theorem
1.12
. In Section
4
, we discuss the proofs of Theorems
1.2
and
1.6
. In Section

. We conclude by discussing polygon visibility graphs in Section
6
and proving Theorem
1.8
.

2
Short applications

As a warm-up, we present a few simple applications of Theorem
1.9
, which will be proved in the next section. First, we show that given a collection of
m
vertical and
n
horizontal segments on the plane such that the intersection graph
G
is
K_{s,s}
-free, then
G
has
O(s(m+n))
edges. This result may sound innocent at first, and it also follows from any of Theorems
1.3
,
1.4
, or
1.5
, but we were unable to find a completely elementary proof. Also, this statement is quite useful – for its applications see
[
1
,
9
,
31
]
.

Then, we provide an alternate proof of the result of Chan and Har-Peled
[
8
]
on incidence graphs of points and halfspaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Finally, we prove the Zarankiewicz-type results concerning intersection graphs of curves which were discussed in Section
1.1
.

As alluded in the introduction, in each of the applications, we need to address the
C_{4}
-free case and the dense case. While there are several ways to resolve the dense case, we found that applying the following result about semialgebraic graphs gives a unified approach, applicable to a wide variety of settings. Formally, a bipartite graph
G
with vertex classes
X,Y
is
semialgebraic
of description complexity
(d,D,m)
if
X,Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and there exist
m
polynomials
f_{1},\dots,f_{m}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, each of total degree at most
D
, and a Boolean function
\phi:\{\mbox{false,true}\}^{m}\rightarrow\{\mbox{false,true}\}
such that for every
x\in X
and
y\in Y
,
x
and
y
are joined by an edge if and only if

\phi(\{f_{i}(x,y)\leq 0\}_{i\in[m]})=\mbox{true}
. The following result first appeared in
[
17
]
by Fox, Gromov, Lafforgue, Naor and Pach, and later quantitatively sharpened by Fox, Pach, Sheffer, Suk and Zahl
[
23
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For every triple of integers
(d,D,m)
and
\beta\in(0,1]
, there exists
\alpha=\alpha(d,D,m,\beta)
such that the following holds. Let
G
be an
n
-vertex semialgrebraic graph of description complexity
(d,D,m)
. If
e(G)\geq\beta n^{2}
, then
G
contains a biclique of size
\alpha n
.

2.1
Vertical and horizontal segments

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a family of vertical line segments and let
\mathcal{B}
be a family of horizontal line segments in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, such that
|\mathcal{A}|=|\mathcal{B}|=n
. If the bipartite intersection graph of
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
is
K_{s,s}
-free, then it has
O(sn)
edges.

Proof.

After a small perturbation, we may assume that no two elements of
\mathcal{A}
intersect, and no two elements of
\mathcal{B}
intersect. Let
G
be the bipartite intersection graph of
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
, and let
d
be the average degree of
G
. By Theorem
1.9
, for any integer
k
,
G
either contains a
C_{4}
-free induced subgraph
G^{\prime}\subseteq G
with
d(G^{\prime})\geq k
, or a dense induced subgraph
G^{\prime}\subseteq G
on
d
vertices with
d(G^{\prime})\geq cd
, for some small constant
c=c(k)>0
.

The family of intersection graphs of horizontal and vertical segments is a subfamily of the family of string graphs. As no string graph contains any subdivision of the 1-subdivision of
K_{5}
as an induced subgraph (see e.g.
[
16
,
20
]
), the result of Kühn and Osthus
[
35
]
implies that any
C_{4}
-free intersection graph of vertical and horizontal segments has average degree at most
O(1)
. Hence, there exists an absolute constant
k
such that
G
contains no
C_{4}
-free induced sugbgraph of average degree at least
k
.

By Theorem
1.9
, there exists
c>0
such that
G
contains an induced subgraph
G^{\prime}
on
d
vertices with
d(G^{\prime})\geq cd
for some
c>0
. But observe that
G
and
G^{\prime}
are semialgebraic graphs. Indeed, a horizontal segment
s
with endpoints
(a,h)
and
(b,h)
can be represented by the point
(a,b,h)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, while a vertical segment
t
with endpoints
(v,x)
and
(v,y)
by
(v,x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Then
s
and
t
intersect if and only if the four inequalities
a-v\leq 0
,
v-b\leq 0
,
x-h\leq 0
, and
h-y\leq 0
are all satisfied. Therefore, we can apply Lemma
2.1
to conclude that there is some constant
\alpha>0
such that
G^{\prime}
contains an
\alpha d
sized biclique. Hence,
s\geq\alpha d
, giving the desired result.
∎

2.2
Points and halfspaces

A simple, yet interesting, class of geometric graphs are the incidence graphs of points and halfspaces in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The Zarankiewicz problem for this class of graphs was studied by Chan and Har-Peled in
[
8
]
, who showed that a
K_{s,s}
-free incidence graph of points and half-spaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
has at most
O(sn)
edges. In this section, we give a proof of this result using our graph-theoretic lemma.

Chan and Har-Peled also considered the case
d>3
, for which they proved that if
G
is a
K_{s,s}
-free incidence graph of
n
points and
n
halfspaces in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then
G
has
O_{s}(n^{2-2/(\lfloor d/2\rfloor+1)})
edges. A construction provided in their paper shows that for
d=5
, the resulting bound
O_{s}(n^{4/3})
is the best possible. Hence, in case
d\geq 5
, the family
\mathcal{F}_{d}
of incidence graphs of points and halfspaces in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is not degree-bounded, in contrast with the case
d\leq 3
. This left the case of dimension 4 open, which we resolve at the end of this section, by showing that
\mathcal{F}_{4}
is not degree-bounded. More precisely, we construct
C_{4}
-free point-halfspace incidence graphs in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
with at least
\Omega(n\log n/\log\log n)
egdes. We start with the case
d=3
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a set of
n
points and
\mathcal{H}
be a set of
n
halfspaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. If the incidence graph of
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{H}
is
K_{s,s}
-free, then there are at most
O(sn)
incidences.

As discussed in the introduction, in order to establish Theorem
2.3
, we need to show that the family of point-halfspace incidence graphs is degree-bounded and has the density-EH property. We first discuss the case of
C_{4}
-free incidence graphs.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a set of points and
\mathcal{H}
a set of halfspaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that the incidence graph of
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{H}
is
C_{4}
-free. Then the incidence graph of
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{H})
has at most
O(|\mathcal{P}|+|\mathcal{H}|)
edges.

Proof.

A halfspace
h
with equation
ax+by+cz\geq d
is
upwards-oriented
if
c>0
, and if
c<0
, we call
h

downwards-oriented
. By choosing a generic coordinate system, we ensure that all halfspaces are either upwards- or downwards-oriented. Due to symmetry, it suffices to bound the number of incidences of
\mathcal{P}
with the set
\mathcal{H}_{u}
of upwards-oriented halfspaces.

Given a subdivision of a graph
H
, call the vertices corresponding to the vertices of
H
as
root vertices
. The following claim is the key geometric input of the proof.

Claim 2.5
.

If
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
is a set of points and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is a set of upwards-oriented halfpsaces, then the incidence graph of
(\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
is not isomorphic to any subdivision of
K_{5}
, where all root vertices are contained in
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Define an auxiliary graph
\Gamma
on the vertex set
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
, where we connect two points if there is a halfspace in
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
containing both. Then,
\Gamma
contains a subdivision of
K_{5}
, and so it is not planar. In particular, if
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
denotes the projection map onto the
xy
plane, then there exists a pair of disjoint edges
p_{1}q_{1},p_{2}q_{2}\in E(\Gamma)
such that
\pi(\overline{p_{1}q_{1}})
and
\pi(\overline{p_{2}q_{2}})
intersect. Hence, there are points
r_{1}\in\overline{p_{1}q_{1}},r_{2}\in\overline{p_{2}q_{2}}
for which
\pi(r_{1})=\pi(r_{2})
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
r_{2}
lies above
r_{1}
. If
h\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
denotes the upwards-oriented halfspace containing
p_{1},q_{1}
, then
h
contains
r_{1}
, and thus
r_{2}
as well. Then,
h
must contain one of
p_{2}
and
q_{2}
, showing that
h
is incident to at least three points of
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
. Thus the incidence graph of
(\mathcal{P}^{\prime},\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
is not isomorphic to any subdivision of
K_{5}
, where all root vertices are contained in
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
, otherwise every element of
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
has degree 2.
∎

The above claim shows that the incidence graph of
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{H}_{u}
contains no induced subdivision of
K_{5}
with all root vertices in
\mathcal{P}
. Using duality, it is easy to conclude that the incidence graph of
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{H}_{u}
also contains no induced subdivisions of
K_{5}
, where all root verties are in
\mathcal{H}_{u}
.

Indeed, we may translate the whole configuration so that all points of
\mathcal{P}
have positive
z
coordinates and so that each halfspace
h\in\mathcal{H}_{u}
is represented by the equation
ax+by+cz\geq 1
, without changing the incidence graph. By applying a duality transformation, we can map each point
x\in\mathcal{P}
to the halfspace given by
\langle x,\cdot\rangle\geq 1
, and each halfspace given by
\langle\cdot,y\rangle\geq 1
to the point
y
. This duality turns all points of
\mathcal{P}
into upwards-oriented halfspaces, and the halfspaces of
\mathcal{H}_{u}
into points, while maintaining the incidence structure.

From this, we conclude that the incidence graph of
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{H}_{u})
contains no induced subdivision of
K_{9}
. Otherwise, there is either an induced subdivsion of
K_{5}
with all root vertices in
\mathcal{P}
, or all root vertices in
\mathcal{H}_{u}
. The proof now follows immediately from the result of Kühn and Osthus
[
35
]
, which states that the family of graph containing no induced subdivision of
K_{t}
for any integer
t
is degree-bounded.
∎

Next, we observe that the family of point-halfspace incidence graphs have the density-EH property. Such graphs are semialgebraic of complexity
(3,2,1)
, so this follows immediately from Lemma
2.1
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a set of at most
d
points and
\mathcal{H}
a set of at most
d
halfspaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, with at least
cd^{2}
incidences. Then the incidence graph of
(P,\mathcal{H})
contains
K_{s,s}
for some
s\geq\Omega_{c}(d)
.

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Let
G
be the incidence graph of
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{H}
, and assume for contradiction that the average degree of
G
is at least
d=d(G)\geq Cs
, for a large constant
C
. By Theorem
1.9
, for any integer
k
,
G
either contains a
C_{4}
-free induced subgraph
G^{\prime}\subseteq G
with
d(G^{\prime})\geq k
, or a dense induced subgraph
G^{\prime}\subseteq G
on
d
vertices with
d(G^{\prime})\geq cd
, for some
c=c(k)>0
.

If
k
is sufficiently large, Lemma
2.4
shows that no
C_{4}
-free incidence graph of points and halfspaces has average degree
k
. Thus, we must have the second outcome, i.e. there exists an induced subgraph
G^{\prime}\subseteq G
on
d
vertices with
e(G^{\prime})\geq cd^{2}
. From Lemma
2.6
, we get that
G^{\prime}
contains a biclique of size
\Omega(d)
, which is a contradiction to the assumption that
G
is
K_{s,s}
-free and
d(G)\geq Cs
for an appropriately chosen constant
C
.
∎

We conclude the section by showing that the family of point-halfspace incidence graphs in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
is not degree-bounded.

Claim 2.7
.

For every integer
n
, there exists a
C_{4}
-free incidence graph of
n
points and
n
halfspaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
with
\Omega(n\log n/\log\log n)
edges.

Proof sketch.

This proof is based on the construction of incidence graphs of points and axis-parallel rectangles in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
\Omega(n\log n/\log\log n)
edges, which is originally due to Chazelle
[
10
]
and was later developed in
[
5
,
8
,
50
]
. We argue that the rectangles can be replaced by their inscribed axis-aligned ellipses without losing many incidences. Finally, we use a lifting trick to transform this configuration into an incidence graph of points and halfspaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
.

The first step of our construction is to obtain a collection
\mathcal{R}
of axis-parallel rectangles of somewhat large area and comparatively small intersections. To do this, we pick a parameter
1\leq m\leq n
and set
u=(\log m)^{4}
and
k=\log m/\log u
, noting that
u^{k}=m
. Then, we choose
m
such that
km=n
. For
i=1,\dots,k
, let us tile the unit square
[0,1]^{2}
with
m=u^{k}
rectangles of size
u^{-i}\times u^{-(k-i)}
. If we denote by
\mathcal{R}_{i}
the collection of rectangles in this tiling and let
\mathcal{R}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{R}_{i}
, then we have
|\mathcal{R}|=mk=n
. Furthermore, let
\mathcal{E}
be the set of axis-parallel inscribed ellipses of the elements of
\mathcal{R}
. Note that each element of
\mathcal{R}
has area
u^{-k}=1/m
, and thus each element of
\mathcal{E}
has area
\pi/4m
. The key property of this collection is that the intersection of any two rectangles of
\mathcal{R}
has area at most
u^{-k-1}=1/um
, and the same is true for any two ellipses of
\mathcal{E}
.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{0}
be a set of
n
points chosen from the uniform distribution on
[0,1]^{2}
. Then each ellipse
E\in\mathcal{E}
contains
n\cdot\pi/4m=\pi k/4
elements of
\mathcal{P}
in expectation. Furthermore, note that if two points
p,p^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
are contained in two different ellipses of
\mathcal{E}
, then the smallest axis-parallel rectangle
R_{p,p^{\prime}}
containing
p
and
p^{\prime}
span a rectangle of area at most
1/um
. Therefore, given
p,p^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
, let us say that
p
and
p^{\prime}
are
close
if the area of the rectangle spanned by
p
and
p^{\prime}
is less than
1/um
. To bound the probability that two random points
p=(p_{x},p_{y})
and
p^{\prime}=(p^{\prime}_{x},p^{\prime}_{y})
are close, we fix
p
and bound the area of the set
\{(p_{x}^{\prime},p_{y}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|p_{x}^{\prime}-p_{x}|\cdot|p_{y}^{\prime}-p_{y}|\leq 1/um\}\cap[0,1]^{2}
(geometrically, this is the intersection of the unit square with the region bounded by two hyperbolas). By integrating, one can derive that the area of this region is
O(\log um/um)
, and so
\mathbb{P}(p\text{ and }p^{\prime}\text{ are close})=O(\log um/um)=O(1/n\log n)
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a set of points we get by removing a point of each close pair, which ensures that the resulting incidence graphs is
C_{4}
-free, and adding some points to
\mathcal{P}
outside of
[0,1]^{2}
to ensure that
\mathcal{P}
has
n
elements. A simple expectation argument now implies that the incidence graph
G
of
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{E})
has at least
\Omega(kn)=\Omega(n\log n/\log\log n)
edges, which completes the first step of our construction.

Let us now present the lifting trick which turns this point-ellipse incidence graph into a point-halfspace incidence graph in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. Each axis-parallel ellipse
E\in\mathcal{E}
is given by
\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:a(x-x_{0})^{2}+b(y-y_{0})^{2}\leq 1\}
for some
a,b,x_{0},y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, we can map
E
to the halfspace in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
given by

\pi(E)=\{(x,y,z,w)\in\mathbb{R}^{4}:ax-2ax_{0}y+bz-2by_{0}w\leq 1-ax_{0}^{2}-by_{0}^{2}\}.

Furthermore, we map every point
p=(t,u)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
to a point
\tau(p)=(t^{2},t,u^{2},u)\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
.
The maps
\pi
and
\tau
have the property that
p\in E
if and only if
\tau(p)\in\pi(E)
. Therefore, the incidence graph of the
n
points
\tau(\mathcal{P})
and
n
halfspaces
\pi(\mathcal{E})
is
C_{4}
-free with
\Omega(n\log n/\log\log n)
incidences, completing the proof.
∎

2.3
Intersection graphs of curves

In this section, we present the proofs of Theorems
1.1
,
1.3
,
1.4
,
1.5
. The proofs of Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
further assume Theorem

, which is proved in Section

. We summarize our statements as follows.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a family of
n
curves such that the intersection graph

of

is

-free.

(i)

G
has
O(s(\log s)n)
edges.

(ii)

If

is

has

(iii)

If every element of

is convex, then

has

edges.

(iv)

If

, where

and

are collections of disjoint curves, then

has

edges.

Proof.

String graphs do not contain any induced subdivision of the 1-subdivision of

. Therefore, by
[
35
]
, the family of string graphs is weakly degree-bounded.

(i)

In particular, there exists an absolute constant
k
such that any

-free string graph has average degree less than

. Let

be the constant promised by Theorem
1.9
, and let

be the average degree of

. Then

contains an induced subgraph

on

[

, there exists

-free, we get

, which gives the desired bound

.

(ii)

The family
\mathcal{F}_{k}
of intersection graphs of

-intersecting families of curves has the density-EH property by Theorem

. Thus Corollary

implies the desired result.

(iii)

The family

of intersection graphs of convex sets has the density-EH property by Theorem 15 in

. Therefore, we are done by Corollary

.

(iv)

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the family of graphs

that can be realized as the intersection graph of two families of disjoint curves. Then

.∎

3
Graph-theoretic lemma

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.12
, and use it to derive Theorem
1.9
. Recall, Theorem
1.12
states that for any integers
k,t\geq 1
and parameter
c\in(0,1)
, there exists a constant
C=C(k,c)
such that any
(c,t)
-sparse graph
G
of average degree
d(G)\geq Ct
contains a bipartite
C_{4}
-free induced subgraph of average degree at least
k
.

We recall that a graph
G
is
(c,t)
-sparse if for any two (not necessarily disjoint) subsets of the vertices
A,B\subset V(G)
with
|A|,|B|\geq t
, the number
e(A,B)
of pairs
(a,b)\in A\times B
joined by an edge is at most
e(A,B)\leq(1-c)|A||B|
. A simple averaging argument shows that in order to check whether a graph is
(c,t)
-sparse, it suffices to verify the above condition for every pair of sets
A,B\subset V(G)
with
|A|=|B|=t
.

We start by showing that Theorem
1.12
implies Theorem
1.9
. That is, for every
k\geq 1
and every graph
G
of average degree
d
, one can either find an induced
C_{4}
-free subgraph of average degree
k
in
G
or an induced subgraph on
d
vertices and
\Omega_{k}(d^{2})
edges.

Proof of Theorem
1.9
assuming Theorem
1.12
.

Let
c=1/2
, and let
C=C(k,c)
be the constant given by Theorem
1.12
. We may assume that
C>2
, so that if we set
t=\lfloor d/C\rfloor
, we have
t\leq d/2
. Theorem
1.12
then implies that
G
either contains a
C_{4}
-free induced subgraph of average degree at least
k
, or
G
is not
(c,t)
-sparse. In the first case, we are done, while if
G
is not
(c,t)
-sparse, then there exist
A,B\subset V(G)
,
|A|=|B|=t
such that
e(A,B)\geq(1-c)|A||B|=\frac{1}{2}t^{2}
. Let
U
be an arbitrary
d
-element subset of
V(G)
containing
A\cup B
. Then
e(G[U])\geq e(G[A\cup B])\geq\frac{1}{4}t^{2}\geq\frac{1}{8C^{2}}d^{2}
, showing that there exists an induced subgraph of
G
with
d
vertices and
\Omega_{k}(d^{2})
edges.
∎

The proof of Theorem
1.12
crucially relies on the following technical lemma. Given integers
v\geq h\geq 1
, the
1
-subdivision of
K_{v}^{(h)}
, denoted by
H_{v}^{(h)}
, is the bipartite graph with vertex classes
A
and
B
, where
|A|=v
,
|B|=\binom{v}{h}
, and for every
S\subset A
of size
h
there is a unique vertex
x_{S}\in B
whose neighbourhood is exactly
S
. Note that any bipartite graph
H=(A,B;E)
with
\deg a\leq h-1
for every
a\in A
is contained in
H_{v}^{(h)}
as an induced subgraph, where
v=|A|+|B|+h
. To see this, split the part of
H_{v}^{(h)}
containing
v
vertices into three parts
V_{A},V_{B},V_{h}
, whose sizes are
|A|,|B|
and
h
, respectively, and embed the vertices of
B
into
V_{B}
in an arbitrary way. Then, embed the vertices
a\in A
one by one, by choosing a vertex of
H_{v}^{(h)}
adjacent to exactly the neighbours of
a
among in
V_{B}
, and to an unused vertex in
V_{A}
(this is needed to ensure that the vertices
a\in A
with the exact same neighbourhood in
H
get embedded to distinct vertices in
H_{v}^{(h)}
), and potentially to some dummy vertices in
V_{h}
(to make its degree to
V_{A}\cup V_{B}\cup V_{h}
exactly
h
).

Lemma 3.1
.

For any integers
k,v,h\geq 1
and
c\in(0,1)
, there exists
K=K(k,v,h,c)
such that the following holds. Every
(c,t)
-sparse graph
G
of average degree
d(G)\geq Kt
which does not contain an induced copy of
H_{v}^{(h)}
must contain an induced
C_{4}
-free subgraph of average degree at least
k
.

We first prove Lemma
3.1
, and then show how it can be used to derive Theorem
1.12
. As this proof is the most involved part of this section, we give a brief outline. We proceed in four steps, by finding induced subgraphs
G\supset G_{1}\supset G_{2}\supset G_{3}\supset G_{4}
with increasingly stronger properties.

In the first step (Lemma
3.2
), we pass to an induced subgraph
G_{1}\subset G
with a partition
V(G_{1})=A_{1}\cup B_{1}
such that
|A_{1}|\geq|B_{1}|/2
,
G_{1}
is
d
-degenerate for some
d\geq d(G)
,
d_{G_{1}}(a)\leq 4d
for every
a\in A_{1}
, and every
a\in A_{1}
has at least
d/4
neighbours in
B_{1}
.

The main new contribution lies in the second step (Lemma
3.4
). By random sampling, we find sets
A_{2}\subseteq A_{1}
and
B_{2}\subseteq B_{1}
, forming the graph
G_{2}=G_{1}[A_{2}\cup B_{2}]
, which does not contain any copy of
K_{2,v+1}
in which the vertex class with two vertices lies in
A_{2}
, and the vertex class with
v+1
vertices lies in
B_{2}
. Beyond that, we ensure some further useful properties in
G_{2}
(e.g., there will be no triangles in
G_{2}
containing vertices of both
A_{2}
and
B_{2}
).

So far, we found a subgraph of
G
with no copies of
K_{2,v+1}
as described above. However, the graph
G_{2}
may still contain other copies of
K_{2,v+1}
, for example those fully contained in
A_{2}
. To eliminate all remaining copies of
K_{2,v+1}
, in the third step (Lemma
3.7
) we show that there exists a bipartite induced subgraph
G_{3}\subset G_{2}
with high average degree, whose vertex partition
V(G_{3})=A_{3}\cup B_{3}
satisfies
A_{3}\subseteq A_{2},B_{3}\subseteq B_{2}
. The ideas used in this step come from the work of Kwan, Letzter, Sudakov and Tran
[
36
]
, who showed that any triangle-free graph of average degree
d
contains an induced bipartite subgraph of average degree
\Omega(\log d/\log\log d)
(see also the nice write-up of Glock
[
28
]
). Note that the result of this step is an induced
K_{v+1,v+1}
-free subgraph
G_{3}\subset G_{2}
of high average degree.

In the fourth step, we use a theorem of Du, Hunter, Girão, McCarty, and Scott
[
14
]
which states that for every
k
and
v
, every
K_{v+1,v+1}
-free graph of average degree
k^{C(v+1)^{3}}
contains a
C_{4}
-free subgraph of average degree
k
, where
C
is some absolute constant (Theorem
3.8
). A less quantitative version is proved earlier by McCarty
[
41
]
, and a better dependence on
v
for fixed
k
was obtained by Hunter and Girão
[
27
]
. This theorem can be applied directly to the graph
G_{3}
, thus guaranteeing an induced subgraph
G_{4}\subseteq G
, completing the proof.

Lemma 3.2
.

For every graph
G
, there is a real number
d\geq d(G)
and an induced subgraph
G_{1}\subseteq G
with the vertex set
V(G_{1})=A_{1}\cup B_{1}
such that
|A_{1}|\geq|B_{1}|/2
,
G_{1}
is
d
-degenerate, and every
a\in A_{1}
has
\deg(a)\leq 4d
and at least
d/4
neighbours in
B_{1}
.

Proof.

Let
G_{0}
be an induced subgraph of
G
of maximum average degree
d
. Then
G_{0}
is
d
-degenerate, and it has minimum degree
d/2
. Otherwise, removing a vertex of degree less than
d/2
gives a graph of larger average degree, contradicting the maximality of
G_{0}
. Let
V(G_{0})=A_{0}\cup B_{0}
be a partition that maximizes the number of edges in a cut, and assume that
|A_{0}|\geq|B_{0}|
.

Let
A^{\prime}\subset A_{0}
be the set of vertices
a
of degree more than
4d
. Then
2d|A^{\prime}|\leq e(G_{0})=(|A_{0}|+|B_{0}|)d/2\leq|A_{0}|d
, which gives
|A^{\prime}|\leq|A_{0}|/2
. Thus, if we set
A_{1}=A_{0}\setminus A^{\prime}
and
B_{1}=B_{0}
, then we have
|A_{1}|\geq|A_{0}|/2\geq|B_{1}|/2
and
d_{G_{0}}(a)\leq 4d
for all
a\in A_{1}
.
On the other hand, using that
A_{0},B_{0}
maximizes the number of edges in a cut, we have
|B_{0}\cap N(a)|\geq d_{G_{0}}(a)/2\geq d/4
for every
a\in A_{0}
. Otherwise, the cut
(A_{0}\setminus\{a\},B_{0}\cup\{a\})
has more edges than the cut
(A_{0},B_{0})
. Therefore, the induced subgraph
G_{1}=G[A_{1}\cup B_{1}]
with partition
A_{1}\cup B_{1}
satisfies all conclusions of the lemma and the proof is complete.
∎

In the second step, we use the following lemma, proved by Ding, Gao, Liu, Luan, and Sun
[
13
]
.

Lemma 3.3

(Lemma 3.1 in
[
13
]
.)
.

Let
H
be a bipartite graph. For any
c,\varepsilon>0
there exists
\beta=\beta(c,H,\varepsilon)
such that every
(c,t)
-sparse graph
G
which does not contain an induced copy of
H
is also
(1-\varepsilon,\beta t)
-sparse.

Lemma 3.4
.

Given integers
\tau,v,h\geq 0
and a real number
c>0
, there exists a constant
K=K(\tau,v,h,c)
such that for every
d\geq Kt
the following statement is true.
Let
G_{1}
be a graph satisfying the conclusions of Lemma
3.2
, i.e. a
d
-degenerate
(c,t)
-sparse graph on the vertex set
A_{1}\cup B_{1}
such that
|A_{1}|\geq|B_{1}|/2
, and
|N(a)|\leq 4d
,
|N(a)\cap B_{1}|\geq d/4
for every
a\in A_{1}
.

If
G_{1}
contains no induced copy of
H_{v}^{(h)}
, there exist subsets
A_{2}\subseteq A_{1}
and
B_{2}\subseteq B_{1}
such that the graph
G_{2}=G_{1}[A_{2}\cup B_{2}]
is
200\tau
-degenerate and
e(A_{2},B_{2})\geq\tau(|A_{2}|+|B_{2}|)
. Additionally, there is no triangle with vertices in both
A_{2}
and
B_{2}
, and there are no copies of
K_{2,v+1}
with the vertex class of two vertices in
A_{2}
, and the other vertex class in
B_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be a sufficiently small parameter with respect to
\tau,v,h
(
\varepsilon\ll\tau^{-3v},v^{-3h}
suffices). Applying Lemma
3.3
, there exists
\beta=\beta(\varepsilon,c,H)
such that
G_{1}
is
(1-\varepsilon,\beta t)
-sparse, and we set
t_{0}=\beta t
. Finally, we choose
K
such that
K\geq 4\binom{v}{h}\beta/\varepsilon
. In particular, we hav
\varepsilon d\geq 4\binom{v}{h}\beta t
.

We obtain the sets
A_{2},B_{2}
by randomly sampling subsets of
A_{1},B_{1}
and appropriately cleaning them to remove triangles and copies of
K_{2,v+1}
. However, in order to be able to analyze this random process, we need two short preliminary observations. The first observation is a simple consequence of the local sparsity condition, and it is in fact the only place in the proof where this condition is used. For a vertex
a\in A_{1}
, let
S_{a}:=\{v\in V(G_{1}):|N(a)\cap N(v)|>4\varepsilon d\}
be the set of vertices which share many common neighbours with
a
. Then, we must have
|S_{a}|<t_{0}
, as otherwise, the number of edges between
N(a)
and
S_{a}
is at least
4\varepsilon d|S_{a}|>\varepsilon|N(a)||S_{a}|
, since
|N(a)|\leq 4d
. This contradicts the fact that
G_{1}
is
(1-\varepsilon,t_{0})
-sparse (since
|N(a)|\geq d/4\geq t_{0}
for all
a\in A_{1}
). Thus, as
d/4\geq Kt/4\geq\beta t/\varepsilon
, we have
|S_{a}|<t_{0}=\beta t\leq\varepsilon d
.

The second observation is that very few independet sets
S\subset N(a)
of size
v
have at least
2\varepsilon d
common neighbours in
A_{1}
.

Claim 3.5
.

For any vertex
a\in A_{1}
, there are at most
\varepsilon^{1/2}\binom{|N(a)|}{v}
subsets
S\subset N(a)\cap B_{1}
of size
v
which are independent and have at least
2\varepsilon d
common neighbours in
A_{1}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{a}
be an auxiliary
h
-uniform hypergraph on the vertex set
N(a)\cap B_{1}
, in which an
h
-element set
e\subset N(a)\cap B_{1}
is an edge if
e
is an independent set and
|\bigcap_{b\in e}N(b)\cap A_{1}|\geq 2\varepsilon d
. Note that every independent set
S\subset N(a)
with at least
2\varepsilon d
common neighbours is a
v
-clique in
\mathcal{H}_{a}
, and therefore it suffices to bound the number of
v
-cliques in
\mathcal{H}_{a}
. Thus, let us select
S
uniformly at random among
v
-element subsets of
N(a)
, and bound the probability that it induces a clique in
\mathcal{H}_{a}
.

For an edge
e\in E(\mathcal{H}_{a})
, let
Z_{e}=(\bigcap_{b\in e}N(b)\cap A_{1})\setminus S_{a}
, so
Z_{e}
is the set of common neighbours of
e
whose codegree with
a
is at most
4\varepsilon d
. As
|\bigcap_{b\in e}N(b)\cap A_{1}|\geq 2\varepsilon d
and
|S_{a}|\leq\varepsilon d
, we have
|Z_{e}|\geq\varepsilon d
for all
e
. Also, let
Z_{e}^{*}
be the set of those vertices
x\in Z_{e}
which have
e\subsetneq N(x)\cap S
, and let
\mathcal{E}_{e}
be the event that
|Z^{*}_{e}|\geq|Z_{e}|/2
. Observe that
\mathcal{E}_{e}
does not happen if
e\not\subset S
, i.e.
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{e}|e\not\subset S]=0
.

On the other hand, for
e\in E(\mathcal{H}_{a})
, we claim
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{e}|e\subset S]\leq 64\varepsilon v
. Indeed, conditioning on the event
e\subset S
, we can write
S=e\cup S^{\prime}
for a random
v-h
element set
S^{\prime}
chosen from the uniform distribution on
N(a)\cap B_{1}\setminus e
. For each
x\in Z_{e}
, we have that

\mathbb{P}\big[x\in Z_{e}^{*}\big|e\subset S\big]=\mathbb{P}\big[N(x)\cap S^{\prime}\neq\varnothing\big]\leq(v-h)\frac{|N(x)\cap N(a)\cap B_{1}|}{|N(a)\cap B_{1}|-v}\leq v\frac{4\varepsilon d}{d/8}\leq 32v\varepsilon,

from which
\mathbb{E}\big[|Z_{e}^{*}|\big|e\subset S\big]\leq 32v\varepsilon|Z_{e}|
. Therefore, we get

\mathbb{P}\big[\mathcal{E}_{e}\big|e\subset S\big]=\mathbb{P}\big[|Z_{e}^{*}|\geq|Z_{e}|/2\big|e\subset S\big]\leq\mathbb{P}\Bigg[Z_{e}^{*}\geq\frac{\mathbb{E}[Z_{e}^{*}|e\subset S]}{64v\varepsilon}\Bigg|e\subset S\Bigg]\leq 64v\varepsilon,

where the last inequality is due to Markov’s inequality. Hence, by the union bound,

\mathbb{P}\Big[\bigvee_{e\in E(\mathcal{H}_{a})}\mathcal{E}_{e}\Big]\leq\sum_{e\in E(\mathcal{H}_{a})}\mathbb{P}\big[\mathcal{E}_{e}\big|e\subset S\big]\mathbb{P}[e\subset S]\leq 64v\varepsilon\sum_{e\in E(\mathcal{H}_{a})}\mathbb{P}[e\subset S]\leq 64v\varepsilon\cdot\binom{|S|}{h}\leq 64\varepsilon v^{h+1},

where we used the linearity of expectation to say that
\sum_{e\in E(\mathcal{H}_{a})}\mathbb{P}[e\subset S]
is the expected number of
h
-element subsets of
S
, that is
\binom{|S|}{h}
.

We also claim that if
S
induces a clique in
\mathcal{H}_{a}
, then at least one of the events
\mathcal{E}_{e}
holds for some
e\in\mathcal{H}_{a}
. This suffices to complete the proof, since it implies that the probability that
S
is a clique in
\mathcal{H}_{a}
is at most
64\varepsilon v^{h+1}\leq\varepsilon^{1/2}
.

Assume that for some
S
no event
\mathcal{E}_{e}
holds, then we will find an induced copy of
H_{v}^{(h)}
in
G_{1}
. As
S
induces a clique in
\mathcal{H}_{a}
,
S
is an independent set, and we can embed the side of
H_{v}^{(h)}
of size
v
into
S
in an arbitrary way. Then, for every
h
-element set
e\subset S
, we choose a vertex
x_{e}\in Z_{e}\backslash Z_{e}^{*}
, that is, a vertex with the property that
N(x_{e})\cap S=e
.
Since
\mathcal{E}_{e}
does not hold, we have
|Z_{e}\backslash Z_{e}^{*}|\geq|Z_{e}|/2\geq\varepsilon d/2
for all
e\subset S
, and thus we can fix some set
T_{e}\subseteq Z_{e}\backslash Z_{e}^{*}
of size exactly
\varepsilon d/2
. Next, we find a vertex
x_{e}\in T_{e}
for every edge
e
such that
\{x_{e}\}_{e\in\binom{S}{h}}
is an independent set. This gives an induced copy of
H_{v}^{(h)}
, finishing the proof.

To find such an independent set, we begin by eliminating from
T_{e}
all vertices
x
such that
|N(x)\cap T_{e^{\prime}}|>\varepsilon|T_{e^{\prime}}|
for some
e^{\prime}\subset S
. Since
G_{1}
is a
(1-\varepsilon,t_{0})
-sparse graph, for each
e^{\prime}
there are at most
t_{0}
such vertices
x\in T_{e}
. Let
R_{e}
denote the set of remaining vertices in
T_{e}
after these deletions, for which we have
|R_{e}|\geq|T_{e}|-\binom{v}{h}t_{0}\geq\varepsilon d/2-\binom{v}{h}\beta t\geq\varepsilon d/2-\varepsilon d/4=|T_{e}|/2
. Now, we greedily pick vertices
x_{e}\in R_{e}
one by one, noting that at each step the previously chosen vertices can be adjacent to at most
\binom{v}{h}\cdot\varepsilon|T_{e}|<|T_{e}|/2\leq|R_{e}|
vertices in
R_{e}
, thus showing that there is always at least one choice for
x_{e}
not adjacent to any of the previously chosen vertices. But then
S
together with
\{v_{e}:e\in\binom{S}{h}\}
induces a copy of
H_{v}^{(h)}
in
G_{1}
. This contradicts the assumption that no such copy exists in
G_{1}
.
∎

With this claim, we can now define
A_{2},B_{2}
. First, let
A^{\prime}\subset A_{1},B^{\prime}\subset B_{1}
be random subsets in which each element is included independently with probability
p=30\tau/d
. Since the graph
G_{1}
is
d
-degenerate, it has an orientation
X
in which every vertex has outdegree at most
d
. To obtain
B_{2}
, we delete from
B^{\prime}
all vertices
b
with
|N^{+}_{X}(b)\cap(A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime})|\geq 200\tau
. Similarly, to obtain
A_{2}
, we delete from
A^{\prime}
all vertices
a
which satisfy one of the following three conditions:

(i)

|N^{+}_{X}(a)\cap(A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime})|\geq 200\tau
,

(ii)

a
belongs to some triangle with vertices in
A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime}
,

(iii)

for some
a^{\prime}\in A^{\prime}\backslash\{a\}
, the set
N(a)\cap N(a^{\prime})\cap B^{\prime}
contains an independent set of size
v+1
.

Observe that the sets
A_{2}
and
B_{2}
defined as above satisfy all necessary conditions, except perhaps
e(A_{2},B_{2})\geq\tau(|A_{2}|+|B_{2}|)
. Namely, since
X
is an orientation of
G_{2}=G_{1}[A_{2}\cup B_{2}]
in which every vertex has outdegree at most
200\tau
, the graph
G_{2}
is
200\tau
-degenerate. Also, since all vertices of
A^{\prime}
contained in some triangle were deleted, there do not exist any triangles with vertices in both
A_{2}
and
B_{2}
. Finally, observe that due to the lack of triangles, any copy of
K_{2,v+1}
with the vertex class with two vertices in
A_{2}
and the other vertex class in
B_{2}
must be induced. But
N(a)\cap N(a^{\prime})\cap B_{2}
does not contain an independent set of size
v+1
for any

, so
G_{2}
does not contain any copies of
K_{2,v+1}
.

To give a lower bound on the number of edges between
A_{2}
and
B_{2}
, we use the following claim.

Claim 3.6
.

If
a\in A_{1}
and
b\in B_{1}
are adjacent vertices such that
|N(a)\cap N(b)|\leq 4\varepsilon d
(so
b\notin S_{a}
), then
\mathbb{P}[a\in A_{2},b\in B_{2}]\geq p^{2}/2
.

Note that this claim is sufficient to complete the proof, since for each
a\in A_{1}
, there are at least
|N(a)\cap B_{1}|-|S_{a}|\geq d/4-\varepsilon d\geq d/5
vertices
b
for which the claim applies. This allows us to lower bound the expected number of edges between
A_{2}
and
B_{2}
as follows:

\mathbb{E}\big[e(A_{2},B_{2})\big]\geq|A_{1}|\cdot\frac{d}{5}\cdot\frac{p^{2}}{2}\geq\Big(\frac{|A_{1}|}{3}+\frac{|B_{1}|}{3}\Big)\cdot\frac{d}{5}\cdot\frac{30\tau}{2d}p\geq\tau\big(|A_{1}|+|B_{1}|\big)p\geq\tau\big(\mathbb{E}\big[|A^{\prime}|\big]+\mathbb{E}\big[|B^{\prime}|\big]\big),

where we used that
|A_{1}|=|A_{1}|/3+2|A_{1}|/3\geq|A_{1}|/3+|B_{1}|/3
since
|A_{1}|\geq|B_{1}|/2
.

As
|A_{2}|\leq|A^{\prime}|
and
|B_{2}|\leq|B^{\prime}|
, we also have that
\mathbb{E}\big[e(A_{2},B_{2})\big]\geq\tau(\mathbb{E}[|A_{2}|]+\mathbb{E}[|B_{2}|])
. Therefore, there exists an outcome in which
e(A_{2},B_{2})\geq\tau(|A_{2}|+|B_{2}|)
, which is precisely what we wanted. Thus, to complete the proof, it remains only to prove Claim
3.6
.

Proof of Claim
3.6
.

For
a,b
to be in the sets
A_{2},B_{2}
, respectively, it is necessary to have
a\in A^{\prime}
and
b\in B^{\prime}
. These events are independent and each happens with probability
p
. Thus, the event
E
that both
a\in A^{\prime}
and
b\in B^{\prime}
has probability
p^{2}
. In the rest of the proof, we show that, conditioning on the event
E
, we have
\mathbb{P}[a\notin A_{2}|E]\leq 1/3
and
\mathbb{P}[b\notin B_{2}|E]\leq 1/6
. This is sufficient to complete the proof, since a union bound then implies that
\mathbb{P}\big[a\in A_{2},b\in B_{2}\big|E\big]\geq 1/2
, and so we have

\mathbb{P}[a\in A_{2},b\in B_{2}]=\mathbb{P}\big[a\in A_{2},b\in B_{2}\big|E\big]\cdot\mathbb{P}[E]\geq p^{2}/2.

We begin by showing
\mathbb{P}[b\notin B_{2}|E]\leq 1/6
. Given that
a\in A^{\prime},b\in B^{\prime}
, the probability that
b\notin B_{2}
is the same as the probability of the event
|N^{+}_{X}(b)\cap(A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime}\cup\{a\})|\geq 200\tau
. In other words,
b
is deleted from
B^{\prime}
only if
|(N^{+}_{X}(b)\backslash\{a\})\cap(A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime})|\geq 199\tau
. Thus, by Markov’s inequality we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big[b\notin B_{2}\big|E\big]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\big[\big|(N^{+}_{X}(b)\backslash\{a\})\cap(A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime})\big|\geq 199\tau\big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{199\tau}\mathbb{E}\big[|N^{+}_{X}(b)\cap(A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime})|\big]=\frac{p|N^{+}_{X}(b)|}{199\tau}=\frac{30\frac{\tau}{d}d}{199\tau}\leq\frac{1}{6}.

We now show that
\mathbb{P}[a\notin A_{2}|E]\leq 1/3
. By the above argument, the probability that
a
was deleted from
A^{\prime}
due to the condition
(i)
is at most
1/6
. The probability that
a
is deleted from
A^{\prime}
due to the condition
(ii)
is at most
1/12
. Indeed, given that
a\in A^{\prime},b\in B^{\prime}
, the probability
a
is in a triangle of
A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime}
which contains
b
is at most
p|N(a)\cap N(b)|\leq 4\varepsilon d\cdot 30\tau/d\leq 120\varepsilon\tau
. Also, the probability that
a
is in a triangle which does not include
b
is at most
e(G_{1}[N(a)])\cdot p^{2}\leq\varepsilon|N(a)|^{2}\cdot 30^{2}\tau^{2}/d^{2}\leq 1000\varepsilon\tau^{2}\cdot(4d)^{2}/d^{2}\leq 2\cdot 10^{4}\varepsilon\tau^{2}
. Note that
e(G_{1}[N(a)])\leq\varepsilon|N(a)|^{2}
follows from
(1-\varepsilon,\beta t)
-sparsity. In total, the probability that
a^{\prime}
is deleted due to
(ii)
is at most
120\varepsilon\tau+2\cdot 10^{4}\varepsilon\tau^{2}\leq 1/12
, using that
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small compared to
\tau
.

As the last step, we show that the probability
a
is deleted due to condition
(iii)
is at most
1/12
. In other words, we want to bound the probability that there is a set
S\subseteq N(a)
of size
v+1
such that
S\subseteq B^{\prime}
and
\bigcap_{b\in S}N(b)
contains a vertex
a^{\prime}\in A^{\prime}\backslash\{a\}
. To do this, we use a union bound over all independent
(v+1)
-sets
S\subset N(a)
. We call an independent set
S

rich
if has at least
2\varepsilon d
common neighbours in
A_{1}
and
poor
otherwise.

By Claim
3.5
, there are at most
\varepsilon^{1/2}\binom{|N(a)|}{v}
rich sets
S
containing
b
, since
S\backslash\{b\}
is an independent set of size
v
with
2\varepsilon d
common neighbours. Similarly, there are at most
\varepsilon^{1/2}\binom{|N(a)|}{v}\cdot|N(a)|
rich sets in
N(a)
overall, since each such set is obtained by adding an element to a
v
-set with at least
2\varepsilon d
common neighbours. Thus, the probability that any rich set in included in
B^{\prime}
is at most

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[B^{\prime}\text{ contains a rich set }

\displaystyle S|E]\leq\!\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset N(a)\\
S\text{ rich, }b\notin S\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!\mathbb{P}[S\subseteq B^{\prime}|E]+\!\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset N(a)\\
S\text{ rich, }b\in S\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!\mathbb{P}[S\subseteq B^{\prime}|E]

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{1/2}|N(v)|^{v+1}p^{v+1}+\varepsilon^{1/2}|N(v)|^{v}p^{v}\leq\varepsilon^{1/2}(4d)^{v}\Big(\frac{30\tau}{d}\Big)^{v}\Big(4d\frac{30\tau}{d}+1\Big)\leq\frac{1}{24},

since
\varepsilon
is chosen to be small enough as a function of
\tau
and
v
.

Furthermore, note that if
S
is poor, the probability that some
a^{\prime}\in A_{1}\backslash\{a\}\cap\bigcap_{b\in S}N(b)
is selected into
A^{\prime}
is at most
2\varepsilon d\cdot p\leq 2\varepsilon\cdot 30\tau\leq\varepsilon^{1/2}
. Thus, using similar estimates as above,

\mathbb{P}\Big[\text{there is a poor $S\subset B$}\text{ and $a^{\prime}\in A^{\prime}\backslash\{a\}\cap\bigcap_{b\in S}N(b)$}\Big|E\Big]\\
\leq\!\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset N(a)\\
S\text{ poor, }b\notin S\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!\mathbb{P}[S\subset B^{\prime}|E]\varepsilon^{1/2}+\!\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset N(a)\\
S\text{ poor, }b\in S\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!\mathbb{P}[S\subset B^{\prime}|E]\varepsilon^{1/2}\leq\varepsilon^{1/2}|N(v)|^{v+1}p^{v+1}+\varepsilon^{1/2}|N(v)|^{v}p^{v}\leq\frac{1}{24}.

Thus, the probability that
a
is deleted from
A^{\prime}
due to the condition
(iii)
is at most
2/24=1/12
, completing the proof of Claim
3.6
, and thus completing the second step as a whole.
∎

∎

Finally, to perform the third step, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\tau
be a positive integer. Suppose
G_{2}
is a
200\tau
-degenerate graph with the vertex set
V(G_{2})=A_{2}\cup B_{2}
such that
e(A_{2},B_{2})\geq\tau(|A_{2}|+|B_{2}|)
and there is no triangle with vertices in both
A_{2}
and
B_{2}
. Then, there are independent sets
A_{3}\subseteq A_{2}
,
B_{3}\subseteq B_{2}
such that
G_{2}[A_{3}\cup B_{3}]
has average degree at least
\Omega(\log\tau/\log\log\tau)
.

Proof.

We may assume that each vertex of the bipartite graph between
A_{2}
and
B_{2}
has degree at least
\tau
. Otherwise, removing vertices with degree less than
\tau
results in a non-empty subgraph which still satisfies the conditions of the theorem. Furthermore, assume that
|A_{2}|\geq|B_{2}|
.

Let
\ell
be the largest integer satisfying
(200\ell^{2})^{\ell}\leq\tau
, so
\ell=\Theta(\log\tau/\log\log\tau)
. Let
B^{\prime}\subseteq B_{2}
, where each vertex of
B_{2}
is included independently with probability
p=\frac{1}{200\tau\ell}
. Since
G_{2}[B_{2}]
is a
200\tau
-degenerate graph, there exists an orientation of this graph, denoted by
X
, in which the outdegree of every vertex
b\in B_{2}
is at most
200\tau
. Given the set
B^{\prime}
, we define
B_{3}
to be the set of vertices
b\in B^{\prime}
satisfying
|B^{\prime}\cap N^{+}_{X}(b)|=0
. By construction,
B_{3}
is an independent set. Furthermore, associate to every
a\in A_{2}
an arbitrary set
N_{a}\subseteq N(a)\cap B_{2}
of size
\tau
. Then, we define
A^{\prime}
to be the set of vertices
a\in A_{2}
for which
|N_{a}\cap B_{3}|=\ell
.

First, we show that
\mathbb{E}\big[|A^{\prime}|\big]\geq\binom{\tau}{\ell}\cdot p^{\ell}/4\cdot|A_{2}|
. For every
a\in A_{2}
, there are
\binom{\tau}{\ell}
sets of size
\ell
in
N_{a}
, and they are all independent sets since there are no triangles containing vertices of both
A_{2}
and
B_{2}
. For each such set
I
, we will show in the next paragraph that
I=N_{a}\cap B_{3}
with probability at least
p^{\ell}/4
. The desired inequality follows by the linearity of expectation. For simplicity, we write
q=\binom{\tau}{\ell}\cdot p^{\ell}
.

For an arbitrary independent set
I\subseteq N_{a}
of size
\ell
, we have
I\subseteq B_{3}
precisely when
I\subseteq B^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}\cap\bigcup_{b\in I}N_{X}^{+}(b)=0
, where the second condition ensures that no vertex of
I
is deleted from
B^{\prime}
. Since
\Big|\bigcup_{b\in I}N_{X}^{+}(b)\Big|\leq 200\tau|I|=200\tau\ell
and each vertex is included in
B^{\prime}
randomly and independently with probability
p
, we have that

\mathbb{P}[I\subset B_{3}]\geq p^{\ell}(1-p)^{200\tau\ell}\geq p^{\ell}\Big(1-\frac{1}{200\tau\ell}\Big)^{200\tau\ell}\geq p^{\ell}/e\geq\frac{1}{3}p^{\ell}.

Hence,

\mathbb{P}[I=N_{a}\cap B_{3}]\geq\mathbb{P}[I\subseteq B_{3}]-\mathbb{P}[I\subsetneq N_{a}\cap B_{3}]\geq\frac{1}{3}p^{\ell}-\tau p^{\ell+1}\geq\frac{1}{4}p^{\ell}.

Here, the inequality
\mathbb{P}[I\subsetneq N_{a}\cap B_{3}]\leq\mathbb{P}[I\subsetneq N_{a}\cap B^{\prime}]\leq\tau p^{\ell+1}
follows as
|N_{a}|=\tau
, so by the union bound, the probability that
B^{\prime}
contains
I
and an additional element of
N_{a}
is at most
\tau p^{\ell+1}
.

The next step is to show
\mathbb{E}\big[e(G_{2}[A^{\prime}])\big]\leq 10^{3}q\tau\mathbb{E}\big[|A^{\prime}|\big]
. If
A^{\prime\prime}
is the set of vertices
a\in A_{2}
with
|N_{a}\cap B^{\prime}|\geq\ell
, then
A^{\prime}\subseteq A^{\prime\prime}
since every vertex of
A^{\prime}
has
\ell=|N_{a}\cap B_{3}|\leq|N_{a}\cap B^{\prime}|
. In order to bound
e(G_{2}[A^{\prime\prime}])
, we pick two adjacent vertices
a,a^{\prime}\in A_{2}
, and observe that they do not have any common neighbours in
B_{2}
, since
N(b)\cap A_{2}
is an independent set for any
b\in B_{2}
. In particular,
N_{a}\cap N_{a^{\prime}}=\varnothing
and so the events
a\in A^{\prime\prime}
and
a^{\prime}\in A^{\prime\prime}
are independent. Thus, the probability that
a,a^{\prime}\in A^{\prime\prime}
is at most
\mathbb{P}[a,a^{\prime}\in A^{\prime\prime}]=\mathbb{P}\big[|B^{\prime}\cap N_{a}|\geq\ell\big]\cdot\mathbb{P}\big[|B^{\prime}\cap N_{a^{\prime}}|\geq\ell\big]\leq\binom{\tau}{\ell}^{2}p^{2\ell}=q^{2}
.

Furthermore, since
G_{2}
is
200\tau
-degenerate,
A_{2}
spans at most
200\tau|A_{2}|
edges, and so

\mathbb{E}\big[e(G_{2}[A^{\prime}])\big]\leq\mathbb{E}\big[e(G_{2}[A^{\prime\prime}])\big]\leq q^{2}e(G_{2}[A_{2}])\leq 200q^{2}\tau|A_{2}|\leq 800q\tau\mathbb{E}[|A^{\prime}|],

where we used
q|A_{2}|\leq 4\mathbb{E}[|A^{\prime}|]
. We are now essentially done. Observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|A^{\prime}|-\frac{e(G_{2}[A^{\prime}])}{10^{4}q\tau}-10^{4}q\tau|B_{3}|\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\big[|A^{\prime}|\big]-10^{4}q\tau\mathbb{E}\big[|B_{3}|\big]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{q}{8}|A_{2}|-10^{4}q\tau p|B_{2}|\geq\frac{q}{8}|A_{2}|-\frac{10^{4}q}{200\ell}|B_{2}|>0.

Hence, there is an outcome of the random process in which
10^{4}q\tau|A^{\prime}|\geq e(G_{2}[A^{\prime}])
and
|A^{\prime}|>10^{4}q\tau|B_{3}|
. Furthermore, since
q\tau=p^{\ell}\binom{\tau}{\ell}\tau\geq\Big(\frac{1}{200\tau\ell}\Big)^{\ell}\Big(\frac{\tau}{\ell}\Big)^{\ell}\tau=\frac{\tau}{(200\ell^{2})^{\ell}}\geq 1
, we can find an independent set
A_{3}\subseteq A^{\prime}
of size
|A_{3}|\geq|A^{\prime}|/10^{4}q\tau>|B_{3}|
. Coupled with the fact that every vertex of
A_{3}
has exactly
\ell
neighbours in
B_{3}
, we have that the average degree of
G_{2}[A_{3}\cup B_{3}]
is at least
\frac{2e(A_{3},B_{3})}{|A_{3}|+|B_{3}|}\geq\frac{2\ell|A_{3}|}{2|A_{3}|}=\ell
. This completes the proof.
∎

In the fourth step, we use the following result of Du, Hunter, Girão, McCarty, and Scott

Let

4
Families of curves with bounded complexity

4.1
Dense intersection graphs of
k
-intersecting families

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.2
, which states that if
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
are two
k
-intersecting families of curves of size
n
, such that at least
cn^{2}
pairs
(a,b)\in\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{B}
intersect, then there are subfamilies
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
such that all strings
a\in\mathcal{A}^{\prime},b\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
intersect. Also, recall that we say
\mathcal{A}
is a
k
-intersecting family of curves if any two (distinct) curves
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
have at most
k
points of intersection.

We make the following standard non-degeneracy assumptions on our curves
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
: for any
a\in A,b\in B
, the set
a\cap b
is finite, for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A,b\in B
, the set
a_{1}\cap a_{2}\cap b
is empty and all curves of
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
are non-self-intersecting and smooth. Further, we assume that there are no tangencies between the curves, i.e. that when curves
a
and
b
cross, the curve
a
passes from one side of
b
to the other. These non-degeneracy assumptions can be achieved using standard arguments by perturbing the curves from the family slightly (and potentially slightly increasing
k
). We also give a direction to each curve, so that it has a starting point and an ending point, and we order the points of the curve accordingly.

Before jumping into the proof, we establish some notation and give a few preliminary observations. Let
G
be the bipartite intersection graph of
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
, which has vertex classes of size
n
and at least
cn^{2}
edges. Remove all vertices of
G
with less than
cn/4
neighbours, and let
G^{\prime}
be the resulting graph. Fewer than
cn^{2}/2
edges are removed by this, and so
e(G^{\prime})\geq cn^{2}/2
. Let
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{B}
be the vertex classes of
G^{\prime}
, and let
c^{\prime}=c/4
. Then
G^{\prime}
has minimum degree at least
c^{\prime}n
, which also implies
|\mathcal{A}^{\prime}|,|\mathcal{B}^{\prime}|\geq c^{\prime}n
. Let
n^{\prime}=c^{\prime}n
, and let
\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}
be random
n^{\prime}
sized subsets of
\mathcal{A}^{\prime},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
, respectively. Then by standard concentration arguments, with high probability, the bipartite intersection graph
G^{\prime\prime}
between
\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}
has minimum degree at least
\frac{1}{2}(n^{\prime}/n)(c^{\prime}n)=c^{\prime}n^{\prime}/2
. In what follows, we work with only
(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime})
, so with slight abuse of notation, we write
\mathcal{A}
instead of
\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime}
,
\mathcal{B}
instead of
\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}
,
n
instead of
n^{\prime}
, and
c
instead of
c^{\prime}/2
. Thus,
|\mathcal{A}|=|\mathcal{B}|=n
and the bipartite intersection graph
G
between
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
has minimum degree
cn
.

For each curve
b\in\mathcal{B}
, we define the set of points

P_{b}=\bigcup_{a\in\mathcal{A}}b\cap a.

We
color
the point
x\in P_{b}
using the color
a
, where
a\in\mathcal{A}
is the curve containing
x
. Since each
b\in\mathcal{B}
intersects at least
cn
different curves from
\mathcal{A}
, we may partition
b
into
k+1
closed subcurves
I_{1}(b),\dots,I_{k+1}(b)
, overlapping only in endpoints, such that the points in
P_{b}\cap I_{j}(b)
receive at least
\frac{c}{2k}n
different colors for each
1\leq j\leq k+1
.

We now state one of the key definitions of the proof. For integers
1\leq j\leq k+1
and
\ell\geq 1
, we say that an
\ell
-tuple of curves
(b_{1},\dots,b_{\ell})\in\mathcal{B}^{\ell}
is
avoiding the index
j
if for all pairs
1\leq r<s\leq\ell
, and all intersection points
x\in b_{r}\cap b_{s}
, we have that

.

6
Polygon visibility graphs and ordered graphs

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.8
. We prepare the proof with a number of general results, starting with a result about graphs of bounded VC-dimension. Then, we will present some results about the degree-boundedness of ordered graphs avoiding some fixed ordered matching, which might be of independent interest.

Given a graph
G
, its
VC-dimension
is the largest positive integer
d
with the following property. There is a
d
-element subset
U\subset V(G)
such that for every
T\subset U
, there is some vertex
v\in V(G)
with
N(v)\cap U=T
. Lovász and Szegedy
[
38
]
proved that graphs of bounded VC-dimension admit a so called
ultra-strong regularity lemma
. This lemma states that if
G
has VC-dimension at most
d
and given
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
, then there is a partition of
V(G)
into sets
V_{1},\dots,V_{t}
such that (i)
t=(1/\varepsilon)^{O(d^{2})}
, (ii)
|V_{1}|,\dots,|V_{t}|\in\{\lfloor|V(G)|/t\rfloor,\lceil|V(G)|/t\rceil\}
, (iii) all but
\varepsilon t^{2}
of the pairs
(i,j)
with
1\leq i<j\leq t
satisfy that either
e(G[V_{i},V_{j}])<\varepsilon|V_{i}||V_{j}|
or
e(G[V_{i},V_{j}])\geq(1-\varepsilon)|V_{i}||V_{j}|
. We only use the following immediate qualitative consequence of this lemma.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
d
be an integer and
\beta\in(0,1]
, then for every
\gamma\in(0,1/2)
there exists
\alpha=\alpha(d,\beta,\gamma)
such that the following holds. Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph of VC-dimension at most
d
. If
e(G)\leq(1-\beta)\binom{n}{2}
, then there exist disjoint
A,B\subset V(G)
such that
|A|=|B|\geq\alpha n
, and the bipartite graph between
G[A,B]
has maximum degree
\gamma|A|
.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon=\min\{\gamma/4,\beta/4\}
, and let
V_{1},\dots,V_{t}
be a partition of
V(G)
given by the ultra-strong regularity lemma. Say that a pair
(i,j)
with
1\leq i<j\leq t
is
bad
if
e(G[V_{i},V_{j}])>\varepsilon|V_{i}||V_{j}|
. Then the number of non-edges of
G
contained in some bad pair is at most
\varepsilon(n/t)^{2}\cdot t^{2}+(n/t)^{2}\cdot\varepsilon t^{2}=2\varepsilon n^{2}
. Hence, as
2\varepsilon<\beta/2
, there must exist a good pair
(i,j)
. Then there are at most
\varepsilon|V_{i}||V_{j}|\leq(\gamma/4)|V_{i}||V_{j}|
edges in the bipartite graph
H=G[V_{i},V_{j}]
.

Let
A,B
be the sets of
|V_{i}|/2
vertices of lowest degree from both
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
, and note that all of them must have degree at most
(\gamma/2)|V_{i}|
. Otherwise, the
|V_{i}|/2
vertices of higest degree would each have degree larger than
(\gamma/2)|V_{i}|
, giving more than
(\gamma/4)|V_{i}||V_{j}|
edges in
H
, contradiction.
∎

We now discuss some results about ordered graphs avoiding a fixed ordered matching. An
ordered graph
is a graph with a linear ordering on its vertex set, denoted by
<
. An ordered graph
G
is
ordered bipartite
if we can write
V(G)=A\cup B
such that
A<B
(that is,
a<b
for every
a\in A
and
b\in B
), and every edge of
G
has one endpoint in
A
and in
B
. The celebrated Marcus-Tardos theorem
[
39
]
is equivalent to the statement that if
M
is an ordered bipartite matching and
G
is ordered bipartite with both vertex classes of size
n
not containing
M
, then
G
has at most
O_{M}(n)
edges. Here, the condition that
G
is ordered bipartite can be relaxed as follows. If
G
is an
n
vertex ordered graph not containing
M
as an ordered subgraph, then
G
has
O_{M}(n)
edges, see
[
49
]
. We prove that the following strengthening also holds.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
M
be an ordered bipartite matching. If
G
is an ordered graph not containg an induced copy of
M
, and
G
contains no
K_{s,s}
, then
G
has
O_{M,s}(n)
edges.

Proof.

Let
V(M)=\{1,\dots,k\}
with the natural ordering. For an integer
\ell
, let
M^{(\ell)}
be an arbitrary ordered bipartite matching satisfying the following. The vertex set of
M^{(\ell)}
can be partitioned into intervals
I_{1},\dots,I_{k}
of size
\ell
such that
I_{a}<I_{b}
if
a<b
, and there is a perfect matching between
I_{a}
and
I_{b}
if
ab\in E(M)
, otherwise there is no edge between
I_{a}
and
I_{b}
. For every
\ell
,
M^{(\ell)}
is clearly an ordered bipartite matching, so there exists
c_{\ell}
such that every
n
-vertex ordered graph which does not contain
M^{(\ell)}
as an ordered subgraph has at most
c_{\ell}n
edges, by
[
49
]
.

Choose
\ell
sufficiently large such that every bipartite graph with vertex classes of size
\ell
and no copy of
K_{s,s}
has at most
\ell^{2}/k^{2}
edges. Such a choice is possible by the Kővári-Sós-Turán theorem. We show that if
G
does not contain
K_{s,s}
, but it contains
M^{(\ell)}
, then
G
contains an induced copy of
M
. If this is true, then every
K_{s,s}
and induced
M
-free ordered graph has at most
c_{\ell}n
edges, finishing the proof.

If
G
contains
M^{(\ell)}
as an ordered subgraph, we can find
\ell
-element sets
J_{1}<\dots<J_{k}
in
V(G)
such that there is a complete matching
N_{ab}
between
J_{a}
and
J_{b}
if

. Note that if
G
contains no
K_{s,s}
, then the number of edges between
J_{i}
and
J_{j}
for every

is at most
\ell^{2}/k^{2}
. For every
ab\in E(M)
, pick an edge
x_{a}x_{b}\in E(N_{ab})
randomly among the
\ell
edges from the uniform distribution. We show that with positive probability,
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\}
induces of copy of
M
. For every
1\leq i<j\leq k
for which

,

\mathbb{P}(x_{i}x_{j}\in E(G))=\frac{e(G[J_{i},J_{j}])}{\ell^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{k^{2}}.

Hence, by the union bound,

\mathbb{P}(\exists ij\not\in E(M):x_{i}x_{j}\in E(G))\leq\frac{\binom{k}{2}}{k^{2}}<1.

Thus, there is a choice of
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\}
inducing a copy of
M
in
G
.
∎

Unfortunately, if
M
has at least 2 edges, it is not true that

may not be linear in

, and very dense random ordered graphs. The reason for this is that the family of ordered graphs avoiding an induced copy of
M
does not have the density-EH property.

However, if the complement of
G
avoids bipartite-induced copies of some ordered bipartite matching, then we can establish the density-EH property. Given an ordered bipartite graph

, say that an ordered graph

contains a

if there exists

such that

, and the bipartite subgraph of

between

and

is isomorphic to

(note that we do not require

[

Theorem 6.3
.

Let

be an ordered graph on

be an ordered bipartite matching, then there exists

, then

contains a biclique of size at least
\varepsilon n
.

Proof.

We divide the proof into three simple claims.

Claim 6.4
.

There exist
A,B\subset V(G)
such that
A<B
,
|A|=|B|\geq\delta n
for some

, and there are at least

and

.

Without loss of generality, let

and

and

, and let

Here, the first inequality follows as there are at most
cn^{2}/2

with

. Hence, there is a choice for

also holds. Let

and

be random

is at least

∎

Let

be the bipartite subgraph between

and

, and let

be the bipartite complement of

. Then

has at most
(1-c^{2})|A||B|

contains no bipartite-induced copy of

.

Claim 6.5
.

For every
\lambda>0
, there exist
A_{1}\subset A
and
B_{1}\subset B
such that
|A_{1}|=|B_{1}|\geq\alpha|A|
for some

, and the maximum degree of

Proof.

It is known (and easy to prove) that ordered graphs with no bipartite-induced copy of a given ordered bipartite graph

. Therefore, Lemma

gives the desired result.
∎

Let

be the number of edges of

, and set

. Let

be the sets guaranteed by the previous claim with respect to

for some

.

Claim 6.6
.

There exists

and

such that
|A_{2}|=|B_{2}|\geq\lambda|A_{1}|
, and there are no edges between

and

in

.

Assume that there exist no such

, then we show that

contains a bipartite induced copy of

. Let

Integrated immune, apoptotic and mitochondrial gene dysregulation in Long COVID and their association with symptom burden at 10 months post-infection
Authors: Yumna Hamid Ali, Uzair Abbas, Muhib Ullah Khalid, Ishfaque Ahmed, Niaz Hussain, Israr Baloch, Haniah Mahboob, Ali Ahsan Zafar, Usama Abdul Musawwir
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractLong COVID is characterized by persistent symptoms following acute SARS-CoV-2 infection, yet its biological mechanisms remain incompletely understood. Emerging evidence suggests that immune dysregulation, mitochondrial dysfunction, and altered cell survival pathways may contribute to prolonged symptomatology. In this cross-sectional study, peripheral blood mononuclear cells were collected from individuals with Long COVID approximately 10 months post-infection and from recovered individuals without Long COVID symptoms. Symptom burden was assessed using a composite domain-based score. mRNA expression of immune and antiviral genes (IL-6, IL-1β, IL-10, SOCS3, HIF-1α, OAS1, MAVS, IFN-α, IFN-γ), anti-apoptotic markers (MCL1, BCL-2, XIAP, LIVIN), cell cycle kinases (CDK4, CDK6), mitochondrial biogenesis and dynamics markers (NRF1, TFAM, PGC-1α, DRP1, MFN1/2, OPA1), and mitophagy regulators (PARKIN, PINK1) were quantified using quantitative real-time PCR. Data was analyzed by SPSS and GraphPad Prism. Individuals with Long COVID demonstrated significantly higher expressions of IL-6, IL-1β, IL-10, SOCS3, HIF-1α, OAS1, MAVS, NRF1, DRP1, PARKIN, MCL1, and LIVIN compared with recovered controls after using the Benjamini–Hochberg False Discovery Rate (FDR) method. Several transcriptional markers, particularly HIF-1α, IL-1β, IL-10, and NRF1, remained independently associated with symptom burden after adjustment for age and sex. Correlation analysis demonstrated coordinated transcriptional co-expression patterns across immune, antiviral, mitochondrial, and apoptosis-related genes. Long COVID at 10 months post-infection is associated with coordinated transcriptional alterations across multiple biological pathways. The association of these changes with symptom burden suggests a potential link between persistent immunometabolic activation and clinical manifestations. Introduction

Long COVID refers to the persistence or development of symptoms following acute SARS-CoV-2 infection, typically beyond three months, irrespective of initial disease severity1. It is increasingly recognized as a multisystem condition affecting the respiratory, neurological, cardiovascular, and musculoskeletal systems, with symptoms such as fatigue, dyspnea, cognitive impairment, and sleep disturbances persisting for months after viral clearance2–5. Recent estimates suggest a global pooled prevalence of approximately 43%, highlighting its substantial public health impact6.

While Long COVID has been widely studied, its underlying biological basis remains incompletely understood. Accumulating evidence suggests that persistent immune activation and inflammatory dysregulation may play a central role. Elevated levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines, immune exhaustion markers, and regulatory mediators have been reported months after acute infection, indicating incomplete immune resolution7,8. In parallel, ongoing antiviral signaling and interferon pathway activation raise the possibility of persistent antigenic stimulation or dysregulated innate immune sensing contributing to prolonged symptomatology9.

Beyond immune alterations, mitochondrial dysfunction and metabolic reprogramming have emerged as important features in Long COVID. Mitochondria are central to cellular energy homeostasis and immune signaling, and SARS-CoV-2 infection has been associated with disrupted mitochondrial dynamics, oxidative stress, and altered mitophagy pathways10–12. These alterations may contribute to fatigue, impaired cellular resilience, and sustained inflammatory responses observed in affected individuals.

Another potential contributor is dysregulation of apoptosis and cell survival pathways. Anti-apoptotic signaling, including activation of BCL-2 family proteins and inhibitor of apoptosis proteins, may promote prolonged survival of dysfunctional immune cells, thereby sustaining inflammation13,14. Such alterations have been described in chronic viral infections and may represent a link between immune persistence and tissue dysfunction.

Importantly, these biological processes are interconnected through immunometabolic signaling pathways. Hypoxia-inducible factor-1α (HIF-1α), for example, integrates inflammatory and metabolic cues, influencing both mitochondrial function and immune cell activation15. Dysregulation of these pathways may therefore contribute to the heterogeneity and persistence of symptoms in Long COVID.

Recent studies have also suggested that Long COVID may represent part of a broader spectrum of post-acute infection syndromes rather than a condition unique to SARS-CoV-2. Longitudinal analyses comparing post-viral sequelae following COVID-19 and influenza have demonstrated overlapping symptom patterns and risk profiles, supporting the concept of shared underlying biological mechanisms across post-infectious conditions16.

Despite these advances, a comprehensive evaluation integrating immune activation, antiviral signaling, mitochondrial remodeling, apoptosis regulation, and cell cycle pathways at extended follow-up remains limited. Furthermore, the relationship between coordinated transcriptional alterations across these biological systems and overall clinical symptom burden has not been well characterized.

Therefore, in this study, we aimed to comprehensively profile mRNA expression of key genes spanning immune, antiviral, mitochondrial, apoptosis-related, and cell cycle pathways in individuals with Long COVID approximately 10 months post-infection, and to evaluate their association with overall symptom burden.

Methods

Ethical considerations

The study adhered to the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and received approval from the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Dow University of Health Sciences (DUHS). All experimental protocols were approved by IRB with reference number IRB/DUHS/Approval/2022–236. A written informed consent was obtained from all participants before enrollment.

Study description and recruitment of participants

This was a cross-sectional analytical study conducted on a subset of 100 individuals selected from a previously established cohort2. Participants with a previous laboratory-confirmed SARS-CoV-2 infection (at a median of 10 months before date of recruitment) were selected.

Participants were divided into two groups: individuals with Long COVID (n = 50) and recovered individuals without persistent Long COVID symptoms (n = 50), hereafter referred to as controls. Importantly, all control participants had a history of SARS-CoV-2 infection but did not meet criteria for Long COVID, thereby ensuring comparability between groups.

Participants were matched for age and gender. Based on World Health Organization criteria, only individuals with asymptomatic or mild acute COVID-19 illness were included. Long COVID was defined according to WHO criteria17 following a detailed clinical examination.

A post hoc power analysis was conducted using G*Power version 3.1, demonstrating that with 50 participants per group, the study had approximately 80% power to detect moderate effect sizes (Cohen’s d ≥ 0.6) at a two-sided alpha level of 0.05.

Exclusion criteria

We excluded participants with a history of severe or critical COVID-19; known diabetes or other metabolic disorders; recent or chronic viral infections (hepatitis B or C, HIV, or tuberculosis); and pregnancy. Individuals receiving hormone therapy, diuretic antihypertensives, immunosuppressive agents, or NSAIDs were also excluded. Those who did not consent to participate or those who got COVID-19 infection more than once were also excluded.

Clinical data and symptom assessment

At enrollment, demographic and clinical data were collected using a structured questionnaire. Symptoms related to Long COVID were recorded across multiple organ systems, including central nervous system, cardiovascular, respiratory, musculoskeletal, gastrointestinal, ear–nose–throat, and reproductive domains.

For analytical purposes, symptoms were grouped into five predefined domains: neuropsychiatric, respiratory, musculoskeletal, gastrointestinal, and reproductive. Symptom presence within each domain was recorded as a binary variable. A composite symptom burden score (range 0–5) was calculated as the total number of affected domains per participant and analyzed as a continuous variable.

This domain-based approach was used to provide a standardized measure of multisystem involvement; however, it does not capture symptom severity, duration, or functional impact.

Sample collection and RNA extraction

Peripheral blood samples were collected using standard phlebotomy procedures during time of recruitment. Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were isolated using Ficoll-Histopaque and total RNA was extracted from 1 million PBMCs using Trizol method according to manufacturer protocols. RNA quality and quantity were assessed prior to analysis.

cDNA synthesis and quantitative real-time PCR

cDNA was synthesized from 500 ng of total RNA using the RevertAid First Strand cDNA synthesis kit according to manufacturer instructions. Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) was performed in duplicate using SYBR Green PCR master mix.

Expression levels were normalized to the HuPO housekeeping gene, selected based on prior studies demonstrating stable expression in PBMC-based gene expression analyses18, and relative expression was calculated using the 2-ΔΔCt method.

Target genes included immune and antiviral genes (IL-6, IL-1β, IL-10, SOCS3, HIF-1α, OAS1, MAVS, IFN-α, IFN-γ), anti-apoptotic markers (MCL1, BCL-2, XIAP, LIVIN), cell cycle kinases (CDK4, CDK6), mitochondrial biogenesis and dynamics markers (NRF1, TFAM, PGC-1α, DRP1, MFN1/2, OPA1), and mitophagy regulators (PARKIN, PINK1). The sequence of primers used were obtained from previously published literature19–22.

Gene selection was based on the hypothesis that Long COVID may involve persistent immune activation and immunometabolic dysregulation. Inflammatory and antiviral mediators (IL-6, IL-1β, IL-10, SOCS3, OAS1, MAVS) were assessed to evaluate sustained immune signaling7,23, while mitochondrial remodeling markers (NRF1, TFAM, DRP1, PARKIN) were included given the central role of mitochondria in immune and metabolic homeostasis20,24. Anti-apoptotic genes (MCL1, BCL-2, XIAP, LIVIN) and cell cycle kinases (CDK4/6) were examined to explore potential alterations in cell survival and proliferative pathways linked to chronic inflammatory states13,21.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 26.0 and GraphPad Prism 8.0 software. Gene expression values were presented as medians and compared between groups using the Mann–Whitney U test. A two-sided p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant at 95% CI.

Given the evaluation of multiple genes and statistical comparisons, false discovery rate (FDR) correction was applied using the Benjamini–Hochberg method to control for multiple testing. Adjusted p-values (q-values) were calculated, and statistical significance was interpreted accordingly.

To examine associations between gene expression and symptom burden, both univariable and multivariable linear regression analyses were performed. In multivariable models, age, sex, and body mass index (BMI) were included as covariates. Regression coefficients (β), 95% confidence intervals (CI), and p-values were reported.

Gene–gene relationships were assessed using Spearman correlation coefficients. Correlation analyses were interpreted as measures of transcriptional co-expression and do not imply causality or biological pathway interaction. A two-sided p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant, with emphasis placed on FDR-adjusted results where applicable.

Results

Demographic characteristics of study participants

The mean age of participants was 41.11 ± 9.11 years with 47 (47%) females. The Long COVID and control groups were comparable for age (43.74 ± 8.48 vs 41.5 ± 12.92 years respectively; p = 0.67), gender (23 vs 24 females respectively; p = 0.89), and BMI (21.1 ± 3.3 vs 22.5 ± 1.7 kg/m2; p = 0.783 with 100% COVID-19 vaccine coverage in both groups.

Symptom burden

Long COVID participants (n = 50) reported symptoms across multiple organ systems, with a median symptom burden of 2 (IQR 2–3) domains. The most frequently affected domains were neuropsychiatric, followed by musculoskeletal and respiratory systems. Additional involvement of reproductive and gastrointestinal systems was also reported.

Gene expression in Long COVID

Peripheral blood mRNA expression of immune activation, antiviral, apoptosis and mitochondrial pathway genes were quantified through qRT-PCR and compared between individuals with Long COVID (n = 50) and controls (n = 50).

Given the evaluation of multiple genes and statistical comparisons, FDR correction was applied using the Benjamini–Hochberg method to control multiple testing. Comprehensive p-values and corresponding FDR-adjusted q-values for all genes are presented in Supplementary Table S1.

Immune activation, regulatory response genes

Long COVID individuals demonstrated higher expression of IFN-γ (p = 0.003), IL-6 (p = 0.011), and IL-1β (p = 0.023), while IFN-α expression did not differ significantly between groups (p = 0.82). IL-10 was markedly elevated (p < 0.001), suggesting concurrent activation of regulatory immune pathways.

Expression of SOCS3 was increased (p < 0.001), while SOCS1 remained comparable between groups (p = 0.73). Additionally, HIF-1α showed pronounced upregulation (p < 0.001).

After applying FDR correction, IL-6 (q = 0.024), IL-1β (q = 0.00 76), IL-10 (q < 0.001), SOCS3 (q < 0.001), and HIF-1α (q < 0.001) remained statistically significant (Fig. 1), whereas IFN-γ did not retain statistical significance after correction.

Anti-viral response genes

Long COVID participants exhibited elevated expressions of antiviral genes including OAS1 (p < 0.001) and MAVS (p < 0.001).

These differences remained significant after FDR correction (both q < 0.001), supporting persistent activation of antiviral transcriptional responses (Fig. 1).

Expression of anti-apoptotic and cell cycle genes in Long COVID

Long COVID participants demonstrated increased expression of MCL1 (p = 0.0004), LIVIN (p < 0.001), and XIAP (p = 0.004) compared to controls.

Following FDR correction, MCL1 (q = 0.0011) and LIVIN (q < 0.001) remained statistically significant, whereas XIAP did not retain significance (Fig. 2).

BCL-2 expression was comparable between groups (p = 0.72). Expression of CDK4 and CDK6 did not differ significantly between groups (p = 0.88 and p = 0.77, respectively).

Mitochondrial biogenesis and dynamic markers

Expression of NRF1 was significantly higher in Long COVID participants (p < 0.001). TFAM and PGC-1α showed numerically higher expression but did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.075 and p = 0.26, respectively). Long COVID participants demonstrated increased expression of DRP1 (p = 0.002), whereas MFN1/2 and OPA1 did not differ significantly.

After FDR correction, NRF1 (q < 0.001) and DRP1 (q = 0.0047) remained statistically significant (Fig. 2).

Mitophagy pathway

Expression of PARKIN was markedly elevated in Long COVID (p<0.001), while PINK1 levels were comparable between groups (p=0.55). PARKIN remained highly significant after FDR correction (q<0.001).

Gene–gene correlation analysis

Pairwise correlation analysis revealed coordinated interactions across apoptosis, immune, antiviral, and mitochondrial pathways. Anti-apoptotic gene MCL1 demonstrated moderate correlations with SOCS3 and HIF-1α.

Within immune pathways, IL-1β correlated with HIF-1α, while IL-10 showed associations with mitochondrial remodeling markers DRP1 and PARKIN. Antiviral signaling gene MAVS exhibited strong correlations with PARKIN and NRF1. Moreover, mitochondrial genes including NRF1, DRP1, and PARKIN formed a correlated module, reflecting coordinated mitochondrial transcription, fission, and mitophagy (Fig. 3).

Association of gene expression with symptom burden

Univariable analysis

Linear regression analysis demonstrated significant positive associations between symptom burden and multiple genes, including HIF-1α, IL-1β, IL-10, NRF1, DRP1, PARKIN, MCL1, and LIVIN.

Multivariable analysis

In multivariable linear regression models adjusted for age, sex, and BMI several genes remained independently associated with symptom burden (Table 1). Strong and consistent associations were observed for HIF-1α (β = 0.071, p < 0.001), IL-1β (β = 0.088, p < 0.001), IL-10 (β = 0.075, p < 0.001), and NRF1 (β = 0.070, p < 0.001). Additional genes, including MAVS, PARKIN, DRP1, SOCS3, OAS1, MCL1, and LIVIN, also demonstrated statistically significant associations, although with smaller effect sizes.

Discussion

This study demonstrates that individuals with Long COVID exhibit persistent symptoms across multiple organ systems, with neuropsychiatric, musculoskeletal, and respiratory domains being most frequently affected. The observed symptom burden highlights the multisystem nature of Long COVID several months after acute SARS-CoV-2 infection.

At the molecular level, we identified coordinated transcriptional alterations across immune, antiviral, mitochondrial, and apoptosis-related pathways in individuals with Long COVID at approximately 10 months post-infection. After applying false discovery rate correction, several key genes including IL-6, IL-1β, IL-10, SOCS3, HIF-1α, OAS1, MAVS, NRF1, DRP1, PARKIN, MCL1, and LIVIN remained significantly altered. These findings support the presence of a consistent transcriptional signature involving interconnected biological pathways.

The symptom profile observed in our cohort is consistent with prior studies reporting persistent neuropsychiatric, musculoskeletal, and respiratory manifestations following COVID-1925–27. These clinical observations provide context for the molecular findings and reinforce the concept of Long COVID as a systemic condition rather than an isolated organ-specific disorder.

A central observation of this study was the upregulation of inflammatory cytokines IL-6 and IL-1β, alongside increased expression of the regulatory cytokine IL-10 and SOCS3. This pattern suggests persistent but regulated immune activation, consistent with previous reports of ongoing immune dysregulation following SARS-CoV-2 infection2,5. The concurrent elevation of pro-inflammatory and regulatory mediators may reflect an incomplete resolution of immune responses.

HIF-1α emerged as one of the most prominent findings, showing strong upregulation and a robust independent association with symptom burden. As a key regulator of cellular metabolism and immune responses, HIF-1α integrates inflammatory and metabolic signaling pathways28. Its consistent association with symptom burden supports the hypothesis that immunometabolic dysregulation may contribute to the persistence and severity of Long COVID symptoms.

The increased expression of antiviral genes OAS1 and MAVS indicates persistent activation of innate antiviral transcriptional responses. While the underlying mechanisms cannot be determined in this cross-sectional study, these findings are consistent with hypotheses of prolonged antigenic stimulation or dysregulated innate immune signaling in Long COVID29,30. The observed co-expression of antiviral and mitochondrial genes further suggests a link between antiviral signaling and cellular metabolic pathways.

Mitochondrial alterations represent another key feature of this study. Elevated expressions of NRF1, DRP1, and PARKIN suggest enhanced mitochondrial biogenesis, fission, and mitophagy. These findings align with emerging evidence of mitochondrial dysfunction and altered bioenergetics in Long COVID11,31,32. Importantly, several of these markers were independently associated with symptom burden, supporting a potential role of mitochondrial stress in clinical manifestations such as fatigue and reduced functional capacity.

We also observed increased expression of anti-apoptotic genes MCL1 and LIVIN, which are known to promote cell survival and inhibit apoptosis13,33. Although causal relationships cannot be established, these findings raise the possibility that prolonged survival of activated or dysfunctional immune cells may contribute to sustained inflammatory responses.

While several genes demonstrated nominal significance in unadjusted analyses, including IFN-γ and XIAP, these did not retain statistical significance following FDR correction. This suggests that their effects may be less consistent or subject to greater inter-individual variability. Nevertheless, given their known roles in antiviral immunity and apoptosis regulation, these findings may still reflect biologically relevant but less robust alterations. Such genes may represent context-dependent or secondary pathways that warrant further investigation.

Gene-gene correlation analysis demonstrated coordinated transcriptional co-expression patterns across immune, antiviral, mitochondrial, and apoptosis-related pathways. These correlations reflect statistical associations and do not imply direct biological interactions or pathway directionality. However, the observed patterns support the presence of an integrated transcriptional response involving multiple interconnected systems.

Importantly, multivariable regression analysis demonstrated that several genes, particularly HIF-1α, IL-1β, IL-10, and NRF1, remained independently associated with symptom burden after adjustment for age, sex and BMI. Additional genes, including MAVS, PARKIN, DRP1, and MCL1, also showed significant but comparatively smaller associations. These findings suggest that transcriptional alterations in immunometabolic and mitochondrial pathways are independently linked to the clinical expression of Long COVID.

Recent studies have suggested that Long COVID may represent part of a broader spectrum of post-acute infection syndromes rather than a condition unique to SARS-CoV-2. Comparative analyses of post-viral sequelae following COVID-19 and influenza have demonstrated overlapping clinical patterns, supporting shared underlying biological mechanisms. This broader perspective may help contextualize the findings of the present study.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the cross-sectional design precludes causal inference and limits interpretation to associations. Second, gene expressions were measured at the mRNA level, which may not directly reflect protein expression or functional activity. Third, the composite symptom burden score was based on domain involvement rather than severity, duration, or functional impact. Finally, although multiple testing correction was applied, the findings should be considered exploratory and require validation in larger and longitudinal studies.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study demonstrates coordinated transcriptional alterations across immune, antiviral, mitochondrial, and apoptosis-related pathways in individuals with Long COVID at 10 months post-infection. Several of these alterations, particularly those related to immunometabolic and mitochondrial pathways, were independently associated with symptom burden. These findings provide insight into potential transcriptional signatures of Long COVID and warrant further longitudinal and functional investigation.

Optimizing hepatitis C diagnosis through reinforcement learning feature selection and multi-model machine learning evaluation
Authors: Kanica Anand Arora, Anuj Sharma, Deepak Prashar, Amr Yousef, Arfat Ahmad Khan, Zaffar Ahmed Shaikh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection remains a leading cause of liver cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma globally, affecting approximately 50 million people with chronic infection worldwide. Traditional diagnostic approaches often rely on extensive biomarker panels, resulting in increased healthcare costs and clinical complexity without corresponding improvements in diagnostic accuracy. This study presents a novel multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) framework for optimizing HCV diagnosis through intelligent feature selection, aiming to identify minimal yet highly effective biomarker subsets while maintaining superior classification performance. We developed a four-agent MARL system comprising Greedy Q-learning, Exploratory Q-Learning, Parsimonious Deep Q-Network (DQN), and Random agents to dynamically select optimal feature subsets from clinical data. The framework was validated using a real-world dataset of 1,004 patients with 14 clinical features from Government Medical College, Bakshi Nagar, Jammu, India. Selected features were evaluated across four machine learning classifiers: Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest, and XGBoost. The MARL framework successfully identified compact feature subsets (3–4 features) achieving exceptional diagnostic performance (accuracy: 0.98–0.99, F1-score: 0.98–0.99). The Parsimonious DQN agent consistently outperformed other approaches across all classifiers. Notably, SGOT emerged as the most critical biomarkers for HCV detection. The framework demonstrated robust stability across six independent experimental runs, with ensemble methods (Random Forest and XGBoost) showing superior performance with RL-selected features. This study demonstrates that reinforcement learning-based adaptive feature selection significantly reduces diagnostic complexity and healthcare costs while maintaining or exceeding the performance of traditional full-feature approaches. The MARL framework offers a scalable, interpretable, and clinically viable solution for early HCV diagnosis, with potential applications across diverse healthcare analytics domains.

Introduction

The Healthcare system in most nations faces severe challenges due to the increase in various diseases. The expansion of diseases has affected the country’s well-being and financial stability. While diseases cannot be completely eradicated, their timely prediction can save the lives of many patients. Previously, healthcare relied on manual systems, which were simpler and easier to implement but had shortcomings such as being time-consuming, error-prone, and inefficient. Currently, the healthcare system has become digital, offering features like online consultations and utilizing technology to the fullest to serve patients remotely. Rapid advancements in Artificial Intelligence (AI) have the potential to revolutionize healthcare by integrating it into clinical practice1.Reporting AI’s role in clinical practice is crucial for successful implementation by equipping healthcare providers with essential knowledge and tools1.

AI is a powerful area of computer science with the potential to fundamentally transform the practice of medicine and the delivery of healthcare2. There are extensive applications of AI in healthcare. The applications of technology and AI in healthcare have the capability to provide several benefits like quick and accurate diagnosis, customized treatment plans, medical research and drug discovery, remote monitoring and telehealth, robotic surgery, virtual patient care, administrative applications, medical and imaging diagnostics, rehabilitation, patient engagement and compliance etc. AI has proven to be highly powerful in diverse areas of disease, including skin cancer, colon cancer, cardiovascular diseases, diabetes, neurological disorders, respiratory diseases, and eye conditions. In this work, we have chosen to focus on one specific liver disease—Hepatitis C—which can become chronic if not treated at an early stage.

Hepatitis C is a viral infection due to which inflammation occurs in the liver. Hepatitis C virus (HCV) belongs to the Hepacivirus genus of the Flaviviridae family. It spreads majorly via infected blood like sharing syringes, ineffective infection control in healthcare system etc. Hepatitis C can be acute or chronic. If the virus remains in the body for up to six months and is treated during this period, it is termed an acute infection. However, if the virus is not treated and persists in the body for more than six months, it becomes chronic, which can lead to severe consequences such as liver fibrosis, cirrhosis, and liver cancer. Chronic diseases are illnesses that endure more than three months3. Chronic HCV infection is one of the main causes of liver cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma worldwide4. Now, two major diagnostic tests are importantly considered for HCV infection. Firstly, Anti-HCV antibody test to detect any previous or current infection. Secondly, HCV Ribonucleic Acid test to detect the active infection. Globally, an estimated 50 million people have chronic HCV infection, with about 1.0 million new infections occurring per year5.World Health Organization(WHO) estimated that in 2022, approximately 2,42,000 people died from hepatitis C, mostly from cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma (primary liver cancer)5. A significant reduction in morbidity and mortality from HCV is the major aim of WHO by 2030.

The diagnosis of HCV can be confirmed through three major tests: Liver Function test (Aspartate Aminotransferase (AST)/Alanine Transaminase (ALT)), HCV antibody test, HCV RNA PCR test. Previously, HCV detection was performed manually, which required great physical effort and was time consuming. The major drawback is that sometimes most of the patients remain asymptomatic for years which later on leads to serious problems, also it becomes difficult to handle complex datasets. To overcome all the cons of the manual system, AI is introduced for the early prediction of HCV like many other diseases. With the help of AI, the chances of infection, progress of the disease as well as the treatment strategies can be handled with ease providing high accuracy rate, quick-response, personalized treatment plans, handling large and complex datasets.

AI-based research includes several machine learning (ML) algorithms that learn the patterns associated with the disease and perform prediction on the basis of dataset. ML can be classified as supervised ML, unsupervised ML, and reinforcement learning (RL). Deep learning (DL) is also implemented for the prediction of many diseases like HCV. But we have chosen RL for our research as most of the previous research is performed using ML and DL. In RL, the agent learns by experiencing the environment. That is, the agent is provided with the environment to experience it by performing actions which are either positive or negative, and the agent learns by doing to get maximum possible rewards. When the agent explores the environment by performing some actions, it gets feedback which is either positive or negative. Positive feedback is known as reward, whereas negative feedback is known as penalty. The agent continuously performs three actions that is take action, change the state or remain in the same state and gets the feedback. Literature review

In this section, we present the literature review.

Zohora et al.6 explored the diagnosis of Type-II diabetes using Q-Learning, Decision Tree, and KNN using the data set of Indian Women Diabetes, PIMA which was developed by the National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney diseases. The dataset consists of 769 subjects having 9 features like age, insulin level, blood pressure, skin thickness, BMI, blood glucose, etc. Type II diabetes is a condition when there is a lack of production of a hormone named insulin in human body which helps in the absorption of glucose that provides energy in the body. When the glucose stays in the bloodstream instead of reaching the cells, it will lead to increase sugar levels in the human body thus causing type II diabetes. Finally, the reinforcement learning technique used here that is Q-learning performed well compared to the supervised learning techniques that work more closely on three key parameters, namely Age, BMI and Glucose.

Kanthavel et al.7 come with the study of osteoarthritis using CNN, DNN, ResNet 150, Dense Vox-Net, and the proposed deep reinforcement learning. A dataset of 4000 X-ray images was used for the prediction of the early stageof knee disorders. 65% is used for training and 35% is used for Testing. The proposed model provides 93.79% accuracy, 93.24% sensitivity, 94.12% specificity, 0.938 precision ratio.

Li et al.8 worked to predict Coronary artery occlusion using Combined Multitask Progressive Time-Series networks, where the main objective of this research is to replace the expensive, Coronary Angiography with AI-based methods. The dataset of 17,885 patients is taken into consideration from triple A hospitals in Shanghai. Out of which 70% is used for training set, 10% is used for validation set and 20% is used for test set. Coronary Artery is the blood vessel that supply blood to the heart muscles but when there is some hindrance in them, there is lack of blood flow and there by lack of oxygen in the heart, this condition will lead to the disease named Coronary Heart Occlusion.

Huang et al.9 introduced RL for feature selection and then Multi-layer perceptron was trained to identify several gene signatures for Renal Cell Carcinoma. Renal cell carcinoma is a type of kidney cancer when there occur some malignant cells inside the kidney within the tiny tubes whose role is to filter waste from the blood to produce urine. The dataset of Renal cell carcinoma is taken from The Cancer Genome Atlas which includes three subtypes: Kidney clear cell carcinoma, Kidney papillary cell carcinoma and Kidney chromophobe. Sequential Reinforcement Active Feature Learning approach is introduced for selecting the gene subsets dynamically.

Songjing et al.10 explored the dataset of approximately1.3 million elderly individuals for the prediction of lung cancer using Deep Q-Network in comparison with Support Vector Machine and Logistic Regression to reduce the risk factor. The dataset to be collected from Behavioral RiskFactor Surveillance System and US Environmental Protection Agency. When there includes the formation of malignant cells in the tissues of the lungs, whose major role is breathing, this uncontrolled formation of the abnormal cells in the lungs will cause lung cancer.

Ostelio et al.5 performed the evaluation of treatment policies for patients having Hepatitis C using RL techniques. The dataset is taken from Veterans Health Administration corporate data warehouse. The algorithms used are Q-learning, DQN, Policy gradient methods, Baseline models. Hepatitis C is a liver problem which includes the inflammation of the liver due to the presence of the hepatitis C virus which belongs from the Flaviviridae family. The major cause of this disease is infected blood, contaminated needles etc.

Kamepalli et al.11 worked for the diagnosis of heart disease using Q-Learning algorithm in comparison with the traditional machine learning classifiers like K-Nearest Neighbour and Decision Tree taking into consideration the dataset from University of California, Irvine ML repository. The dataset is collected from Cleveland heart disease dataset where three main parameters are taken into consideration that is rest blood pressure, cholesterol and age.

Xiaodan et al.12 performed the research for sepsis, a life- threatening emergency due to some irregular response of the human body to an infection, which may further damage the human organs and can even harm the survival rate. The dataset is included from the multi-parameter intelligent monitoring in intensive care from USA. The models to be used for implementation are DQN, Double DQN, Dueling DQN, Policy Gradient methods, Baseline models. The best part is both evaluation metrics and clinical outcomes are used for evaluation.

Ganesh et al.13 introduced Convolutional Long-Short Term Memory using Double Deep Q-Network (ICL-DDQN)for the prediction of lung cancer, leukemia etc. Three different types of Cancer dataset is considered which is based on gene expression microarray and is publicly available for research purposes. Firstly, Grey wolf optimization model is utilizedfor the feature selection, after which deep reinforcement learning is utilized to develop convolutional neural network with long short time memory so as to select the parameters for classification. Convolutional neural network with long short time memory is beneficial for the conversion of features from low-level to higher level.

Barata et al.14 worked for the AI-based decision support for skin cancer. The dataset from HAM10000 is considered for the training purpose having 10,015 dermatoscopic images, ISIC2018ChallengeTestsetusedfor external validation and reader study which includes 1,511 dermatoscopic images from different countries like Austria, Turkey, Newzealand, Sweden etc. and patient-centered dataset specially foe RL. Supervised Learning as well as Reinforcement Learning models are implemented on the dataset. Due to the formation of the malignant cells in the tissues of the skin, the disease named Skin Cancer come about.

A.S.Sabeena et al.15 focused on Diabetic Retinopathy using a Deep Q-Network model trained with Gradient Descent Sea Turtle Foraging algorithm taking into consideration the Indian Diabetic Retinopathy Image Dataset to avoid the loss of patient’s vision. When the increase in the blood sugar levels damages the blood vessels in the retina, which is a light-sensitive tissue in the back of the human eye, this eye condition is termed as Diabetic Retinopathy. Cai et al.16 evaluates the performance of a k-means-ELM hybrid against a standard Extreme Learning Machine for the classification of Liver Fibrosis and Inflammatory activity, using a clinical dataset of 123 cases. K-means-ELM provided the best performance in staging fibrosis, while stand-alone ELM proved slightly superior for classifying inflammatory activity.

Cai et al.17 Using a dataset of 123 clinical cases, this study focuses on the comparison of CR-SCAD classifier to standard baseline methods. CR-SCAD demonstrates similar effectiveness to ELM methods for staging fibrosis, while showing moderate performance in assessing inflammatory activity.

Instead of the traditional single-agent reinforcement learning approach which rely on a single explore-exploit trade-off, this research adopts a multi-policy reinforcement learning approach for investigating feature subset selection performance under different algorithmic approaches. In particular, four reinforcement learning agents- Greedy Q-Learning, Exploratory Q-Learning, Parsimonious Deep Q-Learning (DQN) and a random baseline are employed.

While many RL-based feature selection studies focus on maximizing accuracy with a single mode, they tend to overlook the impact of policy bias, search behavior, and stability on the reliability of medical diagnosis. Conversely, the proposed methodology centers on comparative behavioral policy analysis, facilitating the isolation of stable, minimal and clinically interpretable feature subsets. Each agent performs a unique analytical function: Greedy Q-learning focuses on exploitation, Exploratory Q-learning favors extensive searching to avoid local optima, Parsimonious DQN forces explicit sparsity, and the Random Agent serves as a diagnostic tool for feature redundancy. The innovation here extends beyond just using reinforcement learning for Hepatitis C feature selection, it compares multiple RL policies, providing a deeper understanding of feature importance, trade-offs, and robust performance. This multi-policy formulation shifts the focus from purely maximizing accuracy to ensuring a more interpretable and practical feature selection strategy for clinical settings.

System model and problem formulation

System model

Data preprocessing

To ensure data quality and stability, an initial preprocessing step was carried out prior to the reinforcement learning- based feature selection. The raw data underwent data cleansing and transformation to ensure it was accurate, consistent, and ready for modeling, including reinforcement learning-based feature selection and machine learning classification. Originally containing 14 attributes, the dataset was reduced to 8 features during preprocessing by removing patient name, contact information, gender, age, status and viral load. To comply with ethical standards and ensure confidentiality, all personally identifiable information was removed from the dataset. The demographic variables are removed to minimize potential bias, allowing the model to concentrate exclusively on biochemical markers. Viral load measurements were omitted to prevent data leakage and artificially high performance results, as these tests serve as a confirmatory diagnostic step for HCV. Consequently, the finalized model is trained and validated using eight specific liver function parameters.

Problem formulations

Problem context

It is generally known that the delayed diagnosis of any disease can lead to critical situations. Similarly, Hepatitis C, if not detected at an initial stage, will lead to dire liver problems. Prior investigations on HCV using ML typically depend on traditional feature selection approaches such as filter method, wrapper method, etc. or the features are applied unfiltered, which led to unrecorded acquisition cost, clinical work ability, feature repetition. Due to this, the problems to be faced are discussed as:

Feature selection: The Feature selection procedure to be followed previously is static and myopic, as well as cost-ineffective.

Lack of clinical synergy: The major focus is on accuracy instead of early detection, cost for acquiring features.

This approach uses an optimization framework to select the smallest subset of markers that yields the highest diagnostic efficacy. Assume ‘F’ is the complete set of features and ‘S’ is a selected subset such that S ⊂ F. The objective is to maximize a utility function that balances classification accuracy, represented by the F1-score, against a penalty for model complexity or feature set size. This can be represented as:22\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathrm{J}}\left( {\mathrm{S}} \right)\, = \,\alpha .{\mathrm{F1}}\left( {\mathrm{S}} \right) - \beta .\left| {\mathrm{S}} \right|$$\end{document}

The formulation helps agents achieve a balance between predictive accuracy and practical clinical utility by selecting concise and reliable features.

To ensure a fair comparison, the weighting parameters α and β were chosen empirically to prioritize diagnostic accuracy and ensure model sparsity, remaining constant across all agents. This approach explains that in scenarios with sparsity constraints, agents prefer stable functional markers to high-variance enzyme markers.

Reinforcement learning training and stopping criteria

Training was concluded for each agent once the prescribed termination conditions were satisfied. Specifically, the agents underwent training for a predetermined maximum number of episodes, established to guarantee thorough exploration of the feature selection space. Furthermore, convergence was verified by observing the stabilization of cumulative rewards over successive training episodes. Empirical evidence showed that the reward stabilized well before the maximum episode limit, indicating that both the policy and the selected features had converged. The final subset of features for each agent was determined by the policy derived at the conclusion of training. The model ceased learning when cumulative reward improvements became negligible over consecutive episodes, or when the episode cap was reached.

Computational cost and runtime analysis

Because reinforcement learning with function approximation is both iterative and stochastic, calculating a precise, closed-form computational complexity is challenging. To evaluate the practical computational cost of the proposed framework, we report on its empirical runtime performance. Throughout several independent training sessions, the agent’s runtime was monitored based on actual elapsed wall-clock hours. Experiments were executed on CPU, using the Google Colab framework. Each training session lasted roughly 180 s, reaching convergence within about 100 episodes. The duration increases proportionally with agent and episode counts, whereas the expense per episode is mainly driven by reward computation and classifier training. Experimental results verify that the proposed framework is computationally viable for mid-sized clinical datasets.

Data splitting and evaluation protocol

A rigorous training–testing split was implemented to guarantee unbiased evaluation and prevent data leakage. For each experimental trial, the entire dataset was divided into an analysis set (80%) and a retained validation set (20%) using proportional sampling to maintain group ratios. To enhance statistic reliability, this partitioning process was repeated across multiple independent trials using varied random seeds. Notably, the reinforcement learning-based feature selection process completely excluded the test set. The RL agents were trained solely on the training set, with feature selection, guided exclusively by performance metrics derived from the same data. Once the RL agents finalized their feature selection and downstream models were trained, the holdout test set was applied only once per run. By avoiding information leakage during feature selection, this evaluation approach guarantees that performance metrics represent true generalization to new, unseen data. A separate validation set was not explicitly defined.

Justification of multi-agent design choices

The proposed framework utilizes a comparative multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) approach, where each agent learns autonomously without sharing policies, optimizing joint rewards, or communicating with others. Multiple agents are employed not to cooperate, but to systematically analyze different HCV diagnostic strategies under identical settings. The Greedy agent acts as a fixed, exploitation–oriented benchmark, reflecting a rule-based diagnostic approach. By prioritizing stochastic actions, the exploratory agent investigates the effect of discovery-based learning on feature exploitation and model generalization. The Parsimonious DQN agent aims for concise, clinically interpretable feature sets by penalizing redundancy, thereby fulfilling practical deployment equipments. The random agent serves as a control baseline, enabling comparison with uninformed actions and providing context for the effectiveness of the learned policies. These agents were deliberately chosen to represent a diverse range of learning behaviors, rather than to cooperate with one another. Comparing agent performance illuminates the accuracy-stability-efficiency trade-offs, strengthening the validation of our diagnostic framework.

Statistical analysis

A statistical analysis was performed to critically examine the performance of several RL agents and to validate whether the observed performance differences were statistically meaningful. This statistics was performed depending on the independent experimental runs which is N = 6 in this study, which indicates the variability and average performance of the models. Particularly, the paired t-tests are applied for comparing the results of F1-score between pair of agents for each classifier. In this study, the paired t-tests are selected as the performance scores are acquired from the same set of experimental runs, making the observations dependent.

Pairwise comparisons were performed for all the four classifiers, using the F1-scores generated by different agents across all the six runs. A significance level of p < 0.05 was adopted for all primary analyses. This level of significance suggests that the performance gaps identified are statistically robust and unlikely to be incidental.

Baseline strategy

In the experimental setup, a random agent was included to establish an internal performance baseline. In random agent, the selection of features is performed randomly without depending on any policy which is totally different than the selection process of the RL agents that depends on a particular policy for feature selection. This provides an opportunity to compare the informed agents as well as the uninformed random agent. To make sure significant internal evaluation, this random agent worked as a baseline. The comparison of the RL agents is performed with this baseline agent instead of the conventional filter, wrapper and embedded methods which are actually out of the scope of this study.

Discussion

The purpose of this research is to evaluate the utility of a reinforcement learning framework in selecting features for the diagnosis of hepatitis C virus and to compare its results with those of traditional machine learning models. The study reveals that the RL feature selection framework successfully identified a compact and powerful feature subset, thereby reducing model complexity while maintaining and sometimes enhancing predictive performance. Models utilizing features chosen via reinforcement learning achieved comparable performance metrics, including accuracy, precision, recall and f1- score, to models trained on the entire feature set across diverse classification algorithms such as Random Forest, XGBoost, Logistic Regression, and Decision Tree. A key advantage of RL agents is their ability to adaptively search for optimal feature subsets and uncover intricate, non-linear patterns which are often overlooked by traditional statistical or filter-based techniques. RL’s capacity to consistently choose a stable set of important features across multiple trials validates the dependability of the feature selection approach to identify relevant HCV-related biomarkers. By reducing dimensionality, RL improved computational performance, resulting in faster training speeds and lower resource usage compared to full feature models. This is crucial for practical use cases, where there is a strong demand for fast, inexpensive diagnostic systems. Combining reinforcement and other machine learning methods provides a powerful evidence-based strategy for enhancing diagnostic tools for Hepatitis C Virus (HCV). The performance could be further enhanced employing more sophisticated reinforcement learning agents, more extensive agent’s datasets, or specialized reward mechanisms.

A notable discrepancy exists between individual biomarker importance and agent-level selection consistency within the proposed RL model. SGOT acts as a powerful, independent predictor in model subsets, reflecting its importance in clinical assessments of liver injury. Nevertheless, SGOT, SGPT, Bilirubin and Total Protein were chosen far more frequently across various agents and experimental trials. The agents prioritize stable, generalizable biomarkers under sparsity constraints over those with high yet inconsistent, individual importance. The agent’s reward function balances high predictive accuracy with minimal feature usage, causing them to favor reliable biomarkers that result in compact, high performing subsets across different runs. This finding suggests prioritizing stable functional indicators over fluctuating enzyme levels in Hepatitis C diagnostics, resulting in more reliable, interpretable, and applicable models in real-world settings. Potential future works involves exploring deep reinforcement learning methods, multi-agent RL systems, or hybrid feature selection techniques to enhance the robustness and applicability of the predictive model.

Clinical interpretability and practical deployment

Beyond just diagnostic accuracy, clinical interpretability is crucial for the practical adoption of machine learning – based diagnostic systems. This reinforcement learning approach enhances clinical usability by identifying compact sets of 3–4 common liver markers, thus lowering costs and simplifying testing procedures. Because they directly represent liver synthetic activity, consistent monitoring of stable indicators- specifically SGOT, SGPT, Bilirubin and Total Protein enhances the ease of clinical assessment. Since the reinforcement learning process is an offline development step, the optimized feature subset can be directly applied in standard classification pipelines, ensuring no additional computational burden during deployment. By decoupling the training process from the inference phase, this framework remains practical for clinical adoption while ensuring decisions are both transparent and trustworthy. The framework bridges the divide between sophisticated machine learning and functional clinical decision support by offering both superior diagnostic performance and transparent biomarker selection.

Agent behavior analysis

The surprisingly high accuracy of random agent is due to the inherent properties of the dataset, rather than the superiority of the random search. The study’s clinical features exhibit substantial redundancy, as multiple liver function markers reflect overlapping pathological data related to Hepatitis C progression. As a result, subsets of features chosen at random frequently possess enough discriminative information to yield high classification accuracy. Due to strong disease-related patterns, the modeling task is relatively straightforward, despite not using optimal features. Although it demonstrates high average performance, the random agent falls short in stability, consistency, and interpretability, largely because its chosen feature sets are inconsistent and not optimized for simplicity. Conversely, the proposed agent-based system systematically leverages redundant data to select concise, stable, and clinically relevant feature sets, thereby delivering higher robustness, and interpretability than random exploration can provide.

Comparative analysis of agent performance

The tests reveal that the agents perform at distinctly different levels. The parsimonious agent consistently outperforms others by proving its efficacy in identifying a concise yet highly informative subset of features. By optimizing the trade-off between predictive accuracy and feature sparsity, this algorithm retains key biomarkers while discarding redundant variables. The Exploratory agent takes second place, utilizing its capacity to survey a broader feature space and identify powerful feature combinations. The intermediate performance of the Random agent indicates that despite intermittent discovery of useful feature subsets, the absence of a structured learning framework prevents consistent long-term optimization. Conversely, Greedy agent underperforms likely because it prioritizes immediate rewards over sufficient exploration, resulting in inferior feature sets. In conclusion, effective RL-based feature hinges on balancing exploration with sparse optimization.

Future research directions

For image-based deep learning, spectral-based singular pooling acts as an effective method for preserving dominant structural information when handling high-dimensional inputs19. This work focuses on Reinforcement Learning and classical classifiers for tabular clinical data; however, incorporating spectral information preservation is an interesting direction for subsequent research. Leveraging singular pooling within advanced deep models-such as reinforcement learning frameworks- offers a mechanism to enhance future learning for complex, time-series biochemical patient data. Furthermore, incorporating multimodal data-such as pairing laboratory biomarkers with imaging or longitudinal clinical signals-could leverage singular pooling to preserve key spectral features while minimizing dimensionality. These directions are reserved for future research, as they necessitate significant architectural changes beyond the current study’s scope.

Limitations and future work

This research is subject to several constraints that warrant discussion.

a. Comparison with classical feature selection methods

One of the limitations of the current experimental study is that the conventional feature selection techniques like filter method, wrapper method and embedded method are not compared with the proposed RL agents. The major objective of this study is to understand the internal dynamics of the RL – driven feature selection approach rather than providing their comparison with the conventional feature selection methods. While these methods proved to be a well established baseline, taking into consideration the scope of our research, we have considered random agent as the baseline method in our research. In spite of this, to evaluate the absolute performance of MARL-based agents, conventional methods would provide an additional context and is acknowledged as a limitation of the current study.

b. Single-center dataset and generalizability

The scope of this study confined to one institution, which potentially limits the external validity of the findings.

Variations in population demographics, laboratory standards, and clinical workflows across different locations can impact biomarker and model performance. Consequently, the findings may be limited to the specific characteristics of the analyzed dataset. Therefore, the findings should be viewed as preliminary, requiring additional validation before the proposed model can be applied to larger, diverse patient populations. Future efforts will involve testing the framework on multi-site, heterogeneous populations to verify its stability for real-world healthcare deployment.

c. Experimental Runs

As our evaluation relies on a limited number of training runs, future studies should increase the number of independent runs to 30 or more.

d. Lack of shared reward mechanism in multi-agent learning

The introduced multi-agent reinforcement learning system utilizes individual agents, devoid of direct communication or shared rewards, thereby restricting the extent of cooperative decision-making. Future research will advance beyond independent agent operations by establishing genuine multi-agent collaboration, utilizing shared reward structures and explicit inter-agent communication to facilitate cooperative policy learning.

The model inadequately addresses the cost and availability of data, treating all laboratory parameters as uniformly accessible. In conclusion, despite encouraging preliminary results, the model lacks testing in real-world clinical settings, a critical step for fully assessing its operational readiness.

While our reinforcement learning approach shows promise for HCV diagnosis, we plan to extend the framework to overcome its current limitations.

Conclusion

For the detection of the Hepatitis C virus, this research has demonstrated that reinforcement learning is an efficient and streamlined methodology. By judiciously selecting the most impactful variables, the RL-driven system increased machine learning performance while simultaneously cutting computational costs. When using features selected by reinforcement learning, ensemble methods like Random Forest and XGBoost demonstrated superior performance, achieving strong accuracy, precision, F1-score. These results stress the promise of using RL-driven feature selection alongside machine learning algorithms to create reliable, scalable, interpretable diagnostic platforms. By reducing the dimension of the feature set, the model’s complexity is decreased, leading to more efficient training, better performance on new data, and a lower chance of overfitting. This highlights reinforcement learning as a valuable framework for healthcare analytics, especially in areas where relevant features are intricate and challenging to model by hand.

In conclusion, the RL-ML pipeline provides a highly effective, robust, and clear methodology for detecting HCV at an early stage. Future investigations are poised to incorporate more voluminous datasets, deep reinforcement learning, or hybrid optimization strategies to further boost the reliability and practical clinical use of such automated diagnostic systems.

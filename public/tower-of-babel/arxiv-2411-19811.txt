1.
Introduction and Main Results

Let
\mathcal{P}_{n}
denote the set of complex polynomials of degree
n.
If
P\in\mathcal{P}_{n},
then according to Bernstein’s inequality concerning the estimate of
|P(z)|
over
|z|\leq 1,
we have

\displaystyle\left\|P^{\prime}(z)\right\|_{\infty}\leq n\left\|P(z)\right\|_{\infty},\quad\text{where}\,\,\,\left\|P\right\|_{\infty}:=\underset{\left|z\right|=1}{\max}\left|P(z)\right|.

The inequality is sharp and becomes equality if
P(z)=c~z^{n},

c\neq 0.
This inequality of Bernstein has been generalized and extended in several ways, for more details refer to
[
6
, p. 508]
. Under the same hypothesis, V.K. Jain
[
3
]
proved that if
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\alpha|\leq\frac{n}{2},
then

\displaystyle\left\|zP^{\prime}(z)-\alpha P(z)\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left|n-\alpha\right|\left\|P(z)\right\|_{\infty}.

(1)

Zygmund
[
8
]
extended Bernstein’s inequality in
L_{p}
-norm and proved that if
P\in\mathcal{P}_{n},
then

\|P^{\prime}(z)\|_{p}\leq n\|P(z)\|_{p}\quad\text{for}\,\,\,p\geq 1

(2)

where

\left\|P(z)\right\|_{p}:=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|P(e^{i\theta})\right|^{p}d\theta\right)^{1/p},\,0<p<\infty.

It is well known that the supremum norm satisfies
\left\|P\right\|_{\infty}=\underset{p\to\infty}{\lim}\left\|P\right\|_{p}.
The Mahler measure (see
[
5
]
) of a polynomial
P(z)
with complex coefficients is defined as

\left\|P(z)\right\|_{0}:=\exp\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log\left|P(e^{i\theta})\right|d\theta\right).
This also satisfy the limiting case
\underset{p\to 0}{\lim}\left\|P\right\|_{p}=\|P\|_{0}.

It was not known for quite a long time that whether the inequality (
2
) is true for
0<p<1.
or not. Finally, it was Arestov
[
1
]
who came up with some remarkable results which among other things proved that the inequality (
2
) remains valid for

0\leq p<1
as well.

Recently in
[
7
]
, the inequality (
1
) of Jain was extended to
L_{p}
-norm and it was proved that if
P\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\alpha|\leq n/2,
then

\displaystyle\left\|zP^{\prime}(z)-\alpha P(z)\right\|_{p}\leq\left|n-\alpha\right|\left\|P(z)\right\|_{p}\quad\text{for}\,\,p\geq 0.

(3)

It is not clear from the proofs of (
1
) and (
3
) that whether the condition on
\alpha
is indeed necessary. In this paper, we have been able to relax the condition on
\alpha
and have proved the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

If
P\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
, then for every
\alpha,\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re(\alpha)\leq\frac{n}{2}
,
\Re(\gamma)\leq\frac{n}{2}
and
0\leq p<\infty

\displaystyle\left\|zP^{\prime}(z)-\alpha P(z)\right\|_{p}\leq\left|n-\alpha\right|\left\|P(z)\right\|_{p}

(4)

and

\displaystyle\left\|z^{2}P^{\prime\prime}(z)-(1-\alpha-\gamma)zP^{\prime}(z)+\alpha\gamma P(z)\right\|_{p}\leq\left|n(n-\alpha-\gamma)+\alpha\gamma\right|\left\|P(z)\right\|_{p}.

(5)

The results are best possible and equality in (
4
) and (
5
) holds for
P(z)=cz^{n},~c\neq 0.

Remark 1
.

Consider
P(z)=2z^{n}+1
and
p=2,
then

\displaystyle\left\|zP^{\prime}(z)-\alpha P(z)\right\|_{p}=\left\|2(n-\alpha)z^{n}-\alpha\right\|_{2}=\sqrt{4|n-\alpha|^{2}+|\alpha|^{2}}

and

\displaystyle\left|n-\alpha\right|\left\|P(z)\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle=|n-\alpha|\|2z^{n}+1\|_{2}=\sqrt{5}\left|n-\alpha\right|.

Thus,

\displaystyle\left\|zP^{\prime}(z)-\alpha P(z)\right\|_{p}\leq\left|n-\alpha\right|\left\|P(z)\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle~~4|n-\alpha|^{2}+|\alpha|^{2}\leq 5|n-\alpha|^{2}\Leftrightarrow|\alpha|\leq|n-\alpha|\Leftrightarrow\Re(\alpha)\leq\frac{n}{2}.

This shows that the condition on
\alpha
in (
4
) cannot be relaxed furthermore.

Remark 2
.

If we let
p\to\infty,
one can conclude that the inequality (
1
) holds for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re(\alpha)\leq\frac{n}{2}.
If we take
\alpha=0
in (
4
) we obtain Zygmund’s inequality (
2
).

Taking
\alpha=\gamma=\frac{n}{2}
in (
4
) and (
5
), we get the following result:

Corollary 1
.

Let
P\in\mathcal{P}_{n},
then

\displaystyle\left\|zP^{\prime}(z)-\frac{n}{2}P(z)\right\|_{p}\leq\frac{n}{2}\left\|P(z)\right\|_{p}

and

\displaystyle\left\|z^{2}P^{\prime\prime}(z)+(1-n)zP^{\prime}(z)-\frac{n^{2}}{4}P(z)\right\|_{p}\leq\frac{n^{2}}{4}\left\|P(z)\right\|_{p}.

The results are sharp and equality holds for
P(z)=cz^{n},~c\neq 0.

The next corollary follows by taking
\alpha=1
in (
4
) and
\gamma=0
in (
5
).

Corollary 2
.

Let
P\in\mathcal{P}_{n},
then

\displaystyle\left\|zP^{\prime}(z)-P(z)\right\|_{p}\leq\left|n-1\right|\left\|P(z)\right\|_{p}

and

\displaystyle\left\|z^{2}P^{\prime\prime}(z)+(1-\alpha)zP^{\prime}(z)\right\|_{p}\leq n\left|n-\alpha\right|\left\|P(z)\right\|_{p}\quad(\textnormal{for}\,\,n\geq 2).

The results are best possible and equality holds for
P(z)=cz^{n},~c\neq 0.

If
P\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
and does not vanish in
|z|\leq 1,
then it was conjectured by Erdös and later proved by Lax
[
4
]
that the factor
n
in the right side of Bernstein’s inequality can be replaced by
n/2.
Jain
[
3
]
also generalized this inequality and proved that if
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\alpha|\leq\frac{n}{2},
then

\displaystyle\left\|zP^{\prime}(z)-\alpha P(z)\right\|_{\infty}\leq\dfrac{\left|n-\alpha\right|+\left|\alpha\right|}{2}\left\|P(z)\right\|_{\infty}.

(6)

Next, we present the following result which not only includes an
L_{p}
-norm extension of above inequality but also extends the range of
\alpha.

Theorem 2
.

If
P\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
and
P(z)
does not vanish in
|z|\leq 1
, then for every
\alpha,\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re(\alpha)\leq\frac{n}{2}
,
\Re(\gamma)\leq\frac{n}{2}
and
0\leq p<\infty

\displaystyle\left\|zP^{\prime}(z)-\alpha P(z)\right\|_{p}\leq\left\|\left(n-\alpha\right)z+\alpha\right\|_{p}\frac{\left\|P(z)\right\|_{p}}{\left\|1+z\right\|_{p}}

(7)

and

\displaystyle\left\|z^{2}P^{\prime\prime}(z)-(1-\alpha-\gamma)zP^{\prime}(z)+\alpha\gamma P(z)\right\|_{p}\leq\left\|n(n-\alpha-\gamma)z+\alpha\gamma\right\|_{p}\frac{\left\|P(z)\right\|_{p}}{\left\|1+z\right\|_{p}}.

(8)

The results are best possible and equality holds for
P(z)=az^{n}+b,~|a|=|b|\neq 0
.

Remark 3
.

Inequality (
6
) follows from (
7
) by letting
p\to\infty,
which also shows that (
6
) holds for
\Re(\alpha)\leq\frac{n}{2}.
If we take
\alpha=0
in (
7
) , it reduces to
L_{p}
-norm extension of Erdös-Lax inequality
[
4
]
due to de Bruijn
[
2
]
.

The following Corollary is obtained from Theorem
2
by taking
\alpha=1
in (
7
) and
\gamma=0
in (
8
).

Corollary 3
.

If
P\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
and
P(z)
does not vanish in
|z|\leq 1,
then

\displaystyle\left\|zP^{\prime}(z)-P(z)\right\|_{p}\leq\left\|\left(n-1\right)z+1\right\|_{p}\frac{\left\|P(z)\right\|_{p}}{\left\|1+z\right\|_{p}}.

and

\displaystyle\left\|z^{2}P^{\prime\prime}(z)-(1-\alpha)zP^{\prime}(z)\right\|_{p}\leq n\left|n-\alpha\right|\frac{\left\|P(z)\right\|_{p}}{\left\|1+z\right\|_{p}}.

A polynomial
P\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
is said to be
self-inversive
polynomial if
P(z)=uz^{n}\overline{P\big({1}/{\overline{z}}\big)},
where
u\in\mathbb{C}
with
|u|=1.

Finally, we show that the Theorem
2
also holds true for the class of self-inversive polynomials.

Theorem 3
.

Inequalities (
7
) and (
8
) also holds if
P(z)
is a self-inversive polynomial.
The inequalities are best possible and equality holds for
P(z)=az^{n}+\bar{a},~|a|\neq 0
.

Lemma 2
.

Let all the zeros of an
n
th degree polynomial
f(z)
lie in
|z|\leq r,
then for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re(\alpha)\leq\frac{n}{2},
the zeros of
zf^{\prime}(z)-\alpha f(z)
also lie in
|z|\leq r.

Proof.

Let
\psi(z)=zf^{\prime}(z)-\alpha f(z)
and
w\in\mathbb{C}
with
|w|>r.
Suppose
z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n}
be the zeros of
f(z),
then
|z_{\nu}|\leq r
and
|w|-|z_{\nu}|>0
for
\nu=1,2,\ldots,n.
Now,

\displaystyle\dfrac{\psi(w)}{f(w)}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{wf^{\prime}(w)}{f(w)}-\alpha=\sum_{\nu=1}^{n}\dfrac{w}{w-z_{\nu}}-\alpha

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\nu=1}^{n}\dfrac{(w-z_{\nu})+(w+z_{\nu})}{w-z_{\nu}}-\alpha

\displaystyle=\dfrac{n}{2}-\alpha+\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\nu=1}^{n}\dfrac{(w+z_{\nu})(\bar{w}-\bar{z_{\nu}})}{|w-z_{\nu}|^{2}}.

This implies

\displaystyle\Re\left(\dfrac{\psi(w)}{f(w)}\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{n}{2}-\Re(\alpha)+\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\nu=1}^{n}\dfrac{|w|^{2}-|z_{\nu}|^{2}}{|w-z_{\nu}|^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{n}{2}-\dfrac{n}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\nu=1}^{n}\dfrac{|w|^{2}-|z_{\nu}|^{2}}{|w-z_{\nu}|^{2}}>0.

This further implies that
\psi(w)\neq 0.
Hence,
\psi(z)
cannot have a zero in
|z|>r.
Therefore, we conclude that all the zeros of the polynomial
zf^{\prime}(z)-\alpha f(z)
lie in
|z|\leq r.

∎

Lemma 3
.

Let a polynomial
F(z)
of degree
n
has all its zeros in
|z|\leq 1
and
P(z)
be a polynomial of degree at most
n
such that
|P(z)|\leq|F(z)|
for
|z|=1,
then for every
\alpha,\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re(\alpha)\leq n/2,

\displaystyle|zP^{\prime}(z)-\alpha P(z)|\leq|zF^{\prime}(z)-\alpha F(z)|

(9)

\displaystyle|z^{2}P^{\prime\prime}(z)+(1-

\displaystyle\alpha-\gamma)zP^{\prime}(z)+\alpha\gamma P(z)|

\displaystyle\leq|z^{2}F^{\prime\prime}(z)+(1-\alpha-\gamma)zF^{\prime}(z)+\alpha\gamma F(z)|.

(10)

The bound is sharp and equality holds for some point z in
|z|>1
if and only if
P(z)=e^{i\delta}F(z)
for some
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Since the result holds trivially true, if
P(z)=e^{i\delta}F(z)
for some
\delta\in\mathbb{R}.
Therefore, let
P(z)\neq e^{i\delta}F(z).
Consider the function
\phi(z)=P^{*}(z)/F^{*}(z)
where
F^{*}(z)=z^{n}\overline{F(1/\overline{z})}
and
P^{*}(z)=z^{n}\overline{P(1/\overline{z})}.
Since
F(z)
has its all zeros in
|z|\leq 1,
then
F^{*}(z)
has no zero in
|z|<1.

This implies that the rational function
\phi(z)
is analytic for
|z|\leq 1.
Also,
|P(z)|=|P^{*}(z)|
and
|F(z)|=|F^{*}(z)|
for
|z|=1,
therefore,
|\phi(z)|\leq 1
for
|z|=1.
By invoking Maximum modulus theorem, we obtain;

\displaystyle|\phi(z)|<1\qquad\text{for}\quad|z|<1.

On replacing
z
by
1/z
in the above inequality, we get
|P(z)|<|F(z)|
for
|z|>1.
It follows by Rouche’s theorem that for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\lambda|\geq 1,
the polynomial
P(z)-\lambda F(z)
of degree
n
has all its zeros in
|z|\leq 1.
Applying Lemma
2
to the polynomial
G(z)=P(z)-\lambda F(z),
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re(\alpha)\leq n/2
, we obtain that the polynomial

\displaystyle zG^{\prime}(z)-\alpha G(z)

\displaystyle=z(P^{\prime}(z)-\lambda F^{\prime}(z))-\alpha(P(z)-\lambda F(z))

\displaystyle=(zP^{\prime}(z)-\alpha P(z))-\lambda(zF^{\prime}(z)-\alpha F(z))

has all zeros in
|z|\leq 1.

This implies

\displaystyle|zP^{\prime}(z)-\alpha P(z)|\leq|zF^{\prime}(z)-\alpha F(z)|\qquad\text{ for}\quad|z|>1.

(11)

If inequality (
11
) were not true, then there is some point
w\in\mathbb{C}
with
|w|>1
such that

|wP^{\prime}(w)-\alpha P(w)|>|wF^{\prime}(w)-\alpha F(w)|.
By Lemma
1
,
wF^{\prime}(w)-\alpha F(w)\neq 0.
Now, choose
\lambda=-\frac{wP^{\prime}(w)-\alpha P(w)}{wF^{\prime}(w)-\alpha F(w)}
and note that
\lambda
is a well defined complex number with modulus greater than 1. So, with this choice of
\lambda
, one can easily observe that
w
is a zero of
zG^{\prime}(z)-\alpha G(z)
of modulus greater than one.
This is a contradiction, since all the zeros of this polynomial lie in
|z|\leq 1.

Hence, the inequality (
11
) is true, by continuity (
11
) also holds for
|z|=1.
This proves the inequality (
9
).

Finally, if we take
H(z)=zP^{\prime}(z)-\alpha P(z)
and
K(z)=zF^{\prime}(z)-\alpha F(z)
with
\Re(\alpha)\leq n/2,
then by inequality (
9
),
|H(z)|\leq|K(z)|
for
|z|=1.
Therefore, by using inequality (
9
) again, for
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re(\gamma)\leq n/2,
we get,
|zH^{\prime}(z)-\gamma H(z)|\leq|zK^{\prime}(z)-\gamma K(z)|

|z|\geq 1,
which is equivalent to (

.

Following the similar lines and again using Lemma
5
, one can conclude that the operator
\Lambda_{1}
which sends
P(z)
to

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}P(z)

\displaystyle=\left(z^{2}P^{\prime\prime}(z)+(1-\alpha-\gamma)zP^{\prime}(z)+\alpha\gamma P(z)\right)e^{i\delta}+z^{n}\overline{N(1/\overline{z})}

\displaystyle=\left[\left(n(n-\alpha-\gamma)+\alpha\gamma\right)e^{i\delta}+\overline{\alpha\gamma}\right]a_{n}z^{n}+\dots+\left[\alpha\gamma e^{i\delta}+n\overline{(n-\alpha-\gamma)}\right]a_{0}

is also an admissible operator for every
\alpha,\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re(\alpha)\leq n/2
,
\Re(\gamma)\leq n/2
and
\delta
real. Again, applying lemma
6
with
\phi(x)=x^{p},
where
p>0,
we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}

\displaystyle\left|\left(e^{i2\theta}P^{\prime\prime}(e^{i\theta})+(1-\alpha-\gamma)e^{i\theta}P^{\prime}(e^{i\theta})+\alpha\gamma P(e^{i\theta})\right)e^{i\delta}+e^{in\theta}\overline{N(e^{i\theta})}\right|^{p}d\theta\qquad\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\left|\left(n(n-\alpha-\gamma)+\alpha\gamma\right)e^{i\delta}+\overline{\alpha\gamma}\right|^{p}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}|P(e^{i\theta})|^{p}d\theta.

This completes the proof of lemma
7
.
∎

3.
Proofs of the theorems

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Let
z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{k}
be the zeros of
P(z)
which lie in
|z|>1,
where
0\leq k\leq n,
then all the zeros of the polynomial

\displaystyle T(z)=P(z)\prod_{j=1}^{k}\dfrac{1-\bar{z_{j}}z}{z-z_{j}}

lie in
|z|\leq 1.
Moreover,
|P(z)|=|T(z)|
for
|z|=1,
therefore, by Lemma
3
, we have for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re(\alpha)\leq n/2,

\displaystyle|zP^{\prime}(z)-\alpha P(z)|

\displaystyle\leq|zT^{\prime}(z)-\alpha T(z)|\qquad\text{for}\quad|z|\geq 1,

which in particular gives for
p>0,

\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\left|e^{i\theta}P^{\prime}(e^{i\theta})-\alpha P(e^{i\theta})\right|^{p}d\theta\leq\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\left|e^{i\theta}T^{\prime}(e^{i\theta})-\alpha T(e^{i\theta})\right|^{p}d\theta.

(17)

Since all the zeros of polynomial
T(z)
lies in
|z|\leq 1,
then by lemma
2
, the polynomial

zT^{\prime}(z)-\alpha T(z)
also has all its zeros in
|z|\leq 1
for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re(\alpha)\leq n/2
.
Therefore, if
T(z)=c_{n}z^{n}+c_{n-1}z^{n-1}+\ldots+c_{1}z+c_{0}
, then the operator
\Lambda_{\delta}
defined by

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\delta}T(z)

\displaystyle=zT^{\prime}(z)-\alpha T(z)

\displaystyle=(n-\alpha)c_{n}z^{n}+\ldots-\alpha c_{0}

is admissible. Hence by lemma
6
with
\phi(x)=x^{p}
where
p>0
, we have

\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\left|e^{i\theta}T^{\prime}(e^{i\theta})-\alpha T(e^{i\theta})\right|^{p}d\theta\leq(c(\delta))^{p}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}|T(e^{i\theta})|^{p}d\theta,

(18)

where
c(\delta)=\max(|n-\alpha|,|\alpha|)
.

Since
\Re(\alpha)\leq n/2
then
c(\delta)=|n-\alpha|
. Thus from (
18
), we have

\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\left|e^{i\theta}T^{\prime}(e^{i\theta})-\alpha T(e^{i\theta})\right|^{p}d\theta\leq|n-\alpha|^{p}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}|T(e^{i\theta})|^{p}d\theta.

(19)

Combining inequalities (
17
) and (
19
) and noting that
|T(e^{i\theta})|=|P(e^{i\theta})|
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\left|e^{i\theta}P^{\prime}(e^{i\theta})-\alpha P(e^{i\theta})\right|^{p}d\theta\leq|n-\alpha|^{p}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}|P(e^{i\theta})|^{p}d\theta.

This proves inequality (
4
) of theorem
1
for
p>0
. The inequality (
5
) of theorem
1
follows similarly by replacing
P(z)
by
zP^{\prime}(z)-\alpha P(z)
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re(\gamma)\leq n/2
. To obtain these results for
p=0
, we simply make
p\rightarrow 0+
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2
.

By hypothesis
P(z)
does not vanish in
z<1
, therefore by (
12
) of lemma
4
for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re(\alpha)\leq n/2
and
0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi,

|zP^{\prime}(z)-\alpha P(z)|\leq|zQ^{\prime}(z)-\alpha Q(z)|,

(20)

where
Q(z)=z^{n}\overline{P(1/\overline{z})}
.
Also by (
15
) of lemma
7
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|e^{i\beta}F(\theta)+e^{in\theta}\overline{M(e^{i\theta})}\right|^{p}d\theta\leq\left|\left(n-\alpha\right)e^{i\beta}-\overline{\alpha}\right|^{p}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}|P(e^{i\theta})|^{p}d\theta,

(21)

where
F(\theta)=e^{i\theta}P^{\prime}(e^{i\theta})-\alpha P(e^{i\theta})
and

.
Integrating both sides of (

) with respect to
\beta
from
0
to
2\pi

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|e^{i\beta}F(\theta)+e^{in\theta}\overline{M(e^{i\theta})}\right|^{p}d\theta d\beta\leq\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\left(n-\alpha\right)e^{i\beta}-\overline{\alpha}\right|^{p}d\beta\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}|P(e^{i\theta})|^{p}d\theta.

(22)

Now for every real
\beta,

t\geq 1
and
p>0
, we have

\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}|t+e^{i\beta}|^{p}d\beta\geq\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}|1+e^{i\beta}|^{p}d\beta
.

If
F(\theta)\neq 0
, we take
t=|M(e^{i\theta})/F(\theta)|
, then by (
20
),
t\geq 1
and we get

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\left|e^{i\beta}F(\theta)+e^{in\theta}\overline{M(e^{i\theta})}\right|^{p}d\beta

\displaystyle=|F(\theta)|^{p}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\left|e^{i\beta}+\frac{e^{in\theta}\overline{M(e^{i\theta})}}{F(\theta)}\right|^{p}d\beta

\displaystyle=|F(\theta)|^{p}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\left|e^{i\beta}+\left|\frac{M(e^{i\theta})}{F(\theta)}\right|\right|^{p}d\beta

\displaystyle\geq|F(\theta)|^{p}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}|1+e^{i\beta}|^{p}d\beta.

For

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}|F(\theta)|^{p}d\theta\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}|1+e^{i\beta}|^{p}d\beta\leq\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\left(n-\alpha\right)e^{i\beta}-\overline{\alpha}\right|^{p}d\beta\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}|P(e^{i\theta})|^{p}d\theta.

the desired inequality (

Proof of Theorem

Since

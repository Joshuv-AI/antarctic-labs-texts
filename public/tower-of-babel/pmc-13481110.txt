Endoscopic submucosal injection of umbilical cord mesenchymal stem cells for radiation-induced late rectal injury
Authors: Haoyu Xu, Yu Chang, He Zhou, Fangyu Wang, Juan Wei, Lianming Liao, Ji Xuan
Published: 2026

A case description

Video 1 Demonstrates the endoscopic UC-MSC injection technique: submucosal saline elevation to confirm needle placement, followed by slow, evenly distributed four-point injection around the ulcer margin.

Radiation-induced late rectal injury (RLRI) is a devastating complication of pelvic radiotherapy, characterized by ulcers, mucosal erythema, telangiectasia and fibrosis, 1​ yet there is currently no standardized treatment strategy for this condition. Mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) have shown promising safety and efficacy in the local treatment of active intestinal lesions. 2​ ​3​ We report a case of RLRI treated with endoscopic submucosal injection of umbilical cord-derived mesenchymal stem cells (UC-MSCs; Video 1 ). A 65-year-old female patient with cervical cancer presented with recurrent hematochezia and rectal pain 6 months after completing concurrent chemoradiotherapy. In December 2023, colonoscopy performed at an outside hospital led to a diagnosis of RLRI. Despite conventional treatments including oral and enema mesalazine, oral hemostatic agents and symptomatic analgesia, her symptoms progressively worsened. In June 2024, the patient was admitted to our hospital. Endoscopic examination revealed a large deep rectal ulcer measuring approximately 5×5 cm ( Fig. 1 ). The total dose of UC-MSCs for injection was 3×10⁷ cells, with a total volume of 6 mL. Under colonoscopy guidance, the cell suspension was injected submucosally at four points evenly distributed around the ulcer margin. Normal saline was first injected to elevate the submucosa to confirm the proper needle placement before each MSC injection, with 1.5 mL of cell suspension administered per site ( Fig. 2 ). No adverse events occurred during treatment and 1-year follow-up. The patient’s hematochezia and pain symptoms resolved completely. Endoscopic examination showed complete ulcer healing with scar formation at 1 year. The LENT–SOMA score decreased from 1.64 to 0.07, and Vienna Rectosigmoidoscopic Score (VRS) and Rectal Telangiectasia Density Score (RTD) also improved significantly ( Fig. 3 ). Imaging and histopathological findings confirmed a marked reduction of local inflammation and edema ( Figs. 4 and 5 ). Endoscopic local injection of UC-MSCs is a safe and effective novel therapy for refractory RLRI, which can promote mucosal healing and prevent severe complications.

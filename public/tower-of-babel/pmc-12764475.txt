Integrated optical and radar remote sensing for litho-structural mapping of the Kerdous and Ait Abdellah inliers, western Anti-Atlas, Morocco
Authors: Jaouad Aadaj, Abdelhalim Tabit, Ahmed Algouti, Ali Aydda, Said Moujane, Saloua Agli, Abdelouahed Farah, Maryam Errami, Ali Shebl
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Although field investigations constitute the cornerstone of geological studies, yet its application is often constrained by limited accessibility, time consumption, and high costs particularly in complex or remote terrains. However, recent advances in remote sensing technologies have significantly improved the ability to map large and inaccessible areas. In this regard, this study presents one of the detailed investigations integrating both optical and radar remote sensing data for the Kerdous and Ait Abdellah inliers in the western Anti-Atlas, Morocco. It aims to evaluate the effectiveness of Landsat 8 OLI and Sentinel-1 SAR datasets for litho-structural mapping, while also providing updated and detailed geological maps for both inliers. Several image processing methods were applied to Landsat 8 data for lithological discrimination, while Sentinel-1 data were used to extract structural lineaments. The results obtained show the effectiveness of PCA in refining lithological outcrops by clearly identifying facies such as quartzite, gabbro, rhyolite, and sedimentary breccias. Also, RGB composites (B2, B5, B7) allowed the visualization of formations like limestone, dolomite, and clay, while band ratio analysis revealed facies such as the Lie-de-vin shales, gabbro, and leucogranite. Moreover, lineament analysis reveals significant tectonic implications, highlighting predominant fault orientations of NE–SW in the Ait Abdellah inlier and NW–SE in the Kerdous inlier. Field verification and correlation with existing geological maps demonstrate this study’s contribution to identifying additional geological features, such as recent clay and scree deposits, previously undocumented or poorly represented. The findings highlight the enhanced accuracy and reliability of integrating multisensor remote sensing data for litho-structural mapping, providing a scalable framework for future investigations. However, it should be noted that the main limitations potentially affecting the results are the spatial and spectral resolution of the satellite data and the characteristics of arid and semi-arid terrains.

Introduction

Inliers, representing crystalline basement outcropping at Earth’s surface primarily due to erosion, are critical geological features whose accurate mapping is paramount for successful mineral exploration1. Field-based investigation is widely recognized as the most accurate and fundamental approach for geological mapping, forming the basis for natural resource exploration, hazard assessment, and environmental studies2. However, comprehensive field coverage is often limited by logistical constraints, including difficult terrain accessibility, high costs, and time-consuming operations. The rapid advancements in remote sensing technology have fundamentally transformed geological mapping, significantly reducing both the time and expense involved in map production. Consequently, numerous studies now underscore the critical role of satellite image processing in extracting vital information concerning lithology and other key geological structures3–5.

In this context, numerous recent studies demonstrating Landsat 8 OLI’s (Operational Land Imager) robust capabilities. For instance, Ranjithkumar et al.6 successfully employed Landsat 8 OLI to map intricate lithologies like syenite, pyroxenite, and carbonatite using a combination of color composites, band ratios, PCA, and SVM classification. Similarly, Kamel. M et al.7 effectively utilized Landsat 8 OLI imagery for Neo-proterozoic basement rock mapping in the Igla area of Egypt’s Central Eastern Desert, proving its utility for basement geology through enhancement techniques and supervised classification. Further reinforcing its application in similar geological settings, Hassan and Sadek8 applied Landsat 8 OLI data to delineate lithological units in the Meatiq dome area, CED, Egypt, showcasing its ability to distinguish granitoids, amphibolites, and mafic rocks with advanced band ratio combinations and PC images. Landsat 8 OLI has also proven instrumental in discriminating specific mineral-bearing granitoids as shown by Ousmanou et al.9 in Cameroon, and in mapping diverse volcanic and intrusive units like the Deccan Traps10 and Kula basalts11. Its utility extends to delineating various granitic phases with high precision, as demonstrated by AbdelMaksoud and El-Arafy12 in southern Sinai, utilizing FCC with decorrelation stretch and selective PCA.

Beyond the Anti-Atlas, numerous recent studies have further emphasized the value of integrating optical and radar remote sensing for litho-structural mapping and geological characterization. For instance, advanced approaches combining multispectral and SAR data have proven effective in delineating lithological units and structural features in complex terrains13–16. These works highlight the increasing importance of synergizing diverse satellite datasets to achieve higher accuracy in geological mapping. Such perspectives reinforce the relevance of our study and ensure that its findings are positioned within the wider context of contemporary geological research.

These diverse applications consistently highlight Landsat 8 OLI’s comprehensive spectral band set encompassing visible, near-infrared, and, crucially, short-wave infrared (SWIR) bands (e.g., bands 6 and 7) which are highly effective for identifying and differentiating rock-forming minerals and alteration zones typical of crystalline basement complexes. Moreover, its proven versatility and efficacy in accurately discriminating diverse lithologies and geological structures across complex terrains, particularly arid and semi-arid crystalline basement areas, which makes it particularly suitable for the selected study area within the Anti-Atlas range, Morocco.

The Anti-Atlas orogenic domain, located at the northwestern margin of the West African Craton (WAC), represents the transitional zone towards the orogenic domains of northern Morocco. Structurally, the Anti-Atlas corresponds to a major Atlasic uplift oriented ENE–WSW, superimposed on Hercynian folding. Within this belt, the cores of anticlines expose the Proterozoic crystalline basement in the form of inliers17. Although this domain constitutes a well-known metallogenic province and restricted access to certain areas complicates field-based geological investigations18, it has nonetheless been the focus of numerous studies using remote sensing data across its various sectors. In the eastern Anti-Atlas, Baid et al.19, Es-Sabbar et al.20, and Ouhoussa et al.21 carried out lithological, mineralogical, and structural mapping in the Igoudrane, Ras Kammouna, and Oumjrane-Boukerzia areas, respectively. Furthermore, several studies have focused on lineament mapping using Sentinel-1 radar data; for instance, Adiri et al.22 examined the Sidi Flah-Bouskour inlier. In the western Anti-Atlas, Adiri et al.23 mapped lithological units in the Bas Drâa inlier, one of the largest inliers in the extreme southwest, while Hajaj et al.24 investigated the Ameln Valley shear zone. Unlike other inliers and sectors in the Anti-Atlas, the Aït Abdellah and Kerdous inliers present challenges for remote sensing-based mapping, with the exception of a study conducted in the northern part of the Kerdous inlier by Jellouli et al.25. In this context, the present study aims to address this gap and overcome the scarcity of geospatial research in the region, while also evaluating the potential of combining optical and radar data for litho-structural mapping. This study specifically focuses on the both inliers within Morocco’s western Anti-Atlas. Despite their independent nature, these inliers present geological similarities, yet a notable gap exists in correlated satellite imagery-based mapping for the region. To address this, our research undertakes a crucial comparative analysis across both inliers. We aim to determine the most effective remote sensing processing techniques for lithological mapping using Landsat 8 OLI data, while also automatically extracting lineaments from Sentinel-1 SAR imagery. This integrated approach will culminate in the production of comprehensive litho-structural maps for both areas.

The Ait Abdellah inlier lies in the Taroudant Province of the Souss-Massa Region, approximately 150 km east of Tiznit, within a transitional zone between the coastal plain and the Anti-Atlas mountain chains. Its geographical extent is defined by the following coordinates: Point 1—29° 48′ 13.1″ N, 8° 49′ 17.1″ W; Point 2—29° 48′ 11.8″ N, 8° 38′ 56.0″ W; Point 3—29° 39′ 33.4″ N, 8° 38′ 57.8″ W; and Point 4—29° 39′ 34.6″ N, 8° 49′ 18.0″ W. This inlier corresponds to a Hercynian submeridian bulge oriented north–south, exposing the Proterozoic basement beneath a thin Precambrian and Adoudounian detrital cover27. The oldest rocks belong to the Paleoproterozoic, represented by epimetamorphic schists that have been the subject of geochronological studies in several inliers, including Zenaga, Kerdous, and Tagragra de Tata, which host the oldest known rocks of the Anti-Atlas. The Neoproterozoic is dominated by volcanic and volcano-sedimentary series, mainly conglomeratic28, poorly stratified, and discordant over Cryogenian paleo-reliefs. These reliefs consist largely of quartzites of the Azaghar Formation, equivalent to the Lkest Quartzites (Kerdous Group). Above the conglomerates, a transgressive–regressive Late Neoproterozoic (Infracambrian or Adoudounian) succession is present, composed of thin carbonate deposits (small limestones) alternating with sandstones and siltstones.

The southern part of the Kerdous inlier, situated in the Province of Sidi Ifni (Guelmim-Oued Noun Region) about 50 km southeast of Tiznit, is geographically defined by the following coordinates: Point 1—29° 28′ 35.4″ N, 9° 39′ 21.5″ W; Point 2—29° 28′ 39.1″ N, 9° 23′ 33.3″ W; Point 3—29° 17′ 50.1″ N, 9° 23′ 30.8″ W; and Point 4—29° 17′ 46.4″ N, 9° 39′ 17.4″ W. This inlier is composed of Paleoproterozoic and Infracambrian formations, including schists and granitoids29, as well as quartzitic sandstones of the Lkest Group. The stratigraphy further includes volcano-sedimentary layers and intrusive bodies of granitic to microgranitic composition30,31. Overlying these units are the Adoudounian and lowermost Cambrian formations, with thicknesses reaching up to 2 km, which are in contact with the Neoproterozoic Ouarzazate Supergroup. This supergroup is characterized by conglomerates, sandstones, shales, and limestones, often interbedded with volcanic rocks.

During the Early Cambrian, the Western Anti-Atlas formed the axial zone of the Anti-Atlasic rift. The upper part of the Lower Cambrian succession, extending from the Lower Limestones Formation to the Schist Series Formation, is regarded as a post-rift sequence32. These carbonate and clastic deposits are overlain by the “Lie-de-Vin” Formation, consisting of shales, siltstones, and sandstones, and subsequently by the Middle Cambrian “Feijas Internes” Group, dominated by sandstones.

Both the Ait Abdellah and Kerdous inliers record a complex tectonic history, including the Eburnean orogeny (Paleoproterozoic), the Pan-African orogeny (Neoproterozoic), the Hercynian deformation (Late Paleozoic), and the Alpine orogeny (Cenozoic)33. For lineaments, several studies have shown the effectiveness of using Sentinel 1 Radar data as22 which showed that Sentinel 1 gives better results than the optical sensors, especially with the VH polarization. Also, Mohcine et al.39 demonstrate that Sentine 1 data have a significant performance in automatically lineaments mapping compared to other SAR sensors (PALSAR, Radarsat-1), contrary to the other data which did not correlate with the different parameters studied, which is coherent with previous observations by Javhar et al.40, Shebl and Csamer41,42 comparing multispectral data with sentinel-1 data. Sentinel-1 imagery shows higher efficiency in lineament extraction, especially with VH polarization, which is consistent with previous observations by22. In this context, we collected Sentinel 1 Radar data, acquired in December 21 2023, from the European Space Agency Scientific Data Hub (ESA), (scihub.copernicus.eu) The collected sentinel 1 data is L1 Ground Range Detected (GRD) product of Interferometric Wide (IW) mode two polarizations VH (Vertical-Horizontal) and VV (Vertical–Vertical) (Table 1). In this study we based in VH polarization according to previous studies such as the study by (Mohcine et al.39), Several studies have demonstrated that lineaments extracted from Sentinel-1 VH polarization data exhibit a stronger correlation with geological units, tectonic trends, as well as with slope and shaded relief maps. This is attributed to the VH polarization’s enhanced capability in capturing structural and surface variations. In addition, Surajit et al.43, the distinction between VV and VH polarizations is particularly evident in flood mapping applications. VV polarization generally yields more accurate results due to its heightened sensitivity to surface roughness. In contrast, VH polarization is more responsive to vegetation canopy and excels at capturing volumetric backscatter. This type of scattering is especially useful for analyzing forest structures, identifying crop types, and monitoring vegetation growth stages.

Preprocessing

The initial step in preprocessing Landsat 8 OLI images includes radiometric calibration and atmospheric correction utilizing the FLAASH (Fast Line-of-sight Atmospheric Analysis of Spectral Hypercubes) atmospheric correction model. This model incorporates the Moderate Resolution Radiative Transfer Code (MODTRAN), developed by the Air Force Research Laboratory and Spectral Sciences, Inc., to mitigate atmospheric attenuations, resulting in the production of reflectance images44. In this study, Gram-Schmidt Pan Sharpening (GSPS) method was used to increase the spatial resolution of the multispectral Landsat 8 data bands (2 to 7) in accordance with spatial-resolution of the panchromatic data band 8 that had 15 m of spatial resolution. The GSPS method consists to: (1) simulate a lower spatial resolution panchromatic band from the lower spatial resolution spectral bands, (2) perform a transformation on the simulated band and multipsctral bands, (3) swapp the high spatial resolution panchromatic band with the first transfomed band, and then (4) apply the inverse transform to form the pan-sharpened high spatial resolution spectral bands45.

On the other hand, the collected Sentinel 1 scene was preprocessed using SNAP Toolbox software’s. Several preprocessing steps was applied, including radiometric correction, speckle filtering and terrain correction. Radiometric Calibration consists to the process of transforming raw radar signal measurements into significant physical quantities, particularly the radar backscatter coefficient. This process requires adjustments to the data to consider various influencing factors, such as sensor characteristics, atmospheric conditions, and the radar system’s geometry. The primary objective is to ensure that the radar images faithfully represent the Earth’s surface properties, facilitating accurate analysis and interpretation of the data46.

Radar imagery must undergo preprocessing steps such as speckle filtering and terrain correction (orthorectification). These processes help minimize speckle noise and ensure accurate geometric alignment, allowing the image to closely represent the true surface of the Earth40.

a. Lithological mapping

a. Lithological mapping

For lithology, we processed the enhanced multispectral landsat data based on existing methods in literature, including color composition (CC), principal component analysis (PCA), and band ratio (BR). Color composites are created by combining specific spectral bands to enhance the reflectance characteristics of surface materials, thereby highlighting the spectral signatures of different minerals and rock types47. In this study, we combined the bands 2, 5, and 7 in RGB composition. The B2 (Blue, 0.45–0.51 µm), B5 (Near Infrared, 0.85–0.88 µm), B7 (Shortwave Infrared 2, 2.11–2.29 µm) combination is excellent for highlighting geological formations. The shortwave infrared band (B7) helps in identifying different rock types and mineral deposits41,42, while the near-infrared band (B5) can distinguish between vegetation and bare soil. The blue band (B2) adds detail to the overall image, enhancing the visibility of geological features.

The PCA method serves as an effective method for enhancing redundant spectral information into a smaller set of components, facilitating detailed geological interpretations48–52. It is largely used to minimize the effect of the topography in geological mapping. In this study, we used the RGB composition of the band ratios 4/2, 5/4, and 6/7, the combination of these band ratios, namely the 4/2 ratio, proves useful for mapping iron oxides due to its absorption in the blue wavelength range while exhibiting high reflectance in the red wavelength range. The 6/7 ratio was selected in this study for its ability to map kaolinite, montmorillonite, clay minerals, and carbonate minerals, with distinctive reflectance characteristics on bands 6 and 7. For 5/4 ratio, igneous and metamorphic rocks often display a more pronounced spectral signature in the band 5 (near infrared), whereas sedimentary rocks exhibit higher reflectance in the band 453.b. Automatic lineament extraction

b. Automatic lineament extraction

b. Automatic lineament extraction

Lineament mapping holds paramount importance in geological studies54, as these linear or curvilinear structures are often associated with various geomorphological elements or tectonic formations55. Lineaments can manifest as structural alignments56, geomorphological consequences57, zones of structural weakness58, faults and fractures59,60. They can also serve as markers to delineate different lithological units or variations in vegetation cover61,62. It is imperative to eliminate lineaments that do not correspond to geological contacts, with validation through geological maps and field investigations necessary at the end of the process.

For lineament extraction, a directional filter with four orientations (N0, N45, N90, N135) was applied on the corrected sentinel 1 images using ENVI software. The directional filters are commonly used to identify unknown structural elements such as faults, fractures, folds, and lithology contacts63. In general various algorithms have been developed for automatic lineament extraction using satellite images and digital terrain models. The LINE (LINeament Extraction) module integrated into PCI Geomatica software is particularly popular in geological studies for its automatic detection of lineaments, providing satisfactory results with both optical and radar data64.

In this study, the LINE module was applied with the following parameter settings: a filter radius of 10 pixels, edge gradient threshold of 100, curve length threshold of 30 pixels, line fitting error threshold of 3 pixels, angular difference threshold of 15 degrees, and linking distance threshold of 100 pixels. These values were selected based on a combination of literature references and iterative visual inspection to optimize the extraction of structurally meaningful lineaments while minimizing noise. The chosen thresholds ensured the detection of linear features corresponding to real geological structures, as confirmed by comparison with existing geological maps and the excursions.

Field investigations

Field investigations and validation are crucial for confirming the accuracy of remote sensing results, as they provide essential ground truth data that enhances the reliability of lithological and lineament mapping. This ensures that the interpretations derived from satellite imagery align with the actual geological conditions observed in the Anti-Atlas region. As part of this study, fieldwork was conducted in both the Kerdous and Aït Abdellah inliers to ensure high-quality results and effective integration of field data with image processing techniques.

In the Aït Abdellah inlier, investigations were carried out in the Tazalaght village area as well as in its southern part, targeting both basement and sedimentary cover formations. Similarly, in the Kerdous inlier, field observations focused on the surroundings of Tighirt and Izouniden villages, where the contact between basement rocks and overlying sedimentary units is clearly exposed. These strategic locations were chosen due to their high lithological diversity and the presence of the most representative facies. This approach significantly improved the precision of the interpretations and helped validate the conclusions drawn from the applied remote sensing methods.

Discussion

Lithology

The integration of Landsat 8 OLI satellite data with advanced image processing techniques has significantly enhanced lithological mapping capabilities in the Anti-Atlas region. This study provides a comprehensive analysis of image processing methods, including RGB composite visualization, Principal Component Analysis (PCA), and band ratios, to delineate lithological variations in the Ait Abdellah and Kerdous inliers.

The use of RGB composites, specifically bands b2, b5, and b7, enabled a clear visual representation of lithological diversity, facilitating the identification of facies such as limestone, dolomite, scree, clay, and shale. This RGB approach effectively highlighted the unique spectral signatures associated with distinct units. Additionally, PCA proved to be a powerful technique for lithological discrimination, allowing the identification of further lithologies such as metamorphic and magmatic rocks (shale, quartzite, gabbro, and rhyolite), which are often indistinct in simple RGB composites and band ratios. For sedimentary rocks, PCA enabled the identification of formations such as limestone, dolomite, sedimentary breccias, conglomerates, and alluvium.

Band ratios were also employed to enhance spectral reflectance contrasts between various lithological facies, focusing on specific mineralogical properties. As demonstrated by Ranjbari et al.63, these ratios are particularly effective in distinguishing units with similar spectral signatures, thus complementing PCA and RGB composite results. The facies identified through band ratios include the shales of the Lie-de-vin series, scree, recent alluvial deposits, and magmatic rocks such as gabbro and leucogranite, which are characteristic of the Kerdous inlier (Fig. 18).Fig. 18Density of lineaments in both inliers with their rose diagrams below, and statistical data for each area: (A) Ait Abdellah inlier, (B) Kerdous inlier (Created by Qgis v. 3.34.3. software; https://qgis.org/).

Incorporating manual digitization in GIS further strengthened the process by creating detailed maps that clearly represented the spatial distribution of different lithological units. Comparative studies confirm the value of these techniques across varied geological contexts. For instance, Amer et al.65 illustrated the efficacy of PCA and band ratios in mapping ophiolitic lithologies (serpentinite, metagabbro, metabasalt) and granitic lithologies (grey and pink granites) in the Arabian-Nubian Shield. Similarly, Rezaei et al.66 demonstrated that band ratios effectively differentiate units such as metasediments, volcaniclastic sandstones, and granitoids in the Sangan region, Iran. Furthermore, Khalifa et al.67 highlighted PCA as a prime technique for lithological discrimination due to its time- and cost-efficiency in tectonically active zones. In a similar context, Gasmi et al.68 showed that PCA refines and corrects existing geological maps, revealing new insights into previously unknown surface formations in southern Tunisia.

Applying these three techniques to the Ait Abdellah and Kerdous inliers reveals notable similarities in the facies coloration observed. For example, the RGB color composition showed that Ediacaran limestone, dolomites, scree, and conglomerates are distinct in both regions. PCA analysis also revealed analogies for green shales and limestones, while band ratios highlighted facies such as Jbel Azaghar quartzites, compared to leucogranites in the Kerdous basement.

The observed variations in the appearance of certain lithological facies between the Kerdous and Ait Abdellah inliers can be attributed to the differences in mineralogical composition and the presence of extraneous elements. Alterations in mineralogical composition influence the interaction of lithological facies with electromagnetic radiation, as observed in the granitic basement of the Kerdous inlier and the conglomerates at the center of the Ait Abdellah inlier, resulting in variations in their spectral signatures. Additionally, contamination by neighboring lithological units further complicates spectral reflectance analysis, highlighting the importance of advanced image processing techniques for precise lithological mapping and underscoring the sensitivity of optical data to lithological variation.

Lineament

The combination of remote sensing and field data has enabled the production of a high-precision structural map of the Ait Abdellah and Kerdous inlier. Most of the identified lineaments in follow the main directions of lineaments already present in the geological map of the area. The use of radar data proves to be more effective than optical data. Sentinel-1 data are preferred over optical data such as Landsat 8 OLI or ASTER because the results of lineament extraction from radar satellites provide more accurate directions, unlike optical images sensitive to land cover69,70. Smaller lengths compared to those extracted from optical data (OLI and ASTER) characterize lineaments identified by radar data. This difference once again highlights the effectiveness of radar data, which do not depend on the nature of the soils unlike optical images. In our study, we mainly used the VH image, according to22 it has proven to be more effective than the VV image. A greater number of identifications compared to those from the VV image, highlighting the efficiency of radar data, distinguishes lineaments extracted from VH images.

Analysis of the structural map of Ait Abdellah inlier reveals a general predominance of NE-SW to NNE-SSW oriented lineaments (Fig. 18). Most of the identified lineaments are associated with Jurassic dolerite dykes and others are linked to quartzite basement bombast. Following Hercynian deformation, likely associated with the opening of the Atlantic, intruded NE-SW fractures. In the Anti-Atlas domain, rocks dating from the Triassic and Jurassic periods are exclusively of magmatic origin. This suite of NE-SW oriented dolerite dykes marks the deep basement of the Anti-Atlas71. This dolerite dyke traverses a significant part of the western Anti-Atlas for over 100 km, cutting through both the Precambrian basement and the Paleozoic cover in the Taroudant-Irherm region. Although it was initially attributed to the Hercynian phase according to the 1:100,000 geological map of Tafraout, its oblique arrangement relative to Hercynian structures, as well as its Jurassic age; observed further east at Foum Zguid, where it runs parallel72, raise questions about this attribution. The basement lineaments of the Kerdous inlier exhibit a predominant of SE-NW oriented lineaments with a percentage of NE-SW direction lineaments following Hercynian deformation (Fig. 18) SE-NW direction is notably associated with Mesoproterozoic gabbroic and doleritic dykes. These dykes, formed during the Mesoproterozoic era, play a crucial role in defining the structural framework of the region. Furthermore, this N135° orientation is not limited to these dykes; it is also observed in the trondhjemitic sills and tonalites, which are aligned along the same direction as indicated on the Tlat Ida Goumar 1:50,000 map and BouIzakarn 1:100,000. This consistent orientation suggests a significant tectonic control that has influenced the emplacement of these rocks.

Furthermore, several studies49,50,73 have integrated lineament density as a key factor in the evaluation of geothermal potential. These studies have also demonstrated that lineament density is a crucial parameter linking tectonic structuring with lithological characteristics. In our study, the combination of lineament density and dykes with the major faults known in the area allowed the identification of dominant orientations, their connection to regional tectonic evolution, and the improvement of lithological discrimination by reducing cartographic uncertainties.

Conclusion

This research demonstrates the value of integrating Landsat 8 OLI optical data and Sentinel-1 SAR radar data for the generation of litho-structural maps of the Aït Abdellah and Kerdous inliers in the western Anti-Atlas, Morocco. The combined approach allows for enhanced lithological discrimination and automatic lineament extraction, which was subsequently validated through field investigations. These findings underscore the utility of remote sensing in arid and semi-arid regions, where fieldwork is often constrained by accessibility, cost, and time.

Leveraging the strengths of Landsat 8 OLI for complex lithology discrimination and Sentinel-1 SAR for structural feature detection, this study makes a novel contribution to the geological understanding of the Anti-Atlas inliers. It provides both a validated methodological framework and detailed litho-structural maps, forming a robust basis for future mineral exploration and natural resource management initiatives.

Future prospects should include the integration of higher-resolution data, such as hyperspectral imaging and LiDAR, combined with isotopic and geophysical approaches, in order to refine the tectono-stratigraphic evolution of the Neoproterozoic in the Western Anti-Atlas. Such a multidisciplinary approach will not only enhance scientific understanding of crustal dynamics in the Anti-Atlas domain but also optimize exploration and groundwater management strategies in similar geological contexts worldwide.

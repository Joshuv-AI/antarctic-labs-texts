Successful treatment of B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia with SET-NUP214 rearrangement using venetoclax: A first case report and literature review
Authors: Gang Chen, Chao Min, Jing Xu, Jifu Zheng, Huihui Li, Zhenjiang Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL) harboring the SET-NUP214 fusion constitutes an exceedingly rare and aggressive subtype. While this genetic aberration is a well-recognized hallmark of T-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (T-ALL), its clinical implications and therapeutic landscape in the B-cell lineage remain largely elusive. Herein, we report the first documented case of relapsed/refractory SET-NUP214-positive B-ALL achieving a successful response to a salvage regimen combining venetoclax with Hyper-CVAD chemotherapy.A 25-year-old male presented with profound cytopenias and 95.5% bone marrow blasts. Flow cytometric analysis characterized the malignant population as pro-B-ALL, with immunophenotypic expression of CD34, CD38, HLA-DR, CD7, and CD58, alongside partial CD19 and cCD79a, and myeloid co-expression (CD13/CD33). Molecular profiling identified the SET-NUP214 fusion transcript, accompanied by concurrent JAK3, PHF6, and PTPN11 mutations. The patient experienced a short-term relapse following induction remission with the VDLP regimen, and subsequent re-induction with the VDEP regimen failed. Subsequently, MRD-negative remission was achieved after adding venetoclax during the mid-course of Hyper-CVAD-A chemotherapy. This deep response facilitated a successful bridge to allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (allo-HSCT), with the patient maintaining sustained remission for 24 months post-transplant. Our findings suggest that venetoclax-based combinations may provide a potent reinduction strategy for refractory SET-NUP214-positive ALL, underscoring BCL-2 inhibition as a pivotal therapeutic intervention for this high-risk genomic subset.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s00277-026-07190-y.

Introduction

Hematological malignancies are traditionally categorized by recurrent cytogenetic abnormalities. Among these, the SET-NUP214 fusion gene—resulting from either a balanced translocation t(9;9)(q34;q34) or a cryptic interstitial deletion at chromosome 9q34—represents a rare but recurring aberration. First identified in 1992 by von Lindern et al. in a case of acute undifferentiated leukemia (AUL) [1], this fusion often eludes detection via conventional karyotyping due to the subtle nature of the cryptic del(9)(q34.11;q34.13) or t(9;9)(q34;q34). Consequently, fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) or reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) remain the diagnostic gold standards. Although SET-NUP214-positive acute leukemia is relatively uncommon, it represents the second most frequent cytogenetic abnormality in T-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (T-ALL) [2]. Beyond the T-cell lineage, this fusion has been intermittently identified in acute myeloid leukemia (AML), AUL, mixed-phenotype acute leukemia (MPAL), and other lymphoid neoplasms [3–5]. Clinically, SET-NUP214-associated leukemias are characterized by a highly aggressive phenotype, marked by extensive extramedullary involvement, primary glucocorticoid resistance, and a dismal response to standard induction therapy, culminating in high relapse rates. Owing to its rarity, documented cases of B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL) harboring the SET-NUP214 fusion remain sporadic, with a notable lack of large-scale cohort data. Here, we report a case of relapsed/refractory B-ALL with SET-NUP214 fusion that achieved a deep second complete remission (CR2) following salvage therapy with venetoclax in combination with chemotherapy.

Case report

In June 2023, a 25-year-old male was admitted to the Second Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University (Jiangxi, China) presenting with a one-month history of progressive dizziness, fatigue, and generalized malaise. The patient’s family history was unremarkable for hereditary hematologic disorders. Physical examination revealed marked pallor of the skin and mucosal membranes, indicative of severe anemia. Initial laboratory evaluation demonstrated profound pancytopenia with paradoxical leukocytosis: hemoglobin 56 g/L, red blood cell (RBC) count 1.81 × 10¹²/L, white blood cell (WBC) count 57.86 × 10⁹/L (98.2% lymphocytes; absolute lymphocyte count 56.78 × 10⁹/L), and an absolute neutrophil count (ANC) of 0.70 × 10⁹/L. Peripheral blood smears identified 71% blasts. Bone marrow (BM) aspiration revealed hypercellularity with 95.5% blasts, predominantly comprised of medium-sized immature cells with round-to-irregular nuclei, fine chromatin, and inconspicuous nucleoli. Flow cytometric analysis characterized the malignant population as pro-B-ALL, with immunophenotypic expression of CD34, CD38, HLA-DR, CD7, and CD58, alongside partial CD19 and cCD79a, and myeloid co-expression (CD13/CD33). (See Supplementary Material 1 for details). BM biopsy demonstrated grade MF-1 reticulin fibrosis; immunohistochemical staining was positive for CD19, CD10, and CD34, with a BCL-2 expression rate of 70%. (Detailed pathological results are available in Supplementary Material 1). Cytogenetic analysis revealed a karyotype of 46,XY, del(11)(q22q24) [7]/46,XY [3]. Molecular profiling identified SET-NUP214 fusion transcripts and a WT1/ABL transcript ratio of 28.6%. Furthermore, next-generation sequencing (NGS) detected pathogenic mutations in JAK3, PHF6, JAK1, and PTPN11. (Detailed results are provided in Supplementary Material 1).

Induction therapy with the VDLP regimen (vincristine, daunorubicin, L-asparaginase, and prednisone) commenced four days after admission. Although the patient initially exhibited corticosteroid resistance, complete remission (CR) was ultimately achieved. However, the induction phase was complicated by Serratia marcescens sepsis and a subsequent left plantar abscess, necessitated surgical debridement. This postoperative recovery delayed consolidation therapy by over one month. Two months later (August 2023), the patient experienced a rapid hematological relapse with 17% marrow blasts. Reinduction with the VDEP regimen (vincristine, daunorubicin, etoposide, and prednisone) failed to achieve disease control. In the fourth month after initial presentation (October 2023), the disease had progressed further, with 76% marrow blasts and the emergence of a palpable 1 × 0.8 cm mass in the right upper eyelid, indicative of extramedullary involvement. On October 7, 2023, the Hyper-CVAD-A regimen (cyclophosphamide, vincristine, doxorubicin, and dexamethasone) was initiated; despite an initial transient response—characterized by the clearance of peripheral blasts and regression of the eyelid lesion—the disease progressed on day 10, with blasts re-emerging in the peripheral blood (28%) and a recurrence of the eyelid mass. Faced with this refractory state, venetoclax was integrated into the treatment on day 12. One month later, the patient achieved a deep CR, with MRD negativity confirmed by both flow cytometry and real-time quantitative PCR. Following one subsequent cycle of Hyper-CVAD-B combined with venetoclax, the patient successfully underwent allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (allo-HSCT) six months after presentation (December 2023). As of the submission of this manuscript, the patient remains in MRD-negative CR with only mild graft-versus-host disease (GVHD).

Clinical characteristics of SET-NUP214-positive B-ALL: A literature synthesis

A systematic synthesis of the literature reveals that while SET-NUP214 has been identified in molecular screening cohorts, only 10 cases of B-ALL (including MPAL with B-lineage involvement) have been documented with detailed clinicopathological data to date (Table 1). The present case represents the 11th documented instance with comprehensive clinical follow-up. Mirroring the clinicopathological hallmarks of its T-ALL counterpart, this entity exhibits a striking male predominance (90.9%; 10/11) and primarily affects young adults (median age: 29.4 years; range: 18–45). A defining feature is frequent lineage infidelity, characterized by the aberrant co-expression of CD7, CD13, and CD33 or CD3. The diagnostic spectrum is evenly bifurcated between classic B-ALL (45.5%) and MPAL (54.5%) with diverse lineage combinations. Genomic profiling underscores a complex landscape, with 27.3% of cases displaying complex karyotypes and recurrent co-mutations in PHF6, the JAK-STAT pathway (JAK1/JAK3), and FLT3-ITD. Historically, this subgroup is defined by profound therapeutic resistance, particularly to corticosteroids and intensive induction chemotherapy; notably, only one previously reported patient achieved a transient 5-month remission following a DA (daunorubicin and cytarabine) regimen. To the best of our knowledge, the present case represents the first documented instance of relapsed/refractory SET-NUP214-positive B-ALL achieving MRD-negative remission through the integration of venetoclax with Hyper-CVAD. This deep molecular response served as a successful bridge to allo-HSCT, ultimately resulting in sustained, long-term survival.

Discussion

The SET-NUP214 fusion gene (formerly designated as SET-CAN) arises from either a balanced t(9;9)(q34;q34) translocation or a cryptic del(9)(q34.11q34.13) deletion. While this genetic alteration is a well-characterized hallmark of T-ALL, its occurrence in the B-cell lineage (B-ALL) remains exceptionally rare. To our knowledge, this report constitutes the first documented case of relapsed/refractory SET-NUP214-positive B-ALL successfully salvaged with a venetoclax-based combination regimen.

SET-NUP214-positive leukemia is a clinically aggressive entity, typically exhibiting a distinct demographic predilection for young males (median age: 27.5–33 years) and consistent immunophenotypic aberrations—findings corroborated by both our index case and existing literature [12–14]. A significant diagnostic challenge lies in the fact that conventional cytogenetics often fails to detect this fusion; most cases present with either a normal or highly complex karyotype [15, 16]. Consequently, the diagnosis necessitates high-sensitivity modalities, such as FISH, targeted fusion gene screening, or transcriptome sequencing. In the present case, flow cytometry unequivocally established a B-lymphoid lineage with aberrant myeloid antigen expression (CD19⁺/dim, cCD79a⁺, CD34⁺, CD13⁺/33⁺), notably devoid of T-lineage commitment. However, molecular profiling underscores divergent mutational landscapes between lineages. While SET-NUP214-positive T-ALL is frequently associated with NOTCH1/FBXW7 mutations, PHF6 inactivation, and JAK-STAT pathway signaling aberrations (e.g., JAK1/3, STAT5B), the mutational signature in the B-cell context remains less defined, necessitating further genomic elucidation.

The incidence of the SET-NUP214 fusion in acute leukemia is notably low. Utilizing multiplex-nested RT-PCR, Chen et al. identified a stark lineage disparity in the incidence of this fusion. Subgroup analysis revealed an incidence of 1.27% in T-ALL (ranking second) compared to a mere 0.29% in B-ALL (ranking ninth) [2]. This observation is further supported by recent large-scale genomic profiling of pediatric and adolescent/young adult (AYA) patients by Pölönen et al., which underscores the recurrent nature of the SET::NUP214 fusion as a critical driver within this specific age demographic [17]. Recent studies have reported a broader incidence range in T-ALL, spanning from 0.78% to 10.3% [12, 18], while in the AML setting, Zhu et al. reported a frequency of 0.21% [13]. In a comprehensive cohort of 42 SET-NUP214-positive acute leukemia cases, T-ALL predominated (90.5%), with AUL (4.8%), AML (2.4%), and B-ALL (2.4%) constituting only a fraction of cases [7]. To date, documented B-ALL cases harboring the SET-NUP214 rearrangement remain exceedingly scarce (n = 10). A hallmark of SET-NUP214-positive leukemia is the high prevalence of lineage infidelity or mixed-phenotype features. Although initial remission rates with conventional induction chemotherapy appear deceptively favorable (70.8–91.7%) [7, 12], universal corticosteroid resistance and an inherent propensity for rapid relapse pose formidable clinical challenges. The recurrent nature of SET-NUP214 in B-lineage leukemia is further exemplified by the report from Nowak et al. [6], where cryptic 9q34 deletions were identified in adult patients with normal-karyotype B-ALL. The fact that these patients experienced rapid disease progression and limited survival with standard chemotherapy highlights the aggressive biology of this molecular subset. The same holds true for the SET-NUP214 fusion in acute myeloid leukemia. Shen et al. [19] found that even after allogeneic stem cell transplantation, patients with this fusion continue to face a high risk of relapse, with estimated 1-year and 3-year leukemia-free survival of 69.3% and 38.5%, respectively. This aggressive disease biology was exemplified by our patient, who experienced early hematological relapse post-induction and remained refractory to subsequent conventional salvage regimens. While allo-HSCT significantly improves long-term outcomes in this cohort [7], achieving a deep second remission remains a critical and mandatory prerequisite for a successful bridge to transplantation in the relapsed/refractory setting.

The precise mechanisms by which SET-NUP214 orchestrates leukemogenesis remain to be fully elucidated; however, current evidence suggests a multifactorial oncogenic program. The SET protein, acting as a histone chaperone, disrupts epigenetic homeostasis via the inhibition of protein phosphatase 2 A (PP2A). Concurrently, the FG-repeat domain derived from NUP214 impairs nuclear pore integrity through the aberrant sequestration of hCRM1/XPO1, leading to nucleocytoplasmic transport defects and subsequent genomic instability [20, 21]. Although mouse models indicate that SET-NUP214 can arrest hematopoietic differentiation across erythroid, megakaryocytic, and B-cell lineages, leukemic transformation appears to necessitate cooperating genetic hits [21]. The SET-NUP214 fusion protein has been shown to recruit MLL complexes to the Homeobox A (HOXA) cluster, thereby sustaining a progenitor-like transcriptional program. This process is mediated through direct interaction with the HOXA9/10 promoters, DOT1L-dependent epigenetic activation, and XPO1-mediated nuclear export dysregulation [22–24]. Crucially, SET-NUP214-driven HOXA overexpression creates a survival advantage for leukemic blasts [23, 25]; specifically, HOXA9 has been identified as a direct upstream regulator of BCL-2 in myeloid progenitors [26]. In our case, the robust BCL-2 expression confirmed by immunohistochemistry aligns with this regulatory axis and likely underscores a key survival/resistance mechanism. Given that HOXA/HOXB overexpression serves as a potent predictor of venetoclax sensitivity in AML [27], and that HOXA-positive T-ALLs exhibit profound vulnerability to BCL-2 inhibition [28], the rapid remission achieved in our patient suggests that venetoclax effectively exploits the dependency of SET-NUP214-positive B-ALL on the HOXA/BCL-2 axis.

Venetoclax, a highly selective BCL-2 inhibitor currently approved for the treatment of AML and chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL), has also emerged as a promising therapeutic option in ALL [29–33]. Beyond the present case, a limited number of reports have described the use of venetoclax in relapsed/refractory (R/R) SET-NUP214-positive acute leukemias. Shen et al. documented a patient with SET-NUP214-rearranged AML who experienced a molecular relapse post-transplantation; the patient successfully achieved a second remission and sustained survival following a combination of venetoclax, chemotherapy, and donor lymphocyte infusion (DLI) [19]. Similarly, Chen et al. described a case of chronic myeloid leukemia in myeloid blast crisis (CML-BC) co-harboring BCR-ABL and SET-NUP214 mutations, where a triplet regimen of venetoclax, decitabine, and ponatinib facilitated a successful bridge to allo-HSCT [34]. Furthermore, in the T-cell lineage, Khanlar et al. demonstrated that venetoclax combined with decitabine effectively decimated the MRD burden in a patient with SET-NUP214-positive T-lymphoblastic leukemia/lymphoma [35]. Based on these cases, SET-NUP214-positive acute leukemia of different hematopoietic lineages demonstrates comparable sensitivity to venetoclax treatment. Nevertheless, the definitive efficacy of BCL-2 inhibitors, such as venetoclax, in SET-NUP214-mutated acute leukemias remains to be confirmed by large-scale clinical trials.

In summary, SET-NUP214-positive B-ALL is an exceptionally rare entity characterized by chemoresistance and highly relapse rate, for which allo-HSCT remains crucial for improving prognosis. For patients with relapsed/refractory disease, achieving remission again prior to transplantation poses a significant challenge. This paper reports the first case of a patient with relapsed/refractory SET-NUP214-positive B-ALL who achieved complete remission following venetoclax combined with chemotherapy and attained long-term survival through allo-HSCT. The present case suggests that BCL-2 inhibition may overcome the intrinsic resistance associated with the SET-NUP214 fusion.

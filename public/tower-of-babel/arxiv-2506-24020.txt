Phase-Space Topology in a Single-Atom Synthetic Dimension
Kyungmin Lee, Sunkyu Yu, Seungwoo Yu, Jiyong Kang, Wonhyeong Choi, Daun Chung, Sumin Park, Taehyun Kim

Abstract
We investigate topological features in the synthetic Fock-state lattice of a single-atom system described by the quantum Rabi model. By diagonalizing the Hamiltonian, we identify a zero-energy defect state localized at a domain wall of the synthetic lattice, whose spin polarization is topologically protected. To address the challenge of applying band topology to the Fock-state lattice, we introduce a topological invariant based on phase-space geometry—the phase-space winding number. We show that the Zak phase, representing the geometric phase difference between two sublattices, can also be computed using a phase-space parameter and corresponds directly to the phase-space winding number. This quantized geometric phase reflects the spin polarization of the defect state, demonstrating a bulk–boundary correspondence. The resulting phase-space topology reveals the emergence of single-atom dressed states with contrasting properties—topologically protected fermionic states and driving-tunable bosonic states. Our results establish phase-space topology as a novel framework for exploring topological physics in single-atom synthetic dimensions, uncovering quantum-unique topological protection distinct from classical analogs.

In parallel, recent experimental advances have enabled the realization of ultra-strong

Since the discovery of the quantum Hall effect

One of the emerging topics in this field is to implement fertile physics of high-dimensional topological phenomena within physically accessible platforms, for example, using the concept of synthetic dimensions

Although most of synthetic-dimensional physics have been studied in classical platforms, the use of Fock basis in quantum systems, which enables an infinite number of lattice sites even with single-mode bosonic excitations, has recently been proposed

However, due to the absence of translational symmetry, and thus, the consequent difficulties in defining quasi-momentum, Brillouin zone, and the closed form of topological invariants, the analytical estimation of topological phenomena in Fock-state lattices still remains a challenge.

In this paper, we propose a phase-space analysis of topological Fock-state lattices, which enables the characterization of well-defined topological invariants for a single-atom QRM.
We derive analytic solutions and identify a zero-energy defect state localized at a domain wall, whose spin polarization indicates distinct topological phases.
Furthermore, we define a phase-space winding number as a topological invariant capturing the topological protection in fermionic states in the QRM.
The phase-space winding number corresponds to the quantized geometric phase difference between the two sublattices, reflecting the bulk–boundary correspondence in the system.
Our results provide a novel framework for exploring topological features in a single-atom system, with potential realizations in trapped ions

III
Phase-space topology

To investigate topological features of our infinite and aperiodic system, which are indicated by the topologically protected spin polarization of the defect state around a periodic Dirac cone, we develop the theoretical framework of phase-space topology.

The spin polarization of the zero-energy defect state is topologically protected and robust against noise in the hopping parameters
w
,
v_{r}
, and
v_{cr}
. It depends solely on the relative magnitude between
|v_{r}|
and
|v_{cr}|
:

\displaystyle\left\langle\psi_{0}\left|\hat{\sigma}_{z}\right|\psi_{0}\right\rangle=\text{sgn}(|v_{cr}|-|v_{r}|),

(5)

as derived from Eq. (
2
). Thus, the two regimes
|v_{r}|>|v_{cr}|
and
|v_{r}|<|v_{cr}|
correspond to distinct topological phases, characterized by opposite spin polarizations of the defect state. Since the defect is localized at the interface between two distinct domains (see Fig.
1
), it can be interpreted as a boundary property of the lattice.

As Eq. (
2
) shows, the energy gap vanishes in the limit
n\to\infty
, defining the bulk regime of the lattice.
However, the non-uniform hopping amplitudes prevent a conventional momentum-space analysis of the system.
Furthermore, the semi-infinite structure of the Fock-state lattice hinders the application of real-space topological invariants developed for systems lacking translational symmetry

;
Chen 2023
;
sup
.

To resolve the difficulty in directly applying band topology to Fock-state lattices that lack translational symmetry, we focus on the phase parameter
\phi
, which provides alternative periodicity over the phase space.
We note that the eigenstates are centered along a circular trajectory given by
\alpha(\phi)=C+Re^{i\phi}

As

varies from 0 to

Accordingly, the regimes

and

In contrast to classical systems, where topological phenomena emerge from the band structure, the topological features in our system are rooted in its entanglement structure and phase-space characteristics.
The defect states possess topologically protected spin polarization, and the spin–boson entanglement in Eq. (
2

The phase-space geometry defines the corresponding topological invariant, the phase-space winding number, and the interplay between displacement and squeezing of the eigenstates leads to a conical energy spectrum.
Our results highlight phase-space geometry as a powerful framework for realizing and probing topological phenomena in hybrid quantum spin–oscillator systems

V
Discussion

In this work, we have shown that a single-atom system described by the quantum Rabi model exhibits topological features characterized by its phase-space geometry.
To overcome the absence of translational symmetry and the unconventional band structure of the Fock-state lattice, we introduced a phase-space winding number as a topological invariant, reflecting the quantized geometric phase.
We showed that the proposed invariant describes the topologically protected fermionic spin state in the defect states of the Rabi SSH-like chain and links directly to the Zak phase of the Fock-state lattice.
Furthermore, we analyzed the
\phi
-dependent dynamics in phase space, which leads to a conical energy spectrum at the critical point.
Our results demonstrate topological features emerge from the intrinsic quantum nature of a single-atom synthetic lattice, establishing a compact and controllable framework for exploring topological physics.
We demonstrate that topological phases, typically characterized by spatial symmetries in real-space lattices, can instead be identified through geometric features in the phase space of a synthetic Fock-state lattice.

The Hamiltonian in Eq. (
1
) can be implemented using trapped ions or superconducting circuits, where the coupling strengths
|v_{r}|
,
|v_{cr}|
, and
w
correspond to sideband and carrier Rabi frequencies
Pedernales et al. 2015
;
Leibfried et al. 2003
;
Blais et al. 2007
;
Liu et al. 2007
;
Wallraff et al. 2007
;
Strand et al. 2013
.
In trapped-ion systems, typical red- and blue-sideband Rabi frequencies are on the order of tens of kHz, and
v_{r}
,
v_{cr}
, and
w
can be independently tuned via radio-frequency (RF) amplitude modulation.
We note, however, that the accessible parameter regime may be constrained by the Lamb–Dicke condition
Leibfried et al. 2003
, which limits both the maximum achievable sideband coupling strength and the Fock-state population.
In superconducting circuits, sideband Rabi frequencies can reach tens of MHz
Strand et al. 2013
, allowing for faster dynamics.
However, short coherence times
Blais et al. 2007
may restrict the evolution time, and the effective parameter regimes remain similar, as the topological features of the system are determined by relative coupling strengths rather than absolute values.
In both platforms, the phase factors
\phi_{r}
and
\phi_{cr}
are tunable by controlling the relative phases of RF electronics used for sideband generation, enabling access to the desired phase-space geometry.
These systems offer precise control over the drive parameters, making them suitable for the experimental demonstration of single-atom topological features via adiabatic preparation and manipulation of defect states.

An important implication of our results is the separation between the fermionic and bosonic nature of the defect state.
While the spin polarization of the defect is topologically protected and robust against parameter noise, the bosonic properties, such as the occupation number
\langle n\rangle
and the phase-space distribution, remain tunable via system parameters.
This separation enables the realization of tunable, topologically protected quantum states, where the protected spin degree of freedom acts as a stable quantum memory and the bosonic mode can be exploited for control or interfacing.
Such controllable yet protected quantum states may be useful in hybrid quantum computing architectures that require coherence in one degree of freedom and tunability in another
Liu et al. 2024
;
Andersen et al. 2015
.
Furthermore, by utilizing multiple bosonic modes in hybrid quantum devices
Gan et al. 2020
;
Jeon et al. 2025
;
Gao et al. 2019
, our system can be extended to higher-dimensional synthetic lattices.
These extended systems enable the exploration of novel topological phenomena arising from interactions between spin-protected defect modes.
Such developments open up new possibilities for engineering topological matter, where topology originates from phase-space characteristics.

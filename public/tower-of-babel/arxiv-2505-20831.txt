Estimators and Performance Bounds for Short Periodic Pulses
Sebastian Schertler 1 4 , Oliver Lang 1 4 , Jonas Lindenberger 2 , Stefan Schuster 3 , Stefan Scheiblhofer 3 , Alexander Haberl 3 , Clemens Staudinger 3 , and Mario Huemer 1

Abstract
In many industrial applications, signals with short periodic pulses, caused by repeated steps in the manufacturing process, are present, and their fundamental frequency or period may be of interest. Fundamental frequency estimation is in many cases performed by describing the periodic signal as a multiharmonic signal and employing the corresponding maximum likelihood estimator. However, since signals with short periodic pulses contain a large number of noise-only samples, the multiharmonic signal model is not optimal to describe them. In this work, two models of short periodic pulses with known and unknown pulse shape are considered. For both models, the corresponding maximum likelihood estimators, Fisher information matrices, and approximate Cramér-Rao lower bounds are presented. Numerical results demonstrate that the proposed estimators outperform the maximum likelihood estimator based on the multiharmonic signal model for low signal-to-noise ratios.

IV-C

Sub-Grid Resolution

As the grid resolution for
\widehat{T}=\widehat{P}T_{\text{s}}
is
T_{\text{s}}
, the
mse
(
mse
) cannot be smaller
\frac{T_{\text{s}}^{2}}{12}
, which is the variance for a uniform distribution of width
T_{\text{s}}
. To receive results with a lower
mse
we resample the measurement vector
\mathbf{x}
by a factor of
P_{\text{R}}
to virtually increase the resolution. Then, the smallest possible
mse
with resampling is
\frac{(T_{\text{s}}/P_{\text{R}})^{2}}{12}
.

VI
Conclusion

In this paper two signal models for short periodic pulses with and without known pulse shape were considered, and the corresponding
fim
and closed-form approximations of them were derived. It turned out that the approximate
fim
is singular in the case of an unknown pulse shape.
The
ml
estimators for the signal period were presented, which feature a better threshold behavior and better
mse
performance for low
snr
(and moderate in the case of the
ppks
estimator) compared to estimator based on the multiharmonic signal model.

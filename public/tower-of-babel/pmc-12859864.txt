The mechanism of lncRNA LINC01446 in delayed fracture healing and its clinical significance
Authors: Dawei He, Shilin Zhao, Yaohua Wang, Changhao Liu, Xuming Ding, Huiying Li, Boxuan Ren, Bo Li, Yujuan Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLong non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) exert a vital role in the fracture healing process by interacting with microRNAs (miRNAs) and constructing lncRNA-miRNA regulatory networks. This study focuses on clarifying lncRNA LINC01446’s role in delayed fracture healing (DFH) and exploring its potential regulatory mechanism.MethodsThe expression of LINC01446 and miR-338-3p in serum samples was detected via qRT-PCR, and the predictive ability of LINC01446 for DFH in fracture patients was evaluated by the ROC curve. The targeting interaction between LINC01446 and miR-338-3p was predicted via StarBase and confirmed through the dual-luciferase reporter assay. For the assessment of cell proliferation and apoptosis, the CCK-8 assay and flow cytometry were utilized.ResultsIn DFH, LINC01446 was significantly downregulated, while miR-338-3p was notably increased. The relationship between LINC01446 and miR-338-3p was identified, showing an inverse regulatory pattern. Knockdown of LINC01446 lowered osteogenic marker gene expression, inhibited the proliferation of MC3T3-E1 cells, and induced cell apoptosis. Additionally, the miR-338-3p inhibitor effectively abrogated the suppressive effects of LINC01446 knockdown on osteoblastic proliferation and differentiation, as well as its promotional effect on cell apoptosis (P < 0.05).ConclusionsLINC01446 can affect DFH via miR-338-3p and may act as a potential therapeutic target.Keywords: LINC01446, miR-338-3p, Diagnostics, Osteogenic differentiation, Delayed fracture healing

Introduction

Over the past two decades, the number of newly diagnosed fracture cases worldwide has increased by more than 30%, and the incidence rate continues to rise year by year. Clinically, most fracture patients can achieve normal healing within several weeks to months [1]. However, a considerable proportion of cases still experience delayed healing progression [2]. Studies have shown that the incidence of postoperative nonunion or delayed fracture healing (DFH) accounts for 5–10% of all fracture cases, and can even reach as high as 30% in specific high-risk populations [3]. As one of the most common complications after fracture fixation surgery, DFH not only affects patient prognosis but also poses a major challenge in clinical orthopedic diagnosis and treatment [4]. Furthermore, DFH may lead to increased morbidity and mortality in patients, as well as a significant rise in related medical expenditures [5]. Therefore, in-depth exploration of the regulatory mechanisms underlying DFH and identification of effective targets for promoting bone healing are of great clinical significance for reducing the incidence of DFH [6]. Research has shown that calcitonin can promote fracture healing; in addition, intermittent pneumatic compression and low-intensity pulsed ultrasound both have the potential to stimulate bone formation [7–9]. Presently, the diagnosis of DFH remains primarily based on imaging examinations, and there is a lack of early diagnostic methods. Molecular biomarkers show high value in the early diagnosis of many diseases: they facilitate early disease detection and provide a key basis for personalized treatment [10]. Non-coding RNAs (miRNA, siRNA, circRNA) play significant roles in musculoskeletal diseases such as tendon injury, osteoarthritis (OA), rheumatoid arthritis (RA), and osteoporosis (OP), and all possess therapeutic or diagnostic value [11–16]. Thus, there is an urgent need for biomarkers that can predict DFH at an early stage to enable early diagnosis [17].

Assessments of bone healing outcomes, including X-ray healing scores, bone mineral density, and callus formation, indicate significant differences between the normal healing group and the delayed healing group [18]. The core regulation of the fracture healing process lies in the functional balance and quantity stability of osteoblasts and osteoclasts [19], among which impaired osteoblast function is generally recognized as a direct cause of DFH [20]. Dysregulation of long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) is associated with various diseases, and numerous studies have emphasized their critical role in regulating fracture healing [21]. LncRNAs can regulate the proliferation and differentiation of osteoblasts through the lncRNA-miRNA competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) network, and this mechanism has been fully verified [22]. For instance, existing research has confirmed that lncRNA TUG1 can upregulate FGFR1 through miR-34a, thereby promoting osteoblast proliferation and inhibiting their apoptosis [23]. LncRNA TRPM2-AS participates in the fracture healing process by targeting and regulating the miR-545-3p/Bmp2 pathway [6]. In addition, lncRNA AC132217.4 can also promote osteogenic differentiation and bone healing. [24]. By contrast, lncRNA LINC00339 exerts the opposite effect: it inhibits osteoblast proliferation and promotes apoptosis by targeting miR-16-5p [25].

As mentioned earlier, lncRNAs can regulate the differentiation of osteoblasts, thereby playing a crucial regulatory role in bone physiological processes [6]. LINC01446 is a 3484 bp-long non-coding RNA (ncRNA) localized to the chromosomal region 7p12.1 [26]. LINC01446 exerts an oncogenic role in glioblastoma via the ceRNA mechanism [27]. According to the ceRNA mechanism, lncRNAs regulate downstream genes through microRNAs (miRNAs) [28].

Numerous existing studies have demonstrated that the lncRNA-miRNA network modulates the osteogenic differentiation of bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs), either by promoting or inhibiting this process [29]. miRNAs can regulate the differentiation processes and functional activities of osteoblasts and osteoclasts, and this regulatory role highlights their core regulatory status in bone metabolism and repair processes [30]. Among these, multiple miRNAs have been confirmed to exert regulatory effects on osteoblast development or bone formation by targeting key osteogenic-related factors [31]. For example, overexpression of miR-138 can inhibit the differentiation of human bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (hMSCs) into osteoblasts [32]. MiR-1271-5p can participate in the molecular process of fracture healing by targeting and regulating ZBTB7A [33]. MiR-338-3p localizes to chromosome 17q25.3 and is 22 nt in length [34]. miR-338-3p has been identified as a tumor suppressor in a spectrum of human malignancies, including lung cancer, hepatocellular carcinoma, neuroblastoma, and gastric cancer [35]. Additionally, miR-338-3p regulates osteoclastogenesis via targeting the IKKβ gene [36]. miR-338-3p is downregulated during the osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs and inhibits osteogenic differentiation by targeting Runx2 and Fgfr2 [37].

However, the specific mechanisms of LINC01446 in DFH have not been clarified at present. Based on this, the present study first analyzed LINC01446 expression in DFH patients and explored the regulatory role and potential mechanism of LINC01446 in the proliferation and apoptosis of osteoblasts through cellular experiments, aiming to provide a new therapeutic target for DFH.

Materials and methods

General information

This study enrolled patients admitted to the Department of Orthopedics of Zhangye People’s Hospital Affiliated to Hexi University as research subjects. Among them, 61 patients with DFH were assigned to the delayed group, and another 67 patients with normal fracture healing (NFH) admitted during the same period were placed in the normal healing group. The Zhangye People’s Hospital Affiliated to Hexi University Ethics Committee reviewed and approved the study protocol, and all included participants received full information about the study and signed written informed consent.

Inclusion criteria of study subjects were as follows: (1) Meeting the diagnostic criteria for fracture healing—NFH was defined as no local percussion pain or tenderness at the fracture site, no limitation of limb movement within 4 months after fracture, and blurred fracture line shown by X-ray examination; patients who failed to meet the above criteria within 4 months after fracture were diagnosed as DFH; (2) Confirmation of fracture diagnosis by imaging examinations such as X-ray and CT; (3) Complete clinical data.

The exclusion criteria included: (1) Concomitant malignant tumors; (2) Abnormal function of major organs, including the heart, liver, and kidneys; (3) Concomitant coagulation disorders; (4) History of autoimmune diseases; (5) Pathological fractures possibly caused by bone metabolic diseases or diabetes mellitus.

qRT-PCR

The expressions of LINC01446 and miR-338-3p were detected, as well as the expressions of osteogenic differentiation-related proteins, including Runx2, OCN, and ALP, in MC3T3-E1 cells. Trizol reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) was used to extract total RNA; subsequently, the SuperScript™ II kit (same brand, USA) facilitated reverse transcription of the screened RNA into cDNA, which then served as the template for amplification. GAPDH was chosen as the internal control for LINC01446 during detection, whereas U6 was selected for miR-338-3p. The primer sequences used in this study are listed as follows (F: forward primer; R: reverse primer): LINC01446, F: TTGAAACGCCAGCCAGTT, R: CCATGTATTTCGCGGTGTTC; GAPDH, F: GAAGAGAGAGACCCTCACGCTG, R: ACTGTGAGGAGGGGAGATTCAGT; miR-338-3p, F: 5′-TGCGGTCCAGCATCAGTGAT-3′, R: 5′-CCAGTGCAGGGTCCGAGGT-3′; U6, F: 5′-TGCGGGTGCTCGCTTCGGCAGC-3′, R: 5′-CCAGTGCAGGGTCCGAGGT-3′.

Cell culture and transfection

MC3T3-E1 cells were acquired from the Shanghai Cell Bank (Chinese Academy of Sciences), and culture conditions followed the manufacturer’s instructions. Si-LINC01446, si-NC, miR-338-3p inhibitor, and inhibitor NC were obtained from RiboBio Co., Ltd. (Guangzhou, China).

Luciferase reporter assay

The binding sites between LINC01446 and miR-338-3p were identified with the online database StarBase (https://rnasysu.com/encori/), and LINC01446 wild-type plasmid (WT-LINC01446) and LINC01446 mutant plasmid (MUT-LINC01446) were constructed. Lipofectamine 2000 transfection reagent was used to co-transfect the above plasmids with miR-NC or miR-338-3p inhibitor into the cells, respectively. Finally, the luciferase activity was determined.

Cell proliferation

Using the CCK-8 kit (Beyotime, Beijing, China), we assessed cell proliferation at 24, 48, and 72 h of incubation, respectively. Absorbance at 450 nm was quantified by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay.

Cell differentiation

MC3T3-E1 cells were collected at days 0, 7, and 15 post-differentiation induction, respectively.

Cell apoptosis

After harvesting the cells, they were rinsed with PBS. Next, the cell suspension was mixed with Annexin V-FITC binding solution; subsequently, 5 μL PI was added, and the samples were incubated at room temperature in the dark. This experiment employed the Annexin V-FITC Cell Death Detection Kit (Beyotime, China), and cell apoptosis was finally analyzed via flow cytometry.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS 27.0 and Graphpad 10.1.2. T-test, one-way ANOVA, and two-way ANOVA were used for intergroup comparisons. Following ANOVA, Tukey’s test was performed for multiple comparisons. Multivariate Cox and ROC curve analyses were performed using SPSS 27.0 and GraphPad Prism 10, respectively. The diagnostic efficacy of LINC01446 for patients with DFH was analyzed using the ROC curve. P < 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant.

Result

Comparison of clinical data

After comparative analysis of clinical data between individuals with NFH and those with DFH in this study, there were no statistically significant differences in age, gender, BMI, fracture severity, hypertension, or hyperlipidemia between the DFH group and the NFH group (P > 0.05). These results indicated that the two groups exhibited a considerable degree of consistency and comparability, which enhanced the reliability of the study design (Table 1).

Level and predictive value of LINC01446

Comparison of serum LINC01446 between the DFH and NFH showed that the serum LINC01446 in DFH was significantly lower than that in NFH (Fig. 1A). ROC curve analysis was performed with the LINC01446 level in fracture patients as the test variable and the occurrence of DFH as the outcome variable. The results showed that the area under the curve (AUC) of serum LINC01446 level for predicting DFH patients was 0.8287 (95% CI 0.7564–0.9011) (Fig. 1B). Furthermore, logistic regression analysis showed that LINC01446 may be a potential risk factor for DFH (Table 2).

Targeting the relationship between LINC01446 and miR-338-3p

miR-338-3p and LINC01446 had binding sites (Fig. 2A). RT-qPCR detection showed that miR-338-3p in the DFH group was significantly higher than that in the NFH group (Fig. 2B). In DFH, a significant negative correlation existed between miR-338-3p and LINC01446 (r =—0.5370, P < 0.001, Fig. 2C).

Based on the binding sites of LINC01446 and miR-338-3p, gene fragments of WT-LINC01446 and MUT-LINC01446 were designed. After co-transfection of the miR-338-3p inhibitor with WT-LINC01446 3’UTR, the luciferase activity was significantly increased, while no significant change was observed in the MUT-LINC01446 group (Fig. 2D). After knockdown of LINC01446, its expression level decreased significantly (Fig. 2E). Knockdown of LINC01446 significantly promoted miR-338-3p expression (Fig. 2F).

Regulation of LINC01446 on MC3T3-E1 Cells

The expression levels of osteogenic markers (OCN and RUNX2) were gradually upregulated over time, and ALP significantly increased with time (P < 0.001, Fig. 3A). The expression of LINC01446 increased gradually with the extension of time (P < 0.05, Fig. 3B), while miR-338-3p decreased gradually with the extension of time (P < 0.05, Fig. 3C).

MC3T3-E1 cells were collected on day 15 of osteogenic induction to explore how LINC01446 influences the mRNAs of proteins linked to osteogenic differentiation. Knockdown of LINC01446 significantly reduced the mRNA levels of ALP, OCN, and RUNX2, and transfection with miR-338-3p inhibitor could alleviate this situation (Fig. 3D). In addition, knockdown of LINC01446 notably suppressed cell proliferation (Fig. 3E) and stimulated cell apoptosis (Fig. 3F), and both phenomena could also be improved by transfection with miR-338-3p inhibitor.

Discussion

Delayed healing occurs when a fracture fails to heal within the expected time frame [38]. With the continuous increase in the incidence of fractures, DFH has become increasingly prevalent, which not only reduces the fracture healing rate but also seriously impairs patients’ quality of life [25]. Osteoblast synthesis is a key factor in the bone healing process [39]. In the complex regulatory process of fracture healing, lncRNAs perform an essential role; they are essential for regulating the expression of downstream genes and maintaining the function and homeostasis of fracture healing-related cells such as osteoblasts and osteoclasts [40]. For instance, in nude mouse models with impaired immune function, exosome-derived lncRNA H19 can not only promote osteogenesis but also improve angiogenesis via the angiopoietin 1/Tie2-NO signaling pathway [41]. Bone marrow mesenchymal stem cell (BMSC)-derived lncRNA MALAT1 can act as a ceRNA to activate osteoblast formation by targeting the downregulation of miR-34c [42]. LncRNA CRNDE can improve the fracture healing process by regulating the viability and apoptosis of osteoblasts [43]. Furthermore, lncRNA NEAT1 shows increased expression in DFH patients’ serum and has high diagnostic value [44]. Our study shows that LINC01446 exhibits potential as a promising biomarker for DFH, yet the translation of its associated findings into clinical practice remains in the early stage.

Accumulating evidence has demonstrated that LINC01446 is aberrantly expressed in multiple malignant tumors and exerts an oncogenic role. In glioblastoma multiforme (GBM) tissues, its expression level is elevated, and it can promote tumor progression by sponging miR-489-3p [45]. In gastric cancer (GC) tissues, its expression is significantly higher than that in normal tissues, and it promotes the proliferation and inhibits the apoptosis of GC cells [46]. In hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), LINC01446 is mostly in an activated state, and its activation is correlated with the proliferative status of the tumor. Specifically, LINC01446 drives HCC progression by activating the SRPK2/SRSF1/VEGFA pathway [47].

We found that LINC01446 in DFH was significantly lower than that in NFH, and this finding is inconsistent with its expression in other malignant tumors. The differential expression pattern of LINC01446 (low expression in DFH and high expression in malignant tumors) may result from its distinct biological functions under these two pathological conditions. In DFH, the impaired proliferation ability of bone repair-related cells impedes the healing process, whereas in malignant tumors, tumor cells maintain malignant phenotypes (abnormal proliferation and invasion), and the biological function requirements of the two are different.

LncRNAs and miRNAs perform their biological roles through forming a vast, intricate, interconnected regulatory network, thereby achieving precise regulation of gene expression; among these, lncRNAs can affect cell differentiation by regulating miRNAs [48]. For example, lncRNA CYTOR regulates SOX11 by sponging miR-6512-3p, thereby enhancing osteogenic differentiation of human periodontal ligament stem cells [49]. Knockdown of MALAT1 can improve the biological behavior of MG-63 cells treated with lipopolysaccharide (LPS) by sponging miR-212, providing a new therapeutic idea for delayed healing of femoral neck fractures [50]. From the perspective of the ceRNA hypothesis, lncRNAs can competitively bind to target genes against miRNAs, thereby attenuating the inhibitory effect of miRNAs on target genes, and miRNAs and lncRNAs are usually negatively correlated in expression level [51, 52]. In glioblastoma, LINC01446 can promote cell proliferation by competing with miRNAs and functioning as a ceRNA [45]. In this study, there are targeted binding sites between LINC01446 and miR-338-3p, and their expression levels show a negative correlation.

Bone-forming cells known as osteoblasts synthesize and secrete bone matrix and have a pivotal part in bone formation, remodeling, and healing [53]. Among these processes, osteoblast differentiation is particularly critical for fracture healing, and its differentiation efficiency directly determines the rate of callus formation, mineralization quality, and the final mechanical properties of bone tissue [44]. Therefore, to investigate the aforementioned molecular mechanisms associated with fracture healing, the present study selected human osteoblasts as the in vitro experimental model [54]. During osteoblast differentiation, ALP and RUNX2 are early differentiation markers, while OCN is associated with late differentiation [55]. ALP can promote bone matrix mineralization, and enhancing ALP activity accelerates mineral deposition and bone formation [56]. OCN, as a key protein in the late stage of osteogenic differentiation, is crucial for regulating matrix mineralization and new bone formation [57]. RUNX2 acts as a transcription factor with multiple functions, and it can regulate bone development by controlling osteoblast differentiation and the expression of various extracellular matrix protein genes during osteogenic differentiation [58]. In this study, the expression of ALP, OCN, and RUNX2 in osteoblasts increased gradually as the culture period lengthened. This result confirmed the successful induction of osteoblast differentiation, providing a reliable model for subsequent investigation of the effect of LINC01446 on osteoblast function.

miR-338-3p can inhibit BMSCs differentiation into osteoblasts [59, 60]. In this study, miR-338-3p could significantly inhibit osteoblast proliferation and promote their apoptosis, and this functional trend is consistent with the previously described regulatory pattern of miR-338-3p expression changes on osteoblasts. Rescue experiments demonstrated that when an exogenous miR-338-3p inhibitor was added, the regulatory effect of LINC01446 knockdown on osteoblast function was significantly reversed; this result further confirms that LINC01446 might be implicated in the pathogenesis of DFH through the regulation of miR-338-3p.

miRNAs can regulate bone metabolism by targeting key molecules in the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway [61]. Overexpression of miR-539-3p in osteoblasts can also downregulate multiple key components of the Wnt signaling pathway, disrupt the trabecular microstructure, and ultimately lead to reduced bone formation [37]. miR-1323 can weaken the osteogenic capacity of bone mesenchymal stromal cells and impede fracture healing by inhibiting the BMP4/SMAD4 signaling pathway [36]. The function of SMAD4 is not limited to the BMP4 signaling pathway, but also occupies a central position in the TGF-β1/SMAD signaling pathway [62]. The NF-κB signaling pathway not only possesses multifunctional roles and is widely involved in various biological processes [63], but also serves as a classic signaling pathway closely associated with immune and inflammatory responses during fracture healing [64]. Building on the above findings, future studies will further investigate the aforementioned pathways to gain a more comprehensive understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying DFH. Meanwhile, it should be noted that a limitation of this study is the lack of in vivo experimental validation.

Conclusion

For the first time, this study demonstrates that lncRNA LINC01446 exhibits the potential to serve as a predictive biomarker for DFH. LINC01446 affects osteoblast function by regulating miR-338-3p, providing a novel treatment-related target.

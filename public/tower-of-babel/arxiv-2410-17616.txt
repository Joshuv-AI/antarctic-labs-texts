Exclusive c → u ​ γ c\to u\gamma transitions of B c B_{c} meson
\firstname Nicola \lastname Losacco \fnsep

Abstract
We study the rare decays of the B c B_{c} meson induced by the flavour changing neutral current c → u ​ γ c\to u\gamma transition. In the Standard Model they are strongly suppressed by the Glashow-Iliopoulos-Maiani mechanism, therefore they are sensitive to new physics. The difficulty is to get rid of long-distance contributions. We study such effects in radiative B c B_{c} transitions both to B ∗ B^{*} and to the axial-vector B 1 ′ B_{1}^{\prime} meson.



1
Introduction

The decays induced by the flavour changing neutral current
c\to u
transitions play an important role in the search for new physics (NP) phenomena
[
1
]
.
In the Standard Model (SM) the relevant weak Hamiltonian involves small Wilson coefficients resulting from the efficient GIM cancellation
[
2
]
: the corresponding hadronic amplitudes are highly suppressed for
c\to u\gamma
,
c\to u\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
and
c\to u\nu\bar{\nu}

[
3
]
. Studies devoted to
B_{c}^{+}\to B^{*+}\,\gamma
concluded that in this channel the long-distance (LD) contributions do not have the overwhelming size as in the case of
D
,
D_{s}
and
\Lambda_{c}
decays
[
4
]
. We reconsider the issue, extending the analysis to the
B_{c}^{+}\to B^{\prime+}_{1}\,\gamma
mode, with
B^{\prime+}_{1}
the lightest axial-vector beauty meson. We find that for
B_{c}
decays to spin-1 positive-parity
B_{u}
excitations, the LD
contributions are comparable to the
B^{*+}
case. However, a hadronic suppression in the short-distance (SD) amplitude reduces the role of this channel for searching NP.

2
Effective weak Hamiltonian

To describe the
c\to u\gamma
transition we consider the effective weak Hamiltonian
[
3
]

\mathcal{H}_{eff}=4\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}\left[\sum_{q=d,s}V_{cq}^{*}V_{uq}\left(C_{1}\mathcal{O}_{1}^{(q)}+C_{2}\mathcal{O}_{2}^{(q)}\right)+\sum_{i=3}^{6}C_{1}\mathcal{O}_{i}+\sum_{i=7}^{8}\left(C_{i}\mathcal{O}_{i}+C_{i}^{\prime}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\prime}\right)\right]

(1)

expressed in terms of current-current operators
\mathcal{O}_{1,2}^{(q)}
, QCD penguins operators
\mathcal{O}_{3,\dots 6}
and electromagnetic and gluon dipole operators

\mathcal{O}_{7,8}^{(\prime)}
. The effective coefficient
C_{7}^{\rm eff}
including two-loop QCD matrix elements of the operators
O_{3,\dots,6}
, is in the range
[
3
]

C_{7}^{\rm eff}\in[-0.00151-i0.00556|_{s}+i0.00005|_{CKM},-0.00088-i0.00327|_{s}+i0.00002|_{CKM}],

(2)

where the subscripts indicate the contributions to the imaginary part from the strong phases and the phases of the CKM matrix.

C_{7}^{\prime}
turns out to be small,
C^{\prime}_{7}\sim m_{u}/m_{c}
, therefore the operator
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{7}
can be neglected in SM.
Eq. (
2
) shows the GIM suppression in the SM for the dipole
\mathcal{O}_{7}
, since
C_{7}\sim 10^{-3}
. This coefficient can be significantly enhanced in a SM extension (BSM) scenario. General bounds can be established
[
5
]
:

|C_{7}|,|C^{\prime}_{7}|\lesssim 0.5.

(3)

The amplitude of the transition
B_{c}(p)\to A(p^{\prime},\epsilon)\gamma(q,\lambda)
, where A is a
1^{+}
state is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(B_{c}(p)\to A(p^{\prime},\epsilon)\gamma(q,\lambda))=\left\{A_{PC}\left[p\cdot qg^{\alpha\beta}-q^{\alpha}p^{\beta}\right]+iA_{PV}\varepsilon^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}p_{\mu}q_{\nu}\right\}\epsilon_{\alpha}^{*}\lambda_{\beta}^{*}.

(4)

Short-distance amplitude

The SD amplitude comes from the
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{7}
and
\mathcal{O}_{1,2}
operators Fig.
1
.
The weak annihilation (WA) topology is doubly Cabibbo-suppressed, therefore it can be ignored in the evaluation of the SD.

Electromagnetic dipole (left) and weak annihilation amplitudes (right) for
B_{c}\to B^{\prime}_{1}(B^{*})\gamma

Focusing on the positive parity channel, the hadronic matrix element is parametrized as

\displaystyle\bra{B^{\prime}_{1}(p^{\prime},\epsilon)}\bar{u}\sigma_{\mu\nu}c\ket{B_{c}(p)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon^{*}\cdot q}{\left(m_{B_{c}}+m_{B^{\prime}_{1}}\right)^{2}}\left(p_{\mu}p^{\prime}_{\nu}-p_{\nu}p^{\prime}_{\mu}\right)T^{\prime}_{0}(q^{2})

(5)

\displaystyle+\left(p_{\mu}\epsilon^{*}_{\nu}-p_{\nu}\epsilon^{*}_{\mu}\right)T^{\prime}_{1}(q^{2})+\left(p^{\prime}_{\mu}\epsilon^{*}_{\nu}-p^{\prime}_{\nu}\epsilon^{*}_{\mu}\right)T^{\prime}_{2}(q^{2}),

with
q=p-p^{\prime}
,
\lambda
and
\epsilon
the photon and
B^{\prime}_{1}
polarization vectors. Since the
\bar{u}\sigma_{\mu\nu}\gamma_{5}c
matrix element is obtained using
\sigma_{\mu\nu}\gamma_{5}=-\frac{i}{2}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\sigma^{\alpha\beta}
, with
\varepsilon^{0123}=-1
, the parity-conserving and parity-violating SD amplitudes in Eq. (
4

A^{SD}_{PC}=i\frac{G_{F}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}m_{c}\alpha^{1/2}(C^{eff}_{7}+C^{\prime}_{7})(T_{1}^{\prime}(0)+T_{2}^{\prime}(0)),

(6)

A^{SD}_{PV}=-i\frac{G_{F}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}m_{c}\alpha^{1/2}(C^{eff}_{7}-C^{\prime}_{7})(T_{1}^{\prime}(0)+T_{2}^{\prime}(0)).

(7)

To determine the form factors
T_{i}
we use an approach based on the heavy quark spin symmetry
[
6
,
7
,
8

9
]
. For a generic Dirac matrix
\Gamma
, the matrix elements of quark currents for negative and positive parity doublets, invariant under rotations of the heavy quark spins, can be obtained using the trace formalism
[
10
]
:

\bra{B^{\prime}_{1}(v,k)}\bar{u}\Gamma c\ket{B_{c}(v)}=-\text{Tr}\left[\bar{S}\Omega^{\prime}\left(v,a_{0}k\right)\Gamma H\right],

(8)

where
S
and
H
are the positive and negative parity
spin doublet fields representation of the states
(B_{c},B^{*}_{c})
and
(B_{0},B^{\prime}_{1})
.
The form factor
\Omega^{\prime}
encoding the nonperturbative dynamics is written as
[
6
,
7
]

\Omega^{\prime}\left(v,a_{0}k\right)=\Omega^{\prime}_{1}\left(v,a_{0}k\right)+\not{k}a_{0}\Omega^{\prime}_{2}\left(v,a_{0}k\right).

(9)

The dimensionful parameter
a_{0}
represents the length scale involved in the process: we use the same value for both matrix elements.
Since
m_{c}\ll m_{b}
, the
b
quark remain almost unaffected in the
B_{c}
transition, and the velocity of the final meson is nearly unchanged.
Using Eq. (
8

B^{\prime}_{1}

\bra{B^{\prime}_{1}(v,k;\epsilon)}\bar{u}\sigma_{\mu\nu}c\ket{B_{c}(v)}=-i\sqrt{\frac{m_{B_{c}}}{m_{B^{\prime}_{1}}}}\left[\Omega^{\prime}_{1}\left(k_{\mu}\epsilon^{*}_{\nu}-k_{\nu}\epsilon^{*}_{\mu}\right)+2a_{0}\Omega^{\prime}_{2}m_{B^{\prime}_{1}}\epsilon^{*}\cdot v\left(k_{\mu}v_{\nu}-k_{\nu}v_{\mu}\right)\right].

(10)

The form factors
\Omega_{1,2}^{\prime}
can be obtained using a determination of matrix elements in a non perturbative approach.
In
[
11
]
using a covariant light-front approach the form factors for the

B_{c}\to\left(B_{sJ},B_{dJ}\right)
decays in SM have been determined. We can use these form factors assuming isospin symmetry.

The heavy quark spin symmetry produces relations allowing to determine the tensor form factors. We obtain the finite mass form factors in term of
\Omega_{1}^{\prime}
and
a_{0}\;\Omega_{2}^{\prime}
:

\displaystyle T_{0}^{\prime}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=2i{(m_{B_{c}}+m_{B_{1}^{\prime}})^{2}\sqrt{m_{B_{1}^{\prime}}}\over m_{B_{c}}^{3/2}}a_{0}\,\Omega_{2}^{\prime},

(11)

\displaystyle T_{1}^{\prime}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=-{m_{B_{1}^{\prime}}\over m_{B_{c}}}T_{2}^{\prime}(q^{2}),

(12)

\displaystyle T_{2}^{\prime}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=-i\sqrt{{m_{B_{c}}}\over m_{B_{1}^{\prime}}}\Omega_{1}^{\prime}.

(13)

Eq. (

Long-distance amplitude

LD contributions correspond to processes involving intermediate hadrons. There are two kind of terms. The first one comes from the weak annihilation (WA) amplitude in Fig.
2

1

2

B_{c}\to B^{\prime}_{1}\gamma
the intermediate
\rho^{0}

\omega

\phi

B_{c}\to B^{*}_{u}\gamma

7
Conclusions

Motivated by previous analysis on the rare transition
c\to u\gamma
in the
B_{c}
decays to
B^{*}
, we focus on the
B_{1}^{\prime}
channel to study if it is less polluted by LD contributions.

From a model independent analysis based on heavy quark spin symmetry, we found the relation in Eq. (
12
) between the tensor form factor entering in the SD amplitude for the
B_{1}^{\prime}
channel. Their combination in the amplitude produces a suppression of the SD contribution to the process. Therefore, while LD contributions affect more the
B^{*}
channel, the hadronic suppression in the
B_{1}^{\prime}
case makes it less suitable for accessing NP.

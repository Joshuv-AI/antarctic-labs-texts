P21-activated kinase 4: a potent oncogene, molecular mechanisms and opportunities for targeted therapy
Authors: Guojing Huang, Yalin Yang, Qian Luo, Xiaoqing Wang, Yifan Wang, Rong Liu, Wenting Liu, Shiwen Yan, Xiang Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractP21-activated kinase 4 (PAK4), recognized as a key effector protein of the Rho family of small G proteins Cell division regulatory protein 42 homologue (Cdc42) and Ras-related C3 botulinum toxin substrate 1 (Rac1), is a serine/threonine protein kinase that plays a critical role in a variety of biological processes.By analyzing the Tumour Immune Estimation Resource 2.0 (TIMER 2.0) databases, we observed that the expression level of PAK4 was significantly elevated in a wide range of tumors, and that its elevated expression level was strongly associated with poor patient prognosis. The activation of PAK4 plays an important role in promoting tumorigenesis and progression, which has been revealed by KEGG and GO analyses to play a key regulatory role in classical oncogenic signaling pathways, such as cell cycle, apoptosis, Ras signaling axis, PI3K/AKT, and P53, etc. Moreover, PAK4 plays an important function in tumor cell proliferation, migration invasion, cytoskeleton remodeling, and cell adhesion. In recent years, PAK4 has become a new target for therapeutic studies of antitumor drugs, and multiple types of PAK4 inhibitors have been gradually discovered. This review explores in detail the role of PAK4 in the following classical signaling pathways: PAK4/PI3K/AKT, PAK4/Wnt/β-catenin, PAK4/LIMK1/cofilin, and PAK4/MEK/ERK, and summarizes the PAK4-related inhibitors. The in-depth analysis of the classical pathway of carcinogenesis in this paper reveals the key role of PAK4 in cancer development and provides a theoretical basis for the development of novel PAK4 small molecule inhibitors.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12935-025-04042-z.Keywords: PAK4, Inhibitor, Prognosis, Role, Signaling pathway

Introduction

P21 activated kinase (PAK) was originally discovered as a downstream target of Rho GTPases, which was significant in the restructuring of the cytoskeleton. Initially, research on PAK primarily concentrated on investigating the signaling mechanisms involved in controlling cell adhesion and migration. Subsequently, its contribution to cell viability, growth, and infiltration was further explored. As depicted in Fig. 1, this grouping comprises six PAK proteins found in mammals and PAK1-PAK6 are divided into different groups based on their structures, sequence homology and regulation: Group I (PAK1-PAK3) and Group II (PAK1-PAK6) [23]. Group I PAKs (PAK1- PAK3) can be triggered by the interaction with the Rho GTPases Cell division regulatory protein 42 homologue (Cdc42) and Ras-related C3 botulinum toxin substrate 1 (Rac1), but Group II PAKs (PAK4-PAK6) are independent of GTPases [76]. All PAKs possess a conserved kinase domain at the C-terminal region and a regulatory domain at the N-terminal end, including the p21-binding region (PBD), while the initial subset of PAKs includes an extra autoinhibitory region (AID) as well as a domain that binds to PAK-interacting exchange factors. However, Group II PAKs play important parts in many fundamental cellular processes, among which certain hold particular importance in the growth and advancement of cancer. The latest research shows that PAK4 participates in numerous facets of cellular signal transduction, meanwhile, it is closely related to tumour formation and development. Since the discovery of PAK4, researchers have been working on its role in cancers, in which it is often overexpressed [105].

Domain structure of PAKs. (A) The PAK family of kinases comprises six isoforms. (B) PAK1-PAK6 have been categorized into two separate groups according to their distinct structures, sequence similarity and control: Group I (PAK1- PAK3) and Group II (PAK4-PAK6)

P21-activated kinase 4 (PAK4) is the most characteristic quantity of the Group II PAKs and was initially identified during the quest for filamentous cytoskeletal regulatory proteins that governed filopodia formation as a result of Cdc42 signaling. The gene PAK4 is situated on the chromosomal locus 19q13, segment 2, in humans, and its coding sequence produces a protein with a molecular weight of 64-kDa. The PAK4 protein is primarily found in the region surrounding the nucleus and is transferred to the Golgi apparatus after binding to Cdc42 [34, 102]. Later, the PAK4 protein is present at the cellular periphery in response to growth factors and integrin-mediated signaling. The PAK4 protein is similar to other PAK isoforms, and this protein consists of a PBD domain at the N-terminus and a significantly conserved serine/threonine kinase domain at the C-terminus. Compared with Group I PAKs, the N-terminal sequence of PAK4 is much lower than that of PBD, and unlike Group II PAKs, there is no nuclear localization signal in this region [37, 74]. In addition, the key region of PAK4 is also significantly different from all other PAKs, including three proline-rich SH3 (Src homology 3) domain binding sites. According to the present discoveries, PAK4 is a representative Group II member of the PAK family, which can be growth factor receptor activators. and other extracellular signals and regulates and controls many biological functions are mediated through many downstream binding proteins or kinase substrates, such as tumour growth, cytoskeletal recombination, and gene transcriptional regulation, especially during malignant cell transformation and tumour cell invasion and metastasis [99]. Moreover, PAK4 is a critical proto-oncogene that is upregulated in many tumours. The PAK4 protein is overexpressed in tumour cell lines, for example Gastric Cancer,Hepatocellular Carcinoma,Breast Cancer,Prostate Cancer,Skin Cancer,Gallbladder Cancer,Choriocarcinoma. Studies have found that PAK4 is closely related to apoptosis, tumorigenesis, and cytoskeletal reconstruction. Therefore, therapy targeting PAK4 is important for the management of certain types of tumours.

Expression of PAK4 in cancer

To evaluate the function of PAK4 in tumours, PAK4 expression levels in tumours was studied using the Tumour Immune Estimation Resource 2.0 (TIMER 2.0) database. As in Fig. 2, compared to normal tissues, the expression levels of PAK4 in Bladder urothelial carcinoma (BLCA), Breast invasive carcinoma (BRCA), Cervical squamous cell carcinoma and endocervical adenocarcinoma (CHOL), Esophageal carcinoma (ESCA), Head-neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSC), Kidney renal clear cell carcinoma (KIRC), Kidney renal papillary cell carcinoma (KIRP), Liver hepatocellular carcinoma (LIHC), Lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC), Stomach adenocarcinoma (STAD), Uterine corpus endometrial carcinoma (UCEC), Colon adenocarcinoma (COAD), Kidney chromophobe (KICH), Prostate adenocarcinoma (PRAD), and Thyroid carcinoma (THCA) were relatively high, which indicated that PAK4 may be closely linked to occurrence and development of the following tumours.

Signaling pathways involving PAK4-interacting proteins

The occurrence and development of tumours involve the interaction of multiple signalling pathways. As shown in Fig. 3, we used the STRING database to show the relationship between PAK4 and proteins linked to the classical signaling pathway. Network analysis showed that PAK4-related proteins were directly or indirectly related to key elements of the cell cycle, apoptosis, Ras, PI3K and P53 pathways. Then, according to the KEGG database, we found that PAK4-related proteins were involved in the cytoskeleton, cell adhesion, cellular replication and movement and were directly or indirectly associated with the Wnt, PI3K-AKT and other signalling pathways. As shown in Fig. 4, PAK4 plays a role in the modulation of numerous signaling pathways in the body and may become a target for tumour therapy. Therefore, here we focus on the following four classical pathways: PAK4/PI3K/AKT, PAK4/Wnt/β−catenin, PAK4/LIMK1/cofilin, and PAK4/MEK/ERK.

PAK4/PI3K/AKT signalling pathway

Recent investigations have demonstrated an association between PAK4 and PI3K, an imperative player in various cancer types. PI3K, comprising the regulatory domain p85 and catalytic domain p110, functions as a lipid kinase and can be directly stimulated downstream of the oncogene RAS. Activated PI3K can phosphorylate phosphatidylinositol diphosphate (PIP2) to form phosphatidylinositol triphosphate (PIP3), which leads to the recruitment of PDK1- and AKT-containing PH domains into PIP3, resulting in protein phosphorylation of PDK21 and PDK2 on the membrane, which promotes PDK1 to phosphorylate a residue on AKT. When the second site of AKT is phosphorylated by a different kinase, it leads to the activation of AKT. Activated AKT can regulate cell survival, proliferation, apoptosis and migration by the phosphorylation or inhibition of a series of downstream substrates. Therefore, unregulated AKT activation is usually associated with cancer [30].

Recent research has established a correlation between PAK4 and PI3K in breast cancer. The expression of PAK4 is increased in human breast cancer cells and is associated with tumor metastasis, tumour size and advanced disease. Further studies showed that PAK4 could activate PI3K/AKT signal transduction in the MDA-MB-231 breast cancer cell line and that PI3K/AKT was downstream of PAK4. The heightened expression of PI3K is associated with the upregulation of PAK4 in breast cancer cells., the increase in phosphorylated AKT and the downstream effector mTOR of AKT. However, studies on PAK4 kinase-deficient mutants indicate that PAK4 induces AKT phosphorylation through kinase-dependent and kinase-independent mechanisms [43].

Wnt/β-catenin signalling pathway

In the Wnt/β-catenin signaling cascade, the binding of Wnt ligand to fzzled receptors (FZ or Fzd) on the cell membrane triggers the activation of low-density lipoprotein receptor-related proteins (LRP5 and LRP6). When there is no ligand bound, β-catenin is constantly degraded by the Axin complex, which consists of Axin, APC, CK1, and GSK3. CK1 and GSK3 phosphorylate the amino-terminal region of β-catenin, leading to its ubiquitination and targeting it for degradation. Activation of the Wnt signaling pathway can impede the phosphorylation and degradation of β-catenin, resulting in its accumulation and translocation to the nucleus. Inside the nucleus, β-catenin interacts with T-cell factor (TCF) and lymphoid enhancer (LEF) family transcription factors, thereby initiating the continuous transcription of target genes involved in the Wnt/β-catenin pathway, such as c-myc and CyclinD1, and produce abnormal proteins related to tumorigenesis. Therefore, the stabilization and stimulation of the Wnt/β-catenin signaling cascade play important roles in the cellular process of tumorigenesis [9, 56, 76].

PAK4 is associated with the Wnt/β-catenin pathway. In the cytoplasm, PAK4 and PAK1 both phosphorylate β-catenin at serine 675 to inhibit the ubiquitination of β-catenin without degradation. PAK4 upregulates the expression of β-catenin enhances the transcriptional activity of TCF/LEF. PAK4 is crucial in regulating the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway by mediating the dynamic shuttling of β-catenin between the nucleus and cytoplasm, thus contributing to the maintenance of its stability and activity [53].

Recent research indicates a connection between PAK4 and the Wnt pathway mediated by methylation. SETD6 is pivotal in governing the regulation of this pathway. In the absence of SETD6, the transcriptional activity of β-catenin is inhibited. SETD6 can bind and methylate PAK4 in vitro and in chromatin. When SETD6 is present in chromatin, it augments the direct interaction between PAK4 and β-catenin. The methylation of PAK4 can be linked with β-catenin, which causes β-catenin to accumulate in the nucleus, thus enhancing the transcriptional activity of Wnt/β-catenin. PAK4 serves as a molecular connection connecting SETD6 and β-catenin, resulting in the formation of the SETD6-PAK4-β-catenin complex. This complex has the capability to augment the transcriptional activity of Wnt/β-catenin target genes [95].

PAK4/LIMK1/cofilin signalling pathway

Early research on PAK4 have demonstrated its critical involvement in cytoskeletal remodeling, which directly facilitates the mechanical force generation necessary for tumor cell migration, invasion, and morphological changes. Osteosarcoma, a highly aggressive bone tumor characterized by pronounced invasiveness and a propensity for pulmonary metastasis, exhibits invasion and metastatic potential that are closely associated with cytoskeletal dynamics. Notably, PAK4, LIM domain kinase 1 (LIMK1), and cofilin are all highly expressed in osteosarcoma. PAK4 activation is dependent on hepatocyte growth factor (HGF). Once activated, PAK4 phosphorylates LIMK1, which subsequently phosphorylates and inactivates its downstream target cofilin. This results in impaired F-actin depolymerization activity, leading to enhanced stability, polymerization, and reorganization of intracellular F-actin filaments. Collectively, the PAK4–LIMK1–cofilin signaling axis plays a pivotal role in promoting osteosarcoma proliferation, invasion, metastasis, and cytoskeletal remodeling [55, 113].

It’s noteworthy that PAK4 exhibits elevated expression levels in non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) in humans, and the overexpression of PAK4 is significantly related to clinical stage, degree of differentiation, and lymph node and distant metastases. LIMK1 is overexpressed in NSCLC tissues, and PAK4 can promote migration by directly interacting with LIMK1 and inducing its phosphorylation. Research findings indicate that the signaling pathway involving PAK4, LIMK1, and cofilin has been shown to enhance cell migration in both prostate and gastric cancers. Because of the importance of PAK4 in cytoskeletal reorganization, the PAK4-LIMK1-cofilin pathway seems to play a crucial part in the advancement of prostate cancer, lung cancer, and pancreatic cancer [20, 57, 83, 87].

PAK4/MEK1/ERK signalling pathway

Mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) is a universally conserved serine/threonine protein kinase found in eukaryotic organisms. It is involved in numerous cellular processes, including but not limited to cell proliferation, cell differentiation, cellular movement, and apoptosis. MAPK cascade signal transduction is composed of three different levels of molecules. MAPK is activated by the phosphorylation of MAPKK, and MAPKK is activated by MAPKK kinase (MAPKK). The activation of MAPKKK is facilitated through interactions with small GTPases and/or other proteases, establishing a connection between MAPK and cell surface receptors as well as extracellular signals. In mammals, there are five distinct MAPK signal transduction pathways, with the ERK1/2 pathway specifically overseeing cell growth and differentiation. PAK4 is associated with the MEK1/ERK signalling pathway in the onset and progression of diverse cancer forms.

The expression of miR-199a/b-3p was markedly reduced in gastric cancer tissues compared to normal tissues. The antitumour mechanism of miR-199a/b-3p demonstrated that miR-199a/b-3p was found to suppress the proliferation of GC cells through the downregulation of the PAK4/MEK/ERK signalling pathway. Previous studies have found that the crucial involvement of the MEK/ERK pathway in PAK4-induced cell proliferation has been substantiated by Bin Zeng et al. Overall, the targeting of PAK4 by miR-199a/b-3p demonstrates its inhibitory effect on cancer in GC cells [26, 110].

PAK4: a potential prognostic biomarker

For a deeper comprehension of the relevance of PAK4 expression in cancer, an analysis of the correlation between PAK4 expression and survival was conducted using the Kaplan-Meier Plotter database. As depicted in Fig. 5, PAK4 expression levels in LIHC, LUAD, LUSC, OV, SARC and THYM exhibited a negative correlation with the survival rate (P < 0.05). In these tumour types, the high expression of PAK4 indicates a decrease in the survival rate. In LIHC, high expression of PAK4 is more associated with survival than low expression of PAK4, which could serve as a sensitive prognostic marker of LIHC. Therefore, PAK4 is not solely implicated in the advancement of cancer cells but also has the potential to serve as a valuable biomarker for predicting the prognosis of certain tumors.

Mechanisms of P21-activated kinase 4 activation

The activation of Group I kinases can be realized not only by binding to GTPases of the Rho family but also by a non-GTPase-dependent activation mechanism; that is, PAKs are digested by restriction proteases to produce C-terminal fragments with kinase activity. In contrast to Group I kinases, although Group II kinases can also bind to Cdc42 or Rac, they are not activated through this pathway [27]. After removing the PBD domain, it was found that PAK4 containing only the C-terminal kinase domain has stronger activity than intact PAK4, which means that PAK4 can also regulate enzyme activity in a molecular manner. According to reports, the multifunctional cytokine HGF (hepatocyte growth factor) can activate PAK4. PAK4 can also be activated by the MKK/p38 MAPK signalling pathway. At the same time, many kinases can regulate the activity of PAKs by phosphorylating PAK kinase or its binding protein. Ser474, the autonomous phosphorylation site of kinase PAK4, is extremely important for PAK4 activation. In addition, PAK4 can also be activated as a growth factor receptor, but its specific activity regulation mechanism needs further study.

The role of PAK4 in cancer

The emergence and progression of cancer cells are complex processes that include activation and inactivation of signalling pathways, changes in cell morphology, cytoskeletal remodelling, and cells themselves fighting apoptosis. In these complex processes, PAK4 participates in the malignant alteration of cells, coupled with the infiltration and dissemination of tumor cells, plays a pivotal role in the progression of tumors.

Cell cytoskeletal activity and cell adhesion

Remodelling of the cytoskeleton plays an important part in the migration of cancer cells from one site to another. The infiltration of adjacent malignant tissues by tumor cells necessitates the harmonized control of actin cytoskeleton reorganization and subcellular adhesions [93]. If the cytoskeleton signal enzyme is improperly regulated, it may have adverse effects, such as oncogenic transformation. Rho GTPases play a significant role in eliciting actin cytoskeleton activation by activating numerous effectors, such as Wiskott Aldrich Syndrome Protein (WASP), Verprolin Homologues of the WASP Family, Rho Kinase [91], and PAKs. In particular, PAKs exert an influence on actin polymerization by governing actin and actin depolymerization factor (ADF) regulation. Actin depolymerization factors correspond to actin-binding proteins that control cytoskeletal dynamics through the severing and disassembling of actin filaments [13]. ADF and cofilin are downregulated by LIM domain kinase (LIMK), while LIMK activation is due to phosphorylation of ROCK or PAKs on Thr508 [28]. The primary role of PAK4 is to govern the organization of the actin cytoskeleton, thereby orchestrating cellular morphology, adhesion, and motility [24]. The expression of PAK4 can stimulate the formation of filamentous pseudopods downstream of Cdc42, thereby promoting cytoskeletal changes [2]. In addition, the modulation of cytoskeletal dynamics by the serine/threonine kinase PAK4 is facilitated through the involvement of LIM kinase 1 and cofilin. LIM kinase is a common downstream effect of several signalling pathways that control the signalling nodes of cytoskeletal dynamics through the phosphorylation of the cofilin family [72]. LIM kinases are positioned downstream of the Rho family GTPases due to their ability to phosphorylate specific threonine residues of PAK or the Rho kinase ROCK, which are activated by Rac and Cdc42 kinases. LIMK, a serine/threonine kinase, consists of two primary subtypes: LIMK1 and LIMK2. In terms of structure, LIMK contains two LIM domains, a PDZ domain, and a proline/serine/serine-rich region situated in the central part, with a kinase domain located at the carboxy terminus. The phosphorylation of LIMK1 and LIMK2 occurs at threonine 508 and threonine 505, respectively, catalyzed by the upstream kinase ROCK1, dystrophic kinase-associated Cdc42-binding kinase (Marck), and PAK 1, 4, or 6. LIMK1 exhibits widespread expression in various organ tissues, including the cerebral cortex, parathyroid gland, bronchi, and stomach. LIMK1 is a target protein downstream of PAK4. Moreover, LIMK1 plays an important role in phosphorylating and rendering inactive another protein involved in cytoskeletal regulation, cofilin, it effectively hinders the actin depolymerization activity of cofilin [92].

Cofilin is a member of the ADF/cofilin family, a group of related proteins with similar biochemical activity. In mammals, this family includes cofilin 1, cofilin 2, and ADF. While both cofilin 1 and ADF are present in nonmuscle tissues, cofilin 1 is the predominant subtype, constituting the majority, and ADF makes up around 5–10% of cofilin 1. Cofilin, recognized as an actin depolymerizing factor, facilitates the depolymerization of filamentous actin (F-actin) and undergoes inactivation through phosphorylation. This process is acknowledged to play a role in propelling protrusions and promoting cellular migration [12]. In mammals, the inactivation of ADF/cofilin occurs through the phosphorylation of Ser3. This phosphorylation event doesn’t alter the protein conformation but introduces charge repulsion, thereby impeding its ability to bind to actin [15, 65]. PAK4 activates LIMK1 and inactivates SSH1 by increasing ADF/cofilin activity, resulting in actin filament renewal. Previous studies have shown that PAK4 affects cytoskeletal changes through the phosphorylation of GEF-H1, a RhoGEF that is cellular processes such as cell migration, cellular polarization, the regulation of the cell cycle, and have associations with cancer [22, 29].

PAK4 is intrinsically linked to cell adhesion. Adhesion plays a dual role in the genesis and development of tumour cells: on the one hand, tumour cells must be detached from the primary lesion where they originally adhered; on the other hand, tumour cells need adhesion to move [100, 108]. PAK4, a downstream protein of Cdc42, is believed to modulate the disruption of cell adhesion through its kinase activity. Overexpression of PAK4 causes loss of focal adhesions. The available scientific evidence suggests that downregulation of PAK4 in DU145 prostate cancer cells is associated with an augmentation in the size and quantity of focal adhesions. This heightened focal adhesion formation may result from the incapacity of DU145 cells to adequately respond to HGF-stimulated migration, as HGF-induced cell proliferation is reliant on proper adhesion switching [98]. HGF and its receptor c-Met play critical roles in promoting cancer cell motility and invasiveness. PAK4 can be recruited to c-Met and activated by it.

Cell cycle

In the past few years, the role of PAK4 in controlling the progression of the cell cycle and regulating mitosis has become a research hotspot. Dysregulation of the cell cycle is a significant contributing factor to tumorigenesis. Originally characterized as a cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor, P21 serves to restrict cell cycle advancement. Subsequently, research has unveiled the multifaceted nature of its function, demonstrating both inhibitory and stimulatory roles in cell cycle regulation. Hence, it is plausible that PAK4 participates in cell cycle control and influences the initiation of cell cycle progression, leading to carcinogenesis when PAK4 is improperly expressed [1]. Under conditions of serum deficiency, the absence of PAK4 leads to a reduction in G1 cell population and an elevation in cells residing in the G2/M phase. It has been previously observed that PAK4 levels exhibit a rapid rise during early G1 phase, followed by a subsequent decline within a few hours and sustained low levels throughout the remaining duration [69]. Recent research has additionally discovered that PAK4 can have a significant impact on the G2/M stage of the cellular life cycle by phosphorylating the Ran GTPase. Ran GTPase serves as a controller for various biological processes, such as the formation of the mitotic spindle and chromosome segregation [16]. In the early G1 phase of the cell cycle, p21 actively controls the progression, which is evident from the fact that mitogens increase the levels of p21 via the Ras-Raf-MEK-ERK signaling pathway. PAK4 could potentially interact with other components of the cell cycle machinery, indirectly influencing the expression and stability of p21. The inhibitory effect of p21 on the cell cycle is partially attributed to its ability to associate with CDK2, consequently phosphorylating Rb. Another mode of negative regulation is accomplished by binding to proliferating cell nuclear antigen (PCNA), leading to the inhibition of DNA replication [69].

Cell proliferation, cell migration and invasion

Migration and infiltration play crucial roles in the development of cancer and are crucial for the spread of cancer cells to other parts of the body. The process of cancer cell metastasis includes a cascade of modifications in cellular behavior triggered by oncogenic transformation, which enables invasion of adjacent tissues, migration through various tissues, penetration into blood vessels and lymphatic system, and establishment at distant locations. Accomplishing invasion of the neighboring interstitial tissue necessitates synchronized control of actin cytoskeletal reorganization and subcellular adhesion turnover [101]. The dynamic reorganization of the cytoskeleton is closely associated with cellular motility, and it serves as a critical component in the process of malignant tumor cell migration, invasion, and metastasis [33]. The phosphorylation of LIMK1 and cofilin by PAK4, as well as its regulation of cell microtubules, and interaction with the intracellular domain of integrins, are the main mechanisms through which PAK4 influences cell movement. A novel pathway has been put forward, suggesting that PAK4 directly interacts with the intracellular domain of integrins and selectively modulates cancer cell migration in an integrin-specific fashion. Specifically, PAK4 has the ability to bind with integrin αvβ5 and selectively enhance cell migration mediated by this particular integrin [111]. Based on our observations, it is hypothesized that integrin αvβ5 plays a crucial role in the migration and metastasis of cancer cells both in in vivo and in vitro settings, particularly in response to growth factor stimulation [18]. In vivo, integrin αvβ5 primarily functions as the primary receptor for vitronectin (VN) in cancer cells. Through a direct interaction with the integrin αvβ5 subunit, PAK4 acts as a specific regulator of cell migration mediated by αvβ5. Vitronectin receptors that widely express αvβ5 mediate cell adhesion but cannot initiate cell movement [50]. However, treatment with tyrosine kinase receptor agonists or protein kinase C activators can promote the proliferation and migration of αvβ5-dependent cells on vitronectin [48].

A recent revelation has come to light indicating that the interaction between PAK4 and LIMK1 leads to the phosphorylation of LIMK1. This, in turn, enhances the capacity of LIMK1 to phosphorylate cofilin [24]. The collaborative influence of PAK4 and LIMK1 on cytoskeletal alterations impacting cellular motility suggests that the combined action of PAK4 and LIMK1 enhances the speed of cell migration. Conversely, a reduction in PAK4 expression diminishes cell velocity [7, 24, 112]. Studies on malignant choriocarcinoma have found that overexpressed PAK4 greatly enhances cell migration and invasion.The significant reduction of MT1-MMP mRNA and protein expression, as well as the migration of PAK4 siRNA knockout cells towards JEG3 and JAR cells, underscores the role of PAK4 in regulating MT1-MMP. It’s important to note that MT1-MMP is a collagenase that is anchored to the cell membrane and expressed on the cell surface, which is the key to tumour invasion by degrading the extracellular tissue barrier [82].

Apoptosis

Apoptosis is a basic phenomenon of life and the basic measure to maintain the dynamic balance of cell number in vivo. The initiation of apoptosis is the opening or closing of a series of control switches in the cell after sensing the corresponding signal stimulation. Caspase 8, the initiation factor of apoptosis, can cause cytochrome C to fall off the mitochondria through the signal transduction pathway. When cytochrome C is released into the cytoplasm, it has the capability to bind with apoptosis-related factor 1 (Apaf-1) in the presence of dATP, forming a polymer that facilitates the association with caspase 9, resulting in the formation of apoptotic bodies. The activation of caspase 9 triggers a cascade effect, activating other caspases, including caspase 3, ultimately leading to the initiation of apoptosis [35].

In the process of cell culture, many factors can induce apoptosis, such as Fas ligand, serum deficiency, UV irradiation, and cytokines (TNFα). Nerina Gnesutta et al. studied the role of PAK4 in apoptosis. In HeLa and NIH3T3 cell lines, the overexpression of PAK4 could escape apoptosis through three stimulation methods: serum deficiency, TNF-α/CHX and UV irradiation. PAK4 causes a delay in caspase activation, which leads to a delay in apoptosis. The antiapoptotic function of PAK4 may be related to its kinase activity. Investigation into the anti-apoptotic mechanism of PAK4 revealed that its expression enhances the phosphorylation of the pro-apoptotic protein BAD. Research by the team of Audrey Minden demonstrated that stable PAK4-expressing cell lines exhibit elevated phosphorylation levels of BAD at serine residues 112 and 136. PAK4 directly phosphorylates BAD at serine 112, thereby inhibiting BAD protein activity and subsequently suppressing apoptosis. However, the mechanism underlying the increased phosphorylation of BAD at serine 136 in PAK4-expressing cells requires further investigation [34].

PAK4 represents a promising candidate for therapeutic intervention in the context of cancer

With a growing body of research identifying PAK4 as a pivotal element in cancer advancement, it is not unexpected that it has gained prominence as a viable target for drug development. Despite the identification of various components in oncogenic signaling, the existing clinically effective therapeutics focused on signal transduction primarily target a limited subset, notably receptor tyrosine kinases. In the quest for novel treatments, extensive areas of the signaling landscape have remained underexplored, despite accumulating evidence of their critical roles. As shown in Supplementary Table, PAK4 inhibitors are classified as follows based on their mechanism of action: The first type is an ATP competitive inhibitor. A number of small molecule compounds natural products that have been identified thus far exclusively function as ATP competitive inhibitors. These inhibitors typically interact with the highly conserved ATP-binding pocket within the kinase domain. The second type is an allosteric inhibitor. The noncatalytic site of the enzyme molecule undergoes a conformational change after reversible noncovalent binding of certain compounds, thereby changing the activity state of the enzyme [78]. The third type is miRNA inhibitors. MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are short endogenous noncoding RNAs that modulate gene expression through base-pairing interference with mRNA translation. Research indicates that aberrant expression of miRNAs is associated with the onset and progression of human cancer. In addition, we summarized the combination of PAK4 inhibitors with other drugs.

ATP competitive inhibitor

One of the earliest PAK inhibitors produced is PF-3758309 by Pfizer. The initial inhibitor of PAK4 kinase, PF-3758309, is characterized as a potent pyrrolopyrazole inhibitor that competes with ATP binding to PAK4 [36]. Studies have shown that PF-3758309 inhibits anchorage-independent growth in diverse tumor cell lines when studied in vitro, and it restrains the development of multiple tumor xenograft models when examined in vivo. Within cells, PF-3758309 suppresses the phosphorylation of the PAK4 matrix GEF-H1 (IC50 = 1.3 nM) (GEF-H1: guanine nucleotide exchange factor) and anchorage-independent growth of tumour cell lines (IC50 = 4.7 nM) [67]. The inhibitory impact of PF-3758309 on PAKs not solely hampers tumor growth but also enhances the tumor immune response by elevating the count of spleens and intratumoral T lymphocytes [97]. In models of malignancy, PF-3758309 hinders pathways reliant on PAK4 in proteomic analyses within tumor models and modulates functional processes related to cellular proliferation and survival. In human lung cancer, PF-3758309 inhibits PAK4, and PF-3758309 induces the attenuation of A549 human lung cancer cell metastasis by downregulating two gelatinases (MMP-2 and MMP-9) through a small-molecule inhibitor targeting p21-activated kinase 4. This inhibition extends to the CREB, NF-kB, and β-catenin signaling pathways [80]. In addition, knockout of PAK4 in A549 cells also inhibited anchorage-independent growth and affected cell morphology and significant changes in the actin cytoskeleton [67]. In neuroblastoma, elevated expression of PAK4 was observed in neuroblastoma tissues, correlating with tumor malignancy and prognostic outcomes. PF-3758309 demonstrated a significant capacity to induce G1 phase cell cycle arrest and apoptosis in neuroblastoma cell lines. Furthermore, the inhibitory impact of PF-3758309 on PAK4 led to increased expression of CDKN1A, BAD, and BAK1, along with decreased expression of Bcl-2 and Bax. The ERK signaling pathway was implicated in the regulatory processes of cell cycle and apoptosis triggered by PF-3758309. These findings underscore PAK4 as a promising therapeutic target for neuroblastoma, suggesting PF-3758309 as a potential candidate for neuroblastoma treatment [54]. The main disadvantage of PF-03758309 is that it is a multidrug transporter substrate, resulting in its low bioavailability in humans [17]. The discontinuation of the compound ensued following a solitary clinical trial, attributed to inadequate pharmacokinetic properties (such as low human bioavailability) and a deficiency in efficacy.

GNE-2861 (also referred to as Compound 17), developed by Genentech, is a highly potent inhibitor that selectively targets Group II PAKs (PAK4, PAK5, and PAK6) with IC50 values of 7.5 nM, 36 nM, and 126 nM, respectively, while demonstrating negligible activity against Group I PAKs (PAK1, PAK2, and PAK3).This specificity facilitates the targeted pharmacological modulation of Group II PAKs within cells [89]. The compound acts on the ATP-binding pocket of PAK4 and the pocket behind methionine because acetylene alcohol binds the pocket well and hydrogen bonds with phenylalanine Phe459, which results in good selective inhibition of PAK4. Within the context of breast cancer, targeting PAK4 could potentially disrupt ERα signaling and overcome resistance to tamoxifen. The small-molecule inhibitor GNE-2861, specific to the Group II p21-activated kinase, intervenes in estrogen receptor alpha signaling, ultimately reinstating sensitivity to tamoxifen in breast cancer cells [62, 116]. However, its oral bioavailability is poor.

The study reported that the Nikfarjam M team had developed a novel small molecule inhibitor of PAK4, PAKib, with an IC50 of approximately 500 nM. This inhibitor can directly bind to and inhibit the kinase activity of PAK4. PAKib can induce cell cycle arrest and apoptosis through multiple signaling pathways such as mTOR and MARP. This study found that the combined use of PAKib and gemcitabine could produce a significant synergistic effect, significantly enhancing the latter’s killing effect on pancreatic cancer cells. These findings provide a basis for the clinical application prospects of PAKib [42]. In 2016 and 2017, LC-0882 and GL-1196, LiFeng of China Medical University and ChengMaosheng of Shenyang Pharmaceutical University, reported that the small molecular compounds GL-1196 and LC-0882, after being identified via computer virtual screening, these compounds exhibited noteworthy inhibition of PAK4 kinase, impacting the proliferation and invasion of gastric cancer cells. Nonetheless, details regarding their synthesis method remain unreported. Both GL-1196 and LC-0882 displayed substantial, dose-dependent inhibition of PAK4 kinase activity. As a small-molecule compound, GL-1196 demonstrates the capability to efficiently hinder the proliferation of human gastric cancer cells by suppressing the expression of PAK4/c-Src/EGFR/CyclinD1 and CDK4/6. Furthermore, GL-1196 exhibits inhibitory effects on the invasion of gastric cancer cells by disrupting the PAK4/LIMK1/cofilin pathway. Researchers unexpectedly found that GL-1196 can reduce the formation of filamentous pseudopodia and induce cell elongation by inhibiting PAK4 kinase activity in SGC7901 and BGC823 cells [83, 115].

LCH-7749944 represents a newly identified inhibitor of p21-activated kinase 4, displaying an IC50 of 14.93 µM. It exhibits limited inhibitory effects on PAK1, PAK5, and PAK6, members of the same kinase family as PAK4. In vitro, LCH-7749944 inhibits the migration and invasion of gastric cancer cells. It diminishes chorion formation and cell elongation, concurrently disrupting the PAK4/LIMK1/cofilin and PAK4/MEK-1/ERK1/2/MMP2 pathways, [114] but has not been tested in vivo. In addition, this investigation is specifically centered on gastric cancer cell lines, with an intention to explore the potential of LCH-7749944 as a therapeutic target for various other cancers.

Furthermore, several research teams have designed and synthesized novel small-molecule PAK4 inhibitors that directly target the ATP-binding pocket within the PAK4 kinase domain to exert inhibitory effects. ChenZhou Hao et al. designed and synthesized that the discovery of a novel set of PAK4 inhibitors, based on X-ray crystallography and structure-based drug design (SBDD), involving 4-aminoquinazoline-2-formamide derivatives. Among them, Compound 31 (CZh226) has significant PAK4 selectivity (346 times that of PAK1) and good kinase selectivity. It has been proven that the compound can effectively inhibit the migration and invasion of A549 tumour cells in vitro though regulating the downstream signalling pathway targeted by PAK4. PAK4 was overexpressed in A549 cells. Compound 31 had an antiproliferative effect and it exhibits a dosage-dependent effect on cell migration and invasion. Furthermore, this compound demonstrates favorable kinase specificity, desirable physicochemical attributes, and moderate in vitro and in vivo ADME properties. It has stable metabolism and pharmacokinetic characteristics in vivo, indicating that it may become a new anticancer drug [19, 39].

Byungjun Ryu et al. found a new PAK4 inhibitor, and it’s named KY-04031 (N2-(2-(1h-indole-3-yl)ethyl)-N4-(Ryu et al.)−6-methoxy-1,3,5-triazine-2,4-diamine) through high-throughput screening, which showed moderate inhibitory activity. The substitution of the triazine core in KY-04031 with quinazoline resulted in the development and production of a library of derivatives containing a 2,4-diaminoquinazoline motif. The strong PAK4 inhibitor Compound 9 d (IC50 =0.033 μM) was developed from the library. The compound was designed according to SAR study. An improved interaction of the drug molecule with PAK4 was realized through filling the gap of ATP binding. That resulted in stronger inhibition of PAK4 (24 times higher than KY-04031) and higher selectivity of PAK4. Compound 9 d inhibited the growth of A549 cells in a dose-dependent manner. Besides, it inhibits cell migration and invasion through the PAK4-LIMK1 signalling pathway [39, 79].

Among the novel 2,4-diaminoquinazoline derivatives synthesized and evaluated by Tianxiao Wu et al., compounds 8d and 9c exhibited strong inhibitory activity against PAK4, with IC50 values of 0.060 μM and 0.068 μM, respectively. The study demonstrated that in non-small cell lung cancer A549 cells, these compounds suppress proliferation by inhibiting the transition from the G1 to S phase of the cell cycle, and also inhibit the migration and invasion capabilities of A549 cells. Molecular docking analysis revealed that compound 8 d binds to the ATP-binding site in the catalytic domain of PAK4, forming hydrogen bond interactions with the hinge region via the amine linker between the pyrazole motif and the quinazoline ring. This stable interaction network constitutes the structural basis for its potent inhibitory effect [103]. Among the 2,4-diaminopyrimidine derivatives studied by Qiaohua Qin et al., lead compounds A2, B6, and B8 exhibited the highest inhibitory activity against PAK4, with IC50 values of 18.4 nM, 5.9 nM, and 20.4 nM, respectively. Molecular docking results indicated that compound B6 demonstrated the most stable binding to PAK4, suggesting its potential as a candidate PAK4 inhibitor worthy of further evaluation [73].

Chen Zhouhao et al. discovered through high-throughput screening and hit optimization that a series of 1-phenyl-etetrahydroisoquinoline analogues can be used as PAK4 inhibitors. After evaluation, it was found that compounds 12a and SSD-0501 had the best efficacy among them (IC50=0.42 μM). Compound 12a mainly inhibits the invasion of A549 tumour cells by modulating the PAK4/LIMK1/cofilin signalling pathway and shows antitumour efficacy in an A549 tumour xenotransplantation model. Through immunoprecipitation, Compound 12a predominantly interacts with the N- and C-terminal domains of PAK4 [38]. Furthermore, in the development of 1-phenanthryl-tetrahydroisoquinoline analogues, Shuai Song et al. designed and synthesized compound 21a (IC50 = 8.84 μM) based on the structures of (-)-β-hydrastine and noscapine, which exhibited favorable binding affinity.Compound 21a significantly induced the cell cycle in G1/S phase by regulating the PAK4/LIMK1/cofilin signalling pathway and Suppressed the migratory and invasive capacities of MCF-7 cells in a dose-dependent manner. The suppressive effect primarily arises from the presence of the methoxy group at the sixth carbon position. Compound 21a increased the levels of expression of Bax, caspase 3 and caspase 8 by decreasing the phosphorylation of PAK4 and the expression levels of Bcl-2 in MCF-7 cells. The combination of Compound 21a and PAK4 provides a structural basis for the subsequent structure-oriented development of PAK4 inhibitors [38, 88].

Natural product-derived inhibitors represent an important source for the discovery of novel inhibitor scaffolds.Glaucarubinone is a naturally product of steroids. It was originally extracted from the seeds of Cucurbitaceae plants and developed as an antimalarial drug. However, its anticancer effect has been gradually discovered in recent years. The anticancer activity of PAK1 and PAK4 might be facilitated by their anti-PAK activity to some extent. Some studies have shown that PAK1 and PAK4 are related to glaucarubinone. Dannel Yeo et al. studied the inhibitory impact of glaucarubinone either alone or in conjunction with gemcitabine on the growth of pancreatic cancer cells and its mechanism. The results showed that glaucarubinone inhibited the migratory and invasive abilities of PANC-1, MiaPaCa-2 and PAN02 cell lines in a dose-dependent manner. The co-administration of glaucarubinone and gemcitabine decreased the proliferation of pancreatic cancer cells mainly through decreases in PAK1 and PAK4 activity. When glaucarubinone was combined with gemcitabine in vivo, the tumour volume and tumour weight decreased. These findings further confirm that the co-administration of glaucarubinone and gemcitabine is beneficial in the treatment of pancreatic cancer [45, 106]. Studies have demonstrated that the flavonoid natural product kaempferol can efficiently bind to key amino acid residues (including GLU396, LEU398, and ASP458) within the ATP-binding pocket of PAK4, exhibiting higher affinity than some known inhibitors (e.g., KPT-9274). Molecular docking, MM-GBSA binding free energy calculations, and druggability evaluations consistently support its favorable inhibitory activity and developmental potential. These findings underscore the significant value of natural products in expanding the chemical diversity and mechanistic understanding of PAK4 inhibitors, offering new avenues for the discovery of novel inhibitory agents [11].

Allosteric inhibitors

KPT-9274, a potent orally active inhibitor, targets both PAK4 and nicotinamide phosphoribosyl transferase (NAMPT) with IC50 values below 100 and 125 nM, respectively. NAMPT serves as a crucial enzyme responsible for the conversion of nicotinamide to nicotinamide monophosphate. This enzyme generates NAD+ through a NAMPT-dependent rescue pathway [40, 61, 63, 68]. NAD plays a pivotal role in numerous cellular processes, encompassing DNA repair and cell signaling, all of which hold significant importance in the context of cancer. It’s noteworthy that PAK4 exhibits elevated expression levels in both breast cancer cells and primary breast tumors [14, 57]. KPT-9274 can partly reduce the growth and survival of triple-negative breast cancer cell lines in vitro and the level of PAK4 homeostasis proteins in cells. Moreover, KPT-9274 in vivo inhibited the tumorigenicity of breast cancer in three independent mouse xenograft models [75]. In the study of multiple myeloma, PAK4 promotes the growth of myeloma cells in laboratory experiments or in animal models by activating antiapoptotic and survival signalling pathways (including MEK/ERK). KPT-9274 effectively inhibits the proliferation and viability of myeloma cells and is mediated by regulation of the MEK/ERK pathway. The suppressive impact of KPT-9274 on ERK is related to the reduced DNA-binding capability of ERK-mediated transcription factors (AP1, ETS, CREB, EGR) and reduced the expression level of ERK target genes (CCND2, CCR1, and MYC) [32]. Previous studies have shown that NAMPT overexpression or a dependent increase can be observed in several cancers, including AML [60, 94, 96]. KPT-9274 (a dual targeting PAK4/NAMPT) significantly inhibits the proliferation of AML cell lines, and primary AML cells, as well as induces cell cycle arrest and apoptosis. KPT-9274 improves Long-term survival and inhibits the advancement of the disease in xenograft mouse models of AML. The mechanism may be that KPT-9274 reduces a decrease in NAD+ levels, resulting in compromised mitochondrial and nonmitochondrial respiration [63]. Previous studies have found that KPT-9274 reduces the survival, invasive potential, and migratory capacity of RCC cell lines in renal cancer, which may work by weakening the PAK4/β-catenin pathway and causing NAD exhaustion. KPT-9274 inhibits G2-M transport and induces apoptosis in renal cancer cells [6]. The clinical trial investigating KPT-9274 for advanced solid tumors and non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma (NCT02702492) has been terminated. However, recent reports indicate that two additional clinical trials are currently ongoing: one focusing on solid tumors and non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma (NCT04281420), and another evaluating its use in relapsed or refractory acute myeloid leukemia (NCT04914845). As of the latest updates, both trials remain active and are currently recruiting participants. The results from these clinical studies will provide a more accurate assessment of the clinical applicability and therapeutic potential of KPT-9274 [64].

A recent research reported that a novel allosteric PAK4 inhibitor, Compound 13, was discovered through a collaboration between the research teams of Ao Zhang and Hua Xie. This compound features a unique tetrahydrobenzofuro[2,3-c]pyridine tricyclic scaffold and directly targets the non-ATP-binding site of the PAK4 kinase domain. By inducing conformational changes in the protein, it indirectly inhibits PAK4 kinase activity, effectively blocking key oncogenic signaling pathways such as Wnt/β-catenin. Consequently, it suppresses the proliferation, migration, and invasion capabilities of pancreatic cancer cells and induces apoptosis [52].

MiRNA inhibitors

Numerous investigations have highlighted the significance of microRNAs in tumor biology, exerting control over the expression of target mRNAs to promote tumour growth, invasion, angiogenesis, and immune evasion [41, 90]. The inhibitory impact of miR-145 on human colon cancer cells was achieved in part through the dysregulation of β-catenin nuclear translocation and the downregulation of its transcription targets, namely c-Myc and CyclinD1 [104, 107]. Studies have found that miR-126 is a plausible tumour suppressor and has the capacity to reduce the expression of PAK4 in SKOV3 ovarian cancer cells [58, 86]. Through the miR-target prediction program, it was observed that miR-199a-3p and p21 activated kinase 4 (PAK4) have a stronger affinity. Downregulation of PAK4 can affect the transcription of CyclinD1, which reduces the proliferation of oesophageal cancer cells [71, 84]. Moreover, studies have shown reduced expression of miR-199a/b-3p is associated with poor prognosis in breast cancer patients. MiR-199a/b-3p directly targets and suppresses PAK4 expression, thereby inhibiting the activity of the downstream MEK/ERK signaling pathway. This effectively restrains the proliferation, migration, and invasion of breast cancer cells, ultimately attenuating tumor progression [51]. MiR-199a/b-3p can also inhibit gastric cancer by downregulating the PAK4/MEK/ERK signalling pathway. In a laboratory setting, miR-199a/b-3p demonstrates the capability to impede the growth of two gastric cancer cell lines, namely MGC-803 and SGC-7901, and reduce the expression level of PAK4 [109]. In addition, studies have found that miR-199a/b-3p has the potential to suppress the proliferation of hepatocellular carcinoma cells, induce apoptosis, and inhibit cell cycle progression [77]. In the TG221 transgenic mouse model, miR-199a-3p caused a significant decrease in both the quantity and dimensions of tumor nodules. miR-199a-3p reduces the function of MTOR and PAK4, reduces the phosphorylation and deterioration of the tumour suppressor protein FOXO3, and finally suppresses the carcinogenic FOXM1 transcription factor [21, 25, 44].

PAK4 inhibitors combined with other therapies

In the management of gastric cancer, cisplatin and cisplatin combined chemotherapy have been proven to be effective. However, due to the emergence of resistance to drugs, the efficiency of cisplatin (CDDP) is limited. PAK4 is overexpressed in gastric cancer cells, especially in metastatic gastric cancer [8]. Xueqiong [31]. initially established two gastric cancer cell lines, AGS/CDDP and MKN-45/CDDP, and found that PAK4 might play an important role in CDDP resistance. Further studies demonstrated that PAK4 induced CDDP resistance in gastric cancer cells mainly by activating the PI3K/Akt and MEK/ERK pathways. The combination of LY294002 (PI3K/Akt pathway inhibitor) or PD98509 (MEK/ERK pathway inhibitor) with PF-3758309 (PAK4 inhibitor) improved the efficacy of CDDP compared with LY294002 or PD98509 alone. Hence, targeting PAK4 could be a viable strategy for adjunctive chemotherapy in gastric cancer [10, 31, 70, 85].

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) is a fatal malignant tumour. In early studies, PAK4 amplification was found in patients with PDAC, and PAK4 gene amplification was associated with cell migration, cellar adhesion and nonanchoring appreciation in PDAC [47, 59]. Studies have reported that the PAK4 allosteric inhibitor KPT-9274 induces apoptosis and enhances the efficacy of gemcitabine in PDAC cells by inhibiting the Wnt/β-catenin, MAPK/ERK, and PI3K/AKT signaling pathways. PAK4 represents a critical therapeutic target for overcoming gemcitabine resistance in PDAC [81]. Moreover, Amro Aboukameel et al. evaluated whether a PAK4 allosteric modulator (KPT-9274) had a synergistic effect using gemcitabine and oxaliplatin in two PDAC cell lines in vitro. The results showed that KPT-9274 combined with gemcitabine or oxaliplatin could significantly enhance apoptosis and inhibit proliferation. The combination of KPT-9274 and gemcitabine enhanced the antitumour activity in an animal tumour model, which was consistent based on the in vitro findings. Overall, the compositeness of KPT-9274 with gemcitabine and oxaliplatin provides additional preclinical and clinical assessment for addressing drug-resistant PDAC [3]. PAK4 plays a role in promoting tumor development in KRAS mutation-driven cancers. KRASG12C inhibitors such as sotorasib and adagasib offer assistance in the treatment of patients with KRASG12C-mutated tumors, but these drugs often lead to drug resistance when used as monotherapy. This study found that KPT9274, when used in combination with sotorasib and adagasib, can effectively inhibit the proliferation, apoptosis and cell cycle arrest of pancreatic cancer and non-small cell lung cancer cells with KRASG12C mutations [46]. For patients with advanced pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors (pNETs), the mTOR inhibitor everolimus serves as a standard therapeutic agent. However, the vast majority of patients eventually develop acquired resistance. Dual inhibition of PAK4 and NAMPT by KPT-9274 significantly reduces intracellular NAD⁺ levels and suppresses aberrant activation of the mTOR pathway, thereby reversing everolimus resistance in pNETs and enhancing therapeutic efficacy [66]. The team of Christina M. Annunziata proposed a novel therapeutic strategy for platinum-resistant ovarian cancer. Utilizing a more physiologically relevant 3D multicellular spheroid model that closely mimics in vivo tumor characteristics, the study demonstrated that dual inhibition of NAMPT and PAK4 suppresses spheroid growth and induces cell death in the 3D spheroid system, exhibiting synergistic and significant antitumor effects [49].

Studies have reported that inhibition of PAK4 can remodel the tumor microenvironment and enhance sensitivity to PD-1 blockade immunotherapy [5]. In melanoma, PAK4 inhibition downregulates the NF-κB/STAT3 pathway, reduces the recruitment of immunosuppressive cells, and promotes the migration and infiltration of CD8⁺ effector T cells into tumors, thereby significantly increasing tumor sensitivity to immune checkpoint blockade [4].

Concluding remarks

In the past few decades, a lot of research has been devoted to exploring the role of PAK4 in a wide range of diseases, with recent research focusing on cancer. While PAK4 exhibits elevated expression in embryos, its presence is generally subdued in normal adult tissues. Nevertheless, it experiences heightened expression in various tumor cell lines, indicating a potential significant role for PAK4 in cancer. PAK4 is a pluripotent kinase that can regulate amounts of biological processes from cell movement to survival. Therefore, PAK4 small molecule inhibitors have attracted great interest as a therapeutic strategy, and researchers have been working hard to find specific PAK4 small molecule inhibitors. Currently, several small-molecule inhibitors targeting PAK4 have entered Phase I clinical trials; however, some of these trials have been terminated due to low bioavailability or poor oral bioavailability of the inhibitors. Although antitumour drugs targeting PAK4 kinase have not yet been marketed, an increasing number of new and specific PAK4 inhibitors are being developed. These scientific research results will further promote the rapid development of new target anticancer drugs.

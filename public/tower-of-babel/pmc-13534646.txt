Anisotropy-mediated stress regulation in Mn-substituted VOPO4 enables aqueous zinc batteries with long cycle life
Authors: Zhenjie Yao, Wenyao Zhang, Xinyu Chen, Junjie Cui, Boyuan Zhu, Jiahao Tang, Yangrui Hou, Jiarong Chen, Li Tang, Jianrong Zeng, Hongbing Jia, Markus Antonietti, Junwu Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The structural integrity of layered VOPO4 frameworks critically determines their electrochemical performance in aqueous zinc-ion batteries, yet the highly ordered stacking of VOPO4 renders it susceptible to lattice stress accumulation and chemo-mechanical degradation during cycling, causing rapid capacity fading and a shortened battery lifespan. Herein, we show a strategy to alleviate these limitations through partial metal substitution in the VOPO4 lattice, enabling the modulation of local coordination environments and mitigating lattice and cycling induced stress. As a proof-of-concept, theoretical calculations and experimental validation on Mn-substituted VOPO4 reveal shortened M–O bond along the c-axis and the formation of Mn–O–P–O–V delocalized structure. These modifications introduce subtle lattice distortions and generate percolation channels that enable more facile, ordered Zn2+ migration, facilitating uniform lattice stress distribution. Real-time monitoring of interfacial stress during cycling identifies that Mn incorporation induces electronic redistribution and lattice anisotropy, promoting balanced intra-layer (a/b-axis) stress accommodation. Therefore, Mn-substituted VOPO4 exhibits higher specific capacity and cycling stability, with 91.0% capacity retention over 2000 cycles at 1 A g−1. This intra-layer anisotropy engineering strategy offers a practical route for mitigating dissolution and strain effects during cycling, paving ways for the development of high-energy-density positive electrodes with cycling durability.

Introduction

The rapid integration of renewable energy sources and increasing demand for grid-scale energy storage has propelled aqueous zinc ion batteries (AZIBs) to the forefront of next-generation electrochemical technology, owing to their high capacity, inherent safety and cost effectiveness. However, AZIBs are currently constrained by the limited availability of high-energy-density and durable positive electrode materials. In this context, polyanionic vanadium oxides (VOPO4) have emerged as candidates due to their strong P − O covalent bond induction effect, which attracts the electrons adjacent to the V − O bonds, polarizes the V − O bonds, and reduces the electron density in the Vd orbitals, enabling elevated discharge plateaus (≈ 1.1 − 1.8 V vs Zn2+/Zn). Furthermore, VOPO4 offers a high theoretical capacity (> 300 mAh g−1) and a distinct layered structure conductive to ion transport. Nevertheless, the intra-layer lattice of VOPO4 has an alternating connection of VO6 octahedra and PO4 tetrahedra, forming a rigid, highly ordered two-dimensional network with strong interlayer coupling, resulting in a high-order stacking sequence. During Zn2+ insertion and extraction, lattice strain is readily induced, leading to dislocation formation and localized stress accumulation, thereby accelerating structural degradation. During the electrochemical cycling, particularly Zn2+extraction, anisotropic lattice strain inevitably arises, inducing crystal fatigue and resulting in substantial capacity fading and limited cycle life1. This degradation can be attributed to several mechanisms. First, the extraction of Zn2+ induces contraction of the VO6 octahedra, reducing the a/b lattice parameter of VOPO42. Subsequent migration of vanadium ions and loss of polyanionic PO43− groups may ultimately lead to the irreversible transformation into VOx phases, compromising voltage and cycling stability. Second, strong electrostatic interactions of Zn2+ and VOPO4 during Zn2+ insertion impede ion diffusion kinetics and reduce active material utilization, culminating in structural collapse and diminished specific capacity3. These anisotropic lattice displacements give rise to accumulated strain to irreversible mechanical fatigue, which regarded as the primary cause of degradation in layered oxide positive electrodes.

To address these challenges, a variety of strategies have been pursued, including pre-embedding in conductive matrices (e.g., graphene4, polypyrrole5), defect engineering (e.g., oxygen vacancies6), or electrolyte optimization (e.g., ZnSO4/graphitic carbon nitride quantum dots7, ZnCl2/H3PO48). The intercalated species within the VOPO4 layers can interact with insert Zn2+ ions, thereby weakening their binding affinity to the host lattice, improving electronic conductivity and facilitating Zn2+ transport by expanding interlayer spacing and providing additional ion diffusion pathways. Introducing structural defects diminish electrostatic interactions between Zn2+ and the VOPO4 framework, enhancing ion diffusion kinetics. In parallel, the incorporation of PO43− additives and the use of high concentration electrolytes suppress dissolution by minimizing ion concentration gradients and reducing diffusion coefficients that comply with Fick’s law. Additionally, lowering water activity through hydroxyl-rich additives, capable of integrating into the hydrogen bonding network, effectively mitigates phosphate leaching then dissolution. Although these approaches have alleviated material dissolution and led to incremental improvements in electrochemical performance, the intrinsic lattice evolution of VOPO4 during cycling remains a fundamental challenge. Consequently, the development of integrated strategies that combine intrinsic structural stabilization with modulation of lattice and cycling induced stress is urgently needed to enhance the electrochemical resilience of VOPO4 based positive electrodes.

In this work, a series of metals were incorporated into the VOPO4 lattice to tune the local coordination environment, among which Mn proved most effective in enhancing lattice strain adaptability under deep charge states. Owing to its higher electronegativity, Mn induces pronounced electron redistribution, regulates the anisotropy of both the c-axis and a/b-plane lattices, and modifies lattice distortion and ion migration pathways. Simultaneously, the M–O bond length along the c-axis shortens, weakening interlayer coupling and enabling interlayer slippage, thereby regulating crystal periodicity and dispersing localized stress over a broader area to achieve effective stress relaxation. Spectroscopic analyses reveal the formation of a stable Mn−O − P − O − V delocalized electronic structure. The delocalization configuration suppresses spontaneous migration of transition metal ions during Zn2+ extraction, mitigates VOPO4 dissolution and decomposition and preserving lattice integrity. Real-time monitoring of the interfacial stress under Zn2+ insertion and extraction process indicates Mn-induced electronic redistribution and lattice anisotropy promotes balanced intra-layer (a/b-axis) stress accommodation. Simultaneously, the stabilized lattice framework and interconnected percolation channels facilitate directional Zn2+ migration, suppress volume fluctuations, and minimize residual strain (σ‘residual = 0.46 MPa). As a result, the Mn-substituted VOPO4 demonstrates a high Zn2+ storage specific capacity of 258 mAh g−1 and a markedly enhanced cycling stability, retaining 91.0% of its capacity after 2000 cycles. X-ray 3D nano-computed tomography (nano-CT) analysis shows a slight increase in porosity after cycling (from 20.5% to 21.9%), corroborating the role of intralayer anisotropy tuning in constructing stabilized delocalized structures and achieving homogeneous stress distribution, both essential for mechanical stability and dissolution suppression. This work demonstrates that partial metal ion substitution is a viable strategy for enhancing the reversibility and mechanical stability of VOPO4.

Results and discussion

Structural evolution upon metal incorporation: lattice distortion and stress redistribution

During the charge/discharge process of VOPO4 in AZIBs, a sequential phase transformation from VOPO4 → Zn0.5VOPO4 → ZnVOPO4 occurs. Analysis of the voltage profiles (Fig. 1a) reveals a continuous decline in the discharge/charge voltage plateaus from the 2nd to the 200th cycle. This trend is more distinctly reflected in the corresponding differential capacity (dQ/dV) curves (Fig. 1b), which display four pairs of redox peaks. The low voltage plateaus associated with VOx redox couples (0.73/0.58 V and 0.97/0.89 V, corresponding to the V3+/V4+ and V4+/V5+ transitions, respectively) gradually intensify with cycling, while the high voltage plateaus induced by the PO43− polyanion (1.28/1.07 V and 1.61/1.55 V) progressively weaken and nearly vanish by the 100th cycle. As a result, the discharge capacity retention after 200 cycles is only 73% of the initial value, indicating substantial voltage and capacity decay. Further evidence from V 2p X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) reveals a downshift in both V 2p1/2 and V 2p3/2 binding energies, accompanied by an increased proportion of V4+ oxidation state over cycling, which originates from the loss of the PO43− groups, weakening the inductive effect and lowering overall binding energy (Fig. 1c). Consistently, the P component detected by XPS decreases markedly from 11.57 to 1.48 at.% (Fig. 1d and Supplementaty Table S1), indicating substantial PO43− dissolution. To mitigate the structural decomposition of VOPO4, a series of cation-substituted VOPO4 materials (M = Mn, Fe, Co, Nb, Mo, Ta, and W) were synthesized via an oil bath reflux method7, all of which retained the typical VOPO4 rectangular plate like morphology (length ≈ 17 μm, width ≈ 14 μm) (Supplementary Fig. S1a). Under identical reaction conditions, replacing V2O5 with equimolar Mn salts yielded MnPO4, composed of alternating MnO6 octahedra and PO4 tetrahedra. Its X-ray diffraction (XRD) pattern (Supplementary Fig. S2) matches perfectly with PDF#78-1082, confirming successful low temperature substitution of transition metals into the VOPO4 lattice. Notably, Mn-substituted VOPO4 processes a distinct helically stacked rosette morphology (Supplementary Fig. S1b, c), attributed to the similar coordination chemistry between Mn and V, which enables Mn to substitute V more effectively than the other larger metal ions in the series. Mn, with its higher electronegativity (1.91) compared to V (1.79), leads to a shortened c-axis M − O bond, thereby reducing interlayer electrostatic interactions in the two-dimensional (2D) lattice and facilitating stress relaxation through sliding of the layers9–11. Moreover, Mn substitution promotes intra-layer electron transfer along the a/b-axes, enhancing lattice polarization and strengthening interatomic interactions, which collectively stabilize the helical dislocation stacking structure12. Although Fe and Co possess comparable ionic radii to V, their lower electronegativities (Fe: 1.65, Co: 1.69) elongate the c-axis M − O bonds, preventing the discussed effects and leading to the undermine of structural integrity and morphological fragmentation (Supplementary Fig. S1d, e). While Nb, Mo, Ta, and W are highly electronegative (Nb: 1.86, Mo: 2.10, Ta: 1.92, W: 2.18), their strong d-orbital electrons localization inhibits efficient intra-layer electron transport along a/b-axes, preserving the nanosheet morphology of pristine VOPO4 but yielding structures with weak interlayer cohesion (Supplementary Fig. S1f–i). This localized electronic structure results in unstable, low-density morphologies that are suboptimal for electrochemical applications.

XRD analysis reveals that Mn substitution leads to a downshift in the (001) diffraction peak of VOPO4, accompanied by an increase in the interlayer spacing from 0.747 nm to 0.751 nm (Supplementary Fig. S3). This shift derives from the weakened interlayer interactions induced by Mn incorporation, resulting in an c-axis lattice expansion. In contrast, the larger ionic radii of Fe and Co (Fe: 0.63 Å, Co: 0.69 Å, V: 0.59 Å) strengthens interlayer interactions and causes c-axis lattice contraction. Similarly, incorporation of Nb, Mo, Ta, and W (Nb: 0.69 Å, Mo: 0.62 Å, Ta: 0.68 Å, W: 0.62 Å) also results in lattice contraction due to increased electron localization, which strengthens M − O bonding and interlayer coupling. Therefore, Mn was chosen as the metal for cation substitution to demonstrate its effectiveness in modulating the microstructure of VOPO4. To optimize substitution levels, VOPO4 materials with varying Mn contents (1%, 2% and 5% molar ratios, denoted as 1% Mn-VOPO4, 2% Mn-VOPO4, and 5% Mn-VOPO4) were prepared and systematically evaluated.

The anisotropy of VOPO4 increases progressively with Mn substitution, with 2% Mn substitution exhibiting a well-developed helical rosette structure. In contrast, low Mn concentrations fail to induce sufficient lattice distortion, while excessive Mn led to structural fragmentation (Supplementary Fig. S4). As the Mn content increases, the diffraction peak corresponding to the (001) c-axis crystal plane of VOPO4 gradually shifts to lower angles (JCPDS: 36-1472), and the interlayer spacing expands to 0.757 nm (Supplementary Fig. S5)13–15. Neutron diffraction spectra (Supplementary Fig. S6) revealed concomitant contraction along the a/b planes, the d200 spacing decreased from 3.13 to 3.11 nm and d220 from 2.23 to 2.20 nm, evidencing anisotropic lattice tuning along the c- and a/b-axes by Mn substitution. Electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) spectra reveal a g-factor of 2.008, indicative of oxygen vacancies typically associated with redox-active transition metals. The concentration of oxygen vacancies decreases with increasing Mn content (Supplementary Fig. S7)6. Among the evaluated compositions, the sample with 2% Mn substitution presents the most coherent helical stacking and the lowest oxygen vacancy concentration, thus 2% Mn-VOPO4 was selected as the focus material for further study.

High-angle annular dark field scanning transmission electron microscopy (HAADF-STEM) images confirm the uniform distribution of V, O, P, and Mn elements in the 2% Mn-VOPO4, with no detectable impurities (Supplementary Fig. S8). High-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM) reveals that pristine VOPO4 nanosheets exhibit well-ordered lattice fringes with a crystallographic spacing of 0.32 nm, corresponding to the (200) crystal plane (Fig. 1e and Supplementary Fig. S9a). Notably, 2% Mn-VOPO4 displays a wavy lattice structure with an expanded spacing of 0.37 nm, attributed to partial substitution of V by Mn atoms (Fig. 1f and Supplementary Fig. S9b). Brunauer Emmett Teller (BET) (Supplementary Fig. S10) analysis showed that 2% Mn-VOPO4 possesses a significantly larger surface area than pristine VOPO4 (0.89 m2 g−1), providing enhanced ion transport pathways and more Zn2+ storage sites. This lattice expansion in principle would give more space for ion transport and facilitate Zn2+ deposition. Inverse Fourier transform (IFFT) images further illustrate the ordered lattice fringes of VOPO4 and the pronounced wavy lattice distortion following Mn substitution (Fig. 1g, h). This distortion is ascribed to electron redistribution induced by the higher electronegativity of Mn, which enhances the intra-layer anisotropy and distorts the lattice. Consequently, the modified lattice texture and connectivity of Zn2+ migration channels form in this sample a percolated network that mitigates the detrimental effects of ion migration on the material’s integrity16. Simulated diffraction halo from the (001) selected area electron diffraction (SAED) patterns confirm that the crystalline surface of VOPO4 remains consistent before and after Mn substitution (Fig. 1i and Supplementary Fig. S11)17. The sharp, cubic-arranged diffraction spots verify that Mn effectively replaces the V sites while preserving the single-crystalline nature of the tetragonal phase. Furthermore, Mn-induced electron redistribution generates intra-facial anisotropy, altering the crystal face pitch angle and leading to a lattice twisted helical stacking morphology (Fig. 1j and Supplementary Fig. S12). These lattice modifications offer an additional mechanism for mechanical stress distribution. Geometric phase analysis (GPA) reveals that the pristine VOPO4 nanosheets experience localized stress concentration (Fig. 1h). Atomic force microscopy (AFM) further demonstrates that the stress distribution in VOPO4 nanosheets is uneven, with higher stress at the center and lower stress at the edges (Fig. 1k, m, n and Supplementary Fig. S13). This uneven structural stress distribution originates from the ordered atomic lattice within VOPO4, where the central region is subjected to multiple constraints, leading to stress concentration and inhomogeneity, rendering the nanosheets to be more prone to rupture and dissolution. In contrast, 2% Mn-VOPO4 exhibits significantly alleviated stress accumulation with a more uniform stress distribution (Fig. 1l, o, p). The Mn-induced anisotropic lattice adjustment generates lattice distortions and helical dislocations, modulating the crystal periodicity, generating natural strain fields that distribute stress across a larger area, effectively mitigating stress accumulation and crack formation, and thereby enhancing the structural stability of the materials18–20.

Electronic delocalization structure: strengthened domination of π-symmetric electrons

To elucidate the Mn-induced electronic delocalized structure, Raman analysis (Fig. 2a) was performed. The characteristic bands located at approximately 540, 575, 950, and 1037 cm−1 correspond to δ(O − P − O), υ(Mn−O), υ(O − P), and υ(V = O) vibrational modes, respectively6,21. The formation of Mn−O bonds upon Mn incorporation is particularly evident in the 560–590 cm−1 region (Supplementary Fig. S14)22,23. Due to the asymmetry of the VO6 octahedra, the V = O bonding stretching modes in the 1020–1060 cm−1 exhibit an upshift, indicative of lattice distortion (Fig. 2b). The electronic delocalization and lattice bonding environment were further examined using X-ray absorption near-edge structure (XANES)13. The V L-edge XANES (Fig. 2d) reveals that incorporating Mn in 2% Mn-VOPO4 shifts the V absorption to higher energies at both the L3- and L2-edges and decreases the L3/L2 intensity ratio from 1.054 to 1.038, suggesting an increase in the V5+ oxidation state21. The V K-edge XANES (Fig. 2e) presents a reduced leading-edge peak, reflecting a more symmetric octahedral structure at the V-site after Mn substitution28, consistent with the Raman results. In addition, the slightly shift of the absorption edge towards higher energies further suggests electron depletion and an increased average V valence state in 2% Mn-VOPO4, which aligns with the XPS and L-edge XANES findings. Comparative analysis of Fourier transforms extended X-ray absorption fine structure (FT-EXAFS) of the V K-edge k3 χ(k) function (Fig. 2f and Supplementary Fig. S16) and the pair distribution function (PDF) (Supplementary Fig.S17) proclaim similar structural evolution. The V − O distance in the first shell decreased from 1.68 Å to 1.56 Å, and the V − P distance in the second shell shortens from 2.65 Å to 2.50 Å upon Mn incorporation. These changes imply that Mn modulates the local structure and chemical environment of the V centers, shortened the V − O and V − P bonds, generates localized Coulombic forces that induce helical dislocations and lattice distortions, thereby enhancing structural stability25. The Mn K-edge XANES spectra (Fig. 2g and Supplementary Fig. S18) indicate that the absorption edge of 2% Mn-VOPO4 lie between those of MnO2 and metallic Mn, confirming that Mn exists in a higher oxidation state. The corresponding FT-EXAFS of the Mn K-edge (Fig. 2h) reveals a Mn−O bond length of 1.43 Å, validating the successful replacement of Mn for V within the lattice. The average oxidation state of Mn, determined from XANES fitting, is calculated to be + 3.6 (Fig. 2i), close to the + 4 oxidation state in MnO2. Furthermore, the wavelet transform (WT) analysis of 2% Mn-VOPO4 (Supplementary Figs. S19, S20) discloses two distinguishable intensities associated with V − O and V − P coordination environments, in agreement with the FT-EXAFS results29. Radial distribution function (RDF) calculations (Supplementary Fig. S21) further corroborate the atomic distances obtained from EXAFS and WT analyses. In this structural motif, Mn3+, with an electronegativity close to 1.91, significantly higher than that of V4+ (1.79), exerts a stronger domination of π-symmetric electrons31. In the case of V4+, unpaired electrons occupying π-symmetric t2g orbitals are transferred to the bridging O2− via π-donation. The phosphate group (PO43−) adopts a resonance structure with delocalized electrons, forming four equivalent P − O bonds. Meanwhile, the π-symmetric eg orbitals of Mn3+ remain partially unoccupied and accept electrons through resonance mediated interactions with the phosphate framework. The formation of this extended delocalized Mn−O − P − O − V network facilitates rapid electron transfer, alters the M − O bonds, and induces helical dislocations and lattice anisotropy, which contribute to lattice distortion, stress dispersion alongside mechanical stability improvement. Moreover, Mn incorporation not only strengthens the binding between metal ions and oxygen but also enhances the conjugation with phosphate units, forming a stable Mn−O − P − O − V delocalized structure that may improve the cycling stability of 2% Mn-VOPO4 during repeated charge/discharge processes.

Charge reconfiguration: optimizing Zn2+ diffusion and dynamic bond stability

Density functional theory (DFT) calculations were conducted to understand the variations in charge and bond lengths. The calculated charge density differences (Fig. 3a, b and Supplementary Fig. S23) verify substantial charge redistribution and delocalized electron transfer between V and Mn atoms, with a pronounced reduction in charge accumulation around Mn. Bader’s charge analysis further indicates that the charge on Mn atoms (– 2.12 e) is less negative than the one on V atoms (– 2.30 e), giving another evident of the electron transfer from V to Mn, consistent with EXAFS and XPS findings32. Density of states (DOS) and partial density of states (PDOS) analyses (Fig. 3c) clarify that Mn incorporation promotes electronic reconfiguration, narrowing the energy band gap by 0.51 eV compared to pristine VOPO4, thereby accelerating charge carrier migration. Charge migration induced by Mn led to a shortening of the c-axis M–O bond from 2.06 Å to 1.64 Å thereby weakening the interaction between the apical oxygen and Zn2+ (Fig. 3d, e and Supplementary Fig. S24). Simultaneously, the a/b-axis M–O bond contracted slightly from 1.90 Å to 1.89 Å, strengthening the binding interaction with the PO43− group. Climbing Image Driven Elastic Bands (CI-NEB) simulations were then carried out to examine the Zn2+ diffusion kinetics (Fig. 3f and Supplementary Fig. S25, S26), which manifests that dense covalent bonding and increased electron density within the anionic substructure effectively reduce the Zn2+ diffusion energy barrier from 1.03 eV to 0.65 eV, significantly improving ion diffusion kinetics. To further explore the Zn2+ storage mechanism, in situ Raman spectroscopy was employed to monitor chemical bonding dynamics throughout the electrochemical cycling process. During Zn2+ insertion, the electrostatic interactions between Zn2+ and the apical O atoms of VO6 units induce tensile stress within the lattice, weakening the P − O and V = O bonds, which results in a downshift in Raman bands. Upon Zn2+ extraction, the releases of electrostatic force results in a corresponding upshift of the P − O and V = O bands33–36. Since the instability of the lattice structure of VOPO4, excessive tensile strain presents a situation where the P − O bonds cannot fully recover their initial positions, predisposing the material to irreversible dissolution of PO43− (Fig. 3g and Supplementary Fig. S27). In contrast, a stable Mn−O − P − O − V delocalized electronic structure in 2% Mn-VOPO4 effectively buffers the electrostatic stress originated from intercalated Zn2+ since a reduced c-axis M − O bond, thereby enabling the lattice to recover its initial state, achieving full reversible Zn2+ storage (Fig. 3h and Supplementary Fig. S28). In situ XRD was conducted to evaluate the structural reversibility and mechanical stability provided by this Mn-VOPO4 structure. Repeated deep insertion and extraction of Zn2+ cause severe lattice expansion and contraction, which is typically the cause of crack formation and structural degradation37–39. During discharge, the (001) and (220) diffraction peaks of both VOPO4 and 2% Mn-VOPO4 gradually shift to higher 2θ values, considering the c-axis and a/b-axis lattice contraction induced by electrostatic attraction with intercalated Zn2+40. Conversely, during charging, Zn2+ extraction increases the electrostatic repulsion of interlayer oxygen, leading to c-axis and a/b-axis expansion. However, pristine VOPO4 exhibits irreversible structural deformation: the c-axis and a/b-axis spacing continuously decrease (from 0.665 nm to 0.663 nm, from 0.233 nm to 0.232 nm) after complete Zn2+ extraction (Fig. 3i, j). This irreversibility is attributed to the V − O bond elongation, partial conversion of VOPO4 into amorphous VOx, and the release of PO43− to the solution, resulting in lattice mismatch and structural collapse. These processes further promote the nucleation and accumulation of inter- and intracrystalline microcracks during cycling20,41. In contrast, Mn modification by Mn−O − P − O − V delocalized structure stabilizes the host lattice by modulating interlayer interactions and shortening the M − O bond along both c-axis and a/b-axis. This enables the reversible recovery of the (001) and (220) crystal planes to their original positions after full Zn2+ extraction (Fig. 3k, l), indicating high structural reversibility. By comparison, ions with similar radii to vanadium (Mn) have a similar coordination structure. During the first charging process (Fig. 4f), Zn2+ is initially extracted in an orderly manner from the edge insertion sites of VOPO4 within the voltage range of 0.50 V − 1.19 V. This stage features unrestricted Zn2+ migration pathways and minor interlayer expansion, where elastic deformation dominates, resulting in a steady increase in strain (σ1). As the voltage increases to 1.53 V, Zn2+ migration enters a diffusion-controlled regime since the internal Zn2+ undergoes an increased ion transfer distance and sluggish kinetics. The intrinsic structural rigidity of VOPO4 causes anisotropic lattice expansion, and in combination with the strong electrostatic interactions between Zn2+ and VO6 units, it gives rise to localized structural rupture, PO43− leaching, or potential phase transitions. These structural disruptions further exacerbate Zn2+ migration disorder, enhance electrolyte infiltration and promote rapid volumetric expansion, collectively intensifying strain accumulation and contributing to a pronounced rinse in strain (σ2)44. Upon increasing the voltage to 1.80 V, the remaining Zn2+ continues to be extracted from VOPO4 crystallites, which approaches a new equilibrium state, and strain (σ3) steadily increases. When the electric field reversed, Zn2+ ions are preferentially reinserted into the interlayer in a controlled manner within the voltage window of 1.80 V to 1.23 V, primarily governed by surface active sites. This process induces an alight contraction of the interlayer spacing. However, the structure does not fully recover its elastic deformation, resulting in residual strain (σ4). As the voltage drops to 0.88 V, saturation of surface sites drives Zn2+ to diffuse into the bulk, which may induce interlayer sliding and local structural contraction along the c-axis limited migration pathways, lead to a sharp reduction in strain (σ5). As the voltage approaching 0.50 V, Zn2+ continues to intercalate, whereas previously inserted Zn2+ forms a physical shielding layer that suppresses further insertion. This coordinated intercalation behavior of Zn2+ a gradual decline in strain (σ6)45.

In the case of 2% Mn-VOPO4, a rapid increasing σ‘2 strain is observed during the first charging process within the voltage range of 0.50 V − 1.43 V (Fig. 4g). This behavior is assigned to the distinct Mn−O − P − O − V delocalized framework and anisotropic lattice induced percolation channel, which accelerates fast and orderly Zn2+ extraction, promotes efficient intra-layer stress dispersion, suppress the structural degradation and enables controlled volumetric expansion. Even under diffusion limited Zn2+ extraction from deep lattice sites at 1.80 V, the σ‘3 strain remains steadily increase since the robust lattice network of Mn-VOPO4. Upon discharging, the reduced c-axis M − O bond and interconnected percolation channel enable low diffusion barriers, achieving efficient Zn2+ reinsertion with minimal interlayer contraction over 1.80 V − 1.30 V and a gradual, smooth evolution in σ‘4 strain. With the voltage further decreasing to 0.50 V, the elastic lattice continues to accommodate Zn2+ intercalation from both surface and bulk regions, leading to further gradual σ‘5 strain reduction.

To elucidate stress evolution across different voltage plateaus during cycling, the differential capacity (dQ/dV) curves and corresponding strain (Δσ) profiles for the first cycle of the Zn | |VOPO4 cell were analyzed in parallel (Fig. 4h). Combined with in situ XRD analysis (Fig. 3i, j), the variations in the (001) and (220) lattice spacings were monitored to quantitatively evaluate the evolution of the a/b- and c-axis parameters, thereby revealing the underlying mechanism of strain generation during charge and discharge. During charging, Zn2+ ions were extracted from the interlayers of VOPO4, disrupting the electrostatic stabilization originally provided by interlayer Zn2+ and leading to structural expansion. Concurrently, vanadium underwent gradual oxidation. In Stage I, V3+ is oxidized to V4+, causing contraction of the VO6 octahedra. Simultaneously, the removal of Zn2+ exerts tensile stress on the apical oxygen atoms, resulting in an almost unchanged a/b-axis spacing (0.233 nm) while the c-axis expands to 0.666 nm. The combined effect of the lattice evolution along the a/b axis and the c axis leads to a steady increase in strain (σ1). At stage II, further oxidation of V4+ to V5+ induced continued VO6 contraction, with the a/b-axis stretching to 0.234 nm and the c-axis to 0.667 nm, producing a moderate and continuous strain increase (σ2). In Stage III, PO43− induced the redox potential of vanadium, accelerating the conversion of residual V3+ to V4+. The a/b-axis remained at 0.234 nm, whereas the c-axis abruptly expanded to 0.672 nm, likely due to local structural adjustments of the PO43− groups, resulting in a drastic change in the c lattice parameters. Meanwhile, slow Zn2+ diffusion combined with the intrinsic rigidity of VOPO4 caused anisotropic lattice expansion and strong Zn2+−O electrostatic interactions, which triggered local collapse, microcrack formation, and partial PO43− dissolution. These structural degradations increased the Zn2+ migration barrier, enhanced electrolyte infiltration, and promoted rapid volume expansion, leading to a sharp strain rise (σ3). In Stage IV, deep Zn extraction occurred with concurrent oxidation of V4+ to V5+ and progressive PO43− dissolution, the transformation of VOPO4 to the amorphous VOx phase. The a/b- and c-axis expanded to 0.235 and 0.674 nm, respectively, though the expansion rate slowed, indicating structural stabilization. Continuous Zn2+ extraction from microcrystalline domains caused a gradual strain increase (σ4). During discharge, Zn2+ reinsertion established electrostatic interactions with the apical oxygens of VO6 units, inducing structural contraction and progressive vanadium reduction. In Stage V, Zn2+ insertion initially occurred at surface sites, where PO43− induced reduction of V5+ to V4+ caused a slight a/b-axis contraction (0.236 → 0.235 nm), while the c-axis remained at 0.675 nm. This is due to the structural defects and microcracks formed at the end of charging, which hinder the initial insertion of Zn2+ at the defect sites, resulting in a temporary accumulation of strain and a slow increase in strain (σ5). In Stage VI, PO43− induced reduction of V4+ to V3+ led to further contraction (a/b = 0.234 nm, c = 0.671 nm). As surface sites became saturated, Zn2+ diffusion into the bulk increased, and the enhanced interlayer electrostatic attraction caused rapid contraction along the c-axis pathway, yielding a sharp strain decrease (σ6). In Stages VII and VIII, sequential reduction of V5+ → V4+ → V3+ further contracted both axes, with the a/b-axis shrinking to 0.232 nm and the c-axis shrinking to 0.663 nm. At this stage, interstitial Zn2+ saturation formed a physical shielding layer that inhibited further contraction. Simultaneously, PO43− dissolution weakened lattice rigidity, and the contraction efficiency of strains (σ7) and (σ8) decreases. After full discharge, neither lattice parameter returned to its original value, due to irreversible phase change, PO43− dissolution, and associated structural fractures, which generated σresidual.

In contrast, the Zn | |2% Mn-VOPO4 battery exhibited a distinct strain profile (Figs. 4i, 3k, l). In Stage I, strain (σ‘1) increased slowly, as the oxidation of V3+ → V4+ contracted the VO6 octahedra, but the stable Mn framework counteracts the octahedral contraction under the tensile effect of the released Zn2+, resulting in an increase in the a/b-axis from 0.235 nm to 0.236 nm. At the same time, the free removal of edge Zn2+ leads to an increase in the c-axis from 0.685 nm to 0.686 nm. This was attributed to the initial lattice expansion induced by Mn substitution, providing more space for ion migration. The Mn−O − P − O − V delocalized network buffered local stress, resulting in mild strain growth. In Stage II, V4+ → V5+ oxidation dominated, causing VO6 contraction and a/b-axis reduction to 0.235 nm. Simultaneously, Mn-induced shortening of the c-axis M–O bonds weakened interlayer coupling, promoting ordered Zn2+ extraction and moderate c-axis expansion to 0.690 nm. This is because the anisotropy induced by Mn causes the lattice of the a/b-axis to contract and the lattice of the c-axis to expand to reach a balance, inhibiting structural degradation and achieving controlled volume expansion, yielding a stable strain increase (σ‘2). In Stage III, Mn mediated PO43− induces the rapid oxidation of V3+ → V4+, slightly increasing the a/b-axis (0.236 nm) and expanding the c-axis to 0.698 nm, resulting in a significant increase in the strain (σ‘3). Despite transient lattice reorganization, the presence of anisotropy induced dislocations and diffusion channels effectively released local stress, preventing microcrack formation. In Stage IV, PO43− assisted oxidation of V4+ → V5+ expanded the a/b- and c-axes to 0.240 and 0.717 nm, respectively. The stable Mn−O − P − O − V delocalized lattice structure, the uniform stress distribution induced by anisotropic lattice, and the infiltration channels guiding the orderly exodus of internal Zn2+ prevent local collapse, resulting in a stable increase in strain (σ‘4). During discharge, Zn2+ reinsertion into the Mn-VOPO4 lattice established electrostatic interactions with VO6, accompanied by gradual V reduction. In Stage V, Zn2+ occupation of surface sites (V5+ → V4+) caused minor contractions (a/b = 0.239 nm, c = 0.710 nm). The shortened c-axis bonds, resilient Mn−O − P − O − V framework, and interconnected percolation channels enabled low barrier Zn2+ insertion and balanced octahedral contraction, keeping σ‘5 nearly constant. In Stage VI, V4+ → V3+ reduction and rapid Zn2+ diffusion through percolation channels caused uniform contraction (a/b = 0.238 nm, c = 0.699 nm) and a sharp strain drop (σ‘6). In Stages VII and VIII, further reduction of V5+ → V4+ → V3+. The stable localized structure formed flow channels, and the uniform stress distribution induced by the anisotropic lattice facilitates the free and orderly embedding of Zn2+ deep within the phase, allowing for the accommodation of more Zn2+, causing the a/b-axis and c-axis lattices to continuously contract and return to their initial positions (0.235 nm, 0.685 nm). The strains (σ‘7) and (σ‘8) continue to decrease, approaching the reversible state. The Mn substitution effectively inhibited PO43− dissolution and suppressed irreversible phase transitions. The shortened c-axis bonds reduced Zn2+−O electrostatic interactions, while the combined effects of dislocations and anisotropic stress distribution promoted stress redistribution and buffering, and realized the highly reversible strain during the Zn2+ storage process. Consequently, the σ‘residual increased to only 0.46 MP after 13 cycles, showing only a minor change and revealing the reversible structural evolution stage of Zn2+ insertion/extraction. These findings confirm that Mn-induced lattice stress regulation effectively harmonizes electrochemical and mechanical processes, enhancing positive electrode durability, mitigating structural degradation, and providing direct in situ evidence for the dynamic coupling between mechanical strain and Zn2+ storage in layered positive electrode materials.

Stable Mn-VOPO4 positive electrode: the strengthening effect on driving Zn2+ storage

The electrochemical properties of the proposed Mn-VOPO4 positive electrode are evaluated in Zn(OTF)2 + triethyl phosphate electrolyte. As shown in Fig. 5a, Supplementary Fig S30, and Supplementary Table S4, substitution with Fe and Co induced c-axis lattice contraction, narrowing Zn2+ migration channels and impeding ion diffusion, leading to rate retentions of 51.4% and 36.3%, respectively. Nb, Mo, Ta, and W are characterized by strongly localized d electrons and hindered charge transfer. Despite preserved morphology, these materials exhibited sluggish kinetics and poor rate performance, with W-VOPO4 retaining only 26.8% of its specific capacity. In contrast, Mn substitution, owing to its high electronegativity, facilitated rapid planar electron transitions, markedly reducing internal charge transfer resistance and delivering fast kinetics (66.3%). Upon returning the current density to 0.1 A g−1, 2% Mn-VOPO4 recovered a discharge specific capacity of 250 mAh g−1, nearly matching the initial value, demonstrating rate reversibility and achieving a high energy density of 325 Wh kg−1. Under long-term cycling at 1.0 A g−1 for 2000 cycles (Fig. 5b, Supplementary Fig. S31, and Supplementary Table S4), VOPO4 substituted with Fe, Co, Nb, Mo, Ta, or W experienced small lattice spacings and high residual stress accumulation. This mechanical strain caused repeated structural fatigue, crack propagation, and particle fragmentation, significantly reducing capacity retention (W-VOPO4 only 14.9%). Although Fe-VOPO4 displayed slightly improved stability, it still exhibited considerable stress (4.53 MPa) and limited rate capability. In contrast, 2% Mn substitution shortened the c-axis M − O bond, inducing controlled lattice distortion, uniform stress distribution, and permeation channels that facilitated efficient Zn2+ transport. Concurrently, local charge compensation formed a Mn−O − P − O − V delocalized structure, suppressing PO43− dissolution and preserving structural integrity, resulting in a high specific capacity of 258 mAh g−1, 91.0% capacity retention, and an energy density of 273 Wh kg−1. Thus, Mn substitution in 2% Mn-VOPO4 introduces controlled intra-layer anisotropy and stabilizes the Mn−O − P − O − V delocalized electronic structure. This reconfiguration promotes lattice distortion mediated permeation channels and uniform stress distribution, accelerating Zn2+ transport kinetics while mitigating cycling-induced degradation. The combination of open ionic channels, fast electron conduction, and structural toughness, enabled by Mn’s electronegativity and compatible ionic radius, coordinates lattice and electronic reconstruction, ensuring stable Zn2+ storage. In contrast, the pristine VOPO4 discloses a relevant low specific capacity of 87.9 mAh g−1 at 1.0 A g−1, while 49.6% capacity retention is sustained primarily due to notorious structural degradation and positive electrode material loss. Notably, excessive Mn incorporation in 5% Mn-VOPO4 decreases the density of electrochemically active sites and disrupts the redox activity of vanadium. Incidental pulverization induces exorbitant lattice distortion and heterogeneous interfacial stress, collectively compromising overall electrochemical performance, yielding only marginal improvement over the pristine VOPO416. To further assess the proximity of the experimental capacity to the theoretical limit, peak deconvolution and integration of the V 2p XPS spectra were conducted for 2% Mn-VOPO4 in the fully charged and fully discharged states after 1000 cycles at 1 A g−1 (Supplementary Fig. S32 and Supplementary Table S5). In the fully charged state, V5+ and V4+ account for 63.78% and 36.22%, respectively, corresponding to an average oxidation state of + 4.6. In the fully discharged state, V3+ (85.67%) and V4+ (14.33%) dominate, yielding an average oxidation state of + 3.1. These results indicate that each vanadium atom participates in the transfer of approximately 1.5 electrons during electrochemical cycling. The high cycling stability of 2% Mn-VOPO₄ originates from Mn-substitution-induced lattice anisotropy and the formation of a stable Mn–O–P–O–V delocalized electronic structure. This framework enables efficient sharing and redistribution of π-symmetric electrons throughout the polyanionic network, significantly enhancing the covalent integrity of the PO43− tetrahedra and strengthening their conjugation with adjacent VOx octahedra. Consequently, the phosphate groups are firmly anchored within the lattice, ensuring that the high-voltage contribution remains dominant in the overall capacity while effectively suppressing PO43− dissolution during repeated Zn2+ extraction/insertion cycles. In addition, the reduced Zn2+ occupancy lowers vacancy defect concentrations, enhances interfacial contact, increases the exchange current density, and further suppresses impedance growth. Also, at a high scan rate of 10 mV s−1 (Supplementary Fig. S41), the b values remain above 1.00/0.89, demonstrating that its electrochemical performance enhancement is not merely a result of increased surface area. This capacitive dominated Zn2+ storage mechanism implies a pseudocapacitive insertion/extraction process with negligible phase transformation or structural degradation. The rate-limiting step in this Zn2+ storage mechanism is elucidated through plotting the relationship between capacity (Q) and scan rate (v) over a range of 0.1 to 100 mV s−1 (Fig. 5f). In Region I (v < 10 mV s−1), the capacity remains largely invariant with respect to scan rate, confirming that capacitive control dominates the kinetics at lower scan rates. Conversely, in Region II (10 < v < 100 mV s−1), the capacity decreases linearly with v−1/2, indicating that the Zn2+ storage process becomes increasingly diffusion-limited at higher scan rates. The linear dependence in this regime reflects a capacity limitation governed by semi-infinite linear diffusion, whereas capacitive contributions remain scan rate independent. The pseudocapacitive dominated nature of the electrochemical process accounts for the fast Zn2+ storage kinetics in 2% Mn-VOPO4. Furthermore, the anodic and cathodic peaks exhibit minimal potential separation (< 0.1 V) at scan rates below 2 mV s−1 (Supplementary Fig. S42), indicative of rapid charge transfer and high-rate capability. These findings underpin the distinguished rate performance and long-term cycling stability of the 2% Mn-VOPO4 positive electrode, desired for efficient energy storage applications. Additional galvanostatic intermittent titration technique is used to further monitor the Zn2+ migration behaviors (Fig. 5g). The Zn2+ diffusion coefficient in 2% Mn-VOPO4 is calculated to be 2.63 × 10−9 cm2 s−1, which is nearly four orders of magnitude higher than that of pristine VOPO4 (9.20 × 10−13 cm2 s−1) (Fig. 5h). This substantial enhancement reaffirms that incorporating Mn facilitates efficient Zn2+ transport, primarily by inducing lattice twisted percolation channels. Such structural tuning migrates the electrostatic interaction between Zn2+ and lattice oxygen, resulting in an enlarged two-dimensional interlayer pathway that enhances both ion accessibility and diffusion kinetics46. These findings demonstrate that moderate Mn incorporation not only optimizes the lattice framework and electronic conductivity but also improves structural reversibility, mechanical stability, and Zn2+ storage/migration efficiency. Comparison of key electrochemical parameters (voltage, capacity, rate performance, and cycle life) of 2% Mn-VOPO4 with leading vanadium-based materials (Supplementary Table S11) shows its significant competitive advantage. While materials such as diethylene glycol VOPO447, V2O5·pyridine48, VO polymers49, V2O4.8550, and Zn2V2O751 exhibit comparable voltages, their inherently fragile lattices and associated stress limit rate and cycling stability. In contrast, 2% Mn-VOPO4 combines a Mn−O − P − O − V delocalized electronic structure with anisotropic stress regulation, delivering high specific capacity, rate capability, and cycle life, achieving an energy density of 325 Wh kg−1 at 0.1 A g−1. This structural and electrochemical advantage underscores the potential of 2% Mn-VOPO4 for grid-scale energy storage applications.

Robust lattice structure and electrode integrity under operating condition

The VOPO4 degradation processes was investigated, and samples with varying Mn content were immersed in 3 M Zn(OTF)2 for 48 hours. Static observations (Supplementary Fig. S43) revealed a color transition of pristine VOPO4 from green to orange, accompanied by yellowing of the solution. As Mn content increases, the samples retain their original color more effectively, and the solution become increasingly clearer. This visual change verifies that Mn incorporation strengthens the structural stability during Zn2+ exposure in the electrolyte. To further gain insight into the structural evolution after electrochemical cycling, post-mortem characterization was conducted on the positive electrode after 500 cycles of Zn2+ insertion/extraction. The VOPO4 holds a two-dimensional morphology with extensive fragmentation (Fig. 6a). High-resolution TEM images demonstrate severe lattice distortion and closure of the percolation channels (Fig. 6b), which hinders Zn2+ intercalation. Elemental mapping detects only trace Zn2+ signals (Supplementary Fig. S44), corroborating the hypothesis that PO43− leaching leads to structural collapse. Non-destructive X-ray nano-computed tomography (X-ray nano-CT) was then employed to in situ visualize the evolution of morphological features in three-dimensional space, and the pore characteristics of the positive electrode were analyzed. Given the varying x-ray attenuation coefficients of different elements, the reconstructed contrast images enable the precise identification of distinct phase species within the positive electrode. After cycling, pristine VOPO4 positive electrode displays prominent red void regions, indicative of progressive structural dissolution and active material loss (Fig. 6e, f). The particle size of VOPO4 is significantly reduced due to pulverization, which arises from cycling induced stress concentration and structural degradation. Quantitative analysis revealed an increase in void ratio from 22.2% to 35.8%, accompanied by a significant reduction in the concentration of active material from 47.8% to 24.1% (Fig. 6g). On the other hand, negligible changes in both void regions and particle sizes after cycling can be noticed in the 2% Mn-VOPO4 (Fig. 6h, i), with only a slight increase in porosity from 20.5% to 21.9% (Fig. 6j), proving again the structural robustness and integrity of the Mn-VOPO4 framework. The practical applicability of the positive electrode was further assessed in pouch cells (Fig. 6l). The pristine Zn | |VOPO4 cell retained only 39.6% of its initial capacity after 500 cycles, whereas the Zn | |2%Mn-VOPO4 cell maintained 94.6% under identical conditions. Increasing the loading to 10 mg cm−2 (Supplementary Fig. S46) exacerbated performance degradation in pristine VOPO4, yielding only 24.4% capacity retention at 1 A g−1 after 500 cycles. In contrast, the Mn−O − P − O − V delocalized structure in 2% Mn-VOPO4 mitigated mechanical stress and suppressed dissolution, preserving 80.5% of capacity, highlighting its promise for practical energy storage applications. Moreover, charge/discharge profiles recorded under bending angles (0°, 45°, 90°, 135°, and 180°) showed negligible variation. Two flexible pouch cells connected in series could stably power a red LED (1.2–2.2 V, Supplementary Fig. S47). These underscore the mechanical flexibility of the Mn-VOPO4 positive electrode, and importantly, provide supplementary proof that its structural features effectively accommodate mechanical deformation, including lattice strain and cycling stress, thereby preserving electrochemical stability under dynamic conditions. Furthermore, a schematic illustration of the degradation pathway and Zn2+ storage mechanisms is proposed (Fig. 6m). The pristine VOPO4 undergoes volume contraction upon Zn2+ insertion, ascribing to the strong electrostatic interaction of Zn2+ and lattice oxygen within VO6. This interaction in turn tend to disrupt the connectivity of VO6 and PO4 units during Zn2+ extraction, leading to the dissolution of PO43− into the electrolyte. While Mn incorporation shortens the c-axis M − O bonds within MO6 octahedra, hence weakening the Zn2+−O electrostatic interactions, and a stable Mn−O − P − O − V network enhances electron delocalization and strengthens the coupling between MO6 and PO4 units. In addition, the subtly lattice distortion introduces efficient percolation pathways and homogenizes lattice stress, which facilitate Zn2+ diffusion kinetics and mitigate cycling induced strain, all in concert suppressing the structural degradation, alleviating the deformation stress, and improving both the electrochemical reversibility and mechanical stability of the material41.

In summary, this study proposes a partial metal substitution strategy to enhance the structural integrity, electrochemical reversibility, and mechanical flexibility of VOPO4 positive electrodes in aqueous zinc ion batteries. As a typical example, the introduction of Mn induces controlled intra-layer anisotropy, leading to the formation of a robust Mn–O–P–O–V delocalized electronic framework. This structural reconfiguration facilitates the development of lattice distorted percolation pathways and homogenized structural stress distribution, thereby accelerating rapid Zn2+ transport kinetics and mitigating cycling-induced degradation. Real-time interfacial stress monitoring reveals improved intra-layer (a/b-axis) stress accommodation and c-axis stress relaxation during Zn2+ insertion and extraction in Mn-VOPO4. Comprehensive electrochemical and morphological analyses, including post-mortem TEM, in situ 3D nano-computed tomography and long-term electrochemical cycling tests, confirm the high performance of the 2% Mn-VOPO4 positive electrode, which retains 91.0% of initial capacity over 2000 cycles and delivers stable output under mechanical deformation while preserving high structural integrity. These findings not only elucidate the fundamental structure–function relationships in layered vanadophosphate systems but also offer a promising pathway toward the rational design of high-performance, flexible zinc ion energy storage devices.

Methods

Synthesis of VOPO4

VOPO4 was synthesized using a typical oil-bath reflux method. Briefly, 4.8 g of V2O5 (AR, 99.5%, Shanghai Aladdin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd.), 26.6 mL of H3PO4 (AR, 85%, Shanghai Lingfeng Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd.), and 115.4 mL of deionized water (resistivity ≥ 18.2 MΩ·cm; TE-S20 laboratory water purification system, Haitai Instrument Co., Ltd.) were added to a round-bottom flask. The mixture was magnetically stirred for 30 min using a magnetic stirrer (84-1 A, Shanghai Sile Instrument Co., Ltd.) to ensure thorough mixing, and was then heated under reflux in an oil bath (DF-101S, Yuhua Instrument Co., Ltd.) at 115 °C for 20 h. The resulting product was washed three times with acetone (AR, 99.5%, Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd.) and dried in a vacuum oven at 60 °C to obtain a rectangular plate-like VOPO4.

Synthesis of Mn-substituted VOPO4 (Mn-VOPO4)

A series of Mn-substituted VOPO4 materials with different Mn contents was synthesized using the same procedure, except that 0.09, 0.23, or 0.49 g of Mn(NO3)2⋅4H2O (CP, 98%, Shanghai Macklin Biochemical Co., Ltd.) was added to the precursor mixture. The resulting products were denoted as 2% Mn-VOPO4, 5% Mn-VOPO4, and 10% Mn-VOPO4, respectively.

Material characterizations

The crystal properties of Mn-VOPO4 were analyzed using X-ray diffraction (XRD, MiniFlex600, Cu Kα radiation, Rigaku Corporation). X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, ESCALAB250Xi, Thermo Fisher Scientific) was employed to analyze the electronic delocalization effects in 2% Mn-VOPO4. Peak calibration was conducted using the standard position of the C 1s peak (284.8 eV). A cold field emission transmission electron microscope (TEM, JEM-F200, 200 kV) was used to examine the morphology of the prepared materials. High-resolution TEM (HRTEM, JEOL JEM-2100F, JEOL Ltd.) was utilized to study the lattice evolution and stress distribution of VOPO4 and 2% Mn-VOPO4. High-angle annular dark-field scanning TEM (HAADF-STEM, 200 kV, 1 eV) was used to explore the uniform distribution of elements in the materials. Electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR, Bruker A300, 298 K, 9.85 GHz) spectroscopy was employed to investigate oxygen vacancies. Measure the BET of materials using the TriStar 3020 micro-analyzer. Study the elemental content in the electrolyte after material recycling using inductively coupled plasma optical emission spectroscopy (ICP-OES). Raman spectroscopy (Raman, Leica Microsystems CMS GmbH, Ernst-Leitz-Str in Via, 250 mW, 532 nm) was used to detect chemical bonds and in-plane strain information in the materials. An atomic force microscope (AFM, Dimension Icon, Bruker Corporation) was used to analyze stress distribution in the nanosheet lattice. Hard X-ray (BL17B1, Shanghai Synchrotron Radiation Facility) was used to test V/Mn K-edge X-ray Absorption Fine Structure (XAFS), and soft X-ray (NSRL, BL12B-α, National Synchrotron Radiation Laboratory, Hefei) was used to test V L-edge X-ray Absorption Near Edge Structure (XANES) to evaluate electronic delocalization structure and coordination bond length. XAS data were analyzed using the Athena software package51. Temperature-dependent magnetization was measured using a SQUID-VSM (H = 1 k Oe) system. The PDF curves for high-energy X-ray (BL13SSW, Shanghai Synchrotron Radiation Facility, Synchrotron Radiation Light Source, CeO2 specimen, λ = 0.248 Å) total scattering were measured. The sample detector distance and detector tilt angle relative to the incident radiation and polarization direction were corrected using the Fit2D software. Pair distribution function and neutron diffraction spectra were then processed using PDFGetX3 software to extract anisotropic atomic displacement parameters and coordination bond lengths. Single-mode fiber Bragg gratings (FBG, AG-1550-H, Shandong Shenghai Optical Fiber Technology Co., Ltd.) were used to investigate the internal stress distribution and release mechanisms within the material lattice.

Electrochemical measurements

The as-prepared VOPO4 and Mn-VOPO4 were used as active materials. The active material, acetylene black (Kejing Zhida Technology Co., Ltd.), and poly(vinylidene fluoride) (PVDF, Kejing Zhida Technology Co., Ltd.) were mixed in N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP; AR, 99.5%, Shanghai Aladdin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd.) at a mass ratio of 7:2:1 to form a homogeneous slurry. The slurry was coated onto stainless-steel (99.5%, Wuhu Maotouying Materials Technology Co., Ltd.) or graphene-foil current collectors (99.9%, thickness: 0.1 mm, Qingdao Dongkai Graphite Co., Ltd.) and dried in a vacuum oven (DZF-6050, Shanghai Yiheng Co., Ltd.) at 60 °C for 12 h. The mass loading of the active material was maintained in the range of 1–2 mg cm⁻2.

The electrochemical performance of VOPO4 and Mn-VOPO4 was evaluated using CR2032 coin cells. The VOPO4 and Mn-VOPO4 electrodes (diameter: 1.2 cm) were used as the positive electrodes, Zn foil (99.9%, thickness: 0.05 mm, diameter: 1.2 cm, Qinghe Shenghang Metal Materials Co., Ltd.) was used as the negative electrode, and a glass-fiber membrane (thickness: 0.29 mm, pore size: 1.63 μm, diameter: 1.9 cm, Ouleji New Materials Co., Ltd.) served as the separator. The electrolyte consisted of 100 μL of 3 M Zn(OTF)2 (AR, 99.5%, Shanghai Aladdin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd.) or 100 μL of 3 M Zn(OTF)2 containing 5% triethyl phosphate (TPE; AR, 99.9%, Bide Pharmatech Co., Ltd.).

For pouch cells, PVDF and graphene foil were used as the binder and current collector, respectively. For single-layer pouch cells (3 × 4 cm2), the mass loading of 2% Mn-VOPO4 was 1 mg cm−2. A 0.05 mm Zn foil, cleaned with water and ethanol, was used as the negative electrode. The electrolyte consisted of 400 μL of 3 M Zn(OTF)2 containing 5% TPE. The applied current was 12 mA, corresponding to an areal current density of 1 mA cm−2. The cell delivered a capacity of 2.5 mAh, corresponding to an areal capacity of 0.21 mAh cm−2. The N/P ratio was 13.5, and the N/P mass loading ratio was 35.2:1. During testing, clamps were applied at the four corners of the rectangular pouch cell with a load of 30 kg to ensure full contact between the positive and negative electrodes and the separator.

Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements were carried out using CR2032 coin cells on an electrochemical workstation (VMP3, Bio-Logic, Wuhan Koster Instrument Co., Ltd.) to investigate the charge-transfer resistance of the Mn-VOPO4 electrode. The measurements were performed at the open-circuit voltage with a perturbation amplitude of 10 mV over a frequency range from 200 kHz to 10 mHz, using automatic frequency scanning from high to low frequency. All impedance spectra were fitted using equivalent circuits in ZView software, and the fitted curves showed good agreement with the experimental data. The fitting errors are listed numerically in Supplementary Tables S8 and S9.

Cyclic voltammetry (CV) measurements were performed using an electrochemical workstation (VMP3, Bio-Logic, Wuhan Koster Instrument Co., Ltd.) over the voltage range of 0.5–1.8 V at scan rates of 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 20, 40, 60, 80, and 100 mV s−1 to investigate the reaction mechanism of the Mn-VOPO4 electrode.

Galvanostatic intermittent titration technique (GITT) measurements were conducted using an electrochemical workstation (LAND-CT3002A, Wuhan LAND Electronics Co., Ltd.) for Mn-VOPO4 at a specific current of 0.05 A g−1. A constant current was applied for 300 s, followed by a rest period of 1800 s, with one data point recorded every 5 s.

Galvanostatic charge/discharge, rate capability, and long-term cycling tests were also carried out using the LAND-CT3002A system. The Coulombic efficiency was calculated as the ratio of discharge capacity to charge capacity.

All the above electrochemical measurements were performed in a constant-temperature chamber at 25 °C.

DFT calculations

First-principles calculations were performed using the Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP) within the spin-polarized framework, employing a plane-wave basis set for density functional theory (DFT) calculations52,53. The exchange-correlation functional was described using the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) functional within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA)54. All calculations employed the DFT + D3 method to account for van der Waals (vdW) interactions. The valence states of all atoms were projected onto a plane-wave basis set with a cutoff energy of 400 eV. A 2 × 2 × 1 k-point grid was used to sample the Brillouin zone. The energy convergence criterion for the self-consistent steps was set at 10−5 eV, and the force threshold for geometric optimization was 0.05 eV/atom55. The optimized coordinates for VOPO4 and 2%Mn-VOPO4 is provided in Supplementary Data 1.

The climbing image nudged elastic band (CI-NEB) method was employed to evaluate the energy barriers along the Zn2⁺ migration pathway56,57. During the calculations, all atoms in the intermediate images were relaxed, while the atomic structures of the reactants and products were kept fixed.

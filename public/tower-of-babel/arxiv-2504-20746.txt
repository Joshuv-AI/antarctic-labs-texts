Trotterization is substantially efficient for low-energy states
Kaoru Mizuta, Tomotaka Kuwahara

Abstract
Trotterization is one of the central approaches for simulating quantum many-body dynamics on quantum computers or tensor networks. In addition to its simple implementation, recent studies have revealed that its error and cost can be reduced if the initial state is closed in the low-energy subspace. However, the improvement by the low-energy property diminishes rapidly as the Trotter order grows in the previous studies, and thus, it is mysterious whether there exists genuine advantage of low-energy initial states. In this Letter, we resolve this problem by proving the optimal error bound and cost of Trotterization for low-energy initial states. For generic local Hamiltonians composed of positive-semidefinite terms, we show that the Trotter error is at most linear in the initial state energy Δ \Delta and polylogarithmic in the system size N N . As a result, the computational cost becomes substantially small for low-energy states with Δ ∈ o ⁡ ( N ​ g ) \Delta\in o(Ng) compared to the one for arbitrary initial states, where g g denotes the energy per site and N ​ g Ng means the whole-system energy. Our error bound and cost of Trotterization achieve the theoretically-best scaling in the initial state energy Δ \Delta . In addition, they can be partially extended to weakly-correlated initial states having low-energy expectation values, which are not necessarily closed in the low-energy subspace. Our results will pave the way for fast and accurate simulation of low-energy states, which are one central targets in condensed matter physics and quantum chemistry.



Introduction.—
Simulating time-evolution of quantum many-body systems, called Hamiltonian simulation, is one of the central fields in quantum computation, whose applications range from condensed matter physics to quantum chemistry.
When simulating the dynamics of a size-
N
Hamiltonian over time
t
within an allowable error
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, the ultimate goal is to construct quantum algorithms having as few computational resources as possible in
N,t
, and
1/\varepsilon
.
To this goal, various quantum algorithms have been developed in the past few decades, such as Trotterization
[
1
]
, linear combination of unitaries (LCU)
[
2
]
, and quantum singular value transform (QSVT)
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
.
Trotterization is one of the most famous approaches among them.
It is feasible in near-term quantum computers owing to its simple circuit structures
[
6
]
. In addition, it is also promising for future large-scale quantum computers since it has better gate complexity in the system size
N
than other algorithms
[
7
,
8
]
.

A central open question is whether Trotterization can provably offer a fundamental advantage when the dynamics begin with some interesting classes of initial states.
In general, the gate complexity is determined by the worst-case error among all the possible initial states, such as the commutator-scaling error making Trotterization efficient in the system size
[
8
]
.
On the other hand, we are often interested in some physically-motivated classes of initial states and this generally overestimates the error and the cost.
For instance, Trotterization with Haar random or highly-entangled initial states hosts better scaling than the one for arbitrary states
[
9
,
10
]
.
These examples suggest that the structure of the initial state can play a crucial role in improving simulation efficiency. In this context, low-energy initial states are promising candidates, as they naturally arise in low-temperature phenomena in physics and chemistry
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
.
Recent studies
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
have revealed that Trotterization can have the reduction in its error and cost for low-energy initial states.
They show that the gate complexity is improved in the system size
N
when the initial state energy
\Delta
is smaller than
\order{N^{1/(p+1)}g}
, where
p
denotes the Trotter order and
g
means the maximum energy per site.
However, their results are quite restricted: the advantageous low-energy regime
\Delta\in o(N^{1/(p+1)}g)
and the improvement in the system size shrink as the Trotter order
p
grows.
On the contrary, their estimates give worse errors and cost for low-energy initial states with
N^{1/(p+1)}g\lesssim\Delta<Ng
than the one for arbitrary initial states.
It is mysterious whether there exists a provable advantage in generic low-energy initial states whose energy
\Delta
is smaller than the whole-system energy
Ng
(i.e.,
\Delta\in o(Ng)
).

In this Letter, we provide the optimal solution for Trotterization for generic low-energy initial states with
\Delta\in o(Ng)
, rigorously establishing that they offer a substantial and intrinsic advantage in Hamiltonian simulation (See Table
1
).
We mainly focus on local Hamiltonians composed of positive-semidefinite finite-range interactions and consider initial states closed in the low-energy subspace as well as the previous studies.
We first derive the optimal error bound for low-energy initial states, which accurately reflecting the commutator scaling in Trotterization.
Compared to the previous studies
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
, our error bound is linear in the initial state energy
\Delta
and achieves exponentially better scaling explicitly in the system size
N
.
While the Trotter number for arbitrary initial states amounts to
r\in\order{gt (Ngt/\varepsilon)^{1/p}}

[
8
]
, we obtain

r\sim gt\left(\frac{\Delta t+gt\log(N/\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

(1)

as the one for low-energy initial states.
Cost reduction appears in the broadest low-energy regime
\Delta\in o(Ng)
regardless of the Trotter order
p
.
At the same time, initial states with extremely low energy
\Delta\in\mathrm{polylog}\left(N\right)g
require only
\mathrm{polylog}\left(N\right)
gate complexity under the fixed time
t
and error
\varepsilon
, implying the exponential speedup for the sufficiently low-energy regime.
We emphasize that our error bound and cost respectively achieve the theoretically-best scaling in the initial state energy
\Delta
since they can reproduce the optimal ones of Trotterization for arbitrary initial states
[
10
]
with
\Delta=Ng
.

Our results can be extended to various cases:
The improved Trotter number is obtained for generic local Hamiltonians involving long-range interactions, where Eq. (
1
) acquires at most polylogarithmic corrections.
We also show that the cost reduction is present for weakly-correlated states with a low-energy expectation value,
\braket{\psi|H|\psi}\in o(Ng)
as a corollary of the main results.
Our results reveal the substantial advantage of low-energy states in quantum computation, which is of central interest in physics and chemistry.
These findings underscore the broader principle that the structure of the initial state can crucially influence simulation efficiency.
More broadly, our work initiates a framework for initial-state–dependent Hamiltonian simulation, where algorithmic performance is tailored to physically meaningful input classes.
Our result provides the first optimal bound in this direction for low-energy states, and the commutator-scaling technique developed here is flexible enough to extend to other families such as weakly-correlated thermal or highly-entangled states
[
9
,
10
]
.

Setup and notations.—

We first specify the setup and some notations.
Throughout this Letter, we focus on a
N
-qubit Hamiltonian
H
on a lattice
\Lambda=\{1,2,\cdots,N\}
.
We assume that
H
is a
k
-local Hamiltonian with
k\in\order{1}
written by

H=\sum_{X\subset\Lambda;|X|\leq k}h_{X},\quad h_{X}\geq 0

(2)

where each
h_{X}
nontrivially acts on a domain
X
.
The positive-semidefiniteness of
h_{X}
can be always satisfied by the shift
h_{X}\to h_{X}+\norm{h_X}
.
We define the extensiveness
g
by a value such that

\sum_{X\subset\Lambda;X\ni i}\norm{h_X}\leq g,\quad^{\forall}i\in\Lambda,

(3)

which means the maximal energy scale per site.
The scaling of
g
depends on the range of interactions.
When a Hamiltonian has finite-range interactions (i.e., each
X
giving
h_{X}\neq 0
includes sites within some constant distance), we have the extensiveness
g\in\order{N^0}
.
When a Hamiltonian has long-range interactions (i.e., every interactions between two sites
i,j
, given by
\sum_{X\ni i,j}\norm{h_X}
decays as their distance
\order{\mr{dist}(i,j)^{-\nu}}
), the extensiveness scales as

g\in\begin{cases}\order{N^0}&(\text{if long-ranged, $\nu>d$})\\
\order{\log N}&(\text{if long-ranged, $\nu=d$})\\
\order{N^{1-\frac\nu{d}}}&(\text{if long-ranged, $\nu<d$}),\end{cases}

(4)

where
d
denotes the spatial dimension of the lattice
\Lambda
.

Next, we introduce Trotterization as an approach to Hamiltonian simulation.
We begin with decomposing the Hamiltonian
H
into
\Gamma
terms by

H=\sum_{\gamma=1}^{\Gamma}H_{\gamma},\quad H_{\gamma}=\sum_{X\subset\Lambda}h_{X}^{\gamma}.

(5)

Each pair in
\{h_{X}^{\gamma}\geq 0\}_{X}
commutes with each other, and each term
h_{X}
is reproduced by
h_{X}=\sum_{\gamma}h_{X}^{\gamma}
.
The number of partition
\Gamma
will be a significant factor in our result.
We have
\Gamma\in\order{1}
for generic local Hamiltonians with finite-range interactions, and some Hamiltonians with long-range interactions (See Supplementary Materials
S1
in detail
[
18
]
).
The
p
-th order Trotter formula
T_{p}(t)
is defined by the product of
c_{p}\Gamma
time-evolution operators satisfying the order condition as follows,

T_{p}(t)=\prod_{v=1}^{c_{p}\Gamma}e^{-iH_{\gamma_{v}}\alpha_{v}t}=e^{-iHt}+\order{t^{p+1}},

(6)

We assume that the real numbers
\{\alpha_{v}\}_{v}
satisfy
|\alpha_{v}|\leq 1
without loss of generality.
We have
c_{p}\in\order{1}
as long as
p\in\order{1}
.
The Lie-Suzuki-Trotter formula is one of the examples, whose first and second orders are given by

T_{1}(t)=e^{-iH_{\Gamma}t}\cdots e^{-iH_{1}t},\quad T_{2}(t)=T_{1}(-t/2)^{\dagger}T_{1}(t/2).

(7)

The number
c_{p}
is given by
c_{1}=1
and
c_{p}=2\cdot 5^{p/2-1}
for
p\in 2\mathbb{N}
in this case.

For simulating dynamics over large time
t
, we implement Trotterization
T_{p}(t/r)
for
r
times.
The query complexity
r
, called the Trotter number, is determined so that we can achieve the desirable error
\varepsilon
by

\norm{e^{-iHt}-\{T_p(t/r)\}^r}\leq r\norm{e^{-iHt/r}-T_p(t/r)}\leq\varepsilon.

(8)

The error bound of Trotterization,
\norm{e^{-iHt/r}-T_p(t/r)}
, has been recently revealed in a unified way
[
8
]
.
Owing to its commutator scaling, the error and the cost of Trotterization have better scaling in the system size
N
, as shown in the first row of Table

Trotter error in the low-energy subspace.—

We hereby provide the first main result on the suppressed Trotter errors for low-energy initial states, which will lead to the better cost of Hamiltonian simulation from the low-energy subspace.

For a given Hamiltonian
H
, we define a projection to a subspace with the energy lower than
\Delta
by

\Pi_{\leq\Delta}=\sum_{n;E_{n}\leq\Delta}\ket{E_{n}}\bra{E_{n}}.

(9)

(E_{n},\ket{E_{n}})
gives the spectral decomposition of
H
by
H=\sum_{n}E_{n}\ket{E_{n}}\bra{E_{n}}
.
Then, we suppose that the initial state
\ket{\psi}
is closed in this subspace as
\Pi_{\leq\Delta}\ket{\psi}=\ket{\psi}
.
We mean low-energy by the assumption that
\Delta
is sufficiently small compared to
Ng
, which gives the whole energy scale by

\norm{H}\leq\sum_{i\in\Lambda}\sum_{X;X\ni i}\norm{h_X}\leq Ng.

(10)

We focus on the worst case error of Trotterization for all the possible low-energy initial states, defined as follows,

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{p,\Delta}(t)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ket{\psi};\norm{\ket{\psi}}=1,\\
\Pi_{\leq\Delta}\ket{\psi}=\ket{\psi}\end{subarray}}\left((e^{-iHt}-T_{p}(t))\norm{(e^{-iHt}-T_p(t)) \ket{\psi}}\right)

(11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\norm{(e^{-iHt} -T_p(t))\Pi_{\leq\Delta}}.

(12)

Our first main result is the upper bound on
\varepsilon_{p,\Delta}(t)
, which is better than any previous studies
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
as far as we know.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\epsilon
be an arbitrary value in
(0,1)
.
Then, there exists a value,

\Delta^{\prime}=\Delta+\order{g \log(N/\epsilon)},

(13)

such that the Trotter error from low-energy initial states is bounded by

\varepsilon_{p,\Delta}(t)\in\begin{cases}\order{(gt)^p \Delta't + \epsilon}&(\text{if $\Gamma\in\order{1}$})\\
\order{\{gt \log(N/\epsilon)\}^p \Delta't + \epsilon}&(\text{otherwise})\end{cases}

(14)

for small time
t
satisfying

|t|\in\begin{cases}\order{g^{-1}}&(\text{if $\Gamma\in\order{1}$})\\
\order{\{g\log(N/\epsilon)\}^{-1}}&(\text{otherwise}).\end{cases}

(15)

We note that Eqs. (
13
), (
14
), and (
15
) include constant factors dependent on the locality
k\in\order{1}
and the order
p\in\order{1}
.
See Supplementary Materials
S3
for the detailed proof and the exact bound
[
18
]
.
Here, we discuss the meaning of Theorem
1
.
Our result depends on the number of the partition
\Gamma
in Eq. (
5
).
We focus on the first case,
\Gamma\in\order{1}
, which applies to generic local Hamiltonians with finite-range interactions.
When the initial state has no energy restriction, the worst-case Trotter error increases as the whole energy scale
Ng
as the first row of Table
1
.
However, when the initial state is tied to the low-energy subspace, such large energy scale seems not to be relevant.
Theorem
1
rigorously validates this expectation, where the energy scale
Ng
is replaced by
\Delta^{\prime}
, a value close to
\Delta
.
The deviation
\Delta^{\prime}-\Delta\in\order{g\log(N/\epsilon)}
comes from the leakage from the low-energy subspace since the Trotterization
T_{p}(t)
does not exactly preserve
H
.
The Trotter error is dominated by the initial state energy
\Delta
rather than the system size
N
, which is also numerically demonstrated in some local Hamiltonians (See Supplementary Materials
S5

[
18
]
).

We also make comparison with the previous results in the second and third rows
[
14
,
16
]
, briefly mentioning about our derivation.
Our central contributions compared to them are the reflection of the commutator scaling in the low-energy space and the evaluation of the leakage caused by the Trotter error.
First, the commutator scaling is the characteristics of the Trotter error
[
8
]
, which states that the error bound for low-energy initial states is dominated by

\sum_{\gamma_{0},\cdots,\gamma_{q}}\norm{\Pi_{\leq\Delta'}[H_{\gamma_q},\cdots,[H_{\gamma_1},H_{\gamma_0}]] \Pi_{\leq\Delta'}}t^{q+1},

(16)

for the orders
q\geq p
.
While the nested commutators among local terms
\{h_{X}^{\gamma}\}
having no overlap with each other vanish, the previous studies fail to catch this feature, which leads to the
1
-norm error scaling
\order{(\Delta't)^{p+1}}
.
By contrast, we successfully prove that Eq. (
16
) scales as
\order{(gt)^q q! \Delta't}
(See Supplementary Materials
S2

[
18
]
).
This reflects the property of the nested commutators as well as the case with no energy restriction,
\order{(gt)^q q! Ngt}

[
8
]
.

The second difference is the leakage from the low-energy subspace, related to the choice of
\Delta^{\prime}
by Eq. (
13
).
Both of the previous and our studies rely on the following inequality for an operator
O_{X}
on a domain
X\subset\Lambda

[
19
]
,

\norm{\Pi_{>\Delta'} O_X \Pi_{\leq\Delta}}\leq\norm{O_X}e^{-\frac{\Delta^{\prime}-\Delta-3g|X|}{4kg}}.

(17)

It states that the excitation by a local operator
O_{X}
is exponentially suppressed under a local Hamiltonian.
The value
\Delta^{\prime}
was determined so that the leakage
\norm{\Pi_{>\Delta'} e^{-iH_{\gamma_v}\alpha_vt}\Pi_{\leq\Delta}}

[
14
,
15
]
or
\norm{\Pi_{>\Delta'}T_p(t) \Pi_{\leq\Delta}}

[
16
]
can be suppressed up to a small value
\epsilon
, where the projection
\Pi_{>\Delta^{\prime}}
denotes
1-\Pi_{\leq\Delta^{\prime}}
.
However, the non-locality of Trotterization results in the overestimation of the leakage
\Delta^{\prime}-\Delta
, which becomes proportional to
N
.
By contrast, we clarify that the leakage relevant to the Trotter error comes from the nested commutators, expressed by

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma_{0},\cdots,\gamma_{q}}\norm{\Pi_{> \Delta'}[H_{\gamma_q},\cdots,[H_{\gamma_1},H_{\gamma_0}]] \Pi_{\leq\Delta}}t^{q+1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\in\order{ (gt)^{q+1} q! N \exp\left( -\frac{\Delta'-\Delta}{4kg}\right)},

(18)

for the orders
q\geq p
.
Owing to the locality of nested commutators, the leakage is exponentially improved in
N
as Eq. (
13
).

Finally, we emphasize that our error bound proportional to the initial state energy
\Delta
is optimal.
Recently, it has been shown that there exists a set of a local Hamiltonian and an initial state, whose Trotter error is proportional to
N

[
10
]
.
This means that the Trotter error for generic local Hamiltonians and arbitrary initial states should be at least proportional to
N
.
Since the error bound for low-energy should cover the one for arbitrary initial states with
\Delta=Ng
, the linear scaling in the initial state energy
\Delta
is theoretically best.

Trotter number for low-energy states.—

Next, we discuss the cost of Hamiltonian simulation for low-energy initial states.
For simulating quantum dynamics for large time
t
, we repeat the Trotter step
T_{p}(t/r)
with the Trotter number
r
.
It is determined so that the accumulated error becomes smaller than the allowable error by

\norm{(e^{-iHt} - \{T_p (t/r)\}^r) \Pi_{\leq\Delta}}\leq r\varepsilon_{p,\Delta}(t/r)\leq\varepsilon,

(19)

where the first inequality comes from
[e^{-iHt},\Pi_{\leq\Delta}]=0
.
Using Theorem
1
with setting
\epsilon=\varepsilon/(2r)
, it is sufficient to find
r
such that

\frac{(gt)^{p}(\Delta t+gt\log(N r/\varepsilon))}{r^{p}}\in\order{\varepsilon}

(20)

in the case of
\Gamma\in\order{1}
.
Similar discussion goes for
\Gamma\notin\order{1}
.
Under the natural assumption
t,1/\varepsilon\in\mathrm{poly}\left(N\right)
, we obtain the Trotter number
r
satisfying

r\in\begin{cases}\order{gt \left( \frac{\Delta t + gt \log(N/\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon} \right)^\frac 1p }&(\text{if $\Gamma\in\order{1}$})\\
\order{gt \log(N/\epsilon) \left( \frac{\Delta t + gt \log(N/\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon} \right)^\frac 1p}&(\text{otherwise}),\end{cases}

(21)

whose detailed derivation is discussed in Supplementary Materials
S4

[
18
]
.

We compare our results with the cost for arbitrary initial states.
In the case of
\Gamma\in\order{1}
, Table
1
says that there exists a low-energy regime
\Delta\leq\mathrm{Const.}\times Ng
, where the gate count estimate for low-energy initial states becomes smaller than the one without energy restriction by a constant factor.
The Trotter number has better scaling for
\Delta\in o(Ng)
, and notably, it can be at most
\mathrm{polylog}\left(N\right)
if there exists a sufficiently low-energy regime
\Delta\in\mathrm{polylog}\left(N\right)g
.
Such improvement is present also in the case of
\Gamma\notin\order{1}
as long as the initial state is restricted to the low-energy
\Delta\in\order{Ng/(\log N)^p}
.
Namely, the Trotterization can be efficient for low-energy states also in generic long-range interacting systems.

Compared to the previous results
[
14
,
16
]
, our results give significant improvement both in
\Delta
and
N
.
First, the polynomially better scaling in
\Delta
than the second and third rows in Table
1
comes from our discovery, the presence of the commutator-scaling for low-energy initial states as Eq. (
16
).
Although the previous results tell us the advantage of low-energy states for
\Delta\in\order{N^{1/(p+1)}g}
, which are almost absent for the large order
p
, we clarify the advantage in much broader regimes
\Delta\in\order{Ng}
[for
\Gamma\in\order{1}
] or
\Delta\in\order{Ng/(\log N)^p}
[for
\Gamma\notin\order{1}
].
Furthermore, the explicit scaling in the system size
N
is exponentially improved.
It originates from the accurate evaluation of the leakage
\Delta^{\prime}-\Delta
relevant for the Trotter error by Eq. (
13
).
This improvement is essential for concluding the non-vanishing advantage in the system size scaling for the large order
p
.
Reflecting the optimality of our error bound in
\Delta
, the Trotter number by Eq. (
21
) is also optimal in
\Delta
.
In addition, there is room for improving its system-size dependence only logarithmically.
Thus, our results settle how Trotterization can be efficient for low-energy initial states, which has been vigorously explored in a series of recent studies.

The previous studies and the discussion so far have focused on initial states completely closed in the low-energy subspace.
However, it is often difficult to examine the initial state satisfies such a low-energy condition.
We partially extend our results also for initial states with a low-energy expectation value
\braket{\psi|H|\psi}
, which is easy to be checked by classical or quantum computation.
As a corollary of our results with the so-called concentration bound
[
20
,
21
]
, we show that the error and cost of Trotterization reduce when the initial state is weakly correlated and has an energy expectation value
\braket{\psi|H|\psi}\in o(Ng)
.
See Supplementary Materials
S6

[
18
]
for its detailed discussion.

Conclusion and Discussion.—

In this Letter, we show the substantial efficiency of Trotterization for low-energy initial states.
The error bound of Trotterization is at most proportional to the initial state energy
\Delta
and explicitly logarithmic in the system size
N
, which indicates that the Trotter number can reduce for initial states only with slight low-energy condition
\Delta\in o(Ng)
.
Our results provide the theoretically-best solution to the error bound and cost optimal in the initial state energy
\Delta
, settling a recent series of studies on Trotterization for low-energy states
[
14
,
16
]
.

As a future direction, our results may be extended to broad variants of Trotterization, such as randomly-compiled Trotterization (called qDRIFT
[
22
,
15
]
) and multi-product formulas
[
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
]
.
The extension to time-dependent Trotterization
[
27
,
28
,
7
,
29
,
30
]
is of importance, which will lead to the acceleration of adiabatic state preparation
[
31
,
32
]
.
We finally note that, while our results achieve optimal scaling, the initial state energy
\Delta
is defined upon the shift of each term
h_{X}\to h_{X}+\norm{h_X}
for ensuring its positive-semidefiniteness in general.
It is also significant to seek for possible advantages of low-energy initial states whose energy is measured based on the ground state energy.

S1
Number of terms in Trotterization

Trotterization requires decomposition of a Hamiltonian,

H=\sum_{\gamma=1}^{\Gamma}H_{\gamma},\quad H_{\gamma}=\sum_{X\subset\Lambda}h_{X}^{\gamma},

(S1)

where each pair of
\{h_{X}^{\gamma}\}_{X}
commutes as
[h_{X}^{\gamma},h_{X^{\prime}}^{\gamma}]=0
.
Since the number of partition
\Gamma
is a significant factor in our result, we briefly mention how it scales for various classes of quantum many-body Hamiltonians.
We focus on whether
\Gamma
is
\order{1}
or not.

Local Hamiltonians with finite-ranged interactions.—

Local Hamiltonians with finite-ranged interactions are typical examples satifying
\Gamma\in\order{1}
.
For instance, suppose that a Hamiltonian has nearest neighbor interactions on a one-dimensional lattice as

H=\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}h_{i,i+1},\quad h_{i,i+1}\geq 0.

(S2)

It can be decomposed into two terms
H_{1}=\sum_{i;\text{odd}}h_{i,i+1}
and
H_{2}=\sum_{i;\text{even}}h_{i,i+1}
, where
\Gamma
is equal to
2
.
For any locality
k\in\order{1}
and any spatial dimension
d\in\order{1}
, the number
\Gamma
can be
\order{1}
as well.

Local Hamiltonians with long-ranged interactions composed of commuting operators.—

Some of local Hamiltonians with long-ranged interactions can satisfy
\Gamma\in\order{1}
.
A useful class is the case where the interactions can be classified into some groups having mutually commuting operators.
To be concrete, let us consider a Hamiltonian having long-ranged Heisenberg interactions,

H=\sum_{i,j\in\Lambda}\left(J_{ij}^{x}\frac{X_{i}X_{j}+1}{2}+J_{ij}^{y}\frac{Y_{i}Y_{j}+1}{2}+J_{ij}^{z}\frac{Z_{i}Z_{j}+1}{2}\right).

(S3)

Then, we can decompose it with
\Gamma=3
by the following three terms,
H_{1}=\sum_{i,j\in\Lambda}J_{ij}^{x}\frac{X_{i}X_{j}+1}{2}
,
H_{2}=\sum_{i,j\in\Lambda}J_{ij}^{y}\frac{Y_{i}Y_{j}+1}{2}
, and
H_{3}=\sum_{i,j\in\Lambda}J_{ij}^{z}\frac{Z_{i}Z_{j}+1}{2}
.
Another example in this class is a fermionic Hamiltonian,

H=\sum_{k}\varepsilon(k)\hat{c}_{k}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{k}+\sum_{i,j\in\Lambda}U_{ij}\hat{n}_{i}\hat{n}_{j},

(S4)

where
\hat{c}_{k}
and
\hat{c}_{k}^{\dagger}
are fermionic annihilation and creation operators in the wave number space, and
\hat{n}_{j}
is a number operator in the real space.
The decomposition by
H_{1}=\sum_{k}\varepsilon(k)\hat{c}_{k}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{k}
and
H_{2}=\sum_{i,j\in\Lambda}U_{ij}\hat{n}_{i}\hat{n}_{j}
results in
\Gamma=2
, where the time-evolution under
H_{1}
is implemented by the fast fermionic Fourier transform (FFFT)
[
33
]
.
Various models with any locality and dimension belongs to this class.

Other local Hamiltonians.—

In general, any local Hamiltonian with
k\in\order{1}
can be decomposed into
\Gamma\in\order{N^k}
terms with choosing
h_{X}
as each term
H_{\gamma}
.
We use our results for
\Gamma\notin\order{1}
in such cases.

S2
Nested commutators in the low-energy subspace

Here, we prove the upper bound on the nested commutators in the low-energy subspace as follows.

Theorem S1
.

(Nested commutators in the low-energy subspace)

Let
H_{q^{\prime}}=\sum_{X\subset\Lambda}h_{X}^{q^{\prime}}
be
k
-local and
g
-extensive Hamiltonians with positive-semidefinite terms
h_{X}^{q^{\prime}}\geq 0
.
When there exists a projection
\Pi
such that
\Pi H_{q^{\prime}}\Pi\leq\Delta
for every
q^{\prime}=0,1,\cdots,q
, the nested commutator in the subspace designated by the projection
\Pi
is bounded by

\norm{\Pi[H_{q},[H_{q-1},\cdots,[H_1,H_0]]] \Pi}\leq q!(2kg)^{q}\Delta.

(S5)

The nested commutator among
k
-local and
g
-extensive Hamiltonians
H_{0},\cdots,H_{q}
is known to generally have the upper bound
[
34
,
8
]
,

\norm{[H_q,[H_{q-1},\cdots,[H_1,H_0]]]}\leq q!(2kg)^{q}Ng.

(S6)

Theorem
S1
states that the whole energy scale
Ng
is replaced by the maximum energy of the subspace,
\Delta
.
We note that, in spite of large difference in the derivation for them, the upper bound for low-energy states completely reproduces the one for arbitrary states including its coefficient and scaling in the Trotter order
p
, the locality
k
, and the extensiveness
g
at
\Delta=Ng
.

Proof of Theorem
S1
.—

Since the nested commutator is hermitian or anti-hermitian, its operator norm is equal to

\displaystyle\norm{\Pi[H_{q},[H_{q-1},\cdots,[H_1,H_0]]] \Pi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{\ket{\psi};\braket{\psi|\psi}=1}\left|\bra{\psi}\Pi[H_{q},[H_{q-1},\cdots,[H_{1},H_{0}]]]\Pi\ket{\psi}\right|

(S7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ket{\psi};\braket{\psi|\psi}=1\\
\Pi\ket{\psi}=\ket{\psi}\end{subarray}}\left|\bra{\psi}[H_{q},[H_{q-1},\cdots,[H_{1},H_{0}]]]\ket{\psi}\right|.

In the calculation below, a state
\ket{\psi}
is supposed to satisfy
\braket{\psi|\psi}=1
and
\Pi\ket{\psi}=\ket{\psi}
, which lead to

\braket{\psi|H_{q^{\prime}}|\psi}\leq\Delta,\quad q^{\prime}=0,1,\cdots,q.

(S8)

Let
Y_{q^{\prime}}
denote the union of the domains
\{X_{q^{\prime}}\}
given by

Y_{q^{\prime}}=X_{0}\cup X_{1}\cup\cdots\cup X_{q^{\prime}-1},

(S9)

and then, we define a set of domains in the lattice
\Lambda
by

\mathcal{G}_{q}=\{(X_{0},\cdots,X_{q})\in\Lambda^{q+1}\,|\,^{\forall}q^{\prime}\geq 0,\,|X_{q^{\prime}}|\leq k\,\,\text{and}\,\,^{\forall}q^{\prime}\geq 1,\,X_{q^{\prime}}\cap Y_{q^{\prime}}\neq\phi\}.

(S10)

Then, the nested commutator in Eq. (
S7
) is evaluated by

\displaystyle\left|\bra{\psi}\Pi[H_{q},[H_{q-1},\cdots,[H_{1},H_{0}]]]\Pi\ket{\psi}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X_{0},\cdots,X_{q}\\
(X_{0},\cdots,X_{q})\in\mathcal{G}_{q}\end{subarray}}\left|\braket{\psi|[h_{X_{q}}^{q},[h_{X_{q-1}}^{q-1},\cdots,[h_{X_{1}}^{1},h_{X_{0}}^{0}]]]|\psi}\right|

(S11)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{q}\max_{\sigma}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X_{0},\cdots,X_{q}\\
(X_{0},\cdots,X_{q})\in\mathcal{G}_{q}\end{subarray}}\left|\braket{\psi|h_{X_{q}}^{q}h_{X_{\sigma(q-1)}}^{\sigma(q-1)}\cdots h_{X_{\sigma(0)}}^{\sigma(0)}|\psi}\right|,

where
\sigma
denotes permutation of the indices
\{0,1,\cdots,q-1\}
.
We also omit the superscript
q^{\prime}
from
h_{X_{q}^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime}}
due to its clarity below.
Denoting the index for the operator
h_{X_{\sigma(0)}}
in the right edge by
r=\sigma(0)\in\{0,\cdots,q-1\}
, the summation in Eq. (
S11
) is bounded by

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X_{0},\cdots,X_{q}\\
(X_{0},\cdots,X_{q})\in\mathcal{G}_{q}\end{subarray}}\left|\braket{\psi|h_{X_{q}}^{q}h_{X_{\sigma(q-1)}}^{\sigma(q-1)}\cdots h_{X_{\sigma(0)}}^{\sigma(0)}|\psi}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X_{0},\cdots,X_{q}\\
(X_{0},\cdots,X_{q})\in\mathcal{G}_{q}\end{subarray}}\sqrt{h_{X_{q}}}\norm{\sqrt{h_{X_q}} \ket{\psi}}\norm{\sqrt{h_{X_q}}}\left(\prod_{q^{\prime}\neq r,q}\norm{\sqrt{h_{X_{q'}}}}\right)^{2}\norm{\sqrt{h_{X_r}}}\sqrt{h_{X_{r}}}\norm{\sqrt{h_{X_r}} \ket{\psi}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sqrt{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X_{0},\cdots,X_{q}\\
(X_{0},\cdots,X_{q})\in\mathcal{G}_{q}\end{subarray}}\sqrt{h_{X_{q}}}\norm{\sqrt{h_{X_q}} \ket{\psi}}^{2}\norm{\sqrt{h_{X_r}}}^{2}\prod_{q^{\prime}\neq r,q}\norm{\sqrt{h_{X_{q'}}}}^{2}}\sqrt{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X_{0},\cdots,X_{q}\\
(X_{0},\cdots,X_{q})\in\mathcal{G}_{q}\end{subarray}}\sqrt{h_{X_{r}}}\norm{\sqrt{h_{X_r}} \ket{\psi}}^{2}\norm{\sqrt{h_{X_q}}}^{2}\prod_{q^{\prime}\neq r,q}\norm{\sqrt{h_{X_{q'}}}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad=\sqrt{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X_{0},\cdots,X_{q}\\
(X_{0},\cdots,X_{q})\in\mathcal{G}_{q}\end{subarray}}\braket{\psi|h_{X_{q}}|\psi}\prod_{q^{\prime}\neq q}\norm{h_{X_{q'}}}}\sqrt{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X_{0},\cdots,X_{q}\\
(X_{0},\cdots,X_{q})\in\mathcal{G}_{q}\end{subarray}}\braket{\psi|h_{X_{r}}|\psi}\prod_{q^{\prime}\neq r}\norm{h_{X_{q'}}}}.

(S12)

In the first inequality, we use the relation
A\norm{A\ket{\psi}}\leq\norm{A}\cdot\norm{\ket{\psi}}
for an operator
A
and a state
\ket{\psi}
, and also use the positive-semidefiniteness of
h_{X}
for the existence of
\sqrt{h_{X}}
.
The second inequality comes from the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality.
In the last equality, we use the relation
\norm{\sqrt{A}}=\sqrt{\norm{A}}
for a positive-semidefinite operator
A
, which is easily checked by the spectral decomposition.

We concentrate on evaluate the upper bound on

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X_{0},\cdots,X_{q}\\
(X_{0},\cdots,X_{q})\in\mathcal{G}_{q}\end{subarray}}\braket{\psi|h_{X_{r}}|\psi}\prod_{q^{\prime}\neq r}\norm{h_{X_{q'}}}

(S13)

for
r\in\{0,1,\cdots,q\}
, which appears in Eq. (
S12
).
We begin with taking the summation over
X_{r+1},\cdots,X_{q}
, which leads to

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X_{0},\cdots,X_{q}\\
(X_{0},\cdots,X_{q})\in\mathcal{G}_{q}\end{subarray}}\braket{\psi|h_{X_{r}}|\psi}\prod_{q^{\prime}\neq r}\norm{h_{X_{q'}}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X_{0},\cdots,X_{r}\\
(X_{0},\cdots,X_{r})\in\mathcal{G}_{r}\end{subarray}}\braket{\psi|h_{X_{r}}|\psi}\prod_{q^{\prime}=0}^{r-1}\norm{h_{X_{q'}}}\prod_{q^{\prime}=r+1}^{q}\left(\sum_{X_{q^{\prime}}:X_{q^{\prime}}\cap Y_{q^{\prime}}\neq\phi}\norm{h_{X_{q'}}}\right).

(S14)

Each union
Y_{q^{\prime}}=X_{0}\cup\cdots\cup X_{q^{\prime}-1}
contains at most
q^{\prime}k
sites.
In combination with the extensiveness
g
defined by Eq. (
3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{X_{q^{\prime}}:X_{q^{\prime}}\cap Y_{q^{\prime}}\neq\phi}\norm{h_{X_{q'}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in Y_{q^{\prime}}}\sum_{X_{q^{\prime}}:X_{q^{\prime}}\ni j}\norm{h_{X_{q'}}}

(S15)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle q^{\prime}kg.

Using this inequality, Eq. (
S14
) is further bounded by

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X_{0},\cdots,X_{q}\\
(X_{0},\cdots,X_{q})\in\mathcal{G}_{q}\end{subarray}}\braket{\psi|h_{X_{r}}|\psi}\prod_{q^{\prime}\neq r}\norm{h_{X_{q'}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{q!}{r!}(kg)^{q-r}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X_{0},\cdots,X_{r}\\
(X_{0},\cdots,X_{r})\in\mathcal{G}_{r}\end{subarray}}\braket{\psi|h_{X_{r}}|\psi}\prod_{q^{\prime}=0}^{r-1}\norm{h_{X_{q'}}}

(S16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q!}{r!}(kg)^{q-r}\sum_{X_{r}:|X_{r}|\leq k}\braket{\psi|h_{X_{r}}|\psi}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X_{0},\cdots,X_{r-1}\\
(X_{0},\cdots,X_{r-1})\in\mathcal{G}_{r-1},\\
Y_{r}\cap X_{r}\neq\phi\end{subarray}}\prod_{q^{\prime}=0}^{r-1}\norm{h_{X_{q'}}},

where the second equality is the result of reordering of the way to take summation over
X_{r}
and the others.
In order to evaluate this bound, we define

S(r)\equiv\max_{X_{r}\subset\Lambda:|X_{r}|\leq k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X_{0},\cdots,X_{r-1}\\
(X_{0},\cdots,X_{r-1})\in\mathcal{G}_{r-1},\\
Y_{r}\cap X_{r}\neq\phi\end{subarray}}\prod_{q^{\prime}=0}^{r-1}\norm{h_{X_{q'}}}.

(S17)

for
r\geq 1
. From the locality and extensiveness, we have
S(1)=\max_{X_{1}}\sum_{X_{0}:X_{0}\cap X_{1}\neq\phi}\norm{h_{X_0}}\leq kg
for example.
We set
S(0)=1
for the convenience in the inequality below.
Since
Y_{r}\cap X_{r}\neq\phi
implies that some of
X_{0},\cdots,X_{r-1}
should have nonempty intersection with
X_{r}
, we have

\displaystyle S(r)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{X_{r}:|X_{r}|\leq k}\sum_{r^{\prime}=0}^{r-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X_{0},\cdots,X_{r-1}\\
(X_{0},\cdots,X_{r-1})\in\mathcal{G}_{r-1},\\
X_{r^{\prime}}\cap X_{r}\neq\phi\end{subarray}}\prod_{q^{\prime}=0}^{r-1}\norm{h_{X_{q'}}}

(S18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{X_{r}:|X_{r}|\leq k}\sum_{r^{\prime}=0}^{r-1}\sum_{X_{r^{\prime}}:X_{r^{\prime}}\cap X_{r}\neq\phi}\norm{h_{X_{r'}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X_{0},\cdots,X_{r^{\prime}-1}\\
(X_{0},\cdots,X_{r^{\prime}-1})\in\mathcal{G}_{r^{\prime}-1},\\
Y_{r^{\prime}}\cap X_{r^{\prime}}\neq\phi\end{subarray}}\prod_{q^{\prime}=0}^{r-1}\norm{h_{X_{q'}}}\left(\prod_{q^{\prime}=r^{\prime}+1}^{r}\sum_{X_{q^{\prime}}:Y_{q^{\prime}}\cap X_{q^{\prime}}\neq\phi}\norm{h_{X_{q'}}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{r^{\prime}=0}^{r-1}kgS(r^{\prime})\frac{(r-1)!}{r^{\prime}!}(kg)^{r-r^{\prime}-1},

for
r\geq 1
.
We use Eqs. (
S15
) and (
S17
) in order to obtain the last inequality.
The value
S(r)
satisfies the recursive relation,
S(r)/\{r!(kg)^{r}\}\leq\frac{1}{r}\sum_{r^{\prime}=0}^{r-1}S(r^{\prime})/\{r^{\prime}!(kg)^{r^{\prime}}\}
, with
S(0)=1
and it is easy to check that this implies the inequality,

S(r)\leq r!(kg)^{r},\quad r=0,1,\cdots,

(S19)

by induction.
Thus, using Eq. (
S16
) and the low-energy condition
\sum_{X_{r}}\braket{\psi|h_{X_{r}}|\psi}\leq\Delta
, we arrive at the upper bound of Eq. (
S13
),

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X_{0},\cdots,X_{q}\\
(X_{0},\cdots,X_{q})\in\mathcal{G}_{q}\end{subarray}}\braket{\psi|h_{X_{r}}|\psi}\prod_{q^{\prime}\neq r}\norm{h_{X_{q'}}}\leq q!(kg)^{q}\Delta.

(S20)

The other term in Eq. (
S12
) shares the same upper bound by the same calculation.
Summarizing the relations, Eqs. (
S7
), (
S11
), and (
S12
), we finally obtain

\displaystyle\norm{\Pi[H_{q},[H_{q-1},\cdots,[H_1,H_0]]] \Pi}\leq 2^{q}\sqrt{q!(kg)^{q}\Delta}\sqrt{q!(kg)^{q}\Delta}=q!(2kg)^{q}\Delta.\quad\square

(S21)

Compared to the previous results, this theorem accurately captures the locality of Hamiltonians.
Ref.
[
16
]
has shown the upper bound by

\norm{\Pi_{\leq\Delta} [H_q,[H_{q-1},\cdots,[H_1,H_0]]] \Pi_{\leq\Delta}}\leq 2^{q}(\Delta^{\prime})^{q+1}+q!(kg)^{q}Nge^{-\order{\frac{\Delta'-\Delta}{g}}},

(S22)

where the second term can be negligible by properly defining
\Delta^{\prime}=\Delta+\order{g \log N}
.
However, this bound essentially corresponds to the
1
-norm scaling
\norm{[H_q,[H_{q-1},\cdots,[H_1,H_0]]]}\in(\sum_{q^{\prime}=0}^{q}\norm{H_{q'}})^{q+1}\subset\order{(Ng)^{q+1}}
, and fails to capture many vanishing terms due to the locality in the nested commutator.
On the other hand, as we have computed in Eq. (
S11
), Theorem
S1
captures this property, which results in the linear scaling in
\Delta
.
It is essential for concluding that Trotterization is advantageous in the low-energy regime
\Delta\in\order{Ng}
rather than the one in Refs.
[
14
,
16
]
,
\Delta\in\order{N^{1/(p+1)}}
.

Theorem
S1
will be used for proving the Trotter error for
\Gamma\in\order{1}
in Theorem
1
.
The one for
\Gamma\notin\order{1}
relies on the following corollary, which immediately follows from the derivation of Theorem
S1
.

Corollary S2
.

Let
H=\sum_{X\subset\Lambda}h_{X}
be a Hamiltonian composed of positive-semidefinite terms
h_{X}
, and define the projection to the low-energy space
\Pi_{\leq\Delta}
by Eq. (
9
).
For any state
\ket{\psi}
such that
\braket{\psi|\psi}=1
and
\Pi_{\leq\Delta}\ket{\psi}=\ket{\psi}
, the expectation value of the nested commutator is bounded by

\sum_{X_{0},X_{1},\cdots,X_{q}}|\braket{\psi|[h_{X_{q}},[h_{X_{q-1}},\cdots,[h_{X_{1}},h_{X_{0}}]]]|\psi}|\leq q!(2kg)^{q}\Delta.

(S23)

Proof.—

In the left hand side of Eq. (
S23
), the terms with
(X_{0},X_{1},\cdots,X_{q})\in\mathcal{G}_{q}
survives, and we have

\sum_{X_{0},X_{1},\cdots,X_{q}}|\braket{\psi|[h_{X_{q}},[h_{X_{q-1}},\cdots,[h_{X_{1}},h_{X_{0}}]]]|\psi}|\leq 2^{q}\max_{\sigma}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X_{0},\cdots,X_{q}\\
(X_{0},X_{1},\cdots,X_{q})\in\mathcal{G}_{q}\end{subarray}}|\braket{\psi|h_{X_{q}}h_{X_{\sigma(q-1)}}\cdots h_{X_{\sigma(0)}}|\psi}|.

(S24)

Although the right hand side differs from Eq. (
S11
) by the presence of the superscripts
\{\gamma\}
, the same calculation applies to the above formula owing to
\sum_{X}\braket{\psi|h_{X}|\psi}\leq\Delta
.
\quad\square

S3
Trotter errors in the low-energy subspace

Here, we prove Theorem
1
, which gives the Trotter errors for low-energy initial states.
First, we provide the proof for generic cases where the number of partition
\Gamma
is not specified by Theorem
S3
.
It corresponds to the statement for
\Gamma\notin\order{1}
in Theorem
1
, since it is indeed useful for the case with
\Gamma\notin\order{1}
.
After that, we slightly improve the calculation in the derivation of Theorem
S3
for
\Gamma\in\order{1}
, and obtain Corollary
S4
as the statement for
\Gamma\in\order{1}
in Theorem
1
.
We begin with proving the following theorem especially for
\Gamma\notin\order{1}
.

Theorem S3
.

(Trotter errors in the low-energy subspace for
\Gamma\notin\order{1}
)

Suppose that the
k
-local and
g
-extensive Hamiltonian
H=\sum_{X}h_{X}
(
h_{X}\geq 0
) is decomposed into
H=\sum_{\gamma}H_{\gamma}
, where each
H_{\gamma}
is written by
H_{\gamma}=\sum_{X}h_{X}^{\gamma}
(
h_{X}^{\gamma}\geq 0
).
We consider the error of the
p
-th order Trotter formula
T_{p}(t)
within the low-energy subspace having the energy smaller than
\Delta
.
For an arbitrary real number
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, we set two values
p_{0}
and
\Delta^{\prime}
by

p_{0}=\lceil\log(2k^{-1}N/\epsilon)+1\rceil\in\order{\log(N/\epsilon)},\quad\Delta^{\prime}=\Delta+4kg\log\left(\frac{e^{2}N}{\epsilon}\right)\in\Delta+\order{g \log(N/\epsilon)}.

(S25)

Then, if the time
t
satisfies

|t|\leq\frac{1}{2}(2c_{p}p_{0}kg)^{-1}\in\order{\{\log(N/\epsilon)\}^{-1}},

(S26)

with
c_{p}
being the number of repetitions in the Trotter formula [See Eq. (
6
)], the error of interest is bounded by

\varepsilon_{p,\Delta}(t)\equiv\norm{(e^{-iHt}-T_p(t))\Pi_{\leq\Delta}}\leq\frac{4c_{p}}{p+1}(2c_{p}p_{0}kgt)^{p}\Delta^{\prime}t+\epsilon.

(S27)

Namely, the error in the low-energy space scales as
\varepsilon_{p,\Delta}(t)\in\order{\{gt \log(N/\epsilon)\}^p \{\Delta+ g \log(N/\epsilon)\}t + \epsilon}
.

Proof.—

We use the following formula of the Trotter error
[
8
]
,

\displaystyle e^{-iHt}-T_{p}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-e^{-iHt}\int_{0}^{t}\differential\tau\derivative{\tau}\left(e^{iH\tau}T_{p}(\tau)\right)

(S28)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\int_{0}^{t}\differential\tau e^{-iH(t-\tau)}T_{p}(\tau)D_{p}(\tau),

\displaystyle D_{p}(\tau)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle T_{p}(\tau)^{\dagger}HT_{p}(\tau)-iT_{p}(\tau)^{\dagger}\derivative{\tau}T_{p}(\tau).

(S29)

Then, the Trotter error in the low-energy subspace is bounded by

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{p,\Delta}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\norm{\int_0^t \dd\tau e^{-iH(t-\tau)} T_p(\tau) D_p(\tau) \Pi_{\leq\Delta}}

(S30)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\differential\tau\norm{D_p(\tau)\Pi_{\leq\Delta}}.

The operator
D_{p}(\tau)
determines the bound of the Trotter error.
Using the definition of the Trotter formula
T_{p}(t)
by Eq. (
6
), it is expressed by

D_{p}(\tau)=\sum_{\gamma=1}^{\Gamma}\prod_{v^{\prime}=1,\cdots,c_{p}\Gamma}^{\leftarrow}e^{i\alpha_{v^{\prime}}t\mathrm{ad}_{H_{\gamma_{v^{\prime}}}}}H_{\gamma}-\sum_{v=1}^{c_{p}\Gamma}\alpha_{v}\prod_{v^{\prime}<v}^{\leftarrow}e^{i\alpha_{v^{\prime}}t\mathrm{ad}_{H_{\gamma_{v^{\prime}}}}}H_{\gamma_{v}},

(S31)

where the operator
\mathrm{ad}_{A}B
for operators
A,B
denotes their commutator
[A,B]=AB-BA
.
A product of operators with arrows is defined by

\prod_{v^{\prime}=1,\cdots,V^{\prime}}^{\leftarrow}O_{v^{\prime}}=O_{V^{\prime}}\cdots O_{2}O_{1},\quad\prod_{v^{\prime}=1,\cdots,V^{\prime}}^{\rightarrow}O_{v^{\prime}}=O_{1}O_{2}\cdots O_{V^{\prime}}.

(S32)

Then, we employ the Taylor’s theorem on
e^{\tau\mathrm{ad}_{A}}B
, given by

e^{\tau\mathrm{ad}_{A}}B=\sum_{q=0}^{p_{0}-1}\frac{(\tau\mathrm{ad}_{A})^{q}B}{q!}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\differential\tau_{1}\frac{\tau_{1}^{p_{0}-1}}{(p_{0}-1)!}e^{(\tau-\tau_{1})\mathrm{ad}_{A}}(\mathrm{ad}_{A_{1}})^{p_{0}}B.

(S33)

Repeating this relation for Eq. (
S31
) so that the Taylor’s reminder can be in
\order{\tau^{p_0}}
with a certain order
p_{0}>p
, we obtain the expansion of
D_{p}(\tau)
by

D_{p}(\tau)=\sum_{q=p}^{p_{0}-1}A_{q}\tau^{q}+A_{p_{0}}(\tau),\quad A_{p_{0}}(\tau)\in\order{\tau^{p_0}}.

(S34)

The order
p_{0}
is determined later, and we will see that the choice by Eq. (
S25
) is appropriate.
The operators
A_{q}

S4
Exact scaling of the Trotter number

Here, we derive the exact scaling of the Trotter step

, required for simulating low-energy states.
As discussed in the main text, we have to determine

\norm{\left\{ e^{-iHt} - ( T_p(t/r) )^r \right\} \Pi_{\leq\Delta}}\leq\varepsilon.

(S60)

can be satisfied.
Then, the Trotter number
r
can be determined as follows.

Proposition S5
.

(Exact scaling of Trotter number
r
)

Suppose that the time renormalized by the extensiveness,
gt
, and the inverse error
1/\varepsilon
are at most
\mathrm{poly}\left(N\right)
.
Then, there exists a value
r
satisfying

r\in\begin{cases}\order{gt \left( \frac{\Delta t + gt \log(N/\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon} \right)^\frac 1p }&(\text{if $\Gamma\in\order{1}$})\\
\order{gt \log(N/\epsilon) \left( \frac{\Delta t + gt \log(N/\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon} \right)^\frac 1p}&(\text{otherwise}),\end{cases}

(S61)

which qualifies as the Trotter number satisfying Eq. (
S60
).

Proof.—

We first consider the case with
\Gamma\in\order{1}
.
We set
\epsilon=\varepsilon/(2r)
and use Corollary
S4
, while we will confirm the condition Eq. (
S53
) for the Trotter step
t/r
later.
Let us define a value
r_{0}
by

r_{0}=gt\left(\frac{\Delta t+gt\log(N/\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(S62)

Then, it is sufficient to show that there exists a constant
A>1
such that

\frac{(gt)^{p}\{\Delta t+gt\log(N A r_0 / \varepsilon)\}}{(Ar_{0})^{p}}\leq\varepsilon,

(S63)

where the Trotter number
r
is equal to
Ar_{0}
.
The left hand side is calculated as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{(gt)^{p}\{\Delta t+gt\log(N A r_0 / \varepsilon)\}}{(Ar_{0})^{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(gt)^{p}\{\Delta t+gt\log(N/\varepsilon)\}}{(r_{0})^{p}}A^{-p}\frac{\Delta t+gt\log(N/\varepsilon)+gt\log Ar_{0}}{\Delta t+gt\log(N/\varepsilon)}

(S64)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon A^{-p}\left(1+\frac{\log A}{\log(N/\varepsilon)}+\frac{\log r_{0}}{\log(N/\varepsilon)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon A^{-p+\frac{1}{2}}\left(2+\frac{\log r_{0}}{\log(N/\varepsilon)}\right).

In the last line, we use
\log A\leq A^{1/2}
for
A>1
and

for sufficiently large
N
or
1/\varepsilon
.
When the time
t
and the inverse error
1/\varepsilon
are both in
\mathrm{poly}\left(N\right)
, we have

\displaystyle\log r_{0}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\log(gt)+\frac{1}{p}\log\left(\frac{Ngt+gt\log(N/\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}\right)\in\order{\log N},

(S65)

where we use
\Delta\leq Ng
in the first inequality.
Thus, there exists a constant positive value
B
such that
\log r_{0}/\log(N/\varepsilon)\leq B
.
When we set the positive constant
A
by
A=(2+B)^{2/(2p-1)}
, Eq. (
S63
) is satisfied.
We finally discuss the satisfaction of Eq. (
S53
) for the Trotter step

to apply Corollary
S4
.
According to Eq. (
S62
), it scales as

\displaystyle\frac{t}{r}

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\order{g^{-1} \left( \frac{\Delta t + gt \log(N/\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}\right)^{-\frac 1p}}.

(S66)

On the other hand, Eq. (
S53
) demands
\frac{t}{r}\in\order{g^{-1}}
, where the constant factor depends only on
k,\Gamma,p
.
Thus, under sufficiently large
\Delta t
,
gt
,

1/\varepsilon
, the condition Eq. (
S53
) is automatically satisfied.

The other case including

is discussed in a similar manner.
We set a value
r_{0}
, given by

(S67)

Then, based on Theorem

Numerical demonstration on frustration-free Hamiltonians

Here, we numerically demonstrate Theorem
1

Theorem
1
applies to generic local Hamiltonians with shifting every local term by
h_{X}\to h_{X}+\norm{h_X}
for guaranteeing its positive-semidefiniteness.
As discussed in the main text, the error reduction appears in the low-energy regime
\Delta\in o(Ng)
.
Although it is comparably relaxed from the one in the previous studies
[
14
,
16
]
,
\Delta\in o(N^{1/(p+1)}g)
, it still demands the Hamiltonian to be nearly frustration-free.
Thus, we hereby concentrate on the following completely frustration-free Hamiltonians.
The first one is the Affleck-Kennedy-Lieb-Tasaki (AKLT) Hamiltonian,

H_{\mathrm{AKLT}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}h_{i,i+1},\quad h_{i,i+1}=P\left[({\mbox{\boldmath$S$}}_{i}+{\mbox{\boldmath$S$}}_{i+1})^{2}=2(2+1)\right],

(S69)

where each
{\mbox{\boldmath$S$}}_{i}

i

[
35

The operator
P[\cdot]
means a projection to the subspace satisfying the argument.
The AKLT Hamiltonian is a typical example whose gapped ground state has a symmetry protected topological order.
The second example is the Majumdar-Ghosh (MG) Hamiltonian defined by

H_{\mathrm{MG}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N-2}h_{i,i+1,i+2},\quad h_{i,i+1,i+2}=P\left[({\mbox{\boldmath$S$}}_{i}+{\mbox{\boldmath$S$}}_{i+1}+{\mbox{\boldmath$S$}}_{i+2})^{2}=\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{3}{2}+1\right)\right],

(S70)

where each
{\mbox{\boldmath$S$}}_{i}
is a spin-
1/2
operator
[
36
,
37
]
.
The MG Hamiltonian is known to have two exactly-degenerate ground states.
We demonstrate how the Trotter error
\varepsilon_{p,\Delta}(t)
depends on the system size
N
and the energy bound
\Delta
by exact diagonalization.

We organize the Trotterization
T_{p}(t)
for the orders
p=1,2
by the Lie-Suzuki-Trotter formula, Eq. (

We decompose the AKLT Hamiltonian by
H_{\mathrm{AKLT}}=H_{1}+H_{2}
with
H_{1}=\sum_{i;\text{odd}}h_{i,i+1}
and
H_{2}=\sum_{i;\text{even}}h_{i,i+1}

Similarly, we decompose the MG Hamiltonian
H_{\mathrm{MG}}
into three terms depending on the site

S6
Weakly-correlated states with low-energy expectation value

Throughout the main text, we consider a low-energy initial state
\ket{\psi}
satisfying
\Pi_{\leq\Delta}\ket{\psi}=\ket{\psi}
, which is completely closed in the low-energy subspace.
As a straightforward extension based on the concentration bound or the Chernoff bound
[
20
,
21
]
, we see that the reduction of the error and cost of Trotterization can be present also for weakly-correlated initial states having a low-energy expectation value.

We suppose that the initial state
\ket{\psi}
is a product state whose energy expectation value
\braket{\psi|H|\psi}
is sufficiently small.
The Hamiltonian
H
is assumed to be composed of finite-ranged Hamiltonians.
Then, the concentration bound dictates that the weight of eigenstates highly deviated from
\Delta
exponentially decays as

\braket{\psi|\Pi_{>\braket{\psi|H|\psi}+x}|\psi}\leq\exp\left(-c\frac{x^{2}}{Ng^{2}}\right),

(S71)

where
c
is a positive
\order{1}
constant determined by the locality
k
, the extensiveness
g
, and the lattice geometry
[
20
]
.
The Trotter error for the initial product state
\ket{\psi}
is bounded by

\displaystyle\left(e^{-iHt}-\{T_{p}(t/r)\}^{r}\right)\norm{\left( e^{-iHt} - \{T_p(t/r)\}^r \right) \ket{\psi}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(e^{-iHt}-\{T_{p}(t/r)\}^{r}\right)\Pi_{\leq\norm{\left( e^{-iHt} - \{T_p(t/r)\}^r \right) \Pi_{\leq\braket{\psi|H|\psi}+x}}+x}+\norm{e^{-iHt} - \{T_p(t/r)\}^r}\cdot\Pi_{>\norm{\Pi_{> \braket{\psi|H|\psi} + x} \ket{\psi}}+x}\ket{\psi}

(S72)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle r\varepsilon_{p,\braket{\psi|H|\psi}+x}(t/r)+2\Pi_{>\norm{\Pi_{> \braket{\psi|H|\psi} + x} \ket{\psi}}+x}\ket{\psi},

based on the triangle inequality.
In order to execute Hamiltonian simulation with the guaranteed accuracy
\varepsilon
, it suffices to choose the value
x
by

x=\sqrt{\frac{2N}{c}\log(4/\varepsilon)}g\in\Theta\left(\sqrt{N\log(1/\varepsilon)}g\right),

(S73)

which results in
\Pi_{>\norm{\Pi_{> \braket{\psi|H|\psi} + x} \ket{\psi}}+x}\ket{\psi}\leq\varepsilon/4
by Eq. (
S71
), and to determine an integer
r
satisfying
r\varepsilon_{p,\braket{\psi|H|\psi}+x}(t/r)\leq\varepsilon/2
.
The Trotter number
r
scales as

r\in\Theta\left(gt\left(\frac{\braket{\psi|H|\psi}t+\sqrt{N\log(1/\varepsilon)}gt}{\varepsilon}\right)^{1/p}\right),

(S74)

which is obtained in a similar manner to Supplementary Materials
S4
.
Therefore, compared to the case for arbitrary initial states
[
8
]
, there exists constant speedup under
\braket{\psi|H|\psi}\leq\mathrm{Const.}\times Ng
, and the scaling of the Trotter number is improved under
\braket{\psi|H|\psi}\in o(Ng)
.
Under the deeper low-energy regime
\braket{\psi|H|\psi}\in\order{N^{1/2}g}
, its system-size dependence can be
\order{N^{1/(2p)}}
, which is quadratically better than the one for arbitrary initial states (See the first row in Table
1
).

In contrast to being closed in the low-energy subspace, it is easy to check whether the initial product state has small energy expectation value by classical computation.
Instead, the improvement is at most quadratic in
N
due to the energy distribution.
We also note that the advantage for initial states with a low-energy expectation value becomes visible for the first time by our result.
When we discuss with the previous results
[
14
,
16
]
, the value
\braket{\psi|H|\psi}+x
is demanded to be
o(N^{1/(p+1)})
for the advantage in the scaling to be present.
However, this cannot be satisfied since the width of the energy distribution
x
scales as Eq. (
S73
).
Finally, while we focus on product states under a Hamiltonian with finite-ranged interactions, the concentration bound or the Chernoff bound exist for weakly-correlated states (i.e., states with exponentially-decaying correlation functions or product states with finite-depth local unitary gates)
[
20
]
or for generic
k
-local and
g
-extensive Hamiltonians
[
21
]
.
The advantage is present also for these cases, while the width
x
is modified from Eq. (
S73
) poly-logarithmically in
1/\varepsilon
.

Recently, it has been proven that the Trotter error can reduce for initial states having large entanglement
[
9
,
10
]
.
Moreover, it has been shown that it is impossible to improve the Trotter error for generic product states from the worst case error
\order{Nt^{p+1}}

[
7
]
, since there exists a certain product state having the Trotter error proportional to
N

[
10
]
.
On the other hand, as opposed to expectation from these studies, our result indicates that weakly-correlated initial states with a small amount of entanglement can generally have a smaller Trotter error if we additionally assume that they have small energy expectation values.

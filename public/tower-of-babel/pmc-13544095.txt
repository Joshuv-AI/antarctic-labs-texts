The use of ex-vivo in-vitro maturation of ovarian tissues oocytes (OTO-IVM) for fertility preservation in gynaecologic cancers
Authors: Charissa Shu Ying Goh, Cheryl Jia Wei Lim, Qiu Ju Ng, Shi Hui Lee, Felicia Hui Xian Chin, Yu Hui Lim, Jessie Wai Leng Phoon, Yan Jia Sim, Joo Mong Lee, Melissa Durgahshree, Melinda Chan Ling Hou, Yee Shen Chong, Tat Xin Ee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundFertility preservation in ovarian tumours or advanced gynaecologic malignancies is often challenging. Traditional fertility preservation techniques, such as ovarian tissue cryopreservation or oocyte retrieval, may not always be suitable, especially in cases where immediate cancer treatment is necessary or where hormonal stimulation is contraindicated. Ex-vivo in-vitro maturation of ovarian tissue oocytes (OTO-IVM) offers a promising alternative in these women by enabling the retrieval and maturation of immature oocytes outside the body from the oophorectomy tissue specimen.MethodWe present a case series of four women with gynaecologic malignancies who underwent ex-vivo oocyte retrieval and in-vitro maturation at the time of their oncologic surgeries.ConclusionEvidence supporting live births following OTO-IVM remains limited, and as such, the technique is currently regarded as experimental. Nevertheless, for selected women in whom conventional fertility preservation is not feasible, OTO-IVM may provide the only opportunity for fertility preservation and warrants consideration.

Introduction

Despite the rising incidence of cancer, significant advances in cancer treatment have led to improvement in long term survival. There is a growing focus to improve survivorship in cancer patients. Oncofertility — the intersection of oncology and reproductive medicine — plays a vital role in this survivorship, recognising that fertility preservation is a key aspect of long-term wellbeing for many patients. As more individuals survive cancer and live healthy, fulfilling lives, addressing the potential impact of treatment on reproductive potential becomes essential to ensuring holistic, patient-centred care.

Ovarian stimulation followed by cryopreservation and ovarian tissue cryopreservation are the most established techniques for fertility preservation (ESHRE Guideline Group on Female Fertility Preservation et al., 2020). However, for certain gynaecologic malignancies or advanced oncologic disease, ovarian stimulation and/or ovarian tissue cryopreservation may be contraindicated. Ex-vivo in-vitro maturation of ovarian tissue oocytes (OTO-IVM) offers a promising alternative in these women by enabling the retrieval and maturation of immature oocytes outside the body from the oophorectomy tissue specimen.

Method

The case series include four women with gynaecologic malignancies who underwent OTO-IVM that was managed in our unit's multidisciplinary Oncofertility Clinic in 2025. The patients had ovarian (n = 3) cancer and endometrial (n = 1) cancer.

In the case series, OTO-IVM was offered to patients who were undergoing oophorectomy for gynaecological malignancies as a salvage fertility preservation option. In these patients' cases, conventional methods of fertility preservation such as ovarian stimulation were not possible. Marriage is not a criteria for fertility preservation in our local practice and single women are allowed to cryopreserve oocytes. However, our local laws required the patient to be married for embryo cryopreservation.

During the oncologic surgery, the entire ovary was excised and immediately transferred to a specimen container, usually immersed in pre-warmed Modified HTF HEPES-buffered medium (FUJIFILM Irvine Scientific), and transported to the laboratory within 5 min. For very large ovarian masses, the specimens were transported in a surgical dish and immediately brought to the laboratory. Once in the laboratory, the ovary was placed onto a large Petri dish containing warm modified HTF HEPES-buffered culture medium. All visible follicles were aspirated with a 3 ml-syringe and 21-gauge needle containing pre-warmed HEPES-buffered culture media. Each aspirate was released gently into a separate round culture dish to examine for oocyte-cumulus-complexes (OCC). OCCs with presence of a germinal vesicle or at the metaphase I were identified under a stereozoom microscope (80× magnification) and flushed and transferred to a 4 -well dish containing LAG media (Medicult IVM System; Origio® CooperSurgical®, Denmark) for 2 h incubation. OCCs containing mature oocytes at metaphase II were transferred to standard single-step media (Irvine Scientific) for incubation till time of vitrification or for insemination. After two hours of incubation, the immature OCCs were transferred to pre-equilibrated IVM media (Medicult IVM System; Origio® CooperSurgical®, Denmark) supplemented with 75mIU/ml FSH and 100mIU/ml HCG and incubated at 37 degree Celsius, 6% CO2 for 48 h. Maturity assessment of the oocytes were performed once at 24 h and again at 48 h to identify mature oocytes for denudation. Identified OCCs with mature oocytes were picked up using a sterile disposable pre-plugged polished glass pipette for denudation. OCCs underwent gentle enzymatic and mechanical denudation in commercially-prepared recombinant human hyaluronidase (ICSI Cumulase®; Origio®CooperSurgical®, Denmark) and modified HTF HEPES-buffered culture medium, in a 4-well dish. All mature metaphase II oocytes harvested directly from ex vivo follicular aspiration or derived from in-vitro maturation culture, were subsequently vitrified by commercial cryoprotectant media (NX-Vit; Irvine Scientific) and stored in cryodewar containing −196 degree Celsius liquid nitrogen.

Patient 1

A 35-year-old nulliparous patient was referred to the Oncofertility Clinic in June 2025 after a left ovarian cystectomy in May 2025. The histology of the left ovarian cyst was grade 1 endometrioid carcinoma. A staging CT thorax abdomen and pelvis post-operatively showed an incompletely removed left ovarian tumour and peritoneal thickening (which may possibly be due to post-surgical changes versus peritoneal carcinomatosis). In view of the findings, the patient was counselled for completion left salpingo-oopherectomy, fertility sparing staging surgery, with considerations for complete debulking if the disease was advanced. The patient was keen for OTO-IVM intraoperatively if either or both ovaries were removed. Three immature oocytes were retrieved from the left ovarian tissue specimen with one resultant cleavage stage embryo frozen.

She was diagnosed with Stage IC1 endometrioid carcinoma of the left ovary and was planned for adjuvant chemotherapy with paclitaxel and carboplatin. Before commencing treatment, she elected to undergo a cycle of ovarian stimulation, yielding 13 oocytes, of which 9 cleavage-stage embryos were successfully cryopreserved.

Patient 2

A 31-year-old nulliparous patient was referred to the Oncofertility Clinic in June 2025 with bilateral large ovarian masses with a background history of previous bilateral cystectomy for borderline ovarian tumour. Her preoperative serum Anti-Mullerian Hormone (AMH) was 0.62 ng/ml. She was scheduled for her oncologic surgery with intraoperative frozen section and possible staging procedure. In anticipation that one or both ovaries might require removal, she elected to pursue oocyte cryopreservation via OTO-IVM. She underwent open right salpingo-oophorectomy, left ovarian cystectomy, myomectomy, infracolic omentectomy, and rectal serosal biopsies. Final histology revealed bilateral serous borderline ovarian tumours. The right ovarian specimen was processed for ex-vivo IVM; however, no oocytes were retrieved. She was staged as having Stage IC3 bilateral serous borderline ovarian tumour and was placed under close surveillance. The patient declined postoperative ovarian stimulation for oocyte / embryo cryopreservation.

Patient 3

A 32-year-old nulliparous patient was referred to the Oncofertility Clinic in September 2025 after a suspicious right ovarian mass was detected on saline sonography during fertility evaluation. She had previously undergone three laparoscopic left cystectomies for benign mucinous cystadenoma in 2019, 2022, and 2023, followed by a laparoscopic left salpingo-oophorectomy and myomectomy in 2024 for a left mucinous borderline ovarian tumour. Staging MRI revealed a 10.1 cm multilocular cystic right ovarian mass with enhancing solid components (O-RADS MRI score 4) and no distant metastasis. She was planned for right ovarian cystectomy, with salpingo-oophorectomy if indicated. The patient declined intraoperative frozen section, opting instead to await final histology for definitive management. Intraoperatively, the tumour was found to be densely adherent with multiple peritoneal nodules, prompting an open right salpingo-oophorectomy, multiple myomectomies, and biopsies of nodules on the bladder peritoneum, sigmoid colon, left pelvic sidewall, and omentum. The right ovary was sent for OTO-IVM, yielding seven oocytes retrieved ex vivo, with one cleavage-stage embryo successfully cryopreserved. Final histology confirmed grade 2 mucinous adenocarcinoma of the right ovary, with invasive carcinoma in the biopsied peritoneal, sigmoid, abdominal wall, and omental nodules. She is planned for interval debulking surgery followed by adjuvant chemotherapy. The couple is considering pursuing surrogacy overseas in the future.

Patient 4

A 24-year-old single nulliparous woman was referred to the Oncofertility Clinic in August 2025 for consideration of fertility-sparing treatment of grade 1 endometrioid adenocarcinoma diagnosed after a hysteroscopy dilation and curettage.

Her staging scans revealed a suspicious left external iliac lymph node, and subsequent PET-CT demonstrated FDG-avid para-aortic and left external iliac nodes. Immunohistochemical analysis of the endometrial curettage specimen showed the loss of PMS2 and MLH1 expression. Laparoscopic biopsy of the external iliac lymph nodes confirmed metastatic disease, establishing a diagnosis of stage III endometrioid adenocarcinoma. Given the advanced stage, and mismatched repair-deficient tumour, fertility-sparing therapy was contraindicated.

Despite this, the patient expressed a strong desire for fertility preservation and underwent OTO-IVM at the time of her staging surgery. The patient was counselled on the need for surrogacy for future pregnancies. A total of 76 oocytes were retrieved ex-vivo, with 29 MII oocytes successfully cryopreserved following IVM culture. Final histopathology confirmed grade 2 endometrioid adenocarcinoma with pelvic nodal macrometastasis and para-aortic micrometastasis. She is planned for post-operative adjuvant chemotherapy with paclitaxel, carboplatin and dostarlimab.

Discussion

To-date, there are seven live births reported from oocytes derived from OTO-IVM (Prasath et al., 2014; Segers et al., 2015; Uzelac et al., 2015; Higuchi et al., 2024; Higuchi et al., 2026; Segers et al., 2020; Delattre et al., 2020). The first live birth resulting from OTO-IVM was reported by our centre (Prasath et al., 2014). The patient was a 21-year-old woman with stage IIIC ovarian cancer who had undergone fertility-sparing surgery. Immature oocytes retrieved during oophorectomy were matured in vitro and subsequently fertilized. Three cleavage-stage embryos were obtained and cryopreserved. Following embryo transfer of two cleavage-stage embryos, the patient achieved a singleton live birth. Since then, additional live births from cryopreserved embryos (Segers et al., 2015; Uzelac et al., 2015; Higuchi et al., 2024; Higuchi et al., 2026; Segers et al., 2020; Delattre et al., 2020) and oocytes (Segers et al., 2020; Delattre et al., 2020) derived from OTO-IVM have been reported. Six live births were derived using monophasic IVM culture while the latest live birth reported by Higuchi et al. was derived using biphasic IVM culture in a patient with ovarian fibromatosis (Higuchi et al., 2026). These cases demonstrate that OTO-IVM may serve as a valuable fertility preservation option for patients contraindicated for ovarian stimulation or requiring urgent oncologic surgery (Morice et al., 2024), particularly in those with suspicious ovarian tumours or advanced gynaecologic malignancies.

Data on the use of OTO-IVM specifically for gynaecological cancer patients is limited. Park et al. (Park et al., 2016) described the a case series on OTO-IVM as a salvage fertility preservation procedure in gynaecologic cancers. Among six patients with ovarian and/or endometrial malignancies, five (83.3%) achieved successful cryopreservation. Another prospective observational study by Bunyaeva et al. (Bunyaeva et al., 2021) reported successful cryopreservation in 17 out of 27 patients (62.9%). Decreased ovarian reserves (defined as an AMH level of <1.0 ng/ml) and advanced age may increase risk of unsuccessful cryopreservation from OTO-IVM. In Bunyaeva's study, patients who failed to have any cryopreservation were of advanced age (38–42 years) and had AMH serum level below 0.6 ng/ml. In our study, 75.0% of patients had successful cryopreservation. Likewise, the patient with unsuccessful cryopreservation in our case series had decreased ovarian reserve and a pre-operative AMH serum level of 0.64 ng/ml.

The maturation rate in our study was 42.2%, which is comparable to the average OTO-IVM maturation rate of 33.8% in a 2022 systematic review of OTO-IVM outcomes in oncologic patients (Mohd Faizal et al., 2022). However, there are limitations to these studies due to small sample sizes and heterogeneity among the different oncologic indications and OTO-IVM techniques (Mohd Faizal et al., 2022; De Roo and Tilleman, 2021). With the current evidence, the oocyte maturation rate of OTO-IVM appears lower than that of IVM done for oocytes retrieved via conventional transvaginal oocyte pick up (OPU-IVM) (Mohd Faizal et al., 2022). However, this may be partly due to the difference in the patient populations compared. OTO-IVM is often done as part of a fertility preservation procedure, while OPU-IVM includes a range of indications including treatment of infertile women with polycystic ovarian disease. In patients with ovarian tumours, OPU-IVM will not be suitable as this require trans-vaginal aspiration of oocytes and carry a high risk of puncture and spillage of the ovarian tumour.

Future research in IVM may potentially improve maturation rate. To-date, most of the published data on OTO-IVM was derived from monophasic IVM protocols, in which immature oocytes are directly cultured in maturation medium. In contrast, newer biphasic IVM approaches, including capacitation (CAPA)-IVM, incorporate an initial pre-maturation or capacitation phase before the final maturation phase, with the aim of improving synchronisation of nuclear and cytoplasmic maturation. These approaches have demonstrated improved maturation rates and, in some studies, increased numbers of mature oocytes and embryos compared with conventional monophasic IVM (Mohd Faizal et al., 2022; De Roo and Tilleman, 2021; Kirillova et al., 2021). As maturation rate refers to the proportion of retrieved immature oocytes that reach the mature metaphase-II (MII) stage and become potentially suitable for fertilisation and cryopreservation, improvements in maturation may increase the number of oocytes available for fertility preservation and, consequently, the cumulative chance of future reproductive success.

OTO-IVM is a promising approach; however, its broader clinical adoption should be undertaken with caution, as the current evidence base remains limited. Careful patient counselling is therefore essential, particularly given that oocyte yield with OTO-IVM is generally lower than with conventional ovarian stimulation. This is illustrated in patient 1 of the present case series, where OTO-IVM resulted in a single embryo, compared with nine embryos obtained following conventional stimulation of the contralateral ovary.

Nonetheless, OTO-IVM may provide a valuable salvage fertility-preservation option for patients who are unsuitable for conventional fertility-preservation strategies. This is particularly relevant for patients with ovarian malignancies or advanced-stage uterine malignancies in whom ovarian stimulation, oocyte retrieval, or ovarian tissue cryopreservation may be unsuitable because of oncological or surgical considerations. A key advantage of OTO-IVM is that immature oocytes can be retrieved directly from an ovary that is being removed as part of definitive oncological surgery, without requiring additional ovarian stimulation or prolongation of the surgical procedure. Furthermore, as only isolated oocytes are utilised for subsequent IVM and cryopreservation, this approach avoids the potential risk of reintroducing malignant cells associated with transplantation of cryopreserved ovarian tissue.

Despite these potential advantages, OTO-IVM remains technically demanding and labour intensive, requiring substantial embryology expertise and dedicated laboratory resources. Data regarding the developmental competence and post-thaw survival of OTO-IVM-derived oocytes and embryos, as well as clinical pregnancy and live birth outcomes (Prasath et al., 2014; Segers et al., 2015; Uzelac et al., 2015; Higuchi et al., 2024; Higuchi et al., 2026; Segers et al., 2020; Delattre et al., 2020), remain limited. The most recent American Society of Clinical Oncology (ASCO) guidelines on fertility preservation in patients with cancer (Bhinder et al., 2026) appropriately highlighted both the potential benefits and current pitfalls of IVM. While advances in technology have enabled the retrieval of mature oocytes and embryos via IVM, the process remains less efficient than conventional ovarian stimulation. Furthermore, only a limited number of assisted reproductive technology laboratories report expertise in this technique, and the small number of reported births restricts reliable estimation of clinical outcomes.

The practical challenges associated with OTO-IVM are also reflected in our institutional experience. Although our centre reported the first successful live birth following the use of OTO-IVM-derived oocytes in 2014, no further patients were referred for this procedure until 2025. This may, in part, reflect increasing recognition of the safety and feasibility of ovarian stimulation and oocyte retrieval in selected patients with cancer, which subsequently became the primary fertility-preservation strategy for most patients referred to our service. In addition, limited awareness of OTO-IVM among gynaecological oncologists may have contributed to under-referral. In clinical practice, oncological surgery may proceed without consideration of OTO-IVM as a potential adjunctive fertility-preservation strategy, particularly when the technique is not routinely available or incorporated into preoperative counselling.

The establishment of a joint multidisciplinary Oncofertility Clinic in our centre provided an opportunity for closer collaboration between gynaecological oncologists and reproductive medicine specialists, facilitating integrated counselling and individualised fertility-preservation planning for young women with gynaecological malignancies. This collaborative model improved awareness of OTO-IVM, facilitated appropriate referrals and surgical coordination, and enabled consideration of OTO-IVM at the time of definitive oncological surgery. Such multidisciplinary integration may therefore help identify patients who could benefit from this otherwise highly specialised fertility-preservation approach and may have contributed to renewed clinical interest in OTO-IVM among both clinicians and patients.

For patients undergoing ovarian tissue cryopreservation for other indications, particularly those with non-gynaecological malignancies, OTO-IVM may also serve as an adjunctive strategy by enabling the concurrent retrieval and utilisation of immature oocytes from ovarian tissue collected for cryopreservation (Delattre et al., 2020).

Further research is required to establish the clinical utility of OTO-IVM and to define which patient populations are most likely to benefit. Future studies should include standardised reporting of oocyte maturation, fertilisation and embryo development, post-thaw outcomes, cumulative live birth rates, and long-term follow-up of offspring conceived using OTO-IVM-derived oocytes. Such data will be essential to determine the reproductive potential, safety and appropriate clinical role of OTO-IVM within contemporary fertility-preservation practice.

Sharp power concavity of two relevant free boundary problems of reaction-diffusion type
Qingyou He

Abstract
The porous medium type reaction-diffusion equation and the Hele-Shaw problem are two free boundary problems linked through the incompressible (Hele-Shaw) limit. We investigate and compare the sharp power concavities of the pressures on their respective supports for the two free boundary problems. For the pressure of the porous medium type reaction-diffusion equation, the 1 2 \frac{1}{2} -concavity preserves all the time, while α \alpha -concavity for α ∈ [ 0 , 1 2 ) ∪ ( 1 2 , 1 ] \alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2})\cup(\frac{1}{2},1] does not persist in time. In contrast, in the case of the pressure for the Hele-Shaw problem, α \alpha -concavity with α ∈ [ 0 , 1 2 ] \alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2}] is maintained all the while and 1 2 \frac{1}{2} acts as the largest index. The intuitive explanation for the difference between the two free boundary problems is that, although the Hele-Shaw problem is the incompressible limit of the porous medium-type reaction-diffusion equation, it is no longer a degenerate parabolic equation. Furthermore, for the pressure of the porous medium type reaction-diffusion equation, the non-degenerate estimate is established by means of the derived concave properties, indicating that the spatial Lipschitz regularity in the whole space is sharp.



Key-words.
Degenerate reaction-diffusion equation; Hele-Shaw problem; Sharp power concavity; Non-degenerate estimate.

Under the

-concave results (Lemma

, to overcome the difficulty caused by non-monotonicity of the source term

Lemma 2.3
.

If the initial pressure
P_{0}
satisfies (
1.7
)-(
1.8
) and the second estimate of (

Proof.

The new variable

We are going to use the comparison principle to achieve our goal. To this end, we assume

X=\frac{\tilde{P}_{k}^{2}}{2}-\epsilon t,\quad t\geq 0.

Since

\displaystyle X_{t}=

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{k}\tilde{P}_{kt}-\epsilon

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{rG(0)t}\tilde{P}_{k}[r\tilde{P}_{k}\tilde{P}_{ii}+r\tilde{P}\tilde{P}_{kii}+2\tilde{P}_{ki}\tilde{P}_{i}]

\displaystyle X_{t}=

\displaystyle e^{rG(0)t}[r\tilde{P}X_{kk}-r\tilde{P}\tilde{P}_{ik}^{2}+r\tilde{P}_{i}^{2}\tilde{P}_{kk}+2\tilde{P}_{k}X_{k}]

\displaystyle+re^{rG(0)t}|\tilde{P}_{k}|^{2}\underbrace{\big(G(e^{rG(0)t}\tilde{P})-G(0)+e^{rG(0)t}\tilde{P}G^{\prime}(e^{rG(0)t}\tilde{P})\big)}_{\leq 0}-\epsilon.

Case 1, interior estimate.
At an interior maximum point
(x_{0},t_{0})
of

and

X_{k}=0.

Hence, we obtain

X_{t}\leq e^{rG(0)t}\tilde{P}_{i}^{2}\tilde{P}_{kk}-\epsilon.

(2.25)

It is enough to show

(2.26)

By rotating the coordinates, we can assume that

\tilde{P}_{N}\geq 0,\quad\tilde{P}_{i}=0,\ i=1,2...,N-1

at the point
(x_{0},t_{0})
. Next,
\tilde{P}_{N}

and
\tilde{P}_{N}=0

For the case of

The

\tilde{P}\tilde{P}_{ij}V^{i}V^{j}\leq\frac{1}{2}\tilde{P}_{i}\tilde{P}_{j}V^{i}V^{j}.

Hence, we further get

For the case of

Finally, it concludes that (
2.25
) holds by means of the previously verified (

Case 2, boundary estimate
Assumde that its maximum occurs at a support boundary point
(x_{0},t_{0})
and

Due to

, it holds

X_{t}\leq e^{(m-1)G(0)t}[\tilde{P}_{N}^{2}\tilde{P}_{kk}+2r\tilde{P}_{N}X_{N}]-\epsilon\text{ at }(x_{0},t_{0}).

On account of
\tilde{P}_{N}>0
at the maximum point of
X
, we have

It further follows

X_{N}\tilde{P}_{N}\leq 0\text{ at }(x_{0},t_{0}).

We are going to show that

Since

we get

\tilde{P}_{NN}\leq 0\ \text{at }(x_{0},t_{0}).

Similar to the case of interior estimate, it holds by means of

-concavity that

In conclusion, combining

Sharp power concavity of initial pressure.

From the above discussions, we learn that the initial pressure solves an elliptic equation with Dirichlet boundary condition (

.

Assume that the initial domain
\Omega_{0}
is an convex set with smooth boundary. Then, the initial pressure

, solving the elliptic problem (

and

Then, by Theorem

is
\frac{1}{2}
-concave in
\Omega_{0}
. Lemma

further yields that

is

-concave for any

Next, we choose a sequence of smooth domains to show that the index

is the upper bound for the concavity of the solution to the elliptic equation (

) on the smooth domain. To this end, we define

with

We give a sequence of smooth convex domains satisfying

(3.5)

with

. By the Sobolev compactness embedding
[

]
, after extraction of a subsequence (still denoted by

In addition, due to (

) yields

0\leq P_{k+1}\leq P_{k}\quad\text{in }\Omega_{\infty}\text{ for }k=1,2,....

By means of Dini’s theorem, (

) and (

\|P_{k}-P_{\infty}\|_{\mathcal{C}(\Omega_{\infty})}\to 0\quad\text{as }k\to\infty.

It is immediate to conclude that

is the unique solution of the elliptic problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta P_{\infty}=G(P_{\infty}),\quad&\text{in }\Omega_{\infty},\\
P_{\infty}=0,\quad&\text{on }\partial\Omega_{\infty}.\end{cases}

(3.9)

We randomly fix
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
and suppose that the solution

of the elliptic problem (

) on the any given smooth convex domain is

-concave. By the definition of

-concavity, for

Let

(3.10)

Let
v:=x_{n}^{2}-a^{2}|x^{\prime}|^{2}
in
\Omega_{\infty}
, choosing
a\geq\sqrt{\frac{2+G(0)}{2N}}
, then

\Delta(v-P_{\infty})=2(1-a^{2}N)+G(P_{\infty})\leq-2(a^{2}N-1)+G(0)\leq 0.

Since
v\geq 0
on
\partial\Omega_{\infty}
, the compsrison principle yields

P_{\infty}\leq v\quad\text{in }\Omega_{\infty}.

We take
z=\frac{1}{2}e_{N}
for (
3.10
), then it holds

P_{\infty}(\frac{1}{2}e_{N})\leq\frac{P_{\infty}(t\frac{1}{2}e_{N})}{t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\leq\frac{v(t\frac{1}{2}e_{N})}{t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}=\frac{t^{2-\frac{1}{\alpha}}}{4}\quad\text{ for any }t\in(0,1).

(3.11)

One can directly compute

\liminf\limits_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{t^{2-\frac{1}{\alpha}}}{4}=0\quad\text{ for }\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1],

which implies the inequality (
3.11
) can not hold if
t
is small enough. The contradiction appears because
P_{\infty}(\frac{1}{2}e_{N})>0
can be derived by the strong maximum principle for the elliptic problem (
3.9
).

∎

Preservation of
\frac{1}{2}
-concavity.

This part is devoted to proving the preservation of
\frac{1}{2}
-concavity for the pressure of the Hele-Shaw problem (
1.4
). The key is to obtain the convexity for the spatial support of the pressure.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that the initial density
0\leq u_{0}\leq 1
is compactly supported and the support of the initial pressure
P_{0}
given by
\Omega_{0}:=\{u_{0}=1\}
is convex with smooth boundary, and suppose that the pressure
P
for the Hele-Shaw problem (
1.4
) is smooth to its support boundary, then
P
is
\alpha
-convex all the time for any
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
.

Proof.

At any fixed time, the stiff pressure
P
solves an elliptic equation (
3.4
), the concavity of this pressure is determined by the shape of the spatial domain
\Omega(t)
. Assume that the initial pressure
P_{0}
is
\frac{1}{2}
-concave and the stiff pressure
P
is smooth to its support boundary.

If
P
is not
\frac{1}{2}
-concave all the time, there exists some
\delta>0
, the time
t_{0}
, the unit vector
\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega(t_{0})}
such that

(\sqrt{P})_{\alpha^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime}}=\delta\quad\text{at }(x_{0},t_{0}).

(3.12)

And furthermore, it holds for any unit vector
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
that

(\sqrt{P})_{\alpha\alpha}=\frac{P_{\alpha\alpha}}{2\sqrt{P}}-\frac{P_{\alpha}^{2}}{4P^{\frac{3}{2}}}\leq\delta\quad\text{in }\overline{\Omega(t)}\times\{0\leq t\leq t_{0}\}.

(3.13)

Without loss of generality, assume that
e_{N}=(0,...,1)
is the normal vector at
(y_{0},s_{0})
for any
y_{0}\in\partial\Omega(s_{0})
. We parameterize the nearby of
x_{0}
for the surface
\partial\Omega(s_{0})
by
(x^{\prime},\gamma(x^{\prime},s))
with

P_{\alpha}(y_{0},s_{0})=\gamma_{\alpha}(y_{0}^{\prime},s_{0})=0

for all unit vector satisfying
\alpha\bot e_{N}
,
and Hopf’s lemma for elliptic problem (
3.4
) yields

c_{0}\leq P_{N}(x_{0},s_{0})\leq\frac{1}{c_{0}}\text{ for some }c_{0}>0.

By means of l’Hospital’s rule, we have

\displaystyle 0=\lim\limits_{x\to y_{0}}2\delta\sqrt{P(x,s_{0})}\geq

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{x\to y_{0}}[P_{\alpha\alpha}-\frac{P_{\alpha}^{2}}{2P}]

(3.14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{x\to y_{0}}[P_{\alpha\alpha}-\frac{P_{N\alpha}P_{\alpha}}{P_{N}}]=P_{\alpha\alpha}(y_{0},s_{0}).

Thanks to

P(x^{\prime},\gamma(x^{\prime},s),s_{0})=0,\quad x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\cap B_{\varepsilon}(0)\text{ for some }\varepsilon>0,

we get

P_{\alpha\alpha}+2P_{N\alpha}\gamma_{\alpha}(x^{\prime},s)+P_{NN}|\gamma_{\alpha}(x^{\prime},s)|^{2}+P_{N}\gamma_{\alpha\alpha}(x^{\prime},s)=0.

Hence, at
(y_{0},s_{0})
, it holds for all unit vector satisfying
\alpha\bot e_{N}
that

\gamma_{\alpha\alpha}(x_{0}^{\prime},s_{0})=-\frac{P_{\alpha\alpha}}{P_{N}}\geq 0

which implies that
\Omega(s_{0})
is convex for all
0\leq s_{0}\leq t_{0}
. Using Theorem
A.1
for the elliptic equation with Dirichlet boundary condition,
P(t)
is
\frac{1}{2}
-concave on
\Omega(t)
for
0\leq t\leq t_{0}
, which is contradicted with (
3.12
). Hence, the pressure
P
is
\frac{1}{2}
-concave all the time.

Finally, Lemma
A.1
verifies that
P
is
\alpha
-concave for
0\leq\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2}
all the while.
∎

4
Conclusions and perspectives

We investigate and compare the geometric properties of the porous medium type reaction-diffusion equation (
1.1
) and the corresponding Hele-Shaw problem (
1.4
). Precisely, we proved that
\frac{1}{2}
is the sharp index for the preservation of the concavity over time for the pressure of these two free boundary problems. The key distinction lies in the role of this index: for the porous medium-type reaction-diffusion equation (
1.1
), the index
\frac{1}{2}
uniquely guarantees the preservation of concavity over time. In contrast, for the Hele-Shaw problem (
1.4
),
\frac{1}{2}
acts as an upper bound on the index to preserve concavity as the time progresses. Furthermore, using the concave property, we establish non-degenerate estimates for the porous medium type reaction-diffusion equation, which means that the spatially Lipschitz continuity is sharp.

Based on the results of this paper, the further problem is to prove that the pressure is smooth to its support boundary as
[
10
,
12
]
for the two free boundary problems, which is the a priori assumption in the processes of paper. Furthermore, if we take the nutrient into account for the two free boundary problems
[
35
]
, how are the geometric properties including the concavities of the pressure and free boundary regularities? There were some results
[
23
,
26
,
8
]
on the free boundary regularities for the Hele-Shaw problem of tumor growth with/without nutrient diffusion. For the tumor growth model with nutrient of porous medium type, the study of free boundary and its geometric property is a completely open problem.

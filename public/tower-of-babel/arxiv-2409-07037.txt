A Reynolds-semi-robust and pressure robust Hybrid High-Order method for the time dependent incompressible Navier–Stokes equations on general meshes
Daniel Castanon Quiroz, Daniele A. Di Pietro

Abstract
In this work we develop and analyze a Reynolds-semi-robust and pressure-robust Hybrid High-Order (HHO) discretization of the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations. Reynolds-semi-robustness refers to the fact that, under suitable regularity assumptions, the right-hand side of the velocity error estimate does not depend on the inverse of the viscosity. This property is obtained here through a penalty term which involves a subtle projection of the convective term on a subgrid space constructed element by element. The estimated convergence order for the L ∞ ​ ( L 2 ) L^{\infty}(L^{2}) - and L 2 ​ ( energy ) L^{2}(\text{energy}) -norm of the velocity is h k + 1 2 h^{k+\frac{1}{2}} , which matches the best results for continuous and discontinuous Galerkin methods and corresponds to the one expected for HHO methods in convection-dominated regimes. Two-dimensional numerical results on a variety of polygonal meshes complete the exposition. Key words: Hybrid High-Order methods, time-dependent incompressible flow, general meshes, Re-semi-robust error estimates, pressure robustness MSC 2010: 65N08, 65N30, 65N12, 35Q30, 76D05

Introduction

We consider the time-dependent incompressible Navier–Stokes equations:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}-\nu\Delta\boldsymbol{u}+(\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}+\nabla p

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{f}

\displaystyle\text{in }(0,t_{\rm F}]\times\Omega,

(1a)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{in }(0,t_{\rm F}]\times\Omega,

(1b)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{0}

\displaystyle\text{on }[0,t_{\rm F}]\times\partial\Omega,

(1c)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}(0,\cdot)

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{u}_{0}(\cdot)

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(1d)

where

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, for
d\in\{2,3\}
, denotes an open, bounded, simply connected polyhedral domain with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
and
t_{\rm F}>0
denotes the final time.
In addition,

\boldsymbol{u}:[0,t_{\rm F}]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}

is the fluid velocity field,
p:(0,t_{\rm F}]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the (zero-average) kinematic pressure,

\boldsymbol{u}_{0}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
represents a given initial condition,
and
\boldsymbol{f}:(0,t_{\rm F}]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
represents a given body force. The fluid is assumed to be Newtonian with constant kinematic viscosity
\nu>0
.

In this work we propose a “Reynolds-semi robust” and pressure-robust Hybrid-High Order (HHO) method on general meshes to approximate the solution
of the weak form of problem (
1
).
A numerical scheme is considered “Reynolds-semi robust”
[
45
]
if its velocity error estimates are independent of the Reynolds number (or
\nu^{-1}
).
On the other hand, pressure robustness means that the velocity error estimates are independent of the pressure, a property whose relevance
was first emphasized in
[
40
,
38
]
. Recently, a detailed analysis of vortex-dominated flows has been carried out in
[
36
]
, where the authors conclude that pressure robustness is a crucial prerequisite for the accurate discretisation of non-trivial Navier–Stokes flows; numerical tests where pressure robust methods significantly outperform non-pressure robust methods for transient incompressible flows at high Reynolds numbers are provided, focusing on simplicial meshes.

Reynolds-semi robust numerical schemes have recently made the object of several works.
To obtain this kind of estimates, it is standard to assume additional regularity condition on
\nabla\boldsymbol{u}
. For instance, in the analysis of a continuous interior penalty finite element method
[
14
]
, assuming

\boldsymbol{u}\in L^{\infty}(0,t_{\rm F};\boldsymbol{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega))

a velocity error estimate in the

L^{\infty}(0,t_{\rm F};\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Omega))
-norm was obtained.
Other Reynolds-semi robust numerical schemes using simplicial meshes
have been proposed
[
2
,
4
,
18
,
32
,
31
,
34
,
37
]
. In particular, we refer to
[
45
]
for an outstanding review and further insight into the importance of Reynolds-semi robust numerical methods. At the time of the writing of this work, the best known velocity error estimate in the
{L^{\infty}(0,t_{\rm F};\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Omega))}
-norm is of order
h^{k+\frac{1}{2}}
(where
k
denotes the order of the polynomial approximation and
h
is the mesh size), for instance see the works of
[
4
,
34
,
37
]
, where Reynolds-semi robust numerical methods are proposed which additionally satisfy the pressure robustness property.

In recent years, the mathematical community has become interested in developing numerical schemes that can make use of
general polygonal and polyhedral meshes, as opposed to more standard triangular/quadrilateral (tetrahedral/hexahedral) meshes.
A representative but by far non exhaustive sample concerning incompressible flow problems includes
[
24
,
25
,
27
,
35
,
10
,
7
,
5
,
48
]
;
see also
[
12
,
17
]
concerning non-Newtonian fluids and
[
1
]
concerning the coupling with the heat equation.
Regarding pressure robust methods on general meshes for the Stokes and Navier–Stokes equations, some work has recently been
done using the Virtual Element method, generalized barycentric
coordinates, the staggered Discontinuous Garlekin method, HHO methods, and Discrete de Rham methods; see, e.g.,
[
16
,
41
,
47
,
49
,
30
,
39
,
11
,
19
,
46
,
22
]
. Pressure-robust HHO methods for the Stokes and Navier–Stokes problem have been proposed in
[
26
,
15
,
16
]
; see also
[
9
]
for variants with hybrid pressure.
To the authors’ knowledge, the development of Reynolds-semi robust and pressure-robust numerical schemes on general meshes has not yet been adressed.
The present work fills this gap.
To achieve this, the proposed method uses a slight variation of the divergence-preserving velocity reconstruction proposed by the same authors in
[
16
]
.
Contrary to
[
16
]
, however, we use here the convective form of the nonlinear term in the equation (
1a
) in the spirit of
[
37
,
45
]
, where
\boldsymbol{H}_{\text{div}}
-conforming spaces are used.
Reynolds-semi robustness is obtained through a
new term which penalizes the jumps of a potential operator within a decomposition of some discrete piecewise polinomial spaces (cf. (
14
) and (
15
) below).
With these ingredients, we prove a velocity error estimate in the
{L^{\infty}(0,t_{\rm F};\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Omega))}
-norm of order
h^{k+\frac{1}{2}}
, which equals the best known velocity error estimate on simplicial meshes and corresponds to the typical order of convergence of HHO methods in convectoin-dominated regimes
[
21
]
.

In Section

In Section

Section

Finally, in Section
6
we present numerical experiments to verify our theoretical results.

Continuous setting

Throughout the paper, given an open bounded set
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
as well as two integers
m\geq 0

, we use the Sobolev space
W^{m,p}(D)
for scalar-valued functions with associated norm
\|{\cdot}\|_{W^{m,p}(D)}

|{\cdot}|_{W^{m,p}(D)}

Spaces of vector- and tensor-valued functions are indicated with bold letters.
In the case

.
Additionally, the closed subspaces

and

-functions with zero average in

Given a Banach space
X

t>0

we denote by
L^{p}(0,t;X)
,

, the classical Bochner space.
In the case

, we often use the abbreviation

L^{p}(X)\coloneq L^{p}(0,t_{\rm F};X)
.

Letting
\boldsymbol{U}\coloneq{\boldsymbol{H}}^{1}_{0}({\Omega})
and
P\coloneq L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
, we consider the following weak form of problem (
1
):
Find
\boldsymbol{u}:[0,t_{\rm F}]\rightarrow\boldsymbol{U}
and
p:(0,t_{\rm F}]\to P

with
\boldsymbol{u}(0)=\boldsymbol{u}_{0}\in\boldsymbol{U}
, such that it holds, for all
(\boldsymbol{v},q)\in\boldsymbol{U}\times P
and almost every
t\in(0,t_{\rm F})
,

({d_{t}}\boldsymbol{u}(t),\boldsymbol{v})+\nu a(\boldsymbol{u}(t),\boldsymbol{v})+t(\boldsymbol{u}(t),\boldsymbol{u}(t),\boldsymbol{v})+b(\boldsymbol{v},p(t))-b(\boldsymbol{u}(t),q)=\ell(\boldsymbol{f}(t),\boldsymbol{v}),

(2)

with
(\cdot,\cdot)
denoting the standard
\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\Omega)
-product, while the bilinear forms
a:\boldsymbol{U}\times\boldsymbol{U}\to\mathbb{R}
,
b:\boldsymbol{U}\times P\to\mathbb{R}
, and
\ell:{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}({\Omega})\times\boldsymbol{U}\to\mathbb{R}
are defined by

a(\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{v})\coloneq\int_{\Omega}\nabla\boldsymbol{w}:\nabla\boldsymbol{v},\quad b(\boldsymbol{v},q)\coloneq-\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v})q,\quad\ell(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{v})\coloneq\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{f}\cdot\boldsymbol{v},

and the trilinear form
t:\boldsymbol{U}\times\boldsymbol{U}\times\boldsymbol{U}\to\mathbb{R}
is such that

t(\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{z})\coloneq\int_{\Omega}((\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{v})\cdot\boldsymbol{z}.

3.2
Local and broken spaces and projectors

Let
X
denote a mesh element or face and, for an integer
l\geq 0
, denote by

\mathcal{P}^{l}(X)
the space spanned by the restrictions to
X

of polynomials in the space variables of total degree
\leq l
.
The
L^{2}
-orthogonal projector
\pi_{X}^{l}:L^{1}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{P}^{l}(X)
is such that, for all
\zeta\in L^{1}(X)
,

\int_{X}(\zeta-\pi_{X}^{l}\zeta)w=0\qquad\forall w\in\mathcal{P}^{l}(X).

Vector and matrix versions of the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projector are obtained by applying
\pi_{X}^{l}
component-wise, and are both denoted with the bold symbol
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{X}^{l}
in what follows.
Optimal approximation properties for the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projector are proved in
[
28
, Appendix A.2]
; see also
[
20
, Chapter 1]
, where these estimates are extended to non-star shaped elements.
Specifically, let
s\in\{0,\dots,l+1\}
and
r\in[1,+\infty]
.
Then, it holds, with hidden constant only depending on
l
,
s
,
r
, and the mesh regularity parameter:
For all
T\in{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}
, all
\zeta\in W^{s,r}(T)
, and all
m\in\{0,\dots,s\}
,

|\zeta-\pi_{T}^{l}\zeta|_{W^{m,r}(T)}\lesssim h_{T}^{s-m}|\zeta|_{W^{s,r}(T)},

(4)

and, if
s\geq 1
,

h_{T}^{\frac{1}{r}}\|\zeta-\pi_{T}^{l}\zeta\|_{L^{r}(\partial T)}\lesssim h_{T}^{s}|\zeta|_{W^{s,r}(T)}.

(5)

At the global level, the space of broken polynomial functions on
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
of total degree
\leq l
is denoted by
\mathcal{P}^{l}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})
, and
\pi_{h}^{l}
is the corresponding
L^{2}
-orthogonal projector such that, for all
\zeta\in L^{1}(\Omega)
,
(\pi_{h}^{l}\zeta)_{|T}\coloneq\pi_{T}^{l}\zeta_{|T}
for all
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
.
Regularity requirements in error estimates will be expressed in terms of the broken Sobolev spaces

, spanned by functions in

the restriction of which to every
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
is in

.
We additionally set, as usual,
H^{s}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})\coloneq W^{s,2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})

3.3
Discrete spaces and norms

Let a polynomial degree
k\geq 0
be fixed and set

{k}^{\star}\coloneq\begin{cases}k&\text{if $k\in\{0,1\}$,}\\
k+1&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

We then define a variant of the usual HHO space as follows

\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h}^{k}\coloneq\big\{\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}=((\boldsymbol{v}_{T})_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}},(\boldsymbol{v}_{F})_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}}):\\
\mbox{$\boldsymbol{v}_{T}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}^{{k}^{\star}}(T)$ for all $T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}$ and
$\boldsymbol{v}_{F}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}^{k}(F)$ for all $F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}$}\big\}.

(6)

The restrictions of
\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h}^{k}
and
\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\in\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h}^{k}
to a generic mesh element

are respectively denoted by
\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{T}^{k}
and
\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}=(\boldsymbol{v}_{T},(\boldsymbol{v}_{F})_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{T}})

The vector of polynomials corresponding to a smooth function over

is obtained via the global interpolation operator
\uline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{h}^{k}:{\boldsymbol{H}}^{1}({\Omega})\rightarrow\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h}^{k}

,

\uline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{h}^{k}\boldsymbol{v}\coloneq(({\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{T}^{k^{\star}}\boldsymbol{v}_{|T})_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}},({\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{F}^{k}\boldsymbol{v}_{|F})_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}}).

T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, collecting the components on
T
and its faces, is denoted by
\uline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{T}^{k}
.
We furnish
\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h}^{k}
with the discrete

-like seminorm such that, for all

\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\in\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h}^{k}
,

\|\underline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\|_{1,h}\coloneq\left(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|\underline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{1,T}^{2}\right)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}},

(7)

where, for all
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
,

\|\underline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{1,T}\coloneq\left(\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(T)}^{2}+|\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}|_{1,\partial T}^{2}\right)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\mbox{ with }|\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}|_{1,\partial T}\coloneq\left(\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{T}}\!h_{F}^{-1}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{F}-\boldsymbol{v}_{T}\|^{2}_{{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}({F})}\right)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}.

(8)

The discrete spaces for the velocity and the pressure, respectively accounting for the wall boundary condition and the zero-average condition, are

\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h,0}^{k}\coloneq\left\{\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}=((\boldsymbol{v}_{T})_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}},(\boldsymbol{v}_{F})_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}})\in\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h}^{k}:\boldsymbol{v}_{F}=\boldsymbol{0}\quad\forall F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\rm b}\right\},\qquad P_{h}^{k}\coloneq\mathcal{P}^{k}({\cal{T}}_{h})\cap P.

For all
\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\in\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h}^{k}
, we denote by
{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}^{k^{\star}}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})

.
The following discrete Sobolev embeddings in

For all

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{r}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\|_{1,h}.

(9)

where the hidden constant is independent of both

4
A pressure-robust and Reynolds semi-robust HHO scheme

4.1
Divergence-preserving local velocity reconstruction

Following
[
23
]
, for any element
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
we define the discrete divergence operator
D_{T}^{k}:\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{T}^{k}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}^{k}(T)
such that, for all
\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\in\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{T}^{k}
and all
q\in\mathcal{P}^{k}(T)
,

\displaystyle\int_{T}D_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\,q

\displaystyle=-\int_{T}\boldsymbol{v}_{T}\cdot\nabla q+\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{T}}\int_{F}(\boldsymbol{v}_{F}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{TF})\,q.

(11)

The operator
D_{T}^{k}
satisfies the following crucial commutation property (see
[
20
, Eq. (8.21)]
):

D_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{T}^{k}\boldsymbol{v}=\pi_{T}^{k}(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v})\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in{\boldsymbol{H}}^{1}({T}).

(12)

To achieve pressure robustness, we proceed similarly to
[
16
, Section 2.4]
, constructing divergence-preserving velocity test functions which are then used for the discretization of the body force and the nonlinear term.
Let an element
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
be fixed and, for
\tau\in\mathfrak{T}_{T}
, denote by

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{RT}}^{k}(\tau)
the classical Raviart–Thomas–Nédélec space of degree
k
on
\tau

[
43
,
42
]
.
We recall that a function in
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{RT}}^{k}(\tau)
is uniquely determined by its polynomial moments of degree up to
(k-1)
inside
\tau
and the polynomial moments of degree
k
of its normal components on the simplicial faces of
\tau
, collected in the set
\mathfrak{F}_{\tau}
.
We introduce the Raviart–Thomas–Nédélec space of degree
k
on the matching simplicial submesh
\mathfrak{T}_{T}
of

is well-defined, since its normal components across mesh interfaces are continuous as a consequence of (
16a
) combined with the single-valuedness of interface unknowns.

To discretize the unsteady term in (
2
), for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
we introduce the bilinear form
a_{\text{R},T}:\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{T}^{k}\times\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{T}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
defined as follows:

a_{\text{R},T}(\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T},\uline{\boldsymbol{w}}_{T})\coloneq\int_{T}\boldsymbol{R}_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{w}}_{T}+s_{\text{R},T}(\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T},\uline{\boldsymbol{w}}_{T}),

(31)

where the second term is the following stabilisation bilinear form:

s_{\text{R},T}(\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T},\uline{\boldsymbol{w}}_{T})\coloneq\int_{T}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{R},T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\cdot\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{R},T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{w}}_{T}+\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{T}}h_{F}\int_{F}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{R},TF}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\cdot\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{R},TF}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{w}}_{T},

with difference operators
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{R},T}^{k}:\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{T}^{k}\to\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}^{k^{\star}}(T)
and, for all
F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{T},\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{R},TF}^{k}:\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{T}^{k}\to\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}^{k}(F)
such that, for all
\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\in\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}^{k}_{T}
,

\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{R},T}^{k}(\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T})\coloneq{\boldsymbol{\pi}}^{k^{\star}}_{T}(\boldsymbol{R}_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}-\boldsymbol{v}_{T})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{R},TF}^{k}(\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T})\coloneq{\boldsymbol{\pi}}^{k}_{F}(\boldsymbol{R}_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}-\boldsymbol{v}_{F})\quad\forall F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{T}.

(32)

We also introduce the
\boldsymbol{L}^{2}
-like norm
\|{\cdot}\|_{\text{R},T}:\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{T}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
induced by
a_{\text{R},T}(\cdot,\cdot)
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\|\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{\text{R},T}\coloneq a_{\text{R},T}(\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T},\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(33)

Finally, we introduce the global bilinear form
a_{\text{R},h}:\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h}^{k}\times\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
, and the corresponding global norm
\|{\cdot}\|_{\text{R},h}:\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
defined, respectively, by:
For all
(\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},\uline{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h})\in\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h}^{k}\times\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h}^{k}
,

a_{\text{R},h}(\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},\uline{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h})\coloneq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}a_{\text{R},T}(\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T},\uline{\boldsymbol{w}}_{T}),\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\|_{\text{R},h}^{2}\coloneq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{\text{R},T}^{2}.

The following Lemma summarizes some key properties relevant for the analysis.

Lemma 4

(Properties of
\|{\cdot}\|_{\text{R},T}
)
.

For all
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
and all
\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\in\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{T}^{k}
, it holds

\|\boldsymbol{v}_{T}\|_{{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}({T})}{+h_{T}\|\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{1,T}}\lesssim\|\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{\text{R},T}.

(34)

Remark 5

(Norm
\|{\cdot}\|_{\text{R},h}
)
.

A straightforward consequence of the above results is that the map
\|{\cdot}\|_{\text{R},h}:\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
is a norm in the space
\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h,0}^{k}
.

Proof of Lemma
4
.

Using the fact that
\boldsymbol{v}_{T}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}^{k^{\star}}(T)
along with (
24
) for
n=2
, we get

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{v}_{T}\|_{{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}({T})}^{2}=\|{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{T}^{k^{\star}}\boldsymbol{v}_{T}\|_{{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}({T})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{T}^{k^{\star}}(\boldsymbol{v}_{T}-\boldsymbol{R}_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T})\|_{{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}({T})}^{2}+\|{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{T}^{k^{\star}}\boldsymbol{R}_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}({T})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{T}^{k^{\star}}(\boldsymbol{v}_{T}-\boldsymbol{R}_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T})\|_{{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}({T})}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{R}_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}({T})}^{2}\leq\|\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{\text{R},T}^{2},

where we have used the
\boldsymbol{L}^{2}
-boundedness of
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{T}^{k^{\star}}
in the second line and the definition (
33
) of
\|{\cdot}\|_{\text{R},T}
to conclude.

To bound the second term in the left-hand side of (
34
) we preliminarily observe that, for any face
F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{T}
, it holds

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{F}-{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(F)}

\displaystyle=\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{F}-{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{F}^{k}\boldsymbol{R}_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}+{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{F}^{k}\boldsymbol{R}_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}-{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(F)}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{F}^{k}({\boldsymbol{v}}_{F}-\boldsymbol{R}_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T})\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(F)}+\|{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{F}^{k}\boldsymbol{R}_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}-{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(F)}\eqcolon{\mathfrak{I}}_{1}+{\mathfrak{I}}_{2},

where, in the second step, we have used triangle inequalities and then

{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{F}^{k}\boldsymbol{v}_{F}=\boldsymbol{v}_{F}
(since
\boldsymbol{v}_{F}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}^{k}(F)
) in the first term.
Squaring the above inequality, using (
24
) with
n=2
in the right hand side, and multiplying by
h_{F}
both sides,
we get

\displaystyle h_{F}\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{F}-{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(F)}^{2}\lesssim h_{F}{\mathfrak{I}}_{1}^{2}+h_{F}{\mathfrak{I}}_{2}^{2}.

(35)

The
first term above is bounded as follows:

\displaystyle h_{F}{\mathfrak{I}}_{1}^{2}\overset{\eqref{def:deltasR}}{=}h_{F}\|\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{R},TF}^{k}(\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T})\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(F)}^{2}\overset{\eqref{def:normRT}}{\leq}\|\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{\text{R},T}^{2}.

(36)

For the second term in (
35
), we begin as follows:

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{I}}_{2}

\displaystyle=\|{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{F}^{k}\boldsymbol{R}_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}+{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{T}^{k^{\star}}\boldsymbol{R}_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}-{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{T}^{k^{\star}}\boldsymbol{R}_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}-{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(F)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{R}_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(F)}+\|{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{T}^{k^{\star}}\boldsymbol{R}_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(F)}+\|\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{R},T}^{k}(\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T})\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(F)},

where in the last step, we have used triangle inequalities, the
\boldsymbol{L}^{2}
-boundedness of
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{F}^{k}
, and the definition (
32
) of
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{R},T}^{k}
.
Denoting by
\tau_{\sigma}
the simplex in
\mathfrak{T}_{T}
which contains
\sigma\in\mathfrak{F}_{F}
and using a discrete trace inequality along with
h_{F}\leq h_{T}\lesssim h_{\tau_{\sigma}}
(the second bound being a consequence of mesh regularity), we get

h_{F}\|\boldsymbol{R}_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(F)}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{F}_{F}}h_{\tau_{\sigma}}\|\boldsymbol{R}_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\sigma)}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{F}_{F}}\|\boldsymbol{R}_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(\tau_{\sigma})}^{2}\leq\|\boldsymbol{R}_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(T)}^{2},

where, in the last step, we have used the fact that
{\bigcup_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{F}_{F}}}\tau_{\sigma}\subset T
.
Using the same process, we get

h_{F}\|{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{T}^{k^{\star}}\boldsymbol{R}_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(F)}{{}^{2}}\lesssim\|{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{T}^{k^{\star}}\boldsymbol{R}_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(T)}^{2}\leq\|\boldsymbol{R}_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(T)}^{2}
,
where we have used the
\boldsymbol{L}^{2}
-boundedness of
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{T}^{k^{\star}}
to conclude.
Thus,

\displaystyle h_{F}{\mathfrak{I}}_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{R}_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(T)}^{2}+h_{F}\|\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{R},T}^{k}(\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T})\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(F)}^{2}\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{R}_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(T)}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{R},T}^{k}(\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T})\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(T)}^{2}{\overset{\eqref{def:normRT}}{\leq}\|\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{\text{R},T}^{2}},

where, in the second step, we have used a discrete trace inequality.
Using this bound along with (
36
) in (
35
), we get

\displaystyle h_{F}\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{F}-{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(F)}^{2}\lesssim\|\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{\text{R},T}^{2}.

(37)

Recalling the definition (
8
) of

\|\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{1,T}
and
using a discrete inverse inequality,
we have that

h_{T}^{2}\|\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{1,T}^{2}{\lesssim}\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(T)}^{2}+\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{T}}h_{F}\|\boldsymbol{v}_{F}-\boldsymbol{v}_{T}\|^{2}_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(F)}\lesssim\|\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\|_{\text{R},T}^{2}

where, in the second step, we have used additionally the inequality

valid for regular meshes while, in the last, step we have used (
34
) to bound the first term above and (

) for the second.
Taking the square root, we conclude.
∎

4.2
Viscous term and pressure-velocity coupling

Let an element
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
be fixed.
We define the local gradient reconstruction
{\boldsymbol{G}_{T}^{k}}:\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{T}^{k}\rightarrow{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}^{k}(T)}
such that, for all
\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\in\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{T}^{k}
and all
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\in{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}^{k}(T)}
,

\int_{T}\boldsymbol{G}^{l}_{T}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}:{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=-\int_{T}\boldsymbol{v}_{T}\cdot(\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{\tau}})+\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{T}}\int_{F}\boldsymbol{v}_{F}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\boldsymbol{n}_{TF}.

A global gradient reconstruction
{\boldsymbol{G}_{h}^{k}}:\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h}^{k}\to{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}
can be defined setting, for all
\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\in\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h}^{k}
,
(\boldsymbol{G}_{h}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})_{|T}\coloneq\boldsymbol{G}_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}
for all
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
.

The viscous term and the pressure-velocity coupling are essentially the same as in the standard HHO method; see, e.g.,
[
27
,
10
]
.
We briefly recall them here to make the exposition self-contained.
The viscous bilinear form
a_{h}
:
\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h}^{k}\times\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h}^{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is such that, for all
\uline{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h},\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},\in\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h}^{k}
,

a_{h}(\uline{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h},\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})\coloneq\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\left[\int_{T}\boldsymbol{G}_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{w}}_{T}:\boldsymbol{G}_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}+s_{T}(\uline{\boldsymbol{w}}_{T},\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T})\right],

where, for any
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
,
s_{T}:\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{T}^{k}\times\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{T}^{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

denotes a local stabilization bilinear form designed according to the principles of
[
20
, Assumption 2.4]
,
so that the following properties hold:

(i)

Stability and boundedness.

There exists
C_{a}>0
independent of
h
(and, clearly, also of
\nu
) such that, for all
\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\in\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h}^{k}
,

C_{a}\|\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\|_{1,h}^{2}\leq a_{h}(\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})\leq C_{a}^{-1}\|\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\|_{1,h}^{2}.

(38)

(ii)

Consistency.
For all
\boldsymbol{w}\in\boldsymbol{U}\cap\boldsymbol{H}^{k+2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})
such that
\Delta\boldsymbol{w}\in{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}({\Omega})
, it holds

\|{\cal{E}}_{a,h}(\boldsymbol{w};\cdot)\|_{1,h,*}\lesssim h^{k+1}|\boldsymbol{w}|_{\boldsymbol{H}^{k+2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})},

(39)

where the linear form

{\cal{E}}_{a,h}(\boldsymbol{w};\cdot):\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h,0}^{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

representing the consistency error is such that

{\cal{E}}_{a,h}(\boldsymbol{w};\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})\coloneq-\int_{\Omega}\Delta\boldsymbol{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{v}_{h}-a_{h}(\uline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{h}^{k}\boldsymbol{w},\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}){\qquad\forall\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\in\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h,0}^{k}}.

(40)

A classical example of stabilization bilinear form along with the proofs of properties (
38
) and (
39
) can be found in
[
20
, Section 5.1]
, to which we refer for further details.

Recalling the definition (
11
) of the local divergence
D_{T}^{k}
, the global pressure-velocity coupling bilinear form
b_{h}:\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h,0}^{k}\times\mathcal{P}^{k}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is such that, for all
(\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{h})\in\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h,0}^{k}\times\mathcal{P}^{k}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})
,

b_{h}(\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},q_{h}):=-\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{T}D_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}\,q_{T},

(41)

where
q_{T}\coloneq q_{h|T}
.
The properties of
b_{h}
relevant for the analysis can be found in
[
20
, Lemma 8.12]
.

4.3
Body force

The discretization of the body force uses the divergence-preserving velocity reconstruction introduced in Section
4.1
.
Specifically, we define the bilinear form
\ell_{h}:{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}({\Omega})\times\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h}^{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that, for any
\boldsymbol{\phi}\in{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}({\Omega})
and any
\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\in\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h}^{k}

\ell_{h}(\boldsymbol{\phi},\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})\coloneq\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\phi}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_{h}^{k}\underline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}.

(42)

Proceeding as in the proof of
[
16
, Lemma 4]
(the only difference being the definition of
{\boldsymbol{R}}_{T}^{k}
, as discussed in Remark
1
), we can prove the following consistency property:
For all
\boldsymbol{\phi}\in{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}({\Omega})\cap\boldsymbol{H}^{k}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})

\|{\cal{E}}_{\ell,h}(\boldsymbol{\phi};\cdot)\|_{1,h,*}\lesssim h^{k+1}|\boldsymbol{\phi}|_{\boldsymbol{H}^{k}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})},

where the linear form
{\cal{E}}_{\ell,h}(\boldsymbol{\phi};\cdot):\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h}^{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
representing the consistency error associated with

\mathcal{E}_{\ell,h}(\boldsymbol{\phi};\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})\coloneq\ell_{h}(\boldsymbol{\phi},\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})-\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\phi}\cdot\boldsymbol{v}_{h}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\cdot(\boldsymbol{R}_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}-{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}).

Lemma 8

(Poincaré inequality over a simplex)
.

Let a mesh element
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, a simplex
\tau\in\mathfrak{T}_{T}
, and integer
l\geq 0
be given.
Then, for any
q\in\mathcal{P}^{l}(\tau)
vanishing at one of the vertices of
\tau
, the following holds:

\|q\|_{{L}^{2}({\tau})}\lesssim h_{\tau}\|\nabla q\|_{{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}({\tau})}.

(54)

Proof.

The case
l=0
is trivial. Then we separate the remaining cases
when
l=1
, and
l>1
. We begin with
l=1
.
By assumption, there exists a vertex of
\tau
denoted by
\boldsymbol{x}_{\tau}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

such that
q(\boldsymbol{x}_{\tau})=0
.
Thus,
q
can be expressed as
q(\boldsymbol{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{i}(x_{i}-x_{\tau,i})

where
a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
, and
x_{i}
and
x_{\tau,i}
are the Cartesian coordinates of
\boldsymbol{x}
, and
\boldsymbol{x}_{\tau}
, respectively.
We write

\|q\|_{{L}^{2}({\tau})}^{2}=\int_{\tau}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{i}(x_{i}-x_{\tau,i})\right)^{2}\overset{\eqref{eq.l1l2.bound}}{\lesssim}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{\tau}a_{i}^{2}(x_{i}-x_{\tau,i})^{2}\leq h_{\tau}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{\tau}a_{i}^{2}=h_{\tau}\|\nabla q\|_{{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}({\tau})}^{2}.

Passing to the square root, we get (
54
).
Now, to prove the case
l>1
, let
q\in\mathcal{P}^{l}(\tau)
and denote by
\hat{q}
the standard nodal interpolate of
q
on
\mathcal{P}^{1}(\tau)
(see, e.g.,
[
29
, Eq. (1.36)]
).
Then, it is inferred that

\|q\|_{{L}^{2}({\tau})}{\leq}\|q-\hat{q}\|_{{L}^{2}({\tau})}+\|\hat{q}\|_{{L}^{2}({\tau})}\lesssim h_{\tau}|q|_{H^{1}(\tau)}+\|\hat{q}\|_{{L}^{2}({\tau})}\lesssim h_{\tau}\|\nabla q\|_{{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}({\tau})},

where in the first step we have used a triangle inequality,
in the second step standard approximation properties of
\hat{q}
(
[
29
, Theorem (1.103)]
) followed by an inverse inequality to write
\|q-\hat{q}\|_{{L}^{2}({\tau})}\lesssim h_{\tau}^{2}|q|_{H^{2}(\tau)}\lesssim h_{\tau}|q|_{H^{1}(\tau)}
,
and in the last step the fact that
\hat{q}\in\mathcal{P}^{1}(\tau)

and
\hat{q}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\tau})=0
, so we can use (
54
), since this case has already been proved.
∎

4.5
Convective term

Let an element
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
be fixed.
For every simplicial face
\sigma\in\mathfrak{F}_{h}
, we introduce an arbitrary but fixed ordering of the simplicial elements
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
such that
\sigma\subset\partial\tau_{1}\cap\partial\tau_{2}
, and let
\boldsymbol{n}_{\sigma}\coloneq\boldsymbol{n}_{\tau_{1}\sigma}=-\boldsymbol{n}_{\tau_{2}\sigma}
, where
\boldsymbol{n}_{\tau_{i}\sigma},i\in\{1,2\}
,
denotes the unit vector normal to
\sigma
pointing out of
\tau_{i}
(see Figure
1
).
With this convention, for every scalar-valued function
\zeta
admitting a possibly two-valued trace on
\sigma
, we define the jump and average of
\zeta
across
\sigma
respectively as

\llbracket\zeta\rrbracket_{\sigma}\coloneq\zeta_{|\tau_{1}}-\zeta_{|\tau_{2}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\{\zeta\}_{\sigma}\coloneq\frac{1}{2}\left(\zeta_{|\tau_{1}}+\zeta_{|\tau_{2}}\right).

(57)

For any boundary simplicial face
\sigma\subset F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm b}}
, we set
\llbracket\zeta\rrbracket_{\sigma}\coloneq\{\zeta\}_{\sigma}\coloneq\zeta
. When applied to vector- or tensor-valued functions, the jump and average operators act component-wise.

We introduce the global function
t_{h}:\left[\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h}^{k}\right]^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
such that, for all
(\uline{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h},\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},\uline{\boldsymbol{z}}_{h})\in\left[\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{h}^{k}\right]^{3}
,

\displaystyle t_{h}(\uline{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h},\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},\uline{\boldsymbol{z}}_{h})\coloneq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{R}_{h}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{R}_{h}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{R}_{h}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{z}}_{h}-\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{F}_{h}^{\rm i}}\int_{\sigma}(\boldsymbol{R}_{h}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{\sigma})\llbracket\boldsymbol{R}_{h}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\rrbracket_{\sigma}\cdot\{\boldsymbol{R}_{h}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{z}}_{h}\}_{\sigma}

(58)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{F}_{h}^{\rm i}}\int_{\sigma}\frac{1}{2}|\boldsymbol{R}_{h}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{\sigma}|\llbracket\boldsymbol{R}_{h}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\rrbracket_{\sigma}\cdot\llbracket\boldsymbol{R}_{h}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{z}}_{h}\rrbracket_{\sigma}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{F}_{T}^{\rm i}}t_{T,\sigma}^{k}(\uline{\boldsymbol{w}}_{T},\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T},\uline{\boldsymbol{z}}_{T}).

The first term in the second line is the usual upwind stabilization.
The form
t_{T,\sigma}^{k}:\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{T}^{k}\times\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{T}^{k}\times\uline{\boldsymbol{U}}_{T}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
also works as a penalty term and, setting
{\boldsymbol{w}}_{T}^{0}\coloneq{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{T}^{0}\boldsymbol{w}_{T}
, it is defined as follows:

t_{T,\sigma}^{k}(\uline{\boldsymbol{w}}_{T},\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T},\uline{\boldsymbol{z}}_{T})\coloneq\begin{cases}0&\,\text{if }k=0,\\
\int_{\sigma}\llbracket{\varrho_{\mathfrak{T}_{T}}^{k}}({{{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{\mathfrak{T}_{T}}^{k-1}}}(({\boldsymbol{w}}_{T}^{0}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{R}_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{v}}_{T}))\rrbracket_{\sigma}\llbracket{\varrho_{\mathfrak{T}_{T}}^{k}}({{{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{\mathfrak{T}_{T}}^{k-1}}}(({\boldsymbol{w}}_{T}^{0}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{R}_{T}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{z}}_{T}))\rrbracket_{\sigma}&\,\text{if }k\geq 1\\
\end{cases}.

(59)

Notice that
t_{T,\sigma}^{k}
in (
59
) is linear only in its second and third arguments.

5
Velocity error analysis

Most of the relations that we will write in this section hold for almost every

and

Let
\boldsymbol{u}
solve the continuous problem (

\uline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}
the discrete problem (

We define the velocity error as

\uline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}\coloneq\uline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-\hat{\uline{\boldsymbol{u}}}_{h},

(61)

where
\hat{\uline{\boldsymbol{u}}}_{h}=\uline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{h}^{k}\boldsymbol{u}

It will be also useful to define

{({\boldsymbol{\eta}}_{h})_{|T}\coloneq}{\boldsymbol{\eta}}_{T}\coloneq\boldsymbol{R}_{T}^{k}\hat{\uline{\boldsymbol{u}}}_{T}-\boldsymbol{u}
for all

(62)

We now present the two main results of this manuscript, which bound the velocity error (

Theorem 10

(Velocity error estimate)
.

Let the pair
(\boldsymbol{u},p)
be the solution of the continuous problem (
2
),
and let the pair
(\uline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},p_{h})

be the solution of the discrete problem (
60

Suppose that

\boldsymbol{u}\in L^{2}(\boldsymbol{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{H}^{1}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(\boldsymbol{H}^{2}(\Omega))
,

d_{t}\boldsymbol{u}\in L^{2}(\boldsymbol{H}^{1}(\Omega))
,
and

p:(0,t_{\rm F})\to P\cap{H}^{1}({\Omega})
.
Then, we have the following error estimate:

\displaystyle\|\uline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\|\cdot\|_{R,h})}^{2}+\nu\int_{0}^{t_{\rm F}}\|\uline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}\|_{1,h}^{2}\lesssim e^{G(\boldsymbol{u},t_{\rm F})}\int_{0}^{t_{\rm F}}H(\boldsymbol{u}),

(63)

where

G(\boldsymbol{u},t_{\rm F})\coloneq t_{\rm F}+\int_{0}^{t_{\rm F}}\left(|\boldsymbol{u}|_{\boldsymbol{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}+h\gamma_{k}\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{{\boldsymbol{L}}^{\infty}({\Omega})}+h\widetilde{\delta}_{0k}|\boldsymbol{u}|_{\boldsymbol{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)

and

H(\boldsymbol{u})\coloneq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\nu h_{T}^{2}\|\Delta{\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{T}^{k-1}\Delta{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(T)}}^{2}+\nu\|{\cal{E}}_{a,h}(\boldsymbol{u};\cdot)\|_{1,h,*}^{2}+{\mathfrak{N}}_{1}+{\mathfrak{N}}_{2},

(64)

where, denoting by
\delta_{0k}
the Kronecker delta, we have let

\widetilde{\delta}_{0k}\coloneq 1-\delta_{0k},

(65)

while
\gamma_{k}
is defined by

\gamma_{k}\coloneq\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }k\in\{0,1\}\\
1,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases},

(66)

and the terms
{\mathfrak{N}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{N}}_{2}
are
respectively such that, for almost every
t\in(0,t_{\rm F})
,

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{N}}_{1}

\displaystyle\coloneq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(|\boldsymbol{u}|_{\boldsymbol{W}^{1,\infty}(T)}\|\hat{{\boldsymbol{u}}}_{T}-\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(T)}^{2}+{\gamma_{k}}{h_{T}^{-3}}\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{{\boldsymbol{L}}^{\infty}({T})}\|\hat{{\boldsymbol{u}}}_{T}-\boldsymbol{u}\|_{{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}({T})}^{2}\right)

(67)

\displaystyle+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\boldsymbol{W}^{1,\infty}(T)}\left(h_{T}^{-1}\|{\boldsymbol{\eta}}_{T}\|_{{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}({T})}^{2}+h_{T}|{\boldsymbol{\eta}}_{T}|_{\boldsymbol{H}^{1}(\mathfrak{T}_{T})}^{2}+\|{\boldsymbol{\eta}}_{T}\|_{{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}({T})}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\widetilde{\delta}_{0k}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{\infty}(T)}^{2}\left(\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{\eta}}_{T}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(T)}^{2}+\|\nabla(\boldsymbol{u}-{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{T}^{k}{\boldsymbol{u}})\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(T)}^{2}\right),

and

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{N}}_{2}

\displaystyle\coloneq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(\|\boldsymbol{R}_{T}^{k}(\widehat{\underline{{d_{t}}\boldsymbol{u}}}_{T})-{{d_{t}\boldsymbol{u}}}\|_{{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}({T})}^{2}+\|{d_{t}\boldsymbol{u}}-(\widehat{{d_{t}\boldsymbol{u}}})_{T}\|_{{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}({T})}^{2}\right)

(68)

\displaystyle+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}^{2}\|\nabla((\widehat{{d_{t}\boldsymbol{u}}})_{T}-d_{t}\boldsymbol{u})\|_{{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}({T})}^{2}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{T}}h_{F}\|d_{t}\boldsymbol{u}-(\widehat{{d_{t}\boldsymbol{u}}})_{F}\|_{{\boldsymbol{L}}^{2}({F})}^{2},

where
\widehat{\underline{d_{t}\boldsymbol{u}}}_{T}\coloneq\underline{\boldsymbol{I}}_{T}^{k}(d_{t}\boldsymbol{u})
for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.

Using the theorem above, the approximation properties of the local operator
\boldsymbol{R}_{T}^{k}
presented in Lemma
2
, and the approximation properties (
4
) of
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{T}^{k^{\star}}
for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
along with the following standard property of the
L^{2}
-projector
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{F}^{k}
on
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}
:

\|{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{F}^{k}\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(F)}=\inf_{\boldsymbol{w}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}^{k}(F)}\|\boldsymbol{w}-\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(F)}\leq\|{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{T}^{k}\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{2}(F)},

we have the following corollary.

Corollary 11

(Velocity convergence rates)
.

Under the notations and the assumptions of the previous theorem, and additionally assuming
\boldsymbol{u}\in L^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{H}^{k+1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}))\cap L^{2}(\boldsymbol{H}^{k+2}(\mathcal{T}_{h}))
for
k\in\{0,1\}
,
\boldsymbol{u}\in L^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{H}^{k+2}(\mathcal{T}_{h}))
for
k>1
, and
d_{t}\boldsymbol{u}\in L^{2}(\boldsymbol{H}^{k+1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}))

\displaystyle\|\uline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\|\cdot\|_{R,h})}^{2}+\nu\int_{0}^{t_{\rm F}}\|\uline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}\|_{1,h}^{2}\lesssim e^{G_{1}(\boldsymbol{u},t_{\rm F})}H_{1}(\boldsymbol{u},t_{\rm F}),

(69)

where

G_{1}(\boldsymbol{u},t_{\rm F})\coloneq t_{\rm F}+\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}\|_{L^{1}(\boldsymbol{L}^{\infty})}+h\gamma_{k}\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{L^{1}(\boldsymbol{L}^{\infty})}+h\widetilde{\delta}_{0k}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}\|_{L^{2}(\boldsymbol{L}^{\infty})}^{2}
and

\displaystyle H_{1}(\boldsymbol{u},t_{\rm F})\coloneq

\displaystyle\nu h^{(2k+2)}\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{L^{2}(\boldsymbol{H}^{k+2}(\mathcal{T}_{h}))}^{2}+h^{(2k+1)}\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{L^{1}(\boldsymbol{W}^{1,\infty})}\left(\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{H}^{k+1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}))}^{2}+\gamma_{k}\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{H}^{k+2}(\mathcal{T}_{h}))}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\widetilde{\delta}_{0k}h^{(2k+1)}\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{L^{2}(\boldsymbol{L}^{\infty})}^{2}\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{H}^{k+1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}))}^{2}+h^{(2k+2)}\|d_{t}\boldsymbol{u}\|_{L^{2}(\boldsymbol{H}^{k+1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}))}^{2}.

Remark 12

(No dependency on
\nu^{-1}
or
p
)
.

The right-hand sides of (
63
) and (
69
) do not depend on
\nu^{-1}
or on the pressure
p
, thus we have indeed a Reynolds-semi-robust and pressure-robust method.

Remark 13

(Formulation using only
\boldsymbol{H}_{\text{div}}(\mathfrak{T}_{h})
)
.

One may wonder why we do not use a formulation completely based on a discrete conforming subspace of
{\boldsymbol{H}}_{\text{div}}({\mathfrak{T}_{h}})
.
The main reason is that the functions in this space are not fully continuous at interfaces, hence jump penalization for the discretization of the viscous term would be required for stability.
This would, in turn, result in a stronger coupling among the degrees of freedom of neighbouring elements and, thus, in a larger stencil.

Remark 14

(Estimate including the upwind-norm)
.

Following the same steps in the proof of Theorem
10
,
and similar steps in the proof of Lemma
16
,
we can include the upwind norm in the left hand side of the inequalities (
63
) and (
69
), and have the following estimate instead

\displaystyle\|\uline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\|\cdot\|_{R,h})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t_{\rm F}}\Big(\nu\|\uline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}\|_{1,h}^{2}+\frac{1-\epsilon}{2}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{F}_{h}^{\rm i}}\int_{\sigma}|\boldsymbol{R}_{h}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{\sigma}||\llbracket\boldsymbol{R}_{h}^{k}\uline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}\rrbracket_{\sigma}|^{2}\Big)\lesssim e^{G_{1}(\boldsymbol{u},t_{\rm F})}H_{1}(\boldsymbol{u},t_{\rm F}),

where
\epsilon

.

Remark 15

(Error in the
L^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{L}^{2})
-norm)
.

Using the inequality (
34
) for
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we obtain an order of convergence of

in the

This order of convergence equals, e.g., the one obtained in

However, in Corollary

Specifically, observe that in the definition (

Appendix A
Local inequalities for piecewise polynomials on a submesh

Lemma 17

(Local discrete Lebesgue embedding for piecewise polynomials)
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and, for an integer
l\geq 0
, let
q\in\mathcal{P}^{l}(\mathfrak{T}_{T})
.
Then, for all
(\alpha,\beta)\in[1,+\infty]
, it holds, with the convention that
\frac{1}{\infty}\coloneq 0
,

\|q\|_{L^{\alpha}(T)}\simeq h_{T}^{d\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{\beta}\right)}\|q\|_{L^{\beta}(T)},

(93)

where the hidden constant is independent of
h_{T}
,
T
, and
q
, but possibly depends on
l
,
\alpha
,
\beta
, and the mesh regularity parameter.

Proof.

We first recall the discrete Lebesgue embedding proved in
[
20
, Lemma 1.25]
, which establishes that for all
(\alpha,\beta)\in[1,+\infty]
, all
X\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\cup\mathfrak{T}_{h}\cup\mathcal{F}_{h}\cup\mathfrak{F}_{h}
, and all
\zeta\in\mathcal{P}^{l}(X)

\|\zeta\|_{L^{\alpha}(X)}\simeq|X|^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{\beta}}\|\zeta\|_{L^{\beta}(X)},

(94)

where
|X|
is the Lebesgue measure of
X
, and the hidden constant is independent of
X
, and
\zeta
, but possibly depends on
l
,
\alpha
,
\beta
, and the mesh regularity parameter.
Then, for
\alpha\in[1,+\infty)
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\|q\|_{L^{\alpha}(T)}^{\alpha}=\sum_{\tau\in\mathfrak{T}_{T}}\|q\|_{L^{\alpha}(\tau)}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\simeq\sum_{\tau\in\mathfrak{T}_{T}}|\tau|^{\alpha\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{\beta}\right)}\|q\|_{L^{\beta}(\tau)}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\simeq\sum_{\tau\in\mathfrak{T}_{T}}|\tau|^{\alpha\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{\beta}\right)}\|q\|_{L^{\beta}(T)}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\simeq h_{T}^{\alpha d\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{\beta}\right)}\|q\|_{L^{\beta}(T)}^{\alpha},

where in the second step we have used (
94
) with
X=\tau
,
in the third step the fact that
\tau\subset T
,
and in the fourth step the fact that
|\tau|\simeq h_{\tau}^{d}{\simeq h_{T}^{d}}
by mesh regularity along with
(
3
) for
\mathfrak{T}_{T}
.
Raising to the power of
\frac{1}{\alpha}
the above inequality, we obtain (
93
).
If
\alpha=+\infty
, we use similar steps as before to obtain that

\displaystyle\|q\|_{L^{\infty}(T)}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\tau\in\mathfrak{T}_{T}}\|q\|_{L^{\infty}(\tau)}\lesssim\sup_{\tau\in\mathfrak{T}_{T}}|\tau|^{-\frac{1}{\beta}}\|q\|_{L^{\beta}(T)}\lesssim h_{T}^{-\frac{d}{\beta}}\|q\|_{L^{\beta}(T)}.\qed

Lemma 18

(Local inverse inequalities for piecewise polynomials)
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and, for an integer
l\geq 0
, let
q\in\mathcal{P}^{l}(\mathfrak{T}_{T})
.
Then, for all
p\in[1,+\infty]
, it holds

\|q\|_{L^{p}(T)}\lesssim h_{T}^{-1}\|\nabla q\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{p}(T)},

(95)

where we remind the reader that here
\nabla
stands for the piecewise gradient on
\mathfrak{T}_{T}
.

Proof.

For
p\in[1,+\infty)
, we write

\|q\|_{L^{p}(T)}^{p}=\sum_{\tau\in\mathfrak{T}_{T}}\|q\|_{L^{p}(\tau)}^{p}\lesssim\sum_{\tau\in\mathfrak{T}_{T}}h_{\tau}^{-p}\|\nabla q\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{p}(\tau)}^{p}\lesssim h_{T}^{-p}\|\nabla q\|_{\boldsymbol{L}^{p}(T)}^{p},

where we have used a standard discrete inverse inequality on simplices (cf., e.g.,
[
13
]
) in the second passage and the fact that
h_{\tau}\simeq h_{T}
by mesh regularity to conclude.
If
p=+\infty
, the proof is similar with sums over
\tau\in\mathfrak{T}_{T}
replaced by maximums over
\tau\in\mathfrak{T}_{T}
.
∎

Lemma 19

(Local trace inequality for piecewise polynomials)
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}
, and, for an integer
l\geq 0
, let
q\in\mathcal{P}^{l}(\mathfrak{T}_{T})
.
Then, for all
p\in[1,+\infty]
, it holds

\|q\|_{L^{p}(F)}\lesssim h_{F}^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|q\|_{L^{p}(T)}.

(96)

Proof.

Recalling that
\mathfrak{F}_{F}
collects the simplicial faces contained in
F
and denoting, for all
\sigma\in\mathfrak{F}_{F}
, by
\tau_{\sigma}\in\mathfrak{T}_{T}
the simplex to which
F
belongs, we write

\|q\|_{L^{p}(F)}^{p}=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{F}_{F}}\|q\|_{L^{p}(\sigma)}^{p}\lesssim\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{F}_{F}}h_{\sigma}^{-1}\|q\|_{L^{p}(\tau_{\sigma})}^{p}\lesssim h_{T}^{-1}\|q\|_{L^{p}(T)}^{p},

where we have used a standard discrete trace inequality on simplices in the second step and the fact that
h_{\sigma}^{-1}\lesssim h_{T}^{-1}
,
\tau_{\sigma}\subset T
for all
\sigma\in\mathfrak{F}_{F}
, and
\card(\mathfrak{F}_{F})\lesssim 1
to conclude.
Taking the
q
-th root yields (
96
).
∎

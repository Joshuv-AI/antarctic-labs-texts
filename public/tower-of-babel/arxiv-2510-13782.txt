Structure and magnetism of MnGe thin films grown with a non-magnetic CrSi template
B. D. MacNeil, J. S. R. McCoombs, D. Kalliecharan, J. Myra, M. Pula, J. F. Britten, G. B. G. Stenning, K. Gupta, G. M. Luke, T. L. Monchesky

Abstract
We report on a novel method to grow B20 MnGe thin films which employs an ultrathin CrSi template layer on Si(111). This layer is expected to be non-magnetic, in contrast to MnSi and FeGe buffer layers that have been used previously, allowing an investigation of the intrinsic properties the MnGe in the ultrathin film limit without the influence of a neighboring magnetic layer. Single-phase MnGe(111) films were grown with thicknesses between 2 and 40 nm 40\text{\,}\mathrm{nm} , which exhibited low interfacial roughnesses on the order of 0.6 nm 0.6\text{\,}\mathrm{nm} . The films crystallized in a B20 structure with a small rhombohedral distortion. Magnetometry measurements in out-of-plane fields are consistent with a conical state. However, an unexpected remanent moment develops below 35 K 35\text{\,}\mathrm{K} , concomitant with features in the field dependence of the transport data. This provides indirect evidence for the presence of a low-temperature phase which has been identified by others as either a triple- Q Q topological spin-hedgehog lattice, or a multi-domain single- Q Q conical state.

In this work, we present the development of a B20 bilayer system designed to investigate the intrinsic magnetic properties of MnGe in the ultrathin film limit, where the helical wavevector is comparable to the film thickness. This system enables the interplay between finite size and large spin fluctuations, and the unique electronic structure of MnGe can be explored. We have characterized the structure of MnGe(111) grown on B20 CrSi template layers deposited Si(111) substrates, and investigated their properties with magnetometry and magnetotransport studies. We chose CrSi based on several studies that find no evidence of permanent magnetic order down to low temperature

The rest of this paper is structured as follows: We first review the structure of B20 films on Si(111) substrates, and then describe the methods that we used to produce both a template of B20 CrSi, and B20 MnGe films using this template. We also discuss the effect of temperature and Mn composition in the stability of the B20 MnGe phase. We then outline the structural experiments we conducted to prove that we indeed grew epitaxial MnGe films, and to characterize the strain and distortion in these films due to the Si substrate. Finally, we investigate the magnetic properties of our films by presenting complementary magnetometry and transport data with a field applied parallel to MnGe
[111]

Though it is likely not feasible to confirm the structure
2\text{\,}\mathrm{QL}
of the CrSi template layers using XRD, the observed RHEED is sufficient to conclude that the amorphous
0.5\text{\,}\mathrm{QL}

may exhibit similar RHEED patterns, we note that the apparent drop in quality of the RHEED pattern with increasing temperature signals a structural change in the film. We suggest that this is due to diffusion of Si into the template which forms
\text{CrSi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. As suggested in Refs.
Wetzel_1987
;
Wetzel_1988
;
Mahan_1993

\text{CrSi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
accompanied by poor epitaxy which explains the appearance of transmission spots in the RHEED pattern. The fact that we were only able to grow MnGe or CrSi films atop the
2\text{\,}\mathrm{QL}
CrSi is strong evidence that the template is indeed B20 CrSi, rather than another Cr-Si polymorph. We also wish to note that two recent studies reported the growth of (210)-textured monoclinic and cubic CrSi on Si(111) and claimed that the films exhibited ferromagnetic order between 3 and

including a moment of approximately 1
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}

CrSi sample
supp

The data presented in Figs.
3
and
7
show that B20 MnGe can be stabilized atop a template of B20 CrSi. To date, MnSi and FeGe have also been used. So long as the annealing temperature is kept below
250\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolCelsius}
, diffusion of Mn into the Si substrate appears minimal based on XRD and TEM. This template is also capable of nucleating epitaxial B20 CrSi (while we found that a solid phase reaction could not reliably do so), and even D8
8

\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{Ge}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}

-0.31\%
whereas it is
+1.28\%
for FeGe/CrSi if the CrSi is fully relaxed.

Since the metastable MnGe structure does not appear to crystallize atop any template other than B20 MnSi, FeGe or CrSi, we hypothesize that during the post-deposition annealing step, crystallization initiates at the CrSi interface and proceeds upwards. VII
Summary

We have demonstrated the growth of epitaxial MnGe(111) films on Si(111) using an ultrathin template of B20 CrSi. Contrary to those reported previously, the films investigated in this paper are free from the influence of a neighboring magnetic layer and have thicknesses comparable to the helical wavelength. A large portion of the magnetic phase diagram below the ordering temperature is a single-
Q
conical state. We observed subtle hysteresis in the magnetization below a temperature of
35\text{\,}\mathrm{K}
that has not been reported and is coincident with features in the Hall resistivity. These signals indicate the presence of an additional phase, which is not observed in bulk. However, our magnetometry and transport measurements are not able to distinguish between multi-domain single-
Q
states and triple-
Q
spin-hedgehog states that are debated in the literature. A more direct probe such as neutron or resonant X-ray scattering is required to determine the magnetic structure of this low-temperature phase in the thin film limit.

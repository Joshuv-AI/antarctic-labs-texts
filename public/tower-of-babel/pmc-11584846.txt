Orbital computed tomography imaging characteristics of thyroid-associated ophthalmopathy
Authors: Chao Xiong, Zhangjun Ren, Xuemei Li, Qi Jin, Siyi Wang, Puying Gan, Yaohua Wang, Hongfei Liao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
This study aims to investigate the distribution characteristics and correlations of orbital soft tissue volume expansion in patients with thyroid-associated ophthalmopathy (TAO) by analyzing orbital computed tomography (CT) data and to determine the most appropriate parameters and corresponding cut-off values for imaging classification. Patients with TAO who met the inclusion and exclusion criteria, along with those with lacrimal duct obstruction requiring orbital CT examination, were included in the study. Raw CT data were imported into Mimics and RadiAnt software for analysis, and measurements of orbital volume, orbital fat volume, extraocular muscle volume, optic nerve volume, and exophthalmos were obtained. The results demonstrated that the combination of Mimics and other softwares for processing orbital CT scans allows for the quantitative analysis of various soft tissue volumes within the orbit. The MV/FV ratio was found to effectively reflect the relative changes in extraocular muscle volume, serving as a valuable indicator for identifying different subtypes of TAO and providing significant clinical reference value.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-76624-2.

Introduction

Thyroid-associated ophthalmopathy (TAO)1 is the most prevalent orbital disease in adults, characterized by autoimmune-related inflammation of the orbital tissue closely associated with thyroid disease. The pathological changes primarily involve inflammatory alterations in connective tissue, adipose tissue, extraocular muscles, and interstitial tissue within the orbit. These changes result in increased volume of orbital contents and compression of the eyeball, leading to proptosis. Timely and accurate diagnosis is crucial for TAO patients as early detection and intervention facilitate better control over disease progression. Therefore, orbital imaging plays a pivotal role in diagnosing this condition. Among various imaging modalities, multi-slice spiral CT has gained widespread use due to its high resolution, short scan time consumption, low artifact production, and cost-effectiveness. CT scans enable visual observation of orbital tissue changes such as enlargement of extraocular muscles, increase in orbital fat content, and exophthalmos. Furthermore, reliable quantitative analysis can be repeatedly performed on these alterations through data transmission and post-processing using Mimics software as demonstrated by Regensburg2. This study aims to accurately measure the volume of adipose tissue and extraocular muscles in TAO patients using three-dimensional reconstruction based on orbital CT scans combined with semi-automatic quantitative measurement technology. By systematically analyzing distribution characteristics of these tissues and investigating their potential correlation with clinical data parameters will be established for optimal imaging parameters along with corresponding thresholds for TAO classification.

Research methods

General information

We can confirm that our study was conducted in strict compliance with all relevant ethical guidelines and regulations. Ethical consent and numbers for adherence to these guidelines were included in the study statement, and we ensured that the rights and interests of all participants were protected.

The severity and activity of the disease group were assessed by a proficient specialist in thyroid-associated ophthalmopathy. We used the CAS scale to stage the disease activity, with a CAS score of 3 indicating that the disease was in the active stage, and a score of less than 3 indicating that the disease was in the quiescent stage4. Additionally, demographic data including age, gender, duration of eye disease, serum thyroid function, and other relevant information were collected for the disease group. The control group only provided gender and age information.

Orbital CT scan

The patients in both groups underwent orbital CT scans using the Siemens Somatom Definition AS + 64-slice spiral CT scanner. Prior to the examination, the patients were informed about precautions and instructed to remove any metal objects they were wearing.

Standard positioning involved having the patient lie flat on the CT machine bed with their hands naturally placed at their sides. The head was positioned in a headrest with a slight chin elevation, ensuring that the auriculoorbital line was perpendicular to the CT machine bed and that the coronal plane was parallel to it.

The scanning method employed axial scanning from the frontal sinus to nasal floor. Scanning parameters included a slice thickness of 0.6 mm, reconstruction of 0.6 mm, voltage of 120KV, and current of 100 mA.

Measurement methods involved copying and importing raw CT data from eligible patients into Mimics Research 21.0 and RadiAnt DICOM Viewer software for analysis. Orbital bony cavity volume (OV), orbital fat volume (FV), extraocular muscle volume (MV), optic nerve volume (NV), and exophthalmos (EXO) were measured using these applications. Each measurement was performed three times by the same researcher, with an average value used for analysis.

Definition of CT measurement parameters

Orbital volume (OV): The anterior orbital margin at each horizontal computed tomography level served as its boundary, while boundaries for each canal included inferior orbital fissure, superior orbital fissure, optic nerve canal opening, nasolacrimal canal opening fossa, and pterygopalatine fossa close to orbit.

Exophthalmos measurement (EXO): The plane of measurement was chosen to display the entire orbital segment of the optic nerve as well as the medial and lateral rectus muscles in a horizontal scan. In this level, a straight line was drawn connecting the lateral rim of the orbit on both sides. The perpendicular line between the vertex of the anterior surface of the eyeball and this line was drawn for both eyes, and the length of this line was defined as the degree of exophthalmos (Fig. 1).

Statistical analysis

SPSS 24.0 and GraphPad Prism were utilized for data analysis and generation of statistical charts. The chi-square test was employed to examine gender differences between the two groups. Shapiro-Wilk test was conducted to assess the normality of measurement data, with normally distributed data presented as mean ± standard deviation. Two independent sample t-tests were performed to compare differences between the two groups, while analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used for comparing differences among multiple groups. If measurement to normal distribution, it is expressed as median (interquartile range); Mann-Whitney U test is used for comparison between two groups, Kruskal-Wallis H test for comparison among multiple groups, and Bonferroni correction for post hoc test. Pearson correlation analysis was conducted on measurement data following a normal distribution, while Spearman correlation analysis was employed for non-normally distributed data. Statistical significance level was set at p < 0.05.

Discussion

The initial quantitative study of orbital CT involved two-dimensional measurements, with common parameters being the diameter and cross-sectional area of the extraocular muscles5–7. With the emergence of three-dimensional reconstruction techniques, the parameters for CT quantitative measurement primarily focused on the volume of extraocular muscles, orbital fat, and overall orbital volume8,9. The 3D measurement method is relatively intricate and time-consuming; however, continuous optimization and advancements in processing software have improved measurement speed and accuracy through semi-automatic and automatic identification of target structures10,11. While measuring its volume provides a direct indicator to assess whether an extraocular muscle is enlarged or not, individual variations make it challenging to solely rely on this parameter for accurate differentiation. Changes in soft tissue volumes within the orbit can partially reflect pathological alterations associated with thyroid-associated ophthalmopathy (TAO). Currently, there are several studies exploring correlations between TAO imaging classifications and pathogenesis12,13; nevertheless, a unified standard for subtype definition remains absent.

A total of 54 patients with thyroid-associated ophthalmopathy (TAO) were enrolled in this study, among whom 108 eyes underwent orbital CT measurement. Additionally, a control group consisting of 28 patients with lacrimal duct obstruction (56 eyes) underwent orbital CT examination. In the inclusion and exclusion criteria, we excluded factors that could potentially affect the measurement of orbital soft tissue volume, such as local radiotherapy, orbital mass, inflammatory pseudotumor, and IgG4-related ophthalmopathy. We also excluded factors that could potentially affect the measurement of orbital volume, such as orbital deformity and various types of orbital fractures. Previous studies involving Korean and French populations have suggested a positive correlation between BMI and orbital volume14,15. Moreover, countries with lower average BMI15 have been found to exhibit lower mean exophthalmos16; however, some older studies have reported lower exophthalmos values which may be attributed to the increasing global prevalence of obesity17–19. Therefore, in order to minimize the influence of BMI on our analysis results, we restricted its value within this study.

Orbital fatty tissue volume increased obviously in the TAO group, control group and ms - TAO group and the active statistical differences between the groups. This suggests that entered the stage of the activity in the disease, fat tissue volume did not back to normal. Correlation analysis results revealed a positive association between orbital fat volume, orbital volume, and exophthalmos20 to some extent in both control and TAO groups.

Because the levator palpebrae superioris ​and rectus go on line are basically identical and indistinguishable in the CT image, therefore, be included in the superior rectus measurement range. The superior oblique and inferior oblique muscles are slender and tortuous and difficult to measure. Therefore, the volume of extraocular muscles in this paper is the sum of the volumes of the four rectus muscles. Compared with the control group, the MV was significantly enlarged in TAO patients, and the enlargement increased with the severity of TAO. The statistical difference existed between very severe TAO, mild and moderate to severe TAO. There was a positive correlation between MV and orbital volume in the control group, and a positive correlation between MV and OV, NV, and EXO in the TAO group. These findings suggest that the enlargement of extraocular muscles in TAO patients runs through all stages of the disease, which is consistent with the Bartley diagnostic criteria21 for the enlargement of extraocular muscles.

Optic larger volume in the TAO group compared with control subjects, and with the increasing severity of the trend, but no significant statistical differences in the severity and activity between different subgroups did not reflect the statistical differences. This does not mean that the volume of the optic nerve does not change during the development of the disease, especially in patients with very severe TAO, the functional and structural damage caused by physical damage is significant. The situation in this paper may be the result of the interaction between the intraorbital mechanical pressure and the inflammatory response of the optic nerve.

Currently, the Hertel exophthalmometer is the most commonly utilized instrument for measuring exophthalmos. Despite its portability and simplicity, determining the lowest point of the orbital margin is highly subjective, and measurements are also influenced by skin and soft tissue thickness at the orbital margin, resulting in poor repeatability among different examiners22. In this study, CT images were used to measure exophthalmos slightly differently from Hertel’s exophthalmoscope measurement definition. The measurement plane was defined as the scanning plane displaying the entire orbital segment of the optic nerve along with medial and lateral rectus muscles in a standard position. In this level, a straight line was drawn connecting the lateral rim of the orbit on both sides. The perpendicular line between the vertex of the anterior surface of the eyeball and this line was drawn for both eyes, and the length of this line was defined as the degree of exophthalmos. Results show that the TAO of exophthalmos degree is significantly greater than the control group, change with severity has increasing trend. Correlation analysis in the control group of extraocular muscle mass, orbital fat volume were positively correlated. In TAO group, the MV, NV and MV/OV were positively correlated with them, and the correlation coefficient r of MV was the highest (0.54). The changes of these correlations suggest that the volume of various orbital soft tissues increases during TAO, among which the change of MV has the most significant effect on exophthalmos.

In the correlation analysis of the TAO group, we incorporated several clinical parameters including duration of ophthalmopathy, severity, activity, TRAb, and TSH. The analysis revealed a positive correlation between disease severity and activity, as well as a negative correlation between disease severity and NV. Specifically, there exists an inverse relationship between the severity of the disease and the volume of the optic nerve. It is comprehensible that during progressive advancement of the disease, continuous expansion of orbital soft tissue can exert “compression” on the optic nerve in its early stages; however, with adaptive changes in orbital volume during moderate-severe stages, this compression can be alleviated. Ultimately, at an extremely severe stage where enlarged orbital cavity fails to accommodate further expansion of soft tissue volume within it, pressure forces forward displacement of eyeball leading to evident exophthalmos while simultaneously straightening and posterior crowding of narrow orbital apex compresses the optic nerve resulting in decreased visual acuity. As a cross-sectional study, three-dimensional reconstruction was employed to quantify the orbital soft tissue parameters of both TAO and control subjects, elucidating the distribution characteristics of orbital soft tissue volume expansion and its correlation with clinical data. Nevertheless, this study does possess certain limitations. Firstly, it is important to note that this is a single-center investigation with an insufficient sample size. Future research should aim to expand the dataset in order to obtain more precise outcomes. Secondly, despite making every effort to include patients with varying degrees of severity from the presenting patient pool, selection bias persisted.

In our study, both eyes with different symptoms but the same immune status in the same individual were included for comparison. The severity of the condition may vary slightly between the two eyes of an individual. We believe that, even if such differences exist, they remain instructive for clinical practice, as it is difficult to determine which eye exhibits differences in the timing or severity of immune-related indicators. However, directly defining a group may lead to significant selection bias. In future studies, we will aim to make the study design more rigorous and scientifically robust.

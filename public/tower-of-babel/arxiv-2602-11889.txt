Global Multiplicity and Comparison Principles for Singular Problems driven by Mixed Local-Nonlocal Operators
R. Dhanya, Sarbani Pramanik

Abstract
. We study a singular elliptic problem driven by a mixed local-nonlocal operator of the form − Δ p ​ u + ( − Δ q ) s ​ u \displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u+(-\Delta_{q})^{s}u = λ u δ + u r ​ in ​ Ω \displaystyle=\frac{\lambda}{u^{\delta}}+u^{r}\text{ in }\Omega u > 0 ​ in ​ Ω , u \displaystyle u>0\text{ in }\Omega,\ u = 0 ​ in ​ ℝ N ∖ Ω \displaystyle=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega where p > s ​ q p>sq , 0 < δ < 1 0<\delta<1 and λ > 0 \lambda>0 is a parameter. The nonlinearity exhibits a singular power-type behavior near zero and displays at most a critical growth at infinity. We establish a global multiplicity result with respect to the parameter λ \lambda by identifying a sharp threshold that separates existence, non-existence, and multiplicity regimes, a result that is new for singular problems involving mixed local-nonlocal operators. We also derive a Hopf-type strong comparison principle adapted to this nonlinear setting, which provides the main analytical tool for the global multiplicity result. Additionally, we investigate qualitative properties of solutions that are essential for the variational analysis, such as a uniform L ∞ L^{\infty} -estimate and a Sobolev versus Hölder local minimizer result. The analytical tools developed herein are of independent mathematical interest, with their applicability extending over a broader class of mixed local-nonlocal problems.

1.1.
Strong Comparison Principle

The Strong Comparison Principle (SCP) is a fundamental concept that extends the classical Strong Maximum Principle (SMP) and Hopf’s lemma to a comparison framework. Here, we establish SCP and Hopf-type SCP for mixed operators in the presence of singular nonlinearities.

Theorem 1.5

(Comparison Principle)
.

Let
1<p,q<\infty
with
p>sq
,
0<\delta<1
and
f_{1},f_{2}
be non-negative continuous functions in
\Omega

f_{1}\leq f_{2}
,
f_{1}\not\equiv f_{2}
in
\Omega
. Assume, in addition, one of the following holds:

(i)

(linear case)
p=q=2
and
s\in(0,1)
,

(ii)

(nonlinear case)
p\in(1,\infty)
,
q\geq 2
and
s\in\left(0,\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}\right)
,
q^{\prime}
being the Hölder conjugate of
q
.

If
u_{1},u_{2}\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
, for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
u_{1},u_{2}>0
in
\Omega
and they satisfy

(
Q
)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u_{1}+(-\Delta_{q})^{s}u_{1}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{u_{1}^{\delta}}=f_{1}\text{ in }\Omega

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u_{2}+(-\Delta_{q})^{s}u_{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{u_{2}^{\delta}}=f_{2}\text{ in }\Omega

\displaystyle u_{1}=u_{2}

\displaystyle=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega

then the following hold:

(a)

Strong Comparison Principle:
u_{1}<u_{2}
in
\Omega
,

(b)

Hopf-type Strong Comparison Principle:
\frac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial\nu}<\frac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial\nu}
on
\partial\Omega
,

where
\nu=\nu(x_{0})
is the outward unit normal to
\partial\Omega
at
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
.

For local elliptic operators, both the SCP and Hopf-type SCP are well studied in the literature. In contrast, corresponding results for nonlocal and mixed local–nonlocal operators are more limited, particularly in the presence of singular nonlinearities. We therefore briefly review existing results on the SCP and Hopf-type SCP.

•

Local operators.
In the case of the Laplacian,
[
24
]
established the SCP and then the Hopf-type SCP by deriving a pointwise lower bound for
u_{2}-u_{1}
in terms of the first eigenfunction of the Laplacian and a suitable power of it. For the

-Laplace operator,
[
33
]
proved the SCP and its Hopf-type version under a stricter condition
f_{1}<f_{2}
in
\Omega.
Later,
[
23
]
showed that for
p>2
, relaxing this to
f_{1}\leq f_{2}
,
f_{1}\not\equiv f_{2}
, may lead to the failure of the SCP, indicating the degeneracy of the
p
-Laplacian for
p>2
and the need for a strict inequality.

•

Nonlocal operators.
For nonlocal operators, weaker regularity necessitates a Hopf-type SCP formulated through boundary distance estimates of the form
u_{2}-u_{1}\geq cd(x)^{s}
in
\Omega
, where
d(x)
is the distance function and
c>0
. For the fractional Laplacian, although not stated explicitly, Theorem 4.7 of
[
32
]
effectively proves both the SCP and the fractional Hopf-type SCP. However, to the best of our knowledge, the corresponding nonlinear result for the fractional
p
-Laplacian remains open, where one expects a similar boundary estimate.

•

Mixed local-nonlocal operators.
In this setting,
[
22
]
extended the ideas of
[
39
]
to prove the SCP for singular problems, however, under a technical restriction
s\in\left(0,\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}\right)
,
q^{\prime}
being the Hölder conjugate of
q
. For the mixed linear operator
\mathcal{L}=-\Delta+(-\Delta)^{s}
,
[
43
]
employed a weak Harnack-type inequality followed by a construction-based approach and established both SCP and Hopf-type SCP for the full range
s\in(0,1)
. Nevertheless, the method strongly relies on the linearity of the operator, thus lacking a direct extension for nonlinear operators.

In summary, for mixed operators with singular nonlinearity, the SCP is known in the nonlinear case only when
s\in\left(0,\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}\right)
, while the Hopf-type SCP is available solely for linear operators. This gap highlights both the technical difficulties and the novelty of our result. Our proof stems from the observation that a positive solution to a carefully designed infinite semipositone problem serves as a subsolution to a linearized version of the original problem. The resulting divergence form operator involves only a Hölder continuous coefficient matrix which lacks an explicit expression, rendering standard sub-supersolution methods ineffective. Nevertheless, invoking the implicit function theorem, we obtain a positive solution to the associated semipositone problem involving the linearized local operator and combine it with refined estimates on the nonlocal term to close the argument.

1.2.
Sobolev versus Hölder local minimizers

3.
Sobolev versus Hölder local minimizers

This section is dedicated to the proof of Theorem
1.6
. We consider the energy functional
I:\mathbb{X}_{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, given by

I(u):=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\ dx+\frac{1}{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{N+qs}}\ dx\ dy-\frac{1}{1-\delta}\int_{\Omega}\left(u^{+}\right)^{1-\delta}dx-\int_{\Omega}F\left(x,u^{+}\right)\ dx,

where
\displaystyle u^{+}:=\max\{u,0\}
and
\displaystyle F(x,t):=\int_{0}^{t}f(x,\tau)\ d\tau
. A typical example of the nonlinearity
f
is the power-type function
f(x,t)=t^{r}
,
t\geq 0
, with a superlinear but at most critical growth rate. More generally, we assume that
f:\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a Carathéodory function that satisfies the following:

(f1)

for every
(x,t)\in\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
f(x,t)\geq 0
and
f(x,0)=0
,

(f2)

there exists
r>0
satisfying
\max\{p-1,q-1\}<r\leq r^{*}=\max\{p^{*}-1,q_{s}^{*}-1\}
such that
f(x,t)\leq C_{0}(1+t^{r})
, for all
(x,t)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
, where
C_{0}>0
is a constant and

p^{*}=\begin{cases}\frac{Np}{N-p}&,\text{ if }p<N,\\
\infty&,\text{ if }p>N,\end{cases}\ \ \text{ and }\ \ q_{s}^{*}=\begin{cases}\frac{Nq}{N-sq}&,\text{ if }sq<N,\\
\infty&,\text{ if }sq>N.\end{cases}

Remark 3.1
.

Note that if
sq<q\leq p
, then
q_{s}^{*}<q^{*}\leq p^{*}
, so
r^{*}=p^{*}-1
. On the other hand, if
sq<p<q
, we cannot generally compare
p^{*}
and
q_{s}^{*}
, and either exponent could be larger depending on specific values of
p,q
and
s
.

The functional
I
is naturally associated with the perturbed singular problem:

(3.1)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u+(-\Delta_{q})^{s}u

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{u^{\delta}}+f(x,u)\text{ in }\Omega

\displaystyle u>0\text{ in }\Omega,\ u

\displaystyle=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.

Proposition
3.2
below provides a clear connection between local minimizers of
I
and weak solutions of equation (
3.1
). The main technical difficulty arises due to the singular term
(u^{+})^{1-\delta}
,
0<\delta<1
, which prevents the functional
I
from being of class
C^{1}
on the space
\mathbb{X}_{0}(\Omega)
. Nevertheless,
I
is Gâteaux differentiable (corollary A.3,
[
33
]
) at every
u\in\mathbb{X}_{0}(\Omega)
, provided
u
satisfies the additional condition

u(x)\geq cd(x)\text{ a.e. in }\Omega,\text{ for some }c>0.

Next, we introduce a suitable auxiliary function, which will play an essential role throughout this section. We consider the purely singular problem:

(3.2)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u+(-\Delta_{q})^{s}u=\frac{1}{u^{\delta}}\text{ in }\Omega

\displaystyle u>0\text{ in }\Omega,\ u=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega

which admits a unique positive solution
\underline{u}\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
, for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
, satisfying
\underline{u}(x)\sim d(x)
in
\Omega
(see Theorem 3 and remark 1 of
[
22
]
).

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
u\in\mathbb{X}_{0}(\Omega)
be a local minimizer of
I
and satisfy
u(x)\geq cd(x)
a.e. in
\Omega
, for some
c>0
. Then
u
is a weak solution of (
3.1
) and
u\geq\underline{u}
a.e. in
\Omega
, where
\underline{u}
is the solution of (
3.2
).

Proof.

Since
u(x)\geq cd(x)
a.e. in
\Omega
and is a local minimizer of
I
, it follows that
I
is Gâteaux differentiable at
u
and for every
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
t>0
small enough, we have

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi\ dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q-2}\left(u(x)-u(y)\right)\left(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)\right)}{|x-y|^{N+sq}}\ dx\ dy\\
-\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{u^{\delta}}\varphi\ dx-\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)\varphi\ dx=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0+}\frac{I(u+t\varphi)-I(u)}{t}\geq 0.

Replacing
\varphi
with

gives the reverse inequality, and hence equality holds in the above expression for every

. Thus,

is a weak solution of (
3.1
). Also, since

is non-negative, it is a supersolution of equation (

as the test function in the weak formulation and applying the weak comparison principle, we conclude that

a.e. in
\Omega
.
∎

Remark 3.3

-topology and satisfies

Case II:

\{u_{\varepsilon}\}

\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{n}-\nabla u_{\lambda_{0}}|^{p}\ dx+\frac{1}{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\left|\left(v_{n}-u_{\lambda_{0}}\right)(x)-\left(v_{n}-u_{\lambda_{0}}\right)(y)\right|^{q}}{|x-y|^{N+qs}}\ dx\ dy\\
-\frac{1}{r^{*}+1}\int_{\Omega}|v_{n}-u_{\lambda_{0}}|^{r^{*}+1}\ dx>0.

As a result, we have,

\displaystyle m_{\lambda_{0}}

\displaystyle=\underline{I}_{\lambda_{0}}(v_{n})+o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle=\underline{I}_{\lambda_{0}}(u_{\lambda_{0}})+\frac{1}{p}\|v_{n}-u_{\lambda_{0}}\|^{p}_{1,p}+\frac{1}{q}[v_{n}-u_{\lambda_{0}}]^{q}_{s,q}-\frac{1}{r^{*}+1}\|v_{n}-u_{\lambda_{0}}\|^{r^{*}+1}_{r^{*}+1}+o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle\geq\underline{I}_{\lambda_{0}}(u_{\lambda_{0}})+o_{n}(1),

which implies
\underline{I}_{\lambda_{0}}(u_{\lambda_{0}})=m_{\lambda_{0}}
. Consequently,
u_{\lambda_{0}}
is a local minimizer of
\underline{I}_{\lambda_{0}}
and solves equation (
4.6
) weakly. Finally, arguing similar to the subcritical case, we obtain that
u_{\lambda_{0}}
is a solution of
(P_{\lambda_{0}})
and this completes the proof.
∎

The above lemma thus ensures the existence of at least one admissible value of the parameter
\lambda
for which (

P
λ

) possesses a positive solution. Consequently, the set

\mathcal{A}:=\{\lambda>0:(P_{\lambda})\text{ admits a solution}\}

is non-empty. Defining

(4.11)

\Lambda:=\sup\mathcal{A},

we immediately have
\Lambda>0
. Next, we show that
\Lambda
is finite.

Lemma 4.3

(Non-existence beyond the threshold)
.

The problem (

P
λ

) does not admit any solution for every
\lambda>\Lambda
.

Proof.

We fix two positive real numbers
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
such that

0<r_{1}<\min\{p-1,q-1\},\ \max\{p-1,q-1\}<r_{2}<r,

and consider the following equation with a convex-concave nonlinearity

(4.12)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u+(-\Delta_{q})^{s}u

\displaystyle=\mu\left(|u|^{r_{1}-1}u+|u|^{r_{2}-1}u\right)\text{ in }\Omega

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.

In
[
21
]
, the authors investigated the existence of solutions to (
4.12
). It is established in Theorem 1.2 of
[
21
]
that when
\mu>0
is sufficiently large, (
4.12
) admits only the trivial solution. We fix one such
\mu=\mu_{0}
large enough so that (
4.12
) admits only the trivial solution.

Next, we note that the map
t\mapsto t^{r+\delta}-\mu_{0}t^{r_{1}+\delta}-\mu_{0}t^{r_{2}+\delta},\ t>0
, has a global minimum, say
-\tilde{\lambda}
. Then, for every
\lambda>\tilde{\lambda}
, we have

(4.13)

\frac{\lambda}{t^{\delta}}+t^{r}>\mu_{0}\left(t^{r_{1}}+t^{r_{2}}\right),\ \forall\ t>0.

Now, we fix some
\lambda>\tilde{\lambda}
and show that (

P
λ

) does not admit any solution. If, on the contrary, (

P
λ

) has a positive solution
u_{\lambda}
, then, thanks to (
4.13
),
u_{\lambda}
becomes a supersolution to equation (
4.12

(4.14)

-\Delta_{p}u_{\lambda}+(-\Delta_{q})^{s}u_{\lambda}=\frac{\lambda}{u_{\lambda}^{\delta}}+u_{\lambda}^{r}>\mu_{0}\left(u_{\lambda}^{r_{1}}+u_{\lambda}^{r_{2}}\right)\text{ in }\Omega.

Consequently, similar to the proof of Theorem 1.2 of
[
21
]
, a global minimization of the associated energy functional truncated above by

yields a positive solution of (
4.12
). This contradicts our choice of

. Therefore, there does not exist any solution to (

P
λ

) for every
\lambda>\tilde{\lambda}
. Consequently, the threshold

defined in (

\Lambda
, it follows that (

P
λ

) does not admit any positive solution for every
\lambda>\Lambda
and this completes the proof.
∎

The following two results together establish that the set
\mathcal{A}
is an interval and, more precisely, that

.

Lemma 4.4

(Existence of solution below the threshold)
.

The problem (

P
λ

) has a positive solution
u_{\lambda}
for every
\lambda\in(0,\Lambda)
. Moreover,

is a local minimizer of the energy functional

in the
\mathbb{X}_{0}(\Omega)
-topology.

Proof.

We fix

admits a solution and define

\underline{v}\leq\overline{v}
in
\Omega
. Moreover, none of
\underline{v}
and

). Therefore, by corollary

, (

∎

Lemma 4.5

(Existence of solution at the threshold)
.

The problem (

P
λ

) admits a solution for
\lambda=\Lambda
.

Proof.

We consider an increasing sequence
\{\lambda_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{A}
such that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{n}=\Lambda
. Let
u_{\lambda_{n}}
be the corresponding solutions of
(P_{\lambda_{n}})
obtained by lemma
4.4
. Then for every

u_{\lambda_{n}}\geq\underline{u}_{\lambda_{n}}
in
\Omega
and

(4.15)

\langle-\Delta_{p}u_{\lambda_{n}}+(-\Delta_{q})^{s}u_{\lambda_{n}},\ \varphi\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda_{n}}{u_{\lambda_{n}}^{\delta}}\varphi\ dx+\int_{\Omega}u_{\lambda_{n}}^{r}\varphi\ dx,\text{ for every }\varphi\in\mathbb{X}_{0}(\Omega).

Thus, taking

itself as the test function, we get

(4.16)

\|u_{\lambda_{n}}\|^{p}_{1,p}+[u_{\lambda_{n}}]^{q}_{s,q}=\lambda_{n}\int_{\Omega}u_{\lambda_{n}}^{1-\delta}\ dx+\int_{\Omega}u_{\lambda_{n}}^{r+1}\ dx.

Now, from the construction of

, we obtain that

I_{\lambda_{n}}(u_{\lambda_{n}})\leq\underline{I}_{\lambda_{n}}(\underline{u}_{\lambda_{n}})<0.

Therefore,

\frac{1}{\max\{p,q\}}\left(\|u_{\lambda_{n}}\|^{p}_{1,p}+[u_{\lambda_{n}}]^{q}_{s,q}\right)-\frac{\lambda_{n}}{1-\delta}\int_{\Omega}u_{\lambda_{n}}^{1-\delta}-\frac{1}{r+1}\int_{\Omega}u_{\lambda_{n}}^{r+1}\leq I_{\lambda_{n}}(u_{\lambda_{n}})<0

\text{or, }c_{1}\int_{\Omega}u_{\lambda_{n}}^{r+1}\ dx\leq c_{2}\int_{\Omega}u_{\lambda_{n}}^{1-\delta}\ dx,

where

and

. Therefore, from (
4.16
), we get

(4.18)

\|u_{\lambda_{n}}\|^{p}_{1,p}+[u_{\lambda_{n}}]^{q}_{s,q}\leq\left(\Lambda+\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\right)\int_{\Omega}u_{\lambda_{n}}^{1-\delta}\ dx\leq c_{3}\|u_{\lambda_{n}}\|^{1-\delta}_{r^{*}+1}\leq c_{4}\|u_{\lambda_{n}}\|^{1-\delta}_{\mathbb{X}_{0}(\Omega)}.

Consequently,
\{u_{\lambda_{n}}\}
is bounded in
\mathbb{X}_{0}(\Omega)
and has a weakly convergent subsequence, say
\{u_{\lambda_{n}}\}
, by abuse of notation. Let
u_{\lambda_{n}}\rightharpoonup u_{\Lambda}
as

a.e. in
\Omega
. Taking limit in equation (

\langle-\Delta_{p}u_{\Lambda}+(-\Delta_{q})^{s}u_{\Lambda},\ \varphi\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\Lambda}{u_{\Lambda}^{\delta}}\varphi\ dx+\int_{\Omega}u_{\Lambda}^{r}\varphi\ dx,\text{ for every }\varphi\in\mathbb{X}_{0}(\Omega).

Therefore,

is a solution of (

P
λ

) for
\lambda=\Lambda
.
∎

Finally, we establish the existence of minimal solutions in the range

Lemma 4.6

(Existence of minimal solution)
.

The problem (

P
λ

) admits a minimal solution for every
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
and the minimal solutions are strictly increasing with respect to
\lambda
.

Proof.

We use the method of monotone iteration for every fixed
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
to prove this result. Define an iterative sequence
\{w_{n}\}
as follows:
w_{0}:=\underline{u}_{\lambda}
and for every

,
w_{n}
is the weak solution of

(4.19)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}w_{n}+(-\Delta_{q})^{s}w_{n}

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda}{w_{n}^{\delta}}=w_{n-1}^{r}\text{ in }\Omega

\displaystyle w_{n}>0\text{ in }\Omega,\ w_{n}

\displaystyle=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.

Using the fact that the maps

and
t\mapsto t^{r}
,

, are monotone increasing, the weak comparison principle yields that
w_{0}\leq w_{1}
in
\Omega.
By induction, we obtain that the sequence
\{w_{n}\}
is non-decreasing. Moreover,
w_{0}\leq u_{\Lambda}
and
w_{n}\leq u_{\Lambda}
for all

, where
u_{\Lambda}
is the solution of
(P_{\Lambda})
obtained in lemma
4.5
. As a result, it follows from (
4.19
) that
\{w_{n}\}
is bounded in
\mathbb{X}_{0}(\Omega)

and

a.e. in

. Since
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle\underline{I}^{\prime}_{\lambda}(w_{n}),\ \varphi\rangle=0
, by lemma
4.1
,

a.e. in
\Omega
. Taking limit in (
4.19
) as

, we obtain that the limiting function
\hat{u}_{\lambda}
is a solution of (

P
λ

). It is easy to see that any solution
u
of (

P
λ

), by the weak comparison principle, is bounded below by

. Thus,
\hat{u}_{\lambda}\leq u
in
\Omega
and hence,
u_{\lambda}
is the minimal solution of (

P
λ

).

Finally, we show that the map

and hence

in

-\Delta_{p}\hat{u}_{\lambda_{1}}+(-\Delta_{q})^{s}\hat{u}_{\lambda_{1}}-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\hat{u}_{\lambda_{1}}^{\delta}}=\hat{u}_{\lambda_{1}}^{r}\leq\hat{u}_{\lambda_{2}}^{r}\leq-\Delta_{p}\hat{u}_{\lambda_{2}}+(-\Delta_{q})^{s}\hat{u}_{\lambda_{2}}-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\hat{u}_{\lambda_{2}}^{\delta}}\text{ in }\Omega.

Consequently, by the Strong Comparison Principle (Theorem

in

∎

5.
Existence of a second solution

In this final section, we establish the existence of a second positive solution to (

P
λ

) for every
\lambda\in(0,\Lambda)
. We show that the truncated energy functional
\underline{I}_{\lambda}
, defined in (
4.2
), admits a critical point
v_{\lambda}
satisfying
v_{\lambda}>\underline{u}_{\lambda}
and
u_{\lambda}\not=v_{\lambda}
. Since
\underline{I}_{\lambda}
is a
C^{1}
-functional, this ensures that
v_{\lambda}
is indeed a solution of (

P
λ

), distinct from

. The existence of

is obtained via the Mountain Pass Theorem. To this end, we introduce a generalized notion of Palais-Smale sequences for

and establish compactness results for such sequences.

Definition 5.1

(Generalized Palais-Smale sequence)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a closed set in
\mathbb{X}_{0}(\Omega)
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
. We say a sequence
\{v_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{X}_{0}(\Omega)
is a Palais-Smale sequence for
\underline{I}_{\lambda}
at the level
c
around
\mathcal{F}
, or a
(PS_{\mathcal{F},c})
in short, if

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}dist(v_{n},\mathcal{F})=0,\ \lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\underline{I}_{\lambda}(v_{n})=c\text{ and }\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\underline{I}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(v_{n})=0.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a closed set in
\mathbb{X}_{0}(\Omega)
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
. If
\{v_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{X}_{0}(\Omega)
is a
(PS_{\mathcal{F},c})
sequence for
\underline{I}_{\lambda}
, then
\{v_{n}\}
is bounded in
\mathbb{X}_{0}(\Omega)
and upto a subsequence
v_{n}\rightharpoonup v_{\lambda}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, where
v_{\lambda}
is a weak solution of (

P
λ

).

Proof.

Fix any
c\in\mathbb{R}
and suppose
\{v_{n}\}
is a
(PS_{\mathcal{F},c})
sequence for
\underline{I}_{\lambda}
. Since
\underline{u}_{\lambda}
is positive in
\Omega
, we have

(5.1)

\frac{1}{p}\|v_{n}\|_{1,p}^{p}+\frac{1}{q}[v_{n}]_{s,q}^{q}-\int_{\{v_{n}>\ \underline{u}_{\lambda}\}}\left(\frac{\lambda}{1-\delta}v_{n}^{1-\delta}+\frac{v_{n}^{r+1}}{r+1}\right)dx\\
-\int_{\{v_{n}\leq\ \underline{u}_{\lambda}\}}\left(\lambda\underline{u}_{\lambda}^{-\delta}+\underline{u}_{\lambda}^{r}\right)v_{n}\ dx\leq c+o_{n}(1)

and

(5.2)

\|v_{n}\|_{1,p}^{p}+[v_{n}]_{s,q}^{q}-\int_{\{v_{n}>\ \underline{u}_{\lambda}\}}\left(\lambda v_{n}^{1-\delta}+v_{n}^{r+1}\right)dx-\int_{\{v_{n}\leq\ \underline{u}_{\lambda}\}}\left(\lambda\underline{u}_{\lambda}^{-\delta}+\underline{u}_{\lambda}^{r}\right)v_{n}\ dx=o_{n}(1).

Multiplying (
5.1
) by
(r+1)
and subtracting (
5.2
) from it, we obtain

(5.3)

\left(\frac{r+1-p}{p}\right)\|v_{n}\|_{1,p}^{p}+\left(\frac{r+1-q}{q}\right)[v_{n}]_{s,q}^{q}-r\int_{\{v_{n}\leq\ \underline{u}_{\lambda}\}}\left(\lambda\underline{u}_{\lambda}^{-\delta}+\underline{u}_{\lambda}^{r}\right)v_{n}\ dx\\
+\left(1-\frac{r+1}{1-\delta}\right)\int_{\{v_{n}>\ \underline{u}_{\lambda}\}}\lambda v_{n}^{1-\delta}\ dx\leq(r+1)c+o_{n}(1).

Note that as
r>\max\{p-1,q-1\}
, the coefficients
\frac{r+1-p}{p}
and
\frac{r+1-q}{q}
are strictly positive. A further simplification of (
5.3
) yields

\displaystyle\|v_{n}\|_{1,p}^{p}+[v_{n}]_{s,q}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}+c_{2}\int_{\{v_{n}\leq\ \underline{u}_{\lambda}\}}\left(\lambda\underline{u}_{\lambda}^{1-\delta}+\underline{u}_{\lambda}^{r+1}\right)dx+c_{3}\int_{\{v_{n}>\ \underline{u}_{\lambda}\}}\lambda v_{n}^{1-\delta}\ dx

\displaystyle\leq c_{4}+c_{5}\|v_{n}\|_{r^{*}+1}^{1-\delta}\leq c_{4}+c_{6}\left(\|v_{n}\|_{1,p}+[v_{n}]_{s,q}\right)^{1-\delta}.

Since the constants
c_{4}
and
c_{6}
are independent of
n
, it follows from the above inequality that the sequence
\{v_{n}\}
is bounded in
\mathbb{X}_{0}(\Omega)
. Hence there exists a subsequence, still denoting by
\{v_{n}\}
, that converges weakly in
\mathbb{X}_{0}(\Omega)
. Let
v_{\lambda}
be the weak limit of
\{v_{n}\}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Now,
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\underline{I}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(v_{n})=0
implies that
v_{n}
satisfies

(5.4)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}v_{n}+(-\Delta_{q})^{s}v_{n}

\displaystyle=h_{\lambda}(x,v_{n})+o_{n}(1)\text{ in }\Omega

\displaystyle v_{n}

\displaystyle=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.

in the weak sense and by lemma
4.1
,
\nabla v_{n}(x)\rightarrow\nabla v_{\lambda}(x)
a.e. in
\Omega
. Therefore, observing that
\displaystyle h_{\lambda}\left(x,v_{n}\right)\leq\underline{u}_{\lambda}^{-\delta}+\underline{u}_{\lambda}^{r}+v_{n}^{r}
, where
r\leq r^{*}
, taking limit in equation (
5.4
) as
n\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain that
v_{\lambda}
is a weak solution of

(5.5)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}v_{\lambda}+(-\Delta_{q})^{s}v_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=h_{\lambda}(x,v_{\lambda})\text{ in }\Omega

\displaystyle v_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.

Taking
(\underline{u}_{\lambda}-v_{\lambda})^{+}
as a test function, from equations (

a.e. in

solves (

P
λ

).
∎

Definition 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a closed set in
\mathbb{X}_{0}(\Omega)
and

. We say that
\underline{I}_{\lambda}
satisfies the Palais-Smale condition at the level

around

(PS_{\mathcal{F},c})

In the subcritical case, the compactness of the Sobolev embedding ensures that

satisfies the Palais-Smale condition at every level

has the minimum energy among all weak solutions of (

P
λ

λ

). Also, note that weak comparison principle confirms that if

in

\underline{I}_{\lambda}(u_{\lambda})\leq\underline{I}_{\lambda}(w_{\lambda})

of (

P
λ

).

Lemma 5.4
.

The functional
\underline{I}_{\lambda}
satisfies the Palais-Smale condition in the following settings:

(a)

If

, at all levels

,

(b)

If

and

Proof.

Let

be a real number as in the hypotheses and
\{v_{n}\}
be a
(PS_{\mathcal{F},c})
sequence for
\underline{I}_{\lambda}
. Then by lemma
5.2
, upto a subsequence

in
\mathbb{X}_{0}(\Omega)
, where
v_{\lambda}
is a weak solution of (

P
λ

). We show that, upto a subsequence,

strongly in
\mathbb{X}_{0}(\Omega)
.

(a)

Subcritical case, that is,

.
We have

\langle\underline{I}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(v_{n}),v_{n}\rangle=\|v_{n}\|_{1,p}^{p}+[v_{n}]_{s,q}^{q}-\int_{\Omega}h_{\lambda}\left(x,v_{n}\right)v_{n}\ dx.

Since

and

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(\|v_{n}\|_{1,p}^{p}+[v_{n}]_{s,q}^{q})=\int_{\Omega}h_{\lambda}(x,v_{\lambda})v_{\lambda}\ dx=\|v_{\lambda}\|_{1,p}^{p}+[v_{\lambda}]_{s,q}^{q},

where the second equality holds as

is a weak solution of (

P
λ

). Due to non-negativity, upto a subsequence,
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|v_{n}\|_{1,p}
and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}[v_{n}]_{s,q}
exist. Let
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|v_{n}\|_{1,p}=l_{p}
and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}[v_{n}]_{s,q}=l_{q}
. Then we have

(5.6)

l_{p}^{p}+l_{q}^{q}=\|v_{\lambda}\|_{1,p}^{p}+[v_{\lambda}]_{s,q}^{q}.

We claim that
l_{p}=\|v_{\lambda}\|_{1,p}
and
l_{q}=[v_{\lambda}]_{s,q}
. Since

in
\mathbb{X}_{0}(\Omega)
and norm is weakly lower semicontinuous, we have

\|v_{\lambda}\|_{1,p}\leq\liminf\|v_{n}\|_{1,p}=l_{p}\text{ and }\|v_{\lambda}\|_{s,q}\leq\liminf\ [v_{n}]_{s,q}=l_{q}.

strongly in

is a weak solution of (

P
λ

).

(b)

Critical case, that is,

By the Brezis-Lieb lemma
[

]
, we have

\displaystyle\|v_{n}-v_{\lambda}\|^{p}_{1,p}+[v_{n}-v_{\lambda}]^{q}_{s,q}-\|(v_{n}-v_{\lambda})^{+}\|^{p^{*}}_{p^{*}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|v_{n}\|^{p}_{1,p}-\|v_{\lambda}\|^{p}_{1,p}+[v_{n}]^{q}_{s,q}-[v_{\lambda}]^{q}_{s,q}-\|(v_{n}-v_{\lambda})^{+}\|^{p^{*}}_{p^{*}}+o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle\langle\underline{I}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(v_{n}),v_{n}-v_{\lambda}\rangle+\int_{\Omega}h_{\lambda}(x,v_{n})(v_{n}-v_{\lambda})\ dx-\|(v_{n}-v_{\lambda})^{+}\|^{p^{*}}_{p^{*}}+o_{n}(1)

Lemma A.2

The map

Proof.

Continuity of

Let

|\tau_{k}|<\mu

\displaystyle\left|S(\theta+\tau_{k},z_{k})-S(\theta,z)\right|

\displaystyle\left|(z_{k}-z)-\mathcal{L}_{0}^{-1}\left(z_{k}^{\sigma}-z^{\sigma}\right)+\mathcal{L}_{0}^{-1}\left(\frac{\theta+\tau_{k}}{z_{k}^{\gamma}}-\frac{\theta}{z^{\gamma}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|z_{k}-z\|_{C_{d}(U)}d(x)+\left|\mathcal{L}_{0}^{-1}\left(z_{k}^{\sigma}-\frac{\theta}{z_{k}^{\gamma}}\right)-\mathcal{L}_{0}^{-1}\left(z^{\sigma}-\frac{\theta}{z^{\gamma}}\right)\right|+\left|\mathcal{L}_{0}^{-1}\left(\frac{\tau_{k}}{z_{k}^{\gamma}}\right)\right|.

Continuous Fréchet differentiability of

With a reasoning similar to the continuity part, we can show that for

|S(\theta,z+\varphi)-S(\theta,z)-\partial_{z}S(\theta,z)(\varphi)|\\

\left\|S(\theta,z+\varphi)-S(\theta,z)-\partial_{z}S(\theta,z)(\varphi)\right\|_{C_{d}(U)}\rightarrow 0\text{ as }\|\varphi\|_{C_{d}(U)}\rightarrow 0.

∎

Appendix B
L^{\infty}
-regularity

In this section, we discuss the

-regularity of weak solutions to the following equation

(B.1)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u+(-\Delta_{q})^{s}u

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{u^{\delta}}+f(x,u)\text{ in }\Omega

\displaystyle u>0\text{ in }\Omega,\ u

\displaystyle=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega

where
0<\delta<1
and

satisfies
(f1)
,
(f2)
outlined in Section
3
. Due to the singular nature of the nonlinearity near the boundary of the domain, we cannot directly follow the standard approach. To overcome this issue, we first observe a useful technical result, which allows us to reduce the regularity analysis to that of a non-singular problem.

Lemma B.1
.

If
u\in\mathbb{X}_{0}(\Omega)
is a weak solution to equation (
B.1
), then
u
satisfies

-\Delta_{p}\left((u-1)^{+}\right)+(-\Delta_{q})^{s}\left((u-1)^{+}\right)\leq 1+f\left(x,(u-1)^{+}\right)\text{ in }\Omega,

in the weak sense.

The proof of this lemma is based on an adaptation of the approach from Proposition 5.1 of
[
31
]
, suitably modified to account for the present mixed local-nonlocal setting. The computations related to the nonlocal term closely follow those in Theorem A.1 of
[
25
]
. For brevity, we omit the proof here.

Thanks to lemma
B.1
, it is now sufficient to study the regularity of weak solutions to the following non-singular problem:

(B.2)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u+(-\Delta_{q})^{s}u

\displaystyle=f(x,u)\text{ in }\Omega

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.

The next lemma establishes a uniform a priori
L^{\infty}
-estimate for weak solutions to the above equation (
B.2
), which plays a crucial role in the proof of Theorem
1.6
in Section
3
.

Lemma B.2
.

Let
p,q>1
and
u\in\mathbb{X}_{0}(\Omega)
be a positive weak solution of (
B.2
). Then
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and there exists a constant
M_{0}=M_{0}(p,q,s,\Omega,C_{0})>0
, independent of
u
, such that

\|u\|_{\infty}\leq M_{0}\left(1+\int_{\Omega}|u|^{\beta_{1}(r^{*}+1)}\ dx\right)^{\frac{1}{(r^{*}+1)(\beta_{1}-1)}},

where
\displaystyle\beta_{1}=\begin{cases}\frac{p^{*}-1}{p}+1,\text{ if }r^{*}=p^{*}-1,\\[5.69054pt]
\frac{q_{s}^{*}-1}{q}+1,\text{ if }r^{*}=q_{s}^{*}-1.\end{cases}

Proof.

Firstly, we observe that when
p\geq N
, the standard embedding theorems ensure that
u\in L^{t}(\Omega)
for every
1\leq t<\infty
. Consequently, the right-hand side of equation (
B.2
), namely
f(x,u)
, belongs to
L^{t_{1}}(\Omega)
for some
t_{1}>N
. The desired result then follows directly from the global regularity theorem established in
[
4
]
. Therefore, in what follows, we restrict our attention to the non-trivial case
p<N
.

Furthermore, we split the proof into two cases according to the value of
r^{*}
, namely
r^{*}=p^{*}-1
and
r^{*}=q_{s}^{*}-1
. We first consider the case
r^{*}=p^{*}-1
and establish the result through a two-step analysis.

Claim:
If
r^{*}=p^{*}-1,
then
u\in L^{\beta_{1}p^{*}}(\Omega)
, where
\beta_{1}=\frac{p^{*}-1}{p}+1
.

For
\beta>1
and
T>1
, define a function
\phi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

\phi(t):=\begin{cases}0,\text{ if }t<0,\\[2.84526pt]
t^{(\beta-1)p+1},\text{ if }0\leq t\leq T,\\[2.84526pt]
\left((\beta-1)p+1\right)T^{(\beta-1)p}\left(t-T\right)+T^{(\beta-1)p+1},\text{ if }t>T.\end{cases}

Since
\phi
is Lipschitz continuous with
\phi(0)=0
, it follows that if
u\in\mathbb{X}_{0}(\Omega)
, then
\phi(u)\in\mathbb{X}_{0}(\Omega)
as well. Taking
\phi(u)
as a test function in (
B.2
), we obtain

(B.3)

J_{1}+J_{2}=\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)\phi(u)\ dx,

where
\displaystyle J_{1}=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\left(\phi(u)\right)dx
and

\hskip-45.0ptJ_{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q-2}\left(u(x)-u(y)\right)\left((\phi\circ u)(x)-(\phi\circ u)(y)\right)}{|x-y|^{N+qs}}\ dx\ dy.

Let us define
\displaystyle\phi_{1}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\phi^{\prime}(\tau)^{1/p}\ d\tau
and
\displaystyle\phi_{2}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\phi^{\prime}(\tau)^{1/q}\ d\tau,\ t\in\mathbb{R}
. Both
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
are then Lipschitz continuous functions with
\phi_{1}(0)=\phi_{2}(0)=0
and hence
\phi_{1}(u),\ \phi_{2}(u)\in\mathbb{X}_{0}(\Omega)
whenever
u\in\mathbb{X}_{0}(\Omega)
. Moreover,

(B.4)

J_{1}=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\phi^{\prime}(u)\ dx=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\left(\phi_{1}^{\prime}(u)\right)^{p}\ dx=\|\phi_{1}(u)\|^{p}_{1,p}

and by lemma A.2 of
[
14
]
, we have

(B.5)

J_{2}\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\left|\phi_{2}\left(u(x)\right)-\phi_{2}\left(u(y)\right)\right|^{q}}{|x-y|^{N+qs}}\ dx\ dy\geq 0.

Combining (
B.3
), (
B.4
), (
B.5
) and applying Sobolev inequality, we obtain

\|\phi_{1}(u)\|^{p}_{p^{*}}\leq c_{1}\|\phi_{1}(u)\|^{p}_{1,p}\leq c_{1}\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)\phi(u)\ dx.

Next, we observe that
\phi_{1}(t)=\frac{1}{\beta}\left((\beta-1)p+1\right)^{1/p}\psi(t)
, where
\psi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the function defined by

\psi(t):=\begin{cases}0,\text{ if }t<0,\\[2.84526pt]
t^{\beta},\text{ if }0\leq t\leq T,\\[2.84526pt]
\beta T^{\beta-1}(t-T)+T^{\beta},\text{ if }t>T.\end{cases}

Consequently, using the growth condition
(f2)
, we have

\|\psi(u)\|^{p}_{p^{*}}\leq c_{2}\beta^{p}\int_{\Omega}\left(1+u^{p^{*}-1}\right)\phi(u)\ dx,

where the constant

is independent of
u
. Furthermore, noting that
\phi(t)\leq\left((\beta-1)p+1\right)t^{(\beta-1)p+1}
and

, we deduce the following key estimate:

(B.6)

\|\psi(u)\|^{p}_{p^{*}}\leq c_{2}\beta^{p}\left((\beta-1)p+1\right)\left(\int_{\Omega}u^{(\beta-1)p+1}\ dx+\int_{\Omega}u^{p^{*}-p}\left(\psi(u)\right)^{p}dx\right).

Now, choosing

\displaystyle R^{p^{*}-1}\beta_{1}^{p}\int_{\Omega}u^{p^{*}}dx+\left(\int_{\Omega}\left(\psi(u)\right)^{p^{*}}dx\right)^{p/{p^{*}}}\left(\int_{\left\{u>R\right\}}u^{p^{*}}dx\right)^{\frac{p^{*}-p}{p^{*}}}.

Now, thanks to the dominated convergence theorem, we choose

where the RHS is finite and independent of

(B.9)

\left(\int_{\Omega}u^{\beta_{1}p^{*}}dx\right)^{p/p^{*}}<\infty,

that is,

Next, in the second step, we follow the Moser iteration approach to improve the integrability of

(B.10)

\|\psi(u)\|^{p}_{p^{*}}\leq c_{2}\beta^{2p}\left((\beta-1)p+1\right)\left(\int_{\Omega}u^{(\beta-1)p+1}\ dx+\int_{\Omega}u^{(\beta-1)p+p^{*}}dx\right),

which holds for any

(B.11)

\left(\int_{\Omega}u^{\beta p^{*}}dx\right)^{p/p^{*}}\leq c_{2}\beta^{2p}\left((\beta-1)p+1\right)\left(\int_{\Omega}u^{(\beta-1)p+1}\ dx+\int_{\Omega}u^{(\beta-1)p+p^{*}}dx\right).

Now, to handle the first integral on the RHS, we apply Young’s inequality with conjugate exponents
\frac{b}{a}
and
\frac{b}{b-a}
, where

and

. This yields

(B.12)

\int_{\Omega}u^{(\beta-1)p+1}\ dx\leq\frac{a}{b}\int_{\Omega}u^{(\beta-1)p+p^{*}}dx+\frac{b-a}{b}\ |\Omega|.

Substituting this bound into (

\left(\int_{\Omega}u^{\beta p^{*}}dx\right)^{p/p^{*}}\leq c_{3}\beta^{2p}\left((\beta-1)p+1\right)\left(1+\int_{\Omega}u^{(\beta-1)p+p^{*}}dx\right),

where

is also independent of
u
. Applying the elementary inequality

, for any

(B.14)

\left(1+\int_{\Omega}u^{\beta p^{*}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}(\beta-1)}}\leq c_{4}^{\frac{1}{\beta-1}}\beta^{\frac{2p+1}{p(\beta-1)}}\left(1+\int_{\Omega}u^{(\beta-1)p+p^{*}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p(\beta-1)}},

for some constant
c_{4}=c_{4}\left(p,q,s,\Omega,C_{0}\right)>0
, independent of

.

Now, we iterate this relation over a sequence
\{\beta_{m}\}_{m\geq 1}
defined recursively by

\beta_{1}=\frac{p^{*}-1}{p}+1,\ (\beta_{m+1}-1)p=(\beta_{m}-1)p^{*},\ m\geq 1.

This yields

(B.15)

\left(1+\int_{\Omega}u^{\beta_{m+1}p^{*}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}(\beta_{m+1}-1)}}\leq c_{4}^{\sum_{i=2}^{m+1}\frac{1}{\beta_{i}-1}}\left(\prod_{i=2}^{m+1}\beta_{i}^{\frac{1}{\beta_{i}-1}}\right)^{\frac{2p+1}{p}}\left(1+\int_{\Omega}u^{\beta_{1}p^{*}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}(\beta_{1}-1)}}.

Noting that
\beta_{i+1}-1=\left(\frac{p^{*}}{p}\right)^{i}(\beta_{1}-1),\ i\in\mathbb{N}

\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\beta_{i}-1}

such that for every

\left(1+\int_{\Omega}u^{\beta_{m+1}p^{*}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}\left(\beta_{m+1}-1\right)}}\leq M_{0}\left(1+\int_{\Omega}u^{\beta_{1}p^{*}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}\left(\beta_{1}-1\right)}}.

This implies that

for every

. Finally, applying a limiting argument, we infer that

with the uniform bound

\|u\|_{\infty}\leq M_{0}\left(1+\int_{\Omega}u^{\beta_{1}p^{*}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}(\beta_{1}-1)}}.

This concludes the proof in the case

Now, we turn to the case

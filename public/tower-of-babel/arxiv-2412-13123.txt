Partial actions of groups on monoidal categories
Eliezer Batista, Felipe Castro, Mykola Khrypchenko

Abstract
. In this work, we introduce the notion of a partial action of a group on a strict monoidal category. We propose, in the context of Monoidal categories, new constructions analogous to those existing for partial group actions over an algebra such as the globalization, the subalgebra of partial invariants, and the partial smash product.

Primary 18M05; Secondary 18D25.

1.
Introduction

Partial actions of groups were first introduced in the context of the theory of C*-algebras in order to describe some
\mathbb{Z}
-graded C*-algebras which are not isomorphic to a crossed product
[
13
]
. Afterwards, a complete algebraic description of partial actions of groups on algebras and partial crossed products, including the Globalization Theorem, was given in
[
9
]
. Since then, the theory of partial group actions grew rapidly and gained a relevant place, both within pure algebra and in the theory of dynamical systems
[
8
]
.

In this work, we extend the existing boundaries in the theory of partial actions, proposing notions analogous to partial group action over an algebra, its globalization, the subalgebra of partial invariants and the partial smash product, to the context of Monoidal categories. For this purpose, we follow basically the constructions due to D. Tambara in
[
20
]
for group actions on Monoidal categories.

There are several technical obstacles to be overcome in order to give meaning to partial group actions on monoidal categories. Firstly, a group can act partially on an algebra
A
even when
A
has no unity. Then it would be interesting to broaden our context to categories which admit a tensor product among its objects but not necessarily have a unit object. We call these categories semigroupal categories, among them there are those which are monoidal, that is, semigroupal categories which admit unit object. This notion is very general, including the poset of open subsets of a topological space, in which the tensor product is the intersection of open subsets can be viewed as a semigroupal category.

In order to define a partial action of a group
G
on an algebra
A
, we have to choose a family of ideals
\{A_{g}\}_{g\in G}
and a family of algebra isomorphisms
\{\alpha_{g}:A_{g^{-1}}\rightarrow A_{g}\}_{g\in G}
satisfying some compatibility conditions to be composed. We dedicate some effort to making sense of the notion of a tensor ideal on a semigroupal category. One specific example of a partial group action on an algebra is given when the ideals
A_{g}
are principal ideals generated by central idempotent elements
\mathds{1}_{g}\in A
, in particular, the algebra
A
itself must be unital. These are the partial actions to which the globalization theorem applies
[
9
]
. In semigroupal and monoidal categories, one can define central idempotent objects
[
4
]
and then construct special cases of partial actions. From a partial action of a group
G
on a monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
generated by central idempotents one construct the globalization by embedding the category
\mathcal{C}
into a subcategory of functors from
G
(viewed as a monoidal category) to
\mathcal{C}
. This construction follows the ideas given in
[
9
]
for the globalization of a unital partial action of a group
G
on a unital algebra
A
.

Beyond the globalization, one can define two new monoidal categories out of a partial action of a group
G
on a monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
: the partial equivariantization,
\mathcal{C}^{\underline{G}}
, and the partial smash product
\mathcal{C}\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G
. These are generalizations of the equivariantization
\mathcal{C}^{G}
and the semi-direct product
\mathcal{C}[G]
, introduced by D. Tambara in
[
20
]
. In particular, the smash product makes sense only when the category
\mathcal{C}
also admits additive structure, then we assume that
\mathcal{C}
is a
\Bbbk
-linear, abelian monoidal category

If the semigroupal category is monoidal, it is interesting to explore the relation between idempotent objects and the monoidal unit. That approach was done in
[
4

closed idempotents
are objects
e

\mathcal{C}
with a morphism
\pi:\mathds{1}\rightarrow e
such that
(\pi\otimes e)\circ l^{-1}_{e}:e\rightarrow e\otimes e
and
(e\otimes\pi)\circ r^{-1}_{e}:e\rightarrow e\otimes e
are isomorphisms. The
open idempotents
, in their turn, are objects
e
in
\mathcal{C}
with a morphism
\imath:e\rightarrow\mathds{1}
such that
l_{e}\circ(\imath\otimes e):e\otimes e\rightarrow e
and
r_{e}\circ(e\otimes\imath):e\otimes e\rightarrow e
are isomorphisms. Our approach allows one to define idempotent objects even when there is no unit object in the whole category.

Consider the monoidal category

is a central idempotent in this monoidal category, whose tensor product is given by the intersection. Here, the morphisms

and

Example 2.14
.

Consider the monoidal category

Then we have

\sum_{i=1}^{n}(a_{i}+I)\otimes_{R}(b_{i}+I)=0.

Therefore (see, for example,

\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{ji}\triangleright(b_{i}+I)=0

Remark 3.14
.

Actions of a symmetric group on multifusion categories primarily involve permuting the simple components. In the case of Example
3.13
, the simple objects in the category are mutually orthogonal, therefore their fusion rule is very trivial. More elaborated examples, with group actions on multifusion theory with nontrivial fusion rules can highlight structural aspects of the category. It could be interesting to explore the induced partial action on the Grothendieck ring of the category
\mathcal{C}
.

One interesting physical application for group actions on multifusion categories appears in the construction new topological phases and for describing Symmetry Protected Topological (SPT) phases with non invertible symmetries
[
7
]
. More specifically, the simple objects in a fusion category label Topological Defect Lines (TDLs) in a 1+1D quantum field theory. An action maps TDLs to other TDLs, potentially changing their fusion rules. The introduction of partial actions enables us to describe symmetries localized only in a sector of the physical system, which are not globally defined.

\mathcal{C}
defines a partial representation of the group

in the category of endofunctors of
\mathcal{C}
. This relation between partial actions and partial representations will be further explored in the end of Section 6, where we discuss the universal property of the partial smash product

Theorem 3.15
.

Let

\mathcal{C}

\{\mathds{1}_{g}\}_{g\in G}
. Define the family of functors

\pi(g)(X)=T_{g}(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}})
and on morphisms as
\pi(g)(f)=T_{g}(f\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}})

. Then, we have the following natural isomorphisms:

(I)

\pi(e)\cong\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{C}}
.

(II)

\pi(g)\pi(h)\pi(h^{-1})\cong\pi(gh)\pi(h^{-1})
, for all
g,h\in G
.

(III)

\pi(g^{-1})\pi(g)\pi(h)\cong\pi(g^{-1})\pi(gh)

g,h\in G

Proof.

(I) Take an object

, then

\pi(e)(X)=T_{e}(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{e})=T_{e}(X\otimes\mathds{1}).

Applying

we obtain the natural isomorphism (I).

(II) For

and

, we have

\displaystyle\pi(g)\pi(h)\pi(h^{-1})(X)

\displaystyle=\pi(g)\left(T_{h}\left(T_{h^{-1}}\left(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{h}\right)\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\cong\pi(g)\left(T_{h}\left(T_{h^{-1}}\left(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{h}\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\cong\pi(g)(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{h})

\displaystyle=T_{g}(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{h}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\pi(gh)\pi(h^{-1})(X)

\displaystyle=T_{gh}(T_{h^{-1}}(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{h})\otimes\mathds{1}_{(gh)^{-1}})

\displaystyle\cong T_{gh}(T_{h^{-1}}(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{h})\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{(gh)^{-1}})

The naturality of isomorphisms is easily derived from the definition of functors

The natural isomorphism (III) is completely analogous.

Remark 4.2
.

For the case of a partial action
T=\left(\{\mathcal{C}_{g}=\overline{\mathds{1}_{g}\otimes\mathcal{C}}\}_{g\in G},\;\{T_{g}\}_{g\in G},\;\{\gamma_{g,h}\}_{g,h\in G},\;u\right)

becomes a family of monoidal monads.

This family of monads defined from the partial action also gives an example of a structure known in the literature as a Hopf polyad.

Definition 4.3

(
[
5
]
)
.

A
polyad
consists of a set of data
(D,\mathcal{C},T,\mu,\eta)
, in which

•

D
is a category;

•

\mathcal{C}=\left(\mathcal{C}_{i}\right)_{i\in D^{(0)}}
is a family of categories indexed by the objects of
D
;

•

T=\left(T_{a}\right)_{a\in D^{(1)}}
is a family of functors indexed by the morphisms of
D
, with

, for
a\in\Hom_{D}(i,j)
.

•

\mu=\left(\mu_{a,b}\right)_{(a,b)\in D^{(2)}}
is a family of natural transformations indexed by the pairs of composable morphisms of
D
, with
\mu_{a,b}:T_{a}T_{b}\Rightarrow T_{ab}
;

•

\eta=\left(\eta_{i}\right)_{i\in D^{(0)}}
is a family of natural transformations indexed by the objects of

, with

;

subject to the following axioms:

(i)

Given three sequential composable morphisms

,

\mu_{ab,c}\circ(\mu_{a,b}\ast\mathrm{Id}_{T_{c}})=\mu_{a,bc}\circ(\mathrm{Id}_{T_{a}}\ast\mu_{b,c}),

(ii)

Given a morphism
a:i\rightarrow j
in
D
,

\mu_{a,Id_{i}}\circ(\mathrm{Id}_{T_{a}}\ast\eta_{i})=\mathrm{Id}_{T_{a}}=\mu_{Id_{j},a}\circ(\eta_{j}\ast\mathrm{Id}_{T_{a}}).

The category
D
is called the source of the polyad
(D,\mathcal{C},T,\mu,\eta)
, or, equivalently, one can say that this polyad is a
D
-polyad.

Definition 4.4
.

A

is a polyad
(D,\mathcal{C},T,\mu,\eta)
endowed with a monoidal structure on each category
\mathcal{C}_{i}
, for

, such that each functor

, for

, is comonoidal and the natural transformations
\mu_{a,b}
, for
(a,b)\in D^{(2)}
and
\eta_{i}
, for
i\in D^{(0)}
are comonoidal natural transformations.

.

A

X
and

objects in

and

\xi_{a}:T_{a}({{}\mathop{\underline{\phantom{x}}}{}}\otimes{{}\mathop{\underline{\phantom{x}}}{}})\Rightarrow T_{a}({{}\mathop{\underline{\phantom{x}}}{}})\otimes T_{a}({{}\mathop{\underline{\phantom{x}}}{}})

For the case of a partial group action on a monoidal category, we can state the following Theorem.

Theorem 4.6
.

Consider a partial action
T=\left(\{\mathcal{C}_{g}=\overline{\mathds{1}_{g}\otimes\mathcal{C}}\}_{g\in G},\;\{T_{g}\}_{g\in G},\;\{\gamma_{g,h}\}_{g,h\in G},u\right)
of a group
G
on a monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
with all tensor ideals
\mathcal{C}_{g}
generated by central idempotent objects. Then the following data defines a Hopf Polyad:

(i)

The category
\underline{G}
, whose objects are the elements of the group and whose morphisms are identities.

(ii)

C=\{\mathcal{C}_{g}=\overline{\mathds{1}_{g}\otimes\mathcal{C}}\}_{g\in G}
is the family of tensor ideals in
\mathcal{C}
indexed by elements of
G
.

(iii)

P=\{P_{\mathrm{Id}_{g}}=P_{g}=T_{g}\circ T_{g^{-1}}:\mathcal{C}_{g}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}_{g}\}_{g\in G}
is a family of functors indexed by elements of
G
.

(iv)

\mu=\{\mu_{Id_{g},Id_{g}}=\mu_{g}=T_{g}\gamma_{e,g^{-1}}\circ T_{g}\gamma_{g^{-1},g}T_{g^{-1}}:P_{g}P_{g}\Rightarrow P_{g}\}_{g\in G}
and
\{\eta_{g}=\gamma_{g,g^{-1}}^{-1}\circ u:\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{C}_{g}}\Rightarrow P_{g}\}_{g\in G}
are the families of natural transformations indexed by the elements of
G
.

Proof.

Recall that the category
\underline{G}
the morphisms are,

\Hom_{\underline{G}}(g,h)=\begin{cases}\emptyset,&\text{ if }g\neq h\\
\{\mathrm{Id}_{g}\},&\text{ if }g=h,\end{cases}

then the only possible compositions are of the form

. Therefore, the only functors allowed for a polyad defined on

are

, choose them as the previously defined in

P_{g}=T_{g}\circ T_{g^{-1}}:\mathcal{C}_{g}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}_{g}
. Also, for composable morphisms

of Proposition
4.1
. Similarly, the unit natural transformations
\eta_{g}
, for

can also be chosen to be those defined in
Proposition

4.1
.

The identities

\mu_{\mathrm{Id}_{g},\mathrm{Id}_{g}}\circ\left(\mathrm{Id}_{P_{g}}\ast\mu_{\mathrm{Id}_{g},\mathrm{Id}_{g}}\right)=\mu_{\mathrm{Id}_{g},\mathrm{Id}_{g}}\circ\left(\mu_{\mathrm{Id}_{g},\mathrm{Id}_{g}}\ast\mathrm{Id}_{P_{g}}\right)

were already verified in (

) and (

).

In the case of a partial action with tensor ideals

given by

, all the monads

are strongly monoidal, in particular, they are comonoidal with

Finally, the left and right fusion operators can be written in our context as

5.
Globalization

Definition 5.1
.

Let
T=(\{\mathcal{C}_{g}\}_{g\in G},\{T_{g}\}_{g\in G},\{\gamma_{g,h}\}_{g,h\in G},u)
be a partial action of a group
G
on a semigroupal category
\mathcal{C}
. A

of
T
is a global action
T^{\prime}=(\{T^{\prime}_{g}\}_{g\in G},\{\gamma^{\prime}_{g,h}\}_{g,h\in G},u^{\prime})
of
G

\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
together with a morphism
(F,\{\tau_{g}\}_{g\in G})
from
T
to
T^{\prime}
, such that

(i)

the subcategory
\overline{F(\mathcal{C})}
is a tensor ideal in
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
.

(ii)

\bigcup_{g\in G}\overline{T_{g}F(\mathcal{C})}=\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
.

(iii)

for any
U\in\overline{F(\mathcal{C})}
if
T^{\prime}_{g}(U)\in\overline{F(\mathcal{C})}
, then
U\in\overline{F({\mathcal{C}_{{g^{-1}}}})}
,

(iv)

The functor
F
establishes a semigroupal equivalence between
\mathcal{C}
and
\overline{F(\mathcal{C})}
and an isomorphism between the partial actions

T=(\{\mathcal{C}_{g}\}_{g\in G},\{T_{g}\}_{g\in G},\{\gamma_{g,h}\}_{g,h\in G},u)

and

\widetilde{T}=(\{\overline{F(\mathcal{C})\cap T^{\prime}_{g}(F(\mathcal{C}))}\}_{g\in G},\{\gamma^{\prime}_{g,h}\}_{g,h\in G},u^{\prime}).

Item (ii) in the above definition means that a globalizations should be “minimal” in certain sense, that is, the category
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
where the original category
\mathcal{C}
is embedded is basically the orbit of the image of
\mathcal{C}
by the functor
F
.

One can deduce from
(iii)
that, for
U\in\overline{F(\mathcal{C})}
, if
T^{\prime}_{g}(U)\in\overline{F(\mathcal{C})}
, then
T^{\prime}_{g}(U)\in\overline{F({\mathcal{C}_{{g}}})}
. Indeed, as
U\in\overline{F(\mathcal{C})}
, there exists an object
V\in\mathcal{C}
with

, therefore,
T^{\prime}_{g}(U)\cong T^{\prime}_{g}(F(V))
, using the isomorphism

, we have

T^{\prime}_{g}(U)\cong T^{\prime}_{g}(F(V))=(\tau_{g})_{V}(F(T_{g}(V)))\cong F(T_{g}(V)).

Item
(iii)
assures that
U\in\overline{F(\mathcal{C}_{g^{-1}})}
, then, by this line of isomorphisms and because each tensor ideal
\mathcal{C}_{g}
is closed by isomorphisms, one can see that
V\in\mathcal{C}_{g^{-1}}
and then
T^{\prime}_{g}(U)\in\overline{F(\mathcal{C}_{g})}
.

In what follows, we are going to see that the globalization of a partial action of
G
on a monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
exists, at least for the case of partial actions whose tensor ideals
\mathcal{C}_{g}
are generated by central idempotent objects in
\mathcal{C}
.

Consider the category
[\underline{G},\mathcal{C}]
of functors from the monoidal category
\underline{G}
of Example

to
\mathcal{C}
. This category can be endowed with a natural structure of a semigroupal category. Given

and
\alpha_{1}\colon F_{1}\Rightarrow F^{\prime}_{1},\alpha_{2}\colon F_{2}\Rightarrow F^{\prime}_{2}

one can define their tensor products

\begin{split}F_{1}\bullet F_{2}\colon G&\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}\\
g&\longmapsto F_{1}(g)\otimes F_{2}(g)\end{split}\quad\text{and}\quad\begin{split}\alpha_{1}\bullet\alpha_{2}\colon F_{1}\bullet F_{2}&\Longrightarrow F^{\prime}_{1}\bullet F^{\prime}_{2}\\

Moreover, if

is a monoidal category, then one can define a monoidal unit on

Proposition 5.2
.

There is a global action of the group
G
on the semigroupal (resp. monoidal) category
[\underline{G},\mathcal{C}]

Proof.

For each element
g\in G
and for each functor

\mathcal{T}_{g}(F)(h)=F(hg)

\mathcal{T}_{g}(\mathcal{T}_{h}(F))(k)=\mathcal{T}_{h}(F)(kg)=F(kgh),

\mathcal{T}_{gh}(F)(k)=F(kgh).

Also, for each
g\in G

By construction, we have that

. As

\Tr(X)=\bigoplus_{g\in G}T_{g}(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}).

Lemma 6.8
.

Under the conditions of the Definition
6.7
, for each list of elements
g,g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\in G
there is an isomorphism

\widetilde{\sigma}^{TrX}_{g,g_{1},\ldots g_{n}}:T_{g}\left(\Tr(X)\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathds{1}_{g_{n}}\right)\rightarrow\Tr(X)\otimes\mathds{1}_{g}\otimes\mathds{1}_{gg_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathds{1}_{gg_{n}}

such that
\left(\Tr(X),\{\widetilde{\sigma}^{TrX}_{g,g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}}\}_{g,g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\in G}\right)
defines a partially equivariant object in
\mathcal{C}^{\underline{G}}
.

Proof.

Indeed, for
g,g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\in G
and
X\in\mathcal{C}
, we have

\displaystyle\hskip-11.99998ptT_{g}\left(\Tr(X)\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathds{1}_{g_{n}}\right)=T_{g}\left(\left(\bigoplus_{h\in G}T_{h}(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}})\right)\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathds{1}_{g_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle\cong T_{g}\left(\bigoplus_{h\in G}\left(T_{h}(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}})\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathds{1}_{g_{n}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{h\in G}T_{g}\left(T_{h}(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}})\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathds{1}_{g_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{h\in G}T_{g}\left(T_{h}(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}})\otimes\mathds{1}_{h}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathds{1}_{g_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{h\in G}T_{g}\left(T_{h}(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}})\otimes T_{h}(\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{(gh)^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}g_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}g_{n}})\right)

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{h\in G}T_{g}\left(T_{h}(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{(gh)^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}g_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}g_{n}})\right)

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{h\in G}T_{gh}\left(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{(gh)^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}g_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}g_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{h\in G}T_{gh}\left(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{(gh)^{-1}}\right)\otimes T_{gh}\left(\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{(gh)^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}g_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}g_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{h\in G}T_{gh}\left(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{(gh)^{-1}}\right)\otimes\mathds{1}_{g}\otimes\mathds{1}_{gh}\otimes\mathds{1}_{gg_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathds{1}_{gg_{n}}

Then, ignoring the obvious isomorphism coming from the distributivity of the tensor product with respect to the direct sum, the additivity of the functor
T_{g}
and the absorptions of the respective units, one can write the isomorphism
\widetilde{\sigma}^{\Tr(X)}_{g,g_{1},\ldots g_{n}}
as the composition:

(49)

\begin{split}\widetilde{\sigma}^{\Tr(X)}_{g,g_{1},\ldots g_{n}}&=\left(\bigoplus_{h\in G}\left(T_{gh}(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{(gh)^{-1}})\otimes\left(\varphi^{gh}_{g,gg_{1},\ldots,gg_{n}}\right)^{-1}\right)\right)\\
&\quad\circ\left(\bigoplus_{h\in G}\left(J^{gh}_{X\otimes\mathds{1}_{(gh)^{-1}},\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{(gh)^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}g_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}g_{n}}}\right)^{-1}\right)\\
&\quad\circ\left(\bigoplus_{h\in G}\left(\gamma_{g,h}\right)_{X\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{(gh)^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}g_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}g_{n}}}\right)\\
&\quad\circ\left(\bigoplus_{h\in G}T_{g}\left(T_{h}(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}})\otimes J^{h}_{X\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}},\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{(gh)^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}g_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}g_{n}}}\right)\right)\\
&\quad\circ\left(\bigoplus_{h\in G}T_{g}\left(T_{h}(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}})\otimes\varphi^{h}_{g^{-1},g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}}\right)\right).\end{split}

We shall omit here the long, but straightforward chain of calculations needed to check that the family
\{\widetilde{\sigma}^{\Tr(X)}_{g,g_{1},\ldots g_{n}}\}_{g,g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}}
satisfies the commutativity of the diagram (
46
). Therefore, the data
\left(\Tr(X),\{\widetilde{\sigma}^{\Tr(X)}_{g,g_{1},\ldots g_{n}}\}_{g,g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}}\right)
define a partially equivariant object in
\mathcal{C}^{\underline{G}}
.

Theorem 6.9
.

Let

T=\left(\{\mathcal{C}_{g}=\overline{\mathds{1}_{g}\otimes\mathcal{C}}\}_{g\in G},\{T_{g}\colon\mathcal{C}_{g^{-1}}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}_{g}\}_{g\in G},\{\gamma_{g,h}\}_{g,h\in G},u\right)

be a partial action of a finite group
G
on a monoidal and abelian category
\mathcal{C}
defined by a family of central idempotent objects
\{\mathds{1}_{g}\}_{g\in G}
in which the functors
T_{g}
are additive. Then the trace defines a semigroupal, but not monoidal, category and additive functor
\Tr\colon\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\underline{G}}
.

Proof.

For any morphism
f:X\rightarrow Y
in
\mathcal{C}
, define the map

\Tr(f)=\bigoplus_{g\in G}T_{g}(f\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}).

This is a well defined morphism in
\mathcal{C}
between
\Tr(X)
and
\Tr(Y)
by the universal property of the direct sum. In order to check that
\Tr(f)
is indeed a morphism in the category
\mathcal{C}^{\underline{G}}
, take an element
g\in G
and an object
Z\in\mathcal{C}_{g^{-1}}
, then

\displaystyle\hskip-11.99998pt\left(\Tr(f)\otimes T_{g}(Z)\right)\circ\left(\sigma^{\Tr(X)}_{g}\right)_{Z}=\left(\Tr(f)\otimes\mathds{1}_{g}\otimes T_{g}(Z)\right)\circ\left(\widetilde{\sigma}^{\Tr(X)}_{g}\otimes T_{g}(Z)\right)\circ\left(J^{g}_{\Tr(X)\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}},Z}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\widetilde{\sigma}^{\Tr(Y)}_{g}\otimes T_{g}(Z)\right)\circ\left(T_{g}(\Tr(f)\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}})\otimes T_{g}(Z)\right)\circ\left(J^{g}_{\Tr(X)\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}},Z}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\widetilde{\sigma}^{\Tr(Y)}_{g}\otimes T_{g}(Z)\right)\circ\left(J^{g}_{\Tr(Y)\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}},Z}\right)^{-1}T_{g}\left(\Tr(f)\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}})\otimes Z\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\sigma^{\Tr(Y)}_{g}\right)_{Z}\circ T_{g}\left(\Tr(f)\otimes Z\right).

Therefore,
\Tr
is a functor between the categories
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\underline{G}}
.

The functor
\Tr
is additive because of the universal property of the direct sum and it is semigroupal from the distributivity of the tensor product with respect to the direct sum. It is not monoidal because

\Tr(\mathds{1})=\bigoplus_{g\in G}T_{g}(\mathds{1}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}})\cong\bigoplus_{g\in G}\mathds{1}_{g}.\qed

In general, for
(X,\sigma)\in\mathcal{C}^{\underline{G}}
, we have

\Tr(X)=\bigoplus_{g\in G}T_{g}(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}})\cong\bigoplus_{g\in G}X\otimes\mathds{1}_{g}.

Then
X
is isomorphic to a direct summand of
\Tr(X)
as objects in
\mathcal{C}
. But, in general, this isomorphism cannot be lifted to the category
\mathcal{C}^{\underline{G}}
.

6.3.
The partial smash product
\mathcal{C}\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G

—

Given a partial action
\alpha=\left(\{A_{g}\}_{g\in G},\{\alpha_{g}:A_{g^{-1}}\rightarrow A_{g}\}_{g\in G}\right)
of a group
G
on an algebra
A
, one can define the partial smash product
A\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G

[
9
]
. Basically, the partial smash product, as a vector space, is simply the direct sum

(A\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G)^{(0)}=\bigoplus_{g\in G}A_{g}\delta_{g},

and the multiplication is given by

(50)

(a_{g}\delta_{g})(b_{h}\delta_{h})=\alpha_{g}(\alpha_{g^{-1}}(a_{g})b_{h})\delta_{gh}.

One cannot guarantee, in general, that even this above defined multiplication is associative. Neither is it possible to ensure the existence of unity. Associativity of the multiplication (
50
) can be ensured by requiring that the ideals
A_{g}\trianglelefteq A
be non-degenerate or idempotent.

In the case where
\alpha
is a unital partial action, that is, the ideal
A_{g}
is generated by a central idempotent element
\mathds{1}_{g}\in A
for all
g\in G
, then the ideals become idempotent, making the multiplication (
50
) associative and the element
\mathds{1}_{e}\delta_{e}
becomes automatically the unit for this multiplication. Moreover, formula (
50
) admits an equivalent simplified version as

(a_{g}\delta_{g})(b_{h}\delta_{h})=a_{g}\alpha_{g}(b_{h}\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}})\delta_{gh}.

The partial group actions on semigroupal categories can be interpreted as a categorification of partial group actions on algebras. Among the partial group actions on semigroupal categories, the unital ones are those with a richer class of examples and those which allow one to define new and relevant constructions upon these actions. Then, for the construction of a partial smash product, we will restrict ourselves to unital partial actions, that is partial actions in which the tensor ideals are generated by central idempotent objects. As the categorical analogue of the partial smash product will require additive structures, in this section we shall consider
\mathcal{C}
being a
\Bbbk
-linear abelian and monoidal category.

Definition 6.10
.

Let
T=\left(\{\mathcal{C}_{g}=\overline{\mathds{1}_{g}\otimes\mathcal{C}}\}_{g\in G},\{T_{g}\}_{g\in G},\{\gamma_{g,h}\}_{g,h\in G},u\right)
be a unital partial action of a group
G
on a
\Bbbk
-linear, abelian and monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
. The partial smash product is the category
\mathcal{C}\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G\coloneqq\bigoplus_{g\in G}\mathcal{C}_{g}\delta_{g}
whose objects are

\displaystyle(\mathcal{C}\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G)^{(0)}

\displaystyle=\left\{\bigoplus_{g\in G}X_{g}\delta_{g}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}X_{g}\in(\mathcal{C}_{g})^{(0)}\right\}

and morphisms

\displaystyle(\mathcal{C}\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G)^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\left\{\bigoplus_{g\in G}(f_{g},g)\colon\bigoplus_{g\in G}X_{g}\delta_{g}\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{g\in G}X^{\prime}_{g}\delta_{g}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}f_{g}\colon X_{g}\longrightarrow X^{\prime}_{g}\right\}

with monoidal structure given by

(51)

(X_{g}\delta_{g})\boxtimes(X_{h}\delta_{h})\coloneqq(X_{g}\otimes T_{g}(X_{h}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}})))\delta_{gh}

and

(52)

(f_{g},g)\boxtimes(f_{h},h)\coloneqq(f_{g}\otimes T_{g}(f_{h}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}),gh).

The unit is
\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{C}\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G}\coloneqq\mathds{1}_{e}\delta_{e}=\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{C}}\delta_{e}
.

For the case of
T:\underline{G}\rightarrow{\sf Aut}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{C})
being a global action of
G
by monoidal autoequivalences, then the partial smash product
\mathcal{C}\underline{\rtimes}G
coincides with the semidirect product
\mathcal{C}[G]=\bigoplus_{g\in G}\mathcal{C}
, introduced by D. Tambara in
[
20
]
.

Proposition 6.11
.

Let
T=\left(\{\mathcal{C}_{g}=\overline{\mathds{1}_{g}\otimes\mathcal{C}}\}_{g\in G},\{T_{g}\}_{g\in G},\{\gamma_{g,h}\}_{g,h\in G},u\right)
be a unital partial action of a group
G
on a monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
. Then the partial smash product
\left(\mathcal{C}\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G,\boxtimes,\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{C}\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G}\right)
defines a monoidal category.

Proof.

Even when the monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
is supposed to be strict, the form of the tensor product defined in (
51
) and (
52
) gives rise to nontrivial associator and unit morphisms.

For the associator, consider the product

(X_{g}\delta_{g}\boxtimes X_{h}\delta_{h})\boxtimes X_{l}\delta_{l}=(X_{g}\otimes T_{g}(X_{h}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}))\delta_{gh}\boxtimes X_{l}\delta_{l}=X_{g}\otimes T_{g}(X_{h}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}})\otimes T_{gh}(X_{l}\otimes\mathds{1}_{(gh)^{-1}})\delta_{ghl}.

On the other hand

X_{g}\delta_{g}\boxtimes(X_{h}\delta_{h}\boxtimes X_{l}\delta_{l})=X_{g}\delta_{g}\boxtimes(X_{h}\otimes T_{h}(X_{l}\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}}))\delta_{hl}=X_{g}\otimes T_{g}(X_{h}\otimes T_{h}(X_{l}\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}})\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}})\delta_{ghl}.

Then, we have the following sequence of isomorphisms:

\displaystyle\hskip-11.99998ptX_{g}\otimes T_{g}(X_{h}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}})\otimes T_{gh}(X_{l}\otimes\mathds{1}_{(gh)^{-1}})\delta_{ghl}=

\displaystyle=X_{g}\otimes T_{g}(X_{h}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}})\otimes T_{gh}(X_{l}\otimes\mathds{1}_{(gh)^{-1}})\otimes\mathds{1}_{g}\otimes\mathds{1}_{gh}\delta_{ghl}

\displaystyle\cong X_{g}\otimes T_{g}(X_{h}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}})\otimes T_{gh}(X_{l}\otimes\mathds{1}_{(gh)^{-1}})\otimes T_{gh}(\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{(gh)^{-1}})\delta_{ghl}

\displaystyle\cong X_{g}\otimes T_{g}(X_{h}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}})\otimes T_{gh}(X_{l}\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{(gh)^{-1}})\delta_{ghl}

\displaystyle\cong X_{g}\otimes T_{g}(X_{h}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}})\otimes T_{g}(T_{h}(X_{l}\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{(gh)^{-1}}))\delta_{ghl}

\displaystyle\cong X_{g}\otimes T_{g}(X_{h}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}})\otimes T_{g}(T_{h}(X_{l}\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}})\otimes T_{h}(\mathds{1}_{(gh)^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}}))\delta_{ghl}

\displaystyle\cong X_{g}\otimes T_{g}(X_{h}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}})\otimes T_{g}(T_{h}(X_{l}\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}})\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}})\delta_{ghl}

\displaystyle\cong X_{g}\otimes T_{g}(X_{h}\otimes T_{h}(X_{l}\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}})\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}})\delta_{ghl}.

Then the associator can be written explicitly as

\displaystyle A_{X_{g}\delta_{g},X_{h}\delta_{h},X_{l}\delta_{l}}

\displaystyle=\left(X_{g}\otimes J^{g}_{X_{h}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}},T_{h}(X_{l}\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}})\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}}\right)\circ

\displaystyle\circ\left(X_{g}\otimes T_{g}(X_{h}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}})\otimes T_{g}(T_{h}(X_{l}\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}})\otimes(\varphi^{h}_{g^{-1}})^{-1})\right)

\displaystyle\circ\left(X_{g}\otimes T_{g}(X_{h}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}})\otimes T_{g}((J^{h})^{-1}_{X_{l}\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}},\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{(gh)^{-1}}})\right)

\displaystyle\circ\left(X_{g}\otimes T_{g}(X_{h}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}})\otimes\left(\gamma_{g,h}\right)^{-1}_{X_{l}\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{(gh)^{-1}}}\right)

\displaystyle\circ\left(X_{g}\otimes T_{g}(X_{h}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}})\otimes J^{gh}_{X_{l}\otimes\mathds{1}_{(gh)^{-1}},\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{(gh)^{-1}}}\right)

\displaystyle\circ\left(X_{g}\otimes T_{g}(X_{h}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}})\otimes T_{gh}(X_{l}\otimes\mathds{1}_{(gh)^{-1}})\otimes\varphi^{gh}_{g}\mathds{1}_{g}\right)

The left and right unit morphisms,

\displaystyle L\colon\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{C}\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G}\boxtimes{{}\mathop{\underline{\phantom{x}}}{}}\Rightarrow\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{C}\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G}({{}\mathop{\underline{\phantom{x}}}{}})\colon\mathcal{C}\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G

and

\displaystyle R\colon{{}\mathop{\underline{\phantom{x}}}{}}\boxtimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{C}\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G}\Rightarrow\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{C}\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G}({{}\mathop{\underline{\phantom{x}}}{}})\colon\mathcal{C}\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G

are given, respectively by

\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{C}\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G}\boxtimes X_{g}\delta_{g}=\mathds{1}_{e}\delta_{e}\boxtimes X_{g}\delta_{g}=(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{C}}\otimes T_{e}(X_{g}\otimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{C}}))\delta_{g}=T_{e}(X_{g})\delta_{g}\cong X_{g}\delta_{g}

and

X_{g}\delta_{g}\boxtimes\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{C}\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G}=X_{g}\delta_{g}\boxtimes\mathds{1}_{e}\delta_{e}=(X_{g}\otimes T_{g}(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{C}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}))\delta_{g}=(X_{g}\otimes T_{g}(\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}))\delta_{g}\cong X_{g}\delta_{g},

or, explicitly,

L_{X_{g}\delta_{g}}=u^{-1}_{X_{g}}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad R_{X_{g}\delta_{g}}=X_{g}\otimes(\varphi^{g})^{-1}.

For sake of brevity, we shall omit the proofs of the triangle and the pentagon axioms for the monoidal structure of the category
\mathcal{C}\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G
, consisting only of a long and exhaustive calculation with composition of morphisms which cannot be properly displayed in this paper and is not very instructive. Even in the global case, where the associator consists of the composition of only three morphisms instead of six, as in the partial case, the verification of the pentagon axiom took four pages (see
[
20
]
, Section 8).
∎

Given a partial action
T
of a group
G
on a monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
, one can define a functor

(53)

\begin{split}\pi_{0}\colon\underline{G}&\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G\\
g&\longmapsto\mathds{1}_{g}\delta_{g}\end{split},

in which
\underline{G}
is the monoidal category whose objects are the elements of
G
, the only morphisms are the identities on objects and the tensor product is the product among the elements of the group.

Proposition 6.12
.

The functor presented in Equation (
53
) satisfies the following properties:

(i)

\pi_{0}(e)=\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{C}\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G},

(ii)

\pi_{0}(g)\boxtimes\pi_{0}(h)\boxtimes\pi_{0}(h^{-1})\cong\pi_{0}(gh)\boxtimes\pi_{0}(h^{-1}),

(iii)

\pi_{0}(g^{-1})\boxtimes\pi_{0}(g)\boxtimes\pi_{0}(h)\cong\pi_{0}(g^{-1})\boxtimes\pi_{0}(gh),

(iv)

\pi_{0}(g)\boxtimes\pi_{0}(g^{-1})\boxtimes\pi_{0}(g)\cong\pi_{0}(g),

for any
g,h\in G
.

Proof.

(i)
:

\pi_{0}(e)=\mathds{1}_{e}\delta_{e}=\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{C}\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G}.

(ii)
: Given any
g,h\in G
, we have that

\displaystyle\pi_{0}(g)\boxtimes\pi_{0}(h)\boxtimes\pi_{0}(h^{-1})

\displaystyle=(\mathds{1}_{g}\delta_{g})\boxtimes(\mathds{1}_{h}\delta_{h})\boxtimes(\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}}\delta_{h^{-1}})

\displaystyle=(\mathds{1}_{g}\delta_{g})\boxtimes((\mathds{1}_{h}\otimes T_{h}(\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}}))\delta_{e})

\displaystyle\cong(\mathds{1}_{g}\delta_{g})\boxtimes(\mathds{1}_{h}\delta_{e})

\displaystyle=(\mathds{1}_{g}\otimes T_{g}(\mathds{1}_{h}\otimes 1_{g^{-1}}))\delta_{g}

\displaystyle\cong(\mathds{1}_{g}\otimes\mathds{1}_{gh})\delta_{g}

and

\displaystyle\pi_{0}(gh)\boxtimes\pi_{0}(h^{-1})

\displaystyle=(\mathds{1}_{gh}\delta_{gh})\boxtimes(\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}}\delta_{h^{-1}})

\displaystyle=(\mathds{1}_{gh}\otimes T_{gh}(\mathds{1}_{h^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{(gh)^{-1}}))\delta_{g}

\displaystyle=(\mathds{1}_{gh}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g})\delta_{g}.

(iii)
:

\displaystyle\pi_{0}(g^{-1})\boxtimes\pi_{0}(g)\boxtimes\pi_{0}(h)

\displaystyle=(\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}\delta_{g^{-1}})\boxtimes(\mathds{1}_{g}\delta_{g})\boxtimes(\mathds{1}_{h}\delta_{h})

\displaystyle=((\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}\otimes T_{g^{-1}}(\mathds{1}_{g}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g}))\delta_{e})\boxtimes(\mathds{1}_{h}\delta_{h})

\displaystyle\cong(\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}\otimes T_{e}(\mathds{1}_{h}\otimes\mathds{1}_{e}))\delta_{h}

\displaystyle\cong(\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{h})\delta_{h}

and

\displaystyle\pi_{0}(g^{-1})\boxtimes\pi_{0}(gh)

\displaystyle=(\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}\delta_{g^{-1}})\boxtimes(\mathds{1}_{gh}\delta_{gh})

\displaystyle=(\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}\otimes T_{g^{-1}}(\mathds{1}_{gh}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g}))\delta_{h}

\displaystyle\cong(\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{h})\delta_{h}.

(iv)
: It follows directly from
(iii)
,
(i)
and from the monoidal structure of
\mathcal{C}\underline{\rtimes}G
.
∎

Given a partial action
T
of a group
G
on a monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
, one can define a functor

(54)

\begin{split}\phi_{0}\colon\mathcal{C}&\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G\\
(\mathcal{C})^{(0)}\ni X&\longmapsto X\delta_{e}\\
\mathcal{C}^{(1)}\ni f&\longmapsto(f,e)\end{split}

It is easy to see that the functor
\phi_{0}
is monoidal. Indeed,

\displaystyle\phi_{0}(X)\boxtimes\phi_{0}(Y)

\displaystyle=(X\delta_{e})\boxtimes(Y\delta_{e})

\displaystyle=(X\otimes T_{e}(Y\otimes\mathds{1}_{e}))\delta_{e}

\displaystyle\cong(X\otimes Y)\delta_{e}

\displaystyle\cong\phi_{0}(X\otimes Y),

and

\phi_{0}(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{C}})=\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{C}}\delta_{e}=\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{C}\underline{\rtimes}G}.

Proposition 6.13
.

The functors
\pi_{0}:\underline{G}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G
, defined by (
53
) and
\phi_{0}:\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G
satisfy the following identities, for each object
X\in\mathcal{C}
and each element
g\in G
:

(i)

\phi_{0}(X)\boxtimes\pi_{0}(g)\boxtimes\pi_{0}(g^{-1})\cong\pi_{0}(g)\boxtimes\pi_{0}(g^{-1})\boxtimes\phi_{0}(X)
.

(ii)

\pi_{0}(g)\boxtimes\phi_{0}(X)\boxtimes\pi_{0}(g^{-1})\cong\phi_{0}(T_{g}(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}))
.

Proof.

For item (i), on the one hand, we have

\displaystyle\phi_{0}(X)\boxtimes\pi_{0}(g)\boxtimes\pi_{0}(g^{-1})

\displaystyle=X\delta_{e}\boxtimes\mathds{1}_{g}\delta_{g}\boxtimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}\delta_{g^{-1}}

\displaystyle=X\delta_{e}\boxtimes(\mathds{1}_{g}\otimes T_{g}(\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}))\delta_{e}

\displaystyle\cong X\delta_{e}\boxtimes\mathds{1}_{g}\delta_{e}

\displaystyle=(X\otimes T_{e}(\mathds{1}_{g}\otimes\mathds{1}_{e}))\delta_{e}

\displaystyle\cong(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{g})\delta_{e},

and, on the other hand,

\displaystyle\pi_{0}(g)\boxtimes\pi_{0}(g^{-1})\boxtimes\phi_{0}(X)

\displaystyle=\mathds{1}_{g}\delta_{g}\boxtimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}\delta_{g^{-1}}\boxtimes X\delta_{e}

\displaystyle=(\mathds{1}_{g}\otimes T_{g}(\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}))\delta_{e}\boxtimes X\delta_{e}

\displaystyle\cong\mathds{1}_{g}\delta_{e}\boxtimes X\delta_{e}

\displaystyle=(\mathds{1}_{g}\otimes T_{e}(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{e}))\delta_{e}

\displaystyle\cong(\mathds{1}_{g}\otimes X)\delta_{e}.

Now, for item (ii),

\displaystyle\pi_{0}(g)\boxtimes\phi_{0}(X)\boxtimes\pi_{0}(g^{-1})

\displaystyle=\mathds{1}_{g}\delta_{g}\boxtimes X\delta_{e}\boxtimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}\delta_{g^{-1}}

\displaystyle=\mathds{1}_{g}\delta_{g}\boxtimes(X\otimes T_{e}(\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{e})\delta_{g^{-1}}

\displaystyle\cong\mathds{1}_{g}\delta_{g}\boxtimes(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}})\delta_{g^{-1}}

\displaystyle=(\mathds{1}_{g}\otimes T_{g}(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}))\delta_{e}

\displaystyle\cong(T_{g}(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}))\delta_{e}

\displaystyle=\phi_{0}(T_{g}(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}})).\qed

Definition 6.14
.

Consider a group
G
, two monoidal categories
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
, and a unital partial action

T=\left(\{T_{g}\}_{g\in G},\{\gamma_{g,h}\}_{g,h\in G},u\right)

of
G
on
\mathcal{C}
. A covariant pair
(\phi,\pi)
related to
\mathcal{D}
is a pair of functors
\phi:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
and
\pi:\underline{G}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
such that

(i)

The functor
\phi:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
is monoidal.

(ii)

The functor
\pi:\underline{G}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
satisfies

(i)

\pi(e)=\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{D}},

(ii)

\pi(g)\bar{\otimes}\pi(h)\bar{\otimes}\pi(h^{-1})\cong\pi(gh)\bar{\otimes}\pi(h^{-1})
, for all
g,h\in G
;

(iii)

\pi(g^{-1})\bar{\otimes}\pi(g)\bar{\otimes}\pi(h)\cong\pi(g^{-1})\bar{\otimes}\pi(gh)
, for all
g,h\in G
.

(iii)

The functors
\phi
and
\pi
satisfy the following compatibility relations, for all objects
X\in\mathcal{C}
and for all elements
g\in G
:

(i)

\phi(X)\bar{\otimes}\pi(g)\bar{\otimes}\pi(g^{-1})\cong\pi(g)\bar{\otimes}\pi(g^{-1})\bar{\otimes}\phi(X)
.

(ii)

\pi(g)\bar{\otimes}\phi(X)\bar{\otimes}\pi(g^{-1})\cong\phi(T_{g}(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}))
.

Theorem 6.15
.

Given a covariant pair
(\phi,\pi)
related with a category
\mathcal{D}
, there exists a monoidal functor

\Psi\colon\mathcal{C}\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G\longrightarrow\mathcal{D}

such that the following diagram commutes:
\mathcal{C}\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G
\mathcal{C}
\underline{G}
\mathcal{D}
\phi_{0}
\phi
\pi_{0}
\pi
\Psi

Proof.

Define

\displaystyle\Psi\colon\mathcal{C}\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{D}

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{g\in G}X_{g}\delta_{g}

\displaystyle\longmapsto\bigoplus_{g\in G}\phi(X_{g})\mathop{\bar{\otimes}}\pi(g)

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{g\in G}(f_{g},g)

\displaystyle\longmapsto\bigoplus_{g\in G}(\phi(f_{g})\mathop{\bar{\otimes}}\pi(g)).

Given
X\in(\mathcal{C})^{(0)}
, we have that

\Psi(\phi_{0}(X))=\Psi(X\delta_{e})=\phi(X)\mathop{\bar{\otimes}}\pi(e)=\phi(X)\mathop{\bar{\otimes}}\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{D}}\cong\phi(X).

Now, consider a morphism
f:X\rightarrow Y
in
\mathcal{C}
, then

\Psi(\phi_{0}(f))=\Psi(f,e)=\phi(f)\mathop{\bar{\otimes}}\pi(e)=\phi(f)\mathop{\bar{\otimes}}\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{D}}\cong\phi(f).

Given
g\in G
,

\displaystyle\Psi(\pi_{0}(g))

\displaystyle=\Psi(\mathds{1}_{g}\delta_{g})

\displaystyle=\phi(\mathds{1}_{g})\mathop{\bar{\otimes}}\pi(g)

\displaystyle\cong\phi(T_{g}(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{C}}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}))\mathop{\bar{\otimes}}\pi(g)

\displaystyle\cong\pi(g)\mathop{\bar{\otimes}}\phi(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{C}})\mathop{\bar{\otimes}}\pi(g^{-1})\mathop{\bar{\otimes}}\pi(g)

\displaystyle\cong\pi(g)\mathop{\bar{\otimes}}\pi(g^{-1})\mathop{\bar{\otimes}}\pi(g)\mathop{\bar{\otimes}}\phi(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{C}})

\displaystyle\cong\pi(g)\mathop{\bar{\otimes}}\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{D}}

\displaystyle\cong\pi(g).

In order to verify that the functor
\Psi
is monoidal, first observe that

\Psi(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{C}\underline{\rtimes}G})=\Psi(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{C}}\delta_{e})=\phi(\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{C}})\bar{\otimes}\pi(e)=\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{D}}\bar{\otimes}\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{D}}\cong\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{D}}.

Now, take two objects
X_{g}\delta_{g}
and
Y_{h}\delta_{h}
in
\mathcal{C}\underline{\rtimes}G
, then

\displaystyle\Psi((X_{g}\delta_{g})\boxtimes(Y_{h}\delta_{h}))

\displaystyle=\Psi((X_{g}\otimes T_{g}(Y_{h}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}))\delta_{gh})

\displaystyle=\phi(X_{g}\otimes T_{g}(Y_{h}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}))\bar{\otimes}\pi(gh)

\displaystyle\cong\phi(X_{g})\bar{\otimes}\phi(T_{g}(Y_{h}\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}))\bar{\otimes}\pi(gh)

\displaystyle\cong\phi(X_{g})\bar{\otimes}\pi(g)\phi(Y_{h})\bar{\otimes}\pi(g^{-1})\bar{\otimes}\pi(gh)

\displaystyle\cong\phi(X_{g})\bar{\otimes}\pi(g)\phi(Y_{h})\bar{\otimes}\pi(g^{-1})\bar{\otimes}\pi(g)\bar{\otimes}\pi(h)

\displaystyle\cong\phi(X_{g})\bar{\otimes}\pi(g)\bar{\otimes}\pi(g^{-1})\bar{\otimes}\pi(g)\bar{\otimes}\phi(Y_{h})\bar{\otimes}\pi(h)

\displaystyle\cong\phi(X_{g})\bar{\otimes}\pi(g)\bar{\otimes}\phi(Y_{h})\bar{\otimes}\pi(h)

\displaystyle=\Psi(X_{g}\delta_{g})\bar{\otimes}\Psi(Y_{h}\delta_{h}).\qed

In particular, in the case of the monoidal category
{\sf End}(\mathcal{M})
, for a given category
\mathcal{M}
, a monoidal functor
\phi:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow{\sf End}(\mathcal{M})
means that
\mathcal{M}
is a module category over the monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
. Then, we have the following result.

Corollary 6.16
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
\Bbbk
-linear, abelian and monoidal category,
T=\left(\{T_{g}\}_{g\in G},\{\gamma_{g,h}\}_{g,h\in G},u\right)
a partial action of a group
G
on
\mathcal{C}
generated by central idempotent objects
\{\mathds{1}_{g}\}_{g\in G}
. Let
\mathcal{M}
be a
\mathcal{C}
-module category with the module structure defined by a monoidal functor
\phi:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow{\sf End}(\mathcal{M})
and a functor
\pi:\underline{G}\rightarrow{\sf End}(\mathcal{M})
, such that
\phi
and
\pi
satisfy the conditions of the Theorem
6.15
. Then
\mathcal{M}
is a
\mathcal{C}\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G
-module category.

In particular, if
\mathcal{C}
is a
\Bbbk
-linear, abelian, monoidal category and
T=\left(\{T_{g}\}_{g\in G},\{\gamma_{g,h}\}_{g,h\in G},u\right)
is a partial action of a group
G
on
\mathcal{C}
generated by central idempotent objects
\{\mathds{1}_{g}\}_{g\in G}
, then, as a consequence of Theorem
3.15
, the functor
\pi:\underline{G}\rightarrow{\sf End}(\mathcal{C})
given by
\pi(g)(X)=T_{g}(X\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}})
together with the functor
\phi:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow{\sf End}(\mathcal{C})
given by
\phi(X)(Y)=X\otimes Y
, satisfy the conditions of Theorem
6.15
. Therefore,
\mathcal{C}
is a
\mathcal{C}\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G
-module category with the action given by

X\delta_{g}\odot Y=X\otimes T_{g}(Y\otimes\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}}).

Conversely, any
\mathcal{C}\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G
-module category can be made into a
\mathcal{C}
-module category with the action

X\overline{\otimes}M=X\delta_{e}\odot M.

7.
Conclusions and outlook

We presented the notion of a partial group action on a monoidal category and, following the steps of
[
9
]
, we constructed the standard globalization for a partial action of a group
G
on a monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
. In references
[
15
]
and
[
19
]
, the authors proposed two different, but related, categorical approaches for globalization of a partial group action. It would be interesting to explore the globalization of a partial group action on a monoidal category from an intrinsically categorical point of view.

Following
[
20
]
, we construct the category of partial invariants,
\mathcal{C}^{\underline{G}}
or the associated partial equivariantized category, and the partial smash product category,
\mathcal{C}\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G
. These are, respectively, generalizations of the equivariantized category
\mathcal{C}^{G}
and the semidirect product
\mathcal{C}[G]
, introduced in
[
20
]
, for the case of a global action
T:\underline{G}\rightarrow{\sf End}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{C})
.

The Morita equivalence between the monoidal categories
\mathcal{C}^{G}
and
\mathcal{C}[G]
, for
G
a finite group is also discussed in
[
20
]
. The basic idea comes from the fact that for the monoidal category
\underline{Vect}_{\Bbbk}
of
\Bbbk
-vector spaces, with the trivial group action, that is, each element
g\in G
acts as the identity functor in
\underline{Vect}_{\Bbbk}
. In that case, the equivariantized category
\underline{Vect}_{\Bbbk}^{G}
is equivalent to the category of
\Bbbk G
-modules, and the semidirect product
\underline{Vect}_{\Bbbk}[G]
is equivalent to the category of
G
-graded vector spaces, which is the category of
(\Bbbk G)^{*}
-modules. Then, in this case, the Morita equivalence becomes a particular case of the classical Morita equivalence between the monoidal categories of
H
-modules and
H^{*}
-modules, for
H
being a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra
[
2
]
.

For partial group actions, the relationship between
\mathcal{C}^{\underline{G}}
and
\mathcal{C}\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}G
becomes more complicated. The main reason is that we cannot use the same comparison with the category of
\Bbbk
-vector spaces, because the trivial action, being global, lead to the same classical constructions of the equivariantized category and of the semidirect product. Hence, the subtleties concerning the partial actions are completely eluded. Then, we left the study of Morita equivalence in the partial case for a future investigation.

There are two results coming from the theory of partial group actions whose categorical generalizations are worthy to be studied in future investigations: Consider a unital partial action
\alpha
of a finite group
G
, over an algebra
A
and denote by
\beta
the globalization of
\alpha
, acting over an algebra
B
. Then, in
[
11
]
, it is proved that the partial trace
\underline{Tr}:A\rightarrow A^{\underline{G}}
, given by

\underline{Tr}(a)=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\alpha_{g}(a\mathds{1}_{g^{-1}})

is onto if, and only if, the classical trace of the globalized action,
Tr:B\rightarrow B^{G}
, given by

Tr(b)=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\beta_{g}(b)

is onto (see
[
11
]
, Corollary 2.2). Moreover, if the partial trace is onto, then there is a ring isomorphism between the invariant subalgebras
A^{\underline{G}}
and
B^{G}
(see
[
11
]
, Proposition 2.3). The main difficulty about the categorical generalizations of these results is the fact that, even in the global case, any object in the category
\mathcal{C}
is isomorphic only to a direct summand of its trace. Also, under the same conditions, in
[
9
]
, the authors proved that there is a Morita equivalence between the partial smash product
A\mathbin{\underline{\rtimes}}_{\alpha}G
and the global smash product
B\rtimes_{\beta}G
. This result seems to be more straightforward to be formulated in the categorical case.

Yet, there are some remaining questions related to the partial equivariantization,
\mathcal{C}^{\underline{G}}
. In fact, in Section 4, we showed that a partial action of
G
on
\mathcal{C}
defines a Hopf Polyad

(55)

\left(\underline{G},\;\{\mathcal{C}_{g}\}_{g\in G},\;\{P_{g}=T_{g}\circ T_{g^{-1}}:\mathcal{C}_{g}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}_{g}\}_{g\in G},\;\{\mu_{g}:P_{g}\circ P_{g}\Rightarrow P_{g}\}_{g\in G},\;\{\eta_{g}:\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{C}_{g}}\Rightarrow P_{g}\}_{g\in G}\right).

According to
[
3
]
, Hopf Polyads are nothing but Hopf monads in an appropriate monoidal category. Therefore, it is natural to ask whether the partial equivariantization can be seen as an Eilenberg-Moore category of a monad, following the ideas of
[
18
]
to construct an equivariant category associated to a monad acting on a monoidal category.

Finally, there are some universal constructions associated to partial representations of a group as the Exel groupoid
\Gamma(G)
and the partial group algebra
\Bbbk_{par}G

[
10
]
. The Hopf polyad (
55
) seems to be related to these multi object construction. Certainly, a deeper examination of this relationship would be welcome. Moreover, the partial group algebra
\Bbbk_{par}G
is isomorphic to a partial crossed product
A_{par}(G)\rtimes G
. Then, the categorifications of these constructions are yet to be explored in further investigations.

Confronting impact-parameter dependent model in next-to-leading order of perturbative QCD with combined HERA data
Miguel Guevara

Abstract
In this talk, we present the CGC/saturation approach of Ref. [ 1 ] and its parameters determined from the combined HERA data. This model features an analytical solution for the non-linear Balitsky-Kovchegov (BK) evolution equation and the exponential behavior of the saturation momentum on the impact parameter b b -dependence, characterized by Q s ∝ exp ⁡ ( − m ​ b ) Q_{s}\propto\exp(-mb) . We compare our results with experimental data at small- x x , including the proton structure function F 2 F_{2} , charm structure function F 2 c ​ c ¯ F_{2}^{c\bar{c}} , and exclusive vector meson production. The model shows good agreement across a wide kinematic range. Our findings support using this approach for reliable predictions in upcoming experiments like the Electron-Ion Collider (EIC) and the LHeC.

Introduction

The Color Glass Condensate (CGC)/saturation effective field theory
[
2
]
provides an effective description of high-energy QCD processes. Despite its success, leading-order CGC predictions show high-energy growth inconsistent with experimental data. To resolve this, Ref.
[
1
]
introduced corrections to the BK equation
[
4
]
, improving predictions for high-energy scattering. The model accurately reproduces the scattering amplitude’s large impact parameter
b
-dependence, ensuring compliance with the Froissart theorem
[
6
]
, which is crucial for realistic high-energy predictions. This approach aligns with geometric scaling and perturbative QCD for large momentum transfers. We fit the CGC/saturation dipole model to precise HERA DIS data
[
7
,
8
]
, extracting phenomenological parameters and comparing theoretical predictions to experimental results for various observables such as the proton structure function
F_{2}
and exclusive vector meson production. This study enhances the predictive power of the CGC/saturation framework, providing valuable insights for future experiments like the EIC and LHeC.

Inclusive and Exclusive Processes

The observables in deep inelastic scattering (DIS) can be expressed through the following scattering amplitudes

N_{L,T}\left(Q,Y;b\right)=\int\frac{d^{2}r}{4\pi}\int^{1}_{0}dz\,|\Psi^{\gamma^{*}}_{L,T}\left(Q,r,z\right)|^{2}\,N\left(r,Y;b\right)

(2.1)

Here,
Y=\ln(1/x_{Bj})
,
x_{Bj}
is the Bjorken
x
,
z
is the fraction of the virtual photon momentum carried by the quark, and
Q
is the photon virtuality. The
|\Psi^{\gamma^{*}}_{L,T}\left(Q,r,z\right)|^{2}
are the overlap wave-functions of the photon, detailed in Ref.
[
2
]
.
The main DIS observables are expressed as:

\displaystyle\sigma_{T,L}^{\gamma^{*}p}(Q^{2},x)

\displaystyle\,\,=\,\,

\displaystyle 2\int d^{2}b\,\,N_{T,L}\left(Q,Y;b\right);

(2.2a)

\displaystyle F_{2}\left(Q^{2},x\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{Q^{2}}{4\,\pi^{2}\,\alpha_{\rm e.m.}}\big[\sigma_{T}^{\gamma^{*}p}(Q^{2},x)\,\,+\,\,\sigma_{L}^{\gamma^{*}p}(Q^{2},x)\big];

(2.2b)

\displaystyle F^{c\bar{c}}_{2}\left(Q^{2},x\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{Q^{2}}{4\,\pi^{2}\,\alpha_{\rm e.m.}}\Big[\sigma^{c\bar{c},\gamma^{*}p}_{T}(Q^{2},x)\,\,+\,\,\sigma^{c\bar{c},\gamma^{*}p}_{L}(Q^{2},x)\Big];

(2.2c)

\displaystyle F_{L}\left(Q^{2},x\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{Q^{2}}{4\,\pi^{2}\,\alpha_{\rm e.m.}}\,\sigma_{L}^{\gamma^{*}p}(Q^{2},x);

(2.2d)

The reduced cross-section
\sigma_{r}
is expressed in terms of the inclusive proton structure functions
F_{2}
and
F_{L}
as

\sigma_{r}(Q^{2},x,y)=F_{2}(Q^{2},x)-\frac{y^{2}}{1+(1-y)^{2}}F_{L}(Q^{2},x)

(2.3)

For exclusive diffractive processes
\gamma^{*}+p\rightarrow E+p
(where
E
is a real photon in DVCS or a vector meson), the amplitude and differential cross-section are given by:

{\cal{A}}^{\gamma^{*}p\rightarrow Ep}_{T,L}(x,Q,\Delta)=2i\int d^{2}\boldsymbol{r}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dz}{4\pi}\int d^{2}\boldsymbol{b}(\Psi_{E}^{*}\Psi)_{T,L}\mathrm{e}^{-i[\boldsymbol{b}-(\frac{1}{2}-z)\boldsymbol{r}]\cdot\boldsymbol{\Delta}}\,N\left(r,Y;b\right)

(2.4)

\frac{d\sigma_{T,L}^{\gamma^{*}p\rightarrow Ep}}{dt}=\frac{1}{16\pi}\left|{\cal{A}}^{\gamma^{*}p\rightarrow Ep}_{T,L}\right|^{2}(1+\beta^{2})

(2.5)

where the expressions for the overlap wave functions can be found in Ref.
[
9
]
and
\beta
accounts for the ratio of the real to imaginary parts of the scattering amplitude. Two main observables used in the analysis are the total cross-section
\sigma^{\gamma^{*}p\rightarrow Ep}_{T,L}
, defined as:

\sigma^{\gamma^{*}p\rightarrow Ep}_{T,L}=\int dt\frac{d\sigma_{T,L}^{\gamma^{*}p\rightarrow Ep}}{dt}

(2.6)

and the slope parameter
B_{D}
, obtained from the logarithmic derivative of the differential cross-section at
t=0
:

B_{D}=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{d}{dt}\ln\left(\frac{d\sigma_{T,L}^{\gamma^{*}p\rightarrow Ep}}{dt}\right).

(2.7)

3
CGC/saturation dipole model

3.1
q\bar{q}
dipole-proton scattering amplitude

To compute the total cross-sections, proton structure functions in DIS, exclusive diffractive vector meson production, and DVCS, we use the
q\bar{q}
dipole-proton forward scattering amplitude. Ref.
[
1
]
provides an analytical solution for the nonlinear BK equation at NLO BFKL kernel in the saturation domain, incorporating non-linear evolution and resummation procedures
[
5
]
. The color
q\bar{q}
dipole-proton scattering amplitude is given by:

\displaystyle N\left(z\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}N_{0}\,e^{z\bar{\gamma}}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ ~~~~~\,\mbox{for}\ \ \tau\leq 1;\\
\\
a\,\Big(1-e^{-\Omega\left(z\right)}\Big)+\left(1-a\right)\frac{\Omega\left(z\right)}{1+\Omega\left(z\right)}~~~~\mbox{for}\ \ \tau>1;\end{array}\right.

where
a=0.65
,
\Omega(z)
is:

\Omega(z)=\Omega_{0}\Bigg\{\cosh\left(\sqrt{\sigma}z\right)+\frac{\bar{\gamma}}{\sqrt{\sigma}}\sinh\left(\sqrt{\sigma}z\right)\Bigg\},\;\sigma=\frac{\bar{\alpha}_{S}}{\lambda(1+\bar{\alpha}_{S})};

(3.5)

N_{0}
is a parameter fitted from data. The variable
z
is defined as:

z=\ln\,(r^{2}Q^{2}_{s}(Y,b)).

(3.6)

The critical anomalous dimension and energy behavior of the saturation scale are:

\bar{\gamma}=\bar{\gamma}_{\eta}(1+\lambda_{\eta}),~~~~~~\bar{\gamma}_{\eta}=\sqrt{2+\bar{\alpha}_{S}}-1;

(3.7)

\lambda=\frac{\lambda_{\eta}}{1+\lambda_{\eta}},~~~~\lambda_{\eta}=\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{\bar{\alpha}_{S}}{3+\bar{\alpha}_{S}-2\sqrt{2+\bar{\alpha}_{S}}};

(3.8)

where
\eta=Y-\xi
and
Y=\ln(1/x)
,
\xi=\ln(r^{2}Q_{s}^{2}(Y=0,\mathbf{b}))
. Expanding the linear solution of Eq. (
3.1
) to
\tau<1
, we replace
\bar{\gamma}
by:

\bar{\gamma}\rightarrow\bar{\gamma}+\frac{\ln(1/\tau)}{2\kappa\lambda Y},~~~~\kappa=\frac{\chi^{\prime\prime}(\bar{\gamma})}{\chi^{\prime}(\bar{\gamma})}=\frac{\frac{d^{2}\omega(\bar{\gamma}_{\eta})}{d\bar{\gamma}_{\eta}^{2}}}{\frac{d\omega(\bar{\gamma}_{\eta})}{d\bar{\gamma}_{\eta}}}

(3.9)

this equation was derived in Ref.
[
10
]
and has demonstrated a strong agreement with the experimental data for
x\leq 0.01
.

3.2
Impact parameter dependence of the saturation scale

We introduce a phenomenological parameter,
N_{0}
, determining the scattering amplitude at
r^{2}Q^{2}_{s}=1
. Initial conditions at
Y=0
specify the saturation scale at
b=0
and its dependence on
b
. We use the following expression to represent the saturation momentum
[
11
]
:

\displaystyle Q^{2}_{s}(Y,b)=Q^{2}_{0}(m\,b\,K_{1}(m\,b))^{1/\bar{\gamma}}e^{\lambda Y},

(3.10)

this ensures the correct large
b
behavior. The parameters
Q^{2}_{0}
and
m
are fitted to data, with
Q^{2}_{0}\in[0.15,0.25]\,\rm{GeV}^{2}
and
m\in[0.4,0.85]\,\rm{GeV}
. The value of
\lambda=0.2
is needed to describe DIS data, with
\bar{\alpha}_{S}\approx 0.1
treated as a free parameter.

5
Summary

To conclude, this study demonstrates the effectiveness of the CGC/saturation dipole model in describing small-x DIS data, incorporating an analytical solution of the BK equation and an impact parameter dependence that satisfies the Froissart theorem. The model achieves good agreement with experimental data across various observables and demonstrates predictive power in describing both inclusive and exclusive processes. This model provides a strong framework for studying high-energy QCD phenomena and guiding future DIS experiments, including those at the upcoming Electron-Ion Collider (EIC).

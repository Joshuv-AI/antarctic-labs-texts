Cactus varieties of sufficiently ample embeddings of projective schemes have determinantal equations Thanks: Dedicated in memory of Gianfranco Casnati
Weronika Buczyńska, Jarosław Buczyński, Łucja Farnik

Abstract
For a fixed projective scheme X X , a property P P of line bundles is satisfied by sufficiently ample line bundles if there exists a line bundle L 0 L_{0} on X X such that P ⁡ ( L ) P(L) holds for any L L with L − L 0 L-L_{0} ample. As an example, sufficiently ample line bundles are very ample, moreover, for a normal variety X X , the embedding corresponding to sufficiently ample line bundle is projectively normal. The grandfather of such properties and a basic ingredient used to study this concept is Fujita vanishing theorem, which is a strengthening of Serre vanishing to sufficiently ample line bundles. The r r -th cactus variety of X X is an analogue of secant variety and it is defined using linear spans of finite schemes of degree r r . In this article we show that cactus varieties of sufficiently ample embeddings of X X are set-theoretically defined by minors of matrices with linear entries. The topic is closely related to conjectures of Eisenbud-Koh-Stillman, which was proved by Ginensky in the case X X a smooth curve [ Gin08 ] . On the other hand Sidman-Smith [ SS11 ] proved that the ideal of sufficiently ample embedding of any projective scheme X X is generated by 2 × 2 2\times 2 minors of a matrix with linear entries.

Introduction

Throughout most of this article we work over a fixed algebraically closed field
{\Bbbk}
of any characteristics.

1.1
Secant and cactus varieties

Suppose
X
is a projective variety over
{\Bbbk}
and
r
is a fixed positive integer.
For a closed embedding
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
,
the
r
-th secant variety
\sigma_{r}(X)
of
X
(strictly speaking, of
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
), is defined as

\sigma_{r}(X)=\overline{\bigcup\left\{\left\langle p_{1},\dotsc,p_{r}\right\rangle\mid p_{i}\in X\right\}}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
.
Here,
\left\{p_{1},\dotsc,p_{r}\right\}\subset X
is a smooth finite subscheme,
and
\left\langle\dots\right\rangle
denotes the (projective) linear span of a subscheme in
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
.
Secant varieties have practical applications both in algebraic geometry and also outside this area of mathematics,
see for instance
[
Lan12
, Chapt. 1]
,

[
Lan17
, Chapt. 1]
.
It is a long standing problem to find the defining equations for secant varieties. The first and most natural equations
that vanish on the
r^{\text{th}}
secant variety of the Veronese variety are
(r+1)\times(r+1)
minors
of the catalecticant matrices.
More generally, elementary linear algebra and semicontinuity of the matrix rank show that if

X\subset\left\{\rk M\leq k\right\}
for some matrix
M
with entries that are linear coordinates on
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
,
then
\sigma_{r}(X)\subset\left\{\rk M\leq k\cdot r\right\}
.
By means of this observation, more families of equations were found, see a summary in
in
[
LO13
]
.
A natural method introduced there produces a matrix with linear entries from vector bundles on
X
.
Unfortunately, the extrinsic geometry of secant varieties in
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
tends to be very difficult and behaves wildly,
except in a few tame cases.

In
[
BB14
]
,
[
Gał17
]
, and

[
Gał23b
, Thm 1.18]
the wild beast is given a name by exhibiting a potentially larger,
typically reducible, variety, called
cactus variety

\mathfrak{K}_{r}\left(X\right)
, defined below in (
1.1
).
Again, strictly speaking, the cactus variety depends not only on
X
, but also on its embedding

\iota\colon X\to{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
.
But as in the case of the secant varieties, unless potentially confusing, it is customary to omit
\iota
from the notation.
Moreover, in the definition it is enough to assume that
X
is a projective scheme, not necessarily a variety.

\mathfrak{K}_{r}\left(X\right):=\overline{\bigcup\left\{\langle R\rangle\mid R\subset X,\text{ $R$ is a subscheme of degree at most $r$}\right\}}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{N}.

(1.1)

The point is that (if
X
is a projective variety) on one hand
\sigma_{r}(X)\subset\mathfrak{K}_{r}\left(X\right)
and on
the other hand many natural equations vanishing on
\sigma_{r}(X)
vanish also on the larger set

\mathfrak{K}_{r}\left(X\right)
.
Thus the difference
\mathfrak{K}_{r}\left(X\right)\setminus\sigma_{r}(X)
is a natural, geometric obstruction to the “tameness”
of secant varieties. The goal of this article is to show that under certain assumptions this difference is the only obstruction.
We show that for sufficiently ample embedding
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
its cactus variety
\mathfrak{K}_{r}\left(X\right)
is set-theoretically
defined by minors of a matrix, whose entries are linear forms on
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
. These minors arise naturally from
sections of certain line bundles.

1.2
Cactus variety of a sufficiently ample embedding

For the purpose of this introduction we define the properties satisfied by “sufficiently ample line bundles” on
X

in the following way, and we further discuss this notion in Section
3
.

Definition 1.2
.

Fix a projective scheme
X
over
{\Bbbk}
.
Let
P
be a property of line bundles on
X
.
We say that
P
is satisfied for sufficiently ample line bundles
if there exists a line bundle
L_{0}
such that
P
holds for all
L_{0}\otimes L
with
L
ample.

In particular, “very ampleness” is satisfied by sufficiently ample line bundles,
which in less strict, but more natural language we phrase as
“a sufficiently ample line bundle on
X
is very ample”.
See Section
3
for discussion, references and a concise proof of this fact.

Our main result, in a simplified form, is the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

Fix an integer
r^{\prime}
, and suppose
X
is a projective scheme.
For a sufficiently ample line bundle
L

consider embedding of
X
given by the complete linear system
H^{0}(X,L)
:

\iota\colon X\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}(H^{0}(X,L)^{*})\simeq{\mathbb{P}}^{N}.

Then there exists a matrix
M
whose entries are
are linear coordinates on
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
, equivalently they are sections of
L

– elements of
H^{0}(X,L)
, such that

\mathfrak{K}_{r}\left(X\right)={\left\{\rk M\leq r\right\}}_{\operatorname{red}}\quad\text{ for all }r\leq r^{\prime},

where
{\left\{\rk M\leq r\right\}}_{\operatorname{red}}
is the (reduced) zero set of the homogeneous ideal generated by

(r+1)\times(r+1)
minors of
M
.

In particular, the theorem gives a partial answer to Conjecture of
Sidman and Smith, see
[
SS11
, Conj. 1.2]
.
We discuss this and similar conjectures together with their relations to our results
in §
1.3
.

More precisely, the matrix
M
in Theorem
1.3

generalises the catalecticant matrix in the case of
X={\mathbb{P}}^{n}
, compare to
[
BB14
, Thm 1.5]
.
Given a splitting
L=A\otimes B
as a product of line bundles
A
and
B
on
X
,
there is a corresponding map — multiplication of sections:
H^{0}(X,A)\otimes H^{0}(X,B)\to H^{0}(X,L)
.
For any choice of bases
s_{1},\dotsc,s_{k}
of
H^{0}(X,A)
and
t_{1},\dotsc,t_{l}
of
H^{0}(X,B)

we obtain a
k\times l
matrix
M
, whose
(i,j)
-th entry is
s_{i}t_{j}\in H^{0}(X,L)
.
We claim that when both
A
and
B
are sufficiently ample and satisfy additional technical conditions,
in some sense,
A
and
B
as divisors are close to a “half” of
L
,
the matrix
M
as above has the desired properties of Theorem
1.3
.

1.3
Conjectures on secant varieties to sufficiently ample embeddings

In a series of results by Mumford
[
Mum70
, p. 32, Thm 1]
,
Griffiths
[
Gri83
, Thm p. 271]
,
and Sidman and Smith
[
SS11
, Thm 1.1]

it is shown that for any projective scheme
X
the ideal of a sufficiently ample embedding of
X

is generated by
2\times 2
minors of a matrix with linear entries, specifically, one constructed as above.
In particular,

[
SS11
, Thm 1.1]

prove a version of Theorem
1.3
for
r^{\prime}=1
.
Moreover they phrase the following conjecture
[
SS11
, Conj 1.2]
.
A version of this conjecture for curves is stated also by
Eisenbud, Koh, and Stillman
[
EKS88
, p. 518, Equation (*)]
.

Conjecture 1.4
.

Fix an integer
r
and a projective variety
X
.
Suppose
L
is a sufficiently ample line bundle on
X
,
with
\iota\colon X\to{\mathbb{P}}(H^{0}(X,L))
the embedding by the complete linear system.
Then the ideal of
\sigma_{r}(X)
is generated by
(r+1)\times(r+1)
minors of the matrix with linear entries.

Conjecture
1.4
was proved set-theoretically in the case
X
is a smooth curve
in
[
Rav94
]
, and scheme-theoretically for smooth curves
in
[
Gin08
]
.
On the other hand, if
X
has sufficiently bad singularities, even if it is a curve,
then
[
BGL13
, Thm 1.17]

provides counterexamples to Conjecture
1.4
.
At this point, it should not come as a surprise to the reader, that the real reason for the failure of this conjecture
comes from our favourite obstruction, that is the difference between cactus and secant varieties:

\mathfrak{K}_{r}\left(X\right)\setminus\sigma_{r}(X)
.
For this reason, in the paragraph following
the conjecture
[
SS11
, Conj. 1.2]

the authors mention that “
[
BB14
, Thm 1.3]
suggests that the secant varieties
in Conjecture
1.4
should be replaced by cactus varieties”.
This is exactly what we prove in Theorem
1.3
, at the moment in the set-theoretic version.

Remark 1.5
.

The article
[
SS11
]

contains additional thesis on the resulting matrix
M
, both in Theorem 1.1, and in Conjecture 1.2.
Namely, the authors claim that
M
is
1
-generic, that is the orbit of
M
with respect to
the action of the product of
\mathbf{GL}(H^{0}(X,A))\times\mathbf{GL}(H^{0}(X,B))
does not contain any matrix with a zero entry.
If
X
is a variety (reduced and irreducible), then this condition is automatic from the construction of the matrix
as one arising from the multiplication of sections, and from
[
Eis05
, Prop. 6.10]
.
Otherwise, if
X
is not irreducible or not reduced,
then
1
-genericity claim in
[
SS11
, Thm 1.1]
fails.
For example, if
X=\Spec{\Bbbk}[t]/(t^{2}-t)
or
X=\Spec{\Bbbk}[t]/(t^{2})
, then there is no
1
-generic matrix with linear entries,
such that the ideal of
X
is generated by its
2\times 2
minors in any projective embedding.
Therefore, in our considerations we ignore the issue of
1
-genericity of
M
.

There exist some cases when the cactus and secant varieties are equal. In those situations our results
provide set-theoretical equations of secant varieties.

Corollary 1.6
.

Suppose
X
is a projective variety of dimension
n
and
r^{\prime}
is an integer.
If one of the following conditions hold:

•

X
is smooth and
n\leqslant 3
, or

•

X
is smooth and
\cchar{\Bbbk}\neq 2,3
and
r^{\prime}\leqslant 13
, or

•

X
is smooth and
\cchar{\Bbbk}\neq 2,3
,
r^{\prime}=14
, and
n\leqslant 5
, or

•

n=1
and
X
is a curve with only planar singularities,

then for a sufficiently ample embedding of
X
, in the notation of Theorem
1.3
,
the secant variety
\sigma_{r}(X)
is set theoretically defined by
(r+1)\times(r+1)
minors of
M
for all
r\leqslant r^{\prime}
.

This corollary is an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.3
combined
with
[
BJ17
, Cor. 6.20, §7.2]
. In particular, the explicit conditions
on dimension and
r
are copied from Tables 1–4 in
[
BJ17
]
.
Note that there is some space for improving the number of cases, where secant variety and cactus variety
coincide in characteristic
2
and
3
: one can also show that in any characteristic

\sigma_{r}(X)=\mathfrak{K}_{r}\left(X\right)
for
r\leqslant 5
and
X
non-singular.

Moreover, fine properties of secant varieties to sufficiently ample embeddings has attracted recently
a significant attention, so far mainly for smooth projective curves, as this is the most tractable case, and also
the first testing ground:

[
ENP20
]
,

[
CKP23
]
.
An ongoing independent work in preparation
[
CLPS24
]

also investigates cases of surfaces and more general varieties.

1.4
Overview

In Section
2
we study combinatorial properties of not necessarily polyhedral cones
in real affine spaces with their relations to lattice points.
This serves as a preparation for a discussion of
\Nef(X)
-cone and its relation to Picard group.
In Section
3
,
we present basic facts about sufficiently ample line bundles.
In Section
4
we study
multigraded rings coming from sections of line bundles in a fairly restricted setting, commencing with
a single-graded ring of sections of (powers of) a line bundle and then generalising to a double-graded one.
In particular, in §
4.3
we discuss a version of apolarity in this setting.
In Section
5
we prove our main result. The proof is divided into two parts: algebraic, where
we exploit the apolarity, and combinatorial, where we show that some convex body must have a lattice point,
which then leads to an appropriate splitting of a sufficiently ample line bundle
L=A\otimes B
.
In Section
6
we briefly mention the further or ongoing projects related to this topic.
Finally, in Appendix
A

we provide references and sketch the argument that generalises our result to arbitrary base fields.

2
Cones, lattices and numerical equivalence of divisors

We gather some general results of convex geometry flavour that we need to prove
some statements about nef and ample cones of schemes.

2.1
Lattice cones

Consider a lattice
N={\mathbb{Z}}^{\rho}

and its underlying
{\mathbb{R}}
-vector space

N_{{\mathbb{R}}}=N\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{R}}\simeq{\mathbb{R}}^{\rho}
.
On
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
we consider Euclidean topology: for a subset
\sigma\subset N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
by
\overline{\sigma}
we denote
its closure and by
\interior(\sigma)
or
\interior_{N}(\sigma)
the interior of
\sigma
inside
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
.
We always have the natural inclusion
N\subset N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
arising from
{\mathbb{Z}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
.

A subset
\sigma\subset N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
is called
a
cone
if it is convex and invariant under the positive rescalings:
{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}\cdot\sigma=\sigma
.
For any subset
S\subset N
, we let
\cone(S)
to be the smallest cone in
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
containing
S
,
or equivalently:

\cone(S)=\left\{\textstyle{\sum_{i=1}^{k}}c_{i}s_{i}\mid k\in{\mathbb{Z}},c_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0},s_{i}\in S\right\}.

Every cone has its
dimension
defined as the
{\mathbb{R}}
-dimension of its
{\mathbb{R}}
-linear span.
For two subsets
S_{1},S_{2}\subset N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
their
Minkowski sum

S_{1}+S_{2}
is the set

\left\{s_{1}+s_{2}\mid s_{1}\in S_{1},s_{2}\in S_{2}\right\}
.
For
D\in N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
and a cone
\sigma\subset N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
, the sum
\left\{D\right\}+\sigma

(denoted simply
D+\sigma
) is a
translated cone
.

Our main interest is in cones
\sigma\subset N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
of
full dimension
,
which are characterised by one of the following equivalent conditions:

•

the
{\mathbb{R}}
-linear span of
\sigma
is
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
,

•

\interior(\sigma)\neq\emptyset
,

•

if
V\subset N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
is the linear span of
\sigma
, then for any
L\in\interior_{V}(\sigma)
we have

\cone(-L)+\sigma=N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose
\sigma\subset N_{{\mathbb{R}}}

is a cone of full dimension, and
P\subset N
is any subset.
Then the following two conditions are equivalent:

•

D+\sigma\cap N\subset P
for some
D\in N
,

•

D^{\prime}+\interior(\sigma)\cap N\subset P
for some
D^{\prime}\in N
.

Proof.
Assuming the first item, let
D^{\prime}=D
and the second property is automatically satisfied.
For any
L\in\interior(\sigma)\cap N
,
we have
L+\sigma\subset\interior(\sigma)
.
Thus assuming the second item, take
D=D^{\prime}+L
,
and the first property holds.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose we have two lattices
N^{\prime}\subset N
and cones

\sigma\subset N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
and
\sigma^{\prime}\subset N^{\prime}_{{\mathbb{R}}}
that are of full dimensions
in the respective lattice.
Assume
\sigma^{\prime}\subset\sigma
and
\interior_{N^{\prime}}(\sigma^{\prime})\subset\interior_{N}(\sigma)
.
Then for any
D\in N
we have

N^{\prime}\cap(D+\sigma\cap N)\neq\emptyset

and there exists
D^{\prime}\in N^{\prime}
such that

D^{\prime}+\sigma^{\prime}\subset D+\sigma
.

Proof.
The interior of
\sigma^{\prime}
in
N^{\prime}_{{\mathbb{R}}}
must contain a lattice point
L^{\prime}\in N^{\prime}
,
which is also an interior point of
\sigma
.
Thus
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}=\cone(-L^{\prime})+\sigma\supset-D+N^{\prime}
,
and hence for any
E^{\prime}\in N^{\prime}
for some integer
k\geq 0

we must have
E^{\prime}+k\cdot L^{\prime}\in(D+\sigma)\cap N^{\prime}
.
Therefore
N^{\prime}\cap(D+(\sigma\cap N))\neq\emptyset
as claimed.

To show the second claim pick any
D^{\prime}\in N^{\prime}\cap(D+(\sigma\cap N))
.
Then
D^{\prime}+\sigma^{\prime}\subset D^{\prime}+\sigma\subset D+\sigma
as claimed.
∎

When
\sigma
is a closed and pointed cone of full dimension,
we define the
dual cone
of
\sigma

\sigma^{\vee}:=\left\{\phi\in N_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{*}\mid\phi(D)\geqslant 0\text{ for all }D\in\sigma\right\}.

Since
\sigma
is a closed pointed cone of full dimension, also
\sigma^{\vee}
is a closed pointed cone of full dimension.
For any
H\in\interior(\sigma)

consider the corresponding hyperplane
(H=1)
in
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{*}
:

(H=1):=\left\{\phi\in N^{1}_{{\mathbb{R}}}(X)^{*}\mid\phi(H)=1\right\}.

Then in the Euclidean topology
\sigma^{\vee}\cap(H=1)
is a compact subset of
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{*}\simeq{\mathbb{R}}^{\rho}
.
Moreover,
\sigma^{\vee}\cap(H=1)
generates the cone:

\sigma^{\vee}={\mathbb{R}}_{\geqslant 0}\cdot\left(\sigma^{\vee}\cap(H=1)\right)
.
Thus by double duality

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle=\left\{D\in N_{{\mathbb{R}}}\mid\phi(D)\geqslant 0\text{ for all }\phi\in\sigma^{\vee}\cap(H=1)\right\},\text{ and}

(2.3a)

\displaystyle\interior(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\left\{D\in N_{{\mathbb{R}}}\mid\phi(D)>0\text{ for all }\phi\in\sigma^{\vee}\cap(H=1)\right\}.

(2.3b)

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose
\sigma
is a closed and pointed cone of full dimension and
H
is as above
For any
D\in N_{{\mathbb{R}}}

there exists real number

\operatorname{min}_{D}=\min\left\{\phi(D)\mid\phi\in\sigma^{\vee}\cap(H=1)\right\}.

In addition:

•

D\in\sigma
if and only if
\operatorname{min}_{D}\geqslant 0
, and

•

D\in\interior(\sigma)
if and only if
\operatorname{min}_{D}>0
.

Proof.
Since
\sigma^{\vee}\cap(H=1)
is compact, the continuous function
\phi\mapsto\phi(D)
attains its minimum
\operatorname{min}_{D}
.
The additional claims follow from (
2.3a
)
and (
2.3b
).
∎

Now fix
\sigma
and
H
as above and pick any basis
e_{1},\dotsc,e_{\rho}
of
N
.
We will consider a closed cube
C\subset N_{{\mathbb{R}}}

of size
2
,
whose vertices are
\pm e_{1}\pm\dotsb\pm e_{\rho}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose
D^{\prime}
and
D^{\prime\prime}
are two vectors in
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
.
If for any choice of a vertex
v=\pm e_{1}\pm\dotsb\pm e_{\rho}
of the cube
C

we have

D^{\prime}-D^{\prime\prime}+v\in\sigma
,
then there exists a lattice point in

(D^{\prime}-\sigma)\cap(D^{\prime\prime}+\sigma)\cap N.

Proof.
There is always a lattice point in every translation of
\frac{1}{2}C
(a cube of size
1
).
We claim that

\tfrac{1}{2}(D^{\prime}+D^{\prime\prime})+\tfrac{1}{2}C\subset(D^{\prime}-\sigma)\cap(D^{\prime\prime}+\sigma),

(2.6)

thus the intersection contains a lattice point.
The set
(D^{\prime}-\sigma)\cap(D^{\prime\prime}+\sigma)
is convex so it is enough to check the containment on vertices of
C
only,
which we do below. Let
v=\pm e_{1}\pm\dotsb\pm e_{\rho}
be any vertex of
C
.
By our assumption
D^{\prime}-D^{\prime\prime}+v\in\sigma
.
Since
\sigma
is a cone (invariant under positive rescaling), this is equivalent to

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}(D^{\prime}-D^{\prime\prime})+\tfrac{1}{2}v

\displaystyle\in\sigma.

Subtracting
D^{\prime}
or adding
D^{\prime\prime}
to both sides the above is equivalent to either of the following:

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}(D^{\prime}+D^{\prime\prime})+\tfrac{1}{2}v

\displaystyle\in D^{\prime\prime}+\sigma,\text{ or}

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}(D^{\prime}+D^{\prime\prime})-\tfrac{1}{2}v

\displaystyle\in D^{\prime}-\sigma.

The last two conditions imply (
2.6
) and hence conclude the proof.
∎

2.2
Ample and nef cones

Suppose
X
is a projective scheme over
{\Bbbk}
and let
\Pic(X)
denote the Picard group of
X
.
We say that two line bundles
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are
numerically equivalent
if for any irreducible and reduced
curve
C\subset X
the restricted line bundles
L_{1}|_{C}
and
L_{2}|_{C}
have the same degree.
Let
N^{1}(X):=\Pic(X)/\equiv
, where
\equiv
is the numerical equivalence.
By
[
Kle66
, Prop IV.1.4]

see also
[
Laz04
, Thm 1.1.16]
for more modern treatment but to some extent restricted to
{\Bbbk}={\mathbb{C}}
,
we have
N^{1}(X)\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}^{\rho}
for some integer
\rho
called the
Picard number
of
X
.

By the Nakai-Moishezon-Kleiman criterion

[
Kle66
, Thm III.1.1(i) and (ii)]
,
see also
[
Laz04
, Thm 1.2.23 and Cor. 1.2.24]
,
if
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are numerically equivalent and
L_{1}
is ample then
L_{2}
is ample.
Therefore it makes sense to define the set of ample classes
\Amp_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(X)\subset N^{1}(X)
.
The ample cone
\Amp(X)\subset N_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{1}(X)=N^{1}(X)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{R}}\simeq{\mathbb{R}}^{\rho}

is then defined as
\cone(\Amp_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(X))
.
By the Nakai-Moishezon-Kleiman criterion for
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisors

[
CP90
, Thm 1.3]
we also have
\Amp(X)

described by the same intersection theory inequalities as
\Amp_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(X)
,
and thus
\Amp_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(X)=\Amp(X)\cap N^{1}(X)
.

A line bundle
L
is
nef
if its restriction
L|_{C}
has non-negative degree for every irreducible and reduced
curve
C\subset X
. A numerical class
[L]
in
N^{1}(X)
is
nef
if its representative

L\in\Pic(X)
is nef, which does not depend on the choice of the representative.
The set of nef classes is denoted

\Nef_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(X)
and the corresponding cone (called the
nef cone
) in
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{1}(X)
is

\Nef(X)=\cone\left(\Nef_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(X)\right)
.

Since
X
is projective, we have

\Nef(X)=\overline{\Amp(X)},\quad\text{and}\quad\interior(\Nef(X))=\Amp(X).

(2.7)

In particular,
\Amp(X)
is open and non-empty by projectivity, thus both cones have full dimension.

Let
\overline{NE(X)}\subset N^{1}_{{\mathbb{R}}}(X)^{*}
be the
dual cone
of
\Nef(X)
:

\overline{NE(X)}:=\left\{\phi\in N^{1}_{{\mathbb{R}}}(X)^{*}\mid\phi(D)\geqslant 0\text{ for all }D\in\Nef(X)\right\}.

If
X
is a projective variety, then the dual cone is traditionally called the
Mori cone
,
or the closure of the
cone of curves
, and denoted
\overline{NE(X)}
.
We extend this notation to our more general situation of
X
being the projective scheme.

An immediate consequence of Lemma
2.1

and (
2.7
) is the following proposition.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
X
be projective scheme and consider
a property
P
of line bundles on
X
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

P
is satisfied for sufficiently ample line bundles in the sense of Definition
1.2
,

(ii)

there exists a class
D\in N^{1}(X)
such that
P
holds for any line bundle
L
whose numerical class is
in contained in the translated cone
\sigma:=D+\Nef(X)
.

∎

Trivial examples of properties that are
satisfied for sufficiently ample line bundles are
ampleness
and
nefness
.
Another example is provided by
Fujita vanishing (Corollary
3.4
).
Moreover, sufficiently ample line bundles are

very ample
(Proposition
3.6
).

Note that the starting divisor
D
is not uniquely determined, and a priori we do not require that
D
itself is nef or ample.
However, it is always possible to choose
D
nef or even ample by replacing any
D
by
D+d\Delta

for a sufficiently large multiple
d
, where
\Delta
is any ample divisor.

In this article we do not discuss “optimal” or “best” choices of
D\in N^{1}(X)
such that
a given property
P
holds for all line bundles whose numerical classes are in
D+\Nef(X)
.
We are content with statements just that such
D
exists for the properties considered here.
However, the problem of describing or partially describing the set of all the line bundles that satisfy
a given property is also very interesting and should be addressed in future research.
We only discuss a single, elementary example in Example
3.7
.
We also prove Theorem
5.3
, a version of our main result,
which explicitly lists properties required to obtain the description of cactus varieties as zeroes of minors.
This could be a base for future research towards an effective version of Theorem
1.3
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Suppose
P_{1},\dotsc,P_{k}
are properties of line bundles on a fixed projective scheme
X
and assume
that each
P_{i}
is satisfied for sufficiently ample line bundles.
Then for sufficiently ample line bundles
L
, all properties
P_{1},\dotsc,P_{k}
hold.

Proof.
For each property
P_{i}
there exists a divisor class
D_{i}
such that
P_{i}
is satisfied for all line bundles
with numerical class in
D_{i}+\Nef(X)
.
Without loss of generality we may assume that each
D_{i}
is nef.
Then for
D=D_{1}+\dotsb+D_{k}
we have

D+\Nef(X)\subset D_{i}+\Nef(X)
and thus for
line bundles whose classes are in
D+\Nef(X)
all the properties
P_{i}
hold.
∎

Example 2.10

The conclusion of Lemma

and each

2.3
Structure of Picard group of a product

For two schemes
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
and line bundles
L_{i}
on
X_{i}
(for
i=1,2
),
we consider the product
X_{1}\times X_{2}
with two projections
\pi_{i}\colon X_{1}\times X_{2}\to X_{i}
.
Define the product line bundle
L_{1}\boxtimes L_{2}
on
X_{1}\times X_{2}
as

L_{1}\boxtimes L_{2}:=\pi_{1}^{*}L_{1}\otimes\pi_{2}^{*}L_{2}.

The
\boxtimes
operation always induces a monomorphism

\Pic(X_{1})\times\Pic(X_{2})\hookrightarrow\Pic(X_{1}\times\Pic(X_{2}))
. However, typically, not all line bundles
on the product
X_{1}\times X_{2}
are of the form
L_{1}\boxtimes L_{2}
. For instance, if
C
is an elliptic curve, then
the map
\Pic C\times\Pic C\to\Pic(C\times C)
is never surjective,
see
[
HL19
, §2.1]
.
Similarly, the induced map on the numerical classes

N^{1}(X_{1})\times N^{1}(X_{2})\hookrightarrow N^{1}(X_{1}\times X_{2})
is injective, but not necessarily surjective.

Lemma 2.11
.

Suppose
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are projective schemes and
P
is a property of line bundles on
X_{1}\times X_{2}

which is satisfied by sufficiently ample line bundles.
Then for a sufficiently ample line bundle
L_{1}
on
X_{1}
and a sufficiently ample line bundle
L_{2}
on
X_{2}

the property
P
holds for the line bundle
L_{1}\boxtimes L_{2}
on
X_{1}\times X_{2}
.

Proof.
Consider the map

\boxtimes\colon N^{1}(X_{1})\times N^{1}(X_{2})\hookrightarrow N^{1}(X_{1}\times X_{2})

of free abelian groups.
Since
X_{i}
(and hence also
X_{1}\times X_{2}
) are projective, the cone
\Nef(X_{1}\times X_{2})
is of full dimension.
We apply Lemma
2.2
to

N=N^{1}(X_{1}\times X_{2})
,

N^{\prime}=N^{1}(X_{1})\times N^{1}(X_{2})
,

\sigma=\Nef(X_{1}\times X_{2})
,

\sigma^{\prime}=\Nef(X_{1})\times\Nef(X_{2})

to conclude, that there exists a pair

(D_{1},D_{2})\in N^{1}(X_{1})\times N^{1}(X_{2})
such that
P
is satisfied for any

L_{1}\boxtimes L_{2}
with the numerical equivalence class of
L_{i}
in

D_{i}+\Nef(X_{i})
, as claimed.
∎

3
Sufficiently ample line bundles

The main result in the article is about a property that is fulfilled
for sufficiently ample line bundles.
We commence with a thorough discussion of this notion,
first introduced in
[
Gre84
, Def. 3.1]
, also exploited
in
[
SS11
]
.

3.1
Derived push forward and Fujita vanishing

One of our main tools in the article is the relative Fujita vanishing theorem. In fact, this statement
(Theorem
3.3
) and its classical version (Corollary
3.4
)
are the grandparents of many properties that hold for sufficiently ample line bundles.

In order to fully enjoy the involvement of the grandpas we also exploit a result proved in the book of
Mumford
[
Mum08
, Cor. 2 in Sect. 5, pp. 50–51]
.
Although very useful, its presentation in the book is somewhat hard to digest, as it involves sentences:
“let (…) be as above”, and above we find
“let (…) be as in the theorem (except (…))”.
Thus for our own and reader’s convenience (and also for further reference) we decipher the statement and
quote it as Proposition
3.1
.

For a morphism of schemes
Z\to Y
, a coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
on
Z
and a point
y\in Y
,
we denote by
\kappa(y)
the field such that
y=\Spec\kappa(y)
,
and by
{\mathcal{F}}_{y}={\mathcal{F}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}}\kappa(y)
the fibre sheaf.
In the special case where
Z=X\times Y
, the morphism
X\times Y\to Y
is the projection
and
y=\Spec{\Bbbk}
is a closed point, by a slight abuse of notation we think of
{\mathcal{F}}_{y}
as of a coherent sheaf on
X

using the natural isomorphism
X\simeq X\times\left\{y\right\}
.

Proposition 3.1

(
[
Mum08
, Cor. 2 in Sect. 5, pp. 50–51]
)
.

Let
Z
and
Y
be a locally Noetherian schemes and let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a coherent sheaf on
Z
.
Assume
Y
is reduced and connected.
Then for all integers
q
the following are equivalent

(i)

the function
y\mapsto\dim_{\kappa(y)}H^{q}(Z_{y},{\mathcal{F}}_{y})\
is constant,

(ii)

R^{q}f_{*}({\mathcal{F}})
is a locally free sheaf
and for all
y\in Y
, the natural map

R^{q}f_{*}({\mathcal{F}})\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}}\kappa(y)\to H^{q}(Z_{y},{\mathcal{F}}_{y})

is an isomorphism.

Moreover, if the equivalent conditions
(i)

and
(ii)
are fulfilled for some
q
,
then also the map

R^{q-1}f_{*}({\mathcal{F}})\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}}\kappa(y)\to H^{q-1}(Z_{y},{\mathcal{F}}_{y})

is an isomorphism.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose
X
is a projective scheme
and
Y
be a connected reduced quasiprojective scheme over
{\Bbbk}
.
Fix a coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
on the product
X\times Y
with
\pi_{X}\colon X\times Y\to X

projection to
X
, and
\pi_{Y}\colon X\times Y\to Y
projection to
Y
.
Assume
{\mathcal{F}}
is flat over
Y
and such that

R^{q}(\pi_{Y})_{*}{\mathcal{F}}=0

for all
q>0
.
Then for any closed point
y=\Spec{\Bbbk}\in Y

H^{q}(X,{\mathcal{F}}_{y})=0\qquad\text{ for all }q>0.

Proof.
We will use Proposition
3.1
with
Z=X\times Y
.
Pick a sufficiently large
q
.
Then the condition
(i)
of Proposition
3.1

for that
q
is satisfied, as the cohomologies vanish for
q
greater than the dimension of
X
, in particular,
they are constantly equal to
0
.
Hence by the “moreover” part of Proposition
3.1

the map

R^{q-1}(\pi_{Y})_{*}({\mathcal{F}})\otimes\kappa(y)\to H^{q-1}(X,{\mathcal{F}}_{y})

is an isomorphism.
Moreover, provided
q>1
, by our assumptions

R^{q-1}(\pi_{Y})_{*}{\mathcal{F}}=0
(in particular, it is locally free),
hence condition
(ii)

is satisfied for a smaller value of

q
and hence also condition (i) is satisfied.
By downward induction on
q
, we get the vanishing
H^{q}(X,{\mathcal{F}}_{y})=0

for all
q>0
as claimed.
∎

Theorem 3.3

(Relative Fujita vanishing,

[
Kee03
, Thm 1.5]
)
.

Let
A
be a commutative Noetherian ring, let
Z
be a projective scheme
over
A
, with the morphism
\pi\colon Z\to\Spec A
.
For all coherent sheaves
{\mathcal{F}}
on
Z
,
there exists a
\pi
-ample line bundle
L
on
Z
such that

H^{q}(Z,{\mathcal{F}}\otimes L\otimes D)=0

for
q>0
and all numerically effective line bundles
D
.

The special case, when
A={\Bbbk}
, is known as Fujita Vanishing Theorem, see

[
Fuj83
, Thm (1)]
or
[
Laz04
, Thm 1.4.35]
. It can be also seen as a strengthening
of Serre Vanishing Theorem.

Corollary 3.4

(Fujita vanishing)
.

Let
X
be a projective scheme and fix a coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
on
X
.
Then for a sufficiently ample line bundle
L
on
X
the higher cohomologies vanish:

H^{q}(X,{\mathcal{F}}\otimes L)=0\text{ for all }q>0.

Finally, in the notation of Theorem
3.3
,
we can interpret the
A
-module
H^{q}(Z,{\mathcal{F}}\otimes L\otimes D)
as the global sections
of the coherent sheaf
R^{q}\pi_{*}({\mathcal{F}}\otimes L)
on
\Spec A
.
Thus, for more general projective morphism

\pi\colon Z\to Y
and covering
Y
by open affine subsets, we can conclude
appropriate vanishings for
R^{q}\pi_{*}({\mathcal{F}}\otimes L)
sheaves.
Explicitly, the interesting case is stated in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
X
be a projective scheme and
Y
be a quasiprojective scheme over
{\Bbbk}
.
Fix a coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
on the product
X\times Y
with
\pi_{X}

projection to
X
, and
\pi_{Y}
projection to
Y
. Then for a sufficiently ample line bundle
L
on
X

R^{q}(\pi_{Y})_{*}\Bigl({\mathcal{F}}\otimes\pi_{X}^{*}L\Bigr)=0\qquad\text{ for all }q>0.

Proof.
The statement is local on
Y
, thus we may replace
Y
with its affine open subsets.
Thus assume
Y=\Spec A
for some
{\Bbbk}
-algebra
A
.
Now
L
is sufficiently ample on
X
if and only if
\pi_{X}^{*}L
is sufficiently ample on
X\times\Spec A
.
By
[
Har77
, Thm III.8.5]
we have

R^{q}(\pi_{Y})_{*}\Bigl({\mathcal{F}}\otimes\pi_{X}^{*}L\Bigr)(\Spec A)=H^{q}(X\times\Spec A,{\mathcal{F}}\otimes\pi_{X}^{*}L)

and the latter is
0
by the relative Fujita vanishing, Theorem
3.3
.
This concludes the proof of the first claim.
∎

3.2
Very ampleness

As a warm up before addressing the issues of cactus varieties we review the fact
that sufficiently ample line bundle is very ample.
Although it is known to some experts, it is not easy to reference explicitly, and perhaps it is worth explaining in more detail,
as it also provides a nice application of relative Fujita vanishing in the version of
Lemma
3.5
.

Proposition 3.6

(sufficiently ample line bundles are very ample)
.

Let
X
be a projective variety. Then there exists a very ample line bundle

L\in\Nef(X)

such that for any numerically effective line bundle
D\in\Nef(X)

the line bundle
L\otimes D
is very ample.

Before discussing the proof we comment a little on the content. In general, if
L_{1}
is very ample and
L_{2}
is nef
or even ample, then
L_{1}\otimes L_{2}
is not necessarily very ample, see Example
3.7
.
Moreover, the same example illustrates that
very ampleness
is not invariant under numerical equivalence,
that is if
L_{1}
is very ample and
L_{2}\equiv L_{1}
,
then
L_{2}
is not necessarily very ample.
However, the proposition shows that these two pathologies can only happen “close to the boundary” of the nef cone.
Instead, if we are sufficiently deep in the interior of
\Nef(X)
, then all line bundles are very ample,
as illustrated by the following figure.

For any subscheme

Lemma 3.8
.

Suppose
X
is a projective scheme and
L
is a line bundle on

.
If for any finite subscheme

of degree

we have
H^{1}(X,{\mathcal{I}}_{R}\otimes L)=0

Proof.
A line bundle

More precisely, when for any

of degree

(which is either isomorphic

Considering the twisted ideal-ring short exact sequence

0\to{\mathcal{I}}_{R}\otimes L\to L\to L|_{R}\to 0

we obtain the long exact sequence of cohomology

0\to H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{I}}_{R}\otimes L)\to H^{0}(X,L))\to H^{0}(X,L|_{R})\to H^{1}(X,{\mathcal{I}}_{R}\otimes L)\to\dotsc

Since the

By Fujita vanishing (Corollary

Thus we need some way of controlling the vanishing all at the same time, and this is where

Consider the product

and

.
The

{\mathcal{J}}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{X\times{\mathcal{H}}}
is a flat over

whose fibre
{\mathcal{J}}_{[R]}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
is the ideal sheaf
{\mathcal{I}}_{R}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{X}

Lemma 3.9
.

Suppose
X
is a projective scheme,

is a line bundle on

{\mathcal{H}}={({{\mathcal{H}}}ilb_{2}X)}_{\operatorname{red}}
and

If
R^{q}(\pi_{{\mathcal{H}}})_{*}({\mathcal{J}}\otimes\pi_{X}^{*}L)=0
for all

Proof.
A line bundle is very ample if and only if it is very ample on each of its connected components.
Thus without loss of generality we may assume

is connected, and therefore also
{\mathcal{H}}
is connected.
By definition
{\mathcal{H}}
is also reduced and projective.
The ideal sheaf
{\mathcal{J}}
is flat by definition of the Hilbert scheme and of the universal ideal.

We apply Lemma
3.2

with

Thus
H^{q}(X,{\mathcal{J}}_{[R]}\otimes L)=0

and all

The definition of the universal ideal

and the vanishing of the

.

For future reference we state and prove another lemma on surjectivity of multiplication of section.

Lemma 3.10
.

Suppose
X
is a projective scheme.
The multiplication of sections

H^{0}(X,L_{1})\otimes H^{0}(X,L_{2})\to H^{0}(X,L_{1}\otimes L_{2})

is surjective
when
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are sufficiently ample line bundles.

Proof.
Consider the product
X\times X
and the diagonal embedding

\Delta\colon X\to X\times X
. Then for any line bundles
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
on
X
, we have

(L_{1}\boxtimes L_{2})\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X\times X}}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}=\Delta^{*}(L_{1}\boxtimes L_{2})=L_{1}\otimes L_{2}.

(3.11)

Let
{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{X\times X}
be the ideal sheaf of
\Delta(X)
.
We have a short exact sequence:

0\to{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X\times X}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\to 0.

Twisting it by
L_{1}\boxtimes L_{2}
and exploiting (
3.11
) we obtain:

0\to{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta}\otimes(L_{1}\boxtimes L_{2})\to L_{1}\boxtimes L_{2}\to L_{1}\otimes L_{2}\to 0

Taking into account that

H^{0}(X\times X,L_{1}\boxtimes L_{2})=H^{0}(X,L_{1})\otimes H^{0}(X,L_{2})
,
the corresponding long exact sequence of cohomologies includes:

\dotsb\to H^{0}(X,L_{1})\otimes H^{0}(X,L_{2})\to H^{0}(L_{1}\otimes L_{2})\to H^{1}\left({\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta}\otimes(L_{1}\boxtimes L_{2})\right)\to\dotsb

(3.12)

where the first map is the multiplication of sections by construction.
Therefore, whenever
H^{1}\left({\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta}\otimes(L_{1}\boxtimes L_{2})\right)=0
, then
the multiplication of sections is surjective.

By Fujita vanishing (Corollary
3.4
),
for a sufficiently ample line bundle

Moreover, by Lemma

∎

4
Multigraded rings of sections

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
be an embedded projective scheme.
We will discuss divisorial algebras of sequences of divisors on
X
,
similarly to
[
ADHL15
, §1.3.2]
or

[
KU19
, §1]
, which however have somewhat
different settings (among other differences, these two references require
X
to be a normal variety or prevariety).
The only cases we will be interested in here are those of a single divisor and those of a sequence of two divisors.
In the first case, the traditional name is the
section ring
, and we will stick to this one instead of
the more general “divisorial algebra”. In the second case we use
double section ring
for internal consistency.
Moreover, the only cases we work with here are the cases of very ample Cartier divisors.
By a slight abuse of notation, we will work with the corresponding line bundles instead of divisor,
being aware that strictly speaking the
{\Bbbk}
-algebra structures
(but not their roles in this paper or isomorphism types) depend on
the uniform choices of the divisors in the appropriate linear systems.
We now discuss in detail the two cases (single or double) separately.

4.1
A variant of projective normality

For any line bundle
L
on
X
denote by
S_{L}

the
section ring

S_{L}:={\Bbbk}\oplus\bigoplus_{d>0}H^{0}(X,L^{\otimes d})
, which is an
{\mathbb{N}}
-graded
{\Bbbk}
-algebra
1
1

1

It might be somewhat controversial and/or unorthodox to define the zeroth grading as
{\Bbbk}
instead of

H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X})

and the controversy does not appear.
Otherwise,

might be a different finite
{\Bbbk}
-algebra.
However, for consistency issues
it is more convenient for us to use just
{\Bbbk}
.
.
Let us fix an embedding of
X
via the
complete linear system of a line bundle into projective space

. We denote

be the standard graded polynomial ring and at the same time
the

of
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
.
Let

be the homogeneous ideal of

in this embedding.
Thus, when

is the restriction of
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{N}}(1)
to

•

•

We always have an embedding

Moreover,

is a normal variety.
Equivalently, by

•

is a normal variety and

•

the inclusion above is an equality

Here we are more interested in the second condition,

for more general schemes than normal varieties.
Occasionally, somewhat confusingly, in informal conversations schemes satisfying

in particular, nothing to do with normality of

Since we are primarily interested in complete embeddings of

given by

for a very ample

we will define this as a property of

Definition 4.1
.

We say a line bundle

on a projective scheme

if

as a graded
{\Bbbk}
-algebra is generated in the first degree,

The name “standard graded” refers to the traditional name “standard graded

-algebra”,

[

, p. 115]
,

[

Thus the full name for this notion should be:

As discussed above, whenever

as
a quotient
S_{L}={\Bbbk}[\alpha_{0},\dotsc,\alpha_{N}]/\penalty I_{X}

for the homogeneous ideal

, which is
the homogeneous saturated ideal of

Here the grading of the polynomial ring is standard, that is
\deg\alpha_{j}=1

the number of variables

The reason we stress the above fact is that thanks to this presentation we are allowed to exploit Hilbert function

for both

and its quotients by homogeneous ideals, as illustrated in the standard

Definition 4.2
.

Suppose
X
is a projective scheme and
L
is standard graded line bundle. We say

is a

if the ideal

as above is generated by elements of degree at most

For a homogeneous ideal

the subscheme in

defined by

.

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose
I\subset S_{L}
is a homogeneous ideal such that for some
r,d
with
d\geqslant r

the following assumptions hold:

•

L
is a
d
-embedding

•

\dim_{{\Bbbk}}(S_{L}/I)_{d}=\dim_{{\Bbbk}}(S_{L}/I)_{d+1}=r
, and

•

I
is generated by elements of degree at most
d
.

Then:

•

Z(I)
is a finite subscheme of
X
of degree
r
, and

•

the linear span of
Z(I)\subset X\subset{\mathbb{P}}\left(H^{0}(X,L^{\otimes d^{\prime}})^{*}\right)
is equal
to
{\mathbb{P}}\left(I_{d^{\prime}}^{\perp}\right)
for all

.

•

I
agrees with its saturation from degrees

I_{d^{\prime}}=I(Z(I))_{d^{\prime}}
for
d^{\prime}\geqslant d
.

Proof.
Let
\Phi\colon\Bbbk[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]\to S_{L}
be the quotient morphism whose kernel is the ideal
I_{X}
,
so that
I_{X}=\Phi^{-1}((0))
. In the polynomial ring we consider the grading by the degree of a polynomial
and the induced grading in the quotient ring
S_{L}
.
Let
\widehat{I}=\Phi^{-1}(I)
be the preimage of the ideal
I\subset S_{L}
.
Since
L
is a
d
-embedding, the ideal
\widehat{I}
is generated by elements of degree at most
d
.
Also, by our second assumption on

we have

\dim_{{\Bbbk}}({\Bbbk}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{N}]/\widehat{I})_{d}=\dim_{{\Bbbk}}({\Bbbk}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{N}]/\widehat{I})_{d+1}=r

which means the Hilbert function of the scheme

satisfies

Since

, the assumptions of the Gotzmann persistence theorem,
see
[

, Thm 4.3.3]
, are satisfied
— the maximal jump here is zero and it is attained, so it persists and

\dim_{{\Bbbk}}({\Bbbk}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{N}]/\widehat{I})_{d^{\prime}}=\dim_{{\Bbbk}}(S_{L}/I)_{d^{\prime}}=r\text{ for all }d^{\prime}\geqslant d.

The Hilbert function of

is the same, so we proved the first part of the claim.
Finally, the linear span in the embedding given by

is the zero set
of the elements of degree

that vanish on

and its dimension is equal to

.
∎

An expert reader is probably aware that sufficiently ample line bundles are standard graded.
This statement, Corollary

serves us merely as an example, and we will prove it later as a consequence of a stronger result,
which is relevant in our context.

4.2
Double section ring

In order to prove our results in Section
5
we also need to consider

.
That is, for two line bundles

and
L_{2}
we consider the bigraded algebra:

S_{L_{1},L_{2}}:={\Bbbk}\oplus\bigoplus_{(d,e)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2},\ (d,e)\neq(0,0)}H^{0}(X,L_{1}^{d}\otimes L_{2}^{e}).

Here, similarly as in the definition of

, in the

instead of seemingly more natural

.
Moreover, as mentioned above, in order to define the ring structure on

and consistently, a choice of

in the linear system of

.
Since different consistent choices lead to isomorphic
{\Bbbk}
-algebras, we skip

and

from our notation.

Definition 4.4
.

For a projective scheme
X
and a pair of line bundles

on

we say that
(L_{1},L_{2})

is
doubly standard graded
if
both
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are very ample and
S_{L_{1},L_{2}}
is generated by
(1,0)
and
(0,1)

Note that
S_{L_{1}}\subset S_{L_{1},L_{2}}
, as the sum of
(d,0)
-degree parts for all
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Thus if
(L_{1},L_{2})
is doubly standard graded, then
L_{1}
(and similarly
L_{2}
) is standard graded.
In fact, any

is standard graded for any

.

Lemma 4.5
.

Suppose

is a bigraded algebra with

and
generated by
S_{(1,0)}
and
S_{(0,1)}
.
Then for all

and

and
S_{(d,e)}\otimes S_{(0,1)}\to S_{(d,e+1)}
are surjective.

Proof.
It is enough to prove surjectivity of one map, the other follows by swapping the components of the grading.
We have the surjective map
S_{(1,0)}^{\otimes(d+1)}\otimes S_{(0,1)}^{\otimes e}\to S_{(d+1,e)}
,
which factors through

S_{(1,0)}^{\otimes(d+1)}\otimes S_{(0,1)}^{\otimes e}\to S_{(d,e)}\otimes S_{(1,0)}\to S_{(d+1,e)}

forcing also the latter map to be surjective, as claimed.
∎

By analogy to the (single) standard graded case,

For any closed subscheme

Lemma 4.8
.

Suppose

Proof.
For the item

Since

Now we prove

Corollary 4.10
.

Suppose
X
is a projective scheme.
Then:

(i)

a sufficiently ample line bundle

is standard graded,

(ii)

for sufficiently ample line bundles

the pair

(for a sufficiently ample

the embedding of

In a groaner joke, a mathematician is given the task of removing two nails.
One of the nails is completely hammered into a wooden wall, and the other is stuck out half way.
The mathematician works for hours on an elegant way to pull out the nail that’s nailed in.
After finally removing the difficult one, he hammers in completely the one sticking out in order to reduce
to the previous problem.
This is exactly what we are going to do with item

Proof.
Item
(i)
follows from
(ii)
.
The “in particular” part follows from

(i)
and
[

Pick

such that for all line bundles

and

on

that have their

both of the following properties hold:

•

L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are very ample, and

•

the multiplication of sections

Such

, and
Lemma

To prove
(ii)
,
pick any

and

such that their numerical classes

.
Then for any integers

,
also the class of

is in

with

Thus

4.3
Annihilators and apolarity in the multigraded setting

Following
[
Gał23a
]
,
we recall the apolarity tools in the multigraded setting.
There the variety for which the apolarity is developed is a projective toric variety over
{\mathbb{C}}
,
instead we need it only for
{\mathbb{P}}^{N_{1}}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{N_{2}}
, but over any algebraically closed
{\Bbbk}
.
We thus consider the
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
-graded ring
\Bbbk[\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{N_{1}},\beta_{0},\ldots,\beta_{N_{2}}]
as before
together with its dual ring, which is the divided power ring in variables
x_{0},\ldots,x_{N_{1}},y_{0},\ldots,y_{N_{2}}
.
The apolarity pairing is defined by the following properties:

\lrcorner\colon\Bbbk[\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{N_{1}},\beta_{0},\ldots,\beta_{N_{2}}]\times\Bbbk_{dp}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{N_{1}},y_{0},\ldots,y_{N_{2}}]\\
\to\Bbbk_{dp}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{N_{1}},y_{0},\ldots,y_{N_{2}}],

\displaystyle\alpha_{i}\lrcorner\left(x_{0}^{(a_{0})}\dotsm x_{N_{1}}^{(a_{N_{1}})}\cdot y_{0}^{(b_{0})}\dotsm y_{N_{2}}^{(b_{N_{2}})}\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}x_{0}^{(a_{0})}\dotsm x_{i}^{(a_{i}-1)}\dotsm x_{N_{1}}^{(a_{N_{1}})}\cdot y_{0}^{(b_{0})}\dotsm y_{N_{2}}^{(b_{N_{2}})}&\text{if }a_{i}>0,\\
0&\text{if }a_{i}=0,\end{cases}

\displaystyle\beta_{j}\lrcorner\left(x_{0}^{(a_{0})}\dotsm x_{N_{1}}^{(a_{N_{1}})}\cdot y_{0}^{(b_{0})}\dotsm y_{N_{2}}^{(b_{N_{2}})}\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}x_{0}^{(a_{0})}\dotsm x_{N_{1}}^{(a_{N_{1}})}\cdot y_{0}^{(b_{0})}\dotsm y_{i}^{(b_{i}-1)}\dotsm y_{N_{2}}^{(b_{N_{2}})}&\text{if }b_{i}>0,\\

and

together with bilinearity

Definition 4.11
.

For

we define

In particular, instead of annihilators of elements of some vector space we can equally well talk about

Proposition 4.14

Lemma 4.15
.

Suppose
X
is a projective scheme,
(L_{1},L_{2})
is a doubly standard graded,
d,e\geqslant 0
,
with
(d,e)\neq(0,0)
, and let
p\in{\mathbb{P}}(H^{0}(L_{1}^{d}\otimes L_{2}^{e})^{*})
.
Then for any integers
i,j
,
the
(i,j)
grading of the apolar algebra
\operatorname{Apolar}(p)_{i,j}\neq 0
if and only if
0\leqslant i\leqslant d

and
0\leqslant j\leqslant e
.

Proof.
Since
\operatorname{Apolar}(p)_{i,j}=(S_{L_{1},L_{2}}/\Ann(p))_{i,j}
and the negative gradings of the ring

are all zero,
which proves the claim for

and for

.
By the symmetry in Lemma

(iii)

and for

If

and

, then not all sections from

annihilate

would be

, which

Thus

and also

On the other hand,

and

∎

5
Cactus varieties

In this section we prove our main result, Theorem
1.3
.
For this purpose, throughout this section we work with a fixed projective scheme
X

and a positive integer
r
.

Suppose
L
is a very ample line bundle on
X
, and
A
and
B
are two line bundles on
X
such that

L=A\otimes B
. In this situation we will say that this expression
L=A\otimes B
is a
splitting
of
L
.
The multiplication of sections

H^{0}(X,A)\otimes H^{0}(X,B)\to H^{0}(X,L)
is a linear map that can be represented by the following matrix

M=M_{A,B}
with entries in
H^{0}(X,L)
.
Pick bases
a_{1},\dotsc,a_{\dim H^{0}(X,A)}
and
b_{1},\dotsc,b_{\dim H^{0}(X,B)}
of the spaces
of sections of
H^{0}(X,A)
and
H^{0}(X,B)
respectively and let the
(i,j)
-th entry of
M
is
a_{i}\cdot b_{j}\in H^{0}(X,L)
.
This matrix depends on the choices of the bases, but different choices lead to

\mathbf{GL}(H^{0}(X,A))\times\mathbf{GL}(H^{0}(X,B))
-equivalent matrices.
In particular, for any integer
r^{\prime}
the ideal generated by
(r^{\prime}+1)\times(r^{\prime}+1)
minors of
M
does not depend on the choices.
Thus, simplifying, we will not mention the choices of bases in our further considerations
M
,
we will simply say that
M
is the
matrix associated to the splitting

L=A\otimes B
.

For the induction purposes, we will need to deal with somewhat trivial (but deserving different treatment than general) case

r=0
of Theorem
1.3
, which we deal with here.

Lemma 5.1
.

With the notation as above,
suppose
(A,B)
is double standard graded.
Then the
1\times 1
-minors
of
M
generate the maximal homogeneous
ideal of
S_{L}
.

Proof.
The
1\times 1
-minors of a matrix are just its entries.
Since the multiplication of sections
H^{0}(X,A)\otimes H^{0}(X,B)\to H^{0}(X,L)

is surjective by the definition of double standard graded, the entries of
M
, which are
a_{i}\cdot b_{j}
,
generate
H^{0}(X,L)
. Since
L=A\otimes B
, thus
L
is also standard graded, so that the ideal

(H^{0}(X,L))\subset S_{L}
contains all
(S_{L})_{d}
for any
d>0
, and thus
S_{L}/(H^{0}(X,L))\simeq(S_{L})_{0}={\Bbbk}
,
proving the claim.
∎

Now we want to compare the apolarity theory from §
4.3
to multiplication of sections,
which again is standard knowledge in the case of apolarity in a polynomial ring.
Suppose that
X
is a projective scheme and
(L_{1},L_{2})
is doubly standard graded.
First, we observe that for any
i,j
the standard duality map

H^{0}(L_{1}^{i}\otimes L_{2}^{j})\otimes H^{0}(L_{1}^{i}\otimes L_{2}^{j})^{*}\to{\Bbbk}
coincides with
\lrcorner
in this degree,

\Theta\otimes F\mapsto\Theta\lrcorner F\in{\Bbbk}=(S_{L_{1},L_{2}})_{0,0}
.
Then for fixed integers
d,e,i,j
set
A:=L_{1}^{i}\otimes L_{2}^{j}
and
B:=L_{1}^{d-i}\otimes L_{2}^{e-j}
.
Pick a point
p\in{\mathbb{P}}(H^{0}(L_{1}^{d}\otimes L_{2}^{e})^{*})
,
and consider the evaluation
M_{A,B}(p)
,
for a matrix
M_{A,B}
as above.
For fixed bases, the evaluation is well defined up to a rescaling, depending on the lifting of
p
from
the projective space to the affine space.
But the rank of this matrix
M_{A,B}(p)
is independent of the rescaling.

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose
X
is a projective scheme,
(L_{1},L_{2})

is a doubly standard graded,

d,e\geqslant 0
, with
(d,e)\neq(0,0)
,
and let
p\in{\mathbb{P}}(H^{0}(L_{1}^{d}\otimes L_{2}^{e})^{*})
.
Then for two integers
i,j
such that
0\leqslant i\leqslant d
and
0\leqslant j\leqslant e
and
(i,j)\neq(0,0),(d,e)
,
we have
\dim_{{\Bbbk}}(\operatorname{Apolar}(p)_{i,j})=\rk M_{A,B}(p)
, where
A:=L_{1}^{i}\otimes L_{2}^{j}
and
B:=L_{1}^{d-i}\otimes L_{2}^{e-j}
as above.

Proof.
The assumptions on
d,e,i,j
assure that

(S_{L_{1},L_{2}})_{i,j}=H^{0}(A)
and

(S_{L_{1},L_{2}})_{d-i,e-j}=H^{0}(B)
,
and analogously for
A\otimes B

and
(d,e)
-th grading.
The matrix
M_{A,B}(p)
arises from composing the multiplication of sections and evaluation at

:

(S_{L_{1},L_{2}})_{i,j}\otimes(S_{L_{1},L_{2}})_{d-i,e-j}\to(S_{L_{1},L_{2}})_{d,e}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\to}}{\Bbbk}.

Thus rank of
M_{A,B}(p)
is equal to rank of the following linear map:

\displaystyle(S_{L_{1},L_{2}})_{i,j}

\displaystyle\to((S_{L_{1},L_{2}})_{d-i,e-j})^{*},

\displaystyle\Theta

\displaystyle\mapsto(\Psi\mapsto(\Psi\cdot\Theta)(p)).

By the comparison between apolarity and duality, we have:

(\Psi\cdot\Theta)(p)=(\Psi\cdot\Theta)\lrcorner p=\Psi\lrcorner(\Theta\lrcorner p)
.
Thus explicitly, the linear map above takes
\Theta
to

\Theta\lrcorner p\in((S_{L_{1},L_{2}})_{d-i,e-j})^{*}
.
Then
\Theta
is in the kernel of this linear map if and only if
\Theta\in\Ann(p)
, and

\rk M_{A,B}(p)=\dim_{{\Bbbk}}(S_{L_{1},L_{2}})_{i,j}-\dim_{{\Bbbk}}\Ann(p)_{i,j}=\dim_{{\Bbbk}}\operatorname{Apolar}(p)_{i,j}.

∎

Recall, that for any embedding
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
, the
r
-th cactus variety of
X
is defined as:

\mathfrak{K}_{r}\left(X\right)=\overline{\bigcup\left\{\langle R\rangle\mid R\subset X,\text{ $R$ is a subscheme of degree
at most $r$}\right\}}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{N}.

We split the proof of Theorem
1.3
into two parts.
In the first part, we list conditions on line bundles
A
and
B
that guarantee that the cactus variety of
X

embedded by
L=A\otimes B
is defined by minors of the matrix as above.
This part is algebraic and mimics the proofs in
[

, §5]
, but adapted
to this more general situation. In the second part, we show that for a sufficiently ample line bundle

there exists a splitting
L=A\otimes B
satisfying these conditions.
This proof is largely combinatorial, and in the end it boils down to showing that there exists a lattice point
in some region of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
bounded by a (possibly infinite) collection of linear inequalities.

5.1
Algebraic part: exploiting apolarity in double section ring

Theorem 5.3
.

Pick integers

and

and line bundles

and

on

such that

(i)

(ii)

-embedding in the sense of Definition

,

(iii)

the pair

is doubly standard graded in the sense of Definition

(iv)

Then the cactus variety

For the proof, we need another lemma.
As a consequence of the first one we will be able to use downward induction on

If the assumptions

–

Lemma 5.4
.

In the notation of Theorem

suppose assumptions
(iii)
and
(iv)
hold.
Then also:

(v)

the multiplication of sections

Proof.
If

By induction, it is enough show the claim for

.
Below, for brevity we skip

in the notation for

.
The map of interest

fits as the bottom map into a commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(L_{1})\otimes H^{0}\left(L_{1}^{\otimes(d-r)}\otimes L_{2}^{*}\right)\otimes H^{0}(L_{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(L_{1})\otimes H^{0}\left(L_{1}^{\otimes(d-r)}\right)}

.

\left((S_{L_{1},L_{2}})_{(0,1)}\right)
)

Let

This ideal is also saturated, and corresponds to the ideal sheaf

.
The Hilbert function

of

(which is equal to

,
that is
r
.
It is also non-decreasing,
h_{J}(i,j)\leqslant h_{J}(i+1,j)
and
h_{J}(i,j)\leqslant h_{J}(i,j+1)

.
We also have that

[

By

there are no minimal generators of

in degrees

for

,
and by previous considerations on

.

To show that

By the above considerations,

J_{(d,1)}=J_{(r,1)}\cdot(S_{L_{1},L_{2}})_{(d-r,0)}=J_{(r,1)}\cdot H^{0}(X,L_{1}^{d-r}).

By our assumption

we have

Thus we have:

\displaystyle J_{(d,1)}

\displaystyle=J_{(r,1)}\cdot H^{0}(X,L_{1}^{d-r})=J_{(r,1)}\cdot H^{0}(X,L_{1}^{d-r}\otimes L_{2}^{*})\cdot H^{0}(X,L_{2})

\displaystyle\subset J_{(d,0)}\cdot H^{0}(X,L_{2})=I_{d}\cdot H^{0}(X,L_{2})

\displaystyle\subset\Ann(p)_{(d,0)}\cdot H^{0}(X,L_{2})\subset\Ann(p)_{(d,1)},

as claimed.
Thus indeed
p\in\left\langle R\right\rangle

and
p\in\mathfrak{K}_{r}\left(X\right)

The goal of this section is to show:

Theorem 5.5
.

Suppose

X
.
Then there exist integers

,

, and line bundles

,

–

is

-generated.

4.10
(ii)

and by

(combined with Lemma
2.9
and

2.8

By
D_{0}\in N^{1}(X)
denote the numerical class of
L_{0}
.

,
we have

if and only if the numerical class of

is in

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
L
be an ample line bundle and let

be its numerical class.
Suppose there exist integer

and lattice points
D_{1}
,
D_{2}
such that:

(a)

,

(b)

D=dD_{1}+D_{2}
,

(c)

all of
D_{1}
,
D_{2}
and

(d-r)D_{1}-D_{2}

D_{0}+\Nef(X)
.

Then there exist integer
k
and line bundles

,
L_{2}
that (together with

(i)
–
(iv)

of Theorem
5.3
and such that

.

Proof.
To satisfy
(i)

pick any

such that
r\leqslant k\leqslant d-r
,
which is possible by
(a)
.
Let
L_{1}
be any line bundle whose numerical class is
D_{1}
and define
L_{2}=L\otimes(L_{1}^{*})^{\otimes d}
.
Then the numerical class of
L_{2}
is
D_{2}

by
(b)
.
Moreover, by
(c)
we have:

•

L_{1},L_{2}\in\QDSG
so that
(ii)

and
(iii)
hold,

•

L_{1}^{\otimes(d-r)}\otimes L_{2}^{*}\in\QDSG
so
that
(iv)
holds,

concluding the proof.
∎

Now we work in the lattice
N^{1}(X)
and the corresponding vector space
N^{1}_{{\mathbb{R}}}(X)
.
Assuming
(a)
and

(b)
we translate
the conditions
(c)
of the proposition into some
translated cone conditions.
We may assume that
D
is a class of a sufficiently ample line bundle, which we will write

D=\lambda D_{0}+\Delta
for
\Delta\in\Nef(X)
and a sufficiently large integer
\lambda
(to be determined later).
Write also
D_{1}=D_{0}+\Delta_{1}
.
Then

\displaystyle D_{2}

\displaystyle=D-dD_{1}=(\lambda-d)D_{0}+\Delta-d\Delta_{1},

\displaystyle(d-r)D_{1}-D_{2}

\displaystyle=(d-r)(D_{0}+\Delta_{1})-(\lambda-d)D_{0}-\Delta+d\Delta_{1}

\displaystyle=(2d-r-\lambda)D_{0}-\Delta-(2d-r)\Delta_{1}.

Therefore, in this notation

is equivalent to
the following three classes being in
\Nef(X)
:

\Delta_{1},\quad(\lambda-d-1)D_{0}+\Delta-d\Delta_{1},\quad(2d-r-\lambda-1)D_{0}-\Delta+(2d-r)\Delta_{1}.

(5.7)

In order to proceed, we choose an ample class
H\in\Amp(X)
so that

\Nef(X)
and
\Amp(X)
are defined by a compact set of inequalities
S=\overline{NE(X)}\cap(H=1)\subset N^{1}(X)^{*}
:

\Nef(X)=\left\{E\mid\forall_{\phi\in S}\ \phi(E)\geqslant 0\right\}\text{ and }\Amp(X)=\left\{E\mid\forall_{\phi\in S}\ \phi(E)>0\right\}.

As in Lemma
2.4
, for each
E\in N^{1}(X)
we have a well defined

\operatorname{min}_{E}=\min\left\{\phi(E)\mid\phi\in S\right\}.

We also choose any
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis
e_{1},\dotsc,e_{\rho}
of
N^{1}(X)
, and consider the cube
C
, whose
vertices are
\pm e_{1}\pm\dots\pm e_{\rho}
.

Lemma 5.8
.

Suppose
D_{0}\in\Amp(X)
and
\lambda
is an integer satisfying the following conditions:

•

\lambda\geqslant 2d-r-1
, and

•

\lambda\geqslant\dfrac{3d-r}{d-r}-\dfrac{d(2d-r)\operatorname{min}_{v}}{(d-r)\operatorname{min}_{D_{0}}}
for any choice
of the vertex
v=\pm e_{1}\pm\dots\pm e_{\rho}
of the cube
C
.

Then for any
\Delta\in\Nef(X)

there exists an integral lattice point

\Delta_{1}\in N^{1}(X)
such that all three classes from (
5.7
) are in
\Nef(X)
.

Remark 5.9
.

If we force appropriately stronger bound on
\lambda

in Lemma
5.8

we can also insist that
\Delta_{1}

is in a finer sublattice of
N^{1}_{{\mathbb{R}}}(X)
.
In fact, even if

is any sublattice (not necessarily of full dimension) such that

and both

and
\Delta
are in

, then there exists
\Delta_{1}\in N^{\prime}

such that all classes from (

.
The only modification in the proof (and the statement) is that we must replace the basis of

with a basis of
N^{\prime}
(and correspondingly, the vertices of the cube, and the bound on

).

Proof.
Using elementary linear operations we translate the conditions on nefness of three classes in
(
5.7
) into three conditions on
\Delta_{1}
:

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}

\displaystyle\in\Nef(X),

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}

\displaystyle\in\left(\frac{\lambda-1}{d}-1\right)D_{0}+\frac{1}{d}\Delta-\Nef(X),\text{ and}

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}

\displaystyle\in\left(\frac{\lambda+1}{2d-r}-1\right)D_{0}+\frac{1}{2d-r}\Delta+\Nef(X).

Since
\lambda\geqslant 2d-r-1
, and
d\geqslant 2r
both

of the third class are non-negative, and since

and

are in

, the first condition follows from the third (so the first one is redundant).
Therefore, we must show that there is a lattice point
\Delta_{1}
in the region bounded by a translated

and by a translated

. To obtain this goal,
we use Lemma

.
We have to show that for each vertex

of

the following class is nef

\displaystyle\left(\left(\frac{\lambda-1}{d}-1\right)D_{0}+\frac{1}{d}\Delta\right)-\left(\left(\frac{\lambda+1}{2d-r}-1\right)D_{0}+\frac{1}{2d-r}\Delta\right)+v

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\lambda-1}{d}-\frac{\lambda+1}{2d-r}\right)D_{0}+\left(\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{2d-r}\right)\Delta+v.

Since

, we must have

\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{2d-r}\geqslant 0
.
Thus

Therefore to conclude the proof it is enough to show that the following class is nef:

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\lambda-1}{d}-\frac{\lambda+1}{2d-r}\right)D_{0}+v

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{(2d-r)(\lambda-1)-d(\lambda+1)}{d(2d-r)}\right)D_{0}+v

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{(d-r)\lambda-3d+r}{d(2d-r)}\right)D_{0}+v.

) and (

)
for

\displaystyle\phi\left(\left(\frac{(d-r)\lambda-3d+r}{d(2d-r)}\right)D_{0}+v\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant\left(\frac{(d-r)\lambda-3d+r}{d(2d-r)}\right)\operatorname{min}_{D_{0}}+\operatorname{min}_{v}\geqslant 0.

The last inequality (
\dots\geqslant 0

Future projects

6.1
Non-divisible line bundle
A

In Theorem
5.3
we use a specific splitting

with

which is
a sufficiently large power of some other line bundle.
One may wonder if it is necessary, if we can just take a splitting

.
It is very likely that a generalisation of the arguments in this article can provide such result, by considering

and

6.2
Apolarity for divisorial algebras and Mori Dream Spaces

Apolarity theory outlined in §
4.3

for double section rings, is clearly a very special case of more general theory that should be valid for
any divisorial algebras. This generalisation and most importantly its
applications must be carefully and systematically introduced in a follow up work.
In particular, the special case of a projective normal varieties that are Mori Dream Spaces
(their class group and Cox ring are finitely generated) includes homogeneous spaces other than products
of projective spaces.
One should also compare it to other approaches to apolarity, including

[
ABMM21
]
,
[
Sta23
]
.

6.3
Scheme or ideal theoretic equations

One may ask if the minors obtained in Theorem
1.3

actually generate the ideal of the cactus variety by analogy to

[
SS11
, Thm 1.1]
, or at least if the equations are scheme theoretic.
At the moment we expect this claim to fail. Instead, it is necessary to introduce a potentially non-reduced
scheme structure on
\mathfrak{K}_{r}\left(X\right)
and perhaps the equations are scheme or ideal theoretic for
this enhanced scheme structure.
The article
[
BK24
]
is the first step in this direction.

6.4
Explicit conditions on sufficiently ample line bundles

In specific cases of interest, it could be possible to go through the lines of the proofs in this article
and determine precisely or bound the sufficiently ample condition and describe the set of line bundles
L

satisfying the conclusions of Theorem
1.3
.
We comment this issue also in §
2.2
.

6.5
Other properties of sufficiently ample line bundles

Other than the properties mentioned in this article, what are the interesting classes of properties
that are in general satisfied by sufficiently ample line bundles?
In specific cases, such as smooth curves, this topic is being explored. Can we say something more general
than in dimension
1
?

Appendix A
Appendix: generalisations to non-algebraically closed fields

So far, throughout the paper, we assumed that the base field
{\Bbbk}
is algebraically closed.
In this Appendix we briefly comment on analogous result to Theorem
1.3

when the base field fails to be algebraically closed.

So assume
{\Bbbk}
is an arbitrary field of any characteristics, let
\bar{{\Bbbk}}
be its algebraic closure.
Let
X
be a projective scheme over
{\Bbbk}
. Denote by
\overline{X}=X\times_{{\Bbbk}}\bar{{\Bbbk}}

the extension of
X
to a projective scheme over
\bar{{\Bbbk}}
.
In this setting, the definition of secant or cactus varieties is more tricky than in §
1.1

and it is discussed in detail in
[
BJ17

.

Explicitly, if

then
\sigma_{r}(X)
is the smallest subscheme of
{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}^{N}

whose extension
\overline{\sigma_{r}(X)}=\sigma_{r}(X)\times_{{\Bbbk}}\bar{{\Bbbk}}
contains
\sigma_{r}(\overline{X})
.
Similarly,
\mathfrak{K}_{r}\left(X\right)
is the smallest subscheme of
{\mathbb{P}}_{{\Bbbk}}^{N}
whose
extension
\overline{\mathfrak{K}_{r}\left(X\right)}=\mathfrak{K}_{r}\left(X\right)\times_{{\Bbbk}}\bar{{\Bbbk}}
contains
\mathfrak{K}_{r}\left(\overline{X}\right)
.
See
[
BJ17
, Sect. 5.6, 5.7, 6.1]

for detailed construction involving Hilbert schemes and relative linear spans of families of schemes
and
[
BJ17
, Prop. 6.11]
for the base change claim.

Theorem A.1
.

Suppose
X
is a projective scheme over
{\Bbbk}
and

is a positive integer.
Then there exists a line bundle
\Lambda_{0}
on
X
such that for all ample line bundles
\Lambda
on
X

the embedding of

into

set-theoretically defined by

-minors of a matrix
of linear forms arising from some splitting

and

.

Proof (sketch).

Let
\xi\colon\overline{X}\to X
be the natural morphism coming from

.
We have an inclusion

by

[
Gro95
, Prop. 2.2(i)]
and

is ample
if and only if
\xi^{*}L
is ample: this equivalence follows from
[
Mil86
, Thm 4.2(a)]
and
the fact that ampleness can be expressed as an appropriate vanishing of higher cohomologies
of suitable sheaves
[
Sta24
,

.

\overline{L}_{1}
and

,

By flat base change of cohomology

Primary hepatopancreatobiliary lymphoma: epidemiology, clinical features, and treatment outcomes
Authors: Shenghong Shi, Yaobin Lin, Shan Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Differences in the epidemiology, clinical features, and treatment strategies of primary lymphomas of the liver, pancreas, and biliary system (primary hepatopancreatobiliary lymphomas [PHPBL]) remain unclear. We compared the clinical characteristics of patients with lymphomas of these organ systems to facilitate earlier and more effective interventions. We retrospectively analyzed PHPBL data from the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database (2000–2022). We compared the incidence rates, clinical indicators, and subtype distribution differences among the lymphoma subgroups. Propensity score matching (1:1) was applied to balance covariates between chemotherapy and non‑chemotherapy groups. Multivariate Cox regression models were used to identify prognostic factors. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis was performed. We included 2,509 patients with PHPBL. The overall PHPBL incidence showed a significant declining trend [annual percent change: -1.33%], most particularly for primary pancreatic lymphoma. Pathological type distribution varied across primary sites. Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma was most common in primary hepatic lymphoma, followed by follicular lymphoma in primary pancreatic lymphoma. Mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT) lymphoma most commonly involved the pancreas. Age, pathological type, primary site, SEER stage, marital status, and surgery or chemotherapy were independent prognostic factors. Subgroup multivariable analyses revealed that chemotherapy was independently associated with improved overall survival (OS) in both primary hepatic lymphoma (PHL) and primary biliary lymphoma (PBL), but cancer‑specific survival (CSS) benefit reached significance only in PHL. After propensity score matching, chemotherapy combined with surgery yielded the best survival outcomes in primary pancreatic lymphoma (PPL). Further analysis of surgery in PPL showed marked heterogeneity by histological subtype: surgery significantly improved OS in follicular lymphoma but not in other subtypes. This study revealed unique epidemiological patterns of PHPBL and emphasized the importance of individualized treatment based on pathological subtypes and primary sites. Introduction

Primary hepatopancreatobiliary lymphoma (PHPBL) is an exceptionally rare lymphoproliferative disorder confined to the liver, biliary tract, or pancreas, with an estimated incidence of 0.4–1%1. Its non-specific clinical presentation poses significant diagnostic and therapeutic challenges. PHPBL comprises three subtypes: primary hepatic lymphoma (PHL), primary biliary lymphoma (PBL), and primary pancreatic lymphoma (PPL), all of which are forms of extranodal non-Hodgkin lymphoma2,3. The clinical and imaging features of PHPBL often resemble those of other hepatopancreatobiliary malignancies, such as hepatocellular carcinoma, cholangiocarcinoma, and pancreatic adenocarcinoma, as well as certain inflammatory conditions4. However, their treatment approaches and prognostic outcomes differ markedly. Because of the rarity of PHPBL, no standardized treatment regimen has been established, although combination chemotherapy is widely considered the cornerstone of management.

Although PHPBL is recognized as an aggressive disease, data on its survival prognosis remain limited, with most evidence to date derived from case reports or small case series5–7. Furthermore, previous research has predominantly focused on the clinical features, prognosis, and management of lymphomas arising in more common gastrointestinal sites, such as the stomach, small bowel, or colon8,9. While these studies have provided valuable insights into disease pathogenesis, histology, and treatment response, they have not systematically compared outcomes across different primary locations within the digestive tract. Importantly, distinct primary sites along the gastrointestinal tract may exhibit divergent clinicopathological behaviors and prognoses. Therefore, elucidating variations in clinical characteristics and survival outcomes among these three anatomical primary sites may be critical for the early identification of high-risk patients and for the development of individualized therapeutic strategies. Surgery as any form of cancer-directed surgery recorded in the SEER database.

With a view to facilitating the implementation of timely and effective treatment approaches that could improve the poor prognosis associated with this disease, we sought to identify high-risk patients earlier by delineating the clinical characteristics of primary lymphomas originating in the liver, biliary system, and pancreas. To this end, we used the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database to compare the epidemiological patterns, clinical profiles, and treatment strategies related to these PHPBL subtypes.

Patients and methods

Patients were identified from the SEER database using the International Classification of Diseases for Oncology, 3rd Edition (ICD-O-3). Morphology codes 9590–9599 and 9650–9729 were applied along with site-specific codes: C22.0–C22.1 for the liver/intrahepatic bile ducts, C23.9–C24.1 and C24.8–C24.9 for the biliary tract, and C25.0–C25.9 for the pancreas. The initial search yielded 2,575 cases. We excluded patients with incomplete follow-up information available (n = 62), those diagnosed solely based on clinical assessment (n = 2), and those with unspecified causes of death (n = 2). Finally, 2,509 patients were included in the final analysis. Ethical approval was waived due to the use of anonymized publicly available data. The patient selection process is illustrated in Fig. 1.

Statistical analyses

Age-standardized incidence rates for PHPBL were calculated using the SEER database based on the U.S. standard population for the year 2000. The weighted least-squares method was used to determine the annual percentage change (APC) in the incidence rates for each subtype. To ensure comparability between the chemotherapy and non-chemotherapy groups, a 1:1 propensity score matching (PSM) technique was applied, incorporating factors such as age, race, sex, year of diagnosis, pathological type, Ann Arbor stage, SEER historic stage, marital status, surgery and radiation, with a caliper width set at 0.02. Survival outcomes for each of the three subtypes of PHPBL were assessed using Kaplan–Meier analysis. Factors potentially influencing survival prognosis were first evaluated via univariate Cox regression, and variables with a significance level of P < 0.05 were subsequently included in the multivariate Cox model. Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS v26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and R software (version 3.6.3). A P-value below 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics of patients with PHPBL

In total, 2,509 patients with PHPBL were included in this study. Among the 1,481 patients with PHL, the majority (65.6%, n = 971) were over 60 years of age, and males (62.1%, n = 920) outnumbered females (37.9%, n = 561). Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) was the predominant histological subtype, accounting for 75.1% (n = 1,112) of the PHL cases, followed by mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT) lymphoma (6.6%, n = 98). Similar proportions of patients presented with Ann Arbor stages I–II (33.0%, n = 489) and stages III–IV (31.4%, n = 465). Chemotherapy was administered to 65.6% (n = 972) of patients, while surgical intervention and radiotherapy were utilized in 3.9% (n = 58) and 4.1% (n = 60) of cases, respectively.

The PBL cohort comprised 217 patients, 75.6% (n = 164) of whom were older than 60 years. The sex distribution was nearly balanced, with 51.2% (n = 111) males and 48.8% (n = 106) females. DLBCL was diagnosed in 39.2% (n = 85) of the patients, making it the most common subtype, followed by follicular lymphoma (FL) at 23.5% (n = 51). At diagnosis, 44.7% (n = 97) of the patients with PBL were classified as having Ann Arbor stages I–II, whereas 21.2% (n = 46) had stage III–IV disease. The primary treatment modality was surgery, used in 70.5% (n = 153) of the patients, while chemotherapy and radiotherapy were administered to 47.0% (n = 102) and 5.5% (n = 12) of the patients, respectively.

Of the 811 patients with PPL, 69.3% (n = 562) were over 60 years of age, with a male predominance (62.1%, n = 504). DLBCL was the leading pathological type, identified in 71.1% (n = 577) of the patients, followed by FL in 18.5% (n = 150). More than one-third of the patients (38.7%, n = 314) presented with Ann Arbor stage I–II disease, and 26.6% (n = 216) had stage III–IV disease. Chemotherapy was the mainstay of treatment, administered to 73.1% (n = 593) of the patients, whereas surgery and radiotherapy were employed in 8.3% (n = 67) and 11.7% (n = 95) of the cases, respectively. Distribution of different types of lymphoma in PHPBL

Among the DLBCL cases, the most common primary site was the liver (62.7%), followed by the pancreas (32.5%) and biliary system (4.8%) (Fig. 2A). For FL, the distribution was primarily in the pancreas (60.0%), followed by the biliary system (20.4%), and the liver (19.6%) (Fig. 2B). For MALT lymphoma, the primary sites, in order of frequency, were the liver (64.5%), biliary system (24.3%), and pancreas (11.2%) (Fig. 2C). In Burkitt lymphoma (BL), the liver was the predominant primary site (60.5%), followed by the pancreas (23.5%) and the biliary system (16.0%) (Fig. 2D). For peripheral T-cell lymphoma (PTCL), the liver was the most common primary site (80.6%), followed by the pancreas (16.1%) and biliary system (3.2%) (Fig. 2E).

Annual incidence of PHPBL

Between 2000 and 2022, the overall age-adjusted incidence of PHPBL demonstrated a significant declining trend, with an APC of -1.33% (95% confidence interval [CI]: -2.21% to -0.45%, P < 0.05) (Fig. 3A). This decline was observed in both sexes, with an APC of -1.53% (95% CI: -2.49% to -0.55%, P < 0.05) for males and - 1.21% (95% CI: -2.26% to -0.14%, P < 0.05) for females (Fig. 3B).

When stratified by age, individuals aged < 60 years showed a marked decline in PHPBL incidence (APC: -2.41%, 95% CI: -3.70% to -1.11%, P < 0.05). On the other hand, the decreasing trend in those aged ≥ 60 years was not statistically significant (APC: -0.89%, 95% CI: -1.90% to 0.14%, P > 0.05) (Fig. 3C).

Regarding site-specific trends, the incidence of PHL ranged from 0.63 to 1.29 cases per 1,000,000 person-years, with a non-significant average annual decrease of -1.07% (95% CI: -2.15% to 0.02%, P > 0.05). For PBL, the incidence varied between 0.05 and 0.20 cases per 1,000,000 person-years, also showing a non-significant downward trend (APC: -1.59%, 95% CI: -3.72% to 0.59%, P > 0.05). In contrast, the downward trend in the incidence of PPL was significant, ranging from 0.35 to 0.75 cases per 1,000,000 person-years, with an APC of -1.72% (95% CI: -2.83% to -0.60%, P < 0.05) (Fig. 3D).

Prognostic factors

In the PHPBL cohort, univariate analysis revealed that age; pathological type; primary site; Ann Arbor stage; SEER historic stage; marital status; and use of surgery, radiotherapy, or chemotherapy were significantly associated with overall survival (OS) and cancer-specific survival (CSS) (Table 2). Multivariate analysis including these significant factors identified age, pathological type, primary site, SEER history stage, marital status, surgery, and chemotherapy as independent factors influencing OS. For CSS, the year of diagnosis, age, pathological type, SEER history stage, marital status, surgery, and chemotherapy were independent predictors (Table 3).

Subgroup analysis for identification of patients experiencing limited benefit from chemotherapy

Subgroup analyses, using no chemotherapy as reference category, revealed that patients with certain clinical characteristics did not derive a significant survival benefit from chemotherapy. Specifically, patients with MALT lymphoma experienced significantly shorter OS when treated with chemotherapy (hazard ratio [HR] = 1.871, 95% CI: 1.186–2.952, P = 0.007), whereas those with PTCL showed no significant OS benefit from chemotherapy (HR = 0.668, 95% CI: 0.384–1.162, P = 0.153). Furthermore, the addition of chemotherapy did not confer a significant OS advantage for PHPBL patients who had already undergone surgery (HR = 0.768, 95% CI: 0.557–1.058, P = 0.107) or radiotherapy (HR = 0.854, 95% CI: 0.524–1.394, P = 0.529). Similarly, chemotherapy did not significantly improve OS in the subgroup with PBL (HR = 0.931, 95% CI, 0.646–1.341, P = 0.700) (Fig. 4A).

Consistent with the OS findings, chemotherapy was not associated with a significant improvement in CSS in specific patient subgroups. Notably, patients with MALT lymphoma (HR = 2.628, 95% CI: 1.268–5.448, P = 0.009) and those with PBL (HR = 2.196, 95% CI: 1.293–3.729, P = 0.004) had significantly worse CSS outcomes after chemotherapy. Additionally, no significant CSS benefit was observed for patients with FL (HR = 0.904, 95% CI: 0.549–1.486, P = 0.690) or PTCL (HR = 0.694, 95% CI: 0.373–1.290, P = 0.248). Moreover, PHPBL patients who had previously undergone surgery (HR = 1.673, 95% CI, 1.033–2.710, P = 0.037) or radiotherapy (HR = 1.297, 95% CI, 0.633–2.659, P = 0.477) did not benefit from subsequent chemotherapy. This pattern also held true for patients with localized disease (HR = 0.817, 95% CI, 0.642–1.040, P = 0.101) (Fig. 4B).

Subgroup multivariable analyses revealed that chemotherapy‑based treatments were independently associated with improved OS in both PHL and PBL (Supplementary Tables 1–2), although the association with CSS reached significance only in PHL (Supplementary Tables 3–4).

Survival effects of chemotherapy-based treatment combinations

Before PSM matching, there were significant differences between the chemotherapy group and the non-chemotherapy group in age, sex, pathological type, Ann Arbor stage, SEER historic stage, marital status, surgery, and radiation (P < 0.05). After matching, pathological type and SEER historic stage still differed significantly (P < 0.05), while no statistically significant differences were observed for the other factors (Supplemental Table 5).

In the PHL cohort, OS analysis revealed that the chemotherapy-only (Chemo; 5-year OS: 51.4%) and chemotherapy plus surgery (chemo + Sx) groups (45.6%) had better outcomes than did the chemotherapy plus radiotherapy (chemo + RT) group (42.2%). Patients who did not receive chemotherapy (no-chemo) had the worst prognosis, with a 5-year OS rate of 25.3% (P < 0.001) (Fig. 5A). The chemo + Sx group achieved the highest 5-year CSS rate (64.6%), followed by the chemo-only (59.9%) and chemo + RT groups (61.5%), whereas the no-chemo group had a 5-year CSS rate of 41.6% (P < 0.001) (Fig. 5B). Among the PBL subgroup, the 5-year OS rates for the no-chemo, chemo-only, chemo + Sx, and chemo + RT groups were 53.2%, 53.1%, 48.9%, and 50.0%, respectively (P = 0.670) (Fig. 5C). The corresponding 5-year CSS rates were 80.4%, 56.9%, 58.4%, and 50.0%, respectively (P = 0.014) (Fig. 5D). In the PPL subgroup, the chemo + Sx group was associated with the most favorable OS and CSS in this cohort, followed by chemo-only, the chemo + RT, and no-chemo groups. The 5-year OS rates were 74.7% for the chemo + Sx, 53.7% for the chemo-only, 37.6% for the chemo + RT, and 33.3% for the no-chemo groups (P < 0.001) (Fig. 5E). Similarly, the 5-year CSS rates were 85.1%, 66.8%, 54.6%, and 46.7% for these groups, respectively (P < 0.001) (Fig. 5F).

Prognostic impact of surgery in primary pancreatic lymphoma by histological subtype

Among the 811 patients with PPL, 67 (8.3%) underwent surgery and 744 (91.7%) did not. As shown in Supplementary Table 6, significant differences were observed in Ann Arbor stage distribution (P = 0.003) and year of diagnosis (P = 0.044). Patients in the surgery group had a higher proportion of Ann Arbor stage I–II disease (58.2% vs. 37.0%) and a lower proportion of unknown stage (22.4% vs. 35.8%), indicating that surgically selected patients had earlier-stage disease. No significant differences were found in age (P = 0.594), sex (P = 0.452), race (P = 0.812), pathological type (P = 0.486), SEER historic stage (P = 0.112), marital status (P = 0.810), radiotherapy (P = 0.352), or chemotherapy use (P = 0.195).

To investigate whether the prognostic impact of surgery differed across pathological subtypes, we performed a stratified multivariable Cox regression analysis by histological subtype, adjusting for age, primary site, SEER stage, chemotherapy, and marital status (Supplementary Table 7). The analysis revealed marked heterogeneity in the survival benefit of surgery across subtypes. Among patients with FL, surgery was significantly associated with improved OS (HR = 0.619, 95% CI: 0.384–0.997, P = 0.049). Conversely, in patients with Burkitt lymphoma (BL), surgery was associated with a higher risk of mortality (HR = 1.452, 95% CI: 0.654–3.223, P = 0.360), although this effect did not reach statistical significance, likely due to the limited sample size (n = 8). For patients with DLBCL and MALT lymphoma, no significant survival advantage was observed in the surgical group (DLBCL: P = 0.317; MALT: P = 0.530). Similarly, the “Other” subtypes showed no significant difference (P = 0.808). Of note, no surgical procedures were performed on patients with PTCL in this cohort, precluding a comparative analysis for this subtype.

Discussion

As research on the epidemiology, clinical features, and treatment strategies for PHPBL has been limited to date, we compared these aspects across PHPBL subtypes in a sizeable patient population, based on the SEER database. We showed that, over the past two decades, the overall age-adjusted incidence of PHPBL demonstrated a significant declining trend. For the histological subtype DLBCL, the most common primary site was the liver, followed by the pancreas and biliary system. For the FL subtype, the predominant primary site was the pancreas, followed by the biliary system and liver. Multivariate analysis identified age, pathological type, SEER history stage, marital status, surgery, and chemotherapy as independent prognostic factors for both OS and CSS.

Subgroup analysis of OS revealed that patients with MALT or PTCL, as well as those who had undergone surgery or radiotherapy, received no significant survival benefits from undergoing chemotherapy. However, these subgroup analyses were exploratory and limited by small sample sizes (PTCL n = 62, MALT n = 152). The non-significant results, particularly for PTCL (HR = 0.668, P = 0.153), may reflect insufficient statistical power (Type II error) rather than true biological inefficacy. Therefore, these findings should not be interpreted as evidence that chemotherapy is ineffective for these subtypes. Clinicians should be cautious when deciding whether to abandon chemotherapy based on these data, and should take into account factors such as risk stratification, gene mutations, and complications in a comprehensive manner. Similarly, CSS subgroup analysis indicated that patients with MALT, FL, PTCL, or PBL, along with those who had undergone surgery or radiotherapy, did not benefit from chemotherapy. Again, these analyses were underpowered due to limited sample sizes, and the possibility of false-negative findings cannot be excluded. In terms of treatment combinations, the chemo-only and chemo + Sx regimens yielded superior outcomes in the PHL cohort, whereas all treatment combinations showed limited efficacy in the PBL cohort. For patients with PPL, the combination of chemo + SX demonstrated the most favorable therapeutic effect. These findings underscore the distinct clinical characteristics of PHPBL subtypes and highlight the need to develop individualized treatment strategies in clinical practice.

In the PHL cohort, the most common cellular subtype was DLBCL. Notably, the number of MALT lymphoma cases exceeded that of FL, in line with prior literature10, which may be attributed to the higher prevalence of MALT lymphoma in the digestive system. The etiology of PHL remains incompletely understood, although two main theories are actively posited: infection and immunosuppression11,12. The prevalence of hepatitis B (HBV) and hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection was higher in patients with PHL than in the general population, suggesting a potential causal relationship13. However, due to the absence of HBV/HCV infection data in the SEER database, we were unable to perform a similar analysis.

The current management strategies for primary hepatic MALT lymphoma include surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, and multimodal therapy. Our survival analysis indicated that the outcomes of the chemo + Sx combination were comparable to or better than those of chemo-only. Recent analyses of PHL cases discussed the implementation of various approaches, including partial hepatectomy and liver transplantation14. Studies have highlighted the importance of surgical resection for improving prognosis in cases of localized hepatic tumors15. Our data did not demonstrate a clear advantage of using radiotherapy in PHL, but this may be due to the small number of patients receiving radiotherapy. Nonetheless, radiotherapy has some potential; for instance, Shin et al.16 achieved long-term remission in a patient with primary MALT lymphoma who received only radiotherapy. Recent research has also supported the efficacy of adjuvant radiotherapy or chemoradiotherapy for the treatment of PHL17. Therefore, further exploration and clinical investigation of diverse treatment combinations are warranted as they may contribute to improving the generally poor prognosis associated with PHL.

The incidence of PBL is notably lower than that of PHL and PPL, accounting for approximately 0.4% of all extranodal lymphomas18. Our study identified DLBCL as the most common subtype of PBL, followed by FL and MALT lymphomas. This distribution differed from that in some previous reports, which suggested that MALT lymphoma is the predominant subtype, particularly gallbladder lymphoma, followed by FL and lymphoblastic lymphoma19. This distribution discrepancy may be primarily attributable to ethnic differences, as the SEER database primarily comprises data from Caucasian populations. The etiology of PBL remains unclear. Several studies have noted that half of the reported cases are associated with gallstones, suggesting a potential link with chronic inflammation20.

Pathological diagnosis of PBL is often challenging; consequently, surgical resection is performed in most patients with PBL, sometimes providing both diagnostic and therapeutic benefits. Currently, no consensus on the optimal treatment for PBL has been established, given its rarity. Treatment approaches include surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, or a combination of these strategies. Surgical intervention, which is considered potentially curative in many cases, is also a therapeutic option for patients with biliary obstruction complications, or for those who are unresponsive to chemotherapy21. While chemotherapy is often regarded as the mainstay of treatment and as a key component of postoperative management, we found no significant differences in OS among the various treatment combinations. Moreover, the survival benefit of chemotherapy in terms of both OS and CSS was not statistically significant. Radiotherapy is typically used as an adjuvant to chemotherapy and may improve the survival of patients with residual disease after chemotherapy22. Overall, a better understanding of the biology of PBL and further investigations of suitable treatment combinations in larger clinical cohorts are needed. While chemotherapy remains the cornerstone for both PHL and PBL, the differential impact on CSS suggests that biliary involvement may require additional risk stratification or alternative therapeutic combinations.

PPL accounts for approximately 1.0% of all extranodal lymphomas23. The most common histological subtype of PPL is DLBCL, which frequently occurs in the pancreatic head and predominantly affects elderly males. Our data were consistent with this pattern, indicating that DLBCL is the predominant subtype of PPL, followed by FL.

In terms of treatment, the identical numbers at risk for OS and CSS across all subgroups stem from the Kaplan–Meier definition: “number at risk” counts patients who have neither experienced an endpoint nor been censored. Although event and censoring counts differ between OS and CSS (non-cancer deaths are censored in CSS), the total removed from risk (events + censoring) is identical at each time point, yielding identical risk sets. Our analysis indicated that the combination of chemo + S and Sx yielded the most favorable outcomes for PPL. A 2021 meta-analysis by Facchinelli et al. suggested that the optimal management depends on the histological subtype24. The rituximab, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisone (R-CHOP) regimen is the most commonly used chemotherapy. The addition of rituximab in CD20-positive patients has been shown to improve response rates. Although combined chemoradiotherapy has been used in some cases, its efficacy remains unproven25. The utility of surgery for PPL management has been debated. Splenectomy has been shown to prolong the survival of patients with PPL26. A 2021 meta-analysis reported that patients who underwent surgery for an undiagnosed pancreatic mass (later confirmed as a lymphoma) had a median OS of 11 months, whereas those receiving primary systemic therapy had a median survival of 34.7 months24. However, for selected early-stage cases with localized, well-defined tumors that do not involve major vessels or organs, surgical resection (pancreatoduodenectomy or distal pancreatectomy) aimed at an R0 margin may be considered. This approach may enhance local control and improve survival after adjuvant chemotherapy or radiotherapy. Therefore, treatment strategies should be tailored based on the comprehensive assessment of the individual patient’s condition.

The evolution of chemotherapy regimens during the study period also warrants careful consideration. The introduction and widespread adoption of rituximab in 2006 fundamentally transformed the treatment paradigm for B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphomas, particularly DLBCL and FL, which constitute the majority of our PHPBL cohort27. Substantial evidence indicates that rituximab, typically used in combination with conventional chemotherapy (the R-CHOP regimen), significantly improves complete response rates and overall response rates (including partial response) compared with chemotherapy alone, increasing the cure rate of certain aggressive lymphomas such as DLBCL to 50%–70%28,29. However, because the SEER database does not contain information on specific chemotherapy agents or targeted therapies, we did not perform stratified analyses by immunotherapy. Nevertheless, the observed survival benefits of chemotherapy in our study may partly reflect the effect of modern immunochemotherapy regimens rather than cytotoxic chemotherapy alone. Conversely, patients classified as receiving chemotherapy in the early study period (2000–2006) may have received purely cytotoxic chemotherapy regimens, which could attenuate the apparent survival benefit of chemotherapy in the overall analysis. Future studies incorporating detailed treatment data are needed to conduct refined treatment strategy analyses and to provide more precise, era-specific treatment recommendations for PHPBL.The interpretation of our results must consider several noteworthy limitations inherent to the SEER database. A key constraint is the absence of granular specifics on treatment, encompassing chemotherapy regimens, radiation details, and surgical approaches, which precludes therapy-stratified analyses. The SEER database does not provide information on the specific type of surgical procedure (biopsy, local excision, pancreaticoduodenectomy, or distal pancreatectomy). Therefore, we were unable to distinguish diagnostic procedures from therapeutic resections. This lack of information may introduce bias in estimating the survival benefit associated with surgery. Future studies utilizing clinical registries with detailed procedural coding are warranted to more precisely evaluate the efficacy of different surgical approaches.Additionally, the database does not include information on disease recurrence, modern therapies (immunotherapy/targeted therapy), International Prognostic Index scores, or secondary tumors, possibly contributing to selection bias. Secondly, like any retrospective observational study using the SEER database, selection bias is an inherent issue. Only 8.3% of patients with primary pancreatic lymphoma underwent surgery. Our comparison of baseline characteristics revealed that patients who received surgery had significantly earlier disease stages. Surgical patients were more likely to have localized, resectable disease and better underlying health status—factors that independently predict favorable outcomes. Although we adjusted for available confounders, residual confounding from unmeasured variables (performance status, comorbidities, detailed chemotherapy regimens) cannot be ruled out. Therefore, the survival benefit associated with surgery should be interpreted with caution, and prospective studies with rigorous control of selection bias are warranted to validate these findings.

Conclusions

PHPBL exhibits considerable clinical heterogeneity, with significant variations in prognosis related to both the primary site and the lymphoma subtype. In our investigation of the clinical characteristics of different PHPBL subtypes, we identified factors associated with worse survival, and preliminarily evaluated the prognostic impact of various treatment combinations in different PHPBL subtypes. Our findings are clinically significant, aiding physicians in recognizing high-risk individuals and facilitating the selection of optimal personalized treatment strategies. However, prospective, multicenter, large-scale clinical studies are needed for a comprehensive investigation of the prognostic factors associated with PHPBL and for informing future treatment recommendations.

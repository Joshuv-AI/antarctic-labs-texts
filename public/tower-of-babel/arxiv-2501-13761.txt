Profinite rigidity of Kähler groups: Riemann surfaces and subdirect products
Sam Hughes, Claudio Llosa Isenrich, Pierre Py, Matthew Stover, Stefano Vidussi

Abstract
. This paper establishes strong profinite rigidity results for Kähler groups, showing that certain groups are determined within the class of residually finite Kähler groups by their profinite completion. Examples include products of surface groups and certain groups with exotic finiteness properties studied earlier by Dimca–Papadima–Suciu and Llosa Isenrich. Consequently, there are aspherical smooth projective varieties that are determined up to homeomorphism by their algebraic fundamental group. The main tool is the following: the holomorphic fibrations of a closed Kähler manifold over hyperbolic 2 2 -orbifolds can be recovered from the profinite completion of its fundamental group. We also prove profinite invariance of the BNS invariant.



1.
Introduction

For a smooth complex projective variety
X
, its topological fundamental group will be denoted by
\pi_{1}(X)
and
\pi^{{\rm alg}}_{1}(X)
will denote its algebraic fundamental group. Motivated by the seminal work of Grothendieck
[
35
]
, this paper considers the following questions:

Question 1.1
.

Given an aspherical smooth projective variety with
\pi_{1}(X)
residually finite:

•

To what extent does
\pi^{{\rm alg}}_{1}(X)
determine
X
up to homeomorphism?

•

What about
\pi_{1}(X)
up to isomorphism?

Varieties for which the above questions have positive answers must be very special. Indeed, building on work of Serre
[
60
]
that makes use of the action of
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{C})
, it is known that there exist arbitrarily large collections of aspherical smooth projective varieties that are pairwise not homotopy equivalent but have isomorphic algebraic fundamental groups. See
[
2
,
49
,
63
,
64
]
for a plethora of examples. On the other hand, the main results of this paper show that a strong form of rigidity holds for direct products of hyperbolic Riemann surfaces.

The techniques in this paper apply not only to smooth projective varieties but also to compact Kähler manifolds, so our results are stated in this more general context. Moreover, since the algebraic fundamental group of a smooth complex projective variety is isomorphic to the profinite completion of its topological fundamental group, from here forward this paper will use the language of profinite completions instead of algebraic fundamental groups. The notation
\widehat{G}
will denote the profinite completion of a group
G
.

Recall that a
Kähler group
is a group that can be realised as the fundamental group of a compact Kähler manifold; see
[
1
]
and
[
54
]
for the essential properties of this class of groups. The first main result of this paper is profinite rigidity of products of hyperbolic surface groups amongst residually finite Kähler groups.

Theorem A
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold with residually finite fundamental group. Assume that

\widehat{\pi_{1}(X)}\cong\prod_{i=1}^{r}\widehat{\pi_{1}(S_{i})}

for some
r\geq 1
, where each
S_{i}
is a closed oriented surface of genus at least
2
. Then
\pi_{1}(X)
is isomorphic to
\prod_{i=1}^{r}\pi_{1}(S_{i})
. Moreover, if
X
is aspherical then it is biholomorphic to
\prod_{i=1}^{r}S_{i}
for a suitable choice of complex structure on each of the
S_{i}
.

This theorem relies on a much more general result,
Theorem

C
, which is one of our main results and which will be explained and stated below, once we introduced all relevant notions. Note that Toledo constructed examples of projective varieties with fundamental group that is not residually finite
[
65
]
, so the hypothesis that
\pi_{1}(X)
is residually finite is non-empty. It remains a major open problem to determine whether or not every infinite Kähler group admits a nontrivial finite quotient, but all known examples of infinite Kähler groups admit linear representations over
\mathbb{C}
with infinite image and hence they have nontrivial profinite completion. Note however that some of our results still hold without the residual finiteness assumption; e.g., this is the case of
Theorem

C
below.

Interestingly, the statement of
Theorem

A
is very sensitive to passing to finite index subgroups. Amongst the aforementioned examples building on those of Serre, Bauer–Catanese–Grunewald constructed varieties
X_{1}
,
X_{2}
, both quotients of a fixed product of curves by distinct free actions of a certain finite group, such that
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are not homotopy equivalent but
\widehat{\pi_{1}(X_{1})}\cong\widehat{\pi_{1}(X_{2})}

[
2
, Thm. 7.9]
. Quotients of this kind are said to be
isogenous to a product
. Still, the natural bijection between the sets of finite index subgroups of residually finite groups with the same profinite completion
[
56
, Prop. 4.4]
and
Theorem

A
immediately imply that their construction is the only way to produce examples of closed aspherical Kähler manifolds (with residually finite fundamental group) that have the same algebraic fundamental group as a variety isogenous to a product. To be precise:

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
Y
be a product of finitely many hyperbolic Riemann surfaces,
F
be a finite group acting freely and holomorphically on
Y
, and
{X_{1}=Y/F}
. Suppose that
X_{2}
is an aspherical compact Kähler manifold with
\pi_{1}(X_{2})
residually finite such that
\widehat{\pi_{1}(X_{2})}\cong\widehat{\pi_{1}(X_{1})}
. Then there is a deformation
Y^{\prime}
of the complex structure on
Y
so that
F
still acts holomorphically and freely on
Y^{\prime}
and
X_{2}
is biholomorphic to
Y^{\prime}/F
. In particular,
X_{2}
is also isogenous to a product and has the same associated finite group.

In the case where
X_{1}
is a rigid variety it would be interesting to know whether or not
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
must be equivalent under the action of
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{C})
.

Before stating further results, some classical definitions regarding the
profinite genus
of a group are required.

Genus of a Kähler group

Recall that the
profinite genus

\mathcal{G}(G)
of a finitely generated residually finite group
G
is:

\mathcal{G}(G)=\left\{H\mbox{ finitely generated\ residually finite }\mid\widehat{H}\cong\widehat{G}\right\}/\cong

In other words,
\mathcal{G}(G)
is the set of isomorphism classes of finitely generated residually finite groups with profinite completion isomorphic to
\widehat{G}
. If
{\mathcal{B}}
is a class of residually finite groups, then
{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{B}}(G)
denotes
{\mathcal{G}}(G)\cap{\mathcal{B}}
. A finitely generated residually finite group
G
is then
profinitely rigid
(resp.
\mathcal{B}
-profinitely rigid) if
\mathcal{G}(G)=\left\{G\right\}
(resp.
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{B}}(G)=\left\{G\right\}
), and
G
is
almost profinitely rigid
(resp. almost
\mathcal{B}
-profinitely rigid) if
|\mathcal{G}(G)|<\infty
(resp.
|\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{B}}(G)|<\infty
).

There are very few examples of groups that are profinitely rigid. A well-known open problem attributed to Remeslennikov is whether free groups and surface groups are profinitely rigid. A recent breakthrough is work of Bridson, McReynolds, Reid, and Spitler
[
8
]
that provides examples of profinitely rigid hyperbolic
3
-manifold and
3
-orbifold groups. More relevant to this paper, Bridson, McReynolds, Spitler, and Reid later proved that there are hyperbolic triangle groups that are profinitely rigid amongst all finitely generated and residually finite groups
[
9
]
. See
[
8
,
55
,
57
]
for history and further references on profinite rigidity.

This paper considers the question of
\mathcal{K}
-profinite rigidity, where
{\mathcal{K}}
is the class of residually finite Kähler groups, proving
\mathcal{K}
-profinite rigidity for many important examples. This includes the direct products appearing in
Theorem

A
and certain Kähler subgroups of direct products of surface groups. The examples of Serre
[
60
]
, Bauer–Catanese–Grunewald
[
2
]
, and Stover
[
63
,
64
]
mentioned above show that the
\mathcal{K}
-genus of a Kähler group can consist of more than one isomorphism class. We do not know whether a residually finite Kähler group can have an infinite
{\mathcal{K}}
-profinite genus.

This paper also studies the following notion, which was introduced by Grothendieck
[
35
]
.

Definition 1.3
.

A group
H
is
Grothendieck rigid
if any homomorphism
u:K\to H
from a finitely generated residually finite group
K
to
H
that induces an isomorphism on profinite completions is necessarily an isomorphism.

Lastly, in this paper a hyperbolic orbisurface group will always mean a discrete
cocompact
subgroup of
{\rm PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Note that a lattice in
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
is a Kähler group if and only if it is cocompact. The fact that cocompact lattices with torsion are Kähler follows from a trick due to Kollár
[
1
, p. 7]
. So the fact that an orbisurface group is always cocompact in this paper is no loss of generality. The rank of a finitely generated abelian group
H
means the minimal number of generators of the quotient of
H
by its torsion subgroup. Another main result of this paper is the following.

Theorem B
.

Let
G
be a Kähler group that is either:

•

a hyperbolic orbisurface group,

•

a direct product of a finitely generated abelian group of even rank with finitely many hyperbolic orbisurface groups, or

•

the kernel of a factorwise surjective homomorphism from a product of hyperbolic surface groups onto
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

Then
G
is profinitely rigid amongst residually finite Kähler groups, as well as Grothendieck rigid amongst residually finite Kähler groups.

Kähler groups of the form appearing in the third point of
Theorem

B
do indeed exist. The first examples were constructed by Dimca, Papadima, and Suciu
[
23
]
, then studied further and generalised by Llosa Isenrich
[
43
,
44
,
45
]
. These groups are notable due to the fact that, despite being fundamental groups of closed Kähler manifolds and thus finitely presented, they lack higher finiteness properties: for each
n\geq 2
, there are examples that are of type
\mathsf{F}_{n}
but not of type
\mathsf{F}_{n+1}
. Recall that a group
G
is of type
\mathsf{F}_{n}
if it admits a
K(G,1)
with finite
n
-skeleton. In light of this degeneracy, the fact that these coabelian subgroups are profinitely rigid amongst Kähler groups seems interesting.

Using earlier classification results due to Llosa Isenrich
[
45
]
, one can use
Theorem

B
to obtain the following:

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
\Gamma_{1}
,
\Gamma_{2}
,
\Gamma_{3}
be three hyperbolic orbisurface groups and

G\leq\Gamma_{1}\times\Gamma_{2}\times\Gamma_{3}

be a Kähler group. Then
G
contains a finite index subgroup
H
that is profinitely rigid amongst residually finite Kähler groups.

Fibrations

The main tool used to establish Theorems
A
and
B
is a profinite rigidity result for fibrings showing that holomorphic fibrations from a closed Kähler manifold
X
onto hyperbolic orbisurfaces are detected by
\widehat{\pi_{1}(X)}
. Before stating this more precisely, some classical facts about fibrations of Kähler manifolds over Riemann surfaces are now recalled.

A closed orbi-Riemann surface (from here forward,
orbisurface
) is a closed Riemann surface
S
together with a finite set of points
P\subset S
and a collection of natural numbers
(m_{p})_{p\in P}
such that
m_{p}\geq 2
for all
p\in P
. This orbifold is sometimes denoted
S^{*}
or
(S,P)
, but more often the symbol
S
is used both for the orbifold and for the underlying (unmarked) Riemann surface. Then
S
is
hyperbolic
if its orbifold Euler characteristic

(1.4a)

\chi^{{\rm orb}}(S,P)=\chi(S)-\sum_{p\in P}\left(1-\frac{1}{m_{p}}\right)

is negative.

Definition 1.5
.

A
fibration
from a closed complex manifold
X
onto a Riemann surface
S
is a surjective holomorphic map with connected fibres.

Remark 1.6
.

When
S
has positive genus, fibrations also appear under the name
irrational pencils
in the literature.

If
X
is a closed complex manifold and
f:X\to S
a fibration onto a closed Riemann surface, one can define an orbifold structure
S_{f}^{\ast}=(S,P)
on
S
using the multiplicities of singular fibres of
f
in such a way that the following properties hold:

(1)

The map
f:X\to S_{f}^{\ast}
is holomorphic in the orbifold sense.

(2)

The natural morphism
\pi_{1}(X)\to\pi_{1}(S)
can be lifted to a morphism

f_{\ast}:\pi_{1}(X)\longrightarrow\pi_{1}^{{\rm orb}}(S_{f}^{\ast})

with finitely generated kernel, which is called the morphism
induced by
f
.

(3)

Up to isomorphism of the base, there are only finitely many fibrations
f:X\to S
for which the induced orbifold
S_{f}^{\ast}
is hyperbolic.

(4)

When
X
is Kähler, the morphisms induced by fibrations to hyperbolic orbisurfaces depend only on
\pi_{1}(X)
and not on the specific Kähler manifold
X
.

See
Section

2
for the definition of the group
\pi_{1}^{{\rm orb}}(S_{f}^{\ast})
. The reader should consult
[
20
]
,
[
15
]
, or
[
54
, Ch. 2]
for the definitions of a holomorphic map to an orbisurface and of the orbifold
S_{f}^{\ast}
, along with proofs of the above results.

Consequently, if
G
is a Kähler group then there is a canonical finite collection
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{r}
of hyperbolic orbisurface groups equipped with surjective homomorphisms
\varrho_{i}:G\to\Gamma_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq r
) each induced by a holomorphic fibration
f
with
S_{f}^{\ast}
hyperbolic. In particular, each
\varrho_{i}
has finitely generated kernel.

Definition 1.7
.

The homomorphism
(\varrho_{1},\dots,\varrho_{r}):G\to\Gamma_{1}\times\dots\times\Gamma_{r}
defined in the previous paragraph is called the
universal homomorphism
for
G
.

[
44
, Thm. 9.1]
introduced the universal homomorphism to rephrase results of Corlette–Simpson
[
15
]
and Delzant
[
22
]
in terms of a homomorphism to a direct product.
Section

2
will precisely describe the sense in which this homomorphism is universal. The universal homomorphism played a fundamental role in many recent advances in the study of Kähler groups (e.g.,
[
44
,
45
]
), and the next result shows that it is a profinite invariant amongst Kähler groups.

Theorem

C
.

Let
G
and
H
be Kähler groups,

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle=(\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{r}):G\longrightarrow\Gamma_{1}\times\dots\times\Gamma_{r}

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle=(\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{s}):H\longrightarrow\Sigma_{1}\times\dots\times\Sigma_{s}

be their universal homomorphisms, and
\widehat{\phi}=(\widehat{\phi}_{1},\dots,\widehat{\phi}_{r}),\widehat{\psi}=(\widehat{\psi}_{1},\dots,\widehat{\psi}_{s})
be the induced homomorphisms on profinite completions. If there is an isomorphism
\Theta:\widehat{G}\to\widehat{H}
, then
r=s
and, up to reordering factors,
\Gamma_{i}
is isomorphic to
\Sigma_{i}
for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
. Moreover,
\widehat{\psi}\circ\Theta=\widehat{\phi}
after identifying the groups
\widehat{\Gamma}_{i}
and
\widehat{\Sigma}_{i}
through suitable isomorphisms of profinite orbisurface groups. In particular
\im(\widehat{\psi})\cong\im(\widehat{\phi})
.

Note that this result has no residual finiteness hypothesis. The expression
profinite orbisurface group
is used as shorthand for
profinite completion of an orbisurface group
. The proof of
Theorem

C
will use the fact that hyperbolic orbisurface groups with the same profinite completion are isomorphic, a result due to Bridson, Conder and Reid
[
6
]
.
Theorem

C
should be contrasted with the long-standing open question of Delzant–Gromov
[
18
]
asking for a characterisation of the possible images of universal homomorphisms of Kähler groups; this question was resolved by the second author in the case of a product of three orbisurface groups
[
45
]
.

The BNS invariant

By work of Delzant
[
21
]
, the universal homomorphism of a Kähler group
G
is closely related to its Bieri–Neumann–Strebel invariant
\Sigma^{1}(G)
(or BNS-invariant for short). See
[
5
]
for the original article on
\Sigma^{1}(G)
, as well as
[
54
, Ch. 11]
for an introduction. If
\Gamma
is a finitely generated group, its BNS-invariant is a subset

\Sigma^{1}(\Gamma)\subseteq H^{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{R})-\{0\}

which is invariant by multiplication by positive scalars. It is often viewed as a subset of the sphere

\left(H^{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{R})-\{0\}\right)\!/\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\ast},

but this paper considers it as a subset of
H^{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{R})-\{0\}
. Briefly,
\Sigma^{1}(\Gamma)
is defined as follows. One considers a Cayley graph
{\rm Cay}(\Gamma)
for
\Gamma
, associated to a finite symmetric generating subset. A homomorphism
\chi\in H^{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{R})-\{0\}
is then said to belong to
\Sigma^{1}(\Gamma)
if the subgraph of
{\rm Cay}(\Gamma)
spanned by vertices where
\chi
takes nonnegative values is connected.

The BNS-invariant is famously related to finite generation of kernels of homomorphisms to infinite abelian groups. Now, consider a Kähler group
G
with universal homomorphism

(\varrho_{1},\ldots,\varrho_{r}):G\longrightarrow\Gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{r}

with each
\Gamma_{i}
a hyperbolic orbisurface group. The morphism
\varrho_{i}
induces an injection
H^{1}(\Gamma_{i},\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow H^{1}(G,\mathbb{R})
whose image is denoted by
V_{i}
. Delzant proved that the BNS invariant
\Sigma^{1}(G)
coincides with the complement of the union

\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}V_{i}

of the
V_{i}
. Also note that if
G
and
H
are groups with isomorphic profinite completions, then there is a linear isomorphism
L:H^{1}(G,\mathbb{Q})\to H^{1}(H,\mathbb{Q})
. Let
L_{\mathbb{R}}
denote the
\mathbb{R}
-linear extension
H^{1}(G,\mathbb{R})\to H^{1}(H,\mathbb{R})
of
L
. One can then ask:

Question 1.8
.

Can
L
be chosen so that
L_{\mathbb{R}}(\Sigma^{1}(G))=\Sigma^{1}(H)
?

According to
Theorem

C
, in the Kähler case the respective complements of the BNS invariants of
G
and
H
in
H^{1}(G,\mathbb{R})
and
H^{1}(H,\mathbb{R})
consist of the same number of linear subspaces, and the set of dimensions of these subspaces are the same for
G
and
H
. Note that it is possible to have a triple
V_{1}
,
V_{2}
,
V_{3}
such that
V_{3}\cap(V_{1}\oplus V_{2})\neq\left\{0\right\}
, as shown by the examples built in
[
23
]
, so it is not obvious that an isomorphism on first cohomology must respect BNS invariants. However, the answer to
Question

1.8
is indeed affirmative for Kähler groups.

Theorem

D
.

Let
G
and
H
be Kähler groups such that
\widehat{G}\cong\widehat{H}
. Then there is a linear isomorphism
L:H^{1}(G,\mathbb{Q})\to H^{1}(H,\mathbb{Q})
such that

L_{\mathbb{R}}(\Sigma^{1}(G))=\Sigma^{1}(H).

The proof of
Theorem

D
relies on work of Hughes and Kielak
[
37
]
, which itself was inspired by earlier work on
3
-manifold groups by Liu
[
42
]
and Jaikin-Zapirain
[
38
]
. We shall not give a detailed survey of the relevant prior work here, but simply mention two facts:

•

whether or not a closed
3
-manifold
M
fibres over the circle can be detected on the profinite completion of
\pi_{1}(M)

[
38
]
;

•

Theorem

2.A.
Orbisurfaces and ramified covers

Beyond presenting standard material on orbifolds and ramified covers, this subsection also establishes notation and describes a finiteness result for certain ramified covers that is needed later in the paper. A more detailed exposition concerning orbifolds can be found e.g. in
[
59
, §2]
.

Suppose that
S
is a closed oriented surface and
P\subset S
is a finite set of points together with integer multiplicities
(m_{p})_{p\in P}
, where
m_{p}\geq 2
for each
p
. Let
S^{\ast}
be the orbifold associated with the data consisting of the set
P
and the multiplicities
(m_{p})_{p\in P}
and let
\gamma_{p}
denote a small loop in
S-P
enclosing
p
once. The loop
\gamma_{p}
defines a conjugacy class in the group
\pi_{1}(S-P)
, and the orbifold fundamental group of
S^{\ast}
is the quotient

\pi_{1}^{{\rm orb}}(S^{\ast})=\pi_{1}(S-P)/\llangle\gamma_{p}^{m_{p}}=1,p\in P\rrangle.

When
S^{\ast}
is hyperbolic, i.e., when its orbifold Euler characteristic (
1.4a
) is negative, the group
\pi_{1}^{{\rm orb}}(S^{\ast})
can be realised as a cocompact discrete subgroup of
{\rm PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Conversely, any cocompact discrete subgroup of
{\rm PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
can be realised as the orbifold fundamental group of a hyperbolic orbifold. When the names of the orbifold points will only clutter notation,
S_{g,\underline{n}}
will denote the orbifold whose underlying surface has genus
g\geq 0
and
k\geq 0
marked points of order
n_{i}\geq 2
for
\underline{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
.

The following finiteness result for ramified covers between topological surfaces is required in
Section

4
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
S_{1}
be a closed oriented surface of genus one. For every
g,d\geq 2
, there are only finitely many isomorphism classes of ramified covers
f:S_{g}\to S_{1}
of degree at most
d
by a closed oriented surface
S_{g}
of genus
g
.

Here, we say that two ramified covers
f:S_{g}\to S_{1}
and
f^{\prime}:S^{\prime}_{g}\to S_{1}
are isomorphic if there are homeomorphisms
\phi:S_{g}\to S^{\prime}_{g}
and
\psi:S_{1}\to S_{1}
such that
f=\psi\circ f^{\prime}\circ\phi
, and
\psi
is isotopic to the identity.

Proof.

Let
f:S_{g}\to S_{1}
be as in the statement of the proposition,
\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}\}
be the ramification points of
f
, and
\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}\}
be the corresponding multiplicities. The Riemann-Hurwitz formula yields the equation

2g-2=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(m_{i}-1),

hence
1\leq k\leq 2g-2
. If
\left\{q_{1},\dots,q_{\ell}\right\}=f(\left\{p_{1},\dots,p_{k}\right\})
is the collection of branch points, then
\ell\leq 2g-2
. The surface
S_{g}
and the map
f
are completely determined by the covering space

S_{g}-f^{-1}(\{q_{1},\dots,q_{\ell}\})\longrightarrow S_{1}-\{q_{1},\dots,q_{\ell}\}

induced by
f
. Each such covering space is associated with a conjugacy class of index
d
subgroups of the free group
\pi_{1}(S_{1}-\{q_{1},\dots,q_{\ell}\})
of rank
\ell+1
. There are finitely many such subgroups for each
\ell
and finitely many
\ell
, hence finitely many coverings. This proves the result.
∎

2.B.
Direct products of groups and their subgroups

This section presents a characterisation of finitely presented subgroups of direct products due to Bridson, Howie, Miller, and Short
[
7
]
. Before stating it, some notation is needed.

Consider a direct product
G=G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{r}
of finitely many groups. For any choice of indices
1\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}\leq r
,

p_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}:G\longrightarrow G_{i_{1}}\times\dots\times G_{i_{k}}

denotes the appropriate projection and
G_{i}\leq G
denotes the canonical embedding of
G_{i}
in
G
as the
i^{th}
direct factor. A subgroup
H\leq G

•

is
subdirect
if
p_{i}(H)=G_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq r
;

•

is
full
if
(G_{i}\cap H)\neq\left\{1\right\}
for all
1\leq i\leq r
; and

•

has the
virtual surjection to pairs
(
VSP
) property if
p_{i,j}(H)\leq G_{i}\times G_{j}
has finite index in
G_{i}\times G_{j}
for each
1\leq i<j\leq r
.

Given an arbitrary nontrivial subgroup
H\leq G
, after possibly reordering factors, there is always an embedding of
H
as a full subdirect product
{H\leq G^{\prime}_{1}\times\cdots\times G^{\prime}_{s}}
in a direct product of subgroups

. To see this, if there is a single factor that intersects

with its isomorphic image under the projection, and replace the remaining factors by the projections

In

, Bridson, Howie, Miller, and Short carried out a detailed analysis of the properties of finitely presented residually free groups. In the course of this study, they proved the following general result about subgroups of direct products (see Theorem A in
[

). Note that the only hypothesis on each factor is that it is finitely presented.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{r}
be finitely presented groups and

H\leq\Gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{r}

be a full subdirect product. If

has the VSP property, then it is finitely presented.

There is a converse to

2.2
when the groups

are residually free. Since it is not used in this paper, the reader is referred to
[
7
]
for a precise statement. Finally, the following result about subgroups of direct products of orbisurface groups, whose key argument follows almost verbatim from
[
7
, Prop. 6.12]
, is required later. The proof is included for completeness.

Proposition 2.3
.

If
H\leq\Gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{r}
and
H^{\prime}\leq\Gamma^{\prime}_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma^{\prime}_{s}
are isomorphic full subdirect products of hyperbolic orbisurface groups, then

and, up to permuting factors, there are isomorphisms
\Gamma_{i}\cong\Gamma^{\prime}_{i}
that induce an isomorphism
H\cong H^{\prime}
of subgroups.

Proof.

To see that

, first note that

is a nontrivial normal subgroup of
H
, since
H
is full. Since

is subdirect,

is a nontrivial normal subgroup of the hyperbolic orbisurface group
\Gamma_{i}
. Standard facts about Fuchsian groups then imply that
p_{i}(H_{i})
contains an element of infinite order, hence
H_{i}
also does. The

commute with one another, thus

contains a free abelian subgroup isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{r}
. Maximal abelian subgroups of hyperbolic orbisurface groups are cyclic, which implies that

is the maximal possible rank of a free abelian subgroup of
\Gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{r}

. The same argument holds for

, and thus

(resp.

(resp.

and consider the epimorphism

through some

. Applying the same logic to the epimorphism

2.C.
The universal homomorphism

Let
G
be a Kähler group.
Definition

introduced the universal homomorphism of

, denoted

\varrho\coloneq(\varrho_{1},\ldots,\varrho_{r}):G\longrightarrow\Gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{r}.

This section collects several of its key properties.

Proposition 2.4
.

The morphism
\varrho=(\varrho_{1},\ldots,\varrho_{r})
has the following properties.

(1)

Each homomorphism
\varrho_{i}
is surjective with finitely generated kernel.

(2)

The image
\varrho(G)\leq\Gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{r}
is full subdirect and virtually surjects onto pairs. In particular, it is finitely presented.

(3)

Every epimorphism
\psi:G\to\Gamma
onto a hyperbolic orbisurface group
\Gamma
factors through one of the
\varrho_{i}
and thus also through
\varrho
. If
\psi
moreover has finitely generated kernel, then there is an index
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
and an isomorphism
\theta:\Gamma\to\Gamma_{i}
such that
\theta\circ\psi=\varrho_{i}
.

Proof.

Part (1) was already mentioned in the introduction. For example, see
[
12
, Lem. 4.2]
or
[
20
, Thm. 3]
for a proof. Part (2) is
[
44
, Lem. 9.2]
.

For Part (3), realise
\Gamma
as a cocompact discrete subgroup of
{\rm PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
acting on the hyperbolic plane
\mathbb{H}^{2}
and
G
as the fundamental group of a closed Kähler manifold

. Note that if
\psi
factors through a morphism induced by a holomorphic fibration of
X
onto an orbisurface, the base must be hyperbolic and hence
\psi
factors through one of the
\varrho_{i}
. Now suppose for a contradiction that
\psi
does not factor through such a fibration. According to
[
54
, Thm. 9.31]
, there exists a
\psi
-equivariant map

that is holomorphic for one of the two
{\rm PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-invariant complex structures on
\mathbb{H}^{2}
. Moreover, the proof of
[

, Thm. 9.31]
shows in this case that the image of
\psi
cannot be discrete, which is a contradiction since
\Gamma=\psi(G)
is discrete by assumption. Therefore
\psi
must factor through through a morphism induced by a holomorphic fibration onto a hyperbolic orbisurface, i.e., must factor through some
\varrho_{i}
. For the last claim, dealing with the case where

has finitely generated kernel, see the original proof in
[
13
]
or
[

, Thm. 2.23]
.
∎

The next lemma shows that the universal homomorphism for a Kähler full subdirect product of orbisurface groups is simply the inclusion in the direct product. This observation will be used in
Section

4
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G<\Gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{r}
be a Kähler full subdirect product of hyperbolic orbisurface groups
\Gamma_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq r
. Then the inclusion

\phi:G\longrightarrow\Gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{r}

is the universal homomorphism for

.

Proof.

Since

is finitely presented and full subdirect, it follows from
[

, Thm. 6.4]
(see also
[

, Thm. 4.6]
for the case of hyperbolic surface groups) that the projections

have finitely generated kernel. Thus the

are factors of the universal homomorphism, and it remains to show that there are no other factors. Applying the exact same argument as in the proof of
[

, Prop. 6.12]
and

, we see that any surjection from

onto a hyperbolic orbisurface group must factor through projection onto some

, which proves that there are indeed no other factors.
∎

We are now prepared to prove
Corollary

3.5
.

Proof of
Corollary

3.5
.

Consider a surjective homomorphism
\widehat{\psi}:\widehat{G}\to\widehat{\Sigma}
to a profinite hyperbolic orbisurface group. Then, there is a finite index normal surface subgroup
\Sigma_{0}\unlhd\Sigma
with

. Consider the subgroup
\widehat{\Sigma}_{0}\lhd\widehat{\Sigma}
and its inverse image

The homomorphism

factors through a component of the universal homomorphism for

by
Lemma

3.4
. We apply
Lemma

3.6
, with
\Lambda=\widehat{\psi}\circ\iota_{G}(G)
. Note that
\Lambda
embeds densely into
\widehat{\Sigma}
. This implies that it satisfies all the hypotheses from

3.6
, the one concerning finite normal subgroups being a consequence of

.
Lemma

{(\widehat{\psi}\circ\iota_{G}):G\to\widehat{\Sigma}}

.∎

3.C.
The universal homomorphism is a profinite invariant

The purpose of this section is to prove
Theorem

C
, whose statement is now recalled.

Theorem C
.

Let
G
and
H
be Kähler groups,

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle=(\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{r}):G\longrightarrow\Gamma_{1}\times\dots\times\Gamma_{r}

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle=(\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{s}):H\longrightarrow\Sigma_{1}\times\dots\times\Sigma_{s}

be their universal homomorphisms, and
\widehat{\phi}=(\widehat{\phi}_{1},\dots,\widehat{\phi}_{r}),\widehat{\psi}=(\widehat{\psi}_{1},\dots,\widehat{\psi}_{s})
be the induced homomorphisms on profinite completions. If there is an isomorphism
\Theta:\widehat{G}\to\widehat{H}
, then
r=s
and, up to reordering factors,
\Gamma_{i}
is isomorphic to
\Sigma_{i}
for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
. Moreover,
\widehat{\psi}\circ\Theta=\widehat{\phi}
after identifying the groups
\widehat{\Gamma}_{i}
and
\widehat{\Sigma}_{i}
through suitable isomorphisms of profinite orbisurface groups. In particular
\im(\widehat{\psi})\cong\im(\widehat{\phi})
.

Proof.

If
\Lambda
is a hyperbolic orbisurface group, then the homomorphisms
\widehat{\phi}
and
\widehat{\psi}
are universal with respect to surjective homomorphisms
\widehat{G}\cong\widehat{H}\to\widehat{\Lambda}
by
Corollary

3.5
. For every
i\in\left\{1,\ldots,r\right\}
, the universal property of
\widehat{\psi}
implies that there is a unique
k_{i}\in\left\{1,\ldots,s\right\}
such that there exists a factorisation
\widehat{\phi_{i}}\circ\Theta^{-1}=\widehat{\alpha}_{k_{i}}\circ\widehat{\psi}_{k_{i}}
with
\widehat{\alpha}_{k_{i}}:\widehat{\Sigma}_{k_{i}}\to\widehat{\Gamma}_{i}
surjective. Similarly, the universal property of
\widehat{\phi}
implies that there is a unique
\ell_{k_{i}}\in\left\{1,\ldots,r\right\}
and an epimorphism
\widehat{\beta}_{\ell_{k_{i}}}:\widehat{\Gamma}_{\ell_{k_{i}}}\to\widehat{\Sigma}_{k_{i}}
such that
\widehat{\psi}_{k_{i}}\circ\Theta=\widehat{\beta}_{\ell_{k_{i}}}\circ\widehat{\phi}_{\ell_{k_{i}}}
. This produces a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\Gamma}_{\ell_{k_{i}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\Gamma}_{i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\Gamma}_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\phi}_{\ell_{k_{i}}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\phi}_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\alpha}_{k_{i}}\circ\widehat{\beta}_{\ell_{k_{i}}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rm id}

of epimorphisms. Uniqueness in

implies that
\ell_{k_{i}}=i
. This proves that the map

is injective and the map

is surjective. Exchanging the roles of

and

proves that both maps are bijections, hence

. Thus after reordering factors we can assume that

and

(

). The arguments above then yield

. Thus

and

are Fuchsian groups, it follows from

that

for each

.
∎

4.
Profinite rigidity results for Kähler subgroups of direct products

This section studies the profinite genus of a Kähler subgroup of a direct product of hyperbolic orbisurface groups. The first result is that being a full subdirect product of orbisurface groups is a profinite invariant (
Theorem

4.6
). This is then used to establish profinite rigidity amongst residually finite Kähler groups for direct products of orbisurface groups and their co-
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
Kähler subgroups.

4.A.
Preliminaries

Here we recall general results on residually free groups and separability that are needed in the next subsections.

Definition 4.1
.

A subgroup
H\leq G
is
separable
if it is the intersection of a family of finite index subgroups of
G
.

It follows from the definition of the profinite topology that a subgroup
{H\leq G}
is separable if and only if it is closed in the profinite topology. Moreover, a group is residually finite if and only if the trivial subgroup is separable. A crucial result in this paper is the following theorem of Bridson and Wilton
[
11
, Thm. B]
, where the second statement follows from applying
[
56
, Lem. 4.6]
to their result.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated residually free group. Then all finitely presented subgroups of
G
are separable. In particular, the inclusion
H\hookrightarrow G
of a finitely presented subgroup induces an isomorphism between
\widehat{H}
and the closure
\overline{H}
of the image of
H
in
\widehat{G}
.

The next result follows from a straightforward adaptation of an argument due to Scott
[
58
, Proof of Lem. 1.1]
. A simple topological proof is given for completeness.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
G
be a group with a finite index subgroup
H<G
such that every finitely presented subgroup of
H
is separable in
H
. Then every finitely presented subgroup of
G
is separable in
G
.

Proof.

Suppose
L<G
is finitely presented. Then
L\cap H
is a finite index subgroup of
L
, and thus is also finitely presented. In particular,
L\cap H
is closed in the profinite topology on
H
and hence is closed in the profinite topology on
G
by definition. Then
L
is a finite union of cosets of
L\cap H
, and therefore is also closed.
∎

The following result can be viewed as a version of Grothendieck rigidity for finitely presented groups.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
G
be a group with the property that every finitely presented subgroup is separable, and suppose that
H
is finitely presented and residually finite. If there is a homomorphism
\phi:H\to G
that induces an isomorphism on profinite completions, then
\phi
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

First observe that
\phi
is injective. Indeed, first
G
is residually finite by hypothesis since the trivial group is finitely presented. Thus there is a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{H}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{G}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{H}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{G}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\phi}}

with the vertical homomorphisms injective and the bottom homomorphism an isomorphism, hence
\phi
is injective as claimed. Therefore
H\cong\phi(H)<G
is a finitely presented subgroup, and thus
\phi(H)
is separable in
G
by hypothesis. It then follows from
[
46
, Lem. 2.5]
that
\phi(H)=G
.
∎

Since direct products of hyperbolic orbisurface groups are virtually residually free, the following is a direct consequence of the previous results in this section.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
G
be a direct product of finitely many hyperbolic orbisurfaces and
H<G
be a finitely presented subgroup. Then
H
is separable in
G
. In particular, the inclusion
H\hookrightarrow G
induces an embedding
\widehat{H}\hookrightarrow\widehat{G}
of profinite completions for which
\widehat{H}=\widehat{G}
if and only if
H=G
.

4.B.
Virtual direct products

The previous results in this section are now used to prove profinite rigidity results for Kähler subgroups of direct products of orbisurface groups.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
G
and
H
be residually finite Kähler groups for which there is an isomorphism
\Theta:\widehat{G}\to\widehat{H}
. If

G<\Gamma\coloneq\Gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{r}

is a full subdirect product of hyperbolic orbisurface groups

,

, then there is an embedding

as a full subdirect product. Furthermore, there is an automorphism
\Xi
of
\widehat{\Gamma}
consisting of a reordering of the factors of
\widehat{\Gamma}
followed by automorphisms of the

such that
\Xi(\overline{G})=\overline{H}
, where the closures of

and
H
are taken inside
\widehat{\Gamma}
. Finally,
\overline{G}
(resp.
\overline{H}
) is isomorphic to
\widehat{G}
(resp.
\widehat{H}
).

The inclusion
\phi:G\hookrightarrow\Gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{r}
is the universal homomorphism for

by
Lemma

2.5
.
Theorem

C
then implies that, after possibly reordering factors, the universal homomorphism
\psi
for
H
has target
\Gamma
and there is a factor-preserving automorphism
\Xi
of
\widehat{\Gamma}
inducing a commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\Gamma}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\Gamma}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{H}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Xi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Theta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\phi}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\psi}}

Corollary

4.5
implies that
\widehat{\phi}
is injective with image
\overline{G}
. Thus
\widehat{\psi}=\Xi\circ\widehat{\phi}\circ\Theta^{-1}
is injective and thus so is
\psi
. Moreover, the closure of

is isomorphic to
\widehat{H}
by
Corollary

4.5
again and it coincides with
\Xi(\overline{G})
. Finally,

2.4
implies that the image of

is full subdirect, and this completes the proof.
∎

A direct consequence of
Theorem

4.6
is profinite rigidity of direct products of hyperbolic orbisurface groups among residually finite Kähler groups. Note that direct products of hyperbolic orbisurface groups are indeed Kähler groups. As already mentioned in the introduction, this follows for instance from a trick due to Kollár
[
1
, p. 7]
.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
G=\Gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{r}
be a direct product of hyperbolic orbisurface groups and
H
be a residually finite Kähler group such that there is an isomorphism
\Theta:\widehat{G}\to\widehat{H}
. Then
H\cong\Gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{r}
.

Proof.

According to
Theorem

4.6
, there is an embedding
\psi:H\hookrightarrow\Gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{r}
as a finitely presented full subdirect product inducing an isomorphism of profinite completions. Since Kähler groups are finitely presented,
Corollary

4.5
implies that
\psi
is an isomorphism.
∎

As discussed in the introduction, general Kähler groups are not uniquely determined by their profinite completion. This is even false for subgroups of direct products of surface groups. Indeed, Bessa, Grunewald, and Zalesskii constructed nonisomorphic finite index subgroups
P_{1},P_{2}\leq\Gamma_{1}\times\Gamma_{2}
of a direct product of two hyperbolic orbisurface groups with
\widehat{P}_{1}\cong\widehat{P}_{2}

[
4
, Ex. 3.14]
. Since these groups are finite index in a Kähler group, they are Kähler. However, their examples do have finite
\mathcal{K}
-profinite genus.

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
\Gamma=\Gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{r}
be a direct product of hyperbolic orbisurface groups. If
G
is a finite index subgroup of
\Gamma
, then
{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{K}}(G)
is finite.

Proof.

Suppose
H\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{K}}(G)
. Since
G<\Gamma
has finite index, by projecting onto a smaller direct product and replacing the
\Gamma_{i}
by suitable finite index subgroups if necessary, we can assume that
G
is a full subdirect product.
Theorem

4.6
provides an embedding
H\hookrightarrow\Gamma
and an automorphism
\Xi
of
\widehat{\Gamma}
such that
\overline{H}=\Xi(\overline{G})
. Both
\widehat{G}\cong\overline{G}
and
\widehat{H}\cong\overline{H}
then have finite index in
\widehat{\Gamma}
, and the natural bijection between finite index subgroups of a group and those of its profinite completion gives

|\Gamma:G|=|\widehat{\Gamma}:\widehat{G}|=|\widehat{\Gamma}:\widehat{H}|=|\Gamma:H|,

where the last equality uses the fact that
H
is separable in
\Gamma
by
Corollary

4.5
. Since
\Gamma
is finitely generated, it has only finitely many subgroups of index
|\Gamma:G|
, and therefore there are only finitely many possibilities for
H
.
∎

4.C.
Co-
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
subgroups

We now address the
\mathcal{K}
-profinite genus of Kähler subdirect products of hyperbolic orbisurface groups that are virtually co-
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. First of all, we will need the following consequence of
Proposition

2.3
.

Proposition 4.9
.

Suppose
G,H
are finitely presented full subdirect products of a direct product
\Gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{r}
of hyperbolic orbisurface groups such that there are surjective homomorphisms
f_{i}:\Gamma_{i}\to\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
g_{i}:\Gamma_{i}\to\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with
\ker\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}f_{i}\right)=G
and
\ker\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}g_{i}\right)=H
. Then there is an isomorphism
{\phi:G\to H}
if and only if, up to permuting factors of
\Gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{r}
, there are isomorphisms
\phi_{i}:\Gamma_{i}\to\Gamma_{i}
and
\psi:\mathbb{Z}^{n}\to\mathbb{Z}^{n}
such that
\psi\circ f_{i}=g_{i}\circ\phi_{i}
.

Proof.

If the isomorphisms
\psi
and
\phi_{i}
exist and have the property stated in the proposition, then the morphism

\prod\phi_{i}:\Gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{r}\to\Gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{r}

will isomorphically map
G
to
H
. We now deal with the “only if” direction. If
G
and
H
are isomorphic,
Proposition

2.3
says that (after possibly permuting factors)
there are isomorphisms
\phi_{i}:\Gamma_{i}\to\Gamma_{i}
so that the isomorphism

\phi\coloneq\prod_{i=1}^{r}\phi_{i}:\Gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{r}\longrightarrow\Gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{r}

carries the normal subgroup
G
to the normal subgroup
H
. Therefore
\phi
induces an isomorphism
\psi:\mathbb{Z}^{n}\to\mathbb{Z}^{n}
on the quotients satisfying

\psi\circ\sum_{i=1}^{r}f_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}g_{i}\circ\phi.

This implies the relation
\psi\circ f_{i}=g_{i}\circ\phi_{i}
for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
.
∎

We now study homomorphisms from orbisurface groups to
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Notice that if
\Gamma
is a closed hyperbolic orbisurface group, then there is an epimorphism
p:\Gamma\to\Sigma=\pi_{1}(S)
to the fundamental group of the (not necessarily hyperbolic) surface
S
obtained by forgetting the orbifold structure. Moreover, any surjective morphism
f:\Gamma\to\mathbb{Z}^{2}
factors through
p
, since the kernel of
p
is normally generated by torsion. Let
\mathrm{Epi}^{d}(\Gamma)
denote the set of epimorphisms
f:\Gamma\to\mathbb{Z}^{2}
inducing a degree
d
morphism
\Sigma\to\mathbb{Z}^{2}
(i.e., so that the induced map from
S
to a torus has degree
d
). Note that the degree is considered here as a nonnegative integer.

Proposition 4.10
.

(1)

For a closed hyperbolic orbisurface group
\Gamma
and a degree
d\in\mathbb{N}
with
d\geq 2
, let
\Aut(\Gamma)
act on
\mathrm{Epi}^{d}(\Gamma)
by precomposition. Then the coset space
\mathrm{Epi}^{d}(\Gamma)/\Aut(\Gamma)
consists of a single element.

(2)

Let
\Gamma_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq r
) be closed hyperbolic orbisurface groups and
d_{1},\ldots,d_{r}
be integers greater than
1
. Fix surjections
f_{i}\in\mathrm{Epi}^{d_{i}}(\Gamma_{i})
,
1\leq i\leq r
, and consider the group

G\coloneq\ker(\sum_{i=1}^{r}f_{i})<\Gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{r}.

Then the isomorphism type of
G
depends only on the degrees
d_{i}
and not on the specific choice of the
f_{i}
.

Proof.

To prove the first item of the proposition, we consider a closed hyperbolic orbisurface group
\Gamma
and let
f,g\in\mathrm{Epi}^{d}(\Gamma)
. We consider the induced injections

f^{\ast},g^{\ast}:\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{2},\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Sigma,\mathbb{Z}).

By
[
3
, Cor. 8.2]
, there is a self-homeomorphism of the surface
S
inducing
h\in\Aut(\Sigma)
and
h^{\ast}:\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Sigma,\mathbb{Z})\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Sigma,\mathbb{Z})
so that

(4.10a)

h^{\ast}\circ f^{\ast}=g^{\ast}.

This homeomorphism of
S
can be modified by an isotopy so that it preserves the marked points of the orbifold, and hence we can lift
h
to an automorphism
\overline{h}
of
\Gamma
. Since
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is abelian,
f
and
g
are determined by
f^{\ast}
and
g^{\ast}
, respectively, so (
4.10a
) yields the equality
f\circ\overline{h}=g
. This proves that
\mathrm{Epi}^{d}(\Gamma)/\Aut(\Gamma)
consists of a single element.

The second item of the proposition follows immediately from the first.
∎

We are now ready to return to profinite rigidity results. We start with the following theorem, whose proof confines us to products of surface groups, in contrast to most of our other results that apply more generally to products of orbisurface groups.

Theorem 4.11
.

Suppose that

is a Kähler full subdirect product of hyperbolic surface groups. Suppose that there are homomorphisms

, we can find an embedding

(or equivalently

) is nontrivial.

and

are trivial for

. This implies that
G=G_{0}\times\Gamma_{s+1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{r}
and
H=H_{0}\times\Gamma_{s+1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{r}
where
G_{0}=\mathrm{ker}(\sum_{i=1}^{s}f_{i})
and
H_{0}=\mathrm{ker}(\sum_{i=1}^{s}g_{i})
are full subdirect products of
\Gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{s}
. We now want to argue that the degrees of the ramified coverings corresponding to
f_{i}
and
g_{i}
are the same for each

.

Denote by
H^{1+1}
the image of the cup product
H^{1}\times H^{1}\to H^{2}
. By (
4.11a
) and naturality of the cup product (see also
[
41
]
), for every natural number
n\geq 1
there is a commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1+1}(\mathbb{Z}^{2};\mathbb{Z}/n)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1+1}(\Gamma_{i};\mathbb{Z}/n)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1+1}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{2};\mathbb{Z}/n)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1+1}(\widehat{\Gamma}_{i};\mathbb{Z}/n)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1+1}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{2};\mathbb{Z}/n)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1+1}(\widehat{\Gamma}_{i};\mathbb{Z}/n)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1+1}(\mathbb{Z}^{2};\mathbb{Z}/n)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1+1}(\Gamma_{i};\mathbb{Z}/n)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{i}^{1+1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{f}_{i}^{1+1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{g}_{i}^{1+1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{i}^{1+1}}

Here the vertical maps are isomorphisms: the middle two maps are induced by
\psi
and
\Xi_{i}
(see (

)) and the top and bottom maps come from the goodness of the groups involved. (Goodness is recalled in

.) Now, since
H^{1+1}(\Gamma_{i};\mathbb{Z}/n)=H^{2}(\Gamma_{i};\mathbb{Z}/n)
and
H^{1+1}(\mathbb{Z}^{2};\mathbb{Z}/n)=H^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2};\mathbb{Z}/n)
, the degree of the ramified cover inducing

(resp.

) can be recovered from the values of

(resp.

) is equal to

. Indeed, given a prime

, its highest power dividing these degrees can be recovered by considering the powers of

for which
f_{i}^{1+1}
(or

) vanishes. Since the vertical maps above are isomorphisms, the maps
f_{i}^{1+1}

\widehat{f}_{i}^{1+1}
,

, and

. Therefore the degree of the ramified covers inducing

and

are equal and

2.1
implies that
{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{K}}(G)
is finite.

Finally, to establish (3) we notice that if the
f_{i}
are surjective or trivial, then combining the equality of the degrees of

and

with

, implies that

and

are isomorphic, hence so are

and

.
∎

5.A.
Central extensions and profinite groups

We need to understand profinite properties of central extensions of groups by
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
. Before stating the first relevant result, some notions related to
goodness
are required. Let
G
be a discrete group. As noted by Serre
[
61
, p. 15]
, for
j=0,1
and all finite
G
-modules
M
, the restriction map
H^{j}(\widehat{G},M)\to H^{j}(G,M)
on group cohomology induced by
G\to\widehat{G}
is an isomorphism. A group is then called
good
if this map is an isomorphism for all
j
and
n
-good
if the restriction map continues to be an isomorphism up to and including
j=n
. See
[
56
, §7]
for more on goodness.

The importance of
2
-goodness lies in the connection between
H^{2}(G,M)
and central extensions of
G
by
M
. Specifically, a group
G
is
2
-good if and only if any group extension

1\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow 1

of
G
by a finitely generated group
N
induces an injection
\widehat{N}\hookrightarrow\widehat{E}

[
56
, Prop. 7.10]
. Since the profinite completion is right exact,
2
-goodness is precisely what is needed to deduce that there is always an induced exact sequence

1\longrightarrow\widehat{N}\longrightarrow\widehat{E}\longrightarrow\widehat{G}\longrightarrow 1

of profinite completions.

The following lemma is essentially due to Wilton and Zalesskii
[
69
, Lem. 8.3]
in the context of orientable Seifert fibre spaces (see also
[
50
]
). The proof given below extracts the essence of their argument.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
G_{0}
be a residually finite group that is
2
-good, and assume that for all nontrivial
\psi\in H^{2}(G_{0},\mathbb{Z}^{k})
there exists a prime power
p^{m}
such that the image of
\psi
in
H^{2}(G_{0},(\mathbb{Z}/p^{m})^{k})
is nontrivial. If

(5.1a)

1\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{k}\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow G_{0}\longrightarrow 1

is a central extension, then (
5.1a
) splits if and only if the induced exact sequence

(5.1b)

1\longrightarrow\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{k}\longrightarrow\widehat{G}\longrightarrow\widehat{G}_{0}\longrightarrow 1

of profinite completions splits.

Proof.

As described before the statement of the lemma, exactness of the sequence of profinite completions follows from
2
-goodness of
G_{0}
. Moreover, there is a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{2}(G_{0},\mathbb{Z}^{k})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{2}(G_{0},(\mathbb{Z}/p^{m})^{k})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{2}(\widehat{G}_{0},\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{k})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{2}(\widehat{G}_{0},(\mathbb{Z}/p^{m})^{k})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}

where the horizontal maps are reduction of coefficients modulo
p^{m}
, the left vertical map is taking profinite completions, and the right vertical map is restriction. Furthermore, it is clear that (
5.1a
) splitting implies (
5.1b
) splits. For the remainder of the proof, assume that (
5.1a
) does not split, and hence its characteristic class
\psi\in H^{2}(G_{0},\mathbb{Z}^{k})
is nontrivial.

Choose a prime power
p^{m}
so that the reduction
\psi_{p^{m}}
of
\psi
modulo
p^{m}
is nontrivial. Then
2
-goodness implies that there is
\widehat{\psi}_{p^{m}}\in H^{2}(\widehat{G}_{0},(\mathbb{Z}/p^{m})^{k})
whose restriction to
G_{0}
is
\psi_{p^{m}}
. The exact sequence

1\longrightarrow(\mathbb{Z}/p^{m})^{k}\longrightarrow\widehat{G}_{p}\longrightarrow\widehat{G}_{0}\longrightarrow 1

associated with
\widehat{\psi}_{p^{m}}
then does not split, but it is also the reduction modulo
p^{m}
of (
5.1b
), and so that sequence cannot split. This proves the lemma.
∎

The content of
[
69
, Lem. 8.3]
is the case where
G_{0}
is a surface group and
k=1
. The proof of the following corollary to
Lemma

5.1
corrects a mistake in the direction of a cohomology exact sequence in
[
69
]
and generalises their result to
k\geq 1
and
G_{0}
a product of hyperbolic orbisurface groups.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
k\geq 1
be an integer and
G_{0}
be a finite product of hyperbolic orbisurface groups. Then, a central extension given by a short exact sequence

(5.2a)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}^{k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}

splits if and only if the induced short exact sequence

(5.2b)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{G}_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}

of profinite completions splits.

Proof.

It is well-known that hyperbolic orbisurface groups are good, and the direct product of finitely many good groups is good
[
36
, Prop. 3.4]
, so
G_{0}
is good. In particular,
G_{0}
is residually finite and
2
-good. It remains to check that every nontrivial class in
H^{2}(G_{0},\mathbb{Z}^{k})
has nontrivial reduction modulo some prime power
p^{m}
.

For any prime power
p^{m}
, the exact sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}^{k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}^{k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbb{Z}/p^{m})^{k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}

coming from reduction modulo
p^{m}
induces a long exact sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(G_{0},(\mathbb{Z}/p^{m})^{k})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{2}(G_{0},\mathbb{Z}^{k})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{2}(G_{0},\mathbb{Z}^{k})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{2}(G_{0},(\mathbb{Z}/p^{m})^{k})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{3}(G_{0},\mathbb{Z}^{k})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}

in cohomology. We are only interested in the segment

(5.2c)

H^{2}(G_{0},\mathbb{Z}^{k})\longrightarrow H^{2}(G_{0},\mathbb{Z}^{k})\longrightarrow H^{2}(G_{0},(\mathbb{Z}/p^{m})^{k}).

of this sequence. Note that the group
H^{2}(G_{0},\mathbb{Z}^{k})
is a finitely generated abelian group and the first arrow in (
5.2c
) is simply the multiplication by
p^{m}
.

Choose a nonzero class
\psi\in H^{2}(G_{0},\mathbb{Z}^{k})
. If
\psi
is not a torsion element then it is not divisible by
p
for sufficiently large
p
, hence it has nontrivial image in
H^{2}(G_{0},(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{k})
. Now suppose that
\psi
is torsion. Identify the torsion subgroup of
H^{2}(G_{0},\mathbb{Z}^{k})
with

(5.2d)

\prod_{t=1}^{r}\mathbb{Z}/q_{t}^{m_{t}}\mathbb{Z}

for some prime numbers
q_{t}
and integers
m_{t}
. Since
\psi
is nonzero, it has a nonzero component under the identification of the torsion subgroup of
H^{2}(G_{0},\mathbb{Z}^{k})
with the group in (
5.2d
). Without loss of generality, assume it has a nonzero component in the first factor. Then
\psi
is not divisible by
q_{1}^{m_{1}}
, hence it has nontrivial image in
H^{2}(G_{0},(\mathbb{Z}/q_{1}^{m_{1}})^{k})
. It follows that every nontrivial element of
H^{2}(G_{0},\mathbb{Z}^{k})
has nontrivial reduction modulo some prime power, and thus
Lemma

5.1
applies to give the desired conclusion.
∎

Corollary

5.2
is now used to prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
G=\mathbb{Z}^{k}\times\Gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{r}
, where each
\Gamma_{i}
is a hyperbolic orbisurface group. If
H
is a residually finite Kähler group and
\widehat{\phi}:\widehat{H}\to\widehat{G}
is an isomorphism, then
H\cong G
.

Proof.

Since
H
is residually finite, the isomorphism
\widehat{H}\cong\widehat{G}
induces an embedding
\iota_{H}:H\hookrightarrow\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{k}\times\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}\times\cdots\times\widehat{\Gamma}_{r}
.
Theorem

C
then implies that the composition of
\iota_{H}
with projection away from
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{k}
coincides (up to post-composition by automorphisms of the
\widehat{\Gamma}_{i}
) with the inclusion
H\hookrightarrow\widehat{H}
followed by
\widehat{\psi}
, where
\psi
is the universal homomorphism

\psi:H\longrightarrow\prod_{i=1}^{r}\Gamma_{i}

for
H
. In particular
\widehat{\psi}
is onto. Note that
\psi(H)
is a finitely presented subgroup of
\prod_{i=1}^{r}\Gamma_{i}
by
Proposition

2.4
, hence it is closed in the profinite topology on
\prod_{i=1}^{r}\widehat{\Gamma}_{i}
by
Corollary

4.5
. If
\psi(H)
were a proper subgroup of
\prod_{i=1}^{r}\Gamma_{i}
, then the image of
\widehat{\psi}
would be a proper subgroup of
\prod_{i=1}^{r}\widehat{\Gamma_{i}}
, which is a contradiction. It follows that
\psi
is surjective. We now consider the composition of
\iota_{H}
with the projection

p_{1}:\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{k}\times\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}\times\cdots\times\widehat{\Gamma}_{r}\longrightarrow\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{k},

which has dense image. Since
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{k}
is torsion free,
\im(p_{1}\circ\iota_{H})
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{m}
for some
m\geq k
. In particular, we obtain an embedding

(5.3a)

(p_{1}\circ\iota_{H},\psi):H\hookrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{m}\times\Gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{r}

of
H
as a subdirect product.

The proof is now completed using a simplified version of the argument for
[
31
, Thm. 5.14]
. Since the
\Gamma_{i}
have trivial centre, the centre of
H
is given by
Z(H)=\ker(\psi)=H\cap\mathbb{Z}^{m}
where
H
is identified with its image under the embedding (
5.3a
). We examine the short exact sequence

(5.3b)

1\longrightarrow Z(H)\longrightarrow H\longrightarrow\Gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{r}\longrightarrow 1,

where the surjection from
H
to
\Gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{r}
is given by
\psi
. By right-exactness of taking profinite completions and
2
-goodness of the group
\Gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{r}
, this yields the short exact sequence

(5.3c)

1\longrightarrow\widehat{Z(H)}\longrightarrow\widehat{H}\longrightarrow\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}\times\cdots\times\widehat{\Gamma}_{r}\longrightarrow 1.

Thus the image of
\widehat{Z(H)}
in
\widehat{H}
coincides with
\ker(\widehat{\psi})\cong\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{k}
. It follows that
Z(H)
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
.

Since
\widehat{H}
is isomorphic to
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{k}\times\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}\times\cdots\times\widehat{\Gamma}_{r}
in such a way that the projection
\widehat{H}\to\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}\times\cdots\times\widehat{\Gamma}_{r}
coincides with
\widehat{\psi}
up to an automorphism of the target, the sequence (
5.3c
) splits.
Corollary

5.2
then implies that (
5.3b
) splits. In other words,
H
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{k}\times\Gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{r}
.
∎

Finally, observe that
Lemma

4.4
and finite presentability of Kähler groups imply the following which, using the vocabulary from
Definition

1.3
, says that
G
is Grothendieck rigid amongst residually finite Kähler groups.

Corollary 5.4
.

Suppose that
G
is a Kähler subgroup contained in the product of finitely many hyperbolic orbisurface groups with possibly a free abelian group of finite rank. If
H\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{K}}(G)
and if there is a morphism
\phi:H\to G
that induces an isomorphism on profinite completions, then
\phi
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Consider the inclusion

G<P\coloneq\mathbb{Z}^{k}\times\Gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{r},

where the
\Gamma_{i}
are hyperbolic orbisurface groups. According to
Theorem

4.2
and
Lemma

4.3
, every finitely presented subgroup of
P
is separable in
P
. In particular every finitely presented subgroup of
G
is separable in
P
, hence separable in
G
.
Lemma

4.4
now yields the desired conclusion.
∎

. We now summarize where the various statements from that theorem are proved.

.

5.B.
The three factors case

Here we use the results from the previous sections to prove the following corollary, already stated in the introduction.

Corollary

1.4
.

Let
\Gamma_{1}
,
\Gamma_{2}
,
\Gamma_{3}
be three hyperbolic orbisurface groups and

G\leq\Gamma_{1}\times\Gamma_{2}\times\Gamma_{3}

be a Kähler group. Then
G
contains a finite index subgroup
H
that is profinitely rigid amongst residually finite Kähler groups.

Proof.

Let
G
be as in the statement of the theorem. According to
[
45
, Cor. 1.3]
, we have the following dichotomy:

(1)

either
G
has a finite index subgroup
G_{0}
that is isomorphic to a direct product

\mathbb{Z}^{2k}\times\Sigma_{g_{1}}\times\cdots\times\Sigma_{g_{s}}

where
2k+s\leq 3
and
\Sigma_{g_{i}}
is a surface group of genus
g_{i}\geq 2
;

(2)

or
G
has a finite index subgroup
G_{0}
that embeds as a normal full subdirect product of three hyperbolic surface groups
\Sigma_{g_{1}}\times\Sigma_{g_{2}}\times\Sigma_{g_{3}}
with quotient isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and such that the quotient morphism
\phi:\Sigma_{g_{1}}\times\Sigma_{g_{2}}\times\Sigma_{g_{3}}\to\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is nontrivial (in fact induced by a nonconstant holomorphic map) on each factor.

In Case (1) the assertion is an immediate consequence of
Theorem

5.3
. In Case (2), the restriction of
\phi
to factors is induced by a nonconstant holomorphic map and thus has finite index image
A_{i}\coloneq\phi(\Sigma_{g_{i}})\leq\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Let

A\coloneq\bigcap_{i=1}^{3}A_{i}\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}.

Then the subgroups
\Sigma_{i}^{\prime}\coloneq\phi^{-1}(A)\cap\Sigma_{i}\leq\Sigma_{i}
have finite index, the restriction

\phi^{\prime}:\Sigma^{\prime}_{1}\times\Sigma^{\prime}_{2}\times\Sigma^{\prime}_{3}\longrightarrow A\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}

of
\phi
to
\Sigma^{\prime}_{1}\times\Sigma^{\prime}_{2}\times\Sigma^{\prime}_{3}
is surjective on factors and
G_{0}^{\prime}\coloneq\ker(\phi^{\prime})\leq G_{0}
has finite index. The assertion then follows from
Theorem

4.11
.
∎

6.
Strong rigidity for direct products of Riemann surfaces

Theorem

5.3
says in particular that the profinite completion completely characterises direct products of hyperbolic surface groups (possibly with a free Abelian factor) among all residually finite Kähler groups. We prove here that for closed aspherical Kähler manifolds, more can be said. Namely, we establish the following theorem, which is more general than
Theorem

A
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
X
be a closed aspherical Kähler manifold with residually finite fundamental group. Let
S_{1},\ldots,S_{r}
be closed oriented surfaces of genus greater than
1
and let
k
be a natural number.

(1)

If the profinite completion of
\pi_{1}(X)
is isomorphic to that of

\pi_{1}(S_{1})\times\cdots\times\pi_{1}(S_{r})\times\mathbb{Z}^{2k},

then for each
j\in{1,\ldots,r}
, there exists a closed Riemann surface
F_{j}
homeomorphic to
S_{j}
such that
X
is biholomorphic to a principal torus bundle over
F_{1}\times\cdots\times F_{r}
with fiber of complex dimension
k
.

(2)

If moreover
X
is projective, it has a finite covering space
X_{0}
biholomorphic to a direct product of Riemann surfaces with an Abelian variety.

As we shall see, the proof of this result relies on a classical argument going back to Siu’s work
[
62
]
, as well as on some arguments taken from the work of Delzant and Py
[
19
]
. Note that when
k=0
, the first item above says that
X
is biholomorphic to the direct product of the
F_{j}
, and we recover the statement of
Theorem

A
.

Before embarking into the proof, we remind the reader of the definition of the
Albanese map
for a closed Kähler manifold. If
X
is closed Kähler, one can associate a complex torus
A(X)
of complex dimension
\frac{b_{1}(X)}{2}
to it, along with a holomorphic map
a_{X}:X\to A(X)
that is well-defined up to translation and such that the induced map

(a_{X})_{\ast}:H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})/\mathrm{Torsion}\to H_{1}(A(X),\mathbb{Z})

is an isomorphism. We refer the reader to
[
68
, §12.1.3]
for an exposition of this construction. When
X
is a Riemann surface,
A(X)
is nothing but the Jacobian of
X
.

Proof.

Let
X
be as in the statement of
Theorem

6.1
. According to
Theorem

5.3
, there is an isomorphism

\psi:\pi_{1}(X)\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{2k}\times\pi_{1}(S_{1})\times\cdots\times\pi_{1}(S_{r})

and by a result by Catanese
[
13
, Thm. 5.14]
, there exist closed Riemann surfaces
F_{1},\ldots,F_{r}
respectively homeomorphic to
S_{1},\ldots,S_{r}
such that the surjections
\pi_{1}(X)\to\pi_{1}(S_{i})
defined by
\psi
are induced by some holomorphic fibrations
p_{i}:X\to F_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq r
).

We now argue as in
[
19
, §4.2]
. Let
a:X\to A
be the Albanese map of
X
and
\alpha_{i}:F_{i}\to A_{i}
be the Albanese map of
F_{i}
. Note that
\alpha_{i}
is an embedding. By the universal property of
\alpha
, there exists a holomorphic map
\varphi_{i}:A\to A_{i}
making the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{i}}

commutative for each
i
. Denote by
\varphi:A\to A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{r}
the product of the maps
\varphi_{i}
and by
\alpha:F_{1}\times\cdots\times F_{r}\to A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{r}
the product of the maps
\alpha_{i}
. We can and do assume that
\varphi
is a homomorphism.

Consider the submanifold

Y\coloneq\{y\in A\mid\varphi(y)\in{\rm Im}(\alpha)\}

of
A
. The map
a
can be written as
a=i\circ\Phi
where
\Phi:X\to Y
is holomorphic and
i:Y\to A
is the inclusion. One proves as in
[
19
, Lem. 35]
that
\Phi
is a homotopy equivalence. Finally, exactly as in
[
19
]
, we invoke
Lemma

6.2
, stated below, to conclude that
\Phi
is a biholomorphism. This proves the first item of the theorem, noting that the projection
Y\to{\rm Im}(\alpha)
is a principal torus bundle.

To prove the second item, we invoke (again as in
[
19
]
) Poincaré’s reducibility theorem
[
17
, VI.8]
. If
X
is projective, then so is
A
(see
[
68
]
). Poincaré’s theorem then implies that there is a subtorus
B\subset A
intersecting
\ker\varphi
transversely, in the sense that for any intersection point
x\in B\cap\ker\varphi
, we have

T_{x}A=T_{x}B\oplus T_{x}(\ker\varphi).

Hence, there are finitely many such intersection points. Consider now the natural product map
j:\ker\varphi\times B\to A
taking
(x,y)\in\ker\varphi\times B
to
x+y
. The transversality hypothesis implies that it is a local diffeomorphism, hence a finite covering map, i.e., an isogeny. This implies that
B
is isogenous to the product of the
A_{i}
. Thus after replacing
B
by a finite covering space we can assume that

B=B_{1}\times\cdots\times B_{r},

where
B_{i}
is a finite covering space of
A_{i}
. We thus have a natural covering map

\pi:\ker\varphi\times B_{1}\times\cdots\times B_{r}\longrightarrow A.

The preimage of
Y
under
\pi
is a finite covering space of
Y
(hence of
X
) having the desired product structure.
∎

A proof of the following classical lemma can be found in
[
62
]
(see the proof of Theorem 8 there).

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
f:X_{1}\to X_{2}
be a holomorphic map between two closed Kähler manifolds of the same dimension
n
. Assume that
f
has degree
1
and that the induced map
f_{\ast}:H_{2n-2}(X_{1},\mathbb{R})\to H_{2n-2}(X_{2},\mathbb{R})
is injective. Then
f
is a holomorphic diffeomorphism. This holds in particular if
f
is a homotopy equivalence.

Remark 6.3
.

In (real) dimension greater or equal to
6
, one could appeal to the results of Farrell–Jones
[
24
]
to prove that a manifold

as in
Theorem

6.1
must be homeomorphic to the direct product of the
S_{i}
’s with a torus. However, our proof based on Kähler geometry yields a stronger result.

7.
Profinite invariance of the BNS invariant

The goal of this section is to prove that the BNS invariant
\Sigma^{1}(G)
of a Kähler group
G
is a profinite invariant in a suitable sense, thereby proving
Theorem

D
.

We first recall some notions related to Alexander polynomials and present the
\mathsf{TAP}_{1}(R)
property for a ring

(
Section

7.A
), which was introduced by Hughes and Kielak
[
37
]
. The definition of
\mathsf{TAP}_{1}(R)
will be recalled in
Definition

, but a more detailed introduction can be found in
[
37
]
. We then establish in

that Kähler groups have the

property for every Noetherian unique factorization domain (UFD)

. Note that the corresponding result for

-manifold groups was proven in a series of papers by Friedl and Vidussi
[
25
,
28
,
29
]
. Finally,

is established in

.

7.A.

The following definitions are taken from Friedl and Vidussi’s survey
[

but restated in the language of group homology. Throughout

,

is a unique factorisation domain (UFD), which is moreover assumed to be Noetherian. For a finitely generated

is Noetherian implies that we can find a presentation

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R^{s}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

The

The first twisted Alexander polynomial

and

Remark 7.3
.

The twisted Alexander polynomial, or rather merely its vanishing or nonvanishing, can be defined for more general rings; see
[

, §2.B]
. However, we content ourselves here with the case where

is a Noetherian UFD.

In the following, we shall say that a nonzero character
\varphi:G\to\mathbb{Z}
is fibred if its kernel is finitely generated. Although the terminology algebraically fibred is more commonly used in the literature, we use the term fibred here since there is no risk of confusion with other notions. Being fibred is equivalent to the fact that

is contained in the intersection

\Sigma^{1}(G)\cap-\Sigma^{1}(G).

See
[
5
]
or
[
54
, Ch. 11]
. Here, and as in the introduction, the BNS invariant

is viewed as a subset of

. Following
[

]
, we say that

. When

, the two notions are equivalent. When

is semi-fibred, the Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{1,R}^{\phi,\alpha}(t)
is nonzero for any surjective morphism to a finite group

; see
[

, Lem. 4.1]
, or
[

, Prop. 2.13]
. Property

deals with the converse of this implication.

Definition 7.4
.

A finitely generated group

is in the class
\mathsf{TAP}_{1}(R)
if, for every nontrivial character

to a finite group

is nonvanishing.

Applications of this notion to some profinite rigidity problems are given in
[
37
]
. Here we use it as a key step to establish

D
.

7.B.
Large characters and the TAP property

We continue to consider a finitely generated group
G
. We shall say that a character
\varphi:G\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is

if there exists a finite-index subgroup
H
of
G
, and a free noncyclic quotient
\psi:H\twoheadrightarrow F
, such that
\varphi|_{H}
factors through
\psi
. Note that if
\varphi
is large, the group
H
can be taken to be characteristic.

As in
Section

7.A
, in what follows
R
is a Noetherian UFD.

Proposition 7.5
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated group and let
\varphi\in H^{1}(G;\mathbb{Z})
be nontrivial and primitive. If
\varphi
is large, then there exists a finite quotient
\alpha:G\twoheadrightarrow Q
such that
\Delta_{1,R}^{\varphi,\alpha}(t)=0
.

Proof.

Since
\varphi
is large, there exists a finite index characteristic subgroup
H
of
G
such that
\varphi|_{H}
factors through a surjection
H\twoheadrightarrow F_{k}
. Let
n
be the divisibility of the character
\varphi|_{H}
, i.e., the index of the group
\varphi(H)
in
\mathbb{Z}
. Applying a suitable automorphism of
F_{k}
and then projecting onto a smaller free quotient, we can assume that
k=2
and that
\varphi|_{H}
equals the composition of the surjection
H\twoheadrightarrow F_{2}=F(a,b)
with the morphism
F(a,b)\to\mathbb{Z}
sending
a
to
n
and
b
to
0
. Let
Q=G/H
and let
\alpha
denote the quotient map
G\to Q
. By
[
37
, Lem. 2.11]
or
[
26
, Lem. 3.3]
we have

(7.5a)

\Delta^{\varphi|_{H}}_{1,R}(t)=\Delta^{\varphi,\alpha}_{1,R}(t).

(As explained in
Definition

7.2
, the left-hand side stands for the untwisted Alexander polynomial.) By
[
37
, Lem. 2.9]
, it suffices to prove that
H_{1}(H;R[t^{\pm 1}])
is not a torsion
R[t^{\pm 1}]
-module. Indeed, this implies that the element in (

) is zero and concludes the proof of the proposition.

By Shapiro’s Lemma there is an isomorphism

H_{1}(H;R[t^{\pm 1}])\cong H_{1}(\ker\varphi|_{H};R)

of
R[t^{\pm 1}]
-modules. We denote by
F_{\infty}
the kernel of the homomorphism
F(a,b)\to\mathbb{Z}
defined above. There is a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ker\varphi|_{H}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{\infty}}

and

maps onto the module

. Note that the conjugates

for

form a basis of

. This yields an isomorphism

also is not a torsion module. This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 7.6
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated group. If every nontrivial primitive character of

that is not semifibred is large, then

is in
\mathsf{TAP}_{1}(R)
.

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of
Proposition

7.5
and
[

, Prop. 2.13]
, which says that the first twisted Alexander polynomials of semi-fibred characters do not vanish.
∎

To prove that Kähler groups are
\mathsf{TAP}_{1}(R)
, we will use a characterisation of large characters for Kähler groups in terms of factorisations through hyperbolic orbisurface groups. The following result, which is well-known, follows from the work of Napier–Ramachandran
[
51
]
or from Delzant’s description of the BNS-invariant for Kähler groups
[
21
]
, that was recalled in the introduction.

Theorem 7.7
.

Let
G
be a Kähler group and let
\chi\in H^{1}(G;\mathbb{Z})
be an integral character. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\chi
is large;

(2)

\chi
is not fibred;

(3)

\chi
is not semi-fibred;

(4)

\chi
factors through one of the components of the universal homomorphism of
G
.

Proof.

We have seen in the introduction that
\Sigma^{1}(G)=-\Sigma^{1}(G)
. This implies that the second and third points are equivalent. The first point implies the second since nontrivial infinite index normal subgroups of nonabelian finitely generated free groups are not finitely generated. The third point implies the fourth by
[
51
]
or
[
21
, Thm. 1.1]
. Finally, it is a classical fact that any homomorphism from a hyperbolic surface group to
\mathbb{Z}
factors through a surjection onto a nonabelian free group. Since hyperbolic orbisurface groups are virtually hyperbolic surface groups, this proves that the fourth point implies the first.
∎

Combining
Corollary

7.6
and
Theorem

, we obtain:

Proposition 7.8
.

If
G
is a Kähler group, then
G
is in
\mathsf{TAP}_{1}(R)
.

7.C.
Proving
Theorem

We start by recalling the statement we want to prove:

Theorem D
.

Let
G
and
H
be Kähler groups such that
\widehat{G}\cong\widehat{H}
. Then there is a linear isomorphism
L:H^{1}(G,\mathbb{Q})\to H^{1}(H,\mathbb{Q})
such that

L_{\mathbb{R}}(\Sigma^{1}(G))=\Sigma^{1}(H).

In the above statement, recall that
L_{\mathbb{R}}
stands for the extension of

to a map
H^{1}(G,\mathbb{R})\to H^{1}(H,\mathbb{R})
.

Before proving
Theorem

, we need to recall several notions introduced by Liu
[

]
. Let
A
and
B
be a pair of finitely generated free abelian groups and
\Phi:\widehat{A}\to\widehat{B}
be a continuous homomorphism between their profinite completions. Any basis of

as a

-module can be considered as a basis of

as a
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-module, and similarly for

. Therefore, if we fix two bases

and

of

and

, respectively, the morphism
\Phi
can be represented by a matrix

of size

, with coefficients in
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
. The following definition is due to Liu
[
42
, §3]
.

Definition 7.9
.

The

\mathrm{MC}(\Phi)
of

is the
\mathbb{Z}
-submodule of
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
spanned by the coefficients of

.

One readily checks that this module does not depend on the choices of the bases

and

. Alternatively, one can say that
\mathrm{MC}(\Phi)
is the smallest
\mathbb{Z}
-submodule

such that
\Phi(A)\subset B\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}L
. By definition,
\mathrm{MC}(\Phi)
is finitely generated. It is moreover free, since

is torsion free.

We now consider the homomorphism

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{MC}(\Phi)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}}

obtained by composing the inclusion of
\mathrm{MC}(\Phi)
in
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
with the canonical morphism
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. Since
\mathrm{MC}(\Phi)
is free, we can choose a lift
{\varepsilon:\mathrm{MC}(\Phi)\to\mathbb{Z}}
of this morphism. Once
\varepsilon
is fixed, we define the
\varepsilon

as the homomorphism
\Phi_{\varepsilon}:A\to B
obtained by composing the two morphisms in the following diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{MC}(\Phi)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}=B.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1\otimes\varepsilon}

We also consider the dual

depend on

and

have finite cokernel; see

.

Proof of
Theorem

D
.

Let
G
and
H
be Kähler groups as in the statement of the theorem and let
A\coloneq H_{1}(G,\mathbb{Z})/{\rm Torsion}
and
B\coloneq H_{1}(H,\mathbb{Z})/{\rm Torsion}
. Fix an isomorphism
\Psi:\widehat{G}\to\widehat{H}
between the profinite completions of

and
H
. Then
\Psi
induces an isomorphism

\widehat{H_{1}(G,\mathbb{Z})}\longrightarrow\widehat{H_{1}(H,\mathbb{Z})}

that in turn induces an isomorphism

\Phi:\widehat{A}\longrightarrow\widehat{B}.

We can then apply the previous discussion to
\Phi
and its coefficient module
{\mathrm{MC}(\Phi)\subset\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}}
. Fix a morphism
\varepsilon:\mathrm{MC}(\Phi)\to\mathbb{Z}
as above and consider the dual
\varepsilon
-specialization
\Phi^{\varepsilon}:H^{1}(H,\mathbb{Z})\to H^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z})
. Since
\Phi^{\varepsilon}
has finite cokernel, we obtain an isomorphism
\Phi^{\varepsilon}_{\mathbb{R}}:H^{1}(H;\mathbb{R})\to H^{1}(G;\mathbb{R})
, induced by
\Phi^{\varepsilon}
.

Before continuing the proof we recall that if
(\varrho_{1},\ldots,\varrho_{r}):G\to\Gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{r}
is the universal homomorphism of

, and if

V_{i}={\rm Im}(\varrho_{i}^{\ast}:H^{1}(\Gamma_{i},\mathbb{R})\to H^{1}(G,\mathbb{R}))

for

, then the BNS invariant of

is the complement of the union

(7.9a)

\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}V_{i}\subset H^{1}(G,\mathbb{R}).

Similarly the BNS invariant of
H
is the complement of a finite union

(7.9b)

\bigcup_{j=1}^{r}W_{j}\subset H^{1}(H,\mathbb{R}),

where the

are obtained using the universal homomorphism of

. The number of subspaces in (
7.9a
) and (

) are equal thanks to
Theorem

C
. We note that the
V_{i}
and the

are defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. We will use the following lemma:

Lemma 7.10
.

Given
\varphi\in H^{1}(H,\mathbb{Z})
, if
\Phi^{\varepsilon}(\varphi)
is fibred then
\varphi
is fibred.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of
[
37
, Thm. 4.12]
with

.
∎

We now conclude the proof of
Theorem

D
using the lemma. We want to prove that

(7.10a)

\Phi^{\varepsilon}_{\mathbb{R}}(\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}W_{i})=\bigcup_{j=1}^{r}V_{j}.

We will first prove that this equality holds for rational points. So let

be a rational point. According to

7.7
and

,
\Phi^{\varepsilon}(a)
belongs to the union of the

. Since the

are rationally defined, this implies that

\Phi^{\varepsilon}_{\mathbb{R}}(\bigcup_{j=1}^{r}W_{j})\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}V_{i}.

Note that each subspace
V_{i}
(or

) has (positive) even real dimension, hence

is connected. Since the
V_{i}
have pairwise trivial intersection, the connected components of

are the

. Since
\Phi^{\varepsilon}_{\mathbb{R}}(W_{j}-\{0\})
is connected it must be contained in one of the

. Note that

C
implies that the set of dimensions of the

coincides with the set of dimension of the

. Up to permutation, we assume that

{\rm dim}(W_{1})=\underset{1\leq j\leq r}{{\rm max}}\;{\rm dim}(W_{j})=\underset{1\leq j\leq r}{{\rm max}}\;{\rm dim}(V_{j}).

If

is such that
\Phi^{\varepsilon}_{\mathbb{R}}(W_{1}-\{0\})\subset V_{i}
, we then necessarily have
\Phi^{\varepsilon}_{\mathbb{R}}(W_{1})=V_{i}
. Up to reordering the

we assume

. We thus have

and

\Phi^{\varepsilon}_{\mathbb{R}}(\bigcup_{j=2}^{r}W_{j})\subset\bigcup_{i=2}^{r}V_{i}.

Now, by choosing a subspace

of maximal dimension among

, we prove as above that
\Phi^{\varepsilon}_{\mathbb{R}}(W_{j_{0}})=V_{i}
for some index

. Repeating this argument we see that the inclusion (

) is promoted to equality in (

) as desired.
∎

8.
Kodaira fibrations

Some important and well-studied Kähler groups not covered by the previous results in this paper are fundamental groups of Kodaira fibrations. Recall that a
Kodaira fibration

X
is a complex surface endowed with a holomorphic submersion onto a Riemann surface that has connected fibers and is not isotrivial. It is known that the genus of both the base and the fiber must then be greater than
1
. See
[
14
]
for an introduction to these complex surfaces. The fundamental group of a Kodaira fibration
X
is called a
Kodaira fibration group
. If
b
and
f
are respectively the genus of the base and fiber of the fibration, we say that
X
and
\pi_{1}(X)
have type
(f,b)
. Note that

may fibre over a surface in more than one way, and so it may have multiple types.

The goal of this section is to raise the following structural question about normal subgroups of Kähler groups and explain its relation to the profinite rigidity problem for fundamental groups of Kodaira fibrations.

Question 8.1
.

Let
G
be a Kähler group and

[
34
]
or
[
54
, Th. 7.2]
). One could also consider the more restricted problem where

is the kernel of the homomorphism to a hyperbolic orbisurface group induced by a fibration. A related question appears in
[
52
, p. 3000]
: it is unknown if a finitely generated nonabelian free group can appear as a normal subgroup of a Kähler group. The final result of this paper proves that a positive answer to
Question

8.1
yields a weak form of profinite rigidity for Kodaira fibration groups.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1,}

is commensurable with a surface group, hence itself isomorphic to a surface group by the Nielsen realization problem.

Note that
N
is torsion free, since it is a subgroup of the torsion free group
\widehat{\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{f})}
(see
[
56

for the latter point). It follows that
H
is an extension

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{b})}

where
\Sigma_{g}
is a closed oriented surface of genus
g
. Then
[

, Lem. 7.2]
implies that
H
is good, which in turn yields that the profinite completion of

N\cong\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})

\widehat{\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{f})}
. This implies that
g=f
and proves the first point of the proposition. To prove the second point of the proposition, if
X

, then

∎

Remark 8.3
.

Proposition

8.2
suggests the following problem: is it true that a Kähler group that is also a surface-by-surface group can be realized as a Kodaira fibration group? Although this might be true, we did not try to establish this fact. See
[
67
]
for a related discussion.

Despite the conditional result above, it is not clear whether or not one should expect fundamental groups of Kodaira fibrations to be profinitely rigid amongst residually finite Kähler groups. A result of González-Diez
[
33
]
shows that for each extension of a nontrivial element
\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
not conjugate to complex conjugation to an element of
\Aut(\mathbb{C})
, there exists a Kodaira fibration
X
with a Galois conjugate
X^{\sigma}
such that their universal covers are not isomorphic. It would be interesting to understand the fundamental groups of these examples in terms of whether or not Kodaira fibration groups can have arbitrarily large
{\mathcal{K}}
-profinite genus.

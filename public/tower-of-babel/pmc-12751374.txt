Improving the blast, bacterial blight, and brown planthopper resistance for a restorer line of WDR Hanhui3 by marker-assisted gene pyramiding
Authors: Jijin Chen, Juhao Ruan, Zhening Miao, Deyan Kong, Anning Zhang, Feiming Wang, Xinqiao Yu, Lijun Luo, Guolan Liu, Yi Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe widespread cultivation of water-saving and drought-resistance rice (WDR) represents a crucial strategy for sustainable agriculture in water-limited environments. Hanyou73, an elite WDR hybrid derived from the restorer line Hanhui3, exhibits outstanding drought resistance and stable yield performance. However, its susceptibility to blast, bacterial blight (BB), and brown planthopper (BPH) constrains its long-term cultivation stability.ResultsThree major resistance genes Pi9 (blast), Xa23 (BB) and Bph6 (BPH) were successfully pyramided into Hanhui3 via marker-assisted backcross breeding. The resulting improved line, Hanhui303, retained 96.68% of the recurrent parent genome (RPG). Furthermore, Hanhui303 and Hanhui3 were indistinguishable using 48 standard SSR markers for variety identification. Hanhui303 and its hybrid rice conferred resistance to both blast, BB and BPH. The major important agronomic traits and grain quality of Hanhui303 and its hybrid rice obtained in field trials were similar to those of Hanhui3 and the control hybrid rice.ConclusionsWe developed a novel restorer line, Hanhui303, with significantly enhanced resistance to three major biotic stresses while maintaining the elite agronomic and drought resistance traits of Hanhui3. Due to its high genetic similarity and identical fingerprint profile, Hanhui303 can directly replace Hanhui3 in hybrid seed production. This study provides a practical and efficient strategy for improving multiple resistances in elite parental lines, thereby promoting the sustainable cultivation of WDR hybrids.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-025-07843-6.Keywords: Water-saving and Drought-resistance Rice (WDR), Pi9, Xa23, Bph6, Marker-assisted selection (MAS)

Background

Rice (Oryza sativa L.) is a staple food crop feeding more than half of the world’s population. Climate change increasingly threatens global agriculture by intensifying extreme weather events such as droughts and floods, thereby undermining rice production and aggravating the prevalence of diseases and insect pests [1, 2].

Rice blast, caused by Magnaporthe oryzae is a devastating fungal disease responsible for substantial yield losses worldwide [3]. Currently, 146 resistance genes of the blast have been identified; among them, 41 of these genes have been cloned and functionally authenticated [4]. Blast R gene Pi9 located on the short arm of chromosome 6, belongs to a cluster of allelic genes including Pi2, Pi50, Pigm, Piz, and Piz-t that confer broad-spectrum resistance [5]. The novel allele Pizh of Pigm/Pi9/Pi2 locus was identified and introduced into the cultivar Kongyu131. The resulting introgression line demonstrated high resistance to diverse rice blast isolates [4]. Jiang et al. developed four newly thermo-sensitive genic male sterile lines of rice for blast resistance using the Pi2 gene [6].

BB, caused by Xanthomonas oryzae pv. oryzae, is another major bacterial disease threatening rice production [7]. So far, more than 46 genes are known to confer resistance against BB and 11 of these genes have been cloned. Among them, Xa21 and Xa23 on chromosome 11 exhibited the broadest resistance spectrum and the strongest resistant to bacterial blight [8]. Xa23, derived from a wild rice species, is a single dominant resistance gene effective throughout all growth stages [9].

BPH, caused by Nilaparvata lugens Stål, ranks among the most destructive rice pests in Asia, causing severe annual yield reductions [10, 11]. BPH also causes indirect damage by transmitting viral diseases such as grassy stunt virus and ragged stunt virus [12]. To date, numerous insect-resistant germplasm resources have been identified from cultivated and wild rice, leading to the mapping of 80 BPH resistance genes or quantitative trait loci (QTLs), of which 17 have been cloned [13, 14]. Bph6 was positioned between molecular markers H and Y9 on the long arm of chromosome 4, encoding an extracellular vesicle-localized lectin receptor-like kinase. Notably, Bph6 provided effective pest resistance without compromising yield, demonstrating high potential for application in rice breeding programs [15].

Drought has emerged as an important limiting factor for agricultural production in recent years. Therefore, cultivating water-saving and drought-resistance rice (WDR) is one of the critical strategies to addressing water shortage issues and increasing the yield of low- and medium-yield fields in China. WDR is a new type of rice variety that has an equally as good quality and yield potential as current paddy rice, yet it also has water saving ability and drought resistance [16]. The hybrid WDR variety Hanyou73, derived from the cross between Huhan7A and Hanhui3, has been successfully commercialized due to its exceptional drought resistance and stable yield. It has been officially released in numerous Chinese provinces and several countries across Asia and Africa, highlighting its broad adaptation and considerable promise for large-scale cultivation. Enhancing disease and pest resistance in WDR hybrid parents can promote WDR cultivation in more suitable regions. Generally, restorer lines are more amenable to genetic improvement than sterile lines, as fertility restoration does not complicate the breeding process.

In this study, we pyramided of the Pi9, Xa23, and Bph6 genes into Hanhui3 through recurrent backcrossing and maker-assisted selection. Our objective was to develop a new restorer line mainly in Hanhui3 genetic background for hybrid WDR production with resistance to rice blast, bacterial blight and brown planthopper.

Methods

Plant materials

75-1-127, an introgression rice line in IR31917 genetic background, carrying Pi9 gene, was used as the donor parent for blast resistance [17]. CBB23, a near-isogenic line was derived from a cross between an O. rufipogon accession (RBB16) and a susceptible indica rice variety (JG30), carrying Xa23 gene, was used as the donor parent for BB resistance [18]. Luoyang-6, a chromosome segment substitution line in 9311 background, carrying Bph6 gene, was used as the donor parent for BPH resistance [19]. Huhan7A, a CMS line, was used as the maternal parents to produce the hybrid rice. Hanhui3, a restorer line, was used as the recurrent parent. Hanyou73 was used as a control for the evaluation of the agronomic performance of the hybrid rice. All of these cultivars were provided by the Shanghai Agrobiological Gene Center (SAGC), Shanghai, China.

DNA extraction, markers, and PCR amplification

Genomic DNA was extracted from fresh young leaves at the peak tillering stage using the Cetyltrimethylammonium Bromide (CTAB) method [20]. Three linked markers, 9-Pro, M-Xa23 and RM5757, were used to foreground selection of the Pi9, Xa23, and Bph6 genes, respectively (Supplementary Table S1). A core set of 48 SSR markers which originated protocol for identification of rice varieties (NY/T 1433–2014) were used for genetic background selection (Supplementary Table S2). The PCR was performed in a 20-µL reaction volume, containing 2 uL of genomic DNA (10 ng/uL), 10 uL of Taq PCR master mix (Tiangen BioTech (Beijing) Co., Ltd.), 0.5 uL of each primer (10 μm), and 7 µl of dd H2O. The PCR procedure involved: pre-denaturation at 95 °C for 5 min, then 30 cycles at 95 °C for 30 s, 55 °C for 30 s, and 72 °C for 1 min, and final extension at 72 °C for 5 min. Amplified products were separated on a 6% nondenaturing polyacrylamide gel and visualized with silver staining [21].

In addition, the whole-genome SNP array RICE56K was used to analyze the genetic background recovery rate. The RICE56K chip contains 56,897 SNP markers selected from the dataset of whole-genome resequencing of 3,024 rice core germplasm from 89 countries or regions around the world [22]. We screened 37,152 high-quality SNPs distributed on the 12 chromosomes for background analysis. Total DNA was extracted from the leaves from 10 plants for improved line and the recurrent parent. The analysis was conducted at Huazhi Rice Bio-tech Co., LTD (Changsha, China).

Breeding process

Marker-assisted backcross breeding was employed to transfer Pi9, Xa23, and Bph6 genes into the genetic background of Hanhui3 (Fig. 1A). In the summer of 2014, Hanhui3 was crossed with 75-1-127 and CBB23, respectively. Two true F1 plants, which were confirmed by molecular markers were crossed with each other to obtain F1 plants carrying Pi9 and Xa23 genes. These plants were used as the female parent for further crosses with Luoyang-6 in 2015 in Shanghai. Twenty F1 individuals with heterozygous Pi9, Xa23, and Bph6 genes were found by PCR analysis, and one plant with similar morphological phenotypes to that of Hanhui3 was selected for backcrossing with the recurrent parent in the spring of 2016 in Hainan. The resulting BC1F1 plants were checked for foreground and background selection using gene linkage markers and 48 fingerprint SSR markers, respectively. Then, one positive plant with the minimal fingerprint differences of Hanhui3 were used to generate BC2F1 seeds. The genetic background of BC2F1 plants were surveyed using SSR markers with polymorphic differences in the previous generation. One selected plant carrying heterozygous Pi9, Xa23, and Bph6 genes and with the minimal fingerprint differences to Hanhui3 was self-pollinated to produce BC2F2 seeds. A total of 960 BC2F2 plants, after genotyping, fifteen plants containing homozygous Pi9, Xa23, and Bph6 genes and one plant with similar morphological phenotypes and fingerprint to Hanhui3 was selected for multi-generation self-crossing.

Evaluation of rice blast resistance

The natural disease nursery of rice blast was selected at Jinzhai identification nursery in Anhui Province. The test materials were planted individually in a three-row plot with ten plants per row with a spacing of 15 × 15 cm. Twenty-five days after heading, the panicle blast of 100 panicles in each plot was investigated. Five mixed isolates of M. oryzae, ZA1, ZB1, ZB5, ZB13, and ZB15, were used for artificial inoculation at stage Ⅶ of young panicle differentiation in Shanghai. The isolates were cultured in oat medium (oatmeal 30 g/L, tomato juice 150 mL/L, agar powder 20 g/L, pH 6.5) for 3–5 days at 28 °C in the dark. Thereafter, they were cultured under constant light at 26 °C for 5–6 days to form conidia. The mature conidia were washed with sterile water to prepare an inoculum with a concentration of about 1 × 105 cfu/mL. Each test variety was injected with 20 panicles, and each panicle was injected with 1 ml of mixed isolates solution. After 21 days of inoculation, the loss rate of single panicle was investigated. The investigation and comprehensive evaluation of the rice blast were carried out according to the technical specification of the regional test for the identification and evaluation of blast resistance in rice varieties (NY/T 2646 − 2014).

Evaluation of BB resistance

BB inoculation was carried out according to the leaf-clipping method in Shanghai. At the maximum tillering stage, the leaves of five plants from each plot were inoculated with X. oryzae pv. oryzae strain PXO99, with a bacterial suspension of approximately 3 × 108 cfu/mL. Fourteen days after inoculation, lesion lengths were measured in the 25 inoculated leaves (five leaves from five plants per plot). Resistance to BB was expressed by the lesion length measurements (resistant ≤ 3 cm; moderately resistant = 3.1 ~ 5 cm; moderately susceptible = 5.1 ~ 12 cm; susceptible = 12.1 ~ 20 cm;) used by the standard evaluation system [23].

Evaluation of BPH resistance

A seedling bulk test was conducted to study plant reaction to BPH feeding according to the standard evaluation systems of IRRI with minor modifications [23]. To ensure all seedlings were at the same growth stage for BPH infestation, seeds were pre-germinated. About 30 seeds from individual plant were sown in a 10 cm-diameter plastic pot with a hole at the bottom. Seven days after sowing, seedlings were thinned to 25 plants per pot. At the second-leaf stage, the seedlings were infested with 2nd to 3rd instar BPH nymphs at 10 insects per seedling. When all the susceptible plants were dead, the seedling mortality of other cultivars or lines was recorded. Three replicates were used for each cultivar or line.

Evaluation of drought resistance

The experiment was conducted in the summer of 2023 in shanghai. Two treatments, including drought and control, were set for drought resistance evaluation. The test materials were replicated three times; nine rows were planted in each plot, with nine plants in each row. The row spacing was 20 × 23 cm. Intermittent irrigation was used to keep the field moist from seeding to stage II of panicle differentiation, without leaving any water layer. The panicles were subjected to drought stress at stage II of differentiation. The stress was halted when the leaf death rate reached 50% in the morning. Thereafter, the field water management was restored. The control treatment was set up in the adjacent paddy field, and the whole growth period was as per conventional paddy field cultivation and management. The yield of plots was measured at the maturity stage. The drought resistance index was calculated according to Technical Specification of Identification and Evaluation for Rice Drought Resistance (NY/T 2863–2015).

Evaluation of agronomic and rice grain quality traits

The field trials were conducted in the summer of 2023 in Shanghai. In each field trial, paired parental lines and hybrid rice were arranged near to each other in the plots at the size of at least 13.4 m2 in a randomized complete block design with three replications. The major important agronomic traits of rice, including duration from sowing to heading, plant height, No. of panicles per plant, panicle length, No. of Spikelets per panicle, seed setting rate, 1000-grain weight and yield per plant, were obtained from 10 plants grown in each plot and a total of 30 plants in 3 plots were measured for each variety.

The grain quality was evaluated with the rice grains harvested from the field trials mentioned above. The taste value of cooked rice was measured using a rice taste meter (STA-1 A, Satake, Japan). The appearance quality traits, including chalky grain rate, chalkiness degree and transparency were measured with a ScanMaker grain appearance analyzer (SC-E, WS, China).

Results

Development and genetic background analysis of Hanhui303

Figure 1 illustrates the crossing scheme used to pyramid Pi9, Xa23, and Bph6 into Hanhui3. The overall breeding scheme consisted of a recurrent backcrossing procedure, including four crossings, two generations of backcrossing, and five generations of self-fertilization. At each generation, the genotypes at the Pi9, Xa23 and Bph6 loci in the cross and backcross progeny were determined by molecular markers 9-Pro, M-Xa23 and RM5757, respectively (Supplementary Table S1). The background selection using 48 fingerprint SSR markers in BC1F1, BC2F1 and BC2F2, respectively. At the BC1F1 stage, 35 out of 89 plants were detected to be positive for three resistance genes. A single positive plant, 8 out of 48 SSR fingerprint markers differences and phenotypic similarity with Hanhui3 was then backcrossed with the recurrent parent. Among 73 BC2F1 plants, 33 heterozygous individuals were found. Those 8 polymorphic SSR markers were used for background selection, one plant with two SSR markers differences and recurrent parent phenotype was selected to generate BC2F2. A total of 15 (out of 960) plants were identified as homozygous positive for Pi9, Xa23, and Bph6. These homozygous BC2F2 plants were further checked for two SSR markers (RM21 and RM471). The best plant with a fingerprint pattern and phenotype similar to that of Hanhui3 was selected for self-pollination. The BC2F3 generation was planted under natural drought conditions in Hainan, and 27 individual plants with strong drought resistance and phenotype similar to Hanhui3 were selected.

The genetic background of all BC2F4 lines were surveyed using the SNP array RICE56K. The line with the maximum recurrent parent genome recovery (96.68%) was designated as Hanhui303. A total of 1,274 differential SNP loci were identified, which were predominantly distributed on chromosomes 4, 6, and 11 (Fig. 1B), corresponding to the genomic location of the Bph6, Pi9, and Xa23 genes, respectively. A total of 48 parental polymorphous SSR markers were used to primarily evaluate the genetic difference between Hanhui303 and Hanhui3. The two lines were found to yield indistinguishable fingerprinting patterns, indicating that Hanhui303 is essentially derived from Hanhui3 and the present authenticity test recommended by NY/T 1433–2014 formulated by the Ministry of Agriculture of China is unable to distinguish them from each other (Supplementary Fig. 1).

Blast resistance of Hanhui303 and its hybrid

Naturally induced characterization was conducted in the blast epidemic area of Jinzhai, Anhui Province in 2022. The results showed that Hanhui303 and Huhan7A/Hanhui303 showed resistant to rice blast with 1.33% and 2.48% incidence of neck blast. The recurrent parent (Hanhui3) and Hanyou73 (Huhan7A/Hanhui3) were moderately susceptible to rice blast (Table 1).

Agronomic traits performance of Hanhui303 and its hybrid

To investigate whethe the other agronomic traits of the improved line and its hybrid were identical to those of the control varieties, an evaluation of its agronomic characteristics was conducted in field condition in 2023. Similar duration from sowing to heading, plant height, panicles per plant, spikelets per panicle and 1000-grain weight were recorded between Hanhui303 and Hanhui3. The yield per plant of Hanhui303 was 7.7% higher than that of Hanhui3 due to the seed setting rate increased. Except for the significant differences in spikelets per panicle and seed setting rate, the hybrid combination Huhan7A/Hanhui303 displayed generally similar agronomic phenomenon to Hanyou73 in other six traits (Table 4). In general, the field trail results demonstrated that the major important agronomic traits of Hanhui303 and its hybrid were similar to those of Hanhui3 and Hanyou73, respectively.

Discussion

The development and promotion of WDR represent a significant strategy for ensuring food security in the context of escalating water scarcity and climate variability [16]. The WDR hybrid Hanyou73 (Huhan7A/Hanhui3), derived from the restorer line Hanhui3, has been successfully commercialized due to its exceptional drought resistance and stable yield. However, its susceptibility to rice blast, BB and BPH poses a major threat to its sustainable cultivation [24]. Improving the multiple resistances of the parental line Hanhui3 is therefore imperative to prolong the commercial lifespan and expand the cultivation area of this valuable hybrid system.

Marker-assisted gene pyramiding has proven to be a highly effective strategy for enhancing resistance in rice. Previous studies have demonstrated the successful introgression of resistance genes into elite backgrounds. For instance, Zhang et al. [25] pyramided Pi2, xa5, and Xa23 into Hanhui3, resulting in a line with significantly improved blast and BB resistance and an RPG recovery of 94.2%. Similarly, Luo et al. [26] pyramided six genes (Xa4, Xa21, Xa27, Pi9, Sub1A, and badh2.1) into the restorer line Wanhui6725, conferring broad-spectrum disease resistance, submergence tolerance, and fragrance. However, these studies primarily focused on combining resistance within one or two stress categories (diseases or submergence). In contrast, our study simultaneously targeted three distinct categories of threats, fungal blast, BB and a major insect pest (BPH) by pyramiding Pi9, Xa23, and Bph6 into the WDR restorer line Hanhui3. This multi-dimensional resistance strategy provides a more comprehensive defense portfolio, which is crucial for sustainable production where combined biotic stresses are common.

A critical advancement in our breeding scheme was the integrated application of multiple precision breeding technologies. We employed marker-assisted foreground selection to ensure the precise incorporation of the target genes. This was complemented by stringent phenotypic selection in each generation for key agronomic traits and drought tolerance, preserving the essential characteristics of Hanhui3. For background selection, we utilized both the standard 48 SSR fingerprint markers recommended for cultivar identification (NY/T 1433–2014) and the high-density RICE56K SNP array. This combined approach enabled a highly efficient recovery of the RPG. The resulting improved line, Hanhui303, achieved an RPG recovery of 96.68%, which is notably high and exceeds the theoretical expectation for the BC₂F₄ generation. The distribution of differential SNPs was predominantly confined to the chromosomal regions harboring the introgressed genes (Chr. 4, 6, and 11), indicating minimal linkage drag. Crucially, Hanhui303 and Hanhui3 produced indistinguishable banding patterns with all 48 SSR markers, meaning that, according to the standard protocol for variety identification in China, Hanhui303 is considered essentially derived from Hanhui3. This high genetic identity allows Hanhui303 to serve as a direct and immediate replacement for Hanhui3 in hybrid seed production without the need for extensive re-testing or regulatory re-approval, a significant advantage for practical breeding.

Resistance evaluations unequivocally demonstrated the effectiveness of the pyramided genes. Hanhui303 and its hybrid, Huhan7A/Hanhui303, exhibited high resistance to blast (score 1) under both natural and artificial inoculation conditions, in stark contrast to the susceptible reactions of Hanhui3 and Hanyou73 (score 7). Similarly, they showed strong BB resistance with lesion lengths well below the 3 cm threshold, compared to the moderate susceptibility of the controls. For BPH, Hanhui303 displayed moderate resistance, while its hybrid combination showed a resistant phenotype. Most importantly, this significant enhancement in resistance was achieved without any penalty on agronomic performance, grain quality, or drought tolerance. Field trials confirmed that the major agronomic traits of Hanhui303 and its hybrid were statistically similar or, in some cases, slightly superior to those of their respective controls.

Despite these successes, certain limitations should be acknowledged. The stability of the pyramided resistance needs to be further validated across a wider range of geographical locations and under varying pathogen/insect population pressures. Furthermore, as agricultural challenges and market demands evolve, future breeding efforts should focus on stacking additional traits into this improved background, such as heat tolerance, herbicide resistance, or fine-tuned grain quality traits, to further enhance the resilience and marketability of the Hanyou73 hybrid system.

Conclusion

An introgression restorer line of WDR mainly in Hanhui3 genetic background, designed as Hanhui303, has been developed to contain Pi9, Xa23, and Bph6 genes through MAS and gene pyramiding. The improved line and its derived hybrid show significantly enhanced resistance to blast, BB, and BPH while fully retaining the desirable agronomic performance, grain quality, and drought resistance of the control. Its deployment is expected to expand the cultivation area of the Hanyou73, contributing to more sustainable and resilient rice production.

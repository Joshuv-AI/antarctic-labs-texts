Abstract
. Using virtual localization in Witt sheaf cohomology, we show that the generating series of quadratic Donaldson-Thomas invariants of ( ℙ 1 ) 3 ({\mathbb{P}}^{1})^{3} , valued in the Witt ring of ℝ {\mathbb{R}} , W ⁡ ( ℝ ) ≅ ℤ W({\mathbb{R}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}} , is equal to M ​ ( q 2 ) − 8 M(q^{2})^{-8} , where M ⁡ ( q ) M(q) is the MacMahon function. This confirms a modified version of the conjecture of [ 40 ] . We also show that a localized version of this conjecture holds for certain iterated blow-ups of ( ℙ 1 ) 3 ({\mathbb{P}}^{1})^{3} and other related smooth proper toric varieties.

Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth projective threefold over the complex numbers. For
n\geq 1
, the Donaldson-Thomas invariant
I_{n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
of
X
is defined to be the degree of the virtual fundamental class of the Hilbert scheme
\operatorname{Hilb}^{n}(X)
of degree
n
, dimension zero closed subschemes of
X
. These invariants were first introduced by Donaldson and Thomas in the papers
[
38
]
and
[
10
]
.

In their papers
[
28
,
29
]
, Maulik, Nekrasov, Okounkov and Pandharipande consider the case of a smooth projective toric threefold
X
over
{\mathbb{C}}
, and prove that

(I.1)

\sum_{n\geq 0}I_{n}q^{n}=M(-q)^{\deg(c_{3}(T_{X}\otimes K_{X}))},

where
T_{X}
is the tangent bundle on
X
and
K_{X}
is its canonical sheaf, and
M(q)=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{n})^{-n}
is the MacMahon function, which was first considered by MacMahon
[
27
]
and proven by Stanley
[
37
]
to be the generating series of
3
-dimensional partitions.

The proof uses the virtual localization theorem
[
14
]
of Graber and Pandharipande, together with an explicit trace computation; this calculation is highly non-trivial, and is one of the main achievements of these papers. The formula (
I.1
) was conjectured by Maulik, Nekrasov, Okounkov and Pandharipande to hold for all smooth projective threefolds, which was proven in this generality by Levine-Pandharipande
[
25
]
and by Li
[
26
]
, relying on the toric case, but using entirely different methods from those employed in
[
28
,
29
]
.

Levine has constructed a motivic analogue of virtual fundamental classes in
[
21
]
. This was extended to the equivariant setting in
[
24
]
and a virtual localization formula for this setting is proven in the same paper. Using this, one can define a motivic analogue of DT invariants for a smooth projective threefold
X
over a field
k
, taking values in the Witt ring
W(k)
of
k
. For these invariants to be defined one needs to endow the Hilbert schemes
\operatorname{Hilb}^{n}(X)
of
X
with
orientations
, and in
[
23
]
, Levine showed how to construct an orientation on each
\operatorname{Hilb}^{n}(X)
from a spin structure on
X
, that is, an isomorphism of the canonical sheaf
K_{X}
with the square of a line bundle.

We recall that
W({\mathbb{C}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
, and when working over
{\mathbb{C}}
, one merely recovers the invariants
I_{n}
modulo 2. However,
W({\mathbb{R}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
via the signature map, so one can hope to find new information when working over
{\mathbb{R}}
. Another difference is that when applying virtual localization to obtain the
W(k)
-valued invariants, one cannot use a group action by a torus
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{n}
as in the classical case, instead, one needs to use an action by a non-commutative group scheme admitting irreducible representations of even rank, such as
{\operatorname{SL}}_{2}
or the normalizer
N
of the diagonal torus
T_{1}\cong{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
in
{\operatorname{SL}}_{2}
, generated by
T_{1}
and

\sigma:=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}.

See the discussion in
[
22
, Introduction]
for details.

We will prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Let
k
be a perfect field of characteristic
\neq 2
. For
n\geq 0
, let
\tilde{I}_{n}
be the quadratic Donaldson-Thomas invariant of
({\mathbb{P}}^{1})^{3}
over
k
. Then in
W(k)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
we have

\sum_{n\geq 0}\tilde{I}_{n}q^{n}=M(q^{2})^{-8}.

Of course, if
k
has positive characteristic,
W(k)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}=0
, so our result says nothing. In characteristic zero,
W(k)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
detects exactly the signature for each embedding of
k
into a real closed field. Since
({\mathbb{P}}^{1})^{3}
is defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
,
W({\mathbb{Q}})\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}=W({\mathbb{R}})\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}={\mathbb{Q}}
, and the invariants
\tilde{I}_{n}
are compatible with change of the base field, one does not really achieve any greater generality by taking a general ground field
k
rather than just using
{\mathbb{R}}
. However, the arguments involved in the computation are valid for arbitrary perfect
k
of characteristic
\neq 2
, so we prefer to frame the statement of Theorem
1
in this generality.

The proof uses Levine’s virtual localization theorem together with an explicit trace computation following
[
28
,
29
]
.

In the paper
[
40
]
, the first three nonzero quadratic DT invariants of
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
are computed to be
\tilde{I}_{2}({\mathbb{P}}^{3})=10,\tilde{I}_{4}({\mathbb{P}}^{3})=25
and
\tilde{I}_{6}({\mathbb{P}}^{3})=-50
, and a conjecture is made about the general case.

\operatorname{Hilb}^{n}(X)
has virtual rank zero. By
[

in

\tilde{I}_{1}(X)=\tilde{I}_{3}(X)=0

\tilde{I}_{n}(X)=0
for all odd

. At present, the only cases for which we know that
\tilde{I}_{n}(X)=0
for all odd

(an immediate consequence of Theorem
1
) and
X={\mathbb{P}}^{3}
(see
[
40

).

We note that Theorem
1
does not seem to provide new insights into the case of
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
. The main reason that
\tilde{I}_{n}({\mathbb{P}}^{3})
for
n\geq 8
could not be computed in
[
40
]
was that the method used relies on the
N
-action on
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
only having isolated fixed points. It turns out, however, that the fixed points inside the Hilbert scheme in degrees
n\geq 8
have positive dimension. On
({\mathbb{P}}^{1})^{3}
, the
N
-actions which we use have only isolated fixed points on
\operatorname{Hilb}^{n}
for each
n
up to a bound
M
, with
M
tending to infinity as we vary our choice of
N
-action, and so the same method as used in
[
40
]
computes all quadratic DT invariants. Once we have this information, we use the method of the “equivariant vertex measure” employed in
[
28
,
29
]
to assemble our computations of the individual
\tilde{I}_{n}
to give the formula for the generating function in Theorem
1
.

Note also that in the conjecture we do not ask for a choice of isomorphism
K_{X}\cong L^{\otimes 2}
, only its existence. A chosen isomorphism
\rho:K_{X}\xrightarrow{\sim}L^{\otimes 2}
, i.e., a choice of a spin structure on
X

\operatorname{Hilb}^{n}(X)
for each
n
, but the choice of
\rho
will not affect the orientation on
\operatorname{Hilb}^{n}(X)
for even

. Changing

by a 2-torsion line bundle will similarly have no effect on the invariants
\tilde{I}_{n}(X)
for even
n
. As part of the conjecture is that
\tilde{I}_{n}(X)=0
for odd
n
, these choices for the spin structure on
X
should not play a role.

If

is a blow up at a set of

N

-action on
({\mathbb{P}}^{1})^{3}

generated by the torus

and the element

In this case, we are not able to identify the corresponding DT invariants in

Let

be an

\tilde{Z}^{N}(X,q)=M(q^{2})^{\widetilde{\deg}_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{N}(\text{ob}_{2}(X)))}.

Moreover,

\widetilde{\deg}_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{N}(\text{ob}_{2}(X)))=-\frac{1}{2}\deg_{\mathbb{R}}(c_{3}(\text{ob}_{1}(X)))=\frac{1}{2}\deg_{\mathbb{R}}(c_{3}(T_{X}\otimes K_{X})).

We conclude in §

Remark 4
.

The main reason we have framed Conjecture
2
over the base-field
{\mathbb{R}}
is to make sense of the expression
M(\epsilon\cdot q^{2})^{\epsilon\cdot\deg_{\mathbb{R}}(e(\text{ob}_{2}(X)))}
as an element of
W({\mathbb{R}})[[q]]
, using the signature to identify
W({\mathbb{R}})
with
{\mathbb{Z}}
. Over a general field
k
, the formal sum
\sum_{n\geq 0}\tilde{I}_{n}q^{n}
defines an element of
W(k)[[q]]
and
\epsilon\cdot\deg_{k}(e(\text{ob}_{2}(X))
is an element of
W(k)
.

Recall that a
power structure
on a commutative ring
R
is a map

(1+tR[[t]])\times R\to 1+tR[[t]];\quad(f(t),r)\mapsto f(t)^{r},

satisfying the formal properties one would expect from an expression of the form
f(t)^{r}
(see
[
15
, pg. 50, Definition]
for details). The usual power
f(t)^{n}
defines the unique power structure on
{\mathbb{Z}}
. A power structure on
W(k)
would give a meaning to the expression
M(\epsilon\cdot q^{2})^{\epsilon\cdot\deg_{k}(e(\text{ob}_{2}(X)))}
, so one could generalize the above conjecture to an arbitrary base-field
k
, given a power structure on the Witt ring. There are several constructions of power structures on the Grothendieck-Witt ring (see
[
30
]
,
[
34
]
), but we are not aware of constructions of a power structure on the Witt ring.

The methods developed in
[
21
,
23
]
do give a lifting of the Witt-valued Donaldson-Thomas invariants

discussed here to DT invariants

valued in the Grothendieck-Witt ring

. Via the rank homomorphism

, and the quotient map
\pi:{\operatorname{GW}}(k)\to W(k)
, one recovers the classical DT invariant

and our Witt ring valued invariant
\tilde{I}_{n}(X)\in W(k)
by

I_{n}(X)={\operatorname{\text{rnk}}}(\tilde{I}^{{\operatorname{GW}}}_{n}(X)),\ \tilde{I}_{n}(X)=\pi(\tilde{I}^{{\operatorname{GW}}}_{n}(X)).

As
({\operatorname{\text{rnk}}},\pi):{\operatorname{GW}}(k)\to{\mathbb{Z}}\times W(k)
is injective, one also recovers
\tilde{I}^{{\operatorname{GW}}}_{n}(X)
from
(I_{n}(X),\tilde{I}_{n}(X))
, with the added information that these two invariants agree in
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
after applying the quotient map
{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}/2
to
I_{n}(X)
and the rank map
\overline{{\operatorname{\text{rnk}}}}:W(k)\to{\mathbb{Z}}/2
to
\tilde{I}_{n}(X)
.

1.
Quadratic Donaldson-Thomas invariants

Let
k
be a perfect field of characteristic
\neq 2
, let
X
be a smooth projective threefold over
k
and let
K_{X}
be the canonical line bundle on
X
. We briefly recall from
[
40
, Section 1]
how one constructs quadratic Donaldson-Thomas invariants
\tilde{I}_{n}(X)\in W(k)
of
X
using work of Levine, see
[
21
]
,
[
24
]
and
[
23
]
. Since our quadratic Donaldson-Thomas invariants will live in the Witt ring of our base-field, we begin by recalling some basic facts on this topic.

1.1.
The Witt ring, the Witt sheaf and the Witt spectrum

For a noetherian scheme
X
, we let
W(X)
denote the Witt ring of
X
. This is the group completion of the monoid (under direct sum) of isometry classes of symmetric non-degenerate
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-bilinear forms
b:{\mathcal{V}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}{\mathcal{V}}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
on locally free coherent sheaves on
X
, modulo the classes of
metabolic forms
, where
({\mathcal{V}},b)
is metabolic if
{\mathcal{V}}
admits a short exact sequence

0\to{\mathcal{L}}\to{\mathcal{V}}\to{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}\to 0

with
{\mathcal{L}}
,
{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}
locally free, such that
{\mathcal{L}}={\mathcal{L}}^{\perp}
, where
{\mathcal{L}}^{\perp}
is the kernel of the map
{\mathcal{V}}\to\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}({\mathcal{L}},{\mathcal{O}}_{X})
induced by
b
. Here we use the definition given by Balmer
[
4
, Definition 1.1.27]
for the exact category of locally free coherent sheaves on
X
, with duality
{\mathcal{V}}^{\vee}:=\mathcal{H}om_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}({\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{O}}_{X})
, going back to Knebusch’s original definition
[
18
, §3.5]
(phrased slightly differently) of the Witt ring of a scheme. This in turn extends Witt’s original definition of the Witt ring of a field
[
41
]
to the case of schemes; see also
[
31
, §7]
for a detailed discussion of the Witt ring of a commutative ring.

For a noetherian commutative ring
R
, we write
W(R)
for
W(\Spec R)
. We note that in case 2 is invertible in
R
, then
(P,b)
is metabolic if and only if
(P,b)
is hyperbolic, that is,
(P,b)
is isometric to a space of the form
(Q\oplus Q^{\vee},\phi_{can})
with
\phi_{can}((v,\ell),(v^{\prime},\ell^{\prime}))=\ell(v^{\prime})+\ell^{\prime}(v)
.

The ring structure on
W(X)
is induced from the tensor product over
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
, with
b\cdot b^{\prime}(v\otimes w,v^{\prime}\otimes w^{\prime})=b(v,v^{\prime})b^{\prime}(w,w^{\prime})
. Sending
X
to
W(X)
defines a presheaf of rings on noetherian schemes, yielding in particular its sheafication
{\mathcal{W}}
on
{\mathbf{Sm}}/k_{\text{\rm Nis}}
. We are assuming here that 2 is invertible in
k
, so metabolic forms are hyperbolic in the affine case.

Noting that for

There is a

1.3.
Quadratic DT invariants of smooth projective threefolds

Let
X
be a smooth projective threefold, and for

be the Hilbert scheme of ideal sheaves of length

[

on

. By
[

, Theorem 6.3]
, a spin structure
\tau
on
X
induces an orientation
\rho^{\tau}_{n}
on

for every

.

We can now make the following definition.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective threefold with a spin structure
\tau:K_{X}\xrightarrow{\sim}L^{\otimes 2}
. Let

. The

is the degree of the
{\operatorname{EM}}({\mathcal{W}}_{*})
-valued motivic virtual fundamental class of

:

\tilde{I}_{n}(X):=\deg_{k}^{\rho^{\tau}_{n}}([\operatorname{Hilb}^{n}(X),\phi_{DT,n}]^{\text{\it vir}}_{{\operatorname{EM}}({\mathcal{W}}_{*})})\in W(k).

Remark 1.2
.

In general, it is not clear to what extent the invariant
\tilde{I}_{n}(X)
depends on the choice of the spin structure
\tau:K_{X}\xrightarrow{\sim}L^{\otimes 2}
. However, if

is even, and the choice of line bundle

is fixed, then it follows from the construction of the orientation in
[
23
]
that the orientation

is independent of the choice of isomorphism

. Furthermore, for

to the composition

also leaves

,

is independent of the choice of spin structure on

.

2.1.

As described in

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
\neq 2
. The group-scheme

has the following irreducible representations over

(1)

The trivial representation
\rho_{0}:N\to\operatorname{GL}_{1}
, and the sign representation
\rho_{0}^{-}:N\to\operatorname{GL}_{1}
, with
\rho_{0}^{-}(T_{1})=\{{\operatorname{\rm Id}}\}
and
\rho_{0}^{-}(\sigma)=-{\operatorname{\rm Id}}
.

(2)

For
m
a non-zero integer, the representation
\rho_{m}:N\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}
,

\rho_{m}(t)=\begin{pmatrix}t^{m}&0\\
0&t^{-m}\end{pmatrix},\rho_{m}(\sigma)=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
(-1)^{m}&0\end{pmatrix}.

For
m\neq 0
, the representations
\rho_{m}
and
\rho_{-m}
are isomorphic and
the set of representations
\{\rho_{0},\rho_{0}^{-},\rho_{m}\mid m>0\}
form a complete set of representatives for isomorphism classes of irreducible
N
-representations in
k
-vector spaces.

Proof.

Let
V
be a
k
-vector space and
\rho:N\to{\operatorname{Aut}}_{k}(V)
an irreducible representation. We decompose
V
into irreducible representations for
T_{1}
as
V=\oplus_{m}O(m)^{n_{m}}
, where
t\in T_{1}
acts by
t^{m}
on the one-dimensional
k
-vector space

. Since
\sigma\cdot t=t^{-1}\cdot\sigma
,
\rho(\sigma)
restricts to an isomorphism
\rho(\sigma)_{m}:O(m)^{n_{m}}\to O(-m)^{n_{-m}}
for each
m
, in particular,
n_{m}=n_{-m}
. Also, for
m\neq 0
, the subspace
O(m)^{n_{m}}\oplus O(-m)^{n_{-m}}
is
N
-stable, and for
m=0
, the subspace
O(0)^{n_{0}}
is
N
-stable, so since
V
is irreducible, this means there is a unique

with
n_{m}>0
. Let

.

Suppose that
m=0
. Then
V=O(0)^{n}
,
T_{1}
acts trivially on
V
, and thus the
N
-action factors through an action of
N/T_{1}={\mathbb{Z}}/2
. Since we are assuming the characteristic is not 2, this implies that
n=1
and there are exactly two possible actions: either
\sigma
acts by the identity or
\sigma
acts by
-{\operatorname{\rm Id}}
. This yields the irreducible representations
\rho_{0}
and
\rho_{0}^{-}
; clearly
\rho_{0}
is not isomorphic to
\rho_{0}^{-}
.

If
m>0
, choose a basis

for

, then the rank two subspace spanned by

and

is an

, so

\rho_{m}
.

The representation
\rho_{-m}
is isomorphic to
\rho_{m}
, by conjugating

\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\

Since the character of

for

is different from that of

if

, the two-dimensional representations
\rho_{m}

.

Lemma 5.31
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective threefold over
{\mathbb{R}}
with
H^{i}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X})=0
for
i=1,2
, and let
\text{ob}_{1}(X)
be the obstruction bundle on
\operatorname{Hilb}^{1}(X)=X
for the Donaldson-Thomas perfect obstruction theory
E_{\bullet}^{DT(1)}(X)
. There is a natural isomorphism

\text{ob}_{1}(X)\cong(T_{X}\otimes K_{X})^{\vee}.

Proof.

Let
i_{\Delta}:X\to X\times_{k}X
be the diagonal morphism and let
{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta}
be the ideal of the diagonal. We have the trace map
R\mathcal{H}om({\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta},{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta})\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X\times X}
, which gives a splitting of
R\mathcal{H}om({\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta},{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta})
as

R\mathcal{H}om({\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta},{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta})=R\mathcal{H}om({\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta},{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta})_{0}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{X\times X}.

By the definition of
E_{\bullet}^{DT(1)}(X)
following
[
6
, Lemma 1.22]
, we have

E_{\bullet}^{DT(1)}(X)=Rp_{1*}(R\mathcal{H}om({\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta},{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta})_{0}\otimes p_{2}^{*}K_{X}[2]).

By Grothendieck-Serre duality, noting that
R\mathcal{H}om({\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta},{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta})_{0}
is self-dual, this gives

E_{\bullet}^{DT(1)}(X)^{\vee}=Rp_{1*}(R\mathcal{H}om({\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta},{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta})_{0}[1])

Since
X
is smooth,
h^{i}(E_{\bullet}^{DT(1)}(X)^{\vee})
is a locally free sheaf on
X
for
i=0,1
,
h^{0}(E_{\bullet}^{DT(1)}(X)^{\vee})=T_{X}
and

h^{1}(E_{\bullet}^{DT(1)}(X)^{\vee})=\text{ob}_{1}(X)
(see
[
6
, Proposition 1.20]
). Using the vanishing of
H^{i}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X})
,
i=1,2
, we have

h^{i}(E_{\bullet}^{DT(1)}(X)^{\vee})=R^{i+1}p_{1*}R\mathcal{H}om({\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta},{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta})

for
i=0,1
.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\text{ob}_{1}(X)^{\vee}

\displaystyle=R^{1}p_{1*}R\mathcal{H}om(R\mathcal{H}om({\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta},{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta})_{0},p_{2}^{*}K_{X})

\displaystyle\cong R^{1}p_{1*}(R\mathcal{H}om({\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta},{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta})_{0}\otimes p_{2}^{*}K_{X}).

Using the spectral sequence

R^{i}p_{1*}(R^{j}\mathcal{H}om({\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta},{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta})_{0}\otimes p_{2}^{*}K_{X})\Rightarrow R^{i+j}p_{1*}(R\mathcal{H}om({\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta},{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta})_{0}\otimes p_{2}^{*}K_{X})

and the vanishing of
R^{0}\mathcal{H}om({\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta},{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta})_{0}
and
H^{1}(X,K_{X})
, we see that

R^{1}p_{1*}(R\mathcal{H}om_{0}({\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta},{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta})_{0}\otimes p_{2}^{*}K_{X})\cong p_{1*}(R^{1}\mathcal{H}om({\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta},{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta})\otimes p_{2}^{*}K_{X}).

Similarly, using the spectral sequence

R^{i}p_{1*}(R^{j}\mathcal{H}om({\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta},{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta})_{0})\Rightarrow R^{i+j}p_{1*}(R\mathcal{H}om({\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta},{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta})_{0})

and the vanishing of
R^{0}\mathcal{H}om({\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta},{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta})_{0}
and
H^{1}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X})
, we have

T_{X}=h^{0}(E_{\bullet}^{DT(1)}(X)^{\vee})=R^{1}p_{1*}(R\mathcal{H}om({\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta},{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta})_{0})=p_{1*}(R^{1}\mathcal{H}om({\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta},{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta})).

Since

R^{1}\mathcal{H}om({\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta},{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta})\cong R^{2}\mathcal{H}om(i_{\Delta*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X},{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta}),

we see that
{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta}\cdot R^{1}\mathcal{H}om({\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta},{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta})=0
, so

T_{X}=p_{1*}(R^{1}\mathcal{H}om({\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta},{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta}))\Rightarrow R^{1}\mathcal{H}om({\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta},{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta})\cong i_{\Delta*}T_{X}.

Thus

\displaystyle\text{ob}_{1}(X)^{\vee}

\displaystyle\cong p_{1*}(R^{1}\mathcal{H}om({\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta},{\mathcal{I}}_{\Delta})\otimes p_{2}^{*}K_{X})

\displaystyle\cong p_{1*}(i_{\Delta*}T_{X}\otimes p_{2}^{*}K_{X})

\displaystyle\cong p_{1*}(i_{\Delta*}(T_{X}\otimes i_{\Delta}^{*}p_{2}^{*}K_{X}))

\displaystyle\cong T_{X}\otimes K_{X}.\qed

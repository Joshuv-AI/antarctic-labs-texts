Then, the augmented Sombor (ASO) index of G G is defined as A ​ S ​ O ​ ( G ) = ∑ v i ​ v j ∈ E ⁡ ( G ) ( d i + d j − 2 ) − 1 ​ ( d i 2 + d j 2 ) . Introduction

Chemical graph theory constitutes a significant subfield of mathematical chemistry in which graph-theoretic methods are employed to represent, analyze, and predict the physical and chemical characteristics of chemical compounds

In a chemical graph, vertices model atoms while edges correspond to chemical bonds, thereby providing a mathematically rigorous framework for the study of molecular structure and related properties. Within this setting, real-valued graph invariants are commonly referred to as topological indices.

Among several families of topological indices investigated to date, degree-based indices

Their demonstrated success in correlating with a variety of physicochemical properties, together with their computational efficiency, has established them as indispensable tools in quantitative structure-property relationship (QSPR) studies, an area that underpins the prediction and rational design of new chemical compounds

Lemma 1

\frac{x^{2}+a^{2}}{x+a-2}\leq a+1+\frac{2}{a-1}

Then one can easily see that

Edge-Deletion Property

In this section, we prove that
ASO(G-v_{i}v_{j})<ASO(G)
whenever neither of the graphs
G-v_{i}v_{j}
and
G
contains any isolated edge. To establish this result, we first present the following lemma:

Lemma 3
.

The inequality

\frac{x^{2}+2xy-y^{2}-4x}{(x+y-2)^{3/2}\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}}>-\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}

(9)

holds for all real numbers

and

satisfying
x\geq 2
and
y\geq 1
.

Proof.

Consider

f(x,y)=x^{2}+2xy-y^{2}-4x+\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}(x+y-2)^{3/2}\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}},

where
x\geq 2
and
y\geq 1
.
Then, we have to prove that
f(x,y)>0
. If
1\leq y\leq x
and
x\geq 2
, then we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}

\displaystyle=\frac{y(x+y-2)^{3/2}}{\sqrt{x(x^{2}+y^{2})}}+\frac{3\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}\sqrt{x+y-2}}{2\sqrt{x}}+2x-2y>0.

If
x<y<3x
and
x\geq 2
, then

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}

\displaystyle>\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}\sqrt{x+y-2}}{\sqrt{x}}+2x-2y

\displaystyle>\frac{\sqrt{2xy}\sqrt{x+y-2}}{\sqrt{x}}-2(y-x)

\displaystyle=\frac{2y(x+y-2)-4(y-x)^{2}}{\sqrt{2y(x+y-2)}+2(y-x)}

\displaystyle>\frac{2\left(\frac{3(y-x)}{2}\right)\left(\frac{3(y-x)}{2}\right)-4(y-x)^{2}}{\sqrt{2y(x+y-2)}+2(y-x)}>0.

Therefore, if
1\leq y<3x
and
x\geq 2
, then
\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}>0
, and hence

\displaystyle f(x,y)

\displaystyle\geq f(x,1)=x^{2}+\frac{(x-1)^{3/2}\sqrt{x^{2}+1}}{\sqrt{x}}-2x-1>x^{2}-2x\geq 0.

It remains to prove

for

Since each of the inequalities

,

\displaystyle f(x,y)

\displaystyle>2xy-y^{2}-4x^{2}+\frac{y^{5/2}}{\sqrt{x}}

\displaystyle>-y^{2}-3x^{2}+\frac{y^{5/2}}{\sqrt{x}}

\displaystyle\geq-y^{2}-3\left(\frac{y}{3}\right)^{2}+\frac{y^{5/2}}{\sqrt{\frac{y}{3}}}>0.

∎

The following result shows that the ASO index decreases (increases, respectively) whenever an edge is removed (added, respectively) to a graph under a mild condition.

Theorem 2

Let

Proof.

In this proof, we set

f(x,y):=\sqrt{\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{x+y-2}}.

and we denote by

the degree of a vertex
v_{\ell}\in V(G-v_{i}v_{j})=V(G)
in
G

We note that

\displaystyle ASO(G)-ASO(G-v_{i}v_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{v_{r}\in N_{G}(v_{i})\setminus\{v_{j}\}}\big(f(d_{i},d_{r})-f(d_{i}-1,d_{r})\big)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{v_{s}\in N_{G}(v_{j})\setminus\{v_{i}\}}\big(f(d_{j},d_{s})-f(d_{j}-1,d_{s})\big)

\displaystyle\quad+f(d_{i},d_{j}).

(10)

Set

\Theta_{i}=\sum_{v_{r}\in N_{G}(v_{i})\setminus\{v_{j}\}}\theta_{i}(v_{r})
and
\Theta_{j}=\sum_{v_{s}\in N_{G}(v_{j})\setminus\{v_{i}\}}\theta_{j}(v_{s}),

where

\theta_{i}(v_{r})=f(d_{i},d_{r})-f(d_{i}-1,d_{r})
and
\theta_{j}(v_{s})=f(d_{j},d_{s})-f(d_{j}-1,d_{s}).

Then, (
3
) yields

\displaystyle ASO(G)-ASO(G-v_{i}v_{j})=\Theta_{i}+\Theta_{j}+f(d_{i},d_{j})

(11)

We note that
\max\{d_{i},d_{j}\}\geq 2.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
d_{i}\geq d_{j}
. We observe that
\Theta_{j}=0
for
d_{j}=1
. Hence, whenever we consider
\theta_{j}(v_{s}),
it will be assumed that
d_{j}\geq 2
.

By using the mean value theorem, we observe that, for any vertex
v_{r}\in N_{G}(v_{i})\setminus\{v_{j}\}
, there exists
\xi_{r}
such that
1\leq d_{i}-1<\xi_{r}<d_{i}
and

\theta_{i}(v_{r})=\frac{\xi_{r}^{2}+2\xi_{r}d_{r}-d_{r}^{2}-4\xi_{r}}{2(\xi_{r}+d_{r}-2)^{3/2}\sqrt{\xi_{r}^{2}+d_{r}^{2}}}.

(12)

Similarly, if
d_{j}\geq 2
, then for any vertex
v_{s}\in N_{G}(v_{j})\setminus\{v_{i}\}
, there exists
\xi_{s}
such that
1\leq d_{j}-1<\xi_{s}<d_{j}
and

\theta_{j}(v_{s})=\frac{\xi_{s}^{2}+2\xi_{s}d_{s}-d_{s}^{2}-4\xi_{s}}{2(\xi_{s}+d_{s}-2)^{3/2}\sqrt{\xi_{s}^{2}+d_{s}^{2}}}.

(13)

By Lemma
3
, from (
12
) and (
13
), it follows that

\theta_{i}(v_{r})>-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\xi_{r}}}>-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{d_{i}-1}}\ \text{ and }\ \theta_{j}(v_{s})>-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\xi_{s}}}>-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{d_{j}-1}},

provided that
d_{j}\geq 2
.
Therefore,

\Theta_{i}>-\frac{\sqrt{d_{i}-1}}{2}\ \text{ and }\ \Theta_{j}\geq-\frac{\sqrt{d_{j}-1}}{2},

where the last inequality holds not only for
d_{j}\geq 2
but also for
d_{j}=1
.
Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\Theta_{i}+\Theta_{j}+f(d_{i},d_{j})

\displaystyle>-\frac{\sqrt{d_{i}-1}+\sqrt{d_{j}-1}}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{d_{i}^{2}+d_{j}^{2}}{d_{i}+d_{j}-2}}

\displaystyle>-\frac{\sqrt{d_{i}-1}+\sqrt{d_{j}-1}}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{d_{i}+d_{j}}{2}},

(14)

where the last inequality in (
3
) holds because
(d_{i}-d_{j})^{2}+2(d_{i}+d_{j})>0,
which gives
2[d_{i}^{2}+d_{j}^{2}]>(d_{i}+d_{j})(d_{i}+d_{j}-2).

Also, we have

\frac{\sqrt{d_{i}-1}+\sqrt{d_{j}-1}}{2}\leq\sqrt{\frac{(d_{i}-1)+(d_{j}-1)}{2}}<\sqrt{\frac{d_{i}+d_{j}}{2}}.

(15)

Hence, the desired inequality follows from (
11
), (
3
) and (
15
).
∎

4
Vertex/Edge Connectivity

In the present section, we show that
(K_{1}\cup K_{n-k-1})\vee K_{k}
is the unique graph maximizing the ASO index among all
n
-order connected graphs with vertex connectivity (or edge connectivity)
k
, where
n\geq 4
. First, we establish a result concerning the vertex connectivity.

Theorem 3
.

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n\geq 4
with vertex connectivity
k
. Then

\displaystyle ASO(G)

\displaystyle\leq k\,\sqrt{\frac{(n-1)^{2}+k^{2}}{n+k-3}}+{k\choose 2}\,\sqrt{n+\frac{1}{n-2}}

\displaystyle\quad+k\,(n-k-1)\,\sqrt{n-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{5}{2(2n-5)}}

\displaystyle\quad+{n-k-1\choose 2}\,\sqrt{n-1+\frac{1}{n-3}}

(16)

with equality in (
16
) if and only if
G\cong(K_{1}\cup K_{n-k-1})\vee K_{k}
for
k\leq n-2
, whereas
G\cong K_{n}
for
k=n-1
.

Proof.

For
k=n-1
, we have
G\cong K_{n}
and hence the equality in (
16
) holds. In what follows, we assume that
k\leq n-2
. If
G\cong(K_{1}\cup K_{n-k-1})\vee K_{k}
, then one can easily see that the equality holds in (
16
). Otherwise,
G\ncong(K_{1}\cup K_{n-k-1})\vee K_{k}
. Since
G
has vertex connectivity
k
, there exists a
set
S\subset V(G)
consisting of
k
vertices such that
G-S
has at least two components. If
G-A
consists of at least three components, then inserting an edge connecting the vertices lying in two distinct components of
G-A
enlarges the value of
ASO(G)
(by Theorem
2
) but the vertex connectivity of
G
remains the same. Thereby, it is sufficient to prove the result when the graph
G-A
has only two components; we name them as
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
such that
|V(G_{1})|\leq|V(G_{2})|
. Suppose that
|V(G_{1})|=t
. Then
|V(G_{2})|=n-k-t
and
t\leq\frac{n-k}{2}
. By Theorem
2
, we obtain

ASO(G)\leq ASO((K_{t}\cup K_{n-k-t})\vee K_{k}).

Since
G\ncong(K_{1}\cup K_{n-k-1})\vee K_{k}
, we have
2\leq t\leq\frac{n-k}{2}
, which yields
n\geq 5
and
k\leq n-4
. Thus, we have
2\leq k+t-1\leq n-t-1\leq n-3
. Hence, by Lemma
2
, we obtain

\displaystyle h(k+t-1,k+t-1)<h(n-2,n-2)=n-1+\frac{1}{n-3},

\displaystyle h(n-t-1,n-t-1)<h(n-2,n-2)=n-1+\frac{1}{n-3},

\displaystyle h(n-1,k+t-1)\leq h(n-1,2)=n-1+\frac{4}{n-1},

\displaystyle h(n-1,n-t-1)\leq h(n-1,2)=n-1+\frac{4}{n-1}.

Now,

\displaystyle ASO((K_{t}\cup K_{n-k-t})\vee K_{k})

\displaystyle={k\choose 2}\,\sqrt{h(n-1,n-1)}+{n-k-t\choose 2}\,\sqrt{h(n-t-1,n-t-1)}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~+{t\choose 2}\,\sqrt{h(k+t-1,k+t-1)}+kt\,\sqrt{h(n-1,k+t-1)}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~+k(n-k-t)\,\sqrt{h(n-1,n-t-1)}

\displaystyle<{k\choose 2}\,\sqrt{n+\frac{1}{n-2}}+{n-k-t\choose 2}\,\sqrt{n-1+\frac{1}{n-3}}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~+{t\choose 2}\,\sqrt{n-1+\frac{1}{n-3}}+kt\,\sqrt{n-1+\frac{4}{n-1}}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~+k(n-k-t)\,\sqrt{n-1+\frac{4}{n-1}}

\displaystyle=\left({t\choose 2}+{n-k-t\choose 2}\right)\,\sqrt{n-1+\frac{1}{n-3}}+{k\choose 2}\,\sqrt{n+\frac{1}{n-2}}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~+k(n-k)\,\sqrt{n-1+\frac{4}{n-1}}.

(17)

Let us consider a function

f(x)=x\,(x-1)+(n-k-x)\,(n-k-x-1),~~~2\leq x\leq\frac{n-k}{2}.

Then we obtain

f^{\prime}(x)=2\,\Big(2x-(n-k)\Big)\leq 0~\mbox{ as }2\leq x\leq\frac{n-k}{2}.

Thus
f(x)
is a decreasing function on
2\leq x\leq\frac{n-k}{2}
, and hence

\displaystyle{t\choose 2}+{n-k-t\choose 2}=\frac{1}{2}\,f(t)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\,f(2)=\frac{1}{2}\,\Big((n-k-2)\,(n-k-3)+2\Big)

\displaystyle={n-k-1\choose 2}-(n-k-3).

(18)

Since

n-1+\frac{4}{n-1}\leq n-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{5}{2(2n-5)},

from (
17
) and (
18
), we obtain

\displaystyle ASO((K_{t}\cup K_{n-k-t})\vee K_{k})

\displaystyle<{n-k-1\choose 2}\,\sqrt{n-1+\frac{1}{n-3}}+{k\choose 2}\,\sqrt{n+\frac{1}{n-2}}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~+k(n-k)\,\sqrt{n-1+\frac{4}{n-1}}-(n-k-3)\,\sqrt{n-1+\frac{1}{n-3}}

\displaystyle<{n-k-1\choose 2}\,\sqrt{n-1+\frac{1}{n-3}}+{k\choose 2}\,\sqrt{n+\frac{1}{n-2}}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~+k\,(n-k-1)\,\sqrt{n-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{5}{2(2n-5)}}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~+k\,\sqrt{\frac{(n-1)^{2}+k^{2}}{n+k-3}}.

(19)

For the last inequality in (
4
), it remains to prove that

\displaystyle k\,\sqrt{n-1+\frac{4}{n-1}}-(n-k-3)\,\sqrt{n-1+\frac{1}{n-3}}<k\,\sqrt{\frac{(n-1)^{2}+k^{2}}{n+k-3}},

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle k\left(\sqrt{h(k,n-1)}-\sqrt{h(2,n-1)}\,\right)+(n-k-3)\,\sqrt{h(n-2,n-2)}>0.

(20)

If
k=1
or
k=2
, then by Lemma
2
,
\sqrt{h(k,n-1)}-\sqrt{h(2,n-1)}\geq 0
, and hence (
20
) holds. In what follows, we assume that
k\geq 3
. Then, by Lemma
2
, we have
\sqrt{h(k,n-1)}-\sqrt{h(2,n-1)}<0
. Hence,
in order to prove (
20
), it is sufficient to show that

(n-4)\left(\sqrt{h(k,n-1)}-\sqrt{h(2,n-1)}\,\right)+(n-k-3)\,\sqrt{h(n-2,n-2)}>0

for
3\leq k\leq n-4
.
We define a function
\phi
on the set of real numbers greater than or equal to 3 as

\displaystyle\phi(x)=(n-4)\left(\sqrt{h(x,n-1)}-\sqrt{h(2,n-1)}\,\right)+(n-x-3)\,\sqrt{h(n-2,n-2)},

where
n\geq 7
is a fixed integer satisfying
3\leq x\leq n-4
. Here, we have

\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}\left(\sqrt{h(x,n-1)}\,\right)=\frac{\Psi(x)}{4(n+x-3)^{5/2}\left((n-1)^{2}+x^{2}\right)^{3/2}},

where

\Psi(x)=\left[(n-1)^{2}(n(7n-30)+39)-x^{4}\right]+4x(n-3)[(n-1)^{2}-x^{2}]+6(n-1)^{2}x^{2}.

Since
(n-1)^{2}(n(7n-30)+39)>n^{4}>x^{4}
and
(n-1)^{2}>x^{2}
, we have
\Psi(x)>0
and hence

\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}\left(\sqrt{h(x,n-1)}\,\right)>0.

Consequently, we obtain

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle\leq(n-4)\left[\frac{d}{dx}\left(\sqrt{h(x,n-1)}\,\right)\right]_{x=n-4}-\sqrt{h(n-2,n-2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-4)(2n^{2}-20n+39)}{2(2n-7)^{3/2}\sqrt{2n^{2}-10n+17}}-\sqrt{n-1+\frac{1}{n-3}}

\displaystyle<\frac{(n-4)(2n^{2}-20n+39)}{2(2n-8)^{3/2}\sqrt{2n(n-5)+17}}-\sqrt{n-1+\frac{1}{n-3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(2n^{2}-20n+39\right)\sqrt{2(n-3)}-8(n-2)\sqrt{n-4}\sqrt{2n(n-5)+17}}{8\sqrt{(n-4)(n-3)}\sqrt{2n(n-5)+17}}

\displaystyle<\frac{\left(2n^{2}-20n+39\right)2\sqrt{n-4}-8(n-2)\sqrt{n-4}\sqrt{2n(n-5)+17}}{8\sqrt{(n-4)(n-3)}\sqrt{2n(n-5)+17}}

\displaystyle<0,

where the last inequality holds because
2n(n-5)+17>(n-2)^{2}
and
2n^{2}-20n+39<4(n-2)^{2}
for
n\geq 7
.
Therefore, by keeping in mind Lemma
2
, we have

\displaystyle\phi(x)\geq\phi(n-4)

\displaystyle=(n-4)\left(\sqrt{h(n-4,n-1)}-\sqrt{h(2,n-1)}\,\right)+\sqrt{h(n-2,n-2)}

\displaystyle>(n-3)\sqrt{h(n-4,n-1)}-(n-4)\sqrt{h(2,n-1)}>0.

(21)

The last inequality in (
4
) holds because, for
n\geq 7
,

\left(\frac{n-3}{n-4}\right)^{2}>\frac{h(2,n-1)}{h(n-4,n-1)},

which is equivalent to

-3n^{3}(n-9)-n(116n-319)-407<0.

Therefore,
\phi(k)>0
, which implies (
20
).
This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

We now establish a result concerning the edge connectivity.

Theorem 4
.

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n\geq 4
with edge connectivity
k^{\prime}
. Then

\displaystyle ASO(G)

\displaystyle\leq k^{\prime}\,\sqrt{\frac{(n-1)^{2}+k^{\prime 2}}{n+k^{\prime}-3}}+{k^{\prime}\choose 2}\,\sqrt{n+\frac{1}{n-2}}

\displaystyle\quad+k^{\prime}\,(n-k^{\prime}-1)\,\sqrt{n-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{5}{2(2n-5)}}

\displaystyle\quad+{n-k^{\prime}-1\choose 2}\,\sqrt{n-1+\frac{1}{n-3}}

(22)

with equality in (
22
) if and only if
G\cong(K_{1}\cup K_{n-k^{\prime}-1})\vee K_{k^{\prime}}
for
k^{\prime}\leq n-2
, whereas
G\cong K_{n}
for
k^{\prime}=n-1
.

Proof.

For
k^{\prime}=n-1
, we have
G\cong K_{n}
and hence, the equality in (
22
) holds. In what follows, we assume that
1\leq k^{\prime}\leq n-2
. Thus, we have
1\leq k\leq k^{\prime}\leq n-2
.
By the proof of Lemma
1
, the function

f(x)=\displaystyle{\sqrt{\frac{(n-1)^{2}+x^{2}}{n+x-3}}}

is a strictly increasing function on
x\geq-n+3+\sqrt{2(n^{2}-4n+5)}
and a strictly decreasing function on
1\leq x\leq-n+3+\sqrt{2(n^{2}-4n+5)}
. Thus we have

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{(n-1)^{2}+x^{2}}{n+x-3}}

\displaystyle\geq f\Big(-n+3+\sqrt{2(n^{2}-4n+5)}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{4\,(n^{2}-4n+5)-2\,(n-3)\,\sqrt{2(n^{2}-4n+5)}}{\sqrt{2(n^{2}-4n+5)}}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2\sqrt{2(n^{2}-4n+5)}-2\,(n-3)}

\displaystyle>\sqrt{2\,\sqrt{2}\,(n-2)-2n+6}>0.9\,\sqrt{n}.

(23)

For
1\leq x\leq n-2
, we obtain

\displaystyle x\,\Big(x\,(2n+x-6)-(n-1)^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle>-(n-1)^{2}\,x>-1.8\,\sqrt{n}\,(n-1)(n-2)^{3/2}

\displaystyle>-1.8\,\sqrt{n}\,\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+x^{2}}\,(n+x-3)^{3/2},

that is,

\displaystyle\frac{x\,\Big(x\,(2n+x-6)-(n-1)^{2}\Big)}{2\,\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+x^{2}}\,(n+x-3)^{3/2}}>-0.9\,\sqrt{n}.

(24)

Let us consider a function

\displaystyle g(x)

\displaystyle={x\choose 2}\,\sqrt{n+\frac{1}{n-2}}+x\,(n-x-1)\,\sqrt{n-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{5}{2(2n-5)}}

\displaystyle\quad+x\,\sqrt{\frac{(n-1)^{2}+x^{2}}{n+x-3}}+{n-x-1\choose 2}\,\sqrt{n-1+\frac{1}{n-3}},

for
1\leq x\leq n-2
.
If
1\leq x\leq\frac{n-1}{2}
, then using (
23
) and (
24
), we have

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{(n-1)^{2}+x^{2}}{n+x-3}}+\frac{x\,\Big(x\,(2n+x-6)-(n-1)^{2}\Big)}{2\,\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}+x^{2}}\,(n+x-3)^{3/2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\Big(x-\frac{1}{2}\Big)\,\sqrt{n+\frac{1}{n-2}}+(n-2x-1)\,\sqrt{n-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{5}{2(2n-5)}}

\displaystyle\quad-\Big((n-x-1)-\frac{1}{2}\Big)\,\sqrt{n-1+\frac{1}{n-3}}

\displaystyle>\left(\Big(x-\frac{1}{2}\Big)+(n-2x-1)-(n-x-1)+\frac{1}{2}\right)\,\sqrt{n-1+\frac{1}{n-3}}=0.

If
x>\frac{n-1}{2}
, then using (
23
) and (
24
), we have

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle>\left(\Big(x-\frac{1}{2}\Big)+(n-2x-1)-(n-x-1)+\frac{1}{2}\right)\,\sqrt{n+\frac{1}{n-2}}=0.

Thus,
g(x)
is a strictly increasing function. Since
1\leq k\leq k^{\prime}\leq n-2
, by Theorem
3
, we obtain

\displaystyle ASO(G)

\displaystyle\leq k\,\sqrt{n+\frac{2}{n-2}}+{k\choose 2}\,\sqrt{n+\frac{1}{n-2}}

\displaystyle\quad+k\,(n-k-1)\,\sqrt{n-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{5}{2(2n-5)}}

\displaystyle\quad+{n-k-1\choose 2}\,\sqrt{n-1+\frac{1}{n-3}}

\displaystyle\leq k^{\prime}\,\sqrt{n+\frac{2}{n-2}}+{k^{\prime}\choose 2}\,\sqrt{n+\frac{1}{n-2}}

\displaystyle\quad+k^{\prime}\,(n-k^{\prime}-1)\,\sqrt{n-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{5}{2(2n-5)}}

\displaystyle\quad+{n-k^{\prime}-1\choose 2}\,\sqrt{n-1+\frac{1}{n-3}}.

Moreover, both equalities in the above chain of inequalities hold if and only if
G
is isomorphic to
(K_{1}\cup K_{n-k^{\prime}-1})\vee K_{k^{\prime}}
.
∎

5
Concluding Remarks

The augmented Sombor (ASO) index is a recently introduced topological index that has already attracted attention, particularly for its chemical applications. In this work, we have investigated several mathematical properties of the ASO index. In particular, we establish the best possible upper bound for the ASO index of unicyclic graphs in terms of their order and characterized the corresponding extremal graphs. Also, we have shown that the ASO index decreases (increases, respectively) whenever an edge is removed (added, respectively) to a graph under a mild condition. Using this result, we have characterized the graphs maximizing the ASO index among all connected graphs of order
n
with prescribed vertex connectivity (or edge connectivity) for
n\geq 4
. Despite these contributions, the extremal behavior of the ASO index remains largely unexplored for many classical families of graphs, thereby presenting a variety of promising directions for further investigation.

Measures Induced by Subgroups and Tuples in Free Groups
Shrinit Singh

Abstract
. We study probability measure on Hom ⁡ ( H , G ) \mathrm{Hom}(H,G) , where G G is a finite group and H H a finitely generated subgroup of a finitely generated free group F F , obtained by pushing forward the uniform random homomorphisms Hom ⁡ ( F , G ) \mathrm{Hom}(F,G) via restriction map to Hom ⁡ ( H , G ) \mathrm{Hom}(H,G) . This framework generalizes the word measures arising from single elements of a free group. We formalize the notion of profinite rigidity for subgroups via these induced measures. Our main result shows that a finitely generated subgroup is profinitely rigid if and only if any (equivalently, every) ordered generating tuple is profinitely rigid, thereby extending the notion of rigidity from individual word maps to arbitrary tuples. We also obtain a generalization of a result of [ 4 ] .



1.
Introduction

Word maps and the probability measures they induce have attracted substantial interest in recent years.
Given a word
w\in F_{n}
, evaluation on an
n
-tuple of elements of a finite group
G
yields the word map

w_{G}:G^{n}\to G,

and pushing forward the uniform distribution on
G^{n}
produces the
word measure
on
G
.
It is believed that these word measures encode subtle algebraic information about the word
w
. Partial positive results toward the question of whether a word
is determined (up to
\mathrm{Aut}(F_{n})
) by its measures on all finite groups were obtained by
Parzanchevski–Puder and Wilton
[
4
]

[
6
]
; Hanany–Puder–Meiri
[
3
]
;
and Ascari–Fruchter
[
1
]
.

In this paper we replace a single word by an arbitrary finitely generated subgroup and study the measures obtained
by restricting homomorphisms. Fix a free group
F_{n}
and a finitely generated subgroup
H\leq F_{n}
.
For each finite group
G
the restriction map

r_{H}:\mathrm{Hom}(F_{n},G)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(H,G),\qquad\alpha\mapsto\alpha|_{H},

pushes forward the uniform distribution on
\mathrm{Hom}(F_{n},G)
to a probability measure
\mu_{H,G}

on
\mathrm{Hom}(H,G)
. When
H
is cyclic, generated by a word
w
, this construction recovers the
usual word measure on
G
.

Using the family of measures
\{\mu_{H,G}\}_{G\ \text{finite}}
we introduce a notion of
profinite rigidity

for finitely generated subgroups. Informally, a subgroup
H\leq F_{n}
is profinitely rigid if any isomorphic subgroup

L\leq F_{n}
whose induced measures agree with those of
H
on
\mathrm{Hom}(-,G)
for every finite
G

must already be the image of
H
under an automorphism of
F_{n}
. Equivalently, the statistical behaviour of
restriction of homomorphisms to finite groups determines
H
up to
\mathrm{Aut}(F_{n})
. This generalises the
usual notion of profinite rigidity for words viewed as cyclic subgroups to arbitrary finitely generated subgroups.

Our main theorem reduces subgroup rigidity to the previously studied problem of tuple rigidity:

Theorem 1
.

Let
H\leq_{\mathrm{f.g.}}F_{n}
. Then
H
is profinitely rigid if and only if some (equivalently every)
ordered generating tuple of
H
is profinitely rigid as a tuple in
F_{n}
.

Thus the problem of distinguishing subgroups by the measures they induce is equivalent to the problem of
distinguishing generating tuples by their induced measures. This viewpoint clarifies the
relationship between subgroup- and word-measures and might be helpful in extending several results known for words to the setting of finitely generated subgroups. In particular, we generalise the Parzanchevski–Puder
results on cyclic subgroups and word measures
[
4
]
. As a concrete corollary we obtain

Corollary 1
.

Let
F_{n}=\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle
. Then any subgroup of the form

\langle x_{1}^{m_{1}},x_{2}^{m_{2}},\dots,x_{r}^{m_{r}}\rangle
with
r\leq n
and integers
m_{i}
is profinitely rigid.

Related work and further directions

In related work, Parzanchevski and Puder
[
4
]
established the profinite rigidity of primitive words by analysing free factors and studying the expected number of fixed points in
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
via a subgroup of
S_{n}
arising from random homomorphisms of these free factors into symmetric groups. Using a markedly different approach, Wilton
[
6
]
independently obtained the same rigidity result via geometric and group–theoretic methods. More recently, Zapirain
[
2
]
have extended the same result in virtually free groups using algebra. In the present article, we formalise the notion of profinite rigidity for subgroups, which unifies all these results.

There are many natural unknown questions than results in this direction. I want to draw attention to this problem: Does there exists two finitely generated subgroups
of possibly different ranks which can induce the same set of family of measures on all finite targets? Results of
Parzanchevski–Puder suggest that any set of probabilities having a single element must be coming from a free factor.

Organization of the paper

In §
2
we collect notation and preliminaries on word maps, profinite completions and profinite rigidity.
In §
3
we introduce the measures induced by
k
-tuples and prove equivalences characterising
when two tuples induce the same family of measures on all finite groups. The main theorem and its proof, together with
corollaries and applications appear in
§
4
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Word maps

Let
F_{n}=\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle
be the free group on generators
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
.
For a word

w=\prod_{j=1}^{m}x_{i_{j}}^{\varepsilon_{j}}\in F_{n},

where
i_{j}\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and
\varepsilon_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
, the corresponding
word map
on a group
G
is the map

w_{G}:G^{n}\to G,\qquad w_{G}(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})=\prod_{j=1}^{m}g_{i_{j}}^{\varepsilon_{j}}.

When
G
is finite, the uniform probability measure on
G^{n}
pushes forward along
w_{G}
to a probability measure on
G
. Equivalently, for any subset
S\subseteq G
we define

\mathrm{Pr}_{w}(S)\;=\;\frac{\big|w_{G}^{-1}(S)\big|}{|G|^{n}}.

In particular,
\mathrm{Pr}_{w}(g)
denotes the probability that
w_{G}
evaluates to
g\in G
when its
n
inputs are chosen uniformly and independently from
G
.

Hanany, Puder and Meiri
[
3
]
used this distributional viewpoint to define a notion of rigidity for words:

Definition 2
.

A word
w\in F_{n}
is
profinitely rigid
if whenever
u\in F_{n}
induces the same probability measure as
w
on every finite group (that is,
\mathrm{Pr}_{w}=\mathrm{Pr}_{u}
on every finite group), then
u
lies in the
\mathrm{Aut}(F_{n})
-orbit of
w
.

We briefly recall the profinite completion, which encodes the collection of finite quotients of a finitely generated group in a single compact group.

Let
P
be a finitely generated group and let
\mathcal{N}=\{N\unlhd P\}
denote the family of normal subgroups of finite index in
P
. Order
\mathcal{N}
by
N_{1}\leq N_{2}
if
N_{2}\subseteq N_{1}
; with this order
\mathcal{N}
is a directed set. The natural quotient maps
\phi_{N_{2},N_{1}}:P/N_{2}\to P/N_{1}
for
N_{1}\leq N_{2}
make
\{P/N\}_{N\in\mathcal{N}}
into an inverse system of finite groups. The
profinite completion
of
P
is the inverse limit

\widehat{P}=\varprojlim_{N\unlhd_{\mathrm{f.i.}}P}P/N,

where the limit runs over all finite-index normal subgroups of
P
.

The term “profinitely rigid” for words is justified by the following result (see
[
3
]
):

Theorem 2
.

If two words
w,u\in F_{n}
induce the same probability measure on every finite group, then
w
and
u
are profinitely automorphic.

In fact, the authors prove a more general statement valid for arbitrary elements of any finitely generated group.

2.2.
Profinite rigidity for subgroups

Let
P
be a finitely generated group and
H\leq P
a subgroup. For any group
K
there is a natural restriction map

r:\mathrm{Hom}(P,K)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(H,K),\qquad r(f)=f|_{H}.

When
K
is finite, the uniform distribution on
\mathrm{Hom}(P,K)
pushes forward along
r
to a probability distribution on
\mathrm{Hom}(H,K)
. Concretely, for
h\in\mathrm{Hom}(H,K)
set

\mathrm{Pr}_{H,K}(h)\;=\;\frac{\big|\{\,f\in\mathrm{Hom}(P,K)\mid f|_{H}=h\,\}\big|}{|\mathrm{Hom}(P,K)|}.

Let
P
be LERF (locally extended residually finite) and let
H_{1},H_{2}\leq P
be subgroups. Suppose
\alpha:H_{1}\to H_{2}
is an isomorphism. We say
H_{1}
and
H_{2}

induce the same probability measure
on
\mathrm{Hom}(-,K)
if, for every finite group
K
and every
h\in\mathrm{Hom}(H_{2},K)
,

\mathrm{Pr}_{H_{2},K}(h)=\mathrm{Pr}_{H_{1},K}(h\circ\alpha).

Equivalently, after identifying
\mathrm{Hom}(H_{1},K)
with
\mathrm{Hom}(H_{2},K)
via precomposition with
\alpha
, the pushforward distributions on these Hom-sets coincide for every finite
K
.

Definition 3
.

A subgroup
H\leq G
is
profinitely rigid
in
G
if whenever
L\leq G
with the isomorphism
\alpha:H\to L
is another subgroup that induces the same probability measures on
\mathrm{Hom}(-,K)
(for every finite
K
), then there exists
\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)
with
\varphi(H)=L
and
\phi|_{H}=\alpha
.

The same notion can be formulated for closed subgroups of profinite groups by replacing
\mathrm{Hom}
with the appropriate continuous Hom-sets into finite (discrete) groups.

Note that if two subgroups lie in the same
\mathrm{Aut}(G)
-orbit, then they induce the same distributions on
\mathrm{Hom}(-,K)
for every finite
K
. The central problem is whether the converse holds: does coincidence of the induced distributions force the subgroups to lie in the same
\mathrm{Aut}(G)
-orbit?

3.
Tuples and induced distributions

The decidability of whether two words lie in the same automorphic orbit of a free group (Whitehead’s algorithm) and, more generally, the decidability for
n
-tuples motivates a natural generalisation of profinite rigidity from single elements (word maps) to tuples of elements.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group. For a finite group
G
, denote by
\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,G)
the set of homomorphisms
\Gamma\to G
and by
\mathrm{Epi}(\Gamma,G)
the subset of surjective homomorphisms. Given a
k
-tuple
T=(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{k})\in\Gamma^{k}
and a finite group
G
, the
evaluation map

\operatorname{ev}_{T}:\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,G)\longrightarrow G^{k},\qquad\varphi\mapsto(\varphi(\gamma_{1}),\dots,\varphi(\gamma_{k}))

pushes forward the uniform probability measure on the finite set
\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,G)
to a probability measure on
G^{k}
. Equivalently, for each
\mathbf{g}=(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})\in G^{k}
define the evaluation count

N_{T}(G;\mathbf{g}):=\bigl|\{\varphi\in\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,G):\ \varphi(\gamma_{i})=g_{i}\ \text{for all }i\}\bigr|.

We say two
k
-tuples
T,T^{\prime}\in\Gamma^{k}

induce the same measure
if for every finite group
G
and every
\mathbf{g}\in G^{k}
we have

N_{T}(G;\mathbf{g})=N_{T^{\prime}}(G;\mathbf{g}).

For later use we introduce the following convenient notation. For a
k
-tuple
T\in\Gamma^{k}
and
\mathbf{g}\in G^{k}
set

\mathrm{Hom}_{(T,\mathbf{g})}(\Gamma,G):=\{\phi\in\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,G)\mid\phi(\gamma_{i})=g_{i}\ \forall i\},

\mathrm{Epi}_{(T,\mathbf{g})}(\Gamma,G):=\{\phi\in\mathrm{Epi}(\Gamma,G)\mid\phi(\gamma_{i})=g_{i}\ \forall i\},

and

\mathrm{ImEpi}_{T}(\Gamma,G):=\{\phi(T)\in G^{k}\mid\phi\in\mathrm{Epi}(\Gamma,G)\}.

Finally, for a finite group
G
define the characteristic subgroup

K_{\Gamma}(G):=\bigcap_{\varphi\in\mathrm{Epi}(\Gamma,G)}\ker(\varphi).

The quotient
\Gamma/K_{\Gamma}(G)
is the maximal quotient of
\Gamma
that embeds into a direct power of
G
.

The next theorem is the natural analogue for
k
-tuples of
[
3
, Theorem 2.2]
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group and let

T=(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{k}),\qquad T^{\prime}=(\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{k})\in\Gamma^{k}.

The following statements are equivalent.

(1)

There exists a continuous automorphism
\Theta\in\mathrm{Aut}(\widehat{\Gamma})
of the profinite completion with

\Theta(\gamma_{i})=\delta_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,k
.

(2)

For every finite group
G
, the pushforward probability measures on
G^{k}
induced by
T
and
T^{\prime}
coincide.

(3)

For every finite group
G
and every
\mathbf{g}\in G^{k}
we have

\big|\mathrm{Epi}_{(T,\mathbf{g})}(\Gamma,G)\big|=\big|\mathrm{Epi}_{(T^{\prime},\mathbf{g})}(\Gamma,G)\big|.

(4)

For every finite group
G
the image-sets

\mathrm{ImEpi}_{T}(\Gamma,G)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{ImEpi}_{T^{\prime}}(\Gamma,G)

are equal as subsets of
G^{k}
.

(5)

There exists an automorphism
f
of
Q=\Gamma/K
such that
f(\gamma_{i}K)=\delta_{i}K
for all
i=1,2,\ldots,k
, where
K=\Gamma/K_{\Gamma}(G)
for every finite group
G
.

Proof.

We prove the cycle of implications (1)
\Rightarrow
(2)
\Leftrightarrow
(3)
\Rightarrow
(4)
\Rightarrow
(5)
\Rightarrow
(1).

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2).

A continuous automorphism
\Theta\in\mathrm{Aut}(\widehat{\Gamma})
induces, for each finite group
G
, a bijection

\Theta^{*}:\mathrm{Hom}(\widehat{\Gamma},G)\to\mathrm{Hom}(\widehat{\Gamma},G),\qquad\varphi\mapsto\varphi\circ\Theta^{-1}.

By the universal property of the profinite completion
[
5
, Lemma 3.2.1]
,
\mathrm{Hom}(\widehat{\Gamma},G)\cong\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,G)
, and under this identification
\Theta^{*}
preserves evaluation counts. Hence the pushforward measures determined by
T
and
T^{\prime}
on
G^{k}
coincide for every finite
G
.

(2)
\Leftrightarrow
(3).

For fixed
\mathbf{g}\in G^{k}
any homomorphism
\varphi\in\mathrm{Hom}_{(T,\mathbf{g})}(\Gamma,G)
has image contained in the subgroup
H=\langle g_{1},\dots,g_{k}\rangle\leq G
, and conversely any epimorphism onto such an
H
determines a homomorphism to
G
by composing with the inclusion
H\hookrightarrow G
. Thus

\mathrm{Hom}_{(T,\mathbf{g})}(\Gamma,G)=\bigsqcup_{H\leq G:\ \langle g_{1},\dots,g_{k}\rangle\subseteq H}\mathrm{Epi}_{(T,\mathbf{g})}(\Gamma,H).

The counts for
\mathrm{Hom}_{(T,\mathbf{g})}
are therefore determined by the counts for all
\mathrm{Epi}_{(T,\mathbf{g})}
on subgroups of
G
, and conversely one recovers the epimorphism-counts from the Hom-counts by induction on
|G|
. This shows equivalence of (2) and (3).

(3)
\Rightarrow
(4).

If for a given
\mathbf{g}\in G^{k}
the set
\mathrm{Epi}_{(T,\mathbf{g})}(\Gamma,G)
is nonempty then the left-hand side of (3) is positive, hence the right-hand side is positive as well; thus
\mathbf{g}\in\mathrm{ImEpi}_{T}(\Gamma,G)
iff
\mathbf{g}\in\mathrm{ImEpi}_{T^{\prime}}(\Gamma,G)
. Hence the image-sets coincide.

(4)
\Rightarrow
(5).

Fix a finite quotient
Q=\Gamma/N
and let
\pi:\Gamma\to Q
be the canonical projection. By (4) we have
\mathrm{ImEpi}_{T}(\Gamma,Q)=\mathrm{ImEpi}_{T^{\prime}}(\Gamma,Q)
, and since
\pi\in\mathrm{Epi}(\Gamma,Q)
we get
\pi(T)\in\mathrm{ImEpi}_{T^{\prime}}(\Gamma,Q)
. Thus there exists a surjection
\varphi\in\mathrm{Epi}(\Gamma,Q)
with

\varphi(T^{\prime})=\pi(T)
.

Consider the natural isomorphisms

\bar{\pi}:\Gamma/\ker\pi\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }Q,\qquad\bar{\varphi}:\Gamma/\ker\varphi\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }Q,

induced by
\pi
and
\varphi
. We show that
\mathrm{ker}(\phi)=K
. As
\mathrm{ker}(\phi)\subseteq N
, for every normal subgroup
N
of
\Gamma
with
\Gamma/N\cong G
because the number of
normal subgroups in
\Gamma/\mathrm{ker}(\phi)
with quotient
G
is the same as in
\Gamma/K
and thus the same as in
\Gamma
. We get
\mathrm{ker}(\phi)\leq K
but we also have
[\Gamma:\mathrm{\phi}]=[\Gamma:K]
and hence we have
\mathrm{ker}(\phi)=K
. The composition

f:=\bar{\pi}\circ\bar{\varphi}^{-1}\in\mathrm{Aut}(Q)

is an automorphism of
Q
. By construction, for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
we have

\alpha\big(\varphi(\gamma)\big)=\pi(\gamma)
. Applying this identity to the coordinates of the
k
-tuple
T^{\prime}
and using
\varphi(T^{\prime})=\pi(T)
yields

f\big(\pi(T)\big)=\pi(T^{\prime}).

Hence,
f(\gamma_{i}K)=\delta_{i}K
for all
i=1,2,\ldots,k.

(5)
\Rightarrow
(1).

For each finite group
G
and the associated finite quotient
Q_{G}=\Gamma/K_{\Gamma}(G)
of
\Gamma
, let

S_{Q_{G}}:=\{\sigma\in\mathrm{Aut}(Q)\mid\sigma(\overline{T})=\overline{T^{\prime}}\},

where
\overline{T},\overline{T^{\prime}}\in{Q_{G}}^{k}
denote the images of
T,T^{\prime}
. By (5) each
S_{Q_{G}}
is nonempty. It is straightforward to see that the family
\{Q_{G}=\Gamma/K_{\Gamma}(G)\}_{G}
is cofinal in the inverse system of all finite quotients of
\Gamma
.

If
Q^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow Q
is a quotient map then the projection
\mathrm{Aut}(Q^{\prime})\to\mathrm{Aut}(Q)
sends
S_{Q^{\prime}}
into
S_{Q}
, so the family
\{S_{Q}\}_{Q}
is an inverse system of nonempty finite sets. By the compactness of profinite inverse limits there exists a compatible choice
(\sigma_{Q})_{Q}
with
\sigma_{Q}\in S_{Q}
for all
Q
. The compatible family defines a continuous automorphism
\Theta\in\varprojlim_{Q}\mathrm{Aut}(Q)\cong\mathrm{Aut}(\widehat{\Gamma})

[
3
, Lemma 2.4]
, and by construction
\Theta(\gamma_{i})=\delta_{i}
in
\widehat{\Gamma}
for all
i
. This proves (1).
∎

As an immediate corollary, when
\Gamma=F_{n}
the theorem implies that two
k
-tuples of words in
F_{n}
that induce the same pushforward measures on
G^{k}
for every finite group
G
are in the same
\mathrm{Aut}(\widehat{F_{n}})
orbit.

4.
Profinite rigidity for subgroups

We begin by recalling a useful criterion of Garrido and Zapirain.

Lemma 4

(
[
2
]
, Proposition 3.2)
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated profinite group and let
\alpha:H_{1}\to H_{2}
be an isomorphism between two closed subgroups of
G
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

there exists an automorphism
\widetilde{\alpha}\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)
with
\widetilde{\alpha}|_{H_{1}}=\alpha
;

(2)

for every finite group
K
and every
\beta\in\mathrm{Hom}(H_{2},K)
we have

\mathrm{Pr}_{H_{1},K}(\beta\circ\alpha)=\mathrm{Pr}_{H_{2},K}(\beta).

We now record a version adapted to the discrete LERF setting.

Proposition 5
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated LERF group and let
H_{1},H_{2}\leq G
be finitely generated subgroups. If
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
induce the same probability measures on
\mathrm{Hom}(-,K)
for every finite group
K
, then the closures
\overline{H_{1}},\overline{H_{2}}
in
\widehat{G}
lie in the same
\mathrm{Aut}(\widehat{G})
-orbit.

Proof.

By hypothesis there is an isomorphism
\alpha:H_{1}\to H_{2}
that identifies the induced distributions on
\mathrm{Hom}(-,K)
for every finite
K
. Since
G
is LERF and the
H_{i}
are finitely generated, the natural maps
H_{i}\to\overline{H_{i}}
induce isomorphisms between the profinite completions
\widehat{H_{i}}
and the closures
\overline{H_{i}}\subseteq\widehat{G}
. The isomorphism
\alpha
therefore extends uniquely to a continuous isomorphism
\overline{\alpha}:\overline{H_{1}}\to\overline{H_{2}}
. Because the distributions induced by
H_{i}
agree on all finite targets, the same identities hold for the closures in the profinite completion; explicitly, for every finite
K
and every
\beta\in\mathrm{Hom}({H_{2}},K)
inducing
\overline{\beta}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\overline{H_{2}},K)
, we have

\mathrm{Pr}_{\overline{H_{1}},K}(\overline{\beta}\circ\overline{\alpha})=\mathrm{Pr}_{H_{1},K}(\beta\circ\alpha)=\mathrm{Pr}_{H_{2},K}(\beta)=\mathrm{Pr}_{\overline{H_{2}},K}(\overline{\beta}).

Applying Lemma
4
in the profinite group
\widehat{G}
yields an automorphism
\widetilde{\alpha}\in\mathrm{Aut}(\widehat{G})
with
\widetilde{\alpha}|_{\overline{H_{1}}}=\overline{\alpha}
. In particular
\widetilde{\alpha}(\overline{H_{1}})=\overline{H_{2}}
, as required.
∎

Remark 6
.

It can happen that a word
w
and its inverse
w^{-1}
in a free group
F_{n}
induce different measures on finite groups, while the cyclic subgroups
\langle w\rangle
and
\langle w^{-1}\rangle
induce the same distribution. In that situation Proposition
5
only guarantees that the closures
\overline{\langle w\rangle}
and
\overline{\langle w^{-1}\rangle}
are automorphic in
\widehat{G}
; they do not produce an automorphism taking
w
to
w^{-1}
in the discrete group.

We now formalise the subgroup-version of profinite rigidity used in this paper.

Definition 7
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated LERF group. A finitely generated subgroup
H\leq G
is
profinitely rigid
(in
G
) if whenever
L\leq G
is a finitely generated subgroup that induces the same probability measures on
\mathrm{Hom}(-,K)
for every finite
K
, then there exists
\phi\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)
with
\phi(H)=L
.

The next theorem shows that profinite rigidity of tuples and of the subgroups they generate are equivalent notions.

Theorem 4
.

Let
F_{n}
be a free group and let
w_{1},\dots,w_{k}\in F_{n}
. The tuple
(w_{1},\dots,w_{k})
is profinitely rigid (as a
k
-tuple) if and only if the subgroup
H=\langle w_{1},\dots,w_{k}\rangle
is profinitely rigid (as a subgroup).

Proof.

Suppose first that the tuple
(w_{1},\dots,w_{k})
is profinitely rigid. Let
L\leq F_{n}
be a finitely generated subgroup that induces the same measures on
\mathrm{Hom}(-,K)
as
H
. Then, by Proposition
5
,
\overline{H}
and
\overline{L}
are in the same
\mathrm{Aut}(\widehat{F_{n}})
-orbit; hence there is
\Theta\in\mathrm{Aut}(\widehat{F_{n}})
with
\Theta(\overline{H})=\overline{L}
. We also get the generating
k
-tuple
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}
of
L
, where
\Theta(w_{i})=u_{i}
. By profinite rigidity of the tuple
(w_{1},\dots,w_{k})
, there exists
\phi\in\mathrm{Aut}(F_{n})
with
\phi(w_{i})=u_{i}
for all
i
. Hence
\phi(H)=L
, and
H
is profinitely rigid.

Conversely, suppose
H=\langle w_{1},\dots,w_{k}\rangle
is profinitely rigid and let two
k
-tuples
(w_{1},\dots,w_{k})
and
(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})
induce the same measures on all finite groups. By Theorem
3
, there exists
\Theta\in\mathrm{Aut}(\widehat{F_{n}})
with
\Theta(w_{i})=u_{i}
for all
i
. Hence
\Theta(H)=H^{\prime}:=\langle u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\rangle
, so
H
and
H^{\prime}
are profinitely automorphic. By profinite rigidity of
H
there exists
\phi\in\mathrm{Aut}(F_{n})
with
\phi(H)=H^{\prime}
such that
\Theta|_{H}=\phi
. Thus the two tuples are automorphic in
F_{n}
, proving the equivalence.
∎

Corollary 8
.

A single word
w\in F_{n}
is profinitely rigid if and only if the cyclic subgroup
\langle w\rangle
is profinitely rigid.

Thus Shalev’s conjecture about the profinite rigidity of a word of a free group can be seen as a rank one subgroup rigidity problem.

Corollary 9
.

If
(w_{1},\dots,w_{k})
is a tuple in
F_{n}
whose generated subgroup is a free factor of
F_{n}
, then the tuple is profinitely rigid.

Proof.

Every free factor is profinitely rigid
[
4
]
), so the result follows from Theorem
4
.
∎

The next theorem shows how profinite rigidity behaves under free product decompositions when the factors carry profinitely rigid subgroups.

Theorem 5
.

Let
F_{n}=A_{1}*\cdots*A_{k}
be a free product decomposition of the free group
F_{n}
. For each
i
let
H_{i}\leq A_{i}
be a finitely generated subgroup which is profinitely rigid (as a subgroup of
F_{n}
). Then the subgroup

H=\langle H_{1},\dots,H_{k}\rangle

is profinitely rigid in
F_{n}
.

Proof.

Let
L\leq F_{n}
be a finitely generated subgroup that induces the same
probability measures as
H
on
\mathrm{Hom}(-,K)
for every finite group
K
.
Then there exists an isomorphism
\alpha:H\to L
arising from the equality
of measures. For each
i=1,\dots,k
, set
L_{i}:=\alpha(H_{i})
.

By Lemma
4
, the isomorphism
\alpha
extends to a continuous
automorphism
\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Aut}(\widehat{F_{n}})
. Consequently,
each pair
H_{i}
and
L_{i}
are profinitely automorphic subgroups of
F_{n}
.
Since each
H_{i}
is profinitely rigid, there exists an automorphism

\beta_{i}\in\mathrm{Aut}(F_{n})
extending
\alpha|_{H_{i}}
, and hence

\beta_{i}(H_{i})=L_{i}
.

Consider the free product decomposition

F_{n}=A_{1}*A_{2}*\cdots*A_{k}.

For
i\neq j
, define

A_{i,j}:=\beta_{i}(A_{i})\cap\beta_{j}(A_{j}).

Since intersections of free factors are again free factors, we obtain
a refined free product decomposition

F_{n}=A^{\prime}_{1}*A^{\prime}_{2}*\cdots*A^{\prime}_{k}*A,

where
A
is generated by the subgroups
A_{i,j}
for
i\neq j
,
and each
A^{\prime}_{i}
is a free factor contained in
\beta_{i}(A_{i})
such that

A^{\prime}_{i}\cap A^{\prime}_{j}=\{1\}
whenever
i\neq j
.

Let
\eta_{i}:=\beta_{i}|_{A^{\prime}_{i}}
. Then the images
\eta_{i}(A^{\prime}_{i})
are free
factors with trivial pairwise intersections, and moreover

\eta_{i}(H_{i})=L_{i}
for all
i
. Define a homomorphism

\eta:F_{n}\longrightarrow F_{n}

by extending each
\eta_{i}
on
A^{\prime}_{i}
and taking the identity on
A
.
By the universal property of free products,
\eta
is an automorphism
of
F_{n}
. By construction,
\eta|_{H}=\alpha
, and therefore

\eta(H)=L
.

Hence any finitely generated subgroup inducing the same measures as
H

is automorphic to
H
in
F_{n}
, proving that
H
is profinitely rigid.
∎

As an application, we have the following results.

Corollary 10
.

Let
w_{1},\dots,w_{r}\in F_{n}
be either primitive words or partial surface words, and suppose
F_{n}
admits a free decomposition
F_{n}=H_{1}*\cdots*H_{r}*\cdots
with
w_{i}\in H_{i}
. Then any subgroup of the form

\langle w_{1}^{m_{1}},\dots,w_{r}^{m_{r}}\rangle

is profinitely rigid.

Proof.

Primitive words and partial surface words are profinitely rigid by
[
4
,
1
]
. Hanany–Puder–Meiri
[
3
]
show that powers of profinitely rigid words remain profinitely rigid. The result then follows from Theorem
5
.
∎

Corollary 11
.

Subgroups of the form
\langle x_{1}^{m_{1}},\dots,x_{r}^{m_{r}}\rangle
in
F_{n}
are profinitely rigid.

Emergent Geometry Governs Optimal Control in Driven Stokes Flows
Kyle I. McKee

Abstract
In a canonical Stokes flow geometry, the Hele–Shaw cell, we show that tunable circulations induced by Lorentz forces in a conducting fluid enable particle control. We reveal that energy-optimal control paths correspond to geodesics of an emergent Riemannian metric, which are time-optimal under a maximum-power constraint. Particle paths exhibit metric-governed anisotropic diffusion under random boundary forcing. Our geometric concepts, though developed explicitly for circulation-driven Hele-Shaw flows, generalize to generic driven Stokes flows and so elucidate recent observations in a three-dimensional context.

Optimal Control–
We have considered the control of

To recap, solutions to a sequence of

Generic features of

Nonuniqueness–
Because

Time-Optimality–
Having found the most energy-efficient path between points, one is naturally left wondering about time optimality:
which trajectory minimizes the transport time between two points, subject to a power constraint of the form
P(\tau)\leq P_{\mathrm{max}}
for all time?
As is proved in Appendix

P(t)=P_{\mathrm{max}}

Extensions in 3D and Conclusion–
We have demonstrated that tuneable circulation can be harnessed for control. Controllability is governed by a Riemannian metric that is emergent from the geometry of conducting obstacles and strongly influenced by system symmetries. Energy- and time-optimal control paths correspond to geodesics of this metric. Subject to random circulation forcing, particles diffuse anisotropically according to the metric. While we focused on potential flows with circulation, our geometric ideas apply generally to Stokes flows.

In recent 3D experiments, particles were controlled by rotating disks mounted on
N_{d}
walls of a cube
Amoudruz et al. 2025

. Random boundary forcing yields anisotropic diffusion according to
g^{-1}
. A plot of ellipsoids in the cube, with semiaxes corresponding to eigenvalues and eigendirections of
g^{-1}
, presumably yields flattened ellipsoids tangent to the 2D manifold observed in Fig. 3 of Amoudruz et al.
Amoudruz et al. 2025
. Understanding metrics and geodesics in other Stokes flows (e.g., those driven by electromagnets

Appendix A
Electrical Power Expression

The electrical dissipation in the fluid domain is given by,

P=\int_{D}\bm{J}\bm{\cdot}\bm{E}=\sigma\int_{D}\nabla V(\bm{x})\cdot\nabla V(\bm{x})dV,

(12)

which is clearly positive definite. Noting that
V
is harmonic,
\nabla^{2}V=0
, one finds via the divergence theorem that

P=h\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sigma V_{k}\int_{\partial B_{k}}\sigma\nabla V(\bm{x})\cdot\bm{n}dA=h\sum_{k=1}^{N}V_{k}I_{k},

(13)

where
\bm{n}
is the normal direction pointing out of the fluid so that
I_{k}
is the electrical current exiting the
j^{\mathrm{th}}
body into the fluid. Noting that the depth-averaged circulation is given by
\Gamma_{k}=-hB_{0}I_{k}/\left(12\mu\right)
from (
1
), the electrical power can be written as

P=-\frac{12\mu}{B_{0}}\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}V_{k}\Gamma_{k}.

(14)

Without loss of generality, the
N^{\mathrm{th}}
term of the summation has been ignored since as we fix
V_{N}=0
in our boundary value problem. The voltage
V_{k}
can be written in terms of the known flow solution,

V_{k}=\frac{12\mu}{h^{2}\sigma B_{0}}\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\Gamma_{j}\mathrm{Im}\left\{W_{j}(\partial B_{k})\right\},

(15)

so that the power can be written as,

P=-\frac{144\mu^{2}}{h^{2}\sigma B_{0}^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\Gamma_{k}\Gamma_{j}\mathrm{Im}\left\{W_{j}(\partial B_{k})\right\}.

(16)

Appendix B
Power-Minimizing Control via Lagrange Multiplier Approach

To find the power-minimizing solution, we use a Lagrange multiplier approach and examine the following function,

f(\bm{x},\bm{\lambda})=\bm{\Gamma}^{T}M\bm{\Gamma}+\bm{\lambda}^{T}\left(\Omega\bm{\Gamma}-\dot{\bm{x}}\right),

(17)

where
\bm{\lambda}
represents our Lagrange multiplier vector.

Differentiating with respect to
\bm{\Gamma}
and setting to zero, we find,

2M\bm{\Gamma}=-\Omega^{T}\bm{\lambda}.

(18)

Plugging into the constraint equation, we find that,

\bm{\lambda}=-2\left(\Omega M^{-1}\Omega^{T}\right)^{-1}\dot{\bm{x}},

(19)

which together with (
18
), yields the power-minimizing solution,

\bm{\Gamma}=M^{-1}\Omega^{T}\left(\Omega M^{-1}\Omega^{T}\right)^{-1}\dot{\bm{x}}.

(20)

Appendix C
Derivation of Geodesic Equation

Considering the Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}=g_{kl}\dot{x}_{k}\dot{x}_{l}
, the Euler-Lagrange equations
d\left(\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial\dot{x}_{p}\right)/dt-\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial x_{p}=0
become,

\displaystyle 0=\frac{d}{dt}\left(2g_{kp}\dot{x}_{k}\right)-\frac{\partial g_{kl}}{\partial x_{p}}\dot{x}_{k}\dot{x}_{l}

(21)

\displaystyle 0=2\frac{\partial g_{kp}}{\partial x_{q}}\dot{x}_{q}\dot{x}_{k}+2g_{kp}\ddot{x}_{k}-\frac{\partial g_{kl}}{\partial x_{p}}\dot{x}_{k}\dot{x}_{l}.

(22)

By noting that for any real square matrix
B
,
\bm{x}^{T}B\bm{x}=\bm{x}^{T}\left(B+B^{T}\right)\bm{x}/2
, we find that the latter equation reduces to,

0=\frac{\partial g_{qp}}{\partial x_{k}}\dot{x}_{q}\dot{x}_{k}+\frac{\partial g_{kp}}{\partial x_{q}}\dot{x}_{q}\dot{x}_{k}+2g_{kp}\ddot{x}_{k}-\frac{\partial g_{kl}}{\partial x_{p}}\dot{x}_{k}\dot{x}_{l}.

(23)

Multiplying by the inverse of the metric and rearranging dummy indices gives,

\ddot{x}_{m}=-\frac{1}{2}g_{mp}^{-1}\left(\frac{\partial g_{qp}}{\partial x_{k}}+\frac{\partial g_{kp}}{\partial x_{q}}-\frac{\partial g_{kq}}{\partial x_{p}}\right)\dot{x}_{k}\dot{x}_{q},

(24)

from which (
10
) follows.

Appendix D
Proof of Time-Optimality

We herein deduce the form of the trajectory that minimizes the transport time between two points, subject to maximum power constraint of the form
P(\tau)\leq P_{\mathrm{max}}
for all time. The proof is laid out in two parts. We first deduce that at each moment, it is sufficient to consider the power-minimizing control (
7
) when seeking a time-optimal solution. Then, restricting our attention to such controls, we prove that the geodesic trajectory is time-optimal and that it should be traversed with constant power
P_{\mathrm{max}}
.

First, we note that when controlling a trajectory
\bm{x}(t)
, the circulation only takes the form (
7
) if we choose to minimize instantaneous power. It is not immediately clear that this choice should also correspond to time-optimality, though we will now show this to be the case.
Suppose that there were a time-optimal control, corresponding to the trajectory
\bm{x}(t)
, that utilizes a control different to (
7
). Let
P_{\mathrm{o}}(t)
be the power corresponding to that control, which is achieved over the interval
t\in[0,T_{\mathrm{end}}]
.
Then, the power-optimal control for that trajectory has the property that
\dot{\bm{x}}^{T}g\dot{\bm{x}}\leq P_{\mathrm{o}}(t)
for all time. Moreover, consider a time re-parametrization of the trajectory
\bm{x}(t)
, given by
\tilde{\bm{x}}(t)=\bm{x}(f(t))
. The power required by the power-optimal control to achieve this trajectory is then given by
\left(\dot{f}(t)\right)^{2}\dot{\bm{x}}(f(t))^{T}g\dot{\bm{x}}(f(t))
on the interval
t\in[0,T_{f}]
with
T_{f}
defined by
f(T_{f})=T_{\mathrm{end}}
. Now we let
f(t)
by defined by the following initial-value-problem,

\displaystyle\dot{f}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle+\sqrt{\frac{P_{\mathrm{o}}(f(t))}{\dot{\bm{x}}(f(t))^{T}g\dot{\bm{x}}(f(t))}},

(25)

\displaystyle f(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

(26)

Since for all times,
P_{\mathrm{o}}(t)\geq\dot{\bm{x}}(f(t))^{T}g\dot{\bm{x}}(f(t))
, it follows that
T_{\mathrm{f}}\leq T_{\mathrm{end}}
. By construction, the power associated with this trajectory is given exactly by
P_{o}(t)
. We have thus shown that given a trajectory
\bm{x}(t)
with a generic control and associated power
P_{o}(t)
, it is always possible to construct a trajectory which uses the power optimal control (
7
) and for the same power expenditure traverses the curve defined by
\bm{x}(t)
in lesser or equal time. Thus while seeking the time-optimal control we may restrict our attention to controls that, at each instant, employ the power-optimal control (
7
).

Restricting our attention to such controls we now seek the trajectory which, subject to the power constraint
P\leq P_{\mathrm{max}}
, achieves the fastest transport from
\bm{x}_{\mathrm{A}}
to
\bm{x}_{\mathrm{B}}
. Suppose that the time optimal trajectory is given by
\bm{x}(\tau)
over the time interval
[0,T]
where
\bm{x}(0)=\bm{x}_{A}
and
\bm{x}(T)=\bm{x}_{B}
. Suppose now that
\bm{x}(\tau)
is
not
derived from the geodesic equation laid out in (
10
), and we shall proceed toward a contradiction. Consider the energy functional,

\displaystyle\epsilon[\bm{x}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\dot{\bm{x}}(\tau)^{T}g(\bm{x}(\tau))\dot{\bm{x}}(\tau)d\tau

(27)

\displaystyle\bm{x}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bm{x}_{A}

(28)

\displaystyle\bm{x}(T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bm{x}_{B},

(29)

whose energy-optimal (
\epsilon
-minimizing) solution is given by the Euler-Lagrange equations. Let
\bm{x}_{\mathrm{geod}}
be the corresponding geodesic trajectory. Since this trajectory optimizes the energy among all trajectories, we can be sure that
\epsilon[\bm{x}_{\mathrm{geod}}]\leq\epsilon[\bm{x}]
. By the constraint of our problem statement, we also have
\epsilon[\bm{x}]\leq P_{\mathrm{max}}T
. Noting that geodesics correspond to constant-power trajectories (since the Hamiltonian and Lagrangian are equal and time-independent), we have that
\epsilon[\bm{x}_{\mathrm{geod}}]=P_{\mathrm{geod}}T
, where
P_{\mathrm{geod}}
is the constant geodesic power. Therefore the inequality
\epsilon[\bm{x}_{\mathrm{geod}}]\leq\epsilon[\bm{x}]
implies the weakened inequality
P_{\mathrm{geod}}\leq P_{\mathrm{max}}
. Thus, a geodesic path can always be found that both (1) satisfies the power constraint and (2) connects two points in equal or lesser time as compared to
\bm{x}(\tau)
. Moreover, the fastest geodesic is found by taking
P_{\mathrm{geod}}=P_{\mathrm{max}}
.

We thus deduce that
the time-optimal trajectory under the constraint that
P(t)\leq P_{\mathrm{max}}
is the geodesic curve traversed with
P(t)=P_{\mathrm{max}}
.
To achieve this particular trajectory in practice, one can initialize velocities in the shooting method for finding geodesics so that the power condition is initially satisfied,
\dot{\bm{x}}(0)^{T}g(\bm{x}_{\mathrm{A}})\dot{\bm{x}}(0)=P_{\mathrm{max}}
, which will then be preserved automatically by the conservative dynamics.

Appendix E
Three-dimensional Stokes Flow – Rotating Disks in a Cube

Consider Stokes flow inside a cube
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
with rotating disks mounted on
N_{d}
of its walls, with controllable rotation rates
\bm{\omega}=(\omega_{1},\cdots,\omega_{N_{d}})^{T}
. Let
\partial C_{k}
denote the surface of the
k^{\mathrm{th}}
rotating disk for
k\in\{1,\cdots,N_{d}\}
. The fluid flow can then be written as
\bm{u}(\bm{x})=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{d}}\omega_{k}\bm{u}_{k}(\bm{x})
, where
\bm{u}_{k}(\bm{x})
is the flow corresponding to
\omega_{k}=1
and
\omega_{j\neq k}=0
. We then define
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times N_{d}}
, by
\Omega=(\bm{u}_{1},\cdots,\bm{u}_{N_{d}})
. The control problem is then given by (
4
), where now
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times N_{d}}
and
\bm{x}\in C
.

The power required to generate a flow
\bm{u}
is given by
\int_{\partial C}\bm{u}\bm{\cdot}\bm{\sigma}\bm{\cdot}\bm{n}dS
, with
\bm{\sigma}=\mu\left(\nabla\bm{u}+\nabla\bm{u}^{T}\right)
, where pressure has dropped out because boundaries only
rotate
, from which it follows that
\bm{u}\bm{\cdot}\bm{n}=0
on all surfaces. Substituting our expression for the velocity field, we find that,

\displaystyle P=\sum_{q=1}^{N_{d}}\sum_{r=1}^{N_{d}}\mu\omega_{q}\omega_{r}M_{qr}\equiv\mu\bm{\omega}^{T}M\bm{\omega},

(30)

\displaystyle M_{qr}=\int_{\partial C_{q}}\bm{u}_{q}\bm{\cdot}\left(\nabla\bm{u}_{r}+\nabla\bm{u}_{r}^{T}\right)\bm{\cdot}\bm{n}dS,

(31)

where
M
is positive definite and symmetric. Positive definiteness follows from the fact (
30
) is equal to the integral of a positive definite quantity over
C
. Symmetry follows from the reciprocal theorem
Kim and Karrila 2013
. With these definitions of
\Omega
and
M
, equations (
7
) and (
10
) hold with a metric still defined by
g=\left(\Omega M^{-1}\Omega^{T}\right)^{-1}
. Particles forced by random boundary disk rotations then diffuse according to the anisotropic diffusion tensor
g^{-1}=\Omega M^{-1}\Omega^{T}
.

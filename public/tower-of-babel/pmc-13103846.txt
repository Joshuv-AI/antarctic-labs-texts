Transient Flow Characterization between Matrix and Natural Fractures in Shale Oil Reservoir
Authors: Zhuocong Wang, Sen Wang, Jingyu Liu, Wei Zhang, Dongling Xia
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The transient flow between matrix and natural fractures significantly impacts the exploitation of shale oil reservoirs. Existing characterization models, however, primarily target conventional fractured reservoirs, neglecting the specific mechanisms of shale, including nonlinear flow and stress sensitivity. A new characterization model for shale matrix-natural fracture flow is developed by incorporating these critical mechanisms. The shale reservoir flow equations were first constructed considering nonlinear flow and the stress sensitivity of the matrix. Then, the governing equations are solved numerically using a fully implicit scheme. Based on the equations, the dimensionless shape factor for matrix-fracture flow is derived via the material balance method. Our results demonstrate the time-dependent behavior of the shape factor in shale reservoirs, with values lower than in conventional fractured reservoirs except during initial flow periods. More pronounced nonlinear flow characteristics in the shale matrix result in smaller dimensionless shape factors. In addition, increased stress sensitivity in the shale matrix leads to more significant permeability reduction, yielding smaller dimensionless shape factors. An empirical correlation between dimensionless shape factor and dimensionless time is established through multiple numerical simulations and regression analysis. This model improves matrix-fracture flow characterization in shale systems and can be employed in reservoir numerical simulations to accurately predict the well-production performance, which is essential for the efficient exploitation of shale oil.

Introduction

Shale oil reservoirs, a pivotal resource in the energy sector, are progressively becoming a cornerstone of global energy strategies due to their vast reserves, extensive geographical distribution, and immense development potential. − Recent strides in hydraulic fracturing and horizontal drilling technologies have paved new avenues for the economically viable exploitation of these once-elusive shale oil resources, rendering their efficient development a reality. ,,

Within shale oil reservoirs, the intricate network of natural fractures intertwines with the matrix system, forming a distinctive dual-porosity structure. This geological configuration, characterized by pronounced differences in storage capacity and fluid flow characteristics between the matrix and fractures, dictates the intricate pathways of oil migration. Oil initially seeps slowly from the dense matrix, subsequently surging into the natural fracture network, which acts as efficient fluid conduits, significantly accelerating oil flow. Eventually, the oil converges into hydraulic fractures and is extracted to the surface through meticulously designed wellbore systems. Consequently, a profound understanding of the flow mechanisms between the matrix and natural fractures is imperative for unraveling the flow patterns in fractured reservoirs, precisely forecasting production performance, and optimizing development strategies.

In numerical simulation, researchers continually delve into innovative approaches to accurately replicate the complex fluid transport processes within shale oil reservoirs. Traditionally, discrete fracture models (DFM) and their advanced iterations, such as embedded discrete fracture models (EDFM), have been extensively employed in shale oil reservoir simulations, and the Multiple Interacting Continua (MINC) model also has relevant applications. These models strive to recreate the authentic geological setting by meticulously delineating the morphology and attributes of natural fractures. − These models are applicable to large and sparsely distributed natural fractures. However, for reservoirs characterized by extensive and abundant natural fractures, the utilization of discrete fracture modeling to simulate these fractures often results in complex model construction and prolonged computation times. −

To address this issue, the dual-porosity (DP) model has gained widespread adoption for naturally fractured reservoirs rich in fractures. , Under pseudosteady-state conditions, the transfer between the matrix and fractures occurs within a unit volume, and the mass transfer rate per unit volume is directly proportional to the difference between the fracture pressure and the average matrix block pressure, as given by 1 q=kmρμσ(p̅m−pf) where, q is the fluid exchange rate between the matrix and fractures, g/(cm3·s); k m is the matrix permeability, μm2; μ is the fluid viscosity, mPa·s; ρ is the fluid density, g/cm3; p̅ m is the average matrix block pressure, 10–1 MPa; p f is the fracture pressure, 10–1 MPa; σ is the shape factor, 1/cm2. Studies on Shape Factors in Unconventional Reservoirs

Flow between matrix and natural fractures is a critical process in shale reservoir development, requiring precise characterization. However, existing flow characterization models exhibit significant limitations. Extensive studies − demonstrate complex flow regimes in shale matrix due to the nanoconfinement effect, showing strong nonlinear flow characteristics. Moreover, the matrix exhibits notable stress sensitivityarising from pore structure alterations and dynamic permeability attenuation induced by effective stress changes. Current widely used characterization models for shale matrix-natural fracture flow have not adequately incorporated the coupled effects of nonlinear flow and stress sensitivity, limiting their predictive accuracy and practical applicability.

To address these challenges, this study develops a transient flow model for the shale oil matrix and natural fractures, taking into account both shale reservoir stress sensitivity and nonlinear flow behavior in shale oil. The effects of nonlinear flow and stress sensitivity on matrix-natural fracture flow behavior were analyzed, and a general dimensionless shape factor model was established, providing practical guidance for shale oil production forecasting.

Shale Properties

Nonlinear Fluid Flow in Shale Oil Matrix

Numerous studies demonstrate − extensive development of nanoscale pore networks within shale matrices. These nanoconfined spaces (Figure a) fundamentally alter fluid physicochemical behavior and flow mechanisms, creating substantial divergences from conventional reservoirs. Pronounced adsorption of crude oil components onto nanopore walls (particularly organic pore walls) significantly reduces movable oil saturation and affects phase behavior. The adsorption layer and hydration film on the surface of the pore wall lead to the formation of low-flow or even nonflowing boundary layers in the pores, significantly reducing the effective flow pore size of the fluid. These mechanisms lead to obvious nonlinear flow characteristics in shale oil flow.

With fluid permeability of 0.3 × 10–3 μm2 and viscosity of 4.5 mPa·s, the relationship between pressure gradient and flow velocity for shale oil is obtained as shown in Figure b. Results demonstrate a nonlinear correlation between displacement pressure gradient and flow rate during oil flow in shale reservoirs, indicating Darcy’s law fails to accurately characterize this behavior.

Wang et al. propose that non-Darcy flow comprises two components: (1) At low pressure gradients, flow is nonlinear. (2) When the pressure gradient reaches a specific value, flow transitions to linear. Fluid flow initiates instantaneously when the pressure gradient exceeds zero (>0), confirming the absence of a threshold pressure gradient. Building upon this, they devise a specific nonlinear fluid flow in porous media equation aimed at overcoming these limitations and achieving a more accurate and comprehensive depiction of fluid flow characteristics within shale reservoirs. The formula is as follows: 2 v=−kmμ∇p(11+aeb∇p) where v is the flow velocity, m/s; p is the pressure, MPa; a and b are coefficients characterizing the intensity of nonlinear flow behavior, obtained by fitting experimental data. Parameter a quantifies the apparent flow resistance induced by fluid–solid interactions in nanopores, specifically attributing to boundary layers or adsorption films that effectively reduce the hydraulic radius. This resistance mechanism is dominant at low pressure gradients. Parameter b governs the transition rate from the non-Darcy to the Darcy flow regime. As the pressure gradient increases, the retarding effect of surface forces diminishes relative to the driving pressure forces, and the flow velocity asymptotically approaches a linear relationship. Here, b determines how rapidly this nonlinear effect attenuates.

From the formulation of eq , it is evident that the nonlinear flow equation introduces a significant additional term compared to the traditional equation (such as Darcy’s law), namely, the nonlinear flow term. Traditional Darcy flow primarily applies to media with high porosity and linear fluid flow characteristics. However, shale reservoirs typically exhibit low porosity and nanopore sizes, leading to nonlinear fluid flow behavior. The incorporation of this term enables nonlinear flow equations to more accurately characterize the complex fluid dynamics within shale reservoirs. For instance, with parameters set as a = 1.36, b = 1.1, the relationship between pressure gradient and flow velocity under the same pressure gradient is depicted in Figure , achieving good agreement with experimental data.

It is worth noting that while the coefficients a and b in eq are determined by fitting macroscopic experimental data in this study, they intrinsically represent the upscaled manifestation of multiscale transport mechanisms governed by pore geometry and fluid–solid interactions. Studies utilizing pore-scale modeling have provided rigorous theoretical foundations for these nonlinear flow characteristics. ,− Specifically, molecular dynamics (MD) simulations have been extensively used to elucidate transport mechanisms in nanopores, such as adsorption, which fundamentally determine the flow resistance represented by parameter a. , To bridge the gap between pore-scale physics and continuum models, upscaling methods like Pore Network Modeling (PNM) and the Lattice Boltzmann Method (LBM) are essential. For instance, Yu et al. utilized these multiscale approaches to demonstrate how pore size distribution and connectivity quantitatively impact nonlinear flow regimes. Similarly, subsequent studies , have successfully characterized the transition from linear to nonlinear flow in complex porous media using LBM. Therefore, the parameters a and b in our model serve as effective coefficients that lump these complex pore-scale heterogeneities and multiscale transport effects into a continuum representation.

Stress Sensitivity of Shale Matrix

Shale reservoirs exhibit pronounced stress sensitivity due to extensive nanoscale pore networks, abundant bedding planes/natural microfractures, and clay mineral content. Stress sensitivity is defined as the phenomenon where declining pore fluid pressure increases effective stress on the rock framework. This induces deformation of pore structuresparticularly in microfractures and nanoscale throatsultimately causing significant nonlinear attenuation in absolute permeability. Extensive experimental studies confirm significant stress sensitivity in shale. − To quantitatively characterize this property, shale samples were tested using the pulse decay method, obtaining permeability under varied effective stress conditions (Figure ). Results demonstrate that shale matrix permeability decreases with increasing effective stress. Such stress sensitivity is typically described by exponential or power-law relationships between permeability and effective stress. −

Studies by Riepe et al. and Debschütz et al. demonstrate that sandstone permeability decreases with increasing effective stress following a negative exponential relationship. Model Establishment

A transient flow characterization model for shale matrix-natural fracture systems was derived and formulated by incorporating nonlinear flow and stress sensitivity mechanisms. Initially, flow equations accounting for these mechanisms were constructed and solved, yielding spatiotemporal pressure distributions within the matrix. Since the pressure within the matrix is not uniform, the average pressure at each moment is calculated to represent the overall pressure variation of the matrix. The flow of fluid from the matrix to the fractures is determined using the balance equations. Finally, by applying the definition of matrix-fracture flow, the shape factor’s variation over time and space is solved. A flowchart of this computational approach is shown in Figure .

Model Construction

Shale exhibits prominent characteristics of bedding plane fracture development, with a clear laminated bedding structure within its interior. These bedding planes are not only well-developed but are also accompanied by unfilled bedding plane fractures and partially filled high-angle structural fractures. −

Kazemi et al. modeled the matrix system in a dual-porosity reservoir as a set of parallel thin rectangular prisms, with thin layers between these prisms simulating the fracture system. The stimulated reservoir exhibits an alternating distribution pattern of matrix layers and fracture layers. The bedding structure of shale, as shown in Figure a, resembles that conceptualized in the Kazemi model. However, this model fails to incorporate nonlinear flow and stress sensitivity mechanisms inherent to shale reservoirs, constraining its capability to accurately characterize matrix-fracture flow in actual shale oil reservoirs.

Influence of Stress Sensitivity Effect

For the parameters in Table , a sensitivity analysis is conducted on the stress-sensitive coefficient by varying its values within the range of 0.05 to 0.25. The dimensionless shape factor under different stress-sensitive coefficients is illustrated in Figure . As depicted in Figure , the influence of the stress-sensitive coefficient on the shape factor cannot be overlooked, particularly during the early to midstages of flow. This phenomenon originates from the sharp pressure drop near fractures during early flow (Figure a), which causes a sharp increase in effective stress, leading to significant permeability attenuation (eq ). At this stage, the larger the β value, the higher the degree of permeability attenuation, thereby more significantly weakening flow capacity. During late-stage flow, matrix pressure tends toward dynamic equilibrium, effective stress gradually stabilizes, and the impact differences of different β values on flow capacity consequently diminish. An increase in the stress-sensitive coefficient leads to a decrease in the shape factor at the same dimensionless time. For instance, at t D = 0.12, the shape factors for shale with β of 0.05 and 0.25 are 8.28 and 5.96, respectively.

Empirical Model for Shape Factor

The simulation results demonstrate that the matrix-fracture flow process in shale reservoirs follows a distinct two-stage evolution. Consequently, a double-exponential function was selected as the empirical correlation model. The first exponential term captures the rapid decline of the shape factor in the early stage, primarily driven by the sharp increase in effective stress and the corresponding permeability loss near the fractures. The second exponential term characterizes the slow, long-term decay attributed to the transition from Darcy flow to strongly nonlinear flow as pressure gradients decreases. Compared with single-exponential or power-law models, the double-exponential formulation offers superior flexibility, allowing for an accurate fit of both transient and quasi-steady-state behaviors throughout the entire production cycle. Accordingly, we established the following empirical correlation for the dimensionless shape factor: 26 σD=hejtD+uewtD where h, j, u, and w are the parameters to be fitted, which are linked to the nonlinear flow parameters and stress sensitivity coefficient. The double-exponential form of the empirical formula reflects the two-stage attenuation characteristics of the matrix-fracture flow efficiency in shale reservoirs. Parameters h and u represent the magnitude coefficients of the fast and slow flow components, respectively. They characterize the effective transfer capacity of the matrix block under initial and pseudosteady conditions. Parameters j and w are decay rate constants, representing the speed at which the shape factor diminishes as the pressure disturbance propagates and effective stress evolves. Specifically, the term with the larger decay constant j captures the rapid early stage drop in σ D caused by the sharp increase in effective stress near fractures, while the term with the smaller constant w describes the long-term stabilization of transfer efficiency influenced by the coupled effect of nonlinear flow and residual stress sensitivity. Discussion

Applying the proposed transient flow model to real-world three-dimensional multiscale formations presents significant computational challenges. This is primarily because calculating the shape factor for every matrix block at each time step and coupling it with nonlinear governing equations is a highly rigorous process. To mitigate these costs, studies suggest that adaptive meshing techniques, such as the global-local approach and dynamic refinement strategies, can be employed to optimize grid resolution in regions with high pressure gradients. Concurrently, the integration of artificial intelligence has propelled the optimization of reservoir simulation. While foundational data-driven frameworks demonstrated early potential, the recent emergence of Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) has enabled robust forward modeling without explicit grid discretization.

By leveraging our established empirical formula for the shape factor in conjunction with advanced PINN applications in shale matrix-fracture systems, , transient shape factors can be predicted instantaneously, effectively replacing time-consuming iterative calculations. Furthermore, incorporating rapid solving technologies and nonlinear preconditioning is essential for accelerating convergence in strongly coupled systems. Consequently, integrating the proposed model with these modern computing frameworks offers a promising pathway to achieve accurate production dynamics prediction at the field scale with acceptable computational costs.

Conclusions

This study establishes a novel transient flow characterization model for shale oil matrix-natural fracture systems by incorporating critical mechanisms of nonlinear flow and matrix stress sensitivity. Our results indicate that the dimensionless shape factor (σ D ) exhibits significant temporal variation in shale reservoirs, contrasting sharply with the constant value (π2) in conventional fractured reservoirs. Due to the coupling effect of nonlinear flow and stress sensitivity, σ D gradually decreases as flow progresses, and the flow capacity gradually decreases. This behavior explains the slower matrix pressure depletion observed in shale systems.

The nonlinear flow intensity parameters a and b inversely controls σ D : A 100% increase in a (from 1.0 to 2.0) reduces σ D by 21% at t D = 0.2; At t D = 0.1, σ D decreases by 24% as b rises from 0.7 to 1.7. The nonlinear flow mechanism has a small impact during early flow and is mainly manifested in the middle to late stages of flow. This arises from the transition from Darcy-like flow (high initial pressure gradient) to strong nonlinear flow as pressure gradients decline. A higher stress sensitivity coefficient β accelerates permeability attenuation under increasing effective stress, reducing σ D by up to 28% (e.g., from 8.28 to 5.96 at t D = 0.12 when β increases from 0.05 to 0.25). This effect is most pronounced during early flow, where sharp pressure drops near fractures maximize permeability reduction.

A high-precision empirical model was established based on 600 sets of numerical simulations and multiple linear regression. The coefficients of this model are clearly correlated with nonlinear flow parameters and stress sensitivity coefficients. This study unifies transient matrix-fracture flow in shale oil reservoirs with coupled nonlinear and stress-sensitive mechanisms, providing a theoretical basis for optimizing development strategies and improving production prediction.

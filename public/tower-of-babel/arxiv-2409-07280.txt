S. Bardyla, P. Zlatoš

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate Schur ultrafilters on groups. Using the algebraic structure of Stone-Čech compactifications of discrete groups, we give a new description of Bohr compactifications of topological groups in terms of Schur ultrafilters. This approach allows us to characterize which compact Hausdorff admissible right topological groups (briefly, chart groups) are topological groups. Namely, a chart group G G is a topological group if and only if every Schur ultrafilter on G G converges to the unit of G G .

1.
Introduction

Ultrafilters on topological groups form a well-established topic in Topological Algebra with plenty of applications in Combinatorics and Topological Dynamics, see the monographs
[
30
,
51
]
and references therein. Schur ultrafilters were introduced by Protasov
[
44
]
, as a generalization of idempotent ultrafilters.
In this paper, we investigate Schur ultrafilters on groups and apply them to Bohr compactifications of topological groups and automatic continuity of group operations in chart groups.

Bohr compactifications of topological groups represent another widely studied topic,
see
[
9
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
20
,
29
]
. Since the structure of Bohr compactification is rather non-trivial, the results providing its description are of particular interest.
For instance, Anzai and Kakutani
[
1
]
described
Bohr compactifications of locally compact commutative groups using Pontryagin duality.
A connection between Bohr compactifications and Chu duality was established
by Ferrer and Hernández
[
14
]
.
Gismatullin,
Jagiella and Krupiński
[
19
]
used model theory to describe Bohr compactifications of
certain matrix groups. Bohr compactifications of the arithmetic, lamplighter and Heisenberg groups were described by Bekka in
[
3
,
4
]
. Davey, Haviar and Priestley
[
11
]
studied Bohr
compactifications of fairly general algebraic structures within a category-theoretical framework.
Hart and Kunen
[
27
,
28
]
investigated Bohr compactifications of discrete structures, including groups, rings and semilattices.
Yet another description of Bohr compactifications of discrete commutative groups was given in
[
54
]
, bringing to focus the algebraic structure of Stone-Čech compactifications and Schur ultrafilters. In the present paper, we develop the techniques from
[
54
]
and, using Schur ultrafilters, obtain a description of Bohr compactifications of all Hausdorff topological groups. In addition to Schur ultrafilters, a major role in this description is played by the
ultrafilter semigroup
of
G
, consisting of all ultrafilters convergent to the unit of the corresponding topological group
G
. The ultrafilter semigroups are of independent interest and have been studied by Zelenyuk in
[
52
,
53
]
.

Chart groups appear naturally in Topological Dynamics, as enveloping semigroups of particular dynamical systems, see
[
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
,
35
]
. One of the central problems in the theory of chart groups is when a chart group is a topological group. Namioka
[
42
]
showed that each metrizable chart group is a topological group. This result was later generalized by Moors and Namioka
[
41
]
for first-countable chart groups, by Glasner and Megrelishvili
[
25
]
for Fréchet-Urysohn chart groups, and by Reznichenko
[
46
]
for chart groups with countable tightness. The aforementioned problem is a partial case of a more general problem of automatic continuity of group operations, discussed in
[
2
,
12
,
13
,
15
,
37
,
39
,
48
]
. In this paper, using Schur ultrafilters, we find necessary and sufficient conditions for a chart group to be a topological group.
Also, we introduce and describe
a new universal compactification of right topological groups, which can be viewed as a counterpart of Bohr compactification in the class of chart groups.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section

3

2.
Preliminaries and the main results

In this paper, all topological spaces are assumed to be
Hausdorff
.
We identify the smallest infinite ordinal
\omega
with the set of all non-negative integers.
For an equivalence relation
\rho
on a set
X
and
x\in X
let
[x]_{\rho}=\{y\in X:(x,y)\in\rho\}
. Put
X/\rho=\{[x]_{\rho}:x\in X\}
. The map
h_{\rho}:X\rightarrow X/\rho
given by
h_{\rho}(x)=[x]_{\rho}
is called a
quotient map
. If the set
X
is equipped with a topology, then,
by default, we assume that
X/\rho
carries the
quotient topology
, i.e., the finest topology that makes the quotient map
h_{\rho}
continuous. An equivalence relation
\rho
on a topological space
X
is called
closed
if
\rho
is a closed subset of
X{\times}X
, equipped with the product topology.

For every element
s
of a
semigroup
S
the
right shift

\rho_{s}
is defined by
\rho_{s}(x)=xs
. Dually, for every
s\in S
the
left shift

\lambda_{s}
is defined by
\lambda_{s}(x)=sx
.
A semigroup
S
endowed with a topology
\tau
is called
right topological
if for each
s\in S
the right shift
\rho_{s}
is continuous.
A right topological semigroup
S
is called
semitopological
if for each
s\in S
the left shift
\lambda_{s}
is continuous. Following
[
30
]
, for a right
topological semigroup
S
the set

\Lambda(S)=\{s\in S:\lambda_{s}\hbox{ is continuous}\}

is called a
topological center
of
S
. A group
G
endowed with a topology is called a
topological group
if multiplication and inversion are continuous in
G
.

An equivalence relation
\rho
on a semigroup
S
is called a
congruence
if
(a_{1},b_{1})\in\rho
and
(a_{2},b_{2})\in\rho
imply
(a_{1}a_{2},b_{1}b_{2})\in\rho
for any

a_{1},b_{1},a_{2},b_{2}\in S
. The set
S/\rho
endowed with the semigroup
operation defined by
[x]_{\rho}[y]_{\rho}=[xy]_{\rho}
is called a
quotient semigroup
.
The quotient map
h_{\rho}:S\rightarrow S/\rho

is referred to as the
quotient homomorphism
. For any homomorphism
f
defined on a semigroup
S
let

\operatorname{Ker}(f)=\{(x,y)\in S{\times}S:f(x)=f(y)\}.

Clearly,
\operatorname{Ker}(f)
is a congruence on
S
. A congruence
\rho
on a semigroup
S
is called a
group congruence
if
S/\rho
is a group.
Since
S{\times}S
is a closed congruence on
S
and the intersection of any family of closed congruences is a closed congruence, for every relation
R\subseteq S\times S
there exists the least
closed congruence on
S
which contains
R
.

The Stone-Čech compactification
\beta X
of a discrete space
X
is the set of all ultrafilters on
X
endowed with a topology
\tau
given by the base
\mathcal{B}=\{\langle A\rangle:A\subseteq X\}
, where
\langle A\rangle=\{u\in\beta X:A\in u\}
. Recall that each element
x\in X
is identified with the principal ultrafilter
\{A\subseteq X:x\in A\}
. If
S
is a discrete semigroup, then the semigroup operation on
S
can be canonically lifted to a semigroup operation on
\beta S
as follows: if
u,v\in\beta S
, then
uv
is a filter generated by the base consisting of the sets
\bigcup_{x\in U}xV_{x}
, where
U\in u
and
\{V_{x}:x\in U\}\subset v
are arbitrary. Equivalently,

A\in uv\iff\{s\in S:\lambda_{s}^{-1}(A)\in v\}\in u.

By
[
30
, Theorem 4.1]
, the defined above semigroup operation on
\beta S
is unique among those extending the operation of
S
and satisfying the following two natural conditions:

(i)

for each
u\in\beta S
the right shift
\rho_{u}
is continuous;

(ii)

for each
s\in S
the left shift
\lambda_{s}
is continuous.

Thus, for any discrete semigroup
S
,
\beta S
endowed with the aforementioned operation is a compact right topological semigroup. For more about the algebraic structure of
\beta S
see the monograph
[
30
]
and references therein. Compact right topological semigroups have been extensively studied in the literature; of particular relevance to this
paper are
[
10
,
31
,
36
,
38
,
49
]
, including the monograph
[
45
]
.

An element
e
of a semigroup
S
is called
idempotent
if
ee=e
. The following renowned theorem was proven in
[
13
]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Ellis)
.

Each compact right topological semigroup contains an idempotent.

Since for every infinite discrete group
G
, the remainder
\beta G\setminus G
is a closed subsemigroup of
\beta G
. Theorem
2.1
implies the existence of a free idempotent ultrafilter on every infinite group
G
. It is easy to check that
u
is an idempotent ultrafilter on a group
G
if and only if for every
U\in u
there exist
x\in U
and
F_{x}\in u
such that
xF_{x}\subseteq U
. The notion of a free idempotent ultrafilter can be naturally weakened as follows.

Definition 2.2
.

An ultrafilter
u
on a group
G
is called

(i)

Schur
if for each
U\in u
there exist
x,y\in U
such that
xy\in U
;

(ii)

infinitary Schur
if for each
U\in u
there exist
x\in U
and an infinite subset
V\subset U
such that
xV\subseteq U
.

The set of all Schur ultrafilters on a group
G
is denoted by
\operatorname{Sch}(G)
. If
G
is a topological group, then by
G_{d}
we denote the group
G
endowed with the discrete topology. For a topological group
G
, let
\operatorname{Ult}(G)
be the set of all ultrafilters on
G
convergent to
1_{G}
.
\operatorname{Ult}(G)
is a closed subsemigroup of
\beta G_{d}
and is usually referred to as an
ultrafilter semigroup
of
G
. The following two definitions are crucial for this paper.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
G
be a topological group. We shall consider the following congruences on the Stone-Čech compactification
\beta G_{d}
of the discrete group
G_{d}
:

(i)

\Theta
is the least closed congruence such that
\{(u,1_{G}):u\hbox{ is idempotent}\}\subset\Theta
;

(ii)

\Xi
is the least closed congruence such that
\{(u,1_{G}):u\in\operatorname{Sch}(G)\}\subset\Xi
;

(iii)

\Phi
is the least closed congruence such that

\{(u,1_{G}):u\hbox{ is idempotent}\}\cup\{(u,1_{G}):u\in\operatorname{Ult}(G)\}\subset\Phi;

(iv)

\Psi
is the least closed congruence such that

\{(u,1_{G}):u\in\operatorname{Sch}(G)\}\cup\{(u,1_{G}):u\in\operatorname{Ult}(G)\}\subset\Psi.

Definition 2.4
.

For a topological group
G
and a congruence
\rho
on
\beta G_{d}
, let the map
\mathfrak{h}_{\rho}:G\rightarrow\beta G_{d}/\rho
be defined by
\mathfrak{h}_{\rho}(x)=h_{\rho}(x)=[x]_{\rho}
.

The
Bohr compactification
of a topological group
G
is a pair
(\mathfrak{b}G,b)
, which consists of a compact topological group
\mathfrak{b}G
and a continuous homomorphism
b:G\rightarrow\mathfrak{b}G
such that
b(G)
is dense in
\mathfrak{b}G
, and for each compact topological group
H
and continuous homomorphism
f:G\rightarrow H
, there exists a continuous homomorphism
g:\mathfrak{b}G\rightarrow H
such that
f=g\circ b
, i.e., the following diagram commutes:

\textstyle{G\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{b}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{b}G\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g}
\textstyle{H}

It is easy to see that the above property characterizes the Bohr compactification
\mathfrak{b}G
up to a topological isomorphism.
The following description of
Bohr compactifications of discrete commutative groups was obtained in
[
54
]
.
It can be formulated in terms of the concepts introduced above as follows.

Theorem 2.5

(Zlatoš)
.

For any discrete commutative group
G
the pair
(\beta G/\Xi,\mathfrak{h}_{\Xi})
is the Bohr compactification of
G
.

In this paper, we develop the ideas from
[
54
]
and obtain the following
description of Bohr compactification of an arbitrary topological group.

Theorem 2.6
.

For any topological group
G
the pair
(\beta G_{d}/\Psi,\mathfrak{h}_{\Psi})
is the Bohr compactification of
G
.

Observe that if a group
G
is discrete, then
\operatorname{Ult}(G)=\{1_{G}\}
and, as such,
\Psi=\Xi
.
Theorem
2.6
implies the following generalization of Theorem
2.5
.

Theorem 2.7
.

For any discrete group
G
, the pair
(\beta G/\Xi,\mathfrak{h}_{\Xi})
is the Bohr compactification of
G
.

A right topological group
G
is called
admissible
if the set
\Lambda(G)=\{x\in G:\lambda_{x}\hbox{ is continuous}\}
is dense in
G
. Following
[
25
]
, a compact Hausdorff admissible right topological group is called a
chart
group. For more information on the structure of chart groups, see
[
34
,
40
,
43
]
.
A
universal chartification
of a right topological group
G
is a pair
(\mathfrak{c}G,c)
consisting of a chart group
\mathfrak{c}G
and a continuous homomorphism
c:G\rightarrow\mathfrak{c}G
, which satisfy the following conditions:

(i)

c(G)
is dense in
\mathfrak{c}G
;

(ii)

c(G)\subseteq\Lambda(\mathfrak{c}G)
;

(iii)

for each chart group
H
and continuous homomorphism
f:G\rightarrow H
such that
f(G)\subseteq\Lambda(H)
, there exists a continuous homomorphism
g:\mathfrak{c}G\rightarrow H
such that
f=g\circ c
.

The following theorem describes universal chartifications of right topological groups.

Theorem 2.8
.

For any right topological group
G
the pair
(\beta G_{d}/\Phi,\mathfrak{h}_{\Phi})
is the universal chartification of
G
.

Observe that if a group
G
is discrete, then
\Phi=\Theta
. This way,
Theorem
2.8
yields the following.

Theorem 2.9
.

For any discrete group
G
, the pair
(\beta G/\Theta,\mathfrak{h}_{\Theta})
is the universal chartification of
G
.

As was mentioned in the introduction, one of the central problems in the theory of chart groups is when a chart group is a topological group. The following result gives necessary and sufficient conditions for a chart group to be a topological group.

Theorem 2.10
.

For a chart group
G
the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

G
is a topological group;

(ii)

every Schur ultrafilter on
G
converges to
1_{G}
;

(iii)

there exists a dense subgroup
H\subseteq\Lambda(G)
such that each Schur ultrafilter on
H
converges to
1_{G}
.

Theorem
2.6
is proven in Section
4
, after preliminary work done in Section
3
. Theorems
2.8
and
2.10
are proven in Section
5
.

3.
Schur ultrafilters

In this section, groups are assumed to be discrete and infinite, unless stated otherwise. We start with the algebraic properties of Schur ultrafilters. The item (ii) of the following result is proven in
[
44
, Lemma 5.3]
. For the sake of completeness, we include its brief proof.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a group,
u\in\operatorname{Sch}(G)
and
v\in\beta G
. Then

(i)

if
U\in uv
, then
UU^{-1}\in u
;

(ii)

if
U\in vu
, then
U^{-1}U\in u
.

Proof.

(i) Fix an arbitrary
B\in u
and
U\in uv
. There exist
F\in u
and
\{V_{x}:x\in F\}\subset v
such that
F\subseteq B
and
\bigcup_{x\in F}xV_{x}\subseteq U
. As
u
is a Schur ultrafilter, there exist elements
a,b,c\in F
such that
ab=c
. Put
V=V_{a}\cap V_{b}\cap V_{c}
and fix
t\in V
. Note that
bt\in bV\subseteq bV_{b}\subseteq U
,
ct\in cV\subseteq cV_{c}\subseteq U
and
a=cb^{-1}=ct(bt)^{-1}\in UU^{-1}
. It follows that
UU^{-1}\cap B\neq\emptyset
. Since
B
is arbitrarily chosen,
UU^{-1}\in u
.

(ii) Fix an arbitrary
B\in u
and
U\in vu
. There exist
x\in G
and
F\in u
such that
F\subseteq B
and
xF\subseteq U
. As
u
is a Schur ultrafilter, there exist elements
a,b,c\in F
such that
ab=c
. Note that
xa\in U
,
xc\in U
and
b=a^{-1}c=(xa)^{-1}xc\in U^{-1}U
. It follows that
U^{-1}U\cap B\neq\emptyset
. Since
B
is arbitrarily chosen,
U^{-1}U\in u
.
∎

The following result characterizes Schur ultrafilters.

Proposition 3.2
.

For any ultrafilter
u
on a group
G
the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

u
is a Schur ultrafilter;

(ii)

for each
U\in u
the set
X_{U}=\{x\in U:\exists y\in U\text{ such that }xy\in U\}
belongs to
u
;

(iii)

for each
U\in u
the set
Y_{U}=\{y\in U:\exists x\in U\text{ such that }xy\in U\}
belongs to
u
;

(iv)

UV\in u
for any
U,V\in u
;

(v)

UU^{-1}\in u
for each
U\in u
;

(vi)

U^{-1}U\in u
for each
U\in u
.

Proof.

The implications (ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) and (iii)
\Rightarrow
(i) are obvious.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii): Seeking a contradiction, assume that there exists
U\in u
such that
X_{U}\notin u
. Then
W=U\cap(G\setminus X_{U})\in u
. Since the ultrafilter
u
is Schur, there exist
x,y\in W
such that
xy\in W\subseteq U
. But then
x\in X_{U}
, which contradicts the definition of
W
.

The implication (i)
\Rightarrow
(iii) can be checked similarly.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(iv): Fix any
U,V\in u
. In order to show that
UV\in u
fix an arbitrary
W\in u
. Since the ultrafilter
u
is Schur, there exist
x,y\in U\cap V\cap W
such that
xy\in U\cap V\cap W
. Taking into account that
xy\in UV\cap W
and
W
is arbitrarily chosen, we get that
UV\cap W\neq\emptyset
for all
W\in u
. Since
u
is an ultrafilter, we obtain
UV\in u
.

(iv)
\Rightarrow
(i): Fix any
U\in u
. Since
UU\in u
, the set
UU\cap U
is not empty. Thus, there exist
x,y\in U
such that
xy\in U
, witnessing that the ultrafilter
u
is Schur.

The implication (i)
\Rightarrow
(v) follows from Lemma
3.1
(i) (set
v=1_{G}
). Similarly, the implication (i)
\Rightarrow
(vi) follows from Lemma
3.1
(ii).

(v)
\Rightarrow
(i): Fix any
U\in u
. Since the set
UU^{-1}\cap U
is not empty, there exist
x,y,z\in U
such that
xy^{-1}=z
. Then
zy=x\in U
, witnessing that the ultrafilter
u
is Schur.

(vi)
\Rightarrow
(i): Fix any
U\in u
. Since the set
U^{-1}U\cap U
is not empty, there exist
x,y,z\in U
such that
x^{-1}y=z
. Then
xz=y\in U
, witnessing that the ultrafilter
u
is Schur.
∎

For an ultrafilter
u
on a group
G
, let
u^{-1}=\{U^{-1}:U\in u\}
. Clearly,
u^{-1}
is also an ultrafilter and the map
u\mapsto u^{-1}
is a homeomorphism of
\beta G
.
The proof of the following lemma is trivial.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
u
is a Schur ultrafilter on a group
G
, then so is
u^{-1}
.

Protasov
[
44
, Lemma 5.2]
showed that
uu^{-1}
is a Schur ultrafilter for any
u\in\beta G
. The proof of the following results is based on Protasov’s ideas.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
G
be a group and
u\in\beta G\setminus G
. Then
uu^{-1}
and
u^{-1}u
are infinitary Schur.

Proof.

In order to show that
uu^{-1}
is infinitary Schur, fix any
W\in uu^{-1}
. There exist
U\in u
and
\{V_{x}:x\in U\}\subset u
such that
\bigcup_{x\in U}xV_{x}^{-1}\subseteq W
. Without loss of generality we can assume that
V_{x}\subseteq U
for each
x\in U
. Fix any elements
a\in U
and
b\in V_{a}
. Put
V=V_{a}\cap V_{b}
. Observe that
ab^{-1}\in W
,
bV^{-1}
is an infinite subset of
W
and
ab^{-1}(bV^{-1})=aV^{-1}\subseteq aV_{a}^{-1}\subseteq W
. Thus the ultrafilter
uu^{-1}
is infinitary Schur.

Note that
u^{-1}\in\beta G\setminus G
and
u^{-1}u=u^{-1}(u^{-1})^{-1}
. Thus the arguments above imply that
u^{-1}u
is infinitary Schur.
∎

The next proposition generalizes Lemma
3.4
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
G
be a group and
e
be an idempotent ultrafilter on
G
. Then for any
u\in\beta G\setminus G
the ultrafilters
ueu^{-1}
and
u^{-1}eu
are infinitary Schur.

Proof.

If
e=1_{G}
, then
ueu^{-1}=uu^{-1}
and
u^{-1}eu=u^{-1}u
. By Lemma
3.4
, the ultrafilters
uu^{-1}
and
u^{-1}u
are infinitary Schur. Assume that
e
is a free idempotent ultrafilter.
Fix any
F\in ueu^{-1}
. There exist
U\in u
,
\{E_{x}:x\in U\}\subset e
and
\{V_{h}:h\in H=\bigcup_{x\in U}xE_{x}\}\subset u
such that
\bigcup_{h\in H}hV_{h}^{-1}\subseteq F
. Without loss of generality we can assume that
V_{h}\subset U
for all
h\in H
. Fix any
x\in U
. Since
e
is an idempotent ultrafilter, there exist
b\in E_{x}
and
I_{b}\in e
such that
I_{b}\subseteq E_{x}
as well as
bI_{b}\subseteq E_{x}
. Note that
xb\in xE_{x}\subseteq H
. Fix any
v\in V_{xb}
. It is easy to see that
xbv^{-1}\in F
. Note that the set
C=I_{b}\cap E_{v}
belongs to
e
and thus is infinite. Since
v\in V_{xb}\subseteq U
and
C\subseteq E_{v}
, we get
vc\in H
for all
c\in C
. Since
C\subseteq I_{b}
, we get that
bC\subseteq E_{x}
and, consequently,
xbC\subseteq H
.
Fix any countably infinite subset
\{c_{n}:n\in\omega\}\subseteq C
. Assume that for each
k\leq n
we chose points
w_{c_{k}}\in V_{xbc_{k}}\cap V_{vc_{k}}
such that
vc_{i}w_{c_{i}}^{-1}\neq vc_{j}w_{c_{j}}^{-1}
for any distinct
i,j\leq k
. Since
G
is a group, the set

X=\{x\in G:vc_{n+1}x^{-1}\in\{vc_{i}w_{c_{i}}^{-1}:i\leq n\}\}

is finite. Hence we can choose
w_{c_{n+1}}\in V_{xbc_{n+1}}\cap V_{vc_{n+1}}\cap(G\setminus X)
. After completing the induction for each
n\in\omega
we obtain an element
w_{c_{n}}\in V_{xbc_{n}}\cap V_{vc_{n}}
such that the set
F^{\prime}=\{vc_{n}w_{c_{n}}^{-1}:n\in\omega\}
is infinite.
Since for each

vc_{n}\in H
and
w_{c_{n}}\in V_{vc_{n}}
, we get that
F^{\prime}\subseteq F
. Taking into account that
xbv^{-1}\in F
,
xbc_{n}\in H
and
w_{c_{n}}\in V_{xbc_{n}}
, we deduce that for every

(xbv^{-1})vc_{n}w_{c_{n}}^{-1}=(xbc_{n})w_{c_{n}}^{-1}\in\bigcup_{h\in H}hV_{h}^{-1}\subseteq F.

Hence

is infinitary Schur.

Observe that
u^{-1}\in\beta G\setminus G

u^{-1}eu=u^{-1}e(u^{-1})^{-1}

is infinitary Schur.
∎

If

Proposition 3.7
.

If

Proof.

Consider any

Next we turn to combinatorial properties of Schur ultrafilters. For this we need the following result proved in

Theorem 3.8

(Katětov)
.

Let

be a map such that

Proposition 3.9
.

Let

be a free Schur ultrafilter on a group

and

|\{y\in U:yx\in U\}|>n

Proof.

Seeking a contradiction, assume that there exist a free Schur ultrafilter
u
on

,
U\in u
and

the sets

Claim 3.10
.

There exists
W\in u
such that
W\subseteq U
and
|W\cap L_{x}|\leq 1
for all

.

Proof.

By Zorn’s Lemma, there exists a maximal with respect to the inclusion subset
W_{1}
of

for all

. Using Zorn’s Lemma once again, find maximal

for all

Proceeding this way we either find

for all

and

,

contains at least

implies that there exists

. Since

for any

∎

By Claim

, we can pick

such that for each

.
By Proposition
3.2
(ii),

.

There are two cases to consider:

(1)

;

(2)

.

(1) Fix any

and define a map

as follows:

f(x)=\begin{cases}y_{x},&\hbox{if }x\in T;\\
z,&\hbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

It is clear that

for all

. By Theorem
3.8
, there exist disjoint subsets

of

such that

. Without loss of generality we can assume that

. Let

. But then

(2) Since

for all

for any distinct

for all

Fix any

Consider the map

Since

for all

yields pairwise disjoint subsets

of

. Then

.
By the choice of

Since

is a Schur ultrafilter, Proposition

(iv) implies that

Proposition
3.9
implies the following corollary.

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
u
be a Schur ultrafilter on a commutative group
G
. Then for each
U\in u
and
n\in\omega
there exists
x\in U
such that
|\{y\in U:xy\in U\}|>n
.

Next we are going to show that infinitary Schur ultrafilters and Schur ultrafilters are in some sense antipodes to P-points and selective ultrafilters, respectively.
Recall that an ultrafilter
u
on a countable set
X
is called

(i)

weak P-point
if
u
is not in the closure of any countable subset of
\beta X\setminus(X\cup\{u\})
;

(ii)

P-point
if for every partition
\{C_{n}:n\in\omega\}
of
X
such that
C_{n}\notin u
for all
n\in\omega
, there exists
U\in u
such that the set
U\cap C_{n}
is finite for every
n\in\omega
;

(iii)

selective
if for every partition
\{C_{n}:n\in\omega\}
of
X
such that
C_{n}\notin u
for all
n\in\omega
, there exists
U\in u
such that
|U\cap C_{n}|\leq 1
for all
n\in\omega
.

It is known that each selective ultrafilter is a P-point and each P-point is a weak P-point.
The celebrated ZFC result of Kunen
[
33
]
states that
\beta\omega\setminus\omega
contains a weak P-point. However, the existence of P-points and selective ultrafilters in
\beta\omega\setminus\omega
is consistent with ZFC, but cannot be derived from ZFC alone (see
[
6
,
8
,
50
]
). It is well known that an idempotent ultrafilter
u
on a countable group
G
cannot be a weak P-point, as
u
is an accumulation point of the family
\{xu:x\in G\}\subset\beta G
.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
G
be a countable group and
u\in\beta G
be an infinitary Schur ultrafilter. Then
u
is not a P-point.

Proof.

Seeking a contradiction, assume that there exists a P-point
u\in\operatorname{Sch}^{\infty}(G)
. Since
u
is not an idempotent, there exists
U\in u
such that for each
x\in U
the set
L_{x}=\{y\in U:xy\in U\}
does not belong to
u
. By Proposition
3.2
(iii),
Y_{U}=\bigcup_{x\in U}L_{x}\in u
. Let
U=\{x_{n}:n\in\omega\}
. Put
C_{0}=L_{x_{0}}
and for each
n>0
let
C_{n}=L_{x_{n}}\setminus\bigcup_{i<n}L_{x_{i}}
. Clearly, the family
\{C_{n}:n\in\omega\}
is a partition of
U
(we allow here that some
C_{n}
can be empty). Since
u
is a P-point, there exists
W\in u
such that the set
W\cap C_{n}
is finite for each
n\in\omega
. Since
u
is an infinitary Schur ultrafilter, there exist
x\in W
and an infinite subset
Z\subseteq W\cap L_{x}
such that
xZ\subseteq W
. On the other hand, for any
n\in\omega
the set
W\cap L_{x_{n}}
is finite, as it is contained in
\bigcup_{i\leq n}C_{i}
. The obtained contradiction completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.13
.

Let
u
be a free Schur ultrafilter on a countable commutative group

. Then
u
is not selective.

Proof.

Seeking a contradiction, assume that there exists a free selective ultrafilter
u\in\operatorname{Sch}(G)
.
By Proposition
3.12
,
u
is not infinitary Schur. Then there exists
U\in u
such that for each
x\in U
the set
L_{x}=\{y\in U:xy\in U\}
is finite.

Claim 3.14
.

For each
x\in U
the set
Z=\{z\in U:L_{z}\cap L_{x}\neq\emptyset\}
is finite.

Proof.

Assume to the contrary that there exists
x\in U
such that
L_{x}\cap L_{z}\neq\emptyset
for infinitely many

. Since the set
L_{x}
is finite, the pigeonhole principle yields
y\in L_{x}
and an infinite subset

such that

for all

. Then for any

, we get that
yz=zy\in U
. Hence
L_{y}
contains an infinite subset

, which contradicts our assumption.
∎

Fix an enumeration
\{x_{n}:n\in\omega\}
of
U
.
We shall inductively construct an auxiliary partition of
U
into finite sets
\{B_{n}:n\in\omega\}
. We begin with
B_{0}=\{x_{0}\}
. Assume that finite pairwise disjoint subsets

are already constructed for all

. Let
j=\min\{m\in\omega:x_{m}\notin\bigcup_{i\leq n}B_{i}\}
. Put

B_{n+1}=\{x\in U\setminus\bigcup_{i\leq n}B_{i}:L_{x}\cap L_{z}\neq\emptyset\hbox{ for some }z\in B_{n}\}\cup\{x_{j}\}.

Claim
3.14
implies that each

is finite.
For each
n\in\omega
, let
C_{n}=\bigcup_{x\in B_{n}}L_{x}
. It is easy to see that
C_{n}\cap C_{m}=\emptyset
whenever
|n-m|\geq 2
.
By Proposition
3.2
(iii),
Y_{U}=\bigcup_{x\in U}L_{x}=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}C_{n}\in u
. Since
u
is an ultrafilter, either
\bigcup_{n\in\omega}C_{2n}\in u
or
\bigcup_{n\in\omega}C_{2n+1}\in u
. Without loss of generality, assume that
W=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}C_{2n}\in u
.
Since
\{C_{2n}:n\in\omega\}

consisting of finite sets and the ultrafilter

is selective, there exists

such that
|F\cap C_{2n}|\leq 1
for every

. Fix any

such that
L_{x}\subseteq C_{m}

for all

The following two problems are left open.

Problem 3.15
.

Does there exist a free Schur ultrafilter on a (countable) group
G
that is not infinitary Schur?

Problem 3.16
.

Is the existence of a free Schur ultrafilter on
\mathbb{Z}
that is a P-point consistent with ZFC?

Observe that the positive solution of Problem
3.16
implies the positive consistent solution of Problem
3.15
. Noteworthy, there exists a model of ZFC where
\beta\omega\setminus\omega
contains P-points but all of them are selective (see
[
47
, Section XVIII.4]
). Taking into account Proposition
3.13
, we obtain that the positive answer to Problem
3.16
cannot be derived from the existence of free P-points alone. Also, if
G
is a countable group, then no P-point

can be represented as
u=vw
for some

, because
u
would be an accumulation point of the countable subset

.

Recall that a semigroup
S
endowed with a topology
\tau
is called a
topological semigroup
if the multiplication is continuous in
(S,\tau)
.
The following proposition presents another similarity between Schur and idempotent ultrafilters.

Proposition 3.17
.

Let
G
be a group,
S
be a topological semigroup and
f:\beta G\rightarrow S
be a continuous homomorphism. Then for every Schur ultrafilter
u\in\beta G
the following assertions hold:

(i)

f(u)
is an idempotent;

(ii)

there exists an idempotent ultrafilter
e\in\beta G
such that
f(e)=f(u)
.

Proof.

(i) By the continuity of
f
, the filter
\mathcal{F}
on
S
generated by the family
\{f(U):U\in u\}
converges to
f(u)
. Fix an arbitrary open neighborhood
W
of
f(u)f(u)
. Since
S
is a topological semigroup and the map
f
is continuous, there exists
U\in u
such that
f(UU)=f(U)f(U)\subseteq W
. By Proposition
3.2
(iv), the set
UU
belongs to
u
and, consequently,
f(UU)\in\mathcal{F}
. Since the neighborhood
W
was chosen arbitrarily, we get that
\mathcal{F}
converges to
f(u)f(u)
. Taking into account that in a Hausdorff space each filter converges to at most one point, we get that
f(u)f(u)=f(u)
.

(ii) By item (i), the element
y=f(u)\in S
is an idempotent. Then
f^{-1}(y)
is a compact subsemigroup of
\beta G
. Theorem

implies that
f^{-1}(y)
contains an idempotent

. It is clear that

.
∎

.

For any group

Proposition 3.19
.

For any group

the set of all free Schur ultrafilters is nowhere dense in

Proof.

Lemma
3.18
implies that the set
\operatorname{Sch}(G)\setminus G
is closed in
\beta G\setminus G
. Seeking a contradiction, assume that the interior of
\operatorname{Sch}(G)\setminus G
in
\beta G\setminus G
is not empty. Then there exists an infinite subset

such that
\langle A\rangle\setminus G\subseteq\operatorname{Sch}(G)

A
if necessary, we can assume that
1_{G}\notin A
. We are going to construct by induction an infinite subset
B\subseteq A
such that
BB\cap B=\emptyset
.
Put

A^{[2]}=\{x^{2}:x\in A\}
.
The following two cases are possible:

(i)

the set

(ii)

the set

is infinite.

(i) Fix any

. Since

Then

∎

4.
Bohr compactification of topological groups

The following proposition follows from
[
30
, Theorem 4.8]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
X
be a discrete semigroup,
Y
a compact right topological semigroup, and
f:X\rightarrow Y
a homomorphism such that
f(X)\subseteq\Lambda(Y)
. Then
f
can be extended to a unique continuous homomorphism
\phi:\beta X\rightarrow Y
.

We shall refer several times to the following folklore fact, which is a corollary of
[
7
, Lemma 1.47]
.

Lemma 4.2

(Folklore)
.

Let
X
and
Z
be topological spaces,
\rho
an equivalence relation on
X
, and
f:X\rightarrow Z
,
g:X/\rho\rightarrow Z
be maps such that
f=g\circ h_{\rho}
. Then
g
is continuous if and only if
f
is continuous.

Recall that for a given topological group
G
, by
\Theta
we denote the least closed congruence on
\beta G_{d}
that contains the relation
\{(u,1_{G}):u\hbox{ is idempotent}\}
(see Definition
2.3
).

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
G
be a discrete group and
\rho
a closed congruence on
\beta G
. Then
\Theta\subseteq\rho
if and only if the quotient semigroup
\beta G/\rho
is a chart group satisfying
h_{\rho}(G)\subseteq\Lambda(\beta G/\rho)
.

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
): Let
\rho
be a closed congruence on
\beta G
such that
(e,1_{G})\in\rho
for each idempotent ultrafilter
e
.
Hence for each idempotent ultrafilter
e\in\beta G
and
u\in\beta G
we have
(eu,1_{G}u)=(eu,u)\in\rho
.
[
54
, Theorem 1]
implies that
\beta G/\rho
is a compact right topological group. Clearly,
h_{\rho}(G)
is a dense subgroup of
\beta G/\rho
. It remains to check that
h_{\rho}(G)\subseteq\Lambda(\beta G/\rho)
. Note that for each
u\in\beta G
,

(1)

h_{\rho}(\lambda_{u}(x))=[ux]_{\rho}=[u]_{\rho}[x]_{\rho}=\lambda_{[u]}(h_{\rho}(x)).

Recall that for each
g\in G
the left shift
\lambda_{g}
is continuous in
\beta G
, implying that the map
h_{\rho}\circ\lambda_{g}
is continuous too. Equation
1
follows that
h_{\rho}\circ\lambda_{u}=\lambda_{[u]}\circ h_{\rho}
for every
u\in\beta G
. Thus the map
\lambda_{[g]}\circ h_{\rho}
is continuous for each
g\in G
. By Lemma
4.2
, the left shift
\lambda_{[g]}:\beta G/\rho\rightarrow\beta G/\rho
is continuous for each
g\in G
. Hence
h_{\rho}(G)\subseteq\Lambda(\beta G/\rho)
.

(
\Leftarrow
): Since a homomorphic image of an idempotent is an idempotent and
\rho
is a group congruence, we get
(e,1_{G})\in\rho
for each idempotent
e\in\beta G
. Hence
\Theta\subseteq\rho
.
∎

Recall that for a given topological group
G
, by
\Phi
we denote the least closed congruence on
\beta G_{d}
that contains the relation
\{(u,1_{G}):u\hbox{ is idempotent}\}\cup\{(u,1_{G}):u\in\operatorname{Ult}(G)\}
(see Definition
2.3
). Also recall that for a given congruence
\rho
on
\beta G_{d}
the map
\mathfrak{h}_{\rho}:G\rightarrow\beta G_{d}/\rho
is defined by
\mathfrak{h}_{\rho}(g)=h_{\rho}(g)=[g]_{\rho}
for all
g\in G
(see Definition
2.4
).

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
G
be a right topological group, and
\rho
a closed group congruence on
\beta G_{d}
. Then the map
\mathfrak{h}_{\rho}:G\rightarrow\beta G_{d}/\rho
is continuous if and only if
\Phi\subseteq\rho
.

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
): Since
\rho
is a group congruence,
(e,1_{G})\in\rho
for each idempotent
e\in\beta G_{d}
. Fix any ultrafilter
u\in\operatorname{Ult}(G)
, i.e.,
u
converges to
1_{G}
in
G
. By the continuity of the map
\mathfrak{h}_{\rho}
, the ultrafilter
\mathfrak{h}_{\rho}(u)
on
\beta G_{d}/\rho
generated by the family
\{\mathfrak{h}_{\rho}(U):U\in u\}
converges to
\mathfrak{h}_{\rho}(1_{G})=h_{\rho}(1_{G})
. Since the map
h_{\rho}
is continuous, for each open neighborhood
W\subseteq\beta G_{d}/\rho
of
h_{\rho}(u)
there exists
U\in u
such that
h_{\rho}(U)=\mathfrak{h}_{\rho}(U)\subseteq W
. Hence the ultrafilter
\mathfrak{h}_{\rho}(u)
converges to
h_{\rho}(u)
. The closedness of the congruence
\rho
implies that the space
\beta G_{d}/\rho
is Hausdorff. It follows that
h_{\rho}(u)=h_{\rho}(1_{G})
and, consequently,
(u,1_{G})\in\rho
. Hence
\Phi\subseteq\rho
.

(
\Leftarrow
):
By Proposition
4.3
,
\beta G_{d}/\rho
is a chart group. To establish the continuity of the map
\mathfrak{h}_{\rho}
, it suffices to check that for any
u\in\operatorname{Ult}(G)
the ultrafilter
\mathfrak{h}_{\rho}(u)
on
\beta G_{d}/\rho
generated by the family
\{\mathfrak{h}_{\rho}(U):U\in u\}
converges to
[1_{G}]_{\rho}
.
For this fix arbitrary
u\in\operatorname{Ult}(G)
and open neighborhood
W
of
[1_{G}]_{\rho}
in
\beta G_{d}/\rho
. Since
\Phi\subseteq\rho
we get that
(u,1_{G})\in\rho
. It follows that
h_{\rho}^{-1}(W)
is an open neighborhood of
u
in
\beta G_{d}
. Then
V=h_{\rho}^{-1}(W)\cap G\in u
. Observe that

\mathfrak{h}_{\rho}(V)=h_{\rho}(V)\subseteq h_{\rho}(h_{\rho}^{-1}(W))\subseteq W,

witnessing that
W\in\mathfrak{h}_{\rho}(u)
. Hence the ultrafilter
\mathfrak{h}_{\rho}(u)
converges to
[1_{G}]_{\rho}
.
∎

The following renowned theorem was proven in
[
12
]
.

Theorem 4.5

(Ellis)
.

A locally compact semitopological group is a topological group.

Recall that for each ultrafilter
u
on a group
G
by
u^{-1}
we denote the ultrafilter
\{U^{-1}:U\in u\}
, and for any homomorphism
f:X\rightarrow Y
,
\operatorname{Ker}(f)=\{(x,y)\in X{\times}X:f(x)=f(y)\}
is a congruence on
X
. If, moreover,
Y
is a Hausdorff topological semigroup, then it is easy to check that the congruence
\operatorname{Ker}(f)
is closed.
We are in a position to prove Theorem
2.6
, i.e., to show that for any topological group
G
the pair
(\beta G_{d}/\Psi,\mathfrak{h}_{\Psi})
is the Bohr compactification of
G
.

Proof of Theorem
2.6
.

By Proposition
4.3
,
\beta G_{d}/\Psi
is a chart group. Since for each ultrafilter
u
on
G
,
uu^{-1}
is a Schur ultrafilter (see Proposition
3.5
), and
\{(u,1_{G}):u\in\operatorname{Sch}(G)\}\subset\Psi
, we get that

[u]_{\Psi}[u^{-1}]_{\Psi}=[uu^{-1}]_{\Psi}=[1_{G}]_{\Psi}.

Thus,
[u^{-1}]_{\Psi}
is the inverse element of
[u]_{\Psi}
in the group
\beta G_{d}/\Psi
.
Let
\operatorname{inv}:\beta G_{d}/\Psi\rightarrow\beta G_{d}/\Psi
be the inversion.
Define a map
\iota:\beta G_{d}\rightarrow\beta G_{d}
by
\iota(u)=u^{-1}
. The arguments above imply that for any
u\in\beta G_{d}
,

h_{\Psi}(\iota(u))=\operatorname{inv}(h_{\Psi}(u)).

Since
\iota
is a homeomorphism we obtain that the map
h_{\Psi}\circ\iota
is continuous. By Lemma
4.2
, the map
\operatorname{inv}:\beta G_{d}/\Psi\rightarrow\beta G_{d}/\Psi
is continuous. Observe that for each
p\in\beta G/\Psi
we have the following:

\lambda_{p}=\operatorname{inv}\circ\rho_{\operatorname{inv}(p)}\circ\operatorname{inv}.

Taking into account that
\beta G_{d}/\Psi
is a right topological group, the formula above implies that left shifts are continuous in
\beta G_{d}/\Psi
and, as such,
\beta G_{d}/\Psi
is a compact semitopological group. By Theorem
4.5
,
\beta G_{d}/\Psi
is a compact topological group.
Since
\Phi\subseteq\Psi
, Proposition
4.4
implies that the map
\mathfrak{h}_{\Psi}:G\rightarrow\beta G_{d}/\Psi
is continuous.

In order to show that
\beta G_{d}/\Psi
is the Bohr compactification of
G
, fix any continuous homomorphism
f
from
G
to a compact topological group
H
. Without loss of generality we can assume that
f(G)
is dense in
H
.
Proposition
4.1
yields a continuous homomorphism
\phi:\beta G_{d}\rightarrow H
, such that
\phi(x)=f(x)
for each
x\in G
. Since
f(G)
is dense in
H
, the map
\phi
is surjective. Observe that the congruence
\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)
on
\beta G_{d}
is closed, as
\phi
is continuous and the space
H
is Hausdorff.
By Proposition
3.17
, for each Schur ultrafilter
u
on
G
the element
\phi(u)
is an idempotent, witnessing that
\phi(u)=1_{H}=\phi(1_{G})
. Thus
\{(u,1_{G}):u\in\operatorname{Sch}(G)\}\subset\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)
.
Consider any ultrafilter
u\in\operatorname{Ult}(G)
. By the continuity of
f
, the ultrafilter
f(u)
on
H
generated by the family
\{f(U):U\in u\}
converges to
f(1_{G})=1_{H}
.
The continuity of
\phi
implies that for each open neighborhood
V
of
\phi(u)
there exists
U\in u
such that
\phi(U)=f(U)\subseteq V
. It follows that the ultrafilter
f(u)
converges to
\phi(u)
. Since the space
H
is Hausdorff,

\phi(u)=1_{H}=\phi(1_{G})
and, consequently,
(u,1_{G})\in\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)
. Hence
\{(u,1_{G}):u\in\operatorname{Ult}(G)\}\subset\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)
, witnessing that
\Psi\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)
. By Theorem 1.48 from
[
7
]
, there exists a continuous homomorphism
g:\beta G_{d}/\Psi\rightarrow H
such that
\phi=g\circ h_{\Psi}
. Recall that for each
x\in G
,
\mathfrak{h}_{\Psi}(x)=h_{\Psi}(x)
and
\phi(x)=f(x)
, witnessing that
f(x)=g(\mathfrak{h}_{\Psi}(x))
. Hence the pair
(\beta G_{d}/\Psi,\mathfrak{h}_{\Psi})
is the Bohr compactification of
G
.
∎

The following proposition characterizes compact topological groups.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
G
be a group endowed with a topology. Then
G
is a compact topological group if and only if there exist a discrete group
H
and a continuous surjective homomorphism
g:\beta H\rightarrow G
such that
g(u)=1_{G}
for any Schur ultrafilter
u
on
H
.

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
) Fix a compact topological group
G
. Let
H
be the group
G
endowed with the discrete topology, and
i:H\rightarrow G
be the identity homomorphism. By Proposition
4.1
, there exists a continuous homomorphism
g:\beta H\rightarrow G
that extends
i
. It is clear that
g
is surjective. Proposition
3.17
implies that
g(u)=1_{G}
for any Schur ultrafilter
u
on
H
.

(
\Leftarrow
) Assume that
G
is a group equipped with a Hausdorff topology, and there exists a discrete group
H
together with a continuous surjective homomorphism
g:\beta H\rightarrow G
such that
g(u)=1_{G}
for any Schur ultrafilter
u
on
H
.
The definition of
g
implies that
\Xi\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}(g)
, where
\Xi
is the congruence on
\beta H
from Definition
2.3
.

Define a map
f:\beta H/\Xi\rightarrow G
by
f([x]_{\Xi})=g(x)
. Since

f([x]_{\Xi}[y]_{\Xi})=f([xy]_{\Xi})=g(xy)=g(x)g(y)=f([x]_{\Xi})f([y]_{\Xi}),

we obtain that
f
is a surjective homomorphism. Observe that
g=f\circ h_{\Xi}
. Since the map
g
is continuous, Lemma
4.2
implies that the homomorphism
f
is continuous. By Theorem
2.7
,
\beta H/\Xi
is the Bohr compactification of
H
.
Then
G
, being a continuous homomorphic image of
\beta H/\Xi
, is a compact topological group as well.
∎

5.
Chart groups

We are now in a position to prove Theorem
2.8
, i.e., to show that for any right topological group
G
the pair
(\beta G_{d}/\Phi,\mathfrak{h}_{\Phi})
is the universal chartification of
G
.

Proof of Theorem
2.8
.

Proposition
4.3
implies that
\beta G_{d}/\Phi
is a chart group and
\phi(G)\subseteq\Lambda(\beta G_{d}/\Phi)
. By Proposition
4.4
, the map
\mathfrak{h}_{\Phi}:G\rightarrow\beta G_{d}/\Phi
is continuous.

Fix any chart group
H
and a continuous homomorphism
g:G\rightarrow H
such that
g(G)\subseteq\Lambda(H)
is dense in
H
. Then
g
can be also viewed as a continuous homomorphism from
G_{d}
to
H
. Proposition
4.1
yields a continuous homomorphic extension
\phi:\beta G_{d}\rightarrow H
of
g
. Note that
\phi
is surjective, as
g(G)
is dense in
H
.
Since
\phi
is continuous and
H
is a chart group, we get that
\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)
is a closed group congruence. It follows that
\Theta\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)
.
Consider any ultrafilter
u\in\operatorname{Ult}(G)
. By the continuity of
g
, the ultrafilter
g(u)
on
H
generated by the family
\{g(U):U\in u\}
converges to
g(1_{G})=1_{H}
.
The continuity of
\phi
implies that for each open neighborhood
V
of
\phi(u)
there exists
U\in u
such that
\phi(U)=g(U)\subseteq V
. It follows that the ultrafilter
g(u)
converges to
\phi(u)
. Since the space
H
is Hausdorff,
\phi(u)=1_{H}=\phi(1_{G})
and, consequently,
(u,1_{G})\in\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)
. Thus
\{(u,1_{G}):u\in\operatorname{Ult}(G)\}\subset\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)
, and hence
\Phi\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)
.

Define a map
f:\beta G_{d}/\Phi\rightarrow H
by
f([x]_{\Phi})=\phi(x)
. Since

f([x]_{\Phi}[y]_{\Phi})=f([xy]_{\Phi})=\phi(xy)=\phi(x)\phi(y)=f([x]_{\Phi})f([y]_{\Phi}),

we obtain that
f
is a surjective homomorphism. Observe that
\phi=f\circ h_{\Phi}
. Since the map
\phi
is continuous, Lemma
4.2
implies that
f
is continuous. Finally, for each
y\in G
,

f(\mathfrak{h}_{\Phi}(y))=f(h_{\Phi}(y))=f([y]_{\Phi})=\phi(y)=g(y).

Hence the pair
(\beta G_{d}/\Phi,\mathfrak{h}_{\Phi})
is the universal chartification of
G
.
∎

Recall that
\Lambda(G)
is a subgroup of

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
u
be an idempotent ultrafilter on a chart group
G
. If
\Lambda(G)\in u
, then
u
converges to
1_{G}
.

Proof.

Since the group
G
is compact, there exists
x\in G
such that
u
converges to
x
. Pick an open neighborhood
W
of
x^{2}
. Since
G
is a chart group, there exists
U\in u
such that
Ux\subseteq W

V=U\cap\Lambda(G)\in u
. Since
V\subseteq\Lambda(G)
and
u
converges to
x
, for each

there exists
V_{a}\in u
such that
aV_{a}\subseteq W
. Thus
\bigcup_{a\in V}aV_{a}\subseteq W

u

\bigcup_{a\in V}aV_{a}\in u

W\in u
, witnessing that
u
converges to

. Since
G
is Hausdorff,

.
∎

.

Each Schur ultrafilter on a compact topological group

u

Need for travel health clinic: A cross-sectional study among international travellers availing yellow fever vaccination in a teaching institution
Authors: Deepika Sadawarte, Kosturi Dakshit, Mandar Sadawarte, C. Maria, Vaishnavi Yadav
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Traveller’s health is one of the factors determining the success of his or her visit to the destination. The health aspects before, during and after travel are often neglected which may lead to untoward incidences during or after travel. Also, with the increase in national and international travel many emerging and re-emerging diseases are on the rise. This study was conducted to assess the awareness, knowledge and practices of the travellers and to find out the need for travel health clinics.Methodology:A cross-sectional study among adults attending the Outpatient Department of International Travellers vaccination was conducted for 3 months. Data was collected regarding the awareness, knowledge and practices of 216 travellers by using a pre-validated interview schedule. Systematic random sampling was used to select these travellers. Analysis was performed after entering the data in Microsoft Excel and SPSS software wherein frequency percentages were calculated and the association was found between the awareness and felt need for a travel health clinic.Result:The purpose of most of the travellers was job-related, the majority being males. As the study was conducted in a yellow fever vaccination centre majority knew only this as a travel-related preventable disease condition. The awareness regarding travel-related diseases was found to be less and which led to a felt need by the travellers for pre-travel counselling with a doctor.Conclusion:Travel medicine is an emerging area for health care providers and there is a felt need for a travel health clinic with the increase in the domestic as well as international travel.

Introduction

Travel medicine is an emerging branch of medical science that deals with the health of travellers. It concerns preventive measures as well as the management of any health issues of travellers. In the modern era of globalisation, travelling has become a very common phenomenon with a tremendous increase in speed and frequency.

People travel for many reasons like tourism, business, study purposes, research, visiting friends and relatives, ecotourism, medical tourism and international disaster response.[1] Travelling exposes an individual to different environmental, physical, mental and cultural stress. Also, a person may carry a disease from an endemic zone to a non-endemic zone. According to the World Tourism Organization, there were 1.33 billion worldwide international tourist arrivals in 2017, an increase of 88% from 2015. International travel increased by 6% in January–April 2018 compared to the same period in 2017.[2]

Traveller’s health not only depends upon environmental exposure but also upon the basic health status of the individual.[3] Specifically, travellers are at potential risk for infectious diseases and also, and they can spread a disease globally.[4] The recent COVID-19 pandemic is one of the examples of the quick spread of disease all over the world via travellers which caused worldwide panic and threatened the global economy.

Travel medicine promotes healthy and safe travel and includes many specialties such as infectious disease, preventive medicine, emergency medicine, psychiatry, occupational health and environmental health.[5] Many travel-specific infectious diseases can be prevented by vaccination while some by prophylaxis. CDC (Centres for Disease Control and Prevention) recommends keeping a travel health kit, medicine checklist and visiting a travel health specialist.[6]

Apart from infectious diseases travellers may face jet lag, injuries or violence or psychological problems that impact their physical as well as mental well-being.[7] Traveller must maintain a personal health information card which includes blood group, allergy and chronic disease history and medications.[7]

Travel-related diseases can be prevented by proper advice, vaccination, precautions, prophylaxis, etc., but there is a gap between knowledge and practices.[8] Many travellers are unaware about all vaccine-preventable diseases related to travel and show poor compliance with World Health Organisation (WHO) guidelines.[9] Before travel recommended health check-up by a travel medicine specialist are not undertaken by many travellers due to a lack of awareness, or non-availability of travel health specialists.[10]

WHO and CDC have recommended country-specific guidelines. WHO recommends a list of vaccines which includes routine vaccination, vaccinations for destination and some vaccinations demanded by some countries.[11]

Though travel medicine is an upcoming subject very few studies have been conducted in this domain. In 2013, the total number of foreign tourists arrived in India were 6–97 million.[12] Currently in India there exist very few organised travel health services and very few trained specialists in travel health. So, this study was conducted to fill the gap and understand health awareness and practices among travellers.

Materials and Methods

This institutionally based cross-sectional study was carried out in the Outpatient Department of International Travellers yellow fever vaccination at a teaching institution of a metropolitan city for a period of 3 months from May 2023 to July 2023. The study population consisted of travellers undertaking international travel. Ethical approval was obtained from the Institutional Ethics Committee (EC/OA-162/2022) before commencing the study.

Considering the average attendance of yellow fever OPD is 10 per day, in a week 20 beneficiaries visit. Then considering 1 month average having 4 weeks, in 1 month total beneficiaries is 80. As the study duration is 3 months total beneficiaries would be 240. Considering 10% as the rejection rate, the sample size arrived at was 216. A systematic random sampling method was employed for selecting participants from the vaccination outpatient department.

Data was collected using a pre-validated interview schedule which was validated by five subject experts from the Department of Community Medicine. The interview schedule included questions related to sociodemographic information of the traveller, health and travel-related information, awareness, knowledge and practices of travellers and the felt need of travellers. Written consent was taken from the beneficiaries after explaining to them the details of the study. Confidentiality of the data collected was assured by the investigators.

Collected data was compiled in Microsoft Excel sheet and was analysed using IBM SPSS software version number 27. Descriptive statistics of frequency and percentages were calculated for the awareness, knowledge and practices of the travellers. The association between the awareness and felt need for a travel health clinic by travellers was found out by using the Chi-square test. Statistical significance was set at the probability value (P < 0.05).

Results

The mean age of the study participants was 34.05 years (Range = 18 to 73 years of age). Table 1 shows the sociodemographic and travel-related details of the travellers. The majority (84.26%) of the travellers were males. Most of the participants were residents of the rest of Maharashtra region (48.15%) followed by Mumbai city (33.80%) whereas 2.31% were foreign nationals. The majority of the travellers were graduates (35.65%). All the travellers included in the study were secondary passed. As the study site was a yellow fever vaccination centre the immediate travel destination for the majority (55.09%) was Africa. The most common purpose of the visit was for the job (45.83%). The majority were frequent travellers (57.87%). The most common source of health-related information for the traveller was doctors (39.35%) followed by friends and family (31.94%). A total of 54.17% planned their travel at least 1 month before departure. Pre-travel health advice was received by 44.91% more than 4 weeks before travel.

Out of the 216 travellers, the awareness levels regarding vaccine-preventable travel-related diseases were 65.28%, regarding prophylaxis of diseases related to travel was 37%, regarding mental health issues related to travel was 60.19% whereas awareness about medical tourism was 53.24% and knowledge regarding jet lag with travel was 82.87%.

On enquiring about the travel-related vaccine-preventable diseases multiple responses were recorded with the majority for yellow fever vaccine (98.61%) followed by polio (52.78%), malaria (34.26%), hepatitis B (31.48%), hepatitis A (30.56%), typhoid (26.39%) and dengue (25.00%). It was surprisingly seen that travellers did not think of rabies, HIV, acute gastroenteritis and foodborne diseases as concerns during travel.

The practices followed by the travellers included carrying a health card or health advice prescription (35.65%), taking the vaccine apart from the yellow fever vaccine (40.74%) and consulting a physician (12.50%). The felt needs of the travellers that were encountered during this study were difficulty in finding health check-up guidelines for travel (10.19%), Pre-travel counselling (41.20%) and the need for a travel health clinic (65.74%).

Discussion

The health issues pre-, during and post-travel are most of the time neglected and priority is given to other travel-related issues like itinerary, destination, food and shopping. Domestic and global travel has increased the arena of health-related issues. There are many studies conducted in developed countries related to travel health issues but very few are conducted in India.

In the current study, it was found that the majority of the travellers were travelling for the job purpose (45.83%) whereas tourism was the major reason for travel in studies by Koen Van Herck et al.[3] (70.9%), Seif S. Al-Abri et al.[8] (31.4%) and Toovey S et al.[9] (42%). This difference could be because the current study was conducted in a yellow fever vaccination centre which is a mandatory vaccine for endemic countries and the other studies were conducted in international airports which included people travelling for various reasons.

The majority of the travellers (54.17%) in the current study planned their trip at least 1 month before the travel. Similar findings were seen in the studies by Koen Van Herck et al.[3] (58.3%), Seif S. Al-Abri et al.[8] (52%), Toovey S et al.[9] (55%), Hamer and Connor[13] (53%), Heywood et al.[14] (49.2%). This shows that there is awareness among at least half of the travellers towards planning the trip 1 month prior.

The pre-travel health advice was sought by 44.91% in the current study whereas it was sought by 43.8% in the study by Koen Van Herck et al.,[3] by 83.3% in the study by Seif S. Al-Abri et al.,[8] by 86% in study by Toovey S et al.,[9] by 36% in study by Hamer and Connor[13] and by 35.3% in the study by Heywood et al.[14] the varying percentage is due to the level of awareness and knowledge regarding the need to seek a pre-travel advice.

The main source of health information in the current study was doctors and family physicians (39.35%) which was similar to the study by Koen Van Herck et al.[3] (57.4%) and Heywood et al.[14] (35.3%) whereas in studies by Seif S. Al-Abri et al.[8] and Hamer and Connor[13] it was sought from family and friends by 55.3% and 53% respectively. In the study by Toovey S et al.[9] the most common source of information was a travel agent (28%). The study by Heywood et al.[14] depicts that only 4.2% of the study respondents took advice from a travel health specialist. These figures reveal that there is a need to create awareness among people regarding health issues in travel and for which a travel health specialist should be approached. Also, more travel health physicians are required to match the number of travellers.

The awareness of the travellers towards malaria and its prophylaxis, vaccine-preventable diseases and foodborne diseases were seen distinctly in many studies but awareness towards HIV and rabies was negligible. Among the vaccinations to be taken, travellers generally focus on mandatory vaccines like yellow fever in the case of the current study.

Study by Yang C-J et al.[12] revealed a significant association between the willingness to buy various travel health insurances and the willingness for pre-travel consultation. Similar results were seen in the current study wherein a significant association was seen between the awareness levels of the travellers and the need for a travel health clinic. An editorial by Kulkarni GK[15] points towards the impending need for a special travel health physician course which will encompass the disciplines of vaccination, infectious disease epidemiology, medicine, surgical principles and public health safety measures. A study by Duval B[10] revealed that the probability of visiting a travel clinic was approximately 10 times greater for travellers with the high health risk than for those with low health risks and the probability of visiting a travel clinic was 23 times greater for travellers who were aware of the health risks in their country of destination. Another editorial by Pasi AR[16] recommends that the resources at the port of entry like the infrastructure, trained manpower and experience in implementing international health as per international health regulations may be well utilised to provide travel health services to international travellers.

The limitations of the present study are that being centre-based it catered to only those travellers who utilised the vaccination services and hence could not consider all types of travellers.

The current study highlights that the awareness and knowledge of the travellers are limited to the major diseases of concern or to the mandatory vaccination requirements for their travel. The health risks are not perceived by many travellers and can be easily understood and taken care of if detected in time before the travel. Knowledge among travellers regarding existence of the travel health clinics was minimal whereas the existing facilities in view of travel health are very much limited to general physicians. There is a pressing need to have travel medicine as a special branch which will involve disciplines of epidemiology, medicine and public health. Hence, we recommend that more travel health specialists and travel health clinics should be started to match the number of ever-increasing travellers.

Conclusion

The present study showed that the majority of the travellers were going for the job purpose. The most common source of health-related information for them were doctors and friends and family. Some of them were aware of the vaccine-preventable diseases and health-related issues that can be faced during travel but majority felt the need for a professional who can advise regarding health pre-, during and post-travel. Very few people carried some form of health card with them during travel and many faced difficulty in finding health check-up guidelines for travel. There was a felt need for a travel health physician. Hence, we recommend starting dedicated travel health clinics as there is an impending need for travel-related health advice for all ages.

Abstract
. The Neumann–Poincaré (NP) operator, a fundamental operator in potential theory, has attracted renewed attention for its central role in the analysis of surface plasmon resonances (SPRs). SPRs, characterized by non-radiative electromagnetic waves at material interfaces with opposing permittivities, underpin advanced technologies such as bio-sensing and cloaking devices. While spectral properties of the scalar NP operator and SPR dynamics for scalar waves are well-established, their vectorial counterparts in Maxwell’s framework remain poorly understood. This work bridges this gap by introducing a novel symmetrization principle for the matrix-valued Maxwell Neumann–Poincaré (MNP) operator, enabling a spectral decomposition of traces in the 𝐇 ⁡ ( curl , D ) \mathbf{H}(\mathrm{curl},D) space–a foundational advance for electromagnetic theory. Building on this framework, we rigorously characterize the quantum-ergodic localization of weak surface plasmon resonances at material boundaries in the full Maxwell system, thereby settling a long-standing question concerning their quantitative description.

1.2.
Research background and state of the art

The analysis of SPRs is predominantly carried out using layer potential techniques. This approach involves deriving boundary integral equations under resonance conditions, thereby recasting the problem as a transmission eigenvalue problem on the boundary. Key steps include establishing the self‑adjointness of the NP operator via Calderón‑type symmetrization, proving its compactness in suitable Hilbert spaces (which ensures eigenvalues converge to zero), and expressing plasmons in terms of the corresponding eigenfunctions.

The spectral analysis of NP operators continues to attract significant attention, particularly due to their applications in plasmon resonance phenomena and cloaking based on anomalous localized resonance
[
1
,
8
]
. Considerable research has been devoted to NP operators associated with the Laplace equation
[
13
,
27
,
9
,
7
,
24
]
; their spectral properties are fundamental to advances in nanophotonics and metamaterials. More recently, attention has turned to elastic NP operators, whose non‑compact nature poses distinct analytical challenges
[
10
,
11
,
23
]
. In this context, spherical domains provide tractable cases for explicit spectral decomposition
[
21
,
22
]
. For Maxwell equations, however, symmetrizing even the static MNP operator remains a significant challenge. Early efforts circumvented this issue by using layer‑potential theory
[
2
,
6
]
. In the latter work, the authors also derived a set of non‑orthogonal eigenfunctions for the associated equivalent operator. Resonance frequencies for Maxwell’s equations were later computed numerically in
[
20
]
. Recently, an analysis of electromagnetic scattering resonances and Fano-type resonances in nanoparticles with high refractive indices was provided by
[
5
,
15
,
4
]
.

In this paper, our primary focus is on the surface localization of the corresponding family of weak plasmons that arise under weak resonance conditions. For background on surface localization of plasmons in the Laplace equation setting, we refer to
[
12
]
. To address the surface localization of plasmons in the Maxwell system, we are inspired by the approach developed in
[
17
]
for the Helmholtz equation. There, surface localization of transmission eigenfunctions was analyzed under weak resonance conditions. Numerical findings regarding this surface localization can be found in
[
16
]
. Building on these ideas, we formulate and study the corresponding problem of weak resonance within the full Maxwell framework, aiming to establish analogous surface-localization results for weak plasmons. Our analysis, which leverages the orthogonality of the eigenfunctions corresponding to the MNP operator, addresses three core challenges:

•

Symmetrization
: Overcoming the non-self-adjoint character of the MNP operator by constructing tailored bilinear forms and Calderón-type identities in the trace space.

•

Spectral Analysis
: Determining the eigenvalue distribution through a novel Helmholtz decomposition that links the MNP operator to its scalar NP counterpart, while studying the compactness of its restriction to a subspace by using the equivalence of inner-product-induced norms.

•

Boundary Localization
: Developing spectral decomposition methods to rigorously establish the boundary concentration of plasmonic modes, unifying abstract operator theory with layer potential techniques for both general smooth domains and explicit spherical cases.

It is imperative to acknowledge that the Calderón-type identities we employ, as well as similar bilinear forms, were established in
[
2
]
. However, due to a different focus in their research objectives, a thorough investigation into their other properties was not conducted.

The organization of this paper is as follows. Section
2
introduces the fundamental notation and mathematical preliminaries. Section
3
is devoted to proving Theorems
1.1
–
1.4
. Specifically, we establish the symmetrization and spectral decomposition theory for the static MNP operator along with its adjoint on general domains. Building on these results, Section
4
demonstrates quantum ergodic boundary localization in electromagnetic scattering problems for general geometries, thereby completing the proof of Theorem
1.6
. Finally, Section
5
specializes to spherical geometries, where precise exponential decay estimates for weak SPRs are derived, completing the proof of Theorem
1.7
.

3.
Symmetrization and spectral decomposition for the static MNP Operator and its adjoint

The primary objective of this section is to introduce a new bilinear form and verify that it indeed defines an inner product. We subsequently demonstrate that the static MNP operator can be symmetrized under this new inner product, thereby completing the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Furthermore, we establish the equivalence between the norm induced by this new inner product and the standard Sobolev norm. This equivalence enables the study of compactness properties of the static MNP operator, which in turn leads to the completion of Theorem
1.3
. Following similar arguments, we extend our analysis to the corresponding adjoint operator, thereby completing the proofs of Theorems
1.2
and
1.4
.

3.1.
Symmetrization for the static MNP operator and its adjoint

We begin by introducing a weighted bilinear form in
\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D))
. Let us define a bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\partial D};\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D))}
on
\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D))
by

\displaystyle\langle\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\zeta}\rangle_{\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\partial D};\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D))}:=\int_{\partial D}\boldsymbol{\xi}(\boldsymbol{x})\cdot\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\partial D}[\boldsymbol{\zeta}](\boldsymbol{x})\mathrm{d}s(\boldsymbol{x})

(3.1)

for any
\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\zeta}\in\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D))
.

We first verify the positive definiteness of the bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\partial D};\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D))}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The bilinear form given by (
3.1
) is positive-definite on
\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D))
.

Proof.

For any
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D))
, there exists
\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{3}{2}}_{\star}(\partial D))
such that
\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\partial D}[\boldsymbol{\xi}]=\boldsymbol{\eta}
. That is,
\boldsymbol{\xi}=\mathcal{N}_{\partial D}[\boldsymbol{\eta}]
. Moreover, we can write
\boldsymbol{\eta}=\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}V
with
V\in H^{\frac{3}{2}}_{\star}(\partial D)
.
It follows from (
2.19
) and integration by parts that

\displaystyle\langle\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\xi}\rangle_{\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\partial D};\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D))}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial D}\mathcal{N}_{\partial D}[\boldsymbol{\eta}]\cdot\boldsymbol{\eta}\mathrm{d}s(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=-\int_{\partial D}\mathrm{curl}_{\partial D}\boldsymbol{\eta}\mathcal{S}_{\partial D}[\mathrm{curl}_{\partial D}\boldsymbol{\eta}]\mathrm{d}s(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=-\int_{\partial D}\Delta_{\partial D}V\mathcal{S}_{\partial D}[\Delta_{\partial D}V]\mathrm{d}s(\boldsymbol{x}).

(3.2)

If
\langle\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\xi}\rangle_{\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\partial D};\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D))}=0
, then the positive-definiteness of
-\mathcal{S}_{\partial D}
implies
\Delta_{\partial D}V=0
. Consequently, through integration by parts, we obtain
\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}V=0
. This leads to

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\xi}=\mathcal{N}_{\partial D}[\boldsymbol{\eta}]=\mathcal{N}_{\partial D}[\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}V]=0.

Hence, the bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\partial D};\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D))}
is positive-definite and the proof is complete.
∎

By Lemma
3.1
, the bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\partial D};\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D))}

\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D))

\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\partial D};\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D))}

\mathcal{N}_{\partial D}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Proof of Theorem

with respect to the inner product

Since the argument is entirely parallel to that of Theorem

3.2.
Spectral decomposition for the static MNP operator and its adjoint

In this subsection, by utilizing the equivalence between the inner-product-induced norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\partial D};\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D))}
and the standard Sobolev norm
\|\cdot\|_{\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D))}
, we analyze the compactness of the operator
\mathcal{M}_{\partial D}
restricted to
\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D))
and establish its corresponding spectral decomposition theorem. Parallel arguments are then developed for the spectral decomposition of the adjoint operator
\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\partial D}
restricted to
\nabla_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D))
.

The following lemma establishes the fact that
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
\mathcal{M}_{\partial D}
on
\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D))
if and only if
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
\mathcal{K}^{*}_{\partial D}
on
H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D)
.

Lemma 3.4
.

One has
\sigma(\mathcal{M}_{\partial D};\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D)))=\sigma(\mathcal{K}^{*}_{\partial D})\backslash\{1/2\}
.

Proof.

Assume first that
\lambda\neq\frac{1}{2}
is an eigenvalue of
\mathcal{K}^{*}_{\partial D}
.
By the self-adjointness of
\mathcal{K}^{*}_{\partial D}
with respect to the inner product

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{S}_{\partial D};H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D)}
,
\lambda
is also an eigenvalue of

\mathcal{K}_{\partial D}
. Hence there exists a nonzero
\theta\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D)

such that

\displaystyle(\lambda\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{K}_{\partial D})[\theta]=0,

which leads to

\displaystyle(\lambda\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{M}_{\partial D})[\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}\theta]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{I:indentity5}}}{{=}}\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(\lambda\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{K}_{\partial D})[\theta]=0.

(3.6)

If
\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}\theta=0
, then Corollary 3.7 in
[
14
]
implies that
\theta
is constant, yielding a contradiction.

Conversely, if
\lambda\neq\frac{1}{2}
is an eigenvalue of
\mathcal{M}_{\partial D}
, then applying (
3.6
) and together with the fact that
\mathrm{Ker}(\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D})=\mathbb{R}
(recall
[
14
, Corollary 3.7]
), we deduce that

\displaystyle(\lambda\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{K}_{\partial D})[\theta]=c\chi(\partial D),

where
c
is a constant and
\chi(\partial D)
signifies denotes the characteristic function of the boundary
\partial D
, which is given by

\displaystyle\chi(\partial D)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &0,&&\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\partial D,\\
&1,&&\boldsymbol{x}\in\partial D.\end{aligned}\right.

Since
\mathcal{K}_{\partial D}[\chi(\partial D)]=\frac{1}{2}\chi(\partial D)
and
\lambda\neq\frac{1}{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle(\lambda\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{K}_{\partial D})\left[\theta+\frac{c}{\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}\chi(\partial D)\right]=0.

Finally, we recall property
\mathrm{(3)}
in Lemma
2.2
, which completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Furthermore, we establish the following lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

The norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\partial D};\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D))}
induced by the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\partial D};\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D))}
defined in (
3.1
) is equivalent to the Sobelev norm
\|\cdot\|_{\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D))}
given by (
2.13

Proof.

It is obvious that for any
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D))
,

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\xi}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\partial D};\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D))}

\displaystyle=|\int_{\partial D}\boldsymbol{\xi}\cdot\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\partial D}[\boldsymbol{\xi}]\mathrm{d}s(\boldsymbol{x})|

\displaystyle\leq\|\boldsymbol{\xi}\|_{\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D))}\|\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\partial D}[\boldsymbol{\xi}]\|_{\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{3}{2}}_{\star}(\partial D))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\boldsymbol{\xi}\|^{2}_{\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D))}.

On the other hand, from Proposition
2.6
, there exist
\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{3}{2}}_{\star}(\partial D))
with

and

such that

. As a consequence, for some constant
C>0

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\xi}\|_{\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\partial D};\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D))}=-\int_{\partial D}\Delta_{\partial D}V\mathcal{S}_{\partial D}[\Delta_{\partial D}V]\mathrm{d}s(\boldsymbol{x})=\|\Delta_{\partial D}V\|_{\mathcal{S}_{\partial D};H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D)},

In addition, using (
2.13

2.19

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\xi}\|_{\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D))}

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{N}_{\partial D}[\boldsymbol{\eta}]\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D)}

\displaystyle=\|\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}\mathcal{S}_{\partial D}[\Delta_{\partial D}V]\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\Delta_{\partial D}V\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D)},

which implies the conclusion of this lemma and completes the corresponding proof.
∎

4.
Electromagnetic problems and surface localization in the quantum ergodic sense

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.6
. Specifically, we develop a mathematical framework for weak SPRs in electromagnetic systems by employing the spectral decomposition theorem of static MNP operators, which consequently establishes the surface localization of the weak plasmon sequence in the quantum ergodic sense.

4.1.
Scattering problem

Recall the scattering problem of a time-harmonic electromagnetic wave incident on a plasmonic nanoparticle
D
of the form:

\displaystyle D=\boldsymbol{z}+\delta B,

where
\delta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, the point
\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and
B
is bounded and simply connected domain containing the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with the
C^{1,\gamma}
-smooth boundary for some
\gamma\in(0,1)
. The outward unit normal to
\partial D
is denoted by
\boldsymbol{\nu}
. Assume that the homogeneous medium is filled with electric permittivity
\epsilon_{e}
and magnetic permeability
\mu_{e}
and the nanoparticle
D
is characterized by electric permittivity
\epsilon_{c}
and magnetic permeability
\mu_{c}
. The corresponding material parameters are described by

\displaystyle\epsilon_{D}

\displaystyle=\epsilon_{e}\chi(\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\overline{D})+\epsilon_{c}\chi(D),

\displaystyle\mu_{D}

\displaystyle=\mu_{e}\chi(\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\overline{D})+\mu_{c}\chi(D),

and the wave numbers are

\displaystyle k_{D}=\omega\sqrt{\epsilon_{D}\mu_{D}},\quad k_{D}=\omega\sqrt{\epsilon_{D}\mu_{D}}.

The scattering problem is formulated as follows:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\nabla\times\boldsymbol{E}-\mathrm{i}\omega\mu_{D}\boldsymbol{H}=0&&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\partial D,\\
&\nabla\times\boldsymbol{H}+\mathrm{i}\omega\epsilon_{D}\boldsymbol{E}=0&&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\partial D,\\
&\boldsymbol{\nu}\times\boldsymbol{E}|_{+}=\boldsymbol{\nu}\times\boldsymbol{E}|_{-}&&\text{on }\partial D,\\
&\boldsymbol{\nu}\times\boldsymbol{H}|_{+}=\boldsymbol{\nu}\times\boldsymbol{H}|_{-}&&\text{on }\partial D\end{aligned}\right.

(4.1)

subject to the Silver-Müller radiation condition

\displaystyle\lim_{|\boldsymbol{x}|\rightarrow+\infty}|\boldsymbol{x}|(\sqrt{\mu_{e}}(\boldsymbol{H}-\boldsymbol{H}^{i})(\boldsymbol{x})\times\frac{\boldsymbol{x}}{|\boldsymbol{x}|}-\sqrt{\epsilon_{e}}(\boldsymbol{E}-\boldsymbol{E}^{i})(\boldsymbol{x}))=0,

which holds uniformly in
\boldsymbol{x}/|\boldsymbol{x}|
.
Here and throughout the paper, the subscripts
\pm
denote the limits from the exterior
(+)
and interior
(-)
of
D
, respectively.

4.2.
Strong and weak SPRs

The objective of this subsection is threefold:
\mathrm{(i)}
to derive the equivalent boundary integral equation formulation for the Maxwell system (
4.1
),
\mathrm{(ii)}
to provide rigorous definitions of both strong and weak SPRs, and
\mathrm{(iii)}
to conduct a detailed spectral analysis of weak SPRs.

Let

stand for the fundamental solution to the Helmholtz operator
\Delta+k^{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, denoted by

\displaystyle G(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})=-\frac{e^{\mathrm{i}k|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}|}}{4\pi|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}|},\quad\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}~\text{with}~\boldsymbol{x}\neq\boldsymbol{y}.

(4.2)

By replacing the scalar function

in (
1.1

, we correspondingly modify the operators in (
1.1
), (
2.5
), and (
2.1
) to define

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbf{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{t}(\mathrm{div},\partial D)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\phi}

\displaystyle\mapsto\mathcal{M}^{k}_{\partial D}[\boldsymbol{\phi}](\boldsymbol{x})=\int_{\partial D}\boldsymbol{\nu}_{\boldsymbol{x}}\times\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\times G(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\boldsymbol{\phi}(\boldsymbol{y})\mathrm{d}s(\boldsymbol{y});

\displaystyle\rightarrow H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D)\text{ or }H^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle\mapsto\mathcal{S}^{k}_{\partial D}[\phi](\boldsymbol{x})=\int_{\partial D}G(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\phi(\boldsymbol{y})\mathrm{d}s(\boldsymbol{y});

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\xi}

\displaystyle\mapsto\vec{\mathcal{S}}^{k}_{\partial D}[\boldsymbol{\xi}](\boldsymbol{x})=\int_{\partial D}G(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\boldsymbol{\xi}(\boldsymbol{y})\mathrm{d}s(\boldsymbol{y}).

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbf{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{t}(\mathrm{div},\partial D)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\phi}

In analogy with the definitions of strong and weak Minnaert resonances by

Definition 4.2
.

For a fixed
\omega>0
, we say that system (
4.8
) undergoes a strong SPR if, as
\delta\to 0^{+}
, the homogeneous equation

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{\partial B}(\delta)\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\\
\omega\tilde{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\end{pmatrix}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}})=0

admits a nontrivial solution

\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\\
\omega\tilde{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\end{pmatrix}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}
.

In the following analysis, we will weaken the conditions required for strong resonances to occur.

Definition 4.3
.

For a fixed
\omega>0
, if there exists a nontrivial function

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\\
\omega\tilde{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\end{pmatrix}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}\quad\text{with}\quad\Bigl\|\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\\
\omega\tilde{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\end{pmatrix}\Bigr\|_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}}=\mathcal{O}(1),

such that

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\mathscr{A}_{\partial B}(\delta)\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\\
\omega\tilde{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\end{pmatrix}\Bigr\|_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}}\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad\delta\to 0^{+},

(4.9)

then we say that a weak SPR of system (
4.8
) occurs.

According to Definition
4.3
, the occurrence of a weak SPR in system (
4.8
) is closely related to the existence of a nontrivial element

\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\\
\omega\tilde{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\end{pmatrix}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}

that satisfies

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{\partial B}(0)\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\\
\omega\tilde{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\end{pmatrix}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}})=0.

(4.10)

In other words, if such a nontrivial solution to (

4.3.
Incident field

In our analysis, we consider the incident field configuration under consideration, specifically the excitation by a point source.

Generally, let
\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{E}^{i}\\
\boldsymbol{H}^{i}\end{pmatrix}

be the incident electromagnetic field which is a solution to Maxwell’s equations

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\nabla\times\boldsymbol{E}^{i}-\mathrm{i}\omega\mu_{e}\boldsymbol{H}^{i}=0&&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{3},\\

-\dfrac{\mathrm{i}}{\omega\mu_{e}}\nabla\times(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{G}}(k_{e};\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{s})\boldsymbol{p}).\end{pmatrix}

Remark 4.4

Let

Under the condition

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\nu}_{\boldsymbol{y}}\times(\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\times(\boldsymbol{G}_{i}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\cdot)^{\top})\times\boldsymbol{\nu}_{\boldsymbol{y}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{DE:curl}}}{{=}}\nabla_{\partial D,\boldsymbol{y}}\Gamma_{i}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})+\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D,\boldsymbol{y}}\Lambda_{i}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}),

where

\displaystyle\Gamma_{i}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\cdot)\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D),\quad\Lambda_{i}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\cdot)\in H^{\frac{3}{2}}_{\star}(\partial D).

Hence, we obtain the decomposition

\displaystyle\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\times\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\times\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})_{\mathrm{normal}}+\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\times\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})_{\nabla_{\partial D}}

\displaystyle\quad+\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\times\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})_{\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}},

(4.22)

where

\displaystyle\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\times\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})_{\mathrm{normal}}

\displaystyle:=\begin{pmatrix}-((\nabla\times(\boldsymbol{G}_{1}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})^{\top})\cdot\boldsymbol{\nu}_{\boldsymbol{y}})\boldsymbol{\nu}_{\boldsymbol{y}})^{\top}\\
-((\nabla\times(\boldsymbol{G}_{2}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})^{\top})\cdot\boldsymbol{\nu}_{\boldsymbol{y}})\boldsymbol{\nu}_{\boldsymbol{y}})^{\top}\\
-((\nabla\times(\boldsymbol{G}_{3}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})^{\top})\cdot\boldsymbol{\nu}_{\boldsymbol{y}})\boldsymbol{\nu}_{\boldsymbol{y}})^{\top}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\times\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})_{\nabla_{\partial D}}

\displaystyle:=\begin{pmatrix}-(\nabla_{\partial D,\boldsymbol{y}}\Gamma_{1}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}))^{\top}\\
-(\nabla_{\partial D,\boldsymbol{y}}\Gamma_{2}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}))^{\top}\\
-(\nabla_{\partial D,\boldsymbol{y}}\Gamma_{3}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}))^{\top}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\times\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})_{\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}}

\displaystyle:=\begin{pmatrix}-(\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D,\boldsymbol{y}}\Lambda_{1}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}))^{\top}\\
-(\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D,\boldsymbol{y}}\Lambda_{2}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}))^{\top}\\
-(\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D,\boldsymbol{y}}\Lambda_{3}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}))^{\top}\end{pmatrix}.

A completely analogous argument as the proof of formula (
4.4
) yields a similar decomposition for the double curl:

\displaystyle\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\times\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\times\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\times\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\times\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})_{\mathrm{normal}}

\displaystyle\quad+\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\times\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\times\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})_{\nabla_{\partial D}}

\displaystyle\quad+\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\times\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\times\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})_{\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}}.

(4.23)

It remains to complete the proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Beginning with formulas (
4.16
) and (
4.17
), we first investigate the almost sure decay properties of
\nabla\times\vec{\mathcal{S}}^{k}_{\partial D}[\boldsymbol{\phi}_{j}]
.

For
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\mathcal{T}_{\epsilon}(\partial D)
and
\boldsymbol{y}\in{\partial D}
, using (
4.20
) and (
4.4
) yields that

\displaystyle\nabla\times\vec{\mathcal{S}}^{k}_{\partial D}[\boldsymbol{\phi}_{j}](\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial D}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\times(G(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\boldsymbol{\phi}_{j}(\boldsymbol{y}))\mathrm{d}s(\boldsymbol{y})

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial D}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\times\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})_{\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}}\boldsymbol{\phi}_{j}(\boldsymbol{y})\mathrm{d}s(\boldsymbol{y})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}-\int_{\partial D}\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D,\boldsymbol{y}}\Lambda_{1}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\cdot\boldsymbol{\phi}_{j}(\boldsymbol{y})\mathrm{d}s(\boldsymbol{y})\\
-\int_{\partial D}\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D,\boldsymbol{y}}\Lambda_{2}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\cdot\boldsymbol{\phi}_{j}(\boldsymbol{y})\mathrm{d}s(\boldsymbol{y})\\
-\int_{\partial D}\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D,\boldsymbol{y}}\Lambda_{3}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\cdot\boldsymbol{\phi}_{j}(\boldsymbol{y})\mathrm{d}s(\boldsymbol{y})\end{pmatrix}.

Moreover, it follows from Lemma
4.7
that for
i=1,2,3,

\displaystyle\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D,\boldsymbol{y}}\Lambda_{i}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{g:representation}}}{{=}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{\partial D}\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D,\boldsymbol{y}}\Lambda_{i}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\cdot\boldsymbol{\phi}_{j}(\boldsymbol{y})\mathrm{d}s(\boldsymbol{y})\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{\partial D}[\boldsymbol{\phi}_{j}](\boldsymbol{y}).

As a result, we can derive

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}|\int_{\partial D}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\times(G(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\boldsymbol{\phi}_{j}(\boldsymbol{y}))\mathrm{d}s(\boldsymbol{y})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}\sum^{3}_{i=1}|\int_{\partial D}\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D,\boldsymbol{y}}\Lambda_{i}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\cdot\boldsymbol{\phi}_{j}(\boldsymbol{y})\mathrm{d}s(\boldsymbol{y})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\times\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\cdot)_{\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{N}_{\partial D};\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{3}{2}}_{\star}(\partial D))^{3\times 3}}.

Similarly, it follows from (
4.21
) and (
4.4
) that

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}|\int_{\partial D}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\times\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\times(G(k;\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\boldsymbol{\phi}_{j}(\boldsymbol{y}))\mathrm{d}s(\boldsymbol{y})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\times\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\times\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}(k;\boldsymbol{x},\cdot)_{\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{N}_{\partial D};\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{3}{2}}_{\star}(\partial D))^{3\times 3}}.

Hence, there is come constant
C>0
such that for any compact set
K
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\mathcal{T}_{\epsilon}(\partial D)
,

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}\int_{K}|\boldsymbol{E}_{j}(\boldsymbol{x})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle\leq|\mu_{D}|\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}\int_{K}\left|\int_{\partial D}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\times(G(k_{D};\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\boldsymbol{\phi}_{j}(\boldsymbol{y}))\mathrm{d}s(\boldsymbol{y})\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}\int_{K}\left|\int_{\partial D}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\times\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\times(G(k_{D};\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\boldsymbol{\phi}_{j}(\boldsymbol{y}))\mathrm{d}s(\boldsymbol{y})\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle\leq|\mu_{D}|\int_{K}\|\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\times\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}(k_{D};\boldsymbol{x},\cdot)_{\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{N}_{\partial D};\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{3}{2}}_{\star}(\partial D))^{3\times 3}}\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{K}\|\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\times\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\times\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}(k_{D};\boldsymbol{x},\cdot)_{\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{N}_{\partial D};\overset{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{curl}}_{\partial D}(H^{\frac{3}{2}}_{\star}(\partial D))^{3\times 3}}\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle\leq C,

with
\mu_{D}=\mu_{e}=1,k_{D}=k_{e}=\omega
or
\mu_{D}=\mu_{c}=-\tau_{j},k_{D}=k_{c}=\omega\tau_{j}
.

The same argument applied to the magnetic field
\boldsymbol{H}_{j}
yields that for any compact set
K
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\mathcal{T}_{\epsilon}(\partial D)
,

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}\int_{K}|\boldsymbol{H}_{j}(\boldsymbol{x})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\leq C.

Thanks to Lemma
4.6
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{K}|\boldsymbol{E}_{j}(\boldsymbol{x})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}=o(j^{-1}),\quad\int_{K}|\boldsymbol{H}_{j}(\boldsymbol{x})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}=o(j^{-1})

almost surely in the sense of Definition
1.5
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.6
.
∎

Before proceeding to the proof of Theorem

, we need some auxiliary results. For a wave number

Similarly, define

by

\displaystyle\tilde{v}_{n,m}(k;\boldsymbol{x})=j_{n}(k|\boldsymbol{x}|)Y^{m}_{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}),

The solutions to Maxwell’s equations admit a multipole expansion in vector spherical harmonics (cf.
[
6
]
). We define the exterior transverse electric (TE) and transverse magnetic (TM) multipoles (which satisfy the Sommerfeld radiation condition), along with their interior counterparts, as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{E}^{TE}_{n,m}\\[2.84526pt]
\boldsymbol{H}^{TE}_{n,m}\end{pmatrix},\quad\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{E}^{TM}_{n,m}\\[2.84526pt]
\boldsymbol{H}^{TM}_{n,m}\end{pmatrix},\quad\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\boldsymbol{E}}^{TE}_{n,m}\\[2.84526pt]
\tilde{\boldsymbol{H}}^{TE}_{n,m}\end{pmatrix},\quad\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\boldsymbol{E}}^{TM}_{n,m}\\[2.84526pt]
\tilde{\boldsymbol{H}}^{TM}_{n,m}\end{pmatrix}.

That is,

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{E}^{TE}_{n,m}(k;\boldsymbol{x})\\[2.84526pt]
\boldsymbol{H}^{TE}_{n,m}(k;\boldsymbol{x})\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}-\sqrt{n(n+1)}h^{(1)}_{n}(k|\boldsymbol{x}|)\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{2,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}})\\
-\dfrac{\mathrm{i}}{\omega\mu}\nabla\times\left(-\sqrt{n(n+1)}h^{(1)}_{n}(k|\boldsymbol{x}|)\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{2,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}})\right)\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{E}^{TM}_{n,m}(k;\boldsymbol{x})\\[2.84526pt]
{\boldsymbol{H}}^{TM}_{n,m}(k;\boldsymbol{x})\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{\mathrm{i}}{\omega\epsilon}\nabla\times\left(-\sqrt{n(n+1)}h^{(1)}_{n}(k|\boldsymbol{x}|)\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{2,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}})\right)\\[2.84526pt]
-\sqrt{n(n+1)}h^{(1)}_{n}(k|\boldsymbol{x}|)\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{2,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}})\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\boldsymbol{E}}^{TE}_{n,m}(k;\boldsymbol{x})\\
\tilde{\boldsymbol{H}}^{TE}_{n,m}(k;\boldsymbol{x})\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}-\sqrt{n(n+1)}j_{n}(k|\boldsymbol{x}|)\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{2,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}})\\
-\dfrac{\mathrm{i}}{\omega\mu}\nabla\times\left(-\sqrt{n(n+1)}j_{n}(k|\boldsymbol{x}|)\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{2,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}})\right)\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\boldsymbol{E}}^{TM}_{n,m}(k;\boldsymbol{x})\\
\tilde{\boldsymbol{H}}^{TM}_{n,m}(k;\boldsymbol{x})\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{\mathrm{i}}{\omega\epsilon}\nabla\times\left(-\sqrt{n(n+1)}j_{n}(k|\boldsymbol{x}|)\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{2,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}})\right)\\
-\sqrt{n(n+1)}j_{n}(k|\boldsymbol{x}|)\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{2,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}})\end{pmatrix}.

Defining the composite spherical Hankel and Bessel functions as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{n}(k|\boldsymbol{x}|)

\displaystyle=h^{(1)}_{n}(k|\boldsymbol{x}|)+k|\boldsymbol{x}|(h^{(1)}_{n})^{\prime}(k|\boldsymbol{x}|),

\displaystyle=j_{n}(k|\boldsymbol{x}|)+k|\boldsymbol{x}|j^{\prime}_{n}(k|\boldsymbol{x}|),

\displaystyle\nabla\times\boldsymbol{E}^{TE}_{n,m}(k;\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{n(n+1)}}{|\boldsymbol{x}|}\mathcal{H}_{n}(k|\boldsymbol{x}|)\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{1,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}})+\frac{n(n+1)}{|\boldsymbol{x}|}h^{(1)}_{n}(k|\boldsymbol{x}|)Y^{m}_{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}})\hat{\boldsymbol{x}},

\displaystyle\nabla\times\tilde{\boldsymbol{E}}^{TE}_{n,m}(k;\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{n(n+1)}}{|\boldsymbol{x}|}\mathcal{J}_{n}(k|\boldsymbol{x}|)\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{1,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}})+\frac{n(n+1)}{|\boldsymbol{x}|}j_{n}(k|\boldsymbol{x}|)Y^{m}_{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}})\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}.

Additionally, the functions

, and

admit the series representations (cf.
[
19
]

\displaystyle j_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-1)^{p}z^{n+2p}}{2^{p}p!1\cdot 3\cdot~\cdots~\cdot(2n+2p+1)},

\displaystyle y_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(2n)!}{2^{n}n!}\sum_{p=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-1)^{p}z^{2p-n-1}}{2^{p}p!(-2n+1)\cdot(-2n+3)\cdot~\cdots~\cdot(-2n+2p-1)},

\displaystyle h^{(1)}_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=j_{n}(z)+\mathrm{i}y_{n}(z).

\displaystyle j_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{z^{n}}{(2n+1)!!}\left(1+\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n})\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{n(2n-1)!!}{\mathrm{i}z^{n+1}}\left(1+\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n})\right)

For a spherical boundary

\displaystyle\quad-\left(\frac{r}{r^{\prime}}\right)^{n+2}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n})\right)Y_{n,m}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}})\hat{\boldsymbol{x}},

\displaystyle\nabla\times\nabla\times\vec{\mathcal{S}}^{k}_{\partial D}[\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{1,n}](\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{r^{\prime}}\left(\frac{r}{r^{\prime}}\right)^{n+1}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n})\right)\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{1,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}),

\displaystyle=\frac{k(kr)}{2n+1}\left(\frac{r}{r^{\prime}}\right)^{n+1}\left(1+\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n})\right)\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{2,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}})

as

+\infty

(2)

For
r^{\prime}=|\boldsymbol{x}|<r
(interior domain), one has

\displaystyle\nabla\times\vec{\mathcal{S}}^{k}_{\partial D}[\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{1,n}](\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{r^{\prime}}{r}\right)^{n}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n})\right)\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{2,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}),

\displaystyle\nabla\times\vec{\mathcal{S}}^{k}_{\partial D}[\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{2,n}](\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{r^{\prime}}{r}\right)^{n-1}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n})\right)\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{1,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}})

\displaystyle\quad-\left(\frac{r^{\prime}}{r}\right)^{n-1}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n})\right)Y_{n,m}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}})\hat{\boldsymbol{x}},

\displaystyle\nabla\times\nabla\times\vec{\mathcal{S}}^{k}_{\partial D}[\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{1,n}](\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{r^{\prime}}\left(\frac{r^{\prime}}{r}\right)^{n}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n})\right)\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{1,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}),

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\sqrt{n(n+1)}}{r^{\prime}}\left(\frac{r^{\prime}}{r}\right)^{n}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n})\right)Y_{n,m}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}})\hat{\boldsymbol{x}},

\displaystyle\nabla\times\nabla\times\vec{\mathcal{S}}^{k}_{\partial D}[\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{2,n}](\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=\frac{k(kr)}{2n+1}\left(\frac{r^{\prime}}{r}\right)^{n}\left(1+\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n})\right)\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{2,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}})

as
n
tends to
+\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{q}
be an arbitrary fixed vector in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Then for all
\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
|\boldsymbol{x}|>|\boldsymbol{y}|
, the dyadic Green’s function admits the expansion (cf.
[
6
]
):

\displaystyle G(k;\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y})\mathbf{q}

\displaystyle=-\sum^{+\infty}_{n=1}\frac{\mathrm{i}k}{n(n+1)}\frac{\epsilon}{\mu}\sum^{n}_{m=-n}\boldsymbol{E}^{TM}_{n,m}(k;\boldsymbol{x})\overline{\tilde{\boldsymbol{E}}^{TM}_{n,m}(k;\boldsymbol{y})}\cdot\mathbf{q}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum^{+\infty}_{n=1}\frac{\mathrm{i}k}{n(n+1)}\sum^{n}_{m=-n}\boldsymbol{E}^{TE}_{n,m}(k;\boldsymbol{x})\overline{\tilde{\boldsymbol{E}}^{TE}_{n,m}(k;\boldsymbol{y})}\cdot\mathbf{q}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{k}\sum^{+\infty}_{n=1}\sum^{n}_{m=-n}\nabla v_{n,m}(k;\boldsymbol{x})\overline{\nabla\tilde{v}_{n,m}(k;\boldsymbol{y})}\cdot\mathbf{q},

while for
|\boldsymbol{x}|<|\boldsymbol{y}|
,

\displaystyle G(k;\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y})\mathbf{q}

\displaystyle=-\sum^{+\infty}_{n=1}\frac{\mathrm{i}k}{n(n+1)}\frac{\epsilon}{\mu}\sum^{n}_{m=-n}\overline{\tilde{\boldsymbol{E}}^{TM}_{n,m}(k;\boldsymbol{x})}\boldsymbol{E}^{TM}_{n,m}(k;\boldsymbol{y})\cdot\mathbf{q}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum^{+\infty}_{n=1}\frac{\mathrm{i}k}{n(n+1)}\sum^{n}_{m=-n}\overline{\tilde{\boldsymbol{E}}^{TE}_{n,m}(k;\boldsymbol{x})}\boldsymbol{E}^{TE}_{n,m}(k;\boldsymbol{y})\cdot\mathbf{q}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{k}\sum^{+\infty}_{n=1}\sum^{n}_{m=-n}\overline{\nabla\tilde{v}_{n,m}(k;\boldsymbol{x})}\nabla v_{n,m}(k;\boldsymbol{y})\cdot\mathbf{q}.

Here,
v_{n,m}
and
\tilde{v}_{n,m}
are defined by formulas (
5.4
) and (
5.5
), respectively. Furthermore, from the Maxwell system (
4.1
), we deduce that for every
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\partial D
,

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\nabla\times\nabla\times\boldsymbol{E}-\omega^{2}\epsilon\mu\boldsymbol{E}=0,\\[5.69054pt]
&\nabla\times\nabla\times\boldsymbol{H}-\omega^{2}\epsilon\mu\boldsymbol{H}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(5.6)

Combining (
5.5
), the first equation of (
5.6
), and the explicit forms of
\tilde{\boldsymbol{E}}^{TM}_{n,m}
and
\nabla\times\tilde{\boldsymbol{E}}^{TE}_{n,m}
, we obtain for
|\boldsymbol{x}|>|\boldsymbol{y}|
,

\displaystyle\nabla\times\vec{\mathcal{S}}^{k}_{\partial D}[\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{1,n}](\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=\nabla\times\int_{\partial D}\Big(-\sum^{+\infty}_{p=1}\frac{\mathrm{i}k}{p(p+1)}\frac{\epsilon}{\mu}\sum^{p}_{q=-p}\boldsymbol{E}^{TM}_{p,q}(k;\boldsymbol{x})\overline{\tilde{\boldsymbol{E}}^{TM}_{p,q}(k;\boldsymbol{y})}\cdot\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{1,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{y}})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum^{+\infty}_{p=1}\frac{\mathrm{i}k}{p(p+1)}\sum^{p}_{q=-p}\boldsymbol{E}^{TE}_{p,q}(k;\boldsymbol{x})\overline{\tilde{\boldsymbol{E}}^{TE}_{p,q}(k;\boldsymbol{y})}\cdot\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{1,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{y}})

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{k}\sum^{+\infty}_{p=1}\sum^{p}_{q=-p}\nabla v_{p,q}(k;\boldsymbol{x})\overline{\nabla\tilde{v}_{p,q}(k;\boldsymbol{y})}\cdot\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{1,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{y}})\Big)\mathrm{d}s(\boldsymbol{y})

\displaystyle=-\frac{k}{r\sqrt{n(n+1)}}\frac{1}{\omega\mu}\nabla\times\boldsymbol{E}^{TM}_{n,m}(k;\boldsymbol{x})\mathcal{J}_{n}(kr)\int_{\partial D}\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{1,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{y}})\cdot\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{1,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{y}})\mathrm{d}s(\boldsymbol{y})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}krh^{(1)}_{n}(kr^{\prime})\mathcal{J}_{n}(kr)\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{2,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}).

A similar computation yields, again for
|\boldsymbol{x}|>|\boldsymbol{y}|
,

\displaystyle\nabla\times\vec{\mathcal{S}}^{k}_{\partial D}[\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{2,n}](\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathrm{i}k}{\sqrt{n(n+1)}}\nabla\times\boldsymbol{E}^{TE}_{n,m}(k;\boldsymbol{x})j_{n}(kr)\int_{\partial D}\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{2,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{y}})\cdot\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{2,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{y}})\mathrm{d}s(\boldsymbol{y})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathrm{i}kr^{2}}{r^{\prime}}\mathcal{H}_{n}(kr^{\prime})j_{n}(kr)\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{1,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}})-\frac{\mathrm{i}kr^{2}\sqrt{n(n+1)}}{r^{\prime}}h^{(1)}_{n}(kr^{\prime})j_{n}(kr)Y_{n,m}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}})\hat{\boldsymbol{x}},

\displaystyle\nabla\times\nabla\times\vec{\mathcal{S}}^{k}_{\partial D}[\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{1,n}](\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=-\frac{k}{r\sqrt{n(n+1)}}\frac{1}{\omega\mu}\nabla\times\nabla\times\boldsymbol{E}^{TM}_{n,m}(k;\boldsymbol{x})\mathcal{J}_{n}(kr)\int_{\partial D}\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{1,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{y}})\cdot\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{1,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{y}})\mathrm{d}s(\boldsymbol{y})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathrm{i}kr}{r^{\prime}}\mathcal{H}_{n}(kr^{\prime})\mathcal{J}_{n}(kr)\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{1,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}})-\frac{\mathrm{i}kr\sqrt{n(n+1)}}{r^{\prime}}h^{(1)}_{n}(kr^{\prime})\mathcal{J}_{n}(kr)Y_{n,m}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}})\hat{\boldsymbol{x}},

\displaystyle\nabla\times\nabla\times\vec{\mathcal{S}}^{k}_{\partial D}[\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{2,n}](\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathrm{i}k}{\sqrt{n(n+1)}}\nabla\times\nabla\times\boldsymbol{E}^{TE}_{n,m}(k;\boldsymbol{x})j_{n}(kr)\int_{\partial D}\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{2,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{y}})\cdot\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{2,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{y}})\mathrm{d}s(\boldsymbol{y})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}k^{3}r^{2}h^{(1)}_{n}(kr^{\prime})j_{n}(kr)\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{2,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}).

In addition, one has that for
|\boldsymbol{x}|<|\boldsymbol{y}|
,

\displaystyle\nabla\times\vec{\mathcal{S}}^{k}_{\partial D}[\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{1,n}](\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=\frac{k}{r\sqrt{n(n+1)}}\frac{1}{\omega\mu}\overline{\nabla\times\tilde{\boldsymbol{E}}^{TM}_{n,m}(k;\boldsymbol{x})}\mathcal{H}_{n}(kr)\int_{\partial D}\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{1,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{y}})\cdot\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{1,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{y}})\mathrm{d}s(\boldsymbol{y})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}krj_{n}(kr^{\prime})\mathcal{H}_{n}(kr)\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{2,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}),

\displaystyle\nabla\times\vec{\mathcal{S}}^{k}_{\partial D}[\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{2,n}](\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{i}k}{\sqrt{n(n+1)}}\overline{\nabla\times\tilde{\boldsymbol{E}}^{TE}_{n,m}(k;\boldsymbol{x})}h^{(1)}_{n}(kr)\int_{\partial D}\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{2,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{y}})\cdot\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{2,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{y}})\mathrm{d}s(\boldsymbol{y})

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{i}kr^{2}}{r^{\prime}}\mathcal{J}_{n}(kr^{\prime})h^{(1)}_{n}(kr)\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{1,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}})+\frac{\mathrm{i}kr^{2}\sqrt{n(n+1)}}{r^{\prime}}j_{n}(kr^{\prime})h^{(1)}_{n}(kr)Y_{n,m}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}})\hat{\boldsymbol{x}},

\displaystyle\nabla\times\nabla\times\vec{\mathcal{S}}^{k}_{\partial D}[\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{1,n}](\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=\frac{k}{r\sqrt{n(n+1)}}\frac{1}{\omega\mu}\overline{\nabla\times\nabla\times\tilde{\boldsymbol{E}}^{TM}_{n,m}(k;\boldsymbol{x})}\mathcal{H}_{n}(kr)\int_{\partial D}\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{1,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{y}})\cdot\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{1,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{y}})\mathrm{d}s(\boldsymbol{y})

\displaystyle=\frac{-\mathrm{i}kr}{r^{\prime}}\mathcal{J}_{n}(kr^{\prime})\mathcal{H}_{n}(kr)\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{1,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}})+\frac{-\mathrm{i}kr\sqrt{n(n+1)}}{r^{\prime}}j_{n}(kr^{\prime})\mathcal{H}_{n}(kr)Y_{n,m}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}})\hat{\boldsymbol{x}},

\displaystyle\nabla\times\nabla\times\vec{\mathcal{S}}^{k}_{\partial D}[\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{2,n}](\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{i}k}{\sqrt{n(n+1)}}\overline{\nabla\times\nabla\times\tilde{\boldsymbol{E}}^{TE}_{n,m}(k;\boldsymbol{x})}h^{(1)}_{n}(kr)\int_{\partial D}\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{2,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{y}})\cdot\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{2,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{y}})\mathrm{d}s(\boldsymbol{y})

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{i}k^{3}r^{2}j_{n}(kr^{\prime})h^{(1)}_{n}(kr)\boldsymbol{\phi}^{m}_{2,n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}).

Substituting the explicit expressions for

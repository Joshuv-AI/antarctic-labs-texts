Uniform convergence for Gaussian kernel ridge regression
Paul Dommel, Rajmadan Lakshmanan

Abstract
This paper establishes the first polynomial convergence rates for Gaussian kernel ridge regression (KRR) with a fixed hyperparameter in both the uniform and the L 2 L^{2} -norm. The uniform convergence result closes a gap in the theoretical understanding of KRR with the Gaussian kernel, where no such rates were previously known. In addition, we prove a polynomial L 2 L^{2} -convergence rate in the case, where the Gaussian kernel’s width parameter is fixed. This also contributes to the broader understanding of smooth kernels, for which previously only sub-polynomial L 2 L^{2} -rates were known in similar settings. Together, these results provide new theoretical justification for the use of Gaussian KRR with fixed hyperparameters in nonparametric regression. Keywords: kernel methods · reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces · statistical machine learning · Gaussian kernel



1
Introduction

Kernel methods constitute a powerful class of algorithms, widely employed
in machine learning for pattern analysis and data classification.
They allow for the modeling of complex, non-linear relationships by
implicitly mapping input data into high-dimensional feature spaces.
This approach has proven effective in a variety of application domains,
including bioinformatics (cf.
Ben-Hur and Noble 2005
), finance (cf.
Kim 2003
)
and image classification (cf.
Harchaoui and Bach 2007
), where capturing
intricate non-linear patterns is critical. A prominent technique within
this framework is kernel ridge regression, which integrates the flexibility
of kernel methods with the stability of ridge regression to perform
regularized learning. It aims to reconstruct an unknown function from
observed data, with the choice of the kernel function playing an essential
role for the resulting approximation. Among the many available kernel
functions, the Gaussian kernel is particularly favored due to its
simplicity, strong theoretical foundations, and widespread practical
success, making it a cornerstone of numerous kernel-based algorithms.

In this work, we study the convergence properties of Gaussian kernel
ridge regression (KRR) on the unit cube
[0,1]^{d}
, with a particular
focus on both
L^{2}
and uniform convergence rates. Unlike much
of the existing literature that considers vanishing kernel widths,
we analyze the fixed-width regime, which introduces unique theoretical
challenges due to the infinite smoothness and strong localization
of the Gaussian kernel. To address these, we employ an exponentially
decaying regularization scheme that compensates for the slow approximation
of the general functions by smooth kernels (cf.
Smale and Zhou 2003
).
This novel regularization scheme is justified by the operator theoretic
insides provided in
Dommel and Pichler 2024
.

The main contribution of this work is the derivation of polynomial
convergence rates for Gaussian kernel ridge regression with a fixed
width parameter, both in the
L^{2}
-norm and the uniform norm. Importantly,
the target function is assumed to belong to a Sobolev space of sufficiently
high order, but it is not required to lie within the Gaussian reproducing
kernel Hilbert space or any of its power spaces. The convergence results
hold with high probability, reflecting the inherent randomness of
the sample data. The key approach involves first proving convergence
in the
L^{2}
-norm and then applying an interpolation inequality
developed in
Dommel and Pichler 2024
, which transfers these rates to uniform
convergence as well.

Related Literature

The convergence of estimators obtained through kernel ridge regression
has been a subject of extensive research within the field of kernel
methods. Foundational studies exploring this topic in a broad context
can be found in the works of
Caponnetto and De Vito 2006
,
Steinwart and Christmann 2008

as well as
Steinwart et al. 2009
. A direct study of the approach
for the Gaussian kernel is given in
Xiang and Zhou 2009
and
Eberts and Steinwart 2011
.
Indeed, the latter reference demonstrates that Gaussian kernel ridge
regression achieves the minimax optimal convergence rate in
L^{2}
,
provided that the width parameter tends to zero. In contrast, the
scenario where the kernel width parameter remains fixed has received
comparatively less attention in the literature. This is due to the
fact that, in such cases, the approximation error decays only logarithmically
with the norm of the approximating function, as discussed in
Smale and Zhou 2003

or
Steinwart and Fischer 2021
.

Similarly, the question of uniform convergence in Gaussian kernel
ridge regression has received limited attention in the literature.
This is particularly the case when the target function does not belong
to the associated reproducing kernel Hilbert space. For the case,
where the function is contained in the space, relevant results have
been established by
Dommel and Pichler 2023
and
Fischer and Steinwart 2020
.
To the best of our knowledge,
Fischer and Steinwart 2020
were also
the first to establish uniform convergence for target functions not
belonging to the space. Their analysis focuses on kernel regression
using kernels that induce Sobolev/Besov spaces, addressing functions
in the power spaces induced by the kernel. However, this framework
does not directly extend to the Gaussian kernel, since all of its
interpolation spaces consist solely of infinitely differentiable functions, making
it applicable only to a very narrow class of target functions. In
particular, these spaces do not include any polynomial functions,
which underscores a fundamental distinction in the theoretical treatment
of the Gaussian kernel compared to other types of kernels.

Outline of the paper.

Section
2
addresses reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces,

The following Section
3
reviews the
key prerequisites for the Gaussian kernel, including the bound on

Section

L^{2}
-norm, while the final Section

this paper. Section
6
concludes with a summary of
the results and discusses directions for future research.

2
Preliminaries and notation

2.1
Reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces

Reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces
(RKHS) constitute an exclusive
class of Hilbert spaces, which are considered in a variety of different
mathematical fields. Due to the famous kernel trick (cf.
Bishop 2006
),
RKHS are of particular interest in the field of mathematical learning
theory. In what follows, we introduce these spaces as well as notations,
which we use throughout this paper.

We consider a symmetric, positive definite kernel
k\colon\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}

defined on a compact set
\mathcal{X}
. On the linear span
H_{0}\coloneqq\operatorname{span}\big\{k(\cdot,x)\colon x\in\mathcal{X}\big\}

of differently located kernel functions, we define the inner product

\left\langle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}k(\cdot,x_{i}),\sum_{j=1}^{m}\beta_{j}k(\cdot,y_{j})\right\rangle_{k}\coloneqq\sum_{i,j=1}^{n,m}\alpha_{i}\beta_{j}k(x_{i},y_{i}),

as well as the corresponding norm
\left\|f\right\|_{k}=\sqrt{\left\langle f,f\right\rangle_{k}}
.
The associated reproducing kernel Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{k}

is the closure of
H_{0}
with respect to the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k}
.
The name building property of
\mathcal{H}_{k}
is the
reproducing
property

\left\langle k(\cdot,x),f(\cdot)\right\rangle_{k}=f(x),\hskip 10.00002ptf\in\mathcal{H}_{k},

(2.1)

which holds true for every
x\in\mathcal{X}
. In other words, a point
evaluation at
x\in\mathcal{X}
is an inner product with the kernel
function
k_{x}(\cdot)\coloneqq k(\cdot,x)
. For this reason we refer
to
k_{x}
as
point evaluation function
at
x
.

Closely linked to the kernel
k
and occurring naturally in investigating
RKHS spaces is the operator
L_{k}\colon L^{2}(P)\to\mathcal{H}_{k}

with

(L_{k}w)(y)\coloneqq\int_{\mathcal{X}}w(z)k(x,z)p(z)dx

and
w\in L^{2}(P)
. Here,
p\colon\mathcal{X}\to[0,\infty)
is
a strictly positive density of the probability measure
P
.

L_{k}
is a self adjoint and positive definite operator and therefore
possesses a root
L_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}
such that
L_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}\circ L_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}=L_{k}
.
This root relates the Lebesgue space
L^{2}(P)
with
\mathcal{H}_{k}

in the sense that
\mathcal{H}_{k}=

L_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}(L^{2}(P))

as well as
\left\|f\right\|_{k}=\left\|w\right\|_{L^{2}(P)}
,
if
f=L_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}w
. Similarly, it holds for the inner product
of
f=L_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}w
and
g=L_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}v
that

\left\langle f,g\right\rangle_{k}=\left\langle w,v\right\rangle_{L^{2}}.

(2.2)

A key quantity for analyzing the complexity of the Reproducing Kernel
Hilbert Space (RKHS) are the
maximal marginal degrees of freedom

(see
Rudi et al. 2015
,
Bach 2013
,
Dommel and Pichler 2021
). They
are defined as

\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(\lambda)\coloneqq\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\left\langle(\lambda+L_{k})^{-1}k_{x},k_{x}\right\rangle_{k}=\lambda^{-1}\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\min_{w\in L^{2}}\lambda\left\|w\right\|_{L^{2}(P)}^{2}+\left\|L_{k}w-k_{x}\right\|_{k}^{2},

(2.3)

with argument
\lambda>0
. Importantly, this quantity should not
be confused with the
effective dimension

\mathcal{N}_{\text{eff}}(\lambda)\coloneqq\Tr((\lambda+L_{k})^{-1}L_{k}),

(2.4)

which also frequently appears in the context of kernel methods. Both
are connected through
\mathcal{N}_{x}(\lambda)=\left\langle k_{x},(\lambda+L_{k})^{-1}k_{x}\right\rangle_{k}

as
\mathcal{N}_{\text{eff}}(\lambda)=\E_{x}\mathcal{N}_{x}(\lambda)

and
\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(\lambda)=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{N}_{x}(\lambda)

provided that
x\sim P
(cf.
Rudi et al. 2015
).

2.2
Kernel ridge regression

In what follows, we specify the regression setting considered in this
work and outline the main principles used to analyze the associated
regression error.

The basis of the model is the data set
D=\left\{(x_{1},y_{1}),\dots,(x_{n},y_{n})\right\}
,
which consists of input-output pairs collected from a general experimental
procedure. From a mathematical perspective, these pairs are independent
samples drawn according to an unknown joint distribution
P
. The
input components
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
take values in some compact
set
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and they are distributed with
respect to the corresponding marginal probability measure
P_{x}
.
Throughout this work, we restrict ourselves to the case, where
\mathcal{X}=[0,1]^{d}

equipped with a probability measure
P_{x}
, which has a density

p_{x}
satisfying the condition

0<\inf_{z\in\mathcal{X}}p_{x}(z)\leq\sup_{z\in\mathcal{X}}p_{x}(z)<\infty.

(2.5)

These assumptions follow the framework in
Dommel and Pichler 2024
, which
provides the theoretical foundation for handling the Gaussian kernel
later on.

The output variables
y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
stem from an
additive signal plus noise model, i.e.,
y_{i}=f_{0}(x_{i})+\varepsilon_{i}

with
f_{0}(x_{i})=\E(y\,|\,x=x_{i})
capturing the relationship
between input and output. The random variables
\varepsilon_{i}=y_{i}-f_{0}(x_{i})
,

i=1,\dots,n
, represent the noise in the measurements. They are
also independent and identically distributed, satisfying the orthogonality
property
\E(\left.\varepsilon\right|x)=\E(\left.y-f_{0}(x)\right|x)=0
,
where
(x,y)\sim P
. Moreover, we assume that the noise satisfies
the moment condition

\E\left(\left.\left|\varepsilon\right|^{\ell}\right|x\right)=\E\left(\left.\left|y-f_{0}(x)\right|^{\ell}\right|x\right)\leq C_{\varepsilon}^{\ell}\ell!\hskip 10.00002pt\ell\in\mathbb{N},x\in\mathcal{X},

(2.6)

which is an assumption also employed in the literature (see for example

Fischer and Steinwart 2020
).

Regression aims at reconstructing the function
f_{0}
, commonly
referred to as conditional expectation

or regression function,
from the available data. In the context of reproducing kernel Hilbert
spaces,

kernel ridge regression (KRR) constitutes the most
prominent method for this objective. This approach employs the minimizer
of the objective

\min_{f\in\mathcal{H}_{k}}\lambda\left\|f\right\|_{k}^{2}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(f(x_{i})-y_{i})^{2}

(2.7)

as an estimator of the target function
f_{0}
. Here,
k
is a previously
selected kernel,
\mathcal{H}_{k}
its associated RKHS and
\lambda>0

is a positive regularization parameter, controlling the influence
of the regularizing term. As a consequence of the famous representer
theorem (cf.
Cucker and Zhou 2007
),
the minimizer of the objective (
2.7
) has the explicit
representation

\hat{f}_{\lambda}(\cdot)\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\hat{w}_{\lambda,i}k(\cdot,x_{i})\quad\text{with}\quad\hat{w}_{\lambda}=\left(\lambda+K\right)^{-1}y,

K=(k(x_{i},x_{j}))_{i,j=1}^{n}
and
y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})^{\top}
.
While this representation is crucial from a practical point of view,
an alternative characterization is often more suitable for theoretical
analysis. This characterization builds on the operator
L_{k}^{D}\colon\mathcal{H}_{k}\to\mathcal{H}_{k}

(L_{k}^{D}f)(x)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(x_{i})k(x,x_{i}),

(2.8)

which resembles a discretized version of the integral operator

(L_{k}w)(x)=\int_{\mathcal{X}}w(z)k(x,z)p_{x}(z)dx,

(2.9)

introduced in Section
2.1
above. Specifically, by
setting
\hat{f}_{D}(\cdot)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}k(\cdot,x_{i})
,
the estimator admits the representation

\hat{f}_{\lambda}(\cdot)=\left((\lambda+L_{k}^{D})^{-1}\hat{f}_{D}\right)(\cdot)

(2.10)

(see
Caponnetto and De Vito 2006
). Building on this
representation, we analyze the approximation error
\left\|\hat{f}_{\lambda}-f_{\text{0}}\right\|

following the well-known approach from
Caponnetto and De Vito 2006
, which
involves replacing the discrete operator
(\lambda+L_{k}^{D})^{-1}

with its continuous counterpart
(\lambda+L_{k}^{D})^{-1}
. This
substitution facilitates the analysis by transforming the problem
from a discrete and random setting to a continuous and deterministic
one. In this work, we focus on the Gaussian kernel, for which we develop
the necessary analytical tools in the next section.

3
The Gaussian kernel

The Gaussian kernel,
k(x,y)=e^{-a\left\|x-y\right\|_{2}^{2}}

with
a>0
, is one of the most extensively studied kernels in the
theory and practice of kernel methods. Its simple form and remarkable
effectiveness in real-world applications make it a cornerstone of
many learning algorithms.

In this section, we recall several key results related to the Gaussian
kernel, including properties of the associated integral operator
L_{k}
,
as well as the approximation of smooth functions by elements of the
corresponding reproducing kernel Hilbert space. The starting point
is the relationship between
L_{k}
from (
2.9
) and its
discrete analogue
L_{k}^{D}
from (
2.8
). We connect
the regularized versions of these operators through a concentration
inequality, assuming that the underlying samples
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}

are independent random variables according to the measure
P_{x}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\sim P_{x}
as well
as
L_{k}
and
L_{k}^{D}
defined as in (
2.9
) and (
2.8
),
respectively. Assume further that
\lambda
satisfies

\frac{4}{3}\tau\,g(\lambda)\frac{\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(\lambda)}{n}+\sqrt{2\tau\,g(\lambda)\frac{\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(\lambda)}{n}}\leq\frac{1}{2}

(3.1)

with

g(\lambda)=\ln\left(2e\frac{(\lambda+\mu_{1})\mathcal{N}_{\text{eff}}(\lambda)}{\mu_{1}}\right)

and
\tau>0
. Then, the inequality

\displaystyle\left\|L_{k,\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda+L_{k}^{D})^{-1}L_{k,\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{k}\to\mathcal{H}_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq 2

(3.2)

holds with probability at least
1-2\exp(-\tau)
. Here,
\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(\lambda)

and
\mathcal{N}_{\text{eff}}(\lambda)
are the maximal marginal
degrees of freedom and the effective dimension, respectively.

Proof.

The assertion corresponds to
Dommel and Pichler 2024
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

The operator
L_{k,\lambda}
is considered here as a map
L_{k,\lambda}\colon\mathcal{H}_{k}\to\mathcal{H}_{k}
,
rather than
L_{k,\lambda}\colon L^{2}([0,1]^{d})\to\mathcal{H}_{k}
.
However, this operator is still well-defined as the Gaussian RKHS,
when defined over
[0,1]^{d}
, is continuously embedded in
L^{2}([0,1]^{d})
.

To facilitate the analysis that follows, we define the event

A\coloneqq\left\{\left\|L_{k,\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda+L_{k}^{D})^{-1}L_{k,\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{k}\to\mathcal{H}_{k}}\leq 2\right\},

(3.3)

which depends on the specific configurations of the samples
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
.
This event is a recurring condition in the derivation of generalization
bounds, as it ensures a form of spectral control over the empirical
inverse
(\lambda+L_{k}^{D})^{-1}
. Notably, under the condition
(
3.1
) on the regularization parameter
\lambda
,
this event holds with high probability or, more specifically, with
probability at least
1-2\exp(-\tau)
.

The operator norm in (
3.2
) is taken with respect
to the
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k}
-norm, both on the domain
and the range of the operator. However, since we aim to measure errors
in the
L^{2}
-norm later on, it is necessary to adapt this bound
accordingly. The following proposition provides the required adjustment.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\omega\in A
(cf. (
3.3
)).
Then, the bound

\displaystyle\left\|(\lambda+L_{k}^{D}(\omega))^{-1}L_{k,\lambda}f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\big(\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lambda\left\|f\right\|_{k}^{2}\big)

(3.4)

holds for every
f\in\mathcal{H}_{k}
.

Proof.

Building on the general identity
\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\left\langle L_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}f,L_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}f\right\rangle_{k}

from (
2.2
), we have that

\displaystyle\left\|(\lambda+L_{k}^{D})^{-1}L_{k,\lambda}f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=

\displaystyle\left\|L_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda+L_{k}^{D})^{-1}L_{k,\lambda}f\right\|_{k}^{2}\leq\left\|L_{k,\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda+L_{k}^{D})^{-1}L_{k,\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{k}\to\mathcal{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}^{2}\left\|L_{k,\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}f\right\|_{k}^{2}

by involving the submultiplicativity of the operator norm as well
as
\left\|L_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{k,\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{k}\to\mathcal{H}_{k}}\leq 1
.
Thus, for every
\omega\in A
, it follows that

\left\|L_{k,\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda+L_{k}^{D})^{-1}L_{k,\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{k}\to\mathcal{\mathcal{H}}_{k}}^{2}\left\|L_{k,\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}f\right\|_{k}^{2}\leq 4\left(\lambda\left\|f\right\|_{k}^{2}+\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right),

whenever
f\in\mathcal{H}_{k}
. This is the assertion.
∎

The regularization condition in Proposition
3.1

above critically depend on
\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(\lambda)
and its
growth behavior for
\lambda\to 0
. In the case of the Gaussian kernel,
this behavior is partially characterized. More precisely, there exists
an explicit upper bound on
\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(\lambda)
, which
we present in the next proposition.

Proposition 3.4
.

There are constants
C_{\infty},C_{\text{eff}}>0
and
\lambda_{0}\in(0,1)

such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\infty}\,(\ln\lambda^{-1})^{2d}

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\text{eff}}\,(\ln\lambda^{-1})^{d}

(3.6)

holds for all
0\leq\lambda\leq\lambda_{0}<1
.

Proof.

The first assertion is then a direct consequence of the considerations
in
Dommel and Pichler 2024
and the associated proof. For the
second we defer to Lemma
A.3
in the appendix.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Note that the bound (
3.5
) allows the use of regularization
parameters that are exponentially small in the sample size, while
still satisfying the condition in (
3.1
). This removes
a key obstacle in establishing fast convergence rates for Gaussian
kernel ridge regression with a fixed bandwidth.

The results above lay the foundation for studying Gaussian kernel
ridge regression, as they establish a connection between the discrete
regression problem (
2.7
) and its continuous counterpart

\vartheta_{0,\lambda}\coloneqq\min_{f\in L^{2}}\lambda\left\|f\right\|_{k}^{2}+\left\|f-f_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

(3.7)

which admits the explicit solution

f_{\lambda}\coloneqq L_{k,\lambda}^{-1}L_{k}f_{0}

(3.8)

(see
Cucker and Zhou 2007
). To bound the approximation
error, it is thus necessary to characterize the rate at which
f_{\lambda}

converges to the target function
f_{0}
as
\lambda\to 0
. To this
end, we explore the approximation of functions with a given regularity
by elements of the Gaussian RKHS, next. Specifically, we consider
target functions
f_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathcal{X})
, where
H^{s}(\mathcal{X})

denotes the Sobolev space of functions, which are weakly differentiable
up to order
s
(see
Alt and Nürnberg 2016
for a more
rigorous definition). For such target functions,
Smale and Zhou 2003

provide a bound on the norm-constrained best approximation in the
RKHS, relating the approximation error to the regularity of
f_{0}
.
We restate the specific result next.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
f_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathcal{X})
. Then, it
holds that

\inf_{\left\|f\right\|_{k}\leq R}\left\|f-f_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{f_{0}}(\ln R)^{-\frac{s}{2}},\quad R\geq 1,

(3.9)

where
C_{f_{0}}
is a constant independent of
R
, but dependent
on
f_{0}
.

Remark 3.7
.

The bound (
3.9
) is of
crucial importance for many of the subsequent findings, as it essentially
determines the rate of approximation. However, we strongly conjecture
that the current rate is suboptimal. In particular, we believe it
can be improved to

\inf_{\left\|f\right\|_{k}\leq R}\left\|f-f_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\tilde{C}_{f_{0}}\ln(R)^{-2s}.

for some different constant
\tilde{C}_{f_{0}}
depending on
f_{0}
.
This improved rate would sharpen and strengthen all results that follow.
For this reason, we indicate several points in the analysis where
such an enhancement would have a concrete impact. A thorough investigation
of this conjecture, however, lies beyond the scope of this work and
is left for future research.

Based on the bound (
3.9
) we immediately get in
(
3.7
) for
f_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathcal{X})
that

\vartheta_{0,\lambda}\leq\lambda R^{2}+C_{f_{0}}(\ln R)^{-\frac{s}{2}}=\lambda^{\frac{1}{3}}+C_{f_{0}}(\ln(\lambda^{-\frac{1}{3}}))^{-\frac{s}{2}}=\lambda^{\frac{1}{3}}+3^{\frac{s}{2}}C_{f_{0}}(\ln(\lambda^{-1}))^{-\frac{s}{2}}

by employing the specific choice
R\coloneqq\lambda^{-\frac{1}{3}}.

Moreover, there exists some
\bar{\lambda}
, such that

\lambda^{\frac{1}{3}}\leq(\ln(\lambda^{-1}))^{-\frac{s}{2}}

for all
0<\lambda\leq\bar{\lambda}
and therefore

\displaystyle\vartheta_{0,\lambda}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{\frac{1}{3}}+3^{\frac{s}{2}}C_{f_{0}}(\ln(\lambda^{-1}))^{-\frac{s}{2}}\leq\left(1+3^{\frac{s}{2}}C_{f_{0}}\right)(\ln(\lambda^{-1}))^{-\frac{s}{2}}=\bar{C}_{f_{0}}(\ln(\lambda^{-1}))^{-\frac{s}{2}}

(3.10)

with
\bar{C}_{f_{0}}\coloneqq\big(1+3^{\frac{s}{2}}C_{f_{0}}\big)
.

The results discussed in this section are primarily aimed at establishing

L^{2}
-convergence later on. However, since we are mainly interested
in uniform convergence, it is necessary to establish a connection
between the two types of convergence. This is the focus of the interpolation
results discussed in the following section.

3.1
Interpolation

To analyze convergence later on, it is often more convenient to study
the function

f_{\lambda}=L_{k,\lambda}^{-1}L_{k}f_{0}

and relate it to the initial estimator, rather than working with the
estimator directly. One advantage of this approach is that
f_{\lambda}

admits a more explicit representation in terms of the eigenvalues

(\mu_{\ell})_{\ell=1}^{\infty}
and eigenfunctions
(\varphi_{\ell})_{\ell=1}^{\infty}

of the operator
L_{k}
, which build an orthonormal basis in
L^{2}
(cf.
Cucker and Zhou 2007
).
This representation is

f_{\lambda}(y)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{\ell}c_{\ell}}{\mu_{\ell}+\lambda}\varphi_{\ell}(y)\quad\text{and}\quad(f_{\lambda}-f_{0})(y)=\lambda\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{c_{\ell}}{\mu_{\ell}+\lambda}\varphi_{\ell}(y)

(3.11)

where
c_{\ell}=\left\langle f_{0},\varphi_{\ell}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}

are the basis coefficients of
f_{0}
. Furthermore, it holds that

\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\left|f_{\lambda}(x)-f_{0}(x)\right|\eqqcolon\left\|f_{\lambda}-f_{0}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}\leq\lambda\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\left|\frac{c_{\ell}}{\mu_{\ell}+\lambda}\right|\left\|\varphi_{\ell}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})},

which relates uniform error with the decay of the basis coefficients
as well as the uniform norm of the eigenfunctions. While this decay
is tightly related to the regularity of
f_{0}
, the behavior of

\left\|\varphi_{\ell}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}
is specific
to the choice of the kernel, which, in our case, is the Gaussian kernel.

In what follows, we pick up this idea and derive an interpolation
inequality that links the error in the
L^{2}
-norm and to the error
in the uniform norm. To this end, we recall an upper bound on
\left\|\varphi_{\ell}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}

first and provide the corresponding interpolation result subsequently.

Theorem 3.8
.

For
\mathcal{X}=[0,1]^{d}
, the eigenfunctions
(\varphi_{\ell})_{\ell=1}^{\infty}

of
L_{k}
are bounded by

\sup_{x\in[0,1]^{d}}\left|\varphi_{\ell}(x)\right|\leq C_{\varphi}\ell^{2},

(3.12)

where
C_{\varphi}>0
is a constant independent of the index
\ell
.

Proof.

See
Dommel and Pichler 2024
.
∎

Theorem 3.9

(Interpolation general)
.

Let
k
be the Gaussian kernel and
\mathcal{X}=[0,1]^{d}
. For

f=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}c_{\ell}\varphi_{\ell}\in L^{2}
with
\left\|f\right\|_{\alpha}^{2}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{c_{\ell}^{2}}{\ell^{-2\alpha}}<\infty

it holds for all
\alpha>3
that

\left\|f\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}\leq C_{\infty,2}\left\|f\right\|_{\alpha}^{\frac{3}{\alpha}}\cdot\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\frac{3}{\alpha}},

(3.13)

where
C_{\infty,2}
is a constant independent of
f
.

Proof.

See
Dommel and Pichler 2024

∎

The main limitation of the interpolation inequality above is that
it relates the decay of the coefficients rather than the regularity
of the function itself. Specifically, these coefficients are typically
unknown, as the basis functions
\varphi_{\ell}
are not explicitly
available. However, it is still possible to establish a connection
between the value of the series
\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{c_{\ell}^{2}}{\ell^{-2\alpha}}

and the regularity of the underlying function. We address this condition
first for functions in the
\mathcal{H}_{k}
, and then address the
more interesting case, where the function does not belong to the RKHS.

Proposition 3.10

(Interpolation
f\in\mathcal{H}_{k}
)
.

Let
k
be the Gaussian kernel and
\mathcal{X}=[0,1]^{d}
. For

f\in\mathcal{H}_{k}
it holds for all
\alpha>3
that

\left\|f\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}\leq C_{\infty,k}\left\|f\right\|_{k}^{\frac{3}{\alpha}}\left(\frac{2(\alpha+1)}{c}\right)^{3d+\frac{3d}{2\alpha}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\frac{3}{\alpha}},

(3.14)

where
C_{\infty,k}
is a constant independent of
f
and
c\coloneqq\min\left\{c_{\mu},1\right\}
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{H}_{k}
. Then, there exists a sequence
(a_{\ell})_{\ell=1}^{\infty}

with
\left\|f\right\|_{k}^{2}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}a_{\ell}^{2}

and
f=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}a_{\ell}\mu_{\ell}^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi_{\ell}
.
Moreover, it follows for
\left\|f\right\|_{\alpha}^{2}

that

\left\|f\right\|_{\alpha}^{2}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{\ell}^{2}\mu_{\ell}}{\ell^{-2\alpha}}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{k}^{2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{\ell}}{\ell^{-2\alpha}}

as each coefficient
a_{\ell}^{2}
is bounded by
\left\|f\right\|_{k}^{2}

. Employing the bound (
A.1
) on the eigenvalues
(\mu_{\ell})_{\ell=1}^{\infty}
,
we get for the latter term that

\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{\ell}}{\ell^{-2\alpha}}\leq A_{\mu}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-c_{\mu}\ln(\ell)\ell^{\frac{1}{d}}}}{\ell^{-2\alpha}}=A_{\mu}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}e^{-\ln(\ell)(c_{\mu}\ell^{\frac{1}{d}}-2\alpha)}.

Splitting the series into two parts, it follows that

\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}e^{-\ln(\ell)(c_{\mu}\ell^{\frac{1}{d}}-2\alpha)}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\ell_{0}}e^{-\ln(\ell)(c_{\mu}\ell^{\frac{1}{d}}-2\alpha)}+\sum_{\ell=\ell_{0}}^{\infty}e^{-\ln(\ell)(c_{\mu}\ell^{\frac{1}{d}}-2\alpha)},

where
\ell_{0}\coloneqq\left\lceil\left(c_{\mu}^{-1}2(\alpha+1)\right)^{d}\right\rceil
.
For the latter term observe that
(c_{\mu}\ell^{\frac{1}{d}}-2\alpha)\geq 2

as
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
and thus

\sum_{\ell=\ell_{0}}^{\infty}e^{-\ln(\ell)(c_{\mu}\ell^{\frac{1}{d}}-2\alpha)}\leq\sum_{\ell=\ell_{0}}^{\infty}e^{-2\ln(\ell)}\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\ell^{-2}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6},

by the famous Euler formula. The amount of terms in the first sum
are
\left(c_{\mu}^{-1}2(\alpha+1)\right)^{d}
, with the maximal
value bounded by
\left(c_{\mu}^{-1}2(\alpha+1)\right)^{2d\alpha}.

Thus, the series is bounded by

\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{\ell}}{\ell^{-2\alpha}}\leq\left(c_{\mu}^{-1}2(\alpha+1)\right)^{2d\alpha+d}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6},

which provides the bound

\left\|f\right\|_{\alpha}^{2}\leq A_{\mu}\left\|f\right\|_{k}^{2}\left(\left(c_{\mu}^{-1}2(\alpha+1)\right)^{2d\alpha+d}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\right)\leq\tilde{A}_{\mu}\left\|f\right\|_{k}^{2}\left(c^{-1}2(\alpha+1)\right)^{2d\alpha+d}.

with
c=\min\{c_{\mu},1\}
. Employing this bound in the interpolation inequality (
3.13
),
it follows that

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\infty,2}\left\|f\right\|_{\alpha}^{\frac{3}{\alpha}}\cdot\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\frac{3}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\infty,2}\tilde{A}_{\mu}\left\|f\right\|_{k}^{\frac{3}{\alpha}}\left(c_{\mu}^{-1}2(\alpha+1)\right)^{3d+\frac{3d}{2\alpha}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\frac{3}{\alpha}}

with
C_{\infty,k}\coloneqq C_{\infty,2}\cdot\tilde{A}_{\mu}
. This is
the assertion.
∎

To analyze the decay of the coefficients for more general functions,
we now adopt a different approach. Consider the general function

\displaystyle f_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}c_{\ell}\varphi_{\ell}\in L^{2}(P_{x})

and recall that the corresponding minimizer of the optimization problem
(
3.7
) can be expressed as

f_{\lambda}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{\ell}c_{\ell}}{\lambda+\mu_{\ell}}\varphi_{\ell}.

By expanding the objective (
3.7
) in terms of the basis
coefficients as well, it follows that

\displaystyle\lambda\left\|f_{\lambda}\right\|_{k}^{2}+\left\|f_{\lambda}-f_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{\ell}c_{\ell}^{2}}{(\lambda+\mu_{\ell})^{2}}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\mu_{\ell}c_{\ell}}{\lambda+\mu_{\ell}}-c_{\ell}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\lambda+\mu_{\ell})c_{\ell}^{2}}{(\lambda+\mu_{\ell})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\lambda\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{c_{\ell}^{2}}{(\lambda+\mu_{\ell})}.

Hence, to establish an upper bound on each
c_{\ell}^{2}
it is sufficient
to evaluate the objective value
\vartheta_{0,\lambda}
at the sequence
of eigenvalues
(\mu_{\ell})_{\ell=1}^{\infty}
. To this end, we
employ the bound (
3.10
) on the objective value of

\vartheta_{0,\lambda}
. The following proposition formalizes this
approach and provides a bound on the decay rate of the basis coefficients

(c_{\ell})_{\ell=1}^{\infty}
in terms of the regularity of the
function
f_{0}
.

Proposition 3.11

(Coefficient decay)
.

Let
f_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathcal{X})
. Then,
the coefficients
(c_{\ell})_{\ell=1}^{\infty}
in the basis expansion

f_{0}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}c_{\ell}\varphi_{\ell}
satisfy the
bound

|c_{\ell}|\leq c_{f_{0}}\ell^{-\frac{s}{4d}}

(3.15)

for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

As a consequence of the upper bound (
A.2
) on the eigenvalues,
we have that

\frac{1}{2}c_{\ell}^{2}\leq\mu_{\ell}\sum_{h=1}^{\infty}\frac{c_{h}^{2}}{\mu_{\ell}+\mu_{h}}=\bar{C}_{f_{0}}(\ln(\mu_{\ell}^{-1}))^{-\frac{s}{2}}\leq\bar{C}_{f_{0}}\ln\big(B_{\mu}^{-1}e^{\ell^{\frac{1}{d}}}\big)^{-\frac{s}{2}}=\bar{C}_{f_{0}}(-\ln(B_{\mu})+\ln(e^{\ell^{\frac{1}{d}}})\big)^{-\frac{s}{2}}.

Thus, if
\ell\geq\big(2\ln(B_{\mu})\big)^{d},
it follows that

\bar{C}_{f_{0}}(-\ln(B_{\mu})+\ln(e^{\ell^{\frac{1}{d}}})\big)^{-\frac{s}{2}}\leq\bar{C}_{f_{0}}(\frac{1}{2}\ln(e^{\ell^{\frac{1}{d}}})\big)^{-\frac{s}{2}}=\bar{C}_{f_{0}}2^{\frac{s}{2}}\ell^{-\frac{s}{2d}}.

Setting
\tilde{c}_{f_{0}}\coloneqq\max\left\{\max_{\ell<\big(2\ln(B_{\mu})\big)^{d}}\frac{c_{\ell}}{\ell^{-\frac{s}{2d}}},\bar{C}_{f_{0}}2^{\frac{s}{2}}\right\}

it holds

\frac{1}{2}c_{\ell}^{2}\leq\tilde{c}_{f_{0}}\ell^{-\frac{s}{2d}},

which provides the assertion by taking the root and adjusting the
constant to
c_{f_{0}}\coloneqq\sqrt{2\tilde{c}_{f_{0}}}
.
∎

Remark 3.12
.

Note that the coefficients likely decay significantly faster than
what is suggested by (
3.15
). The slow decay stems again
from the (likely) suboptimal estimates in (
3.9
)
or (
3.10
).

Based on these results of this section we study the convergence of
the estimator in
L^{2}
for Gaussian kernel ridge regression next.

4
Convergence in
L^{2}

In this section, we analyze the convergence of Gaussian kernel ridge
regression with respect to the
L^{2}
-norm. More specifically, we
demonstrate that the approximation error satisfies the bound

\left\|\hat{f}_{\lambda_{n}}-f_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq Cn^{\frac{s}{2d+s}}\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

(4.1)

where
k(x,y)=\exp\left(-a\left\|x-y\right\|_{2}^{2}\right)

is the underlying kernel,
\lambda_{n}\coloneqq\exp\left(n^{-\frac{2}{2d+s}}\right)

the underlying regularization parameter and
C
some constant independent
of
n
. Unlike previous results, including the result in
Eberts and Steinwart 2011
,
which typically consider a varying precision parameter, we focus on
the less-studied case where the precision parameter
a
remains fixed.
This setting is often overlooked due to the fact that the approximation
error decays only logarithmically. Nevertheless, we demonstrate that
fast convergence rates can still be achieved by employing an exponentially
decaying regularization scheme, which effectively compensates for
the limited approximation power.

Recall the underlying estimator

\hat{f}_{\lambda}(\cdot)=(\lambda+L_{k}^{D})^{-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}k(\cdot,x_{i})\right)=\hat{f}_{\varepsilon,\lambda}+\hat{f}_{0,\lambda},

where

\hat{f}_{\varepsilon,\lambda}\coloneqq(\lambda+L_{k}^{D})^{-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}k(\cdot,x_{i})\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{f}_{0,\lambda}\coloneqq(\lambda+L_{k}^{D})^{-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{0}(x_{i})k(\cdot,x_{i})\right),

are the corresponding noise and approximation components, respectively.
These functions yield the error decomposition

\left\|\hat{f}_{\lambda}-f_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 4\left(\left\|\hat{f}_{\varepsilon,\lambda}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\hat{f}_{0,\lambda}-f_{\lambda}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|f_{\lambda}-f_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right),

(4.2)

where
f_{\lambda}=L_{k,\lambda}^{-1}L_{k}f_{0}
is the minimizer
of (
3.7
). Thus, in order to assess the norm of interest (
4.1
),
it is sufficient to bound all three terms on the right hand side of
(
4.2
) individually. Note here that the first two
terms in the decomposition are random, as they depend on the underlying
samples
(x_{1},y_{1}),\dots,(x_{n},y_{n})
, while the final term
is deterministic.

To analyze the approximation error, we now address the different terms
in (
4.2
) separately, following the approach of
Caponnetto and De Vito 2006
.
The key technical tool underlying all subsequent results is a concentration
inequality stated in the following central lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
be a
probability space and
\xi
be a random variable on
\Omega
taking
values in a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}.
Assume there are
two constants
\sigma^{2},L>0
such that

\E\left\|\xi-\E\xi\right\|_{\mathcal{K}}^{m}\leq\frac{1}{2}m!\sigma^{2}L^{m-2}.

(4.3)

Then,

P\left[\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}-\E\xi\right\|_{\mathcal{K}}\leq 2\left(\frac{L}{n}+\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\tau\right]\geq 1-2e^{-\tau}

(4.4)

holds for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\tau>0
. In particular, (
4.3
)
holds if

\displaystyle\left\|\xi\right\|_{\mathcal{K}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L}{2}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{a.s.}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\E\left\|\xi\right\|_{\mathcal{K}}^{2}\leq\sigma^{2}.

Proof.

For the proof we refer to
Caponnetto and De Vito 2006

∎

The subsequent analysis of the error terms builds on this concentration
inequality, combined with the bounds (
3.5
) and (
3.6
)
on
\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(\lambda)
and
\mathcal{N}_{\text{eff}}(\lambda)
.
These bounds require the regularization parameter
\lambda
to be
sufficiently small. However, since we choose a regularization sequence
that tends to zero, we know that these conditions will eventually
be satisfied. For the finitely many points, where the conditions
are not met, the constants involved can be adjusted accordingly. Therefore,
in the analysis, we proceed with these bounds without explicitly restating
the condition on
\lambda
. Similarly, we assume that (
3.1
)
is satisfied, as the regularization sequence is chosen such that the
left hand side of (
3.1
) tends to
0
as
n\to\infty
.

We start with a bound on the norm
\left\|\hat{f}_{\varepsilon,\lambda}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

of the noise component first.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\tau>0
and assume the distribution of
the noise variables satisfies the condition (
2.6
)
with constant
C_{\varepsilon}
. For
\lambda=\lambda(n)=\exp\left(-n^{\frac{2}{2d+s}}\right)

there are events
B_{1},B_{2}
and a constant
C
independent of

n
and
\tau
such that

\left\|\hat{f}_{\varepsilon,\lambda}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\tau^{2}n^{-\frac{s}{2d+s}}

(4.5)

holds for every
\omega\in A\cap B_{1}\cap B_{2}
. The events
B_{i}

have probability at least
1-2\exp(-\tau)
with
i=1,2
.

Proof.

Setting
g_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}k(\cdot,x_{i})

we get from (
3.4
) that

\displaystyle\left\|(\lambda+L_{k}^{D})^{-1}g_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\left(\left\|(\lambda+L_{k})^{-1}g_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lambda\left\|(\lambda+L_{k})^{-1}g_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{k}^{2}\right)

holds for every
\omega\in A
. Building on the concentration inequality
from Lemma
4.1
, we now bound the two norms
on the right hand side above. For that, assume
\varepsilon
has
the same distribution as
\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{n}

and choose some
x\sim P_{x}
. Moreover, set
\xi\coloneqq\varepsilon\cdot(\lambda+L_{k})^{-1}k_{x}

as well as
\xi_{i}\coloneqq\varepsilon_{i}(\lambda+L_{k})^{-1}k_{x_{i}}
.
It immediately follows that

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}=(\lambda+L_{k})^{-1}g_{\varepsilon}

as well as

\E\xi=\E\E\left(\left.\varepsilon(\lambda+L_{k})^{-1}k_{x}\right|x\right)=\E\left((\lambda+L_{k})^{-1}k_{x}\E\left(\left.\varepsilon\right|x\right)\right)=0

by the tower property of the conditional expectation. The corresponding
moments are bounded by

\displaystyle\E\left\|\xi\right\|_{L^{2}}^{m}

\displaystyle=\E\varepsilon^{m}\left\|(\lambda+L_{k})^{-1}k_{x}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{m}

\displaystyle=\E\E\left(\left.\varepsilon^{m}\left\|(\lambda+L_{k})^{-1}k_{x}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{m}\right|x\right)

\displaystyle=\E\left\|(\lambda+L_{k})^{-1}k_{x}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{m}\E\left(\left.\varepsilon^{m}\right|x\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon}^{m}m!\E\left\|(\lambda+L_{k})^{-1}k(\cdot,x)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{m},

where we employed (
2.6
) for the last inequality. For
the remaining expectation, observe the bounds

\displaystyle\E\left\|(\lambda+L_{k})^{-1}k(\cdot,x)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\E\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\mu_{\ell}}{\lambda+\mu_{\ell}}\right)^{2}\varphi_{\ell}^{2}(x)\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{\ell}}{\lambda+\mu_{\ell}}=\mathcal{N}_{\text{eff}}(\lambda)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}

\displaystyle\left\|(\lambda+L_{k})^{-1}k(\cdot,x)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\mu_{\ell}}{\lambda+\mu_{\ell}}\right)^{2}\varphi_{\ell}^{2}(x)\leq\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(\lambda)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{a.s.}.

Note that these inequalities follows by expanding the definition of

\mathcal{N}_{\text{eff}}(\lambda)
and
\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(\lambda)

in terms of the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of
L_{k}
. Thus,
we have that

\E\left\|\xi\right\|_{L^{2}}^{m}\leq\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(\lambda)^{\frac{m}{2}-1}\E\left\|(\lambda+L_{k})^{-1}k(\cdot,x)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=C_{\varepsilon}^{m}m!\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(\lambda)^{\frac{m}{2}-1}\mathcal{N}_{\text{eff}}(\lambda).

Setting
L\coloneqq C_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(\lambda)}
,

\sigma\coloneqq C_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\mathcal{N}_{\text{eff}}(\lambda)}

as well as

B_{1}\coloneqq\left\{\omega\in\Omega\colon\left\|(\lambda+L_{k}^{D})^{-1}g_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 8\left(\frac{C_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(\lambda)}}{n}+\frac{C_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{N}_{\text{eff}}(\lambda)}{\sqrt{n}}\right)^{2}\tau^{2}\right\}

we get that
P(B_{1})\geq 1-2e^{-\tau}
from Lemma
4.1
.
Using the same arguments for the second term, it follows that

B_{2}\coloneqq\left\{\omega\in\Omega\colon\lambda\left\|(\lambda+L_{k}^{D})^{-1}g_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{k}^{2}\leq 8\left(\frac{C_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(\lambda)}}{n}+\frac{C_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{N}_{\text{eff}}(\lambda)}{\sqrt{n}}\right)^{2}\tau^{2}\right\}

has probability
P(B_{2})\geq 1-2e^{-\tau}
. Hence, we have that

\left\|\hat{f}_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 16\left(\frac{C_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(\lambda)}}{n}+\frac{C_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\mathcal{N}_{\text{eff}}(\lambda)}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)^{2}\tau^{2}

for every
\omega\in A\cap B_{1}\cap B_{2}
. Furthermore, using the
bounds (
3.5
) and (
A.4
) as well as
employing the regularization parameter
\lambda=\lambda(n)=\exp\left(-n^{\frac{2}{2d+s}}\right)
,
it follows that

\displaystyle 16\left(\frac{C_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(\lambda)}}{n}+\frac{C_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\mathcal{N}_{\text{eff}}(\lambda)}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)^{2}\tau^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 16C_{\varepsilon}^{2}\tau^{2}\left(C_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{2}}n^{\frac{2d}{(2d+s)}-1}+C_{\text{eff}}^{\frac{1}{2}}n^{\frac{2d}{2(2d+s)}-\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 16C_{\varepsilon}^{2}\tau^{2}\left((C_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{2}}+C_{\text{eff}}^{\frac{1}{2}})n^{\frac{2d}{2(2d+s)}-\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=16C_{\varepsilon}^{2}\tau^{2}((C_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{2}}+C_{\text{eff}}^{\frac{1}{2}}))^{2}n^{-\frac{s}{2d+s}}

as
n^{2\alpha}\leq n^{\alpha}
holds, whenever the exponent
\alpha

is negative. This is the assertion.
∎

After analyzing the noise component, we now turn to the approximation
component. While the approach is similar, the approximation error
converges to
\vartheta_{0,\lambda}
rather than to zero. For the
reader’s convenience, we divide the analysis into two
lemmas. The first establishes an adapted concentration result, and
the second applies a specific choice of the regularization parameter
to derive the corresponding regression rate.

Lemma 4.3
.

There is a event
C
such that

\left\|\hat{f}_{0,\lambda}-f_{\lambda}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 64\left(\frac{\left\|f_{0}-f_{\lambda}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}\sqrt{\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(\lambda)}}{n}+\frac{\left\|f_{0}-f_{\lambda}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}\sqrt{\mathcal{N}_{\text{eff}}(\lambda)}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)^{2}\tau^{2}+24\vartheta_{0,\lambda},

(4.6)

holds for every
\omega\in A\cap C
and
P(C)\geq 1-2\exp(-\tau)
.

Proof.

We establish the claim in two steps. First, we reformulate the norm

\left\|\hat{f}_{0,\lambda}-f_{\lambda}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\left\|(\lambda+L_{k}^{D})^{-1}L_{k}^{D}f_{0}-f_{\lambda}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

to align it with the framework of Lemma
4.1
.
Then, in the second step, we make use of the concentration inequality
(
4.4
), which provides the assertion.

For the first step recall that
\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\left\|L_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}f\right\|_{k}^{2}

and assume that
\omega\in A
(cf. (
3.3
)). Thus,
we have that

\displaystyle\left\|(\lambda+L_{k}^{D})^{-1}L_{k}^{D}f_{0}-f_{\lambda}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|L_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{k,\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{k}\to\mathcal{H}_{k}}^{2}\left\|L_{k,\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda+L_{k}^{D})^{-1}L_{k,\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{k,\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(L_{k}^{D}f_{0}-(\lambda+L_{k}^{D})f_{\lambda})\right\|_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|L_{k,\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda+L_{k}^{D})^{-1}L_{k,\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{k}\to\mathcal{H}_{k}}^{2}\left\|L_{k,\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(L_{k}^{D}f_{0}-(\lambda+L_{k}^{D})f_{\lambda})\right\|_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\left\|L_{k,\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(L_{k}^{D}f_{0}-(\lambda+L_{k}^{D})f_{\lambda})\right\|_{k}^{2},

where we employed that
\left\|L_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{k,\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{k}\to\mathcal{H}_{k}}^{2}\leq 1

and
\left\|L_{k,\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda+L_{k}^{D})^{-1}L_{k,\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{k}\to\mathcal{H}_{k}}^{2}\leq 4
,
whenever
\omega\in A
. Furthermore, we get from
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2a^{2}+2b^{2}

as well as
\left\|\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{k,\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{k}\text{$\to\mathcal{H}_{k}$}}\leq 1

that

\displaystyle 4\left\|L_{k,\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(L_{k}^{D}f_{0}-(\lambda+L_{k}^{D})f_{\lambda})\right\|_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 8\left(\left\|\lambda L_{k,\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}f_{\lambda}\right\|_{k}^{2}+\left\|L_{k,\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}L_{k}^{D}(f_{0}-f_{\lambda})\right\|_{k}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 8\left(\left\|\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}f_{\lambda}\right\|_{k}^{2}+\left\|L_{k,\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}L_{k}^{D}(f_{0}-f_{\lambda})\right\|_{k}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 8\left(\lambda\left\|f_{\lambda}\right\|_{k}^{2}+\left\|L_{k,\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}L_{k}^{D}(f_{0}-f_{\lambda})\right\|_{k}^{2}\right),

where the first term is bounded by
\lambda\left\|f_{\lambda}\right\|_{k}^{2}\leq\vartheta_{0,\lambda}
,
as it is contained in the objective (
3.7
). In other words,
it holds that

\displaystyle\left\|(\lambda+L_{k}^{D})^{-1}L_{k}^{D}f_{0}-f_{\lambda}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 8\left(\vartheta_{0,\lambda}+\left\|L_{k,\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}L_{k}^{D}(f_{0}-f_{\lambda})\right\|_{k}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 8\left(\vartheta_{0,\lambda}+2\left\|L_{k,\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(L_{k}^{D}-L_{k})(f_{0}-f_{\lambda})\right\|_{k}^{2}+2\left\|L_{k,\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}L_{k}(f_{0}-f_{\lambda})\right\|_{k}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 8\left(\vartheta_{0,\lambda}+2\left\|L_{k,\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(L_{k}^{D}-L_{k})(f_{0}-f_{\lambda})\right\|_{k}^{2}+2\left\|L_{k,\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}L_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}(f_{0}-f_{\lambda})\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 8\left(\vartheta_{0,\lambda}+2\left\|L_{k,\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(L_{k}^{D}-L_{k})(f_{0}-f_{\lambda})\right\|_{k}^{2}+2\left\|(f_{0}-f_{\lambda})\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 8\left(3\vartheta_{0,\lambda}+2\left\|L_{k,\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(L_{k}^{D}-L_{k})(f_{0}-f_{\lambda})\right\|_{k}^{2}\right)

as
\left\|(f_{0}-f_{\lambda})\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
is again contained in the objective (
3.7
) as well as (
2.2
). It thus remains to assess the second term. To this end, we now involve
the concentration inequality from Lemma
4.1
.
For
x\sim P_{x}
, we set
\xi\coloneqq(f_{0}-f_{\lambda})(x)\cdot L_{k,\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}k_{x}
,
which obviously fulfills the requirement
n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}=L_{k,\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}L_{k}^{D}(f_{0}-f_{\lambda})
.
To show the assertion, it thus remains, to bound the maximal value
of
\left\|\xi\right\|_{k}
as well as
\E\left\|\xi\right\|_{k}^{2}
.
It holds that

\left\|(f_{0}-f_{\lambda})(x)\cdot L_{k,\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}k(\cdot,x)\right\|_{k}^{2}=(f_{0}-f_{\lambda})(x)^{2}\left\|L_{k,\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}k(\cdot,x)\right\|_{k}^{2}\leq\left\|f_{0}-f_{\lambda}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}^{2}\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(\lambda)

and thus

\left\|(f_{0}-f_{\lambda})(x)\cdot L_{k,\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}k(\cdot,x)\right\|_{k}\leq\left\|f_{0}-f_{\lambda}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}\sqrt{\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(\lambda)}

almost surely. Similarly, we get for the second part that

\displaystyle\E\left\|(f_{0}-f_{\lambda})(x)\cdot L_{k,\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}k(\cdot,x)\right\|_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|f_{0}-f_{\lambda}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}^{2}\E\left\|L_{k,\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}k(\cdot,x)\right\|_{k}^{2}

Setting
L\coloneqq\left\|f_{0}-f_{\lambda}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}\sqrt{\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(\lambda)}

and
\sigma\coloneqq\left\|f_{0}-f_{\lambda}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}\mathcal{N}_{\text{eff}}(\lambda)

and employing Lemma
4.1
provides the assertion.
∎

Based on the inequality (
4.6
), we now derive an
explicit bound on the approximation error
\left\|\hat{f}_{0,\lambda}-f_{\lambda}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
.
Specifically, we employ the same regularization parameter as in Lemma
4.2

and express the bound explicitly in terms of the sample size
n
.
To this end, it is necessary to control both the uniform error
\left\|f_{0}-f_{\lambda}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}^{2}

and the objective value
\vartheta_{0,\lambda}
. We achieve this
by applying the interpolation inequality (
3.13
),
which requires that the target function
f_{0}
is sufficiently smooth
as well as the decay condition given in (
3.10
).

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\lambda=\exp\left(-n^{\frac{2}{2d+s}}\right)

and
f_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathcal{X})
, where
s>12d
. Then, there is
a constant
C_{2}
(independent of
\tau
and
n
) such that the
bound

\left\|(\lambda+L_{k}^{D})^{-1}L_{k}^{D}f_{0}-f_{\lambda}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{2}(1+\tau^{2})n^{-\frac{s}{2d+s}}

holds for every
\omega\in A\cap C
with
P(C)\geq 1-2\exp(-\tau)
.

Proof.

To verify the assertion, we demonstrate that the uniform error

\left\|f_{0}-f_{\lambda}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}^{2}

is bounded in
\lambda
first. To this end, it is sufficient to
show that
\left\|f_{\lambda}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}\leq B

for some constant
B>0
, which is independent of
\lambda
. It holds
that

\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\left|f_{\lambda}(x)\right|\leq\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\mu_{\ell}c_{\ell}}{\mu_{\ell}+\lambda}\right|\left|\varphi_{\ell}(x)\right|\leq\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}|c_{\ell}||\varphi_{\ell}(x)|\leq C_{\varphi}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}|c_{\ell}|\ell^{2}

by employing the bound (
3.12
) on the eigenfunctions
of
L_{k}
. Furthermore, from Proposition
3.11
it
follows that
\left|c_{\ell}\right|\leq\bar{C}_{f_{0}}\ell^{-3+\delta}
for some
\delta>0
(as
s>12
)
and therefore

\left|f_{\lambda}(x)\right|\leq C_{\varphi}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}|c_{\ell}|\ell^{2}\leq C_{\varphi}\bar{C}_{f_{0}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\ell^{-3+\varepsilon}\ell^{2}<\infty,

where the right hand side is independent of
\lambda
. Hence, by
the same as approach as in the proof of Lemma
4.2
,
it follows with
\tilde{C}\coloneqq\left\|f_{0}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}+C_{\varphi}C_{f_{0}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\ell^{-3+\varepsilon}\ell^{2}

that

\displaystyle 64\left(\frac{\left\|(f_{0}-f_{\lambda})\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}\sqrt{\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(\lambda)}}{n}+\frac{\left\|(f_{0}-f_{\lambda})\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}\sqrt{\mathcal{N}_{\text{eff}}(\lambda)}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)^{2}\tau^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 64\left(\frac{\tilde{C}\sqrt{\mathcal{N}_{\infty}(\lambda)}}{n}+\frac{\tilde{C}\sqrt{\mathcal{N}_{\text{eff}}(\lambda)}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)^{2}\tau^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 64\tilde{C}^{2}((C_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{2}}+C_{\text{eff}}^{\frac{1}{2}}))^{2}\tau^{2}n^{-\frac{s}{2d+s.}}.

Moreover, by employing the regularization parameter in (
3.10
),
we get that

24\vartheta_{0,\lambda}\leq 24\bar{C}_{f_{0}}\ln(\lambda^{-1})^{-\frac{s}{2}}=24\bar{C}_{f_{0}}\ln\left(\exp\left(n^{\frac{2}{2d+s}}\right)\right)^{-\frac{s}{2}}=24\bar{C}_{f_{0}}n^{-\frac{s}{2d+s}}.

Adding these rates in (
4.6
) yields the result.
∎

We now summarize the auxiliary results above and provide the final
convergence rate.

Theorem 4.5

(
L^{2}
-rate)
.

Let
f_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathcal{X})

,
where
\mathcal{X}=[0,1]^{d}
and
s\geq 12d
, and
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}

be sufficiently large. Moreover, assume the distribution of the noise
variables satisfies the condition (
2.6
) with constant

C_{\varepsilon}
and set
\lambda=\lambda(n)=\exp\left(-n^{\frac{2}{2d+s}}\right)
.
Then, there is a constant
C_{L^{2}}
, independent of
\tau
and

n
, such that

\left\|\text{$\hat{f}_{\lambda}-f_{0}$}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{L^{2}}(1+\tau^{2})n^{-\frac{s}{2d+s}}

with probability at least
1-8\exp(-\tau)
.

Proof.

Combining the statements of Lemma
4.2
and Lemma
4.4

provides the assertion for every
\omega\in A\cap B_{1}\cap B_{2}\cap C
.
Each of these events has probability at least
1-2\exp(-\tau)
. Thus,
it follows that

P(A\cap B_{1}\cap B_{2}\cap C)\leq 1-P(A^{C})-P(B_{1}^{C})-P(B_{2}^{C})-P(C^{C})\leq 1-8\exp(-\tau),

which provides the assertion.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

To the best of our knowledge, Theorem
4.5
is the first
result establishing a polynomial rate of convergence for Gaussian
kernel ridge regression with a fixed precision parameter. Nevertheless,
the convergence rate obtained, does not yet match the classical minimax
rate
n^{-\frac{2s}{2s+d}}
(cf.
Tsybakov 2009
;
Caponnetto and De Vito 2006
;
Eberts and Steinwart 2011
).
However, to achieve the minimax rate, one only needs to verify that
the approximation error in (
3.7
) decays as
\ln(\lambda^{-1})^{2s}
.
Specifically, given this faster decay, the same analysis with
\lambda(n)=\exp\left(-n^{\frac{1}{2s+d}}\right)

would yield the minimax convergence rate. Furthermore, the currently
strong regularity requirement, namely,
s\geq 12d
, could be relaxed
as well.

5
Uniform convergence

In this section, we establish the main result of this work: the uniform
convergence of the estimator corresponding to Gaussian kernel ridge
regression. To achieve this, we build on the
L^{2}
-convergence
result from Section
4
and combine it with
the interpolation inequality developed in Section
3.1
.
This connection allows us to translate convergence in the
L^{2}
-norm into uniform convergence, providing guarantees on the estimator’s
behavior across the input space
\mathcal{X}
.

To involve the interpolation results, we first require an auxiliary
bound on the maximum value of the noise variable
\varepsilon
. The
following lemma provides the necessary bound to proceed with the analysis.

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume the distribution
\varepsilon
of the noise
satisfies the condition (
2.6
). Then, for
n\geq n_{0}(\tau)\coloneqq 2C_{\varepsilon}(\tau+\ln(n))
, the event

A_{\varepsilon,n}\coloneqq\left\{\omega\in\Omega\colon\max_{i=1,\dots,n}|\varepsilon_{i}|\leq n\right\}

has probability at least
1-2\exp(-\tau)
.

Proof.

As
\varepsilon
satisfies the noise condition (
2.6
),
we have from Markov’s inequality that

\displaystyle P(\varepsilon>a)

\displaystyle=P(t\varepsilon>ta)

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-at)\E\exp(t\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\exp(-at)\E\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{(t\varepsilon)^{\ell}}{\ell!}

\displaystyle=\exp(-at)\E\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{\ell}\E(\varepsilon^{\ell}|x)}{\ell!}\leq e^{-at}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}t^{\ell}C_{\varepsilon}^{\ell},

where
\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}t^{\ell}C_{\varepsilon}^{\ell}<\infty

for every
t\in(0,C_{\varepsilon}^{-1})
. Choosing
t=(C_{\varepsilon}2)^{-1}

it follows that
\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}t^{\ell}C_{\varepsilon}^{\ell}=2

and therefore
P(\varepsilon>a)\leq 2e^{-ca}
with
c\coloneqq(C_{\varepsilon}2)^{-1}
.
Hence, we have that

P\left(\max_{i=1,\dots,n}|\varepsilon_{i}|\leq n\right)\geq 1-\sum_{i=1}^{n}P\left(|\varepsilon_{i}|>n\right)=1-2ne^{-cn}\geq 1-2e^{-\tau},

whenever
n\geq c^{-1}\tau+\ln(n)c^{-1}
. This concludes the proof.
∎

In what follows, we analyze the norm of interest
\left\|\hat{f}_{\lambda}-f_{0}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}
.
To this end, we make use of the triangle inequality

\left\|\hat{f}_{\lambda}-f_{0}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}\leq\left\|\hat{f}_{\lambda}-f_{\lambda}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}+\left\|f_{\lambda}-f_{0}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})},

(5.1)

which decomposes the error into two separate parts. The first term
on the right-hand side of (
5.1
) is random, as it
depends on the data set
(x_{1},y_{1}),\dots,(x_{n},y_{n})
, whereas
the second term is deterministic, depending solely on
f_{0}
and
the regularization parameter
\lambda
. Moreover, handling these
terms requires different techniques, which is why we analyze them
separately. The first term is the subject of the following lemma.

Lemma 5.2

(Interpolation RKHS residual)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be sufficiently large
(cf. Lemma
5.1
). Then, for every
\omega\in A_{\omega,n}

and
\lambda(n)=\exp\left(-n^{\frac{2}{2d+s}}\right)
it holds

\left\|\hat{f}_{\lambda}-f_{\lambda}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}\leq A_{\infty}n^{\frac{6d}{2d+s}}\left\|\hat{f}_{\lambda}-f_{\lambda}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\frac{2}{n^{\frac{2}{2d+s}}}}.

Remark 5.3
.

Note that we do not explicitly evaluate the
L^{2}
-norm at this
stage, although we could, given the explicit choice of the regularization
parameter. This evaluation is deferred to Theorem
5.5
,
where we establish the final convergence rate.

Proof.

We prove the assertion by employing the interpolation inequality (
3.14
).
To this end, we derive a bound on
\left\|\hat{f}_{\lambda}-f_{\lambda}\right\|_{k}

first.

The functions
f_{\lambda}
and
\hat{f}_{\lambda}
are derived
from the identities

\displaystyle f_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\argmin_{f\in\mathcal{H}_{k}}\lambda\left\|f\right\|_{k}^{2}+\left\|f-f_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\argmin_{f\in\mathcal{H}_{k}}\lambda\left\|f\right\|_{k}^{2}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(f(x_{i})-y_{i})^{2},

and thus

\left\|f_{\lambda}\right\|_{k}^{2}\leq\lambda^{-1}\left\|f_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\left\|\hat{f}_{\lambda}\right\|_{k}^{2}\leq\lambda^{-1}\left(\left\|f_{0}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}+\max_{i}\varepsilon_{i}^{2}\right),

by evaluating both objectives for
f=0
. For
\omega\in A_{\varepsilon,n}
,
we have
\max_{i}\varepsilon_{i}^{2}\leq n^{2}
and therefore

\left\|\hat{f}_{\lambda}-f_{\lambda}\right\|_{k}\leq\left\|\hat{f}_{\lambda}\right\|_{k}+\left\|f_{\lambda}\right\|_{k}\leq\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\left\|f_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left(\left\|f_{0}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}+n^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right),

by using the subadditivity of the norm. Employing this bound in the
interpolation inequality (
3.14
), it follows that

\displaystyle\left\|\hat{f}_{\lambda}-f_{\lambda}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\infty,k}\left\|f\right\|_{k}^{\frac{3}{\alpha}}(\alpha+1)^{3d+\frac{3d}{2\alpha}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\frac{3}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\infty,k}\lambda^{-\frac{3}{2\alpha}}\left(\left\|f_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left(\left\|f_{0}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}+n^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{\frac{3}{\alpha}}(\alpha+1)^{3d+\frac{3d}{2\alpha}}\left\|\hat{f}_{\lambda}-f_{\lambda}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\frac{3}{\alpha}}

for arbitrary
\alpha>3
. Choosing the exponent
\alpha(\lambda)=\frac{-3\ln(\lambda)}{2}

with
\lambda(n)=\exp\left(-n^{\frac{2}{2d+s}}\right)
, we get for
the first term that

\displaystyle C_{\infty,k}\lambda^{-\frac{3}{2\alpha}}\left(\left\|f_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left(\left\|f_{0}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}+n^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{\frac{3}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=C_{\infty,k}\lambda^{\frac{1}{\ln(\lambda)}}\left(\left\|f_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left(\left\|f_{0}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}+n^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{\frac{3}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=C_{\infty,k}e\left(\left\|f_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|f_{0}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}+n\right)^{-\frac{2}{\ln(\lambda)}}

\displaystyle=C_{\infty,k}e\left(\left\|f_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|f_{0}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}+n\right)^{\frac{2}{n^{\frac{2}{2d+s}}}},

which converges towards
1
for
n\to\infty
and is thus uniformly
bounded in
n
. Similarly, the factor
(\alpha(\lambda)+1)^{\frac{3d}{2\alpha(\lambda)}}

is uniformly bounded as well. Hence, there is a constant
\tilde{B}{}_{\infty}

bounding all of these terms and it therefore follows that

\displaystyle\left\|\hat{f}_{\lambda}-f_{\lambda}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{B}{}_{\infty}(\alpha(\lambda)+1)^{3d}\left\|\hat{f}_{\lambda}-f_{\lambda}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\frac{2}{n^{\frac{2}{2d+s}}}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{B}{}_{\infty}\left(\frac{3}{2}n^{\frac{2}{2d+s}}+1\right)^{3d}\left\|\hat{f}_{\lambda}-f_{\lambda}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\frac{2}{n^{\frac{2}{2d+s}}}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{B}{}_{\infty}\left(\frac{5}{2}\right)^{3d}n^{\frac{6d}{2d+s}}\left\|\hat{f}_{\lambda}-f_{\lambda}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\frac{2}{n^{\frac{2}{2d+s}}}}.

Substituting
B_{\infty}\coloneqq\tilde{B}{}_{\infty}\left(\frac{5}{2}\right)^{3d}

provides the assertion.
∎

To bound the second term in (
3.12
), it is necessary
to invoke (
3.13
), as the target function
f_{0}

is generally not contained in
\mathcal{H}_{k}
. This is possible,
as the interpolation inequality (
3.13
) depends only
on the decay rate of the basis coefficients, which can be quantified
using Proposition
3.11
. The following lemma combines
these ideas and provides a bound for the second term in (
5.1
).

Lemma 5.4

(Interpolation Approximation residual)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=[0,1]^{d}
and
f_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathcal{X})

with
s>14d
. Furthermore, let
\delta>0
such that
\tilde{\alpha}(\delta)\coloneqq\frac{s}{4d}-\frac{1}{2}-\delta>3
.
Then, there exists a constant
C(\delta,f_{0})
, such that

\left\|f_{\lambda}-f_{0}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}\leq C(\delta,f_{0})\left\|f_{\lambda}-f_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{s_{\delta}}

with

s_{\delta}\coloneqq\frac{s-14d-4d\delta}{s-2d-4d\delta}

holds true.

Proof.

Recall from Proposition
3.11
, that the basis coefficients

f_{0}
decay at least as
|c_{\ell}|\leq c_{f_{0}}\ell^{-\frac{s}{4d}}
.
Hence, as
\frac{s}{4d}>\frac{7}{2}
, there exists an
\delta>0

such that
\tilde{\alpha}\coloneqq\frac{s}{4d}-\frac{1}{2}-\delta>3

as well as

\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{c_{\ell}^{2}}{\ell^{-2\tilde{\alpha}}}\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\ell^{-(1+2\delta)}<\infty.

Furthermore, note that the residual has the representation
f_{0}-f_{\lambda}=\lambda\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{c_{\ell}}{\mu_{\ell}+\lambda}\varphi_{\ell}

and thus the basis coefficients
\frac{\lambda}{\mu_{\ell}+\lambda}c_{\ell}

for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. Employing the interpolation inequality (
3.13
), we therefore get that

\displaystyle\left\|f_{\lambda}-f_{0}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\infty,2}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\lambda}{\mu_{\ell}+\lambda}\right)^{2}\frac{c_{\ell}^{2}}{\ell^{-2\tilde{\alpha}}}\right)^{\frac{3}{\tilde{\alpha}}}\left\|f_{\lambda}-f_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\frac{3}{\tilde{\alpha}}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\infty,2}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{c_{\ell}^{2}}{\ell^{-2\tilde{\alpha}}}\right)^{\frac{3}{\tilde{\alpha}}}\left\|f_{\lambda}-f_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\frac{3}{\tilde{\alpha}}}

\displaystyle=C(\delta,f_{0})\left\|f_{\lambda}-f_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\frac{3}{\tilde{\alpha}}}

with
C(\delta,f_{0})\coloneqq C_{\infty,2}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{c_{\ell}^{2}}{\ell^{-2\tilde{\alpha}}}\right)^{\frac{3}{\tilde{\alpha}}}
.
The corresponding power for the
L^{2}
-norm is

1-\frac{3}{\tilde{\alpha}}=\frac{\tilde{\alpha}-3}{\tilde{\alpha}}=\frac{\frac{s}{4d}-\frac{1}{2}-\delta-3}{\frac{s}{4d}-\frac{1}{2}-\delta}=\frac{s-14d-4d\delta}{s-2d-4d\delta},

which provides the result.
∎

The following theorem summarizes the two rates of the two lemmata
above and provides the final rate for the uniform convergence of the
estimator.

Theorem 5.5

(Convergence rate in
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}
)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=[0,1]^{d}
and
f_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathcal{X})

with
s>14d
. Furthermore, let
\delta>0
such that
\tilde{\alpha}\coloneqq\frac{s}{4d}-\frac{1}{2}-\delta>3

and
s_{\delta}=\frac{s-14d-4d\delta}{s-2d-4d\delta}
. Then, there
exists a constant
A_{\infty}(\delta)
(independent of
n
and
\tau
),
such that

\left\|\hat{f}_{\lambda}-f_{0}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}\leq A_{\infty}(\delta)(1+\tau^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}n^{\frac{s}{2(2d+s)}\cdot s_{\delta}}

(5.2)

holds with probability at least
1-10e^{-\tau}
.

Proof.

We prove the assertion in two steps. First we employ the result of
Lemma
5.2
and determine the corresponding convergence
rate in terms of the sample size
n
. Subsequently, we show that
this rate is always faster than the rate from Lemma
5.4
,
which coincides with (
5.2
). This then provides the
assertion.

For
\lambda(n)=\exp\left(-n^{\frac{2}{2d+s}}\right)
, we get from
Lemma
5.2
that

\left\|\hat{f}_{\lambda}-f_{\lambda}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\tau^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}n^{-\frac{s}{2(2d+s)}}

and consequently

B_{\infty}n^{\frac{6d}{2d+s}}\left\|\hat{f}_{\lambda}-f_{\lambda}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\frac{2}{n^{\frac{2}{2d+s}}}}\leq B_{\infty}C_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\tau^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}n^{-\frac{s-12d}{2d+s}}\cdot\left(n^{\frac{s-12d}{2d+s}}\right){}^{\frac{2}{n^{\frac{2}{2d+s}}}}.

by Lemma
5.2
. Furthermore, if
n\geq\left(\frac{s-12d}{2d}\right)^{\frac{2d+s}{2}}

(i.e.
\frac{2}{n^{\frac{2}{2d+s}}}\cdot\frac{s-12d}{2d+s}<\frac{2d}{2d+s}
),
it holds for the latter term that

\left(n^{\frac{s-12d}{2d+s}}\right){}^{\frac{2}{n^{\frac{2}{2d+s}}}}=n^{\frac{s-12d}{2d+s}\cdot\frac{2}{n^{\frac{2}{2d+s}}}}<n^{\frac{2d}{2d+s}}

and therefore

\displaystyle\left\|\hat{f}_{\lambda}-f_{\lambda}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}

\displaystyle\leq B_{\infty}C_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\tau^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}n^{-\frac{s-12d}{2d+s}}\cdot\left(n^{\frac{s-12d}{2d+s}}\right){}^{\frac{2}{n^{\frac{2}{2d+s}}}}

(5.3)

\displaystyle\leq B_{\infty}C_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\tau^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}n^{-\frac{s-14d}{2d+s}}

(5.4)

\displaystyle\leq B_{\infty}C_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\tau^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}n^{-s_{\delta}}.

(5.5)

This rate is faster than the rate in (
5.2
) and it is
therefore sufficient two show the slower rate for the second term.
From Lemma
5.4
we have that

\displaystyle\left\|f_{\lambda}-f_{0}\right\|_{C(\mathcal{X})}

\displaystyle\leq C(\delta,f_{0})\left\|f_{\lambda}-f_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{s_{\delta}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\delta,f_{0})\left(C_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\tau^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}n^{-\frac{s}{2(2d+s)}}\right)^{s_{\delta}}

\displaystyle=C(\delta,f_{0})C_{L^{2}}^{\frac{s_{\delta}}{2}}(1+\tau^{2})^{\frac{s_{\delta}}{2}}n^{-\frac{s}{2(2d+s)}\cdot s_{\delta}},

which combined with (
5.5
),
provides the rate in (
5.2
).
The bound holds for each
\omega\in A\cap B_{1}\cap B_{2}\cap C\cap A_{\varepsilon,n}
, where the intersection of the events has probability at least
1-10\exp(-\tau)
(cf. the proof of Theorem
4.5
). This finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

As most of the previous results, the final rate for the uniform convergence
could be enhanced by improving the approximation result in Theorem
3.6
.
This would lead to a faster
L^{2}
-rate as well as larger power

s_{\delta}
.

6
Summary and future research

This paper verifies uniform convergence of Gaussian kernel ridge regression
on the unit cube, marking a significant advancement in understanding
its theoretical behavior. Furthermore, it establishes
L^{2}
-convergence
with a fixed bandwidth parameter, demonstrating robust consistency
without the need to adjust the bandwidth. Importantly, these results
build on an exponentially decaying regularization sequence, which
has not been considered in the literature thus far.

The work also opens several promising directions for future research.
While the current results establish bounds on the maximal marginal
degrees of freedom within the unit cube, a natural extension would
be to generalize these bounds to more complex or irregular domains.
Another important direction concerns the approximation of Sobolev
functions using the Gaussian kernel. Although the current bounds offer
meaningful guarantees, they may not yet be sharp. In particular, improving
the decay exponent in the approximation rate would strengthen all results derived in this work and enhance the overall efficiency
of Gaussian kernel-based methods in function approximation.

Appendix A
Auxiliary Results

This section presents two auxiliary results that are essential for
the convergence analysis discussed above. These results address to
the eigenvalues of the Gaussian kernel and the growth of the effective
dimension as the regularization parameter decreases. We establish
the bound on the eigenvalues first.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=[0,1]^{d}
. The eigenvalues
of the Gaussian kernel are bounded from above by

\mu_{\ell}\leq A_{\mu}e^{-c_{\mu}\ln(\ell)\ell^{\frac{1}{d}}}

(A.1)

with
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, there exists a constant
B_{\mu}

such that

\mu_{\ell}\leq B_{\mu}e^{-\ell^{\frac{1}{d}}}

(A.2)

holds for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

We proof the assertion by employing the characterization

\mu_{\ell}=\min\left\{\left\|L_{k}-T\right\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\colon\text{rank}(T)\leq\ell-1\right\}

(A.3)

of the
\ell
th eigenvalue of
L_{k}
(
Steinwart 2017
). To this end, we
develop an explicit approximation of the integral operator
L_{k}

first. Let
X\coloneqq\left\{\frac{1}{n},\frac{2}{n}\dots,\frac{n-1}{n},1\right\}
,

\mathbb{X}\coloneqq\prod_{i=1}^{d}X
and consider the corresponding
interpolation operator

S_{\mathbb{X}}\colon\mathcal{H}_{k}\to\mathcal{H}_{k}\qquad\text{with}\qquad(S_{\mathbb{X}}f)(y)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\alpha_{x}k(y,x),

where the weights
(\alpha_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{X}}
are chosen such
that
(S_{\mathbb{X}}f)(z)=f(z)
for all
z\in\mathbb{X}
. This
operator is well defined, provided that all points in
\mathbb{X}

are distinct, as the corresponding kernel matrix is positive definite.
Setting
T_{n^{d}}\coloneqq(S_{\mathbb{X}}\circ L_{k})
, it follows
from
Wendland 2004
that

\left\|L_{k}f-(S_{\mathbb{X}}\circ L_{k})f\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\left\|L_{k}f\right\|_{k}\exp\Big(\frac{c}{h_{\mathbb{X},\mathcal{X}}}\ln(h_{\mathbb{X},\mathcal{X}})\Big)\leq k(x,x)\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}\exp\Big(\frac{c}{h_{\mathbb{X},\mathcal{X}}}\ln(h_{\mathbb{X},\mathcal{X}})\Big),

where
h_{\mathbb{X},\mathcal{X}}=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\min_{x_{i}\in\mathbb{X}}\left\|x-x_{i}\right\|_{2}

is the filling distance. It is evident that
h_{\mathbb{X},\mathcal{X}}\leq\frac{d}{n}

and we therefore get for
n\geq d^{2}
that

\displaystyle\left\|L_{k}f-(S_{\mathbb{X}}\circ L_{k})f\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=k(x,x)\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}\exp\Big(\frac{c}{h_{\mathbb{X},\mathcal{X}}}\ln(h_{\mathbb{X},\mathcal{X}})\Big)

\displaystyle\leq k(x,x)\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{c}{d}\cdot n(\ln(n)-\ln(d))\Big)

\displaystyle\leq k(x,x)\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{c}{d}\cdot n(\ln(n)-\frac{1}{2}\ln(n))\Big)

\displaystyle\leq k(x,x)\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}\exp\Big(-c_{d}\cdot n\ln(n)\Big),

where
c_{d}\coloneqq\frac{c}{2d}
. The rank of the operator is equal
to the number of points in
\mathbb{X}
and thus
\text{rank}(S_{\mathbb{X}}\circ L_{k})=n^{d}
.
Hence, it follows by (
A.3
) that

\mu_{n^{d}+1}\leq k(x,x)\exp\Big(-c_{d}\cdot n\ln(n)\Big).

Let now
n\geq d^{2}
and consider some integer
\ell\in[n^{d}+1,(n+1)^{d}]
.
Then, we have that

\displaystyle\mu_{\ell}

\displaystyle\leq k(x,x)\exp\Big(-c_{d}\cdot(n-1)\ln(n-1)\Big)

\displaystyle=k(x,x)\exp\Big(-c_{d}\cdot\frac{n-1}{n+1}(n+1)\big(\ln(n+1)-\ln\big(\frac{n+1}{n-1}\big)\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=k(x,x)\exp\big(-c_{d}\cdot\frac{n-1}{n+1}(n+1)\ln(n+1)\Big)\cdot\exp\Big(c_{d}\cdot\frac{n-1}{n+1}(n+1)\ln(\frac{n+1}{n-1})\Big)

\displaystyle\leq k(x,x)\exp\Big(-\frac{c_{d}}{2}\ell^{\frac{1}{d}}\ln(\ell^{\frac{1}{d}})\Big)\cdot\exp\Big(c_{d}\cdot\frac{n-1}{n+1}(n+1)\ln(\frac{n+1}{n-1})\Big),

as
\ell\leq(n+1)^{d}
. Now assume that
n\geq\left(\frac{-\exp(\frac{1}{4})-1}{1-\exp(\frac{1}{4})}\right)

such that
\frac{n+1}{n-1}\leq\exp(\frac{1}{4})
and observe that

\displaystyle k(x,x)\exp\Big(-\frac{c_{d}}{2}\cdot\ell^{\frac{1}{d}}\ln(\ell^{\frac{1}{d}})\Big)\cdot\exp\Big(c_{d}\cdot\frac{n-1}{n+1}(n+1)\ln(\frac{n+1}{n-1})\Big)

\displaystyle\leq k(x,x)\exp\Big(-\frac{c_{d}}{2}\cdot\ell^{\frac{1}{d}}\ln(\ell^{\frac{1}{d}})\Big)\cdot\exp\Big(c_{d}\cdot(n+1)\frac{1}{4}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq k(x,x)\exp\Big(-\frac{c_{d}}{2}\cdot\ell^{\frac{1}{d}}\ln(\ell^{\frac{1}{d}})\Big)\cdot\exp\Big(c_{d}\cdot(\ell^{\frac{1}{d}}+1)\frac{1}{4}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq k(x,x)e^{\frac{c_{d}}{4}}\exp\Big(-\frac{c_{d}}{4}\cdot\ell^{\frac{1}{d}}\ln(\ell^{\frac{1}{d}})\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{A}_{\mu}\exp\Big(-c_{\mu}\ell^{\frac{1}{d}}\ln(k)\Big),

with
c_{\mu}=\frac{c_{d}}{4}
and
\tilde{A}_{\mu}\coloneqq k(x,x)e^{\frac{c_{d}}{4}}
.
Hence, for each
\ell\geq n^{d}+1
with
n\geq n_{0}\coloneqq\max\left\{d^{2},\frac{-\exp(\frac{1}{4})-1}{1-\exp(\frac{1}{4})}\right\}

it holds that

\mu_{\ell}\leq\tilde{A}_{\mu}\exp\Big(-c_{\mu}\ell^{\frac{1}{d}}\ln(k)\Big).

Setting
A_{\mu}=\max\left\{\max_{\ell<n_{0}^{d}}\mu_{\ell}\exp\Big(c_{\mu}\ell^{\frac{1}{d}}\ln(\ell)\Big),\tilde{A}_{\mu}\right\}

provides the first assertion. The second is evident, as
\exp\Big(-c_{\mu}\ell^{\frac{1}{d}}\ln(k)\Big)

decays faster than
\exp\Big(-\ell^{\frac{1}{d}}\Big)
for every constant
c_{\mu}

as
\ell\to\infty
. This is the assertion.
∎

Remark A.2
.

The second bound (
A.2
) is generally weaker than (
A.1
).
However, in many situations, it is more convenient to work with this
bound. The difference is typically minor, involving only an additional
double-logarithmic factor.

The second statement we address in this section is a bound on the
effective dimension
\mathcal{N}(\lambda)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{\ell}}{\lambda+\mu_{\ell}}
.
Based on the upper bound on the eigenvalues above, we may assess this
quantity directly. This yields the following bound.

Lemma A.3

(Bound on
\mathcal{N}_{\text{eff}}(\lambda)
)
.

For
\mathcal{X}=[0,1]^{d}
and the Gaussian
kernel, it holds that

\mathcal{N}_{\text{eff}}(\lambda)\leq C_{\text{eff}}\ln(\lambda^{-1})^{d},

(A.4)

provided that
\lambda<e^{-1}
.

Proof.

We prove the assertion in two steps. First, we assess the decay behavior
of the truncated series

\sum_{k=n^{d}}^{\infty}\exp(-k^{\frac{1}{d}}),

based on which we then derive the bound on the effective dimension.

For any natural number
n\in\mathbb{N}
, it holds that

\sum_{k=n^{d}}^{\infty}\exp(-k^{\frac{1}{d}})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=(n+i-1)^{d}}^{(n+i)^{d}}\exp(-k^{\frac{1}{d}})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=(n+i-1)^{d}}^{(n+i)^{d}}\exp(-(n+i-1)^{d})^{\frac{1}{d}})

as
\exp(-(n+i-1)^{d})^{\frac{1}{d}})
is the largest term in each
of the partial sums. Moreover, each of the partial sums includes at
most
(n+i)^{d}
terms and thus

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=(n+i-1)^{d}}^{(n+i)^{d}}\exp(-(n+i-1)^{d})^{\frac{1}{d}})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(n+i)^{d}\exp(-(n+i-1))

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-(n-1))\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(n+i)^{d}\exp(-i)

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-(n-1))\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(2ni)^{d}\exp(-i)

\displaystyle=2^{d}n^{d}\exp(-(n-1))\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}i^{d}\exp(-i).

For the closing term, it holds that

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}i^{d}\exp(-i)\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{d}\exp(-(x-1))dx=e\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{d}\exp(-x)dx=e\Gamma(d+1)=e\cdot d!.

and thus

\sum_{k=n^{d}}^{\infty}\exp(-k^{\frac{1}{d}})\leq e^{2}d!2^{d}n^{d}\exp(-n),

(A.5)

which concludes the first step.

For the second let
\lambda<e^{-1}
, such that
\left\lceil\ln(\lambda^{-1})^{d}\right\rceil-1\geq 1
.
Then, it holds for
\mathcal{N}_{\text{eff}}(\lambda)
that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\text{eff}}(\lambda)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{\ell}}{\mu_{\ell}+\lambda}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\left\lceil\ln(\lambda^{-1})^{d}\right\rceil-1}\frac{\mu_{\ell}}{\mu_{\ell}+\lambda}+\sum_{n=\left\lceil\ln(\lambda^{-1})^{d}\right\rceil}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{\ell}}{\mu_{\ell}+\lambda}

\displaystyle\leq\ln(\lambda^{-1})^{d}+\lambda^{-1}\sum_{n=\left\lceil\ln(\lambda^{-1})-1\right\rceil^{d}}^{\infty}\mu_{\ell}

as each term in the first sum is bounded by
1
. Employing the bound
(
A.5
) for the rear sum as well as (
A.2
),
it follows that

\displaystyle\ln(\lambda^{-1})^{d}+\lambda^{-1}\sum_{n=\left\lceil\ln(\lambda)-1\right\rceil^{d}}^{\infty}\mu_{\ell}

\displaystyle\leq\ln(\lambda^{-1})^{d}+\lambda^{-1}B_{\mu}e^{2}d!2^{d}\left\lceil\ln(\lambda^{-1})-1\right\rceil^{d}\exp(-\left\lceil\ln(\lambda^{-1})-1\right\rceil)

\displaystyle\leq\ln(\lambda^{-1})^{d}+\lambda^{-1}B_{\mu}e^{3}d!2^{d}\ln(\lambda^{-1})^{d}\lambda

\displaystyle\leq(1+B_{\mu}e^{3}d!2^{d})\ln(\lambda^{-1})^{d}.

Setting
C_{\text{eff}}\coloneqq(1+B_{\mu}e^{3}d!2^{d})
provides
the assertion.
∎

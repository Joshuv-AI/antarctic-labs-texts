Phase transitions with bounded index: Parallels to De Giorgi’s conjecture
Enric Florit-Simon

Abstract
A well-known conjecture of De Giorgi—motivated by analogy with the Bernstein problem for minimal surfaces—asserts the rigidity of monotone solutions to the Allen–Cahn equation in ℝ d + 1 \mathbb{R}^{d+1} , with d ≤ 7 d\leq 7 . We establish close parallels to De Giorgi’s conjecture for general solutions of bounded Morse index, far stronger than the minimal surface analogy would suggest: Namely, any finite index solution to the Allen–Cahn equation with bounded energy density in ℝ 4 \mathbb{R}^{4} is one-dimensional, and—conditionally on the classification of stable solutions—the same holds for all 4 ≤ n ≤ 7 4\leq n\leq 7 . As a geometric application, phase transitions with bounded energy and index in closed four-manifolds have smooth transition layers which behave like minimal hypersurfaces. Consequently, phase transitions exhibit a remarkably rigid behaviour in higher dimensions. This is in stark contrast with the 3D case, in which a wealth of nontrivial entire solutions with finite index (and energy density) is conversely known to exist, by work pioneered by Del Pino–Kowalczyk–Wei. The authors conjectured that any such solution must have parallel ends which are either planar or catenoidal, suggesting it as a parallel to De Giorgi’s conjecture in this framework. We confirm this picture under the bounded energy density assumption.

pure Allen–Cahn theory
:

•

Revisiting carefully the gluing arguments
5
5

5

Fortunately for us, a fair number of the modifications required in
[

]
have already been outlined—in the axially symmetric case—in
[

.
in

]
, the improved curvature estimates on

show that

can be better approximated by an “Ansatz solution”—built as a superposition of 1D Allen–Cahn solutions—than what is optimal in a general, “scaling-invariant” situation.

•

The full power of the argumentation in
[

]
leads then in this setting to

\Delta f_{i}\simeq H_{i}=\kappa_{0}\left(e^{-\sqrt{2}(f_{i}-f_{i-1})}-e^{-\sqrt{2}(f_{i+1}-f_{i})}\right)+Err^{\prime}\,,

where

is now genuinely of lower order—and not only that, but we obtain moreover an associated
stability condition
, also up to a lower order error, for this PDE system. In other words, the
interaction
between layers becomes leading again. This is the main result in
Section

.

After reaching this point, our goal is now to beat the natural scaling
\Delta f_{i}\simeq\frac{1}{|x^{\prime}|^{2}}
for solutions of the system above. We argue differently depending on the value of
n
.

•

In the case
4\leq n\leq 7
, we argue as follows:

–

It is known that the Liouville equation
\Delta v=e^{-v}

does not admit solutions in dimensions
3
to
9
which are stable outside of a ball
[
22
]
. Thanks to the graphicality and the improved errors (which are “lower order”), letting
v=f_{i+1}-f_{i}
instead the same proof of Dancer–Farina can be replicated here, giving a contradiction in case there were more than one end (i.e. if
N>1
).

–

Therefore, necessarily
N=1
. On the other hand, this easily implies then that
u
has energy density
1
at infinity. We conclude that
u
must be 1D by Wang’s Allard-type theorem (see
[
77
]
or
Theorem

2.5
).

•

In the case
n=3

–

Testing stability with a linear cutoff supported in a large annulus
B_{R}^{\prime}\setminus\overline{B_{R_{0}}^{\prime}}
and letting
R\to\infty
gives that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{B_{2R_{0}}^{\prime}}}e^{-\sqrt{2}(f_{i+1}-f_{i})}\leq C\,.

(10)

–

Fix
i\in\{1,...,N\}
; by the Toda system we find then that
\Delta f_{i}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{B_{2R_{0}}^{\prime}})
. This may seem like a really minor improvement from
|\Delta f_{i}|\leq\frac{C}{|x|^{2}}
; on the other hand, we have already broken the natural scaling of the equation.

–

We can define then the “Ansatz” potential

f_{i}^{a}(y):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\setminus\overline{B_{2R_{0}}^{\prime}}}(\log|y-z|-\log|z|)\,\Delta f_{i}(z)\,dz\,.

This “renormalisation trick” can be found, for instance, in
[
15
]
. Letting
c_{i}^{a}:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\setminus\overline{B_{2R_{0}}^{\prime}}}\Delta f_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
, it follows easily that

f_{i}^{a}=(c_{i}^{a}+o(1))\log|y|\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{as}\hskip 10.00002pt|y|\to\infty\,.

–

Setting
f_{i}^{b}:=f_{i}-f_{i}^{a}
, we see then that
\Delta f_{i}^{b}=0
. A priori,
f_{i}^{b}
could be just any harmonic function; on the other hand, it additionally has
sublinear growth
(since both
f_{i}
and
f_{i}^{a}
do).

–

An enhanced version of the improvement of flatness iteration in annuli
6
6

6

Notice that
\Delta v_{i}^{b}
is not limited by a fixed rate (unlike e.g. (
9
)) anymore.
then shows that actually

f_{i}^{b}=b_{i}^{b}+c_{i}^{b}\log|y|+O(|y|^{-\beta})\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{as}\hskip 10.00002pt|y|\to\infty

for every
\beta\in(0,1)
.

–

Since
f_{i}=f_{i}^{a}+f_{i}^{b}
, this shows the asymptotics claimed in
Theorem

1.4
up to improving the expansion for
f_{i}^{a}
. For this, we need to go back to studying the interactions between different layers.

–

As a starting point, we immediately see that
|c_{i+1}-c_{i}|\geq\sqrt{2}
, just by the integrability of the interactions in (
10
). To conclude, we need to show that the inequality is strict: then, the integrability of the interactions improves by an algebraic rate, which easily yields the precise expansion for the
f_{i}^{a}
.

–

Finally, the fact that
|c_{i+1}-c_{i}|=\sqrt{2}
can indeed never occur is obtained by a careful inspection of the structure of the Toda system and of the integral defining

in this setting. A key idea is that the uppermost and lowermost layers are “special”: they are respectively sub- and superharmonic, since they satisfy (by (
8

To simplify the notation, the general framework we assume in the whole of the present section is the following:

3.2
Reduction to an iterative result

We start with a simple lemma that says that having controlled curvatures and small height in a larger annulus implies graphicality over a smaller annulus. We will use this repeatedly.

Lemma 3.3

(Graphicality lemma)
.

Let
1<R_{1}<R_{2}<R_{3}
and
C_{0}
. Then, there is
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(R_{1},R_{2},R_{3},C_{0},n)>0

Assume that
\widetilde{\Sigma}
is an embedded hypersurface with
(\overline{\widetilde{\Sigma}}\setminus\widetilde{\Sigma})\cap(B_{R_{3}}\setminus\overline{B_{R_{3}^{-1}}})=\emptyset

and

\widetilde{\Sigma}\cap(B_{R_{3}}\setminus\overline{B_{R_{3}^{-1}}})\subset\{|x_{n}|\leq\varepsilon_{0}\}\,.

Then

\widetilde{\Sigma}\cap(B_{R_{1}}\setminus\overline{B_{R_{1}^{-1}}})\subset(B_{R_{2}}^{\prime}\setminus\overline{B_{R_{2}^{-1}}^{\prime}})\times[-\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{0}]\,,

N\in\mathbb{N}
and some smooth
f_{i}:B_{R_{2}}^{\prime}\setminus\overline{B_{R_{2}^{-1}}^{\prime}}\to[-\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{0}]
such that

\widetilde{\Sigma}\cap\left((B_{R_{2}}^{\prime}\overline{\setminus B_{R_{2}^{-1}}^{\prime}})\times[-\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{0}]\right)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}{\rm graph}\,f_{i}\,.

Proof.

Step 1.
Inclusion.

We can directly see that
\widetilde{\Sigma}\cap(B_{R_{1}}\setminus\overline{B_{R_{1}^{-1}}})\subset(B_{R_{2}}^{\prime}\setminus\overline{B_{R_{2}^{-1}}^{\prime}})\times[-\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{0}]
, just by taking

\varepsilon_{0}\leq\min\{\sqrt{R_{1}^{-2}-R_{2}^{-2}},\frac{1}{2}(R_{3}-R_{2})\}\,.

Indeed, if
x\in\widetilde{\Sigma}\cap(B_{R_{1}}\setminus\overline{B_{R_{1}^{-1}}})
then
|x|\geq R_{1}^{-1}
, and moreover
|x_{n}|\leq\varepsilon_{0}\leq\sqrt{R_{1}^{-2}-R_{2}^{-2}}
by assumption; we can then compute

|x^{\prime}|=\sqrt{|x|^{2}-|x_{n}|^{2}}\geq\sqrt{R_{1}^{-2}-(\sqrt{R_{1}^{-2}-R_{2}^{-2}})^{2}}=R_{2}^{-1}\,.

Step 2.
Graphicality.

Exactly as in
Lemma

2.9
, by the second fundamental form bound there is
c_{0}=c_{0}(R_{1},R_{2},R_{3},C_{0},n)>0
, with
R_{2}+\varepsilon_{0}+2c_{0}<R_{3}
and
R_{2}^{-1}-2c_{0}>R_{3}^{-1}
, such that:
Given
x\in\widetilde{\Sigma}\cap(B_{R_{2}}^{\prime}\setminus\overline{B_{R_{2}^{-1}}^{\prime}})\times[-\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{0}]
—so that
B_{2c_{0}}(x)\subset(B_{R_{3}}\setminus\overline{B_{R_{3}^{-1}}})
—we can write all of the components of
\widetilde{\Sigma}
intersecting
B_{c_{0}}(x)
as a finite union of graphs with respect to some direction
e_{x}
. Note that the finiteness just comes from the fact that having infinitely many components locally would contradict embeddedness.

Now, by the
\varepsilon_{0}
-flatness assumption, up to making
\varepsilon_{0}>0
small enough depending on
c_{0}
we immediately see that we can actually take

above. Moreover, just by interpolation, we can make the graphs have slope less than any
\delta>0
arbitrary up to making

smaller.

Take the union over

of these graphs. Considering the maximal domains of definition

In words, every time we have a large block of scales where flatness decays, and assuming moreover that the best flatness then remains for all scales up to infinity, then flatness decays up to an extra scale, and moreover this improved flatness also propagates to all further scales up to infinity. We need the following simple lemma for a function which is almost harmonic on a large annulus and has controlled growth.

Lemma 3.8

(Harmonic approximation)
.

Fix
\lambda,\alpha\in(0,1)
. There exists
\delta_{0}\in(0,\lambda]
, depending on
\lambda,\alpha,n
, such that the following holds. Let

•

, where

\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2\rho}\setminus\overline{B_{\rho}})}\leq\rho^{2-\alpha}
, for

.

•

\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2\rho}\setminus\overline{B_{\rho}})}\leq\rho^{\alpha}
, for

.

Then there exists

Proof.

We argue by contradiction. If no such
\delta_{0}>0
exists, considering the sequence
\delta_{0,i}=\frac{1}{i}\to 0
there need to be associated

as in the hypotheses of the theorem but such that

\mbox{no}\ \ a\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\ \ \mbox{satisfies}\hskip 10.00002pt\|v_{i}-a\cdot x\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2}\setminus\overline{B_{1/2}})}\leq\lambda\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for any}\ \ i\ \ \mbox{in the sequence.}

For

large enough, the

satisfy

[

]
. By Ascoli–Arzelà, a subsequence converges locally to some limit

in

grows less than

By the usual Liouville theorem for harmonic functions in

, which follows for instance by separation of variables, the growth assumptions imply that

for some

cannot be a term in

since it blows up at

But then, the uniform convergence in

, as well as uniformly bounded derivatives of any order. We immediately see then that all the terms in the right hand side of (
56
) can be bounded (in norm) by
O(|B|)=O(\mathcal{A})
, thus (by (
54
)) we have

|\operatorname{div}(|\nabla u|^{2}\nabla B)|\leq C|x|^{-3/2}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{in}\hskip 10.00002pt\{|u|\leq 0.9\}\setminus\overline{B_{R_{0}}}\,.

(57)

Combining (
54
) and (
57
), standard interior gradient estimates—on balls of small but fixed radius—give then that

|\nabla B|\leq C|x|^{-3/2}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{in}\hskip 10.00002pt\{u=0\}\setminus\overline{B_{2R_{0}}}

(58)

as well, up to making
R_{0}
large enough.

Finally, to bound
|\nabla\mathrm{II}|
instead, it suffices to realise that

|\nabla\mathrm{II}|\leq|\nabla B|+|\mathrm{II}||B|\,,

(59)

and then combine this with (
58
) and the bound from Step 1.

To be precise, here
\nabla\mathrm{II}
denotes the intrinsic covariant derivative of the tensor
\mathrm{II}
over a level set, and
\nabla B
denotes the Euclidean differential of

, just as before. One has then—as long as

does not vanish—the formula

which is simple to check from the standard definition of covariant derivative of a tensor, and then (

) follows.

Fermi coordinates.
We introduce Fermi coordinates with respect to
\Gamma_{i}
. It will be convenient to follow the notation in

]

. Given

in a neighborhood of

precisely if:

•

•

\Gamma_{i}

Now, since

N_{i}(y):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla f_{i}(y)|^{2}}}\left(-\nabla f_{i}(y),\,1\right).

Perhaps more concisely, but equivalently, the correspondence between

and
(y,z)
is then just
x=(y,f_{i}(y))+zN_{i}(y)

Now, simply by (

), i.e. the curvature estimates, these coordinates are well defined in the open set

for some

.

For each

,
\Gamma_{i}^{z}
will denote the smooth hypersurface consisting of all points with signed distance to

identical to

. By slight abuse of notation, we denote its second fundamental form by

, and its mean curvature by

. Arguing as in
[

, p. 11-13]
, we have:

•

Mean curvature expansion:

H_{i}(y,z)=H_{i}(y,0)+O\left(|z||\mathrm{II}_{i}|^{2}(y,0)\right)=H_{i}(y,0)+O\left(|z||y|^{-3}\right).

•

Laplacian in Fermi coordinates:
Denoting by
\Delta_{i,z}
the Laplace(-Beltrami) operator over
\Gamma_{i}^{z}
,

\Delta=\Delta_{i,z}-H_{i}(y,z)\partial_{z}+\partial_{zz}.

•

Laplacian over
\Gamma_{i}^{z}
:
Let

be a

|\Delta_{i,z}\varphi(y)-\Delta_{i,0}\varphi(y)|\leq C|z||\mathrm{II}_{i}|\left(|\nabla^{2}\varphi(y)|+|\nabla\varphi(y)|\right)\leq C|y|^{-3/2}|z|\left(|\nabla^{2}\varphi(y)|+|\nabla\varphi(y)|\right).

(60)

•

.

•

We set

Moreover,

•

Denote

.

•

.

By the bounds on

Optimal approximation

This is taken exactly from
[
42
]
.

Recall the definition of
g
in (
14
); we need a truncated version. Fix a cutoff function
\zeta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(-2,2)
, with
\zeta\equiv 1
in
(-1,1)
and
|\zeta^{\prime}|+|\zeta^{\prime\prime}|\leq 16
. For all
|y|
large, let (to ease notation, dependence on
|y|
will be omitted when convenient)

\bar{g}(t)=\zeta(8(\log|y|)t)g(t)+[1-\zeta(8(\log|y|)t)]\operatorname{sgn}(t),\hskip 10.00002ptt\in(-\infty,+\infty).

In particular,
\bar{g}\equiv 1
in
(16\log|y|,+\infty)
and
\bar{g}\equiv-1
in
(-\infty,-16\log|y|)
.

Then,
\bar{g}
is an approximate solution to the one-dimensional Allen–Cahn equation, in the sense that

\bar{g}^{\prime\prime}(t)=W^{\prime}(\bar{g}(t))+\bar{\xi}(t)\,,

where
\operatorname{spt}(\bar{\xi})\subset\{8\log|y|<|t|<16\log|y|\}
, and
|\bar{\xi}|+|\bar{\xi}^{\prime}|+|\bar{\xi}^{\prime\prime}|\leq C|y|^{-4}
.

In the following we assume for convenience that
u
has the same sign as
(-1)^{i}
between
\Gamma_{i}
and
\Gamma_{i+1}
. Exactly as in
[
42
, Lemma 4.3]
, we have:

Lemma 4.8
.

For any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
with
|y|>R_{0}
(perhaps after enlarging
R_{0}
) and
i\in\{1,...,N\}
, there exists a unique
h_{i}(y):\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that in the Fermi coordinates with respect to
\Gamma_{i}
,

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}[u(y,z)-g_{*}(y,z)]\bar{g}^{\prime}(z-h_{i}(y))\,dz=0,

where for each
i
, in
\mathcal{M}_{i}
we define

g_{*}(y,z):=g_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\left[g_{j}-(-1)^{j}\right]+\sum_{j=i+1}^{N}\left[g_{j}+(-1)^{j}j\right],

and in the Fermi coordinates
(y,t)
with respect to
\Gamma_{j}
,

g_{j}(y,z):=\bar{g}((-1)^{j}[z-h_{j}(y)]).

Moreover, for any
i\in\{1,...,N\}
, we have

\lim_{|y|\to+\infty}\left(|h_{i}(y)|+|\nabla h_{i}(y)|+|\nabla^{2}h_{i}(y)|+|\nabla^{3}h_{i}(y)|\right)=0;

likewise, defining the approximation error
\phi:=u-g_{*}
, we have

\lim_{|y|\to+\infty}\sup_{z}\left(|\phi_{i}(y,z)|+|\nabla\phi_{i}(y,z)|+|\nabla^{2}\phi_{i}(y,z)|+|\nabla^{3}\phi_{i}(y,z)|\right)=0;

In Fermi coordinates with respect to
\Gamma_{i}
, the equation for
\phi
can be written as

\displaystyle\Delta_{i,z}\phi-H_{i}(y,z)\partial_{z}\phi+\partial_{zz}\phi

\displaystyle=W^{\prime\prime}(g_{*})\phi+\mathcal{N}(\phi)+\mathcal{I}+(-1)^{i}g_{i}^{\prime}\mathcal{R}_{i,1}-g_{i}^{\prime\prime}\mathcal{R}_{i,2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j\neq i}[(-1)^{j}g_{j}^{\prime}\mathcal{R}_{j,1}-g_{j}^{\prime\prime}\mathcal{R}_{j,2}]-\sum_{j}\xi_{j},

where

\mathcal{N}(\phi)=W^{\prime}(g_{*}+\phi)-W^{\prime}(g_{*})-W^{\prime\prime}(g_{*})\phi=O(\phi^{2})

and

\mathcal{I}=W^{\prime}(g_{*})-\sum_{j}W^{\prime}(g_{j})\,,

while for each
j
, in the Fermi coordinates with respect to
\Gamma_{j}
,

\xi_{j}(y,z)=\bar{\xi}((-1)^{j}(z-h_{j}(y)))\,,

and

\mathcal{R}_{j,1}(y,z):=H_{j}(y,z)+\Delta_{j,z}h_{j}(y)\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\mathcal{R}_{j,2}(y,z):=|\nabla_{j}h_{j}(y)|^{2}\,.

As in
[
76
, Lemma 4.6]
, since
u=0
on
\Gamma_{i}
,

can be controlled by
\phi
in the following way.

Lemma 4.9
.

For each

and

, we have

\|h_{i}\|_{C^{2,1/2}(\{|y|>R\})}\leq C\left(\|\phi\|_{C^{2,1/2}(\{|y|>R\})}+M(R)\right),

as well as the improved tangential derivative bound

\|\nabla_{y}h_{i}\|_{C^{1,1/2}(\{|y|>R\})}\leq C\left(\|\nabla_{y}\phi\|_{C^{1,1/2}(\{|y|>R\})}+R^{-1/6}M(R)\right).

\displaystyle\leq C\Big[|y|^{-3}+|y|^{-\frac{7}{2}}M(|y|-100\log|y|)+M(|y|-100\log|y|)^{\frac{7}{6}}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\max_{\beta}\left(\|H_{\beta}+\Delta_{\beta,0}h_{\beta}\|_{C^{1/2}(\{\widetilde{y}\ :\ |\widetilde{y}|>|y|-100\log|y|\})}^{2}+\|\phi\|_{C^{2,1/2}(\{\widetilde{y}\ :\ |\widetilde{y}|>|y|-100\log|y|\})}^{2}\right)\Big].

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\Big(\max_{i}\left\|H_{i}+\Delta_{i,0}h_{i}\right\|_{C^{1/2}(\{|y|>R-100\log R\})}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\|\phi\|_{C^{2,1/2}(\{|y|>R-100\log R\})}\Big)+CM(R-100\log R)+CR^{-3}.

Step 1.
Lower bound for the gradient .

Assume, for contradiction, that there were a subsequence
\varepsilon_{i}\to 0
and some

(or any sequence of points, for that matter), the metrics of the

Let
\widetilde{u}_{i}

Step 2.
Proof of the bound for

Since we want to prove an asymptotic in

Assume, by contradiction, that there were a subsequence (not relabeled) of solutions for which the asymptotic fails. This means that for some

Step 3.
Proof of the sheet separation.

Given
\Lambda\geq 1
, by the previous steps there is
\varepsilon_{0}>0

5.3
Structure for

— Proof of

We keep once again the setting and notation from Sections

If

Integrability of layer interaction

Let

. We start by testing stability (i.e. (

)) with a standard linear cutoff
\eta_{R}\in C_{c}^{1}(B_{2R}\setminus\overline{B_{R_{0}}})

in

where

. Sending

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{B_{2R_{0}}^{\prime}}}e^{-\sqrt{2}(f_{i}-f_{i-1})}\,dx^{\prime}\leq C\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for every}\hskip 10.00002pti=2,...,N\,.

(69)

Fix
i\in\{1,...,N\}
now. Integrating in (
51
) with this fixed value of
i
, and using (
69
) and the integrability of
|y|^{-2-\frac{1}{8}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we conclude that

\Delta f_{i}=h_{i}\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{for some}\ h_{i}\in(L^{1}\cap L^{\infty})(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\setminus\overline{B_{2R_{0}}^{\prime}})\,.

(70)

It is hard to obtain any more information from stability. Nevertheless, the preceding information about the

will already strongly constrain their structure.

Structure of single layers

The first idea, which is inspired by
[
15
]
, is to split
f_{i}=v_{i}^{a}+v_{i}^{b}
with

v_{i}^{a}(y):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{B_{2R_{0}}^{\prime}}}(\log|y-z|-\log|z|)|h(z)\,dz

and
v_{i}^{b}:=f_{i}-v_{i}^{a}
.

Observe that
\int\log|z|\,h(z)\,dz
is “just a renormalisation constant”. On the other hand, it could very well be infinite—the integral defining
v_{i}^{a}
is only seen to be convergent (using the integrability assumptions for
h
, and the fact that
\left|\log|y-z|-\log|z|\right|\leq C\frac{|y|}{|z|}\leq C
for
|z|\geq 2|y|
) upon considering its integrand as a whole.

It is simple to see that, setting
c_{i}^{a}:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{B_{2R_{0}}^{\prime}}}h\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

v_{i}^{a}(y)=(c_{i}^{a}+o(1))\log|y|\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{as}\hskip 10.00002pt|y|\to\infty\,.

This is still far from the type of asymptotics desired in
Section

5.3
. However, at this point we can obtain more precise asymptotics for
v_{i}^{b}
. Observe that
v_{i}^{b}=f-v_{i}^{a}
satisfies
\Delta v_{i}^{b}=0
, i.e. it is harmonic, thus it is not limited by the Laplacian bound anymore. A priori,
v_{i}^{b}
could be any harmonic function; on the other hand, by the growth bound for
v
and the asymptotics for
v_{i}^{a}
, we additionally know that
|v_{i}^{b}(y)|\leq C|y|^{1/2}
.

Via the improvement of flatness in annuli strategy, we will show:

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
w:\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{B_{2R_{0}}^{\prime}}\to\mathbb{R}
be a harmonic function satisfying
|w(y)|\leq C|y|^{1/2}
.

Then, there are
b,c\in\mathbb{R}
such that
w=b+c\log|y|+O(|y|^{-\alpha})
for every
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

We will prove
Proposition

5.1
at the end of the section. For now, we show how to conclude from this:

First, applying
Proposition

5.1
to
w=v_{i}^{b}
, we deduce the existence of
b_{i}^{b},c_{i}^{b}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

v_{i}^{b}(y)=b_{i}^{b}+c_{i}^{b}\log|y|+O(|y|^{-\alpha})\,.

(71)

It remains to improve the asymptotics for
v_{i}^{a}
. Let
c_{i}:=c_{i}^{a}+c_{i}^{b}
; we want to show that
c_{i+1}-c_{i}>\sqrt{2}
.

Separation bounds

Since
e^{-\sqrt{2}(v_{i+1}-v_{i})}
is integrable, and

e^{-\sqrt{2}(v_{i+1}-v_{i})}=e^{-\sqrt{2}(c_{i+1}-c_{i}+o(1))\log|y|}\,,

we directly see that
c_{i+1}-c_{i}\geq\sqrt{2}
.

Assume for contradiction that the strict bound were false for at least one pair of consecutive layers, and let
i_{1}=\min\{i:c_{i+1}-c_{i}=\sqrt{2}\}
and
i_{2}=\min\{i>i_{1}:c_{i+1}-c_{i}>\sqrt{2}\}
. These layers satisfy then

e^{-(v_{i_{1}}-v_{i_{1}-1})}=O(|y|^{-2-\alpha})\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pte^{-(v_{i_{2}+1}-v_{i_{2}})}=O(|y|^{-2-\alpha})\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for some}\hskip 10.00002pt\alpha>0\,,

(72)

and
c_{i+1}-c_{i}=\sqrt{2}
for every
i\in\{i_{1},...,i_{2}-1\}
.

We claim we have the one-sided bounds

v_{i_{1}}(y)\geq c_{i_{1}}\log|y|-C\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002ptv_{i_{2}}(y)\leq c_{i_{2}}\log|y|+C\,.

Indeed, observe that—by (
51
) and (
72
)—the top layer
f_{i_{2}}
satisfies
\Delta f_{i_{2}}=\kappa_{0}e^{-\sqrt{2}(f_{i_{2}}-f_{i_{2}-1})}+O(|y|^{-2-\alpha})
, which has positive leading term on the right. Write

\displaystyle v_{i_{2}}^{a}(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{B_{2R_{0}}^{\prime}}}(\log|y-z|-\log|z|)\Delta v_{i_{2}}\,dz

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\log|y|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{B_{2R_{0}}^{\prime}}}\Delta v_{i_{2}}\,dz+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{B_{2R_{0}}^{\prime}}}\log\frac{|y-z|}{|y||z|}\ \Delta v_{i_{2}}\,dz

\displaystyle=c_{i_{2}}^{a}\log|y|+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{B_{2R_{0}}^{\prime}}}\log\frac{|y-z|}{|y||z|}\ \left[\kappa_{0}e^{-\sqrt{2}(f_{i_{2}}-f_{i_{2}-1})}+O(|y|^{-2-\alpha})\right]\,dz\,dz

\displaystyle\leq c_{i_{2}}^{a}\log|y|+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{B_{2R_{0}}^{\prime}}}\log\frac{|y-z|}{|y||z|}\ \kappa_{0}e^{-\sqrt{2}(f_{i_{2}}-f_{i_{2}-1})}\,dz+C\,.

We can assume that
R_{0}\geq 2
, so that we only need to consider
|z|,|y|\geq 2
. But then, arguing as in
[
49
, Lemma 2.1]
, we can bound

\frac{|y-z|}{|y||z|}\leq\frac{|y|+|z|}{|y||z|}\leq\frac{|y||z|}{|y||z|}=1\,,

and therefore
\log\frac{|y-z|}{|y||z|}\leq 0
, which shows that

\displaystyle v_{i_{2}}^{a}(y)\leq c_{i_{2}}^{a}\log|y|+C\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{or}\hskip 10.00002ptv_{i_{2}}\leq c_{i_{2}}\log|y|+C

as desired. The lower bound for
v_{i_{1}}
follows similarly.

We proceed with the contradiction argument:

Define
Err_{i}(y)
via
v_{i}(y)=c_{i}\log|y|+Err_{i}(y)
. The one-sided bounds show that

Err_{i_{2}}\leq C\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002ptErr_{i_{1}}\geq-C\,.

(73)

On the other hand, by (
69
) we see that, for any
i\in\{i_{1},...,i_{2}-1\}
,

\displaystyle+\infty>\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{B_{R_{0}}}}e^{-\sqrt{2}(v_{i+1}-v_{i})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{B_{R_{0}}}}e^{-\sqrt{2}\left[(c_{i+1}-c_{i})\log|y|+(Err_{i+1}-Err_{i})\right]}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{B_{R_{0}}}}\frac{1}{|y|^{2}}e^{-\sqrt{2}(Err_{i+1}-Err_{i})}\,.

Define the measure
\mu(A):=\int_{A}\frac{1}{|y|^{2}}
,
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{B_{R_{0}}}
. Very much in particular, the above shows that, for any
M>0
, we have

\displaystyle\mu(\{y:(Err_{i+1}-Err_{i})(y)\leq M\})

\displaystyle=\mu(x:\{e^{-\sqrt{2}(Err_{i+1}-Err_{i})(y)}\geq e^{-\sqrt{2}M}\})

\displaystyle\leq e^{\sqrt{2}M}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{B_{R_{0}}}}\frac{1}{|y|^{2}}e^{-\sqrt{2}(Err_{i+1}-Err_{i})}

\displaystyle<\infty\,.

By a union bound then

\mu\left(\left\{y:(Err_{i+1}-Err_{i})(y)\leq M\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for some}\hskip 10.00002pti\in\{i_{1},...,i_{2}-1\}\right\}\right)<\infty\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{as well}\,;

since
\frac{1}{|y|^{2}}
is not integrable in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{B_{R_{0}}}
, or equivalently
\mu(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{B_{R_{0}}})=\infty
,

\mbox{given}\hskip 10.00002ptM>0\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{there exists}\hskip 10.00002pty\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{B_{R_{0}}}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{with}\hskip 10.00002pt(Err_{i+1}-Err_{i})(y)>M\hskip 10.00002pt\forall i\in\{i_{1},...,i_{2}-1\}\,.

This means, in particular, that for this
y
we have

(Err_{i_{2}}-Err_{i_{1}})(y)=\sum_{i=i_{1}}^{i_{2}-1}(Err_{i+1}-Err_{i})(y)>M\,.

But then, since (by (
73
)) we had the uniform bound
Err_{i_{2}}-Err_{i_{1}}\leq C
, the fact that
M>0
can be taken arbitrarily large yields a contradiction.

Improvement of
v_{i}^{a}

Now that we know that

for every

, by the asymptotics for the

we conclude that

for some

. Then, from (

) and the integral defining

we easily deduce that

which—letting

—together with the claimed asymptotics for the

shows
Theorem

1.4
.

k\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle|v(x^{\prime})-\widetilde{a}_{k}\cdot x^{\prime}-\widetilde{b}_{k}-\widetilde{c}_{k}\log|x^{\prime}||\leq C2^{-\alpha k}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{in}\hskip 10.00002ptB_{2^{k+1}}\setminus\overline{B_{2^{k-1}}}\,.

(75)

Step 1.
Coefficient comparison.

We will show that

|a_{k+1}-a_{k}|\leq C2^{(-\alpha-1)k}\log 2^{k}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt|b_{k+1}-b_{k}|\leq C2^{-\alpha k}\log 2^{k}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt|c_{k+1}-c_{k}|\leq C2^{-\alpha k}\,.

(76)

To see this, consider two consecutive scales in (
75
). The triangle inequality gives then

\displaystyle|(\widetilde{a}_{k+1}-\widetilde{a}_{k})\cdot x^{\prime}+(\widetilde{b}_{k+1}-\widetilde{b}_{k})+(\widetilde{c}_{k+1}-\widetilde{c}_{k})\log|x^{\prime}||\leq C2^{-\alpha k}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{in}\hskip 10.00002ptB_{2^{k}}\setminus\overline{B_{2^{k-1}}}\,.

(77)

In particular, we find that

\displaystyle|(\widetilde{b}_{k+1}-\widetilde{b}_{k})+(\widetilde{c}_{k+1}-\widetilde{c}_{k})\log|x^{\prime}||\leq C2^{-\alpha k}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{in}\hskip 10.00002pt\{(\widetilde{a}_{k+1}-\widetilde{a}_{k})\cdot x^{\prime}=0\}\cap(B_{2^{k}}\setminus\overline{B_{2^{k-1}}})\,,

thus (testing with
x^{\prime}
in the inner and outer boundaries of the domain) we find

\displaystyle|(\widetilde{b}_{k+1}-\widetilde{b}_{k})+(\widetilde{c}_{k+1}-\widetilde{c}_{k})\log 2^{k}|\leq C2^{-\alpha k}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt|(\widetilde{b}_{k+1}-\widetilde{b}_{k})+(\widetilde{c}_{k+1}-\widetilde{c}_{k})\log 2^{k-1}|\leq C2^{-\alpha k}\,.

Combining both conditions we see that

\displaystyle|\widetilde{c}_{k+1}-\widetilde{c}_{k}|\leq C2^{-\alpha k}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt|(\widetilde{b}_{k+1}-\widetilde{b}_{k})|\leq C2^{-\alpha k}\log 2^{k}\,.

(78)

Finally, testing (
77
) with some
x^{\prime}\in B_{2^{k}}\setminus\overline{B_{2^{k-1}}}
satisfying
(\widetilde{a}_{k+1}-\widetilde{a}_{k})\cdot x^{\prime}=|\widetilde{a}_{k+1}-\widetilde{a}_{k}||x^{\prime}|
and using (
78
), we deduce the bound for
|\widetilde{a}_{k+1}-\widetilde{a}_{k}|
as well, concluding the proof of (
76
).

Step 2.
Summing the coefficients.

By (
76
), given
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
,

\sum_{k\geq k_{0}}|\widetilde{c}_{k+1}-\widetilde{c}_{k}|\leq C_{\alpha}2^{-\alpha k_{0}}\,.

Let
\bar{\alpha}=\max\{\alpha/2,1-2(1-\alpha)\}
; the point is simply that
\bar{\alpha}<\alpha
but still
\bar{\alpha}\to 1
as
\alpha\to 1
. We can bound then

|\widetilde{a}_{k+1}-\widetilde{a}_{k}|\leq C_{\alpha}2^{(-\bar{\alpha}-1)k_{0}}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt|b_{k+1}-b_{k}|\leq C2^{-\alpha k}\log 2^{k}\leq C2^{-\bar{\alpha}k}\,,

which gives

\sum_{k\geq k_{0}}|\widetilde{a}_{k+1}-\widetilde{a}_{k}|\leq C_{\alpha}2^{(-\bar{\alpha}-1)k_{0}}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\sum_{k\geq k_{0}}|\widetilde{b}_{k+1}-\widetilde{b}_{k}|\leq C_{\alpha}2^{-\bar{\alpha}k_{0}}\,.

Now, the above show in particular that the sequences
\widetilde{a}_{k},\widetilde{b}_{k},\widetilde{c}_{k}
are Cauchy. This shows the existence of some
\widetilde{a}_{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\widetilde{b}_{\infty},\widetilde{c}_{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}
with

|\widetilde{a}_{\infty}-\widetilde{a}_{k}|\leq C_{\alpha}2^{(-\alpha-1)k}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\,|\widetilde{b}_{\infty}-\widetilde{b}_{k}|\leq C_{\alpha}2^{-\bar{\alpha}k}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt|\widetilde{c}_{\infty}-\widetilde{c}_{k}|\leq C_{\alpha}2^{-\alpha k}\,.

(79)

Substituting into (
75
), we deduce that

|v(x^{\prime})-\widetilde{a}_{\infty}\cdot x^{\prime}-\widetilde{b}_{\infty}-\widetilde{c}_{\infty}\log|x^{\prime}||\leq C2^{-\bar{\alpha}k}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{in}\hskip 10.00002ptB_{2^{k+1}}\setminus\overline{B_{2^{k-1}}}\,.

This means precisely that
v=\widetilde{a}_{\infty}\cdot x^{\prime}+\widetilde{b}_{\infty}+\widetilde{c}_{\infty}\log|x^{\prime}|+O(|x^{\prime}|^{-\bar{\alpha}})
. Since we are assuming that
|v(x^{\prime})|\leq C|x^{\prime}|^{1/2}
, we immediately see that actually
\widetilde{a}_{\infty}\equiv 0
. Finally, since
\bar{\alpha}\to 1
as
\alpha\to 1
, up to changing the value of
\alpha
we conclude the proof.
∎

Proof of
Proposition

5.3
.

We next show
Proposition

5.3
. First, the analog of
Lemma

3.8
is:

Lemma 5.4

(Harmonic approximation)
.

Let
\lambda>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. There exists
\delta_{0}>0
, depending on
\lambda,\alpha,\beta
, such that the following holds. Let
v:B_{\frac{1}{\delta_{0}}}\setminus\overline{B_{\delta_{0}}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfy:

•

v
is harmonic.

•

\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2\rho}\setminus\overline{B_{\rho}})}\leq\rho^{1+\alpha}
, for
1\leq\rho\leq\frac{1}{\delta_{0}}
.

•

\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2\rho}\setminus\overline{B_{\rho}})}\leq\rho^{-\alpha}
, for
\delta_{0}\leq\rho\leq 1
.

Then there exist
a\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
b,c\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\|v-a\cdot x-b-c\log|x|\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2}\setminus\overline{B_{1/2}})}\leq\lambda
.

Proof.

Following the same argument as in the proof of
Lemma

3.8
, we reduce the lemma to classifying a harmonic function
v_{\infty}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying the same growth conditions as
v
. The difference now is in the application of the Liouville-type classification for harmonic functions in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
which satisfy the present growth assumptions—which follows e.g. by separation of variables—since it gives that
v_{\infty}=a\cdot x+b+c\log|x|
for some suitable
a,b,c
. The difference with respect to
Lemma

3.8
is that constant functions and
\log|x|
are harmonic functions in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
which grow slower than
|x|^{-\alpha}
as
|x|\to 0
, making them admissible now in the expansion of
v_{\infty}
.
∎

Preliminaries: Rescaling

For convenience, we start by considering the new function
\widetilde{v}:=\frac{1}{2^{k_{1}}}v(2^{k_{1}}x)
.

Remark 5.5
.

The original hypotheses—in scales
from
-k_{0}
to
\infty
now—become, for some suitable new coefficients
\widetilde{a}_{k},\widetilde{b}_{k},\widetilde{c}_{k}
:

•

Excess decay between
-k_{0}
and
0
:

|\widetilde{v}(x^{\prime})-\widetilde{a}_{k}\cdot x^{\prime}-\widetilde{b}_{k}-\widetilde{c}_{k}\log|x^{\prime}||\leq\delta^{k}\varepsilon 2^{k}\mbox{ in }B_{2^{k+1}}\setminus\overline{B_{2^{k-1}}},\,\hskip 10.00002pt-k_{0}\leq k\leq 0\,.

(80)

•

Preservation of best flatness:

|\widetilde{v}(x^{\prime})-\widetilde{a}_{k}\cdot x^{\prime}-\widetilde{b}_{k}-\widetilde{c}_{k}\log|x^{\prime}||\leq\varepsilon 2^{k}\mbox{ in }B_{2^{k+1}}\setminus\overline{B_{2^{k-1}}},\,\hskip 10.00002pt1\leq k\,.

(81)

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
f:B_{2^{k_{0}}}\setminus\overline{B_{2^{-k_{0}}}}\to\mathbb{R}
be defined by

f(x^{\prime}):=\widetilde{v}(x^{\prime})-\widetilde{a}_{0}\cdot x^{\prime}-\widetilde{b}_{0}-\widetilde{c}_{0}\log|x^{\prime}|\,,

(82)

which is also harmonic.

There is
C_{2}
depending on
k_{0}
such that

f(x^{\prime})\leq C_{2}\varepsilon|x^{\prime}|^{-\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for}\hskip 10.00002pt2^{-k_{0}}\leq|x^{\prime}|\leq 1

(83)

and

f(x^{\prime})\leq C_{2}\varepsilon|x^{\prime}|\log^{2}(|x^{\prime}|+1)\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for}\hskip 10.00002pt1\leq|x^{\prime}|\leq 2^{k_{0}}\,.

(84)

Proof.

Let
\alpha_{*}=\frac{1+\alpha}{2}>\alpha
. Then, arguing exactly as in the proof where we showed that
Proposition

5.1
holds (assuming that
Proposition

5.2
does), we obtain:

|\widetilde{a}_{0}-\widetilde{a}_{k}|\leq C_{\alpha}\varepsilon 2^{(-\alpha_{*}-1)k}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\,|\widetilde{b}_{0}-\widetilde{b}_{k}|\leq C_{\alpha}\varepsilon 2^{-\alpha_{*}k}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt|\widetilde{c}_{0}-\widetilde{c}_{k}|\leq C_{\alpha}\varepsilon 2^{-\alpha k}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for every}\hskip 10.00002pt-k_{0}\leq k\leq 0\,,

and

|\widetilde{a}_{0}-\widetilde{a}_{k}|\leq C_{\alpha}\varepsilon\log^{2}2^{k}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\,|\widetilde{b}_{0}-\widetilde{b}_{k}|\leq C_{\alpha}\varepsilon 2^{k}\log 2^{k}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt|\widetilde{c}_{0}-\widetilde{c}_{k}|\leq C_{\alpha}\varepsilon 2^{k}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for every}\hskip 10.00002pt1\leq k\,.

Substituting into (
80
)–(
81
), we deduce that

|v(x^{\prime})-\widetilde{a}_{0}\cdot x^{\prime}-\widetilde{b}_{0}-\widetilde{c}_{0}\log|x^{\prime}||\leq C\varepsilon_{0}2^{-\alpha_{*}k}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{in}\hskip 10.00002ptB_{2^{k+1}}\setminus\overline{B_{2^{k-1}}}

and

|v(x^{\prime})-\widetilde{a}_{0}\cdot x^{\prime}-\widetilde{b}_{0}-\widetilde{c}_{0}\log|x^{\prime}||\leq C\varepsilon_{0}2^{k}\log^{2}2^{k}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{in}\hskip 10.00002ptB_{2^{k+1}}\setminus\overline{B_{2^{k-1}}}\,.

Recalling the definition of
f
, we immediately conclude (
83
)–(
84
).

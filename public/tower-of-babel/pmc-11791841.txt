Establishment and characterization of three human pluripotent stem cell lines from Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease Type 4B3 patients bearing mutations in MTMR5/Sbf1 gene
Authors: Elizabeth H Jacobs, Jacquelyn Schatzman Raposo, Annarita Scardamaglia, Fowzan S Alkuraya, Shahriar Nafissi, Henry Houlden, Stephan Zuchner, Mario A Saporta
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractMyotubularin-Related Protein 5 (MTMR5) is an inactive, poorly characterized D3-phosphatidylinositol phosphatase. Mutations in MTMR5 have been linked to Charcot-Marie-Tooth Disease Type 4B3 (CMT4B3), a rare, early-onset, recessive peripheral neuropathy. Here, we describe the establishment and validation of three human induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC) lines derived from unrelated CMT4B3 patients, each harboring homozygous MTMR5/Sbf1 mutations. Current MTMR5 −/− animal models do not clearly link Sbf1 mutations to severe neuropathy, so such a resource is highly desired to further elucidate the relationship between MTMR5 dysfunction and peripheral nerve degeneration.

Resource utility

Charcot-Marie-Tooth Disease Type 4B3 (CMT4B3) is an early-onset, complex neuropathy caused by mutations in Sbf1, which encodes Myotubularin-Related Protein 5 (MTMR5). To generate a cellular model for CMT4B3, we have established iPSCs via Sendai-based episomal reprogramming of fibroblasts derived from three unrelated CMT4B3 patients bearing homozygous mutations in Sbf1. Characterization and validation.

Resource details

Myotubularin and myotubularin-related proteins (MTMRs) are a group of catalytically active and inactive enzymes that dephosphorylate phosphatidylinositol-3-phosphate and phosphatidylinositol-(3,5)-bisphosphate and possess endosomal, lysosomal and autophagy-related functions (Laporte et al., 2003). Myotubularin-related protein 5 (MTMR5), encoded by the Sbf1 gene, is a large, inactive member of this family whose function is poorly understood.

Multiple myotubularin proteins, including MTMR2, MTMR13 and MTMR5 have been linked to autosomal recessive Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease, and respectively cause CMT4B1 (OMIM #601382), CMT4B2 (OMIM #604563) and CMT4B3 (OMIM #615284). CMT4B3 has a wide phenotypic spectrum, ranging from an isolated demyelinating sensori-motor polyneuropathy (Nakhro et al., 2013) to a complex neuro-developmental syndrome with predominantly axonal motor neuropathy accompanied by ophthalmoparesis and facial nerve involvement, cognitive delay, syndactyly, kyphosis and short stature (Bohlega et al., 2011; Gang et al., 2020).

In this report, we describe the generation, characterization, and validation of three iPSC lines derived from three patients diagnosed with Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease Type 4B3. Skin biopsies were acquired from patients bearing the following recessive homozygous mutations in the Sbf1 gene c.5477_5478delTG (UMi044-A) (Gang et al., 2020), c.305_306delCA (UMi045-A) (unpublished) and c.1327G>A (UMi046-A) (Bohlega et al., 2011) (Fig. 1B). All patients presented with a complex axonal neuropathy with onset during early childhood. Fibroblasts were extracted from skin biopsies and were subject to reprogramming via the CytoTuneTM-iPS 2.0 Sendai virus reprogramming system. For each of the three lines, 5–6 clones were selected and were brought to passage 5 under feeder free conditions. All had optimal iPSC morphology, with a high N:C ratio and compact edges. The clone with the best morphology was expanded and subject to the validation outlined below (Fig. 1A).

Characterization of three human pluripotent stem cell lines from Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease Type 4B3 patients.

In short, pluripotency was assessed via immunostaining at passage 6 for NANOG, OCT4, SOX2 and SSEA4 (Fig. 1D). Images of iPSCs expressing these markers was automatically acquired utilizing the CellInsight™ CX5 High-Content Screening Instrument (ThermoFisher). In companion software, HCS Studio 4.0 Cell Analysis Software, Compartment Analysis 4.0 protocol was used to quantify percentage of nuclei expressing marker of pluripotency (Fig. 1H). Absence of contamination by mycoplasma and clearance of the Sendai viral vector was successfully confirmed at passage 7 (Fig. 1E and 1I.). At passage 8 (UMi044-A), 8 (UMi045-A), and 7 (UMi046-A), the following validation was performed: Sanger sequencing for mutation confirmation of both the donor fibroblast lines and iPSCs was performed (Fig. 1B). Comprehensive Short Tandem Repeat analysis indicated that iPSCs were uncontaminated and purely derivative of donor fibroblasts (on file with journal) Lastly, G-band Karyotyping revealed a normal karyotype for all iPSC lines (Fig. 1C).

To assess differentiation potential, embryoid bodies were generated at passage 10. Immunofluorescence staining was performed to evaluate presence of markers from all germ layers, specifically PAX6, β-III-tubulin (ectoderm), Smooth Muscle Actin, Brachyury/TBXT (mesoderm), alpha-fetoprotein and FOXA2 (endoderm) (Fig. 1F; Table 2). Directed differentiation to motor neurons using dual-SMAD inhibition was performed as described previously by our lab (Saporta et al., 2015) followed by confirmatory staining with motor neuron marker HB9 (Fig. 1G). Imaging was performed using 20X objective and CellInsight™ CX5 Instrument.

Materials and methods

iPSC reprogramming and culture

Skin biopsy-derived fibroblasts were cultured under standard conditions (37°C, 5% CO2) in DMEM/F-12/GlutaMAX™ (ThermoFisher) with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, ThermoFisher) and 1% Penicillin/Streptomycin (Gibco). Fibroblasts were seeded at densities such that a 30%−60% confluence was achieved at 48 h. Transduction with Cyto-Tune™-iPS 2.0 Sendai Reprogramming Kit (ThermoFisher) was performed per manufacturers’ directions. Emergent colonies were manually transferred into GelTrex-coated plates containing Essential-8™ Medium (ThermoFisher). One day following, a half-change to mTeSR™Plus Medium (STEMCELL Technologies) was performed. Subsequently, iPSCs received 100% mTeSR™Plus. At 70% confluence, iPSC were passaged using 0.5 mM EDTA (1:3–1:6 split ratio; no Y-27632.)

Sbf1 genotyping

Genomic DNA was extracted using DNeasy Blood & Tissue Kit (Qiagen). Platinum-II-Taq DNA Polymerase (Invitrogen), touchdown thermocycling and Veriti 96-Well Fast Thermocycler (ABI) were used to amplify mutation-containing regions. PCR product cleanup using Exo-SAP-IT™ PCR Product Cleanup Reagent (ThermoFisher) was performed before Sanger sequencing (EuroFins Genomics, Louisville, KY, USA).

Vector clearance

iPSCs were subject to homogenation, RNA extraction and cDNA conversion the via QiaShredder spin-columns, RNEasy Plus Mini Kit and QuantiTect Reverse Transcription Kit (Qiagen); modified 30-second extension time). PCR amplification using manufacture-recommended thermocycling conditions was performed with Platinum™ II Taq HotStart DNA Polymerase (Invitrogen). PCR product was electrophoresed using 1.5% agarose gel with 0.5 μg/uL Ethidium Bromide (G-Biosciences) and produced bands of expected length upon visualization with FluorChemE Gel Imager (ProteinSimple).

Pluripotency analysis

Cells were prepared for immunofluorescence by fixing with 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA; Electron Microscopy Sciences) for 20 min, permeabilizing with 0.2% Triton-X-100 (ThermoFisher) for 30 min and blocking with 2.5% Normal goat serum (NGS; Invitrogen)/3% Bovine Serum Albumin (BSA; Sigma) for one hour. Cells were incubated overnight at 4°C with AlexaFluor Plus 488-conjugated primary antibodies. Counterstaining with 750 nM 4’,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI, ThermoFisher) for 15 min was performed prior to imaging using CellInsight™ CX5 and HCS Studio Cell Analysis platform.

Embryoid body (EB) differentiation

EB differentiation was initiated by detaching iPSCs and generating a single-cell suspension using TrypLE Express (ThermoFisher). Cells were gently resuspended in Knockout™ DMEM/F12 with 4 mM GlutaMAX™ (ThermoFisher), 20% KnockOut™ Serum Replacement (ThermoFisher), 0.1 mM Non-Essential Amino Acids (Invitrogen), 0.1 mM, β-mercaptoethanol (ThermoFisher) and 10 µM Y-27632 (Selleckchem). Media was replaced every 2 days via decanting. On Day 8, EBs were transferred to GelTrex-coated microplates and media was changed to DMEM-F12/ 10% FBS/1%Pen/Strep. On Day 14, EBs were fixed with PFA, permeabilized with either cold methanol (VWR) (for SMA, AFP) or 0.1% Triton-X-100 (for Pax6, TUBB3, Brachyury/TBXT, FOXA2) and blocked with 2.5% NGS/3% BSA. For immunofluorescence, EBs were incubated with primary antibodies overnight at 4°C. EBs then were incubated with 1:400 secondary antibody for 1 h and counterstained with DAPI. EBs were imaged (20X) with CellInsight™ CX5 microscope.

Karyotyping & STR analysis

High-resolution G-Band Karyotyping (20 cells/line) and STR analysis was performed at Cell Line Genetics (Madison, WI, USA).

Mycoplasma Testing

MycoAlertTM PLUS Detection Kit (Lonza) was used to evaluate mycoplasma spp contamination. At 70% confluence, media changes were stopped for 72 h. Spent media was assayed according to manufacturer’s direction.

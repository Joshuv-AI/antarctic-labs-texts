Sparse Autoencoder Neural Operators: Model Recovery in Function Spaces
Bahareh Tolooshams, Ailsa Shen, Anima Anandkumar

Abstract
We frame the problem of unifying representations in neural models as one of sparse model recovery and introduce a framework that extends sparse autoencoders (SAEs) to lifted spaces and infinite-dimensional function spaces, enabling mechanistic interpretability of large neural operators (NO). While the Platonic Representation Hypothesis suggests that neural networks converge to similar representations across architectures, the representational properties of neural operators remain underexplored despite their growing importance in scientific computing. We compare the inference and training dynamics of SAEs, lifted-SAE, and SAE neural operators. We highlight how lifting and operator modules introduce beneficial inductive biases, enabling faster recovery, improved recovery of smooth concepts, and robust inference across varying resolutions, a property unique to neural operators.

Our Contributions
We address these questions by extending SAEs to lifted SAEs (L-SAEs), which learn concepts in a lifted space, and SAE neural operators (SAE-NO), which formalize model recovery in function spaces. We show that lifting enables recovery while acting as a preconditioner that accelerates learning, and identify conditions where the inference and dynamics of L-SAEs reduce to those of standard SAEs. We further introduce SAE-NOs, demonstrating that i) lifting-induced preconditioning extends to L-SAE-NOs, ii) truncated modes confer an inductive bias that is advantageous for recovering smooth concepts, iii) SAE-NOs recover representations robustly under resolution changes where SAEs fail, and iv) with full-frequency filters and matched spatial support, their dynamics reduce to those of SAE-CNN.

SAE Neural Operators for Model Recovery in Function Spaces

We extend SAEs to neural operators

[

Based on operator learning, we formulate a sparse functional generative model of data (
definition

2.1
), and its corresponding optimization for model recovery on function spaces (

Definition 2.1

(Sparse Functional Generative Model)
.

Consider a linear operator
\mathcal{G}_{{\bm{D}}^{*}}:\mathcal{Z}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
in function spaces, where
\mathcal{Z}
and
\mathcal{X}
are Banach spaces of functions defined on bounded domains
D_{z}\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
and
D_{x}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
, respectively. The data sample
{\bm{x}}\in\mathcal{X}
is said to follow a sparse functional generative model if there exists a sparse latent representation
{\bm{z}}
drawn from the support
\mathcal{Z}
of a probability measure
\mu
, where
{\bm{x}}=\mathcal{G}_{{\bm{D}}^{*}}({\bm{z}})
.

Definition 2.2

(Sparse Functional Model Recovery)
.

Under the sparse functional generative model (
definition

2.1
), the goal of sparse model recovery in function spaces is to estimate the underlying operator
\mathcal{G}_{{\bm{D}}^{*}}
by solving the following bilevel optimization problem:

\min_{{\bm{D}}\in\mathcal{D}}\quad\ \mathbb{E}_{{\bm{z}}\sim\mu}\|\mathcal{G}_{{\bm{D}}^{*}}({\bm{z}})-\mathcal{G}_{{\bm{D}}}(\hat{\bm{z}})\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\hat{\bm{z}}=\argmin_{{\bm{v}}}\ \mathbb{E}_{{\bm{z}}\sim\mu}\|{\bm{v}}-\mathcal{H}_{\theta}(\mathcal{G}_{{\bm{D}}^{*}}({\bm{z}}))\|_{\mathcal{Z}}^{2},

(2)

where the operator
\mathcal{H}_{\theta}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}
aims to approximate the inverse of the operator
\mathcal{G}_{{\bm{D}}^{*}}
. Model recovery is successful when the learned concepts
{\bm{D}}
in
\mathcal{G}_{{\bm{D}}}
closely approximate
{\bm{D}}^{*}
in
\mathcal{G}_{{\bm{D}}^{*}}
.

To recover concepts, we formalize SAE-NO (
definition

2.3
) with an encoder approximating the inverse generative operator and a decoder that captures the underlying operator and its learnable concepts.

Definition 2.3

(Sparse Autoencoder Neural Operators (SAE-NO) for Model Recovery)
.

The goal of model recovery with SAE-NO in function spaces is to infer a sparse code
{\bm{z}}
as samples from the function space
\mathcal{Z}
, representing the data sample from the function space
\mathcal{X}
via the operator
\mathcal{G}_{{\bm{D}}}
:
\quad\text{(encoder)}\quad{\bm{z}}=\mathcal{H}_{\theta}({\bm{x}}),\quad\text{(decoder)}\quad\hat{\bm{x}}=\mathcal{G}_{{\bm{D}}}({\bm{z}})
, where
\mathcal{H}_{\theta}
is the encoder operator, and model recovery involves estimating the concepts
{\bm{D}}
.

We define
SAE-FNOs
as
\mathcal{G}_{{\bm{D}}}({\bm{z}})\coloneqq\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(\sum_{c=1}^{C}{\bm{W}}_{c}\cdot(\mathcal{F}{\bm{z}}_{c})\big)

{\bm{W}}_{c}=\mathcal{F}{\bm{D}}_{c}
is the Fourier transform of the convolution kernel. This reduces sparse functional model recovery to recovery of a sparse convolutional generative model (
definition

A.1

{\bm{z}}
and
{\bm{D}}
. The formulation generalizes SAEs to operator and Fourier domains, enabling concept learning in function spaces. We further extend this with

and
projection
modules from the Fourier Neural Operator framework to enable recovery in lifted space
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
(
definition

2.4
).

Definition 2.4

(Lifted SAE-NO for Model Recovery)
.

The goal of model recovery with lifted SAE-NO in function spaces is to infer a sparse code
{\bm{z}}
as samples from the function space
\mathcal{Z}
, representing the data from
\mathcal{X}
via the operator
\mathcal{G}_{{\bm{D}}_{{\bm{L}}}}
:

\displaystyle\text{(lifting and encoder)}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\bm{z}}=\mathcal{H}_{\theta}({\bm{y}}),\quad{\bm{y}}={\bm{L}}{\bm{x}}

(3)

\displaystyle\text{(decoder and projecting)}

\displaystyle\hat{{\bm{x}}}={\bm{P}}\hat{{\bm{y}}},\quad\hat{{\bm{y}}}=\mathcal{G}_{{\bm{D}}_{{\bm{L}}}}({\bm{z}}),

where
\mathcal{H}_{\theta}
encodes the lifted data
\tilde{\bm{x}}

{\bm{D}}_{{\bm{L}}}

Finally, we informally define
architectural inference equivalence

(Training Dynamics of Lifting)

The training dynamics of the lifted-SAE (L-SAE)

Conclusion
We introduced a unified framework for sparse model recovery to analyze representations across finite- and infinite-dimensional settings. Our analysis shows that the SAE-FNO’s inductive biases enable superior recovery of smooth concepts and generalization across resolutions where standard models fail. This provides a rigorous foundation for both model recovery in function spaces and mechanistic interpretability of large neural operators. While validated here on synthetic data, a critical next step is applying this framework to real-world settings, where we speculate based on the prior success of neural operators that SAE-FNO will be highly effective in extracting meaningful concepts, including learning harmonic representations useful in scientific modelling and capturing global concepts or frequency-based coding in neuroscience.

Neural operators
Neural operators consist of the following three modules:

Proposition C.1

(Architectural Inference Equivalence of Lifting in SAE)
.

The architectural inference of a SAE is equivalent to a lifted-SAE if the projection is tied to the lifting operator (
{\bm{P}}={\bm{L}}^{\top}
), and the lifting operator is orthogonal (
{\bm{L}}^{\top}{\bm{L}}={\bm{I}}
), i.e., under the same learned model
{\bm{D}}={\bm{P}}{\bm{D}}_{L}
, the representation dynamics and output of the networks are equivalent.

Proof.

We provide the proof for the sparse generative model case when the encoder implements a proximal gradient descent type of operation in an unrolled learning setting (e.g.,
6
). For the SAE, the representation refinement at every encoder layer follows:

{\bm{z}}_{t+1}=\mathcal{R}({\bm{z}}_{t}-\alpha{\bm{D}}^{\top}({\bm{D}}{\bm{z}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}))

(11)

By construction, we incorporate the underlying model from the lifted-SAE to the standard SAE:
{\bm{D}}={\bm{P}}{\bm{D}}_{L}
, and re-write the encoder as:

{\bm{z}}_{t+1}=\mathcal{R}({\bm{z}}_{t}-\alpha({\bm{P}}{\bm{D}}_{L})^{\top}({\bm{P}}{\bm{D}}_{L}{\bm{z}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}))=\mathcal{R}({\bm{z}}_{t}-\alpha{\bm{D}}_{L}^{\top}{\bm{P}}^{\top}({\bm{P}}{\bm{D}}_{L}{\bm{z}}_{t}-{\bm{x}}))

(12)

Using the assumption on the lifting-projection,

{\bm{z}}_{t+1}=\mathcal{R}\left({\bm{z}}_{t}-\alpha({\bm{D}}_{L})^{\top}({\bm{D}}_{L}{\bm{z}}_{t}-{\bm{L}}{\bm{x}})\right)

(13)

This final expression is the iterative encoder update for
{\bm{z}}
in the lifted space, where the dictionary is
{\bm{D}}_{L}
and the input is lifted by
{\bm{L}}
. This completes the proof of architectural inference equivalence on the standard and lifted architectures.
∎

Proof.

For the SAE, we learn a dictionary
{\bm{D}}
by minimizing the reconstruction loss for the data sample
{\bm{x}}
. The loss function is:

\mathcal{L}({\bm{D}})=\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert{\bm{x}}-{\bm{D}}{\bm{z}}\right\lVert_{2}^{2}

(14)

The gradient of the loss for
{\bm{D}}
is:

\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial{\bm{D}}}=-({\bm{x}}-{\bm{D}}{\bm{z}}){\bm{z}}^{\top}

(15)

The analytic gradient update, given

{\bm{D}}

{\bm{D}}^{(k+1)}={\bm{D}}^{(k)}-\eta\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial{\bm{D}}^{(k)}}={\bm{D}}^{(k)}+\eta({\bm{x}}-{\bm{D}}^{(k)}{\bm{z}}){\bm{z}}^{\top}

(16)

For lifted-SAE, we learn a dictionary

{\bm{x}}
is lifted by

The analytic gradient given

\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial{\bm{D}}}=-{\bm{P}}^{\top}({\bm{x}}-{\bm{P}}{\bm{D}}_{L}{\bm{z}}){\bm{z}}^{\top}

The gradient update rule for the lifted dictionary
{\bm{D}}_{L}

{\bm{D}}_{L}^{(k+1)}={\bm{D}}_{L}^{(k)}-\eta_{L}\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial{\bm{D}}_{L}^{(k)}}={\bm{D}}_{L}^{(k)}+\eta_{L}{\bm{P}}^{\top}({\bm{x}}-{\bm{P}}{\bm{D}}_{L}{\bm{z}})({\bm{z}})^{\top}

To understand the learning dynamics in the original space, we first incorporate the underlying model from L-SAEs into SAE using the relation
{\bm{D}}^{\prime}={\bm{P}}{\bm{D}}_{L}

{\bm{D}}^{\prime(k+1)}={\bm{D}}^{\prime(k)}+\eta_{L}({\bm{P}}{\bm{P}}^{\top})({\bm{x}}-{\bm{D}}^{\prime(k)}{\bm{z}})({\bm{z}})^{\top}

(20)

In our specific architecture, we constrain the projection matrix to be the transpose of the lifting matrix such that

Proposition C.2

(Architectural Inference Equivalence of Lifting in SAE-CNN)
.

The architectural inference of a SAE-CNN is equivalent to a lifted SAE-CNN (L-SAE-CNN) if the projection is tied to the lifting operator (
{\bm{P}}={\bm{L}}^{\top}
), and the lifting operator is orthogonal (
{\bm{L}}^{\top}{\bm{L}}={\bm{I}}
), i.e., under the same learned model
{\bm{D}}_{c}={\bm{P}}{\bm{D}}_{L,c}
, the representation dynamics and output of the networks are equivalent.

Proof.

We provide the proof for the sparse convolutional generative model case when the encoder implements a proximal gradient descent type of operation in an unrolled learning setting (e.g.,
7
). For the SAE-CNN, the representation refinement for the
c
-th feature map at every encoder layer follows:

{\bm{z}}_{c,t+1}=\mathcal{R}\left({\bm{z}}_{c,t}-\alpha\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{C}{\bm{D}}_{i}*{\bm{z}}_{i,t}-{\bm{x}}\right)\star{\bm{D}}_{c}\right)

(23)

By construction, we incorporate the underlying model from the lifted-SAE-CNN to the standard SAE-CNN:
{\bm{D}}_{c}^{(k)}={\bm{P}}{\bm{D}}_{L,c}^{(k)}
, and re-write the encoder using the adjoint property:

{\bm{z}}_{c,t+1}=\mathcal{R}\left({\bm{z}}_{c,t}-\alpha\left({\bm{P}}^{\top}\left[\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{C}{\bm{P}}{\bm{D}}_{L,i}*{\bm{z}}_{i,t}\right)-{\bm{x}}\right]\star{\bm{D}}_{L,c}\right)\right)

(24)

{\bm{z}}_{c,t+1}=\mathcal{R}\left({\bm{z}}_{c,t}-\alpha\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{C}({\bm{P}}^{\top}{\bm{P}}){\bm{D}}_{L,i}*{\bm{z}}_{i,t}-{\bm{P}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}\right)\star{\bm{D}}_{L,c}\right)

(25)

Using the assumption on the lifting-projection,

{\bm{z}}_{c,t+1}=\mathcal{R}\left({\bm{z}}_{c,t}-\alpha\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{C}{\bm{D}}_{L,i}*{\bm{z}}_{i,t}-{\bm{L}}{\bm{x}}\right)\star{\bm{D}}_{L,c}\right)

(26)

This final expression is the iterative encoder update for
{\bm{z}}_{c}
in the lifted space, where the dictionary is
{\bm{D}}_{L}
and the input is lifted by
{\bm{L}}
. This completes the proof of architectural inference equivalence for SAE-CNN and L-SAE-CNN.
∎

Proposition C.3

The training dynamics of the lifted-SAE-CNN (L-SAE-CNN)

{\bm{z}}

{\bm{D}}_{c}

\eta_{L}
is:

{\bm{W}}_{L,c}^{(k+1)}={\bm{W}}_{L,c}^{(k)}+\dfrac{\eta_{L}}{\sqrt{M}}\mathcal{F}\left({\bm{P}}^{\top}\left({\bm{x}}-\hat{{\bm{x}}}^{(k)}\right)\right)\odot\mathcal{F}({\bm{z}}_{c,t})

(42)

To understand the learning dynamics in the original spatial-domain, we derive the update rule for the effective lifted spatial dictionary
{\bm{D}}^{\prime}_{L,c}
. Taking the inverse Fourier, we get:

{\bm{D}}_{L,c}^{\prime(k+1)}={\bm{D}}_{L,c}^{\prime(k)}+\dfrac{\eta_{L}}{M}\left({\bm{P}}^{\top}\left({\bm{x}}-\hat{{\bm{x}}}^{(k)}\right)\right)\star{\bm{z}}_{c,t}

(43)

We also derive the update rule for the effective spatial dictionary in the original space
{\bm{D}}_{c}^{\prime}
. Left-multiplying the update for
{\bm{D}}_{L,c}^{\prime}
by
{\bm{P}}
, we have:

{\bm{D}}_{c}^{\prime(k+1)}={\bm{D}}_{c}^{\prime(k)}+\dfrac{\eta_{L}}{M}\left(({\bm{P}}{\bm{P}}^{\top})\left({\bm{x}}-\hat{{\bm{x}}}^{(k)}\right)\right)\star{\bm{z}}_{c,t}

(44)

In our specific architecture, we constrain the projection matrix to be the transpose of the lifting matrix such that
{\bm{P}}={\bm{L}}^{\top}
. Substituting this into the update rules yields:

{\bm{W}}_{L,c}^{(k+1)}={\bm{W}}_{L,c}^{(k)}+\dfrac{\eta_{L}}{\sqrt{M}}\mathcal{F}\left({\bm{L}}\left({\bm{x}}-\hat{{\bm{x}}}^{(k)}\right)\right)\odot\mathcal{F}({\bm{z}}_{c,t})

(45)

{\bm{D}}_{L,c}^{\prime(k+1)}={\bm{D}}_{L,c}^{\prime(k)}+\dfrac{\eta_{L}}{M}\left({\bm{L}}\left({\bm{x}}-\hat{{\bm{x}}}^{(k)}\right)\right)\star{\bm{z}}_{c,t}

(46)

{\bm{D}}_{c}^{\prime(k+1)}={\bm{D}}_{c}^{\prime(k)}+\dfrac{\eta_{L}}{M}\left(({\bm{L}}^{\top}{\bm{L}})\left({\bm{x}}-\hat{{\bm{x}}}^{(k)}\right)\right)\star{\bm{z}}_{c,t}

(47)

Substituting for
\hat{{\bm{x}}}^{(k)}
, the full effective spatial-domain update is:

{\bm{D}}_{c}^{\prime(k+1)}={\bm{D}}_{c}^{\prime(k)}+\dfrac{\eta_{L}}{M}\left(({\bm{L}}^{\top}{\bm{L}})\left({\bm{x}}-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{C}\mathcal{F}({\bm{D}}_{i}^{\prime(k)})\odot\mathcal{F}({\bm{z}}_{i,t})\right)\right)\right)\star{\bm{z}}_{c,t}

(48)

Hence, when
{\bm{L}}^{\top}{\bm{L}}={\bm{I}}
, the lifted-SAE-FNO shows equivalent learning dynamics as the standard SAE-FNO for model recovery. ‘
∎

Proposition C.5

(Architectural Inference Equivalence of SAE-CNN and SAE-FNO)
.

The architectural inference of an SAE-CNN is equivalent to a sparse autoencoder neural operator (SAE-NO) if the integral operator of SAE-FNO and parameters of SAEs are defined as a convolution where the SAE-FNO’s frequency-domain weights
{\bm{W}}_{c}
are the Fourier transform of SAE-CNN’s spatial domain kernels:
{\bm{W}}_{c}=\mathcal{F}{\bm{D}}_{c}
.

Proof.

We provide the proof for an encoder that implements a proximal gradient descent type of operation in an unrolled learning setting. For the SAE-CNN, the representation refinement for the
c
-th feature map at every encoder layer follows:

{\bm{z}}_{c,t+1}=\mathcal{R}\left({\bm{z}}_{c,t}-\alpha\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{C}{\bm{D}}_{i}*{\bm{z}}_{i,t}-{\bm{x}}\right)\star{\bm{D}}_{c}\right)

(49)

By construction, we incorporate the underlying model from the SAE-FNO to the SAE-CNN:
{\bm{W}}_{c}=\mathcal{F}{\bm{D}}_{c}
, and re-write the gradient of the encoder using Correlation and Convolution Theorems:

\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\mathcal{F}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{C}{\bm{D}}_{i}*{\bm{z}}_{i,t}-{\bm{x}}\right)\odot\mathcal{F}{\bm{D}}_{c}^{H}\right)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{C}{\bm{W}}_{i}\odot\mathcal{F}{\bm{z}}_{i,t}-\mathcal{F}{\bm{x}}\right)\odot({\bm{W}}_{c})^{H}

(50)

Substituting this back into
eq.

49
gives:

{\bm{z}}_{c,t+1}=\mathcal{R}\left({\bm{z}}_{c,t}-\alpha\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{C}{\bm{W}}_{i}\odot\mathcal{F}{\bm{z}}_{i,t}\right)-\mathcal{F}{\bm{x}}\right)\odot({\bm{W}}_{c})^{H}\right)

(51)

This final expression is the SAE-FNO’s iterative encoder update for
{\bm{z}}_{c}
in the spectral domain, where the dictionary is
{\bm{W}}
. This completes the proof of architectural inference equivalence between SAE-CNN and SAE-FNO.
∎

Proposition C.6

(Training Dynamics of SAE-CNN and SAE-FNO)
.

The training dynamics of the SAE-FNO’s frequency-domain weights
{\bm{W}}_{c}^{(k+1)}={\bm{W}}_{c}^{(k)}+\dfrac{\eta_{L}}{\sqrt{M}}\mathcal{F}\left({\bm{x}}-\hat{{\bm{x}}}^{(k)}\right)\odot\mathcal{F}({\bm{z}}_{c,t})
has an effective update in the original space, expressed as:
{\bm{D}}_{c}^{\prime(k+1)}={\bm{D}}_{c}^{\prime(k)}+\dfrac{\eta}{M}\left({\bm{x}}-\hat{{\bm{x}}}^{(k)}\right)\star{\bm{z}}_{c,t}
. If the architectures satisfy the architectural inference equivalence condition (
proposition

C.5
), then the dynamics is equivalent to that of a SAE-CNN:
{\bm{D}}_{c}^{(k+1)}={\bm{D}}_{c}^{(k)}+\eta\left({\bm{x}}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{C}{\bm{D}}_{i}^{(k)}*{\bm{z}}_{i,t}\right)\star{\bm{z}}_{c,t}
.

Proof.

For a standard SAE-CNN, we learn a dictionary
{\bm{D}}
by minimizing the reconstruction loss for the data sample
{\bm{x}}
. The loss function is:

\mathcal{L}({\bm{D}})=\dfrac{1}{2}\left\lVert{\bm{x}}-\sum\limits_{c=1}^{C}{\bm{D}}_{c}*{\bm{z}}_{c}\right\lVert_{2}^{2}

(52)

The gradient of the loss with respect to the
c
-th kernel
{\bm{D}}_{c}
is:

\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial{\bm{D}}_{c}}=-\left({\bm{x}}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{C}{\bm{D}}_{i}*{\bm{z}}_{i}\right)\star{\bm{z}}_{c}

(53)

where
\star
denotes cross-correlation.

The analytic gradient update, given
{\bm{z}}
, for
{\bm{D}}_{c}
at iteration
k
with a learning rate
\eta
is:

{\bm{D}}_{c}^{(k+1)}={\bm{D}}_{c}^{(k)}+\eta\left({\bm{x}}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{C}{\bm{D}}_{i}^{(k)}*{\bm{z}}_{i,t}\right)\star{\bm{z}}_{c,t}

(54)

For a standard SAE-FNO, we learn frequency-domain weights
{\bm{W}}_{c}=\mathcal{F}{\bm{D}}_{c}
by minimizing the reconstruction loss for the data sample
{\bm{x}}
. Taking normalization into account, the reconstruction operator is
\hat{{\bm{x}}}=\mathcal{G}_{{\bm{W}}}({\bm{z}})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(\sum_{c=1}^{C}{\bm{W}}_{c}\cdot(\mathcal{F}{\bm{z}}_{c})\big)
, where
{\bm{W}}_{c}=\mathcal{F}{\bm{D}}_{c}
, where
M
is the number of modes. The loss function is:

\mathcal{L}({\bm{W}})=\dfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{{\bm{z}}\sim\mu}\left[\left\lVert{\bm{x}}-\mathcal{G}_{{\bm{W}}}({\bm{z}})\right\lVert_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}\right]

(55)

where
{\bm{x}}=\mathcal{G}_{{\bm{D}}^{*}}({\bm{z}})
is the ground-truth signal.

The gradient of the loss with respect to the
c
-th frequency-domain kernel
{\bm{W}}_{c}
is:

\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial{\bm{W}}_{c}}=-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\mathcal{F}\left({\bm{x}}-\hat{{\bm{x}}}\right)\odot\mathcal{F}({\bm{z}}_{c})

(56)

The analytic gradient update, given
{\bm{z}}
, for
{\bm{W}}_{c}
at iteration
k
with a learning rate
\eta
is:

{\bm{W}}_{c}^{(k+1)}={\bm{W}}_{c}^{(k)}+\dfrac{\eta}{\sqrt{M}}\mathcal{F}\left({\bm{x}}-\hat{{\bm{x}}}^{(k)}\right)\odot\mathcal{F}({\bm{z}}_{c,k})

(57)

Applying the inverse Fourier transform and the cross-correlation theorem, we obtain the effective update rule for the spatial dictionary
{\bm{D}}_{c}^{\prime}
:

{\bm{D}}_{c}^{\prime(k+1)}={\bm{D}}_{c}^{\prime(k)}+\dfrac{\eta}{M}\left({\bm{x}}-\hat{{\bm{x}}}^{(k)}\right)\star{\bm{z}}_{c,t}

(58)

This is equivalent to the gradient update of SAE-CNN with a scaling factor of
\frac{1}{M}
on the learning rate, which arises from the inverse Fourier normalization conventions.
∎

Proposition C.7

(Architectural Inference Equivalence of L-SAE-CNN and L-SAE-FNO)
.

The architectural inference of a L-SAE-CNN is equivalent to a L-SAE-FNO if the integral operator of L-SAE-FNO and parameters of SAEs are defined as a convolution where the L-SAE-FNO’s lifted frequency-domain weights
{\bm{W}}_{L,c}
are the Fourier transform of L-SAE-CNN’s lifted spatial domain kernels:
{\bm{W}}_{L,c}=\mathcal{F}{\bm{D}}_{L,c}
.

Proof.

We provide proof for an encoder that implements a proximal gradient descent type of operation in an unrolled learning setting. For the L-SAE-CNN, the representation refinement for the
c
-th feature map at every encoder layer follows:

{\bm{z}}_{c,t+1}=\mathcal{R}\left({\bm{z}}_{c,t}-\alpha\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{C}{\bm{D}}_{L,i}*{\bm{z}}_{i,t}-{\bm{L}}{\bm{x}}\right)\star{\bm{D}}_{L,c}\right)

(59)

By construction, we incorporate the underlying model from the L-SAE-FNO to the L-SAE-CNN:
{\bm{W}}_{L,c}=\mathcal{F}{\bm{D}}_{L,c}
, and re-write the gradient of the encoder using Correlation and Convolution Theorems:

\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\mathcal{F}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{C}{\bm{D}}_{L,i}^{(k)}*{\bm{z}}_{i,t}-{\bm{L}}{\bm{x}}\right)\odot\mathcal{F}{\bm{D}}_{L,c}^{H}\right)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{C}{\bm{W}}_{L,i}^{(k)}\odot\mathcal{F}{\bm{z}}_{i,t}-\mathcal{F}({\bm{L}}{\bm{x}})\right)\odot({\bm{W}}_{L,c})^{H}

(60)

Substituting this back into
eq.

59
gives:

{\bm{z}}_{c,t+1}=\mathcal{R}\left({\bm{z}}_{c,t}-\alpha\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\left(\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{C}{\bm{W}}_{L,i}\odot\mathcal{F}({\bm{z}}_{i,t})\right)-\mathcal{F}({\bm{L}}{\bm{x}})\right)\odot({\bm{W}}_{L,c})^{H}\right]\right)

(61)

This final expression is the L-SAE-FNO’s iterative update for
{\bm{z}}_{c}
in the lifted spectral domain, where the dictionary is
{\bm{W}}_{L,c}
. This completes the proof of architectural inference equivalence between L-SAE-CNN and L-SAE-FNO.
∎

Proposition C.8

(Architectural Inference Equivalence of Lifting in SAE-FNO)
.

The architectural inference of an SAE-FNO is equivalent to a Lifted SAE-FNO (L-SAE-FNO) if the projection is tied to the lifting operator (
{\bm{P}}={\bm{L}}^{\top}
), and the lifting operator is orthogonal (
{\bm{L}}^{\top}{\bm{L}}={\bm{I}}
), i.e., under the same learned model
{\bm{W}}_{c}={\bm{P}}{\bm{W}}_{L,c}
, the representation dynamics and output of the networks are equivalent.

Proof.

We prove this equivalence by establishing a chain of architectural inference equivalences that connects the SAE-FNO to the Lifted SAE-FNO, leveraging the propositions previously established:

1.

From
Proposition

C.5
, the architectural inference of an SAE-FNO is equivalent to a SAE-CNN.

2.

From
Proposition

C.2
, the architectural inference of a SAE-CNN is equivalent to L-SAE-CNN.

3.

From
Proposition

C.7
, the architectural inference of a L-SAE-CNN is equivalent to a L-SAE-FNO.

By the transitive property of these equivalences, we can establish a direct architectural inference equivalence between SAE-FNO and L-SAE-FNO under the same lifting-projection conditions. This completes the proof.
∎

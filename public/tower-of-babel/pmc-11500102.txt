Improving the accuracy of dynamic inclination measurement by machine learning
Authors: Qiwei Liu, Fanmin Kong, Xiaolong Chen, Guangsheng Wang, Kang Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractWith the diminishing availability of oil and gas resources, the Rotary Steerable System has become increasingly important. However, the vibrations and shocks during the drilling process pose challenges to the Measurement While Drilling. In recent years, the application of machine learning in the field of petroleum exploration has gradually expanded, especially in the estimation of geological parameters and lithology discrimination. However, there is still limited research on drilling tool attitude measurement. To address the above-mentioned issues, this study proposes a method that combines drilling tool attitude sensor data with artificial neural networks to improve the accuracy of dynamic inclination measurement. This method utilizes machine learning techniques, combining real-time z-axis acceleration signals and magnetic induction signals, and employs a deep learning model to invert the x and y-axis acceleration signals, thereby achieving high-precision measurement of dynamic inclination angles. Experimental results show that Long Short-Term Memory model, under simulated measurement conditions with different rotational speeds, yields dynamic inclination curve errors ranging from 0.4° to 0.7°, significantly reducing the errors compared to the original measurements. This method not only improves the accuracy of inclination angle measurement but also demonstrates strong adaptability to different rotational speeds, providing more accurate data support for drilling operations.Keywords: Machine learning, Dynamic inclination measurement, Rotary steerable system, Long short-term memorySubject terms: Mechanical engineering, Crude oil

Introduction

With the continuous deepening of oil and gas exploration and development, easily exploitable conventional oil reserves are gradually decreasing, which has attracted much attention to unconventional resources such as shale oil, as well as difficult-to-develop regions such as thin oil reservoirs, marginal oil reservoirs, and residual oil reservoirs1,2. In the past decade, in order to effectively develop these hard-to-reach oil and gas resources, complex well structures such as horizontal wells, large displacement wells, and multilateral wells have rapidly developed3, which has also prompted various petroleum exploration research institutions to engage in directional drilling technology research. Directional drilling technology4,5, which allows the drill bit to drill along a predetermined trajectory, not only significantly reduces drilling costs but also saves a considerable amount of time. The implementation of this technology relies on a series of sensors, including gravity acceleration sensors and magnetic flux sensors, as well as corresponding algorithms to obtain real-time tool position and navigation information. Traditionally, the measurement of the tool position at the bottom of the well was performed when the tool was in a stationary state. However, with technological advancements, the importance of continuously measuring the wellbore trajectory has become increasingly prominent, which has driven the rapid development of Rotary Steerable Systems (RSS). It refers to a drilling system that achieves real-time steering while the drill string is rotating6. Among them, fast and accurate measurement of the tool position at the bottom is crucial to ensure precise drilling to the designated reservoir area. However, the high-speed rotation of the tool, rock cutting by the drill bit, and the friction and collision between the drill string and the wellbore walls all generate strong vibrations and impacts, which pose great challenges to the accurate measurement of gravity acceleration. Therefore, optimizing the gravity acceleration measurement methods to suppress various interference noises is essential for improving the accuracy of accelerometers and, in turn, enhancing the accuracy of dynamic inclination measurements in the well.

A large amount of research has been conducted in the academic community to improve the accuracy of inclination measurement. For example, Liu Baiyan et al. introduced the principle of inclination measurement using accelerometers in an automatic vertical drilling control system, proposed the use of Butterworth low-pass filters to eliminate vibration noise, and performed centrifugal force correction. However, this method cannot handle noise within the passband7. Zhang Wenxiu et al. proposed a gravity acceleration signal extraction method based on cross-correlation detection, selecting radial or tangential magnetometer signals as reference signals, and processing them using correlation detection methods, and calculating the inclination and tool face angle8. Xue Qilong et al. proposed using different signal processing methods for stationary and rotating drilling tools, but this method only uses the z-axis signal when the drilling tool is rotating, which reduces the measurement accuracy9. Yang et al. proposed an adaptive decay square root unscented Kalman filter method, which introduces a decay factor in the UKF to dynamically adjust the filter gain and effectively decouple the vibration signal of the accelerometer. However, this algorithm has many restrictions and requires the noise in the drilling tool to follow a Gaussian distribution10. Tunkiel et al. proposed a “drilling while training” method, which uses a recurrent neural network (RNN) to dynamically model and continuously learn the inclination data in the directional drilling process to achieve real-time prediction and improve drilling accuracy. However, its model training and operation require high computing resources and training time, which limits its application in resource-constrained field environments11. In recent years, with the widespread application of machine learning methods in the scientific and engineering fields, artificial neural networks have also begun to play a role in the field of petroleum exploration12–14. However, the current application of artificial intelligence in the field of petroleum exploration mainly focuses on geological parameter estimation, lithology discrimination, and formation boundary determination, with relatively less application in dynamic drilling tool attitude measurement15–19.

Given this, this article proposes a new method to improve the accuracy of dynamic drilling tool attitude measurement through machine learning, combining the in-depth analysis of the data structure relationship of the RSS and the advantages of artificial neural networks in data structure representation. Firstly, the dynamic measurement signals of acceleration on the x-axis and y-axis are processed using a high-order Butterworth-Bessel filter. This type of filter ensures good phase delay characteristics within the required frequency range., and deep learning is conducted by combining the dynamic measurement signals of acceleration in the z-axis and the magnetic induction signal to construct a neural network model. Under the rotation of the drilling tool, the real-time x-axis and y-axis signals are inverted through the neural network model using the filtered real-time z-axis signal and the magnetic induction signal, and then the dynamic inclination and azimuth are calculated to achieve high-precision measurement. The experimental results show that the LSTM model, under different simulated drilling conditions, can invert the dynamic wellbore inclination curve with an error range of 0.4°-0.7°, which is significantly smaller than the original measured wellbore inclination angle and recent literature. Additionally, the proposed method has significant advantages, including the simplicity of the model structure, the fast model training process, and the low requirement for computing resources. These characteristics make the method particularly suitable for limited computing resources environments and can be efficiently applied to real-time data processing and analysis tasks, creating favorable conditions for achieving “drilling while training” in the future.

Measurement method of well inclination

Methodology of inclination measurement: In the measurement of drilling tool attitude, the core parameters include inclination I, azimuth A, and tool face angle T. The acquisition of these parameters usually requires calculation of the data collected by sensors. It is worth noting that different dynamic inclination measurement and control systems often have differences in the installation structure of sensors. In order to meet the actual measurement needs, this study independently developed a rotating directional measurement and control system, with the measurement and control circuit board shown in Fig. 1. In this system, a coupled measurement installation method is used: the measurement sensor is fixedly combined with the drilling tool to ensure that the sensor can rotate synchronously when the drilling tool rotates.

During the process of rotary drilling, the bottom drilling tool will undergo various complex motion forms, including lateral vibration, longitudinal vibration, eddy current, and stick-slip. These compound motions will significantly affect the triaxial gravity accelerometer installed on the drilling tool, resulting in the inclusion of a large amount of noise components in the collected signals. This interference of noise can lead to significant dynamic errors, causing a serious deviation between the final spatial attitude measurement results and the true values. Specifically, the signals of the x-axis and y-axis in the horizontal direction are particularly affected by vibration, containing a large amount of vibration noise. In contrast, the z-axis is mainly affected by longitudinal vibration, with smaller variations in vibration amplitude, resulting in relatively less noise in the signal9,20.

Dynamic well inclination solving by machine learning

In order to achieve dynamic inclination measurement during the process of drilling rotation and reduce the interference of downhole vibrations on the measurement signal, it is crucial to develop corresponding filtering algorithms. However, due to the complex motion state of downhole drilling tools, researching and developing such filtering algorithms is challenging. It is worth noting that the z-axis accelerometer signal and the magnetic flux sensor signal are less affected by vibrations. Therefore, these two signals can serve as effective references for calibrating the accelerometer signal. This paper proposes a method to improve the accuracy of dynamic drilling tool attitude measurement through machine learning. The core idea of this method is to utilize machine learning techniques to calibrate the measurements that are more affected by vibrations using the measurements that are less affected. Specifically, under rotation conditions, the acceleration signals of the x and y axes are filtered, combined with the acceleration signal of the z-axis and the magnetic flux signal, to conduct deep learning and build a neural network model. When measuring the wellbore inclination, the real-time filtered z-axis signal and magnetic flux signal are used to inversely calculate the real-time x and y-axis signals through the trained neural network model, and further calculate the dynamic inclination, thereby achieving high-precision measurement.

Dataset of inclination parameters

In this study, the drilling inclination angle of 22.5° was selected for data collection. From the collected data, 10,000 continuous samples were selected to form the dataset for the experiment. Each set of samples contains five values, specifically including 3 axial acceleration values and the magnetic flux values in the x and y axes. When constructing the deep neural network model, 6,500 samples were selected from the total of 10,000 samples for model training, validation, and testing. Specifically, these samples were further divided as follows: 70% for the training set, to train and optimize the model; 15% for the validation set, used to measure network generalization and to halt training when generalization stops improving; the remaining 15% as the test set, used to evaluate the performance and generalization ability of the model.

Neural network modeling

In the problem of well inclination data inversion, a neural network is needed to map the relationship between input values and output values. Since the data collected by sensors during drilling operations changes with depth, these data usually exist in sequential form. Not only do the sample data have mutual relationships, but there is also a strong dependency between the data before and after in the sequence. Therefore, this paper adopts a widely used deep learning algorithm model called Long Short-Term Memory network (LSTM)21. LSTM network is a type of recurrent neural network (RNN), which is a class of artificial neural networks used for processing sequential data. Compared to traditional feedforward neural networks, RNNs have memory capabilities, enabling them to learn and understand the context of sequence data. The characteristic of RNN lies in its network structure having recurrent connections, allowing information to circulate in the network for processing sequential data. Its basic structure includes: Input Layer: receiving input of sequential data. Hidden Layer: containing recurrent connections, enabling the network to memorize historical information, and this layer can include multiple time steps. Output Layer: generating the network’s output, which can be classification, prediction, etc., of the sequence. At each time step, RNN receives the current input and the previous hidden layer state, and generates new hidden layer states and outputs. This structure enables RNN to capture temporal correlations in sequences.

However, traditional RNNs suffer from the problem of difficulty in capturing long-term dependencies, where the gradients of the network decay or explode rapidly as the number of time steps increases, making it challenging to effectively learn the dependencies between long sequences22. To address this issue, various improved RNN structures have emerged, with the most common one being the LSTM model. It effectively manages the flow of information by introducing gate mechanisms, thus better capturing long-term dependencies. The LSTM model is a special type of RNN architecture designed to tackle the long-term dependency problem in traditional RNNs. It manages and controls the flow of information by introducing gate mechanisms, resulting in more effective capture of long-term dependencies. As shown in Fig. 5, the core components of an LSTM cell include the cell state and the gate, which consist of the forget gate, input gate, and output gate.

The structure of the long short-term memory (LSTM) cell.

Cell state

The cell state is the core component of the LSTM network, responsible for remembering or forgetting past information. It can retain and pass on information, thereby addressing the vanishing or exploding gradient problem in traditional RNNs. The cell state regulates the flow of information through gate.

Forget gate

The forget gate determines how much past memory to retain at the current time step. It uses a sigmoid activation function to output a value between 0 and 1, representing the degree to which information in each memory cell should be preserved. If the output of the forget gate is close to 1, the information in the corresponding memory cell will be fully preserved. Conversely, if it is close to 0, the information will be completely forgotten.

Input gate

The input gate determines which parts of the memory cell to update in the current time step. It uses a sigmoid activation function to determine which information needs to be updated and calculates a new memory value using the tanh activation function. These pieces of information are then combined to update the memory cell.

Output gate

The output gate determines which parts of the memory cell to output at the current time step. It uses a sigmoid activation function to decide which information from the memory cell will be output, and then passes the output value through a tanh activation function to the output layer.

Through the collaborative action of these cells, the LSTM network is able to more effectively capture long-term dependencies when processing sequential data, thereby effectively mapping the sequential data relationship between the acceleration and magnetic flux sensors.

The training of the neural network model utilizes Mean Squared Error (MSE) as the computational function for training data. MSE is the average of the sum of squared errors between the predicted data and the original data at corresponding points. A smaller MSE value indicates a smaller error between the predicted values and the measured values. The MSE value is calculated using formula (2).

In terms of neural network model training algorithms, the Levenberg-Marquardt (LM) algorithm is utilized for training. The LM algorithm is an optimization algorithm used to solve nonlinear minimization problems. This algorithm combines the advantages of gradient descent and Newton’s method, usually leading to faster convergence to the minimum value. Additionally, the LM algorithm demonstrates a higher level of robustness when dealing with data containing noise and outliers. It can provide relatively stable solutions when facing complex or unfavorable optimization problems. The above algorithm can be used for the selection, creation, and training of neural networks, and its performance can be evaluated using mean square error and regression analysis.

Additionally, to investigate the impact of different dataset distribution schemes on model training effectiveness, we adopted three widely recognized dataset splitting strategies and conducted corresponding training. Specifically, the ratios of data for the training set, validation set, and test set were set to 80:10:10 and 60:20:20, respectively. The experimental results are detailed in Table 2. As the proportion of the training set decreased from 80 to 60%, with a corresponding increase in the proportions of the validation and test sets, although there was a slight rise in the MSE on the training set, the change in MSE on the test set remained relatively stable, with a more noticeable increase observed only under the 60:20:20 scheme. Notably, under the 70:15:15 splitting scheme, the difference in MSE within the training set was minimal, indicating that this scheme, while maintaining sufficient training, also effectively reduced the risk of overfitting, thereby achieving a more stable and relatively lower error on the test set. Results and discussion

In order to evaluate the performance of the trained LSTM model and explore the effectiveness of deep learning algorithms in improving the accuracy of wellbore inclination measurements, a series of experiments were designed and conducted in this study. The details of the experiments are as follows.

Analysis of training effect of LSTM model

To more intuitively assess the training effectiveness of the trained Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network model, an error interval analysis was conducted on the training results. The error interval histogram represents the distribution area of differences between the predicted values and the measured values. As shown in Fig. 7, the error region is mainly distributed between − 0.009 and 0.009, indicating that the data gap between the predicted values and the actual measurements generally does not exceed 0.009, with most error values being less than 0.006. Moreover, the distribution proportion of error intervals across different sample sets is relatively consistent, suggesting that the DNN model has achieved a generally consistent training effect across various sample sets without overfitting.

Error interval histogram of training set, validation set and test set.

The consistency between the LSTM model’s inversion values and the target data was verified by calculating the coefficient of determination for each sample set. As shown in Fig. 8, the red circles represent individual sample points, while the lines represent the fit line of the sample sets. It is clearly visible in the figure that the red circles, representing the Ax and Ay sample sets, closely surround the fit line, and the fit line is very close to the function . Additionally, the coefficient of determination for each sample set is close to 1, indicating a high congruence between the inversion values and the target values, which suggests that the LSTM model has achieved the anticipated training outcomes.

Evaluation of LSTM model for improving accuracy of dynamic inclination measurement

In order to verify the effectiveness of the LSTM model in improving the accuracy of dynamic inclination measurement under rotation conditions, a self-developed dynamic data acquisition module equipped with three-axis accelerometers and magnetometers was used. The module was installed on a rotating test bench to simulate the working state of near-actual rotation during drilling. During the rotation test, the inclination angle was set to 22.5°, the rotation speed was 100r/min, and data from the three-axis accelerometers and magnetic flux sensors were recorded at a sampling frequency of 1 kHz. Although the z-axis accelerometer signal is also affected by vibration to a certain extent under rotation conditions, this influence is relatively small. Therefore, the moving average filtering algorithm is used to process the signal of the z-axis accelerometer to further reduce vibration noise. Subsequently, the filtered z-axis accelerometer signal and the magnetic flux signals of the x and y axes were input into the LSTM model. By inversion, the acceleration signals of the x and y axes are obtained, and the dynamic inclination signal is obtained through data calculation. The experimental results, as shown in Fig. 9(a), depict the blue curve representing the dynamic inclination angle obtained by solving the original accelerometer signal, while the red curve represents the dynamic inclination angle obtained after inversion calculation using the LSTM model. From the figure, it is evident that the original inclination signal fluctuates between 17.5° and 27.5°, exhibiting a significant measurement error compared to the actual inclination. However, the red curve obtained after inversion calculation using the LSTM model remains stable at around 22.5°. Further observation of the zoomed-in plot in Fig. 9(b) reveals that the dynamic inclination curve obtained after inversion calculation fluctuates around 22.5°, with an error range of only 0.4°. This error range is significantly smaller compared to the original data. Therefore, from the comparison plot of the inclination, it is clear that the use of the LSTM model for inversion calculation significantly improves the measurement accuracy of the inclination during drilling rotation.

Evaluation of the accuracy improvement of inclination measurement by LSTM model under different rotational speeds and angles

To investigate the influence of different rotational speeds on the LSTM model,, multiple sets of measurement data were collected and inversion experiments were conducted at different rotation speeds. The experimental results are shown in Fig. 10, where Fig. 10(a) shows the comparison between the original dynamic inclination and the LSTM model inversion results when the wellbore inclination is set to 22.5° and the rotational speed is 150 rpm. Observing Fig. 10(a), it can be noticed that at a rotational speed of 150 rpm, the error of the LSTM model remains almost unchanged compared to 100 rpm. However, when the speed is increased to 300 rpm, as shown in Fig. 10(b), the original inclination signal fluctuates within the range of 12.5° to 32.5°, resulting in an enlarged error compared to the 100 rpm speed. It can be clearly seen from the zoomed-in figure that the fluctuation range of the inverted dynamic inclination curve increases to about 0.7°. Analyzing the reasons, we believe that this is mainly due to the difficulty in ensuring that the sensitive axis direction of the z-axis accelerometer is parallel to the axis of rotation of the drill string during the installation process, resulting in interference from the centrifugal acceleration component on the z-axis gravity acceleration signal. With the increase in rotational speed, this interference becomes more significant, thus affecting the dynamic measurement accuracy of the inclination angle. Nevertheless, the LSTM model still maintains stable performance in improving measurement accuracy. In addition, the performance of the LSTM model was tested under different wellbore inclination angles conditions. Taking an experiment with an inclination angle set at 66.5° and a rotational speed of approximately 250 rpm as an example, a significant improvement in inclination measurement accuracy can be observed by comparing the original dynamic inclination with the LSTM model inversion results. In conclusion, under different rotational speeds and inclination angles, the LSTM model can effectively enhance the accuracy of inclination measurement.

Evaluation of different deep learning algorithms for improving inclination measurement accuracy

Finally, in order to evaluate the performance of different deep learning algorithms in improving the accuracy of inclination measurement, two algorithms were compared in a contrast experiment: the LSTM model and the fully connected neural network (FCNN)23. Specifically, we trained these two models using the same set of inclination data and obtained two sets of predicted inclination angles through data inversion and calculation. To quantify the prediction accuracy, the mean square error (MSE) value was calculated. Taking the experimental conditions of inclination angle set at 22.5° and rotation speed at 50 rpm as an example, an inversion experiment of the inclination angle was conducted. The experimental results are shown in the Fig. 11, where the red curve represents the inversion data of the FCNN model, and the black curve represents the inversion data of the LSTM model. It can be clearly seen from the figure that the error magnitude of the LSTM model is significantly smaller than that of the FCNN model. In addition, error analysis was performed on other different algorithm models, and the corresponding MSE values were calculated, which are listed in Table 3. By comparing the error performance of various methods, it was found that the LSTM model has the smallest error among all the tested methods. In conclusion, based on experimental results and analysis, it can be concluded that the LSTM algorithm exhibits superior performance in deep learning algorithms for improving the accuracy of inclination measurement and is therefore more suitable for adoption.

Conclusion

This paper proposes a method that combines drilling tool attitude sensor data with artificial neural networks to improve the accuracy of dynamic inclination measurement. By employing deep learning on real-time z-axis acceleration signals and magnetic flux signals, the LSTM model is utilized to invert x and y-axis acceleration signals, achieving high-precision measurement of dynamic inclinations. Experimental results indicate that the dynamic inclination angle error range obtained by the LSTM model inversion calculation is between 0.4° to 0.7°, significantly reducing the error range compared to the original data. Furthermore, the neural network model demonstrates good performance under different rotation speeds and inclination angles, verifying the effectiveness of the method under various operating conditions. Comparative analysis with traditional FCNN models and other algorithmic models shows that the LSTM model performs the best in improving inclination angle accuracy. In summary, the machine learning method based on LSTM proposed in this paper can significantly enhance the accuracy of dynamic inclination measurement of drilling tools, holding important practical application value for petroleum exploration and development. Future work will focus on further validating the effectiveness of this method in actual drilling environments.

Focus of future work

The future research direction will focus on the online learning aspect of this method in the wellbore. Specifically, high-performance integrated circuit equipment will be used during the drilling process to perform real-time dynamic modeling and continuous learning of the inclination data in the directional drilling process, using the LSTM model. This will enable real-time prediction and improve drilling accuracy. In addition, due to the complexity of the underground environment, such as variations in temperature, pressure, and rock types, sensor readings and model performance may be affected. Further validation of existing methods will be conducted in actual drilling environments in the future.

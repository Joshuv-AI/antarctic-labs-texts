Primal-Dual Bundle Methods for Linear Equality-Constrained Problems
Zhuoqing Zheng, Tao Liu, Xuyang Wu

Abstract
Dual ascent (DA) and the method of multipliers (MM) are fundamental methods for solving linear equality-constrained convex optimization problems, and their dual updates can be viewed as the minimization of a proximal linear surrogate function of the negative Lagrange dual and augmented Lagrange dual function, respectively. However, the proximal linear surrogate function may suffer from low approximation accuracy, which leads to slow convergence of DA and MM. To accelerate their convergence, we adapt the proximal bundle surrogate framework that can incorporate a list of more accurate surrogate functions, to both the primal and the dual updates of DA and MM, leading to a family of novel primal-dual bundle methods. Our methods generalize the primal-dual gradient method, DA, the linearized MM, and MM. Under standard assumptions that allow for a broad range of surrogate functions, we prove theoretical convergence guarantees for the proposed methods. Numerical experiments demonstrate that our methods converge not only faster, but also significantly more robust with respect to the parameters compared to the primal-dual gradient method, DA, the linearized MM, and MM.



keywords
Bundle method, Primal-dual method, Linear equality-constrained problem, Model-based optimization

Our algorithms are motivated by the following facts: 1) DA and MM can be viewed as model-based methods with a proximal linear model (first-order Taylor expansion plus a proximal term) of the (augmented) Lagrange dual function; 2) a higher approximation accuracy of the model often leads to a faster convergence of the optimization methods

These models incorporate the problem parameters or the function value and gradient information at historical iterates and yield a higher approximation accuracy compared to those in most existing primal-dual methods. The main contributions are summarized as follows:

1)

We propose a family of novel primal-dual bundle methods, which adapt the proximal bundle model, typically used for unconstrained optimization, to DA and MM to accelerate their convergence.

2)

We theoretically analyse the convergence of our methods under standard assumptions that allow for a broad range of surrogate models.

3)

We demonstrate the practical advantages of our methods by numerical experiments. Compared to alternative methods, our methods converge not only faster, but also more robustly in the parameters.

An early effort with a similar idea is
(

In contrast, our methods handle general convex objective functions, apply the proximal bundle model to both the primal and dual updates, and consider both DA and MM.

Algorithm Development

2.1
Preliminaries: DA, MM, and the proximal bundle model

2.1.1
DA and MM

DA and MM are fundamental algorithms for solving linear equality-constrained problems, and several primal-dual methods can be viewed as their variants, such as the primal-dual gradient method
Alghunaim and
Sayed 2020
, the linearized method of multipliers (linearized MM)
Xu 2021
, and the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM)
Boyd et al. 2011
. DA and MM for solving problem (
1
) take the following forms.

•

Dual Ascent (DA): At each iteration
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle x^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\underset{x}{\operatorname{\arg\;\min}}~L(x,v^{k})

(2)

\displaystyle v^{k+1}

\displaystyle=v^{k}+\alpha(Ax^{k+1}-b)

(3)

where
L(x,v)=F(x)+\langle v,Ax-b\rangle
and the step-size
\alpha>0
. DA applies steepest descent
Boyd and Vandenberghe 2004
to minimizing the negative dual function and requires the strong convexity of
F
to guarantee the convergence.

•

Method of Multipliers (MM): For some
\rho>0
and at each iteration
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle x^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\underset{x}{\operatorname{\arg\;\min}}~L_{\rho}(x,v^{k})

(4)

\displaystyle v^{k+1}

\displaystyle=v^{k}+\rho(Ax^{k+1}-b)

(5)

where
L_{\rho}(x,v)=F(x)+\langle v,Ax-b\rangle+\frac{\rho}{2}\|Ax-b\|^{2}
. MM is equivalent to applying the proximal point method
Rockafellar 1976
to solving the dual problem of (
1
). Compared with DA, MM only
requires the objective function to be convex, rather than strongly convex, which significantly broadens the range of solvable problems.

Both DA and MM can be viewed from the perspective of model-based optimization. To see this, note that the update of DA and MM are equivalent to steepest descent on minimizing
-q(v)
and
-q_{\rho}(v)
, respectively, where

\displaystyle q(v)

\displaystyle=\min_{x}~L(x,v),\qquad q_{\rho}(v)=\min_{x}~L_{\rho}(x,v).

(6)

DA and MM can also be expressed as, for some
c_{d}>0
,

\displaystyle v^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\underset{v}{\operatorname{\arg\;\min}}-q(v^{k})-\langle\nabla q(v^{k}),v-v^{k}\rangle+\frac{c_{d}}{2}\|v-v^{k}\|^{2}

(7)

\displaystyle v^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\underset{v}{\operatorname{\arg\;\min}}-q_{\rho}(v^{k})-\langle\nabla q_{\rho}(v^{k}),v-v^{k}\rangle+\frac{c_{d}}{2}\|v-v^{k}\|^{2}

(8)

respectively, where the minimized proximal linear function is a surrogate function (
also called model
) of
q
in DA and
q_{\rho}
in MM. The proximal linear surrogate function has a simple form, but it can lead to low approximation accuracy, which further slows down the convergence of the algorithms.

2.1.2
The proximal bundle model

The proximal bundle model is adopted in bundle methods
Hiriart-Urruty and
Lemaréchal 1993
;
Lemaréchal 1974
;
Wolfe 2009
for solving unconstrained optimization problems. At each iteration
k\geq 0
, the proximal bundle model of a function
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
takes the form of

\hat{\phi}^{k}(x)+\frac{c}{2}\|x-x^{k}\|^{2}

(9)

where
\hat{\phi}^{k}
is an approximation (often a minorant) of
\phi
,
c\geq 0
is a penalty parameter, and
x^{k}
is the iterate at the
k
th iteration.

The proximal bundle model is explicitly or implicitly employed in many optimization methods. For example, when minimizing a function
\phi
, steepest descent and the proximal point method correspond to (
9
) with
\hat{\phi}^{k}(x)=\phi(x^{k})+\langle\nabla\phi(x^{k}),x-x^{k}\rangle
and
\hat{\phi}^{k}(x)=\phi(x)
, respectively, the proximal bundle method in
Hiriart-Urruty and
Lemaréchal 1993
corresponds to (
9
) with the cutting-plane model
Kelley 1960
where

\hat{\phi}^{k}(x)=\max_{t\in S^{k}}~\phi(x^{t})+\langle\nabla\phi(x^{t}),x-x^{t}\rangle

for an index set
S^{k}\subseteq\{0,1,\ldots,k\}
. Other variants of bundle methods include the trust region-based bundle method and the relaxation-based bundle method
(
Hiriart-Urruty and
Lemaréchal 1993
, Sec. XV.)
, which incorporates the cutting-plane model with the trust-region technique and a relaxation technique, respectively. The use of multiple cutting planes often yields a high approximation accuracy of
\hat{\phi}^{k}
on
\phi
, and the resulting proximal bundle method not only converges fast but also exhibits extraordinary robustness in the parameter
c
. Due to this reason, a growing body of works extends the bundle method to solve optimization problems with different structures. For example,
Cederberg et al. 2025
;
Fischer 2025
propose asynchronous bundle methods to solve distributed optimization problems,
Liang et al. 2024
develops a stochastic
composite proximal bundle method to solve stochastic optimization with composite objective functions, and
Hare and
Sagastizábal 2010
;
de Oliveira 2019
apply the bundle method to solve non-convex problems with certain structures.

2.2
Algorithm

Inspired by the generality, quick convergence, and robustness of optimization methods with the proximal bundle model (
9
), we adapt it to the primal and dual updates in DA and MM, respectively.

•

Dual bundle update
: By (
7
)–(
8

\displaystyle v^{k+1}=\underset{v}{\operatorname{\arg\;\min}}-\hat{q}^{k}(v)+\frac{c_{d}}{2}\|v-v^{k}\|^{2}

(10)

\displaystyle v^{k+1}=\underset{v}{\operatorname{\arg\;\min}}-\hat{q}_{\rho}^{k}(v)+\frac{c_{d}}{2}\|v-v^{k}\|^{2}

(11)

\hat{q}^{k}
and

•

Primal bundle update
: The models

\displaystyle x^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\underset{x}{\operatorname{\arg\;\min}}~\hat{F}^{k}(x)+\frac{c_{p}}{2}\|x-x^{k}\|^{2}+\langle v^{k},Ax-b\rangle

(12)

\displaystyle x^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\underset{x}{\operatorname{\arg\;\min}}~\hat{F}^{k}(x)+\frac{c_{p}}{2}\|x-x^{k}\|^{2}+\langle v^{k},Ax-b\rangle+\frac{\rho}{2}\|Ax-b\|^{2}

(13)

where

We refer to the algorithm with the primal update (

\hat{q}^{k}(v)=F(x^{k+1})+\langle Ax^{k+1}-b,v\rangle

Sayed 2020

\hat{F}^{k}(x)=f(x^{k})+\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),x-x^{k}\rangle

\hat{q}_{\rho}^{k}(v)=F(x^{k+1})+\langle Ax^{k+1}-b,v\rangle+\frac{\rho}{2}\|Ax^{k+1}-b\|^{2}

;
Xu 2021

By letting the primal and dual models take specific forms in BDA and BMM, we obtain several variants. For BDA, we have

\begin{cases}\textbf{BDA-P:}\qquad&\text{primal update \eqref{eq:BDA-x} in BDA~}~+~\text{dual update \eqref{eq:DA_v} in DA}\\
\textbf{BDA-D:}\qquad&\text{primal update \eqref{eq:DA_x} in DA~}~+~\text{dual update \eqref{eq:BDA-D} in BDA}\end{cases}

\begin{cases}\textbf{BMM-P:}\qquad&\text{primal update \eqref{eq:BMM-x} in BMM~}~+~\text{dual update \eqref{eq:MM_v} in MM}\\
\textbf{BMM-D:}\qquad&\text{primal update \eqref{eq:MM_x} in MM}~+~\text{dual update \eqref{eq:BMM-D} in BMM.}\end{cases}

Specifically, the -P methods adopt the same primal update as BDA and BMM and the same dual updates as DA and MM. The -D methods take the same primal updates as DA and MM, and the same dual update as BDA and BMM.

2.2.1
Candidates of the primal model and the dual model

We provide several choices for the primal and dual models in BDA and BMM. For the primal objective function
F
, we consider the primal model of the form
\hat{F}^{k}(x)=\hat{f}^{k}(x)+h(x)
, where
\hat{f}^{k}
is a surrogate model of
f
and a natural choice is
\hat{f}^{k}=f
. Below, we first provide four models for
f
, and then adapt them to the dual functions
q
and
q_{\rho}
.

Primal models for
f
. We focus on convex models that take the maximum of affine functions, where each affine function is a minorant of
f
. Consequently, these models are also minorants of
f
. We visualize three of these models in Fig.
1
.

•

Polyak model: The model originates from the Polyak step-size
Polyak 1987
for steepest descent, which takes the following form when minimizing a function
f
:

x^{k+1}=x^{k}-\alpha^{k}\nabla f(x^{k}),\qquad\alpha^{k}=\frac{f(x^{k})-\ell_{f}}{\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|^{2}}

where
\ell_{f}
is a lower bound of
\min_{x}f(x)
. In the update,
x^{k+1}
is a minimizer of

\hat{f}^{k}(x)=\max\{f(x^{k})+\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),x-x^{k}\rangle,\ell_{f}\}

(14)

which is referred to as the Polyak model of
f
.

•

Cutting-plane model: It takes the maximum of historical cutting planes
Kelley 1960
:

\hat{f}^{k}(x)=\max_{t\in S^{k}}~f(x^{t})+\langle\nabla f(x^{t}),x-x^{t}\rangle

(15)

where
S^{k}\subseteq\{0,1,\ldots,k\}
is a subset of historical iteration indexes.

•

Polyak cutting-plane model: The model is designed as the maximum of the Polyak model and the cutting-plane model:

\hat{f}^{k}(x)=\max\{\ell_{f},\max_{t\in S^{k}}~f(x^{t})+\langle\nabla f(x^{t}),x-x^{t}\rangle\}.

(16)

•

Two-cut model: It takes the maximum of the cutting planes of
f
and
\hat{f}^{k-1}
at
x^{k}
:

\hat{f}^{k}(x)=\max\{\hat{f}^{k-1}(x^{k})+\langle\hat{g}^{k},x-x^{k}\rangle,~f(x^{k})+\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),x-x^{k}\rangle\}

(17)

where
\hat{g}^{k}\in\partial\hat{f}^{k-1}(x^{k})
and
\hat{f}^{0}(x)=f(x^{0})+\langle\nabla f(x^{0}),x-x^{0}\rangle
.

Remark 1
.

All four models (
14
)–(
17
) take the maximum of minorants of
f
and they are all majorants of the linear model
f(x^{k})+\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),x-x^{k}\rangle
. Therefore, they achieve higher accuracy in approximating
f
compared to the linear model

f(x)-\hat{f}^{k}\leq f(x)-(f(x^{k})+\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),x-x^{k}\rangle).

Similarly, the Polyak cutting-plane model yields higher approximation accuracy than the Polyak model and the cutting-plane model (see Fig.
1
).

Dual model for
q
.
Adapting the four models to
q
requires both the function value and the gradient of

at
v^{k}
, which requires solving (
2
) exactly. However, the primal update (
12
) only offers an approximate solution. To address this issue, we propose to approximate

and its gradient using

generated by the primal update of BDA. When
x^{k+1}\in\operatorname{\arg\;\min}_{x}~L(x,v^{k})
, it follows that

q(v^{k})=L(x^{k+1},v^{k}),\qquad\nabla q(v^{k})=\nabla_{v}L(x^{k+1},v^{k})

(18)

and the cutting plane of

at

is

L(x^{k+1},v^{k})+\langle\nabla_{v}L(x^{k+1},v^{k}),v-v^{k}\rangle.

Inspired by this, we construct the following approximate cutting plane of

v^{k}
:

C_{q}^{k}(v)=L(x^{k+1},v^{k})+\langle\nabla_{v}L(x^{k+1},v^{k}),v-v^{k}\rangle

(19)

where
x^{k+1}
is computed from (
12
). Let

be a known upper bound of

. Incorporating (

) into the four models (

)–(

), we obtain

\hat{q}^{k}(v)=\begin{cases}\min\{C_{q}^{k}(v),u_{q}\},&\text{Polyak model}\\
\min_{t\in S^{k}}~C_{q}^{t}(v),&\text{Cutting-plane model}\\
\min\{u_{q},\min_{t\in S^{k}}~C_{q}^{t}(v)\},&\text{Polyak cutting-plane model}\\
\min\{\hat{q}^{k-1}(v^{k})+\langle\hat{g}_{q}^{k},v-v^{k}\rangle,C_{q}^{k}(v)\},&\text{Two-cut model}\end{cases}

(20)

where
-\hat{g}_{q}^{k}\in\partial(-\hat{q}^{k-1})(v^{k})
and
\hat{q}^{0}(v)=C_{q}^{0}(v)
in the two-cut model.

Dual model for
q_{\rho}
. We adapt the four models (
14
)–(
17
) to the augmented Lagrange dual function

in the same way of adapting them to

. Similar to (

), we construct the following approximate cutting-plane of
q_{\rho}
at

using

:

C_{q,\rho}^{k}(v)=L_{\rho}(x^{k+1},v^{k})+\langle\nabla_{v}L_{\rho}(x^{k+1},v^{k}),v-v^{k}\rangle

(21)

where
L_{\rho}
is the augmented Lagrangian. Let
u_{q,\rho}
be a known upper bound of
\max_{v}q_{\rho}(v)
. Incorporating (
21
) into the four models (
14
)–(
17
), we obtain

\hat{q}_{\rho}^{k}(v)=\begin{cases}\min\{C_{q,\rho}^{k}(v),u_{q,\rho}\},&\text{Polyak model}\\
\min_{t\in S^{k}}~C_{q,\rho}^{t}(v),&\text{Cutting-plane model}\\
\min\{u_{q,\rho},\min_{t\in S^{k}}~C_{q,\rho}^{t}(v)\},&\text{Polyak cutting-plane model}\\
\min\{\hat{q}_{\rho}^{k-1}(v^{k})+\langle\hat{g}_{q_{\rho}}^{k},v-v^{k}\rangle,C_{q,\rho}^{k}(v)\},&\text{Two-cut model}\end{cases}

(22)

where

\hat{q}_{\rho}^{0}(v)=C_{q,\rho}^{0}(v)

2.2.2
Solving the subproblems in BDA and BMM

When using the models in Section
2.2.1
, the subproblems in BDA and BMM can be solved efficiently. To see this, note that with the models in Section
2.2.1
, the subproblems in the primal updates of BDA and BMM can be simplified as

\underset{x}{\operatorname{minimize}}~\max_{i\in\{1,\ldots,M\}}\{\tilde{a}_{i}^{T}x+\tilde{b}_{i}\}+\frac{x^{T}Cx}{2}+d^{T}x+h(x)

(23)

where
M
is the number of affine functions in
\hat{f}^{k}
. Take cutting-plane model (
15
) for example,

\displaystyle\tilde{a}_{i}=\nabla f(x^{i}),~~~~~\tilde{b}_{i}=f(x^{i})-\langle\nabla f(x^{i}),x^{i}\rangle

\displaystyle d=A^{T}v^{k}-c_{p}x^{k},~~C=c_{p}I~~~~\text{for BDA}

\displaystyle d=A^{T}v^{k}-c_{p}x^{k}-\rho A^{T}b,~~C=c_{p}I+\rho A^{T}A~~~~\text{for BMM}.

Problem (
23
) can be equivalently transformed into a linearly constrained problem

\begin{split}\underset{x,t}{\operatorname{minimize}}~~&~t+\frac{x^{T}Cx}{2}+d^{T}x+h(x)\\
\operatorname{subject~to}~&~\tilde{a}_{i}^{T}x+\tilde{b}_{i}\leq t,~i\in\{1,\ldots,M\}\end{split}

(24)

which reduces to an easy-to-solve convex quadratic program when
h\equiv 0
.

For problem (
24
), it is preferable to solve it by dual approaches since the dimension
M
of the dual variable is usually small (e.g.,
M=5,10
) in practical implementations. To formulate the dual problem, we define

\tilde{A}=(\tilde{a}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{a}_{m})^{T},\quad\tilde{b}=(\tilde{b}_{1};\ldots;\tilde{b}_{m})

and rewrite the constraint in (
24
) as

\tilde{A}x+\tilde{b}\leq t\mathbf{1}.

Lemma 2.1
.

The Lagrange dual of problem (
24
) is

\begin{split}\underset{\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}^{M}}{\operatorname{maximize}}~~~&g(\lambda)\\
\operatorname{subject~to}~~&\mathbf{1}^{T}\lambda=1,~\lambda\geq 0,\end{split}

(25)

where

\begin{split}g(\lambda)=&\inf_{x}\left\{h(x)+\frac{1}{2}x^{T}Cx+d^{T}x+\lambda^{T}\tilde{A}x\right\}+\tilde{b}^{T}\lambda\end{split}

is the dual function. Letting
x_{\lambda}=\operatorname{\arg\;\min}_{x}h(x)+\frac{1}{2}x^{T}Cx+d^{T}x+\lambda^{T}\tilde{A}x
, it holds that

\nabla g(\lambda)=\tilde{A}x_{\lambda}+b.

Furthermore, if
\lambda^{\star}
is optimal to (
25
), then the unique optimal solution of problem (
23
) is
x_{\lambda^{\star}}
.

Proof.

See Appendix
6.1
.
∎

Since
C
is positive definite, the dual function
g
is smooth, and we can solve problem (
25
) by dual gradient-type methods such as FISTA
Beck and Teboulle 2009
, which typically only takes a few iterations to solve problems with low variable dimension. In the implementation of FISTA, the gradient
\nabla g(\lambda)=\tilde{A}x_{\lambda}+b
and the projection onto the constraint set (simplex) of (
25
) can be implemented in the complexity of
O(M)

Condat 2016
. For the subproblem in the primal update (
12
) of BDA,
C=c_{p}I
and

x_{\lambda}=\mathbf{prox}_{h/c_{p}}(-(d+\tilde{A}^{T}\lambda)/c_{p})+\tilde{b},

which is easy to solve if
h=\|\cdot\|_{1}
or
h
is the indicator function of an easy-to-project constraint set. Specifically, when
h=\|\cdot\|_{1}
,
n=100,000
, and
M=15
, applying FISTA to solve (
25
) only takes
19\sim 40
iterations and
0.1\sim 0.2
second in a PC with the Apple M3 8-core CPU. For the subproblem in the primal update (
13
),
C=c_{p}I+\rho A^{T}A
and the computation of
x_{\lambda}
requires the minimization of a strongly convex objective function.

The subproblems in the dual updates (
10
) and (
11
) of BDA and BMM take similar forms as (
23
) with
x
replaced with
v
,
C=c_{d}I
, and
h\equiv 0
, and can be rewritten as a quadratic program following the reformulation procedure from (
23
) to (
24
). The resulting quadratic program can be solved by either the primal or the dual approaches efficiently due to its simple form.

5
Conclusion

We develop BDA and BMM, which adapt the general bundle model to both the primal and dual updates in DA and MM, and improve the performance of algorithms through the high approximation accuracy of the bundle model. BDA and BMM generalize DA, the primal-dual gradient method, MM, and linearized MM. Moreover, the theoretical convergence
results of BDA and BMM are no worse than and sometimes even better
than those of such existing specializations of them in terms
of rate order, problem assumption, etc. The competitive performance of the proposed methods (faster convergence and higher robustness with respect to step-size parameters) over the primal-dual gradient method, DA, the linearized MM, and MM is demonstrated by numerical experiments.

Using telescoping cancellation on the above equation yields that for all

From (

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2\beta}\|\nabla f(x^{k+2})-\nabla f(x^{\star})\|^{2}+\frac{\rho}{2}\|Ax^{k+2}-b\|^{2}\\

\begin{split}\frac{c_{d}}{2}\|v^{k+1}-v^{\star}\|^{2}=&\frac{c_{d}}{2}\|(A^{T})^{\dagger}(\nabla f(x^{\star})-\nabla f(x^{k+2}))-\rho(Ax^{k+2}-b)\|^{2}\\

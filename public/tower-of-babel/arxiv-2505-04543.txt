Introduction

For a positive integer
k
, let us denote
[k]\coloneqq\{1,\dots,k\}
the set of the first
k
positive integers. A
k
-colouring
of a graph
G
is an assignment
\sigma\colon V(G)\rightarrow[k]

\sigma
of

proper
if
\sigma(u)\neq\sigma(v)
for every edge
uv\in E(G)
. Given a colouring
\sigma
and a vertex

, a

is a neighbour

such that the colour
\sigma(u)
appears exactly once in

; we say that
\sigma(u)
is a
solitary colour
of
v
in
\sigma

The colouring
\sigma
is
proper conflict-free
(or simply
pcf

has a witness. We let
\chi_{\rm pcf}(G)
be the smallest integer
k
such that a pcf
k
-colouring of

exists.
The notion of pcf colouring was introduced by Fabrici
et al.

[
8
]
, and was first investigated in the class of graphs of maximum degree
\Delta
by Caro, Petruševski and Škrekovski
[
3
]
. Among other results, they obtained the upper bound
\chi_{\rm pcf}(G)\leq 5\Delta(G)/2
, and conjectured that the optimal upper bound should actually be
\Delta(G)+1
.

Conjecture 1

(Caro, Petruševski, Škrekovski
[
3
, Conjecture 6.4]
)
.

If
G
is a connected graph of maximum degree
\Delta\geq 3
, then
\chi_{\rm pcf}(G)\leq\Delta+1
.

The condition
\Delta\geq 3
is needed because the conjectured bound does not hold for cycles. Indeed
\chi_{\rm pcf}(C_{5})=5=\Delta(C_{5})+3
, and for every other cycle
C
whose length is not a multiple of
3
,
\chi_{\rm pcf}(C)=4=\Delta(C)+2
. The condition that
G
is connected ensures that there is no isolated vertex.
The conjectured upper bound is best possible. For instance, if
G
is a graph obtained by subdividing once any subset of edges from the complete graph

, then
\chi_{\rm pcf}(G)=n=\Delta(G)+1
, while
\chi(G)=2
. In particular, when
G
is obtained by subdividing once every edge of
K_{n}
, the difference between
\chi(G)
and
\chi_{\rm pcf}(G)
is unbounded.

As a first step towards resolving
Conjecture

1

[
6
]
observed that a simple greedy algorithm provides a pcf
(2\Delta+1)
-colouring of any graph

\Delta

\chi_{\rm pcf}(G)<1.656\Delta

Theorem 2
.

Let
G
a graph of maximum degree
\Delta
, then

\chi_{\rm pcf}(G)\leq\Delta+\mathcal{O}\left(\log\Delta\right).

Proper conflict-free colourings can be seen as a relaxation of distance-
2

proper
h
-conflict-free

-colourings

h
-pcf

-colourings where every vertex

\min\,\{h,\deg(v)\}

\Delta

-pcf

is simply a pcf

-colouring of

-pcf

k
-colouring of

For every

\chi_{\rm pcf}^{h}(G)
the minimum

-pcf

-colouring of

exists. A simple greedy algorithm, inspired by the observation of Cranston and Liu

Proposition 3
.

Let
G
be a graph of maximum degree
\Delta
, and

an integer. Then

\chi_{\rm pcf}^{h}(G)\leq(h+1)\Delta+1.

Proof.

Fix any linear ordering

of

, let

be the first
\min\,\{h,\deg(v)\}
neighbours of

in the ordering. For

to

with the first colour which does not appear in

, nor in

be the colouring obtained at the end of this procedure. One easily checks that

, the vertices of

With the additional assumption that

Theorem 4

Theorem 5

Let

The notion of pcf colourings can also be seen as a strengthening of

Proposition 6

(Dai, Ouyang, Pirot, 2024)
.

Let

be a fixed integer. For infinitely many values of
\Delta
, there exists a graph

\chi_{o}^{h}(G)\geq h\Delta+1.

In turn,

.

Conjecture 7
.

Let
h\geq 1
be an integer, and
G
be a graph of maximum degree
\Delta
sufficiently large. Then

\chi_{\rm pcf}^{h}(G)\leq h\Delta+1.

The lower bound provided by

In particular, Liu and Reed

Theorem 8

(Liu, Reed, 2024+)

For every graph

\Delta

The condition on the minimum degree in

This has been considered by Kamyczura and Przybyło

(Kamyczura, Przybyło, 2024)

Let

The condition on

When

Probabilistic tools

Let
B(n,p)
denote a random variable that follows the binomial distribution of parameters
n
and
p
. We will rely on the following form of Chernoff’s bounds that in particular provides large concentration inequalities for binomial random variables.

Lemma 12

(Chernoff’s bounds)
.

Let
\mathbf{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{X}_{n}
be i.i.d.
(0,1)
-valued random variables, and let
\mathbf{S}_{n}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{X}_{i}
. Let us write
\mu\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{S}_{n}\right]
. Then

(i)

for every
0<\delta<1
,

\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbf{S}_{n}\leq(1-\delta)\mu\right]\leq e^{-\delta^{2}\mu/2};

(ii)

for every
\delta>0
,

\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbf{S}_{n}\geq(1+\delta)\mu\right]\leq e^{-\delta^{2}\mu/(2+\delta)}.

In our proofs, we shall mostly use Chernoff’s bounds in the following setting. Those respectively follow from
Lemma

12
(i)
and
(ii)
with
\delta\coloneqq 1/2
and
\delta\coloneqq 1/4
.

Lemma 13
.

Let
n\geq 1
and
p\in(0,1)
. Then

(i)

\mathbb{P}\left[B(n,p)\leq\frac{1}{2}np\right]\leq e^{-np/8}
,

(ii)

\mathbb{P}\left[B(n,p)\geq\frac{5}{4}np\right]\leq e^{-np/36}
.

If
p=\mathcal{O}\left(1/n\right)
, Chernoff’s bounds are not precise enough to obtain tight tail bounds on
B(n,p)
. Instead, we shall rely on the following well-known estimate that follows from a simple union bound (we include the proof for completeness).

Lemma 14
.

Let
0\leq t\leq n
be an integer. Then
\mathbb{P}\left[B(n,p)\geq t\right]\leq\left(enp/t\right)^{t}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{Y}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{X}_{i}
, where the variables
\{\mathbf{X}_{i}\}
are independent and follow a Bernoulli distribution of parameter
p
. Then
\mathbf{Y}\sim B(n,p)
, and
\mathbf{Y}\geq t
iff there exists a
t
-set
I\in\binom{[n]}{t}
such that
\mathbf{X}_{i}=1
for every
i\in I
. For a fixed
I
, this happens with probability
p^{t}
, so using a union bound we obtain

\mathbb{P}\left[B(n,p)\geq t\right]=\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbf{Y}\geq t\right]\leq\binom{n}{t}p^{t}\leq\left(\frac{enp}{t}\right)^{t}.\qed

Suppose we have binary random variables
\mathbf{X}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{X}_{n}
such that
\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbf{X}_{i}=1\right]\geq p
regardless of the realisation of
\mathbf{X}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{X}_{i-1}
, for all
i\in[n]
. These variables are not necessarily independent, but we expect
\sum\mathbf{X}_{i}
to be at least as large as a binomial variable of parameters
n,p
. We formalise this idea in
Lemma

15
.

Lemma 15
.

Let
\mathbf{X}=(\mathbf{X}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{X}_{n})
be a sequence of binary random variables such that

(
\star
)

\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbf{X}_{i}=1\;\middle|\;\mathbf{X}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{X}_{i-1}\right]\geq p

for all
i\in[n]
. Then
\mathbb{P}\left[\sum\mathbf{X}_{i}\leq t\right]\leq\mathbb{P}\left[B(n,p)\leq t\right]
.

Proof.

We shall use a classical coupling argument. Given a sequence
A=(A_{1},\dots,A_{n})
and an integer
i\in[n]
, we define
A_{<i}\coloneqq(A_{1},\dots,A_{i-1})
. Let
\mathbf{U}=(\mathbf{U}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{U}_{n})
be a sequence of independent continuous uniform variables in the interval
[0,1]
. We define a sequence of binary random variables
\mathbf{X}^{\prime}=(\mathbf{X}_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\mathbf{X}_{n}^{\prime})
on the probability space of
\mathbf{U}
. For
x\in\{0,1\}^{n}
and
i\in[n]
, if
\mathbf{X}^{\prime}_{<i}=x_{<i}
, let

\mathbf{X}_{i}^{\prime}\coloneqq\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if $\mathbf{U}_{i}\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbf{X}_{i}=1\;\middle|\;\mathbf{X}_{<i}=x_{<i}\right]$;}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

We first show that
\mathbf{X}\sim\mathbf{X}^{\prime}
.
Let
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\{0,1\}^{n}
. Observe that, by construction,
\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbf{X}^{\prime}_{i}=x_{i}\;\middle|\;\mathbf{X}^{\prime}_{<i}=x_{<i}\right]=\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbf{X}_{i}=x_{i}\;\middle|\;\mathbf{X}_{<i}=x_{<i}\right]
for all
i\in[n]
. Using this observation and the chain rule, we deduce that

\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbf{X}=x\right]=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbf{X}_{i}=x_{i}\;\middle|\;\mathbf{X}_{<i}=x_{<i}\right]=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbf{X}^{\prime}_{i}=x_{i}\;\middle|\;\mathbf{X}^{\prime}_{<i}=x_{<i}\right]=\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbf{X}^{\prime}=x\right].

Therefore,
\mathbb{P}\left[\sum\mathbf{X}_{i}\leq t\right]=\mathbb{P}\left[\sum\mathbf{X}^{\prime}_{i}\leq t\right]
. We now define a second sequence
\mathbf{Y}=(\mathbf{Y}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{Y}_{n})
of binary random variables on the same probability space. For
i\in[n]
, let

\mathbf{Y}_{i}\,\,\coloneqq\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if $\mathbf{U}_{i}\leq p$;}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Clearly,
\mathbf{Y}
is a sequence of independent Bernoulli variables of parameter
p
, hence
\sum\mathbf{Y}_{i}\sim B(n,p)
. We easily check that
\mathbf{Y}_{i}=1
implies
\mathbf{X}_{i}^{\prime}=1
for all
i\in[n]
, thus,
\sum\mathbf{X}_{i}^{\prime}\geq\sum\mathbf{Y}_{i}
. It follows that

\mathbb{P}\left[\sum\mathbf{X}_{i}\leq t\right]=\mathbb{P}\left[\sum\mathbf{X}_{i}^{\prime}\leq t\right]\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\sum\mathbf{Y}_{i}\leq t\right].\qed

3.
A general upper bound for
\chi_{\rm pcf}^{h}

We recall that, in a proper colouring
\sigma
of a graph
G
, a solitary colour of a vertex
v\in V(G)
is a colour of
\sigma
having a unique occurrence in
N(v)
, and each
u\in N(v)
coloured with a solitary colour of
v
is called a witness of
v
.

3.1.
Overview of the proof.

We mix two techniques in order to ensure that the conflict-free constraints are all satisfied. Those that arise from small neighbourhoods can be dealt with greedily, while those that arise from large neighbourhoods can be dealt with randomly.

Therefore, we perform a two-step colouring of the graph
G
. First, we shall use a modified version of the greedy algorithm presented in the proof of
Proposition

3
to construct a proper precolouring
\sigma_{0}
of
G
such that all vertices of “small degree” have the required number of witnesses, while those of “large degree” may still miss one witness each. During the second step, we shall randomly recolour a fraction of the vertices with new colours in order to create one extra witness for each vertex of large degree.
For that, we use a Rödl Nibble approach that takes inspiration from the work of Kamyczura and Przybyło
[
10
]
, but incorporates new ideas to ensure that witnesses from the precolouring
\sigma_{0}
are preserved, and also relies on a different procedure which we believe is conceptually simpler to understand and analyse.

3.2.
The proof

Given a graph
G
and a real number
d\geq 0
, we recall that
V_{\leq d}\subseteq V(G)
denotes the set of vertices with degree at most
d
, and
V_{>d}\coloneqq V(G)\setminus V_{\leq d}
the set of vertices with degree greater than
d
.
Given a function
f\colon V(G)\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, a proper
k
-colouring
\sigma\colon V(G)\to[k]
is said to be
f
-conflict-free
if every vertex
v\in V(G)
has at least
\min\,\{f(v),\deg(v)\}
witnesses in
\sigma
.
In our work, we will exclusively rely on a specific kind of threshold function for

. Let
h\geq h_{0}\geq 0
be integers, and

; we recall that

\langle U\;?\;h:h_{0}\rangle\colon\left\{\begin{array}[]{r l}V(G)&\to\{h,h_{0}\}\\
v&\mapsto\begin{cases}h&\mbox{if $v\in U$;}\\
h_{0}&\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}\end{array}\right.

denotes the threshold function

.

By slightly modifying the greedy algorithm presented in the proof of

, we obtain a colouring which uses
\Delta
fewer colours, at the cost of granting one fewer witness for vertices of large degree.

Lemma 18
.

Let
h\geq 1
be an integer, and

a graph of maximum degree
\Delta
. Then there exists a proper
(h\Delta+1)
-colouring
\sigma_{0}
of

such that
\sigma_{0}
is
\langle V_{\leq\frac{h}{h+1}\Delta}\;?\;h:h-1\rangle
-conflict-free.

Proof.

Let
V^{+}\coloneqq V_{>\frac{h}{h+1}\Delta}
and
V^{-}\coloneqq V_{\leq\frac{h}{h+1}\Delta}
.

Fix a linear ordering
(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})
of
V
such that all vertices of
V^{-}
appear after those of
V^{+}
(e.g. order the vertices by decreasing degree in
G
). For a vertex

, let
\mathcal{W}(v)
be the first
\min\,\{h,\deg(v)\}

in the ordering, and for

\mathcal{W}(v)
be the first
\min\,\{h-1,\deg(v)\}

from

\mathcal{W}(u)
for all

. Let
\sigma_{0}
be the colouring obtained at the end of this procedure. One easily checks that

is proper. Furthermore, for all

are witnesses of

(the fact that

-conflict-free.

We now bound the number of colours used in

•

If

forbids at most

If

Therefore,

∎

Given a graph
G
of maximum degree
\Delta
, an integer

-colouring

of

Lemma 19

Let

be a graph of maximum degree
\Delta\geq 13000

(k+m)

\langle V_{\leq d}\;?\;h:h_{0}+\eta\rangle

Proof.

For
v\in V_{\leq d}
, let
\mathcal{W}(v)
be an arbitrary subset of
\min\,\{h,\deg(v)\}

in
\sigma_{0}

\sigma_{0}

independent binomial random subsets of

Initialisation: for

i\in[m]

do

A_{i}\leftarrow\emptyset

for

do

Add

independently with probability

i_{0}\leftarrow 1

for

S_{i}\leftarrow\{u\in V(G):\mathcal{N}(u)\cap A_{i}\neq\emptyset\}\ \cup\ \bigcup_{j=i_{0}}^{i-1}C_{j}

C_{i}\leftarrow A_{i}\setminus S_{i}
.

fail-safe:

if

such that
|N(v)\cap\bigcup_{j=i_{0}}^{i}C_{j}|>\frac{\deg(v)}{4}

For

\mathbf{A}_{i}
,
\mathbf{S}_{i}
,
\mathbf{C}_{i}
, and

,

and the integer

at the end of the procedure. Let

be the random colouring obtained from

The

Claim 20
.

For

,
\mathbb{P}\left[B_{v}\right]\leq\Delta^{-7}

Proof.

Let

where we have used

13
(ii)
together with the fact that

∎

For

Consider

4.
Bounding
\chi_{\rm pcf}^{h}
for graphs of constrained minimum degree

The first-order term
h\Delta
of the upper bound in
Theorem

5
is tight, by
Proposition

6
: the lower bound
h\Delta+1
is certified by a graph construction of minimum degree
h+1
. In fact, such graphs are highly constrained due to the requirement that neighbourhoods of size at most
h+1
must be rainbow in an
h
-pcf colouring. If the minimum degree
\delta
is sufficiently large with respect to
h
and
\Delta
, then one would expect more flexibility when dealing with small neighbourhoods.
Indeed the upper bound on
\chi_{\rm pcf}^{h}(G)
from
Theorem

9
established by Kamyczura and Przybyło in
[
10
]
shows that only a little more than
\Delta
colours are required for an
h
-pcf colouring in that case, with the extra assumption that
\delta
is sufficiently large with respect to
\Delta
.

We wish to drop the latter extra assumption, and consider a minimum degree constraint as weak as possible that still ensures that
\chi_{\rm pcf}^{h}
is
(1+o(1))\Delta
.
To do so, we again rely on a two-step colouring process, that takes advantage of having large minimum degree.
In the previous section, the first step described by
Lemma

18
provides exactly
h
witnesses for vertices of small degree; as a consequence, during the second step described in
Lemma

19
, we need to ensure that no witness created during the first step is destroyed, which is very costly.
In contrast, the extra minimum degree condition lets us replace the deterministic greedy first step with a random one that creates more witnesses for vertices of small degree, namely
2h
instead of
h
, so that during the second step we may destroy a fraction of those witnesses.
For that, we require that
\delta\geq 16h
, and in
Lemma

25
we show that a uniformly random proper colouring with sufficiently many colours satisfies the required conditions with non-zero probability.

The strategy for the second step is essentially the same as in Section
3
; we still rely on a Rödl Nibble approach to recolour a fraction of the vertices in such a way that new witnesses are created for large-degree vertices. The fact that we are allowed to destroy some witnesses from the precolouring makes this second step much cheaper than the one from the previous section. This is presented in
Lemma

27
.

Lemma 25
.

Let
h\geq 20\log\Delta
be an integer, and
G
be a graph of maximum degree
\Delta
and minimum degree
\delta\geq 16h
. Then, for every

with
\delta\leq d\leq\Delta

admits a proper
\langle V_{\leq d}\;?\;2h:0\rangle
-conflict-free
(\Delta+3d)
-colouring.

Proof.

Let
k\coloneqq\Delta+3d
, and let
\mathscr{C}_{k}(G)
be the set of all proper
k
-colourings of
G
.
Given a proper colouring
\sigma\in\mathscr{C}_{k}(G)
and a vertex
v\in V(G)
, we let
L_{\sigma}(v)\coloneqq[k]\setminus\sigma(N(v))
be the set of colours
x
such that, if we redefine
\sigma(v)\leftarrow x
, we still have
\sigma\in\mathscr{C}_{k}(G)
. We also denote
\sigma\setminus X
the restriction of
\sigma
to
V(G)\setminus X
, for every
X\subseteq V(G)
.

We fix some vertex
v\in V_{\leq d}
, and we write
N(v)\coloneqq\{u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{\deg(v)}\}
.
Let
{\bm{\sigma}}\in\mathscr{C}_{k}(G)
be drawn uniformly at random; we want to analyse the probability of the bad event
B_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(v)
that
v
has fewer than
2h
solitary colours in
{\bm{\sigma}}
.
To do that, we define the sequence of random colourings
{\bm{\sigma}}_{0},\ldots,{\bm{\sigma}}_{\deg(v)}\in\mathscr{C}_{k}(G)
where
{\bm{\sigma}}_{0}\coloneqq{\bm{\sigma}}
, and for each
i\geq 1
,
{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}
is obtained from

be resampling
{\bm{\sigma}}_{i-1}(u_{i})
uniformly at random from
L_{{\bm{\sigma}}_{i-1}}(u_{i})
.
This does not change the probability distribution of the random colourings; each
{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}
is uniformly distributed in
\mathscr{C}_{k}(G)
.

For every integer
0\leq i\leq\deg(v)
, let
\mathbf{S}_{i}
be the number of colours that appear exactly once on
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{i}\}
in
{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}
. Hence
\mathbf{S}_{\deg(v)}
is the number of witnesses of

in
{\bm{\sigma}}_{\deg(v)}
.
For every
i\in[\deg(v)]

\mathbf{X}_{i}\coloneqq\mathbf{S}_{i}-\mathbf{S}_{i-1}

\mathbf{X}_{i}=-1
if

is one of the colours counted by
\mathbf{S}_{i-1}
;
\mathbf{X}_{i}=1
if
{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}(u_{i})\notin{\bm{\sigma}}_{i-1}(\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{i-1}\})
; and
\mathbf{X}_{i}=0
otherwise.
Let
\sigma_{i-1}
be any possible realisation of
{\bm{\sigma}}_{i-1}
. Since there are always at least
3d
colours available when resampling the colour of

, and at most

, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbf{X}_{i}=1\mid{\bm{\sigma}}_{i-1}=\sigma_{i-1}\right]

\displaystyle\geq 2/3,\mbox{ and}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbf{X}_{i}=-1\mid{\bm{\sigma}}_{i-1}=\sigma_{i-1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 1/3.

We now condition on
{\bm{\sigma}}=\sigma_{0}
, and prove that under that condition
\mathbf{S}_{\deg(v)}\geq 2h
with large enough probability. Note that under that condition, the realisation of
{\bm{\sigma}}_{\deg(v)}\setminus N(v)

\sigma_{0}\setminus N(v)
.
Let
\{\mathbf{Y}_{i}\}
be a sequence of Bernoulli random variables such that

\mathbf{Y}_{i}=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if}~\mathbf{X}_{i}=1,\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Clearly for every
i\in[\deg(v)]
and every possible realisation
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{i-1})\in\{0,1\}^{i-1}
of
\{\mathbf{Y}_{i}\}

\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbf{Y}_{i}=1\mid\mathbf{Y}_{1}=y_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{Y}_{i-1}=y_{i-1}\right]\geq 2/3.

Hence by

15

\mathbb{P}\left[\sum\mathbf{Y}_{i}\leq t\right]\leq\mathbb{P}\left[B(\deg(v),2/3)\leq t\right]

. With an application of Chernoff’s bound, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbf{S}_{\deg(v)}<2h\mid{\bm{\sigma}}=\sigma_{0}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\sum\mathbf{Y}_{i}<\deg(v)/2+h\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left[B(\deg(v),2/3)<(1-5/32)\frac{2\deg(v)}{3}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\deg(v)}{123}\right)\leq\Delta^{-2.6}

\displaystyle\mbox{by \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{lem:chernoff}\ref{it:upper}}.

For a vertex
v\in V_{\leq d}
, the outcome of
B_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(v)
is entirely determined by the colours assigned to vertices in
N(v)
. So if we fix the realisation of
{\bm{\sigma}}
outside of
N(v)
, we in particular fix the outcomes of all events

such that
N(v)\cap N[u]=\emptyset
.
Hence we let
\Gamma(v)\coloneqq N[N(v)]\setminus\{v\}
, of size
|\Gamma(v)|\leq d\Delta
.
We let
\Sigma_{0}
be the set of possible realisations of
{{\bm{\sigma}}}_{\left|V(G)\setminus N(v)\right.}
.
With that definition of
\Gamma(v)
, for every

, we infer that

\mathbb{P}\left[B_{\bm{\sigma}}(v)\;\middle|\;\bigcap_{u\in Z}\overline{B_{\bm{\sigma}}(u)}\right]\leq\sup_{\sigma_{0}\in\Sigma_{0}}\mathbb{P}\left[B_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(v)\;\middle|\;{{\bm{\sigma}}}_{\left|V(G)\setminus N(v)\right.}=\sigma_{0}\right]\leq\Delta^{-2.6}\eqqcolon p.

Since
\Delta\geq\delta\geq 16h\geq 320\log\Delta
, we must have
\Delta>2500
. So we have
4p(d\Delta)\leq 4\Delta^{-0.6}<1
. We may therefore apply
Lemma

17
to conclude that, with non-zero probability, no event
B_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(v)
occurs.
∎

25

Corollary 26
.

For every integer

Lemma 27
.

\Delta\geq 20000

Then there exists a proper
(k+m)
-colouring

\langle V_{\leq d}\;?\;h_{0}/2:\eta\rangle

Proof.

For
v\in V_{\leq d}
, let
\mathcal{W}(v)
be an arbitrary subset of
h_{0}
witnesses of
v
in
\sigma_{0}
(here we use the fact that
\deg(v)\geq\delta\geq h_{0}
). We define
p\coloneqq\frac{1}{2\Delta}
. Since
\eta\leq d/100
, we easily verify that

(2)

2emp<0.6<e^{-1/2}.

We execute the following procedure, which is identical to the one in the proof of
Lemma

19
except for the fact that we no longer ensure that witnesses in
\sigma_{0}
remain witnesses in the final colouring.

Initialisation: for

i\leftarrow 1

to

m

do

A_{i}\leftarrow\emptyset

for

u\in V(G)

do

Add
u
to
A_{i}
independently with probability
p
.

i_{0}\leftarrow 1

for

i\in[m]

do

S_{i}\leftarrow\left\{u\in V(G)\,:\,N(u)\cap A_{i}\neq\emptyset\right\}\ \cup\ \bigcup_{j=i_{0}}^{i-1}C_{j}
;

C_{i}\leftarrow A_{i}\setminus S_{i}
.

fail-safe:

if

there exists
v\in V_{>d}
such that
|N(v)\cap\bigcup_{j=i_{0}}^{i}C_{j}|>\frac{\deg(v)}{2}

then

i_{0}\leftarrow i+1
.

For
i\in[m]
, we respectively let
\mathbf{A}_{i}
,
\mathbf{S}_{i}
,
\mathbf{C}_{i}
, and
\mathbf{i_{0}}
be the random values of the variables
A_{i}
,
S_{i}
,
C_{i}
, and
i_{0}
at the end of the procedure. Let
{\bm{\sigma}}
be the colouring obtained from
\sigma_{0}
after recolouring
\mathbf{C}_{i}
with the colour
k+i
for each
i\in\{\mathbf{i_{0}},\dots,m\}
. As explained in the proof of
Lemma

19
, the colouring
{\bm{\sigma}}
is deterministically proper. For
v\in V_{>d}
, let
B_{v}
be the (bad) event that more than
\deg(v)/2
neighbours of
v
belong in
\mathbf{A}_{1}\cup\dots\cup\mathbf{A}_{m}
.

Claim 28
.

For
v\in V_{>d}
,
\mathbb{P}\left[B_{v}\right]\leq\Delta^{-5}.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{Z}_{v}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\mathbf{A}_{i}\cap N(v)|\sim B(m\deg(v),p)
. By

14
, we obtain

\mathbb{P}\left[B_{v}\right]\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbf{Z}_{v}\geq\frac{\deg(v)}{2}\right]\;\leq\;(2emp)^{\deg(v)/2}<\Delta^{-5},

where we have used

2

While we do not ensure deterministically that vertices of

in
{\bm{\sigma}}

Fact 29
.

Let

. Then

is a set of witnesses of
v
in
\sigma
.

For
v\in V_{\leq d}
, let

\mathbf{A}_{1}\cup\dots\cup\mathbf{A}_{m}
. Hence, if

witnesses in

.

Claim 30
.

Let
v\in V_{\leq d}
. Then
\mathbb{P}\left[E_{v}^{-}\right]\leq\Delta^{-5}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{Y}_{v}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\mathcal{W}(v)\cap A_{i}|\sim B(mh_{0},p)
. By

14
, we obtain

\mathbb{P}\left[E_{v}^{-}\right]\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbf{Y}_{v}\geq h_{0}/2\right]\leq\left(2emp\right)^{h_{0}/2}<\Delta^{-5},

where we have used
Equation

2
and the assumption

.
∎

For
v\in V_{>d}
, let
\mathbf{X}_{v}^{i}
be the indicator variable for the event “

”. The following fact is easily deduced from the disjointness of the sets

Fact 31

Let

. At the end of the procedure,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=\mathbf{i_{0}}}^{m}\mathbf{X}_{v}^{i}

{\bm{\sigma}}
.

For every

\eta

be the (bad) event that

.

Claim 32
.

Let
v\in V_{>d}
. Then
\mathbb{P}\left[E_{v}\right]\leq\Delta^{-5}
.

Proof.

Consider
i\in[m]
. Let us fix the realisation
\mathbf{A}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{A}_{i-1}
and the resulting value of
\mathbf{C}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{C}_{i-1}

be the resulting value of the term “

. The distribution of
\mathbf{A}_{i}

independently with probability

5.
Discussion

5.1.
Tightness of the bounds

As discussed in the introduction, the bound
\Delta+\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{h\Delta})
is unlikely to be tight. When
h=1
, we have a bound of the form
\Delta+O(\log\Delta)
, while when
h=2
we have no better than
\Delta+\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\Delta\log\Delta})
with minimum degree
\Omega(\log\Delta)
. We expect the transition to be smoother between
h=1
and larger values for
h
, as is suggested by
Conjecture

11
. At the other end of the spectrum, when
h
gets close to
\delta
, it has been established in
[
7
]
that a linear upper bound in terms of
\Delta
is no longer possible, at least for regular graphs. This was formulated for
\chi_{o}^{h}(G)
, which we recall is a lower bound on
\chi_{\rm pcf}^{h}(G)
.

Proposition 34

(Dai, Ouyang, Pirot, 2024)
.

For every even integer
\Delta\geq 2
and
1\leq t\Delta
, there is a
\Delta
-regular graph
G
such that, letting
h\coloneqq\Delta+1-t
, one has

\chi_{o}^{h}(G)>\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Delta^{2}}{t+1}.

The authors have also established a matching upper bound for
\chi_{o}^{h}(G)
in that regime.

Theorem 35

(Dai, Ouyang, Pirot, 2024)
.

Let
G
be a
\Delta
-regular graph, let
\left\lceil 2(\ln\Delta+\ln\ln\Delta+3)\right\rceil\leq t\leq\Delta
be a given integer, and let
h\coloneqq\Delta+1-t
. Then

\chi_{o}^{h}(G)=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\Delta^{2}}{t}\right).

One could wonder whether a similar upper bound holds for
\chi_{\rm pcf}^{h}(G)
in that same regime. This is indeed the case, as it turns out that, when
h
is sufficiently close to
\Delta
, then
\chi_{o}^{h}(G)
and
\chi_{\rm pcf}^{h}(G)
have a similar behaviour. This follows from the following observation.

Proposition 36
.

For every graph
G
of maximum degree
\Delta
, and every integer
t<\frac{2\Delta}{3}
,

\chi_{\rm pcf}^{\Delta-3t/2}(G)\leq\chi_{o}^{\Delta-t}(G).

Proof.

Let
h\coloneqq\Delta-t
, and let
\sigma
be any
h
-odd colouring of
G
.
Let
v\in V(G)
; we say that
c\in\sigma(N(v))
is an
odd colour of
v
in
\sigma
if it has an odd number of occurrences in
N(v)
.
Let
x\geq 0
be the number of odd colours of
v
that have
>1
occurrences in
N(v)
. There can be at most
\Delta-3x
other (odd) colours in
N(v)
, hence
h\leq\Delta-2x
.
We conclude that
x\leq\frac{\Delta-h}{2}=t/2
, and so
v
has at least
\Delta-3t/2
solitary colours in
\sigma
. This holds for every
v\in V(G)
, so
\sigma
is
(\Delta-3t/2)
-conflict-free.
∎

5.2.
Algorithmic aspects

We note that the proof of
Lemma

18
is constructive with the description of a greedy algorithm that returns the desired solution.
While the statements of
Lemma

19
and
Lemma

27
are purely existential, the only non-constructive part in their respective proofs is the application of
Lemma

16
. If one replaces the latter with the following algorithmic version of the Lovász Local Lemma due to Moser and Tardos
[
13
]
, then both these results can be obtained constructively with a randomised algorithm of expected polynomial time.

Lemma 37

(Algorithmic Lovász Local Lemma)
.

Let
\mathcal{B}=\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{n}\}
be a finite set of random (bad) events that are all determined by a finite set of random independent variables
\mathcal{X}
, and all of which happen with probability at most
p
. Suppose that, for every
i\in[n]
,
B_{i}
is independent from all but at most
d
other events.

If
epd\leq 1
, then there exists a valuation of the variables in
\mathcal{X}
such that no bad event in
\mathcal{B}
happens, and such a valuation can be constructed with a randomised algorithm of expected runtime polynomial in
n
and
|\mathcal{X}|
.

Indeed, in the proofs of
Lemma

19
and
Lemma

27
, the bad events are all determined by the set of independent random variables
\mathcal{X}\coloneqq\{\mathds{1}_{v\in A_{i}}:i\in[m],v\in V(G)\}
.
We conclude that one can obtain the colouring promised by
Theorem

5
in expected polynomial time.

In contrast, the proof of
Lemma

25
relies on the lopsided version of LLL (
Lemma

17
) which seems to be much harder to prove constructively (see
[
9
]
for a partial result in that direction), and more crucially on a much more complex random object, namely a uniformly random proper colouring
{\bm{\sigma}}
of the graph
G
. Sampling
{\bm{\sigma}}
— or a good enough approximation thereof — in polynomial time is by itself a notoriously hard open problem. If one wishes to make that proof constructive, the most promising strategy in our opinion would be to apply directly the Entropy Compression Method used by Moser and Tardos in their proof of
Lemma

37
on the following random algorithm, which we conjecture should return the desired colouring in expected polynomial time.

Fix an arbitrary ordering on
V(G)

k\leftarrow\Delta+3d

\sigma\leftarrow
any proper
k
-colouring of
G
(constructed greedily)

while

there is a vertex
v
of degree at most
d
with less than
2h
witness in
\sigma

do

foreach

colour class
C
in
N(v)

do

foreach

u\in C\setminus\min C

do

\sigma(u)\leftarrow
uniformly random colour from
[k]\setminus\sigma(N(u))
.

return

\sigma

Since the algorithmic aspects of our proofs are not the focus of the paper, we leave that for future work.

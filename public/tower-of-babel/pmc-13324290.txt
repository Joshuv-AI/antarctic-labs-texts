Research on adaptive cascade control switching method of flapping-wing aircraft based on disturbance quantization parameter k and PID architecture
Authors: Zi Han Gao, Xie He, Hong Mei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAiming at the control problems of strong nonlinearity, multi-channel coupling and complex aerodynamic disturbance of flapping-wing aircraft, this study proposes an environment-adaptive cascade control switching method based on the PID architecture. A physical control closed-loop is established using MWORKS.Sysplorer, with classical PID as the outer main loop and PID, nonlinear compensation PID, and SMC as the inner loop to form a nested structure, which is cross-validated via MATLAB/Simulink. Through the disturbance quantization parameter k, operating conditions are classified into high, medium, and low disturbances for strategy switching. At the classical flapping frequency of 15 Hz, the proposed method converges the attitude error to within 0.06 rad, improves the anti-disturbance performance under high disturbance by 46.2% compared with traditional PID, constrains the phase lag to the stable interval corresponding to the natural frequency of 50 rad/s, and optimizes computational efficiency with the average single-step simulation time ≤ 0.02 s. This method addresses the insufficient full-working-condition adaptability of traditional single control strategies, and provides a highly robust implementation approach for the control of flapping-wing aircraft under complex disturbances.Keywords: Adaptive cascade control, Flapping wing aircraft, Perturbation quantization parameter k, Switchable inner loopSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Flapping-Wing Micro Aerial Vehicles (FWMAVs) have become a research hotspot due to their advantages in maneuverability, safety, and energy efficiency1–5, and they exhibit significant advantages in various practical applications6,7. However, limited by dynamic characteristics such as strong coupling and nonlinearity, the precise control of FWMAVs remains a core challenge that urgently needs to be overcome8,9. PID control is widely used in aircraft control due to its simple structure and strong robustness, which can synergistically optimize the control effect through proportional, integral, and derivative components. In UAV formation control10, some studies have improved the stability of multi-UAV cooperation by optimizing PID parameters and error feedback logic, and PID is often integrated with other algorithms such as sliding mode control to expand performance11–13. For example, the sliding mode PID controller not only retains the smooth adjustment characteristics of PID but also adapts to flight requirements in complex airflow environments by virtue of the strong anti-interference ability of sliding mode control.

Scholars have conducted extensive exploration on the adaptability of PID control: Miku SAITO et al.14 applied PID to the attitude control of flapping-wing aircraft, while using Deep Q-Network (DQN) to assist in learning the P gain parameter, enabling the aircraft to switch seamlessly between two flight states. Mel kou, Lamia added PID as an outer loop, which improved the command in terms of accuracy, but it still difficult to meet the requirements of complex flight tasks for Flapping Wing Micro Aerial Vehicles15. To break through the control bottleneck of linear PID16, nonlinear PID control has become a research hotspot due to its ability to adapt to the inherent nonlinear characteristics of the system8. The system is designed in the form of having a Nonlinear Proportional-Integral-Derivative (NPID) controller and a Higher-Order Sliding Mode Controller (HOSMC), which has been successfully applied to the modeling and control of Fixed-wing Unmanned Aerial Vehicle17, providing ideas and references for the nonlinear control of Flapping Wing Micro Aerial Vehicles.

Sliding Mode Control (SMC) has gained widespread attention in UAV control applications because it works well at handling nonlinearities, coupled dynamics, and external disturbances18. For the trajectory control of Six-axis UAV, Said Khoudiri and colleagues19 developed a nonlinear mathematical model using Newton-Euler equations and introduced the Nonsingular Fast Terminal Sliding Mode Controller (NSTSMC). Simulation results demonstrate that compared to the traditional PID controller, this controller not only achieves precise flight path tracking (with minimal deviation from the planned trajectory) but also effectively reduces disturbances and lowers control energy usage.

For Mass-driven fixed-wing UAV, where Moving Mass Control (MMC) increases system complexity and causes inertial coupling problems, Professor Yuan’s team designed an Adaptive Global Fast Terminal Sliding Mode (GFTSM) controller. This controller combines Prescribed Performance Control (PPC) with Fixed-Time Extended State Observer (FTESO)20. The stability of the control system was theoretically proven using the Lyapunov function. Numerical simulations under various operating conditions show that this controller performs significantly better than PID and Active Disturbance Rejection Control (ADRC) in compensating for system uncertainties and enabling quick response to command signals. SMC is an algorithm known for its stability and robustness, and it has been widely used in the design of Robot controller. Chattering is an inevitable issue with this type of controller; over the past decade, many methods have been put forward to solve this problem12,13,20–24. To tackle the inherent chattering problem of traditional SMC, a method that uses Reinforcement learning to optimize nonlinear functions was proposed in reference25. This method is designed to reduce chattering in Quadrotor UAV SMC controllers. Simulation tests under input delay and external disturbances, along with real flight experiments, show that compared to the Classical chattering suppression strategy, this new method offers better trajectory tracking performance.

The Extended State Observer (ESO) provides an effective approach to enhance the anti-disturbance capability of the control system by estimating system uncertainties and unknown external disturbances in real time20,26,27. Compared with other control methods, ESO requires less measurement information, so it can be implemented more reliably in practical applications. Secondly, the ESO proposed in this paper can estimate the coupling effect based on an accurate model with less system information.

Disturbance-aware nonlinear control with stability analysis has become a key research direction in the field of aircraft control, and relevant studies have provided important theoretical references for the design of switching logic in adaptive control frameworks17,27.

The above studies fully demonstrate the universality and tunability of three Subsystem control and their variants. However, to meet more complex actual control requirements, this paper conducts further research on Perturbation adaptation. Aiming at the problems of existing Control strategy such as lack of systematic comparison, insufficient Multi-channel coupling compensation and weak Physical modeling and control collaboration under the strong coupling, nonlinearity and multi-perturbation attributes of FWMAVs, the goals and contributions of this research are as follows:

Construct a Unified verification framework for three Hybrid control strategy: “PID-PID cascade, PID-NLPID, PID-SMC”. Relying on the Physical simulation environment of MWORKS.Sysplorer and combined with MATLAB/simulink Cross-validation, quantize the Relationship between control accuracy, disturbance rejection and engineering cost of each strategy under different Disturbance intensity (characterized by argument k), clarify the Adaptation criterion between strategies and disturbance scenes, and provide a basis for Engineering control selection of Flapping-wing micro air vehicles.

Establish the mapping relationship between k and Disturbance intensity, propose an adaptive switching logic based on dynamic weights to solve the problem of single strategy adapting to all scenarios, and provide technical support for Flapping-wing micro air vehicles from laboratory verification to engineering application.

Realize Multi-channel coupling compensation based on physical modeling, combine Extended State Observer with dynamic weight optimization to break through the limitation of fixed coefficient compensation; ensure the reliability of results through collaborative verification with MWORKS and MATLAB.

Detailed description of the cascade control framework

After completing the design of three sub-policies, we adopted the cascade control method to integrate the outer loop control with the inner loop multi-policy partial. As a control approach that facilitated rapid disturbance rejection, improved control accuracy, and enhanced system stability, cascade control effectively compensated for the limitations of single control21,28–30. Consequently, we proposed an adaptive cascade control framework based on the aforementioned PID schema. As illustrated in Fig. 3, with the disturbance intensity quantization argument k serving as the core trigger condition, the three policies—PID, NLPID, and SMC—functioned as cascade inner loop sub-modules, enabling smooth policy transitions through real-time disturbance estimation.

Adaptive cascade control schematic.

As illustrated in Fig. 3, the introduction of k represented one of the core innovations of this control strategy. Based on typical application scenarios for flapping-wing aircraft, the mapping relationship between k and disturbance was established by categorizing disturbances into three levels: low disturbance (k [1, 3], which corresponded to steady airflow with wind speeds of less than 1 m/s; medium disturbance (k [4, 12], which corresponded to moderate turbulence with gust speeds ranging from 1 to 3 m/s; and high disturbance (k [13, 24], which corresponded to wind speeds exceeding 3 m/s.

The switching thresholds of k = 3 and k = 12 are determined based on multi-objective optimization: with control accuracy (RMSE), control smoothness, and computational efficiency adopted as the objective function, simulations are conducted to traverse all k values within the interval [1, 24], thereby determining the optimal thresholds that yield comprehensive performance superiority. To solve the risk of switching chattering near the threshold, hysteresis switching logic is introduced: the switching threshold from low to medium disturbance is k = 3.5, medium to low is k = 2.5; medium to high is k = 12.5, high to medium is k = 11.5. Meanwhile, a dwell time constraint is introduced to ensure that the time interval between two adjacent switches is not less than 0.1s and avoid high-frequency chattering.

Parameter validation & reproducibility

To ensure the complete reproducibility of research results and enhance the transparency of the control framework, this section provides a detailed clarification of parameter derivation, simulation fidelity, data processing, and core, eliminating ambiguities between theoretical calculations and experimental processes.

First, simulation fidelity and consistency are strictly guaranteed by adopting a unified configuration for all three control strategies to avoid performance bias caused by inconsistent environmental settings. The simulation duration is set to 30 s, with a sampling time of 0.02 s to meet the requirements of numerical solution accuracy. The initial state of the FWMV is set to zero position and zero attitude. Pulse, step, and sinusoidal disturbances are applied at 0–10 s, and the initial disturbance parameter k is calculated from the motion amplitude to verify the anti-disturbance performance under different strategies. The quantitative mapping relationship between k and wind speed, which is fitted by the sysplorer experimental data of the built-in fluid simulation library: k = 2.3v + 0.7, where v is the wind speed.

Second, the simulation model is mathematically consistent with the FWMV six-degree-of-freedom rigid-body dynamics model established in Section II.B, including Newton-Euler translation and attitude kinematics, moment of inertia matrix, propeller dynamics, and inertial coupling. All physical constraints of the FWMV are modeled through SolidWorks and imported into MWORKS for physical property calculation. The ESO, sliding surface, and dynamic weight switching law strictly follow the design criteria in Sections II.C and II.D, with no additional self-adaptive or parameter-tuning constraints in the actual simulation, ensuring the mathematical transparency of control parameters.

Third, the control quantity is normalized to the interval [-1, 1], and all performance indicators (RMSE, DR, CS) are calculated using the explicit formulas defined in Section E. The calculation code, raw data, and processed results are uploaded to Figshare for open access to verify the authenticity of performance evaluation.

Finally, statistical significance is verified through repeated experimental verification. Each control strategy is independently simulated 20 times under the same disturbance conditions, and all performance indicators are reported with mean ± standard deviation to ensure the stability and reliability of the results. The core parameters of the ESO, PID controller, and sliding mode controller are derived from theoretical pole placement and Lyapunov stability criteria, without parametric fitting for specific simulation scenarios, thus preserving the universal applicability of the proposed control method in engineering.

Results

Experiments and results

To verify the effectiveness of the proposed cascade control strategies—PID-PID, PID-NLPID, and PID-SMC—in the multi-dimensional motion control of flapping-wing aircraft, experiments were conducted using MWORKS.sysplorer multifunctional physical modeling software. This software was based on the derived mechanism of the eight-figure flight closed-loop system and each cascade control strategy. The results obtained included the simulation of the tracking process of the flapping-wing vehicle following a figure-8 reference trajectory under preset disturbance conditions, with position tracking RMSE serving as the core evaluation index. Figure 4 presents a comparison of dynamic position errors for different control methods in multidimensional motion. PID control (solid blue line) achieved rapid error convergence in the early stage (− 10 s) due to its cascade structure (outer loop PID + inner loop PID), which relied on purely linear proportional-integral-derivative (PID) regulation, allowing for immediate response without complex preprocessing. In contrast, the curves for PID-NLPID (orange dashed line) and PID-SMC (green dotted line) exhibited delayed oscillatory responses. This lag stemmed from the intricate inner mechanism of cascade control: PID-NLPID required time to initialize the nonlinear correction of integral and derivative terms based on tanh, while PID-SMC needed to complete disturbance observation and sliding surface construction through an ESO. The “preparation stage” of this nonlinear adaptive module in cascade control introduced a time delay in the activation of disturbance rejection capabilities.

Dynamic position error comparison of control algorithms in multidimensional motion.

After 15 s, the fluctuation of PID-SMC (green dotted line) was less than that of PID-NLPID, which required nonlinear correction due to the robustness of sliding mode control for high disturbance scenarios. The oscillation of the pure linear PID-PID intensified, highlighting the limitations of linear control. Research significance and value

Existing research on flapping wing aircraft control primarily concentrated on single strategies or simple comparisons of dual strategies, lacking a systematic multi-strategy adaptive switching mechanism8,11,12,15. This study categorized disturbances based on the quantization parameter k into three levels: high, medium, and low. It revealed, for the first time, the optimal matching relationship between k and the three strategies—cascade PID, PID-NLPID, and PID-SMC. This coverage surpassed that of the literature15, which did not accommodate multi-scenario disturbances.

This paper aligned with current academic discourse, noting that, similar to the findings in literature11, the PID-SMC demonstrated significant disturbance resistance under strong disturbances, extending its applicability to additional disturbance levels through k. Furthermore, this study corroborated the perspective presented in Reference8, indicating that PID-NLPID outperformed cascade PID under medium disturbances and provided insights into its performance variations across different disturbance intensities.

This paper addressed certain deficiencies in the existing field. The proposed adaptive switching system aimed to overcome the limitations of single PID control, which exhibited strong disturbance robustness, and single sliding mode chattering25. This system filled a gap in strategy dynamic matching modeling under multiple disturbance levels. Lyapunov analysis confirmed the relationship between switching stability and the k value, providing a reusable paradigm for strong nonlinear coupling systems. In engineering applications, the disturbance setting aligned with actual flight environments. The approach of ‘main loop PID fixed + secondary loop strategy switching’ reduced the complexity of parameter settings and fulfilled the practical requirements of engineering applications.

However, the research had limitations. For instance, the model simplified the deformation of the flexible wing, relying solely on simulation verification and lacking physical experiments, which posed potential risks. Future work could establish a more refined nonlinear model that incorporates the flexible wing surface to bridge the gap between simulation and experimental results.

Comparative verification under typical operating conditions

The anti-disturbance performance of the environment adaptive cascade control switching method based on the k value was further verified through multi-dimensional indicators, including tracking error, phase margin, and energy efficiency at a flapping frequency of 15 Hz (Fig. 6). Its advantages were more pronounced than those of a single control strategy.

Energy - control performance coupling of PID - based strategies for flapping - wing flight at 15 Hz.

The traditional single strategy proved inadequate for achieving immunity under broadband disturbances. To enhance energy efficiency and accuracy, pure PID lacked robustness in high disturbance scenarios, while pure SMC exhibited high energy consumption and susceptibility to chattering in low disturbance conditions32. This method compared the strong robustness of PID-PID, PID-NLPID, and PID-SMC through a k-value flexible switching sub-strategy33: Fig. 6 (a) illustrated that the method achieved balanced optimization across multiple indicators; Fig. 6 (b) demonstrated that it constrained phase lag within a stable range by means of switching, thereby avoiding the broadband limitations inherent in a single strategy; Fig. 6 (c) confirmed that the method identified the optimal balance between energy consumption and tracking error at various frequencies; and Fig. 6 (d) and (e) indicated that, from an aerodynamic perspective, the lift coefficient stabilized within a narrow range of 1.196–1.1996 by adjusting the control input through k, resulting in optimized efficiency loss and lift distribution.

MATLAB cross-validation

MATLAB/Simulink simulated the proposed method. By inputting various k values to replicate disturbance scenarios corresponding to different aerodynamic velocities, the visualization results of the three-dimensional phase space and phase trajectory (Fig. 7) demonstrated the method’s feasibility from the perspective of nonlinear system dynamics. In contrast to traditional error comparisons, this verification approach delved deeper into the essence of the system:

Phase space and portrait analysis of flapping - wing system under different control gains.

From the phase space theory of nonlinear dynamic systems, the distribution of phase points and the shape of trajectories directly reflected the stability and chaotic characteristics of the system.

When k = 1 and cascade PID control was adopted, Fig. 7 (a) illustrated a regular and compact spherical distribution of phase points in the three-dimensional phase space, indicating that the system state was highly concentrated and stable. The phase trajectory depicted in Fig. 7 (b) formed a dense and clear ellipse, reflecting that the system evolved in a smooth and periodic (or quasi-periodic) manner. This behavior was entirely consistent with the characteristics of PID-PID’s “low complexity and high smoothness, " thereby verifying the effectiveness of the sub-strategy in low-disturbance scenarios.

When k = 10, although the distribution of phase points in Fig. 7 (c) retained the aggregation trend, it exhibited local dispersion and morphological distortion, which reflected an increase in disturbance complexity. In Fig. 7 (d), the density and uniformity of the elliptical contour of the phase trajectory decreased, providing a dynamic basis for switching to the PID-NLPID sub-strategy under medium disturbance.

When k = 20, after the PID-SMC sub-strategy was enabled, the distribution of the phase points in Fig. 7 (e) extended, yet the overall pattern maintained a regular spherical trend without descending into complete chaos. In Fig. 7 (f), the phase trajectory contour remained controllable, reflecting the suppression effect of sliding mode control on high disturbances and demonstrating the rationality of k-triggered switching.

The attitude angle phase diagrams (Fig. 8) under periodic and chaotic perturbations further verified the adaptability of the strategy. Under periodic perturbations (low and medium k values), the attitude angle phase diagrams exhibited regular patterns, and the time response remained stable. In contrast, under chaotic perturbations (high k values), the attitude angle phase diagrams appeared disordered and oscillated violently. In the disturbed periodic scenario, as illustrated in Fig. 8 (a) and (b), the phase diagram of displayed a distinct interval range, while the phase diagram of formed a closed region with a clear outline. This observation reflected that the evolution of the system’s attitude state under periodic disturbance demonstrated certain regularity and stability. The corresponding time response curve also remained relatively stable, indicating the adoption of the control sub-strategy suited for low and medium disturbances at that time.

Disturbance - induced dynamic behaviors: periodic vs. chaotic motion of flapping - wing system.

However, in the disturbed chaotic scenario, the phase diagram of θ in (c) and (d) exhibited a broader interval, while the phase diagram of presented a chaotic annular distribution. This finding indicated that the evolution of the system’s attitude state became complex and difficult to predict under chaotic disturbances. The corresponding time response curves displayed significant oscillations, reflecting the substantial influence of chaotic disturbance on attitude, and a robust strategy was adopted at that time.

Based on the sub-strategy selection of k, the switching threshold verified the asymptotic stability of the switching point, thereby determining the parameter ranges that activated different control schemes. When k ∈ [1, 3], the PID-PID sub-strategy activated to accommodate the low-disturbance steady-state task due to its low complexity and high smoothness. When k ∈ [4, 12], the PID-NLPID sub-strategy engaged to address the low-disturbance steady-state task, maintaining low complexity and high smoothness. When k ∈ [13, 24], the PID-SMC sub-strategy was enabled to suppress extreme disturbances, leveraging the strong robustness of sliding mode control.

Conclusions

This study establishes the mapping relationships among disturbance intensity, control strategy, and performance metrics, providing a quantifiable basis for the engineering selection of control strategies for flapping wing aircraft. Its adaptive cascade architecture offers a referential paradigm for the control design of strongly nonlinear and multi-disturbance systems, presenting both theoretical significance and practical application prospects.

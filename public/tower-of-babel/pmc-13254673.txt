Gut bacterial species, serum metabolites, and serum cytokines associated with broodiness in chickens
Authors: Tian Lu, Yaling Chen, Qin He, Bin Zheng, Denghua Deng, Xinwei Xiong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Increasing evidence suggests that the gut microbiota, serving as a “virtual endocrine organ”, potentially modulates reproductive behavior in poultry via the gut-brain and gut-ovary axes. Broodiness in hens inhibits egg-laying activity and causes major economic losses in native chicken breeds, but its micro-physiological basis remains unclear. This study used shotgun metagenomic sequencing to delineate the cecal bacterial species associated with brooding status in Chinese Kangle chickens. We identified 34 cecal bacterial species exhibiting significantly varying abundances between the broodiness and control groups, including six species (e.g., Bacteroides sp. An51A and Phocaeicola barnesiae) that were significantly enriched in the broodiness group. Additionally, 28 species significantly enriched in the control group were screened. Among them, Subdoligranulum variabile and Oribacterium asaccharolyticum served as key biomarkers for distinguishing brooding status in Kangle chickens and were associated with functional shifts in the cecal microbiome. Non-targeted metabolomic analysis identified 17 differential metabolites, among which seven (e.g., (13E) -11a-hydroxy-9,15-dioxoprost-13-enoic acid and d-arabitol) were defined as metabolic markers of the broody state and were significantly associated with Subdoligranulum variabile and Oribacterium asaccharolyticum. In addition, our results suggest that serum cytokines, such as IFN-γ and IL-22, are potentially associated with the broody state and the alterations in both serum metabolites and the gut microbiota (e.g., Subdoligranulum variabile and Oribacterium asaccharolyticum). These findings provide a new insight into the mechanisms underlying reproductive behavior in poultry and offer a theoretical basis for alleviating broodiness through microecological interventions, thereby improving the reproductive efficiency of indigenous chicken breeds.

Introduction

The poultry industry is an important component of the agricultural economy, and the egg production performance of laying chickens is directly linked to farming profitability. Brooding behavior, or "broodiness," is a natural reproductive instinct in poultry, which is often characterized by a series of behavioral traits, including persistent nest building, turning and retrieving eggs, distinctive clucking sounds, and nest defense behaviors, which can lead to the atrophy of the oviduct and ovaries, subsequently halting egg production, causing economic losses in the poultry farming industry (Romanov et al., 2002). Genetic improvements aimed at enhancing egg production in high-yield commercial hybrid-laying chickens have significantly controlled brooding behavior, but these strategies are predominantly effective in native chicken breeds (Jiang et al., 2010; Namken et al., 2017). Chinese native chicken breeds exhibit desirable traits, including strong environmental adaptability, tolerance to rough feed, good disease resistance, and meat quality that aligns with the Chinese consumer preferences (Chen et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2024a; Xiong et al., 2023). However, the lack of systematic breeding leads to the strong broodiness and low egg production of these breeds, limiting their economic potential (Lei et al., 2024). Several factors contribute to brooding behavior in chickens, including genetic background, light cycles, endocrine hormones, and management practices (Hu et al., 2025). Additionally, proper adjustment of the nutritional composition of feed can effectively regulate the brooding frequency. For example, tryptophan supplementation in the diet alters cecal microbiota and affects microbial metabolites (Khattak and Helmbrecht, 2019). The inclusion of dietary fiber can significantly alter the diversity and abundance of key bacteria in the chicken cecum, which in turn affects the production of microbial metabolites such as short-chain fatty acids (Deehan et al., 2020). These metabolites play a crucial role in maintaining the health and integrity of intestinal epithelial cells and participate in immune homeostasis and neuro-metabolic regulation, which are closely linked to brooding behavior in chickens (Jadhav et al., 2022). These suggest that gut microbiota may be involved in the induction of brooding behavior in chickens.

In recent years, increasing evidence has reported that the gut microbiota may play a crucial role in triggering broodiness in poultry. The gut microbiota was reported to interact directly or indirectly with endocrine hormones, such as follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), anti-Mullerian hormone, prolactin, and luteinizing hormone, thereby affecting ovarian development (Qi et al., 2021). This provides a new perspective, suggesting that the gut microbiota may indirectly regulate reproductive behavior in chickens through interactions with host hormones. Additionally, various microorganisms, such as Streptococcus and Lactobacillus in the ileum and butyrate-producing bacteria and Bacteroides in the cecum, have been identified in the gut of brooding Muscovy ducks (Zhu et al., 2024); the top 20 most abundant gut microbes are significantly correlated with serum hormone levels, indicating the regulation of brooding behavior by the interaction between hormones and gut microbiota. Furthermore, changes in the gut microbiota are positively correlated with elevated serum estrogen and FSH levels in chickens, which can promote ovarian development (Xu et al., 2023) and improve egg production efficiency (Qin et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2022). Moreover, the combined analysis of gut microbiota and serum metabolomics is considered an effective tool for analyzing important economic traits, such as growth efficiency (Feng et al., 2023), feed conversion rate (Elsasser et al., 2023), and egg production performance (Wu et al., 2025b). Therefore, we hypothesized that broody behavior is potentially accompanied by profound changes in gut microbiota structure and serum metabolite profiles; however, systematic experimental evidence to support this hypothesis remains lacking.

Kangle chicken is a superior indigenous dual-purpose (meat and egg production) breed in China; it is characterized by tender and juicy meat, strong disease resistance, and a consistent rate of egg production (Xiong et al., 2023). However, brooding behavior interrupts egg production and reduces reproductive efficiency, limiting the large-scale promotion of this breed. Therefore, gaining a deeper understanding of the mechanisms underlying its brooding behavior is of significant scientific value. Currently, research on poultry broodiness primarily focuses on behavioral observations, hormone measurements, and the identification of key genes (Chen and Wen, 2025; Shen et al., 2012), while there is a lack of systematic studies on the physiological mechanisms underlying broodiness from the perspective of microbiome and metabolome associations. Therefore, in this study, based on metagenomic sequencing and non-targeted metabolomics analyses, we comparatively analyzed the gut microbiota structure and serum metabolite concentrations between broody and non-broody control Kangle chickens; moreover, we aimed to identify key microbial taxa, cytokines, and metabolites significantly associated with the broody state. Finally, we integrated microbiome and metabolome data to perform association network analysis and explore potential "microbe-metabolite" correlations.

Materials and methods

Ethics statement

All procedures involving chickens were performed by strictly following the guidelines for the care and use of experimental animals as approved by the State Council of the People’s Republic of China. This study was approved by the ethics committee of Nanchang Normal University (No. NCNU2021–006).

Experimental Kangle chickens and sample collection

In this study, we used ten female Kangle chickens (age, 365 days), including four broodiness Kangle chickens (broodiness group) and six normal laying Kangle chickens (control group), all hatched and raised at Nanshi Technology Co., Ltd. (Nanchang, China). Under standardized feeding and management conditions, the chickens were provided ad libitum access to the same feed and water. Throughout the study period (from hatching to the end), no chickens showed obvious disease, and none were treated with probiotics or antibiotics. Chickens were selected for the broodiness group based on the following phenotypic traits: after the egg-laying period, they exhibited increased body temperature, messy feathers, reduced appetite, and nest-building behavior, leading to the cessation of egg production (He et al., 2022a; Wu et al., 2019). For the control group, the chickens were selected as much as possible from the same or adjacent cages as the broody chickens. All chickens were fasted for 12 h, followed by blood sample collection in the morning. The blood samples, incubated at room temperature for 2 h, were centrifuged at 3000 × g for 10 min at 4°C. The serum samples were collected and stored at −20°C for subsequent analysis. To obtain cecal contents, all experimental chickens were humanely euthanized. Following dissection, the abdominal cavity was exposed, and the intact cecum was carefully isolated and removed. Subsequently, the contents were obtained by gently massaging the intestine from one end to the other and immediately transferred into pre-labeled cryovials (Zhang et al., 2020b). These samples were immediately immersed in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80°C for further analysis.

Microbial DNA extraction of cecal content

Microbial DNA was extracted from the cecal contents of ten Kangle chickens using a QIAamp Fast DNA Stool Mini Kit (Qiagen, Germany) according to the protocol provided by the manufacturer. The concentration and purity of the DNA samples were analyzed using a Nanodrop-2000 UV-VIS spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, MA, USA); acceptable purity was considered at an OD260/280 ratio of 1.8-2.0, whereas higher and lower ratios were interpreted as indicative of RNA and protein contamination, respectively (Qi et al., 2019). DNA integrity and approximate fragment size distribution were assessed through electrophoresis of each sample (2 μL) conducted using 0.8% (w/v) agarose gels stained with ethidium bromide; high-quality genomic DNA samples generating clear high-molecular weight bands and minimal smearing were used for library construction. Qualified cecal microbial DNA samples were stored at −80°C for metagenomic sequencing.

Metagenomic sequencing and analysis

All ten microbial DNA samples acquired from the cecal contents of Kangle chickens were subjected to metagenomic sequencing using the DNBSEQ-T7 platform according to a protocol we previously described (Xiong et al., 2024b). In brief, sequencing libraries were constructed using the MGI Easy Universal DNA Library Prep Set Kit (MGI Tech Co., Shenzhen, China) strictly following the manufacturer’s operational guidelines. Following quality inspection, the purified PCR products were denatured and circularized to form single-stranded circular DNA libraries. Subsequently, rolling circle amplification was performed to amplify these single-stranded circular DNA molecules, generating DNA nanoballs (DNBs) with multiple copies of the target DNA templates. Utilizing a high-intensity DNA nanochip technique, eligible DNBs were immobilized on a patterned nanoarray, followed by high-throughput sequencing using the DNBSEQ-T7 sequencing platform (MGI Tech Co.).

To obtain clean sequencing data, the raw reads were aligned against the chicken reference genome (bGalGal1. mat. broiler. GRCg7b) using Bowtie2 v2.2.4 to filter out low-quality reads, adapters, and host contaminants (Bolger et al., 2014; Langmead and Salzberg, 2012). Clean data were assembled using MEGAHIT (v1.2.9) (Li et al., 2015). Contigs shorter than 500 bp were discarded to reduce spurious assemblies. Open reading frames (ORFs) were predicted based on the remaining contigs using MetaGeneMark (v2.10) (Li et al., 2025; Zhu et al., 2010), which performs ab initio gene prediction of metagenomic sequences. To construct a non-redundant gene catalog, all predicted ORFs from all samples were clustered with CD-HIT (Fu et al., 2012) considering 95% nucleotide identity and 90% coverage thresholds; within each cluster, the longest ORF was retained as the representative unigene. Taxonomic assignment of the unigenes was performed by aligning their deduced amino acid sequences against the NCBI non-redundant protein (Munyoki et al., 2025) database using DIAMOND software (Buchfink et al., 2015). Based on the alignment results, sequences with e-values ≤ min. e-value × 10 (Pedersen et al., 2016) were screened via the LCA algorithm and then submitted to MEGAN for subsequent taxonomic annotation (Liu et al., 2025). Additionally, unigenes were aligned against sequences in functional databases, including the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) (Xie et al., 2011) and Carbohydrate-Active Enzymes (CAZy) (Tang et al., 2025), for functional annotation using DIAMOND software.

The metabolomic profiles of Kangle chicken serum samples

Metabolomic profiles were determined for all ten Kangle chicken serum samples using the method we previously described (Xiong et al., 2022). Briefly, frozen sera were thawed on ice or at 4°C. Subsequently, 100 μL of each serum sample was mixed with 400 μL of pre-chilled methanol to precipitate proteins and extract small-molecule metabolites. The mixture was vortexed for at least 30 s, incubated on ice for 10–15 min, and centrifuged at 12000 × g for 10 min at 4°C. A Vanquish UHPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, MA, USA) coupled with an Orbitrap Q Exactive series mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, MA, USA) was used for LC-MS/MS analysis. The raw LC-MS data files were processed using Compound Discoverer 3.0 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, MA, USA) for peak detection, retention-time alignment, deconvolution of adducts and isotopes, and peak area integration. Putative metabolite identities were assigned by matching accurate mass and MS/MS fragmentation patterns to in-house and public spectral libraries. Data were further processed on CentOS 6.6 using R (version 3.4.3) and Python (version 2.7.6). Data quality control analysis was performed using the MetNormalizer function in R (version 3.4.3).

Determination of serum cytokines and lipopolysaccharides (LPS)

Serum levels of IL-22, IL-17, IL-6, IL-5, INF-γ, IL-1β, and LPS were quantified in ten Kangle chickens through enzyme-linked immunoassay (ELISA; double antibody sandwich method) as previously described (Zhang et al., 2020a). Comparative analyses and bar-plot visualizations were performed using the ggpubr package in R (version 3.4.3).

Statistical analysis

Based on the relative abundance profiles, positive co-abundance and negative co-exclusion correlations between microbial species and serum metabolites were calculated via the Sparse correlations for compositional data (SparCC) approach (Friedman and Alm, 2012; Segata et al., 2011). Subsequently, co-occurrence network construction and visualization were carried out with the assistance of Cytoscape software (version 3.6.1). Differentially abundant taxa between the broodiness group and the control groups were identified via the Linear Discriminant Analysis Effect Size (LEfSe) algorithm (Shen et al., 2024), with a significance level of α = 0.01 and an LDA score threshold of ≥ 2.0. We used random forest analysis with modified settings (ntree = 1000) to screen indicator bacterial species for judging the brooding status of Kangle chickens (Jin et al., 2024). Spearman correlation analysis was used to explore the potential correlations between differential microbial taxa and KEGG functional pathways or CAZyme entries (Xu et al., 2025; Yin et al., 2023). The Story false discovery rate (FDR) correction method was used for multiple testing corrections (Khan and Chousalkar, 2020). Partial Least Squares Discriminant Analysis (PLS-DA) was adopted to analyze the differences in cecal microbiota and serum metabolite profiles between the broodiness and control groups (Lorenzo-Rebenaque et al., 2023; Olivieri et al., 2025).

Result

Bacterial species associated with broodiness in Kangle chickens identified via metagenomic sequencing

To identify the cecal bacterial species associated with broodiness in Kangle chickens and to assess the function of the gut microbiota, we performed metagenomic sequencing analysis of ten cecal samples. After sequence assembly, the total length of the assembled sequences reached 4,628,181,542 bp, with an average length of 1150 bp (Supplementary Table S1). The phylogenetic composition of cecal microbiota was determined by comparing the assembled sequences with those in the non-redundant database of the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI). In total, 40 phyla and 299 genera were identified in the ten samples, with Bacteroidota and Bacillota representing the most abundant phyla. Bacteroides, Alistipes, and Prevotella were the three most predominant genera within the cecal microbiota. Moreover, a total of 917 cecal bacterial species were detected in ten samples, and Alistipes sp. CAG:831 showed the highest relative abundance.

We compared the bacterial composition of the gut microbiome between the broodiness and control groups to identify bacterial species related to the brooding behavior of Kangle chickens. Initially, the bacterial α-diversity analysis demonstrated that the InvSimpson and Shannon indexes differed significantly between the broodiness and control groups (Fig. 1A, B). PLS-DA results also verified distinct variations in cecal bacterial species between the two groups (Fig. 1C). Moreover, a total of 34 differential bacterial species were identified between groups, which included 6 and 28 species significantly enriched in the broodiness and control groups, respectively (Fig. 1D, Supplementary Table S2). The dominant bacteria in the intestinal tract of the broodiness group mainly consisted of Phocaeicola spp. (Phocaeicola salanitronis and Phocaeicola barnesiae) and Bacteroides spp. (Bacteroides sp. An51A and Bacteroides sp. An279). In the control group, four Bacteroides spp. (e.g., Bacteroides fluxus and Bacteroides gallinarum), two Cloacibacillus spp. (Cloacibacillus porcorum and Cloacibacillus sp. An23), and two Gemmiger spp. (Gemmiger formicilis and Gemmiger sp. An120) were enriched. Subsequently, using species-level metagenomic sequencing data, a random forest analysis was performed to identify specific cecal bacterial biomarkers associated with the broody state in Kangle chickens (Fig. 1E). Consistent with the LEfSe analysis, two cecal bacterial species, Subdoligranulum variabile and Oribacterium asaccharolyticum, exhibited remarkable abundance discrepancies between the broodiness and control groups. Identification of the functional capacity of cecal microbiota associated with broodiness

Subsequently, we compared the potential functional profiles of the cecal microbiome between the broodiness and control groups via sequence alignment and annotation of microbial genes against KEGG and CAZymes databases. In total, we identified 15 differentially enriched KEGG pathways between the broodiness and control groups, including ten significantly enriched functional terms in the broodiness group and five highly abundant functional terms in the control group (Fig. 2A). The enriched functional pathways in the control group were primarily involved in thiamine metabolism (ko00730), styrene degradation (ko00643), folate biosynthesis (ko00790), and carbapenem biosynthesis (ko00332). Spearman’s correlation analysis was performed to assess the relationships between the alterations of cecal bacterial species and the differential KEGG pathways, as well as the connections between varied bacterial species and differentially expressed CAZymes. Five KEGG pathways, including styrene degradation (ko00643), folate biosynthesis (ko00790), and thiamine metabolism (ko00730), showed significantly positive correlations with the two cecal microbial species (P < 0.05). In comparison, seven KEGG pathways, including starch and sucrose metabolism (ko00500), fatty acid degradation (ko00071), and metabolism of xenobiotics by cytochrome P450 (ko00980), were significantly negatively correlated with the two differential bacterial species (P < 0.05) (Fig. 2C). Moreover, four CAZymes enriched in the broodiness group, consisting of one glycosyltransferase (GT2) and three glycoside hydrolases (GH97, GH43, GH26), enriched in the broodiness group, exhibited significant negative correlation with the two different species (P < 0.05). Among the two CAZymes enriched in the control group (CBM48 and CE9), CBM48 was notably positively correlated with Subdoligranulum variabile (P < 0.05), while CE9 exhibited a significant positive correlation with Oribacterium asaccharolyticum (P < 0.05) (Fig. 2D). These results suggested that changes in cecal bacterial species led to shifts in CAZymes and KEGG pathways.

Identification of serum metabolites associated with broodiness of Kangle chickens via LC-MS

To systematically evaluate differences in the serum metabolome between the control and broodiness groups, untargeted serum metabolomic profiles were analyzed using Liquid Chromatography-Mass Spectrometry (LC-MS). After standardized processing, a total of 343 metabolites were identified. PLS-DA revealed significant differences in the serum metabolites between the broodiness and control groups (Fig. 3A). We identified 17 metabolites that showed significant differential enrichment between the broodiness and control groups (Wilcoxon, P < 0.05; Supplementary Table S3), including 3 metabolites (D-arabitol, (13E)-11a-Hydroxy-9,15-dioxoprost-13-enoic acid, and 5′-Methylthioadenosine) enriched in the broodiness group and 14 (e.g., diaminopimelic acid, mesaconate, and uric acid) enriched in the control group. Subsequently, we identified serum metabolites that serve as markers for the broody state in Kangle chickens using a random forest analysis (Fig. 3B). Notably, consistent with the Wilcoxon analysis, seven serum metabolites (glutaric acid, diaminopimelic acid, mesaconate, epiandrosterone, N-acetylserotonin, D-arabitol, and (13E)-11a-Hydroxy-9,15-dioxoprost-13-enoic acid) exhibited significant differences between the broodiness and control groups, and served as unique serum metabolic biomarkers for the broody state of Kangle chickens with stable diagnostic efficacy. The area under the curve (AUC) values were 96.88% and 64% for the broodiness (Supplementary Figure S2A) and control groups (Supplementary Figure S2B), respectively. Differential serum metabolites screened from the broodiness group and control group were separately annotated and categorized based on KEGG pathway databases. Serum metabolites having higher abundances in the broodiness group were notably enriched in the cysteine and methionine metabolism pathway (Fig. 3C). Discussion

The gut microbiota plays a crucial role in the growth, immunity, and metabolism of poultry and is closely associated with brooding behavior (Zhu et al., 2024). In this study, we used metagenomic sequencing to compare the composition of the cecal microbiota in Kangle chickens across physiological stages and identified cecal bacterial species correlated with brooding behavior. Oribacterium asaccharolyticum and Subdoligranulum variabile demonstrated high diagnostic accuracy in distinguishing the brooding states in Kangle chickens. Furthermore, we performed correlation analyses between different cecal bacterial species, serum metabolites, and serum cytokine levels. We detected a close association between changes in the cecal microbiota and the variations in serum metabolomics and cytokine content, which may contribute to the onset of brooding behavior. Our findings provide evidence for the role of gut microbiota and serum metabolites in the brooding behavior of Kangle chickens.

In accordance with previous studies (Gilroy et al., 2021; Segura-Wang et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2024), Bacteroides, Prevotella, and Alistipes were the most dominant phyla in the chicken gut microbiota. Furthermore, random forest and LEfSe analyses identified two differentially abundant bacterial species, Subdoligranulum variabile and Oribacterium asaccharolyticum, which can serve as key bacterial biomarkers for the brooding status of Kangle chickens. Consistent with previous findings in Muscovy ducks (Zhu et al., 2024), species belonging to Firmicutes have been reported to significantly correlate with follicle-stimulating hormones, suggesting that interactions between hormones and the intestinal microbiota modulate follicular development in Kangle chickens. Moreover, Subdoligranulum variabile, a strictly anaerobic species (Van Hul et al., 2020), exhibits probiotic effects, including the suppression of food allergies and alleviation of mucosal inflammation (Abdel-Gadir et al., 2019; Singh et al., 2022). Previous research has demonstrated a positive causal relationship between Subdoligranulum variabile and TSG-6, a multifunctional protein exhibiting anti-inflammatory and tissue-protective properties (Li et al., 2024). Oribacterium asaccharolyticum is an anaerobic gram-positive bacterium with physiological characteristics that suggest its association with the gut microbiota and potential involvement in the metabolism and fermentation of complex carbohydrates, including the degradation of indigestible sugars and the production of short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) (Sizova et al., 2014). Additionally, the stability of gut microbiota is closely associated with nutrient absorption and the immune status of the host (Morrison and Preston, 2016). Moreover, several bacterial taxa enriched in the broodiness group were functionally important. Bacteroides sp. An51A belongs to the genus Bacteroides; previous studies have reported considerable correlations between this genus and different physiological states in birds (Ma et al., 2024; Zhu et al., 2024). Notably, a recent study on Zhedong White geese provided valuable insights into the role of Bacteroides in broody behavior and identified a Bacteroides species as a key driver promoting brooding behavior in geese, potentially through the stimulation of pituitary prolactin secretion (Zhang et al., 2024). Moreover, Prevotella spp. (e.g., Prevotella hominis and Prevotella bryantii) are enriched in the intestines of broody Kangle chickens. This pattern is consistent with the Prevotella -enriched microbial profiles previously reported in geese exhibiting broody behavior or altered reproductive hormone levels (Zhang et al., 2024), which suggests the potential contribution of this genus in the onset of broody behavior via carbohydrate fermentation and production of SCFAs (Grond et al., 2018; Xu et al., 2024) and coupling of energy metabolism with endocrine signaling. Functional analysis further revealed that the broodiness group exhibited significant enrichment of metabolic pathways related to starch and sucrose metabolism (ko00500) and fatty acid degradation (ko00071) in the cecal microbiota. These metabolic pathways are closely associated with energy metabolism and reproduction in birds (Moustakli et al., 2025; Qi et al., 2021). Enrichment of starch and sucrose metabolism suggests that the microbiota in the broodiness group may enhance carbohydrate digestion and absorption (Wang et al., 2023), providing more energy sources to support the physiological processes of the host (Liu et al., 2024c). In contrast, the enrichment of thiamine metabolism (ko00730) and folate biosynthesis (ko00790) pathways, which are closely associated with cell proliferation (Madhok et al., 2010), antioxidant activity (Xie et al., 2024), and nucleic acid synthesis (Frost et al., 2025), provides essential material and environmental support in the control group for maintaining active follicular development and egg-laying performance in chickens.

Serum metabolomics analysis revealed that glutaric acid, diaminopimelic acid, mesaconate, epiandrosterone, N-acetylserotonin, D-arabitol, and (13E)-11α-Hydroxy-9,15-dioxoprost-13-enoic acid exhibited significantly varying abundance between the broodiness and control groups and served as unique serum metabolic biomarkers for the broody state. (13E)-11α-Hydroxy-9,15-dioxoprost-13-enoic acid is a prostaglandin analog (Miyamoto et al., 1976) potentially associated with a low-grade inflammatory state triggered by alterations in gut microbiota structure (Vannuccini et al., 2016). These compounds can influence the function of the central nervous system or ovaries, promoting behavioral changes (Liu et al., 2023) and ovarian atrophy. Excessive prostaglandins can inhibit the development and maturation of follicles (Jia et al., 2010) and may promote apoptosis in ovarian cells (Fraser et al., 1999), directly leading to ovarian atrophy and cessation of egg production. This is consistent with the altered reproductive physiology observed in brooding chickens. Emerging evidence suggests that D-arabitol may influence host metabolic phenotypes by modulating the gut microbiota and SCFA related pathways (Zhong et al., 2025). Previous research reported D-arabitol in serum metabolomic profiles of poultry and its differential abundance under distinct physiological and pathological conditions (Liu et al., 2024b). The higher levels of glutaric acid and mesaconate in the control group reflect adequate nutrition and vigorous metabolism in the organism: Glutaric acid, as a key intermediate in the degradation pathways of lysine and tryptophan (Olivera-Bravo et al., 2015), indicates active amino acid metabolism required to meet the body's protein synthesis demands. Mesaconate, a by-product of itaconic acid catabolism, implies the efficient utilization of branched-chain amino acids and smooth operation of the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle (He et al., 2022b). Together, these processes provide a robust and sustained mitochondrial ATP output for the highly energy-demanding physiological processes of high egg production. Additionally, mesaconate exhibits strong anti-inflammatory effects (Ohm et al., 2024); it can clear oxidative stress and provide a healthy redox environment for active follicular development (Wu et al., 2025a; Zhang et al., 2025). This reduction makes the ovaries of brooding chickens more susceptible to oxidative damage, accelerating follicular atresia.

The direct trigger for brooding behavior is prolactin (PRL). The prolactin receptor (PRLR) belongs to the cytokine receptor family (Standing et al., 2022); binding of PRL to PRLR activates the JAK-STAT signaling pathway, thereby mediating the transmission of signals involved in brooding behavior. Notably, this signaling pathway also functions as a core signaling cascade for various cytokines. Xiong et al. (2024a) reported that differentially expressed genes associated with brooding behavior are significantly enriched in the cytokine-cytokine receptor pathway, indicating a close association between brooding behavior and cytokines. In the present study, IFN-γ and IL-22 significantly differed between the broodiness and control groups and exhibited a significant negative correlation with Subdoligranulum variabile and Oribacterium asaccharolyticum. Broodiness is characterized by elevated prolactin levels, which exert immunomodulatory effects by promoting T-cell activation and upregulating Th1-type cytokines such as IFN-γ (Matera et al., 1999), providing a pro-inflammatory background for mucosal immune responses. Moreover, several studies indicated that, under chronic low-grade inflammation, IL-22 and IFN-γ promote immune responses and are involved in immune regulatory and repair functions, preventing excessive immune reactions that cause damage to the body (Keir et al., 2020). IL-22 promotes intestinal epithelial repair, enhances the expression of antimicrobial peptides, and helps maintain intestinal immune homeostasis (Parks et al., 2015). IFN-γ enhances intestinal barrier function and regulates immune tolerance, thereby preventing excessive immune activation and reducing intestinal damage (Kano et al., 2009; Nava et al., 2010). Additionally, the intestinal microbiota, especially Bacteroides species, upregulate the IL-22-related pathway, thereby contributing to intestinal barrier integrity and preventing excessive inflammation-associated damage (Ihekweazu et al., 2021). Moreover, indole derivatives derived from bacterial tryptophan catabolism function as aryl hydrocarbon receptor (Reda et al., 2024) agonists (Wlodarska et al., 2017), induce IL-22 production and strengthen the intestinal barrier function, thereby coupling gut microbial activity with neuroendocrine and immune regulation (He et al., 2021; Roager and Licht, 2018). Therefore, IL-22 and IFN-γ play an important immune regulatory role in broody chickens and facilitate the maintenance of the stable intestinal barrier and normal physiological conditions.

Conclusion

This study reveals significant differences in cecal microbiota composition, functional activity, serum metabolites, and serum cytokine levels between the broodiness and control groups. Two bacterial species, Subdoligranulum variabile and Oribacterium asaccharolyticum, were identified as the key cecal microbial species associated with the broody state. Changes in the CAZymes enzyme system and KEGG pathways in the cecal microbiota were significantly correlated. Non-targeted serum metabolomic analysis indicated that the enrichment of (13E)-11a-hydroxy-9,15-dioxoprost-13-enoic acid and D-arabitol was associated with broodiness in chickens and the abundance of two key cecal bacterial species. Furthermore, Subdoligranulum variabile and Oribacterium asaccharolyticum in the gut microbiota may influence the broody state in chickens by altering metabolites and serum cytokines. This study reveals the interactions between the cecal microbiota, metabolites, and serum cytokines in broodiness chickens, providing a theoretical foundation for alleviating broodiness and improving the reproductive efficiency of indigenous chickens through microecological interventions.
